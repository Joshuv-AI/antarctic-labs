Nonlinear driving mechanisms of system adaptability in China’s art and cultural tourism industry
Authors: Weiting Shi, Wenkai Shi, Ye He, Yuwei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the transformation of the cultural tourism industry from scale expansion to high-quality development, increasing attention has been paid to the synergistic mechanisms between culture and tourism. However, existing researches have predominantly focused on macro-level coordinated development, while systematic quantitative analyses of art and cultural tourism—centered on artistic creation and expression—remain limited, particularly in terms of factor matching and dynamic adaptation mechanisms. Therefore, this research takes China’s art and cultural tourism as the research object and utilizes panel data from 31 provincial administrative regions from 2014 to 2023. From a spatiotemporal perspective, the study examines the evolutionary characteristics of system adaptability and coupling coordination degree. Furthermore, the nonlinear driving mechanisms of system adaptability are explored by integrating the XGBoost model with SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations). The results indicate that: (1) during the study period, the overall level of system adaptability in China’s art and cultural tourism exhibits a fluctuating upward trend, but remains primarily at a low to intermediate stage, with significant regional disparities; (2) system adaptability and coupling coordination degree show consistent evolutionary trends, while system adaptability is more sensitive in capturing the structural and functional matching between art and cultural tourism; (3) based on the XGBoost–SHAP analysis, ecological environment and public service capacity constitute the fundamental constraints of system adaptability, transportation and information infrastructure provide spatial and functional support, whereas cultural education and innovation investment serve as the core endogenous drivers for sustained improvement; (4) the effects of different driving factors on system adaptability exhibit significant nonlinear and stage-specific characteristics, indicating that the evolution of system adaptability is a dynamic process driven by the coordinated interaction of multiple factors.Keywords: Art and cultural tourism, System adaptability, Coupling coordination degree, Driving factors, ChinaSubject terms: Economics, Economics, Geography, Geography

Introduction

With the advancement of socio-economic development and the upgrading of consumption structures, industrial integration has become a crucial pathway for promoting the transformation of modern service industries. Compared with single-industry development models, cross-industry integration significantly enhances the overall efficiency and development momentum of industrial systems through resource integration and functional reconfiguration. In this context, the integration of the cultural and tourism industries has gradually emerged as a major global trend in tourism development. Cultural tourism, in particular, has become an important component of the tourism market and demonstrates sustained growth potential1.

In the Chinese context, the integration of culture and tourism has been elevated to a national strategy. The 14th Five-Year Plan for Tourism Development, issued by the Chinese government in January 2022, explicitly emphasizes promoting the deep integration of culture and tourism, fostering new forms of cultural tourism products, improving the quality of cultural tourism supply, and strengthening the market-leading role of cultural tourism. In June 2024, the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, together with multiple governmental departments, released the Guidelines on Promoting High-Quality Development of Tourism Public Services, which highlight the importance of integrating cultural and tourism public service functions. Driven by both market demand and policy guidance, cultural tourism products continue to evolve, providing both market and institutional foundations for industrial integration.

From the perspective of the evolutionary trajectory of cultural tourism, the sector has undergone a transformation from a “sightseeing-oriented” model to an “experience-oriented” model and further toward an “immersive” model. In the early stage, cultural tourism primarily relied on historical sites and traditional scenic attractions, focusing on resource display and representing a typical sightseeing-oriented pattern2–5. With the diversification of tourist demand and the development of the experience economy, cultural tourism gradually shifted toward an experience-oriented model emphasizing participation and interaction6. Furthermore, driven by immersive technologies and creative expression, immersive cultural tourism—centered on artistic creation and narrative scenarios—has progressively emerged as a key direction in the integration of culture and tourism.

During this evolutionary process, art and cultural tourism products, characterized by artistic expression and narrative storytelling, have gradually developed and become an important practical form of cultural–tourism integration. For example, performances such as The Longest Day in Chang’an and Song of Everlasting Sorrow in Xi’an, Shaanxi Province, as well as Romance of the Song Dynasty in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, are all rooted in regional historical and cultural resources. Through innovative stage art and immersive experience design, these projects achieve deep integration between artistic expression and tourism consumption. Compared with traditional forms of cultural tourism, such products differ significantly in content production, spatial organization, and consumption experience, exhibiting stronger interactivity and participation while imposing higher requirements on industrial coordination and resource allocation.

Although the coordinated development of the cultural and tourism industries has attracted considerable scholarly attention, the synergistic mechanisms and operational logic of specific cultural segments—particularly cultural tourism centered on artistic production—remain insufficiently explored. Therefore, this study takes the art and cultural industry and the tourism industry in China from 2014 to 2023 as the research object, focusing on the issue of system adaptability in the process of industrial integration and constructing an analytical framework for system adaptability under the context of integrated industrial development. First, a coupling coordination model and an adaptability evaluation model are employed to characterize the level of coordination and structural matching between the two industries, while kernel density estimation is used to analyze the spatiotemporal evolution of system adaptability. On this basis, the XGBoost model is introduced to conduct nonlinear modeling of multidimensional factors influencing system adaptability, and the SHAP method is applied to interpret the marginal contributions and influence pathways of these factors, thereby revealing the internal mechanisms underlying the evolution of system adaptability in the coordinated development of the cultural and tourism industries. The remainder of this research is organised as follows. Section 2 defines the concept of art and cultural tourism. Section 3 provides a systematic review of the relevant literature. Section 4 outlines the research methods and data sources. Section 5 presents the empirical results and conducts a mechanism analysis. Finally, Sect. 6 summarises the main conclusions and discusses the corresponding policy implications.

Concept definition

Art and cultural industry

The concept of the cultural industry can be traced back to the critical theory framework proposed by Theodor Adorno and Max Horkheimer7. In their theoretical context, the cultural industry was viewed as a mass cultural system formed through standardized production mechanisms in capitalist societies, with its core lying in the industrial production of cultural products and their role in shaping social consciousness. At this stage, the concept emphasized the ideological attributes of culture rather than its economic attributes.

With the deepening understanding of the relationship between culture and economy, scholars have gradually redefined the cultural industry from industrial and economic perspectives. The cultural industry is generally regarded as an industrial form that organizes the production of cultural products and services through industrialized modes of production, with its essence lying in the production and dissemination of symbols and meanings8. Subsequently, the concept of the “creative industry” emerged, emphasizing the importance of creative elements in cultural production activities9. From the perspective of product attributes, the cultural industry has been defined as an industrial system centered on creativity, characterized by intellectual property and symbolic value10. Furthermore, from the perspective of production and circulation, the cultural industry has been conceptualized as a collection of industries revolving around the creation, reproduction, and dissemination of cultural texts11. This stage of research marks a shift in the concept of the cultural industry from a critical category to an analytical and measurable category within industrial economics.

Building on these perspectives, existing studies generally define the cultural industry as an economic system that transforms cultural resources into value through creative production and industrial operations, encompassing a wide range of sectors such as cultural manufacturing, cultural services, and cultural dissemination12. However, significant heterogeneity exists across different sub-sectors in terms of production modes, value attributes, and consumption patterns13.

Based on the above definitions, the “art and cultural industry” examined in this research is an important sub-sector of the cultural industry, referring to a system of production activities centered on artistic creation and expression. Its core characteristics lie in originality, aesthetic value, and expressive capacity. Specifically, it includes performing arts (e.g., theatre and stage performances), visual arts (e.g., exhibitions), and integrated art forms (e.g., immersive performances and narrative-based experiences). Compared with the broader cultural industry, the art and cultural industry places greater emphasis on the value realization pathway of “creation–expression–experience,” with its products embodying not only cultural attributes but also strong experiential and consumption-oriented characteristics. Therefore, the “art and cultural industry” should not be regarded as a generalized substitute for the cultural industry, but rather as a distinct sub-sector identified by its focus on artistic production.

Art and cultural tourism

From a broader perspective, cultural tourism refers to tourism activities motivated by the desire to learn about, discover, experience, and consume both tangible and intangible cultural elements of a destination. These elements include not only material cultural forms such as historical heritage and architectural art, but also intangible aspects such as literature, music, lifestyles, values, and traditional customs14. In more specific terms, cultural tourism is commonly defined as tourism activities in which cultural resources serve as the primary attractions6,15, with its core function being the transformation of cultural value into tourism value through cultural display and consumption. However, as the concept of cultural tourism continues to evolve, scholarly perspectives have gradually shifted from a resource-oriented view to an experience-oriented view, emphasizing tourists’ participation and perception during the travel process16,17.

In this context, with the deepening integration of culture and tourism, the development model of cultural tourism has gradually transitioned from “culture-display-oriented” to “experience-and immersion-oriented.” Cultural elements are no longer passively exhibited but are transformed into participatory and perceivable experiences through scenario construction, narrative expression, and interactive design. As a result, tourism forms centered on artistic creation and expression have gradually emerged as an important pathway linking cultural content production with tourism experience consumption. Therefore, the art and cultural tourism discussed in this research not only includes traditional cultural sightseeing activities but also emphasizes a comprehensive tourism form that realizes cultural value through artistic expression and immersive experiences within the context of the experience economy.

Relationship between cultural tourism and art and cultural tourism

From a conceptual perspective, cultural tourism and art-based cultural tourism exhibit a “whole–part” relationship. Cultural tourism, as a broader concept, encompasses all forms of tourism activities based on cultural resources, whereas art and cultural tourism represents a subcategory characterized by artistic creation and expression.

It should be emphasized that art and cultural tourism is not an independent concept outside the cultural tourism system, but rather a segmented form that has emerged alongside the development of the experience economy and creative economy. Therefore, the research object of this study falls within the broader category of cultural tourism, with a specific focus on the art and cultural sub-sector characterized by high levels of interactivity and immersive experience.

Furthermore, to ensure consistency between conceptual definitions and empirical analysis, this study applies corresponding constraints in the construction of the indicator system. Specifically, indicators for the art and cultural industry are selected to reflect the development of artistic creation, performance, and related cultural service activities, rather than adopting the entire cultural industry indicator system in a generalized manner. The tourism industry, in contrast, is measured comprehensively in terms of tourism reception capacity, service quality, and tourism economic performance.

Literature review and research gaps

Literature review

To systematically review the literature on the coordinated development of cultural tourism, this study conducts the analysis from two perspectives: macro-level industrial coordination and segmented cultural tourism types. First, the overall research progress on the integration of the cultural and tourism industries is reviewed. Second, studies focusing on the integration of different cultural carriers with tourism formats are examined. Based on this, the main limitations of the existing literature are identified.

At the macro level, early studies primarily adopted the perspective of industrial integration, regarding the cultural and tourism industries as a composite system characterized by mutual promotion. It is generally argued that the integration of these two industries can achieve coordinated development and value creation through resource integration and functional coupling. Building on this perspective, a substantial body of research has examined the measurement of coordination levels, influencing factors, and spatial disparities. Specifically, many studies construct multidimensional indicator systems based on panel data18, and quantitatively evaluate the level of coordinated development from dimensions such as industrial scale, structural characteristics, and development efficiency19,20. Furthermore, the evolutionary stages and regional differences of coordinated development have been explored. In terms of methodology, existing studies have employed regression analysis, ordered Logit models21, path analysis22, multiple linear regression23, grey relational analysis24, and factor analysis25 to identify the driving factors of coordinated development. In addition, some studies have incorporated theoretical frameworks of coordinated development26, using coupling coordination models to characterize the interaction and coordination between the cultural and tourism industries, thereby revealing their co-evolution from a systemic perspective.

On this basis, some studies have further expanded the analytical scope of cultural tourism coordination by situating the cultural tourism system within broader development frameworks. For example, research has explored the integration of cultural tourism with the digital economy27, graphic design28, and the ecological environment29, examining the interactions among different industrial elements and highlighting the multidimensional coupling characteristics of the cultural tourism system. However, such studies remain largely focused on macro-level system relationships, with relatively limited attention to the heterogeneity of specific cultural forms.

With the deepening of cultural and tourism integration, research perspectives have gradually shifted from the macro-industrial level to the combination of specific cultural forms and tourism formats, giving rise to studies on segmented cultural tourism types30–32. Existing research has investigated various forms of cultural tourism, including literary tourism33,34, digital cultural tourism35,36, intangible cultural heritage tourism37,38, wine cultural tourism39,40, yoga cultural tourism41, and nighttime cultural tourism42. These studies explore the pathways and development characteristics of integration between different cultural carriers and the tourism industry, reflecting a broader transition of cultural tourism from a resource-dependent model to a content-driven model. Meanwhile, in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic, some studies have examined the impact mechanisms of public health emergencies on cultural tourism development43,44, and extended the discussion to emerging fields such as wellness tourism45, emphasizing the integration of health demands with cultural experiences. At the regional level, scholars have often relied on field surveys and questionnaire data46–48, combined with methods such as K-means clustering49 and spatial analysis techniques50, to identify regional development patterns and spatial disparities in cultural tourism51, and to propose corresponding policy recommendations52–57.

Overall, existing studies have provided a relatively comprehensive examination of cultural tourism development from both macro-level industrial coordination and segmented cultural types. Nevertheless, several gaps remain. First, in terms of research objects, prior studies have mainly focused on the overall cultural tourism system or single cultural forms, with limited systematic attention to art-based cultural tourism, which emphasizes artistic creation, immersive experience, and narrative scenarios. Second, in terms of analytical perspective, most studies concentrate on measuring the “level” of coordinated development, while paying insufficient attention to the internal factor matching and dynamic adaptation processes within industrial systems. Third, at the methodological level, existing research is largely based on linear analytical frameworks, and the complex nonlinear relationships among multidimensional factors have not been adequately addressed.

Therefore, building on the existing literature, it is necessary to focus on art and cultural tourism as an important segmented field. From the perspective of system adaptability, this research introduces analytical methods capable of capturing complex nonlinear relationships to conduct an in-depth analysis of the coordinated development process between the cultural and tourism industries, thereby addressing the limitations of previous studies in both research focus and methodological approaches.

Research gaps

Compared with the existing literature, this study makes several key contributions:

Extension and refinement of the research object.

Extension and refinement of the research object.

Previous studies have predominantly examined the coordinated development of the cultural and tourism industries at the macro level or have focused on specific single cultural tourism forms, with relatively limited attention to cultural tourism centered on artistic creation and expression. This study further narrows the research focus to the segmented field of the “art and cultural industry–tourism industry” nexus. By identifying an art-production-oriented subsystem within the cultural industry, this research extends the analytical boundary of cultural tourism integration.

(2)Introduction of a system adaptability perspective.

Introduction of a system adaptability perspective.

Unlike existing studies that primarily emphasize the measurement of coordination “levels” or “degrees,” this study adopts a system adaptability perspective, focusing on the matching relationships and structural coordination among internal elements of industrial systems. In doing so, it shifts the analytical focus from “whether coordination exists” to “how elements are matched,” thereby providing a deeper understanding of the underlying operational logic of cultural–tourism industry integration.

(3)Incorporation of nonlinear analytical methods.

Incorporation of nonlinear analytical methods.

To address the limitations of traditional linear models in capturing complex interactions among multidimensional factors, this study introduces the XGBoost model to perform nonlinear modeling of influencing factors based on system adaptability measurement. Furthermore, the SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) method is employed to interpret the marginal contributions and influence pathways of each variable. This approach effectively reveals the nonlinear effects of different factors across varying ranges, thereby enhancing the explanatory power of system adaptability mechanisms.

(4)Construction of an integrated analytical framework.

Construction of an integrated analytical framework.

This study develops an analytical framework of “system adaptability measurement–influencing factor identification–mechanism interpretation,” achieving an organic linkage between measurement analysis and mechanism exploration. The framework provides a systematic analytical pathway for understanding the dynamic process of coordinated development between the art and cultural industry and the tourism industry.

XGBoost-SHAP model

To identify the key driving force of adaptability within China’s art and cultural tourism system and characterize the nonlinear interaction mechanisms of multidimensional influencing factors, this study employs the XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting) model combined with SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) interpretability analysis for empirical research.

XGBoost is an ensemble learning algorithm based on Gradient Boosting Decision Trees (GBDT). It enhances predictive accuracy through iterative fitting of residual functions and incorporates regularization terms in the objective function to prevent overfitting. Compared to traditional linear regression models, XGBoost does not require predefined functional relationships between variables. It effectively captures nonlinear relationships and interaction effects among multiple indicators, making it more suitable for addressing system-level fit problems in arts and cultural industries and tourism sectors characterized by multi-factor coupling, complex structures and significant regional heterogeneity65–69. This study employs the System Fit Index as the dependent variable. Meanwhile, it constructs an XGBoost regression model using indicators such as socioeconomic foundations, transportation and information infrastructure and cultural education and innovation investments as independent variables in present work. Research framework

This research centres on the issue of system adaptability within the synergistic development of the arts and cultural industries and the tourism sector, and constructs a comprehensive analytical framework comprising “theoretical foundations–state measurement–spatiotemporal evolution–mechanism identification” (see Fig. 1). This framework is employed to systematically characterise the evolutionary process of system adaptability in China’s art and cultural tourism system and its underlying driving mechanisms. Specifically, the theoretical foundations provide conceptual definitions and structural support for the analytical framework; state measurement captures the operational status of the system; spatiotemporal analysis reveals its dynamic evolution; and mechanism identification further elucidates the intrinsic driving logic of system adaptability, thereby forming a progressive research pathway from theory to empirical analysis and ultimately to mechanism interpretation.

Research framework.

In the preliminary theoretical analysis stage, the study first identifies key issues in the development of art and cultural tourism in the Introduction and provides a conceptual definition of the field. The literature review then systematically synthesises existing research on the coordinated development of cultural tourism from both a macro-level industrial synergy perspective and a micro-level typological perspective, while identifying current research gaps in the domain of art and cultural tourism. Building on the extant literature, this study introduces the theoretical perspectives of synergistic development and system adaptability, and develops an analytical logic framework based on the dimensions of “element interaction–structural alignment–dynamic adjustment.” It further advances the central research question: within the context of industrial integration, how the arts and cultural industries and the tourism sector achieve system adaptability through multi-dimensional factor interactions, and what underlying mechanisms drive this process.

First, in the stage of state measurement, an indicator system covering industrial scale and service capacity is developed based on the supply–demand structure of the art-based cultural industry and the tourism industry. The development levels of the cultural and tourism subsystems are then evaluated separately. On this basis, the coupling coordination model and the system adaptability evaluation model are introduced to characterize the interaction relationship and system adaptability status of the two industries from the perspectives of “coordination degree” and “structural matching,” thereby providing fundamental variables for subsequent analysis.

Second, through spatiotemporal evolution analysis, the dynamic characteristics of system adaptability in art-based cultural tourism are examined from both temporal and spatial dimensions. In the temporal dimension, evolutionary analysis and nonlinear kernel density estimation are employed to depict the evolution trajectories, distribution patterns, and dynamic differentiation characteristics of system adaptability and coupling coordination degree. In the spatial dimension, spatial distribution maps are used to identify the spatial patterns of system adaptability across regions. In addition, a comparative analysis of coupling coordination degree and system adaptability is conducted to further reveal their differences in terms of evolutionary consistency, sensitivity, and their capacity to reflect structural matching.

Finally, in the stage of driving mechanism identification, the XGBoost–SHAP analytical framework is introduced to model and interpret the nonlinear effects of multidimensional influencing factors on system adaptability. In model construction, driving variables are selected from multiple dimensions, including ecological environment and public services, transportation and information infrastructure, as well as cultural education and innovation investment, in order to identify the strength and direction of their impacts on system adaptability. Furthermore, based on SHAP value decomposition, the effects of multidimensional factors are analyzed from three aspects—individual variable effects, dimensional structural characteristics, and stage-specific variations—thereby revealing the influence pathways and nonlinear features of system adaptability evolution and achieving a transition from “state measurement” to “mechanism interpretation.”

In constructing the indicator system for the art and cultural subsystem, three dimensions are considered: the supply of art and cultural resources, industrial carriers, and market performance. Specifically, the number of art museums and the number of art performance venues are used to represent the level of regional art and cultural infrastructure supply, reflecting the completeness of public cultural spaces and performance platforms70,71. The number of cultural enterprises above a designated size and their operating revenue are employed to capture the scale and marketization level of the art and cultural industry72. In addition, the total audience attendance at art performances and the number of art performance troupes are selected to reflect the activity level and creative capacity of regional art and cultural activities from the perspectives of production entities and cultural consumption scale.

For the tourism subsystem, the indicator system is constructed from three aspects: tourism reception capacity, tourism service supply, and tourism economic performance. Specifically, the number of travel agencies21 and the number of star-rated hotels73 are used to measure regional tourism service and reception capacity. The star-rated hotels are weighted according to their rating levels, with one to five star hotels assigned scores from 1 to 5, respectively, to obtain a composite score. The number of catering enterprises above a designated size74 and the number of tourism performances are selected to reflect tourism supporting service capacity and the supply level of art-based cultural tourism products. Finally, the total number of tourists and the proportion of tourism revenue in GDP are used to measure the scale of the tourism market and its overall contribution to the regional economy75.

Indicator System for Factors Influencing the Suitability of China’s Art and Cultural Tourism System

Based on the analytical framework of “external environment–supporting conditions–core drivers–demand feedback” in system adaptability theory, the process of system adaptability can be understood as the result of the coordinated interaction of multidimensional factors across different levels. Specifically, the external environment provides fundamental constraints for system operation, supporting conditions determine the system’s carrying capacity, core driving factors promote structural optimization and functional improvement, and demand feedback influences the direction of system evolution through consumption behavior and market responses. Taking into account the development characteristics of the art and cultural industry and the tourism industry in mainland China, as well as relevant researches29,76,77, this study operationalizes the above theoretical framework by mapping each dimension into measurable variables. Specifically, macroeconomic and fiscal conditions are regarded as the external environmental foundation of system operation; ecological environment, transportation infrastructure, and information infrastructure are incorporated as supporting conditions; cultural education and innovation investment are identified as core driving factors; and demand feedback mechanisms are reflected through indicators related to cultural consumption. Indicator System for Influencing Factors of System Adaptability of China’s Art and Cultural Tourism.

(1) Macroeconomic and Fiscal Environment Adaptation Variables.

The level of macroeconomic development and fiscal capacity form the foundational external environment for the coordinated operation of the arts and cultural industry and the tourism industry, serving as crucial prerequisites that influence the overall system’s state of alignment. Regional economic strength not only determines the scale and tier of cultural and tourism consumption, but also indirectly impacts system alignment through fiscal investment, which affects cultural facility construction, public service provision, and industrial guidance capabilities. This study selects per capita GDP (X1), the proportion of tertiary industry value-added in GDP (X2) and per capita local general public budget expenditure (X3) to characterize the environmental support role of regional economic development levels and fiscal resource allocation capacity in the adaptive state of the arts, culture and tourism system.

(2) Ecological Environment and Public Service Capacity-Adaptive Variables.

Ecological and environmental quality and public service capacity serve as critical constraints for the sustainable operation of arts, culture and tourism activities. Meanwhile, it also provides essential safeguards for achieving high-quality alignment within the system. A fine ecological environment enhances cultural tourism experiences and strengthens the stability of system operations. Meanwhile, a comprehensive public service system provides necessary support for scaling up the system. Accordingly, this paper selects four indicators of domestic waste harmless treatment rate (X4), daily urban sewage treatment capacity (X5), per capita park green space area (X6) and proportion of days meeting air quality standards (X7) to characterize the carrying capacity and adaptability conditions for the operation of the arts and cultural tourism system, being from two dimensions of environmental governance level and public service supply capacity.

(3) Transportation Infrastructure Accessibility Adaptation Variable.

Transportation infrastructure plays a crucial connecting and coupling role between the arts and cultural industries and the tourism industry. The level of development directly impacts the spatial matching efficiency between cultural resources and the tourism market. The higher transportation accessibility enhances the conversion efficiency of arts and cultural resources and the flow efficiency of tourism, which increases the spatial adaptability of the system’s operation. This study employs railway operating mileage per unit area (X8) and highway mileage per unit area (X9) to measure regional transportation infrastructure density and the supporting role in the spatial adaptation of the arts, culture, and tourism system.

(4) Information Infrastructure and Digital Communication Adaptation Variables.

Against the backdrop of rapid digital technology advancement, information infrastructure has become a crucial enabler for deep integration between the arts and cultural industries and the tourism sector. The evolution of information and communication technologies has not only transformed the production and dissemination of cultural content but also reshaped tourism consumption scenarios and industrial organization models, which exerts profound influence on systemic adaptation mechanisms. This study employs mobile phone penetration rate (X10) and the proportion of internet broadband users relative to the population (X11) to characterize regional information infrastructure levels and the role in driving the digital adaptation of arts, culture and tourism systems.

(5) Cultural Education and Innovation-Driven Adaptive Variables.

Cultural and educational resources along with innovation investments serve as the endogenous driving force behind the synergistic evolution of the arts and cultural industries alongside the tourism sector. It also constitutes crucial catalysts for achieving deep-level alignment within the system. On one hand, the cultural and educational system provides talent and intellectual support for artistic creation and the supply of cultural products. On the other hand, the rise in residents’ cultural consumption levels reciprocally propels industrial innovation and systemic functional upgrades. Based on this, this paper selects three indicators of the proportion of R&D expenditure in GDP allocated to education, arts and crafts, sports and entertainment (X12), the number of students enrolled in regular higher education institutions (X13), and per capita expenditure on education, culture and entertainment among provincial residents (X14) to characterize the endogenous adaptation mechanism of the arts, culture, and tourism system from three dimensions of innovation investment, talent reserves and cultural consumption demand.

Data sources

The panel data for China’s Art, Culture, and Tourism System Indicator System and the System Suitability Factor Indicator System primarily are gained from official sources such as the China Tourism Statistical Yearbook, China Cultural Heritage Statistical Yearbook, China Cultural Heritage and Tourism Statistical Yearbook, provincial statistical yearbooks and statistical bulletins. Minor data gaps are filled using linear interpolation to ensure data completeness and accuracy. Administrative boundary data and other spatial data are sourced from the China National Geographic Information Public Service Platform (www.tianditu.gov.cn).

Data preprocessing

In the data preprocessing stage, the original indicator data are first systematically organized and subjected to consistency checks. Considering the significant differences in measurement units and value ranges across indicators, the min–max normalization method is employed to eliminate dimensional effects and enhance comparability among indicators. Subsequently, to objectively determine the relative importance of each indicator in the evaluation of system adaptability, the entropy weight method is adopted to calculate indicator weights. This method measures the contribution of each indicator to the overall system variability by computing its information entropy and degree of dispersion, thereby avoiding biases introduced by subjective weighting. Specifically, the proportion of each normalized indicator is first calculated, followed by the computation of its information entropy. Based on this, the coefficient of variation (or divergence) for each indicator is obtained, which is then used to determine the final weights. A higher weight indicates greater variability of the indicator across samples and stronger explanatory power for system adaptability. The calculated weights of all indicators are presented in Fig. 2, where Fig. 2(a) shows the weights of the art and cultural industry indicators and Fig. 2(b) presents the weights of the tourism industry indicators.

Evolution of Spatial Distribution of System Fit and Coupling Coordination.

From the perspective of system adaptability and coupling coordination, China’s arts and cultural tourism system exhibits a spatial distribution pattern characterized by “strong centers and weak peripheries” for adaptability and a spatial structure of “strong east and weak west” for coordination. This stems from the long-standing advantages of central and eastern provinces in converting artistic and cultural resources, the scale of cultural industry entities, the maturity of the arts and culture market and the size of the tourism market. These factors have fostered a relatively stable allocation of resources between the cultural and tourism systems, sustaining high levels of adaptability and coordination. Coastal eastern provinces exhibit significantly higher tourism market value and cultural tourism industry benefit compared to central regions, supporting high-level coupled and coordinated development. In contrast, western provinces face challenges due to weak economic foundations, limited cultural and artistic entities, insufficient market demand and relatively poor transportation and geographical conditions. These factors hinder the conversion of cultural resources into compelling tourism products, leading to uneven development speeds between cultural systems and tourism systems. Consequently, system compatibility and coordination levels remain persistently low. Within the eastern region, Zhejiang Province exhibits a misalignment characterized by relatively low system compatibility but high coordination levels. This discrepancy stems from the tourism system’s significant first-mover advantages in marketization, industrial scale and service capacity. While, the arts and culture system lags in industrialization and resource conversion. The tourism system has spurred the coordinated development of the arts and cultural industries through the robust market absorption capacity and industrial driving effect. However, other types of cultural tourism have simultaneously impacted the arts and cultural industries, resulting in Zhejiang maintaining a high overall level of coupling coordination. While, the system adaptability remains low. In the central region, the arts and cultural tourism industries exhibit coordinated development, balanced factor allocation and outstanding system adaptability levels.

From the perspective of system adaptation and coupling coordination allocation, the national landscape exhibits a spatial evolution trend of “high system adaptation—high coupling coordination expansion, low system adaptation—low coupling coordination convergence”. The number of “double-high” provinces increases from 20 in the year 2014 to 22 in the year 2023, indicating that cultural supply and tourism reception capacity have improved simultaneously in most regions. The number of “double-low” provinces decreases from 10 to 7, narrowing the low-level segment but still revealing stable low-value areas. Regionally, eastern China maintains a stable advantage. Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shandong, Guangdong, Fujian, Hebei, and Liaoning have continuously sustained “double-high” levels, with coordinated system adaptability and coupling, exhibiting relatively stable, synchronous improvement trends. In contrast, Heilongjiang and Hainan remain persistently “double-low”. In the year 2023, Jilin Province and Tianjin Municipality upgrads from “dual-low” to “high coupling coordination—low system compatibility”. The central region shows steady upward development. Central provinces not only exhibit high matching between artistic and cultural supply and tourism reception but also foster more readily positive cycles in system interactions. Compared to the eastern and western regions, their spatial structure demonstrates a more balanced evolutionary trend. The western region exhibits more pronounced internal disparities. Qinghai Province, the Tibet Autonomous Region, and the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region have consistently maintained “low-high” status, forming a core low-value area nationally. In the year 2023, Yunnan Province and Guizhou Province increase from primary alignment in the year 2018 to moderate alignment with corresponding increase in coupling coordination level. Conversely, the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region shows a decline in coupling coordination, indicating a relative lagging development in the arts and culture system.

Analysis of Factors driving the suitability of China’s art and cultural tourism system

Based on panel data from 31 provinces (autonomous regions and municipalities) in mainland China from the year 2014 to 2023, this study employs an XGBoost nonlinear regression model to identify the driving factors of system fit between the arts and cultural industry and the tourism industry. The results indicate that the regression model achieved an R² value of 0.7937 and an RMSE of 0.1258, demonstrating strong explanatory power for system fit. This model accurately captures the comprehensive driving effects of multidimensional factors on the system fit status of China’s arts, culture, and tourism sector.

Further analysis using the SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) method decomposes the marginal contributions of each driving factor to the formation of system fit. Results reveals that the impact of different types of driving factors on the system fit of the arts and cultural tourism industry exhibits significant nonlinear characteristics with marked differences in driving intensity over the 10-year evolution, as shown in Figs. 7 and 8.

Distribution of Explanatory Power for System Fit Drivers.

Distribution of SHAP Effect Strength for System Fit.

Based on the SHAP average absolute value ranking results in Fig. 5, the impact of different driving factors on the system fit of China’s arts and cultural tourism exhibits significant variation. Among these, daily urban sewage treatment capacity (X5), enrollment in regular higher education institutions (X13), R&D expenditure as a percentage of GDP for education, arts, crafts, sports, and entertainment (X12), and railway operational mileage per unit area (X8) constitute the top four core drivers of system adaptability.

The SHAP importance ranking indicates that X5 exhibits a significantly higher average SHAP value than other variables, demonstrating the strongest explanatory power in model predictions of Fig. 5. Combined with SHAP in Fig. 6, it is further revealed that as a city’s daily sewage treatment capacity is at a higher level, the corresponding SHAP values are generally distributed in the positive range. This indicates that well-developed urban environmental infrastructure can significantly enhance the operational adaptability of the arts, culture, and tourism system. This result suggests that ecological and environmental infrastructure has become a crucial “foundational condition” for system adaptability as given the high dependence of arts, culture, and tourism activities on urban public spaces and environmental carrying capacity.

Figure 8 shows that the number of students enrolled in regular higher education institutions (X13) ranks second in SHAP ordering, exhibiting a distinct “high value-positive” distribution pattern. Expanded university scale not only signifies enhanced regional reserves of cultural creativity and specialized talent but also strengthens artistic and cultural content production alongside tourism product innovation through knowledge spillover and innovation diffusion mechanisms. Finally, it promotes functional alignment between the arts and cultural industries and the tourism sector.

Similarly, the proportion of R&D expenditure in education, arts and crafts, sports, and entertainment relative to GDP (X12) also possesses high importance. The SHAP value maintains a significant positive contribution even in high-investment ranges, indicating that cultural innovation investment continuously drives system compatibility. This result reflects that cultural R&D investment has become a key endogenous driver for enhancing the system fit level of the arts, culture and tourism system during the transition of system development from “factor-driven” to “innovation-driven”.

In contrast, the importance of railway operating mileage per unit area (X8) is slightly lower than the top three indicators. The SHAP distribution reveals distinct phased characteristics. It exerts a significant positive impact on system fitness within the low-to-medium railway density range, while the marginal effect diminishes in the high-density range. This indicates that transportation infrastructure primarily provides spatial support during the initial and intermediate stages of system fitness formation.

Building upon univariate analysis, the driving mechanisms of system adaptation can be further summarized at the dimensional level. Firstly, the ecological environment and public service capacity dimension (X4–X7) consistently exhibits stable positive contributions, among which X5 shows the most prominent role. This indicates that this dimension provides essential foundational constraints for the adaptation of the arts, culture, and tourism system. Such factors primarily manifest as “threshold-type” drivers, meaning that it is particularly critical for enhancing system adaptation during the low-to-medium level stages.

Secondly, the cultural education and innovation investment dimension (X12–X14) ranks high in SHAP order and maintains strong marginal contributions even at high levels, indicating that it serves as the core driving force for sustained system adaptation improvement. This result further confirms the structural shift in the evolution of arts and cultural tourism system adaptation, which is from external condition-driven to endogenous innovation-driven.

Thirdly, the transportation and information infrastructure dimension (X8–X11) possesses a vital supporting and enabling role in shaping system fit, though the impact intensity exhibits distinct phase-specific variations. Transportation infrastructure primarily enhances system matching by reducing spatial friction, while information infrastructure strengthens functional-level fit by expanding cultural dissemination and tourism consumption scenarios.

By comparison, the macroeconomic and fiscal environment dimension (X1–X3) does not rank among the dominant driving forces, although its overall contribution remains positive. This suggests that economic development and fiscal support mainly operate as enabling conditions rather than direct drivers of system adaptability, providing the institutional and resource foundations through which other key factors can exert their influence.

Analysis of the compatibility-driven mechanism in China’s art and cultural tourism system

Based on the temporal evolution, spatial distribution, and dynamic kernel density characteristics of system adaptability and coupling coordination degree, together with the XGBoost–SHAP analysis of driving factors, this study provides an integrated explanation of the underlying mechanisms governing the coordinated development of the art and cultural and tourism industries in China from a system evolution perspective.

The results indicate that the evolution of the art-based cultural tourism system is not a linear process driven by a single factor. Rather, it represents a dynamic adaptation process shaped by the interaction of multidimensional external constraints and internal elements, achieved through continuous structural adjustment and functional reconfiguration. Overall, the system exhibits pronounced nonlinear and stage-specific characteristics.

First, from the perspective of temporal evolution and kernel density distribution, system adaptability follows a stage-wise trajectory characterized by an initial decline followed by sustained recovery. Meanwhile, the distribution pattern evolves from a unimodal to a multimodal form, reflecting a dynamic process of regional differentiation followed by rebalancing. In spatial terms, a persistent “east–west gradient” is observed, with high-level regions continuously concentrated in eastern and central China. This pattern suggests that system adaptability is significantly constrained by regional development foundations and the capacity for factor allocation. Collectively, these findings indicate that the system evolves under non-equilibrium conditions and achieves structural optimization through continuous adjustment rather than balanced progression.

Building on this, the XGBoost–SHAP analysis reveals the internal logic underlying the observed spatiotemporal patterns. At the level of individual driving factors, urban wastewater treatment capacity (X5), higher education scale (X13), cultural R&D investment (X12), and railway infrastructure density (X8) constitute the core driver combination of system adaptability in China. Among these, urban wastewater treatment capacity (X5) exhibits particularly strong influence, highlighting the role of ecological environment and public services as fundamental constraints on system operation. From the perspective of public goods provision and urban carrying capacity theory, when environmental conditions and public services do not reach a certain threshold, effective coupling between cultural activities and tourism consumption cannot be achieved, thereby constraining system adaptability. These factors do not directly generate integration value but instead enhance system capacity and operational stability, reducing friction between cultural production and tourism consumption and providing necessary preconditions for system adaptation.

Second, factors related to cultural education and innovation investment (X12 and X13) exhibit consistently positive and high marginal contributions in the SHAP distribution. This suggests that, as the system enters a medium-to-high development stage, the driving mechanism gradually shifts from dependence on external conditions to endogenous innovation. From the perspective of human capital and innovation-driven development theory, higher education expansion and cultural R&D investment not only strengthen cultural content production capacity but also promote deeper alignment between the cultural and tourism industries in terms of value chains and functional structures through talent agglomeration and knowledge spillovers. This facilitates a transition from “factor accumulation” to “structural coordination.”

Third, from a structural perspective, different types of driving factors exhibit clear hierarchical roles in the system adaptation process. Ecological environment and public services function as “threshold-based supports,” forming the foundational conditions for system operation. Transportation and information infrastructure serve as “connective and enabling factors” by enhancing spatial accessibility and information flow efficiency. In contrast, cultural education and innovation investment act as the “core driving force” for sustained system upgrading. This structural differentiation is consistent with the regional divergence and convergence patterns observed in the kernel density analysis, indicating that improvements in system adaptability fundamentally rely on structural optimization driven by the coordinated interaction of multidimensional factors.

Further integrating SHAP value distributions and marginal effect variations, the evolution of system adaptability in China can be characterized as a progressive mechanism of “external support–factor response–structural adaptation–functional upgrading.” In the initial stage, the system primarily depends on external conditions such as ecological environment and infrastructure to ensure basic operation. During the development stage, transportation and information factors facilitate resource mobility and factor integration. In the advanced stage, cultural education and innovation investment drive structural optimization, ultimately enabling a transition from low-level coupling to high-level coordination.

Overall, the evolution of system adaptability in China’s art and cultural tourism system demonstrates a transformation from “constraint by foundational conditions” to “endogenous innovation-driven development.” This process exhibits stage-wise dynamics over time, spatial gradients across regions, and hierarchical dominance and dynamic transitions among multidimensional factors at the mechanism level. In essence, improvements in system adaptability are not the result of simple factor accumulation, but rather a nonlinear evolutionary process achieved through structural reconfiguration and optimization of functional relationships under the joint influence of multiple conditions.

Contributions

The findings of this study are broadly consistent with existing research on the coordinated development of the cultural and tourism industries. Previous studies have demonstrated that these two industries can achieve coordinated development and value enhancement through resource integration and functional coupling7–9. Similarly, this study confirms the existence of significant interactions and a trend of coordinated evolution between the art and cultural industry and the tourism industry.

Building on this foundation, this study makes several further contributions. First, while prior research has mainly focused on measuring the “level” or “degree” of coordination, this research introduces a system adaptability perspective, emphasizing the structural matching among internal elements of the industrial system. This shifts the analytical focus from “whether coordination exists” to “how elements are matched,” thereby enriching the analytical dimensions of cultural tourism integration.

Second, at the methodological level, existing studies are largely based on linear analytical frameworks10–14, which are insufficient to capture complex interactions among multidimensional factors. By incorporating the XGBoost-SHAP approach, this study identifies nonlinear and stage-specific effects of different driving factors. The results reveal that infrastructure factors play a significant role in the early stages, while innovation investment becomes dominant at higher levels, thereby addressing the limitations of traditional methods in capturing dynamic adaptation mechanisms.

Finally, compared with previous studies that focus on the overall cultural tourism system or specific cultural types19–28, this study refines the research scope to art and cultural tourism centered on artistic creation and expression. This segmented perspective provides new empirical evidence for understanding diverse integration pathways within cultural tourism.

Limitations

This study has several limitations: (1) Due to data availability constraints, the analysis is conducted at the provincial level, which may not fully capture micro-level variations at the city or project level. (2) Although the indicator system is designed to be comprehensive, it cannot fully capture qualitative dimensions such as artistic quality and depth of cultural expression, for which official statistical data are currently unavailable. (3) In terms of driving factor analysis, although the XGBoost-SHAP model effectively captures nonlinear relationships, it does not explicitly incorporate spatial heterogeneity. Given that system adaptability exhibits strong spatial dependence, future research should further integrate geographical perspectives.

Future research directions may include: (1) extending the analysis to finer spatial scales (e.g., city level); (2) developing indicators that better capture qualitative cultural attributes; and (3) adopting models that explicitly account for spatial effects to further refine the analysis of driving mechanisms.

Conclusions and policy implications

Conclusions

This study investigates art-based cultural tourism in China using panel data from 31 provincial-level regions over the period 2014–2023. A comprehensive evaluation framework is constructed to assess coordinated development and system adaptability, and the XGBoost-SHAP method is employed to identify key driving factors.

The main conclusions are as follows: (1) China’s art and cultural tourism system remains at the primary to intermediate stages of adaptability. Although both system adaptability and coupling coordination have shown steady improvement, deeper structural alignment between the two industries remains limited. (2) System adaptability and coupling coordination exhibit both consistency and regional heterogeneity in temporal and spatial dimensions. Eastern and central regions demonstrate higher levels of adaptability and coordination, while western regions remain at lower levels, reflecting the significant influence of regional development conditions. (3) Ecological environment and public service capacity constitute the primary foundational drivers of system adaptability. Among all variables, urban wastewater treatment capacity shows the highest contribution, highlighting the dependence of cultural tourism on urban environmental quality and public service capacity. (4) Cultural education and innovation investment serve as the core endogenous drivers of system upgrading. Higher education and cultural R&D investment maintain strong marginal effects at higher levels, facilitating the transition from passive adaptation to active coordination. (5) Transportation and information infrastructure exhibit clear stage-specific effects. While they play a significant role in early and intermediate stages, their marginal effects diminish at higher levels.

Overall, the evolution of system adaptability reflects a transition from externally driven development to endogenous innovation-driven growth. This process represents a dynamic restructuring of resource allocation, functional organization, and value creation between the cultural and tourism industries. This research contributes theoretically by extending the system adaptability perspective in cultural tourism research, methodologically by integrating machine learning and explainable analysis, and practically by providing targeted policy insights for high-quality development.

Policy implications

Based on the evaluation results of system adaptability and coupling coordination, as well as the XGBoost-SHAP analysis, this study suggests that policy priorities for promoting high-quality development of art-based cultural tourism in China should shift from a “scale expansion” orientation to a “system adaptability and structural matching” orientation.

First, policies should strengthen ecological environment and public service capacity to provide foundational support for system operation. Key regions for cultural tourism development should be prioritized in urban renewal and infrastructure improvement programs to reduce friction between cultural production and tourism consumption.

Second, greater emphasis should be placed on cultural education and innovation investment to enhance endogenous development capacity. Policies should support talent cultivation, promote collaboration between universities and cultural tourism enterprises, and encourage the development of original cultural content and innovative tourism products.

Third, transportation and digital infrastructure development should shift from extensive expansion to precision-oriented adaptation. Rather than focusing solely on scale, efforts should be directed toward improving the integration between infrastructure systems and cultural tourism demand.

Finally, differentiated regional policy strategies should be implemented. Regions with low adaptability should prioritize improvements in environmental quality, public services, and infrastructure, while regions with higher adaptability should focus on innovation, artistic expression, and high-end product development to avoid diminishing returns.
