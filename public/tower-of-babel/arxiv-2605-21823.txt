Maximal extension of Schwarzschild-like spacetimes in Lorentz gauge theory
Mohsen Fathi

Abstract
We study the maximal analytic extension of the Schwarzschild-like black hole solution in Lorentz gauge theory. The lapse function is f ⁡ ( r ) = A 0 − 2 − 2 ​ m ¯ / r f(r)=A_{0}^{-2}-2\bar{m}/r , so the horizon is located at r + = 2 ​ m ¯ ​ A 0 2 r_{+}=2\bar{m}A_{0}^{2} and the non-affinity coefficient of the horizon generator is κ = 1 / ( 4 ​ m ¯ ​ A 0 4 ) \kappa=1/(4\bar{m}A_{0}^{4}) . We first analyze the radial null curves in the Schwarzschild-Droste (SD) and ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein (IEF) charts, and then construct the Kruskal-Szekeres (KS) chart adapted to the LGT geometry. The KS extension contains two exterior regions, a black-hole region and a white-hole region. We also present the standard and regular Carter-Penrose (CP) compactifications. The conformal skeleton is Schwarzschild-like, but the physical scale of the horizon, the surface gravity and the constant-radius curves remain controlled by A 0 A_{0} . Hence the solution has the same causal topology as Schwarzschild, while it is geometrically inequivalent to it when A 0 ≠ 1 A_{0}\neq 1 .

Introduction

Black holes are useful laboratories for understanding the real geometrical content of a gravity theory. In general relativity, the Schwarzschild solution is not only the exterior field of a static compact source. Its maximal extension also shows that the surface
r=2M
is only a coordinate singularity, while the physical singularity is located at
r=0
. This became clear from the KS construction and from CP compactification, where the complete spacetime contains two exterior regions, a black-hole region and a white-hole region
[
17
,
24
,
21
,
9
,
14
,
27
,
10
,
19
,
22
,
13
]
. For this reason, when a Schwarzschild-like solution appears in a theory beyond the standard metric formulation, it is natural to ask whether the same global extension still exists.

The question is also relevant in the broader program of testing gravity with compact objects. Modified and gauge formulations of gravity can change the local geometry near the horizon, the behavior of null geodesics and the interpretation of observables
[
12
,
5
,
3
,
8
]
. In such cases, studying shadows, lensing or particle motion is not fully complete unless the causal structure of the spacetime is also under control. Recent examples in other non-standard geometries, such as Lorentzian-Euclidean black holes, also show how null geodesics and CP diagrams can be central to understand the physical meaning of a proposed black-hole metric
[
7
]
.

Here we study this issue in Lorentz gauge theory (LGT). In this framework, the basic variables are a Lorentz connection and a scalar field in the fundamental representation of the Lorentz group. The metric is obtained as an emergent geometrical object after symmetry breaking
[
28
,
16
,
15
]
. Static black-hole solutions in LGT were obtained in Ref.
[
15
]
. They are close to the Schwarzschild form, but they contain an extra constant from the connection sector.

The solution considered in this letter is described by

f(r)=\frac{1}{A_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2\bar{m}}{r}.

(1)

For
A_{0}=1
, the Schwarzschild lapse is recovered. For
A_{0}\neq 1
, however, the metric is not just Schwarzschild written with another radial coordinate. The horizon is shifted to

r_{+}=2\bar{m}A_{0}^{2},

(2)

and curvature scalars keep an explicit dependence on
A_{0}
. Hence
A_{0}
changes the physical scale of the horizon and the geometrical interpretation of the areal radius. Some local and observational aspects of this spacetime have already been studied, including shadows and lensing signatures
[
18
]
, and spinning-particle dynamics
[
26
]
. The missing part, which we address here, is the maximal extension and its compact causal representation.

The plan is simple. We first recall the LGT black hole and the horizon properties. We then discuss radial null curves in the SD and IEF charts. After that we introduce the KS coordinates and draw the full extension. Finally, we give the standard and regular CP diagrams. Our result is that the LGT black hole has a Schwarzschild-like causal skeleton, but the geometrical scale attached to that skeleton is controlled by
A_{0}
. We use units
G=c=1
and the signature
(-,+,+,+)
.

2
The LGT black hole and the horizon

We briefly recall the LGT ingredients which are needed here. The connection 1-form
\bm{\omega}^{a}{}_{b}
defines the curvature 2-form

\bm{R}^{a}{}_{b}=\bm{D}\bm{\omega}^{a}{}_{b}=\mathbf{d}\bm{\omega}^{a}{}_{b}+\bm{\omega}^{a}{}_{c}\wedge\bm{\omega}^{c}{}_{b}.

(3)

The purely connection term is topological. The scalar field
\phi^{a}
and the covariant derivative
\bm{D}\phi^{a}
introduce the dynamical sector. In the notation of Ref.
[
15
]
, the main invariant pieces can be written schematically as

I_{(0)}=\int_{{M}}\left(g_{1}\eta_{ab}\eta^{cd}+g_{2}\epsilon_{ab}{}^{cd}\right)\bm{R}^{a}{}_{c}\wedge\bm{R}^{b}{}_{d},

(4)

I_{(2)}=g_{3}\int_{{M}}\bm{D}\phi^{a}\wedge\bm{D}\phi^{b}\wedge\bm{R}_{ab}\\
+g_{4}\int_{{M}}\epsilon_{abcd}\bm{D}\phi^{a}\wedge\bm{D}\phi^{b}\wedge\bm{R}^{cd},

(5)

and

I_{(4)}=\lambda\int_{{M}}\epsilon_{abcd}\bm{D}\phi^{a}\wedge\bm{D}\phi^{b}\wedge\bm{D}\phi^{c}\wedge\bm{D}\phi^{d}.

(6)

This short review only fixes the origin of the parameter
A_{0}
. The full field-equation analysis is not repeated, since our aim is the global extension of the resulting exact metric.

The static spherically symmetric line element is

\bm{g}=-f(r)\mathbf{d}t^{2}+f(r)^{-1}\mathbf{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\mathbf{d}\Omega^{2},

(7)

where

\mathbf{d}\Omega^{2}=\mathbf{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\mathbf{d}\phi^{2},\qquad f(r)=\frac{1}{A_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2\bar{m}}{r}.

(8)

The SD chart is
(t,r,\theta,\phi)
, and
\bar{m}=m_{S}/(8\pi m_{P}^{2})
. The event horizon is the zero of
f(r)
,

r_{+}=2\bar{m}A_{0}^{2}.

(9)

Thus
A_{0}>1
enlarges the horizon, while
A_{0}<1
contracts it.

The solution is not equivalent to Schwarzschild by a harmless rescaling. For example, the Ricci scalar is

R=\frac{2(A_{0}^{2}-1)}{A_{0}^{2}r^{2}},

(10)

which vanishes only for
|A_{0}|=1
. Also the Kretschmann scalar reads

\mathcal{K}=R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\frac{32\bar{m}^{2}}{r^{6}}+\frac{4}{r^{4}}\left[1-f(r)\right]^{2}.

(11)

For
|A_{0}|=1
, this gives
\mathcal{K}_{\rm S}=48\bar{m}^{2}/r^{6}
. The horizon can also be detected by invariant methods based on scalar polynomial invariants and gradients of invariants
[
1
,
20
,
25
]
. These facts show that
A_{0}
is not a pure coordinate artifact.

2.1
IEF chart and the scalar
\mathcal{U}

The tortoise coordinate of Eq. (
7
) is

r_{*}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{f(r)}=A_{0}^{2}\left[r+r_{+}\ln\left|\frac{r}{r_{+}}-1\right|\right].

(12)

The outgoing and ingoing radial null curves in the SD chart are

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm out}_{(u,\theta,\phi)}:

\displaystyle\qquad t=r_{*}+u,

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm in}_{(v,\theta,\phi)}:

\displaystyle\qquad t=-r_{*}+v,

(14)

where
u
and
v
are the retarded and advanced null labels.

By promoting
v=t+r_{*}
to a coordinate, one obtains

\bm{g}=-f(r)\mathbf{d}v^{2}+2\mathbf{d}v\mathbf{d}r+r^{2}\mathbf{d}\Omega^{2}.

(15)

For later use we introduce

\tilde{t}=v-r=t+(A_{0}^{2}-1)r+2\bar{m}A_{0}^{4}\ln\left|\frac{r}{r_{+}}-1\right|.

(16)

Then

\begin{split}\bm{g}=&-f(r)\mathbf{d}\tilde{t}^{2}+2\left[1-f(r)\right]\mathbf{d}\tilde{t}\mathbf{d}r\\
&+\left[2-f(r)\right]\mathbf{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\mathbf{d}\Omega^{2}.\end{split}

(17)

All metric components are regular at
r=r_{+}
. Hence the IEF chart extends the SD chart through the future horizon, but it does not yet contain the past horizon.

It is useful to define

\mathcal{U}(\tilde{t},r)=\left(1-\frac{r}{r_{+}}\right)\exp\left[\kappa(r-\tilde{t})\right],

(18)

where

\kappa=\frac{1}{4\bar{m}A_{0}^{4}}=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(r_{+}).

(19)

The surface
\mathcal{U}=0
is exactly
{H}:r=r_{+}
. The norm of the metric dual of
\mathbf{d}\mathcal{U}
is

\nabla^{\mu}\mathcal{U}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{U}=\frac{(A_{0}^{2}-1)(r-r_{+})^{2}\left[r+(A_{0}^{2}-1)r_{+}\right]}{A_{0}^{6}r_{+}^{4}}.

(20)

Thus the normal becomes null on
{H}
. In the Schwarzschild limit
|A_{0}|=1
, the same quantity vanishes everywhere in the IEF domain, but for general
A_{0}
it vanishes only on
{H}
. This is why the
\mathcal{U}
-constant curves are useful to visualize the way the horizon is approached.

The Killing vector
\bm{\xi}=\partial_{t}=\partial_{\tilde{t}}
satisfies
\bm{g}(\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi})=-f(r)
, so it becomes null on
{H}
. On this surface one has

\nabla_{\bm{\xi}}\bm{\xi}\big|_{{H}}=\kappa\,\bm{\xi}\big|_{{H}}.

(21)

Since
\kappa\neq 0
, the horizon is non-degenerate. This is the usual surface-gravity normalization for a Killing horizon
[
4
,
22
,
2
]
. Standard results on non-degenerate Killing horizons and bifurcate extensions then apply
[
6
,
23
,
27
,
11
]
. In the present case the KS construction below gives the extension explicitly.

2.2
Radial null curves in the SD chart

The SD chart is useful outside the horizon, but the behavior of Eqs. (
13
) and (
14
) already shows its limitation. Near
r=r_{+}
the logarithm in
r_{*}
diverges, and the null curves pile up at the horizon in the coordinate picture. In these coordinates the radial null slopes are

\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}t}=\pm f(r).

(22)

Therefore the coordinate speed goes to zero at
r=r_{+}
, although this is only a coordinate effect.

Figure

displays the two null families. The solid blue curves are the outgoing family, and the dashed green curves are the ingoing family. For
A_{0}<1
, the horizon is closer to the origin and the asymptotic value of
f(r)
is larger. For
A_{0}>1
, the horizon moves outward and the asymptotic value of
f(r)
is smaller. This changes the opening of the curves in the coordinate diagram. However, this asymptotic opening is not an invariant statement by itself; it also depends on the normalization of
t
. The invariant information is instead carried by
r_{+}
,
\kappa
and the curvature scalars.

2.3
Radial null curves in the IEF chart

For a radial displacement in Eq. (
17
), the null condition gives

\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}}\right)^{2}+\frac{2[1-f(r)]}{2-f(r)}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}}-\frac{f(r)}{2-f(r)}=0.

(23)

The two branches are

\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}}=-1,\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}}=\frac{r-r_{+}}{(2A_{0}^{2}-1)r+r_{+}}.

(24)

The first family is simply

\mathcal{L}^{\rm in}_{(v,\theta,\phi)}:\qquad\tilde{t}=-r+v,

(25)

and crosses
r=r_{+}
smoothly. The second family can be written as

\mathcal{L}^{\rm out}_{(u,\theta,\phi)}:\qquad\tilde{t}=2r_{*}-r+u.

(26)

It is still singular at the future horizon in the same way expected for outgoing rays. This is exactly what one wants from the IEF chart: it is regular for the future-horizon crossing of ingoing rays.

This behavior is shown in Fig.
3
. The dashed green curves pass through
r=r_{+}
without obstruction, while the solid blue curves still feel the horizon as a limiting surface in this coordinate patch.

, or
\tilde{t}+r=\mathrm{constant}

\tilde{t}=2r_{*}-r+u
. The red dashed vertical line marks
r=r_{+}
. Compared with the SD chart, the future-horizon crossing of the ingoing null rays is regular.

3
KS extension

The IEF chart is still not the maximal extension. To see the full geometry, we use the null labels

u=t-r_{*},\qquad v=t+r_{*}.

(27)

The metric becomes

\bm{g}=-f(r)\mathbf{d}u\mathbf{d}v+r^{2}\mathbf{d}\Omega^{2}.

(28)

The remaining coordinate singularity at
r=r_{+}
is removed by defining

U=-\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa u},\qquad V=\mathrm{e}^{\kappa v}.

(29)

These coordinates satisfy

\left(\frac{r}{r_{+}}-1\right)\mathrm{e}^{r/r_{+}}=-UV,

(30)

and the metric takes the regular form

\bm{g}=-\frac{r_{+}}{\kappa^{2}A_{0}^{2}r}\mathrm{e}^{-r/r_{+}}\mathbf{d}U\mathbf{d}V+r^{2}\mathbf{d}\Omega^{2}.

(31)

The coefficient is finite and non-zero at
r=r_{+}
.

We next introduce

T=\frac{V+U}{2},\qquad X=\frac{V-U}{2}.

(32)

Then

X^{2}-T^{2}=\left(\frac{r}{r_{+}}-1\right)\exp\left(\frac{r}{r_{+}}\right),

(33)

and

\bm{g}=\frac{r_{+}}{\kappa^{2}A_{0}^{2}r}\mathrm{e}^{-r/r_{+}}\left(-\mathbf{d}T^{2}+\mathbf{d}X^{2}\right)+r^{2}\mathbf{d}\Omega^{2}.

(34)

In
{M}_{\mathrm{I}}
, the transformation reduces to

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{r/(2r_{+})}\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{+}}-1}\sinh(\kappa t),

(35)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{r/(2r_{+})}\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{+}}-1}\cosh(\kappa t),

(36)

so
T/X=\tanh(\kappa t)
.

The IEF domain inside the KS plane follows from
V=T+X=\mathrm{e}^{\kappa(\tilde{t}+r)}
. Using Eq. (
30
), one obtains

U=T-X=-\left(\frac{r}{r_{+}}-1\right)\exp\left[\frac{r}{r_{+}}-\kappa(\tilde{t}+r)\right].

(37)

Thus the IEF chart covers
T+X>0
and
T<\sqrt{X^{2}+1}
. This is only part of the KS plane, as shown in Fig.
4
.

4
Compact CP diagrams

4.1
Standard compactification

To bring the infinities to finite coordinate distance, we introduce

\hat{V}=\arctan V,\qquad\hat{U}=\arctan U,

(39)

and

\hat{T}=\hat{V}+\hat{U},\qquad\hat{X}=\hat{V}-\hat{U}.

(40)

Equivalently,

\hat{T}=\arctan(T+X)+\arctan(T-X),

(41)

\hat{X}=\arctan(T+X)-\arctan(T-X).

(42)

The radial null directions remain at
45^{\circ}
. The horizons are mapped to
\hat{T}=\hat{X}
and
\hat{T}=-\hat{X}
, while the singularities become the spacelike horizontal boundaries
\hat{T}=\pm\pi/2
.

The resulting diagrams are shown in Fig.

A_{0}
. What changes is the physical value of
r_{+}
assigned to the constant-radius curves and the value of
\kappa
entering the relation between
t
and the KS coordinates.

4.2
Regular compact representation

It is also useful to draw the same conformal structure with a smoother compact representation. We use the Penrose-Frolov-Novikov-type map
[
13
]

\tilde{V}=\arctan\left[\arcsinh(V)\right],\qquad\tilde{U}=\arctan\left[\arcsinh(U)\right],

(43)

with

\tilde{T}=\tilde{V}+\tilde{U},\qquad\tilde{X}=\tilde{V}-\tilde{U}.

(44)

This map is monotonic in both null directions, so it preserves the causal character of radial null curves. The condition
X^{2}-T^{2}=-1
is no longer a horizontal line; it is represented by smooth spacelike curves. This does not remove the singularity. It is only a different compact picture of the same spacetime.

The regular compact diagrams are shown in Fig.

and

5
Discussion and conclusions

We have constructed the maximal extension of the Schwarzschild-like black hole solution in LGT. The lapse function
f(r)=A_{0}^{-2}-2\bar{m}/r
gives the horizon radius
r_{+}=2\bar{m}A_{0}^{2}
and the non-affinity coefficient
\kappa=1/(4\bar{m}A_{0}^{4})
. Curvature invariants also depend on
A_{0}
, so the solution is physically Schwarzschild only for
A_{0}=1
.

The SD chart shows the usual obstruction at
r=r_{+}
. The IEF chart removes this obstruction at the future horizon and extends the exterior region into the black-hole interior. However, it does not contain the past horizon, the white-hole region or the second exterior region. These sectors appear naturally after the KS coordinates are introduced.

In the KS chart the metric is regular at the horizon and the relation
X^{2}-T^{2}=(r/r_{+}-1)\exp(r/r_{+})
determines the constant-radius curves. The horizons are
U=0
and
V=0
, and the singularity is
X^{2}-T^{2}=-1
. Therefore the full extension has the same causal arrangement as Schwarzschild: two exterior regions, one black-hole region and one white-hole region.

The CP diagrams confirm this result in compact form. The standard compactification gives horizontal singular boundaries, while the regular compact representation draws the same singularities as smooth spacelike curves. No singularity is removed. The two compactifications only show the same maximally extended spacetime in different visual forms.

The main message is therefore simple. The LGT Schwarzschild-like black hole has a Schwarzschild-type causal topology, but the geometry is not Schwarzschild unless
A_{0}=1
. The parameter
A_{0}
fixes the horizon scale, the surface gravity and the physical meaning of the constant-radius curves. The extension given here provides a clean starting point for future work on thermodynamics, geodesic motion and observational signatures of LGT black holes.
