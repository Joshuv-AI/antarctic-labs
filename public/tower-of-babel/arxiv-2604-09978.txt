Deep Reinforcement Learning for Cognitive Time-Division Joint SAR and Secure Communications
Mohamed-Amine Lahmeri 1 , Ata Khalili 1 , Yujiao Liu 1 , Anke Schmeink 2 , and Robert Schober 1

Abstract
Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imaging can be exploited to enhance wireless communication performance through high-precision environmental awareness. However, integrating sensing and communication functionalities in such wideband systems remains challenging, motivating the development of a joint SAR and communication (JSARC) framework. We propose a dynamic time-division JSARC (TD-JSARC) framework for secure aerial communications that is relevant for critical scenarios, such as surveillance or post-disaster communication, where conventional localization of mobile adversaries often fails. In particular, we consider a secure downlink communication scenario where an aerial base station (ABS) serves a ground user (UE) in the presence of a ground-moving eavesdropper. To detect and track the eavesdropper, the ABS uses cognitive SAR along-track interferometry (ATI) to estimate its position and velocity. Based on these estimates, the ABS applies adaptive beamforming and artificial-noise jamming to enhance secrecy. To this end, we jointly optimize the time and power allocation to maximize the worst-case secrecy rate, while satisfying both SAR and communication constraints. Using the estimated eavesdropper trajectory, we formulate the problem as a Markov decision process (MDP) and solve it via deep reinforcement learning (DRL). Simulation results show that the proposed learning-based approach outperforms both learning and non-learning baseline schemes employing equal-aperture and random time allocation. The proposed method also generalizes well to previously unseen eavesdropper motion patterns.

Although recent studies have started exploring the potential of joint SAR and communication systems, the related literature is limited. Existing works have considered unmanned aerial vehicle-based bistatic joint SAR and communication

, energy-efficient trajectory design

In general, to achieve joint sensing and communication, dual-function systems often rely on complex joint beamforming and waveform design
[
21
]
. Alternatively, time-division joint sensing and communication provides a practical and lower-complexity framework by separating sensing and communication in the time domain. Existing studies have optimized time and power allocation to balance communication and radar detection performance
[
2
]
and developed scheduling strategies for time-division joint sensing and communication systems
[
20
]
. However, these time-division frameworks have been primarily designed for conventional radar and have not yet been extended to joint SAR and communication systems
[

In light of the aforementioned challenges, along-track interferometry emerges as an advanced synthetic aperture radar technique capable of detecting and tracking ground-moving eavesdroppers even for challenging air-to-ground viewing geometries
[
16
]
. along-track interferometry exploits the phase difference between signals received by two or more synthetic aperture radar antennas separated along the flight track
[
13
]
. This phase difference enables the separation of moving targets from stationary ground clutter, which typically limits the performance of conventional radar systems
[
16
]
.

In this work, we propose a cognitive time-division joint SAR and communication system for secure air-to-ground downlink communication. The system leverages synthetic aperture radar along-track interferometry techniques to estimate the location and velocity of a ground-moving eavesdropper, and uses this information to adapt communication beamforming and artificial noise jamming. To this end, we formulate a joint time and power allocation problem that maximizes the worst-case average secrecy rate while satisfying both synthetic aperture radar sensing and communication constraints. The approach is considered
cognitive
as parameters such as the synthetic aperture radar integration time are continuously adapted to the unknown eavesdropper trajectory. The main contributions of this work can be summarized as follows:

•

We propose a novel cognitive time-division joint SAR and communication framework that integrates synthetic aperture radar-based sensing with secure aerial communications for mitigating ground-moving eavesdroppers.

•

We develop a dynamic uncertainty model for the eavesdropper location that jointly accounts for synthetic aperture radar estimation accuracy and prior kinematic constraints, including maximum velocity and acceleration.

•

We formulate the joint time and power allocation problem as a Markov decision process to maximize the average worst-case secrecy rate under both synthetic aperture radar sensing and communication constraints.

•

We propose a learning-based solution that requires no prior knowledge of the eavesdropper’s trajectory and generalizes effectively to unseen eavesdropper trajectories.

II-A

SAR-based Sensing

In each frame, the ABS first performs SAR sensing over a finite integration interval to estimate the eavesdropper’s position and velocity. Based on this estimate, the subsequent communication sub-frame adapts beamforming and jamming to improve secrecy. A longer sensing interval improves localization accuracy, but reduces the time available for data transmission, which creates a sensing-communication tradeoff. Next, we present the proposed dynamic time-division joint SAR and communication framework.

Beamforming and Jamming

The aerial base station jams the last estimated eavesdropper position (i.e., the center of
\mathcal{R}_{i}^{n}
),

, by directing artificial noise toward this location:

\mathbf{A}[n]=\alpha[n]P_{\mathrm{com}}^{\mathrm{max}}\frac{\mathbf{h}_{e}[l_{i}]\mathbf{h}_{e}^{\dagger}[l_{i}]}{\|\mathbf{h}_{e}[l_{i}]\|_{2}^{2}},

\mathbf{w}[n]=\sqrt{(1-\alpha[n])P_{\mathrm{com}}^{\mathrm{max}}}\frac{\mathbf{h}_{u}[n]}{\|\mathbf{h}_{u}[n]\|_{2}}.

(17)

Note that, due to the unknown instantaneous eavesdropper position, the AN is steered toward the latest available estimate

Secrecy Rate

The achievable rate of the user equipement (bits/s/Hz) in time slot

\displaystyle R_{u}[n]=\log_{2}\Biggl(1+\frac{\Big|\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}[n]\mathbf{h}_{u}[n]\Big|^{2}}{\mathbf{h}^{\dagger}_{u}[n]\mathbf{A}[n]\mathbf{h}_{u}[n]+\sigma_{u}^{2}}\Biggr),

(18)

Similarly, the achievable rate of the eavesdropper is given by:

\displaystyle R_{e}[n]=\log_{2}\Biggl(1+\frac{\Big|\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}[n]\mathbf{h}_{e}[n]\Big|^{2}}{\mathbf{h}^{\dagger}_{e}[n]\mathbf{A}[n]\mathbf{h}_{e}[n]+\sigma_{e}^{2}}\Biggr),

(19)

Consequently, the worst-case secrecy rate between the aerial base station and the user equipement is given by:

R[n]\!=\!\left[R_{u}[n]\!-\!\max_{\mathbf{q}_{e}[n]\in\mathcal{R}^{n}_{i}}R_{e}[n](\mathbf{q}_{e}[n])\!\right]^{+}\!,\forall l_{i}<n\in\mathcal{T}_{i}.

(20)

According to the proposed time-division joint SAR and communication framework, for sensing slots

, we have

III
Problem Formulation and Solution

We aim to maximize the average worst-case secrecy rate by jointly optimizing the time and power allocation. In particular, the scheduling variables include the total number of frames (i.e., the synthetic aperture radar apertures)
I
, the length of each frame
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
, and the length of each sensing aperture
\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
. The resulting optimization problem is formulated as follows:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}):\quad

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{maximize}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,\,T_{i},\,L_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\forall i\in\mathcal{I}\\
\alpha[n]\in[0,1],\forall n\in\mathcal{N}\end{subarray}}\quad\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}R[n]

\displaystyle\text{subject to }(\ref{constraint:time_frame}),\ (\ref{constraint:aperture_length}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{SCR}(L_{i})\geq\mathrm{SCR}_{\min},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I},

(21a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}R_{u}[n]\geq R_{\min}.

(21b)

Constraint (
21a
) ensures a minimum signal-to-clutter-ratio to separate target motion from strong clutter, while constraint (
21b
) guarantees a minimum average rate for the legitimate user.
Problem
(\mathcal{P})
captures a key trade-off in time-division joint SAR and communication: Longer synthetic aperture radar apertures improve spatial resolution and sensing accuracy, enhancing communication in later frames, but reduce transmission time. Moreover, the power-splitting factor
\alpha
balances information transmission via maximum ratio transmission beamforming and artificial noise jamming.

Problem
(\mathcal{P})
is a challenging non-convex mixed-integer optimization problem. First, the eavesdropper trajectory is unknown to the aerial base station. Second, the number of synthetic aperture radar apertures
I
, which determines the problem dimension, is itself an optimization variable. Last, the problem involves both continuous and integer variables, whose strong coupling makes global optimization computationally prohibitive.

To address problem
\mathcal{(P)}
, we reformulate it as an Markov decision process and solve it using deep reinforcement learning. This approach is motivated by the unknown trajectory of the eavesdropper. Additionally, deep reinforcement learning has shown great potential for solving complex and high-dimensional decision-making problems
[
18
]
.

An Markov decision process is defined by the tuple

\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},r,\gamma)

where

the action space,

\gamma\in[0,1)

III-A
1
Action

The original time-allocation variables
I
,
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
, and
\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
can be equivalently represented via a binary scheduling action

. Specifically, at time slot

, the agent selects sensing for

. Given the full action sequence
\{a[n]\}_{n=1}^{N}
, the corresponding time-allocation variables
I
,
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
, and
\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
can be reconstructed accordingly. To maintain a discrete action space, the power-splitting factor
\alpha[n]
(relevant only for

) is excluded from the agent’s action and is addressed separately via a low-dimensional robust optimization problem. To this end, the instantaneous worst-case secrecy rate,

, is maximized for a given time-allocation, leading to the following max-min problem:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{1}):\quad

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{max}}_{\alpha[n]\in[0,1]}\mathop{\text{min}}_{\mathbf{q}_{e}[n]\in\mathcal{R}^{n}_{i}}\quad\Big[R_{u}[n]-R_{e}[n]\Big]^{+}.

We solve the low-dimensional problem,
(\mathcal{P}_{1})
, via finite exhaustive search over
\alpha[n]\in[0,1]
. For a fixed
\alpha[n]
, the inner minimization is equivalent to maximizing the eavesdropper communication signal-to-noise ratio, which can be expressed in terms of

and

(see (
11
) and (
13

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{2}):\quad

III-A
3
Reward

The reward at time slot
n
is designed to encourage high secrecy performance while enforcing sensing and communication constraints. During sensing slots (i.e.,
a[n]=1
), no data transmission occurs and thus no secrecy gain is achieved. Accordingly, we set
r[n]=0
. For communication slots (i.e.,
a[n]=0
), the reward is defined as

r[n]=\begin{cases}-\rho_{2},\quad\quad\text{if}\quad\mathrm{SCR}(L[l[n]])<\mathrm{SCR}_{\min},&\\
R[n]-\rho_{1}\left[R_{\min}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{u}[i]\right]^{+},\text{otherwise},&\end{cases}

(27)

where
\rho_{1}
and

are positive penalty factors enforcing constraints (
21b
) and (
21a

To solve problem
(\mathcal{P})

[

]
. Let
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
denote the policy parameters. The policy

, where
\hat{A}_{n}
denotes the advantage estimate
[

]
. In each interaction step, the agent selects an action according to
\pi_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}(a[n]|\textbf{s}[n])
. If communication is selected, the robust power-splitting factor
\alpha^{\star}[n]
is computed using

. The resulting reward is then used to update the policy. We refer the reader to

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a time-division joint SAR and communication scheme that exploits cognitive synthetic aperture radar along-track interferometry to first estimate the location of a ground-moving eavesdropper and subsequently enables secure downlink communication with a user equipement through adaptive beamforming and jamming. The resulting joint time and power allocation problem is addressed using a learning-based approach that combines discrete scheduling with robust power allocation optimization. Simulation results demonstrated that the proposed solution significantly improves the secrecy rate compared to learning- and non-learning-based benchmark schemes, while maintaining robustness to previously unseen eavesdropper trajectories. Future work will extend the proposed framework to joint trajectory, beamforming, and jamming optimization under more general synthetic aperture radar-along-track interferometry constraints and explore higher synthetic aperture radar-communication integration, e.g., via waveform design.
