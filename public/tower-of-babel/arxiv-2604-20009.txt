A Hierarchy of Edge-Weight Symmetries in Perfect Matchings
Kristóf Bérczi, Viktor Csaplár, Yutaro Yamaguchi

Abstract
Motivated by the exact weight perfect matching problem and recent parameterized algorithms for finding an ℓ \ell -th smallest perfect matching, we study structural properties of edge-weight symmetries in graphs. Recent work by El Maalouly et al. (ESA 2025) showed that excluding all perfect matchings whose weight is at most the ( ℓ − 1 ) (\ell-1) -th smallest possible value in the graph requires fixing at most 2 ​ ( ℓ − 1 ) 2(\ell-1) edges in non-bipartite graphs and at most ℓ − 1 \ell-1 edges in bipartite graphs. A natural open question is whether fixing a single edge is always sufficient to shift the extreme (minimum or maximum) weight of a perfect matching when the global minimum and maximum weights differ. To address this, we define and analyze a hierarchy of progressively weaker edge-weight properties: node-induced weights , even walk and cycle symmetries , perfect matching equality , and the edge min-max property . We derive a basic hierarchy among these conditions and show that they become equivalent in bipartite graphs. For general graphs, we provide tight structural characterizations, based on block and tight cut decompositions, under which even cycle symmetry and perfect matching equality force node-induced weights. Finally, we resolve the motivating open question in the negative by constructing a matching-covered non-bipartite graph that satisfies the edge min-max property (every edge is contained in a minimum-weight perfect matching and a maximum-weight one) but violates perfect matching equality (all perfect matchings have the same weight). This counterexample shows that a single edge is not always sufficient to eliminate all minimum-weight or maximum-weight perfect matchings, thereby proving the tightness of the 2 ​ ( ℓ − 1 ) 2(\ell-1) bound for ℓ = 2 \ell=2 . We also discuss extensions of this framework to b b -factors and arborescences. Keywords: Arborescences, b b -factors, Exact matching, Matching-covered graphs, Node-induced weights, Perfect matchings

The

In contrast, minimum- and maximum-weight perfect matchings can be computed deterministically in polynomial time

A matching-covered graph that does not admit any tight cuts is called a

. We construct another odd walk

starting and ending at

which completes the proof if

is non-bipartite.

If

is bipartite, we proceed in a similar way. Fix an arbitrary node

and set

to be an arbitrary real value. For every node

be its edges in order where

is incident to

We first show that

is well defined, i.e., independent of the choice of the path. Let

results in an even closed walk. By

, the alternating sum of edge weights over this walk is zero, hence

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}w(e_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{k+i+1}w(e^{\prime}_{\ell+1-i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}w(e_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{k+\ell-i}w(e^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}w(e_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{i+1}w(e^{\prime}_{i}).

Since

We now show that

be a

Let

be a graph and

Lemma 4.1

Let

be a connected graph. Then
\dim(\mathcal{N}_{G})=|V|-1

\dim(\mathcal{N}_{G})=|V|

Proof.

Let

. Such an

for every

. This defines a linear map

, mapping

to

. By definition,
\mathcal{N}_{G}=\im\varphi

\dim(\mathbb{R}^{V})=\dim(\ker\varphi)+\dim(\im\varphi)

\dim(\mathcal{N}_{G})=|V|-\dim(\ker\varphi)
. Thus it suffices to determine

for every

First assume that

. For every edge

, hence the values of

alternate along

. Since

has odd length, this forces

to take the same value and its negative at the starting node, hence

on

. Now let

be arbitrary. Since

is connected, there exists a path from

to

. Along each edge

of this path we have

, so values propagate along the path. Since all nodes of

have value

, it follows that

. Hence

, and
\dim(\ker\varphi)=0
.

Now assume that

is bipartite with partition classes

and

. Let

. Then

for every edge

with

and

and

implies

. Similarly, for every

on every edge, hence lies in

. Therefore

.

Combining both cases with
\dim(\mathcal{N}_{G})=|V|-\dim(\ker\varphi)
yields the statement of the lemma.
∎

The following is a direct corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let

be a connected non-bipartite graph. Consider the linear map

The statement follows from the fact that

Let

We now turn to the structural consequence of having a parity-consistent ear decomposition. In this setting, we show that the orthogonal complement of the alternating cycle space coincides exactly with the space of node-induced weight functions.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

be a graph whose block decomposition contains at most one non-bipartite block. Then
\mathcal{C}_{G}=\mathcal{N}_{G}^{\perp}

Proof.

By
4.4

Therefore, assume that

is 2-edge-connected. We prove this by induction on the number of ears in a carefully chosen ear decomposition given by Theorem

contains a non-bipartite block, the construction starts within such a block and begins with an odd cycle in that block.

Now assume that

is obtained from

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{C}_{G_{i+1}})

\displaystyle=(|E(G_{i})|+\ell+1)-(|V(G_{i})|+\ell-1)

\displaystyle=|E(G_{i})|-|V(G_{i})|+2

\displaystyle=\dim(\mathcal{C}_{G_{i}})+1.

We show that

. For this, it is enough to find an even cycle in

that uses the new ear. If the ear is closed, then it is itself an even cycle. Otherwise, let its endpoints be

Case 2:

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{C}_{G_{i+1}})

\displaystyle\leq|E(G_{i+1})|-\dim(\mathcal{N}_{G_{i+1}})

\displaystyle=(|E(G_{i})|+\ell+1)-(|V(G_{i})|+\ell)

\displaystyle=|E(G_{i})|-|V(G_{i})|+1

\displaystyle=\dim(\mathcal{C}_{G_{i}})+1.

We show that

. For this, it is enough to find an even cycle in

that uses the new ear. If the ear is closed, then it is itself an even cycle as, by the choice of the decomposition, it is part of a bipartite block of

In all cases, the inductive step preserves

The goal of this section is to clarify the connection between

5
b
-factors and arborescences

The proof of Theorem
1.1
in
[
4
]
(with symmetry of minimum and maximum) is based on the observation that, when the maximum-weight matching problem is formulated as a linear program and its dual is considered, the complementary slackness conditions imply that a perfect matching is of maximum weight if and only if it consists of so-called tight edges, and it enters each member of a certain laminar family (the support of an optimal dual solution) with exactly one edge. This means that, if we wish to fix edges in such a way that the weight of a maximum-weight perfect matching decreases, it suffices to fix either a non-tight edge, or two tight edges that both enter the same member of the laminar family. In this section, we extend this idea in two directions: minimum-weight
b
-factors and minimum-cost arborescences.

b
-factor, exactly
b(v)
edges of
S
are incident to each node
v
. Because the outer edges form a complete bipartite graph between
\{v_{j}\colon j\in[b(v)]\}
and
\{p_{e,v}\colon e\text{ is incident to }v\}
, we can easily route these choices such that every copy
v_{j}
is covered exactly once. This logic holds in reverse, meaning every perfect matching of
G^{\prime}
can be represented as a
b
-factor of
G
. An important property of this construction is that
G^{\prime}
is bipartite if and only if
G
is bipartite.

Given a weight function
w\colon E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define a weight function
w^{\prime}
on the edges of
G^{\prime}
. For all outer edges, let
w^{\prime}(u_{i}p_{e,u})\coloneqq w(e)/2
and
w^{\prime}(v_{j}p_{e,v})\coloneqq w(e)/2
. For all inner edges, let
w^{\prime}(p_{e,u}p_{e,v})\coloneqq 0
. When an edge
e=uv\in S
, the corresponding perfect matching
P
selects exactly two outer edges associated with
e
, accumulating a total weight of
w(e)/2+w(e)/2=w(e)
. If
e\notin S
,
P
selects the inner edge, contributing a weight of
0
. Thus, a
b
-factor of
G
has the exact same weight as its corresponding perfect matching in
G^{\prime}
.

Now we are ready to prove the statement of the theorem. We consider the case where
G
is bipartite; the non-bipartite case works similarly. Construct
G^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}
as previously described. Since a
b
-factor has the same weight as its corresponding perfect matching, the possible weights of the perfect matchings of
G^{\prime}
are also
x_{1}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{q}
. We can therefore apply
1.1
. This implies there exists an edge set
A\subseteq E^{\prime}
of size at most
\ell-1
such that the minimum weight of a perfect matching containing
A
is
x_{\ell}
.

From
A
, we determine the sets
F
and
D
for our original graph
G
. We assign an edge
e=uv\in E
to
D
if
p_{e,u}p_{e,v}\in A
. We assign
e=uv\in E
to
F
if
A
contains at least one of its outer edges, that is, if
A\cap(\{u_{i}p_{e,u}\mid i\in[b(u)]\}\cup\{v_{j}p_{e,v}\mid j\in[b(v)]\})\neq\emptyset
. From the correspondence described earlier, the perfect matchings of
G^{\prime}
containing
A
correspond exactly to the
b
-factors
S
of
G
satisfying
F\subseteq S\subseteq E\setminus D
. Furthermore, because
A
is a subset of a valid matching in
G^{\prime}
, it cannot contain both an inner edge and an outer edge of the same gadget. Thus, each edge in
A
contributes to at most one element in
D\cup F
, which yields
|D|+|F|\leq|A|\leq\ell-1
, completing the proof.
∎

5.2
Arborescences

The extension to arborescences is based on an LP-based characterization of minimum-cost arborescences.
An
arborescence
is a directed tree in which every node has in-degree
1
, except for a unique node, called the
root
, which has in-degree
0
. An
r
-
arborescence
of a digraph is a spanning subgraph that is an arborescence rooted at a specified node
r
.

Let
D=(V,A)
be a digraph with a fixed root
r\in V
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{D}
denote the set of all
r
-arborescences of
D
. An edge
uv\in A
is said to
enter
a set
Z\subseteq V
if
u\notin Z
and
v\in Z
. For
Z\subseteq V
and
F\subseteq A
, let
d^{\mathrm{in}}_{F}(Z)
denote the number of edges entering
Z
in
F
. We say that
D
is
root-connected
if
d^{\mathrm{in}}_{A}(Z)\geq 1
holds for every nonempty set
Z\subseteq V-{r}
. This is equivalent to requiring that every node is reachable from
r
, and also to the existence of an
r
-arborescence in
D
.

Suppose that
D
is root-connected.
Fix an edge
e=uv\in A
, and let
D^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
D
by removing all edges except
e
that enter
v
and contracting
e
.
Then, there is a trivial one-to-one correspondence between the
r
-arborescences in
D
containing
e
and the
r
-arborescences in
D^{\prime}
.
Indeed, for any
r
-arborescence
F
in
D
containing
e
, the edge set in
D^{\prime}
corresponding to
F-e
is an
r
-arborescence in
D^{\prime}
.
Conversely, for any
r
-arborescence
F^{\prime}
in
D^{\prime}
, an
r
-arborescence in
D
is obtained by adding
e
to the edge set in
D
corresponding to
F^{\prime}
.
This implies that
D^{\prime}
is also root-connected with respect to the same root
r
.

Let
c\colon A\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a nonnegative cost function. For a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
and an edge
a\in A
, define
\mathcal{F}_{a}=\{Z\in\mathcal{F}\colon a\text{ enters }Z\}
. A vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{F}}
is called
c
-feasible
if
y\geq 0
and
\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{F}_{a}}y(Z)\leq c(a)
for every
a\in A
. When
\mathcal{F}=\{Z\colon\emptyset\neq Z\subseteq V-r\}
, a
c
-feasible vector
y
is in fact a dual solution to the minimum-cost
r
-arborescence problem. An edge
a\in A
is called
c
-tight
if
\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{F}_{a}}y(Z)=c(a)
. Bock
[
1
]
and Fulkerson
[
5
]
proved the following min-max formula.

Theorem 5.1

(Bock, Fulkerson)
.

Let
D=(V,A)
be a root-connected digraph with root
r
, and let
c\colon A\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a cost function. Then the minimum cost of an
r
-arborescence of
D
equals

\max\left\{\sum_{Z\subseteq V-r}y(Z)\colon y\text{ is }c\text{-feasible}\right\}.

Moreover, an optimal dual solution
y^{\star}
can be chosen such that the family
\{Z\colon y^{\star}(Z)>0\}
is laminar. For any optimal dual solution
y^{\star}
, an
r
-arborescence
B\in\mathcal{A}_{D}
is minimum-cost if and only if

(i)

every edge
a\in B
is
y^{\star}
-tight, and

(ii)

d^{\mathrm{in}}_{B}(Z)=1
for every nonempty set
Z\subseteq V-r
with
y^{\star}(Z)>0
.

Based on this result, we first prove the following.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
D=(V,A)
be a root-connected digraph with root
r
, and let
c\colon A\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a cost function. Let
F
be an
r
-arborescence of second-smallest cost with respect to
c
. Then there exist edges
e,f\in A
such that

c(F)=\min\bigl\{c(F^{\prime})\colon F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{D},\,\{e,f\}\subseteq F^{\prime}\bigr\}.

Proof.

Let
y
be a fixed optimal
c
-feasible function. Since
F
is not a minimum-cost
r
-arborescence, by
5.1
it either contains an edge that is not
y
-tight, or there exists a nonempty set
Z\subseteq V-r
with
y(Z)>0
such that
F
enters
Z
more than once.

In the first case, let
e
be a non-
y
-tight edge of
F
and let
f
be any other edge of
F
. In the latter case, let
e,f
be two edges of
F
entering
Z
. In either case, by
5.1
, any
r
-arborescence containing both
e
and
f
cannot be of minimum cost. Since
F
is a second-smallest
r
-arborescence and contains both
e
and
f
by definition, we obtain

\min\bigl\{c(F^{\prime})\colon F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{D}\bigr\}<\min\bigl\{c(F^{\prime})\colon F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{D},\,\{e,f\}\subseteq F^{\prime}\bigr\}=c(F),

which completes the proof.
∎

The next theorem is a counterpart of Theorem
1.1
for arborescences.

See

Proof.

We proceed by induction over
\ell
. For
\ell=1
the statement is trivial and
5.2
proves the statement for
\ell=2
. Let
\ell\geq 3
and assume as induction hypothesis that the statement holds for all
\ell^{\prime}<\ell
and let
F
be an
\ell
-th smallest
r
-arborescence. Based on the argument in
5.2
there exist
e,f\in F
such that

x_{1}<\bigl\{c(F^{\prime})\colon F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{D},\,\{e,f\}\subseteq F^{\prime}\bigr\}\leq c(F).

Note that
e=f
is possible, and otherwise the heads of
e
and
f
are different nodes.

Consider the graph
D^{\prime}
obtained from
D
by removing all edges except
e
and
f
that have the same head as
e
or
f
and contracting the edges
e
and
f
.
As seen above, there is a trivial one-to-one correspondence between the
r
-arborescences in
D
containing
\{e,f\}
and the
r
-arborescences in
D^{\prime}
, and
D^{\prime}
is also root-connected as
F\setminus\{e,f\}
forms an
r
-arborescence in
D^{\prime}
. Moreover,
F\setminus\{e,f\}
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-th smallest
r
-arborescence for some
\ell^{\prime}<\ell
.
This is because otherwise there are at least
(\ell-1)

r
-arborescences
F^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{D^{\prime}}
such that
c(F^{\prime}_{1})<c(F^{\prime}_{2})<\dots<c(F^{\prime}_{\ell-1})<c(F\setminus\{e,f\})
, which implies
x_{1}<c(F^{\prime}_{1}\cup\{e,f\})<c(F^{\prime}_{2}\cup\{e,f\})<\dots<c(F^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\cup\{e,f\})<c(F)
, contradicting
c(F)=x_{\ell}
.

By the induction hypothesis, there exists
P^{\prime}\subseteq F\setminus\{e,f\}\in\mathcal{A}_{D^{\prime}}
with
|P^{\prime}|\leq 2(\ell^{\prime}-1)
such that

c(F\setminus\{e,f\})=\min\bigl\{c(F^{\prime})\colon F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{D^{\prime}},\,P^{\prime}\subseteq F^{\prime}\bigr\}.

This concludes that
P=P^{\prime}\cup\{e,f\}
satisfies
|P|\leq 2(\ell^{\prime}-1)+2\leq 2(\ell-1)
and

x_{\ell}=c(F)=\min\bigl\{c(F^{\prime})\colon F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{D},\,P\subseteq F^{\prime}\bigr\},

which completes the proof.
