Polarization-dependent mass modifications of ϕ \phi meson with finite momentum in nuclear matter
Ahmad Jafar Arifi, Philipp Gubler, Kazuo Tsushima

Abstract
We investigate the in-medium properties of the ϕ \phi meson with finite momentum, going beyond the commonly studied case at rest. In a nuclear medium, Lorentz invariance is broken, leading to distinct longitudinal and transverse polarization modes that evolve differently with density and momentum. Within an effective Lagrangian approach, we calculate the polarization-dependent mass shifts and width modifications of the ϕ \phi meson arising from kaon loops and mean-field interactions. The divergent loop integrals are regulated using two different schemes: a covariant form factor and dimensional regularization. Our results show that the mass shift of the transverse polarization is independent of the ϕ \phi -meson momentum, whereas that of the longitudinal polarization decreases quadratically with momentum. This difference originates from the coupling of the longitudinal mode to the vector mean field and derivative-type interactions in the self-energy. These effects have direct implications for experimental observables, especially for upcoming measurements at J-PARC, and provide a new prediction for experiments studying hadron dynamics in dense matter.



I
Introduction

Understanding hadron property changes in dense nuclear environments is a central challenge in nonperturbative quantum chromodynamics (QCD), as medium-induced modifications reflect changes in quark and gluon dynamics and the reduction of the quark condensate, signaling partial restoration of chiral symmetry (in light quark sector)
Brown and Rho 1996
;
Leupold et al. 2010
;
Hayano and Hatsuda 2010

Brown and Rho 1991
and QCD sum rules (QCDSR)
Hatsuda and Lee 1992

\phi
meson is particularly notable: its dominant
s\bar{s}
structure and narrow vacuum width make it a relatively clean probe of in-medium dynamics, less affected by conventional hadronic many-body effects than the
\rho
and
\omega
mesons.

The in-medium properties of the
\phi
meson is known to be sensitive to the strange sigma term of the nucleon

\sigma_{sN}\equiv m_{s}\langle N|\bar{s}s|N\rangle

Gubler and Weise 2016
;
Kim et al. 2022

Yang et al. 2016
;
Abdel-Rehim et al. 2016
;
Borsanyi et al. 2020
;
Bali et al. 2023
;
Aoki et al. 2026
, although there is a tension with the extracted value from experiments. Studies of the
\phi N
interaction

;
Kim et al. 2021
;
Lyu et al. 2022
;

Tolos and Fabbietti 2020

;

;

;

;

;

On the theoretical side, most studies have focused on the

,
\phi
mesons carry finite momentum in a Lorentz symmetry broken environment, generating distinct longitudinal and transverse polarization modes with different in-medium evolution. Such polarization-dependent effects represent a qualitatively new aspect of vector meson dynamics and have been explored mainly within QCDSR analyses

In this work, we study the in-medium
\phi

\phi

\phi

\phi

II
Self-energy of
\Phi
meson

In this work, we study in-medium modifications of the
\phi
-meson mass and width in the laboratory frame, as relevant for J-PARC and other experiments
Yokkaichi et al. 2007
;
Naruki 2012
;
Aoki et al. 2023
;
Aoki et al. 2025
;
Sako et al. 2024
.
Thus, we focus on medium effects on the
\phi
-meson self-energy at finite momentum
p^{\mu}=(p^{0},0,0,|\bm{p}|)
, where the kaon inside the loop is modified.
In a nuclear medium, Lorentz symmetry is broken due to the existence of a preferred frame defined by the medium four-velocity:
u^{\mu}=(1,0,0,0)
, leading to a separation between transverse and longitudinal polarization modes.
The self-energy tensor
{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}
can then be given as
Gale and Kapusta 1991

{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(p)=P_{T}^{\mu\nu}\Pi^{T}(p)+P_{L}^{\mu\nu}\Pi^{L}(p),

(1)

where the splitting vanishes in the
\phi
-meson rest frame, in which both modes become degenerate.
Here,
P_{T}^{\mu\nu}
and
P_{L}^{\mu\nu}
are the transverse and longitudinal projectors which satisfy

P_{T}^{\mu\nu}+P_{L}^{\mu\nu}=-g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{p^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{p^{2}},

(2)

and can be used to separate tensor structures in in-medium self-energies and propagators according to their polarization modes.
Both projectors satisfy the transversality conditions
P_{L/T}^{\mu\nu}p_{\mu}=0
and the following condition
{P_{L}^{\mu}}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}{P_{T}^{\mu}}_{\mu}=-1
.
The projectors have also the property
P_{L/T}^{\mu\sigma}P^{L/T}_{\sigma\nu}=-{P_{\nu,L/T}^{\mu}}
and
P_{T}^{\mu\sigma}P^{L}_{\sigma\nu}=0
.
The transverse projector can be expressed as
P_{T}^{00}=P_{T}^{0i}=P_{T}^{i0}=0
and

\displaystyle P_{T}^{ij}=\delta^{ij}-\frac{\bm{p}^{i}\bm{p}^{j}}{\bm{p}^{2}},

(3)

while the longitudinal polarization projector can then be computed as

P_{L}^{\mu\nu}=-g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{p^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{p^{2}}-P_{T}^{\mu\nu}.

(4)

The longitudinal and transverse self-energy of the
\phi
meson can therefore be computed as

\displaystyle\Pi_{T}(p)=\frac{1}{2}P^{T}_{\mu\nu}~\Pi^{\mu\nu}(p),\qquad\Pi_{L}(p)=P^{L}_{\mu\nu}~\Pi^{\mu\nu}(p).

(5)

II.1
Effective Lagrangian approach

Figure 1:
Self-energy of the
\phi
meson through: (top) a
K\bar{K}
loop term and (bottom) a contact four-point coupling term.

The
\phi
-meson self-energy in nuclear matter is evaluated in an effective Lagrangian approach
Ko et al. 1992
;
Cobos-Martínez et al. 2017
.
The dominant interaction is provided by the
\phi K\bar{K}
coupling,

\mathcal{L}_{\phi K\bar{K}}=ig_{\phi}\phi^{\mu}\left[\bar{K}(\partial_{\mu}K)-(\partial_{\mu}\bar{K})K\right],

(6)

together with the corresponding four-point
\phi\phi K\bar{K}
interaction,

\mathcal{L}_{\phi\phi K\bar{K}}=g_{\phi}^{2}\phi^{\mu}\phi_{\mu}\bar{K}K,

(7)

where the kaon and antikaon fields are written as

K=\begin{pmatrix}K^{+}\\
K^{0}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bar{K}=\begin{pmatrix}K^{-}&\bar{K}^{0}\end{pmatrix}.

(8)

The interaction terms in Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
) arise naturally from a gauged kaon kinetic Lagrangian, in which the
\phi
meson is introduced as a vector gauge field
Ko et al. 1992
;
Cobos-Martínez et al. 2017
.
Starting from the free kaon Lagrangian and promoting the ordinary derivative to a covariant one,
\partial_{\mu}\to D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ig_{\phi}\phi_{\mu}
, the expansion of the gauged kinetic term generates simultaneously the three-point
\phi K\bar{K}
vertex and the four-point
\phi\phi K\bar{K}
interaction.
As a consequence, these couplings are not independent but are constrained by local gauge invariance.

At the level of the
\phi
-meson self-energy, the three-point interaction gives rise to the kaon–antikaon loop contribution, while the four-point
coupling term generates a contact (to be noted by “con” hereafter) diagram as shown in Fig.
1
.
Their combined contribution

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{total}}(p)=\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\rm loop}(p)+\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\rm con}(p)

(9)

ensures the transversality of the vector-meson self-energy,

p_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{total}}(p)=0.

(10)

The
\Pi^{\mu\nu}
computed from a kaon loop and a contact term in Fig.
1
can be expressed as
Ko et al. 1992
;
Cobos-Martínez et al. 2017

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\rm loop}(p)=

\displaystyle~2ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{S^{\mu\nu}_{\rm loop}}{D_{K}(q)D_{\bar{K}}(p-q)},

(11)

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\rm con}(p)=

\displaystyle~2ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{S^{\mu\nu}_{\rm con}}{D_{K}(q)},

(12)

where
q
is the kaon four-momentum and the factor two is coming from the charged and neutral kaon loop
Cobos-Martínez et al. 2017
.
Here, we assume that the kaon is a stable particle and has no width.
The numerators are given by

\displaystyle S_{\rm loop}^{\mu\nu}=(2q-p)^{\mu}(2q-p)^{\nu},\qquad S_{\rm con}^{\mu\nu}=-2g^{\mu\nu},

(13)

while the kaon and antikaon propagators are expressed by

\displaystyle D_{K}(q)

\displaystyle=q^{2}-m_{K}^{2}+i\epsilon,

(14)

\displaystyle D_{\bar{K}}(p-q)

\displaystyle=(p-q)^{2}-m_{K}^{2}+i\epsilon,

(15)

where
m_{K}(=m_{\bar{K}})
is the kaon scalar mass.
Projecting onto the transverse and longitudinal polarization modes (
\lambda=T,L
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{total}}(p)=

\displaystyle~2ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}^{\lambda}_{\rm loop}}{D_{K}D_{\bar{K}}}+\frac{\mathcal{O}^{\lambda}_{\rm con}}{D_{K}}\right),

(16)

where the operator
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{L}=P^{L}_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu\nu},\qquad\mathcal{O}_{T}=\frac{1}{2}P^{T}_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu\nu}.

(17)

II.2
In-medium modifications

In nuclear matter, the
\phi
-meson self-energy can be written as
Cobos-Martínez et al. 2017

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{\lambda}(p)=2ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm loop}^{\lambda*}}{D_{K}^{*}D_{\bar{K}}^{*}}+\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm con}^{\lambda*}}{D_{K}^{*}}\right),

(18)

where

\displaystyle D_{K}^{*}=

\displaystyle~(q^{0*})^{2}-\bm{q}^{2}-m_{K}^{*2}+i\epsilon,

(19)

\displaystyle D_{\bar{K}}^{*}=

\displaystyle~(p^{0*}-q^{0*})^{2}-(\bm{p}-\bm{q})^{2}-m_{K}^{*2}+i\epsilon,

(20)

with the scalar mean field affecting the kaon mass,

m_{K}^{*}\equiv m_{K}-V_{\sigma},

(21)

while the vector mean field affecting the kaon energy, modifying the dispersion relation

q^{0*}=q^{0}+V_{\omega},\qquad(p-q)^{0*}=(p-q)^{0}-V_{\omega}.

(22)

Note that we denote all in-medium quantities with an asterisk.
In the lab frame, we can do a variable shift,

\displaystyle q^{0}\to q^{0}-V_{\omega},

(23)

and can obtain the same formal expression as in Eq. (
18
),
with operators shifted as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{\rm loop}^{\lambda*}\;\to\;{\mathcal{O}}_{\rm loop}^{\lambda*}(p,q^{0}-V_{\omega}).

(24)

Note that, the operator
{\mathcal{O}}_{\rm loop}^{\lambda*}
can depend explicitly on
q^{0}
, and this dependence
is modified by the vector mean field
V_{\omega}
.
As a consequence, the transverse and longitudinal polarization modes become separated.
However,
{\mathcal{O}}_{\rm con}^{\lambda*}
does not depend on
q^{0}
and therefore is not affected by
V_{\omega}
.
After the shift in Eq. (
23
), the in-medium propagators can be defined without
V_{\omega}
dependence as

\displaystyle D_{K}^{*}

\displaystyle=(q^{0})^{2}-\bm{q}^{2}-m_{K}^{*2}+i\epsilon,

(25)

\displaystyle D_{\bar{K}}^{*}

\displaystyle=(p^{0}-q^{0})^{2}-(\bm{p}-\bm{q})^{2}-m_{K}^{*2}+i\epsilon,

(26)

and this notation will be used throughout this work.

II.3
In-medium
\phi
mass and spectral functions

Once the
\phi
self-energy
\Pi(p)
is obtained, the respective in-medium mass and decay width can be determined. When the kinematic condition
m_{\phi}^{*}>2m_{K}^{*}
is satisfied, the self-energy develops an imaginary part, corresponding to the opening of the
\phi\to K\bar{K}
decay channel in the medium.

The in-medium mass of the
\phi
meson is then determined from the real part of the self-energy through

\displaystyle m_{\phi}^{*2}=(m_{\phi}^{0})^{2}+\text{Re}~\Pi(E_{\phi}^{*2},\bm{p}^{2}),

(27)

where
m_{\phi}^{0}
denotes the bare
\phi
-meson mass fixed in vacuum.
The corresponding in-medium decay width for the
\phi\to K\bar{K}
process is given by the imaginary part of the self-energy,

\Gamma_{\phi}^{*}=-\frac{1}{E_{\phi}^{*}}\text{Im}~\Pi(E_{\phi}^{*2},\bm{p}^{2}),

(28)

with
E_{\phi}^{*}=\sqrt{m_{\phi}^{*2}+\bm{p}^{2}}
.
Note that by defining
\gamma=E_{\phi}^{*}/m_{\phi}^{*}
, the in-medium decay width can be rewritten as
\Gamma_{\phi}^{*}=\Gamma_{\phi}^{*\text{0}}/\gamma
. While
\Gamma_{\phi}^{*\text{0}}
captures the dynamical effects of the medium on the
\phi
-meson, the
\gamma
factor accounts for the kinematical effect, originating from Lorentz time dilation, which suppresses the observed width in the moving frame.
This also implies that a fast
\phi
meson has a longer effective lifetime and is more likely to decay outside the nucleus, resulting in smaller medium effects on the observables. Larger modifications, in contrast, are observed experimentally for slow
\phi
mesons
Muto et al. 2007
;
Ichikawa et al. 2025
.

Equations (
27
) and (
28
) are solved self-consistently using the in-medium kaon mass
m_{K}^{*}
to obtain the
\phi
meson mass shift and width. In Sec.
III
, we evaluate the self-energy using two different regularization schemes in order to assess the robustness and model dependence of our results.

To make connection with experiment,
it is useful to introduce the in-medium
\phi
-meson spectral functions using a Breit-Wigner parametrization
Kim and Gubler 2020
,

\displaystyle A_{\phi}^{\lambda}(s,\bm{p}^{2})=\frac{C_{\lambda}}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{s}\Gamma_{\phi}^{*}(\bm{p})}{(s-m_{\phi}^{*2}(\bm{p}))^{2}+s\Gamma_{\phi}^{*2}(\bm{p})}.

(29)

The normalization constant
C_{\lambda}
is determined by the condition

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\;A_{\phi}^{\lambda}(s,\bm{p}^{2})=1.

(30)

Since most dilepton measurements have so far not been able to resolve individual polarization states,
it is natural to introduce a polarization-averaged spectral function, defined as

\displaystyle A_{\phi}^{\mathrm{ave}}(s,\bm{p}^{2})=\frac{1}{3}\biggl[A_{\phi}^{L}(s,\bm{p}^{2})+2A_{\phi}^{T}(s,\bm{p}^{2})\biggr].

(31)

This polarization averaging provides a practical connection between theoretical predictions, transport calculations, and dilepton measurements
Gubler et al. 2025
.

II.4
In-medium kaon mass

The modification of the kaon mass and energy in nuclear matter is an important input in our study.
The kaon mass and energy in medium are evaluated in the QMC model
Tsushima et al. 1998
, which was proposed by Guichon
Guichon 1988
and has been successfully applied to describe both infinite nuclear matter and finite nuclei (see Refs.
Saito et al. 2007
;
Guichon et al. 2018
for reviews).
Here we summarize only the elements relevant to the present work.

In the QMC model, nuclear matter is treated in its rest frame within the mean-field (Hartree) approximation, where the scalar and vector meson fields are spatially constant.
The light quarks
q=(u,d)
inside a hadron bag couple directly to these mean fields, while the strange quark remains unaffected. Here we assume SU(2) symmetry for the light quarks (
m_{q}=m_{u}=m_{d}
).
The corresponding Dirac equations for the quark fields in symmetric nuclear matter,

\displaystyle[i\not{\partial}-m_{q}^{*}\mp\gamma^{0}V_{\omega}^{q}]\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{q}\\
\psi_{\bar{q}}\end{pmatrix}=0,\quad[i\not{\partial}-m_{s}]\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{s}\\
\psi_{\bar{s}}\end{pmatrix}=0,

(32)

lead to an effective light-quark mass

\displaystyle m_{q}^{*}\equiv m_{q}-V_{\sigma}^{q}.

(33)

Since we restrict our study to symmetric nuclear matter, the contribution from the
\rho
meson mean field can be neglected.
The mean-field potentials are then written as

V_{\sigma}^{q}=g_{\sigma}^{q}\sigma,\qquad V_{\omega}^{q}=g_{\omega}^{q}\omega,

(34)

where
g_{\sigma}^{q}
and
g_{\omega}^{q}
are the corresponding quark–meson coupling constants.

The normalized static solution for a ground-state quark or antiquark of flavor
f
in a kaon
K
can be expressed as

\psi_{f}(x)=N_{f}\,e^{-i\epsilon_{f}t/R_{K}^{*}}\,\psi_{f}(\bm{r}),

(35)

where
N_{f}
is the normalization factor and
\psi_{f}(\bm{r})
denotes the spatial and spinor wave function.
The in-medium bag radius,
R_{K}^{*}
, is determined by imposing the stability condition on the hadron mass with respect to variations of the radius.

The corresponding eigenenergies (in units of
1/R_{K}^{*}
) take the form

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{u}\\
\epsilon_{\bar{u}}\end{pmatrix}=\Omega_{q}^{*}\pm R_{K}^{*}V_{\omega}^{q},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{d}\\
\epsilon_{\bar{d}}\end{pmatrix}=\Omega_{q}^{*}\pm R_{K}^{*}V_{\omega}^{q},

(36)

and
\epsilon_{s}=\epsilon_{\bar{s}}=\Omega_{s}.

Then, the excitation energy of
K
and
\bar{K}
mesons with zero momenta are given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}E_{K^{+}}\\
E_{K^{-}}\end{pmatrix}=m_{K}^{*}\pm V_{\omega},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}E_{K^{0}}\\
E_{\bar{K}^{0}}\end{pmatrix}=m_{K}^{*}\pm V_{\omega}.

(37)

In a constant vector field, the Lorentz invariance of the dispersion relation for the
K
and
\bar{K}
mesons moving with momentum
\bm{q}
are given by
Brown et al. 1992
;
Tsushima et al. 1998

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}E_{K^{+}}(\bm{q})\\
E_{K^{-}}(\bm{q})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{m_{K}^{*2}+\bm{q}^{2}}\pm V_{\omega},

(38)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}E_{K^{0}}(\bm{q})\\
E_{\bar{K}^{0}}(\bm{q})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{m_{K}^{*2}+\bm{q}^{2}}\pm V_{\omega},

(39)

which is useful for the calculation of the in-medium
K\bar{K}
loop.
Here, the background nuclear matter is treated as static, uniform, and at rest.
In such a medium the vector field is generated by nucleons that are, on average, at rest, so that the expectation value of the vector mean field remains purely time-like.

The in-medium kaon mass is then calculated as

m_{K}^{*}=\sum_{j=q,\bar{s}}\frac{n_{j}\Omega_{j}^{*}-z_{K}}{R_{K}^{*}}+\frac{4\pi}{3}R_{K}^{*3}B,

(40)

where
z_{K}
accounts for the center-of-mass and gluon fluctuation corrections, and
B
is the bag constant.
The in-medium mass is obtained by imposing the stability condition

\frac{dm_{K}^{*}}{dR_{K}^{*}}=0.

(41)

Here, the quark eigenenergies are

\displaystyle\Omega_{q}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\bar{q}}^{*}=\sqrt{x_{q}^{2}+(R_{K}^{*}m_{q}^{*})^{2}},

(42)

\displaystyle\Omega_{s}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\bar{s}}=\sqrt{x_{s}^{2}+(R_{K}^{*}m_{s}^{*})^{2}},

(43)

where
x_{q,s}
are the lowest bag eigenfrequencies, and
n_{q}(n_{\bar{q}})
denote the numbers of quarks (antiquarks) of flavors
q
and
s
. After a self-consistent calculation, the in-medium kaon mass can be parameterized as

m_{K}^{*}\equiv m_{K}-V_{\sigma}.

(44)

We note that this kaon mass remains unchanged in the moving kaon frame due to its scalar nature.

III
Regularization Schemes

In this work, we consider two different regularization schemes to evaluate the
\phi
-meson self-energy.
We do not consider the three-momentum form factor employed for the zero-momentum case in the previous work of Ref.
Cobos-Martínez et al. 2017
, because this procedure explicitly breaks Lorentz
covariance, and is hence not suitable for the finite-momentum case, which is the focus of the present study.
To overcome this limitation, we employ a covariant form factor as well as dimensional regularization
Ko et al. 1992
.
Using two different schemes allows us to check the scheme dependence and assess the robustness of our results.

III.1
Form factor regularization

We first consider a covariant form factor with a multipole Ansatz to regulate the self-energy of the
\phi
meson shown in Fig.
1
. The total self-energy is given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{\lambda}(p)=

\displaystyle~2ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm loop}^{\lambda*}}{D_{K}^{*}D_{\bar{K}}^{*}}+\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm con}^{\lambda*}}{D_{K}^{*}}\right)F(q,\Lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2ig_{\phi}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}-m_{K}^{*2})^{2}

\displaystyle\times\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm loop}^{\lambda*}}{D_{\Lambda}^{2}D_{K}^{*}D_{\bar{K}}^{*}}+\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm con}^{\lambda*}}{D_{\Lambda}^{2}D_{K}^{*}}\right),

(45)

with
D_{\Lambda}=q^{2}-\Lambda^{2}+i\epsilon
.
The regulator is chosen as

\displaystyle F(q,\Lambda)=\frac{(\Lambda^{2}-m_{K}^{*2})^{2}}{(q^{2}-\Lambda^{2}+i\epsilon)^{2}},

(46)

where the cutoff parameter
\Lambda
is assumed to be independent of the nuclear density.
As mentioned before, the total contribution can be decomposed into loop and contact terms,

\Pi^{\lambda}_{\rm total}=\Pi^{\lambda}_{\rm loop}+\Pi^{\lambda}_{\rm con}
.

The operators appearing in the numerator, defined in Eq. (
17
), are evaluated as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\rm loop}^{T*}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{2}P_{\mu\nu}^{T}(2q-p)^{\mu}(2q-p)^{\nu}=2\bm{q}_{\perp}^{2},

(47)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\rm loop}^{L*}=

\displaystyle~P_{\mu\nu}^{L}(2q-p)^{\mu}(2q-p)^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{4}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}([q^{0}-V_{\omega}]|\bm{p}|-q^{z}E_{\phi}^{*})^{2},

(48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\rm con}^{L(T)*}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{2}P_{\mu\nu}^{T}(-2g^{\mu\nu})=P_{\mu\nu}^{L}(-2g^{\mu\nu})=2,

(49)

where a variable shift of
q^{0}
, given in Eq. (
23
), has been performed.
The operators for the transverse and longitudinal modes differ in structure.
For the transverse mode, the operator does not depend on the
\phi
-meson three-momentum
|\bm{p}|
, whereas for the longitudinal mode it exhibits an explicit momentum dependence, together with the vector mean field
V_{\omega}
.
The absence of momentum dependence in the transverse operator follows from
\bm{p}_{\perp}=0
, such that only the longitudinal momentum component is nonvanishing.
By contrast, the operator for the contact term is identical for both longitudinal and transverse projectors.

After introducing the Feynman parameterization, Wick rotation, and Euclidean integration, the total self-energies for the transverse and longitudinal modes are given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{T}(p)=

\displaystyle~N\int_{0}^{1}dx\Biggl[\int_{0}^{1-x}dy~\frac{(1-x-y)}{C}-\frac{x}{E}\Biggr],

(50)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{L}(p)=

\displaystyle~N\int_{0}^{1}dx\Biggl[\int_{0}^{1-x}dy~(1-x-y)

\displaystyle~\times\left(\frac{1}{C}-\frac{2|\bm{p}|^{2}V_{\omega}^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\frac{1}{C^{2}}\right)-\frac{x}{E}\Biggr],

(51)

where
N={g_{\phi}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}-m_{K}^{*2})^{2}}/{4\pi^{2}}
, with the denominator factors given by

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle~x(1-x)m_{\phi}^{*2}-(x+y)m_{K}^{*2}-(1-x-y)\Lambda^{2},

(52)

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle~x\Lambda^{2}-(1-x)m_{K}^{*2}.

(53)

Note that the contact term corresponds to the contribution proportional to
E
, while the loop term involves
C
.
To compute the real part, the integrals are evaluated using the principal-value calculation, where the numerical implementation avoids singularities by smearing, for example through a Breit–Wigner form,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\to\frac{C}{C^{2}+\Gamma^{2}},\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{1}{C^{2}}\to\frac{C^{2}-\Gamma^{2}}{(C^{2}+\Gamma^{2})^{2}},

(54)

where
\Gamma
is a small regulator parameter.
An explicit analytical calculation is also possible.
The details of calculation and analytical expressions for these integrals are provided in App.
A
.

While the real part depends on the regulator
\Lambda
, the imaginary part is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}~\Pi^{T}_{\mathrm{total}}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{24\pi}m_{\phi}^{*2}\beta^{3},

(55)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}~\Pi^{L}_{\mathrm{total}}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{24\pi}m_{\phi}^{*2}\beta^{3}\left(1+\frac{12}{\beta^{2}}\frac{V_{\omega}^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*4}}\right),

(56)

with
\beta=\sqrt{1-4m_{K}^{*2}/m_{\phi}^{*2}}
.
The imaginary part arises solely from the loop diagram, whereas the contact term contributes only to the real part. Putting into Eq. (
28
), the decay width can be explicitly expressed by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{*T}_{\phi}=

\displaystyle~\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{24\pi\gamma}m_{\phi}^{*}\beta^{3},

(57)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{*L}_{\phi}=

\displaystyle~\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{24\pi\gamma}m_{\phi}^{*}\beta^{3}\left(1+\frac{12}{\beta^{2}}\frac{V_{\omega}^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*4}}\right),

(58)

where the
\gamma
factor has been included.

III.2
Dimensional Regularization

We now turn to dimensional regularization, which was used for instance in Ref.
Ko et al. 1992
.
The
\phi
-meson self-energy can be written as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{\lambda}(p)=

\displaystyle~2ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm loop}^{\lambda*}}{D_{K}^{*}D_{\bar{K}}^{*}}+\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm con}^{\lambda*}}{D_{K}^{*}}\right),

(59)

without introducing an explicit form factor.

For the contact term, the self-energy is identical for transverse and longitudinal polarizations and is given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{con}}^{\lambda}(p)=4ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{1}{D_{K}^{*}},

(60)

where we have used
\mathcal{O}_{\rm con}^{\lambda*}=2
, as in Eq. (
49
).
After applying the Feynman parameterization and momentum shift to complete the square, the self-energy for the loop contribution can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Pi^{\lambda}_{\rm loop}(p)=2ig_{\phi}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{{\mathcal{O}}^{\lambda*}_{\rm loop}}{D_{\Delta}^{*2}},

(61)

where
D_{\Delta}^{*}={q}^{2}-\Delta^{*}+i\epsilon
and
\Delta^{*}=m_{K}^{*2}-x(1-x)m_{\phi}^{*2}
.
The explicit forms of the operators for the transverse and longitudinal modes are the same as in Eqs. (
47
) and (
48
), respectively.

Including both the loop and contact contributions, the transverse self-energy can be written as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{T}(p)=

\displaystyle~4ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{d}{q}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{P_{\mu\nu}^{T}{q}^{\mu}{q}^{\nu}}{D_{\Delta}^{*2}}+\frac{1}{D_{K}^{*}}\biggr),

(62)

where, in
d
dimensions, we have used the identity
\bm{q}_{\perp}^{2}=P_{\mu\nu}^{T}q^{\mu}q^{\nu}
.
The momentum integrals are evaluated using dimensional regularization in
d=4-\epsilon
dimensions.
Keeping only finite terms, the renormalized transverse self-energy reads

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{T}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\biggr(\Delta^{*}\ln\frac{\Delta^{*}}{\mu^{2}}-m_{K}^{*2}\ln\frac{m_{K}^{*2}}{\mu^{2}}

\displaystyle-a(\mu)\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{6}\biggr),

(63)

where the first two terms arise from the loop and contact contributions, respectively.
Here
\mu
denotes the renormalization scale, and
a(\mu)
is a dimensionless subtraction constant that absorbs the ultraviolet divergence.
The factor
m_{\phi}^{2}/6
originates from the combined loop and contact contributions.

For the longitudinal mode, the loop contribution is given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{loop}}^{L}(p)=

\displaystyle\frac{8ig_{\phi}^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int\frac{d^{4}{q}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\left([{q}^{0}-V_{\omega}]|\bm{p}|-{q}^{z}E_{\phi}^{*}\right)^{2}}{D_{\Delta}^{*2}}.

(64)

By adding the contact contributions and following procedures similar to those used for the transverse mode, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{L}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\Biggl[\Delta^{*}\ln\frac{\Delta^{*}}{\mu^{2}}-m_{K}^{*2}\ln\frac{m_{K}^{*2}}{\mu^{2}}

\displaystyle~-2V_{\omega}^{2}\frac{|\bm{p}|^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\left(\ln\frac{\Delta^{*}}{\mu^{2}}+1\right)-a(\mu)\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{6}\Biggr].

(65)

The details of calculations and analytic expressions are provided in App.
B
.

While the real part depends on the renormalization scale
\mu
and the subtraction constant
a(\mu)
, the imaginary parts of the transverse and longitudinal self-energies are again independent of the regularization scheme and are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}~\Pi^{T}_{\mathrm{total}}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{24\pi}m_{\phi}^{*2}\beta^{3},

(66)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}~\Pi^{L}_{\mathrm{total}}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{24\pi}m_{\phi}^{*2}\beta^{3}\left(1+\frac{12}{\beta^{2}}\frac{V_{\omega}^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*4}}\right).

(67)

These expressions agree with those obtained using the covariant form-factor regularization.

V.2
In-medium mass at finite momentum

We now examine the momentum dependence of the in-medium
\phi
-meson mass for both polarization modes, which become distinct in nuclear matter.
Figure
4
shows the density dependence of the longitudinal-mode mass at fixed momenta
p=0,1,2,
and
3~\mathrm{GeV}
. Results obtained with two different regularization schemes display qualitatively similar behavior, although the cutoff dependence becomes more pronounced at finite momentum, as shown by the band, indicating enhanced sensitivity to ultraviolet contributions.

Figure
5
illustrates the momentum dependence of the in-medium mass shift at normal nuclear density. A clear difference between the two modes is observed: while the longitudinal-mode mass decreases quadratically with increasing momentum
\bm{p}
, the transverse-mode mass remains constant, showing no noticeable momentum dependence relative to its value at rest.
In the present approach, the transverse mode does not couple to the vector mean field at leading order [see Eq. (
47
)]. The longitudinal mode, however, couples through derivative-type interactions [Eq. (
48
)], which naturally generate a momentum-dependent contribution to the self-energy.
As the
\phi
meson momentum increases, this coupling strengthens the medium effect, leading to an enhanced separation between longitudinal and transverse modes. In vacuum, where the vector mean field vanishes, both polarizations remain degenerate for all momenta.

For comparison, this momentum dependence of the longitudinal-mode mass is qualitatively consistent with the QCDSR analysis of Ref.
Kim and Gubler 2020
. However, our results for the transverse mode deviate from the QCDSR prediction, which suggests an approximately quadratic increase in mass. In QCDSR, this mode separation originates from the finite density behavior of Lorentz violating condensates. Specifically, as shown in Fig. 3 of Ref.
Kim and Gubler 2020
, the longitudinal mass is dominated by a decreasing trend, whereas the transverse mass involves a competition between quark and gluon condensates. Although the net result in QCDSR is an increasing mass, this prediction is subject to larger uncertainties than the longitudinal one.

V.3
In-medium width at finite momentum

We next discuss the behavior of the in-medium decay width. Figure
6
shows the density dependence of the longitudinal-mode decay width at finite momenta, calculated using two different regularization schemes, without the Lorentz
\gamma
factor.
Both schemes yield qualitatively consistent results, indicating that the predicted in-medium broadening is robust against the choice of ultraviolet regularization, although quantitatively the results depend on the details of the cutoff and scale parameters.

At rest, the width for both modes increases significantly with increasing density.
For finite momenta, although the longitudinal width continues to increase with density, its slope gradually decreases as the
\phi
-meson momentum increases.
This behavior is primarily driven by the momentum-dependent modification of the longitudinal-mode
\phi
-meson mass, which reduces the available decay phase space at higher momenta.
In contrast, the explicit
\bm{p}^{2}
-dependent corrections in Eq. (
58
) contribute only minor effects in this case.
The transverse-mode width, on the other hand, remains essentially identical to its value at rest, consistent with the negligible momentum dependence of the transverse mass.

Figure
7
presents the corresponding results including the Lorentz
\gamma
factor. In vacuum, both the transverse and longitudinal decay widths decrease with increasing momentum due to time dilation. Consequently, even in the absence of medium effects, the decay widths at finite momentum are reduced compared to their rest-frame values. In the nuclear medium, the competition between intrinsic in-medium broadening and Lorentz time dilation leads to a nontrivial momentum dependence of the decay widths. As a result, at finite momenta, the in-medium transverse decay width also differs from its rest-frame value, despite the absence of intrinsic momentum dependence in the transverse self-energy. The separation between longitudinal and transverse widths becomes increasingly pronounced at higher densities and momenta, reflecting the polarization-dependent nature of the medium modifications.

V.4
Unpolarized spectral functions

Finally, we examine the unpolarized spectral functions of the
\phi
meson at normal nuclear density
\rho_{0}
, shown in Fig.
8
. The gray dotted lines represent the vacuum spectral functions, while the blue lines show the in-medium results.
For demonstration, we present the results obtained using dimensional regularization.

At
|\bm{p}|=0
, the longitudinal and transverse spectral functions are identical. The nuclear medium induces substantial broadening and a moderate downward mass shift, resulting in a significant redistribution of spectral strength toward lower energies.
At finite momenta,
|\bm{p}|=1
and
2~\mathrm{GeV}
, the spectral functions of the longitudinal and transverse modes start to split due to polarization-dependent medium effects.
However, because the two modes still strongly overlap, the resulting unpolarized spectral function exhibits a single broad peak.
Starting with momenta around
|\bm{p}|=2~\mathrm{GeV}
, a small two-peak structure begins to emerge.

For higher momentum, the widths of the transverse and longitudinal modes become clearly different, as shown in Fig.
7
. In the unpolarized spectral function, the transverse contribution appears with a factor of two.
However, because the longitudinal mode has a narrower width, its peak height becomes comparable to that of the transverse mode despite the larger transverse weight. As the momentum increases further, the separation between the two modes becomes more pronounced. At
|\bm{p}|=3~\mathrm{GeV}
, the longitudinal and transverse peaks are well separated, producing a clear double-peak structure in the spectral function.

Compared to the previous QCDSR analysis
Kim and Gubler 2020
, an important difference emerges. In that work, the transverse mass increases quadratically with momentum, leading to peak separation already at
|\bm{p}|=1.5
-
2.0~\mathrm{GeV}

Kim and Gubler 2020
. In contrast, in our model the transverse mode remains unchanged, and a well-separated double-peak structure only appears at around
|\bm{p}|=2.0
-
3.0~\mathrm{GeV}
.
In experiments, however, the situation is more subtle, because the medium effect weakens at higher momenta, as the
\phi
meson tends to decay outside the nucleus. It remains to be seen whether a suitable momentum window exists for observing separated peaks in the spectral function.

Furthermore, the E325 experiment observed finite-density effects only for dileptons with
\beta\gamma=|\bm{p}|/m_{\phi}\lesssim 1.25

Muto et al. 2007
;
Ichikawa et al. 2025
. In this kinematic region, our results indicate that the longitudinal and transverse peaks remain strongly overlapping, implying that only a single broadened peak should be observed.
Although the longitudinal and transverse mass peaks strongly overlap for
|\bm{p}|\lesssim 2~\mathrm{GeV}
, measurements of the angular distributions in the
\phi\to K\bar{K}
decay channel
Park et al. 2023
in the future J-PARC E88/Sa
\phi
re experiment
Sako et al. 2024
may allow the two modes to be disentangled and their respective masses to be extracted. Observation of such polarization-dependent mass splitting in nuclear matter would constitute a clear signal of Lorentz-symmetry violation induced by the medium.

VI
Conclusion and Outlook

In this work, we studied the in-medium properties of the
\phi
meson in the quark–meson coupling (QMC) model
Tsushima et al. 1998
, incorporating self-consistent kaon mass modifications and kaon-loop contributions
Cobos-Martínez et al. 2017
.
We explored the density and momentum dependence of the
\phi
meson mass, width, and spectral functions, emphasizing polarization effects and assessing model dependence using covariant form factor and dimensional regularization schemes.

At normal nuclear density, the kaon effective mass decreases by about 13%
Tsushima et al. 1998
, inducing a moderate downward mass shift of the
\phi
meson by 2-4% and an enhancement of its decay width by a factor of 7-8.
The qualitative results are robust against variations in the regularization scheme and consistent with previous studies
Ko et al. 1992
;
Cobos-Martínez et al. 2017
.
For a more rigorous description, additional effects, such as coupled channels, should be included
Cabrera et al. 2017
.

At finite momentum, a pronounced polarization dependence emerges. While the transverse mode remains insensitive to momentum, the longitudinal mode exhibits a quadratic decrease of the in-medium mass, driven by vector mean fields and derivative interactions, leading to growing polarization splitting with increasing momentum and density.
The decay width also shows interesting behavior, with a nontrivial momentum dependence arising from the interplay of intrinsic medium broadening and Lorentz time dilation, producing significant polarization splitting and, at high momentum, a characteristic double-peak structure in the spectral function
Kim and Gubler 2020
.

Finally, our results can be further tested in future experiments at J-PARC. In the kinematic regime of current measurements, such as KEK E325, the polarization modes overlap, producing a single broadened peak. At higher momenta, however, our model predicts a substantial polarization splitting, which is partially different from that obtained
in a QCDSR approach
Kim and Gubler 2020
. Future measurements at J-PARC, such as E88/Sa
\phi
re, will help to disentangle this model dependence through angular correlations in
the
\phi\to K\bar{K}
channel.
Moreover, similar in-medium polarization splitting has also been proposed for the
K_{1}^{\pm}
meson
Park et al. 2024
;
Yeo et al. 2024
. Observation of these effects would provide direct evidence of Lorentz-symmetry breaking in nuclear matter.

the self-energy becomes

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{loop}}^{T}(p)=N\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1-x}dy~\frac{(1-x-y)}{C},

(72)

with
N={g_{\phi}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}-m_{K}^{*2})^{2}}/{4\pi^{2}}
.
For the longitudinal mode, the self-energy reads

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{loop}}^{L}(p)=

\displaystyle\frac{48ig_{\phi}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}-m_{K}^{*2})^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1-x}dy~(1-x-y)

\displaystyle\times\left[\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\left([q^{0}-V_{\omega}]|\bm{p}|-q^{z}E_{\phi}^{*}\right)^{2}}{({q}^{2}+C)^{4}}\right].

(73)

Following the same procedure, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{loop}}^{L}(p)=

\displaystyle~N\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1-x}dy~(1-x-y)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{C}-\frac{2|\bm{p}|^{2}V_{\omega}^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\frac{1}{C^{2}}\right).

(74)

Here, a quadratic dependence on the
\phi
-meson momentum appears, originating from the derivative coupling in the
\phi KK
interaction.
We emphasize that the second term vanishes both in the vacuum
(V_{\omega}=0)
and in the rest frame
(\bm{p}=0)
.
In these limits, the longitudinal and transverse self-energies coincide, ensuring the correct limiting behavior.

Next, we consider the contribution from the contact diagram,
\Pi^{\lambda}_{\rm con}
, which is given by

\displaystyle\Pi^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{con}}(p)=

\displaystyle~4ig_{\phi}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}-m_{K}^{*2})^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{1}{D_{\Lambda}^{2}D_{K}^{*}}.

(75)

This contribution is identical for both transverse and longitudinal polarizations.
The propagators can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D_{\Lambda}^{2}D_{K}^{*}}=

\displaystyle~2\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\frac{x}{\left[xD_{\Lambda}+(1-x)D_{K}^{*}\right]^{3}}

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\frac{x}{\left(q^{2}+E\right)^{3}},

(77)

with
E=x\Lambda^{2}-(1-x)m_{K}^{*2}
.
The self-energy is then given by

\displaystyle\Pi^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{con}}(p)=8ig_{\phi}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}-m_{K}^{*2})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{x}{\left(q^{2}+E\right)^{3}}.

(78)

After Feynman parameterization and Wick rotation, the contact contribution becomes

\displaystyle\Pi^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{con}}(p)=-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}-m_{K}^{*2})^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int dx\frac{x}{E}.

(79)

A.1
Analytical expressions

Here, we provide the analytic expressions for the form factor regularization.

Contact term

First of all, the self-energy of the contact term in Eqs. (
51
) and (
50
) is computed as

\displaystyle\Pi^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{con}}(p)=-N\int\frac{xdx}{x\Lambda^{2}-(1-x)m_{K}^{*2}},

(80)

where the analytic result is given by

\displaystyle\Pi^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{con}}(p)=-\frac{N}{(\Lambda^{2}+m_{K}^{*2})}\left[1+\frac{m_{K}^{*2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{K}^{*2}}\ln\!\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{K}^{*2}}\right)\right].

(81)

\bm{p}
-independent loop term

The self-energy of the loop term common to the longitudinal and transverse modes in Eqs. (
51
) and (
50
)
for the
\bm{p}
-independent part is given by

\displaystyle\Pi^{T/L}_{\mathrm{loop}}(p)=N\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1-x}dy~\frac{(1-x-y)}{C}.

(82)

In the region of
2m_{K}^{*}<m_{\phi}^{*}<\Lambda+m_{K}^{*}
, we obtain the real part of the self-energy analytically as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}~\Pi^{T/L}_{\mathrm{loop}}(p)=

\displaystyle~\frac{N}{12m_{\phi}^{2}\delta^{2}}\biggl[\Theta_{1}+2\beta^{3}\ln\left(\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle~+\gamma\Theta_{2}\left\{\tan^{-1}\left(\tfrac{\delta_{+}}{\gamma}\right)+\tan^{-1}\left(\tfrac{\delta_{-}}{\gamma}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle~+\Theta_{3}\ln\frac{\delta_{K}}{\delta_{L}}\biggr],

(83)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\delta_{K}=

\displaystyle~{m_{K}^{*2}}/{m_{\phi}^{*2}},\qquad\delta=\delta_{K}-\delta_{L},

(84)

\displaystyle\ \delta_{L}=

\displaystyle~{\Lambda^{2}}/{m_{\phi}^{*2}},\qquad\delta_{\pm}=~1\pm\delta,

(85)

and

\displaystyle\beta=

\displaystyle~\sqrt{1-4\delta_{K}},

(86)

\displaystyle\gamma=

\displaystyle~\sqrt{-1+2(\delta_{K}+\delta_{L})-(\delta_{K}-\delta_{L})^{2}}.

(87)

To simplify further, we have also defined

\displaystyle\Theta_{1}=

\displaystyle~2\delta[1-2\delta],

(88)

\displaystyle\Theta_{2}=

\displaystyle~2[2\delta^{2}+5\delta_{K}-\delta_{L}-1],

(89)

\displaystyle\Theta_{3}=

\displaystyle~[2\delta^{3}+3\delta^{2}-6\delta_{K}+1].

(90)

In the same region of
2m_{K}^{*}<m_{\phi}^{*}<\Lambda+m_{K}^{*}
, the imaginary part is obtained as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}~\Pi^{L/T}_{\mathrm{loop}}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{24\pi}m_{\phi}^{*2}\beta^{3}.

(91)

\bm{p}
-dependent loop term

Now, we compute the second integral in Eq. (
51
), that is related to the
\bm{p}
-dependent part for the longitudinal mode, defined as
\Pi^{L2}_{\mathrm{loop}}
.
The self-energy is given by

\displaystyle\Pi^{L2}_{\mathrm{loop}}(p)=-N_{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{\,1-x}dy\;\frac{(1-x-y)}{C^{2}},

(92)

with
N_{2}={2NV_{\omega}^{2}}/{m_{\phi}^{*2}}
.

In the region of
2m_{K}^{*}<m_{\phi}^{*}<\Lambda+m_{K}^{*}
, the real part is analytically given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\,\Pi^{L2}_{\mathrm{loop}}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{N_{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*4}\delta^{2}}\Biggl[-\beta\ln\left(\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\delta_{K}}{\delta_{L}}

\displaystyle~-\frac{\Theta_{4}}{\gamma}\Bigl\{\tan^{-1}\left(\tfrac{\delta_{+}}{\gamma}\right)+\tan^{-1}\left(\tfrac{\delta_{-}}{\gamma}\right)\Bigr\}\Biggr],

(93)

with
\Theta_{4}=1-3\delta_{K}-\delta_{L}
.
In the same region, the imaginary part is obtained as

\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi^{L2}_{\mathrm{loop}}(p)=-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{2\pi}\frac{\beta V_{\omega}^{2}}{{m_{\phi}^{*2}}}|\bm{p}|^{2}.

(94)

Appendix B
Dimensional regularization

In this approach, the
\phi
-meson self-energy can be written as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{\lambda}(p)=

\displaystyle~2ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left[\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm loop}^{\lambda*}}{D_{K}^{*}D_{\bar{K}}^{*}}+\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm con}^{\lambda*}}{D_{K}^{*}}\right],

(95)

without the need of a form factor.
For the loop contribution, we introduce Feynman parameters,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D_{K}^{*}D_{\bar{K}}^{*}}=\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{1}{\left[xD_{K}^{*}+(1-x)D_{\bar{K}}^{*}\right]^{2}},

(96)

and shift the loop momentum
q^{\mu}\to q^{\mu}+(1-x)p^{\mu}
, which leads to

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{loop}}^{\lambda}(p)=2ig_{\phi}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\rm loop}^{\lambda*}}{(q^{2}-\Delta^{*}+i\epsilon)^{2}},

(97)

where
\Delta^{*}=m_{K}^{*2}-x(1-x)m_{\phi}^{*2}
.
For the contact term, the Feynman parameter is not needed.

B.1
Transverse mode

Including both the loop and contact contributions, the transverse self-energy can be written as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{T}(p)=

\displaystyle~4ig_{\phi}^{2}\int\frac{d^{d}{q}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\biggl[\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{P_{\mu\nu}^{T}{q}^{\mu}{q}^{\nu}}{D_{\Delta}^{*2}}+\frac{1}{D_{K}^{*}}\biggr],

(98)

where, working in
d
dimensions, we have used the identity
\bm{q}_{\perp}^{2}=P_{\mu\nu}^{T}q^{\mu}q^{\nu}
.
The momentum integrals are evaluated using dimensional regularization in
d=4-\epsilon
dimensions.
Using the standard integral relations

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{d}q}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{(q^{2}-\Delta)^{2}}=

\displaystyle~\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{2(4\pi)^{d/2}}\,\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{d}{2}\right)\,\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}-1},

(99)

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{d}q}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{1}{q^{2}-\Delta}=

\displaystyle~\frac{-i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\Gamma\left(1-\frac{d}{2}\right)\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}-1},

(100)

the contact and loop contributions to the transverse self-energy become

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{con}}^{T}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{4g_{\phi}^{2}}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\Gamma\left(1-\frac{d}{2}\right)(m_{K}^{*2})^{\frac{d}{2}-1}.

(101)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{loop}}^{T}(p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4g_{\phi}^{2}\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{d}{2}\right)}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\,\left[\int_{0}^{1}dx\,(\Delta^{*})^{\frac{d}{2}-1}-(m_{K}^{*2})^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\right],

(102)

where we have used
g^{\mu\nu}P_{\mu\nu}^{T}=-2
.
Expanding around
d=4-\epsilon
, we use
(4\pi)^{-d/2}=(4\pi)^{-2}(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\ln 4\pi),

\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{d}{2}\right)=-\frac{2}{\epsilon}-1+\gamma_{E}
and

\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}-1}=\Delta\left[1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\ln\Delta\right].

Adding the contact term, the renormalized transverse self-energy reads

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{loop}}^{T}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\biggr(\Delta^{*}\ln\frac{\Delta^{*}}{\mu^{2}}-m_{K}^{*2}\ln\frac{m_{K}^{*2}}{\mu^{2}}

\displaystyle-a(\mu)\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{6}\biggr),

(103)

where the first two terms arise from the loop and contact contributions, respectively.
The real part of the transverse self-energy can be evaluated analytically as

\displaystyle\text{Re}~\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{T}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\biggl[-\frac{m_{\phi}^{*2}}{6}\biggl(\beta^{3}\ln\left(\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\right)+\frac{8m_{K}^{*2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}

\displaystyle~+\ln\frac{m_{K}^{*2}}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{5}{18}\biggr)-a(\mu)\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{6}\biggr]

(104)

with
\beta=\sqrt{1-4m_{K}^{*2}/m_{\phi}^{*2}}
.
Note that in Ref.
Ko et al. 1992
, the constant
5/18
was absorbed into
a(\mu)
.
However, we retain it here for consistency with the numerical integration of the first term in Eq. (
103
).

B.2
Longitudinal mode

For the longitudinal mode, the loop contribution is given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{loop}}^{L}(p)=

\displaystyle\frac{8ig_{\phi}^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int\frac{d^{4}{q}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\left([{q}^{0}-V_{\omega}]|\bm{p}|-{q}^{z}E_{\phi}^{*}\right)^{2}}{({q}^{2}-\Delta^{*})^{2}}.

(105)

Since the cross term vanishes by symmetry, the expression reduces to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}^{L}_{\mathrm{loop}}=\frac{4}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\biggl(({q}^{z})^{2}E_{\phi}^{*2}+({q}^{0})^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}+|\bm{p}|^{2}V_{\omega}^{2}\biggr).

(106)

After performing the Wick rotation and extending the integration to
d
dimensions, we use

\displaystyle(q^{z})^{2}=\frac{1}{d}{q}^{2}_{E},\hskip 28.45274pt(q^{0})^{2}=-\frac{1}{d}{q}^{2}_{E},

(107)

where
q_{E}
denotes the Euclidean four-momentum. This yields

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}^{L}_{\mathrm{loop}}=\frac{m_{\phi}^{*2}}{d}{q}^{2}_{E}+|\bm{p}|^{2}V_{\omega}^{2}.

(108)

The longitudinal loop contribution then becomes

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{loop}}^{L}(p)=-\frac{8g_{\phi}^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int\frac{d^{d}{q}_{E}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{d}{q}^{2}_{E}+|\bm{p}|^{2}V_{\omega}^{2}}{({q}^{2}_{E}+\Delta^{*})^{2}}.

(109)

Using the standard Euclidean integrals

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{d}q_{E}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{1}{(q^{2}_{E}+\Delta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\Gamma\left(2-\frac{d}{2}\right)\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}-2},

(110)

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{d}q_{E}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{q^{2}_{E}}{(q^{2}_{E}+\Delta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{d}{2}\Gamma\left(1-\frac{d}{2}\right)\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}-1}.

(111)

and combining the loop and contact contributions, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{L}(p)=

\displaystyle~-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\Biggl[\Delta^{*}\ln\frac{\Delta^{*}}{\mu^{2}}-m_{K}^{*2}\ln\frac{m_{K}^{*2}}{\mu^{2}}

\displaystyle~-2V_{\omega}^{2}\frac{|\bm{p}|^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\left(\ln\frac{\Delta^{*}}{\mu^{2}}+1\right)-a(\mu)\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{6}\Biggr].

(112)

Note that the
\bm{p}
-dependent part also exhibits a
\mu
dependence.
The real part can be evaluated analytically as

\displaystyle\text{Re}~\Pi_{\mathrm{total}}^{L}(p)=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{\phi}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\biggl[-\frac{m_{\phi}^{*2}}{6}\biggl(\beta^{3}\ln\biggl(\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\biggr)+\frac{8m_{K}^{*2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}

\displaystyle+\ln\frac{m_{K}^{*2}}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{8}{15}\biggr)-a(\mu)\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{6}

\displaystyle-2V_{\omega}^{2}\frac{|\bm{p}|^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{*2}}\left(\ln\frac{m_{K}^{*2}}{\mu^{2}}-1+\beta\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\right)\biggr].

(113)

For the imaginary part, the results are identical to those obtained using the form-factor regularization, and we therefore do not discuss them further here.
