Dynamic visualization of bidirectional lymphatic flow in the dermal backflow areas of lymphedema using contrast-enhanced ultrasound lymphography
Authors: Takahiro Hirayama, Kiyoka Omoto, Tamami Watanabe, Shinobu Matsubara, Naoto Yamamoto
Published: 2026

Dermal backflow (DBF) is important in the diagnosis and treatment of lymphedema. It is thought to result from impaired lymphatic flow, causing reflux into precollectors and avalvular dermal lymphatics.1 Although many studies have elucidated lymphatic structure and function, they have provided primarily static observations, and DBF formation has generally been understood as lymph reflux.

Perfluorobutane is a microbubble contrast agent with a mean diameter of 2 to 3 μm, which is used for contrast-enhanced ultrasound lymphography (CEUS). Omoto et al2 reported its usefulness for breast sentinel lymph node identification and lymphatic channel visualization. When injected into the superficial subcutaneous layer just beneath the dermis, it is thought to be absorbed into the lymphatic system, analogous to other indirect lymphographic techniques.

CEUS was performed with a Canon Aplio i800 with a high-frequency linear transducer, PLI-1205BX/i18LX5, after indocyanine green lymphography. Perfluorobutane microbubbles were prepared according to the manufacturer’s instructions and injected just beneath the dermis. During CEUS, we specifically evaluated the areas showing DBF on indocyanine green lymphography, with particular attention paid to the site where the DBF first emerged.

Using CEUS, we visualized a lymphatic network extending from a collecting lymphatic vessel to the precollecting and superficial dermal lymphatics involved in DBF formation. Within these lymphatics, the microbubbles demonstrated bidirectional movement, including antegrade (A; Supplementary Video 1, online only) and retrograde flow (B; Supplementary Video 2, online only). These to-and-fro movements were observed sequentially (C; Supplementary Video 3, online only), and the microbubble movement within the channel was multidirectional (D/Cover; Supplementary Video 4, online only) in terms of time (D, left) and space (D, right).

These findings suggest that DBF formation in lymphedema involves not only lymphatic reflux but also alternating antegrade and retrograde lymph flows.

Part of this work was presented at the 38th Annual Meeting of the American Venous Forum (AVF2026) in Denver, Colorado, February 28 to March 4, 2026. The present study represents further developments.
