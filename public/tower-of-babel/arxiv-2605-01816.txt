Optimal transport between laws of random probability measures and the strict Monge problem
Alessandro Pinzi

Abstract
We consider an optimal transport problem between laws of random probability measures: given a base cost function, we build the associated OT cost between probability measures that in turn we use to define the OT cost between probability measures over probability measures. This setting admits a finer reformulation in terms of laws of random couplings, which retain more information than ordinary couplings. One of the main contributions of the paper is the characterization of the optimal ones in terms of Kantorovich potentials. Similarly, we also introduce the strict Monge problem, whose admissible competitors are more restrictive than in the usual Monge formulation. In this setting, we will give sufficient conditions under which the value of this problem is the same as the one considered above, in the spirit of the result by A. Pratelli. Then, for p > 1 p>1 , when the underlying cost is the distance to the power p p in a strictly convex Banach space, we will give sufficient conditions under which the optimal random coupling is unique and induced by a solution of the strict Monge problem, resembling the Brenier theorem.



Keywords:
optimal transport, random measures, Lions’ lifting, Monge problem.

Introduction

In this paper, we study an Optimal Transport problem between laws of random probability measures in an iterated fashion: given a cost function
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits\colon\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}\to[0,+\infty]
, we associate to it the optimal transport cost
\mathsf{C}\colon\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})\to[0,+\infty]
defined as

\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\min\left\{\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})d\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\ :\ \pi\in\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\right\},

(1.1)

and we use it again to define an OT cost
{\mathcal{C}}\colon\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}))\times\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2}))\to[0,+\infty]
as

{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2}):=\min\left\{\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})}\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})d\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\ :\ \Pi\in\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})\right\}.

(1.2)

We will mainly work in the setting in which
\mathrm{X}_{1}
and
\mathrm{X}_{2}
are Polish spaces and
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits
is lower semicontinuous, sometimes treating separately the case in which
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits
is continuous. With
\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
we denote the set of transport couplings, see also Section
2.2
. Note that we will always write that the minimum is attained even in the case in which the value is
+\infty
, taking care in saying when it is finitely valued.

Clearly, the general theory already gives a characterization of the optimal transport plans in terms of the optimal dual potentials. However, in this setting (
1.2
) can be reformulated as

{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})=\min\left\{\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})}\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})d\pi(x_{1},x_{2})d{\mathtt{P}}(\pi)\ :\ {\mathtt{P}}\in\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})\right\},

(1.3)

where
\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
is the set of
(laws of) random couplings
, that are measures
{\mathtt{P}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}))
such that the double push-forward of the projections
\operatorname{pr}^{i}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}\to\mathrm{X}_{i}
, for
i=1,2
, returns the given random measures:

\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp\sharp}{\mathtt{P}}={\mathtt{M}}_{1},\quad\operatorname{pr}^{2}_{\sharp\sharp}{\mathtt{P}}={\mathtt{M}}_{2}.

Example 1.1
.

A natural example of this construction is the Wasserstein-on-Wasserstein distance: let
(\mathrm{X},{\mathrm{d}})
be a complete and separable metric space. In the previous setting, consider
\mathrm{X}_{1}=\mathrm{X}_{2}=\mathrm{X}
and
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})={\mathrm{d}}^{p}(x_{1},x_{2})
for a fixed
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Then
\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})={\mathsf{W}}_{p,{\mathrm{d}}}^{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is the
p
-th power of the
L^{p}
-Wasserstein distance over
\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X})
, in the extended sense since we are not yet restricting to measures with finite
p
-moment and the value can be
+\infty
. Then,
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})={\mathcal{W}}_{p,d}^{p}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
is the
p
-th power of the (extended) Wasserstein-on-Wasserstein distance, called also
L^{p}
-Wasserstein distance on random measures. See also
2.3
.

In general, random couplings contain more information than couplings: by a measurable selection argument, we can show that the map

(\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp},\operatorname{pr}^{2}_{\sharp})_{\sharp}\colon\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})\to\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})

(1.4)

is surjective, but in general is not bijective. For example, if
{\mathtt{M}}_{1}=\delta_{\mu_{1}}
and
{\mathtt{M}}_{2}=\delta_{\mu_{2}}
for some
\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})
and
\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})
, then
\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
is a singleton, but
\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
contains
\delta_{\pi}
for all
\pi\in\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
. For more details, see Lemma
3.2
. Thus, in this setting, understanding the structure of optimal random couplings will lead to a better understanding of the problem, rather than simply applying the general theory to (
1.2
).

An example of random couplings are the
fully deterministic
ones, that are induced by maps of the form
{\mathrm{T}}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to\mathrm{X}_{2}
. Indeed, if
{\mathrm{T}}
satisfies
{\mathcal{T}}_{\sharp}{\mathtt{M}}_{1}={\mathtt{M}}_{2}
, with
{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{1}):={\mathrm{T}}(\cdot,\mu_{1})_{\sharp}\mu_{1}
, then

{\mathtt{P}}:=\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})}\delta_{(\operatorname{id},{\mathrm{T}}(\cdot,\mu_{1}))_{\sharp}\mu_{1}}d{\mathtt{M}}_{1}(\mu_{1})\in\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2}).

(1.5)

Our first goal is to characterize optimal random couplings through a suitable extension of
\mathsf{C}
-cyclical monotonicity and
\mathsf{C}
-concavity. Our second goal is to introduce the
strict Monge problem
using fully deterministic competitors, and to understand when it is exact and when optimal random couplings are induced by measurable maps.

Random couplings and Kantorovich potentials.
A crucial turning point in OT theory is
duality
. In the setting of (
1.2
), the standard theory tells us that

{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})}\phi_{1}\,d{\mathtt{M}}_{1}+\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})}\phi_{2}\,d{\mathtt{M}}_{2}\;:\;\phi_{1}(\mu_{1})+\phi_{2}(\mu_{2})\leq{\mathsf{C}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\right\}.

From now on, we impose the following assumption: for some Borel functions
{\mathtt{a}}_{i}\colon\mathrm{X}_{i}\to[0,+\infty]

\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})\leq{\mathtt{a}}_{1}(x_{1})+{\mathtt{a}}_{2}(x_{2})\quad\text{ and }\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i})}\int_{\mathrm{X}_{i}}{\mathtt{a}}_{i}(x_{i})d\mu_{i}(x_{i})d{\mathtt{M}}_{i}(\mu_{i})<+\infty\quad\text{for }i=1,2.

(1.6)

Define also
\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{i}}(\mathrm{X}_{i}):=\{\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i}):{\mathtt{a}}_{i}\in L^{1}(\mathrm{X}_{i},\mu_{i})\}
.
Then,
{\mathsf{C}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})<+\infty
for all
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\in\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}(\mathrm{X}_{2})
and
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})<+\infty
, so that we can apply the general theory for optimal transport: the maximum is attained by a pair of functions
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in L^{1}({\mathtt{M}}_{1})\times L^{1}({\mathtt{M}}_{2})
satisfying that
\phi_{1}
is
{\mathsf{C}}
-concave and
\phi_{2}
is the
{\mathsf{C}}
-transform of
\phi_{1}
.

Example 1.2
.

In the Wasserstein case of Example
1.1
, a natural choice is
{\mathtt{a}}_{1}(x)={\mathtt{a}}_{2}(x):=2^{p-1}{\mathrm{d}}^{p}(x,x_{0})
for some fixed point
x_{0}\in\mathrm{X}
. This choice gives
\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}(\mathrm{X})=\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}(\mathrm{X})=\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathrm{X})
, and (
1.6
) is equivalent to
{\mathtt{M}}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathrm{X}))
. Moreover, in this particular case we have that
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathrm{X}),{\mathsf{W}}_{p,{\mathrm{d}}})
and
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathrm{X})),{\mathcal{W}}_{p,{\mathrm{d}}})
are complete and separable metric spaces (see
2.3
).

At this point, recall that, for couplings
\Pi\in\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
, optimality is characterized by looking closer at the theory of
\mathsf{C}
-concave functions. Indeed, the following are equivalent:

1.

\Pi\in\Gamma_{o,\mathsf{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
;

2.

\Pi
is concentrated on a
\mathsf{C}
-cyclically monotone subset of
\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}(\mathrm{X}_{2})
(see Definition
2.2
);

3.

there exists
\phi_{1}:\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to[-\infty,+\infty)
such that
\Pi
is concentrated on
\partial_{\mathsf{C}}^{+}\phi_{1}
.

In particular, the equivalence between 2. and 3. is given by the celebrated Rockafellar–Ruschendorf theorem, that states that every
\mathsf{C}
-cyclically monotone subset is contained in the
\mathsf{C}
-superdifferential of some
\mathsf{C}
-concave function (see Theorem
2.3
).

One of the main results of this paper (see Theorem
3.9
) is a similar characterization for optimal random couplings. We will introduce the notion of
total

{\mathsf{C}}
-cyclically monotone
subsets
{\mathrm{F}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}\oplus{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})
(see Definition
3.6
) and of
total

{\mathsf{C}}
-superdifferential

\partial_{{\mathtt{t}},{\mathsf{C}}}^{+}\phi_{1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}\oplus{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})
of a function
\phi_{1}\colon\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to[-\infty,+\infty)
(see Definition
3.5
), and we will prove that, given
{\mathtt{P}}\in\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
, the following are equivalent:

1.

{\mathtt{P}}
is optimal;

2.

{\mathtt{P}}
is concentrated on a total
\mathsf{C}
-cyclically monotone set;

3.

there exists
\phi_{1}:\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to[-\infty,+\infty)

\mathsf{C}
-concave such that
{\mathtt{P}}
is concentrated on
\partial^{+}_{{\mathtt{t}},\mathsf{C}}\phi_{1}
.

Moreover, we also have the ‘total’ counterpart of Rockafellar–Ruschendorf theorem, showing that any total
\mathsf{C}
-cyclically monotone subset is contained on the total
\mathsf{C}
-superdifferential of some
\mathsf{C}
-concave function (see Proposition
3.8
).

This result extends
[
PS25a
, Section 4 & 5]
(see also
[
BPS25
]
), where the case
\mathrm{X}_{1}=\mathrm{X}_{2}={\mathrm{H}}
separable Hilbert space, with
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})=|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}
, was treated. However, in this specific setting, the author with G. Savaré proved the result heavily relying on the Lions’ representation
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{\mathbb{M}},{\mathrm{H}})
of
\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathrm{H}})
, while in Section
3
we propose a novel proof that is intrinsic and does not rely on any kind of representations.

Anyway, in Section
4
, we show that a lifting procedure can be performed in this setting as well. This is interesting on its own, and gives also a different proof of the results in Section
3
, that is indeed similar to the one proposed in
[
PS25a
]
.

Lions’ lifting.
Assume that
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}\to[0,+\infty]
is continuous. Let
({\mathrm{Q}},{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{Q}}},\mathbb{M})
be a fixed standard Borel atomless probability space. Define the law maps

\displaystyle\iota_{1}\colon\mathcal{X}_{1}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}),\qquad\quad\iota_{2}\colon\mathcal{X}_{2}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2}),\qquad\quad\iota_{1,2}\colon\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}),\qquad

(1.7)

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto(Z_{1})_{\sharp}\mathbb{M}\hskip 48.93877ptZ_{2}\mapsto(Z_{2})_{\sharp}\mathbb{M}\hskip 55.48277pt(Z_{1},Z_{2})\mapsto(Z_{1},Z_{2})_{\sharp}\mathbb{M}.

where
\mathcal{X}_{i}:=L^{0}\big({\mathrm{Q}},{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{Q}}},\mathbb{M};\mathrm{X}_{i}\big)
is the set of measurable random variables (quotiented by the
\mathbb{M}
-a.e. equivalence relation) endowed with the topology of the convergence in probability. We can use these maps to lift our OT problem: define

\hat{\mathsf{C}}:\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\to[0,+\infty],\quad\hat{\mathsf{C}}(Z_{1},Z_{2}):=\int_{{\mathrm{Q}}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits\big(Z_{1}(q),Z_{2}(q)\big)d\mathbb{M}(q),

and use it to define the optimal transport problem

\hat{{\mathcal{C}}}({\mathtt{m}}_{1},{\mathtt{m}}_{2}):=\min_{{\mathfrak{P}}\in\Gamma({\mathtt{m}}_{1},{\mathtt{m}}_{2})}\left\{\int_{\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}}\hat{\mathsf{C}}(Z_{1},Z_{2})d{\mathfrak{P}}(Z_{1},Z_{2})\right\}\qquad\forall{\mathtt{m}}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}_{i}).

We can relate
{\mathsf{C}}
-concavity and
\hat{\mathsf{C}}
-concavity: using the notation of (
1.6
), we can define
\mathcal{X}_{i,{\mathtt{a}}_{i}}:=\{Z_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}:\int_{{\mathrm{Q}}}{\mathtt{a}}_{i}(Z_{i}(q))d\mathbb{M}(q)<+\infty\}
, for
i=1,2
, which naturally restrict (
1.7
) to

\displaystyle\iota_{1}\colon\mathcal{X}_{1,{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}),\qquad\quad\iota_{2}\colon\mathcal{X}_{2,{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}\to\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}(\mathrm{X}_{2}),\qquad\quad\iota_{1,2}\colon\mathcal{X}_{1,{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}\times\mathcal{X}_{2,{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}\to\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}\oplus{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}).

(1.8)

At this point, we will show that a function
\phi_{1}:\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is
\mathsf{C}
-concave if and only if
\hat{\phi}_{1}:=\phi_{1}\circ\iota_{1}\colon\mathcal{X}_{1,{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is
\hat{C}
-concave, and a similar statements holds for total
\mathsf{C}
-cyclical monotonicity (resp. total
\mathsf{C}
-superdifferential) and
\hat{\mathsf{C}}
-cyclical monotonicity (resp.
\hat{\mathsf{C}}
-superdifferential). These results are proven independently of the ones presented above and as a byproduct they give an alternative proof for the characterization of optimal random couplings.

Example 1.3
.

In the context of Examples
1.1
and
1.2
, since
\mathrm{X}_{1}=\mathrm{X}_{2}=\mathrm{X}
and
{\mathtt{a}}_{1}={\mathtt{a}}_{2}
, we only have one lifted space
\mathcal{X}_{1,{\mathtt{a}}_{1}}=\mathcal{X}_{2,{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}=\mathcal{X}_{p}:=L^{p}({\mathrm{Q}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Q}},\mathbb{M};\mathrm{X})
, that is also a complete and separable metric space when endowed with the
L^{p}
-distance.

It is important to note that, as it is presented here, this strategy only works in the case that
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits
is continuous (still possibly infinitely valued). For the general case, we need to consider a different lifting procedure, see (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), and then the same approach works.
All the details about it are presented in Section
4
.

Strict Monge formulation.
The usual Monge formulation that can be naturally associated to the OT cost
{\mathcal{C}}
is

{\mathcal{C}}_{M}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2}):=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})}\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{1}))d{\mathtt{M}}_{1}(\mu_{1})\,:\,{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})\text{ Borel s.t. }{\mathcal{T}}_{\sharp}{\mathtt{M}}_{1}={\mathtt{M}}_{2}\right\}.

(1.9)

However, this setting allows us to introduce a stricter version of it: taking a competitor for (
1.9
), a natural question is whether the optimal cost
{\mathsf{C}}
between
\mu_{1}
and
{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{1})
can be realized, or at least approximated, by an optimal map
{\mathrm{T}}_{\mu_{1}}\colon\mathrm{X}_{1}\to\mathrm{X}_{2}
. This argument leads to the
strict Monge problem
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{sM}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2}):=\inf\Bigg\{\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})}\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},{\mathrm{T}}(x_{1},\mu_{1}))d\mu_{1}(x_{1})d{\mathtt{M}}_{1}(\mu_{1})\,:

(1.10)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{T}}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to\mathrm{X}_{2}\text{ Borel,\ }{\mathcal{T}}_{\sharp}{\mathtt{M}}_{1}={\mathtt{M}}_{2}\Bigg\},

where in (
1.10
) the map
{\mathcal{T}}\colon\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})
is defined as
{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{1}):={\mathrm{T}}(\cdot,\mu_{1})_{\sharp}\mu_{1}
. In general, not all the maps
{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})
can be represented in this way, and this gives that the problem (
1.10
) is usually defined on a more restrictive class than (
1.9
).

Recall that, according to formula (
1.5
), any competitor for the strict Monge formulation induces a fully deterministic random coupling.
This shows the trivial inequalities
{\mathcal{C}}\leq{\mathcal{C}}_{M}\leq{\mathcal{C}}_{sM}
.
At this point, it is natural to ask: when
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})={\mathcal{C}}_{sM}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
?
Inspired by the general results by A. Pratelli
[
Pra07
]
, we prove the following result (see Theorems
5.5
and
5.6
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
{\mathtt{M}}_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}))
be atomless and concentrated on atomless measures. Then:

1.

Assume
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}\to[0,+\infty)
is continuous and bounded, then for all
{\mathtt{M}}_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2}))
it holds
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})={\mathcal{C}}_{sM}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
.

2.

Assume
\mathrm{X}_{1}=\mathrm{X}_{2}=\mathrm{X}
,
(\mathrm{X},{\mathrm{d}})
is a complete and separable metric space. Let
p\in[1,+\infty)
. If furthermore
{\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathrm{X}))
, then the same holds, that is for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
{\mathrm{T}}^{\varepsilon}:\mathrm{X}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X})\to\mathrm{X}
such that, if
{\mathcal{T}}^{\varepsilon}(\mu_{1}):={\mathrm{T}}(\cdot,\mu_{1})_{\sharp}\mu_{1}
,

{\mathcal{T}}^{\varepsilon}_{\sharp}{\mathtt{M}}_{1}={\mathtt{M}}_{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X})}\int_{\mathrm{X}}{\mathrm{d}}^{p}(x_{1},{\mathrm{T}}^{\varepsilon}(x_{1},\mu_{1}))d\mu_{1}(x_{1})d{\mathtt{M}}_{1}(\mu_{1})\leq{\mathcal{W}}_{p}^{p}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})+\varepsilon.

Its proof strongly relies on Pratelli’s theorem and on the characterization of optimal random couplings, but not on the Lions’ lifting of Section
4
.

Brenier theorem for random measures on Banach spaces.
In
5.2
, not only we show that the values of the Kantorovich and the strict Monge problem coincide, but also that there exists a unique optimal random coupling and it is fully deterministic, under stronger assumptions on the base space and on the initial random measure. In particular, we put ourselves in the context of Examples
1.1
and
1.2
, with the additional assumption that
\mathrm{X}={\mathrm{B}}
is a separable Banach space endowed with a strictly convex norm
\|\cdot\|
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
.

In this scenario, following Example
1.3
, we have the great advantage that the lifted space is itself a separable Banach space with strictly convex norm, being the Bochner space
{\mathcal{B}}:=L^{p}({\mathrm{Q}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Q}},\mathbb{M};{\mathrm{B}})
. We use this structure to transfer ‘Gaussian’ concepts from
{\mathcal{B}}
to
\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}})
.

1.

Following
[
AGS08
, Chapter 6]
, we say that a measure
\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}})
is Gaussian regular if
\mu_{1}(N)=0
for every Gaussian null set
N\subset{\mathrm{B}}
(see
[
Phe78
]
).

2.

We say that a random measure
{\mathtt{M}}_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))
is super Gaussian regular if it is concentrated on Gaussian regular measures and
{\mathtt{M}}_{1}({\mathrm{N}})=0
for all
{\mathrm{N}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}})
such that
\iota^{-1}({\mathrm{N}})
is Gaussian null in
{\mathcal{B}}
, where
\iota\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}})
is the law map defined in (
1.7
).

This approach is inspired by the one presented in
[
PS25a
]
, which was specific for the Hilbert setting with
p=2
. Then we can prove the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
({\mathrm{B}},\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space with strictly convex norm. Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
{\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))
such that
{\mathtt{M}}_{1}
is super Gaussian regular. Then, there exists a unique optimal random coupling
{\mathtt{P}}\in\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{o,p}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
and it has the form (
1.5
) for some Borel measurable
{\mathrm{T}}:{\mathrm{B}}\times\mathcal{P}({\mathrm{B}})\to{\mathrm{B}}
. In particular

{\mathcal{W}}_{p}^{p}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}})}\int_{{\mathrm{B}}}\|x_{1}-{\mathrm{T}}(x_{1},\mu_{1})\|^{p}d\mu_{1}(x_{1})d{\mathtt{M}}_{1}(\mu_{1}),\quad{\mathcal{T}}_{\sharp}{\mathtt{M}}_{1}={\mathtt{M}}_{2},\quad{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{1})={\mathrm{T}}(\cdot,\mu_{1})_{\sharp}\mu_{1}.

It is worth mentioning that the only previous version of this theorem is present in
[
PS25a
,
BPS25
]
and is specific of the case
p=2
and
{\mathrm{B}}={\mathrm{H}}
Hilbert space. Thus, this result is new even in the finite dimensional case
{\mathrm{B}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
for every
d\geq 1
, and the strict convexity of the norm is sharp, as we will show in Remark
5.12
. Moreover, in the case
d=1
a stronger result holds, as we can replace the hypothesis that
{\mathtt{M}}_{1}
is concentrated on Gaussian regular measures with being concentrated on atomless measures, see Remark
5.16
.

Differently from what presented above, this result strongly relies on Lions’ lifting, also because we are defining super Gaussian regularity through it (even if this definition is independent of the choice of the underlying probability space
({\mathrm{Q}},{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{Q}}},\mathbb{M})
). It would be interesting to understand how to develop a strategy for similar results that do not rely on the lifting.

Of course, the proof relies also on a version of the classic Brenier theorem on strictly convex Banach spaces. This result is folklore, but we did not find a clear reference for it and we prove it in
5.2.1
.

studied the strict Monge formulation when
\mathrm{X}_{1}=\mathrm{X}_{2}
is an Hilbert space with underlying cost function the square distance. In a setting similar to that of Section

for which optimal random couplings are unique and induced by a solution to the strict Monge problem. Unlike the general Banach setting considered in Section 5, the Hilbert setting, with cost the squared distance, allows for weaker assumptions than Gaussian regularity: this is possible because the optimal Kantorovich potentials are convex and then differentiable out of a countable union of d.c.-hypersurfaces

). Note that in

Beyond optimal transport, many researchers have been recently attracted to study the space of laws of random probability measures: for applications in Bayesian statistics

There is a vast research area on optimal transport between random measures: for example, in
[

]
, the author studies the optimal transport between two measure-valued random variables
\mu_{1}^{\omega}
and
\mu_{2}^{\omega}
, assuming equivariant properties, due to the fact that they have infinite mass almost surely (see also

2.1
Narrow topology

Let
(\mathrm{Y},\tau)
be a Polish space. We will denote with
\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{Y})
its Borel
\sigma
-algebra and with
\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y})
the probability measures defined over
\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{Y})
. We endow
\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y})
with the narrow topology, i.e. the coarsest topology that makes continuous the functions
\mu\mapsto\int_{\mathrm{Y}}\phi d\mu
for all
\phi\in C_{b}(\mathrm{Y})
(continuous and bounded functions). We will simply write
\mu_{n}\to\mu
if the sequence of probability measures
\mu_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y})
converges to
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y})
in the narrow topology. Given a map
f:\mathrm{Y}_{1}\to\mathrm{Y}_{2}
between two measurable spaces, for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y}_{1})
we will denote by
f_{\sharp}\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y}_{2})
the push-forward measure. Moreover, given a product space
\mathrm{Y}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathrm{Y}_{k}
, we denote by
\operatorname{pr}^{i}
the projection on the
i
-th coordinate, for
i=1,\dots,k
.

The previous construction can be iterated: in fact, the set of probability measures
\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y})
, endowed with the narrow topology, is a Polish space, so that we use the previous setting to define the space
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y}))
with its narrow topology.
We will denote

\text{for all }{\mathtt{M}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y})),\quad\overline{{\mathtt{M}}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y}))\ \text{ defined as }\overline{{\mathtt{M}}}:=\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y})}\mu\otimes\delta_{\mu}d{\mathtt{M}}(\mu).

(2.1)

Given a map
f\colon\mathrm{Y}_{1}\to\mathrm{Y}_{2}
we define by induction the iterated push-forward as
f_{\sharp\sharp}\colon\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y}_{1}))\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y}_{2}))
as
f_{\sharp\sharp}:=(f_{\sharp})_{\sharp}
(see
[
PS25
, Proposition D.8]
).

2.2
\operatorname{OT}_{\mathsf{C}}
- problem,
\mathsf{C}
-concave functions and
\mathsf{C}
-superdifferentiability

Here we recall some classic results on optimal transport. For more detailed expositions we refer to
[
AGS08
,
San15
,
Vil09
]
.
Let
\mathrm{Y}_{1}
and
\mathrm{Y}_{2}
be Polish spaces, and
\mathsf{C}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{1}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}\to[0,+\infty]
a (non-identically
+\infty
) cost function. Given
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y}_{i})
, the optimal transport problem is

\min\left\{\int_{\mathrm{Y}_{1}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}}\mathsf{C}(y_{1},y_{2})d\pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\ :\ \pi\in\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\right\},

(
\operatorname{OT}_{\mathsf{C}}
)

where
\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is the set of couplings
\pi\in\mathcal{P}{}(\mathrm{Y}_{1}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2})
such that
\operatorname{pr}^{i}_{\sharp}=\mu_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. The following theorem guarantees mild sufficient conditions for which the minimum exists and it is finite, and it can be proven by the Direct Method of the Calculus of Variations and the Prohorov’s theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
\mathsf{C}
is lower semicontinuous.
Then, there exists
\pi\in\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
realizing the (possibly infinite) minimum in (

OT
C

). The set of optimal couplings is denoted as
\Gamma_{o,\mathsf{C}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
.

We can actually say a lot more on the structure of optimal couplings, exploiting the dual problem of (

OT
C

) and the theory of
\mathsf{C}
-concave functions.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime},\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}
be two sets and
\mathsf{C}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}\to[0,+\infty)
.

(1)

We say that
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}
is
\mathsf{C}
-cyclically monotone if for all
k\geq 1
, for all
\sigma
permutation of
\{1,\dots,k\}
and
(y_{1,i},y_{2,i})\in\Gamma
,
i=1,\dots,k
, it holds

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{C}(y_{1,i},y_{2,\sigma(i)})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{C}(y_{1,i},y_{2,i}).

(2.2)

Note that, by decomposition of permutation in cycles, it suffices to check that for all
k\geq 1
and
(y_{1,i},y_{2,i})\in\Gamma
it holds

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{C}(y_{1,i},y_{2,i-1})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{C}(y_{1,i},y_{2,i}),

where we use the convention
y_{2,0}=y_{2,k}
.

(2)

A function
\phi_{1}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is said to be
\mathsf{C}
-concave if it is not identically
-\infty
and there exists
A\subset\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\phi_{1}(y_{1})=\inf_{(y_{2},\alpha)\in A}\mathsf{C}(y_{1},y_{2})-\alpha.

(2.3)

Analogously, we can define
\mathsf{C}
-concavity for a function
\phi_{2}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
.

(3)

Given a function
\phi_{1}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
, we define its
\mathsf{C}
-conjugate function
\phi_{1}^{\mathsf{C}}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}\to[-\infty,+\infty]

\phi_{1}^{\mathsf{C}}(y_{2}):=\inf_{y_{1}\in\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}}\mathsf{C}(y_{1},y_{2})-\phi(y_{1}).

(2.4)

Similarly, for
\phi_{2}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
, we define
\phi_{2}^{\mathsf{C}}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
.

(4)

If
\phi_{1}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
and
y_{1}\in\operatorname{dom}\phi:=\{\phi_{1}>-\infty\}
, its
\mathsf{C}
-superdifferential is defined as

\begin{gathered}\partial^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}\phi_{1}:=\left\{(y_{1},y_{2})\in\operatorname{dom}\phi\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}:\mathsf{C}(y_{1},y_{2})-\phi_{1}(y_{1})\leq\mathsf{C}(y_{1}^{\prime},y_{2})-\phi_{1}(y_{1}^{\prime})\quad\forall y_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\right\},\\
\partial^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}\phi_{1}(y_{1}):=\left\{y_{2}:(y_{1},y_{2})\in\partial^{\mathsf{C}}\phi_{1}\right\}.\end{gathered}

(2.5)

With these definitions, it is not hard to show that a function
\phi_{1}
is
\mathsf{C}
-concave if and only if
\phi_{1}^{\mathsf{C}\mathsf{C}}=\phi_{1}
. Moreover the
\mathsf{C}
-superdifferential
\partial^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}\phi_{1}
is
\mathsf{C}
-cyclically monotone. The celebrated Rockafellar–Ruschendorf theorem gives the opposite direction.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}
is
\mathsf{C}
-cyclically monotone. Then, there exists a
{\mathsf{C}}
-concave function
\phi\colon\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
such that
\Gamma\subset\partial^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}\phi
.

The next result is again classic in OT theory and characterizes the optimal couplings exploiting the theory of
\mathsf{C}
-concave functions, see for example
[
ABS24
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathrm{Y}_{1}
and
\mathrm{Y}_{2}
be Polish spaces, and
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y}_{i})
,
i=1,2
. Let
\mathsf{C}:\mathrm{Y}_{1}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}\to[0,+\infty]
be lower semicontinuous and assume there exist
{\mathrm{A}}_{i}\colon\mathrm{Y}_{i}\to[0,+\infty]
such that

{\mathrm{A}}_{i}\in L^{1}(\mu_{i})\quad\text{for }i=1,2,\qquad\mathsf{C}(y_{1},y_{2})\leq{\mathrm{A}}_{1}(y_{1})+{\mathrm{A}}_{2}(y_{2})\quad\forall(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathrm{Y}_{1}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}.

(2.6)

Let
\mathrm{Y}_{i}^{\prime}:={\mathrm{A}}_{i}^{-1}([0,+\infty))
, so
\mathsf{C}:\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}\to[0,+\infty)
, and
\pi\in\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
. The following are equivalent:

1.

\pi\in\Gamma_{o,\mathsf{C}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
;

2.

\pi
is concentrated on a
\mathsf{C}
-cyclically monotone set
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}
;

3.

there exists
\phi\colon\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
such that
\phi\in L^{1}(\mu_{1})
,
\phi^{\mathsf{C}}\in L^{1}(\mu_{2})
and

\int_{\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{\prime}}\phi(y_{1})d\mu_{1}(y_{1})+\int_{\mathrm{Y}_{2}^{\prime}}\phi^{\mathsf{C}}(y_{2})d\mu_{2}(y_{2})=\int_{\mathrm{Y}_{1}\times\mathrm{Y}_{2}}\mathsf{C}(y_{1},y_{2})d\pi(y_{1},y_{2}),

i.e.
\pi
is concentrated over
\partial^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}\phi
.

Let
(\mathrm{Y},{\mathrm{d}})
be a separable and complete metric space, and
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Using the notation of the previous subsection, let
\mathrm{Y}_{1}=\mathrm{Y}_{2}=\mathrm{Y}
and
\mathsf{C}(y_{1},y_{2})={\mathrm{d}}(y_{1},y_{2})^{p}
. Then, the
L^{p}
-Wasserstein distance between
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y})
corresponds to the
p
-root of the value (

OT
C

). We will denote it as
{\mathsf{W}}_{p,{\mathrm{d}}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\in[0,+\infty]
and the set of optimal couplings as

\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathrm{Y}):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Y})\ :\ \int_{\mathrm{Y}}{\mathrm{d}}^{p}(y,y_{0})d\mu(y)<+\infty\text{ for some (and then all) }y_{0}\in\mathrm{Y}\right\}.

with
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathrm{Y}),{\mathsf{W}}_{p})

\big(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathrm{Y})),{\mathcal{W}}_{p,{\mathrm{d}}}\big)\quad\text{ with }\quad{\mathcal{W}}_{p,{\mathrm{d}}}:={\mathsf{W}}_{p,{\mathsf{W}}_{p,{\mathrm{d}}}}.

(2.8)

When the distance
{\mathrm{d}}

3
Optimal transport between laws of random probability measures

Let
\mathrm{X}_{1}
and
\mathrm{X}_{2}
be two Polish spaces, and
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits\colon\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}\to[0,+\infty]
a lower semicontinuous cost function. For all
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i})
, denote

\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\min\left\{\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})d\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\ :\ \pi\in\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\right\}.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.1
.

The map
\mathsf{C}\colon\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})\to[0,+\infty]
is lower semicontinuous.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{1,n}\to\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2,n}\to\mu_{2}
in the respective weak topologies and consider
\pi_{n}\in\Gamma_{o,\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits}(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\liminf\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})=\lim\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n})
. The sequences
\{\mu_{1,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\mu_{2,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are tight, which gives that
\{\pi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight as well, and in particular there exists
\pi\in\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
such that
\pi_{n_{k}}\to\pi
narrowly, for some subsequence. Then

\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\leq\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits d\pi\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits d\pi_{n_{k}}=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1,n},\mu_{2,n}).\qed

Thus, we can iterate the optimal transport construction and consider the problem

{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2}):=\min\left\{\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})}\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})d\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\ :\ \Pi\in\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})\right\}.

(3.2)

The particular structure of this problem allows us to rephrase it as a minimization problem over the so-called
(laws of) random couplings
. We say that
{\mathtt{P}}\in\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}))
if

\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp\sharp}{\mathtt{P}}={\mathtt{M}}_{1},\quad\operatorname{pr}^{2}_{\sharp\sharp}{\mathtt{P}}={\mathtt{M}}_{2}.

Any random coupling
{\mathtt{P}}\in\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
induces a usual coupling

\Pi:=\big(\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp},\operatorname{pr}^{2}_{\sharp}\big)_{\sharp}{\mathtt{P}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})).

(3.3)

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\mathtt{P}}\in\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
and
\Pi
defined as in (
3.3
). Then
\Pi\in\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
and

\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})}\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\leq\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})}\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})d\pi(x_{1},x_{2})d{\mathtt{P}}(\pi).

(3.4)

Moreover, the following equality holds and the minimum (possibly infinite) is always attained:

{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})=\min\left\{\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})}\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})d\pi(x_{1},x_{2})d{\mathtt{P}}(\pi)\ :\ {\mathtt{P}}\in\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})\right\}.

(3.5)

If
\mathcal{C}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})<+\infty
, then a random coupling
{\mathtt{P}}
is optimal, and we write
{\mathtt{P}}\in\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{o,{\mathcal{C}}}
, if and only if
(\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp},\operatorname{pr}^{2}_{\sharp})_{\sharp}{\mathtt{P}}\in\Gamma_{o,\mathsf{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
and it is concentrated on the set of optimal couplings
\mathcal{P}_{o,\mathsf{C}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})
.

Proof.

By the very definition of
\Pi
, (
3.4
) follows:

\displaystyle\int\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\int\mathsf{C}(\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp}\pi,\operatorname{pr}^{2}_{\sharp}\pi)d{\mathtt{P}}(\pi)\leq\int\int\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})d\pi(x_{1},x_{2})d{\mathtt{P}}(\pi).

Thus, the
\leq
in (
3.5
) follows. Regarding the converse inequality, consider the map

(\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp},\operatorname{pr}^{2}_{\sharp})\colon\mathcal{P}_{o,\mathsf{C}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})\to\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2}).

Thanks to Theorem
2.1
, it is a surjective map between Borel measurable sets, and in particular there exists a right inverse map
G\colon\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{2})\to\mathcal{P}_{o,\mathsf{C}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})
that is universally measurable (see
[
Bog07
, Theorem 6.9.1]
). Then, given
\Pi\in\Gamma_{o,\mathsf{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})
, we may consider
{\mathtt{P}}:=G_{\sharp}\Pi
, which gives

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}({\mathtt{M}}_{1},{\mathtt{M}}_{2})=

\displaystyle\int\mathsf{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})d\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\int\int\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})d\big(G(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\big)(x_{1},x_{2})d\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})}\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}}\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})d\pi(x_{1},x_{2})d{\mathtt{P}}(\pi).

The last part follows by noticing that in (
3.4
) the equality holds if and only if
{\mathtt{P}}
is concentrated on
\mathcal{P}_{o,\mathsf{C}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})
.
∎

3.1
Total
\mathsf{C}
-cyclical monotonicity, total
\mathsf{C}
-superdifferential and characterization of optimal random couplings

In this section, we give a characterization of optimal random couplings in terms of the optimal Kantorovich potentials. We work in the following setting: let
\mathrm{X}_{1}
and
\mathrm{X}_{2}
be two Polish spaces,
{\mathtt{M}}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i}))
for
i=1,2
,
\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}\to[0,+\infty]
a lower semicontinuous function satisfying

\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits(x_{1},x_{2})\leq{\mathtt{a}}_{1}(x_{1})+{\mathtt{a}}_{2}(x_{2})\quad\forall(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}

(3.6)

for some Borel measurable map
{\mathtt{a}}_{i}:\mathrm{X}_{i}\to[0,+\infty]
.

Definition 3.3
.

We denote

\begin{gathered}{\mathtt{A}}_{i}(\mu_{i}):=\int_{\mathrm{X}_{i}}{\mathtt{a}}_{i}d\mu_{i},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{i}}(\mathrm{X}_{i}):=\left\{\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i})\ :\ {\mathtt{A}}_{i}(\mu_{i})<+\infty\right\},\\
\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}\oplus{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}):=\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})\ :\ \operatorname{pr}^{i}_{\sharp}\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{i}}(\mathrm{X}_{i}),\ i=1,2\right\}.\end{gathered}

(3.7)

The function
{\mathtt{A}}_{i}:\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i})\to[0,+\infty]
is Borel measurable, see e.g.
[
PS25
, Lemma D.1]
. Consequently, the sets
\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{i}}(\mathrm{X}_{i})\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i})
and
\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{1}\oplus{\mathtt{a}}_{2}}(\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2})
are Borel in their respective narrow topology.

Assumption 3.4
.

The random measures
{\mathtt{M}}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i}))
satisfy

\int_{\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i})}{\mathtt{A}}_{i}(\mu_{i})d{\mathtt{M}}_{i}(\mu_{i})<+\infty.

The general results presented in Section
2.2
will be exploited in the following setting: for
i=1,2

\mathrm{Y}_{i}=\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i}),\quad{\mathrm{A}}_{i}={\mathtt{A}}_{i},\quad\mathrm{Y}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{i}}(\mathrm{X}_{i}),\quad\text{function $\mathsf{C}$ defined in \eqref{eq:60}}.

Note that under Assumption
3.4
, we have:

1.

{\mathtt{M}}_{i}
is concentrated on
\mathcal{P}_{{\mathtt{a}}_{i}}(\mathrm{X}_{i})
for
i=1,2
;

Remark 4.3
.

The space

is not closed with respect to the topology of convergence in probability. Indeed, since all atomless standard Borel probability spaces are isomorphic, we can assume

endowed with the Lebesgue measure. Then, define the real random variables

For all

,

admits an independent randomization. In fact, the random variable

pushes the Lebesgue measure into itself, and is independent of

for all

converges in probability to the random variable

, up to substitute it with one of the two sets

since

In the Wasserstein example, i.e.

5
The strict Monge problem between laws of random measures

The goal of this section is to introduce and study the strict Monge formulation of optimal transport between (laws of) random measures.

Problem 5.1
.

Using the setting of Section
3
, let
{\mathtt{M}}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{i}))
, for

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{T}}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{X}_{1})\to\mathrm{X}_{2}\text{ Borel,\ }{\mathcal{T}}_{\sharp}{\mathtt{M}}_{1}={\mathtt{M}}_{2}\Bigg\},

where we denote

In this scenario, the induced map

In particular, we have the following inequalities

(5.3)

The goal of the following subsections will be:

1.

to give sufficient conditions under which

5.2
Existence and uniqueness of the

-strict Monge problem

5.2.1
The
L^{p}
-Monge problem in Banach spaces

Before proceeding, in this subsection we clarify under which set of assumptions we have a unique solution to the
L^{p}
-Monge problem in a separable Banach space
({\mathrm{B}},\|\cdot\|)
. Recall that a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathrm{B}})
is said to be a
centered non-degenerate Gaussian
, and we write
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{g}({\mathrm{B}})
, if

\text{for all }z\in{\mathrm{B}}^{*}\setminus\{0\}\ z_{\sharp}\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})\text{ is a centered non-degenerate Gaussian}.

Afterwards, we say that a Borel subset
N\subset{\mathrm{B}}
is
Gaussian null
if

\mu(N)=0\quad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{{\mathsf{g}}}({\mathrm{B}}).

Moreover, we will say that a measure is Gaussian regular, i.e.
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathsf{gr}}_{p}({\mathrm{B}})
if
\mu(N)=0
for all Gaussian null set
N\subset{\mathrm{B}}
.

The interest in Gaussian null sets is justified by the next theorem, which is a generalization to Banach spaces of the classic Rademacher’s theorem.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset{\mathrm{B}}
be a non-empty open set and
f:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
. If
f
is locally Lipschitz, then there exists
G\subset\mathcal{O}
Gaussian null such that
f
is Gateaux differentiable at every
x\in\mathcal{O}\setminus G
.

Its proof is a consequence of the main results in
[
Aro76
,
Phe78
]
and follows from this argument: Aronszajn defined a class of null sets for which the points of (Gateaux) non-differentiability of a Lipschitz function form a null set; then, Phelps proved that every Aronszajn null set is Gaussian null, yielding in particular Theorem
5.8
. It is worth mentioning that Csörnyei
[
Csö99
]
proved that the class of Aronszajn null sets coincides with the one of Gaussian null sets.

An important too for us is the dual multifunction, which encodes the strict convexity of the norm:

J:{\mathrm{B}}\rightrightarrows{\mathrm{B}}^{*},\quad J(x)=\left\{z\in{\mathrm{B}}^{*}\,:\,z(x)=\|x\|^{2},\,\|z\|_{*}=\|x\|\right\}.

(5.7)

Notice that
J(0)=\{0\}
and
0\notin J(x)
if
x\neq 0
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
({\mathrm{B}},\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space. The following are equivalent:

1.

\|\cdot\|
is strictly convex, that is

x,y\in{\mathrm{B}},\ x\neq y,\ \|x\|=\|y\|=1\implies\left\|\frac{x+y}{2}\right\|<1;

2.

J(x)\cap J(y)=\emptyset
whenever
x\neq y
.

In this case, given
p>1
, we also have
J_{p}(x)\cap J_{p}(y)=\emptyset
for
x\neq y
, where
J_{p}(x):=p\|x\|^{p-2}J(x)
for
x\neq 0
and
J_{p}(0)=0
.

Proof.

(1.
\Rightarrow
2.) By contradiction, assume there exists
x\neq y
and
z\in J(x)\cap J(y)
. This means that
\|x\|=\|z\|_{*}=\|y\|
. Then we have a contradiction

\left\|\frac{x/\|x\|+y/\|y\|}{2}\right\|\geq\frac{1}{\|z\|_{*}^{2}}z\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{\|z\|_{*}^{2}}\frac{\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}}{2}=1.

(2.
\Rightarrow
1.) Assume there exist
x,y\in{\mathrm{B}}
,
x\neq y
,
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
and
\|\frac{x+y}{2}\|=1
. By Hahn-Banach theorem there exists
z\in{\mathrm{B}}^{*}
such that
\|z\|=1
and
z(\frac{x+y}{2})=1
. Then the trivial inequality

1=\frac{z(x)+z(y)}{2}\leq\frac{\|x\|+\|y\|}{2}=1,

implies
z(x)=z(y)=1
, and in particular
z\in J(x)\cap J(y)
, raising a contradiction.

The last statement easily follows: if
w\in J_{p}(x)\cap J_{p}(y)
, there exist
z\in J(x)
and
z^{\prime}\in J(y)
such that
w=p\|x\|z=p\|y\|z^{\prime}
. By definition of
J
, it immediately follows that
\|x\|=\|y\|
, and then
z=z^{\prime}
, having a contradiction.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

Recall that
J_{p}(x)
coincides with the Gateaux subdifferential of the map
x\mapsto\|x\|^{p}
for
p>1

\|x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime}\|^{p}-\phi^{{\mathtt{c}}}(x_{2}^{\prime})\leq\varphi(x_{1}^{\prime})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(x_{1})-\left(\|x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime}\|^{p}-\phi^{{\mathtt{c}}}(x_{2}^{\prime})-\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\|x_{1}-x_{2}^{\prime}\|^{p}-\|x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime}\|^{p}+\varepsilon\leq\kappa(p,L,R)\|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}\|+\varepsilon,

where we used the Lipschitzianity of
f(r):=|r|^{p}
in
[-(L+R),(L+R)]
, with constant
\kappa(p,L,R)=p(L+R)^{p-1}
. By arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
and switching the role of
x_{1}
and
x_{1}^{\prime}
, we have the local Lipschitzianity of
\varphi
.

Moreover,
\varphi
is another optimal Kantorovich potential associated to
\pi
, which coincides with
\phi

\mu_{1}
-a.e. Indeed, it is the
{\mathtt{c}}
-transform of
\psi\colon{\mathrm{B}}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
,
\psi(x_{2}):=\phi^{{\mathtt{c}}}(x_{2})-\chi_{B_{R}}
, where
\chi_{B_{R}}
is
0
in
B_{R}
and
+\infty
outside, and since
\varphi\geq\phi
, we have

\int{\mathtt{c}}d\pi=\int\phi d\mu_{1}+\int\phi^{{\mathtt{c}}}d\mu_{2}\leq\int\varphi d\mu_{1}+\int\psi d\mu_{2}\leq\int{\mathtt{c}}d\pi.

Then,
\pi
is concentrated on
\partial^{+}_{{\mathtt{c}}}\varphi
. Now, the goal is to show that
\partial_{{\mathtt{c}}}^{+}\varphi(x_{1})
is a singleton for
\mu_{1}
-a.e.
x_{1}\in{\mathrm{B}}
. By definition of
{\mathtt{c}}
-superdifferential,
x_{2}\in\partial_{{\mathtt{c}}}^{+}\varphi(x_{1})
if and only if
\|\cdot-x_{2}\|^{p}-\varphi(\cdot)
is minimal at
x_{1}
, which means that
0\in\partial_{G}\big(\|\cdot-x_{2}\|^{p}-\varphi(\cdot)\big)(x_{1})
. Thanks to Remark
5.10
and the fact that
\varphi
is
\mu_{1}
-a.e. differentiable, we have

J_{p}(x_{1}-x_{2})-D_{G}\varphi(x_{1})\ni 0\quad\text{ for }\mu_{1}\text{-a.e. }x_{1}\in{\mathrm{B}}.

(5.9)

Thus, consider the set
E\subset{\mathrm{B}}
in which
\varphi
is Gateaux differentiable, which satisfies
\mu_{1}({\mathrm{B}}\setminus E)=0
. If
x_{1}\in E
and
x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime}\in\partial_{\mathop{\mathsf{c}}\nolimits}^{+}\varphi(x_{1})
, then
D_{G}\varphi(x_{1})\in J_{p}(x_{1}-x_{2})\cap J_{p}(x_{1}-x_{2}^{\prime})
, and Lemma
5.9
implies
x_{2}=x_{2}^{\prime}
.

This shows that
\partial_{{\mathtt{c}}}^{+}\varphi(x_{1})
is a singleton for
\mu_{1}
-a.e.
x_{1}
. Since
\pi
is concentrated on it, there exists a Borel measurable map
{\mathrm{t}}:{\mathrm{B}}\to{\mathrm{B}}
such that
\pi=(\operatorname{id},{\mathrm{t}})_{\sharp}\mu_{1}
(see Lemma
5.3
). We just proved that any optimal coupling
\pi
is induced by a map. Following a classical disintegration argument, this also implies uniqueness of the optimal coupling.

For the general case in which we do not have a control on the support of
\mu_{2}
, let
\pi\in\Gamma_{o,p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
and define

\pi_{n}:=\pi|_{{\mathrm{B}}\times B_{n}}.

Its first marginal is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{1}
, and its second marginal has support in
B_{n}
. Since
\pi_{n}\leq\pi
, any
\pi_{n}
is optimal (up to renormalization) because of Theorem
2.4
, and from the previous argument is induced by a map
{\mathrm{t}}_{n}:{\mathrm{B}}\to B_{n}
. By uniqueness and the fact that
\pi_{n}\leq\pi_{n^{\prime}}
if
n<n^{\prime}
, we have
{\mathrm{t}}_{n}(x_{1})={\mathrm{t}}_{n^{\prime}}(x_{1})
for
p^{1}_{\sharp}\pi_{n}
-a.e.
x_{1}\in{\mathrm{B}}
. Then, define the measurable sets

\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}:=\big\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathrm{B}}\times{\mathrm{B}}:x_{2}={\mathrm{t}}_{n}(x_{1})\big\},\quad\Gamma_{n}:=\bigcup_{n\geq n^{\prime}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{n^{\prime}},\quad\Gamma=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma_{n}.

Thanks to the previous argument,
\pi_{k}
is concentrated on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
so that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\pi(\Gamma_{n})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\pi_{k}(\Gamma_{n})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\pi_{k}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{k})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\pi_{k}({\mathrm{B}}\times B_{k})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu_{2}(B_{k})=1,

and in particular
\pi(\Gamma)=1
.
Moreover, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\displaystyle\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp}\pi_{k}

\displaystyle\Big(\big\{x_{1}:\exists x_{2}\neq x_{2}^{\prime}\text{ s.t. }(x_{1},x_{2}),(x_{1},x_{2}^{\prime})\in\Gamma\big\}\Big)=\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp}\pi_{k}\Big(\big\{x_{1}:\forall k^{\prime}\ \exists n,n^{\prime}\geq k^{\prime}\text{ s.t. }{\mathrm{t}}_{n}(x_{1})\neq{\mathrm{t}}_{n^{\prime}}(x_{1})\big\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{pr}^{1}_{\sharp}\pi_{k}\Big(\big\{x_{1}:\ \exists n,n^{\prime}\geq k\text{ s.t. }{\mathrm{t}}_{n}(x_{1})\neq{\mathrm{t}}_{n^{\prime}}(x_{1})\big\}\Big)=0.

Since

for all Borel set

, we can pass to the limit on the left hand side and obtain that for
\mu_{1}
-a.e.

there exists a unique

such that
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Gamma
. Then, thanks to Lemma
5.3
,

is deterministic, and uniqueness follows by the same disintegration argument. ∎

Lemma 5.13
.

If
{\mathrm{B}}
has strictly convex norm, then
{\mathcal{B}}
has strictly convex norm.

Proof.

By contraduction, let
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in{\mathcal{B}}
satisfy

\|Z_{1}\|_{\mathcal{B}}=\|Z_{2}\|_{\mathcal{B}}=1

and

\left\|\frac{Z_{1}+Z_{2}}{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}=1.

For a.e.
q\in{\mathrm{Q}}
, using the triangle inequality in
{\mathrm{B}}
and the strict convexity of
r\mapsto r^{p}
, we have

\left(\frac{\|Z_{1}(q)+Z_{2}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}}{2}\right)^{p}\leq\left(\frac{\|Z_{1}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}+\|Z_{1}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}}{2}\right)^{p}\leq\frac{\|Z_{1}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}^{p}+\|Z_{2}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}^{p}}{2}.

Integrating with respect to
\mathbb{M}
, we obtain

1=\left\|\frac{Z_{1}+Z_{2}}{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{p}\leq\frac{\|Z_{1}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{p}+\|Z_{2}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{p}}{2}=1.

Therefore equality holds throughout and, in particular, for
\mathbb{M}
-a.e.
q\in{\mathrm{Q}}

\left(\frac{\|Z_{1}(q)+Z_{2}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}}{2}\right)^{p}=\left(\frac{\|Z_{1}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}+\|Z_{1}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}}{2}\right)^{p}=\frac{\|Z_{1}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}^{p}+\|Z_{2}(q)\|_{\mathrm{B}}^{p}}{2}.

By strict convexity of
t\mapsto t^{p}
, the equality in the triangle inequality and the strict convexity of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{B}}
, this implies
Z_{1}(q)=Z_{2}(q)
for
\mathbb{M}
-a.e.
q\in{\mathrm{Q}}
, and in particular
Z_{1}=Z_{2}
.
∎

The space
{\mathcal{B}}
can be used to transfer properties that are specific of Banach spaces to
\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}})
. We use this paradigm to set the notions of Gaussian distributions and Gaussian null sets in

Definition 5.14
.

We say that:

1.

{\mathtt{M}}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))

{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathcal{B}})
non-degenerate Gaussian such that
{\mathtt{M}}=\iota_{\sharp}{\mathfrak{g}}
. We will write

2.

{\mathrm{N}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}})
is Gaussian null if
\iota^{-1}({\mathrm{N}})
is Gaussian null in
{\mathcal{B}}
. Equivalently, we could ask that
{\mathtt{M}}({\mathrm{N}})=0
for all
{\mathtt{M}}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{\mathsf{g}}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))

3.

{\mathtt{M}}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))
is Gaussian regular if
{\mathtt{M}}({\mathrm{N}})=0
for all Gaussian null sets

;

4.

{\mathtt{M}}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))
is super Gaussian regular if
{\mathtt{M}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathsf{gr}}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))
and is concentrated on
\mathcal{P}_{p}^{\mathsf{gr}}({\mathrm{B}})
.
We will write
{\mathtt{M}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathsf{sgr}}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))

A priori, the definition of Gaussian measures in

(and of Gaussian null sets) may depend on the choice of the triplet

used to define

[

Proposition 5.17

\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))

be such that the
{\mathsf{W}}_{p}
-ball
\{B(\mu_{i},r):i\leq n\}
are disjoint. Define

{\mathtt{M}}_{r}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}}{{\mathtt{M}}(B(\mu_{i},r))}\mathds{1}_{B(\mu_{i},r)}\right){\mathtt{M}}\ll{\mathtt{M}},

which clearly converges to
{\mathtt{N}}
as
r\to 0
.

If
{\mathrm{B}}
is finite dimensional, say
{\mathrm{B}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|
,
[
PS25a
, Theorem 6.25]
shows that taking any non-degenerate Gaussian
{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathcal{P}\big(C^{1}([0,1]^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
, interpreting
C^{1}([0,1]^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
as a subset of
{\mathrm{B}}=L^{p}\big([0,1]^{d},{\mathcal{F}}_{[0,1]^{d}},\lambda^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
, then
{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathcal{P}^{{\mathsf{g}}}({\mathcal{B}})
and the density of
C^{1}([0,1]^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
in
{\mathcal{B}}
gives that
{\mathtt{G}}:=\iota_{\sharp}{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

[

,
{\mathtt{G}}
-a.e.

{\mathtt{G}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathsf{sgr}}(\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathrm{B}}))
.
