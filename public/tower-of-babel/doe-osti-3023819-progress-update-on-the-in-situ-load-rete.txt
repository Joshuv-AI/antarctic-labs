1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

To improve upon our conventional thermally accelerated aging study methods which require
periodic interruption of aging to perform load testing in an Instron machine, a new aging system
is being developed to: (1) automate/facilitate data acquisition/analysis, (2) improve data quality,
and (3) enable uninterrupted aging conditions (compression, temperature, atmosphere) which
represents the service condition. In FY24, a thermal aging vessel instrumented with load cells
was fabricated and tested1. That unit demonstrated the primary function of the vessel: to
continuously monitor the in situ load retention of up to three compressed polymer coupons
undergoing thermally accelerated aging under nitrogen. A secondary objective, not realized in
the unit due to inadequate sealing, was to enable a single initial nitrogen backfill (as opposed to
a continuous purge) and gas sampling of the vessel headspace during thermal aging. Heating of
the vessel was achieved using a custom heater jacket.

Based on the initial experience with the FY24 unit and separate Instron stress relaxation
testing2, the vessel was redesigned in FY25 with the following enhancements: (1) rapid,
continuous compression of the coupons with minimal heat loss, (2) metal gaskets throughout to
reduce leakage, and (3) removable inner compression assembly. Due to shipping delays by
multiple vendors, full assembly of the first two units is running late, so aging data has not yet
been acquired and leakage has not been assessed. The enhanced compression mechanism
has been assembled and functions as intended.

2. IN SITU VESSEL DESIGN

A diagram of the main vessel is shown in Fig. 1. Drawing numbers are given in Table 1. The
housing consists of a custom 5” nipple with bottom and top 8” Conflat (CF) flanges. The
removable compression assembly inside the housing (photo in Fig. 2) includes three 100-lbf
load cells (Interface Model WMC-100) with attached custom bottom compression platens and a
custom top compression platen that slides on three outer threaded rods and a fixed central
screw. The gap between the compression platens is set by locking shaft collars on the threaded
rods. The top compression platen is driven down by turning a single nut on the fixed central
screw until the platen contacts the shaft collars; access to the nut is provided by removing the
blank flange covering a hole in the top flange. Turning the nut using an electric screwdriver or
ratchet will provide a means of rapid, continuous compression. A coaxial spring on the central
screw keeps the top compression platen above the test coupons during the initial thermal soak.
A valve will allow pulling vacuum and backfilling with nitrogen or other desired gas and sealed
feedthroughs allow the load cell cables and thermocouple wires to penetrate the closed vessel
(Fig. 3). Metal gaskets are used throughout to minimize leakage.

3. FUNCTIONAL TEST OF COMPRESSION MECHANISM

Cellular silicone coupons (~63% porous, 1-inch-diameter, ~2-mm-thick) were used to
demonstrate the new single drive nut compression mechanism using the assembly in Fig. 2 at
ambient conditions. With the locking collars set to provide a compressive strain of ~25%, an
electric screwdriver (Fanttik Fold S1 APEX) was used to turn the drive nut at its high-speed
setting. Compression was rapid (reached full compression in ~0.1 sec at ~5 mm/sec) and fairly
consistent among all three coupons (Fig. 5). The minor differences in the loading curves result
from the fact that the top compression platen is permitted to tilt during compression. The small
differences in peak load can be attributed to: (1) slight differences in applied strain (due to slight
differences in the gap and/or original coupon thickness) and/or (2) slight differences in material
load response (due to slightly non-uniform porosity). The first ~6 minutes of stress relaxation
following compression is shown in Fig. 6a. The in situ load cells show smooth, continuous
relaxation of the polymer coupons as expected. The load retention (calculated as the load
divided by the peak load) vs. time is essentially equivalent for all the coupons (Fig. 6b).

4. CONCLUSIONS

The output of the in situ load cells recorded by the data acquisition system indicated that the
enhanced compression mechanism provided rapid, continuous compression as intended. The
stress relaxation at constant strain following compression at ambient conditions was measured
as expected. Leak testing and testing at elevated temperatures will be performed soon.
