Sharp Endpoint Multilinear Estimates for Oscillatory Integrals and Spectral Clusters
Shengwen Gan, Cheng Zhang, Zhifei Zhu

Abstract
. We prove sharp k k -linear L p L^{p} estimates for Carleson–Sjölin oscillatory integral operators with arbitrary separated frequency scales for all k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty . The estimates are sharp, including the endpoint logarithmic behavior for general Carleson–Sjölin phases. Moreover, we obtain log-free endpoint bilinear spectral cluster estimates on every closed three-dimensional Riemannian manifold, resolving a problem of Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov [ 4 , 5 ] . As a consequence, we establish sharp k k -linear L p L^{p} spectral cluster estimates for all k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty .



1.
Introduction

Oscillatory integral operators with curvature form a central bridge between Fourier restriction and spectral cluster estimates. In this paper, we prove sharp multilinear estimates for such operators in the full range of Lebesgue exponents (
1\leq p\leq\infty
) and frequency configurations relevant to products of spectral clusters. One of our results shows that the endpoint behavior in three dimensions depends on a genuine geometric distinction: spectral clusters admit log-free endpoint estimates, while general Carleson–Sjölin oscillatory integral operators may exhibit an unavoidable logarithmic loss.

To motivate the question, we first introduce the model case which is formulated in terms of the Fourier extension operator. Let
n\geq 2
. For
\lambda>1
, define

E_{\lambda}f(y):=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\int_{[0,1]^{n-1}}e^{i\lambda(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2})}f(\xi)d\xi,

where
y=(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Given frequency scales
1\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{k}
, we study estimates of the form

(1.1)

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}E_{\lambda_{j}}f_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}(B^{n}(0,1))}\lesssim C(n,k,p;\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}([0,1]^{n-1})}.

We refer to such an estimate as the
k
-linear restriction estimate with separated frequency scales
. This type of estimate is fundamentally different from the
k
-linear estimate developed by Bennett–Carbery–Tao
[
1
]
and others; see Section
1.2
. We shall study (
1.1
) in a more general setting, with the Fourier extension operators replaced by Carleson–Sjölin oscillatory integral operators.

Let
n\geq 2
. A Carleson–Sjölin operator has the form

T_{\lambda,\phi}^{a}f(y):=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{i\lambda\phi(y,\xi)}a(y,\xi)f(\xi)d\xi,

where
y=(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
is real-valued. We assume throughout that each phase satisfies the
Carleson–Sjölin condition
. For each operator, this means that the phase
\phi(y,\xi)
satisfies the nondegeneracy condition:

(1.2)

\text{rank}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial\xi\partial y}=n-1

and the curvature condition: for each
y
, the hypersurface

(1.3)

S_{\phi}(y)=\{\nabla_{y}\phi(y,\xi):\xi\in\text{supp}_{\xi}a\}

has everywhere nonvanishing Gaussian curvature.

We say that the phase satisfies the
elliptic Carleson–Sjölin condition
if the curvature condition is replaced by the stronger elliptic condition that the second fundamental form of
S_{\phi}(y)
is uniformly definite. Equivalently, writing

P(y,\xi)=\nabla_{y}\phi(y,\xi),\quad A_{j}(y,\xi)=\partial_{\xi_{j}}P(y,\xi),\quad N=\frac{\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n-1}A_{j}}{|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n-1}A_{j}|},

the matrix

\big\langle\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}P(y,\xi),N(y,\xi)\big\rangle

is definite for all
(y,\xi)\in\operatorname{supp}a
. The model elliptic phase is
\phi(t,x,\xi)=x\cdot\xi+\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{2}
. The local Fourier integral operators arising in Sogge’s spectral cluster parametrix for Laplace eigenfunctions on smooth closed manifolds also satisfy this elliptic Carleson–Sjölin condition. See
[
17
, Section 5]
and Lemma
7.3
.

1.1.
Main results

For
n\geq 3
define

p_{0}=\frac{n+1}{n-1},\qquad p_{1}=\frac{n-1}{n-2},\qquad p_{2}=\frac{n+1}{n},\qquad p_{3}=\frac{2(n+1)}{3(n-1)}.

We first present the bilinear estimates. This case already contains the main endpoint phenomenon: in dimension three, general Carleson–Sjölin phases have a sharp logarithmic loss at
p=2
.

Theorem 1.1

(Bilinear estimates)
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
1\leq p\leq 2
and
1\ll\lambda\leq\mu
. Let
T_{\lambda,\phi_{1}}^{a_{1}}
and
T_{\mu,\phi_{2}}^{a_{2}}
be Carleson–Sjölin operators as above. Then

(1.4)

\|T_{\lambda,\phi_{1}}^{a_{1}}f\,T_{\mu,\phi_{2}}^{a_{2}}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\lesssim\,\mathcal{E}(n,2,p;\lambda,\mu)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}.

If
n\geq 4
, then

\mathcal{E}(n,2,p;\lambda,\mu)=\begin{cases}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})},&1\leq p\leq p_{1},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\min\left\{\lambda^{1/2}\mu^{\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n-1}{2p}},\lambda^{n-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{n-1}{p}}\right\},&p_{1}\leq p\leq p_{0},\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle\min\left\{\lambda^{\frac{2n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{p}}\mu^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}},\lambda^{n-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{n-1}{p}}\right\},&p_{0}\leq p\leq 2.\end{cases}

In the last two ranges, if
\mu\leq\lambda^{2}
, then the minimum takes the first term.

If
(n,p)=(3,2)
, then

(1.5)

\mathcal{E}(n,2,p;\lambda,\mu)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log\lambda}.

The logarithmic factor is sharp.

If
n=2,3
and
(n,p)\neq(3,2)
, then

(1.6)

\mathcal{E}(n,2,p;\lambda,\mu)=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}.

Moreover, the estimates are sharp.

Having established the bilinear case, we now extend these results to products of an arbitrary number of functions. The following theorem provides sharp multilinear estimates for
k\geq 3
frequency scales in the range
1\leq p\leq 2
.

(Multilinear estimates for
p\leq 2
)

If

\mathcal{E}=\begin{cases}\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot\min\Big\{\lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{3n-2}{2}-\frac{n+1}{p}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}},\ \lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{2n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{2p}}\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n-1}{2p}}\Big\},&1\leq p\leq p_{2},\\

In the first range, if
\lambda_{k-3}\lambda_{k-1}\leq\lambda_{k-2}^{2}
then the minimum takes the first term. In the last two ranges, if
\lambda_{k-2}\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k-1}^{2}
then the minimum takes the first term.

If
n=4

p_{3}=10/9
and

\mathcal{E}=\begin{cases}\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\min\Big\{\lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{15}{4}-\frac{5}{2p}}\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{7}{4}-\frac{3}{2p}},\ \lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{7}{2}-\frac{5}{2p}}\lambda_{k-2}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{2p}}\Big\},&1\leq p\leq\frac{10}{9},\\

In the first two ranges, if
\lambda_{k-3}\lambda_{k-1}\leq\lambda_{k-2}^{2}
then the minimum takes the first term. In the last two ranges, if
\lambda_{k-2}\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k-1}^{2}
then the minimum takes the first term.

If
n=3
, then
p_{0}=p_{1}=2
,
p_{2}=p_{3}=4/3
, and

\mathcal{E}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{E}_{0}\sqrt{\log\lambda_{k-2}}\sqrt{\log\lambda_{k-3}},&p=1,\\
\mathcal{E}_{0}\sqrt{\log\lambda_{k-2}}&1<p\leq\frac{4}{3},\\
\mathcal{E}_{0}&\frac{4}{3}<p<2,\\
\mathcal{E}_{0}\sqrt{\log\lambda_{k-1}},&p=2,\end{cases}

with

\mathcal{E}_{0}=\begin{cases}\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}\cdot\lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{5}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}\,\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\lambda_{k-1}^{1-\frac{1}{p}},&1\leq p\leq\frac{4}{3},\\
\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}\cdot\lambda_{k-2}^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\,\lambda_{k-1}^{1-\frac{1}{p}},&\frac{4}{3}<p\leq 2,\end{cases}

The logarithmic factors are sharp.

If

(Multilinear spectral cluster estimates)
.

1.2.
Background and relation to earlier work

The linear versions of our oscillatory integral estimates
(\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{k})
go back to the foundational work of Hörmander
[

,

, Carleson–Sjölin
[

\|T_{\lambda,\phi}^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(p)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})},

where

(1.11)

\sigma(p)=\begin{cases}\frac{n-1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right),&2\leq p\leq p_{c},\\
\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p},&p_{c}\leq p\leq\infty,\end{cases}\qquad p_{c}=\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}.

This circle of results is closely connected with the restriction problem, curvature-driven oscillatory integrals, and multilinear harmonic analysis; see, for example, Wolff
[
22
]
, Tao
[
19
,

,

]
, Bennett–Carbery–Tao
[
1
]
, Lee
[
14
]
, Bourgain–Guth
[

, and Hani
[

.

The corresponding linear spectral cluster estimates are due to Sogge
[

,

]
. If
(M,g)
is a closed smooth Riemannian manifold,
\Delta
is the Laplace operator associated to the metric
g
,
\chi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\chi_{\lambda}=\chi(\sqrt{-\Delta}-\lambda)
, then

(1.12)

\|\chi_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(p)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\qquad 2\leq p\leq\infty.

Multilinear

estimates for eigenfunctions were first established by Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov
[

,
4
,
5
]
. In particular, for
1\ll\lambda\leq\mu
they proved

(1.13)

\|\chi_{\lambda}f\,\chi_{\mu}g\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\mathcal{B}(n,\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(M)},

where

\mathcal{B}(n,\lambda)=\begin{cases}\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}},&n\geq 4,\\
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log\lambda},&n=3,\\
\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}},&n=2.\end{cases}

Except for the logarithmic loss in dimension three, these estimates are sharp on the round sphere by the standard highest-weight and zonal examples. For the three-dimensional logarithmic loss, Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov pointed out that they have the same difficulty as the endpoint Strichartz estimates on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

[
5
, Remark 2.13]
. Deng–Zhang–Zhao
[
7
]
recently removed this loss on the round sphere
S^{3}
by using the compact Lie group structure of
SU(2)
.

Our approach is different and applies to arbitrary closed three-dimensional Riemannian manifolds. We prove log-free endpoint bilinear spectral cluster estimates on every closed three-manifold. At the same time, we show that ellipticity of the Riemannian distance phase function is essential: a hyperbolic Carleson–Sjölin model has sharp endpoint examples with the logarithmic factor. Thus the paper identifies the precise geometric mechanism behind the endpoint logarithm. This provides a robust harmonic-analytic tool for studying critical problems of dispersive equations on curved spaces.

Beyond this endpoint issue, we establish sharp multilinear
L^{p}
spectral cluster estimates for all
p\geq 1
. The range
1\leq p\leq 2
is substantially more delicate than the range
p>2
, because the sharp lower bounds are not generated by a single family of spherical harmonics. We show that the extremal behavior is obtained by combining six model packet profiles—beam, beam block, envelope, envelope train, zonal, and zonal train. See Sections
9
and
10
.

1.3.
New phenomena in the range
p\leq 2

The estimates in Theorem
1.1
are substantially more subtle in the range
p\leq 2
. For
p>2
, the estimates are closely related to the classical linear restriction and spectral cluster theory, where the sharp examples are already well understood. In this regime, the multilinear estimates are largely governed by the same concentration mechanisms that appear in the linear problem.

New phenomena emerge once
p\leq 2
. Observe that if one sets
\lambda=\mu
and
f=g
in (
1.4
), then the bilinear estimate reduces to an
L^{2p}
estimate for a single oscillatory integral operator. More generally, if all frequency scales are equal and all functions coincide, then (
1.7
) becomes an
L^{kp}
estimate. Thus the multilinear problem is naturally connected to the linear theory at exponent
kp
. The classical theory largely concerns the range
kp\geq 2k
, corresponding to
p\geq 2
. In that regime the extremizing mechanisms are already known. However, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
extend into the range

2\leq kp\leq 2k,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\frac{2}{k}\leq p\leq 2,

where the linear theory provides much less guidance. In particular, there is no a priori reason to expect the same extremizing configurations that govern the classical
L^{q}
estimates to remain dominant. As we shall see, this range exhibits new multilinear phenomena arising from interactions between different concentration profiles.

For eigenfunctions on the sphere, the classical extremizers are of two distinct types. The first are the zonal harmonics, which concentrate near a point. The second are the highest-weight harmonics (or Gaussian beams), which concentrate along a geodesic. For linear estimates, these two families dominate in different exponent ranges: zonal harmonics are extremal at larger exponents, while Gaussian beams become extremal at smaller exponents. The transition occurs at the critical exponent

p_{c}=\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}.

In the multilinear setting, however, one is no longer restricted to a single extremizing profile. The product structure allows different factors to exhibit different concentration patterns simultaneously. Consequently, a much richer collection of sharp examples becomes available. These mixed configurations have no analogue in the linear theory. Their interaction produces several competing lower bounds, and different configurations dominate in different regions of the
(p,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
-parameter space.

One of the main messages of this paper is that the threshold exponents

p_{0}=\frac{n+1}{n-1},\qquad p_{1}=\frac{n-1}{n-2},\qquad p_{2}=\frac{n+1}{n},\qquad p_{3}=\frac{2(n+1)}{3(n-1)}.

arise from transitions between these competing extremal geometries. Heuristically, the endpoints
p_{0}=p_{c}/2
and
p_{3}=p_{c}/3
correspond to the classical transition between zonal-type and Gaussian-beam-type behavior inherited from the linear theory. In contrast, the endpoints
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
reflect a genuinely multilinear phenomenon arising from interactions between distinct concentration patterns. The piecewise structure of
\mathcal{E}(n,k,p;\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
shows that no single family of extremizers can account for the entire range
1\leq p\leq 2
. Table

This phenomenon becomes even more pronounced for the
k
-linear estimates of Theorem
1.2
. In contrast to the linear theory, where only the zonal and Gaussian beam examples need to be considered, the multilinear problem requires one to analyze all possible combinations of these concentration profiles among the different factors. The various endpoints appearing in Theorem

To the best of our knowledge, this systematic interaction between zonal and Gaussian beam profiles has not previously appeared in the literature on restriction estimates, oscillatory integrals, or spectral cluster bounds. 1.4.
Log-free endpoint estimate

We now explain the proof idea behind the log-free endpoint estimate for three-dimensional spectral clusters. The estimate is

(1.14)

\|\chi_{\lambda}f\,\chi_{\mu}g\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(M)},\qquad 1\ll\lambda\leq\mu,

for every closed three-dimensional Riemannian manifold. This is the only point in the spectral cluster theory where the general oscillatory-integral estimates of Theorem
1.1
are not sufficient: for arbitrary three-dimensional Carleson–Sjölin phases the endpoint bound has a sharp factor
\sqrt{\log\lambda}
. Section
2
shows that this logarithm is forced by hyperbolic asymptotic directions. The spectral cluster phase is different. It is the Riemannian distance phase, and its ellipticity can be exploited in a way that avoids the dyadic summation which produces the logarithm.

The first ingredient is a common-phase bilinear estimate. Suppose two operators have the same elliptic Carleson–Sjölin phase
\varphi(x,\xi)
in dimension three, but possibly different amplitudes and frequencies
1\leq\alpha\leq\beta
:

T^{b}_{\nu}h(x)=\nu\int e^{i\nu\varphi(x,\xi)}b(x,\xi,\nu)h(\xi)\,d\xi.

Proposition
3.2
proves

\|T^{b_{1}}_{\alpha}f\,T^{b_{2}}_{\beta}g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\alpha^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

The proof linearizes the product and writes
\sigma=\alpha/\beta
. For the pair of frequency variables
(\xi,\eta)
one introduces

v=\eta+\sigma\xi,\qquad u=\frac{\sigma|\xi-\eta|^{2}}{2(1+\sigma)},\qquad\omega=\frac{\xi-\eta}{|\xi-\eta|}.

For fixed
\omega
, the variables
(u,v)
are three-dimensional. The definiteness of the second fundamental form implies that the map

(u,v)\longmapsto\nabla_{x}\{\sigma\varphi(x,\xi(u,v))+\varphi(x,\eta(u,v))\}

is uniformly nondegenerate. Hörmander’s
L^{2}
oscillatory integral theorem then gives a gain
\beta^{-3/2}
in these three variables, and the Jacobian of the above change of variables converts this into the factor
\alpha^{1/2}
. No angular-scale decomposition is used, and hence no logarithmic loss appears.

The second ingredient is that the distance phases occurring in Sogge’s spectral cluster parametrix can be reduced to this common-phase situation. After localizing in a small normal-coordinate ball and writing the integration variable in polar coordinates
y=\exp_{o}(r\omega)
, a frozen radial piece of the spectral projector has the form

T_{\lambda,r}h(x)=\lambda\int e^{-i\lambda d_{g}(x,Y_{r}(\xi))}b_{r}(x,\xi,\lambda)h(\xi)\,d\xi,\qquad Y_{r}(\xi)=\exp_{o}(r\Omega(\xi)).

The phases for different radii
r
are not literally the same, so Proposition
3.2
cannot be applied directly. We introduce a larger outer radius
R
and the outer phase

\Phi_{R}(x,\theta)=-d_{g}(x,Y_{R}(\theta)).

The scaled normal-coordinate expansion for the distance function and Gauss’s lemma show that
\Phi_{R}
is an elliptic Carleson–Sjölin phase on each sufficiently small cap. Stationary phase in the intermediate angular variable then gives the outer-radius factorization

T_{\lambda,r}=E_{\lambda,R}^{A_{r}}U_{\lambda,r}+R_{\lambda,r}.

Here
E_{\lambda,R}^{A_{r}}
has the common outer phase
\Phi_{R}
, the angular propagator
U_{\lambda,r}
is uniformly
L^{2}
-bounded, and the remainder
R_{\lambda,r}
is harmless. Thus two frozen pieces with radii
r_{1},r_{2}
are compared only after they have both been propagated to the same outer sphere. The common-phase estimate then yields the uniform frozen bound

\|T_{\alpha,r_{1}}f\,T_{\beta,r_{2}}g\|_{L^{2}(U)}\lesssim\alpha^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2},\qquad 1\leq\alpha\leq\beta.

There are two geometric configurations which require separate bookkeeping. If the two angular caps are separated from both the parallel and antipodal relations, the corresponding covector surfaces are uniformly transverse, and a direct Hörmander
L^{2}
argument gives the same frozen estimate. If the caps are antipodal, one uses signed phases
\Phi_{r}^{\sigma}(x,\xi)=-\sigma d_{g}(x,Y_{r}^{\sigma}(\xi))
,
\sigma=\pm 1
. Section
6
proves the signed factorization and verifies the exact critical-value identity needed to reduce the antipodal case again to the same positive outer phase.

Finally, Sogge’s spectral cluster parametrix writes
\chi_{\lambda}=S_{\lambda}+R_{\lambda}
, with a rapidly smoothing remainder. The main term
S_{\lambda}
is decomposed into finitely many angular caps and a bounded interval of radii. The frozen two-radius estimate is uniform in the radii, while the polar-coordinate decomposition preserves the
L^{2}
norm after summing over caps and integrating in
r
. Minkowski’s inequality and Cauchy’s inequality in the radial variables therefore assemble the frozen estimates into (
1.14
).

2.
Sharpness of the logarithm in three dimensions

In this section, we prove that the logarithmic factors in three-dimensional multilinear oscillatory integral estimates cannot be removed if we only assume the Carleson–Sjölin condition. The logarithmic factors come from hyperbolic asymptotic directions. This example suggests that one should exploit the ellipticity of the phase to prove log-free estimates (see Section
3
).

2.1.
The bilinear endpoint

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}(s,z,\xi)=z\cdot\xi+s\xi_{1}\xi_{2},\qquad y=(s,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

and let
b\in C_{0}^{\infty}
be equal to one for
0\leq s\leq 1
,
|z|\leq c_{0}
, and
|\xi|\leq c_{0}
, with
c_{0}>0
small. Define

T_{\nu,\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}}h(y)=\nu\int e^{i\nu\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}(y,\xi)}b(y,\xi)h(\xi)\,d\xi.

Then, for every large
\lambda
, with
\mu=\lambda^{2}
, there are functions
f_{\lambda},g_{\mu}
with

\|f_{\lambda}\|_{2}=\|g_{\mu}\|_{2}=1
such that

(2.1)

\|T_{\lambda,\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}}f_{\lambda}\,T_{\mu,\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}}g_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\gtrsim\lambda^{1/2}\sqrt{\log\lambda}.

Proof.

For the model phase,

P(y,\xi)=\nabla_{y}\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}(y,\xi)=(\xi_{1}\xi_{2},\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),

and the second fundamental form
\text{II}[\omega,\omega]
is a nonzero multiple of

\omega_{1}\omega_{2}
. Thus it is Carleson–Sjölin but not elliptic. It has the two asymptotic directions parallel to the coordinate axes. The example below stacks wave packets along these two directions.

Let

J=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{10}\log_{2}\lambda\right\rfloor.

Choose a small number
c>0
, depending only on the amplitude support. For
1\leq j\leq J
set

R_{j}=\left\{\xi:2^{j}\lambda^{-1/2}\leq\xi_{1}\leq(1+c)2^{j}\lambda^{-1/2},0\leq\xi_{2}\leq c2^{-j}\lambda^{-1/2}\right\}.

The rectangles are disjoint, lie in
\{|\xi|\leq c_{0}\}
for
\lambda
large, and satisfy

|R_{j}|\approx\lambda^{-1}
and

|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\lesssim\lambda^{-1},\qquad\xi\in R_{j}.

Let
f_{j}=|R_{j}|^{-1/2}{\bf 1}_{R_{j}}
and

f_{\lambda}=J^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}.

Then
\|f_{\lambda}\|_{2}=1
. For
0\leq s\leq 1
and
|z_{1}|,|z_{2}|\leq c\lambda^{-1}
, the phase variation of

\lambda(z\cdot\xi+s\xi_{1}\xi_{2})
on each
R_{j}
is bounded by a small absolute constant if
c
is chosen small. Hence all integrals have the same sign after taking real parts, and

(2.2)

|T_{\lambda,\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}}f_{\lambda}(s,z)|\gtrsim\lambda J^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{J}|R_{j}|^{1/2}\gtrsim\lambda^{1/2}\sqrt{J}.

For the high-frequency factor take

G=\{\eta:0\leq\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\leq c\mu^{-1/2}\}=\{\eta:0\leq\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\leq c\lambda^{-1}\},\qquad g_{\mu}=|G|^{-1/2}{\bf 1}_{G}.

Then
\|g_{\mu}\|_{2}=1
and, on the same box
0\leq s\leq 1
,
|z_{1}|,|z_{2}|\leq c\lambda^{-1}
, the phase variation of

\mu(z\cdot\eta+s\eta_{1}\eta_{2})
is also bounded by a small constant. Therefore

(2.3)

|T_{\mu,\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}}g_{\mu}(s,z)|\gtrsim\mu|G|^{1/2}\approx\lambda.

The box

Q_{\lambda}=\{0\leq s\leq 1,\ |z_{1}|,|z_{2}|\leq c\lambda^{-1}\}

has measure
|Q_{\lambda}|\approx\lambda^{-2}
. Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.3
),

\|T_{\lambda,\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}}f_{\lambda}T_{\mu,\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}}g_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}}\geq\|T_{\lambda,\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}}f_{\lambda}T_{\mu,\Phi_{\mathrm{hyp}}}g_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{\lambda})}\gtrsim(\lambda^{1/2}\sqrt{J})\lambda|Q_{\lambda}|^{1/2}\gtrsim\lambda^{1/2}\sqrt{J}.

Since
J\approx\log\lambda
, this proves (
2.1
). Replacing the characteristic functions above by nonnegative smooth cutoffs changes only the constants.
∎

Common-phase bilinear estimate

In this section, we prove a simplified version of the log-free bilinear estimates in three dimensions by exploiting the ellipticity of the phase. We will reduce the general version to this simple version by factorization (see Section

Lemma 3.1

(Hörmander
[
12
]
)

Let
K_{1}\Subset W\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

is real-valued and that

are bounded in

in the

. Suppose also that

is supported in
K_{1}
in the
x
-variable, uniformly for

.
Assume that for all
x\in W
and all

(3.1)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\psi(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\psi(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}|y-y^{\prime}|,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\gamma}\{\nabla_{x}\psi(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\psi(x,y^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle=O_{\gamma}(|y-y^{\prime}|)\qquad\text{for every multi-index }\gamma.

Then

S_{\lambda}h(x)=\int e^{i\lambda\psi(x,y)}c(x,y)h(y)\,dy

satisfies

\|S_{\lambda}h\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\lambda^{-3/2}\|h\|_{L^{2}(K_{0})},\qquad\lambda\geq 1.

Proof.

Choose
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(W)
with
\chi=1
on
K_{1}
. The kernel of

is

The trivial estimate gives
|K(y,y^{\prime})|\leq C
. If

Then

Schur’s test in the three-dimensional

, proving the claim.
∎

Let

Proposition 3.2

(Common-phase bilinear estimate)
.

\sigma=\alpha/\beta\in(0,1]

\frac{v_{t}}{1+\sigma}\in V_{0},\qquad\rho_{t}^{2}=(1-t)\rho^{2}+t(\rho^{\prime})^{2}\leq(2\delta)^{2},

and therefore

\xi(y_{t},\omega)=\frac{v_{t}+\rho_{t}\omega}{1+\sigma},\qquad\eta(y_{t},\omega)=\frac{v_{t}-\sigma\rho_{t}\omega}{1+\sigma}

belong to
V_{1}
.

For every smooth vector-valued
G(\xi)
, the following identities follow
from the inverse formulas and the chain rule. Since
\rho
is independent
of
v
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{v_{j}}\{\sigma G(\xi)+G(\eta)\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma\partial_{\xi_{j}}G(\xi)+\partial_{\xi_{j}}G(\eta)}{1+\sigma}.

For the
u
-derivative, using

\partial_{u}\rho=\frac{1+\sigma}{\sigma\rho},\qquad\xi-\eta=\rho\omega,

we have, for
u>0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{u}\{\sigma G(\xi)+G(\eta)\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\{DG(\xi)-DG(\eta)\}[\omega]}{\rho}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}G(\eta+s\rho\omega)[\omega,\omega]\,ds.

Both derivative formulas have continuous one-sided extensions to
u=0
. Writing

\xi_{t}=\xi(y_{t},\omega),\qquad\eta_{t}=\eta(y_{t},\omega),

we have

D_{(u,v)}R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y_{t})=\bigl(\partial_{u}R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y_{t}),\partial_{v_{1}}R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y_{t}),\partial_{v_{2}}R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y_{t})\bigr).

Applying (
3.3
)–(
3.4
) to
G=P(x,\cdot)
, we obtain

\partial_{u}R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y_{t})=\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}P\bigl(x,(1-r)\eta_{t}+r\xi_{t}\bigr)[\omega,\omega]\,dr=\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}P(x,\xi_{0})[\omega,\omega]+e_{0}(x,y_{t}),

and, for
j=1,2
,

\partial_{v_{j}}R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y_{t})=\frac{\sigma A_{j}(x,\xi_{t})+A_{j}(x,\eta_{t})}{1+\sigma}=A_{j}(x,\xi_{0})+e_{j}(x,y_{t}).

Since
\xi_{t},\eta_{t}\in V_{1}
, and since

(1-r)\eta_{t}+r\xi_{t}\in V_{1}
for
0\leq r\leq 1
, the choice of
V_{1}

gives

|e_{0}(x,y_{t})|+|e_{1}(x,y_{t})|+|e_{2}(x,y_{t})|\ll c_{0}.

Thus, we get

D_{(u,v)}R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y_{t})=M(x,\omega)+E(x,y_{t}),

where

E(x,y_{t})=\bigl(e_{0},e_{1},e_{2}\bigr)
.
Hence, by the mean value theorem along the
segment
y_{t}
,

R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y^{\prime})-R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}D_{(u,v)}R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y_{t})(y^{\prime}-y)\,dt,

and therefore

|R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y^{\prime})-R_{\sigma,\omega}(x,y)|\gtrsim c_{0}|y^{\prime}-y|,

which gives the required estimate for Lemma
3.1
.

Now using the Jacobian,
B_{\alpha,\beta}F
becomes

B_{\alpha,\beta}F(x)=\frac{\beta^{2}}{1+\sigma}\int_{S^{1}}S_{\beta,\sigma,\omega}H_{\sigma,\omega}(x)\,d\sigma_{S^{1}}(\omega),

where

S_{\beta,\sigma,\omega}h(x)=\int e^{i\beta\Psi_{\sigma,\omega}(x,u,v)}c_{\sigma,\omega}(x,u,v)h(u,v)\,du\,dv

and
H_{\sigma,\omega}(u,v)=F(\xi(u,v),\eta(u,v))
. Lemma
3.1
gives

\|S_{\beta,\sigma,\omega}h\|_{2}\leq C\beta^{-3/2}\|h\|_{2}.

Minkowski’s inequality, Cauchy’s inequality in
\omega
, and the Jacobian yield

\displaystyle\|B_{\alpha,\beta}F\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\beta^{1/2}}{1+\sigma}\left(\int_{S^{1}}\int|H_{\sigma,\omega}(u,v)|^{2}\,du\,dv\,d\sigma_{S^{1}}(\omega)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=C\frac{\beta^{1/2}}{1+\sigma}\bigl(\sigma(1+\sigma)\bigr)^{1/2}\|F\|_{2}=C\alpha^{1/2}\|F\|_{2}.

Taking
F=f\otimes g
and using density proves the proposition.
∎

4.
Scaled geometry of the shell

In this section, we obtain some useful estimates for the Riemannian distance function, which is exactly the phase in Sogge’s spectral cluster parametrix for Laplace eigenfunctions on smooth closed manifolds (see Lemma
7.2
). These estimates will be used to prove the factorization in Section
5
by stationary phase.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth closed three-dimensional Riemannian manifold and fix a point
o\in M
. Consider a strongly geodesically convex normal coordinate ball centered at
o
. In these coordinates,
o=0
and

\exp_{o}
is a diffeomorphism on the domain
B(0,\varepsilon_{*})\subset T_{o}M
.

Fix
C_{0}\geq 2
. After choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, set

I=[\varepsilon/C_{0},C_{0}\varepsilon],\qquad R=4C_{0}\varepsilon.

Thus
R-r\geq 3C_{0}\varepsilon
for every
r\in I
. Let

\Omega:V\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\longrightarrow S^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

be a smooth angular chart near a unit vector
\omega_{0}
. We fix one compact angular support
K_{2}
and one convex open
V_{0}
, called a cap region, with

K_{2}\Subset V_{0}\Subset V.

All amplitudes are supported in
K_{2}
in the angular variable. The fixed inclusion
K_{2}\Subset V_{0}
provides the angular margin used below for the small constants and for the inverse critical map.

For
s\in I\cup\{R\}
, define

(4.1)

Y_{s}(\theta)=\exp_{o}(s\Omega(\theta)),\qquad\Phi_{s}(x,\theta)=-d_{g}(x,Y_{s}(\theta)).

For
R>r
, we refer to the annular region between the geodesic spheres
Y_{r}
and
Y_{R}
as the
shell region
. The
x
-support lies in

U=\{x:|x|<\tau\varepsilon\},

where
\tau>0
is chosen small after
C_{0}
and the cap are fixed. We fix a compact amplitude support and a slightly larger open set

K_{1}\Subset U_{0}\Subset U,

and take all amplitudes supported in
K_{1}
in the
x
variable. All geometric constructions below are made uniformly on the compact set
\overline{U_{0}}
.

For
r\in I
and
\lambda\geq 1
, let

(4.2)

T^{a}_{\lambda,r}h(x)=\lambda\int_{K_{2}}e^{i\lambda\Phi_{r}(x,\xi)}a_{r}(x,\xi,\lambda)h(\xi)\,d\xi.

The amplitudes
a_{r}(x,\xi,\lambda)
form a bounded
C^{\infty}
order-zero family, uniformly in
r\in I
and
\lambda\geq 1
, and are supported in
K_{1}\times K_{2}
. We regard
a_{r}
as a smooth zero-extension to
U\times V_{0}
. Thus later expressions such as
a_{r}(x,\Xi_{r}(x,\theta),\lambda)
are well-defined.

4.1.
Scaled normal-coordinate estimates

Consider the normal coordinates centered at
o
as before, and let
z=\varepsilon Z\in B(0,\varepsilon_{*})\subset T_{o}M
, where
B(0,\varepsilon_{*})
is the strong geodesically convex ball discussed above. For

0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, set

g_{\varepsilon,ij}(Z):=g_{ij}(\varepsilon Z),\qquad\text{ where }Z\in B(0,\varepsilon_{*}).

If
z(t)=\varepsilon Z(t)
, then length and distance scale as

\operatorname{Length}_{g}(z)=\varepsilon\operatorname{Length}_{g_{\varepsilon}}(Z),\qquad d_{g}(\varepsilon P,\varepsilon Q)=\varepsilon d_{g_{\varepsilon}}(P,Q).

We denote by
|Z|
the length of
Z
in the Euclidean metric. The normal-coordinate expansion gives, on every fixed ball,

g_{\varepsilon}(Z)=I_{3}+\varepsilon^{2}h_{\varepsilon}(Z),

where the family
h_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
C^{\infty}
.
Here
I_{3}
denotes the
3\times 3
Euclidean identity matrix in these coordinates.
Consequently, on every fixed bounded set, the Euler–Lagrange equation for

g_{\varepsilon}
-geodesics is a smooth
O(\varepsilon^{2})
perturbation of the Euclidean
geodesic equation:

\ddot{Z}=\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(Z,\dot{Z}).

Here
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
C^{\infty}
on bounded subsets of
(Z,\dot{Z})
.
All constants below are uniform for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. They may depend on
\varepsilon_{*}
and on finitely many derivatives of
g
. We first show some fundamental results in Riemannian geometry.

Lemma 4.1

(Distance in scaled normal coordinates)
.

Fix
A,d_{0}>0
and suppose

P,Q\in B(0,A),\qquad|P-Q|\geq d_{0}.

For all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
,

d_{g}(\varepsilon P,\varepsilon Q)=\varepsilon|P-Q|+\varepsilon^{3}E_{\varepsilon}(P,Q),

where
E_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
C^{\infty}
on the separated set. Equivalently,

d_{g_{\varepsilon}}(P,Q)=|P-Q|+\varepsilon^{2}E_{\varepsilon}(P,Q).

The same estimate remains true after composing
P,Q
with bounded
C^{\infty}
parametrizations whose images stay in the separated set
|P-Q|\geq d_{0}
.

Proof.

For fixed
\varepsilon
, interpolate between the Euclidean metric and
g_{\varepsilon}
by

g_{\varepsilon,u}:=I_{3}+uh_{\varepsilon},\qquad 0\leq u\leq\varepsilon^{2}.

These metrics are bounded in
C^{\infty}
, uniformly in
\varepsilon
and
u
, and
are uniformly close to the Euclidean metric on every fixed ball. Hence, on the
separated compact set

|P|,|Q|\leq A,\qquad|P-Q|\geq d_{0},

the minimizing geodesic, and therefore its length, depends smoothly on

(P,Q,u)
, with bounds uniform in
\varepsilon
, since we assume
B(0,A)
is strongly geodesically convex.

Because
g_{\varepsilon,0}=I_{3}
,

d_{g_{\varepsilon,0}}(P,Q)=|P-Q|.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus in
u
,

d_{g_{\varepsilon}}(P,Q)-|P-Q|=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{2}}\partial_{u}d_{g_{\varepsilon,u}}(P,Q)\,du=\varepsilon^{2}E_{\varepsilon}(P,Q),

where

E_{\varepsilon}(P,Q):=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{u}d_{g_{\varepsilon,s\varepsilon^{2}}}(P,Q)\,ds

is bounded in
C^{\infty}
on the separated set. Finally,

d_{g}(\varepsilon P,\varepsilon Q)=\varepsilon d_{g_{\varepsilon}}(P,Q),

which gives

d_{g}(\varepsilon P,\varepsilon Q)=\varepsilon|P-Q|+\varepsilon^{3}E_{\varepsilon}(P,Q).

The parametrized version follows from the chain rule.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
A,T>0
. Let
Z_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v)
be the
g_{\varepsilon}
-geodesic satisfying

Z_{\varepsilon}(0;P,v)=P,\qquad\dot{Z}_{\varepsilon}(0;P,v)=v,\qquad|P|+|v|\leq A.

Then, for
|t|\leq T
,

Z_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v)=P+tv+\varepsilon^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v),\qquad\dot{Z}_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v)=v+\varepsilon^{2}\dot{R}_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v),

where
R_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
C^{\infty}
on the relevant compact set.
Moreover, if
X,Y
remain in a fixed bounded set and
|Y-X|\geq d_{0}>0
, then
the terminal
g_{\varepsilon}
-unit tangent vector at
Y
of the minimizing

g_{\varepsilon}
-geodesic from
X
to
Y
, pointing from
X
to
Y
, is

\frac{Y-X}{|Y-X|}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

with bounded
C^{\infty}
dependence on the parameters.

Proof.

The rescaled geodesic equation is

\ddot{Z}=\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(Z,\dot{Z}),

where
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
C^{\infty}
on bounded sets. Its
integral form gives

Z_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v)=P+tv+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(Z_{\varepsilon}(s;P,v),\dot{Z}_{\varepsilon}(s;P,v))\,ds.

Standard ODE estimates on the fixed interval
|t|\leq T
give

Z_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v)=P+tv+\varepsilon^{2}R_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v),\qquad\dot{Z}_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v)=v+\varepsilon^{2}\dot{R}_{\varepsilon}(t;P,v),

with
R_{\varepsilon}
bounded in
C^{\infty}
for
|P|+|v|\leq A
.

For the terminal tangent estimate, consider the time-one endpoint map

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(X,v):=Z_{\varepsilon}(1;X,v).

Then Lemma
4.1
gives

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(X,v)=X+v+O(\varepsilon^{2}),\qquad D_{v}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}=I_{3}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

By the inverse function theorem, up to reparametrization, the initial velocity of the geodesic from
X
to
Y
is

v_{\varepsilon}(X,Y)=Y-X+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Therefore, we have the terminal velocity

\dot{Z}_{\varepsilon}(1;X,v_{\varepsilon}(X,Y))=Y-X+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Since
g_{\varepsilon}=I_{3}+O(\varepsilon^{2})
and
|Y-X|\geq d_{0}
, normalizing this vector with
respect to
g_{\varepsilon}
changes the Euclidean normalization only by
O(\varepsilon^{2})
.
This proves the stated terminal tangent estimate.
∎

4.2.
Shell phase and propagated phase

As before, the angular variables lie in the
single chart

K_{2}\Subset V_{0}\Subset V,

and all geometric estimates below are made on the compact set

\overline{U_{0}}
, while amplitudes are supported in
K_{1}
. We shall shrink

\tau
,
\varepsilon
, and the convex cap
V_{0}
only finitely many times,
always preserving these fixed compact inclusions.

It is convenient in this section to use scaled variables. Write

s=\varepsilon\rho,\qquad\rho\in[1/C_{0},C_{0}]\cup\{\rho_{R}\},\qquad\rho_{R}:=R/\varepsilon=4C_{0},\qquad x=\varepsilon X.

For
r=\varepsilon\rho\in I
, define the shell phase and the propagated phase by

S_{r}(\theta,\xi)=d_{g}(Y_{R}(\theta),Y_{r}(\xi)),\qquad\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)=\Phi_{R}(x,\theta)+S_{r}(\theta,\xi).

Here
\Phi
is defined in (
4.1
).
The only distance input used in this section is the scaled expansion from
Lemma
4.1
: on every separated compact set,

d_{g}(\varepsilon P,\varepsilon Q)=\varepsilon|P-Q|+\varepsilon^{3}E_{\varepsilon}(P,Q),

where
E_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
C^{\infty}
on the relevant compact set.

Lemma 4.3
.

After shrinking
\tau
,
\varepsilon
, and the convex angular cap
V_{0}\Subset V
, the following hold uniformly for

s\in I\cup\{R\},\qquad x=\varepsilon X\in U,\qquad\theta,\eta\in V_{0}.

(i)

In the
C^{2}
topology with respect to the variable
\theta
,

(4.3)

\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(\varepsilon X,\theta)=\Omega(\theta)+O(\tau+\varepsilon^{2}).

(ii)

The map
\theta\mapsto\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(x,\theta)
is bi-Lipschitz on

V_{0}
:

|\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(x,\theta)-\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(x,\eta)|\geq c|\theta-\eta|.

Moreover, the corresponding
x
-differentiated upper bounds hold:

(4.4)

|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}\{\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(x,\theta)-\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(x,\eta)\}|\leq C_{\gamma,\varepsilon}|\theta-\eta|\quad\text{for every multi-index }\gamma.

(iii)

The outer phase
\Phi_{R}
satisfies the elliptic Carleson–Sjölin
condition on
U\times V_{0}
:

\operatorname{rank}(\partial_{\theta}\nabla_{x}\Phi_{R}(x,\theta))=2,

and the surface
\{\nabla_{x}\Phi_{R}(x,\theta):\theta\in V_{0}\}
has
definite second fundamental form.

Proof.

For
s=\varepsilon\rho
, put
P=X
and
Q=\rho\Omega(\theta)
. Since

|X|\leq\tau
and
\rho\geq 1/C_{0}
, choosing
\tau
small gives

|X-\rho\Omega(\theta)|\geq\frac{1}{2C_{0}}.

Thus

\varepsilon^{-1}\Phi_{s}(\varepsilon X,\theta)=-|X-\rho\Omega(\theta)|+\varepsilon^{2}E_{\varepsilon}(X,\rho\Omega(\theta)).

Since
\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(\varepsilon X,\theta)
equals the
X
-gradient of the scaled
phase,

\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(\varepsilon X,\theta)=\frac{\rho\Omega(\theta)-X}{|\rho\Omega(\theta)-X|}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

The first term is
\Omega(\theta)+O(\tau)
, proving
(
4.3
).

Let

n_{\rho,X}(\theta)=\frac{\rho\Omega(\theta)-X}{|\rho\Omega(\theta)-X|}.

Let
\theta=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
be local coordinates on
S^{2}
. Then

\partial_{\theta_{a}}n_{\rho,X}(\theta)=\frac{\rho}{|\rho\Omega-X|}(I-n_{\rho,X}\otimes n_{\rho,X})\partial_{\theta_{a}}\Omega,\quad a=1,2.

At
X=0
this is exactly
\partial_{\theta_{a}}\Omega
, since
\Omega\cdot\Omega=1,\ \Omega\cdot\partial_{\theta_{a}}\Omega=0
. Hence

\partial_{\theta}\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(\varepsilon X,\theta)=D\Omega(\theta)+O(\tau+\varepsilon^{2}).

After shrinking the angular cap and then
\tau,\varepsilon
, the bi-Lipschitz estimate
follows from the corresponding estimate for the chart
\Omega
. The derivative
bound (
4.4
) follows from

\partial_{x}^{\gamma}=\varepsilon^{-|\gamma|}\partial_{X}^{\gamma}
and the mean-value theorem
in
\theta
on the separated set.

For
s=R
, the surface
\theta\mapsto\nabla_{x}\Phi_{R}(x,\theta)
is a

C^{2}
-small perturbation of the unit sphere chart
\theta\mapsto\Omega(\theta)
.
The rank condition and definiteness of the second fundamental form therefore
persist after the same shrinking.
∎

4.3.
The Euclidean shell model

Set
\rho_{R}=4C_{0}
, and let
\rho\in[1/C_{0},C_{0}]
. In this section, we consider the shell region between the radii
\rho
and
\rho_{R}
in Euclidean distance. More precisely, define

\displaystyle F_{\rho,X}(\theta,\xi)

\displaystyle:=-|X-\rho_{R}\Omega(\theta)|+|\rho_{R}\Omega(\theta)-\rho\Omega(\xi)|,

\displaystyle G_{\rho}(\theta,\xi)

\displaystyle:=|\rho_{R}\Omega(\theta)-\rho\Omega(\xi)|.

Let
G_{S}(\xi)=D\Omega(\xi)^{T}D\Omega(\xi)
be the spherical metric in the chart. For a square matrix
A
, we write

\operatorname{Sym}A=\frac{1}{2}(A+A^{T}).

Lemma 4.4
.

After possibly shrinking
V_{0}\Subset V
, there are numbers
c_{1}>0
,
\tau_{0}>0
, and constants

c,C>0
such that, whenever

\rho\in[1/C_{0},C_{0}],\qquad|X|\leq\tau_{0},\qquad\xi\in V_{0},\qquad\theta\in V,\qquad|\theta-\xi|\leq c_{1},

one has

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta\theta}^{2}F_{\rho,X}(\theta,\xi)

\displaystyle\geq cI_{2},

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Sym}(\partial_{\theta\xi}^{2}G_{\rho}(\theta,\xi))

\displaystyle\geq cI_{2}.

At
X=0
and
\theta=\xi
,

(4.5)

\partial_{\theta\theta}^{2}F_{\rho,0}(\xi,\xi)=\frac{\rho_{R}\rho}{\rho_{R}-\rho}G_{S}(\xi),\qquad\partial_{\theta\xi}^{2}G_{\rho}(\xi,\xi)=-\frac{\rho_{R}\rho}{\rho_{R}-\rho}G_{S}(\xi).

Proof.

Write
U(\theta,\xi)=\rho_{R}\Omega(\theta)-\rho\Omega(\xi)
and

L=|U|
. For any parameters
a,b
,

(4.6)

\partial_{ab}L=\frac{\partial_{a}U\cdot\partial_{b}U+U\cdot\partial_{ab}U}{L}-\frac{(U\cdot\partial_{a}U)(U\cdot\partial_{b}U)}{L^{3}}.

At
\theta=\xi
,

U=(\rho_{R}-\rho)\Omega(\xi),\qquad L=\rho_{R}-\rho.

Using

\Omega\cdot\Omega=1,\qquad\Omega\cdot\partial_{\xi_{a}}\Omega=0,\qquad\Omega\cdot\partial_{\xi_{a}}\partial_{\xi_{b}}\Omega=-\partial_{\xi_{a}}\Omega\cdot\partial_{\xi_{b}}\Omega,

formula (
4.6
) gives

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta_{a}\theta_{b}}^{2}G_{\rho}(\xi,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{R}^{2}\partial_{\xi_{a}}\Omega\cdot\partial_{\xi_{b}}\Omega+(\rho_{R}-\rho)\rho_{R}\Omega\cdot\partial_{\xi_{a}}\partial_{\xi_{b}}\Omega}{\rho_{R}-\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{R}\rho}{\rho_{R}-\rho}\partial_{\xi_{a}}\Omega\cdot\partial_{\xi_{b}}\Omega,

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta_{a}\xi_{b}}^{2}G_{\rho}(\xi,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\rho_{R}\rho\,\partial_{\xi_{a}}\Omega\cdot\partial_{\xi_{b}}\Omega}{\rho_{R}-\rho}=-\frac{\rho_{R}\rho}{\rho_{R}-\rho}\partial_{\xi_{a}}\Omega\cdot\partial_{\xi_{b}}\Omega.

This gives the second identity in (
4.5
). Since
-|0-\rho_{R}\Omega(\theta)|=-\rho_{R}
is constant, the first identity in
(
4.5
) follows as well.

After shrinking the cap,
G_{S}\geq m_{S}I_{2}
on
V
. Also

0<\kappa_{-}\leq\frac{\rho_{R}\rho}{\rho_{R}-\rho}\leq\kappa_{+}<\infty,\qquad\rho\in[1/C_{0},C_{0}],

because
\rho_{R}-\rho\geq 3C_{0}
. Hence the two matrices in
(
4.5
) are uniformly positive at
(X,\theta)=(0,\xi)
.
The functions are smooth on the shell, and by continuity, the lower
bounds persist for
|X|\leq\tau_{0}
and
|\theta-\xi|\leq c_{1}
after choosing

V_{0}
,
c_{1}
, and
\tau_{0}
sufficiently small.
∎

4.4.
Critical map and nonstationary phase estimates

Lemma 4.5
.

After choosing
V_{0}\Subset V
and
c_{1}
as above, taking
\tau\leq\tau_{0}
, and
taking
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, the following hold uniformly for

r\in I,\qquad x\in\overline{U_{0}},\qquad\xi\in V_{0}.

(i)

The equation

\partial_{\theta}\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)=0

has a unique solution
\theta=\Theta_{r}(x,\xi)
in
|\theta-\xi|\leq c_{1}
, and

(4.7)

|\Theta_{r}(x,\xi)-\xi|\leq C(\tau+\varepsilon^{2}).

(ii)

On the support region
|\theta-\xi|\leq c_{1}
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta\theta}^{2}\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)

\displaystyle\geq c\varepsilon I_{2},

(4.9)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Sym}(\partial_{\theta\xi}^{2}S_{r}(\theta,\xi))

\displaystyle\geq c\varepsilon I_{2}.

Consequently,

(4.10)

|\partial_{\theta}\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)|\geq c\varepsilon|\theta-\Theta_{r}(x,\xi)|

whenever the segment from
\theta
to
\Theta_{r}(x,\xi)
stays in

|\vartheta-\xi|\leq c_{1}
.

(iii)

If the segment between
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
stays in

|\vartheta-\xi|\leq c_{1}
, then

(4.11)

|\nabla_{\xi}(S_{r}(\theta,\xi)-S_{r}(\theta^{\prime},\xi))|\geq c\varepsilon|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|.

(iv)

For fixed
x,r
, the map
\xi\mapsto\Theta_{r}(x,\xi)
is a diffeomorphism
from
V_{0}
onto its image. In scaled variables
X=x/\varepsilon
,

(4.12)

\partial_{\xi}\Theta_{r}(\varepsilon X,\xi)=I_{2}+O(\tau+\operatorname{diam}V_{0}+\varepsilon^{2}).

The family
\Theta_{r}
is bounded in
C^{\infty}
in the scaled variables
(X,\xi)
, uniformly in
r\in I
. The unscaled
x
-derivatives may cost powers of
\varepsilon^{-1}
.

Proof.

Write
x=\varepsilon X
and
r=\varepsilon\rho
.

(i) and (ii). On the shell,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\Phi_{R}(\varepsilon X,\theta)

\displaystyle=-|X-\rho_{R}\Omega(\theta)|+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}S_{r}(\theta,\xi)

\displaystyle=|\rho_{R}\Omega(\theta)-\rho\Omega(\xi)|+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Therefore

(4.13)

\varepsilon^{-1}\Psi_{r}(\varepsilon X,\theta,\xi)=F_{\rho,X}(\theta,\xi)+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Differentiating twice and using Lemma
4.4
gives
(
4.8
) and (
4.9
) for small
\varepsilon
.

By definition, at
X=0
,
\partial_{\theta}F_{\rho,0}(\xi,\xi)=0
. Hence, for

|X|\leq\tau
,

|\partial_{\theta}\Psi_{r}(\varepsilon X,\xi,\xi)|\leq C\varepsilon(\tau+\varepsilon^{2}).

Let
g(\theta)=\partial_{\theta}\Psi_{r}(\varepsilon X,\theta,\xi)
. The Hessian lower
bound gives the strong monotonicity estimate

(4.14)

(g(\theta)-g(\eta))\cdot(\theta-\eta)\geq c\varepsilon|\theta-\eta|^{2}

whenever the segment between
\theta
and
\eta
lies in
|\vartheta-\xi|\leq c_{1}
.
Choose
R_{0}=A_{0}(\tau+\varepsilon^{2})<c_{1}/2
, with
A_{0}
large. On

|\theta-\xi|=R_{0}

(4.15)

g(\theta)\cdot(\theta-\xi)\geq c\varepsilon R_{0}^{2}-C\varepsilon(\tau+\varepsilon^{2})R_{0}>0.

Let

Then

contradicting (

). Hence

and

The strict monotonicity (

uniqueness in

For (

to

\partial_{\theta}\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\theta\theta}^{2}\Psi_{r}(x,\Theta_{r}+t(\theta-\Theta_{r}),\xi)\,dt\right)(\theta-\Theta_{r}).

The lower bound (

(iii). For (

The symmetric part of the averaged matrix is negative definite with size

\varepsilon

(iv). Differentiating the critical equation with respect to

Using (

\displaystyle H_{r}(\varepsilon X,\xi)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon B_{0}(\xi,\rho)+O(\varepsilon(\tau+\operatorname{diam}V_{0}+\varepsilon^{2})),

4.6.
Exact critical value

Lemma 4.7

(Geodesic extension and exact critical value)
.

For every
x\in\overline{U_{0}}
,
r\in I
, and
\xi\in K_{2}
,

\Phi_{R}(x,\Theta_{r}(x,\xi))+S_{r}(\Theta_{r}(x,\xi),\xi)=\Phi_{r}(x,\xi).

Write

-geodesic past

For

Consequently,

after shrinking
\tau
and
\varepsilon
. Since

while

|Z(\rho_{R}-\rho+1)|>\rho_{R}

z=\exp_{o}(\varepsilon Z(t_{*}))=Y_{R}(\theta_{*}).

The leading radial expression in (
4.20

|\theta_{*}-\xi|\leq C(\tau+\varepsilon^{2}).

Thus

lies in the critical neighborhood where Lemma
4.5

applies.

Figure 1.
Geodesic from
x
to

and continued to

.

The coordinate ball was chosen to be strongly geodesically convex, and
\varepsilon
is small
so that the unique minimizing geodesic segments connecting

remain in it. See Figure

5.
Outer-radius factorization

This section proves the analytic consequences of the geometric lemmas from
Section
4
. We keep the frozen radius
r\in I
as an external
parameter throughout; no
r
-derivatives are used. We shall prove that the oscillatory integral operator
T_{\lambda,r}^{a}
in (
4.2
) can be factorized into the following two operators:

\displaystyle U_{\lambda,r}h(\theta)

\displaystyle=\lambda\int e^{i\lambda S_{r}(\theta,\xi)}\chi(\theta,\xi)h(\xi)\,d\xi,

\displaystyle E^{A}_{\lambda,R}h(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda\int e^{i\lambda\Phi_{R}(x,\theta)}A(x,\theta,\lambda)h(\theta)\,d\theta.

The constants below may depend on the fixed cap data and on powers of

\varepsilon^{-1}
, but are uniform in
r\in I
and
\lambda\geq 1
.

5.1.
Auxiliary estimates

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
r\in I
and every
\lambda\geq 1
,

\left\lVert U_{\lambda,r}h\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\theta}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K_{2})}.

Proof.

The kernel of
U_{\lambda,r}U_{\lambda,r}^{*}
is

K_{r}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=\lambda^{2}\int_{K_{2}}e^{i\lambda\{S_{r}(\theta,\xi)-S_{r}(\theta^{\prime},\xi)\}}\chi(\theta,\xi)\overline{\chi(\theta^{\prime},\xi)}\,d\xi.

If the integrand is nonzero, the fixed cutoff construction in
Section
4.5
implies that the segment

\theta_{t}=\theta^{\prime}+t(\theta-\theta^{\prime})
lies in
|\theta_{t}-\xi|<c_{1}
. Hence
Lemma
4.5
gives

\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}\{S_{r}(\theta,\xi)-S_{r}(\theta^{\prime},\xi)\}\right\rvert\geq c\varepsilon|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|.

The higher
\xi
-derivatives of the scaled phase difference satisfy the corresponding
O(|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|)
bounds on the same support. Integration by parts then gives

|K_{r}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})|\leq C_{N}\lambda^{2}(1+\lambda\varepsilon|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|)^{-N},\qquad N>2.

Schur’s test on the two-dimensional angular patch yields

\sup_{\theta}\int|K_{r}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})|\,d\theta^{\prime}+\sup_{\theta^{\prime}}\int|K_{r}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})|\,d\theta\leq C_{N}\varepsilon^{-2}.

Thus
\|U_{\lambda,r}\|_{2\to 2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
K\Subset U
be fixed. Let

A(x,\theta,\lambda)
be a bounded
C^{\infty}
order-zero amplitude family supported in
K\times V_{0}
. Then

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left\lVert E^{A}_{\lambda,R}h\right\rVert_{L^{2}(U)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\theta}},

\displaystyle\left\lVert E^{A}_{\lambda,R}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(U)}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\theta}}.

Proof.

The
L^{\infty}
bound follows immediately from Cauchy’s inequality in the
compact angular patch. For the
L^{2}
bound, the kernel of

(E^{A}_{\lambda,R})^{*}E^{A}_{\lambda,R}
is

K_{s}(\theta,\eta)=\lambda^{2}\int_{U}e^{i\lambda(\Phi_{s}(x,\eta)-\Phi_{s}(x,\theta))}\overline{A(x,\theta,\lambda)}A(x,\eta,\lambda)\,dx.

Lemma
4.3
gives, on
K\times V_{0}
,

|\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(x,\eta)-\nabla_{x}\Phi_{s}(x,\theta)|\geq c|\eta-\theta|,

with the corresponding differentiated upper bounds, where the constants may
depend on
\varepsilon
. Repeated integration by parts in
x
therefore gives

|K_{s}(\theta,\eta)|\leq C_{N,\varepsilon}\lambda^{2}(1+\lambda|\theta-\eta|)^{-N},\qquad N>2.

Schur’s test on the two-dimensional angular variables proves
(
5.1
).
∎

5.2.
Stationary phase

We shall use the following consequences of Section
4
. For

x\in\overline{U_{0}}
,
r\in I
, and
\xi\in K_{2}
, the phase

\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)=\Phi_{R}(x,\theta)+S_{r}(\theta,\xi)

has a unique critical point
\Theta_{r}(x,\xi)
on the support region, with exact
critical value

(5.2)

\Psi_{r}(x,\Theta_{r}(x,\xi),\xi)=\Phi_{r}(x,\xi).

The critical Hessian

(5.3)

satisfies
H_{r}\geq c\varepsilon I_{2}
and
|\det H_{r}|\geq c\varepsilon^{2}
. For later use set

q_{r}(x,\xi):=(2\pi)e^{i\pi\operatorname{sig}(H_{r}(x,\xi))/4}|\det H_{r}(x,\xi)|^{-1/2},

where
\operatorname{sig}(H)
is the number of positive eigenvalues minus the number of
negative eigenvalues. Then
|q_{r}|\approx\varepsilon^{-1}
.
Moreover

\Xi_{r}
is the inverse critical map on
\overline{U_{0}}\times W
, so

\Xi_{r}(x,\Theta_{r}(x,\xi))=\xi,\qquad\Theta_{r}(x,\Xi_{r}(x,\theta))=\theta.

The cutoff
\chi
is equal to one on a fixed neighborhood of the critical graph
and satisfies the segment condition from Section
4.5
. Hence,
by the nonstationary estimate in Lemma
4.5
and
compactness, there is a smaller fixed neighborhood of the critical graph such
that on the support of
\chi
outside this neighborhood,

(5.4)

|\partial_{\theta}\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)|\gtrsim\varepsilon.

Choose
\psi_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U)
and
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(W)
such
that
\operatorname{supp}\psi_{1}\Subset U_{0},\ \psi_{1}=1\text{ near }K_{1},

and
\psi=1
on a fixed neighborhood of

\{\Theta_{r}(x,\xi):x\in K_{1},\ r\in I,\ \xi\in K_{2}\}.

The support of
\psi
is compactly contained in the collar where
\Xi_{r}
is
defined.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
K\Subset U
be a compact set on which the critical geometry above holds,
and let
B_{r}(x,\xi,\theta,\lambda)
be a bounded
C^{\infty}
order-zero
amplitude family supported where
\chi
is supported. Then, for

x\in K
,
\xi\in K_{2}
, and
r\in I
,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int e^{i\lambda\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)}B_{r}(x,\xi,\theta,\lambda)\chi(\theta,\xi)\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad=e^{i\lambda\Phi_{r}(x,\xi)}\left[\lambda^{-1}q_{r}(x,\xi)B_{r}(x,\xi,\Theta_{r}(x,\xi),\lambda)+\lambda^{-2}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,r}^{B}(x,\xi)\right],

where
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,r}^{B}
is a bounded
C^{\infty}
amplitude, with
bounds depending only on finitely many seminorms of
B_{r}
and on the fixed
geometric data. The same assertion applies to any smooth phase family
satisfying the same critical point, nonstationary, Hessian, and exact
critical-value hypotheses, with

defined from the corresponding Hessian.

Proof.

Choose a smooth cutoff
\zeta_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)
equal to one near the critical
graph and supported where
\chi=1
. On the support of
(1-\zeta_{r})\chi
,
(
5.4
) gives

|\partial_{\theta}\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)|\gtrsim\varepsilon.

For this non-stationary part, repeated integration by parts in
\theta
gives, for every

and fixed

,

\|I_{\lambda,{\rm ns}}\|_{C^{N}_{x,\xi}}\leq C_{L,N,\varepsilon}\lambda^{-L}.

Taking

large allows this term to be absorbed into the remainder.

On the critical part, the variable coefficient version of the stationary phase theorem (see e.g. Sogge
[
17

) applies uniformly in

. The critical value is
(
5.2
) and the Hessian is
(

), so the critical contribution is

e^{i\lambda\Phi_{r}(x,\xi)}\left[\lambda^{-1}q_{r}(x,\xi)B_{r}(x,\xi,\Theta_{r}(x,\xi),\lambda)+\lambda^{-2}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,r}^{B}(x,\xi)\right],

with

5.3.
Outer-radius factorization

Proposition 5.4

(Outer-radius factorization)
.

For every bounded
C^{\infty}
order-zero amplitude
a_{r}
satisfying the hypotheses in the definition of
T^{a}_{\lambda,r}
in (
4.2
), define

(5.6)

A_{r}(x,\theta,\lambda):=\psi_{1}(x)\psi(\theta)q_{r}(x,\Xi_{r}(x,\theta))^{-1}a_{r}(x,\Xi_{r}(x,\theta),\lambda)

wherever
\psi_{1}(x)\psi(\theta)\neq 0
, and set
A_{r}=0
elsewhere. This is a
smooth order-zero amplitude because
\operatorname{supp}\psi_{1}\Subset U_{0}

and
\operatorname{supp}\psi\Subset W
, where
\Xi_{r}
is defined. Then

T^{a}_{\lambda,r}h=E^{A_{r}}_{\lambda,R}U_{\lambda,r}h+R_{\lambda,r}h,\qquad h\in L^{2}(K_{2}),

and

\|R_{\lambda,r}h\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}+\|R_{\lambda,r}h\|_{L^{2}(U)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|h\|_{L^{2}(K_{2})},\qquad\lambda\geq 1,\ r\in I.

The amplitude
A_{r}
belongs to a bounded
C^{\infty}
order-zero class in the
active variables
(x,\theta)
.

Proof.

For a trial outer amplitude
A
, the composition kernel of

E^{A}_{\lambda,R}U_{\lambda,r}
is

(5.7)

K^{A}_{\lambda,r}(x,\xi)=\lambda^{2}\int e^{i\lambda\Psi_{r}(x,\theta,\xi)}A(x,\theta,\lambda)\chi(\theta,\xi)\,d\theta.

Apply Proposition
5.3
, with
K=\overline{U_{0}}
, to

B_{r}(x,\xi,\theta,\lambda)=A_{r}(x,\theta,\lambda)
. For

x\in K_{1}
and
\xi\in K_{2}
, the cutoff choices and the inverse identity give

Therefore

(5.8)

Multiplying the stationary-phase expansion by the outside factor
\lambda^{2}
in
(

), we get, on

,

K^{A_{r}}_{\lambda,r}(x,\xi)=\lambda e^{i\lambda\Phi_{r}(x,\xi)}q_{r}(x,\xi)A_{r}(x,\Theta_{r}(x,\xi),\lambda)+\mathcal{K}_{\lambda,r}(x,\xi),

where the kernel remainder satisfies

\|\mathcal{K}_{\lambda,r}\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{U_{0}}\times K_{2})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}.

Together with (
5.8
), this gives the desired original
kernel plus the bounded kernel remainder on

.

Since

is supported in
K_{1}\times K_{2}
, the factor

in

implies the kernel of

is the

on

. The symbol

follow by differentiating (

) and using
the bounds for

,
\Xi_{r}
, and

.
∎

6.1.
Signed critical geometry

Proposition 6.3

(Signed factorization)
.

Here

,

7.
Log-free spectral cluster estimates

In this section, we prove the log-free multilinear estimates for spectral clusters on any closed Riemannian manifold. The proof uses Sogge’s spectral cluster parametrix and reduces to frozen radial pieces, which are then treated by Theorems
1.1
-

and Theorem

.

As in
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
, a first reduction in the proof of Theorem
1.4
is that it suffices to prove it for one fixed nontrivial function
\chi
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose that the assertion of Theorem
1.4
holds for a bump function
\chi_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})

which is not identically zero. Then it holds for any other choice of the bump function.

Proof.

Let
P=\sqrt{-\Delta}
. Choose
t_{0}
with
\chi_{0}(t_{0})\neq 0
, and
then choose
h>0
so small that
\chi_{0}
is bounded away from zero on

(t_{0}-2h,t_{0}+2h)
. Consider a partition of unity of the real line given by

\rho\in C_{0}^{\infty}((-h,h))
with

\sum_{m\in h\mathbb{Z}}\rho(t-t_{0}-m)=1.

For
\widetilde{\chi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, set

b_{m}(t)=\frac{\rho(t-t_{0}-m)\widetilde{\chi}(t)}{\chi_{0}(t-m)},\qquad m\in h\mathbb{Z}.

The denominator is harmless on the support of the numerator, and rapid decay
of
\widetilde{\chi}
gives

\widetilde{\chi}(t)=\sum_{m\in h\mathbb{Z}}b_{m}(t)\chi_{0}(t-m),\qquad\|b_{m}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{N}(1+|m|)^{-N}.

Expanding the product, fix
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\in h\mathbb{Z}
, and write

\nu_{j}=2+|\lambda_{j}+m_{j}|,\qquad g_{j}=b_{m_{j}}(P-\lambda_{j})f_{j}.

Up to a rearrangement in the increasing order
\nu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\nu_{k}
, the assumed estimate for
\chi_{0}
gives

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}\chi_{0}(P-\lambda_{j}-m_{j})g_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0}(n,k,p;\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|g_{j}\|_{2}.

Because

\frac{2+\lambda_{j}}{(1+|m_{j}|)}\leq\nu_{j}\leq(1+|m_{j}|)(2+\lambda_{j}),

from the explicit formula for
\mathcal{E}_{0}
, there is some
N_{0}=N_{0}(n,k,p)
such that

\mathcal{E}_{0}(n,k,p;\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k})\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0}(n,k,p;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1+|m_{j}|)^{N_{0}}.

Therefore,

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\chi}(P-\lambda_{j})f_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{0}(n,k,p;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{2}\left(\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1+|m_{j}|)^{N_{0}-N}\right).

Choosing
N>N_{0}+2
, the last sum is finite. This proves the desired estimate
for
\widetilde{\chi}
.
∎

Following
[
17
, Chapter 4]
, thanks to Lemma

, it is sufficient to prove Theorem

with
\chi
such that

Lemma 7.2
.

There exists

,

Lemma 7.3
.

The hypersurface

has uniformly definite second fundamental form: for any local coordinate system

near
\underline{\omega}\in S^{n-1}

the normal unit vectors to the surface

at the point

\big\langle\partial^{2}_{ww}\nabla_{x}\varphi_{r}(x,w),N(x,w)\big\rangle

Proof.

Set

\mathrm{II}_{ij}(r,x,w)=\big\langle\partial_{w_{i}w_{j}}^{2}\nabla_{x}\varphi_{r}(x,w),N(x,w)\big\rangle.

In normal coordinates, as discussed above,Gauss’s lemma gives

\nabla_{x}\varphi_{r}(0,w)=\omega(w),\ \forall r\in[\varepsilon/C,C\varepsilon].

For a local choice of normal, write

N(0,w)=\sigma\omega(w),
where
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}.

Since
|\omega(w)|^{2}=1
, differentiating gives

\mathrm{II}_{ij}(r,0,w)=-\sigma\,\langle\partial_{w_{i}}\omega,\partial_{w_{j}}\omega\rangle.

Thus, for
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

-\sigma\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{II}_{ij}(r,0,w)\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}=\sum_{i,j}\langle\partial_{w_{i}}\omega,\partial_{w_{j}}\omega\rangle\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}=\left|\sum_{i}\zeta_{i}\partial_{w_{i}}\omega\right|^{2}\geq c|\zeta|^{2}.

By smoothness, uniformly for

Hence

which is the claimed uniform definiteness.

We note that there exists a smooth positive function

. Minkowski’s inequality shows that (

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}T_{\lambda_{j},\varphi_{r_{j}}}^{a_{r_{j}}}f_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\,\mathcal{E}_{0}(n,k,p;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}.

uniformly for
1\ll\lambda_{1}\leq...\leq\lambda_{k}
and
r_{1},...,r_{k}\in[\varepsilon/C,C\varepsilon]
. Therefore, by Lemma
7.3
, the bounds for Carleson-Sjölin operators in Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
give (
7.3
), except for the case
n=3
and
p\leq 2
. We shall prove log-free estimates in this case separately in the following.

7.1.
Log-free estimates in three dimensions

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n=3
. We shall use Theorem
6.4
to establish log-free endpoint estimates in three dimensions.

Lemma 7.4

(Transverse frozen cap estimate)
.

Let

T_{\lambda,j}h(x)=\lambda\int e^{i\lambda\phi_{j}(x,\xi)}b_{j}(x,\xi,\lambda)h(\xi)\,d\xi,\qquad j=1,2,

be oscillatory integral operators on a fixed compact patch

U\Subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with
\xi\in V_{j}\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Assume the phases
and order-zero amplitudes form a bounded
C^{\infty}
family on the supports. Put

P_{j}(x,\xi)=\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}(x,\xi).

Assume
\operatorname{rank}\partial_{\xi}P_{j}=2
and that the associated covector
surfaces are uniformly transverse:

(7.4)

|n_{1}(x,\xi)\times n_{2}(x,\eta)|\geq c_{0}>0,

where
n_{j}
is a smooth unit normal to
P_{j}(x,V_{j})
. Then, for

1\leq\alpha\leq\beta
,

(7.5)

\|T_{\alpha,1}f\,T_{\beta,2}g\|_{L^{2}(U)}\leq C\alpha^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

The constant is uniform for bounded
C^{\infty}
families satisfying these hypotheses.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the linearized estimate

B_{\alpha,\beta}F(x)=\alpha\beta\iint e^{i\alpha\phi_{1}(x,\xi)+i\beta\phi_{2}(x,\eta)}b_{1}(x,\xi,\alpha)b_{2}(x,\eta,\beta)F(\xi,\eta)\,d\xi d\eta.

After a finite subdivision of the frequency supports, transversality allows us
to write
\xi=(s,t)
so that

(7.6)

\left|\det\bigl(\partial_{s}P_{1}(x,\xi),\partial_{\eta_{1}}P_{2}(x,\eta),\partial_{\eta_{2}}P_{2}(x,\eta)\bigr)\right|\geq c_{1}>0

on the support. Indeed, the two
\eta
-columns span the tangent plane of the
second covector surface, and (
7.4
) says that some
tangent direction of the first surface is uniformly transverse to that plane.

Set
\sigma=\alpha/\beta
and
u=\sigma s
. For fixed
t
, put

y=(u,\eta)
and

\Psi_{t}(x,u,\eta)=\sigma\phi_{1}(x,u/\sigma,t)+\phi_{2}(x,\eta).

Then

\nabla_{x}\Psi_{t}=\sigma P_{1}(x,u/\sigma,t)+P_{2}(x,\eta),

and differentiation with respect to
(u,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
gives the columns in
(
7.6
). Thus, after the subdivision and uniformly in

\sigma
and
t
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\geq c|y-y^{\prime}|,

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}\{\nabla_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y^{\prime})\}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma}|y-y^{\prime}|,\qquad|\gamma|\leq N,

with
N>3
. The change
u=\sigma s
removes the small factor
\sigma
from the
first column.

For fixed
t
, let

S_{\beta,t}h(x)=\int e^{i\beta\Psi_{t}(x,u,\eta)}c_{t}(x,u,\eta,\alpha,\beta)h(u,\eta)\,dud\eta,

where
c_{t}=b_{1}(x,u/\sigma,t,\alpha)b_{2}(x,\eta,\beta)
. Lemma
3.1
,
applied in the three variables
(u,\eta)
, gives

\|S_{\beta,t}h\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\beta^{-3/2}\|h\|_{2}

uniformly in
t
and
\sigma
. Since
ds=\sigma^{-1}du
and

\alpha\beta/\sigma=\beta^{2}
,

B_{\alpha,\beta}F(x)=\beta^{2}\int S_{\beta,t}H_{t}(x)\,dt,\qquad H_{t}(u,\eta)=F(u/\sigma,t,\eta).

Minkowski and Cauchy’s inequality in the bounded
t
-interval give

\|B_{\alpha,\beta}F\|_{2}\leq C\beta^{1/2}\left(\int\|H_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}=C\beta^{1/2}\sigma^{1/2}\|F\|_{2}=C\alpha^{1/2}\|F\|_{2}.

Taking
F=f\otimes g
proves (
7.5
).
∎

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
o
be the center of a sufficiently small normal-coordinate ball and set

U=\{|x|<\tau\varepsilon\}
in these coordinates. Suppose

S^{(j)}_{\lambda}f(x)=\lambda\int e^{-i\lambda d_{g}(x,y)}a_{j}(x,y,\lambda)f(y)\,dy,\qquad j=1,2,

where the amplitudes are supported in
x\in K_{1}\Subset U
, where

d_{g}(o,y)\approx\varepsilon,\qquad d_{g}(x,y)\approx\varepsilon,

and where the amplitudes form a bounded order-zero family. Then, for

1\leq\alpha\leq\beta
,

(7.7)

\|S^{(1)}_{\alpha}f\,S^{(2)}_{\beta}g\|_{L^{2}(U)}\leq C\alpha^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

Proof.

Use geodesic polar coordinates around
o
on the
y
-annulus. After finite
radial and angular partitions of unity, each angular piece has the form

(7.8)

S_{\lambda,\nu}^{(j)}f_{j}(x)=\int_{I}\bigl(T^{(j,\nu)}_{\lambda,r}h^{(j,\nu)}_{r}\bigr)(x)\,dr,

where
I\subset[\varepsilon/C,C\varepsilon]
is fixed,

Y_{\nu}(r,\xi)=\exp_{o}(r\Omega_{\nu}(\xi))
, and

T^{(j,\nu)}_{\lambda,r}h(x)=\lambda\int e^{-i\lambda d_{g}(x,Y_{\nu}(r,\xi))}b^{(j,\nu)}_{r}(x,\xi,\lambda)h(\xi)\,d\xi.

Splitting the polar Jacobian symmetrically between the amplitude and the input
gives

(7.9)

\sum_{\nu}\int_{I}\|h^{(j,\nu)}_{r}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}^{2}\,dr\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2},\qquad f_{1}=f,\quad f_{2}=g.

The frozen amplitudes satisfy the symbol bounds required in the frozen
estimates.

We claim that every frozen cap pair satisfies

(7.10)

\|T^{(1,\nu_{1})}_{\alpha,r_{1}}f\,T^{(2,\nu_{2})}_{\beta,r_{2}}g\|_{L^{2}(U)}\leq C\alpha^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2},

for all
r_{1},r_{2}\in I
. The angular partition is chosen fine enough that each
cap pair is either parallel/antipodal or uniformly separated from both of these
relations. In the first case there is a common angular chart and there are signs

\sigma_{j}\in\{+1,-1\}
such that, after conjugating the factors with

\sigma_{j}=-1
if necessary, the phases are precisely the signed phases

\Phi_{r_{j}}^{\sigma_{j}}
. Theorem
6.4
then gives
(
7.10
).

In the remaining case, the two direction sets are separated from both

\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}
and
\omega_{1}=-\omega_{2}
. The scaled distance estimate gives

\nabla_{x}[-d_{g}(x,\exp_{o}(r\omega))]=\omega+O(\tau+\varepsilon^{2})

with smooth dependence on
\omega
. Thus, after taking
\tau
and
\varepsilon
small,
the associated covector surfaces have uniformly transverse normal fields.
Lemma
7.4
gives (
7.10
) for
these cap pairs as well.

Finally combine (
7.8
), (
7.10
),
Minkowski’s inequality, Cauchy’s inequality in the two radial variables, and
(
7.9
). For each cap pair,

\displaystyle\|S_{\alpha,\nu_{1}}^{(1)}f\,S_{\beta,\nu_{2}}^{(2)}g\|_{L^{2}(U)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\int_{I}\|T^{(1,\nu_{1})}_{\alpha,r_{1}}h^{(1,\nu_{1})}_{r_{1}}\,T^{(2,\nu_{2})}_{\beta,r_{2}}h^{(2,\nu_{2})}_{r_{2}}\|_{2}\,dr_{1}dr_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{1/2}\left(\int_{I}\|h^{(1,\nu_{1})}_{r_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}\,dr_{1}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{I}\|h^{(2,\nu_{2})}_{r_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}\,dr_{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Summing over the finitely many cap pairs proves
(
7.7
).
∎

By Lemmas
7.1
-
7.2
we may choose
\chi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
so that

\chi_{\lambda}=S_{\lambda}+R_{\lambda}.

The remainder is harmless. It remains to estimate
S_{\lambda}f\,S_{\mu}g
. Both operators satisfy the hypotheses of Proposition
7.5
. Consequently, for
1\leq\lambda\leq\mu
,

\|S_{\lambda}f\,S_{\mu}g\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

This proves the log-free endpoint bilinear estimate

(7.11)

\left\|\chi_{\lambda}f\chi_{\mu}g\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

For
k\geq 3
,
1\leq p\leq 2
,
1\ll\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}
.
Write

u_{j}=\chi_{\lambda_{j}}f_{j}
with
\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1
. By interpolation, it suffices to prove the log-free endpoint estimates. The standard linear bounds are uniform for this bounded amplitude family:

\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\lambda_{j},\qquad\|u_{j}\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\lambda_{j}^{1/4}.

The bilinear endpoint from (
7.11
) gives,
for
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{j}
,
\|u_{i}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda_{i}^{1/2}.

At

,

\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-3}u_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{k-2}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}\,\lambda_{k-3}^{1/2}\lambda_{k-2}^{1/2}.

At
p=4/3
,

\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{4/3}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}\,\lambda_{k-3}\lambda_{k-2}^{1/2}\lambda_{k-1}^{1/4}.

At

,

\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}\,\lambda_{k-2}\lambda_{k-1}^{1/2}.

8.
Proof of multilinear oscillatory integral estimates

In this section, we prove the upper bounds in Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
. Fix
n\geq 2
,
k\geq 2
. Let
d=n-1
. Let
u_{j}=T_{\lambda_{j},\phi_{j}}^{a_{j}}f_{j}
and
\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
for
j=1,2,\ldots,k
. To give a unified argument, we use the convention that the empty product is
1
and
\lambda_{j}=2
for all
j\leq 0
.

For
d\geq 3
, set

q_{0}=\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2},\qquad r_{0}=\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4},\qquad q_{1}=\frac{2d}{d-2}.

For
d\geq 2
, set
q_{2}=\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
. Then, for
d\geq 3
,

\quad\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{1}{2},\quad\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{0}},\quad\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{2},

and for
d\geq 2
,

\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{2}.

In this section, for
1\leq p\leq 2
, we first prove bilinear and multilinear estimates for
d\geq 3
, and then prove them for
d=1
and
d=2
separately. Finally, we prove similar estimates for
p>2
.

8.1.
Keel-Tao’s theorem

From now on set
d=n-1
. The proof is local in phase space. We first reduce the operator to the usual Carleson–Sjölin normal form.

Lemma 8.1

(Common normal coordinates)
.

Fix
k\geq 2
. Let
T_{\lambda_{j},\phi_{j}}^{a_{j}}
,
1\leq j\leq k
, be Carleson–Sjölin operators, with amplitudes
a_{j}
supported in fixed compact coordinate patches. After a finite decomposition in
y
and in each of the
k
frequency variables, each localized piece of the product
\prod_{j=1}^{k}T_{\lambda_{j},\phi_{j}}^{a_{j}}f_{j}
admits orthonormal
coordinates

y=(s,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
common to all
j
, such that

\det\partial^{2}_{z\xi}\phi_{j}(s,z,\xi)\neq 0

on the support of its localized amplitude. In these coordinates each hypersurface
S_{\phi_{j}}(s,z)

is locally a graph
\{\bigl(H^{(j)}_{s,z}(\zeta),\zeta\bigr)\},

and the curvature condition is equivalent to

(8.1)

\det\partial^{2}_{\zeta\zeta}H^{(j)}_{s,z}(\zeta)\neq 0.

Proof.

The assertion is local in the product support. Fix

y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\qquad\eta_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad j=1,\ldots,k,

with
(y_{0},\eta_{j}^{0})\in\operatorname{supp}a_{j}
. By the
rank condition, the image

\operatorname{Image}\partial_{\xi}\nabla_{y}\phi_{j}(y_{0},\eta_{j}^{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

is a
d
-dimensional subspace for each
j
. Choose a unit vector
\ell

outside the finite union of these subspaces. Let

E:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\ell^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be an isometry, and write

y=y_{0}+s\ell+Ez,\qquad s\in\mathbb{R},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then

\partial_{z\xi}^{2}\phi_{j}=E^{T}\partial_{\xi}\nabla_{y}\phi_{j}.

Since
\ker E^{T}=\mathbb{R}\ell
and
\ell\notin\operatorname{Image}\partial_{\xi}\nabla_{y}\phi_{j}(y_{0},\eta_{j}^{0})
, the projection

E^{T}
is injective on this image. Hence, using the rank condition again,

\det\partial_{z\xi}^{2}\phi_{j}(0,0,\eta_{j}^{0})\neq 0.

After shrinking the supports, this remains true throughout the localized
piece. Compactness gives the required finite decomposition.

On each such piece, the map
\xi\mapsto\zeta=\partial_{z}\phi_{j}(s,z,\xi)

is a local diffeomorphism. Using
\zeta
as the new frequency variable, we
may write

\partial_{s}\phi_{j}(s,z,\xi)=H^{(j)}_{s,z}(\zeta).

Therefore

S_{\phi_{j}}(s,z)=\{(H^{(j)}_{s,z}(\zeta),\zeta)\}.

So the curvature condition is equivalent to

\det\partial_{\zeta\zeta}^{2}H^{(j)}_{s,z}\neq 0.

∎

On such common normal coordinates, each localized factor has the form

(8.2)

T_{\lambda,\Phi}u(s,z)=\lambda^{d/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\lambda\Phi(s,z,\xi)}b(s,z,\xi)u(\xi)\,d\xi,

where
b\in C_{0}^{\infty}
,
\det\partial^{2}_{z\xi}\Phi\neq 0
, and the graph curvature condition (
8.1
) holds. The following Strichartz estimate follows from the Keel–Tao theorem
[
13
]
in the truncated-decay setting, together with the standard logarithmic substitute at the one excluded endpoint.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
2\leq a,b\leq\infty
, and suppose

\frac{2}{a}+\frac{d}{b}\leq\frac{d}{2}.

If
(a,b,d)\neq(2,\infty,2)
, then

(8.3)

\|T_{\lambda,\Phi}u\|_{L_{s}^{a}L_{z}^{b}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{a}-\frac{d}{b}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

The only logarithmic endpoint is the two-dimensional endpoint

(a,b,d)=(2,\infty,2)
, where

(8.4)

\|T_{\lambda,\Phi}u\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{\infty}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log\lambda}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

We prove the estimate on one normal-form patch. The finite sum of patches only changes the
constant. Write

U_{\lambda}(s)u(z):=T_{\lambda,\Phi}u(s,z).

The nondegeneracy
\det\partial_{z\xi}^{2}\Phi\neq 0
gives the fixed-time
L^{2}
bound uniform in
s
and
\lambda
:

\|U_{\lambda}(s)u\|_{L_{z}^{2}}\leq C\|u\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}.

We also need the usual dispersive estimate. The kernel of
U_{\lambda}(s)U_{\lambda}(s^{\prime})^{*}
is

K_{\lambda}(s,z;s^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\lambda^{d}\int e^{i\lambda(\Phi(s,z,\xi)-\Phi(s^{\prime},z^{\prime},\xi))}b(s,z,\xi)\overline{b(s^{\prime},z^{\prime},\xi)}\,d\xi.

If
|s-s^{\prime}|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}
, the trivial bound gives
|K_{\lambda}|\lesssim\lambda^{d}
.
If
|s-s^{\prime}|\gtrsim\lambda^{-1}
, stationary phase in
\xi
, using the graph curvature
condition, gives

|K_{\lambda}(s,z;s^{\prime},z^{\prime})|\leq C\lambda^{d}(\lambda|s-s^{\prime}|)^{-d/2}.

Combining the two regions,

\|U_{\lambda}(s)U_{\lambda}(s^{\prime})^{*}F\|_{L_{z}^{\infty}}\leq C\lambda^{d}(1+\lambda|s-s^{\prime}|)^{-d/2}\|F\|_{L_{z}^{1}}.

Now rescale both time and space to put this in the normalized Keel–Tao form. Set

\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}(t)u(x):=\lambda^{-d/2}U_{\lambda}(t/\lambda)u(x/\lambda).

Then

\|\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}(t)u\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}

and the previous kernel bound becomes

\|\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}(t)\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}(t^{\prime})^{*}F\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\leq C(1+|t-t^{\prime}|)^{-d/2}\|F\|_{L_{x}^{1}}.

Thus the Keel–Tao theorem applies with dispersive exponent
\sigma=d/2
in the
truncated-decay case. Since Keel–Tao admissibility is

\frac{1}{a}+\frac{d/2}{b}\leq\frac{d/2}{2},

it is exactly the condition

\frac{2}{a}+\frac{d}{b}\leq\frac{d}{2}.

The only excluded Keel–Tao endpoint
(a,b,\sigma)=(2,\infty,1)
is precisely

(a,b,d)=(2,\infty,2)
. Therefore, for every other admissible pair,

\|\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}(t)u\|_{L_{t}^{a}L_{x}^{b}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

Undoing the rescaling gives

\|\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}u\|_{L_{t}^{a}L_{x}^{b}}=\lambda^{-d/2+d/b+1/a}\|T_{\lambda,\Phi}u\|_{L_{s}^{a}L_{z}^{b}},

with the usual interpretation when
a=\infty
or
b=\infty
. Hence

\|T_{\lambda,\Phi}u\|_{L_{s}^{a}L_{z}^{b}}\leq C\lambda^{d/2-1/a-d/b}\|u\|_{L^{2}},

which proves (
8.3
).

When
(a,b,d)=(2,\infty,2)
, the same dyadic
TT^{*}
argument is borderline: the time kernel
has size
(1+\lambda|s-s^{\prime}|)^{-1}
, whose dyadic summation over
\lambda^{-1}\lesssim|s-s^{\prime}|\lesssim 1
contributes
\log\lambda
at the
TT^{*}
level. Taking the square root gives

\|T_{\lambda,\Phi}u\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{\infty}}\leq C\lambda^{1/2}\sqrt{\log\lambda}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

This is the asserted endpoint estimate.
∎

We shall repeatedly use the following mixed Hölder bookkeeping. If
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{a_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{b_{j}}=\frac{1}{p}
, then

\Big\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}u_{j}\Big\|_{L_{s,z}^{p}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|u_{j}\|_{L_{s}^{a_{j}}L_{z}^{b_{j}}}.

Since each normal-form patch is compact in the
(s,z)
variables, the same estimate is valid when the two sums are at most
1/p
, after using the finite-measure embeddings.

8.2.
Bilinear estimates for

. The remaining ranges follow by interpolation. We shall use Proposition
8.2

At

\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

8.3.
Multilinear estimates for
d\geq 3

Fix
k\geq 3
and
d\geq 3
. We prove the endpoint estimates, and the remaining ranges follow by interpolation. We shall use Proposition
8.2
with mixed Hölder and the bilinear estimates obtained above.

At
p=2
, apply Hölder and bilinear
L^{2}
estimates

\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\|u_{k-2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-2}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

At
p=p_{0}
, we apply Hölder and bilinear

estimates

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\|u_{k-2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-2}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{d-2}{2(d+2)}}.

Moreover, mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\|u_{k-3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{q_{0}}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{q_{0}}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d-2}{2(d+2)}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}.

The first bound is smaller than the second bound if
\lambda_{k-2}\lambda_{k}\ll\lambda_{k-1}^{2}
. We therefore take the minimum of these two bounds.

At
p=p_{1}
, mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\|u_{k-3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{q_{1}}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{q_{1}}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

At
p=p_{2}
, mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\|u_{k-3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{d}{2(d+2)}}.

At
p=1
, Hölder and bilinear
L^{2}
estimates give a basic bound

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\|u_{k-4}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-3}u_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{k-2}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

(8.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

Moreover, for
d\geq 4
,
(2,d)
is admissible:
\frac{2}{2}+\frac{d}{d}=2\leq\frac{d}{2}
, so mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\|u_{k-3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{d}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{q_{1}}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

and for
d=3
mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\|u_{k-4}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-3}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{6}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{3}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{5}{4}}\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Note that these two bounds are smaller than the basic bound (
8.5
) if
\lambda_{k-3}\lambda_{k-1}\ll\lambda_{k-2}^{2}
. Thus we take the minimum of them.

When
d=3
, there is an extra endpoint
p_{3}=\frac{10}{9}
. Mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{3}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}...\|u_{k-3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{6}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{5/2}L_{z}^{30/7}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{2}{5}},

and

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{3}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}...\|u_{k-4}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-3}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{20/3}L_{z}^{5/2}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{5}{4}}\lambda_{k-2}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{3}{20}}.

The first bound is smaller than the second bound if
\lambda_{k-3}\lambda_{k-1}\ll\lambda_{k-2}^{2}
. Thus we take the minimum of them.

8.4.
Multilinear estimates for
d=1

Fix
k\geq 2
and
d=1
. We shall prove multilinear estimates directly by applying Proposition
8.2
with mixed Hölder.

At
p=2
, mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\cdot\lambda_{k-2}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-1}^{1/4}.

At
p=\frac{3}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-3}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{6}L_{z}^{6}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\cdot\lambda_{k-3}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-2}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-1}^{1/6}.

At
p=\frac{6}{5}
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-5}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-4}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-3}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{12}L_{z}^{3}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-5}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\cdot\lambda_{k-4}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-3}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-2}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-1}^{1/12}.

At
p=1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-6}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\prod_{m=k-5}^{k-2}\|u_{m}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-6}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\cdot\lambda_{k-5}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-4}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-3}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-2}^{1/4}.

In particular, the exponents
p=\frac{3}{2},\frac{6}{5}
are not regarded as endpoints when
k=2,3
, since they follow by interpolating between the
p=1
and
p=2
estimates proved above.

8.5.
Multilinear estimates for
d=2

In the general case the endpoint estimate (
8.4
) has a log factor, so we prove the stated bounds directly by mixed Hölder.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
and
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
and
q=\frac{2p}{2-p}
. For
k=2
,

\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L_{s}^{p^{\prime}}L_{z}^{q}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}^{1-\frac{1}{p}},&1\leq p<2\\
\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log\lambda_{1}},&p=2.\end{cases}

Now assume
k\geq 3
. For
1\leq p\leq\frac{4}{3}
and
r=\frac{2p}{4-3p}
, mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}...u_{k}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-3}\|_{L_{s}^{r}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{2}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{p^{\prime}}L_{z}^{q}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}.

Hence, for
1<p\leq\frac{4}{3}
,

\|u_{1}u_{2}...u_{k}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}\cdot\lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{5}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}\lambda_{k-2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log\lambda_{k-2}}\lambda_{k-1}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

At
p=1
, we have
r=2
, so one obtains the same bound with the extra factor

\sqrt{\log\lambda_{k-3}}
.

For
\frac{4}{3}<p\leq 2
, mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}...u_{k}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{q/2}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{p^{\prime}}L_{z}^{q}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{z}^{2}}.

Thus, for
\frac{4}{3}<p<2
,

\|u_{1}u_{2}...u_{k}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}\cdot\lambda_{k-2}^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\lambda_{k-1}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

At
p=2
, we have
(p^{\prime},q)=(2,\infty)
, so one obtains the same bound with the extra factor
\sqrt{\log\lambda_{k-1}}
.

8.6.
Multilinear estimates for
p>2

Let
k\geq 2
and
p>2
. We shall prove the estimates in Theorem
1.3
by applying Proposition
8.2
and mixed Hölder.

Let
p_{c}=\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
. For
p\geq p_{c}
, Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}...u_{k}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k}^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+1}{p}}.

For
2<p<p_{c}
when
d\geq 2
, and
3\leq p<6
when
d=1
, let
a=\frac{4p}{4-d(p-2)}
and
b=\frac{4p}{d(p-2)}
. We have

\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}=\frac{1}{p}.

Then mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}...u_{k}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-2}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{a}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{b}L_{z}^{p}}

(8.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-2}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{3d}{4}-\frac{d+2}{2p}}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{d}{4}-\frac{d}{2p}}.

For
2<p<3
when
d=1
, let
c=\frac{2p}{3-p}
. We have

\frac{1}{c}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{b}=\frac{1}{p}.

Then mixed Hölder gives

\displaystyle\|u_{1}u_{2}...u_{k}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{k-2}\|_{L_{s}^{c}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L_{s}^{4}L_{z}^{\infty}}\|u_{k}\|_{L_{s}^{b}L_{z}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{k-2}^{1-\frac{3}{2p}}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{4}}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2p}}.

This bound is smaller than (
8.6
) when
\lambda_{k-1}\ll\lambda_{k}
. This completes the proof.

9.
Model examples on the sphere

In this section we construct the model profiles which will be used in the proof of the sharpness results in Section
10
. We work on the round sphere
S^{n}
and write
d=n-1
for the dimension transverse to a geodesic. The degree-
\nu
spherical harmonics are

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(S^{n})=\{Y\in C^{\infty}(S^{n}):-\Delta_{S^{n}}Y=\nu(\nu+n-1)Y\}.

All lower examples below are exact spherical harmonics. Replacing
\nu
by the true spectral parameter
\sqrt{\nu(\nu+n-1)}
changes only constants and hence does not change any power of the frequency.

9.1.
Basic beams and one-packet models

Write

x=(x_{1},x_{2},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad x_{0}=(1,0,0).

For
|\eta|<1
, set

\omega(\eta)=(\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}},\eta)\in S^{d},\qquad a_{\eta}=(1,i\omega(\eta))\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1},

and

q_{\nu,\eta}(x)=\kappa_{\nu,n}(a_{\eta}\cdot x)^{\nu},\qquad\kappa_{\nu,n}=\left(\frac{|S^{n}|\Gamma((n+1)/2)\nu!}{\Gamma(\nu+(n+1)/2)}\right)^{-1/2}.

Lemma 9.1

(Gaussian beam)
.

For every
\nu\geq 1
and every
|\eta|,|\zeta|<1
,

q_{\nu,\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(S^{n}),\qquad\|q_{\nu,\eta}\|_{2}=1,\qquad\kappa_{\nu,n}\approx\nu^{d/4},

and

\langle q_{\nu,\eta},q_{\nu,\zeta}\rangle_{L^{2}(S^{n})}=\left(\frac{1+\omega(\eta)\cdot\omega(\zeta)}{2}\right)^{\nu}.

Consequently, after restricting
|\eta|,|\zeta|\leq\delta_{0}
,

\left|\langle q_{\nu,\eta},q_{\nu,\zeta}\rangle\right|\lesssim e^{-c\nu|\eta-\zeta|^{2}},\qquad\left|\langle q_{\nu,\eta},q_{\nu,\zeta}\rangle\right|\gtrsim e^{-C\nu|\eta-\zeta|^{2}}.

Proof.

We first check that the beams are spherical harmonics. Since

a_{\eta}\cdot a_{\eta}=1+(i\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}})^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}(i\eta_{j})^{2}=0,

one has

\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(a_{\eta}\cdot x)^{\nu}=\nu(\nu-1)(a_{\eta}\cdot a_{\eta})(a_{\eta}\cdot x)^{\nu-2}=0.

Thus
(a_{\eta}\cdot x)^{\nu}
is a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree

\nu
. Its restriction to
S^{n}
is therefore an eigenfunction of

-\Delta_{S^{n}}
with eigenvalue
\nu(\nu+n-1)
, hence belongs to

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(S^{n})
.

It remains to compute the normalization and the overlaps. For

c\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
we use the standard entire expansion

\int_{S^{n}}e^{c\cdot x}\,dS(x)=|S^{n}|\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{(c\cdot c/4)^{m}}{m!\Gamma(m+(n+1)/2)}.

Indeed, this is the usual Bessel-function formula for real
c
, continued
analytically to complex
c
. Apply it with

c=sa_{\eta}+t\overline{a_{\zeta}}
. Since

a_{\eta}\cdot a_{\eta}=\overline{a_{\zeta}}\cdot\overline{a_{\zeta}}=0
,

c\cdot c=2st\,a_{\eta}\cdot\overline{a_{\zeta}}=2st\bigl(1+\omega(\eta)\cdot\omega(\zeta)\bigr).

On the other hand,

\int_{S^{n}}e^{sa_{\eta}\cdot x+t\overline{a_{\zeta}}\cdot x}\,dS(x)=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\frac{s^{j}t^{k}}{j!k!}\int_{S^{n}}(a_{\eta}\cdot x)^{j}(\overline{a_{\zeta}}\cdot x)^{k}\,dS(x).

Comparing the coefficient of
s^{\nu}t^{\nu}
gives

\int_{S^{n}}(a_{\eta}\cdot x)^{\nu}(\overline{a_{\zeta}}\cdot x)^{\nu}\,dS(x)=|S^{n}|\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\frac{\nu!}{\Gamma(\nu+(n+1)/2)}\left(\frac{1+\omega(\eta)\cdot\omega(\zeta)}{2}\right)^{\nu}.

Taking
\zeta=\eta
shows that the constant
\kappa_{\nu,n}
makes

\|q_{\nu,\eta}\|_{2}=1
, and the same identity gives the stated overlap
formula. Stirling’s formula yields

\frac{\Gamma(\nu+(n+1)/2)}{\nu!}\approx\nu^{(n-1)/2}=\nu^{d/2},

so
\kappa_{\nu,n}\approx\nu^{d/4}
.

Finally,

\frac{1+\omega(\eta)\cdot\omega(\zeta)}{2}=1-\frac{|\omega(\eta)-\omega(\zeta)|^{2}}{4}.

After restricting to a sufficiently small ball
|\eta|,|\zeta|\leq\delta_{0}
,
the map
\eta\mapsto\omega(\eta)
is bi-Lipschitz and

|\omega(\eta)-\omega(\zeta)|^{2}/4\leq 1/2
. Therefore

1-C|\eta-\zeta|^{2}\leq\frac{1+\omega(\eta)\cdot\omega(\zeta)}{2}\leq 1-c|\eta-\zeta|^{2}.

Raising to the
\nu
-th power and using

(1-u)^{\nu}\leq e^{-\nu u}
and
(1-u)^{\nu}\geq e^{-2\nu u}
for

0\leq u\leq 1/2
gives the two Gaussian overlap bounds.
∎

Lemma 9.2

(Envelope and zonal)
.

Fix
K\geq 1
. After choosing
r_{*}=r_{*}(K)>0
sufficiently small, the
following holds for every
\nu^{-1}\leq r\leq r_{*}
. There is a normalized

u_{\nu,r}\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(S^{n})
such that, in the Fermi chart

x(t,z)=\left(\cos|z|\cos t,\ \cos|z|\sin t,\ \frac{\sin|z|}{|z|}z\right),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

we have the lower bound

(9.1)

|u_{\nu,r}(x(t,z))|\gtrsim\nu^{d/4}r^{-d/4}

whenever
|t|\leq cr
and
|z|\leq c(r/\nu)^{1/2}
, and the tail bound

(9.2)

|u_{\nu,r}(x(t,z))|\leq C_{K}\nu^{d/4}r^{-d/4}\left(1+\frac{|t|}{r}\right)^{-d/2}

whenever
|t|\leq r_{*}
and
|z|\leq K(r/\nu)^{1/2}
.

In particular, the case
r=\nu^{-1}
, after rotation, gives a normalized
zonal function
p_{\nu,y}\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(S^{n})
satisfying

|p_{\nu,y}(x)|\gtrsim\nu^{d/2}\qquad\text{if }\operatorname{dist}_{S^{n}}(x,y)\leq c\nu^{-1}.

Consequently, if
E\subset B(y,c\mu^{-1})
and
\nu\leq\mu
, then

|p_{\nu,y}|\gtrsim\nu^{d/2}
on
E
.

Proof.

Let
\delta=(\nu r)^{-1/2}
, and choose a fixed nonnegative

\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\{|\theta|\leq 2\varepsilon\})
, equal to one for

|\theta|\leq\varepsilon
. Define

U_{\nu,r}(x)=\int\psi(\eta/\delta)q_{\nu,\eta}(x)\,d\eta,\qquad u_{\nu,r}=U_{\nu,r}/\|U_{\nu,r}\|_{2}.

By the Gaussian overlap estimate in Lemma
9.1
,

\|U_{\nu,r}\|_{2}^{2}\approx\delta^{d}\nu^{-d/2},\qquad\|U_{\nu,r}\|_{2}^{-1}\approx\nu^{d/4}\delta^{-d/2}.

On the box
|t|\leq cr
,
|z|\leq c(r/\nu)^{1/2}
, the factors

(a_{\eta}\cdot x(t,z))^{\nu}
,
|\eta|\leq 2\varepsilon\delta
, have modulus

\gtrsim 1
and phases differing by at most a sufficiently small absolute constant, provided
c
and

\varepsilon
are small. Hence

|U_{\nu,r}(x(t,z))|\gtrsim\nu^{d/4}\delta^{d},

and normalization gives (
9.1
).

For the upper bound, put
\eta=\delta\theta
. Then

u_{\nu,r}(x(t,z))=B_{\nu,r}\int\psi(\theta)\exp\{\nu\log w_{\delta\theta}(t,z)\}\,d\theta,\qquad B_{\nu,r}\approx\nu^{d/4}r^{-d/4},

where

w_{\eta}(t,z)=a_{\eta}\cdot x(t,z)=\cos\rho\cos t+i\bigl(\cos\rho\sin t\,(1-|\eta|^{2})^{1/2}+\beta_{\rho}z\cdot\eta\bigr),\quad\beta_{\rho}=\frac{\sin\rho}{\rho},\quad\rho=|z|.

On the region
|z|\leq K(r/\nu)^{1/2}
, the logarithm is taken on a fixed
branch and
|w_{\delta\theta}|\approx 1
. Write

\Phi(\theta)=\operatorname{Im}(\nu\log w_{\delta\theta}(t,z)),\qquad A(\theta)=\psi(\theta)\exp\{\operatorname{Re}(\nu\log w_{\delta\theta}(t,z))\},\qquad\Lambda=1+\frac{|t|}{r}.

A Taylor expansion of the explicit formula for
w_{\eta}
, using

\nu\delta^{2}=1/r
, gives uniformly on the support of
\psi

\partial_{\theta_{i}}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\Phi(\theta)=-\frac{\sin t\cos t}{r}\delta_{ij}+O_{K}\!\left(1+\varepsilon\frac{|t|}{r}\right),

and the scaled symbol bounds

|\partial_{\theta}^{\alpha}\Phi(\theta)|\leq C_{\alpha,K}\Lambda\quad(|\alpha|\geq 2),\qquad|\partial_{\theta}^{\alpha}A(\theta)|\leq C_{\alpha,K}\Lambda^{|\alpha|/2}.

If
|t|\lesssim r
, the integral is
O(1)
, which is the desired bound. If

|t|\geq C_{0}r
, then
|\sin t\cos t|\approx|t|
on the fixed small coordinate
interval. Taking
C_{0}
large and
\varepsilon
small, the phase has Hessian
of size
\Lambda
. The stationary phase estimate with the above scaled
symbol bounds gives

\left|\int A(\theta)e^{i\Phi(\theta)}\,d\theta\right|\leq C_{K}\Lambda^{-d/2}.

Multiplying by
B_{\nu,r}
proves (
9.2
).

Finally, when
r=\nu^{-1}
, the lower-bound box contains a ball of radius

c\nu^{-1}
after reducing
c
. Rotating the construction gives the stated zonal function.
∎

9.2.
The packet profile

For
0<L\lesssim 1
,
R\geq 1
, write
E_{L,R}
for a rectangular packet box in one of the local charts used above,

E_{L,R}=\{|s|\lesssim L,\ |y|\lesssim R^{-1/2}\},\qquad|E_{L,R}|\approx LR^{-d/2}.

The center of the box may be translated along the reference geodesic. The following lemmas give the individual constructions that make up the profile.

Lemma 9.3

(Beam block:
1\leq R\leq\nu
)
.

Assume
1\leq R\leq\nu
. One can find a normalized
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(S^{n})
and a set
E
contained in a fixed coordinate patch, with

|E|\approx LR^{-d/2},\qquad|u(x)|\gtrsim R^{d/4}\quad(x\in E).

Proof.

Use the chart

X(s,y)=(\cos s,\sin s\,\omega(y)),\qquad s\in[1/4,1/2],\quad|y|\ll 1,

where
\omega(y)=(\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}},y)\in S^{d}
. In this chart the beams from Lemma
9.1
satisfy, after shrinking the chart,

(9.3)

|q_{\nu,\eta}(X(s,y))|\leq C\nu^{d/4}e^{-c\nu|y-\eta|^{2}},\qquad|q_{\nu,\eta}(X(s,y))|\geq c\nu^{d/4}

whenever
|y-\eta|\leq c\nu^{-1/2}
. This follows directly from

q_{\nu,\eta}(X(s,y))=\kappa_{\nu,n}\bigl(\cos s+i\sin s\,\omega(\eta)\cdot\omega(y)\bigr)^{\nu}

and the bi-Lipschitz property of
\omega
near the origin.

Choose a subinterval
I_{L}\subset[1/4,1/2]
with
|I_{L}|\approx L
. Let
D\gg 1
be fixed, and choose a maximal
D\nu^{-1/2}
-separated set

\mathcal{A}\subset\{\eta:|\eta|\leq c_{0}R^{-1/2}\}.

Then

N:=|\mathcal{A}|\approx(\nu/R)^{d/2}.

For
\eta\in\mathcal{A}
, set

T_{\eta}=\{X(s,y):s\in I_{L},\ |y-\eta|\leq c\nu^{-1/2}\},\qquad E=\bigcup_{\eta\in\mathcal{A}}T_{\eta}.

The tubes
T_{\eta}
are disjoint if
c\ll D
, and therefore

|E|\approx NL\nu^{-d/2}\approx LR^{-d/2}.

Figure 3.
Beam block:
1\leq R\leq\nu
.

Set

Q=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{A}}q_{\nu,\eta}.

By the Gaussian overlap bound in Lemma
9.1
,

\|Q\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{\eta,\zeta\in\mathcal{A}}e^{-c\nu|\eta-\zeta|^{2}}\leq C_{D}N.

If
x\in T_{\eta}
, then the
\eta
-beam is
\gtrsim\nu^{d/4}
by (
9.3
), while the remaining beams contribute at most

C\nu^{d/4}\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\{\eta\}}e^{-c\nu|\eta-\zeta|^{2}}.

Taking
D
sufficiently large makes this tail smaller than half of the main term. Hence
|Q|\gtrsim\nu^{d/4}
on
E
. With
u=Q/\|Q\|_{2}
,

|u(x)|\gtrsim\nu^{d/4}N^{-1/2}\approx R^{d/4},\qquad x\in E.

∎

Lemma 9.4

(Train construction)
.

Assume
d\geq 3
,
\nu^{-1}\leq r\leq r_{*}
, and let
P_{a}
denote a rotation of the packet in Lemma
9.2
whose core is centered at the time
a
on the reference geodesic. If
a_{j}
are contained in a fixed coordinate interval and

|a_{j}-a_{\ell}|\geq Dr\qquad(j\neq\ell),

then, for
D
sufficiently large but fixed,

(9.4)

\Big\|\sum_{j}P_{a_{j}}\Big\|_{2}\lesssim N^{1/2},\qquad N=\#\{j\},

and on the union of the cores

F_{j}=\{x(t,z):|t-a_{j}|\leq cr,\ |z|\leq c(r/\nu)^{1/2}\}

one has

(9.5)

\Big|\sum_{j}P_{a_{j}}(x)\Big|\gtrsim\nu^{d/4}r^{-d/4}.

Proof.

Let
R_{a}
be the rotation along the reference geodesic, so that

R_{a}x(t,z)=x(t+a,z)
, and write
P_{a}=R_{a}u_{\nu,r}
. The lower bound on a
single core and the tail estimate away from that core are exactly
(
9.1
) and (
9.2
), after replacing

t
by
t-a
. It remains to prove the almost-orthogonality estimate.

By rotation invariance it suffices to estimate
\langle P_{0},P_{\tau}\rangle
,
where
\tau=b-a
. Put
\delta=(\nu r)^{-1/2}
and

\alpha_{\eta}=(1-|\eta|^{2})^{1/2}
. Lemma
9.1
, applied after
rotating one beam by
\tau
, gives

\langle q_{\nu,\eta},R_{\tau}q_{\nu,\zeta}\rangle=\Gamma_{\tau}(\eta,\zeta)^{\nu},

where

\Gamma_{\tau}(\eta,\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\cos\tau\,(1+\alpha_{\eta}\alpha_{\zeta})+\eta\cdot\zeta+i\sin\tau\,(\alpha_{\eta}+\alpha_{\zeta})\Bigr).

Consequently,

\langle P_{0},P_{\tau}\rangle=c_{\nu,r}\iint\psi(\theta)\overline{\psi(\vartheta)}\Gamma_{\tau}(\delta\theta,\delta\vartheta)^{\nu}\,d\theta\,d\vartheta,\qquad c_{\nu,r}\approx r^{-d/2}.

For
|\tau|\lesssim r
, Cauchy’s inequality gives

|\langle P_{0},P_{\tau}\rangle|\leq 1
. Assume
|\tau|\geq C_{0}r
. Taylor expansion of
the explicit kernel gives

\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\nu\log\Gamma_{\tau}(\delta\theta,\delta\vartheta)\bigr)\leq-c\frac{|\theta-\vartheta|^{2}}{r}+C.

With

u=\frac{\theta+\vartheta}{2},\qquad v=\frac{\theta-\vartheta}{\sqrt{r}},

the Jacobian is
d\theta\,d\vartheta=r^{d/2}\,du\,dv
, which cancels

c_{\nu,r}
. The same expansion gives, in the
u
-variables,

\partial_{u_{i}}\partial_{u_{j}}\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\nu\log\Gamma_{\tau}(\delta\theta,\delta\vartheta)\bigr)=-\frac{\sin\tau}{r}\delta_{ij}+O\!\left(1+\varepsilon\frac{|\tau|}{r}\right).

On the fixed coordinate interval,
|\sin\tau|\approx|\tau|
. Therefore the
inner
u
-integral has a nondegenerate Hessian of size
|\tau|/r
, while the

v
-dependence is Gaussian. The same scaled stationary-phase estimate used in
Lemma
9.2
yields

(9.6)

|\langle P_{0},P_{\tau}\rangle|\leq C\left(1+\frac{|\tau|}{r}\right)^{-d/2}.

Rotating gives the same bound for
P_{a}
and
P_{b}
.

Now

\Big\|\sum_{j}P_{a_{j}}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{j,\ell}|\langle P_{a_{j}},P_{a_{\ell}}\rangle|\leq C\sum_{j}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+D|m|)^{-d/2}\lesssim N,

because the points
a_{j}
are
Dr
-separated on a line and
d/2>1
. This
proves (
9.4
).

Finally, fix
x\in F_{j}
. The main packet satisfies

|P_{a_{j}}(x)|\gtrsim\nu^{d/4}r^{-d/4}
by
(
9.1
). For
\ell\neq j
, the upper bound
(
9.2
) gives

|P_{a_{\ell}}(x)|\leq C\nu^{d/4}r^{-d/4}\left(1+\frac{|a_{\ell}-a_{j}|}{r}\right)^{-d/2},

after decreasing the core constant
c
. Hence

\sum_{\ell\neq j}|P_{a_{\ell}}(x)|\leq C\nu^{d/4}r^{-d/4}\sum_{m\geq 1}(1+Dm)^{-d/2}.

Choosing the fixed separation constant
D
large makes this tail smaller than
the main term. This proves (
9.5
).
∎

Lemma 9.7

(Common active set for admissible triples)
.

,

Proof.

Work in one box
B_{L,R}=\{x(t,z):t\in I,\ z\in Q_{R}\}
. A shift means the origin of a packet grid. For a time scale
\ell\leq L
and shift

For a transverse scale

and

so that

\left|I\cap\bigcap_{a=1}^{A}S_{\ell_{a},\tau_{a}}\right|\gtrsim L,\qquad\left|Q_{R}\cap\bigcap_{b=1}^{B}G_{\rho_{b},\sigma_{b}}\right|\gtrsim R^{-d/2}.

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{d/2}b^{(3d+1)/2}c^{-1/2}\left(cb^{-(d+2)}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{d/2}b^{\frac{3d+1}{2}-\frac{d+2}{p}}c^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}.

This is the first branch in the first range of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume next that
ac\geq b^{2}.

Choose

L=a^{-1},\ R=ac.

Then

|E_{L,R}|\approx a^{-1}(ac)^{-d/2}=a^{-1-d/2}c^{-d/2}.

The
a
-factor is zonal and
A_{a}(L,R)=a^{d/2}.

The
b
-factor is a zonal train and

A_{b}(L,R)=b^{d/2}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{-1/2}=a^{1/2}b^{(d-1)/2}.

The
c
-factor is an envelope and

A_{c}(L,R)=R^{d/4}=(ac)^{d/4}.

The
e
-factor is a beam block or an envelope, and

A_{e}(L,R)=R^{d/4}=(ac)^{d/4}.

Therefore,
Lemma
9.9
gives

\displaystyle L^{(k)}_{p,n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{(2d+1)/2}b^{(d-1)/2}c^{d/2}\left(a^{-1-d/2}c^{-d/2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{\frac{2d+1}{2}-\frac{d+2}{2p}}b^{\frac{d-1}{2}}c^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{2p}}.

This is the second branch in the first range of Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, the ratio of the first branch to the second branch is

\left(\frac{b^{2}}{ac}\right)^{{(d+1)}/{2}-{(d+2)}/{(2p)}}.

Since
1\leq p\leq(d+2)/(d+1)
, the exponent is nonpositive, so the two subcases above select the smaller of the two branches.

We now turn to the remaining ranges. Put

a=\lambda_{k-2},\qquad b=\lambda_{k-1},\qquad c=\lambda_{k}.

The filler factors contribute

\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}.

First suppose

p_{2}\leq p\leq p_{1}=\frac{d}{d-1}.

Choose

L=a^{-1},\ R=ab.

Then

|E_{L,R}|\approx a^{-1}(ab)^{-d/2}=a^{-1-d/2}b^{-d/2}.

The
a
-factor is zonal and
A_{a}(L,R)=a^{d/2}.

The
b
-factor is an envelope and

A_{b}(L,R)=R^{d/4}=(ab)^{d/4}.

The
c
-factor is a beam block or an envelope, and in either case

A_{c}(L,R)=R^{d/4}=(ab)^{d/4}.

Hence,
Lemma
9.9
yields

\displaystyle L^{(k)}_{p,n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{d}b^{d/2}\left(a^{-1-d/2}b^{-d/2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{d-\frac{d+2}{2p}}b^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{2p}}.

This is precisely the branch in Theorem
1.2
for

.

Next assume

There are two frequency regimes.

The

Consequently, Lemma
9.9
gives

\displaystyle L^{(k)}_{p,n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{d}b^{1/2}c^{(d-1)/2}\left(a^{-1-d/2}c^{-d/2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{d-\frac{d+2}{2p}}b^{1/2}c^{\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d}{2p}}.

This is the first branch in the range
p_{1}\leq p\leq p_{0}
.

If instead

The
a
-factor is zonal and

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}b^{d-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{p}}.

\left(\frac{ac}{b^{2}}\right)^{{(d-1)}/{2}-{d}/{(2p)}}.

Since

, the exponent is nonnegative, and the two subcases again select the smaller branch.

,

p_{0}\leq p\leq 2
.

gives

\displaystyle L^{(k)}_{p,n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{(d+1)/2}b^{d-1/2}\left(a^{-1}b^{-d}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\right)a^{\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}b^{d-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{p}}.

This is the second branch in the range

. In this range, the ratio of the first branch to the second branch is

\left(\frac{ac}{b^{2}}\right)^{{1}/{p}-{1}/{2}},

and the exponent is nonnegative because

. Hence the two subcases select the smaller branch.

Combining the four ranges, and recalling that

1.2

Proposition 10.5
.

Let
n=3
,
k\geq 3
, and
p\leq 2
. The
k
-linear estimates in Theorem
1.4
are sharp.

Proof.

Here
d=2
. We use only the admissible non-train subcases from the packet profile: in the envelope range
\nu\leq R\leq\nu^{2}
we always have
L\leq\nu/R
, and in the zonal range
R\geq\nu^{2}
we always have
L\nu\lesssim 1
. Thus the geometric objects which occur below are zonals, beam blocks, and envelopes, but not trains. The unnamed lower-frequency factors are chosen to be zonal functions from Lemma
9.2
, centered at the same point as the packet boxes.

.

\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}
.

a
-factor is zonal and

.

b
-factor is an envelope at the coarser transverse scale

. Indeed

E_{L,R}
.

c
-factor is an envelope
and
A_{c}(L,R)=(ac)^{1/2}.

e
-factor is a beam block or an envelope, and
A_{e}(L,R)=(ac)^{1/2}
.
Hence,
Lemma
9.9
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p,3}^{(k)}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}\right)a^{5/2}b^{1/2}c\,\left(a^{-2}c^{-1}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}\right)a^{\frac{5}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}b^{1/2}c^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Substituting back
a=\lambda_{k-3}
,
b=\lambda_{k-2}
, and
c=\lambda_{k-1}
, this is exactly the power
\mathcal{E}_{0}
in Theorem
1.2
for
1\leq p\leq 4/3
.

We now suppose that

\frac{4}{3}\leq p\leq 2.

Set

a=\lambda_{k-2},\qquad b=\lambda_{k-1},\qquad c=\lambda_{k}.

We use

L=a^{-1},\ R=ab.

Thus

|E_{L,R}|\approx a^{-1}(ab)^{-1}=a^{-2}b^{-1}.

The filler factors contribute
\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}
.
The
a
-factor is zonal and

A_{a}(L,R)=a.

The
b
-factor is an envelope and

A_{b}(L,R)=(ab)^{1/2}.

The
c
-factor is a beam block or an envelope, and

A_{c}(L,R)=(ab)^{1/2}.

Consequently,
Lemma
9.9
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p,3}^{(k)}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}\right)a^{2}b\,\left(a^{-2}b^{-1}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}\right)a^{2-\frac{2}{p}}b^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
a=\lambda_{k-2}
and
b=\lambda_{k-1}
, this is exactly the power
\mathcal{E}_{0}
in Theorem
1.2
for
4/3\leq p\leq 2
.

Combining the two ranges proves the sharp lower bounds for the powers in the case
n=3
. This proves the proposition.

Here
d=1
. We use only the admissible non-train cases from the packet profile. Thus the geometric objects which occur below are zonals, envelopes, and beam blocks; no envelope trains or zonal trains are used. All packets are centered on the same geodesic, and the unnamed lower-frequency factors are chosen to be zonal functions from Lemma
9.2
.

Choose
m\in\{4,5,6\}
by

m=6\quad\left(1\leq p\leq\frac{6}{5}\right),\qquad m=5\quad\left(\frac{6}{5}\leq p\leq\frac{3}{2}\right),\qquad m=4\quad\left(\frac{3}{2}\leq p\leq 2\right).

Set

a=k-m+1,\qquad\alpha=\lambda_{a},\qquad\omega=\lambda_{k-1}.

We use
L=\alpha^{-1},R=\alpha\omega.

Thus,

|E_{L,R}|\approx\alpha^{-1}(\alpha\omega)^{-1/2}=\alpha^{-3/2}\omega^{-1/2}.

The filler factors are zonal and contribute
\prod_{j=1}^{a-1}\lambda_{j}
.
The
\alpha
-factor is also zonal and

A_{\alpha}(L,R)=\alpha^{1/2}.

For the intermediate factors
a+1\leq j\leq k-2
, we use the larger tube with the same longitudinal length and transverse parameter

R_{j}=\alpha\lambda_{j}.

Since
\alpha\leq\lambda_{j}
, we have

\lambda_{j}\leq R_{j}=\alpha\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda_{j}^{2},\qquad L=\alpha^{-1}=\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\alpha\lambda_{j}}=\frac{\lambda_{j}}{R_{j}}.

Hence each such factor is an endpoint envelope, and

A_{\lambda_{j}}(L,R_{j})=(\alpha\lambda_{j})^{1/4},\qquad a+1\leq j\leq k-2.

Because
\lambda_{j}\leq\omega
, the tube defining
E_{L,R}
is contained in this larger envelope tube. Therefore the same lower bound holds on
E_{L,R}
.

The
\omega
-factor is an endpoint envelope, and
A_{\omega}(L,R)=(\alpha\omega)^{1/4}.
The
\lambda_{k}
-factor is a beam block or an envelope, and
A_{\lambda_{k}}(\alpha^{-1},\alpha\omega)=(\alpha\omega)^{1/4}.

Thus, Lemma
9.9
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p,2}^{(k)}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{a-1}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\right)\alpha^{1/2}\left(\prod_{j=a+1}^{k-2}(\alpha\lambda_{j})^{1/4}\right)(\alpha\omega)^{1/4}(\alpha\omega)^{1/4}\left(\alpha^{-3/2}\omega^{-1/2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{a-1}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\right)\alpha^{\frac{m+1}{4}-\frac{3}{2p}}\left(\prod_{j=a+1}^{k-2}\lambda_{j}^{1/4}\right)\omega^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}}.

Taking
m=6
, so that
a=k-5
and
\alpha=\lambda_{k-5}
, gives for
1\leq p\leq 6/5

\mathcal{L}_{p,2}^{(k)}\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-6}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\right)\lambda_{k-5}^{\frac{7}{4}-\frac{3}{2p}}\lambda_{k-4}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-3}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-2}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}}.

Taking
m=5
, so that
a=k-4
and
\alpha=\lambda_{k-4}
, gives for
6/5\leq p\leq 3/2

\mathcal{L}_{p,2}^{(k)}\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-5}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\right)\lambda_{k-4}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{2p}}\lambda_{k-3}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-2}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}}.

Taking
m=4
, so that
a=k-3
and
\alpha=\lambda_{k-3}
, gives for
3/2\leq p\leq 2

\mathcal{L}_{p,2}^{(k)}\gtrsim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-4}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\right)\lambda_{k-3}^{\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3}{2p}}\lambda_{k-2}^{1/4}\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}}.

These are exactly the three powers in Theorem
1.2
for
n=2
. This proves the proposition.
∎

10.2.
Sharpness for
p>2

Proposition 10.7
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
k\geq 2
, and
p>2
. The
k
-linear estimates in Theorem
1.4
are sharp.

Proof.

The proof of sharpness for
p>2
only needs the envelopes in Lemma
9.2
. For
\nu^{-1}\leq r\ll 1
, there is an
L^{2}
-normalized spherical harmonic satisfying

|u_{\nu,r}|\gtrsim(\nu/r)^{d/4}

on a tube of length
r
, transverse radius
(r/\nu)^{1/2}
, and measure
\approx r(r/\nu)^{d/2}
. Put all envelopes on the same geodesic. If
r_{1}\geq\cdots\geq r_{k}
and
r_{j}\geq\lambda_{j}^{-1}
, then the smallest tube gives

(10.2)

\mathcal{L}_{p,n}^{(k)}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\gtrsim\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}}{r_{j}}\right)^{d/4}\left[r_{k}\left(\frac{r_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}\right)^{d/2}\right]^{1/p}.

For
n=2
and
2\leq p\leq 3
, take

r_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{-1}\ (j\leq k-2),\qquad r_{k-1}=r_{k}=\lambda_{k-2}^{-1}.

Then (
10.2
) gives

\mathcal{L}_{p,2}^{(k)}\gtrsim\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\Big)\lambda_{k-2}^{1-\frac{3}{2p}}\lambda_{k-1}^{1/4}\lambda_{k}^{1/4-\frac{1}{2p}}.

Next take

r_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{-1}\ (j\leq k-1),\qquad r_{k}=\lambda_{k-1}^{-1}.

For every
d\geq 1
, this yields

\mathcal{L}_{p,n}^{(k)}\gtrsim\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k-2}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\Big)\lambda_{k-1}^{\frac{3d}{4}-\frac{d+2}{2p}}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{d}{4}-\frac{d}{2p}}.

Finally, taking
r_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{-1}
for all
j
gives

\mathcal{L}_{p,n}^{(k)}\gtrsim\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}^{d/2}\Big)\lambda_{k}^{d/2-\frac{d+1}{p}}.

∎
