Tricriticality and chaos in a generalized Allee-logistic map
Marcelo A. Pires 1, José S. Andrade Jr. 2, Hans J. Herrmann 2,3

Abstract
We present a novel nonlinear dynamical model, the generalized Allee-logistic (GAL) map given by x t + 1 = r ​ x t ​ ( 1 − x t ) ​ G ​ ( x t ) x_{t+1}=rx_{t}(1-x_{t})G(x_{t}) where G ⁡ ( x t ) = m ⁡ ( x t − h ) + 1 − m G(x_{t})=m(x_{t}-h)+1-m incorporates the Allee effect with magnitude m m and threshold h h . The case m = 0 m=0 yields the logistic map with a continuous transition to extinction. Conversely, m = 1 m=1 recovers a previously studied model that undergoes only a discontinuous extinction-to-active transition. Between these extremes, the GAL map exhibits nontrivial phenomena, including tricriticality with a closed-form expression for the tricritical point and a universal crossover function. Under a small external input, we verify Widom-like relations. We also note that the Allee effect disfavors the onset of chaos. Our work establishes additional bridges between analytically tractable chaotic maps, nonequilibrium tricriticality, and Allee effects.

II
Model

We introduce a generalized Allee-logistic (GAL) map given by

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=rx_{t}(1-x_{t})G(x_{t}),

(1)

\displaystyle G(x_{t})

\displaystyle=m(x_{t}-h)+1-m,

(2)

where
x_{t}
represents the population density at time
t=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
,
m\in[0,1]
is the magnitude of the Allee effect,
h\in[0,1]
is the Allee threshold. The parameter
r
is the intrinsic growth rate of the population.
Since
x_{t}-h\leq 1
, the factor
G(x_{t})\leq 1
incorporates the Allee effect through a reduction in the effective population growth.
Clearly, in Eq.(
1
), only the parameters that allow the population fraction to satisfy

x_{t}\in[0,1]
are permitted.

Observe that when:

•

m=0
, we find
G(x_{t})=1
, and the model reduces to the standard logistic map.

•

m=1
, we obtain
G(x_{t})=x_{t}-h
, and the model recovers a previous particular case
40
.

III
Results

The GAL map can be written as

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=f(x_{t})=A_{r,m}x_{t}^{3}+B_{r,m,h}x_{t}^{2}+C_{r,m,h}x_{t},

(3)

where the coefficients are given by:

\displaystyle A_{r,m}

\displaystyle=-rm,

(4)

\displaystyle B_{r,m,h}

\displaystyle=r(2m+mh-1),

(5)

\displaystyle C_{r,m,h}

\displaystyle=r(1-m-mh).

(6)

From now on, to avoid overloading the notation, we will omit the subscripts in Eqs. (
4
-
6
).

III.1
Stationary state

The condition for the stationary state is

\displaystyle x_{t}=x_{t+1}=x_{\infty}\equiv\rho.

(7)

Then, using Eq. (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle f(\rho)-\rho=A\rho^{3}+B\rho^{2}+(C-1)\rho=0.

(8)

The three solutions of Eq. (
8
) are:

\displaystyle\rho_{0}

\displaystyle=0,

(9)

\displaystyle\rho^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{-B\pm\sqrt{B^{2}-4A(C-1)}}{2A}.

(10)

The trivial fixed point
\rho_{0}=0
represents an extinction state, and the nontrivial solutions
\rho^{\pm}
represent an active population. These analytical results and corresponding simulations are displayed in Fig.
1
.

III.2
Critical point

To analyze stability, we first compute the derivative of the map:

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)=3Ax^{2}+2Bx+C.

(11)

The critical condition,
|f^{\prime}(0)|=1
, combined with Eq. (
6
) yields

\displaystyle r_{c}=\frac{1}{1-m-mh}.

(12)

This equation gives the critical point where the extinction state loses stability, as evident in Fig.
1
.

III.3
Tricritical point (TCP)

The TCP marks the point at which continuous and discontinuous transition solutions meet.
This requires the coalescence of
\rho^{\pm}
with
\rho_{0}
.
Consequently, the coefficients of the linear and quadratic terms in Eq. (
8
) must vanish at the transition:

\displaystyle C_{T}=1,\quad B_{T}=0.

(13)

Substituting these conditions in Eqs. (
5
,
6
) leads to a closed-form for the TCP:

\displaystyle(h_{T},r_{T})=\left(\frac{1-2m}{m},\frac{1}{m}\right).

(14)

The condition
0\leq h_{T}\leq 1
with
h_{T}=(1-2m)/m
implies that a TCP exists if
1/3\leq m\leq 1/2
. The transition to extinction can be:

\displaystyle\text{Only continuous if }m<1/3

(15)

\displaystyle\text{Continuous or discontinuous if }1/3\leq m\leq 1/2

(16)

\displaystyle\text{Only discontinuous if }m>1/2

(17)

The above scenarios are illustrated in Fig.

III.5
Temporal scaling

We turn our focus to the relaxation toward the fixed points.
Following
20

x_{t+1}-x_{t}=\frac{x_{t+1}-x_{t}}{(t+1)-(t)}=\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}\approx\frac{dx}{dt}
in the limit of small perturbations near the extinction state
\rho_{0}=0

3

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{dt}\approx Ax_{t}^{3}+Bx_{t}^{2}+(C-1)x_{t}.

(19)

III.5.1
Above the extinction point

When
C>1

from Eq. (
19

we obtain an exponential behavior
x_{t}\sim e^{t/\tau}

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\sim|r-r_{c}|^{-z_{D}},\quad z_{D}=1,

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\sim|r-r_{T}|^{-z_{T}},\quad z_{T}=1.

At the extinction point

Critical case

\displaystyle\sim t^{-\alpha_{D}},\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha_{D}=1.

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle\sim t^{-\alpha_{T}},\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha_{T}=\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore, at the transition we note different exponents given by Eqs. (

III.7
External flux

We add a small external flux term
w
(e.g., migration) to the GAL map:

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=f(x_{t})+w=Ax_{t}^{3}+Bx_{t}^{2}+Cx_{t}+w

(26)

The stationary condition is given by

\displaystyle F(\rho,w)=A\rho^{3}+B\rho^{2}+(C-1)\rho+w=0.

(27)

At criticality (
C\to 1,B\neq 0
) the dominant terms are
B\rho^{2}+w\approx 0
. Hence, the scaling is

\displaystyle\rho(r=r_{c})\sim w^{1/\delta^{w}_{D}},\quad\text{with}\quad\delta^{w}_{D}=2.

(28)

At tricriticality (
C\to 1,B\to 0
) the dominant terms are given by
A\rho^{3}+w\approx 0
. Thus, we find

\displaystyle\rho(r=r_{T})\sim w^{1/\delta^{w}_{T}},\quad\text{with}\quad\delta^{w}_{T}=3.

(29)

These scaling relations reveal that the density
\rho
responds differently to external perturbations at both the critical and tricritical points, as corroborated by Fig.
4
.

III.8
Dynamical susceptibility

To further analyze the response of the stationary state to a small external flux
w
, we compute the dynamical susceptibility

\displaystyle\chi\equiv\partialderivative{\rho}{w}\ .

(30)

Differentiating Eq. (
27
) with respect to
w
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\partialderivative{F}{w}=(3A\rho^{2}+2B\rho+(C-1))\chi+1=0.

(31)

When
C>1
, the linear term dominates in both the critical and tricritical cases. From Eq. (
31
), we can calculate the following scaling relations for the susceptibility:

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle\sim|r-r_{c}|^{-\gamma_{D}}\quad\text{with}\quad\gamma_{D}=1,

(32)

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle\sim|r-r_{T}|^{-\gamma_{T}}\quad\text{with}\quad\gamma_{T}=1.

(33)

These exponents reveal a similar scaling for the dynamical susceptibility, as confirmed in Fig.
4
.

III.9
Universal crossover function

We now return to the case without an external flux. We define deviations from the TCP using Eq. (
13
):

\displaystyle\Delta C

\displaystyle=C-C_{T}=C-1,

(34)

\displaystyle\Delta B

\displaystyle=B-B_{T}=B.

(35)

Substituting into Eq. (
8
) we obtain
A\rho^{2}+\Delta B\rho+\Delta C=0
, then after rescaling we get

\displaystyle Y^{2}+Y-V=0,

(36)

where the scaled order parameter
Y
and scaled control parameter
V
are given by

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\rho\frac{A}{\Delta B},

(37)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=-A\frac{\Delta C}{(\Delta B)^{2}}.

(38)

The relevant solution
Y=\mathcal{F}(V)
of Eq. (
36
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(V)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+(1+4V)^{1/2}\right).

(39)

This function is universal since it does not depend directly on the parameters of the model, but only on the scaled variable
V
, as confirmed by the simulations shown in Fig.
4
(f).

III.10
Crossover exponent

Following
48
, we now adopt a standard crossover scaling ansatz for the TDP:

\displaystyle\rho\sim\Delta_{h}^{\beta^{T}/\phi^{T}}\,\mathcal{H}\!\left(\Delta_{r}\,\Delta_{h}^{-1/\phi^{T}}\right),

(40)

where
\Delta_{h}
is the distance from TCP,
\Delta_{r}
is the distance from the critical point,
\mathcal{H}
is a universal crossover function, and
\phi^{T}
is the crossover exponent.

Note that from Eq. (
37
)-(
38
) we have

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\sim\Delta B\ \mathcal{F}(V),

(41)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\sim\Delta C\ (\Delta B)^{-2}.

(42)

To apply the ansatz Eq. (
40
) to our universal function we must map
(\Delta B,\Delta C)
to
(\Delta_{h},\Delta_{r})
.
A natural mapping consistent with Eqs. (
5
-
6
) is

\Delta B\propto\Delta_{h}
and

\Delta C\propto\Delta_{r}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\sim\Delta_{h}\ \mathcal{F}(\Delta_{r}\ (\Delta_{h})^{-2})

(43)

Comparing Eqs. (
40
,
43
) we obtain
\beta^{T}/\phi^{T}=1
and
-1/\phi^{T}=-2
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\phi^{T}

\displaystyle=1/2

(44)

This is the crossover exponent of the TDP universality class
48
;
32
.

III.11
Finite-time Lyapunov exponent

To quantify the average separation of nearby trajectories
we compute the finite-time Lyapunov exponent (FTLE)

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}\equiv\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\log|f^{\prime}(x_{i})\big|,

(45)

where for the GAL map we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\ln|3Ax_{i}^{2}+2Bx_{i}+C|.

(46)

For the critical case (
C\to 1,B\neq 0
) we know that
x_{t}\sim t^{-1}
. Using the leading term of Eq. (
46
) we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}(r_{c})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}2Bx_{i}\;\sim\;t^{-\theta_{D}}\,\log t,\quad\theta_{D}=1.

(47)

For the tricritical case (
C\to 1,B\to 0
), we know that
x_{t}\sim t^{-1/2}
, therefore

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}(r_{T})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}3Ax_{i}^{2}\;\sim\;t^{-\theta_{T}}\,\log t,\quad\theta_{T}=1.

(48)

Both the critical and tricritical cases exhibit the same form
\lambda_{t}\sim t^{-1}\log t
, as confirmed in
Fig.
5
.

In the long-time limit, we compute the Lyapunov exponent

\lambda=\lim_{T\to\infty}\lambda_{T}

The transition from a fixed-point to chaos is mediated by the Feigenbaum-like scenario (cascade of period-doubling bifurcations) even in the presence of the Allee-effect (

In Fig.
