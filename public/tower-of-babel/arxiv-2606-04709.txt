Introduction

Sigmoidal regulatory functions are the workhorse of mathematical models
of gene regulatory networks (GRNs). For decades, the Hill family

h^{+}(x,\theta,n)=x^{n}/(\theta^{n}+x^{n})
and

h^{-}(x,\theta,n)=\theta^{n}/(\theta^{n}+x^{n})
has supplied the standard
parameterisation, encoding cooperativity through the Hill coefficient

n
and threshold sensitivity through the dissociation constant

\theta

[
33
,
23
]
. The
mechanistic origin of Hill functions in equilibrium binding theory
generalises the Michaelis–Menten kinetics that govern enzymatic chains
and, more broadly, the global-stability analyses developed
in
[
20
,
22
]
for full
reversible Michaelis–Menten reactions. Despite the long-standing
success of Hill functions, they develop derivative singularities at
the origin whenever
n
is non-integer
(
h^{+\prime}(x)\to\infty
as
x\to 0^{+}
for
0<n<1
, with
higher-order derivatives diverging on
n\in(k,k+1)
), lack
closed-form antiderivatives and inverses, and admit only a
logarithmically divergent small-
n
expansion
[
33
,
26
,
1
]
. These pathologies
compromise numerical integration in low-expression regimes,
ill-condition Fisher information matrices for parameter estimation, and
prevent reliable observability analysis in feedback control
applications.

A growing body of work has therefore proposed replacing Hill functions
by their logistic counterparts

f^{+}(x,\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{-\lambda(x-\theta)})
and

f^{-}(x,\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{\lambda(x-\theta)})
, with the
parameter matching
\lambda=n/\theta
preserving local input–output
sensitivity at the half-maximal concentration
[
7
,
8
]
. The logistic functions are globally

C^{\infty}
, possess closed-form derivatives with the self-referential
structure
\partial f^{\pm}/\partial x=\pm\lambda f^{\pm}(1-f^{\pm})
,
admit elementary antiderivatives
\int f^{+}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=\lambda^{-1}\ln(1+e^{\lambda(x-\theta)})
, are inverted in closed form
through the logit transformation, and are uniformly Lipschitz with
constant
\lambda/4
. The logistic formulation also captures basal
expression naturally through
f^{+}(0,\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{\lambda\theta})>0
, without ad hoc additive offsets.

1.1
Two competing logistic-based GRN frameworks

Two distinct logistic-based GRN formulations have emerged in the
literature.

Product-of-logistics models

treat each regulatory
interaction as an independent sigmoid factor and combine factors
multiplicatively to capture parallel (AND-like) regulation, with each
regulator carrying its own threshold
\theta_{i,m}
and the directional
character (
+
for activator,
-
for repressor) encoded inside the
exponent. This formulation, developed
in
[
7
,
8
]
and related
works on stability and reduction of gene-expression
models
[
11
,
12
,
18
,
19
,
21
]
,
is the smooth analytic counterpart of the logical step-function GRN
networks introduced by Glass and
Kauffman
[
4
]
, whose high-dimensional periodicity,
chaos, and bifurcation structure on ring circuits have been further
analysed in
[
13
,
16
]
. It preserves a
transparent biological interpretation: each
\theta_{i,m}
maps to a
measurable binding affinity
K_{d}
or half-maximal effective
concentration
\mathrm{EC}_{50}
, and the value of the regulatory
function at the critical point (
x_{j(i,m)}=\theta_{i,m}
for every

m
) equals
(1/2)^{m_{i}}
, the natural Bernoulli-product probability.
The explicit form is introduced as (
7
) in

Section

2.2
, with an equivalent sign-encoded compact
form
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}=\prod_{m}\sigma(\lambda\,\epsilon_{i,m}(x_{j(i,m)}-\theta_{i,m}))

recorded in
Remark

2
.

Weighted-sum models

[
31
,
27
,
29
,
28
,
32
,
30
,
34
,
6
]

aggregate all regulatory inputs into a single weighted sum passed
through one increasing logistic per gene:

\dot{x}_{i}=\kappa_{i}\,\frac{1}{1+e^{-\mu_{i}\!\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}^{\mathrm{S}}x_{j}-\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}\bigr)}}-\gamma_{i}x_{i},\qquad w_{ij}^{\mathrm{S}}\in\mathbb{R},

(1)

with regulatory direction encoded by the sign of
w_{ij}^{\mathrm{S}}
.
This formulation reduces the number of nonlinear terms to one per
gene, an attractive property for bifurcation analysis, parameter
estimation, and feedback-control synthesis. However, the
negative-weight encoding creates structural pathologies. The midpoint
of the increasing sigmoid satisfies
\sum_{j}w_{ij}^{\mathrm{S}}x_{j}=\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}
; for a single repressor with
w^{\mathrm{S}}<0
,

\theta^{\mathrm{S}}>0
, this gives a midpoint
x_{c}=\theta^{\mathrm{S}}/w^{\mathrm{S}}<0
outside the biologically
admissible domain
x\geq 0
, so the sigmoid never undergoes its
sigmoidal transition over physical concentrations
(
Section

3.2
). The shared threshold prescription

\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j}w_{ij}^{\mathrm{S}}

commonly adopted
in
[
31
,
27
]
produces

\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}=0
for any AND-gate combining one activator
and one repressor, leaving no characteristic biological scale to
identify parameters against.

1.2
The bridge: a bias-corrected weighted formulation

This paper develops a third logistic-based formulation that occupies
the middle ground between (
8
) and
(
1
): a single sigmoid per gene with a

regulator-specific
normalisation and a
combinatorial
bias
term. It directly follows and complements two recent preprints by
Belgacem
[
7
,
8
]
, which form
a two-stage program of work. The first
preprint
[
7
]
introduced and rigorously
catalogued the analytical advantages of replacing Hill functions

h^{\pm}(x,\theta,n)
by logistic counterparts

f^{\pm}(x,\theta,\lambda)
under the matching
\lambda=n/\theta
:
infinite differentiability, uniform Lipschitz bound
|\partial f^{\pm}/\partial x|\leq\lambda/4
, elementary inverses through the logit
transformation, and a non-zero basal output
f^{+}(0,\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{\lambda\theta})>0
. It also established global existence,
smoothness, and uniform boundedness for the product-of-logistics GRN
model with the explicit Lipschitz constant

L_{F}\leq M=\max_{i}(\kappa_{i}\sum_{j}L_{i}^{j}+\gamma_{i})

[
7
, Thm. 1]
and discussed the structural
shortcomings of weighted-sum formulations relative to the product

[
7
, §8.3]
. The second
preprint
[
8
]
deepened the case for the
logistic framework by demonstrating that, for canonical
two-gene oscillators and bistable positive-autoregulation motifs,
non-zero basal output prevents the Hill-induced off-state trap: in a
biophysically grounded
E. coli
parameter regime the logistic
model escapes the basal state in
\sim\!44
min while the Hill model
remains permanently trapped
[
8
, §5]
.

The present paper takes the product-of-logistics framework developed
in
[
7
,
8
]
as its analytic
baseline and asks:
is there a single-sigmoid formulation that
retains the product’s biological interpretability at the critical
point while collapsing the analytical complexity to one sigmoid per
gene
? The answer is the bias-corrected weighted-logistic formulation
below, which inherits the global mathematical guarantees of the
product framework (
Theorem

1
, in the form of

[
7
, Thm. 1]
) and the strictly-positive basal
output that prevents expression shutdown (
Corollary

1
,
echoing the basal-escape mechanism of
[
8
]
)
while collapsing the per-gene structure to one sigmoid.

We start from the unified weighted form

\dot{x}_{i}=\kappa_{i}\,\frac{1}{1+\exp\!\bigl(-\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}(x_{j}-\theta_{ij})\bigr)}-\gamma_{i}x_{i},\qquad w_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}\;\;\text{(sign-encoded direction)},\;\theta_{ij}>0,

(2)

which preserves regulator-specific thresholds
\theta_{ij}
(in
contrast to (
1
), where the threshold
\theta_{i}

is shared across regulators). The price for retaining a single sigmoid
per gene is a loss of agreement with the product-of-logistics at the
critical point: (
2
) attains the value
1/2

when all regulators sit at their thresholds, while
(
8
) attains
(1/2)^{m_{i}}
, where

m_{i}=|\{j:w_{ij}\neq 0\}|
counts the regulators of gene
i
.

We restore agreement at this critical point—and at two other
biologically meaningful reference configurations—by introducing the
combinatorial bias

b_{i}=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\log(2^{m_{i}}-1),

yielding the bias-corrected weighted-logistic regulatory function

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})=1/(1+(2^{m_{i}}-1)e^{-\lambda S_{i}})
with

S_{i}=\sum_{j}w_{ij}(x_{j}-\theta_{ij})
.
The bias depends only on the regulator count
m_{i}
and the steepness

\lambda
, not on the biological parameters
(\kappa_{i},\gamma_{i},\theta_{ij},w_{ij})
of the network; the dimensionless logit shift

\lambda b_{i}=-\log(2^{m_{i}}-1)
depends on
m_{i}
alone. The bias
forces
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}=f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}
at three distinguished configurations.

2
Model Formulation

We formalise four sigmoidal regulatory models for gene networks. After
introducing the elementary logistic building blocks
(
Section

2.1
), we recall the product-of-logistics
framework obtained by mapping Boolean regulatory rules through a
recursive De Morgan map (
Section

2.2
), then
introduce the Samuilik weighted-sum class
(
Section

2.3
), the unified weighted-logistic
formulation without bias (
Section

2.4
), and finally the
bias-corrected weighted-logistic model that is the subject of the
analysis (
Section

2.5
).
Section

2.6

establishes global well-posedness of the bias-corrected weighted ODE.

Throughout,
\mathbf{x}(t)=(x_{1}(t),\ldots,x_{n}(t))^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the
vector of gene-expression levels in a network of
n
genes. Each
gene’s dynamics obey a balance between sigmoidally regulated synthesis
and linear degradation:

\dot{x}_{i}\;=\;\kappa_{i}\,f_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\gamma_{i}\,x_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

(3)

where
\kappa_{i}>0
is the maximal synthesis rate,
\gamma_{i}>0
the
degradation rate, and
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,1)
the regulatory function. We
write
\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{R}_{i}
for the index sets of activators and
repressors of gene
i
, and
m_{i}=|\mathcal{A}_{i}|+|\mathcal{R}_{i}|
for the total
regulator count.

2.1
Logistic activation and repression functions

The elementary building blocks of every sigmoidal GRN formulation
considered here are the
increasing
logistic function modelling
gene activation,

f^{+}(x,\theta,\lambda)\;=\;\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda(x-\theta)}},

(4)

and the
decreasing
logistic function modelling gene repression,

f^{-}(x,\theta,\lambda)\;=\;\frac{1}{1+e^{\lambda(x-\theta)}}\;=\;\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda(\theta-x)}}\;=\;1-f^{+}(x,\theta,\lambda),

(5)

both with steepness
\lambda>0
and regulator-specific threshold

\theta>0
. The analytical advantages of
f^{\pm}
over Hill functions

h^{\pm}(x,\theta,n)=x^{n}/(\theta^{n}+x^{n})
(and the parameter matching

\lambda=n/\theta
that preserves slope at the half-maximum), namely
global
C^{\infty}
-smoothness, uniform Lipschitz bound

|\partial f^{\pm}/\partial x|\leq\lambda/4
, closed-form derivatives

\partial f^{\pm}/\partial x=\pm\lambda f^{\pm}(1-f^{\pm})
, elementary
antiderivatives, logit inversions, and strictly positive basal output

f^{+}(0,\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{\lambda\theta})>0
, are catalogued in
detail in
[
7
,
8
]
and
underlie everything that follows.

2.2
General multi-gene regulatory networks via Boolean-to-logistic recursion

Each gene’s regulatory logic can be encoded as a Boolean function of
its activators and repressors. The continuous-state
product-of-logistics

counterpart of such a rule is obtained by a recursive map
\Phi

from Boolean expressions to functions
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,1)
, defined on the
following primitive constructions
(see
[
7
,
8
]
for the
underlying derivation and a complete catalogue of structural
properties):

•

Positive literal (activation):

\Phi(x_{j})=f^{+}(x_{j},\theta_{ij},\lambda)
.

•

Negation (repression):

\Phi(\lnot x_{k})=f^{-}(x_{k},\theta_{ik},\lambda)=1-f^{+}(x_{k},\theta_{ik},\lambda)
.

•

Conjunction (AND, cooperative co-regulation):

\Phi\bigl(\bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}L_{l}\bigr)=\prod_{l=1}^{k}\Phi(L_{l})
.

•

Disjunction (OR, independent activation), via De Morgan:

\Phi\!\Bigl(\bigvee_{l=1}^{m}C_{l}(\mathbf{x})\Bigr)\;=\;1-\prod_{l=1}^{m}\bigl(1-\Phi(C_{l}(\mathbf{x}))\bigr),

(6)

which follows from
\lnot(\lnot C_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\lnot C_{m})

applied recursively and coincides with the probability that at
least one of
m
independent events occurs.

The recursion is consistent (Boolean values map to the interval
endpoints, AND/NOT/OR satisfy their classical truth tables when
inputs saturate, and
\Phi\in(0,1)
on every state) and produces a
smooth surrogate of the underlying Boolean rule. The De Morgan
product (
6
) guarantees
\Phi\in[0,1]
for any number
of independent regulatory pathways, in contrast to additive
translations of Boolean OR which can reach
m\gg 1
when several
clauses fire simultaneously, inflating the production rate and
breaking the biological bound
x_{i}\leq\kappa_{i}/\gamma_{i}
.

For the canonical case of
m_{i}
regulators in a single AND clause
(
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|
activators and
|\mathcal{R}_{i}|
repressors, all required
simultaneously), the recursion specialises to the

product-of-logistics regulatory function

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x})\;=\;\prod_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}\!\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda(x_{j}-\theta_{ij})}}\;\cdot\;\prod_{k\in\mathcal{R}_{i}}\!\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda(\theta_{ik}-x_{k})}},

(7)

which serves as the analytical baseline of this paper. The
mathematical foundations of (
7
)—global existence,

C^{\infty}
-smoothness, uniform boundedness, an explicit Lipschitz
constant, monotonicity, diagonal-dominance criteria for global
stability, and closed-form bifurcation theorems on canonical
motifs—are established in detail
in
[
7
,
8
]
and we treat them
as established background.

Remark 1

(Critical point of the product)
.

The
critical point
of gene

\mathbf{x}^{(\mathrm{cp})}_{i}

.

(Equivalent compact form with sign-encoded directions)
.

Enumerating the regulators of gene

(8)

Weighted-sum models

The product structure of (

2.4
The unified weighted-logistic formulation (without bias)

A natural single-sigmoid alternative to (

Definition 1

(Unified weighted-logistic GRN model)
.

The

\dot{x}_{i}\;=\;\kappa_{i}\,f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt}}(\mathbf{x})-\gamma_{i}x_{i},\qquad f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt}}(\mathbf{x})\;=\;\frac{1}{1+\exp\!\bigl(-\lambda S_{i}(\mathbf{x})\bigr)},\quad S_{i}(\mathbf{x})\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}(x_{j}-\theta_{ij}),

The total regulator count is

Proposition 1

(Embedding of activation and repression as signed weights)
.

The affine displacement appearing in

admits the split

so activation and repression are encoded by the sign of
w_{ij}

within a single increasing sigmoid in (
10
), with

|w_{ij}|
playing the role of a regulator-specific gain.

Proof.

Direct factoring of
w_{ij}(x_{j}-\theta_{ij})
. The interpretation in
terms of

is immediate from (
4
)–(
5
).
∎

Proposition

1
establishes that activation and repression are
encoded
within the exponential argument
of a single sigmoid in
the weighted formulation, in contrast with the product formulation
(
7

multiplicatively

through distinct functional forms. This unification is the source of
the weighted formulation’s analytical compactness but also the source
of the agreement failure that motivates the bias correction.

Remark 3

(Magnitude rescaling)
.

Under the embedding (

|w_{ij}|

multiplies
\lambda
and rescales each
\theta_{ij}

2.5
Critical-point mismatch and the bias correction

We now record the critical-point value mismatch between the weighted
and product formulations, and introduce the bias-corrected weighted

Lemma 1

(Critical-point values)
.

At the critical point

Proof.

The product value is computed in
Remark

1
. For the

Lemma 2

(Low-steepness and saturation values)
.

With

(a)

(

Proof.

(a)
For

(c)

(On the joint-saturation hypothesis)

Definition 2

(Bias-corrected weighted-logistic GRN model)
.

The

GRN model is

and

as in

. Equivalently,

The bias

and the

Proposition 2

(Canonicity of the bias correction)
.

Fix a network topology
(w_{ij},\theta_{ij})_{i,j}
with regulator

. Consider the
one-parameter family of single-sigmoid regulatory models

(15)

where
S_{i}
is the signed regulator sum of
Definition

1
.
Then:

(i)

(
Critical-point matching uniqueness.
) The constant

c_{i}
is unique with the property that
\tilde{f}_{i}
agrees with the

of gene

\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(\mathrm{cp})};\,c_{i})\;=\;f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(\mathrm{cp})})\;=\;\frac{1}{2^{m_{i}}}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad c_{i}\;=\;-\log(2^{m_{i}}-1).

(16)

(ii)

(
Equilibrium-sharing uniqueness.
) Suppose the network
parameters satisfy the self-consistency conditions
\theta_{ij}=x_{j}^{*}
of

4
. Then
c_{i}=-\log(2^{m_{i}}-1)
is the unique constant for which the ODE

\dot{x}_{i}=\kappa_{i}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{x};\,c_{i})-\gamma_{i}x_{i}
admits the
canonical critical-point equilibrium

shared with the product-of-logistics.

(iii)

(
Combinatorial dependence.
) The canonical bias

c_{i}=-\log(2^{m_{i}}-1)
depends only on the regulator count
m_{i}
;
it is independent of
\lambda
as a logit value and independent of

(\kappa_{i},\gamma_{i},\theta_{ij},w_{ij})

Proof.

(i)
At the critical point
S_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(\mathrm{cp})})=0

(
Remark

1
), so

\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(\mathrm{cp})};\,c_{i})=\sigma(c_{i})
. Equating this

makes the

(ii)
Under self-consistency every regulator of gene

sits
at its threshold at
\mathbf{x}^{*}
, so

. The equilibrium

\dot{x}_{i}=0

(iii)
Both formulas

alone among the network parameters; the

itself depends only on

3
Comparison with the Samuilik Weighted-Sum Formulation

The bias-corrected weighted-logistic formulation (
13
)
differs structurally from the Samuilik weighted-sum
model (
9
) of
Section

2.3
. The
structural shortcomings of (
9
) relative to
product-of-logistics models were previously catalogued
in
[
7
, §8.3]
along two complementary axes:
a critical-point pathology for repressors
(
[
7
, §8.3.1]
) and a threshold-scaling
pathology (
[
7
, §8.3.2]
). We extend that
analysis in two directions. First, we show that the bias-corrected
weighted-logistic formulation
retains
the
single-sigmoid-per-gene structure of (
9
) (the very
feature that motivates weighted-sum models in the first place) while

resolving
both pathologies through regulator-specific
thresholds and the combinatorial bias
b_{i}
. Second, we make the
threshold-aggregation argument quantitative, showing that the standard
prescription
\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j}w_{ij}^{\mathrm{S}}
collapses to a pure inflection condition with no
characteristic biological scale for canonical mixed regulation
(
Section

3.3
).

3.1
Threshold structure

The two formulations differ in the placement and interpretation of
thresholds:

•

In (
13
), each interaction
(i,j)
has its own
threshold
\theta_{ij}>0
, directly identifiable as the regulator
concentration at which interaction
(i,j)
exerts half-maximal effect
on gene
i
. This quantity is independently determined from
single-regulator dose-response measurements (binding affinity
K_{d}
,
half-maximal effective concentration
\mathrm{EC}_{50}
, half-maximal
inhibitory concentration
\mathrm{IC}_{50}
).

•

In (
9
), gene
i
has a single shared threshold

\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}
pooled across all regulators, computed as a
weighted aggregate. The biological meaning of this aggregate is opaque
when multiple regulators with disparate affinities are involved, and
the numerical value depends on the choice of weights, which are
themselves not directly measurable.

The structural distinction propagates to identifiability: thresholds

\theta_{ij}
in the bias-corrected weighted formulation can be
estimated from single-regulator experiments, while the Samuilik
threshold
\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}
requires simultaneous fitting of all
weights and the shared threshold from a single multi-regulator
dose-response surface, an inverse problem that is typically
ill-conditioned and admits weight-threshold tradeoffs that limit
parameter identifiability.

3.2
Repression pathology of the Samuilik model

In the Samuilik model (
9
), repression is encoded by a negative weight

w<0
inside an
increasing
sigmoid:

f^{\mathrm{S},-}(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{1+e^{-\mu(wx-\theta)}},\qquad w<0.

(21)

The midpoint of the sigmoid (where the output equals
1/2
and the
slope is steepest) satisfies
wx_{c}-\theta=0
, so

x_{c}\;=\;\frac{\theta}{w}\;<\;0\qquad\text{when }\theta>0,\,w<0.

The transition therefore occurs at a
negative
concentration,
outside the biologically admissible domain
x\geq 0

the canonical choice

By contrast, the decreasing logistic

employed by the product
(

(

) has its midpoint at

, the
biologically interpretable inhibition midpoint
\mathrm{IC}_{50}
,
and closely approximates the decreasing Hill function

for matched parameters

\lambda=n/\theta
(see
[
7
]
for the
parameter-matching analysis).

3.3
Mixed activation and repression

Consider gene
i
regulated by one activator
x_{1}
(
w_{i1}=+1
) and
one repressor
x_{2}
(
w_{i2}=-1
). In the bias-corrected weighted
framework (
13
), the regulatory function is

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{1}{1+\exp\!\bigl(-\lambda\bigl[(x_{1}-\theta_{i1})-(x_{2}-\theta_{i2})-\lambda^{-1}\log 3\bigr]\bigr)}

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{1}{1+3\,\exp\!\bigl(-\lambda(x_{1}-x_{2}+\theta_{i2}-\theta_{i1})\bigr)}.

(22)

The thresholds
\theta_{i1},\theta_{i2}
enter independently and are
determined from single-regulator dose-response curves for the
activator and the repressor.

In the Samuilik formulation, the same circuit reads

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{S}}(x_{1},x_{2})\;=\;\frac{1}{1+\exp\!\bigl(-\mu_{i}(w_{i1}^{\mathrm{S}}x_{1}+w_{i2}^{\mathrm{S}}x_{2}-\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}})\bigr)},\qquad w_{i1}^{\mathrm{S}}>0,\;w_{i2}^{\mathrm{S}}<0.

(23)

With the standard choice
w_{i1}^{\mathrm{S}}=+1
,

w_{i2}^{\mathrm{S}}=-1
and the threshold prescription

\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j}w_{ij}^{\mathrm{S}}=0
,
the function reduces to

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{S}}(x_{1},x_{2})=1/(1+e^{-\mu_{i}(x_{1}-x_{2})})
. The half-maximal output

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{S}}=1/2
is attained on the line
x_{1}=x_{2}
regardless of any
characteristic concentration; biologically, this asserts that the
regulatory midpoint occurs at
any
concentration at which the
activator and repressor are equal, in contradiction with the standard
biophysical picture in which the midpoint is anchored to specific
binding affinities
K_{d}^{\mathrm{act}}
and
K_{d}^{\mathrm{rep}}
.

By contrast, (
22
) attains
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}=1/4
at the
critical point
x_{1}=\theta_{i1},x_{2}=\theta_{i2}
, a configuration
anchored to two measurable biological scales.

4
Functional Equivalence Analysis

This section contains the principal mathematical results of the paper.
We show that the bias-corrected weighted formulation (
13
)
matches the product-of-logistics (
7
) at three
biologically meaningful reference configurations
(
Theorem

2
); we derive an exact algebraic identity for
the discrepancy between the two formulations
(
Theorem

3
); we compute the closed-form ratio of
local sensitivities at the critical point
(
Proposition

4
); we prove that the structural symmetry

\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=0
enjoyed by the product formulation is broken by
the bias correction for
m_{i}\geq 2

(
Proposition

5
); and we prove that no
state-independent constant shift can yield global equivalence
(
Proposition

6
).

4.1
Three-point matching

Theorem 2

(Three-point matching)
.

The bias-corrected weighted formulation
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}
in
(
13
) matches the product-of-logistics
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}
in
(
7
) at the following three configurations:

(a) Critical point.

At
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(\mathrm{cp})}
, i.e.
x_{j}=\theta_{ij}
for every

j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}\cup\mathcal{R}_{i}
, both functions equal
(1/2)^{m_{i}}
.

(b) Low-steepness limit.

As
\lambda\to 0^{+}
, both functions tend uniformly on compact subsets
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
(1/2)^{m_{i}}
.

(c) Saturation limits.

As every individual sigmoid factor in (
7
) approaches

1
(full activation, no repression),
both
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}\to 1
and
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}\to 1
. Symmetrically, as every factor
approaches
0
, both functions tend to
0
.

Proof.

(a)
At the critical point
S_{i}=0
, so by
(
14
),

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}=1/(1+(2^{m_{i}}-1))=1/2^{m_{i}}
, which matches
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}
by

Remark

1
.

(b)
As
\lambda\to 0^{+}
at fixed
\mathbf{x}
, the exponent in
(
13
) satisfies
-\lambda(S_{i}+b_{i})=-\lambda S_{i}+\log(2^{m_{i}}-1)\to\log(2^{m_{i}}-1)
. Therefore

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})\;\to\;\frac{1}{1+e^{\log(2^{m_{i}}-1)}}\;=\;\frac{1}{2^{m_{i}}}\;=\;\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x}),

where the second equality uses
Lemma

2
(a). Convergence is
uniform on compacts: fix a compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and set

M_{K}:=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in K}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|w_{ij}|\,|x_{j}-\theta_{ij}|<\infty
.
Then
|\lambda S_{i}(\mathbf{x})|\leq\lambda M_{K}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in K
. Both

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}
and
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}
depend on
\mathbf{x}
only through
\lambda S_{i}
(and
the individual
\lambda(x_{j}-\theta_{ij})
for the product), and the
sigmoidal Lipschitz bound
|\sigma^{\prime}|\leq 1/4
gives

|f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})-2^{-m_{i}}|\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda M_{K}
and an analogous
bound for
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}
, both tending to zero uniformly on
K
as

\lambda\to 0^{+}
.

(c)
Under the joint positive saturation condition
u_{j}\to+\infty
for every
j\in[m_{i}]
,
\lambda S_{i}=\sum_{j}u_{j}\to+\infty
,
hence
(2^{m_{i}}-1)e^{-\lambda S_{i}}\to 0
and
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}\to 1
. The
limit
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}\to 1
follows from
Lemma

2
(b). The case of
joint negative saturation
u_{j}\to-\infty
is symmetric and gives

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}},f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}\to 0
by
Lemma

2
(c).
∎

Theorem

2
establishes that the bias-corrected weighted
formulation
registers
with the product-of-logistics at the
three reference configurations: the half-input critical point that
characterises the regulatory threshold, the low-steepness limit that
characterises non-cooperative response, and the saturation limits
that characterise full activation/repression. The bias term
(
13
) is the unique state-independent constant that
achieves the critical-point match (
Proposition

2
);
since the low-steepness limit reduces algebraically to the same
condition
\sigma(\lambda b_{i})=1/2^{m_{i}}
and the saturation
limits hold automatically for any constant bias, the critical-point
condition therefore implies the full three-point match.

4.2
Exact algebraic discrepancy formula

We now express the discrepancy
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}-f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}
in closed form. The
key technical device is a change of variables that absorbs the weight
sign:

u_{j}\;:=\;w_{ij}\,\lambda(x_{j}-\theta_{ij})\;=\;\begin{cases}\;\;\lambda(x_{j}-\theta_{ij}),&j\in\mathcal{A}_{i},\\
-\lambda(x_{j}-\theta_{ij})=\lambda(\theta_{ij}-x_{j}),&j\in\mathcal{R}_{i},\\
\;\;0,&w_{ij}=0.\end{cases}

(24)

Under this convention, both activators and repressors become
sigmoid factors of the form
\sigma(u_{j})=1/(1+e^{-u_{j}})
in the
product formulation:

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}\;=\;\prod_{j:\,w_{ij}\neq 0}\sigma(u_{j})\;=\;\prod_{j:\,w_{ij}\neq 0}\frac{e^{u_{j}}}{1+e^{u_{j}}}\;=\;\frac{e^{U_{i}}}{\prod_{j:\,w_{ij}\neq 0}(1+e^{u_{j}})},

(25)

where
U_{i}:=\sum_{j:\,w_{ij}\neq 0}u_{j}=\lambda S_{i}
. Similarly,
the bias-corrected weighted form rewrites as

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}\;=\;\frac{e^{U_{i}}}{(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{U_{i}}}.

(26)

Let
[m_{i}]=\{1,\ldots,m_{i}\}
denote the index set of the regulators
of gene
i
(re-indexed for notational simplicity). For a subset

T\subseteq[m_{i}]
write
u_{T}:=\sum_{j\in T}u_{j}
and let

\mathcal{T}_{i}:=\{T\subseteq[m_{i}]:T\neq\emptyset,\,T\neq[m_{i}]\}

denote the collection of proper non-empty subsets of
[m_{i}]
. The
cardinality of
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is
2^{m_{i}}-2
.

Theorem 3

(Algebraic identity for the discrepancy)
.

With
u_{j}
as in (
24
) and
U_{i}=\sum_{j}u_{j}=\lambda S_{i}
,

\boxed{\;\;f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x})-f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})\;=\;-\;\frac{e^{U_{i}}\;\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}\bigl(e^{u_{T}}-1\bigr)}{\displaystyle\Bigl[\prod_{j\in[m_{i}]}(1+e^{u_{j}})\Bigr]\,\bigl[(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{U_{i}}\bigr]}.\;\;}

(27)

Proof.

By (
25
)–(
26
),

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}-f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}\;=\;e^{U_{i}}\!\left[\frac{1}{\prod_{j}(1+e^{u_{j}})}\;-\;\frac{1}{(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{U_{i}}}\right]\;=\;\frac{e^{U_{i}}\,\bigl[(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{U_{i}}-\prod_{j}(1+e^{u_{j}})\bigr]}{\prod_{j}(1+e^{u_{j}})\;\bigl[(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{U_{i}}\bigr]}.

Expand the product using the elementary identity

\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}}(1+e^{u_{j}})\;=\;\sum_{T\subseteq[m_{i}]}e^{u_{T}}\;=\;1\;+\;\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}e^{u_{T}}\;+\;e^{U_{i}},

where the last equality separates the empty subset
T=\emptyset

(which contributes
e^{0}=1
) and the full subset
T=[m_{i}]
(which
contributes
e^{u_{[m_{i}]}}=e^{U_{i}}
) from the
2^{m_{i}}-2
proper
non-empty subsets. Substituting,

(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{U_{i}}-\prod_{j}(1+e^{u_{j}})\;=\;(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{U_{i}}-1-\!\!\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}e^{u_{T}}-e^{U_{i}}\;=\;(2^{m_{i}}-2)-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}e^{u_{T}}.

Since
|\mathcal{T}_{i}|=2^{m_{i}}-2
, this rewrites as

-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}\bigl(e^{u_{T}}-1\bigr)
, completing the
proof.
∎

The identity (
27
) provides the

additive
form of the discrepancy. A multiplicative
counterpart is obtained directly from (
25
) and
(
26
):

Corollary 2

(Ratio form of the discrepancy)
.

With
u_{j}
,
U_{i}=\lambda S_{i}
as in
Theorem

3
,

\frac{f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x})}{f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})}\;=\;\frac{(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{\lambda S_{i}}}{\displaystyle\prod_{j\in[m_{i}]}\bigl(1+e^{u_{j}}\bigr)}.

(28)

This ratio equals
1
at the critical point (
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}
) and as

u_{j}\to+\infty
for all
j
(joint positive saturation), and tends to

2^{m_{i}}-1
as
u_{j}\to-\infty
for all
j
(joint negative saturation).

Proof.

Dividing (
25
) by (
26
) gives
(
28
). At
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}
: numerator

(2^{m_{i}}-1)+1=2^{m_{i}}
, denominator
2^{m_{i}}
, ratio
1
. As

u_{j}\to+\infty
all, both numerator and denominator are
\sim e^{U_{i}}
,
ratio
\to 1
. As
u_{j}\to-\infty
all,
e^{U_{i}}\to 0
and

\prod(1+e^{u_{j}})\to 1
, giving ratio
\to(2^{m_{i}}-1)/1=2^{m_{i}}-1
.
∎

Corollary

2
is the natural form for
relative-error
bounds:
the ratio characterises by what factor the product overshoots or
undershoots the bias-corrected weighted formulation, complementing
(
27
), which quantifies absolute pointwise
deviation. In the negative-saturation regime both functions decay to
zero exponentially at the same rate
\sim e^{m_{i}u}
, but the
product has a leading-order coefficient
(2^{m_{i}}-1)
times larger
than the bias-corrected weighted form:
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}/f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}\to(2^{m_{i}}-1)

captures the multiplicative gap between the two formulations’
off-state output. For
m_{i}=2,3,4
the asymptotic ratios are

3,7,15
respectively, a substantial relative discrepancy that should
be accounted for in controller designs that operate at low
expression levels.

The identity (
27
) provides a complete
characterisation of the discrepancy. Three immediate consequences are
worth recording.

Corollary 3

(Vanishing set)
.

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x})=f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})
if and only if

\Phi_{i}(\mathbf{u})\;:=\;\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}\bigl(e^{u_{T}}-1\bigr)\;=\;\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}e^{u_{T}}-(2^{m_{i}}-2)\;=\;0.

(29)

Three natural classes of configurations satisfy this:

(i)

Critical point:
at
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}
, every individual
term
e^{u_{T}}-1
vanishes, so
\Phi_{i}=0

exactly
;
this is the unique point in
\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
at which all

2^{m_{i}}-2
summands vanish simultaneously.

(ii)

Joint saturation,
u_{j}\to+\infty
all:

\Phi_{i}\to 0

asymptotically
(precise rates in

Proposition

3
below).

(iii)

Joint saturation,
u_{j}\to-\infty
all:
likewise

\Phi_{i}\to 0
asymptotically.

For
m_{i}\geq 2
, the equation
\Phi_{i}(\mathbf{u})=0
defines an

(m_{i}-1)
-dimensional real-analytic submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
on which
the discrepancy vanishes exactly through cancellation between subsets

T
with
u_{T}>0
(positive contribution
e^{u_{T}}-1>0
) and subsets
with
u_{T}<0
(negative contribution). The critical point is the
unique point of this submanifold at which each summand individually
vanishes; elsewhere, the manifold avoids the orthants

\{u_{j}\geq 0\;\forall j\}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
and

\{u_{j}\leq 0\;\forall j\}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
entirely, since on
those sets every summand is non-negative (resp. non-positive) and at
least one is strictly so.

Proof.

The first claim follows from (
27
): the
denominator and the prefactor
e^{U_{i}}
are strictly positive, so the
discrepancy vanishes iff the inner sum vanishes. Points (i)–(iii) are
immediate. For the manifold claim,
\Phi_{i}
is real-analytic on

\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
, and a direct computation gives, at
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}
,

\partial_{u_{j}}\Phi_{i}\bigl|_{\mathbf{0}}\;=\;\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{i}:\,j\in T}e^{u_{T}}\bigl|_{\mathbf{0}}\;=\;\bigl|\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{i}:j\in T\}\bigr|\;=\;2^{m_{i}-1}-1\;>\;0,

since the proper non-empty subsets of
[m_{i}]
containing
j
are in
bijection with subsets of
[m_{i}]\setminus\{j\}
other than the full
set, of which there are
2^{m_{i}-1}-1
. The gradient
\nabla\Phi_{i}
is
therefore nonzero at
\mathbf{0}
, and by the implicit function theorem

\{\Phi_{i}=0\}
is an embedded
(m_{i}-1)
-dimensional analytic
submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
in a neighbourhood of
\mathbf{0}
, extending
globally on the full level set. The exclusion of the strictly-positive
and strictly-negative orthants is immediate from
e^{u_{T}}-1
having
the sign of
u_{T}
.
∎

If all

Corollary 5

(Pointwise sign of the discrepancy in the symmetric case)
.

In the symmetric configuration,
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x})-f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})
has the

: the product overshoots the bias-corrected weighted

Proposition 3

(Sharp asymptotic decay rates of the discrepancy)
.

In the symmetric configuration (
u_{j}=u
for all
j
), the
discrepancy admits the leading-order asymptotic estimates

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x})-f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})\;\sim\;\begin{cases}\,-\,m_{i}\,e^{-u},&u\to+\infty,\\[3.0pt]
\,\dfrac{2^{m_{i}}-2}{2^{m_{i}}-1}\,e^{m_{i}u},&u\to-\infty.\end{cases}

(31)

In the general (non-symmetric) configuration with
u_{j}\to+\infty
for
all
j
, the discrepancy is dominated by the contribution of the

(m_{i}-1)
-element subsets:

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x})-f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})\;=\;-\,\Bigl(\sum_{l\in[m_{i}]}e^{-u_{l}}\Bigr)\;+\;O\!\Bigl(e^{-\min_{l\neq l^{\prime}}(u_{l}+u_{l^{\prime}})}\Bigr).

(32)

Proof.

Symmetric case,
u\to+\infty
.
Substitute
u_{j}=u
into
(
30
). The numerator’s inner sum is dominated
by the largest exponent,
k=m_{i}-1
:
\binom{m_{i}}{m_{i}-1}(e^{(m_{i}-1)u}-1)\sim m_{i}\,e^{(m_{i}-1)u}
. The full numerator is therefore

\sim m_{i}e^{m_{i}u}\cdot e^{(m_{i}-1)u}=m_{i}e^{(2m_{i}-1)u}
. The
denominator:
(1+e^{u})^{m_{i}}\sim e^{m_{i}u}
and

(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{m_{i}u}\sim e^{m_{i}u}
, so the denominator is

\sim e^{2m_{i}u}
. The ratio gives
-m_{i}e^{-u}
, as claimed.

Symmetric case,
u\to-\infty
.
The numerator’s inner sum is

\sum_{k=1}^{m_{i}-1}\binom{m_{i}}{k}(e^{ku}-1)\to-(2^{m_{i}}-2)
since

e^{ku}\to 0
for
k\geq 1
and
\sum_{k}\binom{m_{i}}{k}=2^{m_{i}}-2
for

1\leq k\leq m_{i}-1
. The numerator is therefore

\sim e^{m_{i}u}\cdot(-(2^{m_{i}}-2))
, and the denominator

(1+e^{u})^{m_{i}}\to 1
while
(2^{m_{i}}-1)+e^{m_{i}u}\to 2^{m_{i}}-1
. The
ratio gives
\frac{2^{m_{i}}-2}{2^{m_{i}}-1}e^{m_{i}u}
(the minus sign in

Local sensitivity at the critical point

(Slope-ratio formula)

4.4
Curvature mismatch and broken structural symmetry

A more delicate structural difference between the two formulations

Proposition 5

(Curvature mismatch at the critical point)

At the critical point of gene

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{l}}^{2}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}\big|_{\mathrm{cp}}\;

\displaystyle=\;0\qquad\text{for all }m_{i}\geq 1,

Proof.

For

4.5
No state-independent global equivalence

A natural question is whether a more elaborate state-independent shift
could eliminate the discrepancy of
Theorem

3

globally. The answer is negative.

Proposition 6

(Non-existence of state-independent global equivalence)
.

Under the unit-magnitude convention of
Remark

3
,
fix
m_{i}\geq 2

\lambda>0

(40)

In particular, the bias (
13

realising the critical-point match

no other constant achieves global equivalence either.

Proof.

Suppose, for contradiction, that (
40

\sigma^{-1}(y)=\log(y/(1-y))

The LHS is affine in
\mathbf{x}

For

For uniqueness of

The proof reveals a structural fact: the bias (

(Bias correction as zero-order mean-field approximation)

The bias-corrected weighted-logistic regulatory function

By the same derivation as (

(Structural limit of affine single-sigmoid approximations)

Fix

5
Equivalence of Fixed-Weight and Real-Weight
Product-of-Logistics

We now establish a complementary structural fact: within the
product-of-logistics framework, real-valued positive weights are
parameter-equivalent to fixed unit weights. This justifies the
unit-magnitude convention
|w_{ij}|=1
adopted in

Remark

3
for the analysis of

Definition

1
and

the parallel observation in
[

that fixed-weight product-of-logistics enjoys an identifiability
advantage absent from weighted-sum formulations: the rescaling
(

real weights
w_{ij}>0
in the activator and repressor factors does
not enlarge the dynamics-distinguishable parameter space, so weight
freedom and threshold/steepness freedom collapse into a single
identifiable triple per regulator.

Proposition 9

(Equivalence under positive parameter rescaling)
.

Let gene
i
be regulated by one activator
x_{j}
(
w_{ij}>0
) and
one repressor
x_{k}
(
w_{ik}>0
). The weighted product-of-logistics
dynamics

\dot{x}_{i}\;=\;\kappa_{i}\!\left[\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda_{ij}(w_{ij}x_{j}-\theta_{ij})}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda_{ik}(\theta_{ik}-w_{ik}x_{k})}}\right]-\gamma_{i}x_{i}

(44)

admit the parameter-rescaling identity

\dot{x}_{i}\;=\;\kappa_{i}\!\left[\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda_{ij}^{\prime}(x_{j}-\theta_{ij}^{\prime})}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda_{ik}^{\prime}(\theta_{ik}^{\prime}-x_{k})}}\right]-\gamma_{i}x_{i},

(45)

under

\lambda_{ij}^{\prime}=\lambda_{ij}\,w_{ij}>0,\quad\theta_{ij}^{\prime}=\theta_{ij}/w_{ij}>0,\quad\lambda_{ik}^{\prime}=\lambda_{ik}\,w_{ik}>0,\quad\theta_{ik}^{\prime}=\theta_{ik}/w_{ik}>0.

(46)

The two parameterisations produce identical trajectories.

Proof.

For the activator factor, factor out
w_{ij}
:

-\lambda_{ij}(w_{ij}x_{j}-\theta_{ij})=-\lambda_{ij}w_{ij}(x_{j}-\theta_{ij}/w_{ij})=-\lambda_{ij}^{\prime}(x_{j}-\theta_{ij}^{\prime})
. The repressor
factor is symmetric. Strict positivity of all rescaled parameters is
immediate from
w_{ij},w_{ik}>0
.
∎

Remark 7

(Identifiability consequences)
.

Proposition

9
reveals an intrinsic identifiability
limit of the weighted product-of-logistics: the products

\lambda_{ij}\cdot w_{ij}
and ratios
\theta_{ij}/w_{ij}
are
recoverable from input-output data, but the individual triples

(\lambda_{ij},\theta_{ij},w_{ij})
are not. This is independent of
the data quality; it is a property of the model class. Modelling can
therefore be carried out unambiguously with unit weights and
gene/regulator-specific
(\lambda_{ij},\theta_{ij})
, or with
real positive weights and rescaled parameters, depending on convenience.
The Samuilik formulation (
9
) does not admit any
such rescaling: the signed weight
w_{ij}^{\mathrm{S}}
must remain
inside the increasing sigmoid for direction encoding, and any change
of variable that simplifies the activator part propagates to the
repressor part with the wrong sign.

Corollary 6

(Globally unique shared equilibrium in the contractive regime)
.

Assume that, in addition to the self-consistency condition
(

Each formulation admits the configuration

For either formulation, the regulatory function

Proposition 10

(Robustness of the equilibrium correspondence)
.

Let
\bm{\theta}^{0}=(\theta_{ij}^{0})
with
\theta_{ij}^{0}:=\kappa_{j}/(\gamma_{j}2^{m_{j}})

Suppose the actual thresholds satisfy

\bm{\eta}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\rm prod}(\bm{\eta})-\mathbf{x}^{*}

\Psi

recovers (

(
56
). For the component formulas, observe that

and absorbing the overall sign into

6.2
Linearised dynamics at a shared equilibrium

The shared equilibrium of
Theorem

4
is a point at
which the regulatory functions agree, but the local dynamics depend
on the
slopes
of the regulatory functions, which differ by the
factor
2-2^{1-m_{i}}
established in
Proposition

4
. The
resulting Jacobians are related by an explicit transformation.

Proposition 11

(Linearisation comparison at a shared equilibrium)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}^{*}
be a shared critical-point equilibrium as in

Theorem

4
. Define the diagonal matrix

D\;:=\;\operatorname{diag}\bigl(2-2^{1-m_{1}},\ldots,2-2^{1-m_{n}}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},

(60)

and the degradation matrix
\Gamma:=\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n})
. Let
J^{\mathrm{prod}},J^{\mathrm{bcw}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denote the Jacobians of the right-hand sides of
(
47
) and (
48
) at
\mathbf{x}^{*}
. Then

\boxed{\;\;J^{\mathrm{bcw}}\;=\;D\bigl(J^{\mathrm{prod}}+\Gamma\bigr)-\Gamma.\;\;}

(61)

In particular,

J^{\mathrm{bcw}}_{il}\;=\;(2-2^{1-m_{i}})\,J^{\mathrm{prod}}_{il}\qquad\text{for }i\neq l,

(62)

and the off-diagonal entries are uniformly amplified by the row-wise
factor
2-2^{1-m_{i}}\in[1,2)
, while the diagonal entries are
transformed according to (
61
).

Proof.

Writing
\mathbf{F}^{\rm bcw}(\mathbf{x})=\bm{\kappa}\odot\mathbf{f}^{\rm wgt,bc}(\mathbf{x})-\Gamma\mathbf{x}
(with
\odot
denoting
component-wise product) and similarly for the product system, we have

J^{\rm bcw}_{il}=\kappa_{i}\partial_{l}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\gamma_{i}\delta_{il}
.
By
Proposition

4
, at the critical point

\partial_{l}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=(2-2^{1-m_{i}})\,\partial_{l}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})
,
so
\kappa_{i}\partial_{l}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}=(2-2^{1-m_{i}})\kappa_{i}\partial_{l}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}=(2-2^{1-m_{i}})(J^{\rm prod}_{il}+\gamma_{i}\delta_{il})
. Hence

J^{\rm bcw}_{il}\;=\;(2-2^{1-m_{i}})(J^{\rm prod}_{il}+\gamma_{i}\delta_{il})\;-\;\gamma_{i}\delta_{il},

which is the
(i,l)
-entry of
D(J^{\rm prod}+\Gamma)-\Gamma
. The
off-diagonal case
i\neq l
has
\delta_{il}=0
, recovering
(
62
).
∎

Proposition

11
has direct dynamical consequences. The map

M\mapsto D(M+\Gamma)-\Gamma
preserves the diagonal
-\Gamma

contribution of degradation but rescales the gain-of-regulation part
of the Jacobian by row-wise factors
2-2^{1-m_{i}}\in[1,2)
. In
particular:

1.

Trace
:

\operatorname{tr}(J^{\rm bcw})-\operatorname{tr}(J^{\rm prod})=\sum_{i}(2-2^{1-m_{i}}-1)\bigl(J^{\rm prod}_{ii}+\gamma_{i}\bigr)=\sum_{i}(1-2^{1-m_{i}})\,\kappa_{i}\partial_{i}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})
.
The trace changes only through self-regulation contributions
(
w_{ii}\neq 0
); for networks without self-regulation,

\operatorname{tr}(J^{\rm bcw})=\operatorname{tr}(J^{\rm prod})

and the spectral barycentre is preserved.

2.

Off-diagonal amplification
: every off-diagonal coupling
is amplified by the row-wise factor
2-2^{1-m_{i}}
. Bounded above by

2
, this amplification can shift eigenvalues either by enhancing
oscillation frequencies (cooperative crossings) or by destabilising
saddles (when off-diagonals control the sign of subprincipal minors).

3.

Symmetric homogeneous case
: if all genes have the same
regulator count
m_{i}=m
, then
D=(2-2^{1-m})I
and
(
61
) reduces to

J^{\rm bcw}=(2-2^{1-m})J^{\rm prod}+(1-2^{1-m})\Gamma
. For
the standard case
\gamma_{i}=\gamma
for all
i
, the eigenvalues of

J^{\rm bcw}
are an affine transform of those of
J^{\rm prod}
:

\mu^{\rm bcw}\;=\;(2-2^{1-m})\,\mu^{\rm prod}+(1-2^{1-m})\gamma.

(63)

Equivalently,
\mu^{\rm bcw}+\gamma=(2-2^{1-m})(\mu^{\rm prod}+\gamma)
.

The identity (
63
) has a striking consequence: the
affine map
\mu^{\rm prod}\mapsto\mu^{\rm bcw}
has the unique real
fixed point
\mu=-\gamma
. Modes of
J^{\rm prod}
with
\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}=-\gamma

are therefore mapped to modes of
J^{\rm bcw}
with
\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm bcw}=-\gamma
, irrespective of their imaginary parts.

Corollary 7

(Stability dichotomy at shared equilibria)
.

In the symmetric homogeneous case (
m_{i}=m
,
\gamma_{i}=\gamma
for
all
i
), the local stability margin at a shared equilibrium changes
under the bias correction according to the formula

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm bcw}-\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}\;=\;(1-2^{1-m})\,\bigl(\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}+\gamma\bigr).

(64)

Consequently, each eigenvalue
\mu^{\rm prod}
satisfies:

(a)

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}>-\gamma

\;\Longrightarrow\;

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm bcw}>\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}

(
the bias correction destabilises slowly-decaying
or unstable modes
);

(b)

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}<-\gamma

\;\Longrightarrow\;

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm bcw}<\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}

(
the bias correction further stabilises fast-decaying
modes
);

(c)

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}=-\gamma

\;\Longrightarrow\;

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm bcw}=-\gamma

(
the fixed-point real part is preserved exactly
).

The threshold
\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}=-\gamma
corresponds
to the case where the regulation part of the Jacobian contributes a
purely imaginary spectrum to
\mu^{\rm prod}
. For networks without
self-regulation,
J^{\rm prod}=-\gamma I+(\kappa\lambda/2^{m+1})\,A
with
A
the signed adjacency matrix, and
this case arises whenever
A
has purely imaginary eigenvalues—a
common situation on negative-feedback cyclic motifs.

Proof.

Take real parts on both sides of (
63
):

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm bcw}=(2-2^{1-m})\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}+(1-2^{1-m})\gamma
. Subtract
\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}
:

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm bcw}-\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}=(1-2^{1-m})\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}+(1-2^{1-m})\gamma=(1-2^{1-m})(\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}+\gamma)
. The factor

(1-2^{1-m})
is strictly positive for
m\geq 2
, so the sign of the
shift coincides with the sign of
\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}+\gamma
.
∎

Corollary

7
provides an actionable design
implication. In a network model where the product-of-logistics
Jacobian has eigenvalues clustered near
\operatorname{Re}\mu=-\gamma

(the natural decay rate of an isolated gene), the bias-corrected
weighted formulation preserves the stability margin. Modes that decay
slower than
-\gamma
(e.g. due to strong positive feedback) are

magnified
by the bias correction and may bifurcate to
instability that is not present in the product. Conversely, fast-decaying
modes are made even faster. This anisotropic
re-scaling of the spectrum is qualitatively different from a uniform
gain modification.

Example 1

(3-node repressilator-with-activator motif)
.

Consider three genes in a cycle, each with one cross-activator and one
cross-repressor (
m_{i}=2
for all

), and no self-regulation:
gene
1
is activated by gene

and repressed by gene

by gene

by gene

\kappa_{i}=\kappa
and
\gamma_{i}=\gamma

for all

(

\theta_{ij}=\kappa/(4\gamma)

for every interacting pair
(i,j)
. At the shared equilibrium

7
Application: A Two-Gene Negative-Feedback Oscillator with Self-Activation

We illustrate the equilibrium-correspondence and linearisation results
of
Section

6
on a canonical 2-gene regulatory motif: a
negative feedback loop with positive autoregulation, exhibiting
sustained oscillations via Hopf bifurcation. This motif is among the
simplest gene-circuit topologies known to produce limit-cycle
behaviour without delays, and its symmetric structure makes the
spectral predictions of
Propositions

11
and
7

fully explicit.

This setting complements three prior analyses on related two-gene
networks. The Vinoth
et al.

[
35
]
model is a
delay-coupled DDE in which the topology is
mutual cross-activation
plus cooperative self-repression with delays
: the genes are

A\!\to\!B
and
B\!\to\!A
at the activation level and
A\!\dashv\!A
,

B\!\dashv\!B
at the repression level, and oscillations arise via a
delay-induced Hopf bifurcation. Belgacem
[
9
]

analyses the same Vinoth network under two logistic reformulations and
derives the closed-form parameter expressions
\kappa_{1}=4g_{A}
,

\theta_{B}=g_{A}
,
\lambda_{1}=\ln 3/g_{A}
for the weighted-logistic
formulation, with critical self-repression delay

\tau_{c}\approx 1.64
min. The factors
4=2^{m_{i}}
and

\ln 3=\log(2^{m_{i}}-1)
in those parameters are

not coincidences
: they coincide with the saturation
exponent and the bias-correction term of the present framework
(
Definition

2
) at
m_{i}=2
, so Belgacem’s weighted-logistic
formulation is precisely the bias-corrected weighted formulation of
this paper applied to the Vinoth DDE topology, with parameters fixed
by basal-rate and slope-matching against the Hill-linear baseline.
Belgacem
[
10
]
further establishes sustained
limit cycles in the logistic two-gene oscillator through a
delay-driven Hopf-bifurcation analysis, with explicit critical-delay
characterisations and Lyapunov-coefficient computations confirming
the bifurcation criticality; that result, complementary to the
present delay-free analysis, illustrates how the bcw framework
extends naturally into the delay-coupled regime.

In contrast, the present
Section

7
uses a different
topology —
mutual self-activation plus asymmetric cross-regulation
without delays
(specified below) — chosen so that the Hopf
bifurcation is parameter-induced rather than delay-induced and the
characteristic polynomial reduces to a
2\times 2
algebraic problem
without transcendental delay terms. The three settings are
therefore complementary: Belgacem’s two prior analyses

[
9
,
10
]
specialise the bcw
framework to the Vinoth DDE topology and derive delay-dependent Hopf
curves and sustained limit-cycle existence; the analysis below
specialises it to a delay-free oscillator and derives a clean
algebraic eigenvalue map that makes the
D=3/2
frequency
amplification and the
2/3
Hopf-threshold ratio transparent.

7.1
Two-gene oscillator: topology and self-consistent thresholds

Consider two genes with the regulatory wiring

w_{AA}=+1,\quad w_{AB}=-1,\qquad w_{BB}=+1,\quad w_{BA}=+1,

so each gene self-activates (
w_{ii}>0
), gene
A
is repressed by
gene
B
(
w_{AB}<0
), and gene
B
is activated by gene
A

(
w_{BA}>0
). Each gene therefore has
m_{i}=2
regulators
(one self-regulator plus one cross-regulator). The closed feedback
loop
A\!\to\!B\!\dashv\!A
is negative; combined with the local
positive autoregulation, the architecture is a textbook
positive-plus-negative feedback motif of the kind underlying many
biological clocks
[
25
,
24
]
.

Threshold structure under self-consistency.

The motif involves four threshold parameters,
\theta_{AA},\theta_{AB},\theta_{BA},\theta_{BB}
, with
\theta_{ij}
interpreted biologically
as the concentration of regulator
j
at which it exerts half-maximal
effect on the regulation of gene
i
(an
\mathrm{EC}_{50}
for
activation or
\mathrm{IC}_{50}
for repression).
In principle
these four thresholds are independent intrinsic biological quantities

(binding affinities
K_{d}
, etc.) and need not coincide with any
equilibrium concentration. We
choose
them, however, to
satisfy the self-consistency condition of

Theorem

4
, which makes the product-of-logistics
and the bias-corrected weighted formulations share equilibria. The
self-consistency identity is derived as follows: at the
“critical point” where all regulators sit at their respective
thresholds, the product-of-logistics evaluates to
1/2^{m_{i}}=1/4
;
the equilibrium condition
\dot{x}_{i}=0
at this configuration yields

x_{i}=\kappa_{i}/(\gamma_{i}\cdot 2^{m_{i}})
. Imposing that this candidate
equilibrium coincides with the threshold seen by other genes (the
self-consistency identity
\theta_{ij}=\kappa_{j}/(\gamma_{j}2^{m_{j}})
)
determines a specific parameter choice expressed entirely in terms of

(\kappa,\gamma,m)
:

\theta_{A}\;:=\;\frac{\kappa_{A}}{4\gamma_{A}},\qquad\theta_{B}\;:=\;\frac{\kappa_{B}}{4\gamma_{B}},

(65)

with
\theta_{AA}=\theta_{BA}=\theta_{A}
(both involve regulator

A
) and
\theta_{AB}=\theta_{BB}=\theta_{B}
(both involve regulator

B
). These are
chosen parameter values
, deterministic
functions of
(\kappa,\gamma)
; they are not derived by solving the
dynamics. Genes
A
and
B
are distinct species, so
\theta_{A}
and

\theta_{B}
are in general distinct biological scales. We adopt
uniform degradation
\gamma_{A}=\gamma_{B}=\gamma
(necessary for the
clean eigenvalue map
\mu^{\rm bcw}=(3/2)\,\mu^{\rm prod}+(1/2)\gamma
of
Proposition

11
) but
distinct

synthesis rates
\kappa_{A}\neq\kappa_{B}
, so that the two thresholds

\theta_{A}=\kappa_{A}/(4\gamma)
and
\theta_{B}=\kappa_{B}/(4\gamma)

take different numerical values reflecting the gene-specific kinetics.
With this parameter choice, the dynamical system admits the
shared critical-point equilibrium

\mathbf{x}^{*}\;=\;(\theta_{A},\,\theta_{B})\;=\;\left(\frac{\kappa_{A}}{4\gamma},\,\frac{\kappa_{B}}{4\gamma}\right),

(66)

at which both
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}
and
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}
evaluate to
1/2^{m_{i}}=1/4

(
Theorem

4
). The identity
x_{j}^{*}=\theta_{j}

is then a
consequence
of the parameter choice, not its
definition.

7.2
The four formulations

The four sigmoidal formulations of
Section

2
produce the
following explicit dynamics on the motif:

Product-of-logistics.

\displaystyle\dot{A}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{A}\,\underbrace{\sigma\!\bigl(\lambda(A-\theta_{A})\bigr)}_{\text{self-activation}}\cdot\,\underbrace{\sigma\!\bigl(\lambda(\theta_{B}-B)\bigr)}_{\text{repression by }B}\;-\;\gamma\,A,

(67)

\displaystyle\dot{B}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{B}\,\underbrace{\sigma\!\bigl(\lambda(B-\theta_{B})\bigr)}_{\text{self-activation}}\cdot\,\underbrace{\sigma\!\bigl(\lambda(A-\theta_{A})\bigr)}_{\text{activation by }A}\;-\;\gamma\,B.

(68)

Bias-corrected weighted.

With
S_{A}=(A-\theta_{A})-(B-\theta_{B})
and

S_{B}=(B-\theta_{B})+(A-\theta_{A})
, and
b_{i}=-(\log 3)/\lambda
,

\displaystyle\dot{A}\;=\;\kappa_{A}\,\frac{1}{1+3\,e^{-\lambda S_{A}}}\;-\;\gamma\,A,\qquad\dot{B}\;=\;\kappa_{B}\,\frac{1}{1+3\,e^{-\lambda S_{B}}}\;-\;\gamma\,B.

(69)

Unified weighted-logistic (no bias).

\displaystyle\dot{A}\;=\;\kappa_{A}\,\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda S_{A}}}\;-\;\gamma\,A,\qquad\dot{B}\;=\;\kappa_{B}\,\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda S_{B}}}\;-\;\gamma\,B.

(70)

Samuilik weighted-sum.

The canonical Samuilik threshold prescription gives

\theta_{A}^{\mathrm{S}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(w_{AA}^{\mathrm{S}}+w_{AB}^{\mathrm{S}})=\tfrac{1}{2}(1-1)=0
and

\theta_{B}^{\mathrm{S}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(w_{BB}^{\mathrm{S}}+w_{BA}^{\mathrm{S}})=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+1)=1
. Setting the Samuilik
steepness
\mu_{i}=\lambda
to align the comparison with the other
three formulations, this gives

\displaystyle\dot{A}\;=\;\kappa_{A}\,\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda(A-B)}}\;-\;\gamma\,A,\qquad\dot{B}\;=\;\kappa_{B}\,\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda(A+B-1)}}\;-\;\gamma\,B.

(71)

The mixed-sign content of gene
A
(
w^{\mathrm{S}}_{AA}=+1,w^{\mathrm{S}}_{AB}=-1
) collapses
\theta_{A}^{\mathrm{S}}
to zero, an
instance of the pathological threshold-degeneration behaviour
catalogued in
Section

3.3
.

Equilibrium structure

Under the self-consistency condition (
65
), the
product-of-logistics and the bias-corrected weighted formulation
share the canonical equilibrium

, since at this configuration

S_{A}=S_{B}=0
and both functions evaluate to

, giving
\kappa_{i}\cdot\tfrac{1}{4}=\gamma\,\theta_{i}
for each
gene
i

The unbiased weighted formulation (
70

\mathbf{x}^{*}
as an equilibrium under the same self-consistency
parameters: at the point
S_{A}=S_{B}=0
(forced by

),
f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt}}
evaluates to
1/2
rather than
1/4
, hence

.
The system therefore settles to an

, non-shared
attractor (

The Samuilik formulation (

admits the equilibrium

7.4
Linearisation and Hopf bifurcation

The Jacobian of the product-of-logistics at the shared equilibrium

\mathbf{x}^{*}
is

J^{\mathrm{prod}}\;=\;-\gamma\,I+\frac{\lambda}{8}\,K\,W,\qquad K=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{A}&0\\
0&\kappa_{B}\end{pmatrix},\qquad W=\begin{pmatrix}+1&-1\\
+1&+1\end{pmatrix},

(72)

since for
m_{i}=2
each partial derivative

\partial_{l}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}\big|_{\mathbf{x}^{*}}=w_{il}\lambda\sigma^{\prime}(0)\,\sigma(0)=w_{il}\lambda\cdot(1/4)\cdot(1/2)=w_{il}\lambda/8

by
Proposition

4
with one differentiated factor and

m_{i}-1=1
non-differentiated factor evaluated at the critical
point. The matrix

KW
has trace
\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B}
and determinant
2\kappa_{A}\kappa_{B}
, hence (when the asymmetry is moderate,
(3-2\sqrt{2})<\kappa_{A}/\kappa_{B}<3+2\sqrt{2}
) complex conjugate eigenvalues

(\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B})/2\pm i\sqrt{8\kappa_{A}\kappa_{B}-(\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B})^{2}}/2
. The eigenvalues of
J^{\mathrm{prod}}

are therefore

\mu^{\mathrm{prod}}_{\pm}\;=\;-\gamma+\frac{\lambda(\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B})}{16}\pm i\,\frac{\lambda\sqrt{8\kappa_{A}\kappa_{B}-(\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B})^{2}}}{16},

(73)

and the equilibrium
\mathbf{x}^{*}
undergoes a Hopf bifurcation when

\mathrm{Re}(\mu^{\mathrm{prod}}_{\pm})=0
, that is, at

\lambda^{\mathrm{prod}}_{\mathrm{Hopf}}\;=\;\frac{16\,\gamma}{\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B}}.

(74)

By the eigenvalue map of
Proposition

11
(with
m_{i}=2

giving
D=2-2^{1-2}=3/2
) and uniform
\gamma
, the
bias-corrected weighted Jacobian satisfies

\mu^{\mathrm{bcw}}_{\pm}=(3/2)\,\mu^{\mathrm{prod}}_{\pm}+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma
, hence

\mu^{\mathrm{bcw}}_{\pm}\;=\;-\gamma+\frac{3\lambda(\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B})}{32}\pm i\,\frac{3\lambda\sqrt{8\kappa_{A}\kappa_{B}-(\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B})^{2}}}{32},\qquad\lambda^{\mathrm{bcw}}_{\mathrm{Hopf}}\;=\;\frac{32\,\gamma}{3(\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B})}.

(75)

Three structural consequences follow:

•

Equilibrium agreement.
Both formulations share

\mathbf{x}^{*}=(\theta_{A},\theta_{B})
for every
\lambda

(
Theorem

4
).

•

Frequency amplification.
At every fixed
\lambda
,
the oscillation frequency in the bias-corrected formulation is
amplified by the factor
D=3/2
relative to the product:

\mathrm{Im}(\mu^{\mathrm{bcw}}_{\pm})=(3/2)\,\mathrm{Im}(\mu^{\mathrm{prod}}_{\pm})
.

•

Earlier instability.
The bias-corrected formulation
bifurcates at
\lambda^{\mathrm{bcw}}_{\mathrm{Hopf}}=(2/3)\lambda^{\mathrm{prod}}_{\mathrm{Hopf}}
, strictly smaller than the
product threshold. For
\lambda\in[\lambda^{\mathrm{bcw}}_{\mathrm{Hopf}},\lambda^{\mathrm{prod}}_{\mathrm{Hopf}})
the bias-corrected system
oscillates while the product-of-logistics is still globally
exponentially stable: an explicit “destabilising” instance of the
stability dichotomy (
64
).

8
Discussion

8.1
When to use each formulation

The three formulations encode genuinely different modelling
hypotheses, and each is preferable in its own analytical context.

Product-of-logistics
(
8
).

The product-of-logistics is the formulation of choice when:

•

Multi-regulator interactions are interpreted as multiplicative
occupancy events (independent binding sites, AND-like cooperativity).

•

Bifurcation analysis on canonical motifs (toggle switch,
repressilator, oscillators) is the analytical objective. The
structural symmetry
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=0
enforced by the product
(
38
) drives the supercriticality of pitchfork
and Hopf bifurcations through cubic Lyapunov coefficients
(see
[
7
,
8
]
for closed-form
results).

•

Each regulator’s contribution must be analytically separable for
sensitivity analysis, observer design, or modular extension to
combinatorial logic gates via the De Morgan product formula.

Bias-corrected weighted-logistic (
13
).

The bias-corrected weighted formulation is the formulation of choice
when:

•

A single sigmoid per gene is required for tractability of
high-dimensional analysis: linearisation around equilibria, gain
scheduling, model-predictive control synthesis, or large-scale
parameter estimation. Each nonzero off-diagonal entry of the
Jacobian of (
13
) requires
one
sigmoid-derivative
evaluation, whereas the corresponding entry of the
product-of-logistics Jacobian (
7
) requires one
sigmoid derivative
and
a product over
m_{i}-1
remaining
sigmoid values; the resulting per-entry cost is
O(1)
for the
bias-corrected weighted formulation versus
O(m_{i})
for the product.

•

Regulator-specific thresholds must be retained (in contrast
with the Samuilik formulation (
9
), which collapses
them into a shared
\theta_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}
).

•

The dynamical regime of interest is near the critical point
(half-maximal regulatory configurations), where the bias correction
forces zeroth-order matching (exact value
1/2^{m_{i}}
) with the
product. The first-order discrepancy is linear in the displacement
with coefficient
(1-2^{1-m_{i}})\,\partial_{l}f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}
derived from the
slope-ratio formula (
Proposition

4
), and the second-order
curvature mismatch is
\lambda^{2}(2^{m_{i}}-1)(2^{m_{i}}-2)/8^{m_{i}}

(
Proposition

5
).

•

The parameter set lies on (or near) the self-consistency
sub-manifold of
Theorem

4
, where the two
formulations share a canonical critical-point equilibrium and the
linearised dynamics are related by the explicit spectral formula
(
63
). In this regime the bias-corrected
weighted formulation is a strict simplification of the
product-of-logistics with no loss of equilibrium structure (modulo the
spectral re-scaling and the curvature mismatch).

•

Numerical integration efficiency is a concern. Replacing
unbounded linear-additive regulation by the bounded
bias-corrected-weighted form substantially reduces the global
Lipschitz constants of both the vector field and its Jacobian; on the
Vinoth two-gene network, Belgacem
[
9
]
reports a

\sim 70\%
reduction in the vector-field constant and a
\sim 65\%

reduction in the Jacobian Lipschitz constant, permitting
correspondingly larger explicit-integrator step sizes.

Samuilik weighted-sum (
9
).

The Samuilik formulation is preferable only when network sparsity
combined with real-valued continuously graded weights is essential and
the negative-midpoint repression pathology
(
Section

3.2
) is biologically tolerable. We
note that the formulation has been productively used in qualitative
network analyses where the discrepancy with the
product-of-logistics is not the analytical
focus
[
31
,
28
]
, but its
quantitative use as a substitute for the product (or for the
bias-corrected weighted formulation) is structurally limited by the
threshold and repression issues established in

Section

3
.

8.2
Implications for control synthesis

The compactness of the bias-corrected weighted formulation is
particularly attractive for control-theoretic applications. The
single-sigmoid Jacobian below is a structurally simpler object to
plug into the qualitative-control framework
of
[
15
]
, in which undesired oscillations in
a genetic negative-feedback loop are suppressed by a piecewise-affine
controller with uncertain measurements, and into the
negative-feedback-loop control architecture
of
[
14
]
. The Jacobian of (
13
)
at
\mathbf{x}
is

\frac{\partial f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}}{\partial x_{l}}(\mathbf{x})\;=\;w_{il}\,\lambda\,\sigma^{\prime}\!\bigl(\lambda S_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\log(2^{m_{i}}-1)\bigr),

(76)

a single self-referential expression evaluated once per gene per
state, in contrast with the product-of-logistics Jacobian

\frac{\partial f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}}{\partial x_{l}}(\mathbf{x})\;=\;w_{il}\,\lambda\,\sigma^{\prime}(u_{l})\!\!\prod_{j\neq l}\!\sigma(u_{j}),

(77)

which requires
m_{i}
sigmoid evaluations and a product. For
high-dimensional networks (
n\gg 100
) with
m_{i}\gg 1
regulators per
gene, this difference can yield substantial computational savings in
gradient-based optimisation (e.g. inverse-problem parameter
estimation, gradient-flow control), provided the dynamical regime of
interest is consistent with the critical-point matching of

Theorem

2
.

8.3
Outlook on high-dimensional systems

Biological networks at the cellular scale integrate hundreds or
thousands of genes coupled through enzymatic and metabolic feedbacks.
For such large
n
, the analytical advantages of logistic functions
over Hill functions—global
C^{\infty}
-smoothness, closed-form
self-referential derivatives, uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants,
elementary inverses via the logit transformation—become
quantitatively important. Computer-aided high-dimensional analyses of
Glass networks
[
13
]
have already exposed the
combinatorial richness of periodicity, chaos, and bifurcation
structure in such large-scale circuits, and the bias-corrected
weighted-logistic formulation provides a smooth analytic surrogate in
which the same combinatorial structure is preserved while the
analytical complexity collapses to a single sigmoid per gene.
The bias-corrected weighted formulation
extends the logistic-framework advantages to the
single-sigmoid-per-gene regime, with
two complementary roles.
First
, as a substitute for the
product-of-logistics in control-design pipelines where the
multiplicative Jacobian structure is computationally prohibitive but
near-critical-point operation is the design point.
Second
, as
a building block for systematic linearisation schemes whose
reduced-order models inherit the three-point matching at the network
level. Quantifying these advantages on realistic genome-scale networks,
and identifying regimes where bias-corrected weighted formulations
preserve qualitative dynamical features (multistability, oscillations,
extreme events) of the underlying product-of-logistics, are natural
directions for future work. Hybrid-systems extensions—in which the
sigmoid steepness is allowed to diverge, recovering Glass-network
switches at the price of Zeno-like behaviour—may benefit from the
probabilistic-convolution regularisation
of
[
17
]
.

9
Conclusion

We introduced a bias-corrected weighted-logistic model for gene
regulatory networks (
Definition

2
) that bridges the
analytical compactness of single-sigmoid weighted-sum formulations
with the biological interpretability of product-of-logistics models.
The bias term
b_{i}=-\lambda^{-1}\log(2^{m_{i}}-1)
depends only on the
combinatorial structure of the network through the regulator count

m_{i}
, and a canonicity result (
Proposition

2
) shows it
is the
unique
single-sigmoid correction recovering the product’s
critical-point value
1/2^{m_{i}}
and sharing its canonical equilibrium
under self-consistency. The bias-corrected weighted regulatory function
coincides with the product-of-logistics at three biologically
significant reference configurations: the critical point, the
low-steepness limit, and the saturation limits
(
Theorem

2
).

The bias-corrected weighted-logistic ODE inherits the global
analytical guarantees of the product-of-logistics framework
of
[
7
,
8
]
: global
existence of
C^{\infty}
solutions, an explicit Lipschitz constant

L_{F}\leq M=\max_{i}(\kappa_{i}\,\lambda m_{i}/4+\gamma_{i})
, a positively
invariant hyper-rectangle
\prod_{i}[0,\kappa_{i}/\gamma_{i}]

(
Theorem

1
), and strictly-positive basal output

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}(\mathbf{x})>0
preventing the off-state trap
(
Corollary

1
). These properties make the bias-corrected
weighted-logistic formulation a structurally well-posed
single-sigmoid surrogate for the product framework, free of the
Hill-induced pathologies catalogued
in
[
7
,
8
]
.

The discrepancy between the two formulations admits the closed-form
algebraic identity (
27
), expressing

f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{prod}}-f_{i}^{\,\mathrm{wgt,bc}}
as a sum over the proper non-empty subsets of the
regulator index set (
Theorem

3
), with sharp
leading-order asymptotic decay rates in both saturation regimes
(
Proposition

3
). The local sensitivity ratio at the
critical point is exactly
2-2^{1-m_{i}}
(
Proposition

4
),
monotonically approaching
2
as
m_{i}\to\infty
. The structural
symmetry
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=0
that underlies closed-form supercritical
bifurcation theorems for the product is broken by the bias correction
for
m_{i}\geq 2
(
Proposition

5
). Three complementary
structural results characterise the bias as canonical: no
state-independent constant shift can yield global equivalence
(
Proposition

6
); the bias is the unique zero-order
mean-field approximation of the log-sum-exp correction at the critical
point (
Proposition

7
); and no affine single-sigmoid family
can simultaneously match the product’s value, slope, and curvature at
the critical point (
Proposition

8
).

A self-consistency condition
\theta_{ij}=(\kappa_{j}/\gamma_{j})/2^{m_{j}}

on the parameters singles out a parameter sub-manifold on which the
product-of-logistics and bias-corrected weighted dynamical systems
share a canonical critical-point equilibrium with regulatory output

1/2^{m_{i}}
(
Theorem

4
); in the contractive
regime
\gamma_{i}>\kappa_{i}\lambda m_{i}/4
, this shared equilibrium is

globally unique and exponentially attracting
for both systems
(
Corollary

6
), and any threshold perturbation

\bm{\eta}
from self-consistency propagates linearly to
equilibrium deviation through a parameter-Jacobian matrix that itself
obeys the row-wise rescaling
\Psi^{\rm bcw}=D\Psi^{\rm prod}

(
Proposition

10
). At a shared equilibrium, the dynamical
Jacobians of the two systems are related by the explicit transformation

J^{\rm bcw}=D(J^{\rm prod}+\Gamma)-\Gamma

(
Proposition

11
), and the bias correction satisfies a
stability dichotomy (
Corollary

7
) in which modes
of
J^{\rm prod}
with
\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}>-\gamma
are
destabilised, modes with
\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}<-\gamma

are further stabilised, and the threshold modes with

\operatorname{Re}\mu^{\rm prod}=-\gamma
are preserved exactly.

The framework is structurally distinct from the Samuilik weighted-sum
formulation (
9
), which loses regulator-specific
thresholds and exhibits a negative-midpoint repression pathology
(
Section

3.2
). Within the product-of-logistics
framework, real-valued positive weights are equivalent to fixed unit
weights under parameter rescaling
(
Proposition

9
), an identifiability constraint that
does not extend to the Samuilik formulation.

A two-gene negative-feedback oscillator with self-activation
illustrates these results concretely (
Section

7
). Both
the product-of-logistics and the bias-corrected weighted formulation
share the canonical critical-point equilibrium
\mathbf{x}^{*}=(\kappa_{A}/(4\gamma),\,\kappa_{B}/(4\gamma))
with two distinct biological
scales
\theta_{A}\neq\theta_{B}
. The bias-corrected formulation
undergoes Hopf bifurcation at
\lambda^{\rm bcw}_{\rm Hopf}=32\gamma/(3(\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B}))
, strictly smaller than the
product’s threshold
\lambda^{\rm prod}_{\rm Hopf}=16\gamma/(\kappa_{A}+\kappa_{B})
by the factor
2/3
predicted by the
eigenvalue map of
Proposition

11
, and exhibits
sustained oscillations around
\mathbf{x}^{*}
in the intermediate regime

\lambda\in(\lambda^{\rm bcw}_{\rm Hopf},\,\lambda^{\rm prod}_{\rm Hopf})
,
where the product is linearly stable (
Figure

2
). The
bias correction is therefore not a recalibration of equilibria but
a structural shift in the bifurcation diagram.

Future work will pursue: (i) extensions of the bias correction to
heterogeneous steepness and to weighted aggregation rules beyond
unit-magnitude; (ii) systematic linearisation and model-reduction
schemes for high-dimensional bias-corrected weighted networks
preserving stability and bifurcation structure; (iii) parameter
estimation from experimental time-series data, exploiting the
modular identifiability of regulator-specific thresholds; and
(iv) applications to control-theoretic synthesis, observer design,
and stochastic and spatiotemporal extensions of the framework.
