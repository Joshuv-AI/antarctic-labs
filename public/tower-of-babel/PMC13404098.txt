Which RRT initiations does sodium bicarbonate prevent in severe metabolic acidemia?
Authors: Xiang Zhou, Hongyan Liu, Zhengdong Hua
Published: 2026

Fosset and colleagues report a 15.9-percentage-point reduction in renal replacement therapy (RRT) initiation, judged to be of high certainty and corresponding to a number needed to treat of 6.3 [1]. This is likely to be the finding that most directly influences bedside bicarbonate use. Yet bicarbonate was titrated to an arterial pH ≥ 7.30, whereas persistent pH ≤ 7.20 contributed to protocolized recommendations for RRT in the source trials [2]. RRT is therefore a treatment-sensitive decision endpoint: bicarbonate can keep a patient from meeting an acidemia-based criterion for RRT initiation without necessarily reducing RRT initiated for other indications.

This distinction is visible but not resolved in the source reports. In BICARICU-2, RRT was initiated later with bicarbonate, and persistent pH ≤ 7.20 was recorded as an indication in 45 of 109 bicarbonate-treated RRT recipients and 101 of 157 controls [2]. Major adverse kidney events at day 90 were nevertheless similar (83% vs. 84%). Because indications could overlap and denominators included only RRT recipients, these data cannot estimate whether randomization reduced first RRT initiation with versus without persistent acidemia.

The pooled 28-day time-to-RRT curves remain separated, arguing against short deferral as the whole explanation [1]. The narrower unresolved question is whether the absolute reduction was confined to RRT initiations accompanied by persistent acidemia or also extended to initiations without that indication.

Could the authors use the pooled individual-patient data to classify each first RRT initiation by whether persistent acidemia was listed as an initiating indication and estimate, in each randomized group, the 28-day cumulative incidence of initiation with and without this indication, with death before RRT treated as a competing event and source trial accounted for? If the indication variables could not be harmonized, trial-specific estimates would still define the transportability boundary. The relevant denominator is all randomized patients, not only those who received RRT.
