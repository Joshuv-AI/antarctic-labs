Using ASP(Q) to Handle Inconsistent Prioritized Data
Meghyn Bienvenu, Camille Bourgaux, Robin Jean, Giuseppe Mazzotta

Abstract
We explore the use of answer set programming (ASP) and its extension with quantifiers, ASP(Q), for inconsistency-tolerant querying of prioritized data, where a priority relation between conflicting facts is exploited to define three notions of optimal repairs (Pareto-, globally- and completion-optimal). We consider the variants of three well-known semantics (AR, brave and IAR) that use these optimal repairs, and for which query answering is in the first or second level of the polynomial hierarchy for a large class of logical theories. Notably, this paper presents the first implementation of globally-optimal repair-based semantics, as well as the first implementation of the grounded semantics, which is a tractable under-approximation of all these optimal repair-based semantics. Our experimental evaluation sheds light on the feasibility of computing answers under globally-optimal repair semantics and the impact of adopting different semantics, approximations, and encodings.

Introduction

Repair-based semantics are a prominent means of obtaining meaningful answers to queries posed over some data which is inconsistent w.r.t. some logical theory, both in the relational database and ontology-mediated query answering setting (cf.

The most well-known repair-based semantics, called AR in the KR community, requires that the query holds in every repair, while the less cautious brave semantics requires that it holds in some repair, and the more cautious IAR semantics that it holds in the intersection of all repairs

,

,

Several notions of preferred repairs have been proposed
to take into account some preference information and consider only a subset of all the possible repairs to evaluate the queries (cf.
[
14
]

This is due to

Indeed, for a large class of database constraints and ontology languages, the data complexity of query answering under the variants of AR, IAR and brave that use Pareto- or completion-optimal repairs is in the first level of the polynomial hierarchy, while it is in the second level for the globally-optimal repair-based variants.

Most implementations of
repair-based semantics encode query entailment under some intractable semantics into SAT, binary
integer programming, or answer set programming (ASP) to take advantage of the efficient solvers that exist for these problems (see, e.g.,

The two implementations of optimal repair-based semantics follow this path: the one by

ASP (with disjunctive rules) can be used to model problems up to the second level of the polynomial hierarchy, meaning that semantics based on globally-optimal repairs could theoretically be implemented in ASP. However, this requires advanced techniques, such as saturation
[
20
]
, which complicate modeling
“beyond the capabilities of the common ASP laymen”

[

]
.
Moreover, efficiency-wise it has been shown that in many cases of practical interest alternative formalisms are preferred to disjunctive ASP

]

Among alternative formalisms, answer set programming with quantifiers (ASP(Q)) uses quantifiers over answer sets of ASP programs and provides a natural and compact way of modeling problems in the entire polynomial hierarchy

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have presented the first implementation of globally-optimal repair-based
semantics, using ASP(Q), and of the grounded semantics, making it possible for
the first time to compare these semantics with the previously implemented semantics
based upon Pareto- and completion-optimal repairs.
While we are
able to compute query answers for some instances with globally-optimal repairs, the runtimes are significantly longer, with more timeouts, than for the Pareto-
and completion-optimal repairs, in line with the worst-case complexity. This suggests that even if we aim to compute G-AR and G-brave answers, a better strategy is to first test whether the candidate answer holds under P-AR semantics and P-brave semantics (which are the fastest optimal repair semantics) and only call the G-AR and G-brave procedures if the status remains unresolved.

Our evaluation also highlighted
the surprising effectiveness of
the grounded
semantics as an approximation of the optimal repair-based semantics, an insight which moreover can be applied also to non-ASP-based implementations. Importantly, the grounded repair can be computed as a preprocessing task,
thus making it possible to employ a principled inconsistency-tolerant semantics with little overhead compared to standard query answering.
This suggests the interest of developing highly optimized methods for computing and updating the grounded repair for very large datasets.

(w.r.t. ‘weak’ reachability)
\mathcal{B}_{r}=\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{w}}(\mathcal{B})
, which is used to define the prioritized KB
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}_{\succ^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}}
by
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}=(\mathcal{B}_{r},\mathcal{T})
and
\succ^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}
the restriction of
\succ
to
\mathcal{B}^{r}
.

In the proofs of the propositions, we will use the
set of minimal (w.r.t.
\succ
) facts of a conflict
:
for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
let
Min(\mathcal{C})=\{\gamma|\forall\delta\in\mathcal{C},\gamma\not\succ\delta\}
.

We recall that if
\succ^{\prime}
is a total priority relation, there is a unique Pareto-, globally- and completion-optimal repair
[
35
]
, so one can define completion-optimal repairs equivalently as those that are Pareto-optimal or globally-optimal w.r.t. some completion
\succ^{\prime}
of
\succ
.

Proposition 4
.

For
X\in\{P,G,C\}
, if
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{XRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
, then
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}\in\mathit{XRep}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}_{\succ^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}})
.

Proof.

Case
\mathbf{X=P}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
and suppose for a contradiction that
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}\notin\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}_{\succ^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}})
: there exists
\mathcal{R}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{r}
such that there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})
, such that
\beta\succ\alpha
for all
\alpha\in(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}=\{\beta\}\cup(\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{R})\cup(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r})
. We build a Pareto improvement of
\mathcal{R}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{K}_{\succ}
by removing selected elements from
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}
to obtain a contradiction with
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
.

Claim 1
.

For every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}
, there exists
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\beta\succ\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof of claim.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
be such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}
.
First, note that
\beta\in\mathcal{C}
as otherwise
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, which contradicts the
\mathcal{T}
-consistency of
\mathcal{R}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r})\neq\emptyset
as otherwise
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{0}
which contradicts the
\mathcal{T}
-consistency of
\mathcal{R}_{0}
.
By definition of
\mathcal{B}_{r}=\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{w}}(\mathcal{B})
, and since
\beta\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
, if for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}
,
\beta\not\succ\gamma
, it would hold that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{r}
. Since we showed that
\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r})\neq\emptyset
, it follows that
there exists
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\beta\succ\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
.
(end of claim proof)

Let
\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}=\{\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\mid\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K}),\mathcal{C}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}},\beta\succ\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}=\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}\setminus\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}
.

As
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}
is obtained by removing at least one fact per conflict included in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}
, it is
\mathcal{T}
-consistent. Also:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}\setminus\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}})\setminus\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=((\{\beta\}\cup(\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{R})\cup(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}))\setminus\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}})\setminus\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\{\beta\}\text{ since $\beta\not\in\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}$ and $\beta\not\in\mathcal{R}$;}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}\setminus(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}\setminus\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}})

\displaystyle=((\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0})\cup\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}\text{ since $\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}$.}

By construction of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}
, for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}
,
\beta\succ\alpha
. Hence
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}
is a Pareto improvement of
\mathcal{R}
, which contradicts
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
.
Therefore
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}_{\succ^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}})
.

Case
\mathbf{X=G}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{GRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
and suppose for a contradiction that
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}\notin\mathit{GRep}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}_{\succ^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}})
: there exists
\mathcal{R}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{r}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{0}\neq\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
and for every
\alpha\in(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
, there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})
such that
\beta\succ\alpha
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}=\mathcal{R}_{0}\cup(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r})
. We build a global improvement of
\mathcal{R}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{K}_{\succ}
by removing selected elements from
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}
to obtain a contradiction with
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{GRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
.

We first show that for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}
, there exist
\beta_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}))
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus(\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}))
such that
\beta_{\mathcal{C}}\succ\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
be such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}
. First, note that
\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}))\neq\emptyset
as otherwise
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, which contradicts the
\mathcal{T}
-consistency of
\mathcal{R}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r})\neq\emptyset
as otherwise
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{0}
, which contradicts the
\mathcal{T}
-consistency of
\mathcal{R}_{0}
.

Claim 2
.

For every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}
,
Min(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}

Proof of claim.

By definition of
\mathcal{B}_{r}=\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{w}}(\mathcal{B})
, if there was
\alpha\in Min(\mathcal{C})
such that
\alpha\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
, it would hold that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{r}
. Since
\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r})\neq\emptyset
, it follows that
Min(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}
.

(end of claim proof)

We define
{(\beta_{i})}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
with
\beta_{0}\in\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}))
(such
\beta_{0}
exists since
\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}))\neq\emptyset
) and for
i\in\mathbb{N}
:

•

if
\beta_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
then let
\beta_{i+1}\in\mathcal{C}
be such that
\beta_{i}\succ\beta_{i+1}
(such
\beta_{i+1}
exists because
Min(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}
and
\mathcal{R}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{r}
so
\beta_{i}\notin Min(\mathcal{C})
);

•

otherwise (
\beta_{i}\not\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
), let
\beta_{i+1}=\beta_{i}
.

As
\mathcal{D}
is finite and
\succ
is acyclic the sequence is ultimately constant. Let
j=\min_{i\in\mathbb{N}}{(\beta_{i+1}\not\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}))}
.
Let
\beta_{\mathcal{C}}=\beta_{j}
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}=\beta_{j+1}
. By definition of
j
we have
\beta_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\not\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})
, and by construction of
(\beta_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, it holds that
\beta_{\mathcal{C}}\succ\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Let
\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}=\{\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\mid\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K}),\mathcal{C}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}\}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}=\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}\setminus\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}
. As
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}
is obtained by removing at least one fact per conflict included in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}
,
it is
\mathcal{T}
-consistent. Also:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}\setminus\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}})\setminus\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}))\setminus\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})

as for all
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\in\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}
we have
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\not\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}\setminus(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}\setminus\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}\setminus((\mathcal{R}_{0}\cup(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}))\setminus\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}})

\displaystyle=((\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0})\cup\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}.

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}
:

•

If
\alpha\in(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
, by assumption on
\mathcal{R}_{0}
, there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})=\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}
such that
\beta\succ\alpha
.

•

Otherwise,
\alpha\in\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{0}}}
is equal to some
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
and
\beta=\beta_{\mathcal{C}}
is such that
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})=\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}
(by definition of the
\beta_{\mathcal{C}}
) and
\beta\succ\alpha
(since
\beta_{\mathcal{C}}\succ\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
).

Hence
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{0}
is a global improvement of
\mathcal{R}
, which contradicts
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{GRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
.
Therefore
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}\in\mathit{GRep}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}_{\succ^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}})
.

Case
\mathbf{X=C}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{CRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ^{\prime}})
for some
\succ^{\prime}
completion of
\succ
. Thus, by the case
X=P
of the proposition that we have already shown, we have
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}_{{\succ^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}})
. As
{\succ^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}
is a completion of
{\succ}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}
in
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}
we directly obtain that
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}\in\mathit{CRep}(\mathcal{K}_{{\succ}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}})
.
∎

Proposition 5
.

For
X\in\{P,G,C\}
, if
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{XRep}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}_{\succ^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}})
, then there exists
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\in\mathit{XRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
such that
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
.

Proof.

Case
\mathbf{X=P}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}_{{\succ}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}})
.
Following the proof of Lemma 9 from the extended version of
[
9
]
, we build some
\mathcal{R}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
as required by the proposition as follows:
let
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}
, and repeat the following steps until
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is empty:

•

Choose
\alpha\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
such that
\beta\not\succ\alpha
for all
\beta\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.

•

If
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is
\mathcal{T}
-consistent, add
\alpha
to
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
.

•

Remove
\alpha
from
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{*}
be the final
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
.
First, note that
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
by initialization of
\mathcal{R}^{*}
and definition of
\mathcal{R}
. Also
\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
as otherwise there would exist
\alpha\in(\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})\setminus\mathcal{R}
but then
\mathcal{R}\cup\{\alpha\}
would be
\mathcal{T}
-consistent and hence a Pareto improvement of
\mathcal{R}
. Thus
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
.

We show that
\mathcal{R}^{*}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
. Suppose for a contradiction there exists
\mathcal{R}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
such that there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{*}
such that
\beta\succ\alpha
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
. Since
\beta\not\in\mathcal{R}^{*}
, by construction of
\mathcal{R}^{*}
, there exists
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\beta\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{\beta\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
when
\beta
was chosen in the construction of
\mathcal{R}^{*}
(otherwise
\beta
would have been added to
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
).

Claim 3
.

For every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\beta\in\mathcal{C}
,
Min(\mathcal{C})\cap\mathcal{R}=\emptyset
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose for a contradiction
Min(\mathcal{C})\cap\mathcal{R}\neq\emptyset
, i.e., there exists
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
such that
\gamma\not\succ\delta
for every
\delta\in\mathcal{C}
. By definition of
\mathcal{B}_{r}=\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{w}}(\mathcal{B})
,
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{r}
, and in particular
\beta\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
. Hence
\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
is a Pareto improvement of
\mathcal{R}
, which contradicts the assumption
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}_{\succ^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}})
.

(end of claim proof)

As
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is
\mathcal{T}
-consistent,
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{\beta\}\not\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{0}
so there is
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{R}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0})
. Hence
\beta\succ\alpha_{0}
(by definition of
\mathcal{R}_{0}
and
\beta
).

Since
\alpha_{0}
has been chosen after
\beta
in the construction of
\mathcal{R}^{*}
and was already in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
when
\beta
was considered, we necessarily have that
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{R}
. Hence, since
Min(\mathcal{C})\cap\mathcal{R}=\emptyset
, there exists
\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{C}
with
\alpha_{0}\succ\alpha_{1}
.

Because
\beta\succ\alpha_{0}\succ\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{1}
was chosen after
\beta
in the construction of
\mathcal{R}^{*}
thus necessarily
\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{R}
. Thus we can iterate the argument to create an infinite chain
\beta\succ\alpha_{0}\succ\alpha_{1}\succ\ldots
with all
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
. As
\succ
is acyclic and
\mathcal{D}
is finite we obtain a contradiction.
Therefore
\mathcal{R}^{*}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
.

Case
\mathbf{X=G}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{GRep}(\mathcal{K}_{{\succ}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}})
.
As we did in the case
X=P
, let
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}
, and repeat the following steps until
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is empty:

•

Choose
\alpha\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
such that
\beta\not\succ\alpha
for all
\beta\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.

•

If
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is
\mathcal{T}
-consistent, add
\alpha
to
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
.

•

Remove
\alpha
from
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{*}
be the final
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
.
As in the case
X=P
, we obtain

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
.

We show that
\mathcal{R}^{*}\in\mathit{GRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
. Suppose for a contradiction there exists
\mathcal{R}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{0}\neq\mathcal{R}^{*}
and for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
, there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{*}
such that
\beta\succ\alpha
.

Assume for a contradiction that there exists
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
. Since
\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
, there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{*}
such that
\beta\succ\alpha
.
By definition of
\succ
,
\beta\succ\alpha
implies that there exists
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
such that
\{\alpha,\beta\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.
In particular, as
\alpha\not\succ\beta
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
we must have
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{r}
hence
\beta\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
.
Therefore for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\setminus(\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})
there exists
\beta\in(\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r})\setminus\mathcal{R}
such that
\beta\succ\alpha
, which contradicts
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{GRep}(\mathcal{K}_{{\succ}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}})
.
Thus
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{0}
. Hence
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
(otherwise we have a contradiction with
\mathcal{R}
being inclusion-maximal).

Let
\alpha_{0}
be the first element of
(\mathcal{R}^{*}\cup\mathcal{R}_{0})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}
considered in the construction of
\mathcal{R}^{*}
.

•

If
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{R}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
then there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{*}
such that
\beta\succ\alpha_{0}
but then
\beta\not\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
because as we previously showed
\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}=\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
thus
\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}
. Hence
\beta
has been considered before
\alpha_{0}
which contradicts our assumption.

•

If
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{*}
then there exists
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{\alpha_{0}\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
(otherwise
\alpha_{0}
would have been added) but then
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{\alpha_{0}\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
as
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
. Hence
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{0}
and as
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
this contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is
\mathcal{T}
-consistent.

•

Thus
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{R}_{0}
.

Suppose we proved that the
i
first elements
\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{i-1}
of
(\mathcal{R}^{*}\cup\mathcal{R}_{0})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}
considered in the construction of
\mathcal{R}^{*}
are in
\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{R}_{0}
, and let
\alpha_{i}\in(\mathcal{R}^{*}\cup\mathcal{R}_{0})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r}
be the next one.

•

If
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{R}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
then there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{*}
such that
\beta\succ\alpha_{i}
but then
\beta\not\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
has been considered before
\alpha_{i}
which contradicts our assumption.

•

If
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{*}
then there exists
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{\alpha_{i}\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}\cup\{\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{i-1}\}
but then (because
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r}
and
\{\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{i-1}\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{0}
) we have
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{0}
. As
\mathcal{C}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{K})
this contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is
\mathcal{T}
-consistent.

•

Thus
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{R}^{*}\cap\mathcal{R}_{0}
.

Therefore we have recursively that
\mathcal{R}^{*}=\mathcal{R}_{0}
which contradicts our assumption.

Case
\mathbf{X=C}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{CRep}(\mathcal{K}_{{\succ}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}})
.
There exists a completion
{\succ^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}
of
{\succ}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}
in
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}
such that
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{K}_{{\succ^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}})
. Let
\succ^{\prime}
be a completion of
\succ
in
\mathcal{K}
that agrees with
{\succ^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}
on
\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}_{r}}
.
We showed that there exists

Appendix B
Alternative ASP(Q) Encoding for G-AR

For G-AR semantics, we considered the following alternative ASP(Q) encoding.

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathit{AR}}^{G\ 2}=

\displaystyle\forall^{st}\Pi_{\mathit{ReachAll}}\Pi_{\mathit{Rep}}\exists^{st}\Pi_{\mathit{PartGImp}}:C_{AR}^{G\ 2}

with

\displaystyle C_{AR}^{G\ 2}=

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathit{SatIfCause}}

\displaystyle\{\ {:\!\text{-}}\ \mathtt{not~sat,not~global\_improvement.}\}

and
\Pi_{\mathit{PartGImp}}
is obtained from
\Pi_{\mathit{GImp}}
by removing the constraint (
\ {:\!\text{-}}\

\mathtt{not~global\_improvement}
).

This second program for G-AR,
\Pi_{\mathit{AR}}^{G\ 2}
, intuitively checks whether for every
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{SRep}(\mathcal{K})
, there exists some
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
such that either
\mathcal{R}
contains some cause for
q(\vec{a})
or
\mathcal{B}
is a global improvement for
\mathcal{R}
, so that
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{GRep}(\mathcal{K}_{\succ})
implies
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{T})\models q(\vec{a})
. Specifically, if we let
P_{1}=\Pi_{\mathit{ReachAll}}\cup\Pi_{\mathit{Rep}}
and
P_{2}=\Pi_{\mathit{PartGImp}}
, so that
\Pi_{\mathit{AR}}^{G\ 2}=\forall^{st}P_{1}\exists^{st}P_{2}:C_{AR}^{G\ 2}
, we know that
\Pi_{\mathit{AR}}^{G\ 2}
is coherent if for every
M_{1}\in AS(P_{1})
,
\exists^{st}P_{2}\cup\mathit{fix_{P_{1}}(M_{1})}:C_{AR}^{G\ 2}
is coherent, i.e., if for
P^{\prime}_{2}=P_{2}\cup\mathit{fix_{P_{1}}(M_{1})}
, there exists
M_{2}\in AS(P^{\prime}_{2})
such that
C_{AR}^{G\ 2}\cup\mathit{fix_{P^{\prime}_{2}}(M_{2})}
is coherent. We have seen that answer sets of
P_{1}
correspond to repairs, and that given a repair
\mathcal{R}
,
P_{2}
guesses a
\mathcal{T}
-consistent
\mathcal{B}
and derives
\mathtt{global\_improvement}
iff
\mathcal{B}
is a global improvement of
\mathcal{R}
, and the part
\Pi_{\mathit{SatIfCause}}
of the constraint
C_{AR}^{G\ 2}
derives
\mathtt{sat}
iff
\mathcal{R}
contains some cause. Hence, the remaining constraint,

\ {:\!\text{-}}\ \mathtt{not~sat,not~global\_improvement}
, which ensures that either
\mathtt{sat}
or
\mathtt{global\_improvement}
has been derived, enforces the above characterization of
\mathcal{K}_{\succ}\models_{\textup{AR}}^{G}q(\vec{a})
.

Proposition 8
.

\mathcal{K}_{\succ}\models_{\textup{AR}}^{G}q(\vec{a})
iff
\Pi_{\mathit{AR}}^{G\ 2}
is coherent.
