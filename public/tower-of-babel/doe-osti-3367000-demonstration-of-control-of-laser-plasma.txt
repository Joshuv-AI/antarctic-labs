Abstract

The success of laser Driven Inertial Fusion Technology (LaDrIFT) hinges on controlling laser-
plasma instabilities (LPI) for effective and non-deleterious energy coupling, together with the
control of implosion hydrodynamic instabilities (IHI) for target integrity. Conventional approaches
ignore LPI and focus on IHI. LPI control suggests the use of low intensities, short wavelengths,
and thus the slow implosions of thinner shells, while IHI control calls for thicker shells, fast
implosions and thus at higher laser intensities and ablation pressures. These contradicting
requirements severely restrict LaDrIFT design space, flexibility and scalability. This program
demonstrates, with theoretical designs and their preliminary experimental realizations, that STUD
pulses (Spike Trains of Uneven Duration and Delay) can control LPI in high-energy-density (HED)
laser-created plasmas and explore this physics for the first time with high repetition (rep) rate
lasers.
Experiments designed at PRI by the PI in collaboration with the UNL and CSU team leads were
implemented and executed on the ALEPH Ti:Sapphire laser facility at Colorado State University
(CSU) using solid Kapton targets. A P-polarized pump beam at 800 nm was used at 5 degrees,
10, 15, and 20 before settling on the latter for most consistent results. The plasma was first
created by a heater beam which was S polarized also at 800 nm but more energy and much
longer pulses. The pump was always less than 3 ps FWHM. Typically, 2 ps or 3 ps. Thus a 20°
incident P-Pol 2 or 3 ps pump drove electron plasma waves (EPWs) at the critical density (nₙ) via
resonance absorption (RA) and at the quarter-critical density (nₙ/4) via (Stimulated Raman
Scattering) SRS, TPD (Two Plasmon Decay) and their potential interactions. Optical signatures
observed were at 2ω₀ (generated via two EPWs interacting to directly generate EM radiation at
nc), ω₀/2 and 3ω₀/2 (SRS and two-plasmon decay, TPD, occurring at nc/4). These were recorded
with time-integrated fiber spectrometers. Spike parameters were varied: so that spike durations

 1

were tₒₙ = 50–200 fs FWHM, duty cycle fDC = 17%–100%, peak intensity I = 10¹⁵–3×10¹⁶ W/cm²,
in 2 ps and 3 ps overall envelopes containing ten spikes.

It is important to note that the various processes observed were in their kinetic regimes of
saturation and not fluid, because ions did not have sufficient time to move and produce sufficient
IAW physics to lead RA or SRS into fluid evolution regimes. 100’a of ps pulses would reverse this
and fluid evolution and electron-ion collisions (dissipation) would severely complicate the
dynamics.
STUD pulses with low duty cycles (well separated spikes) allowed the EPW to keep growing and
not wavebreak. This is a very significant finding. The combined physics of trapping, untrapping,
and retrapping oscillations at 4-5 times the electron thermal velocity, and the wave breaking to
50-100 times the thermal velocity were disrupted by modulating the laser drive into bursts or
spikes on a time scale short or comparable to the trapping time, depending on the laser intensity
used. The absence of sustained (continuous or overlapped spikes) drive made the kinetic EPWs
grow at the critical density where Landau damping type mechanisms are weak (long wavelength
regime). Large kinetic plasma waves created at the critical density could then radiate 2ω₀ light
directly by beating. This mechanism would predict quadratic scaling of 2ω₀ emission with pump
intensity, since each EPW amplitude would be proportional to I0, and their product to I02. — a 10-
fold increase in second-harmonic signal corresponding to a greater that 3.3 times increase in
peak plasma wave amplitude relative to EPW amplitudes driven by unmodulated or overlapped
Gaussian pulses. Simultaneously, STUD pulses suppressed ω₀/2 and 3ω₀/2 backscatter
(SRS/TPD signatures from nc / 4) by up to 87%, keeping quarter-critical instabilities in their
exponential growth (small signal gain) regimes.

Thus, two simultaneous and significant findings were discovered. Highly uncontrolled and chaotic
EPW behavior seen when spike widths were increased, on the intensity approached maximum
levels available on ALEPH, the intensity scaling of the second harmonic radiation scaled in an
unpredictable 3rd , 4th or in between powers of the pump intensity. While when tamed with distinct
and short enough spikes, EPWs grew significantly more! Previous experiments on Omega with
long pulses, 44 years ago only found fractional and ambiguous scaling such as the power 1.5,
But their dynamic range of intensities was tiny, their pulses were nanosecond scale (and results
time integrated), beam transverse profiles wide and non-uniform and irreproducible shot to shot,
and they had very few shots. We had an abundance of shots, short pulses, very small focal spots,
and recovered consistent RA/EPW growth scaling behavior for the first time thanks to STUD
pulses.

The other observation of extreme interest was that at the lower quarter critical densities, all kinetic
parametric instabilities were tamed with short spikes with long duty cycles at not too high a drive
intensity. The proof was exponential scaling with pump intensity of the half odd integer radiation.
Whereas, with overlapped spikes or wide Gaussians, intensity scaling was flat indicating nonlinear
saturation and no external control. A companion publication (Wang et al., Rev. Sci. Instrum. 97,
053510 (2026)) details the novel step-mirror pulse-train generation technique used at the ALEPH
petawatt-class facility. These results constitute the first systematic, high-repetition-rate
experimental demonstration of STUD pulse LPI control and represent a major step toward
dynamic instability mitigation for inertial fusion energy (IFE). The experimental results are being
submitted for publication shortly.

1. Introduction

1.1 Problem Statement
Laser-plasma instabilities (LPI) — principally Stimulated Raman Scattering (SRS), Two-Plasmon
Decay (TPD), Stimulated Brillouin Scattering (SBS), pose existential threats to LaDrIFT at the
intensities and plasma scale lengths relevant to ignition-class experiments. At the quarter-critical
density (nₙ/4), SRS and TPD generate hot-electron populations that preheat the fuel and degrade
implosion symmetry; SBS misdirects drive beams (out of the plasma) ; and filamentation causes
high intensity concentration seeding SRS and SBS violently. At the critical density (nₙ), RA drives
large-amplitude EPWs that cascade kinetically and nonlinearly, producing energetic electron jets
and steep density-profile modification decoupling the corona from the ablation surface.
Since high power lasers came online in the ‘70s, signatures of LPI have been measured at levels
that substantially degrade coupling efficiency. Conventional mitigation strategies — bandwidth
smoothing, polarization rotation, beam smoothing — address single instability in very narrow
plasma conditions very near threshold and cannot adequately suppress the correlated multi-
instability problem over long time scales and complex spatial beam intensity (speckled)
conditions. No prior experimental program had directly demonstrated time-domain pulse shaping
as prescribed by the STUD pulse program as a viable means of simultaneously controlling both
critical-density and quarter-critical-density instabilities operating far above smooth beam long
pulse beam thresholds.

1.2 Objectives / Purpose
The primary objectives of this program were:
 • Design experiments to test the predictions of elements of the STUD pulse program.
 • Start by treating high frequency instabilities since ALEPH beams are all short pulse.
 • Simulate the development of RA with and w/o STUD pulses accessible on ALEPH.
 Estimate SRS and TPD conditions expected and design the experiment accordingly.
 • Perform the first high-repetition-rate experimental test of STUD pulse LPI mitigation,
 covering both RA at nc and SRS/TPD at nc/4, simultaneously.
 • Characterize scaling of second-harmonic (2ω₀), half-harmonic (ω₀/2), and three-halves-
 harmonic (3ω₀/2) emission as a function of spike duration, duty cycle, and peak spike
 intensity.
 • Demonstrate that STUD pulses allow stable controlled secular growth of EPWs at the
 critical density, suppressing nonlinear kinetic and fluid channels of self-organization and
 plasma wave wave-breaking.
 • Develop and validate the step-mirror-array pulse-shaping technique for generating ten-
 spike STUD trains from a petawatt-class CPA laser, and publish the method as a
 reusable diagnostic tool (see Wang et al., RSI 2026).
 • Establish scaling laws and systematic trends suitable for benchmarking kinetic and fluid
 LPI codes used across the NNSA national laboratory complex.

2.2 Methodology
Facility: Experiments were performed on the ALEPH (Advanced Laser for Extreme Photonics)
Ti:Sapphire chirped-pulse-amplification (CPA) laser at Colorado State University. ALEPH
operates at up to 3.3 Hz repetition rate, delivering λ = 800 nm pulses tunable from 30 fs to several
picoseconds. Stretched 300 ps pulses are amplified through a Ti:Sapphire chain and
recompressed with a gold-grating Treacy compressor.
Target: 63.5 µm Kapton polyimide film mounted on a rastering target array. A 5 J heater beam
(split before compression, 300 ps) was focused to a 100 µm spot to pre-form the plasma 200 ps
ahead of the interaction beam, establishing a favorable density scalelength at 800 nm.
STUD pulse generation: After recompression, the interaction beam passed through a novel 10-
element step-mirror array — ten individually addressable, hexagonal dichroic mirror segments,
each with three actuators controlling tip/tilt and longitudinal position. Each 150 µm of mirror
displacement introduces 1 ps inter-spike delay. Spike duration was adjusted by varying the grating
separation in the compressor via a motorized translation stage. This new technique, developed
as part of this project, is described in full in Wang et al., Rev. Sci. Instrum. 97, 053510 (2026).

Pulse parameter space explored:
   •   Spike duration tₒₙ: 50, 100, 150, 200 fs (FWHM)
 • Overall pulse envelope: 2 ps and 3 ps (ten spikes each)
 • Duty cycle fDC: 17% to 133% (Gaussian emulator limit)
 • Peak spike intensity: 10¹⁵ to 3×10¹⁶ W/cm²

Diagnostics:
 • Time-integrated fiber spectrometers (Ocean Insight USB2000+, Flame NIR, USB4000)
       covering 372–420 nm (2ω₀), 500–560 nm (3ω₀/2), 750–850 nm (ω₀), and 1550–1660

 5

 nm (ω₀/2). Spectral filtering: BG39 bandpass for UV-visible channels; long-pass >1050
 nm for IR.
 • Variable-field electron spectrometer (Shpilman design) for hot-electron energy spectra.
 • X-ray silicon diodes with 6 µm Al filter for hot-electron bremsstrahlung.

Data analysis: For each shot condition, ~10 shots were acquired at ~1 Hz. Signifcant shot-to-shot
variability was observed due to laser energy drift, heater-beam pointing drift (>50 m path through
air), target rastering tolerances, and fiber-coupling repeatability. The analysis focused on the top-
two maximum signals per condition to establish a lower bound on peak performance. A self-
consistent subset of days — identified by rational I-scaling of all three spectral channels
simultaneously — was used to extract scaling exponents.

3. Results and Accomplishments

3.1 Key Accomplishments
 • First experimental demonstration that STUD pulses control LPI simultaneously at both nₙ
 (RA) and nₙ/4 (SRS/TPD) in a single laser-plasma system.
 • Demonstrated quadratic (I²) scaling of 2ω₀ emission for 2 ps and 3 ps STUD pulses —
 consistent with secular, linear-regime EPW growth — versus super-quadratic scaling
 (I³·⁶ for 3 ps Gaussian emulator) indicative of nonlinear saturation.
 • Achieved a 10× increase in 2ω₀ signal (>3× in EPW amplitude) relative to unmodulated
 Gaussian pulses at equivalent energy, with fᴰᶜ = 17% and I = 8.7×10¹⁵ W/cm².
 • Suppressed SRS/TPD backscatter (ω₀/2 and 3ω₀/2) by up to 87% with STUD pulses,
 while Gaussian emulator pulses showed saturation and intensity-insensitive behavior.
 • Demonstrated that instabilities at nₙ/4 remained decoupled from critical-density physics
 under STUD-pulse irradiation.
 • Published a new pulse-generation methodology enabling trains of pulses with arbitrary
 delay and adjustable pulse width from a petawatt-class CPA laser ( see Wang et al., RSI
 2026).

3.2 Results
Resonance Absorption — 2ω₀ Signatures:
STUD pulses with short spike durations (tₒₙ = 50–100 fs) and low duty cycles (fᴰᶜ = 17–33%)
consistently produced 2ω₀ signals exceeding those of Gaussian emulator pulses (fᴰᶜ = 100%) by
a factor of ~ 10 at I = 8.7×10¹⁵ W/cm². The 2ω₀ bandwidth (FWHM) broadened with increasing
intensity for STUD pulses, consistent with a larger EPW amplitude distribution, whereas Gaussian
pulses showed narrower, intensity-saturated bandwidths. Intensity scaling for 2 ps STUD pulses
followed I² across the full measured range; for 3 ps STUD pulses, I² scaling was maintained while
the 3 ps Gaussian emulator exhibited I³·⁶ scaling — a clear signature of nonlinear saturation and
cascaded EPW collapse.

X-ray and Hot-Electron Corroboration:

 6

X-ray diode signals (Al-filtered bremsstrahlung) showed trends that tracked the 2ω₀ results as a
function of duty cycle and overall pulse duration, providing independent corroboration that STUD
pulses suppress large-amplitude EPW disruption and the associated energetic-electron
generation. Hot-electron spectrometer measurements were consistent with reduced suprathermal
electron yield under STUD-pulse irradiation.

SRS and Two-Plasmon Decay — ω₀/2 and 3ω₀/2 Signatures:
Both ω₀/2 and 3ω₀/2 signals scaled exponentially with intensity for STUD pulses (2 ps and 3 ps),
consistent with linear (exponential-growth) SRS/TPD behavior. The 3 ps Gaussian emulator pulse
exhibited a markedly lower growth rate for 3ω₀/2, indicative of saturation. Consequently, STUD
pulses reduced ω₀/2 and 3ω₀/2 backscatter by up to 80–87% relative to equivalent-energy
continuous pulses. The self-consistent subset of the data showed that the same shot days
producing rational RA scaling also produced rational SRS/TPD scaling, confirming that the two
instability channels respond coherently to STUD pulse control.

3.3 Analysis
The physical interpretation is clear: short, well-separated spikes interrupt highly nonlinear self-
organized EPW growth (which would otherwise lead to wave-breaking, particle trapping,
cascaded mode coupling). Each spike drives a fresh linear-kinetic-regime EPW. The quadratic I²
scaling of 2ω₀ reflects the product of two driven plasma waves at nₙ combining to directly emit
radiation at 2ω₀ — ie, the expected signature when EPWs remain below wave-breaking and
complicated interplay with trapping physics. When spikes are long or heavily overlapping (high
fᴰᶜ), plasma memory builds across spikes, allowing nonlinear self-organization to proceed and
saturate the 2ω₀ signal (with super-quadratic and erratic scaling) while also driving strong
SRS/TPD growth and strong bursty nonlinear saturation.

The simultaneous suppression of nₙ/4 instabilities demonstrates that taming RA at nₙ does not
excite quarter-critical instabilities — a concern for dual-region control — and that STUD pulses
decouple the two density regions' instability dynamics. This is a key validation of the STUD pulse
program for LaDrIFT target physics: it is feasible to engineer the pump laser's temporal structure
together with spatial scrambling of speckle profiles to control both instability classes as well as
simpler and tamer instabilities such as SBS and crossed-beam energy transfer in a wholistic
manner.

4. Challenges and Lessons Learned

4.1 Problems Encountered
 • Shot-to-shot reproducibility: Signal variability of up to an order of magnitude per shot
 condition was the primary experimental challenge. Root causes included: (a) heater-beam
 pointing drift over its >50 m air path; (b) target rastering tolerances causing focal-spot
 overlap changes between heater and interaction beams (sensitive due to their 25°
 included angle); (c) step-mirror array alignment drift (slow mirror relaxation, inability to

 7

 resolve individual spikes due to Rayleigh's criterion); (d) fiber-spectrometer coupling
 sensitivity between daily alignments; (e) target debris accumulation.
 • 1ω₀ specular reflection diagnostic failure: The fiber-optic spectrometer subtended only
 ~0.5 sr collection solid angle. The specularly reflected 800 nm signal varied significantly
 in magnitude within a single condition due to angular wander of the reflection, rendering
 the measurement unreliable. Calorimetric collection over a large solid angle would be
 required in future experiments.
 • Laser energy drift: Shot-to-shot standard deviation of ~5% in interaction beam energy;
 long-term drifts mitigated by continual 5th-amplifier correction, but heater-beam energy
 (drawn from a beam splitter after the 4th amplifier) drifted independently, complicating the
 plasma formation reproducibility.
 • Spectrometer saturation: At high intensities, the 2ω₀ signal occasionally saturated the UV
 spectrometer; saturated regions were replaced by least-squares Gaussian fits to adjacent
 spectral channels.

4.2 Changes / Improvements
 • Future experiments should implement calorimetric 1ω₀ reflectometry with integrating-
 sphere collection to capture the full angular spread of the secular reflection.
 • Active heater-beam pointing stabilization (fast-steering mirror with wavefront sensor
 feedback) would substantially reduce plasma formation variability.
 • A fiducial shot sequence (known-intensity reference shots interspersed throughout each
 run) would enable day-to-day cross-calibration of absolute intensities.
 • Real-time monitoring of individual mirror-segment delays via interferometry would
 improve STUD pulse fidelity and reduce alignment uncertainty.
 • More spikes per train (>10) and wider duty-cycle ranges are accessible with the
 improved pulse-train generation system described in Wang et al. (RSI 2026).

8. Impact

8.1 Impact on Primary Discipline
This work establishes, for the first time experimentally, that sub-picosecond temporal pulse
shaping can suppress LPI in both the critical-density and quarter-critical-density regimes
simultaneously. The quadratic I² scaling observed for STUD-pulse-driven RA, compared to super-
quadratic scaling for continuous pulses, provides a direct experimental test of linear-regime vs.
nonlinear-regime EPW physics. This result resolves a long-standing ambiguity in earlier
nanosecond-pulse RA experiments that reported ambiguous scaling exponents between 1 and
1.5 with poor reproducibility. The 87% reduction in SRS/TPD backscatter provides the broadest
simultaneous LPI suppression demonstrated to date.

8.2 Impact on Other Disciplines
The STUD pulse concept has implications beyond ICF: any laser-plasma application where
parametric instabilities limit coupling efficiency — laser-driven particle acceleration, X-ray
generation, laser-driven nuclear reactions — could benefit from analogous temporal pulse
shaping. The demonstrated pulse-train generation technique (Wang et al., RSI 2026) is broadly
applicable to any CPA laser system operating in the picosecond regime.

8.5 Technology Transfer and Future Work
The STUD pulse technique is directly relevant to future ICF/IFE driver designs. Near-term
extensions of this work include:
 • Scaling to larger spike counts (>10) and higher repetition rates using improved mirror
 array technology.
 • Extension to multi-beam geometries to address crossed-beam energy transfer (CBET)
 suppression — the second major goal of the STUD pulse program.
 • Integration of spatial beam modulation (speckle scrambling) synchronized with temporal
 STUD pulse modulation, addressing the full three-pronged LPI control strategy (STUD
 Pulse Program) proposed by the PI.
 • Benchmarking experimental results against kinetic (Vlasov) and fluid LPI codes to
 develop validated predictive models for ICF target design.

9. Overall Project Summary

This program delivered the first experimental proof-of-concept that STUD pulses — Spike Trains
of Uneven Duration and Delay — can control laser-plasma instabilities in high-energy-density
plasmas at both the critical and quarter-critical electron density. Using the ALEPH Ti:Sapphire
CPA laser at Colorado State University and a novel 10-element step-mirror pulse-train generator,
we irradiated laser-created Kapton plasmas with spike trains spanning tₒₙ = 50–200 fs, fᴰᶜ = 17–
100%, and I = 10¹⁵–3×10¹⁶ W/cm².

 12

The central findings are: (1) STUD pulses with short, low-duty-cycle spikes maintain resonance
absorption EPWs in the secular linear-growth regime, producing I² scaling of 2ω₀ emission and a
>3× enhancement in peak EPW amplitude relative to unmodulated pulses; (2) STUD pulses
simultaneously suppress SRS and TPD backscatter at nₙ/4 by up to 87%, keeping these
instabilities in exponential (linear-theory) growth while Gaussian-envelope pulses show saturated,
intensity-insensitive behavior; (3) the two density regions' instability dynamics remain decoupled
under STUD pulse irradiation, validating the theoretical prediction that STUD pulses can address
both instability classes without cross-excitation.

A companion RSI publication (Wang et al., 2026) documents the pulse-generation methodology,
enabling replication and extension of these experiments at other petawatt-class facilities.
Together, these results represent a decisive step toward the dynamic, time-domain control of LPI
for inertial fusion energy applications, opening a new design dimension — laser pulse temporal
structure — that complements existing bandwidth, smoothing, and wavelength-based mitigation
strategies.
