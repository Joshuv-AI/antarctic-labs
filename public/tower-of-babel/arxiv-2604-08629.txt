Thermodynamics and phase transitions of κ \kappa -deformed Schwarzschild-AdS black holes
A. Naveena Kumara, Vishnu Rajagopal, Puxun Wu

Abstract
We investigate the thermodynamics of the Schwarzschild-AdS black hole in the framework of κ \kappa -deformed non-commutative geometry by constructing an effective κ \kappa -deformed Schwarzschild-AdS metric from the κ \kappa -deformed Newtonian potential. In the extended phase space, we derive a modified first law and the corresponding Smarr relation by treating the κ \kappa -deformation parameter as an additional thermodynamic variable and identifying its conjugate potential. Our analysis shows that κ \kappa -deformation induces critical behaviour and phase transitions in an uncharged Schwarzschild-AdS black hole, with a critical ratio P c ​ v c / T c ≃ 0.370 P_{c}v_{c}/T_{c}\simeq 0.370 that is independent of the deformation parameter and close to the Van der Waals value. Interestingly, the G − T G-T curve exhibits a peculiar double-loop structure, deviating from the standard swallow-tail behaviour associated with a first-order phase transition.



I
Introduction

Black holes (BHs) are among the most profound predictions of general relativity, arising as solutions of Einstein’s field equations, and they provide an important arena for exploring the quantum nature of gravity. Interestingly, BHs have also been shown to behave as thermodynamic systems, with temperature and entropy directly related to the surface gravity and the area of the event horizon, respectively

;
33

This remarkable result later acquired further significance through the AdS/CFT correspondence, where it was interpreted as the confinement/deconfinement phase transition in the dual gauge theory
73
. Furthermore, in the extended phase space, the first law of BH thermodynamics and the corresponding Smarr relation can be consistently formulated by identifying the cosmological constant as thermodynamic pressure and its conjugate quantity as thermodynamic volume, while incorporating conserved charges such as electric charge and angular momentum. In this framework, the BH mass is interpreted as enthalpy rather than internal energy

;

Although Einstein gravity provides a robust description of gravitational phenomena, it is expected to break down in the strong-gravity regime, where quantum fluctuations become significant and necessitate the formulation of a quantum theory of gravity for a complete understanding of BHs. Many approaches have been developed to reconcile classical gravity with quantum theory and thereby shed light on the ultimate structure of quantum gravity. Among them, non-commutative (NC) spacetimes have emerged as an important framework for incorporating quantum gravitational effects, in which the quantum structure of spacetime is governed by an NC algebra that naturally introduces a fundamental length scale

II
\kappa
-deformed Schwarzschild-AdS black hole

In this study, we generalise the approach discussed in
61
;
62
into
\kappa
-deformed space-time and construct the
\kappa
-deformed Schwarzschild metric by using the notion of the
\kappa
-deformed Newton’s potential. Interestingly this newly obtained effective
\kappa
-deformed Schwarzschild black hole is spherically symmetric and static. We begin the discussion by considering the standard Schwarzschild metric,

ds^{2}=-\Big(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Big)dt^{2}+\Big(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Big)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(1)

For the Schwarzschild metric, the radial function
f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}
can be written in terms of the Newtonian potential
V(r)=-\frac{M}{r}
as
f(r)=1+2V(r)
. Therefore, any modification of the Newtonian potential directly affects the radial function
f(r)
and leads to an effective metric containing the leading correction terms associated with the modified potential. In
61
, an effective quantum-corrected Schwarzschild metric was obtained by using a quantum-corrected Newtonian potential. Following a similar strategy, we construct an effective
\kappa
-corrected Schwarzschild metric by employing the
\kappa
-deformed Newtonian potential obtained in
58
,

V(r)=-\frac{M}{r}\Big(1-\frac{aM}{12\pi r^{2}}\Big),

(2)

where the
\kappa
-deformation parameter
a
is defined in the natural units. Replacing the standard Newtonian potential with the
\kappa
-deformed potential in Eq.(
1
), we obtain the modified radial function

f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{aM^{2}}{12\pi r^{3}}.

Including the cosmological constant
\Lambda
, the effective
\kappa
-deformed Schwarzschild–AdS metric takes the form

ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+f(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(3)

where

f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{aM^{2}}{12\pi r^{3}}-\frac{\Lambda r^{2}}{3}.

(4)

Thus, the first-order
\kappa
-correction introduces a
1/r^{3}
term into the standard Schwarzschild metric function. Interestingly, such

term also appears in the regular-Bardeen BH solution,
f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{6M\beta^{2}}{2r^{3}}
(obtained by expanding the

around

\kappa
-deformed black hole thermodynamics

III.1
First law and Smarr relation

We study the thermodynamics of the black hole at the event horizon
r=r_{+}
. In the extended phase-space formulation of AdS BH thermodynamics, the pressure is defined in terms of the cosmological constant as
40

P=-\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi}.

(5)

The black hole mass, which is identified with the enthalpy in this framework, can be obtained from the horizon condition
f(r_{+})=0
, namely

f(r_{+})=1-\frac{2M}{r_{+}}+\frac{aM^{2}}{12\pi r_{+}^{3}}+\frac{8\pi Pr_{+}^{2}}{3}=0.

(6)

Solving Eq.(
6
) up to first order in
a
, we obtain the mass of the
\kappa
-deformed Schwarzschild–AdS black hole as

M=\frac{r_{+}}{2}\bigg(1+\frac{8\pi Pr_{+}^{2}}{3}\bigg)+\frac{a}{96\pi}\bigg(1+\frac{8\pi Pr_{+}^{2}}{3}\bigg)^{2}.

(7)

The Hawking temperature, obtained from the surface gravity through
T=\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{+})}{4\pi}
, is given by

T=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{+}}+2Pr_{+}-\frac{a}{96\pi^{2}r_{+}^{2}}\bigg(1+\frac{8\pi Pr_{+}^{2}}{3}\bigg)^{2}.

(8)

The entropy is assumed to follow the Bekenstein–Hawking area law,

S=\frac{A}{4}=\pi r_{+}^{2},

(9)

where
A
denotes the horizon area. For a consistent thermodynamic description, these quantities are expected to satisfy the standard first law,

dM=TdS+VdP.

However, using Eqs.(
7
)-(
9
), we find that

T\neq\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial S}\right)_{P},

which indicates that the conventional first law does not provide a consistent thermodynamic description for the
\kappa
-deformed BH.

The apparent inconsistency between the standard first law and the Bekenstein-Hawking area law can be resolved by introducing a modified first law, in which the modification factor depends on the explicit mass dependence of the energy-momentum tensor
48
. To obtain a consistent thermodynamic description for the
\kappa
-deformed BH, we follow the procedure outlined in
48
, where the modified first law is determined by the explicit form of
T^{0}_{0}(r,M)
. In addition, we treat the NC parameter
a
as an independent thermodynamic variable, with
\Phi_{a}
as its conjugate potential. The modified first law then takes the form

W\,dM=TdS+VdP+\Phi_{a}\,da,

(10)

where

W=1+4\pi\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}r^{2}\frac{\partial T^{0}_{0}}{\partial M}\,dr.

(11)

The component
T^{0}_{0}
of the energy-momentum tensor satisfies the Einstein equation

R^{\mu}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}R+\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\Lambda=8\pi T^{\mu}_{\nu}.

(12)

From this, one obtains

\frac{\partial(rf)}{\partial r}-1+r^{2}\Lambda=8\pi r^{2}T^{0}_{0}.

(13)

Differentiating Eq.(
13
) with respect to
M
and integrating the resulting expression from
r_{+}
to
\infty
, we obtain
71

\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}8\pi r^{2}\frac{\partial T^{0}_{0}}{\partial M}\,dr=\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial f}{\partial M}\right)dr\,.

Substituting the explicit form of
f(r)
from Eq.(
4
) into this relation and then using Eq.(
11
), we obtain

W=1-\frac{aM}{12\pi r_{+}^{2}}.

(14)

In the commutative limit,
W\to 1
, and the modified first law reduces to the standard form. From Eq.(
10
), the temperature is then defined as

T=W\bigg(\frac{\partial M}{\partial S}\bigg)_{P,a}.

(15)

Substituting Eqs.(
7
), (
9
), and (
14

15

8
). Similarly, the thermodynamic volume is obtained from the modified first law as

V=W\bigg(\frac{\partial M}{\partial P}\bigg)_{S,a}.

(16)

Using Eqs.(
7

14
) in Eq.(
16
), we find

V=\frac{4\pi r_{+}^{3}}{3}.

(17)

Similarly, Eq.(
10

\kappa
-deformation parameter
a
as

\Phi_{a}=W\bigg(\frac{\partial M}{\partial a}\bigg)_{S,P}.

(18)

Using Eqs.(

\Phi_{a}=\frac{M^{2}}{24\pi r_{+}^{2}}.

(19)

The Smarr relation for the BH can be derived through a scaling argument based on Euler’s theorem. For this purpose, we treat the BH mass as a function of the extended thermodynamic variables
(S,P,a)
, namely
M=M(S,P,a)
. Under the scaling transformation
r_{+}\to\lambda r_{+}
, the mass satisfies

\lambda^{w}M=M(\lambda^{x}S,\lambda^{y}P,\lambda^{z}a),

(20)

where

,
P
, and

From Eqs.(
6
) and (

Using the definitions of

V

\Phi_{a}
from Eqs.(
15

16

WM=2(TS-PV)+\Phi_{a}a.

(23)

Although the modified first law given in Eq.(

III.2
Critical points and phase transition

From the above expressions, the equation of state of the
\kappa
-deformed Schwarzschild-AdS BH can be written as

T=\frac{1}{2\pi v}+Pv-\frac{a}{24\pi^{2}v^{2}}\bigg(1+\frac{2\pi Pv^{2}}{3}\bigg)^{2},

(24)

where
v=2r_{+}
denotes the specific volume of the BH
40
. The critical behaviour of this
\kappa
-deformed Schwarzschild-AdS BH can be analyzed using the conditions

\bigg(\frac{\partial T}{\partial v}\bigg)_{a,P}=0,\qquad\bigg(\frac{\partial^{2}T}{\partial v^{2}}\bigg)_{a,P}=0,

which determine the inflection point of the equation of state. Solving these equations, we obtain the critical points

\begin{split}v_{c}&=\frac{a(2^{1/3}+1)}{9\pi},\\
P_{c}&=\frac{81\pi(2^{1/3}-1)}{2a^{2}},\\
T_{c}&=\frac{9(2^{-1/3}+2^{1/3}-1)}{(1+2^{1/3})^{2}a}.\end{split}

\kappa
-deformation parameter
a
itself induces criticality in the uncharged Schwarzschild-AdS BH. From Eq.(
25
), we observe that for a fixed value of
a
, there exists a unique critical point, and that all critical quantities remain finite only in the presence of non-commutativity.

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we have constructed an effective Schwarzschild-AdS BH solution in
\kappa
-deformed spacetime by incorporating the deformation through the
\kappa
-deformed Newtonian potential
58
. This approach introduces the leading-order
\kappa
-deformed corrections into the metric and provides a phenomenological framework for investigating BH thermodynamics in
\kappa
-deformed spacetime. Using this effective geometry, we carried out a detailed analysis of the thermodynamic properties of the
\kappa
-deformed Schwarzschild-AdS BH within the extended phase-space framework. Our results show that
\kappa
-deformed space-time noncommutativity significantly modifies the thermodynamic structure of the Schwarzschild-AdS BH and gives rise to new features, such as criticality and phase transitions, which are absent in the commutative limit.

At first sight, the obtained
\kappa
-deformed BH solution appears to violate the standard first law due to the leading-order
\kappa
-deformation correction. However, by employing the formulation based on the general structure of the energy-momentum tensor
48
, and by treating the NC parameter
a
as an independent thermodynamic variable with conjugate potential
\Phi_{a}
, we derived a modified first law containing a correction factor
W
that depends explicitly on the
\kappa
-deformation parameter. We also obtained a corresponding modified Smarr relation through a scaling argument, consistent with the corrected first law, while preserving the standard Bekenstein-Hawking entropy formula. Similar modifications of the first law and Smarr relation have also been reported in studies of BH thermodynamics in Moyal spacetime
70
;
69
;
72
and in other quantum gravity frameworks such as loop quantum gravity
71
.

We further showed that
\kappa
-deformed spacetime non-commutativity induces critical behaviour and the associated phase transition between small and large BHs, in contrast to the standard uncharged Schwarzschild-AdS BH, where such behaviour is absent. The critical ratio
P_{c}v_{c}/T_{c}\simeq 0.370
is found to be close to the Van der Waals value
0.375
and remains independent of the
\kappa
-deformation parameter, even though the critical pressure, temperature, and specific volume themselves depend explicitly on
a
. This universality points to a robust underlying thermodynamic structure associated with the
\kappa
-deformed NC correction.

The stability and phase structure of the
\kappa
-deformed Schwarzschild-AdS BH were analyzed through the equation of state, the isobaric heat capacity, and the Gibbs free energy. Our results show that a small-to-large BH phase transition occurs below the critical point. The isobaric heat capacity
C_{P}
diverges at the transition points, separating stable branches with
C_{P}>0
, corresponding to small and large BHs, from an unstable intermediate branch with
C_{P}<0
. Thus, in addition to inducing criticality,
\kappa
-deformed noncommutativity plays a central role in determining the existence of thermodynamic phase transitions and controlling the associated stability properties. Although the
T-r_{+}
and
C_{P}-r_{+}
behaviours are similar to those of standard charged AdS BHs, such as the RN-AdS BH, the
G
-
T
behaviour is qualitatively different. Instead of exhibiting the standard swallow-tail structure associated with a first-order phase transition, the Gibbs free energy develops a double-loop structure. We also identified a characteristic pressure
P_{*}
, with
P_{*}<P_{c}
, at which the double-loop structure degenerates into a single loop. As the pressure increases further beyond
P_{*}
, this single loop disappears, indicating a zeroth-order phase transition in the region
P_{*}<P<P_{c}
. The
G-T
behaviour observed here closely resembles that of the regular Bardeen-AdS BH, as recently reported in
47
. In addition, the structural resemblance between the
\kappa
-corrected Schwarzschild metric and the regular Bardeen metric suggests that
\kappa
-deformed spacetime non-commutativity may serve as a viable phenomenological framework for addressing BH singularity resolution.

The emergence of similar minimal-length-scale-induced BH phase transitions has also been observed in the context of Moyal spacetime
70
;
69
;
72
and loop quantum gravity
71
. In particular, uncharged BHs modified by minimal length scale corrections can exhibit criticality and phase transitions analogous to those of charged BHs in the standard commutative setting. These results highlight how features arising from quantum gravity inspired frameworks, such as loop quantum gravity and space-time non-commutativity, can manifest themselves in the semiclassical thermodynamic behaviour of BHs. A natural next step would be to extend the present analysis using the Euclidean action approach in
\kappa
-deformed space-time. Such an extension would provide a more complete understanding of BH thermodynamics in the
\kappa
-deformed framework and may also help clarify its holographic interpretation within the AdS/CFT correspondence.
