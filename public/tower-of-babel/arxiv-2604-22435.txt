On formality of diagrams of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces
Grigory Solomadin, Antoine Touzé

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish formality (over ℚ \mathbb{Q} ) for diagrams of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces of any height n ⩾ 1 n\geqslant 1 . This implies spectral sequence (over ℚ \mathbb{Q} ) collapse at page 2 2 for any diagram of EML spaces over any small category. We prove by functor calculus argument that formality does not hold over any fixed commutative ring 𝐤 \mathbf{k} not containing ℚ \mathbb{Q} , where the category of diagrams is over the category generated by finite direct sums of a cyclic group.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
55P62, 55U10, 18F50

1.
Introduction

A space
X
is called
formal
over
\mathbf{k}
if its singular chain complex
C_{*}(X;\mathbf{k})
with coefficients in
\mathbf{k}
is connected by a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms to its singular homology
H_{*}(X;\mathbf{k})
(considered as a complex with zero differential).
There are many variants of formality: one can consider singular chains or cochains, endow them with various additional structures (products, coproducts,…) and ask for a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms preserving the additional structures.
More generally, one can consider
formality of diagrams
. Namely, given a diagram of formal spaces, one can ask furthermore if the zig-zags of quasi-isomorphisms connecting the singular (co)chains of each object of the diagram to its (co)homology can be chosen in a way that is compatible with the maps of the diagram. If so, then one says that the diagram is formal (see definition
2.5
for a more precise formulation).

While formal spaces are not rare, formal diagrams of spaces are much more exceptional. For example, in rational homotopy theory, it is known that spheres, Kähler manifolds, Eilenberg-MacLane spaces, and many others are formal
[
FHT
]
. On the contrary, every map can be interpreted as a small diagram with one arrow and two vertices. By inspecting the Sullivan models, one can then see that the Hopf fibration
S^{3}\to S^{2}
is not formal. More generally, it is known that a homologically trivial formal map must be nullhomotopic
[
le-si-81
, Lemme 3.9]
.

In this article, we study the formality of diagrams of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces (EML spaces, for short). Every Eilenberg-MacLane space can be given the structure of a strict abelian group, and every diagram can be rigidified into a diagram in which the arrows preserve this abelian group structure (see proposition
2.4
). This abelian group structure on spaces induces a commutative differential graded Hopf algebra structure on the level of singular chains, therefore we can ask about formality in this context.
In the present note, we establish formality for any diagram of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces with fixed height
n\geqslant 1
over any
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\mathbf{k}
(see theorem
3.1
).

Theorem 1
.

For any
n\geqslant 1
let
D\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
be any diagram of EML spaces of height
n
.
Let
\mathbf{k}
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.
Then the diagram
c\mapsto C_{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
is formal in the category of augmented commutative differential graded
\mathbf{k}
-algebras if
n=1
, and it is formal in the category of commutative differential graded Hopf
\mathbf{k}
-algebras if
n\geqslant 2
.

The proof of formality relies on simplicial models for EML spaces using iterated bar
\overline{B}
and
\overline{W}
constructions
[
gu-ma-74
]
. If
n=1
or
2
, we actually extend theorem
1
to more general rings
\mathbf{k}
in theorem
3.2
, provided the homotopy groups of the EML-spaces of the diagram are “not too large with respect to
\mathbf{k}
”. For example, formality holds with coefficients
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}[1/k!]
, if one restricts to diagrams of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces with
\pi_{n}
of
\mathbf{k}
-dimension
\leqslant k
, see example
3.3
.

The formality results given in theorems
3.1
and
3.2
seem to be folklore albeit not published in this generality to the knowledge of the authors (the case of tori was addressed in
[
fr-21
]
, and the integral formality of certain diagrams of EML spaces was addressed in
[
no-ra-05
]
).

We review an application of formality to the study of homotopy colimits in section
5
. Namely, for all diagrams of spaces
D
with shape
\mathbf{C}
there are two first quadrant spectral sequences with the second pages given by (co)homology of the category
\mathbf{C}
with coefficients in the singular (co)homology of
D
, and converging to the singular (co)homology of the homotopy colimit of
D
:

E^{2}_{p,q}(D)=H_{p}(\mathbf{C};H_{q}(D;\mathbf{k}))\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k}),

E_{2}^{p,q}(D)=H^{p}(\mathbf{C};H^{q}(D;\mathbf{k}))\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k}).

As a consequence of theorem
1
, we prove the following result (and actually a bit more, by providing an explicit model for the chains of the homotopy colimit) in theorem
5.8
.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Q}
. Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
D
be a diagram of pointed spaces. If every space
D(c)
of the diagram is a nilpotent space with an abelian fundamental group, such that
\pi_{k}(D(c))\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
for
k\neq n
, then both spectral sequences collapse at the second page.

However, general formality statements are quite limited outside the cases singled out in theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.
Indeed, compact tori are EML spaces of height
1
, and computations of toric varieties already show that diagrams of tori may not be formal over
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}
. Namely, assuming natural formality of diagrams of tori over
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, one deduces the algebra isomorphism
[
fr-06
]

H^{*}(X_{\Sigma};\mathbf{k})\cong\Tor_{H^{*}(BT;\mathbf{k})}(\mathbf{k}[\Sigma],\mathbf{k}),

for the toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
with the
T
-action corresponding to any smooth rational polyhedral fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
On the right hand side, the product is defined canonically via Koszul resolution.
However, the products are in general different by
[
fr-21
, Example 1.1]
. Thus the singular cochains of toric diagrams cannot be formal as commutative differential graded algebras over neither of
\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
We remark that this argument does not forbid formality over
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
(since the products then coincide by
[
fr-21
, Theorem 1.2]
), for which one can use theorem
3
below instead.

In section
4
we give another obstruction to formality relying on some
\Ext
-computations in functor categories. We show that formality of singular chains may fail for some diagrams of EML spaces as soon as
\mathbf{k}
is not a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, whatever the height of EML spaces of the diagram is. To be more specific, if
C
is a cyclic group, we denote by
\langle C\rangle
the full subcategory of abelian groups containing all the finite direct sums of copies of
C
. We prove the following result in theorem
4.3
.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
\mathbf{k}
is not a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, and let
C
be a cyclic group such that
C\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\neq 0
. Assume that
D
is a diagram of EML spaces of height
n\geqslant 1
such that
\pi_{n}D(G)
is naturally isomorphic to
G
. Then the diagram
c\mapsto C_{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
is not formal in the category of chain complexes of
\mathbf{k}
-modules.

Using models for singular chains given by iterated bar constructions
\overline{B}
, we reduce the proof of theorem
3
to non-formality of the complex
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[G]
as a functor of the abelian group
G
. In order to prove this, we show that the total ranks (with respect to the usual bigrading) of the groups

(1)

\Ext_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-],-\otimes\mathbb{F}),\ \Ext_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(H_{*}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]),-\otimes\mathbb{F}),

are different in the functor category
\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})
of functors from
\langle C\rangle
to
\mathbf{k}
-modules.
Here,
\mathbb{F}
denotes any quotient field,
\chr\mathbb{F}=p>0
, of
\mathbf{k}
such that
C\otimes\mathbb{F}\simeq\mathbb{F}
holds.
Namely, we prove that the first group in (
1
) is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}
, whereas the second group in (
1
) has rank
>1
. (Formality property would imply that both total ext groups are isomorphic, leading to a contradiction.)

In conclusion, let us compare our work with some recent results obtained by Zakharov. In particular,
[
za-25
, Theorem 1.1.2]
implies that the singular chain functors
A\mapsto C_{*}(K(A,n);\mathbf{k})
are isomorphic to the Dold-Puppe derived functors
A\mapsto L_{*}S(A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k},n)
, and the latter are known to be formal under the hypotheses of our theorems
3.1
and theorem
3.2
. However all these isomorphisms are isomorphisms in the category
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{Ab},D(\mathbf{k}))
of functors from abelian groups to the derived category of
\mathbf{k}
-modules. On the contrary, all our results can be reformulated as statements in the derived category
D(\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{Ab},\mathbf{k}))
of functors from abelian groups to
\mathbf{k}
-modules, which is more rigid, and which is the correct category to consider if one wants to obtain collapsing of spectral sequences as in theorem
2
.

2.
Definitions and basic facts on formality

2.1.
Eilenberg-MacLane spaces

Let
n
be a positive integer.
An
Eilenberg-MacLane space of height
n
(or
EML space of height
n
, for short) is a pointed simplicial set
E
such that
\pi_{k}E=0
for
k\neq n
. An
abelian Eilenberg-MacLane space of height
n
(or abelian EML space of height
n
for short) is a simplicial abelian group
E
such that
\pi_{k}E=0
for
k\neq n
.

For all pointed simplicial sets
X
we denote by
\mathbb{Z}[X]
the free simplicial abelian group on
X
and we let
X^{(n)}=\mathrm{cosk}_{n+1}\mathbb{Z}[X]/\mathbb{Z}[*]
, where
\mathrm{cosk}_{n+1}
refers to the
(n+1)
-st coskeleton of a simplicial set
[
dk-84
]
. We observe that coskeletons of simplicial abelian groups are simplicial abelian groups, hence this construction actually defines a functor

-^{(n)}:\mathbf{sSet}_{*}\to\mathbf{sAb}
.
Let
\alpha_{X}^{n}\colon X\to X^{(n)}
denote the following composition of natural morphisms of simplicial sets (we abuse notations and write
X^{(n)}
both for the simplicial abelian group and its underlying simplicial set)

X\xrightarrow{h}\mathbb{Z}[X]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[X]/\mathbb{Z}[*]\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{cosk}_{n+1}\mathbb{Z}[X]/\mathbb{Z}[*]=X^{(n)},

where
h
is the Hurewicz morphism
[
we-97
, Chapter 8, Application 8.3.9]
. The next lemma allows to translate questions about EML spaces into questions about abelian EML spaces.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
E
is an EML space of height
n
, then
\alpha_{E}^{n}
is a weak homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

By construction,
\pi_{k}E^{(n)}=0
if
k\neq n
. Therefore it suffices to check that
\pi_{n}(\alpha_{E}^{n})
is an isomorphism, which follows from the Hurewicz theorem.
∎

Let
K:\mathbf{Ch}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbf{sAb}
denote the Dold-Kan functor
[
we-97
, Chapter 8, §8.4]
. For all abelian groups
A
, we let
A[n]
be the chain complex equal to
A
in degree
n
and to zero in degrees different from
n
. Then the simplicial abelian group
K(A[n])
is an abelian EML space with
\pi_{n}(A)\simeq A
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Every abelian EML space
E
of height
n
is connected to
K(\pi_{n}E[n])
by a zig-zag of weak equivalences of simplicial abelian groups, which are natural with respect to all morphisms of the simplicial abelian group
E
.

Proof.

Using the Dold-Kan equivalence the lemma is equivalent to the well-known fact that every chain complex of abelian groups
C
such that
H_{k}(C)=0
for
k\neq n
is connected to the chain complex
H_{n}(C)[n]
by a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms natural with respect to
C
.
∎

We can apply the previous lemmas to prove a rigidification property for diagrams of EML spaces.
We first need to introduce a definition. We use the term diagram as a synonym of functor.

Definition 2.3
.

A diagram
D:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
is called a
diagram of EML spaces (of height
n
)

if
D(c)
is an EML space of height
n
for all
c
.
The diagram
D
is called a
diagram of abelian EML spaces (of height
n
)
if it factors as

D\colon\mathbf{C}\xrightarrow{}\mathbf{sAb}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{forget}}\mathbf{sSet}_{*}.

Now applying lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
, we immediately obtain the following rigidification property. This property justifies that we only consider diagrams of abelian EML spaces in the remainder of section, as well as in section
3
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
D:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
be a diagram of EML spaces of height
n\geqslant 1
. Let
D^{\prime}:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
denote the diagram of abelian EML spaces given by
D^{\prime}(c)=K(\pi_{n}D(c)[n])
. Then
D
is connected to
D^{\prime}
by a zigzag of natural transformations which are objectwise weak equivalences of simplicial sets.

2.2.
Formality

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a commutative ring, and let
\mathbf{D}
denote one of the following categories:

(1)

the category

\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
of complexes of
\mathbf{k}
-modules,

(2)

the category
\mathrm{ACDGAlg}_{\mathbf{k}}
of augmented graded commutative differential graded
\mathbf{k}
-algebras,

(3)

the category
\mathrm{CDGHopf}_{\mathbf{k}}
of graded commutative differential graded Hopf
\mathbf{k}
-algebras.

A
quasi-isomorphism
in
\mathbf{D}
is a morphism which induces an isomorphism in homology.
If
M
is an object of
\mathbf{D}
, then we consider its homology
H_{*}(M)
as an object of
\mathbf{D}
with trivial differential. When doing this for Hopf algebras, we implicitly assume a flatness assumption on
H_{*}(M)
to ensure that
H_{*}(M^{\otimes 2})
is isomorphic to
H_{*}(M)^{\otimes 2}
, hence to ensure that the coproduct on
M
induces a coproduct on the homology.

Definition 2.5
.

A diagram
D:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{D}
is
formal

if there is a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms in
\mathbf{D}
, natural with respect to
c
in
\mathbf{C}
, between
D(c)
and
H_{*}(D(c))
.

The previous definition can be applied to diagrams of simplicial sets. To be more specific, for all commutative rings
\mathbf{k}
, we denote by
\mathbf{k}[X]
the free simplicial
\mathbf{k}
-module on
X
and by
C_{*}(X;\mathbf{k})=C_{\mathrm{norm}}\mathbf{k}[X]
the associated normalized complex of
\mathbf{k}
-modules, that is the complex of normalized singular chains of
X
. The homology of this complex is the singular homology of
X
, and we denote it by
H_{*}(X;\mathbf{k})
.
When
X
is a simplicial abelian group, the addition
X\times X\to X
and the Eilenberg-Zilber shuffle map
make
C_{*}(X;\mathbf{k})
into a graded commutative differential
\mathbf{k}
-algebra with unit induced by the inclusion of the constant simplicial abelian group
0
into
X
. Moreover, the diagonal map
X\to X\times X
and the Alexander-Whitney map
induce a coproduct on
C_{*}(X,\mathbf{k})
with counit induced by the unique morphism of simplicial groups
X\to 0
. Together with the previous product, it endows
C_{*}(X;\mathbf{k})
with the structure of a graded commutative differential graded Hopf algebra over
\mathbf{k}

[
gu-ma-74
, Corollary A.7]
.

Thus, given a diagram
D:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
, we can interpret the assignment
c\mapsto C_{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
as a functor from
\mathbf{C}
to
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
, or to
\mathrm{ACDGAlg}_{\mathbf{k}}
or to
\mathrm{CDGHopf}_{\mathbf{k}}
if the functor
D
happens to factor through the category of simplicial abelian groups.

Definition 2.6
.

A diagram
D:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
is
formal in the category
\mathbf{D}
if there exists a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of
\mathbf{D}
, natural with respect to
c
in
\mathbf{C}
, between
C_{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
and
H_{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
.

Remark 2.7
.

In this article, we work with diagrams of pointed simplicial sets. One can alternatively consider pointed topological spaces, and replace
\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
by
\mathbf{Top}_{*}
in the previous definition. But then a diagram
D\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{Top}_{*}
is formal if and only if the diagram
\mathrm{Sing}\circ D\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
is formal. Therefore all the results of the article apply to diagrams of pointed topological spaces as well.

2.3.
Models of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces via bar constructions

Our favorite model for EML spaces will not be the
K(A[n])
appearing in proposition
2.4
, but rather iterated bar constructions, which allow inductive arguments. We will take
[
gu-ma-74
]
as a reference for bar constructions.
We denote by
\overline{B}A
the
reduced normalized bar construction
of a graded commutative dg
\mathbf{k}
-algebra
A
with augmentation
\epsilon\colon A\to\mathbf{k}
. Some explicit formulas for
\overline{B}
will be recalled later, in sections
3
and
4
. The reduced normalized bar construction yields a functor

\overline{B}\colon\mathrm{ACDGAlg}_{\mathbf{k}}\to\mathrm{CDGHopf}_{\mathbf{k}}

and since Hopf algebras are augmented algebras, this functor can be iterated.
If
G
is an abelian group, we may consider the group
\mathbf{k}
-algebra
\mathbf{k}[G]
as a graded commutative dg Hopf
\mathbf{k}
-algebra concentrated in degree zero, and apply
n
times the reduced bar construction, to obtain a graded commutative dg Hopf
\mathbf{k}
-algebra
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[G]
. The relevance of bar constructions for our formality problems comes from the following proposition and its corollary.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a commutative ring. For all abelian EML spaces
E
of height
n
,
C_{*}(E;\mathbf{k})
is connected to
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[\pi_{n}E]
by a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of graded commutative dg-
\mathbf{k}
-Hopf algebras, natural with respect to the abelian EML space
E
.

Proof.

The result is essentially proved in
[
gu-ma-74
, Appendix A]
. Namely, if
G
is an abelian group, viewed as a constant simplicial object, the iterated
\overline{W}
-construction yields an abelian EML space
\overline{W}^{n}G
of height
n
with
n
-th homotopy group naturally isomorphic to
G
by
[
gu-ma-74
, Proposition A.21]
. Then lemma
2.2
yields a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of Hopf algebras between
C_{*}(E;\mathbf{k})
and
C_{*}(\overline{W}^{n}\pi_{n}E;\mathbf{k})
, with
C_{*}(K(\pi_{n}E[n]);\mathbf{k})
in the middle of the zigzag.
Thus, it suffices to show that there is a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of Hopf algebras between
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[\pi_{n}E]
and
C_{*}(\overline{W}^{n}\pi_{n}E;\mathbf{k})
, natural with respect to
\pi_{n}E
.

Write
\pi:=\pi_{n}E
for short. There is an isomorphism
\overline{B}\mathbf{k}[\pi]\simeq C_{*}(\overline{W}\pi;\mathbf{k})
natural with respect to
\pi
by
[
gu-ma-74
, Lemma A.15]
, which finishes the proof
for
n=1
. We prove the general case by induction on
n
. Assume that the results holds for
n-1
, hence there is a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of Hopf algebras between
\overline{B}^{n-1}\mathbf{k}[\pi]
and
C_{*}(\overline{W}^{n-1}\pi;\mathbf{k})
, natural with respect to
\pi
. The bar construction
\overline{B}
preserves quasi-isomorphisms of Hopf algebras, hence by applying it to the latter zig-zag we obtain a new zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of Hopf algebras between
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[\pi]
and
\overline{B}C_{*}(\overline{W}^{n-1}\pi;\mathbf{k})
. Thus, to finish the proof, it suffices to construct a quasi-isomorphism of Hopf algebras

\overline{\lambda}\colon\overline{B}C_{*}(\overline{W}^{n-1}\pi;\mathbf{k})\to C_{*}(\overline{W}^{n}\pi;\mathbf{k})\;,

natural with respect to
\pi
.
Such a quasi-isomorphism is provided by
[
gu-ma-74
, Theorem A.20]
by substituting the simplicial Hopf
\mathbf{k}
-algebra
\mathbf{k}[\overline{W}^{n-1}\pi]
into
A
, in the reference’s notation. This finishes the proof for all
n
.

Note that
[
gu-ma-74
, Theorem A.20]
does not explicitly assert that
\overline{\lambda}
is natural with respect to
\pi
, but it is actually the case, and we will now briefly explain why. Let us change the notations and let us denote now by
\overline{W}A
the normalized chains of the
\overline{W}
construction of a simplicial augmented algebra
A
as in
[
gu-ma-74
, Definition A.14]
. Let
BC_{\mathrm{norm}}A
and
WA
denote the unreduced versions of
\overline{B}C_{\mathrm{norm}}A
and
\overline{W}A
. Thus
\overline{B}C_{\mathrm{norm}}A
and
\overline{W}A
are graded
\mathbf{k}
-submodules of
BC_{\mathrm{norm}}A
and
WA
, and the latter are actually free right
C_{\mathrm{norm}}A
-modules over the former, and also augmented over
\mathbf{k}
. The statement of
[
gu-ma-74
, Theorem A.20]
asserts that there is a unique morphism of augmented right
C_{\mathrm{norm}}A
-modules

\lambda:BC_{\mathrm{norm}}A\to WA

which preserves the reduced submodules, and
\overline{\lambda}
is induced by
\lambda
. In order to justify that
\overline{\lambda}
is natural with respect to
A
, it suffices to prove that
\lambda
is natural with respect to
A
. Let
s
denote the contracting homotopy of
WA
and let
d
denote the differential of
BC_{\mathrm{norm}}A
. Given a morphism of simplicial commutative Hopf algebras
f:A_{1}\to A_{2}
, there is at most one morphism of augmented right
C_{\mathrm{norm}}A_{1}
-modules
\lambda_{12}:BC_{\mathrm{norm}}A_{1}\to WA_{2}
whose restriction to
\overline{B}C_{\mathrm{norm}}A
satisfies
s\lambda_{12}d=\lambda_{12}
. Now by the construction of
\lambda
given in the first line of the proof of
[
gu-ma-74
, Theorem A.20]
the compositions
(Wf)\circ\lambda
and

\lambda\circ BC_{\mathrm{norm}}f
both satisfy these conditions, hence they are equal, which proves the naturality of
\lambda
.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\mathbf{D}=\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
,
\mathrm{ACDGAlg}_{\mathbf{k}}
or
\mathrm{CDGHopf}_{\mathbf{k}}
.
A diagram of abelian EML spaces
D\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
of height
n\geqslant 1
is formal in
\mathbf{D}
is and only if the following functor is formal:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbf{C}&\to&\mathbf{D}\\
c&\mapsto&\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[\pi_{n}D(c)]\end{array}\;.

3.
Formality results for diagrams of EML spaces

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
D\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
be a diagram of abelian EML spaces of a fixed height
n\geqslant 1
. Let
\mathbf{k}
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.
Then
D
is formal in
\mathrm{ACDGAlg}_{\mathbf{k}}
if
n=1
, and it is formal in
\mathrm{CDGHopf}_{\mathbf{k}}
if
n\geqslant 2
.

For low values of
n
, one can relax the hypothesis that
\mathbf{k}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. To be more specific, if
\mathbf{k}
is an arbitrary commutative ring, we say that an abelian group
G
is
\mathbf{k}
-torsion free
if
\Tor_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbf{k},G)=0
, or equivalently if the order of every torsion element
g\in G
is invertible in
\mathbf{k}
. We denote by
\Lambda_{\mathbf{k}}(V)
the exterior algebra of a
\mathbf{k}
-module
V
, and we define the
\mathbf{k}
-dimension of
G
by:

\dim(G,\mathbf{k}):=\sup\{d\;|\;\Lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{d}(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k})\neq 0\}=\sup\{d\;|\;\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\neq 0\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}\;.

Then we have the following enhancement of theorem
3.1
for
n\leqslant 2
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

be a diagram of abelian EML spaces of height

. Assume that for all objects

of the indexing category the next two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

the abelian group
\pi_{n}D(c)
is
\mathbf{k}
-torsion free,

(2)

every positive integer less or equal to

.

Then

is formal in

if

, and it is formal in
\mathrm{CDGHopf}_{\mathbf{k}}
if

.

.

If
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}[1/k!]

\mathbf{k}
-torsion free. In this case, theorem shows that every diagram
D:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
of abelian EML spaces of height

such that

for all

if

By corollary
2.9
, in order to prove theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, we can restrict our attention to formality of reduced bar constructions. So we start by recalling some explicit formulas for reduced bar constructions, following
[
gu-ma-74
]
.

3.1.
Some explicit formulas for bar constructions

We first recall the
un-normalized reduced bar construction

\overline{B}_{u}A
of an augmented graded commutative dg-algebra
A
. Let
A[1]
denote the graded
\mathbf{k}
-module such that
A[1]_{i+1}=A_{i}
. As a graded
\mathbf{k}
-module

\overline{B}_{u}A=\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}A[1]^{\otimes p}\;.

Given homogeneous elements
x_{i}\in A
, we denote by
[x_{1}|\cdots|x_{p}]
the tensor product
x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{p}
viewed as an element of
A[1]^{\otimes p}
. Thus,
[x_{1}|\dots|x_{p}]
has degree
p+\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant p}\deg x_{i}
. For all
i
, we set
\sigma_{i}=\deg x_{1}+\dots+\deg x_{i}
.
Then the product of
\overline{B}_{u}A
is the
shuffle product

*
defined by

[x_{1}|\cdots|x_{p}]*[x_{p+1}|\cdots|x_{p+q}]=\sum_{t\in\Sigma_{p,q}}(-1)^{q\sigma_{p}}\epsilon_{t}\epsilon^{\prime}_{t}[x_{t^{-1}(1)}|\cdots|x_{t^{-1}(p+q)}]

where
\Sigma_{p,q}
denotes the set of
(p,q)
-shuffles,
\epsilon_{t}
is the signature of a shuffle,
\epsilon^{\prime}_{t}
is the sign such that
x_{1}\cdots x_{p+q}
is equal to
\epsilon^{\prime}_{t}x_{t^{-1}(1)}\cdots x_{t^{-1}(p+q)}
in the free graded commutative algebra on
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. The unit is the element
[\;]=1\in\mathbf{k}=A[1]^{\otimes 0}
. The augmentation
\epsilon
is uniquely determined by
\epsilon(1)=1
. The coproduct
\Delta
is the
deconcatenation coproduct
defined by

\Delta[x_{1}|\cdots|x_{p}]=\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant p}(-1)^{(p-i)\sigma_{i}}[x_{1}|\cdots|x_{i}]\otimes[x_{i+1}|\cdots|x_{p}]\;.

Finally, the differential is defined by:

\displaystyle d[x_{1}|\cdots|x_{p}]=

\displaystyle\epsilon(x_{1})[x_{2}|\cdots|x_{p}]+(-1)^{p}\epsilon(x_{p})[x_{1}|\cdots|x_{p-1}]+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{i}[x_{1}|\cdots|x_{i}x_{i+1}|\cdots|x_{p}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{p+\sigma_{i-1}}[x_{1}|\cdots|dx_{i}|\cdots|x_{p}].

Remark 3.4
.

Our signs follow the conventions of
[
gu-ma-74
]
, which differ from the usual sign conventions, found for example in
[
ml-63
]
, but which coincide with them if
A
is concentrated in even degrees.

The
reduced normalized bar construction

\overline{B}A
is the quotient of
\overline{B}_{u}A
by the graded
\mathbf{k}
-submodule generated by the elements
[x_{1}|\cdots|x_{p}]
in which at least one of the
x_{i}
is equal to
1_{A}
. The quotient
\overline{B}_{u}A\to\overline{B}A
is a quasi-isomorphism of graded commutative dg-Hopf
\mathbf{k}
-algebras.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\overline{A}=\ker\epsilon
be the augmentation ideal of
A
.
Then
\overline{B}^{\prime}A=\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}\overline{A}[1]^{\otimes p}
is actually a sub-dg Hopf algebra of
\overline{B}_{u}A
. Restriction to
\overline{B}^{\prime}A
of the quotient
\overline{B}_{u}A\to\overline{B}A
yields a natural isomorphism
\overline{B}^{\prime}A\simeq\overline{B}A
, hence the inclusion
\overline{B}^{\prime}A\hookrightarrow\overline{B}_{u}A
is a quasi-isomorphism.
In particular, we can work indifferently with
\overline{B}^{\prime}A
,
\overline{B}A
or
\overline{B}_{u}A
to study formality.

Next we recall the explicit computation of the homology of reduced bar constructions of symmetric and exterior algebras.
Let
V
be a flat
\mathbf{k}
-module.
Let
S(V[2i])
and
\Lambda(V[2i+1])
denote the symmetric and exterior algebras on
V
, where
V
is viewed as a graded module concentrated in degree
2i
and
2i+1
respectively (to lighten the notation, we drop the index
\mathbf{k}
on
\Lambda
and
S
).
The canonical inclusion
\Lambda^{p}(V[2i+1])\subset V[2i+1]^{\otimes p}\subset S(V[2i])[1]^{\otimes p}
yields an inclusion of dg-Hopf algebras

i_{\Lambda}:\Lambda(V[2i+1])\to\overline{B}S(V[2i])\;.

Similarly, let
\Gamma(V[2i+2])
be the free divided power
\mathbf{k}
-algebra on
V
viewed as a graded module concentrated in degree
2i+2
. If
\mathbf{k}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, then
\Gamma(V[2i+2])
is isomorphic to
S(V[2i+2])
as a graded Hopf algebra. In general,
\Gamma(V[2i+2])
is the Hopf subalgebra of the shuffle tensor algebra on
V[2i+2]
which is equal in degree
p(2i+2)
to the subspace
\Gamma^{p}(V)
of the invariants of
V^{\otimes p}
under the action of
\Sigma_{p}
. Multiplying by
(-1)^{p}
the canonical inclusion
\Gamma^{p}(V[2i+2])\subset V[2i+2]^{\otimes p}\subset\Lambda(V[2i+1])[1]^{\otimes p}
we obtain an inclusion of dg-Hopf algebras:

i_{\Gamma}:\Gamma(V[2i+2])\to\overline{B}\Lambda(V[2i+1])\;.

Lemma 3.6

For all nonnegative integers
i
and for all flat
\mathbf{k}

3.2.
Proof of theorems
3.1
and
3.2

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a commutative ring. By corollary
2.9
, in order to establish theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, it suffices to show that the iterated bar construction
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[G]
viewed as a functor of the abelian group
G
, is formal.
This will be done in propositions
3.7
,
3.11
and
3.12
below.

For all abelian groups
G
, let
G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[1]
denote a copy of the
\mathbf{k}
-module
G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
placed in homological degree
1
. For all
\mathbf{k}
-torsion free abelian groups, there is a natural isomorphism of graded
\mathbf{k}
-algebras
[
br-94
, V Theorem 6.4 (ii)]
(to lighten notations, we drop the index
\mathbf{k}
on exterior algebras):

\psi:\Lambda(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[1])\xrightarrow{\simeq}H_{*}(\overline{B}\mathbf{k}[G])\;.

For all
d\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
we denote by

\mathbf{Ab}_{\leqslant d,\mathbf{k}}
the full subcategory of abelian groups whose objects are the abelian groups
G
satisfying
\Tor_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbf{k},G)=0
and
\dim(G,\mathbf{k})\leqslant d
. In particular if
\mathbf{k}
is a flat
\mathbb{Z}
-module then
\mathbf{Ab}_{\leqslant+\infty,\mathbf{k}}=\mathbf{Ab}
. The following proposition proves the case
n=1
of theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume that all the positive integers less or equal to
d
are invertible in
\mathbf{k}
.
Consider
\Lambda(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[1])
as an augmented dg
\mathbf{k}
-algebra with zero differential.
Then for all
G
in
\mathbf{Ab}_{\leqslant d,\mathbf{k}}
there is a quasi-isomorphism
e:\overline{B}\mathbf{k}[G]\to\Lambda(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[1])
in
\mathrm{ACDGAlg}_{\mathbf{k}}
, natural with respect to
G
.

Proof.

By remark
3.5
it suffices to construct a quasi-isomorphism
e:\overline{B}_{u}\mathbf{k}[G]\to\Lambda(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[1])
.
Let
b_{x}
,
x\in X
, denote the canonical basis elements of the free
\mathbf{k}
-module
\mathbf{k}[X]
on a set
X
.
The object of degree
p
of
\overline{B}_{u}\mathbf{k}[G]
is a free
\mathbf{k}
-module with basis the elements
[b_{g_{1}}|\dots|b_{g_{p}}]
with
g_{i}\in G
. For all integers
p
we denote by
e
the
\mathbf{k}
-linear map defined for
p\leqslant\dim(G,\mathbf{k})
by:

\begin{array}[t]{cccc}e:&\mathbf{k}[G]^{\otimes p}&\to&\Lambda^{p}(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[1])\\
&[b_{g_{1}}|\dots|b_{g_{p}}]&\mapsto&\dfrac{1}{p!}(g_{1}\otimes 1)\wedge\cdots\wedge(g_{p}\otimes 1)\end{array}\;,

and which is zero if
p>\dim(G,\mathbf{k})
.
This
\mathbf{k}
-linear map is obviously natural with respect to
G
. Moreover
e
is a morphism of complexes: a direct computation shows that
ed[b_{g_{1}}|\dots|b_{g_{p}}]=0
(for this computation, note that the product in the
\mathbf{k}
-algebra
\mathbf{k}[G]
is given by
b_{g}b_{h}=b_{g+h}
.) Another direct computation shows that
e
is a morphism of
\mathbf{k}
-algebras, and since the domain and the codomain of
e
are equal to
\mathbf{k}
in degree zero,
e
automatically preserves the augmentation, hence it is a morphism in
\mathrm{ACDGAlg}_{\mathbf{k}}
.

It remains to check that
e
is a quasi-isomorphism. Since the homology algebras of both the source and the target of
e
are abstractly isomorphic to exterior algebras generated in degree
1
, it suffices to check that
H_{1}(e):H_{1}(\overline{B}\mathbf{k}[G])\to G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
is an isomorphism, i.e. that the sequence

\mathbf{k}[G]\otimes\mathbf{k}[G]\xrightarrow{f}\mathbf{k}[G]\xrightarrow{e}G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\to 0\,,

where
f[b_{g}|b_{h}]=b_{h}-b_{gh}+b_{g}
, is exact at
\mathbf{k}[G]
and
G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
. The latter holds for all abelian groups
G
, by the usual computation of the degree
1
homology of an abelian group
G
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Let
C(R,M)
denote the Hochschild complex of a
\mathbf{k}
-algebra

M

\mathbf{k}[G]

.

Remark 3.9
.

The isomorphism of algebras
H_{*}(e):H_{*}(\overline{B}\mathbf{k}[G])\to\Lambda(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[1])

Remark 3.10
.

The isomorphism
\psi
lifts to a quasi-isomorphism of dg-algebras
\psi\colon\Lambda(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[1])\to\overline{B}\mathbf{k}[G]
. Although this quasi-isomorphism is natural in
G

after taking homology
, it cannot be natural in
G
. Indeed, if it was, then
\psi_{1}\colon G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\to\overline{\mathbf{k}[G]}
would be an injective natural transformation. Now the reduced coproduct of the Hopf algebra
\mathbf{k}[G]

\overline{\Delta}\colon\overline{\mathbf{k}[G]}\to\overline{\mathbf{k}[G]}^{\otimes 2}
, hence
\overline{\Delta}\circ\psi_{1}\colon G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\to\overline{\mathbf{k}[G]}^{\otimes 2}
would be a nonzero natural transformation. Such a nonzero natural transformation cannot exist by Pirashvili’s vanishing lemma, see lemma
4.4
below.

Proposition 3.12
.

Assume that

, the divided power algebra

because
\mathbf{k}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. Therefore, by multiple uses of lemma
3.6
and of the fact that bar constructions preserve quasi-isomorphisms, we obtain that
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[G]
is naturally quasi-isomorphic to
\Gamma(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[2i])
if
n=2i
and to
\Lambda(G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}[2i+1])
if
n=2i+1
.
∎

4.
A non-formality result

If
C
is a cyclic group, we denote by
\langle C\rangle
the full subcategory of the category of abelian groups
\mathbf{Ab}
, whose objects are all finite direct sums of copies of
C
.
The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\mathbf{k}
is not a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. Let
C
be a cyclic group such that
C\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\neq 0
and let
n
be a positive integer.
If
D:\langle C\rangle\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
is a diagram of EML spaces of height
n
such that
\pi_{n}D(G)
is naturally isomorphic to
G
, then
D
is not formal in
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Theorem
4.1
shows that theorem
3.2
does not hold if we drop the hypothesis on the
\mathbf{k}
-dimension of the abelian groups
G
: for example, if
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}[1/k!]
and if
C
is a cyclic group of prime cardinal
p>k
, then the category
\langle C\rangle
contains groups of arbitrary large
\mathbf{k}
-dimension, and the diagram
G\mapsto K(G,n)
is not formal in
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
. (Compare with example
3.3
.)

By the rigidification property given in proposition
2.4
, it suffices to prove theorem
4.1
when
D
is a diagram of abelian EML spaces. Then by corollary
2.9
, theorem
4.1
is equivalent to the following statement on iterated bar constructions.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
\mathbf{k}
is not a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, and let
C
be a cyclic group such that
C\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\neq 0
. Then for all positive
n
the functor
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]:\langle C\rangle\to\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
is not formal.

In order to prove theorem
4.3
, we will find an obstruction to formality relying on homological algebra computations in functor categories. Thus we start by recalling some basic facts regarding functor categories.

4.1.
Recollections of functor categories

If
\mathbf{C}
is an essentially small category, we denote by
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})
the category whose objects are the functors from
\mathbf{C}
to
\mathbf{k}
-modules, and whose morphisms are the natural transformations.
We take
[
mi-72
]
as a reference for the basic structural facts on this category.
The category
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})
is abelian and bicomplete. Kernels, cokernels, direct sums or products are computed objectwise, for example the kernel of a natural transformation
f\colon F\to G
is such that
(\mathrm{Ker}f)(C)=\mathrm{Ker}(f_{C}\colon F(C)\to G(C)
. There is a tensor product of functors defined from the tensor product of
\mathbf{k}
-modules by
(F\otimes G)(C)=F(C)\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}G(C)
, and which is a monoidal structure on
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})
. The category
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})
also has enough injectives and projectives. The functors
P^{X}\colon C\mapsto\mathbf{k}[\Hm_{\mathbf{C}}(X,C)]
, where
X
belongs to
\mathbf{C}
, yield a projective generator, and the Yoneda lemma yields a natural isomorphism:

\Hm_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})}(P^{X},F)\simeq F(X)\;.

When
\mathbf{C}
is an additive category, the category
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})
has nice extra features. First of all, a functor
F
is called
reduced
if
F(0)=0
. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{red}}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})
the category of reduced functors and natural transformations. Using the fact that
0
is initial and terminal in
\mathbf{C}
, one sees that every functor
F
has a canonical decomposition
F=\overline{F}\oplus F(0)
, where
\overline{F}
is a reduced functor and
F(0)
denotes the constant functor with value
F(0)
. This decomposition yields a direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{red}}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})\oplus\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbf{k}}\;.

In particular, for all functors
F
and
G
we have

\Ext^{*}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})}(F,G)=\Ext^{*}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})}(\overline{F},\overline{G})\oplus\Ext^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(F(0),G(0))\;.

Another fundamental result that we will need is Pirashvili’s vanishing lemma (see e.g.
[
pi-03
, §2.4]
where the proof given works without change when
\mathbf{C}
is an arbitrary essentially small category and
\mathbf{k}
is an arbitrary commutative ring).

Lemma 4.4

(Pirashvili
[
pi-03
]
)
.

Let
A
,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be three objects of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})
. Assume that
A
is additive, and that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are reduced. Then

\Ext^{*}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})}(A,F_{1}\otimes F_{2})=0=\Ext^{*}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{k})}(F_{1}\otimes F_{2},A)
.

4.2.
Proof of theorem
4.3

We fix a commutative ring
\mathbf{k}
which is not a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, and a cyclic group
C
such that
C\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\neq 0
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The ring
\mathbf{k}
has a quotient field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p>0
such that
C\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}\simeq\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

If
C
is finite of cardinal
q
, then
q
is not invertible in
\mathbf{k}
since
C\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\neq 0
. Hence there is a prime
p
dividing this cardinal which is not invertible in
\mathbf{k}
. If
C
is infinite, the fact that
\mathbf{k}
is not a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra guarantees that there is a prime
p
which is not invertible in
\mathbf{k}
. In both cases, one may take
\mathbb{F}
to be the quotient of
\mathbf{k}/p\mathbf{k}
by some maximal ideal.
∎

We fix a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
as in lemma
4.5
.
For instance, if
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
then
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We introduce the following objects of
\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})
.

•

We denote by
A
the nonzero additive functor
A(G):=G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}
.

•

We denote by
P
the functor
P(G):=\mathbf{k}[G]
, and by
\overline{P}
its reduced part. Thus
P(G)
is the
\mathbf{k}
-algebra of the group
G
, and
\overline{P}(G)
is its augmentation ideal. Note that
P
(and hence its summand
\overline{P}
) is projective since
P\simeq P^{C}=\mathbf{k}[\Hm_{\mathbb{Z}}(C,-)]
.

•

We denote by
H_{i}(n,\mathbf{k})
the functor
G\mapsto H_{i}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[G])
.

Our proof of theorem
4.3
will rely on two preliminary
\Ext
-computations. The first one is a fairly easy consequence of the basic facts recalled in section
4.1
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
i
and
k
be two nonnegative integers. We have

\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(\overline{P}^{\otimes k},A)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}&\text{if $k=1$ and $i=0$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

If
k=0
then
\overline{P}^{\otimes 0}
is the constant functor with value
\mathbf{k}
and
A
is reduced, hence there is no nonzero
\Ext
between them.
If
k=1
the
\Ext
of positive degree vanish because
\overline{P}
is projective, and
\Ext^{0}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(\overline{P},A)=A(C)\simeq\mathbb{F}
by the Yoneda lemma and lemma
4.5
. Finally, the
\Ext
vanishing for
k>1
comes from Pirashvili’s vanishing lemma
4.4
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

For all positive
n
, the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space
\bigoplus_{i,j\geqslant 0}\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(H_{j}(n,\mathbf{k}),A)
has dimension greater than one.

The proof of proposition
4.7
is significantly more technical than that of proposition
4.6
. The reason is that no workable description of the functors
H_{i}(n,\mathbf{k})
is known when
\mathbf{k}
is an arbitrary commutative ring. We postpone the proof of proposition
4.7
to sections
4.3
,
4.4
and
4.5
.
We first finish the proof of theorem
4.3
assuming that proposition
4.7
holds.

Proof of theorem
4.3
.

We proceed by contradiction, so we assume that
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]
is formal. If we regard
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]
as a complex in
\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})
, then formality means that it is connected to the complex
H(n,\mathbf{k})=\bigoplus_{i\geqslant 0}H_{i}(n,\mathbf{k})
(with zero differential) by a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of complexes in
\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})
. Thus, if
J^{A}
denotes an injective resolution of
A
in
\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})
, then the totalization of the bicomplex of
\mathbf{k}
-modules

T:=\Hm_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-],J^{A})

is connected by a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms in
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
to the total complex of
\mathbf{k}
-modules

T^{\prime}:=\Hm_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(H(n,\mathbf{k}),J^{A})\;.

Let us compute the homology of
T
. Let
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]_{s}
denote the object of degree
s
of the complex
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]
. There is a first quadrant spectral sequence:

E_{1}^{st}=\Ext^{t}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]_{s},A)\Rightarrow H^{s+t}(T)\;.

As a graded object, the reduced normalized bar complex of an augmented dg-algebra
A
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{k\geqslant 0}\overline{A}[1]^{\otimes k}
with the convention that
\overline{A}[1]^{\otimes 0}:=\mathbf{k}
,
thus we see by induction on
n
that:

\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]_{s}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{k}&\text{if $s=0$,}\\
0&\text{if $0<s<n$,}\\
\overline{P}&\text{if $s=n$,}\end{cases}

and for
s>n
,
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]_{s}
is a direct sum of copies of functors
\overline{P}^{\otimes k}
with
k>1
. Therefore by proposition
4.6
, we have
E_{1}^{st}\simeq\mathbb{F}
if
(s,t)=(n,0)
and zero otherwise. As a consequence, the spectral sequence collapses and the total homology of
T
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}
.

Now the total homology of
T^{\prime}
is
\bigoplus_{i,j\geqslant 0}\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(H_{j}(n,\mathbf{k}),A)
, which is an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space of dimension greater than one by proposition
4.7
. This contradicts the fact that
T
and
T^{\prime}
have isomorphic homology. Hence
\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[-]
cannot be formal.
∎

4.3.
Proof of proposition
4.7
when
C
is infinite

We first recall a (very) partial description of the homology of iterated bar constructions, contained in
[
ca-54
]
.
For all free abelian groups
G
, let us denote by
U(G[n])
the free divided power ring on a copy of
G
viewed as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module placed in degree
n
. Thus
U(G[n])=\Lambda(G[n])
if
n
is odd and
U(G[n])=\Gamma(G[n])
if
n
is even.

Lemma 4.8
.

There is a decomposition, natural with respect to the free abelian group
G

H_{*}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{Z}[G])=U(G[n])\oplus T(G,n),

where
T(G,n)
is a functor with values in torsion abelian groups.
As a consequence, for all commutative rings
\mathbf{k}
, the graded
\mathbf{k}
-module
U(G[n])\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
is a natural direct summand of
H_{*}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[G])
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}
. Let
G
denote a free abelian group and
let
K(G,n)
denote an Eilenberg-MacLane space such that
\pi_{n}K(G,n)\simeq G
naturally with respect to
G
. The Hurewicz theorem and proposition
2.8
yield a graded isomorphism
G[n]\simeq H_{n}(K(G,n);\mathbb{Z})\simeq H_{n}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{Z}[G])
. This induces an algebra morphism
U(G[n])\to H_{n}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{Z}[G])
, natural with respect to
G
. Then
[
ca-54
, Exp. 11, Thm. 1]
shows that this algebra morphism is injective and in direct sum with the torsion part of the homology.
The statement for an arbitrary ground ring
\mathbf{k}
now follows from the universal coefficient theorem.
∎

By lemma
4.8
,
H(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[G])
admits
\bigoplus_{d\geqslant 0}\Gamma^{d}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
as a natural direct summand if
n
is even and
\bigoplus_{d\geqslant 0}\Lambda^{d}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
if
n
is odd (we don’t specify the homological degrees here, which are not relevant to prove proposition
4.7
). Hence, proposition
4.7
follows from the next lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a commutative ring having a quotient field
\mathbb{F}
of positive characteristic
p
, let
\langle C\rangle
denote the category of free abelian groups of finite rank, and let
A:\langle C\rangle\to\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbf{k}}
denote the functor
G\mapsto G/pG\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
.
If
F\colon\langle C\rangle\to\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbf{k}}
is the functor
F(G)=\Lambda^{1}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}=\Gamma^{1}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}=G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
or
F(G)=\Gamma^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
, then

\Ext^{0}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(F,A)\neq 0\;.

Furthermore, if
F(G)=\Lambda^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
then

\Ext^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(F,A)\neq 0\;.

Proof.

Assume first that
F(G)=G\otimes_{Z}\mathbf{k}
. Then tensoring the quotient map
\mathbf{k}\to\mathbb{F}
by
G
gives a non-zero element in
\Ext^{0}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(F,A)
.
Assume now that
F(G)=\Gamma^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
. Then the quotient map
\mathbf{k}\to\mathbb{F}
together with the Verschiebung map
v\colon\Gamma^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{p}=\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G/pG)\to G/pG
induce a surjective natural transformation

F(G)=\Gamma^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\to\Gamma^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}=\Gamma^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}\xrightarrow{v\otimes\mathbb{F}}G/pG\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}=A(G),

hence a nonzero element in
\Ext^{0}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(F,A)
. Finally, assume that
F(G)=\Lambda^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
.
Let
K^{i}(G)
denote the tensor product
(\Gamma^{p-i}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k})\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}(\Lambda^{i}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k})
, in particular
K^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}=F(G)
and
K^{0}(G)=\Gamma^{p}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
.
There is an exact Koszul complex, see e.g.
[
br-mi-to-16
, sec. 6.2.1]
:

0\to K^{0}(G)\to K^{1}(G)\to\cdots\to K^{p}(G)\to 0\;.

Each
K^{i}(G)
is a tensor product of two reduced functors of
G
if
0<i<p
, hence by Pirashvili’s vanishing lemma the functors
K^{i}
are acyclic for
\Ext^{0}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(-,A)
. Moreover, we have already shown above that
\Ext^{0}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(K^{0},A)\neq 0
. Thus dimension shifting (see e.g.
[
we-97
, Exercise 2.4.3, p.47]
or
[
br-94
, III Section 7]
) gives an isomorphism

\Ext^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(F,A)=\Ext^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(K^{p},A)\simeq\Ext^{0}_{\mathcal{F}(\langle C\rangle,\mathbf{k})}(K^{0},A)\neq 0\;.

∎

4.4.
Proof of proposition
4.7
when
n=1
and
C
is finite

Let us assume that
C=\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
A
be an abelian group and let
{}_{q}A
denote its subgroup of
q
-torsion.
For all free
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
-modules
G
, there is an isomorphism
\Tor^{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}(G,A)\simeq G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}({}_{q}A)
natural with respect to

.

Proof.

The result follows from the fact that every functor

from free

-modules to

preserving all direct sums is completely determined by the abelian group

, see e.g.
[

]
.
∎

The next lemma relies on the known explicit functorial description of the homology of

when

is a field.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
p
be a prime dividing

. For all free
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
-modules
G
, there is a non-zero morphism
H_{2p}(\overline{B}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])\to G/pG
, natural with respect to

.

There is a natural surjective morphism

, where the target is the

-divided power algebra on the

-torsion subgroup

, see
[
br-94

∎

In order to extend the result of lemma

Lemma 4.12
.

If

is a cyclic group of cardinal

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
p
be a prime dividing
q
. For all free
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
-modules
G
, there is a non-zero morphism
H_{2p-1}(\overline{B}\mathbb{Z}[G])\to G/pG
natural with respect to

.

Proof.

For all

, lemmas

yield an isomorphism

.
Thus, the universal coefficient theorem yields a short exact sequence of functors

be the restriction of

is simple (indeed, if

∎

The next lemma proves proposition

Lemma 4.14
.

Let

The quotient map

.
Let

Thus for all positive integers

(2)

0\to H_{j}(\overline{B}\mathbb{Z}[G])\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}\to H_{j}(\overline{B}\mathbf{k}[G])\to H_{j-1}(\overline{B}\mathbb{Z}[G])\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}T\to 0\;.

is isomorphic to
H_{2p-1}(\overline{B}\mathbb{Z}[G])\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}
by (
2
), hence it surjects onto
H_{2p-1}(\overline{B}\mathbb{Z}[G])\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}
, and by lemma
4.13
there is a non-zero natural map from the latter to
G/pG\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}=A(G)
.

Assume now that
T\neq 0
, then we claim that there is a nonzero morphism
H_{2p}(1,\mathbf{k})\to A
. Indeed,
T
is a non-zero
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
-module, hence there is a morphism of abelian groups
T\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Together with the quotient map of the exact sequence (
2
), this morphism yields a surjective natural map:

H_{j}(\overline{B}\mathbf{k}[G])\to H_{j-1}(\overline{B}\mathbb{Z}[G])\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{p}
. And lemma
4.13
provides a nonzero natural map

H_{2p-1}(\overline{B}\mathbb{Z}[G])\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\to G/pG\hookrightarrow G/pG\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}=A(G)

whence the result.
∎

Proof of proposition

when

We recall
[

, Section 14]
,
[

that for all commutative rings
\mathbf{k}
and for all

, the

is a morphism of graded
\mathbf{k}
-modules

\sigma\colon H_{*}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[G])\to H_{*+1}(\overline{B}^{n+1}\mathbf{k}[G])

natural with respect to
G
, which vanishes on products of classes of positive degrees (a.k.a.
decomposable elements
). Moreover, the suspension induces an isomorphism in degrees
0<*<2n
, hence the modules
H_{i+n}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbf{k}[G])
do not depend on
n
if
n>i
, they are called the

and denoted by

.
For
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the stable homology was described as a functor in
[
ca-54
, Exp. 10, §6]
, in particular for all

,

is a finite direct sum of copies of
{}_{p}G
and
G/pG
(with multiplicities known and independent on

, where

We also recall

p
-th divided power
\gamma_{p}
and transpotence
\varphi_{p}
. Suitable words (called

-admissible words

) in the letters

\varphi_{p}
and
\sigma
encode compositions of homology operations which yield injective morphisms

G/pG\to H_{*}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])\qquad\text{ and }\qquad{}_{p}G\to H_{*}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])

whose images generate
H_{*}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])
as a free divided power algebra, see the ‘théorème fondamental’ of
[
ca-54
, Exp. 9, §3]
if

is odd and of
[
ca-54
, Exp. 10, §2]
if

.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
q
be a positive integer and let
p
be a prime dividing
q
. For all
n\geqslant 2
, let
\theta_{*}\colon H_{*}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{Z}[G])\to H_{*}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])
denote the graded morphism induced by the morphism
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
. For all free
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
-modules
G
, there is a morphism
f\colon H_{2p+n-1}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])\to G/pG
, natural with respect to
G
such that
f\circ\theta_{2p+n-1}\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
G
be an arbitrary abelian group.
Let
\alpha=\varphi_{2}
if
p
is odd and
\alpha=\gamma_{2}\sigma
if
p=2
. Then for all
N\geqslant 0
, the word
\sigma^{N+n-1}\gamma_{p}\alpha
is
p
-admissible, and it is associated to an injective composition of homology operations:

{}_{p}G\xrightarrow{\alpha}H_{2}(\overline{B}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])\xrightarrow{\sigma\gamma_{p}}H_{2p+1}(\overline{B}^{2}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])\xrightarrow{\sigma^{N+n-2}}H_{2p+N+n-1}(\overline{B}^{n+N}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]).

In particular, if
N\gg 0
, this composition of operations yields an injective morphism from
{}_{p}G
to the stable homology
H^{\mathrm{st}}_{2p-1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Now we restrict our attention to free
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
-modules
G
, hence lemma
4.10
yields a natural isomorphism
{}_{p}G\simeq G/pG
, thus
H_{2p-1}^{\mathrm{st}}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is naturally isomorphic to
G/pG^{\oplus s}
for some
s>0
. We let
f\colon H_{2p+n-1}(\overline{B}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])\to G/pG
be a coordinate of the morphism
\sigma^{N}\colon H_{2p+n-1}(\overline{B}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])\to H_{2p-1}^{\mathrm{st}}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq G/pG^{\oplus s}
which does not vanish on the image of
\sigma^{n-1}\gamma_{p}\alpha
in
H_{2p+n-1}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])
.

In order to prove the lemma, it remains to construct an element
u\in H_{2p+n-1}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{Z}[G])
such that
f(\theta_{2p+n-1}(u))\neq 0
. Let
x\in{}_{p}G
be a nonzero element and let

, where
\beta_{p}
denotes the Bockstein operation of
H_{2p+n-1}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])
. Since Bocksteins commute with suspensions,
[
ca-54
, Exp. 8, Thm. 1]
shows that

is decomposable (and it is actually zero if
p
is odd). Since
f
vanishes on decomposable elements, we obtain
f(y)=f(\sigma^{n-1}\gamma_{p}\alpha(x))\neq 0
.
Now the bockstein
\beta_{p}
factors through
\theta_{*}
(see e.g.
[
ca-54
, Exp. 8, p.8-03]
), hence
y=\theta_{2p+n-1}(u)
for some element
u\in H_{2p+n-1}(\overline{B}^{n}\mathbb{Z}[G])
, which finishes the proof.
∎

5.
Applications

We now explain how formality results apply to the study of singular (co)homology of homotopy colimits.
Recall that if
\mathbf{M}
is a cofibrantly generated model category (such as topological spaces or simplicial sets), the category
\mathbf{M}^{\mathbf{C}}
of diagrams of shape
\mathbf{C}
in
\mathbf{M}
carries a model structure called the
projective model structure
. The homotopy colimit of a diagram
D\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{M}
is then defined as the colimit of a cofibrant replacement of
D
in this projective model structure.

Recall furthermore that the simplicial replacement of a diagram
D\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{M}
is the simplicial object in
\mathbf{M}
denoted by
\srep_{\bullet}D
which is given in degree
n
by

\srep_{n}D=\bigsqcup_{\sigma=c_{0}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow c_{n}\in N_{n}(\mathbf{C})}D(c_{n})\;,

where
N_{\bullet}(\mathbf{C})
denotes the nerve of
\mathbf{C}
. If
s_{i}^{\prime}
and
d_{i}^{\prime}
denote the degeneracies and the faces of the nerve of
\mathbf{C}
, then the degeneracy
s_{i}\colon\srep_{n-1}D\to\srep_{n}D
sends the component indexed by
\sigma
identically to the component indexed by
s_{i}^{\prime}(\sigma)
, and the face
d_{i}\colon\srep_{n}D\to\srep_{n-1}D
sends the component indexed by
\sigma=c_{0}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow c_{n}
to the component indexed by
d_{i}^{\prime}(\sigma)
, identically if
i<n
, or acts as
D(c_{n}\to c_{n-1})
if
i=n
.
When
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{Top}
or
\mathbf{sSet}
, the homotopy colimit of a diagram can be alternatively computed as the geometric realization of its simplicial replacement, namely it follows from
[
hi-03
, Theorem 18.7.5 (1)]
and
[
du-is-04
, Corollary A.6]
that there is a weak equivalence:

\hoc D\xrightarrow{\sim}|\srep_{\bullet}(D)|\;.

We are interested in the singular homology of the homotopy colimit of a diagram
D
of spaces (topological spaces or simplicial sets). Let
C_{**}(D)
denote the first quadrant homological bicomplex of
\mathbf{k}
-modules whose columns are the singular chain complexes
C_{p*}(D)=C_{*}(\srep_{p}D;\mathbf{k})
and whose horizontal differentials
C_{p\,q}(D)\to C_{(p-1)\,q}(D)
are induced by the simplicial structure of
\srep_{\bullet}D
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For all diagrams
D
in
\mathbf{sSet}
or
\mathbf{Top}
, there is a zig-zag of homotopy equivalences natural with respect to
D
, connecting the chain complexes
\Tot C_{**}(D)
and
C_{*}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k})
.

Proof.

Assume first that
D
is a diagram in
\mathbf{sSet}
. Then
[
hi-03
, Theorem 15.11.6]
gives an isomorphism of simplicial sets
|\srep_{\bullet}D|\simeq\mathrm{diag}\,\srep_{\bullet}D
, and the singular chains of the latter are naturally homotopy equivalent to
\Tot C_{**}(D)
by the Eilenberg-Zilber theorem. Assume now that
D
is a diagram in
\mathbf{Top}
. Let
D^{\prime}
be the diagram of simplicial sets given by the singular set of
D
, i.e.,
D^{\prime}(c)=\mathrm{Sing}_{\bullet}(D(c))
. Then
D
is weakly equivalent to the diagram
|D^{\prime}|\colon c\mapsto|D^{\prime}(c)|
given by geometric realization, hence it suffices to prove the result for
D=|D^{\prime}|
. Note that if
QD^{\prime}\to D^{\prime}
is a cofibrant replacement of
D^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{sSet}^{\mathbf{C}}
then by applying geometric realization one obtains a cofibrant replacement
|QD^{\prime}|\to|D^{\prime}|
in
\mathbf{Top}^{\mathbf{C}}
. Since geometric realization preserves colimits, this gives a weak homotopy equivalence

\hoc|D^{\prime}|\simeq|\hoc D^{\prime}|\;.

Therefore, the singular chain complex
C_{*}(\hoc|D^{\prime}|;\mathbf{k})
is
naturally quasi-isomorphic to

C_{*}(|\hoc D^{\prime}|;\mathbf{k})
. Since for all simplicial sets
X
the adjunction unit
X\to\mathrm{Sing}_{\bullet}|X|
is a weak homotopy equivalence,
C_{*}(|\hoc D^{\prime}|;\mathbf{k})
is naturally quasi-isomorphic to
C_{*}(\hoc D^{\prime};\mathbf{k})
. We have already shown that the latter is naturally
quasi-isomorphic to
\Tot C_{**}(D^{\prime})
. Finally, by using the adjunction unit
X\to\mathrm{Sing}_{\bullet}|X|
again, we obtain that the latter is naturally quasi-isomorphic to
\Tot C_{**}(|D^{\prime}|)
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The zigzag in lemma
5.1
lifts to a zigzag of quasi-isomorphisms in the category of strictly coassociative DG coalgebras.
The standard Alexander-Whitney diagonal on singular chains is coassociative but not strictly cocommutative.
Therefore, applying the
\mathbb{Q}
-dual to this zigzag yields a statement for singular cochain complexes in the category of strictly associative DG algebras, but not commutative ones.

Thus,
C_{**}(D)
is a first quadrant homological bicomplex of
\mathbf{k}
-modules whose total complex is quasi-isomorphic to
C_{*}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k})
. We consider the spectral sequence
(E_{**}^{r},d^{r})_{r\geqslant 1}
associated to its columnwise filtration.
If
H_{d}(D)\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbf{k}}
denotes the functor sending each
c
to the singular homology of
D(c)
, then the first page
(E^{1}_{**}(D),d^{1})
is given by:

\displaystyle E^{1}_{p,q}(D)=\srep_{p}H_{q}(D)\;,

\displaystyle d^{1}:E^{1}_{p\,q}\to E^{1}_{(p-1)\,q}\text{ is induced by the simplicial structure of $\srep_{\bullet}H_{q}(D)$}\;.

Hence the homology of
(E^{1}_{*q}(D),d^{1})
is precisely the homology
H_{*}(\mathbf{C},H_{q}(D))
of the category
\mathbf{C}
with coefficients in
H_{q}(D)

[
lo-98
, Appendix C.10]
. By dualizing
C_{**}(D)
we obtain the first quadrant cohomological bicomplex
C^{**}(D)
whose total complex is quasi-isomorphic to
C^{*}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k})
, hence a spectral sequence whose second page is given by cohomology of the category
\mathbf{C}
with coefficients in
H^{q}(D)
. The next proposition summarizes the situation.

Proposition 5.3

(
[
du
, Theorem 18.3]
)
.

For all diagrams
D
of shape
\mathbf{C}
in
\mathbf{Top}
or in
\mathbf{sSet}
there are first quadrant spectral sequences

E^{2}_{p,q}(D)=H_{p}(\mathbf{C};H_{q}(D;\mathbf{k}))\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k}),

E_{2}^{p,q}(D)=H^{p}(\mathbf{C};H^{q}(D;\mathbf{k}))\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k}).

Remark 5.4
.

The spectral sequences of proposition
5.3
are the simplicial version of the spectral sequence of studied by Bousfield in
[
bo-87
]
. They go back at least to the work of Segal
[
se-68
]
, and they are a particular case of Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence of a covering (MVSS, for short).
To see this last point, assume that
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{P}
is a poset.
Every diagram of topological spaces
D\colon\mathbf{P}\to\mathbf{Top}
has a unique natural transformation to the constant diagram
*\colon\mathbf{P}\to\Top
whose objects are single points. This natural transformation induces a continuous map
p\colon\hoc D\to\hoc*=|N_{\bullet}(\mathbf{P})|
. The
0
-cells of the the latter are in one to one correspondence with the objects of
\mathbf{P}
, and for all objects
c
we let

\st^{\circ}(p):=\{\sigma\in N_{\bullet}(\mathbf{P})\colon\sigma=(p_{0}>\cdots>p_{n}),\ p_{n}=p\},

be the
open star
of
\mathbf{P}
.
The union of open simplices corresponding to
\st^{\circ}(p)
in the realization
|N_{\bullet}(\mathbf{P})|
of the nerve is an open contractible subset.
The topological space
\hoc D
admits an open cover by
\{U(c)\}_{p\in\mathbf{P}}
, where

U(p):=p^{-1}(\st^{\circ}(p))=\st^{\circ}(p)\times D(p),

and this topological space is homotopy equivalent to
D(p)
.
Notice that

U(p_{0})\cap\cdots\cap U(p_{n})\neq\varnothing

iff the elements
p_{i}
,
i=0,\dots,n
, are pairwise comparable in
\mathbf{P}
(and therefore form a uniquely defined simplex in
N_{\bullet}(\mathbf{P})
).
Therefore, if the intersection is nonempty then it is homotopy equivalent to
D(p)
, where
p
is the minimum of the elements
p_{0},\dots,p_{n}
.
The nerve of the cover
\{U(p)\}_{p\in N_{\bullet}(\mathbf{P})}
coincides with the nerve
N_{\bullet}(\mathbf{P})
of
\mathbf{P}
.
The respective
Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence
(e.g. see
[
bo-tu-82
, §8]
) is the spectral sequence of the simplicial filtration on Čech complex

\bigoplus\limits_{p_{0}>\dots>p_{n}\in\mathbf{P}}C_{q}(U(p_{0})\cap\cdots\cap U(p_{n}))\cong\bigoplus\limits_{p_{0}>\dots>p_{n}\in\mathbf{P}}C_{*}(D(p_{n}))\cong C_{*,*}(D).

Hence, MVSS of
D
is isomorphic to
(E^{*}_{**}(D),d^{*})
.
(The cohomological spectral sequence case relates to the homological case in a similar way to the above.)

We denote by
E^{1}_{*}(D)
the chain complex obtained by totalizing the first page of the homological spectral sequence of proposition
5.3
. That is,
E^{1}_{n}(D)=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\srep_{p}H_{q}(D)
with differential
E^{1}_{n}(D)\to E_{n-1}^{1}(D)
induced by the simplicial structure of
\srep_{\bullet}H_{q}(D)
. Similarly, we denote by
E_{1}^{*}(D)
the chain complex obtained by totalizing the first page of the cohomological spectral sequence of proposition
5.3
. The following result gives an application of formality for diagrams of spaces.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
D
be a diagram in
\mathbf{Top}
or in
\mathbf{sSet}
.

(1)

If the diagram of singular chain complexes
c\mapsto C_{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
is formal in
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
, then the homology spectral sequence of proposition
5.3
collapses at page
2
. Actually, the singular chain complex
C_{*}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k})
is quasi-isomorphic to
E^{1}_{*}(D)
.

(2)

If the diagram of singular cochain complexes
c\mapsto C^{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
is formal in
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
, then the cohomology spectral sequence of proposition
5.3
collapses at page
2
. Actually, the singular cochain complex
C^{*}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k})
is quasi-isomorphic to
E_{1}^{*}(D)
.

Proof.

We prove the homological statement (the proof of the cohomological statement is similar). Formality gives a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms for diagrams of chain complexes connecting
H_{*}(D)
with
C_{*}(D;\mathbf{k})
. Applying simplicial replacement to this zig-zag yields a zigzag of bicomplexes:

(3)

E^{1}_{**}(D)\leftarrow\dots\to C_{**}(D)

where
E_{**}^{1}(D)
is the bicomplex with horizontal differential equal to
d^{1}
, and trivial vertical differential. The morphisms of bicomplexes appearing in (
3
) are columnwise quasi-isomorphisms, hence they give rise to isomorphisms of spectral sequences. Hence, collapsing of the spectral sequence of the bicomplex
C_{**}(D)
follows from collapsing of the spectral sequence of the bicomplex
E_{**}^{1}(D)
. Moreover, by totalizing the morphisms of bicomplexes in (
3
) and by using lemma
5.1
, we obtain a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of chain complexes connecting
E_{*}^{1}(D)
with
C_{*}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k})
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

If
\mathbf{k}
is a field, then the
\mathbf{k}
-linear dual of any quasi-isomorphism in
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
is a quasi-isomorphism. Thus if the diagram of singular chain complexes
c\mapsto C_{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
is formal in
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
, then the diagram of singular cochain complexes
c\mapsto C^{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
is also formal in
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbf{k}}
.

Remark 5.7
.

By taking into account the algebraic structures discussed in remark
5.2
, the quasi-isomorphisms of proposition
5.5
can be upgraded. In the homological case, if
c\mapsto C_{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
is formal in
\mathrm{ACDGAlg}_{\mathbf{k}}
(as is the case for abelian EML spaces over
\mathbb{Q}
), then the quasi-isomorphism between
C_{*}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k})
and
E_{*}^{1}(D)
holds in
\mathrm{ACDGAlg}_{\mathbf{k}}
via the Pontryagin product.
In the cohomological case, if
c\mapsto C^{*}(D(c);\mathbf{k})
is formal in the category of strictly associative DG algebras, the quasi-isomorphism between
C^{*}(\hoc D;\mathbf{k})
and
E^{*}_{1}(D)
holds in the category of strictly associative DG algebras via the Alexander-Whitney map.
In both cases, passing to (co)homology ensures that the spectral sequence collapses and yields a strict isomorphism of graded commutative algebras between the second page of the spectral sequence and rational (co)homology of
\hoc D
.

The next theorem is an application of the formality results of section
3
together with the previous considerations. Recall
[
bo-ka-72
, II 4.3]
that a pointed connected space is called
nilpotent
if its fundamental group acts nilpotently on all the homotopy groups
\pi_{i}
for
i>0
. Nilpotent spaces include simply connected spaces, topological groups, as well as spaces with abelian fundamental group and contractible universal cover.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
D
be a diagram in
\mathbf{Top}_{*}
or
\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
. Assume that every object
D(c)
of the diagram is a nilpotent space such that
\pi_{i}D(c)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
for all
i\neq n
and with abelian fundamental group.
Then the two spectral sequences of proposition
5.3
collapse.
Actually the singular chain complex
C_{*}(\hoc;\mathbb{Q})
is quasi-isomorphic to
E^{1}_{*}(D)
and the singular cochain complex
C^{*}(\hoc;\mathbb{Q})
is quasi-isomorphic to
E_{1}^{*}(D)
.

Proof.

Since every diagram of nilpotent topological spaces can be converted into a diagram of pointed simplicial spaces with the same property on rational homotopy groups, we may assume that we work with pointed simplicial sets, i.e., we consider a diagram
D:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{sSet}_{*}
.
Then
[
bo-ka-72
, V 4.2]
provides a functorial
\mathbb{Q}
-localization, that is an endofunctor
L_{\mathbb{Q}}
of nilpotent simplicial sets, together with a natural transformation
\eta_{X}:X\to L_{\mathbb{Q}}X
which induces
an isomorphism
\pi_{i}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\pi_{i}L_{\mathbb{Q}}X
. In particular
\eta
induces a natural transformation of diagrams
D\to L_{\mathbb{Q}}D
, such that for all objects
c
of
\mathbf{C}
, the map
\eta_{c}:D(c)\to L_{\mathbb{Q}}D(c)
induces a quasi-isomorphism on the level of singular chain complexes with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
. Moreover,
L_{\mathbb{Q}}D
is a diagram of EML-spaces of height
n
, hence it is formal in
\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbb{Q}}
by theorem
3.1
. Thus the functor
\mathbf{C}\mapsto\mathrm{DGMod}_{\mathbb{Q}}
defined by
c\mapsto C_{*}(D(c);\mathbb{Q})
is formal.
Now theorem
5.8
follows from proposition
5.5
and remark
5.6
.
∎
