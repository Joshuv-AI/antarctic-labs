Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics reveal lactate-active epithelial–immune cell crosstalk that reprograms the immune microenvironment in colorectal cancer
Authors: Haisheng Lan, Yang Li, Hua Li, Junyu Guo, Cunchuan Wang, Qianli Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•A novel 4-gene lactate metabolism score (LMscore) was developed for CRC prognosis and immunotherapy prediction.•LMscore reprograms epithelial-immune crosstalk via EFNA1-EPHA3-mediated signaling with fibroblasts.•High LMscore defines "cold" tumor phenotype with immunosuppressive cells and poor ICB response.•Targeting COX15 suppresses tumor proliferation and restores antitumor immunity in CRC.

Introduction

The tumor microenvironment (TME) functions as a pivotal regulatory nexus governing tumorigenesis, progression, and metastatic dissemination, emerging as a paramount focus in contemporary oncological research [1]. In this context, metabolic reprogramming is a critical adaptive strategy that enables tumor cells to withstand microenvironmental stress. Concurrently, this strategy drives malignant proliferation and invasion while dynamically reconfiguring the architectural and functional integrity of the tumor immune niche. Among the metabolic pathways, lactate metabolism has been the focus of significant research interest due to its critical role within the TME [2]. Lactate's functions extend beyond its conventional designation as a glycolytic end-product. It functions as a master metabolic signaling molecule that orchestrates epigenetic remodeling, immune cell functionality, and microenvironmental equilibrium. Consequently, it dictates tumor immune evasion and therapeutic susceptibility. Aberrant lactate metabolism in the TME stems directly from the tumor cell-specific "Warburg effect", wherein aerobic glycolysis predominates over oxidative phosphorylation for lactate generation [3]. This metabolic derangement precipitates profound lactate accumulation, triggering multifaceted reprogramming of the TME. Notably, lactate exerts cell-type-restricted effects during microenvironmental cellular reprogramming [4]. In malignant cells, lactate instigates a self-reinforcing loop via histone lactylation-mediated ACAT2 transcriptional activation (H3K18la), culminating in exacerbated lactate sequestration [5,6]. The process of metabolic rewiring provides tumor cells with the necessary substrates for energy and biosynthetic processes. Additionally, it has been observed to potentiate metastatic progression through the strategic modulation of oncogenic signaling cascades [2]. Concurrently, lactate actively sculpts an immunosuppressive milieu: elevated lactate levels selectively recruit myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and regulatory T cells (Tregs) while actively suppressing cytotoxic T cell infiltration [7]. This immunosuppressive phenotype, which is robustly correlated with hyperlactatemia in CRC, underlies the suboptimal responses to immunotherapy observed in this malignancy.

CRC ranks as the third most prevalent and second most lethal global malignancy, presenting formidable therapeutic challenges. Conventional chemotherapy and targeted agents yield partial responses in subsets of patients; however, pervasive drug resistance and recurrence necessitate transformative strategies. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have demonstrated efficacy across a variety of solid tumors; however, they encounter significant limitations in the context of CRC [8,9]. Of particular significance is the role of dysregulated lactate metabolism as a pivotal mechanism underlying immunotherapy resistance. In CRC, the hyper-lactate niche has been shown to suppress immune competence via the lactate metabolism-epigenetic-cholesterol immunosuppression axis. This results in a transition from an immune-sensitive to an immune-desert TME [10]. Mechanistically, T cells rely on glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation for metabolic fitness; however, lactate accumulation could cripple glycolytic flux, abrogating their adaptive capacity to microenvironmental stress [11]. Furthermore, lactate has been demonstrated to compromise T cell mitochondrial bioenergetics, thereby accelerating functional exhaustion and severely attenuating immunotherapeutic efficacy [11]. The high glycolytic state (HGS) cell subpopulation, sustained by key regulators such as PFKP, correlates strongly with adverse clinical outcomes. Fibroblast lactate metabolism dysregulation profoundly alters cellular activation, as lactate-driven TGF-β pathway engagement catalyzes fibroblast-to-cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF) trans-differentiation. The presence of dense extracellular matrix and pro-inflammatory mediators, deposited by these CAFs, creates a physical barrier that obstructs immune cell trafficking [12,13]. The secretion of chemokines by CAFs, particularly CCL15, serves to further recruit immunosuppressive populations, thereby consolidating the inhibitory niche. T cell-intrinsic lactate dysregulation directly impairs effector function. Elevated lactate concentrations have been shown to inhibit glycolysis, disrupt intracellular pH homeostasis, curtail proliferative capacity, and diminish cytokine production, culminating in functional paralysis [14].

Metabolic reprogramming in CRC has emerged as a promising therapeutic target. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that the targeting of glycolytic enzymes, specifically LDHA and PFKP, results in a substantial reduction of lactate burden, enhancement of CD8+ T cell functionality, and potentiation of immunotherapy efficacy [15]. Complementary approaches, such as inhibiting lactate transporters (e.g., MCT4), have been shown to reduce microenvironmental lactate accumulation and restore immune competence [16]. Conventional methodologies are inadequate for addressing the intricate cellular heterogeneity and spatial organization that is characteristic of the TME. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has emerged as a powerful tool for the high-resolution dissection of tumor cell populations, unveiling previously undetected heterogeneity. Spatial transcriptomics further delineates transcriptomic landscapes according to anatomical coordinates, thereby providing unprecedented insights into metabolic reprogramming dynamics [17]. Spatial analyses have revealed the presence of high-grade serous (HGS) cell enrichment within tumor cores, suggesting adaptation to nutrient-deprived niches. Concurrently, these analyses have identified region-specific metabolic signatures, including those related to purine and taurine metabolism, which offer insights into the underlying mechanisms [17].

The causal link between lactate metabolic dysregulation and immunotherapy resistance in CRC—a primary impediment to therapeutic success—has been established. This study integrates single-cell sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, and bulk RNA sequencing to rigorously quantify lactate metabolic features. We have developed the LMscore as a quantitative metric to guide clinical immunotherapy in CRC, thereby establishing a foundation for metabolic reprogramming-focused combinatorial strategies to overcome current therapeutic limitations. This paradigm shift promises to redefine the therapeutic landscape for CRC.

Methods

Preprocessing of single-cell data

The bulk transcriptome sequencing data for colorectal cancer (CRC) and their corresponding clinical information used in this study were sourced from the public UCSC Xena database (https://xenabrowser.net/). Concurrently, the single-cell transcriptome datasets utilized in this research were obtained from the GEO database, specifically including CRC tissue samples and their paired normal tissue samples from the GSE166555. To ensure the comparability of data derived from different sources or batches, a series of data management and integration steps were performed. These steps included batch effect correction and cross-batch integration on the raw gene expression matrices. The "limma" and "sva" R packages were utilized in these steps. Specifically, for the single-cell data, we further applied the Harmony integration algorithm to effectively mitigate batch effects arising from variations in experimental conditions.

In-depth analysis pipeline for single-cell data

During the preprocessing phase of the single-cell transcriptome data, this study employed the "Seurat" R package for the standardized analytical workflow. Initially, the "PercentageFeatureSet" function was used to assess the expression proportion of specific gene sets in each cell. Based on this, cell quality filtering criteria were established: only cells expressing between 200 and 10,000 genes were retained, while those with mitochondrial gene expression exceeding 20% were excluded as potentially low-quality. Subsequently, the filtered high-quality cell data underwent normalization and scaling. The "RunPCA" function was then utilized to perform principal component analysis to capture key biological variations. To further investigate the distribution patterns of cell populations in high-dimensional space, two non-linear dimensionality reduction techniques, UMAP and t-SNE, were employed for visualization. For cell type identification and annotation, the "FindAllMarkers" function was used to identify significantly highly expressed marker genes in each cell subpopulation. Manual annotation of each subpopulation was then performed by referencing established cell type-specific marker gene information from existing literature and public databases. In order to systematically evaluate the functional characteristics related to lactate metabolism across different cell populations, a lactate metabolism-related gene set was compiled and five commonly used scoring strategies—AddModuleScore, ssGSEA, AUCell, UCell, and singscore—were applied to quantitatively assess the metabolic activity of various cell types within the CRC tumor microenvironment. The heterogeneity of lactate metabolism among cell subpopulations was visualized intuitively. The pseudotime trajectory analysis of cell state transitions was inferred based on single-cell gene expression dynamics using the "Monocle 3″ R package. This analysis simulated potential transition paths during tumor cell development or malignant progression. The analysis of cell-cell communication was conducted by constructing ligand-receptor interaction networks across various cell types using the "CellChat" tool.

Processing of CRC spatial sections and spatial cell subpopulation annotation

This study retrieved the spatial transcriptome dataset GSE171351 from the GEO database to serve as an external independent validation cohort. High-resolution whole-transcriptome spatial expression matrices were acquired using the 10x Genomics Visium technology platform. During the data preprocessing stage, the "SCTransform" method within the "Seurat" R package was applied to normalize and stabilize the variance of the raw UMI count matrix, aiming to remove technical variations and enhance the biological comparability of gene expression signals. Subsequently, principal component analysis was performed on the normalized data using the "RunPCA" function to identify transcriptomic subpopulations with similar spatial transcriptional profiles. To further extract biological insights within the spatial context, this study introduced the "stLearn" tool, developed based on the Scanpy framework. This tool integrates prior cell type annotation information, gene co-expression networks, and sample spatial coordinates, and identifies gene modules significantly clustered in specific regions through spatial autocorrelation analysis. Furthermore, leveraging single-cell reference atlases, a Bayesian hierarchical model was applied to deconvolute the spatial transcriptome data, thereby precisely inferring the composition proportions of different cell types at each spatial spot. This analytical strategy effectively identified the heterogeneous spatial distribution of epithelial cells, immune cells, and stromal cells with high and low lactate metabolism features within the tumor tissue. Consequently, it revealed the potential interactive landscape among different cell populations in the tumor microenvironment.

Establishment of a lactate metabolism-related scoring system and its correlation with clinical indicators

Based on the TCGA-CRC cohort, this study employed a stratified random sampling strategy to randomly divide all samples into training and validation sets at a 7:3 ratio. First, univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was performed using relevant R packages for survival analysis to systematically screen candidate genes significantly associated with patient overall survival (OS) (significance threshold p < 0.05). To optimize the model structure and prevent overfitting, Lasso regression was further used for feature dimensionality reduction of the aforementioned genes. The optimal penalty coefficient λ was determined through 1000 cross-validations, thereby selecting the most stable gene set contributing to prognosis. Subsequently, key genes for constructing the multivariate Cox proportional hazards model were finalized by combining stepwise multivariate regression analysis. Based on this model, the Lactate Metabolism Score (LMscore) was calculated for each patient. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were plotted, and the log-rank test was used to compare survival differences between high and low LMscore groups. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were drawn to evaluate the predictive accuracy of the model at different time points. Finally, a nomogram integrating three independent prognostic factors—LMscore, patient age, and clinical stage—was constructed. To further validate the model's stability, the Bootstrap resampling method was used for internal validation, and the consistency between the model-predicted survival rates and the actually observed survival rates was assessed via calibration curves.

Characterization of the immune microenvironment in CRC patients stratified by LMscore

To systematically interrogate the tumor microenvironment (TME) architecture of CRC patients stratified by LMscore, we leveraged the ESTIMATE algorithm. Stromal scores, immune scores, and tumor purity were derived for each patient based on the expression of 141 stromal-related and 225 immune-related genes. This computational framework infers the relative abundance of stromal and immune compartments within tumor specimens and estimates the proportion of neoplastic cells in the tissue. To refine the resolution of immune cell infiltration across LMscore strata, we integrated deconvolution outputs from seven state-of-the-art immune profiling algorithms—including CIBERSORT, MCP-counter, and XCELL—sourced from the TIMER 2.0 platform. Complementarily, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was deployed to quantify the relative enrichment of 28 distinct immune cell types or functional states. The TIDE computational framework was implemented to interrogate potential immune evasion mechanisms and predict sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy. All analytical results were visualized using the ggplot2 package to generate high-resolution representations of multidimensional immune characteristics.

Construction and validation of the crc-caf co-culture model

The experimental section employed the human colorectal cancer (CRC) cell line HCT116 and human CRC-derived cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), both sourced from the Cell Resource Center of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. HCT116 cells were cultured in high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), whereas CAFs were maintained in F12K medium supplemented with 1% penicillin–streptomycin to preserve sterility. All cultures were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂ and subcultured every 2–3 days. For the Transwell co-culture model, log-phase proliferating HCT116 cells were plated into the upper chamber of Transwell inserts, while CAFs were seeded in the lower chamber. To mitigate serum-related confounding effects, the co-culture system was equilibrated in serum-free medium for 24 h, followed by exchange to reduced-serum medium containing 1% FBS for an additional 72 h to collect cells or supernatants for downstream analyses.

Generation of stable sh-COX15-transfected cell lines

To generate a stable COX15-knockdown cellular model, a lentiviral shRNA vector system targeting the COX15 gene, provided by GenePharma, was deployed, with a non-targeting shRNA lentivirus functioning as the negative control. Prior to transduction, HCT116 cells were plated in 6-well plates at a density of 5 × 10⁵ cells per well. Upon achieving 60% confluence, transduction was executed using Lipofectamine 3000 according to the manufacturer's protocol. Forty-eight hours following transduction, puromycin was added to the culture medium at a final concentration of 2 μg/mL to commence selection of stably transduced cells. This selection spanned 14 days, during which the puromycin-containing medium was renewed on a regular schedule to eliminate non-transduced cells, ultimately yielding a stable COX15-knockdown cell line expressing the target shRNA.

Assessment of cell proliferation using the CCK-8 assay

Cell proliferation under diverse experimental conditions was assessed using the CCK-8 assay. HCT116 cells were suspended and standardized to a density of 5 × 10³ cells/mL, then plated into 96-well plates at 100 μL per well, with six replicate wells per condition to ensure robust statistical representation. 10 μL of CCK-8 reagent was introduced into each well, followed by a 2-hour incubation period at 37 °C in darkness. Absorbance at 450 nm was quantified using a multifunctional microplate reader. Cell growth curves were generated with culture time on the x-axis and optical density values on the y-axis, and a four-parameter logistic equation was employed to model dynamic proliferation profiles and quantify intergroup differences.

Statistical analysis

All data processing and statistical analyses were executed within R environment. Key R packages utilized are listed in the Methods section above, and the entire analytical workflow rigorously complied with FAIR data management principles. Statistical significance was consistently represented using asterisks: * for P < 0.05, ** for P < 0.01, and *** for P < 0.001, ns for not significant.

Results

Defining a lactate metabolism signature in the colorectal tumor microenvironment

To deconvolute the complexity of the CRC microenvironment, we leveraged single-cell RNA sequencing data. Following rigorous quality control and dimensionality reduction to mitigate technical noise, we resolved major cellular constituents, including monocytes, macrophages, plasma cells, epithelial cells, T/NK cells, B cells, endothelial cells, and fibroblasts (Fig. 1A-C). The COAD tumor microenvironment (TME) exhibited a comparatively balanced distribution of these cell types, suggesting a homeostatic state that may underpin TME stability and tumor progression. Annotation of cellular identities was grounded in established lineage-specific marker genes, whose expression profiles are visualized in Fig. 1D and E. Substantial inter-sample heterogeneity in cellular abundance was observed, reflecting the complex and variable nature of the TME. A cornerstone of TME pathobiology is metabolic reprogramming, with lactate metabolism emerging as a focal point due to its connection to the Warburg effect. To quantitatively assess lactate metabolic activity at single-cell resolution, we implemented five complementary algorithms: AddModuleScore, UCell, GSVA, AUCell, and Singscore. Interrogation of the resulting scores uncovered marked cell-type-specific variation. Mast cells, myeloid cells, and epithelial cells consistently displayed elevated lactate metabolism. Lactate metabolism remodels the cellular communication network in CRC

To delineate the influence of lactate metabolism on epithelial cell dynamics, we performed pseudotime trajectory inference on epithelial subsets. This analysis partitioned their transcriptional continuum into five distinct states (Fig. 2A-C). Clusters 3 and 13 were predominantly localized to the trajectory endpoint and enriched in tumor cells, indicative of a terminal differentiation phenotype. Stratifying epithelial cells based on the median LMscore revealed significant disparities in the activity of classical signaling pathways, including Estrogen response and Th1/Th2 cell differentiation, between high and low LMscore groups (Fig. 2D). These pathway alterations were most evident within the context of fibroblast and macrophage interactions, pointing to a broader rewiring of cellular crosstalk driven by lactate metabolism. Subsequent reconstruction of the cell-cell communication network using these data demonstrated that high-LMscore epithelial cells not only received intensified signaling inputs from fibroblasts and endothelial cells but also exhibited heightened autocrine and paracrine signaling to these populations and myeloid cells (Fig. 2E). Notably, ligand-receptor pairs EFNA1-EPHA3, BMP7-BMPR2, and TGFA-EGFR constituted the most robust interactions connecting fibroblasts and high-LMscore epithelial cells, nominating them as key mechanistic mediators of this metabolic-stromal crosstalk (Fig. 2F). Integration with single-cell reference data enabled the unambiguous identification of 13 transcriptionally discrete cell subpopulations within CRC tissue sections (Fig. 3A and B). Systematic profiling of metabolic activity using the "scMetabolism" package revealed pervasive enrichment of Pyruvate and Sulfur metabolism pathways across most spatial domains (Fig. 3C). Coincidentally, these regions also displayed elevated activity in Nitrogen metabolism and Arginine and proline metabolism (Fig. 3D and F). A clinically actionable lactate metabolism signature stratifies CRC patients

The established link between lactate metabolism and TME dysfunction prompted us to develop a quantitative metric, the LMscore, to capture this transcriptomic heterogeneity across patients. We derived the LMscore by integrating lactate metabolism gene sets with differentially expressed genes from epithelial cells stratified by LMscore. Subsequent refinement via univariate Cox and Lasso regression pinpointed a core 4-gene signature (COX15, SLC25A13, COX10, MPC1) (Fig. 4A and B). Patients dichotomized by the median LMscore exhibited starkly different outcomes, with the high-LMscore cohort demonstrating significantly shorter overall survival in both training and validation sets (Fig. 4C-E). The prognostic power of the LMscore was confirmed by time-dependent ROC analysis (5-year AUC=0.647; Fig. 4F), and it effectively stratified patients into discrete risk categories (Fig. 4G and H). Multivariate analysis established the LMscore as an independent prognostic factor (Fig. 5A and B). A clinically implementable nomogram incorporating the LMscore accurately predicted survival probabilities (Fig. 5C and D). Furthermore, a comprehensive nomogram integrating the LMscore with standard clinical variables achieved superior predictive accuracy, AUC=0.825 (Fig. 5E), underscoring its translational utility. LMscore heterogeneity governs immune cell infiltration patterns

Accumulating evidence indicates that dysregulated lactate metabolism fundamentally restructures the cellular architecture of the colorectal cancer tumor microenvironment. Recognizing the TME's established role as a determinant of clinical trajectory and therapeutic vulnerability, we systematically mapped the immune infiltration landscape across LMscore-stratified patients. Application of complementary deconvolution algorithms revealed fundamentally divergent immune activation patterns. Tumors harboring high LMScores displayed conspicuous enrichment of canonical immunosuppressive populations—notably M2-polarized macrophages, cancer-associated fibroblasts, and regulatory T cells—consistent with an immune-excluded phenotype. Conversely, robust adaptive immune engagement, characterized by T and B cell responses, predominated in the low-LMscore cohort (Fig. 6A). Global assessment of TME composition via the ESTIMATE algorithm further confirmed significant elevation of stromal and immune scores in high-LMscore tumors (Fig. 6B). Projection of immunotherapy responsiveness using the TIDE framework demonstrated that high-LMscore patients exhibited elevated TIDE, Dysfunction, and Exclusion scores, collectively portending a higher probability of innate resistance to immune checkpoint blockade (Fig. 6C-E). High-resolution profiling of immune cell states and functional pathways via ssGSEA quantification further substantiated profoundly discordant immune phenotypes between the subgroups (Fig. 6F and G). Lactate metabolic reprogramming as a pivotal determinant of immunotherapy response

While tumor mutational burden serves as an established biomarker of immunotherapy response, reflecting genomic instability and neoantigen burden, its relationship with metabolic reprogramming remains elusive. Our analysis revealed a positive association between TMB and lactate metabolism, with high-LMscore tumors exhibiting significantly elevated mutational loads, suggestive of concomitant genomic instability (Fig. 7A). Characterization of the mutational landscape across risk subgroups identified key driver genes—including SYNE1 and MUC16—with significantly higher mutation frequencies in high-LMscore patients, potentially linking their pathogenesis to lactate-driven metabolic dysregulation (Fig. 7B). Stratification based on combined TMB and LMscore thresholds revealed that patients with co-occurring high TMB and high LMscore experienced the most unfavorable outcomes, indicating that LMscore-associated immunosuppression can dominate over the putative benefits of high antigenicity (Fig. 7C and D). These findings position lactate metabolism as a critical source of immunotherapy heterogeneity and establish LMscore as a complementary predictive biomarker. COX15 functions as a central mediator of immunosuppressive metabolism

Given COX15′s prominence within our lactate-reprogramming signature, we investigated its functional role in shaping the immunosuppressive niche. Pan-cohort analysis established that COX15 expression correlated positively with fibroblast infiltration and inversely with cytotoxic immune cell abundance, confirming its association with an immune-cold TME (Fig. 8A-E). Assessment of independent immunotherapy cohorts demonstrated COX15′s consistent predictive power for treatment resistance (AUC > 0.6), validating its biomarker utility (Fig. 8F). TGF-β is chiefly released by malignant epithelium and activated fibroblasts and is a canonical driver of immune exclusion. We therefore asked whether COX15⁺ CAFs govern TGF-β availability. ELISA of conditioned medium showed that CAFs secrete 3-fold more active TGF-β than matched normal fibroblasts; siCOX15 reduced TGF-β output to baseline (Fig. 8G and H). To translate these findings into functional read-outs, we established four co-culture arms: HCT116 alone, HCT116 + CAF-CM, HCT116 + CAF-siCOX15-CM, and HCT116 + CAFs. CCK-8 assays revealed that CAF-CM significantly boosted HCT116 proliferation, an effect abolished when CM was derived from siCOX15-CAFs (Fig. 8I). Discussion

CRC, ranking as the third most globally prevalent malignancy, orchestrates a uniquely configured immunological niche that diverges fundamentally from immunologically "hot" tumors such as melanoma or non-small cell lung cancer. This distinct microenvironment manifests as sparse tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density and attenuated expression of immune checkpoint molecules, collectively accounting for CRC's suboptimal immunotherapeutic responsiveness [18]. Critically, recent paradigm-shifting evidence refutes the notion of CRC as uniformly "immune-cold," instead revealing a complex metabolic landscape where aberrant lactate metabolism functions as a master regulator of tumor progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance. Our integrative analysis demonstrates that CRC patients stratified by elevated LMscore exhibit a profoundly immunosuppressive microenvironment, characterized by synergistic infiltration of M2-polarized macrophages and regulatory T cells (Tregs), concomitant with T cell functional exhaustion [19]. While this profile aligns with the classical "immune-cold" phenotype, our work uncovers its metabolic etiology: dysregulated lactate metabolism directly orchestrates immunosuppressive niche formation through the reprogramming of intercellular communication networks [20,21].

Among the multifaceted metabolic reprogramming pathways in cancer, lactate metabolism possesses unparalleled therapeutic relevance due to its dimensionality in immune modulation and clinical translatability [22]. Crucially, unlike glucose or glutamine metabolism, lactate metabolism directly interfaces with immune cell functionality, establishing a definitive "metabolism-immunity axis" [22,23]. High-LMscore cohorts manifest a tripartite pathological signature characterized by a dominantly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment typified by M2 macrophage and regulatory T cell infiltration alongside T cell dysfunction, a robust correlation with advanced clinical stages (III/IV) indicative of disease progression, and a paradoxical elevation in tumor mutational burden (TMB) concomitant with markedly diminished immunotherapy response rates. These integrated features align with established clinical associations wherein hyperlactatemia correlates with aggressive metastatic progression and adverse prognostic outcomes, collectively underscoring the metabolic underpinnings of immunotherapy resistance in colorectal cancer [24]. Mechanistically, elevated LMscore drives CAFs trans-differentiation, wherein CAFs secrete immunosuppressive chemokines (e.g., CCL15) to construct a physical and functional barrier against immune cell trafficking. Notably, this TME signature mirrors pancreatic cancer's metabolic phenotype, suggesting conserved mechanisms across "immunologically desolate" malignancies. Distinctively, CRC-specific lactate dysregulation associates with somatic alterations in SYNE1 and MUC16, providing tumor-agnostic therapeutic insights.

Although lactate metabolism directly drives tumor malignancy, the impact of high LMscore on immunotherapy is dichotomous. High LMscore directly compromises immunotherapy efficacy, evidenced by elevated TIDE, Dysfunction, and Exclusion scores. Conversely, it identifies actionable targets for novel combinatorial therapeutic strategies. Our analysis demonstrates that high-LMscore patients exhibit significantly lower immunotherapy response rates than low-LMscore patients, consistent with established evidence linking elevated lactate levels to immunotherapy resistance. Targeting lactate metabolism may reverse the immunosuppressive microenvironment and enhance immunotherapy efficacy. Notably, high-LMscore patients with high TMB levels display poor immunotherapy responses, indicating that TMB alone is insufficient as a biomarker and metabolic features must be integrated for precise stratification. Lactate metabolism-targeting approaches, including inhibition of lactate transporters (MCT4) or lactate-producing enzymes (LDHA), reduce microenvironmental lactate, restore T cell function, and thereby improve immunotherapy efficacy [24,25]. TMB and MSI are established immunotherapy response biomarkers, with high TMB typically correlating with favorable responses and MSI-H tumors showing superior immune checkpoint inhibitor efficacy [26,27]. However, these biomarkers exhibit limitations in CRC: approximately 75% of CRC cases are MSI-L or MSS subtypes with limited immunotherapy response [28,29]. CRC generally exhibits low TMB, and coexistence of high TMB with high LMscore further diminishes immunotherapy response rates. Our study reveals a synergistic antagonism between TMB and LMscore: high-LMscore patients harbor elevated TMB yet demonstrate reduced immunotherapy responses, indicating that aberrant lactate metabolism may override the immunogenic advantage of high TMB. Consequently, integrating TMB and LMscore may yield a more accurate immunotherapy response prediction model.

Our study further establishes a direct link between lactate metabolism and intercellular communication. High-LMscore epithelial cells exhibit significantly enhanced interactions with fibroblasts and endothelial cells, mediated by key ligand-receptor pairs (EFNA1-EPHA3, BMP7-BMPR2, TGFA-EGFR). These findings align with lactate metabolism mechanisms: lactate acidifies the microenvironment, promoting fibroblast activation into CAFs, and regulates gene expression via histone lactylation to modulate ligand-receptor expression [30]. Collectively, these mechanisms establish an immunosuppressive microenvironment in high-LMscore patients. Critically, enhanced epithelial-fibroblast interactions represent a core mechanism by which lactate metabolism abnormalities induce immunosuppression, providing a molecular basis for targeted intervention. During mechanistic exploration, COX15 emerged as a central regulator in the lactate metabolic reprogramming index. COX15, a mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase assembly protein critical for respiratory chain function, is implicated in tumor metabolism through mitochondrial regulation of lactate metabolism [31,32]. Our data confirm COX15 overexpression in CRC with lactate metabolic dysregulation, positively correlating with fibroblast infiltration and negatively with immune effector cells. COX15 targeting may reduce tumor microenvironment lactate and restore T cell function. Experimental validation demonstrates that COX15 downregulation significantly reduces TGF-β secretion and tumor proliferation, positioning COX15 as a key target to reverse lactate metabolic disorders and overcome immunosuppression. Compared to conventional immunotherapy, COX15-targeted strategies offer enhanced specificity and reduced toxicity, presenting a promising direction for CRC immunotherapy.

In summary, our study systematically delineates the pivotal role of lactate metabolism in the CRC immune microenvironment, establishing LMscore as a novel indicator for immune microenvironment assessment and immunotherapy response prediction. Future research should prioritize: first, deepening mechanistic understanding of lactate metabolism in immune cell regulation, particularly histone lactylation in immune cells; second, developing novel lactate-targeting therapeutics (e.g., MCT4/LDHA inhibitors) and advancing clinical trials; third, integrating LMscore with TMB, MSI, and other biomarkers to construct a multidimensional prediction model for precise CRC immunotherapy stratification. Ultimately, targeting lactate metabolic reprogramming offers a promising strategy to overcome the "immune-cold" CRC microenvironment, enhance immunotherapy efficacy, and provide new therapeutic hope for CRC patients.
