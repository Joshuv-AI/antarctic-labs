Stabilizer rank bounds for magic-state orbits
Farrokh Labib, Vincent Russo

Abstract
Distinct Clifford orbits of magic states can exhibit different stabilizer ranks at small tensor powers. We establish this for qutrits, where the single-qutrit Clifford group has four inequivalent orbits of magic states — Strange, Norrell, Hadamard-eigenstate, and the qutrit T T -state — but a nontrivial upper bound on the asymptotic exponent had been pinned down for only the qutrit T T -state. For the other three orbits we give explicit stabilizer decompositions, yielding upper bounds on the per-copy asymptotic stabilizer-rank exponent: γ 𝕊 ≤ log 3 ⁡ ( 2 ) / 2 ≈ 0.316 \gamma_{\mathbb{S}}\leq\log_{3}(2)/2\approx 0.316 for the Strange state, and γ H 3 , γ ℕ ≤ log 3 ⁡ ( 4 ) / 3 ≈ 0.421 \gamma_{H_{3}},\gamma_{\mathbb{N}}\leq\log_{3}(4)/3\approx 0.421 for the Hadamard-eigenstate and Norrell orbits, all strictly below the prior γ T 3 ≤ 1 / 2 \gamma_{T_{3}}\leq 1/2 baseline. We also prove the first nontrivial Ω ⁡ ( m / log ⁡ m ) \Omega(m/\log m) asymptotic lower bounds for the Hadamard-eigenstate and Norrell orbits, and exhibit two-qutrit Clifford circuits that convert two copies of these states into an injectable phase state with constant success probability, enabling constant-overhead injection of one non-Clifford diagonal gate per orbit. In the case of qubits, we give a closed-form decomposition of the qubit T T -type orbit at four copies matching the existing γ T ≤ log 2 ⁡ ( 3 ) / 4 ≈ 0.396 \gamma_{T}\leq\log_{2}(3)/4\approx 0.396 exponent via a direct algebraic identity rather than an entangled cat-state construction. An open-source library stabrank accompanies the paper, with Lean 4 proof formalizations of all the decompositions.

Introduction

Sharp bounds on the classical-simulation cost of Clifford-dominated
circuits play a dual role: a fast simulator on a family of circuits
rules out a quantum speedup on that family, while a provable lower
bound on every simulator certifies one. Stabilizer-rank methods sit
at the heart of this story — they reduce the strong-simulation
cost of a Clifford circuit with magic-state ancillae to a single
quantity, the stabilizer rank of the input magic register, and
improvements at any copy count translate directly into faster
simulators. The underlying universal model factors an
n
-qudit
circuit into Clifford gates, which are classically simulable in
polynomial time
[

1
]
,
and non-Clifford magic-state ancillae
[
8
]
,
which supply the resource needed for universal quantum computation.

The relevant invariant is the magic-state stabilizer rank

\chi(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes t})

states whose linear span contains
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes t}

Asymptotic lower bounds

The non-uniform amplitudes of

adapted from the qubit-

of

\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle

, so that the

Proposition 4
.

Let
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle=a_{0}\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}0\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle+a_{1}\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}1\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle+a_{2}\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}2\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{3}

be a single-qutrit state for which there exist indices

i,j\in\{0,1,2\}
with
a_{i}\neq 0
,
a_{j}\neq 0
, and

|a_{i}|/|a_{j}|\geq 2
. Then

\chi\!\left(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes m}\right)\geq\frac{m+1}{3\log_{2}(m+1)}\in\Omega\!\left(\frac{m}{\log m}\right).

(11)

In particular,

\chi(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes m}),\chi(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes m})\in\Omega(m/\log m)
.

Proof.

A qutrit stabilizer state
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\sigma\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
on
m
qutrits has the
canonical form

\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\sigma\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle=3^{-k/2}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{k}}\omega^{Q(y)}\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}x_{0}+Wy\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle,

(12)

for some
k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
, affine offset
x_{0}
, isometric
embedding
W\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{m\times k}
, and quadratic form

Q:\mathbb{F}_{3}^{k}\to\mathbb{F}_{3}
, with
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
. Each
coordinate of
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\sigma\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
therefore lies in

\{0,3^{-k/2}\omega^{j}:j\in\{0,1,2\}\}
. Given a
decomposition

\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes m}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\sigma_{i}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
of length

r=\chi(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes m})
, write
\tilde{c}_{i}=3^{-k_{i}/2}c_{i}
;
then for every coordinate
x\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{m}
,

\langle x|\psi^{\otimes m}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\varepsilon_{i,x}\tilde{c}_{i}.

(13)

The coefficients
\varepsilon_{i,x}
take values in

\{0,1,\omega,\omega^{2}\}
, so each coordinate of

\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes m}
is a subset-sum of the
3r
-tuple

(\tilde{c}_{i},\omega\tilde{c}_{i},\omega^{2}\tilde{c}_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
,
yielding a subset-sum representation of length
3r
of the
coordinate vector. The factor of
3
counts the three cube-roots of
unity
\{1,\omega,\omega^{2}\}
appearing as
\varepsilon_{i,x}

values; the qubit version of this argument lifts each

\tilde{c}_{i}
to four entries

\{\pm\tilde{c}_{i},\pm i\tilde{c}_{i}\}
and yields a
4r
-tuple
instead. By the subset-sum lower bound
of
[
27
, Theorem 3.1]
, any subset-sum
representation of a
q
-tuple containing an exponentially
increasing subsequence of length
\ell
(i.e.,
\ell
entries whose
absolute values each at least double the previous) has length at
least
\ell/\log_{2}\ell
. Hence
3r\geq\ell/\log_{2}\ell
,
giving
\chi(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes m})\geq\ell/(3\log_{2}\ell)
.

It remains to produce an exponentially increasing subsequence of
length
\ell=m+1
in the coordinates of
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle^{\otimes m}
.
By hypothesis, there exist indices
i_{a},i_{b}\in\{0,1,2\}
with

a=|a_{i_{a}}|
,
b=|a_{i_{b}}|
,
a\geq 2b>0
. For each

k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
, set

x^{(k)}=(\underbrace{i_{a},\ldots,i_{a}}_{k},\underbrace{i_{b},\ldots,i_{b}}_{m-k})\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{m},\qquad\text{so that}\qquad\bigl|\langle x^{(k)}|\psi^{\otimes m}\rangle\bigr|=a^{k}b^{m-k}.

(14)

The
m+1
values
\{a^{k}b^{m-k}\}_{k=0}^{m}
are distinct
and form an exponentially increasing sequence of consecutive ratio

a/b\geq 2
, so we may take
\ell=m+1

in (
11
).
∎

The proof above is a direct adaptation of the qubit-
T
-state
argument of
[

: the only
technical change is the factor of

in place of

subset-sum coefficient count, accounting for cube-roots of unity in
the qutrit canonical form rather than fourth-roots. What is new
here is the state-specific verification: which qutrit orbits
satisfy the modulus-ratio hypothesis (
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
and
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle

The stabilizer-rank exponents of Section

deterministic injection gadget
: a fixed entangling two-qutrit

via Clifford byproducts. For

the diagonal qutrit

\mathrm{diag}(1,\omega_{9},\omega_{9}^{2})
supplies one, so

\gamma_{T_{3}}\leq 1/2
is a circuit-runtime bound.

For the three non-
T_{3}
orbits, standard single-shot injection
via a diagonal gate requires the magic state to be a

phase state
(i.e. have uniform amplitudes up to
phases)
[
2
,
19
,
11
]
. Since
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{S}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
,
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
, and
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
all
have non-uniform amplitudes, this route is
closed
[
33
,
32
]
.

3.1
Two-copy probabilistic conversion

A milder
construction
[
8
,
2
]
,
introduced for the qubit phase state
(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}0\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle+e^{i\pi/4}\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}1\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle)/\sqrt{2}

(the standard “T-state” of the qubit distillation literature, in
our convention an
H
-type representative) and adapted to the
qutrit Hadamard-eigenstate, instead feeds two copies of
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle

into a two-qutrit Clifford, measures the second qutrit, and
recovers a Clifford-equivalent phase state on the first qutrit on
a designated branch. The recovered phase state is
then injected via the standard single-shot gadget. The resulting
pipeline consumes
2/q
copies of
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
in expectation per
non-Clifford gate, where
q
is the branch success probability:
an
O(1)
ancilla overhead provided (a) such a Clifford exists
and (b) the recovered phase state implements a non-Clifford gate
under standard injection. The phase state that emerges in
Theorem
6
below implements one specific
non-Clifford diagonal gate per orbit (the
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
and
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle

gates listed in (
16
) and (
19
)); reaching other
non-Clifford diagonal targets requires an additional
gate-synthesis step on top of the two-copy primitive.

A complete enumeration over
\mathrm{Sp}(4,\mathbb{F}_{3})\times\mathbb{F}_{3}
(the
choice of Clifford and choice of measurement outcome, modulo the
Heisenberg–Weyl prefactor as in
Appendix
A
) settles the existence question
for every non-
T_{3}
orbit:

Theorem 6
.

For
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\in\{\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle,\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\}
there exist two-qutrit
Cliffords
C
and measurement branches
k
such that

\langle\mspace{0.5mu}k\mspace{0.5mu}\rvert_{\mathrm{anc}}C(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\otimes\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle)
is proportional
to a phase state whose standard single-shot injection yields a
non-Clifford diagonal gate. For
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle=\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{S}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
no such
protocol exists: every
C
that maps
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{S}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\otimes\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{S}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle

to a phase state injects a Clifford gate.

Proof.

By exhaustive search over the symplectic-quotient representation (Lemma
7
), one identifies valid protocols for
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
and
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
. The raw search outputs the following two-qutrit Cliffords:

\displaystyle C_{\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle}

\displaystyle=H_{1}\mathrm{SUM}H_{1}\mathrm{SUM}H_{1}S_{2}\mathrm{SUM}H_{1}H_{2}^{2},

(15)

\displaystyle C_{\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle}

\displaystyle=H_{1}H_{2}\mathrm{SUM}H_{1}^{\dagger}H_{2}\mathrm{SUM}H_{1}.

Hadamard-eigenstate protocol.

Let
N=\sqrt{3-\sqrt{3}}
and
c=(\sqrt{3}-1)/2
(the same constants used throughout Appendix
C.2
). The two-copy input state is
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\otimes\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
, where the single-copy Hadamard-eigenstate is
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\big(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}0\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle+c\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}1\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle+c\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}2\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\big)
. Applying the two-qutrit Clifford
C_{\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle}
to this input state and projecting the ancilla (leg 2) onto the outcome
k=1
yields the data-qutrit output vector

\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi_{k=1}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle=\langle\mspace{0.5mu}1\mspace{0.5mu}\rvert_{\mathrm{anc}}C_{\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle}(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\otimes\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle)=\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{3}}\big(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}0\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle+e^{i\pi/2}\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}1\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle+e^{i\pi/3}\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}2\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\big),

(16)

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is a normalization scalar satisfying
|\lambda|^{2}=3/8
. Since all three amplitudes of the post-selected state have equal modulus, it is a phase state. The relative phases are
\arg(\psi_{1}/\psi_{0})=\pi/2
and
\arg(\psi_{2}/\psi_{0})=\pi/3
, corresponding to the single-qutrit diagonal gate
\mathrm{diag}(1,i,e^{i\pi/3})
. Since a single-qutrit diagonal Clifford gate must have phases that are multiples of
2\pi/3
, the phase
\pi/2
certifies that the injected gate is non-Clifford.

Norrell protocol.

The two-copy input state is
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\otimes\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
, where
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}0\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle+\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}1\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle-2\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}2\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle)
in the computational basis. First, the gate
H_{1}
acts on the data qutrit as the qutrit Fourier transform, leaving the ancilla unchanged. On the amplitudes
v_{a,b}=c_{N}(a)c_{N}(b)/6
(where
c_{N}=(1,1,-2)^{T}
), the data Fourier transform produces

\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\Psi_{1}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle=(H_{1}\otimes\mathbb{1})(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\otimes\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle)=\frac{1}{6\sqrt{3}}\sum_{j,b}\hat{c}(j)c_{N}(b)\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}j,b\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle,

(17)

where the inner sum evaluates using
1+\omega+\omega^{2}=0
as

\hat{c}(j)=\sum_{a=0}^{2}\omega^{aj}c_{N}(a)=1+\omega^{j}-2\omega^{2j}.

(18)

This evaluates to
\hat{c}(0)=0
,
\hat{c}(1)=-3\omega^{2}
, and
\hat{c}(2)=-3\omega
. Thus, after
H_{1}
, only the data components with
j\in\{1,2\}
survive.

Applying the remaining gates of
C_{\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle}
and projecting onto the ancilla outcome
k=0
yields

\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\psi_{k=0}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle=\langle\mspace{0.5mu}0\mspace{0.5mu}\rvert_{\mathrm{anc}}C_{\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle}(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\otimes\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle)=\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{3}}\big(\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}0\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle-\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}1\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle-\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}2\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\big),

(19)

with
|\mu|^{2}=1/4
, confirming a success probability of
1/4
to obtain a phase state. The relative phases are both
\pi
, defining the injected diagonal gate
\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1)
. Because
\pi
is not a multiple of
2\pi/3
, this gate is non-Clifford.

Finally, for
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle=\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{S}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
, an exhaustive search confirms that no two-copy conversion protocol exists: every
(C,k)
pair that maps
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{S}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\otimes\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{S}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
to a phase state yields relative phases restricted to the Clifford-trivial ones.
∎

Unlike the qubit case
[
8
, Sec. III]
, where the Hadamard gate naturally pair-conjugates real amplitudes to equalize their moduli, the qutrit Fourier transform has no such conjugate-pairing property. Modulus equalization for
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
and
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle
therefore requires the longer alternating sequences of Hadamards, SUMs, and phase gates in (
15
).

Operationally, Theorem
6
provides a constant-overhead route from copies of
\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}M\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\in\{\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}H_{3}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle,\lvert\mspace{0.5mu}\mathbb{N}\mspace{0.5mu}\rangle\}
to one specific non-Clifford diagonal gate per orbit (
