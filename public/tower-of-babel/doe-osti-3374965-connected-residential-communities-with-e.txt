a. What are the major goals and objectives of this project?
 • The goal of the project is to demonstrate the reliability, resiliency, cost-effectiveness, and
 benefits of connected community microgrids to both customers and the electric Utility
 when (1) grid connected by the strategic deployment of distributed energy resources (DERs)
 with a control paradigm to manage demand flexibility, energy efficiency, and grid services,
 and (2) islanded in the event of a grid outage with different tiers of grid-edge hierarchy. To
 meet the goal of the project, the following objectives will be met: (1) design a connected
 nested microgrid electrical system within and between two communities, (2) implement a
 controller and control strategy capable of determining optimized system configuration
 during both grid-connected and islanded operation, (3) explore regulatory issues associated
 with connected microgrids, (4) build and commission the connected community microgrids,
 (5) demonstrate various use cases, and
 (6) analyze technical, economic and customer acceptance from the data collected.
 •
b. What was accomplished under these goals?
 1. Major activities.
 • Vehicle-to-Home (V2H) / Vehicle-to-Load (V2L) / V2G Activities
 i. Conducted V2H and V2L preparation and testing at the model home and at the
 UCI Grid Evolution Laboratory.
 ii. Deployed EV9 vehicles to six homeowners and installed temporary Level 2
 chargers; collected baseline energy-use data.
 iii. Completed V2L demonstrations, including tests in preparation for the Secretary
 of Energy visit.
 iv. Developed V2H simulation models and provided technical inputs for controller
 development.
 v. Collected V2H/V2G interest surveys from homeowners.
 • Microgrid Design, Engineering, and Interconnection Work
 i. Updated microgrid use cases, controller specifications, and communication
 plans between the microgrid controller, SunVault systems, and smart panels.
 ii. Completed multiple engineering analyses, including inrush/blackstart
 evaluations.
 iii. Received updated Concept of Operations (ConOps) and business requirements
 documents from SCE; incorporated recommended point lists and interface
 definitions.
 iv. Responded to multiple rounds of SCE data requests to support its islanding
 study.
 v. Finalized the microgrid study agreement between SunPower and SCE.
 vi. Achieved draft and subsequent revisions of the cybersecurity plan with
 alignment to utility standards.

Section 6. Changes/Problems:
The PD/PI is reminded that the grantee is required to obtain prior written approval from the Contracting
Officer whenever there are significant changes in the project or its direction. Requests for prior written
approval must be submitted to the Contracting Officer. If not previously reported in writing, provide the
following additional information, if applicable: Changes in approach and reasons for change; Actual or
anticipated problems or delays and actions or plans to resolve them; Changes that have a significant
impact on expenditures; Significant changes in use or care of animals, human subjects, and/or
biohazards.
a. Changes in approach and reasons for change.
 • Over the course of the project, the team implemented several adjustments to the project
 approach due to contracting delays, revised technical findings, and organizational
 changes. A no-cost extension for Budget Period 2 was approved, which required shifting
 the timelines for Budget Periods 3 and 4. Contracting delays with SCE, Schneider, and UCI
 postponed microgrid-related activities by approximately five months, prompting an
 extension of Budget Period 1 to preserve the original go/no-go milestones.
 • Additionally, SunPower underwent multiple corporate and financial disruptions—
 including the loss of its auditor, restructuring actions, and withdrawal of SEC filings—
 requiring DOE, SunPower, and UCI to initiate the process of transferring prime recipient
 responsibilities to UCI to maintain project continuity. Updated engineering information
 from SCE and revised component lead times also required adjustments to project
 sequencing and planning.
b. Actual or anticipated problems or delays and actions or plans to resolve them.
 • Several challenges affected project timing. SCE’s interconnection review for the
 community microgrid took longer than anticipated, extending the expected timeline by
 an additional 6–9 months. Microgrid contracting delays similarly pushed engineering
 activities back by roughly five months.
 • Equipment delays also emerged, including extended lead times for the community
 battery (estimated at ~12 months) and supply-chain issues affecting control relay
 delivery.
 • Financial uncertainty at SunPower also introduced project-administration risks. To
 mitigate possible disruption, the project team began planning for a transfer of prime
 recipient responsibilities to UCI.
 • Where possible, tasks were parallelized to reduce scheduling impact, and updated
 timelines were incorporated into revised SOPO and budget documentation.
c. Changes that have a significant impact on expenditures.
 • The project team completed a comprehensive budget update to reflect actual costs,
 revised engineering estimates, and lessons learned as the project matured. Adjustments
 included both increases and decreases across categories such as battery design,
 trenching and wiring, cybersecurity testing, OhmConnect program fees, relay costs, EVSE
 procurement, SunVault usage charges, and personnel costs.
