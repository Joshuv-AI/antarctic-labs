Project Overview

There is a national-level need for high fidelity measurements of material performance in
relevant hypersonic environments. This is true not just for design and development of next
generation materials but for understanding the failure mechanisms of currently available
materials as well. To answer this call, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) has built
the Energy Matter Interaction Tunnel (EMIT), a highly diagnosed facility with a suite of high-end
non-invasive material diagnostics. In order to fully diagnose material failure mechanisms, state
of the art gas-phase diagnostics were developed and deployed allowing for an unprecedented
understanding of how various materials fail in both nominal and off-nominal hypersonic flows.

Optical Emission Spectroscopy (OES) was fielded in hundreds of tests to characterize the gas-
phase emissions and spectral signatures of numerous materials. Two new diagnostics were
developed to measure absolute species concentrations; Laser Induced Fluorescence (LIF), and
Ultrafast Laser Absorption (ULA). This includes development of complementary models of
molecular response for each diagnostic, validation of the models with calibration cells, and
demonstration of the diagnostics in hypersonic flows. Carbon monoxide (CO) was targeted for
demonstration of the diagnostics, and the time-dependent formation of CO from the ablation
of graphite was measured. These results provide valuable data for numerical model validation.
With successful demonstration of these diagnostics for CO measurement, they can be further
utilized as part of the full EMIT diagnostics suite for characterization of other species.

Mission Impact

The LIF and ULA diagnostics stood up during this project allow for quantification of gas-phase
species and temperatures in material ablation conditions. Developing these diagnostics also
resulted in the safety certification of a testing apparatus for CO and NO use. Diagnostics were
demonstrated for measurements of CO and can be extended to other species in the future. The
data these diagnostics provide are critical to anchor modeling and simulation capabilities.
Addressing the current limitations fits the Integrated Deterrence and Multi-Domain Deterrence
laboratory missions. The project also resulted in the hiring of a new staff scientist, as well as the
hiring of two postdoctoral researchers which converted to staff during the project.
