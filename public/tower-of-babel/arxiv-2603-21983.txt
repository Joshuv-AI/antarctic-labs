Quadratic flatness and Regularity for Codimension-One Varifolds with Bounded Anisotropic First Variation Part II
Sławomir Kolasiński, Mario Santilli

Abstract
In a previous paper we proved quadratic flatness and 𝒞 2 \mathscr{C}^{2} -rectifiability for codimension one varifolds V V on open subsets of 𝐑 n + 1 \mathbf{R}^{{n+1}} with bounded anisotropic mean curvature, under the hypothesis that spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ \spt\|V\| has locally finite ℋ n \mathscr{H}^{n} measure. In this paper we explore some fundamental consequences of this result. In particular, we extend Brakke’s perpendicularity theorem to the anisotropic setting and we deduce the locality of the anisotropic mean curvature vector.



1
Introduction

In our previous paper
[
KS25
]
we prove quadratic flatness and
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-rectifiability for
the support of
n
-dimensional varifolds on open subsets of
\mathbf{R}^{{n+1}}
with bounded
anisotropic mean curvature, under the additional hypothesis that the support has locally finite

\mathscr{H}^{n}
measure. In case the varifold
V
is integral and satisfies the absolute continuity
hypothesis
\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits\spt\|V\|\ll\|V\|
, this allows to conclude, in
combination with Allard’s anisotropic regularity theorem
[
All86
]
, that the unit density
layer of
V
is a
\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}
manifold; cf.
[
KS25
, Theorem 1.2]
. The present paper
explores a fundamental consequence of this result, by establishing perpendicularity of the
anisotropic mean curvature vector on this unit-density layer, and compatibility with the approximate
second order structures (i.e. locality of the mean curvature vector).

1.1 Theorem
.

Let
\phi
be a uniformly convex
\mathscr{C}^{3}
-norm on
\mathbf{R}^{{n+1}}
and let

F:\mathbf{G}(n+1,n)\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
be the elliptic integrand naturally associated
with
\phi
(cf.
2.21
). Suppose

\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{{n+1}}
is open,
0\leq H<\infty
and
V
is a

n
-dimensional integral varifold in
\Omega
such that

(1)

\|\delta_{F}V\|\leq H\|V\|

(2)

\displaystyle\text{and $\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits\spt\|V\|$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\|V\|$}\,.

Define
Q=\spt\|V\|\cap\bigl\{a:\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\|V\|,a)=1\bigr\}
.

Then there holds

\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(M,a)=\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(V,a)\in\Nor(M,a)\quad\text{for $\mathscr{H}^{n}$~almost all $a\in Q\cap M$}\,

whenever
M\subseteq\Omega
is an embedded
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-hypersurface with
\mathscr{H}^{n}(M)<\infty
.

The main and most subtle part of the theorem is contained in the assertion about the
perpendicularity of the mean curvature vector
\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(V,\cdot)
. If
F
is the integrand
associated with the Euclidean norm, perpendicularity of the mean curvature vector, for
\|V\|

almost all points, was proved by Brakke in
[
Bra78
, 5.8]
for a general integral varifold

V
whose Euclidean first variation
\delta V
is representable by integration (see also

[
Men13
, Remark 4.9]
). One ingredient of Brakke’s proof is the height decay proved in

[
Bra78
, 5.7]
. In the anisotropic setting, the quadratic flatness proved
[
KS25
]

provides an optimal quadratic height decay for varifolds satisfying the hypothesis of Theorem

1.1
. On the other hand, Brakke’s proof employs the Euclidean structure in a crucial
way, in particular the Pythagorean theorem, to carry on some of the fundamental estimates (cf. Remark
5.3
). Obviously, the latter is an important missing
ingredient in the anisotropic setting. To circumvent this issue, our proof involves the new idea
that one needs to
locally
straighten the anisotropic structure around a point
b
through
a linear map
\varphi_{T}
, depending on the approximate tangent plane
T=\Tan^{k}(\|V\|,b)
of

\|V\|
; see
5.1
. Concretely, this idea translates to studying the first
variation with respect to the integrand
G
of the pushforward
(\varphi_{T}^{-1})_{\#}V
of
V
,
where
G=\varphi_{T}^{\#}F
is given by the pull-back of
F
by the linear map
\varphi_{T}
. A
subtle technical part of this argument requires a special care to deal with the Lebesgue points of
the mean curvature vector of the pulled-back varifold
(\varphi_{T}^{-1})_{\#}V
; see
Lemma
4.3
. We remark that part of this argument actually holds for a
general
\mathscr{C}^{1}
integrand of arbitrary codimension; cf. Theorem
5.2
.

Relying on perpendicularity and quadratic flatness, we can prove prove locality
(see
5.6
) of the mean curvature vector by adapting the ideas of Ambrosio and
Masnou
[
AM03
]
. Carrying this out, however, still involves exploiting peculiarities of
the structure of the anisotropic first variation;
in particular
2.18
. In
[
AM03
]
the quadratic tilt-excess decay is
a consequence of a theorem by Schätzle
[
Sch04
]
, who proved it only for the area
integrand.

We finally comment on the restriction on the unit-density layer in Theorem

1.1
. Indeed, perpendicularity of the
Euclidean
mean curvature vector also
works at higher density points. This is a fundamental consequence of the Euclidean multivalued
Lipschitz approximation, where fast decay estimates are guaranteed by the monotonicity formula.
However, in the anisotropic case the situation becomes far more difficult, since monotonicity
formulas are notoriously not available, and the only available Lipschitz approximation
theorem
[
All86
, §2.6]
seems to provide too weak estimates on the set not covered by the
graph of the constructed Lipschitzian map; see
5.4
. Such a set is
obviously empty if we restrict on the unit-density layer as, by the aforementioned regularity
theorem, this layer is almost covered by one single
\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}
manifold; see

5.4
. We finally point out that perpendicularity of the anisotropic mean
curvature vector in the
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-setting can be easily deduced from the smoothness assumption;
cf.
5.5
.

2.18
Remark

Let

3
Controlling tilt with height

In this section

is the integrand associated to the uniformly convex norm
\phi
as

3.1
.

Recall
[
All72
, 8.9(3)]
and observe

(1)

\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|u-\sgn(u\bullet v)v|=\bigl(1-|u\bullet v|\bigr)^{1/2}=\|S\circ T^{\perp}\|=\|S-T\|\\
\text{whenever
$S,T\in\mathbf{G}_{\natural}({n+1},n)$,
$u,v\in\mathbb{S}^{n}$,
$u\in\ker S$,
and $v\in\ker T$}\,.

3.2 Lemma
.

Let
S,T\in\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)
. There exists
1\leq\Gamma<\infty
depending only
on
\gamma(\phi)
and
c_{2}(\phi)
such that

(1)

\displaystyle\|Q_{F}(T)\|\leq\Gamma\,,\quad\|P_{F}(S)\circ Q_{F}(T)\|+\|P_{F}(S)-P_{F}(T)\|\leq\Gamma\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad P_{F}(S)^{*}\bullet Q_{F}(T)\geq\Gamma^{-1}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,.

Proof.

Let
\nu,\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
be such that
\nu\bullet\eta\geq 0
,
\nu\in T^{\perp}
,
and
\eta\in S^{\perp}
. Note that the first estimate is trivially true because
Q_{F}(T)
is
defined using
\phi(\nu)
and
\mathrm{D}\phi(\nu)
, the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is compact, and

\phi
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
away from the origin. Let
\varphi:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be a
geodesic such that
\varphi(0)=\nu
and
\varphi(l)=\eta
, where

l=\arccos(\eta\bullet\nu)
. Recall
3.1
and note that
since
\eta\bullet\nu\geq 0
there holds

\sqrt{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|=|\eta-\nu|\leq l\leq\tfrac{\pi\sqrt{2}}{4}|\eta-\nu|=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|\,.

Recall the definition of
\beta
from
2.1
and define

f,g:\mathbf{R}\to\mathrm{End}({\mathbf{R}^{{n+1}}})
by

(3)

\displaystyle f(t)=P_{F}(\ker\beta(\varphi(t)))\circ Q_{F}(T)\quad\text{and}\quad g(t)=P_{F}(\ker\beta(\varphi(t)))-P_{F}(T)\quad\text{for $t\in\mathbf{R}$}\,.

Clearly
f(0)=0=g(0)
so

(4)

\displaystyle\|P_{F}(S)\circ Q_{F}(T)\|=\|f(l)-f(0)\|\leq\tfrac{\pi\sqrt{2}}{4}\Lip f\,|\eta-\nu|\leq\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Lip f\,\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|

(5)

\displaystyle\text{and similarly}\quad\|P_{F}(S)-P_{F}(T)\|\leq\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Lip g\,\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|\,.

Since
\Lip f
and
\Lip g
are both controlled by
c_{2}(\phi)
we obtain the second estimate.
To get the third estimate use
[
All86
, 3.2(6)]
together
with
3.1
and write

(6)

\phi(\nu)\phi(\eta)P_{F}(S)^{*}\bullet Q_{F}(T)=\phi(\nu)\phi(\eta)P_{F}^{*}(S)\nu\bullet Q_{F}(T)\nu\\
=\phi(\nu)\phi(\eta)-\bigl(\nu\bullet\nabla\phi(\eta)\bigr)\bigl(\eta\bullet\nabla\phi(\nu)\bigr)\geq\gamma(1-|\eta\bullet\nu|)=\gamma\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,.

This gives a lower bound with constant depending on
c_{0}(\phi)
and
\gamma(\phi)
.
∎

3.4 Lemma

(Caccioppoli-type inequality)
.

Assume

(1)

\displaystyle\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{{n+1}}\quad\text{is open}\,,\quad c\in\Omega\,,\quad T\in\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)\,,\quad V\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(\Omega)\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\text{$F$ is associated to the norm~$\phi$ as in~\ref{mr:anisotropic_mean_curvature_vector}}\,,\quad\|\delta_{F}V\|\quad\text{is Radon}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\zeta:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}\cap\{t:0\leq t\leq 1\}\quad\text{is~smooth}\,,\quad\spt\zeta\quad\text{is compact}\,.

Set
\kappa=\sup\{|\nabla\zeta(x)|:x\in\Omega\}
. There exists
0<\Gamma<\infty
depending only on
\gamma(\phi)
and
c(\phi)
such that

(4)

{\textstyle\int}\zeta^{2}(z)\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(z,S)\leq\Gamma\Bigl(\kappa^{2}{\textstyle\int_{\spt\zeta}}|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\|V_{F}\|(z)\\
+{\textstyle\int}\zeta^{2}(z)|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|\,\mathrm{d}\|\delta_{F}V\|(z)\Bigr)\,.

Proof.

Let

\Delta=\Gamma_{\text{\ref{lem:PQ_estimates}}}(\gamma(\phi),c_{2}(\phi))
be given
by
3.2
. Define
g\in\mathscr{X}(U)
by setting

g(z)=\zeta(z)^{2}Q_{F}(T)(z-c)\quad\text{for $x\in U$}\,.

Note that, since
Q_{F}(T)=Q_{F}(T)\circ{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}
(by
2.18
), we get
for
x\in U
and
S\in\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)

(5)

\bigl|P_{F}(S)^{*}\nabla\zeta(x)\bullet Q_{F}(T)(z-c)\bigr|\\
=\bigl|\nabla\zeta(x)\bullet P_{F}(S)\circ Q_{F}(T)\circ{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)\bigr|\\
\leq|\nabla\zeta(x)|\cdot\|P_{F}(S)\circ Q_{F}(T)\|\cdot|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|\,;

hence, employing Hölder’s inequality and recalling
3.2
we get

(6)

\left|{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)P_{F}(S)^{*}\nabla\zeta(x)\bullet Q_{F}(T)(z-c)\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\right|\\
\leq\kappa\Delta\left({\textstyle\int_{\spt\zeta}}|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\|V_{F}\|(x)\right)^{1/2}\left({\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\right)^{1/2}\,.

We have

(7)

{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{F}(V;x)\bullet Q_{F}(T)\circ{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)\,\mathrm{d}\|\delta_{F}V\|(x)=\delta_{F}V(g)\\
=2{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)P_{F}(S)^{*}\nabla\zeta(x)\bullet Q_{F}(T)(z-c)\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\\
+{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}P_{F}(S)^{*}\bullet Q_{F}(T)\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\,;

thus,

(8)

\Delta^{-1}{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\leq{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}P_{F}(S)^{*}\bullet Q_{F}(T)\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\\
\leq 2\kappa\Delta\left({\textstyle\int_{\spt\zeta}}|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\|V_{F}\|(x)\right)^{1/2}\left({\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\right)^{1/2}\\
+\Delta{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|\,\mathrm{d}\|\delta_{F}V\|(x)\,.

Now, there are two possibilities. Either

\Delta{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|\,\mathrm{d}\|\delta_{F}V\|(x)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta^{-1}{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\,,

which yields

(9)

{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\\
\leq 4\kappa\Delta^{2}\left({\textstyle\int_{\spt\zeta}}|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\|V_{F}\|(x)\right)^{1/2}\left({\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\right)^{1/2}

and, consequently,

{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\leq 16\kappa^{2}\Delta^{4}{\textstyle\int_{\spt\zeta}}|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\|V_{F}\|(x)\,;

or

\Delta{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}|{T}_{\natural}^{\perp}(z-c)|\,\mathrm{d}\|\delta_{F}V\|(x)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta^{-1}{\textstyle\int}\zeta(x)^{2}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V_{F}(x,S)\,.\qed

Suppose

\bigl|{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}\varphi\circ{T}_{\natural}\bigr|\,h(0)\bullet u=\bigl|{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}\varphi\circ{T}_{\natural}\bigr|\,\varphi^{*}h(0)\bullet u=g(0)\bullet u=0\quad\text{for every $u\in T\cap\mathbb{S}^{m}$}\,.\qed

5.3
Remark
.

Brakke’s proof in
[
Bra78
, §5.8]
of the perpendicularity theorem in the Euclidean
setting employs the Euclidean structure in an essential way. Indeed, the Pythagorean theorem
plays a crucial role in the estimate for
a_{3}
and the estimate for
a_{0}
wouldn’t go through if
we did not ensure that
5.2
(
12
) holds. Also the estimate
for
a_{1}
would not be possible if
P_{G}(T)\neq{T}_{\natural}
. Fortunately, perpendicularity of
the mean curvature vector at a point
b
depends only on the behaviour of the varifold in
vicinity of
b
so straightening the metric at that single point
b
, by pushing both the
varifold and the integrand with the linear diffeomorphism
\psi
, solves all the problems coming
from anisotropy.

5.4
Remark
.

In the isotropic case assumption
5.2
(

) is generic by

with non-zero limit as

; hence, does not provide the decay

(

).

In the setting of

]

5.5
Remark
.

If a constant density varifold
V
inside a ball
\mathbf{U}(b,r)
is associated to the graph
M
of
a
\mathscr{C}^{2}
function, then for each

there exists a tangent

5.6 Theorem
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{{n+1}}
is open,
F:\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)\to\mathbf{R}
is associated
to the norm
\phi
as in
2.21
,

V\in\mathbf{IV}_{n}(\Omega)
such that
\|\delta_{F}V\|
is a Radon measure over
\Omega

and
M\subseteq\Omega
is a
n
-dimensional submanifold of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
such that

\mathscr{H}^{n}(M)<\infty
.

Let
A
be the set of
a\in M
such that

(1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(V,a)\perp\Tan^{n}(\|V\|,a)\in\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)\,

(2)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{-n-2}{\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{B}(a,r)\times\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)}}\|{S}_{\natural}-{\Tan^{n}(\|V\|,a)}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V(x,S)<\infty\,.

Then

\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(V,a)=\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(M,a)\quad\text{for $\|V\|$ almost all $a\in A$}\,.

Proof.

We set
\tau(a)={\Tan^{n}(\|V\|,a)}_{\natural}
whenever

\Tan^{n}(\|V\|,a)\in\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)
. In particular, by

2.19
,
[
All72
, 3.5(1)]
and
[
Fed69
, 2.9.9,
2.9.10]
we see that for
\|V\|
a.e.
a\in\Omega
there exists

m\in\mathscr{P}
such that

(3)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\|V\|,a)=m,\quad\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\|\delta_{F}V\|_{\mathrm{sing}},a)=0\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\VarTan(V,a)=\bigl\{m\,\mathbf{v}_{n}(\Tan^{n}(\|V\|,a))\bigr\}

and
a
is a
\|V\|
Lebesgue point of
(F\circ\tau)\,\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(V,\cdot)
. Moreover, by

[
Fed69
, 2.10.19(4), 3.2.16]
we have that

\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\|V\|\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits\mathbf{R}^{{n+1}}\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}M,a)=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Tan^{n}(\|V\|,a)=\Tan(M,a)

for
\|V\|
a.e.
a\in M
. For
d\in\mathscr{P}
we define

A_{d}=A\cap\left\{a:\begin{gathered}\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(V,a)\perp\Tan(M,a)=\Tan^{n}(\|V\|,a),\,\\
\text{$a$ is a $(\|V\|,C)$~Lebesgue point of $(F\circ\tau)\,\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(V,\cdot)$},\,\\
\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\|V\|,a)=d=\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\|V\|\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits M,a)\,,\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\|\delta_{F}V\|_{\mathrm{sing}},a)=0,\\
\VarTan(V,a)=\{d\,\mathbf{v}_{n}(\Tan^{n}(\|V\|,a))\}\end{gathered}\right\}\,

and, recalling hypothesis (
1
), we see that

\|V\|\bigl(A\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}{\textstyle\bigcup_{d\in\mathscr{P}}}A_{d}\bigr)=0\,.

We choose
d\in\mathscr{P}
and
a\in A_{d}
so that

\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits(M\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}A_{d}),a)=\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\|V\|\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits\mathbf{R}^{{n+1}}\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}A_{d},a)=0

and we choose

T=\Tan(M,a)\,,\quad\nu\in T^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}\,,\quad\eta=\nabla\phi(\nu)

and
\zeta:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
a smooth such that
\zeta(t)=0
for
1\leq t<\infty
. Define

g_{r}(z)=\zeta(|z-a|/r)\eta\quad\text{for $0<r<\infty$}\,.

Then we observe that

(5)

-d\phi(\nu)\bigl(\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(V,a)\bullet\eta\bigr){\textstyle\int_{T\cap\mathbf{B}(0,1)}}\zeta(|z|)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(z)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{-n}\delta_{F}V(g_{r})\\
=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{-n}{\textstyle\int}B_{F}(S)\bullet\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)\,\mathrm{d}V(z,S)\,.

Set
W=d\,\mathbf{v}_{n}(M)
and observe that since
M
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
properties
(
2
) and (
5
) hold also for
W

in place of
V
. Recalling that
\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(V,a),\mathbf{h}_{{F}}(W,a)\in\im T^{\perp}
,

\dim\im T^{\perp}=1
, and
\eta\bullet\nu=\phi(\nu)\neq 0
, it suffices to prove that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{-n}{\textstyle\int}B_{F}(S)\bullet\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)\,\mathrm{d}V(z,S)-r^{-n}{\textstyle\int}B_{F}(S)\bullet\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)\,\mathrm{d}W(z,S)=0\,.

From
2.21
we see that
\im\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)\perp\im B_{F}(T)
and

B_{F}(T)\bullet\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)=0
for
z\in\Omega
. We observe that

(6)

{\textstyle\int}B_{F}(S)\bullet\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)\,\mathrm{d}V(z,S)-{\textstyle\int}B_{F}(S)\bullet\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)\,\mathrm{d}W(z,S)\\
={\textstyle\int_{(\mathbf{B}(a,r)\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}A_{d})\times\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)}}(B_{F}(S)-B_{F}(T))\bullet\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)\,\mathrm{d}V(z,S)\\
-{\textstyle\int_{(\mathbf{B}(a,r)\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}A_{d})\times\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)}}(B_{F}(S)-B_{F}(T))\bullet\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)\,\mathrm{d}W(z,S)\,.

Define

(7)

R_{U}(r)={\textstyle\int_{(\mathbf{B}(a,r)\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}A_{d})\times\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)}}(B_{F}(S)-B_{F}(z,T))\bullet\mathrm{D}g_{r}(z)\,\mathrm{d}U(z,S)\\
\text{for $0<r<\infty$ and $U\in\{V,\,W\}$}\,.

We shall show that
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{-n}R_{U}(r)=0
for
U\in\{V,\,W\}
. Since

\phi
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
we see that
B_{F}
is Lipschitzian. Set

L=\Lip B_{F}\Lip\zeta
and note that
|\eta|\leq c_{1}(\phi)
. For brevity define

E(r)=(\mathbf{B}(a,r)\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}A_{d})\times\mathbf{G}({n+1},n)
for
0<r<\infty
. We estimate

(8)

(Lc_{1}(\phi))^{-1}r^{-n}R_{U}(r)\leq r^{-n-1}{\textstyle\int_{E(r)}}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|\,\mathrm{d}U(z,S)\\
\leq\bigl(r^{-n}\|U\|(\mathbf{B}(a,r)\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}A_{d})\bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(r^{-n-2}{\textstyle\int_{E(r)}}\|{S}_{\natural}-{T}_{\natural}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}U(z,S)\Bigr)^{1/2}\,.

The first term clearly converges to
0
and the second stays bounded as
r\to 0^{+}
; hence,
the theorem is proven.
∎
