Gertsch quotient living in the ‘‘poor man’s adele ring" 𝒜 \mathcal{A} : Kurepa-Bell-Wilson congruence
Francis Atta Howard

Abstract
Wilson’s theorem is notably related to left factorials, expressed as K p ≡ 𝐁𝐞𝐥𝐥 p − 1 − 1 ( mod p ) K_{p}\equiv\mathbf{Bell}_{p-1}-1\pmod{p} , for prime p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 . This study examines a Kurepa-Bell-Wilson congruence ( KBW ), K p + 1 p ≡ 𝐁𝐞𝐥𝐥 p − 1 p + W p ( mod p ) \frac{K_{p}+1}{p}\equiv\frac{\mathbf{Bell}_{p-1}}{p}+W_{p}\pmod{p} , and demonstrates that it naturally generates the non-zero "Gertsch quotient ( 𝔾 p \mathbb{G}_{p} )," which, for larger primes modulo p p resides in the poor man’s adele ring 𝒜 \mathcal{A} .

7.1
The congruence
0\pmod{p}

There are no known primes
p\geq 3
for which the Gertsch quotient,
\mathbb{G}_{p}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

From Theorem
4
we know that the Gertsch quotient and Wilson quotient coincide at certain primes say
p=3,7,2887\ldots

\mathbb{G}_{p}\equiv W_{p}\pmod{p}

However, at these primes
p=3,7,2887\ldots
, the Gertsch quotient
\mathbb{G}_{p}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}.
Also, since

\mathbb{G}_{p}\equiv W_{p}\pmod{p}

it is natural to argue that at the prime where
W_{p}\equiv 0\mod{p}
such as
p=5,13,563
, the Gertsch quotient may also be congruent to zero modulo
p
, but this is not the case since

\mathbb{G}_{p}\not\equiv W_{p}\pmod{p}

for
p=5,13,563.
If such
W_{p}\equiv 0\bmod{p}
existed it will be for
p>5\times 10^{8}
or
p\geq 2\times 10^{13}
see
11
;
10
.
Another way to naturally argue is to use the Lerch relation;

\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}q_{p}(a)\bmod{p}\equiv W_{p}.

Now, remark that it is the

(q_{p}(1)+q_{p}(2)+q_{p}(3)+q_{p}(4)+\cdots+q_{p}(p-1))\equiv W_{p}\bmod{p}

therefore just as theorem
2
and theorem
1
, although

\frac{K_{p}-\mathbf{Bell}_{p-1}+1}{p}\!\underset{\text{Wieferich}}{\equiv}-q_{p}(2)\pmod{p}

and

\frac{K_{p}-\mathbf{Bell}_{p-1}+1}{p}\!\underset{\text{Mirimanoff}}{\equiv}q_{p}(3)\pmod{p}

yield
q_{p}(2)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and
q_{p}(3)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for
p=1093,\quad 3511
and
p=11,\quad 1006003
respectively
24
;
23
, it does not make
\mathbb{G}_{p}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

We observe that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{p}\equiv W_{p}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\frac{K_{p}-\mathbf{Bell}_{p-1}+1}{p}\equiv W_{p}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle K_{p}-\mathbf{Bell}_{p-1}\equiv pW_{p}-1\pmod{p^{2}}

\displaystyle K_{p}-\mathbf{Bell}_{p-1}\equiv(p-1)!\pmod{p^{2}}

from the identity

p(p+1)\mathbf{B}_{p-1}\equiv(p-1)!\pmod{p^{2}}
(see equation 2.9 in
9
) we obtain the Kurepa-Bell-Bernoulli
KBB

\displaystyle K_{p}-\mathbf{Bell}_{p-1}\equiv p(p+1)\mathbf{B}_{p-1}\pmod{p^{2}}

(
KBB
)

One could search for primes which make this relation hold.
Also, all these could help us understand the Kurepa-Bell-Wilson
KBW
congruence in section
1
(as well as proposition
3
, corollary
1
, and corollary
2
).
