ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe production of Scandium-47, a promising radionuclide for targeted radionuclide therapy, was investigated via proton-induced reactions on enriched Ca-48 using the EMPIRE nuclear reaction code. Excitation functions for the ⁴⁸Ca(p, n)⁴⁸Sc, ⁴⁸Ca(p,2n)⁴⁷Sc, and ⁴⁸Ca(p,3n)⁴⁶Sc reactions were calculated over a wide proton energy range, and thick-target yields were evaluated using stopping power data. The RNP of ⁴⁷Sc was determined as a function of incident proton energy.ResultsThe results show that radionuclide production strongly depends on proton energy. At low energies (0–12 MeV), the ⁴⁸Ca(p, n) reaction dominates, leading to significant ⁴⁸Sc formation and low RNP (~ 9%). In the intermediate energy range (12–28 MeV), the yield of ⁴⁷Sc increases substantially while maintaining relatively high purity. A more detailed analysis identifies the 16.5–21.5 MeV window as optimal, providing high yield with an RNP of approximately 94%. At higher energies (28–50 MeV), the increasing contribution of the ⁴⁸Ca(p,3n) reaction results in significant formation of long-lived ⁴⁶Sc, reducing radionuclidic purity.ConclusionThese findings demonstrate that careful selection of the proton energy window is essential for optimizing both production yield and RNP, providing practical guidance for cyclotron-based production of ⁴⁷Sc for medical applications.Keywords: 47Sc production, Proton-induced nuclear reactions, Reactions, Radionuclidic purity, EMPIRE nuclear reaction code

Background

Scandium-47 () has emerged as a promising radionuclide for theranostic applications in nuclear medicine due to its favorable decay characteristics. It emits beta particles suitable for targeted radionuclide therapy and a 159 keV gamma ray appropriate for SPECT imaging, enabling both therapeutic and diagnostic applications within a single isotope. Its half-life of approximately 3.35 days is well suited for radiopharmaceutical preparation, distribution, and clinical use (Srivastava and Mausner 2013, Meulen et al. 2015).

The growing clinical interest in has stimulated extensive research into its production using cyclotrons. Cyclotron-based production typically involves irradiation of enriched targets such as or with charged particles, most commonly protons, to induce nuclear reactions that yield . However, these reactions may also produce radionuclidic impurities such as and , which can compromise radionuclidic purity and raise concerns regarding patient safety (Mikolajczak et al. 2021, Kilian and Pyrzyńska 2023). Accurate prediction of impurity formation requires detailed knowledge of reaction cross sections over the relevant energy range.

In practice, experimental cross-section data are often incomplete or unavailable for many reaction channels and energy ranges. Consequently, nuclear reaction modeling codes such as TALYS, ALICE-IPPE, and EMPIRE (Exciton Model Pre-equilibrium Interaction Reaction Evaluation) have become essential tools for predicting excitation functions, thick-target yields, and impurity profiles (Koning et al. 2019, Capote et al. 2009). These computational approaches provide a practical alternative for evaluating production routes and optimizing irradiation parameters prior to experimental implementation.

Despite the clinical promise of , routine cyclotron production with high radionuclidic purity remains challenging (Ioannidis et al. 2024). The co-production of long-lived scandium isotopes, particularly , introduces radiological and regulatory concerns. The present study addresses the gap by introducing a quantitatively validated, model-based framework for optimizing cyclotron production of ⁴⁷Sc. In contrast to earlier experimental approaches, this work integrates theoretical excitation function calculations using the EMPIRE nuclear reaction code with experimental cross-section data from the EXFOR database, and extends the analysis to include thick-target yield (TTY) and radionuclidic purity (RNP) as explicit, energy-dependent optimization parameters. Moreover, experimental optimization of irradiation conditions is both costly and time-intensive, and the available nuclear data are often insufficient across the full range of cyclotron energies (Pupillo et al. 2024). There is therefore a clear need for reliable computational methods capable of predicting impurity formation and guiding production strategies (Kankanamalage et al. 2023, Cingoranelli et al. 2024).

Overall, the production of Scandium-47 continues to attract considerable attention because of its favorable characteristics for theranostic nuclear medicine, combining therapeutic beta emission with gamma radiation suitable for SPECT imaging (Müller et al. 2018). A key outcome of this approach is the identification of a refined optimal proton energy window based on combined yield–purity criteria, rather than yield alone. This predictive methodology reduces reliance on trial-and-error experimentation and offers practical guidance for cyclotron facilities aiming to produce high-purity ⁴⁷Sc. More broadly, the framework presented here demonstrates how computational nuclear reaction modeling can support efficient and scalable production strategies for medically important radionuclides. Ultimately, these findings contribute to the broader effort to enable scalable and clinically viable production of this important theranostic radionuclide (De Dominicis et al. 2025).

Methodology

Nuclear reaction modeling and excitation functions

The excitation functions for the reactions , , and were calculated using the nuclear reaction modeling code EMPIRE (version 3.2.3). EMPIRE provides a comprehensive framework for simulating nuclear reactions by combining optical model calculations, compound nucleus formation, pre-equilibrium emission, and statistical decay mechanisms over a broad energy range(Herman et al. 2007).

In the present work, proton-induced reactions on the target were simulated within the incident energy range of 1–50 MeV, which is relevant for cyclotron-based radionuclide production. This energy interval enables the identification of reaction thresholds, peak production regions, and competing reaction channels contributing to the formation of different scandium radioisotopes.

The interaction between the incident proton and the target nucleus was described using the spherical optical model potential of Koning and Delaroche, which is implemented in the EMPIRE global optical model library. This potential was selected because of its extensive validation for nucleon-induced reactions across medium-mass nuclei and intermediate proton energies (Koning and Delaroche 2003).

Nuclear level densities were treated using the Enhanced Generalized Superfluid Model (EGSM), which accounts for shell effects, pairing correlations, and collective excitations in the residual nucleus. The default EMPIRE parameters associated with the EGSM model were employed without additional adjustment. Gamma-ray transmission coefficients were calculated using the Modified Lorentzian (MLO) strength function available in the EMPIRE package. Pre-equilibrium particle emission was modeled using the exciton model incorporated in EMPIRE. The initial exciton configuration for proton-induced reactions was taken as , and the default transition rates provided in the code were adopted. Equilibrium decay of the compound nucleus was calculated using the Hauser–Feshbach statistical formalism with width fluctuation corrections included (Qaim et al. 2022).

All calculations were performed using the default EMPIRE input parameters unless otherwise specified. No local parameter fitting or manual adjustment of optical model, level density, or pre-equilibrium parameters was introduced. The calculated excitation functions were obtained by summing contributions from compound and pre-equilibrium reaction mechanisms.

Comparison with data extracted from EXFOR

To validate the theoretical predictions, the calculated cross sections were systematically compared with available experimental data retrieved from established nuclear reaction databases, particularly EXFOR (Koning et al. 2019). Experimental measurements of proton-induced reactions on calcium targets, including , , and , have been reported in several activation studies (Ioannidis et al. 2024, Qaim et al. 2022). The comparison procedure involved generating theoretical excitation functions using the EMPIRE nuclear reaction code and superimposing the corresponding experimental cross-section data extracted from the EXFOR database (Koning et al. 2019).

A dedicated data processing and visualization workflow was developed in Python, employing widely used scientific libraries such as pandas for data handling and matplotlib for graphical representation. All computations were performed using current stable versions of these packages to ensure reliability and reproducibility. The theoretical and experimental datasets were plotted on a common energy grid, enabling direct and consistent comparison of excitation functions over the entire energy range of interest.

The level of agreement between theoretical predictions and experimental data was evaluated using both qualitative and quantitative criteria. Visual inspection focused on the accurate reproduction of key physical features, including the magnitude and energy position of peak cross sections, as well as the overall shape and trend of the excitation functions. In addition, quantitative statistical indicators were employed, including the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), root mean square error (RMSE), and the correlation coefficient (R), which provide complementary measures of deviation, dispersion, and linear agreement between the datasets (Li and Zhang 2023).

Stopping power and proton energy degradation

The stopping power, , quantifies the rate of energy (dE) loss of charged particles as they pass through matter (dx) and depends on both the projectile energy and the target material properties. In this study, stopping power data for protons in enriched were obtained from tabulated datasets, like NIST PSTAR Database (Berger 1992). The stopping power decreases with increasing proton energy, reflecting reduced interaction probability at higher energies. Incorporating this energy-dependent behavior is essential for accurately modeling the gradual slowing down of protons within the target and, consequently, for reliable yield calculations (Radioisotopes and No 2012).

Numerical implementation and data processing

The excitation function and stopping power data generated using the EMPIRE nuclear reaction code were systematically processed through numerical methods within a computational framework. The yield integral was evaluated using discrete summation over finely defined energy intervals, with careful alignment of the cross section and stopping power datasets to ensure numerical consistency. The analysis included the determination of thick target yields and radionuclidic purity as functions of incident proton energy. This approach provides a reliable and efficient basis for assessing production scenarios and facilitates the identification of optimal irradiation parameters prior to experimental validation (Havlova et al. 2023).

Discussions

Reaction

The excitation function of the reaction exhibits a characteristic threshold behavior followed by a rapid increase in cross section, reaching a maximum within the proton energy region of approximately 9–12 MeV. This behavior is typical of proton-induced compound nucleus reactions occurring near the reaction threshold. At low incident proton energies, the reaction mechanism is dominated primarily by compound nucleus formation, where the incident proton is fully absorbed by the target nucleus to form an excited intermediate nucleus . The subsequent statistical emission of a neutron leads to the formation of .

As the proton energy increases beyond the peak region, the contribution of pre-equilibrium emission mechanisms gradually becomes more significant. In this energy regime, the incident proton may transfer only part of its energy before particle emission occurs, leading to non-equilibrium processes and increased competition from multi-particle reaction channels. Consequently, the cross section of the channel decreases due to the opening of energetically favorable competing reactions such as and These channels divert part of the reaction flux away from production, reducing the probability of the single-neutron emission process at higher energies.

Comparison with experimental cross-section data

Comparison between the EMPIRE calculations and the experimental cross-section data extracted from the EXFOR database demonstrates generally good agreement in both the magnitude and shape of the excitation function. The calculated peak position closely reproduces the experimentally observed maximum within the 9–12 MeV region. Statistical indicators including the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), root mean square error (RMSE), and correlation coefficient further confirm the reliability of the EMPIRE calculations over the common energy range. The low MAPE, 9.5% and RMSE values 26 mb, together with correlation coefficients approaching unity, 0.95 indicate strong consistency between theoretical and experimental results (Koning et al. 2019, Ditrói et al. 2016).

The present EMPIRE results are also consistent with trends reported using the TALYS nuclear reaction code in previous proton-induced scandium production studies (Misiak et al. 2017). Both EMPIRE and TALYS predict the dominant contribution of compound nucleus reactions near threshold energies and increasing pre-equilibrium effects at higher proton energies. However, small differences in the predicted peak magnitude and high-energy tail are commonly observed due to variations in optical model potentials, level density prescriptions, and pre-equilibrium parameterizations implemented in the two codes. In particular, as reported by some investigators, TALYS calculations often predict a slightly broader excitation function at higher energies because of differences in exciton model treatment and nuclear level density descriptions(Herman et al. 2007, Koning and Rochman 2012, Tárkányi et al. 2019).

From the perspective of practical radionuclide production, these results are particularly important for optimizing cyclotron irradiation parameters. Since the reaction dominates at relatively low proton energies, irradiation within this energy region may lead to significant co-production of during the production of . Therefore, careful selection of the proton energy window is required to suppress the formation of impurities while maximizing the yield and radionuclidic purity of the desired .

The analysis further indicates that proton energies above the threshold of the reaction should be optimized carefully because higher beam energies increase the probability of unwanted radionuclidic contaminants such as , which possesses a relatively long half-life and may complicate medical applications and radioactive waste management. Consequently, the present results provide useful guidance for selecting irradiation energies suitable for high-purity medical radionuclide production in medium-energy cyclotrons.

Reaction

Beyond the reaction threshold, the cross section increases rapidly with increasing proton energy due to the increasing probability of three-neutron emission from the excited compound nucleus. In the low- to intermediate-energy region, the reaction is dominated primarily by the compound nucleus mechanism. After proton absorption, an excited compound system is formed, which subsequently decays through statistical neutron evaporation. In this energy region, the excitation energy is sufficiently distributed among nucleons before particle emission occurs, making the Hauser–Feshbach statistical treatment implemented in the EMPIRE code appropriate for describing the reaction process. The calculated excitation function reaches a maximum cross section of approximately 430–450 mb in the proton energy range of about 31–34 MeV, corresponding to the optimum excitation energy for the evaporation of three neutrons from the compound system.

As the proton energy increases further, pre-equilibrium processes begin to contribute significantly to the reaction mechanism. In the pre-equilibrium stage, particle emission may occur before the compound nucleus reaches complete statistical equilibrium. This contribution becomes increasingly important above approximately 30 MeV, where direct nucleon interactions and exciton-state transitions enhance the probability of fast neutron emission. The inclusion of pre-equilibrium exciton models in EMPIRE therefore plays an important role in reproducing the high-energy behavior of the excitation function. At proton energies above approximately 35 MeV, the calculated cross section gradually decreases due to the opening of competing reaction channels, including higher-order neutron emission reactions such as , as well as other complex particle emission pathways. These competing channels redistribute the reaction flux and reduce the probability of formation(Herman et al. 2007, Koning and Rochman 2012, Tárkányi et al. 2019).

Comparison with experimental cross-section data

The calculated excitation function was compared with experimental cross-section data available in the EXFOR database. The data show a similar trend to the EMPIRE predictions, particularly in the threshold region, the rising slope of the excitation function, and the approximate location of the peak cross section. Statistical indicators including the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) close to 40%, root mean square error (RMSE) ~ 32 mb, and correlation coefficient (R) 0.85, suggesting that the EMPIRE model adequately reproduces the general behavior of the reaction (Koning et al. 2019, Ditrói et al. 2016). However, noticeable deviations are observed in the magnitude of the peak cross section, where EMPIRE tends to slightly overestimate the experimental values. This overestimation may arise from uncertainties in the nuclear level density parameters, optical model potentials, and pre-equilibrium emission contributions employed in the theoretical calculations. In particular, the predicted neutron emission probability is highly sensitive to the choice of level density models at excitation energies corresponding to the peak region. Small variations in these parameters can significantly influence the calculated evaporation rates and consequently increase the predicted cross section. In addition, the pre-equilibrium component incorporated in EMPIRE may enhance neutron emission probabilities at intermediate proton energies, leading to higher theoretical cross sections compared with experimental observations. Experimental uncertainties, including target thickness variations, beam-energy spread, and detector efficiency corrections, may also contribute to the observed discrepancies.

Implications for cyclotron production of

The present analysis provides clear guidance for the cyclotron-based production of using enriched targets. The combined evaluation of thick target yield and radionuclidic purity demonstrates that the selection of proton energy plays a critical role in determining both production efficiency and product quality (Qaim et al. 2019). The results indicate that although higher proton energies (21.5–28 MeV) lead to increased production yields, they also significantly enhance the formation of the long-lived impurity , thereby reducing radionuclidic purity. Conversely, lower energies (12–16.5 MeV) result in reduced impurity formation but suffer from comparatively low production yields. The intermediate energy window of 16.5–21.5 MeV is identified as the optimal range for cyclotron irradiation, providing a favorable balance between high yield and high radionuclidic purity (~ 94%). This energy region corresponds to the peak of the reaction while minimizing the contribution from competing reaction channels (Wang et al. 2022).

From an operational perspective, these findings suggest that cyclotron facilities should carefully tune the incident proton energy and target thickness to ensure that the effective energy degradation occurs predominantly within this optimal range. This may be achieved through appropriate selection of beam energy, degrader materials, and target design. Furthermore, the presence of short-lived impurities such as can be mitigated through optimized cooling times, whereas the production of long-lived must be minimized at the irradiation stage itself due to its persistence and radiological implications. Overall, the study highlights that precise control of irradiation parameters, particularly the proton energy window, is essential for achieving clinically acceptable with high radionuclidic purity and sufficient yield, thereby supporting its application in targeted radionuclide therapy.

Conclusion

In this study, the production of ⁴⁷Sc through proton-induced reactions on enriched ⁴⁸Ca was systematically investigated using EMPIRE-generated excitation functions combined with stopping power data. A comparison with experimental data extracted from EXFOR for ⁴⁸Sc, ⁴⁷Sc, and ⁴⁶Sc production channels showed good overall agreement, confirming the reliability of the model in reproducing experimental trends, as indicated by low MAPE values and correlation coefficients close to unity (0.86–0.95). The calculated thick-target yields and radionuclidic purity exhibit a strong dependence on incident proton energy. The low-energy region is unsuitable for ⁴⁷Sc production due to the dominance of ⁴⁸Sc, whereas higher energies favor the formation of the long-lived impurity ⁴⁶Sc. A refined evaluation of the intermediate energy range identifies the 16.5–21.5 MeV window as optimal, providing a favorable balance between high yield and radionuclidic purity, with RNP values approaching 94%. These results highlight the importance of precise energy selection in cyclotron-based production. While higher energies can enhance yield, they also promote competing reaction channels that degrade product quality. Therefore, careful optimization of irradiation parameters, particularly the proton energy window, is essential for achieving clinically acceptable ⁴⁷Sc. The study also demonstrates the value of computational nuclear reaction modeling as an effective tool for predicting excitation functions, impurity formation, and production yields. By enabling pre-experimental evaluation of irradiation conditions, such approaches reduce reliance on extensive trial-and-error experimentation. Overall, this work provides a systematic framework for optimizing cyclotron production of ⁴⁷Sc and supports the development of reliable, high-purity production routes, contributing to the scalable and clinically viable use of ⁴⁷Sc in theranostic nuclear medicine.
