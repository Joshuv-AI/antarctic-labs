On the Harris–Viehmann conjecture for Hodge–Newton reducible local Shimura data of abelian type
Sandra Nair, Xinyu Zhou

Abstract
. We address a new case of the Harris–Viehmann conjecture, which establishes a parabolic induction formula on the cohomology groups associated to non-basic local Shimura data. It follows that all supercuspidal representations on a Shimura variety are concentrated along the basic locus, making the conjecture relevant to the Langlands program. Historically, many cases of the Harris–Viehmann conjecture have been approached with the additional condition of Hodge–Newton reducibility on the underlying local Shimura datum. Building on previous work by Mantovan (EL/PEL case) and Hong (Hodge case), we extend the proof of the conjecture to unramified non-basic local Shimura data of abelian type under the assumption of Hodge–Newton reducibility. We leverage Shen’s construction of Rapoport–Zink spaces of abelian type at the hyperspecial level.



1.
Introduction

Rapoport–Zink spaces
are local analogues of integral models of Shimura varieties. The first example of a Rapoport–Zink space was explicitly constructed in
[
22
]
, serving as a formal moduli space of
p
-divisible groups with PEL structure. Its

[

\ell

local Shimura varieties
in
[
20

, of which (the generic fibers of) Rapoport–Zink spaces are a special case. They start with a

The

concerns the cohomology of local Shimura varieties with underlying non-basic local Shimura data. Originally conjectured by Harris in
[

]
, later modified by Viehmann in
[

1.2.
History of results on the Harris–Viehmann conjecture:

(1)

Boyer proved the Harris–Viehmann conjecture for Drinfeld modular varieties in
[
3
]
.

(2)

Mantovan proved the existence of the canonical Hodge–Newton filtration for
p
-divisible groups with additional structures in
[
15
]
. She passed to their Rapoport–Zink spaces of PEL type under the assumption that the Newton polygon coincides with the Hodge polygon up to or from the nontrivial break contact point, to prove the Harris–Viehmann conjecture.

(3)

Shen followed up on this in
[
25
]
by considering the more general case when the Newton polygon admits a nontrivial contact point with the Hodge polygon, and assuming that this contact point is a break point of the Newton polygon. This also established the Harris–Viehmann conjecture for Rapoport–Zink spaces of PEL type.

(4)

Hansen gave another proof of the conjecture in
[
8
]
for
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
under the Hodge–Newton reducibility assumption. He used the theory of local Shimura varieties developed by Scholze in
[
24
]
.

(5)

Gaisin and Imai proved the conjecture (assuming Hodge–Newton reducibility) for a generalization of the diamond of a non-basic Rapoport–Zink space at infinite level in
[
6
]
.

(6)

Hamann and Imai proved the conjecture under a mild Hodge–Newton reducibility condition for a general local Shimura variety, by studying the moduli stack of parabolic bundles on the Fargues–Fontaine curve in
[
7
]
.

Our main goal is to establish the Harris–Viehmann conjecture for unramified local Shimura data of abelian type, which are Hodge–Newton reducible with respect to fixed Levi and parabolic subgroups. We work with Rapoport–Zink spaces arising from unramified local Shimura data of abelian type as constructed in
[
26
]
.

3.
Background

We first recall some important definitions.

Definition 3.1

(
[
20
, Definition 5.1]
)
.

A local Shimura datum over
\Q_{p}
is a triple
(G,[b],\{\mu\})
, where:

(1)

G
is a connected reductive group over
\Q_{p}
.

(2)

[b]\in B(G)
is a
\sigma
-conjugacy class.

(3)

\{\mu\}
is a conjugacy class of cocharacters
\mu:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to G_{\overline{\Q}_{p}}

such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

[b]\in B(G,\{\mu\})
(the subset of the Kottwitz set bounded by
\{\mu\}
, c.f.
[
21
]
).

(2)

\mu
is minuscule, i.e.
|\mu|\in\{-1,0,1\}
.

Moreover, we have the following quantities associated with a local Shimura datum:

(1)

The field of definition
E=G_{E}
of
\{\mu\}
is called the reflex field, which is a finite extension of
\Q_{p}
. Let
\cW_{E}
denote the Weil group of
E
.

(2)

The reductive algebraic group
J_{b}
over
\Q_{p}
for
b\in[b]
.
It has the following functor of points:

\displaystyle J_{b}(R)=\{g\in G(R\otimes_{R}\breve{\Q}_{p})\;:\;b=gb\sigma(g)^{-1}\}

for any
\Q_{p}
-algebra
R
. Note that the isomorphism class of
J_{b}
depends only on the
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
b
.
J_{b}
is an inner form of a particular Levi subgroup of
G
within which
b
is a central element, c.f.
[
21
, Remark 1.15]
.

In
[
21
]
, the local Shimura data considered came exclusively from PEL data. Like with the global theory of Shimura varieties, there are more general cases than that:

Definition 3.2

(
[
20
, Remark 5.4(i)]
)
.

A local Shimura datum
(G,[b],\{\mu\})
is of
Hodge type
if there exists an embedding
G\hookrightarrow\GL(V)
for some
\Q_{p}
-vector space
V
, and a local Shimura datum
(\GL(V),[b^{\prime}],\{\mu^{\prime}\})
such that
[b]
and
\{\mu\}
are mapped to
[b^{\prime}]
and
\{\mu^{\prime}\}
respectively. Here,
\{\mu^{\prime}\}
corresponds to
(1^{r},0^{n-r})
for some integer
0\leq r\leq n=\dim(V)
.

(1)

Any local Shimura datum
(G,[b],\{\mu\})
of EL/PEL type (coming from an EL/PEL Shimura datum) is also of Hodge type.

(2)

Let
(G,X)
be a (global) Shimura datum of Hodge type, i.e. there exists some embedding into the Siegel Shimura datum
(G,X)\hookrightarrow(\mathrm{GSp},S^{\pm})
, where
S^{\pm}
are the connected components of the Siegel upper half-space. Let
\mu
be the cocharacter associated to
X
, and
[b]\in B(G,\{\mu\})
. Then
(G_{\Q_{p}},[b],\{\mu\})
is a local Shimura datum of Hodge type that is not of PEL type.

Definition 3.3
.

[
26
, Definition 3.4]

The group
G
is unramified if it is quasisplit over
\Q_{p}
and splits over
\Q_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}}
. Equivalently,
G
admits a reductive model over
\Z_{p}
. A local Shimura datum
(G,[b],\{\mu\})
of Hodge type is
unramified
if
G
is unramified.

Convention 3.4
.

When additional clarification is required, we will write the local Shimura datum as
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
.

Associated to a local Shimura datum
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
is an “adjoint datum”
(G^{\mathrm{ad}},[b_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}],\{\mu_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}\})
, where
G^{\mathrm{ad}}:=G/Z(G)
is the adjoint quotient of
G
. In fact, the quotient map
G\twoheadrightarrow G^{\mathrm{ad}}
induces a map of Kottwitz sets
B(G)\to B(G^{\mathrm{ad}})
. We write
b_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}
for the image of
b_{G}\in B(G)
under this map. We then write

\mu_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}:\mathbb{G}_{m}\xrightarrow{\mu_{G}}G_{\overline{\Q}_{p}}\to G_{\overline{\Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{ad}}

for the induced cocharacter of
G^{\mathrm{ad}}
. It is easy to check the triple
(G^{\mathrm{ad}},[b_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}],\{\mu_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}\})
indeed forms a local Shimura datum. The morphism
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})\to(G^{\mathrm{ad}},[b_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}],\{\mu_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}\})
is a morphism of local Shimura data. This leads us to the following definition:

Definition 3.5
.

A local Shimura datum
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
is called of
abelian type
, if there exists a local Shimura datum of Hodge type
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
such that there is an isomorphism of the associated adjoint local Shimura data
(G^{\mathrm{ad}},[b_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}],\{\mu_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}\})\cong(H^{\mathrm{ad}},[b_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}],\{\mu_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}\})
. A local Shimura datum
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
of abelian type is called
unramified
if
G
is unramified and there is an unramified local Shimura datum
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
of Hodge type with
(G^{\mathrm{ad}},[b_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}],\{\mu_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}\})\cong(H^{\mathrm{ad}},[b_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}],\{\mu_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}\})
.

In particular, any local Shimura datum of Hodge type is also of abelian type, and the following example establishes the latter as a strictly larger class of objects:

Example 3.6
.

[
20
, Example 5.6]

Let
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
for
n\geq 2
. Consider a nontrivial minuscule cocharacter
\mu_{H}:\GG_{m}\to\GL_{n}
and
[b_{H}]\in B(\GL_{n},\mu_{H})
. Take
\mu_{G}=\mu_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}
and
[b_{G}]=[b_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}]
. Then
(G,[b],\{\mu\})
is an unramified local Shimura datum of (strictly) abelian type.

The associated local reflex field

is an unramified extension of
\Q_{p}
, giving rise to
\breve{E}=W_{\Q_{p}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{E}}=W
.

From here onward, we assume that all the local Shimura data mentioned are unramified unless otherwise stated.

4.2.
Hodge–Newton reducibility for the Hodge type

Definition 4.1

(
[
11
, §4.1.4]
)
.

Let
(G,[b],\{\mu\})
be an unramified local Shimura datum. Fix a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
, with Levi
L
and unipotent radical
U
. The datum
(G,[b],\{\mu\})
is called
Hodge–Newton reducible
with respect to
P
and
L
if there exists
\mu\in\{\mu\}
and
b\in[b]\cap L(\breve{\Q}_{p})
with the following properties:

(1)

The cocharacter
\mu
factors through
L
.

(2)

Let
[b]
be the
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
b
in
L(\breve{\Q}_{p})
. Then
[b]\cap L(\breve{\Z}_{p})\mu(p)L(\breve{\Z}_{p})
is not empty.

(3)

In the action of
\mu
and
\nu_{b}
on
\mathrm{Lie}(U)\otimes_{\Q_{p}}\breve{\Q}_{p}
, only non-negative characters occur.

We recall the following concrete example from
[
11
, Section 4.2.1]
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
G=\mathrm{Res}_{\cO_{F}/\Z_{p}}\GL_{n}
, where
\cO_{F}
is the ring of integers of a finite extension
F
of
\Q_{p}
. For a partition
n=\sum_{k=1}^{r}j_{k}
, the Levi factor
L
is of the form

L=\prod_{k=1}^{r}\mathrm{Res}_{\cO_{F}/\Z_{p}}\mathrm{GL}_{j_{k}}

Moreover, we have the following identification of the set of Newton points
[
19
, Example 1.10]
:

\mathcal{N}(G)=\{(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})\in\Q^{n}\;:\;\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...\leq\lambda_{n}\}

Using the Newton map, we can obtain the slopes of the Newton polygon
(\nu_{1},...,\nu_{n})
from
[b]
, and the slopes of the
\sigma
-invariant Hodge polygon
\overline{\mu}=(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{r})
from
\{\mu\}
. Let

m_{c}:=\sum_{k=1}^{c}j_{k}\;\text{ for }0\leq c\leq r

Let
\nu_{m_{c}}
denote the

of the

-block, and let
\nu_{m_{c}+1}
denote the

of the

-block. This distinction is important as a given block might have multiple slopes. The local Shimura datum
(\mathrm{Res}_{\cO_{F}/\Z_{p}}\GL_{n},[b],\{\mu\})
is Hodge–Newton reducible with respect to

•

The Newton polygon meets the Hodge polygon in certain points specified by the Levi subgroup:

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{m_{c}}\nu_{a}=\sum_{b=1}^{c}\mu_{b}\;\text{ for each }1\leq c\leq r.

•

These points are break points of the Newton polygon:

\displaystyle\nu_{m_{c}}<\nu_{m_{c}+1}\;\text{ for each }0\leq c\leq r-1

4.3.
The conjecture and the strategy

We now state the Harris–Viehmann conjecture for Hodge–Newton reducible Rapoport–Zink spaces of Hodge type.

The method used in
[
11
]
(originally devised in
[
15
]
) is as follows:

(1)

By the existence of the EL-realization, we have
\widetilde{G}
and corresponding Levi subgroup
\widetilde{L}
coming from a fixed parabolic
\widetilde{P}
. The closed embedding
G\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}
induces the closed embedding of Rapoport–Zink spaces of Hodge type
\mathrm{RZ}_{G,b}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{RZ}_{\widetilde{G},b}
by
[
12
]
. In
[
15
]
,
\widetilde{P}
is used to construct the formal scheme
\mathrm{RZ}_{\widetilde{P},b}
. By
[
11
]
, the pullback of
\mathrm{RZ}_{\widetilde{P},b}
over
\mathrm{RZ}_{G,b}
gives rise to the space
\mathrm{RZ}_{P,b}
, which is the analogue of a Rapoport–Zink space corresponding to a fixed parabolic
P
of
G
.

(2)

It is then shown that the rigid analytic generic fiber of the space
\mathrm{RZ}_{G,b}
is parabolically induced from the rigid analytic generic fiber of
\mathrm{RZ}_{L,b}
. This is proved by:

Lemma 4.4
.

[
11
, Lemma 4.3.1]
Consider the maps
\pi_{1}:\mathrm{RZ}_{P,b}\to\mathrm{RZ}_{L,b}
and
\pi_{2}:\mathrm{RZ}_{P,b}\to\mathrm{RZ}_{G,b}
. We abuse notation to refer to the corresponding maps on the respective rigid analytic generic fibres. Then, the rigid generic fibres of
\mathrm{RZ}_{G,b}
,
\mathrm{RZ}_{P,b}
and
\mathrm{RZ}_{L,b}
fit into the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{RZ}_{P,b}^{\mathrm{rig}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{RZ}_{L,b}^{\mathrm{rig}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{RZ}_{G,b}^{\mathrm{rig}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

such that:

(a)

s
is a closed immersion.

(b)

\pi_{1}
is a fibration in rigid-analytic balls.

(c)

\pi_{2}
is an isomorphism.

(3)

The (generalized) cohomologies of
\mathrm{RZ}_{G,b}
,
\mathrm{RZ}_{L,b}
with
\mathrm{RZ}_{P,b}
are compared via this lemma.

Conjecture 4.5
.

(Harris–Viehmann conjecture)
[
20
, Conjecture 8.5]

Fix a prime
\ell\neq p
. Let
(G,[b],\{\mu\})
be a non-basic unramified local Shimura datum. Let
L
be a Levi subgroup of
G
such that
b\in L(\breve{\Q}_{p})
for some
b\in[b]
, and let
P
be a standard parabolic containing
L
. Let
J_{b}
be an inner form of a Levi subgroup contained in
L
. Let
I_{b,\{\mu\},L}
be the set of
L(\breve{\Z}_{p})
-conjugacy classes of
L
with cocharacter representative
\mu^{\prime}
such that:

(1)

\mu^{\prime}\in\{\mu\}
.

(2)

[b]\cap L(\breve{\Z}_{p})\mu^{\prime}(p)L(\breve{\Z}_{p})
is non-empty.

Then for any admissible
\overline{\Q}_{\ell}
-representation
\rho
of
J_{b}(\Q_{p})
, the virtual representation of

is expressed by the following parabolic inductive formula:

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}^{\infty}_{G,b})_{\rho}=\mathrm{Ind}_{P(\Q_{p})}^{G(\Q_{p})}\Big(\sum_{\{\mu^{\prime}\}\in I_{b,\{\mu\},L}}\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}^{\infty}_{L,b})_{\rho}\Big).

By
[
20
, Theorem 8.10]
, it follows that
I_{b,\{\mu\},L}
is a singleton when the local Shimura datum

is unramified and of Hodge type. Thus, the precise statement of the Harris–Viehmann conjecture that is proved is in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
as follows:

Theorem 4.6
.

Fix a prime
\ell\neq p
. Let
(G,[b],\{\mu\})
be a non-basic unramified local Shimura datum of Hodge type which is Hodge–Newton reducible with respect to a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
with Levi factor
L
. For any admissible
\overline{\Q}_{\ell}
-representation
\rho
of

, the virtual representation of

is expressed by the following parabolic inductive formula:

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}^{\infty}_{G,b})_{\rho}=\mathrm{Ind}_{P(\Q_{p})}^{G(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}^{\infty}_{L,b})_{\rho}

It follows that the virtual representation
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}^{\infty}_{G,b})_{\rho}
contains no supercuspidal representations of
G(\Q_{p})
.

5.
Rapoport–Zink spaces of abelian type

In this section, we review Shen’s construction of Rapoport–Zink spaces of abelian type in
[
26
]
.

Consider an
unramified
local Shimura datum
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
; we will also write
G
for the hyperspecial integral model of
G
over
\Z_{p}
.
G(\breve{\Q}_{p})
admits the Cartan decomposition

G(\breve{\Q}_{p})=\coprod_{\{\mu\}}G(W)\mu(p)G(W).

where the union is over all conjugacy classes of cocharacters of
G
.
The
Kottwitz map
(c.f.
[
14
]
)

\displaystyle\kappa_{G}:G(\breve{\Q}_{p})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(G)

sends
g\in G(W)\mu(p)G(W)
to the class of
\mu
. There exists an element
c_{b_{G},\mu_{G}}\in\pi_{1}(G)
such that
\kappa_{G}(b_{G})-\mu_{G}=(1-\sigma)c_{b_{G},\mu_{G}}
, with a unique
\pi_{1}(G)^{\Gamma}
-coset.

Fix a representative
b_{G}\in G(\breve{\Q}_{p})
of the class
[b_{G}]\in B(G)
. We get the
affine Deligne-Lusztig variety (ADLV)
of the local Shimura datum:

\displaystyle X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})=\{g\in G(\breve{\Q}_{p})/G(W)\;:\;g^{-1}b_{G}\sigma(g)\in G(W)\mu_{G}(p)G(W)\}

We note that
X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})
admits a natural action by
J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})
. Similarly, from the unramified local Shimura datum
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
of Hodge type associated to
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
, we get the ADLV
X_{\mu_{H}}^{H}(b_{H})
. The ADLVs have natural structures of perfect schemes
[
28
]
. By
[
12
]
,
X_{\mu_{H}}^{H}(b_{H})
is isomorphic to
\overline{\mathrm{RZ}}_{H}^{\mathrm{perf}}
, the perfection of the special fiber of
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}
.

The Kottwitz map
\kappa_{H}:H(\breve{\Q}_{p})\to\pi_{1}(H)
induces a map
\omega_{H}:X_{\mu_{H}}^{H}(b_{H})\rightarrow c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}
of étale sheaves.
Let
X_{\mu_{H}}^{H}(b_{H})^{+}\subset X_{\mu_{H}}^{H}(b_{H})
be its fiber over
c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}
, and
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{+}\subset\mathrm{RZ}_{H}
be the corresponding formal subscheme. Similarly, we have
X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})^{+}\subset X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})
as the fibre of the map
\omega_{G}:X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})\rightarrow c_{b_{G},\mu_{G}}\pi_{1}(G)^{\Gamma}
over
c_{b_{G},\mu_{G}}
.

By
[
26
, Corollary 2.6]
,
X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})^{+}\cong X_{\mu_{H}}^{H}(b_{H})^{+}
. This motivates setting
\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{+}:=\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{+}
. Moreover, by
[
26
, Theorem 2.1]
,
X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})=J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})^{+}
. We define

\displaystyle\mathrm{RZ}_{G}:=J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{+}\cong\bigsqcup_{J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})/J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})^{+}}\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{+}

where
J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})^{+}
is the stabilizer of
X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})^{+}
in
J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})
. By construction,
\overline{\mathrm{RZ}}_{G}^{\mathrm{perf}}
is isomorphic to
X_{\mu_{G}}^{G}(b_{G})
.

This construction is independent of the auxiliary local Shimura data of Hodge type, as there is an isomorphism
\mathrm{RZ}_{H_{1}}^{+}\cong\mathrm{RZ}_{H_{2}}^{+}
for two distinct such choices
(H_{1},[b_{H_{1}}],\{\mu_{H_{1}}\})
and
(H_{2},[b_{H_{2}}],\{\mu_{H_{2}}\})
.

Assuming that the datum
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
admits an associated Hodge-type datum
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
such that the map
\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\to\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}
is surjective, we have the following identifications by
[
26
, Proposition 4.9]
:

(1)

\mathrm{RZ}_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}\cong\mathrm{RZ}_{H}/X_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma}
.

(2)

\mathrm{RZ}_{G}\cong\beta_{G}^{*}(\mathrm{RZ}_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}})
, where
\beta_{G}:\pi_{1}(G)^{\Gamma}\to\pi_{1}(G^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}
is the map induced by the quotient map
G\twoheadrightarrow G^{\mathrm{ad}}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
be an unramified local Shimura datum of abelian type, and
(H,b_{H},\{\mu_{H}\})
be the associated local Hodge type Shimura datum. Assume the natural map
\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}
is surjective. Then we have the following isomorphisms between formal schemes over
\Spf\breve{\Z}_{p}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{+}=\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{+}\cong\mathrm{RZ}_{H}/\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\cong\mathrm{RZ}_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}/\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}.

Proof.

By construction,
\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{+}=\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{+}
. The right-action of
\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}
on
c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}
induces an action on
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}
. More precisely, any
g\in\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}
induces an isomorphism

R_{g}:c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{\sim}c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}.

This induces a Cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{RZ}_{H}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{RZ}_{H}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{g}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{g}}

The isomorphism
R_{g}^{\prime}
is the induced action of
g
on
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}
.

In particular, all fibres of
\omega_{H}
are identified under the action of
\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}
. Thus,
\mathrm{RZ}^{+}_{G}=\mathrm{RZ}^{+}_{H}\cong\mathrm{RZ}_{H}/\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}
.
By
[
26
, Proposition 4.9]
, we have

\mathrm{RZ}_{H}/X_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma}\cong\mathrm{RZ}_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}.

By
[
2
, Lemma 1.5]
, we have a short exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow X_{*}(Z_{H})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(H)\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})\longrightarrow 0

We apply the left exact functor
M\mapsto M^{\Gamma}
in conjunction with our assumption
\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}
to get

0\longrightarrow X_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}\longrightarrow 0

In particular, we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{+}=\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{+}\cong\mathrm{RZ}_{H}/\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\cong(\mathrm{RZ}_{H}/X_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma})/\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}\cong\mathrm{RZ}_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}/\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

The condition that the map
\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\to\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}
is a surjection is true for many unramified reductive groups
H
. For example, it is true if
H
is a similitude group. This includes
\GL_{n},\mathrm{GSp}_{2n},\mathrm{GSpin}_{n}
, etc. However, the map
\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\to\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}
fails to be surjective if
G=\SL_{n}
.

6.
Weil descent datum

In this subsection, we discuss how the Weil descent data work for Rapoport–Zink spaces of abelian type. In particular,
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{\infty})
will be a virtual representation of
G(\Q_{p})\times\mathcal{W}_{E}
, where
\mathcal{W}_{E}
is the Weil group associated to the reflex field
E=E(\mu_{G})
of the local Shimura datum of abelian type
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
. We first recall the formalism of Weil descent datum in terms of the moduli space of
p
-adic local shtukas as in
[
17
]
, which we include for the sake of completeness. Following that, we recount Kim’s construction for the specific case of local Shimura data of Hodge type, as in
[
12
]
. We then define the Weil descent datum for local Shimura data of abelian type, and show that it agrees with the Pappas-Rapoport construction.

6.1.
Weil descent datum by
p
-adic local shtukas

In the following, let
\mathcal{G}
be a parahoric group scheme over
\Z_{p}
with generic fiber
G
. In many cases, we shall assume
\mathcal{G}
is hyperspecial (and
G
is thus unramified).
Let
\cM^{\mathrm{int}}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}
be the integral moduli space of shtukas associated to an integral local Shimura datum
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
and
\mathcal{G}
as defined in
[
23
, Lecture 25.1]
. It is the functor that sends
S\in\mathrm{Perfd}_{k}
(here
k
is the residue field of
\breve{E}
) to the set of isomorphism classes of tuples
(S^{\#},\mathscr{P},\phi_{\mathscr{P}},i_{r})
where:

(1)

S^{\#}
is an untilt of
S
over
\mathrm{Spa}(O_{\breve{E}})
,

(2)

(\mathscr{P},\phi_{\mathscr{P}})
is a
\mathcal{G}
-shtuka over
S
with one leg along
S^{\#}
bounded by
\mu
,

(3)

i_{r}:G_{\mathcal{Y}_{[r,\infty)}(S)}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{P}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{[r,\infty)}(S)}
is a framing isomorphism of
G
-torsors for large enough
r
, under which
\phi_{\mathscr{P}}
is identified with
\phi_{b}=b\times\mathrm{Frob}_{S}
.

In
[
17
, §3.1,§3.2]
, Pappas and Rapoport define a natural Weil datum on
\cM^{\mathrm{int}}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}
from
\cO_{\breve{E}}
to
\cO_{E}
, which we recall now.
Let
\tau
be the relative Frobenius automorphism of
\breve{E}
over
E
, and define the
v
-sheaf
\cM^{\mathrm{int},(\tau)}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}
by

\cM^{\mathrm{int},(\tau)}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}(S):=\cM^{\mathrm{int},(\tau)}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}(S\times_{\Spa(k),\tau}\Spa(k)).

Let
S=\mathrm{Spa}(R,R^{+})
, with
k
-algebra structure
\varepsilon:k\to R
. Let
R_{[\tau]}
be the same ring with
k
-algebra structure
k\xrightarrow{x\mapsto x^{q}}k\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}R
for
q=|\kappa_{E}|=p^{d}
. In particular,
\mathrm{Spa}(R_{[\tau]},R^{+}_{[\tau]})=S\times_{\mathrm{Spa}(k),\tau}\mathrm{Spa}(k)
. Then the Weil datum
\Phi^{\mathrm{PR}}
is:

\displaystyle w:\cM^{\mathrm{int}}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}\longrightarrow\cM^{\mathrm{int},(\tau)}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}

which sends a point
(S^{\#},\mathscr{P},\phi_{\mathscr{P}},i_{r})
valued in
S
to a point
(S^{\#},\mathscr{P},\phi_{\mathscr{P}},i^{\prime}_{qr})
valued in
\mathrm{Spa}(R_{[\tau]},R^{+}_{[\tau]})
. The new framing
i^{\prime}_{qr}
is defined as the composition

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{Y}_{[qr,\infty)}(R_{[\tau]},R^{+}_{[\tau]})}\xrightarrow{(\phi_{b})^{d}}G_{\mathcal{Y}_{[r,\infty)}(R,R^{+})}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{P}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{[r,\infty)}(R,R^{+})}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\phi}\mathscr{P}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{[qr,\infty)}(R_{[\tau]},R^{+}_{[\tau]})}.

6.2.
Weil descent data via Shen’s construction

There is yet another natural way of constructing Weil descent data on
\mathrm{RZ}_{G}
following Shen’s construction of
\mathrm{RZ}_{G}
. The idea is as follows: A Weil descent datum in the case
G=\GL_{n}
is discussed in
[
21
]
. We can define a Weil descent datum on a Rapoport–Zink space
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}
of Hodge type by restriction along a Hodge embedding
H\hookrightarrow\GL_{n}
. Assuming
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}
is associated to
\mathrm{RZ}_{G}
, we can then "transfer" this Weil descent datum from
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}
to
\mathrm{RZ}_{G}
. We note that the above construction recovers the case of local Shimura varieties and Rapoport–Zink spaces of Hodge type from the local Shimura datum
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
.

We first review the Weil descent data in the Hodge type case as constructed in
[
12
, §7.3]
. Assume
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
is an unramified local Shimura datum of Hodge type, and fix a
Hodge embedding

\iota:(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})\to(\GL_{n},[b^{\prime}],\{\mu^{\prime}\})
, i.e.,
\iota
is a map of local Shimura data induced by a closed embedding
\iota:H\to\GL_{n}
of groups. Then by the construction of
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}
in
[
12
]
, we get a closed immersion of formal groups
\iota_{\mathrm{RZ}}:\mathrm{RZ}_{H}\to\mathrm{RZ}_{\GL_{n}}
. The latter space
\mathrm{RZ}_{\GL_{n}}
has a natural and explicit Weil descent datum in terms of
p
-divisible groups. Kim in
[
12
]
then defines the Weil descent datum on
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}
to be the restriction of that on
\mathrm{RZ}_{\GL_{n}}
along the closed immersion
\iota_{\mathrm{RZ}}
. We denote this Weil descent datum as

\Phi^{\mathrm{Kim}}_{(H,\iota)}:\mathrm{RZ}_{H}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{(\tau)},

where, as before,
\tau
is the relative Frobenius on
\breve{E}
over
E
, and
\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{(\tau)}:=\mathrm{RZ}_{H}\times_{\Spf\cO_{\breve{E}},\tau}\Spf\cO_{\breve{E}}
. As the notation suggests,
\Phi^{\mathrm{Kim}}_{(H,\iota)}
a priori depends on the choice of Hodge embedding
\iota
. We check in the next lemma that it is independent of this choice.

Lemma 6.1
.

The Weil descent datum
\Phi^{\mathrm{Kim}}_{(H,\iota)}
is independent of the Hodge embedding
\iota:H\hookrightarrow\GL_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
be an unramified local Shimura datum of Hodge type. Let
\iota:H\hookrightarrow\GL_{n}
and
\iota^{\prime}:H\hookrightarrow\GL_{m}
be two Hodge embeddings. We get a third Hodge embedding
\iota^{\prime\prime}:H\hookrightarrow\GL_{n+m}
by taking the product
\iota^{\prime\prime}:=\iota\times\iota^{\prime}
.
It then suffices to verify that the Weil descent data defined by
\iota
and
\iota^{\prime\prime}
coincide, which is clear since the induced embedding
f:\GL_{n}\to\GL_{n+m}
commutes with Weil descent data on both sides.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

The two Weil descent data
\Phi^{\mathrm{PR}}
and
\Phi^{\mathrm{Kim}}
coincide for (unramified) local Shimura data of Hodge type.

Proof.

By
Lemma

6.1
, it is enough to show this for the case
G=\GL_{n}
.

The effect of the Weil descent datum on Kim’s construction is as follows (using the convention of
[
23
]
):
(X,\xi)\mapsto(X^{(\tau)},\xi^{(\tau)})=(X,\xi\circ\phi_{b}^{d})
, where
\xi:X\to\mathbb{X}
is the quasi-isogeny providing the framing structure, and
\xi^{(\tau)}:X\to\tau^{*}X\to\tau^{*}\mathbb{X}
. The point
(X,\xi)
corresponds to a point in the moduli space of shtukas
\mathrm{Sht}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}
given by
(S^{\#},\mathcal{P},\varphi_{\mathcal{P}},i_{r})
in Pappas-Rapoport notation. The point
(X^{(\tau)},\xi^{(\tau)})
corresponds to a point in
\mathrm{Sht}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}
with the framing modified by Frobenius to the
d
th power, given by
(S^{\#},\mathcal{P},\varphi_{\mathcal{P}},i_{r}\circ\phi_{b}^{d})
. This agrees with the map in Pappas-Rapoport:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sht}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sht}_{(\mathcal{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}^{(\tau)}

\displaystyle(S^{\#},\mathcal{P},\varphi_{\mathcal{P}},i_{r})\mapsto(S^{\#},\mathcal{P},\varphi_{\mathcal{P}},i^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}})

∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
be an unramified local Shimura datum of abelian type,
\mathrm{RZ}_{G}
be its Rapoport–Zink space, and
E_{G}
be its reflex field.
There exists a Weil descent datum
\Phi^{\mathrm{Shen}}
for
\mathrm{RZ}_{G}
relative to some finite extension
E^{\mathrm{opt}}
of
E_{G}
, which depends only on
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
. When
\mathrm{RZ}_{(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})}
is of Hodge type, then
E^{\mathrm{opt}}=E_{G}
, and we recover
\Phi^{\mathrm{Shen}}=\Phi^{\mathrm{Kim}}
.

Proof.

For a local Shimura datum
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
, write
E_{H}
for its reflex field. Let
E^{\mathrm{Hdg}}
be the intersection of all
E_{H}
for all
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
unramified of Hodge type associated to
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
, for each of which the map
\beta_{H}:\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}\to\pi_{1}(H^{\mathrm{ad}})^{\Gamma}
is a surjection. Let
E^{\mathrm{opt}}
be the compositum of
E^{\mathrm{Hdg}}
and
E_{G}
. Fix a datum
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
from the family above such that
E_{H}=E^{\mathrm{Hdg}}
. From the compatibility between
\Phi^{\mathrm{PR}}
and
\Phi^{\mathrm{Kim}}
, the action of the Weil descent datum
\tau_{H}
must be equivariant with the morphism of étale sheaves
\omega_{H}:\mathrm{RZ}_{H}\to\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma}
.

Write
\Phi_{H}:\mathrm{RZ}_{H}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{(\tau_{H})}
for the Weil descent datum relative to
E_{H}
as in
Theorem

6.2
. Since
\mathrm{RZ}_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}\cong\mathrm{RZ}_{H}/X_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma}
, taking quotients by
X_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma}

7.
Relations to the Hodge case for Hodge–Newton reducibility

Recall our strategy is to use the known results of Harris–Viehmann in the Hodge–Newton reducible Hodge type case to deduce results for the Hodge–Newton reducible abelian type case. The following lemma shows that we can always find an associated local Shimura datum of Hodge type that is Hodge–Newton reducible.

We record the following group-theoretic lemma for later use. This should be well-known, but it seems hard to find a reference for it.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over a field

. Let

be the derived subgroup of

where the right-hand side is the contracted product.

Proof.

The map

\pi:G^{\mathrm{der}}\times^{Z(G^{\mathrm{der}})}Z(G)\to G,\quad(g,z)\mapsto gz

is a well-defined homomorphism.

To see this is injective, assume

. So

(z^{-1},z)
is equivalent to
(1,1)
in

\pi
is injective.

On the other hand,

\pi
is also surjective. The equality

for any central
H
is straightforward.
∎

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
be an unramified Hodge–Newton reducible local Shimura datum of abelian type, and let
(L_{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
be the local Shimura datum corresponding to a Levi subgroup
L_{G}\subsetneq G
. Then
(L_{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
is an unramified local Shimura datum of abelian type.

Proof.

Let
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
be an unramified local Shimura datum of Hodge type associated to
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
. Write
L^{\prime}
for the image of
L
under the map

. Then let
L_{H}
be preimage of
L^{\prime}
along the quotient map
H\to H^{\mathrm{ad}}
.
Thus,
L_{H}
is an (unramified) Levi subgroup of
H
, and
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
induces an unramified local Shimura datum
(L_{H},[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
of Hodge type. It is then easy to check
(L_{H},[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
is associated to
(L_{G},[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
. ∎

Recall that for an unramified local Shimura datum
(H,b_{H},\{\mu_{H}\})
of Hodge type, we have a map

\omega_{H}:\mathrm{RZ}_{H}\to c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}\pi_{1}(H)^{\Gamma},

and the fibre over the point

is by definition
\mathrm{RZ}^{+}_{H}
. The same applies to a Levi subgroup
L_{H}
of
H
, i.e.,
\mathrm{RZ}^{+}_{L_{H}}:=\omega^{-1}_{L_{H}}(c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}})
is the fibre of
\omega_{L_{H}}:\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{H}}\to c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}\pi_{1}(L_{H})^{\Gamma}
.

Since

is of Hodge type, we adopt the construction of the correspondence diagram from
[

, §4]
.

From

, we obtain a surjective homomorphism

Note we have
\varphi(c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}})=c_{b_{H},\mu_{H}}
.
Let

be the map induced by taking
\Gamma
-invariants on the map

. We get a map of cosets

We have a natural map

. We define

to be the fibre, and also set

By construction,

,
\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G}}^{+}=\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}^{+}
and
\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{G}}^{+}=\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{H}}^{+}

,
\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}
and
\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{H}}
respectively. We extend the construction to the rigid analytic generic fibre to obtain

,
\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G}}^{+\mathrm{rig}}=\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}^{+\mathrm{rig}}
and
\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{G}}^{+\mathrm{rig}}=\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{H}}^{+\mathrm{rig}}

Let

and
\pi_{1,L_{G}}^{+}=\pi_{1,L_{H}}^{+}:=\pi_{1,L_{H}}\big|_{\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{H}}^{+}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}}^{(K_{H}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{ad}}}):=\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}^{K_{H}^{\prime}}/X_{*}(Z_{H})).

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}}^{\infty}):=\varinjlim_{(K_{H}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{ad}}}\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}}^{(K_{H}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{ad}}}).

Theorem 10.6
.

Let
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
be an unramified non-basic local Shimura datum of abelian type, which is Hodge–Newton reducible with respect to a fixed parabolic subgroup
P_{G}
and Levi factor
L_{G}
. Let
E^{\mathrm{opt}}
be the finite extension of
E_{G}
constructed in
Theorem

6.3
. Then for any admissible
\overline{\Q}_{\ell}
-representation
\rho
of
J_{b_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}}(\Q_{p})
, we have an equality

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}^{\infty}_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}})_{\rho}=\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}(\Q_{p})}^{G^{\mathrm{ad}}(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}^{\infty}_{L_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}})_{\rho}.

of virtual representations of
G^{\mathrm{ad}}(\Q_{p})\times\cW_{E^{\opt}}
.

Proof.

By
[
11
, Proposition 4.3.2]
, there is an equality

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{H}}^{\infty})_{\rho}=\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}^{\infty})_{\rho}.

of virtual representations of
P_{H}(\Q_{p})\times\cW_{E_{H}}
. Recall that by definition,
H^{\mathrm{ad}}=G^{\mathrm{ad}}
,
L_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}=L_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}
and
P_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}=P_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}
.
Also note for any level
K
,

R\Hom_{J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})}(R\Gamma_{c}(\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{H}}^{K}/X_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma}),\rho)\simeq R\Hom_{J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})}(R\Gamma_{c}(\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{H}}^{K}),\rho)_{hX_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma}}

Unwinding the definition of
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}}^{\infty})_{\rho}

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}}^{\infty})_{\rho}=\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}((\mathrm{RZ}_{L_{H}}/X_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma})^{\infty})_{\rho}=\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}((\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}/X_{*}(Z_{H})^{\Gamma})^{\infty})_{\rho}=\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}}^{\infty})_{\rho}.

Now, by
[

\displaystyle\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{K_{H}}=\bigsqcup_{K_{H}\backslash H(\Q_{p})/P_{H}(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}.

\displaystyle K_{H}\backslash H(\Q_{p})/P_{H}(\Q_{p})\;

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}})=\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}(\Q_{p})}^{G^{\mathrm{ad}}(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}^{\infty}_{P_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}})_{\rho}.

Convention 10.7
.

Let
(G,[b_{G}],\{\mu_{G}\})
be an unramified local Shimura datum of abelian type and
(H,[b_{H}],\{\mu_{H}\})
an associated Hodge type. We fix an isomorphism

(G^{\mathrm{ad}},[b_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}],\{\mu_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}\})\xrightarrow{\sim}(H^{\mathrm{ad}},[b_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}],\{\mu_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}\})

of local Shimura data. For a compact open
K_{G}\subset G(\Q_{p})
, we define its
associated
subgroup
K_{H}
of
H(\Q_{p})
in the following way: Let
K_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}
be the image of
K_{G}
under the composition

G(\Q_{p})\to G^{\mathrm{ad}}(\Q_{p})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{\mathrm{ad}}(\Q_{p}).

Then define
K_{H}
as the preimage of
K_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}
along the map
H(\Q_{p})\to H^{\mathrm{ad}}(\Q_{p})
.

We combine our prior results to deduce the main theorem
Theorem

1.1
for the abelian case.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
K_{G}\subset G(\Z_{p})
and
K_{H}\subset H(\Z_{p})
be associated compact open subgroups as defined in
10.7
. Recall that we have the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{K_{H}}=\bigsqcup_{K_{H}\backslash H(\Q_{p})/P_{H}(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}^{K_{H}}

Restricting it to the
+
-components, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{K_{G},+}:=\mathrm{RZ}_{H}^{K_{H},+}=\bigsqcup_{K_{H}\backslash H(\Q_{p})/P_{H}(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}^{K_{H},+}

Since
K_{H}/(Z_{H}\cap K_{H})\cong K_{G}/(Z_{G}\cap K_{G})
by definition, we have the following chain of isomorphisms:

\displaystyle K_{H}\backslash H(\Q_{p})/P_{H}(\Q_{p})

\displaystyle\cong\;(K_{H}/Z_{H}\cap K_{H})\backslash H^{\mathrm{ad}}(\Q_{p})/P_{H^{\mathrm{ad}}}(\Q_{p})

\displaystyle\cong\;(K_{G}/Z_{G}\cap K_{G})\backslash G^{\mathrm{ad}}(\Q_{p})/P_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}}(\Q_{p})

\displaystyle\cong\;K_{G}\backslash G(\Q_{p})/P_{G}(\Q_{p})

Hence we can write

\displaystyle\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{K_{G},+}=\bigsqcup_{K_{G}\backslash G(\Q_{p})/P_{G}(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{H}}^{K_{G},+}

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{K_{G}}\cong J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{K_{G},+}

\displaystyle=J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})\bigsqcup_{K_{G}\backslash G(\Q_{p})/P_{G}(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G}}^{K_{G},+}

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})/J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})^{+}}\bigsqcup_{K_{G}\backslash G(\Q_{p})/P_{G}(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G}}^{K_{G},+}

Re-ordering the last term gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{K_{G}}=\bigsqcup_{K_{G}\backslash G(\Q_{p})/P_{G}(\Q_{p})}\bigsqcup_{J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})/J_{b_{G}}(\Q_{p})^{+}}\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G}}^{K_{G},+}=\bigsqcup_{K_{G}\backslash G(\Q_{p})/P_{G}(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G}}^{K_{G}},

which, by a similar argument as in the proof of
[
11
, Prop 4.3.3]
, then implies:

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{\infty})_{\rho}=\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{G}(\Q_{p})}^{G(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{P_{G}}^{\infty})_{\rho}

Let
L=L_{G}
and
P=P_{G}
. Combining this identity with
Corollary

10.3
gives the desired result:

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{G}^{\infty})_{\rho}=\mathrm{Ind}_{P(\Q_{p})}^{G(\Q_{p})}\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{RZ}_{L}^{\infty})_{\rho}

∎
