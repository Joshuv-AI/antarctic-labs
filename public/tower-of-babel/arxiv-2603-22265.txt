Cohesive membranes under determinant constraints
Nicola Pio Melillo, Dario Reggiani

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the variational derivation of reduced models for elastic membranes with fracture under constraints on the determinant of the deformation gradient. We consider two physically relevant settings: the non-interpenetration regime, in which the deformation is required to be orientation-preserving ( det ∇ u > 0 \det\nabla u>0 ), and the incompressible regime, in which the deformation preserves volume ( det ∇ u = 1 \det\nabla u=1 ). In both cases, the surface energy density is allowed to depend on the jump amplitude, thus encompassing cohesive fracture models with activation threshold . The main technical contribution is the construction of recovery sequences that simultaneously satisfy the determinant constraint and optimize the surface energy. This is achieved through a combination of C ∞ C^{\infty} diffeomorphisms converging to the identity (which rotate the normal to the jump set so as to minimize the reduced surface energy), and a new smooth approximation result for G ​ S ​ B ​ V p GSBV^{p} functions.

Setting of the problem.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathrm{R}{R}^{2}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary, and let
\Sigma_{\rho}:=\Sigma\times(-\rho/2,\,\rho/2)
denote the thin reference configuration of thickness
\rho>0
. We study the asymptotic behavior, as
\rho\to 0
, of the free-discontinuity functional

\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(v):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}W(\nabla v)\,dy+\int_{J_{v}}\psi([v],\nu_{v})\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}&\text{if }v\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma_{\rho};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}),\\[8.0pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise in }L^{0}(\Sigma_{\rho};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
L^{0}(\Sigma_{\rho};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
is the space of measurable functions, and
GSBV^{p}(\Sigma_{\rho};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
denotes the class of generalized special functions of bounded variation whose approximate gradient belongs to
L^{p}
and whose jump set has finite
\mathcal{H}^{2}
-measure. Here
W:\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty]
is the stored-energy density and
\psi:\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathrm{R}{S}^{2}\to[0,+\infty)
is the surface energy density.

To reduce the problem to a fixed domain, we perform the change of variables
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})=(x_{1},x_{2},\rho x_{3})

\mathcal{F}_{\rho}
can be rewritten as the rescaled energy

\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{1}}W\!\left(\nabla_{\alpha}u\,\bigg|\,\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{3}u\right)dx+\int_{J_{u}}\psi_{\rho}([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}&\text{if }u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma_{1};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}),\\[10.0pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\Sigma_{1}:=\Sigma\times(-1/2,\,1/2)
,
\nabla_{\alpha}u:=(\partial_{1}u\mid\partial_{2}u)\in\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 2}
, and
\psi_{\rho}(z,\nu):=\psi(z,\,\nu_{\alpha},\,\nu_{3}/\rho)
.

Without a determinant constraint, the
\Gamma

has been identified by Braides and Fonseca
[
15
]

\mathcal{G}_{0}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{Q}W_{0}(\nabla_{\alpha}u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}\cap\Sigma}\mathcal{B}\psi_{0}([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}&\text{if }u\in\mathcal{A},\\[8.0pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
\mathcal{A}:=\{u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\mid u\text{ is independent of }x_{3}\}
, and the reduced densities are

W_{0}(E):=\inf_{\xi\in\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}}W(E\mid\xi),\qquad\psi_{0}(z,\nu):=\inf_{\zeta\in\mathrm{R}{R}}\psi(z,\,\nu_{\alpha},\,\zeta).

Here
\mathcal{Q}W_{0}
and
\mathcal{B}\psi_{0}
denote the quasiconvex envelope and the
BV
-elliptic envelope, respectively (see Definitions

and

Assumptions on the bulk energy.

We treat two cases. In the

(
A_{1}
)

W\in C(\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 3};[0,+\infty])

(
A_{2}
)

W(F)<+\infty

(

(
A_{4}
)

for every

Condition

In the

Here

Assumptions on the surface energy.

In both cases, the surface energy density

satisfies:

(
B_{1}
)

for every

|\psi(z_{1},\nu)-\psi(z_{2},\nu)|\leq\sigma(|z_{1}-z_{2}|)(\psi(z_{1},\nu)+\psi(z_{2},\nu));

(
B_{2}
)

for every
\nu\in\mathrm{R}{S}^{2}
there exists
C_{2}\geq 1
such that
\psi(z_{1},\nu)\leq C_{2}\,\psi(z_{2},\nu)

|z_{1}|\leq|z_{2}|
, and
\psi(z_{1},\nu)\leq\psi(z_{2},\nu)

C_{2}|z_{1}|\leq|z_{2}|

(
B_{3}
)

there exist a Borel function

such that, for all

\nu\in\mathrm{R}{S}^{2}

(
B_{4}
)

\psi(z,\nu)=\psi(-z,-\nu)
;

(
B_{5}
)

\psi
is upper semicontinuous on
(\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathrm{R}{S}^{2}

We may extend
\psi

(\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}
by positive

\psi(z,\nu)=\begin{cases}|\nu|\,\psi\!\left(z,\dfrac{\nu}{|\nu|}\right)&\text{if }\nu\neq 0,\\[6.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since assumptions
(B_{1})

(B_{5})

\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}

Assumptions
(B_{1})
and
(B_{3})
together imply continuity of

[
41
]
(where

[u]

[

]
(where

\varphi(z)=1+|z|

rather than

Main results.

Our main theorems (Theorems
4.14
and
5.6
) establish that, under assumptions
(A_{1})
–
(A_{4})
(resp.
(\widetilde{A}_{1})
–
(\widetilde{A}_{2})
) and
(B_{1})
–
(B_{5})
, the rescaled functionals
\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}

\Gamma
-converge with respect to the convergence in measure to the functional
\mathcal{G}_{0}
defined in (
1.3
), in both the non interpenetration and incompressible cases.

The
\Gamma
-
\liminf
inequality follows by adapting the arguments of
[
15
]
, using the compactness guaranteed by
(B_{3})
to show that the limit deformation is independent of
x_{3}
and that
(\nu_{u})_{3}=0

\mathcal{H}^{2}
-a.e. on
J_{u}
. A key point is that the coupled growth encoded in
(B_{3})
allows one to absorb the surface energy remainder arising from the third component of the normal.

The
\Gamma
-
\limsup
inequality is considerably more delicate and constitutes the main technical contribution of this work. To date, the
\Gamma
-convergence of
\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
under a determinant constraint has been established only in the non-interpenetration setting and only for Griffith-type energies (
\psi(z,\nu)=|\nu|
) in
[
3
]
. The proof relies on the density of piecewise affine functions with maximal-rank gradient in
GSBV^{p}
, building on a classical result of Gromov and Eliashberg
[
42
,
43
]
and on the observation
[
30
]
that a domain with smooth cuts can be mapped onto a Lipschitz domain via a bi-
W^{1,\infty}
diffeomorphism close to the identity. In our framework, this density result applies only to the estimation of jump set’s length; a substantial refinement is needed to handle the jump-dependent surface energy.

We address this through two main constructions. First, for the incompressible case, since piecewise affine approximants lack the regularity needed to control the incompressibility constraint, we develop an approximation result for
GSBV^{p}
functions (Proposition
3.6
) that provides
W^{k,\infty}
-regular approximants away from the jump set, with controlled determinant. This relies on mapping the cracked domain onto a Lipschitz domain through smooth diffeomorphisms constructed via convolutions with variable kernels (Lemma
3.4
).

Second, we build a family of
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms
f_{j,\rho}
converging to the identity in
W^{k,\infty}
for every
k\geq 1
(Lemma
4.8
and Corollary
5.3
). These maps act as isometries near each segment of the jump set, rotating the normal so as to optimize the third component of
\psi
through the infimum defining
\psi_{0}
. In the incompressible case, the additional constraint
\det\nabla f_{j,\rho}=1
is enforced via an ODE-based correction (Lemma
5.2
).

The crucial intermediate step is to bound the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
by the auxiliary functional

\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}W_{0}(\nabla_{\alpha}u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}\cap\Sigma}\psi_{0}([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}&\text{if }u\in Y,\\[8.0pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
Y
consists of functions in
\mathcal{A}
whose trace on
\Sigma
belongs to
\mathrm{Aff}^{*}(\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\cap\widehat{\mathcal{W}}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
(see Definitions
2.8
and
2.10
). The passage from
\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}
to
\mathcal{G}_{0}
then follows by an adaptation of the relaxation arguments of
[
45
]
, replacing the density of local immersions with Theorem
3.1
.

To the best of our knowledge, these are the first dimension-reduction results for membranes with jump-dependent surface energy under determinant constraints. We believe that our techniques may open the way to further reduced theories involving different geometries (e.g., shells
[
2
,
5
,
20
]
) or weaker growth assumptions on
\psi
or
W
.

(y-x)\cdot\nu>0\end{subarray}}f(y)=a,\qquad\operatorname{ap}\text{-}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\to x\\
(y-x)\cdot\nu<0\end{subarray}}f(y)=b.

The triple
(a,b,\nu)
is unique up to the interchange
(a,b,\nu)\leftrightarrow(b,a,-\nu)
; we write
(f^{+}(x),f^{-}(x),\nu_{f}(x))
and define the
jump

[f](x):=f^{+}(x)-f^{-}(x)
.

The space
BV(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
consists of those

whose distributional gradient

is a finite
\mathrm{R}{M}^{m\times n}
-valued Radon measure, which decomposes as

, where
D^{a}u\ll\mathcal{L}^{n}
and
D^{s}u\perp\mathcal{L}^{n}
. The density of

with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{n}
is denoted
\nabla u
and coincides a.e. with the approximate gradient. The jump set

is countably
(n{-}1)
-rectifiable with approximate normal
\nu_{u}
.

The space
SBV(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
consists of those
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
with
|D^{s}u|(\Omega\setminus J_{u})=0
. For
p\in[1,+\infty)
,

SBV^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}):=\{u\in SBV(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}):\nabla u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{M}^{m\times n}),\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(J_{u})<+\infty\}.

We say that
u\in GSBV(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
if
\varphi(u)\in SBV_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
for every
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathrm{R}{R}^{m};\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
with compactly supported gradient. For
p\in[1,+\infty)
,

GSBV^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}):=\{u\in GSBV(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}):\nabla u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{M}^{m\times n}),\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(J_{u})<+\infty\}.

Definition 2.9
.

The class

consists of all
u\in SBV(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
such that:

•

u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{u}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
;

•

J_{u}
is essentially closed, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\overline{J_{u}}\setminus J_{u})=0
;

•

J_{u}
is a finite union of
(n{-}1)
-dimensional polyhedral pieces.

.

Let

be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary. We denote by

the subclass of

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathrm{R}{R}^{N}
be an open set, let
u\in SBV^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
, and let
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subseteq SBV^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
be such that, as
n\to\infty
,

u_{n}\to u\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}),\qquad|D(u_{n}-u)|(\Omega)\to 0,\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u_{n}}\,\triangle\,J_{u})\to 0.

Then, up to a subsequence (not relabeled), there exist a set
A\subseteq\mathrm{R}{R}^{N}
with
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(A)<+\infty
and a non-negative function
g\in L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,A)
such that:

(i)

A\supseteq J_{u_{n}}
for every
n\in\mathrm{R}{N}
;

(ii)

(u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-})\otimes\nu_{u_{n}}\to(u^{+}-u^{-})\otimes\nu_{u}
in measure with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,A
;

(iii)

up to a further subsequence,

u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-}\to u^{+}-u^{-},\qquad\nu_{u_{n}}\to\nu_{u}\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-a.e.\ on }A;

(iv)

|u_{n}^{+}(x)-u_{n}^{-}(x)|\leq g(x)
for every
n\in\mathrm{R}{N}
and
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-a.e.
x\in A
.

Proof.

We may assume that
u_{n}:\mathrm{R}{R}^{N}\to\mathrm{R}{R}
are scalar-valued, since otherwise we apply the argument component-wise.

Since
u_{n}\in SBV^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R})
, the singular part of
Du_{n}
is concentrated on
J_{u_{n}}
and satisfies

D^{s}u_{n}=(u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-})\,\nu_{u_{n}}\,\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,J_{u_{n}}.

By assumption, for every
k=1,\dots,N
,

|D^{s}_{k}u_{n}-D^{s}_{k}u|(\Omega)\to 0,

where
D^{s}_{k}u
denotes the
k
-th component of
D^{s}u
.

Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u_{n}}\,\triangle\,J_{u})<2^{-n}\qquad\text{for every }n\in\mathrm{R}{N}.

Define

A:=J_{u}\cup\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}J_{u_{n}}.

Then

\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(A)\leq\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u_{n}}\setminus J_{u})\leq\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u})+1<+\infty,

and
A\supseteq J_{u_{n}}
for every
n\in\mathrm{R}{N}
, which proves (i).

We extend the functions
(u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-})\nu_{k,u_{n}}
and
(u^{+}-u^{-})\nu_{k,u}
to all of
A
by setting them equal to zero outside
J_{u_{n}}
and
J_{u}
, respectively. With this convention,

|D^{s}_{k}u_{n}-D^{s}_{k}u|(\Omega)=\int_{A}\bigl|(u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-})\nu_{k,u_{n}}-(u^{+}-u^{-})\nu_{k,u}\bigr|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\to 0.

In particular, for each
k=1,\dots,N
, the sequence
(u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-})\nu_{k,u_{n}}
converges to
(u^{+}-u^{-})\nu_{k,u}
both in
L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,A)
and in measure with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,A
. Summing over
k
, we obtain (ii).

Up to a further subsequence, we may additionally assume that, for each
k=1,\dots,N
,

(u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-})\nu_{k,u_{n}}\to(u^{+}-u^{-})\nu_{k,u}\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-a.e.\ on }A.

By the locality properties of
SBV
functions (see
[
7
, Proposition 3.73]
), up to a change of sign of the normal vectors, we have
\nu_{u_{n}}=\nu_{u}

\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-a.e. on
J_{u_{n}}\cap J_{u}
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u_{n}}\,\triangle\,J_{u})\to 0
, this yields

\nu_{u_{n}}\to\nu_{u}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-}\to u^{+}-u^{-}\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-a.e.\ on }A,

which proves (iii).

Finally, the
L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,A)
convergence established above implies, for each
k=1,\dots,N
, the existence of a non-negative function
g_{k}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,A)
such that

|(u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-})\nu_{k,u_{n}}|\leq g_{k}\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-a.e.\ on }A,\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathrm{R}{N}.

Therefore,

|u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-}|=\sum_{k=1}^{N}|u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-}|\,|\nu_{k,u_{n}}|^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}|(u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-})\nu_{k,u_{n}}|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}g_{k}.

Setting
g:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}g_{k}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,A)
gives (iv) and concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathrm{R}{R}^{N}
be an open set, let
u\in SBV^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
, and let

(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subseteq SBV^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
satisfy, as
n\to\infty
,

u_{n}\to u\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}),\qquad|D(u_{n}-u)|(\Omega)\to 0,\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u_{n}}\,\triangle\,J_{u})\to 0.

Let
E\subseteq\Omega
be a Borel set, and let

\psi:\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}\times\mathrm{R}{S}^{N-1}\to[0,+\infty)

be upper semicontinuous, with

\psi(z,\nu)=\psi(-z,-\nu)\qquad\text{for all }(z,\nu)\in\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}\times\mathrm{R}{S}^{N-1},

and satisfying the linear growth bound

\psi(z,\nu)\leq C\,(1+|z|)\qquad\text{for all }(z,\nu)\in\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}\times\mathrm{R}{S}^{N-1},

for some constant
C>0
. Then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{J_{u_{n}}\cap E}\psi\bigl([u_{n}],\nu_{u_{n}}\bigr)\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\;\leq\;\int_{J_{u}\cap E}\psi\bigl([u],\nu_{u}\bigr)\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.11
, up to a subsequence (not relabeled), there exist a set
A\subseteq\mathrm{R}{R}^{N}
with
A\supseteq J_{u_{n}}
for every
n\in\mathrm{R}{N}
,
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(A)<+\infty
, and a non-negative function
g\in L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,A)
such that

u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-}\to u^{+}-u^{-},\qquad\nu_{u_{n}}\to\nu_{u}\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-a.e.\ on }A,

and

|u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}^{-}|\leq g\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\text{-a.e.\ on }A,\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathrm{R}{N}.

In particular, for every
n\in\mathrm{R}{N}
,

\psi\bigl([u_{n}],\nu_{u_{n}}\bigr)\,\chi_{E\cap J_{u_{n}}}\leq C\,(1+g)\in L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\!\llcorner\,A).

Since
A\supseteq J_{u_{n}}
, we may integrate over
A
, apply the reverse Fatou lemma and use the upper semicontinuity of
\psi
to obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{J_{u_{n}}\cap E}\psi\bigl([u_{n}],\nu_{u_{n}}\bigr)\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{J_{u_{n}}}\psi\bigl([u_{n}],\nu_{u_{n}}\bigr)\,\chi_{E}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\psi\bigl([u_{n}],\nu_{u_{n}}\bigr)\,\chi_{E\cap J_{u_{n}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{J_{u}}\psi\bigl([u],\nu_{u}\bigr)\,\chi_{E}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{J_{u}\cap E}\psi\bigl([u],\nu_{u}\bigr)\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1},

which concludes the proof.
∎

\delta>0
sufficiently small, we may also assume that for every

\|D\Phi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{i}\setminus\Pi_{i})}+\|D\Phi_{i}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{i}\setminus D_{i})}\leq 3,

(3.1)

and

\det M\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\quad\text{for every }M\in\partial\Phi_{i}(x),\ x\in A_{i}\setminus\Pi_{i}.

(3.2)

Define the global map
\Phi:\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{u}}\to V
by

\Phi(x):=\begin{cases}x,&\text{if }x\in\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}A_{i},\\[4.0pt]
\Phi_{i}(x),&\text{if }x\in A_{i}\setminus\Pi_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,k,\end{cases}

where

Since the sets

are pairwise disjoint,

is a bounded open Lipschitz domain, and (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) yield the global bounds

\|D\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{u}})}+\|D\Phi^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}\leq 3,\qquad\det M\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{u}}.

(3.3)

By Remark
3.3
, the image of each
\Pi_{i}
under

consists of two curves
\Pi_{i}^{+}
and
\Pi_{i}^{-}
, each being a union of finitely many line segments; in particular,
\partial V
is piecewise linear.

Step 2: Definition of the auxiliary function and approximation in
V
.

Set

by

v(x):=u\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(x)\bigr),\quad x\in V.

By construction and (
3.3
), we have

v\in W^{1,\infty}(V;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})\cap C(\overline{V};\mathrm{R}{R}^{m}).

Since
V
is a bounded open Lipschitz domain and

, reasoning as in the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
, there exists a sequence
\{w_{j}\}\subset\mathrm{Aff}^{*}(V;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
such that for every

(3.4)

By choosing

, in particular we have

w_{j}\to v\quad\text{uniformly on }\overline{V}.

(3.5)

Step 3: Definition of the approximating sequence.

For
x\in\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{u}}
, set

u_{j}(x):=w_{j}\bigl(\Phi(x)\bigr).

By construction,
u_{j}\in\mathrm{Aff}^{*}(\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{u_{j}}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})\cap\widehat{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})
, and
J_{u_{j}}\subseteq J_{u}
.
Properties (i) and (ii) follow from (
3.4
) and (
3.3
) by the same change-of-variables argument as in
[

, Theorem 3.1]
.

Step 4: Proof of property (iii).

By construction (up to a consistent orientation of the normal vectors), for every
i=1,\dots,k

u_{j}^{\pm}=w_{j}\big|_{\Pi_{i}^{\pm}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u^{\pm}=v\big|_{\Pi_{i}^{\pm}}.

The uniform convergence (
3.5
) therefore gives

u_{j}^{\pm}\to u^{\pm}\quad\text{uniformly on }J_{u}.

In particular,
[u_{j}]\to[u]
uniformly on
J_{u}
. Combined with the fact that

, we infer

\mathcal{H}^{1}\bigl(J_{u}\,\triangle\,J_{u_{j}}\bigr)\to 0\quad\text{as }j\to+\infty.

(3.6)

Collecting the uniform convergence of the traces, (ii), and (
3.6

Property (iii) then follows directly from Corollary

Since piecewise affine homeomorphisms do not provide sufficient regularity for the construction of recovery sequences under the incompressibility constraint, we adopt an alternative approach based on smooth diffeomorphisms mapping the cracked domain onto a Lipschitz domain.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a regular curve such that

S=\{(x_{1},g(x_{1}))\mid x_{1}\in[a,b]\},

where

\nabla\Phi_{\delta}\in W^{k,\infty}\!\left(\mathrm{R}{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\bigl(S\cup B_{\delta}(g(a))\cup B_{\delta}(g(b))\bigr)};\,\mathrm{R}{M}^{2\times 2}\right);

(ii)

\mathrm{R}{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta_{\delta}
is a set with Lipschitz boundary;

(iii)

\Phi_{\delta}(x)=x
for every

;

(iv)

\|\Phi_{\delta}-\operatorname{Id}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathrm{R}{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{S};\mathrm{R}{R}^{2})}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
.

Since we believe this to be a standard result but were unable to locate a precise reference in the literature, and since the argument relies on convolutions with variable kernels, a tool not used elsewhere in the paper, we include a sketch of its proof in the Appendix.

.

As a consequence of (i), (iii), and (iv) of Lemma

\delta>0
sufficiently small, the inverse map

satisfies

(i

)

,

\nabla\Phi_{\delta}^{-1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathrm{R}{R}^{2};\mathrm{R}{R}^{2\times 2})
, and, for every integer

Using the diffeomorphisms constructed in Lemma
3.4

(ii)

in

and

in

(iii)

For every

are equicontinuous on

Proof.

We divide the proof in several steps. Take

Step 1: smoothing the angle.

Let

be a connected component of
\overline{J_{u}}

J_{u}
are well separated, we may argue locally and assume without loss of

J_{u}
consists of a single component. Since

, the set
\Pi
is either a segment or the
union of two segments that meet at a single endpoint
x_{0}
. Let us consider the latter case, as the first one is in fact simpler. We can find a

\Xi:\mathrm{R}{R}^{2}\to\mathrm{R}{R}^{2}

,
where

is a smooth curve that can be written (up to a rotation) as the graph of a

Let us define

Step 2: mapping into the Lipschitz domain.

By definition,

satisfies the assumptions of Lemma

\Phi:\Omega\setminus J_{w}\to\Omega\setminus\Delta=:U

\|\nabla\Phi-I\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{R}^{2})}+\|\nabla\Phi^{-1}-I\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathrm{R}{R}^{2})}\leq\sigma,

(3.7)

\det(\nabla\Phi(x))\geq 1-\sigma\quad\text{for every }x\in\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{w}}.

Let
V\subset\Omega
be the open set such that

By Lemma
3.4
, the set

(3.8)

where

Possibly taking

and that

Setting

Let

By standard properties of convolution,

\|v_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}((U)_{-\delta_{j}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})}\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})},\qquad\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}((U)_{-\delta_{j}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{m\times 2})}\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m\times 2})}.

(3.9)

Moreover, we preliminary observe that since
u\in\mathrm{Aff}^{*}(\Omega;{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{m})
, we can take
\sigma>0
and
\delta_{j}>0
sufficiently small so that for every

\nabla u(y)\in\partial u(\zeta)\quad\text{for every }y\in B_{(1+\sigma)\delta_{j}}\!\left(z\right)\cap\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{u}}.

(3.10)

Let us take
\sigma>0
and
\delta_{j}>0
small so that (

) is verified. We claim that for every

Fix such an

. Since

, for every

we have
\Xi^{-1}(\Phi^{-1}(y))=\Phi^{-1}(y)
, hence

\nabla v_{j}(x)=\int_{B_{\delta_{j}}(x)}\nabla u\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(y)\right)\nabla\Phi^{-1}(y)\,\rho_{\delta_{j}}(x-y)\,dy.

(3.11)

By (

\mathrm{Lip}(\Phi^{-1})\leq 1+\sigma

\Phi^{-1}(B_{\delta_{j}}(x))\subset B_{(1+\sigma)\delta_{j}}\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(x)\right).

(3.12)

There exists
\zeta\in\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{u}}

\nabla u(y)\in\partial u(\zeta)\quad\text{for every }y\in B_{(1+\sigma)\delta_{j}}\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(x)\right)\cap\Omega\setminus\overline{J_{u}}.

(3.13)

Using the piecewise affine structure of

3.12

\int_{B_{\delta_{j}}(x)}\nabla u\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(y)\right)\rho_{\delta_{j}}(x-y)\,dy=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\lambda_{\ell}\nabla u(y_{\ell})\in\partial u(\zeta).

(3.14)

Combining (
3.7

3.11

\det\!\big((\nabla v_{j}(x))^{T}\nabla v_{j}(x)\big)\geq\eta-\omega\!\left(\sigma C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{M}^{m\times 2})}^{2}\right),

\sigma

Since

\|\widetilde{v}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})}\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathrm{R}{R}^{m})},\qquad\|\nabla\widetilde{v}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathrm{R}{M}^{m\times 2})}\leq 2\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathrm{R}{M}^{m\times 2})}.

Moreover, for
\delta_{j}
sufficiently small and every

Finally,

in

and, by the properties of

, this convergence is uniform on

.

Let

Verification of (i).

By (

) and (

(B_{4})

(B_{5})
follow directly from the definition, together with the fact that the infimum of upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous. So it remains to prove

.
Consider two points

and

. Then, given

by property
(B_{1})
of

. Taking the infimum over

on the right-hand side, we obtain

Exchanging the roles of

(B_{1})

For

(B_{3})
of
\psi

To obtain the upper bound, we simply observe that

is arbitrary, we conclude that

\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u)\leq\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}(u)\qquad\text{for every }u\in Y.

For the remaining functions
u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma_{1};\mathrm{R}R^{3})\setminus Y
, the inequality above is trivially satisfied.
Finally, by lower semicontinuity of the
\Gamma\text{-}\limsup
(see
[
25
, Proposition 6.8]
), the assertion follows.
∎

Roughly speaking, what we have been able to prove is that

\mathcal{G}_{0}(u)\leq\Gamma\text{-}\liminf_{\rho\to 0}\,\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}(u)\leq\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\,\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}(u)\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}(u).

We now observe that we are not yet able to represent
\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}
as an integral functional, because the upper
p
-growth condition appearing in Theorem
2.15
is not satisfied by the bulk energy. We now prove that

\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}=\overline{\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}},

where

\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{R}W_{0}(\nabla u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}}\psi_{0}([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}&\text{if }u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}),\\[10.00002pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Here,
\mathcal{R}W_{0}
denotes the rank–one convex envelope of
W_{0}
. In Lemma
4.2
we have shown that
\mathcal{R}W_{0}
is finite-valued, continuous, and satisfies a
p
-growth condition. This will allow us to apply the integral representation theorem to the functional
\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}
. Subsequently, we will show that
\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}=\overline{\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}}
. To this end, we need two preliminary lemmas. The first one is a result from Kohn and Strang
[
47
, Section 5C]
, while the second one is a technical Lemma contained in
[
45
]
(see also
[
3
, Lemma A.1]
for a proof) which is crucial for our asymptotic analysis.

Lemma 4.11
.

We define a sequence
\mathcal{R}_{i}W_{0}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{0}W_{0}=W_{0}
and, for every
i\geq 0
and every
F\in\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 2}
,

\mathcal{R}_{i+1}W_{0}(F)=\inf_{a\in\mathrm{R}{R}^{2},\;b\in\mathrm{R}{R}^{2},\;\lambda\in[0,1]}\Big\{(1-\lambda)\,\mathcal{R}_{i}W_{0}(F-\lambda\,b\otimes a)+\lambda\,\mathcal{R}_{i}W_{0}(F-(1-\lambda)\,b\otimes a)\Big\}.

We have that
\mathcal{R}_{i}W_{0}
is upper semicontinuous for every
i\geq 0
and finite-valued for every
i\geq 2
. Moreover,
\mathcal{R}_{i+1}W_{0}\leq\mathcal{R}_{i}W_{0}
for every
i\geq 0
, and

\mathcal{R}W_{0}=\inf_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{R}_{i}W_{0}.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
V\subseteq\Sigma
be an open set such that
|\partial V|=0
and let
F\in\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 2}
have rank
2
. Then there exists a sequence
\{h_{n,l,q}\}_{n,l,q\geq 1}\subseteq\mathrm{Aff}_{C}(V;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
such that:

(1)

for every
l,q\geq 1
, we have
\lim_{n\to+\infty}h_{n,l,q}=0
in
L^{p}(V;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
;

(2)

for every
n,l,q\geq 1
, the function
x\mapsto h_{n,l,q}(x)+Fx+c
belongs to
\mathrm{Aff}^{*}(V;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
for every
c\in\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}
;

(3)

\lim_{q\to+\infty}\,\lim_{l\to+\infty}\,\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{V}\mathcal{R}_{i}W_{0}(\nabla h_{n,l,q}+F)\,dx\;\leq\;|V|\,\mathcal{R}_{i+1}W_{0}(F).

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}
be as defined above. For every
u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
we have

\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u)\;\leq\;\overline{\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}}(u).

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.

We show that for every

v\in\mathrm{Aff}^{*}(\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{v}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\cap\widehat{\mathcal{W}}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
,

\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}(v)\leq\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{R}W_{0}(\nabla v)\,dx+\int_{J_{v}}\psi_{0}([v],\nu_{v})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

The proof follows the same lines as
[
3
, Proposition 4.14, Step 1]
. The key observation is that the optimizing sequence provided by Lemma
4.12
has support compactly contained in the complement of the jump set of
v
, and therefore does not interact with the surface energy. Consequently, the argument of
[
3
]
, originally carried out for
\psi(z,\nu)=|\nu|
, extends verbatim to a general surface energy density
\psi
satisfying
(B_{1})
–
(B_{5})
.

Step 2.

We now move to the general case
u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
.
From Theorem
4.10
we already know that for every
u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
,

\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u)\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}(u).

We now want to show that for every
u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma,\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
,

\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}(u)\leq\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{R}W_{0}(\nabla u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}}\psi_{0}([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

To this end, by applying Corollary
2.12
and Remark
2.17
, we obtain the existence of a sequence

u_{j}\in\mathrm{Aff}^{*}(\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}},\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\cap\widehat{\mathcal{W}}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})

such that
u_{j}\to u
in measure,
\nabla u_{j}\to\nabla u
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 2})
, and

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{J_{u_{j}}}\psi([u_{j}],\nu_{u_{j}})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq\int_{J_{u}}\psi([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

By applying Lemma
4.2
together with the convergence

\nabla u_{j}\to\nabla u
in
L^{p}
, we obtain

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{R}W_{0}(\nabla u_{j})\,dx=\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{R}W_{0}(\nabla u)\,dx.

Therefore, using the lower semicontinuity of the functional
\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}(u_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\left(\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{R}W_{0}(\nabla u_{j})\,dx+\int_{J_{u_{j}}}\psi([u_{j}],\nu_{u_{j}})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{R}W_{0}(\nabla u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}}\psi([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

Passing to the relaxation, we obtain

\overline{\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}}(u)\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{R}}_{0}}(u),

which proves the claim.
∎

4.3.
Main result

We now have all the tools to prove the main result in the non-interpenetration case.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
\Sigma
be a bounded open subset of
\mathrm{R}R^{2}
with Lipschitz boundary and let
1<p<\infty
.
Let
W
satisfy assumptions
(A_{1})-(A_{4})
, and let
\psi
satisfy hypotheses
(B_{1})-(B_{5})
.
Then,

\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}=\mathcal{G}_{0}

in the space of measurable functions with respect to convergence in measure.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, we may apply relaxation to the functional

\overline{\mathcal{G}^{R}_{0}}
. Therefore, using Theorem
2.15
, for every
u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma,\mathrm{R}R^{3})
we obtain

\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u)\leq\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{R}W_{0})(\nabla u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}}\mathcal{B}\psi_{0}([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

Combining this with Proposition
4.5
, we deduce that

\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{R}W_{0})(\nabla u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}}\mathcal{B}\psi_{0}([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq\mathcal{G}_{0}(u)\leq\Gamma\text{-}\liminf_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u).

Therefore, for every function
u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma,\mathrm{R}R^{3})
,

\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{R}W_{0})(\nabla u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}}\mathcal{B}\psi_{0}([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u).

It only remains to show that
\mathcal{Q}W_{0}=\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{R}W_{0})
.
One inequality, namely “
\geq
”, is trivial.
For the reverse inequality, notice that by the definition of rank-one convexification and by Lemma
4.2
, we have
\mathcal{Q}W_{0}\leq\mathcal{R}W_{0}
.
Applying
\mathcal{Q}
to both sides yields

\mathcal{Q}W_{0}\leq\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{R}W_{0}).

This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.
Dimension Reduction under Incompressibility

In this section we consider the incompressible case. We recall that in this setting the bulk energy
W:\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty]
satisfies
(\widetilde{A}_{1})
–
(\widetilde{A}_{2})
, while
\psi\colon({\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\})\times{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfies
(B_{1})
–
(B_{5})
.
We start observing that by assumption
(\widetilde{A}_{2})
on
W
, given
F=(F_{1}\mid F_{2})\in\mathrm{R}{R}^{3\times 2}
with
|F_{1}\wedge F_{2}|\neq 0
, the following bounds hold

\frac{1}{c}|F|^{p}+\frac{1}{c}\frac{1}{|F_{1}\wedge F_{2}|^{p}}-c\leq W_{0}(F)\leq c|F|^{p}+c\frac{1}{|F_{1}\wedge F_{2}|^{p}}+c.

(5.1)

If instead
\operatorname{rank}(F)\leq 1
, then
W_{0}(F)=+\infty
.

Thanks to (
5.1
),
W_{0}
satisfies all the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
.

Thus, following the same arguments as in Proposition
4.5
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a bounded open subset of
\mathrm{R}{R}^{2}
with Lipschitz boundary and let
1<p<+\infty
. Let
W
be a function satisfying
(\widetilde{A}_{1})
and
(\widetilde{A}_{2})
, and let
\psi
be a function satisfying
(B_{1})
–
(B_{5})
. Then, for every sequence
u_{\rho}\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
such that
u_{\rho}\to u
in measure, one has

\liminf_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u_{\rho})\geq\mathcal{G}_{0}(u).

To prove the analogue of Theorem
4.10
, however, we require the following technical lemma, which is based on a construction from
[
21
, Proposition 5.1]
. For completeness, and in order to make explicit the dependence of certain constants, we include the full proof.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u\in W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}},\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\cap SBV^{p}(\Sigma,\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
and

b\in W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}},\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
for every
k\geq 1
,
such that

\det(\nabla u(x)\,|\,b(x))=1\quad\text{for almost every }x\in\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}}.

Assume moreover that
J_{u}
consists of a finite union of regular curves. Then there exist
\rho>0

and a function

v\in W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma_{\rho}\setminus\overline{J_{v}},\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\cap SBV^{p}(\Sigma_{\rho},\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})

for every
k\geq 1
, such that
v(x,0)=u(x)
,

J_{v}\subseteq J_{u}\times(-\rho/2,\rho/2)
, and

\det(\nabla v(x))=1\quad\text{for almost every }x\in\Sigma_{\rho}.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(u,b)>0
such that for almost every
x\in\Sigma_{\rho}

|\nabla v(x)-(\nabla u(x)\,|\,b(x))|\leq C|x_{3}|.

(5.2)

Proof.

Let
\rho>0
small. Define the function
w\in SBV^{p}(\Sigma_{\rho},{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3})
as
w(x_{\alpha},x_{3}):=u(x_{\alpha})+x_{3}b(x_{\alpha})
. By construction we also have
w\in W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma_{\rho}\setminus\overline{J_{u}\times(-\rho/2,\rho/2)},{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3})
for every
k\geq 1
. Set
J_{u,\rho}:=J_{u}\times(-\rho/2,\rho/2)
for brevity. We have

\det\nabla w(x_{\alpha},x_{3})=\det(\nabla u(x_{\alpha})+x_{3}\nabla b(x_{\alpha})|b(x_{\alpha}))=1+x_{3}P(x_{\alpha})+x_{3}^{2}Q(x_{\alpha}),

(5.3)

where
P
and
Q
are combinations of the components of
\nabla u
,
b
and
\nabla b
which are all elements of
L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
by assumption, hence uniformly bounded. Therefore, for
x_{3}\in(-\rho/2,\rho/2)
, we have
|\det\nabla w(x_{\alpha},x_{3})-1|\leq C|x_{3}|
for every
x_{\alpha}\in\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}}
, where
C=C(u,b)>0
.

Let
\{\Omega^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a family of open sets with smooth boundary such that for every
i\geq 1
,
\Omega^{i}\subset\Omega^{i+1}
,
\Omega^{i}\subset\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}}
and
\Omega^{i}\to\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}}
as
i\to+\infty
in the Hausdorff sense. The sets
\Omega^{i}
might not be connected, however, the number of connected components composing the sets
\Omega^{i}
’s is uniformly bounded in
i
. Set
\Omega^{i}_{\rho}:=\Omega^{i}\times(-\rho/2,\rho/2)
. Then, for every
i\geq 1
, we can find
\Gamma^{i}:\Omega^{i}_{\rho}\to{\mathrm{R}{R}}
such that
\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},\cdot)
is the solution of the following ODE

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{3}\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})=\frac{1}{\det\nabla w(x_{\alpha},\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3}))}&for $x_{3}\in(-\rho/2,\rho/2)$,\\
\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},0)=0;\end{cases}

(5.4)

for
\rho>0
small and for every
x_{\alpha}\in\Omega^{i}
. If
\Omega^{i}
is not connected, we solve the problem in each connected component.
The set
\Omega_{\rho}^{i}
has Lipschitz boundary which gives
w\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega^{i}_{\rho}},{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3})
. Therefore, by standard theory of parameter dependent ODEs, we have that
\Gamma^{i}
is smooth on
\overline{\Omega_{\rho}^{i}}
. Moreover, by the previous bound on
\det\nabla w
, we also have
|\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})-x_{3}|\leq Cx_{3}^{2}
for every
i\geq 1
. Thus, we infer that the function
v^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3}):=u(x_{\alpha})+\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})b(x_{\alpha})
is smooth in
\overline{\Omega_{\rho}^{i}}
for
\rho
small enough.

Differentiating (
5.3
) with respect to
x_{1}
, for
x_{3}\in(-\rho/2,\rho/2)
and
x_{\alpha}\in\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}}
we get

|\partial_{1}\det\nabla w(x_{\alpha},x_{3})|\leq|x_{3}\partial_{1}P(x_{\alpha})|+|x_{3}^{2}\partial Q(x_{\alpha})|\leq C|x_{3}|,

where
C
may now depends also on the second derivatives of
u
and
b
. Differentiating with respect to
x_{1}
the identity
x_{3}=\int_{0}^{\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})}\det\nabla w(x_{\alpha},t)\,dt
we get

0=\int_{0}^{\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})}\partial_{1}\det\nabla w(x_{\alpha},t)\,dt+\det\nabla w(x_{\alpha},\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3}))\partial_{1}\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3}),

for
x_{\alpha}\in\Omega_{\rho}^{i}
. Therefore, recalling that for
\rho
small we have
\det\nabla w\in[1/2,2]
,

|\partial_{1}\Gamma^{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})|\leq 2\int_{0}^{\Gamma(x_{\alpha},x_{3})}|\partial_{1}\nabla w(x_{\alpha},t)|\,dt\leq Cx_{3}^{2}\qquad\mbox{ for $(x_{\alpha},x_{3})\in\Omega_{\rho}^{i}$}.

The bound for
|\partial_{2}\Gamma^{i}|
can be obtained analogously. Notice that the bounds on the derivatives of
\Gamma^{i}
hold with a constant
C=C(u,b)>0
independent of
i
.
Moreover, by uniqueness of solution of the ODE (
5.4
), we have that
\Gamma^{i+1}|_{\Omega^{i}_{\rho}}=\Gamma^{i}
. Thus, we can define a global function
\Gamma\in W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma_{\rho}\setminus\overline{J_{u,\rho}})
for every
k\geq 1
such that
\Gamma|_{\Omega^{i}_{\rho}}=\Gamma_{i}
for every
i\geq 1
and the bounds on the derivatives of
\Gamma
hold for every
x\in\Sigma_{\rho}\setminus\overline{J_{u,\rho}}
. In particular,
J_{\Gamma}\subseteq\overline{J_{u,\rho}}
.

By setting
v(x_{\alpha},x_{3}):=u(x_{\alpha})+\Gamma(x_{\alpha},x_{3})b(x_{\alpha})
, we have
J_{v}\subseteq\overline{J_{u,\rho}}
and,
since
\Gamma
satisfies (
5.4
),
\det(\nabla v(x))=1
for almost every
x\in\Sigma_{\rho}
. Finally, observing that

\nabla v=(\partial_{1}u+\partial_{1}\Gamma b+\Gamma\partial_{1}b\ |\ \partial_{2}u+\partial_{2}\Gamma b+\Gamma\partial_{2}b\ |\ \partial_{3}\Gamma b)

and using in turn that
|\partial_{1}\Gamma|+|\partial_{2}\Gamma|\leq Cx_{3}^{2}
and
|\partial_{3}\Gamma-1|\leq C|x_{3}|
, we obtain (
5.2
) for almost every
x\in\Sigma_{\rho}
with
C=C(u,b)
.
∎

In the next result we show that the map constructed in Lemma
4.8
can be modified so as to satisfy the incompressibility constraint
\det\nabla f_{\rho}=1
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
U
,
V
, and
\Sigma
be the open sets appearing in Lemma
4.8
.
Then, for
\rho>0
sufficiently small, there exists a map
f_{\rho}
satisfying (a)–(f) of Lemma
4.8
and, in addition,

\det\nabla f_{\rho}=1\quad\text{for every }x\in\Sigma_{2}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\Sigma
is connected; otherwise, it suffices to argue on each
connected component separately.
Let
\{g_{\rho}\}_{\rho}
be the family of diffeomorphisms given by Lemma
4.8
.
Consider the sequence of functions

u_{\rho}(x_{\alpha}):=g_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},0).

Since, by Lemma
4.8
,

g_{\rho}\to\mathrm{Id}
in
W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3})
, there exists
\rho>0
sufficiently small such that

\det(\nabla g_{\rho})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}

uniformly in
\Sigma_{2}
. Hence, the sequence

b_{\rho}(x_{\alpha}):=\frac{\partial_{3}g_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},0)}{\det(\nabla g_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},0))}

is well defined.

The assumptions of Lemma
5.2
are therefore satisfied with
J_{u_{\rho}}=\emptyset
.
Moreover, using again that
g_{\rho}\to\mathrm{Id}
in
W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3})
, for
\rho
sufficiently small the ODE (
5.4
) with

w_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},x_{3}):=u_{\rho}(x_{\alpha})+x_{3}b_{\rho}(x_{\alpha})

is solvable on the full interval
(-1,1)
, giving
\Gamma_{\rho}\colon\Sigma_{2}\to{\mathrm{R}{R}}
which solves the ODE. Hence, we infer the existence of a sequence of
C^{\infty}
functions
f_{\rho}
on
\Sigma_{2}
such that
f_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},0)=u_{\rho}(x_{\alpha})
and

\det(\nabla f_{\rho}(x))=1\qquad\mbox{for every $x\in\Sigma_{2}$}.

We recall that the function
f_{\rho}
is defined in the following way,

f_{\rho}(x)=u_{\rho}(x_{\alpha})+\Gamma_{\rho}(x)\,b_{\rho}(x_{\alpha}),

where
\Gamma_{\rho}
solves the ODE and thus the integral equation

\Gamma_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})=\int_{0}^{x_{3}}\frac{1}{\det\nabla w_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},\Gamma(x_{\alpha},s))}\,ds.

We now verify that
f_{\rho}
satisfies properties (a)–(f).

In
\overline{U}_{2}
, by Lemma
4.8
(a),
g_{\rho}
acts as the isometry
O_{\rho}
, so
u_{\rho}(x_{\alpha})=O_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},0)
,
b_{\rho}(x_{\alpha})=O_{\rho}\,e_{3}
(since
\det O_{\rho}=1
), and therefore
w_{\rho}(x)=O_{\rho}\,x
. It follows that
\det\nabla w_{\rho}=1
, so the ODE gives
\Gamma_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})=x_{3}
. By construction,
f_{\rho}(x)=O_{\rho}\,x=g_{\rho}(x)
in
\overline{U}_{2}
, which yields (a)

Similarly, by Lemma
4.8
(b), in
\Sigma_{2}\setminus\overline{V_{2}}
,
g_{\rho}=\mathrm{Id}
, so
u_{\rho}(x_{\alpha})=(x_{\alpha},0)
,
b_{\rho}=e_{3}
,
w_{\rho}=\mathrm{Id}
, and again
\Gamma_{\rho}=x_{3}
, giving
f_{\rho}=\mathrm{Id}
, which yields (b).

We now prove (c). Since
g_{\rho}\to\mathrm{Id}
in
W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
for every
k\geq 1
, we have
u_{\rho}\to(\cdot,0)
and
b_{\rho}\to e_{3}
in
W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
, hence
w_{\rho}\to\mathrm{Id}
in
W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
.
Consequently,

\det\nabla w_{\rho}\to 1\quad\text{in }W^{k-1,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3}),

which implies
\Gamma_{\rho}\to x_{3}
in
W^{k-1,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3})
, and therefore
f_{\rho}\to\mathrm{Id}
in
W^{k-1,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
for every
k\geq 1
.

Point (d) follows from (a) and (c) by the same argument used in the proof of Lemma
4.8
.

It remains to establish (e) and (f). Since (f) is a direct consequence of (c) and (e), it suffices to prove (e).

This follows immediately by estimating, for
i=1,2
and every
x\in\Sigma_{2}
,

\frac{1}{\rho}\left|(\nabla f_{\rho})^{3}_{i}(x)\right|=\frac{1}{\rho}|\partial_{3}\Gamma_{\rho}(x)|\,\frac{|(\partial_{3}g_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},0))_{i}|}{|\det(\nabla g_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},0))|}\leq 2\frac{|(\partial_{3}g_{\rho}(x_{\alpha},0))_{i}|}{\rho}\leq 2|\zeta|,

where we used that
\det\nabla g_{\rho}\to 1
and
\partial_{3}\Gamma_{\rho}\to 1
uniformly as
\rho\to 0
, together with property (e) of Lemma
4.8
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

As in Remark
4.9
, using (b) and (c) of Corollary
5.3
, we infer that for
\rho
sufficiently small, the map
f_{\rho}
is a

diffeomorphism from
\Sigma_{2}
to its image. Moreover, for every

,

\nabla f^{-1}_{\rho}\bigl(f_{\rho}(x)\bigr)=\bigl[\nabla f_{\rho}(x)\bigr]^{-1}.

Again, using this identity, one can show that properties (c),(e) and (f) Corollary
5.3
also hold for
f^{-1}_{\rho}
on the image of
\Sigma_{2}
through
f_{\rho}
.

We are now in a position to prove an analogue of Theorem
4.10
for the upper bound of the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma_{1};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
. Then

\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u)\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\,w}}(u),

where
\overline{\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\,w}}(u)
denotes the relaxation of
\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\,w}(u)
.

Proof.

We divide the proof in three steps.

Step 1: Construction of the recovery sequence
. Let
u\in Y
. Its trace on
\Sigma
belongs to the space

\mathrm{Aff}^{*}(\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\cap\widehat{\mathcal{W}}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}).

In particular, there exists a finite family of open sets
\{T^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
such that

\mathcal{L}^{2}\!\left(\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}T^{i}\right)=0
,

\mathcal{L}^{2}(\partial T^{i})=0
for every
i=1,\dots,N
, and

\nabla u|_{T^{i}}=A_{i}
with
A_{i}\in\mathrm{R}{R}^{3\times 2}
satisfying

|A_{i}^{1}\wedge A_{i}^{2}|\geq\eta>0,\qquad\text{for every }i=1,\dots,N.

By the coercivity of
W
, for each
A_{i}
there exists
b_{i}\in\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}
such that

W(A_{i}\mid b_{i})=W_{0}(A_{i})
. Hence, we can find a piecewise constant function

b:\Sigma\to\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}
such that
b|_{T^{i}}=b_{i}
for every
i=1,\dots,N
and

\det(\nabla u(x)\mid b(x))=1,\qquad W(\nabla u(x)\mid b(x))=W_{0}(\nabla u(x))\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Sigma.

(5.5)

Since
b
satisfies the determinant constraint in (
5.5
), for a.e.
x\in\Sigma

the function
b
can be written as

b(x)=\lambda\,\partial_{1}u(x)+\Lambda\,\partial_{2}u(x)+|\partial_{1}u(x)\wedge\partial_{2}u(x)|^{-2}\,\partial_{1}u(x)\wedge\partial_{2}u(x),

where
\lambda,\Lambda:\Sigma\to\mathrm{R}{R}
are piecewise constant functions.
Notice that
b
is piecewise constant on a finite partition of
\Sigma
, hence

b\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, which in particular implies
\lambda,\Lambda\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
.
Therefore, there exist sequences
\lambda_{j},\Lambda_{j}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that

\lambda_{j}\to\lambda
and
\Lambda_{j}\to\Lambda
in
L^{p}(\Sigma)
as
j\to+\infty
, with

\|\lambda_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\leq\|\lambda\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)},\qquad\|\Lambda_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\leq\|\Lambda\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}.

(5.6)

Moreover, by applying Proposition
3.6
to
u
, we find a sequence

u_{j}\in SBV^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\cap W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{u_{j}}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\quad\text{for every }k\geq 1,

satisfying properties (i)–(iv) of the proposition.

For every
x\in\Sigma\setminus J_{u}
, we define

b_{j}(x):=\lambda_{j}\,\partial_{1}u_{j}(x)+\Lambda_{j}\,\partial_{2}u_{j}(x)+|\partial_{1}u_{j}(x)\wedge\partial_{2}u_{j}(x)|^{-2}\,\partial_{1}u_{j}(x)\wedge\partial_{2}u_{j}(x).

By construction,
J_{b_{j}}\subseteq J_{u_{j}}\Subset\Sigma
and

b_{j}\in W^{k,p}(\Sigma\setminus\overline{J_{b_{j}}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
for every
k\geq 1
.
Furthermore,
b_{j}\to b
pointwise a.e. in
\Sigma
and, by properties (i)–(v) of
Proposition
3.6
and (
5.6
), for every
j\geq 1
,

\|b_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\Sigma;{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3})\leq 8\big(\|\lambda\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}+\|\Lambda\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\big)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 2})}+\frac{64}{\theta^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 2})}^{2}.

(5.7)

This implies that
b_{j}\to b
in
L^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
.

We define
J_{j,\rho}:=J_{u_{j}}\times(-\rho/2,\rho/2)
for
j\geq 1
and
\rho>0
.
Notice that the pair
(u_{j},b_{j})
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
5.2

for every
j\geq 1
. Therefore, letting
j\to+\infty
, we can select a sequence

\rho_{j}\to 0
and construct a family

v_{j}\in W^{k,\infty}(\Sigma_{\rho_{j}}\setminus\overline{J_{j,\rho_{j}}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\cap SBV^{p}(\Sigma_{\rho_{j}};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})\quad\text{for every }k\geq 1,

such that
v_{j}(x_{\alpha},0)=u_{j}(x_{\alpha})
,
J_{v_{j}}\subseteq J_{j,\rho_{j}}
, and

\det(\nabla v_{j}(x))=1\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Sigma_{\rho_{j}}.

Moreover, by (
5.2
), for every
j\geq 1
there exists a constant
C_{j}>0
such that, for a.e.
x\in\Sigma_{\rho_{j}}
,

\big|\nabla v_{j}(x)-(\nabla u_{j}(x_{\alpha})\mid b_{j}(x_{\alpha}))\big|\leq C_{j}|x_{3}|.

(5.8)

We now focus on the jump set of
u_{j}
. By property (v) of Proposition
3.6
, for every
\varepsilon>0
and
j
sufficiently large, there exists an open set
A_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Sigma
such that
J_{u_{j}}\cap A_{\varepsilon}=J_{u}\cap A_{\varepsilon}
,
\mathcal{H}^{1}(J_{u_{j}}\setminus A_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon
, and
J_{u}\cap A_{\varepsilon}
is a finite union of disjoint segments. Thus, arguing as in Step 1 of Theorem
4.10
(with Corollary
5.3
in place of Lemma
4.8
to ensure the incompressibility constraint
\det\nabla f_{j,\rho}=1
and with
J_{u_{j}}\cap A_{\varepsilon}
in place of
J_{u}
), we construct a map
f_{j,\rho}:\Sigma_{2}\to\mathrm{R}{R}^{3}
satisfying properties (a)–(f) of Lemma
4.8
,
\det\nabla f_{j,\rho}=1
on
\Sigma_{2}
, and
f_{j,\rho}\to\mathrm{Id}
in
W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
as
\rho\to 0
. We also find a set
E\subset\Sigma
such that
\mathcal{L}^{2}(E)\leq\varepsilon
,
J_{u}\cap A_{\varepsilon}\subset E
, and
f_{j,\rho}\equiv\text{Id}
on
\Sigma_{2}\setminus\left(E\times(-1,1)\right)
.

Next, for every
\rho\leq\rho_{j}/2
, we denote by

w_{j,\rho}(x):=v_{j}(x_{\alpha},\rho x_{3})

and finally, we define our
recovery sequence
as

u_{j,\rho}:=w_{j,\rho}(g_{j,\rho}(x)),

where
g_{j,\rho}=f^{-1}_{j,\rho}
.
We note that, by Remark
4.9
, for
\rho
sufficiently small we have that
g_{j,\rho}
is a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism such that

g_{j,\rho}(\Sigma_{1})\subseteq\Sigma_{2}
; hence the function
{u}_{j,\rho}

is well defined.
By construction
u_{j,\rho}\in SBV^{p}(\Sigma_{1},\mathrm{R}R^{3})
. Moreover,
J_{{u}_{j,\rho}}\cap\left(A_{\varepsilon}\times(-1/2,1/2)\right)=J_{{u}_{j}}\cap\left(A_{\varepsilon}\times(-1/2,1/2)\right)
, and

J_{{u}_{j,\rho}}\subseteq g^{-1}_{j,\rho}(J_{u_{j}}\times(-1/2,1/2))=f_{j,\rho}(J_{u_{j}}\times(-1/2,1/2)).

We can extend
u_{j}
and
u
to
\Sigma_{1}
by setting them constant in
x_{3}
. Recalling Lemma
5.2
, we infer that for every
j\geq 1

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\|w_{j,\rho}-u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{1};{\mathrm{R}{R}}^{3})}=0.

(5.9)

Furthermore, by a change of variables given by
g_{j,\rho}

and since
g_{j,\rho}\to\text{Id}
in
W^{1,\infty}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\|u_{j,\rho}-u\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\|u_{j,\rho}-w_{j,\rho}\|_{L^{p}}+\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\|w_{j,\rho}-u\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\|w_{j,\rho}-u\|_{L^{p}}=\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\|u_{j}-u\|_{L^{p}}=0,

from which the convergence in
L^{p}
follows.

Step 2: Estimate of the surface energy
. Let us now consider the surface part of
\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
. Arguing as in the beginning of Step 2 of Theorem
4.10
, we infer that

\psi_{\rho}([u_{j,\rho}],\nu_{u_{j,\rho}})\leq C\qquad\mbox{on $J_{u_{j,\rho}}$}.

(5.10)

Therefore, using that
\mathcal{H}^{2}\left(J_{u_{j,\rho}}\setminus\left(A_{\varepsilon}\times(-1/2,1/2)\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon
by construction, we estimate

\displaystyle\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\int_{J_{u_{j,\rho}}}\psi_{\rho}\big([u_{j,\rho}],\nu_{u_{j,\rho}}\big)\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\int_{J_{u_{j,\rho}}\cap\left(A_{\varepsilon}\times(-1/2,1/2)\right)}\psi_{\rho}\big([u_{j,\rho}],\nu_{u_{j,\rho}}\big)\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}

\displaystyle+\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\int_{J_{u_{j,\rho}}\setminus\left(A_{\varepsilon}\times(-1/2,1/2)\right)}\psi_{\rho}\big([u_{j,\rho}],\nu_{u_{j,\rho}}\big)\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\int_{J_{u_{j,\rho}}\cap\left(A_{\varepsilon}\times(-1/2,1/2)\right)}\psi_{\rho}\big([u_{j,\rho}],\nu_{u_{j,\rho}}\big)\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}+C\,\varepsilon.

Reasoning as in Step 2 of Theorem
4.10
, using Corollary
5.3
in place of Lemma
4.8
, together with (
5.10
) and (
5.8
), we obtain

\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\int_{J_{u_{j,\rho}}}\psi_{\rho}([u_{j,\rho}],\nu_{u_{j,\rho}})\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}\leq\int_{J_{u_{j}}}\psi_{0}([u_{j}],\nu_{u_{j}})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}+C\varepsilon.

(5.11)

Since
u_{j}^{\pm}\to u^{\pm}
uniformly on
\Sigma
by Proposition
3.6
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}(J_{u_{j}}\,\triangle\,J_{u})\to 0
, the upper semicontinuity of
\psi_{0}
and the reverse Fatou lemma give

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{J_{u_{j}}}\psi_{0}([u_{j}],\nu_{u_{j}})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq\int_{J_{u}}\psi_{0}([u],\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

(5.12)

Step 3: Estimate of the bulk energy
. Notice that the derivative of
{u}_{j,\rho}
is given by

\nabla{u}_{j,\rho}(x)=\nabla w_{j,\rho}(g_{j,\rho}(x))\nabla g_{j,\rho}(x).

Since
\det(\nabla v_{j})=1
on
\Sigma_{\rho_{j}}
,
\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{j}
for a constant
C_{j}>0
depending on
j
, and
g_{j,\rho}\to\mathrm{Id}
in
W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma_{2};\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
, reasoning as in Step 3 of Theorem
4.10
we obtain

\det\!\left(\nabla_{\alpha}u_{j,\rho}\,\bigg|\,\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{3}u_{j,\rho}\right)=1,\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\left(\nabla_{\alpha}u_{j,\rho}\,\bigg|\,\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{3}u_{j,\rho}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{1};\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 3})}\leq C+\rho C_{j}\qquad\text{a.e.\ in }\Sigma_{1}.

(5.13)

We split the bulk integral into contributions from
E\times(-1/2,1/2)
and
\Sigma_{1}\setminus(E\times(-1/2,1/2))
.
Since
\mathcal{L}^{3}\big(E\times(-1/2,1/2)\leq\varepsilon
, using (
5.13
) and
(\widetilde{A}_{2})
, we infer

\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\int_{E\times(-1/2,1/2))}W\bigg(\nabla_{\alpha}u_{j,\rho}\bigg|\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{3}u_{j,\rho}\bigg)\,dx\leq C\varepsilon.

On
\Sigma_{1}\setminus(E\times(-1/2,1/2))
, since
g_{j,\rho}\equiv\mathrm{Id}
there, using (
5.8
), the continuity of
W
, and the dominated convergence theorem (justified by (
5.13
) and
(\widetilde{A}_{2})
),

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\Sigma_{1}\setminus\big(E\times(-1/2,1/2)\big)}W\!\left(\nabla_{\alpha}w_{j,\rho}\,\bigg|\,\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{3}w_{j,\rho}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\Sigma_{1}}W\!\left(\nabla_{\alpha}w_{j,\rho}(x)\,\bigg|\,\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{3}w_{j,\rho}(x)\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{1}}W\!\left(\nabla u_{j}(x)\,\big|\,b_{j}(x)\right)\,dx.

Finally, by Vitali’s convergence theorem, using that
b_{j}\to b
in
L^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{R}^{3})
,
\nabla u_{j}\to\nabla u
in
L^{p}(\Sigma;\mathrm{R}{M}^{3\times 2})
,
\det(\nabla u_{j}\mid b_{j})=1
, the
p
-growth of
W
on
\mathrm{SL}(3)
, and (
5.5
),

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{1}}W\!\left(\nabla u_{j}(x)\,\big|\,b_{j}(x)\right)\,dx=\int_{\Sigma_{1}}W\!\left(\nabla u(x)\mid b(x)\right)\,dx=\int_{\Sigma_{1}}W_{0}\!\left(\nabla u(x)\right)\,dx.

Taking also into account (
5.11
) and (
5.12
), this proves that

\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u)\leq\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}(u)+C\varepsilon\qquad\text{for every }u\in Y.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(u)\leq\mathcal{G}^{w}_{0}(u)\qquad\text{for every }u\in Y.

For the remaining functions
u\in GSBV^{p}(\Sigma_{1},\mathrm{R}R^{3})\setminus Y
, the inequality above is trivially satisfied.
By semicontinuity of the
\Gamma\text{-}\limsup
, the assertion follows.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the main result in the incompressible case.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\Sigma
be a bounded open subset of
\mathrm{R}R^{2}
with Lipschitz boundary and let
1<p<\infty
.
Let
W
satisfy assumptions
(\tilde{A}_{1})-(\tilde{A}_{2})
, and let
\psi
satisfy hypotheses
(B_{1})-(B_{5})
.
Then,

\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{\rho}=\mathcal{G}_{0}

in the space of measurable functions with respect to convergence in measure.

Proof.

We observe that
W_{0}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
. The proof then follows by combining Proposition
5.1
and Theorem
5.5
with the arguments of Proposition
4.13
and Theorem
4.14
.
∎
