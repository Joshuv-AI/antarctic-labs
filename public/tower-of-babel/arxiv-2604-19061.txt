Three-Module SC-VAMP for LDPC-Coded Nonlinear Channels
Tadashi Wadayama 1 and Takumi Takahashi 2

Abstract
We propose a three-module extension of score-based VAMP (SC-VAMP) for signal recovery in nonlinear channels, where the received signal is obtained by applying a nonlinearity to a linear mixture of the transmitted signal, followed by additive Gaussian noise. The key idea is to introduce a latent variable representing the output of the linear mixing stage, which decomposes the inference problem into three modules: a likelihood module that handles the nonlinear observation via Gauss–Hermite quadrature, a coupling module that enforces the linear constraint between the transmitted signal and the latent variable via LMMSE estimation, and a denoiser module that incorporates the code constraint using belief propagation (BP) decoding. Each module exchanges extrinsic scalar-Gaussian messages with Onsager corrections derived from posterior variances that are computed in closed form or to quadrature accuracy. Numerical experiments with BPSK-modulated LDPC codewords transmitted through a hyperbolic tangent channel demonstrate that the proposed method achieves a clear waterfall in bit error rate (BER), with the gap to the capacity estimate narrowing as the block length increases from 128 to 2304. The framework provides a modular receiver architecture applicable to a broad class of nonlinear channels. Since only the likelihood module depends on the channel nonlinearity, the architecture readily adapts to other channel models by replacing a single module while leaving the coupling and decoder modules unchanged.



I
Introduction

High-dimensional signal recovery from noisy observations is a fundamental problem in modern communication systems.
In coded systems, the transmitter sends a codeword
\bm{x}
drawn from a channel code, and the receiver observes
\bm{y}=\bm{H}\bm{x}+\bm{z}
through a random mixing matrix
\bm{H}
and additive noise
\bm{z}
.
Approximate message passing (AMP)
[
1
]
and its rotationally invariant extensions, vector AMP (VAMP)
[
2
]
and orthogonal AMP (OAMP)
[
3
]
, provide powerful iterative frameworks for such linear inverse problems.
Under appropriate random matrix conditions, these algorithms achieve Bayes-optimal performance and admit rigorous state evolution analyses
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

In practice, however, nonlinearities are often present in the signal path of wireless and optical fiber communication systems.
Power amplifier saturation, analog-to-digital converter (ADC) clipping, and fiber-optic Kerr effects all introduce nonlinear distortions.
The resulting observation model becomes
\bm{y}=f(\bm{H}\bm{x})+\bm{z}
, where
f(\cdot)
represents a nonlinear mapping.
Standard VAMP, designed for linear observations, suffers severe model mismatch when applied directly to such channels.
Generalized AMP (GAMP)
[
7
]
and its VAMP extension (GLM-VAMP)
[
8
]
decompose the GLM into a nonlinear output channel and a linear mixing stage.
While the channel model
\bm{y}=f(\bm{H}\bm{x})+\bm{z}
falls within this GLM class, the algorithm proposed in this paper is not a direct instantiation of GLM-VAMP.
Rather, it reformulates the inference as a compositional SC-VAMP architecture with a uniform score/Fisher-based mean–variance interface that accommodates a BP-based coded prior module within the same extrinsic message-passing protocol.
Specifically, every module represents its posterior mean via Tweedie’s formula, derives its Onsager correction from Fisher information or variance ratios, and exchanges scalar-Gaussian extrinsic messages—differing from the Jacobian/divergence-based updates of conventional GLM-VAMP.

In
[
11
]
, we introduced score-based VAMP (SC-VAMP), whose key architectural feature is a uniform mean–variance extrinsic interface shared by all modules.
Every module—whether it handles a linear operation, a nonlinear observation, or a code constraint—receives a Gaussian pseudo-observation parameterized by a mean–variance pair and produces an extrinsic output through a universal Onsager correction formula.
This makes the framework inherently compositional: heterogeneous modules can be freely connected without redesigning existing ones, and extension from two modules to an arbitrary number is straightforward.
Technically, SC-VAMP computes the posterior mean via Tweedie’s formula
[
12
]
and derives the Onsager correction from score functions and Fisher information, enabling a Jacobian-free implementation that also supports data-driven score learning.

The compositionality of SC-VAMP becomes essential when the forward model has multiple distinct components.
The 2-module SC-VAMP in
[
11
]
treats the observation model as
\bm{y}=f(\bm{x})+\bm{z}
directly, requiring a single module to handle the entire observation process.
When the forward model involves both a linear mixing
\bm{H}
and a nonlinearity
f(\cdot)
, as in
\bm{y}=f(\bm{H}\bm{x})+\bm{z}
, a single observation module cannot cleanly separate these two operations.
A natural solution is to introduce a latent variable
W=\bm{H}X
and consider the Markov chain
X\to W\to Y
, which factors the joint density into three components: the prior
p_{X}
, the coupling
p_{W|X}
, and the likelihood
p_{Y|W}
.
Exploiting the compositional architecture of SC-VAMP, we assign one module to each factor: Module A estimates
W
from
Y
based on
p_{Y|W}
, Module B estimates
X
based on
p_{X}
, and Module C couples
X
and
W
based on
p_{W|X}
.
All three modules follow the SC-VAMP extrinsic message-passing protocol: the likelihood and coupling modules compute posterior moments exactly (or to quadrature accuracy) and project them onto scalar-Gaussian messages, while the BP-based denoiser module uses approximate posterior moments.
More broadly, the proposed architecture is not tied to a particular nonlinearity; by replacing Module A with an appropriate score estimator, the same framework extends naturally to channels of the form
\bm{y}=f(\bm{H}\bm{x})+\bm{z}
.

The proposed framework can also be compared with
turbo equalization

[
13
,
14
]
and its extensions to MIMO detection
[
15
]
, which iterate between a soft equalizer and a soft channel decoder, exchanging extrinsic LLRs.
These methods, however, are typically designed for specific linear channel structures, and the heuristic LLR subtraction used to compute extrinsic information lacks a principled justification, whereas SC-VAMP derives extrinsic messages from the Onsager correction grounded in score functions and Fisher information.

Our contributions are as follows:

1.

We extend the score/Fisher parameterization of SC-VAMP
[
11
]
to a three-module architecture, connecting a nonlinear likelihood module (Gauss–Hermite quadrature
[
16
]
), a linear coupling module (LMMSE), and a coded prior module (BP decoding
[
17
,
18
]
) through a uniform mean–variance extrinsic interface.

2.

We incorporate an LDPC BP decoder as a symbol-domain SISO denoiser within the same Onsager-corrected extrinsic protocol, providing a principled alternative to the heuristic LLR subtraction used in classical turbo receivers.

3.

We demonstrate on the
f=\tanh
channel that the method achieves a clear waterfall BER curve sharpening from
N=128
to
2304
, with a
\sim
3 dB gain from the Onsager correction.

II
Preliminaries

II-A

Notation

Vectors are denoted by bold lowercase letters (e.g.,
\bm{x}
) and matrices by bold uppercase letters (e.g.,
\bm{H}
).
Random variables (and random vectors) are denoted by uppercase letters (
X
,
Y
), and their realizations by lowercase letters (
x
,
y
, or bold
\bm{x}
,
\bm{y}
for vectors).
We write
\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\Sigma})
for a Gaussian distribution with mean
\bm{\mu}
and covariance
\bm{\Sigma}
, and
\mathcal{N}(x;\mu,\sigma^{2})
for the corresponding density evaluated at
x
.
For a continuous random vector
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with density
p_{U}
, the
score function
is
s_{U}(\bm{u})\triangleq\nabla_{\bm{u}}\log p_{U}(\bm{u})
and the
scalar Fisher information
is
J_{U}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|s_{U}(U)\|^{2}\bigr]
.
The conditional counterparts are
s_{U|Y}(\bm{u}|\bm{y})\triangleq\nabla_{\bm{u}}\log p_{U|Y}(\bm{u}|\bm{y})
and
J_{U|Y}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|s_{U|Y}(U|Y)\|^{2}\bigr]
.
Throughout, score functions are applied to pseudo-observations
R=X+\sqrt{v}\,N
, which have smooth densities via Gaussian convolution even when
X
is discrete.

II-B

System Model

Consider a coded system in which the transmitter encodes
K
information bits into an LDPC codeword, maps it to BPSK, producing a random vector
X\in\{+1,-1\}^{N}
.
The received signal is

Y=f(\bm{H}X)+Z,

(1)

where
\bm{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
is the channel matrix with i.i.d. entries
H_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/M)
,
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
is a nonlinear mapping, and
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\sigma^{2}\bm{I}_{M})
is additive white Gaussian noise.
The signal-to-noise ratio is defined as
\mathrm{SNR}=1/\sigma^{2}
.

We introduce the latent random vector
W\triangleq\bm{H}X\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, so that the observation becomes
Y=f(W)+Z
.
This yields the Markov chain
X\to W\to Y
and the joint density

p(\bm{x},\bm{w},\bm{y})=p_{X}(\bm{x})\cdot\delta(\bm{w}-\bm{H}\bm{x})\cdot p_{Y|W}(\bm{y}|\bm{w}),

(2)

where
\bm{x},\bm{w},\bm{y}
denote realizations of
X,W,Y
, respectively,
p_{Y|W}(\bm{y}|\bm{w})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{y};f(\bm{w}),\sigma^{2}\bm{I}_{M})
, and
\delta(\cdot)
denotes the Dirac delta function.
The three factors on the right-hand side correspond to the code constraint, the linear coupling, and the nonlinear likelihood, respectively.
This factorization is the basis of the 3-module decomposition proposed in Section
III
.

II-C

Review of 2-Module SC-VAMP

The SC-VAMP framework proposed in
[
11
]
operates by iterating two SISO (soft-input soft-output) modules that exchange extrinsic mean–variance messages.
Each module acts as an estimator: given an input pair
(\bm{r}_{in},v_{in})
representing a Gaussian pseudo-observation
\bm{r}_{in}=\bm{x}+\bm{z}
,
\bm{z}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},v_{in}\bm{I})
, it computes a posterior mean
\bm{x}_{post}
and produces an extrinsic output
(\bm{x}_{out},v_{out})
.

The posterior mean is computed via Tweedie’s formula
[
12
]
:

\bm{x}_{post}=\bm{r}_{in}+v_{in}\,s_{R_{in}|Y}(\bm{r}_{in}|\bm{y}),

(3)

where
s_{R_{in}|Y}
is the conditional score function of the pseudo-observation
R_{in}
given
Y
.
The posterior variance is
v_{post}=v_{in}\,\alpha(v_{in})
, where
\alpha(v_{in})
is the Onsager coefficient.
The extrinsic output is obtained by “subtracting” the input information:

\bm{x}_{out}=\frac{\bm{x}_{post}-\alpha\,\bm{r}_{in}}{1-\alpha},\qquad v_{out}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\,v_{in},

(4)

with

\alpha=\frac{v_{post}}{v_{in}}=1-\frac{v_{in}}{N}\,J_{R_{in}|Y},

(5)

where
J_{R_{in}|Y}=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|s_{R_{in}|Y}(R_{in}|Y)\|^{2}\bigr]
is the scalar (trace) Fisher information.
The Onsager coefficient
\alpha
prevents “double-counting” of information in loopy message passing and is expressed directly through
J_{R_{in}|Y}
, enabling a Jacobian-free implementation.

III
Proposed Method: Multi-Module SC-VAMP

III-A

Generic Framework

The multi-module SC-VAMP framework naturally extends to general Markov chains
X_{1}\to X_{2}\to\cdots\to X_{L}
by introducing a coupling module for each edge
(X_{k-1},X_{k})
, with the two end modules corresponding to the prior and likelihood factors.
For simplicity of presentation, we focus on the 3-module case with a single latent variable, i.e., the chain
X\to W\to Y
, where
X
is the signal to be estimated and
Y
is the observed signal.

The factorization (
2
) in Section
II-B
naturally suggests three SISO modules, each responsible for one factor:

•

Module A
(Likelihood): estimates
W
from
p_{Y|W}
and the observation
\bm{y}
.

•

Module B
(Prior/Denoiser): estimates
X
from
p_{X}
.

•

Module C
(Coupling): estimates
(X,W)
jointly from
p_{W|X}
.

Module C acts as a central hub, exchanging extrinsic messages with Module A on the
W
-side and with Module B on the
X
-side (Fig.
1
).
Each module refines its estimate by combining the pseudo-observation with its own factor of the joint density, and the Onsager correction ensures that the extrinsic output does not “double-count” the input information.

The proposed 3-module SC-VAMP rests on the following two assumptions.

1.

Assumption 1 (Markov chain).

The random variables
X
,
W
,
Y
form a Markov chain
X\to W\to Y
, i.e., the joint density factorizes as

p_{XWY}(\bm{x},\bm{w},\bm{y})=p_{X}(\bm{x})\,p_{W|X}(\bm{w}|\bm{x})\,p_{Y|W}(\bm{y}|\bm{w}),

(6)

where
p_{W|X}
is a general stochastic kernel (deterministic maps such as
W=f_{1}(X)
are included as special cases via a Dirac delta).

2.

Assumption 2 (Gaussian pseudo-observation approximation).

All inter-module messages can be approximated as Gaussian pseudo-observations parameterized by a mean–variance pair
(\bm{r},v)
.
Specifically, Module B sends
(\bm{r}_{x},v_{x})
to Module C and Module A sends
(\bm{r}_{w},v_{w})
to Module C, each interpreted as

R_{X}=X+\sqrt{v_{x}}\,N_{X},\qquad R_{W}=W+\sqrt{v_{w}}\,N_{W},

(7)

where
N_{X}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}_{N})
and
N_{W}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}_{M})
are independent of each other and of
(X,W)
.
This is the same Gaussian message assumption employed in 2-module SC-VAMP
[
11
]
.

(Coupling)
p_{W|X}
Module B
(Prior)
p_{X}
Module A
(Likelihood)
p_{Y|W}
(\bm{r}_{x}^{C,out},v_{x}^{C,out})
(\bm{r}_{x}^{B,out},v_{x}^{B,out})
(\bm{r}_{w}^{C,out},v_{w}^{C,out})

III-B

Module Definitions (Score-Based)

Each module computes a posterior mean via Tweedie’s formula and produces an extrinsic output with Onsager correction, following the SC-VAMP protocol (
3
)–(
4
).
The uniform interface described in Section
I
allows heterogeneous modules to be composed without modification.

III-B
1
Module C (Coupling)

Given inputs
(\bm{r}_{x},v_{x})
from Module B and
(\bm{r}_{w},v_{w})
from Module A, Module C incorporates solely its own factor
p_{W|X}
by forming the
tilted distribution

\displaystyle\tilde{p}^{C}(\bm{x},\bm{w})\triangleq\frac{1}{Z_{C}}\,

\displaystyle p_{W|X}(\bm{w}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle\cdot\mathcal{N}(\bm{x};\bm{r}_{x},v_{x}\bm{I}_{N})\,\mathcal{N}(\bm{w};\bm{r}_{w},v_{w}\bm{I}_{M}),

(8)

where
Z_{C}(\bm{r}_{x},\bm{r}_{w})
is the normalization constant obtained by integrating out
(\bm{x},\bm{w})
.
The two Gaussian factors are the incoming messages from Modules B and A (Assumption 2), not the marginal priors
p_{X}
or
p_{W}
.
The posterior means equal

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}_{post}^{C}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{p}^{C}}[\bm{x}]=\bm{r}_{x}+v_{x}\,\nabla_{\bm{r}_{x}}\log Z_{C},

(9)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{w}}_{post}^{C}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{p}^{C}}[\bm{w}]=\bm{r}_{w}+v_{w}\,\nabla_{\bm{r}_{w}}\log Z_{C},

(10)

where
\nabla_{\bm{r}_{x}}\log Z_{C}
and
\nabla_{\bm{r}_{w}}\log Z_{C}
are the score functions of the tilted distribution (
III-B1
) with respect to
\bm{r}_{x}
and
\bm{r}_{w}
, respectively (Tweedie’s formula applied to each side).
The posterior variances and Onsager coefficients are

v_{post,x}^{C}=\frac{1}{N}\Tr\!\bigl(\mathrm{Cov}_{\tilde{p}^{C}}(\bm{x})\bigr),\quad\alpha_{x}^{C}=\frac{v_{post,x}^{C}}{v_{x}},

(11)

and similarly
v_{post,w}^{C}=(1/M)\,\Tr\!\bigl(\mathrm{Cov}_{\tilde{p}^{C}}(\bm{w})\bigr)
,
\alpha_{w}^{C}=v_{post,w}^{C}/v_{w}
.
The variance-ratio form
\alpha=v_{post}/v_{in}
is equivalent to the Fisher-information form (
5
) via the identity
v_{post}/v_{in}=1-(v_{in}/N)\,J
; here we use the variance-ratio form because the posterior covariance of Module C is available in closed form (see Section
III-C1
).
The extrinsic outputs on each side are

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{x}^{C,out}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{\bm{x}}_{post}^{C}-\alpha_{x}^{C}\,\bm{r}_{x}}{1-\alpha_{x}^{C}},

\displaystyle v_{x}^{C,out}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{x}^{C}}{1-\alpha_{x}^{C}}\,v_{x},

(12)

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{w}^{C,out}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{\bm{w}}_{post}^{C}-\alpha_{w}^{C}\,\bm{r}_{w}}{1-\alpha_{w}^{C}},

\displaystyle v_{w}^{C,out}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{w}^{C}}{1-\alpha_{w}^{C}}\,v_{w}.

(13)

This procedure—tilted distribution, moment matching, extrinsic output—is equivalent to the EP projection step
[
9
,
10
]
, with the incoming messages as the cavity distribution and the extrinsic output as the updated site approximation.

2
Module A (Likelihood)

Given input
(\bm{r}_{w},v_{w})
from Module C and observation
\bm{y}
, Module A forms the tilted distribution
\tilde{p}^{A}(\bm{w})\propto p_{Y|W}(\bm{y}|\bm{w})\,\mathcal{N}(\bm{w};\bm{r}_{w},v_{w}\bm{I}_{M})
with normalization constant
Z_{A}(\bm{r}_{w},\bm{y})\triangleq\int p_{Y|W}(\bm{y}|\bm{w})\,\mathcal{N}(\bm{w};\bm{r}_{w},v_{w}\bm{I}_{M})\,d\bm{w}
.
The posterior mean is computed via Tweedie’s formula:

\hat{\bm{w}}_{post}^{A}=\bm{r}_{w}+v_{w}\,\bm{s}_{R_{W}|Y}^{(A)}(\bm{r}_{w}\mid\bm{y}),

(14)

where
\bm{s}_{R_{W}|Y}^{(A)}(\bm{r}_{w}\mid\bm{y})\triangleq\nabla_{\bm{r}_{w}}\log Z_{A}(\bm{r}_{w},\bm{y})
is the score of the tilted distribution.
The Onsager coefficient is
\alpha^{A}=v_{post}^{A}/v_{w}
with
v_{post}^{A}=(1/M)\,\Tr\bigl(\mathrm{Cov}_{\tilde{p}^{A}}(\bm{w})\bigr)
, and the extrinsic output follows (
4
).

III-B
3
Module B (Prior/Denoiser)

Given input
(\bm{r}_{x},v_{x})
from Module C, Module B computes

\hat{\bm{x}}_{post}^{B}=\bm{r}_{x}+v_{x}\,\bm{s}_{R_{X}}^{(B)}(\bm{r}_{x}),

(15)

where
\bm{s}_{R_{X}}^{(B)}(\bm{r}_{x})\triangleq\nabla_{\bm{r}_{x}}\log p_{R_{X}}(\bm{r}_{x})
is the unconditional score with
p_{R_{X}}(\bm{r}_{x})=\int p_{X}(\bm{x})\,\mathcal{N}(\bm{r}_{x};\bm{x},v_{x}\bm{I}_{N})\,d\bm{x}
.
Module B has no dependence on
\bm{y}
and acts purely as a denoiser based on the prior
p_{X}
.
In practice (e.g., BP decoding), the Onsager coefficient is approximated by
\alpha^{B}=v_{post}^{B}/v_{x}
(see Section
III-C3
).
The extrinsic output follows (
4
).

III-B
4
Iteration Schedule

One iteration consists of: (i) Module C produces extrinsic outputs to both sides; (ii) Module B updates
(\bm{r}_{x},v_{x})
; (iii) Module A updates
(\bm{r}_{w},v_{w})
; and these feed back into Module C.
Steps (ii) and (iii) are independent and may be executed in parallel.

III-C

Instantiation for Nonlinear Coded Channels

We now instantiate the generic framework for the nonlinear channel model
\bm{y}=f(\bm{H}\bm{x})+\bm{z}
introduced in Section
II-B
.
By setting the latent variable
W=\bm{H}X
, the Markov chain
X\to W\to Y
yields the three factors in (
2
): the prior
p_{X}
(LDPC code constraint), the coupling
p_{W|X}(\bm{w}|\bm{x})=\delta(\bm{w}-\bm{H}\bm{x})
(deterministic linear map), and the likelihood
p_{Y|W}(\bm{y}|\bm{w})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{y};f(\bm{w}),\sigma^{2}\bm{I}_{M})
(nonlinear observation with AWGN).
For each module, we derive concrete computations that replace the abstract score functions in (
9
)–(
15
).
The iteration follows the schedule in Section
III-B
, initialized with
(\bm{r}_{x},v_{x})=(\bm{0},1)
and
(\bm{r}_{w},v_{w})=(\bm{y},\sigma^{2})
.
The
X
-side initialization corresponds to a non-informative prior (zero mean, unit variance for BPSK).
The
W
-side initialization
(\bm{r}_{w},v_{w})=(\bm{y},\sigma^{2})
is exact when
f
is the identity, and is a heuristic for general
f
that only affects the first iteration, as Module A replaces it with a properly calibrated message from the second iteration onward.
After
T
iterations, the hard decision is
\hat{x}_{i}=\mathrm{sgn}(\hat{x}_{post,i}^{B})
.

III-C
1
Module C: SISO-LMMSE

Since
W=\bm{H}X
is a deterministic linear map, the coupling
p_{W|X}(\bm{w}|\bm{x})=\delta(\bm{w}-\bm{H}\bm{x})
eliminates
\bm{w}
as an independent variable.
Substituting into the pseudo-observation model (
7
), we have
R_{W}=\bm{H}X+\sqrt{v_{w}}\,N_{W}
in addition to
R_{X}=X+\sqrt{v_{x}}\,N_{X}
.
Since both observations are linear in
X
with Gaussian noise, the posterior of
X
given
(R_{X},R_{W})
is Gaussian.
The posterior precision is the sum of the individual precisions,
(1/v_{x})\bm{I}_{N}
from
R_{X}
and
(1/v_{w})\bm{H}^{\top}\bm{H}
from
R_{W}
, yielding

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}_{post}^{C}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{x}^{C}\left(\frac{\bm{r}_{x}}{v_{x}}+\frac{\bm{H}^{\top}\bm{r}_{w}}{v_{w}}\right),

(16)

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}_{x}^{C}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{v_{x}}\bm{I}_{N}+\frac{1}{v_{w}}\bm{H}^{\top}\bm{H}\right)^{-1},

(17)

and
\hat{\bm{w}}_{post}^{C}=\bm{H}\,\hat{\bm{x}}_{post}^{C}
.
The scalar posterior variances are

v_{post,x}^{C}=\frac{1}{N}\Tr\bigl(\bm{\Sigma}_{x}^{C}\bigr),\quad v_{post,w}^{C}=\frac{1}{M}\Tr\bigl(\bm{H}\bm{\Sigma}_{x}^{C}\bm{H}^{\top}\bigr).

(18)

Using the eigendecomposition
\bm{H}^{\top}\bm{H}=\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{U}^{\top}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}
, the traces in (
18
) and the Onsager coefficients
\alpha_{x}^{C}=v_{post,x}^{C}/v_{x}
,
\alpha_{w}^{C}=v_{post,w}^{C}/v_{w}
reduce to sums over eigenvalues, e.g.,
\alpha_{x}^{C}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{v_{w}}{v_{w}+v_{x}\lambda_{n}}
.
The extrinsic outputs on the
X
- and
W
-sides then follow the generic formulas (
12
)–(
13
).

III-C
2
Module A: Nonlinear Likelihood

Module A receives the extrinsic output
(\bm{r}_{w}^{C,out},v_{w}^{C,out})
from Module C on the
W
-side as its input pseudo-observation, together with the channel output
\bm{y}
.
Writing
(\bm{r}_{w}^{in},v_{w}^{in})=(\bm{r}_{w}^{C,out},v_{w}^{C,out})
for brevity, Module A estimates
W
by incorporating the likelihood
p_{Y|W}(\bm{y}|\bm{w})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{y};f(\bm{w}),\sigma^{2}\bm{I}_{M})
.
The posterior mean is computed via Tweedie’s formula:

\hat{\bm{w}}_{post}^{A}=\bm{r}_{w}^{in}+v_{w}^{in}\,\bm{s}_{R_{W}|Y}^{(A)}(\bm{r}_{w}^{in}\mid\bm{y}),

(19)

where
\bm{s}_{R_{W}|Y}^{(A)}(\bm{r}_{w}^{in}\mid\bm{y})=\nabla_{\bm{r}_{w}^{in}}\log Z_{A}(\bm{r}_{w}^{in},\bm{y})
is the score of the tilted distribution of Module A.
The posterior variance is
v_{post}^{A}=(1/M)\,\Tr\bigl(\mathrm{Cov}_{\tilde{p}^{A}}(\bm{w})\bigr)
and the Onsager coefficient is
\alpha^{A}=v_{post}^{A}/v_{w}^{in}
.
The extrinsic output follows (
4
) and serves as the updated
(\bm{r}_{w},v_{w})
for Module C in the next iteration.

The score function can be evaluated in various ways depending on the structure of
f
.
When
f
is applied component-wise, i.e.,
[f(\bm{w})]_{m}=f(w_{m})
for a scalar function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, the score decomposes as
[\bm{s}_{R_{W}|Y}^{(A)}(\bm{r}_{w}^{in}\mid\bm{y})]_{m}=s_{m}^{(A)}(r_{w,m}^{in}\mid y_{m})
, where each scalar score is given by

s_{m}^{(A)}(r_{w,m}^{in}\mid y_{m})=\frac{\mathbb{E}[W_{m}\mid r_{w,m}^{in},y_{m}]-r_{w,m}^{in}}{v_{w}^{in}}

(20)

with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W_{m}\mid r_{w,m}^{in},y_{m}]

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\int w\,\mathcal{N}(w;r_{w,m}^{in},v_{w}^{in})\,\mathcal{N}(y_{m};f(w),\sigma^{2})\,dw}{\int\mathcal{N}(w;r_{w,m}^{in},v_{w}^{in})\,\mathcal{N}(y_{m};f(w),\sigma^{2})\,dw}.

(21)

The posterior variance is computed from the same integrals:

v_{post}^{A}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[W_{m}^{2}\mid r_{w,m}^{in},y_{m}]-(\mathbb{E}[W_{m}\mid r_{w,m}^{in},y_{m}])^{2}\bigr),

(22)

where
\mathbb{E}[W_{m}^{2}\mid r_{w,m}^{in},y_{m}]
is obtained by replacing
w
with
w^{2}
in the numerator of (
III-C2
).
Both moments can be efficiently evaluated by Gauss–Hermite quadrature
[
16
]
with a single set of quadrature points.
When
f
is not component-wise—or when
f
is unknown—the score can be learned from data (e.g., via flow matching or denoising score matching), making Module A applicable to general nonlinear channels without requiring an analytical expression for
f
.
When
f
is the identity, Module A reduces to a simple linear Gaussian update that returns
(\bm{r}_{w}^{A,out},v_{w}^{A,out})=(\bm{y},\sigma^{2})
at every iteration; substituting this into Module C reduces to the 2-module SC-VAMP formulation of
[
11
]
for the linear channel
\bm{y}=\bm{H}\bm{x}+\bm{z}
.

To verify this claim, note that when
f=\mathrm{id}
, Module A yields
\alpha^{A}=\sigma^{2}/(v_{w}^{in}+\sigma^{2})
and the extrinsic output
(\bm{r}_{w}^{A,out},v_{w}^{A,out})=(\bm{y},\sigma^{2})
, which is constant across iterations.
Substituting into Module C’s LMMSE equations (
16
)–(
17
) and applying the matrix inversion lemma recovers the observation-module update of the 2-module SC-VAMP in
[
11
]
, confirming that the 3-module iteration reduces exactly to the 2-module case.

III-C
3
Module B: BP Decoder as Denoiser

Module B receives the extrinsic output
(\bm{r}_{x}^{C,out},v_{x}^{C,out})
from Module C on the
X
-side as its input pseudo-observation.
Writing
(\bm{r}_{x}^{in},v_{x}^{in})=(\bm{r}_{x}^{C,out},v_{x}^{C,out})
, Module B incorporates the LDPC code constraint
p_{X}
and acts as a SISO denoiser.
The Gaussian pseudo-observation
r_{x,i}^{in}=x_{i}+\sqrt{v_{x}^{in}}\,n_{i}
with BPSK
x_{i}\in\{+1,-1\}
is converted into input LLRs
L_{i}^{in}=2r_{x,i}^{in}/v_{x}^{in}
, which represent the channel reliability of each bit.
These LLRs are fed into a BP decoder
[
17
,
18
]
(implemented using the Kaira library
[
20
]
), which iteratively exchanges messages along the edges of the LDPC Tanner graph and produces a-posteriori LLRs
L_{i}^{app}
.
The posterior mean for each bit is
\hat{x}_{post,i}^{B}=\tanh(L_{i}^{app}/2)=\mathbb{E}[X_{i}|L_{i}^{app}]
for BPSK signaling, and the posterior variance is

v_{post}^{B}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(1-(\hat{x}_{post,i}^{B})^{2}\bigr).

(23)

The Onsager coefficient is set to
\alpha^{B}=v_{post}^{B}/v_{x}^{in}
, and the extrinsic output returned to Module C is

\bm{r}_{x}^{B,out}=\frac{\hat{\bm{x}}_{post}^{B}-\alpha^{B}\,\bm{r}_{x}^{in}}{1-\alpha^{B}},\qquad v_{x}^{B,out}=\frac{\alpha^{B}}{1-\alpha^{B}}\,v_{x}^{in},

(24)

serving as the updated pseudo-observation
(\bm{r}_{x},v_{x})\leftarrow(\bm{r}_{x}^{B,out},v_{x}^{B,out})
for Module C.

Since BP is an approximate inference algorithm on loopy factor graphs,
\hat{\bm{x}}_{post}^{B}
does not equal the true MMSE estimate
\mathbb{E}[\bm{x}\mid\bm{r}_{x}^{in}]
under
p_{X}
.
The variance-ratio form
\alpha^{B}=v_{post}^{B}/v_{x}^{in}
coincides with the theoretically correct Onsager correction (the average divergence of the denoiser) only for the exact MMSE denoiser; for BP, it serves as a variance-ratio surrogate.
This approximation is standard in turbo-type receivers
[
14
,
11
]
and works well in practice.
In implementation, all Onsager coefficients are clipped to
[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]
(e.g.,
\epsilon=10^{-6}
) to ensure that the extrinsic variances remain positive and finite.
The extrinsic exchange between Module B and Module C structurally corresponds to the decoder–detector interaction in classical iterative detection and decoding (IDD)
[
14
,
15
]
; the SC-VAMP framework subsumes this interaction by replacing heuristic LLR subtraction with the Onsager-corrected mean–variance interface.

V
Conclusion

We proposed a 3-module SC-VAMP framework that separates a nonlinear channel into likelihood, coupling, and code-constraint modules, each equipped with score/Fisher-based Onsager-corrected extrinsic messaging.
The key advantage is modularity: Module A with Gauss–Hermite quadrature handles any element-wise nonlinearity without modifying the other modules.
Experiments confirm that the Onsager correction is indispensable, yielding a
\sim
3 dB gain over variants without proper extrinsic exchange, and that the waterfall steepens consistently from
N=128
to
2304
.
Future directions include state evolution analysis, application to other nonlinearities (e.g., hard/soft clipping, 1-bit quantization), and data-driven score learning
[
11
]
for unknown
f
.
