Fasting insulin to HDL-C ratio is correlated with MASLD severity in Chinese overweight/obesity: a cross-sectional analysis
Authors: Xiaojuan Xu, Xincheng Wang, Lulu Yu, Xiaotian Yang, Peiyao Wang, Yimeng Wang, Zhenyang Guan, Chunjun Li, Tao Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe fasting insulin to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (FINS/HDL-C) is a newly identified insulin resistance (IR) marker. And the correlation between FINS/HDL-C and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) has not yet been explored, our study investigated the association between FINS/HDL-C and MASLD severity.MethodsThis cross-sectional study included 330 participants with overweight/obesity and MASLD. Demographic information and biochemical indicators were collected, and IR markers including FINS/HDL-C, homeostatic model assessment for insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), triglyceride to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (TG/HDL-C), and triglyceride glucose index (TyG), were calculated. MASLD severity was assessed using the controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) and liver stiffness measurement (LSM) determined by FibroScan. The relationship between FINS/HDL-C and MASLD severity was evaluated and compared with that of other IR markers.ResultsParticipants with higher CAP and LSM exhibited significantly higher FINS/HDL-C (P < 0.05). Even in the fully adjusted linear regression model, FINS/HDL-C remains positively correlated with CAP (β = 0.33, 95% CI 0.09 ~ 0.58, P = 0.009) and LSM (β = 0.05, 95% CI 0.02 ~ 0.08, P < 0.001). And FINS/HDL‑C was the only biomarker that showed elevated CAP at Quartile 2. Exploratory analysis suggested that BMI might be involved in the association between FINS/HDL-C and CAP and LSM. FINS/HDL-C had the highest area under the receiver operating characteristic curve for severe hepatic steatosis, at 0.699 (95% CI: 0.639 ~ 0.758).ConclusionsOur findings suggest that FINS/HDL-C is associated with MASLD severity in overweight/obese individuals. It might serve as a valuable biomarker for MASLD risk stratification.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12902-026-02424-6.

Background

The global prevalence of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) has been increasing annually [1], the prevalence of MASLD in China is also at a high level, posing an important challenge to the public health field [2]. Overweight/obesity is an important risk factor for MASLD [3], they together contribute to the development of diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease, hepatic fibrosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma [4–6]. Therefore, early risk stratification and intervention of MASLD is important to delay its progression in overweight/obese individuals. Insulin resistance (IR) is the central pathological mechanism of MASLD progression and is closely related to the risk stratification of MASLD [7]. IR drives the progression of MASLD-associated fibrosis through pro-inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways, as well as by activating fibroblast-like cells [8, 9]. However, considering the gold standard glucose clamp technique for assessing IR is complex, surrogate markers of IR play an important role in practical clinical applications.

Emran et al. proposed the fasting insulin to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (FINS/HDL-C) as a novel marker of IR [10]. Elevated fasting insulin (FINS) promotes hepatic fat accumulation and fibrosis by activating lipid synthesis-related genes [11], mediating inflammatory cytokines and oxidative stress markers [12], and activating apoptosis-related proteins such as caspase-3 [13], thereby exacerbating hepatocyte injury and the pathological changes of fatty liver disease [14]. In contrast, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) regulates insulin sensitivity through anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anti-apoptotic, and reverse cholesterol transport effects, thus playing a protective role against MASLD [15]. Lower HDL-C levels are significantly associated with MASLD risk, hepatic steatosis, and the severity of liver fibrosis [16–18]. Given that elevated FINS and reduced HDL-C jointly promote MASLD progression, we presumed that FINS/HDL-C would also be associated with MASLD. Therefore, we explored the association between FINS/HDL-C and MASLD severity in individuals with overweight/obesity and compared it with that of other IR markers. The homeostatic model assessment for insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), the triglyceride to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (TG/HDL-C), and triglyceride glucose index (TyG) were chosen because these markers have been reported to correlated with MASLD severity [9, 19, 20]. The potential role of body mass index (BMI) in the association was also examined.

Methods

Study population

The study population was drawn from the weight management clinic of Tianjin Union Medical Center, the First Affiliated Hospital of Nankai University, between 2021 and 2025. Inclusion criteria: (1) BMI ≥ 24 kg/m2; (2) participants clinically diagnosed with MASLD (CAP ≥ 248 dB/m). Exclusion criteria: (1) age < 18 years old; (2) participants with type 1 diabetes mellitus or receiving insulin therapy or medication known to affect FINS (including sulfonylureas, glinides, DPP‑4 inhibitors, GLP-1RA and glucocorticoids); (3) participants receiving lipid-lowering therapy; (4) pregnant or breastfeeding women, and participants with psychiatric disorders or serious decline of health status; (5) those with missing index values. Ultimately, a total of 330 participants were included in the final analysis.

The protocol of this research was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Tianjin Union Medical Center (NO.2021C06). All participants signed a written informed consent prior to participation.

Measurements

In our study, demographic information such as gender, age, history of diabetes, weight, height, and calculated BMI was collected by trained nurses. All participants had venous blood drawn after fasting for at least 8 h and the following parameters were immediately measured: FINS, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), urea, creatinine (Cr), uric acid (UA), albumin (Alb), total bilirubin (TBIL), indirect bilirubin (IBIL), direct bilirubin (DBIL), triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), HDL-C, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and alanine aminotransferase (ALT). FINS was measured by chemiluminescent immunoassay using a MAGLUMI X-8 analyzer (Snibe, Shenzhen, China). The analytical sensitivity of the assay was 0.5 µIU/mL. All other laboratory indicators were analyzed using an automated biochemistry analyzer (Beckman Coulter AU5800). All participants underwent vibration-controlled transient elastography after fasting for at least three hours. The patient assumed the supine position, fully abducted their right arm and placed it behind their head, and then held their breath for 10 s. Probe selection (M or XL) followed the device’s automatic recommendation or the operator’s judgment based on the skin-to-liver capsule distance, with the XL probe used when this distance exceeded 2.5 cm. When at least 10 successful measurements were obtained and the LSM interquartile range (IQR)-median ratio was < 30%, CAP (dB/m) and LSM (kPa) were measured in the same valid transient elastography examination.

Definition of MASLD

MASLD is defined according to 2024 Chinese guidelines for MASLD [24]. In our study, CAP and LSM data were collected to assess MASLD severity. According to the above guidelines and previous research, the cutoff values for MASLD and severe hepatic steatosis were CAP ≥ 248 dB/m and CAP ≥ 294 dB/m, respectively [24, 25].Advanced fibrosis was defined as LSM > 12 kPa [24, 26].

Definition of obesity

We adopted the diagnostic criteria recommended in the 2024 Chinese Guidelines for obesity [27] and defined obesity based on BMI, 24 kg/m2 ≤ BMI < 28 kg/m2 was considered overweight, and BMI ≥ 28 kg/m2 was considered obese.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Normally distributed variables were analyzed using Student’s t-test, while non-normally distributed variables were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test or the Kruskal-Wallis test. Categorical variables were analyzed by chi-square test. Post hoc analysis with Bonferroni correction was applied for three-group comparisons. Spearman correlation analysis was adopted to explore the correlations of CAP and LSM with demographic, laboratory indicators and IR markers. Linear regression analyses were employed to explore the relationships between IR markers and CAP and LSM. To control the influence of potential confounding factors, we adjusted for age, gender, BMI, urea, Cr, and UA. Subgroup analysis examined the heterogeneity of the indicators across gender, age, obesity classification, and type 2 diabetes status. Age groups were defined as < 40 and ≥ 40 years, because individuals aged ≥ 40 years have a higher incidence of MASLD [28, 29]. Diabetes status was determined according to the 2025 American Diabetes Association (ADA) criteria [30]and the detailed diagnostic criteria are provided in supplementary material (Tables S1 and S2). Additionally, mediation analysis with bootstrapping (5,000 resamples) was conducted to determine the role of BMI on the associations between IR markers and CAP and LSM. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis compared the diagnostic value of different IR markers for severe hepatic steatosis and advanced fibrosis. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 27.0), R software (version 4.4.1) and GraphPad Prism 10. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Characteristics of the study population

We ultimately recruited 330 participants. Table 1 shows the baseline characteristics of all participants stratified by CAP. There were 222 participants diagnosed with severe hepatic steatosis. In comparison to non-severe hepatic steatosis group, severe steatosis group had more males, with higher levels of BMI, FINS, ALT, AST, FINS/HDL-C, HOMA-IR, TG/HDL-C, and TyG but a lower level of HDL-C (all P < 0.05). Table 2 presents baseline characteristics of all participants stratified by LSM. Participants with higher LSM had a higher proportion of males, as well as significantly higher levels of BMI, FINS, ALT, AST, FINS/HDL-C, and HOMA-IR (P < 0.017). However, intergroup comparisons revealed no significant differences in HDL-C, TG/HDL-C and TyG at the LSM level (P > 0.017).

Association between markers of IR and CAP

Spearman correlation analysis (Table 3) showed significant positive correlations between CAP and FINS/HDL-C, HOMA-IR, TG/HDL-C, and TyG (all P < 0.05). Linear regression analysis (Table 4, Table S3) confirmed that the positive correlations remained significant after adjusting for age, gender, BMI, urea, Cr, and UA (P < 0.05). But among all IR markers, only FINS/HDL-C showed a significant correlation with elevated CAP in Q2 (P < 0.05).

Unadjusted subgroup analysis revealed that the relationship between FINS/HDL-C and CAP remained significant across all subgroups and no significant interaction across age, gender, BMI, and diabetes groups (all P for interaction > 0.05) (Fig. 1A). There was a significant interaction between age and HOMA-IR on CAP (P = 0.024) (Fig. 1B). Diabetes status affects the association between TG/HDL-C and CAP (P = 0.017) (Fig. 1C). The association between TyG and CAP was not significant in overweight individuals (P = 0.130) or the diabetic group (P = 0.069) (Fig. 1D).

Association between markers of IR and LSM

In Spearman correlation analysis, LSM was positively correlated with FINS/HDL-C, HOMA-IR, TG/HDL-C, and TyG (all P < 0.05) (Table 5). However, linear regression analysis showed that after adjusting for age, gender, BMI, urea, Cr, and UA, only FINS/HDL-C and HOMA-IR had robust associations with LSM (Table 6, Table S5).

Unadjusted subgroup analysis confirmed the above results (Fig. 3). Furthermore, the association between FINS/HDL-C and LSM progressively strengthened with increasing severity of impaired glucose metabolism (P = 0.007) (Fig. 3A). The relationship between HOMA-IR and LSM was stronger in males than in females (P = 0.005) (Fig. 3B). TG/HDL-C was associated with LSM only in individuals ≥ 40 years old (P = 0.036) (Fig. 3C). The association between TyG and LSM was significant only in individuals < 40 years old, females, and obese groups (Fig. 3D).

Discussion

Our findings indicated that FINS/HDL-C and other IR markers were all associated with MASLD severity. However, FINS/HDL-C was the only marker associated with elevated CAP even at Q2. Additionally, our study shows that BMI mediate the associations of FINS/HDL-C with CAP and LSM. But the causal relationship between IR and BMI is bidirectional, and their temporal sequence has not yet been determined.

These results are consistent with previous studies. A study involving 700 adults demonstrated that HOMA-IR was significantly correlated with CAP and LSM, with BMI mediating this association. A separate retrospective analysis confirmed that HOMA-IR is an independent risk factor for liver fibrosis [31]. A retrospective observational analysis of 346 overweight/obese individuals with MASLD showed that HOMA-IR, TG/HDL-C, and TyG were all associated with CAP, while only HOMA-IR was positively correlated with LSM [9]. Building on previous studies, our study incorporated FINS/HDL‑C as an additional IR marker. We found that FINS/HDL‑C was not only positively correlated with CAP and LSM, but also served as a more sensitive marker for CAP elevation. This might be attributed to the ratio’s ability to capture abnormalities in both glucose and lipid metabolism: FINS exacerbates hepatic steatosis [32], whereas HDL-C alleviates it by facilitating cholesterol efflux from hepatocytes [33]. Moreover, the elevation of FINS precedes FPG during IR [34], while TG accumulation lags behind IR [35]. This physiological sequence may partially explain why FINS/HDL-C was more sensitive than TyG in showing an increase in CAP. However, given that this is the first study to explore the association between FINS/HDL‑C and MASLD, further clinical and basic research is warranted to validate our findings and elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

Various studies reveal the specific mechanisms between IR and MASLD. IR enhances hepatic de novo lipogenesis, thereby upregulating fatty acid synthases [32, 36]. In addition, IR inhibits lipolysis by reducing fatty acid β-oxidation through inhibition of AMPK [37]. IR also impairs the normal antilipolytic action of insulin, and lipid spillage leads to the release of large amounts of free fatty acids (FFAs) into the bloodstream [38]. FFAs are direct feedstock for hepatic fat, FFAs can also exacerbate steatosis and IR by exacerbating hyperinsulinemia [39]. Hepatic steatosis in turn exacerbates IR. A Mendelian randomization analysis confirmed that hepatic fat content depends on liver injury to increase IR [40]. In conclusion, IR and hepatic steatosis are causative of each other and are tightly linked through intricate mechanisms. IR is also closely associated with liver fibrosis. Hyperinsulinemia induced by IR activates the insulin-like growth factor-1 receptor and the phosphoinositide 3-kinase/Akt pathway, promoting hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) proliferation and directly inducing hepatic fibrosis [41–43]. Hyperinsulinemia also activates key inflammatory pathway factors, exacerbating hepatocyte injury and activating HSCs [44]. Insulin resistance-induced elevated FFAs activate inflammatory and fibrotic pathways by inducing endoplasmic reticulum stress and oxidative stress [43, 45]. The resulting adipose tissue dysfunction promotes HSCs proliferation and collagen production by decreasing adiponectin levels and increasing leptin levels [46, 47], initiating the central mechanisms of liver fibrosis. IR also disrupts gut microbiota. Endotoxin translocation into the portal vein activates liver Kupffer cells, triggering inflammation and fibrosis [48].

Our study is the first to systematically analyze the association between FINS/HDL-C and MASLD severity in population with overweight/obese. However, there are some limitations of this study that need to be discussed. First, the study assessed hepatic steatosis and fibrosis on the basis of vibration controlled transient elastography results without using the gold standard liver biopsy. Second, the cross-sectional design precludes verification of the causal relationship between FINS/HDL-C and MASLD severity. Third, our findings are not applicable to individuals with normal weight, and the generalizability of the conclusions requires further validation.

Conclusions

FINS/HDL-C was correlated with MASLD severity and was the only biomarker showing an elevation in CAP starting from Quartile 2 in Chinese population with overweight/obesity. BMI might be involved in the association between FINS/HDL-C and CAP and LSM. Our research indicates that FINS/HDL-C might serve as a valuable biomarker for assessing MASLD severity. Further prospective studies are needed to verify the causal associations and specific mechanisms.
