Abstract
. The tangent degree τ ⁡ ( X ) \tau(X) of a projective variety X n ⊂ ℙ N X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N} is the number of tangent spaces to X X at smooth points passing through a general point of the tangent variety Tan ⁡ ( X ) ⊆ ℙ N \operatorname{Tan}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N} , if positive and finite; it is equal to zero if dim ( Tan ⁡ ( X ) ) < 2 ​ n \dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))<2n . In this paper we focus on general properties of τ ⁡ ( X ) \tau(X) and of deg ⁡ ( Tan ⁡ ( X ) ) \deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X)) . For example τ ⁡ ( X ) ≠ 1 \tau(X)\neq 1 if N = 2 ​ n N=2n and, as soon as Tan ⁡ ( X ) \operatorname{Tan}(X) does not coincide with the secant variety, we prove a linear lower bound for the degree of Tan ⁡ ( X ) \operatorname{Tan}(X) in terms of its codimension in the spirit of [ 6 ] . Then we consider the cases in which the previous two invariants attain the lower bounds found here, either in small dimension/codimension and/or under the smoothness assumption. Finally for N ≥ 2 ​ n + 1 N\geq 2n+1 we consider varieties X n ⊂ ℙ N X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N} having τ ⁡ ( X ) > 1 \tau(X)>1 and provide their classification in small dimension.

Introduction

The starting point of the topics we consider in this paper is the higher dimensional generalization of the following elementary facts: if
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is an irreducible curve, then

\tau(X)\geq 2
if
\deg(X)\geq 2
and
\tau(X)=2
if and only if
X
is a conic.
We first prove that
\tau(X)\neq 1
for an irreducible variety
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
and that if
\tau(X)=2
, then
X
is birational to its general tangent space via an explicit map (see Theorem
10
and Lemma
11
) and hence it is rational. Let us remark that if the singular locus of
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
consists of at most a finite number of
nodes
, then
\tau(X)\neq 1
can be deduced in various ways. For example, under these hypothesis Severi’s Double Point Formula in (
15
) implies that
\tau(X)
is even (see also Remark
13
), which is clearly false for arbitrary singular varieties. This is analogous to the case of plane curves, where Plücker’s Formulas imply that
\tau(X)
is even for curves having only nodes as singularities. Then we concentrate on the classification of varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
with
\tau(X)=2
and
n\geq 2
. After showing the existence of (singular) rational scrolls in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
of arbitrary large degree having
\tau=2
(see Example
18
), we construct other (singular) examples of arbitrary large dimension and degree, pointing out interesting connections with notable classes of algebraic varieties like
OADP
-varieties,
QEL
-varieties of type
\delta=1
or twisted cubics over Jordan algebras (see Examples
12
and
17
).

We prove that smooth varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
with
\tau(X)=2
are
QEL
-varieties of type
\delta=1
and viceversa (see Proposition
14
) and conclude that these varieties are either
smooth rational normal scrolls
S(1,\ldots,1,2)\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
or odd–dimensional Fano manifolds
X^{2m+1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4m+2}
,
m\geq 1
, with
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\langle\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\rangle

and of index
m+1
(the latter exist at least for
m=1,2
). In particular, the unique even–dimensional smooth varieties
X^{2m}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4m}
with
\tau(X)=2
are rational normal scrolls
S(1,\ldots,1,2)
(see Corollary
15
).
We then prove that irreducible surfaces
X^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
with
\tau(X)=2
are rational scrolls, which can have arbitrary large degree if they are singular (see Theorem
19
).

In the last sections we focus on the degree of the tangent variety
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
of a variety
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
N\geq 2n+1
, and specifically on the cases in which
\tau(X)\geq 2
. If
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
coincides with the secant variety
\operatorname{Sec}(X)
, then
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
(see Theorem
24
) provides a lower bound for
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))
which is quadratic in the codimension of
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. If
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)
, we show that
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 2(N-\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))+1)
(see Theorem
27
) and that this lower bound is sharp (see Example
29
and also Remark
28
).

We proceed with the construction of non-developable
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
N\geq 2n+1
, contained in a developable scroll in planes
Y^{n+1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
, showing that there exist
X^{n}\subset Y^{n+1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
\tau(X)
arbitrary large. In most cases such varieties are singular with the notable exceptions of the so called
Roth varieties
, for which
Y=S(L,Z)
is a cone with vertex a line
L
over a smooth rational normal scroll
Z^{n-2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
(see Theorem
35
).
Surfaces
X^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
N\geq 5
, with
\tau(X)\geq 2
are classified in Theorem
43
, proving that either
X
is a Veronese surface in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
or
X^{2}\subset Y^{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}

with
Y^{3}
a developable scroll in planes over a curve such that the general plane intersects
X
in a curve of degree at least two. In the smooth case the previous result is refined in Corollary
44
, showing that there exist only two classes of examples: the Veronese surface in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
(
\tau=2
) and Roth surfaces
X^{2}\subset S(L,Z)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
of degree
b(N-2)+1
for some
b\geq 2
and having
\tau=b^{2}
.

The main result of
[
11
]
is a characterization of Roth varieties as the unique smooth linearly normal varieties

X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
N\geq n+2
, such that the double divisor of a general projection onto
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
is not ample. Mumford proved that these divisors vary in a base-point free linear system (see
[
11
, Introduction]
). Corollary
44
provides a different geometric characterization of Roth surfaces, leading to the natural question if this is also true for smooth linearly normal non secant–defective varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
N\geq 2n+1
and
n\geq 3
.
As far as we know this problem has not been previously addressed in the literature (neither for
n=2
).

In the first section we present a general introduction to the theory of foci of the family of tangent spaces to an algebraic variety and we collect various facts regarding the second fundamental form of an algebraic variety and developable varieties. Since these results are usually dispersed in the literature we thought that it would be useful to present them in an unitary and concise way,
without any pretension of completeness nor of originality and trying to quote explicitly all the references we are aware of. This vast area of research, lying at the crossroads of algebraic and differential geometry, has many long-standing contributions so we apologize in advance if we forgot some of them.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a quasi projective variety of dimension
n\geq 1
, that we assume to be irreducible, unless otherwise stated, and defined over
\mathbb{C}
. For any
x\in X
we denote by
T_{x}X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
the projective tangent space of
X
at
x
and by
t_{x}X
the Zariski tangent space to
X
at
x
. The locus of smooth points of
X
is the open non-empty subset
X_{\operatorname{reg}}\subseteq X
.

1.1.
Foci of families of linear spaces in
\mathbb{P}^{N}

Let
V
be a smooth quasi-projective variety of dimension
n\geq 1
and let

(1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}^{N}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

be an
n
-dimensional family of linear spaces of dimension
r\geq 1
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. This means that
p:\mathcal{V}\to V

is a smooth projective morphism
such that
p^{-1}(v)\simeq\mathbb{P}^{r}
for every
v\in V
and that
q(p^{-1}(v))=\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a linear subspace
of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
of dimension
r
for every
v\in V
. The morphism
q
is called the
tautological morphism
of the family
p:\mathcal{V}\to V
.
It is important to remark that we are not assuming that the morphism
f:V\to\mathbb{G}(r,N)
induced by the universal property
of the Hilbert scheme is injective.

A point
s\in\mathcal{V}
(or
q(s)\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
) is called a
focal point
or a
focus
, for short, of
p:\mathcal{V}\to V
if the differential of
q

at
s
is not injective. If
p(s)=v
, we shall sometimes use the notation
s\in\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
.

A point
s\in q(\mathcal{V})\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is called a
fundamental point
of
p:\mathcal{V}\to V
if
\dim(q^{-1}(s))>0
(in classical language
one says that through
s
there pass infinitely many elements of the family). Clearly, a fundamental point is a focal
point but the converse is not true in general.

If
\dim(q(\mathcal{V}))<\dim(\mathcal{V})=n+r
, then every
s\in\mathcal{V}
is a focus. In this case the notion is not interesting
and such families will be called
degenerated
.
From now on we shall suppose that the family
p:\mathcal{V}\to V
is non-degenerate, i.e. that
\dim(q(\mathcal{V}))=\dim(\mathcal{V})=n+r
(hence necessarily
N\geq n+r
).

By definition the quasi-projective variety
\mathcal{V}
is smooth and irreducible. By applying generic smoothness to
q:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
, the non-degenerate assumption assures that the ramification locus of
q
does not contain a general
\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}=p^{-1}(v)
. The set of focal points in
\mathcal{V}
, which will be also called the focal locus, is a closed Zariski subset and, under our hypothesis, for
v\in V
general it intersects
\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
in a proper closed subset.

The characteristic map of
p:\mathcal{V}\to V
at
v\in V
is the differential of the induced map
f:V\to\mathbb{G}(r,N)
at
v
:

(df)_{v}:t_{v}V\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(N_{\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}/\mathbb{P}^{N}})\simeq t_{[\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}]}\mathbb{G}(r,N),

where
N_{\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}/\mathbb{P}^{N}}
is the normal bundle of
\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
.
The globalization of this map to
\mathcal{V}\subset V\times\mathbb{P}^{N}

induces a morphism of sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
by restriction to the fiber
\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
:

(2)

\chi(v):t_{v}V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}}\to N_{\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}/\mathbb{P}^{N}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}}(1)^{N-r},

which is injective for
v\in V
general by the non-degenerate assumption (see
[
7
]
for details). Since
N\geq n+r
, then
N-r\geq n
and it is not difficult to see that
s\in\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
is a focus of
p:\mathcal{V}\to V
if and only if the induced morphism
\chi(v)_{s}:k(s)^{n}\to k(s)^{N-r}
satisfies
\operatorname{rk}(\chi(v)_{s})<n
(see also
[
7
]
and
[
9
, §2.2.4]
, revisiting in modern terms C. Segre’s approach in
[
21
]
). In particular for
N-r=n
, or equivalently for
\overline{q(\mathcal{V})}=\mathbb{P}^{N}
, the locus of focal points in
\mathbb{P}_{v}^{r}
is the support of a hypersurface of degree
n
.

The map
p:\mathcal{V}\to V
is, up to shrinking
V
, locally trivial so that we can suppose that for any
v\in V
and
s\in\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
we have

(3)

t_{s}\mathcal{V}\simeq t_{v}V\times t_{s}\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}.

Since
q
restricted to
\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
is an isomorphism,
s\in\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
is a focus if and only if there exists a non zero vector
(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{0})\in t_{s}\mathcal{V}
such that
(dq)_{s}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}
.

In the sequel we shall explain in which sense, according to the classical terminology used by C. Segre, a focus
s\in\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
as above lies in an infinitely near
r
-plane of the family
p
in the direction
\mathbf{w}\in t_{v}V
. We shall start with the easiest example, well-known in elementary differential geometry but useful to understand some
non-standard definitions introduced later.

Let
\mathcal{L}(A)
denote the linear subspace generated by a set
A
in a vector space and let us denote by
\langle A\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
the linear span of a
subset
A\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
. If
A=\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
, then
\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle:=\langle\{a_{1},a_{2}\}\rangle
.

Example 1
.

Let
p:\mathcal{S}\to C
be a non-degenerate one-dimensional family of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, over a smooth curve
C
and let
q:\mathcal{S}\to S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}

be the associate tautological morphism, which we suppose to be birational onto its image
S
(this implies that the associated map
f:C\to\widetilde{C}\subset\mathbb{G}(1,N)
is also birational onto its image
\widetilde{C}
).
Let
c_{0}\in C
be a general point and let
l_{0}=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{c_{0}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the corresponding line. We shall study
S
locally around a (smooth) point
s_{0}\in l_{0}\subset S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

To this aim we can suppose that
S
has, locally around
s_{0}\in l_{0}
, a parametrization
\mathbf{x}:\Delta\to\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
,
\Delta\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
, of the the form

\mathbf{x}(u,v)=a(u)+v\cdot b(u),

i.e. we are supposing that
u
is a coordinate in a neighbourhood of
c_{0}
centred at
0\in C
and that
l_{u}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(a(u),b(u)))\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
are the lines near
l_{0}
in
S
(the value
v=\infty
corresponds to the point at infinity of
l_{u}
).

The projective tangent plane to
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
at a general point
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x}(0,v))
is

T_{(0,v)}S=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(a(0)+v\cdot b(0),b(0),a^{\prime}(0)+v\cdot b^{\prime}(0))),

which clearly coincides with
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(a(0),b(0),a^{\prime}(0)+v\cdot b^{\prime}(0)))
.

The ruled surface
S
is said to be
developable along
l_{0}
if the tangent space at the smooth points of
S
is constant along the smooth points on
l_{0}
, i.e. if it does not depend on
v
. This is clearly equivalent to the condition
\dim(\mathcal{L}(a(0),b(0),a^{\prime}(0),b^{\prime}(0)))=3
. The case
\dim(\mathcal{L}(a(0),b(0),a^{\prime}(0),b^{\prime}(0)))=2
is excluded because otherwise
q
would not be non-degenerate, contradicting our hypothesis.

If
\dim(\mathcal{L}(a(0),b(0),a^{\prime}(0),b^{\prime}(0)))=4
, it is not difficult to see that in this case for all
v\in\mathbb{C}\cup\infty
the differential
(dq)_{(0,v)}:t_{(0,v)}\mathcal{S}\to t_{(0,v)}S
is an isomorphism because

a^{\prime}(0)+v\cdot b^{\prime}(0)\not\in\mathcal{L}(a(0),b(0))

for every
v\in\mathbb{C}\cup\infty
.

If
\dim(\mathcal{L}(a(0),b(0),a^{\prime}(0),b^{\prime}(0)))=3,
then there exists a unique
\overline{v}\in\mathbb{C}\cup\infty
such that
\dim(\mathcal{L}(a(0),b(0),a^{\prime}(0)+\overline{v}\cdot b^{\prime}(0)))=2
. In this case there exists a unique focus
s=(0,\overline{v})\in l_{0}
.
If the surface
S
is developable along a general line
l_{c}=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{c}\subset S
,
c\in C
, then there exists a unique focus
s_{c}\in l_{c}
. If the focus
s_{c}
does not move, then the lines
l_{c}
pass through a fixed point
v_{0}\in S
and
S
is a cone. If the focus
s_{c}
moves, then the points
s_{c}
describe the so-called
edge of regression

of the developable ruled surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
. In both cases
s_{c}
is a singular point of
S
for any
c\in C
.

If the surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is developable along a general line
l_{c}
, then the surface is called developable and the corresponding one-dimensional family of lines
p:\mathcal{S}\to C
will be called
developable
.

The previous condition of developability has nice geometrical interpretations, one of them due to Max Nöther.

Let hypothesis be as above.
Up to shrinking
C
, we can suppose that
\widetilde{C}=f(C)\subset\mathbb{G}(1,N)

is smooth and that
f(c_{0})=[l_{0}]
is a non-singular point of
\widetilde{C}
. Let us recall that

t_{[l_{0}]}\mathbb{G}(1,N)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{L}(a(0),b(0)),\frac{\mathbb{C}^{N+1}}{\mathcal{L}(a(0),b(0))}).

Then it is not difficult to realize that
t_{[l_{0}]}\widetilde{C}
corresponds to the non-zero homomorphism

\alpha\cdot a(0)+\beta\cdot b(0)\mapsto[\alpha\cdot a^{\prime}(0)+\beta\cdot b^{\prime}(0)].

Hence
S
is developable along
l_{0}
if and only if
t_{[l_{0}]}\widetilde{C}
is not injective. In this case the focus on
l_{0}
corresponds to the unique
\overline{v}\in\mathbb{C}\cup\infty
determined by
\mathbb{P}(\ker(t_{[l_{0}]}\widetilde{C})).
We shall generalize this example in §
2.3
.

A variety

We collect a series of well-known results/remarks for further reference.

Remark 2
.

Let
\varrho=\varrho(X):=\dim(X)-\dim(\widetilde{X})\geq 0
be the
Gauss defect of
X
. It is well-known that the closure of a general fiber of
\mathcal{G}_{X}
is a linear space
\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}\subset X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
(see for example
[
20
, Theorem 1.5.10]
). In particular, either
\mathcal{G}_{X}:X\dashrightarrow\widetilde{X}
is birational or
X
is developable and a general tangent space is constant along an open subset of a linear space of dimension
\varrho
. For a non-developable variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
, the birationality onto the image of
\mathcal{G}_{X}
assures that the two families
\overline{p}:T_{X}\to X
and
\widetilde{p}:\widetilde{T}\to\widetilde{X}
coincide in a neighbourhood of a general point
x\in X
.

If
U\subseteq X_{\operatorname{reg}}
is isomorphic to its image
\widetilde{U}\subseteq\widetilde{X}

via
\mathcal{G}_{X}
, then the family of tangent spaces is locally trivial over
U
, being isomorphic over
U
to
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_{X}^{*}({\mathcal{S}}_{|\widetilde{U}}))\to U
. For smooth varieties
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
the locally free sheaf
\mathcal{G}_{X}^{*}({\mathcal{S}}_{|\widetilde{X}})
of rank
n+1
is isomorphic to the
sheaf of principal parts of
(X,\mathcal{O}(1))
, which can be defined directly on
X
as the extension of
\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes O(1)
by
\mathcal{O}(1)
determined by
c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))\in H^{1}(\Omega_{X}^{1})
:

(6)

0\to\Omega_{X}^{1}(1)\to\mathcal{P}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}(1)\to 0,

yielding
\det(\mathcal{P}_{X})\simeq\omega_{X}(n+1)
and that (
6
) is not splitting.

Remark 3
.

The above terminology generalizes the one introduced in Example
1
for the case of surfaces. For a developable surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
as in Example
1
we have that
\widetilde{S}
is the closure of
\widetilde{C}
in
\mathbb{G}(1,N)
, that
\varrho(S)=1
and that the closure of a general fiber of
\mathcal{G}_{S}
is
l_{c}
.

An irreducible curve
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is developable if and only if it is a line. Developable varieties of dimension two and three have been classified. A developable surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, is either a plane, a cone over a non-degenerate curve or the tangent variety to a non-degenerate curve (see for example
[
9
, Proposition 2.5.6]
). We shall come back to the three dimensional case later.

If
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is developable, then necessarily
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\leq\dim(\widetilde{X})+n<2n
. The converse is not true since there exist smooth varieties
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}

of dimension
n\geq 3
such that
N\geq 2n
and
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))<2n
. For example the tangent variety to the Segre fourfold
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{8}
is
a cubic hypersurface.

1.3.
Focal locus of the family of tangent spaces to a variety

The study of the focal and the fundamental locus of the family of projective tangent spaces to a variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
of dimension
n\geq 2
deserves a
special treatment for various peculiarities.

From now on we shall suppose
N\geq 2n
and that the family of tangent spaces to
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is non-degenerate, i.e.
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2n
. In particular,
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is non-developable. Under these hypothesis, the morphisms
\overline{q}:T_{X}\to\operatorname{Tan}(X)
and
q:T^{\circ}_{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
are generically étale. In analogy with the situation illustrated in Example
1
we shall analyse in detail the focal locus of
p:T_{X}^{\circ}\to X_{\operatorname{reg}}
.

We study
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
locally around a general fixed
x\in X_{\operatorname{reg}}
.
Let
\mathbb{P}:\mathbb{C}^{N+1}\setminus\mathbf{0}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the quotient morphism. We can suppose
x=\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{e}_{0})
.
Let

(7)

\mathbf{\Phi}:\Delta^{n}\to U\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}=\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus V(x_{0})

be a local holomorphic parametrization of
X
defined on a sufficiently small polydisc
\Delta^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
with coordinates
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
centered at
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and such that
x=\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{0})
. If
\mathbf{\Phi}=(\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{N})
, then the Jacobian of the functions
\Phi_{i}
is of maximal rank
n
at every point
\mathbf{u}\in\Delta^{n}.

Let

\partial_{i}\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})=(\frac{\partial\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial u_{i}}(\mathbf{u}),\ldots,\frac{\partial\mathbf{\Phi}_{N}}{\partial u_{i}}(\mathbf{u})).

Then

t_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})}X\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}

has a parametrization
\phi_{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to t_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})}X
given
by

\phi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{\lambda})=\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u}),

where
\mathbf{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Let us recall the identifications

t_{\phi_{\mathbf{u}}(\lambda)}T_{\Phi(\mathbf{u})}X=t_{\phi_{\mathbf{u}}(\lambda)}t_{\Phi(\mathbf{u})}X=t_{\Phi(\mathbf{u})}X

so that

t_{(\Phi(\mathbf{u}),\phi_{\mathbf{u}}(\lambda))}T^{\circ}_{X}=t_{\Phi(\mathbf{u})}X\times t_{\phi_{\mathbf{u}}(\lambda)}T_{x}X=t_{\Phi(\mathbf{u})}X\times t_{\Phi(\mathbf{u})}X.

For every
j=1,\ldots,n
, let

\mathbf{w}_{j}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{j}}(\mathbf{u}),\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{\Phi}_{N}}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{j}}(\mathbf{u})).

The image of

dq_{(\Phi(\mathbf{u}),\phi_{\mathbf{u}}(\lambda))}:t_{\Phi(\mathbf{u})}X\times t_{\Phi(\mathbf{u})}X\rightarrow t_{\phi_{\mathbf{u}}(\lambda)}\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{N}

is the affine subspace passing through
\mathbf{\phi}_{\mathbf{u}}(\lambda)
and having direction equal to

(8)

\mathcal{L}(\partial_{1}\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u}),\ldots,\partial_{n}\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u}),\mathbf{w}_{1}(\lambda,\mathbf{u}),\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{n}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})),

whose dimension is

n+\dim(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}_{1}(\lambda,\mathbf{u}),\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{n}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})))\leq 2n.

The (second)
osculating space
T^{(2)}_{x}X
at
x
is the projective closure of the affine space through
x=\Phi(\mathbf{0})
with direction as in (
8
) with
(\lambda,\mathbf{u})=(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0}).
Using higher order derivations one defines
T^{(k)}_{x}X
for every
x\in X
and for every
k\geq 1
in an analogous way. With this definition clearly
T^{(1)}_{x}X=T_{x}X
for any
x\in X
.

We have proved the following result, already known (at least) to Terracini (see
[
24
]
).

Proposition 4
.

Let notation be as above and suppose
\lambda\neq\mathbf{0}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(
i
)

(x,\phi_{\mathbf{0}}(\lambda))\in x\times t_{x}X\subset x\times T_{x}X
is a focus of the family of projective tangent spaces;

(
ii
)

\dim(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}_{1}(\lambda,\mathbf{0}),\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{n}(\lambda,\mathbf{0})))<n;

(
iii
)

There exists

\mathbf{v}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}\cdot\partial_{j}{\mathbf{\Phi}}(\mathbf{0})\in t_{x}X\setminus x

such that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}\cdot\mathbf{w}_{j}(\lambda,\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0};

or, equivalently, such that

(9)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{\Phi}_{k}}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{j}}(\mathbf{0})\cdot\mu_{j}=0

for every
k=1,\ldots,N.

Moreover,
(x,\phi_{\mathbf{0}}(\lambda))\in x\times t_{x}X
,
\lambda\neq\mathbf{0}
, is a focus of the family of tangent spaces to
X
if and only if
(x,\phi_{\mathbf{0}}(\alpha\cdot\lambda))\in x\times t_{x}X
is a focus of the family of tangent spaces to
X
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

In particular, if
(x,\phi_{\mathbf{0}}(\lambda))\in x\times t_{x}X
is a focus, then the line
\langle x,\phi_{\mathbf{0}}(\lambda)\rangle\subset T_{x}X
consists of foci and the support of the intersection of the focal locus of the family of tangent spaces to
X
with
T_{x}X
is a cone with vertex
x
.

Remark 5
.

Generic smoothness and (
8
) yield the following dimension formula for an arbitrary
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
:

(10)

\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=n+\dim(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}_{1}(\lambda,\mathbf{u}),\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{n}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})))

for
(\lambda,\mathbf{u})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\Delta
general and also

(11)

\dim(T_{x}^{(2)}X)=n+\dim(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}_{1}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0}),\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})))

for
x\in X_{\operatorname{reg}}.

Moreover, the previous computations also show that
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
is singular along
X
if
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
(see also
[
9
, Proposition 3.3.3]
). In general, the singular locus of
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
can be bigger than
X
. The next well-known result is again a direct consequence of the above analysis (see also
[
9
, Proposition 3.3.1]
).

Proposition 6
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a non-degenerate variety.
Then
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a developable variety. More precisely, if
y\in\operatorname{Tan}(X)
is general, i.e.
y\in T_{x}X
is general with
x\in X
general, then the projecitve tangent space to
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
remains constant along
\langle x,y\rangle\cap\operatorname{Tan}(X)_{\operatorname{reg}}
and
T_{x}X\subset T_{z}\operatorname{Tan}(X)
for
z\in\langle x,y\rangle
general.

1.4.
Second fundamental form of a projective variety

Let notation and hypothesis be as above and let
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\Delta^{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{-1}(U)=\mathbb{C}^{*}\times U\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+1}\setminus\mathbf{0}
be the lifting of
\mathbf{\Phi}
to the quotient map
\mathbb{P}:\mathbb{C}^{N+1}\setminus\mathbf{0}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
given by

\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}(\alpha,\mathbf{u})=(\alpha,\alpha\cdot\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})).

The
normal space to
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
at
x
is

N_{x}X=\frac{t_{x}\mathbb{P}^{N}}{t_{x}X}\simeq\frac{t_{x}\mathbb{C}^{N}}{t_{x}X},

where
\mathbb{C}^{N}=\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus V(x_{0}).

If
T_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})}X=\mathbb{P}(W_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})})
with
W_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
, then
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}(\alpha,\mathbf{u})\in W_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})}
for every
(\alpha,\mathbf{u})\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\Delta^{n}
and

t_{x}X\simeq\frac{W_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})}}{\langle\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}(1,\mathbf{u})\rangle}\mbox{, respectively }t_{x}\mathbb{P}^{N}\simeq\frac{\mathbb{C}^{N+1}}{\langle\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}(1,\mathbf{u})\rangle}.

Hence

t_{[T_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})}X]}\mathbb{G}(n,N)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(W_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})},\frac{\mathbb{C}^{N+1}}{W_{\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})}})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(t_{x}X,N_{x}X).

Let
\pi:t_{x}\mathbb{C}^{N}\to N_{x}X
be the quotient map and let

\varphi:t_{x}X\to\operatorname{Hom}(t_{x}X,N_{x}X)
be the linear map defined by

\varphi(\lambda)=\pi\circ dq_{(x,\phi_{\mathbf{0}}(\lambda))_{t_{x}X\times\mathbf{0}}}.

If

\mathbf{v}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}\cdot\partial_{j}{\mathbf{\Phi}}(\mathbf{0})\in t_{x}X

and if
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}),

then

(12)

\varphi(\lambda)(\mathbf{v})=\varphi(\lambda,\mu):=[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}\cdot\mathbf{w}_{j}(\lambda,\mathbf{0})]\in N_{x}X.

Since
\phi_{\mathbf{0}}
is linear, the linear map
\varphi
induces a bilinear map

\widetilde{\varphi}_{x}:t_{x}X\times t_{x}X\to N_{x}X,

which is symmetric by (
9
). The image of the dual linear map
(N_{x}X)^{*}\to S^{2}(t_{x}X^{*})
is a vector space of homogenous quadratic forms on
t_{x}X
.

The associated linear system of quadrics
|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|
on
\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
(or the above symmetric bilinear map
\widetilde{\varphi}_{x}
) is called the
second
fundamental form of
X
at
x
. By definition
\dim(|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|)\leq N-n-1
and, letting
\varphi_{x}:\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)\dasharrow|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|^{*}
be the associated rational map, formula (
10
)
translates into

(13)

\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=n+1+\dim(\overline{\varphi_{x}(\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)})

(see also
[
10
,
\lx@sectionsign
5.7, p. 408]
or
[
9
, Theorem 3.3.1, p. 129]
).
In particular for
N\leq 2n
,
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=N
implies
\dim(|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|)=N-n-1
.

Remark 7
.

The map (
12
) is nothing but the differential of the Gauss map, i.e. the linear map

(d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}:t_{x}X\to t_{[T_{x}X]}\mathbb{G}(n,N)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(t_{x}X,N_{x}X).

As above, suppose that
N\geq 2n
, yielding
\dim(N_{x}X)=N-n\geq n
, and also that
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2n
. Then Proposition
4
can be reinterpreted in the following way. Let
\mathbf{w}\in t_{x}X\setminus x
and let
(d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}(\mathbf{w}):t_{x}X\to N_{x}X
be the corresponding linear map. The map
(d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}(\mathbf{w})
is not injective if and only if
(d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{w})
is not injective for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
if and only if the line
\langle x,\mathbf{w}\rangle
consists of foci of the family of tangent linear spaces to
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
(see also
[
10
,
\lx@sectionsign
5.5, p. 408]
).

Let
\mathbf{w}\in t_{x}X\setminus x
, let
Q\in|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|
be a quadric hypersurface and let
H_{[\mathbf{w}],Q}\subset\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)
be the polar hyperplane of
Q
with respect to
[\mathbf{w}]
. Then

\mathbb{P}(\ker((d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}(\mathbf{w})))=\bigcap_{Q\in|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|}H_{[\mathbf{w}],Q}.

In particular
\mathbf{w}\in t_{x}X\setminus x
is a focus if and only if the intersection of the polar hyperplanes of the quadrics in
|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|
with respect to
[\mathbf{w}]
is not empty. Clearly this intersection may be computed as the intersection of the polar hyperplanes of a basis of the second fundamental form.

If
T_{x}X=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}(e_{0})\oplus t_{x}X)
, then
\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)
can be identified with the
hyperplane at infinity
for the previous splitting and
t_{x}X\subset T_{x}X
with the affine space
T_{x}X\setminus\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)
.
Hence the points in
\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)
correspond to the directions through
x
of the lines in
t_{x}X
passing through
x
. For this reason we shall say that, for
\mathbf{v}\in t_{x}X\setminus x
, the point
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)
is the direction of the line
\langle x,\mathbf{v}\rangle\subset t_{x}X
(or of the line
\langle x,[\mathbf{v}]\rangle\subset T_{x}X
).

Two directions
[\mathbf{v}],[\mathbf{w}]\in\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)
are said to be
conjugate
if
(d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}(\mathbf{v})(\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{0}
, which by symmetry occurs if and only if

(d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}(\mathbf{w})(\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{0}.
This means that they are
conjugate
(or
polar
) for every quadric
Q\in|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|
.

A direction
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)
is said to be an
asymptotic direction
if
(dG_{X})_{x}(\mathbf{v})(\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{0}
.
Using the parameterization (
7
) and the definition of the second fundamental form we deduce that a direction
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)
is asymptotic if and only if the multiplicity of intersection of the
tangent line
\langle x,[\mathbf{v}]\rangle
with
X
at
x
is at least 3. Equivalently,
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)
is an asymptotic direction if and only if
[\mathbf{v}]\in\operatorname{Bs}(|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|)
, the base locus of the second fundamental form of
X
at
x
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
x\in X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a general point and suppose that
r\in T_{x}X\setminus x
is a fundamental point for the family of tangent spaces to
X
.
Then
\langle x,r\rangle\cap\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)\in\operatorname{Bs}(|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|)
.

Proof.

Suppose
r\in T_{x}X\setminus x
is a fundamental point and let
\langle x,r\rangle\cap\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)=[\mathbf{w}]
.
Let
\widetilde{C_{r}}\subset X_{\operatorname{reg}}\times r\subset T_{X}^{\circ}
be a smooth curve such that
(x,r)\in\widetilde{C_{r}}
and let
\mathbf{v}\in t_{x}X
be such that

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v})=t_{(x,r)}\widetilde{C_{r}}\subseteq\ker((dq)_{(x,r)})\simeq\ker((d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}(\mathbf{w})),

yielding
(d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}(\mathbf{w})(\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{0}
.
Define
C_{r}:=p(\widetilde{C_{r}})\subset X
. Then
x\in C_{r}
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v})=t_{x}C_{r}\subset t_{x}X
. If
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w})
, then

\mathbf{0}=(d\mathcal{G}_{X})_{x}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})
and
[\mathbf{w}]\in\operatorname{Bs}(|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|)
.

Suppose
\mathbf{v}\not\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w})
. For each
y\in C_{r}\setminus x
the line
L_{y}:=\langle y,r\rangle
is tangent to
X
at
y
, i.e.
L_{y}\subset T_{y}X
and so
\operatorname{mult}_{y}(L_{y}\cap X)\geq 2
. When
y
tends to
x
along
C_{r}
, or equivalently, in the direction specified by
\mathbf{v}
, the line
L_{y}
tends to the line
L_{x}=\langle x,r\rangle\subset T_{x}X
. Since
L_{x}
is already tangent to
X
at
x
in a direction different from
\mathbf{v}
, we deduce
\operatorname{mult}_{x}(L_{x}\cap X)\geq 3
, yielding
[\mathbf{w}]\in\operatorname{Bs}(|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|)
.
∎

We now recall for further reference an important fact on the base locus of the second fundamental form
|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|
. We shall adopt the classical definition of
scroll over a curve of dimension
n
, that is
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is the image of a 1-dimensional family of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
’s such that through the general point of
X
there passes a unique
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
of the family. In other words there exists a family
p:\mathcal{X}\to C
of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
’s over a smooth curve
C
such that the tautological morphism
q:\mathcal{X}\to X
is birational.

Theorem 9

(
[
10
,
\lx@sectionsign
3.21, p. 399]
)
.

Let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
n\geq 3
. If for a general
x\in X
the second fundamental form
|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|
has a fixed component, then
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a scroll in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
’s over a curve and
\mathbb{P}(t_{x}\mathbb{P}^{n-1})=\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
is a fixed component of
|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|
.

Conversely, if
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a scroll in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
’s over a curve, then
\mathbb{P}(t_{x}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{x})=\mathbb{P}^{n-2}\subseteq\operatorname{Bs}(|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|)
.

2.
The
i^{\operatorname{th}}
–ceto of an irreducible projective variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}

Following Severi (see
[
23
]
) we define the
i^{\operatorname{th}}
-ceto of a projective variety
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}

of dimension
n\geq 1
in the following way.

The
n^{\operatorname{th}}
-ceto
\omega_{n}(X)
of
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is the number of tangent spaces to
X
at smooth points
meeting a general
2n
-codimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. The
i^{\operatorname{th}}
-ceto
\omega_{i}(X)

of
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is the
i^{\operatorname{th}}
-ceto of the intersection of
X
with a general
(n-i)
-codimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

In particular
\omega_{n}(X)=0
if and only if
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))<2n
. Letting notation be as in (
5
), we put
\tau(X):=\deg(\widetilde{q})
, which we call the
tangent degree
of
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
. For
N\geq 2n
, we have

\omega_{n}(X)=\tau(X)\cdot\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X)).

If
N\geq 2n
and if we project
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
from a general subspace
L^{N-2n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
then
\omega_{n}(X)
is equal to the number of
pinch points
of the projection
\pi_{L}(X)\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}
.

For a smooth projective variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
, one can compute
\omega_{n}(X)
via Segre classes
of the locally free sheaf of principal parts
\mathcal{P}_{X}=\mathcal{G}_{X}^{*}(\mathcal{S}_{\widetilde{X}})
:

(14)

\omega_{n}(X)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}_{X})}(1)^{2n}.

The value of
\tau(X)=\deg(\widetilde{q})
is harder to compute if
N>2n
.

Severi introduced these invariants to prove the
Double Point Formula
below. If
N\geq 2n
and if
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a smooth variety of degree
d
and if
\sigma(X)
is the number of nodes of a general linear projection of
X
into
\mathbb{P}^{2n}
, then

(15)

2\cdot\sigma(X)=d\cdot(d-1)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}(X)

(see
[
23
]
and
[
1
]
for a modern treatment). Let us remark that for
n=1
(
15
) is nothing but the usual Plücker Formula for curves with at most nodes as singularities
since
\omega_{1}(X)=\deg(X^{*})
in this case.

If
\mu(X)\geq 1
is the (finite) number of secant lines to
X
passing through a general point of
\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
, called the
secant degree
of
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
, then
\sigma(X)=\mu(X)\cdot\deg(\operatorname{Sec}(X))
. One expects
\mu(X)=1
as soon as
\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
and varieties with
\mu(X)>1
are special and nowadays called
weakly defective
according to the terminology introduced by Chiantini and Ciliberto in
[
3
]
. One also expects
\tau(X)=1
if
\tau(X)>0
and
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
. In §
4
we shall see examples of smooth varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
N>2n
and
\tau(X)
arbitrary large.

2.1.
Generalities on
\omega_{n}
for the case
N=2n

For an irreducible curve
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
we have
\tau(X)=0

if and only if
X
is a line and
\tau(X)\geq 2
for any curve
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree
d\geq 2
. Moreover,
\tau(X)=2
if and only if
d=2
, i.e.
if and only if
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a smooth conic.

We now study the case
n\geq 2
to see to what extent one can generalize the previous facts.

Theorem 10
.

Let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
be a variety with
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{2n}
. Then
\tau(X)\geq 2
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{2n}
we have
\tau(X)\geq 1
. For
n=1
, the claim is true. Suppose now
n\geq 2
and
\tau(X)=1
. Then every focal point of the family of tangent spaces to
X
is fundamental by Zariski’s Main Theorem applied to
q:T_{X}\to\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{2n}
.
Let
x\in X
be general and let
F_{x}\subset T_{x}X
be the focal hypersurface. We know that
(F_{x})_{\operatorname{red}}
is a cone with vertex
x
over a base
(\overline{F_{x}})_{\operatorname{red}}\subset\mathbb{P}(t_{x}X)\subset T_{x}X
with
\dim((\overline{F_{x}})_{\operatorname{red}})=n-2
by Proposition
4
.

Moreover, since every point
r\in(F_{x})_{\operatorname{red}}\setminus x
is fundamental, we deduce
(\overline{F}_{x})_{\operatorname{red}}\subseteq\operatorname{Bs}|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|
by Proposition
8
.
If
n\geq 3
, then
X
is a scroll over a curve by Theorem
9
and
(F_{x})_{\operatorname{red}}=\mathbb{P}_{x}^{n-1}\subseteq(T_{x}X\cap X)_{\operatorname{red}}\subset X
is a general linear space in the ruling of
X
. Since through a general point of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{x}
there are no other linear spaces of the ruling, we get a contradiction.

Suppose now
n=2
. Since
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=4
, we have
\dim(|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|)=1
by (
13
), showing that
(F_{x})_{\operatorname{red}}
consists of a line
L_{x}
tangent to
X
at
x
.

Let
F\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be the focal locus of
p:T^{\circ}_{X}\rightarrow X_{\operatorname{reg}}
. Then clearly

X\subseteq\overline{\bigcup_{x\in X_{\operatorname{reg}}}L_{x}}\subseteq F.

Since
\dim(F)\leq 2
by Zariski’s Main Theorem applied to
q:T_{X}\to\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{4}
, we have equality in the first inclusion. From this one deduces that
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is indeed a scroll over a curve because a line through
x
, contained in
X
and different from
L_{x}
would produce another base point in
\operatorname{Bs}(|\operatorname{II}_{x,X}|)
, which is impossible. Then, as above, we get a contradiction.
∎

If
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{2n}
, then
\operatorname{Sec}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{2n}
. By Terracini’s Lemma two general tangent spaces
T_{x_{1}}X
and
T_{x_{2}}X
intersect at a single point. Fixing a general
x\in X
we
define the
Scorza map at
x
for any variety
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
with
\operatorname{Sec}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{2n}
:

\operatorname{Sc}_{x}:X\dasharrow T_{x}X,

by
\operatorname{Sc}_{x}(y)=T_{y}X\cap T_{x}X
for
y\in Y
general (see also
[
5
, §5]
where this map is used in a related context). Clearly
\deg(\operatorname{Sc}_{x})=\tau(X)-1
if
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{2n}
because through a general point of
T_{x}X

there pass
\tau(X)-1
tangent spaces to
X
at smooth points different from
T_{x}X
.
We isolate the following consequence for further reference.

Lemma 11
.

Let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
be a non-degenerate variety with
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{2n}
. If
\tau(X)=2
, then
X
is birational
to a general tangent space to
X
via the Scorza map.

2.2.
Examples of
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
with
\tau(X)=2

We shall present several series of examples for every
n\geq 2
.

Example 12
.

We define the
secant defect of
X
as
\delta(X)=2n+1-\dim(\operatorname{Sec}(X))\geq 0
. For
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
and for a general
p\in\operatorname{Sec}(X)

the
entry locus of
X
with respect to
p
is:

\Sigma_{p}=\overline{\{x_{1}\in X\;:\;\exists\;x_{2}\in X\setminus x_{1}\mbox{ with }p\in\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle\}}\subseteq X.

The generality of
p\in\operatorname{Sec}(X)
assures that
\Sigma_{p}\subseteq X
is equidimensional of dimension
\delta=\delta(X)
.

Since
\deg(\Sigma_{p})\geq 2
, the varieties with the
simplest
secant behaviour are those for which
\deg(\Sigma_{p})=2
. If
\delta(X)=0
, this means that through
a general point of
\operatorname{Sec}(X)
there passes a unique secant line to
X
(the expected behavior).

A projective variety
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is said to be a
QEL
-variety of type
\delta\geq 0
, i.e. a variety with quadratic entry locus of type
\delta
, if

\Sigma_{p}\subset X
is a smooth quadric hypersurface of dimension
\delta=\delta(X)
for
p\in\operatorname{Sec}(X)
general.

Here the relevant remark for us is the following: if
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
is a
QEL
-variety of type
\delta=1
, then
\tau(X)=2
. Indeed,
the tangent lines to
X
at smooth points passing through
p
are tangent lines to
\Sigma_{p}
and
viceversa. Since
\tau(\Sigma_{p})=2
the claim follows.

Let us give some examples of
QEL
–varieties. For
n\geq 1
a smooth rational normal scroll

S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\mathbb{P}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{i}))\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}

embedded via
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)
and with
a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}=n+1
is a
QEL
-variety with
\delta=1
. The smoothness assumption implies
a_{i}\geq 1
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
.

We shall see in Theorem
19
below that the unique smooth surface in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
with
\tau=2
is
S(1,2)
, up to projective equivalence.
For
n\geq 3
there exist smooth
QEL
-varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
of type
\delta=1
which are not scrolls over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For example, consider
\mathbb{G}(1,4)\subset\mathbb{P}^{9}
, which is a
QEL
-variety of type
\delta=4
. Then a general three-dimensional linear section
X^{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{6}
of
\mathbb{G}(1,4)\subset\mathbb{P}^{9}
. Since
\mathbb{G}(1,4)\subset\mathbb{P}^{9}
is a
QEL
-variety of type
\delta=4
, we deduce that
X^{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{6}
is a smooth
QEL
-variety of type
\delta=1
with
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\langle\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\rangle
and
\omega_{X}^{*}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)
(i.e. it is a prime del Pezzo threefold).

A similar example is a general five-dimensional linear section
X^{5}\subset\mathbb{P}^{10}
of the spinor variety
S^{10}\subset\mathbb{P}^{15}
, which is a homogeneous
QEL
-variety of type
\delta=6
. In this case
X^{5}\subset\mathbb{P}^{10}
is a smooth
QEL
-variety of type
\delta=1
with
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\langle\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\rangle
and
\omega_{X}^{*}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(3)
(i.e. it is a prime Mukai fivefold).

Remark 13
.

If
N\geq n+2
, a general secant line is not trisecant
by the well-known Trisecant Lemma. Thus the projection
\pi_{p}:\mathbb{P}^{N}\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
from a general
p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
induces a
2:1
morphism from
\Sigma_{p}
onto its image. The difficulty in computing
\tau(X)
for an arbitrary
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
is due to the fact that the curve
\Sigma_{p}
may be very singular and reducible, preventing the application of Riemann-Hurwitz formula to
the covering
\pi_{p}:\Sigma_{p}\to\pi_{p}(\Sigma_{p})
in order to determine the number of smooth ramification points, which is equal to
\tau(X)
. The problem is similar to the
computation of the degree of the dual curve of an irreducible curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which is deeply affected by the complexity of its singularities (the Plücker formulas work effectively only if
C
has mild singularities).

When
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is smooth or at has a finite number of nodal singularities at worst, one can prove that
\tau(X)
is even by applying Riemann-Hurwitz formula on the
desingularizations of
\Sigma_{p}
and of
\pi_{p}(\Sigma_{p})
.

For smooth varieties we have the following result.

Proposition 14
.

Let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
be a smooth non-degenerate variety of dimension
n\geq 1
. Then
\tau(X)=2
if and only if
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
is a
QEL
-manifold of type
\delta=1
.

Proof.

We have seen above that
QEL
-manifolds
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
of type
\delta=1
have
\tau(X)=2
. Suppose now
\tau(X)=2
. Let
p\in\mathbb{P}^{2n}
be general and let notation be as in Example
12
.
The main results in
[
14
, §5.2]
yield
\deg(\Sigma_{p})=\tau(X)
since
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
is smooth, a fact which can also be deduced from Severi’s Double Point Formula (
15
). Then
\Sigma_{p}\subset X
is an equidimensional curve of degree two
such that through
p
there pass two lines which are limits of secant lines to
\Sigma_{p}
(and tangent lines to
X
) and thus it is necessarily a smooth conic.
∎

Corollary 15
.

Let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
be a smooth variety with
\tau(X)=2
. Then either
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}

is projectively equivalent to
S(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{n-1},2)
or
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
is a linearly normal rational Fano manifold with
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\langle\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\rangle

and index
i(X)=(n+1)/2
.

In particular, if
n=2m\geq 2
, then
X^{2m}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4m}
is projectively equivalent to
S(1,\ldots,1,2)
.
If
n=3
, respectively
5
, then either
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
is a rational normal scroll as above or it is one of the two Fano manifolds of dimension 3, respectively 5, described at the end of Example

12
.

Proof.

Since
\tau(X)=2
and
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
, then
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
is linearly normal. The first part follows from the classification of conic-connected
manifolds in
[
12
]
and the
Parity Theorem
for
QEL
-manifolds (see for example
[
20
, Proposition 4.3.1, part 3)]
), yielding
i(X)=(n+1)/2
. The second part is a consequence of the classification of del Pezzo and Mukai manifolds.
∎

We are not aware of the existence of
QEL
-manifolds
X^{2m+1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4m+2}
of type
\delta=1
with
m\geq 3
,
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\simeq\langle\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\rangle
and index
i(X)=m+1
.
So one may ask if the last part of Corollary
15
describe all smooth varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
with
\tau(X)=2
.

The application of the next result will provide other interesting examples of (possibly singular) varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
with
\tau(X)=2
. First we recall a definition. For an arbitrary variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
, the
vertex
of
X
, denoted by
\operatorname{Vert}(X)
, is the set of points
x\in X
such that for every
x^{\prime}\in X\setminus x
we have

\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\subseteq X
. A variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
such that
\operatorname{Vert}(X)\neq\emptyset
is called a cone. Let us recall that
\operatorname{Vert}(X)
is a linear subspace,
if non-empty.

Lemma 16
.

Let
Y^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
be a
variety such that
\operatorname{Tan}(Y)\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
is a hypersurface of degree four and such that
\tau(Y)=1
.
If
q\in\operatorname{Sing}(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))\setminus\operatorname{Vert}(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))
and if
Y_{q}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
is
the projection of
Y
from
q
, then
\tau(Y_{q})=2
and
\operatorname{mult}_{q}(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))=2
.

Proof.

Let
q\in\operatorname{Sing}(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))\setminus\operatorname{Vert}(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))
. The projection
\pi_{q}:\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{2n}
restricted to
\operatorname{Tan}(Y)
induces a generically finite rational map
\operatorname{Tan}(Y)\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{2n}
of degree

4-\operatorname{mult}_{q}(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))\geq 1
. If
y\in Y
is general, then
\pi_{q}(T_{y}Y)=T_{\pi_{q}(y)}X
, yielding
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{2n}
.
Thus from
\tau(Y)=1
we deduce
\tau(X)=4-\operatorname{mult}_{q}(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))
. Since
\operatorname{mult}_{q}(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))\geq 2
and since
\tau(X)\geq 2
by Theorem
10
,
the claims follow.
∎

Example 17
.

A projective variety
Y^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
is called an
OADP
-variety, i.e. a
variety with one apparent double point
,
if through a general
p\in\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
there passes a unique secant line to
Y
. The name originates from the fact that
Y_{p}
acquires a unique
double point
,
whose tangent cone consists of the union of the projections of the tangent spaces at the two points in
Y
spanning the unique secant passing through
p
. More generally, the number of secant lines to
Y
passing through a general
p\in\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
is the
number of apparent double points of
Y
. Equivalently, a variety
Y^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
is an
OAPD
-variety if and only if it is a
QEL
-variety of type
\delta=0
.

For an
OADP
-variety
Y^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
we necessarily have
\tau(Y)=1
by Zariski’s Main Theorem. Moreover, we also have
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))=2n
. Indeed, the projection from a general
T_{y}Y
onto
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is birational so that the strict transform of
y
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a divisor. Thus
\dim(\overline{\varphi_{y}(\mathbb{P}(t_{y}Y)}))=n-1
and we can apply formula (
13
). Therefore
OAPD
-varieties
Y^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
with
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))=4
produce examples of varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
with
\tau(X)=2
via Lemma
16
.

The only example of an
OADP
-curve is the twisted cubic
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
. As already known to Cayley, we have
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))=4
,
\operatorname{Sing}(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))_{\operatorname{red}}=Y
and
X=Y_{q}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a conic for any
q\in Y
.
This elementary fact has been generalized to the class of projective varieties
Y^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
which are 3-covered by twisted cubics, i.e. such that through three general points
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in Y
there passes a twisted cubic contained in
Y
. Such varieties are
OADP
’s by
[
16
, Corollary 5.4]
and have
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))=4
by
[
18
, Lemma 5.4]
so that a general internal projection
gives examples of
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
with
\tau(X)=2
by Lemma
16
.

The latter class of varieties includes: smooth rational normal scrolls
Y^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
; the Segre embedding of
Y^{n}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times Q^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, where
Q^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is an irreducible quadric hypersurface; four homogeneous varieties
Y^{n}
with
n=3,5,9,27
and many other singular examples produced
as
twisted cubics over a cubic Jordan algebra
(see
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
).

Let us remark that the projection of a variety as above from a point
q\in Y
is a variety
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}

such that through two general points there passes a smooth conic and, in fact, it is a
QEL
-variety of type
\delta=1
(although
X
is singular in most cases).

We shall now outline a construction due to Verra that produces infinitely many examples of
OADP
-varieties
Y^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
such that
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))=4
starting from
an
OADP
-variety
Z^{m}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2m+1}
,
m<n
, with
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(Z))=4
. In this way, for fixed
n\geq 2
, one can construct examples of singular
OADP
-varieties of arbitrary large degree.

Example 18
.

Let
Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2n+1}
be a degenerate
OADP
-variety of dimension
m<n
,
which spans a linear space
V
of dimension
2m+1
.
Take a linear space
W\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}

of dimension
2(n-m)-1
such that
V\cap W=\emptyset
.
Let

S(W,Z)=\overline{\bigcup_{w\in W,z\in Z,\\
w\neq z}\langle w,z\rangle}

indicate the
join of
W
and
Z,
which is therefore a cone
over
Z
with vertex
W
. Let
Y\subset S(W,Z)
be a non-degenerate, not secant-defective variety of dimension
n
which intersects the general ruling

\Pi_{z}:=\langle W,z\rangle\cong\mathbb{P}^{2(n-m)}

of
S(W,Z)

along a linear subspace
P_{z}
of dimension
n-m
.
This implies that the projection with center
W
,
\pi_{W}:\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}\dasharrow V
, restricts to a dominant rational map

\pi:Y\dasharrow Z
. If
P_{z_{i}}=\langle W,z_{i}\rangle
,
1\leq i\leq 2
, are the closures of two general fibers of
\pi
, then

P_{z_{1}}\cap P_{z_{2}}=\emptyset
.

Indeed, the first claim is clear, while the second follows by Terracini’s Lemma because
Y
is not secant-defective and so
two general tangent spaces do not intersect.
These varieties are called
Verra varieties
.

There is an alternative definition of these varieties. Let
Z
be as above.
Consider a rational map
f:Z\dasharrow\mathbb{G}(n-m-1,W)
such that,
if
z_{1},z_{2}\in Z
are general points, then
f(z_{1}),f(z_{2})
are skew
linear subspaces of
W
. Set

Y:=\overline{\bigcup_{x\in Z_{0}}\langle x,f(x)\rangle}

where
Z_{0}\subset Z
is the open subset where
f
is defined and the above property holds. Then
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
is a Verra
variety.

Starting with

2.4.
Classification of irreducible surfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
with
\tau=2

The aim of this subsection is to prove the following classification result.

Theorem 19
.

Let
X^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be an irreducible surface such that
\tau(X)=2
.
Then
X
is a scroll over a rational curve.

Moreover, if
X
is also smooth, then it is projectively equivalent to
S(1,2)\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
.

To this aim we shall briefly recall the theory of second order foci in our particular setting,
as outlined by C. Segre in
[
22
]
. Segre applied this theory to study congruences of planes in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
of order two, i.e.
two-dimensional families of planes in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
as in (
1
) such that
\deg(q)=2
(see the final pages of
[
22
]
).
The first remark by Segre is completely general. Suppose we have a diagram
as in (
1
) and there exists a smooth point
q(r)\in q(\mathcal{V})
such that
\operatorname{length}(q^{-1}(q(r)))>\deg(q)
. Then
r
(or
q(r)
)
is a fundamental point for the family of linear spaces (see
[
4
, Lemma 1.7]
for a proof similar
to Segre’s original argument or use the general fact that a morphism
with finite fibers onto a non-singular variety is flat).

Segre’s idea for studying families of linear spaces as in (
1
), with
N=n+r
and
\deg(q)=2
, or subvarieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}

with
\tau(X)=2
, is to use foci
r\in\mathcal{V}
to produce
a subscheme of
q^{-1}(q(r))
supported at
r
and of length at least three by means of the differential of the map
q
at
r

(for a focus we have
\operatorname{length}_{r}q^{-1}(q(r))\geq 2
by definition).

So, following Segre, one looks for a
second-order infinitely near space at r
as a
focus of the family of
foci
, i.e. an infinitely near variety of foci to the variety of foci. If this occurs, we have finally constructed
a second-order focus
(see
[
22
]
).

Example 20
.

Let us see some examples that illustrate
how the definition of higher-order foci should be introduced under suitable hypothesis like smoothness of
the focal loci or of its support, etc. If
F_{v}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}_{v}
is the focal locus of the family (
1
), we shall indicate by

the locus of second-order foci of the family (
1
), i.e. the locus where
q
ramifies with order at least three in some direction.

Let

,
n\geq 2
and

, be the

-osculating scroll to an irreducible non-degenerate

. The foci of the family of

-osculating spaces on a general osculating space

is the

T^{(n-2)}_{p}C.
If

, the foci of the family of
(n-2)
-osculating spaces on a general osculating space

is the

T^{(n-3)}_{p}C
and so on.
Thus, if

,

,

, consists of foci of the
(s-1)
-order of the family of linear spaces

’s. In particular, for

.

Let

with

Let

We now concentrate on the case of the family of tangent planes to a surface in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.
Let us recall that in this case, for

Lemma 21
.

Let

be a non-degenerate irreducible surface such that

. If

Proof.

Let

be the image via

given by the closure of the lines
L_{x}^{i}\subseteq F^{2}_{x}
,

q_{|\mathcal{F}_{i}}:\mathcal{F}_{i}\to F_{i}

. From

we deduce that

. Then

∎

Lemma 22
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be a non-degenerate irreducible surface such that
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{4}
. Then
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is contained in the locus of second order foci of the family of tangent spaces.

Proof.

Let us fix a general

, while in the second case

In the first case, we can separate analytically the two directions in a neighbourhood of

and define via the family of tangent lines

of

be the analytic curve passing through

and having the previous prescribed tangent directions at every point

By construction

is developable because at each

the infinitesimal near plane

cuts

along
L^{1}_{p}
, respectively

.

If

an irreducible curve, then

, respectively

, is a locus of fundamental points.
If

or if
Y_{D_{x}}=T^{(2)}\widetilde{C}_{x}
, then clearly

so that

is a locus of second-order foci but

(or

) is not. Anyway, a general

is a second order focus, as claimed.
∎

(of Theorem

. Since every
x\in X
is fundamental, we have that
\dim(G)=3
and that
G
dominates
X
via
p:T_{X}\to X
.
The general fiber of
q_{|G}:G\rightarrow X
, denoted by
G_{x}
, has pure dimension one and is contained in the focal locus. Hence, for

x\in X
general, there exists at least one irreducible component of the focal conic
C_{x}
contained in
G_{x}
. So we can apply Lemma
21
to deduce that
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is a scroll over a curve, which is rational by Lemma
11
.

Finally observe that a smooth scroll
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is linearly normal and, being rational, it is necessarily isomorphic to
S(1,2)
.
∎

Remark 23
.

If
n\geq 3
, the previous count of parameters does not work and we constructed examples of smooth irreducible varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n}
with
\tau(X)=2
that are not scrolls over a rational curve (see Examples
12
,
17
and
18
).

3.
A lower bound for the degree of the tangent variety when
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)

Suppose that
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a non-degenerate variety such that
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Let us recall that if
X
is also smooth, then either
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\operatorname{Sec}(X)
or
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2n
and
\dim(\operatorname{Sec}(X))=2n+1
by Zak’s Theorem on tangencies (see for example
[
20
]
). We are looking for an effective lower bound on the degree of
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
and then try to classify varieties attaining the bound in the same spirit of the results in
[
6
]
for
\operatorname{Sec}(X)
. If
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
, then a lower bound on the degree follows from the results in
loc. cit.

Theorem 24

(
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

Let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a non-degenerate variety such that
\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Then

\deg(\operatorname{Sec}(X))\geq\binom{\operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(\operatorname{Sec}(X))+2}{2}.

The following result is elementary but it will be the starting point for our induction argument leading to an optimal lower bound for
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))
in terms of its codimension.

Proposition 25
.

Let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a non-degenerate variety with
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
.
Then
deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 3
. Moreover, if
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=3
, then
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\operatorname{Sec}(X)
is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

In particular, if
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)
, then
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 4
.

Proof.

Since
X
is non-degenerate and since
X\subseteq\operatorname{Sing}(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\subsetneq\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
, we have
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 3
because the singular locus of a quadric hypersurface is a linear subspace. Suppose
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=3
. Since
3=\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq\operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(\operatorname{Tan}(X))+1
, we have that either
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
is a cubic hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
or
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
is a variety of minimal degree of codimension
2
in
\mathbb{P}^{N},
that is a cubic rational normal scroll. Since the singular locus of a rational normal scroll is a linear space, the second case cannot occur. In the former case, we claim that
\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Tan}(X)
, yielding
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\operatorname{Sec}(X)
. Indeed. If
L
is a general secant line to
X
then
L\cap X=\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
with
p_{1},p_{2}\in X
. Since
p_{i}\in\operatorname{Sing}(\operatorname{Tan}(X))
, we have
\operatorname{mult}_{p_{i}}(L\cap\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 2
so Bézout’s Theorem yields
L\subset\operatorname{Tan}(X)
, proving the claim.
∎

From now on we shall concentrate exclusively on the case
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

Remark 26
.

Given a variety
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
and a point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
, we denote by
Y_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
the projection of
Y
from
p
. More generally, if
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a linear subspace such that
X\not\subseteq L
, we denote the projection of
X
from
L
by
X_{L}
. The following are well-known general facts.

If
p\not\in\operatorname{Vert}(Y)
, then
\dim(Y_{p})=\dim(Y)
. If

p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus\operatorname{Vert}(Y)
and if

\pi_{p|Y}:Y\dasharrow Y_{p}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-1}

is the projection from
p
restricted to
Y
, then

(18)

\deg(Y)=\deg(\pi_{p|Y})\cdot\deg(Y_{p})+\operatorname{mult}_{p}Y.

For an arbitrary
p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
we have
\operatorname{Tan}(Y)_{p}=\operatorname{Tan}(Y_{p})
and
\operatorname{Sec}(Y)_{p}=\operatorname{Sec}(Y_{p})
.

In particular, if
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a variety such that
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
and if
p\not\in\operatorname{Vert}(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\cup\operatorname{Vert}(\operatorname{Sec}(X))
, then
\operatorname{Tan}(X_{p})\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X_{p})
. If
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)=\mathbb{P}^{N}
, if
\operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 2
and if
p\not\in\operatorname{Vert}(\operatorname{Tan}(X))
, then
\operatorname{Tan}(X_{p})\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N-1}=\operatorname{Sec}(X_{p})
. So if
\operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 2
and if
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)
, then
\operatorname{Tan}(X_{p})\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X_{p})
for
p\in X
general.

Finally, let us remark that if
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
is a non-degenerate variety such that
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subset\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
is a hypersurface of degree four (the minimal possible degree if
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)
), then

\tau(X_{p})=(4-\operatorname{mult}_{p}\operatorname{Tan}(X))\cdot\tau(X)
as we already remarked in the proof of Lemma
16
. Under the previous hypothesis
\tau(X)=1
implies
\operatorname{mult}_{p}\operatorname{Tan}(X)=2
for
p\in X
general, see
loc. cit.

We are finally ready to provide an effective lower bound on
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))
when
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)
and to generalise some of the previous remarks.

Theorem 27
.

Let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a non-degenerate variety such that
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)
and let
p\in X
be a general point.
Then

\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 2(N-\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))+1).

Moreover, if equality holds and if
N-\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 2
, then
\operatorname{mult}_{p}\operatorname{Tan}(X)=2
,
\deg(\pi_{p|\operatorname{Tan}(X)})=1

and
\tau(X)=\tau(X_{p})
.

Proof.

If
c=N-\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=1
, then
2(N-\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))+1)=4
. Proposition
25

assures
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\geq 4
under our hypothesis, proving the assertion in this case. From now on we suppose
c\geq 2
.
By Remark
26
we can proceed by induction on
c\geq 2
and apply the induction hypothesis
to
X_{p}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
with
p\in X
general, yielding

\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=\deg(\pi_{p|\operatorname{Tan}(X)})\cdot\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X_{p}))+\operatorname{mult}_{p}\operatorname{Tan}(X)\geq 2(c-1+1)+2=2(c+1)

and concluding the proof of the first part.

If
c\geq 2
and if
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2(c+1)
, then
\operatorname{mult}_{p}\operatorname{Tan}(X)=2
and
\deg(\pi_{p|\operatorname{Tan}(X)})=1
as claimed.
Since
\deg(\pi_{p|\operatorname{Tan}(X)})=1,
we have
\tau(X)=\tau(X_{p})
.
∎

Remark 28
.

The result is sharp. Indeed, the quadratic Veronese surface
\nu_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{2})\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
and the Segre fourfold
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{8}
are examples of smooth varieties for which
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a cubic hypersurfaces.

We now show that there exist examples of (smooth) irreducible varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
such that
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)
and with
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2(N-\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))+1)
. If the varieties are smooth, then necessarily
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2n
and
N\geq 2n+1
.

Example 29
.

Let

S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\subset\mathbb{P}^{N},

N\geq 2n+1
, be a smooth rational normal scroll. Recall that
a_{i}>0
for every
i
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=N-n+1

and
\deg(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=N-n+1
.
Moreover, we have

\dim(\operatorname{Sec}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})))=2n+1

and

(19)

\deg(\operatorname{Sec}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})))=\binom{\operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(\operatorname{Sec}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})))+2}{2}=\binom{N-2n+1}{2}

(see for example
[
6
, §5]
).

Then
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})))=2n
and we claim that

\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})))=2(N-2n+1).

We shall use the following elementary remark. A linear system of hypersurfaces of degree
d
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
of dimension
M
such that the Koszul syzygies of a base are generated by linear ones defines a rational map
\phi:\mathbb{P}^{N}\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{s}
with linear fibers, i.e. the closure of each fiber of
\phi
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
(see
[
25
]
).

This implies that a variety
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
defined by quadratic equations whose first syzygies are generated by linear ones is a
QEL
-variety (the associated map
\phi
contracts the secant and tangent lines passing through a point of
\operatorname{Sec}(X)
). In particular, such a variety with
\dim(\operatorname{Sec}(X))=2n+1
has the property that through a general point of
\operatorname{Sec}(X)
, respectively of
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
, there passes a unique secant line to
X
(i.e.
\mu(X)=1
), respectively a unique tangent line to
X
(i.e.
\tau(X)=1
). Therefore
\sigma(X)=\deg(\operatorname{Sec}(X))
and
\omega_{n}=\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))
for this class of varieties.

We apply Severi’s Double Point Formula (
15
) and (
19
) to
deduce:

(20)

(N-2n+1)\cdot(N-2n)=2\cdot\deg(\operatorname{Sec}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=(N-n+1)(N-n)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}.

If
n=1
and if
S(a_{1})\subset\mathbb{P}^{a_{1}}
is a rational normal curve of degree
a_{1}
, then

\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(S(a_{1})))=\omega_{1}(S(a_{1}))=a_{1}\cdot(a_{1}-1)-2\cdot\deg(S(a_{1}))=2\cdot a_{1}-2=2(a_{1}-2+1)

by the previous formula (or directly using (
6
):
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(S(a_{1})))=\deg(c_{1}(\mathcal{P}_{S(a_{1})}))=2d-2
). Since a general linear section of a smooth rational normal scroll is a rational normal scroll of the same degree
and since rational normal scrolls have homogeneous ideal generated by quadratic polynomials with a linear resolution,
we can proceed by induction and, letting
X^{n-1}
be a linear section of
X=S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, show that for every
i=1,\ldots,n-1
:

\omega_{i}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=\omega_{n-i}(X^{n-i})=2(N-i-2(n-i)+1)=2(N-2n+1)+2\cdot i,

and so

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\omega_{i}=2\cdot(n-1)\cdot(N-2n+1)+n(n-1).

Since

(N-n+1)(N-n)-(N-2n+1)(N-2n)=2n(N-2n)+n(n+1)=2n(N-2n+1)+n(n-1),

we get

\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})))=2n(N-2n+1)+n(n-1)-2(n-1)(N-2n+1)-n(n-1)

=2(N-2n+1),

as claimed.

On the contrary, since
\tau(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=1=\mu(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))
for any smooth rational normal scroll as above, one can
compute
\omega_{i}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))
via Chern/Segre classes of
\mathcal{P}_{S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}
, which do not depend
on the
a_{i}
’s but only on
a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}=N-n+1
. Let
S_{n-i}=S(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-i})\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-i}
,
i=0,\ldots,n-1
with
b_{j}>0
and
with
b_{1}+\ldots+b_{n-i}=N-n+1
.
Then, for every
i=0,\ldots,n-1
,
we have

\omega_{n-i}(S_{n-i})=\deg(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}_{S_{n-i}}}(1)^{2(n-i)})=2(N-n+1-n+i).

Using Severi’s Double Point Formula (
15
) we have

2\deg(\operatorname{Sec}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})))=(N-n+1)(N-n)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})),

getting the formula in (
19
) for
a_{i}>0
and
a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}=N-n
by going backwards
in the previous computations.

Suppose now
m\geq 1
and
a_{i}>0
as above and consider

X_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},m}:=S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{m})\subset\mathbb{P}^{N+m}.

This is a singular rational normal scroll of dimension
n+m
with

\operatorname{Vert}(X^{n+m})=\operatorname{Sing}(X^{n+m})=L=\mathbb{P}^{m-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+m}

and having degree

\deg(X_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},m})=N-n+1=N+m-(n+m)+1
. Then:

X_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},m}=S(L,S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}));

\operatorname{Sec}(X_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},m})=S(L,\operatorname{Sec}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})));

\operatorname{Tan}(X_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},m})=S(L,\operatorname{Tan}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))).

In particular, we get
\dim(S(X_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},m}))=2n+m+1
,
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},m})=2n+m
and

\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},m}))=2(N-2n+1)=2(N+m-\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},m}))+1).

In conclusion, all the rational normal scrolls as above are varieties of
minimal tangential degree
with
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)
, showing that the lower bound is sharp.

Corollary 30
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, be an irreducible non-degenerate curve
such that
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2(N-\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))+1)
. Then
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a rational normal curve of degree
N
.

Proof.

Since
N\geq 3
and since
X
is non-degenerate, then
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2
and
\dim(\operatorname{Sec}(X))=3
. Moreover, since
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
is ruled
by the tangent lines (they are general fibers of the Gauss map of the surface
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
) we deduce
\tau(X)=1
. Then the projection of
X

from
N-2
general points in it is an irreducible curve
\widetilde{X}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
with
\tau(\widetilde{X})=2
,
i.e. it is a smooth conic. Then
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is an irreducible rational curve of degree
N
and
hence a smooth rational curve of degree
N
.
∎

In the next section we shall construct examples of smooth irreducible varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
n\geq 2

of minimal tangent degree having
\dim(\operatorname{Sec}(X))=2n+1
and
\tau(X)
arbitrary large, which are not rational
normal scrolls.

The relation between
X
and
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
is not so strict in the sense that there might exist varieties
Y\supset X
such
that
\operatorname{Tan}(Y)=\operatorname{Tan}(X)
. For example taking other subvarieties of
Y
, we may produce infinitely many varieties
with the same tangent variety. If this occurs, then
\operatorname{Sing}(\operatorname{Tan}(X))\supseteq Y\supsetneq X
. Let us generalize
the constructions that already appeared in Examples
18
.

Example 31
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
N\geq 2n+1
and
Y^{n+1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a non-degenerate
variety such that
\widetilde{Y}=\overline{\mathcal{G}_{Y}(Y)}\subset\mathbb{G}(n+1,N)
has dimension
n-1
, i.e.
Y
is a developable scroll
in planes. For
\widetilde{y}\in\widetilde{Y}
general, let
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\widetilde{y}}:=\overline{\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{-1}(\widetilde{y})}
be a general fiber.
Let
X^{n}\subset Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a non-degenerate variety of dimension
n
, not contained in
\operatorname{Sing}(Y)
, not developable
and such that it intersects the general plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\widetilde{y}}

along a curve
C_{\widetilde{y}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\widetilde{y}}
of degree
b\geq 1
. Observe that the last condition is equivalent to
\overline{\mathcal{G}_{Y}(X)}=\widetilde{Y}
.

Let
T_{q}X
be a general tangent space to
X
. If
\widetilde{q}=\mathcal{G}_{Y}(q)
, then
q\in C_{\widetilde{q}}
and

\mathbb{P}^{n+1}=T_{q}Y\supset T_{q}X.

Since
T_{y_{1}}Y=T_{y_{2}}Y
for
y_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\widetilde{q}}
, we deduce that

(21)

\overline{\bigcup_{q\in U\subseteq C_{\widetilde{q}}}T_{q}X}\subseteq T_{y}Y=\mathbb{P}^{n+1}

with
y\in\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\widetilde{q}}
general and fixed and
U\subseteq C_{\widetilde{q}}
an open subset.

Since
X
is not developable and since
\widetilde{y}=\mathcal{G}_{Y}(y)=\widetilde{q}
is general, the tangent spaces
to
X
along
C_{\widetilde{y}}
are not constant. Hence equality holds in (
21
) and

C_{\widetilde{y}}^{\#}:=\overline{\bigcup_{u\in U\subseteq C_{\widetilde{y}}}[T_{u}X]}\subset(T_{y}Y)^{*}

is a curve of degree
\tau\geq 1
such that
through a general point of
T_{y}Y
there pass
\tau\geq 1
tangent spaces to
X
at smooth points of
X
in
C_{\widetilde{y}}
.
We have thus proved that
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\operatorname{Tan}(Y)
and that
\tau(X)\geq\tau\geq 1
. Moreover,
\tau(X)=1
if and only if
C_{\widetilde{y}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\widetilde{y}}
is a line. In particular,

2n=\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(Y))

and
W:=\operatorname{Tan}(Y)
has
a developable ruling by linear spaces of dimension
m\geq 3
birationally parametrized by
\widetilde{W}^{2n-m}\subset\mathbb{G}(m,N)
. Indeed, a general
\mathbb{P}^{m}_{\widetilde{w}}
of the ruling of
\operatorname{Tan}(Y)
cuts
Y
along
the plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\widetilde{y}}
and
X
along the plane curve
C_{\widetilde{y}}
. Hence, for
s\in T_{y}Y
general, the tangent space
T_{s}\operatorname{Tan}(Y)

is constant along
\langle s,\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\widetilde{y}}\rangle
because
T_{s}\operatorname{Tan}(Y)\supseteq T_{y}Y
and
T_{y}Y
is constant along
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\widetilde{y}}
.
If
n=2
, then necessarily
m=3
and
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a developable scroll in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
’s over
\widetilde{Y}
.

Let us focus on the following special case. Let
Z^{n-1}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
be a degenerate variety
spanning a linear space
V
of dimension
N-2
such that
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(Z))=2n-2
and let

L\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a line such that
V\cap L=\emptyset
.
Then the cone
Y^{n+1}:=S(L,Z)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a developable scroll in planes with
\widetilde{Y}
birational to
Z
.
In other words, the projection from
L
onto
Z
can be identified with
\mathcal{G}_{Y}
and
Z
is birational to
\widetilde{Y}
.
In this case
\operatorname{Tan}(Y)=S(L,\operatorname{Tan}(Z))
also holds and
m=2n+1-\dim(\widetilde{\operatorname{Tan}(Z)})\geq 3
.

We claim that
\tau(Z)=1
implies

(22)

\tau(X)=\tau.

Indeed, if
q\in\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\operatorname{Tan}(Y)=S(L,\operatorname{Tan}(Z))
is a general point,
q^{\prime}:=\pi_{L}(q)
and the tangent space to
X
at
x\in X_{\operatorname{reg}}
passes through

q
, then
\pi_{L}(x)
is a smooth point of
Z
and
\pi_{L}(T_{x}X)=T_{\pi_{L}(x)}Z
passes through

. Since

and since

is general,
we have
\pi_{L}(x)=z
for every such

. Hence

and

for

general, yielding

.

For
n=2
the variety
Y^{3}
is either

or

or

where

is an irreducible curve in
\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
,
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
or
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, respectively (see Example
2.3
).
As shown above we have
m=3
and the Gauss image
of
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\operatorname{Tan}(Y)
is birational to the Gauss image of

.

To construct a non-degenerate irreducible surface
X^{2}\subset Y^{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with

and

as above and with

not developable one can remark
that developable non-degenerate irreducible surfaces

. So if one fixes such a

4.
Varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
N\geq 2n+1
and
\tau(X)>1

In this section we consider the existence of smooth varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
N\geq 2n+1
, with
\tau(X)\geq 2

and the classification of non-degenerate irreducible surfaces
X^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
N\geq 5
, with
\tau(X)\geq 2
.

If
\tau(X)\geq 1
, then
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2n
. If
X
is smooth, then either
\operatorname{Sec}(X)=\operatorname{Tan}(X)
or
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
.
In the first case we know examples with
\tau(X)=2
such as
QEL
-manifolds of type
\delta=1
. There are smooth examples for
sporadic values of
n
, coming from particular secant-defective homogenous varieties (see the last part of Example
12
for the
description of some cases) and some series of linearly normal varieties
for arbitrary

, like projections of
\nu_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\subset\mathbb{P}^{\frac{n^{2}+3n}{2}}

from the linear span of
\nu_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{s})
with
\mathbb{P}^{s}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
a linear subspace of dimension
s
with
-1\leq s\leq n-2
.

The case
\operatorname{Tan}(X)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sec}(X)
is also interesting and deserves a particular attention for several reasons which will become clear in the sequel.
To construct smooth varieties
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2n
,
\dim(\operatorname{Sec}(X))=2n+1
and
\tau(X)\geq 2
, we look for smooth
varieties containing a
(n-1)
-dimensional
family of plane curves of degree at least two such that the union of the planes is a
Y^{n+1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
containing
X
and developable along
those planes. Indeed, one proceeds in the opposite direction and analyzes what are the restrictions imposed by the smoothness of
X
to be contained
in a developable scroll in planes. At least for
n=2
it is not difficult to
realize that the only possible candidates are the cones
Y^{n+1}=S(L,Z)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
Z^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
a non-degenerate variety (see Lemma
42
below).
Such varieties effectively exist
as firstly remarked by Roth in
[
19
, §3.5]
for

and then generalized by Ilic in
[
11
]
to arbitrary

.

We start with the following key remark (see also large parts of
[
11
]
), pointing out important connections with the topics previously discussed.

Lemma 32
.

Let
X^{n}\subset Y^{n+1}=S(L,Z)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a smooth non-degenerate variety with
Z^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}=\langle Z\rangle\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
a variety which is not a cone and with
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
a line such that
L\cap\langle Z\rangle=\emptyset
.
Then
L\subset X
and
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
is a rational scroll, which is the external projection of a smooth rational normal scroll in
\mathbb{P}^{M}
with
M\geq N-2
.

If
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is also linearly normal, then
Z=S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
is a smooth rational normal scroll,
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=S(L,\operatorname{Tan}(S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})))
and
\deg(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=2(N-2n+1)
.

Proof.

Since
X_{L}\subseteq Z
, we necessarily have
L\cap X\neq\emptyset
. Also, since

X\subsetneq S(L,X)\subseteq S(L,S(L,Z))=S(L,Z),

we deduce
\dim(S(L,X))=n+1
so that a general
T_{x}X
cuts
L
in a point by Terracini’s Lemma. Hence
\dim(X_{L})=n-1
, yielding
X_{L}=Z
. Suppose

L\cap X\subsetneq L
. Then the projection from
L
would be solved by blowing-up
X
in points of
X\cap L
, eventually infinitely near to one of the points
of intersection in
X\cap L
.
The last exceptional divisor
E_{i}=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
over a point in
X\cap L
would be sent into a linear
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\subseteq Z^{n-1}
, which is impossible because
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is non-degenerate (in this case
Y=S(L,Z)
would be a linear subspace of dimension
n+1
). This contradiction shows
that
L\subset X
.

If
E\subset\operatorname{Bl}_{L}X
is the exceptional divisor and if
\widetilde{\pi_{L}}:\operatorname{Bl}_{L}X\to Z
is the resolution of the projection
\pi_{L}
from
L
, then

Z=\widetilde{\pi_{L}}(E)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
. If
n=2
, then
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
is a rational curve being the image of
E=\mathbb{P}^{1}
by a non-constant morphism
of degree
b\geq 1
, where
b=\deg(\langle p,L\rangle\cap X)
for
p\in Z
general.

We shall prove that
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
is a rational scroll arguing by induction on
n\geq 3
(see also
[
11
, Propositions 4.12 and 4.13]
).
Then, letting
E=\mathbb{P}(N_{L/X}^{*}(1))
, the restriction of
\widetilde{\pi_{L}}
to
E
is given by a globally generated linear system in
|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(N_{L/X}^{*}(1))}(1)|
.
Suppose

(23)

N_{L/X}^{*}(1)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b_{i})

with
b_{i}\geq 0
. If
b_{i}=0
for some
i
, then
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
would be a cone, contrary to our assumption. Hence
b_{i}>0
for every
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
N_{L/X}^{*}(1)
is very ample.
In particular, if
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a general hyperplane through
L
, then
Y^{n-1}=X^{n}\cap H
is a smooth variety (see also
[
11
, Proposition 4.9]
), contains
L
and projects from
L
onto a general hyperplane section
Y_{L}^{n-2}
of
Z^{n-1}
. Now recall that a linearly normal variety whose general curve section is a rational curve
is either a quadric hypersurface, a rational normal scroll or a cone over a Veronese surface in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
by a classical result of Bertini and del Pezzo (see for example
[
8
]
). Then
Z^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
, which is not a cone by hypothesis and which contains linear spaces of dimension
n-2\geq 1
coming from
E
, is the external projection of a smooth rational normal scroll because its general hyperplane section
Y_{L}
is a rational scroll of dimension
n-2\geq 1
by induction.

Let
H\subset X
be a hyperplane section. If
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is linearly normal, then
\dim(|H-L|)=N-2
. Since
Z=X_{L}
, then
\widetilde{\pi_{L}}:\operatorname{Bl}_{L}X\to Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
is given by the complete linear system
|H-L|
and
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
is linearly normal, yielding that
Z=S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
is a smooth rational normal scroll.
∎

The previous result says that smooth varieties contained in developable scrolls in planes
Y^{n+1}
are
quite rare. For example, if
Y^{n+1}=S(L,Z)
with
Z^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
not a cone and different from an external projection of a smooth rational normal scroll, then any irreducible
X^{n}\subset Y^{n+1}
is necessarily singular.

The manifolds appearing in the last part of Lemma
32
share a notable geometric property, assuming they exist.

Lemma 33
.

Let
a_{i}>0
,
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, be such that
a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n-1}=N-n
, let
X^{n}\subset S(0,0,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})=Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a smooth
variety and let
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{z}:=\langle z,L\rangle
with
z\in S(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})
general and
L=\operatorname{Sing}(Y)
. If
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{z}\cap X=L\cup C_{z}
and if
C_{z}
is a smooth curve of degree

b\geq 1
, then
\tau(X)=b^{2}.

Proof.

The number
\tau
of tangent spaces to
X
at points of
C_{z}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{z}
passing through the general point of

\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{z}:=\bigcup_{u\in C_{z}}T_{u}X=\widetilde{q}(\mathcal{P}_{X|C_{z}})

is equal to
\tau(X)
by (
22
). Hence

\tau(X)=c_{1}(\mathcal{P}_{X|C_{z}})=(K_{X}+(n+1)H)\cdot C_{z}

by (
6
).

After blowing-up
L
, the strict transform
\widetilde{C}_{z}
of
C_{z}
on
\operatorname{Bl}_{L}X
is a smooth irreducible fiber of the resolution of indeterminacy
\widetilde{\pi_{L}}:\operatorname{Bl}_{L}X\to Z
of the rational map
\pi_{L}
. By the Adjunction Formula we get

K_{\operatorname{Bl}_{L}X}\cdot\widetilde{C}_{z}=2g(\widetilde{C}_{z})-2=b^{2}-3b.

Hence

K_{X}\cdot C_{z}=\pi^{*}(K_{X})\cdot\widetilde{C}_{z}=K_{\operatorname{Bl}_{L}X}\cdot\widetilde{C}_{z}-(n-2)(E\cdot\widetilde{C}_{z})=b^{2}-3b-(n-2)b=b^{2}-(n+1)b,

yielding

\tau(X)=(K_{X}+(n+1)H)\cdot C_{z}=b^{2}.

∎

In view of the above results we shall concentrate on the construction of smooth

X^{n}\subset S(0,0,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}

with
a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n-1}=N-n
and
a_{i}>0
. This has already been done by Ilic in
[
11
]
, who named these manifolds as
Roth varieties
.

Remark 34
.

Let notation be as above and let
L=\operatorname{Sing}(S(0,0,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}))
. Ilic put the condition
L\subset X
in the definition of Roth variety.
This is
a consequence of smoothness (and not of linear normality of
Z
), as shown in Lemma
32
(this has also been essentially proved in
[
11
]
).

In
[
19

Roth studied smooth surfaces

,

, contained in three dimensional rational normal cones, i.e.
X^{2}\subset S(0,a_{1},a_{2})\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
a_{i}>0
and
a_{1}+a_{2}=N-2
or
X^{2}\subset S(0,0,N-2)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}

A general hyperplane section of the above surfaces is a smooth curve of maximal genus (in the sense of the Castelnuovo bound for the genus of a curve; for this reason they are also called
surfaces of maximal genus
, see
[
19
, §3.5]
and
[
11
]
for a short summary of results on this and on the definition of

). The second case is special in the sense of moduli (see also
[

, Remark 5.7]
). The existence of smooth varieties in

with
a_{i}>0
and

is obvious (one can take a general hypersurface not passing through the vertex

Ilic proved the following characterization of Roth varieties. We formulate it in a slightly modified form due to the previous remarks.

Theorem 35

(
[
11
, Theorem 3.8]
)
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let

S=S(0,0,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}

with
a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n-1}=N-n
and
a_{i}>0
.

For every
b\geq 1
there exists a Roth variety
X^{n}\subset S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
of degree
b(N-n)+1
, whose class in the Weil group
of divisors of
S
is
bH+F
, with
b\geq 1
,
H
the class of a hyperplane section of
S
and
F
the class of a
\mathbb{P}^{n}\subset S
.

Conversely, if
X^{n}\subset S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a smooth manifold of degree
d
, then
b=\frac{d-1}{N-n}
is an integer and
the class of
X
as a Weil divisor on

bH+F
. In particular, Roth varieties are linearly normal.

Remark 36
.

For an arbitrary smooth projective variety
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
of degree
d
the linear system

|(d-n-2)H-K_{X}|
is base-point free as pointed out by Mumford (see the introduction of
[
11
]

for a discussion of this property).

By (
23
) and by the arguments in the proof of Lemma
32
, for a Roth variety

X^{n}\subset S(L,Z)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
of degree

we have

\deg(N^{*}_{L/X})+(n-1)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b_{i}=b\cdot\deg(Z)=b\cdot(N-n),

yielding

K_{X}\cdot L=\deg(N^{*}_{L/X})-2=b(N-n)-n-1

and

((d-n-2)H-K_{X})\cdot L=0.

The non-ampleness of

characterizes (isomorphic projections of) Roth
varieties by the main result of
[
11
]
.

Remark 37
.

For a Roth variety of degree

a general plane curve
C_{z}=\langle L,z\rangle\cap X
is smooth and has degree
b\geq 1
.

Let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
,

, be a Roth variety of dimension

. Then through a general point

there pass

b^{2}
secant lines. Indeed, if
p\in\langle p_{1},p_{2}\rangle
,
p_{i}\in X
, and if
C_{z_{i}}\subset X
are the plane curves of degree

passing through
p_{i}
, then

\langle C_{z_{1}},C_{z_{2}}\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{3}_{p}.

Hence through

there pass

secant lines to

by Bézout’s Theorem. Conversely, all secant lines to

passing through

project from

onto a secant line
to

passing through

. Since through

there passes a unique secant line

to

,
then every secant line to

passing through

is contained in

and hence it is one of the secant lines described above. The linear space

is

although

When

tends to the general point

becomes a tangent line to

at
p_{1}
and the curve

degenerates into a double structure on

. The line

becomes a tangent line

to

at

and

tangent spaces passing through a general point

normal directions to the double structure

passing through

.

When

or when

one can construct (singular) irreducible surfaces

,

, with
\mu(X)=1
and

and also smooth irreducible surfaces with

(see also
[

).

We now proceed to the classification of non-degenerate irreducible surfaces
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
\tau(X)\geq 2
and with

. We start with a general remark.

Proposition 38
.

Let
N\geq 2n+1
and let
X^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a non-degenerate variety such that
\tau(X)\geq 2
. Let

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho(X)}_{w}=\overline{\mathcal{G}_{\operatorname{Tan}(X)}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\operatorname{Tan}(X)}(w))}

with
w\in\operatorname{Tan}(X)
general. Then
\varrho(X)\geq 2
and
\mathbb{P}^{\varrho(X)}_{w}\cap X
contains an equidimensional curve
C_{w}
and
T_{w}\operatorname{Tan}(X)
is tangent to
X
along
C_{w}\cap X_{\operatorname{reg}}
.

Proof.

Let
w\in\operatorname{Tan}(X)
be general. By hypothesis, there exist
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{\tau(X)}\in X_{\operatorname{reg}}
such that
w\in T_{x_{i}}X
and
T_{x_{i}}X\neq T_{x_{j}}X
for all
i\neq j
. Also, we can assume
\langle w,x_{i}\rangle\cap\langle w,x_{j}\rangle=\{w\}
since a general projective tangent space to
X
is not bitangent by the non developability of
X
. We have
T_{w}\operatorname{Tan}(X)=T_{z}\operatorname{Tan}(X)
for
z\in\langle w,x_{i}\rangle\cap\operatorname{Tan}(X)_{\operatorname{reg}}
by
Proposition
6
. Hence,
\langle w,x_{i},x_{j}\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{\varrho(X)}_{w}
, yielding
\varrho(X)\geq 2
.

Let
U_{w}\subset\operatorname{Tan}(X)_{\operatorname{reg}}
be an open analytic ball centered at
w
such that

q_{|q^{-1}(U_{w})}:q^{-1}(U_{w})\rightarrow U_{w}

is a holomorphic
\tau(X)
-covering map. Also, let
q^{-1}(U_{w})=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\tau}U_{w}^{i}
, where
U_{w}^{i}\subseteq T_{X}
is an open analytic neighborhood of
(x_{i},w)
isomorphic to
U_{w}
via
q
. We can shrink
U_{w}
as much as necessary so that
x_{i}\notin U_{w}
for every
i
. Lastly, define
C_{w}^{i}:=q_{|U_{w}^{i}}^{-1}(\langle w,x_{i}\rangle\cap U_{w})\subset U_{w}^{i}
.

Fix
i\in\{1,2,\dots,\tau(X)\}
. Let
z\in\langle w,x_{i}\rangle\cap U_{w}
be general and let
x\in X_{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus x_{i}
be such that
(x,z)\in C_{w}^{j}
for some
j\neq i
, which in particular implies
z\in T_{x}X
.
Then
\overline{p(C_{w}^{j})}\subset X
(Zariski closure) is a curve passing through
x_{j}
and
x
and we let

C_{w}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\tau(X)}\overline{p(C_{w}^{j})}\subset X.

We claim that this curve is contained in
\mathbb{P}^{\varrho(X)}_{w}
.
Indeed if
i\in\{1,\ldots,\tau(X)\}
is fixed, if
z\in\langle w,x_{i}\rangle
is general, then
\langle z,x\rangle\cap\operatorname{Tan}(X)_{\operatorname{reg}}\neq\emptyset
by the generality of
w
and
\langle z,x\rangle\subset T_{x}X
by definition of
C_{w}^{i}
. So, for
y\in\langle z,x\rangle\cap\operatorname{Tan}(X)_{\operatorname{reg}}
general, we have

T_{y}\operatorname{Tan}(X)=T_{z}\operatorname{Tan}(X)=T_{w}\operatorname{Tan}(X),

yielding
y\in\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{w}
. Hence
\langle z,x\rangle\subset\mathbb{P}^{\varrho(X)}_{w}
and
C_{w}\subset\mathbb{P}^{\varrho(X)}_{w}
by the generality of
x
, proving the claim. Let notation be as above. If
x\in C_{w}\cap X_{\operatorname{reg}}
, then
T_{w}\operatorname{Tan}(X)=T_{z}\operatorname{Tan}(X)\supset T_{x}X
by the last part of Proposition
6
, concluding the proof.
∎

Let us remark that that by monodromy of the covering we have that either
C_{w}
is irreducible or there exist

\tau(X)\geq 2
distinct irreducible components of
C_{w}
. Moreover,
\deg(C_{w})\geq 2
holds also
in the irreducible case because
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is not a linear subspace.

Suppose now
N\geq 5
and that
X^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is an irreducible surface with
\tau(X)\geq 2
. Since
\dim(\operatorname{Tan}(X))=4
and since
\varrho(X)\geq 2
we only have two possibilities:
either
\varrho(X)=2
or
\varrho(X)=3
. We treat the two cases separately.

Lemma 39
.

Let
N\geq 5
and let
X^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a non-degenerate irreducible surface such that
\tau(X)\geq 2
and
\varrho(X)=2
. Then
N=5
,

X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
is projectively equivalent to the Veronese surface
\nu_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{2})\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
and
\tau(X)=2
.

Proof.

Let
w\in\operatorname{Tan}(X)
be general and let
C_{w}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{w}\cap X
be the curve constructed in Proposition
38
. The curves
C_{w}\subset X
vary in a two dimensional family
\mathcal{C}
birationally parametrized by the Gauss image of
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
.
These curves cover
X
and through two general points
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
there passes at least one plane curve

, yielding
\deg(C)\geq 2
.
The general secant line
L=\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle
to

is not trisecant so that

, showing that the curves

are conics.
Through a general point

there pass infinitely many secant lines to
X
, yielding
\dim(\operatorname{Sec}(X))\leq 4
. Therefore
\operatorname{Tan}(X)=\operatorname{Sec}(X)\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{N}
, a general point

is also a general point of
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
and the entry locus

is a conic (see for example
[

, Theorem 1.1]
), which coincides with

. Since
\tau(X)\geq 2
, the conic

is irreducible.
A classical result by C. Segre assures that a non-degenerate irreducible surface

with

and containing a

is projectively equivalent to a Veronese surface in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
(see for example the proof of
[

, Theorem 3.4.1]
).
∎

’s over a curve birational to

t\in W
and let

C_{t}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t}\cap X
be the curve constructed in Proposition
38
.

The locus of foci in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t}
of the family of developable
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t}
’s is
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}:=\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t}\cap(\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t})^{\prime}
. Since
\operatorname{Tan}(X)
is not a cone with vertex a fixed plane,
the
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
’s move and describe a variety

Y^{3}=\overline{\bigcup_{t\in U\subseteq W}\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}}

of dimension three, which is an irreducible component of
\operatorname{Sing}(\operatorname{Tan}(X))
. Also the ruling of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
’s is developable and such that
T_{q}Y=\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t}
for
q\in\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
general. In particular
\operatorname{Tan}(Y)=\operatorname{Tan}(X)
.

From the parametrization of
T_{z}\operatorname{Tan}(X)
for
z\in T_{x}X
general in (
8
), we deduce that
T^{(2)}_{x}X=T_{z}\operatorname{Tan}(X)
for
z\in\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t}
general and
x\in C_{t}
general. In particular
T_{x}^{(2)}X
is constant along
C_{t}
and equal to
\mathbb{P}^{4}_{t}:=T_{y}^{(2)}Y
with
y\in\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
general. Let
\mathcal{G}_{X}^{(2)}:X\dasharrow\mathbb{G}(4,N)
be the second Gauss map of
X
, associating to a general
x\in X
the point in
\mathbb{G}(4,N)
corresponding to
T_{x}^{(2)}X
. Then
\overline{G_{X}^{(2)}(X)}=W
and a dense open subset of
C_{t}
is contained in the fiber of
G_{X}^{(2)}
over
t
, which is smooth by generic smoothness. We claim that
C_{t}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
.

Without loss of generality we can suppose
N=5
. By abusing notation we shall also indicate by
t
a local parameter at a general point of the curve
W
. By the generality of
t\in W
and of
x\in C_{t}
, we have that
t
is a local equation of
C_{t}
at
x
because it coincides with the fiber of
G^{(2)}_{X}
through
x.
The hyperplane
\mathbb{P}^{4}_{t}=T^{(2)}_{x}X
cuts
X

near
x
along the divisor
3\cdot C_{t}
, which is locally defined at
x
by the pull-back of the local equation
t^{3}
. Then the infinitesimally near
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t}=\mathbb{P}^{4}_{t}\cap(\mathbb{P}^{4}_{t})^{\prime}
cuts
X
near
x
along the divisor defined by
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}t^{3}
, i.e. along an open subset of the divisor
2\cdot C_{t}
containing
x
. Finally,
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}=\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t}\cap(\mathbb{P}^{3}_{t})^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}^{4}_{t}\cap(\mathbb{P}^{4}_{t})^{\prime}\cap(\mathbb{P}^{4}_{t})^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
cuts
X
near
x
along the divisor defined by
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}t^{3}
, i.e. along an open subset of
C_{t}
containing
x
. The generality of
x
assures that a general point
in each irreducible component of
C_{t}
has a neighbourhood contained in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
, proving the claim and thus concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 42
.

Let
N\geq 5
and let
X^{2}\subset Y^{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a smooth linearly normal non-degenerate irreducible surface with
Y^{3}
a developable scroll in planes. Then

Y=S(0,0,N-2)
and
X^{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a Roth surface of degree
d=b(N-2)+1
with
b\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
Y^{3}=\operatorname{Tan}^{(2)}C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
a non-degenerate irreducible curve. Let
\overline{C}
be the normalization of
C
, let
\mathcal{L}
be the pull-back of
\mathcal{O}_{C}(1)
to
\overline{C}
, let
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L})
be the sheaf of principal parts of
\mathcal{L}
on
\overline{C}
and let
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{L})
be the sheaf of second order parts of
\mathcal{L}
on
\overline{C}
. The latter two are locally free sheaves of ranks two and three. The bundle
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L})
can also be defined via the extension:

0\to\Omega_{\overline{C}}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{L}\to 0

corresponding to
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\in H^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\overline{C}})
. There exists also
a surjective homomorphism

\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{P}(L),

yielding an embedding
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}(L))\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{L}))
. This is just the abstract realization of
\operatorname{Tan}(C)\subset\operatorname{Tan}^{(2)}C
.

If
\mathbb{P}^{N}=\mathbb{P}(V)
, the surjection
V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\overline{C}}\to\mathcal{L}
induced by pull-back of sections
yields surjections of
V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\overline{C}}
onto
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L})
and
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{L})
. The latter one
defines a morphism
g:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{L}))\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
, whose image is
\operatorname{Tan}^{(2)}C
. The morphism
g
is a bijection between the fibers
over a general point of
\overline{C}
and the corresponding osculating plane to
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
. The morphism
g
ramifies along
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}(L))
, whose image via
g
is
\operatorname{Tan}(C)
,
and
\operatorname{Tan}^{(2)}C
has cuspidal singularities along
\operatorname{Tan}(C)
. Indeed, letting
\pi:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}^{2}(L))\to\overline{C}
be the structural morphism, the reinterpretation of the analysis in §
2.3
is that, taking a point
r\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{t}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
,
t\in C
general, the image of
(dg)_{r}:t_{r}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{L}))\to t_{g(r)}\mathbb{P}^{N}
is
t_{g(r)}\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
and the kernel of
(dg)_{r}
maps isomorphically onto
t_{\pi(r)}\overline{C}
.

Any smooth
\overline{X}\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{L}))
, different from a
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
from the ruling, dominates
\overline{C}
,
intersects
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L}))
in a curve
D
and intersects
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
in a smooth plane curve
A_{t}
with
t\in C
general.
Let
r\in A_{t}\cap\mathbb{P}^{1}_{t}
,
t\in C
general (i.e.
r\in D
general). Then
t_{r}\overline{X}\cap t_{r}\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}=t_{r}A_{t}\subset t_{r}\mathbb{P}^{2}_{t}
so that
t_{r}\overline{X}
has one dimensional
image via
(dg)_{r}
and
g(\overline{X})
will be singular at
g(r)
.
The case
Y=S(p,\operatorname{Tan}(C))
is similar and left to the reader.

Suppose
X\subset Y=S(L,C)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
a non-degenerate irreducible curve in its linear span and with

L\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
a line disjoint from
\langle C\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
. From Lemma
32
we deduce that
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a rational normal
curve, that
Y=S(0,0,N-2)
and that
X
is a Roth surface. These surfaces exist by Theorem
35
.
∎

Corollary 45
.

Let

and let
