Extreme and exposed points of shift-invariant spaces generated by Gaussian kernel and hyperbolic secant Thanks: Hagen, Zelent, and Zlotnikov were supported by Grant 334466 of the Research Council of Norway.
Markus Valås Hagen, Alexander Ulanovskii, Denis Zelent, Ilya Zlotnikov

Abstract
. We characterize the extreme and exposed points of the unit ball (with respect to the L 1 L^{1} -norm) in the shift-invariant space generated by the Gaussian function, as well as in the quasi shift-invariant space generated by the hyperbolic secant.



1.
Introduction and Main Results

1.1.
Extreme and exposed points

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
C\subset X
be a closed convex set. An element
f\in C
is called an extreme point of
C
if it does not lie in the interior of any nontrivial line segment contained in
C
; that is, there do not exist distinct points
f_{1},f_{2}\in C
and a scalar
0<\alpha<1
such that
f=\alpha f_{1}+(1-\alpha)f_{2}
. Among the extreme points, an element
f\in C
is said to be an exposed point of
C
if there exists a continuous linear functional
\varphi\in X^{\ast}
such that

\varphi(f)>\varphi(g)\quad\text{for all }g\in C\setminus\{f\}.

Equivalently,
\varphi
attains its maximum over
C
uniquely at
f
.

Let

B(X)=\{f\in X:\|f\|\leq 1\},\qquad S(X)=\{f\in X:\|f\|=1\}

denote the unit ball and the unit sphere of
X
.
We denote the sets of extreme and exposed points of
B(X)
by

{\rm Ext}(X)
and
\,{\rm Exp}(X),

respectively. Note that
\,{\rm Exp}(X)\subset{\rm Ext}(X)\subset S(X)
.

The sets
{\rm Ext}(X)
and
\,{\rm Exp}(X)
play a fundamental role in the geometry of the underlying space through the classical Krein–Milman theorem. They arise naturally in a wide range of problems, including the characterization of linear isometries of function spaces
[
6
]
and applications in prediction theory
[
4
]
.
These sets have been explored in numerous function spaces, with analytic function spaces receiving particularly sustained attention. The most significant and interesting cases arise for function spaces equipped with the

L^{p}
-norm when
p=1
or
p=\infty
(see also Remark
1.1
below). In these settings, the extreme and exposed points of the unit ball have been extensively investigated, especially for the classical Hardy spaces and their variants
[
1
,
5
,
11
,
17
]
, various polynomial spaces
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
21
]
, and certain Paley–Wiener spaces
[
8
,
24
]
. For a broader perspective, we refer the reader to the survey
[
12
]
, which also highlights several interesting open problems.

This paper presents, to the best of our knowledge, the first study of the extreme and exposed points of the unit ball in shift-invariant and quasi shift-invariant function spaces. We focus on the classical generators—the Gaussian kernel and the hyperbolic secant—so that the resulting spaces consist of entire or meromorphic functions on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.
These spaces also play an important role in modern sampling theory and in the theory of Gabor frames. From the perspective of these theories, they exhibit very similar properties due to the relation between the Zak transforms of the Gaussian and hyperbolic secant functions, see
[
15
,
19
,
23
]
. However, as we demonstrate below, their properties differ when examined from the standpoint of Banach space geometry.

1.2.
Shift-invariant spaces and quasi shift-invariant spaces

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and let
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a separated set, i.e.,

\inf\limits_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma,\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}}|\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}|>0.

The quasi shift-invariant space
V_{\Gamma}^{p}(g)
generated by a function
g
consists of the functions

f(x)=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}g(x-\gamma),\quad\{c_{\gamma}\}\in l^{p}(\Gamma).

In what follows, we denote by
c_{\gamma}(f)
the coefficient
c_{\gamma}
in the representation above.

Following tradition, we assume throughout that the set of
\Gamma
‑shifts is relatively dense; that is, there exists an
R>0
such that every interval of length
R
contains at least one point of
\Gamma
. This assumption is not essential for the subsequent analysis.

In the particular case
\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}
, the space
V^{p}(g):=V^{p}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(g)
is called shift-invariant.

The space
V_{\Gamma}^{p}(g)
is well-defined whenever
g
belongs to the Wiener amalgam space
W
, i.e.,

(1.1)

\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}[k,k+1]}<\infty,

since then
\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}
is finite for every
f\in V^{p}_{\Gamma}(g)
, see e.g.
[
25
, Lemma 8.1]
.

As usual, we equip the spaces
V^{p}_{\Gamma}
with the standard
L^{p}
-norm.
The quasi shift-invariant spaces considered below satisfy the following condition:
for every
p\in[1,\infty]
there exist positive constants
K_{1},K_{2}
such that

(1.2)

K_{1}\|c\|_{l^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq K_{2}\|c\|_{l^{p}},\quad\text{ for every $f\in V^{p}_{\Gamma}(g).$}

This property shows that
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(g)
is a closed subspace of
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Note that when
\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}
and
f\in W
, the norm equivalence (
1.2
) holds if and only
if the Fourier transform
\hat{g}
does not completely vanish on any arithmetic progression of step
1
,
see
[
20
]
. For the case of general separated sets
\Gamma
, we refer the reader to
[
26
]
.

Like the Paley–Wiener spaces, (quasi) shift-invariant spaces play a fundamental role in approximation theory. In particular, the sampling and interpolation properties of the spaces
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(g)
are closely connected to the frame properties of the Gabor systems generated by
g
, see, e.g.,
[
2
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
23
,
25
]
. Important examples arise when
g
is the Gaussian kernel or the hyperbolic secant:

(1.3)

G_{a}(x):=e^{-ax^{2}},\quad H_{a}(x)=\frac{1}{e^{ax}+{e^{-ax}}},

where
a>0.

It is well-known that the spaces
V^{p}(G_{a})
and
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(H_{a})
satisfy (
1.2
), see e.g.
[
16
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
.
It is easy to check that every element of
V^{p}(G_{a})
admits analytic extension to an entire function, while every element of
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(H_{a})
admits analytic extension to a meromorphic function.

Remark 1.1
.

For the spaces
V^{p}(G_{a})
and
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(H_{a})
with
1<p<\infty
, the set of extreme points of the unit ball coincides with the unit sphere. This follows directly from the classical equality case in Minkowski’s inequality:
\|f+g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}
iff
f=ag
for some
a\geq 0
.

The present paper examines the geometry of the unit balls of these spaces in the case
p=1.

1.3.
Main results

Theorem 1.2
.

(Gaussian kernel)

(i)
The set
{\rm Ext}(V^{1}(G_{a}))
consists of those functions
f\in V^{1}(G_{a})
satisfying
\|f\|_{1}=1
and such that

(1.4)

\text{There is no point $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$ with $0<{\rm Im\,}\lambda<\pi/a$ for which }f(\lambda)=f(\bar{\lambda})=0.

(ii)
The set
\,{\rm Exp}(V^{1}(G_{a}))
consists of those functions
f\in{\rm Ext}(V^{1}(G_{a}))
for which the following two conditions hold:

(1.5)

\text{There is no point }\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\quad\text{such that}\quad f(\lambda)=f^{\prime}(\lambda)=0;

(1.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2ax}|f(x)|\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2ax}|f(x)|\,dx=\infty.

Remark 1.3
.

Similar conditions to (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) also arise in the setting of polynomials and Paley–Wiener spaces
;
see e.g.,
[
8
, Theorems 1 and 3]
.

Remark 1.4
.

(See Lemma
3.3
)
Let
f\in V^{1}(G_{a}).
Condition (
1.6
) is equivalent to requiring that neither sequence
c_{n}(f)e^{2an}
nor
c_{n}{(f)}e^{-2an}
belongs to
l^{1}({\mathbb{Z}})
, i.e.

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{2an}|c_{n}(f)|=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{-2an}|c_{n}(f)|=\infty.

For the hyperbolic secant, we obtain the description of
{\rm Ext}(V^{1}_{\Gamma}(H_{a}))
and
\,{\rm Exp}(V^{1}_{\Gamma}(H_{a}))
for general separated sets of translates
\Gamma.

Theorem 1.5
.

(Hyperbolic secant generator)
Let
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a separated relatively dense set.

(i)
The set
{\rm Ext}(V_{\Gamma}^{1}(H_{a}))
consists of those functions
f\in V_{\Gamma}^{1}(H_{a})
satisfying
\|f\|_{1}=1
, condition (
1.4
) and the condition

(1.7)

c_{\gamma}(f)\neq 0,\quad\text{for all \,}\gamma\in\Gamma.

(ii)
The set
\,{\rm Exp}(V^{1}_{\Gamma}(H_{a}))
consists of those functions
f\in{\rm Ext}(V^{1}_{\Gamma}(H_{a}))
satisfying conditions (
1.5
) and (
1.6
).

Remark 1.6
.

(See Corollary
4.4
)
For
f\in V^{1}_{\Gamma}(H_{a}),
the condition
e^{2ax}f(x)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
is equivalent to the two conditions

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}e^{a\gamma}c_{\gamma}(f)=0,\quad\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}e^{2a\gamma}|c_{\gamma}(f)|<\infty.

Similarly,
e^{-2ax}f(x)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
if and only if

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}e^{-a\gamma}c_{\gamma}(f)=0,\quad\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}e^{-2a\gamma}|c_{\gamma}(f)|<\infty.

We illustrate the above results with a simple example.

Example 1.7
.

Consider the function

f_{\sigma}(x)=C(e^{-(x-1)^{2}}-\sigma e^{-(x+1)^{2}})=Ce^{-x^{2}-1}(e^{2x}-\sigma e^{-2x})\in V^{1}(G_{1}),\quad\sigma\in{\mathbb{C}},

where
C
is chosen so that
\|f_{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}}=1
. Observe that
f_{\sigma}\in{\rm Ext}(V^{1}(G_{1}))
precisely when
\sigma\notin{\mathbb{R}}.

Moreover,
f_{\sigma}
is not an exposed point of
B(V^{1}(G_{1}))
, since the function
f_{\sigma}(x)e^{2x}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

In contrast, any finite linear combination of translates of the hyperbolic secant clearly fails to lie in
{\rm Ext}(V^{1}_{\Gamma}(H_{a}))
, in view of (
1.7
).

The proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.5
are complex‑analytic and rely on a deep result of Hayman
[
18
]
concerning the relationship between the growth and decay of entire functions of slow growth. This method is effective because of a key periodicity phenomenon: elements of the quasi shift‑invariant spaces generated by the hyperbolic secant
H_{a}
are
2\pi i/a
-periodic, and an analogous property holds for the shift‑invariant space generated by the Gaussian
G_{a}
once the functions are multiplied by
\exp(ax^{2}).

We note that this periodicity was already exploited in
[
15
]
to analyze sampling sets for shift‑invariant spaces generated by Gaussian and hyperbolic secant.

The paper is organized as follows. Sec. 2 recalls standard criteria for extreme and exposed points and presents an auxiliary result from the theory of entire functions that will be essential in the sequel. In Sec. 3 we study shift-invariant spaces generated by the Gaussian kernel and prove Theorem
1.2
. The proof of Theorem
1.5
is presented in Sec. 4.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Extreme and exposed points criteria

Assume that
g
is one of the generators specified in (
1.3
).
Our analysis of the geometry of the unit ball of the (quasi) shift-invariant spaces
V^{1}_{\Gamma}(g)

will rely on the following two standard lemmas.

Lemma 2.1

(Extreme point criterion)
.

Suppose that
f\in V^{1}_{\Gamma}(g)
and
\|f\|_{1}=1
.
Then
f\in{\rm Ext}(V^{1}_{\Gamma}(g))
if and only if there is no real, non-constant, bounded function
\tau
on
{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
f\tau\in V^{1}_{\Gamma}(g)
.

Lemma 2.2

(Exposed point criterion)
.

A function
f\in{\rm\,{\rm Exp}}(V^{1}_{\Gamma}(g))
if and only if
f\in{\rm{\rm Ext}}(V^{1}_{\Gamma}(g))
and there is no real, non-negative, non-constant function
h
on
{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
fh\in V^{1}_{\Gamma}(g)
.

These lemmas are well-known in the settings of the Hardy space
H^{1}
and the Paley-Wiener spaces (see
[
8
,
13
]
). The same arguments apply, without essential modification, to the (quasi) shift-invariant spaces
V^{1}_{\Gamma}(g)
generated by Gaussian kernel or hyperbolic secant.

2.2.
A corollary of Hayman’s theorem

Given an entire function
h
, set

(2.1)

M(r,h)=\sup_{|z|\leq r}|h(z)|,\ \ m(r,h)=\inf_{|z|=r}|h(z)|.

If
h(z)=a_{0}+a_{1}z+...+a_{n}z^{n}
is a polynomial, then clearly, for every positive
\varepsilon
there exists
r_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
m(r,h)>(1-\varepsilon)M(r,h)
for all
r>r_{\varepsilon}.
This is no longer true for transcendental entire functions. However, classical results—most notably the

\cos\pi\lambda
-theorem — show that for functions of “slow” growth, the quantity

m(r,h)
cannot be too small relative to

M(r,h)
.

The following lemma is an immediate corollary of Hayman’s classical result (see Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 in
[
18
]
):

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
\psi
is an entire function satisfying

(2.2)

\log M(r,\psi)\leq C\log^{2}r,\ r\geq 2,\quad\text{for some constant }C.

Then,

(i)

for almost every
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
there exists
r_{\theta}>0
such that

(2.3)

\log|\psi(re^{i\theta})|\geq\frac{\log M(r,\psi)}{2},\quad r\geq r_{\theta};

(ii)

there is a set
E\subset(1,\infty)
satisfying
\int_{E}(1/t)\,dt<\infty
and such that

(2.4)

\log m(r,\psi)\geq\frac{\log M(r,\psi)}{2},\quad r\in(1,\infty)\setminus E.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varphi
be an entire
\pi i/a
-periodic function. Then

\varphi(z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}e^{2az},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

where the series converges uniformly in any strip
-\infty<A\leq\,
Re
\,z\leq B<\infty
.

Indeed, set
w:=e^{2za}
, so that
z=\log w/(2a)
and
{\rm Re\,}z=\log|w|/(2a)
. Since
\varphi
is entire and
\pi i/a
-periodic, it is clear that the function
\psi(w):=\varphi(\frac{\log w}{2a})
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Hence,

\psi(w)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{k}w^{k},

and the series converges uniformly in any ring
0<r<|w|<R<\infty,
which proves the lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
\varphi=\varphi_{1}/\varphi_{2}
is an entire function where
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are entire
\pi i/a
-periodic functions satisfying the estimate

(2.5)

|\varphi_{j}(x+iy)|\leq Ce^{Kx^{2}},\ j=1,2,\quad\text{for all }x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}\text{ and some }C,K>0.

Then
\varphi
also satisfies (
2.5
) with some
C,K>0
.

Although this lemma is a particular case of
[
7
, Theorem 1 in Lecture 2]
, we present an alternative proof based on Lemma
2.3
, which we will refer to several times later.

Proof.

Consider the functions

\psi_{j}(w):=\varphi_{j}\left(\frac{\log w}{2a}\right)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{k}^{(j)}w^{k},\ j=1,2.

Write

\psi_{j}(w)=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{(j)}w^{k}+\sum_{k<0}a_{k}^{(j)}w^{k}=:\psi_{j}^{+}(w)+\psi_{j}^{-}(w).

Clearly, both functions
\psi_{j}^{-}(w),j=1,2,
are bounded for
|w|>1.
Hence, by (
2.5
),
\psi_{j}^{+}
satisfy (
2.2
),
j=1,2
.

Assume
\psi_{2}^{+}\equiv const
, so that
n_{0}:=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:a_{k}^{(2)}\neq 0\}\leq 0
. Then for all
w
with sufficiently large absolute value,

|\psi_{2}(w)|\geq|w|^{n_{0}}\left(|a^{(2)}_{n_{0}}|-\left|\sum\limits_{n<n_{0}}a^{(2)}_{n}w^{n-n_{0}}\right|\right)\geq C|w|^{n_{0}}.

Assume now that
\psi_{2}^{+}
is not a constant function.
By Lemma
2.3
(ii), there is a sequence
r_{k}\to\infty
such that

\log m(r_{k},\psi^{+}_{2})>\frac{1}{2}\log M(r_{k},\psi^{+}_{2})\to\infty,\quad k\to\infty,

where
m
and
M
are defined in (
2.1
). Hence,
|\psi_{2}(w)|>C,|w|=r_{k},k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

The above estimates show that there is a sequence
r_{k}\to\infty
such that

(2.6)

|\psi_{2}(w)|\geq C|w|^{n_{0}}\quad\text{for some }n_{0}\leq 0\,\text{ and }\,|w|=r_{k}\to\infty.

Hence,

(2.7)

|\varphi_{2}(z)|\geq Ce^{2an_{0}x}\quad\text{for some }n_{0}\leq 0\,\text{ and }\,x=x_{k}\to\infty.

Using (
2.7
) and estimate (
2.5
) for
\varphi_{1}
, we conclude that
\varphi(z)
satisfies (
2.5
) for all
z=x+iy
, where
x=x_{k}:=(\log r_{k})/(2a)\to+\infty
.

A similar approach shows that
\varphi(z)
satisfies (
2.5
) for all
z=x+iy,
where
x=\tilde{x}_{k}\to-\infty.

We now apply Lemma
2.3
(i) to functions
\psi^{\pm}_{1}
to find numbers
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and
R=R(\theta)>0
such that both functions
\psi^{+}_{1}(re^{i\theta})
and

\psi^{-}_{1}((1/r)e^{-i\theta})

satisfy (
2.3
) for
r>R
. Similarly to above, we see that
\varphi(z)
satisfies (
2.5
) for all
z=x+iy_{0},y_{0}:=\theta/(2a)
.
Since
\varphi
is entire and
\pi i/a
-periodic, we have the same estimate for every
y=y_{0}+\pi k/a,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Therefore, we may find
y_{1}\leq 0
and
y_{2}\geq\pi/a
and a large positive number
K
such that the function
e^{-Kz^{2}}\varphi(z)
is bounded on the boundary of the rectangle

P(k):=\{z=x+iy:\,\,\tilde{x}_{k}\leq x\leq x_{k},\,y_{1}\leq y\leq y_{2},\,\,\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}.

Hence,

|\varphi(z)|\leq Ce^{Kx^{2}},\quad z=x+iy\in P(k),

where
C,K
do not depend on
k.
Letting
k\to\infty
and using the
\pi i/a
-periodicity of
\varphi
, we see that
\varphi
satisfies
(
2.5
).
∎

3.
Gaussian kernel: Proof of Theorem
1.2

3.1.
Auxiliary lemmas

Recall that every element
f\in V^{\infty}(G_{a})
is an entire function of the form

(3.1)

f(z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}e^{-a(z-n)^{2}},\quad\{c_{n}\}\in l^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}).

This yields:

(3.2)

|f(z)|\leq\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|c_{n}|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|e^{-a(z-n)^{2}}\right|\leq Ce^{ay^{2}},\quad z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}.

We start with the following characterization of the functions from the space
V^{\infty}(G_{a}).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a>0
. The following statements are equivalent.

f\in V^{\infty}(G_{a});

f(z)=e^{-az^{2}}\varphi(z)
, where
\varphi(z)
is a
\pi i/a
-periodic entire function satisfying

(3.3)

|\varphi(z)|\leq Ce^{ax^{2}},\quad z=x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}.

Proof.

Assume
a=1.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).
Let
f\in V^{\infty}(G)
. Set

\varphi(z):=e^{z^{2}}f(z)=e^{z^{2}}\sum\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}e^{-(z-n)^{2}}=\sum\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{2zn}c_{n}e^{-n^{2}}.

Clearly,
\varphi
is
\pi i
-periodic, and by (
3.2
) it satisfies (
3.3
).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). As in the proof of Lemma
2.5
, we use the change of variables
w=e^{2z},
and set

\psi(w):=\varphi\left(\frac{\log w}{2}\right)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}w^{n}.

By (
3.3
) (with
a=1
),

(3.4)

|\psi(w)|\leq Ce^{(\log|w|)^{2}/4},\quad w\neq 0.

Write
\psi=\psi_{+}+\psi_{-},
where
\psi_{\pm}
are defined in the proof of Lemma
2.5
.
Note that
\psi_{+}
satisfies estimate (
3.4
) for all
|w|\geq 1
.
Choosing
R=e^{2n}
in Cauchy’s inequality, we get

|a_{n}|\leq\frac{M(R,\psi)}{R^{n}}\leq C\exp\left(\frac{\log^{2}R}{4}-n\log R\right)=Ce^{-n^{2}}

for all
n\geq 0
.
The same estimate holds for every
n<0.
This means that the numbers
c_{n}:=a_{n}e^{n^{2}}
are bounded, and so
the function

f(z):=e^{-z^{2}}\psi(e^{2z})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{n}e^{-(z-n)^{2}}

belongs to
V^{\infty}(G).
This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
f\in V^{\infty}(G_{a})\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then
f\in V^{1}(G_{a}).

Proof.

We set
a=1.

Assume
f\in V^{\infty}(G)\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Fix any positive
\varepsilon<1
and consider the function

f_{\epsilon}(x):=f\left(\frac{x}{1+\varepsilon}\right)e^{-\varepsilon x^{2}/(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}(f)e^{-(x-n)^{2}/(1+\varepsilon)-\varepsilon n^{2}/(1+\varepsilon)}.

Clearly,
f_{\epsilon}\in V^{1}(G_{1/(1+\varepsilon)})
and
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{1}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{1}\leq 2\|f\|_{1}.
Note also that the Fourier transform of
f_{\varepsilon}
is given by

\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\pi itx}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=\sqrt{\pi(1+\varepsilon)}\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}(f)e^{-\varepsilon n^{2}/(1+\varepsilon)}e^{-2\pi int}\right)e^{-\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)t^{2}}.

Since the trigonometric system
\{e^{2\pi int}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is not complete in
L^{2}(I),
for every interval
I
of length
>1,
there is a function
\Psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
which vanishes for
|t|\geq 2
and satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\pi int}\Psi(t)\,dt=\begin{cases}\ 1,&n=0;\\
\ 0,&\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}.\end{cases}

For every
\alpha>0
we set

\Psi_{\alpha}(t):=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\pi}}e^{\pi^{2}t^{2}/\alpha}\Psi(t),

and denote by
\psi_{\alpha}
its inverse Fourier transform. Clearly,
\Psi_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
, and it vanishes for
|t|\geq 2
, so that
\psi_{\alpha}
is a Schwartz function for every
\alpha>0.
From the Plancherel’s theorem, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\alpha(x-n)^{2}}\psi_{\alpha}(x-m)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\alpha(x-n+m)^{2}}\psi_{\alpha}(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\pi i(n-m)t}\Psi(t)\,dt=\begin{cases}\ 1,&n=m;\\
\ 0,&n\neq m.\end{cases}

We now assume that
\alpha=1/(1+\varepsilon)\in[1/2,1]
. We have

(3.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\psi_{1/(1+\varepsilon)}(x-k)\,dx=c_{k}(f)e^{-\varepsilon n^{2}/(1+\varepsilon)}.

Clearly,

\|\psi_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|x^{2}\psi_{\alpha}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|\Psi_{\alpha}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|(\Psi_{\alpha})^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}<C,

where
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon.

Therefore,

(3.6)

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\psi_{\alpha}(x-n)|<C,\quad\text{for every }\alpha=1/(1+\varepsilon)\in[1/2,1].

Consider the integral

I_{\varepsilon}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\overline{c_{k}(f)}}{|c_{k}(f)|}\psi_{1/(1+\varepsilon)}(x-k)\,dx.

By (
3.6
),

(3.7)

|I_{\varepsilon}|<C\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{1}\leq 2C\|f\|_{1},\quad 0<\varepsilon\leq 1.

On the other hand, since the sequence
\{c_{n}(f)\exp(-\varepsilon n^{2}/(1+\varepsilon))\}\in l^{1}({\mathbb{Z}})
, by the right-hand-side inequality in (
1.2
), the function

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|c_{n}(f)|e^{-\varepsilon n^{2}/(1+\varepsilon)}e^{-(x-n)^{2}/(1+\varepsilon)}

belongs to
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).
By the Dominated convergence theorem, integrating term for term and using (
3.5
), we obtain

I_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|c_{n}(f)|e^{-\varepsilon n^{2}/(1+\varepsilon)}.

Using (
3.7
) and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we get
\{c_{n}(f)\}\in l^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}),
so that
f\in V^{1}(G).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f\in V^{1}(G_{a})
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

e^{2ax}f(x)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}});

(ii)

\{e^{2an}c_{n}(f)\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in l^{1}({\mathbb{Z}});

(iii)

e^{2ax}f(x)\in V^{1}(G_{a})
.

A similar result holds if we set ”
-
” in every exponent in the three conditions above.

Proof.

For brevity, set
a=1
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) and (ii).
Assume (i) holds true. Then
e^{bx}f(x)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),
for every
0\leq b\leq 2,
and so the Fourier transform

\hat{f}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\pi izx}f(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}(f)e^{-2\pi izn}\right)e^{-\pi^{2}z^{2}}

admits an analytic continuation into the strip
0<{\rm Im\,}\,z<\pi^{-1}
, and is continuous up to its boundary. This means that the
1
-periodic series

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}(f)e^{-2\pi izn}

also admits an analytic continuation into the strip
0<{\rm Im\,}\,z<\pi^{-1}
, and is continuous up to its boundary.
This property is obviously true for the series

\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}(f)e^{-2\pi izn}.

This is equivalent to the condition that the power series

\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}w^{n}
admits an analytic continuation to the disk
|w|<e^{2},
and is bounded above by some constant
C
in its closure. By Cauchy’s inequality,

|c_{n}|\leq\frac{C}{e^{2n}},\quad n\geq 0,

which proves that
\{c_{n}e^{2n}\}_{n\geq 0}\in l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N}).

Observe that

e^{2x}f(x)=e^{2x}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}(f)e^{-(x-n)^{2}}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}(f)e^{2n+1}e^{-(x-n-1)^{2}}=:g(x),

and set
c_{n}(g):=c_{n}(f)e^{2n+1}.

Then
g\in V^{\infty}(G).

By (i) and Lemma
3.2
,
g\in V^{1}(G)
, which proves (iii) and (ii).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Let
g
be the function above.
By (ii),
\{c_{n}(g)\}\in l^{1}({\mathbb{Z}})
and since the Gaussian generator satisfies condition (
1.2
), condition (i) holds true.

∎

C_{R}
is uniformly bounded as
R\to\infty
. Integrating
f_{\delta}
over
C_{R}
and letting
R\to\infty
, by the residue theorem, we have

\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_{R}}f_{\delta}(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\text{Res}(f_{\delta},\gamma+i\pi/2)=\pi e^{\delta\pi^{2}/4}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|e^{2\gamma-\delta\gamma^{2}}<K,

where the constant
K
does not depend on
\delta.
By letting
\delta\to 0
, we conclude that the series converges:

(4.9)

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|e^{2\gamma}<\infty.

Now, write

e^{2x}f(x)=e^{2x}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{c_{\gamma}}{e^{x-\gamma}+e^{\gamma-x}}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\left(e^{\gamma}e^{x}-\frac{e^{2\gamma}}{e^{x-\gamma}+e^{\gamma-x}}\right).

By (
4.9
), we see that the function

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{c_{\gamma}e^{2\gamma}}{e^{x-\gamma}+e^{\gamma-x}}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

Therefore,

e^{x}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}e^{\gamma}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),

which implies

(4.10)

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}e^{\gamma}=0.

We conclude that

e^{2x}f(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{c_{\gamma}e^{2\gamma}}{e^{x-\gamma}+e^{\gamma-x}}\in V^{1}_{\Gamma}(H).

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
f\in V^{1}_{\Gamma}(H)
. The function
e^{2x}f(x)
is integrable if and only if both conditions (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) are fulfilled.

Similarly, one may check that
e^{-2x}f(x)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
if and only if we have

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|e^{-2\gamma}<\infty,\quad\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}e^{-\gamma}=0.

Sufficiency.

The proof follows the same strategy as the sufficiency part of Theorem
1.2
(ii), see Section
3.3
. For brevity, we leave the remaining details to the reader.
