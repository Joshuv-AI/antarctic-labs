Sutured manifold hierarchies and the Thurston norm
Alessandro V. Cigna

Abstract
. Classical work of Thurston and Gabai shows that finitely many taut sutured manifold hierarchies determine the Thurston norm of a compact oriented irreducible 3 3 -manifold with toroidal boundary. We give an explicit procedure to extract this information from such hierarchies. This is achieved via the maw dual graph construction, which can be incorporated into a general method for computing the Thurston norm of a manifold. As an application, we compute the Thurston norm of the exterior of all alternating and some nonalternating pretzel links with three components. Using these computations, we give a negative answer to a question of Baker–Taylor. Moreover, we show that if a nonseparating surface S S in a Haken manifold M M with toroidal boundary is disjoint from a boundary torus, then the class [ S ] ∈ H 2 ​ ( M , ∂ M ) [S]\in H_{2}(M,\partial M) does not lie in the interior of a top-dimensional cone of the Thurston norm. In particular, if two components ℓ i \ell_{i} and ℓ j \ell_{j} of a link have zero linking number, then neither represents a class in an open top-dimensional cone of the Thurston norm ball of the link exterior.

1.
Introduction

Given a Seifert surface
S
for a link
L
, when does
S
realise the minimal genus among Seifert surfaces for
L
? More generally, given a properly embedded oriented surface
S
in a compact oriented
3
-manifold
M
, when does
S
attain the minimal topological complexity among surfaces homologous to
S
? Here, by “having
minimal topological complexity
” we really mean “being
Thurston norm-minimising
” (see Section
2
for details). In 1983, Gabai gave a complete answer to these questions in terms of sutured manifold hierarchies.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
5
]
Let
M
be a compact oriented and irreducible
3
-manifold
M
with toroidal boundary and let
S\subset M
be a properly embedded oriented surface. Suppose that
S
meets each component of
\partial M
in parallel oriented curves and no union of components of
S
is zero in homology. Then,
(M,\partial M)
admits a taut sutured manifold hierarchy starting with a decomposition along
S
if and only if
S
is norm-minimising for its class in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
.

This theorem guarantees that taut sutured manifold hierarchies allow one to determine the Thurston norm of an integral class
\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
. In Gabai’s theory, taut foliations play the connecting bridge between the Thurston norm and sutured manifold hierarchies. Indeed, Gabai showed that taut sutured manifold hierarchies carry taut foliations and, for such foliations, Thurston’s Inequality holds.

Theorem 1.2

(Thurston’s Inequality
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a taut foliation of a compact oriented
3
-manifold
M
, with
\mathcal{F}
transverse to
\partial M
. For every properly embedded oriented surface
S\subset M
without sphere nor disk components, the following inequality holds:

|\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[S]\rangle|\leq|\chi(S)|.

Here,
e(\mathcal{F})\in H^{2}(M,\partial M;\Z)
is the relative Euler class of
\mathcal{F}
and
[S]\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\Z)
. Moreover, the equality is achieved if
S
is a union of consistently oriented leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.

In particular, each taut foliation of
M
induces a lower bound on the topological complexity of
any
surface
S\subset M
in a given homology class
\alpha
, and this bound depends just on
\alpha
. In the present article, we highlight that a taut sutured manifold hierarchy indeed provides
global
information about the Thurston norm and describe how to obtain this information. The method is theoretically simple: if we want to show that a surface
S
is norm-minimising, we have to find a taut sutured manifold hierarchy for
(M,\partial M)
starting with a decomposition along
S
. Once such a hierarchy has been found, we can use it to compute the relative Euler class of any taut foliation
\mathcal{F}
carried by it. At this point, Thurston’s Inequality already restrains the Thurston unit ball to lie between the two hyperplanes
\{\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)\,|\;|\langle e(\mathcal{F}),\alpha\rangle|=\pm 1\}
. Moreover, if a surface
S_{1}
is algebraically fully marked with respect to
\mathcal{F}
, meaning that
\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[S_{1}]\rangle=\chi(S_{1})
, then also
S_{1}
is norm-minimising and
[S]
and
[S_{1}]
lie on the same cone over a face of the Thurston ball (see Corollaries
4.3
and
4.4
).

3

[

. Given a compact oriented

.

Let

be a torus link

Going back to the proposed strategy for computing the Thurston norm, one of its limitations is that a single hierarchy is often insufficient to fully recover the shape of the Thurston polyhedron. At the same time, finding hierarchies is a lengthy process and strongly depends on the capability of visualising
M
or on the properties we know about it. However, given a complete groomed sutured manifold hierarchy
\mathcal{H}_{+}
for
(M,\partial M)
, we can easily find a different groomed sutured manifold hierarchy by modifying
\mathcal{H}_{+}
. Indeed, suppose that the last decomposition of
\mathcal{H}_{+}
is along a disk
D
. We can instead consider the hierarchy
\mathcal{H}_{-}
that differs from
\mathcal{H}_{+}
just in the chosen orientation of
D
. How much do the Euler classes of the two obtained foliations differ from each other? This question is answered by the following lemma.

Lemma
5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F_{+}}
be a taut foliation on
M
arising from the groomed sutured manifold hierarchy
\mathcal{H}_{+}
given by

(M,\partial M)\rightsquigarrow...\rightsquigarrow(M_{r},\gamma_{r})\overset{D}{\rightsquigarrow}\text{product sutured balls}

where
D
is a decomposing disk. Let
\mathcal{H}_{-}
be the groomed sutured manifold hierarchy coinciding with
\mathcal{H}_{+}
until
(M_{r},\gamma_{r})
, yet ending with

(M_{r},\gamma_{r})\overset{\overline{D}}{\rightsquigarrow}\text{product sutured balls.}

Let
\mathcal{F}_{-}
be a taut foliation carried by
\mathcal{H}_{-}
. Then, the difference
e(\mathcal{F}_{+})-e(\mathcal{F_{-})}
is Poincaré dual to
(2-|D\cap\gamma_{r}|)i_{*}\delta
, where
i_{*}\delta\in H_{1}(M)
is the image under the inclusion induced map
i_{*}:H_{1}(M_{r})\to H_{1}(M)
of the Poincaré-Lefschetz dual
\delta
to
[D]\in H_{2}(M_{r},\partial M_{r})
.

Using the work of Gabai
[
7
]
, we deduce:

Corollary
5.7
.

Let
M
be an irreducible compact oriented
3
-manifold whose boundary is a union of incompressible tori. Suppose
S
is a properly embedded oriented nonseparating surface in
M
, such that there is a boundary torus
T
disjoint from
S
. Then the class
[S]
in
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
is not in the interior of any top-dimensional cone of the Thurston norm.

When two knots in
S^{3}
have zero linking number, a Seifert surface for one knot can be made disjoint from the other knot and the hypotheses of Corollary
5.7
hold.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be an oriented nonsplit link with components
\ell_{1}\cup...\cup\ell_{n}
. If
\lk(\ell_{i},\ell_{j})=0
for some
i\neq j
, then neither
\ell_{i}
nor
\ell_{j}
are in the interior of any top-dimensional cone over the Thurston ball of
H_{2}(M_{L},\partial M_{L};\R)\cong(\R\ell_{1})\oplus...\oplus(\R\ell_{n})
.

2.
The Thurston norm

We now recall some of the basic definitions and properties of the Thurston norm. The main results of this section, as for the use we will make of them during this article, are Lemma
2.8
and Lemma
2.10
.

Every properly embedded oriented surface
S\subset M
represents a class
[S]\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
. Conversely, every class in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is represented by a properly embedded oriented surface.

A natural question to ask is whether the sum in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
corresponds to some operation on surfaces in
M
. The answer is affirmative: if
[S_{1}]=\alpha_{1}
and
[S_{2}]=\alpha_{2}
, then
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
is represented by the
oriented cut and paste
of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. Here is the definition.

Definition 2.1
.

(Oriented cut and paste) Let
S_{1},S_{2}\subset M
be properly embedded oriented surfaces that are transverse to each other. There is only one oriented way to split
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
along
S_{1}\cap S_{2}
and reglue back to obtain an oriented surface
S
, which locally looks as in Figure
3
. The obtained surface
S
is called the
oriented cut and paste
of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. Notice that
\chi(S)=\chi(S_{1})+\chi(S_{2})
.

Figure 3.
Local view of the oriented cut and paste operation of the surfaces
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. Red arrows indicate the normal orientation.

Definition 2.2
.

(Thurston norm) Let
S\subset M
be a properly embedded orientable surface. Denote with
\chi(S)
the Euler characteristic of
S
. If
S
is connected, call

\chi_{-}(S):=\max(0,-\chi(S)).

If
S
is the disjoint union of connected surfaces
S_{1},...,S_{k}
, define

\chi_{-}(S):=\chi_{-}(S_{1})+...+\chi_{-}(S_{k}).

Equivalently,
\chi_{-}(S)=|\chi(S^{\prime})|
, where
S^{\prime}
is the surface obtained from
S
after discarding all sphere or disk components.
For every class
\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
, let

x(\alpha)=\min_{[S]=\alpha}\chi_{-}(S),

where
S\subset M
varies among the properly embedded oriented surfaces representing
\alpha
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
13
]
)
.

The function
x:H_{2}(M,\partial M)\to\Z
can be extended to a seminorm

x:H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)\to\R_{\geq 0}
, called the
Thurston norm
associated to the manifold
M
.

Sketch of the proof.
Thurston verified that
x
is positively homogeneous on
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
with respect to the multiplication by integer scalars. He also established that
\chi_{-}(S)=\chi_{-}(S_{1})+\chi_{-}(S_{2})
whenever
S
is the oriented cut and paste of incompressible and
\partial
-incompressible surfaces
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
intersecting essentially. Then,
x
can be extended by homogeneity to a convex function
x:H_{2}(M,\partial M;\Q)\to\Q
, which can further be continuously extended to a seminorm
x:H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)\to\R_{\geq 0}
.

Definition 2.4

(Taut surfaces)
.

A properly embedded oriented surface
S\subset M
is
norm-minimising
if
x([S])=\chi_{-}(S)
. The surface
S
is taut if, furthermore, no subset of components of
S
is homologically trivial, and any two parallel components of
\partial S
are coherently oriented.

Example 2.5
.

Suppose
L\subset S^{3}
is an oriented link with components
\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}
. Let
M_{L}:=S^{3}-\overset{\circ}{N}(L)
be its exterior.
By excision we get an identification
H_{2}(M_{L},\partial M_{L})\cong(\Z\ell_{1})\oplus...\oplus(\Z\ell_{n}).
Here,
[S]=a_{1}\ell_{1}+...+a_{n}\ell_{n}
if and only if
\partial S
algebraically intersects
a_{i}
times the meridian of
\ell_{i}
, for each
i
.

A surface representing
\ell_{1}+...+\ell_{n}
is taut if and only if it is a minimal-genus Seifert surface for
L
.

The class
\ell_{1}
is represented by
S\cap M_{L}
, where
S\subset S^{3}
is a Seifert surface for
\ell_{1}
. Therefore, computing
x(\ell_{1})
is analogous to computing the minimal genus of
\ell_{1}
in
M_{\ell_{2}\cup...\cup\ell_{n}}
.

2.1.
The shape of the unit ball

To compute a (semi)norm on
\R^{n}
it is enough to know its unit ball. A useful feature of Thurston norm is that its ball can be recovered by knowing a finite amount of data, thanks to the following result.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
13
]
)
.

The unit ball
B_{x}
of the Thurston norm
x
on
M
is a finite (but possibly unbounded) convex polyhedron with rational vertices and symmetric with respect to the origin.

When
x
is not a norm, i.e. when some connected surface with non-negative Euler characteristic is homologically non-trivial, the null-space of
x
is a vector space contained in
B_{x}
. In particular,
B_{x}
is bounded if and only if
x
is a norm on
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
.

Definition 2.7

(Boundary of the Thurston ball, cones and corners)
.

We refer to the set of points with unit Thurston norm as the
boundary

\partial B_{x}
of the Thurston ball. Since
B_{x}
is a finite polyhedron, there are finitely many affine hyperplanes
H_{1},...,H_{k}
in
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
such that

\partial B_{x}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}(\partial B_{x}\cap H_{i}).

Each nonempty intersection
\partial B_{x}\cap H_{i_{1}}\cap...\cap H_{i_{j}}
is a
closed face
of
\partial B_{x}
. An
open face
is the interior of a closed face. If
F\subset\partial B_{x}
is an open face, the
open cone
over
F
is the set
\{\lambda v\,|\;v\in F,\lambda\in\R_{>0}\}
. If
F
is instead a closed face, the
closed cone
over
F
is the closure of the cone over the interior
F
, defined as above. We say that a primitive class
\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
is a
corner
of the Thurston norm if it does not lie in the interior of any top-dimensional open cone
[
11
]
.

A useful principle to investigate the shape of the Thurston ball of a manifold is the following.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional real vector space endowed with a seminorm
x
, whose unit ball
B_{x}
is a finite (possibly unbounded) polyhedron. Two vectors
\alpha,\beta\in V
lie in the closed cone over a common boundary face of
B_{x}
if and only if

x(\alpha+\beta)=x(\alpha)+x(\beta).

Proof.

If
x(\alpha)=0
, both statements hold. So, we can suppose that neither
\alpha
nor
\beta
has zero seminorm.

If
\alpha
and
\beta
lie in the closed cone over a common boundary face
F
of
B_{x}
, then every point of the segment between
\alpha/x(\alpha)
and
\beta/x(\beta)
lies on
F
. In particular

(1)

x\left(\frac{x(\alpha)}{x(\alpha)+x(\beta)}\frac{\alpha}{x(\alpha)}+\frac{x(\beta)}{x(\alpha)+x(\beta)}\frac{\beta}{x(\beta)}\right)=1,

and this is equivalent to
x(\alpha+\beta)=x(\alpha)+x(\beta)
.

Conversely,
x(\alpha+\beta)=x(\alpha)+x(\beta)
implies the validity of equation (
1
). Thus, the three aligned points

\frac{\alpha}{x(\alpha)},\frac{\beta}{x(\beta)}\text{ and }\frac{\alpha+\beta}{x(\alpha+\beta)}=\frac{x(\alpha)}{x(\alpha)+x(\beta)}\frac{\alpha}{x(\alpha)}+\frac{x(\beta)}{x(\alpha)+x(\beta)}\frac{\beta}{x(\beta)}

all lie on the boundary of the convex
B_{x}
, so the whole segment between
\alpha/x(\alpha)
and
\beta/x(\beta)
does.
∎

2.2.
Torus decompositions

An incompressible torus
T
in
M
allows us to focus on the manifold obtained by cutting
M
along
T
. It turns out that the Thurston norm behaves well with respect to this decomposition.

Lemma 2.9
.

[
10
, Proposition 11.2]
Let
M
be a compact oriented
3
-manifold with incompressible boundary. Let
T\subset M
be a disjoint union of incompressible tori and let
M^{\prime}
be
M
cut along
T
, seen as a submanifold of
M
. Consider a properly embedded oriented surface
S\subset M
transverse to
T
. After indicating with
x_{M}
and
x_{M^{\prime}}
the Thurston norms on
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
and
H_{2}(M^{\prime},\partial M^{\prime})
respectively, the following equality holds

x_{M}([S])=x_{M^{\prime}}([S\cap M^{\prime}]).

When the torus
T
is separating in
M
, the norm
x_{M^{\prime}}
is the sum of the norms of its connected components. In turn, the following lemma will apply.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
V,V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be finite-dimensional real vector spaces and
f_{1}:V\to V_{1}
and
f_{2}:V\to V_{2}
linear maps. For each
i=1,2
, let
x_{i}
be a seminorm on
V_{i}
whose unit ball
B_{x_{i}}
is a finite (possibly unbounded) polyhedron.

The function
x=x_{1}\circ f_{1}+x_{2}\circ f_{2}:V\to\R
is a seminorm whose unit ball
B_{x}
is a finite polyhedron. If
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are closed cones over boundary faces of the unit balls of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
respectively, then
f_{1}^{-1}(C_{1})\cap f_{2}^{-1}(C_{2})
is a closed cone over a boundary face of the unit ball of
x
.
Vice versa, each closed cone
C
over a boundary face of the unit ball of
x
arises as an intersection
f_{1}^{-1}(C_{1})\cap f_{2}^{-1}(C_{2})
, for some
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
closed cones over boundary faces of the unit balls of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
respectively.

Proof.

The proof consists of a repeated use of Lemma
2.8
.

If
\alpha,\beta\in f_{1}^{-1}(C_{1})\cap f_{2}^{-1}(C_{2})
for some closed cones
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
over boundary faces of the unit balls of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
, then

\displaystyle x(\alpha+\beta)

\displaystyle=x_{1}(f_{1}(\alpha)+f_{1}(\beta))+x_{2}(f_{2}(\alpha)+f_{2}(\beta))

\displaystyle=x_{1}(f_{1}(\alpha))+x_{1}(f_{1}(\beta))+x_{2}(f_{2}(\alpha))+x_{2}(f_{2}(\beta))

\displaystyle=x(\alpha)+x(\beta).

Hence
x
is additive on
f_{1}^{-1}(C_{1})\cap f_{2}^{-1}(C_{2})
. Since the closed cones over the faces of
x_{i}
completely cover
V_{i}
for each
i=1,2
, the cones arising as intersections
f_{1}^{-1}(C_{1})\cap f_{2}^{-1}(C_{2})
completely cover
V
. This also shows that the unit ball of
x
is a finite polyhedron.

Now, a top-dimensional closed cone over a boundary face of the unit ball of
x
is a maximal subset of
V
on which
x
restricts to a linear function. Given
\alpha,\beta\in V
, we have

\displaystyle x(\alpha+\beta)

\displaystyle=x_{1}(f_{1}(\alpha)+f_{1}(\beta))+x_{2}(f_{2}(\alpha)+f_{2}(\beta))

\displaystyle\leq x_{1}(f_{1}(\alpha))+x(f_{1}(\beta))+x_{2}(f_{2}(\alpha))+x_{2}(f_{2}(\beta))

\displaystyle=x(\alpha)+x(\beta).

In particular,
\alpha
and
\beta
lie in the same closed top-dimensional cone over a boundary face of
B_{x}
if and only if
f_{i}(\alpha)
and
f_{i}(\beta)
lie in the same closed top-dimensional cone over a boundary face of
B_{x_{i}}
, for each
i=1,2
.
∎

We will use this lemma in the proof of Corollary
5.7
.

3.
Taut sutured manifold hierarchies

3.1.
Sutured manifolds

Sutured manifolds were introduced by Gabai
[
6
]
to construct taut foliations on
3
-manifolds. In this section, we define the objects involved in the statements of Theorems
3.5
and
3.10
.

Definition 3.1

(Sutured manifold)
.

A
sutured manifold
is a pair
(M,\gamma)
, where
M
is a compact oriented
3
-manifold and
\gamma=A(\gamma)\cup T(\gamma)
is a subset of
\partial M
satisfying the following conditions. The set
A(\gamma)
is a union of pairwise disjoint annuli, whereas
T(\gamma)
is a union of pairwise disjoint tori. A
suture
is the core of an annulus of
A(\gamma)
, considered as an oriented simple closed curve. Every region of
R(\gamma):=\partial M-\overset{\circ}{\gamma}
has a normal orientation. We call
R_{+}(\gamma)
the union of the components of
R(\gamma)
whose normal vector points out of
M
, and
R_{-}(\gamma)
the union of the remaining components. The last requirement for
(M,\gamma)
is that the normal orientations on
R(\gamma)
are coherent with the orientation of the sutures: if
\delta\subset\partial R(\gamma)
is a boundary component with the induced orientation, then
\delta
is homologous to a suture in
H_{1}(\gamma)
.

We will say that
(M,\gamma)
is a
product sutured manifold
, if the sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
is homeomorphic to
(R\times[0,1],\partial R\times[0,1])
for some compact oriented surface
R
with non-empty boundary.

If
\partial M
is a union of tori and
S
is a properly embedded oriented surface, call
(M(S),\gamma(S))
the sutured manifold obtained by
cutting

(M,\gamma)
along
S
; i.e.,
M(S):=M-\overset{\circ}{N}(S)
and
\gamma(S):=\partial M-\overset{\circ}{N}(S)
.

Definition 3.2

(Taut foliations)
.

A
foliation
on a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
is a family
\mathcal{F}
of disjoint injectively smoothly immersed surfaces – called
leaves
of
\mathcal{F}
– that altogether cover
M
and such that a small neighbourhood of each point is foliated as an open subset of
\R\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
subdivided into horizontal half-planes
\R\times[0,+\infty)\times\{z\}
. Bearing this local model in mind, we ask points in the interior of
\gamma
to look like points on
\R\times\{0\}\times(0,+\infty)
, points in the interior of
R(\gamma)
as points on
\R\times(0,+\infty)\times\{0\}
, and points on
\partial\gamma
as points on
\R\times\{0\}\times\{0\}
.

The foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
taut
if it is cooriented with normal orientation pointing inward on
R_{-}(\gamma)
and outward on
R_{+}(\gamma)
, and every leaf intersects either a closed transversal or a properly embedded transverse arc with endpoints on
R(\gamma)
. Gabai’s theory produces taut foliations with an additional property, that we will hence include in the definition of taut foliations. We will require that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
(M,\gamma)
has no
2
-dimensional Reeb components
at the boundary, see Figure
4
.

Figure 4.
A
2
-dimensional Reeb component is a subset of leaves of the boundary foliation that cover an annulus. The boundary of the annulus is composed of leaves with the same coorientation, i.e., they both point inside or both outside the annulus. The interior leaves are lines.

The idea behind sutured manifolds is simple: assume
S
is a properly embedded oriented surface in a manifold
M
with toroidal boundary. Suppose that we want to construct a foliation of
M
where
S
is a leaf. Cut
M
open along
S
to obtain the sutured manifold
(M(S),\gamma(S))
. Here, both
R_{+}(\gamma(S))
and
R_{-}(\gamma(S))
are copies of
S
. So, if we find a taut foliation of
M(S)
transverse to
\gamma(S)
and tangent to
R_{+}(\gamma(S))
and
R_{-}(\gamma(S))
, then we can construct a taut foliation of
M
with leaf
S
, by just gluing back
R_{+}(\gamma(S))
and
R_{-}(\gamma(S))
together.

Potentially, this technique can be iterated: say we find a new surface
S^{\prime}
in
M(S)
and cut
M(S)
open along
S^{\prime}
. Then we define a new sutured manifold
(N^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
, where
N^{\prime}=M(S)-\overset{\circ}{N}(S^{\prime})
and
\gamma^{\prime}
is defined so that a foliation of
N^{\prime}
transverse to
\gamma^{\prime}
and tangent to
R(\gamma^{\prime})
can be modified to get a foliation of
(M(S),\gamma(S))
. Then we can decompose
(N^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
along a new surface
S^{\prime\prime}
and so on and so forth.

Finally, suppose that at some point we get a product sutured manifold
(R\times[0,1],\partial R\times[0,1])
. Then the process is done, because we can just choose the product foliation on this last sutured manifold.

This philosophy stands behind the notions of
sutured manifold decompositions
and
sutured manifold hierarchies
. See Section
6
for concrete examples of this process.

The following definition is
[
5
, Definition 3.1]
corrected as in
[
7
, Correction 0.3]
.

Definition 3.3

(Surface decompositions)
.

Let
(M,\gamma)
be a sutured manifold. A
decomposing surface
for
(M,\gamma)
is a properly embedded oriented surface
S\subset M
such that for every component
\lambda
of
S\cap\gamma
one of the following holds:

(i)

\lambda
is a properly embedded nonseparating arc in
\gamma
;

(ii)

\lambda
is a simple closed curve in an annular component
A
of
\gamma
in the same homology class as
A\cap s(\gamma)
;

(iii)

\lambda
is a homotopically nontrivial curve in a toral component
T
of

, and if
\delta
is another component of

\lambda
and

represent the same homology class in

We further require that:

(iv)

No component of
\partial S
bounds a disk in
R(\gamma)
and no component of
S
is a disk
D
with
\partial D\subset R(\gamma)
.

The decomposing surface

is
groomed
if furthermore:

(v)

No subset of toral components of

is homologically trivial in
H_{2}(M)
;

(

)

For each component
V
of

, either:

–

is a union of parallel, coherently oriented, nonseparating closed curves on

, or

–

S\cap V

of

Any decomposing surface

sutured manifold decomposition

(M,\gamma)\overset{S}{\rightsquigarrow}(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}),

where

and

is obtained by smoothing together

close to

according to the coorientation, as in Figure

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(\gamma\cap M^{\prime})\cup N(S^{\prime}_{+}\cap R_{-}(\gamma))\cup N(S_{-}^{\prime}\cap R_{+}(\gamma)),

\displaystyle R_{+}(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=((R_{+}(\gamma)\cap M^{\prime})\cup S^{\prime}_{+})-\overset{\circ}{N}(A(\gamma)),

\displaystyle R_{-}(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=((R_{-}(\gamma)\cap M^{\prime})\cup S^{\prime}_{-})-\overset{\circ}{N}(A(\gamma)),

where

Definition 3.4

(Hierarchies)
.

A
taut sutured manifold hierarchy
for the sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
is a sequence of sutured manifold decompositions

(M,\gamma)\overset{S_{1}}{\rightsquigarrow}...\overset{S_{n}}{\rightsquigarrow}(M_{n},\gamma_{n})

such that

is a product sutured manifold. The hierarchy above is said

if
(M_{n},\gamma_{n})
is a union of product balls. It is
groomed

We can now state a stronger version of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.5
.

[
5
, Corollaries 5.3 and 5.4]
Let
(M,\gamma)
be a sutured manifold such that
M\neq B^{3}
or

, and

. The following are equivalent:

(a)

(M,\gamma)
has a taut sutured manifold hierarchy so that no component of
R(\gamma_{i})
is a compressible torus;

(b)

(M,\gamma)
has a groomed sutured manifold hierarchy so that no component of
R(\gamma_{i})
is a compressible torus;

(c)

(M,\gamma)
has a taut foliation.

Definition 3.6
.

A
product disk
for
(M,\gamma)
is a decomposing disk
D
with
|\partial D\cap\gamma|=2
.

Remark 3.7
.

•

Every product sutured manifold can be decomposed into a disjoint union of product balls through a disjoint collection of product disks. In particular, every taut sutured manifold hierarchy can be completed, meaning that it can be extended to a complete hierarchy.

•

The converse holds as well: if
(M,\gamma)
is a sutured manifold with
R(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
and it is completely decomposable through product disks, then
(M,\gamma)
is a product sutured manifold
[
6
]
.

•

Decomposing along product disks is never a gamble: by
[
5
, Lemma 3.12]
, in a product disk decomposition

,
(M,\gamma)
admits a taut foliation if and only if
(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
does.

(Branched surface)

4.
Euler classes of taut foliations and the Thurston norm

Let
M
be a compact oriented manifold with toroidal boundary, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on
M
transverse to
\partial M
.
Given a non-vanishing section
\sigma
of the plane bundle
T\mathcal{F}|_{\partial M}\to\partial M
, the
Euler class
of
\mathcal{F}
relative to
\sigma
is the obstruction
e(\mathcal{F},\sigma)\in H^{2}(M,\partial M)
to extending
\sigma
to a non-vanishing section of the plane bundle
T\mathcal{F}\to M
. We refer the reader to
[
2
, Chapter 4]
for the rigorous definition of the Euler class, and to Corollary
5.4
for an operative definition via an embedded graph in
M
. From now on, we will always understand
\sigma
to be an outward-pointing section, and we will indicate
e(\mathcal{F})=e(\mathcal{F},\sigma)
.

In the present section, we establish Corollary
4.4
, which is one of the main ingredients in the proposed approach to computing the Thurston norm of a manifold.

4.1.
Thurston’s Inequality and its consequences

As previously anticipated, the Euler class of a taut foliation and the Thurston norm on the ambient manifold are linked by Thurston’s Inequality.

Theorem 4.1

(Thurston’s Inequality)
.

[
13
, Corollary 1]
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a taut foliation of a compact oriented
3
-manifold
M
, with
\mathcal{F}
transverse to
\partial M
. For every
\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
, the following inequality holds

|\langle e(\mathcal{F}),\alpha\rangle|\leq x(\alpha).

Here,
e(\mathcal{F})\in H^{2}(M,\partial M)
indicates the relative Euler class of
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 4.2

(Algebraically fully marked surfaces)
.

[
4
]
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a taut foliation of a compact oriented
3
-manifold
M
, with
\mathcal{F}
transverse to
\partial M
. Let
S\subset M
be a properly embedded oriented surface with no sphere nor disk components. We say that
S
is
algebraically fully marked
if the following equality holds

\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[S]\rangle=\chi(S).

Corollary 4.3
.

[
13
]
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a taut foliation of a compact oriented
3
-manifold
M
, with
\mathcal{F}
transverse to
\partial M
. If
S\subset M
is an algebraically fully marked surface, then
S
is norm-minimising and
x([S])=-\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[S]\rangle
.

Proof.

Since
S
has no sphere nor disk components,
\chi_{-}(S)=|\chi(S)|
. By Thurston’s Inequality,

\chi_{-}(S)=|\chi(S)|=|\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[S]\rangle|\leq x([S]).

By the definition of the Thurston norm, we conclude that
\chi_{-}(S)=x([S])
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a taut foliation on a compact oriented
3
-manifold
M
. If
S
and
S^{\prime}\subset M
are two algebraically fully marked surfaces, then they are norm-minimising and their classes
[S]
and
[S^{\prime}]
lie on the same closed cone over a face of the Thurston ball.

Proof.

If
x([S])=0
, then the thesis automatically holds. We can therefore assume that
S
and
S^{\prime}
have nonzero
\chi_{-}
and are incompressible and
\partial
-incompressible. Indeed, compressions and
\partial
-compressions only increase the Euler characteristic of a surface. We can then isotope
S
and
S^{\prime}
so that their oriented cut and paste
\hat{S}
has no sphere nor disk components. For if such a component arose, an innermost argument shows that a component of
S\cap S^{\prime}
would bound a disk or be boundary-parallel in
S
or
S^{\prime}
. By incompressibility and
\partial
-incompressibility, we could then remove such an inessential intersection and repeat the argument. Here, we are using that
M
is irreducible and
\partial
-irreducible, which follows by Novikov
[
12
]
and the fact that
x([S])\neq 0
. After these isotopies,
\hat{S}
is algebraically fully marked, because we have

\chi(\hat{S})=\chi(S)+\chi(S^{\prime})=\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[S]\rangle+\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[S^{\prime}]\rangle=\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[\hat{S}]\rangle.

Since

x(\hat{S})=-\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[\hat{S}]\rangle=-\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[S]\rangle-\langle e(\mathcal{F}),[S^{\prime}]\rangle=x(S)+x(S^{\prime}),

Lemma
2.8
concludes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Dual Thurston norm and realisability of Euler classes

After identifying
H^{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
with
\Hom(H_{2}(M,\partial M),\R)
, the norm
x
on
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
induces a
dual norm

x^{*}
defined by

x^{*}(\beta):=\sup_{x(\alpha)=1}\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle

for every
\beta\in H^{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
, where
\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
ranges among classes with unit Thurston norm. The dual unit ball
B_{x*}
is the dual polytope to the polyhedron
B_{x}
.

Thurston’s Inequality establishes that the Euler class of a taut foliation has dual Thurston norm at most one. Furthermore, it has norm one if the foliation has a compact leaf of negative Euler characteristic. Gabai showed that the converse is true for vertices of the dual Thurston ball. A proof of the following result can be found in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

[
9
]
Let
M
be a compact oriented irreducible
3
-manifold, possibly with toroidal boundary, and let
\beta\in H^{2}(M,\partial M)
be a vertex of the dual unit ball. There is a groomed sutured manifold hierarchy for
(M,\partial M)
such that any carried taut foliation has relative Euler class
\beta
.

This theorem makes precise the statement that finitely many taut sutured manifold hierarchies are enough to recover the Thurston norm of a manifold.

Remark 4.6
.

Suppose
S\subset M
is algebraically fully marked with respect to a taut foliation
\mathcal{F}
, and
\chi(S)<0
. Then equality

\bigg\langle e(\mathcal{F}),\frac{[S]}{\chi(S)}\bigg\rangle=1

guarantees that the face of the dual unit ball
B_{x*}
containing
-e(\mathcal{F})
is dual to the face of
B_{x}
containing
\frac{[S]}{x([S])}
.

This gives insight into when an integral homology class
\alpha
lies inside a
k
-dimensional cone over the Thurston norm ball. Indeed, if
\alpha
lies in the interior of a top-dimensional cone, all taut foliations
\mathcal{F}
satisfying
x(\alpha)=-\langle e(\mathcal{F}),\alpha\rangle
have the same Euler class (i.e., the class of the dual vertex). Conversely, suppose
\alpha
is a corner of the Thurston norm. In that case,
x(\alpha)=-\langle e(\mathcal{F}),\alpha\rangle
holds for every taut foliation whose minus Euler class is dual to a top-dimensional cone adjacent to
\alpha
.

5.
The maw dual graph construction

In the present section, we review the maw dual graph construction and prove Lemma
5.6
, as well as Corollary
5.7
.

Let
M
be a compact oriented
3
-manifold whose boundary is a (possibly empty) union of tori. Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a taut foliation carried by a cooriented branched surface
\mathcal{B}
whose exterior is a union of balls (e.g., if
\mathcal{B}
is constructed via a complete groomed sutured manifold hierarchy for

). The

\Gamma_{m}(\mathcal{B})
is an embedded simplicial
1

representing the Poincaré dual of the relative Euler class
e(\mathcal{F})\in H^{2}(M,\partial M)
. It can be constructed by suitably weighting the edges of the graph dual to the branched surface
\mathcal{B}
. We now review this construction starting from the system of weights: the maw Euler characteristics of the sectors.

Definition 5.1

(Maw Euler characteristic)
.

Let
s
be a sector of a branched surface
\mathcal{B}
and let
v
be a vertex on
\partial s
, i.e.,
v
is either a triple point on
\partial s
or an extreme of

6.
An approach for computing the Thurston norm

Thurston’s Inequality makes Corollary
5.4
relevant for getting information on the Thurston unit ball of a manifold. In what follows, consider the case of a compact oriented irreducible
3
-manifold
M
with incompressible toroidal boundary (e.g. the exterior of a nonsplit link in the
3
-sphere).

Here is an approach for computing the Thurston norm of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
by means of sutured manifold hierarchies:

(1)

Start by a surface
S
that we believe should be taut.

(2)

If
S
is indeed taut, we can find a complete groomed sutured manifold hierarchy
\mathcal{H}
for
(M,\partial M)
starting with a decomposition along
S
, thanks to Theorem
3.5
.

(3)

Reorganise the data of
\mathcal{H}
into a branched surface
\mathcal{B}
and compute the homology class of the maw dual graph
[\Gamma_{m}(\mathcal{B})]\in H_{1}(M)
. The Thurston unit ball of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
is contained in the strip
\{\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)\,|\;|\langle\alpha,[\Gamma_{m}(\mathcal{B})]\rangle|\leq 1\}
. This is because of Thurston’s Inequality (Theorem
4.1
) and Corollary
5.4
.

(4)

Focus on other classes
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k}\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
for which we know surface representatives. Thurston’s Inequality gives a lower bound
x(\alpha_{i})\geq|\langle\alpha_{i},[\Gamma_{m}(\mathcal{B})]\rangle|
for each
i=1,..,k
. If we manage to find surfaces
S_{1},...,S_{k}
representing
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k}
respectively and each satisfying
\chi([S_{i}])=\langle S_{i},\Gamma_{m}(\mathcal{B})\rangle
, then they are norm-minimising. Moreover, the positive cone spanned by the classes
[S],\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k}
in
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
is contained in a closed cone of the Thurston norm by Corollaries
4.4
, and
5.4
.

(5)

Iterate the process above by constructing new hierarchies starting with a decomposition along
S
or
S_{i}
, for some
i
. For instance, a new sutured manifold hierarchy can be constructed by reversing the orientation of a decomposing disk in the last decomposition of
\mathcal{H}
. The Euler class of this modified hierarchy can then be easily computed by means of Lemma
5.6
.

Provided we know “enough surfaces” in
M
, the above strategy can be repeated until one finds enough cones to cover all
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
, hence fully recovering the Thurston norm.
In the case of link exteriors in

, we can often find many surfaces and try to run the method above. In the case of pretzel links with three components, we obtain:

7.
The wrapping number does not satisfy the triangle inequality

Let
M
be a compact oriented
3
-manifold and let
K\subset M
be a knot. Given an integral homology class
\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
, the
wrapping number
of
\alpha
with respect to
K
is defined as

\wrap_{K}(\alpha):=\min_{[S]=\alpha}|S\cap K|,

where the minimum is taken over all norm-minimising incompressible representatives
S
of
\alpha
that intersect
K
transversely. The function
\wrap_{K}
is nonnegative and satisfies
\wrap_{K}(n\alpha)=|n|\wrap_{K}(\alpha)
for every
n\in\Z
and
\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
. Therefore, it can be extended linearly to a function
\wrap_{K}:H_{2}(M,\partial M;\Q)\to\Q
. If
\wrap_{K}
satisfied the triangle inequality, then it could be continuously extended to a seminorm on
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\R)
. We notice that this is the case within a closed cone of the Thurston norm:

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
M
be a compact oriented
3
-manifold. Let
\alpha
and
\beta\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
be classes that belong to the same closed top-dimensional cone of the Thurston norm. If
x(\alpha)
and
x(\beta)
do not vanish, then

\wrap_{K}(\alpha+\beta)\leq\wrap_{K}(\alpha)+\wrap_{K}(\beta)

for any knot
K\subset M
.

Proof.

Let
S
and
S^{\prime}
be norm-minimising incompressible representatives realising the wrapping numbers
\wrap_{K}(\alpha)
and
\wrap_{K}(\beta)
respectively. Since
x(\alpha)
and
x(\beta)
are nonzero, the surfaces
S
and
S^{\prime}
are also
\partial
-incompressible. This is because a
\partial
-compression does not change the homology class of the surface, but increases its Euler characteristic by one. In particular,
S
and
S^{\prime}
can be isotoped so that each component of
S\cap S^{\prime}
is essential in both
S
and
S^{\prime}
. Therefore, the oriented cut and paste
\hat{S}
of
S
and
S^{\prime}
satisfies

\chi_{-}(\hat{S})=\chi_{-}(S)+\chi_{-}(S^{\prime})

and is norm-minimising. Since
x(\alpha+\beta)=x(\alpha)+x(\beta)>0,
the surface
\hat{S}
is also incompressible. Indeed, if
\hat{S}
was compressible, after a nontrivial compression we would find a representative of
\alpha+\beta
with smaller
\chi_{-}
than
\hat{S}
, contradicting the minimality of
\hat{S}
.

After possibly an isotopy,
K
does not pass through a small neighbourhood of
S\cap S^{\prime}
. We obtain

|\hat{S}\cap K|=|S\cap K|+|S^{\prime}\cap K|=\wrap_{K}(\alpha)+\wrap_{K}(\beta),

which concludes the proof.
∎

In
[
1
, Question 2.1]
, Baker and Taylor asked whether
\wrap_{K}
satisfies the triangle inequality in general, suggesting that the answer is likely negative. The previous computations on pretzel links now confirm this suspicion.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
L_{0}\subset S^{3}
be a torus link
T(2,2c)
, for some

, and let
M_{0}:=M_{L_{0}}
be its exterior. There exist infinitely many knots
K\subset M_{0}
such that
\wrap_{K}
does not satisfy the triangle inequality in
H_{2}(M_{0},\partial M_{0})
.

In order to prove this theorem, we will need a criterion for computing the wrapping number of a homology class. The following lemma will serve this purpose.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
M
be a compact oriented irreducible and
\partial
-irreducible
3
-manifold. Let
K\subset M
be a knot,
M(K):=M-\overset{\circ}{N}(K)
its exterior, and
S
a norm-minimising incompressible surface in
M
transverse to
K
. If
S\cap M(K)
is norm-minimising in
M(K)
, then

\wrap_{K}([S])=x_{M(K)}([S\cap M(K)])-x_{M}([S]),

where
x_{M(K)}
and
x_{M}
indicate the Thurston norms on
H_{2}(M(K),\partial M(K))
and
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
respectively.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=[S]\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
and let
T
be any norm-minimising incompressible representative of
\alpha
. If
T
intersects
K
transversely,

(4)

|T\cap K|=\chi(T)-\chi(T\cap M(K)).

If
T
has no sphere nor disk components, we can rewrite identity (
4
) as

(5)

|T\cap K|=\chi_{-}(T\cap M(K))-\chi_{-}(T)=\chi_{-}(T\cap M(K))-x_{M}(\alpha).

Now, notice that the homology class of
T\cap M(K)
is completely determined by
\alpha
. Indeed,
T\cap M(K)
represents the Poincaré dual of the cohomology class
\alpha_{K}:=\iota^{*}(\operatorname{PD}(\alpha))\in H^{1}(M(K))
, where
\iota:M(K)\to M
is the inclusion and
\operatorname{PD}
is the Poincaré duality map. Hence, the identities in (
5
) imply:

(6)

|T\cap K|\geq x_{M(K)}(\alpha_{K})-x_{M}(\alpha),

where equality holds if
T\cap M(K)
is a norm-minimising representative of
\alpha_{K}
without sphere or disk components.

Notice that if
T
had sphere or disk components instead, these would be zero in homology because
M
is irreducible and
\partial
-irreducible. Moreover, removing these components from
T
would not increase the number of intersections with
K
. We conclude that inequality (
6
) holds for every norm-minimising incompressible representative of
\alpha
. Since the RHS of inequality (
6
) does not depend on the chosen norm-minimising incompressible representative
T
of
\alpha
, we obtain a lower bound for the wrapping number of
\alpha
:

\wrap_{K}(\alpha)\geq x_{M(K)}(\alpha_{K})-x_{M}(\alpha).

Let
S^{\prime}
be the surface
S
after discarding its disk or sphere components. Since
S^{\prime}\cap M(K)
is a subset of components of
S\cap M(K)
, we have

\chi_{-}(S^{\prime}\cap M(K))\leq\chi_{-}(S\cap M(K)).

As
S\cap M(K)
is norm-minimising in
M(K)
, the same holds for
