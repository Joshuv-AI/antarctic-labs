Fabrication and characterization of magnetic trimetallic mesoporous nanocomposite: as an effective nanocatalyst for synthesis of bisphenol derivatives
Authors: Mahtab Diba, Hossein Naeimi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hollow materials have designed with shell structures and porous cavities, as a new generation of functional materials, have shown remarkable performance in various fields. In this study, a magnetic, porous, and reusable nanocatalyst was designed and prepared. The CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst was prepared through a multistep process. Initially, the magnetic trimetallic CoNiFe2O4 was prepared and followed by the deposition of a Silicon dioxide (SiO2) layer to form the CoNiFe2O4@SiO2 yolk–shell structure. The subsequent removal of the silica layer is resulted in the formation of the CoNiFe2O4@C yolk-shell magnetic nanocomposite. In this protocol, bisphenol derivatives were synthesized through a three-component reaction of aldehyde and 2–4-dimethylphenol in a ratio of 1:2 by using this prepared catalyst. The applications of bisphenols are included antioxidant, anticancer, anti-arthrosis, anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties. The identification of the prepared catalyst was carried out and reported using Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR), X-ray diffraction (XRD), Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET), Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), Mapping, Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM), Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and Vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM) techniques. Also, the bisphenol products were identified using melting point, FT-IR, proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR) and carbon-13 nuclear magnetic resonance (13C NMR) analyses. The proposed approach presents multiple benefits such as; mild reaction conditions, ease of operation, reliable safety, excellent yield, effective purification of synthetic organic compounds, and minimal catalyst usage.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-57572-5.

Introduction

Catalytic systems are used in a wide range of chemical reactions because, in addition to increasing the rate of reactions, they direct the reactions towards the production of specific products1,2. Hollow materials designed with porous shells and voids represent a class of functional materials that has sparked a boom in research in many scientific fields3. In particular, the critical importance of this class of advanced functional materials to the primary operating units is important for the purpose of technological applications, from medicine to analytics4. Hollow nanostructures can be classified based on various factors, including structural and compositional complexity, the number and arrangement of shells, the organization of pores and cavities, the distribution of interior and wall spaces, overall morphology, and the number of material phases present5,6. In the field of heterogeneous catalysis, new catalysts with hierarchical porosity and hollow or uncovered internal spaces have advanced rapidly in recent years and have unique properties that are not found in conventional catalysts7. The hollow material supports its three-dimensional structure, which increases the resistance to prevent the aggregation and degradation of reaction sites 8–10. The channels in the shell allow for rapid mass transfer of reactants and products into and out of the void space5,11. Recently, nanoparticle technology and nanoscale materials, especially magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs), have attracted much attention and have shown their applications in research and industrial fields, especially in the chemical, medical, and environmental sectors 12–15. For example, the use of magnetic nanoparticles in the removal of pollutants or reduction of toxicity is promising16. The yolk-shell structure is an efficient and interesting method that has been intensively investigated17. Catalysts prepared by the yolk-shell method have two layers, including an inner layer and an outer layer 18–20. The outer layer acts as a protector for the active metals in the core and prevents their movement and sintering in reactions carried out at high temperatures21,22. The yolk-shell nanocatalysts are of great importance at the molecular level, especially in the design of more complex nanostructures with multiple functionalities 23–25. Various materials such as zeolites, metal oxides, metal–organic frameworks (MOFs), and carbons can be designed to fabricate shells and control the shell thickness, shape, size, and porosity26,27. Shell porosity, pore size, and shell thickness can be tuned to achieve the desired size and shape selection 28–33. The materials of both core and shell parts can be designed to achieve the best synergistic effect in various ways34.

The suitable methods for preparing novel organic compounds that can be used in various fields are the use of multicomponent reactions. The multicomponent reactions are a type of chemical reaction in which more than two starting materials react together to produce a single product. These reactions provide simple and rapid conditions for obtaining a large set of organic compounds. Among the important applications of reactions in the organic chemistry is the possibility of synthesizing new compounds with biological and pharmaceutical properties that can be investigated and produced35. The bisphenol compounds, especially polyphenols, have attracted much attention due to their positive health effects36,37. There is more than one hydroxyl group in the structure of these compounds38. The properties of bisphenols include anticancer, antioxidant, testosterone blocking, anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties of these compounds39. Also, in many natural products, drugs, catalysts have become the focus of intense research. For example, the use of these compounds in the manufacture of sunscreens can be mentioned, as the aromatic ring in these compounds effectively absorbs sunlight rays in the range of 280–315 nm. Given the practicality of these compounds and the importance of bisphenol antioxidants, there is a great desire to synthesize these compounds catalyzed by various homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysts40.

In this study, we hope to synthesize bisphenol derivatives via a single-step three-component reaction involving aldehyde and 2,4-dimethylphenol. This reaction was carried out in the presence of CoNiFe2O4@C as a solid acid catalyst and under solvent-free conditions. The final products were isolated by a simple method, in high purity, and characterized using melting point, FT-IR, 1H NMR and 13C NMR analyses. The CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst was employed for the first time in the synthesis of bisphenols, demonstrating remarkable efficiency that facilitated the formation of several novel products, highlighting its potential as a versatile catalyst for the development of new functional materials.

Experimental

Materials and apparatus

All chemicals, including 2,4-dimethylphenol and aromatic and aliphatic aldehyde derivatives, solvents as well as various metal salts were purchased from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany) and Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA) and used without further purification.

FT-IR spectra were recorded in the 4000–400 cm-1 range using KBr pellets on a Magna 550 FT-IR spectrometer. 1H NMR and 13C NMR spectra were acquired in DMSO-d6 on a Broker Avance-400 MHz instrument, with tetramethylsilane (TMS) as an internal standard. The reaction progress was monitored using thin-layer chromatography (TLC) on silica gel 60 F254 plates. Melting points were determined on a Yanagimoto micro melting point apparatus and are uncorrected. Ultra sonication was carried out using a BANDELIN HD 3200 ultrasonic probe (model KE76). Magnetic measurements were performed at 300 K using a vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM) at the University of Kashan. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were collected on a Philips X’Pert Pro diffractometer using Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.54056 Å) over a 2θ range of 10–80°. The specific surface area and porosity were determined using a BELSORP-mini II surface analyzer (BEL Japan). Elemental analysis was performed by energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) with an Oxford instrument was conducted using a STA 504 instrument at the University of Kashan. Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, MIRA3) operated at 15 kV was used to investigate the morphology of the nanoparticles. Additionally, the TEM images were obtained using a [Zeiss] TEM at [100] kV.

A general and practical method for the preparation of CoNiFe2O4@C magnetic nanocatalysts

Preparation of 3-metal sphere surrounded by a layer of SiO2

In this synthesis, CoCl2.6H2O (1 mmol), NiCl2.6H2O (1 mmol), and FeCl2.6H2O (4 mmol) were completely dissolved in 250 mL of deionized water. To achieve uniform dispersion of nanoparticles and prevent their aggregation, the resulting solution was subjected to ultrasonic irradiation for 20 min, while stirring maintained homogeneous mixing during the addition of other reactants. Subsequently, 100 mL of deionized water was added, and ethylenediamine gradually added drop wise until the solution reached pH 9. The reaction mixture was then stirred under reflux at 85 °C for 5 h. The formed precipitate was separated by centrifugation, washed several times with deionized water and ethanol, and dried at 60 °C for 24 h. Finally, the dried precipitate was calcined at 900 °C to obtain the trimetallic nanoparticle CoNiFe2O4. Subsequently 0.3 g of CoNiFe2O4 nanoparticle were dispersed in 20 mL of ethanol, followed by the addition of 4 mL water. To ensure uniform distribution of the nanoparticles, the mixture was subjected to ultrasonic irradiation for 10 min while being mechanical stirred under a nitrogen atmosphere. Next, 1 mL of tetraethyl orthosilicate (TEOS, 4.5 mmol) and 2 mL of aqueous ammonia solution (25 wt %) were diluted in 20 mL of deionized water and gradually added to the reaction mixture. After complete addition, the mixture was stirred mechanically stirred at room temperature for 3 h. The resulting CoNiFe2O4@SiO2 was separated using an external magnet and the obtained product was washed three times with distilled water and three times with ethanol, followed by centrifugation at 5500 rpm for 5 min during each washing step. Finally, the CoNiFe2O4@SiO2 was dried at 70 °C for 8 h to obtain the final product.

Synthesis of yolk-shell nanocatalyst CoNiFe2O4@C

The CoNiFe2O4@C nanocomposite is a porous, magnetic, and acid-functional nanocatalyst. In the previous step, a silicon dioxide (SiO2) layer was coated on the trimetallic CoNiFe2O4 nanoparticle to protect the core. Subsequently, 0.3 g of the prepared CoNiFe2O4@SiO2 was dispersed in 28 mL of ethanol and 70 mL of deionized water. The mixture was sonicated for 15 min to ensure uniform dispersion. Then, cetyl trimethyl ammonium bromide (CTAB, 2.3 g, 6.3 mmol), resorcinol (0.35 g, 3.2 mmol), and aqueous ammonia (0.1 mL, 25 wt %) were added to the reaction mixture. Subsequently, formaldehyde solution (0.5 mL, 37 wt %) was introduced, and the reaction mixture was mechanically stirred at 60 °C for 18 h to obtain CoNiFe2O4@SiO2@C. The resorcinol–formaldehyde method provides an efficient approach for the formation of a porous carbon shell. Finally, the obtained CoNiFe2O4@SiO2@C composite was calcined at 900 °C under nitrogen-argon atmosphere to produce the yolk-shell nanostructure. The SiO2 layer was then removed by refluxing in sodium hydroxide (NaOH) 0.2 mol/L solution, yielding the final yolk-shell nanocatalyst CoNiFe2O4@C.

A general and convenient method for the synthesis of bisphenol derivatives using CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst

The method used for the synthesis of bisphenols is as follows: For the reaction of bisphenol derivatives from aldehyde (2.0 mmol) and 2,4-dimethylphenol (6.0 mmol), this reaction was carried out using CoNiFe2O4@C solid acid nanocatalyst. Also, In this synthetic method, the aldehyde was reacted with 2,4-dimethylphenol at 100 °C as an optimized reaction temperature in an oil bath without solvent. Reaction progress was monitored by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) using silica gel plates and visualized under UV light. The synthesized product exhibited an Rf value of 0.48 using petroleum ether/ethyl acetate (7:3) as the eluent system. However, finally, after the time elapsed and the reaction was completed, the reaction product reached at ambient temperature, the reaction product was crystallized using two solvents, water and ethanol, both at boiling temperature, and after the precipitation of the product, it was extracted with n-hexane in high purity. Finally, the sediment was dry at 90 °C for 24 h and the final construction of bisphenol derivatives was approved using FT-IR, 1H NMR, 13C NMR and melting point analyses.

Results and discussion

Preparation and characterization of nanocatalyst CoNiFe2O4@C

A practical method was developed for the synthesis of the acidic, porous, and magnetic nanocatalyst CoNiFe2O4@C. In the first step, the CoNiFe2O4 core was synthesized using NiCl2.6H2O (1 mmol), CoCl2·6H2O (1 mmol), and FeCl2·6H2O (4 mmol), while ethylenediamine was employed to adjust the pH to 9. In the second step, CoNiFe2O4@SiO2 was prepared by adding 0.3 g of CoNiFe2O4 ferrite to a reaction mixture containing ammonia and tetraethyl orthosilicate under a nitrogen atmosphere. In the third step, CoNiFe2O4@SiO2@RF was prepared. In this stage, 0.3 g of CoNiFe2O4@SiO2 was mixed with trimethylammonium bromide, resorcinol, formaldehyde, and ammonia. The formaldehyde plays a crucial role as a cross-linking and carbon-source agent during the resorcinol–formaldehyde polymerization process. It reacts with resorcinol to form a three-dimensional phenolic resin network around the CoNiFe2O4@SiO2 structure through methylene bridge formation. Upon carbonization at 900 °C under an inert atmosphere, this polymeric layer is transformed into a stable porous carbon shell, leading to the formation of the yolk-shell CoNiFe2O4@C nanostructure.

Finally, the silica layer was removed by refluxing in NaOH solution, resulting in the formation of the acidic, porous, and magnetic nanocatalyst CoNiFe2O4@C (Fig. 1). The catalytic activity of CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst is mainly associated with its intrinsic Lewis acidic nature. The spinel ferrite structure contains transition metal cations including Co2+, Ni2+, and Fe3+, which act as strong Lewis acid centers due to their electron-deficient character. These metal ions can effectively accept electron density from reactant molecules, thereby facilitating their activation and enhancing the reaction rate. The presence of coordinately unsaturated metal sites on the surface further strengthens the overall acidity of the catalyst. Although the carbon coating may provide minor surface functional groups, the catalytic behavior is predominantly governed by the Lewis acidic sites of the metal oxide core. Therefore, CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst exhibits a clear and dominant Lewis acidic character, which is responsible for its excellent catalytic performance.Fig. 1Schematic illustration of the preparation steps of CoNiFe2O4@C.

Schematic illustration of the preparation steps of CoNiFe2O4@C.

The CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst was characterized using a combination of techniques, including FT-IR, XRD, BET, FE-SEM, TEM, EDS, elemental mapping, and VSM.

The FT-IR spectra of the synthesized nanocatalysts at different stages, including pristine CoNiFe2O4 (a), CoNiFe2O4@SiO2 (b), CoNiFe2O4@SiO2@C (c), and the final CoNiFe2O4@C sample after silica removal (d), are presented in Fig. 2 within the wavenumber range of 400–4000 cm-1. For the bare CoNiFe2O4 sample (spectrum a), two characteristic absorption bands are observed at 468 and 574 cm-1, which are attributed to the stretching vibrations of metal–oxygen (M–O) bonds at the tetrahedral and octahedral sites, respectively. These bands are typical features of the inverse spinel ferrite structure. The persistence of these characteristic peaks throughout all synthetic stages confirms that the crystalline structure of the magnetic core remains preserved during the subsequent coating and silica removal processes.Fig. 2FT-IR spectra of (a) CoNiFe2O4, (b) CoNiFe2O4@SiO2, (c) CoNiFe2O4@SiO2@C and (d) CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst.

FT-IR spectra of (a) CoNiFe2O4, (b) CoNiFe2O4@SiO2, (c) CoNiFe2O4@SiO2@C and (d) CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst.

After silica coating in CoNiFe2O4@SiO2 (spectrum b), a significant increase in the intensity of the broad absorption band around 3431 cm-1 and the peak at 1619 cm-1 is observed. These bands correspond to the stretching and bending vibrations of hydroxyl groups (O–H) and adsorbed water molecules, respectively, which are enhanced due to the presence of surface silanol (Si–OH) groups. In addition, the slight blue shift of the tetrahedral M–O vibration band from 574 to 584 cm-1 suggests strong interfacial interactions between the ferrite core and the SiO2 shell.

The FT-IR spectra of samples (c) and (d) exhibit distinct absorption bands at 2920–2923 cm-1, corresponding to the symmetric and asymmetric stretching vibrations of C-H groups. Moreover, the appearance of peaks at 1596 cm-1 for spectrum (c) and 1597 cm-1 for spectrum (d) is attributed to the aromatic C = C stretching vibrations, confirming the formation of the carbon layer. In addition, the absorption bands located at 1480 and 1442 cm-1 are associated with the bending vibrations of methylene groups within the carbonaceous framework. Following the removal of the silica template through NaOH reflux treatment (spectrum d), several important observations further confirm the successful formation of the CoNiFe2O4@C yolk-shell structure. The decreased intensity of the O–H absorption band at 3434 cm-1 compared with spectrum (b) indicates the effective removal of the silica layer and its associated silanol groups. In addition, the characteristic M–O stretching vibrations reappear clearly at 587 and 470 cm-1, demonstrating that the NaOH reflux process does not damage the spinel ferrite lattice. The slight variation in the transmittance intensity of the core-related vibrations in the final sample, compared with pristine CoNiFe2O4, can be attributed to the shielding effect of the surrounding carbon shell.

Overall, the gradual changes in peak position and functional group intensity throughout the four spectra provide strong evidence for the successful stepwise functionalization of the nanostructure. The transition from the silica-coated intermediate to the carbon-encapsulated ferrite core is confirmed by the simultaneous presence of characteristic M–O, hydroxyl-related, and carbon-associated vibrational bands, indicating the successful synthesis of the final magnetic carbon-coated nanocatalyst.

Investigating the reusability of the catalyst

After the reaction, in order to investigate the recyclability and reusability of the nanocatalyst, this magnetic nanocatalyst was easily separated from the reaction mixture by an external magnet. Then, in order to completely remove impurities and residual materials from its surface, the nanocatalyst was washed twice with ethanol. The washed sample was placed in an oven at 110 °C for 3 h to dry completely. After this step, XRD analysis was performed on the dried sample to ensure the preservation of the crystalline structure and phase stability of the nanocatalyst. The results of the XRD analysis did not show any significant changes compared to the original sample, which indicates the favorable structural stability of the nanocatalyst under reaction conditions. The studies conducted showed that this nanocatalyst can be reused up to six times without a noticeable decrease in performance. Also, the studies demonstrated that after each washing and recycling step, only a negligible decrease in the amount of nanocatalyst was observed. The catalyst mass loss was approximately 2% after the first cycle, around 4% during the third to fifth cycles, and reached about 5% in the final cycle. These results confirm the high stability and excellent recyclability of the synthesized nanocatalyst during consecutive catalytic runs. Therefore, the study conducted on the CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst shows that washing did not have any negative effect on the quality of the nanocatalyst.

Conclusion

In this research, the fabricated nanocatalyst is a reusable magnetic nanocatalyst and also the CoNiFe2O4@C nanocatalyst has an easy and practical manufacturing method. It is also an acidic catalyst that causes rapid progress of acidic reactions. This nanocatalyst is in the form of a yolk-shell, and the presence of holes in this catalyst increases the contact surface of the catalyst with the reaction mixture, which speeds up and completes the reaction. Different derivatives of bisphenols have been synthesized using the CoNiFe2O4@C catalyst. The method used in this research has many advantages, including increasing the reaction efficiency, accelerating the reaction, and being an easy and environmentally friendly purification method.
