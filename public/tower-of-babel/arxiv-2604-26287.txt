Newton–Cartan limit of Klein–Gordon AQFT and the collapse of Galilean modular structure
Leonardo A. Pachón

Abstract
We extend a previously established Galilean/relativistic structural divider in algebraic quantum field theory — the absence of Reeh–Schlieder and of Tomita–Takesaki modular flow on local algebras of any Galilean Haag–Kastler net satisfying a natural axiom set augmented by the Bargmann-charge hypotheses (G7 ∗ )(a) and (G7 ∗ )(d) Pachón 2026a — to curved backgrounds via the Newton–Cartan ( c → ∞ c\to\infty ) limit. We show, for the free Klein–Gordon field on Minkowski and on static globally hyperbolic spacetimes admitting a Post-Newtonian expansion, that a position-independent rest-energy rescaling produces in the limit a Galilean Haag–Kastler net satisfying the axioms of Ref. Pachón 2026a in flat-space form (Minkowski) or in a curved-space modification (Killing-flow invariance and uniqueness of the vacuum replacing full translation invariance) appropriate to the static case. The Bargmann central charge equals the Klein–Gordon mass m m ; the gravitational potential V ⁡ ( x ) V(x) enters the limiting Schrödinger Hamiltonian but not the algebraic structure obstructed by the Galilean Reeh–Schlieder no-go theorem. The strengthened obstruction theorem of Ref. Pachón 2026a extends to the modified curved-space setting on Fock representations, and the limiting net carries no modular flow on local algebras. Schwarzschild is treated as a worked example: the Killing horizon shrinks to a point, the Hartle–Hawking thermal state has no c → ∞ c\to\infty limit, and the Boulware vacuum limits to the gravitational hydrogenic ground state. The Reissner–Nordström metric is included as a sanity check confirming that leading Post-Newtonian misses the electromagnetic content of the background. We discuss how Newton’s constant G G enters the present (background-metric) framework only at the level of the limiting Hamiltonian, and indicate where dynamical-metric extensions would require G G to play a structural role.

non-trivial KMS structure with respect to boosts. This collapse is

interpretation in the relativistic theory is undefined on the
Galilean side, because the vacuum is not separating for any local
algebra.

Structural conclusion.

The Unruh effect and the Hawking effect are both modular-flow
content of relativistic AQFT — one on Rindler wedges in
Minkowski, the other on wedge-like regions adapted to bifurcate
Killing horizons in Schwarzschild. Both collapse in the

two thermodynamic effects vanish through the same algebraic
obstruction.

VII.4
Forward path: dynamical-metric extension

A natural follow-up to the present paper is to lift the
background-metric setting to a dynamical-metric one, and to ask

The technical demands of the dynamical-metric setting — locally

VIII.5
The Galilean Hadamard condition

The relativistic Hadamard condition characterises the
universality-class of physical states on a globally hyperbolic
spacetime by their leading short-distance singularity, which for a
free scalar field of mass
m
takes the
form
Kay and Wald 1991
;
Wald 1994

W^{\mathrm{rel}}(x,x^{\prime})\sim\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\sigma(x,x^{\prime})}+V\log\sigma+W,

(41)

where
\sigma(x,x^{\prime})
is half the squared geodesic distance and
V,W
are smooth functions. The form of (

explicitly on the Lorentzian metric through
\sigma

direct counterpart on a Newton–Cartan structure where the
Lorentzian metric has degenerated to the pair
(h^{ab},\tau_{a})
with

h^{ab}\tau_{b}=0

What
is
well-defined on the Galilean side is the
short-distance structure of the limiting two-point function
(
15

(42)

This expression has three structural features that distinguish it
sharply from (
41

•

Anisotropic scaling.
The singularity occurs only as

t\to t^{\prime}
, with
x-x^{\prime}
scaling as
(t-t^{\prime})^{1/2}
in the natural
Schrödinger heat-kernel scaling. This anisotropy in the

(t,x)

structure: there is no boost-invariant interval
\sigma(x,x^{\prime})
, and
the singularity is one-dimensional rather than codimension-one.

•

Heat-kernel power.
The

the heat-kernel exponent in three spatial dimensions, replacing the

substantially milder than the relativistic Hadamard singularity

•

Equal-time delta.
At equal times

(

Fock vacuum (recovering the c-number CCR (G4)), which is the
standard equal-time anomalous singularity of free Schrödinger

on the diagonal.

This condition is the appropriate Galilean analogue of the
relativistic Hadamard condition: it characterises a state by the
universal heat-kernel-type singularity structure of its two-point

determined by the
geometry through the Gilkey–Seeley
expansion
Gilkey 1995
of the heat kernel of
\hat{H}_{S}=-(\hbar^{2}/2m)\Delta_{h}+V

\tau=\mathrm{i}(t-t^{\prime})/\hbar

\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\hat{H}_{S}(t-t^{\prime})/\hbar}

\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{H}_{S}\tau}

\hat{H}_{S}

Proposition 2

(Limiting Boulware state is Galilean Hadamard)

Proof.

The two-point function on the Schrödinger Fock vacuum is

heat kernel
(\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{H}_{S}\tau})(x,x^{\prime})

a_{0}\equiv 1

Remark 3

(Status of the singular point)
.

At
r=0
the gravitational potential
V(r)=-GMm/r
is
unbounded, the Coulomb-type singularity becoming the source of the
gravitational hydrogenic spectrum (
29
). The
heat-kernel Seeley expansion of
Proposition
2
is uniformly valid only on
compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
, since the Seeley
coefficients
a_{k}
involve increasing powers of
V
and its
derivatives, which fail to be locally bounded near
r=0
. The
Galilean Hadamard condition consequently holds on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{\delta}(0)
for any
\delta>0
but fails uniformly on
neighbourhoods of
r=0
. This is the location where the Killing
horizon at
r_{\mathrm{s}}=2GM/c^{2}
has been concentrated by the

c\to\infty
limit (Section
VI.6
); the relativistic
near-horizon AQFT content has collapsed to a strong-coupling
singularity of the Newtonian potential, which lies outside the
regime of validity of any short-distance asymptotic expansion that
treats
V
perturbatively.

Definition
1
can serve as a state-selection
criterion for Galilean QFT on static Newton–Cartan structures
parallel to the relativistic Hadamard condition: the state space
is the set of states satisfying (
43
),
and Wick polynomials of
\hat{\psi},\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}
are defined
on this state space by point-splitting renormalisation against
the heat-kernel form. The full development of these consequences
parallel to the Brunetti–Fredenhagen–Verch programme on the
relativistic side
Brunetti, Fredenhagen, and Verch 2003
is beyond the scope of the
present paper, but the foundational structural element — the
correct universal short-distance kernel — is identified by
Definition
1
and verified for the limit
state in Proposition
2
.

The structural conclusion: the relativistic Hadamard condition does
not survive the
c\to\infty
limit, but it is replaced on the
Galilean side by Definition
1
, which is
the same kind of short-distance constraint — a leading-order
match with a universal kernel determined by the geometry —
expressed in the appropriate non-Lorentzian-geometric form.
