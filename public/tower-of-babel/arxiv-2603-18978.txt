On Affordable High-Order Entropy-Conservative/Stable and Well-Balanced Methods for Nonconservative Hyperbolic Systems
Marco Artiano, Hendrik Ranocha

Abstract
Many entropy-conservative and entropy-stable (summarized as entropy-preserving) methods for hyperbolic conservation laws rely on Tadmor’s theory for two-point entropy-preserving numerical fluxes and its higher-order extension via flux differencing using summation-by-parts (SBP) operators, e.g., in discontinuous Galerkin spectral element methods (DGSEMs). The underlying two-point formulations have been extended to nonconservative systems using fluctuations by Castro et al. (2013, doi:10.1137/110845379 ) with follow-up generalizations to SBP methods. We propose specific forms of entropy-preserving fluctuations for nonconservative hyperbolic systems that are simple to interpret and allow an algorithmic construction of entropy-preserving methods. We analyze necessary and sufficient conditions, and obtain a full characterization of entropy-preserving three-point methods within the finite volume framework. This formulation is extended to SBP methods in multiple space dimensions on Cartesian and curvilinear meshes. Additional properties such as well-balancedness extend naturally from the underlying finite volume method to the SBP framework. We use the algorithmic construction enabled by the chosen formulation to derive several new entropy-preserving schemes for nonconservative hyperbolic systems, e.g., the compressible Euler equations of an ideal gas using the internal energy equation and a dispersive shallow-water model. Numerical experiments show the robustness and accuracy of the proposed schemes.

This example indicates that the choice of paths is not crucial in determining which solutions are approximated by the scheme.
Instead, the numerical viscosity operator (that matches with the underlying viscosity) decides which weak solution the scheme will converge to.

This is fortunate, since the correct choice of paths is often far from trivial, and many works have thus resorted to the default choice of a straight line path.
This motivates our current manuscript.
By focusing on the entropy, we first propose concrete forms of fluctuations that are easy to interpret and amenable to the algorithmic construction of affordable entropy-conserving methods, without the need to evaluate any integrals (including integrals related to choosing a path).
We provide both necessary and sufficient conditions for entropy conservation and stability, and demonstrate how to use them to construct entropy-preserving methods for several hyperbolic systems.
Moreover, we extend the results to high-order SBP methods on curved meshes in multiple dimensions.
Furthermore, we demonstrate how well-balanced three-point finite volume schemes extend to well-balanced high-order SBP methods on curved meshes.

In contrast to works on path-conservative methods for nonconservative systems, we consider the general form

Moreover, we do not only provide sufficient conditions, but also study their necessity in a more general context without requiring convexity of the entropy.
In fact, while Tadmor’s integral form of an EC flux requires a convex entropy (to obtain the entropy variables

This is essential and has not been appreciated sufficiently, since the same conditions allow the derivation of general conservation relations, e.g., the conservation of the total energy when working with the potential temperature as thermodynamic state variable for atmospheric flows
[

Entropy-preserving methods for conservation laws are linked to split forms, e.g., kinetic energy-preserving methods

In Section

2
Hyperbolic conservation laws

We consider the hyperbolic conservation law

\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\partial_{x}\bm{f}(\bm{u})=\bm{0},

(
1
)

where
\bm{u}(x,t)\in Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are the conserved quantities,
Y
is an appropriate open set, and
\bm{f}\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the flux, supplemented with appropriate initial and boundary conditions.
Tadmor’s classical theory
[
94
,
95
]
builds on the existence of a convex entropy
U\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}
with associated entropy flux
F\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\forall\bm{u}\in Y\colon\quad U^{\prime}(\bm{u})\cdot\bm{f}^{\prime}(\bm{u})=F^{\prime}(\bm{u}).

(8)

Thus, smooth solutions of (
1
) also satisfy the additional conservation law

\partial_{t}U(\bm{u})+\partial_{x}F(\bm{u})=0,

(9)

and the entropy inequality

\partial_{t}U(\bm{u})+\partial_{x}F(\bm{u})\leq 0

(10)

is used as admissibility criterion for (weak) solutions
[
27
]
.

A three-point finite volume semi-discretization of (
1
) can be written as

\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{i}+\frac{1}{\Delta x_{i}}\Bigl(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}\bigl(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{i+1}\bigr)-\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}\bigl(\bm{u}_{i-1},\bm{u}_{i}\bigr)\Bigr)=\bm{0},

(
2
)

where
\bm{u}_{i}
is the approximate cell-averaged solution in the
i
-th cell,
\Delta x_{i}
is the cell-size, and
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}\colon Y\times Y\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a consistent numerical flux function, i.e.,

\forall\bm{u}\in Y\colon\quad\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u},\bm{u})=\bm{f}(\bm{u}).

(11)

Following Tadmor
[
94
,
95
]
, we are interested in numerical schemes that mimic the entropy equality (
9
) or inequality (
10
) discretely.

Definition 2.1
.

A finite volume scheme like (
2
) is entropy-conservative/stable if there is a numerical entropy flux
F^{\mathrm{num}}\colon Y\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
, with
F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u},\bm{u})=F(\bm{u})
, such that

U^{\prime}(\bm{u}_{i})\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{i}=\bm{\omega}_{i}\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{i}\leq-\frac{1}{\Delta x_{i}}\Bigl(F^{\mathrm{num}}\bigl(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{i+1}\bigr)-F^{\mathrm{num}}\bigl(\bm{u}_{i-1},\bm{u}_{i}\bigr)\Bigr),

(12)

where
\bm{\omega}=U^{\prime}
are the entropy variables and equality has to hold for the entropy-conservative case.

In his seminal work, Tadmor
[
94
,
95
]
has obtained local conditions on numerical fluxes ensuring entropy conservation/stability that have been used ever since as a systematic tool to construct entropy-preserving numerical fluxes
[
48
,
23
,
70
,
98
,
106
,
59
,
5
]
.
Although not written out explicitly in detail, the condition for entropy conservation is also observed to be necessary
[
94
, page 95]
.
However, the original derivation relies on the convexity of the entropy
U
, which guarantees the invertibility of the mapping between conserved variables
\bm{u}
and the entropy variables
\bm{\omega}=U^{\prime}(\bm{u})
.
As a preparation for the extension to nonconservative systems, we will next point out that convexity of the entropy is not required for the characterization of entropy-preserving methods and fluxes.

2.1
Tadmor condition for non-convex functionals

In the following, we introduce some standard operators and notations that will be used throughout the paper. We first define the usual mean and jump operators as

\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}=\frac{a_{+}+a_{-}}{2},\qquad\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket=a_{+}-a_{-},

(13)

where
a_{\pm}
denote the values of
a
at the right/left side of an interface.
For numerical fluxes at a specific interface, we adopt the notation

\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}=\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{i+1})
,
where the first argument of
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})
represents the value at the left of the interface and the second argument represents the value at the right.
To simplify notation further, we also write

g(\bm{u}_{\pm})=g_{\pm}

(14)

for any function
g
, e.g., the flux
\bm{f}
or the entropy variables
\bm{\omega}
.

The characterization of entropy-preserving methods and numerical fluxes ultimately relies only on three key ingredients: the numerical flux
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}
, the entropy variables
\bm{\omega}
, and the entropy flux
F
.
Tadmor’s condition can be expressed as a purely algebraic condition summarized in the following lemma, where we still use the same notation as introduced earlier, but we do not assume any further relationship between the functions
\bm{\omega}
,
\bm{f}
, and
F

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open, and
\bm{\omega}\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\bm{f}\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
F\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}\colon Y\times Y\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\forall\bm{u}\in Y\colon\quad\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u},\bm{u})=\bm{f}(\bm{u})

(15)

be given. Then,

\exists F^{\mathrm{num}}\colon Y\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\forall\bm{u}\in Y\colon F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u},\bm{u})=F(\bm{u})
and

\forall\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+}\in Y\colon\quad\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{0})\cdot\Bigl(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})-\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})\Bigr)\geq F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})-F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})

(16)

if and only if

\forall\bm{u}_{+},\bm{u}_{-}\in Y\colon\quad\left(\bm{\omega}_{+}-\bm{\omega}_{-}\right)\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\leq\left(\bm{\omega}_{+}\cdot\bm{f}_{+}-F_{+}\right)-\left(\bm{\omega}_{-}\cdot\bm{f}_{-}-F_{-}\right).

(17)

Moreover, if one of the conditions is satisfied with equality, then equality holds in the other condition as well, and
F^{\mathrm{num}}
in (
16
) is determined uniquely as

F^{\mathrm{num}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{F\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{f}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}.

(18)

Proof.

We first show that (
16
) implies (
17
).
First, we choose
\bm{u}_{0}=\bm{u}_{-}
in (
16
), resulting in

\bm{\omega}_{-}\cdot\Bigl(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})-\bm{f}_{-}\Bigr)\geq F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})-F_{-}.

(19)

Choosing
\bm{u}_{0}=\bm{u}_{+}
in (
16
) yields

\bm{\omega}_{+}\cdot\Bigl(\bm{f}_{+}-\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\Bigr)\geq F_{+}-F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+}).

(20)

Adding the two inequalities, we obtain

\left(\bm{\omega}_{-}-\bm{\omega}_{+}\right)\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\geq\left(\bm{\omega}_{-}\cdot\bm{f}_{-}-F_{-}\right)-\left(\bm{\omega}_{+}\cdot\bm{f}_{+}-F_{+}\right),

(21)

which is equivalent to (
17
).

If equality holds in (
16
), it holds in (
17
) as well.
In this case, we can subtract (
20
) from (
19
) (both with equality) to obtain

F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})=\frac{\bm{\omega}_{+}+\bm{\omega}_{-}}{2}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{+}\cdot\bm{f}_{+}+\bm{\omega}_{-}\cdot\bm{f}_{-}}{2}+\frac{F_{+}+F_{-}}{2},

(22)

i.e.,
F^{\mathrm{num}}
is determined uniquely by (
18
).

Finally, we show that (
17
) implies (
16
).
Abbreviating
\psi=\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{f}-F
, (
17
) is equivalent to

-\left(\bm{\omega}_{+}-\bm{\omega}_{-}\right)\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\geq-\left(\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}\right).

(23)

Following Tadmor, we compute

\displaystyle\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{0})\cdot\Bigl(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})-\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})\Bigr)

(24)

\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}+\bm{\omega}_{+}}{2}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{+}-\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})

\displaystyle-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{-}+\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}-\bm{\omega}_{-}}{2}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{necessary_condition_psi}}}{{\geq}}\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}+\bm{\omega}_{+}}{2}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})-\frac{\psi_{+}-\psi_{0}}{2}-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{-}+\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})-\frac{\psi_{0}-\psi_{-}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}+\bm{\omega}_{+}}{2}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})-\frac{\psi_{+}+\psi_{0}}{2}-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{-}+\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})+\frac{\psi_{0}+\psi_{-}}{2}

\displaystyle=F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})-F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0}),

Remark 2.3
.

The condition (
17

However, it is also a necessary condition, and thus, it provides a complete characterization of entropy-preserving methods.
Moreover, (

In particular, a numerical entropy flux such as
\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}-\psi(\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\})

[

, Eq. (33)]
, where
\psi=\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{f}-F
, cannot be used in this setting.

The following theorem captures the essential structure of Tadmor’s condition while removing the assumption of entropy convexity.
Indeed, at a purely algebraic level, the invertibility of the mapping between conserved and entropy variables (which is guaranteed by the convexity of the entropy function) is not required.
Consequently, the same condition can also be used to ensure the conservation of non-convex functionals.

Theorem 2.5

(Tadmor)
.

The FV scheme

\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{i}+\frac{1}{\Delta x_{i}}\Bigl(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}\bigl(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{i+1}\bigr)-\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}\bigl(\bm{u}_{i-1},\bm{u}_{i}\bigr)\Bigr)=\bm{0}

(
2
)

is entropy-conservative/stable for a given entropy pair
(U,F)
(see Definition
2.1
) if and only if

\left\llbracket\bm{\omega}\right\rrbracket\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}\leq\left\llbracket\psi\right\rrbracket,

(26)

where
\bm{\omega}=U^{\prime}
are the entropy variables and
\psi=\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{f}-F
is the entropy potential.

Proof.

According to Definition
2.1
, entropy conservation/stability means

U^{\prime}(\bm{u}_{i})\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{i}=-\frac{\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{i})}{\Delta x_{i}}\cdot\Bigl(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\bigg)\leq-\frac{1}{\Delta x_{i}}\Bigl(F^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-F^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\Bigr),

(27)

which simply boils down to

\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{i})\cdot\bigg(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\bigg)\geq F^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-F^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}.

(28)

Thus, applying Lemma
2.2
yields the desired result.
∎

3
Nonconservative hyperbolic systems

In this section, we first show that the entropy conservation condition for nonconservative systems (
6
), proposed by Castro et al. in
[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
, is also necessary, generalizing to non-convex entropies.
Then, we propose four concrete semi-discretizations of the nonconservative terms
\bm{H}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}(\bm{u})
, composed of jumps and means of
\bm{H}
and
\bm{g}
, and characterize their entropy-preserving properties.

In this section, we consider nonconservative hyperbolic systems of the form

\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\partial_{x}\bm{f}(\bm{u})+\bm{H}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}(\bm{u})=\bm{0},

(
7
)

where
\bm{u}(x,t)\in Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the state vector,
\bm{f}\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the flux function,
\bm{H}\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
is the nonconservative matrix, and
\bm{g}\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, paired with appropriate initial and boundary conditions.
We underline that here
n
is the number of unknowns and
m
accounts for the number of nonconservative terms.
As for the conservative case, we assume the existence of an entropy function
U\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}
with associated entropy flux
F\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\forall\bm{u}\in Y\colon\quad U^{\prime}(\bm{u})\cdot\bm{f}^{\prime}(\bm{u})+U^{\prime}(\bm{u})\cdot\bm{H}(\bm{u})\bm{g}^{\prime}(\bm{u})=F^{\prime}(\bm{u}).

(29)

Thus, smooth solutions of (
7
) satisfy the additional conservation law

\partial_{t}U(\bm{u})+\partial_{x}F(\bm{u})=0
,
and weak solutions should satisfy the entropy inequality

\partial_{t}U(\bm{u})+\partial_{x}F(\bm{u})\leq 0
.

3.1
An algebraic characterization of the fluctuation form

Next, we formulate the condition (
6
) of Castro et al.
[
16
]
as a purely algebraic condition, similarly to what we have just presented in Section
2.1
for the conservative case.
Note that we do not assume any further relationship between the functions
\bm{D}^{\pm}
,
\bm{\omega}
, and
F
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open, and
\bm{\omega}\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
F\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\bm{D}^{\pm}\colon Y\times Y\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\forall\bm{u}\in Y\colon\quad\bm{D}^{\pm}(\bm{u},\bm{u})=\bm{0}

(30)

be given. Then,

\exists F^{\mathrm{num}}\colon Y\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\forall\bm{u}\in Y\colon F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u},\bm{u})=F(\bm{u})
and

\forall\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+}\in Y\colon\quad\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{0})\cdot\Bigl(\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})+\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})\Bigr)\geq F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})-F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})

(31)

if and only if

\forall\bm{u}_{+},\bm{u}_{-}\in Y\colon\quad\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{+})\cdot\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})+\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{-})\cdot\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\geq F(\bm{u}_{+})-F(\bm{u}_{-}).

(32)

Moreover, if one of the conditions is satisfied with equality, then equality holds in the other condition as well, and
F^{\mathrm{num}}
in (
31
) is determined uniquely as

F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})=\frac{F(\bm{u}_{+})+F(\bm{u}_{-})}{2}+\frac{\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{-})}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})-\frac{\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{+})}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+}).

(33)

Proof.

We first show that (
31
) implies (
32
).
First, we choose
\bm{u}_{0}=\bm{u}_{-}
in (
31
), resulting in

\bm{\omega}_{-}\cdot\Bigl(\underbrace{\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{-})}_{=\bm{0}}+\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\Bigr)\geq F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})-F_{-}.

(34)

Choosing
\bm{u}_{0}=\bm{u}_{+}
in (
31
) yields

\bm{\omega}_{+}\cdot\Bigl(\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})+\underbrace{\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{+},\bm{u}_{+})}_{=\bm{0}}\Bigr)\geq F_{+}-F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+}).

(35)

Adding the two inequalities, we obtain (
32
).

If equality holds in (
31
), it holds in (
32
) as well.
In this case, we can subtract (
35
) from (
34
) (both with equality) to obtain (
33
).

Finally, we show that (
32
) implies (
31
).
Similar to
[
16
]
, we compute

\displaystyle\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{0})\cdot\Bigl(\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})+\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})\Bigr)

(36)

\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})+\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})+\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})+\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{necessary_condition_fluctuation}}}{{\geq}}\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})+\frac{F_{0}-F_{-}}{2}-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{-}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})

\displaystyle+\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})+\frac{F_{+}-F_{0}}{2}-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{+}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})

\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})-\frac{F_{0}+F_{-}}{2}-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{-}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})

\displaystyle+\frac{\bm{\omega}_{0}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})+\frac{F_{+}+F_{0}}{2}-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{+}}{2}\cdot\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})

\displaystyle=F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+})-F^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0})

where we have used the definition of
F^{\mathrm{num}}
in (
33
) in the last step.
∎

Given this algebraic characterization, the necessary and sufficient condition for the FV scheme in fluctuation form follows immediately, cf.
[
16
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The FV scheme in fluctuation form

\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{i}+\frac{1}{\Delta x_{i}}\Bigl(\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{i-1},\bm{u}_{i})+\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{i+1})\Bigr)=\bm{0}

(
4
)

is entropy-conservative/stable for a given entropy pair
(U,F)
(see Definition
2.1
) if and only if

\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{+})\cdot\bm{D}^{+}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})+\bm{\omega}(\bm{u}_{-})\cdot\bm{D}^{-}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\geq\left\llbracket F\right\rrbracket

(37)

where
\bm{\omega}=U^{\prime}
are the entropy variables.

Proof.

The proof follows by applying Lemma
3.1
.
∎

For us, the condition (
37
) is too abstract to be useful for deriving entropy-preserving numerical schemes.
Thus, we focus on concrete forms of the fluctuations in the following.

3.2
Specialized semi-discretizations for nonconservative systems

The simplest discretization of the nonconservative term is the second-order central scheme

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}(\bm{u})\Big|_{x=x_{i}}\approx\bm{H}_{i}\frac{\bm{g}_{i+1}-\bm{g}_{i-1}}{2\Delta x_{i}}.

(38)

This can be written more conveniently for the following analysis as

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}(\bm{u})\Big|_{x=x_{i}}\approx\bm{H}_{i}\frac{\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x_{i}},

(39)

where
\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}=\bm{g}_{i+1}-\bm{g}_{i}
.
A second form can be obtained by writing the central discretization as

\bm{H}_{i}\frac{\bm{g}_{i+1}-\bm{g}_{i-1}}{2\Delta x_{i}}=\bm{H}_{i}\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{g}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{g}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x_{i}}.

Generalizing the arithmetic mean to a numerical flux
g^{\mathrm{num}}
, we obtain the second form

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}(\bm{u})\Big|_{x=x_{i}}\approx\bm{H}_{i}\frac{\bm{g}_{i+1/2}^{\mathrm{num}}-\bm{g}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x_{i}}.

(40)

A third form can be obtained by interpreting the fraction in (
39
) as an average of the discrete gradients at the left and right interface. Introducing a numerical flux
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}
at these interfaces, we obtain the third form

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}(\bm{u})\Big|_{x=x_{i}}\approx\frac{\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x_{i}}.

(41)

Finally, we note that

\frac{\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x_{i}}=\frac{\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{g}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}-\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{g}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x_{i}}-\frac{\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x_{i}}\bm{g}_{i}.

Thus, substituting the arithmetic mean
\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{g}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}
with a generic two-point numerical flux and considering the single generic numerical flux of
\left(\bm{H}\bm{g}\right)^{\mathrm{num}}
, we obtain the fourth form

\bm{H}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}(\bm{u})\Big|_{x=x_{i}}\approx\frac{\left(\bm{H}\bm{g}\right)^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-\left(\bm{H}\bm{g}\right)^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x_{i}}-\frac{\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x_{i}}\bm{g}_{i}.

(42)

This last form is a generalization of the flux differencing form used by Waruszewski et al.
[
104
]
, where they used the particular form
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}\bm{g}^{\mathrm{num}}-\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}\bm{g}_{i}
(at the right interface).
The form (
42
) is particularly interesting because it allows a lot of flexibility; choosing
(\bm{H}\bm{g})^{\mathrm{num}}
and
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}
as

(\bm{H}\bm{g})^{\mathrm{num}}=\left(\mskip-3.0mu\left(\bm{H}\cdot\bm{g}\right)\mskip-3.0mu\right):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{H}_{-}\cdot\bm{g}_{+}+\bm{H}_{+}\cdot\bm{g}_{-}\right),\quad\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{H}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},

(43)

we have that

\displaystyle\left(\mskip-3.0mu\left(\bm{H}\cdot\bm{g}\right)\mskip-3.0mu\right)_{i+1/2}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{H}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}\bm{g}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{H}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{i+1}+\bm{H}_{i+1}\cdot\bm{g}_{i}\right)-\frac{\bm{H}_{i}+\bm{H}_{i+1}}{2}\cdot\bm{g}_{i}=\bm{H}_{i}\frac{\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}}{2}.

(44)

Thus, we recover the first form (
39
).
Choosing

(\bm{H}\bm{g})^{\mathrm{num}}=\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{g}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\quad\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{H}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},

(45)

we obtain the third form (
41
).

Remark 3.3

Note that Definition

of entropy conservation/stability remains valid analogously for all the forms (

40
), (
41
), and (

3.3
A generalized semi-discretization for nonconservative systems

It can be useful to consider linear combinations of different forms of the semi-discretizations introduced above, e.g., to obtain generalized split forms.
Here, we focus on a linear combination of the first and third form, i.e.,

\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{i}+\frac{1}{\Delta x_{i}}\left(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\right)+\alpha\frac{\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x_{i}}+(1-\alpha)\bm{H}_{i}\frac{\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x_{i}}=\bm{0},

(71)

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
In this case, we have the following result.

.

71
) is entropy-conservative/stable for a given entropy pair
(U,F)
(see Definition

where
\bm{\omega}=U^{\prime}

Remark 3.13
.

In general, the parameter
\alpha
represents a degree of freedom when constructing numerical fluxes.
In contrast to the case of hyperbolic conservation laws with (strictly) convex entropy, there may be no solution to the characterizing condition (
72
) for a given
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, or even for any
\alpha
.
If there is a solution, it may exist for several choices of
\alpha
.
We will explore this in some examples in Section
4
.

Remark 3.14
.

Although the condition can be applied by treating all nonconservative terms in a uniform manner, it is also possible to discretize different nonconservative terms using any of the four options, or a combination of them.
Thus, each nonconservative term can be associated to some parameter
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. An example is given in Section
4
.

Remark 3.15
.

When
\alpha=0
, the condition (
72
) becomes fully specified for the nonconservative terms; the only remaining degree of freedom is the choice of the numerical flux
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}
associated with the conservative part of the system, when present.
In contrast, the freedom obtained by considering
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}
in (
68
), or the more general one in (
70
) allows constructing well-balanced methods for nonlinear steady states
[
104
,
5
]
.

4
Constructing entropy-conservative/stable fluxes

In this section, we provide several examples to derive entropy-conservative/stable numerical fluxes for nonconservative hyperbolic systems of equations.
We demonstrate how existing fluxes can be recovered within our framework, and how novel fluxes can be constructed systematically.

4.1
Variable-coefficient advection equation

We first consider the variable-coefficient linear advection equation

\partial_{t}u(t,x)+a(x)\partial_{x}u(t,x)=0,

(73)

with positive speed
a(x)\geq a_{0}>0
and compatible initial and boundary conditions.
This equation can be written in the form (
7
) with
f=0
,
H=a
, and
g=u
.
We consider the entropy pair
[
79
,
61
]

U=\frac{u^{2}}{a},\quad F=u^{2}.

(74)

Thus, the entropy variable
\omega
and the entropy potential
\psi
are

\omega=2\frac{u}{a},\quad\psi=-F=-u^{2}.

(75)

The entropy conservation condition (
72
) with
\alpha=0
leads to

-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\omega H\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket g\right\rrbracket=-2\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket u\right\rrbracket=-\left\llbracket u^{2}\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\psi\right\rrbracket=-2\left\llbracket u\right\rrbracket\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},

(76)

which is always satisfied.
This corresponds effectively to the construction of energy-conservative methods in
[
79
]
.
The resulting semi-discretization is

\partial_{t}u_{i}+a_{i}\frac{\left\llbracket u\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\left\llbracket u\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x_{i}}=\partial_{t}u_{i}+a_{i}\frac{u_{i+1}-u_{i-1}}{2\Delta x_{i}}=0.

(77)

4.2
Coupled Burgers’ equation

Following Castro et al.
[
16
]
, we consider the
nonconservative hyperbolic system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}(u+v)=0,

(78)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+v\partial_{x}(u+v)=0.

This system is of the form (
7
) with

\bm{u}=\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix},\quad\bm{f}=\bm{0},\quad\bm{H}=\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\
0&v\end{pmatrix},\quad\bm{g}=\begin{pmatrix}u+v\\
u+v\end{pmatrix}.

(79)

By summing the two equations, we observe that
q:=u+v
satisfies Burgers’ equation
[
16
]

\partial_{t}q+\partial_{x}\!\left(\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)=0.

(80)

Therefore, the coupled Burgers’ equation (
78
) admits the entropy pair
[
16
]

U=q^{2}/2,\quad F=q^{3}/3.

(81)

Hence, the entropy variables and the entropy potential are

\bm{\omega}=(q,q),\quad\psi=-q^{3}/3.

(82)

The EC condition (
72
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\cdot\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket+(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{H}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket

(83)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u+v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left(u^{\mathrm{num}}+v^{\mathrm{num}}\right)\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket+(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{(u+v)u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket+(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{(u+v)v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\llbracket(u+v)^{3}\right\rrbracket}{3}=-\left\llbracket\psi\right\rrbracket.

The right-hand side of (
83
) can be expanded as

\frac{\left\llbracket(u+v)^{3}\right\rrbracket}{3}=\frac{\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket}{3}\left((u_{+}+v_{+})^{2}+(u_{+}+v_{+})(u_{-}+v_{-})+(u_{-}+v_{-})^{2}\right).

(84)

Thus, the EC condition becomes

\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u+v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left(u^{\mathrm{num}}+v^{\mathrm{num}}\right)+(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{(u+v)^{2}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\\
=\frac{1}{3}\left((u_{+}+v_{+})^{2}+(u_{+}+v_{+})(u_{-}+v_{-})+(u_{-}+v_{-})^{2}\right).

(85)

Choosing arithmetic means
u^{\mathrm{num}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}
and
v^{\mathrm{num}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}
, we obtain

\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u+v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{2}+(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{(u+v)^{2}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}=\frac{1}{3}\left((u_{+}+v_{+})^{2}+(u_{+}+v_{+})(u_{-}+v_{-})+(u_{-}+v_{-})^{2}\right).

(86)

This condition is satisfied if and only if
\alpha=2/3
.
Therefore, the numerical fluxes determined by

u^{\mathrm{num}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\quad v^{\mathrm{num}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\quad\alpha=2/3,

(87)

are entropy-conservative. The resulting finite-volume discretization can be written as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{i}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}+\frac{u_{i}}{3}\frac{\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}=0,

(88)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v_{i}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}+\frac{v_{i}}{3}\frac{\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\left\llbracket u+v\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}=0,

which is equivalent to the scheme presented by Castro et al.
[
16
]
.
It reduces to the standard split-form discretization of Burgers’ equations (
80
), which is obtained using the uniquely determined EC flux.

4.3
General polynomial equation

A generalization of Burgers’ equation involves a monomial flux of degree
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
k>2
, we can write the equation in different nonconservative forms as

\partial_{t}u+u^{m}\partial_{x}u^{n}=0,\quad m,n\in\mathbb{N},\;m+n=k.

(89)

The ansatz (
71
) becomes

\partial_{t}u_{i}+\alpha\frac{h^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+h^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}+\left(1-\alpha\right)\frac{u^{m}_{i}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+u^{m}_{i}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}=0,

(90)

where

h^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}=h^{\mathrm{num}}(u_{i},u_{i+1})=h^{\mathrm{num}}(u_{i+1},u_{i}),\quad h^{\mathrm{num}}(u,u)=u^{m},

(91)

for consistency and symmetry of the fluxes. We seek numerical fluxes
h^{\mathrm{num}}
and a parameter
\alpha
such that the scheme preserves the entropy given by

U=\frac{u^{2}}{2},\quad F=\frac{n}{m+n+1}u^{m+n+1},\quad\psi=-F,\quad\omega=u.

(92)

Hence, the entropy conservation condition (
72
) becomes

\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}h^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket+(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u^{m+1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket=\frac{n}{m+n+1}\left\llbracket u^{m+n+1}\right\rrbracket.

(93)

This shows that there does not always need to be a solution.
Indeed, if
m,n\geq 2
are both even, we can choose
u_{\pm}=\pm 1
to get a contradiction, since the left-hand side is zero while the right-hand side is not.

However, we can interpret the product
h^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket
appearing in the semi-discretization (
90
) as a fluctuation, and derive an expression for it directly.
This reduces to the polynomial division

h^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket=\frac{1}{\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}}\left(\frac{n}{m+n+1}\left\llbracket u^{m+n+1}\right\rrbracket-(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u^{m+1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket\right)=\frac{P(u_{-},u_{+})}{\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}},

(94)

where we defined the polynomial
P(u_{-},u_{+})
.
If
n
is even, (
94
) can only be satisfied for
u_{-}=u_{+}\neq 0
if
m
is odd, since otherwise the left-hand side would be zero while the right-hand side is not. On the other hand, if
n
is odd, the numerical flux is well-behaved and we can always divide (
94
) by
\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket
.
In particular, in the case where
n
is odd and
m
is even, we obtain the compatibility condition

\alpha=\frac{m+1}{m+n+1},

(95)

which we use from now on, for simplicity, to cover the cases where
n
is odd and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Polynomial long division yields

h^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket=\frac{2n}{m+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m+n}(-1)^{k}u_{+}^{m+n-k}u_{-}^{k}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u^{m+1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}u_{+}^{n-1-k}u_{-}^{k}\right),

(96)

which is antisymmetric and consistent, and thus we have proved the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
n
odd. The semi-discretization (
90
) is entropy conservative if
\alpha=\frac{m+1}{m+n+1}
and the fluctuation
h^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket
is given by

h^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket u^{n}\right\rrbracket=\frac{2n}{m+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m+n}(-1)^{k}u_{+}^{m+n-k}u_{-}^{k}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u^{m+1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}u_{+}^{n-1-k}u_{-}^{k}\right).

(97)

To analyze the general case including even
n
, we rewrite the continuous equation as

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}(u^{m+n})-u^{n}\partial_{x}(u^{m})=0.

(98)

A corresponding semi-discretization reads

\partial_{t}u_{i}+\frac{f^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-f^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x}-\alpha\frac{h^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket u^{m}\right\rrbracket+h^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket u^{m}\right\rrbracket}{2\Delta x}-(1-\alpha)\frac{u^{n}_{i}\left\llbracket u^{m}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+u^{n}_{i}\left\llbracket u^{m}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}=0.

(99)

Here, the entropy potential is

\psi=\omega f-F=u\cdot u^{m+n}-\frac{n}{m+n+1}u^{m+n+1}=\frac{m+1}{m+n+1}u^{m+n+1}

(100)

and the EC condition (
72
) reads

\left\llbracket u\right\rrbracket f^{\mathrm{num}}+\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}h^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket u^{m}\right\rrbracket+(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u^{n+1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket u^{m}\right\rrbracket=\frac{m+1}{m+n+1}\left\llbracket u^{m+n+1}\right\rrbracket.

(101)

Decomposing the jumps as
\left\llbracket u^{k}\right\rrbracket=(u_{+}-u_{-})\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}u_{+}^{k-1-l}u_{-}^{l}
gives

f^{\mathrm{num}}=\frac{m+1}{m+n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m+n}u_{+}^{m+n-k}u_{-}^{k}-\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}h^{\mathrm{num}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}u_{+}^{m-1-k}u_{-}^{k}-(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u^{n+1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}u_{+}^{m-1-k}u_{-}^{k}.

(102)

Here
h^{\mathrm{num}}
remains a degree of freedom that may be chosen to be symmetric and consistent with
u^{n}
. Therefore, we can state the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Given
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, the semi-discretization (
99
) conserves the entropy
U=\frac{u^{2}}{2}
if

f^{\mathrm{num}}=\frac{m+1}{m+n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m+n}u_{+}^{m+n-k}u_{-}^{k}-\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}h^{\mathrm{num}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}u_{+}^{m-1-k}u_{-}^{k}-(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{u^{n+1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}u_{+}^{m-1-k}u_{-}^{k},

(103)

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
h^{\mathrm{num}}
is a symmetric and consistent mean value of
u^{n}
.

This shows that the choice of how to write a system in nonconservative form can affect the construction of entropy-conserving methods.

Total energy conservation

4.7.2
Entropy conservation

Now we seek conditions for entropy conservation. The associated flux and entropy variables are

If in addition

4.7.3
Another interpretation of kinetic energy-preserving and pressure equilibrium-preserving fluxes

We have seen in Theorem

Next, we will present another interpretation of kinetic energy preservation
[

,
56
,
71
,

,

]
and pressure equilibrium preservation
[

,

]
.
For simplicity, we set the gravity potential
\phi=0

Since
p=(\gamma-1)\varrho e
for an ideal gas, the system is equivalent to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\partial_{x}(\varrho v)=0,

(148)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho v)+\partial_{x}(\varrho v^{2}+p)=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p+\gamma\partial_{x}(pv)-(\gamma-1)v\partial_{x}p=0.

Pressure equilibrium-preserving (PEP) methods
[
89
,
68
]
keep states of the form
v\equiv\mathrm{const}
and
p\equiv\mathrm{const}
, where the compressible Euler equations reduce to the linear advection equation
\partial_{t}\varrho+v\partial_{x}\varrho=0
.
A consistent discretization of the pressure equation (or equivalently the internal energy equation) yields
\partial_{t}p=0
for these states.
Moreover, the KEP conditions (
138
) reduce the semi-discrete momentum equation in this case to

\displaystyle-\Delta x\,\varrho_{i}\partial_{t}v_{i}

\displaystyle=-\Delta x\,\partial_{t}(\varrho v)_{i}+\Delta x\,v_{i}\partial_{t}\varrho=f^{\varrho v}_{i+1/2}-f^{\varrho v}_{i-1/2}-v_{i}\left(f^{\varrho}_{i+1/2}-f^{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\right)

(149)

\displaystyle=\left(vf^{\varrho}_{i+1/2}+p\right)-\left(vf^{\varrho}_{i-1/2}+p\right)-v\left(f^{\varrho}_{i+1/2}-f^{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\right)=0,

since
v\equiv\mathrm{const}
and
p\equiv\mathrm{const}
.
Thus, discretizing the pressure or internal energy equation consistently and using a KEP method automatically results in a PEP method.
Since the KEP method conserves the total energy, the induced total energy flux is given by (
58
) as

\displaystyle F^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho E}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{F_{\varrho E}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}_{\varrho E}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}_{\varrho E}\cdot\bm{f}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}-\frac{1}{4}\left\llbracket\bm{\omega}_{\varrho E}\right\rrbracket\cdot\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket

(150)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v^{2}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}f^{\varrho}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}f^{\varrho v}+f^{\varrho e}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{pv\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v^{2}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\right)f^{\varrho}+f^{\varrho e}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{pv\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}

because of the KEP conditions (

).
Using
f^{\varrho e}=f^{\varrho e,1}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}
, where

is a consistent discretization of

, yields

F^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho E}=\left(\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v^{2}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\right)f^{\varrho}+f^{\varrho e,1}+2\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{pv\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}.

(151)

Using the product mean

\left(\mskip-3.0mu\left(a\cdot b\right)\mskip-3.0mu\right)=2\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{b\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{ab\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{-}b_{+}+a_{+}b_{-}\right)

(152)

and the entropy-conservative fluxes (

F^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho E}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\log}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left(\mskip-3.0mu\left(v\cdot v\right)\mskip-3.0mu\right)+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\log}}{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho/p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\log}}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\left(\mskip-3.0mu\left(p\cdot v\right)\mskip-3.0mu\right),

Summation-by-parts operators and nonconservative systems

In this section, we first briefly review the concept of SBP operators.
Then, we extend the results obtained for the low-order FV discretization in Section
3
to general high-order SBP operators.
The notation closely follows
[
70
,
78
]
.

5.1
Summation-by-parts operators

Following
[
44
,
70
]
, we recall the definition of an SBP operator.

Definition 5.1

(SBP operator)
.

An SBP operator on a
d
-dimensional element
E
with order of accuracy
p\in\mathbb{N}
consists of the following components

•

Derivative operators
\bm{D}^{j},j\in\{1,\dots d\}
, approximating the partial derivative in the
j
-th coordinate direction; they are exact for polynomials of degree
\leq p
.

•

A mass matrix
\bm{M}
, approximating the
L_{2}
scalar product on
E
.

•

A restriction operator
\bm{R}
, that projects the solution to the boundary nodes.

•

A boundary mass matrix
\bm{B}
approximating the
L_{2}
scalar product on
\partial E
.

•

Multiplication operators
\bm{N}^{j}
for
j\in\{1,\dots d\}
, performing multiplication of functions on the boundary
\partial E
by the
j
-th component
n_{j}
of the outer normal vector
\bm{n}
at the boundary
\partial E
.

Together, these components have to satisfy the SBP property

\bm{M}\,\bm{D}^{j}+(\bm{D}^{j})^{T}\bm{M}=\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\,\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}.

(155)

We consider a nonconservative hyperbolic system in
d
dimensions

\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}\bm{f}^{j}(\bm{u})+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bm{H}^{j}(\bm{u})\partial_{x_{j}}\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u})=0.

(156)

An SBP semi-discretization of (
156
) is given by

\partial_{t}\bm{u}=-\bm{\mathrm{VOL}}-\bm{\mathrm{SURF}},

(157)

where
\bm{\mathrm{VOL}}
contains the volume terms consistent with
\partial_{x_{j}}\bm{f}^{j}(\bm{u})+\bm{H}^{j}\cdot\partial_{x_{j}}\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u})
and
\bm{\mathrm{SURF}}
contains additional surface terms coupling neighboring elements.
We assume the nodal basis to always admit sufficient boundary nodes, e.g., tensor product Gauss-Lobatto-Legendre nodes in quadrilateral/hexahedral elements.

5.2
Nonconservative split forms

In this section, we present the semi-discretization of the volume terms for high-order SBP operators for the nonconservative terms, as a natural extension of the finite volume methods presented in Section
3
.
Gassner et al.
[
42
]
showed how the flux-differencing framework introduced by Fisher and Carpenter
[
37
]
generates common split forms formulations such as
[
50
,
30
]
, given specific choices of the numerical volume flux.
A systematic way to generate and implement split forms is of great interest for high-order discretizations based on SBP operators.
In fact, a simple way to obtain entropy-preserving methods is to prove the same property at the continuous level using only integration by parts, and then replace derivatives by SBP operators (see for example
[
69
,
83
,
11
,
41
]
).
This is often far from trivial, or it may require non-trivial splittings
[
28
]
.
Moreover, split forms can improve the robustness of under-resolved simulations even without provable entropy stability properties
[
50
,
42
,
89
,
91
,
51
]
.

The first discretization (
39
) is based on a pointwise evaluation of the nonconservative product.
A natural SBP generalization is

\bm{H}^{j}(\bm{u}(x_{i}))\cdot\partial_{j}\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}(x))\Big|_{x=x_{i}}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{H}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right),

(158)

where the term
\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})
does not contribute to the sum, since it is constant with respect to the summation index
k
and thus mapped to zero by the consistent SBP operator
\bm{D}^{j}
. However, this term is included to maintain the jump term in the volume flux, similarly to the approach used in the finite volume scheme for
\bm{H}_{i}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket
, which we will later justify.
The second discretization (
40
) is analogous to the flux-differencing form for conservative terms, i.e.,

\bm{H}^{j}(\bm{u}(x_{i}))\cdot\partial_{j}\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}(x))\Big|_{x=x_{i}}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{H}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\bm{g}^{\mathrm{vol},j}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}),

(159)

where we denoted the numerical flux approximating
\bm{g}^{j}
as
\bm{g}^{\mathrm{vol},j}
instead of
\bm{g}^{\mathrm{num},j}
to emphasize its role in the volume terms.
The third discretization (
41
) extends naturally to high-order SBP operators via flux differencing, i.e., the nonconservative volume terms read
[
86
,
5
]

\bm{H}^{j}(\bm{u}(x_{i}))\cdot\partial_{j}\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}(x))\Big|_{x=x_{i}}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right).

(160)

The fourth discretization (
42
) includes a conservative term that we discretize via flux differencing as usual, while the second term is similar to the second form (
159
), i.e.,

\bm{H}^{j}(\bm{u}(x_{i}))\cdot\partial_{j}\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}(x))\big|_{x=x_{i}}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\left(\bm{H}\bm{g}\right)^{\mathrm{vol},j}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k})-2\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k})\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i}).

(161)

Following
[
42
]
, we show these forms presented are naturally related to split forms of the nonconservative product or the discrete equivalent of the product rule for the nonconservative product, i.e,

h(u)\partial_{x}g(u)=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\partial_{x}\bigl(h(u)g(u)\bigr)+h(u)\partial_{x}g(u)-g(u)\partial_{x}h(u)\Bigr),

(162)

and

h(u)\partial_{x}g(u)=\partial_{x}(h(u)g(u))-g(u)\partial_{x}h(u),

(163)

where we dropped the vector-matrix notation, to work with scalar functions
h(u)
and
g(u)
, i.e.
m=n=1
, for the sake of simplicity.
We will restrict our analysis to quadratic terms generated by
\partial_{x}(hg)
.
The first and second discretization for
g^{\mathrm{vol}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{g\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}
are the least interesting as they are equivalent to the strong form.
We encapsulate the different split-forms in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.2

(Discrete nonconservative split-forms)
.

Using the arithmetic mean in the volume fluxes, it is possible to recover the strong form for the first and second forms and the split form (
162
) for the third volume discretization (
160
).
Specifically, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}_{i,k}h_{i}\left\llbracket g\right\rrbracket_{i,k}

\displaystyle=(\bm{H}\bm{D}\bm{g})_{i},

(164)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{D}_{i,k}h_{i}g^{\mathrm{vol}}(u_{i},u_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{D}_{i,k}H_{i}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{g\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}

\displaystyle=(\bm{H}\bm{D}\bm{g})_{i},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}_{i,k}h^{\mathrm{vol}}(u_{i},u_{k})\left\llbracket g\right\rrbracket_{i,k}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}_{i,k}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{h\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}\left\llbracket g\right\rrbracket_{i,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{D}\bm{H}\bm{g}+\bm{H}\bm{D}\bm{g}-\bm{G}\bm{D}\bm{h}\right)_{i},

where
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})
,
\bm{A}=\text{diag}(\bm{a})
, and

\displaystyle\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}:=\frac{a_{k}+a_{i}}{2},\quad\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket_{i,k}:=a_{k}-a_{i}.

(165)

Proof.

The proof follows from direct calculation and the consistency of the derivative operator with respect to constants, i.e.,
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}_{ik}a_{i}=0,\forall a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Using the arithmetic mean, the product of the arithmetic mean and the product mean in the volume flux of the fourth form (
161
), it is possible to recover the discrete product rule (
163
), the split form (
162
) and the strong form, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{D}_{i,k}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{hg\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}-2\bm{D}_{i,k}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{h\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}g_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{D}\bm{H}\bm{g}-\bm{G}\bm{D}\bm{H}\right)_{i},

(166)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{D}_{i,k}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{h\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{g\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}-2\bm{D}_{i,k}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{h\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}g_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{D}\bm{H}\bm{g}+\bm{H}\bm{D}\bm{g}-\bm{G}\bm{D}\bm{h}\right)_{i},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{D}_{i,k}\left(\mskip-3.0mu\left(h\cdot g\right)\mskip-3.0mu\right)_{i,k}-2\bm{D}_{i,k}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{h\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}g_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{H}\bm{D}\bm{g}\right)_{i}.

Proof.

The proof follows the same arguments as the previous lemma.
∎

5.2.1
Order of accuracy for nonconservative volume terms

Theorem 5.5
.

If the volume fluxes
(\bm{H}\bm{g})^{\mathrm{vol},j}
,
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}
,
\bm{g}^{\mathrm{vol},j}
are smooth and symmetric and the functions
\bm{H}^{j}
and
\bm{g}^{j}
are smooth, the forms (
159
), (
160
) and (
161
) are an approximation of the same order of accuracy as the derivative matrices
\bm{D}^{j}
. Here the derivative matrices do not need to be given by SBP operators.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the one-dimensional scalar case. For the sake of brevity and simplicity we will report the proof only for the third form (
160
).
Note that the conservative terms appearing in the second and fourth form can be treated as standard conservative flux differencing terms, e.g.,
[
24
]
or
[
78
, Lemma 3.17]
.
Assuming the derivative operator is of order
p
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}_{ik}h^{\mathrm{vol}}\left(u_{i},u_{k}\right)\left(g_{k}-g_{i}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}_{ik}h^{\mathrm{vol}}\left(u(x_{i}),u(x_{k})\right)\left(g(x_{k})-g(x_{i})\right)

(167)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h^{\mathrm{vol}}\left(u(x_{i}),u(x_{k})\right)g\left(x_{k}\right)}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigg|_{x_{k}=x_{i}}-g(x_{i})\frac{\partial h^{\mathrm{vol}}\left(u(x_{i}),u(x_{k})\right)}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigg|_{x_{k}=x_{i}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta x^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{\mathrm{vol}}\left(u(x_{i}),u(x_{i})\right)\frac{\partial g\left(x_{k}\right)}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigg|_{x_{k}=x_{i}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta x^{p}\right)=h\left(u(x_{i})\right)\frac{\partial g\left(u(x_{k})\right)}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigg|_{x_{k}=x_{i}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta x^{p}\right),

which justifies the absence of the factor 2 compared to the conservative case.
∎

5.3
SBP semi-discretization of nonconservative hyperbolic system

The main components of the SBP discretization can be written as follows.
When nonconservative terms are present, the volume term takes the form

\bm{\mathrm{VOL}}_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)+\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right)\\
+(1-\alpha)\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{H}^{j}\left(\bm{u}_{i}\right)\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right)\Biggr)

(168)

and the surface term reads

\bm{\mathrm{SURF}}:=\bm{M}^{-1}\bm{R}^{T}\left(\bm{B}\ \bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\bm{B}|\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}|\bm{B}|\bm{H}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bm{M}^{-1}\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N_{j}}\bm{R}\ \bm{f}^{j},

(169)

where

\displaystyle\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bm{n}_{j}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num},j}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+}),

(170)

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bm{n}_{j}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num},j}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\left\llbracket\bm{g}^{j}\right\rrbracket,

\displaystyle\bm{H}(\bm{u}_{0})\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bm{n}_{j}\bm{H}^{j}(\bm{u}_{0})\left\llbracket\bm{g}^{j}\right\rrbracket.

For the
E
-th element, the flux at the right boundary is computed as
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-}=\bm{u}_{N}^{E},\bm{u}_{+}=\bm{u}_{1}^{E+1})
, where the subscripts
1
and
N
here correspond to the last and first node of the generic element
E
.

Remark 5.6
.

For a polynomial degree
p
of order zero, the volume terms vanish, since the derivative is exact for constants, i.e.,
\bm{D}\bm{1}=\bm{0}
, recovering the FV method presented in the previous section.

5.4
Entropy conservation/stability

Following the results in the previous section, we introduce a multidimensional definition of entropy conservation/stability for nonconservative systems.

Definition 5.7
.

Two-point numerical fluxes
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num},j}
and
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num},j}
in the
j
-th direction for the hyperbolic nonconservative system (
156
) with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
are entropy-conservative/stable if

\left\llbracket\bm{\omega}\right\rrbracket\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num},j}-\alpha\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\cdot\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num},j}\left\llbracket\bm{g}^{j}\right\rrbracket-(1-\alpha)\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{H}^{j}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket\bm{g}^{j}\right\rrbracket\leq\left\llbracket\psi^{j}\right\rrbracket,\quad j=1,\dots,d,

(171)

where
U
is the entropy function,
\bm{\omega}=U^{\prime}(\bm{u})
the entropy variables, and
\psi^{j}
is the entropy potential in the
j
-th direction.

Theorem 5.8
.

Consider the semi-discretization (
157
) with the volume and surface terms given by (
168
) and (
169
).
If
\alpha
,
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}
,
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol}}
, that are consistent with
\bm{f}
and
\bm{H}
, symmetric and entropy-conservative as in Definition
5.7
, and both the mass matrix
\bm{M}
and the boundary operators
\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}
are diagonal, the semi-discretization is entropy-conservative/stable, if
\alpha
and the numerical fluxes
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}
,
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}
are entropy-conservative/stable according to Definition
5.7
.

Proof.

To derive the semi-discrete rate of change of the entropy within an element
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}U
, we contract to the entropy space by multiplying the left-hand side by
\bm{\omega}\bm{M}\partial_{t}\bm{u}
.
The proof is an extension of the proof of
[
70
,
37
,
24
]
, including here the nonconservative terms, and it represents a particular case of the specified fluctuation form of the proof presented in
[
104
]
.
We first consider the volume terms, assuming
\alpha=1
for simplicity (since the general case follows similarly),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}2\bm{\omega}_{i}\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)+\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\bm{\omega}_{i}\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right)\right)

(172)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Bigg(\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\bm{\omega}_{i}\left[\bm{M}\bm{D}^{j}+\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\bm{D}^{j}-\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\bm{M}\right]_{i,k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\frac{\bm{\omega}_{i}}{2}\left[\bm{M}\bm{D}^{j}+\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\bm{D}^{j}-\bm{D}^{j}\bm{M}\right]_{i,k}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol}}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right)\Bigg).

Since the mass matrices are diagonal in each direction
j\in\{1,..,d\}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\bm{\omega}_{i}\left[\bm{M}\bm{D}^{j}-(\bm{D}^{j})^{T}\bm{M}\right]_{i,k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)+\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\frac{\bm{\omega}_{i}}{2}\left[\bm{M}\bm{D}^{j}-(\bm{D}^{j})^{T}\bm{M}\right]_{i,k}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k})\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right)

(173)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,k}^{N}\left(\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}-\bm{D}^{j}_{k,i}\bm{M}_{k,k}\right)\bm{\omega}_{i}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)+\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\frac{\bm{\omega}_{i}}{2}\left(\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}-\bm{D}^{j}_{k,i}\bm{M}_{k,k}\right)\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k})\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,k}^{N}\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\left(\bm{\omega}_{i}-\bm{\omega}_{k}\right)\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)+\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\frac{\bm{\omega}_{i}+\bm{\omega}_{k}}{2}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k})\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right),

where we exchanged the indices
i,k
in the last step using the symmetry of the volume fluxes
\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}
and
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}
and the anti-symmetry of the
\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left\llbracket\bm{g}^{j}\right\rrbracket
.
Furthermore, with diagonal matrices
\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}
and the anti-symmetry of
\left\llbracket\bm{g}^{j}\right\rrbracket
,

\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\frac{\bm{\omega}_{i}}{2}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\right]_{i,k}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)\left(\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{k})-\bm{g}^{j}(\bm{u}_{i})\right)=0

(174)

and

\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\bm{\omega}_{i}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\right]_{i,k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\right]_{k,k}\bm{\omega}_{k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}\right).

(175)

Thus, the volume contribution to the entropy rate within an element is

\bm{\omega}^{T}\bm{M}\bm{\mathrm{VOL}}^{j}=\sum_{i,k}^{N}\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\left(\bm{\omega}_{i}-\bm{\omega}_{k}\right)\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)\\
+\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\frac{\bm{\omega}_{i}+\bm{\omega}_{k}}{2}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{vol},j}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k})\left(g(\bm{u}_{k})-g(\bm{u}_{i})\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\right]_{k,k}\bm{\omega}_{k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}\right).

(176)

Using the EC Definition
5.7
for
\alpha=1
, and
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, the volume terms become

\displaystyle\bm{\omega}\bm{M}\ \bm{\mathrm{VOL}}^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\left(\psi^{j}_{i}-\psi^{j}_{k}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\right]_{k,k}\bm{\omega}_{k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}\right)

(177)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{M}_{i,i}\bm{D}^{j}\right]_{i,k}\psi^{j}_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\right]_{k,k}\bm{\omega}_{k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}-(\bm{D}^{j})^{T}\bm{M}\right]_{i,k}\psi^{j}_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N_{j}}\bm{R}\right]_{k,k}\bm{\omega}_{k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\right]_{i,k}\psi^{j}_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\right]_{k,k}\bm{\omega}_{k}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}\right).

Hence, the semi-discrete rate of change of the entropy in one element becomes

\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{M}\partial_{t}\bm{u}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\left[\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\right]_{i,k}\psi^{j}_{k}-\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{R}^{T}\left(\bm{B}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\bm{B}|\bm{H}^{num}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}|\bm{B}|\bm{H}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket\right).

(178)

These surface terms can be treated in each direction
j
independently, by adding and subtracting
\psi^{j}_{N}
from the left element and
\psi^{j}_{1}
from the right element, similarly to Lemma
3.5
. Because of the entropy-conservative/stable Definition
5.7
, we finally obtain, without the assumption of
\alpha=1

\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{M}\partial_{t}\bm{u}\leq-\bm{1}^{T}\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{F}^{\mathrm{num}}

(179)

where

\displaystyle\bm{F}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{+},\bm{u}_{-})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bm{n}_{j}\bm{F}^{\mathrm{num},j}

(180)

\displaystyle\bm{F}^{\mathrm{num},j}(\bm{u}_{+},\bm{u}_{-})

\displaystyle=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\cdot\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num},j}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{f}^{j}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{F^{j}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}-\frac{\alpha}{4}\left\llbracket\bm{\omega}\right\rrbracket\cdot\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num},j}\left\llbracket\bm{g}^{j}\right\rrbracket-\frac{1-\alpha}{4}\left\llbracket\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{H}^{j}\right\rrbracket\left\llbracket\bm{g}^{j}\right\rrbracket.

∎

Remark 5.9
.

From the implementation point of view, the presence of antisymmetric terms in the volume nonconservative terms (due to the presence of
\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket
) can be exploited to reduce the computational cost by including the action of
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bm{M}^{-1}\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}\ \bm{f}^{j}
into the derivative operator, as already shown for the conservative terms in
[
74
]
, by defining

\tilde{\bm{D}}^{j}=2\bm{D}^{j}-\bm{M}^{-1}\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N}^{j}\bm{R}.

(181)

Since the nonconservative terms are written in anti-symmetric form, their contribution to the diagonal of the derivative operator vanishes, i.e., they are consistent with zero. Therefore, it is sufficient to compute only either the upper or lower triangle part of the derivative operator.

6
Summation-by-parts operators and curvilinear coordinates

To extend the method to curved meshes within the SBP framework, we follow the classical approach described by Kopriva
[
52
,
53
]
.
For the sake of clarity, we restrict the presentation to 2D.
The extension to 3D is conceptually straightforward, requiring only additional care in the discretization of the metric terms in order to ensure the free-stream preservation property
[
102
,
52
,
53
]
.

In two space dimensions a generic hyperbolic nonconservative system reads

\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\partial_{x}\bm{f}^{1}(\bm{u})+\partial_{y}\bm{f}^{2}(\bm{u})+\bm{H}^{1}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}^{1}(\bm{u})+\bm{H}^{2}(\bm{u})\partial_{y}\bm{g}^{2}(\bm{u})=0.

(182)

The computational domain
\Omega
is divided into
K
non-overlapping quadrilateral elements
E_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,K
.
Each physical element is obtained from the reference element
E_{0}=[-1,1]^{2}
through a mapping

x=X(\xi,\eta),\quad y=Y(\xi,\eta),\quad(\xi,\eta)\in E_{0}.

(183)

If the edges of an element are straight, the mapping corresponds to a bilinear transformation and is linear with respect to each reference coordinate.
When the elements have curved boundaries, the geometry is described using high-order polynomials
[
52
,
53
]
.
As a result, the mapping itself becomes a polynomial in each direction.
In such cases, we model the geometry through a polynomial mapping of degree
p_{\mathrm{geo}}
in each coordinate direction.

Under this transformation, the nonconservative hyperbolic system reads

J\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\partial_{\xi}\tilde{\bm{f}}^{1}+\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\bm{f}}^{2}+\tilde{\bm{H}}\partial_{\xi}\tilde{\bm{g}}^{1}+\tilde{\bm{H}}\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\bm{g}}^{2}=\bm{0},

(184)

where
J=X_{\xi}Y_{\eta}-X_{\eta}Y_{\xi}
is the Jacobian of the transformation, and the
\tilde{\cdot}
quantities are the contravariant counterparts, i.e.,

\tilde{\bm{f}}^{j}=\hat{\bm{n}}^{j}\cdot\bm{f},\quad\tilde{\bm{g}}^{j}=\hat{\bm{n}}^{j}\circ\bm{g},\quad\tilde{\bm{H}}=\left(\bm{H}^{1},\bm{H}^{2}\right),

(185)

where
\circ
denotes the Hadamard (element-wise) product,
\bm{f}=(\bm{f}^{1},\bm{f}^{2})^{T}
,
\bm{g}=(\bm{g}^{1},\bm{g}^{2})^{T}
, and
\hat{\bm{n}}^{j}
are the contravariant vectors (normal directions to the coordinate lines) defined as

\hat{\bm{n}}^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}Y_{\eta}\\
-X_{\eta}\end{pmatrix},\quad\hat{\bm{n}}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-Y_{\xi}\\
X_{\xi}\end{pmatrix},

(186)

where
X_{\xi},X_{\eta},Y_{\xi},Y_{\eta}
are the metric terms.
They satisfy the metric identities
[
102
,
52
,
53
]

\partial_{\xi}Y_{\eta}-\partial_{\eta}Y_{\xi}=0,\quad-\partial_{\xi}X_{\eta}+\partial_{\eta}X_{\xi}=0,

(187)

ensuring that conservation laws are preserved under the coordinate transformation.
The SBP operators are all defined on the reference element
E_{0}
and the metric terms are approximated as

X_{\xi}\approx\bm{D}^{1}\bm{X}=\bm{X}_{\xi},\quad X_{\eta}\approx\bm{X}\left(\bm{D}^{2}\right)^{T}=\bm{X}_{\eta},\quad Y_{\xi}\approx\bm{D}^{1}\bm{Y}=\bm{Y}_{\xi},\quad Y_{\eta}\approx\bm{Y}\left(\bm{D}^{2}\right)^{T}=\bm{Y}_{\eta},

(188)

which guarantees that the free-stream preservation (FSP)
[
103
,
52
]
property holds also at the discrete level, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}Y_{\eta}-\partial_{\eta}Y_{\xi}

\displaystyle\approx\bm{D}^{1}\bm{Y}_{\eta}-\bm{Y}_{\xi}\left(\bm{D}^{2}\right)^{T}=\bm{0},

(189)

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}X_{\eta}-\partial_{\eta}X_{\xi}

\displaystyle\approx\bm{D}^{1}\bm{X}_{\eta}-\bm{X}_{\xi}\left(\bm{D}^{2}\right)^{T}=\bm{0}.

Essentially, due to the metric identities being satisfied on the reference element, the SBP operators are applied to the reference element, which is then mapped to the physical element.
Thus, the volume term reads

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{VOL}}_{i}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\tilde{\bm{f}}^{\mathrm{vol},j}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)+\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\tilde{\bm{H}}^{\mathrm{vol}}\left(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}\right)\left\llbracket\tilde{\bm{g}}^{j}\right\rrbracket_{i,k}+(1-\alpha)\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{D}^{j}_{i,k}\tilde{\bm{H}}\left(\bm{u}_{i}\right)\left\llbracket\tilde{\bm{g}}^{j}\right\rrbracket_{i,k}\right),

(190)

and the surface term reads

\bm{\mathrm{SURF}}:=\bm{M}^{-1}\bm{R}^{T}\left(\bm{B}\bm{\tilde{f}}^{\mathrm{num}}+\alpha\frac{1}{2}|\bm{B}|\tilde{\bm{H}}^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket\tilde{\bm{g}}\right\rrbracket+(1-\alpha)\frac{1}{2}|\bm{B}|\tilde{\bm{H}}\left\llbracket\tilde{\bm{g}}\right\rrbracket\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bm{M}^{-1}\bm{R}^{T}\bm{B}\bm{N_{j}}\bm{R}\bm{\tilde{f}}^{j},

(191)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{f}}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{\bm{f}}^{\mathrm{num},j}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+}),\quad\tilde{\bm{f}}^{\mathrm{num},j}=\hat{\bm{n}}^{j}\cdot\left(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num},1},\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num},2}\right),

(192)

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{\bm{H}}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\cdot\left\llbracket\tilde{\bm{g}}^{j}\right\rrbracket,

\displaystyle\bm{H}(\bm{u}_{0})\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{\bm{H}}(\bm{u}_{0})\cdot\left\llbracket\tilde{\bm{g}}^{j}\right\rrbracket,

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{f}}^{\mathrm{vol},j}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k})

\displaystyle=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\hat{\bm{n}}^{j}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,k}\cdot\left(\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},1}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k}),\bm{f}^{\mathrm{vol},2}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{u}_{k})\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{g}}^{j}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\hat{\bm{n}}^{j}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\circ\left(\bm{g}^{1},\bm{g}^{2}\right).

An extension of entropy-preserving methods for nonconservative hyperbolic systems to curved meshes has already been presented by Waruszewski et al.
[
104
]
for DG methods, which are a special case of SBP methods.
In fact, any SBP method can be extended to curvilinear meshes by formulating the operators on the reference element

For this reason, we do not repeat the proof of the following theorem.
It follows the same arguments as in the Cartesian case, while the treatment of the metric terms for a general fluctuation formulation has already been presented in

The only modification required is to replace the DGSEM differentiation and quadrature operators with general SBP operators, since the derivation relies solely on the SBP property and not on the specific structure of DGSEM.

6.1
Well-balanced methods using curvilinear coordinates

In this section, we present a characterization for steady states conditions of nonconservative hyperbolic systems. The motivation behind this characterization is that it allows extending well-balanced schemes from two-point finite volume fluxes to high-order methods on curved meshes.

Definition 6.2

(Steady states)
.

A solution
\bm{u}(x,t)\in Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of (
7
) is a steady state if

\partial_{t}\bm{u}=-\partial_{x}\bm{f}(\bm{u})-\bm{H}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}(\bm{u})=0,\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(193)

Therefore, given steady-state values
\bm{u}_{i}
, we seek for a semi-discretization that satisfies

\frac{\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}-\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x}+\alpha\frac{\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}+(1-\alpha)\bm{H}_{i}\frac{\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\left\llbracket\bm{g}\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}=\bm{0},

(194)

where we have explicitly introduced again a combination of the first and third form for the discretization of the nonconservative term.
We use the following well-known definition.

Definition 6.3
.

Given
\bm{u}_{i}
a steady state as in Definition
6.2
, a finite volume semi-discretization of (
7
) is said to be a well-balanced scheme if
\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{i}=\bm{0}

For sake of brevity, we will proceed with deriving an algebraic condition, similarly to the previous section, only for the semi-discretization (
194
), while the conditions and results for the other semi-discretization will be only given in a tabular form.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let

be open, and

be given. Then,

\forall\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{+}\in Y\colon\\

(196)

(197)

\forall\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+}\in Y\colon\\
{-}\bm{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})+\frac{\alpha}{2}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{num}}(\bm{u}_{-},\bm{u}_{+})\bigl(\bm{g}(\bm{u}_{+})-\bm{g}(\bm{u}_{-})\bigr)+\frac{(1-\alpha)}{2}\bm{H}(\bm{u}_{+})\bigl(\bm{g}(\bm{u}_{+})-\bm{g}(\bm{u}_{-})\bigr)=-\bm{f}(\bm{u}_{+}).

Proof.

The proof follows immediately by applying the same arguments as in the algebraic characterizations showed in Sections

Baroclinic instability

The entropy-stable numerical flux for the compressible Euler equations with the internal energy as prognostic variable (
146
) has been tested for the 3D baroclinic instability benchmark
[
100
]
(see also
[
99
]
). This configuration has become a standard test for assessing the ability of atmospheric models to reproduce midlatitude baroclinic instability.
The initial condition corresponds to a balanced state, axisymmetric solution of the deep-atmosphere system (see Appendix A of Ullrich et al.
[
100
]
). Baroclinic growth is initiated by superimposing a localized Gaussian perturbation on the zonal wind in the northern midlatitudes. The disturbance undergoes an approximately linear amplification during the first week of simulation, after which nonlinear processes lead to front formation, wave steepening, and eventual breaking.
In our case the simulation is run for 20 days, so that both the quasi-linear phase and the transition to fully nonlinear dynamics are represented. The time integration is done with the fourth-order, nine-stage, low-storage FSAL explicit Runge-Kutta method with adaptive time-stepping developed in
[
72
]
implemented in OrdinaryDiffEq.jl
[
67
]
. The spatial discretization employs an equiangular cubed–sphere grid with
16\times 16
elements on the horizontal and
8
elements in the vertical, with polynomial degree
p=5
on each of the six panels. As volume flux we employ the entropy and energy conservative flux (
143
). The long-term entropy stable behavior is illustrated in Fig.
3
, which reports the evolution of the total entropy measured relative to its initial value, and shows that the entropy stability is maintained throughout the long simulation. The surface pressure at day 10 is shown in Fig.
2
, which is in good agreement with results presented in
[
99
,
104
]
.

Summary and conclusions

In this work, we have provided a framework that enables algorithmic construction of entropy-conservative/stable numerical methods for nonconservative hyperbolic systems.
Based on a purely algebraic characterization of Tadmor’s condition, we have proven a necessary and sufficient condition for entropy conservation and stability for finite volume methods in Section
2
.
This is important since it can be used for any functional, even the preservation of pressure equilibria for the compressible Euler equations with real gas law, as discussed in Section
2.1
.
An extension to finite volume methods in fluctuation form for nonconservative systems is proposed in Section
3
, where we show that the entropy conservation condition of Castro et al.
[
16
]
is also a necessary condition, with its corresponding algebraic characterization.
Specific semi-discretizations of the nonconservative term are proposed which are amenable for algorithmic construction of affordable entropy-conservative and entropy-stable numerical fluxes for nonconservative hyperbolic systems.
In the second part of Section
3
we provide a full characterization of four specific semi-discrete forms for the nonconservative product
\bm{H}(\bm{u})\partial_{x}\bm{g}(\bm{u})
, including their corresponding algebraic characterization and entropy-conservative/stable conditions.
A linear combination of two of the forms presented is then used to construct entropy-conservative and entropy-stable numerical fluxes for nonconservative hyperbolic systems in Section
4
, where several new numerical fluxes for different nonconservative hyperbolic systems are developed, including the Euler equations with internal energy as prognostic variable and a dispersive shallow-water model.
Furthermore, the framework is extended to high-order summation-by-parts (SBP) operators on both Cartesian and curved meshes in Sections
5
and
6
, respectively.
This last extension also includes a discussion about split forms generated by SBP operators for specific choices of some the volume fluxes and a sufficient condition for the extension of well-balanced schemes to SBP operators on curvilinear coordinates.
Numerical experiments reported in Section
7
show the robustness and accuracy of the novel numerical fluxes.
