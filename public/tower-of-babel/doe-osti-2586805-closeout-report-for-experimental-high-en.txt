1 Background/Introduction
 The University of South Alabama is a comprehensive State University that offers under-
graduate and graduate degrees. The current enrollment is approximately 13,800 students. The
Department of Physics, within the College of Arts and Sciences, does not have a graduate pro-
gram but confers baccalaureate degrees. Currently, there are about 25 Physics majors with with
two (2) students enrolled in upper-division courses in this Department. The teaching mission is
a very important component to this department and this is reflected in the large teaching loads
for faculty of 12 contact hours per semester. The University of South Alabama gives 1/3 release
time from teaching for Professor Jenkins so he can devote this time to research work on the
Mu2e experiment.
 This grant funded the research activities of Professor Merrill Jenkins to work on Mu2e from
2021 to 2025 and an undergraduate student in the summer of 2023. Historically, this group was
funded by DoE for approximately 21 years with funding ending in grant year 2013. Funding
resumed for Professor Jenkins in 2021 in the form of a three year grant to support his efforts on
the Mu2e experiment. A renewal for this grant was submitted in 2023 and a one year renewal
was awarded. Jenkins submitted a three year renewal to this one year grant in 2024, which was
declined. This closeout report tabulates the accomplishments over the four years, spanning these
two grants.
 Funding from all grants helped to include approximately seven undergraduate students from
the Physics Department and three graduate assistants from the School of Computer and Informa-
tion Sciences. Funding from this grant supported one student from the College of Engineering,
due to the paucity of Physics majors, to assist in its research. Jenkins’ experience is mainly
with fixed target experiments at Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory. These experiments
were E-705, E-771, E-871 (HyperCP) and E-921 (CKM) before it was canceled for budgetary
reasons. After CKM, Jenkins joined the Compact Muon Solenoid (CMS) experiment as an asso-
ciate member via the High Energy Physics Group at the Florida State University. After leaving
CMS, Jenkins was briefly a member of Belle-II. When the University of South Alabama’s grant
proposal was declined he withdrew from Belle-II and joined the Mu2e experiment (in December
2014). Jenkins used his release time during the academic year and summer research time on the
Mu2e experiment.
 The University of South Alabama High Energy Physics group is part of the Mu2e Cosmic
Ray Veto (CRV) group. Jenkins supports the hardware quality assurance database for CRV
construction. He also played an important role in the early determination of whether a search
for the conversion µ− N → e+ N′ is feasible with the Mu2e spectrometer. Jenkins started studies
to determine if the Mu2e spectrometer can be used for a Majoron search (µ → e J) which was
abandoned.
 More recently, Jenkins worked on the CRV expert controls and monitoring system for Slow
Controls. He has developed a local simulated CRV system that includes a software Input/Output
controller read out by EPICS which is needed to act as input for Control Systems Studios
Phoebus with alarms. [2], [3] With this in place, Jenkins can design the Phoebus display page
layouts for the CRV. The idea is to have the upper-level graphical user interface ready to interface
with the lower-level electronics and readout software when it is installed in the Mu2e experimental
hall. This system is also installed on the Mu2e DAQ computer in the wideband experiment hall
at Fermilab. Soon this system will be interfaced to the exiting FEB electronics used for CRV
panel testing and it will use the archiver in place in the Mu2e experimental hall DCS computers.

2 Project Objectives: Mu2e
2.1 Contribution to Mu2e
 Jenkins has worked on the Mu2e experiment since September 2014 (prior to formal admission
to the experiment) within the Cosmic Ray Veto (CRV) group. Initially, his activities were on
two efforts: the magnetic shields for the CRV Front End Boards (FEB’s) and the development
of the Quality Assurance/Quality Control hardware Database (QA/QC Database). He has
contributed, in the early stages, to a study for the feasibility of a second physics search for the
Mu2e Experiment: µ− N → e+ N′ .
2.1.1 Quality Assurance Hardware Database for the CRV
 Jenkins supports the Quality Assurance/Quality Control hardware database (QA/QC Database)
which documents the construction of the CRV modules. Jenkins lead the the CRV database ef-
fort and he is the Mu2e CRV QA/QC database administrative manager and continues to support
the database.
 The actual database is written by the Fermilab Data Management and Applications group
who provides a Python interface that Jenkins uses to transport data with Python scripts into the
database and read entries from the database. These scripts allows workers without a Fermilab
services account and are not database administrators to make entries and query the database.
This interface will not allow deletions from the database, which only a database administrator
can do. Jenkins communicated with the head of these groups and with students entering results
into the database to refine the tables and the interface as new needs appear. Now that this
system is operational, Jenkins provided Python scripts to access the information so that it is
useful for the CRV constructions groups.
2.1.2 Development of an Expert User GUI to Control/Monitor the CRV
 One of the objectives of the previous grant was to contributed to the CRV online monitoring
and control. Considerable effort was invested to develop an expert-user online graphical user
interface (GUI) to monitor and control the CRV front end boards (FEB). This includes read out
and setting the bias voltages, current readout and temperature readout.
 The GUI interface application chosen by the Mu2e DCS/Slow Controls group is Control
System Studio Phoebus. [3] This application reads/controls process variables defined in a running
EPICS system which reads out or writes to the electronics of the detector. [2] These EPICS
process variables contain the alarm limit conditions that Phoebus uses. Changes in alarm states
are signaled to Phoebus by an event streaming program, apache kafka, which uses an information
organizing program, apache zookeeper. [4], [5] All of these programs and applications are open
source.
 Since there is no complete operational hardware (FEB’s) and software interface (Data Con-
trol System, DCS) for the entire CRV, collectively an Input/Output controller, a software sim-
ulation of this system (softIoC) was constructed. This is required to set up the Phoebus display
pages and the alarm system. Jenkins has accomplished this for the entire CRV and has Phoebus
display pages and alarms for the entire CRV. The necessary files, from the EPICS database
files which define the process variables to the html files used by Phoebus to setup the displays
and alarms are written by a series of Python scripts that Jenkins wrote whose input are from
csv files generated from Excel spread sheets. With this system, the configuration and layout is
easily changed. This system is designed so that it will interface with the actual experimental
Input/Output controller once they are installed.

 The proposed activities for Grant Year 2 (2022 to 2023) do not match the state of the
experiment at the end of calendar year 2022 and with the tasks left over from 2021 and 2022.
Work was done to support the QA/QC hardware database by changing Python scripts from
v2.7 to v3 (talk given to the CRV group: Mu2e-doc-42631); including the Python scripts into
the Mu2e GitHub distribution; adding entries upon request to the database and consultation in
the use of the scripts. The effort to connect CRV components was not started, but it has only
been needed recently with changes with entries of the CMB; Work was started on laying out
an expert shifter slow controls interface (EPICS/Phoebus). The cosmic-ray CRV test has not
started. The work on an online database work is being done by various offline groups. The work
on the µ → e J search has been abandoned after a meeting with the Simulations group.
 Considerable effort was invested in developing a (Control System Studio) Phoebus graphical
user interface to control and monitor the CRV. This program, chosen by the Mu2e DCS/Slow
Controls group for this purpose. The instillation of the chain of programs needed to setup
Phoebus is straight forward and is done easily. However, the pre-built Phoebus package does not
include the alarm package. It is necessary to download a compatible version of java development
took kit (jdk18) then a program builder and management program for java, apache maven. [6]
Once, configured, maven is used to build Phoebus code downloaded from github. [7]
 The description of the development of the setup of the actual Phoebus display pages and
alarm system is not a linear step-by-step description because of the state of the online documen-
tation. It tells you what is available, but it does not give specific enough examples of the crucial
config files and html files that implement these features. Many solutions were found by trial and
error complicated by Phoebus always loaded the last configuration used. This resulted in many
fresh rebuilds of the Phoebus software system.
 The display pages were setup with a few widgets with desired properties and actions then
saved. The html formatted file was studied so that a Python script could be written to populate
the display page with widgets for the entire CRV. Kafka had to be configured with alarm topics,
but the actual alarm tree branches were configured from xml files read in by Phoebus. Several
talks were given to the EPICS DCS/Slow Controls group (mu2e-doc-42717 and mu2e-doc-42849)
on the progress of setting this system up. A contributed talk on this work was given for the
Mu2e Collaboration at SESAPS2022 at the University of Mississippi, Oxford MS. [9]
 Also, Jenkins attended (via zoom) CRV meetings, DCS/EPICS (when classes permitted),
Mu2e bi-weekly Mu2e meetings and Mu2e collaboration meeting sessions and Mu2e IB meetings
as his teaching schedule permitted over the last year.

3.4   Grant Year 4: 2024-2025 - Up to March 31, 2025 (One Year Grant)
 The final grant is for a duration of one year; one year is reported from the time the previous
application was submitted (December 1, 2023) to the end of the Grant Year, March 31, 2025.
Activities were divided between supporting the CRV QC/QA hardware database, procuring a
replacement PC with installing AlmaLinux 9 operating system and installing associated software
to support Mu2e projects and the deployment of the EPICS/Phoebus expert user Graphical User
Interface with Alarms to Mu2e systems. The CRV hardware database portion included helping

 5

the Northern Illinois group with checking the status of some SiPMS needed for a publication
they were preparing and helping the CRV module group pinpoint data entry errors into the
database and correcting them.
 A Levono ThinkStation P3 Ultra-MT-core i5 13600k 3.5 GHz VPro Enterprise computer
was procured and delivered in mid-March, 2024. Funds were split between the grant and the
Department with the hope that left over grant funds could be used to hire summer students. The
AlmaLinux 9 operating system was installed by March 21 and a permanent IP address was setup,
tkinter for python graphics and ROOT were installed by early May. This system was connected
to the new 10 G internet port in the building during the summer. The jdk-22.0.1 development
environment, apache-maven-3.9.6 software management, kafka 2.13-3.70 event streaming
platform and the latest version of EPICS were installed. Subsequently, the latest version of
Phoebus was downloaded and built. This system has been used to develop Phoebus GUI and
alarm system that has been installed on Fermilab computer systems.
 Developing the Phoebus GUI display with alarm system transitioned from simulated chan-
nels to live channels. A series of python scripts were written to build the .xml files used by
Phoebus to make the display pages and the alarm-tree. This allows flexible way to configure
the GUI and alarm tree and easily add more channels as they become available. Python scripts
on the University of South Alabama AlmaLinux 9 system were adapted to write .xml files that
configured straw-man alarm trees. These files were built to learn how to configure five-level
alarm trees. These files were written to directories that can be copied to the Fermilab systems
to setup Phoebus on the Fermilab systems.
 The chain of the latest versions of the applications jdk, maven, zookeeper/kafka were in-
stalled on the Mu2e DCS computer. The latest version of Phoebus was downloaded and built
on the Mu2e DCS system.
 Jenkins learned how to access the Mu2e Wideband laboratory CRV test stand process
variables (PV’s) accessible through the Mu2e DCS. These live channels were defined in a local
file with additional prefix. A set of python scripts were written on the local AlmaLinux 9
computer in a directory assigned to the Wideband computer to setup the alarm tree for all
channels. These files are copied to the DCS to setup the Phoebus alarms system. Briefly, the
Phoebus alarm-server was working with these channels.
 Soon after this work was done, the Fermilab computers were updated with the AlmaLinux
9 operating system and were not available to users. After the Almalinux 9 installation, EPICS
process variables were re-organized so that the Wideband test stand computer PV’s were not
available on the DCS. The Wideband PV’s are available on the Mu2e DCS Test Stand Node.
The good news is that with this re-organization, a new set of PV names were assigned to actual
live-channels on the DCS computer that will be used on the experiment. The channels available
now are mostly rack temperatures in actual racks in the Mu2e DAQ root and power supply
values for these racks and temperature.
 This list of these PV names was sent to Jenkins who wrote a new set of python scripts
in a directory for DCS development. In this directory, python scripts parsed the names and
constructed the alarm-tree. These files are copied to the DCS computers. The advantage of this
system is that it is scalable and as new channels become available, the script will be used to
generate new alarm-trees. The new alarm topics were setup in kafka, required by the Phoebus
alarm server. The latest version of Phoebus was downloaded and built on the DCS computer
and the new alarm tree .xml files were copied over. The alarm-server in Phoebus was configured

 6

with the new tree, Phoebus was started and the alarm-tree was working with live channels on
the Mu2e DCS computer.
 Jenkins could see the utility of adding display page to the Phoebus GUI so that expert users
would not have to switch a different window/program to look at the actual values if an alarm
was raised. Python scripts were developed to generate read-back display pages to show these
values. This display page was used to check to ensure that all channels were included in the
alarm-tree. At a Mu2e DCS meeting a request was made to include a rack temperature vs time
plot. These have been implemented. This system is capable of reading out and monitoring the
available channels that will be in place for the actual Mu2e experiment.
 Jenkins has documented this progress in “slack” and reported this progress in several Mu2e
DCS and Mu2e CRV meetings. He gave a talk for the Mu2e DCS meeting on August 12,
2024. [11]
 Jenkins gave a contributed talk at SESAPS 2024, “The Mu2e Experiment and Progress in
the Year 2024.” The abstract and slides on mu2e-doc-db-50533. The talk was given at UNC
Charlotte (Charlotte, NC) on Thursday, October 25, 2024, session J04 - Muon Physics. Talk
number J04.00003.
