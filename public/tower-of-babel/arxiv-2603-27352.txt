Topological Quarantine and the Standard Candle for Network-Glass Rigidity
Kejun Liu

Abstract
We study rigidity percolation in random covalent networks to establish the pure topological baseline of the floppy-to-rigid transition. Using a generating-function mean-field theory on configuration-model graphs, we report three results. First, we prove—within the locally tree-like (mean-field) approximation—that the onset of the topological giant rigid component (GRC) coincides with the mechanical Maxwell isostatic point ( ⟨ r ⟩ c = 2.4 \langle r\rangle_{c}=2.4 ), providing a clean topological lower bound from which pebble-game deviations in finite-dimensional glasses can be measured. Second, finite-size scaling extended to N = 32 000 N=32\,000 locates a sharp internal geometric marker at ⟨ r ⟩ ∗ = 2.431 ± 0.002 \langle r\rangle^{*}=2.431\pm 0.002 , residing inside the experimental Boolchand intermediate phase window [ 2.35 , 2.47 ] [2.35,2.47] 3 ; 25 , where the rigid backbone crosses the classical ER percolation threshold S ∞ = 1 / 8 S_{\infty}=1/8 . Third, we demonstrate via controlled perturbation simulations that generic enthalpic stress avoidance delays but cannot compress this window, while chemical medium-range order (MRO) is the necessary ingredient—a conclusion directly supported by recent NMR evidence that homopolar defect fractions in GeSe 2 fall well below the predicted rigidity-rescue threshold 7 .

Network model and local rigidity.—
A random covalent network is modeled as a configuration-model graph
14

N

\langle r\rangle

\max(0,\,2k_{i}-3)

C_{i}=k_{i}/2+\max(0,\,2k_{i}-3)

C_{i}\geq 3

k_{i}\geq 3

C_{2}=2<3

C_{k}\geq 3
for
k\geq 3

S_{\infty}

The mean floppy-mode density is

The non-trivial root

exactly within the locally tree-like (mean-field) limit

Discussion.—
Our results establish a hierarchy of physical mechanisms for the Boolchand intermediate phase. The topological baseline pins rigidity onset at
\langle r\rangle_{c}=2.4
with marker
\langle r\rangle^{*}=2.431

\langle r\rangle^{*}
rightward (
\Delta\approx+0.03

P_{\rm ring}^{*}=0.496

Scope and outlook.—
Our framework addresses the
geometric
constraints governing rigidity percolation in the mean-field limit. The pure-entropic configuration-model serves as a
standard candle
—a zero-MRO reference frame against which real glasses deviate quantitatively. Measuring how a real glass deviates from our configuration-model baseline (
\langle r\rangle_{c}=2.4
,
\langle r\rangle^{*}=2.431
,
P_{\rm AA}^{*}\approx 0.41
,
P_{\rm ring}^{*}\approx 0.50
) directly extracts its MRO density and chemical defect fraction. This confirms that MRO is a
functional necessity
: without it, local stoichiometry topologically prohibits macroscopic rigidity. Furthermore, the fragility minimum at
\langle r\rangle\approx 2.40
–
2.44

25

\langle r\rangle^{*}=2.431

T_{g}
involves physics beyond this static picture, integrating our geometric standard candle with mode-coupling theory remains a promising direction. Finally, the same mean-field logic—systemic percolation of locally locked states through a sparse backbone—underlies committed-minority tipping in social networks

;
5
