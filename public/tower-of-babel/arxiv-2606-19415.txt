Corrigendum: order extreme points and solid convex hulls
A. Ianina and T. Oikhberg, M.A. Tursi

Abstract
. We correct some errors found in [T. Oikhberg and M.A. Tursi, Order extreme points and solid convex hulls , The Mathematical Legacy of Victor Lomonosov (ed. R. Aron et.al.), de Gryuter, 2020, 297–315.]



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
46B22, 46B42

The goal of this note is two-fold: (i) to correct some annoying typos in
[
2
]
, and (ii) to correct some of the proofs. The enumeration of sections and statements (theorems, definitions, examples etc.) in the Arxiv preprint is different from that in the published version; the numbers from the published version will be given in square brackets.

(1)
In several statements in Sections 3 [19.3] and 4 [19.4], the convexity assumptions are omitted. Specifically:

\bullet
Proposition 3.1 [19.7] – Separation – should read:

Suppose
\tau
is a sufficiently rich topology on a Banach lattice
X
, and
A\subset X_{+}
is a
\tau
-closed positive-solid bounded
convex
subset of
X_{+}
. Suppose, furthermore,
x\in X_{+}
does not belong to
A
. Then there exists
f\in X^{\tau}_{+}
so that
f(x)>\sup_{a\in A}f(a)
.

\bullet
Theorem 4.1 [19.10] – “Solid” Krein-Milman – should read:

Any
\tau
-compact positive-solid
convex
subset
A
of
X_{+}
coincides with the
\tau
-closed positive-solid convex hull of its order extreme points.

\bullet
Corollary 4.2 [19.11] should read:

Any
\tau
-compact solid
convex
subset of
X
coincides with the
\tau
-closed solid convex hull of its order extreme points.

Without convexity, these results may fail. For instance, for Corollary 4.2, we can take
X=(\mathbb{R}^{2},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
, and
A=\{(x,0):|x|\leq 1\}\cup\{(0,y):|y|\leq 1\}
. The set
A
is closed when
\tau
is the norm topology and solid, but the convex hull of
A
is much larger than
A
itself.

(2)
There is a hole in the proof of Theorem 7.1 [19.30]: on page 17 line -2 [page 313 line -6], it is claimed that, for any
x\in C_{+}\backslash\{0\}
, we have
x\wedge u\neq 0
. The current argument doesn’t permit to reach this conclusion. However, this can be fixed as follows:

Patch on the proof of Theorem 19.30.

Denote by
B\subset X
the band generated by
u
in
X
. By
[
1
, Proposition 1.2.3]
,
B
is closed (and solid); by
[
1
, Proposition 1.2.3]
,
B_{+}
consists of all
z\in X_{+}
so that
z=\vee_{n}(z\wedge nu)
.
As
u
is a quasi-interior point of
Y
, we conclude that
C^{\prime}\subset Y\subset B
, hence
C\subset B
. So,
x=\vee_{n}(x\wedge nu)
, which implies
x\wedge nu\neq 0
for large enough
n
. As
x\wedge nu\leq n(x\wedge u)
, we conclude that
x\wedge u\neq 0
.
∎

(3)
The proof of the implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
in Proposition 4.3 [19.12] is incorrect: the set
D
constructed on page 10 [306], first paragraph, is not solid, hence it is not guaranteed to have any order extreme points.

Proposition 4.3 [19.12] can be established in a rather roundabout way. More specifically, Theorem 7.1 [19.30] implies:

Corollary
.

For a Banach lattice
X
, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

X
has the Radon-Nikodym Property
(
RNP
)
.

(2)

X
has the Krein-Milman Property
(
KMP
)
.

(3)

X
has the Solid Krein-Milman Property
(
SKMP
)
– that is, any closed bounded convex solid subset of
X
is the closure of the solid convex hull of its order extreme points.

(4)

Any closed bounded convex solid subset of
X
has an order extreme points.

Above, item (4) is a
verbatim
repetition of Proposition 4.3[19.12](1), while item (3) is nothing but a restatement of Proposition 4.3[19.12](2).

Proof.

The implication
(3)\Rightarrow(4)
is immediate, and
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
follows by combining Theorem 2.2 [19.2] with Corollary 2.4 [19.4]. The equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
is known, see the references cited in
[
2
]
.

The proof of Theorem 7.1 [19.30] actually produces, for any Banach lattice
X
failing the RNP, a closed bounded convex solid
E\subset X
without any order extreme points. This gives
\lnot(1)\Rightarrow\lnot(4)
, or equivalently,
(4)\Rightarrow(1)
.
∎
