MEDICAL MEETING, RANGOON
Morphia Injector's Septicemia.
At a meeting of the Burma Assistant Surgeonss
Association the following paper was read by
Dr. C. S. Krishnasawmy on the peculiar disease
first described in Rangoon and known as Whit-
more's disease, or Morphia Injector's Septicaemia.
Soon after my taking over sub-charges of the Patholo-
gical and Post-Mortem Department of the Rangoon
General Hospital towards the end of April 1910, I found
that there was an enormous amount of pathological
material available in Rangoon that required accurate
working up and noting down for further research ; so I
began right earnestly to do so under the inspiring
personality of Captain A. Whitmore, who has been my
chief for the last five years and to whom my sincere
thanks are due for the cheerful encouragement and
ungrudging guidance which have largely contributed to
my working out of this disease. Both of us first
published our results in the Indian Medical Gazette of
July 1912 and, later on, one of us (A. W.) again in the
Journal of Hygiene of April 1913, reviewing altogether
about forty cases ; Captain Whitmore opened a discus-
sion at the Bacteriological Section of the eightieth annual
meeting of the British Medical Association in the third
week of July 1912, reviewing the same number of cases.
Major Knapp, i-m.s., published the reports of a few
cases in the Indian Medical Gazette of August 1915.
But, since then, as no further or continuous report of the
disease has been published, I propose to review the
.disease as I have personally seen it for the last six years,
covering over two hundred cases. These cases include
those seen in the mortuary and wards of the Rangoon
General Hospital and those from the Rangoon Jail,
whose reports have been published and whose patholo-
gical examinations were undertaken by us, and the case
I recently had in the Lunatic Asylum sent in by a
medical practitioner from outside.
Early History of the Disease.
During May and June 1910, when we were having a
few cases of human glanders admitted into the Rangoon
General Hospital, which we reported to the Health and
Veterinary Officers of the Rangoon Municipality, I
noticed in the Post-Mortems done during that period
that there were cases of Broncho-Pneumonia having al-
most the same pathological lesions in the lungs as glan-
dei's, but less acute, and in persons who had apparently
nothing to do with horses and from whose lesions
typical glanders bacilli could not be isolated by culture,
thus throwing some doubt on the real nature of the

disease. These lesions also all resembled tuberculosis
of the lungs?a very common disease in Rangoon?but
were more acute, and the persistent absence of the
tubercle bacilli disclosed the different nature of the
disease. As a matter of fact, Captain A. Whitmore,
i.M.S., assured me that such cases have been returned
by h'im as acute tuberculosis before; unfortunately
I could not get more than the records of about one
hundred and twenty post-mortems before I came, and
in these owing to various reasons, bacteriological
.examination results were not noted and therefore I am
unable to find any data whatsoever on which to connect
the present new disease as having been spotted before ;
from that time fairly accurate bacteriological notes of
the post-mortems have been kept, and naturally the
later cases of the series are fuller than the earlier ones,
since the nature of the disease had to be worked out
and studied in its various aspects gradually as more
cases presented themselves.
Designation.
Since the disease had been found mostly (95 per
cent.) in the wastrels of the city of Rangoon, with
morphia injection marks, and in almost every case the
organism has been isolated from the spleen, whetliei
there has been or not any macroscopic lesion of any of
the viscera, we propose the designation of " Morphia
Injector's Septicemia" to ^ the disease and to the
bacillus?" the new bacillus" as it has not been classi-
fied yet.
Before proceeding with the description of the disease
1 propose to avoid all statistical data as they are very
confusing to the audience.
Definition.
The disease is an acute or rarely sub-acute septicemia
due to a new bacillus occurring mostly in the morphia
habitues of Rangoon, frequently characterised by
"ranulomata of the viscera, and often with no other
symptoms than fever and sometimes cough, ending
generally fatally in a few months after the symptoms
have once developed.
Varieties.
There are two varieties, namely " The acute"
lasting up to one month and "the sub-acute" or
"chronic" lasting up to two or three months.
Incubation Period.
In human beings is unknown. Cases have been
recorded to be without symptoms from five months to
one year after the morphia habit has been stopped and
when under close observation. In the guinea-pigs,
when inoculated subcutaneously, the illness was noted
on the third to the fifth day; when inoculated
intraperitoneally the animals were ill in from 18 to 36
hours; when fed with infected material in small
quantities the animals were ill in 10 to 13 days, and
if in large quantities in two or three days. After
a varying period of geneial ill-health the patient
crets irregular, moderate fever mostly in the evenings,
may be with rigors ; later on, he may complain of some
cough More often the patients generally say that they
were all right before the fever, meaning thereby that
the morphia injection did not make them bed-ridden ;
many such cases attend the Out-Patient Department
regularly for dressing their septic morphia ulcers,
themselves emaciated, miserab e looking, and certainly
below normal health. I do not think that the lower
classes of natives of India bother much about their
health until something or othei makes them bed-
t-idden or suffer agony. /
Etiology.
1. Predisposing Causes.?General debility due to
insanitary surroundings, malnutrition, or disease, is a
predisposing factor.
Ninety per cent, of our cases have been poorly nourish-
ed, often emaciated , but very few have been in fairly
good health, especially those cases in whom morphia
marks were not present.
2. Incidence.?About one in twenty post-mortems
in the General Hospital, Rangoon, is a case of this
disease and one in twenty of this disease is a non-
morpliia injector. I am told that the disease is getting
rarer in the General Hospital and that in the Town
Lock-up less number of injection cases come up. Males
are mostly affected, although a few cases in women are
recorded. No age is free, except the young ones in their
teens, which may be due to their not getting into the
morphia habit by that age. The cases have occurred
throughout the year having no special seasonal preval-
ence. No cases have been reported up to now except
in the city of Rangoon. The victim is often a neglected
and emaciated wastrel of the city, a Chinaman, Burman
or a Mabomedan, addicted to the morphia habit, or a
South Indian Hindu of the lower classes who mixes
freely with the preceding. It has not been found at all
amongst the Europeans nor among the better classes
of the Eastern races who neither have the morphia
habit nor mix socially with those who have. The
disease has occurred mostly in morphia injectors, about
ninety-five per cent, of our cases being morphia
injectors.
3. Specific Cause.?The disease is due to a specific
bacillus closely allied in its cultural and pathogenic
properties to the glanders and tubercle bacilli.
4. General Cause.?As it had occurred mostly in
morphia habitues, it is believed that the morphia habit
in any form as carried under the conditions existing in
Rangoon is a general cause.
Signs and Symptoms.
The patient seeks admission into the hospital for
(1) Extensive septic sores over his thighs, upper
arms back, buttocks, and abdomen due to morphia
injection ; the sores might be quite sloughy or almost
healed, or few in number, or extensive in nature.
(2) Moderate fever which is irregular, being either
continuous or remittent, never intermittent unless
complicated. There may be rigor.
(3) Cough and fever of a few weeks' duration.
(4) Subcutaneous abscesses in any portion of the
body (due to the new bacillus).
There is emaciation and the cough is almost dry,
exhausting and barking in character, but the patient
is very ill in comparison with the physical signs and
cough, which resemble those of acute bronchitis only.
Nothing very definite could generally be made out in
the lungs; few dry or moist rales, patchy dullness,
diminished breath sounds, may be noted all over the
lungs, not necessarily at the apex. Pleuritic pain may
be present, usually there is no sputum, but, if present,
might be thin and sanious, or thick and purulent,
especially if a mixed infection is present. The blood
may show an increase of polymorphonuclear leucocytes
as well in septic cases, but usually only a largo
mononucleosis is present; the spleen is generally
enlarged, the urine in cases of specific nephritis may
show cysts, puss cells, albumen, and the new bacillus.
The urine might be turbid. The patient generally
resorts to the hospital when moribund.

Complications and Sequels.
Owing to the very septic nature of the sores the
patient may develop a concurrent pysemia or septi-
cemia with the streptococcus or staphylococcus, but
up to now no such cases have died of tetanus although
the sores are very dirty. If the liver, spleen, kidney,
or prostate is affected, local tenderness or pain, jaundice
or symptoms of nephritis and strangury may be present,
according to the viscus affected.
Pathology, Morbid Anatomy, and Bacteriology.
In most of the cases the lungs are affected showing
macroscopic lesions ; the commonest lesion is a patch of
greyish pink consolidation from about the size of a pea
to a bean, the central portion being pale and generally
soft and friable, but not so soft as the ordinary tuber-
cular caseation; the outer zone is markedly congested
and the seat of minute haemorrhages.
Upon the cut surface of an incised lung the patch
stands up slightly above the surrounding healthy tissue,
and is sharply defined and drier in appearance than a
patch of broncho-pneumonic consolidation due to
pneumococcal infection ; in a few cases there have been
patches in which the central portions have softened
forming minute abscesses ; the suppuration may progress
so as to form quite large cavities, but such cavity forma-
tion is rare. Patches of this acute consolidation are
distributed irregularly throughout the lungs, and the
infection appears to have no predilection for any parti-
cular lobe or part of a lobe. The relatively small
patches of consolidation may coalesce so as to form very
large areas extending over many square inches ; even in
such cases the individual areas can be distinguished by
their hemorrhagic borders, but in other Cases the whole
of the consolidation, extensive though it be, presents a
unifoi'mly pale pinkish appearance. It has seemed to us
possible that these extensive areas of consolidation
indicate that the disease has run a somewhat "chronic "
course, and Ave have provisionally classified them in our
records as " chronic " cases and the others as " atmte."
In favour of such a view is the observation that fre-
quently the bacilli are quite scarce in these large areas,
while in the small areas with acute hemorrhagic
borders the bacilli are always exceedingly numerous.
"Where an area of consolidation lies close to the lung
surface the pleura over it is acutely inflamed and irre-
gularly raised so as to form an injected rough surface
with a tinge of yellow in the centre corresponding in
extent with the size of the inflamed patch beneath.
There is not as a rule much pleural exudate, and adhesions
if present are ill-organised, being simply those due to
an acute pleurisy. In no case have we found adhesion
so organised as to suggest a chronic inflammation com-
parable with that so usually due to tubercular infection,
nor have we so far met with any pleural exudate suffi-
cient in amount or of such a character as to warrant a
suggestion of empyema. The exudate may be consider-
able, but it consists of thick cheesy .material usually
without signs of liquefaction.
Next in the order of frequency we found the spleen,
liver, kidney, prostate, mesenteric and femoral glands,
subcutaneous and intramuscular tissue all specifically
affected, the last two in the shape of abscesses ; other-
wise the lesion is more or less identical with that of the
lung. The macroscopical lesions may vary from the size
of a pin's head to the size of a hazel-nut with litemor-
rhagic periphery and yellowish centre which may or may
not he soft. These patches are generally larger in the
lungs and the kidney than in the other organs. All the
organs so all'ected were enlarged and softer in consis-
tency, especially so the spleen. Liquefaction forming
abscesses are more common in the liver, spleen, and
kidney than in the lung.
(1) The bacillus was isolated from the spleen even
if there were no macroscopical lesions anywhere in the
body, thus favouring the intestinal route theory.
('J) In certain cases large numbers of bacilli have
been seen in the smears and isolated from the femoral
set of glands and not in any other glands, more
numerous in fact than could be explained by
filtration through the blood, thus rather favouring the
morphia injection theory as a causative factor.
(3) In some other cases the bacilli could be isolated
only from the lung and from nowhere else in the body,
thus favouring the respiratory route as a factor. The
lesions anywhere might be discrete or confluent.
Histologically the lesion is a granuloma, but there are
no giant cells. There is an even mixture of polymorpho-
nuclear and large mononuclear leucocytes, and few
pigmented endothelial cells may be surrounding a
central necrotic area ; the outer layer being composed
of small round cells, red blood cells, and polymorphus;
there is capillary congestion and even luemorrhage
surrounding this area. Bacilli in varying degrees of
disintegration could be seen in the centre.
Bacteriology.
The infective organism is a thin, slightly curved,
beaded or granular^ non-gram-staining, Nnon-acid, fast,
motile bacillus, measuring two to six microns in length and
'3 to '5 microns in breadth, and of morphology of glanders
and tubercle bacilli. It stains fairly easily with the
ordinary aniline dyes in solution; stained with Leishman's
stain it shows bipolar staining, the poles being purple and
the body blue and the interpolar space almost clear,
this staining reaction in smears from inflammatory
lesions being fairly characteristic. The bacillus may be
intra or extra cellular in tissues ; in cultures the bacillus
tends to become shorter, stouter, and less curved;
the motility in a young broth culture is of a lashing,
quivering, or serpentine character. Pleomorphism
in human tissues is rare; the bacillus shows no
special affinity for Neisser's stain, probably numerous
peritriehal flagelhe are present, but no definite capsule
has been found. The bacilli are almost coccoid or cocco-
bacillary in form in smears taken from the peritoneum
of the guinea-pig. Although spore-like bodies have
been seen in salt agar cultures and in inoculation
experiments, we are unable to dogmatise on the point,
as our facilities for carrying out these tests have been
meagre. Their resistance, qualitative or quantitative, to
physical or chemical reagents, has not been worked out.
The cultures do not have any special smell.
Growth upon all the usual culture media is rapid and
luxuriant; and occurs under both terobic and anaerobic
conditions, though more luxuriantly under the former.
Classification of the Bacillus.
The bacillus has not yet been classified, although some
specimens of cultures have been sent to bacteriologists
in England. I believe it comes between the myco-
bacteria and the coryne-bacteria, that is, between the
tubercle and the glanders group of bacilli, as in its
cultural properties, inoculation results in animals, and
the disease, as seen in human beings, forms a link
between the tubercular and glanders affection. This
belongs to the class of streptotriclue and sporotriclue,
although it does not resemble any specific variety of
the same described by Dr. Foulerton.
In staininq it resembles the glanders bacillus being
non-grani-staining, non-acid, fast, and rarely beaded
appearance, but it resembles the tubercular bacillus
in its being thin, curved, and granular. It resembles
neither, being motile.

Culturally it is vigorous, growing like glanders, but
differs from it in its appearance on glycerine agar, and
by being wrinkled resembles tubercle culture. Its
growth is sometimes similar to glanders, only it does
not assume the honey or cafe au lait colour which the
glanders culture assumes in a few days on potato.
Inoculation.?It resembles glanders in giving the
typical Strauss's testicular reaction, but resembles tuber-
culosis in producing granulomata in all the viscera. It
is not so acute as glanders, and so rarely produces pus
and does not kill so soon; but the lesions are not so
chronic as tubercle, nor does it produce the typical
cheesy material, nor does it allow the patient to linger
as lonff as tubercle. In fact both tubercle and this
bacillus infection have been found in the same lung.
Like glanders and farcy, it affects the lungs and sub-
cutaneous tissues), and, like tuberculosis, the internal
viscera and the glands.
Histologically the lesions show polymorphonuclear
leucocytes like glanders, and large and small mono-
nuclears as in tubercle ; but no giant cells are seen.
Under these circumstances I do not think any useful
purpose will be served by designating the bacillus
" pseudomallei " or " Pseudo Tubercle " as suggested in
certain quarters, for by giving it a name which is really
a pseudonym, the true classification might be postponed,
and hence for the last six years I have been returning
the disease as "Morphia Injector's Septicemia " (N. B.)
in the post-mortem records of the General Hospital,
Rangoon, N. B. meaning New Bacillus.
There is prevalent in Rangoon a peculiar septicamiic
disease caused by a bacillus with constant characters
of which some are sufficiently peculiar to distinguish it
from all pathogenic bacteria previously known to us.
The disease is mostly prevalent among the morphia
injectors. The pathological lesions, from which such
bacilli can be easily isolated in pure culture, are often
so peculiar in appearance that this appearance alone
would suggest doubts as to the causation of the lesions
by any of the usual pathogenic bacteria, and after a
short experience would warrant an anticipatory
diagnosis of this particular infection. If guinea-pigs
be fed with food or drink contaminated with pure
cultures of this bacillus the animals speedily die, and
after death not only can bacilli be easily recovered
from the various organs, but also lesions in every way
similar to those found in the human subjects are
present, thus satisfying Koch s postulates.
The human disease characterised by the presence of
these bacilli and by the usual presence of these peculiar
lesions is a septicajmic disease attended by symptoms
which may bear very close clinical resemblance to
glanders or tuberculosis, but the distinction is easy if
a proper bacteriological examination is carried out.
Although the disease is particularly prevalent among
morphia injectors, I believe that, just as in guinea-pigs
the infection can be conveyed by contaminated food
and drink so in man such contamination also affords
an additional method and means of infection; more-
over, the excretion of vast numbers of living bacilli in
the urine and probably in the sputum of many of the
infected persons living in crowded insanitary surround-
ings makes contamination of food, earth or floor, air
and water in those places inevitable.
Finally, while our clinical knowledge of the disease is
small, that upon clinical signs only, 110 more than a
strong suspicion of infection can be entertained, jet 0111
bacteriological knowledge is sufficiently exact and its
application sufficiently easy to allow such suspicion to
be readily confirmed or denied by a reference to the
laboratory. ..... .
I hope 1 have succeeded in bimging forward sunicient
facts to stimulate the curiosity of medical men in
other places, and that by their work and observation
it may be made evident whether or not this infection
is of merely local or of much wider interest, and whether
any attempt at scientific therapeusis has been rewarded
with any satisfactory results.
