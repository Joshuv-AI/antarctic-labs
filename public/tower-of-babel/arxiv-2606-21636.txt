Second order mixed moment inequalities based on Gram matrices
Sergio Scarlatti

Abstract
Recently [LT; Theorem 3.1] showed an extension of Walker’s inequality [W] based on N = 3 N=3 random variables. In this note we prove that extension is just a particular three-dimensional instance of a general family of second order mixed moment inequalities based on Gram matrices of arbitrary random vectors. We also discuss some implications of these inequalities on Cramer-Rao lower bound for biased estimators.



1
Introduction and general settings

There have been recent reconsiderations of a result of Walker [W] showing a self-improvement of Cauchy - Schwarz inequality, which is effective for non centered random variables, see [S] and [LT]. In particular [LT] presents a general inequality for
N=3
r.v.’s which includes Walker’s inequality as a special case. The aim of this note is to show that, by using Gram matrices, a much more general result holds, and, furthermore, this generality can be used in statistical estimation theory to build improved bounds on the MSE of biased estimators. To this aim let
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})
be an
N
-dimensional random vector whose components belong to
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, endowed with the inner product
\langle U,V\rangle=E(UV)
. By definition the Gram matrix of
X
is
\text{Gram}(X)=G_{N}\equiv(E(X_{h}X_{k}))_{h,k=1}^{N}
. Hence
G_{N}=({g}_{hk})_{h,k=1}^{N}
is symmetric positive semidefinite.

Fix two distinct indices
i\neq j
, we want to estimate the matrix entry
{g}_{ij}=E(X_{i}X_{j})
in terms of the remaining entries of
G_{N}
. To this aim, let
S=\{1,\ldots,N\}
and define

S^{-}_{ij}\equiv\{1,\dots,N\}\setminus\{i,j\},

(1)

moreover let
G_{S^{-}_{ij}}
be the principal Gram block indexed by
S^{-}_{ij}
, namely

G_{S^{-}_{ij}}\equiv({g}_{hk})_{h,k\in S^{-}_{ij}}.

(2)

Throughout this note, we assume that
G_{S^{-}_{ij}}
is positive definite, equivalently, the family
(X_{h})_{h\in S^{-}_{ij}}
is linearly independent in
L^{2}
.
Corresponding to a fixed index define the vector
g_{i}\equiv({g}_{ih})_{h\in S^{-}_{ij}}
, then set

\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\equiv g_{i}^{T}G_{S^{-}_{ij}}^{-1}g_{j},

(3)

and

\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\equiv{g}_{ii}-g_{i}^{T}G_{S^{-}_{ij}}^{-1}g_{i},\qquad\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\equiv{g}_{jj}-g_{j}^{T}G_{S^{-}_{ij}}^{-1}g_{j}.

(4)

The quantity
\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}
is the Gram inner product of the projections of
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
onto
\operatorname{span}\{X_{h}:h\in S^{-}_{ij}\}
, while
\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}
and
\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}
are the squared norms of the corresponding residual vectors, technically the squared norms of their orthogonal complementary components w.r.t. the inner product. Being shorter, we shall keep on going with the name residual vectors.

2
Statement of the result

Theorem 1
.

Let
N\geq 2
, let
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, and fix
i\neq j
. Assume that the principal Gram block
G_{S^{-}_{ij}}
, with
S^{-}_{ij}=\{1,\dots,N\}\setminus\{i,j\}
, is positive definite. Then the following inequality holds

\left({g}_{ij}-\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\right)^{2}\leq\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\;\;,

(5)

moreover it also holds the estimate

{g}_{ij}^{2}\leq\left(|\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}|+\sqrt{\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}}\right)^{2},

(6)

or equivalently,

\left(E(X_{i}X_{j})\right)^{2}\leq\left(|\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}|+\sqrt{\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}}\right)^{2}.

(7)

Remark 1
.

Since
\Sigma\equiv Cov(X,X)=G(X-E(X))
the same theorem provides estimates on an off-diagonal entry
\sigma_{ij}
,
i\neq j
, of any covariance matrix in term of all the remaining entries. The same holds for the matrix
R\equiv corr(X,X)=G(Z)
, with
Z
being the component-wise standardization of
X
.

Remark 2
.

(a) For
N=3
,
S^{-}_{ij}
contains just one index, say
k
. Then
G_{S^{-}_{ij}}=({g}_{kk})
,
g_{i}=({g}_{ik})
, and
g_{j}=({g}_{jk})
. Hence

\pi_{ij\mid k}=\frac{{g}_{ik}{g}_{jk}}{{g}_{kk}},\qquad\Delta_{i\mid k}={g}_{ii}-\frac{{g}_{ik}^{2}}{{g}_{kk}},\qquad\Delta_{j\mid k}={g}_{jj}-\frac{{g}_{jk}^{2}}{{g}_{kk}}.

(8)

The theorem gives

{g}_{ij}^{2}g_{kk}^{2}\leq\left(\left|{g}_{ik}{g}_{jk}\right|+\sqrt{\left({g}_{ii}g_{kk}-{g}_{ik}^{2}\right)\left({g}_{jj}g_{kk}-{g}_{jk}^{2}\right)}\right)^{2},

(9)

then, by using the algebraic identity

\left(|ab|+\sqrt{(Ac-a^{2})(Bc-b^{2})}\right)^{2}=ABc^{2}-\left(|a|\sqrt{Bc-b^{2}}-|b|\sqrt{Ac-a^{2}}\right)^{2},

inequality (
9
) exactly reproduces Theorem 3.1 of [LT] which extends Walker’s inequality, this last being recovered by choosing one of the
N=3
variables to be the identity.

(b) For
N=4
, if
i=1
and
j=2
, then
S^{-}_{12}=\{3,4\}
,
G_{S^{-}_{12}}=G_{34}
,
g_{1}=({g}_{13},{g}_{14})^{T}
, and
g_{2}=({g}_{23},{g}_{24})^{T}
, the inequality (
6
) takes the explicit form

\displaystyle\left|g_{12}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\frac{g_{44}g_{13}g_{23}-g_{34}g_{13}g_{24}-g_{34}g_{14}g_{23}+g_{33}g_{14}g_{24}}{g_{33}g_{44}-g_{34}^{2}}\right|

(10)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{g_{11}-\frac{g_{44}g_{13}^{2}-2g_{34}g_{13}g_{14}+g_{33}g_{14}^{2}}{g_{33}g_{44}-g_{34}^{2}}}\sqrt{g_{22}-\frac{g_{44}g_{23}^{2}-2g_{34}g_{23}g_{24}+g_{33}g_{24}^{2}}{g_{33}g_{44}-g_{34}^{2}}}.

Estimates for other pairs
(i,j)
are obtained by the same formula after relabelling the indices. From (
10
) we recover (
9
) by setting
g_{14}=g_{24}=g_{34}=0
and multiplying both sides by
|g_{33}|
.

Remark 3
.

If
G_{S^{-}_{ij}}
is only positive semidefinite and not invertible, then the auxiliary variables
(X_{h})_{h\in S^{-}_{ij}}
are linearly dependent in
L^{2}
. In that case one may remove redundant variables, or replace
G_{S^{-}_{ij}}^{-1}
by the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse.

Proof.

Let
V:=\operatorname{span}\{X_{h}:h\in S^{-}_{ij}\}
and let
P
be the orthogonal projection onto
V
. In the case
S^{-}_{ij}=\varnothing
, we use the convention
V=\{0\}
and
P=0
. Define the residual vectors
R_{i}:=X_{i}-PX_{i}
and
R_{j}:=X_{j}-PX_{j}
, then

X_{i}=PX_{i}+R_{i},\qquad X_{j}=PX_{j}+R_{j},

with
R_{i},R_{j}\perp V
. Therefore

g_{ij}=\langle X_{i},X_{j}\rangle=\langle PX_{i},PX_{j}\rangle+\langle R_{i},R_{j}\rangle.

If
S^{-}_{ij}\neq\varnothing
, the projection coefficients of
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
onto
V
are determined by the normal equations

G_{S^{-}_{ij}}c_{i}=g_{i},\qquad G_{S^{-}_{ij}}c_{j}=g_{j}.

Since
G_{S^{-}_{ij}}
is positive definite, it is invertible, and therefore

c_{i}=G_{S^{-}_{ij}}^{-1}g_{i},\qquad c_{j}=G_{S^{-}_{ij}}^{-1}g_{j}.

Hence

\langle PX_{i},PX_{j}\rangle=g_{i}^{T}G_{S^{-}_{ij}}^{-1}g_{j}=\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}.

Similarly,

\|R_{i}\|^{2}=\langle X_{i}-PX_{i},X_{i}-PX_{i}\rangle=g_{ii}-g_{i}^{T}G_{S^{-}_{ij}}^{-1}g_{i}=\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}},

and

\|R_{j}\|^{2}=\langle X_{j}-PX_{j},X_{j}-PX_{j}\rangle=g_{jj}-g_{j}^{T}G_{S^{-}_{ij}}^{-1}g_{j}=\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}.

We notice that if
S^{-}_{ij}=\varnothing
, these identities reduce to
\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}=0
,
\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}=g_{ii}
and
\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}=g_{jj}
.
Thus in general we have

g_{ij}=\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}+\langle R_{i},R_{j}\rangle.

and applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the residual vectors gives

\left(g_{ij}-\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\right)^{2}=\langle R_{i},R_{j}\rangle^{2}\leq\|R_{i}\|^{2}\|R_{j}\|^{2}=\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}},

which proves the inequality (
5
). Taking square roots, we obtain

|g_{ij}-\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}|\leq\sqrt{\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}}.

Therefore, by the triangle inequality, we have

|g_{ij}|=\left|\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}+g_{ij}-\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\right|\leq|\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}|+|g_{ij}-\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}|.

and consequently

|g_{ij}|\leq|\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}|+\sqrt{\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}}.

Squaring it gives

g_{ij}^{2}\leq\left(|\pi_{ij\mid S^{-}_{ij}}|+\sqrt{\Delta_{i\mid S^{-}_{ij}}\Delta_{j\mid S^{-}_{ij}}}\right)^{2}.

which proves the estimate (
6
).
∎

3
Cramer Rao improved estimates

We now indicate how the preceding inequality can be used to refine Cramer-Rao (CR) lower bounds for biased estimators. Let
(P_{\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta}
be a regular one-dimensional statistical model with density
f_{\theta}
, and let
T
be an estimator of
\theta
. Define the estimation error
A_{\theta}\equiv T-\theta
, the bias
b(\theta)\equiv E_{\theta}(A_{\theta})=E_{\theta}(T)-\theta
, and the score
U_{\theta}\equiv\partial_{\theta}\log f_{\theta}
. Under the usual regularity assumptions,
E_{\theta}(U_{\theta})=0
,
E_{\theta}(U_{\theta}^{2})=I(\theta)
, the Fisher information, and

E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}U_{\theta})=1+b^{\prime}(\theta).

(11)

The ordinary CS inequality gives
\left(1+b^{\prime}(\theta)\right)^{2}\leq E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}^{2})I(\theta)
,
hence
E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}^{2})\geq(1+b^{\prime}(\theta))^{2}/I(\theta)
, that is CR inequality for biased estimators. Walker’s improvement corresponds to the estimate
\left(1+b^{\prime}(\theta)\right)^{2}\leq\left(E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}^{2})-b(\theta)^{2}\right)I(\theta)
, see [W], which gives the sharper lower bound

MSE(T)\equiv E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}^{2})\geq b(\theta)^{2}+\frac{\left(1+b^{\prime}(\theta)\right)^{2}}{I(\theta)}.

(12)

The
N
-variables inequality proven in the previous section may produce further refinements whenever auxiliary information is at disposal. Indeed, let
H_{1},\dots,H_{m}
be a set of auxiliary square-integrable random variables, and consider the vector
H=(H_{1},\dots,H_{m})^{T}
. Assume that the Gram matrix
M_{\theta}:=E_{\theta}(HH^{T})
is positive definite and define

a_{\theta}\equiv E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}H),\qquad u_{\theta}\equiv E_{\theta}(U_{\theta}H).

(13)

Consider the
N=2+m
random vector
X=(A_{\theta},U_{\theta},H_{1},\ldots,H_{m})
, by applying the residual inequality (
5
) to the pair
(A_{\theta},U_{\theta})
it is obtained

\left(E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}U_{\theta})-a_{\theta}^{T}M_{\theta}^{-1}u_{\theta}\right)^{2}\leq\left(E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}^{2})-a_{\theta}^{T}M_{\theta}^{-1}a_{\theta}\right)\left(I(\theta)-u_{\theta}^{T}M_{\theta}^{-1}u_{\theta}\right).

Recalling (
11
), whenever
I(\theta)-u_{\theta}^{T}M_{\theta}^{-1}u_{\theta}>0
, this yields the generalized lower bound

E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}^{2})\geq a_{\theta}^{T}M_{\theta}^{-1}a_{\theta}+\frac{\left(1+b^{\prime}(\theta)-a_{\theta}^{T}M_{\theta}^{-1}u_{\theta}\right)^{2}}{I(\theta)-u_{\theta}^{T}M_{\theta}^{-1}u_{\theta}.}

(14)

We can compare formula (
14
), specializing it to
N=3
, with the corresponding formula obtained in [LT] (supplementary material). For this comparison we take
m=1
in (
14
) and set
H=H_{1}\equiv Z
. Then, using the same notations as in [LT], we set

S_{Z}\equiv M_{\theta}=E_{\theta}(Z^{2})
,
a\equiv a_{\theta}=E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}Z)
and
b\equiv u_{\theta}=E_{\theta}(U_{\theta}Z)
.
We also set
c\equiv E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}U_{\theta})=1+b^{\prime}(\theta)
and recall
E_{\theta}(U_{\theta}^{2})=I(\theta)
. Then (
14
) produces the lower bound

\operatorname{MSE}(T)=E_{\theta}(A_{\theta}^{2})\geq\frac{a^{2}}{S_{Z}}+\frac{\left(c-\frac{ab}{S_{Z}}\right)^{2}}{I(\theta)-\frac{b^{2}}{S_{Z}}}=\frac{a^{2}}{S_{Z}}+\frac{\left(S_{Z}c-ab\right)^{2}}{S_{Z}\left(I(\theta)S_{Z}-b^{2}\right)},

(15)

to be compared with the lower bound in [LT] which is
1
1

1

the original [LT] (supplementary material) formula contains some typos

\operatorname{MSE}(T)\geq\frac{a^{2}}{S_{Z}}+\frac{\left(S_{Z}|c|-|ab|\right)_{+}^{2}}{S_{Z}\left(I(\theta)S_{Z}-b^{2}\right)}.

(16)

Thus the difference between the two formulas is the replacement of the quantity
(S_{Z}|c|-|ab|)^{2}_{+}
appearing in (
16
) by the quantity
(S_{Z}c-ab)^{2}
. Since

|S_{Z}c-ab|\geq\bigg||S_{z}c|-|ab|\bigg|\geq(S_{z}|c|-|ab|)_{+}

the first one tends to be sharper (with significant differences in some cases). Notice that for
Z=1
, being
b=0
, Walker’s lower bound is recovered by both formulas.

We now show how our lower bound (
14
) allows for interesting generalizations. Consider Walker example of shrinkage estimators
T=w\widehat{\theta}+(1-w)\mu
, where the estimator
\widehat{\theta}
is unbiased and
\mu
is a reference value for
\theta
(its prior mean in the Bayesian approach). A direct computation proves that, within this class of biased estimators, Walker’s lower bound turns out to be optimal, equalizing the value

MSE(T)=(1-w)^{2}(\mu-\theta)^{2}+\frac{w^{2}}{I(\theta)},

(17)

with
I(\theta)=1/Var_{\theta}(\widehat{\theta})
. Notice that explicit computability and optimality are essentially due to the fact that
T
is an affine function of
\widehat{\theta}
and also an affine function of the score
U_{\theta}
. However such linear dependence in general does not hold for robust biased estimators because of the presence of nonlinear influence functions; in these cases Walker’s bound ceases to be optimal.

To make the point, consider the elementary model
X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,p),0<p<1,
where
m
is known and
p
is the unknown parameter. For
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
define the function
t_{\varepsilon}(x)\equiv\min\left\{1-\varepsilon,\max\left\{\varepsilon,\frac{x}{m}\right\}\right\}
,
then set
T_{\varepsilon}(X)\equiv t_{\varepsilon}(X)
. This estimator is statistically meaningful because the unbiased estimator

T=X/m
may take the boundary values
0
and
1
. Such boundary values are often inconvenient if the next step involves log-odds or likelihood ratios. Clipping moves the estimator away from the boundary, but introduces bias. As before, considering
A_{p,\varepsilon}=T_{\varepsilon}-p
and
b_{\varepsilon}(p)=E_{p}(T_{\varepsilon})-p
, Walker’s lower bound is (
I=\frac{m}{p(1-p)}
):

E_{p}(A_{p,\varepsilon}^{2})\geq LB_{Walker}\equiv b_{\varepsilon}(p)^{2}+\frac{(1+b_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(p))^{2}}{I}.

We can improve over it by including a natural auxiliary variable
T^{2}=(\frac{X}{m})^{2}
and considering our lower bound (
14
) for
N=4
and
H=(1,T^{2})
, obtaining (after some algebra)

E_{p}(A_{p,\varepsilon}^{2})\geq LB_{Walker}+\frac{(IC_{\varepsilon}-BD)^{2}}{(I^{2}v-ID^{2})},

(18)

with
v=\operatorname{var}_{p}(T^{2})>0
,
C_{\varepsilon}=\operatorname{cov}_{p}(A_{p,\varepsilon},T^{2})
,
D=\operatorname{cov}_{p}(U_{p},T^{2})
and
B=1+b^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore, if
m\geq 2
,
T^{2}
is not an affine function of the score
U_{p}
(actually it is a quadratic polynomial), hence
Iv>D^{2}
and (
18
) improves over Walker’s bound in all cases for which
IC_{\varepsilon}\neq BD
. Contrary to that, the alternative choice of auxiliary variables given by
H=(1,T)
produces
Iv=D^{2}
, hence it gives rise to degeneracy. Further examples of meaningful biased estimators can be built for other statistical models showing that our
N=4
bound on the MSE may produce improvements over Walker or Lupu-Tanase bounds, the above one was chosen because of its simplicity.
