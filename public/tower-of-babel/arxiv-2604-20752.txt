Majority C-coloring of graphs
Csilla Bujtás, Magda Dettlaff, Hanna Furmańczyk, Aleksandra Laskowska

Abstract
Inspired by the majority colorings and C-colorings, we introduce and study the majority C-coloring of graphs. In such a vertex coloring, every vertex shares its color with at least half of its neighbors. The maximum number of colors that can be used in a majority C-coloring of a graph G G is called the majority C-chromatic number and denoted by χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G) . An upper bound on χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G) is proved in terms of the order, minimum, and maximum degree. Its sharpness is demonstrated by several results over different graph classes. In particular, χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( P n k ) = χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( C n k ) = ⌊ n / ( k + 1 ) ⌋ \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(P_{n}^{k})=\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(C_{n}^{k})=\lfloor n/(k+1)\rfloor is true for the k k -th power of a path and a cycle if n ≥ k + 1 n\geq k+1 . Further, χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) = ( n − d ) / 3 \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=(n-d)/3 holds if G G is a ( claw , K 4 ) (\mbox{claw},K_{4}) -free cubic graph and contains d d diamonds. It is further shown that the majority C-chromatic number is not monotone under edge deletion. In fact, both the lower and upper bounds are sharp in the inequality chain χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) − 2 ≤ χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G − e ) ≤ χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) + 1 \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)-2\leq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)\leq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)+1 . The minimum and maximum number of edges in an n n -vertex graph G G with χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) = k \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=k are determined for every n n and k k . It is also pointed out that the classical chromatic number χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) and χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G) are incomparable, and the difference χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) − χ ⁡ ( G ) \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)-\chi(G) can take any positive or negative integer. On the other hand, χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) + χ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ n + 1 \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)+\chi(G)\leq n+1 holds for every graph G G of order n n . The decision problem of whether χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) ≥ k \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq k holds is NP-complete for every fixed k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . In contrast, some sufficient conditions for χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( G ) ≥ 2 \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq 2 are proved, and a linear-time algorithm is presented that determines χ ¯ ⩾ ​ ( T ) \overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(T) if T T is a tree.

Introduction

The classical vertex coloring of graphs requires that different colors be assigned to adjacent vertices, and a central question asks for the chromatic number, which is the minimum number of colors in such a coloring. Over the long and celebrated history of chromatic graph theory, several variants, both strengthenings and relaxations, of the coloring constraint were studied. However, the focus remained on the problems where the goal is to use as few colors as possible. In this paper, we explore a different direction when the constraint asks for (a certain number of) neighbors with the same color and we are interested in maximizing the number of colors.

We define the
majority C-coloring
(shortly,
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring
) of a simple graph
G
as a vertex coloring so that each vertex has a
c
ommon color with at least half of its neighbors. A coloring that assigns the same color to all vertices of a graph
G
is always a majority C-coloring, implying that the minimum number of colors is always
1
. Here, the challenging problem is to determine the possible maximum number of colors that can be used in a majority C-coloring of a given graph
G
. We call this maximum number the
majority C-chromatic number
(shortly,
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number
) of
G
and denote it by
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
. A formal definition is given in Section
2
.

Considering, for example, path and cycle graphs, it is easy to observe that the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number can be arbitrarily high over these graph classes. In contrast, complete graphs and a large subclass of complete bipartite graphs provide examples with
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=1
for arbitrarily large order.

Our results and the structure of the paper.

In this paper, we start the study of majority C-coloring by considering it over specific graph classes and proving some basic properties. In Section
2
, we establish two sharp upper bounds on
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
and prove that
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
is not monotone under taking spanning subgraphs. Further, the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number of the spanning subgraph

, obtained by deleting an edge

from

, satisfies

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)-2\leq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)\leq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)+1,

where both the lower and upper bounds are sharp.
Section
3
is devoted to two graph classes, both demonstrating that

can take an arbitrary value over the class.
In particular, for the powers of paths and cycles, the same formula

is proved for every pair

. If

is valid and holds with equality if

, the maximum and minimum number of edges in an

As a consequence of earlier results, we conclude that deciding whether

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}

1.1
Motivation

We shortly introduce the hypergraph and graph coloring models from the literature that inspired the introduction of the majority C-coloring.

C-coloring of hypergraphs.

A hypergraph
{\cal H}=(X,{\cal E})
is a set system
{\cal E}
over the underlying vertex set
X
. More precisely,
{\cal E}\subseteq 2^{X}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
is required. However, in the context of C-coloring, one-element subsets of
X
are also excluded from the hyperedge set
{\cal E}
. If each
e\in{\cal E}
contains exactly two vertices, then
{\cal H}
is
2
-uniform and corresponds to a (simple) graph.

A vertex coloring
\phi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a C-coloring of the hypergraph
{\cal H}=(X,{\cal E})
, if every hyperedge
e\in{\cal E}
contains at least two vertices assigned the same color. The largest number of different colors that can be used in a C-coloring of
{\cal H}
is the upper chromatic number
\overline{\chi}({\cal H})
of the hypergraph.

Although sporadic related studies were conducted earlier (see e.g.
[
1
,
23
]
), systematic research on C-coloring of hypergraphs was not initiated until the seminal papers of Voloshin
[
25
,
26
]
. The extensive study of this topic revealed several unexpected phenomena when the C-coloring constraint is applied together with the classical constraint that prohibits the presence of monochromatic hyperedges
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
15
,
17
]
. For more details and results, we refer the reader to the book
[
27
]
, the survey paper
[
8
]
, and the references therein.

A trivial but important fact is that the C-coloring of a graph (i.e., a
2
-uniform hypergraph)
G
, makes every component monochromatic, and therefore the upper chromatic number equals the number of components of
G
.

k
-improper C-coloring of graphs.

In light of some motivating problems, the following relaxation of the C-coloring constraint was introduced and studied for graphs in
[
6
]
. If
k
is a positive integer and
G
is a graph, then
\phi\colon V(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a
k
-
improper C-coloring
if every vertex
v\in V(G)
has at most
k
neighbors which receive colors different from
\phi(v)
. The largest number of colors that can be used in a
k
-improper C-coloring of
G
is the
k
-improper upper chromatic number
\overline{\chi}_{k\mbox{\footnotesize{-imp}}}(G)
.

The definitions directly imply that for
r
-regular graphs,
\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor
-improper C-colorings correspond to majority C-colorings.

Satisfactory partitions.

Given a graph
G
and a (nontrivial) partition
{\cal P}=\{V_{1},V_{2}\}
of its vertex set
V
, we say that
{\cal P}
is a
satisfactory partition
if each vertex
v\in V_{i}
has at least as many neighbors in
V_{i}
as in
V_{3-i}
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
. It is straightforward to see that
G
is (satisfactorily) partitionable if and only if
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq 2
that is
G
admits a majority C-coloring with two colors.

The results in
[
3
,
13
,
19
,
20
]
show that all cubic graphs except
K_{4}
and
K_{3,3}
are
partitionable in linear time, and all
4
-regular graphs except
K_{5}
are partitionable in linear time. Therefore,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq 2
holds for every
3
- and
4
-regular graph, except for
K_{4}
,
K_{3,3}
, and
K_{5}
. Further, as proved in
[
14
]
, the same inequality
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq 2
holds if
G
is a graph different from a star with a girth of at least
5
. For further results, we refer to the survey paper
[
4
]
.

A more general variant, called the
sum satisfactory
k
-partition
was introduced
[
4
,
12
]
as a (nontrivial) partition
{\cal P}=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{k}\}
of
V
, where each vertex
v\in V_{i}
has at least half of its neighbors in the same class
V_{i}
, for
i\in[k]
. However, the study of this problem was brief and focused solely on algorithmic questions. For this reason, although a
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring with
k
colors corresponds to a sum satisfactory
k
-partition, we decided to use the name majority C-coloring to connect our topic to the C-coloring problems.

Majority coloring.

A coloring of the vertices of a graph is called a
majority coloring
if for every vertex
v
at most half of its neighbours receive the same color as
v
.
It follows from the more general result of Lovász
[
18
]
that every undirected graph admits a majority coloring with two colors. The corresponding problem for directed graphs has been studied in
[
10
,
16
]
.

Both majority coloring and majority C-coloring are based on imposing local constraints on monochromatic neighborhoods: for each vertex, the number of neighbors sharing its color is bounded by a prescribed threshold. However, the two notions differ substantially in their optimization goals. Nevertheless, there exist graph classes for which the majority chromatic number coincides with the majority C-chromatic number; an example is the graph
K_{4,4}
shown in Fig.
1
(c).

We also note that an edge analogue of majority coloring has been considered in
[
9
]
.

Preliminary results

Definition 1

(Majority C-coloring)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph and
k
a positive integer.
A
majority C-coloring
(
C_{\geqslant}
-coloring
, for short) of
G
with
k
colors is a surjective function
\phi:V\rightarrow[k]

such that every vertex
v\in V
satisfies

\bigl|\{\,u\in N(v):\phi(u)=\phi(v)\,\}\bigr|\geq\frac{\deg_{G}(v)}{2}.

(1)

The largest integer
k
for which
G
admits a
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring is called
majority C-chromatic number
(
C_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number
, for short) and it is denoted by
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
. A majority C-coloring of a graph
G
with
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
colors is called a
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-
coloring
of
G
.

For a majority C-coloring
\phi
of
G=(V,E)
that uses
k
colors, we define the color classes
V_{i}=\phi^{-1}(i)
, for
i\in[k]
, and use this notation throughout the paper when
\phi
is clear from the context. If a vertex
v
belongs to the color class
V_{i}
, condition (
1
) can be formulated equivalently as

|N(v)\cap V_{i}|\geq|N(v)\cap(V\setminus V_{i})|.

(2)

Definition
1
directly implies the following basic properties.

Observation 1
.

Let
\phi
be a majority C-coloring of the graph
G
.

(i)

If
v
is a leaf in
G
and
vu\in E(G)
, then
\phi(v)=\phi(u)
.

(ii)

If
\phi
uses
k
colors and
k\geq 2
, then there exists a majority C-coloring with
(k-1)
-colors. In particular, for every
k^{\prime}
with
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
, the graph has a majority C-coloring with
k^{\prime}
colors.

(iii)

If
\phi
uses
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
colors, then every color class induces a connected subgraph in
G
.

(iv)

If
G
is a disconnected graph with components
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
, then
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G_{i})
.

Statement (i) immediately follows from (
1
). Concerning part (ii), we note that by merging two color classes in a
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring, the majority condition (
1
) remains satisfied. Concerning (iii), suppose that a color class induces a subgraph with components
H_{1},\dots,H_{\ell}
, where
\ell\geq 2
. By recoloring the vertices of
H_{1}
with a new color, we obtain a
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring of
G
with
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)+1
colors that is impossible by definition. Statement (iii) and Definition
1
directly imply (iv).

The above observations, together with simple structural arguments, allow us to determine exact values of
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
for some basic graph classes.

Observation 2
.

For the following graph classes, the majority C-chromatic number satisfies:

(i)

For a complete graph
K_{n}
,
n\geq 1
,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(K_{n})=1
.

(ii)

For a cycle
C_{n}
and a path
P_{n}
,
n\geq 3
,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(C_{n})=\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(P_{n})=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

(iii)

For a star
S_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, on
n+1
vertices,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(S_{n})=1
; and for a subdivided star
S(S_{n})
, we have
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(S(S_{n}))=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1
.

(iv)

For a wheel
W_{n}
,
n\geq 3
, on
n+1
vertices,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(W_{n})=1
.

(v)

For a complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
,
m,n\geq 1
,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(K_{m,n})=2
if both
m,n
are even and
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(K_{m,n})=1
otherwise.

Proof.

(i) Note that every vertex of
K_{n}
is of degree
n-1
, so if we color a vertex
v
with any color, the relevant color class in any
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring of
K_{n}
needs to have at least
1+\frac{1}{2}(n-1)=\frac{1}{2}(n+1)
vertices. Thus, it is impossible that
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(K_{n})>1
.

(ii) It is clear that every color class in any
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring of
P_{n}
or
C_{n}
contains at least two vertices. Hence, the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number is at most
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, and a
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring with this number of colors is easy to obtain. Note that the formula of
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(C_{n})
corresponds to
\overline{\chi}_{1\mbox{\footnotesize{-imp}}}(C_{n})
. The latter was already established in
[
6
]
.

(iii) The value for a star follows directly from Observation
1
(i). For a subdivided star, consider the central vertex
r
of
\deg(r)=n
. At least
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
of the adjacent vertices must receive the color of
r
. Hence, at most
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
neighbors of
r
can be colored differently, which, together with Observation
1
(i), yields
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(S(S_{n}))=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1.

An example of such a coloring is presented in Fig.
1
(a).

(iv) Suppose that a majority
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring
\phi
of
W_{n}
uses more than one color and let
r
be the central vertex. Consider a vertex
v
from the cycle such that
\phi(v)\neq\phi(r)
.
The degree constraints then force all vertices from the cycle to receive the same color
\phi(v)
, distinct from
\phi(r)
, which violates the majority condition at
r
. Hence
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(W_{n})=1
.

(v) Observe that each color class in any
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring of
K_{m,n}
needs to contain at least
\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil
vertices from one part of the graph and at least
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
vertices from the other part. This simple fact forces two colors with

even, and one color if any of these numbers is odd. See Fig.
1
(b) and (c) for examples. ∎

At the end of this part, we recall that the decision problem of whether a graph
G
admits a sum satisfactory
k
-partition was proved to be NP-complete
[
4
]
for every fixed
k\geq 2
. As noted in Section
1.1
, this question is equivalent to deciding whether
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq k
holds. Therefore, we may conclude the following key property.

Proposition 3
.

For every fixed integer
k\geq 2
, it is NP-complete to decide whether
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq k

.

2.1
General upper bounds

Proposition 4
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with minimum degree
\delta(G)
and maximum degree
\Delta(G)
. Then

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\leq 1+\frac{n-\Bigl\lceil\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}\Bigr\rceil-1}{\Bigl\lceil\frac{\delta(G)}{2}\Bigr\rceil+1}.

Proof.

Let
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=k
and let
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}
be the color classes in a
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-coloring of
G
. Let
v
be a vertex of maximum degree in
G
. We may assume, without loss of generality, that
v\in V_{1}
. Thus, by the majority condition (
1
),

|V_{1}|\geq|N(v)\cap V_{1}|+1\geq\Bigl\lceil\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}\Bigr\rceil+1.

Similarly, each remaining color class
V_{i}
, for
2\leq i\leq k
, contains a vertex of degree at least
\delta(G)
, and its size can be estimated as
|V_{i}|\geq\bigl\lceil\frac{\delta(G)}{2}\bigr\rceil+1
. We therefore infer

(k-1)\Bigl(\Bigl\lceil\frac{\delta(G)}{2}\Bigr\rceil+1\Bigr)\leq n-\Bigl\lceil\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}\Bigr\rceil-1,

and the desired inequality follows.
∎

Corollary 5
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
. Then

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\leq\frac{n}{\Bigl\lceil\frac{\delta(G)}{2}\Bigr\rceil+1}.

Several results of this paper demonstrate that the upper bounds in Proposition
4
and Corollary
5
are sharp. Proposition
9
will show that these bounds are tight for
C_{n}^{k}
whenever
n
is divisible by
k+1
and
n\geq 2k+2
. Proposition
10
will provide another infinite class of sharp examples by implying that every
(\text{claw},\text{diamond},K_{4})
-free cubic graph
G
of order
n
satisfies
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=\frac{1}{3}n
.

2.2
Edge deletion and spanning subgraphs

In this section, we start with the investigation of how the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number of a graph behaves under the deletion of edges.
It turns out that if a subgraph is obtained by the deletion of a single edge, the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number might still increase or decrease. However, for this case, we can prove sharp lower and upper bounds.

Proposition 6
.

For every graph
G
and every edge
e\in E(G)
, it holds that

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)-2\leq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)\leq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)+1.

(3)

Moreover, both bounds are sharp.

Proof.

Let
e=v_{1}v_{2}
be an edge in
G
and
\phi^{\prime}
a
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-coloring of
G^{\prime}=G-e
. Then we add the edge
e
to
G^{\prime}
, and consider the assignment
\phi^{\prime}
in
G
. The majority C-coloring condition remains satisfied for every vertex
u
different from
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
. However, since
\deg_{G}(v_{i})=\deg_{G^{\prime}}(v_{i})+1
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
, it is possible that
\lceil\frac{1}{2}\deg_{G}(v_{i})\rceil=\lceil\frac{1}{2}\deg_{G^{\prime}}(v_{i})\rceil+1

\displaystyle\leq{(n-1)-(k-1)+2\choose 2}-\frac{(n-1)-(k-1)+2}{2}

\displaystyle={n-k+2\choose 2}-\frac{n-k+2}{2}.

In both cases, the formulas in (

.

Case 2.
2\leq n_{1}<\frac{n}{k}

We modify

to obtain a graph

with a

-coloring that uses

Let

, the cardinality of

and add all edges (that were missing in

.

We first prove that

and

, the number of edges in

. Vertex

Next, we show that
V_{1}^{\prime},\dots,V_{k}^{\prime}

. For every vertex

\displaystyle|N_{G^{\prime}}(u)\cap V_{k}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\geq|N_{G}(u)\cap V_{1}|\geq\Bigl|N_{G}(u)\cap\bigcup_{i=2}^{k-1}V_{i}\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|N_{G^{\prime}}(u)\cap\bigcup_{i=2}^{k-1}V_{i}^{\prime}\Bigr|=\Bigl|N_{G^{\prime}}(u)\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}V_{i}^{\prime}\Bigr|.

, which is outside

in

. We conclude that

and there is a

-coloring of

where the smallest color class consists of an isolated vertex.

Consequently, for every graph

such that a

and hence, the relevant formula in (

.

Case 3.
n_{1}=\frac{n}{k}

In this case, the color classes are of the same cardinality

. Given a graph

\displaystyle\geq m(G)-(2n_{1}-2)+(n_{1}-1)n_{1}-\Bigl\lfloor\frac{(n_{1}-1)^{2}}{2}\Bigr\rfloor

∎

4.2
Order,
\chi(G)
, and
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)

We first show that the chromatic number and the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number of graphs are incomparable. In contrast to this fact, we prove that the results in Section
4.1
imply a sharp inequality between
\chi(G)
,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
, and
n(G)
, where
n(G)
denotes the order of
G
.

Proposition 15
.

For every two positive integers
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, there exists a graph
G
with
\chi(G)=k_{1}
and
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=k_{2}
.

Proof.

If
k_{1}=1
, we take
k_{2}
isolated vertices as a graph
G
, and observe that
\chi(G)=1
and
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=k_{2}
. If
k_{2}=1
then
G=K_{k_{1}}
satisfies the requirements. For
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
, we define
G
as a power of a path. Let
G=P_{n}^{k}
with
k=k_{1}-1
and
n=k_{1}k_{2}
. Since

\omega(G)=k+1=k_{1}
, and a classical vertex coloring of
G
with
k_{1}
colors is easy to obtain, we have
\chi(G)=k_{1}
.
On the other hand, Theorem
7
implies
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{k+1}\rfloor=k_{2}
that verifies the statement.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
15
, the parameters

\chi(G)
and
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
are also independent in the sense that the difference
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)-\chi(G)
can take arbitrarily large positive as well as arbitrarily small negative values.

Theorem 16
.

If
G
is a graph of order
n
, then
\chi(G)+\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\leq n+1
. Further, the inequality is sharp.

Proof.

Let
G
be an arbitrary graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges. Let
\chi(G)=k
and consider the color classes defined by a
k
-coloring of
G
. Since
k
is the minimum number of colors, no two color classes can be merged, and therefore
m\geq{k\choose 2}
.

Suppose now, contrary to the statement, that
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq n+2-k
. Theorem
13
implies that the strict inequality

m<{n-(n+2-k)+2\choose 2}={k\choose 2}

holds regardless of the parity of
n-k
. Since it contradicts the conclusion derived from
\chi(G)=k
, we may infer
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\leq n+1-k
, which finishes the proof of the inequality.

The sharpness can be shown by taking a complete graph
K_{n}
and observing
\chi(K_{n})+\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(K_{n})=n+1
; or by taking an edgeless graph

on
n
vertices and observing
\chi(G)=1
and
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=n
.
∎

5
Linear-time algorithm for trees

By Proposition
3
the problem of deciding whether
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq k
is NP-complete for any fixed
k\geq 2
.
In this section, we first show that majority
C-colorings are closely related to edge cuts under certain conditions. We then investigate the time complexity of determining
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number for trees. In Section
5.2
we present a linear-time algorithm that outputs a
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-coloring for any tree.

5.1
Cut subgraphs with bounded degree

Definition 2
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and a subset
F
of its edges, we denote by
[F]
the subgraph of
G
induced by
F
; i.e.,
[F]
has vertex set
\bigcup_{e\in F}e
and edge set
F
.
We say that
[F]
is a
cut-
s
subgraph
of
G
if the graph
G^{*}=(V,E\setminus F)
has at least
s
components,
s\geq 1
.
Moreover, if a cut-
s
subgraph
[F]
has such a property that for each vertex
x\in V([F])
its degree in
[F]
is at most
\frac{1}{2}\deg_{G}(x)
, it will be referred to as a
cut-
(s,\leqslant)
subgraph
of
G
.

Proposition 17
.

For every graph
G
the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq s
;

(ii)

G
has a cut-
(s,\leqslant)
subgraph.

Proof.

Suppose that
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq s
. Then there exists a majority C-coloring

\phi
of
G=(V,E)
that uses exactly
s
colors.
By the definition of a majority C-coloring, for every vertex
v\in V
, at least half
of its neighbors receive the same color as
v
. Equivalently, at most half of the
neighbors of
v
are colored differently from
\phi(v)
.
Let
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
denote the set of non-monochromatic edges, that is, an edge

e=xy\in E
belongs to
E^{\prime}
if and only if
\phi(x)\neq\phi(y)
.
Since each vertex
x\in V
has at most
\frac{1}{2}\deg_{G}(x)
neighbors with color different
from
\phi(x)
, it follows that

\deg_{[E^{\prime}]}(x)\leq\frac{\deg_{G}(x)}{2}\quad\text{for every }x\in V.

Observe that every edge in
E\setminus E^{\prime}
connects two vertices of the same color.
Hence, each component of the graph
G^{*}=(V,E\setminus E^{\prime})

is monochromatic.
Since
\phi
uses exactly
s
colors, the graph
G^{*}
has at least
s
components.
Consequently, the edge set
E^{\prime}
forms a cut-
(s,\leqslant)
subgraph of
G
.

Now suppose that the graph
G=(V,E)
admits a cut-
(s,\leqslant)
subgraph induced by an edge
set
F\subseteq E
.
Define a vertex coloring
\phi
by assigning the same color to two vertices if and
only if they belong to the same component of the graph
G^{*}=(V,E\setminus F)
.
By the definition of a cut-
(s,\leqslant)
subgraph, the graph
G^{*}
has at least
s

components, and hence the coloring
\phi
uses at least
s
colors.
On the other hand, if two adjacent vertices
x
and
y
receive different colors under

\phi
, then
xy\in F
.
Since
\deg_{[F]}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}\deg_{G}(x)
for every vertex
x\in V
, it follows that
x

has at most half of its neighbors colored differently from
\phi(x)
.
Consequently,
\phi
is a majority C-coloring of
G
using at least
s
colors,
which completes the proof.

∎

In view of the proof, a cut-
(s,\leqslant)
subgraph corresponds precisely to a decomposition of
G
into at least

s
monochromatic components under the associated majority C-coloring.

5.2
Linear-time algorithm for trees

Next we describe a linear-time algorithm that computes the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number and outputs a
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-coloring for trees.

Theorem 18
.

The
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number and a
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-coloring can be determined in linear-time for trees.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tree. According to Proposition
17
,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(T)
is equal to the largest
s
such that
T
has a cut-
(s,\leqslant)
subgraph. First, we describe a procedure
which calculates this largest number
s
for trees.

Consider a tree
T=(V,E)
with a fixed root vertex
v
. For every vertex
x\in V
,
T(x)
denotes the subtree rooted at
x
. The parent vertex of
x
is denoted by
p(x)
.
The following functions
f
,
g
, and
h
are defined for every
x\in V
, excluding leaves and the root of
T
.

•

f(x)
: the largest number of components which can be obtained from
T(x)
by deleting a cut subgraph of the following property: for each vertex
z\in V(T(x))
its degree in this cut subgraph is at most
\frac{1}{2}\deg_{T}(z)
, and
x
has degree at most
\frac{1}{2}(\deg_{T(x)}(x)+1)
in this cut subgraph.

•

g(x)
: the largest number of components which can be obtained from
T(x)
by deleting a cut subgraph of the following property: for each vertex
z\in V(T(x))
its degree in this cut subgraph is at most
\frac{1}{2}\deg_{T}(z)
, and
x
has degree at most
\frac{1}{2}(\deg_{T(x)}(x)-1)
in this cut subgraph.

•

h(x)=g(x)-f(x)+1
.

It is obvious by definition that
f(x)\geq g(x)
holds for every
x\in V(T)
for which these functions are defined, and therefore
h(x)\leq 1
.

Consider a cut subgraph
S
of
T(x)
in which
x
has degree at most
\frac{1}{2}(\deg_{T(x)}(x)+1)
and for each vertex
z\in V(T(x))
its degree in this cut subgraph is at most
\frac{1}{2}\deg_{T}(z)
. Note that in case when all the children of
x
are leaves, such a cut subgraph is empty and then
f(x)=g(x)
. If we delete
S
from
T(x)
, we obtain
f(x)
components. If we remove an edge
e
incident to
x
from
S
, we obtain a cut subgraph
S^{\prime}=S\backslash\{e\}
where
x
has a degree at most
\frac{1}{2}(\deg_{T(x)}(x)-1)
. Deleting
S^{\prime}
from
T(x)
yields
f(x)-1
components. This shows that

and therefore
h(x)\in\{0,1\}
.

The values
f(x)
,
g(x)
and
h(x)
will be determined in postorder.
If a vertex
x
has only adjacent descendants that are leaves, then clearly,
f(x)=g(x)=1
, and
h(x)=1
.
Otherwise, assume that
x
has
d
children not being leaves, say
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
.
Recall that a cut-
(s\leqslant)
subgraph, by definition, cannot contain an edge incident to a leaf.
We will take this fact into account in the subsequent calculations.

•

If
x
has at most
\frac{1}{2}(\deg_{T(x)}(x)+1)-1
children not being leaves, then
f(x)=g(x)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\max\{f(x_{i})-1,g(x_{i})\}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{d}g(x_{i})
. Hence,
h(x)=1
in such a case.

•

If
x
has more than
\frac{1}{2}(\deg_{T(x)}(x)+1)-1
children not being leaves, i.e., if
d\geq\frac{1}{2}(\deg_{T(x)}(x)-1)
, then we can suppose that the children of
x
of degree at least 2 in

are ordered according to decreasing

h(x_{1})\geq h(x_{2})\geq\cdots\geq h(x_{d})
(the adjacent leaves are not taken into account in the calculations). Let

. Then we have

f(x)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{c}g(x_{i})+\sum_{i=c+1}^{d}(f(x_{i})-1),

and analogously

g(x)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{c-1}g(x_{i})+\sum_{i=c}^{d}(f(x_{i})-1).

Now, let us assume that the functions

,
g
, and
h
are calculated for every

, excluding leaves and the root of
T
. If
x
is the root of
T
, i.e. if

, we calculate the largest number of components which can be obtained from

by deleting a cut subgraph of property: for each vertex

its degree in this cut subgraph is at most

, i.e. the value of

.
To calculate

.

s=1+\sum_{i=1}^{c^{\prime}}g(x_{i})+\sum_{i=c^{\prime}+1}^{d}(f(x_{i})-1).

Since
h(x_{i})\in\{0,1\}
for all
1\leq i\leq d
, sorting these numbers takes
O(d)
time. The computation of
f(x)
and
g(x)
can be done in time proportional to the number of children of

not being leaves. Thus, determining the value of
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(T)=s
takes linear time for trees.

The corresponding cut-
(s,\leqslant)
subgraph
S
can be obtained by traversing the tree in preorder. We keep the notation for the children of
x
not being leaves as
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
such that
h(x_{1})\geq h(x_{2})\geq\cdots\geq h(x_{d})
holds. Vertices that are leaves are not considered in this procedure; by definition, the edges between leaves and their parents are not part of the cut-
(s,\leqslant)
subgraph
S
.

1.

If
x=v
or
p(x)x\in S
then
xx_{i}\in S
if and only if
i\leq\frac{1}{2}\deg_{T}(x)
.

2.

If
p(x)x\in S
then
xx_{i}\in S
if and only if
i\leq\frac{1}{2}\deg_{T}(x)-1
.

A corresponding
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-coloring of tree

can be obtained based on the proof of Proposition

, which completes the proof.
∎

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq 2

One of the most important questions of the satisfactory graph partition problem is to decide whether a graph

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq 2

3

(i) Let

Since every vertex in

to

. Formally, we set

Concluding discussions

In this paper, we introduced the majority C-coloring and initiated a research on this topic. To close the paper, we discuss some possible applications of this new model and also pose two natural directions, namely the
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-stability and
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}

7.1
Possible applications of majority C-colorings

Majority C-colorings provide a natural framework for modeling systems in which local decisions must be consistent with prevailing choices in the neighborhood. Such situations arise in networked settings where coordination between adjacent units is essential, including spatial planning, public service allocation, infrastructure coordination, and models of social or organizational behavior. In this framework, vertices represent interacting units, edges encode dependencies, and colors correspond to alternative configurations or policies. The majority condition ensures local stability by preventing isolated or conflicting assignments, while the parameter
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
measures the maximum diversity of configurations compatible with this form of local consistency.

A particularly natural application arises in the organization of urban services. Service zones are modeled as vertices, adjacency reflects infrastructural or operational dependencies, and colors represent different service providers, schedules, or standards. The majority condition guarantees that each zone is coordinated primarily with neighboring zones operating under the same configuration, leading to locally stable service allocation. In this context,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
quantifies how many distinct service configurations can be deployed simultaneously without fragmenting the system or increasing coordination costs. For example, in waste collection or street maintenance,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
indicates how many different schedules or operators can be introduced without fragmenting the service system and increasing coordination complexity.

Relation to Schelling’s model of segregation

Schelling’s model of segregation is an agent-based model developed by economist Thomas Schelling in
[
21
]
. Originally, on a graph of locations, agents of two types occupy vertices, leaving some vacant. An agent is “unhappy” if the fraction of same-type neighbors falls below a tolerance threshold
\tau\in[0,1]
. Unhappy agents move to vacant nodes, and these repeated individual shifts eventually produce large-scale segregated patterns. It is worth to mention that every agent is not guaranteed to be satisfied and in these cases, it is of interest to study the patterns (if any) of the agent dynamics.
Although Schelling’s model does not include external factors that force agents to segregate, it demonstrates how even slight in-group preferences can lead to extreme social segregation.
Even if individuals do not mind diversity, a small preference for neighbors of the same “type” (race or economic status) triggers a chain reaction of self-segregation.

By integrating Schelling’s model with majority C-coloring, we can replace moving with repainting. In our model every node is occupied (no vacancies). Agents evaluate their neighborhood. If the fraction of same-type neighbors is less than
\tau
, rather than move they change their type (color) to the majority type among neighbors.
So a majority C-coloring corresponds to a situation when
\tau=0.5
and everybody is satisfied. Considering a graph and its arbitrary coloring with 2 colors, we are able to recolor its vertices one by one in such a way that every vertex becomes satisfied at the end, what means that the obtained coloring is a majority C-coloring.

Beyond thresholds, Schelling’s model can be refined using utility functions. In a social network context, this often manifests as edge rewiring rather than movement or color changes.
The agent neither changes its location nor its type (color); instead, it modifies its neighborhood by severing ties with dissimilar agents and forming new connections with similar ones.
From a graph theory perspective, the goal is to transform an initial graph through a sequence of edge removals and additions until the existing coloring becomes a majority C-coloring.

7.2
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-stabilty and
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-criticality

In analogy with classical graph coloring, it is natural to consider the behaviour of the parameter
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
under edge deletions (cf.
[
22
]
). In what follows, we address this question from two perspectives, which we refer to as
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-stability and
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-criticality. These two concepts naturally suggest further directions for the study of majority C-colorings.

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-stabilty.

The stability of the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number under edge deletion can be quantified by considering the minimum number of edges whose removal changes the value of
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
(
es_{\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}}(G)
). Such stability parameters, commonly referred to in the literature as edge stability, measure the robustness of graph invariants with respect to edge deletions. They provide a natural direction for further investigation in the context of majority C-colorings.
This notion captures how robust the parameter

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)

is under structural perturbations of the graph, and serves as a natural analogue of edge-stability concepts studied for classical coloring invariants. This means that deleting any
es_{\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}}(G)-1
edges from graph
G
leaves
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
unchanged.

As an illustrative example, consider the path
P_{n}
. If
n
is odd, then
es_{\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}}(P_{n})=1
, since deleting a single pendant edge decreases the value of
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(P_{n})
.
On the other hand, if
n
is even, then the removal of any single edge does not change
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(P_{n})
, and hence
es_{\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}}(P_{n})=2
. In this case, removing two edges is sufficient to alter the value of the parameter.

As we already shown in Proposition
3
, after deleting an edge from graph
G
the majority C-chromatic number,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)
, can decrease or increase. If we consider deleting more edges, the same rule applies.

\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-criticality.

It is natural to ask which classes of graphs have the property that the deletion of any edge necessarily changes the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number. Such graphs with at least one edge will be called
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-
edge-critical graphs
. In other words,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\neq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)
holds for every edge
e
if
G
is
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-critical.

As an example, consider subdivided stars
S(S_{n})
with odd
n
(cf. Observation
2
(iii). In this case, the deletion of any edge increases the value of
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
by one, and hence such graphs are
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-critical. This property, however, does not hold for subdivided stars with even
n
, where there exist edges whose removal does not affect the value of
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
.

Since the deletion of an edge may either increase or decrease the
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-chromatic number (cf. Proposition
3
), it is natural to ask whether both cases may occur for
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-critical graphs. The example in the previous paragraph, that is a subdivided star
S(S_{n})
for an odd
n
, shows that there are
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-critical graphs satisfying
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)=\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)+1
for every
e\in E(G)
. The following proposition shows that the reverse inequality
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)<\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
cannot hold for every edge
e
of a graph
G
.

Proposition 21
.

Every graph
G
with
E(G)\neq\emptyset
contains an edge
e
such that
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)\geq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider connected graphs. Further, if
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)=1
, then
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)\geq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
clearly holds for all
e\in E(G)
. Assume now that
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)\geq 2
and let
\phi
be a
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring of
G
that uses
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
colors. Since
G
is connected, there exists an edge
e=uv
such that
\phi(u)\neq\phi(v)
. We claim that
\phi
remains a
{\rm C}_{\geqslant}
-coloring in
G-e
and therefore,
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)\geq\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)
. Indeed, for a vertex
z\in V(G-e)
and the color class
V_{i}
containing
z
, the majority condition (
1
) clearly remains true if
z\notin\{u,v\}
. If
z\in\{u,v\}
, then
N_{G-e}(z)\cap V_{i}=N_{G}(z)\cap V_{i}
and
\deg_{G-e}(z)=\deg_{G}(z)-1
ensure that (
1
) is satisfied.
∎

By Proposition
21
, there are no
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge critical graphs satisfying
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)>\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)
for all
e\in V(G)
. What is more, we conjecture that
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)=\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)+1
holds for every edge
e\in E(G)
if
G
is an
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge critical graph.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
G
be a
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}
-edge-critical graph with at least two edges and let
e,f\in E(G)
. It is no possible that
\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-e)<\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G)<\overline{\chi}_{\geqslant}(G-f)
.

Our previous considerations provide evidence supporting this conjecture. In particular, it holds for subdivided stars
S(S_{n})
with odd
n
. Moreover, it is easy to verify that odd cycles satisfy the conjecture as well.
