Elements in K 4 K_{4} and regulator maps of Fermat curves
François Brunault, David T.-B. G. Lilienfeldt, Yusuke Nemoto

Abstract
. We construct explicit elements in the group K 4 ( 3 ) K_{4}^{(3)} of the Fermat curves x N + y N = 1 x^{N}+y^{N}=1 for all N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . The construction, which is uniform in N N , uses polylogarithmic complexes and a map of de Jeu to K K -theory. We prove that the elements are non-trivial by showing that their images under Beilinson’s regulator map are non-zero. Notably, we obtain explicit formulas for their regulator integrals involving special values of Zagier’s trilogarithm function. As a corollary, we show that these regulator integrals are asymptotic to 3 2 ​ ζ ​ ( 3 ) ​ N 2 \frac{3}{2}\zeta(3)N^{2} as N → + ∞ N\to+\infty . Moreover, we derive formulas for the regulators of our elements in terms of hypergeometric functions, generalizing results of Otsubo for K 2 K_{2} groups of Fermat curves. Finally, we numerically verify some cases of Beilinson’s conjectures on special values of L L -functions at s = 3 s=3 for N ∈ { 3 , 4 , 6 } N\in\{3,4,6\} .

1.1.
Regulator maps

We prove Theorems
1.1

1.2
by studying the images of our elements under Beilinson’s regulator map
[

16

r_{B}:K_{4}^{(3)}(X_{N})\longrightarrow H^{1}(X_{N}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R}(2))^{+},

where
+

As before, we let
\varepsilon
denote a
2N
-th root of unity with
\varepsilon^{N}=-1

In order to prove Theorem

r_{B}((\tau_{\varepsilon})^{*}(\Xi_{N}))

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
N\geq 3
, we have the equality (up to sign)

\int_{\gamma_{N}}r_{B}((\tau_{\varepsilon})^{*}(\Xi_{N}))=\frac{4}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\left(f_{N,\varepsilon}(t)-\frac{f_{1,-1}(t)}{N}\right)dt+\left(\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{L}_{3}(-1/4)-N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{3}(\varepsilon(1/4)^{1/N})\right),

where
\mathcal{L}_{3}
is Zagier’s trilogarithm (

) and, for
x\in[0,1[
and

, the function
f_{N,z}
is given by

f_{N,z}(x):=\Re(\log(1-z(x(1-x))^{1/N}))\frac{\log(1-x)}{x}.

The appearance of Zagier’s trilogarithm in the above formula is quite remarkable. Its special values are connected with
\zeta(3)
and, as a corollary of Theorem

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\varepsilon=-1
if

is odd and
\varepsilon=-e^{\frac{\pi i}{N}}

is even. We have the asymptotic formula (up to sign)

\int_{\gamma_{N}}r_{B}((\tau_{\varepsilon})^{*}(\Xi_{N}))\sim\frac{3}{2}\zeta(3)N^{2},\qquad\text{ as }N\to+\infty.

An immediate corollary of Theorem
1.4
is that

is non-trivial for large

Theorem 1.5
.

For all
N\geq 3
,
r_{B}((\tau_{\varepsilon})^{*}(\Xi_{N}))
is non-zero.

We further obtain a closed formula for a differential form representing

, which is used in the proof of Theorem

The following result can be viewed as a generalization, from the setting of

Theorem 1.6
.

For all

r_{B}((\tau_{\varepsilon})^{*}(\Xi_{N}))=\dfrac{1}{3}\sum_{1\leq a<N}\varepsilon^{-a}\frac{\Gamma(2a)}{\Gamma(a)^{2}}\left(F\left(\dfrac{a}{N};-1\right)-F\left(1-\dfrac{a}{N};-1\right)\right)x^{a}y^{a-N}\frac{dx}{x},

is the hypergeometric function given by

F(\alpha;x)=\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(2\alpha\right)}\left(\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\left(\dfrac{2}{n+1}-\dfrac{1}{\alpha+i}\right)\dfrac{(\alpha,i)(\alpha,n+i+1)}{(2\alpha,n+2i+2)}x^{i}\right.\\

1.2.
Beilinson’s conjecture

Beilinson’s conjecture
[
1
]
relates the
K
-theory of smooth projective varieties over number fields to special values of their
L
-functions (see Section
8.1
for details in the case of curves). In our case, it predicts that the map
r_{B}\otimes\mathbb{R}
is an isomorphism with determinant (in suitable bases) equal to
L(X_{N},3)
up to a non-zero rational scalar and a specific power of
\pi
(see Section
8.1
).
Here, the
L
-function
L(X_{N},s):=L(h^{1}(X_{N}),s)
is the one associated with the compatible family of
\ell
-adic
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-representations
\{H^{1}_{\mathrm{et}}(X_{N,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\}_{\ell}
. It is given by a convergent Euler product in the region
\Re(s)>3/2
and satisfies a functional equation centered at
s=1
(due to the fact that it is a product of Hecke
L
-functions, see (
8.3
)). Equivalently, by the functional equation, the determinant is predicted to equal
L^{(g)}(X_{N},-1)
up to a non-zero rational scalar and a specific power of
\pi
, where
g
denotes the genus of
X_{N}
.
A version of Beilinson’s conjecture for Chow motives exists, and we now make it explicit for Fermat curves.

Following Otsubo
[
18
]
, in Section
2.2
we consider a motivic decomposition

\displaystyle h^{1}(X_{N})=\bigoplus_{[a,b]\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\backslash I_{N}}X_{N}^{[a,b]},

where
I_{N}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus 2}
consists of pairs
(a,b)
satisfying
a,b,a+b\neq 0
, and the
X_{N}^{[a,b]}
are certain rank
\varphi(N)
motives over
\mathbb{Q}
with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients (see Corollary
2.5
) that exist due to
X_{N}
having a large automorphism group. The
L
-functions of these motives are Hecke
L
-functions (see (
8.3
)) and as a result their analytic properties are well understood.
A weak version of Beilinson’s conjecture for these motives can be stated as follows.

Conjecture 1.7

(Beilinson)
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
(a,b)\in I_{N}
, and
k:=\varphi(N)/2
. Then there exist
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}
in

, a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis
(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k})
of
H_{1}(X_{N}^{[a,b]},\mathbb{Q})^{+}
, and a constant

such that

L^{(k)}(X_{N}^{[a,b]},-1)=c\pi^{-2k}\det\Bigl(\int_{\gamma_{i}}r_{B}(\xi_{j})\Bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}.

In Section
8
, we explain how to numerically evaluate the regulator integrals

\int_{\gamma_{N}}r_{B}(\Xi_{N})

We then proceed to numerically verify Conjecture

in the cases where

Numerically, we have the equalities (up to sign):

1.3.
Prior works

Beilinson
[
2
]
constructed elements in
K^{(n)}_{2n-2}(Y_{1}(N))
, where
Y_{1}(N)
is the modular curve of level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
and
n\geq 2
is an arbitrary integer. The construction uses his theory of the Eisenstein symbol, and the images of these elements under the Beilinson regulator map are related to
L
-values of modular forms of weight
2
at
s=n
. In particular, by Wiles’ modularity theorem, the weak form of Beilinson’s conjecture for
L(E,n)
is known to hold for all elliptic curves
E
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. The elements in
K^{(n)}_{2n-2}(E)
are obtained via push-forward along the modular parametrization
X_{1}(N)\to E
, and are therefore not explicit. Deninger
[
6
]
constructed explicit elements in
K
-groups of elliptic curves with CM by imaginary quadratic fields and used them to verify the weak form of Beilinson’s conjecture. In particular, the weak form of Beilinson’s conjecture is known for the motives considered in Theorem
1.8
. But for more general Fermat motives, not much is known. Neither Deninger nor Beilinson used polylogarithmic complexes in their constructions. In recent work
[
4
]
, the first-named author constructed explicit elements in
K_{4}^{(3)}(Y_{1}(N))
via the Goncharov–de Jeu method, and used these elements to numerically verify the weak version of Beilinson’s conjecture on
L(E,3)
for all elliptic curves
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
of conductor
\leq 50
. Ross
[
24
]
introduced a non-trivial element of
K_{2}^{(2)}(X_{N})
for Fermat curves (as mentioned already above).
Otsubo
[
18
]
gave explicit formulas for the image of the Ross element under regulator maps in terms of hypergeometric functions and numerically verified the weak version of Beilinson’s conjecture for some Fermat motives. The present work is the first to tackle Beilinson’s conjecture for general Fermat curves beyond
K_{2}
.

2.1.
Fermat curves

For

The Fermat curve has natural symmetries defined over

\displaystyle\colon(x_{0}:y_{0}:z_{0})\longmapsto(\varepsilon^{-1}z_{0}:y_{0}:\varepsilon^{-1}x_{0}).

Note that we have suppressed the dependency on

In terms of the affine coordinates

and

\displaystyle\iota

\displaystyle\colon(x,y)\longmapsto(y,x)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\colon(x,y)\longmapsto\left(\frac{\varepsilon x}{y},\frac{1}{y}\right)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\colon(x,y)\longmapsto\left(\frac{1}{x},\frac{\varepsilon y}{x}\right).

If

2.2.
Fermat motives

In this section, we recall the definition of Fermat motives. We refer to
[
18

for details.

Denote by
\mathrm{Corr}^{0}(X_{N},X_{N})
the ring of correspondences with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients. The identity element
1\in\mathrm{Corr}^{0}(X_{N},X_{N})
for the composition is the image of

under the diagonal embedding

. The motive of

is
h(X_{N}):=(X_{N},1)
and it is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients.

Fixing a base point

for

e^{0}=\{P\}\times X_{N},\qquad e^{2}=X_{N}\times\{P\},\qquad e^{1}=1-e^{0}-e^{2}.

The

is then
h^{i}(X_{N}):=(X_{N},e^{i})

\mathbb{Q}

\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients

h(X_{N})\simeq h^{0}(X_{N})\oplus h^{1}(X_{N})\oplus h^{2}(X_{N})\simeq h(\mathbb{P}^{1})\oplus h^{1}(X_{N}),

We define a subset of

I_{N}:=\{(a,b)\in G_{N}\mid a,b,a+b\neq 0\},

which is a set of cardinality

Fix a primitive
N
-th root of unity and let

We have

2.3.
Fermat quotients

For integers
0<r,s<N
, let
C_{N}^{r,s}
be the smooth projective curve birational to the affine curve

v^{N}=u^{r}(1-u)^{s}.

The map of affine curves

(2.16)

(x,y)\longmapsto(u,v)=(x^{N},x^{r}y^{s})

induces a morphism
\pi^{r,s}_{N}\colon X_{N}\to C_{N}^{r,s}
of curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.
When
\gcd(N,r,s)=1
, the curve
C^{r,s}_{N}
is called a
cyclic Fermat quotient
with genus given by the formula

g(C_{N}^{r,s})=(N+2-(\gcd(N,r)+\gcd(N,s)+\gcd(N,r+s)))/2.

The reason for the name is that the morphism
\pi^{r,s}_{N}\colon X_{N}\to C_{N}^{r,s}
is a cyclic Galois covering map with

G_{N}^{r,s}:=\mathrm{Gal}(X_{N}/C_{N}^{r,s})=\{g_{N}^{a,b}\mid ar+bs=0\}=\langle g_{N}^{s,-r}\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}.

With this description in hand, it is not difficult to verify that

H^{1,0}(C_{N}^{r,s}(\mathbb{C}))=H^{1,0}(X_{N}(\mathbb{C}))^{G_{N}^{r,s}}=\langle\omega_{N}^{a,b}\mid(a,b)\in I_{N},\langle a\rangle+\langle b\rangle<N,br\equiv as\mod N\rangle.

Moreover, if there exists
t\in H_{N}=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
such that

\{r,s,N-r-s\}\equiv\{tr^{\prime},ts^{\prime},t(N-r^{\prime}-s^{\prime})\}\mod N,

written
(r,s)\sim_{N}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
, then the curves
C_{N}^{r,s}
and
C_{N}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
are isomorphic over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Remark
2.7
.

It is known that a cyclic Fermat quotient is hyperelliptic if, and only if,
(r,s)\sim_{N}(1,1)
or
N=2n
and
(r,s)\sim_{N}(1,n)
. The hyperelliptic involution of
C^{1,1}_{N}
is given by
(u,v)\mapsto(1-u,v)
and is induced by the involution
\iota
(
2.1
) via the quotient map
\pi^{1,1}_{N}
.

Remark
2.8
.

In the case where

is prime and
(a,b)\in I_{N}
,

is a cyclic Fermat quotient of genus

Following
[

induces an isomorphism of motives over

3.
Polylogarithmic complexes of curves

3.1.
Polylogarithmic functions

We briefly recall the salient features of the relevant polylogarithmic functions and refer the reader to
[
12
]
for details.

3.1.1.
Simple polylogarithms

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the
k
-th polylogarithmic function is defined for complex
z
with
|z|<1
by the series

(3.1)

\mathrm{Li}_{k}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{k}}.

Note that

(3.2)

\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)=-\log(1-z)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{d}{dz}\mathrm{Li}_{k}(z)=\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k-1}(z)}{z}dz.

The inductive formula

\mathrm{Li}_{k}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k-1}(t)}{t}dt

gives the analytic continuation of
\mathrm{Li}_{k}
to a multi-valued function on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
. We have the special values

\mathrm{Li}_{k}(0)=0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mathrm{Li}_{k}(1)=\zeta(k),\quad k\geq 2.

3.1.2.
Bloch–Wigner’s dilogarithm

The Bloch–Wigner dilogarithm
D_{2}\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(3.3)

D_{2}(z)=\begin{cases}\Im(\mathrm{Li}_{2}(z)+\log(1-z)\log|z|),&\text{ if }|z|\leq 1;\\
-D_{2}(1/z),&\text{ if }|z|\geq 1.\end{cases}

It is single-valued, real-analytic on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
and continuous at
0,1,\infty
with values

(3.4)

D_{2}(0)=D_{2}(1)=D_{2}(\infty)=0.

It satisfies a remarkable
5
-term functional equation that we now recall. If
z_{1},\ldots,z_{4}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
are
4
distinct points with coordinates
\tilde{z}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{z}_{4}\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
, we let

r(z_{1},\ldots,z_{4}):=\frac{(\tilde{z}_{1}-\tilde{z}_{4})(\tilde{z}_{2}-\tilde{z}_{3})}{(\tilde{z}_{1}-\tilde{z}_{3})(\tilde{z}_{2}-\tilde{z}_{4})}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}

denote the cross-ratio of their coordinates (with the convention that
\frac{\infty}{\infty}=1
). For any five distinct points
z_{0},\ldots,z_{4}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, define

(3.5)

R_{2}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{4}):=\sum_{i=0}^{4}(-1)^{i}\{r(z_{0},\ldots,\hat{z}_{i},\ldots,z_{4})\}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}],

where we write
\{z\}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}]
for the basis element corresponding to
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
.
Viewing
D_{2}
as a homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}]\to\mathbb{R}
, we then have

(3.6)

D_{2}(R_{2}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{4}))=0,\quad\text{ for all distinct }z_{0},\ldots,z_{4}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}).

Note that any
5
-tuple
(z_{0},\ldots,z_{4})
of distinct points is projectively equivalent to
(0,\infty,1,x,y)
for some
x\neq y\in\mathbb{C}
. In particular, we have

(3.7)

D_{2}\left(\{x\}-\{y\}+\left\{\frac{y}{x}\right\}-\left\{\frac{1-x^{-1}}{1-y^{-1}}\right\}+\left\{\frac{1-x}{1-y}\right\}\right)=0.

The Bloch–Wigner function further satisfies the relation

(3.8)

D_{2}(z)=-D_{2}(1-z)=-D_{2}(z^{-1}),

which can be viewed as a degenerate case of (
3.6
).

3.1.3.
Zagier’s trilogarithm

A generalization of the Bloch–Wigner dilogarithm is Zagier’s trilogarithm
[
29
]

(3.9)

\mathcal{L}_{3}(z):=\Re\Bigl(\mathrm{Li}_{3}(z)-\mathrm{Li}_{2}(z)\log(|z|)+\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)\log(|z|)^{2}\Bigr)

whose definition can be found in
[
12
, (1.3)]
. The function
\mathcal{L}_{3}
is single-valued, real-analytic on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
and continuous at
0,1,\infty
with values

\mathcal{L}_{3}(0)=\mathcal{L}_{3}(\infty)=0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mathcal{L}_{3}(1)=\zeta(3).

It satisfies remarkable functional equations
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]
whose explicit statement we will not need. One advantage of this version of the trilogarithm is that its functional equations do not involve any remainder terms.

Proof.

4.1.
The construction

Let

\mathbb{Q}_{\zeta}:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)

for the base-change of

be its function field.
For integers

with

be the finite subset of closed points of

consisting of the cusps and of the zeros and poles of

Put

and define the element

\xi_{N}^{r,s}(\zeta):=\{x^{N}\}_{2}\otimes(1-\zeta x^{r}y^{s})-\frac{N}{s}\{\zeta x^{r}y^{s}\}_{2}\otimes x^{N}\in B_{2}(F_{N}(\zeta))\otimes F_{N}(\zeta)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

When

\xi^{r,s}_{N}
instead of
\xi^{r,s}_{N}(1)

Proposition 4.1
.

The element
\xi_{N}^{r,s}(\zeta)
is a
2
-cocycle in the Goncharov complex
\Gamma(F_{N}(\zeta),3)
.

Proof.

Writing
u=x^{N}
and
v=x^{r}y^{s}
, observe that
v^{N}=(x^{r}y^{s})^{N}=u^{r}(1-u)^{s}
.
We then have

\displaystyle\partial(\xi^{r,s}_{N}(\zeta))

\displaystyle=(1-u)\wedge u\wedge(1-\zeta v)-\frac{N}{s}(1-\zeta v)\wedge(\zeta v)\wedge u

\displaystyle=(1-u)\wedge u\wedge(1-\zeta v)-\frac{N}{s}(1-\zeta v)\wedge v\wedge u-\frac{N}{s}(1-\zeta v)\wedge\zeta\wedge u

\displaystyle=(1-u)\wedge u\wedge(1-\zeta v)-\frac{N}{s}(1-\zeta v)\wedge v\wedge u

\displaystyle=(1-u)\wedge u\wedge(1-\zeta v)-\frac{1}{s}(1-\zeta v)\wedge v^{N}\wedge u

\displaystyle=(1-u)\wedge u\wedge(1-\zeta v)-\frac{1}{s}(1-\zeta v)\wedge u^{r}(1-u)^{s}\wedge u

\displaystyle=(1-u)\wedge u\wedge(1-\zeta v)-\frac{1}{s}(1-\zeta v)\wedge(1-u)^{s}\wedge u

\displaystyle=(1-u)\wedge u\wedge(1-\zeta v)-(1-\zeta v)\wedge(1-u)\wedge u=0,

where we used the fact that the term
\frac{N}{s}(1-\zeta v)\wedge\zeta\wedge u
is torsion and can thus be ignored.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let

d

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}}\xi_{N}^{r,s}(\zeta\zeta_{d}^{k})

Proposition 4.6
.

Consider integers

such that

and

(ii)

Proposition 4.7
.

Given integers
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
with

\displaystyle\xi^{r,s}_{N}(\zeta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b)\in G_{N}\\
br\equiv as\bmod{N}\end{subarray}}p_{N}^{a,b}\xi^{r,s}_{N}(\zeta)\in B_{2}(F_{N}(\zeta))\otimes F_{N}(\zeta)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

By Proposition

It follows that in the group

\displaystyle\xi^{r,s}_{N}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}g_{N}^{sk,-rk}\right)\xi^{r,s}_{N}(\zeta)\overset{\eqref{projector1}}{=}\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}g_{N}^{sk,-rk}\right)\sum_{(a,b)\in G_{N}}p_{N}^{a,b}\xi^{r,s}_{N}(\zeta)

Proposition 4.9
.

We have the following equalities of cocycles modulo

-coboundaries:

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

During the course of the proof, we will use the relation

For the proof of (i), we observe that

For the proof of (iv), since

Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle r_{3}(2)(\{f\}_{2}\otimes g):=-D_{2}(f)d\arg(g)-\frac{1}{3}\left(-\log|1-f|d\log|f|+\log|f|d\log|1-f|\right)\cdot\log|g|,

[

,
16
]
defined a regulator map

The regulator maps of Beilinson and Goncharov are known to be compatible (up to a factor
2
and a sign) via the map (
3.11
) as a consequence of de Jeu’s computations of regulator integrals in
[
9
,
10

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\varepsilon
be a
2N
-th root of unity with

. Then

r_{3}(2)(\xi_{N}^{1,1}(\varepsilon))=\dfrac{1}{6N}\sum_{1\leq a<N}\varepsilon^{-a}\left(F\left(\dfrac{a}{N};-1\right)-F\left(1-\dfrac{a}{N};-1\right)\right)\widetilde{\omega}_{N}^{a,a}.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
6.2
with equation (
6.4
) yields

\int_{\gamma_{N}}r_{3}(2)(\xi_{N}^{1,1}(\varepsilon))=\int_{\gamma_{N}}(\tilde{\rho}_{N,\varepsilon}^{1}+\tilde{\rho}_{N,\varepsilon}^{2}),

where, by (
6.5
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{N,\varepsilon}^{1}+\tilde{\rho}_{N,\varepsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\left(-\log|1-x^{N}|d\log|x^{N}|+\log|x^{N}|d\log|1-x^{N}|\right)\cdot\log|1-\varepsilon xy|

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{N}{3}\left(-\log|1-\varepsilon xy|d\log|xy|+\log|xy|d\log|1-\varepsilon xy|\right)\cdot\log|x^{N}|

\displaystyle=\Re\left(-\frac{1}{3}\left(-\log(1-x^{N})d\log(x^{N})+\log(x^{N})d\log(1-x^{N})\right)\cdot\log(1-\varepsilon xy)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\frac{N}{3}\left(-\log(1-\varepsilon xy)d\log(xy)+\frac{1}{N}\log(xy)^{N}d\log(1-\varepsilon xy)\right)\cdot\log(x^{N})\right).

First, we compute the term
\log(1-x^{N})d\log x^{N}\log(1-\varepsilon xy)
.
For
|x|<1
,
|y|<1
, we have

\log(1-x^{N})d\log x^{N}\log(1-\varepsilon xy)=N\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{(\varepsilon xy)^{m}}{m}\dfrac{(x^{N})^{n}}{n}\dfrac{dx}{x}\\
=N\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{\varepsilon^{m}}{mn}\omega_{N}^{m+Nn,m+N}\equiv N\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{\varepsilon^{m}}{n(2m+Nn)}\omega_{N}^{m+Nn,m}

modulo exact forms by
[
18
, Lemma 4.19]
.
Put
m=a+Ni
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle N\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{\varepsilon^{m}}{n(2m+Nn)}\omega_{N}^{m+Nn,m}

\displaystyle=N\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(n+1)}\dfrac{1}{2a+N(n+2i+1)}\omega_{N}^{a+N(n+i+1),a+Ni}

\displaystyle\equiv N\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(n+1)}\dfrac{\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)}{(2a+N(n+2i+1))\left(\frac{2a}{N},n+2i+1\right)}\omega_{N}^{a,a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(n+1)}\dfrac{\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},n+2i+2\right)}\omega_{N}^{a,a}.

We compute similarly that

\displaystyle\log x^{N}d\log(1-x^{N})\log(1-\varepsilon xy)

\displaystyle=\log(1-y^{N})d\log(y^{N})\log(1-\varepsilon xy)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(n+1)}\dfrac{\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},n+2i+2\right)}\omega_{N}^{a,a}.

Next, we compute the term
\log(1-\varepsilon xy)d\log(xy)\log(x^{N})
.
We have

\displaystyle\log(1-\varepsilon xy)d\log(xy)\log(x^{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{(\varepsilon xy)^{m}}{m}\dfrac{(y^{N})^{n}}{n}\left(\dfrac{dx}{x}+\dfrac{dy}{y}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{(\varepsilon xy)^{m}}{m}\dfrac{(y^{N})^{n}}{n}\dfrac{y^{N}-x^{N}}{xy^{N}}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{\varepsilon^{m}}{mn}(\omega_{N}^{m,m+N+Nn}-\omega_{N}^{m+N,m+Nn})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{N\varepsilon^{m}}{m(2m+Nn)}\omega_{N}^{m,m+Nn}

modulo exact forms.
Put
m=a+Ni
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{N\varepsilon^{m}}{m(2m+Nn)}

\displaystyle\omega_{N}^{m,m+Nn}=\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\dfrac{N(-1)^{i}}{(a+Ni)(2a+N(n+2i+1)}\omega_{N}^{a+Ni,a+N(n+i+1)}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(a+Ni)\left(\frac{2a}{N}+n+2i+1\right)}\dfrac{\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},n+2i+1\right)}\omega_{N}^{a,a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(a+Ni)}\dfrac{\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},n+2i+2\right)}\omega_{N}^{a,a}.

Finally, we deal with the term
\log(xy)d\log(1-\varepsilon xy)\log(x^{N})
.
We compute that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}

\displaystyle\log(xy)^{N}d\log(1-\varepsilon xy)\log(x^{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\log(x^{N}y^{N})d\log(1-\varepsilon xy)\log(x^{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\log(1-x^{N})(1-y^{N})d\log(1-\varepsilon xy)\log(x^{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\left(\dfrac{x^{Nm}}{m}+\frac{y^{Nm}}{m}\right)\dfrac{y^{Nn}}{n}\sum_{l\geq 1}(\varepsilon xy)^{l}(-1)\left(\dfrac{y^{N}-x^{N}}{xy^{N}}\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l,m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{\varepsilon^{l}}{mn}\left(\omega_{N}^{l+Nm,l+Nn+N}-\omega_{N}^{l+Nm+N,l+Nn}+\omega_{N}^{l,l+Nn+Nm+N}-\omega_{N}^{l+N,l+Nm+Nn}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l,m,n\geq 1}\dfrac{\varepsilon^{l}}{mn}\dfrac{1}{(2l+N(m+n))}\left(N(n-m)\omega_{N}^{l+Nm,l+Nn}+N(n+m)\omega_{N}^{l,l+N(n+m)}\right).

Put
l=a+Ni
. Then we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,m,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(m+1)(n+1)}\dfrac{N}{(2a+N(m+n+2i+2))\left(\frac{2a}{N},m+n+2i+2\right)}

\displaystyle\times\left((n-m)\left(\frac{a}{N},m+i+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)+(n+m+2)\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+m+i+2\right)\right)\omega_{N}^{a,a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,m,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(m+1)(n+1)}\dfrac{1}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},m+n+2i+3\right)}

\displaystyle\times\left((n-m)\left(\frac{a}{N},m+i+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)+(n+m+2)\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+m+i+2\right)\right)\omega_{N}^{a,a}.

The term

\sum_{i,m,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(m+1)(n+1)}\dfrac{(n-m)\left(\frac{a}{N},m+i+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},m+n+2i+3\right)}

is equal to zero since

\frac{(-1)^{i}}{(m+1)(n+1)}\frac{(n-m)\left(\frac{a}{N},m+i+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},m+n+2i+3\right)}

is anti-symmetric with respect to
m
and
n
.
Putting everything together shows that
\int_{\gamma_{N}}r_{3}(2)(\xi_{N}^{1,1}(\varepsilon))
is the integral over
\gamma_{N}
of the differential form

\displaystyle\Re\Bigl(\frac{2}{3}\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(n+1)}\dfrac{\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},n+2i+2\right)}\omega_{N}^{a,a}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{N}{3}\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(a+Ni)}\dfrac{\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},n+2i+2\right)}\omega_{N}^{a,a}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\sum_{i,m,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(m+1)(n+1)}\dfrac{1}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},m+n+2i+3\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left((n+m+2)\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+m+i+2\right)\right)\omega_{N}^{a,a}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{3}\Re\Bigl(\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}\left(\sum_{i,n\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\dfrac{\left(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},n+2i+2\right)}\left(\dfrac{2}{n+1}-\dfrac{1}{\frac{a}{N}+i}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-\sum_{i,m,n\geq 0}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(m+1)(n+1)}\dfrac{(n+m+2)\left(\frac{a}{N},i\right)\left(\frac{a}{N},n+m+i+2\right)}{\left(\frac{2a}{N},m+n+2i+3\right)}\right)\omega_{N}^{a,a}\Bigr).

Observe that

\varepsilon^{a}F\left(\frac{a}{N};-1\right)=\varepsilon^{a}\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{a}{N}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{2a}{N}\right)}\left(\sum_{i,n\geq 0}\left(\dfrac{2}{n+1}-\dfrac{1}{\frac{a}{N}+i}\right)\dfrac{(\frac{a}{N},i)(\frac{a}{N},n+i+1)}{(\frac{2a}{N},n+2i+2)}(-1)^{i}\right.\\
\left.-\sum_{i,m,n\geq 0}\left(\dfrac{1}{m+1}+\dfrac{1}{n+1}\right)\dfrac{(\frac{a}{N},i)(\frac{a}{N},m+n+i+2)}{(\frac{2a}{N},m+n+2i+3)}(-1)^{i}\right).

We thus conclude that

\int_{\gamma_{N}}r_{3}(2)(\xi_{N}^{1,1}(\varepsilon))=\frac{1}{3}\Re\left(\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2a}{N}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{a}{N}\right)^{2}}\varepsilon^{a}F\left(\frac{a}{N};-1\right)\int_{\gamma_{N}}\omega_{N}^{a,a}\right).

Using the formula

\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2a}{N}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{a}{N}\right)^{2}}=B\left(\frac{a}{N},\frac{a}{N}\right)^{-1},

we arrive at the formula

\int_{\gamma_{N}}r_{3}(2)(\xi_{N}^{1,1}(\varepsilon))=\frac{1}{3}\Re\left(\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}F\left(\frac{a}{N};-1\right)B\left(\frac{a}{N},\frac{a}{N}\right)^{-1}\int_{\gamma_{N}}\omega_{N}^{a,a}\right),

which in turn yields

(7.4)

\int_{\gamma_{N}}r_{3}(2)(\xi_{N}^{1,1}(\varepsilon))=\frac{1}{3N}\Re\left(\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}F\left(\frac{a}{N};-1\right)\int_{\gamma_{N}}\tilde{\omega}_{N}^{a,a},\right).

Since
\gamma_{N}
generates
H_{1}(X_{N}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})
as a cyclic
\mathbb{Q}[G_{N}]
-module (see the discussion preceding Lemma
6.2
), we deduce that for all
\gamma\in H_{1}(X_{N}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
we have

\int_{\gamma}r_{3}(2)(\xi_{N}^{1,1}(\varepsilon))=\frac{1}{3N}\Re\left(\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}\varepsilon^{a}F\left(\frac{a}{N};-1\right)\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{\omega}_{N}^{a,a}\right).

Indeed, as
(r,s)
ranges over
I_{N}
, the cycles
\kappa^{r,s}_{N}:=(1-g_{N}^{r,0})(1-g_{N}^{0,s})\delta_{N}
give a basis of
H_{1}(X_{N}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})
(see
[
20
, Theorem 4.1]
).
On
\kappa_{N}^{r,s}
, we have
|x|<1
and
|y|<1
except at the cusps, hence equation (
7.4
) holds for any
\gamma
by an argument similar to
[
18
]
.
Since

\displaystyle\Re\left(\varepsilon^{a}F\left(\frac{a}{N};-1\right)\right)\cdot\Re\left(\widetilde{\omega}_{N}^{a,a}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}(\varepsilon^{a}+\varepsilon^{-a})F\left(\frac{a}{N};-1\right)\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}(\widetilde{\omega}_{N}^{a,a}+\widetilde{\omega}_{N}^{N-a,N-a})

and

\displaystyle\qquad=\dfrac{1}{12N}\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}(\varepsilon^{a}+\varepsilon^{-a})\left(F\left(\frac{a}{N};-1\right)-F\left(1-\frac{a}{N};-1\right)\right)\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{\omega}_{N}^{a,a}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\dfrac{1}{12N}\sum_{1\leq a\leq N}(\varepsilon^{a}-\varepsilon^{-a})\left(F\left(\frac{a}{N};-1\right)-F\left(1-\frac{a}{N};-1\right)\right)\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{\omega}_{N}^{a,a}.

Note that
\omega_{N}^{N,N}
is an exact form and therefore we may drop the term

.
In conclusion, we have shown that

\int_{\gamma}r_{3}(2)(\xi_{N}^{1,1}(\varepsilon))=\dfrac{1}{6N}\sum_{1\leq a<N}\varepsilon^{-a}\left(F\left(\dfrac{a}{N};-1\right)-F\left(1-\dfrac{a}{N};-1\right)\right)\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{\omega}_{N}^{a,a}

for all

,

∎

Lemma 8.3

For an integer

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}k\zeta_{N}^{jk}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{N}^{j}-1}.

Lemma 8.4

(\tau^{-1})_{*}(\gamma_{N})=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{k}{N}g^{k+1,k}(\gamma_{N})-\frac{k}{N}g^{k,k}(\gamma_{N}).

8.3.
Checking the conjecture

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.8
.
The element
\Xi_{N}^{-2,1}
lives in a specific part of the motivic cohomology of

. Indeed, by Proposition
4.7
and functoriality of

The most interesting part of the decomposition above comes from the terms

of

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=0\\

(see
[

, as well as for

in the case

is even, since the Fermat curves

and
X_{2}
have genus

.

For the numerical verification, we focus on those motives

that have rank
2
(see (

) where all rank
2

are listed).
Then there exist elliptic curves
E_{N}
over
\mathbb{Q}
with CM by
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})
such that

as motives with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients.
In order to compute the

-functions in the relevant cases for us, we will give explicit decriptions of

,

, and
E_{6}
below (up to isogeny, which is enough to compute the

]
for example). The latter approach is better suited for higher rank cases.

Proposition 8.7
.

There exist isogenies defined over
\mathbb{Q}
:

\displaystyle E_{3}\sim 27a1:v_{0}^{2}w_{0}=u_{0}^{3}-432w_{0}^{3},

\displaystyle E_{4}\sim 32a2:v_{0}^{2}w_{0}=u_{0}^{3}-u_{0}w_{0}^{2},

\displaystyle E_{6}\sim 108a1:v_{0}^{2}w_{0}=u_{0}^{3}+4w_{0}^{3},

where we use the labels from Cremona’s tables of elliptic curves
[
5
]
.

Proof.

The first two cases are well-known (see
[
17
, Sections 5.2 and 5.3]
), so we will prove the last case. Let
E
be the elliptic curve given by the affine equation
v^{2}=u^{3}+4
.
Consider the morphism

\displaystyle p\colon X_{6}\longrightarrow E,\qquad(x,y)\longmapsto\left(\frac{x^{4}}{y^{2}},y^{3}+\frac{1}{y^{3}}\right).

Observe that

p^{*}\left(\dfrac{du}{2v}\right)=-y^{1}x^{-2}\frac{dy}{y}=\omega_{6}^{4,1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad p^{*}\left(\dfrac{u\,du}{2v}\right)=-y^{-1}x^{2}\frac{dy}{y}=\omega_{6}^{8,-1}\equiv-2\omega_{6}^{2,5},

where we used
[
18
, Lemma 4.19]
. By (
2.12
), we see that
p^{*}(H^{1}_{\rm dR}(E))=H^{1}_{\rm dR}(X_{6}^{[-2,1]})
. Since
p
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, this implies that it induces an isomorphism
X_{6}^{[-2,1]}\simeq h^{1}(E)
.
∎

8.3.1.
Case
N=3

In this case
X_{3}
is isomorphic to the elliptic curve 27a1 and isogenous to the elliptic curve
E_{3}
by Proposition
8.7
. Using the functions
lfun

lindep
of PARI/GP

R_{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}-\frac{4}{5}\pi^{2}L^{\prime}(E_{3},-1).

Here
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}

\frac{1}{2}

Remark
8.8
.

In
[
11
, p. 19]
, de Jeu considers the elliptic curve
E\colon u(1-u)=(-v)^{3}
(Cremona label 27a3), which is isomorphic to
C^{1,1}_{3}
over
\mathbb{Q}
and
3
-isogenous to the elliptic curve
X_{3}
(Cremona label 27a1) over
\mathbb{Q}
. An explicit
3
-isogeny
\psi\colon X_{3}\to E
is given by
(x,y)\mapsto(u,v)=(x^{3},-xy)
. De Jeu produces an element in
K_{4}^{(3)}(E)
by constructing an explicit degree
2
cocycle in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{(3)}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{Q}(E))
. Translated into the Goncharov complex, the cocycle constructed by de Jeu is:

\beta:=\{u\}_{2}\otimes(1-v)-3\{v\}_{2}\otimes u\in B_{2}(\mathbb{Q}(E))\otimes\mathbb{Q}(E)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Pulling it back to
X_{3}
via
\psi
recovers our cocycle

\xi_{3}^{1,1}(-1)=\{x^{3}\}_{2}\otimes(1+xy)-3\{-xy\}_{2}\otimes x^{3}\in B_{2}(\mathbb{Q}(X_{3}))\otimes\mathbb{Q}(X_{3})^{\times}_{\mathbb{Q}},

and this is the observation that originally inspired our construction in Section
4
.

De Jeu integrates
r_{B}(\varphi_{\rm dJ}(\beta))
over a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
H_{1}({E(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}})^{+}
.
By
[
20
, Corollary 2.3]
,
\gamma_{3}
is not integral but we have

3\gamma_{3}\in H_{1}(X_{3}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
. This element gives the desired
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
H_{1}({E(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}})^{+}
after applying
\psi_{*}
. In fact, it can be shown that

\{(\pi_{3}^{1,1})_{*}(3\gamma_{3}),\zeta_{3}\cdot(\pi_{3}^{1,1})_{*}(3\gamma_{3})\}

is a symplectic basis of
H_{1}(C_{3}^{1,1}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
as we now explain. Here,
\zeta_{3}\cdot(\pi_{3}^{1,1})_{*}(3\gamma_{3})
is the cycle induced by the action
\zeta_{3}\cdot(u,v):=(u,\zeta_{3}v)
.
For
\gamma
,
\gamma^{\prime}\in H_{1}(X_{3}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
, let
\gamma\sharp\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
denote the intersection number.
Note that
X_{3}
is generically Galois over
C_{3}^{1,1}
and
\operatorname{Gal}(X_{3}/C_{3}^{1,1})=\langle g_{3}^{1,2}\rangle
.
Therefore we have

(8.11)

\displaystyle(\pi_{3}^{1,1})_{*}(3\gamma_{3})\sharp(\zeta_{3}\cdot(\pi_{3}^{1,1})_{*}(3\gamma_{3}))

\displaystyle=9\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}}(g_{3}^{r,2r}\gamma_{3})\sharp(g_{3}^{0,1}\gamma_{3})

by the projection formula.
It follows from
[
20
, Corollary 3.4]
that the right hand side of (
8.11
) is equal to
1
,
which means that

\{(\pi_{3}^{1,1})_{*}(3\gamma_{3}),\zeta_{3}\cdot(\pi_{3}^{1,1})_{*}(3\gamma_{3})\}
is a symplectic basis of
H_{1}(C_{3}^{1,1}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
.
Therefore, de Jeu’s computation shows that

(2\pi i)^{-2}\int_{\psi_{*}(3\gamma_{3})}r_{B}(\varphi_{\rm dJ}(\beta))\overset{?}{=}\frac{6}{5}L^{\prime}(E,-1),

hence

\int_{\gamma_{3}}r_{B}(\varphi_{\rm dJ}(\xi_{3}^{1,1}(-1)))=\int_{\psi_{*}(\gamma_{3})}r_{B}(\varphi_{\rm dJ}(\beta))\overset{?}{=}-\frac{8}{5}\pi^{2}L^{\prime}(E,-1).

This matches our numerical calculation of
R_{3}
by Theorem
6.1
, which is valid up to sign.

Remark
8.9
.

We end the paper with a few of remarks.

•

In the case
N=5
, the rank of the motive
X_{5}^{[-2,1]}
is
4
by Corollary
2.5
. Therefore checking Beilinson’s conjecture requires two linearly independent elements of
K_{4}^{(3)}(X_{5}^{[-2,1]})
, and we only constructed one element. By Remark
2.8
(valid for
N
prime), we have
X_{5}^{[-2,1]}\simeq h^{1}(C_{5}^{1,3})
. Here,
C_{5}^{1,3}
is the cyclic Fermat quotient
v^{5}=u(1-u)^{3}
of genus
2
. In particular, it is hyperelliptic. Since its Jacobian
J^{1,3}_{5}
is absolutely simple (see
[
15
]
for an easy criterion for simplicity), we cannot hope to construct another element using endomorphisms.

•

In
[
4
, Theorem 7.2]
, the first-named author constructed elements
\xi_{1}(a,b)\in K_{4}^{(3)}(X_{1}(N))
for
(a,b)\in G_{N}
. These can be used to define elements of
K_{4}^{(3)}(E)
for all elliptic curves
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
via modular parametrizations. An interesting but difficult question would be to relate these elements to ours for the elliptic curves
E_{3}
,
E_{4}
and
E_{6}
.

•

When the motives
X_{N}^{[a,b]}
have rank
2
(listed in (
2.13
)), Conjecture
8.2
is known due to work of Deninger
[
6
]
. This situation is similar to the one encountered by Otsubo in the setting of
K_{2}
of Fermat curves. Indeed, he makes a short remark
[
18
, Remark 4.18]
stating that the surjectivity of the regulator map
r_{B}(2)
for these rank
2
motives was already known. Our computations in Section
8.3
numerically recover Deninger’s results for the motives
X^{[1,1]}_{3}
,
X_{4}^{[2,1]}
, and
X^{[4,1]}_{6}
using a different construction of
K_{4}
elements.
It would be interesting (but probably difficult) to compare Deninger’s construction with ours.
