Development and preliminary evaluation of multiplex PCR assays for the detection of bacterial respiratory pathogens in bovine and ovine
Authors: Zhipeng Zhao, Jie Peng, Qiangsheng Lu, Lvfeng Yuan, Meijuan Tian, Shangdong Jia, Xiaohua Du
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Bovine and ovine respiratory diseases are commonly associated with multiple bacterial pathogens, including Mycoplasma bovis (Mb), Mycoplasma ovipneumoniae (Mo), Mannheimia haemolytica (Mh), Pasteurella multocida (Pm), and Mycobacterium bovis (Tb). Among these, Mb and Mo are the primary causative agents of mycoplasma pneumonia in cattle and sheep, respectively. Based on this difference, two multiplex PCR assays were developed for the detection of bacterial respiratory pathogens in cattle and sheep, differing mainly in the primers targeting Mycoplasma spp. The bovine assay simultaneously detected Mb, Mh, Pm, and Tb, while the ovine assay detected Mo, Mh, Pm, and Tb, with expected amplicon sizes of 319/320 bp, 390 bp, 528 bp, and 274 bp. After optimization, both assays were able to simultaneously detect the target pathogens and showed good specificity, sensitivity, and reproducibility, with detection limits of 1.0 × 10³ copies/μL for the bovine assay and 1.0 × 10²–10³ copies/μL for the ovine assay. Preliminary testing using clinical samples demonstrated that the assays could detect the target bacterial pathogens. These results indicate that the developed multiplex PCR assays provide a basis for further validation and potential application in the detection of bacterial respiratory pathogens in cattle and sheep.

Introduction

Respiratory diseases in cattle and sheep are major health concerns in the global livestock industry, typically presenting with clinical signs such as fever, coughing, and dyspnea (Goto et al., 2023; Lacasta et al., 2019; Hermeyer et al., 2012). The etiology of these diseases is complex and involves a wide range of pathogens, including both bacteria and viruses (Gaudino et al., 2022). From a clinical perspective, the detection of bacterial pathogens is particularly important, as it directly informs antimicrobial therapy and disease control strategies. Bacterial pathogens such as Mycoplasma, Mannheimia haemolytica, Pasteurella multocida, and Mycobacterium spp. play key roles in the development and progression of respiratory diseases in cattle and sheep and are often associated with severe clinical outcomes. Specifically, bovine respiratory diseases are primarily caused by Mycoplasma bovis (Mb), Mannheimia haemolytica (Mh), Pasteurella multocida (Pm), and Mycobacterium bovis (Tb), whereas ovine respiratory diseases are mainly associated with Mycoplasma ovipneumoniae (Mo)(Wang et al., 2024), in addition to Mh, Pm, and Tb.

Respiratory diseases caused by these pathogens are associated with high morbidity and mortality worldwide (Pansri et al., 2020), resulting in substantial economic losses estimated at up to $30 billion annually in the global cattle and sheep industry. Epidemiological data indicate that more than half of the diseases affecting cattle and sheep are respiratory in nature, with animals aged 2–6 months being particularly susceptible (Kelly et al., 1986; Fodor, 2009). Reported morbidity rates range from 30% to 80%, while mortality rates range from 10% to 35%. In the United Kingdom, approximately 2.1 million cattle and sheep are affected by respiratory diseases each year, with a mortality rate of 8.5% (approximately 178,000 deaths) (Rose et al., 2012; Thomas et al., 2002). Regional studies further highlight the importance of bacterial pathogens. For instance, Mh and Pm were identified as major contributors to ovine respiratory infections in southern India (Sahay et al., 2020). In Canada, respiratory diseases accounted for 68% of deaths in 6-month-old calves within 60 days of feedlot arrival (Gagea et al., 2006). Additionally, Pm infection was detected in 17% of diseased cattle in Scotland (Hotchkiss et al., 2010).

Accurate and timely pathogen detection is essential for controlling these diseases (Howe et al., 2023). Traditional diagnostic methods, including bacterial culture, serological testing, and clinical observation, are often time-consuming, labor-intensive, and limited in sensitivity (Wang et al., 2025). Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) offers high sensitivity and specificity and enables rapid detection of pathogens. However, most existing PCR assays focus on single pathogens, which limits their efficiency in diagnosing mixed infections (Kamel et al., 2024).

Therefore, in this study, two multiplex PCR assays were developed for the simultaneous detection of common bacterial respiratory pathogens in cattle and sheep. By enabling the detection of multiple pathogens within a single reaction, this approach aims to improve diagnostic efficiency and provide a practical tool for the detection of bacterial respiratory infections in clinical settings.

Methods

Pathogen

Reference strains: Mo (Y98), Mh (DSM 5283), and Pm (CCUG 35587) were preserved by the College of Veterinary Medicine, Gansu Agricultural University. Genomic DNA of Mb (08M) and Tb (ATCC 19210) was provided by the Laboratory of Veterinary Microbiology, Lanzhou Veterinary Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences.

Interfering pathogens: Genomic DNA of Salmonella Enteritidis (DK6), Escherichia coli (ATCC 8739), Staphylococcus aureus (ATCC 25923), Streptococcus agalactiae (ATCC 13813), Bovine coronavirus (BCoV), Bovine respiratory syncytial virus (BRSV), Bovine parainfluenza virus type 3 (BPIV3), and Bovine adenovirus type 3 (BAdV3) were all preserved by the College of Veterinary Medicine, Gansu Agricultural University.

Nucleic acid extraction

Genomic DNA of Mo, Mh, and Pm was extracted according to the instructions of the Fast Pure Bacteria DNA Isolation Mini Kit-BOX 2 (Vazyme, China). DNA concentration was measured using a NanoDrop-2000 (Thermo Fisher NanoDrop, USA), and the extracts were stored at -20°C for later use.

Sample preparation

DNA samples of Mb, Mo, Mh, Pm and Tb were individually diluted to a concentration of 1 ng/µL, designated as Mb, Mo, Mh, Pm and Tb respectively, and stored as template stock solutions for subsequent use. Each of these five template stock solutions was then subjected to a single 10-fold dilution, and the resulting diluted samples were designated sequentially as PO1, PO2, PO3, PO4 and PO5. Four template stock solutions (Mb, Mh, Pm and Tb) were mixed in equal volumes, followed by a 10-fold dilution to generate the sample designated as PO6. PO6 was then subjected to 10-fold serial dilutions, with the resulting samples designated sequentially as PO8 and PO9. Similarly, four template stock solutions (Mo, Mh, Pm and Tb) were mixed in equal volumes and then diluted 10-fold to produce the sample designated as PO7. Single PCR amplification

Single-specific PCR amplification was performed using the prepared ParaOpt 1-5 as templates and ddH2O as the negative control, following the instructions of the 2×Taq Master Mix (TaKaRa, China). The 20 μL reaction system consisted of 10 μL 2×Taq Master Mix, 1 μL each of forward and reverse primers (10 μmol/L), 1 μL template, and 7 μL ddH2O. The PCR program was as follows: pre-denaturation at 94°C for 5 min; 30 cycles of denaturation at 98°C for 10 s, annealing at 56°C for 30 s, and extension at 72°C for 50 s; final extension at 72°C for 5 min. Amplified products were identified by electrophoresis on a 15 g/L agarose gel at 120 V, and electrophoresis results were observed using an ultraviolet gel imaging system. We have also sequenced the PCR amplicons of the five target genes and performed sequence similarity searches on the obtained sequences using the BLAST program on the NCBI website.

Establishment and condition optimization of multiplex PCR assay

Annealing temperature, extension time, and primer concentration were optimized separately. A gradient PCR was performed with 12 temperature gradients ranging from 45°C to 65°C. The clarity and specificity of target bands were observed by nucleic acid electrophoresis to screen the optimal annealing temperature. Under the optimal annealing temperature, the extension time was adjusted (10-40 s, with 10 s intervals) to determine the optimal extension time. Specificity test of multiplex PCR

Using the nucleic acids corresponding to the five target bacteria as templates, with ddH2O as the negative control. Salmonella Enteritidis, Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus agalactiae, bovine coronavirus, bovine respiratory syncytial virus, bovine parainfluenza virus type 3, and bovine adenovirus type 3 were used as interfering pathogens. PCR amplification was performed according to the optimized multiplex PCR system and conditions to verify the specificity of the established multiplex PCR assays.

Sensitivity test of multiplex PCR

Using the nucleic acids of Mb, Mo, Mh, Pm and Tb as templates, recombinant plasmid standards were constructed and designated as pMDTM-T-ftsH, pMDTM-T-P80, pMDTM-T-pepS6, pMDTM-T-lon, and pMDTM-T-pks2, respectively. These recombinant plasmids were mixed in equal volumes and serially diluted 10-fold from 1.0×109 copies/μL to 1.0×100 copies/μL, then detected using the optimized multiplex PCR assays.

Reproducibility test of multiplex PCR

Using the optimized mixed plasmid standards as templates, intragroup and intergroup reproducibility tests were performed. For intragroup reproducibility: the same batch of templates was amplified three times under the same reaction conditions. For intergroup reproducibility: three different batches of mixed plasmid standards were amplified under the same reaction conditions. The reproducibility of the assays was evaluated based on the consistency of electrophoresis bands.

Clinical sample detection

A total of 200 nasal swab samples (100 from bovines and 100 from sheep) were collected from multiple large-scale farms in Gansu Province. All samples were individually packaged, labeled with sample numbers, and stored at -80°C. The two established multiplex PCR assays were used to detect 100 bovine clinical samples and 100 ovine clinical samples, respectively, to verify their clinical detection performance.

No amplification was detected from any of the non-target microorganisms tested. Sensitivity test of multiplex PCR

The sensitivity of the multiplex PCR assays was evaluated using serial dilutions of recombinant plasmid standards. For the bovine respiratory multiplex PCR assay, the limit of detection (LOD) for pMDTM-T-ftsH, pMDTM-T-pepS6, pMDTM-T-lon, and pMDTM-T-pks2 was 1.0 × 103 copies/μL (Fig. 2C). For the ovine respiratory multiplex PCR assay, the LOD was 1.0 × 102 copies/μL for pMDTM-T-pepS6 and pMDTM-T-lon, and 1.0 × 103 copies/μL for pMDTM-T-P80 and pMDTM-T-pks2 (Fig. 2D). These results indicate that the established multiplex PCR assays were able to detect target pathogens at low template concentrations.

Reproducibility test of multiplex PCR

The reproducibility of the multiplex PCR assays was evaluated through intra-group and inter-group repeatability tests using mixed plasmid standards. As shown in Fig. 3A and C, intra-group repeatability testing demonstrated that under the same experimental conditions using the same mixed plasmid standards, consistent amplification patterns were obtained across repeated assays for both the bovine and ovine multiplex PCR systems. All target bands were clearly observed with expected sizes, and no non-specific amplification or missing bands were detected. Inter-group repeatability testing (Fig. 3B and D) further showed that consistent amplification results were obtained when using different sets of mixed plasmid standards under the same conditions. The band patterns remained stable, and all target fragments were reliably amplified without non-specific products. Clinical sample detection

To evaluate the practical applicability of the established assays, the bovine respiratory multiplex PCR assay was applied to 100 clinical samples. As shown in Table 6, 61 samples were positive for at least one target pathogen. Among these, Pm was the most frequently detected pathogen. Of the 61 positive samples, mixed infections were observed in 50.8% (31/61) of cases. Double infections accounted for 31.2% (19/61), among which the combination of “Pm + Mh” was the most common (63.2%, 12/19). Triple infections accounted for 13.1% (8/61), and quadruple infections for 6.6% (4/61). The same set of clinical samples was also tested using single PCR assays. Discussion

The bovine and ovine industry is an important component of the global livestock sector, contributing significantly to food supply and economic development (Boden et al., 2015). However, respiratory diseases remain a major constraint due to their high prevalence, complex etiology, and challenges in prevention and control (Bell et al., 2021). In this context, rapid and accurate pathogen detection is essential for effective clinical management.

Respiratory diseases in cattle and sheep are often associated with mixed infections involving bacterial, viral, and mycoplasmal pathogens, which may present with overlapping clinical symptoms (Flora et al., 2025; Roe, 2025). From a clinical perspective, the identification of bacterial pathogens is particularly important for guiding antimicrobial therapy. In this study, two multiplex PCR assays targeting common bacterial pathogens were established, including Mb, Mo, Mh, Pm, and Tb. The assays successfully enabled the simultaneous detection of four pathogens in a single reaction, which may improve diagnostic efficiency compared to single-target PCR approaches.

The selected target genes (Mb ftsH, Mo P80, Mh pepS6, Pm lon, and Tb pks2) showed high specificity in both experimental validation and sequence-based analysis. In particular, given the high sequence similarity within the Mannheimia genus, the primers targeting Mh were further evaluated by sequence alignment, supporting their ability to distinguish closely related species. In addition, no cross-reactivity was observed with non-target bacterial and viral pathogens, indicating good analytical specificity of the assays.

The sensitivity analysis showed that the limit of detection ranged from 1.0 × 102 to 1.0 × 103 copies/μL, depending on the target pathogen. These values are comparable to those reported for conventional multiplex PCR assays (Jamil et al., 2025), suggesting that the established assays have adequate analytical sensitivity for laboratory detection. However, variation in detection limits among different targets may be related to differences in primer efficiency or amplification competition in the multiplex system.

Reproducibility testing demonstrated consistent amplification patterns across repeated experiments and different template batches, indicating stable assay performance under the tested conditions. In clinical sample detection, the multiplex PCR results were consistent with those obtained by single PCR, and identical numbers of positive samples were detected. In addition, a relatively high proportion of mixed infections was observed, which is consistent with the known epidemiological characteristics of respiratory diseases in cattle and sheep(Kebkiba et al., 2022; Calderón Bernal et al., 2023).

Despite these findings, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the clinical validation of the assays was limited, and no comparison with established reference methods was performed. Second, factors such as sample quality, nucleic acid extraction efficiency, and pathogen load may influence detection performance (Artika et al., 2020). Furthermore, potential interactions among primers in multiplex systems may affect amplification efficiency for certain targets.

In conclusion, this study established two multiplex PCR assays for the simultaneous detection of common bacterial respiratory pathogens in cattle and sheep and provided a preliminary evaluation of their performance. The assays demonstrated acceptable specificity, sensitivity, and reproducibility under the tested conditions, suggesting their potential utility as laboratory detection tools. Further studies with larger sample sizes and comprehensive validation are required to confirm their clinical applicability.
