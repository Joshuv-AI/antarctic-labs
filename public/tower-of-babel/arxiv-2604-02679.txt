RC-positivity, comparison theorems and prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors II
Jiaxuan Fan, Mingwei Wang, Xiaokui Yang, Shing-Tung Yau

Abstract
. In this paper, we solve the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor problem for Higgs bundles over compact complex manifolds. Let ( E , θ ) (E,\theta) be a Higgs bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold ( M , ω g ) (M,\omega_{g}) . Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric h 0 h_{0} on E E such that the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor Λ ω g ​ ( − 1 ​ R D h 0 ) \Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right) of the Higgs connection is positive definite. Then for any Hermitian positive definite tensor P ∈ Γ ⁡ ( M , E ∗ ⊗ E ¯ ∗ ) P\in\Gamma\left(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}\right) , there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metric h h on E E such that Λ ω g ​ ( − 1 ​ R D h ) = P . \Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\right)=P. We also establish quantitative Chern number inequalities for Higgs bundles.



1.
Introduction

This paper continues the investigation initiated in
[
WYY26+
]
, where we solved the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation for holomorphic vector bundles on compact Kähler manifolds, which is a vector bundle version of the Calabi-Yau theorem.
Let’s recall the set-up briefly. On a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
, it is well-known that the class of Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})
represents the first Chern class
c_{1}(M)
up to a factor of
2\pi
, i.e.,

\left[\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})\right]=2\pi c_{1}(M)\in H^{1,1}(M,{\mathbb{R}}).

The celebrated solution to the Calabi conjecture, proven by S.-T. Yau
[
Cal57
,
Yau78
]
, establishes the converse: for any closed real
(1,1)
form
\Omega
representing
2\pi c_{1}(M)
, there exists a unique Kähler metric
\omega\in[\omega_{g}]
such that

(1.1)

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)=\Omega.

The Calabi-Yau theorem has indeed been extended beyond Kähler manifolds to more general Hermitian manifolds. We refer to
[
FY08
,
TW10a
,
TW10b
,
STW17
]
and the references therein.

The classical Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem (
[
Don85
]
,
[
UY86
]
,
[
Don87
]
, see also
[
NS65
]
), establishes a deep equivalence on compact Kähler manifolds: a holomorphic vector bundle admits a Hermitian-Einstein metric if and only if it is polystable in the sense of Mumford-Takemoto. This result forged a critical link between differential geometry (the existence of special metrics solving a non-linear PDE) and algebraic geometry (a purely algebraic condition of stability). C. Simpson’s pivotal insight was to generalize this correspondence to the setting of Higgs bundles and demonstrated that the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau correspondence could be extended to this context.
It is well-known that the concept of Higgs bundles was first introduced by N. Hitchin in
[
Hit87
]
, in the context of his work on self-duality equations over Riemann surfaces. C. Simpson subsequently generalized Hitchin’s construction to higher-dimensional Kähler manifolds, and proved that a Higgs bundle over a compact Kähler manifold admits a Hermitian-Einstein metric compatible with the Higgs structure if and only if it is Higgs polystable. This work lays the foundational groundwork for the Hitchin-Simpson correspondence–a critical bridge linking Higgs bundles and local systems. It illuminates deep interdisciplinary connections spanning algebraic geometry, differential geometry, and representation theory. For comprehensive technical details and contextual background, we refer the reader to Simpson’s seminal works
[
Sim88
,
Sim92
,
Sim94a
,
Sim94b
]
and the extensive references cited therein.

Let
M
be a compact complex manifold and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. Let
\theta\in\Omega^{1,0}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
be an
E^{*}\otimes E
-valued smooth
(1,0)
-form.
(E,\theta)
is called a Higgs bundle if
{\overline{\partial}}\theta=0
. Suppose that
h
is a smooth Hermitian metric on
E
. The Hermitian adjoint of
\theta
with respect to
h
is denoted by
\theta^{\star}_{h}
. The Higgs connection
D^{h}
for the Hermitian Higgs bundle
(E,h,\theta)
is defined as

(1.2)

D^{h}=\nabla^{h}+\theta+\theta_{h}^{\star}

where
\nabla^{h}
is the Chern connection of
(E,h)
. The curvature tensor of the Higgs connection
D^{h}
is denoted by

(1.3)

R^{D^{h}}:=(D^{h})^{2}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E).

A Higgs bundle is called Hermitian-Einstein if there exist a constant
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that the equation

(1.4)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\right)=\lambda_{0}\cdot h

holds as an equality of tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
. It is proved in
[
Sim88
]
that (
1.4
) is solvable if and only if
(E,\theta)
is Higgs polystable over a compact Kähler manifold. Note that, if
\theta=0
, the equation (
1.4
) is the classical Hermitian-Einstein equation.
In what follows, we refer to the tensor
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\right)
as the
Hermitian-Yang-Mills-Higgs tensor
.

The main result of this paper is the following analogue of the Calabi-Yau theorem, addressing the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills-Higgs tensor problem for Higgs bundles on compact complex manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(E,\theta)
be a Higgs bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
such that the Hermitian-Yang-Mills-Higgs tensor of
(E,h_{0},\theta)
satisfies

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)>0.

Then for any Hermitian positive definite tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

(1.5)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\right)=P.

The positivity of
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}
can be weakened to the positivity of the integral of the minimum eigenvalue of

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}
over a Gauduchon manifold (Theorem
5.8
).
The following special case–where
\theta=0
–merits particular attention:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
such that the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor of the Chern connection satisfies

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{{h_{0}}}\right)>0.

Then for any Hermitian positive definite tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

(1.6)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=P.

The uniqueness part of Theorem
1.1
is established by the following comparison theorem for Hermitian-Yang-Mills-Higgs tensors:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(E,\theta)
be a Higgs bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that
h
and
h_{0}
are two smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
. If the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors of their Higgs connections satisfy
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}>0
and

(1.7)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}{}^{*})
, then
h\leq h_{0}
.

When
\theta=0
, we obtain:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that
h
and
h_{0}
are two smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
. If the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors of their Chern connections satisfy
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}>0
and

(1.8)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{{h_{0}}}

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}{}^{*})
, then
h\leq h_{0}
.

We adopt approaches similar to those in
[
WYY26+
]
. Let us clarify the core challenges and distinguishing aspects in proving Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
. First, the Higgs connection typically fails to be metric-compatible, which complicates the control of curvature terms. Second, both the torsion of the Hermitian manifold and the Higgs field
\theta
introduce analytical challenges, requiring refined uniform estimates to control their global behavior. Last but not least, the linearized equations deviate substantially from those in the Kähler case, primarily due to the lack of Kähler identities, requiring a more delicate treatment on the injectivity of the associated elliptic systems (e.g. Theorem
5.4
).

Motivated by the comparison result established in Theorem
1.3
, we demonstrate a quantitative form of Chern number inequalities, extending those known for Hermitian-Einstein metrics on stable Higgs bundles (
[
Sim88
]
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold and
(E,\theta)
be an integrable Higgs vector bundle of rank
r
over
M
. Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
such that the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor of the Higgs connection satisfies

(1.9)

a\cdot h_{0}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)\leq b\cdot h_{0},

for some constants
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then the following Chern number inequality holds

(1.10)

\int_{M}\left((r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)-2rc_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq\frac{r(r-1)\left(b-a\right)^{2}}{8\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Background materials

In this section, we provide a concise overview of foundational concepts related to Higgs bundles. For more exhaustive, in-depth treatments of the subject, we direct readers to the seminal works
[
Hit87
]
,
[
Sim88
]
,
[
Sim92
]
and
[
Sim94a
]
. Let
M
be a compact complex manifold and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. Let
\theta\in\Omega^{1,0}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
be an
E^{*}\otimes E
-valued smooth
(1,0)
-form. Recall that

(1)

If
{\overline{\partial}}\theta=0
, then
(E,\theta)
is called a
Higgs bundle
.

(2)

If
{\overline{\partial}}\theta=0
and
\theta\wedge\theta=0
,
(E,\theta)
is called an
integrable Higgs bundle
.

There is a natural operator
D^{\prime\prime}:\Gamma(M,E)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(M,E)

given by

(2.1)

D^{\prime\prime}v=\overline{{\partial}}v+\theta v.

For any smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
, let
\nabla^{h_{0}}
be the Chern connection of
(E,h_{0})
. There is a decomposition
\nabla^{h_{0}}={\partial}^{h_{0}}+{\overline{\partial}}
where
{\partial}^{h_{0}}
is the
(1,0)
-part of the Chern connection. The Hermitian adjoint of the Higgs field
\theta
with respect to
h_{0}
is denoted by
\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}
, i.e., for any
v,w\in\Gamma(M,E)
,

(2.2)

h_{0}(\theta v,w)=h_{0}\left(v,\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}w\right).

In local holomorphic coordinates
\{z^{i}\}
of
M
and local holomorphic basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E
, if we write
\theta=\theta_{i\alpha}^{\beta}dz^{i}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}
, then
\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}=(\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star})_{\overline{j}\gamma}^{\delta}d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes e^{\gamma}\otimes e_{\delta}\in\Omega^{0,1}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
where

(2.3)

\left(\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\right)_{\overline{j}\gamma}^{\delta}=h_{0,\gamma\overline{\beta}}h_{0}^{\delta\overline{\mu}}\;\overline{\theta_{j\mu}^{\beta}}.

Let
D^{\prime,h_{0}}:\Gamma(M,E)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(M,E)
be the operator defined by

(2.4)

D^{\prime,h_{0}}v={\partial}^{h_{0}}v+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}v.

We call the affine connection

(2.5)

D^{h_{0}}=D^{\prime,h_{0}}+D^{\prime\prime}

the Higgs connection on
(E,h_{0},\theta)
.
In general, it is not a metric compatible connection. The curvature tensor
R^{D^{h_{0}}}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E)
of this connection is defined by

(2.6)

R^{D^{h_{0}}}:=(D^{h_{0}})^{2}=(D^{\prime\prime})^{2}+D^{\prime,h_{0}}D^{\prime\prime}+D^{\prime\prime}D^{\prime,h_{0}}+(D^{\prime,h_{0}})^{2}.

The corresponding
Hermitian-Yang-Mills-Higgs
tensor is defined as

(2.7)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)=g^{i\overline{j}}\left(R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}\;e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

We also adopt the following notation whenever no ambiguity arises:

(2.8)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)=g^{i\overline{j}}h_{0,\beta\overline{\gamma}}\left(R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}\;e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e^{\gamma}}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}{}^{*}).

The formal adjoints of
D^{\prime,h_{0}}
and
D^{\prime\prime}
with respect to Hermitian metrics
g
and
h_{0}
are denoted by
(D^{\prime,h_{0}})^{*}
and
(D^{\prime\prime})^{*}
respectively. These operators can be extended to
\Omega^{p}(M,E)
in the standard manner.

Recall that for
P=P_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Omega^{p}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
and
Q=Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Omega^{q}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
, the product

(2.9)

P\cdot Q\in\Omega^{p+q}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

is defined as

(2.10)

P\cdot Q=(P_{\alpha}^{\beta}\wedge Q_{\beta}^{\gamma})\;e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\gamma}.

Let
h
be another smooth Hermitian metric on
E
. We set
H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. It is easy to see that

(2.11)

H=h_{\alpha\overline{\gamma}}h_{0}^{\beta\gamma}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}

Moreover, for any
s,t\in\Gamma(M,E)
,

(2.12)

h_{0}(Hs,t)=h(s,t).

There following linear algebraic calculation rules are given in
[
WYY26+
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian.

(2)

If
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian, for any
A,B\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
,

(2.13)

h_{0}(P\cdot A,B)=h_{0}(A,P\cdot B),\quad h_{0}(A\cdot P,B)=h_{0}(A,B\cdot P).

(3)

If
A,B\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
are
h_{0}
-Hermitian and
A\geq 0,B\geq 0
, then

(2.14)

h_{0}(A,B)\geq 0.

Moreover, for any section
C\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
, one has

(2.15)

h_{0}(A\cdot C\cdot B,C)\geq 0.

The Higgs connection
D^{h_{0}}
can be extended to
\Omega^{p}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
: for any
P\in\Omega^{p}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

(2.16)

D^{\prime\prime}P={\overline{\partial}}P+\theta(P),\quad D^{\prime,h_{0}}(P)={\partial}^{h_{0}}P+\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}(P).

The following computational formulas will be applied frequently throughout this work.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
P\in\Omega^{p}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
,

(2.17)

D^{\prime\prime}P={\overline{\partial}}P+(-1)^{p}P\cdot\theta-\theta\cdot P,\quad D^{\prime,h_{0}}P={\partial}^{h_{0}}P+(-1)^{p}P\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}-\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot P.

In particular,

(2.18)

\theta(P)=(-1)^{p}P\cdot\theta-\theta\cdot P,\quad\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}(P)=(-1)^{p}P\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}-\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot P.

Proof.

Suppose that
P=P_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}
where
P_{\alpha}^{\beta}
are
p
-forms. It is obvious that

\displaystyle D^{\prime\prime}P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\overline{\partial}}(P_{\alpha}^{\beta})e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}+(-1)^{p}P_{\alpha}^{\beta}\wedge\left(D^{\prime\prime}e^{\alpha}\right)\otimes e_{\beta}+(-1)^{p}P_{\alpha}^{\beta}\wedge e^{\alpha}\otimes\left(D^{\prime\prime}e_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\overline{\partial}}P-(-1)^{p}P_{\alpha}^{\beta}\wedge\theta_{i\gamma}^{\alpha}dz^{i}\otimes e^{\gamma}\otimes e_{\beta}+(-1)^{p}P_{\alpha}^{\beta}\wedge\theta_{i\beta}^{\gamma}dz^{i}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\gamma}.

It is clear that
(-1)^{p}P_{\alpha}^{\beta}\wedge\theta_{i\gamma}^{\alpha}dz^{i}\otimes e^{\gamma}\otimes e_{\beta}=\theta\cdot P
and
P_{\alpha}^{\beta}\wedge\theta_{i\beta}^{\gamma}dz^{i}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\gamma}=P\cdot\theta
. The second formula admits a similar proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

The following identity holds:

(2.19)

{\partial}^{h_{0}}\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}=0,\quad\theta_{h}^{\star}=H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}.

Moreover, for any
s\in\Gamma(M,E)
,

(2.20)

D^{\prime,h}(s)=H^{-1}\left(D^{\prime,h_{0}}(Hs)\right).

Proof.

Let
A\in\Omega^{1}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
and
A^{\star}
be the Hermitian adjoint of
A
with respect to
h_{0}
.
Since the Chern connection is metric compatible, for any
s,t\in\Gamma(M,E)
,

\displaystyle h_{0}\left(\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}A\right)s,t\right)=

\displaystyle h_{0}\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}\left(As\right),t\right)+h_{0}\left(A({\partial}^{h_{0}}s),t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial}h_{0}(As,t)+h_{0}(As,{\overline{\partial}}t)-h_{0}({\partial}^{h_{0}}s,A^{\star}t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial}h_{0}(s,A^{\star}t)+h_{0}\left(s,A^{\star}({\overline{\partial}}t)\right)-h_{0}({\partial}^{h_{0}}s,A^{\star}t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{0}\left(s,{\overline{\partial}}(A^{\star}t)\right)+h_{0}\left(s,A^{\star}({\overline{\partial}}t)\right)=h_{0}\left(s,\left({\overline{\partial}}A^{\star}\right)t\right).

Hence, we obtain the relation

(2.21)

({\partial}^{h_{0}}A)^{\star}={\overline{\partial}}A^{\star}.

In particular,
{\partial}^{h_{0}}\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}=({\overline{\partial}}\theta)^{\star}=0
. Moreover,
by definition (
2.3
),

\displaystyle H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}=

\displaystyle\overline{\theta_{i\delta}^{\gamma}}h_{0,\mu\overline{\gamma}}h_{0}^{\nu\overline{\delta}}H^{\mu}_{\alpha}(H^{-1})_{\nu}^{\beta}d\overline{z}^{i}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\theta_{i\delta}^{\gamma}}h_{0}^{\mu\overline{\eta}}h_{0,\mu\overline{\gamma}}h_{0}^{\nu\overline{\delta}}h_{0,\nu\overline{\tau}}h_{\alpha\overline{\eta}}h^{\beta\overline{\tau}}d\overline{z}^{i}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\theta_{i\delta}^{\gamma}}h_{\alpha\overline{\gamma}}h^{\beta\overline{\delta}}d\overline{z}^{i}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}=\theta_{h}^{\star}.

On the other hand, for any
s,t\in\Gamma(M,E)
,

(2.22)

\displaystyle{\partial}h_{0}(Hs,t)=

\displaystyle h_{0}({\partial}^{h_{0}}(Hs),t)+h_{0}(Hs,{\overline{\partial}}t),

(2.23)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{i}h(s,t)=

\displaystyle h({\partial}^{h}s,t)+h(s,{\overline{\partial}}t).

By using (
2.12
), one obtains

(2.24)

{\partial}_{i}^{h_{0}}(Hs)=H({\partial}_{i}^{h}s).

That is
{\partial}^{h_{0}}(Hs)=H({\partial}^{h}s)
. By (
2.19
), one has

(2.25)

\theta_{h}^{\star}(s)=H^{-1}\left(\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\left(Hs\right)\right).

The summation yields the identity given in (
2.20
).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
A,B\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
, the following identities hold:

(2.26)

D^{\prime,h_{0}}(A\cdot B)=D^{\prime,h_{0}}A\cdot B+A\cdot D^{\prime,h_{0}}B,\quad D^{\prime\prime}(A\cdot B)=D^{\prime\prime}A\cdot B+A\cdot D^{\prime\prime}B.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

(2.27)

{\partial}^{h_{0}}(A\cdot B)={\partial}^{h_{0}}A\cdot B+A\cdot{\partial}^{h_{0}}B.

By (
2.17
),

\displaystyle D^{\prime,h_{0}}A\cdot B+A\cdot D^{\prime,h_{0}}B=

\displaystyle{\partial}^{h_{0}}A\cdot B+A\cdot\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}\cdot B-\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}\cdot A\cdot B

\displaystyle+A\cdot{\partial}^{h_{0}}B+A\cdot B\cdot\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}-A\cdot\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}\cdot B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial}^{h_{0}}(A\cdot B)+A\cdot B\cdot\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}-\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}\cdot A\cdot B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{\prime,h_{0}}(A\cdot B).

The other identity can be established in a similar way.
∎

The following refined Bochner-Kodaira formulas hold on Higgs bundles:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
(E,h_{0},\theta)
be a Higgs bundle.
The following identities hold on
\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
:

(2.28)

(D^{\prime,h_{0}})^{*}+\tau^{*}=\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},D^{\prime\prime}],\quad(D^{\prime\prime})^{*}+\overline{\tau}^{*}=-\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},D^{\prime,h_{0}}],

where
\tau=[\Lambda,{\partial}\omega]
is the torsion tensor of
\omega_{g}
and
\tau^{*}
is the formal adjoint of
\tau
.

Proof.

The following Bochner-Kodaira formula is well-known on a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
(e.g.
[
LY12
, Lemma 4.3]
):

(2.29)

({\partial}^{h_{0}})^{*}+\tau^{*}=\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},{\overline{\partial}}].

Hence, we only need to show the following identity

(2.30)

(\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star})^{*}=\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},\theta]

Let
I_{i}
and
I_{\overline{j}}
be the contraction operators
i_{\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}z^{i}}}
and
i_{\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}}
. It is easy to compute that

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},\theta]=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}I_{i}dz^{k}\wedge\theta_{k}-g^{i\overline{j}}dz^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}I_{i}\theta_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\left(I_{i}dz^{k}\wedge+dz^{k}\wedge I_{i}\right)\theta_{k}=g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\theta_{i}.

Here we use the fact
I_{i}dz^{k}\wedge+dz^{k}\wedge I_{i}=\delta_{i}^{k}
in the last identity. On the other hand, for any
s\in\Omega^{p}(M,E)
and
t\in\Omega^{p+1}(M,E)
, one has

(2.31)

(\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}s,t)=\left(d\overline{z}^{i}\wedge\left(\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\right)_{\overline{i}}s,t\right)=\left(\left(\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\right)_{\overline{i}}s,g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}t\right)=(s,g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\theta_{i}t).

Hence, (
2.30
) holds. The other identity can be shown in a similar way.
∎

The following lemma explicates how the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors of the Chern connection differs from that of the Higgs connection:

Lemma 2.6
.

The following identity holds in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
:

(2.32)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}(\theta\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot\theta).

Proof.

Since
D^{\prime\prime}={\overline{\partial}}+\theta
and
D^{\prime,h_{0}}={\partial}^{h_{0}}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}
,
one has

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left({\overline{\partial}}{\partial}^{h_{0}}+{\partial}^{h_{0}}{\overline{\partial}}+{\overline{\partial}}\circ\theta+\theta\circ{\overline{\partial}}+{\partial}^{h_{0}}\circ\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\circ{\partial}^{h_{0}}+\theta\circ\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\circ\theta\right).

On the other hand, it is easy to see that

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left({\overline{\partial}}{\partial}^{h_{0}}+{\partial}^{h_{0}}{\overline{\partial}}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}.

Moreover, for any
s\in\Gamma(M,E)
,

(2.33)

({\overline{\partial}}\circ\theta+\theta\circ{\overline{\partial}})s=({\overline{\partial}}\theta)(s)=0,\quad\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}\circ\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\circ{\partial}^{h_{0}}\right)(s)=({\partial}^{h_{0}}\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star})(s)=0,

and

(2.34)

\left(\theta\circ\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\circ\theta\right)(s)=-(\theta\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot\theta)(s).

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian.

The following lemma describes the difference between the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors of two different Higgs connections.

Proposition 2.8
.

The following identity holds on
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
:

(2.35)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}D^{\prime\prime}\left(D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}\right).

Proof.

We establish in the proof of
[
WYY26+
, Proposition 3.6]
the following formula on Hermitian manifolds:

(2.36)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1}\right).

By (
2.32
) and (
2.19
), one has

(2.37)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}(\theta\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot\theta)

and

(2.38)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}(\theta\cdot\theta_{h}^{\star}+\theta_{h}^{\star}\cdot\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}(\theta\cdot H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}+H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}\cdot\theta).

By (
2.36
), (
2.37
) and (
2.38
), one obtains

(2.39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1}\right)\\
=&\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left(\theta\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot\theta-\theta\cdot H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}-H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}\cdot\theta\right).\\
\end{split}

On the other hand, by (
2.17
),

(2.40)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}D^{\prime\prime}\left(D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}D^{\prime\prime}\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}-(\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H-H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star})H^{-1}\right).

For degree reasons, one has

(2.41)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}-\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1}\right),

and by Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\theta\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}-\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left(\theta\cdot\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}-\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left(\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}-\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}\right)\cdot\theta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\bigg(\theta\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot\theta-\theta\cdot H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}-H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}\cdot\theta\bigg).

Since

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}D^{\prime\prime}\left(D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\bigg(\theta\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}+\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot\theta-\theta\cdot H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}-H\cdot\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\cdot H^{-1}\cdot\theta\bigg).

By comparing with (

). ∎

3.
Comparison theorems for Higgs bundles

In this section, we establish Theorem
1.3
, which provides a comparison result for Higgs bundles. As applications, we obtain the uniqueness of solutions to the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation for Higgs bundles.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(E,\theta)
be a Higgs bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that
h
and
h_{0}
are two smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
. If their Hermitian-Yang-Mills-Higgs tensors satisfy
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}>0
and

(3.1)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}{}^{*})
, then
h\leq h_{0}
.

As an application of Theorem
3.1
, one has

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
(E,\theta)
be a Higgs bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. If
h
and
h_{0}
are two smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
such that

(3.2)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}>0

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}{}^{*})
, then
h=h_{0}
.

As another application of Theorem
3.1
, one yields

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(E,\theta)
be a Higgs bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that
h
and
h_{0}
are two smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
. If there exists some
\lambda>0
such that
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}>0
and

(3.3)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\leq\lambda\cdot\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}{}^{*})
, then
h\leq\lambda h_{0}
.

Proof.

A straightforward computation shows that the
(1,1)
-component of the curvature tensor
R^{D^{h_{0}}}
is

(3.4)

\Theta^{1,1}:={\partial}^{h_{0}}{\overline{\partial}}+{\overline{\partial}}{\partial}^{h_{0}}+\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}\circ\theta+\theta\circ\theta_{h_{0}}^{\star}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E).

This
(1,1)
-component is clearly invariant under scaling transformations of
h_{0}
.
Let
R^{D^{\lambda h_{0}}}
be the curvature tensor of the Higgs bundle
(E,\lambda h_{0},\theta)
. Then

(3.5)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{\lambda h_{0}}}=\lambda\cdot\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}).

Hence, the result follows by Theorem
3.1
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
3.1
adopts a strategy analogous to that developed in the proof of
[
WYY26+
, Theorem 2]
. The primary differences and difficulties lie in two key aspects: the need to estimate the torsion of the Hermitian manifold, and the presence of the additional Higgs field
\theta
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\Omega=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
be the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor of
(E,h_{0},\theta)
. For simplicity, we define
\Phi\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
by the relation

(3.6)

\Phi:=\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\right)\cdot H,

where
H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
.
By (
2.35
), we obtain the identity

(3.7)

\Phi\cdot H^{-1}=\Omega+\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},D^{\prime\prime}](D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}).

This equation admits the following equivalent form:

(3.8)

\Omega\cdot(H^{-1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E})=\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},D^{\prime\prime}](D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1})+(\Omega-\Phi)\cdot H^{-1}.

We claim that

(3.9)

H\leq\operatorname{Id}_{E}

with respect to
h_{0}
which implies
h\leq h_{0}
.
To prove (
3.9
), we define
\kappa:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

(3.10)

\kappa(x):=\sup_{v\in E_{x},v\neq 0}\frac{h_{0}(Hv,v)}{h_{0}(v,v)}.

The maximal eigenvalue of
H
with respect to
h_{0}
is

(3.11)

\Lambda:=\sup_{x\in M}\kappa(x).

We argue by contradiction and assume that (
3.9
) does not hold. In this case, one has

(3.12)

\Lambda>1.

For any
m\geq 1
, pairing with
H^{m}
in (
3.8
) yields the following result:

(3.13)

\left(\Omega\cdot(H^{-1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E}),H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}=\left(\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},D^{\prime\prime}](D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}),H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}+\left((\Omega-\Phi)\cdot H^{-1},H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}.

The curvature condition asserts that
\Omega-\Phi\geq 0
with respect to
h_{0}
. By Lemma
2.1
,

(3.14)

\left((\Omega-\Phi)\cdot H^{-1},H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}=\left(\Omega-\Phi,H^{m-1}\right)_{h_{0}}\geq 0.

On the other hand, by Bochner-Kodaira formula (
2.28
) for Higgs bundles, one has

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},D^{\prime\prime}](D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}),H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left((D^{\prime,h_{0}})^{*}+\tau^{*}\right)(D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}),H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1},D^{\prime,h_{0}}H^{m}+\tau H^{m}\right)_{g,h_{0}}.

By Lemma
2.4
,
D^{\prime,h_{0}}H^{m}=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{m-1}H^{p}\cdot D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{m-1-p}
. By Lemma
2.1
again, one concludes

(3.15)

\left\langle D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1},D^{\prime,h_{0}}H^{m}\right\rangle_{g,h_{0}}=\left\langle D^{\prime,h_{0}}H,\sum\limits_{p=0}^{m-1}H^{p}\cdot D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{m-2-p}\right\rangle_{g,h_{0}}.

Moreover, they are real functions.
We claim that, for any
0\leq p\leq m-1
, the following pointwise estimate holds:

(3.16)

\left\langle D^{\prime,h_{0}}H,H^{p}\cdot D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{m-2-p}\right\rangle_{g,h_{0}}+\frac{|\tau|_{g}^{2}}{4m^{2}}\left\langle\mathrm{Id}_{E},H^{m}\right\rangle_{h_{0}}\geq-\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{Re}\left\langle D^{\prime,h_{0}}H,\tau H^{m-1}\right\rangle_{g,h_{0}}.

Indeed, since
H
is positive definite, we set

(3.17)

A_{p}:=H^{p/2}\cdot D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{(m-2-p)/2}+\frac{1}{2m}\tau H^{m/2}.

By Lemma
2.1
, one can see clearly that the estimate (
3.16
) is equivalent to the fact that

(3.18)

\langle A_{p},A_{p}\rangle_{h_{0}}\geq 0.

Summing (
3.16
) over
p=0
to
p=m-1
yields

(3.19)

\sum_{p=0}^{m-1}\left\langle D^{\prime,h_{0}}H,H^{p}\cdot D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{m-2-p}\right\rangle_{g,h_{0}}+\frac{|\tau|_{g}^{2}}{4m}\left\langle\mathrm{Id}_{E},H^{m}\right\rangle_{h_{0}}\geq-\mathrm{Re}\left\langle D^{\prime,h_{0}}H,\tau H^{m-1}\right\rangle_{g,h_{0}},

and so

(3.20)

\left\langle D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1},D^{\prime,h_{0}}H^{m}\right\rangle_{g,h_{0}}+\mathrm{Re}\left\langle D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1},\tau H^{m}\right\rangle_{g,h_{0}}\geq-\frac{|\tau|_{g}^{2}}{4m}\langle\mathrm{Id}_{E},H^{m}\rangle_{h_{0}}.

In particular,

(3.21)

\left(\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega_{g}},D^{\prime\prime}](D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}),H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}\geq-\frac{1}{4m}\|\tau\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(M,\omega_{g})}(\mathrm{Id}_{E},H^{m})_{h_{0}}.

Let
K
and
k_{0}
be the maximal and the minimal eigenvalues of
\Omega
with respect to
h_{0}
respectively, i.e.,

(3.22)

K=\sup_{x\in M}\sup_{v\in E_{x},v\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\Omega v\right\|_{h_{0}}}{\left\|v\right\|_{h_{0}}}>0,\quad k_{0}=\inf_{x\in M}\inf_{v\in E_{x},v\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\Omega v\right\|_{h_{0}}}{\left\|v\right\|_{h_{0}}}>0.

By (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and (
3.21
), one has

(3.23)

\left(\Omega,H^{m-1}-H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}+\frac{C}{m}(\mathrm{Id}_{E},H^{m})_{h_{0}}=\left(\Omega\cdot(H^{-1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E}),H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}+\frac{C}{m}(\mathrm{Id}_{E},H^{m})_{h_{0}}\geq 0,

where
C=\frac{1}{4}\|\tau\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(M,\omega_{g})}>0
is a constant.

We shall employ a different method to bound the left-hand side of (
3.23
) and reach a contradiction. The following facts hold.

(1)

For any point
x\in M
, one has

(3.24)

\left\langle\Omega,H^{m-1}-H^{m}\right\rangle_{h_{0}}\leq rK,

where
r=\mathrm{rank}(E)
is rank of
E
.

(2)

For any
x\in M
satisfying
\kappa(x)\geq 1
, one has

(3.25)

\left\langle\Omega,H^{m-1}-H^{m}\right\rangle_{h_{0}}\leq rK+k_{0}\left(\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\kappa(x)^{m}\right).

Indeed, let
f
be the function that

(3.26)

f(x)=x^{m-1}-x^{m},\quad x\in(0,\Lambda].

Let
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{r}\leq\Lambda
be the eigenvalues of
H
at point
x\in M
. The eigenvalues of
f(H)
are
\{f(\lambda_{k})\}_{k=1,2\cdots,r}
. It is clear that

f(x)\leq 1
for
x\in(0,\Lambda]
.
In particular,

f(H)\leq\mathrm{Id}_{E}
.
Therefore,

(3.27)

\left\langle\Omega,H^{m-1}-H^{m}\right\rangle_{h_{0}}\leq\left\langle\Omega,\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right\rangle_{h_{0}}\leq rK.

This establishes
(1)
. For
(2)
, if
x\in M
satisfies
\kappa(x)\geq 1
, then one has

(3.28)

f(\lambda_{r})=f(\kappa(x))=\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\kappa(x)^{m}.

Suppose that
v_{x}\in E_{x}
is an eigenvector with
h_{0}(v_{x},v_{x})=1
corresponding to
\lambda_{r}=\kappa(x)
, then

(3.29)

f(H)\leq\mathrm{Id}_{E}+\left(\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\kappa(x)^{m}\right)\;v_{x}^{*}\otimes v_{x}.

In particular, one has

\displaystyle\left\langle\Omega,H^{m-1}-H^{m}\right\rangle_{h_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle\Omega,\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right\rangle_{h_{0}}+\left(\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\kappa(x)^{m}\right)\left\langle\Omega,v_{x}^{*}\otimes v_{x}\right\rangle_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle rK+k_{0}\left(\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\kappa(x)^{m}\right).

This gives (
3.25
). By claims
(1)
and
(2)
, one obtains

(3.30)

\left(\Omega,H^{m-1}-H^{m}\right)_{h_{0}}\leq rK\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g})+k_{0}\int_{\kappa(x)\geq 1}\left(\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\kappa(x)^{m}\right)\cdot\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}.

and

(3.31)

\frac{C}{m}(\mathrm{Id}_{E},H^{m})_{h_{0}}=\frac{C}{m}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}H^{m}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\leq\frac{Cr}{m}\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g})+\frac{Cr}{m}\int_{\kappa(x)\geq 1}\kappa(x)^{m}\cdot\frac{\omega_{g}}{n!}.

By (
3.23
), (
3.30
) and (
3.31
), we obtain

(3.32)

\left(\frac{Cr}{m}+rK\right)\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g})+\int_{\kappa(x)\geq 1}\left(k_{0}\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\left(k_{0}-\frac{Cr}{m}\right)\kappa(x)^{m}\right)\cdot\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\geq 0.

Fix an arbitrary
\lambda\in(1,\Lambda)
, there exist
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
such that for any
m\geq m_{0}
,

(3.33)

\lambda\left(k_{0}-\frac{Cr}{m}\right)>k_{0}+\delta.

We set
\lambda_{1}=(\lambda+\Lambda)/2
.

(I)

If
\kappa(x)\geq\lambda_{1}>\lambda
and
m\geq m_{0}
, one has

(3.34)

k_{0}\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\left(k_{0}-\frac{Cr}{m}\right)\kappa(x)^{m}\leq\kappa(x)^{m-1}\left(k_{0}-\lambda\left(k_{0}-\frac{Cr}{m}\right)\right)<-\delta\lambda_{1}^{m-1}.

(II)

If
\lambda\leq\kappa(x)<\lambda_{1}
and
m\geq m_{0}
, one has

(3.35)

k_{0}\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\left(k_{0}-\frac{Cr}{m}\right)\kappa(x)^{m}\leq\kappa(x)^{m-1}\left(k_{0}-\lambda\left(k_{0}-\frac{Cr}{m}\right)\right)<0.

(III)

If
1\leq\kappa(x)<\lambda
and
m\geq m_{0}
, one has

(3.36)

k_{0}\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\left(k_{0}-\frac{Cr}{m}\right)\kappa(x)^{m}\leq k_{0}\lambda^{m-1}.

Therefore, we obtain

(3.37)

\int_{\kappa(x)\geq 1}\left(k_{0}\kappa(x)^{m-1}-\left(k_{0}-\frac{Cr}{m}\right)\kappa(x)^{m}\right)\cdot\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\leq-\delta\mu\lambda_{1}^{m-1}+k_{0}\lambda^{m-1}\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g}),

where
\mu>0
is the measure of the set
\{x\in M\ |\kappa(x)\geq\lambda_{1}\}
. By (
3.32
) and (
3.37
), one has

(3.38)

\left(\frac{Cr}{m}+rK+k_{0}\lambda^{m-1}\right)\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g})-\delta\mu\lambda_{1}^{m-1}\geq 0,

and so

(3.39)

\lambda_{1}^{-(m-1)}\left(\frac{Cr}{m}+rK+k_{0}\lambda^{m-1}\right)\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g})-\delta\mu\geq 0.

Note that the constants
\lambda_{1},\lambda,C,r,K,k_{0},\delta
and

are independent of

.
Moreover, since
\lambda_{1}>\lambda>1
, when

, by (
3.39
) we obtain
-\delta\mu\geq 0
. This is a contradiction. The proof is completed.
∎

be a Higgs bundle. Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric

on

such that

(4.2)

.
The formula (

(4.3)

\Phi-\Omega\cdot H=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}D^{\prime\prime}(D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1})\cdot H.

The main result of this section is following uniform

-estimate for

.

.

Remark 4.2
.

The proof of Theorem

follows a variant of the strategy employed in

, Theorem 4.1]
, with careful attention paid to estimating both the torsion of the Hermitian manifold and the Higgs fields.

We begin with the

-estimate which follows by Corollary

.

Proposition 4.3
.

If there exists a constant

(4.9)

It is clear that the map

(5.15)

is an isomorphism and its restriction

is also an isomorphism.
There is an induced map
\widetilde{G}:\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{0})\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{0})

(5.16)

\widetilde{G}=F\circ G\circ F^{-1}.

According to (

), the map

is given by

(5.17)

\widetilde{G}(H)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}D^{\prime\prime}\left(D^{\prime,h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}\right)\cdot H\right)+\Omega\cdot H,

where
\Omega=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{0})
. A straightforward computation shows that

and so the linearization of

at the point

is

(5.18)

\mathscr{L}(\Psi)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}D^{\prime\prime}D^{\prime,h_{0}}\Psi\right)+\Omega\cdot\Psi.

We can extend the definition of

to
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
\Psi\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

(5.19)

{\mathscr{L}}(\Psi^{\star})=({\mathscr{L}}(\Psi))^{\star}

Proof.

Since
{\mathscr{L}}
maps
\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{0})
to
\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{0})
, one has

(5.20)

{\mathscr{L}}(\Psi+\Psi^{\star})=\left({\mathscr{L}}(\Psi+\Psi^{\star})\right)^{\star},\quad{\mathscr{L}}\left(\sqrt{-1}(\Psi-\Psi^{\star})\right)={\mathscr{L}}\left(\sqrt{-1}(\Psi-\Psi^{\star})\right)^{\star}.

Or equivalently,

(5.21)

{\mathscr{L}}(\Psi)+{\mathscr{L}}(\Psi^{\star})=({\mathscr{L}}(\Psi))^{\star}+({\mathscr{L}}(\Psi^{\star}))^{\star},\quad{\mathscr{L}}(\Psi)-{\mathscr{L}}(\Psi^{\star})=({\mathscr{L}}(\Psi^{\star}))^{\star}-({\mathscr{L}}(\Psi))^{\star}.

Hence, (
5.19
) follows.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

The map

(5.23)

\displaystyle 0={\mathscr{L}}(\Psi)=

(5.26)

(5.27)

{\mathscr{L}}(\Psi^{\star})=0

By (
5.24

(5.30)

\Delta_{g}|\Psi|_{h_{0}}^{2}=|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\Psi|_{h_{0}}^{2}+|{\overline{\partial}}\Psi|_{h_{0}}^{2}+h_{0}(\Omega\cdot\Psi,\Psi)+h_{0}(\Omega\cdot\Psi^{\star},\Psi^{\star})+|\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}(\Psi)|_{h_{0}}^{2}+|\theta^{\star}_{h_{0}}(\Psi^{\star})|_{h_{0}}^{2}.

Theorem 5.6

6.
Quantitative Chern number inequalities for Higgs bundles

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.5
:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold and
(E,\theta)
be an integrable Higgs vector bundle of rank
r
over
M
. Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
such that the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor of the Higgs connection satisfies

(6.1)

a\cdot h_{0}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h_{0}}}\right)\leq b\cdot h_{0},

for some constants
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then the following Chern number inequality holds

(6.2)

\int_{M}\left((r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)-2rc_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq\frac{r(r-1)\left(b-a\right)^{2}}{8\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Proof.

Since
(E,\theta)
is integrable,

(6.3)

(D^{\prime\prime})^{2}={\overline{\partial}}\circ{\overline{\partial}}+{\overline{\partial}}\circ\theta+\theta\circ{\overline{\partial}}+\theta\circ\theta.

Since
{\overline{\partial}}\theta=0
and
\theta\wedge\theta=0
,
(D^{\prime\prime})^{2}=0
.
One can also show
(D^{\prime,h_{0}})^{2}=0
.
Therefore for any Hermitian metric
h
on
E
,

(6.4)

R^{D^{h}}=D^{\prime,h}D^{\prime\prime}+D^{\prime\prime}D^{\prime,h}={\partial}^{h}{\overline{\partial}}+{\overline{\partial}}{\partial}^{h}+\theta^{\star}_{h}\circ\theta+\theta\circ\theta_{h}^{\star}+{\partial}^{h}\theta+{\overline{\partial}}\theta_{h}^{\star}.

In particular,

(6.5)

\left(R^{D^{h}}\right)^{(2,0)}={\partial}^{h}\theta,\quad\left(R^{D^{h}}\right)^{(0,2)}={\overline{\partial}}\theta^{\star}_{h},\quad\left(R^{D^{h}}\right)^{(1,1)}={\partial}^{h}{\overline{\partial}}+{\overline{\partial}}{\partial}^{h}+\theta^{\star}_{h}\circ\theta+\theta\circ\theta_{h}^{\star}.

For the sake of simplicity, we denote
R=R^{D^{h}}
. By (
2.21
), we know

(6.6)

R^{(2,0)}=\left(R^{(0,2)}\right)^{\star}.

By the Chern-Weil theory, we have the following Chern forms representations:

(6.7)

c_{1}(E)=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}_{E}R,\quad 2c_{2}(E)=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}R\wedge\mathrm{tr}_{E}R-\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R\wedge R\right)\right).

We consider the
(1,1)
-component of
R
and define

(6.8)

\widetilde{c}_{1}(E):=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}_{E}R^{(1,1)},\quad 2\widetilde{c}_{2}(E):=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}R^{(1,1)}\wedge\mathrm{tr}_{E}R^{(1,1)}-\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R^{(1,1)}\wedge R^{(1,1)}\right)\right).

Moreover, one has the following identities for the
(2,2)
-form components

(6.9)

\left(c_{1}^{2}(E)-\widetilde{c}_{1}^{2}(E)\right)^{(2,2)}=-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R^{(2,0)}\right)\wedge\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R^{(0,2)}\right),

and

(6.10)

\left(c_{2}(E)-\widetilde{c}_{2}(E)\right)^{(2,2)}=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R^{(2,0)}\right)\wedge\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R^{(0,2)}\right)-\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R^{(2,0)}\wedge R^{(0,2)}\right)\right).

In particular,

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left((r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)-2rc_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}-\int_{M}\left((r-1)\widetilde{c}^{2}_{1}(E)-2r\widetilde{c}_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R^{(2,0)}\right)\wedge\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R^{(0,2)}\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}-\frac{r}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(R^{(2,0)}\wedge R^{(0,2)}\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}(\eta^{(2,0)}\wedge\eta^{(0,2)})\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2},

where

(6.11)

\eta^{(2,0)}=R^{(2,0)}-\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}R^{(2,0)}\right)\otimes\frac{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}{r},\quad\eta^{(0,2)}=R^{(0,2)}-\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}R^{(0,2)}\right)\otimes\frac{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}{r}.

By (
6.6
), one can see clearly that

(6.12)

\eta^{(2,0)}=\left(\eta^{(0,2)}\right)^{\star}.

In particular,

(6.13)

\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}(\eta^{(2,0)}\wedge\eta^{(0,2)})\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\geq 0.

As a direct consequence,

(6.14)

\int_{M}\left((r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)-2rc_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}-\int_{M}\left((r-1)\widetilde{c}^{2}_{1}(E)-2r\widetilde{c}_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq 0.

We use the following notations to denote curvature tensors of the Higgs connection:

R^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}=h^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}R^{(1,1)}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}},\quad R^{(2)}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=g^{i\overline{j}}R^{(1,1)}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad s_{h}=g^{i\overline{j}}R^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}.

The following expressions are well-known:

(6.15)

\int_{M}\widetilde{c}^{2}_{1}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s_{h}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n},

and

(6.16)

\int_{M}\widetilde{c}_{2}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s_{h}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|_{g}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}_{h}+|R^{(1,1)}|_{g,h}^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

We define
T\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
:

(6.17)

T_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}=R^{(1,1)}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}-\frac{1}{n}g_{i\overline{j}}R^{(2)}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-\frac{1}{r}R^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}+\frac{1}{nr}g_{i\overline{j}}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}s_{h}.

A straightforward computation shows that

(6.18)

|T|^{2}=|R^{(1,1)}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{r}\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{nr}s_{h}^{2}.

By using (
6.18
), one has

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(2r\widetilde{c}_{2}(E)-(r-1)\widetilde{c}^{2}_{1}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(r|T|^{2}+\frac{n-1}{n}\left(s_{h}^{2}-r\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}\right)\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

Let
\{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r}\}
be the eigenvalues of
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}=\sum R^{(2)}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}^{\beta}
with respect to
h
. Since
ah\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{D^{h}}\leq bh
, it is obvious that
a\leq\lambda_{k}\leq b
, and so

(6.19)

s_{h}^{2}-r\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}\right)^{2}-r\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}^{2}=-\sum_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\geq-\frac{r(r-1)}{2}(a-b)^{2}.

Finally, we establish the Chern number inequality.
∎
