Adjacent cross-sections of the commutant of Hilbert space operators
László Kérchy

Abstract
Applying the techniques resulting the existence of almost invariant half-spaces, similarity models T ^ \widehat{T} can be given for upper triangular operator-matrices T = [ A C 0 B ] T=\left[\begin{matrix}A&C\\ 0&B\end{matrix}\right] . The model T ^ \widehat{T} is also an operator-matrix, containing two diagonal operators in the general case [ K25 ] , and the unilateral shift S S together with a diagonal operator in the particular case when A A is similar to S S [ K26 ] . Well-chosen compressions of operators in the commutant { T ^ } ′ \{\widehat{T}\}^{\prime} form a linear manifold ℒ ^ \widehat{\cal L} satisfying the condition that every X ^ ∈ ℒ ^ \widehat{X}\in\widehat{\cal L} is transformed into Y ^ \widehat{Y} with rank ​ Y ^ ≤ 2 \hbox{\rm rank\,}\widehat{Y}\leq 2 by a canonical mapping. Furthermore, a cyclcity property of { T } ′ \{T\}^{\prime} yields transitivity of ℒ ^ \widehat{\cal L} . In [ K25 ] and [ K26 ] the 3-dimensional cross-sections of ℒ ^ \widehat{\cal L} have been investigated characterizing the canonical bases occurring in the corresponding subspaces of the matrix-algebra M 3 ​ [ ℂ ] M_{3}[\mathbb{C}] . In this paper new conditions are provided by studying matching of adjacent cross-sections. AMS Subject Classification (2020): 47A08, 47A15, 47A20, 47L05. Key words : invariant subspace, hyperinvariant subspace, compression of operator, unilateral shift, transitive subspace of matrices, singular matrices.



1
Introduction

Let
{\cal H}_{1}
and
{\cal H}_{2}
be complex Hilbert spaces with
\dim{\cal H}_{1}=\dim{\cal H}_{2}=\aleph_{0}
and let us consider a (bounded, linear) operator

T=\left[\begin{matrix}A&C\\
0&B\end{matrix}\right]\in{\cal L}({\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2}).

It is clear that the subspace
{\cal H}_{1}(\equiv{\cal H}_{1}\oplus\{0\})
is invariant for
T:\,T{\cal H}_{1}\subset{\cal H}_{1}
.
The commutant
\{T\}^{\prime}
of
T
consists of those operators
Q\in{\cal L}({\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2})
which commute with
T:\,QT=TQ
.
It will be assumed that
T
is not scalar multiple of the identity.
We are interested in the following basic question:

Question 1

Does
T
have a nontrivial hyperinvariant subspace, that is a subspace
{\cal M}\in\hbox{\rm Lat}({\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2})
, different from
\{0\}\oplus\{0\}
and
{\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2}
, which is invariant for every
Q\in\{T\}^{\prime}:\,Q{\cal M}\subset{\cal M}
?

The hyperinvariant subspaces of
T
form the complete lattice
\hbox{Hlat\,}T
.
So the question above asks whether
\hbox{Hlat\;}T\neq\left\{\{0\}\oplus\{0\},{\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2}\right\}
.
Notice that an affirmative answer for Question
1
yields that every nonscalar operator
A
has a proper hyperinvariant subspace.
Indeed, take
T=A\oplus A
and observe that
\hbox{Hlat\,}T=\left\{{\cal N}\oplus{\cal N}:{\cal N}\in\hbox{Hlat\,}A\right\}
.
In that way we would obtain positive answers for the hyperinvariant subspace problem (HSP), and in particular for the invariant subspace problem (ISP), which are arguably the most challenging open questions in operator theory.

A recent approach to (ISP) in an arbitrary (infinite dimensional) Banach space
{\cal X}
was initiated in
[
APTT
]
, and culminated in
[
Tc
]
proving that every operator
T
on
{\cal X}
has an
almost invariant half-space
.
We recall that
{\cal M}
is a half-space in
{\cal X}
if
{\cal M}
and
{\cal X}/{\cal M}
are infinite dimensional.
Furthermore,
{\cal M}
is almost invariant for
T
if
{\cal M}
is invariant for a finite rank perturbation of
T
.
The technique of this approach was refined in the Hilbert space setting in
[
JKP
]
, which led to a structure theorem in the paper
[
HP
]
.

Applying this method to our block-triangular operator

T\in{\cal L}({\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2})
more delicate similarity model
\widehat{T}\in{\cal L}({\cal H}^{(4)})
has been constructed for a translate of
T
in
[
K25
]
.
Here
{\cal H}
is an auxiliary complex Hilbert space with
\dim{\cal H}=\aleph_{0}
and
{\cal H}^{(4)}={\cal H}\oplus{\cal H}\oplus{\cal H}\oplus{\cal H}
.
The model
\widehat{T}
contains two diagonal components:
D,D_{*}
.
The central linear manifold
\widehat{\cal L}\subset{\cal L}({\cal H})
consists of the entries of the operators in
\{\widehat{T}\}^{\prime}
belonging to
{\cal L}(\hbox{dom\,}D,\hbox{dom\,}D_{*})
.
The crucial property of
\widehat{\cal L}
is that
\hbox{\rm rank\,}(\widehat{X}D-D_{*}\widehat{X})\leq 2
holds, for every
\widehat{X}\in\widehat{\cal L}
.
Furthermore, a cyclicity condition of
\{T\}^{\prime}
, which can be assumed in the quest for proper hyperinvariant subspaces, implies that
\widehat{\cal L}
is transitive.
An orthonormal basis
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is fixed in
{\cal H}
.
For every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the subspace
{\cal E}_{3,r}=\vee\{e_{r},e_{r+1},e_{r+2}\}
.
The cross-section
\widehat{\cal L}_{r}
consists of the compressions of the operators in
\widehat{\cal L}
to
{\cal E}_{3,r}
.
This subspace of
{\cal L}({\cal E}_{3,r})
inherits the rank-2 property and the transitivity of
\widehat{\cal L}
.
The algebra-isomorphism
M_{3,r}\colon{\cal L}({\cal E}_{3,r})\to M_{3}[\mathbb{C}]
assigns to each operator its matrix in the orthonormal basis
(e_{r},e_{r+1},e_{r+2})
.
The main result in
[
K25
]
characterizes the arising subspaces
M_{3,r}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r})
in terms of properties of a canonical basis.

In
[
K26
]
the case when
A
is similar to the unilateral shift
S
was investigated.
Now the similarity model
\widehat{T}\in{\cal L}({\cal H}^{(3)})
of a translate of
T
contains
S
and a diagonal operator
D_{*}
.
The linear manifold
\widehat{\cal L}\subset{\cal L}({\cal H})
consists of those entries of the operators in
\{\widehat{T}\}^{\prime}
which belong to
{\cal L}(\hbox{dom\,}S,\hbox{dom\,}D_{*})
.
For every
\widehat{X}\in\widehat{\cal L}
, we have
\hbox{\rm rank\,}(\widehat{X}S-D\widehat{X})\leq 1
, where
D
is a translate of
D_{*}
.
Furthermore, a cyclicity condition of
\{T\}^{\prime}
implies that
\widehat{\cal L}
is transitive.
For every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\widehat{\cal L}_{r}
be again the compression of
\widehat{\cal L}
to the subspace
{\cal E}_{3,r}=\vee\{e_{r},e_{r+1},e_{r+2}\}
.
The cross-section
\widehat{\cal L}_{r}
inherits the transitivity of
\widehat{\cal L}
, and
\hbox{\rm rank\,}\Gamma_{r}(X)\leq 2
is true for every
X\in\widehat{\cal L}_{r}
with a canonical mapping
\Gamma_{r}\in{\cal L}({\cal L}({\cal E}_{3,r}))
.
The main result of
[
K26
]
provides complete characterization of the arising subspaces
M_{3,r}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r})
in terms of conditions concerning the elements of a canonical basis.

Our aim in this paper is to give further conditions for these bases by studying matching of adjacent cross-sections.

In Section 2 compressions of operators are studied.
In Section 3 we examine the general case, while in Sections 4 and 5 we discuss the case when
A
is similar to
S
.
The conditions obtained simplify the featured canonical bases in the compressions, and establish connections between the parameters in the neighboring cross-sections.
If any of the resulting conditions fails, then
T
has a nontrivial hyperinvariant subspace; more precisely, there exists a nonzero vector
x\in{\cal H}_{1}
such that
\{T\}^{\prime}x
is not dense in
{\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2}
.
This is because the rules concerning ranks cannot be broken.

2
Compressions

Let
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis in the Hilbert space
{\cal H}
.
For any subspace
{\cal E}\in\hbox{\rm Lat\,}{\cal H}
, the
compression mapping

C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}}\colon{\cal L}({\cal H})\to{\cal L}({\cal E}),\;X\mapsto P_{\cal E}X\big|_{\cal E}

is a bounded linear transformation.
(Here
P_{\cal E}\in{\cal L}({\cal H})
stands for the orthogonal projection onto
{\cal E}
.)
Given any
{\cal F}\in\hbox{\rm Lat\,}{\cal E}
, we may consider the mapping

C_{{\cal E},{\cal F}}\colon{\cal L}({\cal E})\to{\cal L}({\cal F}),\;X\mapsto P_{\cal F}X\big|_{\cal F}.

The following simple statement establishes connection among these transformations.

Lemma 2

We have
\;C_{{\cal E},{\cal F}}\circ C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}}=C_{{\cal H},{\cal F}}
.

Proof.

For completeness we sketch the easy proof.
Given any
X\in{\cal L}({\cal H})
, let
Y=C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}}(X)\in{\cal L}({\cal E})
and
Z=C_{{\cal E},{\cal F}}(Y)\in{\cal L}({\cal F})
.
For every
x\in{\cal F}
we have
Zx=P_{\cal F}Yx=P_{\cal F}P_{\cal E}Xx=P_{\cal F}Xx
, and so
Z=C_{{\cal H},{\cal F}}(X)
.

\square

We are interested in diagonal subspaces spanned by the basis vectors.
For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let

{\cal E}_{3,r}:=\vee\{e_{r},e_{r+1},e_{r+2}\}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad{\cal F}_{2,r}:=\vee\{e_{r},e_{r+1}\}.

The
matricial mappings

M_{3,r}\colon{\cal L}({\cal E}_{3,r})\to M_{3}[\mathbb{C}]\quad\hbox{ and }\quad M_{2,r}\colon{\cal L}({\cal F}_{2,r})\to M_{2}[\mathbb{C}]

assign to each operator its matrix with respect to the given orthonormal basis.
These are algebra-isomorphisms.
For any
k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{3}(=\{1,2,3\})
, we consider the
partial mapping

P_{k,l}\colon M_{3}[\mathbb{C}]\to M_{2}[\mathbb{C}],\quad X=[\xi_{i,j}]_{3}\mapsto X_{k,l},

where
X_{k,l}
is obtained from
X
by deleting its
k
-th row and
l
-th column.
The following statement will play central role in the sequel.

Lemma 3

For every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

P_{1,1}\circ M_{3,r}\circ C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}_{3,r}}=M_{2,r+1}\circ C_{{\cal H},{\cal F}_{2,r+1}}=P_{3,3}\circ M_{3,r+1}\circ C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}_{3,r+1}}.

Proof.

Given any
\widetilde{X}\in{\cal L}({\cal H})
, let us consider its matrix
[\widetilde{X}]=[\xi_{i,j}]_{\infty}
, where

\xi_{i,j}=\langle e_{i},\widetilde{X}e_{j}\rangle\quad(i,j\in\mathbb{N}).

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\widetilde{X}_{r}:=C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}_{3,r}}(\widetilde{X})
and
X_{r}=[\xi_{i,j}^{r}]_{3}=M_{3,r}(\widetilde{X}_{r})
.
Since

\xi_{i,j}^{r}=\langle e_{r+i-1},\widetilde{X}_{r}e_{r+j-1}\rangle=\langle e_{r+i-1},\widetilde{X}e_{r+j-1}\rangle=\xi_{r+i-1,r+j-1}\quad(i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{3}),

it follows that

P_{1,1}(X_{r})=\left[\begin{matrix}\xi_{2,2}^{r}&\xi_{2,3}^{r}\\
\xi_{3,2}^{r}&\xi_{3,3}^{r}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\xi_{r+1,r+1}&\xi_{r+1,r+2}\\
\xi_{r+2,r+1}&\xi_{r+2,r+2}\end{matrix}\right]=:X_{0}.

On the other hand, let
\widetilde{Y}_{r+1}:=C_{{\cal H},{\cal F}_{2,r+1}}(\widetilde{X})
and
Y_{r+1}=[\eta_{i,j}^{r+1}]_{2}=M_{2,r+1}(\widetilde{Y}_{r+1})
.
Since
\eta_{i,j}^{r+1}=\xi_{r+i,r+j}\;(i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{2})
, it follows that
Y_{r+1}=X_{0}
.

Finally, take
\widetilde{X}_{r+1}=C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}_{3,r+1}}(\widetilde{X})
and
X_{r+1}=[\xi_{i,j}^{r+1}]_{3}=M_{3,r+1}(\widetilde{X}_{r+1})
, where
\xi_{i,j}^{r+1}=\xi_{r+i,r+j}\;(i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{3})
.
Then

P_{3,3}(X_{r+1})=\left[\begin{matrix}\xi_{1,1}^{r+1}&\xi_{1,2}^{r+1}\\
\xi_{2,1}^{r+1}&\xi_{2,2}^{r+1}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\xi_{r+1,r+1}&\xi_{r+1,r+2}\\
\xi_{r+2,r+1}&\xi_{r+2,r+2}\end{matrix}\right]=X_{0},

and the proof is completed.

\square

3
General operators

Let us consider an arbitrary nonscalar operator of the form

T=\left[\begin{matrix}A&C\\
0&B\end{matrix}\right]\in{\cal L}({\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2}).

In view of (HSP) we may assume that
T
and its adjoint
T^{*}
don’t have eigenvalues:
\sigma_{\rm p}(T)=\sigma_{\rm p}(T^{*})=\emptyset
.
Then, clearly
\sigma_{\rm p}(A)=\sigma_{\rm p}(B^{*})=\emptyset
hold too.
Let us select points on the boundaries of the spectra:
\lambda_{0}\in\partial\sigma(A)
and
\mu_{0}\in\partial\sigma(B)
.
These points will be approximated from the resolvent sets
\rho(A)=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(A)
and
\rho(B)=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(B)
, respectively.
Let
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a fixed orthonormal basis in an auxiliary Hilbert space
{\cal H}
.
It can be shown that
T-\mu_{0}I
is similar to an operator
\widehat{T}\in{\cal L}({\cal H}^{(4)})
of the form

\widehat{T}=\left[\begin{matrix}D&*&*&*\\
F&*&*&*\\
0&0&*&*\\
0&0&F_{*}&D_{*}\end{matrix}\right],

where

D=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\lambda_{k}-\mu_{0})e_{k}\otimes e_{k}\;\hbox{ and }\;\rho(A)\ni\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{0}\;\hbox{ sufficiently fast},

D_{*}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\mu_{k}-\mu_{0})e_{k}\otimes e_{k}\;\hbox{ and }\;\rho(B)\ni\mu_{k}\to\mu_{0}\;\hbox{ sufficiently fast};

moreover
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\cup\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
consists of distinct nonzero points, and
\hbox{\rm rank\,}F=\hbox{\rm rank\,}F_{*}=1.

It is clear that
\hbox{Hlat\,}T=\hbox{Hlat}(T-\mu_{0}I)\simeq\hbox{Hlat\,}\widehat{T}
.

Let us consider the linear manifold

\widehat{\cal L}=\left\{Q_{4,1}:[Q_{i,j}]_{4}\in\{\widehat{T}\}^{\prime}\right\}

in
{\cal L}({\cal H})
.
The following rank-2 condition of
\widehat{\cal L}
plays significant role in our study:

\hbox{\rm rank}(D_{*}\widehat{X}-\widehat{X}D)\leq 2\quad\hbox{ holds for every }\;\widehat{X}\in\widehat{\cal L}.

In view of (HSP) we can make also the following assumption:

(H)
\quad\quad\{T\}^{\prime}x
is dense in
{\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2}
, for every nonzero
x\in{\cal H}_{1}
.

(Notice that this situation occurs when the bilateral shift acts on the function space
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})=H^{2}\oplus H^{2}_{-}
.)
The assumption (H) readily implies that
\widehat{\cal L}
is transitive.

For brevity we introduce the notation:

b_{i}=\mu_{i}-\mu_{0},\;a_{j}=\lambda_{j}-\mu_{0},\;\widehat{c}_{i,j}=b_{i}-a_{j}\;(i,j\in\mathbb{N});\;\hbox{ and }\;\widehat{C}=[\widehat{c}_{i,j}]_{\infty}.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
{\cal E}_{3,r}
be as before, and let us consider the cross-section

\widehat{\cal L}_{r}=C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}_{3,r}}(\widehat{\cal L})\in\hbox{\rm Lat\,}{\cal L}({\cal E}_{3,r}).

It is immediate that
\widehat{\cal L}_{r}
is also transitive.
Furthermore the Schur-product
C^{r}\circ X
is a singular matrix, for every
X\in M_{3,r}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r})
.
Here
C^{r}=[c^{r}_{i,j}]_{3}
with
c^{r}_{i,j}=\widehat{c}_{r+i-1,r+j-1}\;(i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{3})
.

In order to provide characterization of these cross-sections we need additional concepts.
Let
C=[c_{i,j}]_{3}\in M_{3}[\mathbb{C}_{0}]
be arbitrary, where
\mathbb{C}_{0}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
We recall that
C_{k,l}=P_{k,l}(C)\;(k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{3})
.
For any
Y=[\eta_{i,j}]_{2}\in M_{2}[\mathbb{C}_{0}]
, let

\rho(Y):=\frac{\eta_{1,1}\eta_{2,2}}{\eta_{1,2}\eta_{2,1}}.

We shall say that a subspace
{\cal L}\in\hbox{\rm Lat\,}M_{3}[\mathbb{C}]
is
C
-normal
, if
\dim{\cal L}=5
and there exists a basis
(L_{1},L_{2},Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3})
in
{\cal L}
of the following form:

L_{1}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&0&0\\
\rho(C_{3,2})q_{2}&0&0\\
\rho(C_{2,2})q_{3}&0&0\end{matrix}\right],\quad L_{2}=\left[\begin{matrix}0&1&0\\
0&\rho(C_{3,1})q_{2}&0\\
0&\rho(C_{2,1})q_{3}&0\end{matrix}\right],

Q_{1}=\left[\begin{matrix}0&0&1\\
0&0&q_{2}\\
0&0&q_{3}\end{matrix}\right],\;Q_{2}=\left[\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
p_{1}&p_{2}&1\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right],\;Q_{3}=\left[\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\rho(C_{1,2})p_{1}&\rho(C_{1,1})p_{2}&1\end{matrix}\right],

with parameters
p_{1},p_{2},q_{2},q_{3}\in\mathbb{C}_{0}
.
The general element in
{\cal L}
is of the form

X=\left[\begin{matrix}z_{1}&z_{2}&w_{1}\\
z_{1}\rho(C_{3,2})q_{2}+w_{2}p_{1}&z_{2}\rho(C_{3,1})q_{2}+w_{2}p_{2}&w_{2}\\
z_{1}\rho(C_{2,2})q_{3}+w_{3}\rho(C_{1,2})p_{1}&z_{2}\rho(C_{2,1})q_{3}+w_{3}\rho(C_{1,1})p_{2}&w_{3}\end{matrix}\right],

where
(z_{1},z_{2},w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
runs through
\mathbb{C}^{5}
.

We know from
[
K25
]
that
the linear manifold
\widehat{\cal L}
is
\widehat{C}
-normal
, which means that the subspace
M_{3,r}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r})
is
C^{r}
-normal
(with parameters
p_{1,r},p_{2,r},q_{2,r},q_{3,r}
), for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
Applying Lemma
3
, we may obtain connection between the parameters of adjacent cross-sections.

Theorem 4

For every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(i)

\;q_{3,r}=q_{2,r}q_{2,r+1}\rho(C^{r+1}_{3,1}),

(ii)

\;p_{1,r+1}=p_{2,r}p_{2,r+1}\rho(C^{r}_{1,1}).

Proof.

In virtue of the general forms of the matrices in the subspaces
M_{3,r}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r})
and
M_{3,r+1}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r+1})
, we obtain by Lemma
3
that for every (
z_{1},z_{2},w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{5}
there exists corresponding
(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{1},\widetilde{w}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{5}
such that

\left[\begin{matrix}z_{2}\rho(C_{3,1}^{r})q_{2,r}+w_{2}p_{2,r}&w_{2}\\
z_{2}\rho(C_{2,1}^{r})q_{3,r}+w_{3}\rho(C_{1,1}^{r})p_{2,r}&w_{3}\end{matrix}\right]=\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad=\left[\begin{matrix}\widetilde{z}_{1}&\widetilde{z}_{2}\\
\widetilde{z}_{1}\rho(C_{3,2}^{r+1})q_{2,r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{2}p_{1,r+1}&\widetilde{z}_{2}\rho(C_{3,1}^{r+1})q_{2,r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{2}p_{2,r+1}\end{matrix}\right].

The entrywise equations are

(1)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}=z_{2}\rho(C_{3,1}^{r})q_{2,r}+w_{2}p_{2,r},

(2)

\;\widetilde{z}_{2}=w_{2},

(3)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}\rho(C_{3,2}^{r+1})q_{2,r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{2}p_{1,r+1}=z_{2}\rho(C_{2,1}^{r})q_{3,r}+w_{3}\rho(C_{1,1}^{r})p_{2,r},

(4)

\;\widetilde{z}_{2}\rho(C_{3,1}^{r+1})q_{2,r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{2}p_{2,r+1}=w_{3}
.

Eliminating
\widetilde{w}_{2}
, we may obtain from (3) and (4):

(5)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}\rho(C_{3,2}^{r+1})q_{2,r+1}p_{2,r+1}-\widetilde{z}_{2}\rho(C_{3,1}^{r+1})q_{2,r+1}p_{1,r+1}

\quad\quad\quad=z_{2}\rho(C_{2,1}^{r})q_{3,r}p_{2,r+1}+w_{3}\left(\rho(C_{1,1}^{r})p_{2,r}p_{2,r+1}-p_{1,r+1}\right)
.

Substituting (1) and (2) into (5) yields

\displaystyle z_{2}\left(\rho(C_{3,1}^{r})q_{2,r}\rho(C_{3,2}^{r+1})q_{2,r+1}p_{2,r+1}-\rho(C_{2,1}^{r})q_{3,r}p_{2,r+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+w_{2}\left(p_{2,r}\rho(C_{3,2}^{r+1})q_{2,r+1}p_{2,r+1}-\rho(C_{3,1}^{r+1})q_{2,r+1}p_{1,r+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-w_{3}\left(\rho(C_{1,1}^{r})p_{2,r}p_{2,r+1}-p_{1,r+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=0.

Since
(z_{2},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
can be freely chosen, the coefficients here must be zero, and so

(6)

\;q_{3,r}=q_{2,r}q_{2,r+1}\rho(C_{3,1}^{r})\rho(C_{3,2}^{r+1})\rho(C_{2,1}^{r})^{-1}
,

(7)

\;p_{1,r+1}=p_{2,r}p_{2,r+1}\rho(C_{3,2}^{r+1})\rho(C_{3,1}^{r+1})^{-1},

(8)

\;p_{1,r+1}=p_{2,r}p_{2,r+1}\rho(C_{1,1}^{r})
.

Direct computations show that

\rho(C_{3,1}^{r})\rho(C_{3,2}^{r+1})\rho(C_{2,1}^{r})^{-1}=\rho(C_{3,1}^{r+1}),\quad\rho(C_{3,2}^{r+1})\rho(C_{3,1}^{r+1})^{-1}=\rho(C_{1,1}^{r}).

Thus, (6)–(8) imply that (i) and (ii) hold.

\square

4
Operators containing a unilateral shift

Let us consider again a block-triangular operator

T=\left[\begin{matrix}A&C\\
0&B\end{matrix}\right]\in{\cal L}({\cal H}_{1}\oplus{\cal H}_{2}).

Let us fix an orthonormal basis
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in the auxiliary Hilbert space
{\cal H}
.
Let us assume that
A
is similar to the unilateral shift

S=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e_{k+1}\otimes e_{k}\in{\cal L}({\cal H}).

We assume also that
\sigma_{\rm p}(T^{*})=\emptyset
, whence
\sigma_{\rm p}(B^{*})=\emptyset
follows.
Select any point
b_{0}\in\partial\sigma(B)
.
Then
T-b_{0}I
will be similar to an operator
\widehat{T}\in{\cal L}({\cal H}^{(3)})
of the form

\widehat{T}=\left[\begin{matrix}S-b_{0}I&*&*\\
0&*&*\\
0&F_{*}&D_{*}\end{matrix}\right],

where

Given distinct numbers

Theorem 5

If
\widehat{\cal L}

-normal, then

(T2)

Proof.

Suppose that

Case 1.

Let us assume first that
M_{3,r+1}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r+1})

The general element in
M_{3,r}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r})
is of the form

\left[\begin{matrix}z_{1}&z_{2}&w_{1}\\

with

, and
(z_{1},z_{2},w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})

\mathbb{C}^{5}
.
Similarly, the general element in
M_{3,r+1}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r+1})
is of the form

\left[\begin{matrix}\widetilde{z}_{1}&\widetilde{z}_{2}&\widetilde{w}_{1}\\

\widetilde{z}_{1}y_{r+1}+\widetilde{z}_{2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(2)y_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{3}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(2)/b_{r+3}&\widetilde{z}_{2}y_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{3}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(2)&\widetilde{w}_{3}\end{matrix}\right],

with
x_{r+1},y_{r+1}\in\mathbb{C}_{0}
, and
(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{1},\widetilde{w}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{3})
runing through
\mathbb{C}^{5}
.
In view of Lemma
3
, we have

P_{1,1}(M_{3,r}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r}))=P_{3,3}(M_{3,r+1}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r+1})).

Hence, for every
(z_{2},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
, there exists
(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
such that

\left[\begin{matrix}z_{2}x_{r}+w_{2}b_{\Delta_{r}}(3)&w_{2}\\
z_{2}y_{r}+w_{3}b_{\Delta_{r}}(2)&w_{3}\end{matrix}\right]=\hskip 170.71652pt

\;\left[\begin{matrix}\widetilde{z}_{1}&\widetilde{z}_{2}\\
\widetilde{z}_{1}x_{r+1}+\widetilde{z}_{2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)x_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)/b_{r+2}&\widetilde{z}_{2}x_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)\end{matrix}\right].

The entrywise equations are

(1)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}=z_{2}x_{r}+w_{2}b_{\Delta_{r}}(3)
,

(2)

\;\widetilde{z}_{2}=w_{2}
,

(3)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}x_{r+1}+\widetilde{z}_{2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)x_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)/b_{r+2}=z_{2}y_{r}+w_{3}b_{\Delta_{r}}(2)
,

(4)

\;\widetilde{z}_{2}x_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)=w_{3}.

From (3) and (4) we may eliminate
\widetilde{w}_{2}
, obtaining

(5)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}b_{r+2}x_{r+1}+\widetilde{z}_{2}(b_{r+2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)-1)x_{r+1}=z_{2}b_{r+2}y_{r}+w_{3}(b_{r+2}b_{\Delta_{r}}(2)-1).

and

, it follows that

Hence (5) takes the form

(
5^{\prime}
)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}b_{r+2}x_{r+1}-\widetilde{z}_{2}\frac{b_{r+2}}{b_{r+1}}x_{r+1}=z_{2}b_{r+2}y_{r}-w_{3}\frac{b_{r+2}}{b_{r}}.

Simplifying by

and substituting (1) and (2) into

yields

(6)

\;(z_{2}x_{r}+w_{2}b_{\Delta_{r}}(3))x_{r+1}-w_{2}\frac{x_{r+1}}{b_{r+1}}=z_{2}y_{r}-w_{3}\frac{1}{b_{r}}
.

Multiplying (6) by

, after rearrangement we obtain

(
6^{\prime}
)

\;z_{2}b_{r}b_{r+1}(x_{r}x_{r+1}-y_{r})-w_{2}b_{r+1}x_{r+1}+w_{3}b_{r+1}=0.

Taking into account that

and

are nonzero, we infer that

cannot be chosen arbitrarily in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, what is a
contradiction
.

Case 2.
Suppose now that

The general element in

, and

, and so

.
On the other hand, we have

b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)x_{r+1}&x_{r+1}\end{matrix}\right],

must hold, which happens if and only if

Equivalently, we should have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left[\begin{matrix}q^{\prime}_{r+1}&q_{r+1}\\

(z_{2},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}

Summing up, the subspace

\dim\widetilde{\cal M}_{r+1}=3
, which happens exactly when

(\widetilde{X}(0,0,1,0),\;\widetilde{X}(0,0,0,1))

is linearly dependent, that is when

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left[\begin{matrix}q^{\prime}_{r+1}&q_{r+1}\\
b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)/b_{r+2}-q^{\prime}_{r+1}/x_{r+1}&b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)-q_{r+1}/x_{r+1}\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)\left(q^{\prime}_{r+1}-\frac{q_{r+1}}{b_{r+2}}\right).

We infer that

q^{\prime}_{r+1}=\frac{q_{r+1}}{b_{r+2}}

must hold.

Since
(\widetilde{X}(1,0,0,0),\,\widetilde{X}(0,1,0,0),\,\widetilde{X}(0,0,1,0))
is a basis, the general element in
\widetilde{\cal M}_{r+1}
is of the form

\widetilde{X}(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{1},0)=\left[\begin{matrix}\widetilde{z}_{1}&\widetilde{z}_{2}\\
\widetilde{z}_{1}x_{r+1}+\widetilde{z}_{2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)x_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{1}q_{r+1}/b_{r+2}&\widetilde{z}_{2}x_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{1}q_{r+1}\end{matrix}\right],

where
(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{1})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
is arbitrary.

Given any
(z_{2},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
, there exists
(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{1})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
such that

X(z_{2},0,w_{2},w_{3})=\widetilde{X}(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{1},0).

The entrywise equations are

(1)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}=z_{2}x_{r}+w_{2}(b_{\Delta_{r}}(3)-q_{r}/x_{r}),

(2)

\;\widetilde{z}_{2}=w_{2}
,

(3)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}x_{r+1}+\widetilde{z}_{2}b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3)x_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{1}q_{r+1}/b_{r+2}=z_{2}y_{r}+w_{3}(b_{\Delta_{r}}(2)-q_{r}/x_{r})
,

(4)

\;\widetilde{z}_{2}x_{r+1}+\widetilde{w}_{1}q_{r+1}=w_{3}
.

From (3) and (4) we may derive

(5)

\;\widetilde{z}_{1}b_{r}b_{r+1}x_{r+1}-\widetilde{z}_{2}b_{r}x_{r+1}=z_{2}b_{r}b_{r+1}y_{r}-w_{3}(b_{r+1}+b_{r}b_{r+1}q_{r}/x_{r}).

Substituting (1) and (2) into (5) we may deduce

(6)

\;z_{2}b_{r}b_{r+1}(x_{r}x_{r+1}-y_{r})-w_{2}x_{r+1}b_{r+1}(1+b_{r}q_{r}/x_{r})+w_{3}b_{r+1}(1+b_{r}q_{r}/x_{r})=0
.

However (6) can hold for every
(z_{2},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
only if all coefficients are zero:

x_{r+1}=\frac{y_{r}}{x_{r}}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\frac{q_{r}}{x_{r}}=-\frac{1}{b_{r}}.

Straightforward computations show that under the conditons

\frac{q_{r}}{x_{r}}=-\frac{1}{b_{r}}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad q^{\prime}_{r}=\frac{q_{r}}{b_{r+1}},

the
\Delta_{r}
-normal subspace
M_{3,r}(\widehat{\cal L}_{r})
is actually strongly
\Delta_{r}
-normal with parameters
x_{r},y_{r},q_{r}\in\mathbb{C}_{0}
.

\square
