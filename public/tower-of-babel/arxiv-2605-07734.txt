Characterizing gapped phases by smeared boundary conformal field theories: Duality in unusual ordering with spontaneously broken generalized symmetries
Yoshiki Fukusumi, Shuma Nakashiba

Abstract
We study the classification of the gapped phases or massive renormalization group (RG) flows dual to the massless RG flows under changing the sign of the coupling constants. Whereas our formalism is based on combining Higgs- or Nambu-Goldstone-type arguments with Cardy’s smeared boundary conformal field theories (SBCFTs), several puzzling structures arise. More specifically, the established Higgs or Nambu-Goldstone type arguments on the duality imply that the natural basis for the gapped states should be constructed from a set of smeared Ishibashi states, which are unphysical in boundary critical phenomena. Hence, the module of the gapped phases can be outside of that of boundary critical phenomena, whereas one can still calculate characterizing quantities by applying SBCFTs to the models. For example, we demonstrate that the massive RG flow dual to the massless RG flow from the tricritical Ising model to the Ising model, one of the simplest massless RG flows, has this unusual structure. This can be regarded as a quantum field-theoretic analogue of order-disorder coexistence in lattice models. More generally, the resultant gapped phases usually spontaneously break non-group-like symmetry (or noninvertible symmetry). Our work provides systematic quantum field theoretic descriptions of such unusual phases with spontaneous symmetry breaking of non-group-like (or noninvertible) symmetries.

, they can transform each other by
\Phi_{\gamma}
. In other words, the action of topological symmetry operators or the NG modes in this dual RG
M_{\text{H/NG}}
is intrinsically different from that of usual massive RG
M_{\text{BCP}}
.

We note implications of these results on the smeared Cardy and Ishibashi states by using some basic idea of operator-state correspondence in QFT and its realization in lattice models. In a familiar class of QFTs, there exists the correspondence between the bulk operator
\Phi_{\alpha}
and quantum states
|\alpha,0,\overline{0}\rangle
. For example, in the radial quantization, by modifying the space time coordinate in plane to a infinitely long cylinder, one can obtain the relation
\Phi_{\alpha}(t=-\infty)|v\rangle=|\alpha,0,\overline{0}\rangle
by identifying the excited state as a consequence of operator insertion at the time
t=-\infty
, where
|v\rangle
is the CFT vacuum and the label
0,\overline{0}
represent the state is a primary state but not a descendant state. One can see the corresponding discussion commonly in reviews and textbooks
Ginsparg:1988ui
;
DiFrancesco:1997nk
. In other words, this QFT structure implies the existence of the many-body excitation operator
U_{\alpha}(=\Phi_{\alpha}(t=-\infty))
in lattice models (or Wilson loop operator in QFTs). For example, in the Heisenberg chain, one can identify a set of
U_{\alpha}
as the LSM twist operator
Lieb:1961fr
;
Schultz:1964fv
, and its ground state expectation value is called polarization
Resta:1994zz
;
PhysRevLett.82.370
;
Aligia_1999
;
Aligia_2005
or polarization amplitude
Kobayashi:2018mnk
;
Furuya_2019
. By taking the logarithm of polarization amplitude, the quantity produces Berry curvature. We assume the same reasoning should be true for UV CFT and corresponding many-body systems at criticality, and we propose the following correspondence,

\{\Phi_{\alpha}=U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbf{A}}

(38)

This is the nonabelian anyonic generalization of the LSM operator
Lieb:1961fr
;
Schultz:1964fv
, and this has been proposed in the works by the first author
Fukusumi:2024ejk
;
Fukusumi:2025fir
. However, this structure itself has been assumed more or less intuitively in theoretical condensed matter, because this is just an expression of the operator-state correspondence in QFTs.
By using this correspondence, one can straightforwardly obtain a general prediction of the behavior of the polarization amplitude characterizing gapped phases, including order-disorder coexisting phases
OBrien:2017wmx
.

It should be stressed that the above relation is robust against RG in the Hamiltonian formalism of QFT
Cardy:1986ie
;
Furuya_2019
. Hence, only by establishing the above relation in the UV theory, these results by Cardy directly provide a generalization of polarization amplitudes both in existing massive RG to smeared Cardy states and the dual massive RG to smeared Ishibashi states. Below, we summarize several observations in the dual Massive flow or usual massive RG flow. In usual massive RG flow described by
M_{\text{BCP}}
, the phenomenology is follows:

•

The massive RG approximated by smeared Cardy states is characterized by AGQD.

•

The invertiblity or non-invertibility of the UV symmetry operator
\{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbf{A}}
also governs the condensation rule for the LSM operator
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbf{A}}
.

•

\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\mathbf{A}}
cannot transfer a smeared Cardy state to another smeared Cardy state.

•

\{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha_{\text{ub}}}\}_{\alpha_{\text{ub}}\in\mathbf{A}_{\text{ub}}}
transfer a smeared Cardy state to another, and
\mathbf{A}_{\text{ub}}
can be interpreted as NG modes.

In parallel with the above, the massive RG
M_{\text{H/NG}}
with the Higgs/NG duality can be summarized as follows:

•

The dual massive RG approximated by smeared Ishibashi states is characterized by fusion rules
N
and (usual) quantum dimensions.

•

The algebraic data of fusion rule or OPE coefficient of the bulk field
\{\Phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbf{A}}
survives under the RG flows and governs the condensation rules.

•

\{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha_{\text{ub}}}\}_{\alpha_{\text{ub}}\in\mathbf{A}_{\text{ub}}}
cannot transfer a smeared Ishibashi state to another smeared Ishibashi state.

•

LSM operator
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbf{A}}
transfers a smeared Ishibashi state to other Ishibashi states following the fusion rule, and LSM operators
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbf{A}}
create gapless modes.

Hence, the role of the symmetry operator, LSM operators behave much differently depending on the existence or nonexistence of the duality. To our knowledge, this phenomenology in SBCFT has rarely been studied.

IV
Dual massive RG flow and the resultant modules

In this section, we study examples of the dual massive RG flows, where the gapped phase/module appearing in the IR cannot be expressed as smeared Cardy states.
As discussed in the previous sections, such examples are those dual to the massless RG flow, or those that have the Higgs/NG duality.
Still, the Hilbert space or module

of the consequent gapped phase can be spanned by the smeared Ishibashi states. In other words, we cannot form Cardy states by any linear combinations of the elements in that module.

In the following, we study the massless RG flow with some unbroken symmetry

will appear as the degenerate ground states or the module appearing after the (dual) massive RG flow. We identify the resultant module as

and this is the main interest of the subsequent discussion.

V
Conclusion

In this manuscript, for a massive RG with the Higgs/NG duality, we have demonstrated the existence of the order which cannot be approximated by the associated boundary critical phenomena with the example of the flow
\mathbf{M}(4,5)\to\mathbf{M}(3,4)
. The combination of the existing Nambu-Goldstone-type arguments and SBCFT is the fundamental. The resultant module with
\mathbb{C}
-linearity is out of the scope of Cardy’s boundary conditions. Moreover, one can directly observe the vanishing or non-vanishing of the expectation value of LSM operators by applying the calculations in
Cardy:2017ufe
. This can be thought of as a non-group-like generalization of polarization amplitude
Resta:1994zz
;
PhysRevLett.82.370
;
Aligia_1999
;
Aligia_2005
;
Kobayashi:2018mnk
and this will provide a new class of LSM type arguments for non-group like (or non-abelian) anyonic excitations. It should be stressed that the vanishing or non-vanishing of the corresponding non-group-like excitations is governed by the fusion coefficients as has been pointed out by Cardy
Cardy:2017ufe
, and the Higgs/NG duality can be thought of as a consequence of such unbroken or preserved non-group-like structure in the RGs.

As a concluding remark, we note several points and problems for further studies. First, the
\mathbb{C}
-linearity in CFTs or TQFTs, especially in massless RG between CFTs or domain walls in TQFTs, has already been studied in the references
Zhao:2023wtg
;
Fukusumi:2025clr
;
Fukusumi:2025xrj
;
Fukusumi:2025fir
;
Fukusumi:2025fvb
. The results in the present manuscript can be thought of as a reinterpretation of the corresponding structures in the dual massive RG flows, based on the Higgs/NG duality. Hence, we expect the results in the present manuscript provide an alternative consistency test of the
\mathbb{C}
-linear formulations. We have mainly discussed two-dimensional CFTs and their RG flow, but the methods in the present manuscript are applicable to higher-dimensional systems. We note a few references for higher-dimensional systems
Kong:2014qka
;
Johnson-Freyd:2020usu
;
Kong:2024ykr
;
Antinucci:2025fjp
.

Second, in the present manuscript, we have assumed locality by restricting our attention to diagonal
A
-type models. Hence, the non-diagonal models are outside of the scope of the present manuscript. However, we remark that by studying gauging or orbifolding operations on the underlying models
Vafa:1986wx
;
Dixon:1986jc
;
Dixon:1986qv
;
Dijkgraaf:1989hb
, one can obtain a series of corresponding RG flows for non-diagonal models. For readers interested in this research direction, we note recent works by the first author
Fukusumi:2024ejk
;
Fukusumi:2025ljx
.

Finally, we point out the subtleties in the connection between a TQFT and a BCFT. In studying the entanglement surface, the Ishibashi states appear naturally in a TQFT
Kitaev:2005dm
;
Qi_2012
;
Das:2015oha
;
Nakayama:2015mva
. On the other hand, if one studies the gluing operation in TQFTs or massive RG without the Higgs-NG duality, the Cardy states should appear
Haldane:1995xgi
;
Han:2017hdv
;
Cardy:2017ufe
;
Ares:2020uwy
;
Li:2022drc
. In other word, which is more natural to connect TQFT to Ishibashi states and/or Cardy states is unclear at the present stage. This problem will be fundamental when studying symmetry enforced gaplessness
Wang:2014lca
;
Wang:2016gqj
;
Sodemann:2016mib
;
Wang:2017txt
;
Chatterjee:2024gje
;
Pace:2024oys
;
Kim:2025tzc
;
Pace:2025rfu
, because the definition of the TQFT plays the central role in
Ando:2026ffy
. It is possible to assume that both Cardy states and Ishibashi states can correspond to a module of TQFTs, but we are not sure whether this perspective is standard or not. The correspondence between TQFTs and SBCFTs has been in development, and we leave this as an open problem.
