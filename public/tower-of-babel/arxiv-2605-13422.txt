Pion parton distribution functions and pion-nucleus induced J / ψ J/\psi production in extended light-front holographic QCD
Jiangshan Lan, Satvir Kaur, Chandan Mondal

Abstract
We determine the pion parton distribution functions (PDFs) from its light-front wave functions, obtained using the holographic Schrödinger equation of light-front chiral QCD combined with the ’t Hooft equation in (1+1)-dimensional QCD at large N c N_{c} . We analyze the large- x x behavior of the valence PDF, ∼ ( 1 − x ) β v eff \sim(1-x)^{\beta^{\rm eff}_{v}} , finding overall consistency with global analyses. These pion PDFs, together with nuclear PDFs, are then used to compute the differential cross sections up to next-to-leading order for inclusive J / ψ J/\psi production in pion–nucleus collisions, which show good agreement with experimental data across different energies and nuclear targets.

2
Light-Front Holographic Framework with Longitudinal Dynamics

LF holographic QCD provides an effective framework to describe hadronic structure by exploiting a correspondence between strongly coupled QCD dynamics and a higher-dimensional gravitational theory in anti-de Sitter (AdS) space
[
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
. In this approach, the bound-state problem in physical spacetime is mapped onto a semiclassical description in terms of a single-variable LF Schrödinger equation, allowing for an analytic treatment of confinement.

For a mesonic system, the LFWFs
\Psi(x,\mathbf{b}_{\perp})
depends on the longitudinal momentum fraction
x
carried by the quark and the transverse separation
\mathbf{b}_{\perp}
between the quark and antiquark. A key step in establishing the holographic connection is the introduction of the invariant transverse variable
[
55
]

\zeta=\sqrt{x(1-x)}\,b_{\perp},

(1)

which provides a measure of the transverse separation and is identified with the fifth dimension of AdS space. This allows the wave function to be expressed in a factorized form separating transverse and longitudinal dynamics,

\Psi(x,\zeta,\phi)=\frac{\phi(\zeta)}{\sqrt{2\pi\zeta}}\,e^{iL\phi}\,X(x),

(2)

where
\phi(\zeta)
and
X(x)
encode the transverse and longitudinal structure, respectively, with
X(x)=\sqrt{x(1-x)}\chi(x)
.

Within this framework, the transverse dynamics is governed by an effective LF Schrödinger equation
[
55
]
,

\left[-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}\zeta^{2}}+\frac{4L^{2}-1}{4\zeta^{2}}+U_{\perp}(\zeta)\right]\phi(\zeta)=M^{2}_{\perp}\,\phi(\zeta),

(3)

where
U_{\perp}(\zeta)
represents the confining potential. A quadratic form of the potential
[
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
,

U_{\perp}(\zeta)=\kappa^{4}\zeta^{2}+2\kappa^{2}(J-1),

(4)

leads to a harmonic oscillator spectrum, with the mass scale
\kappa
setting the strength of confinement and
J=L+S
being the total angular momentum of the meson. This potential thus determines the hadronic spectrum.
This formulation successfully reproduces key features of hadron spectroscopy, including linear Regge trajectories and the emergence of a massless pion in the chiral limit. With Eq. (
4
),
Eq. (
3
) can be solved analytically, yielding the meson masses and eigenstates within the chiral limit:

M_{\perp}^{2}(n_{\perp},J,L)=4\kappa^{2}\left(n_{\perp}+\frac{J+L}{2}\right),

(5)

and

\phi_{n_{\perp}L}(\zeta)\propto\zeta^{1/2+L}\exp\left(\frac{-\kappa^{2}\zeta^{2}}{2}\right)L_{n_{\perp}}^{L}(\kappa^{2}\zeta^{2}),

(6)

where
n_{\perp}
is the transverse quantum number,
L_{n_{\perp}}^{L}
represents the associated Laguerre polynomials.

For the ground-state pion (
n_{\perp}=L=0
), the solution of the transverse LF Schrödinger equation leads to a Gaussian form for the wave function. In particular, the holographic LFWF can be written as

\Psi(x,\zeta)\propto\sqrt{x(1-x)}\chi(x)\,\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa^{2}\zeta^{2}}{2}\right),

(7)

which exhibits confinement in the transverse direction and vanishes at the endpoints
x\to 0,1
.

While the transverse structure is dynamically determined in this approach, the longitudinal dynamics is not treated dynamically in the original formulation; instead the mode
\chi(x)=1

[
62
,
63
]
. To achieve a more complete description of hadronic wave functions, the longitudinal mode can be constrained using the ’t Hooft equation, derived in
(1+1)
-dimensional QCD in the large-
N_{c}
limit
[
56
,
64
,
57
]
. This equation incorporates the effects of confinement in the longitudinal direction and quark masses, providing a dynamical basis for the function
\chi(x)
.

The combination of LF holography with the ’t Hooft equation thus yields a unified framework in which both transverse and longitudinal dynamics are consistently treated
[
57
,
58
,
41
,
59
,
60
,
42
,
61
]
. This enables the construction of realistic LFWFs that can be applied to a wide range of hadronic observables, including parton distribution functions and exclusive processes.

To incorporate dynamical information in the longitudinal direction, we employ the ’t Hooft equation, which arises in
(1+1)
-dimensional QCD in the large-
N_{c}
limit. In this framework, the meson bound-state problem reduces to an effective integral equation for the longitudinal wave function
\chi(x)

[
56
]
, given by

\left(\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{1-x}\right)\chi(x)+U_{\parallel}(x)\,\chi(x)=M_{\parallel}^{2}\,\chi(x),

(8)

where the interaction term is defined as

U_{\parallel}(x)\,\chi(x)=\frac{g^{2}}{\pi}\,\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{1}dy\,\frac{\chi(x)-\chi(y)}{(x-y)^{2}}.

(9)

Here,
m_{q}
and
m_{\bar{q}}
denote the quark and antiquark masses,
g
sets the strength of longitudinal confinement, and
\mathcal{P}
indicates the Cauchy principal value prescription required to regulate the singular kernel.

In contrast to the transverse LF Schrödinger equation, the ’t Hooft equation does not admit closed-form analytical solutions and must be solved numerically
[
64
]
. We determine its eigenvalues and eigenfunctions using a matrix diagonalization method.

The resulting longitudinal wave functions exhibit a nontrivial structure governed by the interplay of quark masses and confinement. Together with Eq. (
7
), they enable the construction of the complete LFWFs using LF holography supplemented by the ’t Hooft approach. The resulting wave function satisfies the normalization condition

\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}^{2}\mathbf{b}_{\perp}\,\left|\Psi(x,\zeta)\right|^{2}=1,

(10)

which is equivalently expressed in momentum space as

\int\frac{{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}}{16\pi^{3}}\,\left|\Psi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right|^{2}=1.

(11)

3
Numerical results

We fix the model parameters by fitting the mass spectrum of the pion family
[
41
]
. In particular, we use
m_{u/d}=0.046~\text{GeV}
for the light quark mass,
\kappa=0.523~\text{GeV}
for the transverse confinement scale, and
g=0.109~\text{GeV}
for the longitudinal confinement scale
[
41
]
.

3.1
Pion PDFs

We compute the pion PDFs starting from the LFWFs in momentum space. The valence quark distribution at the model scale is defined as

f_{v}^{\pi}(x,\mu_{0}^{2})=\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}}{16\pi^{3}}\,\left|\Psi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right|^{2},

(12)

which follows from the normalization of the LFWFs. At the initial scale
\mu_{0}^{2}\approx 0.305~\text{GeV}^{2}
, the pion is described purely in terms of its valence degrees of freedom, with no explicit gluon or sea quark contributions.

The full set of PDFs at higher scales is obtained through QCD evolution. We evolve the distributions from the initial scale to larger momentum scales using the next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) Dokshitzer-Gribov-Lipatov-Altarelli-Parisi (DGLAP) evolution equations
[
65
,
66
,
67
]
, solved numerically
[
68
]
. In this framework, gluons and sea quarks are generated dynamically through perturbative splitting processes. In particular, gluons arise from quark radiation processes
q\rightarrow qg
, while sea quarks are produced through gluon splitting
g\rightarrow q\bar{q}
. These coupled evolution equations redistribute momentum among partons, leading to a buildup of gluon and sea quark densities as the scale increases. Consequently, the gluon and sea distributions become significant at small
x
, while the valence distribution is gradually shifted toward lower momentum fractions.

The initial scale is fixed by matching the first moment of the valence distribution to values extracted from global analyses, namely
2\langle x\rangle_{v}=0.48\pm 0.01
at
\mu^{2}=5~\text{GeV}^{2}

[
10
]
. The evolved PDFs satisfy the momentum sum rule,

\sum_{i=q,\bar{q},g}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x\,f_{i}^{\pi}(x,\mu^{2})=1,

(13)

ensuring that the total momentum of the pion is shared among its constituents.

The behavior of the pion valence PDF at large

which provides a local measure of the fall-off of the distribution as

3.3
Pion-nucleus induced
J/\psi
production

We compute the differential cross section for charmonium production up to NLO in pion–nucleus collisions and compare with existing
J/\psi
data. We employ the Color Evaporation Model (CEM)
[
73
,
74
,
75
]
, where the produced
c\bar{c}
pair hadronizes into a specific charmonium state with a universal probability encoded in a single phenomenological parameter
F
. This approach successfully describes fixed-target and collider
J/\psi
data
[
76
,
77
,
78
,
79
]
. Within this framework, the differential cross section for
J/\psi
production in pion–nucleus interactions is given by
[
80
,
81
,
82
,
83
,
38
]
.

\displaystyle\frac{d{\bf\sigma}}{dx_{\mathrm{F}}}\Big|_{J/\psi}=F\sum_{i,j=q,\bar{q},g}\int^{2m_{D}}_{2m_{c}}dM_{c\bar{c}}\frac{2M_{c\bar{c}}}{S\sqrt{x_{F}^{2}+4M^{2}_{c\bar{c}}/S}}\,

\displaystyle\times\hat{\bf\sigma}_{ij}(s,m^{2}_{c},\mu_{F}^{2},\mu_{R}^{2})f^{\pi^{\pm}}_{i}(x_{1},\mu_{F}^{2})\,{f}^{N}_{j}(x_{2},\mu^{2}_{F})\,.

(15)

where
x_{1,2}=(\sqrt{x_{F}^{2}+4M_{c\bar{c}}^{2}/S}\pm x_{F})/2
and
x_{F}=x_{1}-x_{2}
is the Feynman variable. Here,
S
is the squared center-of-mass energy of the
\pi N
system, and
s
denotes the partonic invariant mass. The masses
m_{c}
,
m_{D}
, and
M_{c\bar{c}}
correspond to the charm quark,
D
meson, and the
c\bar{c}
pair, respectively. The short-distance cross section
\hat{\sigma}_{ij}
is calculated at NLO accuracy
[
80
]
.

The hadronic inputs are the pion PDFs
f^{\pi^{\pm}}
from our model and the nuclear PDFs
f^{N}
, for which we use the nCTEQ15 parametrization
[
84
]
. We choose
\mu_{F}=2m_{c}
and
\mu_{R}=m_{c}
, with
m_{c}=1.5~\text{GeV}
.

This behavior highlights the sensitivity of

4
Conclusion

In this work, we have investigated the pion structure within an extended light-front holographic QCD framework supplemented by the ’t Hooft equation for longitudinal dynamics. This unified approach provides a consistent description of confinement and has been previously validated through meson spectra, electromagnetic form factors, and decay constants, thereby establishing the resulting light-front wave functions as realistic representations of pion structure. Using the resulting wave functions, we extracted the pion PDFs and evolved them using DGLAP equations, where gluon and sea quark distributions are generated dynamically. The valence, gluon, and sea PDFs are in good agreement with global analyses such as xFitter
[
14
]
, JAM
[
16
,
13
,
10
]
, and MAP
[
15
]
.
