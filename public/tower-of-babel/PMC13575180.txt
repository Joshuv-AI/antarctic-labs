Pressure-dependent structural, mechanical, and electronic properties of XAu (X = Er, Tm) intermetallic compounds from first-principles calculations
Authors: A Benmakhlouf, R Mezouar, S Daoud, M A Ghebouli, M Fatmi, K Bouferrache, A Djemli, Mustafa Jaipallah Abdelmageed Abualreish, Aseel Smerat, S Alomairy, Murat Yaylacı
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFirst-principles calculations of the structural parameters, elastic constants, mechanical moduli, Debye temperature, and density of states (DOS) of cubic CsCl-type (B2) XAu (X = Er, Tm) rare-earth intermetallic compounds under hydrostatic compression up to 20 GPa have been performed. The calculations were carried out within the framework of density functional theory (DFT) using the pseudopotential plane-wave (PP-PW) method and the local density approximation (LDA). The calculated elastic constants indicate that both ErAu and TmAu with the CsCl-type (B2) phase satisfy the Born mechanical stability criteria at equilibrium and under pressure up to 20 GPa for ErAu and up to about 19 GPa for TmAu. The zero-pressure structural parameters, elastic constants, and mechanical moduli are generally in good agreement with previously reported theoretical data. The calculated zero-pressure Young’s modulus values are 96.61 GPa for ErAu and 73.94 GPa for TmAu, while the corresponding Debye temperatures are 193 K and 167 K, respectively. The obtained results reveal that hydrostatic pressure enhances the stiffness of both compounds, as reflected by the general increase in the elastic constants, acoustic wave velocities, and Debye temperature. Both ErAu and TmAu exhibit ductile behavior according to Pugh’s ratio and show noticeable elastic anisotropy. The electronic-structure analysis confirms the metallic character of both compounds, while additional GGA + U calculations indicate that the localized 4f states are sensitive to the Hubbard correction without changing their metallic nature. The pressure-dependent elastic constants and related mechanical and thermodynamic properties of XAu (X = Er, Tm) intermetallic compounds have not been previously reported; therefore, the present results provide useful predictions and reference data for future theoretical and experimental studies.Keywords: Density functional theory (DFT), Intermetallic compounds, Rare-earth mono-aurides, Mechanical properties, Hydrostatic pressure, Density of statesSubject terms: Materials science, Physics

Introduction

Intermetallic compounds (IMCs) constitute a distinct class of materials that generally combine metallic and ceramic characteristics, such as high strength, high stiffness, good thermal stability, and complex bonding. These characteristics make this class of materials particularly relevant for fundamental research and advanced technological applications1. Among intermetallic compounds, rare-earth mono-aurides Ln-Au (Ln = Ce, Pr, Nd, Gd, Tb, Dy, Ho, Er, Tm, Yb, and Lu) have attracted considerable attention because of their high strength, very high melting points, high stiffness, good ductility, corrosion resistance, oxidation resistance, high-temperature mechanical performance, and good electrical properties2.

High-temperature materials are essential for technologies operating under severe thermal and mechanical conditions, and gold-based rare-earth intermetallic have been considered promising candidates for such applications because of their remarkable physical and mechanical properties3. From the electronic point of view, previous first-principles investigations of REAu (RE = Tb, Ho, Er, Tm, and Yb) compounds showed their metallic character from density of states and band-structure analyses, and also indicated interesting thermoelectric behavior for some members of this family4. Experimentally, Chao et al. successfully synthesized CsCl-type phases of several gold- and silver-based rare-earth intermetallic compounds and showed that the unit-cell sizes of gold-based compounds are smaller than those of the corresponding silver-based alloys, suggesting stronger gold–rare-earth bonding5. Using the modified analytical embedded atom method, Wu et al. theoretically investigated the thermodynamic properties and lattice parameters of several RE-Au binary systems6. Saccone et al. studied the alloying behavior of Ho–Au, Er–Au, and Tm–Au systems using optical microscopy, scanning electron microscopy, X-ray diffraction, differential thermal analysis, and electron probe microanalysis techniques7. Meschel and Kleppa investigated the thermochemistry of binary alloys of gold with lanthanide metals by high-temperature direct synthesis calorimetry8. Kissell and Wallace reported the magnetic characteristics of some 1:1 compounds of lanthanides with gold and aluminum9. In addition, Gingerich and Finkbeiner determined the dissociation energies and predicted the stability of several gaseous rare-earth mono-aurides10.

From the theoretical side, Ahamad et al. investigated strongly correlated rare-earth mono-aurides Ln-Au using first-principles calculations and reported their structural, elastic, and mechanical properties at zero pressure. Their work was based on the GGA-PBEsol approach11 and the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave method implemented in the WIEN2k code12. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no experimental or theoretical report in the literature on the pressure-dependent mechanical, thermodynamic, and electronic properties of XAu (X = Er, Tm) intermetallic compounds under hydrostatic compression. Recent ab initio studies have further confirmed the importance of first-principles approaches for describing structural, elastic, thermodynamic, electronic, and magnetic properties of intermetallic and related compounds. Benmakhlouf et al.13 investigated cubic CsCl-type MgCa and showed that DFT calculations can provide reliable structural, elastic, and thermodynamic parameters for B2-type intermetallic systems. Bioud et al.14 studied the thermodynamic and mechanical properties of B2-type CoTi and demonstrated the usefulness of elastic constants and Debye temperature in evaluating mechanical and thermal behavior. Zerrougui et al.15 investigated FeX (X = Pt, Pd) phases and emphasized the importance of electronic and magnetic analyses in understanding metallic behavior and magnetic interactions. Chihi et al.16 also combined experimental and theoretical approaches to study the structural, elastic, thermoelastic, and electronic properties of MO3 (M = Cr, Fe, Al) compounds. These studies support the relevance of the present pressure-dependent investigation of structural, elastic, thermodynamic, and electronic properties of ErAu and TmAu intermetallic compounds. Recent first-principles studies have also demonstrated the importance of combining elastic, electronic, and phonon analyses for gold-based and B2-type intermetallic compounds. For instance, Al et al. investigated the mechanical and dynamical properties of X3AuX (X = Ti, Zr, and V) gold-based compounds and showed that elastic stability and phonon spectra provide complementary information on the stability of these systems17. Similarly, Arikan and Bayhan studied the structural, electronic, and phonon properties of ZrRu and ZrZn in the B2 phase, emphasizing the usefulness of electronic band structures, density of states, and phonon dispersion in assessing metallicity and dynamical stability18. These studies further motivate the present investigation of the pressure-dependent structural, mechanical, and electronic properties of ErAu and TmAu in the CsCl-type B2 phase.

The present work aims to investigate the structural, elastic, mechanical, thermodynamic, and electronic properties of ErAu and TmAu intermetallic compounds in the cubic CsCl-type B2 phase under hydrostatic pressure up to 20 GPa. The pressure dependence of the elastic constants, mechanical stability, elastic anisotropy, acoustic wave velocities, Debye temperature, and electronic structure is systematically discussed.

Computational procedure

All first-principles calculations were performed within the (DFT) framework using the CASTEP software19. We adopted a plane-wave basis set and treated exchange-correlation (XC) interactions using the (LDA) parameterized by the Ceperley-Alder and Perdew-Zunger (CA-PZ) functional20,21. To reduce the plane-wave basis size, chemically inert core electrons were described via ultra-soft pseudopotentials22. The kinetic energy cutoff for the plane-wave expansion was set to 700 eV. Brillouin zone integration was performed using a Monkhorst-Pack23 scheme with a 16 × 16 × 16 k-point grid. Full structural relaxation was carried out by optimizing the lattice parameters to minimize the total energy, atomic forces, and unit-cell stresses. Convergence criteria for the geometry optimization were established as follows: total energy variations below 5 × 10− 6 eV/atom, maximum residual stress under 0.02 GPa, maximum ionic displacement below 5 × 10− 4 Å, and Hellmann-Feynman forces restricted to 0.01 eV/Å.

To provide a quantitative description of this compression effect, the relative volume reduction was estimated from the calculated pressure-volume data. For ErAu, the unit-cell volume decreases from about 42.5 Å3 at 0 GPa to about 36.9 Å3 at 20 GPa, corresponding to a reduction of approximately 13%. For TmAu, the volume decreases from about 42.1 Å3 to about 36.5 Å3 over the same pressure range, giving a similar reduction of about 13%. This comparable compressibility behavior indicates that both compounds respond similarly to hydrostatic pressure, which is expected because they crystallize in the same B2 CsCl-type structure and contain neighboring rare-earth elements. The volume contraction reflects the shortening of the Er–Au and Tm–Au bond lengths under pressure, leading to stronger interatomic interactions and, consequently, increased elastic stiffness.

Mechanical properties

Determining elastic constants provides a robust benchmark for evaluating theoretical models27. It is widely recognized that density functional theory (DFT) based first-principles calculations yield highly accurate predictions for the elastic behavior of inorganic materials28. Moreover, characterizing these elastic and mechanical features provides essential insights into fundamental macroscopic thermal parameters, including specific heat, thermal expansion coefficients, Debye temperatures, and melting points28. Contemporary first-principles frameworks have advanced to a level of precision where they can reliably predict the physical properties of materials prior to experimental synthesis, as well as accurately reproduce existing empirical data27. Cubic crystals are governed by three independent elastic constants: C11, C12, and C4429. Consequently, the bulk modulus B is derived using the relation B = (C11 + 2C12)/328,29. The melting temperature Tm is a critical thermal parameter for evaluating solids in high-temperature applications30. For numerous cubic solids, Tm strongly correlates with the bulk modulus B through the empirical linear relationship Tm = 9.3B + 60731, where Tm is given in K and B in GPa. By substituting our determined zero-pressure bulk moduli (B0 = 91.59 GPa for ErAu and 86.26 GPa for TmAu) into this equation, we estimate the melting points of the ErAu and TmAu intermetallic compounds to be 1458.8 K and 1409.2 K, respectively. It should be emphasized that the Born stability criteria provide information only about the mechanical stability of the crystal against homogeneous elastic deformations. These criteria do not prove the full dynamical stability of the structure, which requires phonon-dispersion calculations and the absence of imaginary phonon frequencies throughout the Brillouin zone. Therefore, the present results confirm the mechanical stability of the B2 phase of ErAu and TmAu within the investigated pressure range, while the dynamical stability under pressure remains to be verified by future phonon calculations. This distinction between mechanical and dynamical stability is consistent with previous first-principles investigations. Al et al.17 showed that, for X3AuX gold-based compounds, elastic stability criteria should be complemented by phonon spectra to confirm dynamical stability. Similarly, Arikan and Bayhan18 demonstrated that phonon-dispersion calculations are essential for confirming the dynamical stability of B2-type intermetallic systems. Therefore, although the present Born criteria confirm the mechanical stability of ErAu and TmAu under pressure, future phonon calculations are required to fully verify their dynamical stability.

As shown in Fig. 4, we find that the stability criterion G decreases gradually with the pressure and vanishes at about 19 GPa for TmAu intermetallic compound. Debye temperature

The Debye temperature, θD, is a fundamental thermodynamic quantity that yields critical insights into the phonon spectrum of a crystal, thereby governing its thermal and electrical transport properties. Phonon wavelengths are characteristically short at temperatures exceeding θD, whereas they become significantly extended below this thermal threshold30. Typically, θD exhibits a positive correlation with applied pressure39–41 and an inverse relationship with temperature34,42,43. The origin of the discrepancy in the Debye temperature values reported by Ahamad et al.2. is related mainly to the numerical use of the number of atoms in the Debye-temperature expression. In Ref2., the authors used n = 1 for the CsCl-type structure. However, the B2 CsCl-type unit cell of ErAu and TmAu contains two atoms per formula unit, namely one rare-earth atom and one Au atom. Therefore, n = 2 should be used in the Debye-temperature calculation. Using n = 1 underestimates θD by a factor of 21/3. By using n = 2, together with the elastic constants, density, and sound velocities reported in Ref2., the corrected Debye temperatures listed in Table 5 are obtained. Therefore, the discrepancy is mainly due to a formula-unit/unit-cell counting issue in the Debye-temperature evaluation rather than an error in the elastic-wave velocity formulas.

Electronic structure: band structure, DOS and PDOS

The electronic properties of material play a dominant role on the relationship between the magnetic momentsand the stability of the structure47. Furthermore, the examination of the electronic density of states (DOS) at the Fermi level as a function of the volume helps on the determination of the electronic contribution to the heat capacity Cp in material48. Figure 7a and b show the total density of states (TDOS), which often describes the behavior of the band structures, and the partial density of states (PDOS), which evaluate the individual atom contributions to the TDOS, for both ErAu and TmAu intermetallic compounds. The PDOS of both ErAu and TmAu intermetallic com materials pounds exhibits non-zero values at Fermi level, which indicates the metallic feature of both compounds. For both materials XAu (X = Er, Tm), the density of states (Fig. 7a and b) is dominated by the Au 5d band in the − 6 to − 3 eV energy range and by the rare-earth 4f states near the Fermi level. This shows that the way they bond is similar in both and it is because of the Au 5d and rare earth 4f hybridization. The Au partial DOS is remarkably similar in both intermetalliccompounds, with the 5d band peaking at approximately − 4.9 eV in ErAu material and − 4.8 eV in TmAu compound, with comparable intensities of around 8.0–8.5 electrons/eV. This similarity confirms that the Au sublattice contributes a nearly unchanged electronic background across the two systems, and that the primary source of differentiation between ErAu intermetallic compound and TmAu material lies in the rare-earth 4f manifold.

To further examine the influence of localized rare-earth 4f electrons, additional GGA+U calculations were performed by applying an effective Hubbard parameter Ueff=6 eV to the Er-4f and Tm-4f states. The calculated band structures obtained using both GGA and GGA+U are shown in Fig. 8. The comparison indicates that the inclusion of the Hubbard correction mainly modifies the position and dispersion of the 4f-derived bands, confirming the sensitivity of the electronic structure to the treatment of localized 4f electrons. Nevertheless, several bands still cross the Fermi level in both compounds, demonstrating that the metallic character of ErAu and TmAu is preserved after the inclusion of the Hubbard correction.These additional GGA+U calculations were introduced to address the localized nature of the Er-4f and Tm-4f electrons and to provide a more reliable description of the 4f-derived electronic bands. The results show that the Hubbard correction affects mainly the rare-earth 4f states, while the metallic character remains unchanged. The most significant structural difference between the two DOS profiles concerns the energy position and distribution of the 4f states. TmAu intermetallic compound has a noticeable splitting of the 4f manifold into two sharp peaks at − 2.26 eV and − 0.09 eV (Fig. 7b). These peaks are almost the same size of ~ 13 electrons/eV. In ErAu intermetallic compound, the two peaks are not as symmetrical, the first one is at energy of ~ − 4.28 eV, while the second peakis positioned at energy of ~ − 0.09 eV. The difference between these peaks is about 4.2 eV. This is because the crystal field environments and exchange splitting strengths are different for ErAu material and TmAu intermetallic compound. TmAu material has one more 4f electron than ErAu compound. It can be noticed also that both compounds exhibit a large DOS at the Fermi level driven by the 4f states (Fig. 7a and b) suggests a stronger tendency toward magnetic ordering compared to ErAu intermetallic compound. However, the higher and more symmetric N(EF)) observed in TmAu material (Fig. 7b) suggests a relatively stronger possible magnetic tendency compared to ErAu intermetallic compound. Nevertheless, this statement should be understood only as a qualitative indication based on the DOS profile. The Stoner-like argument alone is not sufficient to determine the true magnetic ground state or the local magnetic moments. In fact, the available Materials Project data for the B2 phases of ErAu and TmAu classify both compounds as non-magnetic metallic systems, with zero total magnetization. This supports the revised interpretation that the finite 4f contribution at the Fermi level should not be considered as direct evidence of a magnetic ground state.

It can also be noticed that both compounds exhibit a noticeable DOS at the Fermi level mainly driven by the rare-earth 4f states, as shown in Fig. 8a and b. This feature may qualitatively suggest a possible magnetic tendency within a Stoner-like picture. However, the present DOS results alone cannot be used to determine the true magnetic ground state or the local magnetic moments of these compounds. Therefore, dedicated spin-polarized calculations are required for a rigorous magnetic assessment. In this context, the additional spin-polarized GGA + U calculations performed in the present revision provide a more appropriate treatment of the localized 4f states. The full simultaneous inclusion of spin-orbit coupling and DFT + U remains a limitation of the present computational setup and is proposed for future work.

Conclusion

we examine the effects of applied pressure up to 20 GPa on the structural characteristics, elastic constants, and related macroscopic properties of the intermetallic compounds ErAu and TmAu crystallized in the CsCl-type (B2) phase. Calculations were performed utilizing pseudopotential plane-wave formalism within the local density approximation (LDA). As pressure increases from 0 to 20 GPa, the elastic constants and derived thermodynamic parameters, including acoustic wave velocities and the Debye temperature (θD), exhibit a predominantly monotonic yet non-linear evolution, albeit with minor fluctuations. Evaluation of the Born stability criteria confirms that the B2 phase remains mechanically stable both at equilibrium and under compression up to approximately 20 GPa for ErAu and 19 GPa for TmAu. At ambient pressure, our LDA-derived elastic constants display slight deviations from the prior theoretical predictions. Additionally, analysis of the total and partial density of states (TDOS and PDOS) corroborates the intrinsic metallic character of both systems. Crucially, a pronounced density of states at the Fermi level, primarily driven by the 4f orbitals, implies that both ErAu and TmAu satisfy the Stoner criterion for magnetic instability.
