Decoupled Azimuth–Elevation AoA Estimation Exploiting Kronecker-Separable Steering Matrices
Faizan A. Khattak, Ian K. Proudler, Stephan Weiss, Fazal-E Asim

Abstract
Uniform rectangular arrays (URA), structured non-uniform rectangular arrays (NURA), and parallelogram-shaped (UPgA & NUPgA) arrays admit steering vectors that can be expressed as the Kronecker product of azimuth and elevation steering vectors. Accordingly, the full steering matrix can be represented as the Khatri–Rao product of the corresponding azimuth and elevation steering matrices. This paper exploits this structure to develop an economical subspace-decoupling framework for two-dimensional angle-of-arrival (AoA) estimation. The proposed method first extracts the joint signal subspace from the spatial covariance matrix. Then it applies a low-complexity decoupling scheme to recover the column spaces of the azimuth and elevation steering matrices. With the estimated decoupled subspaces, conventional one-dimensional algorithms such as MUSIC, root-MUSIC, and ESPRIT can be applied independently along each dimension, followed by pairing through a two-dimensional spectral function. Monte Carlo simulations show that the proposed approach achieves higher accuracy than state-of-the-art methods, i.e., two-dimensional MUSIC, reduced-dimension MUSIC, and two-dimensional ESPRIT, for medium- and large-scale arrays while requiring fewer snapshots, consequently with improved spectral efficiency.

Introduction

Both uniform and non-uniform linear arrays have been extensively studied for angle-of-arrival (AoA) estimation, yielding a rich set of algorithms such as MUSIC, root-MUSIC, and ESPRIT and their many variants
[
16
,
11

15
,
18

5

3
]
. By contrast, planar array geometries—including uniform rectangular arrays (URA) and structured non-uniform rectangular (NURA) arrays—offer clear advantages for modern applications (MIMO
[
7
]
, massive MIMO
[
1
]
, 3-D localization
[
2
,
19
]
, etc.) but have been comparatively less explored. Although there are AoA algorithms for URAs, e.g., reduced-dimension MUSIC (RD-MUSIC)
[
19
]
, its root extraction variant root-RD-MUSIC
[
4
]
, and ESPRIT variants for MIMO radar
[
10
,
6
]
—computationally efficient methods for structured NURAs remain scarce beyond conventional or optimized 2-D MUSIC.
In addition, structured NURAs, which offer greater flexibility in practical deployments than URAs
[
13
]
, further motivate the development of an efficient AoA algorithm.

In this paper, we consider URA configurations and, more generally, any array configuration in which the full steering matrix admits a Khatri-Rao factorization (column-wise Kronecker) into two lower-dimensional steering matrices for azimuth and elevation, and introduce an efficient decoupling of the two spatial dimensions, respectively. By deriving separate signal- and noise-subspace projection matrices for each spatial direction, the proposed approach enables independent application of root-MUSIC, MUSIC, and ESPRIT in each spatial dimension for uniform arrays, and MUSIC or irregular root-MUSIC for structured NURAs
[
18
]
, at a substantially reduced order compared to existing 2-D methods. The decoupling thus yields significant computational savings while improving estimation accuracy, particularly for large arrays and in regimes limited by the number of snapshots or SNR.

II
Signal Model

Let us consider an
M\times N
structured non-uniform array, where the horizontal spacing is non-uniform but row-independent, and the vertical spacing is also non-uniform but remains column-independent, as illustrated in Fig.
1
for a
5\times 5
structured non-uniform array.
Note that this definition encompasses the cases of a URA (both uniform and structured non-uniform) as well as a parallelogram-shaped array (both uniform, i.e., UPgA, and structured non-uniform, i.e., NUPgA).
For simplicity, we focus on a structured NURA which includes the URA and parallelogram array.
The array is assumed to lie in the
xz
-plane, with the azimuth angle

\theta
defined as the angle between the incident direction and the
x
-axis,
and the elevation angle
\vartheta
defined as the angle between the incident
direction and the
z
-axis.
Therefore, both angles are restricted to the interval

\theta,\vartheta\in[0,\pi]
.
The above angle definition follows the proposal in e.g
[
9
]
and enables independent AoA estimation.
The steering vector of a structured NURA is given as a Kronecker product between horizontal and vertical steering vector

\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}}(\mu_{\mathrm{h}},\mu_{\mathrm{v}})={\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{h}}(\mu_{\mathrm{h}})\otimes{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{v}}(\mu_{\mathrm{v}})\in\mathbb{C}^{MN},

(1)

where

{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{h}}(\mu_{\mathrm{h}})=[1,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\mu_{\mathrm{h}}p_{1,\mathrm{x}}},\dots,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\mu_{\mathrm{h}}p_{M-1,\mathrm{x}}}]
,
{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{v}}(\mu_{\mathrm{v}})=[1,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\mu_{\mathrm{v}}p_{1,\mathrm{z}}},\dots,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\mu_{\mathrm{v}}p_{N-1,\mathrm{z}}}]^{\mathrm{T}}
,
p_{m,\mathrm{x}}
and
p_{n,\mathrm{z}}
are sensor positions along
x
and
z
axis with
p_{0,\mathrm{x}}=0,p_{0,\mathrm{z}}=0
.
The operator
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product, while
\mu_{\mathrm{h}}

and
\mu_{\mathrm{v}}

are horizontal and vertical spatial frequencies, respectively, of a source.
For
P
narrowband sources
{\mathbf{x}}(t)\in\mathbb{C}^{P}
in far-field, the sensor measurement at discrete time index
n
is

\displaystyle{\mathbf{y}}[n]={\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}[n]+{\mathbf{w}}[n]{\in\mathbb{C}^{MN}}\;,

(2)

where

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}

\displaystyle=[{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{h}}(\mu_{\mathrm{h},1})\otimes{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{v}}(\mu_{\mathrm{v},1}),\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{h}}(\mu_{\mathrm{h},P})\otimes{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{v}}(\mu_{\mathrm{v},P})]

\displaystyle={\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}\diamond{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}\in\mathbb{C}^{MN\times P}

(3)

is the steering matrix and can be expressed as a Khatri-Rao product, denoted by
\diamond
, between steering matrices in azimuth and elevation direction, and
{\mathbf{w}}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{MN}
represents a noise term.
We further assume that the source spatial covariance matrix is of full rank
P
which includes the case of uncorrelated sources and coherent sources with spatial smoothing, and noise is white with zero mean and variance
\sigma_{{w}}^{2}
. With noise mutually uncorrelated with the sources, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}={\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{xx}}{\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{H}}+\sigma_{{w}}^{2}{\mathbf{I}}_{MN\times MN}\;,

(4)

where
{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{xx}}
is the source covariance matrix.

III
Subspace Recovery of Khatri-Rao Factors from Their Product Under Invertible Transformation

Both MUSIC and ESPRIT algorithms require knowledge of the noise and signal subspace, respectively.
In the following, we show how to estimate the azimuth-only and elevation-only signal subspaces.

III-A

Subspace Information In Kronecker Structure

Via an eigenvalue decomposition of
{\mathbf{R}}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}={\mathbf{Q}}_{s}{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\mathrm{s}}{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{H}}+{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\perp}{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}^{\mathrm{H}},

(5)

where
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathbb{C}^{MN\times P}
contains the eigenvectors that span the signal-only subspace, corresponding to the
P
dominant eigenvalues in
{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\mathrm{s}}
, whereas the remaining eigenvectors in
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}
span the noise-only subspace.
With
{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{xx}}
full rank,
\mathrm{span}({\mathbf{A}})=\mathrm{span}({\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s}})
is guaranteed
[
16
]
, and so we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s}}={\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{G}}\;,

(6)

where
{\mathbf{G}}\in\mathbb{C}^{P\times P}
is an invertible matrix
[
17
]
.
This serves as the foundation for our subspace decomposition approach.
With
{\mathbf{A}}
admitting a Khatri-Rao factorization, the
p
th column of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s}}
is given as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{q}}_{p}=\sum_{k=1}^{P}g_{k,p}{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{h},k}\otimes{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{v},k},

(7)

where
g_{k,p}
is the element at
k
th row and
p
th column of
{\mathbf{G}}
.
With
{\mathbf{q}}_{p}{\in\mathbb{C}^{NM}}
reshaped into an
N\times M
matrix
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s},p}
via an unvec operation, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s},p}=\sum_{k=1}^{P}g_{k,p}{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{v},k}{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathrm{h},k}^{\mathrm{T}}={\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}\mbox{diag}\!\left\{{\mathbf{g}}_{p}\right\}{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}^{\mathrm{T}},

(8)

It is tempting to think that the column space of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s},p}
spans the azimuth-only subspace, and that the row space of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s},p}
spans the elevation-only subspace.
However, this is not the case as
{\mathbf{G}}
may contain some zero elements.
Therefore, a reformulation is necessary, outlined in the next subsection, in order to extract the column spaces of both
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}
.

III-B

Azimuth and Elevation Column Spaces Retrieval

In order to access the column spaces of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}
, we stack

\displaystyle{\mathbf{C}}_{r}=

\displaystyle[\{{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s},1}\}_{r},\dots,\{{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s},P}\}_{r}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}\mbox{diag}\!\left\{{\mathbf{g}}_{1}\right\}\{{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}^{\mathrm{T}}\}_{k},\dots,{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}\mbox{diag}\!\left\{{\mathbf{g}}_{P}\right\}\{{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}^{\mathrm{T}}\}_{r}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{h},r}{\mathbf{G}},

(9)

where
{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{h},r}=\mbox{diag}\!\left\{\{{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}^{\mathrm{T}}\}_{r}\right\}
, and
\{{\mathbf{F}}\}_{r}
is the
r
th column of a matrix
{\mathbf{F}}
.
Since none of the element of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}
are zero,
{\mathbf{C}}_{r}
is full column rank for every value of
r
. Therefore, one can compute the column space of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}
from
{\mathbf{C}}_{r}
, however it will not be very accurate because not all of the available data is being used. Instead we concatenate
{\mathbf{C}}_{r}
for
r=1,\dots,P
horizontally as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{C}}

\displaystyle=[{\mathbf{C}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{C}}_{M}]=[{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{h},1}{\mathbf{G}}\dots{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{h},M}{\mathbf{G}}]

\displaystyle={\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}[{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{h},1}{\mathbf{G}}\dots{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{h},M}{\mathbf{G}}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times MP}.

(10)

With each block
{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{h},m}{\mathbf{G}}\in\mathbb{C}^{P\times P},~m=1,\dots,M
invertible, the column space of
{\mathbf{C}}
is the same as that of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}
, and therefore the orthonormal basis for the column space can be obtained from the SVD of
{\mathbf{C}}
and collecting
P
dominant left singular vectors of
{\mathbf{C}}
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{col}\{{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}\}=\mathrm{span}\{{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{C},1},\dots,{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{C},P}\}\;,

(11)

where
{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{C,n}}
is the
n
th dominant left singular vector of
{\mathbf{C}}
. Similarly, stacking the
r
th row of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s},p}
for
p=1,\dots,P
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}_{r}=[\{{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s},1}^{\mathrm{T}}\}_{r},\dots,\{{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{s},P}^{\mathrm{T}}\}_{r}]={\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{v},r}{\mathbf{G}}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times P}\;,

where
{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{v},r}=\mbox{diag}\!\left\{\{{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}^{\mathrm{T}}\}_{r}\right\}
, and concatenating horizontally to construct

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}

\displaystyle=[{\mathbf{B}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{B}}_{N}]=[{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{v},1}{\mathbf{G}}\dots{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{v},N}{\mathbf{G}}]

\displaystyle={\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}[{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{v},1}{\mathbf{G}}\dots{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathrm{v},N}{\mathbf{G}}]\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times NP}\;.

(12)

The set of orthonormal vectors spanning the column space of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}
can similarly be obtained as

\displaystyle\mathrm{col}\{{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}\}=\mathrm{span}\{{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{B},1},\dots,{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{B},P}\}\;,

(13)

where
{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{B,n}}
is the
n
th dominant left singular vector of
{\mathbf{B}}
.

With (
11
) and (
13
), we now have orthonormal bases for the column spaces of both
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}
.
It can be seen that with this decoupling approach, only
\mathrm{min}\{M,N\}-1
many sources can be detected, which is less than other approaches, i.e.,
MN-1
sources by 2D-MUSIC.

III-C

Decoupled Azimuth and Elevation Spatial Frequencies Estimation

III-C
1
Uniform Rectangular or Parallelogram Arrays

With an orthonormal basis set for the column space of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{h}}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathrm{v}}
obtained from the above decoupling approach, a projection matrix onto the noise subspace can be obtained. So all subspace based AoA estimation methods applicable to a ULA such as MUSIC
[
16
]
, root-MUSIC
[
11
]
, gold-MUSIC
[
14
]
, and ESPRIT
[
15
]
can be applied to determine the horizontal and vertical spatial frequencies of any source contributions independently.
In this paper, we refer to applying root-MUSIC and ESPRIT after obtaining the column space in each direction as decoupled root-MUSIC (De-RMUSIC) and decoupled ESPRIT (De-ESPRIT), respectively.
It is important to note that the proposed method is a purely matrix-based technique rather than a tensor-based approach such as tensor-MUSIC or tensor-ESPRIT
[
8
]
, and therefore is inherently less computationally expensive. This also contrasts with recently proposed methods based on parallel factorization and a tensor SVD
[
12
]
.

III-C
2
Structured Non-uniform Rectangular or Parallelogram Arrays

With the proposed decoupling approach, once the orthonormal set of basis vectors are obtained, the conventional MUSIC or the irregular Vandermonde decomposition-based root-MUSIC algorithm
[
18
]
can be applied in each direction to estimate spatial frequencies both for structured NURA and NUPgAs.
Due to this decoupling,
n_{\mathrm{h}}\times n_{\mathrm{v}}
grid searches required in the 2D-MUSIC reduces to only
n_{\mathrm{h}}+n_{\mathrm{v}}
.
For example, with
1^{\circ}
resolution over a scanning range of
-90^{\circ}
to
90^{\circ}
, we have
n_{h}=n_{v}=181
, giving
32{,}761
searches for 2D-MUSIC, but only
362
searches with the decoupled method, achieving a
90.5
-fold reduction in computational cost.

III-D

Angle Pairing

To correctly pair an estimated azimuth angle with its corresponding elevation angle, here we rely on the conventional method
[
4
]
of evaluating at all pairs of
a_{h}
and
a_{v}
the pseudospectrum

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{MUSIC}}(\mu_{\mathrm{h}},\mu_{\mathrm{v}})={\mathbf{a}}(\mu_{\mathrm{h}},\mu_{\mathrm{v}})^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}^{\mathrm{H}}{\mathbf{a}}(\mu_{\mathrm{h}},\mu_{\mathrm{v}})\;,

(14)

and perform the associations through finding peaks.
This pairing makes sure that estimate azimuth and elevation angles correspond to the same source

V
Conclusion

We have presented a decoupling strategy that separates azimuth and elevation components in array configurations where the overall steering matrix admits a Khatri–Rao product structure between two lower-dimensional steering matrices .
This decoupling approach is applicable in URAs/UPgAs, MIMO radar configurations, and structured NURAs/NUPgAs
[
13
]
.
It allows classical subspace-based algorithms, such as MUSIC, root-MUSIC, and ESPRIT, to be applied independently in each spatial dimension instead of relying on tensor based methods which can be computationally expensive compared to ordinary matrix based methods.
While the suggested approach can only deal with a limited number of sources, our results show that for large uniform arrays, combining root-MUSIC or ESPRIT with the proposed decoupling achieves high accuracy across a wide SNR range—even with a limited number of snapshots. Despite the need for a pairing step to associate azimuth and elevation estimates, the proposed algorithms are computationally competitive with that of
[
10
]
.
Moreover, for structured NURAs, the approach enables independent spectral search MUSIC in each dimension, significantly reducing computational complexity while maintaining accuracy comparable to 2D-MUSIC.
