Strongly 1-bounded inner amenable groups
Ben Hayes, Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli

Abstract
. It is shown that finitely presented icc inner amenable groups yield strongly 1-bounded II 1 factors.



1.
Introduction

Building on Voiculescu’s free entropy theory
[
Voi02
]
, Jung introduced the influential notion of strong 1-boundedness
[
Jun07
]
with several applications to the study of II
1
factors. A striking feature of this property is the ability to account for amenable subalgebras in rigidity arguments. A testament to this is the resolution of the Peterson-Thom conjecture by the first author
[
Hay22
]
using his 1-bounded entropy techniques
[
Hay18
]
in conjunction with strong convergence arguments in random matrix theory
[
BC22
,
BC23
,
CGVvH24
,
MdlS24
,
Par24
]
. Precisely this resolution locates the amenable subalgebras of interpolated free group factors as exactly those with zero
1
-bounded entropy
[
HJK
]
. The purpose of this short note is to emphasize that the more general phenomena of inner amenability
[
Eff75
]
also fits in well with the framework of strong 1-boundedness. This was raised as a question by Y. Ueda to the second author during the 2019 BIRS workshop “Classification problems in von Neumann algebras”.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely presented icc inner amenable group. Then
L(G)
is strongly 1-bounded.

The proof of the above result is short combining various existing results, including those of
[
Hay18
,
ES05
,
TD20
,
Shl21
]
. Our main motivation to release this note is that we find it rather
cute
that our argument splits into two cases depending on whether the group is sofic or not. It remains open whether all countable inner amenable groups
G
satisfy
h(L(G))\leq 0
. We point out that the following is currently known about the von Neumann algebras associated to inner amenable groups
G
:
L(G)
is not properly proximal
[
DKEP23
]
; if
G
is additionally nonamenable
L(G)
is
\{\alpha_{t}\}
-rigid
[
Dri23
]
. However to our knowledge, it remains open whether
L(G)
is
L^{2}
-rigid in the sense of Peterson
[
Pet09
]
. We also define the notion of an inner amenable
C^{*}
-probability space, and conjecture that the GNS closures ought to be strongly 1-bounded.

2.
Proof of the main result

Recall the following: A countable discrete group
G
is called inner amenable if the action of
G
on the set
X=G\setminus\{1\}
by conjugation admits an invariant mean. A countable discrete group
G
is called strongly 1-bounded if the group von Neumann algebra
L(G)
is strongly 1-bounded in the sense of Jung
[
Jun07
]
. We will crucially need the following result:

Proposition 2.1

(Consequence of Proposition 5.1 in
[
ES05
]
)
.

If
G
is inner amenable with conjugation invariant mean
\mu
, and if
N=\{g\in G:\ \mu(X_{g})=1\}
is trivial (where
X_{g}
is the centralizer of
g
, and
\mu
is the conjugation invariant mean), then
G
is sofic.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
G
is inner amenable group which is either finitely presented and sofic or i.c.c. and not sofic, then
G
is strongly 1-bounded.

Proof.

If
G
is sofic and finitely presented, then the result follows from combining
[
TD20
, Corollary 6]
with the main result of
[
Shl21
]
(see also
[
Jun
,
HJKE
]
). So suppose
G
is i.c.c. and not sofic. Consider
G
acting on
X=G\setminus\{1\}
by conjugation and set
\mu
to be the conjugation invariant mean. Define the set
N=\{g\in G:\ \mu(X_{g})=1\}
where
X_{g}
is the centralizer of
g
. Since
G
is not sofic, we have by Proposition
2.1
that
N\neq\{1\}
. Since
G
is i.c.c., it follows that
N
is infinite. For any finite set
F_{n}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\}\subset N
, observe that there exists an element
g_{n}\in G
such that
g_{n}h_{i}=h_{i}g_{n}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Indeed,
\mu(X_{h_{i}})=1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, and so
\mu(\cap_{i=1}^{n}X_{h_{i}})=1
. Therefore
\cap_{i=1}^{n}X_{h_{i}}\neq\emptyset
. Now, let
N=\{h_{1},h_{2},\ldots\}
, and consider
N=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}F_{i}
, where
F_{i}=\{h_{1},\ldots h_{i}\}
. By the previous procedure, we have a sequence
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of non-identity group elements that eventually commutes with all of
N
. Hence, for
\omega\in\beta(\mathbb{N})\setminus\mathbb{N}
, we have
L(N)^{\prime}\cap L(G)^{\omega}
is diffuse. By Proposition 4.5 of
[
Hay18
]
, we see
h(L(N):L(G))=h(L(N):L(G)^{\omega})\leq 0
. But, it is an easy observation to see that
N
is a normal subgroup in
G
. Hence, by Theorem 1.3 of
[
Hay18
]
,
h(L(G))=h(W^{*}({\mathcal{N}}_{L(G)}(L(N))):L(G))=h(L(N):L(G))\leq 0
. By Proposition A.16 of
[
Hay18
]
, we conclude that G is strongly 1-bounded.
∎

Conjecture 2.3
.

All inner amenable groups are strongly 1-bounded.

While inner amenability of a group is not known to be an invariant of its generated von Neumann algebra, it is not hard to show that it is an invariant of its reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra equipped with the canonical trace.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(A,\tau)
be a tracial
C^{*}
-probability space, i.e, a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
equipped with a faithful tracial state
\tau
. We say that
(A,\tau)
is
inner amenable
if there is a sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n}
in
L^{2}(A,\tau)
with

•

\|\xi_{n}\|_{2}=1,

•

\tau(\xi_{n})=0,

•

\|a\xi_{n}-\xi_{n}a\|_{2}\to_{n\to\infty}0,
for all
a\in A
,

Observe that a group
G
is inner amenable if and only if
(C^{*}_{\lambda}(G),\tau)
is inner amenable where
\tau(x)=\langle{x\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\rangle}.

Conjecture 2.5
.

Let
(A,\tau)
be an inner amenable tracial
C^{*}
-probability space. Then
h(W^{*}(A,\tau))\leq 0.
