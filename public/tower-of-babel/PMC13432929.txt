Shengmai Liquid (Chinese Patent Medicine) for the Treatment of Post-COVID-19 Fatigue: A Protocol for a Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial
Authors: Jia-Xin Wang, Yan-Li Xu, Cheng-Jie Ma, Guo-Dong Li, Yu-Xuan Yang, Jie Lian, Bo Li, Yi-Ying Wang, Jian-Ping Liu, Le Ngoc Hung, Ngo Thi Phuong, Le Minh Ha, Dinh Thi Lan Huong, Hui-Juan Cao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionFatigue is one of the most common symptoms of post-COVID syndrome. The Chinese herbal medicine Shengmai liquid has been recorded in ancient books, and modern studies have shown its ability to relieve fatigue. This study aims to assess the clinical efficacy and safety of Shengmai liquid in improving fatigue symptoms in post-COVID syndrome patients.MethodsWe plan to enroll 100 eligible patients with post-COVID-19 fatigue from Beijing Ditan Hospital, randomly allocating them into either the Shengmai liquid or placebo group. Blinding will be maintained for the personnel responsible for evaluating outcomes and performing statistical analysis. The participants will be treated for 8 weeks and followed for another 30 days. We utilized the modified Fatigue Impact Scale as our main endpoint to evaluate changes in patient fatigue. The secondary outcomes will include the Traditional Chinese Medicine Syndrome Score Scale, Hamilton Depression Inventory-17 items, Hamilton Anxiety Scale, Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Scale, World Health Organization Quality-of-Life Scale, and 6-min walk distance. All the statistical analyses will use SPSS software with a 0.05 significance level and follow the intention-to-treat principle.ConclusionThis randomized, single-center, double-blind trial will provide robust clinical evidence regarding the short-term efficacy and safety of Shengmai liquid for the treatment of post-COVID-19 fatigue.

Background

In October 2021, the World Health Organization (WHO) defined post-COVID syndrome (also called long COVID-19) for the first time [1]. The disease involves a series of persistent symptoms within 3 months after infection with SARS-CoV-2, which last for at least 2 months and cannot be explained by other diseases. According to existing research, it is conservatively estimated that at least 65 million people worldwide suffer from post-COVID syndrome [1, 2]. Fatigue, respiratory diseases and sleep disturbances are the 3 most common symptoms of post-COVID syndrome [2, 3]. Specifically, post-COVID-19 fatigue is defined as a profound and debilitating physical and cognitive exhaustion that is disproportionate to recent exertion and is not fully relieved by rest [4].

In a study that followed 3,762 people with post-COVID syndrome, 85% reported fatigue as the most common symptom, 45% needed to reduce their working hours, and 22% were unable to work due to fatigue [5]. The clinical presentation encompasses muscle weakness, cognitive impairment, and post-exertional malaise, where symptoms exacerbate following minor physical or mental effort [6]. Studies have also revealed that fatigue is associated with sleep disturbance [7], anxiety [8], and other symptoms. While the exact etiology remains under investigation, current evidence suggests that post-COVID-19 fatigue is multifactorial, potentially driven by immune system dysregulation, persistent systemic or neuroinflammation, viral persistence, and mitochondrial dysfunction [9, 10]. Furthermore, epidemiological characteristics indicate that patients who are hospitalized for COVID-19, have severe conditions, or have weaker constitutions are more prone to developing post-COVID-19 fatigue [5].

Current medical guidelines for managing post-COVID-19 syndrome are limited, e.g., the 2023 ERS Task Force guidelines for long-term COVID-19 management and the 2024 Canadian government’s treatment guidelines for post-COVID-19 conditions. Both of them recommend exercise rehabilitation as first-line treatment for post-COVID-19 fatigue, but insufficient evidence is available to formally endorse any pharmacotherapy.

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) approaches for managing post-COVID-19 fatigue include acupuncture [11, 12] and herbal medicine. Expert consensus suggests the use of a modified combination of Shengmai liquid and Pingwei san as a general prescription for post-COVID syndrome [13]. However, the evidence supporting this recommendation remains limited in quality. Notably, there is a significant lack of high-quality original research specifically targeting post-COVID-19 fatigue.

While the classic Shengmai formula recorded in ancient texts is indicated for symptoms such as limb weariness and shortness of breath [14], modern clinical practice and this trial frequently use Dangshen (Codonopsis Radix) as a substitute for Renshen [15]. While Renshen is a potent tonifier, Dangshen provides a milder yet effective Qi-tonifying effect, making it more suitable for the long-term recovery of patients with post-viral fatigue. Pharmacologically, both herbs contain similar active components, such as saponins and polysaccharides, which have been shown to enhance immune function and improve mitochondrial energy metabolism [16]. A systematic review and data mining study revealed that Dangshen (Codonopsis Radix) and Maidong (Ophiopogonis Radix), the key components of the modified Shengmai formulation used in this study, are among the most frequently prescribed herbs for treating post-viral and post-COVID-19 fatigue [15, 16]. This high clinical utilization supports the therapeutic rationale of our formulation.

Some basic research has shown that the polysaccharides from Ophiopogonis Radix and Schisandrin B. from Schisandrae Chinensis Fructus in Shengmai liquid exhibit significant antioxidant activity, effectively reducing reactive oxygen species levels and alleviating oxidative stress-induced cellular damage [17], thereby improving fatigued conditions. Shengmai liquid downregulates cortisol levels and modulates hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis function [5], effectively relieving fatigue caused by chronic stress [18]. It also reduces the levels of proinflammatory factors such as TNF-α and IL-6, ameliorating chronic inflammation-associated fatigue [19].

On the basis of the results of the current mechanistic research and clinical studies, we believe that Shengmai liquid may alleviate fatigue after COVID-19. This trial aims to provide robust evidence by comparing the efficacy and safety of Shengmai liquid and placebo on rational symptom scales for post-COVID-19 fatigue.

Methods

Reporting of this protocol follows the SPIRIT statement (see online suppl. Material 1; for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000553019).

Study Design

This study is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. After screening and recruitment, 100 participants will be randomly assigned to Shengmai liquid or placebo groups at a 1:1 ratio. We use SAS 9.4 software to generate the random allocation. Shengmai liquid and placebo are made by the same pharmaceutical company (Jilin Aodong Yanbian Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 10 mL/bottle, Z41021384).

Codonopsis Radix (Dangshen), Ophiopogonis Radix (Maidong), and Schisandrae Chinensis Fructus (Wuweizi) in a weight ratio of 3:2:1. The composition of the placebo includes water, brown sugar and flavors, which were subjected to three rounds of testing and improvement, and the color, odor, and taste of the mixture are similar to those of Shengmai liquid. The Shengmai liquid and placebo will be placed in a medication box with the same appearance, and the number on the label will match the random serial number. To ensure rigorous concealment of allocation, masking will be applied to all involved parties, including clinical practitioners, patients, outcome evaluators, and data analysts.

Participants

Recruitment will be conducted at Beijing Ditan Hospital starting on 30 September, 2025. Participants will be recruited through outpatient clinics with experienced doctors, advertisements, and phone calls. After a phone call or in-person appointment with a doctor, screening will take place at the hospital.

We will include those who (i) meet the diagnostic criteria for post-COVID syndrome, which is defined as persistent syndrome lasting for at least 2 months within 3 months after infection with SARS-CoV-2 and cannot be explained by other diseases [20]; (ii) have a fatigue symptom with a severity score greater than or equal to 33 points (≥ 33 points) assessed by the modified fatigue impact scale (MFIS); (iii) meet the diagnostic criteria for qi and yin deficiency according to the “Guiding Principles for Clinical Research of New Traditional Chinese Medicine Drugs” [21] if the sum of the scores of a single syndrome element is greater than 8 points; (iv) are adults aged no more than 75 years [22] and (v) sign the informed consent form.

Participants will be excluded if they (i) are currently in need of or expected to require high-flow oxygen therapy, positive pressure ventilation, invasive mechanical ventilation, or extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, and other advanced respiratory support measures; (ii) have a documented history of active liver disease excluding nonalcoholic fatty liver changes, including active hepatitis B or C infection, primary biliary cirrhosis, Child-Pugh class B or C liver function impairment, or acute liver failure. Liver function at screening revealed any of the following: serum alanine aminotransferase >3× the upper limit of the normal (ULN); serum aspartate aminotransferase >3×ULN; serum bilirubin >2×ULN; (iii) undergoing dialysis treatment or known to have moderate to severe renal impairment, i.e., an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Values were calculated on the basis of the serum creatinine concentration via the CKD-EPI formula: ≤45 mL/min/1.73 m2 within 6 months before screening; (iv) have moderate to severe congestive heart failure within 6 months before screening according to the New York Heart Association classification criteria, with cardiac function class III or IV; have experienced a stroke, myocardial infarction, or coronary artery stent implantation; or have uncontrolled hypertension defined as systolic blood pressure exceeding 160 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure exceeding 100 mm Hg, with relevant records. Significant or clinically relevant electrocardiogram abnormalities, such as second-degree-type II atrioventricular block, left bundle branch block, etc.; (v) have suffered from influenza A, influenza B, or other infectious diseases within 3 months before screening; (vi) have/ever had severe neurological diseases such as epilepsy, convulsions, or seizures. or mental illness; vii) pregnant or breastfeeding; (viii) have a history of malignant tumors within 5 years before screening, with existing and progressing tumors, and patients who are expected to need treatment during the study period; (ix) have chronic fatigue syndrome or autoimmune diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, etc.; (x) allergy to any component of the study drug; (xi) have diseases judged by the investigator to be unsuitable for participation in this study; (xii) take traditional Chinese medicine containing Codonopsis pilosula, Ophiopogon japonicus, Schisandra chinensis, or Astragalus membranaceus within 2 weeks before screening; and (xiii) participate in other drug- or medical device-related studies at the same time [23]. Participants are allowed to withdraw at any time. If they do, they will be asked to complete an assessment of the relevant outcome measures.

Intervention Measures

The participants in the intervention group will consume Shengmai liquid (Jilin Aodong Yanbian Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 10 mL/bottle, Z41021384) 10 mL three times daily for 56 days. The ingredients of the mixture included Codonopsis, Ophiopogon and Schisandra, and the ingredients included sucrose, sodium benzoate, and ethylparaben. The participants in the control group will also consume placebo liquid three times daily (10 mL each time) for 56 days.

There were no concomitant drugs or therapies used in the interventions in this study. Any medication taken by the subjects as necessary must be recorded in the case report form (see online supplementary Material 2).

Outcome Measures

We will use MFIS as the primary outcome. The MFIS scale consists of 21 questions that can be used to quantify the severity of fatigue effectively and its impact on daily life. It will be assessed at the following time points: before the first treatment, 15 ± 1 days, 30 ± 2 days, 60 ± 4 days after trial commencement, and 30 ± 4 days after stopping medication (see Fig. 1).

The study design.

We will record the secondary outcomes (including improvements in fatigue symptoms, TCM syndrome, depression, anxiety, sleep quality, and quality of life) at 0–2 days, 30 ± 2 days, and 60 ± 4 days during treatment. A list of the secondary outcome measures is shown in Table 1. The TCM syndrome score is included as a secondary outcome to evaluate the improvement of the “Qi and Yin Deficiency” pattern, which is the direct therapeutic target of Shengmai liquid. This assessment aims to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of symptom resolution beyond single-dimensional fatigue scales. The scoring system focuses on the reduction in severity of a cluster of symptoms (e.g., lassitude, shortness of breath, and dry mouth), thereby enhancing the clinical credibility and mechanistic alignment of the trial results.

Statistical Analysis

This study uses a superiority test. The expected between-group difference in the primary outcome score was 6 points [24]. With a significance level of 0.05, 80% power, and a 10% dropout rate [25]. The total sample size was estimated as 100 subjects via PASS 2021 Version 21.0.3 (NCSS, LLC) [24, 25].

The predesigned case report form is used to record and collect raw data (see online Supplementary Material 2). Data entry and verification followed standard procedures with two independent entries. The participants’ records and raw data will be stored according to hospital policies. Only authorized personnel can access the stored data.

All the statistical analyses will use SPSS software (International Business Machines Corporation, IBM) [22] with a 0.05 significance level and follow the intention-to-treat principle. The reasons for missing data were documented. Multiple imputation will handle missing quantitative data. No interim analysis is planned. No subgroup analysis was performed.

Three analysis sets will be used: the full analysis set (FAS, which includes all screened subjects receiving at least one dose of study treatment), the per protocol set (which includes subjects completing all study procedures per protocol with 80–120% medication adherence) and the safety set (which includes all subjects receiving at least one treatment dose with posttreatment safety data). Baseline data will be analyzed via the FAS. Analysis of primary outcome measures will analyze both the FAS and per protocol set, whereas the secondary outcomes will use the FAS only. Safety analyses will use the safety set, including subjects who discontinued early due to adverse events or other reasons.

For continuous variables related to participant baseline characteristics, such as age, height, weight, and vital signs, we will perform independent samples t tests or Mann-Whitney U tests on the basis of whether the data are normally distributed. For participant sex, the chi-square test will be used. For the primary outcome and secondary outcomes, we will perform independent samples t tests or Mann-Whitney U tests according to whether the data are normally distributed. For within-group comparisons across multiple time points, we will apply repeated-measures ANOVA to assess changes before and after treatment.

For safety outcomes, including body temperature, respiratory rate, heart rate, blood pressure, pulse oximetry, routine blood/urine tests, and blood biochemistry, we will use chi-square tests for between-group comparisons. For adverse event rates, we will also employ chi-square tests for between-group comparisons.

Discussion

Currently, many patients suffer from fatigue and weakness due to post-COVID syndrome [26, 27], yet safe and effective treatment options remain limited [28]. The hypothesis of this study is that Shengmai liquid has good safety in the treatment of fatigue after COVID-19, with minimal adverse reactions [29–33]. If its efficacy can be confirmed, it would provide patients with a safe and readily available treatment option.

This study will enroll participants aged 18–75 years to ensure broader population representation [22]. A completely randomized, double-blind design will be employed to enhance reliability. Multiple outcome measures will be implemented, including the MFIS, TCM symptom score, WHOQOL, and 6-min walk distance, complemented by laboratory tests (complete blood count and biochemical markers). This comprehensive approach allows thorough evaluation of participants’ overall health status. A key strength is the extended follow-up period, with the use of the MFIS to assess fatigue levels 1 month posttreatment to evaluate Shengmai liquid's short-term effects.

The research team comprises experts in epidemiology, evidence-based medicine, statistics, and clinical practice, ensuring scientific rigor. However, limitations exist, as all participants will be recruited from Beijing Ditan Hospital, potentially affecting generalizability. While fatigue severity varies among post-COVID-19 patients, dose adjustments were not implemented due to small subgroup sample sizes. Future multicenter studies with larger samples are warranted. Although the sample size is limited, we will publish key findings in open-access, peer-reviewed journals. The results will also be shared through the Clinical Trial Registry. We hope to provide valuable clinical evidence for interventions to prevent fatigue after COVID-19.

Trial Oversight and Monitoring

To ensure the scientific integrity and participant safety, an independent auditing process will be implemented for this trial. Although a formal Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) is typically reserved for large-scale or high-risk pharmaceutical trials, this study will be subject to regular monitoring and auditing by the Clinical Research Management Center of Beijing University of Chinese Medicine (BUCM). An independent monitor, who is not involved in the daily operations of the trial, will perform source data verification at the beginning, midpoint, and completion of the study. This audit will encompass the informed consent process, adherence to the protocol, and the accuracy of the electronic case report forms against source documents. Additionally, the Institutional Review Board (IRB) reserves the right to conduct unannounced audits to ensure compliance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines.
