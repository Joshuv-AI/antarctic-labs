Berry-Curvature Activation by Orbital Flux in a Kagome Altermagnet
Meysam Bagheri Tagani ID, Carmine Autieri ID

Abstract
We investigate topological electronic responses in a kagome altermagnetic metal hosting a compensated coplanar 120 ∘ 120^{\circ} magnetic texture. Using a minimal tight-binding model incorporating nearest-neighbor hopping, noncollinear exchange coupling, intrinsic spin–orbit coupling, and an emergent orbital chiral flux, we demonstrate that frustrated kagome altermagnets provide a natural platform for realizing momentum-dependent spin splitting and Berry-curvature engineering without net magnetization. The noncollinear exchange field alone generates pronounced altermagnetic spin splitting and spin-polarized Fermi surfaces despite the absence of relativistic effects. However, for a strictly coplanar magnetic state, the system preserves a hidden antiunitary symmetry 𝒯 ​ C 2 ​ z \mathcal{T}C_{2z} , which enforces identically vanishing Berry curvature even in the presence of sizeable spin–orbit coupling. We show that finite Berry curvature emerges only after introducing an orbital chiral flux term that breaks the hidden symmetry and generates effective momentum-space gauge fields analogous to a Haldane-type orbital flux. Remarkably, this mechanism produces local Berry curvature hot spots even in the complete absence of spin–orbit coupling and scalar spin chirality, establishing a purely orbital route toward topological altermagnetism. By systematically analyzing the anomalous Hall conductivity as a function of exchange coupling, spin–orbit interaction, and chiral flux, we identify a hierarchy of competing energy scales governing the transition from a symmetry-protected altermagnetic metal to a topological altermagnetic phase with strong Hall response. Our results demonstrate that frustrated kagome altermagnets constitute a versatile platform for engineering topological transport, Berry curvature, and spin-selective electronic structure in compensated magnetic systems.

II
Model, Formalism, and Symmetry Analysis

We investigate itinerant electrons on a kagome lattice hosting a compensated noncollinear altermagnetic texture.
The minimal tight-binding Hamiltonian is written as

H=H_{t}+H_{J}+H_{\rm SOC}+H_{2}+H_{\chi},

(1)

where the individual terms respectively describe nearest-neighbor hopping, noncollinear exchange coupling, intrinsic spin-orbit coupling (SOC), next-nearest-neighbor hopping, and an emergent chiral flux associated with orbital Berry phases.

II.1
Real-Space Hamiltonian

The nearest-neighbor (NN) hopping term is

H_{t}=-t\sum_{\langle ij\rangle,\alpha}c_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{j\alpha}+{\rm H.c.},

(2)

where

c_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}

creates an electron with spin

\alpha=\uparrow,\downarrow

on site
i
.
This term generates the characteristic kagome electronic structure consisting of Dirac cones and a nearly dispersionless flat band.

Noncollinear altermagnetism is introduced through the exchange coupling

H_{J}=J\sum_{i}c_{i}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{M}_{i}\cdot\bm{\sigma})c_{i},

(3)

where

\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})

denotes the Pauli matrices.
We consider the coplanar
120^{\circ}
magnetic texture for the three sites in the unit cell and atomic magnetization as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{A}

\displaystyle=M(1,0,0),

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{B}

\displaystyle=M\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},0\right),

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{C}

\displaystyle=M\left(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},0\right),

(6)

which satisfies

\mathbf{M}_{A}+\mathbf{M}_{B}+\mathbf{M}_{C}=0.

(7)

The magnetic order is therefore fully compensated and carries no net magnetization despite explicitly breaking time-reversal symmetry
\mathcal{T}
.

Intrinsic SOC is modeled by a Kane–Mele-type term
22
:

H_{\rm SOC}=i\lambda\sum_{\langle\!\langle ij\rangle\!\rangle}\nu_{ij}\,c_{i}^{\dagger}\sigma_{z}c_{j}+{\rm H.c.},

(8)

where

\nu_{ij}=\pm 1

depends on the chirality of the second-neighbor hopping path.
This term preserves lattice translations while generating topological gaps near Dirac crossings.

Particle–hole asymmetry and flat-band dispersion are incorporated through next-nearest-neighbor (NNN) hopping, see Fig. 1(a),

H_{2}=-t_{2}\sum_{\langle\!\langle ij\rangle\!\rangle,\alpha}c_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{j\alpha}+{\rm H.c.}

(9)

Finally, we include an emergent chiral-flux contribution,

H_{\chi}=i\chi\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\nu_{ij}\,c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j}+{\rm H.c.},

(10)

which describes complex hopping processes associated with circulating orbital currents on the kagome lattice. The parameter
\chi
acts as an effective gauge flux and explicitly breaks the hidden antiunitary symmetry
\mathcal{T}C_{2z}
of the coplanar altermagnetic phase. Consequently, finite Berry curvature and anomalous Hall responses can emerge once
\chi
becomes nonzero.

II.2
Momentum-Space Representation

Using the spinor basis

\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}=(c_{\mathbf{k}A\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{k}A\downarrow},c_{\mathbf{k}B\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{k}B\downarrow},c_{\mathbf{k}C\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{k}C\downarrow})^{T},

(11)

the Bloch Hamiltonian becomes

\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{k})=\mathcal{H}_{t}(\mathbf{k})+\mathcal{H}_{2}(\mathbf{k})+\mathcal{H}_{J}+\mathcal{H}_{\rm SOC}(\mathbf{k})+\mathcal{H}_{\chi}(\mathbf{k}).

(12)

The primitive lattice vectors are chosen as

\mathbf{a}_{1}=a(1,0),\qquad\mathbf{a}_{2}=a\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right),

(13)

together with

\mathbf{a}_{3}=\mathbf{a}_{1}-\mathbf{a}_{2}.

(14)

Defining

k_{n}=\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{n},\qquad n=1,2,3,

(15)

the NN hopping contribution becomes

\mathcal{H}_{t}(\mathbf{k})=-2t\sum_{n=1}^{3}\cos\frac{k_{n}}{2}\,\Lambda_{n}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2}.

(16)

The three sublattice hopping matrices are

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(17)

The intrinsic SOC term takes the form

\mathcal{H}_{\rm SOC}(\mathbf{k})=2\lambda\sum_{n=1}^{3}\sin\frac{k_{n}}{2}\,\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n}\otimes\sigma_{z},

(18)

where

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n}

are antisymmetric Hermitian matrices describing directed hopping:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&i&0\\
-i&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-i\\
0&0&0\\
i&0&0\end{pmatrix},

(19)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}_{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&i\\
0&-i&0\end{pmatrix}.

(20)

Similarly, the chiral-flux contribution becomes

\mathcal{H}_{\chi}(\mathbf{k})=2\chi\sum_{n=1}^{3}\sin\frac{k_{n}}{2}\,\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2}.

(21)

The NNN hopping generates the diagonal dispersion

\mathcal{H}_{2}(\mathbf{k})=-2t_{2}\left[\cos(k_{1})+\cos(k_{2})+\cos(k_{3})\right]\mathbb{I}_{6}.

(22)

Finally, the exchange contribution is momentum independent:

\mathcal{H}_{J}=J\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{x}&0&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{x}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sigma_{y}&0\\
0&0&-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{x}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sigma_{y}\end{pmatrix}.

(23)

II.3
Magnetic Symmetry and Altermagnetic Spin Splitting

In the following, we set
t=1
as the energy unit. In the absence of magnetic order, the kagome lattice belongs to the hexagonal space group

P6/mmm

with the point group

D_{6h}
.
The coplanar
120^{\circ}
magnetic texture lowers the symmetry while preserving lattice translations and threefold rotational symmetry
C_{3z}
.

The magnetic state explicitly breaks time-reversal symmetry
\mathcal{T}
and generally removes antiunitary symmetries capable of enforcing Kramers spin degeneracy. Consequently, the electronic bands exhibit momentum-dependent spin splitting even in the absence of SOC,

E_{n\uparrow}(\mathbf{k})\neq E_{n\downarrow}(\mathbf{k}).

(24)

Unlike conventional ferromagnets, however, the spin splitting does not originate from a net magnetization. Instead, it emerges from the interplay between crystal symmetry and the noncollinear compensated magnetic order. The resulting spin polarization changes sign under crystalline rotations, producing the characteristic anisotropic momentum-space spin texture of altermagnetic systems. The interplay between kagome geometry, noncollinear altermagnetism, and emergent orbital flux therefore provides a minimal symmetry-complete framework for topological strong altermagnetism in frustrated itinerant magnets.

III
Results

Before introducing the altermagnetic exchange field, it is instructive to establish the baseline electronic structure of the kagome lattice in the absence of all spin-dependent terms. By setting
J=\lambda=\chi=0
, the Hamiltonian reduces to a spin-degenerate nearest-neighbor tight-binding model. Diagonalizing the
3\times 3
sublattice block yields two dispersive bands,

E_{\pm}(\mathbf{k})=t\!\left[-1\pm\sqrt{4\cos^{2}\!\frac{k_{1}}{2}+4\cos^{2}\!\frac{k_{2}}{2}+4\cos^{2}\!\frac{k_{3}}{2}}\right],

(25)

together with a perfectly flat band at
E_{\mathrm{flat}}=2t
. As expected for a non-magnetic system preserving both time-reversal (
\mathcal{T}
) and spatial inversion (
\mathcal{I}
) symmetries, all bands remain exactly spin-degenerate, and the total magnetization vanishes identically.

We consider the first Brillouin zone of the system, which is plotted in Fig. 1(b).
The resulting spectrum, shown in Fig. 1(c), exhibits characteristic Dirac touchings at the
K
and
K^{\prime}
points and is fully symmetric under the point group of the hexagonal Brillouin zone, including the mappings
K\!\leftrightarrow\!K^{\prime}
and
M\!\leftrightarrow\!M^{\prime}

To illustrate the effect of realistic band reconstruction, we include a real second-neighbor hopping term
t_{2}
, which contributes a momentum-dependent diagonal shift

\varepsilon_{2}(\mathbf{k})=-2t_{2}\!\left[\cos k_{1}+\cos k_{2}+\cos k_{3}\right].

For
t_{2}=0.1

\Gamma

To isolate the intrinsic effect of the noncollinear exchange field, we first consider the minimal model with

t_{2}=\lambda=\chi=0
,
such that the electronic structure is governed solely by nearest-neighbor hopping and the coplanar
120^{\circ}
exchange texture. Figures 2(a) and 2(b) show the evolution of the kagome band structure for exchange couplings

J=0.2

and

J=0.5
,
respectively.

In the absence of exchange coupling, the kagome lattice exhibits the well-known Dirac bands together with a perfectly flat band at

E=2t
.
Introducing the noncollinear exchange field fundamentally reconstructs this spectrum. Even without relativistic spin–orbit coupling, the magnetic texture lifts the spin degeneracy over large portions of the Brillouin zone, producing strongly momentum-dependent spin splitting. The spin polarization of the bands, represented by the color scale corresponding to

\langle S_{x}\rangle
,
reveals the emergence of oppositely polarized electronic branches despite the absence of any net magnetization.

This behavior originates from the symmetry properties of the coplanar
120^{\circ}
magnetic state. The noncollinear order breaks time-reversal symmetry while preserving lattice translations and threefold rotational symmetry, thereby removing the antiunitary symmetry constraints that would otherwise enforce Kramers-like spin degeneracy. As a consequence, the exchange coupling alone generates anisotropic momentum-space spin splitting characteristic of altermagnetic systems.

A notable feature appears near the
\Gamma
point, where the Dirac-derived bands remain grouped into nearly degenerate pairs, while the flat-band manifold undergoes substantial exchange splitting. Increasing the exchange strength from

J=0.2

to

J=0.5

significantly amplifies the reconstruction of the spectrum. The spin splitting becomes larger throughout the Brillouin zone, and the originally dispersionless flat band evolves into two strongly spin-polarized dispersive branches shifted toward higher and lower energies. Simultaneously, the spin polarization approaches nearly saturated values

\langle S_{x}\rangle\approx\pm 1
,
indicating that the exchange field dominates over the kinetic energy scale.

Importantly, the spin splitting is highly anisotropic in momentum space. Symmetry-related regions such as
M
and
M^{\prime}
exhibit opposite spin polarization, while the magnitude of the splitting varies strongly along different momentum directions. This anisotropic spin-momentum locking distinguishes the present noncollinear phase from conventional ferromagnetic exchange splitting, where the spin separation is approximately momentum independent.

To further characterize the altermagnetic phase, Fig. 2(c) presents the momentum-resolved spin texture

\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle_{\mathbf{k}}

for the third band at

J=0.5
.
The spin texture displays a pronounced noncollinear winding throughout the Brillouin zone, demonstrating that the electronic spin polarization is intrinsically locked to crystal momentum. While the total spin polarization integrated over the Brillouin zone vanishes identically due to magnetic compensation, the local spin expectation values remain finite and rotate continuously around the high-symmetry points. In particular, the spin polarization is strongly suppressed near the
\Gamma
point but becomes enhanced near the zone boundaries, where the exchange-induced band reconstruction is maximal. The resulting momentum-space spin pattern provides direct evidence for nonrelativistic altermagnetic spin splitting generated purely by the noncollinear exchange field.

The influence of this spin splitting on the itinerant electronic states is further illustrated in Fig. 2(d), which shows the Fermi surface projected onto the

\langle S_{x}\rangle

component for

J=0.5
.
The Fermi surface consists of multiple spin-polarized pockets distributed near the edges of the hexagonal Brillouin zone. Unlike conventional antiferromagnets, where opposite-spin states remain degenerate at each momentum point, the present system exhibits strongly spin-polarized Fermi contours with alternating positive and negative spin character. The local spin polarization changes sign between symmetry-related momentum sectors, reflecting the underlying
120^{\circ}
magnetic point-group symmetry.

This spin-polarized yet magnetically compensated Fermi surface is a defining hallmark of altermagnetism. The coexistence of vanishing net magnetization and strong momentum-dependent spin polarization suggests that kagome noncollinear altermagnets may provide an efficient platform for spin-current generation, anisotropic magnetotransport, and unconventional Hall responses even in the absence of relativistic spin–orbit coupling.

\chi=0

120^{\circ}
exchange texture and intrinsic

\lambda=0.25

To characterize the momentum-space topology generated by the combined action
of noncollinear altermagnetism and SOC, we evaluate the Berry curvature within
the Kubo linear-response formalism. Since the present system is metallic, the physically relevant Berry curvature is obtained by summing over all occupied
states below the Fermi energy,

\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{n}f\big(E_{n}(\mathbf{k})-E_{F}\big)\,\Omega_{z}^{n}(\mathbf{k}),

(27)

where

f(E)

is the Fermi–Dirac distribution and

E_{F}

denotes the Fermi level.

The band-resolved Berry curvature is computed using

\Omega_{z}^{n}(\mathbf{k})=-2\,\mathrm{Im}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\langle n|v_{x}|m\rangle\langle m|v_{y}|n\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{m})^{2}+\eta^{2}},

(28)

where

v_{\mu}=\partial H/\partial k_{\mu}

is the velocity operator and

\eta=0.03

is a small numerical broadening introduced to regularize near-degenerate
states. The velocity operators are evaluated numerically using finite
momentum derivatives of the Bloch Hamiltonian.

A remarkable and highly nontrivial feature of the coplanar kagome
altermagnetic phase is such that the Berry curvature remains identically zero
throughout the Brillouin zone, even in the simultaneous presence of
noncollinear magnetic order and intrinsic SOC, as shown in Fig. 3(b).
At first sight, this result appears counterintuitive because the exchange field
explicitly breaks ordinary time-reversal symmetry and produces pronounced
momentum-dependent spin splitting. Nevertheless, the vanishing Berry curvature originates from a hidden antiunitary symmetry that survives in the
strictly coplanar magnetic state.

The essential observation is that all magnetic moments
\mathbf{M}_{i}
, where
i
is the index of the atoms, remain confined to the kagome plane,

\mathbf{M}_{i}=(M_{i}^{x},M_{i}^{y},0),

(29)

with no out-of-plane spin component. Under the time-reversal operator,

\mathcal{T}:\quad\mathbf{M}_{i}\rightarrow-\mathbf{M}_{i},

(30)

the magnetic texture changes sign. However, the transformed configuration can
be exactly restored by a global
\pi
spin rotation around the
z
axis,

C_{2z}:\quad(M_{x},M_{y},M_{z})\rightarrow(-M_{x},-M_{y},M_{z}).

(31)

Consequently, the combined antiunitary operation

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}C_{2z}

(32)

leaves the full Hamiltonian invariant even in the presence of intrinsic SOC.
The coplanar altermagnetic state therefore retains an effective antiunitary
symmetry despite explicitly breaking conventional time-reversal symmetry.

This hidden symmetry imposes a stringent constraint on the Berry curvature.
Under time reversal,

\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k})\rightarrow-\Omega_{z}(-\mathbf{k}),

(33)

whereas the twofold spin rotation
C_{2z}
transforms momentum according to

(k_{x},k_{y})\rightarrow(-k_{x},-k_{y}),

(34)

while leaving the out-of-plane Berry curvature component unchanged,

\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k})\rightarrow\Omega_{z}(-\mathbf{k}).

(35)

Combining both operations yields

\mathcal{T}C_{2z}:\quad\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k})\rightarrow-\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k}).

(36)

Since the Hamiltonian is invariant under
\mathcal{T}C_{2z}
, the Berry
curvature must satisfy

\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k})=-\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k}),

(37)

which immediately enforces

\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k})=0

(38)

for all crystal momenta.

From a geometric perspective, this result is directly connected to the
vanishing scalar spin chirality of the coplanar
120^{\circ}
texture,

\kappa_{ijk}=\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot(\mathbf{S}_{j}\times\mathbf{S}_{k}).

(39)

Because all spins lie within the same plane,

\kappa_{ijk}=0,

(40)

no spin-chirality-induced emergent magnetic field is generated.
The coplanar kagome altermagnet therefore realizes an unusual electronic phase in which strong
momentum-dependent spin splitting coexists with a completely vanishing Berry
curvature and anomalous Hall response.

This behavior sharply distinguishes the present system from conventional
noncollinear kagome antiferromagnets
35
,
where spin canting or noncoplanarity breaks the hidden

\mathcal{T}C_{2z}
symmetry and immediately generates large Berry-curvature
hot spots and anomalous Hall conductivity. In contrast, the ideal coplanar
altermagnetic state studied here remains topologically compensated even in the
presence of sizeable SOC. The system, therefore, constitutes a symmetry-protected altermagnetic metal: it exhibits giant nonrelativistic spin
splitting while remaining completely Berry-curvature silent.

mechanism capable of breaking the hidden
\mathcal{T}C_{2z}
symmetry. In the
present model, this role is naturally played by the chiral flux term

H_{\chi}
, which mimics interaction-driven orbital circulation and generates an
effective momentum-space magnetic flux. Once
\chi\neq 0
, the antiunitary
compensation is destroyed, allowing Berry-curvature hot spots and anomalous
Hall transport to emerge. This establishes a clear hierarchy of electronic
phases in the kagome altermagnet: a symmetry-protected coplanar
altermagnetic metal for
\chi=0
, and a topological altermagnetic metal once
orbital chiral currents are activated.

Figure 3(c) further demonstrates that intrinsic SOC does not induce any
appreciable out-of-plane spin polarization in the coplanar altermagnetic
phase. Although SOC modifies the in-plane spin texture and slightly reshapes
the momentum dependence of

\langle S_{x}(\mathbf{k})\rangle

and

\langle S_{y}(\mathbf{k})\rangle
,
the out-of-plane component remains strictly zero within numerical precision.
This behavior again follows directly from the hidden antiunitary symmetry

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}C_{2z}
. Since the operator
S_{z}
is odd under

\mathcal{S}
while the Hamiltonian remains invariant, the expectation value
must satisfy

\langle S_{z}(\mathbf{k})\rangle=-\langle S_{z}(\mathbf{k})\rangle,

(41)

thereby enforcing

\langle S_{z}(\mathbf{k})\rangle=0

(42)

throughout the Brillouin zone. Thus, the intrinsic SOC does not produce a canting of the spins out of the kagome plane as long as the chiral flux term remains absent.

Finally, Fig. 3(d) compares the Fermi surface in the presence of SOC with the
SOC-free case shown in Fig. 2(d). The altermagnetic exchange field already
produces strongly spin-polarized Fermi pockets, while SOC slightly distorts
their geometry and enhances the momentum dependence of the in-plane spin
texture. Importantly, however, the spin polarization remains strictly
coplanar and no out-of-plane spin canting develops. The survival of the
hidden antiunitary symmetry
\mathcal{T}C_{2z}
therefore protects the system
against both finite Berry curvature and SOC-induced out-of-plane spin
polarization, leaving the kagome altermagnet in a symmetry-protected
topologically compensated metallic state.

We now turn to the regime

J\neq 0,\qquad\chi\neq 0,\qquad\lambda=0,\qquad t_{2}=0,

(43)

where the electronic structure is governed solely by nearest-neighbor hopping,
the coplanar
120^{\circ}
altermagnetic exchange texture, and the emergent
chiral flux term
H_{\chi}
. In this limit, the Hamiltonian reduces to

H=H_{t}+H_{J}+H_{\chi}.

(44)

Unlike the SOC-driven case discussed in Fig. 3, the present phase is entirely
nonrelativistic: all topological effects originate purely from orbital gauge
structure rather than spin–orbit entanglement. It should be emphasized that the orbital-flux term
H_{\chi}
is independent of the scalar spin chirality of the magnetic texture. Throughout this work, the local moments remain strictly coplanar and therefore satisfy
\kappa_{ijk}=0
. The role of
H_{\chi}
is instead to introduce an orbital gauge structure that explicitly breaks the hidden antiunitary symmetry
\mathcal{T}C_{2z}
responsible for the Berry-curvature silence of the coplanar altermagnetic phase.

A central question is whether finite Berry curvature can emerge in the absence
of both relativistic SOC and noncoplanar spin chirality. At first sight, one
might expect the answer to be negative because the magnetic texture remains
strictly coplanar,

\kappa_{ijk}=\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot(\mathbf{S}_{j}\times\mathbf{S}_{k})=0,

(45)

and therefore no conventional emergent magnetic field associated with scalar
spin chirality is generated. Remarkably, however, we find that the chiral
orbital flux term alone is sufficient to produce pronounced Berry curvature
hot spots throughout momentum space, even in the complete absence of SOC.
Figure 4 demonstrates that the resulting phase constitutes a genuine
nonrelativistic topological altermagnetic metal driven entirely by orbital
circulation effects.

The origin of the finite Berry curvature can be understood from symmetry.
For
\chi=0
, the coplanar kagome altermagnet preserves the hidden
antiunitary symmetry

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}C_{2z},

(46)

which was shown in Fig. 3 to enforce

\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k})=0

(47)

identically throughout the Brillouin zone. The chiral flux term explicitly
breaks this protection.

Under time reversal,

\mathcal{T}H_{\chi}\mathcal{T}^{-1}=-H_{\chi},

(48)

because the hopping amplitudes carry purely imaginary phases. Meanwhile, the
twofold spin rotation
C_{2z}
reverses the in-plane magnetic moments but does
not remove the oriented bond chirality encoded in the kagome flux factors

\nu_{ij}
. Consequently,

\mathcal{S}H_{\chi}\mathcal{S}^{-1}\neq H_{\chi},

(49)

and the antiunitary compensation responsible for the Berry-curvature silence
of Fig. 3 is destroyed. Once
\chi\neq 0
, no remaining symmetry enforces

\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k})=-\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k}),

(50)

allowing finite local Berry curvature to emerge.

The physical meaning of
H_{\chi}
becomes particularly transparent when viewed
as an emergent orbital gauge field. Electrons moving around an elementary
kagome triangle accumulate a geometric phase

\Phi_{\triangle}=\arg\!\left(t_{AB}\,t_{BC}\,t_{CA}\right),

(51)

where the directed complex hopping amplitudes are

t_{AB}=t+i\chi\,\nu_{AB},~t_{BC}=t+i\chi\,\nu_{BC},~t_{CA}=t+i\chi\,\nu_{CA}.

(52)

To leading order in
\chi/t
, the accumulated phase is

\Phi_{\triangle}\approx\frac{\chi}{t}

demonstrating that the chiral term generates an effective staggered orbital
flux through the kagome plaquettes. In this sense,
H_{\chi}
plays a role
analogous to a Haldane-type gauge field on the kagome lattice, producing local
orbital circulation without requiring either relativistic SOC or noncoplanar
spin textures.

[\mathcal{H}_{J},\mathcal{H}_{\chi}(\mathbf{k})]\neq 0,

The Bloch eigenstates, therefore, acquire a momentum-dependent complex

To leading order in
\chi

\Omega_{z}^{n}(\mathbf{k})\propto\chi,

(55)

with matrix elements controlled by the interference between the velocity

H_{\chi}
. The exchange field lifts the
relevant band degeneracies, allowing the orbital flux to generate finite
momentum-space Berry curvature even in the absence of relativistic SOC.

The electronic reconstruction induced by the chiral orbital flux is shown in

primarily opens relativistic hybridization gaps near Dirac crossings, the

complex inter-sublattice hopping processes. The resulting bands remain
strongly spin split due to the underlying altermagnetic exchange field, but
their topology is now governed by orbital circulation rather than by
spin–orbit entanglement.

In particular, the chiral flux reconstructs the flat-band manifold and shifts
the avoided crossings near the
K

strong interband mixing. The color projection of

\langle S_{x}\rangle

demonstrates that the altermagnetic spin polarization survives throughout the Brillouin zone, despite the absence of SOC, confirming that the spin splitting
remains fundamentally nonrelativistic.

hidden
\mathcal{T}C_{2z}
symmetry enforced

\Omega_{z}(\mathbf{k})=0,

(56)

\lambda

To quantify the global topological response, Fig. 4(c) shows the anomalous

The near cancellation of the Hall conductivity originates from the compensated

Although the chiral flux breaks the hidden antiunitary symmetry responsible
for the Berry-curvature silence of Fig. 3, substantial momentum-space
compensation still survives in the purely nonrelativistic limit. Once SOC is
introduced, however, this residual compensation is lifted, leading to a rapid
growth of the anomalous Hall conductivity. The combined action of orbital
flux and SOC therefore converts the system from a locally topological but
globally compensated metal into a genuine anomalous Hall altermagnet.

Figure 4(d) further illustrates the momentum-space topology by projecting the

to hidden Berry-curvature phases discussed in valley Hall systems.

Unlike conventional noncollinear kagome antiferromagnets
where Berry curvature typically originates from relativistic SOC or weak
spin canting that generates finite scalar spin chirality, the present kagome
altermagnet realizes a fundamentally different mechanism. Here the topology
emerges entirely from orbital flux degrees of freedom within a strictly
coplanar and nonrelativistic magnetic background. The resulting state
therefore constitutes a distinct topological altermagnetic phase in which
momentum-dependent spin splitting, orbital gauge flux, and frustrated kagome geometry cooperate to generate Berry curvature without relying on either SOC
or noncoplanar magnetism.

Overall, Fig. 4 establishes a new hierarchy of topological responses in kagome
altermagnets. Figure 3 demonstrated that a coplanar altermagnetic metal with
SOC can remain completely Berry silent due to hidden antiunitary symmetry.
Figure 4 shows that orbital chiral flux destroys this protection and generates
pronounced momentum-space topology even in the absence of SOC. The kagome
altermagnet therefore provides an unusual platform in which relativistic and
orbital mechanisms for Berry curvature can be disentangled and independently
controlled.

IV
Discussion

The results presented above establish a minimal microscopic framework for
Berry curvature in noncollinear altermagnetism on the kagome lattice.
The central finding of this work is that momentum-dependent spin splitting, Berry curvature
generation, and anomalous Hall transport originate from three distinct physical
mechanisms associated with the exchange field
J
, intrinsic spin–orbit
coupling
\lambda
, and orbital chiral flux
\chi
, respectively. Unlike
conventional magnetic kagome systems, where these effects are typically
entangled, the present model disentangles their individual roles and thereby
reveals a clear hierarchy of symmetry-breaking processes governing the
electronic topology.

A concise summary of the different electronic regimes obtained in the present
model is provided in Table I. In the absence of the chiral flux term, the
coplanar
120^{\circ}
magnetic texture preserves the hidden antiunitary symmetry

\mathcal{T}C_{2z}
, which protects the system from developing Berry curvature
despite the existence of strong altermagnetic spin splitting. Consequently,
both the purely exchange-driven phase and the SOC-renormalized coplanar phase
remain anomalous-Hall inactive. Once the orbital chiral flux
\chi
becomes
finite, however, the hidden antiunitary symmetry is explicitly broken and
momentum-space Berry curvature emerges immediately. Nevertheless, our results
demonstrate that finite local Berry curvature alone does not necessarily imply a large integrated Hall response. In the absence of SOC, substantial momentum-space compensation remains, suppressing the net Hall conductivity even though pronounced Berry-curvature hot spots appear throughout the Brillouin zone. Intrinsic spin–orbit coupling, therefore, acts primarily as an amplifier of the Hall response rather than as its fundamental origin.

Recently, it was shown that it is possible to disentangle the ferromagnetic contribution from the zero-magnetization contribution to the anomalous Hall effect.
9
;
6

The present model also confirms that finite Berry curvature and anomalous Hall conductivity are not always associated with net magnetization (due to the spin canting) and scalar spin chirality. Indeed, the kagome altermagnet studied here demonstrates that Berry curvature can emerge even for a strictly coplanar magnetic texture with vanishing scalar spin chirality,
provided an orbital chiral flux is present. The anomalous Hall response, therefore, does not require noncoplanar magnetism and can instead originate from purely orbital gauge structure encoded in the electronic hopping amplitudes.

This distinction is particularly important because it separates the notions of
spin chirality and orbital chirality. In most previous studies of
noncollinear kagome antiferromagnets, these two effects appear simultaneously
and are therefore difficult to disentangle experimentally and theoretically.
In the present model, however, the scalar spin chirality remains exactly zero
throughout the coplanar phase,

\kappa_{ijk}=\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot(\mathbf{S}_{j}\times\mathbf{S}_{k})=0,

(57)

while the orbital flux term
H_{\chi}
independently generates a finite gauge
flux in momentum space. The resulting Berry curvature is therefore of purely
orbital origin and survives even in the complete absence of relativistic spin
physics. This mechanism represents a qualitatively different route toward
topological Hall transport in frustrated magnets.

Although the present work focused primarily on the minimal nearest-neighbor
model, the next-nearest-neighbor hopping parameter
t_{2}
is expected to play an important role in realistic kagome materials. A finite
t_{2}
breaks
particle–hole symmetry, introduces dispersion into the flat band, and shifts
the relative energetic position of the Dirac crossings and van Hove
singularities. Consequently,
t_{2}
can strongly modify the distribution of
Berry-curvature hot spots near the Fermi level and may substantially enhance or
suppress the anomalous Hall conductivity depending on the filling. In
particular, because the Hall response is highly sensitive to avoided crossings
and near-degenerate band manifolds, even moderate values of
t_{2}
could produce
significant changes in the topology of the reconstructed Fermi surface.
Systematic exploration of the
(t_{2},J,\lambda,\chi)
parameter space therefore
represents an important direction for future work.

The relevance of the present model extends beyond an idealized theoretical
construction. A growing number of kagome materials exhibit noncollinear
magnetic order, strong electronic frustration, and anomalous transport
responses, including Mn
3
Sn, Mn
3
Ge, Fe
3
Sn
2
, Co
3
Sn
2
S
2
, and
related kagome metals. Recently, the growth conditions required to obtain the cubic Mn
3
Ge phase with a layered kagome structure were established
21
. In addition, recent experimental advances in
altermagnetic materials suggest that momentum-dependent spin splitting can
persist even in fully compensated magnetic phases. The coexistence of
altermagnetic spin polarization and orbital-flux-driven Berry curvature
therefore provides a realistic route toward designing compensated magnetic
materials with large spin-selective transport responses and minimal stray
fields.

From a broader perspective, the present results demonstrate that frustrated
altermagnets constitute a natural platform for engineering quantum geometry
through lattice symmetry and magnetic texture alone. The coexistence of flat
bands, Dirac singularities, orbital gauge fields, and compensated
momentum-dependent spin splitting creates a remarkably versatile setting in
which topology can emerge independently of conventional ferromagnetism. This
opens several promising directions for future research, including interaction
effects in partially filled flat bands, nonlinear Hall transport,
Berry-curvature-driven orbital magnetization, topological superconductivity,
and nonequilibrium spin-current generation in compensated kagome magnets.

Overall, the kagome altermagnetic metal studied here reveals a fundamentally
new mechanism for generating topological electronic responses in noncollinear
magnets. The combination of compensated altermagnetic spin splitting,
symmetry-controlled Berry-curvature activation, and orbital-flux-driven Hall
transport establishes frustrated kagome altermagnets as a distinct and highly
tunable class of topological quantum matter.
