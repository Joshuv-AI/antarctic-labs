A POS-tagged dataset of Uzbek word lemmas and stems for morphological and NLP research
Authors: Maksud Sharipov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis article presents a comprehensive, multi-domain dataset of stems and lemmas for the Uzbek language, systematically categorized by parts-of-speech (POS). The dataset contains 46,307 unique stems and 64,052 corresponding lemmas classified into 12 distinct grammatical categories, including independent, auxiliary, and intermediate word classes. Data were methodically extracted from authoritative sources such as the Explanatory Dictionary of the Uzbek Language and various digital text corpora. Following a rigorous right-to-left suffix stripping methodology, words were segmented to isolate the base stems, and their corresponding canonical dictionary forms (lemmas) were identified. The finalized linguistic data is structured in a machine-readable tabular (.xlsx) format consisting of three essential columns: stem, lemmas, and POS tag. This dataset provides a foundational open-source resource for natural language processing (NLP) applications in the morphologically complex, agglutinative Uzbek language, actively supporting the development, training, and benchmarking of rule-based and machine-learning tools such as lemmatizers, stemmers, and POS taggers.Keywords: Uzbek, Inflexion, Sound change, Lemma, Lemmatization, Stem, Stemming, Linguistic supply

Background

The Uzbek language is a widely spoken agglutinative language characterized by a highly complex morphology, where new words and grammatical forms are created by attaching multiple suffixes to a stem. Despite its linguistic richness, Uzbek is still considered a low-resource language in the domain of Natural Language Processing (NLP) [6]. Fundamental text processing tasks, such as lemmatization and stemming, rely heavily on mapping inflected word forms to their dictionary forms (lemmas) or unchangeable stems [2]. However, existing morphological tools for Uzbek frequently depend on constrained lexicons or limited rules, reducing their overall applicability and accuracy [1].

The primary motivation for compiling this dataset was to address this resource gap by systematically curating a large-scale, multi-POS repository of Uzbek stems and lemmas. By isolating 46,307 stems and 64,052 lemmas into 12 grammatical categories, this dataset provides an essential foundational framework. It is specifically designed to function as a comprehensive linguistic database and ground-truth lexicon to support dictionary-based, rule-based, and AI-driven morphological analysis algorithms, ultimately accelerating the advancement of computational linguistics for the Uzbek language.

While the foundational lexical items are derived from existing secondary sources, the novel contribution of this work lies in the extensive data transformation—specifically, the rigorous suffix stripping, orthographic normalization, and structured alignment into a newly accessible, machine-readable format.

Data Description

The dataset provides a comprehensive, POS-tagged collection of Uzbek stems and lemmas. The main directory of the dataset, named stem_lemma_pos, is structured into sub-categories based on the three fundamental word super-classes in the Uzbek language:

•mustaqil (Independent): Contains data for independent or content word classes (Noun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb, Numeral, Pronoun).

•yordamchi (Auxiliary): Contains data for auxiliary or function word classes (Auxiliary, Conjunction, Particle).

yordamchi (Auxiliary): Contains data for auxiliary or function word classes (Auxiliary, Conjunction, Particle).

•oraliq (Intermediate): Contains data for intermediate word classes (Modal, Interjection, Imitation).

oraliq (Intermediate): Contains data for intermediate word classes (Modal, Interjection, Imitation).

The data is provided in a tabular format as an Excel (.xlsx) file, encoded in UTF-8 to ensure full compatibility with the Uzbek Latin alphabet. The dataset is complete, meaning there are no missing values; every stem is mapped to at least one corresponding lemma and possesses exactly one POS tag. The dataset is strictly organized into three columns to ensure machine-readability and ease of use in NLP pipelines:

•Column 1 (Stem): Represents the base segment or stem isolated from the words. Each row contains exactly one unique stem.

•Column 2 (Lemmas): Contains a list of dictionary headwords (lemmas) that correspond to the stem. A single stem can be associated with multiple lemmas (representing derivational variants and polysemy), which are separated by commas within the same cell. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The initial lexical inventory was compiled from authoritative Uzbek language references, yielding a raw, uncleaned total of 72,140 headwords and inflected forms—prior to any deduplication or normalization—extracted primarily from the Explanatory Dictionary of the Uzbek Language (EDUL)1. This was supplemented by carefully curated digital text sources to ensure broad coverage of contemporary usage across multiple domains. All extracted strings were normalized to a consistent Uzbek Latin (UZ-Latin) orthography, and duplicate entries were systematically removed. Each lexical item was subsequently assigned one of twelve Part-of-Speech (POS) tags, which were further grouped into three super-classes: Independent (Noun, Adjective, Verb, Adverb, Numeral, Pronoun), Auxiliary (Auxiliary, Conjunction, Particle), and Intermediate (Modal, Interjection, Imitation).

POS annotation and morphological feature assignment were conducted using a compact, Universal Dependencies (UD)-compatible tagset tailored to the agglutinative morphology of the Uzbek language. The morphological decomposition followed a rigorous right-to-left segmentation approach (suffix stripping). Starting from the right edge of each word, grammatical units such as inflectional suffixes and derivational endings were sequentially identified. A specific notation policy was enforced during this phase: segments were separated by backslashes, while a trailing forward slash (/) was used exclusively as a terminal delimiter to mark the absolute word boundary. The forward slash does not represent a morphological segment itself (e.g., MORPH1\MORPH2.../). Furthermore, rare inherited or borrowed prefixes were explicitly flagged with an asterisk (*) before the segmented chain, reserving this symbol strictly for prefix signaling (e.g., *prefix\stem.../). It is important to emphasize that this specific notation policy was strictly an intermediate mechanism utilized during the morphological decomposition phase to accurately track morpheme boundaries. Once the base forms were successfully isolated and verified, all segmentation markers (backslashes, trailing slashes, and asterisks) were computationally removed.

Following segmentation, the stem (the morphologically reduced base) was isolated. These stems reflect the complex morphotactics of the Uzbek language and may occasionally differ from the standard citation form. Concurrently, the canonical dictionary headword representing the semantic core was recorded as the lemma. In instances where multiple related lemmas corresponded to a single surface form, they were documented as an ordered, comma-separated list within the dataset.

To accelerate the annotation process while maintaining high reliability, trained human annotators were assisted by rule-based candidate analyses generated from grammatical rules and a growing lexicon of validated lemma-stem pairs. Affixes were strictly categorized into inflectional and derivational groups, with the order of affixation explicitly preserving the sequential morpheme attachment typical of Turkic languages. Ambiguous cases, such as word forms with multiple plausible POS categories or competing lemma assignments, were resolved through consensus among multiple expert reviewers. The annotation team consisted of two primary annotators and one senior expert reviewer. To ensure the reliability of the manual annotation before the consensus phase, an inter-annotator agreement (IAA) analysis was conducted on a random sample of 5% of the dataset, yielding a Cohen’s Kappa coefficient of 0.84 for POS tagging and 0.81 for lemma assignment, indicating a high level of annotator consistency. Also, the raw absolute agreement rate between annotators was 88.5% for POS tags and 86.2% for lemma assignments across the stratified sample, confirming robust consensus even within low-frequency grammatical categories. Furthermore, the 5% random sample utilized for the Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA) analysis was strictly stratified by POS category. This stratification ensured that low-frequency grammatical classes, such as Interjections and Particles, were proportionately evaluated alongside high-frequency categories like Nouns and Verbs.

Finally, the resource assembly underwent stringent quality control. This included automated regex-based checks for segmentation patterns, duplicate detection, and tag validity. Frequency-based audits were conducted to identify systematic labeling drift, and targeted manual re-evaluations were performed on rare or highly complex morphological patterns to ensure dataset-wide consistency across the full vocabulary.

Limitations

Although the dataset provides extensive coverage of Uzbek stems and lemmas across 12 part-of-speech categories, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the resource is lexicon-based and primarily derived from dictionary entries and curated textual sources; therefore, it may not fully represent newly emerging vocabulary, neologisms, domain-specific terminology, or informal language usage found in social media and conversational texts. Second, while the dataset records stem–lemma relationships and POS categories, it does not include complete morphological feature annotations (e.g., case, number, tense, person, or mood) for individual word forms. Third, due to the rich derivational morphology of Uzbek, certain stems may correspond to multiple semantically related lemmas, and although expert review was employed to ensure consistency, some ambiguity may remain in rare linguistic cases. Finally, the dataset is limited to Uzbek written in the Latin script and does not explicitly cover Cyrillic orthographic variants.

Despite these limitations, the resource constitutes one of the largest openly available collections of Uzbek stems and lemmas and provides a valuable foundation for morphological analysis, lemmatization, stemming, and broader NLP research.

Ethics Statement

The author confirms that the current work does not involve human subjects, animal experiments, or any data collected from social media platforms. Furthermore, no surveys, questionnaires, or primary empirical elicitation methods were employed in the creation of this dataset. All the lexical resources were gathered from publicly available linguistic texts and dictionaries.
