Steady compressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with general temperature dependent viscosities and hard sphere pressure law
Zhengguang Guo & Milan Pokorný

Abstract
We study the existence theory for steady compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier system in a three dimensional bounded domain for the case of viscosities depending on the temperature in the form ( 1 + ϑ ) α (1+\vartheta)^{\alpha} for 0 ≤ α ≤ 1 0\leq\alpha\leq 1 and the hard sphere pressure p ⁡ ( ϱ , ϑ ) = ϑ ​ ϱ ​ h ​ ( ϱ ) p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\vartheta\varrho h(\varrho) , h ⁡ ( ϱ ) h(\varrho) is increasing and singular at ϱ = a > 0 \varrho=a>0 . This paper considers both the heat-flux and Dirichlet boundary conditions for the temperature and Dirichlet boundary conditions for the velocity. The key point to controlling the pressure is based on some estimates of the Bogovskii operator.

Introduction

We consider the following steady compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier system in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a sufficiently regular boundary (
\Omega\in C^{1,1}
at least)

\displaystyle{\rm div}\,(\varrho{\bf u})

\displaystyle=0

(1)

\displaystyle{\rm div}\,(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})-{\rm div}\,\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\varrho{\bf f}

\displaystyle{\rm div}\,(\varrho{\bf u}E)+{\rm div}\,{\bf q}-{\rm div}\,(\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}{\bf u})+{\rm div}\,(p{\bf u})

\displaystyle=\varrho{\bf f}\cdot{\bf u}+\varrho G.

Here,
\varrho\geq 0
is the density of the fluid,
{\bf u}
is the velocity field,
\mathbb{S}
is the viscous part of the stress tensor, the scalar function
p
is the pressure of the fluid which depends on
\varrho
and thermodynamic temperature
\vartheta>0
, the vector
{\bf q}
is the heat flux and the scalar
E
is the specific total energy,
{\bf f}
and
G
represent the external force and external heat source, respectively. System (
1
) describes the steady flow of the heat-conducting compressible fluid, the problem is closed by the set of boundary conditions. In this paper, we assume that
{\bf u}={\bf u}_{0}
on the boundary and
{\bf q}\cdot{\bf n}=L(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0})
or
\vartheta=\vartheta_{D}
. We further distinguish between
{\bf u}_{0}={\bf 0}
or
{\bf u}_{0}
nonzero. However, in the latter, we always assume that
{\bf u}_{0}\cdot{\bf n}=0
, i.e., there is no inflow/outflow of the fluid through the boundary. These conditions will be described in more detail later.

We also need to prescribe the total mass

\int_{\Omega}\varrho\,{\rm d}x=M>0.

(2)

Note that the value
M
cannot be arbitrary; see (
9
).

We consider the stress tensor in the form

\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}=\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u})=\mu(\vartheta)\Big(\nabla{\bf u}+(\nabla{\bf u})^{T}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}\,{\bf u}\mbox{$\mathbb{I}$}\Big)+\xi(\vartheta){\rm div}\,{\bf u}\mbox{$\mathbb{I}$}.

(3)

The heat flux obeys
Fourier’s law

{\bf q}={\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)=-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta.

(4)

Here,
(\cdot)^{T}
and
\mathbb{I}
denote the transpose of a tensor and the identity matrix, respectively.
We assume that the viscosity coefficients behave in the following way. The shear viscosity

\mu(\vartheta)>0
is a Lipschitz continuous function, the bulk viscosity
\xi(\vartheta)\geq 0
is a continuous function, and there exist constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}(1+\vartheta)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\vartheta)\leq C_{2}(1+\vartheta)^{\alpha}

(5)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\xi(\vartheta)\leq C_{2}(1+\vartheta)^{\alpha},

where the exponent
\alpha\in[0,1]
. The heat conductivity
\kappa(\vartheta)>0
is a continuous function, and there are positive constants
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
such that

C_{3}(1+\vartheta)^{m}\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq C_{4}(1+\vartheta)^{m}

(6)

for a parameter
m>0
. We shall discuss below the choice of the parameters
\alpha
and
m
so that we obtain either weak or variational entropy solutions introduced below.

We consider the pressure in the form

p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\vartheta\varrho h(\varrho),

(7)

where the function
h(\varrho)
is non-decreasing in
[0,a)
with
h(0)=h_{0}>0
, continuous in
[0,a)
and continuously differentiable in
(0,a)
(hence
h^{\prime}(\varrho)\geq 0
here) such that

\lim_{z\to a_{-}}h(z)=\infty.

In fact, all our results remain the same provided we consider

p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\varrho h(\varrho)+\varrho\vartheta.

(8)

Only the forms of the internal energy and the entropy are slightly different. Note that in order to keep
\varrho<a
, we require

M<a|\Omega|.

(9)

The specific total energy reads

E(\varrho,{\bf u},\vartheta)=\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}|^{2}+e(\varrho,\vartheta),

(10)

where the symbol
e
stands for the specific internal energy. Using the fact that the pressure and the internal energy are related by the Gibbs relation

\frac{1}{\vartheta}\Big(De(\varrho,\vartheta)+p(\varrho,\vartheta)D\Big(\frac{1}{\varrho}\Big)\Big)=Ds(\varrho,\vartheta),

(11)

where
s(\varrho,\vartheta)
is the specific entropy, the pressure
p(\varrho,\vartheta)
and the internal energy
e(\varrho,\vartheta)
are
then related by the Maxwell relation

\frac{\partial e(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\varrho}=\frac{1}{\varrho^{2}}\left(p(\varrho,\vartheta)-\vartheta\frac{\partial p(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\vartheta}\right).

(12)

The relative entropy satisfies at least formally the entropy equality

{\rm div}\,\Big(\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\Big)+{\rm div}\,(\varrho s{\bf u})=\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}:\nabla{\bf u}}{\vartheta}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta^{2}}+\frac{\varrho G}{\vartheta}

(13)

Consequently, by (
7
) we get

e(\vartheta,\varrho)=g(\vartheta).

(14)

Often, the relation

g(\vartheta)=c_{v}\vartheta

(15)

is used and this form can be used in the case of the heat flux boundary condition. However, in the case of the Dirichlet boundary condition for the temperature, this form is not suitable. The heat capacity at constant volume typically drops to zero as the temperature approaches absolute zero. For example, an ideal Bose gas has
c_{v}
proportional to
\vartheta^{3/2}
, and the specific heat of an ideal Fermi gas is proportional to
\vartheta
. Moreover, heat capacities at constant volume and constant pressure approach a common value as
\vartheta\rightarrow 0_{+}
, see
[
8
]
. Thus, in some cases, we assume that instead of (
15
)

g(\vartheta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}c_{v}\vartheta&\text{ for }\vartheta>A^{-\frac{1}{b}}\\
c_{v}A\vartheta^{1+b}&\text{ for }0\leq\vartheta\leq A^{-\frac{1}{b}}\end{array}\right.

(16)

with some
A
,
b>0
. If it is preferable, we may mollify this function in some small neighborhood of the value
\vartheta=A^{-\frac{1}{b}}
. By suitable choice of
b
and
A
we may achieve that this form of the internal energy differs from the previous one only in the set
\vartheta\in(0,1)
. The corresponding form of the specific entropy is then determined by

s(\varrho,\vartheta)=c_{v}\ln\vartheta-\int_{\varrho_{M}}^{\varrho}\frac{h(z)}{z}\,{\rm d}z

(17)

for the case (
15
) and

s(\varrho,\vartheta)=\int_{\vartheta_{M}}^{\vartheta}\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{s}\,{\rm d}s-\int_{\varrho_{M}}^{\varrho}\frac{h(z)}{z}\,{\rm d}z

(18)

for the case (
16
). Note that in the latter, for large
\vartheta
the first integral grows logarithmically, while for
\vartheta
positive, close to zero, it behaves like
\vartheta^{b}
and thus remains bounded for
\vartheta\to 0_{+}
.

The first result for the steady compressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier equations for large data goes back to P.L. Lions. The author in

2
Weak formulation, main results

Our definition of weak and variational solutions is based on the following identities and inequalities.

Weak form of the continuity equation:

\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi\,{\rm d}x=0

(24)

for all
\psi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Weak form of the momentum equation:

\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}+p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}\,{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}+\varrho{\bf f}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\Big]\,{\rm d}x=0

(25)

for all
{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

We have to distinguish different situations for the total energy balance. For the zero velocity on the boundary and heat flux boundary condition, we use the following definition.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho\Big(\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}|^{2}+e(\varrho,\vartheta)\Big){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi+p(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi+{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\psi\Big]\,{\rm d}x

(26)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho{\bf f}\cdot{\bf u}+\varrho G\Big)\psi\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\partial\Omega}L(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0})\psi\,{\rm d}S

for all
\psi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

For the zero velocity on the boundary, but Dirichlet boundary condition for the temperature, we consider

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho\Big(\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}|^{2}+e(\varrho,\vartheta)\Big){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi+p(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi+{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\psi\Big]\,{\rm d}x

(27)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho{\bf f}\cdot{\bf u}+\varrho G\Big)\psi\,{\rm d}x=0

for all
\psi\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

For the non-zero velocity (but zero normal trace) on the boundary and the heat flux boundary condition, we need to combine the momentum equation (however, formally with test function
\psi{\bf u}_{0}
) and the “total energy balance” with test function
\psi
,
\psi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
. It reads

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho\Big(\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}|^{2}+e(\varrho,\vartheta)\Big){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi+p(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi+{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\psi\Big]\,{\rm d}x

(28)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\big(\varrho{\bf f}\cdot{\bf u}+\varrho G\big)\psi\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\partial\Omega}L(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0})\psi\,{\rm d}S

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla(\psi{\bf u}_{0})+p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}\,(\psi{\bf u}_{0})-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla(\psi{\bf u}_{0})+\varrho{\bf f}\cdot{\bf u}_{0}\psi\Big]\,{\rm d}x

for all
\psi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

We also distinguish several variants for the entropy inequality. For the zero velocity on the boundary and heat flux boundary condition, we consider

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla{\bf u}}{\vartheta}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta^{2}}+\frac{\varrho G}{\vartheta}\Big)\psi\,{\rm d}x

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{L(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0})}{\vartheta}\psi\,{\rm d}S-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\psi}{\vartheta}+\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi\Big)\,{\rm d}x

for all non-negative

.

For the other cases, we only assume that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla{\bf u}}{\vartheta}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta^{2}}+\frac{\varrho G}{\vartheta}\Big)\psi\,{\rm d}x

(30)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\psi}{\vartheta}+\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi\Big)\,{\rm d}x

for all
\psi\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, non-negative.

For zero velocity on the boundary and Dirichlet boundary condition for the temperature, we consider the ballistic energy inequality in the form

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla{\bf u}}{\vartheta}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta):\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta^{2}}+\frac{\varrho G}{\vartheta}\Big)\widetilde{\vartheta}\,{\rm d}x

2.1
Existence of solutions

In the whole paper, we assume that
\Omega\in C^{1,1}
. This makes the construction of approximate solutions possible, even though, for the formal a priori estimates of the solution, it is enough to assume that the domain is Lipschitz. Moreover, we also assume that

Note that due to the form of the pressure and by our construction we have

Equation (

It implies that

\int_{\partial\Omega}L\vartheta_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}S=\int_{\partial\Omega}L\vartheta_{0}\,\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\Omega}\left(\varrho_{\delta}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\delta}+\varrho_{\delta}G\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{1,\partial\Omega}\leq C\left(1+\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,p}\right)

for some

Using the same test function (
\psi=1
) as in (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}_{\delta}(\vartheta_{\delta},\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}}{\vartheta_{\delta}}+\frac{\Big(\kappa(\vartheta_{\delta})+\delta(\vartheta_{\delta}^{B}+\vartheta_{\delta}^{-1})\Big)\left|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}\right|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\delta}^{2}}+\frac{\varrho_{\delta}G}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{L\vartheta_{0}}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}L\,\mathrm{d}S.

5

5

inequality, we have

\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,p}^{p}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta_{\delta},\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{p(1-\alpha)}{2-p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}.

Thus, combining it with the entropy inequality (
61

\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,p}^{p}\leq C\|\vartheta_{\delta}\|_{\frac{p(1-\alpha)}{2-p}}^{\frac{p(1-\alpha)}{2}}.

(62)

Furthermore, we also deduce from (

\delta\|{\bf u}_{\delta}\|_{1,2}^{2}+\left\|\frac{\varrho_{\delta}G}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\nabla\ln\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\delta\left(\left\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{B}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}^{-1}\right\|_{1,\partial\Omega}\leq C.

This, together with (

\displaystyle\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{3m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{1,\partial\Omega}+\left\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\left\|{\bf u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,p}\right)

\displaystyle C\left(1+\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{\frac{p(1-\alpha)}{2-p}}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right).

1

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho_{\delta}(\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{\Psi}-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}_{\delta}(\vartheta_{\delta},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{\Psi}+\varrho_{\delta}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{\Psi}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}x.

consequently also

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left(\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}^{\beta}\,{\rm d}x\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}\,{\rm d}x\right)

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}\left|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\right|\text{ }\mathrm{d}x\leq\left\|\varrho_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{\frac{3p}{3-p}}^{2}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\right\|_{\frac{3p}{5p-6}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\varrho_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,p}^{2}\left\|p_{\delta}\right\|_{\frac{3p\beta}{5p-6}}^{\beta}\leq C\left\|p_{\delta}\right\|_{\frac{3p\beta}{5p-6}}^{\beta},

where, in order to get control for some
\beta>0
, we require
\frac{3p\beta}{5p-6}<1+\beta
. This leads to

\beta<\frac{5p-6}{6-2p}

(70)

and since
p\leq 2
, the restrictions are
\beta<1
and

p>\frac{6}{5}\Rightarrow m>\frac{1-\alpha}{2}.

Next

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}[(1+\vartheta_{\delta})^{\alpha}+\delta\vartheta_{\delta}]|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}|\text{ }\left|\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\right|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{3m}^{\alpha}\right)\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{p}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle+\delta\|\nabla{\bf u}_{\delta}\|_{2}\|\vartheta_{\delta}\|_{3B}\|\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\|_{q_{1}}\leq C\left\|p_{\delta}\right\|_{q\beta}^{\beta},

where (recall that
B
can be taken arbitrarily large)

q=\frac{3mp}{3m(p-1)-\alpha p}.

Thus, we require
\frac{3mp\beta}{3m(p-1)-\alpha p}<1+\beta
which results in

\beta<\frac{3m(p-1)-\alpha p}{3m+\alpha p}.

(71)

In order to obtain some
\beta>0
, we therefore require
3m(p-1)-\alpha p>0
, which implies
m>\frac{1+\alpha}{3}
. We are therefore ready for the limit passage in the continuity and momentum equations as well as in the entropy inequality. Recall that we have (for a suitable subsequence which we, however, relabel)

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\delta}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup{\bf u}

\displaystyle\text{ in }W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\delta}

\displaystyle\to{\bf u}

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{q}(\Omega),\,q<\frac{3p}{p-3}

\displaystyle\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\nabla\vartheta

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{r}(\Omega),\,\text{ for some }r>1

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\delta}

\displaystyle\to\vartheta

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{q}(\Omega),\,q<3m

\displaystyle\varrho_{\delta}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup^{*}\varrho

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{\infty}(\Omega)

\displaystyle p_{\delta}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\overline{p}

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{1+\beta}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\delta\vartheta_{\delta}\nabla{\bf u}_{\delta}

\displaystyle\to 0

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\delta\Big(\vartheta_{\delta}^{B}+\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\Big)\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}

\displaystyle\to 0

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega).

Note that

for

while

if

We find that the limit functions

satisfy

\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi\,{\rm d}x=0

for all

;

\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}+\overline{p}{\rm div}\,{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}+\varrho{\bf f}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\Big]\,{\rm d}x=0

(73)

for all

;

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla{\bf u}}{\vartheta}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta^{2}}+\frac{\varrho G}{\vartheta}\Big)\psi\,{\rm d}x

(74)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{L(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0})}{\vartheta}\psi\,{\rm d}S-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\psi}{\vartheta}+\overline{\varrho s}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\psi\Big)\,{\rm d}x

for all non-negative
\psi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, where
\overline{\varrho s}
is the weak limit of
\varrho_{\delta}s(\varrho_{\delta},\vartheta_{\delta})
. Note that we used the weak lower semicontinuity of several terms in order to pass to the limit on the left-hand side.

\int_{\partial\Omega}L(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0})\,\mathrm{d}S=\int_{\Omega}\left(\varrho\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{u}+\varrho G\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(75)

Indeed, we do not know whether
\overline{p}=p(\varrho,\vartheta)
and
\overline{\varrho s}=\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)
. Before dealing with this problem, which is equivalent in fact with the strong convergence of the density sequence, let us look at the convergence in the weak formulation of the total energy balance.

We would like to pass to the limit
\delta\to 0_{+}
in the following equality

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho_{\delta}\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}|^{2}+g(\vartheta_{\delta})\Big)\mathbf{u}_{\delta}+p(\varrho_{\delta},\vartheta_{\delta})\mathbf{u}_{\delta}-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}_{\delta}(\vartheta_{\delta},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta})\mathbf{u}_{\delta}-\kappa_{\delta}(\vartheta_{\delta})\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}\Big]\cdot\nabla\psi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho_{\delta}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\delta}+\varrho_{\delta}G\Big)\psi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial\Omega}L(\vartheta_{\delta}-\vartheta_{0})\psi\,\mathrm{d}S.

(76)

Note that the only terms we have to consider in more detail are the second and third terms on the left-hand side. To deal with the latter, we need

\frac{\alpha}{3m}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3-p}{3p}<1.

It easily implies

m>1.

The term containing the pressure is slightly more complex. Here, we need

\frac{1}{1+\beta}+\frac{3-p}{3p}<1.

This leads to
\beta>\frac{3-p}{4p-3}
and we need to compare this inequality with the inequalities obtained above (
70
) and (
71
). We have two inequalities and we need to justify that both of them allow for some
\beta>0
so that all three inequalities are fulfilled.

Condition (
70
) gives

\frac{5p-6}{6-2p}>\frac{3-p}{4p-3}.

It results in

p>\frac{3}{2},\quad\text{ i.e., }m>1-\alpha.

Condition (
71
) gives

\frac{3m(p-1)-\alpha p}{3m+\alpha p}>\frac{3-p}{4p-3}.

This simplifies after plugging the form of
p
in

\frac{3m-1-\alpha}{3m+1+\alpha}>\frac{m+1-\alpha}{5m-1+\alpha}

which yields
m>1
. Under this assumption, we may pass to the limit in the weak formulation of the total energy balance to get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+g(\vartheta)\Big)\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\psi+\overline{p}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\psi-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta,\nabla\mathbf{u})\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\psi-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\psi\Big]

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{u}+\varrho G\Big)\psi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial\Omega}L(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0})\psi\,\mathrm{d}S.

(77)

To conclude, we need to deal with the strong convergence of the density sequence.

However, this task is quite standard nowadays. Exactly as in
[
2
, Section 7]
we deduce the effective viscous flux identity in the form

\overline{\varrho p}-\varrho\overline{p}=\Big(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\vartheta)+\xi(\vartheta)\Big)(\overline{\varrho{\rm div}\,{\bf u}}-\varrho{\rm div}\,{\bf u}).

It is also easy to see that

\overline{\vartheta\varrho h(\varrho)}=\vartheta\overline{\varrho h(\varrho)}

as well as

\overline{\vartheta\varrho h(\varrho)\varrho}=\vartheta\overline{\varrho h(\varrho)\varrho}

and exactly as in
[
2
]
(see also
[
13
]
) we can deduce the strong convergence of the density sequence. The proof of Theorem
1
is complete.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\big(\kappa(\vartheta_{\delta})+\delta(\vartheta_{\delta}^{B}+\vartheta_{\delta}^{-1})\big)\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\vartheta_{L}}{\vartheta_{\delta}}-\varrho_{\delta}s(\varrho_{\delta},\vartheta_{\delta})\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\vartheta}+\varrho_{\delta}G+\varrho_{\delta}\mathbf{f\cdot u}_{\delta}\Big)\,{\rm d}x.

.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}(1+\left[\ln\varrho_{\delta}\right]^{+}+\left[\ln\vartheta_{\delta}\right]^{+})\left|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right||\nabla\widetilde{\vartheta}|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\widetilde{\vartheta}\|_{q}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,p}+C\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}\left[\ln\vartheta_{\delta}\right]^{+}\left|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right|\cdot|\nabla\widetilde{\vartheta}|\,{\rm d}x

m>1
we can perform the limit passage in the total energy balance.
Thus, we complete the proof of
Theorem
3
.

4.4
Problem 4 (proof of Theorem
4
)

Finally, we deal with Problem 4. We take the ballistic energy inequality (
57
) and choose a particular function
\widetilde{\vartheta}:=\vartheta_{L}
exactly as in Problem 2. Whence
\vartheta_{L}
is an admissible test function in (
57
). We also choose
\alpha=1
, i.e., we take
p=2
. We have (recall that the regularization of the form
\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}_{\delta}
is meaningless in this case)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta_{\delta},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}}{\vartheta_{\delta}}+\frac{\big(\kappa(\vartheta_{\delta})+\delta(\vartheta_{\delta}^{B}+\vartheta_{\delta}^{-1})\big)\left|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}\right|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\delta}^{2}}+\frac{\varrho_{\delta}G}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\Big)\widetilde{\vartheta}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\underline{\theta}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta_{\delta},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}}{\vartheta_{\delta}}+\frac{\big(\kappa(\vartheta_{\delta})+\delta(\vartheta_{\delta}^{B}+\vartheta_{\delta}^{-1})\big)\left|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}\right|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\delta}^{2}}+\frac{\varrho_{\delta}G}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\Big)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\Big(||\mathbf{u}_{\delta}||_{1,2}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\ln\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{m}{2}}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\delta\Big(\big\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{B}{2}}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big)+\Big\|\frac{\varrho_{\delta}G}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\Big\|_{1}\Big).

Furthermore

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{\delta}(\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}+p(\varrho_{\delta},\vartheta_{\delta})\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta_{\delta},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}+\varrho_{\delta}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{0}\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|p_{\delta}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta_{\delta},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta})\right\|_{1}+C.

Then

\displaystyle C\Big(||\mathbf{u}_{\delta}||_{1,2}^{2}+\big\|\nabla\ln\vartheta_{\delta}\big\|_{2}^{2}+C\left\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\delta\big(\big\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{B}{2}}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{2}^{2}\big)+\left\|\frac{\varrho_{\delta}G}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\right\|_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\big(\kappa(\vartheta_{\delta})+\delta(\vartheta_{\delta}^{B}+\vartheta_{\delta}^{-1})\big)\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\vartheta_{L}}{\vartheta_{\delta}}+\varrho_{\delta}s(\varrho_{\delta},\vartheta_{\delta})\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\vartheta_{L}+\varrho_{\delta}G+\varrho_{\delta}\mathbf{f\cdot u}_{\delta}\Big)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+C_{1}\Big(\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,2}^{2}+\left\|\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|p_{\delta}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{2}\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{2}+1\Big).

Next, we look at the term with
\kappa(\vartheta_{\delta})
in the
integrals.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\delta})\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\vartheta_{L}}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla K(\vartheta_{\delta})\cdot\nabla\vartheta_{L}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}K(\vartheta_{\delta})\Delta\vartheta_{L}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}K(\vartheta_{\delta})\frac{\partial\vartheta_{L}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\,{\rm d}S=\int_{\partial\Omega}K(\vartheta_{D})\frac{\partial\vartheta_{L}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\,{\rm d}S,

where

K^{\prime}(z)=\frac{\kappa(z)}{z},

and the right-hand side is bounded by
C=C(\vartheta_{D},\partial\Omega).

Moreover, it is not difficult to find by integrating by parts that

\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\big(\delta(\vartheta_{\delta}^{B}+\vartheta_{\delta}^{-1})\big)\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\vartheta_{L}}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\Big)\,{\rm d}x=\delta\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(\frac{\vartheta_{D}^{B}}{B}-\frac{1}{\vartheta_{D}}\Big)\frac{\partial\vartheta_{L}}{\partial{\bf n}}\,{\rm d}S.

Note that the specific entropy
s(\varrho_{\delta},\vartheta_{\delta})

is given by (
18
), i.e.,

s(\varrho,\vartheta)=\int_{\vartheta_{M}}^{\vartheta}\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\varrho_{M}}^{\varrho}\frac{h(z)}{z}\,\mathrm{d}z,

where
g(s)
is given by (
16
). The properties of
the functions
g
and
h
imply that

\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\leq C\varrho(1+\left[\ln\varrho\right]^{+}+\left[\ln\vartheta\right]^{+}).

This yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}s(\varrho_{\delta},\vartheta_{\delta})\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\vartheta_{L}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}(1+\left[\ln\varrho_{\delta}\right]^{+}+\left[\ln\vartheta_{\delta}\right]^{+})\left|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,2}+C\int_{\Omega}\left[\ln\vartheta_{\delta}\right]^{+}\left|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,2}+C(\varepsilon)\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{3m}^{\varepsilon}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,2}

and

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}\mathbf{f\cdot u}_{\delta}\,{\rm d}x\leq\left\|\varrho_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,2}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle C\Big(||\mathbf{u}_{\delta}||_{1,2}^{2}+\|\nabla\ln\vartheta_{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}+C\big\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{m}{2}}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\delta\Big(\big\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{\frac{B}{2}}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\nabla\vartheta_{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big)+\left\|\frac{\varrho_{\delta}G}{\vartheta_{\delta}}\right\|_{1}\Big)

(82)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,2}+\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,2}^{2}+\left\|\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|p_{\delta}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{2}\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{2}+\|\vartheta_{\delta}\|_{3m}^{\varepsilon}\|{\bf u}_{\delta}\|_{1,2}+\|{\bf u}_{\delta}\|_{1,2}+1\Big).

The control of pressure is similar to Problem 1. Using
\mathbf{\Phi}=\mathcal{B}(\varrho_{\delta})
as a test function in (
54
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}\varrho_{\delta}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}(\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta_{\delta},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{\Phi}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{M}{|\Omega|}\left(\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}^{\beta}\text{ }\mathrm{d}x\right)\,{\rm d}x.

Exactly as in the proof of Theorem
1
, we may show that

\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}\varrho_{\delta}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}\,{\rm d}x\leq C\big(\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\|{\bf u}_{\delta}\|_{1,2}^{2}+\|{\bf u}_{\delta}\|_{1,2}\|\vartheta_{\delta}\|_{2}+\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\big).

(83)

We employ the estimate (
83
) and get that if
\|\nabla{\bf u}_{0}\|_{\infty}
is sufficiently small and
m\geq 2
(this comes from the estimate of the term
\|{\bf u}_{\delta}\|_{1,2}\|\vartheta_{\delta}\|_{2}
) we can estimate the left-hand side of (
82
) and thus also the left-hand side of (
83
).

Finally, in (
54
) we use the test function
\mathcal{B}(p_{\delta}^{\beta})
and get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}p_{\delta}^{\beta}\text{ }\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}(\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\text{ }\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\mbox{$\mathbb{S}$}(\vartheta_{\delta},\mathbf{u}_{\delta}):\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\text{ }\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{\ \Phi}\,\mathrm{d}x

(84)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left(\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}^{\beta}\text{ }\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}\text{ }\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

We estimate these terms to control the pressure in a better space than
L^{1}
, then we pass to the limit as in previous results.

It is sufficient to consider the first two integrals. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}\left|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\left\|\varrho_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{6}^{2}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\right\|_{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\varrho_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,p}^{2}\left\|p_{\delta}\right\|_{\frac{3}{2}\beta}^{\beta},

where, in order to get control for some
\beta>0
, we require
\frac{3}{2}\beta<1+\beta
. This leads to

\beta<2.

(85)

Next

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}(1+\vartheta_{\delta})|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}|\,\left|\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(1+\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{3m}\right)\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{2}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}\right\|_{\frac{6m}{3m-2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{3m}\right)\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{2}\left\|p_{\delta}\right\|_{\frac{6m\beta}{3m-2}}^{\beta}.

Thus, we require
\frac{3m\beta}{3m-2}<1+\beta
which results in

\beta<\frac{3m-2}{3m+2}.

(86)

In order to get some
\beta>0
we therefore need that
m>\frac{2}{3}
; note, however, that we already have
m\geq 2
. Then, we obtain

\left\|p_{\delta}\right\|_{1+\beta}\leq C\Big(\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,p}^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{1,p}\left\|\vartheta_{\delta}\right\|_{3m}+1\Big)\leq C.

Now, we can start with the limit passage procedure similarly as in Problem
1. Note that for
m\geq 2
the limit passage in the total energy balance does not cause any troubles.
