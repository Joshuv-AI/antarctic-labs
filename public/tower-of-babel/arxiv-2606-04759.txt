Efficient Description of Parametric Amplification of Quantum Pulses
Victor Rueskov Christiansen, Klaus Mølmer, Emanuel Hubenschmid

Abstract
A single quantum pulse undergoing parametric amplification feeds into at most two pulses in the output. In this work, we present an efficient, analytical method for finding the quantum state of these output modes. Our method applies the amplification to the vacuum rather than to the input state, and subsequently applies a transformed version of the operator that creates the input state from vacuum. Given the input and output pulse mode functions, the method is analytical, and therefore computationally very efficient, and it can be readily generalized to multiple non-vacuum input modes. We exemplify the method by computing the output quantum states resulting from the input of a coherent, a Schrödinger cat, and a single photon input quantum state. We further employ the method to obtain the quantum state in one of the two output modes heralded upon detection of vacuum in the other, least populated, mode.

II
Transformation of field operators by a parametric amplifier

We consider pulsed excitations of a bosonic quantum field, propagating in one dimension and being subject to a second-order nonlinearity.
The system may be described by a Hamiltonian that is at most quadratic in its annihilation and creation operators.
Solving the input-output relation, results in a linear relation between the annihilation operator of the output quantum field
\hat{b}_{\text{out}}(\omega)
and the annihilation and creation operator of the input field
\hat{a}_{\text{in}}(\omega)
,
\hat{a}_{\text{in}}^{\dagger}(\omega)
.
Since the nonlinear interaction can mediate between different frequencies, the input-output relation

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\text{out}}(\omega)=\int d\omega^{\prime}\left[F(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\hat{a}_{\text{in}}(\omega^{\prime})+G^{*}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\hat{a}_{\text{in}}^{\dagger}(\omega^{\prime})\right],

(1)

includes the kernels
F(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
and
G(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
for transferring between frequencies
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
. The kernels conserve the bosonic commutation relation of the mode operators. We emphasize that this input-output relation may also describe explicit time-dependent second-order nonlinearities.
An example of such a system is a pulsed optical parametric oscillator (OPO), which is described in Sec.
V
, and used for the examples in Sec.
V
and
VI
.
The results presented in our work can be generalized to fields propagating in more spatial dimensions
49
.

The inverse transformation can be written as
4

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\text{in}}(\omega)=\int d\omega^{\prime}\left[F^{*}(\omega^{\prime},\omega)\hat{b}_{\text{out}}(\omega^{\prime})-G^{*}(\omega^{\prime},\omega)\hat{b}_{\text{out}}^{\dagger}(\omega^{\prime})\right].

(2)

In the following, we omit the subscripts “in” and “out” and refer to
\hat{a}
-operators as operators acting on the input, and
\hat{b}
-operators as operators acting on the output.

We assume the field at the input port of the nonlinear interaction is populated by a single input mode with spectral amplitude
u(\omega)
, the Fourier transform of its temporal wave packet
\tilde{u}(t)
, and we are interested in finding the transformed quantum state at the output port. Our theory can be generalized to multiple modes in the input port, but here we focus on a single mode.

One method to calculate the output quantum state uses a Bogoliubov eigenmode decomposition of
F(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
and
G(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
to reduce the problem to one of single mode squeezing of these eigenmodes
52
. This approach is useful in the case where the input occupies only a few of the eigenmodes, while if this is not the case, the expansion on the eigenmodes of a general input state has a complexity of the same order as the boson sampling problem. This complexity occurs when the nonlinearity is used to amplify or up-convert quantum states of radiation across a range of frequencies or temporal modes
20
.

Another method achieves an effective description by decomposing the output operator in Eq. (
1
) in terms of the input mode and a few additional, orthogonal vacuum input modes. This is the approach taken in Ref.
49
, where it is shown that any input quantum state in a single input mode
u
, will at most occupy two modes,
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, in the output. These modes will also contain parametrically amplified contributions from the vacuum input modes. Therefore, the method provides a resourceful mode decomposition for any given input state to the parametric amplifier.

Both the methods mentioned above rely on ancillary modes for calculating the output quantum state. Here, we utilize the mode decomposition of Ref.
49
and provide a method that only includes the physical output modes and no ancillary modes. The resourceful use of modes enables the most efficient calculation of the output quantum state in terms of Hilbert space dimensionality. Therefore, it prevents the computational complexity encountered in the mixing of many modes to become an issue.
As the decomposition in Ref.
49
is relevant for the derivations presented in this paper, we summarize the argument leading to the two output modes
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
in App.
A
. Furthermore, we offer an alternative argument in App.
B
, and we summarize the decomposition of output modes on a small set of input modes in App.
C
.

Rather than following Ref.
49
and developing the theory around the output operator in Eq. (
1
), in this work we take our starting point in a decomposition of the input operator in Eq. (
2
). Since we know that the non-vacuum input mode leads to output in only two relevant output modes, we are able to expand the input annihilation operator
\hat{a}_{u}
as

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{u}=A\hat{b}_{v_{1}}+B\hat{b}_{v_{1}}^{\dagger}+C\hat{b}_{v_{2}}+D\hat{b}_{v_{2}}^{\dagger},

(3)

with coefficients
A,B,C
and
D
(see Sec.
III
).

If the input state can be expressed as
\ket{\psi_{u}}=f(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})\ket{0}
for some function
f
, we can use (
3
) to find the corresponding output quantum state. Writing the transformation of the nonlinear parametric amplifier as a unitary transformation
\hat{U}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{U}\ket{\psi_{u}}=\hat{U}f(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{U}\ket{0}.

(4)

The evaluation of
\hat{U}f(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})\hat{U}^{\dagger}=\tilde{f}(\hat{b}_{v_{1}},\hat{b}_{v_{1}}^{\dagger},\hat{b}_{v_{2}},\hat{b}_{v_{2}}^{\dagger})
merely entails replacing arguments
\hat{a}_{u}
and
\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger}
in
f
by their expressions following from Eq. (
3
). The resulting operator should then be applied on the squeezed multimode vacuum state
\ket{\xi_{i}}=\hat{U}\ket{0}
. In summary, the input state transforms into the output state as given by

\displaystyle f(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})\ket{0}\rightarrow\tilde{f}(\hat{b}_{v_{1}},\hat{b}_{v_{1}}^{\dagger},\hat{b}_{v_{2}},\hat{b}_{v_{2}}^{\dagger})\ket{\xi_{i}}.

(5)

While the input states we consider here are pure, the formalism also applies to mixed input states, by replacing
\ket{0}
with
\ket{0}\bra{0}
and
f(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})
by a super operator
\mathcal{F}(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})
, which creates the desired mixed state under the action upon
\ket{0}\bra{0}
.

The two different paths to obtain the output quantum state are illustrated in Fig.
1
, where the method applied in this article distinguishes itself by first evaluating the effect of the amplifier on the vacuum state and thereafter performing an additional operation to yield the desired outcome for a non-trivial initial state. Details of the scheme will be presented in the following sections. We remind that while we focus here on a single input pulse mode and the quantum state in two output modes
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, the method is more general and can be applied also with more input and output modes.

III
Decomposition of the input mode

It was shown in
49
that a quantum state occupying a pulse of radiation
u(\omega)
propagating along a quantum channel incident on a parametric amplifier will in general only contribute to the population of two modes,
v_{1}(\omega)
and
v_{2}(\omega)
, of the output quantum field. It was also shown that if the input quantum state obeys the relation

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{u}}=|\braket{\hat{a}_{u}\hat{a}_{u}}|,

(6)

only one pulse in the output,
v(\omega)
, will be populated by the input state contents. In the following we consider the decomposition of the input mode, first for the case where only a single output mode is occupied by the input, and then for the general case of two populated output modes.

III.1
The single-mode transformation

We consider first the case where the input quantum state obeys the single-mode condition in Eq. (
6
). Examples of states for which this holds are the coherent states
\ket{\alpha}
and Schrödinger cat states of the form
\ket{\alpha}\pm i\ket{-\alpha}
. In Ref.
49
it is found that the output mode occupied by the input quantum state is given by

v(\omega)=\frac{\alpha\zeta_{u}f_{u}(\omega)+\alpha^{*}\xi_{u}g_{u}(\omega)}{k},

(7)

where
\alpha=\sqrt{\braket{\hat{a}_{u}\hat{a}_{u}}}
and

\displaystyle f_{u}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta_{u}}\int d\omega^{\prime}F(\omega,\omega^{\prime})u(\omega^{\prime}),

(8)

\displaystyle g_{u}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\xi_{u}}\int d\omega^{\prime}G^{*}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})u^{*}(\omega^{\prime}).

(9)

The constants
\zeta_{u}
,
\xi_{u}
and
k
ensure the normalization of
f_{u}(\omega)
,
g_{u}(\omega)
and
v(\omega)
, respectively, where
k
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}k^{2}&=|\alpha|^{2}(\zeta_{u}^{2}+\xi_{u}^{2})+2\zeta_{u}\xi_{u}\text{Re}(\alpha^{2}\braket{g_{u},f_{u}}).\end{split}

(10)

We use the inner product notation,
\braket{f,g}=\int d\omega f^{*}(\omega)g(\omega)
for mode overlap integrals and define the orthogonality of mode functions accordingly. The annihilation operator for the input mode
u
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{a}_{u}&=\int d\omega d\omega^{\prime}u^{*}(\omega)F^{*}(\omega^{\prime},\omega)\hat{b}_{\textup{out}}(\omega^{\prime})\\
&-\int d\omega d\omega^{\prime}u^{*}(\omega)G^{*}(\omega^{\prime},\omega)\hat{b}_{\textup{out}}^{\dagger}(\omega^{\prime})\\
&=\zeta_{u}\hat{b}_{f_{u}}-\xi_{u}\hat{b}_{g_{u}}^{\dagger}.\end{split}

(11)

We note that
f_{u}
and
g_{u}
are not orthogonal but we can expand them on the output mode
v(\omega)
and an orthogonal component that can be found by a Gram-Schmidt procedure,

\displaystyle\begin{split}h(\omega)&=\frac{f_{u}(\omega)-\braket{v,f_{u}}v(\omega)}{\sqrt{1-|\braket{v,f_{u}}|^{2}}}.\end{split}

(12)

This yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{a}_{u}&=\zeta_{u}\braket{f_{u},v}\hat{b}_{v}+\zeta_{u}\braket{f_{u},h}\hat{b}_{h}^{\dagger}\\
&-\xi_{u}\braket{v,g_{u}}\hat{b}_{v}^{\dagger}-\xi_{u}\braket{h,g_{u}}\hat{b}_{h}^{\dagger}.\end{split}

(13)

From Eq. (
7
), it can be shown that
\xi_{u}\braket{v,g_{u}}=\zeta_{u}\braket{f_{u},v}-\alpha/k
, and it follows that
\xi_{u}\braket{h,g_{u}}=-\zeta_{u}\alpha/\alpha^{*}\braket{h,f_{u}}
. Finally, it can be seen that
\braket{f_{u},h}=\sqrt{1-|\braket{f_{u},v}|^{2}}
, and we arrive at writing the input mode in terms of the output modes as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{a}_{u}&=\zeta_{u}\braket{f_{u},v}\hat{b}_{v}-\left(\zeta_{u}\braket{f_{u},v}-\frac{\alpha}{k}\right)\hat{b}_{v}^{\dagger}\\
&+\zeta_{u}\sqrt{1-|\braket{f_{u},v}|^{2}}\left(\hat{b}_{h}+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha^{*}}\hat{b}_{h}^{\dagger}\right).\end{split}

(14)

At first glance, Eq. (
14
) seems to contradict that the input-dependent part of the output quantum state resides in a single pulsed mode. Taking a closer look at the quantum states, for instance the coherent states, we can explicitly transform the corresponding displacement operator,

\displaystyle\begin{split}D_{u}(\alpha)&=\exp\left(\alpha\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}_{u}\right)\\
&=\exp\left(\left(2\zeta_{u}\textup{Re}(\alpha\braket{v,f_{u}})-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{k}\right)(\hat{b}_{v}^{\dagger}-\hat{b}_{v})\right),\end{split}

(15)

and we see that the
\hat{b}_{h}
components disappear from the transformation. The
\hat{a}_{u}
-operator only acts on one mode in the output field as expected.

While it is common in quantum optics to choose the mode basis in order to diagonalize the Hamiltonian, we note that the minimal mode decomposition in Eq. (
14
) depends on the state populating the input mode, as indicated by the explicit dependence of
\alpha
in the mode expansion coefficients
\zeta_{u}
,
\xi_{u}
.

III.2
The two-mode transformation

We now extend the analysis to the case of general quantum states occupying a single mode input pulse
u
. In this case, the relevant quantum state populates two modes at the output. These output modes can be found by considering the autocorrelation function
49

\displaystyle\begin{split}&g_{1}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=\braket{\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\omega)\hat{b}(\omega^{\prime})}\\
&=\braket{\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{u}}\zeta_{u}^{2}f_{u}^{*}(\omega)f_{u}(\omega^{\prime})+\braket{\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger}}\zeta_{u}\xi_{u}f_{u}^{*}(\omega)g_{u}(\omega^{\prime})\\
&+\braket{\hat{a}_{u}\hat{a}_{u}}\zeta_{u}\xi_{u}g_{u}^{*}(\omega)f_{u}(\omega^{\prime})+\braket{\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{u}}\xi_{u}^{2}g_{u}^{*}(\omega)g_{u}(\omega^{\prime})\\

(16)

The first four terms in the expression for the
g_{1}
-function depend on the input quantum state, while the last term arises from vacuum input. Since the terms that depend on the input quantum state are given by two functions
f_{u}
and
g_{u}
, as defined in eq. (
8
) and (
9
), the autocorrelation function can be decomposed into at most two orthogonal modes,
v_{1}
and
v_{2}

49
(see Appendix
A
). Decomposing the input-output relation in Eq. (
2
) reveals

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{u}

\displaystyle=\int d\omega^{\prime}f_{u}^{*}(\omega^{\prime})\hat{b}_{\textup{out}}(\omega^{\prime})-\int d\omega^{\prime}g_{u}(\omega^{\prime})\hat{b}_{\textup{out}}^{\dagger}(\omega^{\prime}),

(17)

and since
f_{u}
and
g_{u}
can be expressed using the two orthogonal eigenmodes
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
of the autocorrelation function

, we can rewrite the input-mode operator as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{a}_{u}&=\zeta_{u}\braket{f_{u},v_{1}}\hat{b}_{v_{1}}-\xi_{u}\braket{v_{1},g_{u}}\hat{b}_{v_{1}}^{\dagger}\\
&+\zeta_{u}\braket{f_{u},v_{2}}\hat{b}_{v_{2}}-\xi_{u}\braket{v_{2},g_{u}}\hat{b}_{v_{2}}^{\dagger}.\end{split}

(18)

In this case, it is evident that the input quantum state in mode
\hat{a}_{u}
only contributes to the output quantum state in the two output modes
\hat{b}_{v_{1}}
and
\hat{b}_{v_{2}}
. Explicit expressions for the modes
v_{1}(\omega)
and
v_{2}(\omega)
are given in appendix
A

and

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\textup{vac, out}}=P_{0}\sum_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}\frac{H^{\{\mathbf{R}\}}_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{0})}{\sqrt{\mathbf{m}!\mathbf{n}!}}\ket{\mathbf{m}}\bra{\mathbf{n}}.

(26)

Having found, the density matrix of the squeezed thermal output state resulting from the transformation of the vacuum input state, we now turn to the calculation of the output state for any input quantum state.

V
The output quantum state

To compute the output state for any non-trivial input quantum state we assume the construction of such a state by the action of a sequence of creation and annihilation operators, parametrized by a function of these operators
f(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})
, on the vacuum state. This yields an input state written here as a density matrix

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\textup{in}}=f(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})\ket{0}\bra{0}\left[f(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})\right]^{\dagger}.

(27)

By replacing
f
with an appropriate super operator, extension of the theory to mixed input states is straightforward. We must, anyway, apply a density matrix description, because the squeezed vacuum state
\hat{\rho}_{\textup{vac,out}}
is entangled across all relevant and irrelevant modes, and its restriction to the one or two modes of interest results in a mixed Gaussian state
41
;
20
.
We can now obtain the output state for any given input quantum state by replacing
f(\hat{a}_{u},\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger})
and
\hat{\rho}_{\textup{vac,in}}=\ket{0}\bra{0}
with the expressions for
\tilde{f}(\hat{b}_{v_{1}},\hat{b}_{v_{1}}^{\dagger},\hat{b}_{v_{2}},\hat{b}_{v_{2}}^{\dagger})
and
\hat{\rho}_{\textup{vac,out}}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\rho}_{\textup{out}}&=\tilde{f}(\hat{b}_{v_{1}},\hat{b}_{v_{1}}^{\dagger},\hat{b}_{v_{2}},\hat{b}_{v_{2}}^{\dagger})\hat{\rho}_{\textup{vac,out}}\left[\tilde{f}(\hat{b}_{v_{1}},\hat{b}_{v_{1}}^{\dagger},\hat{b}_{v_{2}},\hat{b}_{v_{2}}^{\dagger})\right]^{\dagger}.\end{split}

(28)

For the convenience of the reader, we have implemented all steps applied in our method in a Python library

using the

Python

For the analysis, we assume an optical parametric oscillator (OPO) Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\textup{OPO}}=\Delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\frac{i}{2}\left[\xi(t)(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{2}-\xi^{*}(t)\hat{a}^{2}\right],

(29)

where
\hat{a}
is the operator associated with annihilating one quantum of radiation in the cavity mode,

is the detuning between the oscillator frequency and the (classical) optical pump frequency, and

\displaystyle\hat{L}=\sqrt{\gamma}\hat{a},

(30)

\gamma

For a constant pump

VII
Conclusion

We have presented a formalism for calculating the transformation of an arbitrary pulsed input quantum state by a quadratic Hamiltonian, such as a parametric amplifier. Our method combines analytic and numerically efficient calculations, making it computationally tractable. The method involves three steps: i) decomposing the input-output operator relation into an effective set of modes, ii) finding the (squeezing) transformation of the input vacuum state within these effective modes, and iii) apply the transformed output mode operators to the squeezed vacuum state to obtain the output state in the desired modes. We provide a numerical implementation of the method and give examples of three different input states transformed by an optical parametric oscillator (OPO): a coherent state, a Schrödinger cat state and a Fock state. We observe how the output quantum state can be distributed over two entangled modes, and we have shown how detection of vacuum in one of the output modes may herald purification of the quantum state in the other output mode.

The method may be used for a wide range of systems besides OPO’s, for instance optical parametric amplifiers or traveling wave parametric amplifiers. In this work we consider the input modes to be temporal/spectral modes, but the formalism may also be readily applied to, e.g., transverse spatial modes in 2D and 3D system platforms
2
;
46
;
50
, Laguerre-Gaussian modes carrying orbital angular momentum
34
;
51
, and relativistic spatio-temporal modes
27
, to name a few.

While our calculations rely heavily on the quadratic form of the Hamiltonian and the resulting effective treatment of Gaussian states through their first and second moments, we are able to deal with arbitrary input states. We imagine that it will also be possible to incorporate the effect of a single or a few non-Gaussian operations, such as further heralding upon detection among input or output modes, and possibly also address cumulant, second order mean field, approximations to the dynamics of systems with higher order nonlinearities.

&e_{2}=t(\omega)=\frac{f^{v_{1}}(\omega)-\braket{u,f^{v_{1}}}u(\omega)}{\sqrt{1-|\braket{u,f^{v_{1}}}|^{2}}},\\
&e_{3}=s(\omega)=\frac{f^{v_{2}}(\omega)-\braket{u,f^{v_{2}}}u(\omega)-\braket{t,f^{v_{2}}}t(\omega)}{\sqrt{1-|\braket{u,f^{v_{2}}}|^{2}-|\braket{t,f^{v_{2}}}|^{2}}},\end{split}

(66)

where we use
\braket{f,g}=\int d\omega f^{*}(\omega)g(\omega)
. Having obtained a basis for the modes, we can perform the decomposition of the output operators to yield

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{b}_{v_{i}}&=\zeta^{v_{i}}\left(\braket{f^{v_{i}},u}\hat{a}_{u}+\braket{f^{v_{i}},t}\hat{a}_{t}+\braket{f^{v_{i}},s}\hat{a}_{s}\right)\\
&+\xi^{v_{i}}\left(\braket{u,g^{v_{i}}}\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger}+\braket{t,g^{v_{i}}}\hat{a}_{t}^{\dagger}+\braket{s,g^{v_{i}}}\hat{a}_{s}^{\dagger}\right),\end{split}

(67)

for
i=1,2
. We have now obtained a decomposition into three modes. This allows us to write the decomposition in the matrix form used in Sec.
IV.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{b}}=\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\
F^{*}&E^{*}\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{\hat{a}}

(68)

with

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta^{v_{1}}\braket{f^{v_{1}},u}&\zeta^{v_{1}}\braket{f^{v_{1}},t}&\zeta^{v_{1}}\braket{f^{v_{1}},s}\\
\zeta^{v_{2}}\braket{f^{v_{2}},u}&\zeta^{v_{2}}\braket{f^{v_{2}},t}&\zeta^{v_{2}}\braket{f^{v_{2}},s}\end{pmatrix},

(69)

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{v_{1}}\braket{u,g^{v_{1}}}&\xi^{v_{1}}\braket{t,g^{v_{1}}}&\xi^{v_{1}}\braket{s,g^{v_{1}}}\\
\xi^{v_{2}}\braket{u,g^{v_{2}}}&\xi^{v_{2}}\braket{t,g^{v_{2}}}&\xi^{v_{2}}\braket{s,g^{v_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}.

(70)

Single output mode

We now consider the case where the input state feeds only into a single mode in the output field. This happens when the condition in Eq. (
6
) is fulfilled for the input quantum state. The relevant output mode is identified in the main text as

v(\omega)=\frac{\alpha\zeta_{u}f_{u}(\omega)+\alpha^{*}\xi_{u}g_{u}(\omega)}{k},

(71)

A similar analysis as in the previous section shows that the
v
-mode is now spanned by two input modes,
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, and that the input mode in the single mode case can be written as

\displaystyle u(\omega)=\frac{k\zeta^{v}f^{v}(\omega)-k\xi^{v}g^{v}(\omega)}{\alpha},

(72)

so that the
f^{v}
and
g^{v}
functions can, in turn, be expanded on
u(\omega)
and one other mode, which we can find by the Gram-Schmidt process

\displaystyle\begin{split}&e_{1}=u(\omega),\\
&e_{2}=t(\omega)=\frac{f^{v}(\omega)-\braket{u,f^{v}}u(\omega)}{\sqrt{1-|\braket{u,f^{v}}|^{2}}}.\end{split}

(73)

Finally, the output
\hat{b}_{v}
operator can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{b}_{v}&=\zeta^{v}\left(\braket{f^{v},u}\hat{a}_{u}+\braket{f^{v},t}\hat{a}_{t}\right)\\
&+\xi^{v}\left(\braket{u,g^{v}}\hat{a}_{u}^{\dagger}+\braket{t,g^{v}}\hat{a}_{t}^{\dagger}\right),\end{split}

(74)

where we find using eq. (
72

\xi^{v}\braket{t,g^{v}}=\zeta^{v}\braket{t,f^{v}}

\braket{t,f^{v}}=\sqrt{1-|\braket{u,f^{v}}|^{2}}

E

F

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta^{v}\braket{f^{v},u}&\zeta^{v}\sqrt{1-|\braket{u,f^{v}}|^{2}}\end{pmatrix},

(75)

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{v}\braket{u,g^{v}}&\zeta^{v}\sqrt{1-|\braket{u,f^{v}}|^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(76)
