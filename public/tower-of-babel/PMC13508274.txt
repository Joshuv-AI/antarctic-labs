Efficacy and safety of vericiguat in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and asymptomatic hypotension: insights from a multicenter observational cohort study
Authors: Jiangyue Tian, Shufang Chen, Guihua Zhang, Xiaoning Jia, Luyao An, Xuyang Duan, Yarui Zhao, Changjiang Wei, Huafang Zhang, Xiaowei Li, Kecai Lu, Guihua Yao, Qing Ye, Huixia Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRecent VICTOR study and the VICTORY pooled analysis demonstrate that vericiguat shows significant therapeutic value in a broader population of relatively stable heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) patients. However, the efficacy and safety of vericiguat in HFrEF patients receiving guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) who present with asymptomatic hypotension (systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≤ 100 mmHg) remain unclear and require further investigation.MethodsThis multicenter observational cohort study enrolled 164 patients with HFrEF and asymptomatic hypotension (SBP ≤ 100 mmHg), who voluntarily decide whether to receive vericiguat treatment. Propensity score matching (1:2) was employed to address non-random treatment allocation. The primary outcome was the first occurrence of a ≥ 30% reduction in N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide(NT-proBNP) from baseline at any assessment time point during follow-up. Kaplan-Meier (K-M) analysis and multivariable Cox regression models were applied to assess outcomes.ResultsA total of 61 vericiguat-treated patients and 36 controls completed the study. The incidence of significant NT-proBNP reduction in the vericiguat group was 68.8%, significantly higher than the 47.2% in the control group. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated a significantly higher cumulative incidence of NT-proBNP reduction in the vericiguat group than in the control group (hazard ratio (HR) = 2.54, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.42–4.55, P < 0.001). Multivariate Cox regression confirmed vericiguat as an independent predictor of significant NT-proBNP reduction (Model 3: HR = 2.34, 95% CI: 1.30–4.21, P = 0.004).ConclusionIn patients with HFrEF receiving GDMT with concomitant asymptomatic hypotension (SBP ≤ 100 mmHg), vericiguat treatment is significantly associated with a ≥ 30% reduction in NT-proBNP levels during follow-up. In terms of safety in this study, the small number of adverse events and the absence of major safety signals only suggest no statistically significant differences in safety between the two groups. Further large-scale studies with adequate statistical power are required to better evaluate the safety of vericiguat.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-06086-z.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is associated with high incidence, elevated mortality, and frequent rehospitalizations, imposing a substantial burden on society and families [1–3]. The prognosis of patients with chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) is comparable to that of many cancer patients. However, compared to cancer patients, the risks of death, hospitalization, and disease progression in HFrEF patients may often be underestimated by clinicians [4, 5]. Multiple studies have confirmed that hypotension is strongly associated with adverse outcomes in HFrEF patients [6–8].

Clinical trial data indicate that approximately 10%-15% of HF patients experienced hypotension [9, 10]. In real-world clinical practice, the prevalence of hypotension is even higher due to the coexistence of comorbidities (such as diabetes, chronic kidney disease) as well as the concomitant use of multiple medications (such as antihypertensives, diuretics). Guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) is the cornerstone of HFrEF management. However, in clinical practice, hypotension often limits both GDMT initiation and the achievement of target doses [11]. Although the current “new quadruple therapy” improves the prognosis of HFrEF patients, its hypotensive effects often render it intolerable for patients with baseline hypotension, leading to dose reduction or discontinuation [12].

Vericiguat is a novel soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) stimulator that exerts minimal effects on blood pressure, renal function, and electrolytes [13, 14]. Based on the VICTORIA trial, multiple heart failure management guidelines have listed it as a Class IIB recommendation for HFrEF patients whose symptoms worsen despite GDMT [15, 16]. The recently published VICTOR study and VICTORY pooled analysis further demonstrate that adding vericiguat provides additional survival benefit regardless of whether HFrEF patients are clinically stable [17, 18]. Notably, vericiguat has minimal impact on blood pressure [19]. Unfortunately, key clinical trials typically required systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥ 100 mmHg as an inclusion criterion. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of vericiguat in HFrEF patients with asymptomatic hypotension (systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≤ 100 mmHg) (Hereinafter referred to as AsxHypo) when administered in addition to standard “new quadruple” therapy. Our research seeks to provide clinical insights that may support more individualized therapeutic strategies for patients with HFrEF who are intolerant or suboptimally managed due to low blood pressure.

Methods

Study design

This multicenter observational cohort study enrolled patients with HFrEF and AsxHypo patients from seven public hospitals in Shandong Province, China, between September 2022 and July 2025. Inclusion criteria comprised: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≤ 45% confirmed by echocardiography; (3) symptomatic HF defined as NYHA (the New York Heart Association) class II-IV; (4) initiation of GDMT for HF, including angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor (ACEI)/angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB)/angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor (ARNI), beta-blocker, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA), and sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) ; and (5) SBP ≤ 100 mmHg without symptoms of hypotension, such as dizziness. Exclusion criteria included: (1) severe symptomatic hypotension; (2) severe renal impairment: estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 15ml/min; (3) severe hepatic impairment: Child-Pugh C; (4) concomitant use of other sGC stimulators or phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (PDE-5i); (5) allergy to any sGC stimulator; (6) pregnancy or breastfeeding; (7) participation in another interventional clinical study or treatment with another investigational drug within 30 days before screening.

The enrolled patients were categorized into either the vericiguat group or the control group based on their willingness to use vericiguat. During the follow-up period, patients were monitored regularly through outpatient visits or hospitalizations. After 1:2 propensity score matching (PSM), 97 of the 164 enrolled patients were ultimately included in the analysis: 61 in the vericiguat group and 36 in the control group (Fig. 1).

The primary outcome was defined as the first occurrence of a ≥ 30% reduction in N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide(NT-proBNP) from baseline at any assessment time point during follow-up. The time-to-event was calculated as the number of months from baseline to the first documentation of such reduction. Secondary outcomes were intergroup differences in SBP, diastolic blood pressure (DBP), and eGFR. Safety outcomes included the incidence rates of severe hypotension and syncope during the follow-up period.

Chronic HF follow-up management system

All patients were enrolled in a comprehensive chronic heart failure (CHF) follow-up management system to facilitate effective titration of vericiguat, optimize HF medications, and ensure consistent data collection of study outcomes. The system includes the following: (1) HF team: Composed of HF cardiologists and physician assistants. (2) Patient education: Patients were educated on HF self-management, medication adherence, and self-monitoring techniques, including blood pressure and heart rate monitoring. (3) Follow-up procedures: Patients’ contact information, including phone numbers, addresses, and WeChat IDs, was recorded to support efficient follow-up. During the titration phase, follow-up visits were scheduled every 1–2 weeks. Missed visits triggered proactive contact every two weeks to assess clinical status and adjust schedules as needed. For stable patients on the target dose of vericiguat, follow-up intervals were adjusted to every 3–6 months.

Data collection and definitions

Demographic and clinical data, including age, gender, body mass index (BMI), medications, and comorbidities, were collected by trained staff using standardized questionnaires. Venous blood samples were drawn the following morning after at least 8 h of fasting to measure laboratory parameters such as NT-proBNP, serum creatinine (Cr), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C).

Data processing

All participants included in the final analysis had at least one valid post-baseline NT-proBNP measurement. No data imputation was performed for missing NT-proBNP values at individual visits, and the following rules were applied: (1) If NT-proBNP data were missing at the X-th visit but a ≥ 30% reduction was observed at a subsequent visit with no prior record of such reduction, the endpoint was assigned to this subsequent visit. (2) Participants with only one post-baseline measurement and no occurrence of the primary endpoint were treated as censored observations.

Echocardiographic assessment

Echocardiographic parameters, including left atrial diameter (LAD), left ventricular (LV) end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD), right ventricular diameter (RVD), left ventricular (LV) end-diastolic volume (LVEDV), LV end-systolic volume (LVESV) and LVEF, were measured at baseline and at the study endpoint by trained staff at Qilu Hospital of Shandong University.

Definition

Asymptomatic low systolic blood pressure (LSBP): SBP ≤ 100 mmHg without symptoms of hypotension, such as dizziness. HF exacerbation: Use of intravenous diuretics within 3 months or hospitalization for HF within 6 months.

Details regarding SBP-related dose adjustments or treatment discontinuation are provided in Table S1.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR, 25th and 75th percentiles) according to data distribution. Qualitative variables were expressed as frequency (percentage). Quantitative variables meeting normality assumptions were compared using paired or independent samples t-tests; non-normally distributed quantitative variables were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Intergroup comparisons of categorical variables were performed using chi-square tests. Cox regression models were used to identify factors associated with endpoint events. Multivariate Cox regression further evaluated the association between exposure factors and event incidence. The Kaplan-Meier method analyzed the effect of vericiguat on the primary outcome of significant NT-proBNP reduction. To enhance robustness, sensitivity analyses excluded subjects with baseline LVEF > 40%. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA) and R software version 4.3.0 (R Statistical Computing Foundation). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. To address potential bias from non-random treatment allocation, a non-rebalancing 1:2 PSM algorithm with a caliper width of 0.1was employed. Multivariate logistic regression estimated propensity scores, with vericiguat use as the dependent variable and gender, age, renal function, NYHA class, NT-proBNP level, and LVEF as covariates.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Of the 164 patients initially screened, 97 were ultimately included in the analysis after 1:2 PSM, with 61 (62.80%) receiving vericiguat treatment. Detailed results of covariate balance are presented in Table S6 and Figure S2. The median age of the study population was 55 years, with males accounting for 73.20% (n = 71). The median systolic blood pressure was 95 mmHg and the median NT-proBNP level was 512.00 pg/ml. No significant differences were observed between groups in age, sex, blood pressure, comorbidities, HF etiology, echocardiographic parameters, or laboratory indicators (P > 0.05). GDMT usage rates were comparable between the vericiguat group and the control group: ACEI/ ARB/ ARNI (93.44% vs. 100.00%, P = 0.298), β-blockers (96.72% vs. 100.00%, P = 0.528), MRA (96.72% vs. 94.44%, P = 0.987), and SGLT2i (98.36% vs. 97.22%, P = 1.000) (Table 1).

The impact of vericiguat on outcomes

The median follow-up duration was 18 months, with a maximum follow-up of 30 months. The incidence of significant NT-proBNP reduction was 68.80% in the vericiguat group versus 47.20% in the control group. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated a significantly higher cumulative incidence of NT-proBNP reduction in the vericiguat group compared to the control group (hazard ratio (HR) = 2.54, 95% CI: 1.42–4.55, P < 0.001) (Fig. 2). Univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that NT-proBNP levels and the use of vericiguat were significant predictors of the primary outcome (Table 2). Multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that vericiguat use was an independent predictor of the primary outcome (HR = 2.34, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.30–4.21, P = 0.004; Model 3) (Table 3). At the follow-up endpoint, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of SBP, DBP, and eGFR indicators (P > 0.05) (Table S2). Dynamic assessment of blood pressure changes revealed that SBP decrease slightly at the 3-month follow-up in both groups, followed by a sustained gradual recovery during subsequent visits. Comparisons of SBP between groups throughout the study showed no statistically significant differences. Similarly, the incidence of severe hypotension and syncope did not differ significantly between the two groups (3.28% in the vericiguat group vs. 8.33% in the control group, p = 0.540) (Table S3). Sensitivity analysis

A sensitivity analysis was conducted after excluding patients with baseline LVEF > 40%, including 79 HFrEF patients. Results confirmed that the incidence of significant NT-proBNP reduction remained higher in the vericiguat group than in the control group (HR = 2.36, 95% CI: 1.27–4.37, P = 0.004) (Figure S1). Univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that baseline NT-proBNP levels and vericiguat use were significant predictors of the primary outcome (Table S4). Multivariate Cox regression analysis further confirmed vericiguat use as an independent predictor of NT-proBNP reduction (HR = 2.29, 95% CI: 1.22–4.28, P = 0.010; Model 3) (Table S5).

Dosing of HF medications

In the vericiguat treatment group, most patients (83.60%) initiated treatment at 2.5 mg/day, while the remaining patients (16.40%) started at 1.25 mg/day. By the end of the follow-up period, the mean vericiguat dose in the treatment group was 8.40 mg, with 70.50% of patients reaching the target vericiguat dose (10 mg/day). At baseline, the proportion of patients receiving quadruple therapy in the vericiguat group was 91.80%, with MRA and SGLT2i usage rates of 96.72% and 98.36%, respectively. In the control group, the corresponding rates were 94.44%and 97.22%. At baseline, 26.80% of patients achieved the ARNI target dose, increasing to 59.70% at follow-up. Concurrently, the achievement rate of the beta-blocker target dose rose from 21.60% to 50.50%. At the follow-up endpoint, there was no statistically significant difference between the vericiguat and the control groups in achieving the target ARNI dose (vericiguat group 62.30% vs. control group 55.60%, p = 0.513) or in achieving target beta-blocker dosages between the two groups (54.10% in the vericiguat group vs. 44.40% in the control group, p = 0.358). Discussion

Our study concluded that: (1) In HFrEF patients receiving current optimal GDMT and experiencing AsxHypo, vericiguat treatment is significantly correlated with a ≥ 30% reduction in NT-proBNP levels during follow-up. (2) In this observational study, we adopted an individualized regimen with low-dose and gradual drug titration. Patients maintained regular daily blood pressure monitoring and showed high treatment adherence, which complied with the titration principles for patients with hypotension and reduced the incidence of adverse events to a certain extent. Consequently, few adverse events were recorded and no major safety signals were identified, resulting in inadequate statistical power to rule out clinically meaningful differences. Accordingly, the apparent similarity in adverse event rates between groups should not be regarded as evidence of equivalent safety. Larger studies with sufficient statistical power are needed to further assess the safety of vericiguat.

This patient cohort exhibits unique characteristics: (1) Over 95.00% of patients were receiving SGLT2 inhibitors and 94.80% were treated with ARNI at baseline. (2) Compared to the VICTOR study, all patients in this study had baseline SBP ≤ 100 mmHg and a median NT-proBNP level of 512.00 pg/ml, significantly lower than the 1375.00 pg/ml reported in the VICTOR study.

Patients with HFrEF and hypotension represent a relatively common clinical population. Multiple clinical studies have confirmed that hypotension is closely associated with adverse outcomes in HFrEF patients, primarily due to reduced medication tolerance and difficulty achieving target doses in GDMT [6–8]. For example, a Swedish clinical study demonstrated that low baseline systolic blood pressure correlates with higher cardiovascular event risk, but this association attenuated in patients receiving optimized GDMT, highlighting hypotension as a significant prognostic factor for heart failure [20]. However, nearly all randomized clinical trials exclude this patient population. Consequently, developing standardized management strategies for this population remains a clinical challenge. In 2025, the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) released its first dedicated consensus statement on clinical management and treatment optimization for HFrEF patients with hypotension. This consensus explicitly recommends that therapeutic agents should not be discontinued prematurely in asymptomatic or non-severe hypotensive HFrEF patients. Instead, clinicians should conduct a comprehensive assessment of reversible causes of hypotension and review concomitant medications. This statement further emphasizes that even in patients with hypotensive manifestations, comprehensive optimization of GDMT should be pursued whenever possible to maximize patient outcomes [21].

Vericiguat, a novel soluble sGC stimulator, primarily acts by directly stimulating sGC and enhancing its sensitivity to nitric oxide (NO), restoring the NO-sGC- cyclic guanosine monophosphatec (cGMP) signaling pathway. This mechanism significantly increases coronary blood flow and myocardial microcirculation, inhibits myocardial hypertrophy, fibrosis, and remodeling, while exerting minimal effects on blood pressure, renal function, and electrolyte levels [13, 14, 22–24]. Subsequent VICTORY meta-analysis demonstrated that vericiguat significantly reduces the risk of first heart failure hospitalization or cardiovascular death in HFrEF patients [18]. Previous clinical studies of vericiguat primarily enrolled patients with SBP ≥ 100 mmHg. This study represents the first exploratory investigation in HFrEF patients with SBP ≤ 100 mmHg, confirming that low SBP is not a contraindication to GDMT. This population tolerated vericiguat well and experienced further reductions in NT-proBNP levels, consistent with prior findings [25, 26]. These results provide new evidence-based medical evidence for optimizing treatment strategies in this patient cohort.

The 2021 ESC Heart Failure Guidelines recommend ACEIs, ARBs, ARNIs, beta-blockers, MRAs, and SGLT2i as Class IA therapy for HFrEF [15]. In this study, the baseline quadruple therapy usage rate was 92.7%, with 91.8% in the vericiguat group and 94.4% in the control group. Notably, baseline SGLT2i usage in this study reached 97.9%, substantially higher than in the key VICTORIA trial for vericiguat (4.2% SGLT2i usage) and the subsequent VICTOR trial (59.1% SGLT2i usage). Additionally, baseline ARNI usage was 94.8%, with 59.7% of patients achieving target ARNI dosage at follow-up completion. It was higher than the 52.4% reported by our team in a prior study of sacubitril/valsartan in HFrEF patients with asymptomatic hypotension [27]. These differences fully reflect the treatment regimen in this study better aligns with current advanced diagnostic and therapeutic concepts in the HFrEF field, thereby enhancing the clinical relevance and applicability of the findings. The study results demonstrated that even in patients already receiving comprehensive GDMT treatment, vericiguat is correlated with a substantial reduction in NT-proBNP levels, further confirming its potential to provide additional therapeutic benefit in optimized HFrEF treatment regimens.

Most patients with new-onset HF gradually transition to chronic HF. Following standardized GDMT, clinical symptoms typically stabilize over months to years. However, even in clinically stable patients, the risk of clinical deterioration and residual mortality remains substantial [28]. A meta-analysis of pharmacologic therapies for HFrEF patients demonstrated that adding vericiguat to standard quadruple therapy (ARNI/ACEI/ARB, β-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) resulted in a 70% relative reduction in mortality risk and an increase of 0.7 life-years compared to placebo [29]. The VICTORY study further confirmed that vericiguat significantly reduces the risk of cardiovascular death, all-cause mortality, and sudden death, even in patients receiving optimized baseline therapy. Moreover, the clinical benefits are more pronounced in patients with NT-proBNP < 6000 pg/ml, establishing vericiguat’s irreplaceable role in the comprehensive management of HFrEF patients [18]. Numerous studies on HFrEF have demonstrated that patients with substantial NT-proBNP reduction exhibit markedly lower risks of cardiovascular death and heart failure rehospitalization [30–32]. Results indicate that vericiguat retains significant clinical efficacy even in patients with low baseline NT-proBNP levels, suggesting this treatment regimen may confer potential benefits in subsequent cardiovascular mortality and heart failure hospitalization outcomes.

The results revealed that SBP experienced a slight reduction in both groups at the 3-month follow-up, followed by a continuous increase throughout subsequent assessments. This pattern of blood pressure changes is in line with our previous studies on sacubitril/valsartan and subgroup analyses of the VICTORIA trial [27, 33]. Post-hoc analyses of multiple prior studies have also confirmed that SBP gradually recovers in heart failure patients with baseline hypotension after treatment with diverse anti-heart failure medications [10, 34–36]. The efficacy and safety of these agents are not influenced by baseline blood pressure, which further validates the reliability of our findings.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as a real-world observational study, treatment selection was determined by both patients and clinicians, making it difficult to full eliminate potential selection bias and confounding factors. Although propensity score matching was used to balance baseline characteristics between groups and minimize systematic bias, residue and unmeasured confounders may still have influenced outcomes. Second, incomplete follow-up due to loss to follow-up or financial constraints, along with missing NT-proBNP data at certain time points, may have affected the accuracy and generalizability of our conclusions. Moreover, The primary endpoint of this study was a relative NT-proBNP reduction of ≥ 30%, which is a surrogate endpoint rather than hard clinical endpoints, such as all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, or heart failure hospitalization. Although a ≥ 30% reduction in NT-proBNP has been correlated with improved clinical outcomes in previous investigations, the findings of this study cannot be directly extrapolated to benefits in hard endpoints. Finally, the safety analysis was limited by the small sample size and rare adverse events. The P value of 0.54 reflects insufficient statistical power rather than evidence of no true difference. Thus, the safety findings of this study are exploratory. Future research should include prospective, randomized controlled trials with larger sample size and multicenter designs to validate these observations.

Conclusion

In patients with HFrEF and AsxHypo who are clinically stable and receiving optimized GDMT, vericiguat is correlated with a substantial reduction in NT-proBNP levels. This suggests that vericiguat may offer additional therapeutic benefit in this specific patient population. Given the small number of adverse events and the lack of major safety signals, the between-group comparison with a P value of 0.54 only indicated no statistically significant differences in safety. Equivalent safety between the two groups could not be confirmed. Large-scale studies with adequate statistical power are warranted to better evaluate the safety of vericiguat.
