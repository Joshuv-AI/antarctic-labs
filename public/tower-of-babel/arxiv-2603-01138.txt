Explicit formula of the critical mass and the energy ground state solution for the mixed Local-nonlocal Schrödinger equation for the in-between critical exponents case Thanks: This work is supported by the Natural Science Research Project of Anhui Educational Committee (Grant No. 2023AH040155).


Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Schrödinger equation with the mixed local-nonlocal operator in the presence of a power nonlinearity u ​ | u | p − 2 u|u|^{p-2} , when the exponent p p is between the two critical exponents 2 + 4 ​ s N 2+\frac{4s}{N} (of the fractional Laplacian), and 2 + 4 N 2+\frac{4}{N} ( of the Laplacian). The equation presents a new phenomena that we will call here “the in-between critical exponents” p ∈ ( 2 + 4 ​ s N , 2 + 4 N ) . p\in(2+\frac{4s}{N},2+\frac{4}{N}). In this case, there exists a critical mass c 0 c_{0} , such that for any mass c < c 0 c<c_{0} there is no energy ground state solution, and for any mass c ⩾ c 0 c\geqslant c_{0} there exists an energy ground state solution. Our first main contribution consists in establishing an explicit formula of the critical mass c 0 c_{0} via the best constant of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for the mixed local-nonlocal Laplacian. We also prove the existence of an optimizer of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for the mixed local-nonlocal operator. We then show that the optimizer (after some suitable scaling) is an energy ground state solution u c 0 u_{c_{0}} (with critical mass c 0 c_{0} ). This is a key ingredient to determine sufficient and necessary conditions of existence and non-existence of energy ground state solutions in the in-between critical exponents case. Finally, we show that the energy ground state solution u c 0 u_{c_{0}} is an optimizer of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for the mixed local-nonlocal operator.

1.
Introduction

We study the following Schrödinger equation with a mixed local-nonlocal operator

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\Delta\psi-(-\Delta)^{s}\psi=|\psi|^{p-2}\psi,\ \ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where
N\geqslant 3
,
s\in(0,1)
,
2+\frac{4s}{N}<p<2+\frac{4}{N}
,
and
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the so-called fractional
Laplacian, which can be defined, for any
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
smooth enough, by setting

\mathcal{F}((-\Delta)^{s}u)(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi)
,

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
where
\mathcal{F}
represents the Fourier transform, see
[
24
]
.

Equation (
1.1
) arises in the population dynamics model with both classical and nonlocal diffusion see Dipierro-Lippi-Valdinocci
[
14
]
.
Moreover, Biagi-Dipierro-Valdinoci-Vecchi
[
1
]
pointed out that equation (
1.1
) can apply to study the different types of “regional” or “global” restrictions that may reduce the spreading of a pandemic disease.
As explained in
[
13
]
by Dipierro-Valdinocci,
equation (
1.1
) also describes an ecological niche for a mixed local and nonlocal dispersal.
For a more detailed account, we refer the reader to Bernstein-type regularity results

[
5
]
,
the Aubry-Mather theory for sums of different fractional Laplacians

[
12
]
, numerics
[
2
]
,
probability and stochastics

[
23
,
10
]
.
The sum and the difference of two or more fractional Laplacians appear
in many other fields, we refer the reader to page 2 of Chen-Bhakta-Hajaiej
[
9
]
,
and also refer to
[
19
,
22
,
7
,
8
,
16
]
.

The standing waves to equation (
1.1
) are

\displaystyle\psi(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}u(x),

where
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. It is straightforward to see that a standing wave
\psi
is a solution to equation (
1.1
) if and only if
u
is a solution to the following Schrödinger equation

(MLN)

\displaystyle-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u+\omega u=|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

If
u
is a weak solution of equation (
MLN
),
then for any
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
it satisfies

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\varphi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p-2}u\varphi\mathrm{d}x.

Su-Valdinoci-Wei-Zhang

[
25
,
26
]

and Dipierro-Su-Valdinoci-Zhang

[
15
]
studied the regularity of solutions to equation (
MLN
).
In this current paper,
we address another highly important aspect.
We are looking for standing waves with prescribed mass for equation (
MLN
).
From a physical point of view,
the most interesting solutions,
the so-called
energy ground state solution
,
are the minimizers of the problem

\displaystyle m_{c}:=\inf_{u\in S_{c}}J(u),

where

\displaystyle S_{c}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=c\right\}.

Here, the energy functional is

\displaystyle J(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

We know that each minimizer
u\in S_{c}
of
m_{c}
can be associated to a Lagrange multiplier
\omega>0
such that

(u,\omega)
weakly solves (
MLN
).

There are two important exponents related to (
MLN
):

\displaystyle 2+\frac{4s}{N},

and

\displaystyle 2+\frac{4}{N},

where the exponent
2+\frac{4}{N}
is the mass-critical exponent for the classical Laplacian problem,
and the exponent
2+\frac{4s}{N}
is the mass-critical exponent for the fractional Laplacian problem.
In the mixed local-nonlcoal operator setting,
the exponent
2+\frac{4}{N}
is the mass critical exponent.
Although the exponent
2+\frac{4s}{N}
is not the mass-critical exponent in the mixed local-nonlcoal operator problem, it brings a new phenomenon:
The in-between critical exponents case

\displaystyle 2+\frac{4s}{N}<p<2+\frac{4}{N}.

This new phenomenon arises from the “conflict” between the local operator and nonlocal operator, see Theorem
1.1
.

[
21
]
considered the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation with the mixed fractional Laplacians (
0<s_{1}<s_{2}<1

(MF)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}u+(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}u+\omega u=|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

When
s_{2}=1
,
equation (
MF
) reduces to equation (

Let us recall the main results of
[

]

Let

1.1.
Main Results

In order to characterize the critical mass

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

and
p\in(2+\frac{4s}{N},2+\frac{4}{N})

For

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\leqslant C_{1,s,p}\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{4-N(p-2)}{2(1-s)}}\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{N(p-2)-4s}{2(1-s)}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-2}.

Also,
there exists a non-negative radial optimizer
Q\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for inequality (
1.2
),

\displaystyle-\Delta Q+(-\Delta)^{s}Q+\omega Q=|Q|^{p-2}Q,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

By virtue of Theorem
1.2

Furthermore,

Theorem 1.3

Let

,

s\in(0,1)

and

.
Then we have the following results.

(1)

Let

be the critical mass.

\displaystyle C_{1,s,p}=\left[\frac{2N+4-pN}{2p(1-s)}\right]^{-\frac{2N+4-pN}{4(1-s)}}\left[\frac{pN-2N-4s}{2p(1-s)}\right]^{-\frac{pN-2N-4s}{4(1-s)}}\frac{1}{c_{0}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}},

\displaystyle c_{0}:=C_{1,s,p}^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}\left[\frac{2N+4-pN}{2p(1-s)}\right]^{-\frac{2N+4-pN}{2(1-s)(p-2)}}\left[\frac{pN-2N-4s}{2p(1-s)}\right]^{-\frac{pN-2N-4s}{2(1-s)(p-2)}}.

2.
Sobolev Spaces

Define the homogeneous Sobolev space

\displaystyle D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})||\nabla u|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\},

its semi-norm is defined as

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

For
N\geqslant 3
and
s\in(0,1)
, let
D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space, which is the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the semi-norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Define the inhomogeneous Sobolev space by:

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})||\nabla u|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\},

its norm is defined as

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Define the inhomogeneous fractional Sobolev space by:

\displaystyle H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})|\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}<\infty\},

its norm is defined as

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Lemma 2.1

(Continuous embedded)
.

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

From
[
24
]
,
Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities,
we have that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{2s}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{(2-2s)\frac{2}{2-2s}}\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{2-2s}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2s}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2-2s}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2},

which proves the result.
∎

For
p\in(2,2^{*})
and
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
27
]
, we know that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\leqslant C\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-\frac{N(p-2)}{2}}.

For
q\in(2,\frac{2N}{N-2s})
and
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we know that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{q}\leqslant C\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{2s}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{q-\frac{N(q-2)}{2s}}.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section,
we prove Theorem
1.1
,
i.e.,
the existence and nonexistence of energy ground state solutions in the in-between critical exponents case.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\in(2+\frac{4s}{N},2+\frac{4}{N})
and
c>c_{0}
.
Then we have, for all
c^{\prime}>c
,

\displaystyle m_{c^{\prime}}\leqslant\left[\frac{c^{\prime}}{c}\right]^{\frac{2(p-2)}{4-N(p-2)}+1}m_{c}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle m_{c}<m_{\bar{c}}+m_{c-\bar{c}},

for all
\bar{c}\in(0,c)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in S_{c}
and
\bar{u}(x):=(\frac{c^{\prime}}{c})^{a}u((\frac{c^{\prime}}{c})^{b}x)
,
where

\displaystyle a=\frac{2}{4-N(p-2)},\penalty\ \penalty\ b=\frac{p-2}{4-N(p-2)}.

Then we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\bar{u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle(c^{\prime})^{2a-bN}c^{1-2a+bN}=c^{\prime}.

A straigthfoward computation implies that:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\bar{u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle(\frac{c^{\prime}}{c})^{2a+2b-bN}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=

\displaystyle(\frac{c^{\prime}}{c})^{2a+2bs-bN}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\bar{u}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle(\frac{c^{\prime}}{c})^{pa-bN}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

Then
\bar{u}(x)\in S_{c^{\prime}}
, and since
2a+2b-bN=pa-bN
,
we get that

\displaystyle m_{c^{\prime}}\leqslant

\displaystyle J(\bar{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1,s,p}c^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\leqslant

.

Let

.

that

\displaystyle\min\limits_{t>0}J(u_{c_{0},t})=\min\limits_{t>0}h_{u_{c_{0}}}(t)=0=h_{u_{c_{0}}}(t_{u_{c_{0}}}),

\displaystyle 1=t_{u_{c_{0}}}=\left[\frac{N(p-2)-4s}{2p(1-s)}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{c_{0}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x}{\|u_{c_{0}}\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}\right]^{\frac{2}{4-N(p-2)}},

which implies that

(7.1)

\displaystyle\|u_{c_{0}}\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=\frac{N(p-2)-4s}{2p(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{c_{0}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

(7.2)

\displaystyle\|u_{c_{0}}\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{c_{0}}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\|u_{c_{0}}\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}

Proof of Theorem

In this section,

Since the proof is similar to Theorems

-

Lemma 8.1
.

For
p\in(2+\frac{4s_{1}}{N},2+\frac{4s_{2}}{N})
and
u\in H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
there exists a constant
C_{s_{2},s_{1},p}>0
such that

(8.1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\leqslant C_{s_{2},s_{1},p}\|u\|_{D^{s_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{2N+4s_{2}-pN}{2(s_{2}-s_{1})}}\|u\|_{D^{s_{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{pN-2N+4s_{1}}{2(s_{2}-s_{1})}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-2}.

Proof.

Taking
q=2+\frac{4s_{1}}{N}
and
q=2+\frac{4s_{2}}{N}
in (
2.2
), we have that

(8.2)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2+\frac{4s_{1}}{N}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2+\frac{4s_{1}}{N}}\leqslant

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{D^{s_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{4s_{1}}{N}}.

and

(8.3)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2+\frac{4s_{2}}{N}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2+\frac{4s_{2}}{N}}\leqslant

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{D^{s_{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{4s_{2}}{N}}.

Applying the Hölder inequality for
p\in(2+\frac{4s_{1}}{N},2+\frac{4s_{2}}{N})
, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2+\frac{4s_{1}}{N}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{(2+\frac{4s_{2}}{N})-p}{\frac{4}{N}(s_{2}-s_{1})}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2+\frac{4s_{2}}{N}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p-(2+\frac{4s_{1}}{N})}{\frac{4}{N}(s_{2}-s_{1})}},

and then using (
8.2
) and (
8.3
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C\|u\|_{D^{s_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{2N+4s_{2}-pN}{2(s_{2}-s_{1})}}\|u\|_{D^{s_{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{pN-2N+4s_{1}}{2(s_{2}-s_{1})}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-2}.

∎

We define the Weinstein functional

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{p}(u)=\frac{\|u\|_{D^{s_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{2N+4s_{2}-pN}{2(s_{2}-s_{1})}}\|u\|_{D^{s_{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{pN-2N+4s_{1}}{2(s_{2}-s_{1})}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-2}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}}

Similar to Theorem
1.2
,
we know that there exists
\bar{w}\in H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}
such that

\displaystyle\|\bar{w}\|_{D^{s_{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\bar{w}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=1,\penalty\ \bar{W}_{p}(\bar{w})=C_{s_{2},s_{1},p}^{-1},

where
\bar{w}
is a radial non-negative minimizer for the Weinstein functional
\bar{W}_{p}
,
and
\bar{w}
is a weak solution to the following equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}\bar{w}+\frac{2N+4s_{2}-pN}{pN-2N-4s_{1}}(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}\bar{w}\frac{1}{\|\bar{w}\|_{D^{s_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2(p-2)(s_{2}-s_{1})}{pN-2N-4s_{1}}\bar{w}=\frac{2p(s_{2}-s_{1})}{pN-2N-4s_{1}}|w|^{p-2}w\frac{1}{\|\bar{w}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}}.

We define

(8.4)

\displaystyle\bar{Q}(x)=\lambda\bar{w}(\mu x),

where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mu=\left[\frac{2N+4s_{2}-pN}{pN-2N-4s_{1}}\frac{1}{\|\bar{w}\|_{D^{s_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}},\\
\lambda=\left[\frac{2p(s_{2}-s_{1})}{pN-2N-4s_{1}}\frac{1}{\|\bar{w}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-2}}\left[\frac{2N+4s_{2}-pN}{pN-2N-4s_{1}}\frac{1}{\|\bar{w}\|_{D^{s_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}\right]^{\frac{2s_{2}}{2(s_{1}-s_{2})(p-2)}},\end{cases}

then

satisfies

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}\bar{Q}+(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}\bar{Q}+\omega\bar{Q}=|\bar{Q}|^{p-2}\bar{Q},

with

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{2(p-2)(s_{2}-s_{1})}{pN-2N-4s_{1}}\left[\frac{2N+4s_{2}-pN}{pN-2N-4s_{1}}\frac{1}{\|\bar{w}\|_{D^{s_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}\right]^{\frac{2s_{2}}{2(s_{1}-s_{2})(p-2)}}.

Particularly, we know that
\bar{Q}
is non-negative and radially symmetric.

Using (
8.4
) and the arguments in Lemmas
5.1
-
5.3

\displaystyle\|\bar{Q}\|^{2}_{D^{s_{1},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\frac{2N+4s_{2}-pN}{2p(s_{2}-s_{1})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\bar{Q}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x,

and

\displaystyle\|\bar{Q}\|^{2}_{D^{s_{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\frac{pN-2N-4s_{1}}{2p(s_{2}-s_{1})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\bar{Q}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x,

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\bar{Q}(x)-\bar{Q}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{2}}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\bar{Q}(x)-\bar{Q}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{1}}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\bar{Q}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=0.
