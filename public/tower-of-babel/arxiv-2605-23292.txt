Second-order Poincaré inequalities and localization on the Poisson space
Tara Trauthwein, J. E. Yukich

Abstract
Given a mean zero functional F F of a Poisson measure on a metric space, we apply the Malliavin-Stein method to establish sharpened second-order Poincaré inequalities for F / Var ⁡ ( F ) F/\sqrt{{\rm Var}(F)} in terms of fourth moments of difference operators. The rates of normal approximation are expressed in the Kolmogorov and Wasserstein distances and require fewer error terms than corresponding previous results. When F F is expressible as a sum of score functions which are distributionally close to scores having short-range structure, then we deduce that F / Var ⁡ ( F ) F/\sqrt{{\rm Var}(F)} satisfies Berry-Esseen bounds. The normal approximation criteria of the scores, here called bounded Lipschitz localization, are more general than stabilization criteria and allow for unbounded interactions of scores. This approach yields Berry-Esseen bounds for local U-statistics on metric measures spaces, localizing functionals on hyperbolic space, as well as for Poisson functionals in a space-time setting, with infinite time horizon, including statistics of spatial birth-growth models and Laguerre tessellations.



Key words and phrases:
Stein’s method, central limit theorems, Malliavin calculus, localizing scores, stochastic geometry, Kolmogorov and Wasserstein distances

Primary 60F05; Secondary 60D05

Introduction

Consider a Poisson point process
\mathcal{P}
on a measure space
(\mathbb{X},\nu)
, with
\nu
a
\sigma
-finite measure. Let
F=f({\cal P})
be a measurable function of
{\cal P}
. Under what conditions on
F
can one obtain good proximity bounds between
F
and a standard normal
N
? More precisely, if
{\bf{d}}
is metric on the space of probability laws, when does one obtain the second-order Poincaré inequality

{\bf{d}}\left(\frac{F-\mathbb{E}\,F}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}F}},N\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}F}}\right)\ ?

(1.1)

The Berry-Esseen bound (
1.1
) is unimprovable in general and represents an optimal quantitative central limit theorem for Poisson functionals.

This question, and more generally the study of qualitative and quantitative
normal convergence of functionals of point processes have a long history. Bickel and Breiman
[
7
]
in 1983 established the normal convergence of the total edge length of nearest neighbor graphs on i.i.d. binomial input in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and noted: ‘Our proof is long. We believe this is due to the complexity of the problem.’ Kesten and Lee
[
18
]

in 1996, when proving that the total edge length of the minimal spanning tree on Poisson input on growing cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
converged to the normal wrote: ‘Another drawback of our approach is that it is not quantitative. Further ideas are needed to obtain an
error estimate in our central limit theorem’.
In the case that
(\mathbb{X},\nu)
is Euclidean space,
\nu
Lebesgue measure, and
F=f({\cal P})
is representable as a sum of scores depending a.s. on local data, the work
[
30
]
developed
proximity bounds in the Kolmogorov distance between

\hat{F}:=(F-\mathbb{E}\,F)/\sqrt{{\rm Var}F}
and
N
, but these bounds are suboptimal.

It was not until
2016
that a systematic investigation of proximity bounds between

\hat{F}
and
N
was undertaken with the aid of the Malliavin-Stein method. The pioneering paper of Last, Peccati and Schulte
[
23
]
established the remarkably versatile second-order Poincaré inequality

{\mathbf{d}_{K}}\left(\frac{F-\mathbb{E}\,F}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}F}},N\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{6}\gamma_{i}

(1.2)

where the
formulae for
\gamma_{i},1\leq i\leq 6,
involve integrals of products of difference operators, e.g.,

\gamma_{1}=\left[\int_{\mathbb{X}^{3}}[\mathbb{E}\,(D_{x}F)^{2}(D_{y}F)^{2}]^{1/2}[\mathbb{E}\,(D_{x,y}F)^{2}(D_{y,z}F)^{2}]^{1/2}\mu^{3}(dx,dy,dz)\right]^{1/2},

with similar geometry based formulae for
\gamma_{i},2\leq i\leq 6.
Thus the Kolmogorov distance
{\mathbf{d}_{K}}
is controlled by integrals
of products of moments of the first order difference operators

D_{x}F=f({\cal P}+\delta_{x})-f({\cal P})
as well as of the second order difference operators
D^{(2)}_{x,y}F=D_{x}D_{y}F
. To quote
[
23
]
, the error of normal approximation has a ‘significantly more complex structure’ than the Poincaré inequalities of
[
12
]

and
[
26
]
.

This raises the question: In view of (
1.1
) are there relatively simple verifiable criteria under which the errors
\gamma_{i},1\leq i\leq 6,
in (
1.2
), collapse into terms which are
O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}F}})
? Secondly, does the simplicity of the criteria reveal
quantitative central limit theorems for Poisson functionals previously not known to satisfy asymptotic normality?

We answer both questions in the affirmative when
(\mathbb{X},\nu)
is a metric measure space and show that if the Poisson functional
F
is a sum of scores which are distributionally well-approximated by short-range scores, then
F
satisfies
the Berry-Esseen bound (
1.1
).
The rates are
in general unimprovable and hold for
Poisson functionals on general metric spaces, including hyperbolic space, and including functionals of
Poisson input which may have an infinite time component, as well as for scores
which do not necessarily depend on local data. This extends Penrose and Yukich
[
30
]
, Lachièze-Rey et al.

[
20
]
, Lachièze-Rey
[
21
]
,
Lachièze-Rey et al.
[
19
]
and
Bhattacharjee and Molchanov
[
6
]
; most of these works were either confined to Euclidean space and/or assumed that the scores satisfied strong localization conditions, without allowing for an infinite time component.

Our approach consists of two parts, the first of which extends

[
23
]
and
[
39
]
. Given a Poisson functional
F
, we apply the Malliavin-Stein method to obtain rates of normal convergence of
\hat{F}
in terms of fourth moments of difference operators, and requiring fewer error terms than the corresponding results in
[
23
,
39
]
. This strictly improves the corresponding rate results in
[
23
,
39
]
, as the error terms
\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4}
in
[
23
]
and
\gamma_{7}^{(2)}
in
[
39
]
are shown to be superfluous for the Kolmogorov distance.
Removing these terms is satisfying, as e.g.
\gamma_{4}

requires control of the fourth moment of
F
, often a source of technical difficulty.
The improved second order Poincaré bounds established here, given in terms of the Kolmogorov and Wasserstein distances, are the cleanest and shortest second order Poincaré inequalities currently available for general Poisson functionals, when working with fourth moment conditions on the difference operators. The bounds are stated in full generality in Theorem
2.1
and include functionals of
marked
Poisson point processes.

In a second part, we apply the general Theorem
2.1
to Poisson functionals
expressible as a sum of scores

H:=\sum_{z\in{\cal P}\cap W}\xi((z,M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}),

where
\xi
are measurable functions,
W\subset\mathbb{X}
is a window with finite measure,
{\cal P}
is a Poisson measure on
W
,
\{M_{z}\}_{z\in{\cal P}}
are i.i.d. marks, and
{\hat{{\cal P}}}:=\{(z,M_{z})\}_{z\in{\cal P}}
is the Poisson process
{\cal P}
equipped with independent marks.

Our localization criterion consists of comparing, for each
r>0
, the distribution of
\xi
with the distribution of a suitably chosen
short-range score

\xi^{[r]}
, where for any locally finite
\chi
the score
\xi^{[r]}((z,M_{z}),\chi)
depends only on the input in the radius
r
ball around
z\in\mathbb{X}
, namely on
\chi\cap(B_{r}(z)\times\mathbb{M})
.
We often choose
\xi^{[r]}
to be the restriction of
\xi
to a radius
r
ball, i.e.,

\xi^{[r]}((w,M_{w}),\chi)=\xi((w,M_{w}),\chi\cap(B_{r}(z)\times\mathbb{M}))
, but the approach here allows for more general choices of
\xi^{[r]}
.
We show that as soon as
\xi
and the family
(\xi^{[r]})_{r>0}
satisfy a fifth moment condition as well as either

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{X}}\sup_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}(\xi((z,M_{z}),{\cal P}\cup\mathcal{A})\neq\xi^{[r]}((z,M_{z}),{\cal P}\cup\mathcal{A}))\leq\psi(r),\quad r>0

or the slightly weaker condition

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{X}}\sup_{\mathcal{A}}||\xi((z,M_{z}),{\cal P}\cup\mathcal{A})-\xi^{[r]}((z,M_{z}),{\cal P}\cup\mathcal{A})||_{1}\leq\psi(r),\quad r>0,

where the sup runs over sets
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{X}
of cardinality at most
6
, and
where
\psi
satisfies a mild integrability condition on
(\mathbb{X},\nu)
,
then the Berry-Esseen bound (
1.2
) holds
, i.e.

{\bf{d}}\left(\frac{H-\mathbb{E}\,H}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H}},N\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H}}\right),

(1.3)

where
{\bf{d}}
is either the Kolmogorov or Wasserstein distance and where we assume

{\rm Var}H=\Omega(\nu(W))
.
More generally
as soon as four-tuples of scores

(\xi(z_{1},{\cal P}),...,\xi(z_{4},{\cal P}))

have enough short-range structure, in the sense that they are distributionally close to
four-tuples of short-range scores

(\xi^{[r]}(z_{1},{\cal P}),...,\xi^{[r]}(z_{4},{\cal P}))
, uniformly over all four-tuples, as measured by the bounded Lipschitz distance, then the short-range interactions dominate, which is to say, heuristically speaking, that
H
behaves like a sum of independent random variables
with a distribution which is well-approximated by the normal as at
(
1.3
).

This geometric localization, called
bounded Lipschitz localization
as introduced in
[
10
]
, is weaker and more flexible than the standard stopping set stabilization criterion in
[
20
]
,
[
30
]
.
Though it requires
H
to have enough distributional short-range structure over all possible ranges, as measured by
\psi
, it also allows for interactions of scores at distant points.

Our general quantitative CLT for

\hat{H}:=(H-\mathbb{E}\,H)/\sqrt{{\rm Var}H}
holds
for Poisson input on general metric measure spaces, in contrast to
[
21
,
19
,
20
,
30
,
38
]
. The general main results given by Theorem
2.7
and Corollaries
2.8
and
2.9

provide rates of normal convergence in the Kolmogorov and Wasserstein distances for local U-statistics on metric measure spaces and localizing functionals on
d
-dimensional hyperbolic space, including statistics of
the random geometric graph and the
k
-nearest neighbor graphs in these spaces.
It also yields precise
quantitative central limit theorems for Poisson functionals where previously only qualitative results were available. This includes statistics
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued diffusions on graphs on Poisson random input on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
9
]
.

We also consider Poisson functionals in a
space-time setting
, with
\mathbb{X}
enlarged to
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
, giving rise to sums

H:=\sum_{(z,t_{z})\in{\cal P}\cap(W\times\mathbb{R})}\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}).

Such statistics arise in general space-time growth models with infinite time horizon, including
statistics of
classic birth-growth models and Laguerre tessellations. In such models the score on
an infinite time interval is well approximated by input on a large but finite time interval.
This phenomenon is quantified through ‘time-localization’, a companion concept to ‘space-localization’.
We use Theorem
2.1
to deduce rates of normal convergence for
\hat{H}:=(H-\mathbb{E}\,H)/\sqrt{{\rm Var}H}
; see
Theorem
2.7
. This quantitative central limit theorem yields presumably optimal rates of normal convergence for the afore-mentioned statistics.

This paper focuses on rates of convergence in the univariate central limit theorem but the methods and approach can also be used to achieve rates of convergence in the multivariate central limit theorem for vectors of Poisson functionals, with each entry expressible as a sum of scores satisfying bounded Lipschitz localization, cf. Remark (
i
) in Section

Main results

In this section we present our main results in detail.

Underlying space.

We let
(\mathbb{X},\nu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space, with
\nu
a
\sigma
-finite measure on the
\sigma
-algebra

\mathcal{F}
.

Marked Poisson point processes.

Throughout, we let
\mathcal{P}
be a Poisson process on
\mathbb{X}
with intensity measure
\nu
.
In order to treat marked point processes, let
(\mathbb{M},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{M}},\mathbb{Q})
be a probability space. By
\mathbb{M}
we mean the space of marks whereas
\mathbb{Q}
is the underlying probability measure of the marks. Let
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}:=\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{M}
. By
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
we denote the product
\sigma
-field of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{M}}
and by
\widehat{\nu}
the product measure of
\nu
and
\mathbb{Q}
.
To obtain an unmarked point process, one may consider
the case where
(\mathbb{M},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{M}},\mathbb{Q})
is a singleton endowed with a Dirac point mass, and the ‘hat’ superscript can be removed in all occurrences.
For any given point
x\in\mathbb{X}
, we denote by
M_{x}
the corresponding random mark, which is distributed according to
\mathbb{Q}
and is independent of everything else.

Let
\mathbf{N}_{\widehat{\mathbb{X}}}
be the set of
\sigma
-finite counting measures on
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
, which can be interpreted as point configurations in
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
. Whenever it is clear from context, we drop the subscript and simply write
\mathbf{N}
. The set
\mathbf{N}
is equipped with the smallest
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{N}
such that the maps
m_{A}:\mathbf{N}\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0,\infty\},\mathcal{M}\mapsto\mathcal{M}(A)

A\in\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
. A point process is a random element in
\mathbf{N}
.

Malliavin Derivative.
Given
F
a measurable function of
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
and
(x,m_{x}),(y,m_{y})\in\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
, we let

D_{(x,m_{x})}F(\hat{\mathcal{P}})=F(\hat{\mathcal{P}}\cup\{(x,m_{x})\})-F(\hat{\mathcal{P}})

and

D^{(2)}_{(x,m_{x}),(y,m_{y})}F(\hat{\mathcal{P}})=D_{(x,m_{x})}D_{(y,m_{y})}F(\hat{\mathcal{P}})

be the first and second order difference operators applied to

.

Sharpened Malliavin-Stein bounds on the marked Poisson space

In this section we present significantly improved second-order Poincaré inequalities, which simplify and extend some of the work in
[
23
]
and
[
39
]
. The main result are the normal approximation bounds in Theorem
2.1
,
which both sharpen and extend the
p=2
version of Theorems
3.3 and 3.4 of
[
39
]
, which themselves improve upon Theorems
1.1 and 1.2 in
[
23
]
. In Sections
3
and
4
we apply Theorem
2.1
to
Poisson functionals which may be represented as
sums of BL-localizing score functions, but we
emphasize that this quantitative CLT applies to general functionals
on the marked Poisson space, including those which may not
be conveniently expressed as a sum of BL-localizing scores. Even for functionals on the unmarked Poisson space, this sharper result contains fewer approximating terms than the corresponding results in
[
39
]
and
[
23
]
.

The Kolmogorov distance between the laws of random variables
X
and
Y
is

\mathbf{d}_{K}(X,Y)=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{P}(X\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(Y\leq t)|

whereas the Wasserstein distance is

\mathbf{d}_{W}(X,Y)=\sup_{h\in{\rm{Lip}}(1)}|\mathbb{E}\,h(X)-\mathbb{E}\,h(Y)|,

where
{\rm{Lip}}(1)
denotes the class of Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}
with Lipschitz constant one.
Throughout
N
denotes a standard Gaussian random variable.

Theorem 2.1
.

(sharpened Malliavin-Stein bounds for Poisson functionals)
Let
(\mathbb{X},\nu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space and
(\mathbb{M},\mathbb{Q})
a space of marks with probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
be a Poisson measure on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{M}
of intensity
\nu\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. Let
F
be a measurable function of
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
such that
\mathbb{E}\,F^{2}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}\,F=0
and

\int_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{M}}\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left(D_{(x,m_{x})}F\right)^{2}\right](\nu\otimes\mathbb{Q})(dx,dm_{x})<\infty.

For any point
x\in\mathbb{X}
, define a random variable
M_{x}
independent of everything else, distributed according to
\mathbb{Q}
.
Then it holds that

\mathbf{d}_{W}(F,N)\leq\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\hat{\gamma}_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\hat{\gamma}_{1}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\hat{\gamma}_{2}+\hat{\gamma}_{3}

and

\mathbf{d}_{K}(F,N)\leq\hat{\gamma}_{0}+\hat{\gamma}_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\hat{\gamma}_{2}+\hat{\gamma}_{4}+\hat{\gamma}_{5}+\hat{\gamma}_{6},

where

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\,\left|1-{\rm Var}(F)\right|

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{1}

\displaystyle:=2\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}\,\big[|D_{(y,M_{y})}F|^{4}\big]^{1/4}\cdot\mathbb{E}\,\big[|D^{(2)}_{(x,M_{x}),(y,M_{y})}F|^{4}\big]^{1/4}\nu(dy)\bigg)^{2}\nu(dx)\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{2}

\displaystyle:=2\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}\,\big[|D^{(2)}_{(x,M_{x}),(y,M_{y})}F|^{4}\big]^{1/2}\nu(dy)\bigg)^{2}\nu(dx)\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{3}

\displaystyle:=2\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}\,|D_{(y,M_{y})}F|^{3}\nu(dy)

and

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{4}

\displaystyle:=\bigg(4\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}\,|D_{(y,M_{y})}F|^{4}\nu(dy)\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{5}

\displaystyle:=\bigg(8\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}\,|D^{(2)}_{(x,M_{x}),(y,M_{y})}F|^{4}\nu(dy)\nu(dx)\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{6}

\displaystyle:=\bigg(32\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}\,\big[|D^{(2)}_{(x,M_{x}),(y,M_{y})}F|^{4}\big]^{1/2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\,\big[|D_{(x,M_{x})}F|^{4}\big]^{1/2}\nu(dy)\nu(dx)\bigg)^{1/2}.

Remark 2.2
.

We do not assume
{\rm Var}(F)=1
and instead introduce the term
\hat{\gamma}_{0}
. This is a standard extension and can be useful in cases where the functional
F
is not normalized. Note that one easily derives that

d_{W}\left(\frac{F-\mathbb{E}\,F}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(F)}},N\right)\leq\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}{\rm Var}(F)^{-1}\hat{\gamma}_{1}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\rm Var}(F)^{-1}\hat{\gamma}_{2}+{\rm Var}(F)^{-3/2}\hat{\gamma_{3}}

and

d_{K}\left(\frac{F-\mathbb{E}\,F}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(F)}},N\right)\leq{\rm Var}(F)^{-1}\big(\hat{\gamma}_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\hat{\gamma}_{2}+\hat{\gamma}_{4}+\hat{\gamma}_{5}+\hat{\gamma}_{6}\big)

by linearity of the operators
D,D^{(2)}
.

In the following, we compare Theorem
2.1
with existing general results, all of which are less sharp and less general whenever the difference operators satisfy fourth moment assumptions.

1.

Removal of complicated terms in
[
23
]
.
In
[
23
, Theorem 1.2]
, the bound on the Kolmogorov distance is given by the sum of terms
\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{6}
. We remove two terms from the bound on the Kolmogorov distance:
\gamma_{3}
and
\gamma_{4}
. The term
\gamma_{3}
(corresponding to our
\hat{\gamma}_{3}
) only contains the first Malliavin derivative to a third power - this differs from the other terms and can in some cases lead to worse bounds. It is a (presumably) necessary term for the Wasserstein distance, but can be removed in the Kolmogorov case. The term
\gamma_{4}
in
[
23
]
has a structure that differs strongly from the other terms: it contains the term
\mathbb{E}\,F^{4}
, which is often difficult to bound and complicates matters significantly. Methods for removing some of the terms from the Kolmogorov bound first appeared in
[
19
]
, based on ideas from
[
SZ19
]
. The result in
[
19
]
required however strong additional assumptions on the functional
F
. In
[
39
]
, these additional assumptions were removed and a shortened bound was shown without any additional starting assumptions. We proceed in a similar way and show that
\gamma_{3}
and
\gamma_{4}
are unnecessary in the Kolmogorov second-order Poincaré inequality.

2.

Removal of term
\gamma_{7}
in
[
39
]
.

The development in
[
39
]
focused on achieving a bound under a lower
(2+\epsilon)
-moment assumption on
F
, and while the terms
\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4}
from
[
23
]
were removed in the Kolmogorov distance, an additional term
\gamma_{7}
appeared in the bound as an artifact of the more general proof. While
[
39
, term
\gamma_{7}
]
was much more in line with the remaining terms and thus easier to treat, we stress that when working with fourth moment assumptions on the difference operators, this term is superfluous and no such term is necessary in Theorem
2.1
.

3.

Extension to marked spaces.
In
[
23
]
, Theorem 1.1 gives a bound for the Wasserstein distance for functionals
F
of generic spaces
\mathbb{X}
. It is possible to introduce marks by choosing
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{M}
to be a marked space, but the resulting terms
\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{3}
will be different, i.e. the integrals over the marked spaces will be grouped with integrals over the space
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
, which is often unfavorable. In our result, by a careful rewriting of the proof, we achieve a setting where each point is equipped with an independent mark when assessing the add-one costs, i.e. integrals over the mark space
\mathbb{M}
are grouped with the expectation. A similar adaptation of the arguments of
[
23
]
to marked input is achieved in
[
20
]
.

BL-localization in space and time

Considerable attention has been given to systematically establishing rates of normal convergence for functionals of Poisson input on general spatial domains. The rates of normal convergence, expressed with respect to either the Kolmogorov, Wasserstein,
\ell_{2}
or
\ell_{3}
distances, all rely on Stein’s method; see e.g.
[
2
,
30
,
23
,
20
,
21
,
19
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
.
The rates of convergence in
[
23
,
39
]
, like those of Theorem
2.1
, are valid for Poisson input on general metric spaces, though evaluation of the terms

\gamma_{i},i=1,...,6,
may be challenging.

When the Poisson functional may be expressed as a sum of score functions satisfying either a stopping set stabilization criterion as in
[
20
]
or an
L^{4}
stabilization criterion as in
[
21
]
, then evaluation of the terms

\hat{\gamma}_{i},i=1,...,6,
is facilitated and leads to rates of convergence which are unimprovable in general. Up to now, these results hold in Euclidean space and not a general metric space
[
30
,
20
,
21
,
37
,
38
]
; moreover neither the stopping set stabilization nor the

L^{4}
stabilization criterion is satisfied by scores arising in certain statistics of random point sets in Euclidean space, including e.g. interacting diffusions on sparse graphs in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When the Poisson functional is a sum of score functions satisfying a weak spatial dependency condition here termed
BL-localization
and which is weaker than existing stabilization criteria, then one may systematically simplify the terms
\hat{\gamma}_{i},i=1,...,6,
in Theorem
2.1
, thereby establishing rates of convergence in the Kolmogorov and Wasserstein distances. The results are valid in an arbitrary metric measure space and the rates are optimal in general.
This is achieved by establishing bounds on the moments of the difference operators and showing that these moment bounds control the order of growth of the integrals in the expressions for
\hat{\gamma}_{i},1\leq i\leq 6,
in Theorem
2.1
. Establishing BL-localization of scores is often more transparent than finding good bounds for moments of difference operators by ad hoc methods.

In addition to considering score functions defined purely in space, we widen our scope and consider sums of scores on Poissonian space-time input on
X\times\mathbb{R}
. We shall be interested in summing scores on points in the unbounded space-time domains
W\times\mathbb{R}
, that is to say we consider input with possibly unbounded time component. We treat such statistics subject to the scores satisfying
localization in time
as well as
localization in space
. Roughly speaking, localization in time says that the difference between scores at finite time
t_{0}
and infinite time becomes negligible as
t_{0}
increases.
This condition, apparently new, is a useful companion to space-localization and it manifests in statistics of
generalized birth-growth models
[
35
]
and generalized Delaunay tessellations obtained by consideration of the projection of a parabolic hull process in space-time
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as in
[
16
]
. These statistics are related to statistics of points retained in a parabolic thinning of a space-time Poisson point processes in a half-space, which arises in solutions to the inviscid
Burgers’ equation
[
1
,
3
]
with random initial data. These applications are developed in Section
6
.

Setup.
Let

the independent mark associated to
z\in X
, distributed according to
\mathbb{Q}
. For any set
{\cal A}\subset X
, we denote by
\hat{{\cal A}}
the random set containing the points
(z,M_{z})
, where
z\in{\cal A}
. We take
\mu
to be a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\mathbb{R}
and equip
\mathbb{R}
with the Euclidean metric.

The set
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}_{X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}}
denotes the set of locally finite point measures on
X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}
. By
{\cal P}
we mean a Poisson measure on
X\times\mathbb{R}
of intensity
\nu\otimes\mu
. The set
\hat{{\cal P}}
denotes the points
(z,t_{z},M_{z})
with
(z,t_{z})\in\mathcal{P}
;
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
has the law of a Poisson process on
X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}
with intensity measure
\nu\otimes\mu\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

We consider scores

\xi:(X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})\times\mathbf{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

assumed to be measurable functions from
(X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})\times\mathbf{N}
to
\mathbb{R}
. The scores give rise to the Poisson functionals

H:=H(\hat{\mathcal{P}})=\sum_{(z,t_{z})\in\mathcal{P}\cap(W\times\mathbb{R})}\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\hat{{\cal P}}).

The sets
X\times\mathbb{R}
control the space-time domains carrying the (unmarked) input of
\xi
, whereas
W\times\mathbb{R}
is the window over which the scores are summed. Two natural cases arise:

•

W\equiv X
, i.e. the point process is constrained to a window and we sum scores over all points in
W
;

•

W\subsetneq X
, i.e. the score function
\xi
takes as input a larger, possibly infinite (in space) point process
\hat{{\cal P}}
, but we only sum scores on points inside the window
W
.

These are discussed in more detail in Subsection
2.4
. Additionally, it is natural to include scores which do not depend on time. This is treated in Corollary
2.8
.

Denote by
B_{r}(x)=B(x,r)
the open ball of radius
r
around the point
x\in X
with respect to the metric
d
. Given a subset
X_{0}\subset X
, we define
d(x,X_{0}):=\inf_{z\in X_{0}}d(x,z)
. We shall always write
\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\chi)
for
\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\chi\cup\{(z,t_{z},m_{z})\})
, where
\chi\in\mathbf{N}
and
(z,t_{z},m_{z})\in X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}
.

BL
-localization of scores in space-time.

The definition below is crucial to our development, as it defines the notions of
BL
-localization in space
and
BL
-localization in time
. To derive central limit theorems, it suffices that four-tuples of scores are distributionally well-approximated by four-tuples of short-range scores, as measured by the bounded Lipschitz distance. A short-range score in space is one which depends only on data in a ball with fixed radius. A short-range score in time depends only on data up to a certain time, and is zero after this time.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, denote generic points in

\mathbb{R}^{p}
by
{\mathbf{x}}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})
.
By
BL(\mathbb{R}^{p})
we mean the class of Lipschitz(
1
) functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}

with supremum norm bounded by
1
, that is

\sup_{{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\frac{|f({\mathbf{x}})-f({\mathbf{y}})|}{||{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}||}\leq 1,\quad\sup_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}|f({\mathbf{x}})|\leq 1,

where
\|{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|
is the Euclidean distance on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

Given
\mathbb{R}^{p}
-valued random vectors
{\bf X}:=(X_{1},...,X_{p})
and
{\bf Y}:=(Y_{1},...,Y_{p})
, defined on possibly different probability spaces, the

bounded Lipschitz distance
between the laws of
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
is

d_{BL}({\cal L}({\bf X}),{\cal L}({\bf Y}))=\sup_{f\in BL(\mathbb{R}^{p})}|\mathbb{E}\,f({\bf X})-\mathbb{E}\,f({\bf Y}))|.

BL
-localization establishes closeness in the
d_{BL}
metric between the laws of the random variables
\xi
and their short-range versions
\xi^{[r]}
and
\xi^{(s)}
. This motivates the term ‘localization’ in contrast to ‘stabilization’, a notion comparing specific realizations of the scores - either exactly (via stopping sets) or approximately in
L^{q}
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
BL
-localization of scores in space and in time) Given
\theta>0
, and
\theta^{\prime}\in(0,1/2]
the score
\xi
is said to
BL(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
-localize on the space-time domain
(X,d,\nu)\times(\mathbb{R},\mu)
if there exist families of scores
(\xi^{[r]})_{r>0}
and
(\xi^{(s)})_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
and non-increasing functions
\psi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,2]
, with
\psi(0)=2
, and
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,2]
such that

•

for any locally finite
\chi\in\mathbf{N}
, and any
z\in X
,
t_{z}\in\mathbb{R}
we have for any
r>0
,

\displaystyle\xi^{[r]}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\chi)=\xi^{[r]}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\chi\cap(B_{r}(x)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}))\qquad\mathbb{Q}-\text{a.s.}

(2.1)

and for any
s\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\xi^{(s)}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\chi)={\bf{1}}(t_{z}<s)\xi^{(s)}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\chi\cap\mathbb{X}\times(-\infty,s)\times\mathbb{M})\qquad\mathbb{Q}-\text{a.s.};

(2.2)

•

for any
z_{1},...,z_{4},x,y\in X
, and
t_{z_{1}},...,t_{z_{4}},t_{x},t_{y}\in\mathbb{R}
, and any
\mathcal{A}_{i}\subseteq\{(z_{1},t_{z_{1}}),...,(z_{4},t_{z_{4}}),(x,t_{x}),(y,t_{y})\}
for
i=1,\ldots,4
, we have for all

d_{BL}\bigg(\big(\xi((z_{i},t_{z_{i}},M_{z_{i}}),\hat{\mathcal{P}}\cup\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i})\big)_{i=1,...,4},\ \big(\xi^{[r]}((z_{i},t_{z_{i}},M_{z_{i}}),\hat{\mathcal{P}}\cup\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i})\big)_{i=1,...,4}\bigg)\leq\psi(r),

(2.3)

and for all

d_{BL}\bigg(\big(\xi((z_{i},t_{z_{i}},M_{z_{i}}),\hat{\mathcal{P}}\cup\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i})\big)_{i=1,...,4},\ \big(\xi^{(s)}((z_{i},t_{z_{i}},M_{z_{i}}),\hat{\mathcal{P}}\cup\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i})\big)_{i=1,...,4}\bigg)\leq\phi(s),

(2.4)

•

we have

{\cal I}_{\psi}(\theta):={\cal I}_{\psi,X}(\theta):=\max\big(1,\sup_{x\in X}\int_{X}\psi\left(\frac{d(x,z)}{2}\right)^{\theta}\nu(dz)\big)<\infty;

(2.5)

and

{\cal I}_{\phi}(\theta^{\prime}):=\max\big(1,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(t)^{\theta^{\prime}}\mu(dt)\big)<\infty.

(2.6)

The collections
(\xi^{[r]})_{0<r<\infty}
and

(\xi^{(s)})_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
,
satisfying (
2.1
) and (

), respectively, are said to be families of short-range scores in space and short-range scores in time, respectively.
Lastly we put for all
p>0

\displaystyle M^{\xi}_{p,X}:=\max\bigg\{

\displaystyle{1},\sup_{r\in(0,\infty]}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(z,t_{z})\in X\times\mathbb{R}\\
\mathcal{A}\subset X\times\mathbb{R},|\mathcal{A}|\leq p+1\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,|\xi^{[r]}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\hat{{\cal P}}\cup\hat{\mathcal{A}})|^{p},

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(z,t_{z})\in X\times\mathbb{R}\\
\mathcal{A}\subset X\times\mathbb{R},|\mathcal{A}|\leq p+1\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,|\xi^{(s)}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\hat{{\cal P}}\cup\hat{\mathcal{A}})|^{p}\bigg\},

(2.7)

where
\xi^{[\infty]}=\xi
.

The
BL
-localization definition may appear complex, but as we shall see shortly in Subsection

, its verification reduces to checking some relatively straightforward conditions on a single score.
The bound

always holds and thus the requirement that
\psi
and

are bounded by

is not a constraint. Likewise, imposing

is convenient for the purpose of presentation, but of no consequence in applications. Note that
BL(\theta_{1})
localization implies

localization in space or time, whenever

.

In practice, we often choose
\xi^{[r]}
and
\xi^{(s)}
to be the restrictions of
\xi
given as

\xi((z,t_{z},m_{z}),\chi\cap(B_{r}(z)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}))\quad\text{and}\quad{\bf{1}}(t_{z}<s)\xi((z,t_{z},m_{z}),\chi\cap(\mathbb{X}\times(-\infty,s)\times\mathbb{M}))

respectively. However in some cases we will choose

to be dependent on the geometry of the balls

. This flexibility in the choice of

widens the scope of applications.

Remark 2.4
.

BL(\theta,\frac{1}{2})
-space-time localization and
M^{\xi}_{2,X}<\infty
imply that

is a.s. finite. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,|H|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\,\sum_{(z,t_{z})\in{\cal P}\cap W\times\mathbb{R}}|\xi(z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}})|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Mecke}}}{{=}}\int_{W\times\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\,|\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}})|(\nu\otimes\mu)(dz,dt_{z})

\displaystyle=\int_{W\times\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathbb{E}\,|\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}})|-\mathbb{E}\,|\xi^{(t_{z})}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}})|\right)(\nu\otimes\mu)(dz,dt_{z})

\displaystyle\leq 48(M^{\xi}_{2,X})^{1/2}\nu(W)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(t_{z})^{1/2}\mu(dt_{z})<\infty,

where the inequality

\left(\mathbb{E}\,|\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}})|-\mathbb{E}\,|\xi^{(t_{z})}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}})|\right)\leq 52(M^{\xi}_{2,X})^{1/2}\phi(t_{z})^{1/2}

follows from Lemma
4.2
below applied to the localizing pair
|\xi|,|\xi^{(t_{x})}|

and with

there.

Remark 2.5
.

Localization in time is satisfied in two natural cases. The first is whenever the probability of the score being non-zero decays fast with respect to the magnitude of the time component. This is the case in space-time models where points in the distant future are unlikely to contribute to the statistic
H
, as in
spatial birth-growth models and generalized beta-Delaunay tessellations.
The second natural case arises when the scores
\xi
do not depend on any time coordinate. In this case, one can choose
\phi\equiv 2
and
\mu=\delta_{0}
, the Dirac mass at
0
. See Corollary
2.8
for details.

Remark 2.6
.

Condition (
2.6
) is in fact a condition on
\mu((-\infty,0))
as well as on the decay of
\phi
at
+\infty
. Indeed, since
\phi
is non-decreasing, we have
\phi(t)\geq\phi(0)
for every
t<0
. It follows from (
2.6
) that
\mu((-\infty,0))<\infty
. Heuristically , this means that
\mu
is a finite measure on the negative half-line and hence not too many points of the point process will be situated here, while there can be many points on the positive half-line but their importance and contribution to the total sum decreases with
\phi
fast enough to ensure finiteness. Alternatively, it is possible to choose
\mu
such that
\mu((0,+\infty))<\infty
, in which case the choice of
\phi
is irrelevant and not too many points will be situated on the positive half-line. Their importance however may be large.

The main results

Our main foundational result for the Poisson functionals

H:=H(\hat{{\cal P}})

goes as follows. It is a consequence of Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.7
.

(rates of normal convergence for sums of
BL
-space-time localizing scores on metric spaces)
Let
\xi:(X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})\times\mathbf{N}_{X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a score function on the marked space domain
X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}
and
{\cal P}
a Poisson process on
X\times\mathbb{R}
with intensity
\nu\otimes\mu
and
\hat{{\cal P}}
its marked version. Define

H:=H(\hat{{\cal P}})=\sum_{(z,t_{z})\in{\cal P}\cap(W\times\mathbb{R})}\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\hat{{\cal P}}).

Assume that
\xi
satisfies
BL\big(\tfrac{1}{240},\tfrac{1}{120}\big)
space-time localization according to Def.
2.3
, with
M^{\xi}_{5,X}<\infty
.
For
\psi
as in Def.
2.3
and
q>0
, put

G_{q}:=\int_{X}\psi(d(x,W))^{q}\nu(dx)=2^{q}\nu(W)+\int_{X\setminus W}\psi(d(x,W))^{q}\nu(dx).

If
G_{1/120}<\infty
, then there is a

C_{K}<\infty
given by

C_{K}:=c\cdot{\cal I}_{\psi,X}\big(\tfrac{1}{240}\big)^{3}\cdot{\cal I}_{\phi}\big(\tfrac{1}{120}\big)^{7/2},

where
c<\infty
is a universal scalar, such that

\mathbf{d}_{K}\left(\frac{H-\mathbb{E}\,H}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H}},N\right)\leq C_{K}\cdot(M_{5,X}^{\xi})^{2/5}\cdot\frac{G_{1/120}^{1/2}}{{\rm Var}H}

(2.9)

and there is a
C_{W}<\infty
given by

C_{W}:=c\cdot{\cal I}_{\psi,X}(\tfrac{1}{50})^{3}\cdot{\cal I}_{\phi}(\tfrac{3}{200})^{4}

such that

\mathbf{d}_{W}\left(\frac{H-\mathbb{E}\,H}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H}},N\right)\leq C_{K}\cdot(M_{5,X}^{\xi})^{2/5}\cdot\frac{G_{1/120}^{1/2}}{{\rm Var}H}+C_{W}\cdot(M_{5,X}^{\xi})^{3/5}\cdot\frac{G_{3/200}}{({\rm Var}H)^{3/2}}.

(2.10)

In many applications scores do not depend on input in a time variable and hence we give a version of Theorem
2.7
which looks at scores defined on the space
X
only.

Corollary 2.8
.

(rates of normal approximation for sums of
BL
-space-localizing scores) Let
\xi:(X\times\mathbb{M})\times\mathbf{N}_{X\times\mathbb{M}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a score function on the marked space domain
X\times\mathbb{M}
and
{\cal P}
a Poisson process on
X
with intensity
\nu
and
\hat{{\cal P}}
its marked version. Define

H:=H(\hat{{\cal P}})=\sum_{z\in{\cal P}\cap W}\xi((z,M_{z}),\hat{{\cal P}}).

Assume that
\xi
satisfies
BL(\frac{1}{240})
-space localization
, i.e., assume
\xi
satisfies conditions (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) with
\theta=\frac{1}{240}
(where any input

in time is ignored), and with the short-range scores
\xi^{[r]}
satisfying (
2.1
). Define
M^{\xi}_{5,X}
as

M^{\xi}_{5,X}:=\max\big(1,\sup_{r\in(0,\infty]}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in X\\
\mathcal{A}\subset X,|\mathcal{A}|\leq 6\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,|\xi^{[r]}((z,M_{z}),\hat{{\cal P}}\cup\hat{\mathcal{A}})|^{5}\big).

and
G_{1/120}<\infty.

Then the bounds (
2.9

(2.11)

Proof.

Extend the definition of
\xi
and
\xi^{[r]}
to
X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}
in the natural way by simply ignoring the input in time. The moment bound

is not affected by this extension.
Choose

, the Dirac mass at

and
\xi^{(s)}\equiv 0
. Then (
2.2
) holds and (

) holds with

. Display (

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}(\theta^{\prime})=\phi(0)^{\theta^{\prime}}=2^{\theta^{\prime}},

for any

In many cases the values of

Remark 2.10
.

(A word on the proof)
At first sight it is not clear that BL-localization should suffice for controlling the fourth moments under the integrals in Theorem
2.1
. However when applying the difference operators
D_{x}
and
D_{x,y}
to
H
we are led to controlling expectations of products of difference operators applied to scores
\xi
at the respective entries of four-tuples
(z_{1},...,z_{4})\in X^{4}
. We portray products of second order difference operators on four such scores as an ostensible difference with an analogous product of difference operators on corresponding short-range scores
\xi^{[r]}
, provided
r
is chosen to be the maximum distance between the pair
(x,y)
and the four-tuple
(z_{1},...,z_{4})
.
The said difference is only in appearance, as the difference operator on the analogous product of short-range scores vanishes. By writing products of second-order difference operators in this manner, we may use
BL-localization (
2.3
) to control their expectations with an error proportional to
\psi(r)
, and thus by
\psi(d(x,y)/2)
, as necessarily
r\geq d(x,y)/2
. Hence integrated moments of products of second-order difference operators may be controlled by the integrals of
\psi
as at (
2.5
). This, together with similar bounds on first-order difference operators, leads to efficient control of all integrals in the error terms of Theorem
2.1
, whence Theorem
2.7
.

How to use the main theorem

Theorem
2.7
and its two corollaries include several set-ups which apply to broad classes of Poisson functionals.

(i)

(dilating windows)
One is given a sequence of windows
(W_{\lambda})_{{\lambda}\geq 1}
and spaces
(X_{\lambda})_{{\lambda}\geq 1}
,
W_{\lambda}\subseteq X_{\lambda}
, which are embedded into
a common metric measure space
(\mathbb{X},d,\nu)
, with
W_{\lambda}\uparrow\mathbb{X}
. With
{\cal P}
a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{X}
with intensity measure
\nu
and with
{\cal P}_{\lambda}
the restriction of
{\cal P}
to
X_{\lambda}
we put

H_{\lambda}:=H_{\lambda}(\hat{\mathcal{P}})=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{P}\cap W_{\lambda}}\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\hat{{\cal P}}_{\lambda}),

(2.13)

which is a sum of scores on dilating windows.
Then Theorem

2.7

goes through verbatim, with
H
replaced by
H_{\lambda}
,
with
X
replaced by
\mathbb{X}
, with
M^{\xi}_{p,X}
replaced by
M^{\xi}_{p,\mathbb{X}}
, and
with
I_{\psi,X}
replaced by
I_{\psi,\mathbb{X}}
. The constants
C_{K},C_{W}
are thus independent of
{\lambda}
.
When
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

W_{\lambda}:=[-\frac{1}{2}{\lambda}^{1/d},\frac{1}{2}{\lambda}^{1/d}]^{d}
we obtain a set-up
treated extensively in the literature, though under the stronger assumption that
\xi
satisfies stopping set stabilization.
Here we only assume localization and do not restrict to Euclidean space.
Several applications shall make use of this set-up.

(ii)

(point processes of increasing intensity)
We consider the normal approximation for sums of scores on a fixed measure space
but with point processes of increasing intensity.

Let
(X,d_{\lambda},\nu_{\lambda})_{{\lambda}\geq 1}
be a sequence of metric measure spaces and
{\cal P}_{\lambda}
a Poisson point process on
X
with intensity measure
\nu_{\lambda}
where possibly
\nu_{\lambda}(X)\uparrow\infty
as

. Let
W\subseteq X
. Put

H_{\lambda}:=H_{\lambda}(\hat{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}})=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\cap W}\xi_{\lambda}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\hat{{\cal P}}_{\lambda}).

(2.14)

where
\xi_{\lambda}:(X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})\times\mathbf{N}_{X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Consider the analog of (
2.5
):

{\cal I^{\prime}}_{\psi}(\theta):=\max\big(1,\sup_{{\lambda}\geq 1}\sup_{x\in X}\int_{X}\psi\left(\frac{d_{\lambda}(x,z)}{2}\right)^{\theta}\nu_{\lambda}(dz)\big)<\infty.

(2.15)

Given
\theta>0
, and
\theta^{\prime}\in(0,1/2]
the scores
(\xi_{\lambda})_{{\lambda}\geq 1}
are said to

-localize on their respective space-time domains
((X,d_{\lambda},\nu_{\lambda})\times(\mathbb{R},\mu))_{{\lambda}\geq 1}
if for each
{\lambda}

there exist short-range functions
\xi_{\lambda}^{[r]},\xi_{\lambda}^{(s)}
such that
(
2.1
)- (
2.4
) hold uniformly in
{\lambda}
and with
{\cal I^{\prime}}_{\psi}(\theta)
replacing
{\cal I}_{\psi}(\theta)
.
There are two natural cases of interest.

(a)

(increasing intensity on a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
Consider the heavily studied case
W\equiv X=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}
with
X
equipped with the Euclidean metric and
{\cal P}_{\lambda}
a Poisson measure with intensity
{\lambda}\rho(x)dx
on

, with
dx
Lebesgue measure and
\rho:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Berry-Esseen bounds for the sums

\sum_{x\in{\cal P}_{\lambda}}\xi(x,{\cal P}_{\lambda})
,
were established in

[
20
]
under the assumption that the scores satisfy stopping set stabilization. Theorem

2.7
yields comparable bounds for the sums
H_{\lambda}
at (
2.13
) under the weaker assumption that the scores satisfy localization (
2.3
), where the integrability condition (
2.15
) given by

{\cal I^{\prime}}_{\psi}(\theta):=\max\big(1,\sup_{{\lambda}\in[1,\infty)}\sup_{x\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}}\int_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}}\psi\left(\frac{{\lambda}^{1/d}|x-z|)}{2}\right)^{\theta}{\lambda}\rho(z)dz\big)<\infty

(2.16)

is satisfied as soon as

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi\left(\frac{|x-z|)}{2}\right)^{\theta}||\rho||_{\sup}dz<\infty.

(2.17)

(b)

(increasing intensity on a manifold) Let
W\equiv X\equiv{\cal M}
with
{\cal M}
a
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with Riemannian metric
d_{g}
and where
{\cal P}_{\lambda}
is a Poisson measure with intensity
{\lambda}\rho(x)dx
on
{\cal M}
, with
dx
the Riemannian volume measure. Theorem

2.7
, with
X={\cal M}
and
{\cal M}
equipped with the metric
{\lambda}^{1/d}d_{g}
, yields
Berry-Esseen bounds for the sums

\sum_{x\in{\cal P}_{\lambda}}\xi(x,{\cal P}_{\lambda})

whenever the scores satisfy localization (
2.3
), subject to the integrability condition (
2.15
), which takes the form

\max\big(1,\sup_{{\lambda}\in[1,\infty)}\sup_{x\in{\cal M}}\int_{{\cal M}}\psi\left(\frac{{\lambda}^{1/d}d_{g}(x,z)}{2}\right)^{\theta}{\lambda}\rho(z)dz\big)<\infty.

(2.18)

When
{\cal M}
is a
C^{1}
smooth manifold then
rates of normal convergence with spurious logarithmic factors were obtained in
[
31
]
albeit under the relatively strong assumption that
\xi
belongs to the class
\Sigma(k,r_{0})
, i.e., the collections of scores having a stopping set whose radius is
the maximum of a fixed constant

r_{0}
and the distance to the
k
th nearest neighbor, and also under the assumption that the metric is the extrinsic Euclidean metric.
Here we obtain Berry-Esseen rates
for the sums
H_{\lambda}
at (
2.13
)
without logarithmic factors, assuming only that
\xi
satisfies localisation (
2.18
), and where distances are with respect to

{\cal M}
, which is arguably more natural.

(iii)

(scores defined on the full space
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
The bounds for Theorem
2.7
simplify when
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d
is the Euclidean metric,
\nu
is Lebesgue measure,
W:=W_{\lambda}:=[-\frac{1}{2}{\lambda}^{1/d},\frac{1}{2}{\lambda}^{1/d}]^{d}
, and
{\cal P}
is a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
with intensity
\nu\otimes\mu
. This gives rise to a sum of scores

H_{\lambda}^{\infty}:=\sum_{(z,t_{z})\in\mathcal{P}\cap W_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R}}\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\hat{{\cal P}}).

If
\xi
satisfies
BL(\frac{1}{240},\frac{1}{120})
-space-time-localization (Def.
2.3
) and
M^{\xi}_{5,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is finite, and if in addition

\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(r)^{1/240}r^{d-1}dr<\infty,

(2.19)

then we assert that

\mathbf{d}_{K}\left(\frac{H_{\lambda}^{\infty}-\mathbb{E}\,H_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H_{\lambda}^{\infty}}},N\right)\leq C_{K}(M^{\xi}_{5,\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{2/5}\frac{\sqrt{{\lambda}}}{{\rm Var}H_{\lambda}}

and

\mathbf{d}_{W}\left(\frac{H_{\lambda}^{\infty}-\mathbb{E}\,H_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H_{\lambda}^{\infty}}},N\right)\leq C_{K}(M^{\xi}_{5,\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{2/5}\frac{\sqrt{{\lambda}}}{{\rm Var}H_{\lambda}}+cC_{W}\cdot(M^{\xi}_{5,\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{3/5}\frac{{\lambda}}{({\rm Var}H_{\lambda})^{3/2}},

where
C_{K}=c\mathcal{I}_{\phi}(\frac{1}{120})^{7/2}
and
C_{W}=c\mathcal{I}_{\phi}(\frac{3}{200})^{4}
.

To prove this assertion, note that
G_{q}=2^{q}\lambda+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus W_{\lambda}}\psi(d(x,W_{\lambda}))^{q}dx
. It suffices to show that the integral is
O({\lambda}^{(d-1)/d})
when
q=1/120
or
q=3/200
. Let
{\cal H}^{d-1}
denote
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.
For any
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
we let

K_{r}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x-y|\leq r\}
.
The co-area formula tells us that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus W_{\lambda}}\psi\big(d(y,W_{\lambda})\big)^{q}dy\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\partial((W_{\lambda})_{r})}\psi(r)^{q}{\cal H}^{d-1}(dx)dr

=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\partial((W_{\lambda})_{r})}\psi(r)^{q}{\cal H}^{d-1}(dx)dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial((W_{\lambda})_{r})}\psi(r)^{q}{\cal H}^{d-1}(dx)dr.

The first integral is
O({\lambda}^{(d-1)/d})
. To evaluate the second integral, recall
(see e.g.
the proof of Lemma 5.12 of
[
20
]
) that for any convex
K
we have

{\cal H}^{d-1}(\partial K_{r})\leq c\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial K)(1+r^{d-1}),\quad r>0.

Thus under the integrability condition
(
2.19
),
we find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus W_{\lambda}}\psi\big(d(y,W_{\lambda})\big)^{1/120}\nu(dy)

\displaystyle=O({\lambda}^{(d-1)/d})+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial W_{\lambda})\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(r)^{1/120}(1+r^{d-1})dr

\displaystyle=O({\lambda}^{(d-1)/d})

and hence
G_{q}=O(\lambda)
for
q=1/120
. Since
\psi
is bounded by
2
, the result follows for
q=3/200
.

Next, we need to show that
\mathcal{I}_{\psi,X}(\theta)
is bounded by a constant independent of
\lambda
when
\theta=1/120
or
\theta=1/50
. For this, note that

\mathcal{I}_{\psi,X}(\theta)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{2}\right)^{1/240}dy=2^{d}d\kappa_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(r)^{1/240}r^{d-1}dr,

where
\kappa_{d}
denotes the volume of a unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This suffices to conclude.

Conditions which imply BL-localization

Showing BL-localization requires checking conditions (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), which at first glance may appear challenging. However there are several conditions which are easier to verify and which imply localization. They are as follows.

(i)

(Bounding the probability of the difference) Assume that for any
(z,t_{z})\in X\times\mathbb{R}
and
{\cal A}\subset X
with
|{\cal A}|\leq 6
and any
r>0
, one has

\mathbb{P}\big(\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}})\neq\xi^{[r]}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}})\big)\leq\tilde{\psi}(r)

(2.20)

for a non-increasing function
\tilde{\psi}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
.
Then we can choose
\psi(r):=\max(2,8\tilde{\psi}(r))
and achieve (
2.3
). An analogous result holds for showing localization in time (
2.4
).

(ii)

(Stabilization) Given a score
\xi:(X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})\times\mathbf{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the quantity
R^{\xi}:=R^{\xi}((z,t_{z},m_{z}),\chi)
is a radius of stabilization at the point
(z,t_{z},m_{z})\in X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}
if for any
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}

\xi\big((z,t_{z},m_{z}),\chi_{R^{\xi}}\big)=\xi\big((z,t_{z},m_{z}),\chi_{R^{\xi}}\cup(\chi_{R^{\xi}}^{\prime})^{c}\big)

(2.21)

where
\chi_{R^{\xi}}=\chi\cap(B(z,R^{\xi})\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})
and
(\chi_{R^{\xi}}^{\prime})^{c}=\chi^{\prime}\cap(B(z,R^{\xi})^{c}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})
. Suppose there is
\tilde{\psi}
such that

\sup_{(z,t_{z})\in X\times\mathbb{R}}\sup_{|{\hat{{\cal A}}}|\leq 6}\mathbb{P}(R^{\xi}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}})\geq r)\leq\tilde{\psi}(r),\quad r>0.

(2.22)

In this case,
\xi
satisfies (
2.3
) on the metric measure space
(X,d,\nu)
with
\psi(r)=\min(2,8\tilde{\psi}(r))
and with the short-range scores
\xi^{[r]}
defined as in (
2.8
).
Indeed, we have for
r>0

\displaystyle d_{BL}\big(\big(\xi((z_{i},t_{z_{i}},M_{z_{i}}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}}_{i})\big)_{i=1,...,4},

(2.23)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\big(\xi((z_{i},t_{z_{i}},M_{z_{i}}),({\hat{{\cal P}}}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}}_{i})\cap B_{r}(z_{i})\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})\big)_{i=1,...,4}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}(R^{\xi}((z_{1},t_{z_{1}},M_{z_{1}}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}}_{1})\geq r\text{ or }\ldots\text{ or }R^{\xi}((z_{4},t_{z_{4}},M_{z_{4}}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}}_{4})\geq r)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{P}(R^{\xi}((z_{i},t_{z_{i}},M_{z_{1}}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}}_{i})\geq r)

\displaystyle\leq 8\sup_{z\in X}\sup_{|{\hat{{\cal A}}}|\leq 6}\mathbb{P}(R^{\xi}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),{\hat{{\cal P}}}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}})\geq r).

(2.24)

Thus if
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and if
R^{\xi}
has an exponentially decaying tail, then
\xi
is automatically
BL
-localizing.

(iii)

(
L^{q}
stabilization) Assume there is a
q\geq 1
and a non-increasing function
\tilde{\psi}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for any
r>0

\sup_{(z,t_{z})\in X\times\mathbb{R}}\sup_{|\hat{{\cal A}}|\leq 6}\mathbb{E}\,\big[|\xi((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\hat{\mathcal{P}}\cup\mathcal{A})-\xi^{[r]}((z,t_{z},M_{z}),\hat{\mathcal{P}}\cup\mathcal{A})|^{q}\big]^{1/q}\leq\tilde{\psi}(r).

(2.25)

Then (
2.3
) holds with
\psi(r)=\min(2,4\tilde{\psi}(r))
. An analogous statement holds for time localization (
2.4
).

For some scores it may also be easier to show that (
2.3
) or (
2.4
) hold with
d_{BL}
replaced by a larger distance such as the total variation distance.

Comparison between our results and the literature

A comparison between our results (specifically Corollary
2.9
) and those of
[
20
]
(Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2) and
[
21
]
is in order:

(i)

We obtain rates of convergence in the Kolmogorov and Wasserstein distances
\mathbf{d}_{K}
and
\mathbf{d}_{W}
, whereas
[
20
]
is limited to
\mathbf{d}_{K}
.

(ii)

BL-localization in space is weaker than the stabilization criteria required in
[
30
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
20
]
, and the localization criteria in
[
35
]
, thus encompassing a broader range of statistics, including those in Sections
5
and
6
.

(iii)

We consider Poisson input on general metric spaces, whereas
[
20
]
is restricted to Euclidean space and other metric spaces satisfying a polynomial growth condition on the measure of spheres.

(iv)

Theorem
2.7
extends
[
21
]
in several directions, as
[
21
]
is confined to
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it does not allow for infinite time horizons, and it requires that
\xi
satisfy
L^{4}
stabilization, albeit with requirements on the rates of decay which are weaker than those corresponding to the rates given by
BL(\theta)
.

(v)

Our results and those in
[
20
]
both allow for the possibility that scores concentrate around lower-dimensional sets, though in different ways. In
[
20
]
the concentration requires that the probability of non-vanishing scores decays exponentially fast with respect to the boundary of a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; in our set-up the concentration is expressed in terms of a time variable with respect to the space-time sets
W\times\{{\bf 0}\}\subset X\times\mathbb{R}
,
X
a metric space, and allows for models with infinite time horizon.

(vi)

[
20
]
treats Poisson and binomial input on a fixed window in Euclidean space (and other spaces satisfying a growth condition on the measure of spheres), letting the intensity go to infinity. When
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, when the input is Poisson, and when the scores
\xi_{\lambda}
in
[
20
]
satisfy
\xi_{\lambda}(x,{\cal X})=\xi({\lambda}^{1/d}x,{\lambda}^{1/d}{\cal X})
, then the approach here applies, only assuming localization and not the stronger stabilization criterion. See Item (
iia
) in Subsection
2.4
for further details.

(vii)

The work
[
10
]
uses the cumulant method to establish qualitative CLT’s for sums of
BL
-localizing scores on general input on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, though localization (
2.3
) must hold when the four tuple of scores is replaced by a
p
-tuple,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and moreover for each
p\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
\psi:=\psi_{p}
must be decreasing
faster than any power, clearly a much stronger condition than what we require here for Poisson input.

Refinements and extensions

(i)

(Rates of multivariate normal convergence) In Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
, we establish bounds on the fourth moment of the first and second difference operators
D,D^{(2)}
applied to
H
. We use these bounds to control the terms
\hat{\gamma}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\gamma}_{6}
in Theorem
2.1
. Likewise, these bounds may be used to derive quantitative CLTs for
multivariate Poisson functionals
where each entry is a sum of
BL
-localizing scores, thus going beyond the case where each entry is assumed to be a sum of stabilizing scores, as in
[
38
]
. Indeed, the error terms in Theorem 1.1 in
[
37
]
and Theorem 1 in
[
40
]
, especially for the multivariate
\mathbf{d}_{2}
and
\mathbf{d}_{3}
distances, similarly consist of fourth moments of difference operators of
H
. This is an avenue which we do not pursue in this paper.

(ii)

(Bounded scores) If
|\xi|
is bounded, or if
X=W
then by modifying the proof accordingly it can be seen that the constants
C_{K}
and
C_{W}
can be reduced using
\mathcal{I}_{\psi,X}(\theta)
and
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}(\theta^{\prime})
with values of
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
which exceed
1/240
and
1/120
, respectively. For example, when
|\xi|
is bounded then the values of
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
in our main results can be increased by a factor of
5
. This is a consequence of Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
which show that differences of products of moments of four
bounded
random variables are controlled by a bounded Lipschitz distance (usually less than one) and not the fifth root of a bounded Lipschitz distance, as is the case when the random variables have a fifth moment. Thus, for example, the values of
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
in Theorem
2.7
and Corollaries
2.8
and
2.9

can be increased to
1/48
and
1/24
respectively, and the function
G_{1/120}
may be replaced by the smaller function
G_{1/24}
.

(iii)

(Extensions to score functions depending additionally on windows)
The score functions
\xi
can additionally be made to depend on either the surrounding windows
W
or on the domains
X
by defining the function as

\xi:(X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})\times\mathbf{N}\times\mathcal{B}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.

Our approach in the sequel goes through virtually unchanged with this additional dependence. It is of particular importance when accounting for boundary effects which could arise in e.g. statistics of Voronoi cells.

(iv)

(Further extensions)
A simplifying step in our proofs uses the trivial
inequality
\min(a,b)\leq a^{1/2}b^{1/2}
for
a,b>0
.
Using instead the inequality
\min(a,b)\leq a^{\varepsilon}b^{(1-\varepsilon)},\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, it follows that
space-time localization hypotheses of the type
BL(\theta,\theta)
can be replaced by a

BL(2\theta\varepsilon,2\theta(1-\varepsilon))
condition for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. We have chosen
\varepsilon=1/2
for simplicity, but allowing for general
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
is potentially useful when determining minimal conditions under which integrability conditions (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) on
\psi
and
\phi
are both satisfied.

(v)

The bounds in Theorem
2.7
on
C_{K}
and
C_{W}
can be tightened, to allow for the case where the integrals in the terms
\mathcal{I}_{\psi,X}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}
are small. Define

A:=c\max(1,M^{\xi}_{5,X})^{2/5}\mathcal{I}_{\psi,X}(\tfrac{1}{60})^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\phi}(\tfrac{1}{60})^{2}

and set

{\cal J}_{\psi,X}(\theta):=\sup_{x\in X}\int_{X}\psi\left(\frac{d(x,z)}{2}\right)^{\theta}\nu(dz)

and

{\cal J}_{\phi}(\theta^{\prime}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(t)^{\theta^{\prime}}\mu(dt).

Then define

\displaystyle\omega_{1}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{J}_{\psi,X}(\tfrac{1}{240})\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(\tfrac{11}{1200})\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(\tfrac{1}{120})^{1/2}G_{1/120}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\omega_{2}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{J}_{\psi,X}(\tfrac{1}{120})\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(\tfrac{1}{120})\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(\tfrac{1}{60})^{1/2}G_{1/60}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\omega_{3}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(\tfrac{3}{200})G_{3/200}

\displaystyle\omega_{4}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(\tfrac{1}{50})^{1/2}G_{1/50}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\omega_{5}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{J}_{\psi,X}(\tfrac{1}{60})^{1/2}\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(\tfrac{1}{60})G_{1/60}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\omega_{6}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{J}_{\psi,X}(\tfrac{1}{120})^{1/2}\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(\tfrac{1}{120})G_{11/600}^{1/2}.

Note that
A
can never be smaller than
c
,
but the terms
\mathcal{J}_{\psi,X}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\phi}(\theta^{\prime})
depend on
\phi,\psi
, on the underlying measure and on the space
X
and might be small, especially when compared to the term
G_{q}
with varying
q
. The bound (
2.9
) in Theorem
2.7
can be replaced by the more accurate bound

\mathbf{d}_{K}\bigg(\frac{H-\mathbb{E}\,H}{\smash{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H}}},N\bigg)\leq\frac{A}{{\rm Var}H}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{4}+\omega_{5}+\omega_{6})

and (
2.10
) by

\mathbf{d}_{W}\bigg(\frac{H-\mathbb{E}\,H}{\smash{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H}}},N\bigg)\leq\frac{A}{{\rm Var}H}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})+\frac{A^{3/2}}{({\rm Var}H)^{3/2}}\omega_{3}.

Proof of Theorem
2.1

Before starting the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we recall some definitions and properties related to Malliavin Calculus on the Poisson space.

Let
(\mathbb{W},\mathcal{W},\mu)
denote a
\sigma
-finite measure space and
\mathbf{N}
the set of locally finite
\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
-valued measures on
(\mathbb{W},\mathcal{W})
. Let
h\in L^{1}(\mathbf{N}\times\mathbb{W})
and
\eta
a Poisson measure on
\mathbb{W}
of intensity
\mu
.

Let
h\in L^{2}(\mathbf{N}\times\mathbb{W})
. Then for
\mu
-a.e.
w\in\mathbb{W}
we have

h(\eta,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{I}_{n}(h_{n}(w,.)),

where
\operatorname{I}_{n}(h_{n})
is the
n^{th}

Wiener-Itô integral
of
h_{n}
and

h_{n}(w,w_{1},...,w_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\mathbb{E}\,D^{(n)}_{w_{1},...,w_{n}}h(\eta,w).

Define the symmetrization
\tilde{h}_{n}
of
h_{n}
by

\tilde{h}_{n}(w_{1},...,w_{n+1})=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}h_{n}(w_{k},w_{1},...,w_{k-1},w_{k+1},...,w_{n+1}).

Then we say that
h\in\operatorname{dom}\delta

if
h\in L^{2}(\mathbf{N}\times\mathbb{W})
and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)!\int_{\mathbb{W}^{n+1}}\tilde{h}_{n}^{2}\,d\mu^{(n+1)}<\infty

and we define the
Skorohod integral

\delta(h)
of
h
by

\delta(h):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{I}_{n}(h_{n}).

See
[
39
, display (2.9) and thereafter]
and
[
24
, displays (25) and (42)]
for details on the above notions.

Now let
h\in L^{1}(\mathbf{N}\times\mathbb{W})\cap L^{2}(\mathbf{N}\times\mathbb{W})
and define for each
\tau\in(0,1)
and all
x\in\mathbb{W}
:

P_{\tau}h_{n}(\eta,x):=\int\mathbb{E}\,[h_{n}(\eta^{\tau}+\xi,x)|\eta]\Pi_{(1-\tau)\mu}(d\xi),

where
\eta^{\tau}
is a
\tau
-thinning of
\eta
and
\Pi_{(1-\tau)\mu}
denotes the law of a Poisson process with intensity
(1-\tau)\nu_{{\lambda}}
. Note that
P_{\tau}
is known as the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator
. We refer to
[
24
, display (71)]
and thereafter for details. For the convenience of the reader, we include a result giving useful properties of
P_{\tau}h_{n}
, which is taken from
[
39
, equation (2.13) and Lemma 6.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
h\in L^{2}(\mathbf{N}\times\mathbb{W})
and let
\tau\in(0,1)
. Then
P_{\tau}h
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\,\int_{\mathbb{W}}\int_{\mathbb{W}}\left(D_{z}P_{\tau}h(\eta,w)\right)^{2}\mu(dz)\mu(dw)<\infty.

(3.1)

and
P_{\tau}h\rightarrow h
in
L^{2}(\mathbf{N}\times\mathbb{W})
as
\tau\rightarrow 1
. Moreover, for
w,z\in\mathbb{W}
and all
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,|P_{\tau}h(\eta,w)|^{p}\leq\mathbb{E}\,|h(\eta,w)|^{p}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,|D_{z}P_{\tau}h(\eta,w)|^{p}\leq\mathbb{E}\,|D_{z}h(\eta,w)|^{p}.

We are now ready to start the proof of Theorem

Proof of Berry-Esseen bounds for sums of BL-localizing scores

First we show that closeness in the
d_{BL}
distance of four-tuples of random variables implies closeness of their products, whence BL-localization in space-time
implies that products of powers of scores
are well approximated by products of short-range scores. This establishes that first and second order difference operators on such products also localize. The Mecke formula yields that the first order difference operator
D_{(x,t_{x},M_{x})}

applied to the Poisson functional
H

has a finite fourth moment bounded by a multiple of

\psi(d(x,W))^{1/50}\phi(t_{x})^{1/50}
uniformly in
x\in X
and it also implies that the second order difference operator
D^{(2)}_{(x,t_{x},M_{x}),(y,t_{y},M_{y})}

applied to
H
has a fourth moment which decays like

\psi\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{2}\right)^{1/60}\psi\left(d(x,W)\right)^{1/60}\phi(t_{x})^{1/60}\phi(t_{y})^{1/60}

uniformly for all
(x,t_{x}),(y,t_{y})\in X\times\mathbb{R}
, up to multiplication with terms independent of
(x,t_{x}),(y,t_{y})
. The aforementioned bounds on the difference operators suffice to deduce Theorem
2.7
from
Theorem
2.1
, namely they suffice to control the terms
\hat{\gamma}_{i},1\leq i\leq 6
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(closeness in
d_{BL}
implies closeness of mixed moments, bounded case)
Let
X_{1},...X_{4},X_{1}^{\prime},...,X_{4}^{\prime}
be random variables such that

d_{BL}\big((X_{1},...,X_{4}),\ (X_{1}^{\prime},...,X_{4}^{\prime})\big)\leq\alpha,

where
\alpha\geq 0
.
Assume that there exists
L\in[1,\infty)
such that
|X_{i}|,|X_{i}^{\prime}|\leq L
for all
i=1,...4
. Then for all non-negative integers
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}
with
q=p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4}>0
it is the case that

|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{p_{i}}-\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{\prime p_{i}}|\leq 2qL^{q}\alpha.

Proof.

If
\alpha=0
, the distribution of
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{4})
coincides with that of
(X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{4}^{\prime})
and the bound holds trivially. We thus assume henceforth
\alpha>0
. The goal is to control

|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{p_{i}}-\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{\prime p_{i}}|
via a judicious choice of a
Lipschitz function on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

To this end, define
f:[-L,L]^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
by
f(s,t,u,v)=s^{p_{1}}t^{p_{2}}u^{p_{3}}v^{p_{4}}
and observe that
f
is continuous and hence Lipschitz on the bounded domain
[-L,L]^{4}
.
By the Kirszbraun extension theorem we may extend
f
to a function
\tilde{f}
on all of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
in such a way that the extension
\tilde{f}
preserves the Lipschitz norm of
f
, which we assert is bounded by
2qL^{q-1}
.

Indeed, by the mean value theorem for multivariate functions, we have

|f(s)-f(t)|\leq\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\infty}\cdot\|s-t\|,

where
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
The supremum of each partial derivative of
f
is at most
qL^{q-1}
, hence
\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\infty}\leq 2qL^{q-1}
, whence our assertion. Now note that
|f|
is bounded by
L^{q}
and define the truncated function
g_{L^{q}}
by

g_{L^{q}}(x):=\begin{cases}-L^{q}&\text{if }x<-L^{q}\\
x&\text{if }-L^{q}\leq x\leq L^{q}\\
L^{q}&\text{if }x>L^{q}.\end{cases}

Then
g_{L^{q}}
is Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
1
. Define the function
\tilde{f}_{L^{q}}:=g_{L^{q}}\circ\tilde{f}
and note that
\tilde{f}_{L^{q}}
is Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant bounded by
2qL^{q-1}
, it is bounded by
L^{q}
and its restriction to
[-L,L]^{4}
is identical to
f
. The function
\tilde{f}_{L^{q}}/\max(2qL^{q-1},L^{q})
is thus
in
BL(\mathbb{R}^{4})
and

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{p_{i}}-\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{\prime p_{i}}\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\mathbb{E}\,\tilde{f}_{L^{4}}\big(X_{1},...,X_{4}\big)-\tilde{f}_{L^{4}}\big(X_{1}^{\prime},...,X_{4}^{\prime}\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\max(2qL^{q-1},L^{q})\cdot d_{BL}\big((X_{1},...,X_{4}),\ (X_{1}^{\prime},...,X_{4}^{\prime})\big).

The proof is complete upon bounding
\max(2qL^{q-1},L^{q})
by
2qL^{q}
.

We obtain a similar result for unbounded random variables having a
(q+1)
-moment,
though the difference in products of moments is only with precision proportional to

\alpha^{1/(q+1)}
, which is a loss in accuracy as typically
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Lemma 4.2

(closeness in

q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathbb{E}\,|X_{i}|^{q+1},\mathbb{E}\,|X_{i}^{\prime}|^{q+1}\leq M
for all
i=1,...,4
. Then for all non-negative integers
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}
with
p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4}=q
, it is the case that

|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{p_{i}}-\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{\prime p_{i}}|\leq(36q+16)\max(1,M^{q/(q+1)})\alpha^{1/(q+1)}.

Proof.

The case
\alpha=0
is trivial and we assume henceforth
\alpha>0
. We deduce this from Lemma
4.1
.
For all
L\geq 1
and
i=1,...,4
, define the truncated variable
X_{i,L}:=X_{i}\cdot{\bf{1}}(|X_{i}|\leq L)
, and similarly for
X_{i}^{\prime}
. The exact value of
L
will be chosen later.

We start by showing that

d_{BL}\big((X_{1,L},...,X_{4,L}),\ (X_{1,L}^{\prime},...,X_{4,L}^{\prime})\big)\leq\frac{16M}{L^{q+1}}+\alpha.

Let
f
be a bounded Lipschitz function with Lipschitz coefficient bounded by
1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\quad|\mathbb{E}\,f(X_{1},...,X_{4})-\mathbb{E}\,f(X_{1,L},...,X_{4,L})|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\,\bigg|\big(f(X_{1},...,X_{4})-f(X_{1,L},...X_{4,L})\big)\cdot\bigg(\Pi_{i=1}^{4}{\bf 1}(|X_{i}|\leq L)+{\bf 1}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{4}\{|X_{i}|>L\})\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\,\bigg|\big(f(X_{1},...,X_{4})-f(X_{1,L},...X_{4,L})\big)\cdot{\bf 1}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{4}\{|X_{i}|>L\})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\big(\sum_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|\geq L)\big)\leq\frac{2}{L^{q+1}}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{E}\,|X_{i}|^{q+1}\leq\frac{8M}{L^{q+1}},

where the third to last inequality uses that
|f|
is bounded by
1
. The same bound holds for
|\mathbb{E}\,f(X_{1}^{\prime},...,X_{4}^{\prime})-\mathbb{E}\,f(X_{1,L}^{\prime},...,X_{4,L}^{\prime})|
and the bound on the
d_{BL}
distance of the truncated versions follows.

We now use Lemma
4.1
on the truncated versions
X_{i,L}
and
X_{i,L}^{\prime}
. This yields

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i,L}^{p_{i}}-\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i,L}^{\prime p_{i}}|\leq 2qL^{q}\cdot\left(\frac{16M}{L^{q+1}}+\alpha\right)

As a next step, we extend this bound to the untruncated variables. In particular, we want to show that

|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{p_{i}}-\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{\prime p_{i}}|\leq\frac{16M}{L}+\frac{32qM}{L}+2qL^{q}\alpha.

Note that it suffices to show that

\mathbb{E}\,|\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{p_{i}}-\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i,L}^{p_{i}}|\leq\frac{8M}{L}

and the same for
X_{i}^{\prime},X_{i,L}^{\prime}
. We only prove the bound for
X_{i}
, as the one for
X_{i}^{\prime}
follows by identical methods.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,|X_{1}^{p_{1}}X_{2}^{p_{2}}X_{3}^{p_{3}}X_{4}^{p_{4}}-X_{1,L}^{p_{1}}X_{2,L}^{p_{2}}X_{3,L}^{p_{3}}X_{4,L}^{p_{4}}|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,|X_{1}^{p_{1}}X_{2}^{p_{2}}X_{3}^{p_{3}}(X_{4}^{p_{4}}-X_{4,L}^{p_{4}})+X_{1}^{p_{1}}X_{2}^{p_{2}}(X_{3}^{p_{3}}-X_{3,L}^{p_{3}})X_{4,L}^{p_{4}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+X_{1}^{p_{1}}(X_{2}^{p_{2}}-X_{2,L}^{p_{2}})X_{3,L}^{p_{3}}X_{4,L}^{p_{4}}+(X_{1}^{p_{1}}-X_{1,L}^{p_{1}})X_{2,L}^{p_{2}}X_{3,L}^{p_{3}}X_{4,L}^{p_{4}}|.

It thus suffices to show that

\mathbb{E}\,|(X_{1}^{p_{1}}-X_{1,L}^{p_{1}})X_{2}^{p_{2}}X_{3}^{p_{3}}X_{4}^{p_{4}}|\leq\frac{2M}{L}.

(4.1)

This can be checked as follows. The Hölder and Markov inequalities imply that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,|(X_{1}^{p_{1}}-X_{1,L}^{p_{1}})X_{2}^{p_{2}}X_{3}^{p_{3}}X_{4}^{p_{4}}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\,[|X_{1}|^{p_{1}}{\bf 1}(|X_{1}|\geq L)\cdot|X_{2}|^{p_{2}}|X_{3}|^{p_{3}}|X_{4}|^{p_{4}}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\geq L)^{1/(q+1)}\Pi_{i=1}^{4}(\mathbb{E}\,|X_{i}|^{q+1})^{p_{i}/(q+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\mathbb{E}\,|X_{1}|^{q+1})^{1/(q+1)}\frac{1}{L}\cdot M^{q/(q+1)},

\displaystyle=\frac{2M}{L}.

where we use
\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\geq L)\leq\mathbb{E}\,|X_{1}|^{q+1}/L^{q+1}
and

p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4}=q
. We thus deduced

|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{p_{i}}-\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{\prime p_{i}}|\leq\frac{16M}{L}+\frac{32qM}{L}+2qL^{q}\alpha.

If
M\geq 1
, we set
L=\left(\frac{2M}{\alpha}\right)^{1/q+1}
, else, we choose
L=\left(\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)^{1/q+1}
. In both cases,
L\geq 1
as required. Bounding
2^{q/(q+1)}
by
2
and
2^{-1/(q+1)}
by
1
, we get, if
M\geq 1
,

|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{p_{i}}-\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{\prime p_{i}}|\leq(36q+16)M^{q/(q+1)}\alpha^{1/(q+1)}

and if
M\leq 1
,

|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{p_{i}}-\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{4}X_{i}^{\prime p_{i}}|\leq(36q+16)\alpha^{1/(q+1)}.

This concludes the proof.

Lemma 4.5
.

(fourth moment bounds for first order difference operators applied to
H
)
Let
\xi:(X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M})\times\mathbf{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a score function satisfying
BL(\frac{1}{50},\frac{1}{50})
-space-time localization on
(X,d,\nu)\times(\mathbb{R},\mu)
as in Def.
2.3
with functions
\psi,\phi
.
Then there is a constant
c>0
such that for all
(x,t_{x})\in X\times\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{E}\,\big[(D_{(x,t_{x},M_{x})}H)^{4}\big]\leq cC_{5}{\cal I}_{\psi,X}(\tfrac{1}{50})^{4}I_{\phi}(\tfrac{1}{50})^{4}\psi(d(x,W))^{1/50}\phi(t_{x})^{1/50}.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

To ease the notation, we prove the result for unmarked Poisson points and then indicate the minor changes needed for the marked case. Recall that
{\cal P}
is a Poisson point process on
X
with intensity
\nu
.

We start by noting that

D_{(x,t_{x})}H={\bf{1}}(x\in W)\xi((x,t_{x}),\mathcal{P})+\sum_{(z,t_{z})\in\mathcal{P}\cap(W\times\mathbb{R})}D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi((z,t_{z}),\mathcal{P}).

For any
x\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,(D_{(x,t_{x})}H)^{4}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\bigg({\bf{1}}(x\in W)\xi((x,t_{x}),\mathcal{P})+\sum_{(z,t_{z})\in\mathcal{P}\cap(W\times\mathbb{R})}D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi((z,t_{z}),\mathcal{P})\bigg)^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 8{\bf{1}}(x\in W)\mathbb{E}\,\xi((x,t_{x}),{\cal P})^{4}+8\mathbb{E}\,\bigg(\sum_{(z,t_{z})\in{\cal P}\cap(W\times\mathbb{R})}D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi((z,t_{z}),{\cal P})\bigg)^{4}.

(4.5)

Step 1.
We consider the first term in (
4.5
). Note that for
s=t_{x}
, we have

\xi^{(s)}((x,t_{x}),\mathcal{P})=0,

by the properties of
\xi^{(s)}
.
In particular, using Lemma
4.3
with
s=t_{x}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\,\xi((x,t_{x}),{\cal P})^{4}\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\mathbb{E}\,\xi((x,t_{x}),{\cal P})^{4}-\mathbb{E}\,\xi^{(t_{x})}((x,t_{x}),{\cal P})^{4}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\phi(t_{x})^{1/5}.

Moreover, recalling
\psi(0)=2
, we have

{\bf{1}}(x\in W_{\lambda})\leq\psi(d(x,W))/\psi(0).

It follows that the first term in (
4.5
) is bounded by

cC_{5}\phi(t_{x})^{1/5}\psi(d(x,W)).

(4.6)

Step 2.

We expand the second term into the sum of five sums, depending on whether
points
(z_{1},t_{z_{1}}),(z_{2},t_{z_{2}}),(z_{3},t_{z_{3}}),(z_{4},t_{z_{4}})\in\mathcal{P}
coincide or not and then apply the Mecke formula to each sum. We obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left(\sum_{(z,t_{z})\in{\cal P}\cap W\times\mathbb{R}}D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi((z,t_{z}),{\cal P})\right)^{4}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=I_{1}(4)+4I_{2}(3,1)+3I_{2}(2,2)+6I_{3}(2,1,1)+I_{4}(1,1,1,1),

where for any
\ell\in\{1,...,4\}
, we let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}
be non-negative integers such that
p_{1}+\ldots+p_{\ell}=4
and define

I_{\ell}(p_{1},...,p_{\ell}):=\int_{(W\times\mathbb{R})^{\ell}}\mathbb{E}\,\big[\Pi_{i=1}^{\ell}D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi((z_{i},t_{z_{i}}),{\cal P}\cup\{(z_{1},t_{z_{1}}),\ldots,(z_{\ell},t_{z_{\ell}})\})^{p_{i}}\big]\\
(\nu\otimes\mu)(dz_{1},dt_{z_{1}})\ldots(\nu\otimes\mu)(dz_{\ell},dt_{z_{\ell}}).

We need to show that
I_{\ell}(p_{1},...,p_{\ell})
is bounded by a constant times

C_{5}({\cal I}_{\psi}(\frac{1}{50}))^{4}(I_{\phi}(\frac{1}{50}))^{4}\psi(d(x,W))^{1/50}\phi(t_{x})^{1/50}

for
\ell,p_{1},...,p_{\ell}

\big|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{\ell}\big(D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi_{i}\big)^{p_{i}}\big|=\big|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{\ell}\big(D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi_{i}\big)^{p_{i}}-\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{\ell}\big(D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi_{i}^{[r]}\big)^{p_{i}}\big|.

(i), we deduce that for all

with

.
Likewise we put

\displaystyle\quad={\bf{1}}(t_{z_{i}}<t_{x})\xi^{(t_{x})}\big((z_{i},t_{z_{i}}),(\mathcal{P}\cup\mathbf{z}_{\ell}\cup\{(x,t_{x})\})\cap(\mathbb{X}\times(-\infty,t_{x}))\big)

\displaystyle\quad={\bf{1}}(t_{z_{i}}<t_{x})\xi^{(t_{x})}\big((z_{i},t_{z_{i}}),(\mathcal{P}\cup\mathbf{z}_{\ell})\cap(\mathbb{X}\times(-\infty,t_{x}))\big)

\displaystyle\quad=\xi^{(t_{x})}\big((z_{i},t_{z_{i}}),\mathcal{P}\cup\mathbf{z}_{\ell}\big),

and hence

.

By Lemma
4.4
(i), we deduce that for all
\ell\in\{1,...,4\}

\big|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{\ell}\big(D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi_{i}\big)^{p_{i}}\big|\leq cC_{5}\phi(s)^{1/5},

(4.10)

with
s=\max(t_{z_{1}},...,t_{z_{\ell}},t_{x})
.
Hence (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) yield

\big|\mathbb{E}\,\Pi_{i=1}^{\ell}\big(D_{(x,t_{x})}\xi_{i}\big)^{p_{i}}\big|\leq cC_{5}\min(\psi(r)^{1/5},\phi(s)^{1/5})\leq cC_{5}\psi(r)^{1/10}\phi(s)^{1/10}.

(4.11)

Now note that

d(x,W)\leq r=\max(d(x,z_{1}),\ldots,d(x,z_{\ell})).

Indeed, if
x\in W
, then
d(x,W)=0
and the inequality holds trivially. If

, then

.
The non-increasing property of

and

\displaystyle\psi(r)\leq\psi(d(x,z_{1}))^{1/(\ell+1)}\cdot\ldots\cdot\psi(d(x,z_{\ell}))^{1/(\ell+1)}\cdot\psi(d(x,W))^{1/(\ell+1)}

(4.12)

and similarly for our choice of

\displaystyle\phi(s)\leq\phi(t_{z_{1}})^{1/(\ell+1)}\cdot\ldots\cdot\phi(t_{z_{\ell}})^{1/(\ell+1)}\cdot\phi(t_{x})^{1/(\ell+1)}.

(4.13)

Thus

\xi(\hat{x},\chi\cap B_{r}(x)\times\mathbb{M})&\text{else}.\end{cases}

The scores
\xi((z,M_{z}),\hat{\chi})
and
\xi^{[r]}((z,M_{z}),\hat{\chi})
coincide if
r\in[\delta,\infty)
and hence the
5
th moment of
\xi^{[r]}((z,M_{z}),\hat{\chi})
is bounded by
M_{5}
. Recalling that the

distance is bounded by
2
, we have

d_{BL}\big(\big(\xi((z_{i},M_{z_{i}}),\hat{{\cal P}}_{\lambda}\cup\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i})\big)_{i=1,\ldots,4},\big(\xi^{[r]}((z_{i},M_{z_{i}}),\hat{{\cal P}}_{\lambda}\cup\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i})\big)_{i=1,\ldots,4}\big)\leq 2{\bf{1}}(r<\delta).

Put
\psi(r):=2{\bf{1}}(r<\delta)
. Then for any
\theta>0

\sup_{x\in X}\int_{X}\psi\left(\frac{d(x,z)}{2}\right)^{\theta}\nu(dz)=2^{\theta}\sup_{x\in X}\nu(B_{2\delta}(x))=2^{\theta}c_{\delta}<\infty,

which yields

. As for

for some

, we have

G_{q}=2^{q}\nu(W)+\int_{X\setminus W}2^{q}{\bf{1}}(d(x,W)<\delta)\nu(dx)=2^{q}(\nu(W)+\nu(W_{\delta})).

This concludes the proof.

Stabilization and localization in hyperbolic space

In this section we deduce from the space version of Corollary
2.9
good Poincaré bounds for localizing functionals in hyperbolic space. Prior attempts to establish either quantitative or qualitative CLTS for such functionals sometimes involved
mapping the problem to Euclidean space

]

Moreover by

Proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}\psi(d(x,z)/2)^{\theta}\nu(dz)

\displaystyle=d\kappa_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sinh^{d-1}(u)\exp(-\theta\,\tau(u/2))du

BL-localizing statistics in space-time

Functionals
H
of dynamic geometric models are expressed as sums of scores of space and time variables in a natural way. When the time variable ranges over a bounded interval
[a,b]
of
\mathbb{R}
then one may apply the set-up of BL-localization in space
(with a marked Poisson point process
{\cal P}
on

\mathbb{X}
and with marks in
[a,b])
and appeal to Corollary
2.8
to establish normal approximation bounds for
(H-\mathbb{E}\,H)/\sqrt{{\rm Var}H}
.

However when the time variable ranges over all of
\mathbb{R}

this set-up does not apply, as
H
a priori is not necessarily finite as seen in Remark
2.4
. Still, many functionals of interest consist of sums of scores which localize in space and in time, as in Definition
2.3
. Two prime examples concern (i) the number of accepted particles
in spatial birth-growth models with random unbounded particle speeds in infinite time, and
(ii) extreme points in Laguerre tessellations, or equivalently, shocks in the zero-viscosity solution to the Burgers PDE with random inital data. In the following we take the underlying space to be
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but the methods could be extended to study growth processes on more general metric spaces.

Spatial birth-growth models

For
{\lambda}\geq 1
, let
W_{\lambda}:=[-\frac{1}{2}{\lambda}^{1/d},\frac{1}{2}{\lambda}^{1/d}]^{d}
and denote by
{\cal P}_{\lambda}
a Poisson process on
W_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
whose intensity
\nu
equals Lebesgue measure.
Let the generic points of
{\cal P}_{\lambda}
be denoted by
(z,t_{z})
.

Fix a time horizon
t_{0}\in(0,\infty]
and note that we allow
t_{0}=\infty
.
Let
{\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}:=\{(z,t_{z},R_{z})\}_{(z,t_{z})\in{\cal P}_{\lambda}}
where
t_{z}
represent times at which particles arrive
on the substrate
W_{\lambda}
at location

R_{z},z\in{\cal P}_{\lambda},
are i.i.d. random variables with values in
\mathbb{M}:=(0,\infty)

Order the points in
{\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}
according to the increasing order of their time coordinates, with the first point being that with the smallest time coordinate.
This gives
{\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}:=\{(z_{i},t_{z_{i}},R_{z_{i}})\}_{(z_{i},t_{z_{i}})\in{\cal P}_{\lambda}}

Let
R_{i}:=R_{z_{i}}^{\emph{\tiny{birth-growth}}}
denote the random
speed at which the particle (seed) at
z_{i}
grows radially in all directions,
provided the seed is accepted. The acceptance rule is as follows.
The seed having smallest time coordinate is accepted and subsequently arriving seeds are accepted if they do
not belong to the union of the (growing) balls of
previously accepted seeds. A statistic of interest is

H_{\lambda}:=H^{\text{birth-growth}}_{\lambda}
, the total number of seeds in
{\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}
which are accepted up to time
t_{0}
.
When all seeds grow with the same constant speed, then
one obtains the model of crystal growth
introduced by Kolmogorov and Johnson and Mehl in the 1930’s. We do not restrict to constant speeds, and to rule out trivialities arising from seeds with degenerate growth, we assume that particle speeds
are a.s. bounded below by some small but positive constant
\rho_{\emph{\tiny{birth-growth}}}
.

Corollary 6.1
.

(normal approximation for the total number of particles accepted in birth-growth models with unbounded random particle speeds)
We assume exponential decay of

R^{\emph{\tiny{birth-growth}}}
,
namely there is a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}(R_{z}\geq r)\leq\exp(-Cr),\ r>0.

(6.1)

Fix
t_{0}\in(0,\infty]

H_{\lambda}
satisfies the bounds

\mathbf{d}_{K}\left(\frac{H_{\lambda}-\mathbb{E}\,H_{\lambda}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H_{\lambda}}},N\right)\leq C_{K}\cdot\frac{{\lambda}^{1/2}}{{\rm Var}H_{\lambda}}

and

\mathbf{d}_{W}\left(\frac{H_{\lambda}-\mathbb{E}\,H_{\lambda}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}H_{\lambda}}},N\right)\leq C_{K}\frac{{\lambda}^{1/2}}{{\rm Var}H_{\lambda}}+C_{W}\frac{{\lambda}}{({\rm Var}H_{\lambda})^{3/2}},

where

are constants depending only on

.

For spatial birth-growth models where
particle growth speeds are governed by a deterministic possibly non-linear function, the paper
[
35
]
used dependency graph methods to
establish a quantitative CLT for the number of accepted points; the rates of normal convergence in
[
35
]
have extraneous logarithmic factors. The case of unbounded and random particle growth speeds has received scant attention even for finite time horizons. This is due to the difficulty of controlling long-range interactions, which, to quote the authors of

[
5
]
:

‘create[s] a causal chain of influences that
seems quite difficult to study with the currently known methods of stabilization for Gaussian
approximation’.

The authors of
[
5
]
circumvent this obstacle by restricting to finite-time horizon models and by assuming
that a rejected seed could still block seeds arriving in the future, which is arguably less realistic.

Here
we remove this assumption
, putting us in the framework of the classic birth-growth models. We allow for
random unbounded speeds of particle growth, infinite time horizons, and the rates of normal approximation
contain no extraneous logarithmic factors.

Birth-growth models with unbounded, random growth speeds fit neither into the framework of stabilization established in
[
20
]
nor into the set-up of
[
35
]
. This is because the stabilization criteria in these papers require that adding or changing points outside the stabilization ball has no effect on the score, whereas with unbounded growth speeds, it is always possible
that a far away point may arrive arbitrarily close to time
t=0
while simultaneously having a very large growth speed, so that it covers the point at which the score is calculated, thus potentially affecting the score.

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{6}\mathbb{P}(R_{w_{i}}\geq m_{r})+\mathbb{P}(R_{z_{1}}\geq m_{r}).

The exponential decay of
R_{.}

at (
6.1
) implies that all four terms decay exponentially fast in
r
.
The first integral is
O(m_{r}r^{d}\exp(-Cm_{r}))
, which decays exponentially fast. The sum of the last two terms decays as
O(\exp(-Cm_{r}))
.
To show the decay of the second integral, we may assume without loss of generality that
z_{1}={\bf 0}
and abbreviating
B_{r}(z_{1})
by
B_{r}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}^{c}}\mathbb{P}\left(R_{z}\geq\max(m_{r},d(z,\partial B_{r})/m_{r})\right)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}^{c}}{\bf 1}(d(z,\partial B_{r})\leq m_{r}^{2})\exp(-Cm_{r})dz

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{r}^{c}}{\bf 1}(d(z,\partial B_{r})>m_{r}^{2})\exp(-Cd(z,\partial B_{r})/m_{r})dz.

The first integral is
O(m_{r}^{2}r^{d-1}\exp(-Cm_{r}))
, which decays exponentially fast, since

m_{r}=r^{\gamma}
.
Let
\kappa_{d}
be the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Evaluating the second integral in polar coordinates gives

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}^{c}}{\bf 1}(d(z,\partial B_{r})>m_{r}^{2})\exp(-Cd(z,\partial B_{r})/m_{r})dz

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}{\bf 1}(d(sw,\partial B_{r})\geq m_{r}^{2})\exp(-C(s-r)/m_{r})s^{d-1}dwds

\displaystyle=\int_{r+m_{r}^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\exp(-C(s-r)/m_{r})s^{d-1}dwds

\displaystyle=d\kappa_{d}\int_{r+m_{r}^{2}}^{\infty}\exp(-C(s-r)/m_{r})s^{d-1}ds

\displaystyle=d\kappa_{d}m_{r}\int_{m_{r}}^{\infty}\exp(-Cu)(m_{r}u+r)^{d-1}du

\displaystyle=O(m_{r}^{d}r^{d-1}\exp(-Cm_{r})).

Thus
\mathbb{P}(E_{r}(z_{1})^{c})=O(\max(m_{r}^{d},m_{r}^{2})r^{d}\exp(-Cm_{r})).

This estimate holds uniformly over any
{\hat{{\cal A}}}
.

Controlling the size of the backwards cluster on the high probability event
E_{r}(z_{1})
.
Conditioning on
E_{r}(z_{1})
and its complement
E_{r}^{c}(z_{1})
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(D({\hat{z}}_{1},{\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}\cup\hat{{\cal A}})\geq r)\leq\mathbb{P}(\{D({\hat{z}}_{1},{\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}\cup\hat{{\cal A}})\geq r\}\cap E_{r}(z_{1}))+\mathbb{P}(E_{r}^{c}(z_{1})).

(6.5)

We show that the first term on the right-hand side of (
6.5
) is also decreasing exponentially fast in
r
.
Let
{\cal A}^{\prime}\subset{\cal A}
be the (possibly empty) subset of points in
{\cal A}
belonging to
B_{r}(z_{1})
.
When all edges of
G({\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}\cup\hat{{\cal A}^{\prime}})
incident to points in
B_{r}(z_{1})
have spatial length (interaction range) less than
m_{r}^{2}
, then one needs at least
\ell^{\prime}_{r}:=\lfloor(r-m_{r}^{2})/(2m_{r}^{2})\rfloor
distinct sites

\{y^{\prime}_{i}:i=1,\ldots,\ell^{\prime}_{r}\}
in
{\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}\cup\hat{{\cal A}^{\prime}}
to cover the distance from
z_{1}
to
B^{c}_{r-m_{r}^{2}}(z_{1})
on
G({\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}\cup\hat{{\cal A}^{\prime}})
.
Among these sites
\{y^{\prime}_{i}\}
there might be the fixed atoms
w_{1},\ldots,w_{6}
, with each atom contributing at most
2m_{r}^{2}
to the length of the chain; after removing them, and now putting

\ell_{r}:=\lfloor(r-m_{r}^{2})/(14m_{r}^{2})\rfloor,\quad r\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

one finds that there there exist
distinct
sites
y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell_{r}}\in{\cal P}_{\lambda}\cap B_{r}(z_{1})
,

different from

w_{1},\ldots,w_{6}
, and such that
|y_{i}-y_{i-1}|<14m_{r}^{2}
,

i=1,\ldots,\ell_{r}
, where we set
y_{0}:=z_{1}
.

Looking backwards in time
from time
t=t_{0}
, and enumerating the arrival times of the particles at
y_{i}
in
reverse chronological order we note

t_{0}\geq T_{1}>T_{2}>\ldots>T_{\ell_{r}}>T_{\ell_{r}+1}=0
.

Considering the path
\{y_{i}:i=1,\ldots,\ell_{r}\}
, using the Markov inequality
and the Mecke formula one bounds the considered probability by

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{P}(\{D({\hat{z}}_{1},{\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}})\geq r\}\cap E_{r}(z_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\,\bigg[\sum_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell_{r}})\in{\cal P}_{\lambda}^{\ell_{r}}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell_{r}}{\bf{1}}{(|y_{i}-y_{i-1}|<14m_{r}^{2})}{\bf{1}}(T_{i}>T_{i+1})\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\ell_{r}}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell_{r}}{\bf{1}}(|y_{i}-y_{i-1}|<14m_{r}^{2})dy_{1}...dy_{\ell_{r}}\times\mathbb{E}\,\Bigl[\prod_{i=1}^{\ell_{r}}{\bf{1}}(T_{i}>T_{i+1})]\Bigr]

where we use the independence of the spatial locations of seeds and their arrival times.
Note

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\Bigl[\prod_{i=1}^{l_{r}}{\bf{1}}(T_{i}>T_{i+1})\Bigr]\leq\int_{0}^{m_{r}}ds_{l_{r}}\int_{s_{l_{r}}}^{m_{r}}ds_{l_{r}-1}\dots\int_{s_{3}}^{m_{r}}ds_{2}\int_{s_{2}}^{m_{r}}ds_{1}=\frac{m_{r}^{l_{r}}}{l_{r}!}.

It follows that uniformly in
{\hat{{\cal A}}}
one obtains

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\{D({\hat{z}}_{1},{\hat{{\cal P}}}_{\lambda}\cup{\hat{{\cal A}}})\geq r\}\cap E_{r}(z_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\kappa_{d}(14m_{r}^{2})^{d}\bigr)^{\ell_{r}}\frac{m_{r}^{\ell_{r}}}{\ell_{r}!}\leq Ce^{-cr^{1-2\gamma}(1-2d\gamma-3\gamma)}

(6.6)

Put

. The choice of

and hence

implies that the right-hand side of (

) is at most
c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}r^{b})
for some

. Thus the backwards cluster at

extends outside the ball

with an exponentially small
(in

.

Laguerre tessellations and the stochastic Burgers PDE

In this section, we study a counting statistic
N_{\lambda}
relevant in the context of Laguerre tessellations and the stochastic Burgers PDE. It is defined as follows. Let
{\cal P}
be a Poisson process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
of intensity measure
dx\otimes\mu(dh)
, where
\mu
is a measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Fix
t>0
. Given a point
(x,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, we define the upward and downward paraboloids at
(x,h)
:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\uparrow}[(x,h)]:=\left\{(z,h_{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}:h_{z}\geq h+\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{2t}\right\}

\displaystyle\Pi^{\downarrow}[(x,h)]:=\left\{(z,h_{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}:h_{z}\leq h-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{2t}\right\}.

We construct a thinning
{\cal P}^{\text{thin}}
of
{\cal P}
by including a point
(x,h)\in{\cal P}
in
{\cal P}^{\text{thin}}
if and only if the boundary of the upwards paraboloid
\Pi^{\uparrow}[(x,h)]
is not included in the union of all upwards paraboloids centered at points of
{\cal P}\setminus\{(x,h)\}
, i.e. for any
(x,h)\in{\cal P}
,

(x,h)\in{\cal P}^{\text{thin}}\Leftrightarrow\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[(x,h)]\not\subseteq\bigcup_{(z,h_{z})\in{\cal P}\setminus\{(x,h)\}}\Pi^{\uparrow}[(z,h_{z})].

This condition is equivalent to requiring that the entire paraboloid
\Pi^{\uparrow}[(x,h)]
is not covered by the union of all other paraboloids.
For any
{\lambda}\geq 1
, we define observation windows
as
W_{\lambda}:=\left[-\frac{1}{2}{\lambda}^{1/d},\frac{1}{2}{\lambda}^{1/d}\right]^{d}
. The statistic
N_{\lambda}
is now given by
N_{\lambda}:={\cal P}^{\text{thin}}\cap(W_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})
, the number of points of the thinned process
{\cal P}^{\text{thin}}
inside the observation window
W_{\lambda}
.
N_{\lambda}

depends on
t
but we will usually suppress this dependence for notational convenience. Abusing notation we represent points in
{\cal P}_{\lambda}
by
(z,h_{z})
instead of
(z,t_{z})
so as to avoid a notation clash with the time parameter
t
.

Laguerre tessellations.

The union of the paraboloids which are not entirely covered has a lower boundary, termed the paraboloid process, given by
the variational formula

F(t,w)=\inf_{(z,h_{z})\in{\cal P}}\left(h_{z}+\frac{|z-w|^{2}}{2t}\right)

where the
\inf
runs over all points of
{\cal P}
, the
Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
.
The union of all the points
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for which the infimum is realized in
(z,h_{z})
gives rise to the
Laguerre cell
generated by
(z,h_{z})
, namely

C((z,h_{z}),{\cal P}):=\left\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{|z-w|^{2}}{2t}+h_{z}\leq\frac{|z^{\prime}-w|^{2}}{2t}+h_{z^{\prime}}\ {\rm{for\ all}}\ (z^{\prime},h_{z^{\prime}})\in{\cal P}\right\}.

Note that this cell is not necessarily non-empty. It is also a curious geometric observation that a cell center need not belong to the cell which it generates. The collection of non-empty cells thus generated
is a stationary random tessellation known as the
Laguerre tessellation
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
induced by
{\cal P}
.
Note that
(x,h)\in{\cal P}^{\text{thin}}
if and only if
x
is a
cell center of a non-trivial cell.
Hence the statistic
N_{\lambda}
counts the number of points
(z,h_{z})\in{\cal P}\cap(W_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})
which generate non-trivial cells; note that while the cell center must lie in
W_{\lambda}
the same need not be true for the cell itself.

When the measure
{\cal P}
has density growing like
h^{\beta}
in the height parameter
h
with
\beta\in(-1,\infty)
,
(respectively
(-h)^{-\beta}
with
\beta\in((d+1)/2,\infty)
; respectively
e^{h/2}
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)
(respectively on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0]
; respectively on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
), then the dual to the Laguerre tessellation is known as the

\beta
-Delaunay tessellation (respectively
\beta^{\prime}
-Delaunay tessellation; respectively Gaussian tessellation).
The dual tessellations were introduced and studied in
[
16
]
.

When the weights
h_{z},z\in{\cal P}
, are a.s. bounded, then the geometry of the Laguerre tessellations is locally determined
[
14
]
.
However if the weights are not bounded, as in the three afore-mentioned tessellations, then
the geometry is not in general locally determined.
On the other hand, as shown in Lemma 4 of
[
16
]
,
the
\beta
,
\beta^{\prime}
and Gaussian-Delaunay tessellations have a geometry which localizes
in space in the sense that with high probability its structure on a cylinder

{\rm{Cyl}}(0,r):=B_{d}(0,r)\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
of fixed radius is not affected by points in
{\cal P}
lying
far enough away from the cylinder.
This high probability localization is enough to
show that geometric characteristics of the
Laguerre tessellation, such as the number count
N_{\lambda}
of cell centers in
W_{\lambda}
, satisfies
mixing conditions (called absolute regularity in
[
16
]
) ensuring a qualitative central limit theorem.

Stochastic Burgers PDE.

The statistics
N_{\lambda},{\lambda}\geq 1,
also arise
when considering statistical properties of the zero-viscosity solution
to the Burgers PDE, which in
general dimension
d
is given by

v_{t}+v\nabla v=\epsilon_{{\lambda}}\nabla^{2}v,

(6.7)

with viscosity
\epsilon_{{\lambda}}\to 0
, and initial data

v(0,w)=-2\epsilon_{{\lambda}}\nabla h
,
with
h(w)
standing for the random initial potential at
w
.

Equation (
6.7
) models turbulence and a standard simplifying assumption (see
[
1
]
) is to let the initial potential be zero-range Poissonian shot noise, i.e.
e^{v(0,w)}=\sum_{(z,h_{z})\in{\cal P}}e^{h_{z}}\delta(w-z)
,
where
{\cal P}
is a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
. This corresponds to the case where there is no interaction between potentials at distinct points of the ensemble
{\cal P}
, see Albeverio et al.
[
1
]
.

The Burgers festoon
\Phi({\cal P})
is the hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
which is the
boundary of the union
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
of translates of the down paraboloid
\{(z,h_{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}:h_{z}<-|z|^{2}/2t\}
, and having the property that
U
contains no points of
{\cal P}
and
U
is maximal in the sense that it is not contained
in any other such union.
The graph of the hypersurface
\Phi({\cal P})
is given by the variational formula

H(t,w)=\sup_{(z,h_{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}\left(h_{z}-\frac{|z-w|^{2}}{2t}\right)

where the sup runs over all
(z,h_{z})
such that
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(z,h_{z})]\cap{\cal P}=\emptyset
.

H(t,w)
is known as the ‘hull process’
[
28
]
, it is a concatenation of faces of downwards parabolas, and it plays an important role in the construction of the geometric solution to (
6.7
) as well in establishing variance asymptotics for the number of vertices of convex hulls of i.i.d. samples
[
28
]
.
The solution
v(t,w)
to (
6.7
) is the space derivative
\frac{\partial H}{\partial w}(t,w)
when the ensemble
{\cal P}
can be realized
as a marked point set
\{(z_{i},M_{i})\}
with the random variables
M_{i}
satisfying distribution and integrability conditions
[
1
]
. When
d=1
, the velocity is the saw-tooth curve

v(t,w)\sim\frac{w-z_{i}^{*}}{t},

where given
(t,z)
,
z_{i}^{*}:=z_{i}^{*}(t,z)\in\{z_{i}\}
is the point where the collection

\{h_{z}-\frac{|z-w|^{2}}{2t};\ (z,h_{z})\in\Phi({\cal P})\}

attains its maximum.

More generally, if the random initial potential is rapidly oscillating then only its local maxima determine
v(t,w)
, as seen in Burgers
[
8
]
; when the location of these maxima are well approximated by the maxima of Poissonian shot noise, the zero viscosity solution converges to the space derivative
H_{w}(t,w)
; see

[
3
,
25
]
.

Of special importance is the duality between the hull process and the paraboloid process
F(t,w)
. The
vertices of the hull process
are the points of the Poisson point process belonging to
H(t,w)
.
As seen in the discussion in Section 3 of
[
28
]
and especially the identity (3.17) ibid,
the duality relation between the hull and paraboloid processes yields that
the vertices of the hull process coincide with the thinned points

{\cal P}^{\text{thin}}
. Thus
N_{\lambda}
also counts the number of vertices of the hull process in
W_{\lambda}
.

When
d=2
,
N_{\lambda}
is the number of shocks to the zero viscosity solution
\partial H/\partial w
which belong to
W_{\lambda}
, whereas in

d=3
, one may interpret
N_{\lambda}
as modeling the number of cosmic voids in the window
W_{\lambda}
in the turbulence model of
the evolution of the universe
[
3
]
, whenever the location of the maxima of the initial potential are well approximated by the maxima of shot noise.

Corollary 6.2
.

(normal approximation of the count statistics
(N_{\lambda})_{{\lambda}\geq 1}
)
Let the intensity density of
{\cal P}
be
dxd\mu(h)=dxh^{\beta}{\bf{1}}(h>0)dh
for some
\beta>-1
. Then for each
t>0
, the count statistic
(N_{\lambda})_{{\lambda}\geq 1}
satisfies the normal approximation bounds

\mathbf{d}_{K}\left(\frac{N_{\lambda}-\mathbb{E}\,N_{\lambda}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}N_{\lambda}}},N\right)\leq C_{K}\cdot\frac{{\lambda}^{1/2}}{{\rm Var}N_{\lambda}}

and

\mathbf{d}_{W}\left(\frac{N_{\lambda}-\mathbb{E}\,N_{\lambda}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}N_{\lambda}}},N\right)\leq C_{K}\frac{{\lambda}^{1/2}}{{\rm Var}N_{\lambda}}+C_{W}\frac{{\lambda}}{({\rm Var}N_{\lambda})^{3/2}},

where
C_{K},C_{W}
are constants depending only on
d,\beta
and
t
.

This corollary establishes
proximity bounds to the normal, adding to the the qualitative central limit theorem of Theorem 7 of
[
16
]
, which does not attempt to find rates, and it also improves upon the rates in Theorem 2.1 of
[
35
]
(the case
\alpha=2
), which contains extraneous logarithmic factors and which assumes
\beta\geq 0
.
During the course of writing this paper we learned of the preprint of Bhattacharjee and Gusakova
[
4
]
which
establishes Corollary
6.2
; it
employs the theory of region-stabilizing scores and hence the approach does not overlap ours.

Proof of Corollary
6.2
.
We express
N_{\lambda}
as a sum of score functions defined on the point process
{\cal P}
and apply Corollary
2.9
with
X=W=W_{\lambda}
in conjunction with
Remark (
iii
) in Subsection
2.4
. In what follows, we set
t=1/2
for ease of presentation, though when
t\neq 1/2
, the only change amounts to all constants henceforth depending on
t
.

We put
\xi((z,h_{z}),{\cal P})=1
if
(z,h_{z})
belongs to the festoon
\Phi({\cal P})
, i.e. if it is retained in the thinning
{\cal P}^{\text{thin}}
, otherwise the score is zero. More precisely, we define
\xi
as

\xi((z,h_{z}),{\cal P}):=\begin{cases}1&\exists\ (w,h_{w})\in\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[(z,h_{z})],\text{ s.t. }\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]\cap({\cal P}\setminus\{(z,h_{z})\})=\emptyset\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note the duality relation
(w,h_{w})\in\Pi^{\uparrow}[(z,h_{z})]\Leftrightarrow(z,h_{z})\in\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]
. It follows that a point
(z,h_{z})\in{\cal P}
is such that
\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[(z,h_{z})]
is not covered by
\bigcup_{(x,h_{x})\in{\cal P}\setminus\{(z,h_{z})\}}\Pi^{\uparrow}[(x,h_{x})]
if and only if there is a point
(w,h_{w})\in\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[(z,h_{z})]
such that
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]\cap{\cal P}\setminus\{(z,h_{z})\}
is empty.
Now it holds that

N_{\lambda}=\sum_{(z,h_{z})\in{\cal P}_{\lambda}}\xi((z,h_{z}),{\cal P}).

In what follows we do not put
\xi^{[r]}
to be the space-restricted score as at (
2.8
) but instead use a slight modification as follows. With
B(y,r)
denoting the
d
-dimensional ball centered at
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
r\in(0,\infty)
, we denote by
{\rm{Cyl}}(y,r)
the cylinder
B(y,r)\times\mathbb{R}
. Define space-localized scores short-range scores

\xi^{[r]}((z,h_{z}),{\cal P}):=\begin{cases}1&\exists\ (w,h_{w})\in\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[(z,h_{z})]\cap{\rm{Cyl}}(z,r),\\
&\qquad\text{s.t. }\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]\cap{\rm{Cyl}}(z,r)\cap({\cal P}\setminus\{(z,h_{z})\})=\emptyset\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The difference between this definition of
\xi^{[r]}
and the one given in (
2.8
) is that the former only considers whether the intersection of paraboloids and a radius
r
cylinder is covered, whereas the latter requires that entire paraboloids are covered. For the time-restricted score, we use
\xi^{(s)}
as in (
2.8
).

The scores and their short-range counterparts are all bounded by
1
and thus
M_{5,\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{\xi}=1
.
It remains to show
BL(\tfrac{1}{240},\tfrac{1}{120})
space-time localization of scores.
We will in fact show that
\xi
satisfies
BL(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
space-time localization for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}>0
.

BL
-localization of
\xi
in time.

We show that the scores satisfy
BL(\theta^{\prime})
time-localization for all
\theta^{\prime}>0
, and in fact

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\xi((z,h_{z}),{\mathcal{P}}\cup{\mathcal{A}})\neq\xi^{(s)}((z,h_{z}),{\mathcal{P}}\cup{\mathcal{A}})\bigg)\leq\phi(s),\quad s\geq 0,

(6.8)

where
\phi
decays exponentially fast uniformly over point sets
{\mathcal{A}}
of cardinality six.
Without loss of generality we may assume
s\geq 4
, as we may take
\phi(s)=2
for
0\leq s\leq 4
.
By stationarity we may take

(z,h_{z})=({\bf 0},h_{{\bf 0}})
.

Suppose
h_{\bf 0}\geq s
. Then
\xi^{(s)}(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),{\cal P}\cup{\cal A})=0
and we prove that
\mathbb{P}(\xi(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),{\cal P}\cup{\cal A})\neq 0)
decreases exponentially fast in
h_{\bf 0}
and hence also in
s
. Assume
\xi(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),{\cal P}\cup{\cal A})\neq 0
. Then there is a point
(w,h_{w})\in\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]
such that
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]\cap({\cal P}\cup{\cal A})\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\}=\emptyset
. Note that for any point
(w,h_{w})\in\Pi^{\uparrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]
, at least half of
\Pi^{\downarrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]
is included inside
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]
, hence half of
\Pi^{\downarrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]
does not contain any points of
{\cal P}
. The inclusion can be seen e.g. assuming without loss of generality that
w=(a,0,...,0)
with
0\leq a\leq\sqrt{2(h_{w}-h_{\bf 0})}
and checking analytically that any point
(x,h_{x})\in\Pi^{\downarrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]
with
x_{1}\geq 0
also belongs to
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]
.
The volume of
\Pi^{\downarrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]
is given by
c_{\Pi^{\downarrow}}h_{\bf 0}^{\beta+1+\frac{d}{2}}\geq c_{\Pi^{\downarrow}}h_{\bf 0}^{\frac{d}{2}}
, where
c_{\Pi^{\downarrow}}=\frac{d\kappa_{d}\sqrt{2}^{d-2}}{\beta+1}B(\frac{d}{2},\beta+2)
and where
B
is the Beta function. It follows that
\mathbb{P}(\xi(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),{\cal P}\cup{\cal A})\neq 0)\leq\exp(-\frac{1}{2}c_{\Pi^{\downarrow}}h_{\bf 0}^{d/2})\leq\exp(-\frac{1}{2}c_{\Pi^{\downarrow}}s^{d/2})
.

Next, suppose
0\leq h_{\bf 0}\leq s
. If

\xi(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),({\mathcal{P}}\cup{\mathcal{A}})\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,s)))
vanishes then so must
\xi(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),{\mathcal{P}}\cup{\mathcal{A}})
.
On the other hand, suppose
\xi(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),({\mathcal{P}}\cup{\mathcal{A}})\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,s)))\neq 0
,
but
\xi(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),{\mathcal{P}}\cup{\mathcal{A}})=0
. In this case, there is a point
(w,h_{w})\in\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]
such that
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]
does not contain any point of
({\cal P}\cup{\cal A})\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,s))
other than
({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})
, but it must contain a point of
({\cal P}\cup{\cal A})\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[s,\infty))
. This implies in particular that
h_{w}\geq s
. Partition
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[s,\infty)
into unit volume cubes and let
q_{1},q_{2},...
be an enumeration of those cubes having non-empty intersection with
\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]\times[s,\infty)
. If there is a point
(w,h_{w})\in q_{i}
such that
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]
does not contain any point of
{\cal P}\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,s))
, then a constant proportion of
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w_{q_{i}},h_{q_{i}})]
also does not contain any point of
{\cal P}\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,s))
,
where
(w_{q_{i}},h_{q_{i}})
is the centre of the cube
q_{i}
. The number of cubes
q_{i}
at height
h
is at most of order
h^{d/2}
.
Define

p_{i}:=\mathbb{P}(\exists(w,h_{w})\in q_{i}:\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]\cap{\cal P}\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,s))=\emptyset).

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\xi(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),{\cal P}\cup{\cal A})\neq 0)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}p_{i}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{s}^{\infty}h^{d/2}\exp\bigg(-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{s}{\bf{1}}((x,h_{x})\in\Pi^{\downarrow}[({\bf 0},h)])h_{x}^{\beta}dh_{x}dx\bigg)dh.

When
s\leq h\leq 2s
, then
\Pi^{\downarrow}[({\bf 0},h)]
contains
\Pi^{\downarrow}[({\bf 0},s)]
and the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{s}{\bf{1}}((x,h_{x})\in\Pi^{\downarrow}[({\bf 0},h)])h_{x}^{\beta}dh_{x}dx
has a lower bound of order
s^{d/2}
.

When
h\geq 2s
, then
h-s\geq\frac{1}{2}h
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{s}{\bf{1}}((x,h_{x})\in\Pi^{\downarrow}[({\bf 0},h)])h_{x}^{\beta}dh_{x}dx

\displaystyle=\kappa_{d}2^{d/2}\int_{0}^{s}(h-h_{x})^{d/2}h_{x}^{\beta}dh_{x}

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{d}h^{d/2}\int_{0}^{s}h_{x}^{\beta}dh_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{d}}{\beta+1}h^{d/2}s^{\beta+1}\geq\frac{\kappa_{d}}{\beta+1}h^{d/2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\xi(({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0}),{\cal P}\cup{\cal A})\neq 0)

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{s}^{2s}h^{d/2}\exp(-cs^{d/2})dh+c\int_{2s}^{\infty}h^{d/2}\exp(-ch^{d/2})dh

\displaystyle\leq c\exp(-cs^{d/2}).

Hence time localization (
2.4
) follows, as asserted.

BL
-localization of
\xi
in space.
To show BL localization in space we follow the proof ideas from
[
35
]
.
It suffices to show

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\xi((z,h_{z}),{\mathcal{P}}\cup{\mathcal{A}})\neq\xi^{[r]}((z,h_{z}),{\mathcal{P}}\cup{\mathcal{A}})\bigg)\leq\psi(r),\quad r\geq 0,

(6.9)

with
\psi
the exponential function.
Since the proof relies on probability
bounds for certain regions being devoid of points of
{\cal P},
we assume without loss of generality that
{\cal A}=\emptyset
.

Fix
r_{0}\geq 4
and
h_{0}\geq 0
. For any
r\geq r_{0}
consider the event

E_{r}:=\left\{\xi(({\bf 0},h_{{\bf 0}}),{\cal P})\neq\xi^{[r]}(({\bf 0},h_{{\bf 0}}),{\cal P})\right\}.

By stationarity it suffices to show
\mathbb{P}(E_{r})
decays exponentially fast in
r
. We rewrite
E_{r}=E_{r,1}\cup E_{r,2}
where

\displaystyle E_{r,1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi^{[r]}(({\bf 0},h_{{\bf 0}}),{\cal P})=1\text{ and }\xi(({\bf 0},h_{{\bf 0}}),{\cal P})=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\exists(z,h_{z})\in\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]\cap{\rm Cyl}({\bf 0},r):\Pi^{\downarrow}[(z,h_{z})]\cap({\cal P}\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\})\cap{\rm Cyl}({\bf 0},r)=\emptyset

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{and }\Pi^{\downarrow}[(z,h_{z})]\cap{\cal P}\cap{\rm Cyl}^{c}({\bf 0},r)\neq\emptyset\Big\}

and

\displaystyle E_{r,2}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\xi^{[r]}(({\bf 0},h_{{\bf 0}}),{\cal P})=0\text{ and }\xi(({\bf 0},h_{{\bf 0}}),{\cal P})=1\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\forall(w,h_{w})\in\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]\cap{\rm Cyl}({\bf 0},r),\ \Pi^{\downarrow}[(w,h_{w})]\cap{\cal P}\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\}\cap{\rm Cyl}({\bf 0},r)\neq\emptyset,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{and }\exists(z,h_{z})\in\partial\Pi^{\uparrow}[({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})]\cap{\rm Cyl}^{c}({\bf 0},r):\Pi^{\downarrow}[(z,h_{z})]\cap{\cal P}\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\}=\emptyset.

On event
E_{r,1}
we have
|z|\leq r
and

h_{z}\geq\frac{r^{2}}{8}\geq 2.

(6.10)

This follows by considering the two cases
|z|\geq r/\penalty 2
or

d(z,\partial B_{d}({\bf 0},r))\geq r/\penalty 2
, as in both cases
(z,h_{z})
belongs to an up paraboloid
\Pi^{\uparrow}[(w,h_{w})]
where the point
(w,h_{w})
is such that
|w-z|\geq r/2
. As before, partition
B({\bf 0},r)\cap[\frac{r^{2}}{8},\infty)
into cubes
q_{i}
of unit volume and it follows that if
(z,h_{z})\in q_{i}
, then a proportion of
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w_{q_{i}},h_{q_{i}})]\cap{\rm Cyl}({\bf 0},r)
must be devoid of points of
{\cal P}\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\}
. Note that the volume of
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w_{q_{i}},h_{q_{i}})]\cap{\rm Cyl}({\bf 0},r)
is lower bounded by
ch_{q_{i}}^{\beta+1}r^{d}
for some constant
c>0
. Indeed, the intersection of
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w_{q_{i}},h_{q_{i}})]\cap{\rm Cyl}({\bf 0},r)
with
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}
is the set
B({\bf 0},r)\cap B(w_{q_{i}},\sqrt{2h_{q_{i}}})
. Since
h_{q_{i}}\geq r^{2}/8
and
w_{q_{i}}\in B({\bf 0},r)
, this intersection contains a ball of radius
r/4
. It follows that
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w_{q_{i}},h_{q_{i}})]\cap{\rm Cyl}({\bf 0},r)
contains a cone with base a ball of radius
r/4
and height
h_{q_{i}}
, the volume of which is of the order
r^{d}h_{q_{i}}^{\beta+1}
.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(E_{r_{1}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists(z,h_{z})\in q_{i}:\Pi^{\downarrow}[(z,h_{z})]\cap({\cal P}\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\})\cap{\rm Cyl}({\bf 0},r)=\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\frac{r^{2}}{8}}^{\infty}h^{d/2}\exp(-cr^{d}h^{\beta+1})dh

\displaystyle\leq c\exp(-cr^{d})

To estimate
\mathbb{P}(E_{r,2})
, note that for
(z,h_{z})\in\partial(\Pi^{\uparrow}[({\bf 0},h_{{\bf 0}})])\cap{\rm{Cyl}}^{c}({\bf 0},r)
we must have

h_{z}\geq h_{{\bf 0}}+\frac{r^{2}}{2}\geq\frac{r^{2}}{2}.

(6.11)

Partition
B({\bf 0},r)^{c}\times[\frac{r^{2}}{2},\infty)
into cubes
q_{i}
of unit volume as before, and note that if
(z,h_{z})\in q_{i}
, then a proportion of
\Pi^{\downarrow}[(w_{q_{i}},h_{q_{i}})]
is also devoid of points of
{\cal P}\setminus\{({\bf 0},h_{\bf 0})\}
. This event has probability bounded by
c\exp(-ch_{q_{i}}^{d/2+\beta+1})
, as explained in the proof of time-localization. The number of cubes at height
h
is again at most of order
h^{d/2}
and it follows that

\mathbb{P}(E_{r,2})\leq c\int_{\frac{r^{2}}{2}}^{\infty}h^{d/2}\exp(-ch^{d/2})dh\leq c\exp(-cr^{d}).

Since
\mathbb{P}(E_{r})\leq\mathbb{P}(E_{r,1})+\mathbb{P}(E_{r,1})
where both
\mathbb{P}(E_{r,1})
and
\mathbb{P}(E_{r,2})

decay exponentially fast in
r
, this shows BL-localization in space.

Corollary
2.9
thus establishes the asserted proximity bounds to the normal.
