ABSTRACT
Key PointsQuestionIs middle meningeal artery embolization (MMAE) with n-butyl cyanoacrylate (n-BCA) plus standard of care (SOC) safe and effective for the treatment of patients with chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH) compared with SOC alone?FindingsIn this randomized clinical trial with 376 participants, MMAE using the study device plus SOC reduced the rate of recurrence and re-accumulation of hematoma and need for surgery over 6 months (11.6% with MMAE plus SOC vs 22.1% with SOC alone; 47% reduction in odds).MeaningThese findings suggest that adjunctive treatment of cSDH using MMAE with n-BCA resulted in reduced rates of recurrence or re-accumulation and need for surgery.

Introduction

Historically, standard treatment of symptomatic chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH) has been surgical evacuation. This approach, however, does not address the underlying pathophysiology of chronic inflammation and neovascularization of the dura and is associated with an incidence of recurrence of up to 30%.1,2,3 Middle meningeal artery embolization (MMAE) may provide a mechanism to limit vascular supply to the membrane and thus promotes reabsorption rather than accumulation of the hematoma. Thus, MMAE has attracted increasing interest in the management of cSDH, either as an adjunct to surgery or as a first-line treatment in patients not undergoing surgery. The rapid adoption of MMAE procedures over the last few years coupled with the increasing evidence regarding its efficacy and the aging population could potentially make MMAE the dominant neurovascular procedure performed in the future.4,5

Prior randomized clinical trials focusing on MMAE with liquid embolic agents suggest reduced chances of hematoma expansion and clinical decline, but with mixed results.6,7,8,9 The TRUFILL n-Butyl Cyanoacrylate (n-BCA) Liquid Embolic System is an artificial embolization device composed of n-BCA, ethiodized oil, and tantalum powder, which has been commercially available in the United States since 2000 for the embolization of cerebral arteriovenous malformations for presurgical devascularization. We hypothesized that this embolic agent could be well suited for MMAE in patients with cSDH. The objective of the Middle Meningeal Artery Embolization for the Treatment of Subdural Hematomas With TRUFILL n-BCA (MEMBRANE) study was to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the study device for MMAE with standard of care (SOC) compared with SOC management alone in patients presenting with cSDH. In this article, we report results up to the 6-month follow-up.

Methods

Study Design

MEMBRANE is a prospective, multicenter, open-label, randomized clinical trial conducted at 30 hospitals in the United States (28 sites) and China (2 sites). All sites were required to have a neurosurgical program capable of treating subdural hematomas, and investigators were required to have previous experience with n-BCA (≥15 cases) and MMAE procedures with an embolic agent for the treatment of subdural hematoma (≥5 cases).

The study design and procedures have been published previously10; complete details are provided in the protocol (Supplement 1). Additional details appear in the eMethods in Supplement 2. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles that have their origin in the Declaration of Helsinki, as well as in compliance with the local regulatory requirements, as detailed in the eMethods in Supplement 2. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants and/or their legal representative. All site principal investigators and study investigators are listed in eTable 17 in Supplement 2. Reporting followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) reporting guideline.

Participants

The study population was recruited from patients presenting with previously untreated but symptomatic cSDH not requiring emergent surgery or decompression. Patients were assessed by the site physicians to determine whether the patient would undergo either surgery or nonsurgical management of the hematoma per SOC. Investigators assessed potential study participants as candidates for the study and screened for trial enrollment based on the protocol inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Eligible participants were aged 18 to 90 years, had a diagnosis of cSDH with mass effect determined by brain imaging (computed tomography [CT] or magnetic resonance imaging) and correlated clinical symptoms, and had a prerandomization modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 3 or less. In the nonsurgical cohort, the cSDH was required to have a midline shift of less than 10 mm and hematoma thickness greater than 10 mm at screening (presurgical evacuation; there was no size requirement for surgical patients). For all participants, treatment with the study device was considered feasible in the opinion of the treating physician. Complete inclusion and exclusion criteria have been published previously and are detailed in eTable 1 in Supplement 2. Initial screening procedures as well as randomization and masking are described in the eMethods in Supplement 2.

Procedures

In the surgical cohort, participants underwent a surgical procedure for cSDH decompression before randomization. For all surgical procedures, anesthesia administration and surgical evacuation were performed using standard practice. Participants randomized to embolization then underwent the MMAE procedure within 10 days after surgery within the same hospital admission, while participants randomized to surgery alone received no further intervention. The nonsurgical cohort received nonsurgical medical management (NSMM) alone or NSMM with MMAE. NSMM included modifying or stopping oral anticoagulation therapy, initiating statins, observation, repeat imaging, and lifestyle modification. Participants in the NSMM with MMAE group underwent the MMAE procedure within 10 days after randomization. Participants were not permitted to undergo MMAE during the follow-up period (no crossover). The use of antithrombotic medications was permitted during the study.

Embolization Procedure

The study device has been described previously10 and in the eMethods in Supplement 2. Anesthesia administration was performed using standard practice. Digital subtraction angiography of the middle meningeal artery was performed immediately before infusion of n-BCA. Selection of the target artery segment and angiography were performed using standard microcatheterization techniques, and images were submitted to the independent imaging core laboratory. Operators were trained to attempt to achieve distal penetration of the embolic agent.

Follow-Up

Participants were followed up at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after the procedure, in person or via telehealth (except for the 6-month visit, which had to be in the clinic). A CT scan was required for each visit (performed at the study site or elsewhere) and was submitted to the independent imaging core laboratory. While mRS and Markwalder Grading Scale (MGS) scores, adverse events (AEs), medications, adherence to SOC recommendations, and resource utilization data were collected at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months, the mRS and MGS scores at 3, 6, and 12 months were collected independently. Results at 12 months will be reported in a future publication.

Outcomes

The primary effectiveness end point was residual or re-accumulation of cSDH (>10 mm) at 6 months (assessed by an independent core laboratory) or requiring a surgical procedure on the cSDH within 6 months after randomization. The primary safety end point was the incidence of all AEs through 6 months of follow-up. Secondary effectiveness end points are described in the eMethods in Supplement 2. A post hoc analysis of the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) at the 6-month visit was performed. Health care utilization and quality-of-life end points included hospital and intensive care unit days and change from baseline in EuroQol 5-Dimension 5-Level (EQ-5D-5L) score at 6 months.

Statistical Analysis

Sample Size Calculation

Superiority of MMAE plus SOC over SOC alone in the primary effectiveness end point was tested using a Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test, with stratification by surgical cohort. Considering anticipated event rates for the primary effectiveness end point derived from the literature and assuming a common odds ratio (OR) of 0.34, a sample size of 376 participants, after accounting for 10% attrition, was calculated to provide 80% or greater power for the primary effectiveness end point at a 1-sided significance level of .05.11,12,13,14,15,16,17,18 For the primary safety end point, we expected an 80% or greater probability of observing AEs with an incidence of 1% or greater in each treatment group, assuming that approximately 188 participants received MMAE plus SOC and 188 participants received SOC alone. All statistical analyses were specified a priori in the statistical analysis plan, in accordance with the sample size and power calculation, in agreement with the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the studied indication.

Analysis Populations and Methods

The intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis set consisted of all enrolled participants who were randomized in the study. Participants were analyzed according to their randomized treatment regardless of the actual treatment received. The per-protocol (PP) analysis set was a subset of the ITT analysis set, excluding participants with significant eligibility deviations as well as those who did not receive treatment as assigned, in addition to other major protocol deviations. The as-treated (AT) analysis set consisted of all participants randomized in the study who received study treatment. Participants were analyzed by the actual treatment they received. Missing data for the primary effectiveness end point were handled with multiple imputation, which was also applied to secondary end points, including mRS score at 3 months, mean change in hematoma volume at 6 months, and surgical procedures for cSDH within 12 months. For the secondary end point analysis of good functional outcome, a noninferiority margin of –0.12 was selected based on results from the EMBOLISE study (Jared Knopman, MD, unpublished data, February 2024). Hypotheses in the study were tested in a gatekeep strategy per protocol. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 or later (SAS Institute) and R version 4.4.3 (R Project for Statistical Computing). Analysis methods are presented in the eMethods in Supplement 2.

Results

Patient Enrollment and Characteristics

Between May 27, 2021, and February 6, 2024, 390 participants were enrolled at 30 sites (28 sites in the United States and 2 sites in China). Of those enrolled participants, 376 were randomized and included in the ITT analysis set, with 188 participants receiving MMAE plus SOC (133 surgery with MMAE; 55 with NSMM with MMAE) and 188 receiving SOC alone (132 with surgery alone; 56 with NSMM) (Figure 1). The AT analysis set included 371 participants, while the PP analysis set included 312 participants. The proportion of participants with missing primary end point data was as follows: in the surgery with MMAE group, 27 (20.3%); surgery alone, 38 (28.8%); NSMM with MMAE, 15 (27.3%); and NSMM alone, 19 (33.9%).

Procedural Characteristics

Procedural and embolization characteristics are summarized in eTable 8 and eTable 9 in Supplement 2. Mean (SD) MMAE procedural time (from arterial puncture time to when the last catheter was withdrawn) was 71.2 (32.37) minutes, and conscious sedation was used in 31 of 180 MMAE procedures (17.2%). Successful embolization in the opinion of treating physician (answered by the physician as yes or no) was achieved in 178 of 180 participants (98.9%) who had an MMAE procedure.

Primary Effectiveness End Point

In the ITT analysis set, the incidence of primary effectiveness end point events was 17 of 146 participants (11.6%) who received MMAE plus SOC and 29 of 131 participants (22.1%) who received SOC alone. The final estimate of the common OR was 0.53 (90% CI, 0.31-0.91; P = .04), indicating a statistically significant treatment benefit for MMAE with the study device plus SOC versus SOC alone (Figure 2A). Multiple imputation was applied for missing data. While the surgical and NSMM cohorts were not powered separately, both ORs favored MMAE plus SOC treatment, and statistical significance was achieved in the surgical cohort. Sensitivity analyses supported the treatment benefit in the direction of the study device without qualitative interactions between cohort × treatment (eFigure in Supplement 2). Although attrition for the primary effectiveness end point was slightly higher than anticipated, the assumption that data were missing at random was evaluated by comparing disposition, demographic, and baseline characteristics; no notable differences were observed in patients with or without missing data in the primary effectiveness end point. The sensitivity analysis of using the observed data showed an estimate of the common OR of 0.46, with a 1-sided P value of .02 using observed data alone in the ITT analysis set (eTable 10 in Supplement 2). Homogeneity across regions (United States vs outside United States) and across sites was assessed, and no evidence of heterogeneity was observed in the study.

Primary Safety End Point

In the AT analysis set, 414 AEs (nonserious and serious) occurred in 130 of 181 participants (71.8%) in the MMAE plus SOC group and 392 AEs occurred in 124 of 190 participants (65.3%) in the SOC alone group (eTable 11 in Supplement 2). Only 1 AE was adjudicated by the clinical events committee (CEC) as having a causal relationship to the study device, which was a nonserious AE of transient facial nerve palsy; the patient recovered the same day.

Serious AEs occurred in 51 of 181 participants (28.2%) and 60 of 190 participants (31.6%) in the MMAE plus SOC and SOC alone groups, respectively. A total of 22 all-cause deaths were reported (6 in the MMAE plus SOC group and 16 in the SOC alone group), none of which were determined by the CEC to be related to study device or MMAE procedure; 7 deaths were considered to be neurologic deaths. Eight strokes were reported in 6 participants (3 who received an MMAE procedure and 3 who received SOC alone); 2 strokes were determined to not be related to the study device and possibly related to the MMAE procedure.

Secondary Safety End Points

Results of the secondary safety end points are summarized in Table 2. The percentage of participants with a good functional outcome at 3 months (mRS 0-2 or no worsening from baseline if baseline mRS ≥3) was numerically greater in the MMAE plus SOC group than in the SOC alone group (85.3% [122 of 143] vs 77.0% [104 of 135], respectively). The final estimate for risk difference with imputed data was 0.07 (90% CI, –0.001 to 0.15); the lower 90% CI bound was greater than the noninferiority margin of –0.12 (Jared Knopman, MD, unpublished data, February 2024), indicating that the study device was noninferior to SOC alone for functional outcome (Figure 2B).

The rate of death, stroke, myocardial infarction, or thromboembolic complications up to 6 months was numerically lower in the MMAE plus SOC group compared with the SOC alone group (7.4% vs 11.0%, respectively) (Figure 3B). New-onset seizures were reported in 11 participants in the MMAE plus SOC group and 6 in the SOC alone group at 3 months, with no further incidence at 6 months (Table 2); 12 of these new-onset seizures were reported to be related to the original surgical procedure. In a post hoc analysis of the ITT analysis set, the incidence of participants presenting at baseline with mild or severe cognitive impairment who improved to normal according to MMSE scores at 6 months was 31 of 115 participants (27.0%) who received MMAE plus SOC and 13 of 103 participants (12.6%) who received SOC alone. Health Care Utilization End Points

Results of the health care utilization and quality-of-life end points are summarized in eTable 16 in Supplement 2. Significant differences in favor of MMAE plus SOC over SOC alone were observed for mean (SD) length of stay for rehospitalizations (9.0 [8.9] days vs 13.5 [13.2] days, respectively; P = .046) and participants with at least 1 ICU day during rehospitalization (9 of 181 [5.0%] vs 22 of 190 [11.6%], respectively; P = .02).

Discussion

The MEMBRANE study demonstrated a statistically significant treatment benefit on the primary effectiveness end point of using MMAE with the study device plus SOC vs SOC alone to treat cSDH across surgical and nonsurgical cohorts. The clinical significance of the primary effectiveness end point in MEMBRANE was based on literature and guidelines recommending a cutoff of greater than 10 mm hematoma thickness for surgical treatment,19 while requiring a surgical procedure was recommended as an unambiguous clinical measure of treatment failure.11,16,19,20,21,22 In addition, residual or re-accumulation of the cSDH of greater than 10 mm is considered to be of prognostic importance given its association with mortality, decreased quality of life, and complications from new or repeat procedures.23 Accordingly, most studies on cSDH management, including prior studies of MMAE, use this cutoff of 10 mm for patient inclusion and/or outcome assessment.8,11,16,19,20,22 The composite primary end point of residual or re-accumulation of cSDH or surgical procedure on the cSDH within 6 months after randomization includes both radiographic and clinical outcomes and accounts for any worsening of the SDH, including worsening that does not lead to surgery. The incidence of primary effectiveness end point events was 11.6% of those who received MMAE plus SOC and 22.1% who received SOC alone corresponding with an absolute risk reduction of 10.5 percentage points and a number-needed-to-treat of 10 patients. Achieving procedural success during the index procedure is of particular importance for reducing the risks associated with subsequent surgery of cSDH. In the current study, complete resolution of cSDH at 6 months was achieved in 73.0% of participants who received MMAE plus SOC, and only 3.8% of participants required a subsequent surgery. Additionally, the mRS score at 3 months was noninferior to SOC, suggesting that the adjunctive MMAE procedure did not adversely affect participants and was not linked to functional worsening.

The MEMBRANE study showed similar rates of stroke, myocardial infarction, and peripheral thromboembolic complications with MMAE plus SOC versus SOC alone. While a numerically higher rate of total AEs was observed in the MMAE plus SOC group (71.8% vs 65.3% in the SOC alone group), the rate of serious AEs was lower in the MMAE plus SOC group (28.2% vs 31.6% in the SOC alone group). The 15 device- or procedure-related AEs in the MMAE plus SOC group included 5 seizures and 2 strokes. The rate of new-onset seizures was numerically higher in the MMAE plus SOC group (6.1% vs 3.2% with SOC alone), while the mortality rate was numerically higher in the SOC alone group compared with the MMAE plus SOC group (8.4% vs 3.3%, respectively). Overall, the MMAE procedure was shown to be safe, with a low risk of complications and no procedure- or device-related long-term neurologic complications in the treatment cohort. While the MEMBRANE study showed no long-term neurological AEs following embolization with the study device, other embolization studies have demonstrated long-term neurological effects; therefore, additional studies may be warranted to more fully characterize the long-term safety of MMAE procedures.

Four recent multicenter randomized clinical trials of MMAE plus SOC using other embolic agents for the treatment of cSDH have shown mixed results—2 trials (EMBOLISE6 and STEM8) met their primary effectiveness end point, whereas the 2 other trials (MAGIC-MT7 and EMPROTECT9) did not. In addition, only 2 of these studies (STEM8 and MAGIC-MT7) included patient cohorts receiving surgical or nonsurgical management, while the EMBOLISE6 and EMPROTECT9 studies only included surgical usual care as the comparison group. In this context, the MEMBRANE study adds evidence supporting n-BCA as an effective liquid embolic option for adjunctive treatment of cSDH in patients receiving surgical or nonsurgical management. Other key differences between the MEMBRANE study and previous studies include a 6-month follow-up for the primary end point (compared with 3 months in EMBOLISE6 and MAGIC-MT7) and inclusion of a US patient population (compared with enrollment from sites in China only for MAGIC-MT7 and Europe for EMPROTECT9). The primary effectiveness analysis in MEMBRANE was prespecified as a directional hypothesis test evaluating whether MMAE with the study device reduces treatment failure compared with SOC. Accordingly, a 1-sided test with a significance level of .05 and corresponding 2-sided 90% CIs were specified a priori in the statistical analysis plan, in accordance with the sample size and power calculation, in agreement with the FDA. Although this study represents the first randomized evaluation of this the study device for cSDH, the embolic agent has been used intracranially for 2 decades, providing substantial prior evidence regarding safety, embolic behavior, and procedural risk. The primary uncertainty, therefore, concerned effectiveness in a new indication rather than direction of effect, supporting control of the 1-sided type I error at 5%. In comparison, the STEM8 study analysis was designed to provide the trial with 90% power to show superiority with embolization vs control with respect to the primary efficacy outcome at a 2-sided α of .05.

In the MEMBRANE study, the investigator’s choice to allocate each participant to surgical or nonsurgical treatment was based on the neurological impact and severity of the symptoms related to the hematoma with or without consideration of hematoma thickness. The rationale to include no quantitative thickness threshold for the surgery group was to leave the option available to surgeons to operate on a less thick hematoma depending on symptoms and impact on patient quality of life. This approach is consistent with that used in the STEM8 and MAGIC-MT7 studies, in which the decision to choose surgical management was based solely on the treating surgeon’s judgement.7,8

Current treatment strategies carry a high risk of failure and complications,1,2,3 particularly in older patients with cSDH with comorbidities, which highlights the need for better options.11,14,17,24 The MEMBRANE study demonstrated that MMAE with the study device plus SOC reduced cSDH recurrence and surgical procedure rates during follow-up, with few serious or procedure-related AEs. Future work could include postapproval studies to improve procedural effectiveness, pooled patient-level analyses, and histologic assessments of embolization effects. Comparative studies would also provide valuable data; at the time of designing the MEMBRANE study in 2019, no comparator device had been approved for this indication. In the current absence of head-to-head comparative studies, a collaborative meta-analysis using patient-level data of available studies is at the planning stage.

The study achieved rapid enrollment despite the challenges of the COVID-19 pandemic, in part due to the study design permitting study visits via telehealth and CT scanning at nonstudy facilities. The study protocol did not mandate withholding anticoagulation therapy, thus allowing the inclusion of patients with a range of comorbidities that require anticoagulant medication. Ultimately, the study analysis almost reached superiority in good functional outcome and demonstrated that the MMAE procedure was safe, with a very low risk of complications and no procedure- or device-related long-term neurologic complications in the MMAE plus SOC–treated cohort; only 1 AE was adjudicated as having a causal relationship to the study device, a nonserious AE of transient facial nerve palsy. Additionally, a post hoc analysis suggested that participants with cognitive impairment at baseline were more likely to improve to reference range MMSE scores at 6 months in the MMAE plus SOC group compared with the SOC alone group (27.0% vs 12.6%, respectively).

Limitations

Potential limitations of the study include its conduct during the COVID-19 pandemic and the older age of participants, which may have affected adherence to follow-up visits.25 In addition, the open-label study design, site-determined surgical or nonsurgical management allocation before randomization, and differences in surgical and nonsurgical management strategies between centers has the potential to introduce selection bias and heterogeneity to the analyses. The current lack of guidelines for nonsurgical management of cSDH, including recommended medication regimens, may have impacted the consistency of nonsurgical interventions utilized in the NSMM cohorts. While results from only 6 months of follow-up are currently available, 12-month follow-up findings will be reported in due course.

Conclusions

In this randomized clinical trial, the 6-month results demonstrated a positive treatment effect of MMAE using the study device plus SOC vs SOC alone for the primary effectiveness end point, with approximately 1 recurrence or surgical failure prevented for every 10 patients treated. The safety profile was comparable with SOC alone. Furthermore, MMAE plus SOC treatment was shown to be noninferior to SOC alone based on the analysis of good functional outcome at 3 months.
