Generic vector fields on isolated complex hypersurface germs
Diogo da Silva Machado and Jose Seade

Abstract
. We study holomorphic vector fields on isolated hypersurface singularities and derive global obstructions to the existence of holomorphic vector fields on compact singular varieties. For a hypersurface germ ( V , 0 ¯ ) (V,{\underline{0}}) with an isolated singularity, we characterize the generic elements in the space of holomorphic vector fields with isolated singularity in terms of the GSV-index. Letting τ ⁡ ( V , 0 ¯ ) \tau(V,{\underline{0}}) denote the Tjurina-Greuel number, we prove that the minimal possible index is bounded below by 1 + ( − 1 ) dim ⁡ ( V ) ​ τ ​ ( V , 0 ¯ ) 1+(-1)^{\mathrm{dim}(V)}\tau(V,{\underline{0}}) . We further prove that equality holds if and only if the vector field admits an extension to ℂ n + 1 \mathbb{C}^{n+1} with a nondegenerate singularity at 0 ¯ {\underline{0}} , and that such extensions, when they exist, form an open dense subset of the set of vector fields with an isolated singularity at 0 ¯ {\underline{0}} . This yields a description of the generic vector fields on weighted homogeneous hypersurface germs. As a consequence, we obtain a characterization of weighted homogeneous hypersurface germs. Also, as applications to singular hypersurfaces in complex manifolds, we derive constraints on compact singular varieties admitting holomorphic vector fields. In particular, we show that an irreducible compact singular complex curve carrying a nontrivial holomorphic vector field with zeros is rational and has at most two singular points. We further prove that, for singular surfaces in Kähler 3-folds satisfying suitable positivity assumptions on the adjoint line bundle, the geometric genus is greater of equal than the irregularity.



Introduction

It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
) that if
(V,{\underline{0}})
is the germ of a complex analytic hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with an isolated singularity, then the space
\widehat{\Theta}(V,{\underline{0}})
of germs of holomorphic vector fields on
V
is infinite dimensional, and the subset
\Theta(V,{\underline{0}})\subset\widehat{\Theta}(V,{\underline{0}})
consisting of vector fields with an isolated singularity at
{\underline{0}}
is open and dense.
The aim of this article is to characterize the generic elements of
\Theta(V,{\underline{0}})
.

For instance, if the germ
(V,{\underline{0}})
is that of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
at
{\underline{0}}
, then the Poincaré-Hopf index of an element
v=(v_{1},...,v_{n+1})\in\Theta{({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1},{\underline{0}})}

is the intersection number:

\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\;\frac{\mathcal{O}_{{n+1},{\underline{0}}}}{\langle v_{1},...,v_{n+1}\rangle}\,.

Hence this index is positive and the vector fields with index 1 form an open and dense set in
\Theta{({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1},{\underline{0}})}
. These are the vector fields with a non-degenerate singularity at
{\underline{0}}
(i.e., vector fields whose differential is non-singular at
{\underline{0}}
).

We recall that given a hypersurface germ
(V,{\underline{0}})
as above, one has the GSV-index of vector fields
[
10
]
, which can be defined as the Poincaré-Hopf index of a continuous extension of the vector field to a local Milnor fiber.

For instance, if
V
is homogeneous, then the radial vector field
v(z)=(z_{1},....,z_{n+1})
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
leaves
V
invariant and is pointing outwards from every small sphere
{\mathbb{S}_{\varepsilon}}
centered at
{\underline{0}}
. This means that restricted to the boundary of a Milnor fiber
F_{V}
we can assume that deforming it slightly, this becomes tangent to
F_{V}
near its boundary, which is isotopic to the link
L_{V}:=V\cap{\mathbb{S}_{\varepsilon}}
. Hence, by
[
14
, Theorems 6.5 and 7.2 ]
and the theorem of Poincaré-Hopf for manifolds with boundary, one has that the GSV index of its restriction to
V
is
1+(-1)^{n}\mu
, where
\mu
is the Milnor number of the germ
(V,{\underline{0}})
, which is

\mu:=\mu_{\underline{0}}(V)\,=\,\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\;\frac{\mathcal{O}_{{n+1},{\underline{0}}}}{J(f)}\;,

where

J(f)=\langle\partial f/\partial z_{1},....,\partial f/\partial z_{n+1}\rangle

is the Jacobian ideal of a map
f
that defines the germ
(V,{\underline{0}})
. Hence
\mu\geq 1
unless the germ is non-singular, and it is
1

only when
V
has degree 2.

In particular, if
n
is odd, the index may be negative, even for holomorphic vector fields. However, by
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
(cf.
[
17
, Theorem 5.3]
), for any hypersurface germ
(V,{\underline{0}})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},{\underline{0}})
as above, there exists a maximal constant
K
, depending only on
(V,{\underline{0}})
, such that every holomorphic vector field germ
v
on
V
satisfies:

{\rm Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))\geq K.

Furthermore, the holomorphic vector fields on
V
with the smallest index form a dense open subset in the space
\Theta{(V,{\underline{0}})}
of germs of vector fields on
(V,{\underline{0}})
.

The question of determining the value of
K
arises naturally; in this work, we provide several characterizations of this constant (see, e.g., Lemma
1.1
).

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1},{\underline{0}})}
be a holomorphic germ that defines
(V,{\underline{0}})=\{f=0\}
. Recall that its Greuel-Tjurina number was defined in
[
12
]
as

\tau_{\underline{0}}(V)=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{\,}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{{n+1},{\underline{0}}}}{\langle f,J(f)\rangle}\,.

For an isolated singularity,
\tau_{\underline{0}}(V)\leq\mu_{{\underline{0}}}(V)
, with equality iff
f
is analytically equivalent to a weighted homogeneous polynomial
[
16
]
.

We prove:

Theorem 1
.

Let
(V,{\underline{0}})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},{\underline{0}})
be a hypersurface germ with an isolated singularity, and let
\Theta=\Theta{(V,{\underline{0}})}
be the space of holomorphic vector field germs on

, assume

irreducible. Denote by

(i)

For all

(ii)

There exists

if and only if there exists

(iii)

For

\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))=1+(-1)^{n}\tau_{\underline{0}}(V)
if and only if

is open and dense in

We formulate the following conjecture that we prove in part.

Conjecture
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

(V,{\underline{0}})
is weighted homogeneous germ.

(b)

There exists a holomorphic vector field on the germ
(V,{\underline{0}})
which is everywhere
transversal to the link
L_{V}
.

(c)

There exists a holomorphic vector field that extends to a neighborhood of
the origin in the ambient space with a non-degenerate isolated singularity
at

.

Implications
(a)\Longrightarrow(b)
and

are well known:
if
(V,{\underline{0}})
is a weighted homogeneous germ, then one has a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action
which defines a holomorphic vector field on the ambient space with a nondegenerate singularity at

is everywhere transversal to the link. In particular, items (ii), (iii) and (iv) of Theorem 1 apply to all weighted homogeneous singularities.

Theorem 2 is an immediate consequence of Theorem 1. It provides a characterization of weighted homogeneous singularities and partially answers the conjecture:

Theorem 2
.

Let
(V,{\underline{0}})
be a hypersurface germ in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
.

(i)

For even n: if there exists a holomorphic vector field that extends to a neighborhood of

(ii)

which is everywhere

(V,{\underline{0}})
is weighted homogeneous.

To prove the conjecture it remains to show that the two statements in Theorem 2 actually are equivalent in all dimensions.

In Section
5
we turn to compact singular varieties. The local theory developed in the previous sections highlights that the existence of holomorphic vector fields is strongly constrained by the geometry of the singularities.

From a global perspective, it is well known that compact complex manifolds generally do not admit nontrivial global holomorphic vector fields (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 1]
). However, the methods of
[
6
]
do not extend directly to singular spaces, since the complex of sheaves of regular holomorphic forms is no longer isomorphic to the classical Koszul complex. Using the local results obtained above, we derive several obstructions to the existence of global holomorphic vector fields on compact singular varieties.

We then turn to singular varieties of dimensions
1
and
2
in Section
6
. For smooth compact surfaces, Carrell
et al

[
7
, Theorem 1]
, classified smooth complex surfaces admitting a nontrivial holomorphic vector field with zeros. The singular case is substantially subtler.

We prove for curves that an irreducible compact singular complex curve admitting a nontrivial holomorphic vector field with zeros is rational and has at most two singular points.

In dimension 2 we show that if a surface
V
with isolated singularities embedded in a compact smooth Kähler threefold admits such a vector field, then

g(V)=\mathrm{dim}H^{2}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V})\geq\mathrm{dim}H^{1}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V})=q(V);

That is, its geometric genus is larger than or equal to its irregularity.

1.
The main lemma

To prove Theorem 1 we use the following lemma.

Lemma 1.1
.

Under the conditions of Theorem 1, one has

(a)

K\geq 1+(-1)^{n}\tau_{\underline{0}}(V)
;

(b)

K=1+(-1)^{n}\tau_{\underline{0}}(V)
iff some holomorphic vector field on
V
extends holomorphically near
{\underline{0}}
in the ambient space with a nondegenerate isolated singularity at
{\underline{0}}

{\rm Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))=1+(-1)^{n}\tau_{\underline{0}}(V)

Proof.

item (a):
Let
v
be a holomorphic vector field on
V
with an isolated singularity at
{\underline{0}}
. There exist infinitely many holomorphic extensions of
v
to a neighborhood of
{\underline{0}}
in the ambient space
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, each with an isolated singularity (see
[
4
]
). For hypersurface germs, the GSV index coincides with the homological index
[
11
]
. Hence, if
\widetilde{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1})
is a holomorphic extension of

[
11

provides algebraic formulas for this index in terms of the coordinates of

For odd
n
:

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))=\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle f,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}-\tau_{\underline{0}}(V).

where
\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}
denotes the ring of germs of holomorphic functions at
({\mathbb{C}}^{n+1},{\underline{0}})
and
f=0
is a local equation for
V
in a neighborhood of
{\underline{0}}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
.

We observe that

(2)

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\langle f,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\rangle}\geq 1.

Indeed, if

{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\langle f,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\rangle}=0,

then
\langle f,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\rangle=\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}
. Consequently, there would exist germs
g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{n+1}
in
\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}
such that

1=g_{0}f+\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}g_{i}v_{i},

which contradicts the fact that
{\underline{0}}\in\operatorname{Sing}(\widetilde{v})\cap\operatorname{Sing}(V)
, since

f({\underline{0}})=v_{1}({\underline{0}})=\ldots=v_{n+1}({\underline{0}})=0.

Therefore,

\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))\geq 1-\tau_{\underline{0}}(V).

For even
n
:

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))=

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}-

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v}),v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}+\tau_{\underline{0}}(V).

Since
\left\langle\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v}),v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle\supset\left\langle\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle
, we have that

\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}\geq{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v}),v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle},

with equality holding if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v})\in\left\langle\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle.

We will show that for even
n
, the condition
\displaystyle\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v})\in\left\langle\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle
never holds. Consequently, we have

(4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}>{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v}),v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle},

\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))\,\,\,\geq\,\,\,\,1+\tau_{\underline{0}}(V).

In fact, let

and assume
\displaystyle k(z)\in\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{n+1}\rangle
. We will show that this leads to a contradiction.

Condition
\displaystyle k(z)\in\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{n+1}\rangle
implies the existence of holomorphic functions
A_{1},\dots,A_{n+1}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{n+1},{\underline{0}}}
such that

(5)

k(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}A_{i}(z)v_{i}(z).

Substituting this into the tangency condition

, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}v_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}A_{i}v_{i}\right)f\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}v_{i}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}-A_{i}f\right)=0\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\langle v,G\rangle=0,

where

is the vector field with components
G_{i}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}-A_{i}f

Claim.

G_{1},\dots,G_{n+1}\in\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}
form a regular sequence.

In order to prove the claim, let us consider the zero locus
Z(G)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}:G(z)=0\}
. Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that
Z(G)\neq\{{\underline{0}}\}
. Then, for each

\mathbb{B}(1/l)
denotes the open ball centered at the origin with radius

{\underline{0}}
is an accumulation point of

. By the Curve Selection Lemma (see
[
14

\gamma:[0,\epsilon)\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that
\gamma(0)={\underline{0}}
and, for all

\gamma(t)\neq{\underline{0}}
and

.

Case 1:
If
k(z)\equiv 0
, then we can consider

and consequently

The condition

means

\displaystyle\nabla f(\gamma(t))\equiv{\underline{0}}.

On the other hand, by considering the composition
h(t)=f(\gamma(t))
, the chain rule gives us

h^{\prime}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}f(\gamma(t))=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}(\gamma(t))\gamma_{j}^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}0.\gamma_{j}^{\prime}(t)=0.

Therefore,
h(t)\equiv C
(complex constant). But, since
h(0)=f(\gamma(0))=f({\underline{0}})=0

.

Thus, we have

(8)

\displaystyle f(\gamma(t))=h(t)\equiv 0.

The relations (
7

a contradiction, since

Case 2:
If
k(z)\not\equiv 0
,

G(\gamma(t))={\underline{0}}

\nabla f(\gamma(t))=f(\gamma(t))A(\gamma(t)).

Consider the composition
h(t)=f(\gamma(t))

h^{\prime}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}f(\gamma(t))=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}(\gamma(t))\gamma_{j}^{\prime}(t).

Using the relation

Since

f({\underline{0}})=\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}({\underline{0}}),\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n+1}}({\underline{0}})=0,

we have that
f\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}
(i.e.
\overline{f}=\overline{0}
) and therefore

\left\langle\displaystyle\overline{v_{1}},\ldots,\overline{v_{n+1}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\displaystyle\overline{f},\overline{v_{1}},\ldots,\overline{v_{n+1}}\right\rangle=\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{\,\,\mathfrak{m}^{2}}.

Hence, by Nakayama’s lemma, we obtain

\left\langle\displaystyle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle=\mathfrak{m},

or, equivalently,

{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}=1.

Thus,
{\underline{0}}
is a non-degenerate isolated singularity of
\widetilde{v}
.

For even
n
: if
K=1+\tau_{\underline{0}}(V)

v
on
V
such that

\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))\,\,\,=\,\,\,\,1+\tau_{\underline{0}}(V)
. Thus, given
\widetilde{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1})
a holomorphic extensions of
v
, with an isolated singularity at
{\underline{0}}
, it follows from formula (

) that

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}-{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v}),v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}=1.

We will show that

which implies

i.e.,

Claim 2.

\displaystyle\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v})({\underline{0}})\neq 0
. In particular,
(V,{\underline{0}})
is weighted homogeneous.

In order to prove the Claim 2, we consider again the notation

.
Assume

\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}=\{[a(z)]:a(z)\in\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}\}

we consider the ideal

Endowed with the corresponding adjacent strutures of vector spaces over
{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}}{\hskip 22.76228pt\left\langle[k]\right\rangle\hskip 22.76228pt}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n+1,{\underline{0}}}}{\left\langle\displaystyle k,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right\rangle}

{\mathbb{C}}

In particular, we conclude that

4.
Example of an open family of vector fields with constant GSV

We have established that for isolated singularities, the GSV index is bounded below by
1+(-1)^{n}\tau_{{\underline{0}}}(V)
. However, if
(V,{\underline{0}})
is not weighted homogeneous, this lower bound is typically not attained. A natural question arises: what is the behavior of the index in the non-weighted homogeneous case, and how far is the “generic index” from this limit? While a complete answer falls outside the scope of this paper, we illustrate this behavior with a concrete example, demonstrating that the index remains locally constant within an open family of perturbations.

Let
V=\{f=0\}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{3},{\underline{0}})
be the non-weighted homogeneous hypersurface defined by the germ:

f(x,y,z)=z^{2}+x^{3}+y^{7}+xy^{5}

and consider the tangent vector field
\widetilde{v}\in\mathrm{Der}(-\log V)
:

\widetilde{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=(z^{2}+x^{3}+y^{7}+xy^{5})\partial_{x}+(2z)\partial_{y}-(7y^{6}+5xy^{4})\partial_{z}.

In this setting, the singular set of
V
is
{\rm Sing}(V)=\{{\underline{0}}\}
. The restriction
v:=\widetilde{v}|_{V}
has an isolated singularity on
V
at the origin. For this hypersurface, the Tjurina number is
\tau_{{\underline{0}}}(V)=11
and the Milnor number is
\mu_{{\underline{0}}}(V)=12
.

Since
\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v})=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
, we obtain

{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{(\mathbb{C}^{3},{\underline{0}})}}{\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{df}{f}(\widetilde{v}),v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\right\rangle}={\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{(\mathbb{C}^{3},{\underline{0}})}}{\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x},f,\frac{\partial f}{\partial z},\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right\rangle}=\tau_{\underline{0}}(V)

and, by applying the Gómez-Mont formula (
3
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))=\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{(\mathbb{C}^{3},{\underline{0}})}}{\left\langle v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\right\rangle}=18.

Note that
\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))=18>1+\tau_{{\underline{0}}}(V)
, confirming the gap expected for non-weighted homogeneous germs.

Furthermore, we define an open family of tangent vector fields
\widetilde{v}_{\lambda}:=\lambda\widetilde{v}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Since
\lambda\neq 0
is a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{(\mathbb{C}^{3},{\underline{0}})}
, the ideals defining the index:

\langle\lambda v_{1},\lambda v_{2},\lambda v_{3}\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\left\langle\frac{df}{f}(\lambda\widetilde{v}),\lambda v_{1},\lambda v_{2},\lambda v_{3}\right\rangle

are independent of the parameter
\lambda
. Consequently, the GSV index is constant along this family:

\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(\lambda v,(V,{\underline{0}}))=\mathrm{Ind}_{\rm GSV}(v,(V,{\underline{0}}))=18,\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}.

5.
Global Consequences of the Local Theory

Let
V
be a compact complex hypersurface with isolated singularities
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}
in a complex manifold
X
. For each singular point
p_{i}
, we denote by
K_{p_{i}}
the smallest index
{\rm Ind}_{{\rm GSV}}(v,(V,p_{i}))
, for every germ
v
of holomorphic vector field on
(V,p_{i})
. The sum
\sum K:=\sum_{i=1,\ldots,s}K_{p_{i}}
determines an obstruction for the existence of global holomorphic vector fields on
V
:

Theorem 3
.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
be a complex projective hypersurface with isolated singularities. Suppose that
V
admits a global holomorphic vector field with isolated singularities, singular at each singularity of
V
(and possibly at some other isolated points in the smooth part of
V
). Then the following inequality holds:

\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}\left[1-(1-{\rm deg}(V))^{l}\right]\geq\sum K.

Proof.
Let
V
have degree
k
and singular set
{\rm Sing}(V)=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\}
. Suppose
v
is a global holomorphic vector field on
V
such that its singular set
\text{Sing}(v)
consists of the points
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{s}\}
and (possibly) a finite set of points
\{q_{1},\dots,q_{l}\}
in the smooth locus
V_{reg}
. Then, the sum of the local GSV indices at
p_{i}
and the Poincaré-Hopf indices at
q_{j}
is determined by the
n
-th Chern class of the virtual tangent bundle (cf.
[
2
, Theorem 5.6.3]
) ):

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\text{Ind}_{\text{GSV}}(v,(V,p_{i}))+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\text{Ind}_{\text{PH}}(v,q_{j})=\int_{V}c_{n}(T\mathbb{P}^{n+1}-[V]),

where
[V]\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(k)
denotes the line bundle of
V
in
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
.

Since
c_{1}([V])
is the Poincaré dual of the fundamental class of
V
, we can compute the
n
-th Chern class as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{V}c_{n}(T\mathbb{P}^{n+1}-[V])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}c_{n}(T\mathbb{P}^{n+1}-[V])c_{1}([V])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{n}c_{n-l}(T\mathbb{P}^{n+1})c_{1}([V]^{\ast})^{l}\right)c_{1}([V])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}c_{n-l}(T\mathbb{P}^{n+1})(-1)^{l}c_{1}([V])^{l+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n+2}{n-l}(-1)^{l}(k)^{l+1}\int_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(1))^{n+1}

where in the last step we have used the fact that

\displaystyle c_{n-l}(T\mathbb{P}^{n+1})=\binom{n+2}{n-l}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(1))^{n-l}

and

c_{1}([V])=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(k))=kc_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(1).

Hence, since
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(1))^{n+1}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{V}c_{n}(T\mathbb{P}^{n+1}-[V])=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n+2}{n-l}(-1)^{l}(k)^{l+1}=\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}(1-(1-k)^{l}).

Thus, we get

\sum K\leq\sum_{i=1}^{s}\text{Ind}_{\text{GSV}}(v,(V,p_{i}))+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\text{Ind}_{\text{PH}}(v,q_{j})=\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}(1-(1-k)^{l}).

\square

Corollary 1
.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
be a complex projective hypersurface with isolated singularities, where
n
is odd. Suppose that
V
admits a global holomorphic vector field with isolated singularity, singular at each singularity of
V
(and possibly at some other isolated points in the smooth part of
V
). Then the following inequality holds:

(n+1)-\sum_{l=1}^{n}(1-{\rm deg}(V))^{l}\geq\#{\rm Sing}(V).

Proof.
Suppose
v
is a global holomorphic vector field on
V
with isolated singularities. By using item (i) of Theorem 1, we obtain

\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-11.38092pt\text{Ind}_{\text{GSV}}(v,(V,p))\geq\#{\rm Sing}(V)\,\,-\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-10.21434pt\tau_{p}(V)\geq\#{\rm Sing}(V)\,\,-\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-10.21434pt\mu_{p}(V),

consequently, it follows from the Theorem 4 that

\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}(1-(1-{\rm deg}(V))^{l})\geq\#{\rm Sing}(V)\,\,-\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-10.21434pt\mu_{p}(V)

or, equivalently,

(n+1)-\sum_{l=1}^{n}(1-{\rm deg}(V))^{l}+\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-10.21434pt\mu_{p}(V)-(1-{\rm deg}(V))^{n+1}\geq\#{\rm Sing}(V).

Since (see
[
8
]
)

\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-10.21434pt\mu_{p}(V)-(1-{\rm deg}(V))^{n+1}=-{\rm pol}(V)\leq 0

where
{\rm pol}(V)
denotes the polar degree of
V
, we obtain the desired inequality

(n+1)-\sum_{l=1}^{n}(1-{\rm deg}(V))^{l}\geq\#{\rm Sing}(V).

\square

6.1.
Singular curves

We know that the classification of non-singular compact curves is governed entirely by the geometric genus
g
, partitioning them into three main classes: rational (
g=0
), elliptic (
g=1
), and general type (
g\geq 2
).

In the singular case,
as an application of the preceding results, we obtain the following complete topological classification:

Theorem 4
.

Let
V
be an irreducible singular complex curve in a compact complex surface. If
V
admits a non-trivial global holomorphic vector field with zeros, then
V
is a rational curve, with at most two singular points.

Proof.
Suppose that
V
admits a global holomorphic vector field
v
, with isolated singularities. According to
[
2
, Theorem 3.2.2]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-8.5359pt{\rm Ind}_{{\rm GSV}}(v,(V,p))+\sum_{q\in{\rm Sing}(v)\cap V_{reg}}\hskip-19.91684pt{\rm Ind}_{{\rm PH}}(v,q)=\chi(V)-\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-8.5359pt\mu_{p}(V),

where
{\rm Ind}_{{\rm PH}}(v,q)
denotes the classical Poincaré-Hopf index of
v
at
q
. Since each
{\rm Ind}_{{\rm PH}}(v,q)
a positive number, we obtain

(13)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\ \sum K\leq\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}{\rm Ind}_{{\rm GSV}}(v,(V,p))\leq\chi(V)-\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\mu_{p}(V).

By using item (i) of Theorema 1, we get

(14)

\displaystyle\#{\rm Sing}(V)\,\,-\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-10.21434pt\mu_{p}(V)\leq\#{\rm Sing}(V)\,\,-\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}\hskip-10.21434pt\tau_{p}(V)\leq\sum K,

and relations (
13
) and (
14
) tell us that

\#{\rm Sing}(V)\leq\chi(V).

On the other hand, we know that

\chi(V)=2-2g-\displaystyle\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}(r_{p}-1),

where
r_{p}
denotes the number of analytic branches (local irreducible components) of the germ
(V,p)
. Thus, we obtain

1\leq\#{\rm Sing}(V)\leq\#{\rm Sing}(V)+\displaystyle\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(V)}(r_{p}-1)\leq 2-2g,

which implies that
g=0
and
\#{\rm Sing}(V)\leq 2

\square

6.2.
Surfaces in Kähler 3-folds

We now let
V
be a compact hypersurface with isolated singularities in a compact non-singular 3-fold
X
.

Since
V
is a normal compact complex surface, the Euler characteristic of its structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{V}
can be written in terms of its classical analytic invariants, namely the irregularity
q(V)=\mathrm{dim}H^{1}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V})
and the geometric genus
g(V)=\mathrm{dim}H^{2}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V})
:

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{V})=1-q(V)+g(V).

On the other hand, applying the Baum-Fulton-MacPherson Riemann-Roch Theorem
[
1
]
to the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{V}
, we have:

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{V})=\int_{V}\text{td}_{2}(TX-[V])=\frac{1}{12}\left(\mathcal{K}_{vir}^{2}+\int_{V}c_{2}(TX-[V])\right),

where
\text{td}_{2}
denotes the degree 2 component of the Todd class and
\mathcal{K}_{vir}
is the virtual canonical class. Combining these equations, we have the following formula:

(15)

\int_{V}c_{2}(TX-[V])=12(1-q(V)+g(V))-\mathcal{K}_{vir}^{2},

which will be used in the proof of the following theorem. We assume
X
is a compact Kähler 3-fold and
V\subset X
a hypersurface such that the adjoint line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+V)
is nef in the analytic sense.

Theorem 5
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler 3-fold and let
V\subset X
be a hypersurface with isolated singularities. Assume that the adjoint line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+[V])
is nef on
X
. If
V
admits a non-trivial global holomorphic vector field with zeros, then

g(V)\geq q(V).

Proof.
Suppose that
V
admits a global holomorphic vector field
v
with isolated singularities. According to
[
18
, Ch. IV, Corollary 3.8]
,

\sum_{p\in\mathrm{Sing}(V)\cup\mathrm{Sing}(v)}\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{GSV}}(v,(V,p))=\int_{V}c_{2}(TX-[V]).

Since
\mathrm{dim}(V)
is even, each index
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{GSV}}(v,(V,p))
is a positive integer (cf.
[
13
, Theorem 2]
). Consequently, we have

0<\int_{V}c_{2}(TX-[V])=12(1-q(V)+g(V))-\mathcal{K}_{vir}^{2}.

Hence, we obtain

\frac{1}{12}\mathcal{K}_{vir}^{2}<1-q(V)+g(V).

By hypothesis, the adjoint bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+[V])
is nef on
X
, which guarantees that
\mathcal{K}_{vir}^{2}\geq 0
. Therefore, it follows that

0\leq\frac{1}{12}\mathcal{K}_{vir}^{2}<1-q(V)+g(V).

Since
q(V)
and
g(V)
are integers, the strict inequality
0<1-q(V)+g(V)
implies that
g(V)-q(V)\geq 0
. This concludes the proof.
\square

Remark 6.2
.

Inequality
g(V)\geq q(V)
is not a general topological property of compact surfaces (for instance, ruled surfaces over curves of genus
h\geq 1
satisfy
q>g
). Theorem 5 provides a strong geometric obstruction: the existence of a holomorphic vector field with isolated singularities on
V
, bounded by a nef adjoint ambient condition, structurally forbids the hypersurface from having excess irregularity.
