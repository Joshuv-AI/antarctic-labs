TriAttnNet based deep learning model for automated cotton pest detection and disease classification
Authors: Mohan Ajmeera, P Chiranjeevi, A Krishna Mohan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a deep learning model to detect cotton plant pests and classify diseases, which must overcome limited datasets, class imbalance, and feature redundancy. At the preprocessing phase, the Gaussian blur filtering and Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) are used to sharpen images by improving their clarity and contrast. To increase and diversify the data, Spa-GAN-based data augmentation is used to produce realistic synthetic samples. To obtain an accurate Region of Interest (RoI), an Attention-Guided Multi-Scale Residual U-Net (AGMS-U-Net) is considered to segment local and global structural information. The proposed framework makes three major contributions: (i) a new attention-based feature extractor, TriAttnNet, that incorporates spatial, channel, and contextual attention to represent diseases on a fine-grained level (ii) a new optimization strategy, Hybrid Mongoose Ray Chaotic Optimization (HMRCO), which includes chaotic strategies to better tune the parameters and explore the feature space and (iii) classification layer with focal loss for final decision. Experimental analyses prove that the suggested method is much more effective than the current state-of-the-art models, providing a powerful and understandable solution to precision agriculture and sustainable cotton crop health monitoring. Experimental results show that TriAttnNet achieves 98.66% accuracy, 98.71% recall, and 98.81% F1-score, which is better than the state-of-the-art algorithms, such as EfficientNetB1-CBAM (96.38%) and BERT-ResNet-PSO (95.69%). The proposed system is computationally feasible and interpretable, and it is interpretable to provide a practical solution to precision agriculture and sustainable monitoring of the health of cotton crops.Keywords: Spatial generative adversarial networks, Hybrid mongoose ray chaotic optimization, Cotton plant pest identification and categorization, TriAttnNetSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Cotton is one of the most commercially important crops in natural fibres industry, and the crop yield is often diminished by insect assaults and leaf diseases that require timely detection1,2. The ability to monitor pest situations, especially in the early stages, is crucial to ensuring cotton growth and quality3–5. Initial machine learning research like the classification of cotton disease using TensorFlow6 showed feasibility, but there were scalability concerns7,8. Later work includes ensemble deep learning techniques which increased robustness in the field of smart agriculture9 and incremental detection techniques in the field of aerial remote sensing combined with blockchain for large scale monitoring10,11.

This is highly beneficial for farmers in choosing biological control techniques or efficient chemicals to stop the spread of pests. Farmers find it challenging and time-consuming to classify appropriately by hand. Crop disease is thought to be identified by the spots on the leaf, which are regarded as the primary indicator of disease presence12–14. Nectar is held in place by a tiny, cup-shaped structure found under each leaf on the cotton plant. Many bugs are drawn to the plant because of these deposits and its wet stems15.

In recent years, many scholars have created a variety of computational models based on vision in order to address these issues in the field of agriculture16–18. These models cover many different technologies, including Deep learning (DL), Transfer learning (TL), Convolutional neural networks (CNN), and machine learning (ML)19. Typically, specialists use their naked eyes to assess the symptoms. However, in the case of large farms, this required constant professional monitoring, which is costly20,21. However, because farmers rely on their own experience to diagnose symptoms, a faulty diagnosis might result in erroneous management measures, including applying pesticides excessively or at the wrong time. The automatic identification of plant illnesses is a crucial area of study because it might be useful for tracking large agricultural fields and identifying disease signs as soon as they manifest on plant leaves22,23. Introduced an improved abnormality segmentation method for automatically recognize crop diseases by heuristic concatenated deep learning model24,25. Installed U Net + + architecture for segmenting the leaf diseases, improving the accuracy of the diseased area26. It is essential to create a quick, automated, low-cost, and accurate method for identifying plant diseases. The high intra class variability, however, still remains a challenge for clustering methods, the optimization algorithms may end up converging to a local minimum, and feature selection strategies are still limited on how to cope with redundant features. The gaps indicate the need of an inte-grated approach that involves clustering, optimization, and feature selection to obtain stable classification of cotton disease.

This research proposes a comprehensive TriAttnNet-HMRCO framework for automated cotton pest detection and disease classification. The AGMS-U-Net module enhances lesion localization by accurately segmenting disease-affected regions and reducing background noise, enabling more precise feature learning. Spa-GAN augmentation improves data diversity by generating realistic synthetic samples, which helps mitigate data scarcity and class imbalance. The TriAttnNet module effectively integrates spatial, channel, and contextual attention mechanisms to capture fine-grained disease characteristics and improve discriminative feature representation. In addition, the HMRCO optimization strategy enhances parameter tuning and feature selection through hybrid chaotic and swarm-based optimization, improving convergence stability and preventing local minima. Related work

In 2025, L.K. Dhruw et al.27 developed convolutional neural network (CNN)- based model, a Vision Transformer (ViT) models for cotton disease and pest classification using a dataset of eight classes, inte-grating local feature extraction and global context capture. The hybrid model performed best, but was constrained by dataset size and generalization to field variations.

In 2025, Zhao et al.28 have developed a multi-task image captioning framework (CottonCapT6) based on Cross Vision Transformer (CrossViT) and Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer (T5) for cotton disease and pest diagnosis. The model resulted in detailed caption and high performance of Consensus-based Image Captioning Evaluation (CIDEr) which enhanced the semantic understanding. It was however, restricted to other crops and varied field conditions and further studies were needed in actual application conditions.

In 2025, M.A. Rahman et al.29 developed a deep learning system combining the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) with several models including EfficientNet and DenseNet was developed for cotton leaf disease detection using an augmented dataset. This strategy enabled good accuracy and feature focus, but achieved this under the condition of using a large number of dataset augmentations which may need to be tested in real field conditions.

In 2025, A. Aslam et al.30 proposed a multi-convolutional neural network (MCNN) framework that combines the features of MobileNet and VGG16 and uses ensemble classification for cotton disease detection in seven classes. Synthetic data generation was used, and accuracy was high; however, there was a lack of real-world generalization and robustness because of the use of synthetic and augmented data sets.

In 2026, M.E. Haque et al.31 presented a CNN-ensemble architecture with attention mechanism, CottonLeafNet, to classify cotton leaf disease based on multiple datasets and lightweight CNN backbones. The model achieved state-of-the-art accuracy with fast inference and interpretability via Grad-CAM; however, its performance still showed moderate drop in cross-dataset generalization.

In 2024, K. Qiu et al.32 presented SpemNet-Cotton Disease and Pest Identification model using Efficient Multi-Scale Attention and Stacking Patch Embedding to improve feature learning and multi-scale representation. The approach enhanced the classification accuracy of various datasets, but may be unsuitable for lightweight deployment in edge devices due to its complexity and the use of designed modules.

In 2024, Gao et al.33 developed an intelligent cotton pest and disease detection framework based on Transformer technology and knowledge graph to improve real-time agricultural di-agnosis, which is based on edge computing. Although this method enhances feature recognition and facilitates efficient mobile inference, the effectiveness of the approach relies on the diversity of datasets and needs further optimization for its wider real-world deployment.

In 2021, M. Zekiwos and A. Bruck34 developed CNN-based deep learning model for cotton leaf disease and pest diagnosis using K-fold cross-validation to improve generalization across classes. The ap-proach achieved good automated classification performance, but with a comparatively small da-taset and with limited robustness in a variety of real field conditions.

In 2021, R.F. Caldeira et al.35 presented a deep learning method to detect cotton leaf lesions by adopting CNN based architectures like GoogLeNet and ResNet50 for field image analysis. While it improved automated disease screening, the method was limited by the early stage symptom ambiguities and lower robustness in complex real world agriculture environments.

In 2023, N. Zhang et al.36 developed a hyperspectral image-based severity classification method for cotton verticillium wilt (VV) disease severity based on optimized support vector machine (SVM) classification models using evolutionary algorithms, including grey wolf optimizer (GWO). The method was able to classify the severity of the disease and to detect it early, but it required spectral preprocessing and was limited in scale, making it impractical to be used in the field.

In 2022, A.I. Jajja et al.37 discussed a Compact Convolutional Transformer (CCT) model for de-tecting whitefly attack on cotton crop based on newly constructed image dataset. The combination of convolutional and transformer features showed better classification performance, but did not perform well in other datasets and was not generalizable to larger pest and field situations.

In 2021, X. Liang38 suggested a framework of few-shot cotton leaf spot classification based on metric learn-ing by using Siamese style CNN and KNN based decision space to enhance the recognition of cotton leaf spot under limited samples. This method improved the feature similarity learning process for disease detection but faced some challenges such as smaller datasets and dependency on the segmentation quality in real-world scenarios.

In 2022, Memon, et al.39, offered a meta deep learning based scheme for cotton leaf disease identification based on CNN-based architectures and transfer learning and data augmentation. The method proved to enhance the generalization ability across several disease classes but relied on variability augmentation in the datasets and limited field variability representation.

In 2025, Z. Wang et al.40 developed a light-weight cotton disease and pest classification model, RF-Cott-Net, using MobileViTv2 with early exit and quantization-aware training. The approach has the characteristics of high accuracy, low computation and efficient edge deployment. However, it was only evaluated on one dataset, which would limit its generalizability in the field under various conditions.

In 2024, P. Bishshash et al.41, an extensive database of cotton leaf disease samples taken under a wide range of field conditions and utilized for training deep learning models to classify diseases and pests. The work enabled better automated detection and model benchmarking and was used under conditions that were specific to the datasets, but it was not general enough for the real world.

Problem statement

In recent years, deep learning techniques for cotton disease and pest detection using convo-lutional architectures has been studied. CNN-based models (such as ResNet, GoogLeNet, and MobileNet) can achieve good performance on the local feature extraction task but they lack the ability to learn long-range dependence and global contextual information. In small agricultural datasets, Vision Transformer (ViT) and CrossViT have problems due to the high computational expense and large datasets needed to achieve global and multi-scale representation. CBAM is better for feature fo-cus, but relies on CNN backbones and do not have deeper context reasoning. EfficientNet is a computation-efficient model, but suffers in the presence of noise in the field, and lacks explicit lesion localization. While LSTM is better suited for spatial image learning, KNN does not support representation learning or scalability and CCT is efficient, but still has difficulty in representing fine-grained diseases and domain variability. Furthermore, new advances in agricultural vision have also brought a focus to the creation of large-scale benchmark datasets like42 that focus on real-world variability, occlusion, and complex field conditions, making it increasingly clear that a well-designed dataset can help with better model generalization. In general, existing methods have several common drawbacks, including a lack of modeling the multi-level spatial–contextual dependency, a heavy dependence on a large and augmented dataset, a lack of robustness to real-world ag-ricultural variability, and the absence of integrated frameworks that combine feature extraction, attention learning and classification, which motivate the development of more integrated and attention-oriented architectures for accurate cotton pest and disease classification.

Proposed methodology

The proposed TriAttnNet presents an integrated approach for cotton plant pest detection and disease classification in agriculture. By combining DL techniques with traditional image processing methods, this methodology addresses challenges such as limited data availability, model robustness, and interpretability. It begins with the pre-processing techniques such as Gaussian blur filtering, Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE), and generation of synthetic training samples through Spatial Generative Adversarial Networks (Spa-GAN) and employs. Proposed TriAttnNet, an attention-driven feature extraction model combining spatial, channel, and contextual attention for fine-grained symptom detection. Introduced HMRCO, a hybrid optimization algorithm that enhances parameter tuning, convergence stability, and robust feature selection. Developed TriAttnNet, a tri-branch classification network integrating ResNet-152 & InceptionV3, and Classification with focal loss to boost accuracy and handle data imbalance. Data collection

The dataset of this study is taken from the publicly available Kaggle repository, Cotton Plant Disease Dataset43. The data is made up of high-resolution images of the leaves of cotton plants that have been classified into various categories of healthy and diseased samples. In particular, it comprises five significant diseases of cotton plants, such as Aphids, Armyworm, Bacterial Blight, Powdery Mildew, and Target Spot, and a healthy leaf category. These classifications encompass a broad spectrum of the biotic stress conditions that are very pertinent to cotton farming and pest control.

The dataset is a wide range of samples that are required to train and validate DL models in pest detection and disease classification. Nevertheless, because of the nature of the limitations, including the unequal distribution of classes, the variability of the illumination, and the small size of the sample, additional preprocessing and augmentation measures are utilized to enhance the quality and diversity of the data to be used to train a robust model. Preprocessing

Preprocessing is used to remove noise and enhance the quality of the image by applying a Gaussian blur filter and CLAHE to remove noise and enhance the contrast, respectively. The preprocessing pipeline followed a denoise-then-enhance strategy which eliminates the undesirable background noise, asymmetrical lighting, and brings out the disease-related areas on the cotton leaf, including pest spots, blight spots, and mildew spots.

Gaussian blur filtering

Gaussian blur filtering44 is an image smoothing method that is very popular for eliminating the high-frequency noise without altering the necessary structures of the image. In order to address the challenges of low visibility and unequal lighting in the images of cotton leaves, CLAHE45 is used following the Gaussian blur filtering. In contrast to the global histogram equalization, which can over-enhance noise, CLAHE operates on small contextual blocks (tiles) and uses a contrast limiting factor to avoid enhancement of irrelevant details. Data augmentation using spatial generative adversarial networks (Spa-GAN)

Despite preprocessing, the dataset still suffers from a limited sample size and class imbalance, which leads to model overfitting and reduced generalization. To address this, Spa-GAN46 is applied for data augmentation. Unlike conventional GANs, Spa-GAN integrates a spatial attention mechanism that ensures the generator focuses on disease-relevant regions of cotton leaves (blight lesions, pest infection spots, mildew patches) while generating synthetic images. This leads to higher-quality augmented data with enhanced variability and structural consistency.

Segmentation

AGMS U-Net and TriAttnNet are applied for different purposes in the proposed pipeline. AGMS U-Net has conducted a process of segmentation, which isolates areas of cotton leaves that are pest and disease-infected to allow proper localization. After identifying these regions, TriAttnNet serves as the supporting element of feature extraction and classification, whereby spatial, channel, and contextual attention are used to take in the fine-grained disease features and identify the disease type. Convolutional U-Net networks typically become blurred, and thus fail to address the multi-scale context; hence, the introduced AGMS U-Net advances detection of the lesions by controlling the local, the global, and attention-generating refinement. Simulation results

Simulation setup

The TriAttnNet model is realized through Python language on TensorFlow and PyTorch frameworks and is executed on 64-bit operating system with an Intel(r) Core i7 processor, 4.2 GHz and 32 GB RAM. The experiments are performed using an NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU (12 GB) to achieve efficient training and inference. The Cotton Plant Disease dataset from Kaggle is split into training (70%), validation (15%) and testing (15%) sets and the same data partitioning is used for all comparative models to provide an fair evaluation. All baseline methods, including RF-CottonNet40, YOLOv9c47, BERT-ResNet-PSO48, EfficientNetB1-CBAM29, Ensemble Method49, CNN50 and 2D/3D deep learning models51 are tested under the same experimental conditions with the same dataset and evaluation protocol. Multiple metrics, including Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F-Score, Sensitivity, Specificity, Negative Predictive Value (NPV), Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), False Positive Rate (FPR), and False Negative Rate (FNR) are used to conduct a comprehensive and unbiased comparison of model performance. All models are trained and evaluated in their recommended or standard configuration, so as to provide a fair and unbiased comparison.

Discussion

The fused representation, , is a discriminative feature space that is balanced between depth and diversity. Gaussian blur filtering and CLAHE are used to improve the quality of the image, minimize noise, and enhance the contrast of the image, allowing for the detection of small patterns and features of disease. In addition, the AGMS-U-Net further enhances the localization process by providing accurate segmentation of the affected regions in the image, which allows the model to focus on the relevant visual cues.TriAttnNet module that combines spatial, channel, and contextual attention mechanisms, is crucial for learning discriminative and robust feature representations. The design has been optimized to get better discrimination among similar-looking disease classes, as it is attention-driven. Furthermore, by focusing on the difficult-to-classify samples, the use of focal loss contributes to reducing class imbalance and thus enhances stability and performance for all evaluation metrics.The experimental results show that the proposed model can effectively achieve fine-grained classes in agricultural image classification and is superior to the existing state-of-the-art models in most cases. Although the model has performed well, it may still make errors in the following scenarios: severe occlusion, early-stage disease with subtle visual symptoms, and feature discrimination.

Conclusion

The proposed framework is an effective deep learning based pipeline for cotton pest detection and classification. The model incorporates Gaussian blur filtering and CLAHE image enhancement, as well as Spa-GAN for enhancing the diversity of the dataset. The enables Accurate Segmentation of Disease Regions (AGMS-U-Net) network and the Attention Mechanism (TriAttnNet) provide rich and discriminative feature representations via spatial, channel, and contextual attention for accurate segmentation of disease regions. In addition, the HMRCO optimization strategy further enhances the performance of the model, and focal loss effectively addresses the problem of class imbalance. Experimental results show that the proposed method is superior to the state-of-the-art methods in terms of various evaluation metrics, and it is effective for fine-grained agricultural image classification. But problems like occlusion, low-resolution images and domain variations can still pose a problem when put in practice. Future research will be conducted on domain adaptation, lightweight architectures, and real field datasets to enhance robustness and facilitate practical implementation in precision agriculture systems.
