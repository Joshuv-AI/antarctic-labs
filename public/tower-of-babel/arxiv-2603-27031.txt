The Lee-Yang model and its generalizations through the lens of long-range deformations
Fanny Eustachon

Abstract
In two dimensions, the non-unitary class of conformal minimal models, ℳ ⁡ ( 2 , 2 ​ m + 1 ) \mathcal{M}(2,2m+1) , has been recently conjectured to arise as renormalization-group fixed points of scalar field theories with complex i ​ φ 2 ​ m − 1 i\varphi^{2m-1} interaction, m ∈ ℕ m\in\mathbb{N} , m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 . We test a variation of this conjecture through the perturbative study of two separate long-range constructions based on respectively the minimal model and its potential Landau-Ginzburg formalism. For m > 2 m>2 , inconsistencies are found when subsequently relating both constructions. In contrast, the long-range Lee-Yang model, the m = 2 m=2 case, is shown to be analogue to the long-range Ising model.

Introduction

In two dimensions, using the power of representation theory, a classification of local conformal field theories (CFTs) has been determined, within which minimal models
\mathcal{M}(p,q)

In a quantum field theory (QFT) setting, while the enhancement of scale invariance to conformal invariance lacks a general proof, the converse holds. CFTs are intrinsically scale invariant by nature, and are expected to lie on infrared fixed points of the renormalization group flow. The corresponding UV theory is called a Landau-Ginzburg description.
