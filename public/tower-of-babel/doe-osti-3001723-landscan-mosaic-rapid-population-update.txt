 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
During a natural disaster such as Hurricane Melissa, understanding where people are located is critical
for situational awareness, operational planning and humanitarian support and consequence assessment.
Traditional population datasets focus on mapping populations based on residential, or "business-as-usual"
scenarios. However, natural disasters can create disruptions in daily routines of population in addition to
the magnitude of the population displacement, depending on the type, duration, context, and location of the
event.
The Geospatial Science and Human Security Division at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) pro-
duced this latest LandScan Mosaic Rapid Population Update for Jamaica following Hurricane Melissa, a
category 5 hurricane that made landfall on Jamaica on October 28 2025. This Rapid Population Update
captures the immediate population displacement following the hurricane using a combination of open-
source building damage assessment data from Microsoft, flood exposure data from the Global Flood Mon-
itoring service, reported population displacement information, and humanitarian shelter locations from the
Jamaican Office of Disaster Preparedness and Emergency Management and the underlying LandScan Mo-
saic Jamaica as a base population.
Based on analysis of open-source data, we find that of nearly 450,000 buildings in western Jamaica, more
than 11% have experienced moderate or greater damage or flooding. As of November 4, 2025, reports in-
dicated 19 deaths and approximately 30,000 displaced individuals, the majority of whom were located at
humanitarian shelters. Western Jamaica was the most heavily affected region, and we estimate the current
population across the five westernmost parishes (Hanover, Saint Elizabeth, Saint James, Trelawny, and
Westmoreland) to be 651,811. Saint Elizabeth parish experienced the largest decline from the pre-event
baseline, with an estimated population reduction exceeding 10,000 people.

 2. LANDSCAN MOSAIC METHODOLOGY
This Rapid Update begins with baseline building-level population estimates from LandScan Mosaic, which
incorporates building area, floor counts, and use type to provide a spatially detailed population distribution[11].
These baseline populations are then adjusted using building damage estimates, flood exposure, active shel-
ter locations, and reported population displacement to estimate current population following Hurricane
Melissa. This Rapid Population Update Framework is flexible, allowing for application in a wide range of
crisis situations, including conflict and natural disasters, and provides timely, actionable information for
emergency response and resource allocation [13].

2.1 BUILDING DAMAGE
Building-level damage was derived from two post-event building footprint snapshots from the Microsoft
Hurricane Melissa Building Damage Assessment in Jamaica[7], based on imagery collected on October
31. We merged the datasets by retaining all buildings from the larger set and supplementing with non-
overlapping buildings from the smaller set, covering both southern and northern affected areas. Microsoft
provided damage values representing the proportion of each structure affected. These estimates were in-
tegrated into a complete building footprint dataset covering Jamaica to assign damage values to all struc-
tures.

2.2 FLOOD EXPOSURE
Flood exposure from the GloFAS Global Flood Monitoring (GFM) program[2] was used to generate a
flood exposure score that combines binary SAR-based flood classifications with prediction likelihood and
exclusion mask datasets to account for potential detection errors across Jamaica. GFM data from October
29–30 and November 2–3 were used, though coverage was incomplete on some dates. We assigned each
building the maximum flood exposure observed across all dates, integrating these values into the complete
building footprint dataset for population modeling.

2.3 SHELTERS AND DISPLACEMENT
Active humanitarian shelters [8] were geocoded and buffered to create catchment areas. In the five western
parishes most affected by Hurricane Melissa (Hanover, Westmoreland, Saint James, Saint Elizabeth, and
Trelawny), 30,000 displaced people were distributed proportionally to buildings within these catchments.
Buildings outside catchments retained baseline populations unless affected by flood or damage. Shelters in
other parishes were excluded, assuming displaced populations would travel to the nearest western shelter.

2.4 POPULATION MODELING
LandScan Mosaic provides a high-resolution baseline gridded population dataset that estimates human
occupancy at the building level, integrating building footprints, use type, floor count, and occupancy rates
to represent spatial distribution of population in Jamaica prior to the rapid population update; for more
information on LandScan high-resolution gridded population data methodologies, see [11, 13].
Baseline building-level population estimates were adjusted based on building damage, flood exposure, and
shelter allocations. For each building, we used the maximum exposure score derived from either damage
or flood exposure. This approach ensures that buildings with no visible structural damage but that were
completely flooded are properly accounted for, as well as buildings that experienced physical damage
without flooding. The resulting exposure score ranges from 0 to 1, where 1 represents a building that was

 1

either extensively damaged, completely flooded, or both, and 0 represents a building that was undamaged
and not flooded.
Pre-event building populations were adjusted using a down-weighting approach, mapping observed dam-
age rankings to FEMA HAZUS[3] damage states—slight, moderate, extensive, and complete—as shown
in Table 1. Building-level populations were then normalized at both the national and first-level adminis-
trative (Parish) scales, with the five western Parishes grouped together to account for potential population
displacement across administrative boundaries. Finally, the adjusted building-level populations were ag-
gregated and converted into a gridded format suitable for spatial analysis.

The total population used in the LandScan Mosaic Rapid Update for Jamaica is 2,823,713, based on the
CIA World Factbook[1]. The 14 Parishes, along with their associated data, are listed in Table 2 and are
further discussed in Section 4.
We conducted an initial analysis of building-level exposure to damage, flooding, and the combination of
both across Jamaica, summarized in Table 3. Moderate to complete damage or flooding affected approx-
imately 4% of all buildings nationwide. Impacts were concentrated in the western Parishes, which ac-
counted for nearly one-third of the national building stock. In this region, roughly 11% of the 449,313
buildings experienced at least moderate damage or flooding, with the highest prevalence in the moderate
and extensive categories. These patterns indicate that while most structures remained largely unaffected,
western Jamaica bore a disproportionate share of severe impacts, highlighting regional vulnerability and
potential needs for targeted recovery interventions.

 3. OPEN-SOURCE DATA FOR JAMAICA
3.1 POPULATION DISPLACEMENT
Population reporting during this ongoing crisis remains fluid, with multiple sources providing varying
counts of displaced persons, both inside and outside shelters, and limited information on their parish-level
distribution. Most estimates converge around 30,000 individuals. The ECHO Flash Update (November 3,
2025) reports approximately 30,000 displaced persons [4], consistent with the International Organization
for Migration (IOM) Displacement Tracking Matrix map of shelters and flood-affected areas in western
Jamaica as of October 30, 2025 [5]. Other sources report lower counts, with UNICEF estimating 13,000
persons in shelters [12], and PAHO reporting 7,208 as of October 29, 2025 [9].
For the purposes of this report, we adopt the ECHO estimate of 30,000 displaced persons [4]. Because
these IDP values are not disaggregated by parish, we removed this population from the normalized total of
the five westernmost parishes (Hanover, Westmoreland, Saint James, Saint Elizabeth, and Trelawny) and
then reallocated it to active shelter locations, as detailed in Section 4.3.

3.2 PRE-HURRICANE TOTAL POPULATION
As reported by the CIA World Factbook, there were 2,823,713 people living in Jamaica prior to Hurricane
Melissa in October 2025 [1]. This CIA World Factbook number represents the estimated 2024 population
of the country.

3.3 SHELTER LOCATIONS
Shelter POI data was compiled from two sources. The first being the Office of Disaster Preparedness and
Emergency Management site. This POI dataset gave the location of every relief shelter in the country of
Jamaica for each of its 14 Parishes, and the current status (active, or closed) [8].
The IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix was used to validate active shelter locations for IDP allocation.
As of October 31, 2025, over 520 shelters were active [5]. 3 shows these shelters along with estimated
occupant counts as of October 30, 2025.

3.4 BUILDING DAMAGE
Building Damage estimates were obtained from the Microsoft AI for Good Lab[7]. based on analysis of
Vantor imagery captured on 10/29/25 and was updated again on November 2nd, 2025, this Building Dam-
age Assessment layer only covers the western portion of Jamaica, where Hurricane Melissa had the most
impact. In the resulting dataset, a building is classified as undamaged if its roof remains intact, and as dam-
aged if the model identifies any portion of the structure as affected (i.e., damage percentage greater than
zero).

3.5 FLOOD EXPOSURE
In addition to building damage, Flood Exposure was used to identify buildings that were uninhabitable
or otherwise impacted, ensuring populations were appropriately reallocated. Flood Exposure data from
the Global Flood Monitoring (GFM) Service under the EU Copernicus Emergency Management Service
[2, 10, 6] covered October 29 – November 3, 2025, and included three components: binary flood extent,
classification likelihood (0–100), and an exclusion mask.

We combined the flood extent and confidence values into a single 0–1 exposure score 2. Fully confident
flooded pixels retained their maximum weight, while areas with uncertain classification were partially
weighted to reflect potential flooding. For each building, we used the maximum exposure score across the
Flood Exposure and Building Damage datasets. This approach ensures that buildings not flagged as dam-
aged but exposed to flooding were down-weighted in population allocation, preventing overestimation of
inhabitable population.

3.6 DEATHS AND INJURIES
As of November 3rd, 2025, Jamaica had 19 reported deaths and 96 injuries, according to the European
Commission’s Directorate-General for European Civil Protection and Humanitarian Aid Operations [4].
This number is expected to increase as further casualty reports are released. However, because the figures
are relatively low and not geographically specific, they were not subtracted from the total population re-
ported by the CIA World Factbook for this Rapid Population Update.
