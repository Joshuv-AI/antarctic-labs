A two-step approach to Chow quotients Thanks: Authors partially supported by INdAM–GNSAGA
Gianluca Occhetta, Luis E. Solá Conde

Abstract
. The Chow quotient of a projective variety by the action of a complex torus is known to have a very complicated geometry, even in the case of simple varieties, such as rational homogeneous varieties. In this paper we propose an approach in which the geometry of the Chow quotient is encoded in a projective toric variety and a finite subgroup of its birational automorphisms. We then illustrate how to apply our strategy in the case of some particular rational homogeneous varieties.

Primary 14L30, 14E30; Secondary 14L24, 14M17

1.
Introduction

The construction and study of quotients by algebraic group actions is a central theme in algebraic geometry, with deep connections to birational geometry and the theory of moduli spaces. Among the various notions of quotient, Chow quotients, introduced by Kapranov
[
10
]
, occupy a distinguished position: while they are often difficult to compute explicitly, they tend to encode rich geometric information and admit meaningful modular interpretations in special cases. Classical examples include their relationship with Grothendieck–Knudsen moduli spaces
[
12
]
, as well as with spaces of complete collineations and complete quadrics (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
).

In this paper, we investigate the problem of constructing Chow quotients of smooth projective varieties under the action of algebraic tori. Our approach is motivated by the observation that, despite their global nature, Chow quotients often admit a description in terms of simpler, more combinatorial objects arising from torus-invariant open subsets.

This perspective was explored in our previous work
[
2
]
, where we studied the action of a maximal torus on the complete flag variety of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. In that setting, we showed that, in favorable situations, the computation of the Chow quotient can be naturally divided into two steps. First, one computes certain combinatorial quotients associated with torus-invariant open subsets. Second, one analyzes the birational relations among these quotients in order to reconstruct the global geometry. In the case of rational homogeneous varieties endowed with the action of a maximal torus in their automorphism group, these birational relations are governed by the action of the corresponding Weyl group.

The main goal of the present paper is to identify general conditions under which this strategy applies. More precisely, we establish a set of hypotheses ensuring that the Chow quotient can be reconstructed as an inverse limit of combinatorial quotients associated with torus-invariant open subsets. This provides a conceptual and effective framework for computing Chow quotients in a relevant class of examples, namely those in which fixed points are isolated and their weights with respect to a given linearization on an ample line bundle are extremal; following
[
3
]
, we say that the action is fully definite on every fixed point:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety, endowed with the action of an algebraic torus
H
, fully definite on every fixed point. Then, for every fixed point
x\in X
there exists an
H
-equivariant isomorphism of
T_{X,x}
with an open affine neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
in
X
, whose combinatorial quotients
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
are projective. Moreover there exists a natural morphism from the Chow quotient of
X
to
\prod_{x\in X^{H}}\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
, which is an isomorphism to the normalization of its image.

The hypotheses of the Theorem are satisfied in the case of rational homogeneous varieties endowed with the action of a maximal torus, but also in the case of some spherical varieties (with the action of appropriate tori). As an application of this result, we consider the case of
\mathbb{P}(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
, quadrics and Grassmannians. .
In particular, we recover the Chow quotients of Grassmannians of lines –originally computed by Kapranov
[
10
]
– from our perspective, obtaining a streamlined and conceptually transparent derivation.

Outline:

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the necessary background on Chow quotients and torus actions. Section
3
contains the proof of our main statement relating Chow and combinatorial quotients of torus invariant neighborhoods of points in which the action is fully definite. Finally, in Sections
4
and
5
we present the explicit computation of the Chow quotient in the cases of Grassmannians and quadrics.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we will work over the field of complex numbers. For a free abelian group
M
, we denote by
M_{\mathbb{R}}
the associated real vector space. When dealing with rational homogeneous varieties, that is, projective quotients of semisimple algebraic groups, we will freely use the marked Dynkin diagram notation, as presented, for instance, in
[
16
, Section 3.1]
.

2.1.
Torus actions and quotients

We recall basic facts on algebraic torus actions; see
[
5
,
15
]
for details. Let
H
be an algebraic torus of rank
r
acting nontrivially on a smooth projective variety
X
, and denote by
\Mo(H)
its character lattice. The fixed locus decomposes as
X^{H}=\bigsqcup_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}}Y
, where each
Y
is smooth and irreducible.

2.1.1.
Linearizations and weight polytopes

Let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
. A
linearization of

L
is an
H
-action on
L
compatible with the projection
L\to X
. Such linearizations always exist and differ by a character of
H
. For
Y\subset X^{H}
, the weight of the
H
-action on
L
is constant along
Y
; we denote it by
\mu_{L}(Y)\in\Mo(H)
.

The
weight polytope of
(X,L)
is the convex hull

\Delta:=\Conv\{\mu_{L}(Y)\mid Y\in\mathcal{Y}\}\subset\Mo(H)_{\mathbb{R}}.

The induced action on sections yields a decomposition

H^{0}(X,L)=\bigoplus_{\tau\in\widetilde{\Gamma}}H^{0}(X,L)_{\tau},

where
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is the set of occurring weights. The
polytope of sections

\Gamma
is the convex hull of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
. If
L
is base point free, then
\Gamma=\Delta
(cf.
[
5
, Lemma 2.4]
).

2.1.2.
GIT quotients

For
u\in\Gamma
, define the graded algebra

\mathcal{A}_{u}:=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 0\\
mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\end{subarray}}H^{0}(X,mL)_{mu},

which is finitely generated. The associated projective variety

\mathcal{G}\mkern-1.0muX_{u}:=\Proj\mathcal{A}_{u}

is the
GIT quotient of
(X,L)
corresponding to the linearization determined by
u
.

2.1.3.
Chow quotients

Let
H
act on
X
. There exists an
H
-invariant open subset
U\subset X
admitting a geometric quotient
\mathcal{G}U
, whose points correspond to closures of orbits with fixed dimension and homology class. This induces an embedding
\mathcal{G}U\hookrightarrow\Chow(X)
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
10
]

The
Chow quotient
of
X
by
H
is the closure of this image in
\Chow(X)
. Its normalization is denoted by
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX
. The universal family induces morphisms
p:\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX
and
q:\mathcal{U}\to X
.

A key property (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 0.4.3]
) is that
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX
dominates all GIT quotients:

Theorem 2.2
.

For every
v\in\Gamma
there exists a birational morphism
\pi_{v}:\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX\to\mathcal{G}\mkern-1.0muX_{v}
.

2.1.4.
Limit quotients

The GIT quotients
\{\mathcal{G}\mkern-1.0muX_{v}\}_{v\in\Gamma}
form an inverse system via natural morphisms
f_{v,u}:\mathcal{G}\mkern-1.0muX_{v}\to\mathcal{G}\mkern-1.0muX_{u}
whenever
X^{\mathrm{ss}}_{v}\subset X^{\mathrm{ss}}_{u}
. Although the inverse limit may be reducible, there exists a distinguished irreducible component containing
\mathcal{G}U
. Its normalization
\mathcal{L}\!X
is called the
normalized limit quotient
.

It comes with compatible birational morphisms
\chi_{v}:\mathcal{L}\!X\to\mathcal{G}\mkern-1.0muX_{v}
and satisfies the following universal property:

Remark 2.3
.

Any compatible system of birational morphisms to the
\mathcal{G}\mkern-1.0muX_{v}
factors uniquely through
\mathcal{L}\!X
.

In our setting, the Chow and limit quotients coincide (cf.
[
1
, Corollary 2.6]
):

Proposition 2.4
.

If
H
acts on a smooth projective variety
X
, then
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX\simeq\mathcal{L}\!X
.

2.1.5.
Combinatorial quotients

Let
X
be a toric variety with fan
\Sigma
, and let
H\subset T
be a subtorus of the acting torus. The induced projection of lattices
q:\No(T)\to\No(T/H)
defines a fan
q_{*}(\Sigma)
, called
quotient fan of
\Sigma
by
q
, whose cones are obtained by intersecting projected cones. The associated toric variety is the
combinatorial quotient
of
X
by
H
.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
11
]

If
X
is a normal projective toric
T
-variety, endowed with the action of a subtorus
H\subset T
, the Chow quotient of
X
by
H
coincides with the combinatorial quotient.

Construction 2.6
.

In this paper we will be especially interested in the case of combinatorial quotients of affine spaces. In order to do so, we consider a complex torus
T
and a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of its lattice of
1
-parametric subgroups
\No(T)
.
The
\mathbb{Z}
-basis determines a positive orthant
\sigma_{0}\subset\No(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
, which is the fan of an affine toric variety which is an affine space
U
, with a set of affine coordinates determined by the
\mathbb{Z}
-basis.
We then consider the associated homomorphism of character lattices
\pi:\Mo(T)_{\mathbb{R}}\to\Mo(H)_{\mathbb{R}}
; using the dual
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\Mo(T)
, and choosing a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\Mo(H)
, we can write this map as given by a matrix, that we call
weight matrix
, since its columns are the weights of the
H
-action on the coordinates of
U
. A
transposed Gale dual
of this weight matrix represents the associated epimorphism
q:\No(T)_{\mathbb{R}}\to\No(T/H)_{\mathbb{R}}
, and the combinatorial quotient of
U
is defined by the quotient fan
q_{*}(\Sigma(\sigma_{0}))
of the fan of faces of
\sigma_{0}
.

We note here that the determination of the quotient fan may be computationally expensive. However, we expect that, in the case in which
U
is an affine open neighborhood in a rational homogeneous space, one should be able to describe the quotient fan in representation theoretical fashion.

3.
Affine covers

Definition 3.1
.

Let
H
be an algebraic torus acting on a finite dimensional complex vector space
U
. We say that the action is
fully definite
if
U^{H}=\{0\}
and there exists a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\Mo(H)
with respect to which all the weights of the action have nonnegative coordinates. An action of
H
on a projective smooth algebraic variety is said to be
fully definite at

x\in X^{H}
, if it is fully definite on
T_{X,x}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H
be an algebraic torus acting on a finite dimensional complex vector space
U
. Assume that the action is fully definite. Then every GIT quotient of
U
by the action of
H
is projective.

Proof.

Let us start by considering a set of coordinates
(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
in
U
with respect to a basis of
H
-eigenvalues of
U
. Since the action is fully definite, we may identify
H\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
,
\Mo(H)\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
so that we may write, for every
h\in H
:

h(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=(h^{m_{1}}x_{1},\dots,h^{m_{N}}x_{N})\text{, with }m_{i}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

In order to compute the GIT quotients of
U
, we consider it as an affine toric variety with respect to the
N
-dimensional torus

T=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})|\,\,x_{i}\neq 0\mbox{ for every $i$}\}\subset U.

Denoting by
P
the nonnegative orthant in
\Mo(T)=\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, we have that
U=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[P\cap\Mo(T)])
, and, following
[
6
, Definition 2.16, Remark 3.6]
, the GIT quotients of
U
are in bijective correspondence with the chamber decomposition of the image of
P
via the natural map:

\pi:\Mo(T)_{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow\Mo(H)_{\mathbb{R}}\mbox{ (sending the canonical basis element $e_{i}$ to $m_{i}$)}.

This chamber decomposition is given by the intersections of the images via
\pi
of the faces of
P
. Given an element
v\in\pi(P)\cap\Mo(H)
, the corresponding GIT quotient is the
T/H
-toric variety associated to the (a priori non bounded) polytope
\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P
(see
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
). Thus in order to prove that they are projective, it suffices to show that
\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P
is bounded for every
v
.

But if
\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P
were unbounded for some
v
, there would exist
u_{0}\in\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P
, and a vector
u\in\ker(\pi)
such that
u_{0}+ku\in\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P
, for every
k\geq 0
, from which it would follow that
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}u
would be contained in
P\cap\ker(\pi)
. This contradicts that, with respect to a certain set of coordinates, all the
m_{i}
’s have nonnegative coordinates, and no
m_{i}
is equal to zero.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

In the situation of Lemma
3.2
, the combinatorial quotient
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU
by the action of
H
is the inverse limit of the GIT quotients of
U
by the action of
H
, and it is projective.

Proof.

Note that, after some translations of notation, the fact that the combinatorial quotient
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU
is the limit quotient of
U
by
H
follows from
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Finally
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU
is projective because as a toric variety, its associated polytope is the Minkowski sum of the polytopes of its GIT quotients (cf.
[
6
, Definition 2.2]
).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that
the morphisms from
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU
to geometric quotients of
U
are toric contractions.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
H
be an algebraic torus acting on a smooth projective variety
X
, and
x\in X
be a fixed point at which the action is fully definite. Then there exists a unique
H
-invariant open neighborhood
U_{x}\subset X
of
x
such that:

•

there exists an
H
-equivariant isomorphism
U_{x}\simeq T_{X,x}
;

•

U_{x}
is the set of points
y\in X
such that
x\in\overline{Hy}
.

Proof.

The existence of an
H
-invariant open neighborhood
U_{x}\subset X
of
x
which is
H
-equivariantly isomorphic to
T_{X,x}
is guaranteed by
[
3
, Theorem 2.5]
. Since the action is fully definite on
x
, it is then obvious that
0\in T_{X,x}
is contained in the closure of every
H
-orbit in
T_{X,x}
, hence it holds also that
x\in\overline{Hy}
for every
y\in U_{x}
. Finally, every point
y\in X
such that
x\in\overline{Hy}
belongs to
U_{x}
because
U_{x}
is an
H
-invariant neighborhood of
x
.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

In the assumptions of Lemma
3.5
, the second property implies that
U_{x}
is unique, and we call it the
Bruhat neighborhood of
x
. Since
X
is assumed to be smooth, the complement
X\setminus U_{x}
is a finite union of prime Cartier divisors

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
H
be an algebraic torus acting on a smooth projective variety
X
, and
x\in X
be a fixed point at which the action is fully definite. Then there exists an ample divisor
D
on
X
supported on the
H
-horizon of
x
.

Proof.

Consider a downgrading of the action of
H
to a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action with sink
x
, and let
D^{\prime}
be a very ample effective divisor not containing
x
. Then

D^{\prime}_{+}=\lim_{t\to 0}tD^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant effective divisor in the linear system
|D^{\prime}|
not containing
x
. We notice that
D^{\prime}_{+}
is also
H
-invariant, hence
D^{\prime}_{+}\cap U_{x}=\emptyset
. Then, denoting by
D_{1},\dots,D_{k}
the irreducible components of the
H
-horizon of
x
, we may conclude by taking
D=mD^{\prime}_{+}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}D_{i}
for
m>>0
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety, endowed with the action of an algebraic torus
H
. Assume that the action is fully definite at a fixed point
x\in X^{H}
. Then every GIT quotient of
U_{x}
is also a GIT quotient of
X
, and in particular there exists a contraction:

\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}.

Proof.

Let us remark that, since the Picard group of the affine space
U_{x}
is trivial, then a GIT quotient of
U_{x}
is determined by a linearization
v
of the action of
H
on
\mathcal{O}_{U_{x}}
.
As in the proof of Lemma
3.2
we consider the structure of
U_{x}\simeq T_{X,x}
as a toric variety with respect to an open torus
T\subset U_{x}
, and isomorphisms
T\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\dim X}
,
H\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
so that the natural inclusion
H\subset T
induces a homomorphism
\pi:\mathbb{Z}^{\dim(X)}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
given by a matrix whose columns are nonzero and have nonnegative coefficients.
The
T
-action on the polynomial ring
\HH^{0}(U_{x},\mathcal{O}_{U_{x}})
decomposes it as

\HH^{0}(U_{x},\mathcal{O}_{U_{x}})\simeq\bigoplus_{m\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathbb{C}\chi^{m},

where
P
denotes the nonnegative orthant in the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\simeq\Mo(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
A linearization of the action is then determined by an element
v\in\pi(P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, and the corresponding GIT quotient
(\mathcal{G}U_{x})_{v}
is the
T/H
toric variety whose moment polytope is
\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P
. From the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we know that
\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P
is bounded. The unstable points for this linearization are precisely the points
y\in U_{x}
such that
\chi^{m}(y)=0
for every
m\in\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

On the other hand, we consider the ample divisor
D
supported on the
H
-horizon of
x
provided by Lemma
3.7
, and the vector spaces

\HH^{0}(X,rD)=\{f\in\mathbb{C}(X)|\,\,\div(f)+rD\geq 0\},\quad r\geq 0.

We have that:

\begin{array}[]{l}\HH^{0}(X,rD)\subset\HH^{0}(X,(r+1)D),\mbox{ for every }r,\\[4.0pt]
\displaystyle\HH^{0}(U_{x},\mathcal{O}_{U_{x}})=\sum_{r\geq 0}\HH^{0}(X,rD).\end{array}

Then there exists
r>0
such that:

\bigoplus_{m\in\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathbb{C}\chi^{m}\subset\HH^{0}(X,(r-1)D).

The linearization of
v
on
\mathcal{O}_{U_{x}}
extends to a linearization of the
H
-action on the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(rD)
, and the corresponding space of invariant sections is still generated by
\{\chi^{m}|\,\,m\in\pi^{-1}(v)\cap P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
. But, by our choice of
r
, all these sections vanish on the support of
D
, that is, the
H
-horizon of
x
in
X
. It then follows that the set of semistable points with respect to
v
in
U_{x}
and on
(X,D)
is the same, from which it follows that
\mathcal{G}U_{x}
is a GIT quotient of
X
, polarized with the line bundle
D
. This concludes the proof of the first part.

Now, since we have shown that every GIT quotient of
U_{x}
is also a GIT quotient of
X
, it follows that
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX
, which is the limit quotient of
X
, admits a birational, dominant morphism to the limit quotient of
U_{x}
, which is
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
. Since both
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX
and
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
are normal, the map is a contraction.
∎

Note that, by construction, the quotients
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
,
x\in X^{H}
, are birationally equivalent, hence there exists an irreducible subvariety
V\subset\prod_{x\in X^{H}}\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
dominating birationally every
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
, and the induced birational map
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX\to\prod_{x\in X^{H}}\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
factors through the normalization
\overline{V}
of
V
:

\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX\longrightarrow\overline{V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny norm.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}V\subset\prod_{x\in X^{H}}\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}.

Then the proof of Theorem
1.1
is concluded by showing:

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety, endowed with the action of an algebraic torus
H
, fully definite on every fixed point. Then the induced map

\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX\to\overline{V}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX\to\overline{V}
is a birational map among normal varieties, it is enough to show that it is finite. If it contracted a curve
C
, then that curve would be contracted by the morphisms
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX\to\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
, for every
x\in X^{H}
. This would be saying that the cycles parametrized by
C
have the same irreducible components in every
U_{x}
, contradicting that the
U_{x}
’s form a covering of
X
.
∎

The hypotheses of the above Proposition are fulfilled when
X
is a rational homogeneous space, that is the quotient
G/P
of a semisimple algebraic group by a parabolic subgroup by the action of a maximal torus
H\subset P
. In fact, in this case the fixed points of the action are known to be the elements of the form
\sigma B
, where
\sigma
is an element of the Weyl group
W=\No_{G}(H)/H
. The weights of the action –with respect to a linearization of an ample line bundle
L(w)
associated to a weight
w\in\Mo(H)
– are of the form
-\sigma(w)\in\Mo(H)
,
\sigma\in W
. We can then easily see that these values are the vertices of the polytope they generate.

Given an element
\sigma\in W
, multiplication by (a representative in
\No_{G}(H)
of)
\sigma^{-1}
provides an isomorphism
\sigma^{-1}:U_{\sigma B}\to U_{eB}
, that descends to an isomorphism
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{\sigma B}\to\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB}
, which we still denote by

\sigma^{-1}
. Composing this with the birational map
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{\sigma B}
induced by the intersection, we obtain a birational automorphism of
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB}
, that we call the
mutation map associated to
\sigma
; this name is meant to suggest a relation with the concept of cluster algebra, introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky (see
[
8
,
19
,
14
]
), which we intend to explore in the future.

We get a group homomorphism:

\mu:W\to\Bir(\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB}),

that contains all the necessary information to construct
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX
out of
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB}
. Finally, since the variety
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB}
is toric, with respect to a torus
T^{\prime}
of dimension
\dim(G/B)-\dim(H)
, it is reasonable to identify
\Bir(\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB})
with
\Bir(T^{\prime})
, or with the group of birational automorphisms of some particularly simple toric compactification of
T^{\prime}
(typically a geometric quotient of
U_{eB}
). Summing up, the process of computing the Chow quotient of
X=G/P
by the action of
H
can be tackled in two steps:

1.

compute the combinatorial quotient
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB}
;

2.

construct
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX
out of
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB}
by using the mutation group
\mu(W)\subset\Bir(\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{eB})
.

4.
Quotients of Picard number one

A first comment on the procedure described above is that the birational complexity of the combinatorial quotients should reflect on the intricacy of the second step. The next statement illustrates this fact on the simplest possible situation:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X=G/P
be a rational homogeneous manifold, endowed with the action of a maximal torus
H\subset P\subset G
. Assume that the combinatorial quotient
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{x}
of the Bruhat neighborhood of an
H
-fixed point of
X

Let us now consider in the geometric quotient
\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
the points whose homogeneous coordinates
[z_{2}:\dots:z_{n}]
are the following:

p_{1}:=[1:1:\dots:1],\,\,p_{2}:=[1:0:\dots:0],\,\,\dots,\,\,p_{n}:=[0:0:\dots:1].

Proposition 5.7
.

The normalized Chow quotient of the Grassmannian of lines
{\rm A}_{n}(2)
by the action of the maximal torus
H
is isomorphic to the variety obtained from
\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
by blowing up
\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n}\}
and then the linear subspaces generated by them in order of increasing dimension.

Proof.

Let
X^{\prime}
be the variety obtained from
\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
blowing up
\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n}\}
and then the linear subspaces generated by them in order of increasing dimension.

The Bruhat neighborhoods of the Grassmannian
X={\rm A}_{n}(2)
that we are considering are of the form
U_{w(I_{0})}
,
w\in W
, and
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{I_{0}}
is the permutohedral variety obtained upon
\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
by blowing up
p_{2},\dots,p_{n}
and then the linear subspaces generated by them in order of increasing dimension. Hence it follows that the combinatorial quotient
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{w(I_{0})}
is the permutohedral variety obtained upon
\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
by blowing up
w(p_{2}),\dots,w(p_{n})
and then the linear subspaces generated by them in order of increasing dimension.

Since the subgroup of
W
generated by
r_{2},\dots,r_{n}
acts on the set of points
\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n}\}
as its group of permutations (
r_{i}
exchanges
p_{i}
and
p_{i-1}
), it follows that there are contractions
X^{\prime}\to\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{w(I_{0})}
for every
w
in
W
, that commute with the birational transformation among the
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{w(I_{0})}
’s. By Proposition
3.9
, it then follows that we have a morphism
X^{\prime}\to\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX
. One may easily check that there are no curves contracted simultaneously by every contraction
X^{\prime}\to\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muU_{w(I_{0})}
, hence it follows that
X^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}\mkern-1.0muX
.
∎
