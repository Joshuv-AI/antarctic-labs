1 Introduction

1.1 Project Background

The Los Alamos Neutron Science Center (LANSCE) is located at Technical Area 53 (TA-53) at Los
Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) in Los Alamos, New Mexico. LANSCE is driven by an 800-
megaelectronvolt (MeV) proton accelerator that delivered its first beam in 1972. The LANSCE accelerator
is unique in that it accelerates both H– (to full energy of 800 MeV) and H+ ions (up to 100 MeV currently
but has accelerated high-power H+ beam to 800 MeV in the past) and supports five separate experimental
areas that operate simultaneously, with each having different timing and beam current requirements. Many
of the LANSCE accelerator front-end components date back to original commissioning in 1972, including
the ion sources, Cockcroft-Walton (CW) generators, and the Drift Tube LINAC (DTL), which accelerates
the beam up to 100 MeV.
The purpose of the LANSCE Accelerator Modernization Project (LAMP) is to replace and modernize
multiple accelerator systems and components to improve beam delivery, reliability, and availability. LAMP
modernization will improve system maintainability and prepare the facility for operation through 2050. The
scope of modernization currently includes the LANSCE front end (up to 100 MeV). This scope includes
modernizing the two ion sources, replacing the two CW generators with a modern radio frequency
quadrupole (RFQ), and replacing the DTL which is exhibiting severe degradation and has significantly
exceeded its expected lifespan. Modernization of accelerator systems beyond the 100 MeV beam energy
are not within the currently approved LAMP scope; however, end-to-end modeling through the entire
accelerator including the proton storage ring (PSR) will need to be performed to verify the project’s
performance requirements for all five experimental areas.
LAMP will be executed as a congressional line-item project under U.S. Department of Energy Order 413.3b
[1] for the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA). The design will follow the Program
Requirements Document (PRD) for LAMP [2] and other requirements that flow down from the PRD.

1.2 Purpose and Scope

This Configuration Management Plan (CMP) describes the process that LAMP uses to define, manage,
and control Configuration Items (CIs). In addition, this document describes how the project will manage
changes and updates to CIs. This CMP will be used from preliminary design approval through the end of
LAMP Project. The CMP may be used prior to approval of the preliminary design, on a case-by-case basis
at the discretion of the project director and technical director.

Project CIs will first be processed using their respective management plan, depending on the type of CI as
listed above: (1) Document and Records, (2) Drawing and CAD Model, (3) Physics Model, and (4)
Software. Apart from CIs that fall under the Documents and Records Management Plan, changes to the CIs

 • The lower tier is the Technical Change Request (TCR) process, executed internally by the
 LAMP Technical Change Control Board (Technical CCB, or TCCB) following the TCR
 process described in this CMP.
 • The second, higher tier is the Baseline Change Control (BCC) process, executed by the
 Baseline Change Control Board (Baseline CCB, or BCCB) following the BCC described in
 the PPEP [8].
Process for managing technical changes is described in this CMP. A technical change affects the design
or implementation of a CI but does not alter the approved project baseline (scope, cost, or schedule).
Process for managing baseline changes is described in the PPEP. A baseline change affects the approved
project baseline, meaning it alters the officially approved scope, cost, schedule, Level 0 requirements, or
Key Performance Parameters (KPPs). In situations when a technical change impacts the approved project
baseline, a technical change becomes a baseline change. In that case, the TCCB determines that scope,
schedule, or cost (or any combination thereof) exceed the thresholds of Technical Change Request Level 1
(defined in Section 1.4), and the change request is escalated to the BCCB for formal approval.

1.4 Technical Change Requests

In the case of a proposed change to a technical CI, the TCR process is used to formally review the CI change
with technical subject matter experts (SMEs) and technical stakeholders. The TCR approval indicates
agreement by the LAMP technical and management team to update the configuration managed design.
Table 1 provides an overview of the approval levels (1 through 3; Level 1 has the highest impact on scope,
schedule, and/or cost and requires the highest level of approval). Table 1provides scope, schedule, and cost
thresholds for Approval Level 3 (lowest), Level 2, and Level 1 (highest). As soon as one of the thresholds
is exceeded, the TCR will get elevated to the next level. For example, if a design change impacts form, fit,
requirement, or interface, it will fall into Level 2 even if the cost is less than $50k. Similarly, if a change
exceeds $50k, it will fall into Level 2 even if the change does not affect form, fit, or requirement. Most
TCRs are Level 3 and Level 2 and are processed internally by the project team. In situations where the
baseline could be affected, the TCR is assigned Level 1, and the associated impacts to risk, cost, schedule,
or scope need to be approved using the BCC process. If the BCP is approved, the associated TCR is
approved, and the CI will be updated in the appropriate CM database.

1.5 Configuration Management Approach and Objectives

The LAMP Project will identify, review, and approve all CIs to be placed under configuration control in
the appropriate CM database. For the technical CIs that are placed under configuration control, the LAMP
project technical staff will follow the process set by this CMP to make changes, if needed.
LAMP CM supports all LAMP subsystems in their CM/configuration control programs. The objectives of
LAMP CMP include the following:
 • Ensure that integrity and continuity of technical changes are documented and recorded within
 the structure of technical scope, cost, and schedule baselines.
 • Provide identification, control, and status reporting necessary to assist management in
 achieving timely system readiness, visibility, traceability, and field support.
 • Provide managers at all levels with sufficient information for making appropriate, timely
 decisions throughout the life of the project.
 • Ensure that the evaluation of proposed configuration changes is timely and includes a
 thorough consideration of the change’s total impact on technical, scope, cost, schedule,
 operational capability, and support documentation.

3 Configuration Management and Planning

3.1 Commencement of Configuration Control

To manage the LAMP design, the LAMP Project will conduct preliminary design reviews as defined in the
Design Management Plan [9]. The preliminary design will be reviewed and approved by the project director
with concurrence from the technical director, FDAR, chief engineer, and chief scientist. Once the
preliminary design is reviewed and approved, the LAMP project will then place the preliminary design
under configuration control.
Unless decided otherwise by the project, LAMP project will commence configuration control after the
approved preliminary design. The approved preliminary design will consist of drawings, documents,
models, software programs, and other items as needed.
The entire configuration control process consists of four ongoing stages:
 • Configuration Item Identification
 • Configuration Change Management
 • Configuration Status Accounting
 • Configuration Verification and Audits

3.2 Configuration Item Identification

The Project’s major categories of CIs are outlined in Section 1.3. Configuration Item Identification involves
selecting, naming, and documenting items (hardware, software, documentation, plans, etc.) that require
controlled management. Key criteria include selecting items that are critical to project success, expected to
change, or require maintenance. Criteria for selecting configuration items may include the following:
 • Criticality & Project Impact:
 The item is a Critical Technology Element (CTE), implements a critical capability, or is
 essential to project success.
 • Risk & Schedule Importance:
 The item is schedule-critical, high-risk, or its failure would result in significant financial or
 mission impact.
 • Control & Verification Needs:
 The item requires enhanced control, traceability, and verification due to its importance or
 complexity.
 • Configuration Traceability:
 The item requires accurate records of its configuration, including status and changes
 throughout its lifecycle.
 • Interdependence & Interfaces:
 The item interfaces with other configuration items (including those controlled by other
 organizations) or has dependencies where changes propagate.
 • Volatility (Change Frequency):
 The item is expected to change frequently during development or operations.
 • Shared Use & Organizational Impact:
 The item is used across multiple teams, organizations, or users.
 • Contractual or Requirement-Driven:
 The item is defined or required by contractual, regulatory, or formal project requirements.
 • Procurement Considerations:
 The item may require long-lead procurement or special acquisition management.

4 Configuration Change Management

4.1 Technical Change Request

A TCR process is used to identify, review, approve, and document CI changes that affect form, fit, function,
performance, or processes. The TCR form also includes areas for identifying impacts to cost, schedule, and
scope. Approval of the TCR signifies the agreement by the managerial and technical community to update
the CM database; however, if the CI change affects risk, scope, cost, schedule (see Section 1.4), the BCC
process will need to be followed. The respective CAM will determine if the BCC process needs to be
followed (see Section 1.3 and Table 3).

4.2 Identifying Changes to Configuration Items

Proposed changes to CIs can be identified by several methods:
 • Periodic technical meetings could identify technical changes and risk mitigation strategies
 that need to be accommodated to facilitate successful attainment of project deliverables.
 • Periodic project status reports and technical meetings could identify issues that require
 changes.
 • Suggestions by TLs could identify technical changes that will result in improved performance
 or are needed to solve technical issues.
 • TLs could identify CI changes at any point during design development from preliminary to
 final design, as well as during or after the fabrication and commissioning.
 • TLs could identify CI changes if issues are discovered in as-built form, fit, function, or
 specifications of parts.

LAMP-SE-PLAN-002; Configuration Management Plan

 • TLs could identify CI changes due to rework or design modifications during testing of first
 articles or during startup.
 • Any LAMP team member could identify changes from preliminary design approval to the
 end of the LAMP project.

4.3 Technical Change Control Board

The TCR process uses the TCCB to review and approve proposed CI changes. The LAMP technical director
is the chair of the TCCB. TCRs are approved by the LAMP technical director with concurrence from the
TCCB. The goal is for the TCCB members to reach a consensus, but the chair has the ultimate authority to
approve or reject TCRs. Other TCCB members act primarily as advisors. If a TCCB member strongly
disagrees with the TCCB Chair’s decision, the TCR can be elevated to the project director.
TCCBs include the technical director, project director, chief engineer, chief scientist, TLs, and observers.
Other TCCB members will be assigned appropriately by the technical director and will include
representation from the following disciplines:
 • Project Management
 • Project Controls
 • Chief Engineer
 • Chief Scientist
 • Environment, Safety, and Health
 • Quality Assurance
 • Risk Management

4.4 Technical Change Request (TCR) Form

A TCR form is used to document the TCR and capture the approval for proposed CI change. Blank TCR
form will be posted on the LAMP collaboration site so that all LAMP personnel will have access. TCRs
must include identification information and analysis regarding a proposed change:
 • Initiating references that identify reasons for the proposed change
 • Detailed description of the proposed change
 • Justification for the change
 • Analysis of the impacts of implementing the change (e.g., performance, system
 specifications, safety, etc.)
 • Identification of documents and procedures that are impacted by the change
 • Project milestone impacts
 • Risk, cost, schedule, and scope impacts
 • TCCB approval/denial
 • BCP waiver/approval

4.5 Baseline Change Control Process

The Baseline Change Control process is used to adjust the baseline cost, schedule, and scope. An approved
TCR is reviewed by the relevant CAM to determine if a TCR needs to be elevated to the Baseline Change
Control process. If the elevation to BCC is required, then the BCC process is used by the CAM, as defined
in PPEP [8] (Section Baseline Change Control).

5 Configuration Status Accounting
Configuration Status Accounting provides an accurate, timely information base for approved design
information throughout the design life cycle. Its purpose is to capture and maintain CM information to
ensure that current and historical configurations of the design information can be accurately determined
throughout the product life cycle.
Information about LAMP and the LAMP configuration information is captured, correlated, stored, and
maintained as the design evolves through all phases of its life cycle.
Current, accurate information concerning change decisions, design changes, variances, investigations of
design problems, and other relevant design information is retrievable.
The complete collection of LAMP configuration information is organized, indexed, and readily available.
Current and historical configurations of LAMP, including part identifiers and product configuration
information for specifications, engineering drawings, and part models, are accurately determinable and
traceable.
Team members have the capability to access LAMP configuration-related information.

6 Configuration Verification and Audits
Configuration verification and audits are the control activities for verifying that CIs are updated and
managed as specified by this CMP.

6.1 Configuration Verification

Configuration verification is accomplished by three controls:
 • Periodic reviews will be performed by the SEM, who supports configuration verification by
 periodically reviewing open TCRs and engaging responsible individuals to complete the
 updates.
 • Requirement reviews serve to assess and verify that CI documents have been identified to
 ensure that the requirements are properly supported. These reviews also ensure that the
 requirements have been adequately decomposed and that key interfaces have been defined
 and agreed upon.
 • Design reviews serve to assess and verify that the design meets established requirements.
 They provide critical verification that the requirements captured in the CM databases are
 correctly implemented in the LAMP design.
Each of these processes is used to verify that LAMP design is managed from preliminary design approval
to the end of the LAMP Project.

6.2 Configuration Audits

A configuration audit is a review of configuration verification records, configuration information, and
inspection of physical items that are performed at the conclusion of LAMP design development.
Configuration audits ensure that the LAMP system instance being audited is consistent with its
configuration definition information.
Due to the complexity of LAMP design, the configuration audit will be accomplished in a series of
incremental audits and through a check of a representative sample of CI records produced. The audit may
be initiated by the LAMP technical director, FDAR, chief engineer, or quality assurance representative,
with coordination with TLs. The chairperson and representatives of each affected party to the audit
participate in audit planning and preparation. Audit plans and agendas are reviewed and agreed to before
the audit. During the conduct of an audit, participants record significant questions, discrepancies or
anomalies, and recommended courses of action. The chairperson reviews audit findings and determines
appropriate actions. Affected parties agree to action items and plan for their successful closure. Audit issues
may be elevated to the project manager and project director for tracking and closure.

7 Subcontractor Control
The requirements of this CMP will be passed down to the subcontractors, and accordingly, to their lower-
tier subcontractors through contractual mechanisms. The TLs are responsible for verifying that all official
documents (i.e., design specification, nonconformance reports, design analysis, results, etc.) developed by
subcontractors in the support of the LAMP project are placed under CM in compliance with this CMP. This
verification will require TLs to maintain oversight of CIs that are being used, created, or modified by
subcontractors. The TL is responsible for ensuring that CIs are placed under CM and are updated according
to this CMP.

8 Configuration Management Awareness and Training
Following the release of this document, LAMP management will communicate its contents to the TLs and
project team at large. TLs and CAMs are responsible for ensuring implementation within their project
teams. This CMP will be posted on the LAMP collaboration site so that all LAMP personnel will have
access. The SEM will present the CMP at one of the LAMP TL meetings. The SEM will serve as the point
of contact.
