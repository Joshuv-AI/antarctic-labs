Effects of combination metformin and nintedanib therapy in a mouse model of irradiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis
Authors: Ji-Hun Lee, Han-Bi Jeong, Eun-Seon Yoo, Na-Won Kim, Sun-Min Seo, Young-Jun Park, Yang-Kyu Choi, Jin-Hee Seo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTRadiation-induced lung injury, a well-recognized complication of thoracic radiotherapy, can progress to pulmonary fibrosis, sharing pathological features with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Although nintedanib is widely used as an antifibrotic agent, the therapeutic benefit of combination strategies remains partially understood. Therefore, in this study, we aimed to evaluate the effects of combined metformin and nintedanib treatment in a mouse model of irradiation-induced lung fibrosis. Female C57BL/6 mice were subjected to localized irradiation of 90 Gy in the left lung and treated daily with metformin, nintedanib or both drugs for 6 weeks. Lung fibrosis was assessed using gross examination, hydroxyproline quantification, histopathological scoring and quantitative image analysis. Changes in fibrosis- and injury-related proteins were examined by western blotting, including transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), a profibrotic cytokine; SMAD family member 7 (SMAD7), an inhibitory regulator of TGF-β signaling; and surfactant protein D (SP-D), a lung epithelial injury-associated protein. Nintedanib-containing treatments improved histological lung injury in irradiated mice, including reduced collagen deposition, low pathological scores and preserved air space structure. Hydroxyproline content was also lower in the combination treatment group than in the other groups. The nintedanib-containing treatments suppressed irradiation-induced upregulation of transforming growth factor-β expression, and SMAD7 expression was selectively upregulated following combined treatment. In addition, elevated SP-D level following irradiation was partially reduced following nintedanib administration. These findings suggest that combined metformin and nintedanib treatment may provide a broader protective profile against radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis, particularly in association with SMAD7 upregulation, although further studies are required to clarify whether the combination provides significant advantages over nintedanib monotherapy.Keywords: idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, irradiation, metformin, nintedanib, combination therapy

INTRODUCTION

Lung cancer is one of the most prevalent cancers globally and remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality in South Korea [1, 2]. Radiotherapy is frequently selected as a primary therapeutic approach for lung cancer [3]; however, although highly effective as both a primary treatment and an adjunct therapy for the deliverance of high doses of radiation to eradicate cancer cells, radiotherapy inevitably exposes the surrounding normal lung tissue to ionizing radiation [4]. The consequential long-term radiation exposure can lead to secondary adverse effects in lung tissues, and radiation-induced lung injury is a common yet serious complication that can develop following chronic pneumonitis [5].

Radiation-induced lung injury occurs via two stages: development of radiation pneumonitis and radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis [6]. These pathological phases resemble the manifestations of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), a chronic and progressive pulmonary disorder of unknown etiology that is characterized by pneumonitis and pulmonary fibrosis [7]. Patients with IPF experience severe dyspnea and impaired pulmonary function, together with histological features typical of the fibrotic lesions associated with interstitial pneumonia.

Current therapeutic approaches for IPF primarily rely on antifibrotic agents [8]. Among the antifibrotic agents, nintedanib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, has been shown to effectively slow fibrotic progression and is regarded as a standard treatment for IPF [9]. In addition to the approved antifibrotic drugs, other classes of medications, including antidiabetic agents, have been investigated as potential therapeutic agents for IPF. For example, metformin, a widely used medication for type 2 diabetes mellitus, reportedly exerts antifibrotic effects and improves fibrotic outcomes in experimental models of pulmonary fibrosis [10].

Recently, increasing attention has been directed towards combination therapies that utilize established drugs for IPF [11, 12]. For example, pirfenidone, another antifibrotic agent, has been reported to enhance antifibrotic efficacy when combined with nintedanib in bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis models [13]. These findings suggest that combining drugs with distinct mechanisms of action may provide therapeutic benefits beyond those achieved with monotherapy and indicate that investigating the potential benefits of novel drug combinations is a promising strategy for IPF treatment. Thus, in the present study, we aimed to evaluate the effects of combined metformin and nintedanib treatment in a radiation-induced murine model of pulmonary fibrosis.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Animal experiments

Female C57BL/6J mice (age: 9 weeks) were supplied by DBL (Eumseong, Republic of Korea). The mice were randomly allocated to five groups: normal control (NC), irradiation alone (IR), irradiation plus metformin (IRM), irradiation plus nintedanib (IRN) and combination treatment (IRMN) (Fig. 1A). To ensure uniformity and minimize initial variations, grouping was conducted based on matching body weights. Animals were maintained in a specific-pathogen-free environment with standard housing conditions (12-h light/dark cycle, ambient temperature: 21–25°C and relative humidity: 45–55%), with free access to standard chow and water.

Summary of the experimental design. (A) Experimental schedule and group allocation. The experiment was conducted for 6 weeks, with daily drug treatment initiated after irradiation. Mice were divided into five groups according to irradiation and drug treatment regimens. (B) Irradiation procedure. Mice received localized irradiation in the ventral position of the left lung using a 7-mm collimator at a dose of 90 Gy. The schematic illustrations of the mouse and lungs in Figure 1B were generated with the assistance of an AI tool (ChatGPT, OpenAI).

Localized irradiation was conducted at the Korea Institute of Radiological & Medical Sciences (Seoul, Republic of Korea). Prior to radiation exposure, intraperitoneal administration of alfaxalone (75 mg/kg) and xylazine (10 mg/kg) was used to anesthetize the mice. The mice were then immobilized on an acrylic board with tape, and the left lung of each mouse received a targeted 90 Gy X-ray dose (270 kV, 10 mA; dose rate of 10 Gy/min). Although this bolus dose is higher than typical clinical fractionated protocols, we chose it to reliably induce severe pulmonary fibrosis within our study period, in line with established mouse models [14]. Radiation delivery was restricted to the intended lung region using a 7-mm collimator (Fig. 1B). Post-irradiation, daily intraperitoneal treatments (10 ml/kg) commenced and continued for 6 weeks. Mice received either 0.9% saline (vehicle), metformin (125 mg/kg), nintedanib (20 mg/kg) or both therapeutic agents combined. The metformin dose was selected based on previous experimental pulmonary fibrosis studies reporting antifibrotic effects at comparable doses [9, 10]. The nintedanib dose was selected based on preclinical lung injury and fibrosis studies in which daily nintedanib administration produced antifibrotic effects without overt intolerance [13]. These doses were used to evaluate biological efficacy in the present radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis model and were not intended to represent direct clinical dose equivalence. At the end of the experimental period, all subjects were deeply anesthetized with isoflurane (JSK, Goyang, Republic of Korea) via inhalation and euthanized by exsanguination via the abdominal aorta to harvest lung tissues. Gross lesion areas of the injured lung were quantified by three independent, blinded observers using ImageJ software (version 1.54g; National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA); the resulting data were averaged for final analysis.

Hydroxyproline quantification

Approximately 10 mg of tissue was collected from the grossly identifiable lesion-containing region of the irradiated left lung. In NC mice, tissue was collected from the corresponding anatomical region of the left lung. The lung tissue was homogenized in distilled water at a 1:10 (w/v) ratio before hydrolysis by incubation with 100 μl of hydrochloric acid at 110°C for 24 h. The samples were then vortexed and centrifuged at 10000 × g for 3 min; 50-μl aliquots of each supernatant were transferred in duplicate to a 96-well plate. The samples were then completely evaporated in a dry oven at 60°C, and hydroxyproline content was measured using a Hydroxyproline Assay Kit (MAK008; Sigma-Aldrich, Darmstadt, Germany), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, wells were sequentially treated with a chloramine-T/oxidation buffer mix and a diluted DMAB solution, followed by a 90-min incubation. Finally, absorbance was recorded at 560 nm using a SpectraMax ABS spectrophotometric multi-well plate reader (Molecular Devices, San Jose, CA, USA).

Histopathological evaluation

Excised lung tissues were preserved in neutral-buffered formalin for 24 h, processed for routine paraffin embedding, and cut into 5-μm sections. These tissue slices were deparaffinized using xylene and rehydrated using descending ethanol gradients. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Sirius Red stains were applied before dehydrating, clearing and coverslipping the slides. All H&E staining procedures were performed using an automatic stainer (Epredia, Kalamazoo, USA), while Sirius Red staining was performed using a VitroView Sirius Red Stain Kit (VitroVivo, Rockville, USA), according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. A BX51 light microscope equipped with DP71 software (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) was used for slide examination and image acquisition. Pathological scores were determined based on the evaluation of inflammatory infiltrates and fibrotic areas within the injured lung regions using the scoring criteria presented in Table 1, and the air space fraction and Sirius red-positive areas were quantified using ImageJ software (LOCI). All histopathological evaluations were conducted by a single experienced pathologist blinded to group allocation.

Western blot analysis

Western blotting was employed to evaluate the protein levels of transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), SMAD family member 7 (SMAD7), surfactant protein D (SP-D) and β-actin. Tissue lysates were prepared from the same lesion-containing region of the irradiated left lung used for hydroxyproline quantification, and corresponding left lung regions were collected from NC mice. The samples were prepared in T-PER buffer enriched with phosphatase and protease inhibitors. Following protein quantification via the Bradford method using a bovine serum albumin standard, equivalent protein quantities were mixed with 2-mercaptoethanol-containing Laemmli buffer and boiled at 95°C for 10 min. Proteins were separated using sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and subsequently transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride membranes. After blocking, membranes were probed with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C, followed by a 1-h incubation with appropriate secondary antibodies. Target proteins were visualized through enhanced chemiluminescence using Pierce ECL Western Blotting Substrate (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) on a ChemiDoc™ Touch Imaging System (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). As all samples could not be loaded onto a single gel, a common reference sample was included on each gel for inter-gel normalization. Band intensities were first normalized to β-actin and then adjusted using the intensity of the common reference sample included on each gel for inter-gel comparison. The uncropped full-length blot images, clearly showing the common reference samples for each gel, are provided in the Supplementary Fig. S1. Representative blot images were cropped and arranged according to group allocation for figure presentation. All utilized antibodies and dilution ratios are listed in Table 2.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess the normality of the data distribution. Group comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for normally distributed variables or the Kruskal–Wallis test for non-parametric data. Depending on the initial test, Tukey’s or Dunn’s post hoc analyses were utilized for multiple comparisons. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

RESULTS

Successful induction of pulmonary fibrosis was assessed by weekly measurement of body weight and quantification of the affected fibrotic area in the left lung lobe. No marked changes were observed in the average body weight of mice in all groups throughout the experimental period, except for a transient decrease in the first week following irradiation (Fig. 2). Moreover, no overt adverse clinical signs were observed during the experimental period; however, because non-irradiated drug-only control groups were not included, systemic toxicity was not specifically evaluated. All radiation-exposed groups exhibited pale regions in the left lung lobe, indicating fibrotic involvement (Fig. 3A). Notably, the affected fibrotic area was markedly reduced in the IRMN group compared with that in the other radiation-exposed groups, with a significant difference compared with that in the IR group (Fig. 3B).

The hydroxyproline content in the injured lung lobe was higher in the IR and IRM groups than in the NC group, with a significant difference observed between the IRM and NC groups (Fig. 3C). In contrast, the nintedanib-treated groups (IRN and IRMN) exhibited significantly lower hydroxyproline content than the IR group. Furthermore, the IRMN group showed lower hydroxyproline content than the IRN group.

Histological evaluation revealed prominent fibrotic lesions and inflammatory infiltrates in the irradiated lung tissues (Fig. 4A). Inflammatory infiltrates consisted of mixed cell populations, including macrophages and lymphocytes, which were localized to the alveolar spaces. Fibrotic lesions were characterized by the accumulation of eosinophilic, amorphous material within the pulmonary parenchyma. Pathological scores were calculated based on the evaluation of inflammatory and fibrotic changes (Fig. 4C), with results showing mean pathological scores of 5.8 for the IR group and 5.0 for the IRM group. In contrast, significant low scores of 2.6 and 2.2 were obtained for the IRN and IRMN groups, respectively, compared with those for the IR group.

Air space fraction was quantified to assess structural alterations in the injured lung areas (Fig. 4D). The NC group exhibited a mean value of 64.08%, whereas the IR and IRM groups showed substantially lower values of 38.60% and 42.33% than the NC group. Air space fraction was significantly increased to 58.66% in the IRN group and to 59.09% in the IRMN group compared with that in the IR group.

Collagen deposition in the injured area was assessed by quantifying the Sirius red-positive area (Fig. 4E). The NC group showed a mean Sirius red-positive area of 4.41%, whereas the IR group exhibited a significantly increased area of 27.56%. All treatment groups demonstrated reduced Sirius red-positive areas compared to the IR group. The IRM group showed a mean value of 17.93%, whereas lower values of 13.66 and 13.50% were obtained for the IRN and IRMN groups, respectively.

The western blot analysis also revealed changes in the protein expression among the experimental groups (Fig. 5A). The TGF-β level was elevated in the IR and IRM groups compared with that in the NC group (Fig. 5B); however, no significant difference in its level was observed between the nintedanib-treated groups, the IRN and IRMN groups. SMAD7 expression in the IRMN group was significantly higher than that in the NC, IR and IRN groups (Fig. 5C), and no significant increase in expression was observed in the other treatment groups. The SP-D levels in the IR and IRM groups was significantly higher than that in the NC group (Fig. 5D), whereas SP-D expression in the IRN group was significantly lower than that in the IR group. The IRMN group also exhibited reduced SP-D levels compared to the IR group, although the level in these groups was higher than that in the NC group.

DISCUSSION

IPF is a chronic and progressive pulmonary disease that is characterized by recurrent alveolar epithelial injury and aberrant repair, leading to excessive interstitial fibrosis driven by myofibroblasts [15]. The prognosis of IPF is poor, with a mean survival time of approximately 3.8 years, and mortality is primarily caused by chronic respiratory failure. Although the precise pathogenesis of IPF remains unclear, genetic susceptibility, aging and environmental or iatrogenic factors are considered major contributing factors [16]. Radiation-induced lung injury is a well-recognized trigger for fibrotic lung remodeling, as radiation exposure induces cellular damage and inflammatory responses that activate the profibrotic signaling pathways [17]. Accordingly, in the present study, we employed a radiation-induced IPF mouse model, in which a previously validated focal irradiation protocol was used to deliver a precise dose of radiation to a defined lung region, thereby recapitulating the pathophysiological processes of radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis [14].

Multiple approaches have been explored to evaluate the effects of combination therapies with drugs exhibiting distinct mechanisms of action in the treatment of IPF [11, 18, 19]. Combination therapy is considered to be more effective than monotherapy as it enables simultaneous targeting of the different pathobiological processes that are involved in disease progression [20]. This strategy is particularly relevant given that patients with IPF often have underlying comorbidities requiring concurrent medication use and that IPF tends to exhibit more severe clinical manifestations in such populations [21]. Accordingly, identifying drug combinations that offer enhanced therapeutic efficacy is of clinical importance.

In this study, combined treatment with metformin and nintedanib attenuated radiation-induced lung injury to a greater extent than either agent alone. The histological analyses consistently showed that the IRMN group exhibited reduced inflammatory cell infiltration, low pathological scores, preserved air space structure and decreased collagen deposition as compared to both irradiation controls and mice treated with metformin monotherapy. These findings indicate that the combination regimen exerted broader protective effects on both inflammatory and fibrotic components of lung injury.

The TGF-β axis is well known to play a central role in fibrotic pathways involved in IPF pathogenesis [22, 23]. Activation of the TGF-β signaling pathway following lung injury promotes the differentiation of myofibroblasts from fibroblasts and facilitates an epithelial–mesenchymal transition, ultimately leading to increased extracellular matrix component production and progressive pulmonary fibrosis. Moreover, SMAD7 functions as an endogenous inhibitor of this pathway by suppressing SMAD2/3 activation, restraining downstream TGF-β/SMAD signaling [24, 25]. Mechanistically, nintedanib, through the inhibition of multiple receptor tyrosine kinases, may suppress fibroblast activation and downstream profibrotic signaling that converges with the TGF-β/SMAD pathway.

Concurrently, metformin may influence fibrotic remodeling through AMPK-related regulation of fibroblast activation, epithelial injury responses and inflammatory signaling. In this study, TGF-β expression, which was considerably upregulated following irradiation, decreased in the nintedanib-treated groups, consistent with the findings of a previous study [26]. Notably, SMAD7 expression was selectively upregulated in the combination treatment group. This increase in SMAD7 suggests modulation of inhibitory SMAD signaling and may represent a compensatory or inhibitory regulatory response in the TGF-β/SMAD axis, rather than direct proof of enhanced pathway suppression or mechanistic synergy [27]. This limitation should be carefully considered when interpreting the combination treatment effect.

SP-D is a lung collectin produced mainly by alveolar type II epithelial cells and has been implicated in epithelial injury responses and innate immune regulation, including modulation of inflammatory signaling in damaged lung tissue [28–30]. The SP-D level increases following pulmonary injury and contributes to tissue protection by modulating the inflammatory responses and metabolic processes within damaged lung tissues. Accordingly, irradiation-induced stress elevated SP-D expression in the injured lungs in the present study. Nintedanib treatment reduced the SP-D level, whereas a less pronounced reduction was observed in the combination treatment group. Although changes in SP-D may reflect altered epithelial injury and repair responses, SP-D expression alone cannot definitively distinguish between reduced injury and preserved epithelial function. Given the essential role of SP-D in modulating innate immune signaling and exerting anti-inflammatory effects [28–30], the sustained SP-D level in the combination treatment group could indicate a metformin-mediated preservation of epithelial function and immune homeostasis. This suggests that although nintedanib strongly suppresses fibrotic remodeling, the addition of metformin may aid in maintaining the protective capacity of the alveolar epithelium against radiation-induced stress [31, 32], rather than merely reducing injury markers.

IPF and radiation-induced lung fibrosis are multifactorial diseases that involve complex interactions among epithelial injury, inflammation and fibrogenesis. In this context, therapeutic strategies that target multiple pathological processes may offer advantages over single-agent approaches. The present study findings support the potential benefit of combining metformin and nintedanib to mitigate radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis. Nevertheless, certain limitations should be acknowledged. First, the precise molecular interactions underlying the observed synergy were not fully elucidated. Therefore, future studies utilizing SMAD7 gain- or loss-of-function approaches are warranted to verify the causal role of SMAD7 in the observed antifibrotic effects. Second, although radiation-induced lung injury involves a critical inflammatory phase, we did not assess specific inflammatory cytokines or immune cell infiltration profiles. Future investigations should include the profiling of inflammatory mediators (e.g. interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α) and immune cell populations to better understand the immunomodulatory effects of the combination therapy. Furthermore, functional respiratory outcomes were not assessed in this study; thus, long-term functional evaluations are necessary to clarify the full translational relevance of this combination strategy. Finally, because non-irradiated drug-only control groups were not included in the present study, we cannot completely exclude the possibility of subtle treatment-related adverse effects. Future studies should include such control groups to systematically evaluate drug-specific safety profiles.
