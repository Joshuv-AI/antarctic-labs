Leading UV Formula for Finite-Volume Vertex Operator Expectation Values in the Sine-Gordon Model from Kink NLIE
Árpád Hegedűs, Apor Roth

Abstract
We study the ultraviolet (UV) limit of finite-volume expectation values of vertex operators in the sine-Gordon model using the kink nonlinear integral equation (NLIE) description of the conformal limit. By analysing the integrable formulation of vacuum expectation values in the small-volume regime, we conjecture an explicit analytic expression for the leading asymptotic term in the small-volume expansion, formulated in terms of kink functions. This establishes a direct connection between the integrable finite-volume description and the expected conformal asymptotics determined by the 3-point functions of the underlying conformal field theory (CFT). The proposed formula is tested against the analytic expression known from complex Liouville conformal field theory using high-precision numerics, showing agreement to at least 19 significant digits.

Introduction

Solving conformal field theories (CFTs) remains a central challenge in many branches of theoretical physics, from string theory to statistical mechanics. Conformal symmetry imposes strong constraints, and once the fundamental CFT data - scaling dimensions and 3-point structure constants - are known, all correlation functions in principle become accessible.

Determining this data, however, is in general highly nontrivial and in most cases remains out of reach.
In two dimensions, the infinite-dimensional Virasoro algebra
Belavin:1984vu
provides a powerful analytic framework for constructing exact CFT data.
In higher-dimensional theories, progress relies predominantly on the conformal bootstrap programme, both in its analytic
Poland:2022qrs
and numerical
Poland:2018epd
implementations, which has led to precise constraints on universal CFT data.

A particularly remarkable exception is provided by planar theories arising in AdS/CFT dualities
Maldacena:1997re
, where integrability plays a central role
Beisert:2010jr
.
In the case of the AdS
5
/CFT
4
correspondence, the spectral problem of
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory has been essentially solved through integrability-based methods, most notably the Y-system formulation
Gromov:2009tv
, and its modern reformulation in terms of the Quantum Spectral Curve (QSC)
Gromov:2013pga
;
Gromov:2014caa
.
On the other hand, while the computation of three-point functions has seen substantial progress within the hexagon framework
Basso:2015zoa
, the inclusion of all wrapping and gluing effects remains challenging.
For CFTs endowed with integrable structures, it is natural to expect that three-point couplings
admit relatively compact representations
in terms of functions characterizing the integrable description of the spectral problem of the underlying system.

In the context of the AdS
5
/CFT
4
spectral problem, techniques originating from two-dimensional integrable quantum field theory have provided important guiding principles for the development of methods used to determine the spectrum.
This suggests that integrability-based approaches to three-point functions in two-dimensional CFTs may also provide structural insights relevant to the AdS/CFT correspondence.

The aim of the present work is to develop a framework for representing three-point couplings in two-dimensional CFTs directly in terms of functions that emerge from the integrable description of the spectrum.

This programme was initiated in
Bajnok:2022ucr
, where the Lee-Yang and three-state Potts models were studied.
The analysis was restricted to a particular class of three-point couplings involving two identical operators and a third operator corresponding to the perturbation that generates a massive integrable deformation of the CFT.
More precisely, couplings of the form
C_{{\cal O}\Phi{\cal O}}
were considered, where
{\cal O}
denotes the repeated operator and
\Phi
the perturbing field.

2
Preliminaries

The central idea of our approach to investigating the conformal data of a CFT is to place the theory
in finite volume, equivalently defining it on a cylinder of circumference
L
.
In finite volume, the energy spectrum of a CFT is entirely determined by the scaling dimensions and the
central charge of the model. Thanks to the state-operator correspondence, each energy level can be
associated with a specific operator of the theory. The volume dependence of an energy level corresponding
to an operator
{\cal O}
is fully fixed by its conformal dimensions:

\begin{split}E_{\cal O}(L)=\frac{2\,\pi}{L}\left(\Delta_{+}^{\cal O}+\Delta_{-}^{\cal O}-\frac{c}{12}\right),\end{split}

(2.1)

where
\Delta_{\pm}^{\cal O}
denote the left and right conformal dimensions of
{\cal O}
, and
c
is
the central charge of the CFT.

In finite volume, the matrix elements of local fields provide access to the
3-point couplings of the theory via

\begin{split}\langle{\cal O}_{1}|{\cal O}|{\cal O}_{2}\rangle=\left(\frac{2\,\pi}{L}\right)^{\Delta_{+}^{\cal O}+\Delta_{-}^{\cal O}}C_{{\cal O}_{1}{\cal O}{\cal O}_{2}}.\end{split}

(2.2)

In this work, we aim to develop an integrability-based description of
the three-point couplings
C_{{\cal O}_{1}{\cal O}{\cal O}_{2}}
in the ultraviolet limit of the sine-Gordon theory.
Due to limitations of the available analytical tools, we restrict ourselves to three-point couplings
involving at least two identical operators (
{\cal O}_{1}={\cal O}_{2}
in (
2.2
)),
which correspond to diagonal matrix elements in the finite-volume setting.

2.1
The model

In this work, we study the sine-Gordon model, defined by its Euclidean action as

\begin{split}{\cal A}_{SG}=\int\bigg\{\frac{1}{4\pi}\partial_{z}\varphi(z,\bar{z})\,\partial_{\bar{z}}\varphi(z,\bar{z})-\frac{2{\bf\mu}^{2}}{\sin\pi\beta^{2}}\cos(\beta\,\varphi(z,\bar{z}))\bigg\}\,\frac{i\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{2},\end{split}

(2.3)

where
z=x+i\,y
and
\bar{z}=x-i\,y
denote the Euclidean space-time coordinates.

Following
Jimbo:2010jv
, we treat the model as a perturbed complex Liouville CFT:

\begin{split}{\cal A}_{SG}={\cal A}_{L}-\frac{{\bf\mu}^{2}}{\sin\pi\beta^{2}}\int e^{i\,\beta\,\varphi(z,\bar{z})}\frac{i\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{2},\end{split}

(2.4)

where
{\cal A}_{L}
is the action of the unperturbed CFT:

\begin{split}{\cal A}_{L}=\int\bigg\{\frac{1}{4\pi}\partial_{z}\varphi(z,\bar{z})\,\partial_{\bar{z}}\varphi(z,\bar{z})-\frac{{\bf\mu}^{2}}{\sin\pi\beta^{2}}e^{-i\,\beta\,\varphi(z,\bar{z})}\bigg\}\,\frac{i\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{2}.\end{split}

(2.5)

The central charge of this CFT is

\begin{split}c_{L}=1-6\frac{\nu^{2}}{1-\nu},\qquad\nu=1-\beta^{2},\end{split}

(2.6)

and its primary fields are parameterized by a continuous real parameter
\alpha
:

\begin{split}\Phi_{\alpha}(z,\bar{z})=e^{\tfrac{i\,\alpha\beta\nu}{2(1-\nu)}\,\varphi(z,\bar{z})},\end{split}

(2.7)

with scaling dimensions
2\Delta_{\alpha}
, where

\begin{split}\Delta_{\alpha}=\frac{\nu^{2}}{4(1-\nu)}\alpha\,(\alpha-2).\end{split}

(2.8)

We use the same normalization for the fields as in
Jimbo:2010jv
.
Namely, the normalization is chosen such that the shift of
\alpha
by the unit
2(1-\nu)/\nu

corresponds, in the CFT language, to normal ordered multiplication by the primary field

e^{i\beta\varphi(z,\bar{z})}
.

In this study, following
Jimbo:2010jv
, we focus on the fundamental range
0\leq\alpha<2
.
Extensions to a wider range of
\alpha
can be achieved via an appropriate analytical continuation.

The primary fields (
2.7
) together with their descendants form a complete basis in the space of operators of the theory.
Exploiting the fermionic basis, it was demonstrated in
Jimbo:2010jv
that specific ratios of vacuum expectation values of these fields can be expressed in terms of a single function
\omega(\lambda,\mu|\alpha)
. More precisely, the expectation values are determined by a finite set of coefficients arising in the asymptotic expansions of
\omega(\lambda,\mu|\alpha)
as
\lambda,\mu\to\pm\infty
:

\begin{split}\omega(\mu,\lambda|\alpha)\!=\!\!\!\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{\infty}\!\!e^{-\epsilon_{1}\mu\tfrac{2k-1}{\nu}}e^{-\epsilon_{2}\lambda\tfrac{2j-1}{\nu}}\omega_{\epsilon_{1}(2k-1),\epsilon_{2}(2j-1)}(\alpha),\quad\!\epsilon_{1,2}\!=\!\pm,\quad\epsilon_{1}\,\mu\!\to\!\infty,\quad\!\epsilon_{2}\,\lambda\!\to\!\infty.\end{split}

(2.9)

The coefficients define an infinite matrix
\omega_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha),\,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

In this paper, we study its UV series expansion, for
j,k=1,\dots,\infty
, using an integrable description of the spectral problem in the UV CFT limit of the model, in order to determine the leading term in the UV expansion of vacuum expectation values.

The significance of this matrix stems from the fact that ratios of vacuum expectation values of primary fields
admit a direct representation in terms of its matrix elements
Jimbo:2010jv
:

\begin{split}\frac{\langle\Phi_{\alpha+2\,m\,\tfrac{1-\nu}{\nu}}(0)\rangle}{\langle\Phi_{\alpha}(0)\rangle}=\mu^{2\,m\alpha-2\,m^{2}\,(1-\tfrac{1}{\nu})}\,C_{m}(\alpha)\,\underset{1\leq j,k\leq m}{\mbox{det}}\,\Omega_{kj}(\alpha),\qquad m=1,2,\dots{}\end{split}

(2.10)

where
C_{m}(\alpha)
is given by

\begin{split}C_{m}(\alpha)=\prod\limits_{j=0}^{m-1}C_{1}(\alpha+2j\tfrac{1-\nu}{\nu}),\end{split}

(2.11)

with

\begin{split}&C_{1}(\alpha)=i\,\nu\Gamma(\nu)^{4x(\alpha)}\,\frac{\Gamma(-2\nu x(\alpha))}{\Gamma(2\nu x(\alpha))}\,\frac{\Gamma(x(\alpha))}{\Gamma(x(\alpha)+1/2)}\,\frac{\Gamma(-x(\alpha)+1/2)}{\Gamma(-x(\alpha))}\cot(\pi x(\alpha)),\\
&\text{and}\qquad x(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{1-\nu}{2\nu}.\end{split}

(2.12)

The elements of the matrix
\Omega_{kj}(\alpha)
are expressed in terms of
\omega_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)
as

\begin{split}\Omega_{kj}(\alpha)=\omega_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)+\frac{i}{\nu}\,\delta_{j,k}\,\cot\left[\frac{\pi}{2\nu}(2k-1+\nu\,\alpha)\right],\qquad j,k=1,...,m.\end{split}

(2.13)

Finally, the parameter
\mu
appearing in (
2.10
) is precisely the coupling constant in the sine-Gordon action (
2.3
). It is related to the soliton mass
{\cal M}
via
Zamolodchikov:1995xk
:

\begin{split}\mu={\cal M}^{\nu}\,\Pi(\nu)^{\nu},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\Pi(\nu)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2\nu}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1-\nu}{2\nu}\right)}\,\Gamma\left(\nu\right)^{-\tfrac{1}{\nu}}.\end{split}

(2.14)

Thus, all quantities appearing in (
2.10
) have now been specified, with the exception of
\omega_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)
, which we now turn to.
The entire volume dependence of the expectation-value ratios in (
2.10
) is encoded in these matrix elements. In the infinite-volume limit, where
\omega_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)\to 0,
(
2.10
) is in agreement with the Lukyanov-Zamolodchikov formula

We now remove trivial constant factors from the original definition given in
Jimbo:2010jv

\begin{split}\omega_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)=\frac{i}{\pi\,\nu}\,\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha),\qquad j,k\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{split}

(2.15)

The nontrivial content of the construction is encoded in

\begin{split}\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,e^{(2k-1)x}\,{\mathfrak{m}}(x)\,{\cal G}^{(\alpha)}_{1-2j}(x),\qquad j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\end{split}

(2.16)

Here,

\begin{split}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)\equiv{\mathfrak{m}}[Z(x)]={\cal L}_{+}[Z(x+i\,0)]+{\cal L}_{-}[Z(x-i\,0)],\qquad\text{with}\qquad{\cal L}_{\pm}[Z]=\frac{e^{\pm i\,Z}}{1+e^{\pm i\,Z}}.\end{split}

(2.17)

The functions

\begin{split}{\cal G}^{(\alpha)}_{1-2j}(x)-\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\,G_{\alpha}(x-y)\,{\mathfrak{m}}(y)\,{\cal G}^{(\alpha)}_{1-2j}(y)=e^{(1-2j)x},\qquad j\in\mathbb{Z},\end{split}

3
The UV behaviour of the counting-function

The counting-function
Z(x)
is the solution of the NLIE (
2.21
).
It governs the

2.17
) it determines the integration measure, which in turn introduces the

Consequently, a consistent description of the UV behaviour of the expectation values requires
the knowledge of the UV behaviour of the counting function. In this section, we address this problem.

The leading-order qualitative UV behaviour of

\begin{split}Z(x)\,x\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Z_{+}(x-{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}})&\mbox{ for }{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}}\lesssim x,\\

6
Summary and discussion

In this paper, starting from an integrable formulation of expectation values of local fields in the sine-Gordon model
Jimbo:2010jv
,
we proposed an explicit formula (
5.12

\frac{\langle\Phi_{\alpha+2\,m\frac{1-\nu}{\nu}}(0)\rangle}{\langle\Phi_{\alpha}(0)\rangle},\qquad m=1,2,...,\qquad 0\leq\alpha<2,\qquad 0<\nu<1,

expressed in terms of kink functions that describe the UV limit of the model.
The formula was obtained through analytical small-volume calculations and high-precision numerical analysis,
which together led us to its explicit form.

The leading coefficient can be interpreted as a diagonal

Importantly, unlike expressions written in terms of elementary or special functions, it does not admit an immediately evaluable closed-form expression.
Instead, it specifies how the leading coefficient can be systematically obtained from the solutions of the kink NLIE,
thereby establishing a concrete link between the integrable structure of the model and its conformal UV data.

The results can be applied to concrete twisted NLIE cases with

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),-}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}}\!dx\,e^{(2k-1)\,x}\,{\mathfrak{m}}_{as}(x-\log\tfrac{2}{\ell})\,{\cal G}_{1-2j}^{(\alpha),(-)}(x).

(A.32)

We first consider
\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),+}(\alpha).

Using the plateau-regime expansions (
5.1
) and (
5.4
), one obtains the following small-
\ell
series representations in the range
-{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}}<x<\infty
:

\begin{split}{\cal G}_{1-2j}^{(\alpha),(-)}(x+2\,{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}})&\simeq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{\ell}\right)^{\tfrac{2\alpha-4\,n}{p+1}}\,c_{n}^{(-(2j-1),\alpha)}\,e^{\tfrac{\alpha-2\,n}{p+1}x},\\
Z_{-}(x+2\,{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}})-z_{0}&\simeq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{4n}{p+1}}\,c_{n}\,e^{-\frac{2\,n\,x}{p+1}},\\
{\mathfrak{m}}_{as}(x+{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}})&={\mathfrak{m}}[Z_{+}(x)+Z_{-}(x+2\,{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}})-z_{0}]\simeq{\mathfrak{m}}_{+}(x)\!+\!\!\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{4n}{p+1}}\,{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}_{as,n}(x),\end{split}

(A.33)

where the coefficients
{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}_{as,n}(x)
can be expressed in terms of
c_{n}
and derivatives of the measure,

\frac{d^{s}}{dZ^{s}}{\mathfrak{m}}[Z(x)]\big|_{Z=Z_{+}},
although their explicit form is not needed for our purposes.

Substituting (
A.33
) into (
A.31
), one obtains the structure

\begin{split}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),+}(\alpha)\!\simeq\!\left(\frac{2}{\ell}\right)^{\!\!2(j+k-1)+\tfrac{2\alpha}{p+1}}\!\!\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{4\,n}{p+1}}\!\!\!\left\{c_{n}^{-(2j-1),\alpha)}\!\!\!\!\!\mathop{\int\limits_{-\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}}^{\infty}}\!\!\!\!dx\,e^{(2k-1)x}\,{\mathfrak{m}}_{+}(x)\,e^{\tfrac{\alpha-2\,n}{p+1}x}\!+\!\dots\!\!\right\}\!,\end{split}

(A.34)

where the dots denote terms proportional to
c_{s}^{(-(2j-1),\alpha)}
with
s\leq n-1.

The explicitly displayed contribution is precisely the one entering
\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}^{eff}(\alpha).

Comparing (
A.34
) with (
2.24
), it follows that one of the contributions
to the
n
th term of
\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)
originating from
\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),+}(\alpha)
is

c_{n}^{(-(2j-1),\alpha)}\,I_{n,0}^{(k)},
with
I_{n,0}^{(k)}
defined in (
5.8
).
Based on our numerical observations, the remaining terms do not contribute to the
vacuum expectation values at leading order in the UV limit. Therefore,

c_{n}^{(-(2j-1),\alpha)}\,I_{n,0}^{(k)}
is the only contribution of

\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),+}(\alpha)
to
\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j|n}^{eff}(\alpha)
.

As argued in
enjhep
the contributions arising from
\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),-}(\alpha)
are not consistent with the field-theoretic expectations for the small-
\ell
behaviour, and are therefore expected to cancel against contributions from other sources.

A.3
The computation of the
\ell\to 0
series of
\delta\tilde{\omega}^{(as)}_{kj}(\alpha)

Our starting point is (
A.23
). We first decompose it into two contributions:

\begin{split}\delta\tilde{\omega}^{(as)}_{kj}(\alpha)=\delta\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as)}(\alpha)+\delta\tilde{\omega}_{{\delta},kj}^{(as)}(\alpha).\end{split}

(A.35)

One of these terms involves only the leading-order asymptotic solutions:

\begin{split}{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as)}(\alpha)&=\left(\frac{2}{\ell}\right)^{\!\!2j-1}\!\mathop{\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}}\!dx\,{\cal G}_{1-2j}^{(\alpha),(-)}(x+\log\tfrac{2}{\ell})\,\left[{\mathfrak{m}}_{as}(x)-{\mathfrak{m}}_{-}(x+\log\tfrac{2}{\ell})\right]\,\times\\
&\times\left[\left(\frac{2}{\ell}\right)^{\!\!2k-1}\,{\cal G}_{2k-1}^{(-\alpha),(+)}(x-{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}})-e^{(2k-1)x}\right].\end{split}

(A.36)

The other term is proportional to the asymptotic correction:

\begin{split}{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{{\delta},kj}^{(as)}(\alpha)=\left(\frac{2}{\ell}\right)^{2j-1}\!\mathop{\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}}\!dx\,{\cal G}_{1-2j}^{(\alpha),(-)}(x+\log\tfrac{2}{\ell})\,\left[{\mathfrak{m}}_{as}(x)-{\mathfrak{m}}_{-}(x+\log\tfrac{2}{\ell})\right]\,\frac{{\delta}{\cal G}_{2k-1,m}^{(-\alpha),(as)}(x)}{{\mathfrak{m}}_{as}(x)}.\end{split}

(A.37)

Our numerical computations indicate that (
A.37
) does not contribute to the leading-order UV term of the vacuum expectation values. We therefore do not analyze its UV expansion analytically.
Thus, to obtain the leading-order contribution, only (
A.36
) needs to be considered. As explained in the previous section, we decompose it further into two integrals:

\begin{split}{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as)}(\alpha)={\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),+}(\alpha)+{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),-}(\alpha),\end{split}

(A.38)

where

\begin{split}{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),+}(\alpha)&\!=\!\!\left(\frac{2}{\ell}\right)^{\!\!2(j+k-1)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathop{\int\limits_{-\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}}^{\infty}}\!\!\!\!\!dx\,{\cal G}_{1-2j}^{(\alpha),(-)}(x+2\,\log\tfrac{2}{\ell})\,\left[{\mathfrak{m}}_{as}(x+{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}})-{\mathfrak{m}}_{-}(x+2\,\log\tfrac{2}{\ell})\right]\\
&\times\left[{\cal G}_{2k-1}^{(-\alpha),(+)}(x)-e^{(2k-1)x}\right],\end{split}

(A.39)

and

\begin{split}{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),-}(\alpha)\!&=\!\left(\frac{2}{\ell}\right)^{2j-1}\!\!\!\mathop{\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}}}\!\!dx\,{\cal G}_{1-2j}^{(\alpha),(-)}(x)\left[{\mathfrak{m}}_{as}(x-{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}})\!-\!{\mathfrak{m}}_{-}(x)\right]\!\\
&\times\,\left[\left(\frac{2}{\ell}\right)^{2k-1}\,{\cal G}_{2k-1}^{(-\alpha),(+)}(x-2\,{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}})\!-\!\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)^{2k-1}e^{(2k-1)x}\right].\end{split}

(A.40)

We begin with
{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),+}(\alpha).
Using the expansions collected in (
A.33
), one obtains:

\begin{split}{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),+}(\alpha)&=\left(\frac{2}{\ell}\right)^{2(j+k-1)+\tfrac{2\alpha}{p+1}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{4n}{p+1}}\bigg\{\,c_{n}^{(-(2j-1),\alpha)}\!\\
\times&\int\limits_{-{\log\tfrac{2}{\ell}}}^{\infty}\!dx\,\,e^{\tfrac{\alpha-2\,n}{p+1}x}\,\left({\mathfrak{m}}_{+}(x)-{\mathfrak{m}}[z_{0}]\right)\,\left({\cal G}_{2k-1}^{(-\alpha),(+)}(x)-e^{(2k-1)x}\right)+\dots\bigg\}.\end{split}

(A.41)

where the dots denote terms proportional to
c_{s}^{(-(2j-1),\alpha)}
with
s\leq n-1.

The explicitly displayed contribution is the one entering
\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}^{eff}(\alpha).

Comparing (
A.41
) with (
2.24
), it follows that one of the contributions
to the
n
th term of
\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)
originating from
{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),+}(\alpha)
is

c_{n}^{(-(2j-1),\alpha)}\,I_{n,1}^{(k)},
with
I_{n,1}^{(k)}
defined in (
5.8
).

Based on our numerical studies, the remaining terms, as well as the contributions coming from

{\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{0,kj}^{(as),-}(\alpha)
defined in (
A.40
), do not contribute to the vacuum expectation values
at leading order in the UV limit, and therefore do not contribute to

\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}^{eff}(\alpha).

Just to summarize, in this appendix we have analyzed a general term in the systematic UV expansion (
2.24
) of
\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)
. We focused on those contributions that are fully determined by the asymptotic solution (
3.12
). We showed that, at order
n
, among the large number of terms, one can naturally identify a specific contribution which, according to our numerical evidence, is the only one that contributes at leading order to the vacuum expectation values in the UV limit. By definition, these leading contributions form the quantity
\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}^{eff}(\alpha)
.

We emphasize that we do not derive the formula (
5.7
) for
\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}^{eff}(\alpha)
. What we establish is only that the terms appearing in (
5.7
) correspond to a single distinguished contribution within the full set of terms that make up the
n
th - order coefficient in the UV expansion of
\tilde{\omega}_{2k-1,1-2j}(\alpha)
, namely the contribution that determines the leading UV behaviour of vacuum expectation values.

We are not able to prove analytically that the remaining contributions cancel in the leading-order
UV expression for vacuum expectation values. This cancellation is supported solely by high-precision numerical evidence, with agreement up to at least 19-digit precision.

Appendix B
The large argument expansion of the kernel
G_{\alpha}(x)

The knowledge of the large argument series expansion of the kernel
G_{\alpha}(x)
(
2.19
), is useful for two reasons.
First, it offers an alternative useful series representation of the kernel for its numerical computation
in a regime, where the accurate numerical evaluation of the original Fourier-representation (
2.19
)
becomes challenging due to the highly oscillating nature of the integrand.

Second, such a series representation of
G_{\alpha}(x),
allows one to compute the qualitative large argument
behaviours of the kink functions from the linear equations they satisfy. Namely, the second series
representations in (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) can be derived from the equations
(
5.2
) and (
5.3
) respectively,
with the help of the large argument series of
G_{\alpha}(x).

The large-argument asymptotic expansion of
G_{\alpha}(x)
can be derived from its Fourier-integral representation (
2.19
) by means of the residue theorem.
We present the corresponding series expansions at
\pm\infty
, which take the form

\begin{split}G_{\alpha}(x)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x\to+\infty}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{C}_{k}^{(\alpha)}\,e^{-(1+2k)x}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\tilde{C}_{k}^{(\alpha)}\,e^{-\tfrac{2k-\alpha}{p}x},\qquad 0\leq\alpha<2,\\
G_{\alpha}(x)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x\to-\infty}}{{=}}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{C}_{-1-k}^{(\alpha)}\,e^{(1+2k)x}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{C}_{-k}^{(\alpha)}\,e^{\tfrac{2k+\alpha}{p}x},\qquad 0\leq\alpha<2,\\
\mbox{with}\qquad\hat{C}_{k}^{(\alpha)}&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\cot\left[(1+2k)\tfrac{\pi\,p}{2}+\alpha\right],\qquad\tilde{C}_{k}^{(\alpha)}=\frac{1}{p\,\pi}\tan\left[\tfrac{\pi}{2\,p}(2k-\alpha)\right].\end{split}

(B.1)

We note that throughout this paper we use the notation

G_{-\alpha}(x)=G_{\alpha}(-x)
, which is obtained by the formal
substitution
\alpha\to-\alpha
in the Fourier representation
(
2.19
).
This should not be confused with the analytic continuation
of the function
G_{\alpha}(x)
from positive to negative values
of
\alpha
, since the two procedures do not yield the same
result.
