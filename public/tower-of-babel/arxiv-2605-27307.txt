1
Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a finite nonempty family of
3
-element sets. After fixing an ordering of the underlying point set
V=\bigcup_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}T
, one obtains a signed pair-triple incidence matrix
\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}
; the rows are indexed by the members of
{\mathcal{T}},
the columns are indexed by two element subsets of members of
{\mathcal{T}},
and the entries are the usual boundary signs (see Section
2
for definitions). In this paper we study the smallest positive eigenvalue of the corresponding signed incidence Gram matrix:

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{T}}):=\lambda_{\min}^{+}\left(\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}\right),

as a spectral-combinatorial parameter of the set system
{\mathcal{T}}
. The matrix
\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}
is positive semidefinite, so,
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
is its spectral gap. Changing the total order on
V
results in a conjugation of
\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}
by an orthogonal matrix, hence,
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
is independent of the chosen total order on
V.
Equivalently, if the triples in
{\mathcal{T}}
are viewed as triangles of a graph with vertex set
V
, then
\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}
is the restricted edge up-Laplacian of the triangle family. Although the definition is spectral, the main results of the paper are extremal-combinatorial: large values of
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
force strong local overlap, complete triangle families give exact clique thresholds, and the exact budget function is nonmonotone between consecutive clique sizes.

Our main point is that
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
has a strong structural meaning. Let
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
denote the support graph of
{\mathcal{T}},
namely the graph formed by all vertices and edges contained in at least one triangle of
{\mathcal{T}}.
We prove that when
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
is large, every edge of
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
lies in many triangles in
{\mathcal{T}},
and consequently,
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
has large minimum degree. In particular, every support edge lies in at least
\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-2
triangles and every vertex of
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
has degree at least
\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-1.
This is the first theme of the paper.

Our second theme is extremal. For each positive integer
t,
define:

\displaystyle\phi(t)=\max_{|{\mathcal{T}}|=t}\lambda({\mathcal{T}}),\qquad\Lambda(t)=\max_{1\leq s\leq t}\phi(s).

The natural benchmark is the clique threshold
t=\binom{n}{3}.
If
{\mathcal{T}}
is the full triangle set of
K_{n}
, then
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})=n.
We show that this is the best possible: if
|{\mathcal{T}}|<\binom{n}{3},
then
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\leq n-1,
while if
|{\mathcal{T}}|=\binom{n}{3}
and
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})>n-1,
then
{\mathcal{T}}
must in fact be the full triangle set on an
n
-clique. Thus,
\binom{n}{3}
is the exact threshold for reaching level
n
.

We then show that the behavior immediately above this threshold is already nontrivial, and in fact, persists on the natural scale of consecutive clique thresholds. In particular, there is an explicit forbidden interval immediately after
\binom{n}{3}
on which
\phi(t)\leq n-1.
So although
K_{n}
reaches level
n
, this level does not persist even for the next admissible triangle counts. More strongly, the first passage above level
n-1
after
\binom{n}{3}
is delayed by
\Theta(n^{2})=\Theta\left(\binom{n+1}{3}-\binom{n}{3}\right)
additional triangles. This shows that the clique counts
\binom{n}{3}
are the correct horizontal scale for the extremal problem. This gap phenomenon shows that the clique construction alone does not describe the behavior immediately above the threshold. In particular,

\displaystyle\phi\left(\binom{n}{3}\right)=n,\quad\phi\left(\binom{n}{3}+1\right)\leq n-1,\quad\phi\left(\binom{n+1}{3}\right)=n+1.

At the global scale, we determine the exact behavior of the monotone envelope
\Lambda.
More precisely, we prove that

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)=\max\left\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:\binom{n}{3}\leq t\right\}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)=\Theta(t^{\frac{1}{3}}).

Thus the largest possible value of
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
grows only on the cubic-root scale in the number of triangles, and in fact the monotone envelope
\Lambda
admits an exact staircase description. By contrast, the exact-budget extremal function is highly nonmonotone. Nevertheless, we prove that:

\displaystyle\phi(t)=\Theta(t^{\frac{1}{3}}).

In particular,
\phi(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty.

Algebraic Connectivity and
\lambda

One useful way to interpret

the usual graph Laplacian of

the second smallest eigenvalue of

If

Denote by

the vertex connectivity of

) for a connected, non-complete graph

\displaystyle\mu_{2}\leq\kappa(G)\leq d_{\min}(G).

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a nonempty set of
3
-element subsets and
\Gamma
the support graph of
{\mathcal{T}}.
For each edge

of

denote by

the number of triangles in

One of our first results regarding

\displaystyle\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-2\leq\,\min\{d_{e}({\mathcal{T}}):e\in E(\Gamma)\},\quad\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-1\leq d_{\min}(\Gamma),

and both inequalities are sharp for complete graphs on at least

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{1}=\{\{1,2,3\}\},\quad{\mathcal{T}}_{2}=\{\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,4\}\},\quad{\mathcal{T}}_{3}=\{\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,4\},\{1,3,4\}\},

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{T}}_{1})=3,\quad\lambda({\mathcal{T}}_{2})=2,\quad\lambda({\mathcal{T}}_{3})=1,\quad\lambda({\mathcal{T}}_{4})=4.

Laplacian and incidence matrix viewpoints.

The linear algebraic operators used in this paper are familiar from two standard settings, but here they are used in a different extremal role. Higher-dimensional Laplacians, originating in Eckmann’s discrete Hodge theory
[
5
]
and appearing in later combinatorial formulations such as Duval–Reiner
[
4
]
, Horak–Jost
[
9
]
, and Lim
[
13
]
, are usually studied as invariants of a fixed simplicial complex. In that setting, one asks how spectral gaps control expansion, isoperimetry, mixing, or homological vanishing; see, for example, Cheeger-type inequalities and related vanishing results for simplicial complexes
[
10
,
14
]
, as well as Lew’s sharp lower bounds under missing-face minimal-degree hypotheses
[
12
]
. There is also a parallel linear-algebraic tradition in extremal set theory and design theory, where one studies ranks,
\bmod\,p
ranks, singular values, or spectra of incidence and inclusion matrices of set systems
[
6
,
11
,
7
]
. Here, the signed edge-triangle incidence matrix is used to define the spectral parameter itself: we vary the triple family
{\mathcal{T}}
and study how large
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
can be, and what combinatorial properties are forced by a large value of
\lambda({\mathcal{T}}).
The parameter
\lambda
also arises naturally from the total edge Hodge Laplacian:

\displaystyle L_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}=\delta_{0}\delta_{0}^{T}+\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}.

A basic identity shows that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1})=\min\big\{\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L),\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\big\},

where
L
is the usual graph Laplacian. Thus, once the graph Laplacian is not the bottleneck, the problem of controlling the smallest positive eigenvalue of the total edge Laplacian reduces exactly to controlling
\lambda({\mathcal{T}}).
This gives a direct motivation for treating
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
as an object in its own right.

Taken together, our results show that
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
is a meaningful structural parameter for triangle families: large values force overlap and a support graph of large minimum degree, clique thresholds are rigid, and there is a genuine forbidden region immediately above each clique threshold, while the associated monotone envelope admits an exact global description. More broadly, this paper illustrates that higher-dimensional Laplacian eigenvalues can encode concrete combinatorial constraints, much as classical spectral parameters do in graph theory.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces notation and records the linear-algebraic facts used later. In Section
3
, we present the main combinatorial results. Subsection
3.1
proves the main structural consequences of large

. Subsection
3.2
establishes the exact clique-threshold rigidity and the exact formula for
\Lambda.
Subsection
3.3
proves the forbidden interval and nonmonotonicity results. In subsection
3.4
, we give an explicit description of the clique
2
-down Laplacian of the graph
K_{c}\vee\overline{K_{b}}
constructed by joining
b
disjoint vertices to each vertex in a copy of
K_{c}.
In subsection
3.5
, we use this explicit value along with a Frobenius type construction to derive the exact growth rate of
\phi.
Section
4
concludes with some further questions about the finer structure of extremal and near-extremal triangle families.

We now precisely state and prove the min-gap identity that demonstrates that studying
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
is the right lens through which to view the optimization problem of

.

G
be a graph,
{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1})=\min\big\{\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L),\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\big\}.

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1})=\min\big\{\lambda_{2}(L),\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\big\}.

It suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1})=\min\big\{\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow}),\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\big\},

(1)

since the nonzero part of the spectra of

is the same as the nonzero part of the spectra of

. Since

is the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1})

\displaystyle=\min_{x\perp\ker(L_{1}),\ \|x\|=1}x^{T}L_{1}x

\displaystyle=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\im\delta_{0},\ g\in\im\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T},\\
\|f\|^{2}+\|g\|^{2}=1\end{subarray}}(f+g)^{T}(\delta_{0}\delta_{0}^{T}+\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}})(f+g)

(by Theorem

(c))

\displaystyle=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\im\delta_{0},\ g\in\im\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T},\\
\|f\|^{2}+\|g\|^{2}=1\end{subarray}}f^{T}(\delta_{0}\delta_{0}^{T})f+g^{T}(\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}})g

(

2.1
(b))

\displaystyle=\min_{0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 1,\ \alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=1}\Big(\min_{f\in\im\delta_{0},\ \|f\|=\alpha}f^{T}L_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow}f\ +\ \min_{g\in\im\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T},\ \|g\|=\beta}g^{T}L_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\uparrow}g\Big)

\displaystyle(\ker L_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\uparrow})^{\perp}=(\ker\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}})^{\perp}=\im\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T},

2.2
The
\lambda

In this short subsection, we formalize the notion of the
\lambda

Definition 2.3

(Support graph)
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a nonempty finite set of
3
-element subsets of a totally ordered set
X.
The support graph of
{\mathcal{T}}
, denoted
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
, is the graph whose vertex set is
\bigcup_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}T
, and two distinct vertices
x,y
are adjacent if and only if there is a
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
with
x,y\in T.

In particular, every vertex and every edge in
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
is contained in at least one triangle in
{\mathcal{T}}.
The above discussion motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a nonempty finite set of
3
-element subsets of a totally ordered set
X,
and let
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
be the support graph for
{\mathcal{T}}.
We define
\lambda({\mathcal{T}}):=\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\uparrow}).

Finally, we note that
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})=\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{2,\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}),{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow})
is independent of the ordering of the vertex set of
V(\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}))=\bigcup_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}T
, because changing the total order on
V
results in conjugating
L_{2,\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}),{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow}
by a diagonal orthogonal matrix (see Lemma A1 in
[
3
]
). Thus, we may view
\lambda
as a spectral parameter of finite families of
3
-element subsets, independent of the total order on the vertex set and of the host graph.

This gives the motivation for treating
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
as a parameter of the triangle family
{\mathcal{T}}
itself, not the underlying graph.

3
Extremality, Rigidity, and Compression

3.1
Large Minimum Degree Subgraphs and Triangle Overlaps

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph. We denote by
d_{\min}(G)
the minimum vertex degree in
G:

\displaystyle d_{\min}(G)=\min_{v\in V(G)}d_{G}(v).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a nonempty finite set of
3
-element subsets of some totally ordered set
X
, and let
n
be a positive integer such that
n\leq\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil.
Let
G
denote
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}).
Then,

(a)

for every edge
e=\{x,y\}
in
G
, there are at least
(n-2)
triangles in
{\mathcal{T}}
containing the edge
e,

(b)

for every edge
e=\{x,y\}
in
G
, we have
|N_{G}(x)\cap N_{G}(y)|\geq(n-2),

(c)

for every vertex
x
in
G,
there are at least
(n-2)
triangles in
{\mathcal{T}}
containing
x
as a vertex, and
d_{G}(x)\geq(n-1)
and,

(d)

d_{\min}(G)\geq(n-1)
and
|V(G)|\geq n.

Proof.

The statements are immediate for
n=1
and
n=2
. We assume
n\geq 3
. Let
1_{e}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{E(G)}
be the indicator vector of
e
. Let
0<d_{e}=|\{T\in{\mathcal{T}}:e\text{ is an edge of }T\}|
, and
g=\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}(1_{e}).
Then,

\displaystyle g=\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}(1_{e})\in\im\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}=\im L_{2,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow}=\left(\ker L_{2,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow}\right)^{\perp}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{T}})=\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\uparrow})=\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{2,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow})\leq\frac{\langle g,L_{2,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow}g\rangle}{\|g\|^{2}}=\frac{\|\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}g\|^{2}}{\|g\|^{2}}.

Now,
g
has exactly
d_{e}
nonzero coordinates, each
\pm 1.
So,
\|g\|^{2}=d_{e}.
On the other hand, the triangles in
{\mathcal{T}}
containing the edge
e
share the edge
e,
and each has a vertex and two edges not contained in any other triangle in
{\mathcal{T}}
containing the edge
e.
Thus,
(\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}g)_{e}=\sum_{e\subset T\in{\mathcal{T}}}[T:e]^{2}=d_{e},
and,
\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}g
has exactly
2d_{e}
additional nonzero coordinates, each with entries
\pm 1.
Consequently,
\|\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}g\|^{2}=d_{e}^{2}+2d_{e}.
We thus obtain:

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\leq\frac{\|\delta_{1,{\mathcal{T}}}^{T}g\|^{2}}{\|g\|^{2}}=\frac{d_{e}^{2}+2d_{e}}{d_{e}}=d_{e}+2.

Since
d_{e}
is a positive integer, we conclude that
n\leq\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil\leq d_{e}+2.
This proves part (a). Part (b) follows from part (a) by observing that any edge
e
in
G
is contained in at least
(n-2)
triangles in
{\mathcal{T}}
, and the third vertices of these triangles are pairwise distinct, and are common neighbors of
x
and
y
. To prove part (c), note that
x
is contained in at least one edge in
G
, say,
e.
Then, by part (a), there are at least
(n-2)
triangles in
{\mathcal{T}}
containing the edge
e
, and each of these triangles also contain the vertex
x.
Since two distinct triangles in
G
can share at most one edge, each of these triangles contains an edge distinct from
e
that is incident with
x.
The other endpoints of these edges are pairwise distinct, and distinct from
x
. Thus,
d_{G}(x)\geq(n-2)+1=n-1
. This proves part (c). Part (d) follows from part (c).
∎

Theorem
3.2
is the main local structural engine of this paper. It shows that a large spectral gap
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
forces every support edge to have large codegree, and therefore prevents a sparse or weakly overlapping support graph. In what follows,
{\mathcal{T}}
will be assumed to be a nonempty finite set of
3
-element subsets of a totally ordered set
X
, unless otherwise stated.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a graph,
{\mathcal{T}}
a nonempty set of triangles in
G,
and
\lambda=\lambda({\mathcal{T}}).
Then,
G
has a subgraph
H
, namely
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}),
such that
|V(H)|\geq\lceil\lambda\rceil
,
d_{\min}(H)\geq\lceil\lambda\rceil-1,
and every edge in
H
is contained in at least
(\lceil\lambda\rceil-2)
triangles in
H
.

Proof.

Let
H=\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}).
Then, by Theorem
3.2
,
H
is a subgraph of
G
with the desired properties.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a graph with at least one triangle and let
L_{1}^{\uparrow}
be the edge up-Laplacian of the clique complex of
G.
Denote
\lambda=\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1}^{\uparrow}(G)).
Let
H
be the subgraph of
G
consisting of the vertices and edges of
G
that are contained in at least one triangle in
G
. Then,
|V(H)|\geq\lceil\lambda\rceil
,
d_{\min}(H)\geq\lceil\lambda\rceil-1,
and every edge in
H
is contained in at least
(\lceil\lambda\rceil-2)
triangles in
H
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be the set of all triangles in a graph
G.
Then,
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})=\lambda_{\min}^{+}(L_{1}^{\uparrow}(G))=\lambda,
and so,
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})=H
satisfies the desired properties by Theorem
3.3
.
∎

3.2
Rigidity and Extremal Constructions

Theorem
3.2
gives local density around each vertex and edge in the support graph. The next counting lemma gives an upper bound on the number of vertices in
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
in terms of
|{\mathcal{T}}|
and
\lambda({\mathcal{T}}),
which will be crucial in the proofs of the rigidity results.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
|{\mathcal{T}}|=t>0,\ \lambda:=\lambda({\mathcal{T}})>2,
and
G=\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}).
If
v=|V(G)|
and
e=|E(G)|,
then:

\displaystyle v\leq\frac{2e}{\lceil\lambda\rceil-1},\quad e\leq\frac{3t}{\lceil\lambda\rceil-2},\quad\text{and, }v\leq\frac{6t}{(\lceil\lambda\rceil-1)(\lceil\lambda\rceil-2)}.

Proof.

Denote
n=\lceil\lambda\rceil
. By Theorem
3.2
, each vertex
x
is contained in at least
(n-1)
edges in
G
. Thus,

\displaystyle v(n-1)\leq\sum_{x\in V(G)}d_{G}(x)=2e\text{, or, }v\leq\frac{2e}{n-1}.

To prove the second inequality, we count the number of pairs
(p,T)
, where
p\in E(G),T\in{\mathcal{T}},
and
p
is an edge of
T.
By Theorem
3.2
, each edge
p
of
G
is contained in at least
(n-2)
triangles in
{\mathcal{T}}
, thus, the number of such pairs is at least
e(n-2).
On the other hand, each triangle contains exactly three edges in
G
. Thus, the number of such pairs is exactly
3t.
Thus,

\displaystyle e(n-2)\leq 3t,\text{ or, }e\leq\frac{3t}{n-2}.

Combining these two inequalities, we obtain:

\displaystyle v\leq\frac{2}{(n-1)(n-2)}(e(n-2))\leq\frac{6t}{(n-1)(n-2)},

as needed.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

For positive integer
t
, we define:

\displaystyle\phi(t)=\max_{|{\mathcal{T}}|=t}\lambda({\mathcal{T}}),

and,

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)=\max_{1\leq s\leq t}\phi(s).

Here,
\phi
is well defined, since every family
{\mathcal{T}}
of size
t
uses at most
3t
vertices, hence, up-to relabeling, there are only finitely many such families.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
n\geq 3
be a positive integer. If
|{\mathcal{T}}|<\binom{n}{3}
, then
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\leq n-1.
Furthermore, if
|{\mathcal{T}}|=\binom{n}{3}
and
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})>n-1,
then
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})=n
,
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
is isomorphic to
K_{n}
, and the triangles in
{\mathcal{T}}
are precisely the set of all possible triangles on
V(\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})).

Proof.

We prove the first statement first. Assume on the contrary that
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})>n-1.
Then,
\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil\geq n
. By Lemma
3.5
,

\displaystyle|V(\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}))|\leq\frac{6|{\mathcal{T}}|}{(\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-1)(\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-2)}<\frac{6\binom{n}{3}}{(n-1)(n-2)}=n.

On the other hand, by Theorem
3.2
, we have
|V(\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}))|\geq\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil\geq n,
which is a contradiction. This proves the first statement.

Assume now that
|{\mathcal{T}}|=\binom{n}{3}
and
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})>n-1
. Let
G=\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
and
v=|V(G)|.
By Theorem
3.2
, we have
v\geq\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil\geq n.
On the other hand, by Lemma
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle v\leq\frac{6|{\mathcal{T}}|}{(\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-1)(\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-2)}\leq\frac{6\binom{n}{3}}{(n-1)(n-2)}=n.

Thus,
v=n.
Since
|{\mathcal{T}}|=\binom{n}{3}
and each triangle
T
in
{\mathcal{T}}
is a triangle in
G,
we have that
{\mathcal{T}}
is the set of all possible triangles in
G,
and
G\cong K_{n}.
Finally, by Example 2.3 in
[
1
]
, we have
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})=n,
as needed.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

For any positive integer
n\geq 3,

\displaystyle\phi\left(\binom{n}{3}\right)=n,

and up to relabeling of the vertices, the unique
{\mathcal{T}}
achieving this is the full set of triangles in
K_{n}.
Equivalently,

\displaystyle\min\{|{\mathcal{T}}|:\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\geq n\}=\binom{n}{3},

and the unique minimizer is the full triangle set of
K_{n}.

Proof.

By standard computation of the spectrum of
L_{1}^{\uparrow}(K_{n})
(see Example 2.3 in
[
1
]
), the full set of triangles
{\mathcal{T}}
of
K_{n}
satisfies
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})=n.
This shows that
\phi\left(\binom{n}{3}\right)\geq n.
On the other hand, if
|{\mathcal{T}}|=\binom{n}{3}
and
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})>n-1,
then, by Theorem
3.7
,
{\mathcal{T}}
must be the full triangle set of
K_{n},
and so,
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})=n.
This proves that
\phi\left(\binom{n}{3}\right)=n.
Furthermore, by Theorem
3.7
, we have
\phi(t)<n
whenever
t<\binom{n}{3}.
This proves the second statement.
∎

We have the two following results as immediate corollaries.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
t\geq 3
be a positive integer. Then,

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)=\max\left\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:\binom{n}{3}\leq t\right\},

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle\text{for every }n\in{\mathbb{N}},\ \binom{n}{3}\leq t<\binom{n+1}{3}\implies\Lambda(t)=n.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)=\Theta(t^{\frac{1}{3}}).

Proof.

It suffices to prove the first two statements. In Corollary
3.8
, we have proved that
\min\{|{\mathcal{T}}|:\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\geq n\}=\binom{n}{3}
and that
\phi\left(\binom{n}{3}\right)=n.
This proves the first statement. Now, applying Theorem
3.7
with
n+1,
we have that
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\leq n
whenever
|{\mathcal{T}}|<\binom{n+1}{3}.
Therefore, if
t<\binom{n+1}{3},
then
\Lambda(t)\leq n.
On the other hand,
\phi\left(\binom{n}{3}\right)=n,
and so,
\Lambda(t)\geq n
whenever
t\geq\binom{n}{3}.
Thus, if
\binom{n}{3}\leq t<\binom{n+1}{3},
then
\Lambda(t)=n.

∎

Remark 1
.

Let
t\geq 1.
If
n
is the unique positive integer such that
\binom{n}{3}\leq t<\binom{n+1}{3},
then by Theorem
3.9
and a simple algebraic manipulation, we obtain:

\displaystyle(6t)^{\frac{1}{3}}-1\leq n\leq\Lambda(t)<n+1<(6t)^{\frac{1}{3}}+3,\text{ or, }(6t)^{\frac{1}{3}}-1\leq\Lambda(t)<(6t)^{\frac{1}{3}}+3.

3.3
Forbidden intervals and nonmonotonicity
\phi

In this subsection, we study the exact budget extremal function

\displaystyle\phi(t)=\max_{|{\mathcal{T}}|=t}\lambda({\mathcal{T}}).

inside the interval
\binom{n}{3}<t<\binom{n+1}{3}.
We first observe that for all sufficiently large positive integer
n
, there is a genuine forbidden interval on which
\phi(t)\leq(n-1),
and hence
\phi
is nonmonotone. Recall that every positive integer
t\in\left(\binom{n}{3},\binom{n+1}{3}\right)
may be written uniquely in one (and only one) of the following forms:

\displaystyle t=\binom{n}{3}+\binom{s}{2},\quad 2\leq s\leq n-1,\text{ or, }

\displaystyle t=\binom{n}{3}+\binom{s}{2}+p,\quad 1\leq p<s\leq n.

Theorem
3.7
identifies the exact first budget where level
n
can be reached. The next result shows that this first appearance is isolated rather than persistent: immediately above
\binom{n}{3},
there is an interval on which
\phi
is strictly below
n,
in fact, on this interval,
\phi\leq n-1.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
n\geq 3
be a positive integer, and
m\geq 1
be the largest positive integer such that
\frac{3}{2}(m-1)(m+2)<\binom{n-1}{2}.
Then, for any
\binom{n}{3}+1\leq t\leq\binom{n}{3}+\binom{m+1}{2}-1
, we have
\phi(t)\leq n-1.

Proof.

Fix
\binom{n}{3}+1\leq t\leq\binom{n}{3}+\binom{m+1}{2}-1
, and assume on the contrary that there exists
{\mathcal{T}}
with
|{\mathcal{T}}|=t
and
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})>n-1.
Then,
\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil\geq n.
Let
G=\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
and
v=|V(G)|.
Since the number of triangles in
G
is greater than
\binom{n}{3}
, we have
v\geq n+1.
By Lemma
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle v\leq\frac{6t}{(\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-1)(\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-2)}\leq\frac{6t}{(n-1)(n-2)}.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(m-1)(m-2)\leq\frac{3}{2}(m-1)(m+2)<\binom{n-1}{2}\implies m\leq n-1.

Consequently,
t\leq\binom{n}{3}+\binom{n}{2}-1<\binom{n+1}{3}.
Thus,

1.

either
t=\binom{n}{3}+\binom{s}{2}
for some
2\leq s\leq n-1,
or,

2.

t=\binom{n}{3}+\binom{s}{2}+p
for some
1\leq p<s\leq n-1.

Consider the first case. In this case, we further have
s\leq m
since
t<\binom{n}{3}+\binom{m+1}{2}.
In particular,

\displaystyle 6\binom{s}{2}\leq 6\binom{m}{2}\leq 3(m-1)(m+2)=2\cdot\frac{3}{2}(m-1)(m+2)<2\binom{n-1}{2}=(n-1)(n-2).

Thus,

\displaystyle n+1\leq v\leq\frac{6t}{(n-1)(n-2)}=\frac{6\left(\binom{n}{3}+\binom{s}{2}\right)}{(n-1)(n-2)}=n+\frac{6\binom{s}{2}}{(n-1)(n-2)}<n+1,

a contradiction. Thus,
t
cannot be of this form.

Consider now the case
t=\binom{n}{3}+\binom{s}{2}+p
for some
1\leq p<s\leq n-1.
In particular,
s\leq m
and
t\leq\binom{n}{3}+\binom{s}{2}+(s-1)\leq\binom{n}{3}+\binom{m}{2}+(m-1).
We then have:

\displaystyle 6\left(\binom{m}{2}+(m-1)\right)=3(m-1)(m+2)<2\binom{n-1}{2}=(n-1)(n-2).

Thus,

\displaystyle n+1\leq v\leq\frac{6t}{(n-1)(n-2)}\leq\frac{6\binom{n}{3}+3(m-1)(m+2)}{(n-1)(n-2)}=n+\frac{3(m-1)(m+2)}{(n-1)(n-2)}<n+1,

a contradiction. Thus, we conclude
t
cannot have this form either. The proof concludes.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

For
n\geq 6,
we have

\displaystyle\phi\left(\binom{n}{3}+1\right)\leq n-1.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
n\geq 9
, and let
\binom{n}{3}<|{\mathcal{T}}|<\binom{n+1}{3}
and
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})>n-1.
Then,
n+1\leq|V(\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}))|\leq n+3.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
and the hypothesis
|{\mathcal{T}}|>\binom{n}{3}
, we have:

\displaystyle n+1\leq|V(\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}))|\leq\frac{6|{\mathcal{T}}|}{(\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-1)(\lceil\lambda({\mathcal{T}})\rceil-2)}<\frac{6\binom{n+1}{3}}{(n-1)(n-2)}=(n+3)+\frac{6}{n-2}<n+4.

The proof follows.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

The function
\phi
is nonmonotone. Indeed, for every positive integer
n\geq 6
, if we set

\displaystyle n_{1}=\binom{n}{3},\quad n_{2}=\binom{n}{3}+1,\quad n_{3}=\binom{n+1}{3},

then
n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{3},
and

\displaystyle\phi(n_{1})=n,\quad\phi(n_{2})\leq n-1,\quad\phi(n_{3})=n+1.

Hence,

\displaystyle\phi(n_{2})<\phi(n_{1})<\phi(n_{3}).

Definition 3.14
.

Let
n\geq 3
be a positive integer. Denote:

\displaystyle H(n)=\min\left\{s\in{\mathbb{N}}:\phi\left(\binom{n}{3}+s\right)>n-1\right\}.

By Corollary
3.8
,
H(n)\leq\binom{n+1}{3}-\binom{n}{3}=\binom{n}{2}.
Thus,
H(n)
is defined and
H(n)=O(n^{2}).

Theorem 3.15
.

For all sufficiently large positive integers
n
, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}}{6}-\frac{5n}{2\sqrt{3}}+3<H(n)\leq\binom{n}{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle H(n)=\Theta\left(\binom{n+1}{3}-\binom{n}{3}\right)=\Theta(n^{2}).

Proof.

Denote
m=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{\sqrt{3}}-2\right\rfloor.
Then,

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}(m-1)(m+2)\leq\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{3}}-3\right)\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{3}}\right)=\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{3\sqrt{3}n}{2}<\binom{n-1}{2}.

Thus,
m
satisfies the inequality in Theorem
3.10
, and thus,
H(n)\geq\binom{m+1}{2}.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\binom{m+1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{3}}-3\right)\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{3}}-2\right)=\frac{n^{2}}{6}-\frac{5n}{2\sqrt{3}}+3.

Thus,

\displaystyle H(n)\geq\binom{m+1}{2}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{6}-\frac{5n}{2\sqrt{3}}+3.

However, since the right hand side is irrational, the inequality above is strict. This proves the theorem.
∎

3.4
The
2
-down Laplacian Spectrum of
K_{c}\vee\overline{K_{b}}

In this section, we explicitly compute the spectrum of the graph
K_{c}\vee\overline{K_{b}}
constructed by joining
b
pairwise disjoint vertices to a copy of
K_{c}.
Taking
{\mathcal{T}}
to be the full triangle family of this graph, we thus obtain
\lambda({\mathcal{T}}).
This explicit value will be crucial in our discussions in the next section. In this section, we will identify the edges and triangles in a graph
G
with the corresponding standard basis vector, when the ambient vector space is clear.

Definition 3.16
.

Let
c\geq 3,b\geq 1
be positive integers. Denote by
G_{c,b}
the graph on vertex set
\{1,\dots,b+c\},
and edge set:

\displaystyle\big\{\{i,j\}:1\leq i<j\leq c\big\}\ \bigcup\ \big\{\{i,j\}:c+1\leq i\leq c+b,\ 1\leq j\leq c\big\}.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
c\geq 3,b\geq 1
be positive integers. Denote by
{\mathcal{T}}_{c,b}
the set:

\displaystyle\big\{\{i,j,k\}:\{i,j,k\}\text{ is a }3-\text{clique in }G_{c,b}\}.

Our goal in this subsection is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
c\geq 3,b\geq 1
be positive integers. Let
L_{2,G_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}
denote the clique
2
-down Laplacian of
G_{c,b}.
Then, the spectrum of
L_{2,G_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&c&b+c\\
\binom{c}{3}+(b-1)\binom{c-1}{2}&(b-1)(c-1)&\binom{c}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

where eigenvalues with multiplicity
0
are omitted.

Since
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}_{c,b})=G_{c,b},
we have the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 3.19
.

Let
c\geq 3,b\geq 1
be positive integers. Then,

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{T}}_{c,b})=\begin{cases}c+1,&b=1\\
c,&b\geq 2.\end{cases}.

Note that
{\mathcal{T}}_{c,1}
is the full set of triangles on a copy of
K_{c+1}
and so,
\lambda({\mathcal{T}}_{c,1})=c+1.

We split the proof of Theorem
3.18
into several propositions.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
c\geq 3,b\geq 2
be positive integers. Let
L_{2,G_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}
denote the clique edge down-Laplacian of
G_{c,b}.
Then,
c
is an eigenvalue of
L_{2,G_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}
with multiplicity at least
(b-1)(c-1)
, and
(b-1)(c-1)
linearly independent eigenvectors of
L_{2,G_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}
associated with the eigenvalue
c
are given by:

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq c,\ i\neq x}([\{i,x,y\}:\{x,y\}]\cdot\{i,x,y\}-[\{i,x,b+c\}:\{x,b+c\}]\cdot\{i,x,b+c\}),

for
2\leq x\leq c,\ c+1\leq y\leq b+c-1.

Proof.

Denote
G=G_{c,b}
,
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{T}}_{c,b}
,
\delta_{1}=\delta_{1,G,{\mathcal{T}}},

L_{2}^{\downarrow}=L_{2,G,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow},
and
L_{1}^{\uparrow}=L_{1,G,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\uparrow}.
Fix
2\leq x\leq c
and
c+1\leq y\leq b+c-1,
and denote

\displaystyle v_{x,y}:=\sum_{1\leq i\leq c,\ i\neq x}([\{i,x,y\}:\{x,y\}]\cdot\{i,x,y\}-[\{i,x,b+c\}:\{x,b+c\}]\cdot\{i,x,b+c\})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}.

We prove that
v:=v_{x,y}
is an eigenvector of
L_{2}^{\downarrow}
with eigenvalue
c.
For any edge
e
in
G
, we have:

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{1}^{T}v\right)_{e}=\begin{cases}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq c,\\
i\neq x\end{subarray}}[\{i,x,y\}:\{x,y\}]^{2}=(c-1),&e=\{x,y\}\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq c,\\
i\neq x\end{subarray}}-[\{i,x,b+c\}:\{x,b+c\}]^{2}=-(c-1),&e=\{x,b+c\}\\
[\{i,x,y\}:\{x,y\}]\cdot[\{i,x,y\}:\{i,y\}]=-[\{i,x,y\}:\{i,x\}]=-1,&e=\{i,y\};\\
&i\in[c],i\neq x\\
-[\{i,x,b+c\}:\{x,b+c\}]\cdot[\{i,x,b+c\}:\{i,b+c\}]&e=\{i,b+c\};\\

\left(L_{2}^{\downarrow}v\right)_{T}=\left(\delta_{1}\left(\delta_{1}^{T}v\right)\right)_{T}
is given by:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}0=c(v_{x,y})_{T},&T\subset[c]\\

G
, we conclude that

is an eigenvector of
L_{2}^{\downarrow}
with eigenvalue
c.
Furthermore, in

the coordinate indexed by the triangle

is nonzero, while this coordinate is
0
for any
v_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}
if
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\neq(x,y)
. Thus, the constructed
(b-1)(c-1)
vectors

are linearly independent. This shows that
c
is an eigenvalue of
L_{2}^{\downarrow}
with multiplicity at least
(b-1)(c-1),
and the proposition follows.
∎

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
c\geq 3,b\geq 1
be positive integers. Let
L_{1,G_{c,b}}^{\uparrow}
denote the clique edge up-Laplacian of
G_{c,b}.
Then,
b+c
is an eigenvalue of
L_{1,G_{c,b}}^{\uparrow}
, and
\binom{c}{2}
linearly independent eigenvectors of
L_{1,G_{c,b}}^{\uparrow}
associated with the eigenvalue
b+c
are given by:

\displaystyle\{x,y\}+\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i=c+1}^{b+c}\left(-\{x,i\}+\{y,i\}\right)

for
x,y\in[c],\ x<y.

We provide a proof in Appendix
A
.

Since the nonzero part of the spectra of
L_{1}^{\uparrow}
and
L_{2}^{\downarrow}
coincide, we have the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 3.22
.

Let
c\geq 3,b\geq 1
be positive integers. Let
L_{2,G_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}
denote the clique
2
-down Laplacian of
G_{c,b}.
Then,
b+c
is an eigenvalue of
L_{2,G_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}
with multiplicity at least
\binom{c}{2}.

We can now prove Theorem
3.18
.

Proof of Theorem
3.18
.

If
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
, we have:

\displaystyle\left(L_{2,G_{c,b},{\mathcal{T}}_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}\right)_{T,T}=3.

Thus,

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left(L_{2,G_{c,b},{\mathcal{T}}_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}\right)=3|{\mathcal{T}}|=3\left(\binom{c}{3}+b\binom{c}{2}\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(b-1)(c-1)c+\binom{c}{2}(b+c)=3\left(\binom{c}{3}+b\binom{c}{2}\right)=\text{tr}\left(L_{2,G_{c,b},{\mathcal{T}}_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}\right).

Note that if
b=1
, then
(b-1)(c-1)c=0
, hence the omitted eigenvalue
c
does not contribute to the sum in this case. Since
L_{2,G_{c,b},{\mathcal{T}}_{c,b}}^{\downarrow}
is positive semidefinite, all remaining eigenvalues must be
0
. We obtain the multiplicity of the
0
eigenvalue as:

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{T}}|-(b-1)(c-1)-\binom{c}{2}=\binom{c}{3}+b\binom{c}{2}-(b-1)(c-1)-\binom{c}{2}=\binom{c}{3}+(b-1)\binom{c-1}{2}.

This proves the theorem.
∎

3.5
Growth rate of
\phi

In this subsection, we derive the explicit growth rate of
\phi.
Despite the highly nonmonotone characteristic of
\phi
, we observe that
\phi
has the same cubic root growth rate as
\Lambda.
Denote by
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
the set of non-negative integers. We need the following number theoretic lemma.

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{N}},a\geq 3
. For every positive integer
N\geq 3a\binom{a}{2}+2a(a-1),
there exist
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle N=x\binom{a}{2}+y\binom{a+1}{2}+z\binom{a+2}{2}.

Consequently, for every positive integer
N\geq 2a^{3}+2a^{2}+1,
there exist
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle N=\left(\binom{a}{3}+x\binom{a}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+1}{3}+y\binom{a+1}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+2}{3}+z\binom{a+2}{2}\right).

Proof.

For
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
denote:

\displaystyle I_{q}=\left\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:q\binom{a}{2}+2a(a-1)\leq n\leq q\binom{a}{2}+qa\right\}.

We claim that if
N\in I_{q},
then, there exist
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
x+y+z=q
and

\displaystyle N=x\binom{a}{2}+y\binom{a+1}{2}+z\binom{a+2}{2}.

To prove this claim, first observe that

\displaystyle 2a(a-1)\leq N-q\binom{a}{2}\leq qa.

However,
(a,2a+1)=1,
and
a(2a+1)-a-(2a+1)+1=2a(a-1).
By Frobenius, there exist
y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle N-q\binom{a}{2}=ya+z(2a+1)=y\left(\binom{a+1}{2}-\binom{a}{2}\right)+z\left(\binom{a+2}{2}-\binom{a}{2}\right).

Or,

\displaystyle N=(q-y-z)\binom{a}{2}+y\binom{a+1}{2}+z\binom{a+2}{2}.

However, since
N-q\binom{a}{2}\leq qa,
we have:

\displaystyle(y+z)a\leq ya+z(2a+1)\leq qa,\text{ or, }y+z\leq q.

That is,
q-y-z\geq 0.
Thus, taking
x=q-y-z\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},
we have the desired expression of arbitrary
N\in I_{q}.

Now, if
q\geq 3a,
then,

\displaystyle q\binom{a}{2}+2a(a-1)<(q+1)\binom{a}{2}+2a(a-1)\leq q\binom{a}{2}+qa.

Therefore, for
q\geq 3a,
we have
I_{q}\cap I_{q+1}\neq\emptyset.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\bigcup_{q=3a}^{\infty}I_{q}=\left\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:n\geq 3a\binom{a}{2}+2a(a-1)\right\}.

That is, if
N\geq 3a\binom{a}{2}+2a(a-1),
then there exist
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that:

\displaystyle N=x\binom{a}{2}+y\binom{a+1}{2}+z\binom{a+2}{2}.

Consequently, for every

\displaystyle N\geq 3a\binom{a}{2}+2a(a-1)+\sum_{i=0}^{2}\left(\binom{a+i}{3}+\binom{a+i}{2}\right)=2a^{3}+2a^{2}+1,

there exist
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that:

\displaystyle N=\left(\binom{a}{3}+x\binom{a}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+1}{3}+y\binom{a+1}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+2}{3}+z\binom{a+2}{2}\right).

∎

Theorem 3.24
.

For every positive integer
a\geq 3,

\displaystyle t\in{\mathbb{N}},t\geq 2a^{3}+2a^{2}+1\implies\phi(t)\geq a.

Consequently, for
t\in{\mathbb{N}},t\geq 81,

\displaystyle\phi(t)\geq\left\lfloor\left(\frac{t}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right\rfloor.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\phi(t)=\Theta(t^{\frac{1}{3}}).

Proof.

Let
a,N,x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}},a\geq 3
be such that

\displaystyle N=\left(\binom{a}{3}+x\binom{a}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+1}{3}+y\binom{a+1}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+2}{3}+z\binom{a+2}{2}\right).

Then, take
{\mathcal{T}}
to be the disjoint union of the full triangle families
{\mathcal{T}}_{a,x},{\mathcal{T}}_{a+1,y},
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{a+2,z}.
Then,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{T}}|=\left(\binom{a}{3}+x\binom{a}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+1}{3}+y\binom{a+1}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+2}{3}+z\binom{a+2}{2}\right)=N.

Let
G=\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
. Then,
L_{2,G,{\mathcal{T}}}^{\downarrow}
has a block diagonal form with three blocks, where the blocks correspond to the
2
-down Laplacian matrix of
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}_{a,x}),\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}_{a+1,y}),
and
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}}_{a+2,z}).
Thus,

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{T}})=\min\left\{\lambda({\mathcal{T}}_{a,x}),\lambda({\mathcal{T}}_{a+1,y}),\lambda({\mathcal{T}}_{a+2,z})\right\}\geq\min\{a,a+1,a+2\}\geq a.

Thus,
\phi(N)\geq a.

Now, by Lemma
3.23
, for fixed
a\in{\mathbb{N}},a\geq 3
, for any
t\geq 2a^{3}+2a^{2}+1
, there exist
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}
with

\displaystyle t=\left(\binom{a}{3}+x\binom{a}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+1}{3}+y\binom{a+1}{2}\right)+\left(\binom{a+2}{3}+z\binom{a+2}{2}\right).

By the observation above,
\phi(t)\geq a.
That is,
\phi(t)\geq a
whenever
t\geq 2a^{3}+2a^{2}+1.
This proves the first statement.

Now, fix
t\in{\mathbb{N}},t\geq 81.
Set
a=\left\lfloor\left(\frac{t}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right\rfloor
. Then,

\displaystyle 2a^{3}+2a^{2}+1<3a^{3}\leq 3\left(\left(\frac{t}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)^{3}=t.

By the discussions above,

\displaystyle\phi(t)\geq a=\left\lfloor\left(\frac{t}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right\rfloor.

Using the elementary fact that
x\in{\mathbb{R}},x\geq 1\implies\lfloor x\rfloor\geq\frac{x}{2},
we have that

\displaystyle\phi(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{t}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

This proves that
\phi(t)=\Omega(t^{\frac{1}{3}}).
Furthermore, by Theorem
3.9
,
\Lambda(t)=\Theta(t^{\frac{1}{3}}),
and
\phi(t)\leq\Lambda(t)
for every
t
by definition. Thus,
\phi(t)=O(t^{\frac{1}{3}}).
We thus conclude that
\phi(t)=\Theta(t^{\frac{1}{3}}).

∎

4
Conclusion

We studied the smallest positive eigenvalue
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
of the restricted edge up-Laplacian of a triangle set
{\mathcal{T}}
, treating it as a combinatorial parameter of the set itself. We showed that large
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
forces strong overlap among the triangles of
{\mathcal{T}},
and forces the support graph
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
to have a large minimum degree. We also proved that
\binom{n}{3}
is the exact threshold for attaining level
n
, with rigidity at equality, and that the threshold is followed by a genuine forbidden interval on which the extremal function
\phi(t)
remains at most
(n-1).
Moreover, at the global scale, we determine the exact behavior of the monotone envelope
\Lambda:

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)=\max\left\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:\binom{n}{3}\leq t\right\}.

Thus, the cubic-root growth law
\Lambda(t)=\Theta(t^{\frac{1}{3}})
appears as an immediate corollary, while the same growth rate of
\phi(t)=\Theta(t^{\frac{1}{3}})
required more sophisticated arguments.

Taken together, these results show that
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
already supports a meaningful structural and extremal picture: it detects local overlap, forces a support of large minimum degree, exhibits rigid clique thresholds, has a genuine forbidden region immediately above each clique threshold, and has a monotone envelope
\Lambda
with an exact staircase description.

Two natural problems remain open. First, one would like a more precise description of the extremal function inside each interval
\binom{n}{3}<t<\binom{n+1}{3},
especially the size and structure of the forbidden region immediately above
\binom{n}{3}.
Second, one would like a structural description of extremal and near-extremal families: when
\lambda({\mathcal{T}})
is large relative to
|{\mathcal{T}}|
, or close to the staircase level
\Lambda(|{\mathcal{T}}|)
, how close must the support graph
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
be to a clique or other rigid dense configuration? These questions indicate that the structural and extremal study of
\lambda
and
\phi
is only beginning.
