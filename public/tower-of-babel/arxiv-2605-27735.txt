An IQP Born Machine for Calorimeter Image Generation at 64 Qubits with Compiled-IQP Deployment
Jamal Slim, Saverio Monaco, Florian Rehm, Dirk Krücker, Kerstin Borras

Abstract
We train an instantaneous quantum polynomial-time (IQP) Born machine on real high-energy-physics calorimeter shower images at 64 64 qubits and compile the trained model into a single sampling-hard IQP circuit for quantum deployment. The pipeline has three components: a Mixture-of-IQP ( MoIQP ) architecture, whose Walsh-diagonal MMD 2 loss is classically trainable by Van den Nest Fourier Monte Carlo; the Pearson-Stabilized Correlation Kernel ( PSCK ), a positive-definite MMD kernel that biases descent toward correlation-sensitive directions through a data-evaluated Jacobian of the empirical Pearson matrix; and an exact deferred-measurement compilation of MoIQP into a single IQP circuit on n + ⌈ log 2 ⁡ L ⌉ n+\lceil\log_{2}L\rceil qubits ( cIQP ). Across five seeds at L = 8 L=8 , 1500 1500 epochs, the model reaches MAE ρ = 0.069 ± 0.008 \mathrm{MAE}_{\rho}=0.069\pm 0.008 against a 0.052 0.052 encoding-fidelity floor on the training split and 0.071 ± 0.008 0.071\pm 0.008 on a held-out test split, versus a Liu–Wang baseline at MAE ρ = 0.100 \mathrm{MAE}_{\rho}=0.100 . The compiled cIQP reproduces the MoIQP marginal to 0.591 ± 0.012 0.591\pm 0.012 times the Monte Carlo noise floor.

Introduction

Calorimeter shower simulation is one of the more demanding parts of the LHC analysis pipeline and is projected to consume millions of CPU-years per year during the high-luminosity run
calochallenge2024
;
atlas_atlfast3
. Classical generative surrogates have made a dent in this over the last five years: GANs
paganini2018calogan
;
chang2024qgan_clic
, normalizing flows
krause2023caloflow
;
buckley2024icaloflow
;
buss2025allshowers
, and diffusion models
mikuni2024caloscore_v2
;
favaro2025calodream
reach near-Geant4 fidelity with two to three orders of magnitude speed-up, and several are already in experiment software stacks. Quantum generative models have been proposed as a further alternative
delgado2022qcbm_hep
;
kiss2022conditional_born
;
chang2023full_qgan_hep
;
hoque2024caloqvae
, the motivation being that the Born-rule probability representation can express certain correlation structures with fewer parameters than a classical parameterization
coyle2020born_supremacy
.

The obstacle to scaling quantum generative models on near-term hardware is that variational training of generic circuit Born machines requires repeated quantum sampling and is prone to barren plateaus
mcclean2018bp
;
cerezo2021costfn
. A useful way around this was opened by restricting the model class to
instantaneous quantum polynomial-time
(IQP) circuits
shepherd2009iqp
;
bremner2011iqp_hardness
;
bremner2016bjs
. Two results make this class well suited to scalable generative modeling. Van den Nest
vandennest2010classical_sim
showed that expectation values of Pauli-
Z
words at the output of an IQP circuit can be estimated classically in time linear in the number of qubits and gates by a Fourier Monte Carlo algorithm. Rudolph et al.
rudolph2024trainability
then observed that the MMD
2
loss with a Walsh-diagonal kernel decomposes as a mixture of such Pauli-
Z
expectations. Together these give a fully classical training procedure for IQP Born machines. Sampling from the trained circuit, on the other hand, is believed to be classically intractable
bremner2011iqp_hardness
;
marshall2024iqp
, which is the point of the
train on classical, deploy on quantum
workflow
recio2025train_classical_deploy_quantum
.

That workflow has been explored recently on synthetic and biological data. Recio-Armengol et al.
recio2025train_classical_deploy_quantum
trained models up to
1000
qubits in classical simulation. Lerch et al.
kumar2026data_dependent_init
trained
150
-qubit models on genomic data with a data-dependent initialization scheme. Balló-Gimbernat et al.
ballo2025shallow_iqp_graph
ran trained shallow-IQP graph generators on superconducting hardware at up to
153
qubits. In high-energy physics the situation is different. Prior IQP and generic quantum-circuit Born-machine work has stayed at 8 to 12 qubits, typically modeling joint distributions of two or three observables
delgado2022qcbm_hep
;
kiss2022conditional_born
. We are not aware of a demonstration that combines a real HEP imaging task with the roughly
10^{2}
-qubit scale needed for non-trivial spatial granularity.

A second point worth flagging is that all existing IQP-MMD work uses the Liu and Wang heat kernel
liu2018differentiable
, a bandwidth-mixed Walsh-diagonal Gaussian that spreads its Fourier weight fairly evenly across low-order Pauli-
Z
correlators. That uniform weighting is not ideal when the signal of physical interest is concentrated in pairwise correlation structure, as it is for calorimeter showers, where the spatial energy-correlation pattern is what separates electromagnetic from hadronic showers and signal from background. In our hands the Liu and Wang baseline saturates at a correlation reconstruction error about twice the encoding-fidelity floor and shows a systematic
\sim\!30\%
amplitude compression on recovered Pearson correlations.

Walsh-diagonal MMD
2

If the kernel

\displaystyle\mathrm{MMD}^{2}(\bm{\theta})

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{\beta\neq\emptyset}\hat{k}_{\beta}\,\big(\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}-\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;\delta\bm{z}(\bm{\theta})^{\!\top}\,\mathrm{diag}(\hat{\bm{k}})\,\delta\bm{z}(\bm{\theta}),

\hat{k}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{heat}}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\exp(-2\sigma_{b}^{2}|\beta|)

III.1
MoIQP
as a uniform mixture

p^{\textsc{MoIQP}{}}_{\bm{\Theta}}(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{L}\!\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}|\langle x|U(\bm{\theta}^{(\ell)})|0\rangle|^{2},

(5)

L

\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}^{\textsc{MoIQP}{}}\;=\;\frac{1}{L}\!\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}^{(\ell)}},

(6)

so that the Van den Nest training procedure carries over unchanged. The per-component
Z
-correlators are averaged before the MMD loss is evaluated. The number of trainable parameters scales as

, and the gradient cost scales the same way. We use

A naive quantum deployment of Eq. (

Construction.

Initialize

\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{\ell}|\ell\rangle

IV
The Pearson-Stabilized Correlation Kernel

IV.1
Why the heat kernel undershoots

The Liu and Wang heat kernel has Walsh spectrum
\hat{k}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{heat}}\propto\exp(-2\sigma^{2}|\beta|)
at a single bandwidth
\sigma
. The finite-bandwidth mixture used in practice preserves this exponential decay in
|\beta|
with an effective scale. The MMD
2
loss weights each Walsh-basis residual
\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\theta}-\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}
by
\hat{k}_{\beta}
, so weight-2 residuals enter the loss a factor
\exp(-2\sigma^{2})
below weight-1 residuals.

The gradient signal on the pairwise correlations
\rho
, which are fixed by the weight-2 observables together with the means, is correspondingly weaker than the signal on the single-qubit means
\langle Z_{i}\rangle
themselves. The training history reflects this imbalance. The model drives the means close to the data in the first
\sim\!50
epochs, then spends the remaining epoch budget slowly reducing the weight-2 residuals and does not reach the encoding-fidelity floor on
\rho
. The visible consequence is a model whose correlation sign pattern is correct but whose amplitudes are systematically compressed (Fig.
6
, top right).

We address this with a kernel that biases descent directly toward correlation-sensitive parameter directions while preserving positive-definiteness, classical trainability, and quantum sampling hardness.

IV.2
Pearson Jacobian

For the binary encoding (see Sec.
V.1
), the empirical Pearson correlation between calorimeter cells
f
and
g
under the trained model is

\rho_{fg}(\bm{\theta})\;=\;\frac{\mathrm{Cov}_{\bm{\theta}}[S_{f},S_{g}]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{\bm{\theta}}[S_{f}]\,\mathrm{Var}_{\bm{\theta}}[S_{g}]}},

(8)

with
S_{f}(x)=\sum_{k}2^{B-1-k}\,b_{f,k}(x)
the integer level of feature
f
and
b_{f,k}=(1-Z_{fB+k})/2
. Both numerator and denominator are linear functionals of the
Z
-correlators in the
\mathcal{O}_{2}
basis,
\mathrm{Cov}[S_{f},S_{g}]=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}2^{2B-2-k-k^{\prime}}(\langle Z_{fB+k}Z_{gB+k^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle Z_{fB+k}\rangle\langle Z_{gB+k^{\prime}}\rangle)/4
, and similarly for the variances. The full functional
\rho_{fg}
thus factors through the model
Z
-marginals.

We define the
Pearson Jacobian

\mathsf{J}
as the matrix of partial derivatives that links an infinitesimal change in any Pauli expectation
\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle
to the change it induces in each pairwise correlation
\rho_{fg}
, evaluated at the data:

\mathsf{J}_{(fg),\beta}\;\equiv\;\left.\frac{\partial\rho_{fg}}{\partial\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle}\right|_{\langle\bm{Z}\rangle=\langle\bm{Z}\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}}.

(9)

Rows are indexed by the
P=\binom{D}{2}
unordered feature pairs
(fg)
and columns by the
K=|\mathcal{O}_{2}|
weight
\leq 2
Pauli observables
\beta
, so
\mathsf{J}
is a
P\times K
matrix.

Because the evaluation point is fixed at the data expectation
\langle\bm{Z}\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}
rather than at the running model,
\mathsf{J}
depends only on the dataset; it is therefore a constant linear map, built once before training begins and reused at every gradient step. A closed-form expression for its entries is given in Appendix
B
.

IV.3
The
PSCK
kernel

The Pearson-Stabilized Correlation Kernel reads

K_{\mathrm{PSCK}}\;=\;\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\omega}_{\mathrm{heat}})\;+\;\eta\,\mathsf{J}^{\!\top}\mathsf{J},

(10)

with
\eta\geq 0
a scalar mixing weight; we use
\eta=5
throughout. Eq. (
10
) is the sum of a positive-definite diagonal kernel (Liu and Wang) and a positive-semidefinite rank-
P
correction, and is therefore positive-definite for any
\eta>0
. The training loss is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PSCK}}(\bm{\Theta})\;=\;\delta\bm{z}(\bm{\Theta})^{\!\top}K_{\mathrm{PSCK}}\,\delta\bm{z}(\bm{\Theta})\;=\;\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{heat}}+\eta\,\mathcal{L}_{\rho\text{-tan}},

(11)

with
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{heat}}=\delta\bm{z}^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\omega}_{\mathrm{heat}})\delta\bm{z}
the Liu and Wang MMD
2
and
\mathcal{L}_{\rho\text{-tan}}=\|\mathsf{J}\,\delta\bm{z}\|^{2}
the squared
L_{2}
residual of the linearized Pearson-correlation reconstruction.

IV.4
Gauss–Newton on Pearson MSE

The added term
\mathcal{L}_{\rho\text{-tan}}
is the Gauss–Newton linearization of the Pearson MSE

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Pearson}}=\sum_{f<g}(\rho_{fg}(\bm{\theta})-\rho_{fg}^{\mathrm{data}})^{2},

at the data point. Expanding
\rho_{fg}
to first order around
\langle Z\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}
gives

\rho_{fg}(\bm{\theta})-\rho_{fg}^{\mathrm{data}}\approx\sum_{\beta}\mathsf{J}_{(fg),\beta}(\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}-\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}),

so the following approximation holds,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Pearson}}\approx\|\mathsf{J}\,\delta\bm{z}\|^{2}=\mathcal{L}_{\rho\text{-tan}}.

Eq. (
11
) is therefore a sum of a valid Walsh-diagonal MMD
2
that guarantees distribution matching in the limit and a tangent-space target on Pearson correlations. The linearization is exact at the optimum
\delta\bm{z}\to 0
, and because
\mathsf{J}
is evaluated at the data it never moves, which avoids the moving-target pathology of Gauss–Newton methods at non-stationary linearization points.

IV.5
Classical trainability and sampling hardness

Since
\mathsf{J}
acts only on the residual vector
\delta\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
and not on the individual
Z
-correlators, the additional gradient terms from the rank-
P
correction are classically computable in time
\mathcal{O}(P\cdot K)
once the
\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}
values have been obtained by the Van den Nest Monte Carlo estimator. Three properties of the underlying Liu–Wang construction are preserved.

•

Classical trainability
— the gradient
\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PSCK}}/\partial\theta_{G}
needs only the same Van den Nest Monte Carlo evaluations as Liu–Wang training, followed by a fixed
K\times K
matrix multiplication.

•

Sampling hardness
— the kernel affects only the loss landscape, not the deployed circuit, so the trained model is still IQP (or, after compilation,
cIQP
) and its sampling complexity is unchanged.

•

Positive-definiteness
— at our chosen
\eta=5
the condition number of
K_{\mathrm{PSCK}}
is
\sim\!29
at
n=64
, comfortably within a numerically stable regime.

VI
Discussion

VI.1
Scope

Three main results come out of the work. First, IQP Born machines can be trained on real HEP calorimeter image data at 64 qubits. With the
PSCK
kernel the model reaches within
0.017
of the encoding-fidelity floor on pairwise correlation reconstruction, averaged across five independent seeds at
L=8
,
1500
epochs. As far as we are aware this is the largest qubit count at which an IQP Born machine has been fit to real HEP data. Second, the deferred-measurement
cIQP
compilation is exact to sub-Monte-Carlo precision and consistent across training seeds. It provides an explicit quantum-deployment path for the trained
MoIQP
whose joint sampling hardness follows from the standard Bremner–Jozsa–Shepherd argument on the compiled circuit. Third, neither
PSCK
nor the Liu and Wang baseline has a barren plateau in the
16
–
64
-qubit regime we study, at small-perturbation initialization and on sparse Erdős–Rényi graph connectivity.

VI.2
Limitations

The training objective fixes only weight
\leq 2

Z
-correlators, so it cannot constrain higher cumulants of individual feature marginals. On the three calorimeter cells with strongly non-Gaussian distributions,
f_{0}
through
f_{2}
, the residual
W_{1}
is about
20
integer levels out of
256
. This is the price of the
\mathcal{O}_{2}
training basis, not a failing of
PSCK
or of the architecture. The natural extension to weight-3 intra-feature observables is sketched in Sec.
VI.4
.

We verify
cIQP
against its
MoIQP
target by an independent Van den Nest MC estimator on the compiled
n+a
qubit circuit. Actual hardware execution, on superconducting qubits in the spirit of Ref.
ballo2025shallow_iqp_graph
or on trapped ions, is the next experimental step and is outside the present scope.

All experiments use one calorimeter dataset and one Erdős–Rényi graph family at average degree
6
. We have not checked performance on other detector geometries, on datasets with qualitatively different correlation structure, or on denser or more structured gate graphs.

At
n=64
the model is close to encoding-floor limited on
\rho
. Pushing
\mathrm{MAE}_{\rho}
below
0.05
would require going to
B=10
and
n=80
, or a different encoding. The mechanics are straightforward; whether this is architecturally novel, as opposed to just a re-encoding, is a separate question.

VI.3
Relation to existing work

VI.3.1
Train-on-classical, deploy-on-quantum framework.

This work lives inside the framework of Refs.
rudolph2024trainability
;
recio2025train_classical_deploy_quantum
, which showed that MMD
2
for IQP Born machines decomposes as a classically tractable Pauli-
Z
mixture and proposed the train-on-classical / deploy-on-quantum workflow. What we add is the
PSCK
kernel, the
MoIQP
architecture, the
cIQP
compilation, the IQP-native sampling-free evaluation protocol, and the HEP calorimeter application.

VI.3.2
Shallow-IQP graph generators.

Balló-Gimbernat et al.
ballo2025shallow_iqp_graph
ran shallow-IQP generative models for Erdős–Rényi and bipartite graph distributions on real superconducting hardware at up to
153
qubits, with Liu and Wang heat-kernel MMD training. Our contributions are complementary to theirs: a correlation-aligned kernel (
PSCK
), a mixture architecture (
MoIQP
), a deferred-measurement compilation (
cIQP
), and a continuous-feature imaging application in place of their discrete graph-statistics application. Their observation that local features survive better than global ones at large
n
on hardware lines up with ours that low-cumulant marginal structure is captured exactly by weight
\leq 2
training while higher-cumulant detail is not.

VI.3.3
HEP quantum-circuit Born machines.

Refs.
delgado2022qcbm_hep
;
kiss2022conditional_born
;
chang2023full_qgan_hep
train QCBMs and qGANs on HEP data at 8 to 12 qubits, with 2 or 3 features, on generic variational architectures rather than IQP. This work extends the qubit count and feature dimensionality by about an order of magnitude, which is possible because the IQP MMD objective is classically trainable to begin with.

VI.4
Future work

The most direct next step is to add weight-3 intra-feature observables to
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, which fixes the third cumulant of each feature marginal. The cost is
D\cdot\binom{B}{3}=8\cdot 56=448
additional observables per epoch at
B=8
, and the
PSCK
construction extends naturally by appending the corresponding columns to
\mathsf{J}
. We would expect this to close the residual mismatch on
f_{0}
through
f_{2}
without affecting the correlation result on
\rho
.

Running the compiled
cIQP
on superconducting or trapped-ion hardware in the spirit of Ref.
ballo2025shallow_iqp_graph
is the obvious next experimental step. The compiled gate set is the same Walsh-product family used there, so native transpilation should be straightforward.

The Pearson Jacobian
\mathsf{J}
of Eq. (
9
) is one specific Gauss–Newton linearization of a downstream functional of the model marginals. The same construction goes through for any physically motivated functional whose Jacobian with respect to
\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}
admits a closed form. Examples include moment-conditional quantities, mutual-information surrogates, and sliced-Wasserstein direction projections. This is a plausible direction for correlation-aware IQP training in domains beyond HEP.

VII
Conclusion

We have trained an IQP Born machine on real HEP calorimeter image data at
64
qubits. Three pieces go into the result. The
MoIQP
mixture architecture widens the IQP model class without breaking classical trainability. The
PSCK
kernel adds a rank-
P
Pearson-Jacobian correction to the heat-kernel baseline and recovers correlation amplitudes that the baseline cannot. The
cIQP
compilation maps the trained
MoIQP
to a single IQP circuit on
n+\lceil\log_{2}L\rceil
qubits for quantum deployment. Across five independent training seeds at
L=8
,
1500
epochs the model reaches training-split
\mathrm{MAE}_{\rho}=0.069\pm 0.008
against a
0.052
floor and test-split
\mathrm{MAE}_{\rho}=0.071\pm 0.008
against a
0.055
floor, with a five-seed train-test gap of
0.0023\pm 0.0006
that sits below the encoding-floor train-test gap of
0.0033
. Per-feature distributional metrics, computed exactly through Walsh–Hadamard inversion of intra-feature
Z
-correlators without sampling, split the eight calorimeter cells cleanly into a near-Gaussian set captured at the single-level scale and a heavy-tailed set whose mismatch traces directly to the absence of weight
\geq 3
observables in the training objective. A gradient scan from 16 to 64 qubits rules out exponential gradient decay for both
PSCK
and the Liu and Wang baseline in the regime we study, and
cIQP
deployment is verified to sub-Monte-Carlo precision across all five seeds. The
PSCK
construction is general: any physically motivated downstream functional with a closed-form Jacobian on the model marginals fits the same template.

Appendix A
Walsh–Hadamard compilation of
MoIQP
into a single IQP circuit on
n+a
qubits

This appendix gives the full derivation of the
cIQP
construction of Sec.
III.2
and Fig.
4
. The argument has four parts:

1.

the controlled-IQP unitary as a product of ancilla-indexed Pauli-
Z
rotations;

2.

the inverse-WHT relation between base angles and compiled angles;

3.

the deferred-measurement equivalence to
MoIQP
through a Walsh-sum identity on the ancilla outcome;

4.

the gate-count and hardness analysis.

All four parts are cross-checked numerically, which verifies bit-level agreement between
cIQP
and
MoIQP
marginals up to Monte Carlo noise.

Notation and setup.

Let the base gate graph be
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{|\mathcal{G}|}\}
with each
G_{j}\subseteq\{0,\dots,n-1\}
a subset of data qubits of weight
|G_{j}|\in\{1,2\}
. Denote the single-IQP unitary on parameters
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{G}|}
by

U(\bm{\theta})=H^{\otimes n}\,D(\bm{\theta})\,H^{\otimes n},\quad D(\bm{\theta})=\prod_{j}e^{i\,\theta_{j}\,Z_{G_{j}}},

(12)

with
Z_{G_{j}}=\prod_{i\in G_{j}}Z_{i}
. The
MoIQP
distribution is

p^{\textsc{MoIQP}{}}(x)=\tfrac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}|\langle x|U(\bm{\theta}^{(\ell)})|0\rangle|^{2}.

(13)

Let
a=\lceil\log_{2}L\rceil
and
L_{\mathrm{pad}}=2^{a}
. When
L<L_{\mathrm{pad}}
we pad the component list with zero-angle components (trivial identities, contributing equally to the mixture), so
L=2^{a}
throughout this appendix without loss of generality. Ancilla qubits are labeled
n,n+1,\dots,n+a-1
; for
S\subseteq\{0,\dots,a-1\}
with indicator vector
S\in\{0,1\}^{a}
we write
Z_{S}^{\mathrm{anc}}=\prod_{k\in S}Z_{n+k}
, with
Z_{\emptyset}^{\mathrm{anc}}=I
.

A.1
The
cIQP
unitary as a product of
Z\otimes Z
rotations

Construct the compiled diagonal unitary on
n+a
qubits as

D^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}\;=\;\prod_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{G}|}\prod_{S\subseteq\{0,\dots,a-1\}}e^{i\,\tilde{\phi}_{j,S}\,Z_{G_{j}}\otimes Z_{S}^{\mathrm{anc}}},

(14)

with compiled angles
\{\tilde{\phi}_{j,S}\}
to be determined. Every factor is a diagonal exponential of a Pauli-
Z
string and all such strings commute, so the product is order independent and
D^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}
is diagonal in the full computational basis of
n+a
qubits. The compiled IQP unitary is

U^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}\;=\;H^{\otimes(n+a)}\,D^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}\,H^{\otimes(n+a)},

(15)

which is IQP-form on
n+a
qubits by construction.

A.2
Ancilla-conditional equivalence

Fix an ancilla basis state
|\ell\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{a}}
and consider
D^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}(I^{\otimes n}\otimes|\ell\rangle\langle\ell|)
. Since
Z_{S}^{\mathrm{anc}}|\ell\rangle=(-1)^{\ell\cdot S}|\ell\rangle
,

\displaystyle D^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}\cdot\big(|\phi\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}\otimes|\ell\rangle\big)

\displaystyle=\left[\prod_{j,S}e^{i\,\tilde{\phi}_{j,S}\,(-1)^{\ell\cdot S}\,Z_{G_{j}}}\right]|\phi\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}\otimes|\ell\rangle

\displaystyle=\left[\prod_{j}e^{i\,\Phi_{j}(\ell)\,Z_{G_{j}}}\right]|\phi\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}\otimes|\ell\rangle,

(16)

with the effective data-register angle

\Phi_{j}(\ell)=\sum_{S\subseteq\{0,\dots,a-1\}}(-1)^{\ell\cdot S}\,\tilde{\phi}_{j,S}.

(17)

Pick
\tilde{\phi}_{j,S}
so that
\Phi_{j}(\ell)=\theta_{j}^{(\ell)}
for each
\ell
. Eq. (
17
) is an invertible Walsh transform in
(\ell,S)
, with inverse

\tilde{\phi}_{j,S}\;=\;\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}(-1)^{\ell\cdot S}\,\theta_{j}^{(\ell)}

(18)

(the inverse Walsh–Hadamard transform of the per-gate angle sequence across components). Substituting Eq. (
18
) into Eq. (
17
) and using Walsh orthogonality,
\sum_{S}(-1)^{(\ell+m)\cdot S}=L\,\delta_{\ell,m}
, gives
\Phi_{j}(\ell)=\theta_{j}^{(\ell)}
as required.

A.2.2
Where the summation lives.

The MoIQP distribution of Eq. (
5
) is built around one explicit classical sum
\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell}(\cdots)
. That sum does not appear literally anywhere in the
cIQP
circuits of Fig.
9
. The Walsh–Hadamard compilation relocates it into three distinct quantum-mechanical operations, distributed along the time-axis of the compiled circuit.

First, the leading Hadamards on the ancilla register,
H^{\otimes a}\,|0\rangle^{\otimes a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{\ell}|\ell\rangle
,
create
the summation as a coherent superposition over the mixture index.

Second, the compiled diagonal
D^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}
attaches
D(\bm{\theta}^{(\ell)})
to each branch
|\ell\rangle
. By Eq. (
23
),

D^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}|+\rangle^{\otimes(n+a)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{\ell}D(\bm{\theta}^{(\ell)})\,|+\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes|\ell\rangle.

(22)

Third, the ancilla measurement together with the discard of the ancilla outcome (the trace over the ancilla register)
collapses
the coherent sum into the classical mixture
\Pr(x)=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell}|A_{\ell}(x)|^{2}
, which is Eq. (
26
). The quantum advantage of
cIQP
over the naive per-sample classical mixture of Fig.
3
is that nothing ever evaluates or samples the mixture index
\ell
classically; the Born rule does the averaging automatically at measurement time.

A.3
Deferred-measurement equivalence

Apply
U^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}
to
|0\rangle^{\otimes(n+a)}
. The leading
H^{\otimes(n+a)}
prepares
|+\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes|+\rangle^{\otimes a}=|+\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{\ell}|\ell\rangle
. Applying
D^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}
and using Eq. (
16
),

D^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}|+\rangle^{\otimes(n+a)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}D(\bm{\theta}^{(\ell)})|+\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes|\ell\rangle.

(23)

The trailing
H^{\otimes(n+a)}=H^{\otimes n}\otimes H^{\otimes a}
then gives

U^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}|0\rangle^{\otimes(n+a)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}U(\bm{\theta}^{(\ell)})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes H^{\otimes a}|\ell\rangle.

(24)

Measuring all
n+a
qubits in the computational basis yields joint probability

\Pr(x,y)=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\left|\sum_{\ell}(-1)^{y\cdot\ell}\,A_{\ell}(x)\right|^{2},

(25)

with
A_{\ell}(x)=\langle x|U(\bm{\theta}^{(\ell)})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}
. Marginalizing over
y
,

\displaystyle\Pr(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y}\Pr(x,y)=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{\ell,m}A_{\ell}(x)A_{m}^{*}(x)\sum_{y}(-1)^{y\cdot(\ell+m)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{\ell,m}A_{\ell}(x)A_{m}^{*}(x)\cdot L\,\delta_{\ell,m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell}|A_{\ell}(x)|^{2}\;=\;p^{\textsc{MoIQP}{}}(x).

(26)

The data-register distribution, averaged over (equivalently, traced over) the ancilla outcome, is exactly the
MoIQP
distribution. This is a marginal statement, not a conditional one. The per-outcome conditional distribution
\Pr(x|y)
is in general not equal to
p^{\textsc{MoIQP}{}}(x)
at a fixed
y
, nor does fixing
y
select a particular component
\ell
. No post-selection on
y
is required. Operationally, a user of
cIQP
measures all
n+a
qubits, keeps the first
n
, and discards the last
a
.

A.4
Gate count and gate weights

From Eq. (
14
) the compiled diagonal contains one
Z\otimes Z
-phase rotation per
(j,S)
pair. Ignoring accidental zero-angle prunings, the number of compiled gates is

|\mathcal{G}_{\textsc{cIQP}{}}|\;=\;|\mathcal{G}|\cdot L,

(27)

i.e. each base gate expands into
L
compiled gates, one per ancilla subset
S
. For the headline
n=64
,
L=8
configuration this gives
|\mathcal{G}_{\textsc{cIQP}{}}|
in the range
2048
to
2296
across the five seeds, since the base gate count
|\mathcal{G}|
itself varies from
256
to
287
between the seed-specific ER graph realizations; see Appendix
D
. Each compiled gate has weight
|G_{j}|+|S|
, so compiled gate weights range from
1
(weight-1 base gate with
S=\emptyset
) to
2+a=5
(weight-2 base gate with
|S|=3
).

A.5
Sampling hardness

We distinguish two sampling tasks: the joint
(n+a)
-qubit distribution and the data-only
n
-qubit marginal. The generative-modeling deployment only ever uses the marginal of Eq. (
26
). The joint is not operationally relevant.

For the
joint
,
U^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}
is IQP-form on
n+a
qubits by construction. The original Bremner–Jozsa–Shepherd
bremner2011iqp_hardness
argument was stated for IQP circuits with
Z
-polynomial degree at most
3
. Our compiled circuit has maximum
Z
-polynomial degree
2+a
, which for
a=3
is
5
and falls inside the Bremner–Montanaro–Shepherd extension
bremner2016bjs
. A sparse Erdős–Rényi graph at
\langle k\rangle=6
of the kind we use is qualitatively the kind of sparse-random structure that extension covers under the usual anti-concentration hypotheses. Classical efficient sampling of the joint
(n+a)
-qubit output would therefore imply a polynomial hierarchy collapse to its second level under the conjectures of the BMS framework. This is a conditional statement, tied to whichever hypotheses one needs to apply BMS to the specific gate-weight distribution of our compiled circuit; a tight worst-case hardness theorem for the exact compiled graph at
a=3
is not claimed and would be of independent interest. For the
marginal
, sampling hardness of the joint does not transfer automatically to the data-register distribution, since marginalizing over an entangled ancilla can in principle reduce distributional complexity. We do not claim a rigorous hardness reduction from BMS to
MoIQP
-marginal sampling. What we do claim, and what is operationally relevant, is that the compiled circuit reduces
MoIQP
marginal sampling to a single quantum-hardware run of an IQP-form circuit with no classical post-mixing, and that each of the
L
underlying single-IQP components admits the standard BJS/BMS hardness argument in isolation. Whether the uniform mixture of
L
BJS-hard distributions is itself BJS-hard is a separate theoretical question, not answered by our construction.

A.6
Numerical verification

Two independent numerical routines cover this construction and are provided with the code companion of this paper. One runs exact wavefunction simulation at small
n
(
\leq 4
) and verifies machine-precision agreement between the
cIQP
data-register marginal and the direct
MoIQP
distribution, with measured error
\max_{x}|\Pr_{\textsc{cIQP}{}}(x)-p^{\textsc{MoIQP}{}}(x)|<5\times 10^{-16}
for every configuration tested (random graphs,
L\in\{4,8\}
), at the floating-point roundoff level. The second routine operates at
n=64
through Van den Nest Monte Carlo and compares
cIQP
and
MoIQP
at the level of low-order
Z
-correlators,

\mathrm{MAE}\!\left[\{\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}^{\textsc{cIQP}{}}-\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}^{\textsc{MoIQP}{}}:\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{2}\}\right]<5\cdot M^{-1/2}.

(28)

Across all five training seeds at
n=64
,
L=8
and
M=2\times 10^{5}
latents, the measured ratio is
\mathrm{MAE}/M^{-1/2}=0.591\pm 0.012
.

Appendix B
Closed-form Pearson Jacobian

In this appendix we derive the closed-form analytical Jacobian
\mathsf{J}_{(fg),\beta}=\partial\rho_{fg}/\partial\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}\big|_{\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}=\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\mathrm{data}}}
used in the rank-
P
correction of
PSCK
in Eq. (
9
). The derivation expresses
\rho_{fg}
as an explicit algebraic function of the low-order
Z
-correlators
\{\langle Z_{\beta}\rangle_{\bm{\theta}}:\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{2}\}
and applies the chain rule analytically. The intermediate formulas are verified against central-difference finite differences on the training data to a relative precision of
5\times 10^{-11}
(code is also supplied), and the full pipeline is verified by an independent from-scratch re-implementation that agrees with the code Jacobian to
8\times 10^{-11}
.

B.1
Derivations

Setup and bit-level expectations.

Feature
f\in\{0,\dots,D-1\}
is encoded as
B
bits
b_{f,0},\dots,b_{f,B-1}\in\{0,1\}
on qubits
fB,fB+1,\dots,fB+B-1
, with reconstruction

S_{f}\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{B-1}w_{k}\,b_{f,k},\qquad w_{k}\;=\;2^{B-1-k}

(29)

so that
W\equiv\sum_{k}w_{k}=2^{B}-1
. The single-qubit expectation
z_{1;f,k}\equiv\langle Z_{fB+k}\rangle
and two-qubit expectation
z_{2;(fk),(gl)}\equiv\langle Z_{fB+k}\,Z_{gB+l}\rangle
give the bit-level moments

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[b_{f,k}]

\displaystyle\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}(1-z_{1;f,k})\;\equiv\;E_{f,k},

(30)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[b_{f,k}\,b_{g,l}]

\displaystyle\;=\;\tfrac{1}{4}(1-z_{1;f,k}-z_{1;g,l}+z_{2;(fk),(gl)}),

(31)

valid for
(f,k)\neq(g,l)
. The bit-level covariance and variance follow,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(b_{f,k},b_{g,l})

\displaystyle\;=\;\mathbb{E}[b_{f,k}\,b_{g,l}]-E_{f,k}\,E_{g,l},

(32)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(b_{f,k})

\displaystyle\;=\;E_{f,k}(1-E_{f,k}).

(33)

Feature moments as quadratic forms.

By bilinearity,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[S_{f},S_{g}]

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{k,l}w_{k}w_{l}\,\mathrm{Cov}(b_{f,k},b_{g,l}),\quad(f\neq g)

(34)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[S_{f}]

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{k}w_{k}^{2}\,\mathrm{Var}(b_{f,k})+2\sum_{k<l}w_{k}w_{l}\,\mathrm{Cov}(b_{f,k},b_{f,l}),

(35)

and
\rho_{fg}=\mathrm{Cov}[S_{f},S_{g}]/(\sigma_{f}\,\sigma_{g})
with
\sigma_{f}=\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[S_{f}]}
.

Outer chain rule.

\frac{\partial\rho_{fg}}{\partial\mathrm{Cov}[S_{f},S_{g}]}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{f}\,\sigma_{g}},\quad\frac{\partial\rho_{fg}}{\partial\mathrm{Var}[S_{h}]}=-\frac{\rho_{fg}}{2\,\mathrm{Var}[S_{h}]}

(36)

for
h\in\{f,g\}
, zero otherwise.

Inner chain rule,
z_{1}
correlators.

The single-qubit correlator
z_{1;f,k^{\star}}
appears in the diagonal term
\mathrm{Var}(b_{f,k^{\star}})
of
\mathrm{Var}[S_{f}]
, in the intra-feature covariances
\mathrm{Cov}(b_{f,k^{\star}},b_{f,l})
for
l\neq k^{\star}
, and in the cross-feature covariances
\mathrm{Cov}(b_{f,k^{\star}},b_{g,l})
for
g\neq f
. The elementary derivatives are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{Var}(b_{f,k^{\star}})}{\partial z_{1;f,k^{\star}}}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}(1-2E_{f,k^{\star}}),

(37)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{Cov}(b_{f,k^{\star}},b_{f,l})}{\partial z_{1;f,k^{\star}}}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{2}E_{f,l}\quad(l\neq k^{\star}),

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{Cov}(b_{f,k^{\star}},b_{g,l})}{\partial z_{1;f,k^{\star}}}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{2}E_{g,l}\quad(g\neq f).

(39)

Summing the contributions,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{Var}[S_{f}]}{\partial z_{1;f,k^{\star}}}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}w_{k^{\star}}^{2}(1-2E_{f,k^{\star}})

\displaystyle\qquad+2w_{k^{\star}}\sum_{l\neq k^{\star}}w_{l}\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{2}E_{f,l}\right),

(40)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{Cov}[S_{f},S_{g}]}{\partial z_{1;f,k^{\star}}}

\displaystyle=w_{k^{\star}}\!\left(-\tfrac{W}{4}+\tfrac{1}{2}T^{E}_{g}\right),

(41)

with
T^{E}_{g}\equiv\sum_{l}w_{l}\,E_{g,l}
. The corresponding derivatives with respect to
z_{1;g,l^{\star}}
follow by swapping
f\leftrightarrow g
and
k^{\star}\leftrightarrow l^{\star}
.

Inner chain rule,
z_{2}
correlators.

A two-qubit correlator
z_{2;(f,k),(g,l)}
enters exactly one feature moment depending on whether it is intra- or cross-feature,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{Cov}[S_{f},S_{g}]}{\partial z_{2;(fk),(gl)}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\,w_{k}w_{l}\qquad(f\neq g),

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{Var}[S_{f}]}{\partial z_{2;(fk),(fl)}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,w_{k}w_{l}\qquad(k<l).

(43)

The factor of
2
in the intra-feature case is the symmetry factor for
(k,l)
and
(l,k)
in Eq. (
35
). All other
z_{2}
entries yield zero derivative.

Full Jacobian.

Combining the outer and inner chain rules, for
\beta=\{fB+k^{\star}\}
,

\frac{\partial\rho_{fg}}{\partial z_{1;f,k^{\star}}}\;=\;\frac{w_{k^{\star}}}{\sigma_{f}\,\sigma_{g}}\!\left(-\tfrac{W}{4}+\tfrac{1}{2}T^{E}_{g}\right)\\
-\frac{\rho_{fg}}{2\,\mathrm{Var}[S_{f}]}\!\left[-\tfrac{1}{2}w_{k^{\star}}^{2}(1-2E_{f,k^{\star}})+2w_{k^{\star}}\!\!\sum_{l\neq k^{\star}}\!w_{l}\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{2}E_{f,l}\right)\right],

(44)

and analogously for
\beta=\{gB+l^{\star}\}
. For a cross-feature
\beta=\{fB+k,gB+l\}
,

\frac{\partial\rho_{fg}}{\partial z_{2;(fk),(gl)}}\;=\;\frac{w_{k}\,w_{l}}{4\,\sigma_{f}\,\sigma_{g}}.

(45)

For an intra-feature
\beta=\{fB+k,fB+l\}
with
k<l
,

\frac{\partial\rho_{fg}}{\partial z_{2;(fk),(fl)}}\;=\;-\frac{\rho_{fg}}{2\,\mathrm{Var}[S_{f}]}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\,w_{k}\,w_{l},

(46)

and similarly for
\beta=\{gB+k,gB+l\}
with
f\to g
. All other Jacobian entries vanish identically.

B.2
Complexity

The full
(P\times K)
Jacobian with
P=D(D-1)/2
and
K=DB+D\binom{B}{2}+\binom{D}{2}B^{2}=|\mathcal{O}_{2}|
is computed in
\mathcal{O}(PB^{2}+D^{2}B^{2})
time via the formulas above. Because
\mathsf{J}
is evaluated at the data, it is built once at the start of training and reused for every gradient step. At
D=8
,
B=8
this is
28\times 2080=5.8\times 10^{4}
Jacobian entries and the build time is a few milliseconds, negligible against the per-step Van den Nest cost.

The verification code runs a central-difference finite-difference verification of the full Jacobian against the training data routine at

PSCK

C.6
Limitations

Three explicit limitations of the scan. First, single graph realization per
n
. Each
n
uses one ER graph at graph-seed
43
, so graph-to-graph fluctuations at fixed
(n,\langle k\rangle)
are not sampled. The residual variance in the
PSCK
pgv fit (
R^{2}=0.015
) is plausibly dominated by that source. A multi-graph extension would tighten the empirical slope bound on
PSCK
, though we would not expect it to change the qualitative “no exponential decay” finding.

Second, small-
\sigma
initialization regime only. The scan is at
\sigma=0.1
. Lerch et al.
kumar2026data_dependent_init
show that MMD-type losses hit barren plateaus at
\sigma\sim\pi/2
(full-angle initialization), which we stay away from. Extrapolation of our
\sigma=0.1
results to other amplitudes is not supported by the data we show.

Third, fixed
L
. The scan runs at
L=4
; the
L
-dependence of the gradient-variance scaling is not tested here. For large
L
the
MoIQP
loss becomes a sum of
L
single-IQP gradient variances under independent parameter sampling, for which a
\sqrt{L}
tightening of concentration would naively be expected, but we have not run that experiment.
