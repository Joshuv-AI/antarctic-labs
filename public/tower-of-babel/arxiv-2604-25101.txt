A Posteriori Error Estimation for Parabolic Equations with Enriched Galerkin Finite Element Methods
Hyun-Geun Shin, Yi-Yung Yang, Sanghyun Lee

Abstract
This paper introduces a novel a posteriori error estimation framework for the enriched Galerkin (EG) finite element method applied to linear parabolic equations. While the EG method has been recognized for its local conservation property and computational efficiency compared to discontinuous Galerkin methods, its mathematical analysis in the context of a posteriori error estimation for parabolic problems remains unexplored. In this work, we prove reliability and efficiency using the residual-based approach. Furthermore, we integrate these error estimators into an adaptive mesh refinement strategy, demonstrating their effectiveness in achieving efficient and reliable error control through several numerical examples. The proposed approach provides a significant advancement in the mathematical foundation and practical applicability of the EG method for time-dependent problems.

Introduction

Numerical errors in computational simulations pose significant challenges, as they directly affect the accuracy, reliability, and efficiency of numerical solutions. Over the years, considerable efforts have been devoted to mitigating such errors, leading to significant advancements in a posteriori error estimation methods
[
1
]
. By coupling these estimators with dynamic adaptive algorithms, one can effectively manage computational resources to minimize error and optimize performance.

In this work, we investigate a linear parabolic partial differential equation (PDE) discretized using the enriched Galerkin (EG) finite element method
[
39
,
7
]
. The EG method augments continuous Galerkin finite element spaces with piecewise constant functions, thereby achieving local conservation at a lower computational cost than classical nonconforming discontinuous Galerkin finite element methods
[
25
,
27
]
. Recently, EG methods have been employed in a variety of multiphysics applications, including two-phase flow in porous media
[
28
]
, poroelasticity
[
20
,
29
,
10
]
, hyperbolic equations
[
23
]
, thermoporoelasticity
[
45
]
, and flow problems with fractures
[
19
,
26
]
.

To provide reliable quantification of numerical errors, we develop a residual-based a posteriori error estimator that is specifically adapted to the PDE under consideration and the EG framework. We then validate this estimator through adaptive mesh refinement, demonstrating effective error control and improved accuracy. To the best of our knowledge, there has been no systematic investigation of a posteriori error estimates for EG methods applied to parabolic PDEs.

Traditionally, residual-based a posteriori error analysis has been well-established for conforming finite element methods, with original developments in the context of linear elliptic problems before extension to parabolic settings
[
2
,
15
]
. For instance,
[
34
]
considered a normalized heat equation solved via backward Euler and piecewise linear finite elements, establishing reliability through Clement’s interpolant and efficiency using bubble functions. Subsequently,
[
9
]
proposed error estimators and an adaptive algorithm echoing
[
34
]
, aided by the MNS refinement strategy
[
32
]
. Such approaches primarily focused on error control in the
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
norm.

As the field evolved, elliptic reconstruction methods broadened the scope of a posteriori estimates. Initially introduced by
[
31
]
and applied in a fully discrete setting by
[
24
]
, elliptic reconstruction has since spurred various enhancements. For example,
[
5
,
6
]
showed its applicability to both mesh refinement and coarsening, whereas
[
21
]
integrated it into fractional step
\vartheta

[

]
, analyses of delay PDEs
[
43
]
, derivation of lower bounds
[
17
]
, and max-norm estimates
[
30
]
. Further research extended reconstruction ideas to space-time approaches, notably for Crank–Nicolson
[
3
]
and non-conforming discontinuous Galerkin (DG) schemes
[
22
,
18
,
21
]
.

Meanwhile, a posteriori error estimators have also been generalized to various non-conforming and locally conservative numerical approaches. For instance,
[
8
]
investigated non-hierarchical Galerkin techniques, applying them to virtual element methods, while
[
11
]
examined weak Galerkin finite elements. In
[
35
]
, adaptive immersed finite element methods were proposed for interface problems. Despite these advances, actual adaptive computations—often the primary motivation behind a posteriori error analysis—are sometimes absent in the literature. Notable exceptions include
[
34
]
, where mesh adaptation employed Delaunay triangulation, and
[
6
]
, which utilized the ALBERTA library
[
38
]
for exploring mesh modifications.

To efficiently reduce the overall error while preserving physically compatible solutions, it is crucial to maintain local conservation on the mesh. From this perspective, discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods have emerged as a widely adopted and thoroughly studied class of approaches, offering both rigorous error control and local conservation properties. For instance,
[
16
]
established a posteriori error estimates for a DG method in space using elliptic reconstruction and a decomposition of continuous and discontinuous solution components.

Following the approach of Verfürth
[
42
]
, originally developed for conforming finite elements applied to elliptic PDEs, we perform a residual-based a posteriori error analysis for the EG method with NIPG, SIPG, and IIPG formulations. Although the linear parabolic PDE is discretized using the EG method in space and the backward Euler scheme in time, our analysis focuses exclusively on spatial error estimation at each time step. The proposed error estimator is employed to drive adaptive mesh refinement and is shown to provide computable upper and lower bounds for the residual error. These theoretical findings are further validated by a series of numerical experiments demonstrating convergence of the estimators, confirming their practical relevance.

The a posteriori error estimates are derived in Section

Finally, in Section

4
A posteriori error analysis

A posteriori error estimates use the residual to quantify the error between the numerical solution and the exact solution, thereby providing a practical and computable guide for adaptive mesh refinement.
The residual of the numerical solution
p^{n}_{h}\in V^{\text{EG}}_{h,k,n}
to (
13
) is defined to be

\mathcal{R}^{n}(w):=F^{n}_{\theta}(w)-S_{\theta}(p^{n}_{h},w),\qquad\forall w\in H^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h,n})

(16)

which is in the dual space
\left(H^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h,n})\right)^{*}
associated with the dual norm

\|\mathcal{R}^{n}\|_{*}:=\sup_{w\in H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\dfrac{\mathcal{R}^{n}(w)}{\|w\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h,n})}}.

(17)

In particular, because of the consistency of the solution
p^{n}
, i.e.
F^{n}_{\theta}(w)=S_{\theta}(p^{n},w)
,

\mathcal{R}^{n}(w)=S_{\theta}(p^{n},w)-S_{\theta}(p^{n}_{h},w)=S_{\theta}(e^{n},w),

(18)

where
e^{n}=p^{n}-p^{n}_{h}
. Furthermore, by the Galerkin orthogonality

S_{\theta}(p^{n}-p^{n}_{h},w_{h})=S_{\theta}(e^{n},w_{h})=0,\qquad\forall w_{h}\in V^{\text{EG}}_{h,k,n},

(19)

we have

\mathcal{R}^{n}(w)=S_{\theta}(e^{n},w)=S_{\theta}(e^{n},w-w_{h})=\mathcal{R}^{n}(w-w_{h})

(20)

and

\|\mathcal{R}^{n}\|_{*}{=}\sup_{w\in H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h,n})}\dfrac{\mathcal{R}^{n}(w-w_{h})}{\|w\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h,n})}}.

(21)

In the following sections, we will first provide an upper bound estimate for the residual, referred to as reliability, to control the error based on the mesh size. Then, using these reliable error estimators, we will establish a local lower bound for the residual relative to a given error estimator—referred to as local efficiency—which reflects the sharpness of the error estimate.
