The typicality of symmetry-induced entanglement
Christian Boudreault, Nicolas Levasseur

Abstract
Abstract In the presence of a globally conserved charge N ^ \hat{N} , a natural question is whether a given separable state can be separated into charge-conserving components. We dub this problem the Symmetric Separability Problem (SSP). On random states, the SSP is answered negatively with probability one for almost all N ^ \hat{N} . Using a witness to the failure of symmetric separability, the number entanglement (NE) introduced in 24 , we show that most symmetric and separable states are actually far from being symmetrically separable, with the NE featuring Gaussian concentration around a strictly positive mean value. We discuss some consequences of our results for quantum tasks in the presence of a superselection rule or in the absence of a common reference frame. Progress is made on the question of the size of the separable space constrained by N ^ \hat{N} . We also touch upon the question of the complexity of SSP, and multiparty entanglement.

Introduction

The existence of nonclassical correlations might be the most profound outcome of the quantum revolution, both conceptually and practically. Entanglement, nonlocality and contextuality have advanced our understanding of matter and information
43
;
27
, and pushed back the limits of computational and communication tasks that can be realistically implemented
2
;
3
;
37
;
12
;
22
;
6
;
32
;
18
. Finding which quantum states possess nonclassical correlations, quantifying the amount present, and characterizing the type of correlations is thus an urgent, but difficult endeavor. The outstanding Quantum Separability Problem (QSP) seeks to decide whether a given density matrix
\rho
of a two-party system
\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}
can be written in the form

\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{A,i}\otimes\rho_{B,i},

(1)

where
\rho_{A,i}
and
\rho_{B,i}
are densities on
A
and
B
, respectively. The state is
separable
and has only classical correlations if it can be written in the form (
1
), and is entangled otherwise. As simple as it looks, the Quantum Separability Problem is known to be NP-hard in many cases, and believed to be so in general
14
;
10
. In the presence of a globally conserved charge, the natural question becomes whether a state can be separated into charge-conserving components. For a charge
\hat{N}
such that
[\rho,\hat{N}]=0
, the separable state (
1
) is
symmetrically separable for

\hat{N}
(
symsep
for short) if

[\rho_{A,i}\otimes\rho_{B,i},\hat{N}]=0\qquad,\qquad\forall i.

(2)

If
\rho
is formally separable in the sense of (
1
) but not symmetrically separable in the sense of (
2
), it contains entanglement in at least one charge sector, and this entanglement will be unlocked by charge measurement
24
. We call this type of entanglement
symmetry-induced
because prior to a choice of symmetry, and subsequent measurement, the separable state (
1
) contains only classical correlations. To the best of our knowledge, the Symmetric Separability Problem (SSP) — deciding whether a given separable and symmetric state is symmetrically separable — has no known general solution. In fact, SSP boils down to QSP within the restricted class
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
of
\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}
-symmetric states, where
\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}
is a localized version of
\hat{N}
. Within this class, separability and symmetric separability coincide (Fig.
1
). Thus when
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
is of measure zero in
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}
(the set of
\hat{N}
-symmetric states), separable and symmetric states fail to be symmetrically separable with probability one.

To cope with the algorithmic complexity of finding a general solution to the problem of entanglement, a complementary line of attack is to adopt a measure-theoretic point of view aiming at quantifying
how much
entanglement is typical in physical states. Such knowledge is of value for theoretical investigations as well as numerical simulations, where one is led to make assumptions about typical quantum states or make use of statistical ensembles of states which presuppose minimal prior knowledge about a quantum system
45
. Celebrated findings from this approach include the typicality of nonseparable mixed states, along with a tradeoff between purity and separability
44
;
46
, the typicality of highly entangled pure states, and the existence of large subspaces in which all pure states are close to maximally entangled
17
. The toolbox of measure theory comprises a varied supply of theorems collectively known as
concentration of measure
, the most familiar example being Lévy’s Inequality Lemma, which states informally that any function of bounded gradient on a sphere will strongly concentrate around its mean value
20
. Because (normalized) pure states and (normalized) purifications of mixed states live on spheres, the lemma will apply to functions of bounded gradient on these states. The entanglement entropy (EE), a witness of pure-state entanglement, is a notable example of the phenomenon
17
. With overwhelming probability, a random pure state will have an EE almost equal to the (near maximal) average pure-state EE, and will be (highly) entangled.

To further investigate symmetric separability, we apply the concentration of measure phenomenon to another entanglement witness, the
number entanglement
introduced in
24
, which is specifically tailored to diagnose the presence of symmetry-induced entanglement. For degenerate
\hat{N}
, we show that on
\hat{N}
-symmetric and separable states (i.e. on the set
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
of Fig.
1
) the number entanglement strongly concentrates around its (strictly positive) mean value. Thus, typical states in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
will be found at some positive distance from symmetrically separable ones. (We will attempt to numerically evaluate that distance.) To emphasize, the concentration result shows that not only are typical states symmetrically inseparable, but also
generically far
from being symmetrically separable. Thus, while it is a contribution to the Symmetric Separability Problem, the result is logically independent (i.e. neither proves the other) from a general algebraic solution to this problem.

II
Reduction to separability

The closed, convex set of density matrices (states) on a Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
will be denoted
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{H})
, or simply
\mathscr{D}
when the context is clear. We consider two-party states in
\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}
, i.e.
\rho\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B})
, where
\mathscr{H}_{A}
and
\mathscr{H}_{B}
have respective dimensions
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
. For a set of Hermitean operators
\hat{N}=\{\hat{n}_{j}\}
on
\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}
, we define the
\hat{N}
-symmetric states

\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}=\{\rho\in\mathscr{D}\colon[\rho,\hat{n}_{j}]=0\text{ for all }\hat{n}_{j}\in\hat{N}\}.

(3)

In the following, we will consider the observables
\hat{N}=\{\hat{N}_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}\}
, and
\hat{N}=\{\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}+1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}\}
, as well as their common ‘localization’:

\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}=\{\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B},1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}\}.

(4)

Then
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
will denote the set of symmetrically separable states, satisfying (
1
) and (
2
) with the appropriate
\hat{N}
.
It is an easy exercise to show

Proposition 1
.

\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}=\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
.

The proof consists in showing that Eqn. (
2
) readily implies
[\rho_{A,i}\otimes\rho_{B,i},\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}]=0=[\rho_{A,i}\otimes\rho_{B,i},1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}]
. Thus, SSP within
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}
is reduced to SSP within
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
. Let us define the twirl

\mathcal{G}[\rho]\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{T}^{2}}dg\;T(g)\rho\,T^{\dagger}(g),

(5)

where
T(g)=e^{ig_{1}\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}+ig_{2}1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}}
, and
dg
is the Haar measure over the two-dimensional torus
\mathcal{T}^{2}
. It follows from translation-invariance of the Haar measure that
\mathcal{G}[\rho]
commutes with both
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}
and
1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}
. The range of
\mathcal{G}
is thus in
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
.
Actually,

Proposition 2
.

\mathcal{G}
is the (unique) retraction of
\mathscr{D}
onto
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
, i.e. it collapses
\mathscr{D}
onto
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
, and
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
is the set of fixed points of
\mathcal{G}
.

Because each
T(g)
is of the form
e^{ig_{1}\hat{N}_{A}}\otimes e^{ig_{2}\hat{N}_{B}}
, it follows by linearity that
\mathcal{G}
sends separable states to (
\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}
-symmetric) separable states. In fact, we have the stronger result

Proposition 3
.

\mathscr{D}^{\textup{sep}}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}=\mathcal{G}[\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}]=\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
.

Corollary 1
.

\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}=\mathscr{D}^{\textup{sep}}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
.

Therefore, SSP for
\hat{N}
is reduced to QSP within the class of
\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}
-symmetric states. Importantly, when
\hat{N}
is degenerate and not a multiple of the identity,
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{sep}}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
is of measure zero in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, and states in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
fail to be symsep with probability one. Because of the SSP-to-QSP reduction, it is tempting to believe that for some symmetries at least, the SSP problem is NP-complete, as it is for the ‘trivial’ case
\hat{N}=1

14
;
10
. An interesting question is: For which
\hat{N}
is SSP an NP-complete problem? Progress towards an answer might come from knowledge of the relative size of
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
within
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}
(more specifically, of
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{sep}}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
within
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
). One can show that
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}
and
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
naturally split into the convex hulls of their charge sectors as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}

\displaystyle=\textup{conv}\bigcup_{n}\mathscr{D}(h_{A}^{n}\otimes h_{B}^{n})

(6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}

\displaystyle=\textup{conv}\bigcup_{n}\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}(h_{A}^{n}\otimes h_{B}^{n}),

where charge-
n
densities act on subspaces of the form
h_{A}^{n}\otimes h_{B}^{n}
with
h_{A}^{n}\subset\mathscr{H}_{A}
,
h_{B}^{n}\subset\mathscr{H}_{B}
. Numerical evidence
44
;
46
suggests that each ratio

\frac{\textup{vol}\,\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}(h_{A}^{n}\otimes h_{B}^{n})}{\textup{vol}\,\mathscr{D}(h_{A}^{n}\otimes h_{B}^{n})}

(7)

is exponentially small in the dimension of
h_{A}^{n}\otimes h_{B}^{n}
. Because charge sectors are orthogonal, we conjecture that at least when all sector dimensions are large the relative size of
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
within
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}
will be exponentially small (in some combination of sector dimensions).

To conclude this section, let us mention that when
\hat{N}
is
nondegenerate
, it follows that
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}=\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
, i.e. SSP and QSP coincide. This is because any
\hat{N}
-symmetric state is diagonalized in the
unique
eigenbasis of
\hat{N}
(also an eigenbasis of
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}
and
1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}
), whence
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}=\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
. Corollary
1
in turn implies
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}=\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
. Another extreme case is when
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}
and/or
1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}
is
completely
degenerate, i.e. a multiple of the identity, for
\hat{N}
commutes with it. Then again, SSP and QSP coincide. Nontrivial behavior of the NE is to be searched for in between these extremes.

III
Concentration

In this section, we show that the NE concentrates on
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, our main result, and explain what it tells us about the Symmetric Separability Problem. We begin by recalling facts about the concentration of entanglement entropy, some of which will be used later on.

III.1
Concentration for pure-state entanglement entropy

The von Neumann entropy of a state
\rho
(pure or mixed) is

S(\rho)=-\text{Tr}(\rho\log\rho).

(8)

As usual, for two-party states
\rho\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B})
the bipartite entanglement entropy (EE) on
A
is obtained by first tracing out
B
, and computing the von Neumann entropy of the resulting reduced matrix,

S(\rho_{A})=S(\text{Tr}_{B}\rho).

(9)

On pure states, the EE is sensitive only to nonclassical correlations between
A
and
B
, and symmetric under the interchange of
A
and
B
. We will write
\psi=\lvert\psi\rangle\langle\psi\lvert
for the density corresponding to a pure state
\left|\psi\right\rangle
. Normalized pure states live on the sphere
S^{2d_{A}d_{B}-1}
up to a global phase. As was proved in
17
, pure-state EE is Lipshitz continuous:

Lemma 1
.

For pure states
\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle\in\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}
, and
d_{A}\geq 3
,

|S(\psi_{A})-S(\phi_{A})|\leq\eta\lVert\lvert\psi\rangle-\lvert\phi\rangle\rVert_{2},

(10)

with
\eta=\sqrt{8}\log d_{A}
, and where
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2}
is the Euclidean norm.

The most celebrated instance of measure concentration, Lévy’s Inequality Lemma, deals specifically with Lipshitz functions on spheres:

Lemma 2

(Lévy’s Lemma)
.

Let
f:S^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Lipshitz function with constant
\eta
, i.e.

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\eta\lVert x-y\rVert_{2},\hskip 22.76228pt\forall x,y\in S^{n},

(11)

and let
X
be a random variable on
S^{n}
equipped with the Haar measure. Then

\textup{Prob}(|f(X)-\mathbb{E}f(X)|>\alpha)\leq 2e^{-c(n+1)\alpha^{2}/\eta^{2}},

(12)

where
\mathbb{E}f(X)
is the expectation value of
f(X)
, and
c
is a positive constant that may be chosen as
c=(18\pi^{3})^{-1}
.

An immediate consequence of these lemmas is that the EE of a Haar-random pure-state will, with overwhelming probability, be nearly equal to the average pure-state EE. Together with the provable fact that the average EE is close to the maximal value
\log d_{A}
when
d_{A}\ll d_{B}
, one concludes that
generic
pure states have near-maximal bipartite entanglement
17
.

III.2
Number entanglement

It is a very general observation that failure to comply to a symmetry opens up the possibility of creating correlations by destroying quantum coherences with the use of a measurement or projection to a charge sector. The nonselective measurement of a single observable
\hat{N}
, a measurement for which the outcome is not recorded, will result in the state
\rho|_{\hat{N}}=\sum_{N}\Pi_{N}\rho\Pi_{N}
, where
\Pi_{N}
is the projector onto the sector of charge
N
. Clearly, a nonzero von Neumann entropy
difference

S(\rho|_{\hat{N}})-S(\rho)>0
implies
[\rho,\hat{N}]\neq 0
. Violation of the symmetry is key to the measurement-induced generation of correlations.

This idea is applied to the problem of symmetric separability as follows. We consider separable states (
1
) with a globally conserved charge,
[\rho,\hat{N}]=0
, and ask whether the state satisfies symmetric separability, Eqn. (
2
). If not, then it should be possible to increase the state’s entropy by destroying quantum coherences through measurement. As before, we treat only the case of observables of the form
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}
or
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}+1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}
, with their localization written
\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}
. The
number entanglement
(NE) of
\rho
with respect to
\hat{N}
was defined in
24
as

\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})=S(\rho|_{\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}})-S(\rho).

(13)

By construction, it measures the increase in von Neumann entropy produced by a local measurement of
\hat{N}_{A}
. (From now on, we follow the common practice of simply writing
\hat{N}_{A}
for
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}
.) Note that the NE is invariant under the exchange of
A
and
B
. Also note that on pure states,
\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\phi})=S(\phi|_{\hat{N}_{A}})
. The next proposition shows that a nonzero NE witnesses the failure of symmetric separability.

Lemma 3
.

Symmetric separability of
\rho
implies
\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})=0
.

The proof is given in the Appendix, following the original proof in
24
, and consists in showing that symmetric separability implies that
\rho
is already block-diagonal in eigenbases for
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}
before any measurement is performed, so
S(\rho|_{\hat{N}_{A}})=S(\rho)
, and no entropy is created by the measurement. Actually, a stronger result is immediately obtained by construction of the NE:

Proposition 4
.

\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})\equiv 0
on
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
.

The previous lemma follows since
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
is a subset of
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
.

III.3
Concentration of the number entanglement

In this section, we determine how much symmetry-induced entanglement is typical among separable
\hat{N}
-symmetric states for degenerate
\hat{N}
. Our goal is to derive a concentration result for the NE in the set
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
. To that end, purifying mixed states will allow us to apply the Lipshitz continuity of pure-state EE, Lemma
1
, and Levy’s concentration inequality, Lemma
2
, to the case of mixed-state NE, Eqn.
13
. The results will rely on the construction of a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, and the partition of
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
into spherical shells equipped with the Haar measure. The construction depends on the realization of a manifold of purifications for
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, the
purifying manifold

\chi_{0}
, and a map
\tau\colon\chi_{0}\to\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
. (See Figure
2
.) The purifying manifold is a smooth manifold within
(\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B})^{\otimes 2}
, whose dimension
D
, also denoted
\text{p-dim }\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, is called the
purification dimension
. The purifying manifold
\chi_{0}
, which is assumed triangulable for simplicity
5
, is covered by a finite collection of domains of integration
\{\mathcal{B}_{i}\}_{i=1,...,M}
, where each
\mathcal{B}_{i}
is diffeomorphic to a closed
D
-dimensional real ball. Each
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, with its pulled-back Euclidean measure, can in turn be split into spherical shells
\mathcal{S}_{ik}
where the restricted Euclidean measure gives rise to the Haar measure. The measure
\tilde{\mu}
on
\chi_{0}
is finally pushed forward to
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
,

\mu(S)=\tilde{\mu}(\cup_{\rho\in S}\;\tau^{-1}(\rho)).

(14)

i
is a
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphism,
j
is
C^{\infty}
injective, while
\pi,\tau
, and
\text{Tr}_{\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}}
are

. Rigorously speaking, the map

As before, we consider observables of the form
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}
or
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}+1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}
. The next proposition, an extension of Lemma
1
, shows that the NE of (purified) densities is of bounded gradient on the purifying manifold:

Proposition 5
.

When
\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}
has dimension
d_{A}d_{B}\geq 3
, the NE is Lipshitz continuous on
\mathscr{D}
, i.e.

|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\sigma})-\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})|\leq\eta\big\lVert\lvert\Pi_{\sigma}\rangle-\lvert\Pi_{\rho}\rangle\big\rVert_{2},

(15)

with
\eta=4\sqrt{2}\log(d_{A}d_{B})
, and where
\lvert\Pi_{\sigma}\rangle,\lvert\Pi_{\rho}\rangle
are any purifications of
\sigma,\rho
on a common space. Specializing to
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
and its purifying manifold, we have :

|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\sigma})-\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})|\leq\eta\big\lVert\lvert\Pi_{\sigma}\rangle-\lvert\Pi_{\rho}\rangle\big\rVert_{2}^{(D)},

(16)

with
D=\textup{p-dim }\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
,
D
-dimensional Euclidean norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2}^{(D)}
, and
\eta=4\sqrt{2}\log D
. Also,
\lvert\Pi_{\sigma}\rangle,\lvert\Pi_{\rho}\rangle
are any purifications of
\sigma,\rho
in a common domain of integration
\mathcal{B}_{i}
.

It then follows from Lemma
2
that

Proposition 6
.

Separable states that are symmetric for
\hat{N}
present a strong concentration of NE around the mean
m_{ik}
in each spherical shell of the purifying manifold: for
\rho\in\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})
,

\textup{Prob}_{\mu}(|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m_{ik}|>\alpha)\leq 2v_{ik}e^{-cD\alpha^{2}/\eta^{2}},

(17)

where
m_{ik}=v_{ik}^{-1}\int_{\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})}d\mu(\sigma)\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\sigma})
, and
D
is the purification dimension.

The NE strongly concentrates around its local mean
m_{ik}
on each shell. Due to (Lipshitz) continuity, the NE must agree where shells meet, forcing local means
m_{ik}
to coalesce around the global mean
m
. We thus obtain

Proposition 7
.

For an observable
\hat{N}
with large enough purification dimension
D
, the NE on
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
strongly concentrates around its mean value:

\textup{Prob}_{\mu}(|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m|>\alpha)\lesssim O(e^{-cD\alpha^{2}/\eta^{2}}),

(18)

where
m
is the (strictly positive) mean NE of
\hat{N}
averaged over all states in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
,
c
is a positive constant that may be chosen as
c=(18\pi^{3})^{-1}
, and
\eta=4\sqrt{2}\log D
.

In the above inequality, we have dropped a term that becomes negligible at (moderately) large values of
D
. (See details in the Appendix.) Therefore, our result applies to all degenerate observables of the form
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}
or
\hat{N}_{A}\otimes 1_{B}+1_{A}\otimes\hat{N}_{B}
, except those with small purification dimension
\textup{p-dim }\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, i.e. operators of lower dimension or operators embodying stringent symmetries with few symmetric states and/or symmetric states that are mostly inseparable.

Proposition
7
reveals the existence of a ‘statistical distance’ between the sets
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
and
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
, in the sense that separable and symmetric states strongly cluster at a finite distance from
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
. States that are close to being symmetrically separable are statistically hard to find. The concentration sharpens overwhelmingly with increasing (purification) dimension. IV.2
Symmetry-induced entanglement as resource

Symsep states and symmetric LOCCs can be considered as free states and operations in certain contexts, especially in the absence of a shared reference frame where they represent fungible information that can be created locally and communicated meaningfully with no additional resource (e.g. a clock or a system of axes). In this context, entanglement will correspond to a (nonlocal, nonfungible) resource, and will allow for the realization of tasks beyond the capabilities of free states alone, e.g. local distinguishability of quantum states, and teleportation
40
;
33
. Regular entanglement (
\rho\in\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}\backslash\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
) and symmetry-induced entanglement (
\rho\in\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}\backslash\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
) both represent resources for these tasks, although with distinct behavior. Symmetry-induced entanglement is likely to be more robust than regular entanglement, however, since
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
is a nullset in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
which in turn has positive volume in
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}}

29
.

Surely, not all resources are equally useful for all tasks. It is well-known that most quantum states are
too entangled
to be useful for quantum speedup in measurement-based quantum computing
8
;
11
. This is explained by the fact that correlations in highly entangled states are well approximated stochastically, so these states cannot solve problems in the complexity class
\mathsf{BQP}\setminus\mathsf{BPP}
. In
11
for instance, it was proven that an
n
-qubit pure state
\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle
cannot provide superpolynomial speedup if
E_{g}(\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle)=n-O(\log n)
, where
E_{g}
is the geometric measure. A concentration result is then obtained for
n\geq 11
,

\text{Prob}\big(E_{g}(\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle)<n-\Omega(\log n)\big)<e^{-n^{2}},

(31)

showing that the fraction of universal resources among
n
-qubit pure states is less than
e^{-n^{2}}
.

The phenomenon of symmetry-induced entanglement does not exist for pure states. For mixed states however, it is of practical interest to determine how symmetry-induced entanglement can be used as a resource for computation, and whether states with symmetry-induced entanglement generically have too little or too much of it to be useful. Because the NE is a symmetry-induced entanglement monotone
24
, Proposition
7
implies that generic states in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
possess a nonzero amount of symmetry-induced entanglement close to average with overwhelming likeliness. Numerical work suggests that this average amount is decreasing with increasing system dimension. See Figs.
3
,
5
,
6
.

IV.3
Multiparty entanglement

When we consider multipartite systems with conserved total charge
\hat{N}=\sum_{i}\hat{N}_{i}
, we should adapt the usual definitions, and consider that a state is
symmetrically partially separable
if it is symmetrically separable with respect to some fixed bipartition, and
symmetrically biseparable
if a convex decomposition
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}
exists where each
\rho_{i}
is symmetrically partially separable (with respect to a bipartition that may depend on
i
). A state will contain symmetry-induced
genuine multipartite entanglement
(GME)
35
if it is not symmetrically biseparable. The proofs of our propositions can be partly generalized to those cases, as we now explain.

Partial and full separability.
Consider a system
\Omega
with
K
subsystems. Then
\rho
is symmetrically partially separable over the fixed bipartition
A\cup B=\Omega
if
\rho\in\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}(\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B})
. This is exactly the case treated already, and all our results apply word for word. The case of symmetrically fully separable states
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{K}
, with termwise symmetry, is also easily covered. We immediately find that
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}=\mathscr{D}^{\textup{sep}}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
(with an obvious redefinition of
\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}
), so that this set has zero measure in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
when
\hat{N}
is degenerate. The NE vanishes identically on
\mathscr{D}_{\hat{N}_{\textup{local}}}
, and concentrates around its mean value on
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
.

Biseparability.
To treat the case of symmetrically biseparable states of system
\Omega
, it is convenient to define them formally over the space

\mathscr{H}_{\textup{bipartitions}}=\bigoplus_{i}\mathscr{H}_{A_{i}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B_{i}},

(32)

where
\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}
is the set of all bipartitions of
\Omega
,
A_{i}\cup B_{i}=\Omega
for all
i
. A state
\rho
is
\hat{N}
-symmetric and biseparable,
\rho\in\mathscr{D}^{\text{2-sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, if

\rho\in\textup{conv}\bigcup_{i}\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}(\mathscr{H}_{A_{i}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B_{i}}).

(33)

A state is symmetrically biseparable,
\rho\in\mathscr{D}^{\textup{sym-2-sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, if

\rho\in\textup{conv}\bigcup_{i}\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}_{i,\textup{local}}}(\mathscr{H}_{A_{i}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B_{i}}),

(34)

where
\hat{N}_{i,\textup{local}}
localizes
\hat{N}
on the bipartition
(A_{i},B_{i})
. By the same argument as before,
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}_{i,\textup{local}}}
is a nullset in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
. Even if we lack a monotone for symmetric biseparability (that is, a generalization of the NE), our results already confirm concentration within each sector
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}(\mathscr{H}_{A_{i}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B_{i}})
. However, we
cannot
conclude that
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{sym-2-sep}}_{\hat{N}}
is of measure zero in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{2-sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, because sectors might be nonorthogonal. In other words, a randomly chosen state in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{2-sep}}_{\hat{N}}
(i.e. symmetric and biseparable) might possess
symmetry-induced
GME with probability less than one. The probability will depend on
\hat{N}
, more specifically on the degree of orthogonality of the sectors
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}(\mathscr{H}_{A_{i}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B_{i}})
, and their dimensions. See Eqn.
6
and its explanation.

Although incomplete, these results are fully compatible, if somewhat complementary, to the fate of entanglement identified in
29
for generalized quantum evolutions to equilibrium (along temperature, time, and spatial separation) with and without the presence of superselection. By imposing extremely severe constraints on the possibilities of states to evolve towards more local, separated forms, symmetries delay the partial or complete disappearance of entanglement (e.g. sudden death). Symmetry-constrained entanglement is more robust than its unconstrained counterpart. It is compelling to study symmetry-induced entanglement as a nonfungible resource for multiparty tasks
40
;
33
;
34
. Is it possible, for instance, to extend to the symmetry-induced case the fact that GME-activatable states coincide with partially separable ones
28
? Another question beyond the scope of this article is the extent to which the decisive role of GME in quantum metrology
19
and quantum cryptography
9
conveys to symmetry-induced GME. Our results might also help characterize the symmetries for which the set of symmetric biseparable states is so thin as to essentially preclude the sudden death of symmetry-constrained GME (e.g. when the sectors
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}(\mathscr{H}_{A_{i}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B_{i}})
are orthogonal). Systems would carry an extremely robust form of GME in the present of such symmetries.

In a different vein, a duality between separability and purity was numerically detected in
44
, and conjectured to hold in general. Entanglement is typical for pure states (pure separable states form a nullset
30
;
31
, and generic pure states are highly entangled
17
), while separability is more and more common as purity is lowered, becoming necessary below a certain threshold (in dimension
d
,
\text{Tr}(\rho^{2})<1/(d-1)
implies separability
13
). It would be interesting to determine if a relationship holds, within the subset
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
, between purity and symmetric separability, given that purities 1 and
1/d
both ensure symmetric separability in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
.

IV.4
Assumptions and scope

Here we discuss some assumptions that were made, and the corresponding scope of our results.

The purifying manifold.
When using the purifying manifold of
\hat{N}
in the proofs, we are assuming that it contains purifications for most states in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
. That is, we assume that lower-dimensional Euclidean pieces of
\chi
— see Appendix
A.3
— as well as its non-Euclidean pieces are somehow negligible compared to the purifying manifold
\chi_{0}
. If however this was not the case, for instance if
\chi_{0}
was vacuous, then our conclusions would be invalidated. Can we characterize the observables
\hat{N}
for which the purifying manifold assumption is wrong, that is, for which the purifying manifold does not represent most states in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
in any physically reasonable sense? We note however that when the assumption is satisfied, our results are independent of the choice of a particular triangulation (or particular finite open cover).

Specific
\hat{N}
.
We stress that our concentration result describes a structural property of operators that only have in common that they are degenerate, and have a well-defined purification dimension
\text{p-dim}\,\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
which is not too small. All operators ‘typical’ in this sense fall within the scope of our propositions. The drawback of abstraction, however, is the difficulty to apply our results to a specific
\hat{N}
without prior knowledge of
\text{p-dim}\,\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
. Establishing results for specific, physically motivated operators is the object of futur investigation.

V
Conclusion

In this work we have studied the Symmetric Separability Problem — deciding whether a given separable and
\hat{N}
-symmetric state is symmetrically separable — or, equivalently, regular separability within a restricted class of states with extended, ‘localized’ symmetry. When
\hat{N}\neq 1
is degenerate, separable and symmetric states fail to be symmetrically separable with probability one.

We have used the NE, a monotone for symmetry-induced entanglement, to show that a bipartite state which is both
\hat{N}
-symmetric and separable will contain an amount of symmetry-induced entanglement which is superexponentially close to the (strictly positive) average amount on
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
. Simply put, in the presence of a typical degenerate symmetry, generic separable states will be far from being symmetrically separable, and will contain a finite amount of symmetry-induced entanglement locked in their charge sectors.

We have discussed the relevance of our results for computation and communication tasks, especially in the presence of a superselection rule, or in the absence of a shared reference frame. In this scenario, symmetrically separable states and symmetric LOCCs represent free fungible information and processes. States in
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
are unique in being constructible out of (fungible) product states and (fungible) LOCCs, and should be considered as the only genuinely unentangled states in the absence of a shared frame, while all other states correspond to nonlocal resources.

Finally, we have shown that our results are also of import to multiparty entanglement, and are consistent with recent findings on the fate of entanglement over general system evolution. Our work supports the view that symmetry-constrained entanglement is more robust than its unconstrained counterpart, and could help characterize systems with optimal GME resilience.

Lemma 5

(Hayden

When

\displaystyle|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\sigma})-\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})|\leq

(86)

where
m_{ik}=v_{ik}^{-1}\int_{\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})}d\mu(\sigma)\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\sigma})
,
D
is the dimension of the purifying manifold, and
\eta=4\sqrt{2}\log D
.

Proposition 21
.

If there exists a state
\rho\in\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})
for which
\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})>0
, then the mean NE is nonzero on
\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})
. In other words, the mean NE is zero on
\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})
iff
\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})\equiv 0
on
\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})=\epsilon>0
, and let
\sigma
be
(\epsilon/\eta)
-close to
\rho
in Bures distance,
D_{B}(\sigma,\rho)<\epsilon/\eta
. From Corollary
3
,
|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\sigma})-\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})|<\epsilon
so
\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\sigma})>0
. Therefore the set
S=\{\rho\in\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})\colon\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})>0\}
is open. If nonempty, we have
\mu(S)>0
, and the mean NE is

m_{ik}=\int_{\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})}d\mu(\sigma)\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\sigma})=\int_{S}d\mu(\sigma)\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\sigma})>0.

(87)

∎

Because
\mathscr{D}^{\textup{symsep}}_{\hat{N}}
is of zero measure in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
(unless
\hat{N}
is nondegenerate), Proposition
21
indicates that a generic
\hat{N}
will have a nonzero mean NE,
m_{ik}>0
, on each
\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})
. We contend that these local means strongly concentrate around
m=\sum_{ik}v_{ik}m_{ik}
, the global mean NE on
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
. Indeed, by Proposition
20
the NE strongly concentrates around its local mean
m_{ik}
on each shell.

We also contend that the NE will have asymptotically vanishing variance on each shell. Indeed, using the cumulative distribution function
v_{ik}^{-1}\textup{Prob}_{\mu}(|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m_{ik}|\leq\alpha)
, we may express the variance of NE on shell
\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})
as:

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left((\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m_{ik})^{2}\right)

(88)

\displaystyle=v_{ik}^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha\;\alpha^{2}\frac{d}{d\alpha}\textup{Prob}_{\mu}(|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m_{ik}|\leq\alpha).

The cumulative distribution function is monotone increasing and, following Proposition
20
, it is comprised in the tight interval between
\ell(\alpha)=1-2e^{-cD\alpha^{2}/\eta^{2}}
and 1. Thus its slope can be large only on a small set, and on average is expected to be close to that of
\ell(\alpha)
. Therefore,

\text{Var}(\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho}))\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha\;\alpha^{2}\frac{d}{d\alpha}\ell(\alpha)=O\left((\log D)^{2}/D\right).

(89)

By (Lipschitz) continuity, Proposition
17
, the NE must agree where shells meet, and this in turn forces local means
m_{ik}
to remain close to the global mean
m=\sum_{ik}v_{ik}m_{ik}
. The phenomenon also tends to keep their variance
\text{Var}(\{m_{ik}\})
small.

Define
\epsilon_{ik}=|m-m_{ik}|
. It follows from what has just been said that most of the
\epsilon_{ik}
are small, and from the properties of the variance, that
\text{Var}(\{\epsilon_{ik}\})=\text{Var}(\{m_{ik}\})
is also small. Now for any
\alpha>\epsilon_{ik}
, we have
|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m|>\alpha\implies|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m_{ik}|>\alpha-\epsilon_{ik}

\tau(\mathcal{S}_{ik})

:

\textup{Prob}_{\mu}(|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m|>\alpha)\leq\textup{Prob}_{\mu}(|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m_{ik}|>\alpha-\epsilon_{ik})\leq 2v_{ik}e^{-cD(\alpha-\epsilon_{ik})^{2}/\eta^{2}}.

(90)

Summing up the contributions from all shells, we find

\textup{Prob}_{\mu}(|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m|>\alpha)\leq 2\sum_{ik}v_{ik}e^{-cD(\alpha-\epsilon_{ik})^{2}/\eta^{2}},

(91)

for a
\mu
-random states
\rho\in\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}

\mathbb{E}h(X)\leq h(\mathbb{E}X)+\text{Var}(X)\,\text{sup}\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(x)}{2},

(92)

with
h(x)=\exp(-cDx^{2}/\eta^{2})
, and random variable
X
having value
\alpha-\epsilon_{ik}
with probability
v_{ik}
. We find

\sum_{ik}v_{ik}e^{-cD(\alpha-\epsilon_{ik})^{2}/\eta^{2}}\leq e^{-cD(\alpha-\bar{\epsilon})^{2}/\eta^{2}}+\text{Var}(\{\epsilon_{ik}\})O\left(\frac{D^{3}}{\eta^{6}}e^{-O(D^{2}/\eta^{4})}\right),

(93)

where
\bar{\epsilon}=\sum_{ik}v_{ik}\epsilon_{ik}\to 0
. Remembering that
\eta=O(\log D)
, we see that the last term vanishes even for moderate values of
D
, unless the
\epsilon_{ik}
’s have a violently diverging variance. We have argued above that such was not the case. We conclude that (
91
) can be restated as:

Proposition 22
.

For an observable
\hat{N}
with large enough purification dimension
D

\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
strongly concentrates around its mean value:

\textup{Prob}_{\mu}(|\Delta S_{\hat{N}}({\rho})-m|>\alpha)\lesssim O(e^{-cD\alpha^{2}/\eta^{2}}),

where

\hat{N}
averaged over all states in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{sep}}_{\hat{N}}
,

\eta=4\sqrt{2}\log D
