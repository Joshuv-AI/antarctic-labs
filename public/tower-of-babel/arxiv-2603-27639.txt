Sharper upper bounds for q q -ary and constant-weight B 2 B_{2} codes
Stefano Della Fiore

Abstract
We derive refined entropy upper bounds for q q -ary B 2 B_{2} codes by exploiting the Fourier structure of the i.i.d. difference distribution D = X − Y D=X-Y . Since the pmf of D D is an autocorrelation, its Fourier series is a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial of degree at most q − 1 q-1 . This leads to a natural convex relaxation over candidate difference distributions, equivalently expressible through an infinite family of positive semidefinite Toeplitz constraints. The resulting formulation admits a simple Gram interpretation and yields certified upper bounds through truncated semidefinite programs. Combined with the prefix–suffix method, this gives improved asymptotic rate upper bounds for q q -ary B 2 B_{2} codes; in particular, for q ∈ { 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 } q\in\{9,10,11,12,13\} the resulting values improve on the best bounds known in the literature. We also study binary constant-weight B 2 B_{2} codes. Extending the distance-distribution method of Cohen, Litsyn, and Zémor to the constant-weight setting, and combining it with Litsyn’s asymptotic linear-programming bound for constant-weight codes, we derive a new upper bound on the constant-weight B 2 B_{2} rate.



I
Introduction

Let
[0,q-1]=\{0,1,\dots,q-1\}
, and let
C_{n}\subset[0,q-1]^{n}
be a
q
-ary code
of length
n
with
|C_{n}|=M
. We define the asymptotic rate of a family of codes

\{C_{n}\}
by

R=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log_{q}|C_{n}|,

while logarithms without subscript are taken in base
2
.

Definition I.1

A
q
-ary code
C_{n}=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{M}\}\subset[0,q-1]^{n}
is a
B_{2}
code if all sums

c_{i}+c_{j},\qquad 1\leq i\leq j\leq M,

computed over the reals, are distinct.

For
q=2
, this is equivalent to the usual notion of a
\bar{2}
-separable code.
Upper bounds on the rate of
q
-ary
B_{2}
codes were obtained in

[
2
,
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
]
; see also the references therein.
The constant-weight binary setting was recently investigated in

[
9
]
.

A standard route to asymptotic upper bounds is to relate the code cardinality to the entropy
of suitably chosen random variables associated with prefixes and suffixes of codewords.
For
q
-ary
B_{2}
codes, the key local object is the integer difference

D=X-Y,

where
X
and
Y
are i.i.d.
q
-ary random variables. The strength of the resulting
rate bound depends on how sharply one can upper bound
\Ent(D)
. In our earlier work

[
6
]
, this entropy term was bounded through a coarse relaxation based only on
the collision constraint

\mathbb{P}(X=Y)=\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}\mathbb{P}(X=a)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{q},

which led to a closed-form upper bound on
\Ent(D)
, and hence to an explicit asymptotic
rate bound.

The starting point of the present paper is that the difference distribution
D=X-Y
carries
substantially more structure than this single scalar constraint. Its probability mass function
is an autocorrelation, and therefore its Fourier series is a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial
of degree at most
q-1
. Equivalently, its coefficients satisfy an infinite family of Toeplitz
positive semidefinite constraints. This observation leads to a natural convex relaxation over
candidate difference distributions. The relaxation is strong enough to improve the entropy bound,
yet remains numerically tractable through truncated semidefinite programs (SDPs). At the same time, the
Toeplitz formulation admits a direct Gram interpretation, which makes the origin of the
semidefinite constraints transparent.

This Fourier–Toeplitz viewpoint yields a refinement of the entropy step in the prefix–suffix
method and leads to a new upper bound on the asymptotic rate of
q
-ary
B_{2}
codes.
Numerically, the resulting bound improves on the best values previously available to us for

q\in\{9,10,11,12,13\}
.

The paper also contains a second result, concerning binary constant-weight
B_{2}
codes of
length
n
and weight
w=\alpha n(1+o(1))
. In this setting the relevant tool is not the
prefix–suffix decomposition, but the distance-distribution method underlying the binary bound of
Cohen, Litsyn, and Zémor
[
4
]
. We show that this argument admits a
constant-weight refinement. The
B_{2}
property still provides the required combinatorial control
on the distance distribution, while the matching lower estimate is obtained from Litsyn’s
asymptotic linear-programming bound for constant-weight codes
[
5
]
. This leads to a new
upper bound on the asymptotic rate
r_{B_{2}}^{\mathrm{cw}}(\alpha)
, improving the recent bound of Sima, Li, Shomorony, and Milenković
[
9
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sections II and III we develop the Fourier and Toeplitz
relaxations for the difference distribution and derive the corresponding entropy bound. In
Section IV we combine this bound with the prefix–suffix argument to obtain a new upper bound for

q
-ary
B_{2}
codes, and in Section V we report the resulting numerical values. Section VI is
devoted to the constant-weight binary setting, where we derive a Cohen–Litsyn-type upper bound
and compare it with the recent result of
[
9
]
.

II
Fourier representation of the difference distribution

Let
X
be a random variable with values in
[0,q-1]
and pmf
P=(p_{0},\dots,p_{q-1})
,
and let
Y
be an independent copy of
X
. Consider the integer difference

D=X-Y.

Then
D
takes values in
\{-(q-1),\dots,q-1\}
and its pmf

r=(r_{-(q-1)},\dots,r_{q-1})
is

r_{k}=\mathbb{P}(D=k)=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}p_{i}p_{i+k},\qquad k=-(q-1),\dots,q-1,

(1)

with the convention
p_{j}=0
for
j\notin\{0,\dots,q-1\}
.

Introduce the characteristic function of
X
,

\phi(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[e^{i\theta X}]=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}p_{j}e^{ij\theta},\qquad\theta\in[0,2\pi],

and the characteristic function of
D
,

\psi_{D}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[e^{i\theta(X-Y)}]=\phi(\theta)\overline{\phi(\theta)}=|\phi(\theta)|^{2}.

(2)

On the other hand, by Fourier series,

\psi_{D}(\theta)=\sum_{k=-(q-1)}^{q-1}r_{k}e^{ik\theta}=:R(\theta),

(3)

where
R(\theta)
is a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most
q-1
with real symmetric coefficients

r_{-k}=r_{k}
. Combining (
2
) and (
3
),

R(\theta)=|\phi(\theta)|^{2}\geq 0,\qquad\theta\in[0,2\pi].

(4)

Moreover, the coefficients satisfy the inversion formula

\displaystyle r_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}R(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\phi(\theta)|^{2}e^{-ik\theta}\,d\theta,\qquad|k|\leq q-1.

In particular,

r_{0}=\mathbb{P}(X=Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}p_{i}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\phi(\theta)|^{2}\,d\theta.

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

r_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}p_{i}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{q}.

(5)

III
Entropy bounds via Fourier positivity and Toeplitz Gram matrices

Let
X,Y
be i.i.d. on
[0,q-1]
with pmf
P
and let
D=X-Y
. Define

h_{q}^{\star}:=\sup\Ent(D),

where the supremum is over all
P
, and
\Ent
denotes Shannon entropy (in bits), with the convention
0\log 0:=0
.

In
[
6
]
we used the relaxed bound

\Ent(D)\leq\bar{h}_{q}:=\Ent\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{q},\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2q},\dots,\tfrac{1}{2q}}_{2q-2\ \text{times}}\Big)=\frac{1}{q}\log q+\frac{q-1}{q}\log(2q),

(6)

obtained by maximizing entropy under the sole constraint
r_{0}\geq 1/q
.

III-A

Fourier-analytic relaxation

Definition III.1

For fixed
q\geq 2
define
h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}
as the optimal value of

\displaystyle\text{maximize}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=-(q-1)}^{q-1}r_{k}\log r_{k}

(7)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle r_{k}\geq 0,\quad\sum_{k=-(q-1)}^{q-1}r_{k}=1,\quad r_{-k}=r_{k},

\displaystyle r_{0}\geq\frac{1}{q},

\displaystyle R(\theta):=\sum_{k=-(q-1)}^{q-1}r_{k}e^{ik\theta}\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\theta\in[0,2\pi].

Lemma III.2

For every
q\geq 2
,

h_{q}^{\star}\leq h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}\leq\bar{h}_{q}.

Proof:

Any i.i.d. difference pmf
r
satisfies the probability and symmetry constraints, the collision constraint

r_{0}\geq 1/q
by (
5
), and Fourier nonnegativity
R(\theta)=|\phi(\theta)|^{2}\geq 0
by
(
4
). Hence it is feasible for (
7
), giving
h_{q}^{\star}\leq h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}
.
Dropping
R(\theta)\geq 0
enlarges the feasible set and yields (
6
), so
h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}\leq\bar{h}_{q}
.
∎

III-B

Toeplitz formulation and Gram interpretation

Define
r_{k}=0
for
|k|\geq q
. For
N\geq 1
let the Toeplitz matrix
T^{(N)}(r)
be

T^{(N)}(r)_{ij}=r_{i-j},\qquad i,j\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}.

Since
r
comes from an autocorrelation as in (
1
), then Toeplitz positive semidefiniteness is immediate from a Gram construction.
Let
p=(p_{0},\dots,p_{q-1})
and extend it to a length-
(N+q-1)
vector

\tilde{p}=(p_{0},\dots,p_{q-1},0,\dots,0)
. For
i\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}
let
\tilde{p}^{(i)}
be the shift of
\tilde{p}

by
i
positions (padding with zeros). Then

\langle\tilde{p}^{(i)},\tilde{p}^{(j)}\rangle=\sum_{t}\tilde{p}_{t-i}\tilde{p}_{t-j}=\sum_{u}p_{u}p_{u+(i-j)}=r_{i-j}.

Hence
T^{(N)}(r)
is the Gram matrix of the vectors
\{\tilde{p}^{(0)},\dots,\tilde{p}^{(N-1)}\}
and therefore

T^{(N)}(r)\succeq 0
. The relaxation keeps the necessary Gram condition while optimizing directly over
r
.

Definition III.3
(Toeplitz SDP formulation)

For fixed
q\geq 2
define
h_{q}^{\mathrm{SDP}}
as the optimal value of

\displaystyle\text{maximize}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=-(q-1)}^{q-1}r_{k}\log r_{k}

(8)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle r_{k}\geq 0,\quad\sum_{k=-(q-1)}^{q-1}r_{k}=1,\quad r_{-k}=r_{k},

\displaystyle r_{0}\geq\frac{1}{q},

\displaystyle T^{(N)}(r)\succeq 0\quad\text{for all }N\geq 1.

The correspondence between nonnegative trigonometric polynomials and positive definite Toeplitz forms is classical, see for example
[
1
,
10
,
11
]
. Hence we get the following equivalence.

Proposition III.4

For every
q\geq 2
we have
h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}=h_{q}^{\mathrm{SDP}}
.

IV
Improved bounds for
B_{2}
codes

We now show how the improved entropy bound
h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}
for

D=X-Y
enters the derivation of an upper bound on the rate
of
q
-ary
B_{2}
codes. The proof below follows the same structure as in

[
6
]
and is self-contained.

Let
C_{n}
be a
q
-ary
B_{2}
code of length
n
with
M=|C_{n}|
codewords.
Split each codeword into a prefix of length
e
and a suffix of length
f=n-e
,
and partition
C_{n}
into classes
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}
according to the prefix.
Let
M_{i}=|P_{i}|
and denote by
\ell_{i}\in[0,q-1]^{e}
the common prefix of
P_{i}
.
Thus each class can be written as

P_{i}=\{(\ell_{i},w):w\in W_{i}\},\qquad|W_{i}|=M_{i}\,,

where
W_{i}
is the set of suffixes of the codewords in the subcode
P_{i}
.

Now, let
(X,Y)
be a random ordered pair of suffixes obtained by choosing uniformly
an ordered pair of codewords inside the
same
prefix class, and then taking
their suffixes. Then
(X,Y)
is uniform over
\sum_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}^{2}
possibilities. Hence

\Ent(X,Y)=\log\sum_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}^{2}.

Lemma IV.1

If
C_{n}
is a
B_{2}
code, then the map
\Phi(w_{1},w_{2})=w_{1}-w_{2}
is injective on
ordered pairs of distinct suffixes (taken within a prefix class).
In other words, the multiset of differences

\{\,w_{1}-w_{2}\mid(w_{1},w_{2})\in W_{i}\times W_{i},\ w_{1}\neq w_{2},\ i=1,\dots,r\,\}

contains no repetitions.

Proof:

We provide a short self-contained proof in the same spirit as in

[
6
]
. Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that there exist
four
distinct
codewords

(\ell_{h},w_{1}),(\ell_{h},w_{2})\in P_{h},\qquad(\ell_{m},w_{3}),(\ell_{m},w_{4})\in P_{m},

such that

\Phi(w_{1},w_{2})=\Phi(w_{3},w_{4}),

(9)

either with
h=m
and
(w_{1},w_{2})\neq(w_{3},w_{4})
, or with
h\neq m
.

Case 1:
h=m
.

If
\{w_{1},w_{2}\}\cap\{w_{3},w_{4}\}\neq\emptyset
, then (
9
) either forces

(w_{1},w_{2})=(w_{3},w_{4})
or contradicts the
B_{2}
property of the code
C_{n}
.

Otherwise
\{w_{1},w_{2}\}\cap\{w_{3},w_{4}\}=\emptyset
.
From (
9
) we have
w_{1}-w_{2}=w_{3}-w_{4}
, hence
w_{1}+w_{4}=w_{2}+w_{3}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle(\ell_{h},w_{1})+(\ell_{h},w_{4})

\displaystyle=(2\ell_{h},\,w_{1}+w_{4})

\displaystyle=(2\ell_{h},\,w_{2}+w_{3})

\displaystyle=(\ell_{h},w_{2})+(\ell_{h},w_{3}).

(10)

The two unordered pairs of codewords

\{(\ell_{h},w_{1}),(\ell_{h},w_{4})\}
and
\{(\ell_{h},w_{2}),(\ell_{h},w_{3})\}

are distinct (the four codewords are distinct), yet (
10
)
shows they have the same sum, contradicting the
B_{2}
property.

Case 2:
h\neq m
.

From (
9
) we again obtain
w_{1}+w_{4}=w_{2}+w_{3}
, hence

\displaystyle(\ell_{h},w_{1})+(\ell_{m},w_{4})

\displaystyle=(\ell_{h}+\ell_{m},\,w_{1}+w_{4})

\displaystyle=(\ell_{h}+\ell_{m},\,w_{2}+w_{3})

\displaystyle=(\ell_{h},w_{2})+(\ell_{m},w_{3}).

Thus two distinct unordered pairs of codewords have the same sum, again
contradicting the
B_{2}
property.
In both cases we reach a contradiction.
∎

Let
Z:=X-Y\in\mathbb{Z}^{f}
denote the componentwise difference of the two
suffixes (over the integers), i.e.,
Z=(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{f})
with
Z_{j}=X_{j}-Y_{j}
.
From Lemma
IV.1
we obtain the entropy decomposition

\Ent(X,Y)=\Ent(Z)+\mathbb{P}(Z=0)\,\Ent(X,Y\mid Z=0).

Lemma IV.2

Let
Z=X-Y
be the suffix difference defined above. Then

\mathbb{P}(Z=0)\,\Ent(X,Y\mid Z=0)\leq\frac{r}{M}\log M.

In particular, with the choice

e=\Big\lfloor\log_{q}(2M)-\log_{q}\log M\Big\rfloor,\qquad r=q^{e},

(11)

we have that
\mathbb{P}(Z=0)\,\Ent(X,Y\mid Z=0)\leq 2
.

Proof:

Let
S_{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|P_{i}|^{2}
. By construction,
(X,Y)
is uniform over the

S_{2}
ordered pairs of suffixes drawn within the same prefix class, hence each
such ordered pair has probability
1/S_{2}
.

The event
Z=0
is equivalent to
X=Y
, which corresponds exactly to the

M=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|P_{i}|
diagonal ordered pairs (one per codeword). Therefore

\mathbb{P}(Z=0)=\mathbb{P}(X=Y)=\frac{M}{S_{2}}.

(12)

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

S_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|P_{i}|^{2}\geq\frac{\big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}|P_{i}|\big)^{2}}{r}=\frac{M^{2}}{r},

hence from (
12
) we obtain
\mathbb{P}(Z=0)\leq r/M
.

Moreover, since all diagonal pairs have the same probability
1/S_{2}
,
the conditional distribution of
(X,Y)
given
Z=0
is uniform over its support,
which has size
M
. Thus

\Ent(X,Y\mid Z=0)=\log M.

Finally, with (
11
) we have
r=q^{e}\leq 2M/\log M
, and therefore

\mathbb{P}(Z=0)\,\Ent(X,Y\mid Z=0)\leq\frac{r}{M}\log M\leq 2\,.

∎

Write
Z=(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{f})
. By subadditivity,

\Ent(Z)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{f}\Ent(Z_{j}).

Fix
j\in\{1,\dots,f\}
. Let
r^{(j)}
be the pmf of
Z_{j}=X_{j}-Y_{j}
. Let
I\in\{1,\dots,r\}
denote the random index of the prefix class from which
the ordered pair of codewords is drawn.
Conditioned on the prefix class index
I=i
, the pair
(X_{j},Y_{j})
is i.i.d. on
[0,q-1]
, hence the conditional difference pmf
r^{(j)}_{|I=i}
is feasible
for (
7
). Since the feasible set of
(
7
) is convex and
r^{(j)}=\sum_{i}\mathbb{P}(I=i)\,r^{(j)}_{|I=i}
is a convex combination of these conditional pmfs, it follows
that
r^{(j)}
is feasible for (
7
) as well. Therefore

\Ent(Z_{j})\leq h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}},

and consequently

\Ent(Z)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{f}\Ent(Z_{j})\leq f\,h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}.

We now fix
e
as in (
11
). Then Lemma
IV.2
gives

\mathbb{P}(Z=0)\Ent(X,Y\mid Z=0)\leq 2
, and hence

\log\sum_{i=1}^{r}|P_{i}|^{2}=\Ent(X,Y)\leq f\,h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}+2.

(13)

On the other hand, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\frac{M^{2}}{r}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}|P_{i}|^{2}.

(14)

Using (
14
) and (
13
) we obtain

2\log M-\log r\leq f\,h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}+2.

With the choice (
11
) we have

\log r=e\log q=\log(2M)-\log\log M+O(1),

hence

2\log M-\log r=\log M+\log\log M+O(1).

(15)

Moreover, (
11
) also gives

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle=\frac{\log M}{\log q}+O(\log\log M),

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=n-e=n-\frac{\log M}{\log q}+O(\log\log M).

(16)

Substituting (
15
)–(
16
) into

2\log M-\log r\leq f\,h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}+2
and absorbing constants yields

\Big(1+\frac{h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}}{\log q}\Big)\log M\leq n\,h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}+O(\log\log M).

Dividing by
n
and using
\log\log M=o(n)
(since
M\leq q^{n}
) we obtain

\frac{1}{n}\log M\leq\frac{h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}\log q}{\log q+h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}}+o(1).

Therefore we get the following theorem.

Theorem IV.3

For integer
q\geq 2
, let
R_{b}
be the asymptotic rate of
q
-ary
B_{2}
codes. Then

R_{b}\leq\frac{h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}}{\log q+h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}},

where
h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}
is the optimal value of the Fourier-analytic
convex program (
7
), equivalently of the Toeplitz
semidefinite program (
8
).

V
Numerical evaluation and comparison with known bounds

We evaluate the bound by solving a truncated Toeplitz SDP, imposing

T^{(N)}(r)\succeq 0
only for
N\leq N_{\max}
. This relaxes the infinite
constraint set and therefore yields a certified upper bound:

h_{q}^{\star}\leq h_{q}^{\mathrm{F}}=h_{q}^{\mathrm{SDP}}\leq h_{q,N_{\max}}.

Using Theorem
IV.3
, we obtain the explicit rate upper bound

R_{b}\leq R_{q}^{\mathrm{new}}:=\frac{h_{q,N_{\max}}}{\log q+h_{q,N_{\max}}}.

I
reports values for
N_{\max}=100
and compares them with

.

VI
A Cohen–Litsyn upper bound for constant-weight
B_{2}
codes

Let
C^{(w)}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}
be a binary
B_{2}
code of constant weight

w=\alpha n(1+o(1)),\qquad 0<\alpha<1,

and rate

R=\frac{1}{n}\log|C^{(w)}|.

Since all codewords have the same weight, only even distances occur. Let

B^{(w)}_{2i}=\frac{1}{|C^{(w)}|}\bigl|\{(x,y)\in C^{(w)}\times C^{(w)}:d(x,y)=2i\}\bigr|

denote the distance distribution. We write

h(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)

for the binary entropy function.

As in Cohen et al.
[
4
]
and as in the previous section, the
B_{2}
property implies that the map

(x,y)\mapsto x-y

is injective on ordered pairs of distinct codewords. If
d(x,y)=2i
, then

x-y\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n}
has exactly
i
entries equal to
+1
, exactly
i

entries equal to
-1
, and
n-2i
zero entries. Hence the number of such
difference vectors is

\binom{n}{i}\binom{n-i}{i}=\binom{n}{2i}\binom{2i}{i}.

Therefore

|C^{(w)}|\,B^{(w)}_{2i}\leq\binom{n}{i}\binom{n-i}{i},

that is,

B^{(w)}_{2i}\leq\frac{\binom{n}{i}\binom{n-i}{i}}{|C^{(w)}|}.

Setting
i=\xi n(1+o(1))
we get

\frac{1}{n}\log B^{(w)}_{2\xi n}\leq h(2\xi)+2\xi-R+o(1).

(17)

Fig. 1:
Comparison of upper bounds on the asymptotic rate of binary
constant-weight
B_{2}
codes. The curve labeled “Ours” corresponds to
Theorem
VI.1
, while “Sima et al.” denotes the bound

R_{S}(\alpha)
from
[
9
]
.
The upper bound for general codes from
[
4
]
and the constructive lower bound from
[
9
]
are also shown for reference.

Let
\delta_{\mathrm{LP}}(R,\alpha)
denote the constant-weight linear programming
upper bound on the relative minimum distance, namely

\delta_{\mathrm{LP}}(R,\alpha)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\beta\leq\alpha\\
h(\beta)=R\end{subarray}}2\,\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)-\beta(1-\beta)}{1+2\sqrt{\beta(1-\beta)}}.

By the argument leading to equation (29) of Lemma 3 of
[
5
]
, one can show there exists an index

i=\xi n(1+o(1))
with

2\xi\leq\delta_{\mathrm{LP}}(R,\alpha)

such that

B^{(w)}_{2\xi n}\geq|C^{(w)}|\,\frac{\binom{w}{\xi n}\binom{n-w}{\xi n}}{\binom{n}{w}}\,k_{n,w,\xi},

where
k_{n,w,\xi}
is the polynomial prefactor arising from Litsyn’s estimate, in particular,
k_{n,w,\xi}=n^{-O(1)}
, so it is negligible on the exponential scale. Taking logarithms, we obtain

\frac{1}{n}\log B^{(w)}_{2\xi n}\geq R-h(\alpha)+\alpha h\!\left(\frac{\xi}{\alpha}\right)+(1-\alpha)h\!\left(\frac{\xi}{1-\alpha}\right)+o(1).

(18)

Combining (

Define

\Psi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\xi)=\log\!\left(\frac{1-2\xi}{\sqrt{(\alpha-\xi)(1-\alpha-\xi)}}\right),

\Psi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\xi)\geq 0

For every
\alpha\in(0,1)
