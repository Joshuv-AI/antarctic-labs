Abstract
Ambient Internet of Things (A-IoT) targets energy harvesting (EH), battery-less devices as a simple connectivity solution for extensive ultra-low-power deployments. These devices typically face intermittent energy availability, making uplink reports increasingly susceptible to access collisions and energy outages. In this paper, we build upon the cellular standardization of A-IoT and examine the paging-triggered contention-based random access (CBRA) framework for uplink reporting. We analyze the effects of energy availability and collisions on these systems and introduce an EH-aware access control mechanism. In this mechanism, the reader broadcasts an access probability in the paging message, which helps regulate the number of devices attempting random access. Results show that, unlike the baselines, the proposed method scales well under dense deployments by keeping collisions nearly constant, improving access efficiency, and substantially reducing the number of paging rounds required for successful reporting. These results highlight the importance of lightweight reader-side access control for reliable and resource-efficient reporting in A-IoT environments.

Introduction

The next wave of massive Internet of Things (IoT)will involve ultra-low-power devices that operate under
\mu
W power budgets and are powered by energy harvesting (EH). This development has motivated the adoption of Ambient IoT (A-IoT)within the 3rd generation partnership project (3GPP)to enable maintenance-free and energy-neutral operation
[
1
]
. These devices will be capable of short bursts of signaling and data exchange, while supporting reduced coverage and complexity compared to narrowband IoT (NB-IoT), and reduced capability (RedCap)
[
2
,
3
]
.

In A-IoT, communication starts with the reader sending a paging signal,
i.e.
, a reader-to-device (R2D)phase. After receiving this signal, devices compete for uplink access,
i.e.
, device-to-reader (D2R)phase, using two types of Random Access (RA)methods: contention-based random access (CBRA)and contention-free random access (CFRA), which depend on predefined access occasions (AOs).
CFRAis suitable when paging a single device or a small group of devices, as it allows the reader to pre-assign resources individually, thereby reducing overall latency in the RAprocess. However, as the number of devices increases, pre-assigning resources becomes inefficient. In situations where the reader needs to trigger multiple devices, CBRAis a more scalable option
[
1
]
. The basic CBRAprocedure follows the principles of slotted ALOHA, where devices randomly select AOs. If there is a collision, the access attempt fails, requiring the device to try again during subsequent paging opportunities
[
4
,
5
]
.

A significant challenge in A-IoTis that the availability of devices is heavily influenced by their energy storage status, which is often duty-cycled. This means that devices may miss paging signals as they strive to harvest enough energy for future AOs
[
6
]
. Moreover, even if they do receive a paging signal, they may be unable to complete the CBRAdue to energy outages or collisions with other device transmissions. As a result, devices often make repeated access attempts during times of network congestion, which can further deplete their stored energy. These issues can lead to increased latency and wasted energy, while EH-driven device unavailability can severely affect paging reception, access completion, and inventory latency
[
6
,
7
]
.

Existing studies on A-IoThave focused on 3GPPstandardization aspects, including the paging-triggered CBRA
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
3
]
. However, previous research has mainly described these standardized access mechanisms, whereas reader-side control strategies that explicitly account for intermittent device availability to regulate CBRAcontention remain largely unexplored. In this paper, we address this gap by proposing an EH-aware access probability control mechanism for CBRAuplink reporting that remains aligned with the ongoing 3GPPA-IoTframework, while improving scalability and reporting reliability under dense deployments. Specifically, we model asynchronous paging-triggered CBRAuplink reporting for EH-powered A-IoTdevices under Bernoulli reader requests. We also provide a compact approximation for the per-round CBRAsuccess probability. Additionally, we propose an EH-aware access-probability control per AO, broadcast via paging, that improves reliability/efficiency compared to a fixed standard CBRA.
The main findings show that the proposed EH-aware access control maintains a nearly constant collision probability as the number of devices increases, in contrast to the rapid increase in collisions of the baseline scheme. Moreover, the proposed method improves resource efficiency and reduces the number of paging rounds required for successful reporting.

Before a device can transmit its report, it must successfully complete an A-IoTCBRAhandshake, which is initiated by an R2Dpaging message from the reader in each paging round
[
8
]
, as shown in Fig.
3
. Specifically, the paging message targets connected devices and configures a set of candidate AOsfor the subsequent D2Rtransmission of the Access Random ID (MSG1). Each paging round offers
R
orthogonal CBRAresources (AOs), which can be interpreted as time-frequency opportunities reserved for RA.

AOsare not referenced to a globally fixed-slot structure such as TTI. Instead, the beginning of each AOset is explicitly indicated by an R2Dpaging message, which also indicates whether additional AOswill follow in subsequent trigger messages.
Each triggered A-IoTdevice then performs a
one-shot
random selection of an AOwithin this configured set by drawing a discrete uniform index
i\in\{0,\ldots,R-1\}
. Note that the index
i

does not represent a contention back-off. Instead, the device uses a counter only as an implementation aid to
locate
the preselected AOwithin the same paging round in an asynchronous, trigger-defined sequence. When the counter reaches zero, the device transmits MSG1 in that AOusing a 16-bit random ID. After MSG1 is transmitted or the access opportunity expires, the counter is reset and a new index
i
is drawn the next time the device is triggered.

We assume that a device can attempt CBRAonly if it has sufficient stored energy at the paging instant as indicated earlier.
Such
eligible
devices spend
E_{\mathrm{RA}}
energy units attempting CBRA. The reader listens to all
R
AOs, and if exactly one device selects a particular AO, the corresponding access attempt is successful, and the device may proceed with the remaining handshake and report delivery. If two or more devices select the same AO, a collision occurs, and we assume that all attempts mapped to that AOfail. In that case, a device may re-attempt in subsequent paging rounds, subject to its energy availability.

Each trigger-defined set provides

R
AOs,
defined by the number of time/frequency-domain resources.
If a paging round contains
Y
trigger-defined sets, the total number of AOsconfigured in that paging round is
YR
. For analytical convenience, we index paging rounds by a sequence number
\tau=1,\ldots,T
, being
T
the total number of paging rounds during the simulation.
In each paging round, every energy-eligible device in the polled group attempts the paging-triggered CBRAround with an
access probability
q
and, conditioned on attempting, selects one of the configured AOsuniformly with probability
1/R
. This baseline captures a classical slotted-ALOHA access rule.
An access attempt succeeds only if exactly one device selects the corresponding AO; otherwise, a collision occurs and all devices mapped to that AOfail and may retry in a later paging round, subject to energy availability.

II-B

Energy consumption and harvesting model

We assume harvested energy is stored in a capacitor with capacitance
C
. The capacitor charges when the harvesting current
I_{h}
exceeds the load current
I_{c}
, and discharges when the load draws more current than is harvested. The load includes all energy-consuming elements of the system, including the MCU, sensors, radio, and inherent capacitor leakage. They are collectively modeled as an equivalent resistance
R_{eq}

\displaystyle R_{eq}^{(s)}=\frac{V_{supply}}{I_{c}^{(s)}},

(1)

where

I_{c}^{(s)}
the current consumption in state
s

\displaystyle V^{(s)}(\Delta t,V_{0})=I_{h}R_{eq}^{(s)}\left(1-e^{-\frac{\Delta t}{R_{eq}^{(s)}C}}\right)+V_{0}e^{-\frac{\Delta t}{R_{eq}^{(s)}C}},

(2)

where, without loss of generality,
I_{h}
is considered constant during the time interval
\Delta t
.
To model
I_{h}
, we use EHdata from the power management integrated circuit collected in
[
9

.
Finally, the energy

\displaystyle E=\frac{1}{2}CV^{2}.

We introduce a lightweight reader-side mechanism for adaptive CBRAcontrol. The main idea is to regulate the number of devices contending in the paging round
\tau

q_{\tau}

The per-round probability that a polled device successfully completes the CBRAMSG1 transmission depends on both energy availability and contention. Since an energy-available device attempts access with probability

p_{1,2}^{(i)}(\tau)=p_{e}^{(i)}q_{\tau}.

An ideal scheduler assigns distinct AOsto all contending devices, resulting in collision-free access. If the number of competing devices exceeds the number of available AOs, the scheduler randomly selects a number of devices matching the available AOsand assigns one to each to prevent collisions during that paging round. While this approach is not feasible in practice, it serves as an upper bound for performance.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we examined asynchronous CBRAuplink reporting in A-IoTnetworks using a Bernoulli-based reader polling. We evaluated four access policies: an ideal collision-free benchmark, a naive CBRAbaseline in which every energy-available device attempts access (
q=1
), a static slotted-ALOHA baseline with
q=R/N
, and a proposed EH-aware control in which the reader broadcasts an access probability to regulate the offered load per AO. The results indicated that the naive policy becomes significantly limited by collisions as the number of devices increases, which greatly reduces both resource efficiency and access delay.
The static slotted-ALOHA baseline alleviates this effect by limiting the number of contenders per AO, but it still operates with a relatively high collision level.
In contrast, the proposed EH-aware method maintains a nearly constant level of collisions and achieves a considerably higher number of successful MSG2 receptions per used AO, while also requiring substantially fewer paging rounds for each successful MSG2 compared to the two fixed baselines. These findings suggest that lightweight access-probability control at the reader side is an effective mechanism for enhancing the reliability and efficiency of A-IoTreporting under conditions of intermittent energy availability.
