EDRNet-based adolescent idiopathic scoliosis screening method using bare-back images
Authors: Xingyu Duan, Linan Wang, Zhengyong Tao, Mengqi Zhu, Dingqi You, Yuxin Gao, Zongqiang Yang, Wensheng Liao, Ningkui Niu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAdolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) affects 2–3% of adolescents. Current screening relies on X-rays, which limits large-scale application due to radiation exposure and equipment requirements. This study aimed to develop a radiation-free, efficient screening method using bare-back images and an efficient dilated residual network (EDRNet).MethodsIn this study, 300 adolescent patients with scoliosis and 300 adolescent patients without spinal diseases were selected from among those treated at the Department of Orthopedics of Ningxia Medical University General Hospital and the Department of Spinal Surgery of Henan Provincial People’s Hospital between January 2021 and December 2024. Their bare-back images were used as the study subjects. The dataset was expanded using data augmentation techniques, and an efficient dilated residual network was proposed. Five classification models for scoliosis based on back images were constructed: VGG16, GoogLeNet, ResNet34, MobileNetV2, and the EDRNet proposed in this study. To evaluate the performance of the models, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score were used to analyze and compare the prediction results. Additionally, ROC curves and bubble charts were used to compare the classification ability of EDRNet with that of clinicians on bare-back images.ResultsWhen using both original and augmented data for predictive analysis, all five deep learning models demonstrated good predictive performance. Among them, the proposed EDRNet model exhibited the best predictive performance across all models. In comparing the classification ability on bare-back images, EDRNet (AUC = 0.91) was lower than that of the deputy chief physician (AUC = 0.93) but higher than that of the attending physician (AUC = 0.88) and the resident physician (AUC = 0.86), and EDRNet’s speed was superior to that of the three physicians.ConclusionsThe scoliosis classification model based on EDRNet shows good predictive accuracy and can achieve efficient scoliosis screening through the analysis of bare-back images. It has broad application prospects, especially in large-scale adolescent scoliosis screening.Keywords: Scoliosis, Deep learning, Screening

Background

Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis (AIS) is a three-dimensional abnormal curvature of the spine that occurs during adolescence (typically between 10 and 18 years of age), with an unknown etiology. It manifests as abnormal curvature of the spine in the sagittal, coronal, and rotational planes, usually occurring in patients without other systemic diseases, with a prevalence typically between 2 and 3% [1]. AIS not only affects the patient’s physical appearance, but as the condition progresses, the increased spinal curvature leads to changes in the thoracic cage morphology, which can further impact the function of the respiratory and circulatory systems [2–4]. Therefore, large-scale screening for AIS is crucial to curb the development of the disease at an early stage. Currently, the screening and diagnosis of AIS typically rely on clinical evaluation by specialized doctors and imaging tests such as X-rays [5, 6]. The spinal Cobb angle is an important quantitative indicator of spinal curvature, widely used in the clinical diagnosis and treatment decision-making for scoliosis [7, 8]. A Cobb angle of 10 degrees or greater is diagnostic for scoliosis, but the Cobb angle typically needs to be measured from spinal X-rays [9, 10]. Due to the potential health risks of radiation exposure to adolescents and the bulkiness of X-ray equipment, there are certain limitations in using them for large-scale AIS screening tasks. Currently, large-scale AIS screening requires a significant amount of medical personnel and specialized equipment, which is not efficient. Therefore, improving the convenience and accuracy of scoliosis screening while avoiding radiation exposure has become a research focus in recent years.

Against this backdrop, researchers at home and abroad are committed to developing radiation-free and convenient methods for screening scoliosis. For example, Yang J et al. [11] combined the Faster R-CNN algorithm with the ResNet101 algorithm to propose an image analysis-based method for screening scoliosis. This method locates the spinal region through object detection and performs classification using image classification algorithms, thereby achieving automated screening for scoliosis. Zheng Q et al. [12] explored the application of two-dimensional photogrammetry in patients with AIS. By measuring five postural indices, they found that it has good reliability and clinical value in screening and monitoring AIS patients, providing a new basis for radiation-free assessment methods. Given the limitations of existing methods in terms of equipment requirements, cost, and screening efficiency, this study aims to build a scoliosis screening model using deep learning technology. The EDRNet model is trained on scoliosis data from the past nearly four years, obtained from two hospitals: Ningxia Medical University General Hospital and Henan Provincial People’s Hospital. If applied in practice, this model could potentially allow for quick large-scale AIS screening using only bare-back photos of the subjects, without the need for many experts or large equipment. Moreover, this screening method is radiation-free and can provide a solution to the problem of low efficiency in large-scale AIS screening.

Materials and methods

Data collection and grouping

This study selected 300 adolescents with scoliosis (Cobb ≥ 10°) and 300 adolescents without scoliosis (10°>Cobb≥0°) from patients treated at the Orthopedics Department of Ningxia Medical University General Hospital and the Spine Surgery Department of Henan Provincial People’s Hospital between January 2021 and December 2024. The participants were aged 10–18 years, and photos of their bare-backs were taken during their visits. The screening process for subjects is detailed in Fig. 1. The study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University and the Ethics Review Committee of Henan Provincial People’s Hospital. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards established by the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, including parents or legal guardians of those under 16.

The imaging diagnostic process for patients followed a strict three-tier quality control system: First, two senior physicians with over 10 years of experience in spinal surgery independently reviewed the images, using standardized Cobb angle measurement methods to quantitatively evaluate full spine anteroposterior and lateral X-ray images to determine the degree of scoliosis. Second, when the measurement results or diagnostic opinions of the two physicians differed, an expert review mechanism was immediately initiated, inviting senior spinal surgery experts with over 25 years of AIS clinical diagnosis and treatment experience to discuss and ultimately reach a consensus diagnosis, ensuring the accuracy and reliability of the diagnosis. For model training and evaluation, bare back images of patients with a dual clinical and imaging diagnosis of AIS were used as positive samples, labeled with the value “1”; back images of healthy subjects confirmed to have normal spinal morphology through screening were used as negative control samples, labeled with the value “0”. This labeled dataset was strictly used for the subsequent training, validation, and performance evaluation of the deep learning algorithm.

The dataset was randomly divided into training, validation, and test sets in a 7:1:2 ratio to ensure the scientific validity and reliability of the model training, validation, and evaluation.

Data preprocessing

In deep learning, the number of data samples significantly influences the model’s predictive performance. Generally, a larger dataset results in more accurate predictions. Therefore, this study, based on 600 bare-back images, applied a series of data augmentation techniques to expand the training set and enhance the model’s predictive capabilities. Specifically, horizontal flipping was performed on the images to increase data diversity and reduce the risk of overfitting, thereby improving the model’s generalization ability. Additionally, random noise was added, and local warping or elastic deformation was applied to the images, introducing various transformations that enhance the model’s adaptability and robustness in real-world scenarios. Furthermore, contrast adjustments were made to the images to improve the model’s performance under different lighting and contrast conditions [13]. Model construction

To more comprehensively validate and evaluate the performance of deep learning models in predicting scoliosis, this study constructed five classification models: VGG16 [14], GoogLeNet [15, 16], ResNet34 [17, 18], MobileNetV2 [19, 20], and EDRNet. VGG16, GoogLeNet, ResNet34, and MobileNetV2 are currently mainstream image classification models, and their main structural details are shown in Fig. 3. These models represent different network architectures and design concepts, which can effectively demonstrate the performance differences of various methods on the same task.

Structural features of VGG16, GoogLeNet, ResNet34, and MobileNetV2 models

VGG16

The design philosophy of VGG16 is simple yet efficient, centering on the use of small 3 × 3 convolution kernels and the deep stacking of convolutional layers to enhance the network’s representational power. The architecture primarily comprises multiple convolutional and pooling layers, culminating in fully connected layers for classification, thereby exhibiting outstanding feature extraction capabilities and excellent transfer learning performance.

GoogLeNet

GoogLeNet’s primary contribution lies in the introduction of the Inception module and global average pooling technology [21, 22], which significantly reduce the parameter count while enhancing computational efficiency and accuracy. The network optimizes resource utilization through multi-path parallel computation and dynamic selection of convolution kernel sizes.

ResNet-34

ResNet-34 effectively addresses the degradation problem in deep neural network training by incorporating residual connections, facilitating more efficient learning and optimization. Comprising 34 layers, the network is built from multiple basic residual blocks, each featuring two 3 × 3 convolutional layers and shortcut connections that link input to output, promoting seamless information flow. This design ensures robust training performance even as network depth increases, markedly improving representational capacity and optimization efficiency.

MobileNetV2

MobileNetV2 introduces the inverted residual structure, distinguishing it from traditional residual networks. Unlike conventional residual networks, where inputs are directly summed with outputs via skip connections, the inverted residual structure processes inputs through an expansion convolution layer, followed by a depthwise separable convolution, and concludes with a linear bottleneck for compression. This approach ensures that information flows through a low-dimensional space, minimizing information loss.

EDRNet

The Efficient Dilated Residual Network (EDRNet), proposed in this study, integrates the ECA attention mechanism [23, 24], dilated convolutions [25], and a residual network architecture to enhance performance while reducing computational overhead. The model’s architecture is illustrated in Fig. 4. By augmenting channel feature representation, the network effectively emphasizes critical information and suppresses irrelevant data. The incorporation of dilated convolutions expands the receptive field without incurring additional parameter overhead, enabling the model to capture a broader range of features. Leveraging the ResNet framework, EDRNet enhances feature extraction capabilities, prevents overfitting, and excels in multi-scale feature extraction tasks.

In this study, we also compared the classification performance of EDRNet with that of clinicians. Given that clinicians only provided binary diagnostic decisions, the ROC curves in this study were constructed from single operating points, and the Bootstrap confidence intervals only reflect sampling error rather than performance variations across different diagnostic thresholds. This represents a fundamental difference from traditional ROC analysis based on probability outputs.

Parameter settings

In this study, the experiments were conducted on a server equipped with an NVIDIA GTX-4080 GPU and 16 GB of RAM. The model was developed using Python 3.7 and the PyTorch 1.10 deep learning framework. During the training process, the SGD optimizer [26] was employed, with a batch size set to 64 and a learning rate of 0.001. Each model underwent 300 training epochs.

Variable distribution analysis: training vs validation vs test sets

Through random sampling, the dataset was partitioned into three subsets: training set (n = 420), validation set (n = 60), and test set (n = 120). Statistical analysis revealed no significant differences among the groups (p > 0.05). The consistent variability across datasets (Cobb angle SD≈30°, age SD ≈ 2 years, BMI SD ≈ 4 kg/m2) validates the representativeness of the sampling. This homogeneous distribution ensures that the model performance metrics reflect true generalization capability rather than dataset bias, providing a robust framework for validating the clinical applicability of the model across the full spectrum of deformity severity.

Model training and validation

To fully utilize the advantages of the EDRNet model, we applied various data augmentation strategies to preprocess the bare back images. This approach enriched the training samples and effectively reduced the overfitting problem. The experimental results showed that as the amount of data increased, the EDRNet model exhibited a smoother loss curve with a faster descent rate during the training process, and the training loss was below 10%. Comparison between EDRNet and clinicians

To compare the performance of EDRNet with that of clinicians, this study invited three spine surgeons (in a single-blind experiment) with varying levels of clinical experience: a deputy chief physician (20 years in spinal surgery at a tertiary Grade A hospital), an attending physician (8 years in spinal surgery at a tertiary Grade A hospital), and a resident physician (2 years in spinal surgery at a tertiary Grade A hospital). Without interfering with each other, all three doctors completed the visual assessment of 120 bare-back images from the test set within 11 minutes (specifically, 489 seconds, 602 seconds, and 665 seconds, respectively). In contrast, EDRNet completed the task in just 36.41 seconds (using an NVIDIA GTX-4080 graphics card with 16 GB of memory). Based on the classification results, ROC curves and bubble charts (Fig. 9) were plotted to provide a more intuitive comparison of classification capabilities. As shown in the figure, although the AUC of EDRNet (0.91) is lower than that of the deputy chief physician (0.93), it is higher than that of the attending physician and the resident physician. For scoliosis screening, the technical proficiency of an attending physician is fully adequate, so based on classification performance, EDRNet is also competent, and its speed advantage is quite evident.

Discussion

This study selected 600 adolescents from patients who received treatment at the Orthopedics Department of Ningxia Medical University General Hospital and the Spinal Surgery Department of Henan Provincial People’s Hospital from January 2021 to December 2024, and used their bare back images as the study objects. It utilized deep learning technology to construct a scoliosis classification model based on back image features. The study employed VGG16, GoogLeNet, ResNet34, MobileNetV2, and the proposed EDRNet to establish screening models and systematically analyzed the experimental results. The dataset was divided into training, validation, and test sets in a 7:1:2 ratio. To enhance the model’s predictive accuracy, three data augmentation techniques were introduced to expand the training set: horizontal flipping, random noise addition, and contrast adjustment. The experimental results demonstrated that the proposed EDRNet model exhibited significant advantages on the original dataset, enabling more accurate identification of image features. Additionally, further experiments were conducted on the augmented dataset. The results indicated that after data augmentation, the performance of all models improved, validating the effectiveness of data augmentation techniques. Furthermore, by comparing the classification performance of EDRNet and clinicians using the test set data, it was shown that EDRNet’s screening capability is not inferior to that of attending physicians, with a notable speed advantage. Through the above validation analysis, the effectiveness of EDRNet in the task of scoliosis classification and prediction based on bare-back images was proven.

Compared to X-rays, this study employed back images combined with deep learning technology for scoliosis classification, demonstrating more significant practical value and academic importance. From the perspective of large-scale scoliosis screening, capturing bare-back images eliminated radiation exposure, which was particularly crucial for specific populations requiring frequent examinations, such as children and adolescents. Moreover, mobile X-ray machines were quite inconvenient for scoliosis screening. Furthermore, capturing bare-back images had a low technical barrier, making it easily promotable/scalable, and it eliminated the need for a large number of specialized doctors for face-to-face screening, thereby saving substantial human resource costs. The efficient feature extraction from back images by deep learning technology optimized the screening process. In large-scale screening, scoliosis classification methods based on deep learning could achieve automation and rapid processing, reducing manual intervention and diagnostic time. This not only enhanced overall screening efficiency and shortened the diagnostic cycle, but also contributed to improving the accessibility of screening in resource-constrained settings. Additionally, by leveraging efficient image classification technology, early-stage scoliosis patients could be quickly identified, reducing errors associated with manual diagnosis, and ensuring that more cases could be detected and treated promptly. Consequently, early detection and intervention became feasible, effectively improving the early treatment rate of scoliosis.

In recent years, the application of Artificial Intelligence in the medical field has continuously expanded, particularly in disease diagnosis and medical image analysis [27–29]. Prediction models based on machine learning and deep learning have demonstrated excellent performance in medical diagnosis [30, 31]. Previous studies have fully demonstrated the potential of deep learning in musculoskeletal imaging diagnosis. A study based on the Xception architecture constructed 2D and 3D CNN models for automated diagnosis of supraspinatus tears on rotator cuff MRI [32], with the 2D model demonstrating superior performance, achieving an AUC of 0.921 and a diagnosis time of only 0.17 seconds. Its accuracy, sensitivity, and F1-score all outperformed junior physicians and were comparable to senior experts. The model demonstrated stable performance on both 1.5T and 3.0T MRI data, exhibiting strong generalizability, effectively assisting primary care physicians in achieving rapid and accurate identification, thereby improving diagnostic efficiency and consistency. Other researchers constructed a large-scale annotated dataset using 23,663 musculoskeletal X-ray images [33], training two Xception models to automatically classify 45 types of projection angles and left/right laterality, respectively, with accuracies reaching 0.975 and 0.976. The processing time per image was only milliseconds, demonstrating efficient and robust clinical pre-sorting capability that optimized radiology workflow. In the field of spinal diseases, a study proposed a deep learning detection framework integrating 3D ResNet18 with Transformer cross-attention [34], capable of automatically identifying lumbar and cervical degenerative lesions simultaneously on both sagittal and axial T2-weighted MRI planes. The model employed detection heads to replace traditional segmentation to avoid boundary annotation difficulties, and was trained with a unified Dice+Focal loss, achieving an F1 score up to 0.971 in internal validation. Through transfer learning, it attained an F1 score of approximately 0.93 using only 200 cervical spine cases, demonstrating powerful cross-center generalization capability and providing a scalable AI solution for multi-disease, multi-center, high-precision spinal imaging screening. Similarly, leveraging deep learning, the results of this study indicate that classifying scoliosis using bare-back images is feasible, and deep learning techniques can improve classification accuracy.

Furthermore, while the back images used in this study primarily focused on spinal appearance features, screening accuracy may be influenced by various demographic and physiological factors. Age and gender, as critical variables in adolescent development, not only affect spinal growth patterns but also lead to significant differences in back soft tissue thickness, muscle tone, and body fat distribution. For instance, changes in spinal flexibility during the rapid growth period of puberty (ages 12–15) may increase diagnostic difficulty, while female patients typically have thicker subcutaneous fat layers, which may obscure mild signs of vertebral rotation. Height and BMI, as anthropometric indicators, also impact model performance: in taller individuals, the spinal region occupies a smaller proportion of the image at a fixed capture distance, potentially resulting in loss of detail; in patients with high BMI, thickened back soft tissue can reduce the visibility of bony landmarks, decreasing the model’s accuracy in recognizing spinal contours. Notably, deeper skin pigmentation may alter image contrast and interfere with the convolutional neural network’s ability to extract texture features. Although this study partially mitigated this issue through contrast enhancement preprocessing, the model’s robustness still requires validation across diverse skin tone populations. The potential impact of these factors suggests that future research should collect diverse cohort data with complete demographic information and explore multimodal learning frameworks that fuse clinical metadata with image features, thereby enhancing the model’s generalization capability and diagnostic consistency in real-world complex scenarios.

Limitations of study

This study had some limitations: (1) Due to the inherent privacy of bare-back image data, the dataset for this study was relatively limited. Although small sample studies can also draw reliable conclusions after data augmentation [35], to improve the model’s accuracy, future work needs to expand the dataset by increasing the sample size to further enhance the model’s robustness and precision; (2) The sample in this study was primarily concentrated in normal and severe scoliosis severity levels, while samples of mild and moderate scoliosis were relatively limited. Consequently, the current model’s performance in the four-class classification task (normal, mild, moderate, severe) has certain limitations, particularly for classifying mild and moderate scoliosis, where its performance is suboptimal. In future work, after supplementing samples across all severity categories, we will conduct further in-depth research to predict varying severity levels of scoliosis, which will better assist clinicians in assessing disease severity; (3) In clinical practice, scoliosis screening typically integrates multiple assessment modalities including visual inspection, palpation, forward bend test, among others. However, this study relied exclusively on a single static bare back image for diagnosis. Consequently, the performance comparison with physicians primarily reflects their interpretive capability for this specific image type and cannot be fully equated with the comprehensive screening level achieved in actual clinical settings; (4) This study lacks independent external validation data from other hospitals, primarily constrained by sample scarcity and the high sensitivity of medical data. As a rigorous alternative, we employed a 7:1:2 random split for training/validation/test sets (the test set remained unknown prior to evaluation and was used only once), and validated the model’s value through blinded comparison with clinicians. Future plans include accumulating data from external hospitals to establish an independent external validation set for further assessment of generalization capability; (5) Clinicians provided only binary diagnostic decisions without recording continuous confidence scores, resulting in ROC analysis constructed from a single operating point that cannot reflect performance variations across different diagnostic thresholds. Although this simplified approach authentically simulates the fixed decision-making pattern of clinical practice and AUC values effectively rank discriminative abilities (Deputy Chief Physician 0.93 > EDRNet 0.91 > Attending Physician 0.88 > Resident Physician 0.86), it fails to capture the continuous spectrum of diagnostic confidence, thereby limiting the generalizability of the results. Future research will require physicians to rate diagnostic confidence using a scale (0–100 points) to generate standard ROC curves and perform DeLong tests for comprehensive statistical evaluation of differences between the model and expert diagnoses.

Conclusion

In summary, this study utilized bare-back images and deep learning technology (EDRNet) to build an adolescent idiopathic scoliosis classification model, which could effectively and rapidly screen for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis. This technology is expected to play an important role in large-scale screening of adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, improving efficiency, and thus safeguarding the healthy development of adolescents.
