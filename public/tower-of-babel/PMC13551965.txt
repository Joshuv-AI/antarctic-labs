Association between oral food processing ability and nutritional status of older adults based on factor analysis
Authors: Jingru Gao, Xiaoqing Wu, Yong Chen, Xingxiang Yang, Qian Yu, Feng Zhong, Tianlin Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThe potential association between oral food processing ability and nutritional status in the older adults aged 75 years and above has been poorly investigated. Thus, this study is based on factor analysis to explore the relationship between the two aspects of older adults with different genders.MethodsThe nutritional status was assessed by the Short-form Mini-Nutritional Assessment and body composition measurement. Oral food processing ability was evaluated by subjective questionnaires (10-item Eating Assessment Tool and Chewing Function Questionnaire) and specific objective experiments for the detection of bite force, tongue pressure, lip sealing pressure, swallowing function, taste, and salivation. The dimensionality of the collected data was reduced through factor analysis, and we performed both descriptive and differential analyses of the resulting factors. Correlation and regression analysis were carried out to uncover the potential connections between oral food processing ability and nutritional status in older adults.ResultsFive factors extracted are salivary secretion, masticatory level, food guiding, taste function, and swallowing ability. The masticatory level and food guiding factor are significantly associated with malnutrition in an L-shaped dose-response relationship after adjustment for potential confounding factors. With the improvement of the masticatory level and food guiding, the risk of malnutrition decreases rapidly within the low-level range. Once the ability reaches a certain threshold, the risk of malnutrition tends to stabilize. Elevated taste thresholds and impaired swallowing function are positively correlated with the occurrence of malnutrition. Moreover, the masticatory level is the more important influencing factor in the elderly male group. In females, the impact of the other three factors (food guiding, taste function, and swallowing ability) on nutritional status is worth further exploring.ConclusionsThe main related factors for nutritional status among older adults include chewing, food guiding, taste, and swallowing function, but there are significant differences between males and females. Formulating oral health care measures based on different genders has important implications for reducing the risk of malnutrition.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s40795-026-01411-3.

Introduction

The aging process of the world is accelerating, and China is also rapidly transforming into an aging country. It is predicted that in 2050, there will be a large explosion in the elderly population, with up to 400 million aged 65 years old and above and 150 million aged 80 years old and above in China [1]. Population aging is expected to persist as an extended trend in China, and the Chinese government has implemented a range of policies, including the Healthy China Initiative (2019–2030), to proactively address the challenges posed by the aging population [2]. Healthy aging is a public health problem that is widely considered by society at present. Aging is associated with various chronic diseases, of which malnutrition is a highly prevalent condition. The overall nutritional risk of older adults is higher, and it is reported that the prevalence rate of malnutrition in the Chinese community elderly population is 41.2% [3]. Malnutrition in older adults is defined as a “defective or inadequate nutritional status” characterized by inadequate diet, poor appetite, loss of muscle strength, and weight loss [4]. Malnutrition among the elderly restricts the achievement of healthy aging and vastly increases the medical and social burden. In addition to disease, physical, psychological, and socio-economic determinants, studies have shown a close relationship between dietary intake and contents and nutritional status in the elderly [5, 6]. Therefore, it is of great significance to explore the factors affecting the dietary intake of the elderly. By identifying these highly correlated factors and using them as key intervention points, a structured framework can be established to implement evidence-based care [7], which can directly reduce malnutrition risk of the elderly.

Oral function is an important factor affecting dietary intake [8]. The processing of the food in the mouth, involving many changes in food properties and oral physiological reactions, is accompanied by a series of operations coordinated by the teeth, lips, tongue, salivary glands, and oral and facial muscles. This process is to convert non-swallowable food into swallowable pellets, fully tasting the texture and flavor of the food, and finally delivering them smoothly to the esophagus. However, the oral food processing ability of older adults is often less noticed by the public. Our previous study [9] demonstrated that poor oral health and functions are associated with malnutrition among older adults. Oral functions of older adults deteriorate with age and sickness [8], leading to degeneration in chewing, swallowing, salivation, and sensory perception. At the same time, the burden of oral diseases in older people is growing worldwide [10]. This severely damages the processing of the food in the mouth and increases the number of indigestible food particles, which will make older adults more vulnerable to dietary restrictions and result in malnutrition. Inadequate intake of nutrients and malnutrition may alter oral homeostasis and lead to an increased risk of oral health problems in turn [11, 12]. However, there is no relevant study classifying the oral food processing function among older adults systematically to explore the relationship between the two at present. Meanwhile, we find that the gender differences in the elderly are understudied, which may have certain significance for providing targeted nutrition.

According to the findings of our previous research, malnutrition may be related to the chewing, swallowing, and taste function of the elderly. This study intends to systematically classify oral food processing ability through factor analysis to better evaluate the relationship between oral food processing ability and nutritional status of older adults. Moreover, we will focus on gender differences in oral function among older adults (75 years old and above) to achieve gender-specific and age-specific personalized nutrition interventions. The results of the study will provide a novel idea for the oral function intervention of malnutrition in older adults and a reference for guiding the development of an elderly rational diet to optimize the nutritional intake of the elderly and improve their overall health level.

Methods

Study design and subjects

Our cross-sectional study adopted an exploratory research design and was conducted based on a population from a cohort living in Licha Town, Qingdao, China, which was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300078236) in 2023-12-01. Using the method of random cluster sampling, a total of 11 natural villages in town of Qingdao City were randomly selected. All 75-year-old and above elderly people in these villages were selected. Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 1,003 elderly people were included in the survey. The inclusion criteria are as follows: (1) age ≥ 75 years old; (2) have resided in the survey area for more than 6 months; (3) have clear awareness and the ability to provide reliable information about investigation; (4) self-care, able to walk to the venue independently; (5) volunteer to participate in the study and sign the informed consent. The exclusion criteria for the participants are as follows: (1) older patients with various advanced diseases; (2) suffering from cognitively related diseases such as dementia or Alzheimer’s disease; (3) sensory disability (deafness and blindness); (4) have no self-care ability; (5) patients or family members are unwilling to cooperate with the investigation or communication. Before the study, all researchers received unified training, and the instruments used were calibrated. Subjects completed a series of physical examinations and questionnaires to assess their nutritional status and oral parameters. This study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee Medical College of Qingdao University (QDU-HEC-2023250), and subjects voluntarily signed informed consent after full understanding.

Assessment of oral food processing capacity

Professional researchers determined the condition of their teeth, including the number of teeth and missing teeth, whether there are dentures, loose teeth, dental pain and caries, and the status of oral health, such as gingival bleeding, oral ulcerations, halitosis, and dry mouth. We selected the 10-item Eating Assessment Tool (EAT-10) and Chewing Function Questionnaire (CFQ) to subjectively evaluate the oral function of subjects. EAT-10 is a self-administered survey instrument for the subjective assessment of dysphagia [13], with 10 questions related to swallowing. A total score ≥ 3 is defined as a low swallowing function. CFQ is believed to be suitable for the assessment of a self-perceived chewing function [14], which is composed of 10 items related to the texture of different food types and the problems encountered while chewing, with higher scores indicating more impaired masticatory function. Additionally, we performed specific experiments by using precise instruments to evaluate oral food processing ability. The designed device (FlexiForce A201-100, Tekscan, South Boston, Massachusetts, USA) in the previous study [15] was used for bite force measurement. Participants were asked to bite the pressure sensor with their left and right first molars for a few seconds until the maximum bite force was recorded. Utilizing the Iowa Oral Performance Instrument (IOPI®, Medical LLC, Redmond, Washington, USA) [16], tongue and lip sealing pressure measurements were taken objectively. The bulb was located in the center of the oral cavity between the tongue and the hard palate when measuring tongue pressure, and while measuring lip sealing pressure, it was placed between the two lips. Older adults were asked to press with their maximum ability and then recorded the values. As for the Water Swallow Test (WST) to assess swallowing function, subjects were seated comfortably and asked to drink 30 mL water as much as possible in one gulp. Drinking time and signs of choking were recorded, with Grades I-II considered as suspicious dysphagia and III-V indicating dysphagia. The participants were also tested in the taste function by the tongue drop method. Salty, sweet, sour, and bitter start from the lowest concentration to taste until the subjects can identify what the taste is, and this concentration is recorded as the taste threshold. A lower threshold indicates a better taste function. We also investigated the oral saliva of subjects. The participants were instructed to spit all accumulating unstimulated and stimulated (in the case of chewing a square piece of Parafilm® (PM-996, Bemis Inc, Neenah, USA) [17]) saliva in a pre-weighed plastic cup for 5 min and we calculated the amount of saliva and their flow rates, respectively.

Assessment of malnutrition

The Short-form Mini-Nutritional Assessment (MNA-SF) tool, which has high accuracy and a valid time saving, was used to identify the nutritional status of older adults [18] in our study. Those who scored > 11 on the scale were considered to be in the normal nutritional status group, otherwise, subjects were identified as malnourished. Additionally, we used an InBody 270 multi-frequency body composition analyzer (Biospace, Seoul, Korea) to measure the body composition through a tetra-polar 8-point tactile electrode system [19] to confirm the validity of the previous grouping of nutritional status. It is reported that common indicators for assessing nutritional status among older adults include body mass index (BMI) and measurements of hip circumference, waist circumference, waist-hip ratio (WHR), and so on [20]. The purpose of measuring body composition is to objectively reflect nutritional status through the quantification of body water, muscle mass, body fat, lean mass, protein, and inorganic salt.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were imported into SPSS 26.0 for statistical analysis. The level of statistical significance is set at < 0.05 in our study. Descriptive data were summarized as mean with standard deviation if they were normally distributed continuous numerical variables or median with interquartile range for non-normally variables and frequency (percentages) for categorical variables. Due to a large number of indicators of oral processing ability measured and the presence of multicollinearity, we adopted factor analysis to interpret the experimental results in dimensionality reduction. Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) measurement of sample adequacy and Bartlett’s test of sphericity (BTS) were initially performed to verify the appropriateness of using factor analysis for this study. When the requirements were met with KMO > 0.600 or BTS < 0.05, the sample was considered adequate for factor analysis. Moderate KMO values are relatively common in exploratory factor analysis of elderly nutrition and oral health research, especially when analyzing multi-dimensional datasets. Previous studies in this field have reported KMO values as low as 0.51 [21]. The principal components method was used to extract the principal factors that can explain most of the original variables. Factors to be retained were based on a Scree plot (the eigenvalues of the factors retained should be more than 1). The factor loading matrix was derived from the orthogonal rotation using a varimax method to simplify the structure and render it easier to make a more practical interpretation. Factor loadings of more than 0.5 would be considered as significantly contributing to the factors in our study. Then, the names were given to reflect each extracted factor. Based on the distribution of the quantitative variables, we chose the T-test or rank sum test to compare the differences between the two groups. The Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used for categorical variables, and the rank sum test was used for ranked data. Binary logistic regression was conducted to obtain the adjusted odds ratios (OR) with the 95% confidence intervals (95% CI), ascertaining the association between relevant factors and nutritional status. We employed restricted cubic spline models with 3 knots to further investigate the dose-response relationship between different oral processing abilities and malnutrition after adjusting for potential confounding factors. Additionally, we looked at the link between body composition and oral parameters through Pearson’s correlation and Spearman’s rank correlation analysis.

Results

Factor analysis

In this study, the observed KMO is 0.586, and BTS is < 0.001, making the sample of this study adequate for factor analysis. The load factor of each factor is all higher than 0.5, and the most factors are greater than 0.7, indicating that the factor structure has a certain degree of internal consistency. Five major components are identified through factor analysis, based on a Scree Plot where the eigenvalue is more than 1 (Fig. 1). These five extracted components can explain 69.133% of total variables. The result of Table 1 describes the exact factor loadings for each variable. The first component extracted, which accounts for 18.856% of the total variance, is labeled as “Salivary secretion”. Unstimulated/stimulated salivary flow and flow rate belong to this component. We name the second component as “Masticatory level”, which contributes 17.146% of the total variance, including the number of teeth missing and remaining, CFQ score, and left/right molar bite force. Meanwhile, the third component accounts for 12.457% of the total variance, loaded by the tongue, left and right lip sealing pressure, and it is labeled as “Food guiding”. The fourth component is named “Taste function”, which accounts for 12.098% of the total variance. Salty, sweet, bitter, and sour tastes are observed in this component. Moreover, the last component explains 8.576% of the total variance. This component consists of WST and EAT-10 and is labeled as “Swallowing ability”.

Sociodemographic characteristics and factor scores of the populations according to nutritional status

Two hundred ten eligible participants (87 men and 123 women) were included in the study. The sociodemographic characteristics and factor scores of the subjects are displayed in Table 2. There are significant differences in age distribution between groups (p = 0.007). It reveals that the older the individual, the more susceptible they are to suffering from malnutrition. Malnutrition is more prevalent in men but not statistically significant (p = 0.09). We compared the differences of five factors between the two groups, finding that all variables except salivary secretion have significant differences (p < 0.05). The masticatory level (p = 0.037) and food guiding (p = 0.026) factor scores of the subjects in the malnourished group are significantly lower than those of the normal nutritional status group, indicating that their oral chewing ability and tongue and lip function are far less than the normal. Furthermore, factor scores, including taste function (p = 0.003) and swallowing ability (p = 0.027), are higher in the malnourished, reflecting a higher likelihood of taste thresholds and swallowing disorders.

Association of oral processing function with malnutrition among older adults

According to the results of the binary regression analysis, as described in Table 4, the factors associated with malnutrition are masticatory level (p = 0.026), food guiding (p = 0.015), and swallowing ability (p = 0.003). This indicates that taste function and swallowing ability factor have a positive correlation with malnutrition, whereas masticatory level and food guiding factor have a negative correlation. To further visualize the dose-response relationships, the results of the restricted cubic spline analyses are depicted in Fig. 2. The data reveal an L-shaped association of malnutrition with masticatory level and food guiding. The prevalence of malnutrition decreases with the value of two factors increasing, and it shows a linear dose-relationship in food guiding (pfornonlinearity=0.1119), yet masticatory level shows a non-linear dose-relationship (pfornonlinearity=0.0038).

Factors of oral processing ability in different groups by gender

Table 5 summarizes the differences in factors extracted among the subjects we investigated between different gender groups. From the results, the salivary function of women is significantly worse than men (p < 0.001), and the power of the tongue and lips to guide food is much lower than that of men (p = 0.018), while the taste threshold of men is significantly higher than that of women (p < 0.001). There are no significant differences between sexes in chewing and swallowing aspects.

Oral processing capacity of different nutritional status under different genders

We compared the oral processing ability of different nutritional status groups by genders, and the findings are shown in Table 6. In female groups, there are significant differences in food guiding (p = 0.011), taste function (p = 0.003), and swallowing ability (p = 0.007) between the malnourished and normal groups, suggesting that these three factors may play important roles in the occurrence of female malnutrition. As for males, however, statistically significant differences between the two groups are not discovered in our study (p > 0.05).

Discussion

The process of breakdown of the foods in the mouth always includes stages of first bite, mastication, mixing with saliva, bolus formation, and swallowing [22]. Chen et al. first proposed the concept of oral food processing in 2009, emphasizing the importance of oral function during eating [23], which is viewed as the first step in food digestion. These are less mentioned in other relevant reports, but in fact, these still have some implications for addressing malnutrition [9] in previous studies. Five factors extracted in our study are salivary secretion, masticatory level, food guiding, taste function, and swallowing ability.

The second factor that contains the status of tooth count, bite force, and the encountered problems while chewing reflects the masticatory ability of older adults. The higher the factor score, the better the chewing ability. A cross-sectional study by Samnieng et al. found that malnourished older adults had lower chewing ability than those with normal nutrition [24], which is similar to the results of our study. As far as we know, it is the teeth that play an important role in the overall masticatory process, and the number of teeth and chewing ability are related to each other [25]. It is reported that adults (≥ 75 years) lose a minimum of ten teeth per year [26], and aging is a risk factor for tooth loss [25], showing that the situation of tooth loss in older adults is currently relatively serious. Missing teeth can cause changes in food choices and dietary intake, resulting in nutritional imbalances. On the other hand, the absence of teeth will decrease posterior occlusal contact [27], consequently influencing the size of the bite force. The bite force also decreases with age regardless of gender [28], which means more difficulties in chewing and an inadequate breakdown of foods. This will greatly affect the digestion and absorption of food and may lead to malnutrition in the long term. In our study, we find that masticatory level is an important factor in the occurrence of malnutrition in men but not among women. The possible explanation is that women might have a more remarkable ability to accommodate the effects of tooth loss than men [29]. Additionally, the total occlusal force of men is higher than that of women [30], which means that the reduction of occlusal force among men may be greater. Thus, the change of masticatory ability has a greater impact on men, affecting nutritional status. We also observe a positive correlation between masticatory level and body composition. The decline in body composition, such as muscle and musculoskeletal mass, has serious consequences for the oral component [31], thus reducing the strength of the muscles involved in chewing and swallowing. In turn, the decreased masticatory ability will affect nutritional intake, and it will cause sarcopenia and nutritional deterioration in severe cases. Therefore, it is recommended that the elderly, especially men, pay attention to their oral hygiene with the purpose of avoiding non-physiological tooth loss. If tooth loss has occurred, they should install dentures as early as possible and exercise chewing function to meet the needs of nutritional intake.

The third factor is characterized by tongue and lip pressure, mainly embodying the ability to guide food through the mouth and smoothly into the esophagus. During the food processing in the mouth, the tongue plays an important role in selecting and transferring the food bolus particles and finally initiating the swallowing process [32]. The tongue mixes the food with the saliva through its constant stirring to form the swallowable food bolus, which is finally propelled into the oropharynx by the tongue’s squeeze-back motion [33]. That is, the movement of the food in the mouth depends on the tongue’s movement and coordination to a great extent. A study has mentioned that a decline in tongue pressure would significantly hinder food bolus formation and propagation, thus leading to malnourishment following decreased oral intake [34]. As for the role of the lips, they are constructed to prevent the escape of solids or liquids from the oral cavity while eating and assist the tongue and teeth in food processing. The low level of lip strength is thought to be related to difficulty eating and drinking [35], interfering with food selection and nutrition intake. In conclusion, the lower the tongue and lip sealing pressure (the score of food guiding factor), the more likely they are to suffer from malnutrition in older adults. It is worth noting that a sex difference was noted in the tongue and lip-seal strength [36, 37] in the present studies. Specifically, it appears that women have reduced tongue and lip strength compared to men [38], which follows the same gender trend seen in our study. The guiding role of the tongue and lips in oral food processing is negatively correlated with the occurrence of malnutrition for women, but no significant association is observed in men. This may signify that women rely on their tongues and lips more than men during food processing, suggesting that we should pay more attention to the weakening of the strength of the female tongue and lip muscles. Future studies to obtain additional data, such as muscle quality and hormone levels, are necessary to investigate the sex differences. Similarly, the lips and tongue also belong to facial muscles, so the food guiding is positively correlated with body composition. Thus, preserving tongue and lip strength among older adults, especially in women, may contribute to promoting a healthy diet and preventing nutritional deficiency.

Taste is essential to eating, and its primary function is to facilitate the intake of essential nutrients and the rejection of harmful substances [39]. Aging is associated with a decrease in the perception of all tastes [40]. Bitter, salty, and sour are thought to serve as defenses and protective mechanisms to prevent ingestion of potentially toxic foods, and the increasing sweetness can alter the oral microbiome, destroying the dental structure [41, 42]. The increased taste threshold means reduced taste sensitivity, so older adults need to ingest more flavored substances, which greatly adds to the risk of ingesting harmful foods and damaging the oral structure. At the same time, taste controls appetite, and the decline in taste will lead to poor appetite, unfitted food choices, inadequate nutrient intake, and malnutrition. Moreover, the study of Alia et al. has shown gender differences in the ability to recognize different tastes [43], and on average, females perform better in correctly identifying tastes, which is consistent with our findings. Of all the factors, taste has the greatest influence on the occurrence of malnutrition in women, probably because they are very sensitive to taste, so taste changes have a very large impact on the ingestion, absorption, and digestion of the food. There’s one possible explanation that feminine sensitivity to taste may be due to the difference in hormones, which can regulate the responses to the taste stimuli [44]. Furthermore, we also find that body compositions are negatively related to taste function in our study. The main reason might be the body’s compensatory mechanisms and the adaptive behaviors of the elderly [45]. As aging, taste generally declines [46]. When taste becomes less sensitive, the brain and the body rely more on other senses to obtain the pleasure and satisfaction from eating. They may choose foods with rich flavors, textures, and colors [47], which are often of higher nutritional density. This inadvertently improves nutrient intake. Additionally, elderly with the lower taste thresholds may be overly sensitive to the bitter, sour in food. This may cause them to reject many healthy foods [48], narrowing their dietary choices and thus affecting nutritional diversity. Many elderly with high taste thresholds have found a suitable diet pattern through long-term adjustments. Conversely, some elderly with sensitive tastes may have a significantly increased nutritional risk if they have long-term oral problems [49]. But the specific mechanism is unclear and is awaiting further clarification. Therefore, addressing the decline in taste perception among older adults (especially in women) and adopting sensory interventions such as flavor enhancement have been recommended to promote healthy habits and prevent nutritional deficiency.

It is acknowledged that swallowing is the termination of food processing by the mouth, mainly relying on the push of the tongue. When the pressure between the tongue, the roof of the mouth and the foods decreases, it will retard the process of swallowing and lead to dysphagia. Dysphagia, a prevalent geriatric syndrome, results from structural or functional damage to the mouth, pharynx, larynx, and esophagus that hinders the safe delivery of food or fluid to the stomach [50]. A systematic review based on longitudinal data suggested that dysphagia is an important risk factor for malnutrition [51]. The elderly tend to be at risk of dysphagia, and there is no doubt that they are at increased risk of malnutrition. The loss of muscle associated with swallowing due to aging will have a negative influence on the efficient flow of swallowed substances, thereby generating long mealtimes and insufficient food intake. Namasivayam-MacDonald et al. found that the elderly with dysphagia symptoms were nearly twice as likely to suffer from malnutrition and had longer mealtimes [52], which aligns with our views. Another survey demonstrated that the average daily energy intake of older adults with dysphagia was below the recommended appropriate intake (AI) for energy [53], indicating that swallowing problems affect the quantity and quality of nutrient intake and cause malnutrition in the long term. Besides, the research showed that women needed more times, performed more swallows, and had more masticatory cycles in comparison with men [54] when feeding. Perhaps this is why the ability to swallow is a crucial factor in the nutritional status of women but not among men. To sum up, it is vital to evaluate the swallowing ability among older adults in time, particularly for women. Methods such as adjusting the diet texture and training food intake gradually can be adopted to improve the nutritional status more safely and effectively.

Another crucial factor in oral food processing is saliva, which has many essential functions. First of all, saliva has the functions of fusion and lubrication, and it is imperative for mastication and swallowing [55]. Saliva tightly binds to food particles to form an alimentary bolus during the process of chewing and makes a bolus smoothly transported in the digestive system. From a biological perspective, saliva secretes various proteins [56], which directly affect nutrient absorption by breaking down starch and lubricating food. Secondly, saliva is the medium that bathes the taste receptors and affects taste sensitivity [57, 58]. Clinically, a more important role is to maintain oral health, protecting the teeth and oropharyngeal mucosa from infections [55]. Changes in the composition or amount of saliva produced can generate serious consequences. From the perspective of functional approaches, when salivary secretion is reduced in volume, its ability to lubricate and sense judgment is decreased, further affecting the process of chewing and swallowing, thus increasing the risk of malnutrition among older adults. In terms of behavioral pathways, the reduction in saliva secretion could theoretically affect the eating experience of the elderly [59], leading to a decrease in food intake and thereby impacting nutrient intake. However, our finding is that the association between salivary secretion and the risk of malnutrition is not statistically significant. The results don’t support the above interpretation, and we further elaborate on the potential physiological, methodological, and contextual factors that may underlie this discrepancy. Physiologically, the secretion volume of saliva needs to reach a certain threshold to have a substantial impact on actual swallowing functions, taste perception, or oral hygiene. Changes in secretion volume below this threshold may not be sufficient to directly translate into measurable functional alterations. Methodologically, the collection method we used may not have fully reflected the clinical relevance of saliva functions. Flow rate is merely one aspect of saliva function. We did not measure the composition of saliva, and changes in it may also affect oral comfort, taste perception, and swallowing efficiency. And it may have failed to capture the context-specific functions that are more relevant to actual dietary intake. Contextually, other related topics [60] have been examined most often among hospitalized elderly people, while our subjects are healthy and live in the Chinese countryside. These uncertainties should be taken into account when interpreting the results, and further investigation is needed. In addition, our study suggests that there is a positive correlation between salivation and body composition, and saliva secretion is significantly lower in women than in men–consistent with the gendered aging framework, as in the previous study [61]. The decline in hormone levels after menopause in women has been shown to alter the function of salivary glands [62], resulting in a reduction in saliva production compared to men of the same age, which may be the underlying cause of the gender differences observed in our study.

Compared with early studies, our study explores the oral food processing ability from the viewpoints of salivary secretion, masticatory level, food guiding, taste ability, and swallowing function through factor analysis, which is different from other papers focusing on the impact of various nutrients on the nutritional status of older adults. Moreover, we adopt the specific instrument measurement, not just oral questionnaires, to assess oral parameters, which is rarely seen in other researches. Another significant feature is that the research focuses on older adults aged 75 and above and pays attention to gender differences, which will provide more comprehensive solutions to severe malnutrition problems to realize the healthy aging proposed by the Chinese government.

At the same time, some restrictions on our research must also be taken into consideration. Firstly, it should be emphasized that as a cross-sectional study, it limits causal judgment between oral food processing ability and nutritional status. Hence, further prospective cohort investigations in the future are required to clarify the relationships. Secondly, there is a limitation in sample size, which may not accurately reflect the overall situation. Especially after gender stratification, the sample size within each layer is smaller, which may reduce the testing efficiency and lead to a significant reduction in the statistical power of the gender-specific analysis. Therefore, future studies should consider increasing the sample size or adopting sensitivity analyses, such as gender interaction terms, to balance stratified exploration with statistical reliability. Besides, due to the presence of some self-report questionnaires, it may introduce recall bias and subjectivity, inflating or underestimating the real results. The scope of application of the results is limited to community-dwelling elderly individuals who are not hospitalized, have good physical health (without severe comorbidities), and have diverse socioeconomic backgrounds. For the other different populations the results cannot be generalized because of selection biases. And due to the KMO value is not high based on the factor analysis, which may have the potential risk of information loss and affect the generalization of the conclusions. In subsequent studies, it may be advisable to adjust the factor extraction method and rotation method to verify the reliability of the factor analysis results. This study employs exploratory factor analysis with the aim of initially exploring the potential structure among the variables, rather than verifying the pre-defined factor model. Therefore, the extracted factor structure represents an exploratory solution, and its stability may be altered as the sample size increases or homogeneity improves. Subsequent studies can further verify the model fit through confirmatory factor analysis.

Drawing an association between oral food processing ability and nutritional status among older adults would be of great value. In light of this, the related department should implement targeted oral health education and popular science to raise awareness of oral health care. Meanwhile, the medical staff conducts the early assessment and screening of oral functions regularly for older adults as a way to detect malnutrition as early as possible. Based on the findings, experts are supposed to make great efforts to design foods suitable for the oral characteristics and functions of older adults and offer guidance on healthy diets and habits to boost nutritional status and overall well-being.

Conclusions

In summary, there is a very close link between oral food processing capacity and nutritional status among older adults. The main related factors for nutritional status include chewing, food guiding, taste, and swallowing function. Maintaining oral function has important implications for reducing the risk of malnutrition according to our study. We draw attention to the fact that sex-specific oral health education should be carried out, and we should develop specialized foods suitable for the oral feeding needs of older adults, which may be a more feasible option to ensure dietary intake and achieve better nutritional intervention outcomes for the elderly, solving the problem of malnutrition.
