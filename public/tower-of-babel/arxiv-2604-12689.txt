Homogenization in one-dimensional higher-order non-local models of phase transitions
Fabrizio Caragiulo, Sergio Scalabrino, Edoardo Voglino

Abstract
We study the limit behavior of Cahn–Hilliard-type functionals in which the derivative is replaced by higher-order fractional derivatives and modulated by an oscillating factor. Depending on the ratio between the oscillation scale and the interface length, we identify three different regimes and prove Γ \Gamma -convergence in each regime to a suitable sharp-interface limit functional. In the extreme regimes, we prove a separation-of-scales effect that enables us to highlight the difference relative to the local models.

Results

We introduce precise notions of double-well potential and homogenization kernel, recall the definition of fractional Sobolev spaces, and then state our main results.
We say that a real continuous function
W
is a regular
double-well potential
, if

1.

there exist
\alpha_{W},\beta_{W}>0
such that

\alpha_{W}(1-\left|z\right|)^{2}\leq W(z)\leq\beta_{W}(1-\left|z\right|)^{2},

for
\left|z\right|\leq 2
;

2.

\inf_{\{\left|z\right|\geq 2\}}W(z)>0
.

We call a measurable function
a\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
a
homogenization kernel
if:

1.

a
is 1-periodic in both variables,

2.

\alpha_{a}=\operatorname{ess}\inf a>0
,
\beta_{a}=\operatorname{ess}\sup a<+\infty
.

A
uniform family of homogenization kernels
is a family
(a_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
of homogenization kernels such that all the kernels of the family can be bounded by two constants
\alpha_{a}\equiv\alpha_{a_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}>0
,
\beta_{a}\equiv\beta_{a_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}<+\infty
.

Let

from which it easily follows that

is continuous with respect to the parameter

.

Remark 2.7
.

Let

be any homogenization kernel and

be any double-well potential. If either

1.

is even, or

2.

is even in both variables,

As noted in the introduction, we can relate

Remark 3.8

(On values far from minima)
.

We observe that the properties of the double-well potential imply that for any

.

be a sequence of functions each in

(18)

and, again, only a finite number of disjoint intervals, say

and

4
Modification lemmas

In this section, we will first prove two useful approximation results and an immediate corollary. Indeed, to prove the liminf inequalities in the
\Gamma
-limit in section
7
, it will be useful to modify a minimizing sequence to obtain better properties while increasing the energy only by an arbitrarily small amount.
This can be challenging for non-local functionals, as any modification, no matter how localized, affects the functional over the entire domain.

In the
Flattening lemma

4.1
, we modify a function
u
when its values are close to a minimum of the double-well potential, substituting the value with the actual minima, and estimate the energy cost of the procedure.
One of the advantages of this flattening process is that, once a function has been made constant near the edge of its domain, we can extend it to infinity without increasing the norms of its derivatives, nor the energy of the double-well potential.

In Lemma
4.3
, we prove that, indeed, for any function
v
flattened and extended as above, the difference
\Phi^{a}(v,I)-\Phi^{a}(v)
can be controlled up to an error that can be made arbitrarily small.

Lemma 4.1

\begin{split}[u-1]^{2}_{H^{\ell+s}((t_{j-1},c))}&\leq C_{N}\Big((c^{\prime}-t_{j-1})^{-2s}\|(u-1)^{(\ell)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((t_{j-1},c))}\\
&\qquad+(c^{\prime}-t_{j-1})^{2(1-s)}\|(u-1)^{(\ell+1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((t_{j-1},c))}\Big)\end{split}

(25)

which in turn can be estimated using (

) and (

In conclusion, from (

\begin{split}\Phi^{a}\big(v,(b,c)\big)&\leq\bigg(1+\frac{1}{N}+\frac{C_{N}}{(c^{\prime}-c^{\prime\prime})^{2s}}\bigg)\Phi^{a}\big(u,(b,c)\big)\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{N}\frac{\beta_{W}}{\alpha_{W}}\int_{c^{\prime\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}W\big(u(x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\\

Remark 4.2

(Multiple flattenings)
.

The result of Lemma
4.1
also holds, with suitable modifications, for functions close to the double-well minima on multiple intervals. We spell out the details in the following two cases, for later reference. Let

b<b^{\prime}<b^{\prime\prime}<c^{\prime\prime}<c^{\prime}<c,\quad\min\{c^{\prime}-c^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime}-b^{\prime}\}\geq 1,

and let
u
such that either

\begin{split}&\text{(1)}\quad\big||u(x)|-1\big|\leq\eta,\quad\text{for }x\in(b,b^{\prime\prime})\cup(c^{\prime\prime},c),\\
&\text{(2)}\quad\big||u(x)|-1\big|\leq\eta,\quad\text{for }x\in(b^{\prime},c^{\prime}),\end{split}

namely, in case (1),
u
is close to some minimum on the outer subintervals, in case (2) is close to some minimum in the middle subinterval.
Then there exists
v\in H^{k+s}(b,c)
such that, respectively,

\begin{split}&\text{(1)}\quad\begin{cases}v\equiv u,&\text{on }(b^{\prime\prime},c^{\prime\prime}),\\
v\equiv\pm 1,&\text{on }(b,b^{\prime})\cup(c^{\prime},c);\end{cases}\\
&\text{(2)}\quad\begin{cases}v\equiv u&\text{on }(b,b^{\prime})\cup(c^{\prime},c),\\
v\equiv\pm 1&\text{on }(b^{\prime\prime},c^{\prime\prime}).\end{cases}\end{split}

In either case, the following inequality holds

\begin{split}\Phi^{a}\big(v,(b,c)\big)&\leq\bigg(1+\frac{C}{N}+\frac{C_{N}}{(\min\{c^{\prime}-c^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime}-b^{\prime}\})^{2s}}\bigg)\Phi^{a}\big(u,(b,c)\big).\end{split}

This result follows with analogous computations after the proper choice of
\varphi_{j}
, which this time will satisfy

\|\varphi_{j}^{(\ell)}\|_{\infty}\leq C\Big(\frac{N}{\min\{c^{\prime}-c^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime}-b^{\prime}\}}\,\Big)^{\ell}.

Lemma 4.3

(Vanishing tails)
.

Let
a
be any homogenization kernel,
\eta
as in Lemma
3.5
, and let
v\in H^{k+s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
be such that

\begin{cases}v(x)=\pm\operatorname{sgn}(x),&\text{ for }|x|\geq c^{\prime},\\
\big||v(x)|-1\big|\leq\eta,&\text{ for }|x|\geq c^{\prime\prime},\end{cases}

for some
c^{\prime}>c^{\prime\prime}>1
. Then,
\Phi^{a}(v)<+\infty
, and, if

c^{\prime\prime}\geq\rho\Phi^{a}(v),

where
\rho
is constant appearing in Lemma
3.5
, then, for every
T>\max\{c^{\prime},3c^{\prime\prime}\}
, for
k\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\Phi^{a}(v)

\displaystyle-\Phi^{a}_{T}(v)\leq C\Big(\frac{c^{\prime\prime}}{T-c^{\prime}}\Big)^{2s}.

While, for
k=0
and
s>\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle\Phi^{a}(v)

\displaystyle-\Phi^{a}_{T}(v)\leq C\Big(\frac{c^{\prime}}{T-c^{\prime}}\Big)^{2s}+\frac{C}{T^{2s-1}}.

Proof.

Since
v(x)=\pm 1
for
|x|\geq c^{\prime}
, we have

\int_{-T}^{T}W(v)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{-c^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}W(v)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(v)\,\mathrm{d}x.

This implies that
\Phi^{a}(v)-\Phi^{a}_{T}(v)
only depends on the non-local term of
\Phi^{a}
.

Case
k\geq 1
.

Since
v^{(k)}\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus(-c^{\prime},c^{\prime})
, we have

\begin{split}\Phi^{a}(v)-\Phi^{a}_{T}(v)&\leq 2\beta_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-T,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|v^{(k)}(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=2\beta_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-T,T)}\int_{-c^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}\frac{|v^{(k)}(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\frac{\beta_{a}}{s}\int_{-c^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}\frac{|v^{(k)}(x)|^{2}}{|x-T|^{2s}}+\frac{|v^{(k)}(x)|^{2}}{|x+T|^{2s}}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\frac{2\beta_{a}}{s|c^{\prime}-T|^{2s}}\int_{-c^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}|v^{(k)}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{2\beta_{a}}{s(T-c^{\prime})^{2s}}\|v^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((-T,T))}.\end{split}

(26)

In particular,
\Phi^{a}(v)<\infty
.
We have now to investigate the dependence of
\|v^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((-T,T))}
on
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime}
.
Set
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2T}
, for
k\geq 2
, we apply Lemma
3.5
to the functions

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=v\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(x-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\Big)\quad\text{for }x\in(0,1),

and the intervals
J_{\pm}\coloneqq\varepsilon I_{\pm}+1/2
, where

I_{+}=\big(c^{\prime\prime},2c^{\prime\prime}\big),\qquad I_{-}=-I_{+}.

Indeed,
||v|-1|\leq\eta
on
I_{\pm}
and so
u_{\varepsilon}
on
J_{\pm}
; while

|J_{\pm}|=\varepsilon|I_{\pm}|\geq\varepsilon\rho\Phi^{a}(v)\geq\varepsilon\rho F_{\varepsilon}^{\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon}),

for
\rho
the same constant as in Lemma
3.5
and
\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\right)
.

Then, as a consequence of Lemma
3.5
, there exist
x_{+}\in I_{+}
and
x_{-}\in I_{-}
such that

|v^{(k-1)}(x_{\pm})|\leq 1.

We note that for
k=1
,
|v(x_{\pm})|\leq 1+\eta
already for any
x_{\pm}\in I_{\pm}
, and there is no need to use the Lemma.

In any case, since
\left|x_{+}-x_{-}\right|>2c^{\prime\prime}
, we can now apply the Proposition
3.3
on the intervals

(-2c^{\prime\prime},2c^{\prime\prime})\supset(x_{-},x_{+}),

obtaining:

\begin{split}\|v^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((-2c^{\prime},2c^{\prime}))}&\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{c^{\prime\prime}}\big|v^{(k-1)}(x_{+})-v^{(k-1)}(x_{-})\big|^{2}+(c^{\prime\prime})^{2s}\big[v^{(k)}\big]^{2}_{H^{s}((-2c^{\prime},2c^{\prime}))}\Big)\\
&\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{c^{\prime\prime}}+(c^{\prime\prime})^{2s}\Phi^{a}_{2c^{\prime}}(v)\Big)\\
&\leq C\big(1+(c^{\prime\prime})^{2s}\Phi^{a}_{T}(v)\big).\end{split}

(27)

On the interval
(2c^{\prime\prime},T)
we apply instead Proposition
3.2
, and the
Young’s inequality
:

\begin{split}\|v^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((2c^{\prime\prime},T))}&\leq C\Big(\Big(1+\frac{1}{(T-2c^{\prime\prime})^{2k}}\Big)\|v\pm 1\|_{L^{2}((2c^{\prime\prime},T))}^{2}+[v]_{H^{k+s}((2c^{\prime\prime},T))}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq C(\|v\pm 1\|_{L^{2}((2c^{\prime\prime},T))}^{2}+[v]_{H^{k+s}((2c^{\prime\prime},T))}^{2})\\
&\leq C\Phi^{a}_{T}(v).\end{split}

(28)

Together, (
27
), (
28
) and an analogous inequality for
(-T,-2c^{\prime\prime})
imply

\begin{split}\|v^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((-T,T))}\leq C\big(1+(c^{\prime\prime})^{2s}\Phi^{a}_{T}(v)\big),\end{split}

which, combined with (
26
) and
\Phi_{T}^{a}(v)\leq\Phi^{a}(a)
, grants the claim.

Case
k=0
.

Without loss of generality, we consider
v(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)
for
|x|\geq c^{\prime}
. Then,

\Phi^{a}(v)-\Phi^{a}_{T}(v)\leq 2\beta_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-T,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|v(x)-\operatorname{sgn}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

(29)

We will estimate the contribution relative to positive
y\in(T,+\infty)
; the computations for negative
y
are analogous.

\begin{split}\int_{T}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|v(x)-1|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=&\int_{T}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{-c^{\prime}}\frac{4}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\int_{T}^{+\infty}\int_{-c^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}\frac{|v(x)-1|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

(30)

By a direct computation (recall that

),

\int_{T}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{-c^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{2s(2s-1)}\frac{1}{(T+c^{\prime})^{2s-1}}\leq\frac{C}{T^{2s-1}}.

(31)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\int_{T}^{+\infty}\int_{-c^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}\frac{|v(x)-1|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y&=\frac{1}{2s}\int_{-c^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}\frac{|v(x)-1|^{2}}{|T-x|^{2s}}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2s|T-c^{\prime}|^{2s}}\int_{-c^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}|v(x)-1|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

However, as

by Morrey’s embedding on the interval

, we have

\displaystyle|v(x)-1|

\displaystyle=|v(x)-v(c^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq C[v]_{H^{s}((-T,T))}|x-c^{\prime}|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\Phi^{a}(v))^{\frac{1}{2}}|x-c^{\prime}|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C|x-c^{\prime}|^{s-\frac{1}{2}},

from which it follows that

\int_{T}^{+\infty}\int_{-c^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}}\frac{|v(x)-1|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C\frac{(c^{\prime})^{2s}}{|T-c^{\prime}|^{2s}}.

(32)

Then we conclude by combining (
29
), (
30
), (
31
), and (
32
).

∎

.

We adapt the reasoning of Proposition 14 in

. The inequality

We fix a

such that
\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}u(x)=\pm\omega

(33)

By the asymptotic behavior of

We use the flattening Lemma

, for some

\begin{split}\Phi^{a}_{T}(v)&\leq\left(1+\frac{C}{N}+\frac{C_{N}}{(c^{\prime\prime})^{2s}}\right)\Phi^{a}_{T}(u)\\

5
Coercivity of the rescaled energy

In this section, we show that the rescaled energies
\Phi^{a}
enjoy a coercivity property stronger than that of Proposition
2.2
: sequences that are bounded in energy have
k
-th derivative bounded in
L^{2}
, and so in
H^{s}
.

A first consequence will be Proposition
5.5
: the transition energies
m^{\omega}(a)
(recall Definition
2.5
) are strictly positive. Hence, the limit energies of Theorem
2.3
are not trivially zero.
Furthermore, this improved coercivity allows the extraction of subsequences strongly converging in
H^{k}
, which will be critical to prove the lower bound in the case
\delta\ll\varepsilon
(see section
7
).

To deal with the case of
k=0
, for a given a sequence
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
bounded in energy we will need a suitable sequence
(\sigma_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
of smooth functions, each regularizing
\operatorname{sgn}(v_{\varepsilon})
. This function
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
deals with the issue that the sequence
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is not bounded in
L^{2}
, letting us recover that
v_{\varepsilon}-\sigma_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
H^{s}
.

Throughout the rest of this section
(a_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is a uniform family of homogenization kernels and
A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}
is defined as in Remark
3.8
for some fixed
\eta\in(0,1)
as in Lemma
3.5
.

Lemma 5.1

(Regularization of sign functions)
.

Let
\tau\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and let

(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence in
H^{s}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, such that

\sup_{\varepsilon}\Phi^{a_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}_{\tau/\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})<\infty.

Then, there exists a sequence
(\sigma_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
in
C^{\infty}((-\tau/\varepsilon,\,\tau/\varepsilon))
, depending on the fixed
\eta\in(0,1)
and
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
, such that

(i)

\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)=\operatorname{sgn}\big(v_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)
for
x\notin A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}
.

(ii)

|\sigma_{\varepsilon}|\leq 1
everywhere.

(iii)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon}||(\sigma_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}||_{L^{\infty}\left(\left(-\tau/\varepsilon,\,\tau/\varepsilon\right)\right)}<+\infty
.

Proof.

We just have to prove that it is possible to define
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
in
A^{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
such that
(ii)
and
(iii)
hold. We recall that the sets
A^{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
contain all transition intervals, are bounded in measure uniformly in
\varepsilon
, and they are the union of a finite number (uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon
) of intervals. Moreover,
v_{\varepsilon}
is
\eta
-close to a minimum of the double-well (i.e.
\left||v_{\varepsilon}|-1\right|<\eta
) on the complement of
A^{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
.

Let
I
be any connected component of
A^{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
. If
\operatorname{sgn}(v_{\varepsilon})
takes the same value at both edges of the interval, then we simply define
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
to be constant in
I
. Otherwise, we first note that

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{\varepsilon}\,[v_{\varepsilon}]^{2}_{H^{s}((-\tau/\varepsilon,\,\tau/\varepsilon))}\leq(\alpha_{a})^{-1}\Phi^{a_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}_{\tau/\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})<+\infty.

Since it is always possible to define
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
such that
|\sigma_{\varepsilon}|\leq 1
and
|(\sigma_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}|\leq\frac{4}{|I|}
on
I
, we only have to show that the length of
I
is bounded from below independently of
\varepsilon
.
Indeed, if
I=(t,t^{\prime})
,

|v_{\varepsilon}(t)-v_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})|\geq 2(1-\eta),

so that, by the Hölder regularity of
v_{\varepsilon}
(see Theorem
3.4
):

2(1-\eta)\leq CS|I|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}.

In the end,

|(\sigma_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}|\leq\frac{4}{|I|}\leq C\left(\frac{S}{2(1-\eta)}\right)^{\frac{2}{2s-1}}\quad\text{on }I.

We conclude noting that this bound can be extended to the whole
A^{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
, as it does not depend on the particular
I
chosen.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Given a sequence
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
as in the previous lemma, it is clear that, for
\varepsilon
small enough,
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
can be extended (as
\pm 1
) on the whole real line while still satisfying the three properties
(i), (ii), (iii)
. With a small abuse of notation, we will still denote by
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
the extensions.

Observe, though, that the sequence of the norms
\|\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((-\tau/\varepsilon,\,\tau/\varepsilon))}
is not bounded in
\varepsilon
, as the measure of the complement of
A^{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
is diverging.
Therefore, neither the sequence of norms

||\sigma_{\varepsilon}||_{H^{s}((-\tau/\varepsilon,\,\tau/\varepsilon))}

is bounded in general. Still, as the next result will show, the
H^{s}
seminorms are uniformly bounded.

Lemma 5.3
.

Under the same hypotheses of Lemma
5.1
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}

\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\left|\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x+\xi)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\min\{|\xi|,\xi^{2}\}.

(35)

Moreover, the seminorms
[\sigma_{\varepsilon}]_{H^{s}((-\tau/\varepsilon,\,\tau/\varepsilon))}
are uniformly bounded.

We notice that the constant
C
depends only on
\eta
and on
\sup_{\varepsilon}\left\lVert(\sigma_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}
.

Proof.

We observe at first that the derivatives
(\sigma_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}
are equibounded and they different from
0
only on
A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}
(defined between (
18
) and (
19
) in Remark
3.8
). Therefore, for
\xi>0
,

\left|\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x+\xi)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|\leq\int_{[x,{x+\xi}]\cap A^{\eta}_{\varepsilon}}\left|\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\big|[x,x+\xi]\cap A^{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\big|.

Then, recalling that
A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{M}I_{n}
, we can compute the integral for
|\xi|\geq|A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}|
:

\begin{split}\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\left|\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x+\xi)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x&\leq C\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\big|[x,x+\xi]\cap A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\big|[x,x+\xi]\cap I_{n}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{M}\Big(2\int_{0}^{|I_{n}|}t^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+(\xi-|I_{n}|)|I_{n}|^{2}\Big),\end{split}

(36)

where the first term bounds the contributions of those
x
such that
[x,x+\xi]\not\supset I_{n}
and the second one corresponds to
[x,x+\xi]\supset I_{n}
. Since
\sum_{n=1}^{M}|I_{n}|=|A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}|
, from (
36
) we get

\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\left|\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x+\xi)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{M}\left(\xi|I_{n}|^{2}-\frac{|I_{n}|^{3}}{3}\right)\leq C|A^{\eta}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\xi.

(37)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\left|\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x+\xi)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x&\leq\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\Bigg|\int_{0}^{1}(\sigma_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(x+t\xi)\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\xi^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\big|(\sigma_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(x+t\xi)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq C|A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}|\xi^{2}.\end{split}

(38)

Combining (
37
) and (
38
), recalling that
|A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}|\leq C_{\eta}
uniformly on
\varepsilon
, we get (
35
), from which the bound on the
H^{s}
seminorm follows easily:

\begin{split}\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\frac{\left|\sigma_{\varepsilon}(y)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\tau/\varepsilon}^{\tau/\varepsilon}\frac{\left|\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x+\xi)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}}{\xi^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\min\{1,|\xi|\}}{|\xi|^{2s}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi<+\infty,\end{split}

since
\min\{1,|\xi|\}|\xi|^{-2s}
is integrable for
2s\in(1,2)
.
∎

We can now prove the coercivity property of
\Phi^{a}
.

Theorem 5.4

(Coercivity in
H^{k+s}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
)
.

Let
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence in
H^{k+s}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R})

,

\left|v_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|\equiv 1,\quad\text{for }\left|x\right|\geq\frac{\tau}{\varepsilon},

and
S\coloneqq\sup_{\varepsilon}\Phi^{a_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}_{\tau/\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})<\infty
.

For
k\geq 1
, the sequence of the derivatives
(v_{\varepsilon}^{(k)})_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
:
there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
S
such that for all
\varepsilon
:

\|v_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|v_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}((-\tau/\varepsilon,\,\tau/\varepsilon))}\leq C.

(39)

For
k=0
, recall the sequence
(\sigma_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
provided by Lemma
5.1
and Remark
5.2
. Then, the sequence
(v_{\varepsilon}-\sigma_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
:
there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
S
, such that for all
\varepsilon
:

\|v_{\varepsilon}-\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|v_{\varepsilon}-\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((-\tau/\varepsilon,\,\tau/\varepsilon))}\leq C.

(40)

Moreover, for each choice of

Let us recall the notation

Theorem 6.1
.

For every
u\in BV((0,1),\{\pm 1\})

(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
in

such that

(50)

If we further suppose

to be continuous, then (

.

Proof.

Let us enumerate the elements of the jump-set,

S(u)=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{M}\},\quad 0\eqqcolon t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{M}<t_{M+1}\coloneqq 1.

and let
2\displaystyle\tau=\min_{i\neq j}|t_{i}-t_{j}|
be the minimal distance among jump points or between a jump point and an edge of the interval. We also let

s_{j}=\operatorname{sgn}\big(u(t_{j}^{+})-u(t_{j}^{-})\big),\quad j=1,\ldots,M

be the “direction” of the jumps. L

By Corollary
4.4
, for any
\lambda\in[0,+\infty]
,

m^{\pm}_{\lambda}=\inf_{T}m^{\pm}_{\lambda}(T).

Consequently, for any
N>0
and
\lambda\in[0,+\infty]
, there exist
T>0
and a function
v^{\pm}_{\lambda}\in H^{k+s}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
such that
v^{\pm}_{\lambda}(x)\equiv\pm\operatorname{sgn}(x)
for
\left|x\right|>T
and, recall Definition
2.5

\begin{split}\Phi^{a(\cdot\,/\lambda)}(v^{\pm}_{\lambda})&\leq m_{\lambda}^{\pm}+\frac{1}{N},\quad\text{for }\lambda\in(0,+\infty),\\
\Phi^{\bar{a}}(v^{\pm}_{0})&\leq m_{0}^{\pm}+\frac{1}{N},\\
\Phi^{a_{\inf}}(v^{\pm}_{\infty})&\leq m_{\infty}^{\pm}+\frac{1}{N}.\\
\end{split}

(51)

Moreover, it is also possible to suppose that
T>1
and, when
\lambda\neq+\infty
, even that
T>\lambda
.
We note that by construction,
||v^{\pm}_{\lambda}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=||v^{\pm}_{\lambda}||_{L^{\infty}((-T,T))}<+\infty
and as well, if
k\geq 1
,
||(v^{\pm}_{\lambda})^{(k)}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=||(v^{\pm}_{\lambda})^{(k)}||_{L^{2}((-T,T))}<+\infty
.

We shall approximate
u
, both in measure and in energy, by gluing together rescaled versions of the approximate optimal profiles
v_{j}\coloneqq v^{s_{j}}_{\lambda}
(from now on we consider
\lambda
fixed and drop the explicit dependence). For this purpose, we partition

\displaystyle I_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(b_{j-1},b_{j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,M,

\displaystyle b_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{t_{j}+t_{j+1}}{2},\quad j=1,\ldots,M-1,\,b_{0}\coloneqq 0,\,b_{M}\coloneqq 1.

Then, we define the sequences

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{M}v_{j}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\delta}(x-t_{j}^{\delta})\Big)\mathsf{1}_{I_{j}}(x),&\text{ for }\lambda\in(0,+\infty),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{M}v_{j}\Big(\mkern 1.0mu\frac{x-t_{j}^{\delta}}{\varepsilon}\mkern 1.0mu\Big)\mathsf{1}_{I_{j}}(x),&\text{ for }\lambda\in\{0,+\infty\},\\
\end{cases}

(52)

where

, but their precise value will be determined later with respect to

For
\varepsilon
small enough, precisely such that

\tau_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\varepsilon T+\delta<\tau,

\int_{I_{i}}\int_{I_{j}}\frac{|u^{(k)}_{\varepsilon}(y)-u^{(k)}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{(\tau-\tau_{\varepsilon})^{1+2s}}\big(\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{i})}\big).

In order to prove the claim, note that the integrand can be non-zero only if

. Indeed,
|x-y|\geq 2\tau
when
|i-j|>1
, while, if

,

u^{(k)}_{\varepsilon}(x)-u^{(k)}(y)\equiv 0,

for any

and

. Thus, the integrand is non-zero if

|x-y|\geq\tau-\varepsilon T-\delta\geq\tau-\tau_{\varepsilon},

and consequently

\displaystyle\int_{I_{i}}\int_{I_{j}}\frac{|u^{(k)}_{\varepsilon}(y)-u^{(k)}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{I_{i}}\int_{I_{j}}\frac{|u^{(k)}_{\varepsilon}(y)-u^{(k)}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{(\tau-\tau_{\varepsilon})^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(\tau-\tau_{\varepsilon})^{1+2s}}\big(\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{i})}\big).

We note that, for
\varepsilon
small enough,
u_{\varepsilon}
may differ from

only inside the intervals
t_{j}+(-\tau_{\varepsilon},\tau_{\varepsilon})

of total length
2\tau_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
, thus proving convergence in measure. As for the energy, from (

, we have:

\begin{split}&F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u_{\varepsilon})-\sum_{j=1}^{M}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u_{\varepsilon},I_{j})\\
&\qquad=\sum_{i\neq j}\varepsilon^{2(k+s)-1}\int_{I_{i}}\int_{I_{j}}a\Big(\frac{x}{\delta},\frac{y}{\delta}\Big)\frac{\big|{u^{(k)}_{\varepsilon}(y)-u^{(k)}_{\varepsilon}(x)}\big|^{2}}{|y-x|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\

If
k\geq 1
, we have by construction (see (

If

Main step, construction of
\mathfrak{v}^{j,T}_{\varepsilon}

Outside of the same sets

defined for

Again by construction,

The only obstruction is thus the possible presence of other large intervals

on which
||v^{j}_{\varepsilon}|-1|\leq\eta

Let

, and suppose that such an interval exists, say

by first flattening

on such intervals, then removing a portion of the flattened part.

will then be a function of

We apply the flattening Lemma
4.1
in the version of Remark
4.2
, case (2), with

and
c=\tau/\varepsilon
,

and

as just above, while

Then we get
\overline{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}\in H^{k+s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\begin{split}\overline{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}&\equiv\operatorname{sgn}(v_{\varepsilon}^{j}),\quad\text{in }(b^{\prime\prime},c^{\prime\prime}),\\
\overline{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}&\equiv v^{j}_{\varepsilon},\qquad\text{in }(I^{\prime})^{\mathsf{c}},\\
\Phi^{a^{j}_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}_{\tau/\varepsilon}(\overline{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon})&\leq\Big(1+\frac{C}{N}+\frac{C_{N}}{L^{2s}}\Big)\Phi^{a^{j}_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}_{\tau/\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon}^{j}),\end{split}

(60)

both
C
and
C_{N}
are independent on
L
.

Since
\delta/\varepsilon\to 0
, for
\varepsilon
small enough we can find
b^{\prime\prime\prime}
,
c^{\prime\prime\prime}
such that

\begin{split}\frac{L}{8}\geq b^{\prime\prime\prime}-b^{\prime\prime}\geq\frac{L}{10},\qquad\frac{L}{8}\geq c^{\prime\prime}-c^{\prime\prime\prime}\geq\frac{L}{10},\quad\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}(c^{\prime\prime\prime}-b^{\prime\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{split}

Then, we remove the interval
(b^{\prime\prime\prime},c^{\prime\prime\prime})
defining

\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}(x)&\text{for }x\leq b^{\prime\prime\prime},\\
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}(x+c^{\prime\prime\prime}-b^{\prime\prime\prime})&\text{for }x>b^{\prime\prime\prime},\end{cases}

in particular, in place of
(b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
, now
||\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}|-1|\leq\eta
on an interval of length

c^{\prime}-b^{\prime}-(c^{\prime\prime\prime}-b^{\prime\prime\prime})\leq\frac{L}{2}.

Clearly,

\begin{split}\Phi^{a^{j}_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}\big(\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j},(-\infty,b^{\prime\prime\prime})\big)&=\Phi^{a^{j}_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}\big(\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j},(-\infty,b^{\prime\prime\prime})\big),\\
\Phi^{a^{j}_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}\big(\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j},(b^{\prime\prime\prime},+\infty)\big)&=\Phi^{a^{j}_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}\big(\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j},(c^{\prime\prime\prime},+\infty)\big),\end{split}

where the second equality follows from
\varepsilon(c^{\prime\prime\prime}-b^{\prime\prime\prime})/\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, since
a(x,y)
is
1
-periodic. We are left with the contribution corresponding to the non-local part of
\Phi^{a^{j}_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}\big(\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j})
for
(x,y)\in(-\infty,b^{\prime\prime\prime})\times(b^{\prime\prime\prime},+\infty)

If
x,y
are both close to

L/10
to be precise,
\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}(x)=\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}(y)\in\{\pm 1\}
, and thus the corresponding contribution is null.

Otherwise

and, if

,

\begin{split}&\int_{-\infty}^{b^{\prime\prime\prime}}\int_{b^{\prime\prime\prime}}^{+\infty}a_{\varepsilon}^{j}(x,y)\frac{\left|(\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j})^{(k)}(x)-(\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j})^{(k)}(y)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\

&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{L/10}^{+\infty}\frac{\left|(\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j})^{(k)}(x)\right|^{2}}{\xi^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&\leq\frac{C}{L^{2s}}\|(\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j})^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\leq\frac{C}{L^{2s}}\|(\overline{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon})^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\end{split}

which is bounded by

thanks to Theorem
5.4
.

On the other hand, if

, we let
\mathfrak{s}^{j}_{\varepsilon}

from which,

\int_{-\infty}^{b^{\prime\prime\prime}}\int_{b^{\prime\prime\prime}}^{+\infty}a_{\varepsilon}^{j}(x,y)\frac{\left|\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}(x)-\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{L^{2s}}\left\lVert\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}-\mathfrak{s}_{\varepsilon}^{j}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{C}{L^{2s-1}}.

Again, we conclude thanks to Theorem
5.4

\left\lVert\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}-\mathfrak{s}_{\varepsilon}^{j}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\left\lVert\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}-\overline{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}^{j}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

In conclusion,

\begin{split}\Phi^{a^{j}_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}(\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j})&\leq\Phi^{a_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}^{j}}\left(\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}\right)+o_{L\to\infty}(1)\\

Final step.

By Theorem
5.4
,
the sequence

is bounded in

and uniformly.
Therefore, we can extract a subsequence converging to some

We also denote

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon}\Phi^{a^{j}_{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}(\mathfrak{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(\mathfrak{v}^{j})\,\mathrm{d}x+\bar{a}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus D_{R}}f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,
