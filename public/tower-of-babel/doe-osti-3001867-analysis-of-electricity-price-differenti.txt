Introduction

The Challenge
• Operation Gigawatt is a vision for Utah to meet its growing energy needs with a focus
  on doubling energy generation. One of the greatest challenges to meet this goal will be
  the transmission and distribution system.​
• Utah seeks analysis to inform discussions on new generation, the likely network
  congestion based on new power flows, and modeling of the state's energy infrastructure.
The Approach
• A baseline understanding of recent historical congestion in the existing transmission
  system will be developed based on electricity market prices. Products will visualize and
  provide metrics on congestion within Utah and between Utah and its neighbors during
  2015-2023.

Energy market prices offer insight into network congestion

• A locational marginal price (LMP) is the price for electric energy at a Wholesale Energy Market
 specific location (i.e., node) in the system. LMPs are the sum of three
 components: Price Nodes in Utah
 Image by Julie Mulvaney Kemp, Berkeley Lab
 • Energy component: The price for electric energy at the “reference
 point,” which is the load-weighted average of the system nodal
 prices.

 • Congestion component: The marginal cost of congestion at this
 Reporting Market, Zone
 location relative to the load-weighted average of the system nodal
 prices.

 • Loss component: The cost of losses at this location relative to the
 load-weighted average of the system nodal prices. This component is
 typically small.

• Differences in LMPs between two locations provide a signal for how
 valuable alleviating congestion (e.g., by expanding transmission capacity)
 between the locations could be.

 Background: Methodology
 We are analyzing market signals for the value of additional transmission according to spatial differences in observed prices.

 Mean marginal transmission market value between nodes A and B
Interpretations Definition

 = mean absolute price difference between nodes A and B

 • Average “locational spot price of transmission”
 • Average hourly payment from holding two financial transmission right options of equal size: one from A
 to B and one from B to A
 • If the link were an existing line, the average congestion (i.e., shadow) price of the line’s transmission
 constraint.

 • Does not fully capture all benefits of transmission (e.g., reliability contribution, reduction in un- or mis-

 • Ignores dynamic impacts of transmission investment
 • Is a marginal quantity and therefore does not reflect saturation effects that come with building
