Executive Summary
Motivation and Importance
The increasing demand for solar energy faces significant barriers, including inefficient
construction processes, high project costs limiting LCOE reductions, and diminishing availability
of "good" sites with favorable interconnection. Conventional solar installations are often
constrained by long construction timelines and the need for flat, large plots. Furthermore, the
industry struggles with a shortage of skilled labor. Planted Solar aims to address these
challenges by fundamentally transforming solar deployment from a construction project into a
streamlined manufacturing process. Our novel approach combines high-density racking,
integrated terrain-following design software, and installation automation. Unlike competing
solutions that either assist with standard array assembly or offer dense packing without terrain
adaptability or automation, Planted's technology uniquely brings together a new array product
with in-place automated manufacturing to achieve the lowest possible cost, greater site
flexibility, and accelerated deployment.

Project Goals and Objectives
In the scope of this project, Planted Solar will integrate additional technology elements that are
necessary to implement the company’s existing array design effectively on sloped/undulating
sites, small/irregular sites, and sites with significant sub-surface risks (such as brownfield sites
with contaminated soil or shallow rocks). Planted Solar will validate these technical advances on
full-scale arrays that exhibit some of these characteristics. End of project goals include
bankability assessments for these pilot systems, firm orders for further systems with similar
characteristics, and techno-economic analyses addressing this segment of the PV market.

Public Benefit
The project contributed to solar capacity expansion and national energy goals by enabling
greater energy generation per acre of land (2X more), allowing larger projects on existing sites
or requiring less land for target capacities. It opens up solar development on highly undulating
terrain without costly grading, making previously impractical or uneconomical sites viable. This
shifts solar projects to non-prime land, reducing competition with agriculture or other
high-value uses and making site identification easier. The design also allows for sheep grazing
under the arrays, supporting land dual-use and vegetation management. Ultimately, Planted's
advancements mitigate technical risks, reduce LCOE, and increase siting flexibility, accelerating
the deployment of abundant and affordable clean energy.

Background
A handful of factors have become critical barriers to beneficial deployments of solar projects.
First, installing conventional solar arrays entails construction projects which are massively
inefficient compared to other factory-streamlined processes. Long construction timelines and
high project costs inhibit further substantial reductions in LCOE, which ultimately determines
the incentive to deploy.

Site availability, particularly sites with good interconnection, has also become rare as more and
more “good” sites have been built:

Analysis from Paces shows trends of sites across the United States (“Suitable sites” = parcels
with +1 MW of feeder capacity and +15 buildable acres.)

 Page 6 of 39

Racking and installation methods that accommodate the high variation in site conditions are
needed. Skilled labor has also become harder and harder to find. With this project, Planted aims
to reduce these barriers by developing high-density racking, integrated terrain-following design
software, and installation automation that makes it more flexible, easier, and faster to install
solar projects.

While others are beginning to apply automation to installing solar fields, they are focusing on
techniques that assist with assembly of standard arrays, but do not fundamentally transform
them from construction projects into a true mass-manufacturing analog. Other densely-packed,
low-profile array designs may appear similar to our approach, but rely on manual assembly in
the field, and cannot follow uneven terrain. Planted’s technology uniquely combines a novel

 Page 7 of 39

array design with an automated in-place manufacturing approach. The synergy between these
two innovations is essential to achieving the lowest possible cost. No system other than
Planted’s offers that combination of performance: e.g. the few that offer high energy density
and good utilization of irregularly-shaped plots require flat land and are more expensive; the
one that offers comparable slope/undulation tolerance in other respects is like a standard SAT
but even more expensive; and the competing options with lowest LCOE are not the most
flexible ones.

Project Objectives:
Specific objectives per the Statement of Project Objectives were:

1.0 - Develop/demonstrate technology needed to install on undulating/sloped terrain and
land with sub-surface risks: Planted Solar’s existing, demonstrated array design already allows
connections to articulate so the array can conform to sloped/undulating topography. To take
advantage of this capability and efficiently and successfully install an array on a
sloped/undulating site, we need to be able to quickly and accurately map terrain, vary the
prescribed post depth from post to post so the array properly conforms to the terrain and
satisfies articulation and no-self-shading constraints, and enable the installation robot to adapt
dynamically during installation to account for deviations between the topographic map and
ground truth. Task 5.0 continued this effort in the second budget period.

2.0 - Develop/demonstrate technology needed to install on small/irregular plots: The small
unit cell size in Planted Solar’s existing, demonstrated array design in principle enables high
coverage of small/irregular parcels (unlike conventional technologies constrained by the need to
orient modules in long rows). To take advantage of this capability, the array layout software
must be able to automatically design arrays, with minimal supervision by engineers, that
conform to a variety of additional constraints. Task 6.0 continued this effort in the second
budget period.

3.0 - Complete techno-economic analyses: Planted Solar will develop detailed cost,
performance, and technology impact models that address varied scenarios, including varied
combinations of factors such as geographical location, climate, soil conditions, slope,
undulations, plot size/shape, module type and performance, input unit costs, O&M, financial
parameters, energy storage, and other site-specific details. We will assess the impact of these
factors on capital cost and LCOE and compare the results with alternative conventional and
emerging technologies. As part of this Task we will identify and analyze the leading competitive
array designs, and also recruit solar project developers with proven track records of early
technology adoption to join an informal “early stage product council” to receive briefings on
Planted’s technology, demonstrate customer engagement, and offer voice-of-customer input.

Public Benefit
The project contributed to solar capacity expansion and national energy goals and benefit U.S.
electricity customers by lowering energy costs, expanding the nation’s ability to deploy solar
generation (thereby reducing air emissions), and enhancing grid resiliency.

Cost savings. Over the next two decades, the U.S. will need very large additions of utility-scale
and distributed solar to decarbonize electricity—multiple credible pathways envision solar

 Page 9 of 39

providing ~40% [ref 1] or more of U.S. electricity by mid-century. In that context, assume a
“business-as-usual” baseline built with conventional single-axis-tracking (SAT) PV. Now suppose
20 GW of that buildout instead uses Planted arrays—a high but plausible target—corresponding
to ~40 TWh/year of generation. If Planted reduces levelized cost of energy (LCOE) by ~25%, total
savings to customers would be ≥ $500 million/year at a $50/MWh baseline. Actual savings
would likely be higher because siting flexibility (higher density; use of irregular,
sloped/undulating, or brownfield plots) allows projects to locate nearer load and existing
interconnections, reducing the need for long-distance transmission. While location-specific,
national studies suggest transmission can add a material premium to delivered energy costs
(often on the order of several percent up to a third of plant-level LCOE), so avoided transmission
buildout can be comparable to generation-side savings. A reasonable first estimate for customer
savings is on the order of ~$1 billion per year.

Solar capacity and siting. Identifying and permitting sites for both distributed and utility-scale
solar has become more challenging nationwide due to local land-use concerns, habitat and
agricultural impacts, and transmission interconnection backlogs. Planted addresses these
constraints by (1) cutting land needs by more than half (vs. a conventional system producing the
same energy) and (2) expanding the envelope of cost-effective sites to include highly sloped or
undulating terrain, irregular boundaries, and land with sub-surface risks or on brownfields.
Under these constraints, the “last” tens of gigawatts of otherwise planned solar may be delayed
or not built in some regions without technologies like Planted that unlock more sites and denser
layouts—mitigating the need for long new transmission corridors.

Planted’s system generates approximately 570 MWh/acre/year, compared to 280
MWh/acre/year for conventional single-axis tracker (SAT) systems—a 2–3x increase in energy
generation per acre. This translates to a land savings of over 50% for a project targeting the
same annual energy yield. For example, a 10 MWDC SAT system that generates ~14 GWh/year
typically requires ~50 acres; a Planted system generating the same energy would require ~25
acres.
Beyond raw density, Planted’s array allows effective development on irregular, sloped, and
undulating terrain without grading or trenching, thereby expanding siting options to land
previously considered unsuitable—including brownfields, non-contiguous parcels, and terrain
with up to 30% grade or 5°/m undulation (vs. 15% and ~2°/m for typical SAT systems).

Distributed generation and grid resiliency. Distributed generation (including behind-the-meter,
community-choice/aggregation programs where applicable, and community solar) improves
resiliency by placing generation near consumption, easing peak-time transmission stress and
enabling local service during transmission outages. Site size and shape often limit these
projects; Planted’s ability to approximately double site energy output and conform to irregular
or noncontiguous parcels increases the amount of distributed generation that can be built
cost-effectively. The value to customers is difficult to quantify precisely and depends on local
tariffs and avoided-cost structures, but an order-of-magnitude estimate of ~$100 million/year

soft-cost reductions and interconnection reforms take effect.

Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
Throughout the project, Planted Solar achieved substantial progress in transforming solar
deployment from a construction project into a streamlined manufacturing process, particularly
addressing the challenges of sloped/undulating and small/irregular sites.

Key Positive Outcomes and Significance:

 ●​ Successfully demonstrated a technology that:
 ○​ Streamlines project planning and engineering and reduces project completion
               time by ~50%;
 ○​ Reduces the amount of steel used by ~70%;
 ○​ Generates 2-3X more energy per acre
 ○​ Increases siting flexibility and reduces land use competition by better exploiting
 irregularly shaped plots, undulating terrain (without the need to grade/fill it),
 and brownfield sites; and
 ○​ Reduces levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) by at least 20%, typically 25+%, and
 up to ~30% if land is expensive, or if the site has challenging terrain features or
 soil conditions.
 ●​ Completed the project on-time and on-budget, making efficient use of DOE funding
 and private funding matching.
 ●​ Automated Installation Efficiency (GNG-A & GNG-B): The project successfully
 demonstrated a post placement accuracy of ≥99% on sloped/undulating terrain (over
 200 posts) and an acceptable module attachment yield of ≥99% (over 200 modules).
 This is highly significant as it validates the core premise of automated, efficient assembly
 in challenging environments, drastically reducing the need for rework and accelerating
 deployment. This capability directly mitigates the inefficient construction processes,
 making solar installation faster and more reliable.
 ●​ Advanced Site Layout Software (GNG-C): Planted Solar's automated layout creation for
 small/irregular sub-plots achieved completion in less than 5 minutes, with comparable
 area utilization to manual engineer designs. This is a crucial breakthrough for maximizing

                                          Page 35 of 39

 energy generation on "non-prime" or constrained land. It significantly reduces design
 time and cost, making previously uneconomical sites viable and increasing siting
 flexibility, which is a key public benefit.
 ●​ Strong Customer Engagement and LCOE Advantage (GNG-D & GNG-E): Securing signed
 contracts for pilot arrays on sloped and small sub-plot sites (GNG-D) demonstrates
 strong market interest and acceptance of Planted's innovative approach. Furthermore,
 the preliminary techno-economic report showing a >10% LCOE advantage for Planted
 technology on pilot projects (GNG-E) is a critical indicator of commercial viability. This
 economic benefit directly addresses the high project costs limiting LCOE reductions and
 reinforces the path towards broad commercial adoption of clean energy.
 ●​ Product Bankability and Certification: The completion of UL2703 certification for the
 array technology is a significant step towards meeting industry standards and is a
 keystone for bankability review. Contracting with DNV Energy for a comprehensive
 bankability report further strengthens the commercialization potential by providing
 independent validation of the technology's financial attractiveness. This helps de-risk
 investments for future deployments.
 ●​ Operational Refinements and Innovation: Continuous improvements to the post
 installer machines and array product designs, informed by GNG demos and extensive
 small-scale demonstrations, highlight a robust development process. The rigorous
 updates to O&M procedures, including module maintenance and installation guidelines,
 and compatibility testing with major module manufacturers, directly enhance the
 long-term viability and attractiveness of Planted's solution. The development of a new
 grass maintenance robot specifically for high-density arrays underpins the commitment
 to addressing practical O&M challenges and further boosts bankability and customer
 interest.

Challenges Encountered and Lessons Learned:
The primary challenge encountered under this award was permitting delays by external entities
for the demonstration systems (Milestones 2.5.2, 2.5.3, 2.6.2, 2.6.3, and EOP milestones).
Despite Planted Solar submitting permits on time, the review and approval process by
Authorities Having Jurisdiction (AHJ) and local city departments (e.g., for electrical permits,
zoning) extended beyond the planned timeline, pushing back the completion of the demo
system builds.

Lessons Learned:

 ●​ Factor in Extended Permitting Timelines: The project highlighted the critical need to
 significantly buffer timelines for external regulatory processes, especially for novel
 technologies that may require additional scrutiny or education for local authorities.
 ●​ Contingency Planning for Field Demonstrations: Future project planning should
 incorporate more flexible schedules for physical demonstrations, acknowledging that
 these are often contingent on external approvals. This could involve parallel work

                                         Page 36 of 39

 streams where possible or pre-identifying alternative sites with potentially faster
 approval processes.

Pivots or Reduction in Scope: As noted in the "Project Objectives" section, Task 4.0 was
removed due to an executive order.

In conclusion, the project has largely met its ambitious objectives, delivering significant
technical advancements and demonstrating clear pathways to commercialization. The
challenges encountered, primarily external permitting delays, underscore the importance of
robust project management and proactive engagement with regulatory bodies in future
large-scale deployments. The successful development of high-density racking, terrain-following
software, and installation automation positions Planted Solar as a leader in accelerating solar
deployment by reducing LCOE, increasing siting flexibility, and mitigating technical risks.

customer base ready to deploy solar energy at scale. The path forward will focus on commercial

 ●​ Increased reliability of the technology
