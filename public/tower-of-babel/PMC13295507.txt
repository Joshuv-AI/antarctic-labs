Lower airway transcriptome signatures in asthma: a newly described cytokine C10orf99 identification
Authors: Dong Zhang
Published: 2026

To the Editor

Airway epithelial cells are important barrier cells of the bronchi and serve as a crucial carrier for studying airway inflammation and remodeling in asthma [1, 2]. Their gene expression profiles are shaped by the air-liquid interface (ALI) differentiation state [1, 3]. Based on the ALI differentiation state of human bronchial epithelial cells (HBEs), we evaluated whether and to what extent house dust mite (HDM) stimulation mimics asthma-related transcriptome changes in the lower airway.

This study recruited 8 healthy volunteers, all of whom met the criteria for healthy clinical characteristics. HBEs were obtained via bronchial brushing, subjected to ALI differentiation, and then used for RNA sequencing. Using differential gene criteria, we identified asthma-related genes and modules in HBEs after ALI differentiation. Among these differentially expressed genes (DEGs), we evaluated the function of the novel cytokine C10orf99 in the airways.

We identified 1506 asthma-related genes among the DEGs, of which 753 were upregulated in HDM-stimulated samples compared to healthy controls, and 753 were downregulated (Fig. 1A-C). Among the 753 upregulated genes, the novel cytokine C10orf99 exhibited a correlation with immune system process, extracellular region, and inflammatory response in asthma (Fig. S1A, B and C). The human gene C10orf99 and its murine homolog gene (2610528A11Rik) encode the G protein-coupled receptor 15 ligand (GPR15L). The C10orf99 murine homolog gene (2610528A11Rik) was significantly increased in HDM-induced acute or chronic asthma, whereas 2610528A11Rik remained unchanged in OVA-induced acute or chronic asthma (Fig. S1B and C). A study reported that C10orf99 regulates the proinflammatory response of keratinocytes and skin barrier formation [4]. Tseng et al. uncovered that C10orf99 is an epithelial inflammation-derived pruritogen [5]. Based on these DEGs, we performed Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) functional analyses (Fig. 1D and E). We found that these genes are closely associated with immune system process, extracellular region, inflammatory response, cytokine production, cell-cell junction, epithelium migration, cell death, etc. Then, the role of the C10orf99 murine homolog gene was validated in mice: we observed inflammatory cell infiltration, mucous secretion, airway epithelial barrier damage, and collagen deposition after intranasal instillation of recombinant mouse GPR15L protein (Fig. 2A and B).

In summary, the gene signature of ALI differentiation of human airway epithelial cells identified in this study provides an effective tool for identifying asthma subtypes with underlying diseases driven by distinct molecular mechanisms. Additionally, this study characterized the function of the novel cytokine C10orf99 in the airway.
