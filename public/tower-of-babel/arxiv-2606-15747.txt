Painlevé XXXIV asymptotics for the defocusing mKdV equation with step-like initial data in transition regions
Engui Fan 1 , Zhaoyu Wang 2 , Yidan Zhang 3

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem for the defocusing modified Korteweg-de Vries (mKdV) equation with a step-like initial data. Based on the Riemann-Hilbert problem associated with the mKdV equation, we derive the long-time asymptotic expansion of the solution to the defocusing mKdV equation in two transition regions using the nonlinear steepest descent method. It comes out that the leading term in the expansion is shown to match the corresponding background constants. The subleading term, however, decays at the order 𝒪 ( t − 2 / 3 ) \mathcal{O}(t^{-2/3}) , and its coefficient is derived from the associated Painlevé XXXIV model. Key words: Defocusing mKdV equation, Riemann-Hilbert problem, nonlinear steepest descent method, long-time asymptotics, Painlevé XXXIV. MSC 2020: 35Q51; 35Q15; 35C20; 35P25; 34M55.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the long-time
asymptotics in transition regions for the defocusing modified Korteweg-de Vries (mKdV) equation with step-like
initial data

(1.1)

\displaystyle q_{t}(x,t)-6q^{2}(x,t)q_{x}(x,t)+q_{xxx}(x,t)=0,

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle q(x,0)=q_{0}(x)\to\begin{cases}c_{l},&x\to-\infty,\\
c_{r},&x\to+\infty,\end{cases}

where
c_{l}>c_{r}>0
, and under the assumption that the solution
q(x,t)
approaches to some nonzero real constants as
x\to\pm\infty
respectively,
i.e.,

(1.3)

q(x,t)\to\begin{cases}c_{l},&x\to-\infty,\\
c_{r},&x\to+\infty,\end{cases}

The defocusing mKdV equation (
1.1
) is a canonical model in mathematical physics,
describing various nonlinear phenomena such as acoustic waves and phonons in certain
anharmonic lattices
[
55
,
43
]
, and Alfvén waves in cold, collisionless
plasmas
[
35
,
36
]
. Early works on the long-time asymptotics of the mKdV equation (
1.1
) concentrated on initial data vanishing as
x\to\pm\infty
.
We also refer the readers to
[
15
,
41
]
for long-time asymptotics of the defocusing mKdV equation with vanishing initial data in lower regularity spaces.

The study of long-time asymptotics for Cauchy problems of nonlinear integrable systems with step-like initial data has a long history
and can be traced back to the pioneering work
[
30
]
of Gurevich and Pitaevskii on the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation.
Working within the framework of Whitham modulation theory
[
50
]
, they predicted the emergence of
highly oscillatory structures, now referred to as dispersive shock waves in the long-time dynamics.
The rigorous mathematical justification of this phenomenon was subsequently done by Khruslov
[
34
]

via the inverse scattering transform in terms of the Marchenko integral equations and the so-called asymptotic soliton.
Rigorous asymptotic analysis of step-like Cauchy problems for integrable systems began with the papers
[
6
,
12
]
.
Since then, the long-time asymptotics of integrable systems with step-like initial data have been extensively
studied: for the KdV equation
[
22
,
23
]
;
for the focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS)
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
;
for the focusing mKdV equation
[
37
,
42
,
38
,
39
,
28
]
;
as well as for some nonlocal integrable systems
[
45
,
46
,
44
,
54
]
.

Since the scaling transformation

q\to-q,\ \ x\to-x,\ \ t\to-t

keep the form of the defocusing mKdV equation (
1.1
) unchanged,
it is sufficient to consider two cases
c_{l}\geq c_{r}\geq 0
and
c_{l}>0>c_{r}>-c_{l}
, which lead to quite different asymptotic analysis that a kink soliton region appears in the later case
[
48
]
.
For the case
c_{r}>c_{l}>0
, the long-time asymptotics of dispersive shock wave solution has been obtained in
[
48
]
. For the case
c_{l}>c_{r}>0
,
we obtained long-time asymptotics for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) in three main regions
in our recent paper
[
53
]
. More precisely, we summarize as follows (see Figure
1
):

•

Left region
\mathcal{R}_{\textup{I}}
:=
\left\{(x,t):\xi<-\frac{c_{l}^{2}}{2},\ \xi:=\frac{x}{12t}\right\}
,
in which the leading term is given by the constant
c_{l}
and the sub-leading term is expressed in terms of the parabolic cylinder function;

•

Middle region
\mathcal{R}_{\textup{I}}
:=
\left\{(x,t):-\frac{c_{l}^{2}}{2}<\xi<-\frac{c_{r}^{2}}{2}\right\}
, in which the leading term is given by a slowly varying factor, while the sub-leading term is derived from the Airy function;

•

Right region
\mathcal{R}_{\textup{III}}
:=
\left\{(x,t):-\frac{c_{r}^{2}}{2}<\xi<-\frac{c_{r}^{2}}{6}\right\}\cup\left\{(x,t):\xi>-\frac{c_{r}^{2}}{6}\right\}
, in which the leading term is given by the constant
c_{r}
, and the sub-leading term arises either from the Bessel function or from the non-analytic part of the reflection coefficient on the real line.

The remaining problem is to characterize the long-time asymptotic behavior in the regions adjacent to these three regions, namely the transition regions,

•

The first transition region
\mathcal{T}_{\textup{I}}:=\left\{(x,t):\left|\xi+\frac{c_{l}^{2}}{2}\right|t^{\frac{2}{3}}<C,\ \ C>0\right\}
,

•

The second transition region
\mathcal{T}_{\textup{II}}:=\left\{(x,t):\left|\xi+\frac{c_{r}^{2}}{2}\right|t^{\frac{2}{3}}<C\right\}
,

as illustrated in Figure
1

Our main results in two regions

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be the global solution of the Cauchy problem (

1.2

for the defocusing mKdV equation with a step-like initial data under Assumption

, and denote
by

q(x,t)

in the transition regions.

(I)

For
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{\textup{I}}
, we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle q(x,t)=c_{l}+t^{-2/3}f_{\textup{I}}(\xi,s)+\mathcal{O}(t^{1/3-2\epsilon}),

where
\epsilon
is any real number with
\frac{1}{2}<\epsilon<\frac{2}{3}
and

\displaystyle s=-t^{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{g_{\textup{I}}^{(1)}(c_{l})}{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left|g_{\textup{I}}^{(2)}(c_{l})\right|^{\frac{1}{3}}},\;f_{\textup{I}}(\xi,s)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left|g_{\textup{I}}^{(2)}(c_{l})\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left(s^{2}\frac{\textup{Ai}^{\prime}(s)}{\textup{Ai}(s)}-2s\right).

Here,
\textup{Ai}(s)
is the Airy function and

\displaystyle g_{\textup{I}}^{(1)}(c_{l})=6(2c_{l})^{\frac{1}{2}}(c_{l}^{2}+2\xi),\quad g_{\textup{I}}^{(2)}(c_{l})=3(2c_{l})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(c_{l}^{2}+2\xi)+4(2c_{l})^{\frac{3}{2}}.

(II)

For
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{\textup{II}}
, we have

(1.5)

\displaystyle q(x,t)=c_{r}+t^{-2/3}f_{\textup{II}}(\xi,s)+\mathcal{O}(t^{1/3-2\epsilon}),

where
\epsilon
is any real number with
\frac{1}{2}<\epsilon<\frac{2}{3}
and

\displaystyle s=-t^{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{g_{\textup{II}}^{(1)}(c_{r})}{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left|g_{\textup{II}}^{(2)}(c_{r})\right|^{\frac{1}{3}}},

\displaystyle f_{\textup{II}}(\xi,s)=2\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left|g_{\textup{II}}^{(2)}(c_{r})\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left(2^{\frac{1}{3}}a(s)q(-2^{\frac{1}{3}}s)+u(s)+\frac{s}{2}\right),

with

\displaystyle g_{\textup{II}}^{(1)}(c_{r})=6(2c_{r})^{\frac{1}{2}}(c_{r}^{2}+2\xi),\quad g_{\textup{II}}^{(2)}(c_{r})=3(2c_{r})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(c_{r}^{2}+2\xi)+4(2c_{r})^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Moreover,
q(s)
satisfies the Painlevé
II
equation

q^{\prime\prime}(s)=sq(s)+2q^{3}(s)+\frac{\arg r(c_{r})}{\pi}-\frac{1}{2},

with asymptotics

q(s)=\begin{cases}-\left(\frac{\arg r(c_{r})}{\pi}-\frac{1}{2}\right)s^{-1}+\mathcal{O}\left(s^{-4}\right),&\qquad s\to+\infty,\\
\sqrt{-\frac{s}{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(s^{-1}\right),&\qquad s\to-\infty,\end{cases}

and
u(s)
satisfies the Painlevé
XXXIV
equation

u^{\prime\prime}(s)=4u(s)^{2}+2su(s)+\frac{u^{\prime}(s)^{2}-\frac{\arg r(c_{r})^{2}}{\pi^{2}}}{2u(s)},

with asymptotics

u(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\frac{\arg r(c_{r})}{2\pi\sqrt{s}}+\mathcal{O}\left(s^{-2}\right),&\qquad s\to+\infty,\\
-\frac{s}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(s^{-2}\right),&\qquad s\to-\infty.\end{array}\right.

Establishing transition asymptotics for integrable systems is usually a challenging task, which typically involve the nonlinear special functions – Painlevé transcendents and exhibit some universal features. For instance, one encounters the Painlevé II transcendents and its higher order analogues in
[
13
,
17
,
20
,
47
,
31
,
7
,
49
,
51
]
, the Painlevé I transcendents and its higher order analogues in
[
5
,
16
]
, and a model RH problem associated with the Painlevé IV equation in
[
11
]
.
In the present work, we shows that the analysis in both transition regions involves RH problems relevant to the Painlevé XXXIV transcendents, which is different from the classical local parametrices used in the analysis in the other regions
[
53
]
.
To be precise, the Painlevé XXXIV equation, which reads as

u^{\prime\prime}(s)=4u(s)^{2}+2su(s)+\frac{u^{\prime}(s)^{2}-(2b)^{2}}{2u(s)},

depends on a parameter
b
. This equation can be obtained from the well-known Painlevé II equation

q^{\prime\prime}(s)=sq(s)+2q^{3}-\nu,\quad\nu=2b+\frac{1}{2},

and its Hamiltonian
[
32
,
25
]
.
It is worthwhile to point out that the transition asymptotics of the focusing mKdV equation for step-like initial data

q_{0}(x)\to\begin{cases}0,\quad\text{as}\;x\to+\infty,\\
c,\quad\text{as}\;x\to-\infty,\;c>0,\end{cases}

is related to the RH problem built from Laguerre polynomials
[
4
]
, and the Painlevé XXXIV transcendents play an important role in asymptotic studies of critical behaviors arsing from integrable differential equations
[
24
,
14
]
, random unitary ensembles
[
32
]
and orthogonal polynomials
[
52
]
.
However, the results in the two transition regions appear to be different; see Theorem
1.1
. In fact, both results are derived from the same Painlevé XXXIV model, which is used (as shown in Appendix
A
) to construct the local RH problem within the nonlinear steepest descent analysis of the original mKdV RH problem. The apparent difference arises from the choice of coefficients; see Appendix
A
for further details.

2.1.
Direct scattering transform

As the member of mKdV hierarchy
[
1
]
, the Lax pair of
the defocusing mKdV equation (
1.1
) is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Phi_{x}+ik\sigma_{3}\Phi=Q\Phi,\\
&\Phi_{t}+4ik^{3}\sigma_{3}\Phi=V\Phi,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Phi=\Phi(k;x,t)
is a
2\times 2
matrix-valued function with
the spectral parameter
k\in\mathbb{C}
. Here,
Q
and
V

are some matrices associated with the potential function
q(x,t)
, defined by

\displaystyle Q=Q(x,t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&q(x,t)\\
q(x,t)&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle V=V(x,t)=4k^{2}Q(x,t)+2ik\sigma_{3}\left(Q_{x}\left(x,t\right)-Q^{2}\left(x,t\right)\right)+2Q^{3}(x,t)-Q_{xx}(x,t).

For
j\in\{l,r\}
, substituting
q(x,t)=c_{j}
into (
2.1
), we obtain
the corresponding “background” Lax pair

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Phi^{b}_{j,x}+ik\sigma_{3}\Phi^{b}_{j}=Q^{b}_{j}\Phi^{b}_{j},\\
&\Phi^{b}_{j,t}+4ik^{3}\sigma_{3}\Phi^{b}_{j}=V^{b}_{j}\Phi^{b}_{j},\end{aligned}\right.

where

(2.3)

Q^{b}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}0&c_{j}\\
c_{j}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad V^{b}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}-2ic_{j}^{2}k&4c_{j}k^{2}+2c_{j}^{3}\\
4c_{j}k^{2}+2c_{j}^{3}&2ic_{j}^{2}k\end{pmatrix}.

The explicit solutions to the “background” Lax pair (
2.2
) are given by

(2.4)

\Phi_{j}^{b}(k;x,t)=\Delta_{j}(k)e^{-i(X_{j}(k)x+\Omega_{j}(k)t)\sigma_{3}},\quad j\in\{l,r\},

where

(2.5)

\displaystyle X_{j}(k)=\sqrt{k^{2}-c_{j}^{2}},\quad\Omega_{j}(k)=2(2k^{2}+c_{j}^{2})X_{j}(k),\quad j\in\{l,r\},

and

(2.6)

\Delta_{j}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{j}(k)+\chi_{j}^{-1}(k)&i\left(\chi_{j}(k)-\chi_{j}^{-1}(k)\right)\\
-i\left(\chi_{j}(k)-\chi_{j}^{-1}(k)\right)&\chi_{j}(k)+\chi_{j}^{-1}(k)\end{array}\right),\quad j\in\{l,r\}.

Here, the function
\chi_{j}
is defined by

\chi_{j}:\mathbb{C}\backslash[-c_{j},c_{j}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad\chi_{j}(k)=\left(\frac{k-c_{j}}{k+c_{j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},\quad j\in\{l,r\},

with the branch cut being chosen such that
\chi_{j}(k)=1+\mathcal{O}(k^{-1})
as
k\rightarrow\infty

For
j\in\{l,r\}
, we denote
\Phi^{b}_{j}(k;x):=\Phi^{b}_{j}(k;x,0)
and
Q_{0}(x):=Q(x,0)
. To proceed, we consider the Lax pair (
2.1
)
for

and define the Jost functions

Proposition 2.1
.

Under the Assumption

and

defined by (

) have the following properties for

(a)

For each

is holomorphic for

admits the following symmetries

\sigma_{1}\overline{\Phi_{j}(\overline{k};x)}\sigma_{1}=\Phi_{j}(k;x),\quad[\Phi_{j}(k;x)]_{1}=\sigma_{1}[\Phi_{j}(-k;x)]_{2},\quad\overline{\Phi_{j}(-\bar{k};x)}=\Phi_{j}(k;x).

For

2.2.
RH characterization of the mKdV equation

In order to construct a basic RH problem for the long-time asymptotic analysis, it is required to operate
