Clinical significance of serum complement levels and C3/C4 ratio in IgA nephropathy: histopathological correlates and prognostic implications
Authors: Gamze Ergun, Emrullah Tasdemir, Umut Kasapoglu, Arzu Ozdemir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractImmunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide. Complement activation plays a central role in its pathogenesis; however, the prognostic significance of serum complement levels remains unclear. In this single-center retrospective study, we evaluated the prognostic value of serum C3, C4 levels, and C3/C4 ratio in 116 biopsy-proven primary IgAN patients with available complement measurements, followed for a median of 37.5 months. Poor prognosis was defined as a ≥ 50% decline in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) or progression to end-stage renal disease; 39 patients (33.6%) reached this endpoint. Serum C3 was significantly lower in the poor prognosis group (113.0 vs. 128.0 mg/dL, p = 0.0004), as was the C3/C4 ratio (3.39 vs. 4.27, p = 0.0009), while serum C4 did not differ significantly (p = 0.520). ROC analysis identified serum C3 (AUC = 0.703, 95% CI: 0.595–0.801, cutoff 121.1 mg/dL) and C3/C4 ratio (AUC = 0.689, 95% CI: 0.575–0.791, cutoff 3.62) as significant predictors. Bootstrap validation confirmed cutoff robustness (C3: 97.0–144.0 mg/dL; C3/C4: 3.03–4.31). Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated significantly worse renal survival with low C3 (p = 0.011) and low C3/C4 ratio (p = 0.001). Serum C3 correlated with tubular atrophy score (r = −0.197, p = 0.036) and C3/C4 ratio with crescent score (r = −0.272, p = 0.003). In expanded multivariate Cox regression, ACE inhibitor/ARB use was independently associated with better prognosis (HR = 0.136, 95% CI: 0.031–0.605, p = 0.009). Serum C3 and C3/C4 ratio at diagnosis are associated with renal prognosis and histopathological severity in IgAN and may serve as adjunctive prognostic markers alongside established parameters.

Introduction

Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide and a leading cause of chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal disease, with approximately 30–40% of patients progressing to ESRD within 20–30 years of diagnosis [1]. The pathogenesis of IgAN is intricately linked to complement system dysregulation. Deposition of galactose-deficient IgA1-containing immune complexes in the glomerular mesangium triggers complement cascade activation, predominantly via the lectin and alternative pathways [2,3]. This leads to the generation of pro-inflammatory mediators, mesangial proliferation, podocyte injury, and progressive tubulointerstitial fibrosis [4]. The recent approval of iptacopan – a factor B inhibitor targeting the alternative complement pathway – for IgAN underscores the therapeutic importance of complement [5].

Although Oxford MEST-C scoring provides valuable histopathological prognostic information, it requires an invasive procedure. Serum complement levels are routinely measured, inexpensive, and widely accessible. Despite their biological relevance, few studies have systematically evaluated the prognostic utility of serum C3, C4, and their ratio in IgAN [6,7]. This study aimed to investigate these associations in a cohort of biopsy-proven primary IgAN patients.

Material and methods

Study design and participants

This single-center retrospective study was conducted at the Nephrology Clinic of Bakırköy Dr. Sadi Konuk Training and Research Hospital, Istanbul, Turkey. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of the University of Health Sciences (decision number 2024-05-32, March 18, 2024) and the study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was waived due to the retrospective observational design. Patients aged ≥18 years with biopsy-proven primary IgAN, follow-up ≥6 months, and available serum C3 and C4 measurements at biopsy were included. Exclusion criteria were: secondary IgAN, active infection, concurrent ANCA vasculitis, familial Mediterranean fever, and incomplete data.

Complement assay methods

Serum C3 and C4 levels were measured by immunoturbidimetric assay (Beckman Coulter AU5800 analyzer) as part of the standard immunological workup performed at the time of renal biopsy. Laboratory reference ranges were: serum C3 90–180 mg/dL and serum C4 16–47 mg/dL. Complement measurements were unavailable in 15 patients (8.2%) due to technical sample issues; these patients were excluded. A comparison of baseline characteristics between included (n = 116) and excluded (n = 15) patients showed no significant differences in eGFR (55.8 vs. 55.0 mL/min/1.73 m2, p = 0.803), proteinuria (2.3 vs. 2.7 g/d, p = 0.791), creatinine (1.4 vs. 1.6 mg/dL, p = 0.504), or age (40.5 vs. 35.0 years, p = 0.357), indicating no evidence of systematic selection bias.

Treatment strategies

Treatment followed the KDIGO guidelines for IgAN. Renin-angiotensin system (RAS) blockade with ACE inhibitors or ARBs was prescribed to all eligible patients. Immunosuppressive therapy was initiated based on disease severity. Glucocorticoids were administered orally (prednisolone 0.5–1.0 mg/kg/d with tapering) or as pulse methylprednisolone (1 g intravenously for 3 consecutive days). Treatment data were recorded from hospital electronic records.

Data collection and outcome definition

Oxford MEST-C scores were obtained from biopsy reports or reevaluated by experienced nephropathologists. Poor prognosis was defined as ≥50% reduction in eGFR from baseline or initiation of renal replacement therapy.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as median (minimum–maximum). Between-group comparisons used the Mann–Whitney U test; categorical variables were compared with chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. Spearman’s correlation assessed associations with MEST-C scores. ROC curve analysis with the Youden index identified optimal cutoff values; bootstrap resampling (1,000 iterations) provided 95% CIs for AUC and cutoff stability. Renal survival was analyzed by Kaplan–Meier method with log-rank testing. The expanded multivariate Cox model included: C3/C4 ratio, eGFR, proteinuria, albumin, hemoglobin, age, hypertension, ACE-I/ARB use, glucocorticoid use, and Oxford T score. Collinearity was assessed using Spearman correlation. All analyses were performed using Python version 3.12 (Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE, USA). p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

The final cohort included 116 patients; 79 (68.1%) were male with a median age of 40.5 years (17–84). After a median follow-up of 37.5 months (3–158), 39 patients (33.6%) reached the poor prognosis endpoint. ACE-I/ARB use was significantly higher in the good prognosis group (96.1% vs. 76.9%, p = 0.004), and pulse steroid use was more frequent in the poor prognosis group (41.0% vs. 20.8%, p = 0.037), consistent with more aggressive treatment in severe disease (Table 1).

Oxford MEST-C scores

T score and C score were significantly higher in the poor prognosis group (p < 0.001 and p = 0.004, respectively). Serum C3 negatively correlated with T score (r = −0.197, p = 0.036) and C3/C4 ratio negatively correlated with C score (r = −0.272, p = 0.003). ROC analysis

Serum C3 (AUC = 0.703, 95% CI: 0.595–0.801, p = 0.0004) and C3/C4 ratio (AUC = 0.689, 95% CI: 0.575–0.791, p = 0.0009) demonstrated significant discriminatory ability. Serum C4 was not significant (AUC = 0.463, 95% CI: 0.350–0.582, p = 0.520) (Table 4). Bootstrap validation confirmed cutoff robustness: serum C3 cutoff 121.1 mg/dL (bootstrap 95% CI: 97.0–144.0 mg/dL) and C3/C4 ratio cutoff 3.62 (bootstrap 95% CI: 3.03–4.31). A median split analysis corroborated these findings: patients with C3 below the cohort median (123.0 mg/dL) had significantly worse renal survival (log-rank p = 0.009).

Cox regression analysis

In univariate Cox regression, C3/C4 ratio (HR = 0.723, 95% CI: 0.573–0.913, p = 0.007), eGFR, creatinine, urea, proteinuria, albumin, hemoglobin, ACE-I/ARB use, and T score were significant predictors. The expanded multivariate model (n = 93), adjusting for C3/C4 ratio, eGFR, proteinuria, albumin, hemoglobin, age, hypertension, ACE-I/ARB use, glucocorticoid use, and Oxford T score, identified ACE-I/ARB use as an independent predictor of better prognosis (HR = 0.136, 95% CI: 0.031–0.605, p = 0.009). C3/C4 ratio showed a trend but did not reach statistical significance (HR = 0.989, p = 0.955), explained by collinearity with eGFR (Spearman r = 0.339, p = 0.0002) (Table 6).

Discussion

This study comprehensively evaluated the prognostic utility of serum C3, C4, and C3/C4 ratio in 116 biopsy-proven primary IgAN patients. The main findings are: (1) serum C3 and C3/C4 ratio are significantly lower in patients with poor renal prognosis; (2) C3/C4 ratio predicts poor outcomes with AUC = 0.689 and serum C3 with AUC = 0.703; (3) lower C3 correlates with tubular atrophy severity (T score) and lower C3/C4 ratio correlates with crescent formation (C score); and (4) ACE-I/ARB use is independently associated with better prognosis in the fully adjusted multivariate model.

The AUC values of 0.703 and 0.689 for serum C3 and C3/C4 ratio, respectively, indicate moderate discriminatory ability and must be interpreted with appropriate caution. These biomarkers should not be used as standalone prognostic tools but rather as adjuncts to established parameters including eGFR, proteinuria, and Oxford MEST-C scoring. External validation in independent, preferably prospective, cohorts is necessary before clinical implementation.

Complement activation is central to IgAN pathogenesis. Following mesangial deposition of Gd-IgA1 immune complexes, complement is activated predominantly through lectin and alternative pathways [2,3]. The absence of significant C4 reduction (p = 0.520) is biologically consistent with C4-independent complement activation as the dominant mechanism in IgAN. The C3/C4 ratio demonstrated a stronger association with crescent formation (C score; r = −0.272, p = 0.003) compared to serum C3 alone (r = −0.151, p = 0.109), consistent with previous histopathological studies [8].

The expanded multivariate Cox model identified ACE-I/ARB use as independently associated with better renal prognosis (HR = 0.136, 95% CI: 0.031–0.605, p = 0.009). C3/C4 ratio showed a trend (HR = 0.989) but lost significance in the fully adjusted model. Collinearity between C3/C4 ratio and eGFR (Spearman r = 0.339, p = 0.0002) explains this attenuation: both parameters reflect downstream consequences of complement-mediated renal injury, consistent with previous IgAN complement biomarker studies [6].

An important limitation is the absence of systematic renal biopsy complement immunofluorescence data. Tissue C3, C4, and C1q deposition patterns provide mechanistic insight into complement pathway dominance and may predict disease severity independently of serum levels [7]. Previous studies have demonstrated that glomerular C3 deposition correlates with more severe Oxford scores. Additionally, composite biomarker scores such as the HALP score have been evaluated as prognostic tools in IgAN [9], suggesting that multi-parameter approaches may outperform single biomarkers. The integration of serum complement profiling with tissue staining and Gd-IgA1 quantification should be a priority for future prospective studies. Blood pressure was available only as a binary hypertension variable; the 15 excluded patients did not differ from included patients (all p > 0.35).

Conclusion

Serum C3 levels (AUC = 0.703) and C3/C4 ratio (AUC = 0.689) at the time of renal biopsy are significantly associated with renal prognosis and histopathological severity in IgAN. Bootstrap validation confirmed the robustness of the identified cutoff values. While the AUC values indicate moderate discriminatory ability, these parameters may serve as adjunctive – rather than standalone—prognostic markers alongside eGFR, proteinuria, and Oxford MEST-C scoring. ACE-I/ARB use was identified as an independent predictor of better prognosis. In the era of complement-targeted therapies such as iptacopan, serum complement profiling may additionally help identify patients most likely to benefit.
