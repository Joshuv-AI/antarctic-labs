 INTEGRATING CARBON DIOXIDE REMOVAL

  To meet California’s legislatively mandated 2045 carbon neutrality target (AB 1279, Muratsuchi), analyses from the
  California Air Resources Board (CARB) and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) have shown that carbon
  dioxide removal—activities that permanently remove carbon dioxide (CO2) from the atmosphere—will be necessary to
  remove greenhouse gas emissions that are otherwise too costly or difficult to reduce. This policy brief explores the policy,
  legal, and technical considerations for integrating carbon dioxide removal into the Cap-and-Trade Program.

 but parity between carbon dioxide removal costs and allowances is unlikely before the 2030s. For this reason, introducing
 carbon dioxide removal credits into the Program may not make sense until the 2030s; in the meantime, parallel public
 procurement could bridge the cost gap. This will ensure that large-scale removals are possible in the future when needed.
 Until the carbon dioxide removal credits are ready for integration into the Program, proposals such as SB 308 (2023) and
 SB 643 (2025) present policy options for how state agencies could act as buyers of carbon dioxide removal credits, funded
 through the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund (GGRF). As well as stimulating demand, public funds can also go toward
 investing in carbon dioxide removal deployment via supply-side measures, such as research and development investment,
 infrastructure, and targeted tax incentives.

 Lessons from other jurisdictions
 A number of other countries are exploring how to integrate carbon dioxide removal into their own carbon markets,
 presenting examples for the design of California’s policy:

 European Union: Adopted a Carbon Removal Certification Framework (2024) to prepare for integrating high-
 quality carbon dioxide removal credits into its Emissions Trading System.

 United Kingdom: Operate their own Emission Trading System and is consulting on how to admit carbon
 dioxide removal credits.

 Sweden: Use competitive reverse auctions to contract carbon dioxide removal from bioenergy with carbon
 capture and storage, such as the Stockholm Exergi facility.

 Canada: The Clean Fuel Regulations award credits for carbon dioxide removal if it directly lowers the life-cycle
 emissions of a fuel, similar to the LCFS.

 Japan: Launched a national Carbon Credit Market that trades both offsets and engineered carbon dioxide
 removal credits.

 Australia: Expanding their Carbon Farming initiative to include permanent carbon dioxide removals, including
 mineralization and geologic carbon storage..
