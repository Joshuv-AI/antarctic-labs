Abstract
. For the family of Lozi maps L a , b L_{a,b} , we consider parameter pairs for which the f ixed point X X has no homoclinic points and the period-two orbit { P , P ′ } \{P,P^{\prime}\} is attracting. For such parameters, let ℓ \ell be the set of accumulation points of the unstable manifold W X u W_{X}^{u} that do not lie on W X u W_{X}^{u} . We construct a polygon 𝒟 \mathcal{D} whose forward images under L a , b L_{a,b} form nested sequences of sets that eventually become trapping. We show that this geometric construction gives a characterization of ℓ \ell as the intersection of these iterates. Using this structure, we prove that the non-wandering set for L a , b 2 L_{a,b}^{2} is contained in the union of ℓ \ell and the set of f ixed points of L a , b L_{a,b} . As a consequence, the Lozi map, restricted to the complement of ℓ \ell in the plane, has zero topological entropy. This result extends a recent one of Misiurewicz and Štimac to a broader set of parameters.

1.
Introduction

The Lozi map is a piecewise af
f
ine counterpart to the Hénon map: it is a planar homeomorphism given by

L_{a,b}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad L_{a,b}(x,y)=(1+y-a|x|,bx),

and it depends on two parameters
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\neq 0
. Almost half a century after R. Lozi proposed this map in
[
10
]
, a complete understanding of its dynamical properties still requires substantial research. One such aspect is topological entropy
h_{top}
, a core dynamical invariant that measures the exponential growth of orbits for a given system.

A general formula for the topological entropy of Lozi maps in terms of the parameters is not known, but there are some partial results on its discontinuity and jumps (see
[
14
]
), as well as its monotonicity along specific directions in the parameter space (see
[
5
,
6
,
13
]
). Although the parameter region where
h_{top}(L_{a,b})
is maximal has been completely determined in
[
4
]
, the zero entropy locus of the Lozi family is still not fully understood.

In
[
13
]
, Yildiz reviewed a result of Ishii and Sands which states that
h_{top}(L_{a,b})=0
when
-1\leqslant b<0
and
a\leqslant b-1
, as well as when
0<b\leqslant 1
and
a\leqslant 1-b
. In addition, Yildiz also shows that entropy is zero for parameters in a small neighborhood of the point
(a,b)=(1,0.5)
.

In a recent paper
[
12
]
, Misiurewicz and Štimac extended this last result to a larger parameter set in which there are no homoclinic points for the fixed point
X
in the first quadrant, and the period-two orbit
\{P,P^{\prime}\}
is attracting. We denote that set
\mathfrak{R}
and formally introduce it at the beginning of Section
3
. Within that set, Misiurewicz and Štimac observed a subset
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, in which they showed that the branches of the unstable manifold of
X
,
W_{X}^{u}
, are attracted to the set
\ell=\{P,P^{\prime}\}
because
L_{a,b}
acts globally on these branches as an af
f
ine map. This allowed them to conclude that
h_{top}(L_{a,b})=0
for all
(a,b)\in\mathcal{R}
.

In this paper, we generalize this result to the set
\mathfrak{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}
, where the aforementioned dynamical behavior is disrupted. In particular, this gives rise to a more complex accumulation set
\ell
, which is also suggested by certain counterexamples that Ishii and Sands pointed out to the author in a private communication; see Remark
3.11
. More precisely, we consider the set
\ell:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}W_{X}^{u}\setminus W_{X}^{u}
of all accumulation points of
W_{X}^{u}
which do not lie on
W_{X}^{u}
. We construct a geometric mechanism that generates
\ell
. First, we define a polygon
\mathcal{D}
and show that
\mathcal{D}
is an eventually trapping region for
L_{a,b}^{2}
(in the sense of Definition
3.4
). We then consider the nested sequences
(L_{a,b}^{2n}(\mathcal{D}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
(L_{a,b}^{2n+1}(\mathcal{D}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. As it turns out, the connected components of
\ell
can be represented as intersections of these two sequences (Lemma
3.7
, Corollary
3.8
). This representation ultimately permits us to localize the non-wandering dynamics for
L_{a,b}^{2}
:

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
(a,b)\in\mathfrak{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}
, the non-wandering set of
L_{a,b}^{2}
is contained in the union of
\ell
and the f
ixed points of
L_{a,b}
, that is,

\Omega(L_{a,b}^{2})\subseteq\ell\cup\{X,Y\}.

Such structure of the non-wandering set gives an immediate consequence for the topological entropy of
L_{a,b}
: we isolate all entropy-producing dynamics inside a geometrically defined limit set
\ell
.

Corollary 1.2
.

For all parameters
(a,b)\in\mathfrak{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}
, we have
h_{top}(L_{a,b}\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\ell})=0
.

As already stated, there are examples of parameters
(a,b)\in\mathfrak{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}
indicating that the Lozi map can have positive entropy in that parameter region. Therefore,
\ell
can in general have a nontrivial structure and contain more points than the periodic orbit
\{P,P^{\prime}\}
. This intricate structure of
\ell
depends on the specific dynamical behavior of
L_{a,b}
and the unstable manifold
W_{X}^{u}
and thus merits its own separate study. However, in this paper, we construct a geometric mechanism that generates
\ell
and localizes the dynamical complexity of
L_{a,b}
to this set. Moreover, notice that this construction of an eventually trapping region suggests that
\ell
behaves like an attractor. Still, we cannot draw an analogy with the strange attractors from the Misiurewicz parameter set introduced in
[
11
]
: in our case,
\ell
is the
\omega
-limit set of
W_{X}^{u}
but does not contain it since
W_{X}^{u}
is locally an arc for our parameters (Corollary
3.6
). Hence, this new, invariant set
\ell
motivates a potential new study of another class of attractors that could arise in the Lozi family.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
, we give an overview of relevant notions and results from topological dynamics and the dynamics of Lozi maps, together with the notation. Section
3
consists of two parts. In Subsection
3.1
, we construct an eventually trapping region
\mathcal{D}
and use it to characterize the accumulation set
\ell
. We use this characterization to prove our main theorem and its consequence in Subsection
3.2
.

2.1.
Topological dynamics

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a topological space and
f\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
a continuous function. For

, let
f^{n}=f\circ f\circ\ldots\circ f
(
n

is the identity map. In addition, if
f
is invertible, let
f^{-n}=f^{-1}\circ f^{-1}\circ\ldots f^{-1}
(
n
times). A set
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is said to be
f
-
invariant
if
f(\mathcal{Y})\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
.

For a point
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the
forward orbit
of
x
is the set
\mathcal{O}^{+}(x,f)=\{f^{n}(x)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
. If
f
is invertible, the
backward orbit
of
x
is
\mathcal{O}^{-}(x,f)=\{f^{-n}(x)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
, while the
full orbit
of
x
is
\mathcal{O}(x,f)=\mathcal{O}^{+}(x,f)\cup\mathcal{O}^{-}(x,f)
.

We say that a point
x\in\mathcal{X}
is a
non-wandering point
if for every neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
x
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{n}(\mathcal{U})\cap\mathcal{U}\neq\emptyset
. The set of all non-wandering points will be denoted by
\Omega(f)
. If
f
is a homeomorphism, we know that
\Omega(f)
is both
f
- and
f^{-1}
-invariant. A point that does not lie in
\Omega(f)
is called a
wandering point
for
f
.

Furthermore, for a subset
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{X}
, the
\omega
-
limit set
of
\mathcal{A}
is def

\begin{split}\omega(\mathcal{A},f)&=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}\bigcup_{k\geqslant n}\{f^{k}(a)\colon a\in\mathcal{A}\}\\
&=\{x\in\mathcal{X}\colon\exists\text{ a sequence }n_{k}\nearrow\infty\text{ and a sequence }(a_{k})_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\text{ such that }f^{n_{k}}(a_{k})\xrightarrow{k\rightarrow\infty}x\},\end{split}

where
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}\mathcal{Y}
is the topological closure of a set
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
. The set
\omega(\mathcal{A},f)
is closed,
f
-invariant and
f^{-1}
-invariant if
f
is a homeomorphism.

Let
\mathcal{X}
now be a compact metric space with a metric
d
. Moreover, let
n
be a positive integer and
\varepsilon>0
. A f

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is called an
(n,\varepsilon)
-
separated set
if for any two distinct points
x,y\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
such that
d(f^{i}(x),f^{i}(y))>\varepsilon
. Let
s(n,\varepsilon)
denote the maximal cardinality of an
(n,\varepsilon)
-separated set. The
topological entropy

h_{top}(f)
of
f
is def
ined as

h_{top}(f)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(s(n,\varepsilon)).

Here,
s(n,\varepsilon)
can be interpreted as the number of orbit segments of length
n
which one can detect up to precision
\varepsilon
. Therefore,
h_{top}(f)
estimates the average exponential growth of the number of detectable orbit segments. It is also well-known that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
h_{top}(f^{n})=nh_{top}(f)
(see, e.g.,
[
1
, Proposition 2.5.5]
). In addition, by Equation (3.3.1) in
[
7
, p. 130]
, we have that

h_{top}(f)=h_{top}(f\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{\Omega(f)}).

Remark 2.1
.

In this paper, we are interested in the topological entropy of the Lozi map
L_{a,b}
. This map is def
ined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which is not a compact set. Therefore, as in
[
14
]
, we take the one-point compactif

and extend

continuously to that set.

Finally, we state a classic result for homeomorphisms of the plane as it was formulated (and proved) in
[
3
]
. For a homeomorphism
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a
domain of translation
for
f
is an open, connected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose boundary is
p\cup f(p)
, where
p
is a properly embedded line in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
p

and

Theorem 2.2

(Brouwer Plane Translation Theorem, BPTT)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a f
ixed-point-free, orientation-preserving homeomorphism of the plane. Then every point of the plane is contained in a domain of translation for
f
.

In this paper, we will use one consequence of BPTT, which is also a known fact. However, for completeness, we will state and prove it.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a f
ixed-point-free, orientation-preserving homeomorphism of the plane, then every point in the plane is a wandering point for
f
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a point in the plane. By BPTT, there is a domain of translation
\mathcal{U}
for
f
which contains
x_{0}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is an open set, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the open ball centered at
x_{0}
of radius
\varepsilon
is contained in
\mathcal{U}
, that is,
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\subset\mathcal{U}
.
Then
f(B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))\subseteq f(\mathcal{U})
and since by BPTT we have
\mathcal{U}\cap f(\mathcal{U})=\emptyset
, it follows that
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap f(B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))=\emptyset
. Now, by
[
3
, Corollary 1.3]
we have that
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap f^{n}(B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))=\emptyset
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Therefore,
x_{0}
has a neighborhood which is disjoint from all of its forward images, so
x_{0}
is a wandering point for
f
. Since
x_{0}
was arbitrary, the claim follows.
∎

We consider parameter pairs
(a,b)
, such that

(recall that the period-two orbit

Remark 3.11
.

For parameters
(a,b)\in\mathcal{R}
, we know from
[
12
]
that the accumulation set
\ell
is
\ell=\{P,P^{\prime}\}
, and that
h_{top}(L_{a,b}\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\ell})=h_{top}(L_{a,b})=0
. Therefore, we see that Corollary
1.2
also holds for parameter values in
\mathcal{R}
, and it generalizes
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
to a larger set of parameters.

Guided by this, one might think that
\ell=\{P,P^{\prime}\}
could hold for all parameters
(a,b)\in\mathfrak{R}
, that is, that the topological entropy of
L_{a,b}
is zero on that whole parameter set. However, in a private communication, Y. Ishii and D. Sands have pointed out to the author that for
(a,b)=(1.6,0.61)
, the Lozi map
L_{a,b}
has saddle period-six points whose stable and unstable manifolds intersect transversely. In addition, they also remark that other counterexamples exist, such as parameter pairs in a small neighborhood of
(a,b)=(1.5,0.5)
. These f
indings indicate positive topological entropy of
L_{a,b}
(see, e.g.,
[
2
]
), and moreover, they imply that the accumulation set
\ell
can have a nontrivial structure for parameters in
\mathfrak{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}
. Therefore, a complete characterization of the zero entropy locus for the Lozi map remains unresolved, and determining it precisely continues to be an open problem that calls for further investigation.
