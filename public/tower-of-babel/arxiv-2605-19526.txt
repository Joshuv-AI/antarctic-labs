Kleitman’s theorem over vector spaces: parity phenomena in canonical and global stability
Chenhui Lv, Zixiang Xu

Abstract
In 1966, Kleitman determined the maximum size of a family of subsets of [ n ] [n] with bounded symmetric difference. Liao, Liu and Yan recently established a vector-space analogue in the cases n = d + 1 n=d+1 and n > 2 ​ d n>2d , and asked for the sharp bound in the remaining range. We resolve this problem completely by proving the exact vector-space analogue of Kleitman’s theorem for every n ≥ d + 1 n\geq d+1 , and we also determine all extremal configurations. We further develop a stability theory for the vector-space diameter problem. Unlike the Boolean cube, the lattice of subspaces has no translation symmetry, and this makes the stability theory substantially different from its classical counterpart. The geometry of subspace balls leads to two natural notions: canonical stability, which forbids containment only in the canonical extremal configurations, and global stability, which forbids containment in arbitrary balls or adjacent double balls of the corresponding radius. We determine sharp canonical stability in even diameter, sharp canonical and global stability in odd diameter, and prove a nontrivial general upper bound for global stability in even diameter. In particular, these two notions exhibit a sharp parity split: in odd diameter they collapse to the same problem, whereas in even diameter they lead to genuinely different extremal behavior.

Theorem 1.1

With this notation, the natural vector-space analogue of Theorem

Having established the full vector-space analogue of Kleitman’s theorem, it is natural to investigate the corresponding stability theory. In the set-theoretic setting, this direction was initiated by Frankl

[

pushed this further by establishing a second-level stability theorem, thereby refining the picture of near-extremal families in the Boolean setting. We also note that analogous higher-order stability phenomena have been studied for other fundamental extremal results, most notably the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem

For the stability questions considered in this paper, the relevant range is when the ambient dimension is larger than the diameter parameter by a definite margin. In this regime, the extremal structures given by Theorem
1.2
simplify substantially. Indeed, once
n\geq d+2
, the boundary phenomena occurring at
n=d+1
disappear, and the extremal families are described by a small collection of canonical geometric configurations.

When
d=2t
is even, the extremal families are precisely the lower and upper radius-
t
balls

\mathcal{L}_{t}:=\mathcal{B}(\bm{0},t)=\bigcup_{k=0}^{t}\mathcal{V}(k),\qquad\mathcal{U}_{t}:=\mathcal{B}(V,t)=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\perp}=\bigcup_{k=n-t}^{n}\mathcal{V}(k).

When
d=2t+1
is odd, the extremal families are precisely the canonical double balls

\mathcal{D}_{t}(X):=\mathcal{B}(\bm{0},X,t)=\bigcup_{k=0}^{t}\mathcal{V}(k)\cup\{A\in\mathcal{V}(t+1):X\leq A\},\qquad X\in\mathcal{V}(1),

together with their orthogonal images

\mathcal{D}_{t}(X)^{\perp}=\mathcal{B}(V,X^{\perp},t).

These canonical extremal families suggest the most direct analogue of the usual stability question: one asks for the largest families of bounded diameter that are not contained in any of the extremal configurations appearing in Theorem
1.2
. This leads to what we call
Type A
stability, and should be viewed as the primary vector-space analogue of the classical stability problem.

The vector-space setting also gives rise to a stronger stability question. In the Boolean cube, all balls of a fixed radius are equivalent under translations, so excluding the extremal balls is essentially the same as excluding all balls of that radius. This is no longer true for subspaces: the geometry of
\mathcal{B}(X,t)
depends on
\dim X
. Thus, besides excluding only the canonical extremal configurations from Theorem
1.2
, it is natural to exclude containment in every radius-
t
ball, or in every union of two adjacent radius-
t
balls. This is our
Type B
stability problem.

To state these notions uniformly, let
\mathfrak{E}
be a collection of subfamilies of
\mathcal{V}
. We say that a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
is
(\mathfrak{E},d)
-admissible
if

\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F})\leq d
and

\mathcal{F}\not\subseteq\mathcal{H}
for every

\mathcal{H}\in\mathfrak{E}.

In other words,
\mathcal{F}
has bounded diameter but is not contained in any forbidden configuration from the class
\mathfrak{E}
. With this language, the stability problems studied in this paper correspond to the following natural choices of
\mathfrak{E}
.

For even diameter
d=2t
, we define

\mathfrak{E}_{A}^{\mathrm{2t}}:=\{\mathcal{L}_{t},\mathcal{U}_{t}\},\qquad\mathfrak{E}_{B}^{\mathrm{2t}}:=\{\mathcal{B}(X,t):X\in\mathcal{V}\}.

Thus Type A excludes only the two canonical extremal balls from Theorem
1.2
, whereas Type B excludes containment in an arbitrary radius-
t
ball.

For odd diameter
d=2t+1
, we define

\mathfrak{E}_{A}^{\mathrm{2t+1}}:=\{\mathcal{D}_{t}(X),\mathcal{D}_{t}(X)^{\perp}:X\in\mathcal{V}(1)\},\qquad\mathfrak{E}_{B}^{\mathrm{2t+1}}:=\{\mathcal{B}(X,Y,t):X,Y\in\mathcal{V},\ \Delta(X,Y)=1\}.

Thus Type A excludes only the canonical double balls appearing in Theorem
1.2
, whereas Type B excludes containment in an arbitrary union of two adjacent radius-
t
balls.

We first determine the sharp Type A stability theorem in the even case.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix a prime power
q
and an even integer

.
Assume that

.
If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
is

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}_{q}+\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{t-1}_{q}+\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{t}_{q}.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if there exists a
1
-dimensional subspace

X\in\mathcal{V}(1)
such that

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{B}(X,t)

or

\mathcal{F}^{\perp}=\mathcal{B}(X,t).

To describe the odd extremal families, fix
X\in\mathcal{V}(1)
and
Y\in\mathcal{V}(k)
with

, and define

\mathcal{HM}(n,k,X,Y):=\{A\in\mathcal{V}(k):X\leq A,\ \dim(A\cap Y)\geq 1\}\cup\{A\in\mathcal{V}(k):A\leq X+Y\}.

When

\mathcal{HM}^{*}(n,3,Y):=\{A\in\mathcal{V}(3):\dim(A\cap Y)\geq 2\}.

The odd case exhibits an unexpected rigidity phenomenon: although Type B is a priori stronger than Type A, the two notions in fact coincide. Accordingly, the sharp odd stability theorem takes the same form for both notions.

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix a prime power
q
and an odd integer

Assume that

Let

The remaining case is Type B in even diameter, which appears to be substantially more delicate. Although we do not determine the exact extremal families in this case, we still obtain a nontrivial general upper bound that is significantly smaller than the Type A extremal value in Theorem

Theorem 1.5

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2t+1)q^{2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}}\cdot\frac{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{t-1}_{q}+\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{t}_{q}}{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}}\geq\frac{1}{(2t+1)q^{2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}}\cdot\frac{(q^{n-t+1}-1)(q^{n-t}-1)}{(q^{n}-1)(q^{t}-1)}\geq c_{q,t}\cdot q^{n},

One of the main phenomena uncovered in this paper is that this hierarchy exhibits a sharp parity split: in the even case Type A and Type B are genuinely different, whereas in the odd case they coincide.

Useful tools

We begin with the metric structure on

\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{a}_{q}\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{b}_{q}.
Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
If
\mathcal{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{V}(a)
and
\mathcal{B}\subsetneq\mathcal{V}(b)
are cross-
t
-intersecting, then

|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{b}_{q}-\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}q^{(a-i)(b-i)}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{a}{i}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-a}{b-i}_{q}+1.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

\mathcal{A}=\{A\}
and

\mathcal{B}=\{\,B\in\mathcal{V}(b):\dim(A\cap B)\geq t\,\}

for some
A\in\mathcal{V}(a)
;

(2)

\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{a}_{q}=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{b}_{q}
,

\mathcal{A}=\{A\in\mathcal{V}(a):\dim(A\cap B)\geq t\}

and
\mathcal{B}=\{B\}

for some
B\in\mathcal{V}(b)
.

Applying Theorem
2.8
with
a=k
,
b=n-k
, and
t=1
, we obtain a sharp estimate for the sum of two complementary layers.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
n\geq d+1
and
1\leq k<n-k
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
with
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F})\leq d
.
If
\mathcal{F}(k)\neq\varnothing
and
\mathcal{F}(n-k)\neq\varnothing
, then

|\mathcal{F}(k)|+|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}-q^{k(n-k)}+1.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

\mathcal{F}(k)=\{X\}
and

\mathcal{F}(n-k)=\{\,Y\in\mathcal{V}(n-k):\dim(X\cap Y)\geq 1\,\}

for some
X\in\mathcal{V}(k)
;

(2)

\mathcal{F}(k)=\{\,X\in\mathcal{V}(k):\dim(X\cap Y)\geq 1\,\}

and

\mathcal{F}(n-k)=\{Y\}

for some
Y\in\mathcal{V}(n-k)
.

Proof of Lemma
2.9
.

Since
n\geq d+1
, we have

\left\lceil\frac{n-d}{2}\right\rceil\geq 1.

By Lemma
2.2
, the pair
(\mathcal{F}(k),\mathcal{F}(n-k))
is cross-
1
-intersecting in
V
. If
2\leq k<n-k
, then neither layer can be the whole Grassmann layer: if, for instance,
\mathcal{F}(k)=\mathcal{V}(k)
and
Y\in\mathcal{F}(n-k)
, then one can choose
X\in\mathcal{V}(k)
with
X\cap Y=\bm{0}
, contradicting cross-
1
-intersection; the other case is similar. Hence both layers are proper subfamilies, and the conclusion follows from Theorem
2.8
.

It remains to consider the case
k=1<n-k
.
If
|\mathcal{F}(1)|=1
or
|\mathcal{F}(n-1)|=1
, then the conclusion follows immediately from Lemma
2.1
.
So assume that
|\mathcal{F}(1)|\geq 2
and
|\mathcal{F}(n-1)|\geq 2
.
Since
(\mathcal{F}(1),\mathcal{F}(n-1))
is cross-
1
-intersecting, we have
X\leq Y
for every
X\in\mathcal{F}(1)
and
Y\in\mathcal{F}(n-1)
. Hence

|\mathcal{F}(1)|+|\mathcal{F}(n-1)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-2}{1}_{q}+\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-2}{n-3}_{q}=2\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-2}{1}_{q}<\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{1}_{q}=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{1}_{q}-q^{n-1}.

∎

The previous lemma has the following immediate consequence, which shows that the trivial upper bound

|\mathcal{F}(k)|+|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}

can only be attained in a completely degenerate way.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
n\geq d+1
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
with
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F})\leq d
. For every
0\leq k<n-k
, we have

|\mathcal{F}(k)|+|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if either

|\mathcal{F}(k)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}

and

\mathcal{F}(n-k)=\varnothing,

or

\mathcal{F}(k)=\varnothing

and
|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}.

Proof of Corollary
2.10
.

For
k=0
, the two layers are
\mathcal{V}(0)=\{\bm{0}\}
and
\mathcal{V}(n)=\{V\}
. They cannot both meet
\mathcal{F}
, since
\Delta(\bm{0},V)=n>d
. Hence the stated bound and equality condition are immediate. Now let
1\leq k<n-k
. If both
\mathcal{F}(k)
and
\mathcal{F}(n-k)
are nonempty, Lemma
2.9
gives

|\mathcal{F}(k)|+|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}-q^{k(n-k)}+1<\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}.

Therefore equality in the trivial bound can occur only when one of the two layers is empty, and then the other must be the whole corresponding Grassmann layer.
∎

Iterating Corollary
2.10
over all complementary pairs already forces a strong global structure.
The next lemma records the resulting rigidity in the even-diameter case.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
with
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F})\leq d
, and put
t:=\lfloor d/2\rfloor
.
If
n\geq d+1
and

|\mathcal{F}(k)|+|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}

for
k=0,1,\ldots,t,

then either
\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
satisfies one of the following:

(1)

n=d+1
, and for each
k=0,1,\ldots,t
, either

|\mathcal{F}(k)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}

and
|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|=0,

or vice versa;

(2)

n\geq d+2
, and

|\mathcal{F}(k)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}

and

|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|=0

for
k=0,1,\ldots,t.

Proof of Lemma
2.11
.

Clearly, exactly one of
\mathcal{F}(0)
and
\mathcal{F}(n)
is nonempty.
If
\mathcal{F}(n)\neq\varnothing
, then we pass to
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
.
Thus we may assume that
\mathcal{F}(0)\neq\varnothing
.

By Corollary
2.10
, for each
0\leq k\leq t
, either

|\mathcal{F}(k)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}

and
\mathcal{F}(n-k)=\varnothing,

or

\mathcal{F}(k)=\varnothing
and

|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}.

For part (1), it suffices to observe that for every
X\in\mathcal{V}(k)
and every
Y\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{V}(n-k)
, we have
\Delta(X,Y)\leq d
.
Indeed, if
\dim Y\leq n-k-1
, then

\Delta(X,Y)\leq\dim X+\dim Y\leq k+(n-k-1)=d.

If
\dim Y\geq n-k+1
, then

\dim(X\cap Y)=\dim X+\dim Y-\dim(X+Y)\geq k+(n-k+1)-n\geq 1,

and hence

\Delta(X,Y)\leq\dim(X+Y)-\dim(X\cap Y)\leq n-1=d.

For part (2), we argue by induction on
t
.
The case
t=0
is immediate.
Assume the statement holds for
t-1
. Then

|\mathcal{F}(t-1)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}

and

|\mathcal{F}(n-t+1)|=0.

If
\mathcal{F}(n-t)\neq\varnothing
, choose
Y\in\mathcal{F}(n-t)
. Since

|\mathcal{F}(t-1)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}=|\mathcal{V}(t-1)|,

we may choose
X\in\mathcal{F}(t-1)
with
X\cap Y=\bm{0}
. Then

\Delta(X,Y)=\dim X+\dim Y=n-1\geq d+1,

since
n\geq d+2
, a contradiction. Therefore
\mathcal{F}(n-t)=\varnothing
, and so

|\mathcal{F}(t)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}.

∎

In the odd-diameter case there is one additional middle layer, and the previous rigidity statement must be supplemented by a structural description of that layer.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
satisfy

.
If

,

, and

|\mathcal{F}(t+1)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{t}_{q},

\mathcal{F}(n-t-1)

, and

\mathcal{F}(t+1)=\varnothing

\mathcal{F}
or

, and for each

, and

|\mathcal{F}(t+1)|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{t}_{q},

2.12
.

Thus, after possibly replacing
\mathcal{F}
by
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
, we may assume that
\mathcal{F}(0)\neq\varnothing
.

If

is

-intersecting in

.

Assume now that

. Then Lemma

2.3
Orthogonal complements and normalization of the support

The final tool in this section is the orthogonal-complement map.
Its role is not to provide a direct counting bound, but rather to allow us to move freely between lower layers and upper layers while preserving all distance information.
This gives a convenient normalization of the support of
\mathcal{F}
.

Recall that if
(X,\delta)
and
(Y,\rho)
are metric spaces, then a bijection
f:X\to Y
is called an
isometry
if

\rho(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))=\delta(x_{1},x_{2})

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X.

Lemma 2.13

(
[
22
]
)
.

The bijection
f(U):=U^{\perp}
is an isometry on
(\mathcal{V},\Delta)
.

Given
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
, recall that

\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F}):=\max_{A,B\in\mathcal{F}}\Delta(A,B),

and define

D(\mathcal{F}):=\max_{A,B\in\mathcal{F}}|\dim A-\dim B|.

Since

|\dim A-\dim B|\leq\Delta(A,B)

for all
A,B\in\mathcal{F},

we always have

D(\mathcal{F})\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F}).

Define

m_{\mathcal{F}}:=\min\{\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})\cup\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F}^{\perp})\}.

The next elementary lemma shows that, after possibly passing to
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
, the support interval of
\mathcal{F}
may always be assumed to start from a value at most
\frac{n}{2}
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Denote
D(\mathcal{F})=D
, then

m_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\frac{n-D}{2}.

Proof of Lemma
2.14
.

If
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq[x,x+D]
, then

\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F}^{\perp})\subseteq[n-D-x,n-x].

Hence

m_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\frac{x+(n-D-x)}{2}=\frac{n-D}{2}.

∎

Combining Lemmas
2.13
and
2.14
, we may therefore assume, whenever convenient, that

m_{\mathcal{F}}=\min\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})\leq\frac{n-D}{2}.

This normalization will be used repeatedly in the sequel.

3
Full version of Kleitman’s theorem in vector spaces: Proof of Theorem
1.2

The cases
d=2,3
require only minor modifications of the layerwise argument below. They can also be verified by the small-diameter analysis in
[
22
]
, with the same equality characterization. Hence, in the remainder of the proof we assume that
d\geq 4
and put

t:=\lfloor d/2\rfloor\geq 2.

Let

D:=D(\mathcal{F})\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F})\leq d.

Set
m:=\min\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
.
By Lemmas
2.13
and
2.14
, after possibly replacing
\mathcal{F}
by
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
, we may assume that

\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq[m,m+D]
and

m\leq\frac{n-D}{2}.

Set

M:=\max\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F}).

Then
M=m+D
, and hence

m+M=2m+D\leq n.

(1)

We shall estimate
\mathcal{F}
layer by layer.
By Lemma
2.2
, for every
k\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
the layer
\mathcal{F}(k)
is
(k-t)
-intersecting in
V
.
Thus, whenever
k\geq t
and
n\geq k+t
, Theorem
2.3
gives

|\mathcal{F}(k)|\leq\max\left\{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-k+t}{t}_{q},\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k+t}{t}_{q}\right\}.

(2)

We now divide the proof according to the position of the support of
\mathcal{F}
.

Case 1:
m\geq t+1
.

In this case, (
1
) gives

n\geq m+M\geq M+t+1.

In particular,
n\geq k+t+1
for every
k\leq M
, so (
2
) applies to every layer of
\mathcal{F}
.

We first estimate the part of the support at or below
n/2
.
For
k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, (
2
) gives

|\mathcal{F}(k)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-k+t}{t}_{q}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}^{-1}\sum_{m\leq k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor}|\mathcal{F}(k)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=t+1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-k+t}{t}_{q}}{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=t+1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{(q^{n-k+t}-1)\cdots(q^{n-k+1}-1)}{(q^{n}-1)\cdots(q^{n-t+1}-1)}

\displaystyle<\sum_{k=t+1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}q^{-t(k-t)}<\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}q^{-tr}=\frac{1}{q^{t}-1}.

(3)

Similarly, for
k\geq\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1
, (
2
) gives

|\mathcal{F}(k)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k+t}{t}_{q}.

Using
k\leq M\leq n-t-1
, we get

\displaystyle\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}^{-1}\sum_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor<k\leq M}|\mathcal{F}(k)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor<k\leq M}\frac{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k+t}{t}_{q}}{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor<k\leq M}\frac{(q^{k+t}-1)\cdots(q^{k+1}-1)}{(q^{n}-1)\cdots(q^{n-t+1}-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor<k\leq M}q^{-t(n-k-t)}<\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}q^{-tr}=\frac{1}{q^{t}-1}.

(4)

Combining (
3
) and (

), we obtain

|\mathcal{F}|<\frac{2}{q^{t}-1}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}\leq\frac{2}{3}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}.

This is strictly smaller than

\sum_{i=0}^{t}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}_{q},

and hence also strictly smaller than the odd-diameter extremal value.
Thus, the equality case cannot occur in Case 1.

Henceforth we may assume
m\leq t.

Let

r:=\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil.

Thus
r=t
if

, and

if

Case 2: There is a middle layer.

Assume that

\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})\cap[r+1,n-t-1]\neq\varnothing.

Choose

We shall show that this forces a strict loss from the extremal bound, except for a boundary odd case which is still handled by Lemma
2.12
.

First decompose

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\bigl(|\mathcal{F}(k)|+|\mathcal{F}(n-k)|\bigr)+\bigl(|\mathcal{F}(t)|+|\mathcal{F}(n-t)|\bigr)+\sum_{k=t+1}^{n-t-1}|\mathcal{F}(k)|.

2.10
,

We now estimate the remaining terms.

First estimate: the pair of layers
\mathcal{F}(t)
and
\mathcal{F}(n-t)
.

Suppose first that
\mathcal{F}(n-t)=\varnothing
.
Fix

For every

Since

, every
X\in\mathcal{F}(t)

,

|\mathcal{F}(t)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}-q^{\xi t}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-\xi}{t}_{q}.

\displaystyle\frac{q^{\xi t}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-\xi}{t}_{q}}{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}}

The dual argument gives the corresponding estimate when

\mathcal{F}(t)
and

\frac{q^{t(n-t)}}{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t}_{q}}=\frac{(q^{n}-q^{n-t})(q^{n-1}-q^{n-t})\cdots(q^{n-t+1}-q^{n-t})}{(q^{n}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)\cdots(q^{n-t+1}-1)}\geq\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-q^{-i}).

. Also notice that as

0\leq i\leq 2t
and assume that
M-i\geq t+1
.
Let
W\in\mathcal{V}(M-t-i)
be such that
W\leq A
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}(M-i)
.
If

|\mathcal{F}(M-i)|>\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q},

then for every
0\leq j\leq 2t
and every
B\in\mathcal{F}(M-j)
, we have

\dim(B\cap W)\geq\begin{cases}\left\lceil M-t-\dfrac{i+j}{2}\right\rceil,&i+1\leq j\leq 2t,\\[5.69054pt]
M-t-i,&0\leq j\leq i.\end{cases}

Proof of the claim.

Set
w:=M-t-i
and
s_{ij}:=\left\lceil M-t-\frac{i+j}{2}\right\rceil
.
Then by Lemma
2.2
, the pair
(\mathcal{F}(M-i),\mathcal{F}(M-j))
is cross-
s_{ij}
-intersecting.

First suppose
i+1\leq j\leq 2t
.
Assume, for a contradiction, that there exists
B\in\mathcal{F}(M-j)
with
\dim(B\cap W)=s_{ij}-\ell
for some
\ell\geq 1
.
The cross-intersection condition also gives

\ell\leq t.

For every
A\in\mathcal{F}(M-i)
,
since
W\leq A
and
\dim(A\cap B)\geq s_{ij}
, we have

\displaystyle\dim(A\cap(B+W))

\displaystyle\geq\dim((A\cap B)+(A\cap W))

\displaystyle\geq\dim(A\cap B)+\dim W-\dim(B\cap W)\geq w+\ell.

Thus every
A\in\mathcal{F}(M-i)
contains
W
and meets
B+W
in dimension at least
w+\ell
.
Therefore

|\mathcal{F}(M-i)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{\dim(B+W)-w}{\ell}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-w-\ell}{t-\ell}_{q}.

Since

\dim(B+W)-w=t+\ell-j+\left\lfloor\frac{i+j}{2}\right\rfloor\leq 2t

and
1\leq\ell\leq t
, we get

|\mathcal{F}(M-i)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{2t}{\ell}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-\ell}_{q}\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{2t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}<\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q},

a contradiction.

Now suppose
0\leq j\leq i
.
Assume that there exists
B\in\mathcal{F}(M-j)
with
\dim(B\cap W)=w-\ell
for some
\ell\geq 1
.
The cross-intersection condition also gives

\ell\leq t-i+\left\lfloor\frac{i+j}{2}\right\rfloor.

For every
A\in\mathcal{F}(M-i)
, we again have

\displaystyle\dim(A\cap(B+W))

\displaystyle\geq\dim((A\cap B)+(A\cap W))

\displaystyle\geq\dim(A\cap B)+\dim W-\dim(B\cap W)\geq s_{ij}+\ell.

Hence

|\mathcal{F}(M-i)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{\dim(B+W)-w}{s_{ij}+\ell-w}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-s_{ij}-\ell}{M-i-s_{ij}-\ell}_{q}.

Here

\dim(B+W)-w=t+i-j+\ell,

and

s_{ij}+\ell-w=\ell+i-\left\lfloor\frac{i+j}{2}\right\rfloor.

Consequently the first Gaussian coefficient is bounded by
\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}
, while the second is bounded by
\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}
.
Thus

|\mathcal{F}(M-i)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q},

again a contradiction. This finishes the proof.
∎

Claim 6.3
.

Let
1\leq i\leq 2t
and assume that
M-i\geq t+1
.
Let
W\in\mathcal{V}(M-t-i)
be such that
W\leq A
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}(M-i)
.
Then

|\mathcal{F}(M-i)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}.

Proof of the claim.

Suppose not.
Choose
B\in\mathcal{F}(M)
.
Applying Claim
6.2
with
j=0
, we obtain
W\leq B
.
By Lemma
2.2
, the pair
(\mathcal{F}(M-i),\mathcal{F}(M))
is cross-
\left\lceil M-t-\frac{i}{2}\right\rceil
-intersecting. Hence every
A\in\mathcal{F}(M-i)
contains
W
and meets
B
in dimension at least
\left\lceil M-t-\frac{i}{2}\right\rceil
.
Counting such subspaces gives

|\mathcal{F}(M-i)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{t+i}{\left\lceil i/2\right\rceil}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n-\left\lceil M-t-i/2\right\rceil}{t-\left\lceil i/2\right\rceil}_{q}\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q},

a contradiction.
∎

By Lemma
2.2
, the top layer
\mathcal{F}(M)
is
(M-t)
-intersecting in
V
. We then focus on this layer.

We first consider the case where
\mathcal{F}(M)
is a trivial
(M-t)
-intersecting family and

|\mathcal{F}(M)|>\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}.

Then there exists
W\in\mathcal{V}(M-t)
such that
W\leq A
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}(M)
. Applying Claim
6.2
with
i=0
, we get, for every
0\leq j\leq 2t
and every
B\in\mathcal{F}(M-j)
,

\dim(B\cap W)\geq\left\lceil M-t-\frac{j}{2}\right\rceil.

Therefore

\Delta(B,W)=(M-j)+(M-t)-2\dim(B\cap W)\leq t.

Since
M-m\leq 2t
, every nonempty layer of
\mathcal{F}
is of the form
\mathcal{F}(M-j)
for some
0\leq j\leq 2t
.
Thus
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(W,t)
, contradicting
(\mathfrak{E}_{B}^{\mathrm{2t}},2t)
-admissibility.

Hence the above case cannot occur.
We now bound every layer of dimension at least
t
. For the top layer, either
\mathcal{F}(M)
is trivial and

|\mathcal{F}(M)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q},

or
\mathcal{F}(M)
is nontrivial, in which case Lemma
6.1
gives

|\mathcal{F}(M)|\leq q^{4t+2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}.

Thus

|\mathcal{F}(M)|\leq\max\left\{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q},q^{4t+2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}\right\}.

Now let
1\leq i\leq 2t
with
M-i\geq t+1
and
\mathcal{F}(M-i)\neq\varnothing
.
If
\mathcal{F}(M-i)
is a trivial
(M-t-i)
-intersecting family, then Claim
6.3
gives

|\mathcal{F}(M-i)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}.

If it is nontrivial, then Lemma
6.1
gives

|\mathcal{F}(M-i)|\leq q^{4t+2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}.

Finally, if
\mathcal{F}(t)\neq\varnothing
, Lemma
6.1
gives the same bound

|\mathcal{F}(t)|\leq q^{4t+2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}.

Since
\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}>q^{2t^{2}}
and
t\geq 2
, we have

q^{4t+2}<q^{2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}.

Consequently every nonempty layer
\mathcal{F}(k)
with
k\geq t
satisfies

|\mathcal{F}(k)|<q^{2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}.

There are at most
2t+1
nonempty layers of dimension at least
t
, because all nonempty dimensions lie in the interval
[m,M]
and
M-m\leq 2t
.
Therefore

\sum_{k=t}^{M}|\mathcal{F}(k)|<(2t+1)q^{2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}.

For the lower layers we use the trivial estimate
|\mathcal{F}(k)|\leq\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}
for
0\leq k\leq t-1
.
Hence

|\mathcal{F}|=\sum_{k=m}^{t-1}|\mathcal{F}(k)|+\sum_{k=t}^{M}|\mathcal{F}(k)|<\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}_{q}+(2t+1)q^{2}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{3t}{t}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n}{t-1}_{q}.

This finishes the proof.
