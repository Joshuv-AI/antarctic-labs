Singularity for graph-directed conjugate equations indexed by a two-vertex digraph
Kazuki Okamura

Abstract
. We study graph-directed conjugate functional equations on the unit interval indexed by the complete digraph with self-loops on two vertices. We focus on the singularity and regularity of the solutions for compatible systems of weak contractions. First, we show that both solutions are singular in the affine case unless the two systems coincide; second, we obtain a dichotomy between singularity and smoothness for a class of linear fractional systems; and finally, we give a sufficient condition for singularity in a non-linear setting.

We follow the setting of

We adopt the Matkowski–Rus definition of weak contractions

We say that a map

We say that a doubly-indexed family of functions

\displaystyle[0,1]

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle h_{i,j}

\displaystyle\phi
-contraction, and furthermore a compatibility condition holds, specifically,

0=h_{i,0}(0)<h_{i,0}(1)=h_{i,1}(0)<h_{i,1}(1)=1,\ i\in\{0,1\}.

\displaystyle(D^{h}_{i,n})_{n}

\displaystyle n

By the compatibility condition (
2.1

I^{h}_{i}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})=I^{h}_{i}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n},0)\cup I^{h}_{i}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n},1).

For every

We denote the length of an interval

3.
Affine case

In this section,
we consider the case where

f_{0,0}(x)=p_{0}x,f_{0,1}(x)=(1-p_{0})x+p_{0},f_{1,0}(x)=p_{1}x,f_{1,1}(x)=(1-p_{1})x+p_{1},

\displaystyle p_{0},q_{0},p_{1},q_{1}\in(0,1)
.
These maps are strictly increasing contractions on
\displaystyle[0,1]

Theorem 3.1
.

(i) If
\displaystyle p_{0}\neq q_{0}

\displaystyle p_{1}\neq q_{1}

\displaystyle x\in[0,1]\setminus D^{f}_{0}

Since each map is affine,

\frac{|I^{g}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|}{|I^{f}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|}=\frac{q_{0i_{1}}}{p_{0,i_{1}}}\frac{q_{i_{1},i_{2}}}{p_{i_{1},i_{2}}}\cdots\frac{q_{i_{n-1},i_{n}}}{p_{i_{n-1},i_{n}}}.

Hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{q_{i_{n}i_{n+1}}}{p_{i_{n}i_{n+1}}}=1.

(3.1)

Set

\mathscr{R}\coloneqq\left\{\frac{q_{0}}{p_{0}},\frac{1-q_{0}}{1-p_{0}},\frac{q_{1}}{p_{1}},\frac{1-q_{1}}{1-p_{1}}\right\}.

Then for every
\displaystyle n\geq 1

\frac{q_{i_{n}i_{n+1}}}{p_{i_{n}i_{n+1}}}\in\mathscr{R}.

We consider the case where
\displaystyle p_{0}\neq q_{0}
and
\displaystyle p_{1}\neq q_{1}
.
Then no element of
\displaystyle\mathscr{R}
equals
\displaystyle 1
.
Hence (
3.1
) fails.

Therefore, it holds that for every
\displaystyle x\in[0,1]\setminus D^{f}_{0}
where
\displaystyle\varphi_{0}
is differentiable,
the derivative of
\displaystyle\varphi_{0}
at
\displaystyle x
is
\displaystyle 0
.

We consider the case where
\displaystyle p_{0}=q_{0}
and
\displaystyle p_{1}\neq q_{1}
.
Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle(i_{n})_{n}

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle n\geq N

\displaystyle i_{n}=0

If (

Linear fractional case

We say that a square matrix

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}

\displaystyle 0<a+b\leq c+d

\displaystyle\{h_{0},h_{1}\}

In this section,

f_{0,0}(x)=f_{1,0}(x)=\frac{x}{2},\ \ f_{0,1}(x)=f_{1,1}(x)=\frac{x+1}{2},

(4.3)

(i) If
\displaystyle\Phi\left(A_{i,j}^{\top};\alpha_{i}\right)\neq\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle(i_{n})_{n}

\displaystyle n\geq 1

By the definition of

The case where
\displaystyle n=1
is obvious.
Assume that
\displaystyle\min\{s_{n},r_{n}+s_{n}\}>0
.
Then
\displaystyle\displaystyle\min_{x\in[0,1]}r_{n}x+s_{n}=\min\{s_{n},r_{n}+s_{n}\}>0
.
By (
4.2
), it holds that
\displaystyle d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}>0
and
\displaystyle c_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}>0
.
By (
4.3
), we see that

\displaystyle\dfrac{b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}}{d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}}\in[0,1]
and
\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}}{c_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}}\in[0,1]
.
Since

(s_{n+1},r_{n+1}+s_{n+1})

=\left(b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}r_{n}+d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}s_{n},(a_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}})r_{n}+(c_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}})s_{n}\right),

(4.6)

we obtain that

s_{n+1}=d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}\left(r_{n}\frac{b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}}{d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}}+s_{n}\right)>0

and

r_{n+1}+s_{n+1}=(c_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}})\left(r_{n}\frac{a_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}}{c_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}}+s_{n}\right)>0.

Since
\displaystyle b_{i,0}=0<d_{i,0}
,
\displaystyle\Phi\left(A_{i,0}^{\top};x\right)
is well-defined for every
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle i=0,1
.
Since
\displaystyle\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}>-1>-\frac{d_{i,1}}{b_{i,1}}
,
\displaystyle\Phi\left(A_{i,1}^{\top};\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}\right)
is well-defined for
\displaystyle i=0,1
and
\displaystyle n\geq 1
.
Therefore, we obtain that for every
\displaystyle n\geq 1
,

\frac{r_{n+1}}{s_{n+1}}=\Phi\left(A_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}^{\top};\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}\right)

(4.7)

By the definition of
\displaystyle f_{i,j}
in (
4.1
), it holds that
\displaystyle D^{f}_{0}
is the set of dyadic rationals on
\displaystyle[0,1]
and

\frac{|I^{g}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|}{|I^{f}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|}=2^{n}\left(\frac{p_{n}+q_{n}}{r_{n}+s_{n}}-\frac{q_{n}}{s_{n}}\right)=2^{n}\frac{p_{n}s_{n}-q_{n}r_{n}}{s_{n}(r_{n}+s_{n})}.

Since

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|I^{g}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|}{|I^{f}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|}=\varphi_{0}^{\prime}(x)>0
,
we obtain that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|I^{g}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n+1})|/|I^{f}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n+1})|}{|I^{g}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|/|I^{f}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|}=1.

This is equivalent to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\det(A_{i_{n},i_{n+1}})s_{n}(r_{n}+s_{n})}{s_{n+1}(r_{n+1}+s_{n+1})}=\frac{1}{2}.

By (
4.6
), this is equivalent to

\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=\frac{1}{2},

where we let

t_{n}\coloneqq\frac{\det(A_{i_{n},i_{n+1}})(\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}+1)}{(b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}+d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}})((a_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}})\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}+c_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}})}.

If
\displaystyle i_{n+1}=0
, then
\displaystyle b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}=0
and hence

t_{n}=\frac{\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}+1}{\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}+\frac{c_{i_{n},0}+d_{i_{n},0}}{a_{i_{n},0}}}.

(4.8)

If
\displaystyle i_{n+1}=1
, then
\displaystyle a_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+b_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}=c_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}+d_{i_{n},i_{n+1}}>0
and hence

t_{n}=\frac{\frac{d_{i_{n},1}}{b_{i_{n},1}}-1}{\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}+\frac{d_{i_{n},1}}{b_{i_{n},1}}}.

(4.9)

For
\displaystyle y\in[0,1]\setminus D_{0}^{f}
, let

\mathbb{N}_{i,j}(y)\coloneqq\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon(i_{n},i_{n+1})=(i,j)\right\},\ i,j\in\{0,1\},

and

\mathbb{N}_{i}(y)\coloneqq\mathbb{N}_{i,0}(y)\cup\mathbb{N}_{i,1}(y),\ i\in\{0,1\}.

Then
\displaystyle\{\mathbb{N}_{i,j}(y)\}_{i,j}
are disjoint and
\displaystyle\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{N}_{0}(y)\cup\mathbb{N}_{1}(y)
.
Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}
be the set of
\displaystyle y\in[0,1]\setminus D_{0}^{f}
such that
\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{i,j}(y)
is finite for some
\displaystyle(i,j)
.

Assume that
\displaystyle x\in[0,1]\setminus\mathcal{N}
.
Recall (
4.4
).
Then,
by (
4.8
) and (
4.9
),

\lim_{n\to\infty;n\in\mathbb{N}_{i}(x)}\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}=\alpha_{i},\ i\in\{0,1\}.

By this and (
4.7
),
it holds that

\displaystyle\Phi\left(A_{i,j}^{\top};\alpha_{i}\right)=\alpha_{j}
for every
\displaystyle i,j\in\{0,1\}
.
By the assumption,
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{N}
.
By the Borel normal number theorem,
\displaystyle\ell(\mathcal{N})=0
.

Thus we see that
\displaystyle\varphi_{0}
is singular.
In the same manner, we also see that
\displaystyle\varphi_{1}
is singular.

(ii) We first remark that
\displaystyle\Phi(A;x)=\Phi(cA;x)
for every
\displaystyle c\neq 0
and every square matrix
\displaystyle A
.
Since
\displaystyle a_{0,0},a_{1,0},b_{0,1}
and
\displaystyle b_{1,1}
are all positive,
we can assume that
\displaystyle a_{0,0}=a_{1,0}=b_{0,1}=b_{1,1}=1
.

Since
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}=c_{0,0}+d_{0,0}-2
,
the equation
\displaystyle\Phi\left(A_{0,0}^{\top};\alpha_{0}\right)=\alpha_{0}
is equivalent to the equation
\displaystyle(c_{0,0}+d_{0,0}-1)(d_{0,0}-2)=0
.
Since
\displaystyle c_{0,0}+d_{0,0}>a_{0,0}=1
, we obtain that
\displaystyle d_{0,0}=2
.
By this and
\displaystyle b_{0,0}=0
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle A_{0,0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c_{0,0}&2\end{pmatrix}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle g_{0,0}(x)=\dfrac{x}{c_{0,0}x+2}
.

Since
\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=d_{1,1}-2
,
the equation
\displaystyle\Phi\left(A_{1,1}^{\top};\alpha_{1}\right)=\alpha_{1}
is equivalent to the equation
\displaystyle(c_{1,1}-d_{1,1}+2)(d_{1,1}-1)=0
.
Since
\displaystyle d_{1,1}>b_{1,1}=1
, we obtain that
\displaystyle d_{1,1}=c_{1,1}+2
.
By this and
\displaystyle a_{1,1}+1=c_{1,1}+d_{1,1}
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle A_{1,1}=\begin{pmatrix}2c_{1,1}+1&1\\
c_{1,1}&c_{1,1}+2\end{pmatrix}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle g_{1,1}(x)=\dfrac{(2c_{1,1}+1)x+1}{c_{1,1}x+c_{1,1}+2}
.

It also holds that
\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=c_{i,i},\,i=0,1
.

We see that the equation
\displaystyle\Phi\left(A_{0,1}^{\top};\alpha_{0}\right)=\alpha_{1}
is equivalent to the equation
\displaystyle(c_{0,0}+1)c_{0,1}+c_{0,0}(d_{0,1}-1)=2c_{1,1}(c_{0,0}+1)
.
By (
4.3
),
it holds that
\displaystyle d_{0,1}=c_{0,0}+2
.
Hence
\displaystyle c_{0,1}=2c_{1,1}-c_{0,0}
.
Hence
\displaystyle a_{0,1}=c_{0,1}+d_{0,1}-1=2c_{1,1}+1
.
Thus we obtain that
\displaystyle A_{0,1}=\begin{pmatrix}2c_{1,1}+1&1\\
2c_{1,1}-c_{0,0}&c_{0,0}+2\end{pmatrix}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle g_{0,1}(x)=\dfrac{(2c_{1,1}+1)x+1}{(2c_{1,1}-c_{0,0})x+c_{0,0}+2}
.

We see that the equation
\displaystyle\Phi\left(A_{1,0}^{\top};\alpha_{1}\right)=\alpha_{0}
is equivalent to the equation
\displaystyle c_{1,0}-c_{0,0}d_{1,0}=-c_{1,1}
.
By (
4.3
),
it holds that
\displaystyle c_{1,0}+d_{1,0}=c_{1,1}+2
.
By solving the system of linear equations, we obtain that

c_{1,0}=\frac{c_{0,0}c_{1,1}+2c_{0,0}-c_{1,1}}{c_{0,0}+1}\textup{ and }d_{1,0}=\frac{2(c_{1,1}+1)}{c_{0,0}+1}.

By this and
\displaystyle b_{1,0}=0
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle A_{1,0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\frac{c_{0,0}c_{1,1}+2c_{0,0}-c_{1,1}}{c_{0,0}+1}&\frac{2(c_{1,1}+1)}{c_{0,0}+1}\end{pmatrix}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle g_{1,0}(x)=\dfrac{(c_{0,0}+1)x}{(c_{0,0}c_{1,1}+2c_{0,0}-c_{1,1})x+2(c_{1,1}+1)}
.

Now by computations, we see that the functions
\displaystyle\varphi_{0}
and
\displaystyle\varphi_{1}
defined in (
4.5
) satisfy (
2.4
).
∎

5.
Non-linear case

In this section, we consider the case where (
4.1
) holds and each
\displaystyle\{g_{i,0},g_{i,1}\}
is merely assumed to be a compatible system,
\displaystyle i=0,1
.
For
\displaystyle h\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,
let
\displaystyle\|h\|_{\textup{Lip}}\coloneqq\sup\left\{\frac{|h(x)-h(y)|}{|x-y|}\colon x\neq y\right\}
.
The following result is similar to
[
6
, Theorem 7.3]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
\displaystyle\prod_{i,j\in\{0,1\}}\left\|g_{i,j}\right\|_{\textup{Lip}}<\frac{1}{16}
, then both of the solutions
\displaystyle\varphi_{0}
and
\displaystyle\varphi_{1}
of (
2.4
) are singular.

Proof.

We show that
\displaystyle\varphi_{0}
is singular.
Define
\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{i,j}(y)
as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}
be the set of
\displaystyle y\in[0,1]\setminus D^{f}_{0}
such that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\left|\mathbb{N}_{i,j}(y)\cap\{1,\dots,N\}\right|}{N}=\frac{1}{4}

(5.1)

for every
\displaystyle i,j\in\{0,1\}
.

Assume that
\displaystyle x\in[0,1]\setminus D^{f}_{0}
and
\displaystyle\varphi_{0}^{\prime}(x)>0
.
Let
\displaystyle(i_{n})_{n}
be the unique sequence in
\displaystyle\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\displaystyle x\in I^{f}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
for each
\displaystyle n\geq 1
.
We remark that
\displaystyle\|g_{i,j}\|_{\textup{Lip}}\leq 1
.
We see that for every
\displaystyle N\geq 1
,

\left|I^{g}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})\right|\leq\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\left\|g_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}\right\|_{\textup{Lip}}=\prod_{i,j\in\{0,1\}}\left\|g_{i,j}\right\|_{\textup{Lip}}^{N_{i,j}}

where we let
\displaystyle N_{i,j}\coloneqq\left|\mathbb{N}_{i,j}(x)\cap\{1,\dots,N-1\}\right|
.
Assume that
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{S}
.
Then by the assumption and (
5.1
),

\lim_{N\to\infty}2^{N}\left|I^{g}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})\right|=0.

On the other hand,

\lim_{N\to\infty}2^{N}\left|I^{g}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})\right|=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{|I^{g}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})|}{|I^{f}_{0}(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})|}=\varphi_{0}^{\prime}(x)>0.

Hence
\displaystyle x\in[0,1]\setminus\mathcal{S}
.
By the Borel normal number theorem,
\displaystyle\ell(\mathcal{S})=1
.
Thus we see that
\displaystyle\varphi_{0}
is singular.
In the same manner, we can show that
\displaystyle\varphi_{1}
is singular.
∎

The above proof is partly different from the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 7.3]
, since we can apply the monotone differentiation theorem.
