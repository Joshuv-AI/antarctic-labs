Congruences and ramified primes in fields of coefficients of newforms
Nuno Freitas and Filip Gawron

Abstract
. We investigate the splitting behavior of ℓ \ell in the coefficient field of a newform f f of level N N , under the assumption that f f is congruent modulo a prime above ℓ \ell to another newform g g whose level divides N / p 2 N/p^{2} for some prime p | N p|N . In particular, we show that the maximal real subfield of the ℓ \ell -th cyclotomic field, ℚ ⁡ ( ζ ℓ + ζ ℓ − 1 ) \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1}) , is contained in the coefficient field of f f . We conclude by presenting explicit examples that illustrate these results.



1.
Introduction

Let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\varepsilon)
be a newform of weight
k\geq 2
, level
N
and nebentypus
\varepsilon
. It is a standard fact that
f
admits a Fourier expansion

f(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)q^{n}

and a field of coefficients

\mathbb{Q}_{f}:=\mathbb{Q}(\{a_{n}(f)\}_{n\geq 1})

which is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Although modular forms have been extensively studied, there are still many open questions about the field of coefficients
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
. Here we are particularly interested in the following.

Question 1.1
.

What can be said about the splitting behaviour of primes in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
in terms of the level
N
and the nebentypus
\varepsilon
?

It is well known that
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
always contains all the values taken by
\varepsilon
(e.g.
[
11
, Corollary 3.1]
). An example of a result in terms of
N
is due to Brumer (see
[
2
, Theorem 5.5]
) who proved that if

\ell^{\alpha}
divides
N
, where
\ell
is a prime, then
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
contains
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{\beta}}+\zeta_{\ell^{\beta}}^{-1})
for some
\beta=\beta(\alpha)
, an increasing function of
\alpha
.
A result of similar flavor is
[
8
, Proposition 4.7]
, where the authors proved that if
f
has level
Mp^{2}
, with
p\nmid M
, and possesses certain inertial type at
p
(depending on an integer
n
), then
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
contains
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1}).

Let
G_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\text{Gal}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}/\mathbb{Q})
. A major tool for studying modular forms is its associated Galois representations. More precisely, Deligne
[
5
]
showed that, to every newform
f
as above, and any prime
\lambda\mid\ell
in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
, there is a Galois representation

\rho_{f,\lambda}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\text{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f,\lambda})
,
where
\mathcal{O}_{f,\lambda}
is the ring of integers of the completion of
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
at
\lambda
. Furthermore, this representation is unramified outside
\ell N
and has a prime-to-
\ell
Artin conductor equal to the prime-to-
\ell
part of
N
. For any prime
p\neq\ell
, we will refer to the restriction
\rho_{f,\lambda}|_{I_{p}}
, where
I_{p}\subset G_{\mathbb{Q}}
is an inertia subgroup at
p
, as the
inertial type
of
f
at
p
.

Before stating our main result, we need to introduce some notation.
Let
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\text{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}})

be the modulo
\lambda
reduction of
\rho_{f,\lambda}
and
denote by
N^{\prime}
the prime-to-
\ell
part of the Artin conductor of
its semisimplification
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}^{ss}
.
We will prove that, whenever
v_{p}(N)>1
and
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}^{ss}
‘loses’ ramification at a prime
p\neq\ell
, i.e.,
v_{p}(N^{\prime})<v_{p}(N)
, then
\ell
ramifies in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
.
More precisely, we will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\varepsilon)
be a newform of weight
k\geq 2

and field of coefficients
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
. Let
p
be a prime such that
p^{2}\mid N
.
Let
\ell\neq p
be an odd prime,
\lambda
a prime of
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
dividing
\ell
, and

N^{\prime}
the prime-to-
\ell
part of the Artin conductor of
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}^{ss}
.
Assume that

v_{p}(N^{\prime})<v_{p}(N).

(a)

If
v_{p}(N)=2
, then

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f}.

In particular, for any prime
\lambda^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
over
\ell
we have

\frac{\ell-1}{2}\mid e(\lambda^{\prime}|\ell),

where
e(\lambda^{\prime}|\ell)
is the ramification degree of
\lambda^{\prime}
over
\ell
in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
. Moreover, if
v_{p}(N^{\prime})=0
and
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}
is irreducible then

e(\lambda|\ell)\geq\ell-1.

(b)

If
v_{p}(N)>2
, then
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f}
.

This theorem generalizes
[
7
, Lemmas 15 and 16]
as it allows for nontrivial nebentypus, but it also provides stronger conclusions. Indeed,
case (b) gives the containment of the full cyclotomic field in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
and, moreover, when
v_{p}(N^{\prime})=0
, our result establishes the existence of a prime above
\ell
with ramification degree bigger than what can be deduced from the field containment alone.

The ideas in the proof of this theorem are as follows. The statements about the fields contained in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
follow from careful analysis of the inertial type of
f
at
p
and their reductions. Under the assumption
v_{p}(N^{\prime})<v_{p}(N)
, the classification of the possible inertial types is due to Carayol (see Proposition
2.1
), and we use it as the starting point. The argument is similar to the one in
[
7
]
.
The last statement of part
(a)
is the only non-symmetric statement with respect to the primes
\lambda
of
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
above
\ell
. Its proof goes by imitating the trick used in
[
12
, §2, Cor. 2]
, which relies on the fact that when the ramification degree of
\mathbb{Q}_{f}/\mathbb{Q}
is small enough, the kernel of the reduction
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f,\lambda})\to\text{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}})

is torsion-free.

Remark 1.3
.

For simplicity, we stated Theorem
1.2
for classical newforms, but it also holds for Hilbert newforms of arithmetic weight. Indeed, their field of Hecke eigenvalues has finite degree over
\mathbb{Q}
, the associated Galois representations exist, and the analog of Carayol’s lemma holds (see
[
9
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.5]
), so all the necessary tools are there, and in fact, the same proof goes through.

In the last section, we will use a level raising theorem due to Diamond and Taylor
[
14
]
to obtain examples of the application of Theorem
1.2
. In particular, we discuss a systematic method to generate newforms with primes that do not divide the level and are highly ramified in their field of coefficients.

2.
Auxiliary results

Let
f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}
be a newform in
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\varepsilon)
. We keep the notation of the introduction. The representation
\rho_{f,\lambda}

is unramified outside
N\ell
, and for all primes
q\nmid N\ell
, the minimal polynomial of

\rho_{f,\lambda}(\text{Frob}_{q})
is
x^{2}-a_{q}x+q^{k-1}\varepsilon(q)
, where
\text{Frob}_{q}\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a Frobenius element at
q
. The determinant of
\rho_{f,\lambda}
is equal to
\omega_{\ell}^{k-1}\varepsilon
, where
\omega_{\ell}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{*}
is the

\ell
-adic cyclotomic character. Moreover, the set
\{\rho_{f,\lambda}\}
forms a compatible system. This means that, in particular, after choosing embeddings
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\hookrightarrow G_{\mathbb{Q}}
at all primes
p
, we have that, for
p\neq\ell
, the Weil–Deligne representation associated (via Grothendieck Monodromy Theorem) with
\rho_{f,\lambda}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

is independent of
\lambda
and
\ell
; see also
[
3
, Théorème A]
or
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Furthermore, Weil–Deligne representations fall into three types: Principal Series, Special (or Steinberg), and Supercuspidal (see e.g.
[
7
, §1]
), and those with infinite image of inertia are exactly the Special representations.

Recall that
N^{\prime}
is the prime-to-
\ell
part of the Artin conductor of
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}^{ss}
. When
v_{p}(N^{\prime})<v_{p}(N)
, Carayol
characterized all possible Weil–Deligne types of
f
at
p
(see
[
4
, Proposition 2]
) which we list in Proposition
2.1
, noting that there is a case
in
loc. cit
that does not occur under the assumption
p^{2}|N
in Theorem
1.2
.
Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
be a finite extension with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
.
For a representation
\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\text{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})

we denote by
a(\rho)
the conductor exponent of
\rho
.
Let also
\overline{\rho}
be the reduction of
\rho
modulo
the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
and
{\overline{\rho}}^{ss}
its semisimplification.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\text{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
be as above.
Assume
a(\rho)\geq 2
and

a(\overline{\rho}^{ss})<a(\rho).

Then
\rho
satisfies one of the following cases.

(1)

\rho
is a principal series representation given by

\rho=\chi_{1}\oplus\chi_{2}

with
\overline{\chi_{1}}
unramified. In this case,
a(\rho)=a(\chi_{2})+1
and
a(\overline{\rho}^{ss})=a(\overline{\chi_{2}}).

(2)

\rho
is a special representation given by

\rho=\chi\otimes sp(2)

with
a(\chi)=1
and
a(\overline{\chi})=0
. In this case, we have
a(\rho)=2
and
a(\overline{\rho})=0.

(3)

\rho
is a supercuspidal representation given by

\rho=\text{Ind}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}}}^{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\chi,

where
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
is the unique unramified quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and the character
\chi
does not factor through the norm
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(where we view
\chi
as a character of
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
by local class field theory). In this case,
a(\rho)=2
,
a(\chi)=1
and
a(\overline{\chi})=0.

Finally, we will also need the following lemma (see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 6.2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
be a finite extension with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, ramification degree
e
, and uniformizer
\pi
. The subgroup

I_{n}+\pi M_{n\times n}(\mathcal{O})

of
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
is pro-
\ell
group and, moreover, if
e<\ell-1
then it is torsion-free.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Assume the hypothesis of Theorem
1.2
, and keep
all the previous notations. We set

\rho:=\rho_{f,\lambda}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\rho}:=\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}.

Let
I_{p}\subset G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
be the inertia subgroup.
Since the proof of Theorem
1.2
proceeds by analysing various cases, to simplify the presentation, we will split it into lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that we are in the case (2) of Proposition
2.1
. Then

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f}
.

Proof.

The fact that
\chi|_{I_{p}}
is not trivial but has trivial reduction implies that the values of
\chi
on
I_{p}
contain a non-trivial
\ell
-power root of unity
\zeta_{\ell^{k}}
. Since
\ell>2
the same is true for
\text{det}(\rho|_{I_{p}})=\varepsilon|_{I_{p}}=\chi^{2}|_{I_{p}}
. Since the values
of
\varepsilon
lie in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
, we conclude that
\zeta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Q}_{f}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The case
(b)
of Theorem
1.2
holds.

Proof.

The assumption
v_{p}(N)>2
implies that we are in case (1)
of Proposition
2.1
. Moreover, the conductor
formula
a(\rho)=a(\chi_{1})+a(\chi_{2})
gives
a(\chi_{1})=1
and
a(\chi_{2})\geq 2
.
Thus, by local class field theory, we can view
\chi_{i}|_{I_{p}}
for
i=1,2
as characters of

\chi_{i}|_{I_{p}}:\big(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\big)^{*}\times(\text{finite~p-group}),

where
\chi_{1}
is nontrivial on the first factor (so in particular
p
>2) and trivial on the second factor, and
\chi_{2}
is nontrivial on the second factor. In particular, we can find
x_{0}\in

I_{p}
such that
\chi_{1}(x_{0})=1
and
\chi_{2}(x_{0})=\zeta_{p}
.
Now, since
a(\overline{\chi}_{1})=0
, the argument in the proof of Lemma
3.1
applied to
\chi_{1}
, gives that we can also find
x_{1}\in I_{p}
such that
\chi_{1}(x_{1})=\zeta_{\ell}
.

Now, for
0\leq n<p
, we set
\sigma_{n}:=x_{1}x_{0}^{n}\in I_{p}
.
We have

\tr\rho(\sigma_{n})=\chi_{1}(x_{1}x_{0}^{n})+\chi_{2}(x_{1}x_{0}^{n})=\zeta_{\ell}+\chi_{2}(x_{1})\zeta_{p}^{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{f,\lambda},

and summing over
n
gives

\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}\tr\rho(\sigma_{n})=p\zeta_{\ell}\implies\zeta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Q}_{f,\lambda}.

Since
\rho_{f,\lambda}|_{I_{p}}
is independent of
\lambda\nmid Np
,
we conclude that

\zeta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Q}_{f,\mathfrak{q}}

for all primes
\mathfrak{q}\nmid Np
in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
. This means that the extension
\mathbb{Q}_{f}(\zeta_{\ell})/\mathbb{Q}_{f}
is of degree 1 because almost all primes are totally split in it, hence
\zeta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Q}_{f}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that we are in case (1) or (3) of Proposition
2.1
.
Suppose further that
v_{p}(N)=2
and
v_{p}(N^{\prime})=1
.
Then
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f}
.

Proof.

Assume first we are in case (1) of Proposition
2.1
. We have

a(\chi_{1})=a(\chi_{2})=a(\overline{\chi_{2}})=1,\ \ a(\overline{\chi_{1}})=0,

By local class field theory, both characters factor through
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}
. Furthermore, we can find
x_{0},x_{1}\in I_{p}
such that

\chi_{1}(x_{0})=1,\ \ \chi_{2}(x_{0})=\zeta_{q},\ \ \chi_{1}(x_{1})=\zeta_{\ell}

for some prime
q\neq\ell
dividing
p-1
. Now, setting
\sigma_{n}=x_{1}x_{0}^{n}
for
0\leq n<q
and proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
,
gives
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f}
.

Assume now that we are in case (3) of Proposition
2.1
.
We have
\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}}}\simeq\chi\oplus\chi^{s}
, where
\chi^{s}
denotes the conjugated character of
\chi
with respect to
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Also, the inertia subgroup inside
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}}\subset G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is
I_{p}
.
Hence, since
a(\chi)=1
and
a(\overline{\chi})=0
, there is
\sigma\in I_{p}
such that
\chi(\sigma)=\zeta_{\ell}
. We have

\rho(\sigma)=\chi(\sigma)\oplus\chi^{s}(\sigma),\quad\text{ and }\quad\chi(\sigma)\chi^{s}(\sigma)=\varepsilon(\sigma).

If
1\neq\varepsilon(\sigma)\in\mathbb{Q}_{f}
, then
\varepsilon(\sigma)
has order multiple of
\ell
and we get directly
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f}
.
If
\varepsilon(\sigma)=1
, then
\tr\rho(\sigma)=\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1}\in\mathbb{Q}_{f,\lambda}
and arguing as at the end of the proof of Lemma
3.2
yields
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f}
.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

In the case of Hilbert modular forms over a totally real field
F
, the analog of the group
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}
in Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
would be
\left(\mathbb{Z}/N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})\mathbb{Z}\right)^{*}
, where
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of
F
dividing the level, and the rest of the proof stays the same.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}
is irreducible and that we are in case (1) or (3) of Proposition
2.1
.
Assume also
v_{p}(N)=2
and
v_{p}(N^{\prime})=0
.
Then
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f}
and
e(\lambda|\ell)\geq\ell-1
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the kernel of reduction
\text{GL}(\mathcal{O}_{f,\lambda})\to\text{GL}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}})
. The assumptions imply that the image of the inertia
\rho(I_{p})
is a finite, nontrivial subgroup of
H
. The inequality
e(\lambda|\ell)\geq\ell-1
now follows immediately from Lemma
2.2
. Since
H
is a pro-
\ell
group, we can find
\sigma\in I_{p}

such that
\rho(\sigma)
has order
\ell
.
Therefore, either

\text{det}(\rho(\sigma))=\zeta_{\ell}^{n}\neq 1\quad\text{ or }\quad\tr\rho(\sigma)=\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1}.

Either way, we conclude that
\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1}\in~\mathbb{Q}_{f,\lambda}
.
Since
\rho_{f,\lambda}|_{I_{p}}
is independent of the prime
\lambda\nmid Np
, the same applies to the determinant and trace of
\rho_{f,\lambda}(\sigma).
We conclude that
\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1}\in\mathbb{Q}_{f,\mathfrak{q}}
for all primes
\mathfrak{q}\nmid Np
in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
. The conclusion follows as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The first statement in part (a) follows from Lemmas
3.1
,
3.3
and
3.5
, and the last statement from Lemma
3.5
.
Part (b) follows from Lemma
3.2
.
∎

4.
Examples of congruences and ramified primes

In this section, we give various examples related to our main theorem.

We say that two newforms
f
and
g
are
congruent
, if there is a prime
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
in the compositun
\mathbb{Q}_{f}\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{g}
such that
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}^{ss}\simeq\overline{\rho_{g,\lambda^{\prime}}}^{ss}
, where
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
are primes of, respectively,
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{g}
induced by
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
.
Moreover, such an isomorphism is equivalent to

a_{q}(f)\equiv a_{q}(g)\pmod{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\quad\text{ for all primes }q\nmid\ell pN^{\prime},

In the special case
\mathbb{Q}_{g}=\mathbb{Q}
,
we have
\lambda^{\prime\prime}=\lambda
and we say that

f
and
g
are
congruent modulo
\lambda
.

Example 4.1
.

Let
\ell=11
,
p=23
and
N=7406=23^{2}\cdot 14
.
The newform
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))

with LMFDB
[
10
]
label
7406.2.a.bt

satisfies
[\mathbb{Q}_{f}:\mathbb{Q}]=20
and
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
contains
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{11}+\zeta_{11}^{-1})
. Moreover, there are three primes
\lambda,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
above
11
satisfying

e(\lambda|11)=10,\ \ e(\lambda_{1}|11)=e(\lambda_{2}|11)=5.

Let
E
be the elliptic curve with Cremona label
14a1
.
There is a congruence modulo
\lambda
between
f
and the newform

g\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(14))

corresponding to the isogeny class of
E
,
that is,

a_{q}(f)\equiv a_{q}(E)\pmod{\lambda}

for all primes
q\nmid 11\cdot 7406
.
A quick consultation of LMFDB shows that
\overline{\rho_{E,11}}
is irreducible. Hence
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}
is irreducible as well, and we have

\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}\simeq\overline{\rho_{E,11}}
.
Since
E
has multiplicative reduction at 2 and 7 and minimal discriminant
\Delta(E)=2^{9}\cdot 7^{2}
, by the theory of the Tate curve, we know that
\overline{\rho_{E,11}}
ramifies at 2 and 7. Thus
N^{\prime}=14
.

Hence,
f
satisfies the hypothesis of part
a)

of Theorem
1.2
including the "moreover part", therefore
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{11}+\zeta_{11}^{-1})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{f}
and
e(\lambda|11)\geq 10
which is compatible with the observations above.

The following example is the only case of ramification loss not covered by Theorem
1.2
. In particular, it shows that the assumption
p^{2}\mid N
is necessary to guarantee ramification in the field of coefficients.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\ell=7
,
p=5
and
N=55
.
The newform
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
with LMFDB label
55.2.a.b

has field of coefficients
\mathbb{Q}_{f}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
. The prime

\lambda=(3+\sqrt{2})
divides
7
in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
and there is a congruence modulo
\lambda
between
f
and the unique newform
g\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(11))
which corresponds to the isogeny class of an elliptic curve of conductor 11. In this case, we have

5\|N,\qquad 5\nmid N^{\prime},

and clearly there is no ramification at
7
in
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
.

To finish, we will describe a systematic computation to generate newforms with primes (very) ramified in their field of coefficients based on
part a) of Theorem
1.2
and Example
4.1
.

Indeed, to recreate the situation
v_{p}(N)=2
and
v_{p}(N^{\prime})=0
(Table
1
) or
v_{p}(N^{\prime})=1
(Table
2
) we will use the following proposition, which is a special case of
[
14
, Theorem A]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\lambda
be a prime of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
over an integer prime
\ell>3
. Let
g\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N^{\prime}),\varepsilon^{\prime})
be a newform of level
N^{\prime}
, weight
2
, and nebentypus
\varepsilon^{\prime}
with
\ell\nmid N^{\prime}
, such that the residual representation

\overline{\rho_{g,\lambda}}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\text{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}})

is irreducible and has Artin conductor
N^{\prime}
. Let
\alpha\in\{1,2\}.
If one of the following is satisfied

1)

\ell\mid p+1
and

(a)

p\nmid N^{\prime}
,
\ell\mid a_{p}(g)
, and
\alpha=2
, or

(b)

p\|N^{\prime}
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}
is unramified at
p
, and
\alpha=1
;

2)

\ell\mid p-1
and

(a)

p\nmid N^{\prime}
and
\alpha=2
, or

(b)

v_{p}(\mathfrak{f}(\varepsilon^{\prime}))=v_{p}(N^{\prime})\geq 2
and
\alpha=1
, where
\mathfrak{f}(\varepsilon^{\prime})
is the conductor of
\varepsilon^{\prime}.

then there exists a nebentypus
\varepsilon
and a newform
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{\alpha}N^{\prime}),\varepsilon)
such that

\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}\simeq\overline{\rho_{g,\lambda}}.

Calculate

and the splitting of

In Tables

Let us now examine the algorithm closely. To check the irreducibility of
\overline{\rho_{g,\lambda}}
it suffices to find an irreducibile characteristic polynomial of
\overline{\rho_{g,\lambda}}(\text{Frob}_{q})
for a prime
q\nmid\ell pN^{\prime}
(because for odd representation of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
being irreducible and absolute irreducible are equivalent). To check that the conductor of the representation is indeed
N^{\prime}
, by the famous theorem of Khare and Wintenberger, it is enough to check that
g
is not congruent to any other modular form of weight 2 and with level dividing
N^{\prime}
(since
N^{\prime}
is small, there will be only a few of such forms if any).
Step 4 is the hardest one because, in general, calculating the space
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\varepsilon)
with nontrivial
\varepsilon
is hard. Notice, however, that for all
f
in the table, the letter following "2." is "a", which means that
f
has trivial nebentypus. This is not a coincidence; in fact, the following lemma reduces the search to forms with trivial nebentypus.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
g\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N^{\prime}))
be a newform.
Let
\alpha\in\{1,2\}
and
\lambda,\ell,p
be primes such that
g
satisfies one of the conditions of Proposition
4.3
. Assume also
\ell\nmid\varphi(N^{\prime})
, where
\varphi
is Euler totient function. Then we can find
f
congruent to
g
modulo
\lambda
in
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{\alpha}N^{\prime})).

Proof.

Observe that
p>3
because
\ell\mid(p-1)(p+1)
and
\ell>3
.
By Proposition
4.3
, there exists a newform
f^{\prime}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{\alpha}N^{\prime}),\varepsilon)
congruent to
g
modulo
\lambda
. Assume that
\varepsilon
is nontrivial; otherwise, there is nothing to show. Comparing determinants, we must have
\overline{\varepsilon}=1
which means that the order of
\varepsilon
is an
\ell
-power, say
\ell^{\beta}
.
Moreover,
\varepsilon
being a Dirichlet character of modulo
p^{\alpha}N^{\prime}
, we have
\ell^{\beta}\mid\varphi(p^{\alpha}N^{\prime})
.

If
p\mid N^{\prime}
, then
\varphi(p^{\alpha}N^{\prime})=p^{\alpha}\varphi(N^{\prime})
is not divisible by
\ell
by assumption, a contradiction. Therefore,
p\nmid N^{\prime}
and
\varphi(p^{\alpha}N^{\prime})=(p-1)p^{\alpha-1}\varphi(N^{\prime})
.
We conclude that
\ell^{\beta}\mid p-1
and
\varepsilon
factors via
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}
and so it
has conductor
p
. Furthermore, we are in case 2) (a) of Proposition
4.3
and, in particular,
\alpha=2
.

Denote by
\rho^{\prime}
the representation
\rho_{f^{\prime},\lambda}
. By assumption, we have

\overline{\rho_{f^{\prime},\lambda}}\simeq\overline{\rho_{g,\lambda}}.

Since
p\nmid N^{\prime}
, the inertia type of
g
at
p
is unramified principal series, i.e,

\rho_{g,\lambda}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\simeq\psi_{1}\oplus\psi_{2}

for some unramified characters
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}.

Consider the following data:

•

the restriction
\rho_{r}:=\rho^{\prime}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{r}}}
for every prime
r\mid\ell N^{\prime},

•

the Principal Series representation
\rho_{p}:=\psi_{1}\varepsilon\oplus\psi_{2}\varepsilon^{-1}
of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},

•

the
\ell
-adic cyclotomic character
\psi=\omega_{\ell}.

From
[
6
, Lemma 1.13, Lemma 1.14]
the representation
\overline{\rho_{g,\lambda}}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{l})}}
is absolutely irreducible. This allows us to apply
[
1
, Théorème 3.2.2]
to get a newform
f
such that
\rho_{f,\lambda}
is unramified outside
p\ell N^{\prime}
with determinant
\psi
, and moreover,

\rho_{f,\lambda}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{r}}}\simeq\rho_{r},\ \ \text{for every }r\mid\ell pN^{\prime},

\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda}}\simeq\overline{\rho_{g,\lambda}}.

The local conditions, together with the determinant, imply that
f
is a newform in
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(p^{2}N^{\prime}))
.
∎

Showing that two newforms are congruent can still be hard (if Sturm’s bound is big). In our case, we got an advantage because we already know that there is some newform congruent to
g
, so following the famous logic of Sherlock Holmes, instead of showing that a certain form
f
is congruent to
g
, it is enough to show that all the other newforms are not congruent to
g
, which is a lot easier.

Example 4.6
.

From Table
1
we have another method for obtaining examples of the case
v_{p}(N^{\prime})=2
and
v_{p}(N^{\prime})=1.
Indeed, as a consequence of Carayol’s Theorem (Proposition
2.1
), reducing any
f
from Table
1
modulo any prime
\lambda^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
kills some ramification modulo
p
. If
e(\lambda|\ell)<\ell-1
and
\overline{\rho_{f,\lambda^{\prime}}}
is irreducible, then by Theorem
1.2
the only possiblity is

v_{p}(N)=2\ \ \text{and}\ \ v_{p}(N^{\prime})=1.

Remark 4.7
.

Finally, let us comment on the case b) of Theorem
1.2
. The case
v_{p}(N)>2
leads rapidly to very high levels and very big coefficient fields. Indeed, to apply the method above, the level of
f
would have to be divisible by
p^{3}
, where
\ell\mid p-1
for some
\ell>3
, and we are in case 2)(b) of Proposition
4.3
.
The third smallest such
p
is
p=29
(here
\ell=7
), leading to
29^{3}=24389\mid N
. Moreover, the condition
v_{p}(\mathfrak{f}(\varepsilon^{\prime}))=v_{p}(N^{\prime})\geq 2
forces choosing
g
with non-trivial nebentypus, leading to harder computations and higher degree coefficient fields. For example, already for the two smallest cases

(\ell,p)=(5,11),(11,23)
the possibilities for
g
have coefficient fields of degrees
\geq 100
.
