A fully decoupled and structure-preserving relaxation Crank–Nicolson finite element method for Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson model
Dongqian Li, Huini Liu, Yin Yang, Peimeng Yin

Abstract
We propose a fully decoupled, structure-preserving relaxation Crank–Nicolson finite element method (FEM) for the coupled Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson (GPP) system modeling ultracold plasmas. By introducing suitable auxiliary variables to reformulate the nonlinear interaction and charge density terms, the original system is recast into an equivalent form that enables a linear, fully decoupled numerical scheme. The proposed method preserves key physical invariants, including the mass of each component and a modified discrete energy, at the fully discrete level. We establish the well-posedness and uniqueness of the scheme and rigorously derive optimal error estimates, achieving second-order accuracy in time and optimal ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -th order convergence in space for P k P^{k} finite element approximations. Numerical experiments confirm the theoretical results and demonstrate the effectiveness of the method in preserving conservation properties and accurately capturing complex dynamical behaviors of the coupled GPP system.

The contributions, innovations, and significance of this work are summarized as follows:

•

For the coupled Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson system, which involves multiple interacting species together with Poisson coupling, we develop a relaxation Crank–Nicolson finite element method by introducing only two auxiliary variables to reformulate the four nonlinear interaction terms and the two charge-density terms.

•

The proposed method is fully decoupled and linear at each time step. It requires neither iterative procedures for nonlinear terms nor the solution of a coupled algebraic system, and thus is computationally efficient. Despite its linear and decoupled structure, the method preserves the discrete mass of each component and a modified discrete energy, thereby inheriting important structure-preserving properties of the continuous model.

•

We establish the well-posedness and uniqueness of the fully discrete scheme and derive rigorous optimal error estimates. In particular, the method achieves second-order accuracy in time and optimal
(k+1)
-th order convergence in space in the
L^{2}
-norm, where
k
is the polynomial degree of the finite element space.

•

While retaining second-order accuracy in time, the proposed method significantly reduces the computational cost. In contrast to existing splitting-type methods for the coupled GPE, which require up to seven groups of linear systems, the proposed approach involves only two groups of decoupled linear systems; for the coupled GPP model, only one additional Poisson equation needs to be solved.

•

Numerical experiments are presented to verify the theoretical error estimates and the conservation properties, and to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in accurately capturing the dynamics of the coupled Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson system.

3
Error estimates for the fully discrete system

We define the nodal interpolation operator
\Pi_{h}:V\rightarrow V_{h}
as
(\Pi_{h}u)(\mathbf{x}_{i})=u(\mathbf{x}_{i})
with predefined nodes
\{\mathbf{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. The Ritz projection operator
R_{h}:\mathring{V}\rightarrow\mathring{V}_{h}
is defined by the following problem: for any
v\in\mathring{V}
, find
R_{h}v\in\mathring{V}_{h}
such that

A_{1}(R_{h}v-v,w)=0,\quad\forall w\in V_{h}.

(51)

The following lemmas describe the properties of the Ritz projection operator
R_{h}
.

Lemma 5
.

The operators
\Pi_{h}
and
R_{h}
satisfy the following properties

\displaystyle\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|+h\|\nabla(v-\Pi_{h}v)\|+h\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|_{\infty}\leq Ch^{k+1}\|v\|_{k+1},\quad\forall v\in H^{k+1}(\Omega),

(52)

\displaystyle\|v-R_{h}v\|+h\|\nabla(v-R_{h}v)\|\leq Ch^{k+1}\|v\|_{k+1},\quad\forall v\in\mathring{V}\cap H^{k+1}(\Omega).

(53)

Proof.

The result of the interpolation error estimate (
52
) can be found in
[
10
]
. We now proceed to prove the Ritz projection error estimate (
53
).

\|\nabla(v-R_{h}v)\|^{2}=A_{1}(v-R_{h}v,v-R_{h}v)=A_{1}(v-R_{h}v,v-\Pi_{h}v)+A_{1}(v-R_{h}v,\Pi_{h}v-R_{h}v).

(54)

Since
\Pi_{h}v-R_{h}v\in V_{h}
, by applying (
51
), we have
A_{1}(v-R_{h}v,\Pi_{h}v-R_{h}v)=0
. Hence, using (
19
), we have

\|\nabla(v-R_{h}v)\|^{2}=A_{1}(v-R_{h}v,v-\Pi_{h}v)\leq\gamma_{2}\|\nabla(v-R_{h}v)\|\|\nabla(v-\Pi_{h}v)\|,

(55)

which upon applying the interpolation error estimate (
52
) for
\nabla(v-\Pi_{h}v)
, we obtain

\|\nabla(v-R_{h}v)\|\leq Ch^{k}\|v\|_{k+1}.

(56)

To derive the
L^{2}
-norm error estimate, we introduce the dual problem

\displaystyle-\Delta

\displaystyle\varphi=e:=v-R_{h}v\;\text{in}\;\Omega,

(57)

\displaystyle\varphi\;\text{is periodic on}\;\partial\Omega.

The corresponding weak formulation is to find
\varphi\in\mathring{V}_{h}
such that

A_{1}(\varphi,\nu)=(e,\nu),\;\forall\nu\in\mathring{V},

(58)

by taking
\nu=e
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|e\|^{2}=A_{1}(\varphi,e)=A_{1}(\varphi-\Pi_{h}\varphi,e)+A_{1}(\Pi_{h}\varphi,e)\leq\gamma_{2}\|\nabla(\varphi-\Pi_{h}\varphi)\|\|\nabla e\|,

(59)

where we apply (
51
) and (
19
) again.

Because of the quasi-optimal interpolant error in
H^{1}
, and the elliptic regularity
[
10
]
, combined with the
H^{1}
-error (
56
), we have

\|e\|^{2}\leq\gamma_{2}\|\nabla(\varphi-\Pi_{h}\varphi)\|\|\nabla e\|\leq Ch\|\varphi\|_{2}\cdot h^{k}\|v\|_{k+1}\leq Ch^{k+1}\|e\|\cdot\|v\|_{k+1},

(60)

which together with (
56
) imply (
53
).

Similarly, we define the complex interpolation operator
\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}_{h}
as
(\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}u)(\mathbf{x}_{i})=u(\mathbf{x}_{i})
and complex Ritz projection
\mathbf{R}_{h}:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}_{h}
: for given
v\in\mathbf{V}
, find
\mathbf{R}_{h}v\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle A_{0}(v-\mathbf{R}_{h}v,w)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall w\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(61)

\displaystyle\langle v-\mathbf{R}_{h}v,1\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

(62)

where (
62
) ensures the uniqueness of the Ritz projection
\mathbf{R}_{h}v
. Then, it holds the following result.

Lemma 6
.

The operators
\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}
and
\mathbf{R}_{h}
satisfy the following properties

\displaystyle\|v-\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}v\|+h\|\nabla(v-\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}v)\|+h\|v-\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}v\|_{\infty}\leq Ch^{k+1}\|v\|_{k+1},\quad\forall v\in\mathbf{H}^{k+1}(\Omega),

(63)

\displaystyle\|v-\mathbf{R}_{h}v\|+h\|\nabla(v-\mathbf{R}_{h}v)\|\leq Ch^{k+1}\left\|v-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vd\mathbf{x}\right\|_{k+1},\quad\forall v\in\mathbf{V}\cap\mathbf{H}^{k+1}(\Omega).

(64)

Proof.

We assume that
v=a+b\mathbf{i}\in\mathbf{V}\cap\mathbf{H}^{k+1}(\Omega)
, which implies that
\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}v=\Pi_{h}a+\Pi_{h}b\mathbf{i}
. Then we have
\|v\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)}=\|a\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}(\Omega)}+\|b\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}(\Omega)}
. Note that
a,b\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
, then

\displaystyle\|v-\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}v\|+h\|\nabla(v-\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}v)\|+h\|v-\mathbf{\Pi}_{h}v\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq(\|a-\Pi_{h}a\|+h\|\nabla(a-\Pi_{h}a)\|+h\|a-\Pi_{h}a\|_{\infty})+(\|b-\Pi_{h}b\|+h\|\nabla(b-\Pi_{h}b)\|+h\|b-\Pi_{h}b\|_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}(\|a\|_{k+1}+\|b\|_{k+1})\leq Ch^{k+1}\|v\|_{k+1},

where we have applied (
52
). This completes the proof of (
63
).

To verify (
64
), we assume,
v=a+b\mathbf{i}
, and define
v_{0}=v-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vd\mathbf{x}=a_{0}+b_{0}\mathbf{i}
. Then it follows that
a_{0}=a-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}ad\mathbf{x}
,
b_{0}=b-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}bd\mathbf{x}
, and
a_{0},b_{0}\in\mathring{V}\cap H^{k+1}(\Omega)
.

It is easy to verify that
\mathbf{R}_{h}v=R_{h}v,\;\forall v\in{\mathring{V}}\cap{H}^{k+1}(\Omega)
, and
\mathbf{R}_{h}c=c
, where
c
is a constant. Then,

\|v_{0}-\mathbf{R}_{h}v_{0}\|=\left\|v-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vd\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{R}_{h}v+\mathbf{R}_{h}\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vd\mathbf{x}\right)\right\|=\|v-\mathbf{R}_{h}v\|.

Step 1.
We begin by proving the following estimates

(117)

\displaystyle\left\|{e_{Z,-}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|\leq\left\|{\eta_{Z,-}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|+\left\|{\xi_{Z,-}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|\leq C\left({{\tau^{2}}+{h^{k+1}}}\right).
