The potential clinical value of combining serum miR-769-5p with lung ultrasound for the diagnosis of acute lung injury
Authors: Zhicheng Ke, Weijiong Guan, Jianwen Chen, Xuewang Yue, Suyun Li, Xiaoxia Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAcute lung injury (ALI) represents a life-threatening condition triggered by diverse etiological factors. The expression and function of miR-769-5p within the context of ALI remain poorly characterized.PurposeThis study aims to examine the expression levels of miR-769-5p in the serum of ALI patients and assess its synergistic diagnostic potential when combined with the lung ultrasound (LUS) score for ALI.MethodsSerum miR-769-5p expression levels were quantified using reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). The diagnostic accuracy of miR-769-5p and the LUS score for ALI was evaluated via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Binary logistic regression was used to analyze their impact on ALI. Serum nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Pearson correlation assessed relationships between miR-769-5p and NF-κB, ROS, and LUS score. In lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-A549 cells, RT-qPCR and flow cytometry evaluated how miR-769-5p modulated NF-κB, ROS, and apoptosis.ResultsALI patients exhibited significantly decreased serum miR-769-5p expression. Combining miR-769-5p with the LUS score enhanced diagnostic accuracy, with both correlating significantly with ALI risk. Serum NF-κB and ROS levels were elevated in ALI and inversely correlated with miR-769-5p. In LPS-A549 cells, miR-769-5p overexpression suppressed LPS-induced NF-κB activation, ROS generation, and cellular apoptosis.ConclusionsThe combined application of miR-769-5p and the LUS score demonstrated enhanced diagnostic efficacy. Furthermore, miR-769-5p overexpression effectively attenuated NF-κB activation, ROS production, and cellular apoptosis.

Introduction

Acute lung injury (ALI) is a devastating syndrome driven by heterogeneous etiological triggers, including trauma, infection, and shock. It manifests as an acute onset and rapidly progressive hypoxemic respiratory failure, associated with a high mortality rate [1, 2]. The pathological core involves disruption of the alveolar-capillary barrier’s epithelial and endothelial structure, leading to markedly increased microvascular permeability. This process drives a substantial influx of inflammatory cells into the alveolar spaces, initiating a cascade that includes pneumonia, pulmonary edema, hypoxemia, and can ultimately progress to acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) [3]. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS), a major component of Gram-negative bacterial cell walls, is a well-established inducer for modeling ALI and investigating its pathogenesis. Upon entering the host, LPS is recognized by innate immune cells, triggering a cascade of pro-inflammatory mediator release. This further exacerbates damage to the alveolar epithelium and endothelium, compromising respiratory function and imposing a significant long-term disease burden on individuals and society [4, 5]. Consequently, finding effective biomarkers for early ALI diagnosis is crucial for guiding targeted interventions and improving patient prognosis.

Lung ultrasound (LUS) is a well-established standard tool for diagnosing ALI [6]. This technique permits comprehensive bedside detection of key respiratory abnormalities—from pleural effusion and pneumothorax to alveolar-interstitial syndromes, lung consolidation, abscess development, and recruitment/derecruitment dynamics—in one integrated examination. LUS accurately distinguishes localized from diffuse ventilation loss, providing direct guidance for individualized optimization of positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP). Its advantages-including portability, bedside feasibility, reproducibility, cost-effectiveness, and radiation-free operation-have established its widespread utility in point-of-care diagnosis and management of critical illnesses. Quantitative LUS scoring systems offer enhanced visualization of apical and focal pathologies while facilitating assessment of lung injury severity and prognosis, demonstrating significant advantages in pulmonary disease diagnosis and prognostic evaluation [7]. Evidence indicates that the LUS score correlates with disease severity in ALI patients, and its synergistic integration with other biomarkers significantly enhances prognostic prediction accuracy.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs), endogenous short non-coding RNAs spanning 19–24 nucleotides, execute gene silencing through sequence-specific interactions with 3’UTR regions of target mRNAs, ultimately provoking mRNA decay or translational inhibition [8]. Emerging evidence indicates that miRNAs are involved in various pathological processes, including inflammation, tissue injury, and cancer [9, 10]. Notably, miR-769-5p has been implicated in the pathogenesis of several diseases. For example, miR-769-5p expression is downregulated in gastric cancer (GC) tissues, and its overexpression has been shown to suppress the malignant behavior of GC cells [11]. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), the expression level of miR-769-5p was significantly down-regulated, and overexpression significantly inhibited cell proliferation and promoted apoptosis [12]. Moreover, miR-769-5p expression is upregulated in both serum and lung tissues of patients with severe asthma receiving oral corticosteroid (OCS) treatment, compared to those not treated with OCS [13]. Collectively, these findings highlight the functional relevance of miR-769-5p across different disease models. However, its specific role and diagnostic potential in ALI remain unexplored.

Based on this background, the present study was designed to quantify the expression level of miR-769-5p in the serum of ALI patients and to determine whether its integration with LUS enhances diagnostic accuracy, thereby providing new evidence for the early diagnosis of ALI and potential therapeutic targets.

Materials and methods

Patient recruitment and sample collection

This study enrolled 60 patients with ALI, and 60 healthy volunteers with matched baseline characteristics, such as gender, age, and body mass index (BMI), were recruited as the control group during the same period. Inclusion criteria comprised: (1) diagnosis of ALI according to the Chinese Thoracic Society guidelines, confirmed through clinical history, symptoms, and physical signs; (2) current treatment compliance and research cooperation. Exclusion criteria included: (1) symptom onset within 24 h without definitive diagnosis; (2) comorbidities including severe infections, malignancies, hematologic disorders, autoimmune diseases, viral hepatitis, tuberculosis, bronchial asthma, or other respiratory conditions; (3) incomplete medical records or non-cooperation. The study was performed in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. After the Ethics Committee approval of Dongying People’s Hospital (Dongying Hospital of Shandong Provincial Hospital Group) and written consents were obtained from the participants involved. Peripheral blood was drawn within 24 h of ALI diagnosis. Samples underwent centrifugation (3,000 ×g, 10 min) for plasma isolation before cryopreservation at -80 °C.

Lung ultrasound examination

All patients were positioned in dorsal decubitus for standardized examination by a single trained sonographer. The protocol involved systematic scanning of 12 predefined thoracic zones per established methodology: bilateral anterior, lateral, and superior/inferior posterior chest walls. Quantitative assessment followed validated imaging criteria, assigning scores based on the most severe sonographic manifestation per zone: 3 points for consolidation, 2 points for severe ventilation loss (multiple coalescent B-lines), 1 point for moderate ventilation impairment (isolated B-lines), and 0 points for normal aeration (A-lines). The aggregate score (range: 0–36) represented the comprehensive LUS score, with higher values indicating greater pulmonary pathology severity.

Cell culture

The human alveolar epithelial cell line A549 (Procell, CL-0016) was propagated in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Sigma-Aldrich) under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂). To establish an in vitro ALI model based on previously reported methods, cells were exposed to 100 ng/ml LPS (Sigma-Aldrich, L5293) for 24 h to simulate ALI in vitro [14].

Mimics-miR, inhibitors-miR, and transfection experiments

To regulate miR-769-5p levels, transient transfections were performed using:

miR-769-5p mimic (mimics-miR) and its scrambled negative control (mimics-NC),

miR-769-5p inhibitor (inhibitors-miR) and corresponding inhibitor control (inhibitors-NC). All oligonucleotides were synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). At 70–80% confluence, transfections were conducted using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen). Fresh complete medium replaced transfection complexes after 6 h, followed by 48-hour incubation before subsequent assays.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Serum NF-κB levels were determined using a human NF-κB ELISA kit (Cloud-Clone Corp., China, CB10686-Hu); ROS metabolites were measured with a human ROS ELISA kit (Cloud-Clone Corp., CB10719-Hu). Post-clarification of serum samples via centrifugation (10,000 ×g, 15 min, 4 °C), standards and sample dilutions (1:4) were applied to pre-coated wells. Microplates were then incubated (37 °C, 90 min), washed five times, and exposed to biotinylated detection antibodies for 60 min. Following TMB substrate incubation (15 min), reactions were terminated with 2 M H₂SO₄. Optical density at 450 nm was quantified using a microplate spectrophotometer (BioTek, Synergy H1), with concentrations calculated against standard curves. All samples were analyzed in duplicate.

Reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA extraction was performed with TRIzol™ reagent following manufacturer protocols (Invitrogen, 15596026), with purity and concentration verified by NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometry (Thermo Scientific). Reverse transcription of 1 µg total RNA was performed with the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit (Takara, RR047A) under the following conditions: 37 °C for 15 min, followed by 85 °C for 5 s inactivation. Quantitative PCR reactions (20 µL total volume) contained 10 µL SYBR Premix Ex Taq™ II (Takara, RR820A), 0.8 µL each primer (10 µM), 2 µL cDNA template, and 6.4 µL nuclease-free water. U6 snRNA was used as the endogenous control for miRNA quantification. The sequences of the primers used were as follows: miR-769-5p (forward: 5′-ACACTCCAGGTGGGTGAGACCTCTGGGGGTGTG-3′, reverse: 5′-CTCAACTGGTGTGTGG-3′) and U6 (forward: 5′-CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3′, reverse: 5′-AACGCTTCACGAATTTGGT-3′).

Flow cytometry analysis

Cells were incubated with 10 µM 2’,7’-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA; Beyotime Biotechnology, S0033S). Quantitative analysis of ROS levels was performed by measuring mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) with FlowJo v10.8 software. For apoptosis detection, cell suspensions were processed using an Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (MultiSciences, AP101). 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and 10 µL propidium iodide (PI, 20 µg/mL) were added sequentially, with incubation at room temperature in the dark for 15 min. Samples were acquired within 30 min post-staining using the following detector settings: FITC channel (530 nm) for Annexin V and PE channel (585 nm) for PI.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses employed IBM SPSS Statistics 23 and GraphPad Prism 9.3.1. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was also performed using GraphPad Prism. Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Group comparisons utilized independent samples t-tests (two groups) or one-way ANOVA (≥ 3 groups), with statistical significance established at p < 0.05.

Results

Diagnostic value of miR-769-5p combined with LUS score for ALI

RT-qPCR analysis revealed significantly downregulated serum miR-769-5p expression in ALI patients versus controls (Fig. 1A). Baseline characteristics (Table 1) showed no significant differences in sex, age, BMI, or heart rate between groups (p > 0.05), whereas the LUS score was markedly elevated in ALI patients (p < 0.001), confirming its association with ALI development. To assess the combined diagnostic utility of serum miR-769-5p and LUS scoring, ROC analysis was conducted (Fig. 1B). As tabulated in Table 2, the standalone miR-769-5p biomarker exhibited an AUC of 0.7660 (95% CI: 0.6814–0.8505, p < 0.001) with sensitivity and specificity values of 76.67% and 76.67% respectively. The LUS score alone achieved an AUC of 0.8132 (95% CI: 0.7358–0.8906, p < 0.001; sensitivity 86.67%, specificity 63.33%). Importantly, the composite biomarker approach significantly enhanced diagnostic performance (AUC = 0.8711, 95% CI: 0.8095–0.9327, p < 0.001), with an 83.33% sensitivity and 80.00% specificity. These findings indicate that both miR-769-5p and the LUS score effectively discriminate ALI patients from healthy individuals, while their combined application substantially enhances diagnostic precision. Binary logistic regression (Table 3) identified the LUS score (OR = 1.689, 95% CI: 1.340–2.129, p < 0.001) as a risk factor for ALI, and miR-769-5p expression (OR = 0.298, 95% CI: 0.156–0.567, p < 0.001) as a protective factor for ALI. Specifically, elevated LUS scores correlated with heightened ALI risk, while diminished miR-769-5p expression was associated with an elevated risk of ALI. These findings collectively demonstrate statistically significant associations between LUS scores, miR-769-5p expression levels, and ALI risk.

Inverse correlation of miR-769-5p with NF-κB, ROS levels, and LUS score

Serum levels of NF-κB and ROS were quantified by ELISA. Compared to controls, ALI patients exhibited significantly elevated serum NF-κB and ROS concentrations (Fig. 2A-B). Pearson analysis identified significant inverse relationships between miR-769-5p expression and both: NF-κB levels (r = -0.5306, p < 0.0001; Fig. 2C); ROS concentrations (r = -0.6709, p < 0.0001; Fig. 2D); LUS score (Fig. 2E; r = -0.5682, p < 0.0001). These results indicate that in the ALI state, the pro-inflammatory factor NF-κB and the oxidative stress marker ROS are significantly upregulated, while the expression level of miR-769-5p has a significant negative correlation with these key pathological indicators and the LUS score that reflects the severity of lung injury.

MiR-769-5p attenuates NF-κB, ROS, and apoptosis in LPS-A549 cells

An in vitro lung injury model was established by treating A549 cells with 100 ng/ml LPS for 24 h. LPS-challenged cells exhibited significantly elevated NF-κB activity and ROS levels versus controls (Fig. 3A-B), validating model establishment. Concurrently, miR-769-5p expression was markedly downregulated in LPS-treated cells (Fig. 3C). Transfection experiments confirmed successful miR-769-5p modulation: miR-769-5p mimics (LPS+mimics-miR) significantly increased expression versus mimic-negative control (LPS+mimics-NC), while inhibitors (LPS+inhibitors-miR) substantially reduced expression versus inhibitor-negative control (LPS+inhibitors-NC) (Fig. 3C). Functional assessments revealed that miR-769-5p overexpression (LPS+mimics-miR) significantly suppressed LPS-induced NF-κB activation compared to LPS+mimics-NC, whereas miR-769-5p knockdown (LPS+inhibitors-miR) exacerbated NF-κB levels versus LPS+inhibitors-NC (Fig. 3D). ROS levels showed analogous response patterns: miR-769-5p upregulation attenuated ROS production, while its depletion amplified oxidative stress (Fig. 3E). Apoptosis analysis demonstrated that miR-769-5p overexpression reduced LPS-induced apoptosis, whereas its inhibition significantly increased apoptotic rate (Fig. 3F). Collectively, these findings indicate that miR-769-5p downregulation in LPS-induced lung injury models potentiates pathological processes, while its overexpression exerts protective effects by ameliorating NF-κB activation, ROS accumulation, and cellular apoptosis.

Discussion

ALI represents a severe clinical syndrome triggered by inflammatory responses, exhibiting mortality rates ranging from 35% to 40% [15]. Although corticosteroids are frequently considered potential therapeutics due to their broad anti-inflammatory activity, their clinical application is constrained by adverse effects such as muscle weakness, impaired physical function, and allergic reactions [16]. Therefore, identifying novel biomarkers with improved diagnostic safety and efficacy remains an unmet need in ALI management. In recent years, miRNAs have emerged as a promising class of non-invasive biomarkers, owing to their high specificity, detectable presence in body fluids, good stability, and close association with disease progression [17, 18]. In the present study, we demonstrated that integrating serum miR-769-5p expression with the LUS score significantly improved the diagnostic accuracy for ALI. Complementing these clinical observations, in vitro mechanistic investigations further revealed that upregulation of miR-769-5p effectively attenuates injury in alveolar epithelial cells, supporting its potential role in modulating ALI-related pathological processes.

This study provided the first systematic validation of the diagnostic value of miR-769-5p in ALI. We demonstrated that serum miR-769-5p expression was significantly downregulated in ALI patients, and its abnormal expression level effectively distinguished ALI from controls. Existing studies have shown that miRNAs can function in ALI and, when combined with LUS scores, improve diagnostic performance. For instance, exosomal miR-150 exerts protective effects by targeting microvascular endothelial cells and modulating the MAPK signaling pathway, thereby partially mitigating the pathological progression of ALI [19]. Furthermore, miR-29a-3p has been reported to ameliorate ALI by reducing PANoptosis in alveolar epithelial cells [20], and miR-221-3p, together with arterial blood gas parameters and LUS scores, has demonstrated high accuracy in predicting survival outcomes in infants with neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (NRDS) [21]. Consistent with these findings, our results indicated that combining miR-769-5p levels with the LUS score significantly enhanced diagnostic accuracy. Subsequent analysis revealed significantly elevated levels of NF-κB and ROS in ALI patient sera. Notably, miR-769-5p expression exhibited significant negative correlations with NF-κB, ROS, and LUS scores. This integrated approach offered a more stable and potentially safer diagnostic strategy for ALI, supporting its clinical translation as a non-invasive biomarker.

Multiple studies have demonstrated the functional involvement of miR-769-5p in pulmonary and inflammation-induced diseases. For instance, in NSCLC, miR-769-5p significantly inhibits cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro, and reduces tumor growth and metastasis in vivo [22]. Additionally, in the context of periodontal inflammation, miR-769-5p expression is downregulated in gingival tissues from patients with chronic periodontitis (CP) and in periodontal ligament cells (PDLCs) treated with LPS. Overexpression of miR-769-5p in LPS-treated PDLCs promotes cell viability while suppressing inflammatory responses and apoptosis [23]. Furthermore, the anti-apoptotic and anti-inflammatory effects of suppressing circ_0097010 can be reversed by reducing miR-769-5p abundance [24]. In line with these findings, the present study further explores whether miR-769-5p exerts anti-apoptotic and anti-inflammatory effects in the inflammation-induced condition of ALI.

To further investigate the underlying mechanisms, an in vitro ALI model was established using A549 cells treated with LPS. In this model, we observed that the levels of NF-κB and ROS increased significantly, suggesting that the activity of the NF-κB signaling pathway was enhanced and the state of oxidative stress was activated. Many studies have reported that NF-κB is the core pathway of inflammation and is up-regulated in the pathogenesis of ALI [25, 26]. ROS is the key mediator of oxidative stress, which has also been confirmed to participate in the pathological progression of ALI [27, 28], which is consistent with the observation results of this study. Further analysis showed that the expression of miR-769-5p was significantly down-regulated in the LPS-induced lung injury cell model. Importantly, overexpression of miR-769-5p can effectively inhibit the activation of NF-κB, reduce the production of ROS, and reduce the level of apoptosis, suggesting that miR-769-5p may play a protective role in ALI by regulating the NF-κB signaling pathway and oxidative stress.

However, this study has certain limitations. First, the sample was derived from a single clinical center, which may limit the representativeness of the sample and the generalizability of the results. Future research should validate these conclusions using multi-center, larger-scale clinical cohorts. Second, in terms of experimental methods, this study has only verified the mechanism in one cell line (A549 cell) at present, and it is necessary to further confirm its universality in other alveolar epithelial cell lines or primary cell models in the future. Finally, the mechanistic exploration was primarily based on cell models; the expression pattern and biological function of miR-769-5p in ALI pathogenesis have not been systematically validated in animal models. Its regulatory mechanism in vivo environments requires further investigation. Therefore, future studies should establish ALI animal models to thoroughly investigate the specific roles of miR-769-5p in pulmonary inflammation, epithelial barrier function, and injury repair, as well as to systematically evaluate the efficacy and safety of therapeutic interventions. These efforts will provide more robust experimental evidence for the clinical translation of miR-769-5p as a diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target for ALI.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrated that serum miR-769-5p expression was reduced in patients with ALI, indicating its potential as a diagnostic biomarker. Furthermore, combining miR-769-5p levels with LUS score significantly improved diagnostic accuracy. In vitro experiments confirmed that miR-769-5p overexpression effectively alleviated alveolar epithelial cell injury by suppressing NF-κB activation, reducing oxidative stress, and inhibiting cellular apoptosis. Together, these findings provide novel insights into combined diagnostic strategies and targeted therapeutic approaches for ALI.
