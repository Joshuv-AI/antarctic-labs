Summary:

Wind River was one of three sites that collected 3d sonic anemometer data for an ICOS subcanopy
observation study. The three sites were deﬁned by the following features and terrain: a deciduous broadleaf
forest in ﬂat terrain (Lanžhot, Czech Republic), a coniferous forest in mountainous terrain (Renon, Italy), and
a tall conifer forest in mountain-valley terrain (Wind River, USA). The Wind River subcanopy towers were
deployed in a high LAI, old-growth evergreen conifer forest and collected approximately 11 months of data.
The site is an ecologically rich temperate rainforest in the western Cascade Mountains, and the biological
carbon sink and source strength has been measured since 1998 using eddy covariance on the top of a 74 m
tall ﬂux tower (currently called the Wind River NEON tower). Additionally, forest inventory records date back
to the 1920s. In 2024, four subcanopy towers were installed near the Wind River NEON tower to measure
wind ﬂow in the understory canopy layer for better understanding canopy ﬂow coupling and decoupling in
the subcanopy and how this affects the interpretation of overstory ﬂuxes. The subcanopy tower installation
was done by Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and Washington State University (WSU) with
collaborations from the University of Utah and the National Ecological Observatory Network (NEON).

Wind River Topography and Wind Regime:

The Wind River site is located towards the north end of a 23 km long valley which terminates into the
Columbia River about 15 km south of the tower (Fig. 1a). While the local slope in the immediate ﬂux tower
footprint is less than 4%, steeper slopes rise approximately 1500 m above the valley ﬂoor on both sides. The
tower is located between two major topographical features: Bunker Hill (730 m elevation) to the east-
southeast and Trout Creek Hill (895 m elevation) to the northwest. These features cause blockage and air
ﬂow is redirected around them (as seen in Fig. 1b). At a larger scale, strong differential heating between the
Cascade Mountains and Wind River Valley creates along-axis, diel ﬂow in the valley in the warmer months.
These mountain-valley winds are characterized by a diel wind reversal: stronger up-valley ﬂow (from the
southeast) occurs during the daytime, and weaker down-valley and downslope ﬂow (from the west) occurs
at night. Nighttime drainage and canopy decoupling in the forest are expected to be signiﬁcant. Daytime
canopy decoupling may also be signiﬁcant due to the tall and high LAI canopy.

Wind River Experimental Forest and NEON tower description:

 • Old-growth (~500 yr old) Douglas-ﬁr
 (Pseudotsuga menziesii (Mirb.) Franco)–
 western hemlock (Tsuga heterophylla
 (Raf.) Sarg.) forest. Mean canopy (zhc) is
 ~50m.

 • The distribution of leaf area index (LAI ~
 8.8 m2/m2) is bottom heavy with the
 maximum LAI between 10 and 30 m. PAR
 transmittance to the bottom of the
 canopy is about 8% – 10% of above
 canopy PAR.

 • The understory LAI (z < 3 m) is estimated to be 0.86 m2/m2.
 Understory trees include Paciﬁc yew (Taxus brevifolia) and
 Paciﬁc dogwood (Cornus nuttallii). Dominant understory
 shrub species are vine maple (Acer circinatum), salal
 (Gaultheria shallon), dwarf Oregon grape (Berberis nervosa)
 and ferns.

 • The Wind River NEON ﬂux tower (WREF) (45.8205°, -121.9520°) is instrumented with:

 • 3-D sonic/closed path IRGA, 4 stream net radiometer and phenocam at 74 m (z/zhc ~1.5)

       •   2-D sonic at 0.19, 11.12, 22.97, 34.82, 40.74, 52.57, and 58.47 m

       •   Air temp/RH at 0.19, 11.12, 22.97, 34.82, 40.74, 52.57, and 58.47 m

       •   Solar radiation at 0.19, 11.12, 22.97, 34.82, 40.74, 52.57, and 58.47 m

       •   [CO2] and isotopes at 0.19, 11.12, 22.97, 34.82, 40.74, 52.57, and 58.47 m

 • For the above sensors, data are collected by NEON and available here:

Wind River subcanopy tower description:

 • Towers are named tower A, tower B, tower C, and tower D. Tower D was closest to the NEON tower.
 • The towers were placed in a northwest-southeast transect radiating out from the NEON tower.
 Farthest tower (tower A) was 200m from the base of the NEON tower.
 • Towers had one or two Campbell Scientiﬁc CSAT3 sonic anemometers placed at a height of 2.7 m
 above ground level (z/zhc ~ 0.05). Details are in Table 1.

 • High frequency (20 Hz) ASCII data ﬁles from the subcanopy towers are found here:
 https://wdh.energy.gov/project/ttp under the IDs WREF 3D Sonic Anemometer - Tower A / Raw Data
 ttp/wref.sonic.tower-a.00 (same naming convention for towers b, c, and d)

Additional Instruments:

The subcanopy tower deployment followed a larger ﬁeld campaign at Wind River called the Lidar
Experiments for Assessing Flow over Forests (LEAFF). For LEAFF, Doppler proﬁling (shown at bottom) and
scanning lidar were deployed at the site to measure wind and turbulence over the forest canopy.
