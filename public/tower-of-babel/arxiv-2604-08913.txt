Connections Between Determinantal Point Processes and Gramians in Control Journal: Automatica
Mohamad H. Kazma, Ahmad F. Taha

Abstract
Determinantal point processes (DPPs) are probability models over subsets of a ground set that favor diverse selections while suppressing redundancy. That is, they tend to assign higher likelihood to collections whose elements complement one another instead of repeating the same information. For example, in recommendation systems, a DPP prefers showing users several relevant items that differ in content or style, rather than many near-duplicates of essentially the same item. Although DPPs have been studied extensively in machine learning, random matrix theory, and popularized through components of YouTube’s search recommendation system, they have not been considered in the context of dynamic systems; time domain analysis is not a feature of DPPs. This paper establishes interesting connections between DPPs and control theory. By showing that the observability (controllability) Gramian parameterized by sensor (control) node subsets is a DPP, we provide a probabilistic and spectral perspective on sensor (actuator) selection for linear dynamic systems. This notion of probability here does not represent stochastic uncertainty in the system dynamics; it instead represents a likelihood measure over sensor (actuator) configurations induced by the Gramian. To that end, we derive an effective observable rank condition, characterize the balance between individual node contributions and diversity, and establish node inclusion monotonicity and negative dependence properties. Finally, we show that this formulation recovers classical greedy optimization guarantees and admits a maximum a posteriori interpretation of the sensor/actuator node selection problem. Numerical case studies on three network topologies corroborate the theoretical results. The broader objective and impact of this paper is facilitating research at the intersection of two seemingly unrelated fields, and potentially guiding solving difficult control problems via the more scalable DPPs that naturally incorporates diversity while eliminating redundancy in set selections.

Contributions.
The paper contributions are as follows.

•

We show that the observability Gramian of an LTI system satisfies the conditions of a DPP, such that each sensor configuration is assigned a probability proportional to the determinant of the corresponding Gramian submatrix. This connection casts the sensor node selection problem as probabilistic subset selection, where configurations with near orthogonal observability directions are assigned higher likelihood and configurations with redundant measurements are suppressed. We further decompose the DPP probability to offer a geometric interpretation of this selection problem.

•

We derive several properties from the aforementioned result. Specifically,
(i)
we introduce an effective observable rank that quantifies how many observability modes contribute under a given sensor configuration;
(ii)
we prove that improving system observability monotonically increases the inclusion probability of every sensor configuration;
(iii)
we show that the complement of the selected node set characterizes the unobserved portion of the state-space, and
(iv)
that the determinantal structure suppresses redundant node selection. These properties offer probabilistic insights for the sensor node selection problem that is not captured by existing methods.

•

We recover the submodularity of the node selection problem using the Schur complement and reformulate the problem as a DPP maximum a posteriori (MAP) problem. This allows for greedy approximation guarantees while providing a probabilistic interpretation resulting in near optimal configurations instead of one deterministic set. We validate the proposed framework on three network topologies.

2.2
Determinantal point processes

A DPP is a probability distribution over subsets of a ground set
\mathcal{Y}=\{1,\ldots,p\}
in which the inclusion probabilities are governed by determinants of a positive semidefinite matrix
[
15
]
. The L-ensemble parameterization defines subset probabilities by principal minors of a positive semidefinite kernel
\boldsymbol{L}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}(Y)\propto\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
. The following definitions formalize the L-ensemble and its marginal kernel.

Definition
1

(
L-ensemble
[
15
]
)
.

Given a positive semidefinite matrix
\boldsymbol{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
, the
L-ensemble
DPP defines a probability measure
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
for any subset
Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
on
2^{\mathcal{Y}}
by

\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}(Y):=\frac{\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})}{\sum_{Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y}}\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})}=\frac{\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})}{\det(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})},\quad Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y},

(7)

where
\boldsymbol{L}_{Y}:=[\boldsymbol{L}_{ij}]_{i,j\in Y}
is the principal submatrix indexed by
Y
,
\boldsymbol{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
is the identity matrix, and
\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{\emptyset})=1
by convention.

The normalizing constant in
(
7
)
follows from
\sum_{Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y}}\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})=\det(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})
; please refer to
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
for the proof.
The L-ensemble encodes the likelihood of each subset
Y
through the determinant of the corresponding submatrix. Matrix
\boldsymbol{L}\succeq\boldsymbol{0}
admits a Gram factorization
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{G}^{\top}\boldsymbol{G}
for some
\boldsymbol{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times p}
with columns
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{p}
, where
\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
equals the squared volume of the parallelepiped spanned by
\{\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\}_{i\in Y}

[
25
]
. Near orthogonal columns span a large parallelepiped, yielding high probability, while nearly collinear columns result in smaller
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}(Y)
. The matrix
\boldsymbol{L}
captures two attributes of each node. The first is
quality
, measured by diagonal entries
\boldsymbol{L}_{ii}=\|\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\|^{2}
, which quantify the strength of node
i
. The second is
diversity
, measured by entries
\boldsymbol{L}_{ij}=\boldsymbol{g}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{g}_{j}
, which quantify the similarity between nodes
i
and
j
. Subsets that are simultaneously high quality and mutually diverse receive the highest probability under
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
. The normalizing constant
\det(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})
aggregates the squared volumes of all
2^{p}
possible subsets, ensuring that
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
is a valid probability measure on the power set
2^{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Definition
2

(
Marginal kernel
[
15
]
)
.

The marginal kernel of an L-ensemble DPP is defined as

\boldsymbol{K}:=\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}=\boldsymbol{I}-(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}.

(8)

For any
A\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
, we have
\mathbb{P}(A\subseteq Y)=\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{A})
. If
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p}
are the eigenvalues of
\boldsymbol{L}
, then
\boldsymbol{K}
has eigenvalues
\kappa_{i}=\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i}+1)\in[0,1)
. The marginal kernel
\boldsymbol{K}
gives the node inclusion probabilities induced by the DPP. In particular,
\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}=\mathbb{P}(i\in Y)
is the marginal probability that item
i
is included in a random subset drawn from
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
. Nodes with large kernel values
\boldsymbol{L}_{ii}
(high quality) have
\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}
close to one, while nodes with small values have
\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}
close to zero. Furthermore,
\boldsymbol{K}_{ij}
determines how likely nodes
i
and
j
are to be included together in a subset. The joint inclusion probability of nodes
i
and
j
satisfies
\mathbb{P}(i,j\in Y)=\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}\boldsymbol{K}_{jj}-\boldsymbol{K}_{ij}^{2}
, such that
\boldsymbol{K}_{ij}^{2}
quantifies the repulsion between nodes
i
and
j
(large when
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}
and
\boldsymbol{g}_{j}
are nearly collinear and vanishing when they are orthogonal). The eigenvalue mapping
\kappa_{i}=\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i}+1)
compresses the spectrum of
\boldsymbol{L}
into the interval
[0,1)
. In the DPP sampling algorithm
[
10
,
15
]
, the
i
-th eigenvector of
\boldsymbol{L}
can be selected independently with probability
\kappa_{i}
, where the expected cardinality of the selected subset is given as
\mathbb{E}[|Y|]=\trace(\boldsymbol{K})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\kappa_{i}
, which counts the number of effectively activated modes.

Furthermore, DPPs satisfy a negative dependence relation where for any
S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
with
S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\emptyset
, the probability of including both
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
is upper bounded by the product of their individual inclusion probabilities as follows

\mathbb{P}(S_{1}\cup S_{2}\subseteq Y)\leq\mathbb{P}(S_{1}\subseteq Y)\cdot\mathbb{P}(S_{2}\subseteq Y).

(9)

Inequality
(
9
)
follows from the determinantal structure of the marginal kernel given by
\mathbb{P}(A\subseteq Y)=\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{A})
satisfying
\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S_{1}\cup S_{2}})\leq\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S_{1}})\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S_{2}})
whenever
S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\emptyset

[
18
]
. Under independent selection the inequality holds with equality.
Negative dependence is therefore the probabilistic mechanism through which the DPP enforces diversity, whereby, each additional item included in
Y
reduces the probability of including similar items. In
Section
3
, we exploit this determinantal structure by constructing the L-ensemble matrix directly from the observability Gramian, such that the repulsive structure of the DPP aligns with the geometric redundancy among nodes.

In this section, we establish that the observability Gramian

is a valid L-ensemble DPP matrix. We then derive the marginal kernel
\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}(\boldsymbol{W}_{o}+\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}

\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}
gives the inclusion probability of node

3.1
Gramians and DPPs

We consider systems in which each node represents a state variable of the LTI system
(
1
)
, where the observability Gramian
\boldsymbol{W}_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a matrix whose
(i,j)
entry quantifies the inner product of the observability contributions of states
i
and
j
over the horizon
N
. The following theorem shows that
\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
itself serves as a valid L-ensemble DPP matrix, and that the DPP probability of any node subset

is proportional to
\det((\boldsymbol{W}_{o})_{Y})

Theorem
1

(
Gramian as a DPP
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{L}:=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with
\mathcal{Y}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then
\boldsymbol{L}\succeq\boldsymbol{0}
, the diagonal entry
\boldsymbol{L}_{ii}=(\boldsymbol{W}_{o})_{ii}
quantifies the individual observability energy of node
i
, the off-diagonal entry
\boldsymbol{L}_{ij}=(\boldsymbol{W}_{o})_{ij}
measures the observability correlation between nodes
i
and
j
, and the L-ensemble
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
satisfies, for every
Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
,

\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}(Y)=\frac{\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})}{\det(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})},

where
\boldsymbol{L}_{Y}:=[(\boldsymbol{W}_{o})_{ij}]_{i,j\in Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{|Y|\times|Y|}
is the principal submatrix of
\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
indexed by
Y
.

Proof.

Since
\boldsymbol{W}_{o}=\boldsymbol{O}^{\top}\boldsymbol{O}
, positive semidefiniteness
\boldsymbol{L}\succeq\boldsymbol{0}
follows immediately. For any
Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
,
\boldsymbol{L}_{Y}
is the corresponding principal submatrix, which is also positive semidefinite. The L-ensemble probability
(
7
)
with kernel
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
yields
(
10
)
directly. For the normalizing identity, write
\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I}=\boldsymbol{I}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{l}_{i}\boldsymbol{e}_{i}^{\top}
, where
\boldsymbol{l}_{i}
is the
i
-th column of
\boldsymbol{L}
and
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
is the
i
-th standard basis vector. Applying the matrix determinant lemma inductively (equivalently, expanding
\det(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})
by multilinearity of the determinant in each column
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}+\boldsymbol{l}_{i}
) yields
\det(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})=\sum_{Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y}}\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
, where the sum ranges over all
2^{n}
subsets and
\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{\emptyset})=1

[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
. This concludes the proof.

\blacksquare

Theorem
1
establishes that the observability Gramian directly defines a valid DPP over the node index set
\mathcal{Y}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. While this result is trivial, obtained by setting
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
, this connection provides a probabilistic interpretation of node selection. The DPP measure
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
assigns higher probability to node subsets

for which the principal submatrix
\boldsymbol{L}_{Y}
has a large determinant, equivalently, to configurations that are both observable and diverse. We note that Equation
(

)
does not introduce randomness in the system dynamics; it assigns a deterministic nonnegative weight
\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
to each subset

. The normalization in
(

)
, equivalently
\det(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})=\sum_{Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y}}\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
, converts these weights into a probability mass function over the subsets of ground set

.

is the normalized observability Gramian of the configuration

The index

\mathcal{D}(Y)=0
when these directions are linearly dependent.
Definition

3.2
The marginal kernel as a modal observability filter

The eigenvectors
\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{v}_{n}
, defined as the columns of
\boldsymbol{V}
in the eigendecomposition
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{V}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{V}^{\top}
, define the
observability modes
of the system. The corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0
measure the energy concentrated in each mode. The marginal kernel
\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}
preserves these modal directions while mapping each eigenvalue through
\lambda_{i}\mapsto\kappa_{i}=\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i}+1)\in[0,1)
; it converts unbounded Gramian energy into bounded activation probabilities accordingly.

Theorem
2

(
Marginal kernel and modal activation
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
be the DPP matrix with eigendecomposition
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{V}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{V}^{\top}
, where
\boldsymbol{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is orthogonal,
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
, and
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0
. Then, the marginal kernel written as

\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{L}+\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}=\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{diag}\!\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+1},\ldots,\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n}+1}\right)\boldsymbol{V}^{\top}

(13)

has the following properties.

(i)

The diagonal entry
\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}=\mathbb{P}(i\in Y)
is the marginal probability that node
i
is included in a random subset drawn from
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
.

(ii)

The eigenvalues
\kappa_{i}\hskip-2.84544pt=\hskip-2.84544pt\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i}+1)\hskip-2.84544pt\in\hskip-2.84544pt[0,1)
are the inclusion probabilities of the
i
-th
observability mode
, where mode
i
is selected independently with probability
\kappa_{i}
.

(iii)

The expected number of selected nodes is given by

\mathbb{E}[|Y|]=\trace(\boldsymbol{K})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}+1},

(14)

and the variance is
\mathrm{Var}[|Y|]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i}+1)^{2}
.

Proof.

With
\boldsymbol{L}\succeq\boldsymbol{0}
, this orthogonal diagonalization holds even when eigenvalues are repeated, and given that
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{V}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{V}^{\top}
, applying the scalar function
f(\lambda)=\lambda/(\lambda+1)
to the spectrum gives
\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{V}\,f(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})\,\boldsymbol{V}^{\top}
, which yields the spectral form
(
13
)
. For
(i)
, by the definition of the marginal kernel
[
15
]
,
\mathbb{P}(i\in Y)=\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{\{i\}})=\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}
. For
(ii)
, the DPP sampling algorithm
[
10
,
15
]
selects eigenvector
\boldsymbol{v}_{i}
independently with probability
\kappa_{i}=\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i}+1)
via a Bernoulli trial, and then projects the selected eigenvectors onto node coordinates. For
(iii)
, linearity of expectation gives
\mathbb{E}[|Y|]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(i\in Y)=\trace(\boldsymbol{K})
, and since the mode selections are independent Bernoulli variables,
\mathrm{Var}[|Y|]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}(1-\kappa_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i}+1)^{2}
. This concludes the proof.

\blacksquare

From the above theorem, the expected cardinality
(
14
)
defines an
effective observable rank
as
n_{\mathrm{eff}}:=\mathbb{E}[|Y|]=\trace(\boldsymbol{K})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}+1}
, which counts the number of observability modes that are significantly activated. Modes with large eigenvalues contribute
\kappa_{i}\approx 1
, while modes with small eigenvalues contribute
\kappa_{i}\approx 0
. As such,
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
satisfies
0\leq n_{\mathrm{eff}}\leq\rank(\boldsymbol{L})
and quantifies the effective dimension of the observable subspace. The classical rank condition
\rank(\boldsymbol{W}_{o})
indicates only whether the system is observable or not, whereas the full eigenvalue spectrum
\{\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{W}_{o})\}
provides a vector of values with no single scalar that reflects how many modes carry significant energy under such node selection formulation. The effective observable rank
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
characterizes the expected number of nodes selected by the DPP. The following corollary complements this by quantifying how the inclusion probability of a single node changes once a set of nodes has already been selected.

Corollary
1

(
Conditional inclusion
)
.

Let
S\subset\mathcal{Y}
satisfy
\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S})>0
, and let
j\notin S
. The conditional inclusion probability is

\mathbb{P}(j\in Y\mid S\subseteq Y)=\boldsymbol{K}_{jj}-\boldsymbol{K}_{j,S}\,\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{-1}\,\boldsymbol{K}_{S,j},

(15)

where
\boldsymbol{K}_{j,S}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times|S|}
and
\boldsymbol{K}_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{|S|\times|S|}
are the corresponding submatrices of
\boldsymbol{K}
.

Proof.

By the inclusion property (
Definition
2
),
\mathbb{P}(S\cup\{j\}\subseteq Y)=\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S\cup\{j\}})
and
\mathbb{P}(S\subseteq Y)=\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S})
. By Bayes’ rule,
\mathbb{P}(j\in Y\mid S\subseteq Y)=\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S\cup\{j\}})/\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S})
, which equals the Schur complement of
\boldsymbol{K}_{jj}
in
\boldsymbol{K}_{S\cup\{j\}}
with respect to
\boldsymbol{K}_{S}

[
15
]
. This yields
(
15
)
and concludes the proof.

\blacksquare

Equation
(
15
)
quantifies how the inclusion probability of node
j
decreases as more nodes are selected. The term
\boldsymbol{K}_{j,S}\,\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{-1}\,\boldsymbol{K}_{S,j}
measures the observability correlation of node
j
with the already selected set
S
as encoded in
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
. When the Gramian row of node
j
is nearly a linear combination of the rows indexed by
S
, the conditional probability drops. This is relevant in practice when additional sensor nodes are to be selected after an initial set
S
has been chosen.

4
Inclusion Properties and Node Selection

Having established the Gramian DPP relation and the marginal kernel, we now derive properties of the DPP that have direct implications for sensor node selection. We then recover the submodularity of
\mathrm{log}\mathrm{det}
from a geometric argument and reformulate
Problem
1
as a MAP problem.

4.1
Inclusion monotonicity and node redundancy

The first result establishes that if one system is more observable than another in the positive semidefinite sense, then the DPP assigns a higher inclusion probability to every sensor configuration under the more observable system.

Corollary
2

(
Inclusion monotonicity
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{L}^{(1)}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}^{(1)}
and
\boldsymbol{L}^{(2)}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}^{(2)}
. If
\boldsymbol{W}_{o}^{(1)}\preceq\boldsymbol{W}_{o}^{(2)}
, then for every sensor configuration
S\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
, the inclusion probability under the more observable system is at least as large,

\mathbb{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}^{(1)}}(S\subseteq Y)\leq\mathbb{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}^{(2)}}(S\subseteq Y),\quad\forall\,S\subseteq\mathcal{Y},

(16)

where
\mathbb{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}^{(i)}}(S\subseteq Y)=\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(i)})
is the probability that the configuration
S
is included in a random subset
Y
drawn from the DPP with kernel
\boldsymbol{L}^{(i)}
.

Proof.

The function
f(\lambda)=\lambda/(\lambda+1)
is operator monotone on
[0,\infty)
. Therefore
\boldsymbol{L}^{(1)}\preceq\boldsymbol{L}^{(2)}
implies

\boldsymbol{K}^{(1)}=f\!\left(\boldsymbol{L}^{(1)}\right)\preceq f\!\left(\boldsymbol{L}^{(2)}\right)=\boldsymbol{K}^{(2)},

such that
\boldsymbol{0}\preceq\boldsymbol{K}^{(1)}\preceq\boldsymbol{K}^{(2)}\preceq\boldsymbol{I}
. For any
S\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
, the principal submatrix retains the ordering
\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)}\preceq\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(2)}
. If
\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)}
is singular, then
\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)})=0\leq\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(2)})
. Otherwise,
\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)}
is nonsingular, then congruence by
(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)})^{-1/2}
applied to
\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(2)}-\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)}\succeq\boldsymbol{0}
gives
\big(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)}\big)^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(2)}\big(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)}\big)^{-1/2}\succeq\boldsymbol{I}
. Thus,
\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(2)})\geq\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)})
. Now for both cases,
\mathbb{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}^{(1)}}(S\subseteq Y)=\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(1)})\leq\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S}^{(2)})=\mathbb{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}^{(2)}}(S\subseteq Y)
; this yields
(
16
)
and concludes the proof.

\blacksquare

Corollary
2
establishes a form of
stochastic dominance
for the Gramian DPP. Stochastic dominance of one distribution over another means that for every event, the probability under the dominating distribution is at least as large. Here,
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}^{(2)}}
dominates
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}^{(1)}}
in the sense that every sensor configuration
S\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
has at least as high an inclusion probability under the larger kernel. The ordering
\boldsymbol{L}^{(1)}\preceq\boldsymbol{L}^{(2)}
holds whenever the Gramian of system 2 dominates that of system 1. This is evident whenever we modify
(\boldsymbol{A},\boldsymbol{C})
to improve observability, thereby monotonically increasing the inclusion probabilities of all configurations. The next result introduces a direct interpretation of the unselected nodes as a DPP with kernel
\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{K}
, which characterizes what is unobserved by the selected configuration.

Corollary
3

(
Complement characterization
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
and let
Y\sim\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
be the random subset of selected sensor nodes. The complement
\bar{Y}:=\mathcal{Y}\setminus Y
, representing the unselected nodes, is itself a DPP with marginal kernel
\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{K}
, where
\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}(\boldsymbol{W}_{o}+\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}
. That is, the complement inclusion probability satisfies

\mathbb{P}(\bar{Y}\supseteq S)=\det\big((\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{K})_{S}\big),\quad\forall\,S\subseteq\mathcal{Y}.

(17)

The diagonal entry
(\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{K})_{ii}=1-\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}
is the probability that node
i
is excluded from the selected set.

Proof.

Since
\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}(\boldsymbol{W}_{o}+\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}
, the eigenvalues of
\boldsymbol{K}
satisfy
\kappa_{i}=\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i}+1)\in[0,1)
, so
\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{K}
is positive semidefinite with eigenvalues
1-\kappa_{i}=(\lambda_{i}+1)^{-1}\in(0,1]
. By the complement property of DPPs
[
25
]
, if
Y
is a DPP with marginal kernel
\boldsymbol{K}
, then
\bar{Y}
is a DPP with marginal kernel
\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{K}
, yielding
(
17
)
.

\blacksquare

The above corollary provides a characterization of the unobserved portion of the state-space. At the node level, exclusion is quantified by
1-\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}
. In the modal basis, the complement kernel has eigenvalues
1-\kappa_{i}=(\lambda_{i}+1)^{-1}
, so modes with large observability eigenvalues have small modal exclusion probabilities, while weak modes remain largely unobserved. The kernel
\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{K}
therefore quantifies the residual observability that remains unobserved by the selected sensor configuration.

Corollary
4

(
Negative dependence
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
and
Y\sim\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
. For any two disjoint sensor configurations
S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
, the joint inclusion probability satisfies

\mathbb{P}(S_{1}\cup S_{2}\subseteq Y)\leq\mathbb{P}(S_{1}\subseteq Y)\cdot\mathbb{P}(S_{2}\subseteq Y).

(18)

In particular, for any two nodes
i\neq j
,

\mathbb{P}(i,j\in Y)=\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}\boldsymbol{K}_{jj}-\boldsymbol{K}_{ij}^{2}\leq\mathbb{P}(i\in Y)\cdot\mathbb{P}(j\in Y),

where the gap
\boldsymbol{K}_{ij}^{2}
increases with the observability correlation between nodes
i
and
j
.

Proof.

By the inclusion property,
\mathbb{P}(A\subseteq Y)=\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{A})
for any
A\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
. For disjoint
S_{1},S_{2}
, Fischer’s inequality for positive semidefinite matrices gives
\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S_{1}\cup S_{2}})\leq\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S_{1}})\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{S_{2}})

[
18
]
, which yields
(
18
)
. The pairwise identity follows from
\det(\boldsymbol{K}_{\{i,j\}})=\boldsymbol{K}_{ii}\boldsymbol{K}_{jj}-\boldsymbol{K}_{ij}^{2}
.

\blacksquare

The aforementioned result implies that the DPP
avoids redundancy
by construction. Selecting node
i
reduces the probability of also selecting node
j
in proportion to the squared off-diagonal correlation
\boldsymbol{K}_{ij}^{2}
. Nodes whose Gramian rows are nearly proportional, such that
(\boldsymbol{W}_{o})_{ij}^{2}\approx(\boldsymbol{W}_{o})_{ii}(\boldsymbol{W}_{o})_{jj}
, are strongly repelled. The DPP framework enables sampling without redundancy as compared to independent sampling that does not account for diversity.

Based on the above results, a probabilistic interpretation of node selection is provided. The system dynamics and the Gramian remain deterministic; the probability model enters through the DPP measure in
(
7
)
and its Gramian form in
(
10
)
, which define a distribution over subsets
Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
. This interpretation supports solving the combinatorial
Problem
1
by leveraging DPP properties to characterize optimal node configurations.

4.2
Submodularity and MAP reformulation

We now show that the
\mathrm{log}\mathrm{det}
objective inherits a diminishing returns property from the DPP matrix geometry. The following lemma establishes its submodularity and monotonicity.

Lemma
1

(
Submodularity and monotonicity
)
.

The set function
f(Y):=\log\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
is submodular on
\mathcal{Y}
. That is, for any
A\subseteq B\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
and
j\notin B
,

f(A\cup\{j\})-f(A)\geq f(B\cup\{j\})-f(B).

(19)

Moreover, if
\boldsymbol{L}\succeq\boldsymbol{I}
, then
f
is monotone nondecreasing, that is,
f(A)\leq f(A\cup\{j\})
for all
A\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
and
j\notin A
.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{G}^{\top}\boldsymbol{G}
with columns
\{\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{Y}}
. For
S\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
and
j\notin S
, the marginal gain can be written as

f(S\cup\{j\})-f(S)=\log h_{j}^{2}(S),

where
h_{j}^{2}(S):=(\boldsymbol{L})_{jj}-\boldsymbol{L}_{j,S}\boldsymbol{L}_{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{L}_{S,j}
is the Schur complement of
\boldsymbol{L}_{S}
in
\boldsymbol{L}_{S\cup\{j\}}

[
15
]
. Equivalently,

h_{j}^{2}(S)=\left\|(\boldsymbol{I}-\Pi_{S})\boldsymbol{g}_{j}\right\|_{2}^{2}
,
with
\Pi_{S}
the orthogonal projector onto
\mathrm{span}\{\boldsymbol{g}_{i}:i\in S\}
. If
A\subseteq B
, then
\mathrm{span}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{span}(B)
. Hence
\|(\boldsymbol{I}-\Pi_{B})\boldsymbol{g}_{j}\|_{2}\leq\|(\boldsymbol{I}-\Pi_{A})\boldsymbol{g}_{j}\|_{2}
, and
h_{j}(B)\leq h_{j}(A)
and
(
19
)
follows. For monotonicity, we have
\boldsymbol{L}\succeq\boldsymbol{I}
by definition. Then
\boldsymbol{L}_{S\cup\{j\}}-\boldsymbol{I}\succeq\boldsymbol{0}
, and by Schur complement we obtain
h_{j}^{2}(S)-1\geq 0
. Therefore
\log h_{j}^{2}(S)\geq 0
for all
S\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
and
j\notin S
; this concludes the proof.

\blacksquare

Lemma
1
recovers the submodularity of
\mathrm{log}\mathrm{det}(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
from a geometric argument. The marginal gain of adding node
j
equals the log Schur complement of
\boldsymbol{L}_{A}
in
\boldsymbol{L}_{A\cup\{j\}}
, which decreases as more nodes are added to subset
A
; this exhibits the diminishing returns property that defines submodularity.

Based on the results established above, the node selection
Problem
1
can be reformulated as a
k
-DPP MAP inference problem. Restricting the process to subsets of fixed cardinality
|Y|=r
yields the
k
-DPP, which can be sampled efficiently using elementary symmetric polynomial recursions
[
15
]
. The MAP problem of the
k
-DPP is equivalent to finding the most likely subset of size
r
. While this problem is NP-hard, greedy and local search heuristics are used in practice
[
8
]
. The following proposition states the MAP reformulation of
Problem
1
.

Proposition
1

(
k
-DPP MAP reformulation
)
.

For
\boldsymbol{L}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
and a budget
r<n
, the
k
-DPP MAP problem is

Y^{\star}=\argmax_{Y\subseteq\mathcal{Y},\,|Y|=r}\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}^{r}(Y),

(20)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}^{r}(Y)=\frac{\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})}{e_{r}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})}
and
e_{r}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})=\sum_{|Y^{\prime}|=r}\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y^{\prime}})
is the
r
-th elementary symmetric polynomial of the eigenvalues of
\boldsymbol{L}
. Since
e_{r}
is a positive constant independent of
Y
, solving
(
20
)
is equivalent to solving
Problem
1
.

Proof.

For fixed
r
, the denominator
e_{r}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
is a positive constant independent of
Y
, therefore maximizing
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}^{r}(Y)
is equivalent to maximizing
\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
. Since
\log(\cdot)
is strictly increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, this is equivalent to maximizing
\mathrm{log}\mathrm{det}((\boldsymbol{L}_{Y}))
. This completes the proof.

\blacksquare

The
k
-DPP is the cardinality constrained counterpart of the L-ensemble (
Definition
1
); it preserves the repulsive structure of the DPP while restricting to subsets of exactly
r
nodes. By
Proposition
1
, maximizing
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{L}}^{r}(Y)
over subsets of size
r
reduces to maximizing
\mathrm{log}\mathrm{det}((\boldsymbol{W}_{o})_{Y})
. As a result of
\mathrm{log}\mathrm{det}(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
being monotone submodular (
Lemma
1
), the greedy algorithm that sequentially adds the node with the largest marginal gain yields a
(1-1/e)
approximation
[
20
]
.

While a single MAP solution can be obtained greedily, the same formulation yields a distribution over all subsets of size
r
through the
k
-DPP. This enables sampling many feasible sensor configurations, where each sample is generally suboptimal relative to the MAP set, yet is weighted by
\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
and therefore concentrates on diverse subsets. Exact
k
-DPP sampling runs in
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}+nr^{2})
time via the spectral algorithm in
[
15
]
, which combines an eigendecomposition step with elementary symmetric polynomial recursion and sequential node sampling. This, along with the greedy guarantee for a single MAP solution (
Lemma
1
), provides a computationally tractable way to generate an ensemble of diverse candidate sensor configurations.
Furthermore, when the marginal kernel in
(
8
)
is parameterized through a structured low rank approximation, DPP inference and parameter learning can be performed with complexity that can be sublinear in the number of nodes
[
5
]
. For large scale systems with
\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{W}_{o}(\boldsymbol{W}_{o}+\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}
, a small effective observable rank in
(
14
)
indicates that a few observability modes dominate, which supports low rank approximations of
\boldsymbol{W}_{o}
for sublinear in
n
computations and efficient solution of
(
20
)
with diverse sensor configurations for large networks.

Furthermore, Equation
(
20
)
admits a continuous relaxation through the multilinear extension
F(\boldsymbol{x}):=\log\det(\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{x})(\boldsymbol{L}-\boldsymbol{I})+\boldsymbol{I})
, where
\boldsymbol{x}\in[0,1]^{n}
and
F(\boldsymbol{1}_{Y})=\log\det(\boldsymbol{L}_{Y})
at every vertex
[
8
]
. Under the cardinality constraint
\sum_{i}x_{i}=r

6
Paper Summary

This paper is first to establish connections between Gramians in control and DPPs; it shows that the observability Gramian under sensor selection parameterization of a discrete-time LTI system is a valid DPP. By casting sensor node selection as probabilistic subset selection under a
\mathrm{log}\mathrm{det}
measure, we derive a marginal kernel result, an effective observable rank that quantifies how many observability modes are activated under a specific sensor configuration, a decomposition of the DPP probability that separates individual node observability energy from node diversity, and an inclusion monotonicity result. We conclude by validating the results on three networks. As a result of the probabilistic interpretation of the proposed framework, solving the sensor and actuator selection problem for time varying and nonlinear systems is a promising future work direction which can be achieved by considering empirical Gramians as the DPP matrix, and thus analogous results to those established here can be derived.
