Stacky geometry and logarithmic topology of transversely affine foliations
Pedro Barbassa, Gabriel Barbosa, Maurício Corrêa

Abstract
. We study the stacky and logarithmic-topological structures associated with transversely affine affine foliations. To such a foliation we attach its holonomy group and the corresponding quotient stack, which provides the natural geometric base for the multiplicative developing coordinate. Holomorphic and meromorphic reparametrisations are then identified with endomorphisms of this stack and of its compactification, yielding a geometric Singer-type theorem. We then classify these reparametrisations according to the geometry of the holonomy group. On the logarithmic-topological side, we pass to the Kato–Nakayama space, where the residues define canonical boundary characters, govern the induced linear dynamics on the boundary tori, and give rise to a canonical logarithmic lift of the developing map. In this way, the quotient stack controls the linear part of the theory, while the Kato–Nakayama space captures its logarithmic-topological and dynamical content.



1.
Introduction

Singer’s classical Theorem on Liouvillian first integrals points to a strong rigidity phenomenon in the transversely affine setting: the relevant transcendental operations are generated by quadratures, exponentials of primitives, and algebraic extensions
[
29
]
. In the present geometric setting, it is natural to recast this pattern in terms of closed logarithmic
1
-forms, monodromy representations, and developing maps
[
4
,
13
]
. From this perspective, a first integral, or more generally a transverse developing object, may be viewed as defining a fibration over an appropriate target, and Singer-type operations may then be interpreted as base changes or endomorphisms of that target. The purpose of the present article is to develop such a geometric counterpart for transversely affine foliations, combining a stack-theoretic description of the multiplicative developing coordinate and its reparametrisation theory with a logarithmic-topological description on the Kato–Nakayama space. The latter is not merely an auxiliary topological construction: it is the natural setting in which the logarithmic boundary acquires a concrete geometric and dynamical form. There the residues define canonical boundary characters, the pull-back of the logarithmic form induces a real foliation of codimension two, and the boundary torus fibres carry linear dynamics whose topology, closure, and asymptotic behaviour are governed by the residue data. In this way, the Kato–Nakayama space provides the boundary-dynamical side of the theory and makes visible features that do not appear at the level of the interior developing map alone.

More precisely, let
(\omega,\eta)
be transversely affine data on
U
, so that

d\omega=\eta\wedge\omega

and

d\eta=0

[
4
,
26
]
. On the universal cover
p:\widetilde{U}\to U
, the closed form
p^{*}\eta
admits a primitive
\Phi
, and hence a multiplicative developing coordinate

z=e^{\Phi}

satisfying

dz/z=p^{*}\eta.

At the same time, the form
e^{-\Phi}p^{*}\omega
is closed, so that its primitives define the affine developing data. Their analytic continuation is governed by the affine monodromy representation

\xi:\pi_{1}(U)\to\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{*}\ltimes\mathbb{C},

whose linear part is the character

\rho:\pi_{1}(U)\to\mathbb{C}^{*}.

Writing

\Gamma=\mathrm{im}(\rho),

the quotient stack

[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]

is the natural target for the multiplicative developing coordinate: it records the linear monodromy and organises the residual reparametrisation freedom. By contrast, the translational part of
\xi
survives only as an extension class, so that the full affine theory enters here chiefly as a comparison structure; see
Propositions

3.9
and
3.10
.

The second point of view begins once one passes from the interior to the logarithmic boundary. The Kato–Nakayama space
X^{\log}
is locally a real oriented blow-up of the divisor, with torus fibres over the boundary strata; see
Definitions

7.2
and
7.1
. On these fibres, the residues of
\eta
define canonical boundary characters, while the pull-back
\pi^{*}\eta
induces a real codimension-two foliation on
X^{\log}
itself. The restriction of this foliation to the boundary tori is linear in the angular variables, and its behaviour is controlled by the arithmetic of the residue vector: according to the rational relations among the residues, one obtains closed leaves, Kronecker-type dense leaves, or leaves dense in a proper subtorus. At the same time, the radial part of the same logarithmic data distinguishes attracting, repelling, and purely angular boundary components. In this way, the Kato–Nakayama space furnishes the logarithmic-topological counterpart to the stacky picture: Kummer refinements remove torsion in the meridional monodromy, whereas
X^{\log}
retains the angular and dynamical content of the same residue data.

We prove in
Proposition

2.4
that the holonomy group
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
is generated by the exponentials of the logarithmic residues together with the exponentials of a finite generating system of periods. Starting from the multiplicative developing coordinate on the universal cover, we construct the canonical stacky developing morphism

F:U\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]

in
Proposition

3.7
. After introducing the quotient-stack formalism and the strictification of endomorphisms in
Proposition

3.5
, we prove in
Proposition

3.15
that every holomorphic stacky first integral for the same transversely affine foliation is obtained, up to
2
-isomorphism, by post-composition of
F
with an endomorphism of the stacky base. This picture is enlarged in
Theorem

4.5
, where Kummer, or root-stack, refinements are interpreted as the geometric counterpart of Singer’s algebraic-extension step.

The classification of the resulting reparametrisations is then carried out regime by regime. The structure of finitely generated subgroups of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
and the corresponding proper or non-proper dichotomy are analysed in
Propositions

5.2
and
5.5
. In the non-discrete regimes, semi-equivariance is rigid: holomorphic and meromorphic endomorphisms are necessarily monomial, as shown in
Propositions

5.12
and
5.14
. In the torsion and elliptic regimes, the corresponding descriptions are obtained in
Propositions

5.16
,
5.18
and
5.26
. Finally,
Theorems

6.2
and
6.3
compute the automorphism group of the open stacky base and the compactified automorphism
2
-group.

The logarithmic-topological side is then developed in detail on the Kato–Nakayama space. After establishing the local topology of
X^{\log}
and the boundary characters determined by the residues, we analyse the induced real codimension-two foliation, its local logarithmic normal form, and the linear dynamics it induces on the boundary tori; see
Propositions

7.3
,
7.4
,
7.5
and
7.6

Propositions

7.8

These two sides meet in the logarithmic lift of the developing map. Using the explicit local formula of
Lemma

Theorem

a canonical morphism

extending the interior developing map. Its compactified radial behaviour is described in

Propositions

8.7
,
8.8
and
8.9
and
Theorem

8.10

The logarithmic Betti meaning of this picture is made explicit in

8.12

Taken together,

governs both the transverse stacky quotient and the logarithmic-topological boundary model. The quotient stack
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
controls the linear and reparametrisation-theoretic aspect of the theory, while the Kato–Nakayama space provides the natural topological and dynamical setting in which the boundary geometry of transversely affine foliations becomes visible.

2.
Transversely affine foliations, holonomy, residues, and the matrix model

In this section, we introduce the basic notions of foliations with transversely affine structures, together with their monodromy representation.
Then we study the holonomy group generated by the linear part of the representation
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
and show that it is generated by periods and the exponential of residues.

2.1.
Transversely affine structures as flat meromorphic connections on the normal bundle

We recall the intrinsic (McQuillan–style) definition of a transverse affine structure, following Cousin–Pereira
[
4
]
.
Let
X
be a complex manifold and let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one holomorphic foliation on
X
with normal bundle
N_{\mathcal{F}}
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{F}
is given by a section

\omega\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,N_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}\bigr)

whose zero locus has codimension at least
2
and satisfying
\omega\wedge d\omega=0
.

Definition 2.1

(Singular transverse affine structure as a connection)
.

A
singular transverse affine structure
for
\mathcal{F}
is a flat meromorphic connection

\nabla:N_{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow N_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(*D)

along a reduced divisor
D\subset X
such that
\nabla(\omega)=0
.
We say that
D
is
minimal
if the connection form of
\nabla
is not holomorphic at any point of
D
.

On an open set
U\subset X
where
N_{\mathcal{F}}
is trivial, choosing a trivializing section identifies
\nabla
with a meromorphic
1
-form

\eta_{0}\in\Omega_{X}^{1}(*D)(U)
through

\nabla|_{U}(f)=df+f\,\eta_{0}.

Writing
\omega
in the same trivialization as a meromorphic
1
-form
\omega_{0}
on
U
, the equation
\nabla(\omega)=0
becomes

d\omega_{0}=\omega_{0}\wedge\eta_{0},\qquad d\eta_{0}=0,

and changing trivialization by a nowhere vanishing function
g\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)^{*}
sends

(\omega_{0},\eta_{0})\longmapsto(g\,\omega_{0},\ \eta_{0}-d\log g).

This is exactly the gauge relation used throughout the paper.

2.2.
Affine monodromy and its linear part

Let
U=X\setminus D
and assume that
(\mathcal{F},\nabla)
is transversely affine on
U
in the sense above. Fix a base point
q\in U
. By
[
4
, §2]
, the transverse affine structure determines a monodromy representation

\xi:\pi_{1}(U,q)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{*}\ltimes\mathbb{C},

whose linear part is the multiplicative character

\rho:\pi_{1}(U,q)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
\rho(\gamma)=\exp\!\left(\int_{\gamma}\eta_{0}\right).

This coincides with the monodromy of the rank-one local system defined by
\nabla
.

On any path
\gamma
contained in the regular locus of
(\omega_{0},\eta_{0})
one has

\rho(\gamma)=\bigl\{z\mapsto z\,\exp(\int_{\gamma}\eta_{0})\bigr\},

and

\xi(\gamma)=\bigl\{z\mapsto z\,\exp(\int_{\gamma}\eta_{0})+\int_{\gamma}\exp(\int\eta_{0})\,\omega_{0}\bigr\}.

Thus
\xi
fits into the natural projection

\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{*}\ltimes\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{*},

with
\rho
as its linear part:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(U,q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{*}\ltimes\mathbb{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}

In the present article, the stacky base is attached only to this linear part. Writing

\Gamma:=\mathrm{im}(\rho)\subset\mathbb{C}^{*},

we consider

[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]

and its compactification

[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma].

The translational part of
\xi
defines a
1
-cocycle with values in the
\rho
-twisted additive local system, and therefore belongs to the full affine theory. By contrast, the residual Singer reparametrisation freedom is governed by post-composition on the multiplicative base, hence by endomorphisms of
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
and
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
.

Remark 2.2
.

If the transversely affine structure is trivial, in the sense that
\eta=0
, then all residues are zero and
\Gamma=\{1\}
. So the stacky bases are

\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}.

The Singer reparameterizations are translations induced by deck transformations via
\xi
. In general, reparameterizations are not arbitrary automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, but those coming from the image of
\pi_{1}(U)
under the monodromy.

2.3.
Transversely projective structures and their monodromy

We recall the standard projective formalism (see
[
13
, §2]
and the references therein) in a form compatible with the stack-theoretic viewpoint developed later in this article.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one foliation.
A (singular)
transversely projective structure
on
(X,\mathcal{F})
along a reduced divisor
D
is a rank two vector bundle
E
on
X

together with a flat meromorphic connection

\nabla_{E}:E\longrightarrow E\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(*D),

and a (meromorphic) line subbundle
L\subset E
on
X\setminus Z
(for some analytic subset
Z
of codimension at least
2
)
such that the induced Riccati foliation on the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\mathbb{P}(E)
is tangent to the section
\mathbb{P}(L)
and pulls back to
\mathcal{F}

on
X\setminus Z
.
Equivalently (in a local trivialization), one may encode the projective structure by a triple of meromorphic
1
-forms

(\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
satisfying the structure equations

d\omega_{0}=\omega_{0}\wedge\omega_{1},\qquad d\omega_{1}=2\,\omega_{0}\wedge\omega_{2},\qquad d\omega_{2}=\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2},

with
\mathcal{F}=\ker(\omega_{0})
on the regular locus.

A transverse projective structure yields a projective monodromy representation (defined up to conjugacy)

\xi_{\mathrm{proj}}:\pi_{1}(U,q)\longrightarrow\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad U=X\setminus D,

obtained by parallel transport of the flat connection on
E
and projectivization.

2.4.
Affine structures as reductions

The transverse affine structures of
Definition

2.1
are the transverse projective structures whose projective monodromy
reduces to the affine subgroup

\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{*}\ltimes\mathbb{C}\ \subset\ \mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad z\longmapsto az+b.

Geometrically, this is the case where the induced Riccati foliation on
\mathbb{P}(E)
admits an invariant section at infinity, so that the
natural
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-coordinate can be chosen on the affine chart
\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and the monodromy acts by affine transformations.
In this situation, the
linear part
of the affine monodromy is the character
\rho
of
Section

2.2
, and the stack-theoretic base
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
records the multiplicative developing coordinate, that is, the linear part, rather than the full affine cocycle.

We will return to this inclusion in

: Ogus’ logarithmic Riemann–Hilbert correspondence applies to the flat connection

in

whose projectivization encodes

,

.

2.5.
Gauge invariance

Accordingly, the multiplicative character

\rho:\pi_{1}(U,q)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*},\qquad\rho(\gamma)=\exp\!\left(\int_{\gamma}\eta\right),

is invariant under the usual gauge transformation

\eta\longmapsto\eta+\frac{df}{f},\qquad f\in\mathcal{O}_{U}^{*}.

Indeed, for every loop
\gamma
in
U
, one has

\int_{\gamma}\frac{df}{f}\in 2\pi i\,\mathbb{Z},

since this integral computes the winding number of
f\circ\gamma
around
0
. Therefore

\exp\!\left(\int_{\gamma}\left(\eta+\frac{df}{f}\right)\right)=\exp\!\left(\int_{\gamma}\eta\right)\cdot\exp\!\left(\int_{\gamma}\frac{df}{f}\right)=\exp\!\left(\int_{\gamma}\eta\right),

and hence
\rho
, and therefore also
\Gamma=\operatorname{im}(\rho)
, is unchanged.

The next proposition shows that
\Gamma
is generated by two types of
constants: the local residue exponentials contributed by the irreducible
components of
D
, and the global period exponentials arising from a
chosen generating family of
H_{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that
\eta
extends logarithmically across
D
. For each irreducible component

D_{i}\subset D
, choose a smooth point of
D_{i}
away from the other components of
D
,
and let
\gamma_{i}
be a positively oriented meridian around
D_{i}
, viewed as a based loop
in
U
after choosing a path from the base point
q
to the corresponding transversal disk.
Choose further loops
\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{m}
such that the homology classes

[\gamma_{i}],\ [\delta_{j}]\in H_{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})

generate
H_{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})
as an abelian group. Set

g_{i}:=\rho(\gamma_{i})
,
and
h_{j}:=\rho(\delta_{j}).

Then
\Gamma=\operatorname{im}(\rho)
is generated by the elements
g_{i}
and
h_{j}
. Equivalently,

\Gamma=\left\langle\exp\!\bigl(2\pi i\,\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(\eta)\bigr),\ \exp\!\left(\int_{\delta_{j}}\eta\right)\right\rangle.

Proof.

Since the target
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is abelian, the character
\rho
factors through the abelianization
of
\pi_{1}(U,q)
, hence through
H_{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})
. Equivalently, because
d\eta=0
on
U
, the quantity

\exp\!\left(\int_{\gamma}\eta\right)

depends only on the homology class
[\gamma]\in H_{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})
.
By assumption, the classes
[\gamma_{i}]
and
[\delta_{j}]
generate
H_{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})
. Therefore,
for every class
[\alpha]\in H_{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})
one may write

[\alpha]=\sum_{i}a_{i}[\gamma_{i}]+\sum_{j}b_{j}[\delta_{j}]\qquad(a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}).

Since
\rho
induces a homomorphism on
H_{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})
, it follows that

\rho([\alpha])=\prod_{i}\rho(\gamma_{i})^{a_{i}}\prod_{j}\rho(\delta_{j})^{b_{j}}=\prod_{i}g_{i}^{a_{i}}\prod_{j}h_{j}^{b_{j}}.

Hence
\Gamma=\operatorname{im}(\rho)
is generated by the elements
g_{i}
and
h_{j}
.
For a positively oriented meridian
\gamma_{i}
around
D_{i}
, the residue formula for logarithmic

1
-forms gives

\int_{\gamma_{i}}\eta=2\pi i\,\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(\eta),

and therefore

g_{i}=\rho(\gamma_{i})=\exp\!\bigl(2\pi i\,\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(\eta)\bigr).

Likewise,

h_{j}=\rho(\delta_{j})=\exp\!\left(\int_{\delta_{j}}\eta\right).

This proves the final assertion.
∎

2.6.
Realizable endomorphisms of
\Gamma

Choose the period–residue generating system

(g_{1},\dots,g_{k},h_{1},\dots,h_{m})

of
\Gamma
furnished by
Proposition

2.4
. It determines a surjective homomorphism

\Phi:\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}\longrightarrow\Gamma,\qquad\Phi(n_{1},\dots,n_{k},t_{1},\dots,t_{m})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}^{n_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}h_{j}^{t_{j}},

and hence an exact sequence of abelian groups

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}\xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ }\Gamma\longrightarrow 0,

where
L=\ker(\Phi)
is the subgroup of relations among the chosen generators.

We first describe
\mathrm{End}(\Gamma)
in terms of the presentation (
2.1
). Later we determine which of these abstract endomorphisms are realised by holomorphic semi-equivariant pairs

(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma),
with
\widetilde{\psi}(\gamma z)=\sigma(\gamma)\widetilde{\psi}(z).

The first step is given by the following standard description.

Proposition 2.5
.

There is a natural identification

\mathrm{End}(\Gamma)\cong\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m},L)\big/\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m},L),

where

\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m},L):=\{A\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m})\mid A(L)\subset L\}.

Equivalently, after choosing a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}
, one may describe
\mathrm{End}(\Gamma)
by integer matrices preserving the relation subgroup
L
.
Likewise,
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
is identified with the subgroup of classes represented by elements of
\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m},L)
whose induced endomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}/L
is invertible.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}/L
and
\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}
is a free abelian group, every endomorphism of
\Gamma
lifts to an endomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}
. Any such lift must preserve
L
, and therefore yields an element of
\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m},L)
. In this way one obtains a surjective homomorphism

\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m},L)\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\Gamma).

Its kernel consists of those endomorphisms
A
of
\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}
whose induced map on
\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}/L
is zero. Equivalently, these are the endomorphisms satisfying

A(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m})\subset L.

Such maps are exactly the elements of
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m},L)
. This proves the stated description of
\mathrm{End}(\Gamma)
.
The statement for automorphisms is immediate: a class in

\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m},L)\big/\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{k+m},L)

defines an automorphism of
\Gamma
if and only if the induced endomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{k+m}/L\cong\Gamma
is invertible.
∎

After describing
\mathrm{End}(\Gamma)
abstractly, it remains to determine which endomorphisms are induced by holomorphic semi-equivariant maps on the atlas
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. This is carried out in
Section

5
.

3.
The stacky base, developing maps, and the stacky Singer theorem

This section collects the stack-theoretic material needed for the interaction with the foliation data.
We first introduce the quotient-stack language and the strictification of endomorphisms, then construct the canonical morphism
F:U\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
from the developing map, and finally prove the stacky Singer theorem.
Throughout we follow the Morita/bibundle viewpoint for quotient stacks and orbifolds as in
[
2
,
12
,
15
,
22
]
.

We work on the site
An
of complex manifolds with the analytic étale topology.
All groups are regarded with the discrete topology.

3.1.
Quotient stacks, action groupoids, bibundles and strict pairs

Definition 3.1
.

Let a discrete group
\Gamma
act holomorphically on a complex manifold
Y
on the left.
The quotient stack
[Y/\Gamma]
has objects over
T
given by a principal right
\Gamma
-bundle
P\to T
and a holomorphic map
\phi:P\to Y

satisfying
\phi(p\cdot\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}\phi(p)
.

Definition 3.2
.

The action groupoid
Y\rtimes\Gamma\rightrightarrows Y
has arrows
Y\times\Gamma
with

s(y,\gamma)=y,
and
t(y,\gamma)=\gamma\cdot y.

Its classifying stack is
[Y/\Gamma]
.

For quotient stacks by discrete groups, one may describe morphisms via bibundles (Morita morphisms). In our one-dimensional setting we can also use strict pairs. We follow the Hilsum–Skandalis/bibundle formalism and its Morita invariance; in the one-dimensional free-action case every bibundle morphism is locally represented on the atlas by a semi-equivariant pair
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
, while the bibundle viewpoint remains the invariant global language.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
act on
Y
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
on
Y^{\prime}
.
A
1
-morphism
[Y/\Gamma]\to[Y^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime}]
is represented by:

(1)

a manifold
P
with a principal right
\Gamma
-bundle map
\pi:P\to Y
;

(2)

a commuting left
\Gamma^{\prime}
-action on
P
;

(3)

a
\Gamma^{\prime}
-equivariant holomorphic map
\Phi:P\to Y^{\prime}
.

A
2
-morphism between two such bibundles is a
\Gamma^{\prime}\times\Gamma
-equivariant isomorphism
P\to P^{\prime}
compatible with the structure maps.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
be a discrete group acting on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
by multiplication and consider
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
.
A
strict endomorphism
of
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
is a pair
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
consisting of a holomorphic map

\widetilde{\psi}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
and a group homomorphism
\sigma:\Gamma\to\Gamma
such that

\widetilde{\psi}(\gamma z)=\sigma(\gamma)\,\widetilde{\psi}(z)\qquad\forall\,\gamma\in\Gamma,\ z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}.

Two strict pairs
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
and
(\widetilde{\psi}^{\prime},\sigma)
are
2
-isomorphic
if there exists
a\in\Gamma
with

\widetilde{\psi}^{\prime}(z)=a\,\widetilde{\psi}(z)
for all
z
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Every endomorphism
\psi:[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
is
2
-isomorphic to a strict endomorphism
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
.
Two strict pairs
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
and
(\widetilde{\psi}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
represent
2
-isomorphic endomorphisms if and only if

\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{\psi}^{\prime}=a\,\widetilde{\psi}
for some constant
a\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

Pull back
\psi
along the atlas
\mathbb{C}^{*}\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
.
A morphism of quotient stacks corresponds to a bibundle between action groupoids; over a connected atlas one can choose a local section and read off a strict
map on objects
\widetilde{\psi}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
and an induced homomorphism on arrows
\sigma:\Gamma\to\Gamma
satisfying semi-equivariance.
A
2
-morphism corresponds to a natural transformation between the corresponding functors of groupoids, i.e. an assignment to each object
z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
of an arrow

z\to z
, hence an element of the stabilizer at
z
. Since the action on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is free, stabilizers are trivial, and the only freedom comes from changing the strict lift
by a right
\Gamma
-equivariant automorphism of the pulled-back torsor, i.e. a holomorphic map
\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\Gamma
.
Because
\Gamma
is discrete and
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is connected, such a map is constant, giving the claimed form of
2
-isomorphisms.
∎

Throughout the paper, whenever we write

\mathrm{End}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]),\ \mathrm{Aut}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]),\ \mathrm{End}([\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]),

or analogous notation for quotient stacks, we mean
1
-endomorphisms in the analytic

2
-category of quotient stacks, considered up to
2
-isomorphism. By

Proposition

3.5
, such morphisms may be represented on the atlas by strict
semi-equivariant pairs
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
, and all later classifications of
endomorphisms are to be understood in this sense; compare also

[
2
,
17
]

3.2.
Developing maps and canonical stack morphism

First, we recall here the following well-known fact.
Let
(\omega,\eta)
satisfy
d\omega=\eta\wedge\omega
and
d\eta=0
on
U
.
On any simply connected open set
V\subset U
there exists a meromorphic function
\varphi
on
V
with
\eta|_{V}=d\varphi
.
Setting
m:=e^{-\varphi}
, one has
d(m\omega)=0
on
V
, hence there exists a meromorphic function
F_{V}
on
V
with

dF_{V}=m\omega,\qquad\mathcal{F}|_{V}=\ker(\omega)=\ker(dF_{V}).

In fact,
Since
d\eta=0
and
V
is simply connected, write
\eta=d\varphi
.
Then
dm=-e^{-\varphi}d\varphi=-m\eta
, hence

d(m\omega)=dm\wedge\omega+m\,d\omega=(-m\eta)\wedge\omega+m(\eta\wedge\omega)=0.

Thus
m\omega
is closed and hence exact on
V
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
p:\widetilde{U}\to U
be the universal cover.
There exists a holomorphic function
\Phi
on
\widetilde{U}
with
p^{*}\eta=d\Phi
.
Setting
z:=e^{\Phi}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}(\widetilde{U})
, one has

\frac{dz}{z}=p^{*}\eta,\qquad z(\gamma\cdot x)=\rho(\gamma)\,z(x)\quad(\gamma\in\pi_{1}(U)).

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{U}
is simply connected and
d(p^{*}\eta)=p^{*}(d\eta)=0
, choose
\Phi
with
d\Phi=p^{*}\eta
.
For a deck transformation
\gamma
, the difference
\Phi\circ\gamma-\Phi
is constant, and integrating
p^{*}\eta
along a loop representing
\gamma

shows this constant equals
\int_{\gamma}\eta
. Exponentiating gives the formula for
z
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\ker(\rho)\subset\pi_{1}(U)
be the kernel of the character
\rho
.
The developing coordinate
z
determines canonically a morphism of analytic stacks

F:U\longrightarrow[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma].

More precisely, if

P:=\widetilde{U}/\ker(\rho),

then
P\to U
is a principal right
\Gamma
-bundle, the map
z
descends to a
\Gamma
-equivariant holomorphic map

\bar{z}:P\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*},

and the pair
(P,\bar{z})
is the corresponding
U
-point of the quotient stack
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.6
, the developing coordinate

z:\widetilde{U}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}

satisfies

z(\gamma\cdot x)=\rho(\gamma)\,z(x)\qquad(\gamma\in\pi_{1}(U),\ x\in\widetilde{U}).

In particular, if
\gamma\in\ker(\rho)
, then
\rho(\gamma)=1
, and therefore

z(\gamma\cdot x)=z(x).

Thus
z
is constant on
\ker(\rho)
-orbits and descends to a holomorphic map

\bar{z}:P=\widetilde{U}/\ker(\rho)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}.

Since
\ker(\rho)
is a normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(U)
, the quotient group

\pi_{1}(U)/\ker(\rho)

acts on

. By definition of
\Gamma=\operatorname{im}(\rho)
, the homomorphism
\rho
induces an isomorphism

\pi_{1}(U)/\ker(\rho)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma.

Through this identification,

becomes a principal right
\Gamma
-bundle.
The descended map
\bar{z}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant. Indeed, let

, and choose
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(U)
such that
\rho(\gamma)=g
. If

denotes the class of

, then

\bar{z}([x]\cdot g)=\bar{z}([\gamma\cdot x])=z(\gamma\cdot x)=\rho(\gamma)\,z(x)=g\,\bar{z}([x]).

The situation is summarized by the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P=\widetilde{U}/\ker(\rho)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{z}}

together with the right
\Gamma
-action on

and the standard multiplicative action of

on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
By the defining description of the quotient stack

, a morphism

is equivalent to the datum of a principal right
\Gamma
-bundle over
U
together with a
\Gamma
-equivariant map from that bundle to

. The pair

therefore defines a morphism

Equivalently, one may view

as the following cartesian presentation of

∎

3.3.
The linear classifying base, the translational class, and the affine torsor

We now make explicit the geometric relation among the linear monodromy, the multiplicative developing coordinate, and the affine torsor determined by the full affine monodromy. In the following, denote
p:\widetilde{U}\to U
the universal cover.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let

\rho:\pi_{1}(U,q)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}

be the linear part of the affine monodromy, and set

\Gamma:=\operatorname{im}(\rho).

Then:

(i)

The representation
\rho
determines a classifying morphism

b_{\rho}:U\longrightarrow B\Gamma=[*/\Gamma],

which classifies the underlying
\Gamma
-torsor of the linear monodromy.

(ii)

The associated rank-one local system

L_{\rho}:=\widetilde{U}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{C}

is the pullback along
b_{\rho}
of the universal linear object

[\mathbb{C}/\Gamma]\longrightarrow B\Gamma,

where
\Gamma
acts on
\mathbb{C}
by multiplication.

(iii)

The stack

[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]

is the complement of the zero section inside
[\mathbb{C}/\Gamma]
. Accordingly, a morphism

F:U\longrightarrow[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]

is equivalent to the datum of the classifying map
b_{\rho}
together with a nowhere-vanishing section of the pullback local system
L_{\rho}
.

Proof.

Let

P_{\rho}:=\widetilde{U}/\ker(\rho)\longrightarrow U.

Since
\ker(\rho)
is a normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(U,q)
, the quotient group

\pi_{1}(U,q)/\ker(\rho)

is canonically identified with
\Gamma
, and therefore
P_{\rho}\to U
is a principal right
\Gamma
-bundle. By the defining property of the classifying stack
B\Gamma
, this bundle determines the classifying morphism

b_{\rho}:U\longrightarrow B\Gamma.

This proves (i).

For (ii), the associated bundle of
P_{\rho}
via the standard multiplicative action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{C}
is

P_{\rho}\times^{\Gamma}\mathbb{C},

which is canonically identified with
\widetilde{U}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{C}=L_{\rho}
. On the universal stack
B\Gamma
, the same construction gives the universal linear object
[\mathbb{C}/\Gamma]\to B\Gamma
, so
L_{\rho}
is its pullback along
b_{\rho}
.

Finally, (iii) follows from the quotient-stack description. The open substack
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
is obtained from
[\mathbb{C}/\Gamma]
by removing the zero section. Hence a morphism

F:U\longrightarrow[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]

amounts to a principal right
\Gamma
-bundle over
U
together with a
\Gamma
-equivariant map from that bundle to
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Equivalently, it is the same as a section of the associated bundle
L_{\rho}
which is nowhere zero. This proves the claim.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

\xi:\pi_{1}(U,q)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{*}\ltimes\mathbb{C}

be the affine monodromy representation, and let

\rho:\pi_{1}(U,q)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}

be its linear part. Writing

\xi(\gamma)(z)=\rho(\gamma)z+\tau(\gamma),

the translational part
\tau
satisfies

\tau(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\tau(\gamma_{1})+\rho(\gamma_{1})\tau(\gamma_{2}).

Hence
\tau
is a
1
-cocycle with values in the locally constant sheaf
\mathbb{C}_{\rho}
associated with
L_{\rho}
, and defines a class

[\tau]\in H^{1}(U,\mathbb{C}_{\rho}).

Moreover:

(i)

The affine monodromy determines the affine torsor

\mathscr{T}_{\xi}:=\widetilde{U}\times_{\xi}\mathbb{C}

over
U
.

(ii)

The torsor
\mathscr{T}_{\xi}
is naturally a torsor under the additive local system
L_{\rho}
.

(iii)

The isomorphism class of
\mathscr{T}_{\xi}
is classified by the cohomology class
[\tau]\in H^{1}(U,\mathbb{C}_{\rho})
.

(iv)

The class
[\tau]
is trivial if and only if, after an affine change of coordinate on
\mathbb{C}
, the representation
\xi
reduces to its multiplicative part.

Proof.

The identity

\tau(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\tau(\gamma_{1})+\rho(\gamma_{1})\tau(\gamma_{2})

is obtained by comparing the translational parts in the composition law

\xi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\xi(\gamma_{1})\circ\xi(\gamma_{2}).

Thus
\tau
is a
1
-cocycle with values in the
\rho
-twisted additive local system, and therefore determines a cohomology class

[\tau]\in H^{1}(U,\mathbb{C}_{\rho}).

The affine monodromy acts on
\widetilde{U}\times\mathbb{C}
by

\gamma\cdot(x,z)=\bigl(\gamma x,\,\rho(\gamma)z+\tau(\gamma)\bigr),

and the quotient is exactly the affine torsor

\mathscr{T}_{\xi}=\widetilde{U}\times_{\xi}\mathbb{C}.

This proves (i).
For (ii), if two points of the same fibre are represented by
(x,z)
and
(x,w)
, then their difference is
z-w\in\mathbb{C}
. Under the deck action this difference transforms as

(\rho(\gamma)z+\tau(\gamma))-(\rho(\gamma)w+\tau(\gamma))=\rho(\gamma)(z-w),

which is exactly the transformation rule for the local system
L_{\rho}
. Hence
\mathscr{T}_{\xi}
is naturally a torsor under
L_{\rho}
.
For (iii), the descent of the affine line along the covering
\widetilde{U}\to U
is encoded by the translational cocycle
\tau
, with coefficients in the additive local system
\mathbb{C}_{\rho}
. Two such cocycles define isomorphic affine torsors if and only if they differ by a coboundary, corresponding to a change of origin in the affine coordinate. Therefore the isomorphism class of
\mathscr{T}_{\xi}
is classified by
[\tau]
.
Finally, (iv) is the same statement in concrete form: the class
[\tau]
vanishes if and only if
\tau
is a coboundary, and this is equivalent to the existence of an affine change of coordinate on
\mathbb{C}
which removes the translational part of
\xi
. In that case the monodromy becomes purely multiplicative.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let

H:=\operatorname{im}(\xi)\subset\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{C})
and
\Gamma:=\operatorname{im}(\rho)\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}.

Assume that affine developing data have been chosen, so that they define a stacky morphism

\mathrm{Dev}_{\xi}:U\longrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{\xi}=[\mathbb{C}/H].

Then the affine and multiplicative developing objects fit into the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{B}_{\xi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\;[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Dev}_{\xi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b_{\rho}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

where:

(i)

b_{\rho}
classifies the linear monodromy;

(ii)

F
classifies the multiplicative developing coordinate, equivalently the linear local system together with a nowhere-vanishing section;

(iii)

\mathrm{Dev}_{\xi}
classifies the full affine developing object, equivalently the linear local system together with its translational torsor class.

Thus the affine and multiplicative theories meet over the same linear base

B\Gamma=[*/\Gamma].

Here
\mathscr{B}_{\xi}=[\mathbb{C}/H]
keeps the translational affine data, whereas
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
keeps only the multiplicative linear coordinate.

Proof.

The projection

H\longrightarrow\Gamma,\ (a,b)\longmapsto a,

induces the morphism

\mathscr{B}_{\xi}=[\mathbb{C}/H]\longrightarrow B\Gamma=[*/\Gamma],

while the structure morphism

[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]\longrightarrow B\Gamma

is induced by the unique
\Gamma
-equivariant map
\mathbb{C}^{*}\to*
. The morphism
b_{\rho}
was constructed in
Proposition

3.8
, and the multiplicative developing map
F
is therefore a lift of
b_{\rho}
through the open part
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
of the universal linear object.
Likewise, once affine developing data are fixed, they define an
H
-equivariant map from the corresponding principal
H
-bundle to
\mathbb{C}
, hence a morphism

\mathrm{Dev}_{\xi}:U\to[\mathbb{C}/H]=\mathscr{B}_{\xi}.

Statements (i)–(iii) are just the interpretations established in
Propositions

3.8
and
3.9
.
Finally, a canonical morphism
\mathscr{B}_{\xi}\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
would require, on the level of atlases, an
H
-equivariant holomorphic map

\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}

compatible with the projection
H\to\Gamma
. In general no such map exists, because the translational part of
H
does not preserve the multiplicative structure of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Thus the natural comparison between the two theories is through the common base
B\Gamma
.
∎

3.4.
Leaf stacks

Let
U^{\mathrm{sm}}=U\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{F})
.
Following McQuillan
[
14
, p. 117–118]
, one may encode the foliation on
U^{\mathrm{sm}}
by its holonomy or homotopy groupoid and consider the corresponding quotient stack.
For the purposes of the present article, however, the only property we use is the standard universal property of the quotient stack: a morphism out of

is equivalently a morphism out of
U^{\mathrm{sm}}
equipped with descent data along the foliation groupoid. Equivalently, leafwise-constant morphisms factor essentially uniquely through the quotient; compare
[
2

12
,
15

17

To keep the leaf-stack discussion self-contained, we isolate the

hypotheses on a foliation groupoid needed in this work.
They are standard and are satisfied by the holonomy groupoid in the smooth category; in the analytic category one may either
assume existence of an analytic holonomy/homotopy groupoid, or replace it by a Haefliger-type groupoid (which always exists) and work Morita-invariantly.

(
Local submersion
) the source and target maps

(3)

(
Local connectivity
) the
s
-fibres are locally connected (so that “locally constant” descent data are forced to be constant on connected domains);

(4)

(
Morita invariance
) any two such groupoids for the same foliation are Morita equivalent.

In practice one may take

to be the

, when it exists in the analytic category under consideration, or the

of local transverse germs.
Both presentations are Morita equivalent and therefore define the same quotient stack; see for instance
[
2
,
12
,
15
,
16
]
.

3.5.
The stacky Singer theorem: lift, functional equation, and descent

We now prove

in a form that explicitly produces the semi-equivariant functional equation.

Definition 3.14
.

A
stacky first integral
for
\mathcal{F}
is a representable meromorphic morphism

\overline{F}:U^{\mathrm{sm}}\dashrightarrow[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]

such that, on some dense Zariski open subset

the restriction
\overline{F}|_{U^{\circ}}
is holomorphic with image in
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
, and every local lift

has the property that, on a dense open subset of
V
where
f
is submersive, its fibres are exactly the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.

If

F:U^{\circ}\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]

is representable, then locally on
U^{\circ}
it is described, in an orbifold chart, by a holomorphic lift

Replacing
f
by
\gamma f
with
\gamma\in\Gamma
does not change its fibres, and hence does not change the induced codimension-one distribution on the locus where
f
is submersive. Thus the foliation defined by

is well defined in local orbifold charts. Accordingly, when two representable morphisms

are compared, the condition that they define the same foliation means that, after passing to local lifts

there exists a dense open subset

on which both maps are submersive and

\ker(df|_{V^{\circ}})=\ker(df^{\prime}|_{V^{\circ}}).

Equivalently, on
V^{\circ}
the fibres of

and
f^{\prime}
determine the same foliation.

.

if necessary, there exist a meromorphic endomorphism

\overline{F}

4.
Kummer refinements

The purpose of this section is to give a precise geometric formulation of a central principle of the paper: in the present setting,
Singer’s elementary operations are realised by morphisms between one-dimensional stack-theoretic transverse bases.

The holonomy constants, that is, the exponentials of residues and periods, determine the group

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
(see
Proposition

2.4
), and therefore determine the transverse stack

[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
together with its compactification
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
.
The reparametrization component of Singer’s theorem is thus expressed by endomorphisms of these stacks
(see
Propositions

3.15
and
5
).
The algebraic-extension component admits an equally geometric description: it is effected by Kummer, equivalently root, base change along the boundary, that is, by passage to root stacks

[
3
,
5
,
30
]
.
In order to place these two operations within a single formal setting, we introduce a modest
2
-categorical construction.

4.1.
The Morita 2-category and Kummer/root refinements

We adopt throughout the Morita–bibundle formalism of differentiable and complex stacks, following
[
2
,
12
,
15
,
22
]
. Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group acting holomorphically on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by transformations of the form
z\mapsto\gamma z
, and suppose that this action preserves the distinguished subset
\{0,\infty\}
.
We always regard
\Gamma
with the discrete topology (so the quotient stack is defined even when the action is not proper).

Definition 4.1
.

A
Morita morphism

[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]\to[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma^{\prime}]
is a
1
-morphism of stacks represented by a bibundle
in the sense of
Definition

3.3
;
2
-morphisms are bibundle isomorphisms.
We write
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mor}}([\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma],[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma^{\prime}])
for the resulting groupoid.

On a connected atlas, Morita morphisms admit strict semi-equivariant representatives
(
Proposition

3.5
;
compare also
[
2
,
17
]
).
This is why functional-equation methods apply in our setting and why the wild regime becomes rigid.

In Singer’s differential-algebraic closure, one allows
algebraic extensions
. Geometrically, for logarithmic structures,
this corresponds to Kummer/root constructions along boundary divisors
[
3
]
.
On the base
\mathbb{P}^{1}
this is encoded by adjoining
N
-th roots at
\{0,\infty\}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Fix
N\geq 1
. Define the
N
-Kummer refinement
of
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
by

[\mathbb{P}^{1}/(\mu_{N}\times\Gamma)],

where
(\zeta,\gamma)\in\mu_{N}\times\Gamma
acts by
(\zeta,\gamma)\cdot z=(\zeta\gamma)\,z
.
Similarly, consider
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/(\mu_{N}\times\Gamma)]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

There is a canonical representable map (a Morita morphism)

\kappa_{N}:[\mathbb{P}^{1}/(\mu_{N}\times\Gamma)]\longrightarrow[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]

induced on atlases by
z\mapsto z^{N}
and on groups by the homomorphism

\mu_{N}\times\Gamma\to\Gamma
,
(\zeta,\gamma)\mapsto\gamma^{N}
.

Proof.

Equivariance is immediate:

((\zeta,\gamma)\cdot z)^{N}=(\zeta\gamma)^{N}z^{N}=\gamma^{N}z^{N},

since
\zeta^{N}=1
.
Thus
z\mapsto z^{N}
defines a strict semi-equivariant map and hence a stack morphism.
Representability follows because
\mu_{N}
acts faithfully on the Kummer refinement and is killed by
\kappa_{N}
exactly as in the root-stack description
of
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\mu_{N}]

[
3
]
.
∎

For
\Gamma=\{1\}
, the quotient
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\mu_{N}]
is the
N
-th root stack along
\{0\}+\{\infty\}
.
More generally,
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/(\mu_{N}\times\Gamma)]
is the corresponding
\Gamma
-equivariant root refinement
.
The comparison between root stacks and the Kato–Nakayama boundary is one of the main themes of
[
5
,
30
]

and explains why rational residues (torsion meridian multipliers) are naturally treated by Kummer refinements.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathbf{Singer}
be the
2
-subcategory of the Morita
2
-category generated by:

(a)

all objects
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
and their Kummer refinements

;

(b)

all endomorphisms
\psi\in\mathrm{End}([\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma])
(Singer reparametrizations);

(c)

.

A

is a
1
-morphism in
\mathbf{Singer}
.

Let

be the canonical stacky developing map (

).
A geometric Singer operation

produces a new stacky first integral
by post-composition
F\mapsto\Psi\circ F
on the largest open locus where the composition is defined.
If

is a Kummer refinement

, the resulting operation corresponds to passing to an

as in the usual algebraic-extension step of Singer.
A typical operation is encoded by a

4.2.

.

Let

be a transversely affine affine structure on

is holomorphic, the composite

is a stacky first integral for the foliation defined by

.

(ii)

For every

, let

be the Kummer morphism. Then the pullback of

along

is again a stacky first integral for the same foliation on a dense open subset.

(iii)

be a stacky first integral for the same foliation on a dense open subset

. Then, after shrinking

if necessary, there exist an integer

and a meromorphic morphism

such that

is

-isomorphic to

where

denotes the Kummer refinement of the canonical developing map.

Proof.

For (i), let

On any local orbifold chart on which

is represented by a holomorphic transverse coordinate

the map
G
is represented, on the locus where
\Psi
is defined, by the meromorphic one-variable map

, where
\psi
is a local lift of

. Hence

is constant along the fibres of

, and therefore along the leaves of the foliation. On the open subset where

is holomorphic and non-critical, one has

Thus

is given by

\overline{\psi}:[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]\dashrightarrow[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma^{\prime}]

such that

-isomorphic to

on a dense open subset. If a Kummer refinement is required in order to trivialise the meridional torsion and make the transverse coordinate single-valued along the boundary, one replaces

and composes with the induced meromorphic morphism from the refined compactified base. This yields the required factorisation.
∎

On the same notation as above, consider the universal cover

. The closed form

admits a primitive

, and the multiplicative developing coordinate

satisfies

. It is at this stage that the operations

and
\exp
(and, implicitly,

) enter.
They produce the

and the canonical morphism

(

).

If the residue exponentials are torsion, Kummer refinement along

(equivalently, passage to root stacks) realizes the algebraic extension step

,

,

]
. This alters the

by replacing

with

,
but does not give rise to any new analytic reparametrizations.

A
Singer reparametrization
of a first integral is nothing other than post-composition by an endomorphism

\psi:[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
(or, meromorphically, by
\psi:[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]\dashrightarrow[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]

(or

Because the target is one-dimensional, holomorphicity and semi-equivariance impose strong global constraints.

In short:
Singer’s transcendence in our setting sits in the constants generating

5.
Classification of the stacky base by regime

This section contains the classification-theoretic core of the paper. We begin with a log–argument description and properness criteria for finitely generated subgroups
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
, then turn to holomorphic semi-equivariant pairs and the rigidity of the non-proper cases, next treat the proper discrete torsion and elliptic regimes, and finally record the meromorphic classification on the compactified base. The purpose is to present the regime-by-regime analysis in a single sequence, without separating the topological, holomorphic, and meromorphic parts.

5.1.
Subgroups of
\mathbb{C}^{*}

In this subsection we study finitely generated subgroups
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
and the topology of their quotients.

Definition 5.1
.

Let

\ell:\mathbb{C}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}),

and

\ell(z)=(\log|z|,\arg z).

Write
\ell(\Gamma)=(A_{\Gamma},B_{\Gamma}),
where
A_{\Gamma}\subset\mathbb{R}
is the radial subgroup and
B_{\Gamma}\subset\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
is the angular subgroup.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
be a finitely generated subgroup. Then the following hold.

(i)

If
A_{\Gamma}=\{0\}
, then
\Gamma\subset S^{1}
, and
\overline{\Gamma}
is finite if and only if
B_{\Gamma}
is finite; otherwise
\overline{\Gamma}=S^{1}
.

(ii)

If
A_{\Gamma}\neq\{0\}
, then
\Gamma
contains an element

with
|q|\neq 1
. In particular, every non-fixed orbit of the action

\Gamma\curvearrowright\mathbb{P}^{1}

accumulates at
0
and
\infty
.

(iii)

The following are equivalent:

(a)

the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\mathbb{C}^{*}
is properly discontinuous;

(b)

\Gamma
is discrete in the Euclidean topology of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
;

(c)

either
\Gamma=\mu_{N}
for some
N\geq 1
, or

\Gamma\cong\mu_{N}\times q^{\mathbb{Z}}

for some
N\geq 1
and
q\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
with
|q|\neq 1
.

(iv)

In particular, if
\Gamma
is torsion-free and discrete, then

\Gamma=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
q\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
with
|q|\neq 1
.

Proof.

Statement (i) is the standard classification of finitely generated subgroups of the compact group
S^{1}
: such a subgroup is either finite or dense in
S^{1}
.

For (ii), if
A_{\Gamma}\neq\{0\}
, then by definition the radial part of
\Gamma
is nontrivial, so there exists
q\in\Gamma
with
|q|\neq 1
. Iterating
q
or
q^{-1}
shows that the orbit of any point of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
accumulates at
0
and at
\infty
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

We now prove (iii).

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b).
If
\Gamma
were not discrete in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, there would exist a sequence of distinct elements
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma
converging to some
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then
\gamma^{-1}\gamma_{n}\to 1
, so for any sufficiently small compact neighbourhood
K
of
1
one would have

(\gamma^{-1}\gamma_{n})\cdot K\cap K\neq\varnothing

for infinitely many
n
, contradicting proper discontinuity.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c).
Consider the homomorphism

\ell:\mathbb{C}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
and
\ell(z)=\log|z|.

Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated and discrete in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, the subgroup

\Gamma\cap S^{1}

is finite, hence equal to
\mu_{N}
for some
N\geq 1
. If
\ell(\Gamma)=\{0\}
, then
\Gamma\subset S^{1}
, so
\Gamma=\mu_{N}
. Assume now that
\ell(\Gamma)\neq\{0\}
. Because
\ell(\Gamma)
is a finitely generated discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
, it is of the form

\ell(\Gamma)=\lambda\mathbb{Z}\ \text{for some }\lambda>0.

Choose
q\in\Gamma
with
\ell(q)=\lambda
, so that
|q|\neq 1
. For any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\ell(\gamma)=m\lambda=\ell(q^{m}),

hence
\gamma q^{-m}\in\Gamma\cap S^{1}=\mu_{N}
. Therefore every element of
\Gamma
may be written uniquely as

\gamma=\zeta q^{m},\ \zeta\in\mu_{N},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since
|q|\neq 1
, one has

\mu_{N}\cap q^{\mathbb{Z}}=\{1\},

and therefore

\Gamma\cong\mu_{N}\times q^{\mathbb{Z}}.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(a).
If
\Gamma=\mu_{N}
, the action is finite and therefore properly discontinuous. If

\Gamma\cong\mu_{N}\times q^{\mathbb{Z}}\ \text{with }|q|\neq 1,

let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
be compact. Since multiplication by
q^{m}
rescales the modulus exponentially, only finitely many integers
m
satisfy

q^{m}K\cap K\neq\varnothing.

As the torsion factor
\mu_{N}
is finite, it follows that only finitely many elements of
\Gamma
move
K
so that it still meets itself. Hence the action is properly discontinuous.

Finally, (iv) follows immediately from (iii): if
\Gamma
is torsion-free, then the finite factor
\mu_{N}
is trivial.
∎

Consider
D_{i}
the irreducible components of the divisor
D
and
\eta
the meromorphic 1-form of the transversely affine structure on the foliation. Write each meridian multiplier as

\lambda_{i}:=\exp\big(2\pi i\,\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(\eta)\big)=\exp(2\pi i(a_{i}+ib_{i}))=e^{-2\pi b_{i}}\cdot e^{2\pi ia_{i}},

so
|\lambda_{i}|=e^{-2\pi b_{i}}
and
\lambda_{i}/|\lambda_{i}|=e^{2\pi ia_{i}}
.
Define the finitely generated additive subgroup

B:=\langle b_{1},\dots,b_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{R},\qquad|\,\Gamma\,|:=\langle|\lambda_{1}|,\dots,|\lambda_{k}|\rangle\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

Then
|\,\Gamma\,|=\exp(-2\pi B)
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that
\mathrm{rk}(B)\geq 2
. Then
B
is dense in
\mathbb{R}
, and consequently

|\Gamma|=\exp(-2\pi B)

is dense in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. In particular, the orbit space
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
is not
T_{0}
, hence in particular not Hausdorff.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{rk}(B)\geq 2
, there exist
b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}\in B
such that
b^{\prime}/b^{\prime\prime}\notin\mathbb{Q}
. The subgroup

\{mb^{\prime}+nb^{\prime\prime}:m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subset B

is then dense in
\mathbb{R}
by the classical Kronecker–Diophantine approximation argument. Hence
B
itself is dense in
\mathbb{R}
.
Now the map

t\longmapsto e^{-2\pi t}

is continuous and maps dense subsets of
\mathbb{R}
to dense subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Therefore,

|\Gamma|=\exp(-2\pi B)

is dense in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Restrict the action to the closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Since the orbit

|\Gamma|\cdot 1

is dense in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, every orbit in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
has dense closure, and in particular distinct points have the same orbit closure. Hence the quotient

\mathbb{R}_{>0}/|\Gamma|

is not
T_{0}
. It follows a fortiori that
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
is not
T_{0}
, and therefore cannot be Hausdorff.
∎

On the above case the topology on the quotient is too wild, so the robust object to work with is the quotient stack
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
. The following case split is the one we use throughout the paper:

(a)

Torsion/DM
:
\Gamma=\mu_{N}
, then
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
is a DM curve.

(b)

Proper cyclic/elliptic
:
\Gamma=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|q|\neq 1
. Then
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]\simeq E_{q}
is an elliptic curve.

(c)

Unitary wild
:
|\,\Gamma\,|=\{1\}
but the angular subgroup is infinite (typically dense in
S^{1}
). Then
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
is non-Hausdorff.

(d)

Radial wild
:
\mathrm{rk}(B)\geq 2
. Then
|\,\Gamma\,|
is dense and
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
is not
T_{0}
.

(e)

Mixed wild
:
\mathrm{rk}(B)=1
but the angular subgroup has accumulation at
1
; then the action still fails to be proper, and rigidity results apply.

In our setting
\Gamma=\mathrm{im}(\rho)\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
is a finitely generated abelian group (generated by residue and period exponentials),
but its
embedding
in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
can be highly non-discrete (dense unitary, mixed, etc.).

The action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is left translation in the topological group
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
For such actions, Hausdorffness of the orbit space is controlled by closedness of the subgroup.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
G
be a Hausdorff topological group and
H\subset G
a subgroup.
Then the quotient space
G/H
is Hausdorff if and only if
H
is closed in
G
.

Applied to the case
G=\mathbb{C}^{*}
, this shows that a monodromy subgroup which is not closed necessarily gives rise to a non-Hausdorff orbit space. In particular, in the wild regimes, the resulting orbit space fails to be a curve in any reasonable geometric sense. Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated, the question of closedness may moreover be decided explicitly.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
be a finitely generated subgroup.
Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\Gamma
is closed in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

(b)

\Gamma
is discrete in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

(c)

Either
\Gamma
is finite (hence
\Gamma=\mu_{N}
), or
\Gamma
is virtually cyclic of the form

\Gamma\cong\mu_{N}\times q^{\mathbb{Z}}

for some
N\geq 1
and some
q\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
with
|q|\neq 1
.

Consequently,
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
is Hausdorff if and only if one is in case (c).

Proof.

Let
\log|\cdot|:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be the continuous homomorphism.
The image
A:=\log|\Gamma|\subset\mathbb{R}
is a finitely generated subgroup, hence either
A=0
or
A=\alpha\mathbb{Z}
for some
\alpha>0
.
If
A=0
, then
\Gamma\subset S^{1}
; a finitely generated subgroup of
S^{1}
is either finite or dense, hence it is closed iff finite.
If
A=\alpha\mathbb{Z}
, choose
q\in\Gamma
with
\log|q|=\alpha
, so
|q|\neq 1
.
Then
\Gamma
contains an element of modulus
\neq 1
, and by
Proposition

5.2
any discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is either finite or

q^{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|q|\neq 1
.
Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated and contains
q^{\mathbb{Z}}
as a finite-index subgroup, it is of the stated form
\mu_{N}\times q^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In this situation
\Gamma
is discrete and therefore closed.
Finally, (a)
\Leftrightarrow
Hausdorffness follows from
Lemma

5.4
.
∎

If
\Gamma=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|q|=1
and
q
not a root of unity, then
\Gamma
is not discrete (its powers are dense in
S^{1}
),
hence not closed, and
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
fails to be Hausdorff by
Lemma

5.4
.
Nevertheless the quotient stack
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
is perfectly well-defined and is the correct transverse base in the wild regime.

Corollary 5.6
.

Assume
\Gamma
is finitely generated and closed. Then
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
is a Euclidean Riemann surface: it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{*}
(finite case) or to an elliptic curve
(infinite case
\Gamma=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
|q|\neq 1
).
In particular, in dimension
1
these coarse quotients are algebraic:
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is affine and every compact Riemann surface (hence every elliptic curve)
is projective.

Proof.

First notice that the quotient
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
is Hausdorff. If
\Gamma=\mu_{N}
, then
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma\simeq\mathbb{C}^{*}
via
z\mapsto z^{N}
.
If
\Gamma=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|q|\neq 1
, then logarithmic coordinates identify
\mathbb{C}^{*}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
with

\mathbb{C}/(2\pi i\mathbb{Z}+(\log q)\mathbb{Z})
, a complex torus of dimension
1
.
∎

5.2.
GAGA in the proper cases

In the proper cases occurring in the classification, the transverse base is no longer of a genuinely non-algebraic kind, but becomes a one-dimensional algebraic object: either an elliptic curve, or, in the finite-monodromy case, a proper Deligne–Mumford stack. The rôle of the present subsection is therefore simply to record that, in these regimes, the analytic automorphism theory considered in the paper coincides with the corresponding algebraic one.
This observation clarifies the structure of the classification. It shows that the proper regimes recover the expected algebraic models, whereas the non-proper cases are precisely those in which one must work intrinsically with analytic quotient stacks. The proposition below makes this passage precise.

Proposition 5.7

(GAGA for automorphisms)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be either

(i)

a proper Deligne–Mumford analytic curve arising as the analytification of a proper algebraic Deligne–Mumford stack over
\mathbb{C}
, or

(ii)

an elliptic curve.

Then every analytic auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{X}
is algebraic. In particular, the natural comparison functor identifies the analytic and algebraic automorphism groups; in the Deligne–Mumford case, it also identifies the corresponding automorphism
2
-groups.

Proof.

In case (i), the statement is a direct consequence of GAGA for proper Deligne–Mumford stacks. One may appeal to Porta–Yu’s comparison theorem for proper higher analytic stacks, which applies in particular to proper Deligne–Mumford
1
-stacks
[
24
]
. In the one-dimensional situation considered here, Hall’s relative GAGA theorem for families of curves gives an equivalent algebraization statement
[
9
]
. Thus analytic morphisms, closed substacks, and isomorphisms algebraize uniquely, and the same conclusion applies to auto-equivalences and to their
2
-automorphisms.
In case (ii), the statement is classical GAGA for projective complex curves: every holomorphic self-map of an elliptic curve is algebraic, and every biholomorphism is therefore an algebraic automorphism
[
27
]
.
For the final assertion, note that in the Deligne–Mumford case a
2
-morphism between two auto-equivalences is a global section of the corresponding Isom-stack, which is finite over the source. Proper GAGA therefore identifies analytic and algebraic
2
-morphisms as well.
∎

When
\Gamma
is not closed, the action is non-proper and the quotient falls outside the scope of any proper GAGA comparison of this kind. One is then forced to work intrinsically on the analytic stack side. In particular, it becomes essential to distinguish between strict morphisms attached to a chosen presentation and Morita morphisms of the quotient stack itself: the former describe symmetries of a fixed action groupoid, whereas the latter capture the intrinsic automorphism
2
-group of the quotient stack. This is precisely the situation relevant to the non-proper regimes studied later; see
[
2
,
17
,
18
,
25
]
.

5.3.
Normal form

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
be holomorphic. Then there exist a unique
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and a holomorphic function
H\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{*})
such that

f(z)=z^{n}\exp(H(z)).

Moreover,
f
is a biholomorphism if and only if

f(z)=az

or

f(z)=a/z

for some
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
\pi(w)=e^{w}
, be the universal cover. Choose a holomorphic function
h:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\exp(h(w))=f(e^{w}).

Then

h(w+2\pi i)-h(w)\in 2\pi i\mathbb{Z}.

By connectedness, this difference is constant, say
2\pi in
.
Thus
h(w)-nw
is
2\pi i
-periodic and descends to a holomorphic function
H\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{*})
, giving

f(z)=z^{n}\exp(H(z)).

The integer
n
is uniquely determined, since it is the winding number of
f
around
0
on a small circle.

Assume now that
f
is a biholomorphism. Then
f^{-1}
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, so
f
has neither zeros nor critical points on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Applying the same normal form to
f^{-1}
, one sees that the degree of
f
on
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong\mathbb{Z}
must be
\pm 1
.
Hence
n=\pm 1
. If
H
were non-constant, then
\exp(H)
would have non-trivial logarithmic derivative, and
f^{\prime}(z)
would vanish somewhere on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, contradiction.
Thus
H
is constant, say
\exp(H)=a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and therefore

f(z)=az

or

f(z)=a/z.

Conversely, both maps are biholomorphisms.
∎

Combining
Propositions

3.5
and
5.8
gives the general form for automorphisms of
\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}
:
they are induced by
\widetilde{\psi}(z)=az
(with
\sigma=\mathrm{id}
) and
\widetilde{\psi}(z)=a/z
(with
\sigma(\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}
),
modulo the
2
-isomorphism ambiguity
a\sim\gamma a
.
Thus the group of
1
-isomorphism classes of automorphisms is
(\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma)\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2)
, where
\mathbb{Z}/2
acts by inversion.
We make this precise in
Section

6
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
h:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be entire.

(i)

If there exist
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
which are
\mathbb{R}
-linearly independent and satisfy

h(w+\omega_{1})=h(w)
and
h(w+\omega_{2})=h(w)

for all
w
, then
h
is constant.

(ii)

If there exists a sequence
u_{n}\to 0
with
u_{n}\neq 0
such that

h(w+u_{n})-h(w)

is constant in
w
for every
n
, then
h
is affine-linear.
If moreover
h
is
2\pi i
-periodic, then
h
is constant.

Proof.

For (i), a fundamental parallelogram for the lattice
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}\omega_{1}+\mathbb{Z}\omega_{2}
is compact, hence
h
is bounded on it.
By periodicity,
h
is bounded on all of
\mathbb{C}
, so Liouville’s theorem implies that
h
is constant.

For (ii), fix
n
and write

h(w+u_{n})=h(w)+c_{n}

with
c_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Dividing by
u_{n}
and letting
u_{n}\to 0
, one obtains by uniform convergence on compact sets that
h^{\prime}(w)
is constant.
Hence
h
is affine-linear.
If
h
is
2\pi i
-periodic, then its linear term must vanish, so
h
is constant.
∎

5.4.
Properness, discreteness, and accumulation

Definition 5.10
.

We say that
\Gamma
has the
accumulation property
if either:

(1)

it contains an element
q
with
|q|\neq 1
; or

(2)

it contains a sequence
\gamma_{n}\to 1
in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
with
\gamma_{n}\neq 1
.

Lemma 5.11
.

If
\Gamma
is a finitely generated abelian group of abstract rank
\geq 2
embedded in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, then it has the accumulation property.

Proof.

Write generators as
\gamma_{j}=r_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}
.
Consider the homomorphism
\ell:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})
,
\ell(z)=(\log|z|,\arg z)
.
The subgroup
\ell(\Gamma)
is a finitely generated abelian subgroup of a Lie group.
If it had rank
\geq 2
and were discrete, it would define a rank
\geq 2
lattice in
\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})
, which is impossible because the second factor is compact.
Equivalently, diophantine approximation yields nontrivial integer combinations of the generators converging to
(0,0)
, hence elements of
\Gamma
converging to
1
in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
∎

Proposition 5.12
.

Assume
\Gamma
has the accumulation property.
Let
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
be a strict holomorphic endomorphism of
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
, i.e.
\widetilde{\psi}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
holomorphic and

\sigma:\Gamma\to\Gamma
a homomorphism with
\widetilde{\psi}(\gamma z)=\sigma(\gamma)\widetilde{\psi}(z)
.
Then there exist
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\widetilde{\psi}(z)=az^{n},
and
\sigma(\gamma)=\gamma^{n}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma.

Proof.

Let
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
be semi-equivariant and write
\widetilde{\psi}(z)=z^{n}\exp(H(z))
by
Proposition

5.8
.
Lift
H
to an entire function
h:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
via
h(w)=H(e^{w})
, so
h
is
2\pi i
-periodic.
Semi-equivariance gives
\exp(H(\gamma z)-H(z))=\sigma(\gamma)\gamma^{-n}
, hence for each fixed
\gamma\in\Gamma
the difference

H(\gamma z)-H(z)
is constant in
z
. Writing
\gamma=e^{u}
(choosing any logarithm
u
), this becomes

h(w+u)-h(w)=c(u)\quad\text{(constant in $w$)}.

Case 1:

\Gamma
contains
q
with
|q|\neq 1
.
Choose a logarithm
u_{0}=\log q
. Then
h(w+u_{0})-h(w)=c_{0}
for some constant
c_{0}
.
Iterating gives
h(w+nu_{0})=h(w)+nc_{0}
.
But
h
is
2\pi i
-periodic, so comparing
w
and
w+2\pi i
shows
c_{0}=0
.
Hence
h
is periodic with both
2\pi i
and
u_{0}
. Since
|q|\neq 1
, the periods are
\mathbb{R}
-linearly independent.
By
Lemma

5.9
,
h
is constant, so
H
is constant.

Case 2:

\Gamma
contains a sequence
\gamma_{m}\to 1
with
\gamma_{m}\neq 1
.
Choose logarithms
u_{m}=\log\gamma_{m}
with
u_{m}\to 0
.
Then
h(w+u_{m})-h(w)
is constant in
w
for each
m
.
By
Lemma

5.9
,
h
is constant (because it is
2\pi i
-periodic). Hence
H
is constant.

In either case,
\widetilde{\psi}(z)=az^{n}
with
a=e^{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Substituting into semi-equivariance yields
a(\gamma z)^{n}=\sigma(\gamma)az^{n}
, hence
\sigma(\gamma)=\gamma^{n}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

If
\Gamma
has accumulation, the only realizable group endomorphisms
\sigma\in\mathrm{End}(\Gamma)
coming from holomorphic endomorphisms of

are the power maps
\sigma(\gamma)=\gamma^{n}
(matrices

In particular, the only realizable automorphisms are

and
\sigma(\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}
(matrices
\pm I
).

For the compactified base, we have the following result.

Proposition 5.14
.

Assume
\Gamma
has accumulation.
If

and

for some

and

.

Let
\mathrm{Div}(R)
be the divisor of zeros and poles of

on

, a finite set.
Semi-equivariance implies
\mathrm{Div}(R)
is
\Gamma
-invariant.
If
\Gamma
has accumulation, the only finite
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is contained in

(because any point of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
has infinite orbit accumulating in

).
Hence
\mathrm{Div}(R)\subset\{0,\infty\}

has no zeros or poles in

.
Therefore

.
∎

5.5.
Finite monodromy case

Assume
\Gamma=\mu_{N}
is the group of
N
-th of the unity. The stacky base
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\mu_{N}]
is a Deligne–Mumford curve and is canonically identified with the

N
-th root stack of
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\{0\}+\{\infty\})

[
3
,
1
]
.
In this regime, the developing coordinate becomes single-valued after taking an
N
-th power, which produces an honest meromorphic first integral.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
F:U\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\mu_{N}]
be the canonical stacky developing map.
Choose a multiplicative developing coordinate
z
on the universal cover
\widetilde{U}
so that
z(\gamma x)=\rho(\gamma)z(x)
and

\rho(\pi_{1}(U))=\mu_{N}
.
Then the function
z^{N}
is invariant under deck transformations and descends to a single-valued holomorphic map

f:U\to\mathbb{C}^{*}.
After compactifying the target,
f
extends to a rational map
X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
whose stacky refinement is exactly

U\to[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\mu_{N}]\simeq\sqrt[N]{(\mathbb{P}^{1},\{0\}+\{\infty\})}
.

Proof.

For
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(U)
we have
z(\gamma x)=\rho(\gamma)z(x)
with
\rho(\gamma)\in\mu_{N}
, hence
(z^{N})(\gamma x)=z(x)^{N}
.
Thus
z^{N}
is invariant and descends to
U
.
The compactified statement follows by composing with
\mathbb{C}^{*}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
and taking the corresponding stacky compactification.
∎

The DM stack
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\mu_{N}]
is the weighted projective stack
\mathbb{P}(1,N)
in standard conventions.
Equivalently, it is the root stack along
\{0\}+\{\infty\}

[
3
]
.
Thus the finite monodromy case is the algebraic Singer regime: after a finite (Kummer) step, one obtains a genuine rational target and classical
equivariant rational reparametrizations.

Proposition 5.16
.

Strict holomorphic endomorphisms of
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\mu_{N}]
are pairs
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
with

\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{m},\qquad\widetilde{\psi}(z)=z^{m}g(z^{N}),

where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
g:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
is holomorphic.
Modulo
2
-isomorphism,

is determined up to multiplication by a constant root of unity.

Proof.

Any homomorphism
\sigma:\mu_{N}\to\mu_{N}
is of the form

\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{m}

for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus semi-equivariance for a strict endomorphism
(\widetilde{\psi},\sigma)
is the condition

\widetilde{\psi}(\zeta z)=\zeta^{m}\widetilde{\psi}(z)\qquad(\zeta\in\mu_{N}).

Equivalently,

\widetilde{\psi}(z)/z^{m}

is
\mu_{N}
-invariant. Since the quotient map

\mathbb{C}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*},\ z\longmapsto z^{N}

is the analytic quotient by
\mu_{N}
, it follows that there exists a holomorphic map

g:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}

such that

\widetilde{\psi}(z)=z^{m}g(z^{N}).

Conversely, every map of this form satisfies

\widetilde{\psi}(\zeta z)=\zeta^{m}\widetilde{\psi}(z),

hence defines a strict holomorphic endomorphism.
The statement about
2
-isomorphism follows from
Proposition

3.5
: two strict pairs with the same
\sigma
are
2
-isomorphic exactly when their lifts differ by multiplication by a constant element of
\mu_{N}
.
∎

5.6.
Elliptic regime case

Assume
\Gamma=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|q|\neq 1

Proposition 5.17
.

The action of

on

Proof.

Write

Then multiplication by

\log q

Thus

Free proper actions yield a quotient manifold and the quotient stack agrees with it.
∎

Proposition 5.18
.

Write

with
\Lambda=2\pi i\mathbb{Z}+\log q\,\mathbb{Z}
.
Every holomorphic endomorphism of
E_{q}
is affine:

[w]\longmapsto[\alpha w+\beta],

where
\beta\in\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies
\alpha\Lambda\subset\Lambda
.
In particular,
\mathrm{End}(E_{q})

\mathbb{Z}
for generic

Proof.

Let

be holomorphic and let

be the universal covering map.
Since
\mathbb{C}
is simply connected,

lifts to a holomorphic map

such that

\pi\circ F=f\circ\pi
.
For every

and every

Because

Thus there exists

Differentiating gives

is

By the maximum principle, that descended function is constant; hence

The relation

Conversely, every affine map

The description of the endomorphism ring is the standard one for elliptic curves; see for instance
[

∎

In the elliptic regime

\mathbb{C}

The

.

Write

Proposition 5.19

Let

The previous results explain how residues constrain

in the elliptic case. We now record the parallel constraint on reparametrizations.

Proposition 5.25
.

Assume
\Gamma=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|q|\neq 1
.

(i)

Let
\psi:[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
be a stack endomorphism admitting a strict semi-equivariant representative

\widetilde{\psi}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}

satisfying

\widetilde{\psi}(qz)=q^{n}\widetilde{\psi}(z)

for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then

\widetilde{\psi}(z)=cz^{n}

for some
c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
The induced endomorphism on
E_{q}
is multiplication by
n
composed with translation by the class of
c
.

(ii)

Let
R\in\mathbb{C}(z)
and let
\sigma:\Gamma\to\Gamma
satisfy

R(\gamma z)=\sigma(\gamma)R(z)\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma,\ z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}).

Then

R(z)=cz^{n}

for some
c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
is infinite cyclic, any homomorphism
\sigma:\Gamma\to\Gamma
is determined by

\sigma(q)=q^{n}

for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We first prove (ii). Let
\mathrm{Div}(R)
be the divisor of zeros and poles of
R
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
The semi-equivariance relation implies that
\mathrm{Div}(R)
is invariant under the action of
q^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
If
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
belonged to the support of
\mathrm{Div}(R)
, then the whole orbit

q^{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot a=\{q^{m}a:m\in\mathbb{Z}\}

would also lie in the support.
This orbit is infinite because
q
is not a root of unity, whereas the support of
\mathrm{Div}(R)
is finite.
Hence the support of
\mathrm{Div}(R)
is contained in
\{0,\infty\}
, and therefore

is a monomial:

R(z)=cz^{n}

for some
c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
For (i), the same rigidity argument applies to any meromorphic extension of
\widetilde{\psi}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and yields

\widetilde{\psi}(z)=cz^{n}.

Equivalently, one may write

\widetilde{\psi}(z)=z^{n}e^{H(z)}

with

holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
and compare with the relation

\widetilde{\psi}(qz)=q^{n}\widetilde{\psi}(z)

to conclude that

is constant.
The descended map on

is then the usual multiplication-by-
n
isogeny followed by translation by the class of
c
.
∎

By
Proposition

5.18
, every holomorphic endomorphism of
E_{q}
is the composition of a translation and an isogeny.
On the universal cover
\mathbb{C}
such a map is affine, say
w\mapsto\alpha w+\beta
, with
\alpha\Lambda\subset\Lambda
.
In multiplicative coordinates
z=e^{w}
, it is formally given by
z\mapsto e^{\alpha\log z}
and is single-valued on

,
but it need not be single-valued as a holomorphic map

unless

.
Thus the strict atlas description captures only the monomial part, whereas the full elliptic endomorphism theory requires Morita, or bibundle, morphisms; see
[
15
,
12
,
2
]
.

Relative to a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\Lambda
, the condition

is encoded by an integer

matrix.
On the holonomy group
\Gamma=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
itself one has
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)=\{\pm 1\}
, whereas
\mathrm{End}(E_{q})
is richer because it also contains translations.

Theorem 5.26
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
act on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by
\gamma\cdot z=\gamma z
on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
and fixing

.
A strict meromorphic endomorphism is a pair

(a)

(b)

6.
The automorphism groups and the 2-group of the compactified base

For an analytic stack
\mathcal{X}
, the automorphisms of
\mathcal{X}
naturally come in two layers. First, there are the self-equivalences

F:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}.

Secondly, if
F
and
G
are two such self-equivalences, there may exist a
2
-isomorphism

\eta:F\Rightarrow G

between them. Thus the automorphisms of
\mathcal{X}
do not form merely an ordinary group: they form a groupoid, endowed with composition, and hence a weak
2
-group; see, for instance,
[
19
,
20
]
.

From this
2
-categorical automorphism object one extracts two ordinary groups.

(i)

The group

\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}(\mathcal{X})

is the group of equivalence classes of self-equivalences of
\mathcal{X}
, where two self-equivalences are identified whenever they are related by a
2
-isomorphism.

(ii)

The group

\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}(\mathcal{X}):=\mathrm{Aut}_{2}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}})

is the group of
2
-automorphisms of the identity functor of
\mathcal{X}
. This does not produce new self-equivalences of
\mathcal{X}
; rather, it measures the intrinsic isotropy carried by the stack itself, that is, the residual symmetry which persists even when the underlying self-map is the identity.

In the present article, this distinction is forced by the nature of the transverse object. The natural transverse base of a transversely affine affine foliation is the quotient stack

[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
or
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma].

Hence the appropriate notion of symmetry is the automorphism
2
-group of the stack. The invariant
\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}(\mathcal{X})
is the one that governs the classification of reparametrizations, since it tells us which self-equivalences are distinct up to
2
-isomorphism. By contrast,
\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}(\mathcal{X})
becomes relevant at the boundary, where the quotient stack carries nontrivial inertia.

6.1.
Quotient stacks
[U/G]
and their automorphism
2
-groups

For an analytic quotient stack

\mathcal{X}=[U/G],

the appropriate automorphism object is the
2
-group of self-equivalences of the presenting action groupoid, taken up to Morita equivalence. This is the standard point of view in the theory of quotient stacks and automorphism
2
-groups; see
[
17
,
19
,
25
]
.

Let
G
be a complex Lie group acting holomorphically on a complex manifold
U
, and consider the action groupoid

U\times G\rightrightarrows U,\qquad s(u,g)=u,\qquad t(u,g)=g\cdot u.

A strict self-functor of this groupoid is determined by a pair
(f,\alpha)
, where

f\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{hol}}(U),\qquad\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(G),

subject to the semi-equivariance relation

f(g\cdot u)=\alpha(g)\cdot f(u)
for all

g\in G,\ u\in U.

If
(f,\alpha)
and
(f^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
are two such strict pairs, a
2
-isomorphism between them is given by a holomorphic map

b:U\to G

satisfying

f^{\prime}(u)=b(u)\cdot f(u),\qquad b(g\cdot u)\,\alpha(g)=\alpha^{\prime}(g)\,b(u)\qquad\text{for all }g\in G,\ u\in U.

Thus two strict models may define the same automorphism of the quotient stack even when they are not literally equal. This is the basic stack-theoretic gauge ambiguity.
The resulting automorphism
2
-group
\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([U/G])
has two invariants which will be used:

\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([U/G])\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ \pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([U/G]).

Concretely, in the quotient model, the right group consists of holomorphic maps

b:U\to G

such that
b(u)\in\mathrm{Stab}(u)
for every
u
, together with the evident equivariance condition. In particular, if the action of
G
on
U
is free, then

\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([U/G])=1.

A second important point is that not every self-equivalence of the quotient stack need be strict on a fixed atlas. The correct notion is Morita self-equivalence, or equivalently a self-bibundle of the presenting groupoid. Thus the strict pairs
(f,\alpha)
provide concrete representatives when available, but the automorphism theory of the stack itself is intrinsically Morita-invariant. This is summarized by the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U\times G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[U/G]}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}

For the present article, it is essential to retain the quotient stack itself rather than only its coarse quotient. The reason is that the stack remembers stabilizer data which the coarse space forgets. In non-proper regimes, this is exactly why the stacky base

[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]

retains isotropy and monodromy information that would be lost at the level of an ordinary quotient.

6.2.
Inertia and
2
-automorphisms of the quotient bases

For an analytic stack
\mathcal{X}
, the group
\pi_{1}\mathrm{Aut}_{2}(\mathcal{X})
is governed by the inertia stack; see
[
17
, §3.4]
. More precisely, if
x:W\to\mathcal{X}
is a
W
-valued point, its inertia group is the automorphism group of
x
in the groupoid
\mathcal{X}(W)
. The inertia stack
I\mathcal{X}
is the stack whose
W
-points are pairs
(x,\alpha)
, where
x\in\mathcal{X}(W)
and
\alpha
is an automorphism of
x
. A
2
-automorphism of the identity functor is therefore nothing but a functorial choice of such automorphisms for all points of
\mathcal{X}
, that is, a global section of
I\mathcal{X}
. Equivalently, one has a canonical identification

\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}(\mathcal{X})\cong\Gamma(\mathcal{X},I\mathcal{X}).

We now specialize this general observation to the quotient stacks
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
and
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
,
where
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
is discrete and acts by multiplication.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
be a discrete subgroup acting by multiplication.

(i)

The action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is free. Consequently,

\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma])=1.

(ii)

For the compactification
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
, the inertia is supported at the fixed points
0
and
\infty
. More precisely, there is an equivalence

I[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]\simeq[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]\sqcup(B\Gamma)_{0}\sqcup(B\Gamma)_{\infty},

where
(B\Gamma)_{0}
and
(B\Gamma)_{\infty}
denote the residual gerbes at
0
and
\infty
. Consequently,

\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma])\cong\Gamma\times\Gamma.

Proof.

The action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is free, since
\gamma z=z
with
z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
implies
\gamma=1
. Hence the quotient stack
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
has trivial inertia, and therefore

\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma])=1.

For
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
, the only points with nontrivial stabiliser are
0
and
\infty
, both fixed by
\Gamma
. The usual description of the inertia stack of a quotient stack
[X/G]
as the stack of pairs
(x,g)
with
gx=x
therefore yields the claimed form of
I[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
; see
[
19
, §2]
and
[
25
, §2]
. Taking global sections shows that

\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma])

is given by an independent choice of an element of
\Gamma
at
0
and at
\infty
, hence is naturally isomorphic to
\Gamma\times\Gamma
.
∎

Notice that item
(i)
tell us that
\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma])
is 1-truncated, that is

(6.1)

\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma])=\mathrm{Aut}_{1}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]).

The open base behaves, from the point of view of
2
-symmetry, like an honest curve, whereas the compactified base necessarily carries nontrivial
2
-categorical information concentrated at its stacky boundary points. This is the mechanism that becomes relevant as soon as one allows meromorphic reparametrizations.

6.3.
Automorphisms of the open base

Since
\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma])=1
by
Proposition

6.1
, no stacky ambiguity remains at the level of the open base, and the automorphism problem reduces to the classification of self-equivalences up to ordinary
2
-isomorphism.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
be any discrete subgroup, not necessarily proper as a subgroup of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Then every class in
\mathrm{Aut}_{1}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma])
is represented by one of the two types of maps

z\longmapsto az
or

z\longmapsto a/z,

with
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Two maps of the same type define the same class if and only if their parameters differ by multiplication by an element of
\Gamma
. Consequently,

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}([\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma])\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma)\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2),

where the nontrivial element of
\mathbb{Z}/2
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
by inversion.

Proof.

Any automorphism of
[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]
may be represented by a strict self-equivalence of the action groupoid of the
\Gamma
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Its lift to the atlas must therefore be a biholomorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, hence necessarily of the form

z\longmapsto az
or

z\longmapsto a/z,

by
Proposition

5.8
. The semi-equivariance relation forces the induced automorphism of
\Gamma
to be the identity in the first case and inversion in the second. Two such lifts determine the same automorphism of the quotient stack when they differ by multiplication by a constant element of
\Gamma
, which yields the quotient by
\Gamma
on the parameter
a
. Composition is immediate and gives the stated semidirect product structure.
∎

The induced automorphism of
\Gamma
is therefore only the discrete
\pm 1
part. The parameter
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma
is additional geometric data of the stack automorphism and cannot be recovered from the action on
\Gamma
alone.

6.4.
Automorphisms of the compactified base

For the compactified base, the situation changes for a simple reason: the inertia at
0
and
\infty
is nontrivial, so the automorphism object is genuinely
2
-categorical.

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume
\Gamma\neq\{1\}
. Then the automorphism
2
-group
\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma])
is determined by the following data.

(i)

Its group of connected components is

\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma])\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma)\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2),

represented by the Möbius transformations
z\longmapsto az
or

z\longmapsto a/z.

modulo multiplication of
a
by elements of
\Gamma
.

(ii)

Its group of automorphisms of the identity is

\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Aut}_{2}([\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma])\cong\Gamma\times\Gamma.

(iii)

The action of
\pi_{0}
on
\pi_{1}
is given as follows: a scaling

z\longmapsto az

acts trivially on
\Gamma\times\Gamma
, whereas an inversion

z\longmapsto a/z

interchanges the two factors and inverts them:

(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{\infty})\longmapsto(\alpha_{\infty}^{-1},\alpha_{0}^{-1}).

Proof.

Any self-equivalence of
[\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Gamma]
must preserve the locus where the inertia is nontrivial, namely the set
\{0,\infty\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Hence, on the atlas
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, it is represented by a Möbius transformation preserving
\{0,\infty\}
, and is therefore necessarily of the form

z\longmapsto az
or

z\longmapsto a/z.

As in the open case, two such lifts define the same
1
-automorphism class if and only if their parameters differ by multiplication by an element of
\Gamma
. This yields the asserted description of
\pi_{0}
.
The description of
\pi_{1}
is
Proposition

6.1
(ii): the only nontrivial
2
-automorphisms of the identity arise from the stabilizers at
0
and at
\infty
, and these two choices are independent.
It remains to compute the action of
\pi_{0}
on
\pi_{1}
. A scaling fixes
0
and
\infty
and acts trivially on their stabilizers, hence acts trivially on
\Gamma\times\Gamma
. By contrast, an inversion exchanges
0
and
\infty
and conjugates multiplication by
\gamma
into multiplication by
\gamma^{-1}
. This yields the formula

(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{\infty})\longmapsto(\alpha_{\infty}^{-1},\alpha_{0}^{-1}),

as claimed.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

In the case
\Gamma=\{1\}
, one recovers the familiar automorphism groups

\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong\mathbb{C}^{*}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2)
and
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),

and there is no nontrivial
\pi_{1}
.

7.
The Kato–Nakayama space and the boundary dynamics

This section has two closely related aims. First, it describes the local topology of the Kato–Nakayama space
X^{\log}
as a real oriented blow-up with torus fibres over the boundary strata, and identifies the canonical boundary data carried by the logarithmic form
\eta
, namely the boundary characters determined by the residues. Secondly, it studies the real codimension-two foliation induced by
\pi^{*}\eta
, both through its local logarithmic normal form and through the linear dynamics it induces on the boundary tori, including the topology and closures of the resulting angular leaves. In this way, the section makes explicit the topological, geometric, and dynamical content of the logarithmic boundary.

The discussion uses the standard construction of Kato and Nakayama
[
11
]
; for comparisons with root-stack constructions and their role in logarithmic topology, see also
[
5
,
30
]
.

7.1.
The Kato–Nakayama space and the boundary characters

Let
j:U\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion.

Definition 7.1
.

The standard fine saturated log structure associated with the divisor
D
is

\mathcal{M}_{X}:=\mathcal{O}_{X}\cap j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U}^{*},

with structure morphism
\alpha:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
given by inclusion. This is the usual logarithmic structure attached to a normal crossings boundary.

The above definition means that the local sections of
\mathcal{M}_{X}
are holomorphic functions that do not vanish on the complement of
D
.

Definition 7.2
.

The Kato–Nakayama space
X^{\log}
is the topological space whose points are pairs
(x,h)
, where
x\in X
and

h:\mathcal{M}_{X,x}^{\mathrm{gp}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}

is a group homomorphism satisfying

h(u)=u(x)/|u(x)|

for every unit
u\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{*}\subset\mathcal{M}_{X,x}
.
It comes equipped with the continuous projection

\pi:X^{\log}\to X,\qquad\pi(x,h)=x.

This is the Kato–Nakayama realisation of the fs log space
(X,\mathcal{M}_{X})
, see
[
11
]
.

Let
x\in X
be a point at which exactly
k
irreducible components of
D
meet, and choose local coordinates

(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
on a neighbourhood
U_{x}\subset X
such that

D\cap U_{x}=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{k}=0\},\qquad D_{i}\cap U_{x}=\{z_{i}=0\}\quad(1\leq i\leq k).

In this simple normal crossings situation, the Kato–Nakayama space admits the familiar local description as a real oriented blow-up of the boundary. More precisely, there is a canonical homeomorphism

(7.1)

\pi^{-1}(U_{x})\simeq(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-k},

under which the projection
\pi
is given by

(r_{1},\theta_{1},\dots,r_{k},\theta_{k},z_{k+1},\dots,z_{n})\longmapsto(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,r_{k}e^{i\theta_{k}},z_{k+1},\dots,z_{n}).

Thus
\pi^{-1}(U_{x}\setminus D)\to U_{x}\setminus D
is a homeomorphism, while the fibre over a point of the stratum
D_{I}^{\circ}
with
|I|=\ell
is canonically a torus
(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\ell}
.
Indeed, in the chosen chart the logarithmic structure is generated by
z_{1},\dots,z_{k}
together with the units of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. A point of
X^{\log}
over
U_{x}
is therefore specified by a point of
U_{x}
together with a choice of argument for each local boundary parameter
z_{i}
. Writing

z_{i}=r_{i}e^{i\theta_{i}},\qquad r_{i}\geq 0,\qquad\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{1},

one sees that the compatibility condition with units determines the phase whenever
z_{i}\neq 0
, whereas if
z_{i}=0
the angular variable remains free. This yields exactly the model (
7.1
). See
[
11
, §2]
for the construction of
X^{\log}
and its local model; compare also
[
5
, §3–§4]
and
[
30
, §2]
.

In the local model (
7.1
), one has, for each
1\leq i\leq k
,

(7.2)

\pi^{*}\!\left(\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\right)=\frac{dr_{i}}{r_{i}}+i\,d\theta_{i}\qquad\text{on }\pi^{-1}(U_{x}\setminus D).

Indeed, on
\pi^{-1}(U_{x}\setminus D)
one may write
z_{i}=r_{i}e^{i\theta_{i}}
, so that

\log z_{i}=\log r_{i}+i\theta_{i}

after choosing a local branch upstairs. Differentiating gives

\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}=d(\log r_{i}+i\theta_{i})=\frac{dr_{i}}{r_{i}}+i\,d\theta_{i},

and pulling back by
\pi
yields (
7.2
). This is the standard decomposition of a logarithmic differential into its radial and angular parts on
X^{\log}
.

Assume now that the transversely affine structure is logarithmic along
D
, so that
\eta\in\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
and
d\eta=0
on
U
. In the above snc chart one may write

(7.3)

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}+\beta,\qquad\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C},\qquad\beta\ \text{holomorphic and closed}.

Substituting (
7.2
) into (
7.3
), one obtains on
\pi^{-1}(U_{x}\setminus D)

(7.4)

\pi^{*}\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\left(\frac{dr_{i}}{r_{i}}+i\,d\theta_{i}\right)+\pi^{*}\beta.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
x\in D_{I}^{\circ}
, with
I\subset\{1,\dots,k\}
, and choose local coordinates

(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
on an open neighbourhood
U_{x}\subset X
such that

D\cap U_{x}=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{k}=0\},
and
D_{i}\cap U_{x}=\{z_{i}=0\}.

Write

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\,\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}+\beta,\qquad\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C},

with
\beta
holomorphic. If

\pi:X^{\log}\to X

is the Kato–Nakayama space, then the form induced by
\pi^{*}\eta
on the fibre
\pi^{-1}(x)
is

i_{x}^{*}(\pi^{*}\eta)=i\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\,d\theta_{i},

where

i_{x}:\pi^{-1}(x)\hookrightarrow X^{\log}

is the inclusion.

Proof.

In the local model of
X^{\log}
, one has

\pi^{-1}(U_{x})\simeq(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-k},

and the projection is given by

(r_{1},\theta_{1},\dots,r_{k},\theta_{k},z_{k+1},\dots,z_{n})\longmapsto(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,r_{k}e^{i\theta_{k}},z_{k+1},\dots,z_{n}).

On the interior, where
r_{i}>0
, by (
7.4
) one has the identity

\pi^{*}\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\left(\frac{dr_{i}}{r_{i}}+i\,d\theta_{i}\right)+\pi^{*}\beta.

Now fix
x\in D_{I}^{\circ}
. This means that:

(1)

if
i\in I
, then
z_{i}(x)=0
, hence
r_{i}=0
along the fibre;

(2)

if
i\notin I
and
1\leq i\leq k
, then
z_{i}(x)\neq 0
, so both
r_{i}
and
\theta_{i}
are fixed along the fibre;

(3)

the coordinates
z_{k+1},\dots,z_{n}
are also fixed along the fibre.

Thus the fibre

\pi^{-1}(x)

is parametrized only by the angular variables
\theta_{i}
with
i\in I
, and therefore

\pi^{-1}(x)\simeq(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{|I|}.

Let
v\in T(\pi^{-1}(x))
be tangent to the fibre. Then necessarily:

(1)

dr_{i}(v)=0
for every
i
, because the radial coordinates are constant along the fibre;

(2)

d\theta_{j}(v)=0
for
j\notin I
, because these angular coordinates are also frozen along the fibre;

(3)

\pi^{*}\beta(v)=0
, because
\beta
comes from the base and
\pi\circ i_{x}
is constant on the fibre.

Therefore, when one evaluates
\pi^{*}\eta
on vectors tangent to the fibre, only the angular terms with indices in
I
survive:

\pi^{*}\eta(v)=i\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\,d\theta_{i}(v).

Since this holds for every tangent vector
v
, it follows that

i_{x}^{*}(\pi^{*}\eta)=i\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\,d\theta_{i}.

This proves the formula.
∎

For a boundary stratum
D_{I}^{\circ}
, let
\partial_{I}X^{\log}:=\pi^{-1}(D_{I}^{\circ})
. By
Proposition

7.3
, if
x\in D_{I}^{\circ}
then the restriction of
\pi^{*}\eta
to the boundary fibre

\pi^{-1}(x)\simeq(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{|I|}

is the closed
1
-form

i_{x}^{*}(\pi^{*}\eta)=i\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\,d\theta_{i}.

Accordingly, the canonical object attached to the boundary fibre is not, in general, a single-valued function on
(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{|I|}
, but the corresponding monodromy character

(7.5)

\chi_{I,x}:\pi_{1}\bigl(\pi^{-1}(x)\bigr)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{|I|}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*},

defined by

(7.6)

\chi_{I,x}(m_{i})_{i\in I}=\exp\!\left(2\pi i\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}m_{i}\right).

Equivalently, on the universal cover
\mathbb{R}^{|I|}\to(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{|I|}
with coordinates
t_{i}
, one has the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-valued function

\widetilde{\chi}_{I,x}(t_{i})_{i\in I}=\exp\!\left(2\pi i\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}t_{i}\right),

whose deck-transformation law is governed by (
7.5
). Its image is the subgroup of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
generated by the residue exponentials
\exp(2\pi i\alpha_{i})
,
i\in I
.
Indeed, if
e_{i}\in\pi_{1}((\mathbb{S}^{1})^{|I|})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{|I|}
denotes the
i
-th coordinate loop, then

\int_{e_{i}}i_{x}^{*}(\pi^{*}\eta)=2\pi i\,\alpha_{i},

and exponentiation yields

\chi_{I,x}(e_{i})=\exp(2\pi i\alpha_{i}).

This is the classical residue–monodromy rule for logarithmic regular-singular rank-one connections, now read directly on the boundary torus of
X^{\log}
; see
[
7
, §I.3]
.

7.2.
The induced real foliation and the linear dynamics on the boundary tori

The boundary characters constructed above constitute the first layer of the logarithmic boundary structure attached to
\eta
. We now pass to the second layer, namely the real foliation canonically induced by
\pi^{*}\eta
on the interior of
X^{\log}
and by its restriction to the boundary tori.

Proposition 7.4
.

Set

\eta_{1}:=\Re(\pi^{*}\eta),\eta_{2}:=\Im(\pi^{*}\eta)

as real
1
-forms on
\pi^{-1}(U)
. Then
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are closed, and the distribution

(7.7)

\mathcal{D}:=\ker(\eta_{1})\cap\ker(\eta_{2})\subset T_{\pi^{-1}(U)}

is integrable. On any simply connected open subset
W\subset X^{\log}
on which
\pi^{*}\eta
admits a primitive
\Phi
, the leaves of
\mathcal{D}
are the connected components of the level sets of
\Phi
.

Proof.

Since
d\eta=0
on
U
, one has

d(\pi^{*}\eta)=\pi^{*}(d\eta)=0

on
\pi^{-1}(U)
. Hence both
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are closed, and Frobenius integrability follows immediately. If

\pi^{*}\eta=d\Phi

on
W
, then

\eta_{1}=d(\Re\Phi)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{2}=d(\Im\Phi).

Therefore
\mathcal{D}
is tangent to the common level sets of
\Re\Phi
and
\Im\Phi
, equivalently to the level sets of
\Phi
.
∎

Work in an snc chart as above, and write

(7.8)

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}+\beta,\qquad\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C},\;\beta\ \text{holomorphic and closed}.

On the region where
r_{i}>0
for all
i
, set
x_{i}:=\log r_{i}
, choose lifts
\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, and define

w_{i}:=x_{i}+i\theta_{i}.

After shrinking to a simply connected open set if necessary, write
\beta=dh
with
h
holomorphic.

Proposition 7.5
.

On the universal cover of the real coordinates
(x_{1},\theta_{1},\dots,x_{k},\theta_{k})
, the distribution (
7.7
) is tangent to the level sets of the holomorphic function

(7.9)

\widetilde{F}(w_{1},\dots,w_{k}):=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}w_{i}+h.

Equivalently, the leaves are the connected components of the sets

\Re(\widetilde{F})=\mathrm{constant},
and
\Im(\widetilde{F})=\mathrm{constant}.

If
\beta=0
, this reduces to the complex affine function

F(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}w_{i}.

Proof.

By (
7.2
), one has

dw_{i}=d(\log r_{i}+i\theta_{i})=dr_{i}/r_{i}+i\,d\theta_{i}.

Hence

d\widetilde{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\,dw_{i}+dh=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\Bigl(\frac{dr_{i}}{r_{i}}+i\,d\theta_{i}\Bigr)+\beta=\pi^{*}\eta

by (
7.4
). Therefore

d(\Re\widetilde{F})=\Re(\pi^{*}\eta),
and
d(\Im\widetilde{F})=\Im(\pi^{*}\eta).

Since
\mathcal{D}=\ker(\Re(\pi^{*}\eta))\cap\ker(\Im(\pi^{*}\eta))
, it follows that
\mathcal{D}
is tangent exactly to the common level sets of
\Re(\widetilde{F})
and
\Im(\widetilde{F})
, equivalently to the level sets of
\widetilde{F}
.
∎

Proposition 7.6
.

Keep the local notation above and write
\alpha_{i}=a_{i}+ib_{i},
with
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, on the universal cover of the angular variables, the leaves of
\mathcal{D}
are the connected components of the common level sets of the two real-valued functions

(7.10)

\Phi_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\log r_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\theta_{i}+\Re(h),\qquad\Phi_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\log r_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\theta_{i}+\Im(h).

Equivalently,

\mathcal{D}=\ker(d\Phi_{1})\cap\ker(d\Phi_{2}).

Thus the residues determine the complete logarithmic normal model of the leaves, while the holomorphic remainder governs the regular part of the local geometry.

Proof.

Writing
w_{i}=\log r_{i}+i\theta_{i}
and
\alpha_{i}=a_{i}+ib_{i}
, one computes

\alpha_{i}w_{i}=(a_{i}+ib_{i})(\log r_{i}+i\theta_{i})=(a_{i}\log r_{i}-b_{i}\theta_{i})+i(b_{i}\log r_{i}+a_{i}\theta_{i}).

Taking real and imaginary parts in (
7.9
) therefore yields exactly the formulas in (
7.10
). The identity

\mathcal{D}=\ker(d\Phi_{1})\cap\ker(d\Phi_{2})

is just
Proposition

7.5
rewritten in real coordinates. The final statement follows from the fact that the coefficients of the logarithmic variables
\log r_{i}
and
\theta_{i}
are the real and imaginary parts of the residues.
∎

At every regular point, a leaf of
\mathcal{D}
is a smooth real submanifold of codimension
2
, and is therefore locally diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}
. Indeed, on the regular locus the forms
d\Phi_{1}
and
d\Phi_{2}
are linearly independent, so the claim follows immediately from the implicit function theorem applied to (
7.10
).

Proposition

7.6
is useful not only for its explicit formulas, but also for the geometric distinction it makes clear. The residues determine the logarithmic normal and angular behaviour of the leaves near the boundary—namely the meridional winding, the radial dilation, and the induced linear foliation on the boundary tori—but they do not determine the global diffeomorphism type of a full leaf of
\mathcal{D}
. The remaining local regular contribution is encoded by the holomorphic term
h
, while the global geometry of the leaves also depends on the periods and on the topology of
U
.

Let
D_{I}^{\circ}
be a stratum along which exactly
\ell=|I|
components of
D
meet. On the corresponding boundary fibre
(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\ell}
, it follows from
Proposition

7.3
that

\pi^{*}\eta\big|_{(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\ell}}=i\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\,d\theta_{i}.

Writing
\alpha_{i}=a_{i}+ib_{i}
with
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\eta_{1}=\Re(\pi^{*}\eta)\big|_{(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\ell}}=-\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}\,d\theta_{i},\qquad\eta_{2}=\Im(\pi^{*}\eta)\big|_{(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\ell}}=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\,d\theta_{i}.

Thus the restriction of the real distribution
\mathcal{D}
to a boundary torus is the common kernel of two constant covectors on
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
, namely the covectors with coefficients
(-b_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(a_{i})_{i\in I}
. In particular, the induced foliation on the boundary torus is linear in the angular coordinates, and its geometry is governed entirely by the residue vector
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}
.

The case
\ell=2
already contains the essential dichotomy and provides a transparent model for the general discussion. Let
T^{2}
be a boundary fibre with angular coordinates
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
and write the corresponding residues as
\alpha_{i}=a_{i}+ib_{i}
. Consider the determinant

\Delta:=\det\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
b_{1}&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}=a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}.

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
T^{2}
be a boundary fibre with angular coordinates
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
and residues
\alpha_{i}=a_{i}+ib_{i}
.

(a)

If
\Delta\neq 0
, then the restrictions of
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
to
T^{2}
are linearly independent. Consequently,

\mathcal{D}\cap T(T^{2})=\{0\},

and the intersection of any leaf of
\mathcal{D}
with
T^{2}
is discrete.

(b)

If
\Delta=0
and
(a_{1},a_{2})\neq(0,0)
, then the induced foliation on
T^{2}
is the linear foliation defined by

a_{1}\,d\theta_{1}+a_{2}\,d\theta_{2}=0.

Its leaves are closed circles if
-a_{1}/a_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and dense in
T^{2}
if
-a_{1}/a_{2}\notin\mathbb{Q}
.

(c)

If
\Delta=0
,
(a_{1},a_{2})=(0,0)
, and
(b_{1},b_{2})\neq(0,0)
, then the induced foliation on
T^{2}
is defined by

b_{1}\,d\theta_{1}+b_{2}\,d\theta_{2}=0.

Again, the leaves are closed precisely in the rational-slope case and dense in the irrational-slope case.

Proof.

This is the standard Kronecker-foliation dichotomy on the two-torus; we record the short argument in the present notation.
On
T^{2}
, the forms
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are constant coefficient real
1
-forms. Their coefficient vectors are linearly independent if and only if
\Delta\neq 0
. In that case their common kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is trivial, so the restricted distribution
\mathcal{D}\cap T(T^{2})
vanishes identically; hence a leaf of
\mathcal{D}
can meet the torus only in a discrete set.
Assume now that
\Delta=0
. Then the two covectors are proportional, so their common kernel is one-dimensional, provided they do not both vanish. If
(a_{1},a_{2})\neq(0,0)
, the foliation is given by the single linear equation

a_{1}\,d\theta_{1}+a_{2}\,d\theta_{2}=0.

If instead
(a_{1},a_{2})=(0,0)
and
(b_{1},b_{2})\neq(0,0)
, it is given by

b_{1}\,d\theta_{1}+b_{2}\,d\theta_{2}=0.

In either case one obtains a linear foliation on the real two-torus. On the universal cover
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the leaves are affine lines of slope
-a_{1}/a_{2}
, respectively
-b_{1}/b_{2}
. Such a line projects to a closed circle precisely when its direction is rational, and otherwise its image is dense in
T^{2}
. This gives the stated dichotomy.
∎

Let
T^{\ell}=(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\ell}
be a boundary torus, and write
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\to T^{\ell}
for its universal covering.
As explained above, the restriction of
\mathcal{D}
to
T^{\ell}
is the kernel of the two constant real covectors determined by the real and imaginary parts of the residue vector. Accordingly, if

a=(a_{i})_{i\in I}
and
b=(b_{i})_{i\in I}
, we set

(7.11)

V:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\mid a\cdot v=0,\ b\cdot v=0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell}.

The leaves on the universal cover are then translates of the linear subspace
V
.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
L\subset T^{\ell}
be a leaf of the foliation induced by
\mathcal{D}
on a boundary torus
T^{\ell}
.
Then the closure
\overline{L}
is a translate of a subtorus of
T^{\ell}
.
More precisely, let
V_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{\ell}
be the smallest
\mathbb{Q}
-subspace such that

V\subset V_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
. Then
\overline{L}
is a translate of the subtorus whose tangent space is

V_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
.
In particular,
L
is closed if and only if
V
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

This is a standard fact on linear subgroups of compact tori; we record the short proof in the present notation.
On the universal cover
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
, the leaves are the affine subspaces parallel to
V
.
Their images in
T^{\ell}=\mathbb{R}^{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
are therefore translates of the image of
V
, that is, of the subgroup

\exp(2\pi iV)\subset T^{\ell}.

The closure of a connected subgroup of a compact torus is again a compact connected subgroup, hence a subtorus. Its Lie algebra is the smallest real subspace defined over
\mathbb{Q}
that contains
V
, namely
V_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
.
The final assertion is the standard closedness criterion for linear subgroups of a torus: the image of
V
in
T^{\ell}
is closed if and only if

V\cap\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
is a full lattice in
V
, equivalently if and only if
V
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
T^{\ell}=(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\ell}
be a boundary torus over a stratum
D_{I}^{\circ}
, and let

V=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\mid a\cdot v=0,\ b\cdot v=0\}

be the linear subspace defined in (
7.11
). Write

r_{I}:=\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle a,b\rangle\in\{0,1,2\},\qquad\Lambda_{I}:=(2\pi)\mathbb{Z}^{\ell},\qquad s_{I}:=\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(V\cap\Lambda_{I}).

Then every leaf
L\subset T^{\ell}
of the induced angular foliation is diffeomorphic to

(7.12)

L\cong V/(V\cap\Lambda_{I})\cong\mathbb{R}^{\ell-r_{I}-s_{I}}\times(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{s_{I}}.

In particular:

(i)

the angular dimension of the leaf is
\ell-r_{I}
;

(ii)

L
is compact if and only if
V
is rational with respect to the lattice
\Lambda_{I}
;

(iii)

in general, the closure
\overline{L}
is the translated subtorus described in
Proposition

7.8
, and
L
is dense in
\overline{L}
whenever
V
is not rational.

Proof.

On the universal cover
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\to T^{\ell}
, the angular foliation is constant and its leaves are the affine subspaces parallel to
V
. Fix one such affine subspace
x_{0}+V
. Two points of
x_{0}+V
have the same image in
T^{\ell}
exactly when they differ by an element of the lattice
\Lambda_{I}=(2\pi)\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
. Hence the projected leaf is

L\cong(x_{0}+V)/(V\cap\Lambda_{I})\cong V/(V\cap\Lambda_{I}).

Since
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}V=\ell-r_{I}
, the quotient of the real vector space
V
by the free abelian subgroup
V\cap\Lambda_{I}
has the standard form

V/(V\cap\Lambda_{I})\cong\mathbb{R}^{\ell-r_{I}-s_{I}}\times(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{s_{I}},

where
s_{I}=\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(V\cap\Lambda_{I})
. This proves (
7.12
) and the formula for the angular dimension.
The compactness criterion is equivalent to the condition that
V\cap\Lambda_{I}
be a full lattice in
V
, namely
s_{I}=\ell-r_{I}
, which is exactly the rationality of
V
with respect to
\Lambda_{I}
. The final statement is the content of
Proposition

7.8
.
∎

The vectors
a
and
b
are, by construction, the real and imaginary parts of the residue vector
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}
. It follows that the boundary dynamics is governed entirely by the arithmetic of these residues, more precisely by the rational relations they satisfy. In particular, the naive dichotomy between closed and dense leaves is only a first approximation: in general, a leaf need not be dense in the whole torus, but rather in the subtorus canonically determined by the rational span of the residue data.

8.
The lifted developing map on

and the compactified target

We now pass from the local boundary constructions of

to a global logarithmic lift of the developing map. The first step is to construct the local logarithmic representatives and to prove that they descend to a canonical morphism

The second is to analyse the behaviour of this lift near the boundary, distinguishing radial asymptotics, meridial monodromy, and the rôle of Kummer base change.

8.1.
The local logarithmic formula

Definition 8.1
.

Let

be the covering corresponding to

For the construction below, one works over the interior

, while

supplies the angular boundary variables. The only rôle of the covering in

is to kill the multiplicative monodromy measured by

Fix an snc chart in which

is given by

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}\,\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}+\beta,\qquad d\beta=0.

Let

be a simply connected open subset contained in this chart. Choose a primitive

Lemma 8.2

The function

satisfies

(8.2)

\frac{dz_{W}^{\log}}{z_{W}^{\log}}=\pi^{*}\eta\ \text{on }W\cap\pi^{-1}(U).

Proof.

Taking logarithmic derivatives in (

) gives

d\log z_{W}^{\log}=dh+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}\,d\log r_{i}+i\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}\,d\theta_{i}.

Since

which is exactly (

∎

Changing the branch of

replaces

by multiplication with

\exp(2\pi in_{i}\alpha_{i}).

Hence any change of branches multiplies

by a product of residue exponentials, and therefore by an element of
\Gamma
.

Theorem 8.3
.

There exists a canonical morphism of analytic stacks

F^{\log}:X^{\log}\longrightarrow[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma]

whose restriction to the interior

coincides with the developing morphism

Locally on simply connected logarithmic charts, the morphism

is represented by the functions

of

, and these local representatives differ on overlaps by multiplication by elements of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let

and

be two simply connected logarithmic charts as in

8.2
. On the overlap

, both local functions

and

satisfy the same logarithmic differential equation

Hence

on

, so the ratio
z_{W}^{\log}/z_{W^{\prime}}^{\log}
is locally constant there, and therefore constant on each connected component of the overlap.
The ambiguity in this constant is measured by
\Gamma
. Indeed, changing the primitive

replaces

by multiplication by an exponential constant, while changing the branches of the logarithms replaces it by multiplication by a product of residue exponentials. By the definition of

defines a canonical descent datum only modulo the natural action of

We use here the standard description of quotient stacks in terms of descent data, or equivalently in terms of principal

-bundles together with
\Gamma

; see

17
,
18
,

determines a morphism

By construction, over the interior

one recovers the original developing morphism

.
∎

Corollary 8.4
.

In the local expression of

, one has

(8.3)

\log|z_{W}^{\log}|=\Re(h)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\Re(\alpha_{i})\log r_{i}.

Suppose that along a boundary face the radial term

. Then

tends to

, in

, respectively

Proof.

Formula (

) follows immediately from the definition of

; if it tends to
+\infty
, then

with limit value

or

along the corresponding boundary face.

∎

8.2.
Boundary asymptotics, meridional monodromy, and the roles of Kummer and
X^{\log}

The compactified target

records the radial limits of the logarithmic lift, but not the full boundary behaviour. Near the divisor one must distinguish radial asymptotics, meridial monodromy, and the angular boundary characters carried by

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
D_{i}
be an irreducible component of the simple normal crossings divisor

be closed. Then the logarithmic residue

\alpha_{i}:=\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(\eta)

is locally constant on the smooth locus of

D_{i}

Proof.

Fix a smooth point of
D_{i}
away from the other components of
D
, and choose a local coordinate
f_{i}
defining
D_{i}
.
Write

\eta=a\,\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}+\beta,

where
a
and
\beta
are holomorphic and
\beta
has no pole along
D_{i}
.
Since

0=d\eta=da\wedge\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}+d\beta.

The form

is holomorphic, so the polar part of

along

is exactly

da\wedge\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}.

Its vanishing implies that
da
restricts to zero along the tangent directions of

Therefore

is locally constant on the smooth locus of
D_{i}
.
Since

is irreducible and the smooth locus is dense,

∎

The sign of

\infty

whose three pieces correspond respectively to attracting, repelling, and purely angular boundary components.

Definition 8.6

Thus

as a disjoint union of unions of irreducible components.

Proposition 8.7
.

Let

be a smooth point of a single irreducible component

Since

Taking absolute values in

Taking logarithms yields the displayed identity.
Now

If

If

.
If

∎

8.3.
The logarithmic Betti meaning of the linear part of a transversely affine foliation

Let
(\omega,\eta)
be a transversely affine affine foliation on
U=X\setminus D
, with
\eta\in\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)|_{U}
and
d\eta=0
. We reinterpret the logarithmic lift and the boundary formalism developed to the language of the rank-one logarithmic Riemann–Hilbert correspondence. The closed logarithmic form
\eta
defines the rank-one logarithmic flat connection

(8.4)

\nabla_{\eta}:=d+\eta

on the trivial line bundle over
U
. On a simply connected open subset
W\subset X^{\log}
, the function
z_{W}^{\log}
defined in (

) is a local horizontal multiplicative section of
\nabla_{\eta}
.

It is useful to isolate the usual residue–monodromy rule in the form naturally dictated by the logarithmic boundary.

Corollary 8.11
.

Near a smooth point of a component of
D
, choose a local transverse parameter
t
and write

(8.5)

\eta=\alpha\,\frac{dt}{t}+\cdots.

On
X^{\log}
one has the logarithmic coordinate

w=\log r+i\theta,
and
t=e^{w},

and hence
dt/t=dw
. A local horizontal multiplicative section therefore contains the factor
\exp(\alpha w)
. The meridian acts by
w\mapsto w+2\pi i
, so the corresponding multiplier is

\exp(2\pi i\alpha).

In particular, if
\alpha=\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(\eta)
, then the meridional holonomy around
D_{i}
is
\exp(2\pi i\alpha)
.

Proof.

The identity
dt/t=dw
follows from
w=\log r+i\theta
, and

t=e^{w}
. Substituting this into the local expression of the horizontal multiplicative section of
\nabla_{\eta}
, one sees that the only non-single-valued factor is
\exp(\alpha w)
. Under a full meridian,
w
is translated by
2\pi i
, so the multiplier is
\exp(2\pi i\alpha)
. This is the standard residue–monodromy rule; compare
[
7
, §I.3]
.
∎

We now examine the effect of a root construction along the boundary. In the present setting, this is the logarithmic version of the classical algebraic-extension step in Liouvillian towers.

Proposition 8.12
.

Assume that

(8.6)

\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(\eta)\in\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\qquad\text{for every irreducible component }D_{i}\subset D.

Then, for every meridian
\gamma_{i}
around
D_{i}
, one has

\rho(\gamma_{i})\in\mu_{N}.

In particular, the subgroup of
\Gamma
generated by the meridional holonomies is finite.

Proof.

For each meridian
\gamma_{i}
,
Corollary

8.11
gives
\rho(\gamma_{i})=\exp\!\bigl(2\pi i\,\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(\eta)\bigr)
. Under the assumption (
8.6
), the exponent is an integral multiple of
2\pi i/N
. Hence
\rho_{(}\gamma_{i})^{N}=1
, and therefore
\rho(\gamma_{i})\in\mu_{N}
.
∎

Thus rational residues force the local monodromy at the boundary to be torsion. The next step is to show that this torsion disappears after the corresponding Kummer base change.

Proposition 8.13
.

Assume (
8.6
). After pulling back the transversely affine foliation by the local
N
-th root construction along
D
, all meridional holonomies become trivial. Equivalently, on the Kummer cover every boundary character factors through the trivial character in the meridional directions.

Proof.

Work locally in an snc chart in which

(8.7)

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}\,\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}+\beta,\qquad\alpha_{i}\in\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z},\qquad d\beta=0.

Introduce root coordinates by

z_{i}=w_{i}^{N}.

Then

(8.8)

\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}=N\,\frac{dw_{i}}{w_{i}},

so the pulled-back logarithmic form becomes

(8.9)

\eta^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}N\alpha_{i}\,\frac{dw_{i}}{w_{i}}+\beta^{\prime}.

Since each
N\alpha_{i}
is an integer, the residue of
\eta^{\prime}
along
w_{i}=0
is integral. Therefore the corresponding meridional multiplier is
\exp(2\pi i\,N\alpha_{i})=1
, and the meridional holonomy is trivial after Kummer base change.
The same statement may be read directly on the logarithmic boundary. Before base change, on the universal cover of a boundary torus one has

(8.10)

\widetilde{\chi}_{I}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{\ell})=\exp\left(2\pi i\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}\theta_{i}\right).

Under the root construction, the angular variables rescale by

\theta_{i}=N\varphi_{i},

so the transformed character becomes

(8.11)

\widetilde{\chi}_{I}^{\prime}(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{\ell})=\exp\!\left(2\pi i\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}N\alpha_{i}\varphi_{i}\right).

Because each
N\alpha_{i}
is an integer, a full turn in any meridional direction acts trivially. This is exactly the foliation-theoretic form of the Kummer unwinding of rational residues.
∎

Corollary 8.14
.

Assume that all residues of
\eta
are rational. Then, after a suitable local Kummer base change along
D
, the linear part of the transversely affine affine structure has trivial meridional monodromy. Equivalently, the rank-one Betti local system attached to
\nabla_{\eta}
becomes meridionally trivial, and the boundary characters become trivial on the corresponding logarithmic boundary circles.

Proof.

This is immediate from
Propositions

8.12
and
8.13
.
∎

Before formulating the final synthesis, let us make explicit the precise sense
in which Kummer base change and the Kato–Nakayama boundary capture two
different aspects of the same residue data.
Fix an irreducible component
D_{i}\subset D
and write

\alpha_{i}:=\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(\eta),\qquad g_{i}:=\exp(2\pi i\alpha_{i})\in\Gamma.

The element
g_{i}
is the meridional multiplier of the rank-one logarithmic
connection
\nabla_{\eta}
around
D_{i}
. If
g_{i}
has finite order
r
, then,
after the local root construction

f_{i}=t^{r},

the pulled-back logarithmic form has residue
r\alpha_{i}
along
t=0
, and the
corresponding meridional multiplier becomes

\exp(2\pi i\,r\alpha_{i})=g_{i}^{r}=1.

Thus a suitable Kummer base change removes the torsion in the meridional part
of the monodromy.
By contrast, on the logarithmic boundary one does not remove this information,
but rather records it in angular form. Indeed, if
x\in D_{i}
is a smooth point,
then on the boundary fibre
\pi^{-1}(x)\simeq S^{1}
the restriction of

\pi^{*}\eta
determines the character

\chi_{i,x}:\pi_{1}(\pi^{-1}(x))\cong\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*},\qquad m\longmapsto\exp(2\pi i\,\alpha_{i}m).

More generally, on a higher-codimension stratum
D_{I}^{\circ}
, the same
construction yields the character

\chi_{I,x}(m_{i})_{i\in I}=\exp\!\left(2\pi i\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}m_{i}\right).

In this sense, Kummer base change and the Kato–Nakayama boundary do not play
the same rôle: the former trivialises torsion in the meridional monodromy,
whereas the latter retains the angular boundary data determined by the same
residues.

Theorem 8.15
.

Let
(\omega,\eta)
be logarithmic transversely affine data on
U=X\setminus D
,
with

\eta\in\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D),\qquad d\omega=\eta\wedge\omega,\qquad d\eta=0.

Then the logarithmic boundary of the transversely affine structure is
canonically realised on the Kato–Nakayama space
X^{\log}
in the following
sense:

(i)

for every boundary stratum
D_{I}^{\circ}
and every point
x\in D_{I}^{\circ}
,
the residues of
\eta
determine a boundary character

\chi_{I,x}:\pi_{1}(\pi^{-1}(x))\to\mathbb{C}^{*};

(ii)

the pull-back
\pi^{*}\eta
induces on
\pi^{-1}(U)\subset X^{\log}
a
canonical real foliation of codimension two, whose restriction to the boundary
tori is linear in the angular variables and whose topology and closures are
governed by the arithmetic of the residue vector;

(iii)

the interior developing map admits a canonical logarithmic lift

F^{\log}:X^{\log}\to[\mathbb{C}^{*}/\Gamma],\qquad\Gamma=\operatorname{im}(\rho).

Proof.

Assertion (i) is given by

Propositions

7.3
and
8.11
. Assertion
(ii) is the content of

Propositions

7.4
,
7.5
,
7.6
,
7.7
,
7.8
and
7.9
.
Assertion (iii) is exactly
Theorem

8.3
.
∎

Combined with

8.14
