Modular invariance of characters
of quasi-lisse vertex algebras

Abstract




Abstract.

We study spaces of conformal blocks associated with line bundles over elliptic curves, with coefficients in a vertex algebra. For vertex algebras satisfying suitable finiteness and semisimplicity conditions, which are met by all admissible affine vertex algebras as well as admissible
W
-algebras associated with nilpotent elements of standard Levi type, we prove the holonomicity of the sheaf of conformal blocks over the moduli space of bundles. Furthermore, we show that the space of flat sections of the associated Jacobi-invariant connection is spanned by trace functions on modules. This result provides a substantial generalization of the celebrated theorem of Yongchang Zhu to quasi-lisse vertex algebras. As a special case, we deduce that for affine vertex algebras at admissible level, the dimension of the space of conformal blocks coincides with the number of admissible weights at that level.

1
Introduction

Let
(V,\omega)
be a conformal vertex algebra of central charge
c
. In the seminal work
[
85
]
Zhu has proved that if
V
is lisse and rational, then the trace functions
S_{W}
on irreducible
V
-modules
W
, defined by

\displaystyle S_{W}(u,\tau)=\tr_{W}u_{0}q^{L_{0}-c/24},

(1.1)

span the space of genus-one conformal blocks of
V
, and constitute a finite dimensional vector valued modular form on
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Zhu’s theorem is a key part of the mathematical description of conformal field theories and has important applications to the structure theory of vertex algebras.

The purpose of this article is to establish an analogue of Zhu’s theorem for a broad class of
quasi-lisse
vertex algebras

[
10
]

by replacing ordinary conformal blocks on elliptic curves by conformal blocks associated with pairs
(E,\mathcal{L})
consisting of an elliptic curve
E
together with a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over
E
. The corresponding trace functions depend on both the elliptic moduli parameter
\tau
and another parameter
\alpha
corresponding to the line bundle. The trace functions are equivariant under an action of the Jacobi group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}
extending that of the modular group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
h
be an element of the weight-one space
V_{1}
of
V
, and suppose that
h_{0}
acts on
V
semisimply with integer eigenvalues. We refer to such a vector as a current, and to the eigenspace decomposition with respect to
h_{0}
as the charge grading of
V
. If
X
is a smooth complex algebraic curve and
\mathcal{L}
a holomorphic line bundle over
X
, one may associate to
(V,\omega,h)
a twisted chiral algebra over
X
, and a sheaf over the moduli space of pairs
(X,\mathcal{L})
which we refer to as

charged conformal blocks

[
16
]
[
43
, Chapter 7]
. In the genus-one case we consider the family
T\rightarrow B
of pairs
(E,\mathcal{L})_{(\alpha,\tau)}
parametrised by
(\alpha,\tau)\in B=\mathbb{C}\times\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is the complex upper half plane,
E=E_{\tau}
is the elliptic curve with modulus
\tau
and
\mathcal{L}
is the holomorphic line bundle of degree zero determined by
\alpha
. See Section
2
below for details.

To formulate our results we introduce a charged analogue of the quasi-lisse condition. Let
R_{V}
denote Zhu’s Poisson algebra and let
R_{V}^{h}
be the Poisson algebra obtained as the quotient of
R_{V}
by the commutative algebra ideal generated by vectors of nonzero charge. We say that
(V,\omega,h)
is
stably quasi-lisse
if
the Poisson variety
\operatorname{Spec}R_{V}^{h}
has finitely many symplectic leaves.
This condition is satisfied in many important classes of examples which we discuss below, including
all quasi-lisse affine vertex algebras and all quasi-lisse
W
-algebras for an appropriate choice of current
h
.

Our first result concerns the geometric structure of the sheaf of charged conformal blocks.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(V,\omega,h)
be a charged conformal vertex algebra which we assume finitely strongly generated and stably quasi-lisse. Then the sheaf
\mathcal{C}((E,\mathcal{L}),V)
of charged conformal blocks over
B
carries the structure of a holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-module with regular singularities on the lattice
N\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
, for some positive integer
N
. In particular, away from this lattice
\mathcal{C}
is a vector bundle with integrable connection
\nabla
.

When
h=0
the charge grading is trivial and the condition of being stably quasi-lisse reduces to the quasi-lisse condition. In this case Theorem
1.1
recovers the finite dimensionality result of
[
10
]
.

Our second result identifies charged conformal blocks with trace functions on an appropriate category of
V
-modules. Since quasi-lisse vertex algebras generally possess non ordinary modules, the traces (
1.1
) are not well defined in general. Instead we consider the trace functions

\displaystyle S_{W}(u,\alpha,\tau)=\tr_{W}u_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}-c/24},

(1.2)

where
y=e^{2\pi i\alpha}
and, as above,
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
. These trace functions converge for a much larger class of
V
-modules
W
. Indeed in Section
7
below we introduce the notion of a
stable

V
-module and a corresponding notion of
stable rationality
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(V,\omega,h)
be as in Theorem
1.1
and assume furthermore that
V
is stably rational. Let
W^{1},\ldots,W^{k}
be the complete set of irreducible stable
V
-modules up to isomorphism. Then the trace functions
S_{W^{i}}(u,\alpha,\tau)
converge on the domain

0<-N\im(\alpha)<\im(\tau),

span the space
\mathcal{C}((E,\mathcal{L})_{(\alpha,\tau)},V)
of charged conformal blocks at each point of this domain, and are flat sections of
\nabla
.

The restriction on the domain of convergence is quite natural and reflects the fact that the trace functions generally possess poles along the lattice
N\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
. Nevertheless it is easy to see that every point in the complement of this lattice is equivalent under the action of the Jacobi group on
B
to a point in the domain of convergence, see Lemma
8.3
.

Our third result establishes the Jacobi invariance of charged trace functions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(V,\omega,h)
be as in Theorem
1.2
. The connection
\nabla
is equivariant with respect to the natural action of the Jacobi group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}
on
B
. More precisely the sheaf of flat sections of
\nabla
, and in particular the span of the trace functions
S_{W^{i}}
, is invariant under the Jacobi transformations

\displaystyle\begin{split}[S\cdot A]\left(u,\frac{\alpha}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)&=e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}S(e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}(c\tau+d)^{L_{[0]}}u,\alpha,\tau),\\
[S\cdot(m,n)](u,\alpha+m\tau+n,\tau)&=e^{-2\pi i\kappa(m^{2}\tau+2m\alpha)}S(e^{-2\pi imh_{[1]}}u,\alpha,\tau),\end{split}

(1.3)

where the index
\kappa
is defined by
h_{[1]}h=2\kappa\left|0\right\rangle
.

A few words about the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Using the integrability of the connection
\nabla
we prove an isospectrality result in Section
6
, which in turn guarantees that flat sections of
\nabla
possess series expansions in
y
and
q
of a particular form. Then in Section
7
we introduce a charged analogue of the Zhu algebra
A(V)
, which we call the
stable Zhu algebra

A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
. This algebra plays for stable modules the same role that
A(V)
plays for positive energy modules. Assuming semisimplicity of
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
, we show that charged conformal blocks are exhausted by trace functions.

Our results apply in particular to admissible affine vertex algebras and admissible
W
-algebras associated with nilpotent elements of standard Levi type.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra and
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
the simple affine vertex algebra at admissible level
k
. Taking for
h
the Weyl vector
\rho\in\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}
, one finds that the stable Zhu algebra
A^{\mathrm{stab}}(L_{k}(\mathfrak{g}))
is a finite dimensional semisimple quotient of the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]
on the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. In particular
(L_{k}(\mathfrak{g}),\omega,\rho)
is stably quasi-lisse and stably rational. Hence the corresponding trace functions span the space of charged conformal blocks. Restricting to
u=\left|0\right\rangle
we recover the fact, which follows easily from results of Kac and Wakimoto
[
54
]
, that the graded dimensions of admissible
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules of admissible level
k
constitute a vector valued Jacobi form of index
\kappa=kh^{\vee}\dim(\mathfrak{g})/24
. In the case of the universal
W
-algebra
\mathscr{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
, whose Zhu algebra is the finite
W
-algebra
U(\mathfrak{g},f)
, the stable Zhu algebra
A^{\mathrm{stab}}(\mathscr{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f))
, for an appropriately chosen current
h
, is isomorphic to the finite
W
-algebra
U(\mathfrak{l},f)
associated with the minimal Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}
containing
f
, and the stable Zhu algebra
A^{\mathrm{stab}}(\mathscr{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f))
of the simple quotient
\mathscr{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
of
\mathscr{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is a quotient of
U(\mathfrak{l},f)
. Similarly,
X^{h}_{W_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)}
is the closed subvariety of the Slodowy slice
f+\mathfrak{l}^{e}
at
f
in
\mathfrak{l}
. We thus show that all quasi-lisse
W
-algebras are stably quasi-lisse, and all quasi-lisse admissible
W
-algebras of standard Levi type are stably rational as well, see Theorem
4.9
and Theorem
7.9
below.
This represents a large extension of Zhu’s modularity results
[
85
]
to quasi-lisse vertex algebras.

The appearance of modular and Jacobi forms in representation theory has a long history. In
[
55
]
Kac and Peterson determined modular properties of characters of integrable representations of affine Kac-Moody algebras by expressing them in terms of theta functions, and deduced consequences such as asymptotic growth of representations. In
[
42
]
Feigin and Fuchs developed analogous character formulas for minimal representations of the Virasoro algebra. Further impetus came from the role of the moonshine module vertex algebra
V^{\natural}
in monstrous moonshine
[
45
,
18
]
.

These phenomena are naturally explained by Zhu’s theorem. Furthermore Zhu’s theorem plays an important role in conformal field theory, and the resulting representation of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on the space
\mathcal{C}^{\nabla}(V,\omega)
of global flat sections, appears in Huang’s proof of the Verlinde formula
[
51
]
. Modularity also has important structural applications for vertex algebras. For example,
[
38
,
76
]
use properties of weight
2
cusp forms, including Deligne’s bound on Fourier coefficients
[
25
]
, to obtain estimates on the weight-one spaces of orbifolds of vertex algebras. These estimates were used in
[
36
]
to show that every holomorphic vertex algebra of central charge
24
with nontrivial weight-one space is an orbifold of the Leech lattice vertex algebra.

Jacobi invariance is similarly important. Schellekens used it to constrain the weight-one Lie algebra of a holomorphic conformal vertex algebra of central charge
24
, leading to his list of
71
possibilities
[
82
]

[
37
, Section 6]
; see also
[
28
]
. For lisse rational vertex algebras the Jacobi property was established in
[
73
,
63
]
by expanding (
1.2
) as a Taylor series in
\alpha
, identifying the coefficients with
n
-point functions, and applying Zhu’s results. Closer in spirit to the present work is
[
47
]
, where supertrace functions of topological
N=2
vertex algebras were shown to be flat sections of superconformal blocks, see also
[
46
]
. The lisse condition is assumed in all these works.

It has long been expected that analogues of Zhu’s theorem should hold under somewhat weaker hypotheses. Indeed the space
\mathcal{C}(E_{\tau},V)
of ordinary conformal blocks is finite dimensional not only for lisse vertex algebras, but for all quasi-lisse vertex algebras
[
10
]
. This class includes the simple affine vertex algebras
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
at admissible levels
[
5
]
. Although the representation category of admissible
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is very large, it was shown in
[
1
]
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and in
[
7
]
in general that the
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules in category
\mathcal{O}
for
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
are precisely the admissible modules of Kac and Wakimoto, who had shown that the characters of the latter are modular invariant
[
56
,
54
]
.

The connection
\nabla
on genus-one conformal blocks gives rise to modular linear differential equations (MLDE) satisfied by vertex algebra characters. These equations have been studied since the early days of conformal field theory
[
70
]

[
31
]
, and later in the context of vertex algebras, providing useful constraints, even without assumptions of rationality
[
58
,
10
,
66
,
65
]
.

The 4d/2d duality of
[
13
]
relating
4
-dimensional
N=2
SUSY theories with vertex algebras provides further motivation. Modular properties of Schur indices were observed in
[
81
]
, and in view of the 4d/2d duality, would be natural consequences of modularity for the corresponding vertex algebras. The associated MLDEs have been investigated in detail in
[
15
]
, and flavoured analogues have been studied in
[
78
]
. Here the inclusion of “flavour” roughly corresponds to the passage from trace functions of the form (
1.1
) to that of (
1.2
). In particular, the vertex algebras associated with
\mathcal{S}
-class theories
[
13
,
15
,
14
]
in genus
0
have been constructed in
[
8
]
as chiral quantisations of Moore-Tachikawa symplectic varieties
[
19
]
. These vertex algebras are known to be quasi-lisse in type
A
and believed to be so in general, and their characters are conjecturally quasi-modular and have interesting relations to multiple
q
-zeta values
[
72
]
. Many non-admissible affine vertex algebras are also quasi-lisse
[
11
]
.

Thus motivated, it is reasonable to seek a description of conformal blocks in terms of trace functions on appropriate modules for the whole class of quasi-lisse vertex algebras. By including moduli of line bundles in our approach, trace functions on a sufficiently large category of modules are rendered convergent and, under a reasonable rationality condition, span the corresponding spaces of conformal blocks.

There are many generalisations of Zhu’s theorem in the literature. Dong, Li and Mason proved a generalisation to twisted modules
[
27
]
, a result fundamental to orbifold constructions of holomorphic vertex algebras
[
64
,
75
,
37
,
34
]
. Generalisations to vertex superalgebras with “good” and “bad” statistics were obtained in
[
29
]
and
[
30
]
, respectively. A version for vertex superalgebras graded by rational conformal weights was established in
[
32
]
, and used in
[
9
]
to lift the Kac–Wakimoto modular transformation formulas for admissible affine vertex algebras from characters to trace functions; see also
[
26
,
69
]
. Modularity of trace functions involving intertwining operators has been applied by Huang to rigidity of tensor categories
[
49
,
50
]
. There has also been substantial interest in modularity for lisse but non-rational vertex algebras
[
74
]
. Miyamoto showed that modularity is restored by adjoining suitable “pseudotrace functions”, and the analysis of these functions was a key ingredient in McRae’s proof that semisimplicity of Zhu’s algebra implies rationality for lisse vertex algebras
[
71
,
22
]
. We note that while relatively few non-rational lisse vertex algebras are known
[
60
,
2
,
84
]
, there is by contrast a rich supply of non-rational quasi-lisse examples
[
8
,
11
,
3
]
. We expect extensions of our results in all these directions to be possible.

Finally, our results describe charged conformal blocks in terms of stable
V
-modules, characterised among weight modules by a support condition. Other categories of modules, such as spectral flows of stable modules, arise by modifying this condition. It would be interesting to relate charged conformal blocks associated with these different categories, perhaps in connection with the proposal of Creutzig and Ridout to recover Verlinde-type formulas from distributional characters
[
23
]
, see also
[
61
,
40
]
.

Elliptic curves, line bundles, and modular forms

In this section we recall background material on modular forms and elliptic functions, and line bundles over elliptic curves. Throughout the paper
\mathcal{H}
denotes the upper half complex plane, and we reserve the symbols
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
for a complex variable and
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
y=e^{2\pi i\alpha}
.

2.1
Modular forms and elliptic functions

For each integer
k\geq 1
the Eisenstein series is defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{2k}(q)=(2\pi i)^{2k}\left[-\frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!}+\frac{2}{(2k-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2k-1}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}\right],

(2.1)

where the
B_{2k}
are the Bernoulli numbers:
B_{2}=1/6
,
B_{4}=-1/30
,
B_{6}=1/42
, etc. This series is convergent for
|q|<1
, and we set
G_{2k}(\tau)=\widetilde{G}_{2k}(q)
for
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
. The holomorphic function
G_{2k}(\tau)
is also referred to as an Eisenstein series.

For all
k\geq 1
the Eisenstein series satisfies

\displaystyle G_{2k}\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=(c\tau+d)^{2k}G_{2k}(\tau)-2\pi ic(c\tau+d)\delta_{k,1}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all $\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}\right)\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})$,}

i.e.,
G_{2k}(\tau)
is a modular form of weight
2k
for
k\geq 2
. The Eisenstein series
G_{2}(\tau)
is sometimes referred to as a quasimodular form.

For each integer
k\geq 1
we consider the Laurent series

\displaystyle\widetilde{\wp}_{k}(z,q)=z^{-k}+\pi i\delta_{k,1}+(-1)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n+1}{k-1}\widetilde{G}_{2n+2}(q)z^{2n+2-k}.

(2.2)

At
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
the series is convergent for
0<|z|<r
where
r
is the minimal norm of a nonzero point in the lattice
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau\subset\mathbb{C}
. In this domain the limit coincides with a meromorphic function, defined on all of
\mathbb{C}
with poles on
\Lambda
, which we denote
\wp_{k}(z,\tau)
.

We remark that (
2.2
) differs slightly from the convention used by Zhu in
[
85
, equation (3.6)]
. Indeed
\wp_{1}(z,\tau)=\wp_{1}^{\text{Zhu}}(z,\tau)+\pi i-G_{2}(\tau)z
and
\wp_{2}(z,\tau)=\wp_{2}^{\text{Zhu}}(z,\tau)+G_{2}(\tau)
, while
\wp_{k}(z,\tau)=\wp_{k}^{\text{Zhu}}(z,\tau)
for
k\geq 3
.

We consider, for each integer
k\geq 1
, the series

\displaystyle P^{+}_{k}(w,q)=\frac{(2\pi i)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{n^{k-1}w^{n}}{1-q^{n}}+(-1)^{k}\frac{n^{k-1}w^{-n}q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}\right],

(2.3)

convergent in the domain
|q|<|w|<1
. Notice that
2\pi iw\partial_{w}P^{+}_{k}(w,q)=kP^{+}_{k+1}(w,q)
. This is precisely the series
P_{k}(w,q)
introduced in
[
85
, equation (3.9)]
.

If we denote the limit of
P^{+}_{k}(w,q)
also by the same symbol, then we have
P^{+}_{k}(e^{2\pi iz},e^{2\pi i\tau})=(-1)^{k}{\wp}_{k}(z,\tau)

[
85
, equations (3.11)–(3.13)]
. Let us be careful to note, however, that substitution of
w=e^{2\pi iz}
into the formal series defining
P^{+}_{k}(w,q)
does not yield the formal series defining
(-1)^{k}\widetilde{\wp}_{k}(z,q)
. Indeed (
2.3
) involves the summation
w+w^{2}+w^{3}+w^{4}+\cdots
, and the substitution
w=e^{2\pi iz}
here is ill defined.

It will also be convenient for us to have the following variant:

\displaystyle P^{-}_{k}(w,q)=\frac{(2\pi i)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{n^{k-1}w^{n}q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}+(-1)^{k}\frac{n^{k-1}w^{-n}}{1-q^{n}}\right],

(2.4)

a series in
\mathbb{C}((w^{-1}))[[q]]
convergent in the domain
|q|<|w^{-1}|<1
. As before we have
2\pi iw\partial_{w}P^{-}_{k}(w,q)=kP^{-}_{k+1}(w,q)
, and
P^{-}_{k}(e^{2\pi iz},e^{2\pi i\tau})=(-1)^{k}{\wp}_{k}(z,\tau)
when the left hand side converges.

We recall the theta function
[
77
, p. 17]

\displaystyle\theta(z,\tau)=\theta_{11}(z,\tau)=i\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}q^{(n+1/2)^{2}/2}e^{2\pi i(n+1/2)z},

which is holomorphic on

and vanishes only at

. It satisfies the relations (see the table at
[
77
, p. 19]
)

\displaystyle\theta(z+1,\tau)

\displaystyle=-\theta(z,\tau),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\theta(z+\tau,\tau)

\displaystyle=-q^{-1/2}e^{-2\pi iz}\theta(z,\tau).

(2.6)

It is known that
\theta^{\prime}(0,\tau)=-2\pi\eta(\tau)^{3}
, where
\theta^{\prime}(z,\tau)
denotes the derivative in
z
of
\theta
, and
\eta(\tau)
is the Dedekind eta function

\eta(\tau)=q^{1/24}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n}).

Now we define

\displaystyle\psi(z,\alpha,\tau)=\frac{\theta^{\prime}(0,\tau)\theta(z+\alpha,\tau)}{\theta(z,\tau)\theta(\alpha,\tau)}

(2.7)

(see
[
47
, A.9]
) which we see satisfies

\displaystyle\psi(z+1,\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle=\psi(z,\alpha,\tau),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\psi(z+\tau,\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle=e^{-2\pi i\alpha}\psi(z,\alpha,\tau).

(2.9)

We now consider the Laurent series expansion of

in powers of

. We define

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{1}^{+}(z,y,q)&=\sum_{n\geq 0}y^{-n}-\frac{e^{2\pi iz}}{e^{2\pi iz}-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{y^{-1}q^{n}}{1-y^{-1}q^{n}}e^{2\pi inz}-\frac{yq^{n}}{1-yq^{n}}e^{-2\pi inz}\right),\\

(2.10)

These series converge in the region
|q|<|y^{\mp 1}|<1
. The only difference between
P_{1}^{+}
and
P_{1}^{-}
is the first summation, and in both cases the summation is an expansion of the same rational function
y/(y-1)
. Now
[
69
, Proposition 5]
asserts that

\psi(z;-\alpha,\tau)=-2\pi iP_{1}^{\pm}(z;e^{2\pi i\alpha},e^{2\pi i\tau}).

On the other hand we write

P_{1}^{\pm}(z,y,q)=-\frac{1}{2\pi iz}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}E_{m}^{\pm}(y,q)\,(2\pi iz)^{m-1}.

(2.11)

The series
P_{1}^{\pm}
differ only in the constant coefficient, so we have

E_{m}(y,q):=E_{m}^{+}(y,q)=E_{m}^{-}(y,q)\in\mathbb{C}[y^{\pm 1}][[q]],\hskip 20.00003ptm\geq 2,

while

E_{1}^{\pm}(y,q)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}P_{1}^{\pm}(y,q)-\frac{1}{2}.

A more general class of
twisted Weierstrass functions
has been considered in
[
69
]
(see also
[
27
]
)

P_{k}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\theta\\
\phi\end{smallmatrix}\right](z,\tau)=\frac{(-2\pi i)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}\!\!\!{}^{\prime}\,\,\,\frac{n^{k-1}e^{nz}}{1-\theta^{-1}q^{n}},

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen so
e^{2\pi i\lambda}=\phi
, and the prime means omit the summand
n=0
if
(\theta,\phi)=(1,1)
. Then, our
P_{1}^{\pm}(z,y,q)
are particular expansions of
P_{1}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}y\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right](z,\tau)

\displaystyle P_{k}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\theta\\

2.2
Elliptic curves and line bundles

This section establishes the connection between the analytic functions in Section
2.1
and the algebro-geometric framework of elliptic curves and degree-zero line bundles, which form the foundation for charged conformal blocks.

We introduce a family
T\rightarrow B
of pairs
(E,\mathcal{L})
consisting of a smooth elliptic curve
E
and a holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}
of degree
0
over
E
. The base of the family will be
B=\mathbb{C}\times\mathcal{H}
, points of which we will denote
(\alpha,\tau)
. We now describe the fibres. We consider the following group action of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
on
B\times\mathbb{C}^{2}
:

\displaystyle(m,n)\cdot(\alpha,\tau,v,z)=(\alpha,\tau,e^{-2\pi im\alpha}v,z+m\tau+n).

(2.12)

The quotient
T=B\times\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is the total space of our family, and in the fibre over the point
(\alpha,\tau)
the projection
\mathcal{L}\rightarrow E
is
(v,z)\mapsto z
.

As above we write
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
, let us here and throughout also write
y=e^{2\pi i\alpha}
. The fibre
(E,\mathcal{L})_{(\alpha,\tau)}
can also be presented as the quotient of
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by the following action of
\mathbb{Z}
:

m\cdot(v,w)=(y^{-m}v,q^{m}w).

Remark 2.2
.

The parameter space
(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathcal{H}
carries an action of the Jacobi group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, pairs
(E_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1})
,
(E_{2},\mathcal{L}_{2})
identified under this action are isomorphic, and the isomorphisms can be given in general by explicit formulas (see Section
8
below and
[
33
, Proposition 3.2]
). In particular, if we write
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
for the line bundle associated with the parameter
\alpha
, then
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha+m\tau+n}\cong\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
.

We now describe meromorphic sections of
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
and relate them to the functions introduced in Section
2.1
above.

Let
X
be a smooth complex curve of any genus
g
. The exponential exact sequence of sheaves

0\rightarrow 2\pi i\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}\rightarrow 0,

yields a long exact sequence in cohomology and in particular

\Pic(X)=H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathbb{Z}

takes a line bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\Pic(X)
to its degree. The kernel of the degree map is isomorphic to the quotient

\Jac(X)=H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})/H^{1}(X,\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathbb{C}^{g}/\mathbb{Z}^{g}

i.e., a complex
g
-dimensional torus. The other characterisation of degree is in terms of meromorphic sections. Namely a section
\sigma
of
\mathcal{L}
defines a divisor
\sum_{p\in X}s_{p}[p]
where
s_{p}
is the degree of vanishing of
\sigma
at
p
, and
\deg(\mathcal{L})
is
\sum_{p\in X}s_{p}
. From this description of the degree, it follows that for
\deg(\mathcal{L})<0
one has
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})=0
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
of degree
d
. We write
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
for the dimension of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
the vector space of global sections. The Riemann-Roch theorem asserts that

h^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})-h^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\otimes\omega_{X})=d+1-g,

where
\omega_{X}
is the canonical bundle, i.e., the bundle of
1
-forms since
X
is a curve. For a point
p\in X
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we can consider the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(kp)
associated to the divisor
kp
, characterised by its space
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(kp))
of global sections being the set of meromorphic functions on
X
, regular outside
p
, and with pole at
p
of order not greater than
k
. The degree of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(kp)
is
k
. We can similarly form
\mathcal{L}(kp)\cong\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(kp)
. Note also that
\mathcal{L}(kp)^{-1}\cong\mathcal{L}^{-1}(-kp)
.

If
E
is a curve of genus
1
and
\mathcal{L}
a line bundle on
E
, then
\omega_{E}\cong\mathcal{O}_{E}
and we obtain

h^{0}(E,\mathcal{L}(kp))-h^{0}(E,\mathcal{L}^{-1}(-kp))=k.

For
k>0
the second term on the LHS vanishes so
h^{0}(E,\mathcal{L}(kp))=k
. For
k=0
all we can conclude is that
h^{0}(E,\mathcal{L})=h^{0}(E,\mathcal{L}^{-1})
. But if
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{E}
we know both sides here are
1
. If
\mathcal{L}
is nontrivial then, since it has degree
0
, any nonzero global meromorphic section of
\mathcal{L}
must have as many poles as it has zeroes. So if such a nonzero section has no poles, it has no zeroes either, which is impossible for
\mathcal{L}
nontrivial.

In short, for a smooth elliptic curve
E
the dimensions
h^{0}(E,\mathcal{L}(kp))
are given for
k\geq 0
by

\displaystyle 1,1,2,3,4,\ldots\hskip 10.00002pt\text{if $\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{O}_{E}$}

\displaystyle 0,1,2,3,4,\ldots\hskip 10.00002pt\text{if $\mathcal{L}\not\cong\mathcal{O}_{E}$}.

These spaces of sections are described in terms of Weierstrass elliptic functions and theta functions. For
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{E}
the sum of all
H^{0}(E,\mathcal{L}(kp))
is spanned by the functions
1
and
\wp_{k}(z,\tau)
for
k\geq 2
. Let
(E,\mathcal{L})
be the elliptic curve and line bundle corresponding to
(\alpha,\tau)
with
\alpha\notin\Lambda
now. If we define

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=\frac{\theta(z-\alpha,\tau)}{\theta(z,\tau)},

(2.13)

then from (
2.5
) we see that

\displaystyle\varphi(z+1)

\displaystyle=\varphi(z),

\displaystyle\varphi(z+\tau)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i\alpha}\varphi(z).

Hence
\varphi(z)v
defines a meromorphic section of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
with a simple pole at
z=0
(and a simple zero at
z=\alpha
).

Note that the function
\psi(z,-\alpha,\tau)
, defined in (
2.7
) above, is just
\varphi(z)
up to a normalisation which depends on
\alpha
and
\tau
but not
z
. The normalisation

is more convenient for our purposes than
\varphi
, because the coefficients

of its expansion (
2.11
) lie in a Noetherian ring, Proposition
2.1
.

3
Vertex algebras and chiral algebras

We refer to
[
52
]
for background on the theory of vertex algebras. For the material on chiral algebras constructed from vertex algebras via associated bundle constructions we follow
[
43
, Chapters 6 and 7]
. The definitive reference on chiral algebras is
[

]
.

This section has two parts. Section
3.1
fixes notation and conventions for vertex algebras and conformal vertex algebras used throughout the paper. Section
3.2
recalls the associated-bundle construction of chiral algebras and discusses the notion of charged conformal block on curves, providing the geometric framework used in later sections.

We now recall a few pieces of notation. For a Laurent series
f(z)=\sum_{n}f_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)
the formal residue
\res_{z}f(z)
is just the coefficient
f_{-1}
. If
\rho(w)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}w^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[\![w]\!]
is an invertible power series, i.e.,
a_{1}\neq 0
, the following formal change of coordinates formula holds:

\displaystyle\res_{z}f(z)=\res_{w}\left(f(\rho(w))\rho^{\prime}(w)\right).

(3.1)

One also has the formal integration by parts formula:

\displaystyle\res_{z}(f(z)g^{\prime}(z))=-\res_{z}(f^{\prime}(z)g(z)).

When we write
(1+t)^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
in general, we mean

\displaystyle(1+t)^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n}{j}t^{j},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\binom{n}{j}=\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-j+1)}{j!}.

(3.2)

3.1
Vertex algebras

Definition 3.1
.

A
vertex algebra
consists of a vector space
V
, a vacuum vector
|0\rangle\in V
, a linear map

\displaystyle V\rightarrow\text{End}(V)[\![z,z^{-1}]\!],\hskip 10.00002pta\mapsto Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{(n)}z^{-n-1}

(so
a_{(n)}b=\res_{z}z^{n}Y(a,z)b
for all
a,b\in V
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
), satisfying the following axioms:

•

(Quantum field property) For all
a,b\in V
we have
Y(a,z)b\in V(\!(z)\!)
.

•

(Vacuum axiom)
Y(|0\rangle,z)=\Id_{V}
, and for all
a\in V
one has
Y(a,z)\left|0\right\rangle\in V[\![z]\!]
and
a_{(-1)}|0\rangle=a
;

•

(Borcherds identity)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{j\geq 0}&\binom{m}{j}(a_{(n+j)}b)_{(m+k-j)}c\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}a_{(m+n-j)}(b_{(k+j)}c)-\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j+n}\binom{n}{j}b_{(n+k-j)}(a_{(m+j)}c)\end{split}

(3.3)

for all
a,b,c\in V
, and
k,m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The
translation operator

T\in\text{End}(V)
is defined by
T(a)=a_{(-2)}|0\rangle
for all
a\in V
, and (
3.3
) implies that
[T,Y(a,z)]=Y(Ta,z)=\partial_{z}Y(a,z)
, for all
a\in V
. A useful special case of the Borcherds identity is the
commutator formula

\displaystyle[a_{(m)},b_{(n)}]=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{m}{j}(a_{(j)}b)_{(m+n-j)}.

(3.4)

Definition 3.2
.

A
conformal vector
in a vertex algebra
V
is a vector
\omega\in V
with
Y(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}z^{-n-2}
, such that
L_{-1}=T
,
L_{0}
is diagonalizable on
V
, and
\{L_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfies the Virasoro relations:

\displaystyle[L_{n},L_{m}]=(n-m)L_{n+m}+\frac{n^{3}-n}{12}\delta_{n,-m}c\Id_{V},

(3.5)

for some constant
c\in\mathbb{C}
. The invariant
c
is called the
central charge
of
(V,\omega)
. A
conformal vertex algebra
is a vertex algebra endowed with a conformal vector for which the eigenvalues of
L_{0}
are integers bounded below.

If
a\in V
is an eigenvector of
L_{0}
then we refer to the eigenvalue as its conformal weight and we denote it by
\Delta_{a}
. Given

n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Delta_{a_{(n)}b}=\Delta_{a}+\Delta_{b}-n-1.

We denote by
V_{\Delta}
the vector subspace of vectors of conformal weight
\Delta
. It is useful to introduce the following notation for

:

\displaystyle Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}z^{-n-\Delta}.

(3.6)

In particular we have
a_{(n)}=a_{n-\Delta+1}
, and
a_{0}:V_{\Delta}\rightarrow V_{\Delta}
for all

and
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
.

A
current
is a vector

of conformal weight
1
, such that
h_{0}=h_{(0)}
acts semisimply on

and

\displaystyle h_{n}h=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for $n\geq 2$}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pth_{1}h=2\kappa\left|0\right\rangle

for some constant
\kappa\in\mathbb{C}
which we call the

. In OPE notation

h(z)h(w)\sim\frac{2\kappa\left|0\right\rangle}{(z-w)^{2}}.

We call the eigenvalues of

the
charge
; we denote by
V^{(k)}
the space of vectors in

with charge

.

Let
\rho(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{n}t^{n}=\exp(\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}t^{n+1}\partial_{t})(a_{1})^{t\partial_{t}}t
be an invertible power series, i.e.,

\displaystyle Y(a,z)=R(\rho)Y(R(\rho_{z})^{-1}a,\rho(z))R(\rho)^{-1},

(3.7)

where
\rho_{z}
is the series defined by
\rho_{z}(t)=\rho(z+t)-\rho(z)
, and

\displaystyle R(\rho)=\exp\left(-\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}L_{n}\right)a_{0}^{-L_{0}}.

(3.8)

This formula for

We recall Zhu’s modified vertex algebra structure, which is useful in formulating the modular transformation properties of trace functions. For all

\displaystyle Y[a,z]=Y(e^{2\pi izL_{0}}a,\phi(z)),

The vertex algebra

We note that

3.2
Associated chiral algebras

In this section we follow closely the presentation in
[
43
]
. It is possible to view invertible power series as continuous automorphisms of the topological algebra
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{C}[[t]]
. Let
V
be a conformal vertex algebra. Then the group
\aut(\mathcal{O})
of all continuous automorphisms of
\mathcal{O}
has a representation on
V
via the construction
\rho\mapsto R(\rho)
defined in (
3.8
).

If
X
is a smooth complex curve and
p\in X
a point, the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{p}
is naturally an
\aut(\mathcal{O})
-torsor, i.e., carries a simple transitive right
\aut(\mathcal{O})
-action. Informally elements of
\mathcal{O}_{p}
are choices of local coordinate in a neighbourhood of

, more precisely in the formal disc
D_{p}=\spec{\mathcal{O}_{p}}
, and
\aut(\mathcal{O})
is the group of all changes of coordinate. Through an associated bundle construction one obtains an infinite dimensional vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
over
X
whose fibre over

is
\mathcal{O}_{p}\times_{\aut(\mathcal{O})}V
. The bundle
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{V}\otimes\omega_{X}
carries the structure of a chiral algebra
[
16
]
. We do not explain precisely what a chiral algebra is here, except to say that there is an action

\mu_{p}:\Gamma(D_{p}^{\times},\mathcal{A})\times\mathcal{V}_{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{p},

where
D_{p}^{\times}
is the punctured formal disc, and that a choice of coordinate
z\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
at the point
p
induces identifications
\mathcal{V}_{p}\rightarrow V
and
\Gamma(D_{p}^{\times},\mathcal{A})\rightarrow V[\![z]\!]
upon which the action becomes

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(f(z)a,b)=\res_{z}f(z)Y(a,z)b.

(3.9)

The associated bundle construction and Huang’s formula (
3.7
) together guarantee that this action is well-defined, independently of the choice of coordinate
z
.

Within this context we have the definition of the space of conformal blocks on
X
at
p
:

Definition 3.3

A conformal block is a linear functional
\Phi:\mathcal{V}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which annihilates the image
\Gamma(X\backslash p,\mathcal{A})\cdot\mathcal{V}_{p}
. The vector space of conformal blocks is denoted
\mathcal{C}(X,p,(V,\omega))
or just
\mathcal{C}(X,p,V)
.

Although we will not require it in this work, the construction can be adapted to include multiple points

on
X

-modules at the points instead of
V
.

(V,\omega,h)
be a charged conformal vertex algebra. A genus-one charged conformal block is a function

S:V\times\mathbb{C}\times\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},

written
S(u,\alpha,\tau)
, linear in
u
and holomorphic in
(\alpha,\tau)
, such that for each
(\alpha,\tau)
we have
S(-,\alpha,\tau)\in\mathcal{C}((E,\mathcal{L})_{(\alpha,\tau)},V)
, and
S
satisfies the differential equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}S(u,\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle=S(\res_{t}\wp_{1}(t,\tau)h(t)u,\alpha,\tau)

(3.11)

\displaystyle\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt2\pi i\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}S(u,\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle=S(\res_{t}\wp_{1}(t,\tau)L(t)u,\alpha,\tau).

(3.12)

We shall denote the vector space of genus-one charged conformal blocks by
\mathcal{C}^{\nabla}(V,\omega,h)
.

Using the material on elliptic functions in Section
2
we now cast the definition of charged conformal blocks more explicitly. First we briefly explain for the case of ordinary conformal blocks.

We consider the elliptic curve
E_{\tau}=\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
and the local coordinate at the point
0
induced by the standard coordinate
z
on
\mathbb{C}
. We denote this local coordinate also by
z
. Using this choice the fibre
\mathcal{V}_{0}
is identified with
V
. The bundle
\mathcal{V}
is trivial, and elements of
\Gamma(E_{\tau}\backslash 0,\mathcal{V}\otimes\omega_{E})
are linear combinations of
a\,dz
and
\wp_{k}(z,\tau)a\,dz
for
k\geq 2
and
a\in V
. The space
\mathcal{C}(E_{\tau},V)
is thus identified with the space of linear functionals
\Phi:V\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi(a_{(0)}b)&=0\quad\text{for all $a,b\in V$},\\
\text{and}\quad\Phi(\res_{t}\wp_{2}(t,\tau)Y(a,t)b)&=0\quad\text{for all $a,b\in V$}.\end{split}

(3.13)

We note that

\Phi(\res_{t}\wp_{k}(t,\tau)Y(a,t)b)=0

for all
k\geq 2
follows from the case
k=2
by substituting
Ta
in place of
a
and transfering the derivative to the elliptic function using the integration by parts formula.

We now describe charged conformal blocks. It is instructive to work over
\mathbb{C}
and then pass to the quotient as in (
2.12
). Consider the map
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by
f(z)=z+\tau
. Denote by
z_{p}
the local coordinate at
p
induced by the global coordinate
z
. Let
a\in V^{(k)}
and consider the local sections
(v,z_{p},a)
and
(v,z_{f(p)},a)
. The quotient by the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-action identifies
(v,p)
with
(e^{-2\pi i\alpha}v,f(p))
, so

f^{*}((v,z_{f(p)},a))=(e^{-2\pi i\alpha}v,z_{p},a)=(v,z_{p},e^{-2\pi i\alpha h_{0}}a)=(v,z_{p},e^{-2\pi ik\alpha}a).

If
k\alpha\notin\Lambda
then the function
\psi(z,-k\alpha,\tau)
is well-defined and meromorphic in
z
and, because of the transformation formula (
2.8
), we obtain a section

\psi(z,-k\alpha,\tau)(v,z,a)\,dz\in\Gamma(E\backslash 0,\mathcal{V}\otimes\omega_{E}).

In fact, this section has a simple pole at
0
. We now have a characterisation of charged conformal blocks similar to (

):

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi(a_{(0)}b)&=0\quad\text{for $b\in V$ and $a\in V^{(k)}$ if $k\alpha\in\Lambda$},\\
\Phi(\res_{t}\wp_{2}(t,\tau)Y(a,t)b)&=0\quad\text{for $b\in V$ and $a\in V^{(k)}$ if $k\alpha\in\Lambda$},\\
\text{and}\quad\Phi(\res_{t}\psi(t,-k\alpha,\tau)Y(a,t)b)&=0\quad\text{for $b\in V$ and $a\in V^{(k)}$ if $k\alpha\notin\Lambda$}.\end{split}

(3.14)

Lemma 3.8
.

If
\Phi
is a charged conformal block on
V=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}V^{(k)}
then
\Phi(V^{(k)})=0
for each
k\neq 0
.

Proof.

We have
h\in V^{(0)}
, so if
b\in V^{(k)}
we have that
\Phi
annihilates
h_{(0)}b=kb
. For
k\neq 0
we have immediately that
\Phi(b)=0
.
∎

4
Finiteness of charged conformal blocks

In this section we prove finite dimensionality of spaces of charged conformal blocks under appropriate hypotheses on the vertex algebra
V
. In order to do this, it is convenient to first formulate a notion of
formal coinvariants
inspired by (
3.14
), but expressed purely in terms of series in
q
and
y
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
N
be a positive integer, and let
R^{(N)}\subset\mathbb{C}((y^{-1}))[[q]]
denote the subring generated by the series
\widetilde{G}_{2k}(q)
for
k\geq 1
, by
E_{k}(y^{c},q)
for
k\geq 2
and
|c|\leq N
, and by
E_{1}^{-}(y^{c},q)
for
0<c\leq N
. (Note that
E_{1}^{\pm}
differs from
P_{1}^{\pm}
by a constant, and
P_{1}^{+}(y^{-1},q)=-P_{1}^{-}(y,q)
). We also denote

R=R^{(1)},\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{R}=\bigcup_{N\geq 1}R^{(N)}.

Lemma 4.2
.

For each fixed
N\geq 1
, the ring
R^{(N)}
is Noetherian.

Proof.

For each integer
c
with
|c|\leq N
we consider the ring

\mathcal{E}^{(c)}:=\mathbb{C}\bigl[\widetilde{G}_{2k}(q),\,E_{m}(y^{c},q)\ \big|\ k\geq 1,\ m\geq 2\bigr]\subset\mathbb{C}((y^{-1}))[[q]].

By Proposition
2.1
the ring
\mathcal{E}^{(c)}
is Noetherian.

Since
N
is fixed, there are only finitely many integers
c
with
|c|\leq N
. The image of the morphism
\bigotimes_{|c|\leq N}\mathcal{E}^{(c)}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}((y^{-1}))[[q]]
is Noetherian and
R^{(N)}
is the subring generated, over this image, by the finite set of elements
E_{1}^{-}(y^{c},q)
for
0<c\leq N
. Therefore
R^{(N)}
is Noetherian.
∎

We say that a conformal vertex algebra
V
is
strongly generated
by a subset
U\subset V
if
V
is spanned by vectors of the form
u^{(1)}_{(-n_{1})}\cdots u^{(r)}_{(-n_{r})}\left|0\right\rangle
with
u^{(j)}\in U
and
n_{j}\geq 1
. We call
V

finitely strongly generated
if it is strongly generated by a finite set
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\}
.

Inspired by (
3.14
), we formulate a definition of
formal coinvariants
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(V,\omega,h)
be a charged conformal vertex algebra. Assume
V
is finitely strongly generated by elements
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\}
, and let
N=\max\{|c_{i}|\}
where
c_{i}
is the charge of
u_{i}
. The space of
formal coinvariants

\widetilde{H}
is the quotient
{R^{(N)}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V^{(0)}/S
, where
S
is the
{R^{(N)}}
-submodule spanned by elements

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{(0)}b\quad&\text{for $a,b\in V^{(0)}$},\\
\res_{t}\widetilde{\wp}_{2}(t,q)Y(a,t)b\quad&\text{for $a,b\in V^{(0)}$},\\
\res_{t}P_{1}^{+}(t,y^{c},q)Y(u_{i},t)b\quad&\text{for $u_{i}\in V^{(c)}$ and $b\in V^{(-c)}$ where $c\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$},\\
\text{and}\quad\res_{t}P_{1}^{-}(t,y^{c},q)Y(u_{i},t)b\quad&\text{for $u_{i}\in V^{(c)}$ and $b\in V^{(-c)}$ where $c\in\mathbb{Z}_{<0}$}.\end{split}

Technically this definition of the space of formal coinvariants depends on the auxiliary datum of the choice of strong generators. However this choice is not essential, nor is the value of
N
appearing in the definition, so long as it is finite.

We recall that the vector space quotient
R_{V}=V/V_{(-2)}V
carries the structure of a Poisson algebra, when endowed with commutative product
a\cdot b=a_{(-1)}b
and Poisson bracket
\{a,b\}=a_{(0)}b
. In
[
85
]
Zhu introduced this algebra, as well as the finiteness condition
\dim(R_{V})<\infty
which serves as a sufficient condition guaranteeing
\dim\mathcal{C}(E_{\tau},V)<\infty

[
85
, Lemma 4.4.1]
. In fact the much weaker condition
\dim R_{V}/\{R_{V},R_{V}\}<\infty
(implied in particular by the
quasi-lisse
condition) is already sufficient
[
10
, Proposition 5.2]
.

We form an analogous definition in the charged case.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(V,\omega,h)
be a charged conformal vertex algebra with associated Zhu Poisson algebra
R_{V}
. Write
I
for the commutative algebra ideal in
R_{V}
generated by
\bigoplus_{c\neq 0}V^{(c)}
. The quotient
R_{V}/I
is a Poisson algebra which we denote
R_{V}^{h}
. We refer to the corresponding affine Poisson scheme
X_{V}^{h}=\specm{R_{V}^{h}}
as the
relative associated variety
.

It is easy to see, but not completely obvious, that
R_{V}^{h}
is a Poisson algebra. It is clear that
R_{V}^{h}
is naturally a quotient of
V^{(0)}
, and any element of
I\cap R_{V}^{(0)}
is expressed as a linear combination of terms of the form
a\cdot b
where
a\in R_{V}^{(c)}
and
b\in R_{V}^{(-c)}
for some
c\neq 0
. But then for any
x\in R_{V}^{(0)}
we have
\{a\cdot b,x\}=a\cdot\{b,x\}+b\cdot\{a,x\}\in I
.

For convenience let us denote

C(V)=V^{(0)}_{(-2)}V^{(0)}+\sum_{c\neq 0}V^{(c)}_{(-1)}V^{(-c)},

so that
R_{V}^{h}=V^{(0)}/C(V)
.

Definition 4.5
.

We shall say that
V
is
stably quasi-lisse
if
X_{V}^{h}
has finitely many symplectic leaves.

In particular if
V
is stably quasi-lisse then
\dim R_{V}^{h}/\{R_{V}^{h},R_{V}^{h}\}<\infty

[
10
,
39
]
. Notice that if we take
h=0
then the charge grading collapses to
V=V^{(0)}
, and
R_{V}^{h}=R_{V}
so that we recover the usual quasi-lisse condition. On the other hand if
V
is quasi-lisse, and
h\neq 0
, it is not clear if
V
is stably quasi-lisse. The issue is that
I\subset R_{V}
is not necessarily an ideal for
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
. Nevertheless, we shall examine several classes of examples below, and in these cases the stable quasi-lisse condition indeed follows from the quasi-lisse condition.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{\Delta,c}V_{\Delta}^{(c)}
be a charged conformal vertex algebra. Suppose that

•

V
is finitely strongly generated by elements
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\}
, and let
N=\max\{|c_{i}|\}
where
c_{i}
is the charge of
u_{i}
,

•

V
is stably quasi-lisse.

Then the space of formal coinvariants
\widetilde{H}
is finitely generated as an
R^{(N)}
-module.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{0}
be chosen so that
W=\bigoplus_{\Delta\leq\Delta_{0}}V^{(0)}_{\Delta}
contains a vector subspace of
V^{(0)}
complementary to
C(V)
. Note that
W
is finite dimensional.

The vertex algebra
V
is spanned by the elements

\displaystyle u^{i_{1}}_{(-n_{1})}u^{i_{2}}_{(-n_{2})}\cdots u^{i_{s}}_{(-n_{s})}\left|0\right\rangle,

(4.1)

where
n_{j}\geq 1
for each
j
. Any element of
V_{(-2)}V
can be written, by repeated application of the Borcherds identity if necessary, as a linear combination of terms of the form (
4.1
) in which
n_{1}\geq 2
. Set
a=u^{i_{1}}
and
b=u^{i_{2}}_{(-n_{2})}\cdots u^{i_{s}}_{(-n_{s})}\left|0\right\rangle
.

If
a\in V^{(0)}
then
\res_{t}\widetilde{\wp}_{2}(t,q)Y(a,t)b\in S
and so
a_{(-2)}b
is equal to a linear combination of elements of strictly smaller conformal weight modulo
S
. If
a\in V^{(c)}
for some
c\neq 0
then
\res_{t}P_{1}^{\pm}(t,y^{c},q)Y(a,t)b\in S
and the same conclusion holds for
a_{(-1)}b
. Thus all elements of
{R}^{(N)}\otimes V^{(0)}
are congruent modulo
S
to elements of
{R}^{(N)}\otimes W
.
∎

In the following corollary, and in many places throughout the paper, we will require that our vertex algebra
V
be finitely strongly generated, and the number
N
defined as in the statement of Proposition
4.6
will play a role. On the understanding that
N
will always be as in Proposition
4.6
, we shall not repeat its definition in full. The following result is analogous to
[
35
, Lemma 10.8]
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
V
be a charged conformal vertex algebra. Suppose that
V
is finitely strongly generated and stably quasi-lisse. Then
\mathcal{C}((E,\mathcal{L})_{(\alpha,\tau)},V)
is finite dimensional whenever

0<-N\im(\alpha)<\im(\tau).

We will strengthen this result in Corolloary
8.4
below: in fact
\mathcal{C}((E,\mathcal{L})_{(\alpha,\tau)},V)
is finite dimensional whenever
N\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
.

Proof.

Fix
\tau
and
\alpha
lying in the prescribed domain. Then the series defining the generators of
R^{(N)}
converge at
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
and
y=e^{2\pi i\alpha}
, so there is a well defined evaluation homomorphism
R^{(N)}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Proposition
4.6
implies that the vector subspace
S\otimes_{R^{(N)}}\mathbb{C}
of
V
is of finite codimension. Let
\Phi
be a charged conformal block, in the sense of Definition
3.5
. Then
\Phi\in V^{*}
annihilates
S\otimes_{R^{(N)}}\mathbb{C}
by equation (
3.14
). It follows that the vector space

\mathcal{C}((E,\mathcal{L})_{(\alpha,\tau)},V)
is finite dimensional.
∎

In the remainder of this section we establish the stable quasi-lisse condition for several classes of affine vertex algebras and
W
-algebras. The following result and its proof are closely related to
[
9
, Lemma 6.2]
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra and
k
a level for which the simple affine vertex algebra
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is quasi-lisse. Let
\omega
be the usual Sugawara conformal vector and
\rho
the Weyl vector. Then
(L_{k}(\mathfrak{g}),\omega,\rho)
is stably quasi-lisse. Furthermore the space of formal coinvariants
\widetilde{H}
in this case is finitely generated over
R^{(1)}
.

Proof.

Let
V=V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
denote the universal affine vertex algebra, and
L=L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
the simple quotient. If
E^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,E^{\alpha_{\ell}}\in\mathfrak{g}
are the simple root vectors and
F^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,F^{\alpha_{\ell}}\in\mathfrak{g}
the corresponding negative root vectors, then
V
and
L
are both strongly generated by the
E^{\alpha_{i}}_{(-1)}\left|0\right\rangle
and
F^{\alpha_{i}}_{(-1)}\left|0\right\rangle
which have charge, respectively,
+1
and
-1
.

It is well known that
R_{V}\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
, i.e.,
\spec(R_{V})
is
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as an affine space, which may be identified with
\mathfrak{g}
via the Killing form. The surjection
R_{V}\rightarrow R_{V}/I
corresponds geometrically to restriction to the locus where
E^{\alpha}=F^{\alpha}=0
, in particular
\spec(R_{V}^{h})=\mathfrak{h}
.

Passage to the quotient
L
of
V
corresponds geometrically to intersection with the associated variety
X_{L}=\spec(R_{L})
. For a simple affine vertex algebra
L=L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
, it is known that
L
is quasi-lisse if and only if
X_{L}\subset\mathcal{N}
, where
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is the nilpotent cone
[
5
]
. Thus the spectrum of
R_{L}^{h}
is the intersection
\mathfrak{h}\cap X_{L}\subset\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathcal{N}=\{0\}
. It follows that
R_{L}^{h}
is finite dimensional, which implies in particular that
L
is stably quasi-lisse.

The second statement follows from the choice of
h
, so that our strong generators all have charge
\pm 1
.
∎

Proposition
4.8
applies, in particular, to all simple affine vertex algebras
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
of admissible level

, since these are quasi-lisse
[
5
, Theorem 5.14]
. We recall briefly that an admissible level for the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

[
54
]
is a rational number of the form

where

are coprime, and

if
(r^{\vee},u)=1
in which case
k
is said to be a principal admissible level, and
p\geq h
if
(r^{\vee},u)=r^{\vee}
in which case
k
is said to be a coprincipal admissible level.

The first examples of quasi-lisse

of

level

were discovered in
[
11
]
in connection with the Deligne exceptional series. In particular
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is quasi-lisse for
\mathfrak{g}=D_{4},E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
and

integral in the range

.

We next discuss the stable quasi-lisse condition for affine

-algebras. Let
\mathscr{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)=H_{DS,f}^{0}(V^{k}(\mathfrak{g}))

Let

associated with

. The adjoint action of

Theorem 4.9
.

A quasi-lisse
W

\mathscr{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)

\rho_{f}

W

\mathscr{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)

Proof.

Let
G

\mathfrak{g}
, and

the Levi subgroup of

\mathfrak{l}
. We have the restriction map

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{l}^{*}]^{L}.

(4.7)

We choose
\mathfrak{h}
a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
contained in
\mathfrak{l}
, and denote by
W
and
W_{L}\subset W
the Weyl groups of
G
and
L
, respectively. By the Chevalley restriction theorem, the embedding (
4.7
) is identified with the embedding
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}]^{W}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}]^{W_{L}}
which clearly presents the latter algebra as a finitely generated module over the former, and hence we see that the morphism
\mathfrak{l}^{*}/\!/L\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!/G
is finite.

By
[
80
]
, the restriction maps
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}\to\mathbb{C}[S_{f}]
and
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{l}^{*}]^{L}\to\mathbb{C}[S_{f}^{\mathfrak{l}}]
are embeddings, and their images are identified with Poisson centers of
\mathbb{C}[S_{f}]
and
\mathbb{C}[S_{f}^{\mathfrak{l}}]
, respectively. Let us denote by
Y
and
Y_{L}
the spectra of the images
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}
and
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{l}^{*}]^{L}
under the morphisms

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}\to\mathbb{C}[S_{f}]

\displaystyle\cong R_{\mathscr{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)}\to R_{\mathscr{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)}

\displaystyle\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{l}^{*}]^{L}\to\mathbb{C}[S_{f}^{\mathfrak{l}}]

\displaystyle\cong R_{\mathscr{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)}^{\rho_{f}}\to R_{\mathscr{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)}^{\rho_{f}},

respectively. Then we have

\displaystyle Y_{L}\cong Y\times_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!/G}\mathfrak{l}^{*}/\!/L,

and it therefore follows that

Y_{L}
is finite over
Y
.

Since
\mathscr{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is quasi-lisse, or equivalently
Y=\{0\}
as topological spaces, we deduce that
Y_{L}=\{0\}
as topological spaces as well. Hence

\operatorname{Specm}(R_{\mathscr{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)})
is contained in
S^{\mathfrak{l}}_{f}\cap\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{l}}
, where
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{l}}
is the nilpotent cone of
\mathfrak{l}
. Whence the first assertion.

Next we suppose that
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is quasi-lisse, or equivalently that

X_{L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})}\subset\mathcal{N}
. By
[
5
, Theorem 4.21]
, we have
X_{H_{DS,f}(L_{k}(\mathfrak{g}))}=S_{f}\cap X_{L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})}\subset S_{f}\cap\mathcal{N}
. Hence,
for any
f\in X_{L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})}
, we have that
H_{DS,f}(L_{k}(\mathfrak{g}))
is nonzero and quasi-lisse. Since
\mathscr{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is a quotient of
H_{DS,f}(L_{k}(\mathfrak{g}))
by
[
5
, Theorem 4.15]
, we deduce that it is quasi-lisse as well, and thus stably quasi-lisse by the first assertion.
∎

5
Trace functions and charged conformal blocks

Let
(V,\omega,h)
be a charged conformal vertex algebra. In this section we prove that certain trace functions on an appropriate category of
V
-modules, which we call
stable
V
-modules
, are flat sections of charged conformal blocks in a formal sense. The precise statement is Proposition
5.2
below.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
M
be a
V
-module. We shall say that
M
is a weight module if it possesses a decomposition
M=\bigoplus_{\Delta,k}M_{\Delta}^{(k)}
into eigenspaces for
L_{0}
and
h_{0}
. Here we denote by
M_{\Delta}^{(k)}
the eigenspace in which
L_{0}=\Delta
and
h_{0}=k
. We shall say that
M
is a stable
V
-module if

•

\dim M_{\Delta}^{(k)}<\infty
for all
\Delta
and
k
,

•

there exists
h
such that
M_{\Delta}^{(k)}=0
for all
\Delta<h
,

•

for each
\Delta
there exists
K
such that
M_{\Delta}^{(k)}=0
for all
k>K
.

If
V=V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
then
V
-modules in category
\mathcal{O}
are examples.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(V,\omega,h)
be a charged conformal vertex algebra and let
u\in V
. Let
M
be a stable
V
-module. Then the trace function

\tr_{M}u_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}},

viewed as a formal series in powers of
y
and
q
, satisfies the following identities:

\displaystyle\tr_{M}(\res_{z}Y[a,z]b\,dz)_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for $a,b\in V^{(0)}$}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\tr_{M}(\res_{z}\widetilde{\wp}_{2}(z,q)Y[a,z]b\,dz)_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for $a,b\in V^{(0)}$}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\tr_{M}(\res_{z}P_{1}^{+}(z,y^{c},q)Y[a,z]b\,dz)_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for $a\in V^{(c)}$, $b\in V^{(-c)}$ where $c\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\tr_{M}(\res_{z}P_{1}^{-}(z,y^{c},q)Y[a,z]b\,dz)_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for $a\in V^{(c)}$, $b\in V^{(-c)}$ where $c\in\mathbb{Z}_{<0}$}.

(5.4)

In short, the proposition identifies trace functions on stable
V
-modules as charged conformal blocks relative to Zhu’s vertex algebra structure
Y[-,z]
on
V
.

Proof.

Recall

Y[u,z]=e^{2\pi i\Delta_{u}z}Y(u,e^{2\pi iz}-1).

From the commutator formula and Borcherds identity, we have the following useful formulas:

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\pi i(\res_{z}e^{-2\pi inz}Y[a,z]bdz)_{0}&=(\res_{w}(1+w)^{-n+\Delta_{a}-1}Y(a,w)bdw)_{0}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{\Delta-1-n}{j}(a_{(j)}b)_{0}\\
&=[a_{-n},b_{n}],\end{split}

(5.5)

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\pi i\left(\res_{z}\frac{e^{2\pi iz}}{e^{2\pi iz}-1}Y[a,z]bdz\right)_{0}&=(\res_{w}w^{-1}(1+w)^{\Delta_{a}}Y(a,w)bdw)_{0}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{\Delta_{a}}{j}(a_{(j-1)}b)_{0}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(a_{-n}b_{n}+b_{-1-n}a_{1+n}\right).\end{split}

(5.6)

We prove (
5.1
). By the commutator identity (
5.5
) we have

\displaystyle 2\pi i(\res_{z}Y[a,z]b\,dz)_{0}=[a_{0},b_{0}]

as endomorphisms of
M
. Since
[L_{0},a_{0}]=[h_{0},a_{0}]=0
, and by symmetry of the trace, we have

\displaystyle\tr_{M}a_{0}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=\tr_{M}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}a_{0}=\tr_{M}b_{0}a_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

and so we are done.

Before proving (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we make some preparations. Let
c\in\mathbb{Z}
arbitrary and suppose
a\in V^{(c)}
and
b\in V^{(-c)}
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\displaystyle\tr_{M_{\Delta}^{(k)}}a_{-n}b_{n}=\tr_{M_{\Delta-n}^{(k-c)}}b_{n}a_{-n}

and hence

\displaystyle\tr_{M_{\Delta}^{(k)}}a_{-n}b_{n}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=y^{c}q^{n}\tr_{M_{\Delta-n}^{(k-c)}}b_{n}a_{-n}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}.

If we now assume that
n>0
then we can iterate the manipulation just carried out to obtain the following

\displaystyle\tr_{M_{\Delta}^{(k)}}a_{-n}b_{n}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(y^{c}q^{n})^{m}\tr_{M_{\Delta-mn}^{(k-mc)}}[b_{n},a_{-n}]y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}},

which is well-defined as the sum on the right hand side truncates (and becomes finite) due to the positive energy condition on
M
. Summing over all
\Delta
and
k
gives

\displaystyle\tr_{M}a_{-n}b_{n}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(y^{c}q^{n})^{m}\tr_{M}[a_{-n},b_{n}]y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}},

or more compactly

\displaystyle\tr_{M}a_{-n}b_{n}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=-\frac{y^{c}q^{n}}{1-y^{c}q^{n}}\tr_{M}[a_{-n},b_{n}]y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}.

(5.7)

Similarly, for
n>0
we have

\displaystyle\tr_{M}b_{-n}a_{n}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=+\frac{y^{-c}q^{n}}{1-y^{-c}q^{n}}\tr_{M}[a_{n},b_{-n}]y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}.

(5.8)

We now prove (
5.3
) and (
5.4
). Let
a\in V^{(c)}
and
b\in V^{(-c)}
. By combining (
5.6
) and (
5.5
), and then (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) we see that

\displaystyle 2\pi i\tr_{M}(\res_{z}\left(-\frac{e^{2\pi iz}}{e^{2\pi iz}-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{y^{-c}q^{n}}{1-y^{-c}q^{n}}e^{2\pi inz}-\frac{y^{c}q^{n}}{1-y^{c}q^{n}}e^{-2\pi inz}\right)\right)Y[a,z]b\,dz)_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tr_{M}\left(a_{-n}b_{n}+b_{-1-n}a_{1+n}\right)y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{y^{-c}q^{n}}{1-y^{-c}q^{n}}\tr_{M}[a_{n},b_{-n}]-\frac{y^{c}q^{n}}{1-y^{c}q^{n}}\tr_{M}[a_{-n},b_{n}]\right)y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

(5.9)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tr_{M}\left(a_{-n}b_{n}+b_{-1-n}a_{1+n}\right)q^{L_{0}}y^{h_{0}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tr_{M}\left(b_{-n}a_{n}+a_{-n}b_{n}\right)y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\tr_{M}a_{0}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}.

If
c>0
then we have

\displaystyle\tr_{M^{(k)}}a_{0}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=\tr_{M^{(k)}}([a_{0},b_{0}]+b_{0}a_{0})y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=\tr_{M^{(k)}}[a_{0},b_{0}]q^{L_{0}}y^{h_{0}}+y^{-c}\tr_{M^{(k+c)}}a_{0}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}y^{-mc}\tr_{M^{(k+mc)}}[a_{0},b_{0}]y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

Summing on
k
yields

\displaystyle\tr_{M}a_{0}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=\frac{1}{1-y^{-c}}\tr_{M}[a_{0},b_{0}]y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

where the fraction is shorthand for its geometric series expansion. This completes the proof of (
5.3
). If
c<0
then we similarly have

\displaystyle\tr_{M^{(k)}}a_{0}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=y^{c}\tr_{M^{(k-c)}}b_{0}a_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=y^{c}\tr_{M^{(k-c)}}(-[a_{0},b_{0}]+a_{0}b_{0})y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}y^{mc}\tr_{M^{(k-mc)}}[a_{0},b_{0}]y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}},

which yields

\displaystyle\tr_{M}a_{0}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=-\frac{y^{c}}{1-y^{c}}\tr_{M}[a_{0},b_{0}]y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}.

This completes the proof of (
5.4
). Notice that in both of these calculations, traces on certain charge eigenspaces are rewritten in terms of traces on spaces of more positive charge. Because of the stability condition on
M
, these sums truncate. This is one of the reasons we have distinguished the cases
c>0
and
c<0
.

We now prove (
5.2
). Let
a,b\in V^{(0)}
. We note that (
5.9
) holds for
c=0
, in which case it reduces to

\displaystyle\tr_{M}a_{0}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=2\pi i\tr_{M}(\res_{z}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z,q)Y[a,z]b\,dz)_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}.

(5.10)

If we replace
a
with
L_{[-1]}a
in (
5.10
), then on the left hand side we have
(L_{[-1]}a)_{0}=2\pi i(L_{0}a+L_{-1}a)_{0}=0
and on the right hand side we have
Y[L_{[-1]}a,z]b=\partial_{z}Y[a,z]
. By integration by parts the derivative can be moved to
\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z,q)
, which gives us

\displaystyle\tr_{M}(\res_{z}\widetilde{\wp}_{2}(z,q)Y[a,z]b\,dz)_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=0.

∎

we proved the following useful identity.

Let

be a stable

\displaystyle\tr_{M}a_{0}b_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}=\tr_{M}(\res_{z}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z,q)Y[a,z]b\,dz)_{0}y^{h_{0}}q^{L_{0}}.

The normalised trace function

6
Holonomicity

Let us denote
X=\{(y,q)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid 0\leq|q|<|y^{-1}|^{N}<1\}
and let
U
be a connected and simply connected open subset of
\{(y,q)\in X\mid q\neq 0\}
. In this section we show that the differential equations (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) equip the sheaf
\mathcal{C}|_{U}
with the structure of an integrable connection
\nabla
. Furthermore the equations have a regular singularity at
q=0
.

6.1
Integrability of the connection

For notational convenience we shall introduce operators

\displaystyle[R_{L}S](a,y,q)

\displaystyle=-S(\res_{z}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z,q)L[z]a,y,q),

\displaystyle\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt[R_{h}S](a,y,q)

\displaystyle=-S(\res_{z}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z,q)h[z]a,y,q).

(The minus signs are introduced to make the commutation relations of
R_{L}
and
R_{h}
more cleanly reflect those of
L
and
h
, as in the dual action of a Lie algebra, see below.) Then the differential equations (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) are expressed as

\displaystyle\left(y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}R_{h}\right)S

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\left(q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}R_{L}\right)S

\displaystyle=0.

Explicitly the connection
\nabla:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes\Omega_{U}
becomes

\displaystyle\nabla(S)=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}q}R_{L}\right)S\otimes dq+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\frac{1}{2\pi iy}R_{h}\right)S\otimes dy.

(6.1)

Proposition 6.1
.

The connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{C}|_{U}
is integrable.

Proof.

We must verify
\nabla^{2}(S)=0
. We compute directly

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}(S)

\displaystyle=\left[\partial_{q}+\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}q}R_{L},\partial_{y}+\frac{1}{2\pi iy}R_{h}\right]S\otimes dq\wedge dy

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}qy}[R_{L},R_{h}]+\frac{1}{2\pi iy}(\partial_{q}R_{h})\right)S\otimes dq\wedge dy,

where the term
(\partial_{q}R_{h})
stands for the endomorphism

[(\partial_{q}R_{h})S](a,y,q)=-S\left(\res_{z}(\partial_{q}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z,q))h[z]a,y,q\right).

We note that a similar term
(\partial_{y}R_{L})
vanishes because
\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z,q)
is independent of
y
. For the purpose of computing
[R_{L},R_{h}]
, we now do an auxiliary computation, in which we write
\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z)
in place of
\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z,q)
. We have

\displaystyle\res_{z}\res_{w}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z)\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(w)[L[z],h[w]]

\displaystyle=\res_{z}\res_{w}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(z)\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(w)\left(h^{\prime}[w]\delta(z,w)+h[w]\partial_{w}\delta(z,w)\right)

\displaystyle=\res_{w}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(w)^{2}h^{\prime}[w]+\res_{w}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(w)\widetilde{\wp}_{1}^{\prime}(w)h[w]

\displaystyle=-\res_{w}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(w)\widetilde{\wp}_{1}^{\prime}(w)h[w].

Therefore

\displaystyle([R_{L},R_{h}]S)(a,y,q)=S(\res_{w}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(w)\widetilde{\wp}_{1}^{\prime}(w)h[w]a,y,q).

Now we recall the identity

\displaystyle(2\pi i)^{2}q\partial_{q}\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(w,q)-\widetilde{\wp}_{1}(w,q)\widetilde{\wp}^{\prime}(w,q)=\widetilde{\wp}_{3}(w,q).

Notice that the right hand side is an elliptic function (see
[
33
, Section 7]
), so

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}(S)=-\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}qy}A^{*}S\otimes dq\wedge dy

where

\displaystyle{{[A^{*}S](u,y,q)=S(\res_{w}\widetilde{\wp}_{3}(w,q)h[w]u,y,q),}}

and this vanishes by definition of
\mathcal{C}
.
∎

6.2
Regularity

We continue in the setup of Section
6.1
. We make use of the following standard result, in this case paraphrased from
[
59
, Proposition 8.8]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\nabla)
be a coherent sheaf equipped with integrable connection. Then
\mathcal{F}
is locally free, i.e., isomorphic to the sheaf of sections of a vector bundle.

In particular, the restriction
\mathcal{C}|_{U}
is a vector bundle of finite rank
r
with connection
\nabla
. Since we assume
U
simply connected,
\mathcal{C}|_{U}
is trivial and we may choose a trivialisation
\mathcal{C}|_{U}\rightarrow U\times\mathbb{C}^{r}
with frame
\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})
. Let
u=u(y,q)
be the coordinate column vector of a horizontal section
S\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{C})
with respect to
e
. Then
u
satisfies a first-order system

q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}u=B(y,q)\,u,\hskip 20.00003pty\frac{\partial}{\partial y}u=A(y,q)\,u

(6.2)

for some matrices
A,B\in\Mat_{r}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
. Indeed the entries of the matrices
A,B
are expressed as elements of the ring
R^{(N)}\subset\mathbb{C}(\!(y^{-1})\!)[\![q]\!]
and these series are convergent in
X
.

The intersection of
X
with the locus
q=0
is the set
L=\{y\in\mathbb{C}\mid|y|>1\}
. The matrices
A
and
B
have series expansions

A(y,q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}(y)\,q^{n}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptB(y,q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(y)\,q^{n}

where
A_{n}(y),B_{n}(y)\in\mathbb{C}(\!(y^{-1})\!)
are convergent on
L
.

We wish to use regularity of the connection at
q=0
to prove that
u(y,q)
has a series expansion of Frobenius type, in analogy with the classical theory of ordinary differential equations with regular singular point. To be able to do this, we need the following simple but key isospectrality result.

Lemma 6.3
.

The characteristic polynomial
\det(\lambda I-B_{0}(y))
of
B_{0}(y)
is independent of
y
.

Proof.

The integrability condition
\nabla^{2}=0
implies the identity

[y\partial_{y}-A,\;q\partial_{q}-B]=0,

(6.3)

equivalently

(y\partial_{y})B-(q\partial_{q})A+[A,B]=0.

(6.4)

Taking the coefficient of
q^{0}
in (
6.4
) gives

y\partial_{y}B_{0}=[A_{0},B_{0}],

(6.5)

where
A_{0}(y)
is the
q^{0}
-term of
A(y,q)
.

For
k\geq 1
we have, by Leibniz and (
6.5
),

y\partial_{y}(B_{0}^{k})=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}B_{0}^{i}\,(y\partial_{y}B_{0})\,B_{0}^{k-1-i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}B_{0}^{i}\,[A_{0},B_{0}]\,B_{0}^{k-1-i}=[A_{0},B_{0}^{k}].

Taking traces yields
y\partial_{y}\tr(B_{0}^{k})=0
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence all coefficients of

\det(\lambda I-B_{0})
are
y
-independent (e.g. by Newton identities), proving the result.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
u
be a solution of (
6.2
). Then
u
can be expressed by a convergent series of the form

\displaystyle u(y,q)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{K}q^{r_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}(y,q),

(6.6)

where the
r_{\alpha}
are constants, no two of which differ by an integer, and each function
u_{\alpha}(y,q)
has a series expansion of the form

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}(y,q)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mu=0}^{M_{j}}\log(q)^{\mu}q^{j}u_{\alpha,m,j}(y).

(6.7)

Furthermore, each function
u_{\alpha,\mu,0}(y)
has an expansion of the form

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\mu,0}(y)=\sum_{\beta=1}^{L}\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}y^{h_{\beta}}\log(y)^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}C_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu,0,\ell}y^{-\ell},

(6.8)

where the
C_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu,0,\ell}
are
r
-component column vectors written relative to the frame
e
.

Proof.

The first order equation
q\partial_{q}u=B(y,q)u
can be converted into equations of order
r
on each of the components, in the usual way. By
[
68
, Theorem 3.3]
it is possible to write
u(y,q)
in the form (
6.6
) and (
6.7
), where for each
\alpha=1,\ldots,K
the function
r_{\alpha}=r_{\alpha}(y)
potentially has nontrivial dependence on
y
.

In particular each
u_{\alpha}(y,q)
takes the form (here
M=M_{0}
)

q^{r(y)}\Big(\sum_{\mu=0}^{M}\log(q)^{\mu}v_{m}(y)\Big)+O(q^{r(y)+1}),\hskip 20.00003ptv_{M}(y)\neq 0.

(6.9)

We apply
(q\partial_{q}-B)
to (
6.9
) and, since

q\partial_{q}(q^{r(y)})=r(y)\,q^{r(y)}
and
q\partial_{q}\log(q)^{M}=M\log(q)^{M-1}
lowers the logarithmic degree, we see that the coefficient of
q^{r(y)}\log(q)^{M}
in
(q\partial_{q}-B)u
is

r(y)v_{M}(y)-B_{0}(y)v_{M}(y).

Thus
(q\partial_{q}-B)u=0
implies
B_{0}(y)v_{M}(y)=r(y)v_{M}(y)
, so
r(y)
is an eigenvalue of
B_{0}(y)
. By Lemma
6.3
, the eigenvalues of
B_{0}(y)
are independent of
y
, hence
r(y)
is constant.

If we now substitute (
6.7
) into the equation
y\partial_{y}u=A(y,q)u
, it is clear that the leading coefficients
u_{\alpha,\mu,0}(y)
satisfy

y\partial_{y}u=A(y,0)u.

We recall that the entries of
A(y,q)
, considered as power series, lie in
R^{(N)}
and in particular are polynomial in the functions
P_{k}^{\pm}(y^{c},q)
, and at
q=0
these simplify to

Q_{k}(y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{k-1}y^{-n}.

In particular the coefficients of
A(y,0)
are holomorphic at
y=\infty
. It follows that
u_{\alpha,\mu,0}(y)
has a Frobenius expansion of the type (
6.8
).
∎

7
Stable rationality and the stable Zhu algebra

Definition 7.1
.

Let
(V,\omega,h)
be a charged conformal vertex algebra, and let us denote by
V\text{\textendash}\mathsf{mod}^{\omega,h}
the category of finitely generated stable
V
-modules. We say
V
is stably rational if
V\text{\textendash}\mathsf{mod}^{\omega,h}
is a semisimple category.

Let
(V,\omega,h)
be a charged conformal vertex algebra, and let
M=\bigoplus_{\Delta,k}M_{\Delta}^{(k)}
be a nonzero stable
V
-module. If
\Delta_{0}
is the minimal value of
\Delta
for which
M_{\Delta}=\bigoplus_{k}M_{\Delta}^{(k)}
is nonzero, then we denote
M_{\Delta_{0}}
by
M_{\operatorname{top}}
. If
k_{0}
is the maximal value of
k
for which
M_{\Delta_{0}}^{(k_{0})}
is nonzero, then we denote
M_{\Delta_{0}}^{(k_{0})}
by
M_{\operatorname{peak}}
.

Remark 7.2
.

The explanation for the terminology, in which
M_{\operatorname{top}}
refers the eigenspace with lowest eigenvalue of
L_{0}
, traces back to the fact that modules of “highest weight” in representation theory correspond in quantum theory to those whose energy spectrum is bounded below.

7.1
The stable Zhu algebra

We begin by recalling the definition of the Zhu algebra
A(V)
of a conformal vertex algebra
(V,\omega)

[
85
]
. Let
J\subset V
denote the vector subspace spanned by elements of the form

\res_{w}w^{-2}(1+w)^{\Delta_{a}}Y(a,w)b\,dw,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{$a,b\in V$}.

By the Borcherds identity (
3.3
) we have

\displaystyle\bigl(\res_{w}w^{-2}(1+w)^{\Delta_{a}}Y(a,w)b\bigr)_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\binom{\Delta(a)}{j}(a_{(-2+j)}b)_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(-1)^{j}\bigl(a_{-1-j}b_{1+j}-b_{-1-j}a_{1+j}\bigr).

Hence, if
M
is a positive-energy
V
-module and
M_{\operatorname{top}}
denotes its lowest conformal-weight subspace, then the assignment

V\to\en(M_{\operatorname{top}}),\hskip 20.00003pta\mapsto a_{0},

induces a linear map

V/J\to\rm{End}(M_{\operatorname{top}}).

The remarkable observation of Zhu is that
V/J
acquires the structure of an associative algebra with the product

a*b=\res_{w}w^{-1}(1+w)^{\Delta(a)}Y(a,w)b\,dw,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{$a,b\in V$},

unital with unit
\left|0\right\rangle
, and the map above is a homomorphism of associative algebras. The algebra
(V/J,*)
is denoted
A(V)
. For any irreducible left
A(V)
-module
N
, there exists an irreducible positive energy
V
-module
M
such that
M_{\operatorname{top}}=N
as
A(V)
-modules.

We record the following useful result
[
85
, Equation (2.1.4)]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

For all
a,b\in A(V)
the relation holds

a*b-b*a=2\pi ia_{([0])}b.

Assume now that
(V,\omega,h)
is a charged conformal vertex algebra. Then
A(V)
inherits a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading by charge from that on
V
, we write
A(V)=\bigoplus_{c\in\mathbb{Z}}A(V)^{(c)}
.

Let
M
be a stable
V
-module, so that
M_{\operatorname{top}}
is a left
A(V)
-module. Then since
a_{0}
raises the
h_{0}
-eigenvalue by
c
for
a\in V^{(c)}
, the space
M_{\operatorname{peak}}
is annihilated by
a_{0}
for all
a\in V^{(c)}
with
c>0
. This motivates us to define:

Definition 7.4
.

The stable Zhu algebra of the charged conformal vertex algebra
(V,\omega,h)
is the quotient algebra

A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)=A(V)^{(0)}\big/\bigl(A(V)A(V)^{(>0)}\bigr)^{(0)}.

By construction, the action of
A(V)
on
M_{\operatorname{top}}
induces an action of
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
on
M_{\operatorname{peak}}
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
P
be an irreducible left
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
-module. Then there exists an irreducible stable
V
-module
M
for which
M_{\operatorname{peak}}=P
as
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
-modules.

Proof.

Through the surjection
A(V)^{(0)}\rightarrow A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
we obtain a left action of
A(V)^{(0)}
on
P
. We extend the action to an action of
A(V)^{(\geq 0)}
by setting
A(V)^{(>0)}P=0
. We now consider the
A(V)
-module

\widetilde{N}=A(V)\otimes_{A(V)^{(\geq 0)}}P.

It is clear that
\widetilde{N}
inherits a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading from the charge grading on
A(V)
. Furthermore under this grading we have
\widetilde{N}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}}\widetilde{N}^{(k)}
and
\widetilde{N}^{(0)}=P
.

Since
h
lies in the centre of
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
, it acts as a scalar in
P
, and so
h
acts in the graded pieces of
\widetilde{N}
by distinct scalars. It follows that
A(V)
-submodules of
\widetilde{N}
are graded. Let
S\subset\widetilde{N}
be a proper
A(V)
-submodule, then we claim
S\subset\bigoplus_{k<0}\widetilde{N}
. Indeed it is clear that
S^{(0)}\subset\widetilde{N}^{(0)}
is stable under the action of
A(V)^{(0)}
hence also of
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
, hence trivial by irreducibility of
P
.

We now define
I\subset\widetilde{N}
to be the sum of all proper
A(V)
-submodules, which as we have seen above is contained in
\bigoplus_{k<0}\widetilde{N}
, so we may form the irreducible
A(V)
-module
N=\widetilde{N}/I
which is
\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
-graded with
N^{(0)}=P
.

Finally we apply the Zhu induction to
N
to obtain an irreducible
V
-module
M
with
M_{\operatorname{top}}=N
and hence
M_{\operatorname{peak}}=P
.
∎

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
M
be an irreducible stable
V
-module. Then
M_{\operatorname{peak}}
is an irreducible
left
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
-module.

Proof.

Let
0\neq P\subset M_{\operatorname{peak}}
be an
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
-submodule, and let
N\subset M
be the
V
-submodule generated by
P
. Since
M
is irreducible, one has
N=M
.

By the usual Zhu algebra argument,
N_{\operatorname{top}}
is generated
from
P
by the action of
A(V)
. Passing to the highest
h_{0}
-eigenspace, this action
factors through
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
. Hence

N_{\operatorname{peak}}=P.

Since
N=M
, it follows that
P=M_{\operatorname{peak}}
.
∎

Proposition 7.7
.

If
V
is stably rational then
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
is finite dimensional and semisimple.

Proof.

Let
P
be a finite-dimensional left
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
-module. Without loss of generality
P
is a sum of generalised eigenspaces of
h
of some fixed eigenvalue
k
, and likewise
L
of eigenvalue
\Delta
. By a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
7.5
, there exists a stable
V
-module
M
such that

M_{\operatorname{peak}}\cong P

as
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
-modules. Indeed we form
\widetilde{N}
as in that proof, and then apply Zhu induction directly to obtain a stable
V
-module.

Since
V
is stably rational, the category
V\text{\textendash}\mathsf{mod}^{\omega,h}
is semisimple. Hence

M\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}M^{i}

for irreducible stable
V
-modules
M^{1},\dots,M^{k}
. Taking peak spaces, we recover
P
as the intersection of
M_{\Delta,k}
with

M_{\operatorname{peak}}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}M^{i}_{\operatorname{peak}}.

Each
M^{i}_{\operatorname{peak}}
is an irreducible
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
-module by Lemma
7.6
. Therefore
P
is semisimple. Since
P
was arbitrary, every
finite-dimensional left
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
-module is semisimple. Thus
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
is semisimple.

Assume now that
V\text{\textendash}\mathsf{mod}^{\omega,h}
has only finitely many simple objects up to isomorphism. By
Theorem
7.5
, every irreducible left
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
-module arises as

M_{\operatorname{peak}}
for some irreducible stable
V
-module
M
. Hence
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
has only finitely
many irreducible left modules up to isomorphism. Moreover, each such irreducible module is
finite-dimensional, since it is the peak space of a stable module. Therefore

A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
is a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra.
∎

In the rest of this section we discuss stable rationality of affine vertex algebras and
W
-algebras.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
V=L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
be a simple affine vertex algebra at an admissible level, and take
h=\rho
. Then the category of finitely generated stable
V
-modules coincides with category
\mathcal{O}
. In particular,
V
is stably rational. Hence, by Proposition
7.7
, the stable Zhu algebra
A^{\operatorname{stab}}(V)
is finite-dimensional and semisimple.

Proof.

Let

M=\bigoplus_{\Delta,r}M_{\Delta}^{(r)}

be a finitely generated stable
V
-module. Thus each
M_{\Delta}^{(r)}
is
finite-dimensional, the
L_{0}
-spectrum of
M
is bounded below, and, for each fixed

\Delta
, the
\rho_{0}
-eigenvalues occurring in
M_{\Delta}
are bounded above.

Let
a\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since
a
has conformal weight
1
, the mode
a_{(n)}
lowers
conformal weight by
n
. Hence, for
n>0
, the operator
a_{(n)}
acts locally
nilpotently on
M
. It follows that
\mathfrak{g}\otimes t\mathbb{C}[t]
acts locally nilpotently on

M
.

Next, let
x_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
be a positive root vector. Then
(x_{\alpha})_{(0)}

preserves conformal weight and satisfies

[\rho_{0},(x_{\alpha})_{(0)}]=\alpha(\rho)(x_{\alpha})_{(0)},\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha(\rho)>0.

Since the
\rho_{0}
-spectrum on each
M_{\Delta}
is bounded above,
x_{\alpha}(0)

acts locally nilpotently on
M_{\Delta}
, and hence on
M
. Thus
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
acts
locally nilpotently on
M
.

As
M
is finitely generated and
L_{0},\rho_{0}
act semisimply, we may choose a finite
set of homogeneous generators. The preceding local nilpotence then implies that the
weights of
M
are contained in a finite union of sets of the form

\lambda_{i}-\sum_{\beta\in\widehat{\Delta}_{+}}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\beta.

Moreover, for each fixed pair
(\Delta,r)
, only finitely many
\mathfrak{h}
-weights
can occur in such a finite union of downward affine cones. Since
M_{\Delta}^{(r)}
is
finite-dimensional by stability, it follows that the affine weight spaces of
M
are
finite-dimensional. Therefore
M
belongs to category
\mathcal{O}
.

Conversely, let
M\in\mathcal{O}
. Then
M
has finite-dimensional

\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
-weight spaces and its weights are contained in a finite
union of downward affine cones. This implies that the
L_{0}
-spectrum is bounded
below. Moreover, for each fixed conformal weight
\Delta
and each
r
, only
finitely many finite
\mathfrak{h}
-weights
\mu
with

can occur in
this finite union of cones. Hence

is a finite direct sum of
finite-dimensional
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
-weight spaces, and is therefore
finite-dimensional. Finally, for each fixed
\Delta
, the
\rho_{0}
-eigenvalues are
bounded above. Thus

is stable.

Therefore the category of finitely generated stable
V
-modules coincides with the
category of
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules in
\mathcal{O}
. Since
k
is admissible, this category
is semisimple by
[
7
, Main Theorem]
. Hence
V
is stably rational. The final
assertion follows from Proposition
7.7
.
∎

7.2
Exhaustion by trace functions

In this subsection we complete the proof of Theorem

. Let

[

]
, applied first to the

. If

.

By Proposition

, applied to a local trivialisation of
\mathcal{C}
, the block

)–(

(

q
,

,

indexes a
y
-sector

, and

at

. We have

\displaystyle S(a,y,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{K}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mu=0}^{M_{\alpha,j}}q^{r_{\alpha}}\log(q)^{\mu}\,q^{j}\,S_{\alpha,\mu,j}(a,y),

(7.2)

\displaystyle\text{in which}\hskip 10.00002ptS_{\alpha,\mu,0}(a,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta=1}^{L}\sum_{\nu=0}^{N_{\alpha,\mu,\beta}}y^{h_{\beta}}\log(y)^{\nu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}C_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu,0,\ell}(a)\,y^{-\ell}.

(7.3)

Each

For

R^{(N)}
as well as the differential equation and the relations are homogeneous if we assign degrees as follows

\displaystyle\deg(\widetilde{G}_{k}(q))

\displaystyle=k

\displaystyle\deg(u)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{u}

\displaystyle\deg(P_{k}(y^{c},q))

\displaystyle=k,

\displaystyle\deg(\partial_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=1.

It follows that the entries
A_{ij}
of
A
are homogeneous in the sense that
\deg(A_{ij})=1+\Delta(u_{j})-\Delta(u_{i})
for each
i,j
. It is convenient, then, for us to say that a matrix
B\in\Mat_{r\times r}(R^{(N)})
has degree
a
if
\deg(B_{ij})=a+\Delta(u_{j})-\Delta(u_{i})
for each
i,j
. Then degree is clearly multiplicative. The equation
S^{\prime}=AS
is homogeneous, as are the equations
S^{\prime\prime}=(A^{\prime}+A^{2})S
,
S^{(3)}=(A^{\prime\prime}+3AA^{\prime}+A^{3})S
, etc., obtained by successive differentiation. Here we have used that
\partial_{\alpha}
acts in
R^{(N)}
with degree
+1
. Let us write these equations in general as
S^{(i)}=P_{i}S
. For fixed
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
we may eliminate variables to obtain an ODE of order
N
for
S_{j}
, namely

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}f_{i}S_{j}^{(i)}=0.

Later we will normalise so that
f_{N}=1
, but for now we may clear denominators and assume
f_{i}\in R^{(N)}
for all
i
. The coefficients
f_{i}
must then satisfy

\sum_{j=0}^{N}(P_{i})_{jk}f_{i}=0,\hskip 10.00002ptk=1,\ldots,r.

Since
\deg((P_{i})_{jk})=i+\Delta(u_{k})-\Delta(u_{j})
, we have
\deg(f_{i})=\varepsilon-i
for some
\varepsilon
.

The final observation is that for
f\in R^{(N)}
of degree
k
, the poles of
f(\alpha,\tau)
on the lattice
N\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
are of order at most
k
. It follows, then, that after normalisation so that
f_{N}=1
, the function
f_{i}
has poles of order at most
N-i
. The ODE is thus Fuchsian.
∎

The spaces

and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

\displaystyle\ad_{h_{[1]}}(L_{([m])})

\displaystyle=h_{m}

\displaystyle\ad_{h_{[1]}}^{2}(L_{([m])})

\displaystyle=\ad_{h_{[1]}}(h_{[m]})=\delta_{m,-1}\left<h,h\right>=\delta_{m,-1}\left<h,h\right>

Notice that

\displaystyle\text{\eqref{eqn:C1'}}={}

\displaystyle-2\pi ic\nabla_{u}e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}(c\tau+d)^{\nabla_{u}+1}S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2\pi ic\nabla_{u}e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}(c\tau+d)S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}(c\tau+d)^{L_{[0]}}u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2\pi ic\nabla_{u}(c\tau+d)S^{\prime}((v^{\prime},z^{\prime},u),\alpha^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}).

The main term (
C10
) continues as

\displaystyle\text{\eqref{eqn:C10}}={}

\displaystyle e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}\sum_{i}P^{i+2}(\tau^{\prime})S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}(c\tau+d)^{L_{[0]}}L_{[i]}u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}P^{i+2}(\tau^{\prime})S^{\prime}((v^{\prime},z^{\prime},L_{[i]}u),\alpha^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle S^{\prime}((v^{\prime},z^{\prime},\res_{t}\wp_{1}(t,\tau^{\prime})L[t]u),\alpha^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}).

Next is

\displaystyle\text{(C2)}={}

\displaystyle-2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}(c\tau+d)^{\nabla_{u}+2}S((v,z,\res_{t}\wp_{1}(t,\tau)e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}h[t]u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2\pi i{c\alpha}e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}(c\tau+d)^{\nabla_{u}+1}\sum_{i}P^{i+1}(\tau)S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}h_{[i]}u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2\pi i{c\alpha}e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}\sum_{i}(c\tau+d)^{\nabla_{u}-i}P^{i+1}(\tau^{\prime})S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}h_{[i]}u),\alpha,\tau)

(C20)

\displaystyle+(2\pi ic)^{2}\alpha e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}(c\tau+d)^{\nabla_{u}}S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}h_{[1]}u),\alpha,\tau).

(C2’)

Note that

\displaystyle\text{\eqref{eqn:C2'}}={}

\displaystyle(2\pi ic)^{2}\alpha e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}(c\tau+d)S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}(c\tau+d)^{L_{[0]}}h_{[1]}u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(2\pi ic)^{2}\alpha(c\tau+d)S^{\prime}((v^{\prime},z^{\prime},h_{[1]}u),\alpha^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}).

The main term (
C20
) continues as

\displaystyle\text{\eqref{eqn:C20}}={}

\displaystyle-2\pi i{c\alpha}e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}\sum_{i}(c\tau+d)^{\nabla_{u}-i}P^{i+1}(\tau^{\prime})S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}h_{[i]}u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2\pi i{c\alpha}e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}S((v,z,\res_{t}\wp_{1}(t,\tau^{\prime})e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}(c\tau+d)^{L_{[0]}}h[t]u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2\pi i{c\alpha}S^{\prime}((v^{\prime},z^{\prime},\res_{t}\wp_{1}(t,\tau^{\prime})h[t]u),\alpha^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}).

Next is (C3) which simplifies as:

\displaystyle\text{(C3)}={}

\displaystyle\frac{\left<h,h\right>}{2}(2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d})^{2}e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}(c\tau+d)^{\nabla_{u}+2}S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{\left<h,h\right>}{2}(2\pi i{c\alpha})^{2}e^{2\pi i\kappa\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\alpha^{2}}S((v,z,e^{2\pi i\frac{c\alpha}{c\tau+d}h_{[1]}}(c\tau+d)^{L_{[0]}}u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{\left<h,h\right>}{2}(2\pi i{c\alpha})^{2}S^{\prime}((v^{\prime},z^{\prime},u),\alpha^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}).

Next we note that (
I2
) + (C20) = 0, (D1) + (C3) = 0 (assuming
\kappa=\left<h,h\right>/2
), (D2) + (C1’) = 0, (D3) + (C2’) = 0. The only remaining term is (C10), so we have proved (
8.3
) at last.

A.2
Verification of invariance under elliptic transformations

Let
(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})=(m,n)\cdot(\alpha,\tau)
as in Section
8
. The partial derivatives in
\tau
and
\alpha
transform as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-m\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}.

y
-equation

S((v,z,u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\left(e^{-2\pi i\kappa(m^{2}\tau+2m\alpha)}S((v,z,e^{2\pi imh_{[1]}}u),\alpha,\tau)\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2\pi i(2m\kappa)e^{-2\pi i\kappa(m^{2}\tau+2m\alpha)}S((v,z,e^{-2\pi imh_{[1]}}u),\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle+e^{-2\pi i\kappa(m^{2}\tau+2m\alpha)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}S((v,z,e^{-2\pi imh_{[1]}}u),\alpha,\tau).

h[t]
