Is there a room for radical salvage resection in initially-unresectable non-small cell lung cancer patients after EGFR-TKI based therapy?
Authors: Feng Su, Hanzhang Chen, Guilin Peng, Jianxing He, Jingpei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) constitute the standard first-line systemic therapy for patients with unresectable epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Despite their initial efficacy, the majority of patients inevitably develop acquired resistance or experience local recurrence. This study aimed to evaluate the safety and feasibility of minimally invasive radical salvage surgery following TKI-based therapy in a cohort of patients with initially unresectable NSCLC.MethodsThis retrospective single-center study evaluated patients who underwent radical salvage lung resection following TKI-based therapy. Surgical safety and short-term efficacy were assessed, and findings were compared with results from previously published studies.ResultsTwenty-six patients underwent salvage surgery following first-line treatment with third-generation TKIs; 19 received TKI monotherapy, while seven received a combination of TKIs and chemotherapy. All patients underwent anatomical lung resection, with two cases requiring conversion to open thoracotomy. R0 resection was achieved in 96.2%. An R0 resection was achieved in 96.2% of cases. The median operative time was 145 minutes (range, 105–240 minutes), and the median intraoperative blood loss was 20 mL (range, 10–700 mL). Postoperative complications occurred in four patients (15.4%), including two major events in individuals who had received preoperative TKI therapy for more than 6 months. Compared to those with shorter treatment durations, patients with prolonged preoperative TKI exposure exhibited a longer postoperative hospital stay, although operative time, blood loss, and chest tube duration were comparable. Our perioperative outcomes were consistent with those reported in six prior retrospective studies.ConclusionsRadical salvage lung resection following TKI therapy is a feasible and safe option for carefully selected patients with initially unresectable EGFR-mutant NSCLC. This approach yields high rates of complete (R0) resection with acceptable morbidity. However, patients who have undergone extended preoperative TKI treatment may present with increased technical challenges and warrant meticulous perioperative planning.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Radical salvage resection after third-generation tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) therapy for initially unresectable epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) achieved a 96.2% R0 resection rate with an acceptable overall complication rate of 15.4%.

• Video-assisted minimally invasive surgery (MIS) was feasible in the majority (88.5%) of these technically challenging salvage cases.

• Prolonged preoperative TKI treatment (≥6 months) was associated with longer postoperative hospital stays and a potential increase in major complications.

What is known and what is new?

• Third-generation TKIs are the standard first-line treatment for EGFR-mutant NSCLC, but salvage surgery for initially unresectable disease remains rare due to concerns regarding surgical safety, technical difficulty, and fibrosis-induced adhesions.

• This study provides robust evidence that radical salvage resection is both safe and highly effective in carefully selected patients, demonstrating high R0 rates and MIS feasibility.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Salvage resection should be actively considered as a viable curative option for responding patients rather than being dismissed due to perceived technical risks.

• Multidisciplinary teams should carefully weigh the duration of preoperative TKI therapy against surgical complexity.

• Enhanced preoperative assessment and perioperative management protocols are warranted specifically for patients receiving long-term TKI therapy prior to salvage surgery.

Introduction

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) constitute the standard first-line systemic therapy for patients with EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), as endorsed by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines (1). Despite their substantial initial clinical efficacy, the development of acquired resistance is nearly universal, and durable complete remission achieved through systemic therapy alone remains elusive. Even in cases demonstrating significant radiographic tumor regression, residual viable disease or subsequent recurrence frequently persists (2,3). For patients harboring persistent or recurrent tumors following EGFR-TKI therapy, salvage surgical resection represents a potential therapeutic strategy to enhance long-term survival outcomes. However, robust evidence regarding the safety profile and short-term perioperative outcomes of this approach remains scarce in the current literature.

To address this knowledge gap, we conducted a retrospective analysis to evaluate the safety and short-term oncologic outcomes of minimally invasive radical salvage surgery in a cohort of initially unresectable NSCLC patients. Furthermore, we performed a comprehensive review of previously published studies to contextualize our findings within the existing body of evidence regarding the safety and efficacy of salvage surgery in this clinical setting. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2026-0504/rc).

Methods

Study population

This retrospective study enrolled patients who underwent video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) for minimally invasive radical salvage lung resection following EGFR-TKI-based therapy at The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University (Guangzhou, China) between July 2022 and April 2024. Eligibility criteria for inclusion were as follows: (I) initial diagnosis of lung cancer deemed unresectable due to distant metastasis, extensive pulmonary parenchymal invasion, or bulky lymph node involvement; (II) histopathological or cytological confirmation of NSCLC prior to systemic therapy; (III) identification of an EGFR driver mutation in tumor biopsy specimens; (IV) administration of EGFR-TKIs with or without concurrent chemotherapy; (V) absence of initial surgical intent; and (VI) subsequent performance of curative-intent surgical resection for the primary tumor. Patients were excluded if they presented with metachronous or recurrent malignancies, received definitive systemic therapy with curative intent, underwent planned neoadjuvant treatment, exhibited carcinoid tumor histology, received wedge resection, or underwent surgery exclusively for diagnostic or palliative purposes (Figure 1). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University (No. 2022-181). Due to the retrospective design, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Clinical evaluation and treatments

Initial staging for all patients included contrast-enhanced chest computed tomography (CT). Fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (FDG-PET) and brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) were selectively performed in cases presenting with bulky mediastinal lymphadenopathy or suspected central nervous system metastases. Histopathological confirmation and molecular profiling for driver mutations were obtained via fiberoptic bronchoscopy or percutaneous needle biopsy, depending on tumor location. Targeted therapy primarily consisted of third-generation TKIs including furmonertinib (4), osimertinib (5) and almonertinib (6). Platinum-based chemotherapy combined with pemetrexed was administered to patients with distant metastasis or uncommon EGFR mutations. Tumor response was monitored every 2–3 months during systemic therapy and evaluated according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) (7). Surgical resection was considered when the following standardized criteria were met: (I) the primary lung tumor demonstrated a partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or localized recurrence following an initial clinical response; (II) extrapulmonary metastases had either achieved complete radiographic regression or were deemed technically resectable; (III) complete resection was judged achievable by the attending thoracic surgeon; and (IV) no absolute contraindications to surgery or severe related adverse events were present. All decisions regarding preoperative clinical assessment, systemic treatment selection, response evaluation, surgical feasibility, and postoperative management were made through consensus by a multidisciplinary tumor board comprising thoracic surgeons, medical oncologists, and radiation oncologists. Patients in whom complete resection was deemed high-risk or unfeasible were managed with definitive radiotherapy or systemic therapy and were excluded from the study cohort.

Statistical analysis

Baseline demographic and clinical data collected prior to systemic therapy included sex, age, comorbidities, smoking history, pathological diagnosis, EGFR mutation subtype and clinical tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage according to the Eighth Edition of the Lung Cancer Staging Classification. During systemic therapy, details regarding treatment regimen, duration, and best radiographic response were prospectively documented. Preoperative clinical characteristics, surgical approach, operative time, estimated blood loss, postoperative complications, and final pathological findings were systematically recorded. Postoperative adjuvant treatment decisions were formulated by a multidisciplinary tumor board based on ypTNM staging and molecular residual disease status.

Follow-up assessments were scheduled at 3- to 6-month intervals at the discretion of the treating oncologist. Clinical, pathological, and perioperative outcomes were summarized using descriptive statistics. The primary endpoints of this study were surgical feasibility and safety, defined by rates of R0 resection, incidence of major postoperative complications, and length of postoperative hospital stay. Secondary endpoints included recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS).

Continuous variables were reported as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) or means with standard deviations (SDs), depending on distribution; categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Given the small sample size and non-normal distribution of key variables, between-group comparisons were performed using non-parametric tests: the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables and Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Effect sizes were reported with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Survival outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.1.0; Vienna, Austria; https://www.r-project.org).

Study search and evaluation

A systematic literature search was performed using the keywords “lung cancer”, “targeted therapy”, and “salvage surgery”. Eligible studies were those evaluating the safety and efficacy of radical salvage surgery in patients with initially advanced or metastatic NSCLC who achieved disease control following EGFR-TKI based targeted therapy. Studies involving immunotherapy, chemotherapy alone, or non-targeted systemic agents were excluded.

Data extracted from eligible studies included the first author, year of publication, sample size, and perioperative outcomes. Two authors (F.S. and J.L.) independently performed data extraction to ensure accuracy. Heterogeneity among studies was assessed using the I2 statistic; a two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 26 patients (10 male, 16 female) who underwent salvage resection for initially unresectable NSCLC following systemic targeted therapy and were included in the analysis (Table S1). The median age was 54.5 years (range, 33–76 years). At diagnosis, 12 patients presented with comorbidities such as low body mass index, hypertension, diabetes, and Parkinson’s disease. Notably, Patient 26 had multiple comorbidities, including hypertension, diabetes, and a history of both myocardial and cerebral infarction. Preoperative pulmonary function tests showed a median predicted forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) of 89.9% (range, 50.8–138.6%) and a median predicted diffusion capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO) of 78.0% (range, 55.6–120.1%). Patient 24 was diagnosed preoperatively with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

Most patients were initially considered unresectable due to mediastinal lymph node involvement or pleural metastases (Table S2). Patient 8 and Patient 23 presented with ipsilateral lobe lesions and suspected nodal involvement, while Patient 9, though staged as T3N0M0, had a large tumor encasing the pulmonary artery (Figure S1). All patients were diagnosed with EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma based on biopsy. Most harbored common mutations; however, two had uncommon exon 18 variants and one carried a compound mutation (8). First-line therapy primarily consisted of third-generation TKI monotherapy (furmonertinib, osimertinib, or almonertinib). Chemotherapy was added in seven cases due to distant metastases or uncommon EGFR mutations (Table S3). Four patients required non-first-line therapy following progression or resistance, with most switching to alternative TKIs. Patient 1 received a combination of TKI, chemotherapy, and anti-VEGF therapy. Treatment-related adverse events were minimal: only one patient developed leukopenia, while all others completed TKI-based therapy without severe complications. Salvage surgery was considered when tumors became resectable or showed local progression during systemic therapy. Based on preoperative treatment duration, patients were classified into two groups: the long-term group (n=11), treated with TKIs for ≥6 months before surgery, and the short-term group (n=15), who underwent surgery within 6 months. Notably, Patient 9, initially suspected of distant metastases, had the longest preoperative TKI duration of 101 months before surgery. Comparative analyses between groups were conducted using nonparametric statistical tests.

All patients underwent anatomic lung resection (lobectomy or greater) with systematic mediastinal lymph node dissection, except one who received segmentectomy due to chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (Table 1). The mean operative time was 161.9±42.7 minutes, and the median estimated blood loss was 20 mL (range, 10–700 mL). When stratified by preoperative TKI duration, the long-term group had a median operative time of 180 minutes (range, 110–240 minutes), compared with 140 minutes (range, 100–220 minutes) in the short-term group (P=0.14). Median blood loss was comparable between groups: 20 mL (range, 10–700 mL) vs. 20 mL (range, 10–50 mL; P=0.66). One patient in each group required conversion to thoracotomy. The mean number of lymph nodes resected was 15.1±8.3, with no significant intergroup difference (P=0.24). Chest tubes were typically removed on postoperative day 3 (range, 2–21), and the average length of postoperative hospital stay was 5 days (range, 3–21 days). While chest tube duration was similar (P=0.16), patients in the long-term group tended to have longer length of postoperative hospital stay (median 6 days, range, 3–21 days) compared with the short-term group (median 4 days, range, 3–8 days; P=0.08). Four postoperative complications were observed, three in the long-term group. Patient 1 developed pneumothorax, and Patient 7 experienced a bronchopleural fistula requiring bronchoscopic stent placement (9). Especially, Patient 8 developed acute respiratory distress syndrome requiring non-invasive ventilation, fiberoptic bronchoscopy sputum suction and anti-infection therapy in intensive care unit. In the short-term group, Patient 19 developed pleural effusion necessitating additional chest tube drainage. Comprehensive comparative results are summarized in Table 2.

Major pathological regression was observed in nine patients, with one patient (Patient 2) achieving complete response. Patient 19 and Patient 26 demonstrated complete regression of the primary tumor but harbored residual disease in mediastinal lymph nodes. The R0 resection rate was 96.2% (25/26). The reverse Kaplan-Meier method yielded a median follow-up of 27.5 months; median RFS and OS were not reached. Recurrence was observed in seven of 22 patients with >6 months of follow-up, most commonly in the lung and pleura (Table S5). All patients received postoperative targeted therapy, except Patient 1, who resumed therapy only after confirmed recurrence 6 months postoperatively. Basically, the regimen of postoperative therapy was in accordance with preoperative treatment. When tumor recurrence, drug resistance, or histologic transformation were observed, the transformation would be decided after the discussion of multidisciplinary team.

Six retrospective studies met inclusion criteria (2,11-15) (Figure S2 and Tables S6,S7). While most focused on EGFR-mutant tumors, three also included cases with anaplastic lymphoma kinase alterations. Patients generally presented with stage III–IV disease prior to therapy, though the choice of TKIs and surgical extent varied. The mean preoperative treatment duration was 236 days. Minimally invasive radical salvage surgery was performed in 78% (range, 44–94%), with the remainder undergoing thoracotomy. Across studies, 91% (range, 79–97%) achieved R0 resection, the mean operative time was 164.5 minutes, and 20% of patients developed postoperative complications. No significant differences in perioperative outcomes were observed between short- and long-term TKI groups.

Discussion

This study provides preliminary evidence supporting the safety and feasibility of radical salvage surgery in patients with initially unresectable NSCLC following third-generation EGFR-TKI therapy. Complete resection was achieved in all cases, with an R0 resection rate of 96.2% (25/26) and a postoperative complication rate of 15.4% (4/26). Surgical outcomes were largely comparable between patients who underwent short- versus long-term preoperative TKI treatment, with the exception of prolonged hospitalization in the latter group. Perioperative results were consistent with those reported in previous studies, further supporting the role of salvage surgery as a potentially curative option for carefully selected patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC after targeted systemic therapy.

In clinical practice, however, the definition of “unresectable” NSCLC remains heterogeneous and often subjective. Criteria commonly cited include T4 invasion, multistation N2 lymph node involvement, and stage III disease (16,17). This lack of uniformity complicates both treatment decision-making and cross-study comparisons. In our cohort, we adopted a broad definition encompassing T4 tumors, bulky N2 disease, distant metastases, and major vascular invasion, reflecting the diverse and complex presentations frequently encountered in real-world clinical settings.

Advances in cancer biology have transformed NSCLC management, particularly in tumors with defined genetic drivers (18). For patients with unresectable EGFR-mutant NSCLC, TKIs remain the recommended first-line therapy (1,19). However, their efficacy is constrained by the near-universal development of intrinsic or acquired resistance (20). While third-generation TKIs prolong survival, resistance typically emerges within 2 years (4,10,21). The relatively young median age of our cohort (54.5 years) suggests a patient population more resilient to extended therapy and intensive interventions, yet also more vulnerable to resistance over time. This demographic may therefore derive particular benefit from salvage surgery, which offers definitive local control. As fourth-generation TKIs are still under investigation, strategies to address resistant or residual disease remain urgently needed.

Upon disease progression, therapeutic strategies encompass dose escalation of the current EGFR-TKI, transition to alternative targeted agents, incorporation of immunotherapy, or initiation of systemic second-line therapies. For patients exhibiting limited or oligoprogressive disease (PD), local ablative modalities such as surgical resection or radiotherapy represent viable treatment options (22,23). Emerging evidence from multiple studies has demonstrated superior survival when consolidative radiotherapy is combined with continued EGFR-TKI therapy compared with maintenance TKI monotherapy alone (24-26). Despite these therapeutic advances, a standardized, evidence-based protocol for the management of initially unresectable NSCLC following tumor regression or localized recurrence remains undefined in current clinical practice. Surgical resection offers distinct advantages in this context. Beyond providing the potential for complete eradication of resistant tumor clones, it enables comprehensive histopathological and molecular characterization of the residual disease, which may inform subsequent therapeutic decisions and identify mechanisms of resistance. Although randomized controlled trial data are limited, accumulating evidence supports the safety, feasibility, and potential survival benefit of conversion surgery following effective systemic therapy in carefully selected patients (14,27). In the present study, Patient 7 was categorized as having PD according to RECIST criteria preoperatively. However, detailed radiological reassessment revealed oligo PD, rather than systemic or diffuse progression. Based on this refined assessment, the multidisciplinary tumor board determined that local surgical intervention could achieve optimal local disease control and potentially prolong survival. Patient 7 achieved a RFS of 14 months during follow-up. This case underscores the critical importance of distinguishing between oligoprogression and systemic progression.

Salvage resection refers to surgery performed for local recurrence or residual disease following definitive therapies (28). Most recent studies, including those analyzed in this review, are retrospective and involve small cohorts. In practice, candidate selection and surgical standards remain debated. Indications may include early-stage NSCLC after failed ablative therapy, patients initially deemed unfit for surgery who underwent nonoperative treatment, or advanced disease with local progression (29,30). Surgical strategies range from pneumonectomy to lobectomy, segmentectomy, or wedge resection, performed via thoracotomy or minimally invasive approaches (31,32). Our cohort focused specifically on minimally invasive radical salvage resection in advanced NSCLC initially considered unresectable. Only anatomical resections were included to maintain curative intent, and perioperative outcomes were systematically evaluated. These results aligned closely with findings from prior studies, collectively supporting the feasibility of this approach.

Minimally invasive surgery is a standard of care for resectable NSCLC due to its association with improved recovery and outcomes (33). Nonetheless, salvage procedures can be technically demanding in patients with extensive tumor infiltration, disseminated lymph node metastases, or treatment-induced fibrotic adhesions, features frequently observed in this cohort. Chu et al. previously reported a 77% success rate for minimally invasive resection following neoadjuvant therapy (34), with higher pretreatment nodal burden predicting conversion to open surgery. Similarly, patients in our study presented with advanced disease and complex anatomy. Despite these challenges, 88.5% (23/26) successfully underwent radical minimally invasive salvage resection. 4 patients exhibited postoperative complications and all complications was treated successfully, and no short-term mortality was observed.

Comparisons between subgroups revealed no significant differences in operative time, intraoperative blood loss, or chest tube duration between short- and long-term TKI groups. However, the long-term group demonstrated a trend toward longer hospitalization. Among the four patients who experienced postoperative complications, two had severe events occurred in patients with extended preoperative therapy. Patients who received prolonged preoperative targeted therapy exhibited dense fibrotic adhesions. Such adhesions increase surgical difficulty, elevating the risk of conversion, surgical trauma, and potential for postoperative morbidity. These findings highlight that, while salvage surgery after EGFR-targeted therapy is feasible, optimal outcomes require careful patient selection and meticulous perioperative planning, particularly for those with prolonged TKI exposure.

Moreover, the optimal timing of salvage surgery following targeted therapy remains undefined. Potential intervention points include achieving sufficient tumor regression to render the lesion resectable, attaining maximal clinical response, or intervening upon the development of resistance to systemic therapy. In the present cohort, salvage surgery was performed at variable timepoints based on comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation, which integrated both tumor response dynamics and overall patient fitness. Previous studies have established predictive pathological models using radiomics features for NSCLC following neoadjuvant therapy (35,36). In this study, patients who achieved major pathological response (MPR) generally demonstrated corresponding clinical tumor shrinkage. However, discordance was observed. Patient 19, who achieved pathological complete response (pCR), exhibited PD on preoperative imaging, while several patients with PR demonstrated unsatisfactory pathological regression. This discrepancy likely reflects the complex and heterogeneous correlation between clinical and pathological regression in initially unresectable NSCLC. Such discordance undoubtedly complicates the selection of the optimal surgical time window. Due to the limited sample size, subgroup analysis stratified by timing of intervention could not be performed. To our knowledge, this represents the first study to specifically evaluate outcomes of radical salvage surgery following EGFR-targeted therapy in patients with initially unresectable NSCLC. While our findings suggest that the procedure is both safe and technically feasible, larger prospective studies are warranted to clarify the optimal surgical window and identify predictive biomarkers for patient selection.

Among patients with follow-up exceeding 6 months, recurrence occurred in seven cases. Several patients achieved favorable post-recurrence survival following aggressive multimodal adjuvant treatment strategies. At the initial postoperative visit, all patients except Patient 1 resumed TKI-based therapy irrespective of pathological findings. However, the relatively short median follow-up duration in this study precludes definitive conclusions regarding the long-term survival benefits of adjuvant TKI therapy following salvage resection. Larger multicenter cohorts with extended longitudinal observation are required to definitively establish the role and optimal duration of adjuvant targeted therapy in this specific clinical scenario.

This study has several limitations. First, the statistical power was compromised by the small sample size and retrospective nature of the investigation. Notably, several patients had limited follow-up duration, which may compromise the reliability of our conclusions. Furthermore, heterogeneity in treatment regimens and surgical indications, both within our cohort and across published studies, compromised the reliability of subgroup analyses. Especially, in pursuit of optimal therapeutic efficacy, preoperative treatment, salvage resection, and postoperative management were determined through multidisciplinary team consensus, which resulted in inconsistency in treatment scheduling. This may have contributed to inter-patient heterogeneity. Moreover, although we observed that extended preoperative TKI treatment was associated with prolonged hospitalization and increased risk of postoperative complications, these findings should be interpreted with caution. The arbitrary 6-month cutoff represented an exploratory approach with inherent selection bias. Prospective trials with extended follow-up are warranted to validate these preliminary observations. Lastly, the unavailability of individual-level data from prior studies precluded a more detailed comparative evaluation.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study provides preliminary evidence supporting radical salvage surgery as a potential local-control strategy for patients with initially unresectable EGFR-mutant NSCLC following TKI-based therapy. Minimally invasive resection was feasible in most cases and associated with favorable perioperative outcomes. However, the long-term survival benefit and optimal integration of salvage surgery into multimodal management remain uncertain. Larger prospective studies with longer follow-up are needed to validate these findings and define the clinical utility of this approach.
