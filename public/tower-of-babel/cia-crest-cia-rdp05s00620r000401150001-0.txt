25 October 1977

Dear Jim,

Thanks for your very nice note and compliments
on my remarks to the Union League.

Let me add that, hopefully, I was exaggerating
just a bit when I said that we must notify eight
committees of Congress for covert action. I sincerely
hope that the creation of select committees on
intelligence in each house of Congress will lead to
the atrophying of requests from a number of the other
committees to receive this information. This has
already happened on the Senate side to some extent.

In short, since foreign relations and appropriations
are both expressly represented on the select committees
we can hope that those committees will count on their
representatives to keep them posted if necessary. In
the long run it may well work out that we in effect
report only to two committees. Were it not for that
hope, I would share your concern very much.

I do look forxvard to seeing you down here when
your time permits. In the meantime, it was nice to
have a chance to say hello at the Union League.

All the best.

Dear Stan:

You spoke before an unusually demanding audience
which found your remarks and -yo u r answers to

responsibilities.

conclusion, after six years in the bull pen, that
any highly secret operation that requires notifying
the full membership of eight committees is destined m
never to take place.

calling you to see if it would be possible for me
to get together with you on some future visit to

Guest of Honor and Speaker

ADMIRAL STANSFIELD TURNER

Director of Central Intelligence
'‘Secrecy and Morality in Intelligence’'

One of President Carter’s Cabinet level appointments that has been
most generally approved by the country has been his naming Adm.
Stansfield Turner as Director of Central Intelligence. The CIA has been
the subject of much controversy in recent years, and Adm. Turner has
the difficult and extremely important job of restoring to the Central
Intelligence Agency the degree of public confidence it must have to
operate efficiently.

At a Public Affairs Luncheon on October 5, 1977 ULC members
and their guests will have an opportunity to meet Adm. Turner and to
hear directly from him an account of the present status of CIA and his
plans for its future. There will, as usual, be a question and answer
session after the talk.

Adm. Turner is native of Highland Park, 111. He was graduated
from the U. S. Naval Academy in 1946, then entered Oxford College as
a Rhodes Scholar for work on a master’s degree in philosophy, politics
and economics. He held a variety of sea assignments, was promoted to
Rear Admiral in 1970, and then assumed command of a Carrier Task
Group of the U. S. 6th Fleet. He served as president of the Naval
War College for two years, then in 1974 became Commander of the
U. S. Second Fleet and the NATO Striking Force Atlantic. In 1975 he
was made Commander in Chief, Allied Forces Southern Europe, at which
time he was promoted to the rank of Admiral.

The speaker will begin promply at 1:15 pm, following luncheon at 12:30.

names prior to October 3.
