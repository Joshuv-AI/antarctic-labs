ABSTRACT
This report provides a performance summary of the “Isotopics by Nuclides” tool in InterSpec.
The primary objective of the NA-241 FY25 project was the detection and characterization of
non-homogeneous uranium samples. However, the tool also demonstrates effectiveness in
determining the enrichment of homogeneous uranium or plutonium from single gamma
spectra. This paper presents a subset of evaluated data to avoid distribution limitations while
offering potential users’ insight into the tool's performance.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The “Isotopics by Nuclides” tool in InterSpec, developed primarily for NA-241 (Office of
International Nuclear Safeguards) in fiscal year 2025, demonstrates high sensitivity for
detecting non-homogeneous uranium samples. It also effectively determines uranium and
plutonium enrichments or burnup from HPGe or CZT detector spectra.

1. INTRODUCTION
This document evaluates the performance of the “Isotopics by Nuclides” tool using datasets
with known ground truths and no associated sensitivities. Uranium (U) and plutonium (Pu)
standards measurements from the NA-241 “Rodeo” project were analyzed using the H3D M400
CZT detector. Uranium standards were also evaluated using an ORTEC micro-Detective HPGe
detector. Additionally, a parameterized study of non-homogeneous uranium objects using
GADRAS-simulated data is presented.

For all datasets, ground-truth information was available to the author. To avoid bias, a single
spectrum was used to configure Regions of Interest (ROIs), peak skew, continuum types, and
other settings. This configuration was then applied uniformly across all spectra in the dataset,
regardless of enrichment, dwell time, or other factors. Any modifications necessary for
individual spectra are clearly noted.

Configurations were developed based on visual assessment to achieve good peak fits in regions
deemed useful. These configurations, or close variations, are now included as presets in the
tool. Minimal effort was invested in refining these configurations, leaving room for future
improvements such as dynamic adaptation of configurations.

1.1. Preparation of data
Experimental U and Pu standards measurements were provided as aggregated collections of
spectra, with truth-level enrichment information (mass percent U-235 or Pu-240) attached.
Documentation of measurement conditions was available but not consulted, as the tool aims to
operate independently of such information.
The measurements of U test objects provided Christopher Blessinger and Jozsef Zsigrai were
accompanied by coarse descriptions of the measurement configuration, but detector
characterizations, or in some cases representative backgrounds were not available.
The GADRAS simulated data analyzed was performed using GADRAS 19.5.2, and used the
standard ORTEC Detective-X detector response function, and included the standard NORM
background for Albuquerque NM.
The only preparation step performed for the data was to add non-linear deviation pairs to the
energy calibration. The same set of deviation pairs were used for all spectra collected by the
same detector. The careful addition of the deviation pairs was found to markedly improve
results; over the course of ~1 MeV, even HPGe detectors can commonly have non-linearities in
energy calibration that start to become comparable to the peak width, so can greatly impact the
solution fit (for example, the best Pu240 information sometimes come from the 160.3 keV line,
that overlaps with the 161.5 keV line from Pu239 – so small shifts in energy calibration can
greatly affect the ratio of Pu240 fit). The impact of non-linearities in energy calibration likely
affects this tool more than other peak-based tools because this tool does not directly fit the
peaks, but rather constructs peaks based off of the fit relative activities, fit FWHM, fit shielding,
etc., and then compares them to data during the optimization process, to extract maximum

 9

information from the spectrum; other peak-based tools generally first fit the peaks, then fit
these other quantities from them, making the other tools less-susceptible to variations in
energy calibration. A future version of the tool may automatically fit for the non-linearities as
part of computing the solution.

1.2. H3D M400 CZT Uranium Measurements
As part of the NA-241 “Rodeo” project, the Institut de radioprotection et de sûreté nucléaire
(IRSN) provided measurements of a set of uranium standards with the H3D M400 detector. The
measurements were made at a distance of 8 cm, with live-times between 10 minutes and 3.5
hours, and no collimation. A representative analysis of one spectrum, in the tool, can be seen
in figure 1. Results for all measurements can be seen in figure 2. A GADRAS characterization of
a different H3D M400 was provided to the author, so the full-energy efficiency function was
extracted from that and used for the detector’s intrinsic efficiency in the analysis; the modified
Hoerl correction function can compensate for differences between the used detector efficiency
and the actual detectors efficiency, so this aspect of the analysis was not critical.

1.4. HEU + LEU Measurement

 Figure 6. The relative efficiency curves fit (top) and spectrum (bottom) of the 200 g LEU and few grams
 HEU sample. The relative efficiency curves show good separation between enrichments, and attribute
nearly all U238 counts to the LEU curve (black); the HEU curve (green) attributes 34% of 185 keV counts
 to the HEU curve

 14

A 2-hour measurement of a 200 g LEU (2.95%) sample adjacent to a small HEU (90%) sample
was provided by József Zsigrai of the European Commission Joint Research Centre. The analysis
yielded fit enrichments of 3.29% ± 0.29% (one sigma uncertainty) for LEU and 100% for HEU.
The 185 keV peak from U-235 was attributed as 34% from HEU and 66% from LEU, consistent
with expectations. The detector efficiency was not known, so a generic Coaxial efficiency (26%
efficient as compared to a 3x3 NaI detector for Co60) was used. The LEU enrichment was fit 1.2
(statistical only) sigma from true value, while no U238 was attributed to the HEU, which is
approximately correct (less than ~0.3 grams of U238 would be in the HEU sample – not enough
to significantly contribute to the spectrum), giving re-assurance the method is compatible with
actual measurements that have good statistics.

Chris Blessinger of Oakridge National Laboratory also provided a number of mixed isotopic
measurements (both of a non-homogeneous uranium test object, and of mixed enrichment
uranium triangle tiles), but they were not of high-enough statistics (a combination of too short
of dwell times, and too large of distances) to draw conclusions.

1.5. DU shielded HEU (simulation)
To test expected performance of discriminating HEU shielded by DU, GADRAS was used to
create a “grid” of inject spectra that the developed tool was then used to analyze. Similar to the
other cases, a tool configuration was developed on one spectrum, and then used for all other
spectra; this configuration is included in the tool presets as “HPGe U inside U”. The inject
spectra in GADRAS were generated under the following conditions:
 • The 90% HEU is a solid sphere, with the 0.2% DU immediately surrounding the HEU –
 GADRAS default compositions of both materials
 • The default Detector Response Function (DRF) for the Ortec Detective-X detector, with
 default energy calibration
 • A 4-hour live time, with a distance of 15 cm from the center of the source
 • Terrestrial and cosmic background were included for Albuquerque NM

 • Poisson statics applied

The results of the fit for all inject spectra can be seen in Figure 6, with an example spectrum fit
and relative efficiency curves for one configuration in Figure 7. Multiple enrichments were
considered detected if enrichments of both curves were defined well (e.g., not near-zero counts
attributed to the curve), the curves were clearly separated for a majority of the energy range,
and the fit enrichments separated by more than 2 sigma.
An interesting trend that can be seen in the table of Figure 6 is that increasing amounts of DU,
up to 3 mm, generally yielded better enrichment values as well as discrimination between the
multiple enrichments. This is perhaps due to these samples providing more DU signal, allowing
the algorithm to have more “purchase” in separating the efficiency curves.
Another interesting take-away is that even through 5 mm of DU (generally considered “infinite
thickness” for U235, as only ~1E-6 gammas at 185 keV will make it through unaffected – the
reason this study only went to 5 mm), the presence of multiple enrichments was still clearly
detected.
One aspect that can use further study is the propensity of the HEU enrichment to go to 100%; a
likely explanation is so few U238 counts from the HEU, particularly the lower-energy U238
energies, making it through the DU, that there is just not sufficient data power to attribute
many of the U238 counts of the HEU, to the HEU.

2. SUMMARY AND FUTURE
The developed tool demonstrates promising performance in detecting and characterizing
uranium and plutonium enrichments across various measurement conditions, including non-
homogeneous conditions. A number of issues were identified (primarily needing to include
fitting for non-linear deviation pairs, some nuclear data inaccuracies, and some improvements
for finding globally best solution), but none appear to be significantly challenging to correct.
Work with Y-12 has been proposed for FY26 to take and analyze additional non-homogeneous
measurements to characterize the tools performance with real data; a couple weeks of
software development time has been included to correct known deficiencies, as well as any
others found.
