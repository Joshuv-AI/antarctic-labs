Asymptotic behavior of solutions to elliptic problems with Robin boundary conditions
Mengyao Chen, Massimo Grossi, Qi Li

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the asymptotic behavior, as β → 0 \beta\to 0 , of positive solutions to the semilinear elliptic Robin problem { − Δ ​ u = u p , in ​ Ω , u > 0 , in ​ Ω , ∂ u ∂ ν + β ​ u = 0 , on ​ ∂ Ω , \begin{cases}-\Delta u=u^{p},&\text{in }\Omega,\\ u>0,&\text{in }\Omega,\\ \frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases} where p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 , β > 0 \beta>0 , and Ω \Omega is a bounded smooth domain. We will prove that, for all p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 , the solution u β u_{\beta} behaves like a constant as β → 0 \beta\to 0 . However, the value of this constant is strongly influenced by the value of p p . Indeed, • if 0 ≤ p < 1 0\leq p<1 , u β u_{\beta} blows up uniformly in Ω \Omega as β → 0 \beta\to 0 . • if p = 1 p=1 (eigenvalue problem), u β u_{\beta} converge to a constant. • if p > 1 p>1 u β u_{\beta} converge uniformly to zero. In the critical and supercritical regime p ≥ N + 2 N − 2 p\geq\frac{N+2}{N-2} , the existence of solutions is no longer guaranteed a priori. In this case, when Ω \Omega is a ball and 0 < β < 2 p − 1 0<\beta<\frac{2}{p-1} we prove the existence of a radial positive solution. Keywords: elliptic equation; Robin boundary conditions; asymptotic behavior. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J20.



1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, with
N\geq 2
, and let
\nu
denote the unit outward normal to
\partial\Omega
. In this paper we study the semilinear elliptic problem with Robin boundary conditions

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=u^{p},\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u>0,\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0,\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
p\geq 0
and
\beta>0
.

Throughout the paper, we assume that when
p=1
the problem (
1.1
) is replaced by the eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda_{1,\beta}u,\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u>0,\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0,\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.2)

This allows us to state the results in a unified way for all
p\geq 0
.

We work in the Sobolev space

H^{1}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|=\left(\int_{\Omega}\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\bigr)\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

For each fixed
\beta>0
, we say that
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) if

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}uv\,dS=\int_{\Omega}u^{p}v\,dx,\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Let
u_{+}=\max\{u,0\}
. In order to find a weak solution to (
1.1
) for
0\leq p\leq\frac{N+2}{N-2}
, it is enough to look for nontrivial critical points of the functional

J_{\beta}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}\,dS-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}u_{+}^{p+1}\,dx.

The corresponding solutions
u_{\beta}
is a weak solution and, by elliptic regularity, a classical one.

1.1.
Background

Robin boundary conditions interpolate between the classical Dirichlet and Neumann cases, which formally correspond to the limiting regimes
\beta=\infty
and
\beta=0
. There is a vast literature on these two problems. The existence of solutions and their qualitative properties, such as uniqueness, nondegeneracy, and asymptotic behavior, have been widely investigated over the last few decades. Given the breadth of the subject, we do not attempt a complete survey and refer the reader, for instance, to
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
7
,
11
,
15
,
16
]
and the references therein.

Compared with the Dirichlet and Neumann problems, the Robin case has been less extensively studied, although several existence and qualitative results are available in the literature. We refer, among others, to
[
4
,
5
,
2
,
3
,
6
,
15
,
14
,
8
]
for results concerning eigenvalue estimates, existence, uniqueness, and asymptotic properties under Robin boundary conditions.

A first important distinction concerns the range of the exponent
p
. The behavior of the problem is substantially different in the sublinear, linear, subcritical, critical, and supercritical regimes, and it is useful to briefly review what is known in each case.

1.2.
Known results

We begin with the case
p=0
, for which (
1.1
) reduces to the torsion problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=1,\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0,\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.3)

By the maximum principle, the solution is unique. In
[
14
]
it was proved that
u_{\beta}
is bounded if and only if
\lambda_{1,\beta}>0
, where
\lambda_{1,\beta}
denotes the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
under Robin boundary conditions. In addition, the estimate

\lambda_{1,\beta}^{-1}\leq\|u_{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 6N\lambda_{1,\beta}^{-1}\log\bigl(2^{11}3\sqrt{3}N(1+\beta^{-1}\sqrt{\lambda_{1,\beta}})\bigr)

(1.4)

was obtained.

The range
0\leq p<1
corresponds to a sublinear nonlinearity and essentially exhibits the same phenomena as the torsion problem. In this regime the equation has a regularizing character, and one expects positive solutions to exist for every
\beta>0
. At the same time, as
\beta\to 0
, the Robin condition approaches the Neumann one, and this suggests the onset of a blow-up phenomenon. One of the main goals of this paper is precisely to describe this behavior and to establish the exact leading-order asymptotics of positive solutions in this range of exponents.

When
p=1
, problem (
1.1
) becomes closely related to the Robin eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda_{1,\beta}u,\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u>0,\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0,\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.5)

For each
\beta>0
, Daners
[
4
]
proved the existence of a positive lower bound for
\lambda_{1,\beta}
, depending on
N
,
\beta
, and
|\Omega|
. Indeed, by Lemma
2.2
we have

\lambda_{1,\beta}\leq\beta\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{|\Omega|},

which implies that
\lambda_{1,\beta}\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
.
Later, it was shown in
[
5
]
that

\lambda_{1,\beta}\geq\bar{\lambda}_{1},

where
\bar{\lambda}_{1}
is the first Robin eigenvalue of a ball having the same measure as
\Omega
. Bucur and Giacomini
[
2
]
obtained related results for sublinear problems, showing in particular how the spectral properties of the Robin Laplacian influence the qualitative behavior of solutions when
0\leq p<1
.

For subcritical exponents,

1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2},

one may consider the variational quantity

I_{\beta}=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}\,dS}{\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}}.

(1.6)

By arguments similar to those in
[
16
]
, one can verify that
I_{\beta}
is achieved by the compactness of the Sobolev embeddings
, and then a suitable scaling yields a positive solution of (
1.1
). On the other hand, uniqueness is more delicate and in general depending on the shape of the domain. In
[
3
]
, using a priori estimates together with a careful asymptotic analysis, Dai and Fu proved uniqueness of positive solutions when
\beta
is sufficiently small. In
[
6
]
, they studied the problem in annular domains and showed that the positive solution is unique for
\beta
small enough, while uniqueness fails for
\beta
sufficiently large. The question remains open for arbitrary
\beta>0
, even in the case where
\Omega
is a ball. We also note that when
\beta
is large, the Robin condition approaches the Dirichlet one; therefore, in domains where uniqueness is known for the corresponding Dirichlet problem, one may expect uniqueness for sufficiently large
\beta
as well.

For the critical exponent

p=2^{*}-1,\qquad 2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2},

the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)
is no longer compact, and therefore the functional
J_{\beta}
does not satisfy the Palais–Smale condition globally. In
[
15
]
, Wang proved that
J_{\beta}
satisfies the
(PS)_{c}
condition provided

0<c<\frac{1}{2N}\mathcal{S}^{N/2},

where
\mathcal{S}
denotes the best Sobolev constant for the embedding
D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. As a consequence, he showed that (
1.1
) admits a positive solution if
\beta
is sufficiently small. In addition, when
\Omega=B_{1}(0)
, by means of the Pohozaev identity he proved that (
1.1
) admits a radial solution if and only if
\beta\in(0,N-2)
.

We now turn to the supercritical regime

p>2^{*}-1.

To the best of our knowledge, there are no general existence results for the supercritical Robin problem (
1.1
). Motivated by the ideas in
[
15
]
, and also by related results of Serrin and Zou
[
13
]
, we prove an existence result in the radial setting when
\Omega
is a ball.

1.3.
Main results

The main purpose of this paper is to study the asymptotic behavior of positive solutions of (
1.1
) as
\beta\to 0
for all
p\geq 0
. According to the previous discussion, existence is known in the relevant ranges at least for
\beta>0
sufficiently small, and our analysis describes the precise asymptotic profile of such solutions.

Before presenting the asymptotic results, we first establish the uniqueness of positive solutions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
p\geq 0
. We have,

(1)

if
0\leq p\leq 2^{*}-1
and
p\neq 1
then the problem (
1.1
) has one positive solution for
\beta
small enough;

(2)

if
p=1
then the eigenvalue problem has one positive solution for any
\beta
and
||u_{\beta}||_{\infty}=1
;

(3)

if
p>2^{*}-1
, then equation (
1.1
) has at most one positive solution for
\beta
small enough.

We now begin to discuss the asymptotic behavior of the solution
u_{\beta}
for small
\beta
corresponding to the various values of
p
.

First let us consider
p\in[0,1]
. Then the unique solution
u_{\beta}
of (
1.1
) satisfies

u_{\beta}\sim C(\beta),

where
C(\beta)
is a constant that tends to
0
,
1
, or infinity depending on the value of
p
.
Below is the precise statement.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
p\in[0,1)
and
N\geq 2
. If
u_{\beta}
is a positive solution to equation (
1.1
), then

u_{\beta}=|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}|\partial\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}\beta^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}+o\bigl(\beta^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}\bigr),\qquad\text{as }\beta\to 0,

where
|\Omega|
and
|\partial\Omega|
denote the volume of
\Omega
and the surface measure of
\partial\Omega
, respectively.

Remark 1
.

For
p=0
, it was proved in
[
8
]
that

\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta}}{\beta}=\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{|\Omega|}.

(1.7)

Combining (
1.4
) and (
1.7
), one obtains

|\Omega|\,|\partial\Omega|^{-1}\beta^{-1}\leq\|u_{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C|\log\beta|}{\beta},\qquad\text{as }\beta\to 0.

Thus, Theorem
1.2
also provides an estimate of growth of
\|u_{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
as
\beta\to 0
.

The linear case requires a separate discussion where the associated first Robin eigenfunction is considered.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
u_{\beta}>0
is the first eigenfunction of

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda_{1,\beta}u,\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0,\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1.\end{cases}

Then

u_{\beta}\to 1,\qquad\text{as }\beta\to 0.

Next we consider the superlinear case.

Although our results will hold for all
p>1
, it is important to emphasize that there is a significant difference between the subcritical case
1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
and the critical and supercritical case
p\geq\frac{N+2}{N-2}
. Indeed, while in the subcritical case it is fairly standard to deduce the existence of a solution of (
1.1
), in the critical and supercritical case, the existence of solutions is a more delicate issue. Indeed, the lack of compactness (in the critical case), or even of the Sobolev embedding (in the supercritical case), prevents the use of standard variational methods. In this framework, our first result concerns the existence of a solution for
p>\frac{N+2}{N-2}
in the case of the ball.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that

p>\frac{N+2}{N-2},\qquad 0<\beta<\frac{2}{p-1},

and assume that
\Omega
is a ball. Then equation (
1.1
) admits a radial positive solution.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
will be given in Section
5
. The problem of the existence of solutions to (
1.1
) in general domains is far from being understood.

On the other hand, assuming the existence of a solution
u_{\beta}
in
\Omega
in the supercritical case, the asymptotic behavior turns out to be the same as in the subcritical case,

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
p>1
and
N\geq 2
. If
u_{\beta}
is a positive solution to equation (
1.1
), then

u_{\beta}=|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}|\partial\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\beta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+o\bigl(\beta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigr),\qquad\text{as }\beta\to 0.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we collect some preliminary results and prove Theorem
1.4
. In Section 3 we study the sublinear case
0\leq p<1
, in Section 4 we deal with the linear case, and in Section 5 we analyze the superlinear regime
p>1
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce an auxiliary function and provide some preliminary results.
For each
p\geq 0
, suppose that
u_{\beta}
is a positive solution to equation (
1.1
).
Define

c_{\beta}=\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{p}\,dx.

(2.1)

Obviously,
c_{\beta}>0
. Moreover, we introduce the auxiliary function

v_{\beta}=u_{\beta}-d_{\beta},

where
d_{\beta}
is given by

\boxed{d_{\beta}=\frac{c_{\beta}}{\beta|\partial\Omega|}.}

(2.2)

Thus,
v_{\beta}
solves the following equation:

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{\beta}=(v_{\beta}+d_{\beta})^{p},\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\dfrac{\partial v_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}+\beta v_{\beta}=-\beta d_{\beta},&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2.3)

Since

\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\beta}\,dS=-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}dS=\int_{\Omega}-\Delta u_{\beta}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{p}\,dx,

(2.4)

we have

\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{\beta}\,dS=0.

(2.5)

Then we have the following conclusion.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
p\geq 0
, we have that
v_{\beta}>0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

By the maximum principle we get

\min_{\bar{\Omega}}v_{\beta}(x)=\min_{\partial\Omega}v_{\beta}(x).

Let
y_{0}\in\partial\Omega
be such that

v_{\beta}(y_{0})=\min_{\bar{\Omega}}v_{\beta}(x).

In the following, we prove that
v_{\beta}>0
in
\Omega
.
If not, we may assume that
v_{\beta}\leq 0
in
\Omega
. Consequently, we get
v_{\beta}(y_{0})<0
.
It follows from (
2.5
) that there exists
y_{1}\in\partial\Omega
such that

v_{\beta}(y_{1})>0
. Thus
v_{\beta}
attains its maximum on
\partial\Omega
.
Let
y_{2}\in\partial\Omega
be such that

v_{\beta}(y_{2})=\max_{\bar{\Omega}}v_{\beta}>0.

This implies that

\frac{\partial v_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}(y_{2})\geq 0.

So we have

\frac{\partial v_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}(y_{2})+\beta v_{\beta}(y_{2})\geq\beta v_{\beta}(y_{2})>0,

which contradicts the boundary condition in (
2.3
).
Therefore
v_{\beta}>0
in
\Omega
.
∎

The constant
d_{\beta}
will play an important role in our proofs. In the following lemma, which is a direct application of Lemma
2.1
, we estimate it for the different values of
p
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
d_{\beta}
is defined in (
2.2
); then we have the following results.

(1) If
p\in[0,1)
, then

d_{\beta}\geq|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}|\partial\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}\beta^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}.

(2.6)

(2) If
p=1
, let
\lambda_{1,\beta}
denote the first eigenvalue of the Robin problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta\phi=\lambda_{1,\beta}\phi,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}+\beta\phi=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Then we have

\lambda_{1,\beta}\leq\beta\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{|\Omega|}\to 0,\quad\hbox{as }\beta\to 0.

(2.7)

(3) If
p>1
, then

d_{\beta}\leq|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}|\partial\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\beta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(2.8)

Proof.

By (
2.3
) and (
2.5
), we get

\beta d_{\beta}|\partial\Omega|=\int_{\partial\Omega}-\frac{\partial v_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}dS=\int_{\Omega}-\Delta v_{\beta}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(v_{\beta}+d_{\beta})^{p}dx.

Then, by Lemma
2.1
, we derive

\beta d_{\beta}|\partial\Omega|\geq\int_{\Omega}d_{\beta}^{p}dx=d_{\beta}^{p}|\Omega|.

(2.9)

Thus, if
p\geq 0
and
p\neq 1
, using that
d_{\beta}>0
and (
2.9
), we obtain the claims (1) and (3).

Finally, we prove (2). Let
u_{\beta}>0
be the first eigenfunction; then a direct computation yields

\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\beta}dS=-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}dS=\int_{\Omega}-\Delta u_{\beta}dx=\lambda_{1,\beta}\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}dx.

Define

v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}=u_{\beta}-\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta}c_{\beta}}{\beta|\partial\Omega|},\quad c_{\beta}=\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}dx.

Then we get

\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}dS=0,

(2.10)

and

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}=\lambda_{1,\beta}\Big(v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}+\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta}c_{\beta}}{\beta|\partial\Omega|}\Big),\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}}{\partial\nu}+\beta v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}=-\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta}c_{\beta}}{|\partial\Omega|},&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2.11)

Arguing as in Lemma
2.1
, we derive that
v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}>0
. Moreover, we obtain

\lambda_{1,\beta}\int_{\Omega}\Big(v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}+\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta}c_{\beta}}{\beta|\partial\Omega|}\Big)dx\geq\lambda_{1,\beta}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta}c_{\beta}}{\beta|\partial\Omega|}dx=\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta}^{2}c_{\beta}|\Omega|}{\beta|\partial\Omega|}.

On the other hand, by (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we have

\int_{\Omega}-\Delta v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}dx=-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial v_{\beta,\lambda_{1,\beta}}}{\partial\nu}dS=\lambda_{1,\beta}c_{\beta}.

Thus, we can get

\lambda_{1,\beta}c_{\beta}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1,\beta}^{2}c_{\beta}|\Omega|}{\beta|\partial\Omega|}.

Since
c_{\beta}>0
and
\lambda_{1,\beta}>0
, we have

\lambda_{1,\beta}\leq\frac{|\partial\Omega|\beta}{|\Omega|}.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

(Lemma 5.2,
[
15
]
) Suppose that
\partial\Omega
is sufficiently smooth,
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,

\varphi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
.
If
u
is a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f,\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\varphi,&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

then

\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\big).

Proof.

This is a classical result.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
p>1
. If
u_{\beta}
is a positive solution to equation (
1.1
), then
u_{\beta}\to 0
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
as
\beta\to 0
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. We may assume that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\|u_{\beta}\|_{\infty}\geq C>0
as
\beta\to 0
.
Let

\tilde{u}_{\beta}=\frac{u_{\beta}}{\|u_{\beta}\|_{\infty}};

then
\tilde{u}_{\beta}
solves the following equation:

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{u}_{\beta}=u_{\beta}^{p-1}\tilde{u}_{\beta},\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}+\beta\tilde{u}_{\beta}=0,&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2.12)

A direct computation yields

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\beta}|^{2}dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\tilde{u}_{\beta}^{2}dS=\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{p-1}\tilde{u}_{\beta}^{2}dx.

(2.13)

Since
0<\tilde{u}_{\beta}\leq 1
, by (
2.1
), (
2.2
), and (
2.8
), we can deduce that

\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{p-1}\tilde{u}_{\beta}^{2}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{p-1}dx\leq|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq|\partial\Omega|\beta.

(2.14)

Combining (
2.13
) with (
2.14
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\beta}|^{2}dx\to 0,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

(2.15)

On the other hand, we observe that

\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{p-1}\tilde{u}_{\beta}^{2}dx=\|u_{\beta}\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{u}_{\beta}^{p+1}dx\geq C^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{u}_{\beta}^{p+1}dx.

Combining this with (
2.14
), we obtain
\tilde{u}_{\beta}\to 0
in
L^{p+1}(\Omega)
as
\beta\to 0
. Since
p>1
, we derive that

\int_{\Omega}\tilde{u}_{\beta}^{2}dx\to 0,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

(2.16)

It follows from (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) that
\tilde{u}_{\beta}\to 0
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
as
\beta\to 0
. Moreover, we have
\tilde{u}_{\beta}\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
. Hence,
\tilde{u}_{\beta}=o(1)
as
\beta\to 0
, which contradicts
\|\tilde{u}_{\beta}\|_{\infty}=1
.
∎

Based on Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.4
, we can prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:
Suppose that
u
and
v
are two distinct positive solution. Let
w=u-v
, then
w
is a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=a(x)w,\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}+\beta w=0,&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
a(x)
is defined by

a(x)=p(\theta u+(1-\theta)v)^{p-1},\quad\theta\in(0,1).

We first claim that

a(x)\to 0,\quad\text{uniformly in}\ \Omega,

as
\beta\to 0
. Indeed, if
p<1
, then by Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
we get
that
u
and
v
blow up uniformly in
\Omega
. This implies that the claim is true. If
p>1
, then it follows from Lemma
2.4
that
u
and
v
converge to zero uniformly in
\Omega
. Moreover, we can also prove this claim.

Note that
w\neq 0
, then
\|w\|_{\infty}>0
. Define

\tilde{w}=\frac{w}{\|w\|_{\infty}},

then we get

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{w}=a(x)\tilde{w},\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\tilde{w}}{\partial\nu}+\beta\tilde{w}=0,&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

Since
|\tilde{w}|\leq 1
and
a(x)\to 0
uniformly in
\Omega
, by elliptic equation regularity, we can conclude that
\tilde{w}\to\tilde{w}_{0}
in
C_{loc}^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
and
\tilde{w}_{0}
is a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{w}_{0}=0,\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\tilde{w}_{0}}{\partial\nu}=0,&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

This implies that
\tilde{w}_{0}=C
for some constant
C
. We observe that
\|\tilde{w}\|_{\infty}=1
, so we get
C=1
or
C=-1
.
Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
C=1
, then for
\beta
small enough we get
w>0
.
Direct computation yields that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla vdx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}uvdS=\int_{\Omega}u^{p}vdx,

and

\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\nabla udx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}vudS=\int_{\Omega}v^{p}udx,

Thus, we have

\int_{\Omega}u^{p}v-v^{p}udx=0.

(2.17)

It follows from (
2.17
) that

0=\int_{\Omega}u^{p}v-v^{p}udx=\int_{\Omega}uv(u^{p-1}-v^{p-1})dx=\int_{\Omega}b(x)uvwdx,

where
b(x)
is defined by

b(x)=(p-1)(\vartheta u+(1-\vartheta)v)^{p-2},\quad\vartheta\in(0,1).

We observe that
b(x)<0
if
p<1
and
b(x)>0
if
p>1
,
Since
u
,
v
and
w
are positive, we have

\int_{\Omega}b(x)uvwdx=\begin{cases}<0,\quad&\text{if}\ p<1,\\
>0,\quad&\text{if}\ p>1.\end{cases}

This is a contradiction which ends the proof.
∎

3.
The case of
p\in[0,1)

In this section, we study the asymptotic behavior of positive solutions to equation (
1.1
).

In order to give an idea of the general phenomenon, let us briefly consider the case of the ball, i.e.
\Omega=B(0,1)
.
In this case, by Theorem
1.1
, positive solutions to (
1.1
) are radial.

Moreover, by means of maximum principle, we get

u(r)=u(|x|)\geq u(1),\quad\forall r<1.

It follows from (
2.4
) that

\beta u(1)=\int_{0}^{1}u^{p}r^{N-1}dr\geq\frac{[u(1)]^{p}}{N},

which implies that

u(1)\geq N^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}\beta^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}\to\infty,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

Thus, we observe that radial solutions to equation (
1.1
) blow up in the ball. Naturally, for a general bounded domain
\Omega
, one might ask:

do all positive solutions to equation (
1.1
) still blow up as
\beta\to 0
?

Moreover, what is their exact asymptotic behavior as
\beta\to 0
? In order to answer these questions, we use the auxiliary function
v_{\beta}
defined in Section 2.
We first prove an estimate for
v_{\beta}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
0\leq p<1
; then for
\beta
small enough, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

\frac{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}}{d_{\beta}}\leq C_{0},

where
d_{\beta}
is defined in (
2.2
).

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. If not, we may suppose that

\frac{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}}{d_{\beta}}\to+\infty,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

That is,

\frac{d_{\beta}}{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

It follows from (
2.6
) that

d_{\beta}\geq|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}|\partial\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}\beta^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}\to\infty,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

Then we must have

\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}\to\infty,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

Let

\tilde{v}_{\beta}=\frac{v_{\beta}}{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}}.

Then
\|\tilde{v}_{\beta}\|_{\infty}=1
and
\tilde{v}_{\beta}
solves

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{v}_{\beta}=\frac{1}{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}^{1-p}}\Big(\tilde{v}_{\beta}+\frac{d_{\beta}}{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}}\Big)^{p},\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\tilde{v}_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}+\beta\tilde{v}_{\beta}=\frac{-\beta d_{\beta}}{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}},&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Thus, by elliptic regularity, we deduce that there exists
\tilde{v}_{0}
such that
\tilde{v}_{\beta}\to\tilde{v}_{0}
in
C_{loc}^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
, where
\tilde{v}_{0}
is a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=0,\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0,&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.1)

This implies that
\tilde{v}_{0}=C
. By (
2.5
), we get

\int_{\partial\Omega}\tilde{v}_{\beta}dS=0,

and passing to the limit, we obtain
C=0
. We deduce that
\tilde{v}_{\beta}=o(1)
as
\beta\to 0
, which contradicts the fact that
\|\tilde{v}_{\beta}\|_{\infty}=1
.
∎

Define

\hat{v}_{\beta}=\frac{v_{\beta}}{d_{\beta}}.

(3.2)

Based on the previous lemma, we have
\hat{v}_{\beta}\leq C_{0}
. Thus, we obtain the following claim.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
0\leq p<1
; then it holds that
\hat{v}_{\beta}\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
.

Proof.

Note that
\hat{v}_{\beta}
is a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta\hat{v}_{\beta}=\frac{1}{d_{\beta}^{1-p}}\Big(\hat{v}_{\beta}+1\Big)^{p},\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}+\beta\hat{v}_{\beta}=-\beta,&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

By (
2.6
), we have
d_{\beta}\to\infty
. It follows from Lemma
3.1
that
0<\hat{v}_{\beta}\leq C_{0}
. Thus, by elliptic regularity, we can deduce that there exists
\hat{v}_{0}
such that
\hat{v}_{\beta}\to\hat{v}_{0}
in
C_{loc}^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
, where
\hat{v}_{0}
is a solution of equation (
3.1
). Therefore, we can derive that
\hat{v}_{0}=C
for some constant
C
.
By using (
2.5
), we have

\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{v}_{\beta}dS=0.

Next, passing to the limit, we obtain that
C=0
. So we prove that
\hat{v}_{\beta}\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

Note that

u_{\beta}=v_{\beta}+d_{\beta}=(\hat{v}_{\beta}+1)d_{\beta}.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
that

u_{\beta}=(1+o(1))d_{\beta},

as
\beta\to 0
.
Thus, by the definition of
c_{\beta}
, we get

d_{\beta}\beta|\partial\Omega|=c_{\beta}=\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{p}dx=(1+o(1))^{p}|\Omega|d_{\beta}^{p},

which implies that

d_{\beta}=(1+o(1))^{\frac{1}{1-p}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}|\partial\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}\beta^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}.

So we deduce that

u_{\beta}=(1+o(1))d_{\beta}=(1+o(1))^{\frac{1}{1-p}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}|\partial\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}\beta^{-\frac{1}{1-p}}.

∎

4.
The case of
p=1

In this section, based on
(2)
of Lemma
2.2
, we will prove the asymptotic behavior of the first eigenfunction.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
:

Multiplying equation (
1.5
) by
u_{\beta}
, we get

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\beta}|^{2}dx+\beta\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{2}dS=\lambda_{1,\beta}\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{2}dx,

which implies that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\beta}|^{2}dx\leq\lambda_{1,\beta}\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{2}dx\leq\lambda_{1,\beta}|\Omega|.

By using Lemma
2.2
, we get

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{\beta}|^{2}dx\to 0,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

Thus, there exists some constant
C
such that
u_{\beta}\to C
as
\beta\to 0
. Since
0<u_{\beta}\leq 1
, we must have
0\leq C\leq 1
. If
C<1
, then
\sup u_{\beta}<1
for
\beta>0
small enough. This contradicts the fact that
\|u_{\beta}\|_{\infty}=1
. Hence, we prove that
u_{\beta}\to 1
as
\beta\to 0
.
∎

5.
The case of
p>1

We first observe that for
p>\frac{N+2}{N-2}
there are no known existence results in the literature. For this reason, we begin by providing an existence result in the case of the ball. The existence of solutions in the supercritical case for small
\beta
in general domains remains an interesting open problem.

5.1.
The case
p>\frac{N+2}{N-2}
and
\Omega=B_{1}

As recalled earlier, in the supercritical case it is more difficult to obtain existence results for solutions. However, in the case of the ball, the existence of
slow decay solutions
for the problem in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
allows one to deduce the existence of a solution in the case of the ball.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
p>\frac{N+2}{N-2}
,
0<\beta<\frac{2}{p-1}
and
\Omega
is a ball. Then there exists a radial solution of equation (
1.1
).

Proof.

We recall a key result obtained in
[
13
]
(see also
[
12
]
) where it was proved that the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=u^{p},\quad&\text{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\
u>0,&\text{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},\end{cases}

(5.1)

has the unique radial solution
U
and

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{a}U(|x|)=b^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

(5.2)

where
a=\frac{2}{p-1}
and
b=a(N-2-a)
. The similar result can be also founded in
[
13
]
.

From
[
12
]
, one can prove that

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{a+1}U^{\prime}(|x|)=-ab^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(5.3)

Indeed, let
s=\log r
and
v(s)=r^{a}U(r)
, then we have
U(r)=e^{-s}v(s)
. Compute

r^{a+1}U^{\prime}(r)=v^{\prime}(s)-av(s).

(5.4)

Rupflin and Struwe
[
12
]
showed that

\lim_{s\to\infty}v^{\prime}(s)=0,\quad\lim_{s\to\infty}v(s)=b^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Thus, we can get

\lim_{s\to\infty}\Big(v^{\prime}(s)-av(s)\Big)=-ab^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

So by (
5.4
), we can get
r^{a+1}U^{\prime}(r)\to-ab^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
as
r\to\infty
.

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
\Omega=B_{1}(0)
. Define

U_{\delta}(r)=\delta^{\frac{2}{p-1}}U(\delta r),

then it is immediate that
U_{\delta}
verifies equation in (
1.1
) for all
\delta>0
. Now, we need to find a suitable
\delta
such that the boundary condition in (
1.1
) holds. Since
U^{\prime}(0)=0
, we have

U_{\delta}^{\prime}(1)+\beta U_{\delta}(1)=\delta^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(\delta U^{\prime}(\delta)+\beta U(\delta))=\delta^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(\beta U(0)+o(1)),\quad\text{as}\ \delta\to 0.

By (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we have

U_{\delta}^{\prime}(1)+\beta U_{\delta}(1)=\delta^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(\delta U^{\prime}(\delta)+\beta U(\delta))=b\Big(\beta-\frac{2}{p-1}+o(1)\Big),\quad\text{as}\ \delta\to\infty.

Since
0<\beta<\frac{2}{p-1}
, then we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Big(U_{\delta}^{\prime}(1)+\beta U_{\delta}(1)\Big)>0,\quad\lim_{\delta\to\infty}\Big(U_{\delta}^{\prime}(1)+\beta U_{\delta}(1)\Big)<0.

Thus, there exists
\hat{\delta}=\hat{\delta}(\beta)>0
such that

U_{\hat{\delta}}^{\prime}(1)+\beta U_{\hat{\delta}}(1)=0.

So
U_{\hat{\delta}}
solves equation (
1.1
).
∎

5.2.
General bounded domain

Based on Lemma
2.4
, we have that any positive solutions to equation (
1.1
) converge to 0 as
\beta\to 0
. It is natural to ask: what is the exact asymptotic behavior of these positive solutions?
In order to find the asymptotic behavior of positive solutions to equation (
1.1
), we use the auxiliary function
v_{\beta}
which is defined in Section 2.

Firstly, we establish some estimates for the function
\hat{v}_{\beta}
, where
\hat{v}_{\beta}
is defined in (
3.2
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
p>1
; then for
\beta
small enough, there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that
\|\hat{v}_{\beta}\|\leq C_{1}
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. If not, we may assume that

\|\hat{v}_{\beta}\|_{\infty}\to\infty,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

That is,

\frac{d_{\beta}}{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}}=\frac{1}{\|\hat{v}_{\beta}\|_{\infty}}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

Note that
\|\tilde{v}_{\beta}\|_{\infty}=1
, and
\tilde{v}_{\beta}
solves

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{v}_{\beta}=\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\Big(\tilde{v}_{\beta}+\frac{d_{\beta}}{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}}\Big)^{p},\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\tilde{v}_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}+\beta\tilde{v}_{\beta}=\frac{-\beta d_{\beta}}{\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}},&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

By (
2.8
) and Lemma
2.4
, we have
\|v_{\beta}\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
. Thus, by elliptic regularity, there exists
\tilde{v}_{0}
such that
\tilde{v}_{\beta}\to\tilde{v}_{0}
in
C_{loc}^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
, and
\tilde{v}_{0}
is a solution to equation (
3.1
). This implies that
\tilde{v}_{0}=C
.
Finally, arguing as in Lemma
3.1
, we can prove that
C=0
. This is a contradiction, which completes the proof.
∎

Based on Lemma
5.2
, we establish the following result.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
p>1
; then we have
\hat{v}_{\beta}\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
.

Proof.

Note that
\hat{v}_{\beta}
is a solution to

\begin{cases}-\Delta\hat{v}_{\beta}=d_{\beta}^{p-1}\Big(\hat{v}_{\beta}+1\Big)^{p},\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{\beta}}{\partial\nu}+\beta\hat{v}_{\beta}=-\beta,&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

By (
2.8
), we get

d_{\beta}\leq|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}|\partial\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\beta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\ \beta\to 0.

By virtue of Lemma
5.2
, we have
0<\hat{v}_{\beta}\leq C_{1}
. Thus, by elliptic regularity, we can deduce that there exists
\hat{v}_{0}
such that
\hat{v}_{\beta}\to\hat{v}_{0}
in
C_{loc}^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
, and
\hat{v}_{0}
is a solution to equation (
3.1
). Therefore, we deduce that
\hat{v}_{0}=C
for some constant
C
.
Finally, arguing as in Lemma
3.2
, we can prove that
C=0
. Consequently, we obtain that
\hat{v}_{\beta}\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Using the fact that
u_{\beta}=(\hat{v}_{\beta}+1)d_{\beta}
and Lemma
5.3
, we obtain

u_{\beta}=(1+o(1))d_{\beta},

as
\beta\to 0
. Thus, by the definition of
c_{\beta}
, we get

d_{\beta}\beta|\partial\Omega|=c_{\beta}=\int_{\Omega}u_{\beta}^{p}dx=(1+o(1))^{p}d_{\beta}^{p}|\Omega|,

which implies that

d_{\beta}=(1+o(1))^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}|\partial\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\beta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Therefore, we deduce that

u_{\beta}=(1+o(1))d_{\beta}=(1+o(1))^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}|\partial\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\beta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

∎
