Asymptotically Optimal Learning for Parametric Prophet Inequalities
Jung-hun Kim, Anna Grebennikova, Vianney Perchet

Abstract
We study learning in prophet inequalities with i.i.d. rewards drawn from an exponential-type parametric family with an unknown parameter θ \theta , a class that includes exponential, Pareto, and bounded-support power-family distributions. We first characterize the optimal full-information asymptotic competitive ratio for this family. In the unbounded-support case, the limit is ( θ / ( θ − c + ) ) c + / θ / Γ ⁡ ( 1 − c + / θ ) , {\left({\theta}/({\theta-c_{+}})\right)^{c_{+}/\theta}}/{\Gamma(1-c_{+}/\theta)}, while in the bounded-support case, the limit is 1 1 . We then propose a confidence-based dynamic-programming policy for online learning. By exploiting the explicit parametric structure, the policy achieves the same optimal asymptotic competitive ratio using only online observations, without external offline samples. We further derive distribution-specific convergence rates for canonical examples. Finally, numerical experiments on synthetic instances illustrate the performance of our algorithm.



1
Introduction

Prophet inequalities are a fundamental model of online decision-making and
optimal stopping
(
10
)
. A decision-maker sequentially observes
rewards and must irrevocably decide when to stop and accept the current reward.
The benchmark is the prophet, who observes all realizations in advance and
obtains the maximum reward. The goal is to design a stopping rule whose expected
reward is competitive with this prophet benchmark. This framework has attracted
significant attention due to its rich mathematical structure and its broad range
of applications, including posted-price mechanisms
(
15
)
,
online ad allocation
(
1
)
, and hiring processes in labor
markets
(
2
)
.

Classical prophet inequalities assume that the reward distributions are known.
Under this assumption, for i.i.d. reward distributions,

9
established the classical
1-1/e
guarantee, and
subsequent work characterized the optimal full-information i.i.d. worst-case
constant
\kappa\approx 0.745

(
4
;
5
)
.

However, full distributional knowledge is rarely available in applications.
This has motivated a growing literature on prophet inequalities with unknown
distributions. A central message of this literature is that, for arbitrary
unknown distributions, learning is severely limited without sufficient offline
samples: in the i.i.d. setting, one cannot improve over the competitive ratio

1/e
with
o(n)
offline samples
(
3
)
. Achieving
near-optimal worst-case guarantees for arbitrary distributions requires
substantial distributional information, such as
\Theta(n)
or more offline
samples
(
17
)
. Thus, in the absence of structure, learning
prophet inequalities is constrained by strong sample-complexity barriers.

A complementary perspective is to seek distribution-specific asymptotic
performance beyond distribution-free worst-case guarantees.

7
pursue this direction using nonparametric
relative-rank-based rules, establishing first-order asymptotic optimality in the
Gumbel and reverse-Weibull domains. However, because their rules use only
relative ranks, they cannot exploit parametric information in the observed
reward values. This restriction is especially pronounced in the Fréchet
heavy-tailed regime: no relative rank-based rule can attain the
full-information Fréchet
heavy-tailed limit, as discussed later. Even where the relative rank-based framework
applies, its nonparametric rates can be slower than those achievable under
stronger parametric structure.

Another closely related direction is online learning in structured prophet
inequality models.
12
study prophet inequalities under a linear
reward structure with an unknown parameter and a known feature distribution.
Their objective, however, is to recover the classical distribution-agnostic
guarantee of
1-1/e
. This guarantee is robust but not distribution-specific:
it can be strictly below the optimal ratio achievable within a given
distribution class, and is also below the optimal full-information i.i.d. worst-case constant
\kappa\approx 0.745
.

These works motivate the following question:

Can one exploit parametric distributional structure to learn the unknown parameter online and match the full-information distribution-specific
benchmark?
We answer this question
affirmatively for an exponential-type parametric family, which includes
light-tailed, heavy-tailed, and bounded-support models such as exponential,
Pareto, and bounded-support power-family distributions. Our goal is not to recover a distribution-agnostic worst-case constant, but to
learn the parameter from online observations alone and match the full-information
distribution-specific asymptotic competitive ratio.
Our main contributions
are summarized as follows.

•

Optimal full-information asymptotics.

We characterize the asymptotically optimal competitive ratio for the proposed
exponential-type family when the distribution parameter
\theta
is known.
In the unbounded-support case, the limiting optimal ratio is

\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-c_{+}/\theta)}\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-c_{+}}\right)^{c_{+}/\theta},

which is governed by the endpoint-growth parameter
c_{+}
. In the
bounded-support case, the optimal competitive ratio converges to
1
.

•

Online learning with asymptotically optimal guarantees.

We propose a confidence-based dynamic-programming policy for online
learning. The policy first estimates the unknown distribution parameter
from an initial exploration phase, constructs an upper confidence bound,
and then applies the corresponding plug-in DP thresholds. By exploiting the
explicit parametric structure, the proposed policy achieves the same
asymptotically optimal competitive ratio as the optimal known-parameter
policy, using only online observations and no external offline samples.

•

Distribution-specific convergence guarantees.

We derive refined convergence guarantees for representative distributions,
including exponential, Pareto, and bounded-support power-family rewards.
For exponential and bounded-support examples, our policy matches the
convergence rates of the corresponding full-information optimal policies.
For Pareto heavy-tailed rewards, we obtain a Pareto-specific convergence
guarantee showing convergence to the full-information limiting ratio. These results show how tail or endpoint structure
governs the speed of convergence, and are supported by numerical
experiments.

Related Work

Prophet inequalities with known distributions.

The classical prophet inequality was initiated by

13
. Building on this line of work,
18
showed that a single
threshold achieves the optimal
1/2
competitive ratio for independent non-identical rewards.
For i.i.d. rewards,
9
established stronger guarantees of
1-1/e
,
and later work characterized the optimal full-information i.i.d. competitive ratio as constant

\kappa\approx 0.745

(
4
)
.
These works assume that the reward distribution is known, whereas our focus is on learning the
unknown parameter of a structured distribution online.

Unknown distributions and sample-based prophet inequalities.

3
studied i.i.d. prophet inequalities with an
unknown distribution and showed that, with no samples or even
o(n)
samples, the best possible
ratio is
1/e
. They also showed that
n-1
samples yield a
1-1/e
guarantee and that

O(n^{2})
samples suffice to approach the optimal full-information constant
\kappa
.
17
improved the i.i.d. sample complexity to

O(n/\varepsilon^{6})
for an
\kappa-O(\varepsilon)
guarantee.
Our work is
complementary: instead of using external offline samples from an arbitrary distribution, we exploit a
parametric form and learn the unknown parameter from the online observations.

Rank-based stopping and extreme-value theory.

7
studied optimal stopping for i.i.d. observations
from an unknown continuous distribution using relative-rank-based rules. Their
analysis is closely connected to extreme-value theory: they establish first-order
asymptotic optimality in the Gumbel and reverse-Weibull domains, whereas in the
Fréchet domain vanishing relative regret is impossible. Our endpoint regularity
conditions admit a similar extreme-value interpretation. In contrast to their nonparametric, rank-based approach, we
exploit parametric rewards to attain optimal distribution-specific
asymptotic ratios across light-tailed, heavy-tailed, and bounded-support models
within our family, with sharper convergence rates in canonical examples.

Structured and parametric learning in prophet inequalities.

12
study prophet inequalities
under a linear reward structure with an unknown parameter and a known feature
distribution, and recover
the classical
1-1/e
guarantee. Their setting is structurally different
from ours, and the resulting guarantee is distribution-agnostic rather than
distribution-specific. In particular, it can be below the optimal ratio for a
given distribution class and the optimal worst-case full-information i.i.d.
constant
\kappa\approx 0.745
. Parametric learning in optimal
stopping was also studied by
16
for
exponential rewards with an unknown mean. In contrast, we consider
finite-horizon prophet inequalities for a parametric exponential-type family
with an unknown distribution parameter, and learn this parameter online to
achieve the optimal distribution-specific competitive ratio.

3
Problem Statement

We consider the classical prophet-inequality
framework over a finite horizon
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where an i.i.d. nonnegative random
reward
X_{i}\geq 0
is revealed sequentially to the decision maker (or
gambler) at each stage
i\in[n]
. For the reward distribution, we consider a
exponential-type
parametric family
, defined as follows.

Definition 1

(Exponential-Type Parametric Family)
.

We consider a parameter family with latent
\theta>0
and an upper support

x_{F}\in(x_{0},\infty]
, whose CDF is

F_{\theta}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x<x_{0},\\[2.84526pt]
1-\exp(-\theta\phi(x)),&x_{0}\leq x<x_{F},\\[2.84526pt]
1,&x\geq x_{F},\end{cases}

where
\phi:[x_{0},x_{F})\to[0,\infty)
is a known strictly increasing function with

\phi(x_{0})=0
and

\phi(x)\to\infty\text{ as }x\uparrow x_{F}.

Online stopping objective.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. rewards drawn from
F_{\theta}
under an
unknown
parameter
\theta
.
A policy is a stopping time
\tau\in\{1,\dots,n\}\cup\{n+1\}
adapted to the sequential observations

(X_{t})_{t\geq 1}
: after observing
X_{t}
, the decision-maker either stops and receives reward
X_{t}
,
or continues to
t+1
(and can never return to
t
). We define
X_{n+1}:=0
to be the reward for the case when the stopping rule continues over all periods in
[n]
. The benchmark is the prophet value

\mathrm{OPT}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\max_{t\in[n]}X_{t}]
, and the goal is to design a policy (not knowing
\theta
)
with a large competitive ratio

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau;\theta)=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[X_{\tau}]}{\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)},

where
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
denotes expectation under

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}F_{\theta}
; we write

\mathbb{E}
when
\theta
is clear.

4
Preliminary Results

In this section, we derive an estimation guarantee for the
exponential-type parametric family and introduce benchmark policies for
comparison.

4.1
Estimator and Confidence Bound

Let
Y_{i}:=\phi(X_{i})
for
i\in[n]
. Then
Y_{i}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)
, where

\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)
denotes the exponential distribution with rate
\theta
. Since an exponential random variable with rate
\theta
has mean
1/\theta
, we have

\theta=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}]}.

This motivates the plug-in estimator

\widehat{\theta}_{m}=\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}\right)^{-1},

and the corresponding estimated cumulative distribution function

F_{\widehat{\theta}_{m}}(x)=1-\exp\bigl(-\widehat{\theta}_{m}\phi(x)\bigr).

We next provide a confidence bound for
\widehat{\theta}_{m}
; see Appendix
A.1
for the proof.

Lemma 2
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, when
m\geq\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log(2/\delta)
for
\delta\in(0,1]
, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\left|\frac{\theta}{\widehat{\theta}_{m}}-1\right|\leq\varepsilon.

4.2
Baseline Policies

We next discuss two natural baseline policies. The first is a
distribution-specific nonparametric baseline based on relative ranks. The
second is a distribution-agnostic sample-based baseline using the confidence
bound from the previous section.

Rank-based distribution-specific baseline.

A natural nonparametric baseline is the relative-rank-based rule of

7
, which uses only the relative ranks of the
observations. It achieves first-order asymptotic optimality, in the sense of
vanishing relative regret, in the Gumbel and reverse-Weibull domains.
Specifically, for the exponential distribution, their guarantees imply

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau;\theta)\geq 1-O\!\left({\log\log\log n}/{\log n}\right),

whereas for the uniform distribution on a bounded interval, they imply

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau;\theta)\geq 1-O\!\left({\log n}/{n}\right),

as
n\to\infty
. These convergence rates towards the optimal limit of
1
are useful nonparametric baselines, but they are not tailored to
parametric subfamilies where sharper rates are possible. Moreover, as
discussed later, the relative rank-based framework does not recover the full-information
distribution-specific limit in the Fréchet heavy-tailed domain.

Distribution-agnostic sample-based baseline.

Another natural reference is the
Samples-CFHOV
rule of

17
. We adapt it to our online setting by estimating
the unknown parameter from the initial observations and then applying
sample-based thresholds under the estimated model. With a suitable exploration
length, this baseline achieves a competitive ratio arbitrarily close to the
optimal i.i.d. worst-case constant
\kappa\approx 0.745
; see
Appendix
A.5
. However, it remains distribution-agnostic and does
not target the sharper full-information distribution-specific baseline.

Our main policy instead uses confidence-based DP thresholds for online learning, tailored to the
exponential-type family. This allows us to exploit the parametric structure and
achieve the optimal distribution-specific asymptotic competitive ratio,
including for heavy-tailed Pareto-type rewards.

5
Optimal Competitive Ratio

To characterize the full-information asymptotic benchmark, we impose the
following endpoint regularity condition.

Assumption 3

(Endpoint regularity)
.

Let
\psi:=\phi^{-1}
, so that
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[x_{0},x_{F})
. Assume that
\psi
is eventually twice differentiable and that one of the
following two support regimes holds.

(i) Unbounded-support case:
x_{F}=\infty
.
There exist upper-tail growth constants
c_{+},c_{+}^{\prime}\in[0,\theta)
s.t.

\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{\psi(y)}=c_{+},\qquad\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}=c_{+}^{\prime}.

(ii) Bounded-support case:
x_{F}<\infty
. Let
\bar{\psi}(y):=x_{F}-\psi(y)
.
There exist endpoint-gap decay constants
c_{-},c_{-}^{\prime}\in[0,\infty)
such that

\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\bar{\psi}^{\prime}(y)}{\bar{\psi}(y)}=-c_{-},\qquad\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\bar{\psi}^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\bar{\psi}^{\prime}(y)}=-c^{\prime}_{-}.

Remark 4

(The two endpoint constants coincide)
.

Assumption
3
is written with two limits in each endpoint
regime in order to separate the endpoint-growth condition from the
corresponding smoothness condition. However, the two constants are not
independent. Whenever both limits exist, they necessarily coincide:

c_{+}=c_{+}^{\prime}\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{-}=c_{-}^{\prime}.

The proof is given in Appendix
A.2
. Thus, throughout the
paper, we write simply
c_{+}
in the unbounded-endpoint case and
c_{-}

in the bounded-endpoint case.

Endpoint indices and finite prophet benchmarks.

Since
\phi
is strictly increasing, its inverse
\psi
is also
strictly increasing. Hence
\psi^{\prime}(y)\geq 0
wherever the derivative
exists, and the endpoint indices
c_{+}
and
c_{-}
are necessarily
nonnegative. The restriction
c_{+}<\theta
is imposed only in the unbounded-endpoint
case. It is an integrability condition: if
c_{+}>\theta
, then
1
1

1

In such cases, the prophet benchmark is infinite, so the standard
competitive-ratio formulation is not well defined.

\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=\infty
, and in the boundary case
c_{+}=\theta
, the
mean may still be infinite in general; see Appendix
A.3
.
In contrast, when
x_{F}<\infty
, the support is bounded above, so

\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]\leq x_{F}<\infty
. Therefore no analogous restriction on
c_{-}
is needed.

Canonical examples.

This family includes several common distributions as special cases.
In the unbounded-endpoint case, the exponential distribution corresponds to

\phi(x)=x
on
[0,\infty)
, for which
\psi(y)=y
and hence
c_{+}=0
. Another example is the Pareto family, obtained from

\phi(x)=\log(x/x_{0})
on
[x_{0},\infty)
. In this case

\psi(y)=x_{0}e^{y}
, so
c_{+}=1
. In the bounded-support case, a natural example is bounded-support power-family distributions with

\phi(x)=\log\!\left(\frac{x_{F}-x_{0}}{x_{F}-x}\right)
for
x\in[x_{0},x_{F}),

for which
\bar{\psi}(y)=(x_{F}-x_{0})e^{-y}
and hence
c_{-}=1
.
This includes the uniform distribution on
[x_{0},x_{F}]
as the special case
\theta=1
.

Remark 5

(Extreme-value interpretation)
.

The endpoint conditions above have a natural interpretation in extreme-value
theory. Let

U(t)=F_{\theta}^{-1}(1-1/t)=\psi\!\left({\log t}/{\theta}\right).

If
x_{F}=\infty
and
\psi^{\prime}(y)/\psi(y)\to c_{+}
, then
U
is regularly
varying with index
c_{+}/\theta
. Hence, when
c_{+}>0
,
F_{\theta}
belongs
to the Fréchet domain of attraction with extreme-value index

\gamma=c_{+}/\theta
; the case
c_{+}=0
is consistent with Gumbel-type
behavior. If
x_{F}<\infty
and

\bar{\psi}^{\prime}(y)/\bar{\psi}(y)\to-c_{-}
, then
x_{F}-U(t)
is regularly varying
with index
-c_{-}/\theta
, corresponding to the reverse-Weibull domain with
extreme-value index
\gamma=-c_{-}/\theta
. The derivation is given in
Appendix
A.4
.

Under Assumption
3
, we now analyze the asymptotically optimal
competitive ratio for the exponential-type family. The characterization depends
on the tail parameter
c_{+}
in the unbounded-support case and on the endpoint
parameter
c_{-}
in the bounded-support case. We first provide the optimal ratio for the unbounded support case, and the proof is provided in
Appendix
A.6
.

Theorem 6

(Optimal ratio for the unbounded-support case)
.

Suppose Assumption
3
(i) holds. Let
\tau^{*}
be the optimal full-information stopping rule. Then, for
c_{+}\in[0,\theta)
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[X_{\tau^{*}}]=\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-c_{+}}\right)^{c_{+}/\theta}\psi\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)(1+o(1)),

\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)=\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{c_{+}}{\theta}\right)\psi\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)(1+o(1)),

and therefore

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau^{*};\theta)=\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-c_{+}}\right)^{c_{+}/\theta}}{\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{c_{+}}{\theta}\right)}:=\rho(c_{+},\theta).

We next provide the optimal ratio for the bounded-support case; the proof is in
Appendix
A.7
.

Theorem 7

(Optimal ratio for the bounded-support case)
.

Suppose Assumption
3
(ii) holds. Let
\tau^{*}
be the optimal full-information stopping rule. Then, for
c_{-}\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[X_{\tau^{*}}]=x_{F}-\left(\frac{\theta+c_{-}}{\theta}\right)^{c_{-}/\theta}\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)(1+o(1)),

\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)=x_{F}-\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{c_{-}}{\theta}\right)\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)(1+o(1)),

and, since
\bar{\psi}(\log n/\theta)\to 0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau^{*};\theta)=1.

Remark 8

(Connection to extreme-value asymptotics)
.

The full-information limits above are consistent with EVT-based
characterizations of i.i.d. prophet inequalities

(
11
;
14
)
. In the
unbounded-support case, the extreme-value index is

\gamma=c_{+}/\theta\in[0,1)
, so

\rho(c_{+},\theta)={(1-\gamma)^{-\gamma}}/{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}.

In the bounded-support case,
\gamma=-c_{-}/\theta\leq 0
, and the standard
maximization competitive ratio converges to
1
. Our main contribution is to
attain these full-information benchmarks under an unknown parameter using only
online observations.

Canonical Examples: Exponential, Pareto, and Bounded-Support Families

In this section, we instantiate the general framework for exponential, Pareto, and

\phi
allows us to

7.1

X_{i}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)

\phi(x)=x

[0,\infty)

Conclusion

We studied prophet inequalities for an exponential-type parametric family with
an unknown parameter. We characterized the full-information
distribution-specific asymptotic benchmark and proposed a confidence-based
DP learning policy that attains it using only online observations. Our
example-specific analyses and experiments show how parametric structure enables
distribution-specific optimal performance beyond distribution-agnostic
worst-case guarantees, including in heavy-tailed settings.

Limitations.

Our guarantees rely on an exponential-type parametric family with a one-dimensional unknown parameter. An interesting direction for future work is to extend this approach
to richer parametric, contextual, or misspecified models.

A.2
Equality of the asymptotic constants

We prove the two endpoint regimes separately.

Unbounded-support case.

Define

f(y):=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{\psi(y)},\qquad g(y):=\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}.

Then
f(y)>0
eventually,
f(y)\to c
, and
g(y)\to c^{\prime}
. Differentiating
f
, we obtain

f^{\prime}(y)=\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)\psi(y)-(\psi^{\prime}(y))^{2}}{\psi(y)^{2}}=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{\psi(y)}\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{\psi(y)}\right)=f(y)\bigl(g(y)-f(y)\bigr).

If
c>0
, then

f^{\prime}(y)\to c(c^{\prime}-c).

Since
f(y)
converges to the finite limit
c
, its derivative cannot converge to a nonzero constant; otherwise
f
would eventually grow or decrease linearly. Hence
c(c^{\prime}-c)=0
, and since
c>0
, we get
c^{\prime}=c
.

It remains to consider
c=0
. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
c^{\prime}>0
. Then for all sufficiently large
y
,

0<f(y)\leq\frac{c^{\prime}}{4},\qquad g(y)\geq\frac{c^{\prime}}{2}.

Therefore,

f^{\prime}(y)=f(y)\bigl(g(y)-f(y)\bigr)\geq\frac{c^{\prime}}{4}f(y).

Equivalently,
(\log f(y))^{\prime}\geq c^{\prime}/4
for all sufficiently large
y
. Hence
f(y)
grows at least exponentially along the tail, contradicting
f(y)\to 0
. Thus
c^{\prime}=0
, and therefore
c=c^{\prime}
also when
c=0
.

Bounded-support case.

Let

w(y):=\bar{\psi}(y)=x_{F}-\psi(y).

Then
w(y)>0
,
w(y)\to 0
, and
w^{\prime}(y)<0
eventually. Define

p(y):=-\frac{w^{\prime}(y)}{w(y)},\qquad q(y):=-\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(y)}{w^{\prime}(y)}.

The assumptions imply
p(y)\to c
and
q(y)\to c^{\prime}
. Moreover,
p(y)>0
eventually. Differentiating
p
, we get

p^{\prime}(y)=-\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(y)}{w(y)}+\left(\frac{w^{\prime}(y)}{w(y)}\right)^{2}.

Since

\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(y)}{w(y)}=\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(y)}{w^{\prime}(y)}\frac{w^{\prime}(y)}{w(y)}=\bigl(-q(y)\bigr)\bigl(-p(y)\bigr)=p(y)q(y),

we obtain

p^{\prime}(y)=p(y)\bigl(p(y)-q(y)\bigr).

If
c>0
, then

p^{\prime}(y)\to c(c-c^{\prime}).

Since
p(y)
converges to the finite limit
c
, the derivative
p^{\prime}(y)
cannot converge to a nonzero constant. Hence
c(c-c^{\prime})=0
, and because
c>0
, we get
c^{\prime}=c
.

Now suppose
c=0
. We claim that
c^{\prime}=0
. Assume, toward a contradiction, that
c^{\prime}>0
. Then for all sufficiently large
y
,

0<p(y)\leq\frac{c^{\prime}}{4},\qquad q(y)\geq\frac{c^{\prime}}{2}.

Thus

p^{\prime}(y)=p(y)\bigl(p(y)-q(y)\bigr)\leq-\frac{c^{\prime}}{4}p(y).

Consequently, for some
y_{0}
and all
y\geq y_{0}
,

p(y)\leq p(y_{0})\exp\left(-\frac{c^{\prime}}{4}(y-y_{0})\right).

In particular,

\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}p(s)\,ds<\infty.

But
p(y)=-w^{\prime}(y)/w(y)
, so

\log w(y)=\log w(y_{0})-\int_{y_{0}}^{y}p(s)\,ds.

The finiteness of the integral implies that
\log w(y)
remains bounded below as
y\to\infty
, and hence
w(y)
cannot converge to
0
. This contradicts
w(y)=\bar{\psi}(y)\to 0
. Therefore
c^{\prime}=0
.

Thus
c=c^{\prime}
in the bounded-support case as well.

A.3
Why we assume
c,c^{\prime}\in[0,\theta)

Lemma 18
.

Let

Y\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\theta),\qquad X=\psi(Y),

where
\theta>0
and
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[x_{0},\infty)
is strictly increasing. Define

c:=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{\psi(y)},\qquad c^{\prime}:=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)},

whenever the limits exist. If either
c>\theta
or
c^{\prime}>\theta
, then

\mathbb{E}[X]=\infty.

Proof.

Since
Y\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)
and
X=\psi(Y)
, we have

\mathbb{E}[X]=\mathbb{E}[\psi(Y)]=\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta y}\psi(y)\,dy.

Thus it suffices to show that the integral on the right-hand side diverges.

Case 1:
c>\theta
.

Choose
\varepsilon>0
such that

c-\varepsilon>\theta.

Since

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{\psi(y)}\to c,

there exists
y_{0}
such that

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{\psi(y)}\geq c-\varepsilon,\qquad y\geq y_{0}.

Using

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{\psi(y)}=\frac{d}{dy}\log\psi(y),

we integrate from
y_{0}
to
y\geq y_{0}
and obtain

\log\psi(y)-\log\psi(y_{0})=\int_{y_{0}}^{y}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(u)}{\psi(u)}\,du\geq(c-\varepsilon)(y-y_{0}).

Hence

\psi(y)\geq\psi(y_{0})e^{-(c-\varepsilon)y_{0}}e^{(c-\varepsilon)y}=:C_{1}e^{(c-\varepsilon)y},\qquad y\geq y_{0},

for some constant
C_{1}>0
. Therefore

\mathbb{E}[X]\geq\theta C_{1}\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-\theta y}e^{(c-\varepsilon)y}\,dy=\theta C_{1}\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}e^{(c-\varepsilon-\theta)y}\,dy.

Since
c-\varepsilon-\theta>0
, the last integral diverges. Thus

\mathbb{E}[X]=\infty.

Case 2:
c^{\prime}>\theta
.

Choose
\varepsilon>0
such that

c^{\prime}-\varepsilon>\theta.

Since

\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\to c^{\prime},

there exists
y_{1}
such that

\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\geq c^{\prime}-\varepsilon,\qquad y\geq y_{1}.

Using

\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}=\frac{d}{dy}\log\psi^{\prime}(y),

we integrate from
y_{1}
to
y\geq y_{1}
and obtain

\log\psi^{\prime}(y)-\log\psi^{\prime}(y_{1})=\int_{y_{1}}^{y}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\psi^{\prime}(u)}\,du\geq(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon)(y-y_{1}).

Hence

\psi^{\prime}(y)\geq\psi^{\prime}(y_{1})e^{-(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon)y_{1}}e^{(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon)y}=:C_{2}e^{(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon)y},\qquad y\geq y_{1},

for some constant
C_{2}>0
. Integrating once more,

\displaystyle\psi(y)-\psi(y_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{1}}^{y}\psi^{\prime}(u)\,du

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{y}e^{(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon)u}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{2}}{c^{\prime}-\varepsilon}\Bigl(e^{(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon)y}-e^{(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon)y_{1}}\Bigr).

Therefore, for all sufficiently large
y
,

\psi(y)\geq C_{3}e^{(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon)y}

for some constant
C_{3}>0
. Consequently,

\mathbb{E}[X]\geq\theta C_{3}\int_{y_{2}}^{\infty}e^{-\theta y}e^{(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon)y}\,dy=\theta C_{3}\int_{y_{2}}^{\infty}e^{(c^{\prime}-\varepsilon-\theta)y}\,dy

for some
y_{2}
large enough. Since
c^{\prime}-\varepsilon-\theta>0
, this integral diverges, and hence

\mathbb{E}[X]=\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

A.4
Extreme-value interpretation of the endpoint regularity condition

In this appendix, we justify the connection between Assumption
3

and the classical extreme-value domains of attraction.

Recall that

F_{\theta}(x)=1-\exp(-\theta\phi(x)),\qquad x\in[x_{0},x_{F}),

with
\psi:=\phi^{-1}
, and define the upper quantile function

U(t):=F_{\theta}^{-1}(1-1/t),\qquad t>1.

Since

1-\frac{1}{t}=1-\exp(-\theta\phi(U(t))),

we obtain

\exp(-\theta\phi(U(t)))=\frac{1}{t},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\phi(U(t))=\frac{\log t}{\theta}.

Therefore

U(t)=\psi\!\left(\frac{\log t}{\theta}\right).

We now analyze the two endpoint regimes separately.

Lemma 20

(Quantile growth under endpoint regularity)
.

Let
U(t)=\psi((\log t)/\theta)
.

(i)

Suppose
x_{F}=\infty
and

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{\psi(y)}\to c_{+}\geq 0\qquad\text{as }y\to\infty.

Then for every
x>0
,

\frac{U(tx)}{U(t)}\to x^{c_{+}/\theta}\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty.

In particular:

•

if
c_{+}>0
, then
U
is regularly varying at infinity with index
c_{+}/\theta
;

•

if
c_{+}=0
, then
U
is slowly varying at infinity.

(ii)

Suppose
x_{F}<\infty
, define

\bar{\psi}(y):=x_{F}-\psi(y),

and assume

\frac{\bar{\psi}^{\prime}(y)}{\bar{\psi}(y)}\to-c_{-}\qquad\text{as }y\to\infty

for some
c_{-}\geq 0
. Then for every
x>0
,

\frac{x_{F}-U(tx)}{x_{F}-U(t)}\to x^{-c_{-}/\theta}\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty.

In particular, if
c_{-}>0
, then
x_{F}-U(t)
is regularly varying at infinity with
index
-c_{-}/\theta
.

Proof.

We start with the unbounded-endpoint case.
Fix
x>0
. Since

U(t)=\psi\!\left(\frac{\log t}{\theta}\right),

we have

\log\frac{U(tx)}{U(t)}=\log\frac{\psi\!\left(\frac{\log t+\log x}{\theta}\right)}{\psi\!\left(\frac{\log t}{\theta}\right)}.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

\log\frac{U(tx)}{U(t)}=\int_{\log t/\theta}^{(\log t+\log x)/\theta}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(u)}{\psi(u)}\,du.

Let

a_{t}:=\frac{\log t}{\theta},\qquad b_{t}:=\frac{\log x}{\theta}.

Then
a_{t}\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, while
b_{t}
is constant. Hence

\int_{a_{t}}^{a_{t}+b_{t}}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(u)}{\psi(u)}\,du-c_{+}b_{t}=\int_{a_{t}}^{a_{t}+b_{t}}\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime}(u)}{\psi(u)}-c_{+}\right)\,du.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, by the assumption
\psi^{\prime}(u)/\psi(u)\to c_{+}
, there exists
M

such that

\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(u)}{\psi(u)}-c_{+}\right|\leq\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }u\geq M.

For
t
large enough, the whole interval
[a_{t},a_{t}+b_{t}]
lies beyond
M
if
b_{t}\geq 0
,
and similarly the same estimate applies when
b_{t}<0
. Therefore

\left|\int_{a_{t}}^{a_{t}+b_{t}}\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime}(u)}{\psi(u)}-c_{+}\right)\,du\right|\leq\varepsilon|b_{t}|.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary,

\log\frac{U(tx)}{U(t)}\to c_{+}b_{t}=\frac{c_{+}}{\theta}\log x.

Exponentiating yields

\frac{U(tx)}{U(t)}\to x^{c_{+}/\theta}.

This proves part (i).

For the bounded-support case, note that

x_{F}-U(t)=x_{F}-\psi\!\left(\frac{\log t}{\theta}\right)=\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log t}{\theta}\right).

Hence

\log\frac{x_{F}-U(tx)}{x_{F}-U(t)}=\log\frac{\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log t+\log x}{\theta}\right)}{\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log t}{\theta}\right)}=\int_{\log t/\theta}^{(\log t+\log x)/\theta}\frac{\bar{\psi}^{\prime}(u)}{\bar{\psi}(u)}\,du.

Using exactly the same argument as above together with

\frac{\bar{\psi}^{\prime}(u)}{\bar{\psi}(u)}\to-c_{-},

we obtain

\log\frac{x_{F}-U(tx)}{x_{F}-U(t)}\to-\frac{c_{-}}{\theta}\log x.

Exponentiating gives

\frac{x_{F}-U(tx)}{x_{F}-U(t)}\to x^{-c_{-}/\theta}.

This proves part (ii).
∎

Corollary 21

(Connection with extreme-value domains)
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
20
:

(i)

If
x_{F}=\infty
and
c_{+}>0
, then
F_{\theta}
belongs to the

\gamma=\frac{c_{+}}{\theta}.

(ii)

If
x_{F}<\infty
and
c_{-}>0
, then
F_{\theta}
belongs to the

\gamma=-\frac{c_{-}}{\theta}.

If

Proof.

Part (i) follows from the standard quantile characterization of the Fréchet domain:
for distributions with unbounded upper endpoint,

if and only if its upper quantile function

belongs to the reverse-Weibull
domain of attraction with index
\gamma<0
if and only if

\gamma
.

The final statement is immediate from Lemma
20
(i).

A.6
Proof of Theorem
6

For each
i\in[n]
, let
V_{i}
denote the optimal expected reward when only periods

i,i+1,\dots,n
remain, and set
V_{n+1}:=0
. Then the optimal stopping rule is the threshold rule

\tau^{*}=\inf\{i\in[n]:X_{i}\geq V_{i+1}\},

and the value function satisfies

V_{i}=\mathbb{E}[\max\{X_{i},V_{i+1}\}],\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

Since the last observation must be accepted,

V_{n}=\mathbb{E}[X_{1}].

Define, for
a\geq x_{0}
,

r_{\theta}(a):=\mathbb{E}[(X_{1}-a)^{+}]=\int_{a}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{1}>t)\,dt=\int_{a}^{\infty}e^{-\theta\phi(t)}\,dt.

Then the dynamic-programming recursion becomes

V_{i}=V_{i+1}+r_{\theta}(V_{i+1}),\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1.

We first derive the asymptotic behavior of
r_{\theta}(a)
as
a\to\infty
.
Write
a=\psi(y)
. With the change of variables
t=\psi(u)
, so that
dt=\psi^{\prime}(u)\,du
, we obtain

r_{\theta}(\psi(y))=\int_{y}^{\infty}e^{-\theta u}\psi^{\prime}(u)\,du=e^{-\theta y}\psi^{\prime}(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta s}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\,ds.

Since
\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)/\psi^{\prime}(y)\to c_{+}
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
y_{\varepsilon}
such that

\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\psi^{\prime}(u)}\leq c_{+}+\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }u\geq y_{\varepsilon}.

Hence, for
y\geq y_{\varepsilon}
and
s\geq 0
,

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}=\exp\!\left(\int_{y}^{y+s}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\psi^{\prime}(u)}\,du\right)\leq e^{(c_{+}+\varepsilon)s}.

Also, for each fixed
s\geq 0
,

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\to e^{c_{+}s}\qquad(y\to\infty).

Since
c_{+}+\varepsilon<\theta
, dominated convergence in (
A.6
) yields

r_{\theta}(\psi(y))\sim e^{-\theta y}\psi^{\prime}(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\theta-c_{+})s}\,ds=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)e^{-\theta y}}{\theta-c_{+}}.

Next define

\Psi(x):=1+\int_{V_{n}}^{x}\frac{dt}{r_{\theta}(t)},\qquad x\geq V_{n},

and let

W_{i}:=\Psi(V_{i}),\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

Then
W_{n}=1
.

Now define

Q(y):=\Psi(\psi(y)).

By the chain rule and (
A.6
),

Q^{\prime}(y)=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{r_{\theta}(\psi(y))}\sim(\theta-c_{+})e^{\theta y}.

Since
Q(y)\to\infty
, l’Hôpital’s rule implies

Q(y)\sim\frac{\theta-c_{+}}{\theta}e^{\theta y}.

Equivalently,

\Psi(x)\sim\frac{\theta-c_{+}}{\theta}e^{\theta\phi(x)}\qquad(x\to\infty).

We next study the increments
W_{i}-W_{i+1}
. From (
A.6
),

V_{i}-V_{i+1}=r_{\theta}(V_{i+1}).

Since
r_{\theta}
is decreasing,

W_{i}-W_{i+1}=\int_{V_{i+1}}^{V_{i}}\frac{dt}{r_{\theta}(t)}\geq\frac{V_{i}-V_{i+1}}{r_{\theta}(V_{i+1})}=1.

Therefore,

W_{j}\geq W_{n}+(n-j)=1+n-j,\qquad j=1,\dots,n.

For the upper bound, let
a:=V_{i+1}
. Then
V_{i}=a+r_{\theta}(a)
, and so

W_{i}-W_{i+1}=\int_{a}^{a+r_{\theta}(a)}\frac{dt}{r_{\theta}(t)}\leq\frac{r_{\theta}(a)}{r_{\theta}(a+r_{\theta}(a))}.

Also,

r_{\theta}(a)-r_{\theta}(a+r_{\theta}(a))=\int_{a}^{a+r_{\theta}(a)}e^{-\theta\phi(t)}\,dt\leq r_{\theta}(a)e^{-\theta\phi(a)}.

Hence

r_{\theta}(a+r_{\theta}(a))\geq r_{\theta}(a)\bigl(1-e^{-\theta\phi(a)}\bigr),

which implies

W_{i}-W_{i+1}\leq\frac{1}{1-e^{-\theta\phi(a)}}.

Now consider

G(a):=\left(\frac{1}{1-e^{-\theta\phi(a)}}-1\right)\Psi(a)=\frac{e^{-\theta\phi(a)}}{1-e^{-\theta\phi(a)}}\Psi(a).

Using (
A.6
), we have

G(a)\to\frac{\theta-c_{+}}{\theta}\qquad(a\to\infty).

Thus
G
is bounded on
[V_{n},\infty)
, so there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{1}{1-e^{-\theta\phi(a)}}\leq 1+\frac{C}{\Psi(a)},\qquad a\geq V_{n}.

Applying this to
a=V_{i+1}
in (
A.6
), we obtain

W_{i}-W_{i+1}\leq 1+\frac{C}{W_{i+1}}.

Summing (
A.6
) from
i=1
to
n-1
gives

W_{1}-W_{n}\leq n-1+C\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{W_{i+1}}.

Using (
A.6
),

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{W_{i+1}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{n-i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k}=O(\log n).

Since
W_{n}=1
, it follows that

W_{1}\leq n+O(\log n).

Combining this with the lower bound (
A.6
), we obtain

W_{1}=n+O(\log n).

Since
W_{1}=\Psi(V_{1})
, combining (
A.6
) and (
A.6
) yields

\frac{\theta-c_{+}}{\theta}e^{\theta\phi(V_{1})}=n(1+o(1)).

Hence

\phi(V_{1})=\frac{\log n}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\theta}\log\!\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-c_{+}}\right)+o(1).

Let

y_{n}:=\frac{\log n}{\theta},\qquad d:=\frac{1}{\theta}\log\!\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-c_{+}}\right).

Then (
A.6
) reads

V_{1}=\psi(y_{n}+d+o(1)).

Now, since
\psi^{\prime}(y)/\psi(y)\to c_{+}
, for every fixed
u
,

\log\frac{\psi(y+u)}{\psi(y)}=\int_{y}^{y+u}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(t)}{\psi(t)}\,dt\to c_{+}u.

Therefore

\frac{\psi(y_{n}+d)}{\psi(y_{n})}\to e^{cd}=\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-c_{+}}\right)^{c_{+}/\theta}.

It follows that

V_{1}=\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-c_{+}}\right)^{c_{+}/\theta}\psi\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)(1+o(1)).

Since
V_{1}=\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau^{*}}]
, this proves the first asymptotic statement.

We now turn to the prophet benchmark. Define

Y_{i}:=\phi(X_{i}).

Then
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
are i.i.d.
\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)
random variables, and

M_{n}=\psi(Y_{(n)}),\qquad Y_{(n)}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}Y_{i}.

The density of
Y_{(n)}
is

f_{n}(y)=n\theta e^{-\theta y}(1-e^{-\theta y})^{n-1},\qquad y\geq 0.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}n\theta e^{-\theta y}(1-e^{-\theta y})^{n-1}\psi(y)\,dy.

With the substitution

z=\theta y-\log n,\qquad y=\frac{\log n+z}{\theta},

we obtain

\frac{\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]}{\psi((\log n)/\theta)}=\int_{-\log n}^{\infty}e^{-z}\left(1-\frac{e^{-z}}{n}\right)^{n-1}\frac{\psi((\log n+z)/\theta)}{\psi((\log n)/\theta)}\,dz.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

e^{-z}\left(1-\frac{e^{-z}}{n}\right)^{n-1}\to e^{-z-e^{-z}},

and, since
\psi^{\prime}(y)/\psi(y)\to c_{+}
,

\frac{\psi((\log n+z)/\theta)}{\psi((\log n)/\theta)}\to e^{(c_{+}/\theta)z}.

We now justify dominated convergence in (
A.6
). For
z<0
, since
\psi
is increasing,

\frac{\psi((\log n+z)/\theta)}{\psi((\log n)/\theta)}\leq 1,

and

\left(1-\frac{e^{-z}}{n}\right)^{n-1}\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{n-1}{n}e^{-z}\right),

so the integrand is bounded by

e^{-z}\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}e^{-z}\right)

for all large
n
, which is integrable on
(-\infty,0]
.

For
z\geq 0
, choose
\varepsilon>0
such that
c+\varepsilon<\theta
. Then for all large
n
,

\frac{\psi((\log n+z)/\theta)}{\psi((\log n)/\theta)}=\exp\!\left(\int_{(\log n)/\theta}^{(\log n+z)/\theta}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(t)}{\psi(t)}\,dt\right)\leq e^{((c_{+}+\varepsilon)/\theta)z}.

Since
\left(1-\frac{e^{-z}}{n}\right)^{n-1}\leq 1
, the integrand is bounded by

e^{-(1-(c_{+}+\varepsilon)/\theta)z},

which is integrable on
[0,\infty)
.

Therefore dominated convergence applies to (
A.6
), and by (
A.6
),

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]}{\psi((\log n)/\theta)}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-z-e^{-z}}e^{(c_{+}/\theta)z}\,dz.

With the change of variables
u=e^{-z}
, so that
dz=-du/u
, this becomes

\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-c_{+}/\theta}e^{-u}\,du=\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{c_{+}}{\theta}\right).

Hence

\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]=\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{c_{+}}{\theta}\right)\psi\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)(1+o(1)).

Finally,

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau^{*};\theta)=\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau^{*}}]}{\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]}\to\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-c_{+}}\right)^{c_{+}/\theta}}{\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{c_{+}}{\theta}\right)}.

This proves the theorem.

\frac{x_{F}-\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]}{\bar{\psi}((\log n)/\theta)}=\int_{-\log n}^{\infty}e^{-z}\left(1-\frac{e^{-z}}{n}\right)^{n-1}\frac{\bar{\psi}((\log n+z)/\theta)}{\bar{\psi}((\log n)/\theta)}\,dz.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{R}
. By (
A.7
),

\frac{\bar{\psi}((\log n+z)/\theta)}{\bar{\psi}((\log n)/\theta)}\to e^{-(c_{-}/\theta)z}.

Also,

e^{-z}\left(1-\frac{e^{-z}}{n}\right)^{n-1}\to e^{-z-e^{-z}}.

We now justify dominated convergence in (

For

, since
\bar{\psi}
is decreasing,

\frac{\bar{\psi}((\log n+z)/\theta)}{\bar{\psi}((\log n)/\theta)}\leq 1,

so the integrand is bounded by

, which is integrable on
[0,\infty)
.

For

\varepsilon\in(0,c_{-})
.
Using
\psi^{\prime}(y)/(x_{F}-\psi(y))\to c_{-}
, one checks by integrating over

and absorbing the part below a fixed

into a constant
that there exists

such that for all large

\frac{\bar{\psi}((\log n+z)/\theta)}{\bar{\psi}((\log n)/\theta)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}e^{-((c_{-}+\varepsilon)/\theta)z},\qquad-\log n\leq z<0.

Also,

\left(1-\frac{e^{-z}}{n}\right)^{n-1}\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{n-1}{n}e^{-z}\right)\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}e^{-z}\right)

for all large

C_{\varepsilon}e^{-(1+(c_{-}+\varepsilon)/\theta)z}\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}e^{-z}\right),

which is integrable on
(-\infty,0]

Therefore dominated convergence applies to (
A.7

A.7

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{F}-\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]}{\bar{\psi}((\log n)/\theta)}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-z-e^{-z}}e^{-(c_{-}/\theta)z}\,dz.

With the change of variables

\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{c_{-}/\theta}e^{-u}\,du=\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{c_{-}}{\theta}\right).

L_{i}(\theta,\eta;N)

Lemma 25

(Uniform asymptotic for the DP value)
.

Let

K=[\underline{\eta},\overline{\eta}]\subset(c_{+},\infty)

be a compact interval.
Define

C(\eta):=\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta-c_{+}}\right)^{c_{+}/\eta}.

Then, as
N\to\infty
,

V_{1}(\eta;N)=C(\eta)\psi\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\eta}\right)(1+o(1)),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
.

Proof.

We write the proof in several steps.

Step 1: Uniform asymptotic for
r_{\eta}
.

Fix
\eta\in K
. For
a=\psi(y)
, the change of variables
t=\psi(u)
gives

r_{\eta}(\psi(y))=\int_{y}^{\infty}e^{-\eta u}\psi^{\prime}(u)\,du=e^{-\eta y}\psi^{\prime}(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\eta s}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\,ds.

Since
\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)/\psi^{\prime}(u)\to c_{+}
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
y_{\varepsilon}
such that

\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\psi^{\prime}(u)}-c_{+}\right|\leq\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }u\geq y_{\varepsilon}.

Choose
\varepsilon>0
so small that

c_{+}+\varepsilon<\underline{\eta}.

Then for all
y\geq y_{\varepsilon}
and all
s\geq 0
,

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}=\exp\!\left(\int_{y}^{y+s}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\psi^{\prime}(u)}\,du\right)\leq e^{(c_{+}+\varepsilon)s}.

Moreover, for each fixed
s\geq 0
,

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\to e^{cs}\qquad(y\to\infty).

Now define

I_{\eta}(y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\eta s}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\,ds.

From (
A.8
),

e^{-\eta s}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\leq e^{-(\underline{\eta}-c-\varepsilon)s},

and the right-hand side is integrable on
[0,\infty)
. Therefore dominated convergence gives

\sup_{\eta\in K}\left|I_{\eta}(y)-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\eta-c)s}\,ds\right|\to 0.

Since

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\eta-c_{+})s}\,ds=\frac{1}{\eta-c},

we obtain

\sup_{\eta\in K}\left|\frac{r_{\eta}(\psi(y))}{\psi^{\prime}(y)e^{-\eta y}}-\frac{1}{\eta-c_{+}}\right|\to 0.

Equivalently,

r_{\eta}(\psi(y))=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)e^{-\eta y}}{\eta-c}(1+o(1)),

uniformly for
\eta\in K
.

Step 2: Uniform asymptotic for the scale transform
\Psi_{\eta}
.

Let

\mu(\eta):=\mathbb{E}_{\eta}[X_{1}],\qquad\Psi_{\eta}(x):=1+\int_{\mu(\eta)}^{x}\frac{dt}{r_{\eta}(t)},\qquad x\geq\mu(\eta).

Define also

Q_{\eta}(y):=\Psi_{\eta}(\psi(y)).

Then, by the chain rule,

Q_{\eta}^{\prime}(y)=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{r_{\eta}(\psi(y))}.

Using (
A.8
),

\sup_{\eta\in K}\left|\frac{Q_{\eta}^{\prime}(y)}{(\eta-c_{+})e^{\eta y}}-1\right|\to 0.

We now integrate this asymptotic. Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
. By (
A.8
), there exists

y_{0}
such that for all
y\geq y_{0}
and all
\eta\in K
,

(1-\rho)(\eta-c_{+})e^{\eta y}\leq Q_{\eta}^{\prime}(y)\leq(1+\rho)(\eta-c_{+})e^{\eta y}.

Integrating from
y_{0}
to
y\geq y_{0}
,

(1-\rho)\frac{\eta-c_{+}}{\eta}\bigl(e^{\eta y}-e^{\eta y_{0}}\bigr)\leq Q_{\eta}(y)-Q_{\eta}(y_{0})\leq(1+\rho)\frac{\eta-c_{+}}{\eta}\bigl(e^{\eta y}-e^{\eta y_{0}}\bigr).

Since
Q_{\eta}(y_{0})
is bounded uniformly in
\eta\in K
, dividing by
e^{\eta y}
and letting

y\to\infty
gives

\sup_{\eta\in K}\left|\frac{Q_{\eta}(y)}{((\eta-c_{+})/\eta)e^{\eta y}}-1\right|\to 0.

Equivalently,

\Psi_{\eta}(x)=\frac{\eta-c_{+}}{\eta}e^{\eta\phi(x)}(1+o(1))\qquad(x\to\infty),

uniformly for
\eta\in K
.

Step 3: Uniform growth of the transformed DP sequence.

For
\eta\in K
and horizon
N
, define

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}:=\Psi_{\eta}(V_{i}(\eta;N)),\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

Then

W_{N}^{(\eta,N)}=\Psi_{\eta}(\mu(\eta))=1.

Since

V_{i}(\eta;N)-V_{i+1}(\eta;N)=r_{\eta}(V_{i+1}(\eta;N)),

and
r_{\eta}
is decreasing, we get

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}-W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}=\int_{V_{i+1}(\eta;N)}^{V_{i}(\eta;N)}\frac{dt}{r_{\eta}(t)}\geq\frac{V_{i}(\eta;N)-V_{i+1}(\eta;N)}{r_{\eta}(V_{i+1}(\eta;N))}=1.

Hence

W_{j}^{(\eta,N)}\geq 1+N-j,\qquad j=1,\dots,N.

For the upper bound, let
a:=V_{i+1}(\eta;N)
. Then

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}-W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}=\int_{a}^{a+r_{\eta}(a)}\frac{dt}{r_{\eta}(t)}\leq\frac{r_{\eta}(a)}{r_{\eta}(a+r_{\eta}(a))}.

Also,

r_{\eta}(a)-r_{\eta}(a+r_{\eta}(a))=\int_{a}^{a+r_{\eta}(a)}e^{-\eta\phi(t)}\,dt\leq r_{\eta}(a)e^{-\eta\phi(a)},

so

r_{\eta}(a+r_{\eta}(a))\geq r_{\eta}(a)\bigl(1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}\bigr),

and therefore

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}-W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}\leq\frac{1}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}.

Now define

G_{\eta}(a):=\left(\frac{1}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}-1\right)\Psi_{\eta}(a)=\frac{e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}\Psi_{\eta}(a).

Using (
A.8
),

G_{\eta}(a)=\frac{e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}\cdot\frac{\eta-c}{\eta}e^{\eta\phi(a)}(1+o(1))=\frac{\eta-c}{\eta}\frac{1+o(1)}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}},

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Since
\eta\geq\underline{\eta}>0
and
\phi(a)\to\infty
, it follows that

G_{\eta}(a)
is uniformly bounded for all sufficiently large
a
. By continuity, it is then
bounded on all relevant compact ranges as well. Thus there exists a constant
B_{K}>0
,
depending only on
K
, such that

\frac{1}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}\leq 1+\frac{B_{K}}{\Psi_{\eta}(a)},\qquad a\geq\mu(\eta),\ \eta\in K.

Applying this to
a=V_{i+1}(\eta;N)
in (
A.8
) gives

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}-W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}\leq 1+\frac{B_{K}}{W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}}.

Summing (
A.8
) from
i=1
to
N-1
, and using (
A.8
),

\displaystyle W_{1}^{(\eta,N)}-1

\displaystyle\leq(N-1)+B_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}}

\displaystyle\leq(N-1)+B_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N-i}=(N-1)+B_{K}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{k}.

Hence

W_{1}^{(\eta,N)}\leq N+O(\log N),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Combining with (
A.8
), we obtain

W_{1}^{(\eta,N)}=N+O(\log N),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
.

Step 4: Recovering
V_{1}(\eta;N)
.

Since
W_{1}^{(\eta,N)}=\Psi_{\eta}(V_{1}(\eta;N))
, combining (
A.8
) and
(
A.8
) yields

\frac{\eta-c_{+}}{\eta}e^{\eta\phi(V_{1}(\eta;N))}(1+o(1))=N+O(\log N),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Therefore

\frac{\eta-c_{+}}{\eta}e^{\eta\phi(V_{1}(\eta;N))}=N(1+o(1)),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
, and hence

\phi(V_{1}(\eta;N))=\frac{\log N}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\eta}\log\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta-c_{+}}\right)+o(1),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
.

Set

y_{N}(\eta):=\frac{\log N}{\eta},\qquad d(\eta):=\frac{1}{\eta}\log\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta-c_{+}}\right).

Then (
A.8
) says

V_{1}(\eta;N)=\psi\bigl(y_{N}(\eta)+d(\eta)+o(1)\bigr),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Since
K
is compact,
d(\eta)
is bounded on
K
; let

D:=\sup_{\eta\in K}|d(\eta)|<\infty.

Also,

\inf_{\eta\in K}y_{N}(\eta)=\frac{\log N}{\overline{\eta}}\to\infty.

Because
\psi^{\prime}(y)/\psi(y)\to c
, for every fixed
D^{\prime}>0
,

\sup_{|u|\leq D^{\prime}}\left|\log\frac{\psi(y+u)}{\psi(y)}-c_{+}u\right|\to 0\qquad(y\to\infty).

Applying this with
u=d(\eta)+o(1)
, which is uniformly bounded, gives

\frac{\psi(y_{N}(\eta)+d(\eta)+o(1))}{\psi(y_{N}(\eta))}=e^{c_{+}\,d(\eta)}(1+o(1)),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Since

e^{c_{+}\,d(\eta)}=\exp\!\left(\frac{c_{+}}{\eta}\log\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta-c_{+}}\right)\right)=\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta-c_{+}}\right)^{c_{+}/\eta}=C(\eta),

we conclude that

V_{1}(\eta;N)=C(\eta)\psi\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\eta}\right)(1+o(1)),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. This proves the lemma.

\frac{d}{dv}T_{\eta}(v)=1-e^{-\eta\phi(v)}=F_{\eta}(v)\geq 0,

and
T_{\eta}
is decreasing in
\eta
, because
r_{\eta}(v)
is decreasing in
\eta
.

Also
V_{N}(\eta;N)=\mathbb{E}_{\eta}[X_{1}]
is decreasing in
\eta
.
Therefore, a backward induction yields that for every fixed
i
,

\eta_{1}\leq\eta_{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_{i}(\eta_{1};N)\geq V_{i}(\eta_{2};N).

Step 4: Apply the comparison on the good event.

Condition on the exploration samples and on the good event
\mathcal{G}_{m}
. Then the algorithm
uses
\eta=\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}
, and by (
A.9
),

\theta\leq\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}\leq\theta_{+}.

By (
A.9
),

\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}\mid X_{1},\dots,X_{m},\mathcal{G}_{m}]\geq V_{1}(\theta^{(\mathrm{U})};N).

By the monotonicity (
A.9
),

V_{1}(\theta^{(\mathrm{U})};N)\geq V_{1}(\theta_{+};N).

Hence

\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}\mid X_{1},\dots,X_{m},\mathcal{G}_{m}]\geq V_{1}(\theta_{+};N).

Taking expectations and using
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_{m})\geq 1-\delta
,

\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}]\geq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_{m})\,V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)\geq(1-\delta)V_{1}(\theta_{+};N).

∎

Lemma 27

(Uniform asymptotic for the DP value in the bounded-support case)
.

Let

K=[\underline{\eta},\overline{\eta}]\subset(0,\infty)

be compact, and define

C_{-}(\eta):=\left(\frac{\eta+c}{\eta}\right)^{c/\eta}.

Then, as
N\to\infty
,

V_{1}(\eta;N)=x_{F}-C_{-}(\eta)\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\eta}\right)(1+o(1)),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
.

Proof.

We write the proof in several steps.

Step 1: Uniform asymptotic for
r_{\eta}
.

Fix
\eta\in K
. For
a=\psi(y)
, the change of variables
t=\psi(u)
gives

r_{\eta}(\psi(y))=\int_{y}^{\infty}e^{-\eta u}\psi^{\prime}(u)\,du=e^{-\eta y}\psi^{\prime}(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\eta s}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\,ds.

Since
\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)/\psi^{\prime}(u)\to-c_{-}
, for each fixed
s\geq 0
,

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}=\exp\!\left(\int_{y}^{y+s}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\psi^{\prime}(u)}\,du\right)\to e^{-c_{-}s}\qquad(y\to\infty).

If
c_{-}>0
, choose
\varepsilon\in(0,c_{-})
. For all sufficiently large
y
and all
s\geq 0
,

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\leq e^{-(c_{-}-\varepsilon)s}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in K}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\eta s}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\,ds-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\eta+c_{-})s}\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\underline{\eta}s}\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}-e^{-c_{-}s}\right|ds

\displaystyle\to 0,

by dominated convergence, since the integrand is dominated by

e^{-(\underline{\eta}+c_{-}-\varepsilon)s}+e^{-(\underline{\eta}+c_{-})s}\in L^{1}([0,\infty)).

If
c_{-}=0
, choose
0<\varepsilon<\underline{\eta}
. Then, uniformly over

\eta\in K
,

e^{-\eta s}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y+s)}{\psi^{\prime}(y)}\leq e^{-(\eta-\varepsilon)s}\leq e^{-(\underline{\eta}-\varepsilon)s},

and

e^{-(\eta+c_{-})s}=e^{-\eta s}\leq e^{-\underline{\eta}s}.

Since

e^{-(\underline{\eta}-\varepsilon)s}+e^{-\underline{\eta}s}\in L^{1}([0,\infty)),

dominated convergence also applies.

Therefore

\sup_{\eta\in K}\left|\frac{r_{\eta}(\psi(y))}{\psi^{\prime}(y)e^{-\eta y}}-\frac{1}{\eta+c_{-}}\right|\to 0.

Equivalently,

r_{\eta}(\psi(y))=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)e^{-\eta y}}{\eta+c_{-}}(1+o(1)),

uniformly for
\eta\in K
.

Step 2: Uniform asymptotic for the scale transform
\Psi_{\eta}
.

Let

\mu(\eta):=\mathbb{E}_{\eta}[X_{1}],\qquad\Psi_{\eta}(x):=1+\int_{\mu(\eta)}^{x}\frac{dt}{r_{\eta}(t)},\qquad x\in[\mu(\eta),x_{F}).

Define also

Q_{\eta}(y):=\Psi_{\eta}(\psi(y)).

Then, by the chain rule,

Q_{\eta}^{\prime}(y)=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(y)}{r_{\eta}(\psi(y))}.

Using (
A.9
),

\sup_{\eta\in K}\left|\frac{Q_{\eta}^{\prime}(y)}{(\eta+c_{-})e^{\eta y}}-1\right|\to 0.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
. Choose
y_{0}
so large that
\psi(y_{0})>\sup_{\eta\in K}\mu(\eta)
and,
for all
y\geq y_{0}
and all
\eta\in K
,

(1-\rho)(\eta+c_{-})e^{\eta y}\leq Q_{\eta}^{\prime}(y)\leq(1+\rho)(\eta+c_{-})e^{\eta y}.

Integrating from
y_{0}
to
y\geq y_{0}
,

(1-\rho)\frac{\eta+c_{-}}{\eta}\bigl(e^{\eta y}-e^{\eta y_{0}}\bigr)\leq Q_{\eta}(y)-Q_{\eta}(y_{0})\leq(1+\rho)\frac{\eta+c_{-}}{\eta}\bigl(e^{\eta y}-e^{\eta y_{0}}\bigr).

Since
Q_{\eta}(y_{0})
is bounded uniformly in
\eta\in K
, dividing by
e^{\eta y}
and letting

y\to\infty
gives

\sup_{\eta\in K}\left|\frac{Q_{\eta}(y)}{((\eta+c_{-})/\eta)e^{\eta y}}-1\right|\to 0.

Equivalently,

\Psi_{\eta}(x)=\frac{\eta+c_{-}}{\eta}e^{\eta\phi(x)}(1+o(1))\qquad(x\uparrow x_{F}),

uniformly for
\eta\in K
.

Step 3: Uniform growth of the transformed DP sequence.

For
\eta\in K
and horizon
N
, define

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}:=\Psi_{\eta}(V_{i}(\eta;N)),\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

Then

W_{N}^{(\eta,N)}=\Psi_{\eta}(\mu(\eta))=1.

Since

V_{i}(\eta;N)-V_{i+1}(\eta;N)=r_{\eta}(V_{i+1}(\eta;N)),

and
r_{\eta}
is decreasing, we get

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}-W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}=\int_{V_{i+1}(\eta;N)}^{V_{i}(\eta;N)}\frac{dt}{r_{\eta}(t)}\geq\frac{V_{i}(\eta;N)-V_{i+1}(\eta;N)}{r_{\eta}(V_{i+1}(\eta;N))}=1.

Hence

W_{j}^{(\eta,N)}\geq 1+N-j,\qquad j=1,\dots,N.

For the upper bound, let
a:=V_{i+1}(\eta;N)
. Since
X_{1}\leq x_{F}
almost surely,

r_{\eta}(a)=\mathbb{E}_{\eta}[(X_{1}-a)^{+}]\leq x_{F}-a,

and therefore
a+r_{\eta}(a)\leq x_{F}
. Hence

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}-W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}=\int_{a}^{a+r_{\eta}(a)}\frac{dt}{r_{\eta}(t)}\leq\frac{r_{\eta}(a)}{r_{\eta}(a+r_{\eta}(a))}.

Also,

r_{\eta}(a)-r_{\eta}(a+r_{\eta}(a))=\int_{a}^{a+r_{\eta}(a)}e^{-\eta\phi(t)}\,dt\leq r_{\eta}(a)e^{-\eta\phi(a)},

so

r_{\eta}(a+r_{\eta}(a))\geq r_{\eta}(a)\bigl(1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}\bigr),

and therefore

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}-W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}\leq\frac{1}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}.

Now define

G_{\eta}(a):=\left(\frac{1}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}-1\right)\Psi_{\eta}(a)=\frac{e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}\Psi_{\eta}(a).

Using (
A.9
),

G_{\eta}(a)=\frac{e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}\cdot\frac{\eta+c}{\eta}e^{\eta\phi(a)}(1+o(1))=\frac{\eta+c}{\eta}\frac{1+o(1)}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}},

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Since
\eta\geq\underline{\eta}>0
and
\phi(a)\to\infty
as

a\uparrow x_{F}
, it follows that
G_{\eta}(a)
is uniformly bounded for all sufficiently large
a
.
On the compact set

\{(\eta,a):\eta\in K,\ \mu(\eta)\leq a\leq a_{0}\}

it is continuous, hence uniformly bounded there as well.
Therefore there exists a constant
B_{K}>0
, depending only on
K
, such that

\frac{1}{1-e^{-\eta\phi(a)}}\leq 1+\frac{B_{K}}{\Psi_{\eta}(a)},\qquad a\in[\mu(\eta),x_{F}),\ \eta\in K.

Applying this to
a=V_{i+1}(\eta;N)
in (
A.9
) gives

W_{i}^{(\eta,N)}-W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}\leq 1+\frac{B_{K}}{W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}}.

Summing (
A.9
) from
i=1
to
N-1
, and using
(
A.9
),

\displaystyle W_{1}^{(\eta,N)}-1

\displaystyle\leq(N-1)+B_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{W_{i+1}^{(\eta,N)}}

\displaystyle\leq(N-1)+B_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N-i}=(N-1)+B_{K}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{k}.

Hence

W_{1}^{(\eta,N)}\leq N+O(\log N),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Combining with (
A.9
), we obtain

W_{1}^{(\eta,N)}=N+O(\log N),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
.

Step 4: Recovering
V_{1}(\eta;N)
.

Since
W_{1}^{(\eta,N)}=\Psi_{\eta}(V_{1}(\eta;N))
, combining (
A.9
) and
(
A.9
) yields

\frac{\eta+c_{-}}{\eta}e^{\eta\phi(V_{1}(\eta;N))}(1+o(1))=N+O(\log N),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Therefore

\frac{\eta+c_{-}}{\eta}e^{\eta\phi(V_{1}(\eta;N))}=N(1+o(1)),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
, and hence

\phi(V_{1}(\eta;N))=\frac{\log N}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\eta}\log\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta+c_{-}}\right)+o(1),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
.

Set

y_{N}(\eta):=\frac{\log N}{\eta},\qquad d(\eta):=\frac{1}{\eta}\log\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta+c_{-}}\right).

Then (
A.9
) says

x_{F}-V_{1}(\eta;N)=\bar{\psi}\bigl(y_{N}(\eta)+d(\eta)+o(1)\bigr),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Since
K
is compact,
d(\eta)
is bounded on
K
; let

D:=\sup_{\eta\in K}|d(\eta)|<\infty.

Also,

\inf_{\eta\in K}y_{N}(\eta)=\frac{\log N}{\overline{\eta}}\to\infty.

Because
\bar{\psi}^{\prime}(y)/\bar{\psi}(y)\to-c_{-}
, for every fixed
D^{\prime}>0
,

\sup_{|u|\leq D^{\prime}}\left|\log\frac{\bar{\psi}(y+u)}{\bar{\psi}(y)}+c_{-}u\right|\to 0\qquad(y\to\infty).

Applying this with
u=d(\eta)+o(1)
, which is uniformly bounded, gives

\frac{\bar{\psi}(y_{N}(\eta)+d(\eta)+o(1))}{\bar{\psi}(y_{N}(\eta))}=e^{-c_{-}\,d(\eta)}(1+o(1)),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. Since

e^{-c_{-}\,d(\eta)}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{c_{-}}{\eta}\log\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta+c_{-}}\right)\right)=\left(\frac{\eta+c_{-}}{\eta}\right)^{c_{-}/\eta}=C_{-}(\eta),

we conclude that

V_{1}(\eta;N)=x_{F}-C_{-}(\eta)\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\eta}\right)(1+o(1)),

uniformly in
\eta\in K
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Since
\theta_{+}\to\theta
, we may choose
\rho\in(0,\theta)
and define

K=[\theta-\rho,\theta+\rho]\subset(0,\infty),

such that
\theta_{+}\in K
for all sufficiently large
n
. Therefore, by
Lemma
27
applied with
\eta=\theta_{+}
,

V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)=x_{F}-C_{-}(\theta_{+})\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\theta_{+}}\right)(1+o(1)).

We next compare the argument of
\bar{\psi}
with
(\log n)/\theta
:

u_{n}:=\frac{\log N}{\theta_{+}}-\frac{\log n}{\theta}=\frac{\log(1-m/n)}{\theta_{+}}+\log n\left(\frac{1}{\theta_{+}}-\frac{1}{\theta}\right).

Since
m=o(n)
, the first term is
o(1)
. Also

\theta_{+}-\theta=O(\theta\varepsilon_{m}),

so the second term is
O(\varepsilon_{m}\log n)=o(1)
. Thus

u_{n}\to 0.

Let

y_{n}:=\frac{\log n}{\theta}.

Now, since
\bar{\psi}^{\prime}(y)/\bar{\psi}(y)\to-c
,

\log\frac{\bar{\psi}(y_{n}+u_{n})}{\bar{\psi}(y_{n})}=\int_{y_{n}}^{y_{n}+u_{n}}\frac{\bar{\psi}^{\prime}(t)}{\bar{\psi}(t)}\,dt\to 0,

and therefore

\frac{\bar{\psi}((\log N)/\theta_{+})}{\bar{\psi}((\log n)/\theta)}\to 1.

Also
C_{-}(\theta_{+})\to C_{-}(\theta)
. Combining (
A.9
) and
(
A.9
),

V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)=x_{F}-C_{-}(\theta)\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)(1+o(1)).

By the bounded-support analogue of Theorem
6
,

\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)=x_{F}-\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{c_{-}}{\theta}\right)\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)(1+o(1)).

We can obtain

\frac{1}{\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)}=\frac{1}{x_{F}}\left(1+\frac{\Gamma(1+\frac{c_{-}}{\theta})}{x_{F}}\bar{\psi}(\log n/\theta)+o(\bar{\psi}(\log n/\theta))\right),

then we have

\frac{V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)}{\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)}=1-\frac{C_{-}(\theta)-\Gamma(1+c_{-}/\theta)}{x_{F}}\bar{\psi}(\log n/\theta)+o(\bar{\psi}(\log n/\theta)).

From Assumption
3
, in the bounded-support case, if
c>0
, then

\log\bar{\psi}(y)=-c_{-}y+o(y),\qquad y\to\infty.

Therefore,

\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)=n^{-c_{-}/\theta+o(1)}.

When
c_{-}=0
, since
\psi(y)\uparrow x_{F}
as
y\to\infty
, we have

\bar{\psi}(y)=x_{F}-\psi(y)\to 0
, and hence

\bar{\psi}\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\theta}\right)=o(1).

Combining these estimates, we obtain

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau;\theta)\geq 1-\delta-O\!\left(n^{-c_{-}/\theta+o(1)}\right),\qquad c_{-}>0,

whereas for
c_{-}=0
,

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau;\theta)\geq 1-\delta-o(1).

The proof of the theorem is concluded by

.

A.10
Proof of Theorem
11

We split the proof into five steps.

Step 1: Dynamic programming values and the plug-in thresholds.

For the true exponential model
X_{i}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)
, let
V_{i}
be the optimal
dynamic-programming value when stages
i,i+1,\dots,n
remain, and set
V_{n+1}:=0
.
Then

V_{i}=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\max\{X_{i},V_{i+1}\}\bigr],\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

Since
X_{n}
must be accepted at the last stage,

V_{n}=\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=\frac{1}{\theta}.

Using
\max(X,a)=a+(X-a)^{+}
and

\mathbb{E}\bigl[(X-a)^{+}\bigr]=\int_{a}^{\infty}\Pr(X>t)\,dt=\int_{a}^{\infty}e^{-\theta t}\,dt=\frac{e^{-\theta a}}{\theta},

we get the recursion

V_{i}=V_{i+1}+\frac{e^{-\theta V_{i+1}}}{\theta},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1.

Now define

U_{i}:=\theta V_{i}.

Then

U_{n+1}=0,\qquad U_{n}=1,\qquad U_{i}=U_{i+1}+e^{-U_{i+1}},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1.

In particular, the sequence
(U_{i})_{i=1}^{n+1}
does not depend on
\theta
.

Next fix a deterministic upper bound
\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}\geq\theta
, and define the plug-in
threshold values by

\widehat{V}_{n+1}:=0,\qquad\widehat{V}_{n}:=\frac{1}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}},\qquad\widehat{V}_{i}=\widehat{V}_{i+1}+\frac{e^{-\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}\widehat{V}_{i+1}}}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}.

Let

\widehat{U}_{i}:=\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}\widehat{V}_{i}.

Then
\widehat{U}_{n+1}=0
,
\widehat{U}_{n}=1
, and

\widehat{U}_{i}=\widehat{U}_{i+1}+e^{-\widehat{U}_{i+1}},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1.

Since
(\widehat{U}_{i})
and
(U_{i})
satisfy the same backward recursion with the same terminal
condition, they are identical:

\widehat{U}_{i}=U_{i}\qquad\text{for all }i.

Therefore

\widehat{V}_{i}=\frac{U_{i}}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}\qquad\text{for all }i.

Step 2: Performance of the plug-in threshold policy under the true
\theta
.

Consider the stopping policy that, at time
i
, uses threshold

t_{i}:=\widehat{V}_{i+1}=\frac{U_{i+1}}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}.

Let
L_{i}
be the expected reward of this plug-in threshold policy from stages
i,i+1,\dots,n

under the true model
X_{j}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)
, and set
L_{n+1}:=0
.
Thus

L_{i}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[X_{i}\mathbf{1}\{X_{i}\geq t_{i}\}+L_{i+1}\mathbf{1}\{X_{i}<t_{i}\}\right].

We claim that the plug-in policy loses at most a multiplicative factor

\theta/\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}
compared with the optimal DP.

Lemma 28
.

For every
i\in[n]
,

L_{i}\geq\frac{\theta}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}V_{i}.

In particular,

L_{1}\geq\frac{\theta}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}V_{1}.

Proof.

Let

R:=\frac{\theta}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}\in(0,1],\qquad w_{i}:=\theta L_{i}.

We first derive a recursion for
L_{i}
. Since
X_{i}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)
,

\Pr(X_{i}\geq t_{i})=e^{-\theta t_{i}}.

Also, by the memoryless property,

\mathbb{E}[X_{i}\mid X_{i}\geq t_{i}]=t_{i}+\frac{1}{\theta}.

Therefore

\displaystyle L_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[X_{i}\mathbf{1}\{X_{i}\geq t_{i}\}]+\mathbb{E}[L_{i+1}\mathbf{1}\{X_{i}<t_{i}\}]

\displaystyle=\Pr(X_{i}\geq t_{i})\,\mathbb{E}[X_{i}\mid X_{i}\geq t_{i}]+(1-\Pr(X_{i}\geq t_{i}))L_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\left(t_{i}+\frac{1}{\theta}\right)e^{-\theta t_{i}}+(1-e^{-\theta t_{i}})L_{i+1}.

Multiplying both sides by
\theta
, we get

w_{i}=(\theta t_{i}+1)e^{-\theta t_{i}}+(1-e^{-\theta t_{i}})w_{i+1}.

Since

\theta t_{i}=\theta\widehat{V}_{i+1}=\frac{\theta}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}U_{i+1}=R\,U_{i+1},

this becomes

w_{i}=(RU_{i+1}+1)e^{-RU_{i+1}}+(1-e^{-RU_{i+1}})w_{i+1}.

We now prove by backward induction that

w_{i}\geq R\,U_{i}\qquad\text{for all }i\in[n].

Base case:
At stage
n
, the policy accepts
X_{n}
for sure because
t_{n}=\widehat{V}_{n+1}=0
.
Hence

L_{n}=\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=\frac{1}{\theta},\qquad w_{n}=\theta L_{n}=1.

Since
U_{n}=1
and
R\leq 1
,

w_{n}=1\geq R=RU_{n}.

Induction step:
Assume that
w_{i+1}\geq RU_{i+1}
. Then

\displaystyle w_{i}

\displaystyle=(RU_{i+1}+1)e^{-RU_{i+1}}+(1-e^{-RU_{i+1}})w_{i+1}

\displaystyle\geq(RU_{i+1}+1)e^{-RU_{i+1}}+(1-e^{-RU_{i+1}})RU_{i+1}

\displaystyle=RU_{i+1}+e^{-RU_{i+1}}.

On the other hand, since
U_{i}=U_{i+1}+e^{-U_{i+1}}
,

RU_{i}=RU_{i+1}+Re^{-U_{i+1}}.

Thus it remains to show that

e^{-RU_{i+1}}\geq Re^{-U_{i+1}}.

But for every
u\geq 0
and every
R\in(0,1]
,

\frac{e^{-Ru}}{e^{-u}}=e^{(1-R)u}\geq 1\geq R.

Hence
e^{-Ru}\geq Re^{-u}
, and therefore

w_{i}\geq RU_{i}.

This closes the induction.

Finally, since
w_{i}=\theta L_{i}
and
U_{i}=\theta V_{i}
, the inequality
w_{i}\geq RU_{i}
is
equivalent to

L_{i}\geq\frac{\theta}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}V_{i}.

∎

As an immediate consequence, if
\tau
denotes the stopping time of the plug-in threshold policy,
then

\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}]=L_{1}\geq\frac{\theta}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}V_{1}=\frac{\theta}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}\frac{U_{1}}{\theta}.

Since for i.i.d. exponential rewards

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}\right]=\frac{H_{n}}{\theta},\qquad H_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k},

we obtain

\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}]}{\mathbb{E}[\max_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}]}\geq\frac{(\theta/\theta^{(\mathrm{U})})V_{1}}{H_{n}/\theta}=\frac{\theta}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}\frac{U_{1}}{H_{n}}.

Thus the plug-in threshold policy suffers only the multiplicative loss

\theta/\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}
relative to the optimal exponential dynamic program.

Step 3: The online algorithm with
m
forced exploration stages.

Now consider the actual online algorithm:
it rejects the first
m
observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
(forced exploration),
uses them to compute
\widehat{\theta}_{m}
and
\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}
, and from stage
m+1
onward
uses the plug-in DP thresholds computed from
\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}
.
Since the
X_{i}
’s are i.i.d., the first
m
rejected observations are distributed exactly like
independent training samples, and they are independent of the future observations

X_{m+1},\dots,X_{n}
.
With
\varepsilon_{m}=\sqrt{4\log(2/\delta)/m}
, let

\mathcal{G}:=\left\{\left|\frac{\theta}{\widehat{\theta}_{m}}-1\right|\leq\varepsilon_{m}\right\},

which holds at least
1-\delta
probability.
Conditional on the event
\mathcal{G}
, we have
\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}\geq\theta
, so by the lemma,
the expected reward collected from stages
m+1,\dots,n
is at least

\frac{\theta}{\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}}V_{m+1}\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon_{m}}{1+\varepsilon_{m}}V_{m+1}=\frac{1-\varepsilon_{m}}{1+\varepsilon_{m}}\frac{U_{m+1}}{\theta}.

On the complement event
\mathcal{G}^{c}
, the reward is nonnegative. Therefore, using

\Pr(\mathcal{G})\geq 1-\delta
,

\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}]\geq(1-\delta)\frac{1-\varepsilon_{m}}{1+\varepsilon_{m}}\frac{U_{m+1}}{\theta}.

Dividing by
\mathbb{E}[\max_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}]=H_{n}/\theta
, we conclude that

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau):=\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}]}{\mathbb{E}[\max_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}]}\geq(1-\delta)\frac{1-\varepsilon_{m}}{1+\varepsilon_{m}}\frac{U_{m+1}}{H_{n}},\qquad\varepsilon_{m}=\sqrt{\frac{4\log(2/\delta)}{m}}.

Step 4: Asymptotics of
U_{m+1}
.

It remains to estimate
U_{m+1}
. Let

W_{i}:=e^{U_{i}}.

Since
U_{i}=U_{i+1}+e^{-U_{i+1}}
, we have

W_{i}=e^{U_{i+1}+e^{-U_{i+1}}}=W_{i+1}e^{1/W_{i+1}},

and hence

W_{i}-W_{i+1}=W_{i+1}\bigl(e^{1/W_{i+1}}-1\bigr).

Because

for all

,

W_{i}-W_{i+1}\geq 1.

Summing from

to

gives

W_{j}\geq W_{n}+(n-j)=e+n-j.

In particular,
W_{i}\geq e
for all
i
, so
1/W_{i+1}\leq 1/e
. For
0\leq y\leq 1/e
,
Taylor’s theorem implies
e^{y}-1\leq y+y^{2}
, and therefore

W_{i}-W_{i+1}\leq W_{i+1}\left(\frac{1}{W_{i+1}}+\frac{1}{W_{i+1}^{2}}\right)=1+\frac{1}{W_{i+1}}.

Summing again from
i=j
to
n-1
,

W_{j}-W_{n}\leq(n-j)+\sum_{i=j}^{n-1}\frac{1}{W_{i+1}}.

Using the lower bound
W_{i+1}\geq e+n-i-1
,

\sum_{i=j}^{n-1}\frac{1}{W_{i+1}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-j-1}\frac{1}{e+k}=O(\log(n-j+1)).

Hence

W_{j}=(n-j+1)+O(\log(n-j+1)).

Taking logarithms yields

U_{j}=\log W_{j}=\log(n-j+1)+O\!\left(\frac{\log(n-j+1)}{n-j+1}\right)=\log(n-j+1)+o(1).

Setting
j=m+1
, we obtain

U_{m+1}=\log(n-m)+o(1).

Finally, since

H_{n}=\log n+\Theta(1),

we conclude that

\frac{U_{m+1}}{H_{n}}=\left(1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\right)\frac{\log(n-m)}{\log n}.

Therefore

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau)\geq(1-\delta)\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\frac{U_{m+1}}{H_{n}}=(1-\delta)\frac{1-\Theta\bigl(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/m}\bigr)}{1+\Theta\bigl(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/m}\bigr)}\,\left(1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\right)\frac{\log(n-m)}{\log n}.

Step 5: Optimization with respect to
m
.

Therefore, for all sufficiently large
n
,

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau)\geq(1-\delta)\,\frac{1-\Theta\!\left(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/m}\right)}{1+\Theta\!\left(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/m}\right)}\cdot\frac{\log(n-m)}{\log n}\left(1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\right).

To make this precise, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}>0
such that

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau)\geq(1-\delta)\,\frac{1-c_{1}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/m}}{1+c_{2}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/m}}\cdot\left(1-c_{3}\frac{m}{n\log n}\right)\left(1-c_{4}\frac{1}{\log n}\right),

where in the second factor we used

\frac{\log(n-m)}{\log n}=1+\frac{\log(1-m/n)}{\log n}=1-O\!\left(\frac{m}{n\log n}\right),

which holds because
m=o(n)
and

\log(1-x)=-x+O(x^{2})\qquad(x\to 0).

Now set

a_{n}:=\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{m}}.

Since
m=\omega(\log(1/\delta))
, we have
a_{n}\to 0
. Using

\frac{1-c_{1}a_{n}}{1+c_{2}a_{n}}=1-\frac{(c_{1}+c_{2})a_{n}}{1+c_{2}a_{n}}\geq 1-C_{1}a_{n}

for some constant
C_{1}>0
and all sufficiently large
n
, we obtain

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau)\geq(1-\delta)\left(1-C_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{m}}\right)\left(1-c_{3}\frac{m}{n\log n}\right)\left(1-c_{4}\frac{1}{\log n}\right).

Using
(1-x)(1-y)\geq 1-x-y
for
x,y\geq 0
, it follows that

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau)\geq(1-\delta)\left(1-C_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{m}}-c_{3}\frac{m}{n\log n}-c_{4}\frac{1}{\log n}\right).

Hence

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau)\geq(1-\delta)\left(1-O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{m}}\right)-O\!\left(\frac{m}{n\log n}\right)-O\!\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\right).

A.11
Proof of Theorem
13

Let
V_{i}
be the optimal DP value when periods
i,i+1,\dots,n
remain,
and set
V_{n+1}=0
. For exponential rewards,

V_{i}=V_{i+1}+\frac{e^{-\theta V_{i+1}}}{\theta},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1,

with terminal value
V_{n}=1/\theta
. Define

U_{i}:=\theta V_{i}.

Then

U_{n}=1,\qquad U_{i}=U_{i+1}+e^{-U_{i+1}},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1.

Let

W_{i}:=e^{U_{i}}.

Then
W_{n}=e
, and

W_{i}=e^{U_{i}}=e^{U_{i+1}+e^{-U_{i+1}}}=W_{i+1}\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{W_{i+1}}\right).

Therefore

W_{i}-W_{i+1}=W_{i+1}\left(\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{W_{i+1}}\right)-1\right).

Since
e^{x}\geq 1+x
, we have

W_{i}-W_{i+1}\geq 1.

Thus

W_{i}\geq W_{n}+n-i=e+n-i.

In particular
W_{i+1}\geq e+n-i-1
. Since
W_{i+1}\geq e
, we have

1/W_{i+1}\leq 1/e
. Hence Taylor’s theorem gives

e^{x}-1\leq x+Cx^{2},\qquad 0\leq x\leq 1/e,

for some absolute constant
C>0
. Applying this with

x=1/W_{i+1}
, we obtain

W_{i}-W_{i+1}\leq 1+\frac{C}{W_{i+1}}\leq 1+\frac{C}{e+n-i-1}.

Summing from
i=1
to
n-1
, we get

W_{1}=n+O(\log n).

Consequently,

U_{1}=\log W_{1}=\log n+O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right).

Since
V_{1}=U_{1}/\theta
, the optimal full-information expected reward is

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[X_{\tau^{*}}]=\frac{1}{\theta}\left(\log n+O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\right).

On the other hand, for exponential rewards,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[\max_{i\in[n]}X_{i}\right]=\frac{H_{n}}{\theta},

where
H_{n}
is the
n
-th harmonic number. Using

H_{n}=\log n+\gamma+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),

where
\gamma\approx 0.57721
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant,
we obtain

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau^{*};\theta)=\frac{U_{1}}{H_{n}}=\frac{\log n+O(\log n/n)}{\log n+\gamma+O(1/n)}.

Therefore

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau^{*};\theta)=1-\frac{\gamma}{\log n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{\log^{2}n}\right).

This proves the claim.

A.12
Proof of Theorem
14

We first provide a lemma for the lower bound.

Lemma 29
.

Algorithm
1
with DP valuation updates (
2
) satisfies

\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)]}\geq(1-\delta)\xi_{\theta}(\alpha^{\star})\frac{V_{m+1}(\theta)}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)]},

where
\alpha^{\star}=\min_{i}\frac{V_{i}(\theta^{(U)})}{V_{i}(\theta)}
and
\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)=\frac{\theta\alpha}{\theta-1+\alpha^{\theta}}
.

Proof.

The proof consists of 5 steps.

Step 1: Dynamic-programming recursion.
Let
V_{i}(\theta)
be the optimal expected reward when stages
i,i+1,\dots,n
remain. Then
V_{n+1}(\theta):=0
, and at the last stage one must accept, so

V_{n}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=\frac{\theta x_{0}}{\theta-1}.

For
i=n-1,\dots,1
,

V_{i}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[\max\{X_{i},V_{i+1}(\theta)\}].

Since
V_{i+1}(\theta)\geq V_{n}(\theta)>x_{0}
, we may use the Pareto tail formula: for any
a\geq x_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}[(X-a)^{+}]=\int_{a}^{\infty}\Pr(X>t)\,dt=\int_{a}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t}\right)^{\theta}dt=\frac{x_{0}^{\theta}}{\theta-1}a^{1-\theta}.

Hence

V_{i}(\theta)=V_{i+1}(\theta)+\frac{x_{0}^{\theta}}{\theta-1}V_{i+1}(\theta)^{1-\theta},\qquad i=n-1,\dots,1.

Define the normalized values

U_{i}(\theta):=\frac{V_{i}(\theta)}{x_{0}}.

Then

U_{n}(\theta)=\frac{\theta}{\theta-1},\qquad U_{i}(\theta)=U_{i+1}(\theta)+\frac{1}{\theta-1}U_{i+1}(\theta)^{1-\theta},\qquad i=n-1,\dots,1.

Step 2: The prophet benchmark.
Let
M_{n}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}X_{j}
. Writing
X_{j}=x_{0}U_{j}^{-1/\theta}
with
U_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
, one gets

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)]=x_{0}\,\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1-1/\theta)}.

By the Gamma-ratio asymptotic,

\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1-1/\theta)}=n^{1/\theta}(1+o(1)),

so

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)]=x_{0}\,\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)n^{1/\theta}(1+o(1)).

Step 3: Recursion for the plug-in policy after exploration.
With
\varepsilon_{m}=\sqrt{4\log(2/\delta)/m}
, let

\mathcal{G}_{m}:=\left\{\left|\frac{\theta}{\widehat{\theta}_{m}}-1\right|\leq\varepsilon_{m}\right\},

which holds at least
1-\delta
probability from Lemma
2
. On the event
\mathcal{G}_{m}
, fix the realized value of
\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}
. The policy rejects the first
m
observations and then uses thresholds

t_{i}:=V_{i+1}(\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}),\qquad i=m+1,\dots,n-1,

and accepts at stage
n
. Let
L_{i}
be the expected reward of this plug-in policy starting from stage
i

\theta

L_{n}=\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=V_{n}(\theta).

For
i=n-1,\dots,m+1
, conditioning on whether

L_{i}=\Pr(X_{i}\geq t_{i})\,\mathbb{E}[X_{i}\mid X_{i}\geq t_{i}]+\Pr(X_{i}<t_{i})\,L_{i+1}.

Now

\Pr(X_{i}\geq t_{i})=\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t_{i}}\right)^{\theta},\qquad\mathbb{E}[X_{i}\mid X_{i}\geq t_{i}]=\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}t_{i}.

Therefore

L_{i}=\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t_{i}}\right)^{\theta}\frac{\theta t_{i}}{\theta-1}+\left(1-\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t_{i}}\right)^{\theta}\right)L_{i+1}.

Step 4: A comparison lemma.
We first prove a deterministic comparison bound.

Lemma 30
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Suppose that

t_{i}\geq\alpha\,V_{i+1}(\theta),\qquad i=m+1,\dots,n-1.

Then for every
i=m+1,\dots,n
,

L_{i}\geq\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)\,V_{i}(\theta),\qquad\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)=\frac{\theta\alpha}{\theta-1+\alpha^{\theta}}.

Proof.

We argue by backward induction on
i
. For
i=n
, we have
L_{n}=V_{n}(\theta)
. Since

\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)\leq 1\qquad(\alpha\in(0,1]),

it follows that

L_{n}=V_{n}(\theta)\geq\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{n}(\theta).

To verify
\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)\leq 1
, note that this is equivalent to

\theta\alpha\leq\theta-1+\alpha^{\theta}.

Define

h(\alpha):=\theta-1+\alpha^{\theta}-\theta\alpha.

Then
h(1)=0
, and

h^{\prime}(\alpha)=\theta\alpha^{\theta-1}-\theta=\theta(\alpha^{\theta-1}-1)\leq 0\quad\text{for }\alpha\in(0,1].

Hence
h(\alpha)\geq h(1)=0
. Now suppose that for some
i\in\{m+1,\dots,n-1\}
,

L_{i+1}\geq\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i+1}(\theta).

Write

t_{i}=\beta_{i}V_{i+1}(\theta)

with
\beta_{i}\geq\alpha
. Since
t_{i}=V_{i+1}(\theta^{(\mathrm{U})})
and
\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}\geq\theta
, one also has
\beta_{i}\leq 1
; indeed, if
\eta^{\prime}\geq\eta
, then under the coupling

X_{j}(\eta)=x_{0}U_{j}^{-1/\eta},\qquad U_{j}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1),

we have
X_{j}(\eta^{\prime})\leq X_{j}(\eta)
almost surely for every
j
. Therefore any stopping rule earns no more under
\eta^{\prime}
than under
\eta
, so

V_{i}(\eta^{\prime})\leq V_{i}(\eta)\qquad\text{for all }i.

Applying this with
\eta^{\prime}=\theta^{(\mathrm{U})}
and
\eta=\theta
yields
t_{i}\leq V_{i+1}(\theta)
, i.e.
\beta_{i}\leq 1
. Using the recursion for
L_{i}
and the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle L_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t_{i}}\right)^{\theta}\frac{\theta t_{i}}{\theta-1}+\left(1-\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t_{i}}\right)^{\theta}\right)L_{i+1}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t_{i}}\right)^{\theta}\frac{\theta t_{i}}{\theta-1}+\left(1-\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t_{i}}\right)^{\theta}\right)\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i+1}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i+1}(\theta)+\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t_{i}}\right)^{\theta}\left(\frac{\theta t_{i}}{\theta-1}-\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i+1}(\theta)\right).

Substituting
t_{i}=\beta_{i}V_{i+1}(\theta)

L_{i}\geq\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i+1}(\theta)+\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\beta_{i}V_{i+1}(\theta)}\right)^{\theta}\left(\frac{\theta\beta_{i}V_{i+1}(\theta)}{\theta-1}-\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i+1}(\theta)\right).

On the other hand, from the recursion for
V_{i}(\theta)
,

\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i}(\theta)=\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i+1}(\theta)+\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)\frac{x_{0}^{\theta}}{\theta-1}V_{i+1}(\theta)^{1-\theta}.

Thus it is enough to show

\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\beta_{i}V_{i+1}(\theta)}\right)^{\theta}\left(\frac{\theta\beta_{i}V_{i+1}(\theta)}{\theta-1}-\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i+1}(\theta)\right)\geq\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)\frac{x_{0}^{\theta}}{\theta-1}V_{i+1}(\theta)^{1-\theta}.

After cancelling the common factor
x_{0}^{\theta}V_{i+1}(\theta)^{1-\theta}
, this is equivalent to

\beta_{i}^{-\theta}\left(\frac{\theta\beta_{i}}{\theta-1}-\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)\right)\geq\frac{\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)}{\theta-1},

or, equivalently,

\theta\beta_{i}\geq\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)\bigl(\theta-1+\beta_{i}^{\theta}\bigr).

But

\xi_{\theta}(\beta)=\frac{\theta\beta}{\theta-1+\beta^{\theta}},

so the last inequality is exactly

\xi_{\theta}(\beta_{i})\geq\xi_{\theta}(\alpha).

This holds because
\beta_{i}\geq\alpha
and
\xi_{\theta}
is increasing on
(0,1]
. Indeed,

\xi_{\theta}^{\prime}(\beta)=\frac{\theta(\theta-1)(1-\beta^{\theta})}{(\theta-1+\beta^{\theta})^{2}}\geq 0\qquad\text{for }\beta\in(0,1].

Hence
L_{i}\geq\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)V_{i}(\theta)
, completing the induction. ∎

\alpha_{n}^{*}:=\min_{m+1\leq i\leq n}\frac{V_{i}(\theta^{(\mathrm{U})})}{V_{i}(\theta)}.

Since

Dividing by

Lemma 31

(Pareto DP value asymptotic)
.

Let

D(\beta):=\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}\right)^{1/\beta},\qquad\beta>1.

For Pareto rewards with shape parameter
\beta>1
, let
V_{i}(\beta)

be the optimal DP value when periods
i,i+1,\dots,n
remain. Then, with

r:=n-i+1
,

V_{i}(\beta)=x_{0}D(\beta)r^{1/\beta}\left(1+\frac{\beta-1}{2\beta^{2}}\frac{\log r}{r}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\right).

Equivalently,

V_{i}(\beta)=x_{0}D(\beta)(n-i+1)^{1/\beta}\left(1+\frac{\beta-1}{2\beta^{2}}\frac{\log(n-i+1)}{n-i+1}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n-i+1}\right)\right).

Proof.

Let
r=n-i+1
be the number of stages remaining from time
i
, and define

\widetilde{U}_{r}(\beta):=\frac{V_{n-r+1}(\beta)}{x_{0}}.

Then

\widetilde{U}_{1}(\beta)=\frac{\beta}{\beta-1},

and, for
r\geq 2
,

\widetilde{U}_{r}(\beta)=\widetilde{U}_{r-1}(\beta)+\frac{1}{\beta-1}\widetilde{U}_{r-1}(\beta)^{1-\beta}.

Set

Y_{r}(\beta):=\widetilde{U}_{r}(\beta)^{\beta}.

Then

Y_{r}(\beta)=Y_{r-1}(\beta)\left(1+\frac{1}{(\beta-1)Y_{r-1}(\beta)}\right)^{\beta}.

Let

a_{\beta}:=\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}.

Expanding the right-hand side gives

Y_{r}(\beta)-Y_{r-1}(\beta)=a_{\beta}+\frac{\beta}{2(\beta-1)}\frac{1}{Y_{r-1}(\beta)}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{Y_{r-1}(\beta)^{2}}\right).

Since the rough estimate

Y_{r}(\beta)=a_{\beta}r+O(\log r)

follows by the same upper and lower bounds as in the previous proof, we have

\sum_{s=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{Y_{s}(\beta)}=\frac{1}{a_{\beta}}\log r+O(1),\qquad\sum_{s=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{Y_{s}(\beta)^{2}}=O(1).

Therefore,

Y_{r}(\beta)=a_{\beta}r+\frac{\beta}{2(\beta-1)}\cdot\frac{1}{a_{\beta}}\log r+O(1).

Since
a_{\beta}=\beta/(\beta-1)
, this simplifies to

Y_{r}(\beta)=a_{\beta}r+\frac{1}{2}\log r+O(1).

Taking the
1/\beta
-th power,

\widetilde{U}_{r}(\beta)=\left(a_{\beta}r+\frac{1}{2}\log r+O(1)\right)^{1/\beta}.

Thus

\widetilde{U}_{r}(\beta)=a_{\beta}^{1/\beta}r^{1/\beta}\left(1+\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\log r+O(1)}{a_{\beta}r}+O\!\left(\frac{\log^{2}r}{r^{2}}\right)\right).

Using
a_{\beta}=\beta/(\beta-1)
, we obtain

\widetilde{U}_{r}(\beta)=D(\beta)r^{1/\beta}\left(1+\frac{\beta-1}{2\beta^{2}}\frac{\log r}{r}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\right).

Multiplying by
x_{m}
gives the desired expansion for
V_{i}(\beta)
.
∎

From Lemma
31
,

V_{i}(\beta)=x_{0}\,D(\beta)\,(n-i+1)^{1/\beta}(1+o(1)),

uniformly for
\beta>1
. Hence for
i>m
,

\frac{V_{i}((1+\eta_{m})\theta)}{V_{i}(\theta)}=\frac{D((1+\eta_{m})\theta)}{D(\theta)}(n-i+1)^{\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{m})\theta}-\frac{1}{\theta}}(1+o(1)).

Because
\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{m})\theta}-\frac{1}{\theta}<0
, the minimum is attained at the
largest remaining horizon, namely
i=m+1
. Therefore

\alpha_{n}^{\star}\geq\underline{\alpha}_{n}\,(1+o(1)),\qquad\underline{\alpha}_{n}:=\frac{D((1+\eta_{m})\theta)}{D(\theta)}(n-m)^{\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{m})\theta}-\frac{1}{\theta}}.

Now let

g(\beta):=\log D(\beta)=\frac{1}{\beta}\log\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}.

Then

g^{\prime}(\beta)=-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\beta-1}+\log\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}\right),

so that

-\theta g^{\prime}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\theta}\left(\frac{1}{\theta-1}+\log\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}\right)=B_{\theta}.

A first-order Taylor expansion gives

g((1+\eta_{m})\theta)-g(\theta)=\theta g^{\prime}(\theta)\eta_{m}+O(\eta_{m}^{2})=-B_{\theta}\eta_{m}+O(\eta_{m}^{2}).

Also,

\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{m})\theta}-\frac{1}{\theta}=-\frac{\eta_{m}}{(1+\eta_{m})\theta}=-\frac{\eta_{m}}{\theta}+O(\eta_{m}^{2}).

Therefore

\displaystyle\log\underline{\alpha}_{n}

\displaystyle=g((1+\eta_{m})\theta)-g(\theta)+\left(\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{m})\theta}-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\log(n-m)

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\log(n-m)}{\theta}+B_{\theta}\right)\eta_{m}+O(\eta_{m}^{2}\log n)

\displaystyle=-A_{\theta}(n,m)\eta_{m}+O(\eta_{m}^{2}\log n),

where
A_{\theta}(n,m):=\frac{\log(n-m)}{\theta}+B_{\theta}

Because
\eta_{m}\log(n-m)\to 0

1-\underline{\alpha}_{n}=A_{\theta}(n,m)\eta_{m}+o(\eta_{m}\log n).

Next we expand
\xi_{\theta}

\xi_{\theta}(\alpha)=\frac{\theta\alpha}{\theta-1+\alpha^{\theta}},

\xi_{\theta}(1)=1,\qquad\xi_{\theta}^{\prime}(1)=0,\qquad\xi_{\theta}^{\prime\prime}(1)=1-\theta.

Thus, for

On the other hand,

Using the Gamma-ratio expansion,

\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1-1/\theta)}=n^{1/\theta}\left(1+a_{\theta}\frac{1}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right),

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)]=x_{0}\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)n^{1/\theta}\left(1+a_{\theta}\frac{1}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{V_{m+1}(\theta)}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)]}

\displaystyle=\frac{D(\theta)}{\Gamma(1-1/\theta)}\left(\frac{N}{n}\right)^{1/\theta}\left(1+a_{\theta}\frac{\log N}{N}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\left(1-a_{\theta}\frac{1}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right).

Since

Suboptimality of Relative Rank-only
Rules for Fréchet tails

We first provide a proposition to show the suboptimality of relative rank-based
rules for Fréchet tails.

Proposition 32

(Relative rank-only rules are suboptimal for Fréchet tails)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. nonnegative continuous random variables whose
upper quantile function

U(t):=F^{-1}(1-1/t)

is regularly varying with index
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Let
\tau_{n}
be any
stopping rule based only on relative ranks. Then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau_{n}}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}\leq 1-\gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right),

where
X_{(n)}
is the largest order statistic.
Moreover,

1-\gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)<\frac{(1-\gamma)^{-\gamma}}{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}.

Consequently, no relative-rank-only rule can attain the full-information
Fréchet-specific limit

\rho_{\gamma}:=\frac{(1-\gamma)^{-\gamma}}{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}.

In the parametric family of this paper,
\gamma=c_{+}/\theta
, so

\rho_{\gamma}=\rho(c_{+},\theta)
.

Proof.

Let
A_{\tau_{n}}
denote the absolute rank selected by the rule, with

A_{\tau_{n}}=1
corresponding to selecting the maximum
X_{(n)}
. Since

\tau_{n}
uses only relative ranks, the classical secretary upper bound
[
6
]
gives

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{\tau_{n}}=1)\leq\frac{1}{e}.

On
\{A_{\tau_{n}}=1\}
, the selected value is
X_{(n)}
. On the complement,
the selected value is at most
X_{(n-1)}
. Since relative ranks are independent
of the order statistics,

\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau_{n}}]\leq p_{n}\,\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]+(1-p_{n})\,\mathbb{E}[X_{(n-1)}],\qquad p_{n}:=\mathbb{P}(A_{\tau_{n}}=1).

Therefore

\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau_{n}}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}\leq p_{n}+(1-p_{n})\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n-1)}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}.

It remains to identify the limiting ratio of the two largest order statistics.
Since
U
is regularly varying with index
\gamma\in(0,1)
, standard
Fréchet order-statistic convergence gives

\left(\frac{X_{(n)}}{U(n)},\frac{X_{(n-1)}}{U(n)}\right)\Rightarrow\left(\Gamma_{1}^{-\gamma},\Gamma_{2}^{-\gamma}\right),

where
\Gamma_{j}=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{j}
and
E_{1},E_{2},\dots
are i.i.d.
\mathrm{Exp}(1)
. Since
\gamma<1
, the corresponding first moments
converge, and hence

\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}{U(n)}\to\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{1}^{-\gamma}]=\Gamma(1-\gamma),

while

\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n-1)}]}{U(n)}\to\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{2}^{-\gamma}]=\Gamma(2-\gamma).

Thus

\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n-1)}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}\to\frac{\Gamma(2-\gamma)}{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}=1-\gamma.

Combining this with the secretary upper bound yields

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau_{n}}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}\leq\frac{1}{e}+\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)(1-\gamma)=1-\gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right).

Finally, the full-information Fréchet-specific limit in our parametric
family is

\rho_{\gamma}=\frac{(1-\gamma)^{-\gamma}}{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}.

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
, one has

\rho_{\gamma}>1-\gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right).

Indeed, using the expansion

\log\Gamma(1-\gamma)=\gamma_{E}\gamma+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(k)}{k}\gamma^{k},

where
\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}\approx 0.57721
denotes the
Euler–Mascheroni constant,
we get

\log\rho_{\gamma}=-\gamma_{E}\gamma+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\frac{\zeta(k)}{k}\right)\gamma^{k}>-\gamma_{E}\gamma,

because
\zeta(k)<k/(k-1)
for
k\geq 2
. Hence

\rho_{\gamma}>e^{-\gamma_{E}\gamma}\geq 1-\gamma_{E}\gamma>1-\gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right),

since
\gamma_{E}<1-1/e
. This proves the strict separation.
∎

Although the preceding results already imply the suboptimality of rank-based rules for Pareto tails, we include the
following proposition for completeness, showing that relative-rank-only rules
are suboptimal for Pareto tails.

Proposition 33

(Relative rank-only rules are suboptimal for Pareto tails)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. Pareto random variables with scale
x_{0}>0

and shape parameter
\theta>1
:

F_{\theta}(x)=1-\left(\frac{x_{0}}{x}\right)^{\theta},\qquad x\geq x_{0}.

Let
\tau_{n}
be any stopping rule based only on relative ranks. Then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau_{n}}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}\leq 1-\frac{1}{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right).

Moreover,

1-\frac{1}{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)<\frac{\left(\theta/(\theta-1)\right)^{1/\theta}}{\Gamma(1-1/\theta)}=\rho(1,\theta).

Consequently, no relative-rank-only rule can attain the full-information
Pareto-specific asymptotic competitive ratio
\rho(1,\theta)
.

Proof.

Let
A_{\tau_{n}}
denote the absolute rank of the selected observation, with

A_{\tau_{n}}=1
corresponding to selecting the maximum
X_{(n)}
. Since

\tau_{n}
is based only on relative ranks, the classical secretary upper bound
implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{\tau_{n}}=1)\leq\frac{1}{e}.

On the event
\{A_{\tau_{n}}=1\}
, the selected value is
X_{(n)}
. On the
complement, the selected value is at most
X_{(n-1)}
. Hence, using the
independence between the order statistics and the rank vector,

\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau_{n}}]\leq\mathbb{P}(A_{\tau_{n}}=1)\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]+\bigl(1-\mathbb{P}(A_{\tau_{n}}=1)\bigr)\mathbb{E}[X_{(n-1)}].

Dividing by
\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]
gives

\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau_{n}}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}\leq p_{n}+(1-p_{n})\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n-1)}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]},\qquad p_{n}:=\mathbb{P}(A_{\tau_{n}}=1).

It remains to compute the order-statistic ratio. Let

\gamma:=1/\theta\in(0,1)
. If
U\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
, then

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}x_{0}U^{-\gamma}
. Therefore, if

U_{(1)}\leq U_{(2)}\leq\cdots\leq U_{(n)}
are the uniform order statistics,

X_{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}x_{0}U_{(1)}^{-\gamma},\qquad X_{(n-1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}x_{0}U_{(2)}^{-\gamma}.

Using the densities of
U_{(1)}
and
U_{(2)}
,

\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]=x_{0}n\int_{0}^{1}u^{-\gamma}(1-u)^{n-1}\,du=x_{0}\frac{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(1-\gamma)}{\Gamma(n+1-\gamma)},

and

\mathbb{E}[X_{(n-1)}]=x_{0}n(n-1)\int_{0}^{1}u^{1-\gamma}(1-u)^{n-2}\,du=x_{0}\frac{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(2-\gamma)}{\Gamma(n+1-\gamma)}.

Thus

\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n-1)}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}=\frac{\Gamma(2-\gamma)}{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}=1-\gamma=1-\frac{1}{\theta}.

Combining this identity with the secretary upper bound yields

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau_{n}}]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}]}\leq\frac{1}{e}+\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)=1-\frac{1}{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right).

Finally, the full-information Pareto-specific limit is

\rho(1,\theta)=\frac{\left(\theta/(\theta-1)\right)^{1/\theta}}{\Gamma(1-1/\theta)}=\frac{(1-\gamma)^{-\gamma}}{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}.

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
, this quantity is strictly larger than

1-\gamma(1-1/e)
. Hence the rank-only upper bound is strictly below

\rho(1,\theta)
, proving the claim.
∎

A.14
Full-information Convergence Rate for Pareto Rewards

Proposition 34

(Full-information convergence rate for Pareto rewards)
.

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Pareto}(\theta)
.
Let
\tau^{*}
be the optimal full-information stopping rule. Then

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau^{*};\theta)=\rho(1,\theta)\left(1+\Theta\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\right).

Proof.

Let
V_{i}(\theta)
denote the optimal DP value when periods

i,i+1,\dots,n
remain. Then
V_{n+1}(\theta)=0
, and

V_{i}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[\max\{X_{i},V_{i+1}(\theta)\}],\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

Since the final observation must be accepted,

V_{n}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]=\frac{\theta x_{0}}{\theta-1}.

For
a\geq x_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}[(X_{1}-a)^{+}]=\int_{a}^{\infty}\Pr(X_{1}>t)\,dt=\int_{a}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t}\right)^{\theta}dt=\frac{x_{0}^{\theta}}{\theta-1}a^{1-\theta}.

Therefore,

V_{i}(\theta)=V_{i+1}(\theta)+\frac{x_{0}^{\theta}}{\theta-1}V_{i+1}(\theta)^{1-\theta},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1.

It is convenient to re-index by the number of remaining periods. Define

\widetilde{U}_{r}:=\frac{V_{n-r+1}(\theta)}{x_{0}},\qquad r=1,\dots,n.

Then

\widetilde{U}_{1}=\frac{\theta}{\theta-1},

and for
r\geq 2
,

\widetilde{U}_{r}=\widetilde{U}_{r-1}+\frac{1}{\theta-1}\widetilde{U}_{r-1}^{1-\theta}.

Set

Y_{r}:=\widetilde{U}_{r}^{\theta}.

Then

Y_{r}=Y_{r-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{(\theta-1)Y_{r-1}}\right)^{\theta}.

Let

a_{\theta}:=\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}.

Using
(1+u)^{\theta}\geq 1+\theta u
for
u\geq 0
, we get

Y_{r}-Y_{r-1}\geq a_{\theta}.

Hence

Y_{r}\geq Y_{1}+a_{\theta}(r-1)\geq c_{\theta}r

for some constant
c_{\theta}>0
.

For the upper bound, since
Y_{r}\to\infty
, Taylor’s theorem gives, for all large
r
,

(1+u)^{\theta}\leq 1+\theta u+C_{\theta}u^{2}

with
u=1/((\theta-1)Y_{r-1})
. Therefore

Y_{r}-Y_{r-1}\leq a_{\theta}+\frac{C_{\theta}}{Y_{r-1}}.

Using the lower bound
Y_{r-1}\geq c_{\theta}(r-1)
, we obtain

Y_{r}-Y_{r-1}\leq a_{\theta}+\frac{C_{\theta}}{r}.

Summing over
r
, this yields

Y_{r}=a_{\theta}r+O(\log r).

Consequently,

\widetilde{U}_{r}=Y_{r}^{1/\theta}=\left(a_{\theta}r+O(\log r)\right)^{1/\theta}.

Thus

\widetilde{U}_{r}=a_{\theta}^{1/\theta}r^{1/\theta}\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{\log r}{r}\right)\right).

Taking
r=n
, we get

V_{1}(\theta)=x_{0}\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}\right)^{1/\theta}n^{1/\theta}\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\right).

Since
V_{1}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau^{*}}]
, this gives the full-information DP value.

We now compute the prophet benchmark. Let
M_{n}=\max_{i\in[n]}X_{i}
. Write

X_{i}=x_{0}U_{i}^{-1/\theta},\qquad U_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1).

Then

M_{n}=x_{0}U_{(1)}^{-1/\theta},

where
U_{(1)}=\min_{i\in[n]}U_{i}
. Since
U_{(1)}
has density

n(1-u)^{n-1},\qquad u\in[0,1],

we obtain

\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]=x_{0}n\int_{0}^{1}u^{-1/\theta}(1-u)^{n-1}\,du.

Using the beta function,

\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]=x_{0}nB\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta},n\right)=x_{0}\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1-1/\theta)}.

By the standard Gamma-ratio expansion,

\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1-1/\theta)}=n^{1/\theta}\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right).

Therefore

\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]=x_{0}\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)n^{1/\theta}\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right).

Combining the asymptotic expansion for
V_{1}(\theta)
with the expansion for

\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]
, we obtain

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau^{*};\theta)=\frac{V_{1}(\theta)}{\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]}=\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}\right)^{1/\theta}}{\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)}\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\right).

This proves the claim.
∎

\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{b_{k}}=\frac{\eta+1}{\eta}\log N+O_{\eta}(1),

and

\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{b_{k}^{2}}=O_{\eta}(1).

Summing the increment formula gives

b_{N}=\frac{\eta}{\eta+1}N+\frac{\eta}{2(\eta+1)}\cdot\frac{\eta+1}{\eta}\log N+O_{\eta}(1).

Thus

b_{N}=\frac{\eta}{\eta+1}N+\frac{1}{2}\log N+O_{\eta}(1).

Since
a_{N}=b_{N}^{-1/\eta}
, we get

a_{N}=\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta+1}N+\frac{1}{2}\log N+O_{\eta}(1)\right)^{-1/\eta}.

Therefore

a_{N}=\left(\frac{\eta+1}{\eta N}\right)^{1/\eta}\left(1-\frac{\eta+1}{2\eta^{2}}\frac{\log N}{N}+O_{\eta}\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right).

Finally,

x_{F}-V_{1}(\eta;N)=Da_{N},

which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 37

For every
\theta>0

\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)=x_{F}-D\,\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+1/\theta)}.

\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)=x_{F}-D\,\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\right)n^{-1/\theta}\left(1-\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta^{2}}\frac{1}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right).

Proof.

Define

U_{i}:=\left(\frac{x_{F}-X_{i}}{D}\right)^{\theta}.

Then for
u\in[0,1]
,

\Pr(U_{i}\leq u)=\Pr\!\left(X_{i}\geq x_{F}-Du^{1/\theta}\right)=1-F_{\theta}(x_{F}-Du^{1/\theta})=u.

Hence
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
are i.i.d.
\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
. If

U_{(1)}:=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}U_{i},

then

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}=x_{F}-D\,U_{(1)}^{1/\theta}.

Since
U_{(1)}
has density
n(1-u)^{n-1}
on
[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{(1)}^{1/\theta}]

\displaystyle=n\int_{0}^{1}u^{1/\theta}(1-u)^{n-1}\,du

\displaystyle=n\,B\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta},n\right)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+1/\theta)}.

Therefore

\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)=x_{F}-D\,\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+1/\theta)}.

The asymptotic formula follows from the standard Gamma-ratio expansion

\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+1/\theta)}=n^{-1/\theta}\left(1-\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta^{2}}\frac{1}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right).

∎

By Lemma
35
,

\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}]\geq(1-\delta)V_{1}(\theta_{+};N).

Hence

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau;\theta)\geq(1-\delta)\frac{V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)}{\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)}.

Recall

N:=n-m,\qquad\varepsilon_{m}:=\sqrt{\frac{4\log(2/\delta)}{m}},\qquad\theta_{+}:=\theta\frac{1+\varepsilon_{m}}{1-\varepsilon_{m}}.

Since

m=\omega(\log(1/\delta)\log^{2}n),

we have

\varepsilon_{m}\log n\to 0.

Also
m=o(n)
, and therefore
\theta_{+}\to\theta
. Thus, for all sufficiently
large
n
,
\theta_{+}\in K:=[\theta/2,2\theta]
.

Define

C(\eta):=\left(\frac{\eta+1}{\eta}\right)^{1/\eta},\qquad G_{\theta}:=\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\right).

By Lemma
36
,

V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)=x_{F}-DC(\theta_{+})N^{-1/\theta_{+}}\left(1-\frac{\theta_{+}+1}{2\theta_{+}^{2}}\frac{\log N}{N}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right).

Since
\theta_{+}=\theta+O(\varepsilon_{m})
, smoothness of
C
gives

C(\theta_{+})=C(\theta)+O(\varepsilon_{m}).

Moreover,

\frac{1}{\theta_{+}}=\frac{1-\varepsilon_{m}}{\theta(1+\varepsilon_{m})}=\frac{1}{\theta}\left(1-2\varepsilon_{m}+O(\varepsilon_{m}^{2})\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\log\!\left(\frac{N^{-1/\theta_{+}}}{n^{-1/\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{\theta_{+}}\right)\log n-\frac{1}{\theta_{+}}\log\!\left(1-\frac{m}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=O(\varepsilon_{m}\log n)+O(m/n).

Since both terms are
o(1)
,

N^{-1/\theta_{+}}=n^{-1/\theta}\left(1+O\!\left(\varepsilon_{m}\log n+\frac{m}{n}\right)\right).

Also,

\frac{\log N}{N}=O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right).

Combining these estimates,

V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)=x_{F}-DC(\theta)n^{-1/\theta}\left(1+O\!\left(\varepsilon_{m}\log n+\frac{m}{n}\right)\right)+O\!\left(D\frac{\log n}{n^{1+1/\theta}}\right).

Equivalently,

V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)=x_{F}-DC(\theta)n^{-1/\theta}+O\!\left(Dn^{-1/\theta}\left(\varepsilon_{m}\log n+\frac{m}{n}\right)+D\frac{\log n}{n^{1+1/\theta}}\right).

On the other hand, by Lemma
37
,

\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)=x_{F}-DG_{\theta}n^{-1/\theta}+O\!\left(Dn^{-1-1/\theta}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)}{\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{F}-DC(\theta)n^{-1/\theta}+O\!\left(Dn^{-1/\theta}\left(\varepsilon_{m}\log n+\frac{m}{n}\right)+D\frac{\log n}{n^{1+1/\theta}}\right)}{x_{F}-DG_{\theta}n^{-1/\theta}+O(Dn^{-1-1/\theta})}.

Since
x_{F}>0
,

\frac{1}{\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)}=\frac{1}{x_{F}}\left(1+\frac{DG_{\theta}}{x_{F}}n^{-1/\theta}+O(n^{-2/\theta}+n^{-1-1/\theta})\right).

Multiplying the expansions gives

\displaystyle\frac{V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)}{\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{D}{x_{F}}\bigl(C(\theta)-G_{\theta}\bigr)n^{-1/\theta}

\displaystyle\quad-O\!\left(n^{-1/\theta}\left(\varepsilon_{m}\log n+\frac{m}{n}\right)+n^{-2/\theta}+\frac{\log n}{n^{1+1/\theta}}\right).

Define

\kappa_{\theta}:=\frac{D}{x_{F}}\left[C(\theta)-G_{\theta}\right]=\frac{D}{x_{F}}\left[\left(\frac{\theta+1}{\theta}\right)^{1/\theta}-\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\right].

Since
C(\theta)>\Gamma(1+1/\theta)
, we have
\kappa_{\theta}>0
. Hence

\frac{V_{1}(\theta_{+};N)}{\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)}\geq 1-\kappa_{\theta}n^{-1/\theta}-O\!\left(n^{-1/\theta}\left(\varepsilon_{m}\log n+\frac{m}{n}\right)+n^{-2/\theta}+\frac{\log n}{n^{1+1/\theta}}\right).

Multiplying by
1-\delta
, we obtain

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau;\theta)\geq(1-\delta)\left[1-\kappa_{\theta}n^{-1/\theta}-O\!\left(n^{-1/\theta}\left(\varepsilon_{m}\log n+\frac{m}{n}\right)+n^{-2/\theta}+\frac{\log n}{n^{1+1/\theta}}\right)\right].

Finally, because

\varepsilon_{m}\log n\to 0,\qquad\frac{m}{n}\to 0,

this simplifies to

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau;\theta)\geq(1-\delta)\left(1-\kappa_{\theta}n^{-1/\theta}-O(n^{-1/\theta})\right),

which implies

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau;\theta)\geq(1-\delta)\left(1-O(n^{-1/\theta})\right).

This proves the theorem.

Proof of Proposition
17

Let
V_{i}(\theta;n)
denote the optimal DP value when periods

i,i+1,\dots,n
remain. For readability, we suppress the horizon
n

and write
V_{i}(\theta)
.
Let

D:=x_{F}-x_{0}.
The optimal full-information rule is the usual
threshold rule, and

V_{i}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\max\{X_{i},V_{i+1}(\theta)\}],\qquad V_{n+1}(\theta)=0.

At the last stage,

V_{n}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[X_{1}]=x_{0}+\frac{D}{\theta+1}=x_{F}-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}D.

For
a\in[x_{0},x_{F}]
,

r_{\theta}(a):=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[(X_{1}-a)^{+}]=\int_{a}^{x_{F}}\Pr_{\theta}(X_{1}>t)\,dt.

Since

\Pr_{\theta}(X_{1}>t)=\left(\frac{x_{F}-t}{D}\right)^{\theta},

we get

r_{\theta}(a)=\int_{a}^{x_{F}}\left(\frac{x_{F}-t}{D}\right)^{\theta}dt=\frac{(x_{F}-a)^{\theta+1}}{(\theta+1)D^{\theta}}.

Therefore the DP recursion is

V_{i}(\theta)=V_{i+1}(\theta)+\frac{(x_{F}-V_{i+1}(\theta))^{\theta+1}}{(\theta+1)D^{\theta}},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1.

Define the normalized endpoint gap

S_{i}:=\frac{x_{F}-V_{i}(\theta)}{D}.

Then

S_{n}=\frac{\theta}{\theta+1},

and

S_{i}=S_{i+1}-\frac{1}{\theta+1}S_{i+1}^{\theta+1}.

It is more convenient to index by the number of remaining periods. Set

a_{k}:=S_{n-k+1},\qquad k=1,\dots,n.

Then

a_{1}=\frac{\theta}{\theta+1},\qquad a_{k+1}=a_{k}-\frac{1}{\theta+1}a_{k}^{\theta+1},\qquad k=1,\dots,n-1.

Now define

b_{k}:=a_{k}^{-\theta}.

Since

a_{k+1}=a_{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta+1}a_{k}^{\theta}\right)=a_{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\theta+1)b_{k}}\right),

we have

b_{k+1}=b_{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\theta+1)b_{k}}\right)^{-\theta}.

Thus

b_{k+1}-b_{k}=b_{k}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{(\theta+1)b_{k}}\right)^{-\theta}-1\right].

We first record a rough estimate. Since

(1-z)^{-\theta}=1+\theta z+O(z^{2})

for small
z
, and since
b_{k}
is increasing, the preceding display implies

b_{k+1}-b_{k}=\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{b_{k}}\right).

This gives

b_{k}=\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}k+O(\log k),

and in particular
b_{k}\asymp_{\theta}k
.

We now refine the expansion. Taylor’s theorem gives

(1-z)^{-\theta}=1+\theta z+\frac{\theta(\theta+1)}{2}z^{2}+O(z^{3}).

Applying this with

z=\frac{1}{(\theta+1)b_{k}},

we obtain

b_{k+1}-b_{k}=\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}+\frac{\theta}{2(\theta+1)}\frac{1}{b_{k}}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{b_{k}^{2}}\right).

Using
b_{k}=\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}k+O(\log k)
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{b_{k}}=\frac{\theta+1}{\theta}\log n+O(1),

and

\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{b_{k}^{2}}=O(1).

Summing the increment formula yields

b_{n}=\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}n+\frac{\theta}{2(\theta+1)}\cdot\frac{\theta+1}{\theta}\log n+O(1).

Hence

b_{n}=\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}n+\frac{1}{2}\log n+O(1).

Since
a_{n}=b_{n}^{-1/\theta}
, we get

a_{n}=\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}n+\frac{1}{2}\log n+O(1)\right)^{-1/\theta}.

Therefore

a_{n}=\left(\frac{\theta+1}{\theta n}\right)^{1/\theta}\left(1-\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta^{2}}\frac{\log n}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right).

Since

x_{F}-V_{1}(\theta)=Da_{n},

we conclude that

V_{1}(\theta)=x_{F}-D\left(\frac{\theta+1}{\theta n}\right)^{1/\theta}\left(1-\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta^{2}}\frac{\log n}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right).

Equivalently, with

C_{\theta}:=\left(\frac{\theta+1}{\theta}\right)^{1/\theta},

we have

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[X_{\tau^{*}}]=V_{1}(\theta)=x_{F}-DC_{\theta}n^{-1/\theta}\left(1-\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta^{2}}\frac{\log n}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right).

We now compute the prophet benchmark. Define

U_{i}:=\left(\frac{x_{F}-X_{i}}{D}\right)^{\theta}.

Then
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
are i.i.d.
\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)
. If

U_{(1)}:=\min_{i\in[n]}U_{i},

then

\max_{i\in[n]}X_{i}=x_{F}-DU_{(1)}^{1/\theta}.

The density of
U_{(1)}
is

n(1-u)^{n-1},\qquad u\in[0,1].

Thus

\mathbb{E}[U_{(1)}^{1/\theta}]=n\int_{0}^{1}u^{1/\theta}(1-u)^{n-1}\,du=nB\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta},n\right).

Using the beta-gamma identity,

nB\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta},n\right)=\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+1/\theta)}.

Therefore

\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)=x_{F}-D\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+1/\theta)}.

The standard Gamma-ratio expansion gives

\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+1/\theta)}=n^{-1/\theta}\left(1-\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta^{2}}\frac{1}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right).

Hence, defining

G_{\theta}:=\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\right),

we obtain

\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)=x_{F}-DG_{\theta}n^{-1/\theta}\left(1-\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta^{2}}\frac{1}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right).

Combining the two expansions, we write

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[X_{\tau^{*}}]=x_{F}-DC_{\theta}n^{-1/\theta}+O\!\left(D\frac{\log n}{n^{1+1/\theta}}\right),

and

\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)=x_{F}-DG_{\theta}n^{-1/\theta}+O\!\left(Dn^{-1-1/\theta}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau^{*};\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[X_{\tau^{*}}]}{\mathrm{OPT}_{n}(\theta)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{D}{x_{F}}\left(C_{\theta}-G_{\theta}\right)n^{-1/\theta}+O\!\left(n^{-2/\theta}+\frac{\log n}{n^{1+1/\theta}}\right).

Finally,
C_{\theta}-G_{\theta}>0
. Equivalently, with

\alpha=1/\theta>0
,

C_{\theta}=(1+\alpha)^{\alpha},\qquad G_{\theta}=\Gamma(1+\alpha),

and the standard inequality

\Gamma(1+\alpha)<(1+\alpha)^{\alpha},\qquad\alpha>0,

implies
C_{\theta}>G_{\theta}
. Hence

\mathrm{CR}_{n}(\tau^{*};\theta)=1-\Theta\!\left(n^{-1/\theta}\right).

This proves the lemma.

Additional Experimental Details

We provide additional details for the experiments in Figure
1
.
All experiments are synthetic i.i.d. simulations. They were run on lightweight CPUs without GPU acceleration. For each horizon
n
, we
generate a reward sequence
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
, run all algorithms on the same
sequence, and compare the selected reward with the realized prophet reward

\max_{i\in[n]}X_{i}
.

Distributions.

We consider three canonical families. The exponential experiment uses

F_{\theta}(x)=1-\exp(-\theta x),\qquad x\geq 0,

where
\theta
is sampled independently from
[0.25,1.25]
in each trial.
The Pareto experiment uses

F_{\theta}(x)=1-\left(\frac{x_{0}}{x}\right)^{\theta},\qquad x\geq x_{0},

with
x_{0}=1
and
\theta=2
. We fix
\theta
in
this case because the optimal asymptotic competitive ratio depends on

\theta
, so this choice keeps the theoretical benchmark fixed across trials. The bounded-support experiment uses

F_{\theta}(x)=1-\left(\frac{x_{F}-x}{x_{F}-x_{0}}\right)^{\theta},\qquad x\in[x_{0},x_{F}],

with
x_{0}=1
,
x_{F}=2
, and
\theta=1
, corresponding to the uniform
distribution on
[1,2]
. We use this instance as a representative
bounded-support setting where the theoretical benchmarks, including the

GZ-RANK
guarantee, are directly comparable.

Algorithms and parameters.

We compare Algorithm
1
with three baselines: the
Gusein–Zade secretary-type rule, the online Samples-CFHOV baseline, and the
rank-based Goldenshluger–Zeevi rule. The DP plug-in policy uses

\delta=0.05
, and both the DP plug-in policy and the Samples-CFHOV baseline
use the exploration lengths prescribed by the corresponding theoretical
bounds. For Samples-CFHOV, we set
\eta=0.9
and
C=10
; when its required
exploration length exceeds the horizon
n
, the baseline is not run for that
horizon.

Following
the practical tuning suggested by
7
,
we set
k=\lceil 3\log\log n\rceil
for the exponential experiment, since the
exponential distribution belongs to their Gumbel subclass with
\beta=1
.
For the bounded-support uniform experiment, which corresponds to the
reverse-Weibull subclass with
\alpha=1
, we set
k=\lceil 3\log n\rceil
.
For the Pareto experiment, the distribution lies in the Fréchet domain, where

7
show that first-order asymptotic optimality is
not achievable by any stopping rule. Thus there is no theoretically prescribed
rank-based tuning for this case; we nevertheless include the same rank-based
implementation as a heuristic benchmark, using
k=\lceil 3\log\log n\rceil
.

Horizons and repetitions.

We use

n\in\{100,300,1000,3000,10000,30000,100000\}.

For each horizon, we run independent trials with seeds
0,1,\ldots,R-1
. We
use
R=500
repetitions for the exponential and bounded-support experiments,
and
R=10000
repetitions for the Pareto experiment because the heavy-tailed
rewards lead to higher variance.

Reported metric.

For trial
s
, let
\tau_{s}
be the stopping time and

M_{s}=\max_{i\in[n]}X_{i}^{(s)}
. We report the aggregate empirical competitive
ratio

\widehat{\mathrm{CR}}_{n}=\frac{\sum_{s=1}^{R}X_{\tau_{s}}^{(s)}}{\sum_{s=1}^{R}M_{s}},

which estimates
\mathbb{E}[X_{\tau}]/\mathbb{E}[\max_{i\in[n]}X_{i}]
.
