1 System Scope Summary
The Neutron Time-of-Flight (nTOF) detectors located in the Alcove and at the North Pole (Spec A and Spec NP
respectively) need filtering in the beam path to enable them to take data at NIF yields above 5MJ. The filtering
consists of 12cm of filter material divided into four, 3cm thick “pucks.” The pucks are 7cm in diameter. Each puck
is mounted into a rotatable stage, also called a Filter Flipper, that can bring it into and out of the beam path, see
Figure 1-1. These stages are bolted onto a plate that connects the filter stages to the mounting hardware used to
position them in the NIF Target Bay. The top-level filter mount assemblies will be permanently mounted in the
facility; removal is possible if needed for NIF maintenance, but the design intent is for the assemblies to remain
in their locations after alignment is completed.

 Figure 1-1 – Filter puck rotation stages called “Filter Flippers” (1015179097-AA)

The Spec A Filter Mount Assembly (1015104616-AA) will be fielded on Line of Sight (LOS) 116-316, mounted
onto the handrail (AAA11-116442-AA) of the permanent platform (AAA11-108373-AA) in the area, see Figure 1-2
and Figure 1-3. This places the filtering 8.15 meters from Target Chamber Center (TCC). The analysis of the Spec
A Filter Mount Assembly within this document covers everything from the Filter Pucks up to, and including, the
handrail of the platform. Analysis of the load path beyond the handrail was not performed and is not covered in
this document.
The Spec NP Filter Mount Assembly (1015201856-AA) will be hung above the North Pole collimator (AAA15-
100600-AA) at LOS 018-303. The mount will be secured by bolts through holes drilled in the structural steel I-
beam above the Target Chamber at Target Bay Level 4, see Figure 1-4 and Figure 1-5. This places the filtering 9.21
meters from TCC. The analysis of the Spec NP Filter Mount Assembly within this document covers everything
from the Filter Pucks up to the structural I-beam. Analysis of the load path beyond the bolts in the I-beam was
not performed and is not covered in this document.

The Filter Flippers can be locked in two positions: inserted in LOS of the nTOF detector or removed from the LOS.
To move between the positions, a spring-loaded plunger is lifted and the Filter Flipper is rotated either in or out
of the LOS until the plunger catches the corresponding locking recess on Filter Flipper Support Arm
(1015178392-AA). Figure 1-6 shows the two locked positions of the Filter Flipper. The spring-loaded plunger will
then snap into a retaining hole to lock the Flipper in the position.
The full Spec A Filter Mount Assembly, which can be seen in Figure 1-2, weighs 10.3kg, and the Spec NP Mount
Assembly, seen in Figure 1-4, weighs 13.0kg. This includes the filter pucks, which are pure silver, each of which
weigh 1.2kg. It is possible, however, that tungsten could be used as a filter material, which would weigh 2.2kg
per puck, bringing the weight of the Spec A and NP Filter Mount Assemblies to 14.3kg and 17.0kg, respectively.
One thing to note is that the Filter Flippers are different colors in the figures of this document, as can be seen in
Figure 1-6. This is only to differentiate the two parts in the model, as the front and rear Flipper bodies are mirror
images of each other. All other aspects of their design are the same.

Figure 1-6 – Filter Flipper Assembly showing inserted and removed positions of Filter Subassemblies. The front
 filter is in the inserted position and the rear filter is in the removed position.

1.1 Hazards and Failure Modes
The HY nTOF project FMEA (1015593074-AA [1]), covers hazards and proposes mitigations that were included in
the design of the filter assemblies. The hazards listed in the FMEA that are mitigated by the analysis presented in
this Safety Note are those involving structural failures of the assembly due to static and/or seismic (rare event)
loading. Structural failures for the Spec A and NP filter assemblies due to static or seismic loading are evaluated
in Section 2 and Appendices A, B, and B.1. The assemblies are mounted in an elevated location, meaning that a
significant structural failure could result in components falling onto equipment or personnel in the area. Other
effects of structural failure range from pinch/crush hazards to the extremities (e.g. if the Flippers fail to lock into
position) to missing data on a high yield shot (e.g. if the Flippers are not in position).

                                     LSEO_2025 – 1015535645-AA                                       Page 9 of 105

The Spec A and Spec NP filter assemblies will be analyzed in Nominal Operation and Rare Event-Seismic loading
conditions to ensure personnel and machine safety. The main concerns for assemblies 1015104616 and
1015201856 are the bolted joints coming apart and causing equipment to fall. Seismic loading is warranted
because these assemblies will be permanently installed and their elevated mounting location could present a
personnel hazard during a seismic event should they fail structurally.
The following analysis describes the load path of the Spec A and Spec NP filter assemblies and demonstrates
their adherence to DSS safety standards. The filter assemblies for the three remaining nTOF LOS (Spec E, PL, and
SP) will be covered in a separate ESN (1015465210)[2].

2 Engineering Analysis
During nominal operation, Filter Holder assemblies see loads from component weights. For rare event seismic
loading, component weights were analyzed under seismic accelerations. Threaded fasteners were analyzed with
guidance from the LLNL Engineering Design Safety Standards (DSS) Section 1.2: Threaded Fasteners[3]. Seismic
accelerations were taken from the NIF In-Structure Spectra for the 50.6ft level, node 6490 (References [4] and
[5]). This is where the Spec NP Filter Assembly (1015201856) will be mounted and was more conservative than
the Spec A mounting location due to its higher seismic loading factor. Appropriate safety factors for components
were taken from DSS Section 1.1: Safety Factors[6], and all components satisfy the required safety factors
therein. Initial analysis was performed for the seismic loading case before analyzing for nominal operation. If the
safety factors of a load path under seismic loading were larger than the safety factors required for nominal
operation, then analysis of nominal operation safety factors was not performed for that load path. This is
acceptable because the seismic loading cases encompass the nominal operation loading cases.
The Filter Flipper Assemblies (1015179097-AA) are variable between two positions (“Inserted” and “Removed”,
see Figure 1-6), with positive locking at both positions. The analysis for each load path was conducted at the
worst-case position for the filters which was with the filters in the “Removed” position, since it lifts them further
from the mounting plane, creating a larger moment on the bolted joints and plates. As stated above, both silver
and tungsten were considered as filter material, so tungsten was used in the calculations of this MESN to provide
a “worst-case” analysis due to the significant difference in weight between an equal volume of the two materials.
The mount components of the Filter Holder assemblies have limited rotation and translation capabilities over
some of the load path connections to allow for proper installation and alignment. The analysis for these
components was conducted in a neutral orientation, as opposed to at an extreme; see an example of this in
Figure 2-1 below. This slightly lowered some of worst-case forces on some of the bolts, but not enough to affect
the safety factors significantly. Moving from a neutral orientation to the maximum deflection shifts the center of
gravity by a maximum of 21.0mm in Spec A and 12.5mm in Spec NP in the worst loading cases. By engineering
judgment, this has minimal effect on the safety factors, so a neutral orientation is acceptable for analysis. Item
6.1 in Appendix A is a sample calculation demonstrating the magnitude of the shift in safety factor between a
neutral and extreme mounting position. It is important to note that, while the fixtures have the ability to move
when not installed, they are unlikely to move after installation – even during a seismic event – if all the bolts are
torqued properly, see “slip” calculations in Appendix A Items 5 and 9.

2.1.1 Filter Puck C-clip Retaining Ring
The first part of the load path, starting at the filters (1015148545-AA) is the C-clip Retaining Ring (1015535034-
AA) that holds the Filter Pucks (1015148545-AA) inside the Filter Flipper body (1015040943-AA and 1015526369-
AA). It is snap-fit into a groove on the inside of the Filter Flipper, see Figure 2-3. The clip, made of steel, and the
Filter Flipper Body lip that holds the clip in place, made of Al 6061-T6, need to resist the forces of the Filter Puck
trying to push itself out under seismic acceleration.

2.1.2 Filter Flipper to Filter Flipper Support Arm Assembly
The loads from the Filter Pucks and the Filter Flipper are then transferred to the Filter Flipper Support Arm
Assembly (1015178392). The Flipper is captured in the Support Arm by a 10.9 steel shoulder bolt (1015509215)
and an 18-8 Spring Plunger (1015534960), see Figure 2-4. The shoulder bolt allows the Filter Flipper to pivot on
the Support Arm, and takes any shear loading that acts through the plane of the Support Arm. The Spring
Plunger allows the Filter Flipper to be locked into two positions: swung up, out of the nTOF LOS (“removed”) or
down, into the LOS (“inserted”). This puts a shear on the plunger end that needs to counteract seismic
accelerations on the Filter Flipper.

2.2.1 Shoulder Bolted Joint, Appendix A – Item 1.0
Appendix A, Item 1.0 shows the calculation for the Shoulder Bolt connecting the Filter Flipper Body to the Filter
Flipper Support Arm. Figure 2-9 indicates that only the Spec A Shoulder Bolt was calculated, but both Spec A and
Spec NP shoulder bolts would see the same loads at the joint, so the safety of both load paths is encapsulated by
the Spec A calculation; the Spec NP Shoulder Bolt is not calculated separately. The stress added to the bolt
comes by the entire Flipper subassembly accelerating to the side, with most of the loads being taken by the
“legs” of the subassembly. This calculation assumes that the bolt takes all the load when a lot of the load would
be taken by the “legs” of the Flipper in reality, so the bolted stress analysis is conservative. The calculation
essentially calculates the force that the bolt may see if one of the Flipper legs broke and the load pushes the
remaining leg away from the support arm. By engineering judgement, a catastrophic failure of that nature is
unlikely without the Flipper Support Arm bending (Section 2.3.3), so if the shoulder bolt yields under axial stress,
the Flipper will still be captured on the base. Therefore, even though the safety factor is less than the 6.00
required by the DSS for single bolt joints, the joint is acceptable. The direct shear loading on the shoulder bolt is
assessed in Section 2.3.2.

2.2.2 Appendix A Items 2.0, 3.0, and 4.0
Appendix A Items 2.0, 3.0, and 4.0 were not explicitly calculated. Item 2.0 is the Spring Plunger threading into
the Filter Flipper body. This joint will only see loading of the threads/joint when the plunger is operated to move
the orientation of the Filter Flipper. Since this is what the plunger was explicitly designed for, the threads of the
plunger are safe by engineering judgement.
Item 3.0 is the Spec A four-bolt pattern holding the Filter Flipper Support Arm to the Base Plate. The loads on this
joint were assumed to be less than the loads on the Spec NP joint due to the smaller mounting angle to the
vertical compared with the Spec A Base Plate. By engineering judgement, the Spec A four-bolt pattern holding
the Support Arm to the Base Plate is qualified under the Spec NP joint calculation, analyzed in Appendix A, Item
7.0.
Item 4.0 is the Spec A four bolt pattern holding the Base Plate to the mounting arms. Similar to Item 3.0, by
engineering judgement, the Spec A joint is encompassed by the Spec NP joint calculations, analyzed in Appendix
A, Item 8.0.

2.2.3 Spec A Mounting Arm to Transfer Plate Joint, Appendix A – Item 5.0
The bolted joint analysis for the Spec A Mounting Arms to the Transfer Plate was performed in Appendix A, Item
5.0. This calculation is conservative because only two bolts per arm were assumed to hold them to the Transfer
Plate instead of the nominal four per arm. The two Mounting Arms were assumed to act as one structure due to
them being connected together at the Base Plate.
Item 5.0 also includes a calculation to determine if the clamping force of the bolts is enough to prevent motion
of the Mounting Arms under seismic loading. Note that this is not safety critical, but is data critical. The washer is
captured by the bolt, and the coefficient of friction between steel and aluminum (washer to plate) is less than
that of steel to steel (washer to bolt head). By engineering judgement, the slippage of the washer against the
plate is more likely than the bolt head against the washer, so the calculation of washer to plate encapsulates the
bolt head to washer interface calculation.

2.2.4 Spec A Transfer Plate to Handrail Plate Joint, Appendix A – Item 6.0
Appendix A, Item 6.0 calculated the bolted joint strength of the four bolts holding the Spec A Handrail Plate to
the Transfer Plate while clamped around the handrail.

2.2.5 Filter Flipper Support Arm to Spec NP Base Plate Joint, Appendix A – Item 7.0
Appendix A, Item 7.0 calculated the bolted joint strength of the four bolts holding the Filter Flipper Support Arm
to the Spec NP Base Plate.

2.2.6 Spec NP Base Plate to Mounting Arms Joint, Appendix A – Item 8.0
Appendix A, Item 8.0 calculated the bolted joint strength of the four bolts holding the Spec NP Base Plate to the
two Mounting Arms. Despite the base plate having slots for the bolts, the clamping force was not calculated
because slipping of this joint is unlikely by engineering judgement. If slipping does occur, it will only have a minor
impact on data and does not present additional safety implications. The engineering judgement that this is an
unlikely failure derives from two scenarios. In the first, direct seismic loading is unlikely to have a significant
effect because the center of gravity is very close to the center of rotation of the plate in relation to the bolt slots,
meaning that forces applied to the center of gravity (e.g. seismic demands) would produce a small/negligible

                                      LSEO_2025 – 1015535645-AA                                      Page 21 of 105

rotation force on the plate. In the second scenario, the seismic demands would induce a lateral sway of the
system due to bending of the mounting arms; this was considered unlikely because of the thickness of the
Mounting Arms (12mm each over 265mm of bending length) and the stiffness added to the Mounting Arms by
the Base Plate itself. Additionally, the angles of the slots limit the movement of the plate to only be rotational. By
engineering judgement, the plate will not rotate under load.
Prying forces were also considered when analyzing the bolt load but were not included in the calculation
because of the engineering judgement that the bolts were too close to the edge and the plate was too stiff
(including the added stiffness of the plates bolted to it) to produce significant prying forces.

2.2.7 Spec NP Mounting Arms to Transfer Plate Joint, Appendix A – Item 9.0
Appendix A, Item 9.0 calculated the bolted joint strength of the four bolts holding the two Spec NP Mounting
Arms to the Transfer Plate. The two Mounting Arms were assumed to act as one structure due to them being
connected together at the Base Plate.
Item 9.0 also includes a calculation to determine if the clamping force of the bolts is enough to prevent motion
of the Mounting Arms under seismic loading. Note that this is not safety critical, but is data critical. The washer is
captured by the bolt, and the coefficient of friction between steel and aluminum (washer to plate) is less than
that of steel to steel (washer to bolt head). By engineering judgement, the slippage of the washer against the
plate is more likely than the bolt head against the washer, so the calculation of washer to plate encapsulates the
bolt head to washer interface calculation.

2.2.8 Spec NP Transfer Plate to I-Beam Joint, Appendix A – Item 10.0
Appendix A, Item 10.0 calculated the bolted joint strength of the four bolts holding the Spec NP Transfer Plate to
the NIF structural I-beam. This is a bolt-in-nut joint with washers below both the bolt head and nut.

Appendix B.1, Item 1.0 calculated the safety factor of the lip on the Filter Flipper Body holding the C-clip

Flippers would be mounted in reality.

2.3.2 Body Shear Stress in Shoulder Bolt and Spring Plunger, Appendix B – Items 1.0 and 2.0
The calculation for the shear stress in the Shoulder Bolt body was performed in Appendix B, Item 1.0. The
Shoulder Bolt is not expected to see axial loading that would cause thread shear, even though it is calculated in
Section 2.2.1. Also, the flipper is held in place by the nature of its design and the spring plunger. If the shoulder
bolt yields under axial stress, the flipper will still be captured on the base, meaning catastrophic failure will not
occur. Due to this, engineering judgment was used to make the determination that the joint is safe in thread
shear, even if the safety factor is less than 6, as required by the DSS for a single bolt joint. Although the shear on
the shoulder bolt is encapsulated in the Appendix A calculation, the calculations in Appendix B where the bolt is
treated as a solid pin are more realistic since the axial forces of the bolt are likely taken up by the bulk material
around the bolt rather than by the bolt itself.
The Spring Plunger’s safety factor in body shear strength was analyzed in Appendix B, Item 2.0. This joint is
assumed to be loading only in shear due to how the Filter Flipper is captured by the assembly and the
assumption that the Shoulder Bolt acts as a perfect pivot point.

2.3.3 Filter Flipper Support Arm Bending, Appendix B – Item 3.0
Appendix B, Item 3.0 calculated the safety factor of the Filter Flipper Support Arm bending under loads caused
by the Filter Flipper. The calculation assumes that the center of gravity of the Flipper is in-plane with the centroid
axis of the Flipper Support Arm. This is justified since the Filter Flipper is nearly symmetric in that plane. The
analysis is performed in the worst-case loading, where the axis of the Puck is vertical, so the bending of the bar is
maximized by having the vertical component of the seismic load acting in the direction of the thin dimension of
the bar.

2.3.4 Beam Bending of Spec A and Spec NP Mounting Arms, Appendix B – Items 4.0 and 8.0
The Mounting Arms for both Spec A and Spec NP were analyzed in bending to determine if they can resist
seismic loading; this calculation was performed in Appendix B, Item 4.0 and 8.0 respectively. The joints were
assumed to be cantilevered from the Transfer Plate connection, and that the bending moment on each arm from
the offset Center of Gravity (CoG) was zero because each plate takes half the load.

2.3.5 Beam Bending of Spec A and Spec NP Transfer Plates, Appendix B – Items 5.0 and 9.0
The Transfer Plates for both Spec A and Spec NP were analyzed in bending to determine if they could resist
seismic loading; these calculations were performed in Appendix B, Item 5.0 and 9.0 respectively. The Spec A
Transfer Plate was assumed to be cantilevered at the halfway point of the handrail and only have bolted
connection loads at the end. That made this calculation conservative because the loads above the Transfer Plate
in the load path would be more distributed across the four bolts that connect each Mounting Arms to the
Transfer Plate instead of at a single bolt.
The Spec NP Transfer Plate calculation was similarly conservative because it is assumed that all loads occurred at
the rearmost bolt at the connection to the I-beam and the foremost bolt at the connection to the Mounting
Arms. This increased the bending moment on the beam, resulting in larger calculated stresses.

2.3.6 Spec A Mounting Handrail, Appendix B – Item 6.0
The handrail that the Spec A Filter Mounting Assembly is clamped to is an Al 6061-T6 weldment. Appendix B,
Item 6.0 analyzed the handrail in bending and torsion to ensure that the load was safe. The calculations
accounted for the reduced strength of the heat affected zone.

2.3.7 Spec NP Mounting Handrail, Appendix B – Item 7.0
The Spec NP Base Plate was analyzed in bending in Appendix B, Item 7.0. This calculation also shows that the
Spec A Base Plate is safe since the loading on it will be lower than on the Spec NP plate.

2.3.8 Spec NP I-beam Bolt Pull-Out, Appendix B.1 – Item 2.0
Appendix B.1, Item 2.0 analyzed the strength of the I-beam to resist the bolts pulling out of it. The calculation is
conservative because the I-beam material was assumed to be mild steel and the preload on the bolts was
assumed to remain a constant force even if yielding in the I-beam occurs. By assuming mild steel, when the I-
beam is likely a stronger structural steel means that the safety factor is artificially low. Similarly, if any yielding
occurs on the I-beam, the preload force of the bolts will reduce, meaning that holding it constant for the pull-out
calculation artificially reduces the safety factor even more.
