Keeping it Cool: Actionable Pathways for HVAC and Cooling Systems Waste
Reduction
Process cooling and heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems fulfill mission
critical functions in many types of manufacturing, buildings, and data centers by providing
regulation of temperature, humidity, air quality and circulation control. As the use of these systems
becomes more prevalent, organizations with waste reduction goals should consider lifecycle
waste impact of this equipment. A typical commercial or industrial facility can generate several
tons of Rooftop Units (RTU)/chiller-related waste per unit during its lifecycle of 15–20 years.1
This tipsheet offers succinct, actionable strategies to help minimize waste while improving
operational performance. TABLE 1 highlights the material composition of a typical 10-ton RTU
unit, showing that most components, especially metals, are recyclable. For larger units, the non-
recyclable materials drop from approximately 25% to about 10%, and ferrous and non-ferrous
materials, such as copper and aluminum, take up a greater mass fraction.

Waste Reduction Practices for Cooling and HVAC Equipment
Comprehensive waste minimization from RTUs, chillers, and air handlers requires integrated
management of three critical waste streams—used oils, refrigerants, and solid materials—
alongside proactive maintenance strategies that prevent waste generation at the source. Used oil
and refrigerant management through leak prevention, condition-based monitoring, and certified
recycling partnerships transform waste streams into resource recovery opportunities, while solid
waste reduction emphasizes component-level repair over wholesale replacement to capture
valuable materials like copper coils and aluminum heat exchangers. A holistic approach can
significantly reduce landfill waste, extend asset lifecycles, lower total cost of ownership, and
position organizations as industry leaders in responsible facility management. TABLE 3 below
summarizes several key waste reduction practices and their impact on waste reduction and asset
life extension.

By implementing these waste reduction strategies, facilities can increase economic value
recovery and enhance operational performance. By incorporating rigorous maintenance
practices, responsible refrigerant management, and thoughtful cost analyses, RTU owners can
achieve their waste reduction goals and transform potential liabilities into bottom-line
improvement opportunities.
