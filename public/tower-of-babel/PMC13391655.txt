Survival, late toxicity and ability to return to work after proton radiotherapy for low-grade gliomas
Authors: Dominika Wojton-Dziewońska, Tomasz Skóra, Joanna Anioł, Agata Popow-Gierba, Renata Kopeć, Kamil Kisielewicz, Anna Mucha-Małecka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeLevel I evidence to inform decisions on radiation modality and chemotherapy regimen choice in adult-type diffuse gliomas CNS WHO grade 2 are awaited. The retrospective analysis was performed to assess overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS) and late toxicity including ability to return to work in 2016 CNS WHO grade 1 and grade 2 gliomas (low-grade gliomas [LGG]) patients treated with intensity modulated proton beam radiotherapy (IMPT).MethodsPatients with 2016 CNS WHO LGG after surgery, treated with IMPT at our institution from 2016 to 2021 were eligible for this analysis. Data were retrospectively collected from medical and imaging records.Results143 patients were identified. Diffuse astrocytoma isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutant was the predominant phenotype. Median follow-up was 66.5 months. Five-year OS and PFS were 78.21%, 76.52%, 85.92% and 80.34%, 65.59%, 86.07% for the whole cohort, diffuse astrocytoma IDH mutant and gemistocytic astrocytoma combined, and oligodendroglioma IDH mutant 1p19q codeleted respectively. Mean residual tumor volume was 61.3 cm³ and mean planning target volume (PTV) was 335.7 cm³. At 12, 36 and 60 months after IMPT 61 (42.66%), 61 (42.66%) and 59 (41.26%) patients held down in full-time employment. Each additional cubic centimeter of PTV significantly reduced ability to return to the initial full-time work by 0.5% (Odds Ratio [OR] = 0.995, p = 0.046). 4 cases of CTCAE grade 3 late toxicity were reported (2 cases of optic neuropathy, 1 late fatigue, and 1 muscle weakness).ConclusionsIrradiation brain volume is associated with maintaining ability to work.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s11060-026-05720-2.

Introduction

Maximal safe resection remains the undoubted mainstay in glioma management [1]. Further decisions are driven by risk assessment including neurological performance, residual tumor volume [2], and histopathological features [3, 4]. If adjuvant radiotherapy is recommended, use of highly conformal techniques is mandatory to minimize late treatment sequelae. Intensity Modulated Proton Therapy (IMPT) is being increasingly used, as this modality significantly reduces maximal, mean and integral doses of radiation to organs at risk (OARs) [5]. However, results from randomized controlled trials assessing clinical impact of dosimetric difference between photon and proton beam techniques are not yet available [6]. RTOG 9802 trial results [7] in conjunction with post hoc analysis of isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutant grade 2 gliomas from this trial support the use of adjuvant systemic treatment in adult-type diffuse IDH-mutant low-grade gliomas (LGG) after radiotherapy [8]. There is a paucity of prospective data comparing procarbazine, lomustine and vincristine (PCV) regimen with temozolomide (TMZ), but retrospective analysis indicates superior outcomes with the use of PCV, especially in oligodendrogliomas [9, 10]. Polish national health insurance system covers the costs of IMPT for Central Nervous System World Health Organization (CNS WHO) grade 1 and grade 2 glioma patients, if a major reduction of dose to critical structures in comparison with to photon techniques is expected. This study is the first mature report on IMPT in adult patients with 2016 WHO Classification brain grade 1 and grade 2 glioma after resection or biopsy in Poland.

Methods

A single institution retrospective analysis of treatment outcomes, toxicities and patient-reported outcomes was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by our Institutional Review Board (Approval number 3/2026) and is registered in the local Register of Scientific Research Projects under the reference 33/2026. All patients gave written informed consent for using clinically derived data for research purposes. For employment status evaluation patients were eligible if they were alive at the time of survey and if they permitted phone contact for collection of data. Patients’ clinical data, tumor characteristics, treatment and outcomes were retrospectively collected from medical and imaging records.

Patient and treatment characteristics

The analysis included 143 consecutive adults ((≥ 18 years) diagnosed with CNS WHO grade 1 or 2 glioma according to the 2016 CNS WHO criteria treated with IMPT from 2016 to 2021 at Cyclotron Centre Bronowice in cooperation with Maria Sklodowska-Curie National Research Institute of Oncology in Krakow, Poland. Target definition was based on computed tomography (CT) scans with slice thickness of 1,5 mm co-registered with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) performed within 2 weeks preceding radiotherapy commencement. Gross tumor volume (GTV) comprised of residual tumor defined as the FLAIR/T2 hyperintense area including any potential T1 enhancing tumor (this part of the treated volume was separately contoured and reported as ‘residual tumor volume’ in Table 1) and adjacent tumor bed. Clinical target volume (CTV) comprised of GTV with additional isotropic margin of 1 to 1.5 cm (for grade 1 and grade 2 tumors respectively) adjusted to anatomical barriers such as falx, tentorium and cerebrospinal fluid. A 3 mm PTV isotropic margin was finally added to CTV. IMPT was delivered to a median dose of 54 Gy relative biological effectiveness (RBE) in 1.8 Gy RBE per fraction. In the most common (75 [52.45%] of plans) setting, 3 beams with 1 of the beams allowed to be directed towards the organ at risk placed beyond the target volume, were used. Otherwise, 2 beams or 4 were mostly utilized (Table 2). Dose to the brain excluding PTV (Brain-PTV) was reported to compare IMPT with photon alternative plan. PTV concept in planning was used because IMPT planning was performed with the use of Varian Proton Convolution Superposition algorithm, which does not support robust optimization. Radiotherapy was delivered with the use of pencil beam scanning produced by Proteus C-235 Cyclotron, equipped with two gantries. After completion of IMPT patients were followed with routine clinical examination along with MRI at multiple time points (every 3 months for first 2 years, then in the absence of unresolved imaging changes, every 6 months for the next 3 years and then annually) from diagnosis until the last follow-up, adhering to the standard institutional protocol. Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were measured from the last day of radiation therapy till the date of death or progressive disease according to Response Assessment in Neuro Oncology (RANO) 2.0 criteria as development of a new contrast enhancement or at least a 25% increase of sum of perpendicular diameters of T2 hyperintense lesion, that exhibited further increase over sequential MRI scans [11] or death, respectively. Transient tumor enlargement or new T1-weighted contrast enhancing area within the 80% isodose line, mimicking tumor progression occurring within the first 12 months after IMPT completion, with subsequent spontaneous regression or stabilization with no need for any intervention was referred to as pseudoprogression [11, 12]. Resolution between true progression and transient radiographic changes was solved by consecutive MRI scans and MR spectroscopy, if indicated, along with clinical examination. Late radiation-induced contrast enhancement (RICE), located outside the residual tumor volume were reported separately. Exclusion of true tumor progression was made upon radiologist and radiation oncologist shared opinion and additional MR spectroscopy. In case of equivocal changes patient was referred to a neurosurgeon. Toxicity was scored using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v.5. For employment status patients were interrogated prospectively at follow-up visits and by phone contact with those, who had given their permission.

Statistical analysis

Patient, disease, and treatment characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and tested using the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression (proportional hazards models) were employed to model the potential impact of predictors on a time to event. Hazard Ratios (HRs), alongside the 95% confidence intervals (CI), were presented. Variables that showed significant or close to significant impact on a dependent variable in univariate analyses were included in multiple models, so to maintain events per variable (EPV) value above 15. Significance level was set to P-values ≤ 0.05. All the analyses were conducted in R software, version 4.6.0.

Results

Clinical characteristics

Patients included 73 men (51.05%) and 70 (48.95%) women. The median age was 38 years (range, 18–74). Mean residual tumor volume was 61.26 cm³. In 141 patients WHO CNS grade 1 or 2 gliomas were histopathologically verified within less than 1 year before radiotherapy. Two patients had surgery with histopathological diagnosis obtained 5 years before IMPT and refused biopsy upon disease progression. IDH mutation was present in 121 patients. 89 diffuse astrocytomas IDH mutant, 5 gemistocytic astrocytomas IDH mutant and 27 oligodendroglioma IDH mutant and 1p19q codeleted were identified (Table 1). The majority (105 [73.43%]) of patients presented with seizures. Prior to IMPT commencement 24 (16.78%) patients were using dexamethasone at a median dose of 2 mg (range, 1–20, interquartile range [IQR, ] 3) daily. At the end of IMPT 52 (36.36%) patients were using steroids at a median dose of 4 mg (range, 1–18, IQR, 4) daily.

Proton beam therapy

One hundred nine patients were irradiated after first surgery (107 were treated upfront and 2 patients were treated at recurrence without repeated neurosurgical intervention due to patients’ refusal) and 34 received IMPT after 2 or more surgical interventions (Table 2). Acute radiation toxicity included alopecia G1 and G2 in 92 (64.34%) and 36 (25.17%) patients. Skin erythema G1 and G2 was reported in 96 (67.13%) and 22 (15.38%) patients respectively. Headache G1 and G2 was reported in 25 (17.48%) and in 9 (6.29%) patients.

Systemic treatment

One patient was taking concurrent temozolomide (TMZ) according to his personal preferences. Drug was prescribed by clinical oncologist from other site (patient wrote a statement that he was aware that combined radio-chemotherapy in his case is not a recommended treatment adhering to our protocol and guidelines). Majority of the patients received adjuvant systemic treatment with adjuvant procarbazine, lomustine and vincristine (PCV; 73, 51.05%) or adjuvant TMZ (26; 18.18%).

Treatment outcomes

Survival

After a median follow-up of 66.5 months (range 0.43–111) after completion of radiotherapy 106 patients (74.13%) were still alive. The OS rates for the whole cohort at 3 and 5 years were 88.11% (95% CI 82.96% − 93.58%) and 78.21% (95% CI 71.71% − 85.30%) respectively (see Table 2; Fig. 1). For diffuse astrocytoma IDH mutant and gemistocytic astrocytoma IDH mutant combined 3-year and 5-year OS were 88.30% (95% CI 82.03% − 95.04%) and 76.52% (95% CI 68.40% − 85.61%) respectively. For oligodendroglioma IDH mutant 1p19q codeleted 3-year and 5-year OS were 96.55% (95% CI 90.13% − 100.00%) and 85.92% (95% CI 74.02% − 99.74%) respectively. Among 37 patients, who died, 33 deaths (89.19%) were related to progression of brain tumor. Four deaths were attributed to other conditions (1 patient each): acute adrenal insufficiency, cardiac disease, covid infection and bone marrow transplant failure during myeloid leukemia treatment. In univariate analysis higher residual tumor volume, larger PTV, V20Gy and V25 Gy to the brain outside PTV, higher mean dose to brain outside PTV and presence of at least 2 comorbidities were associated with shorter OS (Supplementary materials). Adverse effect of ≥ 2 comorbidities on OS persisted in multivariable analysis (Hazard ratio [HR] for death = 10.41, 95% CI 2.802–38.673, p < 0.001). However, EPV value for the multivariable models for OS is low (5.3) (Supplementary materials).

At the time of analysis 96 (67.13%) were progression-free. For the whole cohort 3-year and 5-year PFS were 80.34% (95% CI 74.07% − 87.14%) and 70.92% (95% CI 63.80% -78.85%) respectively. For diffuse astrocytoma IDH mutant and gemistocytic astrocytoma IDH mutant combined 3-year and 5-year PFS were 77.60% (95% CI 69.61% − 86.52%) and 65.59% (95% CI 56.59% − 76.01%) respectively. For oligodendroglioma IDH mutant 1p19q codeleted 3-year and 5-year PFS were 93.10% (95% CI 84.32% − 100.00%) and 86.07% (95% CI 74.28% − 99.72%) respectively.

Univariate Cox proportional hazards models showed that larger residual tumor volume, larger PTV, V20 Gy, V25Gy and mean to brain outside PTV, as well as presence of at least two comorbidities were associated with significantly shorter PFS. Gemistocytic astrocytoma IDH mutant adversely affected PFS as compared to diffuse astrocytoma IDH mutant (HR = 5.218, 95%CI 1.818–14.975, p = 0.002) and this association persisted in multivariable model (HR = 6.338, 95% CI 2.118 018.966, p = 0.001). Multivariable models for PFS, lacking sufficient reliability due to low EPV (3.9), revealed that residual tumor volume and presence of at least of 2 comorbidities were significantly associated with increased risk of progression or death (HR = 1.007, 95% CI 1.001–1.013, p = 0.022 and HR = 32.905, 95% CI 9.441 -114.689, p < 0.001 respectively), whereas oligodendroglioma IDH mutant 1p19q versus diffuse astrocytoma IDH mutant was associated with improved PFS (HR = 0.238, 95% CI 0.075–0.757, p = 0.015).

Failures

Among 47 patients, who had progression, 42 (89.36%) of recurrences occurred within the 80% isodose line. One (2.13%) case of progression was marginal (between 20 and 80% isodose) and 4 (8.51%) were located outside the target volume.

Seizures control

Among 122 patients diagnosed with epilepsy, seizures completely subsided and in 83 (68.03%) and in 15 (12.3%) were better controlled 36 months after IMPT. All these patients continued antiepileptic medications according to their neurologist recommendations. One patient presented with new onset epilepsy without tumor progression.

Return to work

At 12 months after IMPT 140 patients were alive and available for personal or phone contact. Sixty-one (42.66%) patients stayed in full-time employment, holding the same scope of responsibilities. At 12 months after IMPT 69 (48.25%) patients after IMPT did not return to full-time work because of their health condition (66 patients) or death (3 patients). Fifty-seven patients lost their job, and 9 patients got a part-time job upon disability certificate, enabling them to have their duties reduced. Eight patients retired before radiotherapy commencement. Five patients were initially unemployed, and their status remained unchanged over the follow-up. At 36 months 61 (42.66%) patients had full time jobs. At 60 months, 59 patients were still alive and full-time working (see Table 3). In univariate analysis each additional cubic centimeter of residual disease and each additional cubic centimeter of PTV reduces the chance of return to work by 0.6% and 0.3% respectively. In multivariate analysis (EPV 20.3) each additional cm3 of PTV significantly reduced ability to stay in employment by 0.5% (Odds Ratio [OR] = 0.995, p = 0.046). Neither other radiotherapy parameters reported, nor chemotherapy use were predictive of ability to return to work (Table 4).

Toxicity

Pseudoprogression was observed in 23 patients (22 asymptomatic, 1 patient presented with transient blurred vision). In one patient presenting with pseudoprogression deemed to be real progression upon MR spectroscopy, histopathologic examination excluded presence of vital tumor. Median time to pseudoprogression onset was 4.33 months from the last day of radiotherapy. Median time of pseudoprogression persistence was 5.33 months. Most prevalent late toxicity was permanent alopecia CTCAE v5.0 grade 1. Second most common late toxicity was fatigue grade 1. 1 case of severe fatigue grade 3 was reported as well. Late RICE, referred to as radionecrosis, presenting as nodular contrast enhancing lesions occured after a median time of 14.28 months after radiotherapy completion, was observed in 23 patients. Radionecrosis with maximum diameter of 2 to 24 mm was observed near ventricular system (11 cases) or at the distal beam edge (12 cases). Nineteen cases were clinically asymptomatic. One patient presenting with grade 3 left-sided muscle weakness underwent neurosurgical intervention with histopathological evaluation excluding true progression. Maximum dose (Dmax) to the brain, defined as the hottest point in the brain was located inside PTV and was 58.73 Gy RBE (108.8% of prescribed dose). Mean and median dose to the brain outside PTV was 12.4 Gy RBE and 1.1 Gy RBE, indicating sharp dose gradient outside PTV. One patient presented with transient grade 1 late headache, 1 patient presented with CTCAE grade 2 late right sided muscle weakness and grade 2 late dysphasia. Two patients presented with grade 3 optic nerve disorder (presenting as visual field deficits, Dmax to optic nerve 54 Gy RBE and 53GyRBE respectively). One patient was diagnosed with cataract (time to cataract onset 6 years after IMPT; Dmax 8,9 Gy RBE to lens; concomitant use of 4 mg dexamethasone daily after surgery prescribed over 6 months). Neither grade 4 nor 5 late toxicity were observed.

Discussion

One third of LGG patients perform significantly lower in working memory, executive functions and attention, verbal function, verbal and non-verbal memory than healthy population, while the majority present comparably to healthy population [13] and preservation of neurocognitive function is a major objective for well performing patients. In an online survey, two thirds of LGG patients, who failed to return to work, declared that outcomes of surgery, chemotherapy, photon and proton radiotherapy prevented them from returning to work [14]. Our analysis of neurocognitive functioning and quality of life evaluation was insufficient, missing appropriate batteries of tests. According to our analysis, irradiated brain volumes are predictive of ability to stay in employment. PTV derivates from residual tumor volume and adjacent tumor cavity, surrounded by a margin of healthy brain tissue (CTV), enlarged by a setup margin. This finding emphasizes the role of factors influencing this volume, including extent of surgery, adequate imaging and radiation oncologist decisions. Current recommendation for IDH-mutant WHO CNS grade 2 gliomas is GTV expansion of 10 mm to produce CTV [15]. Our CTV margins were probably excessive, and we changed our practice over time, reducing margins after 2021. Interestingly, prediction model for the selection of patients to proton treatment developed by researchers from Aarhus, Denmark, using CTV volume showed high predictive value of the CTV volume for selection to this radiation modality [16]. The volume of brain irradiated to total dose of 50.4 Gy did not impact 2-year quality of life of patients in EORTC 22,033–26,033 study [17]. In a cohort of 20 patients, 11 of them having a tumor with a maximum diameter ˃6 cm, neither large tumor was associated with worse baseline cognitive impairment [18], nor change over time in cognitive functioning was observed [19]. IMPT reduces maximal, mean and integral doses in organs at risk as compared to photon techniques [5]. However, it remains to be clarified, to what extent this advantage translates into clinical benefit [20]. In our cohort 91 (64%) of patients did not use any steroids over the whole IMPT treatment. Late complications were infrequent with 4 cases of grade 3 toxicity. One of 2 cases of optic neuropathy was reported in patient with basically impaired vision in course of the disease. The unresolved case of grade 3 muscle weakness in patient with pretreatment residual lesion of 122.2 cm³ occurred 10.3 months following IMPT completion. As histopathological evaluation of patient’s post-treatment specimen revealed no glioma, further careful follow-up is needed. Because of the risk of misinterpretation for progressive disease caution should be exercised during evaluation of posttreatment imaging changes. Unless long term results of ongoing clinical trials become available, the paucity of empirical data justifying choice of proton therapy [21] to date prudent use of obtained retrospective data is permissible. The relative biological effectiveness of proton beams for simplicity is generally considered a constant value of 1.1 equivalent to that of photon. In vitro experiments show that RBE increases along beam pathway from 1 at the entrance, reaching 1.4 in the middle of the spread out of Bragg peak area to over 3 in the distal fall-off area. Given the variability of the RBE and especially RBE at the end-of range, an attempt should be made to avoid placing the distal edge of the beam close to organs at risk [22], as increased linear energy transfer (LET) may result in unexpected effect on irradiated brain outside the treated target volume.

Conclusions

Major limitation of this study is a lack of adherence to the current 2021 CNS WHO classification. However, reported former 2016 WHO classification subtypes in combination with clinical data largely reflect a course of molecularly defined adult-type diffuse gliomas and serve as confirmation of impact of molecular diagnostics, surgical and radiotherapy and systemic treatment advances. To our knowledge, this association between irradiated brain volume and ability to return work has not been directly evaluated before. Comprehensive analysis of neurocognitive changes revealed no neurocognitive decline after IMPT [23] .Further studies, including return to work as primary endpoint, would give an answer, what the real cost of IMPT for everyday life is.
