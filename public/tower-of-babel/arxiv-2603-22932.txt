On modules over a Hopf brace


Abstract
. Let ℍ = ( H 1 , H 2 ) \mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2}) be a Hopf brace in a symmetric monoidal category 𝖢 {\sf C} . In this article it is proved that the category of modules over ℍ \mathbb{H} is isomorphic to the category of modules over the smash product algebra H 1 ​ ♯ ​ H 2 H_{1}\sharp H_{2} . Furthermore, the category of modules over ℍ \mathbb{H} in the sense of Zhu is characterized by the condition that a certain action lies in the cocommutativity class of H 2 H_{2} .

1.
Introduction

The Quantum Yang-Baxter Equation (QYBE) was introduced by Chen N. Yang
[
17
]
and Rodney J. Baxter
[
3
]
between the late 1960s and the early 1970s in order to study integrable systems in the field of quantum physics and statistical mechanics. At present, it is considered as one of the most important equations in the field of mathematical physics, serving as a bridge between abstract algebra and theoretical physics. Despite the simplicity of its formulation, obtaining a complete classification of the solutions to the QYBE is by no means a simple problem and it is still open nowadays. In an effort to tackle it, Drinfeld
[
6
]
proposed to study the simplest class of solutions, the set-theoretical ones.

In this line, L. Guarnieri and L. Vendramin
[
10
]
introduce the notion of skew brace as a non-abelian version of the brace structure previously given by W. Rump
[
15
]
, proving that skew braces induce non-degenerate and non-necessarily involutive solutions to the Yang-Baxter Equation. These structures occupy nowadays a central role in the classification of set-theoretical solutions. Thus, based on the fact that Hopf algebras are the quantum analogues of groups, I. Angiono, C. Galindo and L. Vendramin
[
2
]
provide the notion of a Hopf brace as the quantum version of a skew brace, that is, passing from a group-theoretic structure to one where the underlying structures are vector spaces. More concretely, if
(H,\epsilon,\Delta)
is a coalgebra, a Hopf brace structure
\mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2})
on
H
consists of a pair of Hopf algebras

H_{1}=(H,1,\cdot,\epsilon,\Delta,S)\textnormal{ and }H_{2}=(H,1_{\circ},\circ,\epsilon,\Delta,T),

such that the following compatibility condition between the products holds:

g\circ(h\cdot k)=(g_{1}\circ h)\cdot S(g_{2})\cdot(g_{3}\circ k)

for all
g,h,k\in H
. As established by the authors, cocommutative Hopf braces also yield solutions to the QYBE, and such solutions are involutive in the case that
H_{1}
is a commutative Hopf algebra
[
2
, Corollaries 2.4 and 2.5]
.

Once the notion of brace in the abelian setting was established, Rump gave a definition of module over a brace

(A,+,\circ)

[
15
, Definition 3]
. A remarkable point in this definition is that it only relies on a representation of the second group
(A,\circ)
, due to the fact of considering abelian groups. When the notion of brace is extended to non-abelian contexts, Rump’s first approach needs to be extended to include not only the second structure but also the first one. In this line, H. Zhu
[
18
]
gives a quite restrictive notion of modules over a Hopf brace. On the other hand, R. González
[
8
]
obtains a wider definition that contains Zhu’s one, and they happen to be equivalent if the underlying coalgebra is cocommutative. However, if we do not ask for any extra condition, both definitions differ. For example, while González’s one includes
\mathbb{H}
as a
\mathbb{H}
-module, Zhu’s one does not, so González’s approach seems to be more suitable in non-cocommutative contexts.

At this point, modules over a Hopf brace need to involve (at least) two structures of modules over two different (although related) Hopf algebras. However, Y. Kozakai and C. Tsang point out in
[
11
, Remarks 2.2 and 3.2]
that modules in the sense of Zhu can be regarded as modules over an algebra, indeed, as the semidirect product of the underline algebras of the Hopf brace. However, they do not obtain an equivalence between both categories of modules.

Thus, in this work we deal with two clearly distinct questions. The first one is related to the possibility of interpreting the category of modules over a Hopf brace as a category of modules over an algebra. In Section
3
, using the definition proposed by González, we prove that the category of modules over a Hopf brace
\mathbb{H}
is isomorphic to the category of modules over a smash product algebra arising from the underlying Hopf brace
\mathbb{H}
(see Theorem
3.11
). Also, as a consequence of this theorem, we can assure that the category of modules over a Hopf brace in González’s sense can be seen as a category of Doi-Hopf modules (see Corollary
3.12
).

The second question is addressed in Section
4
and it concerns the relation between González’s and Zhu’s modules. In Theorem
4.2
we characterize Zhu’s modules in terms of the so-called cocommutativity class (see
[
1
]
). This characterization may help in skipping long calculations when Zhu’s approach could be needed.

2.
Preliminaries about Hopf algebras and Hopf braces

We work throughout this article in a strict symmetric monoidal framework. By Mac Lane’s coherence theorem, the strictness assumption entails no loss of generality, since any monoidal category is monoidal equivalent to a strict one. Therefore, the statements proved below remain valid in the general non-strict setting, up to the standard modifications. In what follows,
{\sf C}
denotes a strict symmetric monoidal category with tensor product
\otimes
, unit object
K
, and symmetry
c
. For any morphism
f\colon M\rightarrow N
and any object
P
in
{\sf C}
, we shall use the notation
P\otimes f
and
f\otimes P
instead of
{\rm id}_{P}\otimes f
and
f\otimes{\rm id}_{P}
, respectively. For the reader’s convenience, in the first part of this section we recall some standard definitions that we will use repeatedly in the development of the article.

Definition 2.1
.

An algebra in
C
is a triple
A=(A,\eta_{A},\mu_{A})
where
\eta_{A}\colon K\rightarrow A
(unit) and
\mu_{A}\colon A\otimes A\rightarrow A
(product) are morphisms in
C
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle\mu_{A}\circ(\eta_{A}\otimes A)={\rm id}_{A}=\mu_{A}\circ(A\otimes\eta_{A}),\quad\mu_{A}\circ(\mu_{A}\otimes A)=\mu_{A}\circ(A\otimes\mu_{A}).

Note that, given
B=(B,\eta_{B},\mu_{B})
another algebra in
C
, the tensor product
A\otimes B
admits an algebra structure whose unit and product are defined as follows:

\eta_{A\otimes B}\coloneqq\eta_{A}\otimes\eta_{B},\quad\mu_{A\otimes B}\coloneqq(\mu_{A}\otimes\mu_{B})\circ(A\otimes c_{B,A}\otimes B).

Moreover, a morphism
f\colon A\rightarrow B
in
{\sf C}
is said to be an algebra morphism if it preserves the unit and the product, that is,

f\circ\eta_{A}=\eta_{B},\quad f\circ\mu_{A}=\mu_{B}\circ(f\otimes f).

Dually, we will say that a triple
C=(C,\varepsilon_{C},\delta_{C})
is a coalgebra in
C
if
\varepsilon_{C}\colon C\rightarrow K
(counit) and
\delta_{C}\colon C\rightarrow C\otimes C
(coproduct) are morphisms in
C
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{C}\otimes C)\circ\delta_{C}={\rm id}_{C}=(C\otimes\varepsilon_{C})\circ\delta_{C},\quad(\delta_{C}\otimes C)\circ\delta_{C}=(C\otimes\delta_{C})\circ\delta_{C}.

Likewise, if
D=(D,\varepsilon_{D},\delta_{D})
is another coalgebra in
C
, the tensor product
C\otimes D
is a coalgebra with the following counit and coproduct:

\varepsilon_{C\otimes D}\coloneqq\varepsilon_{C}\otimes\varepsilon_{D},\quad\delta_{C\otimes D}\coloneqq(C\otimes c_{C,D}\otimes D)\circ(\delta_{C}\otimes\delta_{D}).

Besides, a morphism
g\colon C\rightarrow D
in
{\sf C}
is a coalgebra morphism if the equalities

\varepsilon_{D}\circ g=\varepsilon_{C},\quad\delta_{D}\circ g=(g\otimes g)\circ\delta_{C}

hold, i.e.,
g
preserves the counit and it is comultiplicative.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
C
be a coalgebra and let
A
be an algebra in
{\sf C}
. Let
f,g:C\rightarrow A
be two morphisms in
{\sf C}
. We define the convolution product of
f
and
g
as

f\ast g\coloneqq\mu_{A}\circ(f\otimes g)\circ\delta_{C}.

The set of morphisms in
{\sf C}
from
C
to
A
, denoted by
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\sf C}}(C,A)
, is a monoid with the convolution product whose unit is given by
\eta_{A}\circ\varepsilon_{C}=\varepsilon_{C}\otimes\eta_{A}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A=(A,\eta_{A},\mu_{A})
be an algebra in
{\sf C}
. A left
A
-module in
{\sf C}
is a pair
(M,\varphi_{M})
where
\varphi_{M}\colon A\otimes M\rightarrow M
(action) is a morphism in
{\sf C}
which satisfies the following equalities:

(1)

\varphi_{M}\circ(\eta_{A}\otimes M)={\rm id}_{M},

(2)

\varphi_{M}\circ(A\otimes\varphi_{M})=\varphi_{M}\circ(\mu_{A}\otimes M).

Let
(N,\varphi_{N})
be a left
A
-module. A morphism
f\colon M\rightarrow N
is a morphism of left
A
-modules (or, in other words,
f
is
A
-linear) if

(3)

\displaystyle f\circ\varphi_{M}=\varphi_{N}\circ(A\otimes f).

With the previous morphisms, these objects constitute a category denoted by
{}_{A}{\sf Mod}
.

In a dual way, it is possible to define the category of right
C
-comodules for a coalgebra
C=(C,\varepsilon_{C},\delta_{C})
. A right
C
-comodule in
{\sf C}
is a pair
(M,\rho_{M})
where
\rho_{M}\colon M\rightarrow M\otimes C
(coaction) is a morphism in
{\sf C}
which satisfies the following equalities:

(M\otimes\varepsilon_{C})\circ\rho_{M}={\rm id}_{M},

(\rho_{M}\otimes C)\circ\rho_{M}=(M\otimes\delta_{C})\circ\rho_{M}.

Let
(N,\rho_{N})
be a right
C
-comodule. A morphism
f\colon M\rightarrow N
is a morphism of right
C
-comodules (or, in other words,
f
is
C
-colinear) if

\displaystyle\rho_{N}\circ f=(f\otimes C)\circ\rho_{M}.

With the previous morphisms, these objects constitute a category denoted by
{\sf Mod}^{C}
.

When an object in the category is endowed with both an algebra and a coalgebra structure that are compatible with each other, we obtain the notion of a bialgebra.

Definition 2.4
.

A 5-tuple
B=(B,\eta_{B},\mu_{B},\varepsilon_{B},\delta_{B})
is said to be a bialgebra in
C
if
(B,\eta_{B},\mu_{B})
is an algebra in
C
,
(B,\varepsilon_{B},\delta_{B})
is a coalgebra in
C
and
\eta_{B}
and
\mu_{B}
are coalgebra morphisms (equivalently, if
\varepsilon_{B}
and
\delta_{B}
are algebra morphisms).

If
D=(D,\eta_{D},\mu_{D},\varepsilon_{D},\delta_{D})
is another bialgebra in
{\sf C}
, a morphism in
{\sf C}

f\colon B\rightarrow D
is a bialgebra morphism if it is simultaneously an algebra and a coalgebra morphism.

In the following definition we recall the notion of Hopf algebra.

Definition 2.5
.

A 6-tuple
H=(H,\eta_{H},\mu_{H},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H})
is said to be a Hopf algebra in
C
when
(H,\eta_{H},\mu_{H},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H})
is a bialgebra such that there exists an endomorphism
\lambda_{H}\colon H\rightarrow H
, called the antipode, satisfying the following identity:

(4)

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}\ast{\rm id}_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}={\rm id}_{H}\ast\lambda_{H}.

The equation (
4
) means that the antipode,
\lambda_{H}
, is the inverse of the identity for the convolution product in
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\sf C}}(H,H)
and, as a consequence, it is unique.

A Hopf algebra
H
is said to be commutative when the underlying algebra is commutative, i.e., if the equality
\mu_{H}\circ c_{H,H}=\mu_{H}
, whereas
H
is said to be cocommutative when the underlying coalgebra is cocommutative, that is, in case that
c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H}=\delta_{H}
.

The antipode of a Hopf algebra
H
is antimultiplicative and anticomultiplicative, that is, it satisfies:

\lambda_{H}\circ\mu_{H}=\mu_{H}\circ c_{H,H}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes\lambda_{H}),

(5)

\delta_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}=(\lambda_{H}\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}.}

Moreover the antipode preserves the unit and the counit, i.e.,

\lambda_{H}\circ\eta_{H}=\eta_{H}
,

\varepsilon_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}.

As a consequence, if
H
is commutative, then
\lambda_{H}
is an algebra morphism and, if
H
is cocommutative,
\lambda_{H}
is a coalgebra morphism. In any of these cases the equality

(6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}={\rm id}_{H}

holds, i.e.,
\lambda_{H}
is involutive.

Given
B=(B,\eta_{B},\mu_{B},\varepsilon_{B},\delta_{B},\lambda_{B})
another Hopf algebra in
{\sf C}
, a morphism
f\colon H\rightarrow B
is a Hopf algebra morphism if it is a bialgebra morphism. Note that, in this case,
f
commutes with the antipodes, that is,

\displaystyle\lambda_{B}\circ f=f\circ\lambda_{H}.

Definition 2.6
.

An object
P
in
C
is profinite if there exists an object
P^{\ast}
in
{\sf C}
, called the dual of
P
, and a
C
-adjuntion
-\otimes P\dashv-\otimes P^{\ast}
. We will denote by
a_{P}
and
b_{P}
the unit and the counit of the previous
C
-adjuntion, respectively. Recall that the unit and the counit are collections of natural isomorphisms in
C
given by

a_{P}=\{a_{P}(X):X\rightarrow X\otimes P\otimes P^{\ast};\;X\in{\sf C}\},\quad b_{P}=\{b_{P}(X):X\otimes P^{\ast}\otimes P\rightarrow X;\;X\in{\sf C}\}

satisfying the triangular identities.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra in
C
. If
H
is profinite, then its dual
H^{\ast}
is a Hopf algebra in
{\sf C}
where

\displaystyle\eta_{H^{\ast}}=(\varepsilon_{H}\otimes H^{\ast})\circ a_{H}(K),\quad\varepsilon_{H^{\ast}}=b_{H}(K)\circ(H^{\ast}\otimes\eta_{H}),

\displaystyle\mu_{H^{\ast}}=((b_{H}(K)\circ(H^{\ast}\otimes b_{H}(K)\otimes H))\otimes H^{\ast})\circ(H^{\ast}\otimes H^{\ast}\otimes((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H^{\ast})\circ a_{H}(K)),

\displaystyle\delta_{H^{\ast}}=((b_{H}(K)\circ(H^{\ast}\otimes(\mu_{H}\circ c_{H,H})))\otimes H^{\ast}\otimes H^{\ast})\circ(H^{\ast}\otimes((H\otimes a_{H}(K)\otimes H^{\ast})\circ a_{H}(K))),

\displaystyle\lambda_{H^{\ast}}=((b_{H}(K)\circ(H^{\ast}\otimes\lambda_{H}))\otimes H^{\ast})\circ(H^{\ast}\otimes a_{H}(K)).

Definition 2.7
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
A
an algebra in
{\sf C}
. A left
H
-module
(A,\varphi_{A})
is said to be a left
H
-module algebra if
\eta_{A}
and
\mu_{A}
are
H
-linear morphisms, that is, if the following equalities

\varphi_{A}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{A})=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\eta_{A},

\varphi_{A}\circ(H\otimes\mu_{A})=\mu_{A}\circ\varphi_{A\otimes A},

hold, where
\varphi_{A\otimes A}\coloneqq(\varphi_{A}\otimes\varphi_{A})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,A}\otimes A)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes A\otimes A)
.

Example 2.8
.

Given a Hopf algebra
H
,
(H,\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}})
is a left
H
-module algebra where
\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}}
is the so-called adjoint action defined by

\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}}\coloneqq\mu_{H}\circ(\mu_{H}\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H).

The next theorem is a very well known result in the theory of Hopf algebras, and provides a generalization of the semidirect product of groups, as we will make explicit later:

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
(A,\varphi_{A})
a left
H
-module algebra, and define

A\sharp H=(A\otimes H,\eta_{A\sharp H}\coloneqq\eta_{A}\otimes\eta_{H},\mu_{A\sharp H}\coloneqq(\mu_{A}\otimes\mu_{H})\circ(A\otimes\Psi^{H}_{A}\otimes H)),

where
\Psi^{H}_{A}\coloneqq(\varphi_{A}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,A})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes A)
.
Then,
A\sharp H
is an algebra in
{\sf C}
, known as the smash product algebra.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
C
a coalgebra in
{\sf C}
. A left
H
-module
(C,\varphi_{C})
satisfying that
\varepsilon_{C}
and
\delta_{C}
are
H
-linear morphisms is said to be a left
H
-module coalgebra. In other words,
(C,\varphi_{C})
is a left
H
-module coalgebra if the equalities

(7)

\varepsilon_{C}\circ\varphi_{C}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{C},

(8)

\delta_{C}\circ\varphi_{C}=\varphi_{C\otimes C}\circ(H\otimes\delta_{C})

hold. Note that (
7
) and (
8
) are equivalent to the fact that
\varphi_{C}
is a coalgebra morphism.

Let
H
and
B
be Hopf algebras in
{\sf C}
. If
(B,\varphi_{B})
is both a left
H
-module algebra and a left
H
-module coalgebra, then it will be called a left
H
-module algebra-coalgebra.

A fundamental concept for this article is the notion of cocommutativity class, introduced in
[
1
]
by Alonso Álvarez, Fernández Vilaboa and González Rodríguez. Informally, it is a condition given for a module which is weaker than the usual cocommutativity.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra in
{\sf C}
. A left
H
-module
(M,\varphi_{M})
belongs to the cocommutativity class of
H
if it satisfies the following equality:

(9)

\displaystyle(\varphi_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)=(\varphi_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes M).

Note that, thanks to the symmetric character of
{\sf C}
, (
9
) is equivalent to

(10)

\displaystyle(H\otimes\varphi_{M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)=(H\otimes\varphi_{M})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes M).

Obviously, in the case that
H
is a cocommutative Hopf algebra, (
9
) automatically holds.

This condition arises naturally in a variety of contexts as will be shown in the following examples.

Example 2.12
.

The cocommutativity class turns out to be a necessary condition for the smash product algebra to be a Hopf algebra. More precisely, let
A
and
H
be Hopf algebras such that
(A,\varphi_{A})
is a left
H
-module algebra. If, in addition,
(A,\varphi_{A})
is a left
H
-module coalgebra satisfying (
9
), then

A\sharp H=(A\otimes H,\eta_{A}\otimes\eta_{H},\mu_{A\sharp H},\varepsilon_{A}\otimes\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{A\otimes H},\lambda_{A\sharp H})

is a Hopf algebra where
\lambda_{A\sharp H}\coloneqq\Psi^{H}_{A}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes\lambda_{A})\circ c_{A,H}.

Note that, when
{\sf C}={\sf Set}
, any Hopf algebra is a group. Consequently, in this setting, the smash product Hopf algebra
\sharp
coincides with the well-known semidirect product group
\ltimes
.

Example 2.13
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra whose antipode
\lambda_{H}
is an isomorphism in
{\sf C}
. The cocommutativity class also characterizes when the adjoint action of
H
is a coalgebra morphism, i.e.,
\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}}
is a coalgebra morphism if and only if
(H,\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}})
belongs to the cocommutativity class of

.

that
\varepsilon_{H}\circ\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H}.
Secondly, for the adjoint action the following identity

(11)

,

\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}}

=(\mu_{H}\otimes H)\circ(\mu_{H}\otimes((\lambda_{H}\otimes\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)))\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})

(by coassociativity of

\delta_{H}
and naturality of

)

By composing the previous equality on the right with
((\lambda_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\mu_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes((\lambda_{H}\otimes\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H)))\circ(c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes\delta_{H})\\

Besides, by composing on the right with

and then using the naturality of

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\mu_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes((\lambda_{H}\otimes\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H)\circ c_{H,H}))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\\

since

As a result, if we compose the equality (

((\lambda_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H

\displaystyle(\lambda_{H}\otimes\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H)\circ c_{H,H}=(\lambda_{H}\otimes\varphi_{H}^{{\rm ad}})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\circ c_{H,H},

which is equivalent to (

because

and

are isomorphisms.

Observe that, in order to show that

is a coalgebra morphism if

is in the cocommutativity class of

is an isomorphism. However, this assumption is not overly restrictive, since it holds when

is profinite

12

or whenever the Hopf algebra

is commutative or cocommutative thanks to (

Definition 2.14
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
A
an algebra in
{\sf C}
. A right
H
-comodule

is said to be a right

-comodule algebra if
\eta_{A}
and
\mu_{A}
are
H
-colinear morphisms, that is, if the following equalities

(14)

\displaystyle\rho_{A}\circ\eta_{A}=\eta_{A}\otimes\eta_{H},

(15)

\displaystyle\rho_{A}\circ\mu_{A}=(\mu_{A}\otimes H)\circ\rho_{A\otimes A},

hold, where
\rho_{A\otimes A}\coloneqq(A\otimes A\otimes\mu_{H})\circ(A\otimes c_{H,A}\otimes H)\circ(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{A})
. One shows easily that equations (

) and (

\rho_{A}
is an algebra morphism.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and let
(A,\rho_{A})
be a right
H
-comodule algebra. We define the category of left-right
(A,H)
-Doi-Hopf modules as the one whose objects are triples
(M,\varphi_{M},\rho_{M})
such that
(M,\varphi_{M})
is a left
A
-module,
(M,\rho_{M})
is a right
H
-comodule and the following identity holds:

\rho_{M}\circ\varphi_{M}=(\varphi_{M}\otimes\mu_{H})\circ(A\otimes c_{H,M}\otimes H)\circ(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{M}).

The morphisms in this category are left
A
-linear and right
H
-colinear morphisms. In what follows we will denote it by
{}_{A}{\sf M}^{H}
.

If
H
is a profinite Hopf algebra and
(A,\varphi_{A})
is a left
H
-module algebra in
{\sf C}
, then
A
it is also a right
H^{\ast}
-comodule algebra via

\widehat{\rho}_{A}\coloneqq(\varphi_{A}\otimes H^{\ast})\circ(H\otimes c_{H^{\ast},A})\circ(a_{H}(K)\otimes A).

Dually, if
(A,\rho_{A})
is a right
H^{\ast}
-comodule algebra, then

is a left
H
-module algebra with the action

\widetilde{\varphi}_{A}\coloneqq(A\otimes b_{H}(K))\circ(\rho_{A}\otimes H)\circ c_{H,A}.

Therefore, if
(A,\varphi_{A})
is a left
H
-module algebra, then we have a functor

{\sf S}\colon{}_{A\sharp H}{\sf Mod}\longrightarrow{}_{A}{\sf M}^{H^{\ast}}

defined on objects by

{\sf S}((M,\psi_{M}))\coloneqq(M,\varphi_{M}^{\ast},\rho_{M}^{\ast}),

where
\varphi_{M}^{\ast}
,
\rho_{M}^{\ast}
are

\displaystyle\varphi_{M}^{\ast}\coloneqq\psi_{M}\circ(A\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes M),

\displaystyle\rho_{M}^{\ast}\coloneqq(\psi_{M}\otimes H^{\ast})\circ(A\otimes H\otimes c_{H^{\ast},M})\circ(\eta_{A}\otimes a_{H}(K)\otimes M),

and by the identity on morphisms. Also, there exists another functor

{\sf R}\colon{}_{A}{\sf M}^{H^{\ast}}\longrightarrow{}_{A\sharp H}{\sf Mod}

defined on objects by

{\sf R}((M,\varphi_{M},\rho_{M}))\coloneqq(M,\psi_{M}^{\sharp}\coloneqq(\varphi_{M}\otimes b_{H}(K))\circ(A\otimes((\rho_{M}\otimes H)\circ c_{H,M}))),

and by the identity on morphisms. These functors are mutually inverse, leading to the following theorem:

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
H
be a profinite Hopf algebra in
C
. If
(A,\varphi_{A})
is a left
H
-module algebra, then the categories

{}_{A}{\sf M}^{H^{\ast}}
and
{}_{A\sharp H}{\sf Mod}
are isomorphic.

The previous theorem is a monoidal version, with different sides in the category of Hopf modules, of a result proved by Fischman in her doctoral thesis, whose proof can be found in
[
5
, Proposition 1.6]
.

We now introduce the notion of a Hopf brace in a general monoidal framework. These objects have been introduced by Angiono, Galindo and Vendramin
[
2
]
in the vector space setting as the quantum version of a skew brace
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
(H,\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H})
be a coalgebra in
C
and let us assume that
H
admits two different algebra structures in
C
:
(H,\eta_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{1})
and
(H,\eta_{H}^{2},\mu_{H}^{2})
. We will say that a 9-tuple

(H,\eta_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{1},\eta_{H}^{2},\mu_{H}^{2},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H}^{1},\lambda_{H}^{2})

is a Hopf brace in
C
if the following requirements hold:

(i)

H_{1}=(H,\eta_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{1},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H}^{1})
is a Hopf algebra in
C
.

(ii)

H_{2}=(H,\eta_{H}^{2},\mu_{H}^{2},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H}^{2})
is a Hopf algebra in
C
.

(iii)

The following identity involving the products
\mu_{H}^{1}
and
\mu_{H}^{2}
holds:

(16)

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{2}\circ(H\otimes\mu_{H}^{1})=\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\mu_{H}^{2}\otimes\Gamma_{H_{1}})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H),

where

(17)

\Gamma_{H_{1}}\coloneqq\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H).

Following the notation introduced in
[
8
]
, we will denote Hopf braces by
\mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2})
or, when there is no confusion between the Hopf algebras involved, only by
\mathbb{H}
. A Hopf brace
\mathbb{H}
is said to be cocommutative if
c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H}=\delta_{H}
.

These structures constitute a category whose morphisms are defined as follows: Given another Hopf brace
\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},B_{2})
, a morphism
f\colon\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}
is a morphism of Hopf braces if
f\colon H_{1}\rightarrow B_{1}
and
f\colon H_{2}\rightarrow B_{2}
are both Hopf algebra morphisms in
{\sf C}
. The category of Hopf braces will be denoted by
{\sf HBr}
. Cocommutative Hopf braces form a full subcategory of
{\sf HBr}
which we will denote by
{\sf cocHBr}
.

Example 2.18

(Skew braces)
.

Since a Hopf algebra in the symmetric monoidal category of sets,
Set
, is just a group, particularizing the previous notion of a Hopf brace with
{\sf C}={\sf Set}
yields the notion of a skew brace, in which we have a pair of groups,
(G,\cdot)
and
(G,\circ)
, satisfying the equation

a\circ(b\cdot c)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ c),\textnormal{ for all }a,b,c\in G,

which corresponds to (
16
) in this setting. We will denote skew braces by
(G,\cdot,\circ)
and, if we want to refer only to the first group structure involved, we will identify
(G,\cdot)=G_{\cdot}
, while for the second we will use
(G,\circ)=G_{\circ}
. If
G_{\cdot}
is an abelian group, then a skew brace is a brace in the sense of Rump
[
15
]
.

Example 2.19
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field. By linearization, it is well know that if
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a skew brace, then the respective group algebras of
G_{\cdot}
and
G_{\circ}
,
\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}]
and
\mathbb{K}[G_{\circ}]
, constitute a Hopf brace in
{\sf C}={}_{\mathbb{K}}{\sf Vect}
, denoted by
\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{G}]=(\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}],\mathbb{K}[G_{\circ}])
.

Example 2.20
.

Any Hopf algebra
H=(H,\eta_{H},\mu_{H},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H})
leads to a trivial Hopf brace in
{\sf C}
:
\mathbb{H}_{{\rm triv}}=(H,H)
. That is because, when both Hopf algebras coincide in a Hopf brace structure, the compatibility condition (
16
) always holds.

Given a Hopf brace
\mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2})
, it is satisfied that
\eta_{H}^{1}=\eta_{H}^{2}

[
2
, Remark 1.3]
. Therefore, from now on we will denote both units by
\eta_{H}
. Besides,
(H_{1},\Gamma_{H_{1}})
is a left
H_{2}
-module algebra
[
2
, Lemma 1.8]
and, reciprocally, this property characterizes the Hopf brace structure. Indeed, given a pair of Hopf algebras sharing the same underlying coalgebra,
H_{1}=(H,\eta_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{1},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H}^{1})
and

, then

\mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2})\in{\sf HBr}\iff\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\Gamma_{H_{1}}\otimes\Gamma_{H_{1}})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)=\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(H\otimes\mu_{H}^{1}),

as can be consulted in
[
9

. Moreover, by (

\delta_{H}
, associativity of
\mu_{H}^{1}
and (
4

, we obtain the following expression for

:

(18)

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{2}=\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(H\otimes\Gamma_{H_{1}})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H).

Furthermore, the cocommutativity class also provides a sufficient condition for
\Gamma_{H_{1}}
to be a coalgebra morphism.

Theorem 2.21
.

Let
\mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2})\in{\sf HBr}

(H_{1},\Gamma_{H_{1}})
belongs to the cocommutativity class of

It can be easily verified that

\Gamma_{H_{1}}
preserves the coproduct. Indeed,

\hskip 10.81218pt\delta_{H}\circ\Gamma_{H_{1}}

=(\mu_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{1})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ((\delta_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}^{1})\otimes(\delta_{H}\circ\mu_{H}^{2}))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)

(by the condition of coalgebra

morphism for
\mu_{H}^{1}

\hskip 10.81218pt\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)

and (

By the naturality of

\delta_{H}
and the associativity of

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\mu_{H}^{2}\otimes\Gamma_{H_{1}})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H),

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{2}\circ(H\otimes\mu_{H}^{1})=\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H).

The properties satisfied by

are very similar to those satisfied by
\Gamma_{H_{1}}

\mu_{H}^{2}
admits the following expression in terms of

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{2}=\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H).

Besides,

-module algebra

Let
H_{1}

\hskip 10.81218pt\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)

3.
From modules over a Hopf brace to modules over an algebra

The weakest notion for the category of modules over a Hopf brace that can be found in the literature is the one introduced by González Rodríguez in
[
8
]
in order to prove the Fundamental Theorem of Hopf modules in the Hopf brace setting.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2})
be a Hopf brace in
C
. A left module over
\mathbb{H}
is a triple
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2})
where
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})
is a left
H_{1}
-module,
(M,\varphi_{M}^{2})
is a left
H_{2}
-module and the compatibility condition

(22)

\displaystyle\varphi_{M}^{2}\circ(H\otimes\varphi_{M}^{1})=\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(\mu_{H}^{2}\otimes\Gamma_{M})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes M)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M)

holds, where
\Gamma_{M}
is defined by

(23)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{M}\coloneqq\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{1}\otimes\varphi_{M}^{2})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M).

Given left
\mathbb{H}
-modules
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2})
and
(N,\varphi_{N}^{1},\varphi_{N}^{2})
, a morphism

f\colon(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2})\rightarrow(N,\varphi_{N}^{1},\varphi_{N}^{2})

is said to be a morphism of left
\mathbb{H}
-modules if
f\colon(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})\rightarrow(N,\varphi_{N}^{1})
is
H_{1}
-linear and
f\colon(M,\varphi_{M}^{2})\rightarrow(N,\varphi_{N}^{2})
is
H_{2}
-linear. Left
{\mathbb{H}}
-modules constitute a category which we will denote by
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that, by the naturality of
c
, the coassociativity of
\delta_{H}
and (
2
) for
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(\mu_{H}^{2}\otimes\Gamma_{M})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes M)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes\varphi_{M}^{2})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes M)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M),

where
\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}
is defined as in (

(24)

\displaystyle\varphi_{M}^{2}\circ(H\otimes\varphi_{M}^{1})=\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes\varphi_{M}^{2})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes M)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M).

Example 3.3
.

Given
\mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2})

Let
H=(H,\eta_{H},\mu_{H},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H})

{\sf C}
and consider
\mathbb{H}_{\textnormal{triv}}=(H,H)

-actions together with equality (

\mathbb{H}
-module. To obtain such conditions, let us define the category of left

-module whose action

-modules, where

-modules. This category is isomorphic to

\varphi_{M}^{2}\coloneqq\varphi_{M}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M),
and on morphisms by the identity.

Proof.

In the development of this proof, we will use repeatedly the following identity:

(32)

\displaystyle\Psi_{H_{1}}^{H_{2}}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H)=H\otimes\eta_{H},

whose justification is given below:

\hskip 10.81218pt\Psi_{H_{1}}^{H_{2}}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H)

=(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ((\delta_{H}\circ\eta_{H})\otimes H)

=((\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes H)\circ c_{H,H}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H)

(by the condition of coalgebra morphism for
\eta_{H}
)

=H\otimes\eta_{H}

(by (
1
) for
(H_{1},\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}})
and the naturality of
c
)
.

Let us start the proof by showing that
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})
is a left
H_{1}
-module. On the one hand, condition (
1
) is straightforward by (
1
) for
(M,\varphi_{M})
, whereas, on the other hand, condition (
2
) is a consequence of

\hskip 10.81218pt\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(H\otimes\varphi_{M}^{1})

=\varphi_{M}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes(\varphi_{M}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes M)))

=\varphi_{M}\circ(((\mu_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(H\otimes(\Psi_{H_{1}}^{H_{2}}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes\eta_{H}))\otimes M)

(by (
2
) for
(M,\varphi_{M})
)

=\varphi_{M}\circ(\mu_{H}^{1}\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes M)

(by (
32
) and the unit property for
H_{2}
)

=\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(\mu_{H}^{1}\otimes M).

By similar arguments,
(M,\varphi_{M}^{2})
is also a left
H_{2}
-module. Then, to conclude that
{\sf G}
is well-defined on objects, it only remains to show that (
24
) holds for
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2})
. Developing right hand side of (
24
), we obtain that

\hskip 10.81218pt\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes\varphi_{M}^{2})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes M)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M)

=\varphi_{M}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes M)\circ(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes(\varphi_{M}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M)))\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes M)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M)

=\varphi_{M}\circ(((\mu_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(H\otimes(\Psi_{H_{1}}^{H_{2}}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}))\otimes H)\circ(\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes M)

(by (
2
) for
(M,\varphi_{M})
)

=\varphi_{M}\circ(((\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes M)

(by (
32
) and the unit property for
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
)

whereas, from the left hand side of (
24
) it is obtained that

\hskip 10.81218pt\varphi_{M}^{2}\circ(H\otimes\varphi_{M}^{1})

=\varphi_{M}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H\otimes(\varphi_{M}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes M)))

=\varphi_{M}\circ(((\mu_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes\Psi_{H_{1}}^{H_{2}}\otimes\eta_{H}))\otimes M)

(by (
2
) for
(M,\varphi_{M})
)

=\varphi_{M}\circ(\Psi_{H_{1}}^{H_{2}}\otimes M)

(by the unit property for
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
)

=\varphi_{M}\circ(((\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes M)
.

That is, computing both sides of (
24
) yields the same result, and hence (
24
) holds for
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2})
.

Regarding morphisms,
{\sf G}
is well-defined because, if
f\colon(M,\varphi_{M})\rightarrow(N,\varphi_{N})
is a morphism in
{}_{H_{1}\sharp H_{2}}{\sf Mod}
, then

f\colon(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})\rightarrow(N,\varphi_{N}^{1})
is
H_{1}
-linear. Indeed,

\hskip 10.81218ptf\circ\varphi_{M}^{1}

=f\circ\varphi_{M}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes M)

=\varphi_{N}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes f)

(by (
3
) for
f\colon(M,\varphi_{M})\rightarrow(N,\varphi_{N})
)

=\varphi_{N}^{1}\circ(H\otimes f)
.

Similar arguments can be followed to prove that
f\colon(M,\varphi_{M}^{2})\rightarrow(N,\varphi_{N}^{2})
is also
H_{2}
-linear.
∎

The functors constructed in the previous lemmas yield the required isomorphism of categories.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be a Hopf brace in
{\sf C}
. The categories
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}
and
{}_{H_{1}\sharp H_{2}}{\sf Mod}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

First, it is easy to obtain that
{\sf G}\circ{\sf F}={\sf id}_{{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}}
. Indeed, if
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2})\in{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}
, then

({\sf G}\circ{\sf F})((M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2}))={\sf G}((M,\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(H\otimes\varphi_{M}^{2})))=(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2}),

where the last equality is direct by (
1
) for
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})
and
(M,\varphi_{M}^{2})
.

Converserly, if
(M,\varphi_{M})\in{}_{H_{1}\sharp H_{2}}{\sf Mod}
, then

\displaystyle({\sf F}\circ{\sf G})((M,\varphi_{M}))=\,

\displaystyle{\sf F}((M,\varphi_{M}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes M),\varphi_{M}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M)))

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(M,\varphi_{M}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes(\varphi_{M}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M)))),

and

\hskip 10.81218pt\varphi_{M}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes(\varphi_{M}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M)))

=\varphi_{M}\circ(((\mu_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(H\otimes(\Psi^{H_{2}}_{H_{1}}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}))\otimes H))\otimes M)

(by (
2
) for
(M,\varphi_{M})
)

=\varphi_{M}

(by (
32
) and the unit property for
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
)
.

Therefore,
{\sf F}\circ{\sf G}={\sf id}_{H_{1}\sharp H_{2}},
what concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be a profinite Hopf brace in
{\sf C}
. The categories
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}
,
{}_{H_{1}\sharp H_{2}}{\sf Mod}
and
\;{}_{H_{1}}{\sf M}^{H_{2}^{\ast}}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

The proof follows from the previous theorem and Theorem
2.16
.
∎

Hence, as a consequence of the previous corollary, modules over a profinite Hopf brace
\mathbb{H}
can be seen as Doi-Hopf modules, more concretely, as left-right
(H_{1},H_{2}^{\ast})
-Doi-Hopf modules.

On the other hand, note that, as was pointed out in Example
2.12
, if
(H_{1},\Gamma^{\prime}_{H_{1}})
lies in the cocommutativity class of
H_{2}
, then
H_{1}\sharp H_{2}
is a Hopf algebra in
{\sf C}
. Therefore, under such condition, modules over
\mathbb{H}
are identified with modules over the Hopf algebra
H_{1}\sharp H_{2}
.

Example 3.13
.

In Example
2.18
we have seen that, if
{\sf C}={\sf Set}
, any Hopf brace is a skew brace. Hence, as a particular case of Theorem
3.11
with
{\sf C}={\sf Set}
, the following is obtained: If
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a skew brace, then the category of modules over
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is isomorphic to the category of modules over the semidirect product group
G_{\cdot}\ltimes G_{\circ}
,

{}_{(G,\cdot,\circ)}{\sf Mod}\cong{}_{G_{\cdot}\ltimes G_{\circ}}{\sf Mod},

where an object in
{}_{(G,\cdot,\circ)}{\sf Mod}
is a triple
(M,\rhd,\blacktriangleright)
such that
(M,\rhd)
is a module over the group
G_{\cdot}
and
(M,\blacktriangleright)
is a module over the group
G_{\circ}
satisfying the following identity:

g\blacktriangleright(h\rhd m)=((g\circ h)\cdot g^{-1})\rhd(g\blacktriangleright m),\textnormal{ for all }g,h\in G\textnormal{ and }m\in M.

As we observed in Example
2.19
, if
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a skew brace, then
\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{G}]=(\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}],\mathbb{K}[G_{\circ}])
forms a Hopf brace in
\;{}_{\mathbb{K}}{\sf Vect}
. Therefore, again by Theorem
3.11
, we obtain that the category of modules over
\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{G}]
is isomorphic to the category of modules over the Hopf algebra
\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}]\sharp\mathbb{K}[G_{\circ}]
. As was pointed out in
[
13
, Example 3.14]
(see also
[
16
]
),
\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}]\sharp\mathbb{K}[G_{\circ}]=\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}\ltimes G_{\circ}]
. Thus, we obtain the following chain of categorical isomorphisms:

{}_{\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{G}]}{\sf Mod}\cong{}_{\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}]\sharp\mathbb{K}[G_{\circ}]}{\sf Mod}={}_{\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}\ltimes G_{\circ}]}{\sf Mod}.

These chain of isomorphisms extend
[
11
, Remarks 2.2 and 3.2]
, where the authors only construct a functor from
{}_{\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{G}]}{\sf Mod}
to
{}_{\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}\ltimes G_{\circ}]}{\sf Mod}
, but they do not find the equivalence.

Now, by Corollary
3.12
, if we assume that
G
is a finite group, then it is also obtained that

{}_{\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{G}]}{\sf Mod}\cong{}_{\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}\ltimes G_{\circ}]}{\sf Mod}\cong{}_{\mathbb{K}[G_{\cdot}]}{\sf M}^{{\mathbb{K}[G_{\circ}]}^{\ast}},

extending the chain above one step further.

4.
On the notion of modules over a Hopf brace in Zhu’s sense

An alternative definition of left module over a Hopf brace, different from the one provided in the previous section, was given by Zhu in
[
18
, Definition 3.1]
. To avoid confusion with the notion considered in Section
3
, we will henceforth refer to these structures as
{\sf Z}
-modules, where this name comes from the fact that these are modules in the sense of Zhu. The definition is as follows:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2})
be a Hopf brace in
{\sf C}
. A left
{\sf Z}
-module over
\mathbb{H}
is a triple
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2})
where
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})
is a left
H_{1}
-module,
(M,\varphi_{M}^{2})
is a left
H_{2}
-module and the equality

(33)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\varphi_{M}^{2}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\varphi_{M}^{1})\\
=\,&(\varphi_{M}^{1}\otimes H)\circ(\mu_{H}^{2}\otimes((\Gamma_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes M)))\\
&\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes M)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes M)\end{split}

holds.

A morphism of left
{\sf Z}
-modules over
\mathbb{H}
is defined as in the case of Definition
3.1
and we will denote the corresponding category by
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}^{{\sf Z}}
.

As was proved in
[
18
, Lemma 3.2]
, given a Hopf brace
\mathbb{H}=(H_{1},H_{2})
in
{\sf C}
, if
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})
is a left
H_{1}
-module and
(M,\varphi_{M}^{2})
is a left
H_{2}
-module, then equality (
33
) is equivalent to equalities (
22
) and

(34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\varphi_{M}^{2}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)\\
=\,&(\varphi_{M}^{1}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes((\Gamma_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes M)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M).\end{split}

As a consequence,
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}^{{\sf Z}}
is a full subcategory of
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}
. In general, these two categories are different since
(H,\mu_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{2})
is an example of module over
\mathbb{H}
(see Example
3.3
), but it is not in the sense of Definition
4.1
because the triple
(H,\mu_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{2})
does not satisfy condition (
34
). However in
[
18
, Remark 3.3]
, Zhu observes that under cocommutativity conditions, equality (
34
) is trivial, so this would mean that both notions would be equivalent. However, it is not necessary to be that restrictive. The following result shows the conditions under which an object in
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}
satisfies condition (
34
), and therefore it is an object in the category
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}^{{\sf Z}}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
be a Hopf brace in
{\sf C}
. Given
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2})
an object in the category
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}
, then
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1},\varphi_{M}^{2})
is an object in
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}^{{\sf Z}}
if and only if
(M,\Gamma_{M})
belongs to the cocommutativity class of
H_{2}
.

Proof.

Let us assume that (
28
) holds for
(M,\Gamma_{M})
. Then, the following is obtained:

\hskip 10.81218pt(\varphi_{M}^{1}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes((\Gamma_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes M)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)

=(\varphi_{M}^{1}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes((\Gamma_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)

(by (
28
) for
(M,\Gamma_{M})
)

=((\varphi_{M}^{1}\circ(H\otimes\Gamma_{M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M))\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)

(by coassociativity of
\delta_{H}
)

=(\varphi_{M}^{2}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)

(by (
25
))
,

that is, (
34
) holds.

Conversely, if (
34
) holds, we obtain

\hskip 10.81218pt(\Gamma_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)

=(\varphi_{M}^{1}\otimes H)\circ(\lambda_{H}^{1}\otimes((\varphi_{M}^{2}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)

(by coassociativitiy of
\delta_{H}
)

=(\varphi_{M}^{1}\otimes H)\circ(\lambda_{H}^{1}\otimes((\varphi_{M}^{1}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes((\Gamma_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes M)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)))

\hskip 10.81218pt\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)
(by (
34
))
.

=(\varphi_{M}^{1}\otimes H)\circ((\lambda_{H}^{1}\ast{\rm id}_{H})\otimes((\Gamma_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes M)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes M)

(by

coassociativitiy of
\delta_{H}
and (
2
) for
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})
)

=(\Gamma_{M}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,M})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes M)

(by (
4
) for
H_{1}
, the counit property and (
1
) for
(M,\varphi_{M}^{1})
),

i.e.,
(M,\Gamma_{M})
is in the cocommutativity class of
H_{2}
.
∎

Therefore, modules in the sense of Zhu are nothing but modules for which
\Gamma_{M}
lies in the cocommutativity class, providing a significantly simpler characterization of the category
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}^{{\sf Z}}
than that given by conditions (
33
) and (
34
). Thus, as an immediate consequence of the previous theorem, we have the following corollaries:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
be a Hopf brace in
{\sf C}
. Then,
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}^{{\sf Z}}={}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}^{{\sf cc}}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
be a Hopf brace in
{\sf C}
. The triple
(H,\mu_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{2})
is an object in
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}^{{\sf Z}}
if and only if
(H_{1},\Gamma_{H_{1}})
belongs to the cocommutativity class of
H_{2}
.

Remark 4.5
.

The previous result was previously obtained in
[
7
, Remark 2.9]
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
be a cocommutative Hopf brace. Then
{}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}^{{\sf Z}}={}_{\mathbb{H}}{\sf Mod}
.

Remark 4.7
.

The content of the previous corollary corresponds to the observation made in
[
8
, Remark 2.1]
.
