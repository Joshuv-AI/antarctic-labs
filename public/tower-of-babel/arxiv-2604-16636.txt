Hochschild cohomology and lifts of endomorphisms
Niels Lauritzen and Jesper Funch Thomsen

Abstract
. We study when algebra endomorphisms can be lifted to first–order flat lifts. To a first–order flat lift of an algebra and an endomorphism, we associate a canonical class in Hochschild cohomology with coefficients in a naturally twisted bimodule. The cohomology class vanishes exactly when the endomorphism admits a multiplicative lift. For an Azumaya algebra of constant rank over a formally smooth center, we prove that an endomorphism lifts if and only if the induced endomorphism of the center preserves the Poisson structure given by the lift of the algebra.



1.
Introduction

A fundamental theme in deformation quantization is the interplay between
a noncommutative algebra and the Poisson geometry of its semiclassical limit.
In
[
1
]
, Belov-Kanel and Kontsevich
conjectured that the automorphism group of the
n
-th Weyl algebra
A_{n}(\mathbb{C})

is canonically isomorphic to the group of polynomial symplectomorphisms
of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
, equipped with its standard Poisson structure.
Their key technique proceeds by
reducing the Weyl algebra to positive characteristic
p
,
where
A_{n}(k)
becomes an Azumaya algebra whose center

Z\cong k[x_{1}^{p},\ldots,x_{n}^{p},\operatorname{{\partial}}_{1}^{p},\ldots,\operatorname{{\partial}}_{n}^{p}]

is a polynomial algebra. Here
A_{n}(k)
admits
a canonical (flat) lift to
A_{n}(W_{2}(k))
— the
Weyl algebra over the Witt vectors
W_{2}(k)
of length two. The existence of a lift of
a Weyl algebra endomorphism
f
of
A_{n}(k)
to
A_{n}(W_{2}(k))
shows that the

f
is a symplectomorphism when restricted to the center.
Belov-Kanel and Kontsevich exploit this structure to construct a homomorphism from the automorphism
group of the Weyl algebra to the group of Poisson automorphisms of the center.
The same circle of ideas, developed independently by Tsuchimoto
[
5
]
,
also establishes the stable equivalence of the Jacobian and Dixmier conjectures
[
2
]
.

In
[
4
]
we work entirely over a perfect positive characteristic field
k
and show that
a
k
-algebra endorphism of
A_{n}(k)
admits a lift to
A_{n}(W_{2}(k))

if and only if it preserves the canonical Poisson bracket on the center.
The goal of this paper is to generalize that result in the framework of

Hochschild cohomology
.

We work in the following general setup.
For any field
k
and any
k
-algebra
A
, an endomorphism
f
of
A
together with a first-order flat lift of
A
determines a canonical class in Hochschild cohomology of the bimodule
A

twisted by
f
from the left and right; after choosing an
R
-linear lift of
f
, this class is represented by a natural
2
-cocycle.
This cohomology class vanishes if and only if
the endomorphism lifts to an endomorphism of the lift of
A
.
Our main observation is that if
f
preserves the center and the
Poisson bracket on the center given by the lift of
A
, then the restriction to the center of the associated
2
-cocycle is symmetric.

If
A
is an Azumaya algebra of constant rank over a formally smooth center
Z
in this setup, we prove in Theorem
4.6
that
f
admits
a lift if and only if
f
preserves the Poisson bracket on
Z
. The condition
f(Z)\subseteq Z
is automatic
in this case — see Proposition
4.4
for the general statement.

We thank M. Bökstedt for patiently answering our questions about Hochschild cohomology.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

In the following,
k
will denote a field and
A
a
k
-algebra.
We denote by
A^{\operatorname{o}}
the opposite algebra of
A

and put
A^{e}=A\otimes_{k}A^{\operatorname{o}}
. The bar complex of
A
is the complex
(B_{n}(A),d_{n})
of
free
A^{e}
-modules given by

B_{n}(A)=A^{\otimes_{k}(n+2)}
and

d_{n}(a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\otimes a_{n+1})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}

for
n\geq 0
. It is a free resolution of
A
by the multiplication map
B_{0}(A)\rightarrow A
. For an

A
-bimodule
M
, there is a canonical
k
-isomorphism

(2.0.1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{e}}(B_{n}(A),M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(A^{\otimes_{k}n},M)=:C^{n}(A/k,M)

given by
h\mapsto(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\mapsto h(1\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\otimes 1))
for
n\geq 0
.
The complex
C^{\bullet}(A/k,M)
is called
the
Hochschild complex
of
M
. It has differentials given by

\displaystyle(\delta^{n}f)(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle=a_{1}\cdot f(a_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\cdot a_{n+1}

for
n\geq 0
.
The
n
-th Hochschild cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
M
is defined as

\operatorname{HH}^{n}(A/k,M):=H^{n}(C^{\bullet}(A/k,M))

for
n\geq 0
. The isomorphisms (
2.0.1
) is a morphism of complexes and we have the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

\operatorname{HH}^{i}(A/k,M)=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A^{e}}(A,M).

We call an
A
-bimodule
M
,

B
-diagonal if
bm=mb
for every
b\in B
, where
B
is a subalgebra of
A
.

3.
First order flat lifts of endomorphisms

Let
R
be a commutative local ring with non-zero principal maximal ideal
pR
such that
p^{2}=0
, and let
k=R/pR
be its residue field.
The map
k\to pR
given by
r+pR\mapsto pr
is a well-defined isomorphism; we denote its inverse by
p^{-1}:pR\to k
.

Let
A
be a
k
-algebra. By a first-order flat lift of
A
we mean a flat
R
-algebra
\widetilde{A}
together with an identification

\widetilde{A}\otimes_{R}k\cong A
. We let
\pi:\widetilde{A}\rightarrow A
denote the reduction map, and for
a\in A
we write
\tilde{a}\in\widetilde{A}
for an arbitrary lift of
a
.
Since
p^{2}=0
, the
\widetilde{A}
-bimodule
p\widetilde{A}=\ker(\pi)
is annihilated by
p
, so its left and right
\widetilde{A}
-actions factor through
A=\widetilde{A}/p\widetilde{A}
; hence
p\widetilde{A}
is naturally an
A
-bimodule.
Flatness ensures that multiplication by
p

induces an
A
-bimodule isomorphism between
A
and
p\widetilde{A}
. Explicitly, its inverse is given by:

p^{-1}:p\widetilde{A}\longrightarrow A,\quad p\tilde{a}\longmapsto\pi(\tilde{a})=a,

and we denote by
\iota:A\to p\widetilde{A}
the inverse map
a\mapsto p\tilde{a}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Since
R
is artinian local, every flat
R
-module is free. In particular,
\widetilde{A}
is a free
R
-module.
In fact, if
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a
k
-basis for
A
, then
\{\tilde{e}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
forms an
R
-basis for
\widetilde{A}
.

Now, consider a
k
-algebra homomorphism
f:A\to A
and an
R
-linear first order lift
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{A}\to\widetilde{A}
(satisfying
\pi\circ\widetilde{f}=f\circ\pi
).
We define the multiplicative defect
D:\widetilde{A}\times\widetilde{A}\to\widetilde{A}
by:

D(x,y)=\widetilde{f}(x)\widetilde{f}(y)-\widetilde{f}(xy).

Because
p^{2}=0
and
\widetilde{f}
is
R
-linear, the defect is invariant under adding elements of
p\widetilde{A}
.
Consequently,
D
factors through the reduction map, yielding a well-defined map
L_{\widetilde{f}}:A\otimes_{k}A\to p\widetilde{A}
given by:

L_{\widetilde{f}}(a\otimes b):=D(\tilde{a},\tilde{b}).

Thus,
\widetilde{f}
is multiplicative (and hence an
R
-algebra homomorphism) if and only if
L_{\widetilde{f}}(a\otimes b)=0
for all
a,b\in A
.
If
\widetilde{f}
is multiplicative, then automatically
\widetilde{f}(1)=1
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f:A\to A
be a
k
-algebra homomorphism and
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{A}\to\widetilde{A}
an
R
-linear lift of
f
. The
k
-linear map
C_{\widetilde{f}}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(A\otimes_{k}A,A)
defined by

C_{\widetilde{f}}(a\otimes b):=p^{-1}L_{\widetilde{f}}(a\otimes b)

is a
2
-cocycle in
C^{2}(A/k,M)
, where
M=A
viewed as an
A
-bimodule via the twisted action

a\cdot m\cdot b:=f(a)mf(b)

for
m\in M
and
a,b\in A
.

The cohomology class
[C_{\widetilde{f}}]\in H^{2}(A/k,M)
is independent of the choice of the
R
-linear lift
\widetilde{f}
, and
[C_{\widetilde{f}}]=0
if and only if
f
admits a lift to an
R
-algebra homomorphism
\widehat{f}:\widetilde{A}\to\widetilde{A}
.

Proof.

By the definition of the Hochschild differential for the twisted bimodule
M
, we have for all
a,b,c\in A
:

\displaystyle(\delta^{2}C_{\widetilde{f}})(a\otimes b\otimes c)

\displaystyle=f(a)C_{\widetilde{f}}(b\otimes c)-C_{\widetilde{f}}(ab\otimes c)+C_{\widetilde{f}}(a\otimes bc)-C_{\widetilde{f}}(a\otimes b)f(c)

\displaystyle=p^{-1}\Big(\widetilde{f}(\tilde{a})L_{\widetilde{f}}(b\otimes c)-L_{\widetilde{f}}(ab\otimes c)+L_{\widetilde{f}}(a\otimes bc)-L_{\widetilde{f}}(a\otimes b)\widetilde{f}(\tilde{c})\Big).

Expanding
L_{\widetilde{f}}
via the multiplicative defect
D
, the term inside the parenthesis becomes:

\displaystyle\quad\,\widetilde{f}(\tilde{a})\big(\widetilde{f}(\tilde{b})\widetilde{f}(\tilde{c})-\widetilde{f}(\tilde{b}\tilde{c})\big)-\big(\widetilde{f}(\tilde{a}\tilde{b})\widetilde{f}(\tilde{c})-\widetilde{f}(\tilde{a}\tilde{b}\tilde{c})\big)

\displaystyle+\big(\widetilde{f}(\tilde{a})\widetilde{f}(\tilde{b}\tilde{c})-\widetilde{f}(\tilde{a}\tilde{b}\tilde{c})\big)-\big(\widetilde{f}(\tilde{a})\widetilde{f}(\tilde{b})-\widetilde{f}(\tilde{a}\tilde{b})\big)\widetilde{f}(\tilde{c}).

Due to the associativity of the algebra
\widetilde{A}
, all terms cancel pairwise, yielding
(\delta^{2}C_{\widetilde{f}})(a\otimes b\otimes c)=0
. Thus,
C_{\widetilde{f}}
is a
2
-cocycle.

Now, suppose
\widetilde{f}_{1}
and
\widetilde{f}_{2}
are two
R
-linear lifts of
f
. Their difference
\widetilde{f}_{2}-\widetilde{f}_{1}
maps into
p\widetilde{A}
and therefore defines a
k
-linear map
h:A\to A
via:

(\widetilde{f}_{2}-\widetilde{f}_{1})(\tilde{a})=\iota(h(a)).

Direct substitution shows that

C_{\widetilde{f}_{2}}(a\otimes b)-C_{\widetilde{f}_{1}}(a\otimes b)=f(a)h(b)-h(ab)+h(a)f(b)=(\delta^{1}h)(a\otimes b).

Hence,
C_{\widetilde{f}_{2}}-C_{\widetilde{f}_{1}}
is a coboundary, meaning the cohomology class
[C_{\widetilde{f}}]
is independent of the lift.

Finally, if
f
lifts to an
R
-algebra homomorphism
\widehat{f}:\widetilde{A}\to\widetilde{A}
, then
L_{\widehat{f}}=0
, making
C_{\widehat{f}}=0
and thus
[C_{\widetilde{f}}]=0
. Conversely, suppose
[C_{\widetilde{f}}]=0
. Then
C_{\widetilde{f}}=\delta^{1}h
for some
1
-cochain
h\in\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(A,A)
, meaning:

C_{\widetilde{f}}(a\otimes b)=f(a)h(b)-h(ab)+h(a)f(b)\quad\forall a,b\in A.

We define a new
R
-linear lift
\widehat{f}:\widetilde{A}\to\widetilde{A}
by correcting
\widetilde{f}
with
h
:

\widehat{f}(x):=\widetilde{f}(x)-\iota(h(\pi(x))).

One readily verifies that
L_{\widehat{f}}(a\otimes b)=0
for all
a,b\in A
, proving that
\widehat{f}
is the desired
R
-algebra homomorphism.
∎

It is well known that a first order flat lift of
A
gives rise to a Poisson bracket on the
center of
A
. This is very helpful in studying the cocycle defined in Proposition
3.2
.

3.1.
Restriction of the cocycle to the center

Let
Z
be the center of
A
. For
z_{1},z_{2}\in Z
,

\{z_{1},z_{2}\}=p^{-1}([\tilde{z}_{1},\tilde{z}_{2}])=p^{-1}(\tilde{z}_{1}\tilde{z}_{2}-\tilde{z}_{2}\tilde{z}_{1})

defines a Poisson bracket on the center
Z

of
A
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that
f(Z)\subseteq Z
and let
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{A}\rightarrow\widetilde{A}
be an
R
-linear lift of

f
. Let
c\in C^{2}(Z/k,M)
be the restriction of
C_{\widetilde{f}}
to
Z^{\otimes_{k}2}
. Then
c
is a
2
-cocycle and

c(x\otimes y)-c(y\otimes x)=\{f(x),f(y)\}-f(\{x,y\})

for
x,y\in Z
.

In particular,
the induced endomorphism
f:Z\rightarrow Z
preserves
the Poisson bracket if and only if the
2
-cocycle
c\in C^{2}(Z,M)

is symmetric, i.e.,

c(x\otimes y)=c(y\otimes x)
for
every
x,y\in Z
.

Proof.

Since the Hochschild differential commutes with restriction along
Z\hookrightarrow A
, the restriction
c
of
C_{\widetilde{f}}
is again a
2
-cocycle.

For
x,y\in Z
,

\displaystyle c(x\otimes y)-c(y\otimes x)=

\displaystyle p^{-1}(\widetilde{f}(\tilde{x})\widetilde{f}(\tilde{y})-\widetilde{f}(\tilde{x}\tilde{y})-\widetilde{f}(\tilde{y})\widetilde{f}(\tilde{x})+\widetilde{f}(\tilde{y}\tilde{x}))=

\displaystyle p^{-1}(\widetilde{f}(\tilde{x})\widetilde{f}(\tilde{y})-\widetilde{f}(\tilde{y})\widetilde{f}(\tilde{x})-\widetilde{f}(\tilde{x}\tilde{y}-\tilde{y}\tilde{x}))=

\displaystyle\{f(x),f(y)\}-f(\{x,y\})

This shows in particular that the antisymmetrization
c(x\otimes y)-c(y\otimes x)
depends only on
f
and the lift
\widetilde{A}
, and not on the chosen
R
-linear lift
\widetilde{f}
.
∎

Under favorable conditions, symmetric cocycles have trivial cohomology class in the Hochschild cohomology.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
Z/k
be a commutative algebra which is formally smooth over
k
in the category of commutative
k
-algebras, and let
M
be a diagonal
Z
-bimodule. Then every symmetric

2
-cocycle in the Hochschild complex
C^{\bullet}(Z/k,M)
is a
coboundary.

Proof.

We interpret
\operatorname{HH}^{2}(Z/k,M)
in terms of square-zero extensions. Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(Z^{\otimes 2},M)
be a
2
-cocycle.
This defines a
k
-algebra structure on the vector space direct sum
E=Z\oplus M
, with multiplication given by

(a,m)\cdot(b,n)=(ab,an+mb+\phi(a\otimes b)).

Because
\phi
is a Hochschild
2
-cocycle,
E
is an associative algebra, and the projection
E\to Z

is a surjective
k
-algebra homomorphism whose kernel
M
satisfies
M^{2}=0
.

Since
\phi
is symmetric (i.e.,
\phi(a\otimes b)=\phi(b\otimes a)
) and
M
is a diagonal
Z
-bimodule (so
am=ma
for all
a\in Z
and
m\in M
),
the multiplication on
E
is commutative. Thus,
E
is a commutative square-zero extension of
Z
by
M
.

By definition, the
k
-algebra
Z
is formally smooth if and only if every extension of
Z
by a nilpotent ideal in the category of commutative
k
-algebras splits. Therefore, there exists a
k
-algebra section
s\colon Z\to E
. This section must be of the form
s(a)=(a,\eta(a))
for some
k
-linear map
\eta\colon Z\to M
. Preserving multiplication requires

(ab,\eta(ab))=s(ab)=s(a)s(b)=(a,\eta(a))(b,\eta(b))=(ab,a\eta(b)+\eta(a)b+\phi(a\otimes b)),

which rearranges to

\phi(a\otimes b)=\eta(ab)-a\eta(b)-\eta(a)b.

This is precisely the statement that
\phi=-d\eta
in the Hochschild complex, meaning
\phi
is a coboundary.
∎

4.
Lifts of endomorphisms of Azumaya algebras

If
A
is a
k
-algebra and at the same time an Azumaya algebra over its center
Z
, then the multiplication map

\mu:A\otimes_{Z}A^{\operatorname{o}}\rightarrow A
admits an
A^{e}
-module splitting
s

and
A
is a finitely generated projective
Z
-module (see
[
3
, III, Théorème 5.1, Théorème 1.4]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

If
A
is an Azumaya algebra with center
Z
and

M
is an
A
-bimodule, then
the restriction map
C^{i}(A/k,M)\rightarrow C^{i}(Z/k,M)
induces an
injective map

\operatorname{HH}^{i}(A/k,M)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{HH}^{i}(Z/k,M)

on Hochschild cohomology.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda=A\otimes_{Z}A^{\operatorname{o}}
. The complex
P_{\bullet}:=A^{e}\otimes_{Z^{e}}B_{\bullet}(Z/k)
is a projective resolution of
A^{e}\otimes_{Z^{e}}Z\cong\Lambda
. The multiplication map
\mu:\Lambda\rightarrow A
lifts to an
A^{e}
-chain map
r_{\bullet}:P_{\bullet}\rightarrow B_{\bullet}(A/k)
given by

r_{n}((a\otimes b^{\operatorname{o}})\otimes z_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes z_{n+1})=az_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes z_{n+1}b.

By adjunction, we have an isomorphism of complexes

\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{e}}(P_{\bullet},M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{Z^{e}}(B_{\bullet}(Z/k),M)=C^{\bullet}(Z,M),

which upon taking cohomology yields a canonical isomorphism
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A^{e}}(\Lambda,M)\cong\operatorname{HH}^{i}(Z/k,M)
.

Under this identification, the map induced by
r_{\bullet}^{*}
on cohomology corresponds precisely to the restriction map

\operatorname{HH}^{i}(A/k,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{HH}^{i}(Z/k,M)
and
r_{\bullet}^{*}
computes the pullback
\mu^{*}:\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A^{e}}(A,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A^{e}}(\Lambda,M)

for
i\geq 0
.

Since
A
is an Azumaya algebra,
\mu
admits an
A^{e}
-module splitting
s
such that
\mu s=\operatorname{id}_{A}
. Therefore
s^{*}\mu^{*}=\operatorname{id}
, making
\mu^{*}
—and consequently the restriction map—injective.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Suppose
M
is a
Z
-diagonal and
s(1)=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes_{Z}A^{\operatorname{o}}
. Then the map
e_{M}:M\rightarrow M
given by
e_{M}(m)=\sum_{i}x_{i}my_{i}

is a well-defined
Z
-module projection onto
M^{A}=\{m\in M\mid am=ma,\text{for every }a\in A\}
. Then one may prove that the restriction map,
followed by the homomorphism induced by
e_{M}
, yields an
isomorphism

\operatorname{HH}^{i}(A/k,M)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{HH}^{i}(Z/k,M^{A})

on Hochschild cohomology. Let
\sigma
be a lift of
s(1)=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes_{Z}A^{\operatorname{o}}
in
A^{e}
, and let
e=s\mu
. Then
e
is right multiplication by
\sigma
, and the induced chain map on
P_{\bullet}
is

e_{n}(u\otimes\xi)=u\sigma\otimes\xi.

Under the adjunction map, precomposition with
e_{n}
corresponds to postcomposition with
e_{M}
on Hochschild cochains
C^{n}(Z,M)
.
Since
e_{M}
is a split idempotent with image
M^{A}
, the image of the induced
endomorphism on
C^{\bullet}(Z,M)
is the direct summand subcomplex
C^{\bullet}(Z,M^{A})
. Passing to
cohomology yields the claimed isomorphism.

We now prove that a unital
ring
endomorphism
f
of an Azumaya algebra of constant rank over an
arbitrary
commutative ring
Z
satisfies

f(Z)\subseteq Z
. We have only been able to find this result in the literature for reduced rings and therefore give a complete proof below.
First we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
B
be an Azumaya algebra of degree
n
over a commutative ring
C
, let
k
be a field, and let

u:B\to S

be a ring homomorphism into a central simple
k
-algebra
S
of degree
n
. Then the
k
-subalgebra of
S
generated by
u(B)
is all of
S
.

Proof.

Let
T
be the
k
-subalgebra of
S
generated by
u(B)
. Suppose
T\neq S
. Choose a simple quotient

T\twoheadrightarrow S_{0},

and put
K=Z(S_{0})
. The image of
C
in
S_{0}
is central, hence lies in
K
. Therefore the composite

B\to S_{0}

induces a
K
-algebra map

\theta:B\otimes_{C}K\to S_{0}.

Its image is the
K
-subalgebra generated by the image of
B
, hence all of
S_{0}
.

Now
B\otimes_{C}K
is central simple of degree
n
over
K
, so a nonzero surjective homomorphism from it is an isomorphism. Thus

\dim_{k}S_{0}=\dim_{k}(B\otimes_{C}K)=n^{2}[K:k]\geq n^{2}

contradicting that
\dim_{k}S_{0}<\dim_{k}S=n^{2}.

Hence
T=S
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
A
be an Azumaya algebra of constant degree over a commutative ring
R
, and let

f:A\to A

be a ring endomorphism. Then
f(R)\subseteq R
.

Proof.

Let

N:=\sum_{a\in A}R\,f(a)\subseteq A

be the
R
-submodule generated by
f(A)
. It suffices to prove that

N=A.

Fix a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset R
, and set

k(\mathfrak{m})=R/\mathfrak{m}
and
S=A/\mathfrak{m}A
.
Since
A
has constant degree
n
,
S
is a central simple
k(\mathfrak{m})
-algebra of degree
n
.
Consider the composite

\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}:A\xrightarrow{f}A\to S.

Let
H=\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)\subseteq S
. The image of
N
in
S
is exactly the
k(\mathfrak{m})
-span
k(\mathfrak{m})H
. Since
H
is a subring,

k(\mathfrak{m})H
is precisely the
k(\mathfrak{m})
-subalgebra generated by
H
. By Lemma
4.3
,

k(\mathfrak{m})H=S.

Hence the image of
N
in
A/\mathfrak{m}A
is all of
A/\mathfrak{m}A
, i.e.

N+\mathfrak{m}A=A.

Since
\mathfrak{m}
was arbitrary, we have
(A/N)\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{m})=0
for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset R
.
As
A/N
is a finitely generated
R
-module, it follows that
A/N=0
, and hence
N=A
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The constant-degree hypothesis is necessary. Indeed, let
A=\mathbb{C}\times\operatorname{Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Then the ring endomorphism
given by

(a,M)\mapsto\left(a,\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&\overline{a}\end{pmatrix}\right)

maps the central element
(i,0)
to a non-central element.

Finally we state and prove the main application and result of this paper.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
k
