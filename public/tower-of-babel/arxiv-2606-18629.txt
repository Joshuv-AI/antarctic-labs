Holographic Dual of PT Symmetric BCFT
Ryota Maeda, Nanami Nakamura, Tadashi Takayanagi

Abstract
We present a holographic dual of a two dimensional conformal field theory with non-hermitian but Parity-Time (PT) symmetric boundary conditions, by applying the AdS/BCFT duality and by introducing an imaginary valued scalar field localized on an end-of-the-world brane. We find that as we increase the strength of the non-hermitian PT symmetric interactions, the system experiences a spontaneous PT symmetry breaking. We also consider its Wick rotated setup as a new quantum quenched state and show that its growth of entanglement entropy can be larger than the standard results obtained from standard Cardy states.

1. Introduction

Even though the hermitian property of a given Hamiltonian guarantees that the energy spectrum is real valued, it is known that the opposite is not true. Even if the Hamiltonian is not hermitian
H^{\dagger}\neq H

30

;
17
;

These and other interesting class of examples, pioneered by

3

thermal or finite temperature aspects of such non-hermitian systems.

Motivated by these successful developments in non-hermitian quantum systems, it is intriguing to consider their gravity duals via the holography

;
38

33
;
20
;
41

If this is concretely realized, we can efficiently analyze strongly interacting non-hermitian quantum systems from classical gravity calculations.
There have been several works in this direction to construct gravity duals of PT invariant CFTs

In these works, typically bulk matter fields are chosen to be complex valued so that they are dual to the non-hermitian deformations of the original hermitian CFTs, which is translationally invariant. Due to the back-reactions via the bulk Einstein equation, the bulk AdS metric gets also complex valued in the PT broken phases. Since we still do not have a solid theory of holography with a complexified metric, this involves major challenges. Thus it is helpful to find well-controllable examples.

In this letter, instead, we introduce the non-hermitian interactions only at the boundary
x=x_{*}

The CFT action looks like

\displaystyle S_{CFT}=S^{(0)}_{CFT}+i\lambda\int_{x=x_{*}}d\tau d^{d-2}yO(\tau,y)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -i\lambda\int_{x=-x_{*}}d\tau d^{d-2}yO(\tau,y).

(1)

This motivates us to study this PT invariant system (

For simplicity, we will choose

The latter assumption guarantees that the theory is given by the boundary conformal field theory (BCFT) as it preserves a part of conformal symmetry even at the boundaries. In the AdS/BCFT construction, we consider a three dimensional pure AdS gravity and insert the EOW brane with a massless scalar field

7. Novel Quantum Quenches from BCFT

Consider the thermal AdS phase of our set up with the imaginary scalar in the range

\displaystyle\langle B(\Delta\varphi/2)|e^{-\Delta xH}|B(\Delta\varphi/2)\rangle.

and this flips the sign of

\displaystyle\Delta S_{A}(t)\simeq{c\over 6a}t,

. However, the gradient of the linear growth increases as
\Delta\varphi
gets larger. It reaches the maximum at
\Delta\varphi=\varphi_{c}
, where we find the double growth:
\Delta S_{A}(t)\simeq{2\pi c\over 3\beta}t
. Even though the original argument in
8
;
21
using the replica method and conformal map looks universal, this assumes that the upper and lower boundaries at
\tau^{\prime}=\pm{\Delta x\over 2}
map into a single boundary of the upper half plane. In general for
\Delta\varphi\neq 0
, we need to insert a boundary condition changing operator as they have the marginal deformations with the different signs:
\pm i{\Delta\varphi\over 2}\int dx^{\prime}O(x^{\prime})
. Refer also to
24
for another realization of such a generalized global quench using de Sitter branes and to
40
for related setups in free fermion/boson CFTs as well as lattice models. See also
32
for the enhancement of effective temperature for generic quantum states.

8. Discussions

In this article, we presented an explicit and simple holographic dual of a two dimensional conformal field theory with PT symmetric boundary conditions, by applying the AdS/BCFT duality. We showed that as the strength of the non-hermitian PT symmetric interactions parametrized by
\Delta\varphi
increases, our system experiences a spontaneous PT symmetry breaking. We also considered its Wick rotated setup as new quantum quenched states and show that its growth of entanglement entropy can be larger than the standard results obtained from standard Cardy states. Clearly, our model can be generalized in many different directions: higher dimensional setup, or adding a potential term in the scalar field. The analysis of their phase structures will be an interesting future problem.

Appendix A
Simple example of PT-invariant BCFT

Consider a
c=1
free massless scalar CFT in two dimensions. We write the scalar field as
X(\tau,x)
. We introduce the boundaries at
x=0
and
x=\pi
. We consider the Dirichlet boundary condition, which preserves the conformal invariance, as follows:

\displaystyle X(\tau,0)

\displaystyle=x_{0}+i\lambda

\displaystyle X(\tau,\pi)

\displaystyle=x_{0}-i\lambda.

(34)

When
\lambda
takes real values, this boundary condition becomes non-hermitian and PT invariant because the changes of the values of
X
at the boundaries
x=0,\pi
are described by the exactly marginal boundary perturbation of the form (
1
).

Now let us examine the spectrum of this BCFT. The standard mode expansion reads

\displaystyle X(\tau,x)

\displaystyle=x_{0}+i\lambda-2i\lambda{x\over\pi}+2\sum_{n\neq 0}{a_{n}\over n}e^{-n\tau}\sin nx,

(35)

where
a_{n}
is quantized by the commutation relation
[a_{n},a_{m}]=n\delta_{n+m,0}
. By choosing the vacuum by
a_{n}|0\rangle=0
for
n>0
as usual, the energy for each excitation is found to be (we remove the Casimir energy part)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=-{\lambda^{2}\over 2\pi^{2}}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_{-n}a_{n}.

(36)

This shows that due to the non-hermitian boundary condition, the energy can be negative, though it is bounded from below because
\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_{-n}a_{n}\geq 0
.

It is useful to study this setup of BCFT (or open string picture) by performing the Wick rotation
(\tau^{\prime},x^{\prime})\equiv(x,\tau)
i.e. from closed string theory viewpoint.
We now have the initial state at
\tau^{\prime}=0
given by the boundary state
10
, expressed as
|B_{1}\rangle
defined by the Dirichlet boundary condition
X=x_{0}+i\lambda
. Then we have a Euclidean time evolution until
\tau^{\prime}=\pi
. Finally it is projected to the final state given by the other boundary state defined by the Dirichlet boundary condition
X=x_{0}-i\lambda
. The left and right-moving mode of the scalar field
X=X_{\text{L}}+X_{\text{R}}
is expanded as follows:

\displaystyle X_{\text{L}}

\displaystyle=x_{(L)}-p_{(L)}(i\tau^{\prime}+x^{\prime})+i\sum_{n\neq 0}{\alpha_{n}\over n}e^{in(i\tau^{\prime}+x^{\prime})}

(37)

\displaystyle X_{\text{R}}

\displaystyle=x_{(R)}-p_{(R)}(i\tau^{\prime}-x^{\prime})+i\sum_{n\neq 0}{\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\over n}e^{in(i\tau^{\prime}-x^{\prime})},

(38)

where the canonical quantization leads to
[x_{(L)},p_{(L)}]=[x_{(R)},p_{(R)}]=i
and
[\alpha_{n},\alpha_{m}]=[\tilde{\alpha}_{n},\tilde{\alpha}_{m}]=n\delta_{n+m,0}
. When we compactify the scalar on a circle with the radius
R
, the zero modes are quantized as

p_{(L)}={k\over R}+{wR\over 2}
and
p_{(R)}={k\over R}-{wR\over 2}
, where
k
and
w
are integers.

At
\tau^{\prime}=0
, the quantum state is given by the boundary state
\ket{B_1}
for our Dirichlet condition:

\displaystyle\ket{B_1}

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i(x_{0}+i\lambda){k\over R}}e^{\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}{1\over n}\alpha_{-n}\tilde{\alpha}_{-n}}|k,w=0\rangle.

(39)

Its hermitian conjugation is given by

\displaystyle\bra{B_1}

\displaystyle\propto\langle k,w=0|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i(x_{0}-i\lambda){k\over R}}e^{\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}{1\over n}\alpha_{-n}\tilde{\alpha}_{-n}}.

(40)

The partition function for this free scalar field theory on the interval
0\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\pi
is given by

\langle B_{1}|e^{-\pi H}\ket{B_1}
, which is well-defined in spite of the non-hermitian boundary condition. Also it is clear that the density matrix
\rho=e^{-{\pi\over 2}H}|B_{1}\rangle\langle B_{1}|e^{-{\pi\over 2}H}
is hermitian. Note that as opposed to our current setup, if we consider the real valued deformation (i.e.
i\lambda
is real valued), which is hermitian,
|B_{1}\rangle
is no longer hermitian conjugate to
\langle B_{1}|
and thus
\rho
is not hermitian. Such non-hermitian density matrices (or called transition matrices) were discussed in the context of BCFT in
26
;
25
;
27
, where interesting phase transitions in pseudo entropy
35
were found.

Appendix B
Evaluation of On-Shell Action

B.1
Thermal AdS

In the thermal AdS solution, since
x
direction has the periodicity
2\pi a
, the width
\Delta x
is given by
\Delta x=2\pi a-2x_{*}
.
Note that
2x_{*}/a
was in the range
[0,\pi]
.
Thus in the current regime, the width of BCFT must satisfy
\pi\leq\Delta x/a\leq 2\pi
.

Now let us evaluate the free energy at a fixed inverse temperature
\beta
, that is given by the on-shell action (
3
).
The bulk geometry satisfies

R=-6,\qquad K|_{\Sigma}=2+O(\epsilon^{4}).

(41)

Here we set the cutoff
z\geq\epsilon
, to remove the divergence of on-shell action.
Moreover, we get
K=0
on the EOW brane. In addition, to remove the divergence at asymptotic boundary, we have to add local counter term:

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{c.t.}}

\displaystyle=-{1\over 16\pi G_{N}}\int\sqrt{g^{(0)}}(R^{(0)}+2)-{1\over 8\pi G_{N}}\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{h^{(0)}}K^{(0)}

(42)

\displaystyle={\beta\Delta\bar{x}\over 4\pi G_{N}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}{\differential{z}\over z^{3}}-{\beta\Delta\bar{x}\over 8\pi G_{N}}{2\over\epsilon^{2}},

where
\Delta\bar{x}=\sqrt{h(\epsilon)}\Delta x\simeq\left(1-{\epsilon^{2}\over 2a^{2}}\right)\Delta x
.
The boundary term on
\Sigma
cancels as well, and total action reads

\displaystyle I_{T}

\displaystyle={\beta\over 4\pi G_{N}}\int_{M}{\differential{z}\differential{x}\over z^{3}}-{\beta\Delta\bar{x}\over 4\pi G_{N}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}{\differential{z}\over z^{3}}

(43)

\displaystyle+{\beta\over 8\pi G_{N}}\int_{Q}\differential{x}\sqrt{{h\over h^{2}+\dot{z}^{2}}}\dot{\phi}^{2}

\displaystyle={\beta\over 4\pi G_{N}}\left[\Delta x\left(-{1\over 2a^{2}}+{1\over 2\epsilon^{2}}\right)+2\int_{0}^{x*}\left(-{1\over 2a^{2}}+{1\over 2z(x)^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-{\beta\Delta x\over 4\pi G_{N}}\left(1-{\epsilon^{2}\over 2a^{2}}\right){1\over 2\epsilon^{2}}-{2\beta\over 8\pi G_{N}}\int_{0}^{z_{*}}\differential{z}\derivative{x}{z}{h\over z^{2}}

\displaystyle=-{\beta\over 8\pi G_{N}a^{2}}\left(\Delta x+4x_{*}-{\Delta x\over 2}\right)

\displaystyle+{\beta\over 4\pi G_{N}}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{z_{*}}\differential{z}{3\over h(z)^{3/2}\sqrt{2U(z_{*})-2U(z)}}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{z_{*}}\differential{z}{3\over h(z)^{1/2}\sqrt{2U(z_{*})-2U(z)}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=-{\beta\,\Delta x\over 16\pi G_{N}a^{2}}

\displaystyle=-{1\over 16\pi G_{N}}\cdot{\beta\over\Delta x}\left(2\pi-X_{T}[\Phi_{T}^{-1}(\Delta\varphi)]\right)^{2}

In the last line, we used

{\Delta x^{2}\over a^{2}}=\left({2\pi a-2x_{*}\over a}\right)^{2}=\left(2\pi-X_{T}(\Phi_{T}^{-1}(\Delta\varphi))\right)

(44)

B.2
BTZ with connected EOW brane

In the BTZ phase with a connected EOW brane, the evaluation of on-shell action slightly changes.
Now the bulk region generally includes the horizon
z=a
, so
a
is no longer an independent parameter but a function of temperature:
a=\beta/2\pi
.
Then it naively seems that we cannot choose
\Delta x/\beta
and
\Delta\varphi
independently.
However, we have one new parameter: the periodicity of
x
.
We write this as
x\sim x+2\pi A
, so that
\Delta x=2\pi A-2x_{*}
.
That is to say,
\Delta x
and
x_{*}
do not rely to each other, so we can freely choose
\Delta x
(while
x_{*}
is still a function of
\Delta\varphi
).

The counter term is

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{c.t.}}

\displaystyle=-{1\over 16\pi G_{N}}\int\sqrt{g^{(0)}}(R^{(0)}+2)-{1\over 8\pi G_{N}}\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{h^{(0)}}K^{(0)}

(45)

\displaystyle={\bar{\beta}\Delta x\over 4\pi G_{N}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}{\differential{z}\over z^{3}}-{\bar{\beta}\Delta x\over 8\pi G_{N}}{2\over\epsilon^{2}}.

Here we introduced
\bar{\beta}=\sqrt{h(\epsilon)}\beta\simeq\left(1-{\epsilon^{2}\over 2a^{2}}\right)\beta
.

The boundary term on
\Sigma
cancels, and on-shell action becomes

\displaystyle I_{B}

\displaystyle={\beta\over 4\pi G_{N}}\int_{M}{\differential{z}\differential{x}\over z^{3}}-{2\bar{\beta}x_{*}\over 4\pi G_{N}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}{\differential{z}\over z^{3}}

(46)

\displaystyle+{\beta\over 8\pi G_{N}}\int_{Q}\differential{x}{h\over\sqrt{h+\dot{z}^{2}}}\dot{\phi}^{2}

\displaystyle={\beta\over 4\pi G_{N}}\Bigg[\Delta x\left(-{1\over 2a^{2}}+{1\over 2\epsilon^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{x*}\left(-{1\over 2a^{2}}+{1\over 2z(x)^{2}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle-{\beta\Delta x\over 4\pi G_{N}}\left(1-{\epsilon^{2}\over 2a^{2}}\right){1\over 2\epsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle-{2\beta\over 8\pi G_{N}}\int_{0}^{x_{*}}{h\over\sqrt{h+\dot{z}^{2}}}{(2h-zh^{\prime})\sqrt{h+\dot{z}^{2}}\over 2z^{2}h}

\displaystyle=-{\beta\over 8\pi G_{N}a^{2}}\left(\Delta x+2x_{*}-{\Delta x\over 2}\right)

\displaystyle+{\beta\over 4\pi G_{N}}\left[\int_{0}^{x_{*}}\differential{x}{1\over z(x)^{2}}-\int_{0}^{x_{*}}\differential{x}{1\over z(x)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=-{\beta\,\Delta x\over 16\pi G_{N}a^{2}}-{2\beta x_{*}\over 8\pi G_{N}a^{2}}

\displaystyle=-{\pi\over 4G_{N}}{\Delta x\over\beta}-{1\over 2G_{N}}X_{B}\left[\Phi_{B}^{-1}(\Delta\varphi)\right]

In the third equality, we used that
h=1-z^{2}/a^{2}
satisfies
2h-zh^{\prime}=2
, and in the final line we used
a=\beta/2\pi
.

B.3
BTZ black hole with disconnected EOW brane phase

Then, let us consider the another solution, which hold two disconnected EOW branes.
This is obtained by setting
\phi=\mathrm{const.}
on each brane, whose difference is identified to
\Delta\phi
.
The EOW branes extend along
x=\mathrm{const.}
, so the solution is the same as the standard AdS/BCFT without any scalar field with vanishing tension
39
;
15
.
The on-shell action becomes

\displaystyle I_{D}

\displaystyle={\beta\over 4\pi G_{N}}\int_{-x*}^{x_{*}}\differential{x}\int_{\epsilon}^{a}{\differential{z}\over z^{3}}-{\bar{\beta}\Delta x\over 4\pi G_{N}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}{\differential{z}\over z^{3}}

(47)

\displaystyle=-{\beta\,\Delta x\over 16\pi G_{N}a^{2}}

\displaystyle=-{\pi\,\Delta x\over 4G_{N}\beta}.

In the last line, we used the relation between black hole temperature and horizon radius:
a=r_{h}^{-1}=\beta/2\pi
.

B.4
Summary

We summarize the free energy of three phases.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Thermal~AdS}

(48)

\displaystyle I_{T}=-{1\over 16\pi G_{N}}\cdot{\beta\over\Delta x}\left(2\pi-X_{T}[\Phi_{T}^{-1}(\Delta\varphi)]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\mathrm{BTZ~BH~(connected)}

\displaystyle I_{B}=-{\pi\over 4G_{N}}{\Delta x\over\beta}-{1\over 2G_{N}}X_{B}\left[\Phi_{B}^{-1}(\Delta\varphi)\right]

\displaystyle\mathrm{BTZ~BH~(disconnected)}

\displaystyle I_{D}=-{\pi\over 4G_{N}}{\Delta x\over\beta}

For a given
\Delta x/\beta
and
\phi
, the phase with lowest free energy is realized.
We can see that the first term of
I_{B}
is completely same to
I_{D}
.
Therefore, since the second term of
I_{B}
is negative,
I_{D}
is always larger than
I_{B}
.
This means that the BTZ black hole with disconnected EOW brane phase never becomes dominant in this time, unlike the usual real scalar case
26
.
By comparing
I_{T}
and
I_{B}
, the phase transition occurs when

{\Delta x\over\beta}=\left({\Delta x\over\beta}\right)_{\mathrm{c}}

(49)

with

\displaystyle{\pi\over 4G_{N}}\left({\Delta x\over\beta}\right)^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}+{1\over 2G_{N}}X_{B}[\Phi^{-1}_{B}(\Delta\varphi)]\left({\Delta x\over\beta}\right)_{\mathrm{c}}

(50)

\displaystyle-{1\over 16\pi G_{N}}\left(2\pi-X_{T}[\Phi_{T}^{-1}(\Delta\varphi)]\right)^{2}=0

\displaystyle\left({\Delta x\over\beta}\right)_{\mathrm{c}}

\displaystyle={1\over 2\pi}\Bigg[2X_{B}[\Phi^{-1}_{B}(\Delta\varphi)]

(51)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{4X_{B}[\Phi^{-1}_{B}(\Delta\varphi)]^{2}+\left(2\pi-X_{T}[\Phi_{T}^{-1}(\Delta\varphi)]\right)^{2}}\Bigg]

At low temperature
{\Delta x\over\beta}\leq\left({\Delta x\over\beta}\right)_{\mathrm{c}}
, thermal AdS phase is realized, while at high temperature
{\Delta x\over\beta}\geq\left({\Delta x\over\beta}\right)_{\mathrm{c}}
,
BTZ black hole with connected EOW brane phase appear.

E.1
Linear Algebra statement

Definition E.1

(pseudo-Hermitian)
.

A linear operator
H
is said to be pseudo Hermitian if there exists a Hermitian operator
\tau
such that

H^{\dagger}=\tau H\tau^{-1}.

(69)

Theorem E.2

(pseudo-Hermiticity and spectrum)
.

Assume
H
is diagonalizable and has a discrete spectrum.
Then, the following two are equivalent:

1.

H
is pseudo-Hermitian.

2.

The eigenvalues of
H
are either real or come in complex conjugate pairs.

Proof.

(
1.\Rightarrow 2.
) We denote the eigenvalue as
E_{n}
and right eigenvector as
\ket{n}
.
Applying
H^{\dagger}
to
\tau\ket{n}
, we find

H^{\dagger}\tau\ket{n}=\tau H\tau^{-1}\tau\ket{n}=E_{n}\tau\ket{n}.

(70)

Thus,
\tau\ket{n}
is a right eigenvector of
H^{\dagger}
with eigenvalue
E_{n}
.
Since the set of eigenvalues of
H^{\dagger}
is the complex conjugate of that of
H
, there exists an eigenvalue
E_{m}
of
H
that satisfies
E_{m}=E_{n}^{*}
.
Therefore, the eigenvalues of
H
are either real or come in complex conjugate pairs.

(
2.\Rightarrow 1.
) We denote the eigenvalues of
H
as
E_{n}
, and the corresponding right and left eigenvectors as
\ket{n}
and
\bra{\tilde{n}}
, respectively.
Since
H
is diagonalizable, we can construct a complete biorthogonal basis

\innerproduct{\tilde{m}}{n}=\delta_{mn},\qquad\sum_{n}\ket{n}\bra{\tilde{n}}=1.

(71)

Hamiltonian and its Hermitian conjugate are written as

H=\sum_{n}E_{n}\ket{n}\bra{\tilde{n}},\qquad H^{\dagger}=\sum_{n}E_{n}^{*}\ket{\tilde{n}}\bra{n}.

(72)

Here we defined
\bra{n}
= (
\ket{n}
)
†
, and
\ket{\tilde{n}}=\left(\bra{\tilde{n}}\right)^{\dagger}

From the assumption, there exists an index

for each

E_{n^{\prime}}=E_{n}^{*}
. (If
E_{n}
is real, we can set
n^{\prime}=n

We define
\tau

\tau=\sum_{n}\ket{\tilde{n'}}\bra{\tilde{n}}.

(73)

Choosing the phase of
\ket{n'}
appropriately, we can make
\tau
Hermitian.
its inverse is given by

\tau^{-1}=\sum_{n}\ket{n}\bra{n'}.

(74)

Then, we can check that
H^{\dagger}=\tau H\tau^{-1}

\displaystyle\tau H\tau^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m,n}E_{m}\ket{\tilde{l}'}\innerproduct{\tilde{l}}{m}\innerproduct{\tilde{m}}{n}\bra{n'}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}E_{n}\ket{\tilde{n}'}\bra{n'}=H^{\dagger}.

∎

In Eq,(

By using this, we can express a trace of a linear operator

\Tr(A)=\sum_{n}\matrixelement{\tilde{n}}{A}{n}.

(76)

Definition E.3

(quasi-Hermitian)

A linear operator

H^{\dagger}=\Theta H\Theta^{-1}.

(77)

Theorem E.4

(quasi-Hermiticity and real spectrum)
.

Assume
H
is diagonalizable and having a discrete spectrum.
Then, the following two is equivalent:

1.

H
is quasi-Hermitian.

2.

The eigenvalues of
H
are all real.

Proof.

(
1.\Rightarrow 2.
) In general, a positive definite linear operator can be decomposed into the form
\Theta=R^{\dagger}R
where

From the definition of quasi-Hermiticity, we have

H^{\dagger}=R^{\dagger}RHR^{-1}(R^{\dagger})^{-1}\ \Leftrightarrow\ (R^{\dagger})^{-1}H^{\dagger}R^{\dagger}=RHR^{-1}.

(78)

Therefore,
RHR^{-1}
is a Hermitian operator, and its eigenvalues are all real.
Since
R
is an invertible matrix,

and

(

and

As in the same way to the proof of Thm.

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\tilde{m}}{n}=\delta_{mn},\qquad\sum_{n}\ket{n}\bra{\tilde{n}}=1,

\displaystyle H=\sum_{n}E_{n}\ket{n}\bra{\tilde{n}},\qquad H^{\dagger}=\sum_{n}E_{n}\ket{\tilde{n}}\bra{n}.

(80)

constructing

Consider the Euclidean path-integral in the setup of non-hermitian system.
For a scalar field

\displaystyle Z(\beta)=\int d\phi\langle\phi|e^{-\beta H}|\phi\rangle.

We can rewrite this by using

\displaystyle Z(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m}\int d\phi\langle\phi|n\rangle\langle\tilde{n}|e^{-\beta H}|m\rangle\langle\tilde{m}|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\langle\tilde{n}|e^{-\beta H}|n\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}e^{-\beta E_{n}}.

Thus it coincides with
\mbox{Tr}[e^{-\beta H}]

This argument is applicable to any non-hermitian
H

We remark that different from usual Hermitian case, this partition function does not necessarily describe thermal equilibrium of the system.

For PT-symmetric Hamiltonian, we can explicitly check that Eq.(

Here we labeled the real eigenenergies by
