Existence of holomorphic Lie algebroid connections in higher dimensions
Indranil Biswas, Anoop Singh

Abstract
. Let ( V , ϕ ) (V,\phi) be a holomorphic Lie algebroid over an irreducible smooth complex projective variety X X of dimension at least three, and let E E be a holomorphic vector bundle on X X . We establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a holomorphic ( V , ϕ ) (V,\phi) –connection on E E .



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
14H60, 53D17, 53B15, 32C38

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth projective variety over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers.
Holomorphic connections on holomorphic vector bundles over
X
were introduced by
Atiyah
[
At
]
. Holomorphic Lie algebroid connections (see Definition
2.3
) over
X

provide a natural generalization of holomorphic connections, where
the tangent Lie algebroid
(TX,\mathrm{Id}_{TX})
is replaced by
an arbitrary holomorphic Lie algebroid
(V,\phi)
over
X
(see Definition
2.1
for
holomorphic Lie algebroids).

It may be mentioned that by choosing the Lie algebroid appropriately, a wide range of other
algebraic and differential geometric objects can be interpreted as Lie algebroid
connections. For instance, Higgs bundles,
[
Hi
]
,
[
Si1
]
, twisted Higgs bundles,

[
Ni1
]
,
[
GGN
]
, logarithmic connections,
[
De
]
,
[
Ni2
]
, meromorphic connections,

[
Bo
]
,
[
BS
]
, and a broad subclass of Simpson’s notion of
\Lambda
-modules
can all be understood as Lie algebroid connections for suitable choices of the Lie
algebroid
(V,\phi)
(see,
[
Si2
]
,
[
To
]
,
[
AO
]
).

As far as holomorphic connections are concerned, not every holomorphic vector bundle over an
irreducible smooth complex projective variety
X
admits a holomorphic connection. The existence of
holomorphic connections is a highly restrictive condition. A holomorphic vector bundle over a
compact Riemann surface does not always admit a holomorphic connection. There is a criterion
that determines when such a connection exists. On a compact connected Riemann surface, a
holomorphic vector bundle
F
admits a holomorphic connection if and only if the degree of each
indecomposable component of
F
is zero
[
At
]
,
[
We
]
.

Further, if
X
is a compact connected Kähler Calabi-Yau manifold, meaning
c_{1}(TX)=0
,
then any holomorphic vector bundle over
X
admitting a
holomorphic connection also admits a flat holomorphic connection
[
BD
]
.

See
[
ABKS
]
for a criterion for the existence of Lie algebroid connections on holomorphic vector bundles
over a compact connected Riemann surface.

Atiyah proved in
[
At
, Proposition 21]
a criterion for the existence of holomorphic connections on an
irreducible smooth projective variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}
of complex dimension at least
3
. In this
work, we aim to generalize Atiyah’s criterion to the setting of Lie algebroid connections in higher
dimensions. More precisely, we establish the following result (see Theorem
3.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
is an irreducible
smooth complex projective variety of dimension at least three. Let
(V,\phi)
be
a holomorphic Lie algebroid on
X
such that the anchor map
\phi:V\longrightarrow TX
is surjective. A holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
X
admits a holomorphic

(V,\phi)
-connection if and only if the restriction of
E
to the intersection of
X
with a sufficiently
positive hypersurface in
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
admits a holomorphic Lie algebroid connection.

In addition to the general case, we also consider Lie algebroids associated to reduced
effective divisors. For a reduced effective divisor
Z\subset X
, we consider the Lie algebroid

j:W=TX\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z)\hookrightarrow TX.

We prove that, under the
same dimensional hypothesis, the existence of a holomorphic
(W,j)
-connection on a vector bundle
E
over

X
is equivalent to the
existence of such a connection on its restriction to some smooth
hypersurface
X_{n}
of sufficiently large degree
n
, which intersects
Z
properly; see Theorem
3.2
.

2.
Lie algebroids and connections

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
. The holomorphic tangent and
cotangent bundles of
X
will be denoted by
TX
and
\Omega^{1}_{X}
respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

A Lie algebroid on
X
is a holomorphic vector bundle
V\longrightarrow X
,
together with an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
–linear homomorphism

\phi:V\longrightarrow TX,

called the
anchor
map
, and a structure of a
\mathbb{C}
–Lie algebra on the sheaf of locally defined holomorphic sections of
V

[-,\,-]:V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V\longrightarrow V

such that

[s,\,f\cdot t]=f\cdot[s,\,t]+\phi(s)(f)\cdot t

for all locally defined
holomorphic sections
s,\,t
of
V
and all locally defined holomorphic functions
f
on
X
.

Note that the pair
(TX,{\rm Id}_{TX})
is a Lie algebroid; the Lie algebra structure on

TX
is given by the Lie bracket operation of vector fields.

Definition 2.2
.

A Lie algebroid
(V,\phi)
on
X
is called
split
if there is a holomorphic

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
–linear homomorphism
\eta:TX\longrightarrow V
such that

\phi\circ\eta=\text{Id}_{TX}.

(2.1)

A Lie algebroid
(V,\phi)
on
X
is called
nonsplit
if it is not split.

Definition 2.3
.

Take a Lie algebroid
(V,\phi)
on
X
.
Let
\phi^{*}:\Omega^{1}_{X}\longrightarrow V^{*}
be the dual of
\phi
.
A
Lie algebroid connection
on a holomorphic vector bundle
E
on
X

is a
\mathbb{C}
–linear holomorphic map

\nabla:E\longrightarrow E\otimes V^{*}

such that

\nabla(fs)=f\nabla(s)+s\otimes\phi^{*}(df),

where
s
is any locally
defined holomorphic section of
E
and
f
is any locally defined
holomorphic function on
X
. A Lie algebroid connection on
E
will also be called a
(V,\phi)
–connection on
E
or simply
a
\phi
–connection on
E
.

When
(V,\phi)=(TX,{\rm Id}_{TX})
, a
(V,\phi)
–connection on
E
is a
holomorphic connection on
E
in the usual sense
[
At
]
.

For any Lie algebroid
(V,\phi)
, and any
n\geq 0
, there is a
\mathbb{C}
–linear homomorphism

d^{n}_{V}:\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}V^{*}\longrightarrow\bigwedge\nolimits^{n+1}V^{*}

which is constructed as follows:

d_{V}^{0}(f)=\phi^{*}(df)

(2.2)

for any locally defined holomorphic function
f

on
X
. To construct
d^{n}_{V}
for
n\,\geq\,1
, take locally defined holomorphic sections
\omega\in\bigwedge^{n}V^{*}
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\in V
; then

d^{n}_{V}(\omega)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\phi(v_{i})(\omega(v_{1},\ldots,\widehat{v}_{i},\ldots,v_{n+1}))

(2.3)

+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j}\omega\left([v_{i},v_{j}],v_{1},\ldots,\widehat{v}_{i},\ldots,\,\widehat{v}_{j},\ldots,v_{n+1}\right).

It can be easily checked that
d^{n+1}_{V}\circ d^{n}_{V}=0
for every
n\geq 0
, and it follows that

\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{\bullet}V^{*},d_{V}\right)\ =\ \bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}V^{*},d^{n}_{V}\right)

is a differential graded complex; it
is called the Chevalley-Eilenberg-de Rham complex for
(V,\phi)
(see
[
BMRT
]
,
[
LSX
]
,

[
BR
]
for details).
Note that when
(V,\phi)=(TX,{\rm Id}_{TX})
, then
(\bigwedge\nolimits^{\bullet}V^{*},d_{V})

is the holomorphic de Rham complex of
X
.

Given a Lie algebroid connection
\nabla:E\longrightarrow E\otimes V^{*}
, consider the
following composition of operators

E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\otimes V^{*}\xrightarrow{\,\nabla\wedge{\rm Id}_{V^{*}}+{\rm Id}_{E}\otimes d^{1}_{V}}E\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}V^{*}.

It is straightforward to check that this operator
E\longrightarrow E\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}V^{*}

is
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
–linear, and hence it is given by a holomorphic section

{\mathcal{K}}(\nabla)\in H^{0}(X,\,\text{End}(E)\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}V^{*}).

This section
{\mathcal{K}}(\nabla)
is called the
curvature
of
\nabla
. The
Lie algebroid connection
\nabla
is called
integrable
(or
flat
) if
we have
{\mathcal{K}}(\nabla)\,=\,0
.

Observe that on a smooth complex projective variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}
, the flatness of a

(V,\phi)
-connection is not guaranteed when
\mathrm{rank}(V)\geq 2
, even in the case where
\dim X=1

or when the Lie algebroid
(V,\phi)
admits a splitting.

Consider the following direct sum of
\mathbb{C}
-modules:

J^{1}_{V}(E):=E\oplus(E\otimes V^{*}).

(2.4)

We can equip
J^{1}_{V}(E)
with two
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structures. The first one is coordinate-wise
multiplication

f\cdot(s,\sigma):=(fs,f\sigma),

and the second one is given by

f\cdot(s,\sigma):=(fs,f\sigma+s\otimes d_{V}f),

(2.5)

where
s
(respectively,
\sigma
) is a locally defined holomorphic section of
E

(respectively,
E\otimes V^{*}
) and
f
is a locally defined holomorphic function on
X
. The
\mathbb{C}
-module

J^{1}_{V}(E)
equipped with the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structure in (
2.5
) is called the
first order
V
-jet bundle

associated to
E
. This first order
V
-jet bundle
J^{1}_{V}(E)
fits into
the following short exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules,

0\longrightarrow E\otimes V^{*}\xrightarrow{\,\iota_{E}\,}J^{1}_{V}(E)\xrightarrow{\,p_{E}\,}E\longrightarrow 0,

(2.6)

where
\iota_{E}
and
p_{E}
are the canonical inclusion and projection respectively.
In general, the short exact sequence in (
2.6
) may not split holomorphically as
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.
It is easy to see that a holomorphic
(V,\,\phi)
–connection on
E
is a holomorphic splitting of (
2.6
).

Let

\mathrm{at}_{V}(E)\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes V^{*})

(2.7)

denote the extension class of the short exact sequence in (
2.6

It was noted before that giving a holomorphic splitting of (

(V,\phi)
-connection on
E

E
admits a
(V,\phi)
-connection if and only if we have
\mathrm{at}_{V}(E)=0

3.
Criterion for existence of Lie algebroid connection

Let
X
be embedded in the projective space
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
. Given a general hypersurface
H_{n}
of degree
n

in
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
, let
X_{n}:=X\cap H_{n}
denote the hypersurface of degree
n
in
X
. Given a
holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
X
, denote by
E\big|_{n}
the restriction

E\big|_{X_{n}}
of
E
to
X_{n}
.

Consider the following short exact sequence of coherent sheaves over
X_{n}

0\longrightarrow TX_{n}\xrightarrow{\,\iota\,}TX\big|_{n}\xrightarrow{\,\tau_{n}\,}\ \mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})\big|_{X_{n}}=N_{X_{n}}\longrightarrow 0,

(3.1)

where
N_{X_{n}}
is the normal bundle of
X_{n}
, and
\tau_{n}
is the projection to the normal bundle;
the above isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})\big|_{X_{n}}=N_{X_{n}}
is given by the Poincaré adjunction
formula. Given a Lie algebroid
(V,\phi)
over
X
, let

\psi:V\big|_{n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})\big|_{X_{n}}

(3.2)

be the following composition of homomorphisms

V\big|_{n}\xrightarrow{\,\phi\big|_{X_{n}}\,}\ TX\big|_{n}\xrightarrow{\tau_{n}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})\big|_{n}.

Denote

V_{n}:=\mathrm{Ker}(\psi)=(\phi\big|_{X_{n}})^{-1}(TX_{n})\subset V\big|_{n},

(3.3)

where
\psi
is the homomorphism in (
3.2
); the restriction of
\phi\big|_{X_{n}}
to

V_{n}
will be denoted by
\phi_{n}
. Then the pair

(V_{n},\phi_{n})

(3.4)

is a Lie algebroid over
X_{n}
. Moreover, we get a short exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}
-modules

0\longrightarrow V_{n}\xrightarrow{j_{n}}V\big|_{n}\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathrm{Im}(\psi)\longrightarrow 0

(3.5)

If
\psi
in (
3.2
) is surjective — this happens, for example, if
\phi
is surjective — then

\mathrm{Im}(\psi)\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})\big|_{X_{n}}=N_{X_{n}}.

Consequently, when
\phi
is surjective, from (
3.5
) we get the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow V_{n}\xrightarrow{j_{n}}V\big|_{n}\xrightarrow{\,\psi\,}\ \mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})\big|_{n}\longrightarrow 0.

(3.6)

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(X)\geq 3
and the anchor map
\phi:V\longrightarrow TX
is surjective. A holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
X
admits a holomorphic
(V,\,\phi)
-connection if
and only if the restriction
E\big|_{X_{n}}
admits a holomorphic
(V_{n},\phi_{n})
-connection
for sufficiently large
n
, where
(V_{n},\phi_{n})
is the Lie algebroid constructed in (
3.4
).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules on
X
. Then, we have the exact
sequence of coherent sheaves

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow i_{*}\left(\mathcal{F}\big|_{X_{n}}\right)\longrightarrow 0,

(3.7)

where
i:X_{n}\hookrightarrow X
is the inclusion map. Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1):=i^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{N}}(1)
. Then,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
is very ample and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)
. Since

\dim X\geq 3
, it follows from the Serre’s duality theorem
[
Ha
, Theorem 7.6, p. 243]
and Serre’s theorem

[
Ha
, Theorem 5.2, p. 228]
that for
n\gg 0
, we have

\mathrm{H}^{i}\left(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right)=0,\quad\forall\,i=1,2.

(3.8)

Consider the long exact sequence of cohomologies associated with the short exact sequence
(
3.7
)

\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(X,\,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{F})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(X,i_{*}\left(\mathcal{F}\big|_{X_{n}}\right)\right)=\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(X_{n},\mathcal{F}\big|_{X_{n}}\right)\,

\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}\left(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right).

Using (
3.8
) in this exact sequence we get an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\,\cong\,}\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(X_{n},\mathcal{F}\big|_{X_{n}}\right).

(3.9)

Since
\phi
is surjective (by assumption), we have the short exact sequence in (
3.6
). Consider
its dual exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\big|_{n}\longrightarrow V^{*}\big|_{n}\longrightarrow V^{*}_{n}\longrightarrow 0.

(3.10)

Tensoring the short exact sequence in (

\mathrm{End}(E)\big|_{X_{n}}
the following
short exact sequence is obtained:

0\longrightarrow\left(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right)\big|_{n}\longrightarrow\left(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes V^{*}\right)\big|_{n}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(E)\big|_{n}\otimes V^{*}_{n}\longrightarrow 0.

(3.11)

Setting
\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})
in the short exact sequence in
(

0\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2X_{n})\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\longrightarrow\left(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right)\big|_{n}\longrightarrow 0.

(3.12)

Consider the long exact sequence of cohomologies associated to (
3.12
):

\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(X,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(X_{n},\left(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right)\big|_{n}\right)

\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}\left(X,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2X_{n})\right)\longrightarrow.

(3.13)

Note that from (
3.8
) we have

\mathrm{H}^{2}\left(X,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2X_{n})\right)=0=\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(X,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right)

(3.14)

for all
n\gg 0
. This and (
3.13
) combine together to give that

\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(X_{n},\left(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right)\big|_{n}\right)=0

(3.15)

for all
n\gg 0
.

Consider the long exact sequence of cohomologies for the short exact sequence in (
3.11
):

\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(X_{n},\left(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X_{n})\right)\big|_{n}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{n},\left(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes V^{*}\right)\big|_{n})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{n},\mathrm{End}(E)\big|_{n}\otimes V^{*}_{n})\longrightarrow.

In view of (
3.15
), it gives an injective homomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{n},\left(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes V^{*}\right)\big|_{n})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{n},\mathrm{End}(E)\big|_{n}\otimes V^{*}_{n}).

(3.16)

Also, from the isomorphism in (
3.9
) we have

\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes V^{*})\ \xrightarrow{\,\cong\,}\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{n},\left(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes V^{*}\right)\big|_{n})

(3.17)

for all
n\gg 0
. Now, from (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), the homomorphism

i^{\sharp}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes V^{*})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{n},\mathrm{End}(E)\big|_{n}\otimes V^{*}_{n})

induced by the natural inclusion map
i:X_{n}\hookrightarrow X
is injective, so

i^{\sharp}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes V^{*})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{n},\mathrm{End}(E)\big|_{n}\otimes V^{*}_{n}).

(3.18)

Note that
i^{\sharp}(\mathrm{at}_{V}(E))=\mathrm{at}_{V_{n}}(E\big|_{n})
by the functoriality of
the Atiyah class. Thus, from (
3.18
) it follows immediately that for all
n\gg 0
, we have
\mathrm{at}_{V_{n}}(E\big|_{n})=0
if and only if
\mathrm{at}_{V}(E)=0
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Let
X
be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension
d\geq 1
.
Let
\mathrm{Div}(X)
be a group of all (Weil) divisors on
X
. Recall that every divisor

Z\in\mathrm{Div}(X)
can be written uniquely as (finite sum)
Z=\sum_{i}a_{i}Z_{i}
, where

a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and each
Z_{i}\subset X
is a prime divisor, i.e., an irreducible closed subvariety
of codimension
1
. Let
Z=\sum_{i}a_{i}Z_{i}
and

Z^{\prime}=\sum_{j}b_{j}Z^{\prime}_{j}
be two divisors on
X
. We say that
Z
and
Z^{\prime}

meet properly
if
for every pair of prime divisors
Z_{i}
and
Z^{\prime}_{j}
appearing with non-zero coefficients
a_{i}\neq 0

and
b_{j}\neq 0
in
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
, respectively, one has

\dim(Z_{i}\cap Z^{\prime}_{j})=d-2.

Now assume that
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are
reduced effective divisors
. Then each of them is a union of distinct
prime divisors with coefficient
1
. Under this assumption, if
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
meet properly, the intersection

Z\cap Z^{\prime}
has pure dimension
d-2
, and hence
Z\cap Z^{\prime}
defines a Weil divisor on both
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
.

Let
Z
be a reduced effective divisor on
X
. Then, consider the following Lie algebroid

j:W=TX\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z)\hookrightarrow TX

(3.19)

with (non-surjective) anchor map as inclusion morphism
j
. Let
X_{n}
be a smooth
hypersurface of degree
n
in
X
. Suppose that
Z
and
X_{n}
meet properly, then

Z_{n}:=Z\cap X_{n}

is a divisor on
X_{n}
. We get a Lie algebroid

j_{n}:W_{n}:=TX_{n}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}(-Z_{n})\hookrightarrow TX_{n}

(3.20)

on
X_{n}
. Using similar techniques as in Theorem
3.1
, we can show the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X)\geq 3
, and let
Z\subset X
be a reduced effective divisor
on
X
. Consider the holomorphic Lie algebroid
j:W=TX\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z)\hookrightarrow TX

on
X
. Then
E
admits a
(W,j)
-connection if and only if for some smooth
hypersurface
X_{n}
of sufficiently large degree
n
, which intersects
Z
properly, the vector
bundle
E\big|_{X_{n}}
on
X_{n}
admits a
(W_{n},j_{n})
-connection, where
(W_{n},j_{n})
is
defined in (
3.20
).

Remark 3.3
.

Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
provide restriction criteria for the existence of Lie algebroid connections in higher dimensions under natural assumptions on the anchor map. These results extend Atiyah’s classical theorem to a broad class of Lie algebroids (e.g. Atiyah algebroid associated with a vector bundle), including those arising from logarithmic structures. Establishing analogous criteria for arbitrary
Lie algebroids or foliations remains an important open problem.
