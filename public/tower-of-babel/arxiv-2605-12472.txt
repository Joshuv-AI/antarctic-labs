An Improved Lower Bound on Support Size of Capacity-Achieving Inputs for the Binomial Channel: Extended version
Mohammadamin Baniasadi † , Luca Barletta ∗ , and Alex Dytso ∗∗

Abstract
We study the binomial channel and the structure of its capacity-achieving input and output distributions. It is known that the capacity-achieving input distribution is discrete and supported on finitely many points. The best previously known bounds show that the support size of the capacity-achieving distribution is lower-bounded by a term of order n \sqrt{n} and upper-bounded by a term of order n / 2 n/2 , where n n is the number of trials. In this work, we derive a new lower bound on the support size of order n ​ log ⁡ log ​ n \sqrt{n\log\log n} , up to explicit constants. The proof consists of three main steps. First, we derive new upper and lower bounds on the capacity with a gap that vanishes as n → ∞ n\to\infty , which yields C ⁡ ( n ) = 1 2 ​ log ⁡ n ​ π 2 ​ e + o ⁡ ( 1 ) C(n)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{n\pi}{2e}+o(1) . Second, we show that the Beta-binomial output distribution induced by the reference input X r ∼ Beta ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , 1 / 2 ) X_{r}\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1/2,1/2) is asymptotically optimal: it approaches the capacity-achieving output distribution in relative entropy and, after a comparison step, in χ 2 \chi^{2} divergence. Third, we prove a quantitative χ 2 \chi^{2} approximation lower bound showing that this Beta-binomial output cannot be approximated too well by the output induced by a K K -point input. Combining these ingredients forces the capacity-achieving input distribution to have at least order n ​ log ⁡ log ​ n \sqrt{n\log\log n} mass points.

I
Introduction

We consider the binomial channel with input-output law

P_{Y|X}(y|x)=\binom{n}{y}x^{y}(1-x)^{n-y},\quad x\in[0,1],\,y\in\{0,\ldots,n\}.

(1)

The channel capacity is

C(n)=\max_{P_{X}:\,X\in[0,1]}I(X;Y)=I(X^{\ast};Y^{\ast}),

(2)

where
P_{X^{\ast}}
and
P_{Y^{\ast}}
are the capacity-achieving input and output distributions, respectively.

The binomial channel naturally arises in molecular communications, and the interested reader is referred to

[
5
,
4
,
6
,
9
]

and references therein. The channel is also useful in the study of the deletion channel

[
13
,
1
]
. The capacity of the binomial channel was first considered in

[
12
]
, where the minimax redundancy theorem of
[
19
]

was used to show that the capacity scales asymptotically as
\frac{1}{2}\log n
. The exact capacity for the case

n=1
was computed in
[
5
]
, where the binary distribution supported on
\{0,1\}
was shown to be capacity-achieving. However, sharp non-asymptotic bounds on the capacity remain limited.

The structure of the capacity-achieving input distribution has also been studied. For example, the authors of

[
5
]
designed an algorithm for computing the capacity and a capacity-achieving input distribution using a dual representation of the maximization problem. It is also known, by the Witsenhausen technique

[
18
]
, that there exists a capacity-achieving distribution with at most
n+1
mass points.

More recently,
[
20
]
studied structural properties of capacity-achieving distributions for the binomial channel. In particular, it was shown that all capacity-achieving distributions are discrete. This discreteness was then used to establish strong concavity of the mutual information, which implies uniqueness of
P_{X^{\star}}
. It was also shown that the capacity-achieving distribution is symmetric around
\frac{1}{2}
. The same work improved the Witsenhausen upper bound of
n+1
mass points to an upper bound of order
\frac{n}{2}
, and proved a lower bound of order
\sqrt{n}
. The authors also derived capacity bounds. However, the gap between these bounds does not vanish with
n
, which limits their use for obtaining sharper cardinality lower bounds.

In this work, we improve the lower bound on the number of mass points of
P_{X^{\ast}}
from order
\sqrt{n}
to order
\sqrt{n\log\log n}
. We also derive new upper and lower bounds on the capacity whose gap vanishes as
n\to\infty
. This vanishing-gap estimate is a key ingredient in the support-size argument: it shows that the output distribution induced by a capacity-achieving input must be close to a natural Beta-binomial reference output.

The techniques used in this work are inspired by a recent result on the additive Gaussian channel with a peak-power constraint
[
17
]
, where improved lower bounds were obtained on the support size of the capacity-achieving input distribution. The main idea of
[
17
]
is to combine the golden formula with lower bounds from best-approximation theory, which quantify how well a target output distribution can be approximated by finite Gaussian mixtures
[
14
]
. In the present paper, we adapt this strategy to the binomial setting by replacing Gaussian-mixture approximation with approximation by finite binomial mixtures.

I-A

Main Contributions and Outline

The main contribution of this paper is a new lower bound on the support size of the capacity-achieving input distribution for the binomial channel. In particular, we improve the previously known lower bound of order
\sqrt{n}
to a lower bound of order
\sqrt{n\log\log n}
, up to explicit constants; see Theorem
4
.

The proof is based on several ingredients, which may also be of independent interest.
First, in Section
II
we discuss the preliminaries we need in our proofs.
Then, in Section
III
, we derive new explicit upper and lower bounds on the capacity. The lower bound is obtained by evaluating the mutual information at the reference input
X_{r}\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1/2,1/2)
, while the upper bound follows from the Xie–Barron minimax redundancy construction; see Theorems
1
and
2
. These bounds imply

C(n)=\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)+o(1),

(3)

and, more importantly for our purposes, the gap between the lower and upper bounds vanishes as
n\to\infty
; see Proposition
1
.

Second, we show that the output distribution
P_{Y_{r}}
induced by the reference input
P_{X_{r}}
is close to the capacity-achieving output distribution. More precisely, in Section
III
, we prove that the relative entropy
{\mathsf{D}}\left(\left.P_{Y_{r}}\,\right\|P_{Y^{\ast}}\right)
vanishes at the same rate as the capacity gap, and then convert this estimate into a
\chi^{2}
bound; see Theorem
3
and Proposition
2
. This step is what connects the capacity estimates to the support-size question.

Third, in Section
IV
, we use a finite-mixture approximation lower bound for binomial mixtures; see Theorem
5
. This result shows that the reference Beta-binomial output
P_{Y_{r}}
cannot be approximated too well, in
\chi^{2}
divergence, by the output induced by an input with only
K
mass points. Combining this approximation lower bound with the upper bound on
\chi^{2}(P_{Y^{\ast}}\|P_{Y_{r}})
forces the capacity-achieving input to have sufficiently many mass points.

Finally, Section
V
contains the proofs, and Section
VI
concludes with several open questions.

II
Preliminaries

II-A

KKT Conditions

The next result provides KKT conditions for the optimization problem in (
2
), allowing the study of the support properties of an optimal input distribution (see for example
[
3
]
).

Lemma 1
.

P_{X^{\ast}}
is a capacity-achieving input distribution if and only if the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle{\mathsf{D}}\left(\left.P_{Y|X}(\cdot|x)\,\right\|P_{Y^{\ast}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(n),\qquad x\in[0,1],

(5)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{D}}\left(\left.P_{Y|X}(\cdot|x)\,\right\|P_{Y^{\ast}}\right)

\displaystyle=C(n),\qquad x\in\mathrm{supp}(P_{X^{\ast}}),

(6)

where
P_{Y^{\ast}}
is the induced output distribution.

II-B

Conditional Expectation Operators

Define the forward conditional expectation operator
A:\mathbb{R}^{\{0,\dots,n\}}\to\mathbb{R}[x]
by

(Ag)(x)=\mathbb{E}[g(Y)|X=x]=\sum_{y=0}^{n}g(y)P_{Y|X}(y|x).

(7)

Using the falling factorial
y^{\underline{k}}=y(y-1)\cdots(y-k+1)
, the moments of the binomial distribution yield

A(y^{\underline{k}})=n^{\underline{k}}x^{k}.

(8)

Thus,
A
maps polynomials in
y
of degree
k
to polynomials in
x
of degree
k
.

Define the backward (posterior) conditional expectation operator
B:L^{1}(P_{X})\to\mathbb{R}^{\{0,\dots,n\}}
by

(Bf)(y)=\mathbb{E}[f(X)|Y=y],

(9)

where the expectation is taken with respect to the posterior distribution
P_{X|Y}(x|y)
induced by the prior
X
. By Bayes’ rule, the posterior is proportional to
x^{y-1/2}(1-x)^{n-y-1/2}
, which is a
\text{Beta}(y+1/2,n-y+1/2)
distribution. The
k
-th moment of this Beta distribution is

B(x^{k})=\frac{(y+1/2)_{k}}{(n+1)_{k}},

(10)

where
(a)_{k}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1)
is the rising factorial. Thus,
B
maps polynomials in
x
of degree
k
to polynomials in
y
of degree
k
.

These operators satisfy the adjoint relation: for any
f\in L^{1}(P_{X})
and
g\in\mathbb{R}^{\{0,\dots,n\}}
,

\mathbb{E}_{Y}[g(Y)(Bf)(Y)]=\mathbb{E}_{X}[f(X)(Ag)(X)].

(11)

II-C

Orthogonal Polynomials

The orthogonal polynomials for the
\text{Beta}(1/2,1/2)
distribution are the shifted Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind, defined as
\tilde{T}_{k}(x)=\cos(k\arccos(2x-1))
. They satisfy the orthogonality condition:

\mathbb{E}_{X}[\tilde{T}_{k}(X)\tilde{T}_{m}(X)]=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }k=m=0,\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }k=m\geq 1,\\
0&\text{if }k\neq m.\end{cases}

(12)

Since
A
is a bijection between polynomials of degree
k\leq n
, we can uniquely define a polynomial
H_{k}(y)
of degree
k
such that

(AH_{k})(x)=\tilde{T}_{k}(x).

(13)

We claim that
H_{k}(y)
are orthogonal polynomials for
Y
. Let
m<k\leq n
. Since
B
is bijective on polynomials of degree up to
n
, there exists a polynomial
q_{m}(x)
of degree
m
such that
Bq_{m}=y^{m}
. Using the adjoint relation (
11
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Y}[H_{k}(Y)Y^{m}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Y}[H_{k}(Y)(Bq_{m})(Y)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{X}[q_{m}(X)(AH_{k})(X)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{X}[q_{m}(X)\tilde{T}_{k}(X)]=0,

(14)

because
\tilde{T}_{k}(x)
is orthogonal to all polynomials of degree strictly less than
k
.

II-D

Squared Norms of
H_{k}(Y)

Let
h_{k}=\mathbb{E}_{Y}[H_{k}(Y)^{2}]
. Using the adjoint relation again:

\displaystyle h_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Y}[H_{k}(Y)(BB^{-1}H_{k})(Y)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{X}[(B^{-1}H_{k})(X)(AH_{k})(X)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{X}[(B^{-1}H_{k})(X)\tilde{T}_{k}(X)].

(15)

Since
\tilde{T}_{k}(x)
is orthogonal to lower-degree terms, we only need the leading coefficient of
B^{-1}H_{k}(x)
. The leading term of
\tilde{T}_{k}(x)
for
k\geq 1
is
2^{2k-1}x^{k}
.
From (
8
),
A(y^{k})=n^{\underline{k}}x^{k}+\text{lower order terms}
, which implies
A^{-1}(x^{k})=\frac{1}{n^{\underline{k}}}y^{k}+\dots

Thus,

H_{k}(y)=A^{-1}(\tilde{T}_{k})(y)=\frac{2^{2k-1}}{n^{\underline{k}}}y^{k}+\dots

(16)

From (
10
),
B(x^{k})=\frac{1}{(n+1)_{k}}y^{k}+\dots
, which implies
B^{-1}(y^{k})=(n+1)_{k}x^{k}+\dots

Composing these gives

(B^{-1}H_{k})(x)=\frac{2^{2k-1}}{n^{\underline{k}}}(n+1)_{k}x^{k}+\dots

(17)

Since
x^{k}=2^{-(2k-1)}\tilde{T}_{k}(x)+\dots
, we can write

(B^{-1}H_{k})(x)=\frac{(n+1)_{k}}{n^{\underline{k}}}\tilde{T}_{k}(x)+\dots

(18)

Therefore, for
k\geq 1
:

h_{k}=\frac{(n+1)_{k}}{n^{\underline{k}}}\mathbb{E}_{X}[\tilde{T}_{k}(X)^{2}]=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{n+j}{n-j+1}.

(19)

II-E

Parseval’s Identity

We expand the density ratio in the orthogonal basis
H_{k}(y)
:

\frac{P_{Y}(y)}{P_{Y^{*}}(y)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}H_{k}(y).

(20)

The coefficients are given by

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{Y^{*}}\left[\frac{P_{Y}(Y^{*})}{P_{Y^{*}}(Y^{*})}H_{k}(Y^{*})\right]=\frac{1}{h_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{Y}[H_{k}(Y)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{X}[(AH_{k})(X)]=\frac{1}{h_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{X}[\tilde{T}_{k}(X)].

(21)

Let
\epsilon_{k}=\mathbb{E}_{X}[\tilde{T}_{k}(X)]
. Note that
\epsilon_{0}=1
and
h_{0}=1
. By Parseval’s identity, the
\chi^{2}
divergence is

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(P_{Y}\|P_{Y^{*}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Y^{*}}\left[\left(\frac{P_{Y}(Y^{*})}{P_{Y^{*}}(Y^{*})}-1\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{2}h_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\epsilon_{k}^{2}}{h_{k}}.

(22)

III
Main Results

We now summarize the main statements proved in this paper.

III-A

Asymptotically Tight Capacity Bounds

The next theorem provides a lower bound on the capacity by evaluating mutual information at the reference input
X_{r}\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1/2,1/2)
with probability density function (pdf)

f_{X_{r}}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{x(1-x)}},\quad x\in(0,1).

(23)

We will also need the expression for the output distribution induced by
X_{r}
, which is the Beta-binomial distribution
[
10
]
:

P_{Y_{r}}(y)=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(y+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(n-y+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}{\pi\,\Gamma(y+1)\Gamma(n-y+1)},\qquad y=0,\dots,n.

(24)

Theorem 1
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

C(n)\geq\psi(n+1)-\log\!\bigl(1+\sqrt{3n+1}\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{3\pi}{2e}\right),

(25)

where
\psi(\cdot)
denotes the digamma function. In particular,

C(n)\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)+r_{{\rm LB}}(n),

(26)

where

r_{{\rm LB}}(n):=-\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{3n}\right)+\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3n+1}}\right)+\frac{1}{n+1}.

(27)

Proof:

The proof is given in Section
V-A
.
∎

The following upper bound is based on the Xie–Barron minimax redundancy construction
[
19
]
.

Theorem 2
.

For all
n\geq 28
, we have

C(n)\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)+r_{{\rm UB}}(n),

(28)

where

r_{{\rm UB}}(n):=-\log\!\left(1-2\left(\frac{2e}{n\pi}\right)^{1/4}\right)+\frac{10}{\log\frac{n\pi}{2e}}.

(29)

Proof:

The proof is given in Section
V-B
.
∎

Combining Theorems
1
and
2
, we arrive at the following bound on the gap between the upper and lower bounds.

Proposition 1
.

The gap between the capacity upper and lower bounds of Theorems
1
and
2
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gap}(n):=r_{{\rm UB}}(n)-r_{{\rm LB}}(n)\leq\frac{17}{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)}

(30)

for
n\geq 444
. Therefore, the gap vanishes as
n\to\infty
.

Proof:

Recall that

\displaystyle{\rm Gap}(n)

\displaystyle=r_{{\rm UB}}(n)-r_{{\rm LB}}(n)

(31)

\displaystyle=-\log\!\left(1-2\left(\frac{2e}{n\pi}\right)^{1/4}\right)+\frac{10}{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{3n}\right)-\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3n+1}}\right)-\frac{1}{n+1}.

(32)

Using
-\log(1-x)\leq x+x^{2}
for
0<x<1/2
, and noting that for sufficiently large
n
(e.g.,
n\geq 444
)

x=2\left(\frac{2e}{n\pi}\right)^{1/4}<\frac{1}{2},

we obtain

-\log\!\left(1-2\left(\frac{2e}{n\pi}\right)^{1/4}\right)\leq 2\left(\frac{2e}{n\pi}\right)^{1/4}+4\left(\frac{2e}{n\pi}\right)^{1/2}.

Next, using
\log(1+x)\leq x
for
x\geq 0
,

\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{3n}\right)\leq\frac{1}{6n}.

Moreover,

-\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3n+1}}\right)-\frac{1}{n+1}\leq 0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm Gap}(n)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{10}{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)}+2\left(\frac{2e}{n\pi}\right)^{1/4}+4\left(\frac{2e}{n\pi}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{6n}.

(33)

For all
n\geq 444
, the polynomial-decay terms are dominated by
n^{-1/4}
. Hence

\displaystyle{\rm Gap}(n)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{10}{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)}+C_{1}n^{-1/4}

(34)

\displaystyle=\frac{10}{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)}+5n^{-1/4}

(35)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{17}{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)},\qquad n\geq 444,

(36)

where in (
35
) we picked
C_{1}=5
.
∎As we will see in Section
V-D
, characterizing the capacity up to a vanishing gap in
n
is essential to establish the new lower bound on the support size of the capacity-achieving distribution
P_{X^{\ast}}
.

III-B

Asymptotically Optimal Output Distribution

In Proposition
1
, we have shown that the input
X_{r}
is asymptotically optimal from the rate perspective. We now show that the output distribution induced by
X_{r}
also approaches the optimal output distribution.

The next result makes this connection precise.

Theorem 3
.

Let
P_{Y^{\ast}}
be the output distribution induced by the capacity-achieving input
P_{X^{\ast}}
, and let
P_{Y_{r}}
be defined as in (
24
). Then, the following statements hold:

•

There exists an absolute constant
c_{\star}>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
and every

y\in\{0,\dots,n\}
,

\frac{P_{Y_{r}}(y)}{P_{Y^{\ast}}(y)}\geq c_{\star},

(37)

where one can take
c_{\star}=\frac{1}{4e\,3^{7/4}\pi^{7/4}}
; and

•

For
n\geq 444
,

{\mathsf{D}}\left(\left.P_{Y_{r}}\,\right\|P_{Y^{\ast}}\right)\leq\frac{17}{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)}.

(38)

Proof:

The proof is given in Section
V-C
.
∎

For our purposes, it will be easier to work with
\chi^{2}
-divergence. To that end, we have the following result.

Proposition 2
.

For

,

\chi^{2}(P_{Y^{\ast}}\|P_{Y_{r}})\leq\zeta(c_{\star}^{-1})\frac{17}{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)},

where

\zeta(t)=\frac{(t-1)^{2}}{t-1-\log(t)}.

(40)

Proof:

By combining
[

, Eq. (169)]
and

where

(42)

Putting together (

) and (

) yields

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(P_{Y^{\ast}}\|P_{Y_{r}})

\displaystyle\leq\zeta(\beta_{1}^{-1})\frac{17}{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)}

III-C

A Lower Bound on the Support Size

We now state the main result of this work, which is an improved lower bound on the cardinality of
P_{X^{\ast}}
.

Theorem 4
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

|\mathrm{supp}(P_{X^{\ast}})|\geq\max\left\{2,\,\mathrm{e}^{C(n)},\,\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{n\,\log^{+}\!\left(\frac{\log\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2e}\right)}{37850}\right)}\right\},

(45)

where
C(n)
is the capacity defined in (
2
) and
\log^{+}(x)=\max\{0,\log(x)\}
.

Proof:

See Section
V-D
.
∎

We make the following remarks:

•

The first two bounds in (
45
) have been shown in
[
21
]
.

•

In view of Theorems
1
and
2
, we have that
\mathrm{e}^{C(n)}=\Theta(\sqrt{n})
. Therefore, for large

the bound in (

) can be expressed as

|\mathrm{supp}(P_{X^{\ast}})|\geq\Omega\!\left(\sqrt{n\log\log n}\right).

(46)

•

The explicit constants are not optimized. In particular, the improvement over the order-

.

IV
On the Best Approximation Theory of Finite Binomial Mixtures

The key result for providing a lower bound on the support size is the following theorem. It quantifies the best possible approximation of the reference Beta-binomial distribution
P_{Y_{r}}
by a binomial mixture with finitely many components. Its proof follows from an adaptation of the trigonometric moment method for mixture of Gaussian distributions of
[
14
, Thm. 7]
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
X
be a discrete random variable with
K
mass points in
[0,1]
. Let
Y
be the output of the binomial channel induced by
X
. Let
Y_{r}
be the output distribution induced by the reference input
X_{r}\sim\text{Beta}(1/2,1/2)
. Then, for any integer
L
such that
K<L\leq\frac{n+2}{2}
, we have

\chi^{2}(P_{Y}\|P_{Y_{r}})\geq B_{n}(L):=\frac{L-K}{2L\prod_{j=1}^{2L-2}\frac{n+j}{n-j+1}}.

(47)

Proof:

The proof proceeds by leveraging the spectral properties of the conditional expectation operators associated with the binomial channel and applying the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem to a moment matrix of arbitrary dimension
L
.

For any integer
m\leq n
, we can truncate the sum to lower bound the divergence (
II-E
):

\chi^{2}(P_{Y}\|P_{Y_{r}})\geq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\epsilon_{k}^{2}}{h_{k}}.

(48)

Let
L
be an integer such that
K<L
and
2L-2\leq n
. Define the
L\times L
moment matrix
M
with entries
M_{i,j}=\mathbb{E}_{X}[\tilde{T}_{i}(X)\tilde{T}_{j}(X)]
for
0\leq i,j\leq L-1
. Since
X
is supported on
K
mass points,
M
can be written as the sum of
K
rank-1 matrices, implying
\text{rank}(M)\leq K
.

Let
D
be the corresponding matrix for the reference distribution
X_{r}
, so
D_{i,j}=\mathbb{E}_{X_{r}}[\tilde{T}_{i}(X_{r})\tilde{T}_{j}(X_{r})]
. The matrix
D
is diagonal with entries
D_{0,0}=1
and
D_{i,i}=1/2
for
i\geq 1
. The singular values of
D
are
1
(with multiplicity 1) and
1/2
(with multiplicity
L-1
).

By the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem
[
7
]
, the Frobenius distance from
D
to any matrix of rank at most
K
is lower-bounded by the sum of the squared
L-K
smallest singular values of
D
. Since
K\geq 1
, the
L-K
smallest singular values are all
1/2
. Thus,

\|M-D\|_{F}^{2}\geq(L-K)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{L-K}{4}.

(49)

Using the trigonometric identity
\cos(i\theta)\cos(j\theta)=\frac{1}{2}(\cos((i+j)\theta)+\cos(|i-j|\theta))
, the product of Chebyshev polynomials linearizes as

\tilde{T}_{i}(x)\tilde{T}_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{T}_{i+j}(x)+\tilde{T}_{|i-j|}(x)\right).

(50)

Thus, the entries of the moment matrix
M
are given by
M_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon_{i+j}+\epsilon_{|i-j|})
.
To elegantly express the difference matrix
M-D
, we define a modified sequence
\tilde{\epsilon}_{k}
:

\tilde{\epsilon}_{k}=\begin{cases}\epsilon_{k}&\text{if }k\geq 1,\\
0&\text{if }k=0.\end{cases}

(51)

Since
\epsilon_{0}=1
, it is straightforward to verify that for all
0\leq i,j\leq L-1
,

M_{i,j}-D_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\epsilon}_{i+j}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{|i-j|}).

(52)

Indeed, for
i\neq j
,
D_{i,j}=0
and the indices
i+j,|i-j|\geq 1
. For
i=j\geq 1
,
D_{i,i}=1/2
and
\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\epsilon}_{2i}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{2i}=M_{i,i}-1/2
. For
i=j=0
,
D_{0,0}=1
and
\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0})=0=M_{0,0}-1
.

Using the inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
, we bound the squared entries:

(M_{i,j}-D_{i,j})^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\epsilon}_{i+j}^{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{|i-j|}^{2}).

(53)

Summing over all
0\leq i,j\leq L-1
:

\|M-D\|_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=0}^{L-1}(\tilde{\epsilon}_{i+j}^{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{|i-j|}^{2})=\sum_{k=1}^{2L-2}S_{k}\tilde{\epsilon}_{k}^{2},

(54)

where
S_{k}=\frac{1}{2}(N_{1}(k)+N_{2}(k))
, with
N_{1}(k)
being the number of pairs
(i,j)\in\{0,\dots,L-1\}^{2}
such that
i+j=k
, and
N_{2}(k)
the number of pairs such that
|i-j|=k
. The
k=0
term vanishes because
\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}=0
.
For
1\leq k\leq L-1
,
N_{1}(k)=k+1
and
N_{2}(k)=2(L-k)
, giving
S_{k}=L-\frac{k-1}{2}\leq L
.
For
L\leq k\leq 2L-2
,
N_{1}(k)=2L-1-k
and
N_{2}(k)=0
, giving
S_{k}=L-\frac{k+1}{2}\leq L
.
Since
\tilde{\epsilon}_{k}=\epsilon_{k}
for
k\geq 1
, we obtain:

\|M-D\|_{F}^{2}\leq L\sum_{k=1}^{2L-2}\epsilon_{k}^{2}.

(55)

Combining this with (
49
) yields:

\sum_{k=1}^{2L-2}\epsilon_{k}^{2}\geq\frac{L-K}{4L}.

(56)

Notice from (
19
) that
h_{k}
is strictly increasing with
k
because each factor
\frac{n+j}{n-j+1}>1
for
j\geq 1
. Thus,
h_{k}\leq h_{2L-2}
for all
1\leq k\leq 2L-2
. Returning to the
\chi^{2}
divergence (
48
) with
m=2L-2
:

\chi^{2}(P_{Y}\|P_{Y_{r}})\geq\sum_{k=1}^{2L-2}\frac{\epsilon_{k}^{2}}{h_{k}}\geq\frac{1}{h_{2L-2}}\sum_{k=1}^{2L-2}\epsilon_{k}^{2}\geq\frac{L-K}{4Lh_{2L-2}}.

(57)

Substituting the expression for
h_{2L-2}
yields the final lower bound:

\chi^{2}(P_{Y}\|P_{Y_{r}})\geq\frac{L-K}{2L\prod_{j=1}^{2L-2}\frac{n+j}{n-j+1}}.

(58)

This completes the proof of Theorem
5
.
∎

The lower bound in Theorem
5
depends on the free integer parameter
L
satisfying
K<L
and
2L-2\leq n
. To obtain the strongest possible bound, we should maximize
B_{n}(L)
over all admissible values of
L
. This optimization does not have a closed-form solution. Thus, we establish an explicit lower bound in the next proposition.

Proposition 3
.

Let
L
be an integer such that
K<L\leq\frac{n+2}{2}
. Then

B_{n}(L)\geq\frac{L-K}{2L}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(2L-2)^{2}}{\,n-2L+3\,}\right).

(59)

Proof:

Set

. Then

\displaystyle\log\!\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{n+j}{n-j+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\log\!\left(1+\frac{2j-1}{n-j+1}\right)

(60)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{2j-1}{n-j+1}

(61)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n-m+1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(2j-1)

(62)

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{2}}{n-m+1}

(63)

where in (

\log(1+x)\leq x
for
x>-1

, we have the final result

∎

VI
Conclusion

We have shown that the support size of the capacity-achieving input distribution of the binomial channel must grow faster than the previously known order
\sqrt{n}
. More precisely, we proved a lower bound of order
\sqrt{n\log\log n}
, up to explicit constants. The proof combines a quantitative lower bound on the
\chi^{2}
divergence from a reference Beta-binomial output with new upper and lower bounds on the capacity with vanishing gap.

Several questions remain open. The exact asymptotic growth of the support size is still unknown, and there remains a substantial gap between the lower bound proved here and the best known upper bound of order
n/2
. It would also be of interest to refine the explicit constants, obtain sharper non-asymptotic estimates on the output distribution ratio
P_{Y^{\ast}}/P_{Y_{r}}
, and investigate whether the approximation viewpoint developed here can be transferred to related discrete-output channels.
