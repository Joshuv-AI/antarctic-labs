Effects of rearing system and environmental enrichment on production performance and welfare of slow-growing broilers
Authors: Haixuan Deng, Liuting Wu, Yibiao Zhang, Leilei Peng, Kunlong Qi, Yang Liu, Yinuo Wang, Yingjie Wang, Gang Shu, Xiaoling Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough consumers place significant emphasis on healthy poultry products, research on welfare-oriented production systems for broilers remains relatively scarce. This study aimed to explore a tailored production system to improve the welfare of slow-growing broilers. Totally 1350 females of Mountain Plum Blossom chickens from Southwest China were randomly allocated into four production systems, each with three replicates per group and 90 chickens per replicate. The systems included plastic net rearing without environmental enrichment (PNR+NEE group), free-range rearing without environmental enrichment (FR+NEE group), plastic net rearing with environmental enrichment (PNR+EE group), and free-range with environmental enrichment (FR+EE group). The environmental enrichment provided in this experiment consisted of sand bathing pool, perches, ramps, platforms, and hide houses. Results showed that the broilers reared in the PNR+NEE and PNR+EE groups had higher final weight than those in the FR+NEE and FR+EE groups (P < 0.05). However, the FR groups, with or without environmental enrichment, showed a higher eviscerated percentage and lower abdominal fat weight and abdominal fat index than the PNR groups without environmental enrichment (P < 0.05). Meanwhile, the FR groups, with or without environmental enrichment, showed higher redness (a*) and yellowness (b*) values in breast muscles than the PNR groups with or without environmental enrichment (P < 0.05). Monounsaturated fatty acids in both breast and thigh muscle from the FR+NEE and FR+EE groups were higher than those from the PNR+NEE and PNR+EE groups (P < 0.05). Broilers in the FR+EE and PNR+EE groups had better welfare indicators, with lower feather scores and TI duration than the FR+NEE and PNR+NEE groups (P < 0.05). Notably, FR treatment increased serum dopamine levels compared with PNR treatments, regardless of with or without environmental enrichment (P < 0.05). In addition, serum TNF-α levels were lower in the FR+EE and PNR+EE groups than in the FR+NEE and PNR+NEE groups (P < 0.05). These results indicated that the production system with environmental enrichment optimized both animal welfare and meat quality, providing a practical system for broiler production.Keywords: Slow-growing broiler, Environmental enrichment, Rearing system, Animal welfare

Introduction

The increasing consumer demand for improved animal welfare and high-quality poultry products has prompted the poultry industry to reconsider conventional broiler production system (Campbell et al., 2025). Traditional cage and indoor pen systems are widely used because they maximize space utilization and production efficiency (Jin et al., 2019). However, accumulating evidence suggests that the severe spatial restriction in such systems can limit natural behaviors and locomotor activity, leading to a higher incidence of skeletal disorders and chronic stress in broilers (Kwon et al., 2024; Shields and Greger, 2013). Consequently, alternative rearing systems that better accommodate behavioral needs are gaining increasing attention in poultry production.

Among these alternatives, free-range systems have been widely explored for their potential to improve both animal welfare and meat quality (Qiaoxian et al., 2020). Free-range systems allow broilers to express natural behaviors through increased space and environmental complexity, while environmental enrichment further enhances welfare conditions in poultry production (Ventura et al., 2010; Colditz et al., 2024). Environmental enrichment refers to modifications of the captive environment designed to increase behavioral opportunities and stimulate physical and cognitive activity, thereby improving the animals’ physiological and psychological well-being (Tahamtani et al., 2020).

Various enrichment strategies have been investigated in broiler production systems. For example, the provision of sand baths and perches has been shown to improve physical condition and reduce undesirable behaviors such as feather pecking (Brantsæter et al., 2017; Yildirim et al., 2021). Moreover, structural enrichments such as ramps, platforms, and barriers can provide additional resting areas and stimulate locomotor activity, thereby reducing stress levels (Kang et al., 2024). Outdoor enrichment structures, including artificial shade and perches, may further enhance environmental complexity and improve thermal comfort for broilers with outdoor access (Chen et al., 2013; Santos et al., 2014). Collectively, these studies highlight the important role of environmental enrichment in promoting behavioral expression and improving the welfare status of broiler chickens.

Despite the reported benefits of environmental enrichment, its effectiveness may vary depending on multiple factors, including lighting conditions (Kang, et al., 2024), stocking density (Anderson et al., 2021), and genotype (Mattioli et al., 2024). Moreover, most previous studies have examined enrichment strategies within a single housing system. Systematic evaluations that integrate environmental enrichment across different production systems remain limited. Therefore, a comprehensive assessment combining multiple rearing systems and enrichment strategies is necessary to better understand their combined effects on broiler welfare and productivity.

Although free-range systems offer potential welfare benefits, they may also increase the exposure of birds to environmental pathogens and climatic challenges (Jeni et al., 2021), Therefore, chicken breeds with stronger immune capacity and better skeletal integrity are generally considered more suitable for free-range systems. Compared with fast-growing broilers, slow-growing broilers typically exhibit improved locomotor ability, enhanced disease resistance, and better adaptability to outdoor environments (Abdourhamane İ and Petek, 2024). These characteristics make slow-growing broilers particularly suitable for welfare-oriented production systems.

The Mountain Plum Blossom chicken is a slow-growing broiler breed originating from the Qinba Mountain region of Southwest China, reaching a market weight of approximately 2.5 kg at 180 days of age (Ran et al., 2023). In the present study, we evaluated the effects of different production systems on growth performance, carcass traits, meat quality, skeletal development, physiological parameters, and welfare indicators in this breed. The objective of this study was to provide a comprehensive assessment of welfare-oriented production systems and to provide fundamental data for the application of welfare-oriented rearing systems in broiler production.

Materials and methods

Animals and treatments

This study was conducted in Tongjiang County, Sichuan province, P. R. China (latitude 31° 53′ N, longitude 107° 05′ E; elevation 607.5 meters above sea level). A total of 1350 female Mountain Plum Blossom chickens with body weight 1.4 ± 0.25 kg on 105 d were adopted. All of them were vaccinated for Marek’s Disease. The experimental lasted from 105 d to 180 d. Birds were randomly assigned to 4 systems: plastic net rearing (PNR+NEE) system, plastic net rearing with environmental enrichment (PNR+EE) system (Fig. 1A), free-range rearing (FR+NEE) system, and free-range rearing with environmental enrichment (FR+EE) system (Fig. 1B). Each group had three replicates and 90 birds per replicate.

Rearing System. During the experiment, the mean outdoor temperature and humidity were 25.4 °C and 75%, respectively, while the mean indoor temperature and humidity were 26.2 °C and 60%, respectively. Photoperiod for indoor rearing systems was 16 light:8 dark, with lights turned on at 6:00 and off at 22:00 per day, the same photoperiod was also applied in the houses of the free-range system. The PNR birds were raised in plastic net-floor pens, with pen size 8 m × 2 m, their stocking density was 5.625 birds per square meter. The FR groups were set in a pear orchard with 21,010.5 m². Each replicate unit had a mobile house (8 m × 2 m, stocking density: 5.625 birds/m²) and 20 m × 20 m outdoor areas (stocking density: 0.225 birds/m²) facilitated by fence. The outdoor areas were planted pear trees with a density of approximately 1 tree (height of 2.1 m) per square meter average.

Environmental Enrichment. The enrichment facilities comprised a sand bathing pool (dimensions: 140 cm × 100 cm × 5 cm), perches (150 cm × 40 cm), ramps (67 cm × 83 cm × 50 cm), platforms as described by Bailie et al. (2013), and hide houses (140 cm × 100 cm × 55 cm), all constructed from wooden planks and polyethylene composite boards. The PNR+NEE group were not provided with any environmental enrichment. Conversely, the PNR+EE group had the enrichment facilities installed centrally. The FR+NEE group did not fix environmental enrichment outdoor, whereas the FR+EE group had enrichment facilities far away from 5 meters outdoor.

Assessment of production performance

Growth Performance. Body weight and feed intake were recorded at 105 and 180 days of age to calculate the average daily gain (ADG, g/d/chicken). Feed consumption was determined for each replicate by weighing the residual feed daily, thereby calculating the feed conversion ratio (FCR).

Slaughter Performance and Carcass Traits. At 180 days, five hens per replicate were randomly selected from four treatment groups, respectively, and humanely euthanized via intravenous injection of sodium pentobarbital 20% (P0225-5 G, Ekear, Shanghai, China) following cervical dislocation. Carcass traits, including eviscerated weight, semi-eviscerated weight, breast muscle weight, abdominal fat weight, and organs weight, were measured. The organ index was calculated by dividing the organ weight by the body weight to ascertain the respective yields ((Weight of organ / Live weight) × 100).

Meat Quality. Samples were collected from the left-side breast and drum muscles to measure pH value, flesh color, and shear force, in accordance with the guidelines of Chinese Performance Terms and Measurement Method for Poultry (NY/T 823-2020). The pH value was measured by a portable pH meter (Testo 205, TestoInc, Lenzkirch, Germany), with pH1 recorded at 45 min post-slaughter and pH2 measured after 24 h of storage at 2–8°C. Parameters L*, a*, and b* values were determined through triplicate measurements taken at three sites of each sample via a colorimeter (CR-300, Minolta Camera, Osaka, Japan), which equipped with illuminant D65 and an 8.0-mm diameter aperture, in according to the methodology described by Deng et al. (2022).

Samples were grounded and analyzed using a near-infrared analyzer (F4020, Spectrastar, Guangdong, China) to determine moisture, crude protein, and crude fat content, with the methodology described by Prieto et al. (2014). After boiled for five minutes, the meat was carefully stripped of fascia and fat, then sectioned into cubes aligned with the muscle fibers, with a diameter of 12 mm and a length of 3 cm, for further assessment via a tenderness analyzer (TA.XTC-18, Bosin Tech, Shanghai, China). The peak force for each sample was recorded. For amino acid analysis, 0.5 g samples underwent hydrolysis in 6 mol/L HCl at 105°C for 24 h, followed by detection with a Hitachi analyzer (L-8900, Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan). The fatty acid composition was determined according to the procedure described by Suliman et al. (2023).

Assessment of animal welfare

The Welfare Quality® assessment was carried out on 180 d, with 10 birds per replicate randomly selected from each treatment group.

Fearfulness. To avoid disturbing visual assessments on behaviors, tonic immobility (TI) tests were conducted prior to other evaluations. The TI procedure followed Fogelholm's method, birds were laid on their backs in a 50 cm U-shaped wooden cradle. Their chests were gently pressed for 10 s and then released gradually. We repeated the induction if the birds recovered within 30 s, with no more than three attempts per bird. The duration of tonic immobility was recorded once the response occurred, with a maximum duration of 10 min per test (Fogelholm et al., 2019).

Appearance Evaluation. Feather coverage was assessed following Pichová's method (Pichová et al., 2017), using a handheld infrared thermography camera (Fotric 348+-L25, Shanghai, China). The EasyIR 1.0.1 software calculated the percentage of exposed skin area (pixels with elevated temperature) at selected abdominal and dorsal regions, with scores assigned as follows: 0 (no exposed skin), 1 ( ≤ 20% exposed), or 2 ( > 20% exposed), according to established criteria.

Footpad Dermatitis. Footpad dermatitis (FPD) and hock burn were evaluated using a 5-point scale, where scores ranged from 0 (indicating no lesions) to 4 (indicating ulceration/scabbing, hemorrhage, or deep dermatitis for FPD, and brown/black discoloration with multiple lesions totaling more than 0.5 cm² for hock burn), with the highest score recorded from either foot or leg, as described by Riber et al. (2020).

Development of Tibia. The development of the tibia was assessed through the quantification of fluctuating asymmetry (FA), following the methodology outlined by Buijs et al. (2012). Bilateral tibia length and tibia circumference were measured using vernier calipers, and FA values were derived from these measurements. The FA was calculated using the formula |R–L|/((R+L)/2), where R and L denote the measurements from the right and left sides of each bilateral trait (tibia length, tibia circumference), respectively.

Serum Biochemical Indices. Before slaughter, blood samples (3.5 mL per bird) were collected from the brachial vein and stored in non-anticoagulant vacuum tubes. Immediately after collection, the blood samples were centrifuged at 4,000 × g for 10 m at 4 °C. The determination of serum biochemical indices was performed using commercially kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of Bioengineering, Nanjing, China). The determined indices included: norepinephrine (NE), 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT), dopamine (DA), total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD), malondialdehyde (MDA), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-2 (IL-2), interleukin-6 (IL-6).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted utilizing SPSS software version 27.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). For datasets exhibiting normal distribution, two-way analysis of variance was employed, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons, to assess growth performance, slaughter performance, meat quality, and skeletal characteristics. Conversely, non-normally distributed data pertaining to welfare evaluation were analyzed using the Kruskal-Wallis test. A significance threshold was established at P < 0.05. The correlation relationships among welfare indicators, slaughter performance, and meat quality were examined using Spearman’s correlation coefficients for continuous data. Data visualization and correlation analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 9.4.1, USA).

Discussion

The present study systematically evaluated the effects of several production systems on production performance and welfare indicators in slow-growing broilers. The results indicated that incorporating environmental enrichment in free-range rearing systems can further improve broiler welfare and meat quality, providing a practical model for broiler production.

Growth performance is a key indicator for evaluating production efficiency in poultry systems (Li et al., 2017). In the current study, welfare-oriented system slightly decreased body weights but increased the eviscerated yield, which is directly associated with improved economic return due to its close relationship with meat yield (Zhao et al., 2024). In contrast, the plastic net rearing system resulted in a higher abdominal fat percentage, possibly indicating that restricted movement limits energy expenditure and promotes lipid deposition in broilers (Chen et al., 2023). These finding are consistent with the previous studies reporting greater abdominal fat accumulation in caged broilers compared with birds raised in alternative production system (Yan et al., 2021; Song et al., 2022).

Organ indices are widely used as indicators of organ development and physiological function in poultry (Zhang et al., 2025a). In the present study, free-range broilers exhibited larger gizzard and proventriculus indices compared with plastic net rearing birds. A prior research has demonstrated that the ingestion of fibrous materials, grit, and other natural particles in outdoor environments stimulates mechanical digestion and promotes the development of digestive organs in broilers (González-Alvarado et al., 2008). In the present experiment, liver weight and liver index increased while abdominal fat rate decreased in free-range broilers, suggesting that free rearing strengthened liver metabolism and reduced fat accumulation (Niamat et al., 2025). In addition, our results showed that welfare-oriented production systems increased both the heart weight and heart index of broilers. Increased locomotor activity in free-range environments has been shown to stimulate cardiovascular development and elevate heart index in broilers (Wang et al., 2021). Similarly, higher activity levels can enhance energy expenditure and reduce lipid deposition (Li et al., 2025). Therefore, the increased heart index and reduced abdominal fat percentage of broilers in free-range systems are likely associated with increased physical activity facilitated by environmental enrichment and outdoor access.

Indicators of meat quality are essential for assessing the quality of poultry meat. Meat color is primarily determined by myoglobin concentration in muscle tissue and strongly influences consumer purchasing decisions (Majdi Abdelfaraj et al., 2023). In the present study, broilers raised in welfare-oriented systems exhibited higher redness (a*) and yellowness (b*) values, which is consistent with previous reports on free-range chickens (Wang et al., 2009). It is hypothesized that increased physical activity in free-range environments promotes the development of oxidative muscle fibers and elevates myoglobin content, which may contribute to the observed changes in meat color (Altmann et al., 2023). The elevated shear force observed in FR and EE treatments reflects a increase in chewiness, which may be attributed to enhanced muscle fiber development induced by higher physical activity in free-range and enriched systems. Flavor is another critical determinant of consumer acceptance of meat products and results from complex interactions between taste and aroma compounds (Sitz et al., 2005). Amino acid and fatty acid composition are important contributors to meat flavor characteristics (Liu et al., 2023b). Amino acids such as glutamic acid, alanine, aspartic acid, and glycine are recognized as key contributors to umami flavor in chicken meat (Zhang et al., 2022). In the present study, broilers raised in enrichment systems exhibited higher glycine content in thigh muscle compared with normal broilers, although total amino acid contents did not differ significantly among groups. These results potentially suggest that enrichment conditions may promote the deposition of certain flavor-related amino acids without substantially altering overall amino acid.

Free-range rearing also improved external welfare indicators and skeletal integrity, which may be associated with increased locomotor activity. Feather condition is a recognized indicator of broiler welfare status (Saraiva et al., 2016), and improved feather coverage observed in free-range birds may be attributed to greater space allowance and reduced feather pecking associated with lower stocking density (Baxter et al., 2018). Environmental enrichment can further stimulate behavioral activity and promote adaptive behavioral responses in poultry (Bonnefous et al., 2022). We found that environmental enrichment exerted a more remarkable improving effect on feather condition of broilers in plastic net rearing systems than in free-range systems. FA is regarded a measure of developmental stability, with higher asymmetry values resulting in worse skeletal functional morphology (Carla et al., 2015). We found that the free-range system and environmental enrichment had positive effects on reducing tibia length FA in broiler chickens. These findings support the conclusion that rearing systems restricting movement can negatively affect skeletal health and overall welfare.

Physiological and immunological responses further indicated that welfare-oriented systems promoted improved biological resilience. Serum biochemical parameters are sensitive indicators of physiological status (Liu et al., 2023a; Zhang et al., 2025b), and elevated dopamine levels in the EE groups suggest enhanced positive affective states and greater engagement in natural foraging behavior (Kang et al., 2020; Moe et al., 2014). Outdoor systems and environmental enrichment were also associated with enhanced antioxidant capacity, as reflected by higher T-AOC, a comprehensive indicator of systemic redox balance (Han et al., 2020). Although free-range exposure increased serum TNF-α levels and spleen index, indicating greater immune activation likely due to environmental antigen challenge (Tong et al., 2015), enrichment appeared to modulate this response by reducing TNF-α and increasing IL-2 levels, thereby promoting a more balanced immune regulation (Stefanetti et al., 2023). Collectively, these results may suggest that production systems encouraging activity and behavioral expression could enhance skeletal robustness, antioxidant defense, and immune adaptability in broilers.

The eviscerated yield serves as a crucial metric reflecting poultry meat production, with direct implications for economic efficiency (Yushanaji et al., 2025). In this study, broilers exhibiting lower hock burn scores and tibia length FA value had higher eviscerated yield. This indicates that improving animal welfare conditions may promote economic benefits. Additionally, the heart index was found to be significantly negative correlated with hock burn score, TI duration, and tibia length FA value in broilers, indicating that a higher heart index may be associated with improved animal welfare status. High heart index is likely due to increased physical activity (Ren et al., 2024), which concurrently elevates overall welfare levels. Increased exercise levels raise myoglobin content, thereby enhancing the a* value of meat (Özbek et al., 2020). Our study identified a significant correlation between the a* value of meat and the TI duration, tibia breaking strength, and tibia index in broilers, which suggests that improving broiler welfare may be linked to enhancing both skeletal development and meat quality. Generally, the welfare-oriented production systems improved carcass traits and meat quality of broilers, which may be associated with elevated locomotor activity.

In the present study, the free-range area was large and covered with dense vegetation, which prevented us from systematically recording the behaviors and physical activity of broilers. Consequently, we failed to obtain intuitive data on their exercise indicators. While the indoor lighting regime was standardized, free-range birds were also exposed to natural daylight. Differences in photoperiod therefore served as a potential confounding factor. Additionally, all experimental data were collected from female chickens only. In future research, we will include broilers of both sexes for comparative measurements and strive to acquire behavioral and activity data of chickens reared in different systems.

Conclusion

The welfare-oriented production systems effectively enhanced the welfare status and meat quality of slow-growing broilers. The free-range conditions with environmental enrichment system significantly improved the meat color, skeletal development and immunity of chickens, while reducing abdominal fat deposition and fearfulness response. These findings indicate that environmental enrichment can optimize broilers’ immune function and welfare status, which may be associated with increased locomotor activity.
