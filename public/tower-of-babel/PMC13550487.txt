A multiscale spatio-temporal convolutional attention network for depressive disorder diagnosis via coarse-grained signal reconstruction based on EEG
Authors: Yixia Zhu, Boyi Huang, Zihao Yan, Wei Liu, Xiaodong Luo, Bin Zhou, Gang Li, Yongwei Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDepression disorder (DD) is a common mental disorder and a leading contributor to the global burden of disease. However, accurate diagnosis remains challenging due to the reliance of conventional approaches on subjective assessments. To address this limitation, this study proposes a Multiscale Spatio-temporal Convolutional Attention Network (MSTANet) for EEG-based depression detection. The proposed framework integrates multiscale coarse-grained signal reconstruction with localized spatio-temporal attention modeling, where the spatial dimension explicitly captures inter-channel relationships between bilateral frontal EEG signals (Fp1 and Fp2). MSTANet adopts a multi-parallel convolutional architecture combined with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) modules and a fully convolutional network (FCN), enabling effective multiscale spatio-temporal feature learning while maintaining a simplified dual-channel frontal EEG acquisition scheme for improved clinical practicality. Ablation and comparative experiments demonstrate that the proposed model outperforms existing methods, achieving an accuracy of 93.61%. To further assess robustness and clinical generalizability, a dual-center validation scheme was conducted. The model was developed exclusively on the HZTPH Dataset (N = 80) and evaluated on a completely independent external cohort from the JHSH Dataset (N = 230), which was not involved in training, validation, or parameter optimization. MSTANet achieved 93.61% accuracy on the development dataset and maintained 90.21% accuracy on the external validation cohort, exhibiting a minimal cross-center performance degradation. Introduction

Depression disorder (DD) is a highly prevalent mental disorder with low treatment rates and high recurrence, characterized by persistent sadness, loss of interest or pleasure, often accompanied by impaired cognitive function and autonomic symptoms such as sleep or appetite disturbances1. The etiology of depression is highly complex and cannot be fully explained by any single established biological or environmental pathway; it results from the combined effects of genetic, environmental, psychological, and physiological factors2. According to World Health Organization (WHO) statistics, over 264 million people worldwide suffer from depression, imposing a significant global economic burden. The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study identifies depression as one of the leading causes of disability globally3. Depression is widely recognized as one of the most pressing mental health issues today.

Diagnosis and treatment of depression face multiple challenges because of significant individual variation in symptoms4, leading to high rates of misdiagnosis, especially among patients with bipolar depression, where around 40% are incorrectly diagnosed with depressive disorder5. Currently, behavioral and symptomatic assessments remain the primary clinical tools for diagnosing depression6, guided by criteria from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5) and the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11)7,8. On one hand, the assessment process heavily relies on physician experience and is susceptible to subjective judgment9. On the other hand, atypical symptoms such as irritability and impulsive behavior are common in adolescent populations10, which lead to misdiagnosis and delayed treatment. Consequently, developing biomarkers with objective and quantitative characteristics and objective, quantifiable diagnostic tools has become an urgent need in psychiatry.

Researchers have explored various objective biomarker approaches to overcome the limitations of traditional subjective diagnosis. In neuroimaging, techniques such as functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI)11, magnetoencephalography12,13, and electroencephalography (EEG)14,15 have been widely adopted. However, fMRI equipment typically associated with high costs, with scan times exceeding 30 min, making it challenging to implement in primary healthcare settings. MEG devices are also incredibly scarce and expensive, posing significant challenges for clinical adoption. Molecular biology research focuses on serum biomarkers. Indicators such as brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)16 and inflammatory factors17 have been shown to correlate with depression severity. However, these markers lack specificity and are susceptible to interference from physical illnesses. The required invasive blood sampling often leads to low patient acceptance. In contrast, EEG technology precisely reflects dynamic brain processes. Its equipment is affordable, and examinations typically take 10 min, enabling rapid outpatient screening. With its unique advantages—noninvasiveness, cost-effectiveness, convenience, and rich signal information—EEG stands out18. It demonstrates significant potential in studying neurological disorders, including depression, positioning it as an ideal candidate for achieving an objective diagnosis19–22.

Traditional EEG signal processing relies on preprocessing techniques such as filtering and noise reduction23. Raw data obtained through conventional single-scale analysis struggles to capture global dynamic patterns and reveal the signal’s complexity completely. Costa et al.24 pioneered the multiscale entropy (MSE) nonlinear analysis method based on sequence coarsening. This approach overcomes the limitation of fixed temporal scales in traditional entropy analysis by coarsening signals (reconstructing sequences) across different time scales, effectively capturing multiscale dynamic patterns of brain activity. In depression research, nonlinear EEG analysis consistently indicates reduced functional complexity in patients25,26, reflecting diminished neuronal activity adaptability and information processing efficiency. MSE methods, through their coarse-graining process, quantify complexity variations across multiple temporal scales, proving particularly suitable for EEG analysis in depression. For instance, a study27 demonstrated that a depression recognition model based on MSE of EEG signals achieved a maximum classification accuracy of 96.42% using KNN when the scale factor was set to 3, further highlighting the significant value of MSE in effectively extracting depression-related EEG features.

Utilizing machine learning to diagnose depression from EEG represents a crucial direction. Traditional methods rely on manual feature extraction28. The rise of deep learning ushered in a new era, particularly with the convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Their powerful automatic feature learning capabilities offer novel approaches for processing complex EEG data8. Early single-scale convolutional kernels struggled to balance local details and global correlations. In 2014, the Inception module introduced parallel structures and multi-sized convolutional kernels to extract features at different scales, enhancing performance while controlling parameter size29. Acharya et al.30 pioneered the application of deep neural networks and CNNs to depression diagnosis, achieving favorable classification results. To enable deeper learning models to focus more precisely on critical information, the Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) attention mechanism was introduced. By automatically learning dependencies between channels, it enhances responses from key electrode channels31, further improving the model’s ability to concentrate on essential information. Beyond these convolutional enhancements, recent studies have further explored Transformer-based architectures to capture global spatio-temporal dynamics; for instance, Tao et al.32 proposed a Multimodal Spatio-Temporal Attentiona Transformer (DepMSTAT) for effective feature fusion, while Yang et al.33 developed a Spike Memory Transformer (SMT) to achieve energy-efficient autonomous detection. These studies collectively demonstrate that deep learning and enhanced CNNs can serve as clinically effective computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) systems for depression.

This study proposes a novel Multiscale Spatio-temporal Attention Network (MSTANet) for efficient and accurate EEG-based depression diagnosis. The framework explicitly models joint temporal dynamics and localized inter-channel relationships between bilateral frontal EEG signals (Fp1 and Fp2). By integrating multiscale coarse-grained signal reconstruction with a multiscale spatio-temporal attention network, the proposed approach captures EEG dynamics across different temporal resolutions while preserving critical frontal asymmetry information. The MSTANet architecture consists of three key components: (1) a multiscale deep convolutional network for parallel feature extraction across coarse-graining scales, (2) an SE module for adaptive channel-wise feature recalibration, and (3) a fully connected network classifier for final discrimination. Focusing exclusively on dual frontal channels (Fp1 and Fp2), the proposed framework substantially improves clinical practicality and scalability. Systematic ablation studies and comparative experiments with representative models (e.g., AttenSleep) demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed method. Overall, this work presents a robust and clinically feasible EEG-based depression detection framework and highlights the translational potential of coarse-grained deep learning combined with an optimized dual-channel frontal EEG configuration for large-scale screening applications.

Participants

HZTPH dataset

Participants were rigorously screened according to predefined inclusion criteria. EEG data were collected at Huzhou Third People’s Hospital, where patients with depression were recruited from outpatient services and healthy controls from hospital staff and the local community. The resulting dataset is hereafter referred to as the HZTPH Dataset. All participants completed standardized clinical assessments, including the HAMD-17 questionnaire. Healthy controls scored ≤ 7, whereas patients with depression scored > 17, ensuring clear diagnostic separation. Only right-handed participants were included to reduce hemispheric variability. Alcohol consumption and psychotropic drug use were prohibited for at least 8 h prior to EEG acquisition, which was conducted in a quiet, electromagnetically shielded environment. Ethical approval was obtained from the institutional ethics committee.

The final dataset comprised 80 participants, including 42 patients with depression (11 males, 31 females; age: 19–61 years, 40.48 ± 11.64 years) and 38 healthy controls (11 males, 27 females; age: 21–57 years, 36.97 ± 11.44 years). No significant age differences were observed between groups, while HAMD-17 scores differed significantly. Detailed demographic information is provided in Table 1.

EEG data acquisition and preprocessing

EEG signals were acquired using a Nicolet TS215605 system equipped with 16 electrodes covering standard scalp locations. Although the system supports multi-regional recording, the experimental design focused on frontal EEG activity; therefore, data collection was limited to the Fp1 (left frontal) and Fp2 (right frontal) electrodes. This selection was guided by neurophysiological evidence indicating that depressive disorder is associated with functional abnormalities in the prefrontal cortex, including altered frontal oscillatory activity and hemispheric asymmetry, particularly in the alpha and beta bands, which are linked to impaired emotion regulation and negative affective bias34,35. As bilateral frontal electrodes, Fp1 and Fp2 provide a physiologically meaningful and clinically feasible means of capturing frontal functional imbalance relevant to depressive pathology36,37. EEG acquisition was performed using Net Station software. Electrode placement followed the international 10–20 system, with impedance maintained below 10 kΩ and a sampling rate of 250 Hz. Participants cleaned their scalps prior to recording. Subjects remained with eyes closed for 5 min, after which a 10-minute resting-state EEG recording was conducted once signals stabilized.

The EEG data underwent a rigorous preprocessing pipeline to minimize artifacts, with particular attention to the frontal channels (FP1/FP2), which are especially susceptible to ocular and myogenic contamination. First, a band-pass filter from 4 to 30 Hz was applied. The high-pass cutoff at 4 Hz attenuated slow drifts and electrooculogram-related activity, while the low-pass cutoff at 30 Hz suppressed high-frequency electromyogram noise. Following filtering, the signals were downsampled from 250 Hz to 125 Hz to reduce data volume while preserving the frequency content of interest. The continuous EEG recordings were then segmented into non-overlapping 12-second epochs, and baseline correction was applied to each epoch to remove residual DC offsets and ultra-slow drifts, ensuring signal stability across time windows.

Independent Component Analysis (ICA) was subsequently performed to decompose the preprocessed EEG into temporally independent components. Components exhibiting characteristics of eye blinks, lateral eye movements, and muscle activity were first automatically identified using the ICLabel classifier and then manually re-examined to ensure accurate rejection. The remaining components were used to reconstruct the artifact-reduced EEG signals, which were then subjected to further analysis in the Theta, Alpha, and Beta frequency bands.

Coarse-grained signal reconstruction

This study proposes multiple coarse-grained signal reconstruction methods based on multiscale reconstruction principles and moving average techniques, aiming to enhance signal processing and analysis capabilities for more precise depression diagnosis. The integration of these approaches provides more efficient technical support for depression detection. The following sections will give a detailed introduction to the relevant methods, including their theoretical foundations, implementation processes, and specific applications in depression detection, further elucidating their significant role in signal processing and reconstruction.

Multiscale analysis

Multiscale analysis originates from the fundamental concept of signal or data coarsening, known as the “coarsening” process. This method creates signals with varying scales or resolutions by averaging different numbers of consecutive data points. Widely applied in fields such as signal processing, image processing, and time series analysis, it helps reveal data’s multi-level structure and dynamic characteristics, thereby providing more comprehensive, multidimensional information. The moving average method is a statistical technique based on time series data. Its core principle involves calculating the average of a fixed number of consecutive data points through incremental shifting, smoothing fluctuations within the sequence. To clarify the adaptability of the proposed coarse-grained reconstruction strategies, they are briefly related to the intrinsic properties of depressive EEG signals, which involve altered nonlinear dynamics and scale-dependent temporal abnormalities, thereby motivating the use of multiscale temporal representations. Accordingly, the three proposed methods, non-OL, OLMOP, and OLROP, are designed with distinct temporal sampling mechanisms within the coarse-graining process, resulting in different sensitivities to EEG dynamics. In the non-OL method, long-term trends and low-frequency components are emphasized through non-overlapping averaging, making this method suitable for capturing global alterations in depressive EEG at coarser scales, while fine temporal details are sacrificed. In contrast, in the OLMOP method, maximal temporal continuity is preserved through one-point sliding overlap, and short-range dependencies and subtle fluctuations are maintained, which are critical for modeling reduced adaptability and nonlinear micro-dynamics in depressive EEG. In the OLROP method, a balance between temporal sensitivity and robustness is achieved by partially retaining overlap, enabling stable representations under moderate nonstationarity. Unlike traditional multiscale entropy or frequency-domain approaches, in which each scale is compressed into low-dimensional descriptors, complete time-domain sequences are preserved at each scale by the proposed reconstruction methods, allowing scale-specific and cross-scale temporal features to be learned by deep neural networks in an end-to-end manner.

Within each time window, EEG signals were transformed into a feature matrix of size 2 × 1500, where 2 corresponds to the FP1 and FP2 channels and 1500 denotes the number of segmented data points. In total, 2661 samples were obtained. To evaluate the influence of different coarse-graining strategies on model performance, multiscale processing was performed using scale factors ranging from 2 to 100, with scale factor 1 representing the original signal. Each scale factor generated signal matrices with different temporal resolutions; for example, under the OLMOP method, scale factors of 2, 3, and 100 produced matrices of size 2 × 1499, 2 × 1498, and 2 × 1401, respectively.

For each coarse-graining method, the feature matrix at scale factor 1 was combined with those from other scales, and all matrices were padded with zeros to ensure uniform dimensions, resulting in a 4 × 1500 multiscale feature matrix. Through this process, 100 multiscale signals were obtained, each of which was used as input to the classification model to provide enriched temporal representations.

MSTANet model design

This study proposes a novel deep learning model, the MSTANet, which integrates a multiscale deep convolutional network, a separable convolutional network, an attention mechanism, and a fully convolutional network (FCN) classification module. The design of the deep convolutional network (Block1) and the separable convolutional network (Block2) is inspired by EEGNet38, and the corresponding architecture is illustrated in Fig. 4.

EEGNet architecture diagram.

The deep convolutional network comprises an initial input convolutional layer (Conv2D), a normalization operation (BatchNorm2D), a deep convolutional block (DeepWiseConv2D), a normalization and activation operation (BatchNorm2D (ELU)), a pooling layer (AveragePool2D), and a dropout operation. Conv2D extracts preliminary feature representations by mapping raw EEG signals into a higher-dimensional space; BathNorm2D accelerates training and enhances model stability, while ELU maintains consistent performance when processing signals of varying amplitudes; DeepWiseConv2D performs convolutions independently on each input channel, significantly reducing computational load and parameter count; AveragePool2D preserves feature map information more smoothly, mitigating noise and outlier effects introduced by max pooling; Dropout is a widely used regularization technique in neural networks, which is designed to reduce model overfitting.

Separable Convolutional Networks comprise SeparableConv2D blocks, BatchNorm2D (ELU), AveragePool2D, and Dropout. SeparableConv2D consists of DeepWiseConv2D and Pointwise Convolution, further reducing computational complexity by decomposing traditional convolutional operations into two smaller convolutional operations.

This study innovatively introduces three key improvements in order to enhance the model’s data attention capability and generalization performance: a multi-parallel convolution structure, an attention mechanism, and an FCN classifier. The improved model based on these enhancements is named MSTANet, as shown in Fig. 5.

Multiscale deep convolutional network

This study extends the deep convolutional network by adding five parallel Block1 units to MSTANet and assigning each Block1 a distinct kernel size to capture spatio-temporal features more comprehensively in EEG signals. This multi-parallel convolution architecture simultaneously processes features across different scales, enhancing the model’s representational capacity for complex EEG signals and further improving model performance.

Attention mechanism

In this study, the SE attention mechanism is introduced after the convolutional module. The SE attention mechanism is a lightweight channel attention module designed to effectively model dependencies among feature map channels through two sequential operations, namely “Squeeze” and “Excitation”39. Specifically, an SE module is employed to explicitly capture correlations between feature channels and to adaptively recalibrate channel-wise feature responses. In MSTANet, two SE modules with distinct parameters (denoted as SE1 and SE2) are incorporated after Block1 and Block2, respectively. The SE1 module is utilized to enhance salient features during the initial feature extraction stage, whereas the SE2 module is applied to further adjust feature weights during the deeper feature fusion stage. Through this design, MSTANet is enabled to more effectively attend to features that are critical for the classification task.

To further clarify the design rationale of the proposed architecture, the selection logic of key structural parameters is explained as follows. The selection of distinct kernel sizes in the parallel Block1 units draws inspiration from the Inception architecture40 to capture multiscale temporal dynamics, while the foundational convolutional structure aligns with the established EEGNet design41. Furthermore, the parameters of the SE modules were configured to explicitly model channel dependencies, a strategy validated by Tang et al.42 for enhancing EEG feature decoding.

FCN classifier

Traditional deep learning models typically employ fully connected layers (FCLs) for final classification. However, this approach tends to introduce excessive parameters, leading to model overfitting and hindering the processing of high-dimensional input data. MSTANet utilizes an FCN as its classifier to overcome this limitation. FCN preserve spatial information integrity while progressively reducing feature map dimensions through convolutional layers to output classification results. This architecture reduces model parameters and enhances generalization capabilities43.

Experimental setup and implementation details

All experiments were implemented using the PyTorch framework and conducted on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3080Ti GPU with 12 GB memory. Network training was performed using the AdamW optimizer, in which weight decay was decoupled from gradient-based optimization to enhance generalization. The initial learning rate was set to 1 × 10 − 3 and was dynamically adjusted using a cosine annealing scheduler to promote stable convergence during later training stages. The batch size was fixed at 128, and all models were trained for 200 epochs. Cross-entropy loss was adopted as the objective function for binary classification. To ensure fair comparison and reproducibility, the proposed MSTANet model was trained and evaluated under a unified preprocessing pipeline, data partitioning strategy, training configuration, and evaluation protocol. Baseline methods (EEGNet, AttenSleep, 1D-LSTM, 2D-LSTM, and MSFBCNN) were implemented by closely following their original methodological descriptions in the literature, with minor task-specific adjustments. All experiments employed five-fold cross-validation with a fixed random seed (42) to reduce randomness.

Comparison modelling

AttenSleep model

AttenSleep is a deep learning model that leverages a SE-based architecture for automatic sleep stage classification from single-channel EEG signals44. This task is crucial in researching multiple physiological functions, including sleep quality assessment, immune system monitoring, memory consolidation, and metabolic analysis. Traditional sleep staging relies on manual polysomnography analysis, a cumbersome and time-consuming process, making developing efficient automated classification methods a research priority. The AttenSleep model comprises three main modules: First, a feature extraction module consisting of a multi-resolution CNN and adaptive feature relabeling extracts multiscale low-frequency and high-frequency features from EEG signals, optimizing feature extraction by modeling inter-feature dependencies. Next, the temporal context encoder captures time-dependent patterns in EEG signals through a multi-head attention mechanism, enhancing the model’s ability to process temporal information. Finally, the classifier module performs classification using an FCL and a category-sensitive loss function, addressing data imbalance issues. Given the model’s advanced capabilities in multiscale feature extraction and attention mechanisms, which align closely with the innovative direction of this study, and its focus on minimal channel inputs, it was selected as the key comparison model.

1D-LSTM model

The 1D-LSTM model is a specialized LSTM designed for processing one-dimensional data, widely applied in fields such as time series analysis, signal processing, and text analysis45. As a specialized form of RNN, LSTM incorporates “gate” mechanisms, including the forget gate, input gate, and output gate, to address the vanishing gradient and exploding gradient issues encountered by traditional RNNs when handling long sequences. This enables LSTM to capture long-term dependencies within the data. LSTM is particularly effective for one-dimensional data, as such data often exhibits intricate temporal patterns and long-term dependencies. The 1D-LSTM architecture typically comprises an input layer, LSTM layers, and an output layer. The input layer receives and preprocesses one-dimensional data such as time series, signal samples, or text vectors. The LSTM layer consists of multiple LSTM units, each dynamically controlling information flow through gate mechanisms to learn temporal features from the input data. Finally, the output layer performs classification, regression, or sequence generation according to task requirements. Through backpropagation over time and optimization algorithms, 1D-LSTM can be efficiently trained and optimized. The model’s strength lies in its ability to process sequences of arbitrary length while effectively capturing long-term dependencies within the data, making it a powerful tool for handling complex one-dimensional data. Given its exceptional capability in processing temporal signals and its widespread recognition as a benchmark model for temporal modeling, this study selects it as a crucial comparative baseline.

2D-LSTM model

2D-LSTM is an extended LSTM model designed explicitly for processing two-dimensional data, suitable for tasks such as image, video, and complex two-dimensional signal processing. Traditional LSTMs excel at capturing long-range dependencies in one-dimensional sequences but cannot effectively handle two-dimensional data with spatial dimensions. 2D-LSTM simultaneously unfolds LSTM units across two dimensions, capturing dependencies in both temporal and spatial dimensions. It is particularly well-suited for data containing spatio-temporal or spatial features46. The model’s input layer receives preprocessed two-dimensional data. In contrast, the LSTM layer controls information flow through forget, input, and output gates to process spatio-temporal features in each dimension. The output layer is designed according to task requirements and can be used for classification, regression, or sequence generation. 2D-LSTM finds extensive application in video classification, image segmentation, medical image analysis, and other domains, significantly enhancing model performance when handling two-dimensional data with complex dependencies. By optimizing training mechanisms, 2D-LSTM captures both global and local features within the data, providing a powerful solution for spatio-temporal data processing. Given the pronounced spatio-temporal characteristics of the EEG signals processed in this study and 2D-LSTM’s exceptional performance in capturing complex spatio-temporal features, this model was selected as the reference benchmark.

MSFBCNN model

MSFBCNN (multiscale filter bank convolutional neural network) is a deep learning model designed explicitly for motor imagery brain-computer interface tasks based on EEG signals47. Traditional EEG signal processing methods often face challenges in feature extraction due to low signal-to-noise ratios and complex temporal correlations. To address these issues, MSFBCNN incorporates a multiscale filter bank convolutional layer, simultaneously capturing rich features across different frequency scales and providing a more comprehensive representation of EEG signals. Furthermore, the model employs nonlinear operators and Dropout connections to prevent overfitting while reducing computational complexity. MSFBCNN integrates feature extraction and classification into a single network through its hierarchical end-to-end design, significantly enhancing overall performance. Extensive research demonstrates that MSFBCNN exhibits outstanding classification accuracy across multiple datasets and exceptional transfer learning capabilities in cross-subject classification tasks. Remarkable results are achieved even with minimal training samples. Given its design philosophy in multiscale feature extraction, which aligns with the approach in this study, and its proven excellence in processing EEG signals, MSFBCNN has been selected as a key benchmark for performance comparison.

Results

This study proposes a novel deep learning model—MSTANet. A series of ablation experiments was designed and conducted to thoroughly investigate the advantages of the MSTANet architecture and the performance enhancements. The experiments systematically analyzed the specific impact of structural modifications on model performance by introducing the SE module and adjusting the number of parallel Block1 configurations. The experimental results are presented in Table 5. Specifically, 1ParaConv uses only one Block1, 1ParaConv_SE represents adding SE after the Block1 module, and 1ParaConv_2SE indicates adding SE after both the Block1 and Block2 convolutional modules. 2ParaConv_2SE denotes two parallel Block1s with SE added, 3ParaConv_2SE indicates three parallel Block1s with SE added, and so on, with 7ParaConv_2SE representing seven parallel Block1s with SE added. 6ParaConv_2SE_FCN denotes six parallel Block1 units with SE added, replacing the original fully connected classifier (FCL) with an FCN. This structure constitutes the final proposed MSTANet model. In ablation experiments, to mitigate the impact of random factors, all experiments used a fixed random seed (set to 42) and employed identical data partitioning and training strategies to ensure reproducibility and comparability.

As shown in the table above, this experiment added SE after a single Block1 convolution block first, achieving a classification accuracy of 86.85%. Subsequently, adding SE after the Block2 convolution block improved accuracy to 87.22%. Therefore, adding SE after both Block1 and Block2 is reasonable. Subsequently, as the number of parallel Block1 units increased, the classification accuracy progressively improved: using five parallel Block1 units yielded an accuracy of 89.55%; increasing to 6 parallel Block1 units further raised the accuracy to 89.74%, achieving the optimal performance; however, increasing to 7 parallel Block1 units caused the accuracy to drop to 88.76%. Finally, the MSTANet model was constructed by converting the FCL into an FCN. Through ablation experiments, this study validated the effectiveness of each improvement in the MSTANet model and revealed the interactions between its components. These experimental results provide crucial theoretical foundations and empirical support for further optimizing the model architecture and enhancing classification performance.

To further justify the design choice of the attention module in MSTANet, an extended experimental comparison was conducted by incorporating several representative and widely used attention mechanisms beyond the Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block. Specifically, Selective Kernel Networks (SKN) and Gated Convolutional Networks with Hybrid Connectivity (GCNHC) were implemented, together with Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) and Efficient Channel Attention (ECA), to enable a comprehensive and fair evaluation. All attention mechanisms were assessed under identical datasets, preprocessing procedures, and cross-validation protocols to ensure consistency. As summarized in Table 6, the SE-based configuration achieved the best overall performance, with an accuracy of 92.56 ± 0.65% and an F1 score of 92.90 ± 0.62%, outperforming SKN, ECA, GCNHC, and CBAM across all evaluated metrics. These results further support the suitability of the SE attention mechanism for EEG-based depression detection within the proposed MSTANet framework.

The comparative experiment selected multiple representative deep learning models for performance evaluation. Results showed that the AttenSleep model achieved a classification accuracy of 75.20%, the 1D_LSTM model reached 80.98%, the MSFBCNN model attained 84.82%, and the EEGNet model achieved 85.23%. While these models modeled and optimized EEG signal feature extraction to varying degrees, overall accuracy still holds room for improvement. In contrast, the proposed MSTANet model significantly enhances feature modeling capabilities for EEG signals by incorporating a multiscale spatio-temporal attention mechanism and coarse-grained signal reconstruction. It demonstrates the most outstanding classification performance. Ultimately, the MSTANet model achieves a classification accuracy of 92.56%, markedly outperforming all comparison models. This validates its effectiveness and advanced capabilities in EEG signal analysis and depression recognition tasks.

To evaluate the generalization ability of the proposed MSTANet model, an external validation was conducted on the JHSH Dataset. This independent cohort comprised 230 participants (96 DD patients and 134 HC) and was completely excluded from model training, internal validation, and hyperparameter tuning. As shown in Fig. 6, the model achieved an overall classification accuracy of 90.21%, with the majority of participants correctly classified. Compared with the internal five-fold cross-validation accuracy of 93.61% obtained on the HZTPH Dataset, the modest decline of 3.4% indicates that the dual-channel spatiotemporal features learned by MSTANet are robust and generalize effectively across centers, despite potential differences in EEG acquisition devices, participant demographics, or environmental factors. These results provide strong evidence that the model’s performance is not driven solely by center-specific characteristics or artifacts of the training set.

Observing the data in the Fig. 7, when employing the OLMOP coarse-graining method, the classification accuracy exhibits an upward trend within a narrow range of scale factors (< 25). At a scale factor of 24, the maximum value reaches 93.61%, significantly improving over the original 92.56%. Although the accuracy slightly declines beyond a scale factor of 25, overall performance maintains an upward trajectory. For the non-OL method, classification accuracy also shows a significant in-crease within the scale factor range of 10 to 25, peaking at 93.57% at scale factor 26. Although it declines thereafter, the overall performance remains solid. For the OLROP method, classification accuracy also markedly improved across scale factors 1 to 35, peaking at 93.53%. Although performance declined after scale factor 36, overall stability was maintained.

To further interpret the performance trends observed in Fig. 7, the distribution of learned attention weights across different multiscale branches is illustrated in Fig. 8. As shown, the average channel-wise SE attention weights are not uniformly distributed among scales. Higher attention weights are assigned to intermediate temporal scales, particularly Scale 3 (kernel size 30) and Scale 4 (kernel size 16), while lower weights are observed at finer scales such as Scale 5 (kernel size 8) and Scale 6 (kernel size 4). This attention allocation pattern is consistent with the performance results reported in Fig. 7, where the OLMOP coarse-graining method achieves its highest classification accuracy at moderate scale factors (around 24). The relatively increased attention assigned to mid-range temporal scales suggests that features extracted at these scales contribute more significantly to depression discrimination, whereas very fine-scale components receive reduced emphasis. Overall, the attention weight distribution in Fig. 8 provides complementary evidence that the proposed MSTANet adaptively prioritizes informative temporal scales, which aligns well with the observed accuracy improvements under appropriate coarse-graining configurations.

In summary, the following key findings were observed: Compared with directly using raw features, the depression detection approach based on multiscale EEG feature fusion was found to significantly improve classification accuracy on the MSTANet dataset. Using the OLMOP coarse-graining method with a scale factor of 24, the maximum accuracy of 93.61% was achieved, demonstrating a notable enhancement. Among the three coarse-graining strategies, OLMOP exhibited superior classification performance during the initial phase (scale factors 1 to 30), and considering model stability and short-term performance, it was identified as the optimal choice. Furthermore, the generalization capability of the proposed MSTANet model was confirmed through external validation on the independent JHSH Dataset, which was completely excluded from model training, internal validation, and hyperparameter tuning, where an overall accuracy of 90.21% was achieved. These results collectively demonstrate the effectiveness of multiscale EEG feature fusion in depression detection and highlight the robustness and practical advantages of the OLMOP method.

Discussion

This study proposes a novel MSTANet, which achieves the first deep integration of multiscale coarse-grained signal reconstruction methods with multiscale spatio-temporal attention networks to construct a new depression detection model. MSTANet processes signal features at different coarse-grained scales through multiple parallel convolutional structures, enhances the representational power of key channels by introducing a Self-Attention (SE) module, and utilizes an FCN for classification. Key findings are as follows: MSTANet demonstrates outstanding performance in depression recognition (accuracy: 92.56%, precision: 90.70%, recall: 95.22%, F1 score: 92.90%). The following sections will systematically discuss the innovation and superiority of the model architecture and the novelty and effectiveness of the EEG signal coarse-graining method, elucidating the core contributions and clinical significance of this study.

The core innovation of the MSTANet model is its multi-parallel convolutional architecture. This structure effectively captures EEG complexity while preventing interference between temporal, frequency, and spatial characteristics48. This approach has been widely applied in medical detection and diagnostic tasks49–51, demonstrating powerful classification performance for depression52,53 indicates that reduced MSE at fine temporal scales (especially in the central frontal region) and diffuse MSE elevation at coarser scales significantly correlate with antidepressant treatment response, reflecting the characteristic abnormalities in prefrontal function during depression. The multi-parallel convolution structure, by processing features across different granularity scales, is thus effective in capturing this bidirectional pathological pattern. Inspired by the aforementioned EEG feature extraction approach, this experiment breaks through the traditional “single-scale feature extraction” method, optimizing model performance while revealing the multiscale electrophysiological characteristics of EEG in depression. This study incorporates an SE attention mechanism after each convolutional stage. By dynamically adjusting channel weights, this mechanism enhances EEG feature representation capabilities—a technique previously applied in EEG classification54,55 and spatio-temporal feature fusion tasks56. Final experiments demonstrate that adding dual SE modules (SE1 + SE2) outperforms both the original and single SE modes. This finding extends Tang et al. ’s57 theory that “channel attention enhances EEG feature selection” proving that embedding attention mechanisms is crucial for improving depression detection performance. Furthermore, a comparison with other attention mechanisms (SKN, ECA, CBAM, GCNHC) confirmed the clear advantage of the SE module (Table 6). SE likely benefits from recalibrating channel-wise features without disturbing the spatial relationships among EEG electrodes—something spatial attention methods like CBAM can inadvertently disrupt. This supports the design choice of placing SE blocks after each convolutional stage in MSTANet. Finally, the model replaced the FCL with an FCN classifier. This technique reduces the number of parameters while improving performance and is widely recognized as more efficient and effective than FCL58. Consistent with previous studies59,60, our research also found that replacing FCL with FCN improved model performance.

Additionally, compared to the five existing state-of-the-art models, the MSTANet model achieves a significant breakthrough in depression prediction. For instance, the 1D_LSTM and 2D_LSTM models suffer from drawbacks such as large learning parameter counts, computational complexity, and slow training speeds, particularly when applied to high-dimensional data like EEG. However, their overall accuracy remains relatively low, which is consistent with our findings. Previous studies have often enhanced accuracy by incorporating convolutional structures to address this limitation. For example, both studies indicate that integrating CNN and LSTM architectures leads to improved classification performance61,62. The AttenSleep model, a novel attention-based deep learning architecture, can also capture temporal dependencies among extracted features44. Although it overcomes traditional models’ limitations in capturing cross-period features, its accuracy remains relatively low in this experiment. MSFBCN47, a pure convolutional neural network architecture, demonstrates relatively superior performance but still significantly underperforms compared to MSTANet (p < 0.05). Furthermore, traditional models like EEGNet, despite employing multiscale convolutions, struggle to balance local details and global rhythmic features due to their single-branch structure63. A previous study using an end-to-end depression diagnosis framework based on EEGNet achieved a maximum accuracy of only 90.98% for depression recognition64, consistent with our experimental results. Beyond CNN- and LSTM-based models, recent Transformer architectures such as DepMSTAT and SMT have also been applied to depression detection. However, Transformers typically rely on large-scale datasets to generalize well, as they lack the inductive biases of CNNs (e.g., locality and translation invariance) and must learn these properties from data. On small clinical EEG datasets, such models often underperform and overfit more easily. In contrast, MSTANet balances accuracy and data efficiency through depthwise separable convolutions and a multiscale parallel design, making it suitable for resource-limited settings. While DepMSTAT is designed for multimodal fusion (e.g., acoustic and visual features), the current MSTANet focuses on dual-channel EEG, though its architecture can naturally be extended to multimodal inputs in future work. Overall, compared with Transformer-based methods, MSTANet achieves superior accuracy (92.56%) with practical efficiency on limited data, underscoring the strengths of its multiscale spatio-temporal attention design. Based on the comprehensive performance evaluation above, our proposed novel learning model demonstrates advanced capabilities.

Beyond innovations in model architecture, three EEG coarse-graining reconstruction methods, namely non-OL, OLMOP, and OLROP, were introduced in this study as preprocessing strategies to enrich temporal representations. Previous coarse-graining studies can be broadly categorized into three groups. The first group incorporates composite filtering and multiscale dynamic analysis to suppress noise and enhance intra- and cross-channel dynamics65. The second group extends binary coarse representations to multi-valued forms in order to improve discriminative capability66. The third group integrates adaptive weighting or information-entropy schemes into the coarsening process to emphasize informative patterns67. In parallel, EEG-based depression detection has extensively employed multiscale analysis techniques, including wavelet transforms, MSE, and attention-based temporal modeling, to represent EEG signals across different temporal resolutions. These methods typically rely on predefined basis functions, entropy formulations, or frequency-domain decompositions to derive scale-dependent features68–70. While wavelet transforms provide effective time–frequency representations, they map EEG signals into coefficient spaces that may not be directly optimal for end-to-end temporal feature learning in convolutional neural networks71,72. Similarly, standard MSE reduces each temporal scale to a single scalar measure, summarizing signal variability into low-dimensional statistical descriptors and inevitably discarding fine-grained temporal structure73,74. Further supporting the physiological relevance of the proposed approach, the optimal performance at a scale factor of 24 under OLMOP yields a temporal window of approximately 192 ms (24 × 8 ms at 125 Hz), which aligns directly with the dominant periods of theta and alpha oscillations implicated in prefrontal dysfunction in depression. Moreover, this scale avoids both the noise-prone finer scales dominated by high-frequency artifacts and the less-specific coarser scales that increasingly reflect delta-band activity unrelated to resting-state depression markers. Complementarily, the one-point sliding overlap of OLMOP preserves maximal temporal continuity, enabling sensitive detection of subtle transient dynamics characteristic of depressive EEG that non-overlapping methods may miss.

In contrast, the proposed method is formulated as an input-level, time-domain augmentation strategy that preserves the complete temporal sequence after coarse-graining. By reconstructing EEG signals into multiscale temporal matrices rather than scalar features, this approach enables MSTANet to exploit deep convolutional layers to automatically learn high-dimensional, scale-specific representations and cross-scale dependencies in an end-to-end manner. This design retains the intrinsic temporal dynamics of brain activity while allowing the network to adaptively identify discriminative patterns relevant to depression detection, without reliance on predefined bases or handcrafted statistical indices. Moreover, unlike fixed-scale averaging or hierarchical pooling operations embedded within network architectures, the proposed reconstruction preserves cross-scale temporal relationships while maintaining computational efficiency. Experimental results demonstrated varying degrees of improvement in MSTANet classification performance, indicating that coarse-graining can effectively enhance EEG feature representation and support further investigation into electrophysiological biomarkers and potential clinical applications.

To evaluate the generalization capability of MSTANet, an external validation was conducted using an independent cohort from the JHSH Dataset, which was not used during model training, validation, or parameter optimization. An overall classification accuracy of 90.21% was achieved, with the majority of participants correctly classified. Compared with the internal five-fold cross-validation accuracy of 93.61% obtained on the HZTPH Dataset, the modest performance decrease of 3.4% suggests that the dual-channel configuration captures stable physiological biomarkers from the Fp1 and Fp2 channels across different acquisition environments and patient populations. Furthermore, the small generalization gap indicates that the multiscale temporal attention mechanism primarily learns intrinsic depression-related patterns rather than center-specific characteristics, supporting the robustness and clinical applicability of MSTANet across heterogeneous recording conditions.

Limitations

Although data from two independent clinical centers were included, the overall sample size remains moderate, which may limit the exploration of rare patterns and subtle inter-individual variability. Moreover, the current analysis was conducted under a binary diagnostic framework, with limited stratification across depression severity levels, potentially constraining fine-grained clinical interpretation. While the dual-channel frontal EEG configuration offers practical advantages for real-world deployment, its restricted spatial coverage may limit sensitivity to more distributed neural alterations. Frontal EEG signals are also susceptible to noise and residual artifacts, which may affect model robustness despite careful preprocessing. Future work will focus on expanding the cohort and incorporating severity-aware modeling to further enhance clinical interpretability and robustness.

Conclusions

Significant enhancement in EEG-based depression identification performance was achieved, with an internal accuracy of 93.61% and a robust external validation accuracy of 90.21%. This study was built upon two foundational innovations: first, the pioneering integration of multiple multiscale signal reconstruction methods into a deep learning framework, through which signal feature recognition was effectively improved; second, the innovative development of the MSTANet model, in which a multiscale spatio-temporal attention mechanism was incorporated. Within this framework, features across different coarse-graining scales were processed via parallel convolutional structures, critical information was enhanced through the introduction of an SE module, and precise classification was achieved using an FCN. Crucially, the model design was focused on key electrodes in the frontal region. This simplified dual-channel frontal configuration was shown to reduce data acquisition complexity, thereby substantially enhancing clinical convenience and practical feasibility in resource-constrained settings such as primary care. The effectiveness of the proposed model enhancements, including the coarse-graining strategy, multiscale convolutional architecture, and SE module, was validated through ablation experiments. Comparative evaluations, confirmed by rigorous statistical testing (p < 0.001), demonstrated that MSTANet’s classification performance was significantly superior to that of all competing models. Furthermore, model generalizability and reliability were systematically verified through a dual-center evaluation scheme, in which the JHSH Dataset was strictly excluded from model training, internal validation, and hyperparameter optimization. Despite pronounced inter-subject variability, remarkable performance stability was observed on a large-scale, independent external cohort (N = 230) collected from a geographically distinct center. In summary, supported by comprehensive internal and external validation, robust technical support for the precision diagnosis of depression was provided, and an integrated coarse-grained deep learning framework was established. The clinically convenient dual-channel frontal optimization scheme was shown to represent a promising direction for future EEG signal processing research. This method was demonstrated to hold potential for pilot applications in community hospitals and primary healthcare facilities, offering a practical and effective tool for early screening and precision diagnosis of depression.
