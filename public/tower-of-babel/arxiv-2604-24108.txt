Optimal control for a fourth-order nonisothermal tumor growth model of Caginalp type


Abstract
We study a distributed optimal control problem for a nonisothermal Caginalp-type phase-field model that describes tumour growth under thermal therapy. The PDE system couples a possibly viscous Cahn–Hilliard equation, governing the evolution of the healthy and tumor phases, with an equation for the heat balance, and a reaction-diffusion equation for the nutrient concentration. Chemotaxis and active transport effects are taken into account, and hyperthermia appears as a control variable. We introduce a suitable tracking-type cost functional and show the existence of optimal controls. Then, we analyse the differentiability of the control-to-state operator and establish necessary first-order conditions expressed through a variational inequality involving the adjoint state variables. Key words: tumor growth model, Cahn–Hilliard system, nonisothermal model, optimal control, adjoint system, necessary optimality conditions. AMS (MOS) Subject Classification: 35Q93, 49J20, 49K20, 35Q92, 92C50.

Introduction

In this paper, we study an optimal control problem for a nonisothermal tumor growth model, which was introduced and proved to be well-posed in our previous work
[
7
]
. Let
T>0
be the final time and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a sufficiently regular spatial domain in dimension
d=2,3
, having outward unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega
given by
\boldsymbol{\nu}
. The domain
\Omega
represents the portion of the body under investigation. The main variables whose evolution is studied are:

•

the relative temperature
\theta
in
\Omega
;

•

the phase-field variable
\varphi
, describing the difference in volume fraction between tumor and healthy cells (in particular, the regions
\{\varphi=1\}
and
\{\varphi=-1\}
correspond to tumor and healthy tissue, respectively), together with its associated chemical potential
\mu
;

•

the concentration
\sigma
of a nutrient (e.g., oxygen or glucose) dissolved in the blood.

For an assigned control
u
, representing a thermal therapy administered to the patient in the region under consideration, the corresponding state
(\theta,\varphi,\mu,\sigma)
is the unique solution of the PDE system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\theta+\ell\varphi)-\Delta\theta=u,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi-\Delta\mu=(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\mathbbm{h}(\varphi),

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\mu=\tau\partial_{t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi+\beta(\varphi)+\pi(\varphi)-\chi\sigma-\Lambda\theta,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma-\Delta(\sigma-\chi\varphi)=-\lambda_{C}\sigma\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)+\lambda_{B}(\sigma_{B}-\sigma)-\lambda_{D}\sigma\mathbbm{k}(\theta),

(1.1d)

posed in the parabolic cylinder
Q\coloneqq\Omega\times(0,T)
and supplemented with no-flux boundary conditions

\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\theta=\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\varphi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\mu=\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\sigma=0

(1.2)

on
\Sigma\coloneqq\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
, reflecting the idealised assumption that the portion
\Omega
of the body is isolated from its surroundings, and with the initial conditions

\theta(0)=\theta_{0},\quad\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0},\quad\sigma(0)=\sigma_{0}

(1.3)

in
\Omega
. Here, the well-known (possibly viscous) Cahn–Hilliard
reaction-diffusion subsystem (
1.1b
)–(
1.1d
) governs the evolution of the tumor phase parameter
\varphi
and the nutrient
concentration
\sigma
, and is coupled with the temperature equation (
1.1a
). In particular, the coupling between the relative
temperature
\theta
and the tumor variable
\varphi
is of Caginalp type (cf.
[
5
]
).
For the reader’s convenience, we briefly recall the main features of the model, referring to the introduction of
[
7
]
for a more comprehensive discussion.
The positive constants
\ell
and
\Lambda
are related to the latent heat of the tissue.
We suppose that tumor cells proliferate by consumption of the nutrients and die because of apoptosis, with
\lambda_{p}
and
\lambda_{a}
being, respectively, the proliferation and apoptosis constant rates. Moreover, following
[
7
]
, we assume that
\theta
has cytotoxic effects proportional to the temperature, which we incorporate in the right-hand side of equation (
1.1b
) through the term
-\lambda_{E}\theta
, where
\lambda_{E}
is a fixed positive parameter.
The function
\mathbbm{h}
, multiplying the mass source term in equation (
1.1b
), guarantees that the phenomena
we have just described are proportional to the tumor cells available in a certain area. For
example,
\mathbbm{h}
may be a monotone increasing function which is 0 where
\{\varphi=-1\}
and 1 where
\{\varphi=1\}
. The nonnegative constant
\chi
denotes a transport coefficient accounting for chemotaxis and active transport effects.
The term
\beta(\varphi)+\pi(\varphi)
represents the derivative of a double-well potential. We allow for a fairly general polynomial growth, so that, in particular, the classical choice
\beta(\varphi)+\pi(\varphi)=\varphi^{3}-\varphi
is admissible. In contrast, singular potentials of logarithmic type as well as double-obstacle potentials fall outside the frame of the present analysis.
The parameter
\tau\geq 0
may be either strictly positive or zero. When
\tau>0
, the term
\tau\partial_{t}\varphi
in (
1.1c
) acts as a viscous regularisation in the phase-field dynamics. The case
\tau=0
instead corresponds to the classical (non-viscous) Cahn–Hilliard system. Both regimes are addressed in our analysis. From a mathematical viewpoint, the presence of the viscous term improves the regularity of solutions, which may be advantageous in view of possible extensions to singular potentials.
Concerning the right-hand side of (
1.1d
), the term
-\lambda_{C}\sigma\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)
models nutrient consumption by tumor cells, with a higher consumption rate in regions where the tumor cell density is larger. The term
\lambda_{B}(\sigma_{B}-\sigma)
describes the supply of nutrients from the pre-existing vasculature. Finally, the function
\mathbbm{k}
accounts for the influence of temperature on nutrient uptake, for instance, through vasodilation effects.

The control problem.

Within this framework, a well-chosen heat source
u
could drive the system toward a tumor reduction or, more generally, toward a desired evolution or final state. We introduce some target functions
\theta_{Q},\varphi_{Q}
, and
\theta_{\Omega},\varphi_{\Omega}
representing, respectively, the desired evolution of the temperature and of the phase parameter, and the desired temperature and tumor proportion at the final time
T
. For example, the target
\varphi_{\Omega}
could be a suitable configuration for surgery. Then, we define the cost functional

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}(\theta,\varphi,u)\coloneqq\ &\frac{b_{1}}{2}\int_{Q}|\theta-\theta_{Q}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{b_{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\theta(T)-\theta_{\Omega}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{b_{3}}{2}\int_{Q}|\varphi-\varphi_{Q}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{b_{4}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi(T)-\varphi_{\Omega}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{b_{5}}{2}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(1.4)

The first four terms in (
1.4
) are of standard tracking-type, while the last regularising term prevents the administration of an excessive amount of therapy, thereby avoiding damage to other vital organs.
Firstly, we aim at minimising the cost functional
\mathcal{J}
subject to the PDE system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) and to the following admissible control set

\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\coloneqq\left\{u\in\mathcal{U}\,|\,u_{\textrm{min}}\leq u\leq u_{\textrm{max}}\text{ a.e. in }Q\right\},

(1.5)

where
\mathcal{U}\coloneqq L^{\infty}(Q)
, and
u_{\textrm{min}},u_{\textrm{max}}
are assigned elements of
\mathcal{U}
satisfying
u_{\textrm{min}}\leq u_{\textrm{max}}
almost everywhere in
Q
. Notice that
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
is a nonempty, closed, bounded and convex subset of
\mathcal{U}
. In particular, there exists a constant
M>0
such that
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{M}
, where the open set
\mathcal{U}_{M}
is given by

\mathcal{U}_{M}\coloneqq\left\{u\in\mathcal{U}\,\middle|\,\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q)}<M\right\}.

(1.6)

Rephrasing the optimal control problem more compactly, it reads as follows:

\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}}\mathcal{J}(\theta,\varphi,u).

(1.7)

The subsequent, more challenging, goal is to characterise the optimal controls, i.e., the minimisers, through their necessary first-order optimality conditions.

4
The optimal control problem

The well-posedness of the state system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) has been previously studied in the contribution
[
7
]
, to which we refer for further details on the matter. For the reader’s convenience, the following theorem collects the main results in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that hypotheses
1
–
5
hold. Then, for every
u\in\mathcal{U}_{M}
, the PDE system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) has a unique strong solution
(\theta,\varphi,\mu,\sigma)

belonging to the following spaces

\displaystyle\theta\in H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap C^{0}([0,T];V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)\cap L^{\infty}(Q),

(4.1)

\displaystyle\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;V^{\prime})\cap H^{1}(0,T;V)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;W),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mu\in L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sigma\in H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap C^{0}([0,T];V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W),

(4.4)

which satisfies the estimate

\begin{split}&\lVert\theta\rVert_{H^{1}(H)\cap L^{\infty}(V)\cap L^{2}(W)\cap L^{\infty}(Q)}+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}(V^{\prime})\cap H^{1}(V)\cap L^{\infty}(W)}\\
&\quad+\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{\infty}(V)\cap L^{2}(W)}+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H^{1}(H)\cap L^{\infty}(V)\cap L^{2}(W)}\leq C_{M}.\end{split}

(4.5)

Moreover, for every couple of controls
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1,2}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{M}
the corresponding strong solutions
\{(\theta_{i},\varphi_{i},\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})\}_{i=1,2}
satisfy the following continuous dependence inequality

\begin{split}&\lVert\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)\cap L^{2}(V)}+\lVert\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)\cap L^{2}(W)}+\lVert\tau^{1/2}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(V)}\\
&\quad\quad+\lVert\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}+\lVert\tau^{1/2}(\nabla\mu_{1}-\nabla\mu_{2})\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}+\lVert\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)\cap L^{2}(V)}\\
&\quad\leq C_{M}\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}.\end{split}

(4.6)

Proof.

For the existence and regularity of a solution
(\theta,\varphi,\mu,\sigma)
satisfying (
4.1
)–(
4.4
)
and (
4.5
) we refer to
[
7
, Theorems 3.5 and 3.6]
. Part of the continuous dependence estimate (
4.6
) has been established in
[
7
, Theorem 3.8]
, namely the following

\begin{split}&\lVert\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)\cap L^{2}(V)}+\lVert\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)\cap L^{2}(W)}+\lVert\tau^{1/2}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(V)}\\
&\quad\quad+\lVert\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)\cap L^{2}(V)}\leq C_{M}\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}.\end{split}

(4.7)

We now prove the full (
4.6
) for the couple of controls
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1,2}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{M}
and the corresponding states
\{(\theta_{i},\varphi_{i},\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})\}_{i=1,2}
. For the sake of brevity, we introduce the notation

(\theta,\varphi,\mu,\sigma)\coloneqq(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2},\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2},\mu_{1}-\mu_{2},\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}).

For each solution, we sum the equation (
1.1b
) multiplied by
\tau\geq 0
and the equation (
1.1c
). Then, we take the difference of the resulting equations, test it by
\mu
, and integrate over the time interval
(0,T)
. We obtain:

\begin{split}&\tau\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mu|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\mu|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad=\tau\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{E}\theta)\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{1})+(\lambda_{P}\sigma_{2}-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta_{2})\big(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{1})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi_{2})\big)\right]\mu\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[-\Delta\varphi+\big(f(\varphi_{1})-f(\varphi_{2})\big)-\chi\sigma-\Lambda\theta\right]\mu\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

We bind the right-hand side from above, exploiting boundedness and Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbbm{h}
from hypothesis
3
, local Lipschitz continuity of
f
from
4
, as well as uniform boundedness of
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
and
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
from
4.1
. Thus, we make use of the Hölder inequality—also recalling that
\sigma_{2}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{4}(\Omega))
— followed by the Young inequality:

\begin{split}&\tau\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mu|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\mu|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[(\tau+1)\big(|\sigma|+|\theta|\big)+|-\Delta\varphi|+\big(|\sigma_{2}|+1\big)|\varphi|\right]|\mu|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left[(\tau+1)\big(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}+\lVert\theta\rVert_{H}\big)+\lVert-\Delta\varphi\rVert_{H}+\big(\lVert\sigma_{2}\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+1\big)\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\right]\lVert\mu\rVert_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\mu\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{T}\left[(\tau^{2}+1)\big(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\theta\rVert_{H}^{2}\big)+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W}^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

At this point, in view of (
4.7
) we deduce that

\begin{split}\tau\lVert\nabla\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}+\lVert\mu\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}&\leq C(\tau^{2}+1)\big(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}+\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}\big)+C\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}\\
&\leq C(\tau^{2}+1)\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}+C\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}\end{split}

and conclude the proof.
∎

Note that
4.1
allows us to define the so-called control-to-state or solution operator. More precisely, we introduce the state-space

\begin{split}\mathcal{X}\coloneqq&\left[H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap C^{0}([0,T];V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)\cap L^{\infty}(Q)\right]\\
&\times\left[W^{1,\infty}(0,T;V^{\prime})\cap H^{1}(0,T;V)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;W)\right]\\
&\times\left[L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)\right]\times\left[H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap C^{0}([0,T];V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)\right],\end{split}

and observe that the solution operator

\mathcal{S}\colon\mathcal{U}_{M}\to\mathcal{X},\qquad u\mapsto(\theta,\varphi\,\mu,\sigma),

which maps every control
u\in\mathcal{U}_{M}
to the corresponding strong solution of the state system
(\theta,\varphi\,\mu,\sigma)\in\mathcal{X}
, is well-defined and bounded.
Moreover, thanks to the continuous embedding

\begin{split}\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq&\left[C^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)\right]\times\left[C^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)\right]\\
&\times L^{2}(0,T;H)\times\left[C^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)\right],\end{split}

and to the continuous dependence estimate (
4.6
) from
4.1
,
\mathcal{S}\colon\mathcal{U}_{M}\to\mathcal{Y}
is Lipschitz continuous.
With this new notation, we define the reduced cost functional as

\mathrm{J}(u)\coloneqq\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S}_{1}(u),\mathcal{S}_{2}(u),u),

where
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
select, respectively, the first and the second component of the solution operator
\mathcal{S}
.
Then, the optimal control problem can be stated as

\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}}\mathrm{J}(u),

(4.8)

which means that we search a minimiser for the functional
\mathcal{J}
subject to the PDE system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) and constrained to
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

The optimal control problem (
4.8
) has at least one minimiser
u^{*}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
.

Proof.

The proof is quite standard, and it relies on the direct method of the Calculus of Variations. First of all, we notice that, since
\mathrm{J}
is proper and nonnegative,
\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}}\mathrm{J}(u)
is finite and nonnegative. Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{+\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
be a minimizing sequence, i.e.,

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}}\mathrm{J}(u)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathrm{J}(u_{n}),

with corresponding states denoted as
(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n},\mu_{n},\sigma_{n})\coloneqq\mathcal{S}(u_{n})
. Since
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(Q)
, there exists a
u^{*}\in L^{\infty}(Q)
such that

\displaystyle u_{n}\to u^{*}

\displaystyle\quad\text{weakly-}\ast

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}(Q)

(4.9)

along a non-relabelled subsequence.
Moreover, since
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
is convex and closed in
L^{\infty}(Q)
, it is weakly-
\ast
closed. Thus, the limit
u^{*}
belongs to the admissible set
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
. Regarding the states, first we recall the estimate (
4.5
), the compactness of the embeddings
W\subset V\subset H
and
W\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, the
Banach–Alaouglu (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
) and Aubin–Lions theorems (see
[
35
, Section 8, Corollary 4]
). Then, by these tools, it turns out that there exists a quadruplet
(\theta^{*},\varphi^{*},\mu^{*},\sigma^{*})\in\mathcal{X}
such that

\displaystyle\theta_{n}\to\theta^{*}

\displaystyle\quad\text{weakly-}\ast

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)\cap L^{\infty}(Q),

(4.10)

strongly

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }C^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V),

(4.11)

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}\to\varphi^{*}

\displaystyle\quad\text{weakly-}\ast

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }W^{1,\infty}(0,T;V^{\prime})\cap H^{1}(0,T;V)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;W),

(4.12)

strongly

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }C^{0}([0,T];V)\cap C^{0}(\overline{Q}),

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\to\mu^{*}

\displaystyle\quad\text{weakly-}\ast

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W),

(4.14)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}\to\sigma^{*}

\displaystyle\quad\text{weakly-}\ast

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }H^{1}(0,T;H)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;V)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W),

(4.15)

strongly

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }C^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V),

(4.16)

along a further subsequence that, again, we do not relabel. We claim that
(\theta^{*},\varphi^{*},\mu^{*},\sigma^{*})
coincides with
\mathcal{S}(u^{*})
. To prove this, it is enough to write the system satisfied by each element of the sequence, and pass to the limit as
n\to+\infty
, exploiting
convergences (
4.9
)–(
4.16
). In particular, the uniform convergence of
\varphi_{n}
implied by (
4.13
) is crucial to pass to the limit in the nonlinearity
f(\varphi_{n})
. The passage to the limit in the other nonlinear terms can be easily handled, for instance
\lambda_{D}\sigma_{n}\mathbbm{k}(\theta_{n})
converges to
\lambda_{D}\sigma\mathbbm{k}(\theta)
strongly in
C^{0}([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
due to (
4.16
), (
4.11
) and the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbbm{k}
ensured by
3
.
Consequently,
(\theta^{*},\varphi^{*},\mu^{*},\sigma^{*})
is the strong solution of the system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) associated with the admissible control
u^{*}
. Moreover, by the weak
lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}
norm and the weak convergence (
4.9
) combined with the continuity of the
L^{2}
norm and the strong convergences (
4.11
), (
4.13
), we have

\mathrm{J}(u^{*})\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathrm{J}(u_{n})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}}\mathrm{J}(u).

Therefore,
u^{*}
is a minimiser of
\mathrm{J}
over
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Our next goal is to characterise the optimal controls by means of first-order necessary optimality conditions. The standard approach consists in proving that the solution operator is Fréchet differentiable between suitable spaces, which in turn easily implies the Fréchet differentiability of the reduced cost functional
\mathrm{J}
.
As a first step in this direction, we introduce the so-called linearised system, which will play a crucial role in the explicit characterisation of the derivative of
\mathcal{S}
. Its formulation and analysis are the subject of the following section.

5
The linearised system

Let
u\in\mathcal{U}_{M}
be fixed, and let
(\theta,\varphi,\mu,\sigma)
denote the associated state. For a given (sufficiently small) perturbation
h\in L^{\infty}(Q)
, we consider the perturbed control
u+h
and its corresponding state. Owing to the continuous dependence result stated in
4.1
, this state remains close to
(\theta,\varphi,\mu,\sigma)
.
To analyse the difference between the two state systems, we formally linearise the nonlinear terms by means of first-order Taylor expansions. In this way, we find that the perturbation variables
(\zeta,\xi,\eta,\rho)
—which we shall henceforth refer to as the linearised variables—satisfy the following linear system (written now with formal equations and conditions):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\zeta+\ell\xi)-\Delta\zeta=h,

(5.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\xi-\Delta\eta=(\lambda_{P}\rho-\lambda_{E}\zeta)\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)+(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi,

(5.1b)

\displaystyle\tau\partial_{t}\xi-\Delta\xi+f^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi-\chi\rho-\Lambda\zeta=\eta,

(5.1c)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\rho-\Delta(\rho-\chi\xi)=&-\lambda_{C}\rho\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)-\lambda_{C}\sigma\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi\\
&-\lambda_{B}\rho-\lambda_{D}\rho\mathbbm{k}(\theta)-\lambda_{D}\sigma\mathbbm{k}^{\prime}(\theta)\zeta,\end{aligned}

(5.1d)

in
Q
, complemented with the homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\zeta=\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\xi=\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\eta=\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\rho=0

(5.2)

on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
, and the initial conditions

\zeta(0)=\xi(0)=\rho(0)=0

(5.3)

in
\Omega
.
We emphasise that although the formal derivation above requires
h
to be sufficiently small in
L^{\infty}(Q)
, the linear system (
5.1
) is well-defined under the milder assumption
h\in L^{2}(Q)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For every control

\displaystyle\eta\in L^{2}(0,T;V),

(5.4a)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\partial_{t}\xi,v\rangle_{V}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x\\

v\in V

\Omega
.

5.3

5.6
). In the following, we analyse the terms
I_{1},\dots,I_{4}
singularly.
Regarding
I_{1}
, we apply the Young inequality, where
\epsilon
is as before, and the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality to the
\eta
term, subtracting
\overline{\eta}
. Note that
C_{P}
is a fixed constant
related to this inequality. Finally, we employ the Hölder inequality and exploit (
5.6
) and (
5.8
), obtaining:

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{P}\rho-\lambda_{E}\zeta)\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\frac{\epsilon}{C_{P}}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\eta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left(|\rho|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left(|\rho|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}|\overline{\eta}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{\epsilon}\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}.\end{split}

(5.11)

We turn our attention to
I_{2}
and proceed similarly:

\begin{split}I_{2}&=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(|\sigma|+1)|\xi||\eta|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\eta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{W}^{2}+1)\lVert\xi\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}|\overline{\eta}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{\epsilon}\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)}^{2}\\
&\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{\epsilon}\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2},\end{split}

(5.12)

having used the Hölder and the Poincaré inequalities, and the uniform estimates (
4.5
), (
5.6
), (
5.8
). The term
I_{3}
is first of all rewritten equivalently, and then integrated by parts, recalling the initial condition
\xi(0)=0
and (
4.5
) together with (
3.6
) and (
5.6
):

\begin{split}I_{3}&=-\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi\partial_{t}\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(\varphi)\partial_{t}(|\xi|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&{}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\bigl(f^{\prime}(\varphi)|\xi|^{2}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\partial_{t}\varphi|\xi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\xi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\partial_{t}\varphi\rVert_{H}\lVert\xi\rVert_{W}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)}^{2}+\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)}\lVert\partial_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{2}(W)}\right)\leq C\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}.\end{split}

(5.13)

The term
I_{4}
is again dealt with by integrating by parts, exploiting the vanishing initial conditions:

\begin{split}I_{4}&=\chi\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\rho\partial_{t}\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s=\chi\int_{\Omega}(\rho\xi)(t)\,\mathrm{d}x-\chi\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\rho\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\chi\lVert\rho\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)}\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)}+\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}\rho|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\xi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}\rho|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{\epsilon}\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2},\end{split}

(5.14)

by the Hölder inequality and the estimate (
5.6
). Finally, we collect the inequalities (
5.10
)–(
5.14
), choosing
\epsilon
small enough so that the related terms on the right-hand side can be absorbed in the corresponding addends on the left-hand side. This leads to the inequality

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\Lambda}{\ell}\int_{\Omega}|\zeta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\xi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\lambda_{B}\int_{\Omega}|\rho|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]+\frac{\Lambda}{\ell}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+\tau\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}\xi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}\rho|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\leq C\left(\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\sigma\rVert_{W}^{2}\lVert\zeta\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right).\end{split}

Thus, as the function
s\mapsto\lVert\sigma(s)\rVert_{W}^{2}
is in
L^{1}(0,T)
by (
4.5
), we can apply the Gronwall inequality and obtain:

\begin{split}&\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)\cap L^{2}(V)}+\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(V)}+\lVert\tau^{1/2}\partial_{t}\xi\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}\\
&\quad+\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{2}(V)}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{H^{1}(H)\cap L^{\infty}(V)}\leq C\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}.\end{split}

(5.15)

Moreover, by a comparison argument applied first to (
5.4b
)
and then to (
5.4a
), we have:

\lVert\partial_{t}\xi\rVert_{L^{2}(V^{\prime})}\leq C\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)},\quad\lVert\partial_{t}\zeta\rVert_{L^{2}(V^{\prime})}\leq C\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}.

(5.16)

∎

6
The Fréchet differentiability of the solution operator

In this section, we show that the solution operator
\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{U}_{M}\to\mathcal{Y}
is not only Lipschitz continuous, but in fact Fréchet differentiable.

Theorem 6.1
.

The solution operator
\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{U}_{M}\to\mathcal{Y}
is Fréchet differentiable. Moreover, for any
u\in\mathcal{U}_{M}
, the Fréchet derivative of
\mathcal{S}
at
u
is characterised by

D\mathcal{S}(u)[h]=(\zeta^{h},\xi^{h},\eta^{h},\rho^{h})

for all
h\in\mathcal{U}
, where
(\zeta^{h},\xi^{h},\eta^{h},\rho^{h})
denotes the solution of the linearised system (
5.1
)–(
5.3
).

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
be fixed but arbitrary and denote
\mathcal{S}(u)=(\theta,\varphi,\mu,\sigma)
. We aim to show that

\lim_{\lVert h\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}\to 0}\frac{\lVert\mathcal{S}(u+h)-\mathcal{S}(u)-(\zeta^{h},\xi^{h},\eta^{h},\rho^{h})\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\lVert h\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}}=0,

(6.1)

which proves the asserted Fréchet differentiability.
To this end, we set
\mathcal{S}(u+h)=(\theta^{h},\varphi^{h},\mu^{h},\sigma^{h})
and introduce the remainder terms

\Theta^{h}\coloneqq\theta^{h}-\theta-\zeta^{h},\quad\Phi^{h}\coloneqq\varphi^{h}-\varphi-\xi^{h},\quad\Upsilon^{h}\coloneqq\mu^{h}-\mu-\eta^{h},\quad\Pi^{h}\coloneqq\sigma^{h}-\sigma-\rho^{h}.

Since
u\in\mathcal{U}_{M}
and
\mathcal{U}_{M}
is open, there exists a constant
C_{u}>0
such that
u+h\in\mathcal{U}_{M}
for all
h\in\mathcal{U}
satisfying
\lVert h\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}\leq C_{u}
. As we are interested in the limit as
\lVert h\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}\to 0
, we may restrict ourselves, without loss of generality, to perturbations
h
of sufficiently small norm.
We claim that

\lVert(\Theta^{h},\Phi^{h},\Upsilon^{h},\Pi^{h})\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq C_{M}\lVert h\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}^{2},

(6.2)

for a suitable constant
C_{M}>0
. Clearly, estimate (
6.2
) immediately implies (
6.1
).
In order to prove (
6.2
), we analyse the PDE system satisfied by
(\Theta^{h},\Phi^{h},\Upsilon^{h},\Pi^{h})
, which is obtained by subtracting the state system written for
u
and its linearization (cf.
5.1
) from the state system corresponding to
u+h
. By writing the equations formally, it reads as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\Theta^{h}+\ell\Phi^{h})-\Delta\Theta^{h}=0,

(6.3a)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\Phi^{h}-\Delta\Upsilon^{h}=&\,(\lambda_{P}\sigma^{h}-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta^{h})\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\\
&-\left[(\lambda_{P}\rho^{h}-\lambda_{E}\zeta^{h})\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)+(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi^{h}\right],\end{aligned}

(6.3b)

\displaystyle\tau\partial_{t}\Phi^{h}-\Delta\Phi^{h}+f(\varphi^{h})-f(\varphi)-f^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi^{h}-\chi\Phi^{h}-\Lambda\Theta^{h}=\Upsilon^{h},

(6.3c)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\Pi^{h}-\Delta(\Pi^{h}-\chi\Phi^{h})=&\,\lambda_{C}\big(-\sigma^{h}\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})+\sigma\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)+\rho^{h}\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)+\sigma\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi^{h}\big)-\lambda_{B}\Pi^{h}\\
&+\lambda_{D}\big(-\sigma^{h}\mathbbm{k}(\theta^{h})+\sigma\mathbbm{k}(\theta)+\rho^{h}\mathbbm{k}(\theta)+\sigma\mathbbm{k}^{\prime}(\theta)\zeta^{h}\big),\end{aligned}

(6.3d)

coupled with vanishing initial and Neumann boundary conditions.

Rewriting the nonlinearities.
We begin by rewriting the nonlinear contributions in a more convenient form. Consider first the right-hand side of (
6.3b
). By adding and subtracting suitable terms, we obtain

\begin{split}&(\lambda_{P}\sigma^{h}-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta^{h})\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\\
&\quad\quad-\left[(\lambda_{P}\rho^{h}-\lambda_{E}\zeta^{h})\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)+(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi^{h}\right]\\
&\quad=(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\left[\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)-\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi^{h}\right]\\
&\quad\quad+\left[\lambda_{P}(\sigma^{h}-\sigma)-\lambda_{E}(\theta^{h}-\theta)\right]\left(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\right)+(\lambda_{P}\Pi^{h}-\lambda_{E}\Theta^{h})\mathbbm{h}(\varphi).\end{split}

Now we take into account Taylor’s expansion with integral remainder, which states that for every
g\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
the following equality holds

g(s_{2})=g(s_{1})+g^{\prime}(s_{1})(s_{2}-s_{1})+(s_{2}-s_{1})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}g^{\prime\prime}(zs_{2}+(1-z)s_{1})(1-z)\,\text{d}z

for every
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Applying this formula with
g=\mathbbm{h}
,
s_{2}=\varphi^{h}
, and
s_{1}=\varphi
, we deduce

\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)-\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi^{h}=\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}+R_{\mathbbm{h}}(\varphi^{h}-\varphi)^{2},

where the remainder term is given by

R_{\mathbbm{h}}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\mathbbm{h}^{\prime\prime}\big(z\varphi^{h}+(1-z)\varphi\big)(1-z)\,\text{d}z.

Notice that, since
\mathbbm{h}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and both
\varphi^{h}
and
\varphi
are uniformly bounded in view of (
4.5
),
R_{\mathbbm{h}}
is uniformly bounded by a constant depending only on
M
(cf. (
1.6
)). An analogous argument applies to the nonlinear potential term in (
6.3c
). Using Taylor’s formula with integral remainder again, we obtain

\begin{split}f(\varphi^{h})-f(\varphi)-f^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi^{h}=f^{\prime}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}+R_{f}(\varphi^{h}-\varphi)^{2},\end{split}

where

R_{f}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime\prime}\big(z\varphi^{h}+(1-z)\varphi\big)(1-z)\text{d}z.

Also in this case,
R_{f}
is uniformly bounded.
Proceeding similarly, we deal with the right-hand side of equation (
6.3d
), obtaining:

\begin{split}&\lambda_{C}\big(-\sigma^{h}\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})+\sigma\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)+\rho^{h}\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)+\sigma\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi^{h}\big)-\lambda_{B}\Pi^{h}\\
&\quad\quad+\lambda_{D}\big(-\sigma^{h}\mathbbm{k}(\theta^{h})+\sigma\mathbbm{k}(\theta)+\rho^{h}\mathbbm{k}(\theta)+\sigma\mathbbm{k}^{\prime}(\theta)\zeta^{h}\big)\\
&\quad=\lambda_{C}\Big\{-\sigma\left[\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)-\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\xi^{h}\right]-(\sigma^{h}-\sigma)\big(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\big)-\Pi^{h}\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\Big\}\\
&\quad\quad-\lambda_{B}\Pi^{h}+\lambda_{D}\Big\{-\sigma\left[\mathbbm{k}(\theta^{h})-\mathbbm{k}(\theta)-\mathbbm{k}^{\prime}(\theta)\zeta^{h}\right]-(\sigma^{h}-\sigma)\big(\mathbbm{k}(\theta^{h})-\mathbbm{k}(\theta)\big)-\Pi^{h}\mathbbm{k}(\theta)\Big\}\\
&\quad=\lambda_{C}\Big\{-\sigma\left[\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}+R_{\mathbbm{h}}(\varphi^{h}-\varphi)^{2}\right]-(\sigma^{h}-\sigma)\big(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\big)-\Pi^{h}\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\Big\}\\
&\quad\quad-\lambda_{B}\Pi^{h}+\lambda_{D}\Big\{-\sigma\left[\mathbbm{k}^{\prime}(\theta)\Theta^{h}+R_{\mathbbm{k}}(\theta^{h}-\theta)^{2}\right]-(\sigma^{h}-\sigma)\big(\mathbbm{k}(\theta^{h})-\mathbbm{k}(\theta)\big)-\Pi^{h}\mathbbm{k}(\theta)\Big\},\end{split}

where
R_{\mathbbm{k}}
is defined as before, i.e.,

R_{\mathbbm{k}}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\mathbbm{k}^{\prime\prime}\big(z\theta^{h}+(1-z)\theta\big)(1-z)\,\text{d}x,

and uniformly bounded. With the notation introduced,
(\Theta^{h},\Phi^{h},\Upsilon^{h},\Pi^{h})
satisfies the following PDE system written in a variational formulation:

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}(\Theta^{h}+\ell\Phi^{h}),v\rangle_{V}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Theta^{h}\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

(6.4a)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\partial_{t}\Phi^{h},v\rangle_{V}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Upsilon^{h}\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\left[\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}+R_{\mathbbm{h}}(\varphi^{h}-\varphi)^{2}\right]v\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad\quad+\int_{\Omega}\left[\lambda_{P}(\sigma^{h}-\sigma)-\lambda_{E}(\theta^{h}-\theta)\right]\left(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\right)v\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad\quad+\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{P}\Pi^{h}-\lambda_{E}\Theta^{h})\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)v\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{aligned}

(6.4b)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\tau\langle\partial_{t}\Phi^{h},v\rangle_{V}+\!\int_{\Omega}\!\nabla\Phi^{h}\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\!\int_{\Omega}\!\!\left[f^{\prime}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}+R_{f}(\varphi^{h}-\varphi)^{2}-\chi\Phi^{h}-\Lambda\Theta^{h}\right]v\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\Upsilon^{h}\,v\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{aligned}

(6.4c)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\partial_{t}\Pi^{h},v\rangle_{V}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\Pi^{h}-\chi\Phi^{h})\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{C}\Big\{-\sigma\left[\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}+R_{\mathbbm{h}}(\varphi^{h}-\varphi)^{2}\right]-(\sigma^{h}-\sigma)\big(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\big)\Big\}v\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad\quad-\int_{\Omega}\Pi^{h}\big(\lambda_{C}\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)+\lambda_{B}+\lambda_{D}\mathbbm{k}(\theta)\big)v\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad\quad+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{D}\Big\{-\sigma\left[\mathbbm{k}^{\prime}(\theta)\Theta^{h}+R_{\mathbbm{k}}(\theta^{h}-\theta)^{2}\right]-(\sigma^{h}-\sigma)\big(\mathbbm{k}(\theta^{h})-\mathbbm{k}(\theta)\big)\Big\}v\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{aligned}

(6.4d)

for every
v\in V
, a.e. in
(0,T)
. In order to prove the inequality (
6.2
), we are going to perform some a priori estimates.

First estimates.
We integrate the equation (
6.4a
) in time, multiply the resulting identity by a (large) constant
R>0
to be chosen later, and choose
v=\Theta^{h}
as a test function. Then, we integrate once more in time over the interval
(0,t)
. Exploiting the homogeneous initial condition for
\Theta^{h}
, we obtain:

\begin{split}&R\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\Theta^{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{R}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(1\ast_{t}\Theta^{h})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad=R\ell\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\Phi^{h}\Theta^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{R}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\Theta^{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+R\ell^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|\Phi^{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

(6.5)

where we have bounded the right-hand side by the Young inequality.

Next, we sum (
6.4b
) tested with
v=\Phi^{h}
and (
6.4c
) tested with
v=-\Delta\Phi^{h}
: the use of the latter function is not allowed but (
6.4c
) can be equivalently rewritten as the equation

\tau\partial_{t}\Phi^{h}-\Delta\Phi^{h}+f^{\prime}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}+R_{f}(\varphi^{h}-\varphi)^{2}-\chi\Phi^{h}-\Lambda\Theta^{h}=\Upsilon^{h}\quad\hbox{a.e. in $Q$},

and here we can test by
(-\Delta\Phi^{h})
. Integrating by parts, a cancellation of the terms involving
\Upsilon^{h}
occurs. The integration in time over
(0,t)
and the use of the null initial conditions entail that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Phi^{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi^{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}|-\Delta\Phi^{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{P}\sigma-\lambda_{A}-\lambda_{E}\theta)\left[\mathbbm{h}^{\prime}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}+R_{\mathbbm{h}}(\varphi^{h}-\varphi)^{2}\right]\Phi^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[\lambda_{P}(\sigma^{h}-\sigma)-\lambda_{E}(\theta^{h}-\theta)\right]\left(\mathbbm{h}(\varphi^{h})-\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\right)\Phi^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{P}\Pi^{h}-\lambda_{E}\Theta^{h})\mathbbm{h}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[f^{\prime}(\varphi)\Phi^{h}+R_{f}(\varphi^{h}-\varphi)^{2}-\chi\Phi^{h}-\Lambda\Theta^{h}\right](-\Delta\Phi^{h})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.\end{split}

(6.6)

Our next goal is to estimate the terms
I_{1},\dots,I_{4}
separately.
Concerning the term
I_{1}
, we first exploit that
\theta
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(Q)
by virtue of (
4.5
) in
4.1
, that
\mathbbm{h}
is Lipschitz continuous in view of hypothesis
3
, and that the remainder
R_{\mathbbm{h}}
is uniformly bounded, as observed above. We then apply Hölder’s inequality together with the continuous embeddings
V\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
and
W\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Recalling again from (
4.5
) that
\sigma
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;V)
, we deduce:

\begin{split}|I_{1}|&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(|\sigma|+1)\big(|\Phi^{h}|+|\varphi^{h}-\varphi|^{2}\big)|\Phi^{h}|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\big(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+1\big)\big(\lVert\Phi^{h}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\varphi^{h}-\varphi\rVert_{H}\lVert\varphi^{h}-\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)\lVert\Phi^{h}\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq C\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\Phi^{h}\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\lVert\varphi^{h}-\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\varphi^{h}-\varphi\rVert_{W}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\Phi^{h}\rVert_{V}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right\}.\end{split}

In order to bind the last addend, we invoke the inequality (
2.6
) and use the continuous dependence estimate (
4.6
) from
4.1
, obtaining

\begin{split}|I_{1}|&\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert-\Delta\Phi^{h}\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\Phi^{h}\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{4}\end{split}

(6.7)

To estimate
I_{2}
, we again exploit the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbbm{h}
, Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities (with a small parameter
\epsilon>0
to be fixed later), the interpolation inequality (
2.7
), and the continuous dependence estimate (
4.6
). We deduce that

\begin{split}|I_{2}|&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\big(|\sigma^{h}-\sigma|+|\theta^{h}-\theta|\big)|\varphi^{h}-\varphi||\Phi^{h}|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\big(\lVert\sigma^{h}-\sigma\rVert_{H}+\lVert\theta^{h}-\theta\rVert_{H}\big)\lVert\varphi^{h}-\varphi\rVert_{H}\lVert\Phi^{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\Phi^{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\lVert\varphi^{h}-\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H)}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\lVert\sigma^{h}-\sigma\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\theta^{h}-\theta\rVert_{H}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert-\Delta\Phi^{h}\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\Phi^{h}\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(H)}^{4}.\\
\end{split}

(6.8)

We now turn to
I_{3}
. In this case, it suffices to exploit the boundedness of
\mathbbm{h}
, together with Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities. In view of (
6.5
), which later will be added to (
6.6
), we infer that

\begin{split}|I_{3}|&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\big(|\Pi^{h}|+|\Theta^{h}|\big)|\Phi^{h}|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\frac{R}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\Theta^{h}\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{R}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\lVert\Pi^{h}\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\Phi^{h}\rVert_{H}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(6.9)

Finally,
I_{4}
can be handled arguing as before. We use the continuity of
f^{\prime}
ensured by hypothesis
4
, the uniform boundedness of
\varphi
provided by (
4.5
), and the uniform boundedness of the remainder
R_{f}
, as discussed above. Applying Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities and the continuous dependence estimate (
4.6
), we arrive at

\begin{split}|I_{4}|&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\big(|\Phi^{h}|+|\varphi^{h}-\varphi|^{2}+|\Theta^{h}|\big)|-\Delta\Phi^{h}|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\

7
First-order necessary conditions of optimality

In this section, we derive first-order necessary optimality conditions characterising the optimal controls whose existence has been established in
4.2
.
Let
u^{*}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
be an optimal control and let
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
be arbitrary. Since the admissible set
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
is convex, for every

the convex combination
(1-s)u^{*}+su
still belongs to
\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
. Hence, by the optimality of
u^{*}
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{J}(u^{*}+s(u-u^{*}))-\mathrm{J}(u^{*})}{s}\geq 0.

(7.1)

Recall that
\mathrm{J}(u)=\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S}_{1}(u),\mathcal{S}_{2}(u),u)
. The cost functional
\mathcal{J}
, regarded as a mapping from

\mathcal{Z}\coloneqq C^{0}([0,T];H)\times C^{0}([0,T];H)\times L^{2}(0,T;H)

into
\mathbb{R}
, is convex and Fréchet differentiable. Moreover, by
6.1
, the solution operator
\mathcal{S}\colon\mathcal{U}_{M}\to\mathcal{Y}
is Fréchet differentiable. In particular, its components
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are Fréchet differentiable as well. Owing to the continuous embeddings

\displaystyle C^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow C^{0}([0,T];H),

\displaystyle C^{0}([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow C^{0}([0,T];H),

the mappings
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are in fact Fréchet differentiable also as operators from
\mathcal{U}_{M}
into
C^{0}([0,T];H)
. Since the composition of Fréchet differentiable mappings is again Fréchet differentiable, it follows that
\mathrm{J}
is Fréchet differentiable on
\mathcal{U}_{M}
.
Hence, passing to the limit in (
7.1
) as
s\to 0^{+}
, we obtain

D\mathrm{J}(u^{*})[u-u^{*}]\geq 0

for all
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
.
By explicitly computing this derivative via the chain rule, we arrive at the following result, which provides a first version of the optimality necessary condition we are looking for.

7.2
Conclusions

We now rewrite the optimality conditions in terms of the solution to the adjoint problem.

Theorem 7.3

(First-order necessary conditions of optimality)
.

Assume that the hypotheses
1
–
6
hold and let
\tau
be greater than or equal to
0
. Let
u^{*}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
be an optimal control with associated optimal state
(\theta^{*},\varphi^{*},\mu^{*},\sigma^{*})
. Then, for every
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{ad}}
it holds that

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\mathrm{z}+b_{5}u^{*})(u-u^{*})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\geq 0,

(7.20)

where
\mathrm{z}
is the first component of the solution
(\mathrm{z},\mathrm{p},\mathrm{q},\mathrm{r})
to
the adjoint system (
7.3
)–(
7.5
) related to
u^{*}
.

Proof.

First, we recall the linearised equations (
5.4
) in which the increment
h
in (
5.4a
) is just
u-u^{*}
.
Then, we proceed by testing (
5.4a
) with
\mathrm{z}
, (
5.4b
) with
\mathrm{p}
, (
5.4c
) with
\mathrm{q}
, and (
5.4d
) with
\mathrm{r}
, and summing the resulting identities. Next, we test the equations (
7.6a
), (
7.6b
), (
7.6c
), and (
7.6d
) with
\zeta
,
\xi
,
\eta
, and
\rho
, respectively, and subtract these from the previous sum to obtain, after some cancellations, the following equality:

\begin{split}&\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\zeta\mathrm{z}+\ell\xi\mathrm{z}+\xi\mathrm{p}+\tau\xi\mathrm{q}+\rho\mathrm{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right]\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})\mathrm{z}\,\mathrm{d}x-b_{1}\int_{\Omega}(\theta^{*}-\theta_{Q})\zeta\,\mathrm{d}x-b_{3}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}-\varphi_{Q})\xi\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Integrating in time over the interval
(0,T)
and exploiting the initial/final conditions (
5.3
), (
7.5
) leads to:

\begin{split}&b_{2}\int_{\Omega}(\theta^{*}(T)-\theta_{\Omega})\zeta(T)\,\mathrm{d}x+b_{4}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}(T)-\varphi_{\Omega})\xi(T)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})\mathrm{z}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-b_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\theta^{*}-\theta_{Q})\zeta\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-b_{3}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{*}-\varphi_{Q})\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(7.21)

Finally, combining (
7.21
) with the previously obtained (
7.2
), the thesis follows.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

The inequality (

), which holds for all

, shows that

coincides with the

-projection of

onto the closed and convex set
