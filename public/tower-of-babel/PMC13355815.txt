Hspa1b attenuates hypoxia/reoxygenation-induced cardiomyocyte injury through dual suppression of P53-driven apoptotic and ferroptotic pathways
Authors: Chang Liu, Wensheng Qi, Yue Li, Tingting Fan, Yun Lan, Lei Pang, Haichun Ma, Yin Cai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
While the cardioprotective role of heat shock proteins (HSPs) in cardiovascular diseases is well established, the isoform-specific functions of HSP70 members in ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury remain unclear. This study investigates the role of Hspa1b, a stress-inducible HSP70 isoform, in cardiac I/R injury and elucidates its underlying mechanisms. In vivo, male C57BL/6 J mice were subjected to myocardial I/R surgery. In vitro, H9C2 cardiomyocytes were transfected with siRNA targeting Hspa1b or p53 and subjected to a hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) model. Cellular injury was quantified via lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release, while viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay. Apoptotic (Bax, Bcl2, and cleaved Caspase3) and ferroptotic (GPX4, XCT/SLC7A11) markers were analyzed by Western blotting. We found that I/R injury in mouse hearts upregulated Hspa1b and p53 protein levels, accompanied by increased infarct size and elevated plasma CK-MB levels. Similarly, H/R treatment in H9C2 cells increased Hspa1b and p53, which coincided with increased apoptosis and ferroptosis. Knockdown of Hspa1b exacerbated H/R-induced cellular injury, as evidenced by further increases in LDH release and reductions in cell viability, and amplified the changes in apoptotic and ferroptotic markers. Crucially, co-silencing Hspa1b and p53 partially rescued these effects, restoring cell viability and suppressing death pathways. Hspa1b confers cardioprotection against H/R injury by suppressing p53-mediated apoptosis and ferroptosis. These findings identify Hspa1b as a key protective regulator that mitigates I/R injury through dual regulation of cell death pathways.

Significance Statement Myocardial ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury remains a major unmet challenge in cardiovascular medicine, with limited therapies targeting its underlying molecular drivers. Current approaches fail to address the dual activation of apoptosis and ferroptosis, which is a critical gap in cardioprotective strategy. Here, we identify Hspa1b (a stress-inducible HSP70 isoform) as an upstream suppressor of p53-driven apoptotic and ferroptotic death pathways. Critically, we demonstrate that genetic silencing of Hspa1b exacerbates hypoxia/reoxygenation injury by amplifying p53 activity, whereas concurrent p53 knockdown rescues cardiomyocyte viability. This establishes Hspa1b as a pharmacologically tractable regulator of p53-mediated dual cell death, providing the important evidence of isoform-specific HSP70 cardioprotection and opening avenues for targeted therapeutics.

Background

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of global morbidity and mortality, particularly in the aging population, with myocardial infarction (MI) representing one of the most critical clinical manifestations.1 Despite advances in reperfusion therapies such as percutaneous coronary intervention and thrombolysis, the restoration of blood flow to ischemic myocardium paradoxically exacerbates tissue damage through ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury.2 This phenomenon significantly limits the clinical benefits of revascularization, highlighting the urgent need to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of I/R injury and develop targeted cardioprotective strategies.

Myocardial I/R involves a complex interplay of cellular pathways, including apoptosis3 ferroptosis,4 and metabolic dysfunction.5 Apoptosis, a tightly regulated form of programmed cell death, contributes to cardiomyocyte loss through mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening, reactive oxygen species burst, and activation of caspase-dependent pathways6, 7 and the death receptor pathway.8, 9 Ferroptosis, an iron-dependent form of- cell death driven by lipid peroxidation and glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) inactivation and dysfunction of the cystine/glutamate antiporter system xc - (mediated by XCT/SLC7A11), has emerged as key mediators of reperfusion-phase injury.4 The impaired activity of XCT/SLC7A11 limits cystine uptake, depleting glutathione (GSH) reserves and compromising cellular antioxidant defenses, thereby exacerbating lipid peroxidation and ferroptotic cell death.10 The synergistic roles of these pathways highlight apoptosis and ferroptosis as critical therapeutic targets for attenuating I/R injury.

Heat shock protein 70 (HSP70), a stress-inducible molecular chaperone,11 is widely recognized for its cardioprotective effects in myocardial I/R injury12, 13, 14 and heart failure.15 Its cytoprotective functions include inhibition of apoptosis via suppression of STIM1/IP3R16 and attenuation of ferroptosis through HIF-1α SUMOylation.17 However, the HSP70 family comprises multiple isoforms, including the nearly identical Hspa1a and Haspa1b, which share 99% sequence identity but exhibit distinct regulatory and functional profiles.18, 19 While the cardioprotective role of HSP70 has been reported, the specific contributions of Hspa1b, a major inducible isoform, remain poorly characterized.

Notably, Hspa1b has been implicated in diverse pathologies, including cancer,20 metabolic disorders,21, 22, 23, 24 and CVDs.25, 26, 27 In lung cancer, genetic variants that reduce Hspa1b expression are associated with increased cancer risk.20 In the heart, the rs3036297 polymorphism reduces Hspa1b expression via miRNA binding and is associated with sudden cardiac death,26 while Tet2‑mediated Hspa1b expression protects against cardiac hypertrophy.25 Moreover, a Mendelian randomization study found that higher genetically predicted Hspa1b levels are inversely associated with diabetic complications, suggesting a protective role.21 Importantly, available evidence indicates that Hspa1b polymorphisms increase disease risk primarily through reduced expression rather than impaired chaperone function,26 and Hspa1b upregulation does not directly cause pathology but may reflect an endogenous protective response.21 Collectively, these findings suggest that Hspa1b may be protective in certain contexts, yet its role in myocardial I/R injury remains largely unexplored. Specifically, whether Hspa1b suppresses apoptosis and ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes, and through what mechanisms, has not been definitively established.

Therefore, the present study was designed to investigate the cardioprotective effects of Hspa1b in hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R)-induced cardiomyocytes injury, with a focus on apoptosis and ferroptosis. Our findings provide novel insights into HSP70 isoform specificity and highlight Hspa1b as a therapeutic target for myocardial protection.

Method

Myocardial ischemia/reperfusion model in mice

All animal experiments were performed in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines and approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of The Hong Kong Polytechnic University (approval no.23-24/711-HTI-R-NSFC). Male wildtype C57BL/6J mice (12 weeks old) were obtained from and housed in the Centralized Animal Facilities of The Hong Kong Polytechnic University under a temperature- and light-controlled environment (12 h light/dark cycle). Standard rodent chow (Lab Diet, PMI Nutrition International, MO, USA) and water were available ad libitum. A total of 20 mice were randomly assigned to two groups: sham operation (n = 10) and myocardial I/R (n = 10). In each group, 5 mice were used for infarct size measurement, and a separate cohort of 5 mice was used for Western blotting and qPCR analyses. Sample sizes were determined based on previous study using the same I/R model without formal a priori power calculation.1 For myocardial I/R induction, mice were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection of ketamine (100 mg/kg) and xylazine (10 mg/kg). Following tracheal intubation was initiated mechanical ventilation to maintain respiration. A left thoracotomy was conducted to expose the heart, and the left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery was occluded with a 7-0 silk suture for 30 min, followed by 2 h of reperfusion. Sham-operated controls underwent identical surgical procedures without LAD ligation. No unexpected adverse events, such as severe arrhythmia or premature death, were observed during or after the I/R procedure in any of the experimental animals. Accordingly, all animals that underwent surgery were included in the final analysis. No inclusion or exclusion criteria were applied during the experiment. Due to the nature of the surgical procedure, the surgeon performing the I/R or sham operation was not blinded to group allocation. However, all outcome assessments were conducted by investigators who were blinded to the group assignments. The blinding code was not revealed until all analyses were finalized.

Infarction size determination

MI size was quantified using dual Evans blue/TTC (2,3,5-triphenyltetrazolium chloride) staining as previously described.1 Briefly, the LAD was re-occluded, and Evans blue (5%) was perfused to demarcate the area at risk (AAR). Hearts were sectioned and incubated in 1% TTC to distinguish viable (red) from infarcted (pale) myocardium. Digital images of cross-sectional slices were analyzed using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, MD, USA). Infarct size was calculated as a percentage of the AAR.

Measurement of creatine kinase-MB

The activity of creatinine kinase-MB (CK-MB) was measured using a commercial enzymatic assay kit (Cloud-Clone Corp., Wuhan, Hubei, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Cell culture and treatment

The H9C2 rat cardiomyocyte cell line (American Type Culture Collection, Manassas, VA, USA) was cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (100 U/mL, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Cells were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37°C with 5% CO2 under standard atmospheric oxygen conditions.

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting rat Hspa1b, p53, and scramble siRNA (negative control) were purchased from Ribobio (Guangzhou, Guangdong, China). H9C2 cardiomyocytes were transfected with 50 nM siRNA using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. To mimic myocardial I/R injury in vitro, transfected cells were subjected to H/R stimulation. Briefly, the culture medium was replaced with serum- and glucose-free DMEM, and cells were placed in a hypoxia chamber (1% O2, 5% CO2, 94% N2, 37°C) for 6 h. For reoxygenation, cells were returned to complete culture medium and incubated under normoxic conditions for 12 h. All cell-based assays were performed in four or five independent biological replicates using H9C2 cardiomyocytes at distinct passages. For each experiment, triplicate technical replicates (wells) per condition were analyzed.

Cell counting kit-8 assay

Cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) assay. Briefly, cells seeded in 6-well plates were treated as described. At the designated time point, 100 μL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well. After incubation at 37°C for 4 h, absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. Cell viability was normalized to untreated control groups.

Lactate dehydrogenase leakage assay

Cytotoxicity was evaluated using the lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) Cytotoxicity Assay Kit (Roche, Mannheim, Germany) by quantifying LDH release into the culture supernatant. Following 12 h of reoxygenation, 46 μL of supernatant was collected from each well, mixed with an equal volume of LDH detection reagent (prepared according to the manufacturer’s protocol), and incubated at room temperature for 15 min. Absorbance was measured at 562 nm using a microplate reader. Cytotoxicity was normalized to untreated control groups.

Quantitative polymerase chain reaction

Total RNA was isolated from H9C2 cardiomyocytes using the TaKaRa MiniBEST Universal RNA Extraction Kit (TaKaRa, Japan). Subsequently, RNA was reverse transcribed into complementary DNA with the PrimeScript RT Master Mix kit (Takara) following the manufacturer’s protocols. Gene expression analysis was conducted on an Applied Biosystems ViiA7 Real-time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The thermal cycling protocol included an initial denaturation at 95°C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of amplification (95°C for 5 s and 60°C for 31 s). Target-specific primer pairs were designed as follows: p53-Rat-Forward: ACAGCGTGGTGGTACCGTAT, p53-Rat-Reverse: GGAGCTGTTGCACATGTACT, β-actin-Rat-Forward: ACAACCTTCTTGCAGCTCCTC, β-actin-Rat-Reverse: CCTTCTGACCCATACCCACC. Relative mRNA level was quantified using the 2-ΔΔCt method, with β-actin serving as the endogenous control for normalization.

Western blotting

Frozen heart tissues and H9C2 cardiomyocytes were homogenized in ice-cold lysis buffer (Cell Signaling Technology) containing a protease inhibitor cocktail (Roche). Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA assay kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Equal protein aliquots were resolved on 8%–12.5% SDS-PAGE gels and electrophoretically transferred to PVDF membranes using standard protocols.28 Primary antibodies (all diluted 1:1000) included: caspase 3, cleaved caspase 3, Bax, Bcl2, GPX4, XCT, Hspa1b, β-tubulin (Cell Signaling Technology), and β-actin (1:1000, ABclonal Technology, Woburn, MA, USA). Membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies, washed, and then probed with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-mouse/anti-rabbit secondary antibodies (1:5000) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were detected using Clarity Max ECL substrates (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) and imaged on a ChemiDoc MP Integrated Imaging system (Bio-Rad). Band intensities were quantified with ImageJ (National Institutes of Health, MD, USA) and normalized to β-tubulin or β-actin as loading controls.

Statistical analysis

Data are expressed as mean ± SD. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 (San Diego, CA, USA). Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and equality of variances was assessed using the F‑test (for two groups). For comparisons between two groups, if data followed a Gaussian distribution and variances were equal, an unpaired Student’s t‑test (two‑tailed) was used; if normally distributed but variances were unequal, Welch’s t‑test was used; if data did not follow a normal distribution, the Mann–Whitney U test (two‑tailed) was used. For comparisons among three or more groups, one‑way ANOVA was applied, followed by Tukey’s multiple comparisons test. For experiments involving two independent variables, two‑way ANOVA was used, also followed by Tukey’s multiple comparisons test. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Given the cellular heterogeneity of myocardial tissue, we further investigated Hspa1b induction specifically in cardiomyocytes using an in vitro H/R model. H9C2 cardiomyocytes exposed to 6-h hypoxia and 12-h reoxygenation exhibited a striking ∼24-fold increase in Hspa1b mRNA compared to normoxic controls, whereas Hspa1a exhibited only a modest induction (Figure 1e). Consistent with transcript-level findings, Hspa1b protein abundance was significantly elevated in H/R-stimulated cells (Figure 1f). Despite a significant increase in its transcript levels, this did not translate into a corresponding increase in Hspa1a protein expression following H/R stimulation (Figure 1f). Notably, H/R stimulation significantly increased LDH release and reduced cell viability (Figure 1g, h). Given the pronounced and specific induction of Hspa1b across both in vivo and in vitro models, subsequent investigations focused on elucidating the functional role of Hspa1b in H/R-induced cardiomyocyte injury. These results collectively demonstrate that Hspa1b is robustly upregulated in I/R-injured myocardium and H/R-challenged cardiomyocytes, implicating its potential involvement in the pathophysiology of ischemic cardiac injury.

Knockdown of Hspa1b exacerbates hypoxia/reoxygenation-stimulated cell injury in H9C2 cardiomyocytes

To investigate the functional role of Hspa1b in cardiomyocyte injury, we silenced endogenous Hspa1b in H9C2 cells using siRNA. Transfection with Hspa1b-specific siRNA resulted in a significant downregulation of both Hspa1b mRNA (Figure 2a) and Hspa1b protein (Figure 2b) compared to scrambled siRNA controls. Under basal conditions, Hspa1b knockdown had no significant effect on LDH release or cell viability. However, following H/R, Hspa1b-silenced cells exhibited noticeably elevated LDH release (Figure 2c) and a significant reduction in post-H/R viability compared to control cells (Figure 2d). Given the observed exacerbation of H/R injury in Hspa1b-silenced cells, we also assessed ferroptosis markers. H/R significantly downregulated the ferroptosis suppressors GPX4 and XCT. p53 mediates Hspa1b-dependent protection against hypoxia/reoxygenation-induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis and ferroptosis

To test whether p53 contributes to the exacerbated apoptosis and ferroptosis in Hspa1b-silenced cells, we performed dual knockdown of Hspa1b and p53 in H9C2 cardiomyocytes subjected to H/R. Hspa1b knockdown elevated p53 protein levels (Figure 5a). To examine whether p53 reciprocally regulates Hspa1b, we knocked down p53 alone under normoxic conditions and found no significant change in Hspa1b expression (Figure S1). These results indicate a unidirectional relationship in which p53 acts downstream of Hspa1b. Co-silencing p53 and Hspa1b attenuated H/R-induced apoptosis, as demonstrated by a significant reduction in cleaved caspase-3 levels compared to Hspa1b knockdown alone (Figure 5b). Similarly, dual depletion of p53 and Hspa1b partially reversed the ferroptotic damage observed in Hspa1b-silenced H9C2 cells, restoring GPX4 and XCT expression to levels comparable to H/R-treated controls (without Hspa1b knockdown) (Figure 5c). These molecular improvements correlated with functional recovery, dual knockdown significantly reduced LDH release (Figure 5d). Discussion

Myocardial I/R injury remains a critical challenge in cardiovascular therapeutics, necessitating deeper insights into molecular regulators of cardiomyocyte survival. In the present study, we identified Hspa1b, a stress-inducible member of the HSP70 family, as a key protector against I/R-induced cardiomyocyte death. We demonstrated that Hspa1b is more responsive to post-ischemic myocardium and H/R-challenged cardiomyocytes, with its genetic silencing exacerbating H/R-induced apoptosis and ferroptosis. Mechanistically, Hspa1b mitigates ischemic injury by restraining the accumulation of p53, a central regulator of both apoptotic and ferroptotic pathways. Specifically, Hspa1b knockdown exacerbates p53-driven suppression of the anti-apoptotic Bcl2/Bax ratio and ferroptosis markers (GPX4, XCT/SLC7A11). This multi-faceted injury, however, is substantially alleviated through dual silencing of Hspa1b and p53, which rescues cardiomyocyte injury. These findings establish Hspa1b as a novel regulator coordinating cardiomyocyte survival through p53-dependent suppression of dual cell death modalities.

Our findings align with prior studies demonstrating the cardioprotective role of HSP70 in ischemic injury,31 yet extend this understanding by revealing isoform-specific functional divergence within the HSP70 family. While earlier work attributed cytoprotection broadly to HSP70, such as its anti-apoptotic effects through preventing recruitment of procaspase-9 to the Apaf-1 apoptosome,32 our study identifies Hspa1b as the predominant isoform responsive to ischemic stress, with minimal involvement of Hspa1a. This functional disparity may arise from structural and regulatory distinctions between the two isoforms, despite their near-identical coding sequences (>99% identity), Hspa1a and Hspa1b differ in promoter regions and 3′ UTRs,33 which likely dictate their differential stress inducibility. Notably, Hspa1b’s selective upregulation in H/R injury mirrors observations in doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity,34 suggesting a conserved role in cardiac stress adaptation.

The cardioprotective capacity of HSP70 has also been linked to diverse mechanisms, including JNK/p38-MAPK pathway modulation35 and ferroptosis suppression in non-cardiac contexts.36, 37 Our work bridges these fragmented insights by establishing Hspa1b as a dual regulator of apoptosis and ferroptosis through a unified p53-dependent axis. Under ischemic stress, p53 activates apoptosis through transcription-dependent mechanisms and direct mitochondrial permeabilization, culminating in caspase-3 activation, a pathway well-documented in heart failure and I/R injury.30, 38, 39, 40 Intriguingly, p53 also drives ferroptosis via suppression of SLC7A11 and GPX4,41, 42 thereby depleting glutathione and amplifying lipid peroxidation. This dual capacity positions p53 as a bifunctional switch directing cardiomyocyte fate toward divergent death modalities. Emerging studies highlight the therapeutic potential of targeting p53 in ferroptosis-driven pathologies. For instance, p53 inhibition mitigates acute lung injury post-intestinal I/R,43 while modulating p53 acetylation attenuates cardiac fibrosis and heart failure by restoring SLC7A11/GPX4 expression.42, 44 Notably, in doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity, the p53/SLC7A11/GPX4 axis has been identified as a key mediator of ferroptotic damage, with its blockade offering cardioprotection.41 Our findings position Hspa1b as an upstream repressor of p53’s dual death-promoting functions in I/R injury, naturally constraining p53 accumulation and coordinately suppressing both apoptosis (via Bcl2/Bax/caspase-3) and ferroptosis (via SLC7A11/GPX4). This regulatory hierarchy, where Hspa1b governs p53 stability, represents a novel cardioprotective mechanism, distinct from direct p53-targeted strategies.

The regulatory relationship between Hspa1b and p53 raises critical questions about how they interact at the molecular level. While HSP70 has been shown to modulate p53 stability in cancer45, 46 and neural injury,47 Hspa1b may promote p53 degradation through ubiquitin-proteasome pathways, potentially via indirect recruitment of co-chaperones such as CHIP (C-terminus of HSP70-interacting protein).48 This hypothesis is strongly supported by evidence showing that Hspa1b silencing elevates p53 protein levels without affecting p53 mRNA, and that Hhspa1b enhances p53 ubiquitination and degradation in doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity.34 However, whether Hspa1b directly facilitates p53 ubiquitination remains unproven, a critical gap for future exploration.

Several limitations should be acknowledged in the present study. First, while H9C2 cells provide mechanistic clarity, validation in primary cardiomyocytes or Hspa1b knockout animal models would enhance clinical relevance. Second, although we identified p53 as a critical downstream effector, the upstream triggers of Hspa1b induction during ischemia remain elusive. Previous studies implicate HIF-1α in regulating HSP70 expression under hypoxia in chondrocytes and pulmonary endothelial cells;49, 50 however, our data reveals that HIF-1α inhibition (via LW6 pretreatment) fails to suppress Hspa1b upregulation in H/R-challenged cardiomyocytes (Figure S2). This suggests stress-specific regulatory divergence, where cardiac Hspa1b induction may bypass canonical hypoxia pathways. The upstream regulatory mechanisms driving Hspa1b induction during ischemia remain to be explored. Lastly, the therapeutic implications of targeting the Hspa1b-p53 axis require cautious evaluation. While suppressing p53 ameliorates ischemic injury, its tumor-suppressive functions raise concerns about long-term oncogenic risks. Future studies should explore tissue-specific delivery systems (e.g., cardiac-targeted nanoparticles or adeno-associated virus vectors) to spatially restrict p53 inhibition within ischemic myocardium.

In conclusion, the present study establishes Hspa1b as a master regulatory switch coordinating cardiomyocyte fate under ischemic stress. By bridging HSP70 biology with p53’s pleiotropic roles in cell death, our work resolves longstanding ambiguities about isoform-specific functions within the HSP70 family. These findings highlight Hspa1b as a promising therapeutic target to block both apoptosis and ferroptosis simultaneously. Future efforts to develop Hspa1b activators or p53-targeted delivery systems could translate these discoveries into therapies that mitigate I/R injury without compromising p53’s tumor-suppressive functions, ultimately advancing precision medicine in CVD.
