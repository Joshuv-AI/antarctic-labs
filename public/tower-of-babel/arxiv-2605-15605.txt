Representability of the automorphism group of finitely generated vertex algebras
Terry Gannon, Robin Mader, Arturo Pianzola

Abstract
We study the automorphism groups attached to a free algebra with multiple, possibly infinitely many, composition laws. As an application, we prove that the automorphism group of finitely generated vertex algebras over noetherian rings are affine group schemes.



Key words:
Vertex Algebra, Automorphism Group, Affine Group Scheme, Representability.

MSC 2000:
Primary: 17B69; secondary: 14F20, 14L15.

Let

The nature of

Let

\left\{\begin{aligned} M\times M&\to M,\\

First we show the existence of an

containing

-magma

. This is the

We construct

recursively. For any two sets

, denote the set

.
For

via

M^{k}(X)=\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\\
m\in\mathcal{M}\end{subarray}}M^{j}(X)_{m}M^{k-j}(X).

Let

For

,

This makes

into an

-magma. Given

as follows. Say

There exists a unique decomposition

Lemma 1
.

The above map
\theta\mapsto\widetilde{\theta}
is an injective homomorphism of groups
\operatorname{GL}_{R}(F)\to\operatorname{Aut}(A(X)).
In fact, this map induces a group isomorphism between
\operatorname{GL}_{R}(F)
and

\operatorname{Aut}(A(X),F)=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(A(X))\mid\theta(F)=F\}.

Proof.

Let
\sigma,\theta\in\operatorname{GL}_{R}(F)
. By definition,
(\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\theta})|_{X}=\sigma\circ\theta|_{X}
, but there is only one
\mathcal{M}
-algebra map extending
\sigma\circ\theta
, namely
\widetilde{\sigma\circ\theta}
.
Since
\widetilde{\operatorname{id}}=\operatorname{id}
, the lemma follows.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
A
be an
\mathcal{M}
-algebra over

which contains a copy of

as an
R
-submodule. Let
\operatorname{Aut}(A,F)=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)\mid\theta(F)=F\}.
If

generates

then:

(i)

The map

\operatorname{Aut}(A,F)\to\operatorname{Aut}(A(X),F),\hskip 10.00002pt\theta\mapsto\widetilde{\theta}=\widetilde{\theta|_{F}}.

(1)

is a group monomorphism.

(ii)

There exists a unique surjective
\mathcal{M}
-algebra homomorphism
\eta\colon A(X)\to A
that is the identity on

(iii)

Let

denote the kernel of
\eta
. The image of the map in (

\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(A(X),F)
with
\varphi(I)=I
.

Proof.

(i)
The map is clearly a group homomorphism. Since

generates

we can appeal to Lemma

(ii)
By assumption

has a copy of

We can thus view the elements of

as elements of

. Now (ii) is clear by the universal property of

and the assumption that

generates

(iii)
For

-algebra homomorphisms with the same restriction to

Thus

. The same reasoning applies to

and so

.
Conversely, let

.
Since
\eta
is surjective,

\varphi

on the quotient

From

.
∎

Thus
A=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}

The grading thus defines a function

\lambda

Lemma 8
.

Assume that
A=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}
is finitely generated, and that each of the
A_{\lambda}
is free of finite rank. Then there exists a finite set
X
with the following properties:

(i)
X
leads to an
\mathcal{M}
-algebra homomorphism
\eta\colon A(X)\to A
as in Proposition
2

(ii) There exists on
A(X)
a
\Lambda
-grading where each element of
M(X)
is homogeneous.

(iii)
\eta\colon A(X)\to A
is a
\Lambda
-graded homomorphism of
\mathcal{M}
-algebras over
R.

Proof.

Choose
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
so that
F=A_{\lambda_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus A_{\lambda_{n}}
generates
A.
Let
X=\{x^{1},\dots,x^{N}\}
be a basis of
F
consisting of homogeneous elements. Then
F
is a direct summand of
A
and we can define the map
\eta\colon A(X)\to A
that is the identity of
F.

Next we attach to every element
a
of the
\mathcal{M}
-magma
M(X)
a degree
{\rm deg}(a)
in
\Lambda.
We do this by induction on the length of
a.
If
a\in M^{1}(X)
, then
a=x^{i}\in X.
By assumption
x^{i}\in A_{\lambda_{j}}
for
some
1\leq j\leq n
and we set
{\rm deg}(a)=\lambda_{j}.
Assume
\ell>1
and that we have defined the degree for elements of length less than
\ell.
If
a\in M^{\ell}(X),
then
a=b_{m}c
for some unique elements
b\in M^{i}(X),c\in M^{j}(X)
and
m\in\mathcal{M}
where
1\leq i,j<\ell
and
i+j=\ell.
If
{\rm deg}(b)=\beta
and
{\rm deg}(c)=\gamma,
we set
{\rm deg}(a)=d(\beta,m,\gamma).
Since the elements of
M(X)
are an
R
-basis of
A(X),
we have our desired
\Lambda
-grading on the module
A(X).
By construction if
x\in A(X)_{\beta},

y\in A(X)_{\gamma}
and
m\in\mathcal{M}
, then
x_{m}y\in A(X)_{\lambda}
where
\lambda=d(\beta,m,\gamma).
The map
\eta
is thus a
\Lambda
-graded homomorphism of
\mathcal{M}
-algebras over
R.

∎

Theorem 9
.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
be a finitely generated vertex algebra over a ring

Assume that each of the
R

is of finite type, and that we are in one of the following two situations:

(a)
R
is noetherian and
V
is locally free as an
R
-module.

(b) The
V_{n}
are free.

Then
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}_{\rm gr}(V)
is an affine
R
-group of finite type which is locally isomorphic to a closed subgroup of
\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}_{N}
for some
N.

Proof.

Case (a): Since
V
is locally free, the
V_{\lambda}
are locally projective, hence projective. Choose a finite number of these, say
V_{n_{1}},\dots,V_{n_{\ell}},
that generate
V,
and let
F=\bigoplus V_{n_{i}}
be their sum. This is a projective
R
-module of finite type, hence locally free. By further localizing
R
we may assume that each of the
V_{n_{i}}

are free. Note that
F
is a direct summand of
V
. Let
N
be its rank.

By Theorem
5
we may assume that
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(V,F)
is a closed subgroup of
\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}_{N,R}
.
Since any degree preserving automorphism automatically preserves
F
, we find that
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}_{\rm gr}(V)
is a subgroup sheaf of
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(V,F)
. We claim that
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}_{\rm gr}(V)

\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(V,F).

Since
F

an element of
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(V,F)
is in

But this is clearly a polynomial condition on the coordinate ring of
\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}_{N,R}

Remark 11
.

Theorem
