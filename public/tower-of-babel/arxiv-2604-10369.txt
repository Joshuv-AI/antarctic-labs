Optimal FPT-Approximability for Modular Linear Equations
Konrad K. Dabrowski, Peter Jonsson, Sebastian Ordyniak, George Osipov, Magnus Wahlström

Abstract
We show optimal FPT-approximability results for solving almost satisfiable systems of modular linear equations, completing the picture of the parameterized complexity and FPT-approximability landscape for the Min- r r -Lin ( ℤ m ) (\mathbb{Z}_{m}) problem for every r r and m m . In Min- r r -Lin ( ℤ m ) (\mathbb{Z}_{m}) , we are given a system S S of linear equations modulo m m , each on at most r r variables, and the goal is to find a subset Z ⊆ S Z\subseteq S of minimum cardinality such that S − Z S-Z is satisfiable. The problem is NP-hard, and UGC-hard to approximate within any constant factor for every r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 and m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , which motivates studying it through the lens of parameterized complexity with solution size as the parameter. From previous work (Dabrowski et al. SODA’23/TALG and ESA’25) we know that Min- r r -Lin ( ℤ m ) (\mathbb{Z}_{m}) is W[1]-hard to FPT-approximate within any constant factor when r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 , and that Min- 2 2 -Lin ( ℤ m ) (\mathbb{Z}_{m}) is in FPT when m m is prime and W[1]-hard when m m has at least two distinct prime factors. The case when m = p d m=p^{d} for some prime p p and d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 has remained an open problem. We resolve this problem in this paper and prove the following: • FPT algorithm for prime powers : we prove that Min- 2 2 -Lin ( ℤ p d ) (\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}) is in FPT for every prime p p and d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 . This implies that Min- 2 2 -Lin ( ℤ m ) (\mathbb{Z}_{m}) can be FPT-approximated within a factor of ω ⁡ ( m ) \omega(m) , where ω \omega is the number of distinct prime factors of m m . • Matching lower bound : we show that, under the ETH, Min- 2 2 -Lin ( ℤ m ) (\mathbb{Z}_{m}) cannot be FPT-approximated within ω ⁡ ( m ) − ε \omega(m)-\varepsilon for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . Our main algorithmic contribution is a new technique coined balanced subgraph covering , which generalizes important balanced subgraphs of Dabrowski et al. (SODA’23/TALG) and shadow removal of Marx and Razgon (STOC’11/SICOMP). In a nutshell, we relax Min- 2 2 -Lin ( ℤ p d ) (\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}) by allowing ambiguous assignments, which may map variables to subsets of values (as opposed to only individual values). We then apply balanced subgraph covering to impose additional constraints on the relaxation, solve it by a reduction to MinSat , and use the additional constraints to convert any ambiguous assignment into a proper value assignment of the same cost. For the lower bounds, we develop a framework for proving optimality of FPT-approximation factors under the ETH. We consider a variant of s ​ t st - MinCut where the edges of the input graph are partitioned into bundles of size ℓ \ell and each bundle can be removed at cost 1 1 . Starting from a recent inapproximability result of Bafna et al. (STOC’25), we prove that s ​ t st - MinCut with edge bundles of size ℓ ≥ 2 \ell\geq 2 cannot be FPT-approximated within ℓ − ε \ell-\varepsilon under the ETH, and then reduce the latter problem into Min- 2 2 -Lin ( ℤ m ) (\mathbb{Z}_{m}) with ω ⁡ ( m ) = ℓ \omega(m)=\ell .

Systems of linear equations are omnipresent in computer science and

]
.
In particular, equations and congruences over

We consider the

Even

Technical overview.

Let us give a brief overview of our tools and techniques; more
details are found in
Sections
1.1
–
1.3
.
We begin with the positive result. Let
m\geq 2
be a constant. An
instance of
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
)
consists (somewhat informally) of a
set of variables
V
, a set
S
of binary equations over
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, and
an integer
k
, and the question is whether there is an assignment

\varphi\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}_{m}
which satisfies all but at most
k

equations. Here, a generic binary equation over
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
takes the form

au+bv=c\pmod{m}
where
a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
and
u,v\in V
are
variables.

We simplify the situation in several ways.
By
[
DJO+25b
, Proposition 4]
, it is sufficient to prove that

Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
)
(for
d\geq 2
and an arbitrary prime
p
)
is in FPT.
Furthermore, it is known from
[
DJO+25b
, Lemma 5]
that we can
restrict our attention to instances with two types of constraints, a
crisp (unbreakable) constraint
s=1
and constraints
u=av
for
u,v\in V
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
. For simplicity,
we further separate the latter into equations
u=av

where
a\bmod p\neq 0
(i.e.
a
is a
unit
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
) and equations
u=pv
.
We refer to these as
special
instances.
Of these equation types,
u=av
induces a permutation of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
, while
u=pv

acts like a “coarsening” of the domain
(e.g. for
m=9
,
u=3v
maps
v\in\{1,4,7\}
to
u=3
,
v\in\{2,5,8\}

to
u=6
, and
v\in\{0,3,6\}
to
u=0
). While the FPT literature contains
many methods for dealing with systems of permutation constraints
[
CCH+16
,
IWY16
,
IYY18
,
Wah17
]
,
their interaction with constraints
u=pv
causes significant complications.

For our solution to
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
)
, we first show a support statement,
starting from the formulation of equation systems as
biased graphs
used in previous work
[
DJO+25a
,
Wah17
]

and generalize it into a statement we call
balanced subgraph covering
;
see Section
1.1
. Informally, this result takes
an inconsistent system
G
of permutation constraints, with a hidden almost
consistent induced subsystem
H
, and returns a consistent subsystem

G^{\prime}
of
G
, with some annotations, such that with sufficient probability

G^{\prime}
captures the hidden system
H
. We thus generalize the
shadow covering
,
(a.k.a.
shadow removal
) technique of Marx and
Razgon
[
MR14
]
into the setting of biased graphs.

With this tool in hand, our algorithm has two aspects.
First, we formulate a
Boolean relaxation
of the problem, where
we reduce the instance to a Boolean bijunctive formula, in order to use the powerful
FPT-algorithm for
MinSat
by Kim et al.
[
KKPW25
]
as a backend solver.
This formulation is not precise, but it is an overapproximation of the
solution space, whose solution gives partial information about a variable value
\varphi(v)
.
Technically, it assigns a
coset
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
for every value
\varphi(v)
;
e.g. in the simplest interesting case of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
such a solution may encode

\varphi(v)=1\pmod{2}
without settling on either
\varphi(v)=1
or
\varphi(v)=3
.
This will handle constraints
u=pv
, but fail to resolve permutation constraints
such as
u=3v
. To address this, we apply the balanced subgraph covering tool
to a collection of auxiliary biased graphs that capture the structure
of these permutation constraints. The solutions returned by this tool
can then be used to add additional constraints to the Boolean formula,
in a way that allows a
disambiguation step
– a guarantee
that every assignment to the Boolean formula can be fully resolved
into an assignment
\varphi\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
without increasing
the number of violated constraints. Thus the
MinSat
solver,
in the presence of these additional constraints, acts as a complete FPT
algorithm for
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
)
.

For the negative result, we first note that
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
is a direct sum of
\omega(m)
different rings
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
by the Chinese Remainder Theorem.
We show that
Min-
2
-Lin(
R
)
for a ring
R

that is the direct sum of
p
rings
R_{i}
does not have an
FPT-approximation with ratio
p-\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0

unless ETH is false.
In the course of the proof,
we define an auxiliary problem

-Split Min Cut
and show that it
cannot be FPT-approximated within

for any
\varepsilon>0
.

-Split Min Cut
is a natural generalization of
the problem

which has frequently been used in W[1]-hardness proofs (e.g.
[

,
KKPW25
,
OW23
,
DJO+23
,
MR09
]
).
To show hardness of

-Split Min Cut

we start from a recent result of

]

that

-CSP

To reduce to

we construct dense instances of

-CSP

1.1
Balanced Subgraph Covering

Our algorithm builds on a combinatorial statement about so-called
biased graphs
.
Biased graphs were studied by Zaslavsky
[
Zas89
,
Zas91
]

and are an important topic in matroid theory; see e.g.
[
Oxl11
,
Wal26
,
Zas12
]
.
They have also seen applications in parameterized complexity
[
DJO+25a
,
Wah17
]
.
Before we give the definitions, let us review an example. Let
G
be a
graph, and consider a simple cycle
C
in
G
to be
balanced
if
it has even length. A subgraph
H
of
G
is balanced if every cycle
in
H
is balanced – i.e. if
H
is bipartite.
The problem of deleting at most
k
edges from
G
so that the remaining graph is balanced is now effectively
Edge Bipartization
, which has a long history in parameterized complexity (along with its vertex deletion variant,
Odd Cycle Transversal
)
[
PPW19
,
GGH+06
,
RSV04
,
KMRS20
]
.

Biased graphs provide a rich generalization of these notions: a
biased graph
is a pair
(G,\mathbb{B})
where
G
is a graph and
\mathbb{B}
is the set of cycles in
G
that are considered balanced (provided via oracle access).
\mathbb{B}
must satisfy a closure property: for any cycle
C
in
G
that is not balanced, and any chord path
P
that connects two vertices of
C
, at least one of the two resulting new cycles in
C+P
is also unbalanced.
Both the edge- and vertex-deletion versions of the above “cleaning task” (i.e. remove at most
k
edges/vertices from
G
such that the resulting graph is balanced) are FPT, with a running time of
O^{*}(4^{k})
, even with oracle access to
\mathbb{B}

[
Wah17
,
DJO+25a
]
.
As a prominent application, let
\Gamma
be a group, and let
G
be a graph. Refer to an oriented edge in
G
as a triple
(e,u,v)
where
e\in E(G)
and
u
and
v
are the endpoints of
e
, representing orienting
e
from
u
to
v
.
Let
\gamma
be a function labelling the oriented edges of
G
by
\Gamma
, where for every edge
e=uv\in E(G)
we have
\gamma(e,u,v)=\gamma(e,v,u)^{-1}
. Let a cycle
C
be balanced if its edge labels, taken in the order and orientation of
C
, multiply to identity in
\Gamma
. Then this defines a biased graph
(G,\mathbb{B})
,
known as a group-labelled graph or
\Gamma
-gain graph
,
and the corresponding cleaning task serves as a “meta algorithm” that captures many problems
such as
Edge Bipartization/OCT
,
Multiway Cut
,
Subset Feedback Edge/Vertex Set

and more
[
Wah17
]
. The setting also provides linear-time FPT algorithms
[
IYY18
]

and approximation results
[
LW20
]
.

However, we are looking at a more general task. Let
(G,\mathbb{B})
be a biased graph and let
H
be a subgraph of
G
. For a set of vertices
S\subseteq V(G)
, let
\delta(S)
be the set of edges in
G
with one endpoint in
S
, and define the
cost of
H
to be

\cost(H)=|\delta(V(H))|+|E(G[V(H)]\setminus E(H)|

i.e. the number of edges that need to be deleted to create and “cut off”
H
from the rest of
G
. We define a
domination order
on balanced subgraphs of
G
: given two balanced subgraphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, we say that
H_{2}

dominates

H_{1}
if
V(H_{1})\subseteq V(H_{2})
and
\cost(H_{1})\geq\cost(H_{2})
, and
strictly dominates
if one of the inequalities is strict. We say that
H
is an
important balanced subgraph
of
G
if no balanced subgraph strictly dominates
H
. Note that distinct subgraphs can be
domination equivalent
, i.e. mutually dominate each other. Thus, we lift the definition: for a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
, we define
\cost(S)
as the minimum cost of a balanced subgraph
H
of
G
with
V(H)=S
, and say that
S
is an
important subset
if there is an important balanced subgraph
H
with
V(H)=S
. It is known that for every vertex
v\in V(G)
there are at most
4^{k}
important
connected
subsets (i.e.
G[S]
is connected) containing
v
of cost at most
k
, and they can be enumerated in
O^{*}(4^{k})
time
[
DJO+25a
]
.
However, if
S
(respectively
H
) is not required to be connected, then the situation is much more intangible.

Now the setting is as follows. Given a graph
G
, with an unknown balanced subgraph
H
of cost at most
k
, not necessarily connected, we wish to
cover

H
by another balanced subgraph
H^{\prime}
, such that
V(H)\subseteq V(H^{\prime})
and
H^{\prime}
(in some sense) captures the cost of
H
.
We show the following.

Theorem 2
.

There is a randomized algorithm that
takes as input a biased graph
(G,\mathbb{B})

and an integer
k
, with oracle access to
\mathbb{B}
,
and in
O^{*}(4^{k})
time outputs
a vertex set
S\subseteq V(G)
and edge set

F\subseteq E(G)
such that the following hold.

1.

\delta_{G}(S)\subseteq F
.

2.

(G-F)[S]
is balanced.

3.

For every (not necessarily connected) balanced
subgraph
H
of
G
of cost at most
k
,
with probability at least
1/2^{O(k^{2})}
we have (1)
V(H)\subseteq S
and (2) there are at most
k
edges in
F
with an endpoint in
V(H)
.

For a set
U\subseteq V
, refer to a
cleaning set
for
U
as
F_{U}\subseteq E(G)
such that
G[U]-F_{U}
is balanced. Returning to bipartite graphs, the theorem then informally says: for any bipartite subgraph
H
of
G
, with
\cost(H)\leq k
,
with probability
1/2^{O(k^{2})}
the set
F
that is returned contains an optimal cleaning set for
V(H)
.
The result can be derandomized by using
cover-free families

[
BG17
]
.
In the case when
H
is assumed to be an important balanced subgraph, we can summarize Theorem
2
in a simpler way.

Corollary 3
.

There is a randomized algorithm that
takes as input a biased graph
(G,\mathbb{B})
and an integer
k
,
and in
O^{*}(4^{k})
time outputs
a balanced subgraph
G_{B}
of
G

such that for any important balanced subgraph
H
of cost at most
k
,
with probability at least
1/2^{O(k^{2})}

every connected component of
H
is domination equivalent to a
connected component of
G_{B}
. In particular, in this case
we have
V(H)\subseteq S
and
N_{G}(V(H))\cap S=\emptyset
.

This result is a generalization of
shadow covering
(a.k.a.
shadow removal
) for edge cuts in undirected graphs, unifying the concept with the
important connected balanced subgraphs
of Dabrowski et al.
[
DJO+25a
]
. The shadow covering procedure was originally created by Marx and Razgon, under the name
random sampling of important separators
, for an FPT algorithm for
Multicut

[
MR14
]
, and has become an important part of the toolbox for FPT algorithms for graph separation problems (see e.g.
[
CHM13
,
CCHM15
,
HJL+23
]
).
The shadow covering result was subsequently improved and generalized by Chitnis et al.
[
CCHM15
]
.
Our procedure matches the success probability of
[
CCHM15
]
; the success probability for shadow covering in undirected graphs was very recently improved to
1/2^{O(k\log k)}

[
CLL+26
]
.

However, for our application the formulation in Theorem
2
is more relevant than Corollary
3
. We will use the theorem for the setting of group-labelled graphs, where the group is the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
for various
p^{d}
; see below.

Min-

and
d\geq 1
.

When

, the ring

and FPT algorithms for

-
2
-
Lin

(see e.g.
[
DJO+25a
]
).
The first interesting case for us is when

,
i.e.
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
)
, which has been an open problem
resisting attacks with the existing FPT techniques.
We concentrate on this example to illustrate some ideas
behind our algorithm.
As pointed out in the technical overview, we can
focus on special instances. Hence,
we only consider
instances of
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
)
where all binary equations are
of the form

and
u=3v\bmod 4
,
and all unary equations are crisp and of the form
s=1
.
It is worth noting that
the equation
u=3v\bmod 4
is symmetric, i.e.
3x=y\bmod 4
is equivalent to
x+y=0\bmod 4
,
which is in turn equivalent to
x=3y\bmod 4
.

Non-bipartite obstructions.

Before diving into the algorithm,
let us give some examples that hint at the connection
between
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
)
and
balanced (in fact, bipartite) subgraph covering.
We will make the link more concrete in the next subsection.
It is not hard to see that
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
)

encodes
Edge Bipartization
,
i.e. the problem asking whether
k
edges can
be removed from an undirected graph
G
to make it bipartite.
Given a graph
G
, define a system of equations
I_{G}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}

with the vertices of
G
as the variables and
equations
3u=v\bmod 4
for every edge
uv
in
G
.
Force all variables of
I_{G}
to take values in
\{1,3\}
,
e.g. by adding equations
2v=2\bmod 4
for all

in

(where
2v=2
can be implemented as

,

,

in the restricted instances we consider). The only allowed assignments to the endpoints of
an edge are
(1,3)
and
(3,1)

Thus, any assignment

of cost

can be viewed as a

(with the two colours
being

and

However,
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
)
encodes more complicated problems.
Start again with a graph

, as described above.
Pick an arbitrary subset

add equations

for all

.
Now the algorithm may “opt out” of making some parts of

and
2
on those variables.
However, this requires cutting that part of the graph
away from the bipartite region, and also deleting
all unary constraints
2u=2\bmod 4
for

that fall
outside the bipartite region.

MinCSP relaxation.

To solve
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
)
, we
leave the realm of linear equations and consider
a more general problem called
MinCSP
.
Let
A
be a set of values called the
domain
,
and
\mathbf{A}
be a set of relations over
A
.
An instance of
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A})

is a triple
(V,\mathcal{C},k)
,
where
V
is a set of variables,

\mathcal{C}
is a (multi)set of constraints,
and
k
is an integer.
Each constraint is of the form
R(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})
,
where
R\in\mathbf{A}
is a relation of arity
r
,
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
are (not necessarily distinct)
variables from
V
.
An assignment
\alpha:V\to A
satisfies
a constraint
R(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})

if
(\alpha(x_{1}),\dots,\alpha(x_{r}))\in R
,
and violates it otherwise.
The question is whether
(V,\mathcal{C})
admits
an assignment that violates at most
k
constraints.

To cast
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
)
as a
MinCSP
,
let
A_{4}=\{0,1,2,3\}
and
\mathbf{A}_{4}=\{R_{3},R_{2},U_{1}\}

where

R_{3}=\{(0,0),(1,3),(2,2),(3,1)\}
,

R_{2}=\{(0,0),(1,2),(2,0),(3,2)\}
, and

U_{1}=\{1\}
.
An equation
3u=v
is then equivalent
to a constraint
R_{3}(u,v)
,
an equation
2u=v
to

R_{2}(u,v)
, and
a unary equation
v=1

to
U_{1}(v)
.
The special case of
MinCSP
with Boolean domain,
i.e.
\{0,1\}
, is called
MinSat
.
A common approach in FPT algorithms (for

MinCSP
s and other problems)
is to encode them as
MinSat

using Boolean relations
that can be handled by the general algorithm
of Kim et al.
[
KKPW25
]
.
However, this approach does not seem directly feasible
for solving
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}_{4})
.
Instead, the same pipeline can be used to handle
a relaxation of the problem in FPT time.

Let
A^{*}_{4}=\{0,1,2,3,\overline{13}\}
,
i.e.
A_{4}
extended with a new
ambiguous
value
\overline{13}
,
which is interpreted as “1 or 3”.
The relaxation of
R_{3}
is then
R^{*}_{3}=R_{3}\cup\{(\overline{13},\overline{13})\}
, and
the relaxation of
R_{2}
is
R^{*}_{2}=R_{2}\cup\{(\overline{13},2)\}
.
We define
\mathbf{A}_{4}^{*}=\{R^{*}_{2},R^{*}_{3},U_{1}\}
.
The reduction from
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}^{*}_{4})
to
MinSat
is deferred to
Section
4.2.2
:
here we mention without proof that
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}^{*}_{4})
is in FPT.
Note that
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}_{4}^{*})
is a proper relaxation of
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}_{4})
:
on the one hand, every yes-instance of
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}_{4})

is automatically a yes-instance of
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}^{*}_{4})
.
However,
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}^{*}_{4})
accepts strictly more instances:
for example, the set of equations

s=1,\ 2s=v,\ 2u_{1}=v,\ 3u_{1}=u_{2},\ 3u_{2}=u_{3},\ 3u_{3}=v_{1}

(1)

has no zero-cost assignment in
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
because
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}

are forced to take values in
\{1,3\}
,
and the last three equations form an odd cycle.
On the other hand, the relaxation admits a zero-cost assignment
that maps
s
to
1
,
v
to
2
and

u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
to
\overline{13}
.

Intuitively, the only obstructions that
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}_{4}^{*})

“misses” are the odd cycles of
R_{3}
-constraints,
such as the triangle in the example above.
In this case, the relaxed solution is allowed to be ambiguous,
while a proper solution is not.
Our idea is to add more constraints to the relaxed instance
to handle these obstructions, which would allow

disambiguating
any relaxed solution without increasing cost.
Here, important balanced subgraphs come into play.
Construct the
unit graph

G

with the variables of
I
as vertices and
an edge
uv
for every equation
3u=v
in
I
.
Fix a hypothetical optimal assignment
\alpha:V(I)\to\mathbb{Z}_{4}

and let

V_{1,3}
be the set of vertices taking values
1
and
3

in
\alpha
.
Then, the subgraph
H
of
G[V_{1,3}]
obtained
after removing the edges corresponding to
the constraints violated by
\alpha
must be bipartite.
Since at most
k
edges are removed,

H
is a balanced subgraph of cost at most
k
in
the biased graph
(G,\mathbb{B})
, where

\mathbb{B}
is the family of even cycles.

Applying the algorithm from
Theorem
2

to
(G,\mathbb{B})
with parameter
k
,
we expect to cover
H
with sufficient probability.
In particular,
we obtain a pair
(S,F)
such that

(G-F)[S]
is bipartite, and
with sufficient probability,

V_{1,3}\subseteq S
and
|V(F)\cap V_{1,3}|\leq k
,
i.e. all variables taking values
1
and
3
are
covered by
S
and at most
k
variables taking
values
1
and
3
are endpoints
of the edges in
F
.
Now, we can modify the relaxation to take care of
all non-bipartite obstructions.
First, we remark without proof that the unary relations

U_{0,2}=\{0,2\}
,
U_{0,2,1}=\{0,2,1\}
and

U_{0,2,3}=\{0,2,3\}
can be added to
\mathbf{A}^{*}_{4}

while maintaining that
\textsc{MinCSP}(\mathbf{A}^{*}_{4})
is in FPT.
We add the following annotations to the instance:

•

For every variable
v
outside
S
,
add constraint
U_{0,2}(v)
in
k+1
copies,
forbidding
v
from taking values
1
,
3
and
\overline{13}
.

•

Then, for every variable

,
guess a value

uniformly at random
and add constraint
U_{0,2,g(v)}(v)
in
k+1

With probability
1/2^{k}
,

\alpha
is an optimal assignment to the relaxation
with the additional constraints:
indeed, no variable outside
S
takes value in
\{1,3\}
,
and only
\leq k
variables in
V(F)
take a value in
\{1,3\}
, so
only
\leq k
of our guesses need to be correct.
Thus, if we start with a yes-instance,
with sufficient probability it remains a yes-instance after
the annotations are added.

What we gain from the annotations is that every
assignment
\alpha^{*}:V(I)\to\{0,1,2,3,\overline{13}\}

can be disambiguated
into a proper assignment
\alpha:V(I)\to\{0,1,2,3\}

without increasing the cost.
To be specific,
recall that
(G-F)[S]
is bipartite
and fix a consistent labelling
\lambda:S\to\{1,3\}
.
Let
\alpha(v)=\alpha^{*}(v)
whenever

\alpha^{*}(v)\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, and let

\alpha(v)=\lambda(v)
if
\alpha^{*}(v)=\overline{13}
.
The correctness follows by observing
that the variables assigned
\overline{13}
by
\alpha^{*}

must belong to a bipartite subgraph in
(G-F)[S]
.
Indeed, every such variable must be in
S
,
and it cannot be connected to
an endpoint of an edge in
F

because the annotations and
R^{*}_{3}
-constraints
would force it to take value
1
or
3
but not
\overline{13}
.
Thus, it is safe to use
\lambda
on all
variables assigned
\overline{13}
.

Generalizing to
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}

We switch our attention to
the general problem

2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}

.
In essence, the algorithm for
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
)

already contains the main ingredients –
the relaxation (solved by a reduction to
MinSat
) and
the annotations using balanced subgraph covering.
However, adapting them, especially for the case with
d>2

requires further insights.
To generalize the relaxation,
we add an ambiguous value for every subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}

that excludes zero and
can be defined using a binary linear equation.
These turn out to be
proper cosets
,
i.e. subsets of values
\{i+r\cdot p^{j}:r\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}\}

\overline{147}
and
\overline{258}

For
\mathbb{Z}_{8}

\overline{15}
,
\overline{26}

\overline{37}

\mathbb{Z}_{9}
,

9
.
The odd cycle obstructions in
\mathbb{Z}_{4}

to non-identity cycles in
\mathbb{Z}_{9}

where the edge labels are units and
their product is not

To summarize, the high-level recipe for

to disambiguate the relaxed assignment

1.3
Hardness of FPT Approximation

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer.
Dabrowski et al.
[
DJO+24
]
showed
that if
m
is not a prime power, then
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
)
is not FPT-approximable within
2-\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
, unless the
ETH
is false. In fact, this result holds for all commutative rings
that are not

lineal
rings
[
MM16
]
and
for rings that are the direct sum of two or more non-trivial rings.
The proof is a reduction from
Paired Min Cut
; this problem
was shown to be W[1]-hard in
[
MR09
, Theorem 7]
,
and variants of it are often used in hardness reductions.
Dabrowski et al. strengthened this result by
proving that
Paired Min Cut

(in a special form equivalent to
2
-Split Min Cut
below)
is not FPT-approximable within
2-\varepsilon
for any

\varepsilon>0
unless the
ETH
is false.
We generalize their result by considering the following
problem.

p
-Split Min Cut

Instance
:

A graph
G
with special vertices
s,t
such that
G-\{s,t\}
is the disjoint union of

p
graphs
G_{1},\dotsc,G_{p}
,
a set of pairwise disjoint edge sets (a.k.a.
bundles
)
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\binom{E(G)}{1}\cup\binom{E(G)}{p}
, each set containing at most one edge from each
graph
G_{i}
,
and an integer
k
.

Parameter:

k
.

Question
:

Is there an
st
-cut
Z\subseteq E(G)
that is the union of
k
bundles in
\mathcal{B}
?

We show that
p
-Split Min Cut
is not FPT-approximable within
p-\varepsilon
under the ETH,
and use this result to improve the
2-\varepsilon
lower bound for
FPT-approximability of
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
)
to
\omega(m)-\varepsilon
. This
matches the upper bound obtained from our algorithm, and implies the
hardness part of Theorem
1
.
Thus, this is the final piece of the puzzle that completes
the picture of FPT-approximability of
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
)
.
In the reductions used by Dabrowski et al.,
the step from
Paired Min Cut
to
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
)
with
\omega(m)=2

generalizes quite naturally to a reduction
from
p
-Split Min Cut
to
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
)
with
\omega(m)=p
.
However, generalizing
(2-\varepsilon)
-inapproximability of
Paired Min Cut

to
p
-Split Min Cut
with
p>2
poses several challenges, which we discuss below.

As in
[
DJO+24
]
, we start from
the
Max-2-CSP
problem parameterized by the number of variables.
The problem can be equivalently stated as

Multicoloured Subgraph Isomorphism (MSI)
.
In MSI we are given two graphs
G
,
H

and a partition of
V(G)=\biguplus_{a\in V(H)}V_{a}(G)

into
|V(H)|
colour classes, with one class per vertex of
H
.
One should think of
G
as large and
H
as small;
the problem is parameterized by
|V(H)|
.
We are looking for a mapping from
H
to
G
,
and a colour class
V_{a}(G)
in the input
represents the set of possible images of
a
in
G
.
The goal is to find a mapping
h

such that
h(a)\in V_{a}(G)
for all
a\in V(H)

and
h
maps as many edges of
H
to edges of
G
as possible.

In a recent breakthrough
[
GLR+25
]
,
Guruswami et al. showed that, assuming ETH,
MSI cannot be approximated within any constant factor.
Roughly, for every
0<\varepsilon<1
it is hard
to find a mapping that preserves

Groups and Rings.

A

\Gamma
is a set
of elements
together with a binary operation
\circ

such that (1)
\circ
is associative, (2) there is a identity element
0
in
\Gamma
with respect to
\circ

\Gamma

such that
g\circ g^{-1}=0

We say that
\Gamma
is

when
\circ
is commutative.
A

is an Abelian group (whose operation

is called
addition
)
equipped with a second binary operation
\cdot
(called

) that is (1) associative,
(2) is distributive over the addition operation, and (3) has an identity element
1
.
We will exclusively consider
commutative rings
,
where multiplication is a commutative operator.
Let
R=(R,+,\cdot)
be such a ring where

0
denotes the additive identity element and

1
the multiplicative identity element.
In this paper we will be mostly working with finite rings
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
,
where the elements are integers
\{0,\dots,m-1\}
and the operations
are defined modulo
m
.
A
unit
in
R
is an invertible element for the multiplication of the ring, i.e. an element
d\in R
is a unit if there exists
d^{\prime}\in R
such that

d\cdot d^{\prime}=1
.
For instance, the units in
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
are
1
and
3
.
One may note that the units of
R
under multiplication form an Abelian group.

The
direct sum
of two rings

R_{1}=(D_{1};+_{1},\cdot_{1})
and
R_{2}=(D_{2};+_{2},\cdot_{2})

is denoted
R_{1}\oplus R_{2}=(R;+,\cdot)
.
Its domain

consists of the ordered pairs

\{(d_{1},d_{2})\mid d_{1}\in D_{1},d_{2}\in D_{2}\}

and the operations are defined coordinate-wise:

(d_{1},d_{2})+(d^{\prime}_{1},d^{\prime}_{2})=(d_{1}+_{1}d^{\prime}_{1},d_{2}+_{2}d^{\prime}_{2})
and

(d_{1},d_{2})\cdot(d^{\prime}_{1},d^{\prime}_{2})=(d_{1}\cdot_{1}d^{\prime}_{1},d_{2}\cdot_{2}d^{\prime}_{2})

The following is one (out of many possible) ways
of formulating the well-known Chinese Remainder Theorem.

Proposition 6
.

Let

be an integer and
p_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot p_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}

is isomorphic to the direct sum

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{n_{i}}}
, and the

defines a ring isomorphism from

to

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{n_{i}}}
.

Biased graphs are combinatorial

To introduce biased graphs,

Note that this is equivalent to the form given in the introduction:
for any unbalanced cycle

whose endpoints

and internal vertices (if any) are disjoint from

Parameterized Complexity.

In parameterized
algorithmics
[
DF99
,
FG06
,
Nie06
]

the runtime of an algorithm is studied with respect to
the input size
n
and a parameter
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
The basic idea is to find a parameter that describes the structure of
the instance such that the combinatorial explosion can be confined to
this parameter.
In this respect, the most favourable complexity class is FPT (
fixed-parameter tractable
),
which contains all problems that can be decided by an algorithm
running in
f(p)\cdot n^{O(1)}
time, where
f
is a computable
function.
Problems that can be solved within such a time bound are said to be

fixed-parameter tractable
(FPT).

A
parameterized problem
is, formally speaking, a subset of
\Sigma^{*}\times{\mathbb{N}}

where
\Sigma
is the finite input alphabet. Reductions between parameterized problems need to take
the parameter into account. To this end, we will use
parameterized reductions
(or FPT-reductions).
Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
denote parameterized problems with
L_{1}\subseteq\Sigma_{1}^{*}\times{\mathbb{N}}

and
L_{2}\subseteq\Sigma_{2}^{*}\times{\mathbb{N}}
.
A parameterized reduction from
L_{1}
to
L_{2}
is a
mapping
P:\Sigma_{1}^{*}\times{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\Sigma_{2}^{*}\times{\mathbb{N}}

such that
(1)
(x,k)\in L_{1}
if and only if
P((x,k))\in L_{2}
, (2) the mapping can be computed
by an FPT-algorithm with respect to the parameter
k
, and (3) there is a computable function
g:{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}

such that for all
(x,k)\in L_{1}
if
(x^{\prime},k^{\prime})=P((x,k))
, then
k^{\prime}\leq g(k)
.
One may, for instance, note that if
L_{1}
is in FPT
and
L_{2}
FPT-reduces to
L_{1}
, then
L_{2}
is
in FPT, too.

Parameterized Approximation.

Let
c\geq 1
be a constant.
Formally, a factor-
c
approximation
algorithm takes an instance
(I,k)
of a minimization problem
\Pi
,
accepts if
(I,k)
is a yes-instance and
rejects if
(I,c\cdot k)
is a no-instance.
The running time of an FPT-approximation
algorithm is bounded by

f(k)\cdot\lVert I\rVert^{O(1)}
where
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}

is some computable function.
We use the following simple observation
several times in the sequel. Assume
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}

to be minimization problems and
\Pi_{1}
is FPT-approximable
within factor
c
. If there is an FPT-reduction
from
\Pi_{2}
to
\Pi_{1}

that does not change the parameter, then
\Pi_{2}

is FPT-approximable within factor
c
, too.

MinSat.

A Boolean relation
R
of arity
r
is a subset of tuples in
\{0,1\}^{r}
.
A set of relations is called a
constraint language
.
Let
\mathbf{A}
be a Boolean constraint language.
An instance of a
satisfiability problem over
\mathbf{A}
,
denoted by
\textsc{Sat}(\mathbf{A})
, consists of
a set of variables
V
and a (multi)set of constraints
\mathcal{C}
, each constraint
of the form
R(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})
, where
R\in\mathbf{A}
is a relation of arity
r
and

x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
are (not necessarily distinct)
variables from
V
.
An assignment
\alpha:V\to A

satisfies
a constraint

R(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})
if
(\alpha(x_{1}),\dots,\alpha(x_{r}))\in R
,
otherwise we say that it
violates
the constraint.
The
cost
of
\alpha
in an instance
(V,\mathcal{C})
of
\textsc{CSP}(\mathbf{A})

is the number of constraints violated by
\alpha
in
\mathcal{C}
.

The optimization problem we are interested in
is known as the
Minimum-Cost Satisfiability
over
\mathbf{A}
, or
\textsc{MinSat}(\mathbf{A})
for short.

\textsc{MinSat}(\mathbf{A})

Instance
:

An instance
I=(V,\mathcal{C})
of
\textsc{CSP}(\mathbf{A})

with a subset
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
of crisp constraints,
and an integer
k
.

Parameter:

k
.

Question
:

Does
I
admit an assignment of cost at most
k
?

The constraints in
\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

are called
soft
.
As with
Min-
r
-Lin(
R
)
,
we use crisp constraints merely for convenience
as they can be modelled by
k+1
copies of
the same constraint.
The cost of an assignment that violates a crisp constraint
is defined to be infinite.

Kim et al.
[
KKPW25
]
classified the complexity
of
\textsc{MinSat}(\mathbf{A})
parameterized by
k
for every Boolean
constraint language
\mathbf{A}
.
We will use the positive part of their classification.
Every Boolean relation
R\subseteq\{0,1\}^{r}

is definable by a propositional formula on
r
variables:
formally, a tuple
(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})\in\{0,1\}^{r}

belongs to
R

if and only if

\phi_{R}(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})
is true.
A relation
R
is
bijunctive
if
it is definable by a conjunction of
1
- or
2
-clauses.
Given two literals
l_{1},l_{2}
, we write
(l_{1}\rightarrow l_{2})

(implication) as a short-hand for the 2-clause
(\neg l_{1}\vee l_{2})
,
and we write
(l_{1}\leftrightarrow l_{2})
(equivalence) as a short-hand
for the conjunction of two clauses
(l_{1}\rightarrow l_{2})\land(l_{2}\rightarrow l_{1})
.

Every bijunctive relation
R
admits a unique (up to permuting conjuncts)
complete

bijunctive formula
\phi_{R}
that defines it.
To derive
\phi_{R}
from
R
, for every pair of indices
1\leq i<j\leq r

3.1
Important Balanced Subgraphs and Vertex Subsets

To state the result we are using, we need additional terminology.

(Theorem 5 in
[

]
)

of connected important balanced subgraphs of

containing

that dominates

.

As an illustrative example, we relate this to the notion of important separators due to Marx

Indeed, the vertex set of an important balanced subgraph

This leads us to the following definitions.

An edge subset

The

Note that

For a pair of vertex sets

A vertex set

Proposition 10
.

Every important subset is the vertex set
of an important balanced subgraph.

Proof.

Let

be an important subset.
By definition, there exists a balanced subgraph

of

and of the same cost as

To see that

suppose a balanced subgraph

strictly dominates

Then its vertex set

strictly dominates

.
∎

With this terminology, we derive the following consequence of Theorem

Lemma 11

Let

be a biased graph,

be a vertex and

Let

Let

We claim that

Lemma 12

3.3
Derandomization

For the derandomization, we note that the only part of the sampling
procedure that uses randomness is Step 2 in

\textsf{RandomCover}(G,\mathbb{B},k)
(Fig.
5
)
where we sample a set
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
of important connected subsets.
Furthermore, by the proof of Theorem
2
,
for any important balanced subgraph
H
of cost at most
k
there are
two disjoint families
\mathcal{A}_{H},\mathcal{B}_{H}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
with
|\mathcal{A}_{H}|\leq k

and
|\mathcal{B}_{H}|\leq k4^{k}
such that the output
(S,F)
of
\textsf{RandomCover}(G,\mathbb{B},k)

covers
H
if and only if
\mathcal{A}_{H}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and

\mathcal{B}_{H}\cap\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

We are looking to replace the sampling
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}

by the iteration of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
over all members of a set family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{\mathcal{X}}

with the guarantee that one member of
\mathcal{F}
meets the requirements.
Thus we are, in derandomization terms, looking for an “extremely
lopsided” universal set. Fortunately, the literature does contain the
construction of such a family for our parameters, known as an

(n,(r,s))
-cover-free family
(with suitable parameters
n
,
r
,
s
).
We recall the definition from Bshouty and Gabizon
[
BG17
]
, rephrased
in an equivalent form.

Definition 13

(Bshouty and Gabizon
[
BG17
]
)
.

Let
r,s,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
r,s<n
.
An
(n,(r,s))
-cover free family
(
(n,(r,s))
-CFF) is a set

\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}

such that for every disjoint pair of sets
A,B\subseteq[n]

with
|A|=r
and
|B|=s
, there is a member
F\in\mathcal{F}

such that
F_{i}=1
for every
i\in A
and
F_{i}=0
for every
i\in B
.

Bshouty and Gabizon showed a near-optimal, near-linear time
construction of an
(n,(r,s))
-CFF for any parameters
n,s,r
,
in the following sense. There is an information-theoretic lower bound
that states that there is a function
N(r,s)
such that any

(n,(r,s))
-CFF requires size
\Omega(N(r,s)\log n)
,
and they gave an algorithm that constructs an
(n(r,s))
-CFF
of size
N(r,s)^{1+o(1)}\log n
in time
N(r,s)^{1+o(1)}n\log n
.
For the details of the function

, see
[
BG17
]
.
We focus, as above, on the setting

and
s=k4^{k}
.

Theorem 14

Let
(G,\mathbb{B})
be a biased graph and

Let

Let us now take a closer look at the shadow covering result of Marx and Razgon
[

]
.
We note some more terminology; note that the following is written for vertex cuts. Let

be a graph and
W\subseteq V(G)
a set of terminals. For

, the

is the set of vertices not reachable from any vertex of
W
in
G-S
.
A set

with
W\subseteq U
is
W
-closest

if there is no set
U^{\prime}\subset U
with
W\subseteq U^{\prime}
such that
|N(U^{\prime})|\leq|N(U)|
.
Note that this is the opposite direction from how important separators are defined;
indeed, Marx and Razgon show that if

is a

-closest set, then for every vertex

,

contains an important
(v,W
)-vertex separator
[

]
[Lemma 3.10]. In this scenario,

is the shadow of

.

Their formulation of the result that came to be known as shadow covering is then the following.

Theorem 15

(Shadow covering
[
MR14
, Theorem 3.6]
)
.

There is a randomized algorithm that, given a graph
G
, a set
W\subseteq V(G)
and an integer

, produces a set
Z\subseteq V(G)\setminus W
such that the following holds.
For every
W
-closest set
U
with
|N(U)|\leq k
with probability at least
1/2^{O(k^{3})}
the following hold.

1.

N(U)\cap Z=\emptyset
, and

2.

V(G)\setminus N[U]\subseteq Z
.

Again, let
U
be a
W
-closest set and assume that
Z
meets the conditions in the theorem.
Then
S:=N(U)
acts as a separator and
V(G)\setminus N[U]
is the shadow of
N(U)
.
The set
Z
then contains the shadow of
S
and is disjoint from
S
itself.
They also show a derandomized version, and follow-up work has generalized the notion to directed graphs and improved the success probability to
1/2^{O(k^{2})}

[
CCHM15
]
and subsequently, very recently (for undirected graphs) to
1/2^{O(k\log k)}

[
CLL+26
]
.
We focus on the above basic form here.

To connect this to important balanced subgraphs, recall the construction discussed in Section
3.1
. Let
G
be a graph and
W\subseteq V(G)
a set of terminals. Attach a sufficiently large number of unbalanced cycles to every vertex of

and define all other cycles of the graph to be balanced.
Let

be the resulting biased graph. Then a balanced subgraph
H
of

of cost at most

is any subgraph
H
(of cost at most
k
) such that
W\cap V(H)=\emptyset
.

To state the connection precisely, let us redefine
W
-closest sets to refer to edge cuts, so that a set
U\subseteq V(G)
with

is

-closest if for any set
U^{\prime}\subset U
with
W\subseteq U^{\prime}
we have
|\delta_{G}(U^{\prime})|>|\delta_{G}(U)|
.
Then it is easy to see that

is a

-closest set if and only if

is an important vertex subset for

is an important balanced subgraph (of cost

). Thus Corollary

implies Theorem

for the case of edge cuts.

We note in passing that collections of unbalanced cycles for biased graphs are closed under union; i.e. if

and
(G,\mathbb{B}_{2})
are biased graphs then so is
(G,\mathbb{B}_{1}\cap\mathbb{B}_{2})
.
Thus, the above construction can if desired be combined with other biased graph constructions, resulting in balanced subgraphs being defined as both being disjoint from

and balanced in a second biased graph sense.

However, we briefly note a difference in application of Theorem
15
and Theorem
2
. Marx and Razgon use Theorem
15
to solve
Multicut
parameterized by the solution size, by applying a greedy reduction to the shadowless case; i.e. they show, at a certain stage of their algorithm, that the solution
S
they are looking for can be assumed to have the property that
V(G)
minus
S
and the shadow of

is a
W
-closest set (for a known set of terminals

from

, assuming that

in Theorem

has been guessed correctly.
This greedy aspect has been highly successful for many other FPT graph separation algorithms
(see e.g.
[

,
CCHM15
,
HJL+23
]
),
but as has been noted, it fails to apply in certain situations, e.g. in a setting of weighted graph cuts. Indeed,
Weighted Multicut
was shown to be FPT only more recently, via an application of the

procedure
[
KPSW22
]
.
A particularly interesting case is
Directed Multiway Cut
which, along with a few related problems, is FPT in the unweighted setting, using shadow covering techniques,
but is W[1]-hard in the weighted case
[
CHM13
,
HJL+23
]
.

In our algorithm for
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}

rather than Corollary

In particular, we use no greedy “shadow bypass” step, and our algorithm could in principle be extended to the weighted case. We omit this, but see discussion in Section

.

4.2.1
Cosets and Base-
p
Representations

An
ideal
of a ring
(R,+,\cdot)
is a subset
I\subseteq R
of the elements
closed under addition and absorbing multiplication, i.e.
(I,+)
is a subgroup of
(R,+)
and
for every
r\in R
and every

x\in I
, the product
r\cdot x
is in
I
.
The
cosets
of an ideal
I
are the sets

r+I=\{r+x\mid x\in I\}
for every
r\in R
.
Note that if
r\in I
, then
r+I=I
,
and a coset
r+I
is called
proper
if
r\notin I
.
For example, in
\mathbb{Z}_{27}
the elements divisible
by
3
form an ideal, sometimes denoted by
(3)
,
and the proper cosets are
1+(3)
and
2+(3)
.
Note that the cosets of an ideal form a partition of the ring elements.

We introduce a way of describing certain cosets of
the elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
.
Every
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
admits a base-
p
representation, which is
a tuple
(a_{d-1},\dots,a_{0})

where each
a_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is called a
digit
:
formally,
a=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_{j}\cdot p^{j}
.
The
order
of
a
, denoted by
\ord(a)
, is the number
of trailing zeros in its base-
p
representation;
equivalently, it is the largest integer
i
such that

a
is divisible by
p^{i}
.
The digits
a_{d-1},\dots,a_{\ord(i)}
are called
significant
,
ordered so that more significant digits have larger subscript.
To extract
j
significant digits of
a
,
we use the notation
\mathrm{s}_{j}(a)
for
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
and
j\geq 1

which equals
(a/p^{\ord(a)})\bmod p^{j}
.

In the course of the algorithm we encounter variables
whose exact value is unknown but the constraints
determine the order and some but not all significant digits.
For example, consider an equation
3v=18\mod 27
.
It implies that
v
may take values
6
,
15
or
24
.
The base-
3
for this set of values may be described as

(*,2,0)
, meaning that the order must be
1
,
the least significant digit must be
2
, and
the next significant digit denoted by
*
may be arbitrary.
More generally, a coset
denoted by
\textsf{coset}({s},{\ell}:p^{i})
corresponds to a tuple

\underbrace{*\ \cdots\ *}_{j}\ \underbrace{s_{\ell}\ \cdots\ s_{1}}_{\ell}\ \underbrace{0\ \cdots\ 0}_{i},

where
i+\ell+j=d
,

\ell\geq 1
,
s_{1}\neq 0
and

s=(s_{\ell},\ldots,s_{1})
is called the
suffix
of the coset.
Formally,
\textsf{coset}({s},{\ell}:p^{i})=\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}:\ord(a)=i\text{ and }\mathrm{s}_{\ell}(a)=s\}
.

)

Let
(I,k)
be an instance of
\textsc{Min-{2}-Lin}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}})
.
In this section we prove Lemma
23
,
i.e. if
(I,k)
is a yes-instance,
then the instance
(I^{+},k)
of
MinSat
computed by
SolveMinLin
is a yes-instance with probability at least

.

We first describe the intuition behind the graphs
(G_{i},\mathbb{B}_{i})
.
To translate between the edges of the graphs

G_{i}
and the corresponding equations in
I
,
let us define
\textsf{eqn}_{i}:E(G_{i})\to\mathcal{C}(I)
that maps
every edge of

to the corresponding equation in
I
,
and we let
\textsf{ed}_{i}
be its inverse, i.e.

maps an equation in

to the set of corresponding edges in

in

The definitions are naturally extended to sets of edges and equations.

Suppose
\alpha:V(I)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
is an assignment and

is the set of constraints that
\alpha
violates in

.
Recall that

On a high level, each graph

is concerned with the variables in

whose values under

, and captures the constraints

To formalise this, say that

Then one can show that

is in fact a balanced subgraph of

consistent with the edge labels.
To obtain such a labelling, assign a vertex

in

to

For instance, the graph

is induced by

is a unit, and

,
and the label

Assume for a

In the latter case,

We conclude that the cost of

∎

4.3.2
Soundness

Let
(I,k)
be an instance of
\textsc{Min-{2}-Lin}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}})
.
In this section we prove Lemma
24
,
i.e. if the instance
({I}^{+},k)
of
MinSat
computed by

SolveMinLin
is
a yes-instance, then
(I,k)
is a yes-instance.

First, note that since
{I}_{\textsf{B}}
is a subinstance if
{I}^{+}
,
if
{I}^{+}
admits an unambiguous assignment of cost
k
,
then
(I,k)
is a yes-instance by Lemma
22
.
Thus, to prove soundness,
it suffices to show that one
can disambiguate any Boolean assignment to
{I}_{\textsf{B}}

without increasing its cost.
Towards this goal, we introduce some notation to extract information
from an ambiguous Boolean assignment.

Let
\beta
be a Boolean assignment to
{I}^{+}
of finite cost,
i.e. satisfying all crisp constraints.
Consider a variable
v
.
Intuitively, the clauses in
{K}_{\textsf{B}}(v)
force
\beta
to choose
a coset for
v
.
More formally, we have the following.

Proposition 28
.

Let
\beta
be a Boolean assignment satisfying
{K}_{\textsf{B}}(v)

that assigns at least one variable in
{V}_{\textsf{B}}(v)
to
1
.
Then there is a unique
\textsf{coset}({a},{\ell}:p^{i})
with maximum

\ell
such that
\beta
assigns
1
to the variable

\mathbf{x}_{v}[{a},{\ell}:p^{i}]
and all variables implied by it in
{K}_{\textsf{B}}(v)
,
and
0
to all other variables.

With this in mind,
order
of a variable
v\in V(I)
under
\beta
as

\ord_{\beta}(v)=0
if
\beta
assigns
0
to all Boolean variables in
{V}_{\textsf{B}}(\beta)
,
and

\ord_{\beta}(v)=i
if
\beta(\mathbf{x}_{v}[{a},{\ell}:p^{i}])=1
for some
i
.
Note that since
\beta
has finite cost, it satisfies
{K}_{\textsf{B}}(v)
, so
there is exactly one such
i
.
Moreover, if
v
is nonzero under
\beta
,
then
\beta
also fixes some least significant digits.
To extract them, for a nonzero
v
,
let
\mathbf{x}_{v}[{a},{\ell}:p^{i}]
be the Boolean variable in
{V}_{\textsf{B}}(v)

assigned
1
under
\beta
with maximum
\ell
,
and let
\suff(v)=a
and
\wgt(v)=\ell
.
The next observations follow from the definitions of
Boolean constraints in
{I}_{\textsf{B}}
corresponding to the equations of
I
.

Proposition 29
.

Let
\beta
be an assignment that
satisfies all crisp constraints in
{I}_{\textsf{B}}
.
Suppose
\beta
satisfies
{C}_{\textsf{B}}
for some equation
C
in
I
.

1.

If
C
is of the form
v=r\cdot u
for some unit
r
, then

\ord_{\beta}(v)=\ord_{\beta}(u)
,
\wgt_{\beta}(v)=\wgt_{\beta}(u)
and

\suff_{\beta}(v)=r\cdot\suff_{\beta}(u)\bmod p^{\wgt_{\beta}(u)}
.

2.

If
C
is of the form
v=p\cdot u
and
u
or
v
is ambiguous, then

\ord_{\beta}(v)=\ord_{\beta}(u)+1
,

\wgt_{\beta}(v)=\wgt_{\beta}(u)
and

\suff_{\beta}(v)=\suff_{\beta}(u)
.

We refer to
\wgt_{\beta}(v)
as the
weight
of
v
under
\beta
,
and say that
v
is
\ell
-ambiguous
if
it is ambiguous and has weight
\ell
under
\beta
.
We are ready to prove
Lemma
24
.

Proof of Lemma
24
.

Let
\beta
be a Boolean assignment to
{I}^{+}
that satisfies all crisp constraints.
Let
\ell
be the minimum weight of an ambiguous variable under
\beta
.
It suffices to show that we can modify
\beta
by setting
more Boolean variables to
1
while increasing
the minimum weight of ambiguous variables under
\beta

and without affecting the cost of
\beta
:
indeed, the process can be repeated until there are no ambiguous variables left.

Let
Z=\{C\in C(I):\beta\text{ does not satisfy }{C}_{\textsf{B}}\}
be
the set of equations in
I
that
\beta
violates.
We will construct a new Boolean assignment
\beta^{\prime}

that extends
\beta
(i.e. assigns
1
to a superset of Boolean variables)
so that no variable is
\ell
-ambiguous under
\beta^{\prime}
,
and then argue that
\beta^{\prime}
satisfies
{C}_{\textsf{B}}
for all
equation
C\notin Z
.
For that, we consider the graph

G^{\prime}=(G_{d-\ell-1}-F^{\prime}_{d-\ell-1}-\textsf{ed}_{d-\ell-1}(Z))[S^{\prime}],

where
S^{\prime}\subseteq S^{\prime}_{d-\ell-1}
contains
vertices
v^{i}\in S^{\prime}_{d-\ell-1}
such that

,
as defined in step
4a
.

Since
G^{\prime}
is a subgraph of

(G_{d-\ell-1}-F^{\prime}_{d-\ell-1})[S^{\prime}_{d-\ell-1}]
,
it admits a consistent labelling

\lambda:V(G^{\prime})\to\Gamma_{d-\ell-1}
.
We will use
\lambda
to obtain
\beta^{\prime}
from
\beta
.
To do this, we observe that a copy of every
\ell
-ambiguous variable occurs in
G^{\prime}
.

Claim 24.1
.

If
v
is
\ell
-ambiguous under
\beta
,
then
v^{i}\in V(G^{\prime})
where
i=\ord_{\beta}(v)
.

Proof of claim:

If
v
is
\ell
-ambiguous and has order

,
then

, so

and

occurs as a vertex in

.
Moreover, crisp constraints in step

forbid variables outside

from having order

,
so

.
Thus,
v^{i}\in S^{\prime}
and
v^{i}\in V(G^{\prime})
.

\diamond

Let
U
be a set of
\ell
-ambiguous variables whose copies
induce a connected component of
G^{\prime}
.
Pick an arbitrary variable

and suppose
v^{i}\in V(G^{\prime})
.
Note that
\suff_{\beta}(v)
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell}}
,
and hence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\ell+1}}
.
Thus,
\suff_{\beta}(v)
belongs to the group
\Gamma_{d-\ell-1}
,
and there exists

such that

\lambda(v^{i})\cdot c=\suff_{\beta}(v)\bmod p^{\ell}
.
For each

, set

where
\ord_{\beta}(u)=i
.
Note
\suff_{\beta^{\prime}}(u)=\lambda(u^{i})\cdot c

and
\ord_{\beta^{\prime}}(u)=\ord_{\beta}(u)
,
so the weight of

increases by
1
in
\beta^{\prime}
compared to
\beta
,
while the order is unchanged.
This completes the construction of
\beta^{\prime}
.
By Claim
24.1
,
no variable is
\ell
-ambiguous under
\beta^{\prime}
, and
it remains to show that
\beta^{\prime}
satisfies
{C}_{\textsf{B}}

for all
C\notin Z
.

Consider an equation
C\in C(I)\setminus Z
on variables
u
and
v
.
Clearly, if
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
agree on
u
and
v
,
then
{C}_{\textsf{B}}
is satisfied by
\beta^{\prime}
.
We claim that this case covers all equations

such that
\textsf{ed}_{d-\ell-1}(C)
intersects

.
Note that
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
disagree on
{K}_{\textsf{B}}(v)

if and only if

is
\ell
-ambiguous under
\beta
.

Claim 24.2
.

If
\textsf{ed}_{d-\ell-1}(C)
intersects

,
then
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
agree on
{C}_{\textsf{B}}
.

Proof of claim:

Let
u
and

be the variables of

, and
suppose
u^{i}v^{j}\in\textsf{ed}_{d-\ell-1}(C)\cap F^{\prime}_{d-\ell-1}
.
We claim that
u
and

are not
\ell
-ambiguous under
\beta
.
Indeed, if
u\notin S_{i}
, then
u^{i}\notin V(G^{\prime})
by Claim
24.1
.
Otherwise, we have
u\in S_{i}
and
u\in T_{i}

by the construction in step

;
then

contains crisp constraints
introduced in step

implying that

has order

, then it is unambiguous.
By analogous reasoning, neither

is
\ell
-ambiguous.

\diamond

In the remaining cases, we have
\textsf{ed}_{d-\ell-1}(C)\cap F^{\prime}_{d-\ell-1}=\emptyset
,
while
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
disagree on at least one of

and

.

Case 1:

\beta(u)\neq\beta^{\prime}(u)
and
\beta(v)\neq\beta^{\prime}(v)
,
i.e. both

and
v
are
\ell
-ambiguous under
\beta
.
Let
i=\ord_{\beta}(u)
and
j=\ord_{\beta}(v)
.
The edge
u^{i}v^{j}
is present in
G^{\prime}
since

u^{i},v^{j}\in V(G^{\prime})
by Claim
24.1
,

u^{i}v^{j}
is not in

by Claim

and
not in
\textsf{ed}_{d-\ell-1}(Z)

.
Thus,

and

which implies that

\suff_{\beta^{\prime}}(v)=\lambda(v^{i})\cdot c

We consider the two possible subcases.

1.

Then we have

.

2.

Case 2:

\beta(u)\neq\beta^{\prime}(u)

4.3.3
Derandomization

There are two randomized ingredients in the algorithm in Figure
6
:
the first is the procedure
RandomCover
being called in step
3b
;
the second is the choice of
s_{v}
for each
v\in T_{i}
uniformly at random in step
4d
.
The first one was derandomized in Theorem
14
.
For the second one, we may combine perfect hash families with exhaustive enumeration.
Perfect hash families are a standard tool in derandomizing FPT algorithms.

Definition 30
.

An
(n,\kappa)
-perfect hash is family
\mathcal{H}

of functions
h:[n]\to[\kappa]
such that,
for all subsets
S\subseteq[n]
of size
\kappa

there is a function
h\in\mathcal{H}
that is
injective on
S
, i.e.
h(i)\neq h(j)
for all distinct
i,j\in S
.

We will need the following result.

Theorem 31

(Naor et al.
[
NSS95
]
)
.

For any
n,\kappa\geq 1
, one can construct
an
(n,\kappa)
-perfect hash family of size

e^{\kappa}\kappa^{O(\log\kappa)}\cdot\log n
in time

e^{\kappa}\kappa^{O(\log\kappa)}\cdot n\log n

Now let
D
be a set.
There are
|D|^{\kappa}
functions from
[\kappa]
to
D
.
By composing an
(n,\kappa)
-perfect hash
with all functions
f:[\kappa]\to D
,
we obtain the following.

Lemma 32
.

For any
n,\kappa\geq 1
and any set
D
of size
d
,
one can construct a family
\mathcal{F}
of functions

f:[n]\to D
such that for all
subsets
S\subseteq[n]
with
|S|=\kappa

and every function
g:S\to D
,
there is a function
f\in\mathcal{F}

that agrees with
g
on
S
, i.e.
f(x)=g(x)
for all
x\in S
.
Moreover, the size of
\mathcal{F}
is

(ed)^{\kappa}\kappa^{O(\log\kappa)}\cdot\log n

and it can be constructed in time

(ed)^{\kappa}\kappa^{O(\log\kappa)}\cdot n\log n
.

Thus, to derandomize step
4d
,
we may use the lemma above to
construct a family
\mathcal{F}
with parameters

n=|T_{i}|
,
\kappa=2k(d-i-2)
and

D
being the set of units in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d-i}}
, for each iteration
i
.
We replace guessing
s_{v}
for each
v\in T_{i}

by enumerating all functions
f\in\mathcal{F}

and setting
s_{v}=f(v)
to produce the
implication constraints in step
4d
.
The domain size is bounded as
|D|<|\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d-i}}|
,
hence for each iteration Lemma
32

introduces a multiplicative overhead of at most

p^{O(kd^{2})}\log n
.

We summarize as follows. We take the opportunity to also chase down a rough bound on the running time and dependency on
p
and
d
.

Theorem 33
.

There is a deterministic FPT algorithm for
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
)

for any prime
p
and
d\geq 1
running in time
O^{*}(2^{(k+p^{d})^{O(1)}})
.

Proof.

As discussed above, we modify
\textsf{SolveMinLin}(I,k)
, given in Figure
6
,
so that Step
3
uses Theorem
14
instead of a call to
RandomCover

and Step
4d
uses Lemma
32
instead of random guessing.
In both cases, the algorithm iterates exhaustively over the respective family of objects
and accepts if and only if at least one resulting branch accepts the instance.

For correctness, we only need to verify completeness.
Let
\alpha:V(I)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
be an optimal assignment to
(I,k)
,
and let
{\alpha}_{\textsf{B}}
be the corresponding Boolean assignment.
As in the proof of Lemma
23
, for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,d-2\}

there is a balanced subgraph
H_{i}
of
G_{i}
of cost at most
2k
.
Thus, the output of Theorem
14
on
(G_{i},\mathbb{B}_{i},2k)

contains one pair
(S_{i},F_{i})
which covers
H_{i}
.
Moreover, as in Lemma
23

at most
2k(d-i-2)
variables
v
in
T_{i}
have
\ord(\alpha(v))=i
.
The implications introduced in step
4d

are satisfied by
{\alpha}_{\textsf{B}}
for all variables
v
in
T_{i}

with
\ord(\alpha(v))\neq i
because the left-hand-side
of the implications is false under
{\alpha}_{\textsf{B}}
,
and by Lemma
32
there is
f\in\mathcal{F}
such that
for all
v\in T_{i}
with
\ord(\alpha(v))=i
,

\alpha(v)=f(v)\cdot p^{i}
, so these implications
are satisfied by
{\alpha}_{\textsf{B}}
for at least one
f\in\mathcal{F}
.
Thus there is a branch where the instance is accepted.

For running time, all operations are of lower-order term except
the product of iteration over
O(d)
outputs from Theorem
14
with parameter
2k
,

O(d)
outputs from Lemma
32
,
and the call to Theorem
8
in the end.
The former two parts together introduce a total overhead of

2^{O(k^{2}d+kd^{3}\log p)}\log^{O(d)}n.

When we treat
d
as a constant,
this is already in our time budget. For taking
p^{d}
as an additional parameter,
we note the well-known fact that
\log^{t}n\leq n+t^{O(t)}
.
Finally, Theorem
8
has a deterministic form which runs in
O^{*}(2^{(k+r)^{O(1)}})
time,
where
r
is the maximum arity of a constraint in the Boolean formula
{I}^{+}
;
see Theorem 1.6 in
[
KKPW25
]
. We have
r\leq dp^{d}

5.3

We finally turn our attention to equations over
\mathbb{Z}_{m}

We consider a more general class of rings since it makes
the proof conceptually easier.
Let

, and two idempotents

The elements

Idempotents are intimately connected with various decompositions of rings.
Let

denote nontrivial rings (i.e. they contain at least two elements each) where

denote the additive and multiplicative identity, respectively, in ring

.
Assume

is isomorphic to the direct sum

and consider the set

S=\{(0_{R_{1}},\dots,0_{R_{i-1}},1_{R_{i}},0_{R_{i+1}},\dots,0_{R_{p}})\;|\;1\leq i\leq p\}

We see that

(and thus

orthogonal non-trivial idempotents.

contains a set of orthogonal non-trivial idempotents of size

and

. We exemplify with the ring

where

gives us an isomorphism

-Split Min Cut

6
Conclusion

We prove that
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
)
is FPT-approximable within factor
\omega(m)
of the optimum,
and show that FPT-approximation within
\omega(m)-\varepsilon

for any
\varepsilon>0
is ruled out under ETH.
The core part of the algorithm is an FPT algorithm for
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
)
for every prime
p
and
d\geq 1
.
For the lower bound, we define a structurally simple problem
q
-Split Min Cut
and show that it cannot be FPT-approximated within a factor of
q-\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
under ETH.

We remark that the algorithmic result is slightly stronger, and it follows that

Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
)
is in FPT parameterized by
k
,
p
and
d
.
Furthermore,
the algorithm can be extended to handle the weighted variant of the problem,
where the input comes with a function
w:C\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
assigning
positive integer weights to every equation, and a weight budget
W
,
which is
not
part of the parameter;
the goal is to find a solution
Z
such that
|Z|\leq k
and
w(Z)\leq W
.
Such variants of other FPT problems have been considered previously
[
KKPW25
,
KPSW22
]

and often require additional algorithmic insights.
In particular, the FPT algorithm for
Multicut
by Marx and Razgon
[
MR14
]
, where the shadow covering procedure was introduced,
fails to handle weights due to a greedy aspect in the “shadow removal” step.
We note that our algorithm, despite using a generalization of shadow covering, has no such greedy aspect.

The running time of our algorithm is
O^{*}(2^{k^{O(1)}})
where the exponent degree is non-trivial to analyze but we know it is
at least 4. Can this be improved? The current bottleneck is the algorithm for
MinSat
that we rely upon;
thus one improvement would be to replace this algorithm by a special-purpose FPT algorithm for our problem. Apart from this, the next bottlenecks are
2^{O(k^{2})}
from balanced subgraph covering and
2^{O(k)}
for everything else (assuming that
p
and
d
are constants).

The following is a related question: can the success probability of the balanced subgraph covering procedure be improved? The success probability of shadow covering, which is a special case, was recently improved to
1/2^{O(k\log k)}
by Chu et al.
[
CLL+26
]
, using a result of Korhonen and Lokshtanov
[
KL23
]
that important separators have small
blocking sets
, i.e. for any graph
G
and disjoint vertex sets
X
and
Y
in
G
, there is a set of size
k
that intersects all important
(X,Y)
-separators of size at most
k
.
A possible generalization to balanced important subgraphs would be the following question. Given a biased graph
(G,\mathbb{B})
and a vertex
v\in V(G)
, is there a set
Z
of
O(k)
edges such that every important connected subset
S
in
(G,\mathbb{B})
with
v\in S
has
\delta(S)\cap Z\neq\emptyset
?
This is an intriguing possibility, which could improve the success probability of balanced subgraph covering to
1/2^{O(k\log k)}
.

For a much more ambitious research direction, the problems we study are instances of the problem class
\textsc{MinCSP}(\Gamma)
for finite-domain constraint languages
\Gamma
.
It is natural to ask for which languages
\Gamma
the problem
\textsc{MinCSP}(\Gamma)
admits an FPT algorithm, or a constant-factor FPT-approximation.
While this is fully settled in the Boolean domain
[
BELM16
,
KKPW25
]
,
in the more general setting the question is very much open; only preliminary or ad-hoc cases are known (see
[
KPSW26
]
for an overview).
For constant-factor FPT-approximation, it would be particularly interesting to obtain matching upper and lower bounds for broader classes of problems than
those studied in this paper.

With the
MinCSP
motivation in mind,
perhaps the easiest extension of
Min-2-Lin
that our algorithm does not cover is the
conservative
, a.k.a.
list
case of
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
)
, where in addition every variable
v
has a list of values
D_{v}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}
that it is allowed to take. These can be treated as unary constraints, or as a separate part of the problem formulation; this makes no difference to the problem complexity.
Is
List
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{d}}
)
FPT?

Finally, our results for
p
-Split Min Cut
can be used to prove optimality of constant factors achieved by FPT-approximation algorithms, as exemplified by the case of
Min-
2
-Lin(
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
)
.
For example, they directly imply that the approximation factor of
2
in
[
DJO+23
, Theorem 10]
cannot be improved under the ETH.
Many more W[1]-hardness reductions in the literature can be rethought as reductions from
p
-Split Min Cut
for some
p\geq 2
, and it is natural to ask whether the lower bounds we obtain match the factors achieved by the best known FPT-approximation algorithms for these problems.
In this regard, one could consider e.g.

Directed Odd Cycle Transversal

[
LRSZ20
]
,

Directed Multicut with
t
Terminal Pairs
for
t\geq 4

[
PW18
]
, and

Directed Symmetric Multicut

[
ERW22
,
OPW24
]
.
