 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

An aquifer pumping test was conducted on the Lost Lake Aquifer Zone (LLAZ) at the recently
installed recovery well RWM001R in accordance with the approved test plan (Dixon, 2024). The
objective of the testing was to determine baseline well performance parameters and aquifer
hydraulic conductivity. This testing consisted of two step-drawdown tests to determine well
performance properties and two constant pumping rate aquifer tests with recovery periods to
determine aquifer hydraulic properties. Well performance parameters determined included
specific capacity, well efficiency, and head loss coefficients. The results of the step-drawdown
testing are presented in Tables ES1 and ES2 whereas the results of the constant pumping rate
testing are presented in Table ES3. The average specific capacity of RWM001R operating at pump
capacity (~54 gpm) was 2.2 gpm/ft using data from the final step of both tests. The average well
efficiency determined from both step-drawdown tests was 56% using data from the final step of
both tests. For both step-drawdown tests, well efficiency was inversely related to the pumping rate
with the average efficiency decreasing from approximately 79% to 56%. Aquifer head loss
coefficients from both tests were comparable (Table ES2).

Aquifer response to pumping at RWM001R was measured in several nearby observation wells
screened within the LLAZ during constant-rate testing. These data were used to evaluate aquifer
hydraulic properties using the Hantush-Jacob (1955, 1961a, and b) leaky aquifer model as
implemented in the computer code AQTESOLV (Table ES1). The average transmissivity (T) of
the LLAZ based on the constant rate testing was determined to be 1.75 ft2/min with a standard
deviation of 0.73 ft2/min. The average storativity of the aquifer was determined to be 0.0007 with
a standard deviation of 0.0003. The average hydraulic conductivity of the LLAZ near RWM001R
was determined to be 53.2 ft/day with a standard deviation of 21.6 ft/day. The average hydraulic
conductivity of the overlying confining layer (GCCZ), K’, was determined to be 0.0007 ft/day
with a standard deviation of 0.0003 ft/ day.

At the time the original recovery well network was installed, extensive hydraulic testing was
conducted to determine aquifer hydraulic properties. A transmissivity of 2.32 ft2/min
(K = 55.7 ft /day, b =60 ft) and storativity of 0.001 was reported for RWM 1 which was replaced

by RWM001R (Geraghty and Miller, 1987). More recently, aquifer properties were estimated
near RWM 1 as part of testing at RWM019 (Dixon, 2019). From this testing, transmissivity was
estimated for MSB003CR and MSB004CR and was found to be 1.37 and 2.06 ft2/min,
respectively. The results from the testing of RWM001R are comparable to the previous testing.

1.0 Introduction

Groundwater beneath the M-Area Hazardous Waste Management Facility is contaminated with
volatile organic compounds (VOCs) including trichloroethylene and tetrachloroethylene. Savannah
River Site (SRS) operates a network of recovery wells designed to hydraulically contain and capture
the high concentration VOC plume in the Lost Lake Aquifer Zone (LLAZ) (Figure 1). The recovery
wells are connected to the M-1 Air Stripper and the system is permitted by the South Carolina
Department of Environmental Services (SCDES) to operate at a total flow of 610 gpm.

Although modified over time, the original recovery well network was installed in the 1980s. Extensive
aquifer testing was conducted using the original well network and estimates of specific capacity, well
efficiency, transmissivity, and storage coefficient were made for the LLAZ (Geraghty and Miller,
1987). Results from previous testing are summarized in Table 1. Transmissivities for the LLAZ
ranged from 0.344 to 9.28 ft2/min (using constant rate drawdown tests). More recently, aquifer testing
was conducted at RWM 3, RWM 5, RWM-6, RWM-7, RWM 8, RWM018, and RWM019. Near
RWM 3 and RWM 5, LLAZ transmissivity was estimated be 0.992 ft2/min. For the LLAZ near RWM
8, transmissivity ranged from 0.715 to 1.167 ft2/min with an average of 0.946 ft2/min (Dixon, 2019).
For the LLAZ near RWM018, transmissivity ranged from 0.660 to 1.086 ft2/min with an average of
0.816 ft2/min (Dixon, 2018). For the LLAZ near RWM019, transmissivity ranged from 0.550 to 3.298
ft2/min with an average of 1.695 ft2/min (Dixon, 2021). For the LLAZ near RWM-6, transmissivity
ranged from 0.4547 to 2.255 ft2/min with an average of 1.244 ft2/min (Dixon, 2023). For the LLAZ
near RWM-7, transmissivity ranged from 1.393 to 3.002 ft2/min with an average of 2.011 ft2/min
(Dixon, 2023).

The objectives of this project were to conduct performance testing on recovery well RWM001R and
to perform aquifer testing to determine hydraulic properties of the LLAZ near RWM001R. After
several years of declining performance, RWM-1 was replaced by RWM001R. Hydrologic tests
conducted on RWM001R included step-drawdown and constant rate pumping tests.

This report discusses the hydrologic tests conducted at RWM001R. The information provided in this
report may serve as input to subsequent updates to the groundwater flow and contaminant transport
model for A/M Area.

2.0 Hydrologic Test Methods and Objectives

The objectives of this testing were to determine the specific capacity, well efficiency, and pumping
capacity of RWM001R and to estimate aquifer hydraulic properties including transmissivity and
storativity for the LLAZ near RWM001R. These objectives were met by conducting step-drawdown
testing and constant rate pumping testing at RWM001R. All testing was conducted with the other
recovery wells in the network operating at near steady-state conditions. This was done so that any
observed aquifer response could be attributed to testing at RWM001R. The following sections
describe the test methods used to meet the project objectives.

2.1 Review of Previous Aquifer Testing Near RWM001R

At the time of installation (circa 1984) of the recovery well network, several step-drawdown and
aquifer pumping tests were conducted to estimate the performance properties of the recovery wells
and the hydraulic properties of the LLAZ. The results of this work are presented by Geraghty and
Miller (1987) and are summarized in Table 1. A transmissivity of 2.32 ft2/min (K = 55.7 ft /day,
b =60 ft) and storativity of 0.001 was reported for RWM 1 (Geraghty and Miller, 1987). More recently,
aquifer properties were estimated near RWM 1 as part of testing at RWM019 (Dixon, 2019). From
this testing, transmissivity was estimated for MSB003CR and MSB004CR and was found to be 1.37
and 2.06 ft2/min, respectively.

2.2 Hydrogeologic Conceptual Model

The location of RWM001R, and nearby monitoring wells is shown in Figure 2. Generalized lithologic
cross-sections are shown in Figure 3. Figure 4 shows well screens for nearby observation wells. A
detailed description of the hydrostratigraphic setting in A/M area is provided by (Aadland and Bledsoe,
1990) and details pertinent to this test are summarized here. The generalized hydrostratigraphy
pertinent to the study area consists of 1) the M-Area Aquifer Zone (MAAZ), 2) the Green Clay
Confining Zone (GCCZ), 3) the LLAZ, and 4) the upper clay of the Crouch Branch Confining Unit
(UC_CBCU).

The MAAZ is the water table aquifer, and it overlies the GCCZ. The GCCZ ranges in thickness from
about 4 to 12 ft across the study area, with a thickness of 7 ft at RWM001R. The GCCZ serves as the
leaky confining layer in the subsequent analysis of the RWM001R pump test data. The LLAZ ranges
in thickness from about 45 to 70 ft across the study area with a thickness of 51 ft at RWM001R. The
 13

LLAZ is bounded on the bottom by the UC_CBCU. RWM001R is screened in both the MAAZ and
LLAZ (Figure 4) and water is likely withdrawn from both aquifers during pumping.

2.3 RWM001R Step-Drawdown Test

Two step-drawdown tests were conducted at RWM001R to assess well performance and to identify
the optimum pumping rate for RWM001R. The step-drawdown tests were conducted as a series of
sequential short duration constant rate pumping tests with each step conducted for a uniform duration
at a progressively increasing pumping rate. The first step-drawdown test was conducted with 5
step-discharge rates of 18.9, 27.7, 38.8, 48.7, and 56.3 gallons per minute (i.e. ~10 gpm step
increments increasing to pump maximum). Each individual step increment was conducted for 1 hour.
Recovery was monitored following the final pumping step. The second step-drawdown test followed
the first constant rate pumping test and was conducted with 3 step-discharge rates of 18, 36, and
54 gpm. Each individual step increment was conducted for 2 hours. As with the first test, recovery
was monitored following the final pumping step. The water level in RWM001R was monitored using
a vented data logging pressure transducer during both step-drawdown tests. Flow rates were also
collected with a data logger.

Data collected from both step-drawdown tests were analyzed to determine well loss parameters. The
specific capacity of a pumping well is defined as discharge per unit drawdown (Q/s) as measured in
the pumping well (Kruseman and Ritter, 1994). It provides an indicator of initial well performance
and is useful in quantifying subsequent declines in performance over time that may arise as pumping
progresses. The specific capacity of RWM001R was assessed by plotting drawdown as a function of
discharge for each pumping interval.

Head loss coefficients for RWM001R were determined by comparing discharge, Q, to the ratio of
drawdown and pumping rate (s/Q). The ratio s/Q is defined as specific discharge. Jacob (1947)
defined the relationship between well loss and drawdown as follows:

2.4 Constant Rate Pumping Tests

In addition to step-drawdown testing, two constant rate pumping tests were conducted at RWM001R.
During the test, the system configuration (recovery wells in use and pumping rates) was maintained
as close to constant as possible so that the measured aquifer response could be attributed to the test
well.

An extensive monitoring well network exists near RWM001R with several of those wells screened in
the LLAZ. A subset of these wells was used to monitor aquifer response due to pumping at the test
well (Figure 2 and Table 2). Figure 4 shows a plot of screen intervals for the observation wells chosen
to monitor aquifer response to testing.

For all testing, vented data logging pressure transducers were used to monitor aquifer response.
Pressure transducers were submerged below the water column in the wells and recorded the pressure

 15

due to the weight of the water column above the transducer. Changes in water level result in a change
in pressure sensed by the transducer. The pressure measured by the transducer was recorded in feet
of water above the sensor. These data were converted to drawdown by calculating the difference
between the height of water above the sensor at static conditions and the height of water above the
sensor during pumping. Barometric pressure was monitored continuously at the test site using a data
logging barometer (In-Situ, Inc., Barotroll).

2.5 Analysis of Pumping Test Data

RWM001R is partially screened in the water table aquifer as shown in (Figure 4). Therefore, it is
likely that water was withdrawn from both the MAAZ and the LLAZ during the pumping test. Thus,
the total flow from RWM001R is the sum of the flow from the MAAZ and the LLAZ. If the LLAZ
pumping test data were analyzed using the total flow from RWM001R, aquifer properties such as
transmissivity and storativity would be overestimated. The configuration for the RWM001R testing
did not facilitate distinguishing the proportion of flow coming from each aquifer. Therefore, an
indirect method was developed to apportion flow between the two aquifer systems using modeled
transmissivities for the MAAZ (SRNS, 2017) and measured transmissivities for the LLAZ near
RWM-1 (Dixon, 2021). This method is described in more detail in Section 3.3.

The LLAZ is considered a leaky confined aquifer being bounded by the GCCZ at the top and the
UC_CBCU on the bottom. The GCCZ in M-Area has been described as discontinuous (Marine and
Bledsoe, 1984) and identified as a leaky confining layer (Dixon, 2018; Dixon, 2019; Dixon, 2021;
Dixon, 2023; Hiergesell, 1992). Therefore, the method chosen for analyzing the bulk of data from the
aquifer pumping tests considers leakage from an overlying confining layer. Initial estimates of aquifer
properties were made using the Theis solution for confined aquifers (Theis, 1935).

The Hantush-Jacob method was implemented using the computer code AQTESOLV (Duffield, 2007).
Parameters used in the Hantush-Jacob model for leaky aquifers include the saturated thickness of the
aquifer, the thickness of the overlying confining layer, and the zone of penetration of the pumping and
observation wells. The hydrogeologic conceptual model described in Section 2.2 was used to establish
the layer thicknesses used in AQTESOLV.

Derivative analysis was used to aid in interpretation of the pumping test data. Derivative analysis is
useful for identifying flow regimes, wellbore storage effects, and selecting appropriate aquifer models.
AQTESOLV was used to conduct the derivative analysis of the drawdown data. Derivative plots were
created by plotting the derivative of the drawdown type curve as a function of time on a log axis.
These plots were compared to standard plots in the AQTESOLV library to identify flow regime and
aquifer type.

2.6 Barometric Effects

Fluctuations in barometric pressure can impact water level measurements in a confined aquifer even
when vented pressure transducers are used because the well serves as a direct connection to the
atmosphere for the aquifer. Any change in atmospheric pressure is transmitted to the aquifer through
the opening provided by the well screen. For wells near the pumping well, barometric effects may be
minimal in comparison to the head change induced by pumping. However, for wells further away
where the head change in the aquifer is smaller, barometric effects can be significant. Data collected
during the constant rate testing at RWM001R were corrected for barometric effects using one of two
methods.

pump test.

For more distant wells where the observed response was on the same order of magnitude as barometric
pressure fluctuations, a software package developed by the Kansas Geological Survey (KGS) was used
to deconvolute the water level data. Based on pretest observation data, the KGS software was used to
compute a barometric response function (BRF) which is unique for each well. The BRF was then used
to correct water level measurements thereby removing the barometric response.

3.0 Results

Both well performance and aquifer testing were conducted at RWM001R. The test methods employed
are described in Section 2.0. Pretest monitoring began at most observation wells in October 2024.
The first of two step-drawdown tests was conducted on March 26, 2025, and was completed the same
day. Constant pumping rate aquifer testing began on March 27, 2025, and continued for 13 days.
Unexplained perturbations in water level responses were noticed in several observation wells during
the test period. Therefore, the test was ended, and the aquifer was allowed to recover. Following a
recovery period, a second step-drawdown test was conducted on April 15, 2025, and was completed
the same day. The second constant pumping rate aquifer test began on April 16, 2025, and continued
for 12 days followed by a 3-day recovery monitoring period. All testing was conducted with the
recovery network operating at near steady-state conditions to minimize extraneous effects from nearby
extraction wells and to ensure that any observed aquifer response could be attributed to testing at
RWM001R. The following sections provide a discussion and analysis of the results obtained from the
hydrologic testing.

3.1 Barometric Efficiency

Prior to aquifer testing, water level measurements were recorded for several weeks to evaluate the
effects of barometric pressure. Barometric efficiencies were calculated for each observation well
using the methods described in Section 2.6 and are presented in Table 3. Values ranged from 56 to
83% with a median of 68% (average 68%).

The calculated efficiencies (Table 3) were used to correct water level data for near-filed wells prior to
analysis using the methods outlined in Section 2.6. Figure 6 shows a subset of the hydrologic data
collected for well MSB15A during the aquifer testing at RWM001R. The effects of barometric
pressure changes are evident as uncorrected water levels trend inversely with barometric pressure.
This plot also shows the effectiveness of the corrections made to the data as the corrected water levels
show minimal correlation to barometric pressure.

For more distant wells, where the water level responses were on the same order of magnitude as
barometric pressure fluctuations, data were corrected using software provide by KGS as described in
Section 2.6. Figure 7 shows a plot of data collected at well MSB99C, which is located 1232 ft from
RWM001R. The calculated barometric response function (BRF) was effective at removing the
temporal barometric pressure fluctuations from the water level response data. Figure 7 clearly shows
that analysis of the data would be impossible without removing barometric effects.

3.2 RWM001R Step Drawdown Testing

Two step-drawdown test was conducted on RWM001R with the first being conducted on March 26,
2025, and the second conducted on April 15, 2025. The goal of these tests was to determine well
performance characteristics. Due to the short duration of both step-drawdown tests and the magnitude
of drawdown observed in the pumping well, it was unnecessary to make corrections for barometric
effects.

3.2.1 First Step Drawdown Test

The first step-drawdown test consisted of 5 steps with discharge rates of 18.9, 27.7, 38.8, 48.7, and
56.3 gallons per minute (Figure 8). Each individual step increment was conducted for 1 hour.
Recovery was monitored following the final pumping step. Data collected from RWM001R during

 21

the first step-drawdown test are presented in Figure 8. The total drawdown observed in RWM001R
was 26.5 ft at a flow rate of 56.3 gpm. The specific capacity of RWM001R was calculated at the end
of each pumping interval (Table 4). Based on the results of the first step-drawdown test, the specific
capacity of RWM001R was determined to be 2.1 gpm/ft (estimated from final step). Specific
discharge (inverse of specific capacity) was determined for each pumping period and plotted as a
function of pumping rate (Figure 9). The slope and intercept of this plot were used to estimate the
Jacob (1947) head loss coefficients, B and C. The aquifer head loss coefficient (B) was determined to
be 1.8058 ft/ft3/min, and the well loss coefficient (C) was determined to be 0.1922 min2/ft5 (Table 5).

Well efficiency was calculated for each pumping period and plotted as a function of pumping rate
(Figure 10). The efficiency of RWM001R at the end of the final pumping period was estimated to be
56%. The head loss coefficients were used to predict drawdown at the end of each pumping period
for the step-drawdown test (Figure 11). Good agreement is noted between the predicted and observed
drawdown. The head loss coefficients, B and C, were also used to calculate drawdown as function of
pumping rate (Figure 11).

3.2.2 Second Step Drawdown Test

The second step-drawdown test followed the first constant rate pumping test and consisted of 3 steps
with discharge rates of 18, 36, and 54-gpm. Each individual step increment was conducted for 2 hours.
Recovery was monitored following the final pumping step. Data collected from RWM001R during
the second step-drawdown test are presented in Figure 12.

The total drawdown observed in RWM001R was 23.0 ft at a flow rate of 54 gpm. The specific capacity
of RWM001R was calculated at the end of each pumping interval (Table 4). Based on the results of
the second step-drawdown test, the specific capacity of RWM001R was determined to be 2.4 gpm/ft
(estimated from final step). Specific discharge (inverse of specific capacity) was determined for each
pumping period and plotted as a function of pumping rate (Figure 13). The slope and intercept of this
plot were used to estimate the Jacob (1947) head loss coefficients, B and C. The aquifer head loss
coefficient (B) was determined to be 1.7435 ft/ft3/min, and the well loss coefficient (C) was determined
to be 0.1802 min2/ft5 (Table 5).

Well efficiency was calculated for each pumping period and plotted as a function of pumping rate
(Figure 14). The efficiency of RWM001R at the end of the final pumping period was estimated to be

57%. The head loss coefficients were used to predict drawdown at the end of each pumping period
for the step-drawdown test (Figure 15). Good agreement is noted between the predicted and observed
drawdown.

3.2.3 Summary of Step Drawdown Testing

The results from both step-drawdown tests were comparable and indicate that a higher maximum
pumping rate could be achieved from RWM001R with a larger pump and deeper placement. Figure
16 and Figure 17 provide plots of predicted drawdown in RWM001R as a function of pumping rate
along with the screened interval and pump placement. Under the maximum operating conditions of
the first step-drawdown test (~56 gpm), the water level in RWM001R is estimated to be about 13.7 ft
above the pump. Likewise, for the second step-drawdown test, at 54 gpm the water level in
RWM001R is estimated to be about 14.9 ft above the pump. Figure 18 shows a schematic of the
hydrostratigraphy at RWM001R along with the predicted head loss curve. Based on this plot,
RWM001R can be operated at the maximum capacity of the current pump configuration (~55gpm).

3.3 RWM001R Constant Rate Pumping Tests

Following the first step-drawdown test, the aquifer was allowed to recover to near pre-test conditions.
The first RWM001R constant rate pump test commenced on March 27, 2025, at 08:15 AM and
continued through April 9, 2025, at 09:10 AM for a period of 13 days. Post-test monitoring of recovery
in the observation wells continued until April 15, 2025. The average and median flow rates were
55.4 gpm for the 13-day test period. The flow from RWM001R was nearly constant during the
pumping period (Figure 19). Water levels were monitored in the observation wells identified in Table
2 except for MSB002CR and MSB004CR. Anomalous perturbations in water levels were noted in
several observation wells during the first constant rate pumping test. The drawdown plot for MSB15A
clearly shows the observed fluctuations in water levels (Figure 20). The source of these fluctuations
could not be determined, and the test was discontinued. Data from the first constant rate pumping test
were not analyzed for aquifer properties due to the noted fluctuations. The maximum drawdown
observed in RWM001R during the testing was 29.3 ft at a flow rate of 55.4 gpm. This yields a specific
capacity of 1.9 gpm/ft.

The second constant-rate pumping period began on April 16, 2025, at 08:05 AM and continued
through April 28, 2025, at 01:00 PM for a period of 12 days. Post-test monitoring of recovery in the

 23

observation wells continued until May 1, 2025. The average and median flow rates were 54 gpm for
the 12-day test period. The maximum drawdown observed in RWM001R during the testing was
27.6 ft at a flow rate of 54 gpm. This yields a specific capacity of 2.0 gpm/ft. The flow from
RWM001R was nearly constant during the pumping period (Figure 21). Water levels were monitored
in the observation wells identified in Table 2 except for MSB105C and MSB39B. Transducers in
these wells were relocated to MSB002CR and MSB004CR based on water level responses observed
during the first constant-rate pumping test.

Drawdown data suitable for analysis was obtained from wells MSB15A, MSB003CR, MSB004CR,
MSB99A, and MSB26A. A response to testing was noted in MSB002CR but a transducer malfunction
prevented data analysis. Drawdown data for the wells subjected to analysis are presented in Figure 22
through Figure 26. Maximum corrected drawdown is presented Table 6.

As discussed in Section 2.5, RWM001R is screened in the MAAZ and in the LLAZ. During pumping
of RWM001R, water is likely withdrawn from both aquifers. Drawdown was measured in MAAZ
well MSB11D during the constant rate pumping test using a water level tape. These data were used
along with the median MAAZ transmissivity value (95 ft/day) from the groundwater flow model
(SRNS, 2017) to estimate the extraction rate of water from the MAAZ. Figure 27 shows the fit to the
data using the Neuman solution for pumping an unconfined aquifer (Neuman, 1974). For the analysis,
transmissivity was fixed, and the pumping rate was varied to obtain the best fit to the data. From this
analysis, it was determined that an equal proportion of flow for the MAAZ and LLAZ aquifers for
RWM001R was appropriate. Therefore, for the LLAZ analyses, flow was reduced to 50% of the total
flow from RWM001R. RWM001R was pumped at approximately 54 gpm during both constant rate
pumping tests. Thus, the flow rate used to analyze aquifer response in the LLAZ was set to 27 gpm.

It is important to note that the ratio of flow between the two aquifers is not fixed and is a function of
time and continued pumping. With continued pumping, the saturated thickness of the MAAZ will
decrease, and it will contribute less to the total flow as the aquifer sediments dewater due to gravity
drainage. Therefore, over time, the ratio will change to account for increased flow from the LLAZ.
The drawdown response measured in MSB11D (Figure 27) shows the effects of gravity drainage
(specific yield) indicating that the dewatering sediments of the MAAZ are likley contributing to the

 24

flow measured at RWM001R. Therefore, the assumed 1:1 ratio is acceptable for interpreting the short-
term pumping test data.

Drawdown data were analyzed to estimate the hydraulic properties of the LLAZ near RWM001R
using AQTESOLV (Duffield, 2007). The hydrogeologic conceptual model presented in Section 2.2
was used to establish boundaries and dimensions for the analysis (Table 7). The Hantush-Jacob (1955,
1961a and b) leaky confined model was chosen for the analysis as discussed in Section 2.5. An aquifer
thickness of 51 ft and an aquitard thickness of 7 ft was used in all analyses. Data collected during the
RWM001R constant rate pump test were filtered using AQTESOLV to improve computational
efficiency and, to improve the quality of fit to the observed data. Pumping rates for RWM001R were
established based on ACP round sheets.

Drawdown data from each well were analyzed independently to determine aquifer hydraulic properties
near RWM001R. Estimates of transmissivity, storativity, and leakage were obtained from the analysis.
Hydraulic conductivity of both the LLAZ and the overlying confining layer (GCCZ) was calculated
from the AQTESOLV output. The results of the analyses are presented in Table 8 and Figure 28
through Figure 32.

Transmissivity values ranged from 1.21 to 2.95 ft2/min with an average value of 1.75 ft2/min (ı = 0.73
ft2/min). Hydraulic conductivity ranged from 37.0 to 83.4 ft/day with an average value of 53.2 ft/day,
which is comparable to a clean sand (Freeze and Cherry, 1979). Storativity values ranged from 0.0004
to 0.0013 with an average value of 0.0007 (ı = 0.0003). Leakage values (r/B) ranged from 0.0688 to
0.6261 with an average value of 0.2145 (ı = 0.2324). The hydraulic conductivity of the overlying
confining layer (GCCZ), K’, ranged from 0.0003 to 0.0009 ft/day with an average value of
0.0007 ft/day, which is indicative of silt/clay (Freeze and Cherry, 1979).

4.0 Conclusions

An aquifer pumping test was conducted on the LLAZ at the recently installed recovery well
RWM001R in accordance with the approved test plan (Dixon, 2024). The objective of the testing was
to determine baseline well performance parameters and aquifer hydraulic conductivity. This testing
consisted of step-drawdown testing to determine well performance properties, constant pumping rate
aquifer testing to determine aquifer hydraulic properties, and post-test aquifer recovery monitoring
also used to estimate aquifer hydraulic properties. Well performance parameters were calculated
 25

based on two step-drawdown tests and included specific capacity, well efficiency, and head loss
coefficients. The results from both step-drawdown tests were similar. The specific capacity of
RWM001R was determined to be approximately 2.1 gpm/ft of drawdown based on the final step of
the first step-drawdown test and 2.4 gpm/ft of drawdown based on the final step of the second step-
drawdown test. Well efficiency was inversely related to pumping rate and decreased from 79% to
56% over a pumping range of approximately 18.9 to 56.3 gpm for the first step-drawdown test.
Likewise, for the second step-drawdown test, well efficiency decreased from 80% to 57% over a
pumping range of approximately 18.0 to 54.0 gpm. The aquifer head loss coefficient was determined
to be 1.8 ft/ft3/min, and the well loss coefficient was determined to be 0.19 min2/ft5 based on the first
step-drawdown test. For the second step-drawdown test, the aquifer head loss coefficient was
determined to be 1.7 ft/ft3/min, and the well loss coefficient was determined to be 0.18 min2/ft5 based
on the first step-drawdown test. The results of the step-drawdown testing indicate that RWM001R
can be operated at the maximum capacity of the current pump configuration (~55gpm).

Aquifer response to pumping at RWM001R was measured in several nearby observation wells
screened within the LLAZ over the course of two constant rate pumping periods. Unexplained
perturbations in aquifer pressure were observed during the first constant rate pumping period and data
from this period were not analyzed for aquifer properties. Data from the second constant rate pumping
period and subsequent recovery period were used to evaluate aquifer hydraulic properties using the
Hantush-Jacob (1955, 1961a, and b) leaky aquifer model as implemented in the computer code
AQTESOLV (Table ES1). Because RWM001R is screened in both the MAAZ and LLAZ, flow was
apportioned between the two aquifers based on previously reported hydraulic properties (SRNS 2017
and Dixon, 2021) and the measured aquifer response in the MAAZ.

The average transmissivity (T) of the LLAZ based on the constant rate testing was determined to be
1.75 ft2/min with a standard deviation of 0.73 ft2/min. The average storativity of the aquifer was
determined to be 0.0007 with a standard deviation of 0.0003. The average hydraulic conductivity of
the LLAZ near RWM001R was determined to be 53.2 ft/day with a standard deviation of 21.6 ft/day.
The average hydraulic conductivity of the overlying confining layer (GCCZ), K’, was determined to
be 0.0007 ft/day with a standard deviation of 0.0003 ft/ day.
