A comparison of the spin-phonon behaviour of Fe 2 ​ P \text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P} -based magnetocaloric materials
Mikael S. Andersson, Simon R. Larsen, Erna K. Delczeg-Czirjak, Antonio Corona, Jacques Ollivier, Wiebke Lohstroh, Helen Y. Playford, Cheng Li, Pascale P. Deen, Johan Cedervall

Abstract
Magnetic refrigeration can provide an environmentally friendly technology to reduce significantly the energy consumption of cooling devices without using harmful gases. To retain the sustainability of the device, all parts must be made from abundant materials, excluding e.g. rare earth elements. As such, materials based on Fe 2 ​ P \text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P} have shown great potential for magnetocaloric devices, particularly enhanced by the pliant tunability arising through chemical substitutions. In this study, two magnetocaloric compounds, Fe 2 ​ P \text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P} and FeMnP 0.55 ​ Si 0.45 \text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}} , have been studied using magnetometry, neutron diffraction, theoretical modelling and inelastic neutron scattering with the aim to understand the ferromagnetic transition, closely related to the magnetocaloric effect. Analysis of the diffraction data of Fe 2 ​ P \text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P} showed that it is the Fe 3g -site that drives the magnetic transition as the Fe 3f does not have any magnetic contribution at the magnetic transition temperature. For FeMnP 0.55 ​ Si 0.45 \text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}} , the magnetic transition is more gradual with coexistence of the para- and ferromagnetic phases close to the magnetic transition. In the ferromagnetic phase of FeMnP 0.55 ​ Si 0.45 \text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}} , magnetic moments are observed for both sites. The temperature dependent magnetic structure behaviour found from neutron diffraction are well in agreement with our first principles calculations. Both Fe 2 ​ P \text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P} and FeMnP 0.55 ​ Si 0.45 \text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}} showed two distinct regions, at different length scales, in their S( Q , ω \omega ) spectra. The two length scales can be modelled using a different set of magnetic spin states (S), using S Fe = 2 and S Mn = 2.5, consistent with the ground state of the magnetic atoms in ( Fe , Mn ) 2 ​ ( P , Si ) \text{(}\text{Fe}{,}\text{Mn}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{(}\text{P}{,}\text{Si}\text{)} -compounds. QENS at low Q (Q < 0.5 Å) shows similar magnetic processes in both compounds with uncorrelated magnetism below the magnetic transition temperature. The occurrence of the uncorrelated state in both Fe 2 ​ P \text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P} and FeMnP 0.55 ​ Si 0.45 \text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}} highlights that the magnetic anisotropy does not play a major role in the formation of this state. Furthermore, this emphasises the existence of a two part system in FeMn ( P , Si ) \text{FeMn}\text{(}\text{P}{,}\text{Si}\text{)} -based compounds, that drives the magnetic transition and in turn the magnetocaloric effect. This means that short range magnetic clusters exist above and below the magnetic ordering temperature of the compound, strongly influencing the magnetic behaviour.

Magnetic refrigeration has been envisaged as an effective cooling phenomenon since the discovery of the giant magnetocaloric effect in
\text{Gd}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{5}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{5}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}

A magnetic phase transition will induce a large entropic change and this is particularly pronounced for a paramagnetic to ferromagnetic transition at T
C
. The entropy (S) of a material is strongly affected by changes in internal degrees of freedom that include phonon modes, spin waves and phonon-electron interactions. As such, understanding the interplay between spin, electron, and lattice degrees of freedom is essential to optimise magnetocaloric devices.

Intrinsic spin-phonon coupling has previously been studied for magnetocaloric materials
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{5}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{5}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

\text{MnFe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P}
and
\text{LaFe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{13-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{13-x}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}
using inelastic neutron scattering (INS)

;
Biniskos et al. 2018
;
Cedervall
et al. 2019a
;

\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P}
revealed that in addition to long range magnetic correlations, and concomitant spin waves, a weakly antiferromagnetic correlated nanoscale clustered state exists below T
C

C

Cedervall
et al. 2019a

In this study, we aim to explore the magnetic transition in materials used in magnetic refrigeration to gain insight into the magnetocaloric behaviour. This is performed by comparing the properties of
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P}
and
\text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}}
using mainly neutron diffraction and inelastic neutron scattering. Neutron scattering possess a unique probe that enables the exploration of the spatial and energy scales of the magnetic spin correlations and the magnon-phonon properties close to the magnetic transitions. We study the effect of substitution on the ferromagnetic transition and relevant magnetic exchange interactions that drives the magnetocaloric effect.

II
Methods

II.1
Synthesis and sample quality

Powder samples were produced using the drop synthesis method, where the volatile elements (P, Mn) are dropped into a melt of the non-volatile elements (Fe, Si)
Carlsson et al. 1973

>

II.2
Magnetometry

Magnetic characterisation of the samples was carried out using a Quantum Design MPMS SQUID magnetometer and a Quantum Design MPMS3 SQUID magnetometer. The temperature dependence of the magnetisation has been probed using an applied magnetic field of H = 4 kA/m (
\mu_{0}H
= 5 mT). The field dependence was studied in the field range of

IV
Conclusions

In this study, we have investigated the magnetic phase transitions in the compounds
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P}
and
\text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}}
by using magnetometry, neutron diffraction, theoretical modelling and inelastic neutron scattering. Close to T
C
in
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P}
, a canted magnetic structure with magnetic moments solely on the Fe
3g
-site is observed using neutron diffraction. This clearly shows that it is the Fe
3g
-site that drives the magnetisation in
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P}
, in agreement with previous theoretical calculations. In contrast,
\text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}}
show a more gradual magnetic transition with magnetic moments on both sites. However, QENS data at low Q highlights the similar behaviour in the magnetic process in
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P}
and
\text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}}
with the existence of uncorrelated magnetism in both compounds below T
C
, independent of the difference in anisotropy in the compounds. This shows that anisotropy is not the most important parameter for the magnetisation process in
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P}
-based materials.

The inelastic neutron scattering show temperature dependent features with two distinct length scales for both compounds. In the long range regime, the magnetic structure of both
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{P}
and
\text{FeMnP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.55}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.55}}\text{}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.45}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.45}}\text{}}
revealed complex magnetic interactions. To fully simulate the S(
Q
,
\omega
) spectra, magnetic exchange interaction parameters up to the sixth nearest neighbour (J1 to J6) needs to be considered. The
\mathbf{J_{i,j}}
’s used in the linear spin wave simulations were directly incorporated from first principles calculations, giving good agreement with the experimental S(
Q
,
\omega
) spectra only needing to evaluate the spin states (S
Fe
and S
Mn
).

The low Q scattering could only be explained using a subset of the exchange parameters (J1 and J2), highlighting the existence of a two part system. It is the two part system that drives the magnetic transition and in turn also the magnetocaloric effect in
\text{FeMn}\text{(}\text{P}{,}\text{Si}\text{)}
