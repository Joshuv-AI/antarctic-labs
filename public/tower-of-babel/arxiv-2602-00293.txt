Abstract




1.
Main Result and Discussion

1.1.
Statement of Main Results

In dynamical systems, given a point
x
, we often look for descriptions of long-term behavior of the forward orbit
\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
.
In ergodic theory we use the notion of empirical measure, which is the sequence of measures defined by
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_{x_{i}}
.
Given a measure
\mu
, its basin of attraction
B_{\mu}
is the set of points whose sequence of empirical measures weakly converge to
\mu
:

B_{\mu}:=\left\{x:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_{x_{i}}\to\mu\right\}.

A measure
\mu
is called a physical measure if
B_{\mu}
has positive Lebesgue measure.
A fixed point
p
supporting a physical measure
\delta_{p}
is an example of a statistical attractor.
A point
x\in B_{\delta_{p}}
is not attracted to
p
in the classical sense, but given an arbitrarily small neighborhood of
p
, for sufficiently large timescales the orbit will almost always be inside that neighborhood.
A classical attractor (such as a fixed point
p
with
|f^{\prime}(p)|<1
) is a trivial example of a statistical attractor.

A hyperbolic repelling fixed point is a fixed point
p
with
|f^{\prime}(p)|>1
.
It has opposite behavior to an attractor, because every other point in its neighborhood will leave at an exponential rate.
We now have a natural question to ask:
Can a hyperbolic repelling fixed point support a physical measure?
We answer this question positively for circle coverings.
First, we restrict our class of circle coverings.

Definition 1.1
.

\mathcal{D}
is the set of circle local homeomorphisms
f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
that are topologically conjugate to the doubling map (
x\mapsto 2x\text{ mod }1
).

We introduce some notation.
For a map
f\in\mathcal{D}
, let
p
denote its unique fixed point, and
q
denote the unique preimage of
p
other than itself. We have:

Theorem 1.2
.

\exists f\in\mathcal{D}:f^{\prime}(p)>1
,
f
is
C^{\infty}
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus\{q\}
,
and
|B_{\delta_{p}}|=1
.

The map
f
is merely continuous at
q
, and does not have any regularity there.
We can also get
C^{1}
regularity on the circle by sacrificing some measure of
B_{\delta_{p}}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

\exists f\in\mathcal{D}\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1}):f^{\prime}(p)>1
,

f
is
C^{\infty}
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus\{q\}
,
and
|B_{\delta_{p}}|>0
.

It follows from the construction that the map from Theorem
1.3
has
f^{\prime}(q)=0
,
but that is not the mechanism which gives the result. In fact there are many examples of maps
f\in\mathcal{D}
with
q
as a critical point,
that admit a unique ergodic invariant measure
\mu
equivalent to Lebesgue.
We also conjecture that a stronger result is compatible with the restrictions of Theorem
1.3
:

Conjecture 1.4
.

\exists f\in\mathcal{D}\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1}):f^{\prime}(p)>1
, and
|B_{\delta_{p}}|=1
.

Also observe that for the doubling map (
x\mapsto 2x\text{ mod }1
), we have
|B_{\delta_{p}}|=0
.
This implies that the maps in our results are conjugate to the doubling map by a singular conjugacy map.
The topological conjugacy with the doubling map also implies that the basins in our results are are also meagre.

1.2.
Literature Study

We mention a few relared results.

1.2.1.
Known Results in
\mathcal{D}

The physical measures of several classes of maps within
\mathcal{D}
are well-studied.
By classical results, maps in
\mathcal{D}
with uniform expansion and
C^{1+\alpha}
regularity admit an ergodic invariant probability measure
\mu
which is equivalent to Lebesgue, which is also the unique physical measure.
This is not true in general for uniformly expanding
C^{1}
maps, as shown in
[
1
]
.
In fact, it was shown in
[
2
]
that generic
C^{1}
expanding circle maps admit a unique physical measure which is fully supported and singular.

A contrasting class are the intermittency maps of Maneville-Pomeau
[
3
]
,
and Liverani, Saussol, and Vaienti in
[
4
]
.
These maps feature a topologically repelling neutral fixed point
p
with
|f^{\prime}(p)|=1
.
For lower orders of tangency at
p
,
these maps admit an invariant ergodic and
absolutely continuous probability measure
\mu
as its unique physical measure.
On the other hand, for higher orders they admit
\delta_{p}
as its unique physical measure.
Related to these are the doubly intermittent maps introduced in
[
5
]
.
It was shown in
[
6
]
that there are three possible cases for this class:
an absolutely continuous probability measure
\mu
as unique physical measure,

\delta_{p}
as unique physical measure, or no physical measure at all.
The fact that
f^{\prime}(p)=1
in these maps is essential
to have the typical orbit spend a lot of time ”sticking” close to
p
.
In our results,
f^{\prime}(p)>1
, so we must use a different mechanism to show statistical attraction to
p
.

1.2.2.
Similar Results in Other Settings

Hofbauer and Keller
[
7
]
considered full continuous families of unimodal maps
f_{t}
parameterized by
t\in[0,1]
, each with a unique positive fixed point
z_{t}
which is hyperbolic repelling.
For any such family, they proved the existence of uncountably many parameter values
t
such that
\delta_{z_{t}}
is the unique physical measure of
f_{t}
.
A key component of their proof is the rich combinatorics of the kneading sequence within their family,
which is defined as the itinerary of the unique critical point.

The existence of a saddle fixed point that supports a physical measure was also shown more recently for
C^{1}
surface diffeomorphisms in
[
8
]
.
Their measure also has a nowhere dense basin of attraction.

2.
Outline of Proof

The general strategy for this paper is to find maps
f\in\mathcal{D}
that have a particular first return time induced map
F
,
such that previous results on wild attractors from
[
9
]
apply on
F
.
A technical step of independent interest is The Realization Lemma (Lemma
2.6
),
which gives us a (unique) map
f\in\mathcal{D}
, given a desired first return time map
F
.

For the rest of the paper, for any given map
f\in\mathcal{D}
,
we will identify the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with the unit interval
[0,1]
with endpoints identified,
the unique fixed point
p=0=1
, and the other preimage of
p
as a point
q\in(0,1)
.
We will then treat
f
as an orientation preserving full branch map with 2 branches, with respect to
the partition
\mathcal{P}=\{I_{1}=[0,q],I_{2}=[q,1]\}
.
We now study the first return map of
f
on
I_{2}
.
First we introduce this related space of full branch maps.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of orientation-preserving full branch maps, with a countable generating partition
\mathcal{P}^{F}=\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of subintervals that (monotonically in
n
) accumulate only at the left endpoint.

We fix some notation. Given
F\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists a decreasing sequence of endpoints
(z_{n})
such that:

(1)

K_{n}=[z_{n+1},z_{n}]

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}
,
\tau
its first return time on
I_{2}
, and
F
its first return map on
I_{2}
. Then
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
We now show how the dynamics of certain points by
F
can give us information of their dynamics by
f
.
For a point
x\in I_{2}
, we have the sequence of return times
\{\tau_{i}(x)\}_{i\geq 0}
,
defined by
\tau_{i}(x)=\tau(F^{i}(x))
.
Define
E
to be the set of points whose sequence of return times has a strictly increasing tail:

(2)

E=\{x\in I_{2}|\exists T\geq 0:\forall i\geq T,\tau_{i+1}(x)>\tau_{i}(x)\}.

Note that
E
is fully invariant by
F
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}
, and
F:I_{2}\to I_{2}
be its first return map on
I_{2}
.
Then
E\subset B_{\delta_{0}}
.

We introduce some tools to estimate the Lebesgue measure of
E
.
Given
F\in\mathcal{F}
defined on
I_{2}
,
for each subinterval
K_{n}
, we define the following set:

(3)

K_{n}^{-}:=\bigcup_{m\geq n+1}K_{m}=[q,z_{n+1}].

Note that
K_{n}^{-}
is the union of the subintervals to the left of
K_{n}
. We also define:

(4)

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{x\in K_{n}:F(x)\in K_{n}^{-}\right\}\subset K_{n}.

Note that
L_{n}
is a subinterval of
K_{n}
that shares its left endpoint.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{*}
be the family of maps in
\mathcal{F}
such that there exists a monotonically increasing sequence
(p_{n})
of positive numbers such that:

(5)

\frac{|L_{n}|}{|K_{n}|}>p_{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad P:=\prod_{n\geq 1}p_{n}>0.

This condition essentially says that
|L_{n}|/|K_{n}|\to 1
fast enough.
Then we let:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{*}^{weak}

\displaystyle:=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:\forall n,f|_{L_{n}}:L_{n}\to K_{n}^{-}\text{ is convex.}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{*}^{strong}

\displaystyle:=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:\forall n,f|_{K_{n}}:K_{n}\to I_{2}\text{ is convex.}\}

It turns out these subfamilies are sufficient to obtain estimates on
|C|
and
|E|
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}
.
Let
F:I_{2}\to I_{2}
be the first return map on
I_{2}
.

(1)

If
F\in\mathcal{F}_{*}^{weak}
, then
|E|>0
.
In particular,
|B_{\delta_{0}}|>0
.

(2)

If
F\in\mathcal{F}_{*}^{strong}
, then
|E|=|I_{2}|
.
In particular,
|B_{\delta_{0}}|=1
.

The following Propositions
2.4
and
2.5
are now sufficient to prove our main Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
respectively.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists
f\in\mathcal{D}
such that:

•

f^{\prime}(p)>1
.

•

f:I\to I
is
C^{\infty}
on
I\setminus\{q\}
.

•

The first return map
F:I_{2}\to I_{2}
is an element of
\mathcal{F}_{*}^{strong}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

There exists
f\in\mathcal{D}
such that:

•

f^{\prime}(p)>1
.

•

f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1}).

•

f:I\to I
is
C^{\infty}
on
I\setminus\{q\}
.

•

The first return map
F:I_{2}\to I_{2}
is an element of
\mathcal{F}_{*}^{weak}
.

The proofs of Propositions
2.4
and
2.5
relies on the following Realization Lemma, which is of independent interest.

Lemma 2.6

(Realization Lemma)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{I_{1},I_{2}\}
be a partition of
I
to intervals.

\forall f_{1}:I_{1}\to I
orientation preserving nonsingular homeomorphism
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
defined on
I_{2}
,
there exists a (unique) homeomorphism
f_{2}:I_{2}\to I
,
such that the full branch map
f
defined by
(f_{1},f_{2})

is an element of
\mathcal{D}
and has first return map
F
on
I_{2}
.

We briefly outline the contents of the remaining sections.
In Section
3
we study the dynamics of points in
C,E
with respect to
f
, to prove Proposition
2.2
.
In Section
4
we apply the results of
[
9
]
to estimate the Lebesgue measure of
E
, proving Proposition
2.3
.
Section
5
proves Lemma
2.6
and gives insight to how we can construct
f\in\mathcal{D}
, given a prescribed
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
Finally, Sections
6
and
7
contain the explicit constructions of

that proves Propositions
2.4
and
2.5
respectively.

3.
Induced Dynamics

In this section we prove Proposition
2.2
, which shows how the dynamics of the return time by the induced map can determine the empirical measure.
Fix a map
f\in\mathcal{D}
and let
F\in\mathcal{F}
be its first return map on
I_{2}
.
Recall the sequence of return times
\{\tau_{i}(x)\}_{i\geq 0}
, and the set of points with eventually strictly increasing return times
E
as defined in (
2
).
We define the sequence of cumulative return times:

\displaystyle\forall i\geq 1,R_{i}(x):=\sum_{0\leq j<i}\tau_{j}(x)

For consistency, we define
R_{0}=0
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Fix a point
x\in E
. It is sufficient to prove the following:

\lim_{J\to\infty}\frac{1}{J}\sum_{0\leq j<J}\delta_{x_{j}}=\delta_{0}.

It is sufficient to prove that for any interval neighborhood
U
of
0
, the proportion of visits to
U
tends to 1, that is:

\displaystyle\lim_{J\to\infty}\frac{\#\{0\leq j<J:x_{j}\in U\}}{J}=1.

Now fix an interval neighborhood
U
of
0
. Let
r
be the right endpoint of
U
.
Let
N
be the smallest integer such that
f^{N}(r)\in I_{2}
.
Because
f
is an orientation preserving homeomorphism, points outside of
U
must visit
I_{2}
in time
N
or less.
Thus for the range of time between
R_{i}(x)
and
R_{i+1}(x)
, the orbit of
x_{j}
must spend at least
\tau_{i}(x)-N
time inside
U
.
Because
x\in E
, for
i
sufficiently large, we have
\tau_{i}(x)>>N
.
Therefore as
J\to\infty
, the proportion of times that
x_{j}
spends in
U
tends to
1
.
∎

4.
Dynamics of F

In this section we estimate the Lebesgue measure of
E
.
Let
f\in\mathcal{D}
, and
F\in\mathcal{F}
be its first return map on
I_{2}
.
Recall the definition of
E
in (
2
) and the left subintervals
L_{n}\subset K_{n}
in (
4
).
Recall also that the partition
\mathcal{P}^{F}=\{K_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is defined by first return time
\tau
(i.e.
x\in K_{n}\iff\tau(x)=n
).
Therefore, we obtain the following expression for
E
in terms of the left subintervals
L_{n}
.

E=\{x\in I_{2}|\exists T\geq 0:F^{i}(x)\in\bigcup_{m\geq 1}L_{m}\text{ for all }i\geq T\}.

This new expression allows us to extend the definition of
E
for general maps
F\in\mathcal{F}
without specifying that it is the first return map of some
f\in\mathcal{D}
.
Now we define a family of related subsets of
I_{2}
:

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle:=\{x\in I_{2}|F^{i}(x)\in\bigcup_{m\geq 1}L_{m}\text{ for all }i\geq 0\}.

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in I_{2}|F^{i}(x)\in\bigcup_{m\geq 1}L_{m}\text{ for all }i\text{ such that }0\leq i<n\}.

By definition,
\bigcap_{n\geq 1}C_{n}=C\subset E
.
With these definitions, Proposition
2.3
is essentially proven in
[
9
]
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

We prove the first item. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{*}^{weak}
. Note that the identity function
id:I_{2}\to I_{2}
is convex. By Proposition 5.1. of
[
9
]
, we have
|E|>|C|=|id^{-1}(C)|\geq I_{2}P>0
.
By Proposition
2.2
,
|B_{\delta_{0}}|\geq|E|>0
.

Now we prove the second item. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{*}^{strong}
.
By Section 6 of
[
9
]
, we have
|E|=|I_{2}|
.
Thus
E
is a subset of
I_{2}
with full Lebesgue Measure.
Because
f
is full branch and
E\subset B_{\delta_{0}}
,
we obtain
|B_{\delta_{0}}|=1
.
∎

5.
Realization Lemma

In this section we prove the Realization Lemma (Lemma
2.6
),
then study the regularity of the full branch map
f
obtained from the Lemma.

5.1.
Realization Lemma

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{I_{1},I_{2}\}
be a partition of
I
to intervals,

f_{1}:I_{1}\to I
an orientation preserving nonsingular homeomorphism,
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
defined on
I_{2}
.
We will explicitly construct an
f_{2}
which proves Lemma
2.6
.

We first define a new family of subintervals:

(6)

\displaystyle\forall n\geq 1,J_{n}:=f_{1}^{-(n-1)}(I_{2}).

Essentially,
J_{n}
is the set of points in
I
such that its first visit to
I_{2}
is in time
(n-1)
.
In particular,
J_{1}=I_{2}
.
Note that the family
\{J_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
partitions
I
.
The subintervals
J_{n}
accumulate (monotonically in
n
) only at the left endpoint
0
,
in a similar manner to the subintervals
K_{n}
of
\mathcal{P}^{F}
.

Recall we want
f_{2}
to be defined such that
F
is the first return map of
f
on
I_{2}
.
Fix a partition element
K_{n}
, which we want to have points with return time
n
.

f_{2}|_{K_{n}}
must satisfy the following equation:

(7)

\displaystyle\forall n\geq 1,(f_{1})^{n-1}\circ f_{2}|_{K_{n}}=F|_{K_{n}}.

To satisfy (
7
), we must define
f_{2}|_{K_{n}}
as follows:

(8)

\displaystyle\forall n\geq 1,f_{2}|_{K_{n}}:=(f_{1})^{-(n-1)}\circ F|_{K_{n}}.

We obtain the image of
f_{2}|_{K_{n}}
:

(9)

\displaystyle\forall n\geq 1,f_{2}|_{K_{n}}(K_{n}):=(f_{1})^{-(n-1)}\circ F|_{K_{n}}(K_{n})=(f_{1})^{-(n-1)}(I_{2})=J_{n}.

We now know each piece
f_{2}|_{K_{n}}:K_{n}\to J_{n}
is a nonsingular orientation-preserving homeomorphism.
Because
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
partitions
I_{2}

and
\{J_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
partitions
I
with the same accumulating behavior,
gluing all the pieces gives a nonsingular orientation-preserving homeomorphism
f_{2}:I_{2}\to I
.
Note that this construction is also unique, because any other choice for the pieces of
f_{2}
cannot satisfy (
7
).
This defines a map
f\in\mathcal{D}
with branches
f_{1}.f_{2}
, and by (
7
),
f
has
F
as its first return map on
I_{2}
.
We can show that
f
has generating partition with the following general lemma:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f
be a full branch map with respect to a countable partition
\mathcal{P}=\{I_{n}\}
.
Let
\Delta
be a union of elements of
\mathcal{P}
.
Let
F:\Delta\to\Delta
be the first return map, which is full branch with respect to a countable partition
\mathcal{P}^{F}=\{J_{m}\}
.
If
F
has generating partition, then
f
has generating partition.

Proof.

Take a pair of (non-exceptional) points
x_{1},y_{1}\in I_{n}\in\mathcal{P}
.
Pick
I_{m}\subset\Delta
, and let
x=f^{-1}|_{I_{m}}(x_{1})
,
y=f^{-1}|_{I_{m}}(y_{1})
.
Thus
x,y\in\Delta
.
Because
F
has a generating partition,

\exists M\geq 0
such that
x,y
lie on different elements of the refinement
\mathcal{P}_{M}^{F}
.
Let
J
be the partition element of
\mathcal{P}_{M}^{F}
that contains
x
(and does not contain
y
).
Observe that
F^{M}:J\to\Delta
is a homeomorphism.
By the definition of
F
,
\exists N\geq 0
such that

f^{N}:J\to\Delta
is a homeomorphism.
Recall
\Delta
is a union of elements of
\mathcal{P}
,
thus the refinement
\mathcal{P}_{N+1}
has a collection of elements that partition
J
.
Therefore,
x
and
y
must also lie on different elements of
\mathcal{P}_{N+1}
.
∎

We now have all the properties required to prove the Realization Lemma.

Proof of Lemma
2.6
.

We have constructed (a unique) homeomorphism
f_{2}
,
a full branch map
f
with
f_{1},f_{2}
as branches.
Furthermore by (
7
),
f
has
F
as its first return map on
I_{2}
.
By Lemma
5.1
, the map
f
has generating partition, thus
f\in\mathcal{D}
.
∎

Graph Pieces

We introduce the notation of graph pieces, to help visualize Lemma

Given a pair of integers

\phi_{a,b}:K_{a}\to J_{b}

\phi_{a,b}:=f_{1}^{a-b}\circ f_{2}|_{K_{a}}.

5.3.
Regularity by Gluing

We make an elementary observation on the regularity of
f_{2}
.
Suppose
f_{1},F
have a certain regularity (say
C^{r},r\geq 1
).
Then by (
8
) and by composition,
f_{2}
has the same regularity on each interior
\mathring{K}_{n}
.
This means to prove the regularity of
f_{2}
, we only need to prove it for the gluing of consecutive pieces.
In fact, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 5.2

(Gluing Lemma)
.

Let
f_{1}:(0,q)\to(0,1)
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism,
let
F\in\mathcal{F}
have
C^{\infty}
branches,
and let
f_{2}
be the unique second branch given by Lemma
2.6
.
Then the following three statements are equivalent:

(i)

f_{2}
is
C^{\infty}
on
(q,1)
.

(ii)

\forall n\geq 1
,
\phi_{n,n}
and
\phi_{n+1,n+1}
glue in a
C^{\infty}
way.

(iii)

\forall n\geq 1
,
\phi_{n,1}
and
\phi_{n+1,2}
glue in a
C^{\infty}
way.

Proof.

Recall by (
11
) that
f_{2}
is the gluing of pieces
\{\phi_{n,n}\}_{n\geq 1}
.
By construction,
f_{2}
has
C^{\infty}
regularity on each interior
\mathring{K}_{n}
, thus (i) and (ii) are equivalent.
Now fix an index
n
. We have the following equalities:

\displaystyle f_{1}^{n-1}\circ\phi_{n,n}

\displaystyle=\phi_{n,1}

\displaystyle f_{1}^{n-1}\circ\phi_{n+1,n+1}

\displaystyle=\phi_{n+1,2}.

Because
f_{1}^{n-1}
is a
C^{\infty}
