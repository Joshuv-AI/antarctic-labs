Comparison of the treatment outcomes and identification of the risk factors associated with unfavorable outcomes among extra pulmonary and pulmonary tuberculosis patients under NTEP
Authors: Aditi Gupta, Abhishek Gupta, Anuradha Nadda, Navdeep Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIndia contributes approximately 27% to the global Tuberculosis burden, with Pulmonary TB representing the predominant form of the disease. Extrapulmonary Tuberculosis contributes to around 20% of total TB burden worldwide. Diagnosing epTB is more challenging compared to pTB. Despite adequate awareness and efforts, it still remains under-diagnosed and underreported even in high burden countries like India. Identification of the risk factors associated with unfavorable outcomes can definitely improve morbidity and mortality burden of the disease.Material methodsMedical records of 2624 patients above 18 years of age and treated for TB under NTEP over 1 year (1 June 2023 to 31 May 2024) at district Mohali were reviewed retrospectively. Treatment outcomes were classified as favorable (cured/treatment completed) and unfavorable (death, failure, lost to follow-up, regimen change). Multivariable binary logistic regression was performed to identify factors associated with unfavorable outcomes and death.ResultsOut of 2624, 1729 (65.9%) had pTB and 895 (34.1%)epTB. Favorable treatment outcome was observed in 1603 (61%) pTB and 878 (33.5%) epTB. Deaths were more in pTB(69,3.9%) as compared to epTB(14,1.6%). On regression analysis for death as an outcome, factors like recurrent TB (AOR 3.695; CI [1.356–10.070]; p-0.011), age 41–60 years (AOR 4.138; CI [2.217–7.723]; p-<0.001), Age > 60 years (AOR 2.409; CI [1.246–4.658]; p-0.009), were independently associated with higher odds of unfavorable treatment outcome.ConclusionRecurrent TB in young adults is associated with unfavorable treatment outcomes whereas BMI above 25 is associated with unfavorable outcome in epTB patients. Enhanced clinical awareness about the disease presentation for early diagnosis, availability of diagnostic tests for microbiological confirmation and detection of resistance are important for early diagnosis and optimal management of TB.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-026-13544-8.Keywords: Extra pulmonary TB, Tuberculosis, Treatment outcome, Risk factors, Favorable, India

Background

Globally in year 2023, an estimated 10.8 million people fell ill with Tuberculosis (TB) where India claimed for the highest burden of the disease. A global total of 8.2 million newly diagnosed TB cases were reported in 2023, 7.5 million in 2022 and 7.1 million in 2019 [1]. However, extra pulmonary Tuberculosis (epTB) constituted 16% of the 7.5 million reported TB cases across the world in 2020 and out of which 19% were in South East Asia. Nonethless, many more might still be undiagnosed or unreported [2]. Diagnosis of epTB remains a significant challenge for the physicians as obtaining an adequate sample for testing is not always feasible from different anatomical sites of epTB. Moreover, even when accessible samples are available, the diagnostic yield remains lower than that of pulmonary TB (pTB) [3]. Available evidence for epTB from India is predominantly descriptive and use of multi-logistic regression analysis remains limited. Identifying the risk factors and demographic characteristics associated with epTB is crucial for early diagnosis and effective management. Treatment outcome remain challenging in epTB owing to the diagnostic challenges in resource limited countries; treatment outcome in most cases are classified as treatment completed rather than cured. We have further analyzed unfavorable outcomes as failure, LTFU and death. Gradually more research is needed to understand epTB and pTB outcomes and their determining factors to a greater extent. Despite its lower transmission rates, its pertinent to lessen the transmission to negligible especially when the WHO has set a goal of TB elimination by 2035.epTB remains underemphasized due to its lower incidence, difficult diagnosis and reduced infectivity compared to pTB. For successful TB elimination, epTB must receive equal attention or even more. Various forms of epTB contribute significantly to TB related morbidity, lifelong sequeale and disabilites [4]. This study aims to evaluate the epidemiology, clinical characteristics, risk factors and outcome of epTB and pTB in the population of Mohali district, Punjab, India. We have analyzed pTB and epTB together at one site at same time period with 2624 patients.

Materials and methods

Study type

Retrospective cohort study.

Study settings

We conducted a retrospective study at Dr. BR Ambedkar state institute of medical sciences (AIMS), Mohali which is a government run tertiary care hospital and medical college at Mohali district in Punjab, India.

Duration of study

Medical records of all the patients above 18 years of age and treated for TB under NTEP over 1 year (1 June 2023 to 31 May 2024) at district Mohali were reviewed retrospectively.

Study population

All Patients above 18 years of age, diagnosed with TB and initiated on treatment under the National TB elimination programme from 1 June 2023 to 31 May 2024 at Mohali district were included in the study (Fig. 1).Under NTEP, All the patients of Mohali district who are suspected or diagnosed with Tuberculosis are being referred to our centre for diagnosis and treatment. Patients with cough of more than 2 weeks were evaluated for TB by undergoing CXR and sputum CBNAAT. TB diagnosis was made by the clinician as Pulmonary or extrapulmonary based on either microbiological confirmation with AFB smear/CBNAAT/culture or clinico-radiological basis. Patients who had pulmonary as well as extrapulmonary involvement were categorized as PTB according to NTEP. epTB sites namely pleura, lymph node, genitourinary, CNS, skeletal, abdomen, cutaneous, ocular, ENT and heart were included as per Index TB guidelines. Patients with incomplete data were excluded from the study. All the patients were initiated on four drug regimen including Rifampicin, Ethambutol, Isoniazid and Pyrazinamide for the first two months followed by three drug regimen HRE for next 4 months. The continuation phase was extended based on the disease site and response achieved at 6 months. Patients with Skeletal and CNS TB received a minimum treatment of 18 months and 12 months respectively. Lymph node TB treatment was extended upto 9 months depending upon the clinical response and ultrasound findings whereas Pulmonary TB was treated for six months only [5]. Follow up visits were conducted at two months, six months and upon treatment completion or as needed. Accredited social health activist(ASHA) workers visited patient home to dispense medicines, assess for adverse drug reactions and ensure treatment adherence. Recurrent TB was treated on the same lines but with more emphasis on bacteriological confirmation and drug sensitivity testing. Drug resistant TB (DR TB) was managed as per PMDT 2021 guidelines [6]. Favorable outcome included cured and treatment completed Unfavourable outcomes-Death, loss to follow up, treatment failure and regimen change combined to increase analytical power (especially for EPTB, where individual adverse outcomes were uncommon) and to maintain consistency with national TB programmatic outcomes frameworks.

Flow diagram of case selection for analysis and their TB treatment outcome in District Mohali, (n = 2624)

Data collection and statistical analysis

Data was retrieved from the Nikshay app at AIMS, Mohali centre. Nikshay data includes patient details such as name, age, gender, address, enrollment date, patient category, type and site of disease, CBNAAT, culture and DST, treatment initiation date, regimen, outcome, HIV, Diabetes, smoking status etc. Patients with missing data were excluded from the study and analysis was restricted to the variables with complete data. The demographic details of patients included were sex, age, height, weight and Nikshay ID. Disease-related details encompassed previous history of TB, diabetes, HIV status, treatment outcome. Data was entered in Microsoft excel 2007 and analyzed by using R software version 3.6.1. Multivariable binary logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with unfavorable treatment outcomes and death. Variables with p < 0.20 in univariable analysis were entered into the multivariable model. Adjusted odds ratios (AOR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were reported. Model assumptions were assessed, and statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. In our study missing data accounts for 5.6% of the dataset and missing data was primarily related to sociodemographic or comorbidity variables rather than treatment outcomes. Sensitivity analysis showed no major difference in age, sex distribution, treatment outcome between included and excluded cases (Supplementary table-1), suggesting limited impact of missing data. Association was considered significant at a p-value < 0.05.

Ethical consideration

The study protocol was approved by ethical committee of AIMS, Mohali vide letter no. AIMS/IEC-HR/2024/63 dated 20.08.2024. The need for written consent was waived off by the committee. The ethical standards of the Helsinki Declaration were abided strictly.

Characteristics of pTB and epTB disease

Most of the TB cases were new (epTB 850, 95%; pTB 1448, 83.7%). Drug resistance was observed in only 2.5% of pTB patients and none in epTB as microbiological confirmation was attained only in 116 (13%) epTB patients.

Diagnosis and investigations

1395(80.7%) pTB patients were microbiologically confirmed compared to only 116(13%) epTB. Majority epTB were diagnosed on clinico -radiological basis as per index TB guidelines [5].

Comparison between pTB and epTB cases

895 (34%) developed epTB and 1729 (66%) pTB out of 2624 patients. Elderly were more affected in pTB.

As shown in Table 2, treatment success rate was better in epTB (878,98.1%) as compared to pTB (1603,92.7%). Discussion

The current study was conceived to identify demographic details, clinical characteristics and treatment outcome of epTB and pTB. Male predominance was observed in pTB whereas epTB had around equal distribution among males and females in the currentstudy.Cherain [9] et al. conducted a multi-centric retrospective study with 2219 epTB patients in India and found similar gender distribution. Young adults developed the disease more often than elderly [10] whereas elderly were further less affected in epTB compared to pTB. Various other studies found similar age distribution trends where the most productive age group were more affected [9, 11, 12].

Microbiological confirmation was very low in epTB and similar trend has been observed in various studies from different countries [13, 14]. Enormous interventions, resources and interdepartmental coordination is required to increase the microbiological confirmation in epTB contrary to pTB where it is very easy to collect sputum for confirmation. Good quality sputum collection can be easily taught to the patient whereas in epTB, invasive intervention is required for microbiological confirmation. Yield of the sample depends on the anatomical site involved in epTB. Pus from abscess, bony joint and lymph node has much more yield than other specimens like pleural fluid, CSF, urine [15]. Recurrent TB is an important risk factor for the development of TB disease more often in Ptb when compared to epTB [11, 16]. EPTB site is often misclassified due to lack of availability of diagnostic tests and infrastructure at peripheral hospitals. Specialized investigations like endoscopy, colonoscopy, image guided FNAC /biopsy, surgical excision biopsy, lumbar puncture, gastric lavage are required to diagnose EPTB otherwise dissemination cannot be confirmed. Site confirmation is essential for follow up, outcome analysis and risk factor analysis. Disseminated TB is often associated with immunosuppression whereas specific sites might not have such strong associations. Age, low BMI, end stage renal disease, immunosuppression are associated with dissemination strongly [17].

As per WHO, many new TB cases are attributable to five risk factors namely undernutrition, HIV infection, alcohol use disorder, smoking, and diabetes [1]. In our study, diabetes was associated with pTB not epTB aligning with Singhal et al [13]. Association of HIV TB is well known [14] but in our study it cannot be ascertained as incidence of HIV is very low in our area. Malnutrition is a defined risk factor for the development of TB disease [1]. Its association is more pronounced with pTB as compared to epTB possibly due to paucibacillary nature of epTB. It has been proven in mice studies that protein calorie malnutrition enhances bacterial growth leading to markedly elevated bacillary loads in lungs of protein deficient mice [18]. BMI > 25 was associated with unfavorable outcome in epTB whereas for pTB, association of BMI with unfavorable outcome was statistically insignificant. Malnutrition impairs cell mediated immunity and micronutrient deficiency further worsens immune dysfunction [19]. Obesity however is associated with chronic inflammation, immune dysregulation and various comorbidities like Diabetes, metabolic syndrome and cardiovascular diseases which could worsen the outcome [20].

Unfavorable outcome in PTB is associated with HIV co infection, drug resistance, delayed diagnosis, malnutrition, poor adherence, comorbidity like diabetes [21–23]. whereas unfavorable outcome in epTB is associated with old age [11, 24] relapse [11] Diabetes [11] female gender [25] and HIV24 [24, 25]. Treatment failure is less frequent in epTB as compared to pTB as it is a pauci-bacillary disease and initial bacillary load is directly related to failure and drug resistance [26]. Very limited published data is available in India on treatment outcome among epTB patients. In our study, some variables show significant effect but pooled estimates show non-significant overall effect. The effects of nine study variables vary in magnitude/direction and pooled estimate is an average effect across all the study variables. When variability among study variables is large then significant findings for some study variables do not guarantee a significant pooled effect, as in our study. Effect of Individual variables should be considered in our study and heterogeneity should be considered as a limitation with respect to interpreting the overall effect as generalisable for all variables. Henceforth, The epTB regression model should be interpreted cautiously because the number of favourable outcomes was small, limited statistical power and wide confidence interval for some variables.

In comparison, the outcome is better in epTB worldwide but diagnosis is often delayed and difficult. Outcome in epTB varies with anatomical site of involvement whose incidence further varies with age and gender [27]. Strengthening clinician’s knowledge is prudent for early diagnosis. The ICMR provides comprehensive guidelines and diagnostic flowcharts for the management of epTB [28]. Regular capacity building of healthcare workers and improved diagnostic infrastructure will increase microbiological confirmation rates and early detection of drug resistance. ASHA workers act as a link between community and the healthcare facilities in strengthening treatment adherence and outcome, National program demands robust and reliable assistance from the government to provide coverage to the largest population of the world.

Strength, limitations and future perspectives

Strength of the study lies in the large sample size. However, retrospective nature makes it difficult to gather all the information related to the patient and the disease. Patients with incomplete data were omitted from the study. Their outcomes could have been poorer and were missed from the analysis. Strain or virulence of infecting organism could be an important determining factor for future analysis. Despite above limitations, this study has provided useful information regarding factors associated with poor outcome in TB which can be used for better planning of TB management nationwide.

Conclusion

Recurrent TB in young adults is associated with unfavorable treatment outcomes in both the forms whereas BMI above 25 is associated with unfavorable outcome in epTB patients. More efforts are required for microbiological confirmation of epTB otherwise the opportunity to detect drug resistance and improve cure rate is lost. In addition, targeted policies including regular training of medical professionals and healthcare workers to increase clinical awareness for the diagnosis and management of pTB and epTB are necessary to decrease the disease burden. The Laboratory network must be strengthened by ensuring the availability of culture and DST facilities at all treatment centres. Processing of epTB samples requires specialized trainings to improve diagnostic yield. Furthermore, enhancing the coverage of contact tracing and TB preventive therapy is crucial to achieving TB elimination.
