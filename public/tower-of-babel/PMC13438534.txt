A novel model for assessing lymph node metastasis and the immune microenvironment in breast cancer by integrating digital pathology images and transcriptomics
Authors: Yongliang Sha, Huijie Zhuang, Yiqiu Wang, Hao Guo, Hui Cheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/aimsAccurate prediction of the lymph node status is essential for clinical decision-making in breast cancer. This work aimed to develop a multimodal deep learning model that integrates gene expression data with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained whole slide images (WSIs) to predict lymph node metastasis (LNM) and assess the tumor microenvironment in breast cancer.MethodsThe Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database was used to gather the clinicopathological data, transcriptome information, and WSIs of patients with breast cancer. WSIs from Xuzhou Central Hospital were used as external validations. Four deep learning models were used to build the LNM pathological model. The pathogenic model and hub genes provided the foundation for constructing the nomogram. The potential mechanism was evaluated through the performance of functional enrichment. The calibration, discrimination, and clinical usefulness of the pathological model, multi-gene model, and nomogram were evaluated. Furthermore, the correlation between the nomogram and prognosis, clinicopathological features, and the quantity of immune cells was evaluated. Using immunohistochemistry and popliteal lymph node metastasis tests, the expression of beta-1,3-galactosyltransferase-4 (B3GALT4) in tumor tissues and its association with LNM and CD8+ T cells were examined.ResultsThe findings of the present study demonstrated that in comparison to any single model, the nomogram’s area under the curve (AUC) was better, at 0.99 [95% CI: 0.98–1.00]. The calibration curve demonstrated a high degree of agreement. The bulk of the threshold probabilities in this model were linked to favorable net benefits. Compared to the low-risk group, patients with high-risk breast cancer had more CD8+ T cells, a more advanced stage, and worse overall outcomes (P < 0.05). Breast cancer lymph node metastasis was facilitated by B3GALT4-induced CD8+ T cell exhaustion.ConclusionsThis model may provide a higher predictive value for LNM. B3GALT4 could serve as a risk biomarker of lymph node metastatic breast cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05475-2.

Introduction

Breast cancer has the greatest death rate among female malignancies globally, and its frequency has been rising recently. The primary causes of death for people with breast cancer are metastasis and recurrence [1]. For individuals with breast cancer, lymph node metastasis (LNM) is a significant predictor of overall survival and recurrence. Nowadays, lymph node dissection or ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (US-FNA) is used to clinically confirm LNM [2]. However, these invasive treatments are associated with several hazards, including paresthesia, lymphedema, chronic discomfort, and decreased shoulder mobility. Therefore, developing a novel technique to precisely forecast the LN status before surgery is essential.

Large-scale high-throughput data have shown promise in forecasting the metastasis and immune microenvironment of breast cancer in recent decades. To evaluate the tumor immune microenvironment and prognosis of breast cancer, Chen et al. [3] discovered a 16-gene profile made up of genes linked to pyroptosis. A new glycolysis-related gene signature was developed by Huang et al. [4] to predict the prognosis and effectiveness of immunotherapy in breast cancer. However, its clinical prediction accuracy needs to be raised. Many molecular and non-molecular variables are known to play a role in the development of lymph node metastasis [5, 6]. In theory, prediction models that rely solely on one type of molecular biomarker are not the most effective [7, 8]. Multidimensional data integration for disease diagnosis and prediction has gained popularity in recent years. By combining pathological, clinical, and gene expression data, Yao et al. [9] developed a multi-modal deep learning model to forecast the probability of metastases and recurrence of breast cancer. A radiogenomic multi-modal model based on whole-transcriptome sequencing (WTS) and machine learning was developed by Lai et al. [10] to precisely assess the condition of the axillary lymph nodes and the response to medication therapy. Prediction accuracy rose with the incorporation of multi-omics data, albeit at the cost of expensive sequencing.

Whole-slide images (WSIs) provide more information on lymphatic dissemination by displaying a patient’s histological characteristics at the single-cell and single-nucleus resolution, including cancer cell penetration into lymphatic channels [11]. Consequently, WSIs could offer a more profound comprehension of the metastatic process. As a result, determining LN metastases directly from initial tumor samples may have enormous clinical benefit and allow for preventative surgery. The characterization of these pathogenic characteristics in WSIs has evolved recently, thanks to the application of deep learning (DL) techniques [12]. Xu et al. [13] proposed a deep learning model that used manually segmented tumor areas from WSIs as input to predict ALN status. They found three nuclear characteristics that were most relevant to the diagnosis. Ding et al. [14] created a multi-modal DL model that combined digital pathological images and clinicopathological data from core needle biopsy specimens. However, Marmé et al. [15] showed that hematoxylin/eosin-stained images did not provide a significant benefit in predicting sentinel lymph node status across all three external validation cohorts. In any case, WSIs from initial tumor biopsy samples offer a less intrusive method of predicting LN metastases.

The tumor microenvironment (TME), which is made up of different kinds of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), such as T lymphocytes, B lymphocytes, neutrophils, macrophages, natural killer cells (NK cells), fibroblasts, and dendritic cells, must be communicated with by cancer cells when they begin to form metastatic nodules [16, 17]. Cancer cells can initiate immunosuppressive mechanisms, such as regulatory T cells (Tregs) and CD8+ fatigued T cells, as well as an anti-tumor immune response, such as CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and NK cells, during the metastatic process [18, 19]. Therefore, more research is needed to examine the connection between breast cancer’s TME and lymph node metastases.

Our goal in this work was to combine WSIs with genomic information to create a predictive model that can reliably forecast a patient’s lymph node status and prognosis for breast cancer. Next, we evaluated the tumor immune microenvironment of breast cancer using the risk model. The function of beta-1,3-galactosyltransferase-4 (B3GALT4) in the LN metastasis of breast cancer in vivo was then investigated using a popliteal LN metastasis model. We also conducted further immunohistochemical staining experiments to investigate the relationship between B3GALT4 and CD8+ T cells in various stages of lymph node metastasis in breast cancer. Materials and methods

Sample preparations

This study included 106 breast cancer patients who had axillary lymph node dissections (ALNDs) performed in the Xuzhou Central Hospital’s Department of General Surgery between May 2018 and May 2024. Criteria for Inclusion: (1) Newly diagnosed invasive breast cancer confirmed by postoperative pathology; (2) No previous radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or immune-targeted therapy before surgery; (3) Availability of complete clinicopathological data; (4) Dissected axillary lymph nodes at levels I and II. Criteria for Exclusion: (1) Concurrent infectious or autoimmune diseases; (2) Presence of other malignancies; (3) Male patients; (4) Certain forms of breast cancer, such as tubular, medullary, mucinous, inflammatory, or Paget’s disease; (5) Preoperative confirmation of distant metastasis; (6) Pregnancy or lactation. Aseptically obtained tumor tissues and matched normal breast tissues were gathered at least 2 cm from the tumor margin. The Xuzhou Central Hospital Ethics Committee approved this study (XZZXYY2018034), and all patients and their families provided written informed consent. We downloaded breast cancer sample dataset from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-BRCA; https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). We retrieved three data types from TCGA-BRCA: pathological images, clinical data and gene sequencing data.

Data acquisition and image preprocessing

The basis for training the tissue categorization model is TCGA training set, which consists of 211,694 patches extracted from 450 WSIs of patients with breast cancer. The performance of the tissue categorization model was assessed using 36,075 image patches from 106 WSIs of patients with breast cancer from Xuzhou Central Hospital between May 2018 and May 2024. For every patient, we also gathered clinical characterisation and follow-up information. Age, gender, and the pathological TNM stage were among the clinical data gathered. Patients without complete survival information, clinical features, or postoperative H&E-stained pathological images were not included. In order to find appropriate patient data for the final model input, we lastly assessed the quality of the pathological photos stained with H&E. Patients were grouped according to whether their cancer had metastasized or recurred; those who had neither were given a ‘0’, and those who had either were given a ‘1’.

The automatic segmentation of the WSIs was digitized by the sliding window paradigm. At 20× magnification (0.5 μm/pixel resolution), whole slide pictures were separated into 224 × 224 pixel patches. Processed images were visually checked, and empty patches were excluded from further analysis. Subsequently, color normalization of all images was performed using the Vahadane algorithm. To prevent patient-level leakage and avoid inflated performance, all data splitting was performed strictly at the patient level. The dataset was divided into training and testing sets using an 70:30 ratio. To address class imbalance and reduce overfitting, we applied extensive augmentation (flips, rotations, and contrast adjustments), class re-weighting with focal loss, and standard regularization strategies. A cosine-annealing learning-rate schedule and early stopping were employed to stabilize convergence and prevent overfitting. This protocol ensures complete isolation between splits and guarantees that the reported performance reflects true model generalization rather than memorization of patient-specific imaging patterns.

Tumor feature extraction and pathological model construction

The deep learning framework comprises two stages: patch-level classification followed by WSIs analysis. In the patch-level prediction phase, each image patch was annotated with a dichotomous classification label (0: negative, 1: positive) corresponding to the histopathologically confirmed status of lymph node metastasis. Leveraging transfer learning within a deep neural network architecture, four computer vision models (ResNet50, DenseNet121, EfficientNet-B0, and Vision Transformers [ViTs]) [20] pre-trained on the fused ImageNet multi-dimensional corpus (https://www.image-net.org/index.php) were implemented for hierarchical feature representation. The images were processed through each network, and the output features were extracted from the last fully connected layer of each model, yielding a final set of multi-dimensional image features. Subsequently, we calculated the mean value of each dimension of the deep features and obtained patient-level features. The features of multiple image patch sets were averaged to represent the feature set for each patient. Given that the dataset was manually created, we implemented measures to mitigate the risk of overfitting, including data augmentation techniques such as random cropping, rotation, and flipping to enhance the diversity of the training data. The network training parameters included a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻4, a batch size of 32, and the Adam optimizer with its default learning rate. The weighted binary cross-entropy function served as the loss criterion.

To finish the prediction of patients’ LNM, we constructed a model for each feature set using the patient-level features from four neural networks. An internal training set (70%) and an internal validation set (30%) were randomly selected from the TCGA-BRCA cohort. We collected H&E-stained slides of breast cancer patients from Xuzhou Central Hospital as external validation set. We evaluated each model on the validation set during training using the following metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC). Later, the accuracy-based optimized model was evaluated in external validation sets, internal training sets, and internal validation sets.

Identification of the hub genes and construction of the LNM diagnostic model

There were 183 patients without LNM and 189 individuals with LNM in the TCGA-BRCA cohort. To identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs), we employed the limma program, setting thresholds of |log2FC| > 1 and an adjusted P-value < 0.05. The volcano plot provided a visual representation of the differences in gene expression patterns between patients with and without LNM. To elucidate the functional roles and pathway processes of the DEGs associated with different LN statuses, we performed gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment investigations using the “ClusterProfiler” software. By building several decision trees, the random forest algorithm can achieve the most accurate variable classification. This approach was then used to determine which variables were the most informative. Finally, logistic regression was used to create a multi-gene prediction model. The ability of the risk score to predict the LNM of patients with breast cancer was assessed using time-dependent receiver operator characteristic (ROC) analysis using the R package “survival ROC,” accounting for both specificity and sensitivity. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was calculated in order to assess the prediction accuracy.

Nomogram model construction and evaluation

The hub genes and pathogenic model were combined into a nomogram. The degree of agreement between the estimated and actual lymph node status was assessed using calibration curves. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test was used to evaluate the discrepancy between the anticipated outcomes and the actual observations. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to evaluate the nomogram’s clinical utility. The AUC was calculated to assess the prediction accuracy.

Clinical information analysis

For every patient with breast cancer in the TCGA dataset, we calculated their risk score (RS). The median number was then manually chosen as the cutoff point to differentiate between high and low-risk categories. The ‘survival ROC’ R package was used to determine the risk score’s predictive power for breast cancer patients’ survival through time-dependent ROC analysis. To evaluate the predictive accuracy, the AUC was computed. An improved ability to predict patient survival was indicated by a higher AUC value. The RS of other subgroups, such as stage, progesterone receptor (PR), estrogen receptor (ER), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), were also compared. ROC curve analysis was then used to assess the nomogram model’s prediction ability for breast cancer subtypes.

Immune microenvironment analysis

We used a previously published technique to evaluate the immune infiltrations in breast cancer in order to look into the connection between the immunological milieu of the disease and lymph node metastases. To measure the degree of immunological infiltration, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was utilized to compute enrichment scores (meta-scores) using the ‘’GSVA’’ package (version 1.36.2) in R. The “pheatmap” and “ggplot2” programs were used to depict the results of unsupervised clustering using the calculated meta-scores from the TCGA dataset. The TCGA dataset’s breast cancer samples were split into two groups based on the median level of risk score. To investigate the functional differences between the high- and low-risk groups, we employed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA, version 4.0.1) to identify variations in KEGG and GO pathway enrichment based on their distinct gene expression patterns.

To evaluate the relationships between the level of CD8+ T cell infiltration and breast cancer LNM, we downloaded the single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) data of the GSE167036 dataset, which includes eight pairs of primary breast tumors (PTs) and lymph nodes metastatic tumors (LNMTs). The Seurat program’s two-sided Wilcoxon Rank Sum test was used to assess the differences in CD8+ T cell exhaustion and activation markers between PTs and LNMTs. A marker was considered significant if its Bonferroni-adjusted p-value was less than 0.05. GSEA was then applied to identify pathways and gene sets associated with exhausted CD8+ T cells that were enriched in LNM. The enrichment results were visualized using Cluster Profiler v4.6.0.

Cell culture, transfection, and grouping

RPMI 1640, fetal bovine serum (FBS), penicillin-streptomycin, and trypsin-EDTA (Thermo Scientific) were the reagents utilized for cell culture. Tianjin Medical University supplied the human breast cancer cell line MDA-MB-468, which consistently expressed the firefly luciferase reporter gene (MDA-MB-468-Luc). On Oct. 11, 2022, cells were identified using short tandem repeat (STR) detection technologies. The virus/mycoplasma was assessed using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). The cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium with 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution and 10% FBS added. The culture media was replaced every three to four days. For 16 h, MDA-MB-468-Luc cells were cultured with either an empty vector or a medium containing B3GALT4-overexpressing lentivirus, with 4 µg/ml puromycin (Beyotime) added. Following this period, the medium was changed, and the cells were grown for an additional three days.

qRT-PCR

Trizol (Thermo Scientific) was used to extract mRNA in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Following the manufacturer’s instructions, cDNA was created using Bio-Rad’s iScript reverse transcription supermix. Using a StepOnePlus heat cycler and SYBR Select Master Mix (Thermo Scientific), qRT-PCR was carried out. Fifty cycles of 95 °C for 30 s, 60 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 30 s, followed by five minutes of 95 °C cycling. Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the ΔΔCt technique, where ΔCt = Ct (target gene) – Ct (reference gene) and ΔΔCt = ΔCt (experimental group) – ΔCt (control group). It was found that the relative gene expression was 2–ΔΔCt [21]. The reference gene chosen was glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH). The following is a list of primer sequences: B3GALT4 (Forward CACCCTCCCCCATATCAGGA; Reverse GTTTCGGGTGCTGGAGTACA); GAPDH (Forward: GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT; Reverse: GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG).

Western blot

To lyse the cells, we added 0.5 mL of RIPA cell lysis buffer, which contained 1 mM PMSF. After that, we centrifuged for ten minutes at 12,000 rpm. The complete cell protein is present in the supernatant, which we collected. The operation handbook was closely followed when experimenting. The obtained protein samples were boiled in boiling water for 10 min after 5x SDS-PAGE protein loading buffer was added at a 1:4 ratio to fully denature the proteins. After transferring all proteins to a PVDF membrane and using 5% skim milk powder, we performed 10% SDS-PAGE on 30 µg of total protein and left it at room temperature for two hours. After adding diluted rabbit anti-B3GALT4 (1:1000) polyclonal antibodies and an internal standard rabbit anti-β-actin monoclonal antibody (1:1000), we incubated the sample overnight at 4 °C. After that, we incubated for two hours at room temperature with goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP (1:10,000). Lastly, we used ECL to develop, took pictures, and used Image J software to analyze the gray levels.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Dimethylbenzene was used to deparaffinize the paraffin-embedded tumor tissues (4 μm), and an ethanol series (100% ethanol and 95% ethanol) was used to hydrate them. After that, the slides were left at room temperature for 15 min in a 0.3% hydrogen peroxide solution (H2O2) in methanol. Next, in Dako Cytomation target retrieval solution pH 6, microwave antigen retrieval was performed. After blocking the slides with serum-free protein block solution, the primary antibodies against CD8A (1:1000, Abcam), PDCD1 (1:1000, Abcam), GZMB (1:1000, Abcam), and B3GALT4 (1:100, Abcam) were applied overnight at 4 °C. After the slides were treated with the corresponding secondary antibody for an hour at room temperature, they were detected the next day using diaminobenzidine (DAB). A light microscope was used to observe the response and take pictures. The scoring criteria are as follows: 0 denotes no staining, 1 light yellow, and 2 brown. 0: < 25%; 1: 26% − 50%; 2: 51% − 75%; and 3: ≥ 76% are the staining areas. The intensity and area scores (① × ②) were multiplied to determine the immunohistochemistry score. A score of < 2 was considered negative, while a score of ≥ 2 was considered positive. The frequency of CD8+ T cells was determined by identifying the CD8A-positive cells in 200 × 200 images of tumor tissues.

Footpad implantation and popliteal-lymph-node metastasis assay

The twelve female BALB/c nude mice were provided by Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co. Ltd. (Beijing, China), and were thereafter randomly divided into two groups of six. To create the LNM model, a total of 1.5 × 106 cells were injected into each mouse’s footpad after being suspended in 100 µL of Hanks’ solution. One group of mice received injections with MDA-MB-468-Luc cells stably overexpressing B3GALT4 (OE-B3GALT4 group), while the other group received MDA-MB-468-Luc cells with only vectors (Vector group). After 49 days, fluorescence signals were monitored using an In Vivo Imaging System (IVIS) (PerkinElmer, USA). The fluorescence intensity per unit area of metastatic popliteal lymph nodes was analyzed using Caliper Life Sciences software. Subsequently, the mice were euthanized, and the popliteal lymph nodes were dissected from the lower limbs. The diameter was measured, and the volume was later calculated. All procedures involving tumor-bearing subjects were approved by the Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute and Hospital’s Institutional Animal Care and Use Ethics Committee (Approval No. AE-2021112), which specified a maximal allowable tumor size or burden of 1.5 cm (diameter). Throughout the study, this limit was strictly observed, and no tumors exceeded 1.5 cm.

Statistical analysis

The R program (version 4.0.0) and Prism 8.0 (GraphPad program, Kaplan-Meier (K-M) plot, and dot plot) were used to perform the statistical analyses. For continuous variables, the Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was employed, while for categorical variables, the Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test was employed [22]. The K-M analysis was used to examine the survival rates of breast cancer patients. For overall survival research, patients were divided into two groups based on the median value of either gene expression or risk score. As part of correlation analysis, Spearman’s correlation coefficient was computed. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Patients characteristics

Table 1 summarizes the demographic distribution and clinicopathological characteristics of the two cohorts. The TCGA BRCA dataset included 450 breast cancer samples, of which 372 included clinical feature information. The final 372 were used to build the model after careful evaluation by two experienced pathologists. Among the patients included in the research, 189 were positive for axillary lymph node metastasis (N1/N2/N3 = 189) and 183 were negative (N0 = 183). All of the patients were between the ages of 49 and 68. 742 days was the median follow-up period for OS. The external validation cohort (n = 106) included patients ranging in age from 43 to 60. The baseline feature distributions of the training and test sets were similar to those of the external validation set, suggesting that the external validation set may be used to evaluate the accuracy of the prediction models built using the training set.

Construction and evaluation of the LNM pathological model

Internal training (261 patients) and internal validation (111 patients) sets were randomly selected from the TCGA-BRCA cohort. The ResNet50 model yielded 2048 dimensions of features, the DenseNet121 model yielded 2048 dimensions, the ViTs model yielded 768 dimensions, and the EfficientNet-B0 model yielded 1024 dimensions. Using feature sets at the patient level, four deep learning models were constructed. The model, which contained accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and AUC values of 0.96, 0.95, 0.96, 0.95, and 0.99, respectively, achieved the best model prediction, according to the results. Table 2 provides the performance metrics for each of the four classifiers. The accuracy trend of the models as the number of training iterations increased was displayed in Fig. 2. In the training and validation sets, we observed that ResNet50’s accuracy trend was more consistent than that of the other three models. Thus, the scores of the internal training, internal validation, and external validation sets were calculated using the model with ResNet50 feature sets.

The model showed excellent discrimination ability, with an AUC of 0.98 (95% CI: 0.97–1.00) in the internal training (Fig. 3A) and 0.97 (95% CI: 0.94–0.99) in the internal validation (Fig. 3B). The confusion matrix for the internal training and validation data confirmed the high accuracy of the model (Fig. 3C), with the majority of predictions being made correctly. Furthermore, we determined the risk scores for each sample within the TCGA-BRCA cohort (Fig. 3D). The TCGA-BRCA cohort’s risk ratings for breast cancer patients with LNM were then considerably higher than those of patients without LNM, according to the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (P < 0.001, Fig. 3E).

A total of 171 DEGs were screened between the LNM (-) and LNM (+) groups in the TCGA database. The specific distribution of DEGs was visualized by the volcano plot (Fig. 5A). According to functional enrichment studies, these DEGs were substantially enriched in the epidermal growth factor receptor signaling pathway, adaptive immunological response, TCF transactivating complex, and branch elongation of an epithelium (Fig. 5B-C). Random Forest (RF), an ensemble learning tool, has become a powerful computational framework widely used in computational biology and biomedical informatics research fields, particularly for screening genes linked to disease. In Fig. 5D, we ranked every gene based on the feature importance score determined by the Gini index (RF) and identified the top 15 genes as potential essential genes. The model showed excellent discrimination ability, with an AUC of 0.69 (95% CI: 0.64–0.74) in the TCGA-BRCA cohort (Fig. 5E).

Association of combined model with clinical characteristics and prognosis

According to Fig. 7A, the innovative evaluation’s sensitivity and specificity for diagnosing stage I–II and III–IV breast cancer were 94.68% and 97.14% for samples of varying stages, and 94.38% and 100.00% for samples of different stages. To determine the relationship between the combined model and breast cancer stage, we used the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. We discovered that the risk score was significantly higher in late clinical stages (P < 0.001, Fig. 7B), suggesting a possible relationship between the risk score and tumor progression. We also contrasted the risk scores for several molecular subtypes of breast cancer. The results showed that ER, PR, and HER2 status did not significantly correlate with the risk score (P = 0.47, 0.34, and 0.11, respectively; Fig. 7C). We investigated the predictive value of the risk model for LNM in the different molecular subtypes of breast cancer using the AUC. Based on the study, the AUC value was 0.98 (95% CI: 0.97–1.00) for the LumA set, 0.98 (95% CI: 0.97–1.00) for the LumB set, 0.98 (95% CI: 0.97–1.00) for the HER2 set, and 0.98 (95% CI: 0.98–1.00) for the Basal set (Fig. 7D). The distribution of risk scores for the various subtypes of breast cancer was statistically different, as determined by the Wilcoxon test (P = 0.02, Fig. 7E). These findings suggest that the algorithm may also be used to forecast various breast cancer subtypes.

Using the median risk score (0.6) to categorize breast cancer patients in the TCGA dataset into high and low-risk groups, we discovered that patients in the low-risk category performed better (P = 0.041, Fig. 7F). For 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival, the model’s AUCs were 0.78, 0.63, and 0.62, respectively (Fig. 7G). We then examined the predictive significance of the risk score in patients with tumor stages I–IV. In stages I–II, patients with lower risk scores had significantly improved prognoses (P = 0.0027, Fig. 7H), however, in phases III–IV, the results did not improve as much (P = 0.97). This phenomenon may be caused by the minimal number of patients with stage I and stage IV breast cancer, as well as the growing emphasis on early breast cancer prevention.

Association between risk model and immune microenvironment

The microenvironment has a significant influence on tumor development and lymph node metastasis. To examine the relationship between the risk score and immune cell infiltration, we conducted correlation analysis using the ssGSEA approach. All 24 immune cell types, except for CD8+ T cells, plasma cells, and type 17 T helper cells, did not differ significantly between the high- and low-risk groups (Figures S1-S2, all P > 0.05). The high-risk group had greater proportions of CD8+ T cells (P = 0.036) (Fig. 8A). Pathway analysis showed that hemostasis (P = 0.008, Fig. 8B), the adaptive immune system (P = 0.01, Fig. 8C), vesicle-mediated transport (P = 0.008, Fig. 8D), and breast cancer ductal invasive (P = 0.008, Fig. 8E) were significantly up-regulated in the high-risk group. There were a few genes (top 6: CTSK, MMP3, TACC1, B3GALT4, GREM1, and NOX4; Fig. 8F) that were significantly enriched in breast cancer ductal invasive pathways, suggesting a significant role of these genes in regulating breast cancer progression and lymph node metastasis. Some of these potential genes have been associated with cancer development in previous research. The candidate gene set is then cleared of these genes. B3GALT4 is one of the remaining potential genes that has not yet been thoroughly investigated, and there is no literature relating to its association with breast cancer lymph node metastases. Clarifying the relationship between B3GALT4 and lymph node metastasis is vital because of the robust statistical evidence of B3GALT4 in the course of breast cancer.

There have been reports linking cytotoxic CD8+ T lymphocytes to breast cancer lymph node metastases. Additionally, our findings supported those of previous studies utilizing the GSE167036 dataset, which showed that a higher proportion of CD8+ T cells was present in LNMTs (Fig. 8G). We also examined the gene levels associated with CD8+ T cells in PTs and LNMTs. Here, we observed that immune-related genes involved in antigen presentation (HLA-A/B/C, HLA-DRB1, HLA-DMA/B, and CD74) were significantly up-regulated in the LNMTs, whereas T cell cytotoxicity genes (GZMB, PRF1, and IFNG) were significantly down-regulated in LNMTs (Fig. 8H). The enhanced activities of DEGs in T cell differentiation, negative control of activation, regulation of the apoptotic process, and fatigued CD8+ T cells were further validated by GSEA (Fig. 8I-J). These findings demonstrated that although the LNMT’s TME had a markedly higher number of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, its anti-tumor function was suppressed.

Correlation of B3GALT4 with lymphatic metastasis of breast cancer

To investigate the connection between B3GALT4 expression and lymph node status, we first determined the mRNA expression level of B3GALT4 in breast cancer using the TCGA dataset. The results demonstrated that B3GALT4 expression was significantly up-regulated in LNM (+) breast cancer tissues compared to LNM (-) breast cancer tissues (Fig. 9A, P < 0.05). To get further evidence, we examined the prognostic value of B3GALT4 in relation to lymph node metastases in breast cancer. The ROC curve analysis showed that B3GALT4 was a good discriminator for the prediction of LMN (Fig. 9B) (AUC 0.991, 95% CI: 0.983–0.998). Both LNM (-) and LNM (+) breast cancer tissues have B3GALT4 protein expression verified by IHC. As shown in Fig. 9C, in both LNM (-) and LNM (+) tumors, the B3GALT4 protein was predominantly expressed on the tumor cell membrane. We found a significantly higher expression level of B3GALT4 in LNM (+) tumors than in LNM (-) tumors (P < 0.05). As suggested by a prior work, we established a popliteal LNM model using nude mice in order to better understand the role of B3GALT4 in lymphatic metastasis of breast cancer. In summary, OE-B3GALT4 or an empty vector was used to transfect MDA-MB-468-Luc cells, and qRT-PCR and Western blot were used to confirm the transfection efficiency (Fig. 9D). Following that, these cells were injected into the footpads of naked mice. After 49 days, in vivo bioluminescence imaging was used to assess the effect of B3GALT4 on lymphatic metastasis. Surprisingly, B3GALT4 overexpression significantly facilitated the lymphatic metastasis of breast cancer cells (Fig. 9E). Additionally, the OE-B3GALT4 group’s popliteal lymph nodes were heavier than the Vector group’s, indicating that B3GALT4 encouraged breast cancer’s lymphatic metastasis (Fig. 9F-G). Furthermore, compared to the Vector group, the OE-B3GALT4 group showed a greater rate of LN metastatic disease (Fig. 9H). All of these results indicated a significant promotion of lymphatic metastasis in vivo by B3GALT4 overexpression.

Correlation of B3GALT4 with CD8 in breast cancer tissues

The associations between B3GALT4 and gene markers of exhausted CD8+ T cells (TGFβ1, HLA-A, and PDCD1), cytotoxic CD8+ T cells (CD274, CD96, and IL-10), and CD8+ T cells (CD8A) in the TCGA-BRCA dataset were investigated using Spearman correlation analysis. CD8A (P = 0.009), TGFβ1 (P < 0.001), HLA-A (P < 0.001), and PDCD1 (P = 0.004) all exhibited significant relationships with B3GALT4. As shown in Fig. 10A, B3GALT4 was negatively associated with CD274 (P < 0.001), CD96 (P = 0.002), and IL-10 (P = 0.001).

High-expression and low-expression groups were created from tumor samples based on median B3GALT4 immunohistochemistry scores. We then looked at the differences in CD8+ T cells between the two groups. The B3GALT4 high-expression group had a considerably larger percentage of CD8+ T cells than the B3GALT4 low-expression group (P < 0.05). The cytotoxic gene GZMB and the exhaustion-related gene PDCD1 were then compared between the two groups. In contrast to the B3GALT4 low-expression group, we observed that CD8+ T cells in the B3GALT4 high-expression group had higher expression of the fatigued gene PDCD1 and lower expression of the cytotoxic gene GZMB (Fig. 10B-C).

We further analyzed the protein expression of CD8A, PDCD1, and GZMB in both LNM (-) and LNM (+) breast cancer tissues. The results showed that LNM (-) breast cancer samples had a lower number of CD8+ T cells compared to LNM (+) breast cancer tissues (P < 0.001). Consistent with CD8, LNM (-) breast cancer samples had considerably lower PDCD1 expression than LNM (+) breast cancer tissues (P < 0.001). On the other hand, LNM (-) breast cancer samples had considerably higher GZMB expression than LNM (+) breast cancer tissues (P < 0.001) (Fig. 10D). Together, these results indicated that B3GALT4 correlated with T cell exhaustion in LNM (+) breast cancer.

Discussion

The condition of the lymph nodes significantly influences the stage, course of treatment, and prognosis of breast cancer. To predict lymph node metastasis, prior research has mostly used information from a single imaging sequence; however, these methods have shown only a limited degree of predictive power [23]. Therefore, more accurate methods are desperately needed to determine lymph node status before treatment planning. In this work, we used digital pathology pictures with gene expression data to develop an integrated model to predict LNM in patients with breast cancer. The model demonstrated strong discrimination and calibration in both the derivation and validation cohorts. The prognosis, tumor stage, and subtype of breast cancer may also be predicted using the integrated model. In the end, our approach proved more predictive in evaluating the immunological microenvironment, particularly the function of CD8+ T cells. Remarkably, in breast cancer, B3GALT4 overexpression was substantially linked to a higher incidence of LNM and CD8+ T cell exhaustion.

With the advancement of artificial intelligence, deep learning techniques have increasingly been applied to the analysis of pathological images. Researchers primarily focused on immunochemical images at the single-cell level. As deep learning progressed rapidly, WSIs were analyzed for various purposes, including precise diagnosis [24], cancer prognosis prediction [25], and evaluation of drug resistance [26]. Despite the growing application of WSIs in computational pathology, the development of deep learning models for predicting lymph node involvement remains limited. Previous studies have primarily focused on WSIs-based lymph node prediction in specific cancer types, including gastric cancer [27], prostate cancer [28], and early-stage colorectal carcinoma [29]. Here, we developed a deep learning-based pathological model based on breast cancer WSIs that achieved high predictive performance, as evidenced by consistently strong AUC and sensitivity values in both internal and external validation cohorts. This innovative approach could assist clinicians in making more informed intraoperative decisions.

Although several multigene assays have been employed in breast cancer to predict LNM, more accurate methods are still needed. A key innovation of this study was the multimodal integration of histopathological features with transcriptomic data. By combining deep learning-derived morphological patterns from H&E-stained WSIs with gene expression signatures, our composite model and nomogram demonstrated superior predictive accuracy and enhanced robustness for lymph node assessment. This integrative approach provides a more comprehensive biological characterization of nodal status, capturing both tissue-level architectural features and molecular-level alterations. In this study, we found that combining features from WSIs with DEGs (WFDC2, B3GALT4, and DIO2) in the nomogram provided superior discriminative power for lymph node metastasis prediction, emphasizing the enhancement of predictive accuracy through the application of multimodal data. DCA was performed to evaluate the clinical utility of the models by estimating their net benefit across various threshold probabilities. The combined model demonstrated substantially greater net benefits than the single-predictor model. These results indicate that the integrated models could support diagnostic physicians in making more accurate staging assessments.

One important prognostic marker for breast cancer is the condition of the lymph nodes, which also affects treatment decisions. Through histopathology image analysis, this method has been utilized to estimate pan-cancer prognosis in addition to using deep learning and WSIs to predict lymph node metastasis for particular cancers [30]. In this study, we found that the combined model exhibited robust performance in stratifying breast cancer patients according to survival outcomes. H&E-stained histopathology pictures show various morphological patterns in breast tissue that correspond to various molecular subtypes of breast cancer. We also evaluated our model’s ability to predict the molecular subtypes of breast cancer. Notably, our model exhibited distinct distribution patterns across both molecular subtypes and clinical stages, revealing biologically meaningful correlations between histomorphological patterns and tumor biology. Tafavvoghi et al. [31] employed a One-vs-Rest strategy alongside an XGBoost model to perform molecular subtyping using only H&E-stained WSIs. Their model achieved a macro F1 score of 0.73 for molecular subtyping and an F1 score of 0.95 for distinguishing between tumor and non-tumor tissue. These results suggest that our approach could offer a practical framework for rapidly identifying molecular subtypes and predicting patient prognosis.

According to earlier research, the tumor microenvironment may have an impact on the biological activity, immunotherapy, and prognosis of the tumor and could be a predictor of LNM [32–35]. Comprising functionally varied lymphocyte subsets such as cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, CD4+ helper T cells, and fatigued CD8+ T cells, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) constitute a crucial immunogenic component of the tumor microenvironment (TME) [36]. Takada et al. [37] found that patients with breast cancer who had low tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density had a significantly greater frequency of axillary lymph node metastasis, which is consistent with our findings. The functional dynamics of T cell populations in metastatic versus non-metastatic lymph nodes remain poorly understood, despite the growing awareness of tumor-immune interactions. Moreover, it is unknown how metastatic lymph nodes affect the immunomodulatory processes of the original tumor biology. Wang et al. [38] found that tumor-involved (N+) lymph nodes had greater frequencies of PD-1+ CD8+ and TIM-3+ CD8+ T cells than tumor-uninvolved (N-) nodes. In this investigation, we found that intratumoral CD8+ T cells were more common in LNMTs than PTs. This conclusion aligns Shariati’s work [39], which found a correlation between the number of metastatic lymph nodes in breast cancer and the frequency of CD8+ T cells. The variable expression of immunological checkpoint molecules dynamically regulates the functional plasticity of CD8+ T lymphocytes, allowing for the discrimination of different functional states according to their inhibitory receptor profiles. Yan et al. [40] discovered a unique exhausted CD8+ T cell subpopulation (called LAG3/c8) in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) by using single-cell RNA sequencing analysis. This subpopulation showed co-upregulation of several immune checkpoint molecules, such as lymphocyte-activation gene 3 (LAG-3) and programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1). There is growing evidence that cytotoxic CD8+ T lymphocytes are strongly linked to the presence of lymph node metastases as well as other important clinicopathological prognostic variables in breast cancer, such as hormone receptor status and tumor grade [36]. Our study revealed that compared with PTs, the frequencies of exhausted CD8+ T cells were increased in LNMTs, while the frequencies of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells were decreased in LNMTs. These results raised the possibility of a mechanistic involvement in encouraging lymph node metastases in patients with breast cancer. Consistent with our results, PDCD1+ CD8+ T cells were found at a higher frequency in breast cancer patients with lymph node metastasis [41]. Furthermore, comprehensive functional analyses revealed that CD8+ T cells in LNMTs exhibit significantly impaired cytotoxic activity, as evidenced by reduced granzyme B production and diminished IFN-γ secretion, relative to their counterparts in primary tumors [42]. Collectively, these findings suggest a mechanism by which tumor cells may more readily survive within lymph nodes.

Notably, through comprehensive genomic profiling, we identified and independently validated a novel three-gene signature (WFDC2, B3GALT4, and DIO2) that robustly predicted LNM in breast cancer. B3GALT4 belongs to the β­1,3­galactosyltransferase gene family, which is notably overexpressed in breast cancer tissues [43]. While emerging studies have implicated B3GALT4 in breast cancer tumorigenesis and disease progression [44], its specific role in mediating lymph node metastasis remains unexplored. The current investigation is the first to investigate the role of B3GALT4 in predicting the progression of breast cancer to lymph nodes. B3GALT4 may play a part in encouraging lymphatic metastasis in breast malignancies, as evidenced by the fact that it was found to be strongly expressed in breast cancer tissues with lymphatic metastases. Breast cancer cells were injected into the footpad of naked mice to create a popliteal LN metastasis model, which is a sensitive and quantitative way to measure lymphatic metastasis in vivo, in order to scientifically validate these findings. We discovered that in vivo, B3GALT4 overexpression may significantly promote lymphatic metastasis. Collectively, this study provides the first evidence that B3GALT4 promotes lymphatic metastasis in breast cancer, underscoring its broad clinical relevance in the metastatic process.

Here, we further investigated the connection between CD8+ T cells and B3GALT4 gene expression in breast cancer. First, we examined the connections between B3GALT4 and gene markers of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, exhausted CD8+ T cells, and CD8+ T cells. Based on TCGA data, we found that B3GALT4 expression was negatively correlated with CD274, CD96, and IL-10 and positively correlated with CD8A, TGFβ1, HLA-A, and PDCD1. The immunohistochemical study revealed that whereas GZMB expression was significantly decreased in the B3GALT4 high expression group, PDCD1 was up-regulated in these groups. In contrast to our earlier research, which showed that B3GALT4 was significantly overexpressed in breast cancer tissues and positively connected with a poor prognosis, our study then showed that the density of CD8+ T cells in breast cancer with high B3GALT4 expression was significantly higher than in tissues with low B3GALT4 expression. This could be because the B3GALT4 highly expressed groups had a notably higher number of exhausted CD8+ T cells, which made up a significantly larger percentage of all CD8+ T cells. This might imply that B3GALT4 encouraged CD8+ T cell exhaustion, which subsequently influenced the lymph node spread of breast cancer. It is still necessary to investigate the precise mechanism by which B3GALT4 causes T cell exhaustion.

It is important to emphasize that our study model has some limitations. First, due to the absence of WSIs and RNA-seq data of paired PTs and LNMTs in TCGA dataset, we had to create a lymph node metastasis predictive model in breast cancer by comparing the pathological and transcriptomic information of breast cancer patients with or without lymph node metastasis, which makes it impossible to determine with certainty whether the model will perform similarly on different populations or more heterogeneous groups. Second, One of the main problems faced by machine learning models is the risk of overfitting. Overfitting of the model to certain patterns and noises in the dataset on which it was trained causes the model to fail to show the expected performance on new and different datasets. In this study, data augmentation techniques were applied to minimize the risk of overfitting. However, it is not possible to determine the real-world performance of the model without testing it on various datasets. Third, it was conducted with data from a single hospital, which may restrict the generalization of the findings to other institutional settings. Future efforts will be dedicated to further improving the model’s performance by pretraining on large-scale, multi-center data cohorts.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we identified and tested a novel deep learning-based model that may enhance the prediction of overall prognosis for patients with breast cancer and precisely predict lymph node metastases using genetic data and H&E staining WSIs. Additionally, this study demonstrated that compared to PTs, LNMTs had higher infiltrations of exhausted CD8+ T lymphocytes in the tumor microenvironment. Our findings demonstrated that B3GALT4 expression was significantly correlated with LNM risk and exhibited a strong positive association with tumor-infiltrating exhausted CD8+ T cells. In summary, our findings offer new insights into LNM prediction and may help inform targeted treatments for breast cancer patients.
