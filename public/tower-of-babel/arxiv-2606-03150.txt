Singular limit of lattice graphs
Zhentao He, Chao Ji

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish new connections between lattice graphs and metric grids, providing a unified framework for the study of singular limit problems and Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequalities on lattice graphs. The main technical ingredients are restriction and extension estimates, which enable us to compare variational problems posed on lattice graphs, metric grids and ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . As applications, we first prove that extensions of action ( 2 < p < 2 ∗ 2<p<2^{*} ) and energy ( 2 < p < 2 + 4 d 2<p<2+\frac{4}{d} ) ground states of the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation on d d -dimensional lattice graphs converge strongly in H 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) to the corresponding ground states on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} as the edge length tends to zero. As a by-product of the arguments developed for the singular limit problem on lattice graphs, we obtain multiplicity results for fixed-mass critical points of the energy functional on lattice graphs. Furthermore, employing a strategy analogous to that used in the singular limit analysis, we investigate the optimal constants of Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequalities on lattice graphs for 2 < p < 2 ∗ 2<p<2^{*} . Beyond the classical subcritical framework, we also study the singular limit of action ground states in the Sobolev supercritical regime ( d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and p > 2 ∗ p>2^{*} ), the singular limit of energy ground states in the mass-supercritical regime ( p > 2 + 4 d p>2+\frac{4}{d} ) on lattice graphs, and the optimal constants in Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequalities in the Sobolev critical case d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and p = 2 ∗ p=2^{*} on lattice graphs. Notably, we settle an open problem posed by Dovetta [Adv. Math. 444 (2024), 109633] by establishing a new Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality.

As in

\Delta u(x,y)\approx\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(u(x+\varepsilon,y)+u(x-\varepsilon,y)+u(x,y+\varepsilon)+u(x,y-\varepsilon)-4u(x,y)\right).

Formally, the right-hand side of (

\mathbb{R}^{d}

[

In

restricted to the associated Nehari manifold

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}}:

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}:J^{\prime}_{\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u)u=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}:\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\omega\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\right\}.

Moreover, for every

is also an energy ground state with mass

E_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}

on the mass constraint

H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\mu\right\}.

In this case,

Applying these convergence results, Dovetta also derived qualitative properties of ground states and multiplicity results for fixed-mass critical points on metric grids. 1.1.
The functional setting

For every
d\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
, define

\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}:=\left\{\varepsilon x:=(\varepsilon x_{1},...,\varepsilon x_{d}):x=(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\},

where

\mathbb{Z}^{d}:=\left\{x=(x_{1},...,x_{d}):x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq d\right\}.

We denote by
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\varepsilon}:=(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
the lattice graph (with edge length
\varepsilon
, see e.g. Figure
1
for
d=2,3
) with the set of vertices

\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}:=\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}

and the set of edges

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}=\left\{(x,y):x,y\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d},\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}-y_{i}|=\varepsilon\right\}.

Let us write
x\sim y
(
y
is a neighbour of
x
) if
(x,y)\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
.
We denote the space of functions on
\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
by
C(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
. For
u\in C(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, its support set is defined as
\operatorname{supp}(u):=\{x\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}:u(x)\neq 0\}
. Let
C_{c}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
be the set of all functions with finite support.

Let
\mu
be the counting measure on
\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
, i.e., for any subset
A\subset\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
,
\mu(A):=\#\{x:x\in A\}
. For any function
f
on
\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
, we write

\int_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}fd\mu:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}f(x),

whenever it makes sense.

For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
denotes the linear space of
p
-th integrable functions on
\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}|u(x)|^{p}\right)^{1/p},&1\leq p<\infty,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}|u(x)|,&p=\infty.\end{cases}

For any functions
u,v\in C(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, we define the associated gradient form as

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{d}(u,v)(x):=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x)).

Let
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{d}(u):=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{d}(u,u)
, and define

(1.3)

|\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u|(x):=\sqrt{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{d}(u)(x)}=\left(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{y\sim x}\lvert u(y)-u(x)\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

For brevity, we write
\lVert|\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u|\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}
as
\lVert\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}
.

Let
H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
be the completion of
C_{c}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
under the norm

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}:=\left(\lVert\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}+\lVert u\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\left(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\sum_{y\sim x}\lvert u(y)-u(x)\rvert^{2}+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

For any function
u\in C(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, the graph Laplacian of
u
is defined by

(1.4)

\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{d}u(x):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x)),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}.

1.2.
Main results

We can now state our main results, starting with the ground state problem for the (NLS) equation. Let us begin with action ground states. For every
p>2
and every
\omega>0
, we introduce the action functional
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}:H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}

\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon\omega}{2}\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\|u\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}

and the associated Nehari manifold

(1.5)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}:

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})\backslash\{0\}:\hat{J}^{\prime}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u)u=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})\backslash\{0\}:\|\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}+\varepsilon\omega\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}=\varepsilon\|u\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}\right\}.

Letting

(1.6)

\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega):=\inf_{v\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}}\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(v)

be the corresponding minimization problem,

is called a ground state of
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
if
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u)=\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)
. By
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
, there always exist ground states of
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
in
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
for every
\omega>0
,
\varepsilon>0
and
p>2
. Ground states are solutions of the following (NLS) equation

(1.7)

-\frac{\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{d}}{\varepsilon}u+\omega u=\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}.

In addition, by
[
20
, Theorem B.4]
, ground states have constant sign. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume that they are positive throughout this paper.

where

(1.10)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u):=\frac{\varepsilon\|u\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}^{p}-\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}^{2}}{\varepsilon\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}^{2}}.

Adapting the analysis of
[
31
,
32
]
ensures that there always exist nonnegative ground states of
\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
at mass
\mu
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
\mu>0
and
p\in(2,2+\frac{4}{d})
. Moreover, thanks to the connectivity of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
, we know that the nonnegative ground states of
\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
are positive. For such ground states we have the following convergence result, where we also use the notation

\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mu):=\inf_{v\in H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}E_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
p\in(2,2+\frac{4}{d})
and
\mu>0
be fixed.

(i)

If
d\in\{1,2,3,4\}
and
p\in(2,2+\frac{4}{d})
, or
d\geq 5
and
p\in\left(2,\frac{2^{*}}{2}+1\right]
, then there exists a constant
C_{d,p,\mu}>0
, depending only on
d
,
p
and
\mu
, such that for every
\varepsilon>0
small enough,

\left|\varepsilon^{d-1}\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\left(\frac{\mu}{\varepsilon^{d}}\right)-\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mu)\right|\leq C_{d,p,\mu}\varepsilon.

If
d\geq 5
and
p\in\left(\frac{2^{*}}{2}+1,2+\frac{4}{d}\right)
, then there exists a constant
C_{d,p,\mu}>0
, depending only on
d
,
p
and
\mu
, such that for every
\varepsilon>0
small enough,

\left|\varepsilon^{d-1}\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\left(\frac{\mu}{\varepsilon^{d}}\right)-\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mu)\right|\leq C_{d,p,\mu}\varepsilon^{\frac{d-2}{2}(2^{*}-p)}.

(ii)

For every positive ground state
u_{\varepsilon}
of
\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
in
H^{1}_{\frac{\mu}{\varepsilon^{d}}}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
, there exists
x_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that,

\hat{\mathcal{A}}u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot-x_{\varepsilon})\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}\varphi_{\mu},\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\varphi_{\mu}\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the unique positive ground state of
E_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
at mass
\mu
which attains its maximum at the origin and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is the extension operator from
\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
introduced in Section
3
. Furthermore,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon})=\omega_{\mu},

where
\omega_{\mu}
is the value of the parameter
\omega
for which
\varphi_{\mu}
solves (
1.2
).

As in Theorem
1.2
, we are also interested in the asymptotic behavior of energy ground states of
\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
at mass
\mu/\varepsilon^{d}
for
d\geq 2
and
p>2+\frac{4}{d}
. Now, we consider the case that
d\geq 2
,
p>2+\frac{4}{d}
and
\mu>0
. By adapting the arguments in
[
21
]
, in the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we show that, for any fixed
\mu>0
and for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, there exists a positive ground state
u_{\varepsilon}
of
\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
at mass
\frac{\mu}{\varepsilon^{d}}
.
However, the following theorem shows that the family
\{\hat{\mathcal{A}}u_{\varepsilon}\}
diverges in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

. Therefore, in contrast to Theorems
1.1
-
1.3
,
\{\hat{\mathcal{A}}u_{\varepsilon}\}
cannot converge to any function in

.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
p>2+\frac{4}{d}
and
\mu>0
be fixed. Then

\varepsilon^{d-1}\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\frac{\mu}{\varepsilon^{d}})\rightarrow-\infty,\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

and, for every
\varepsilon>0
small enough, and for any positive ground state
u_{\varepsilon}
of
\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
in
H^{1}_{\frac{\mu}{\varepsilon^{d}}}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
,

\hat{\mathcal{A}}u_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}+\infty,\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is the extension operator from
\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
introduced in Section

.

It is well known that, for every
p\in(2+\frac{4}{d},2^{*})
,
\inf_{v\in H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}E_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v)=-\infty
. Thus, roughly speaking, by Theorem
1.4
, we show that, for
d\geq 2
and
p\in(2+\frac{4}{d},2^{*})
, the energy ground state problem on lattice graphs serves as an asymptotic approximation of energy ground state problem on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

One may also ask whether the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
remains valid on metric grids introduced in
[
11
]
, since it is straightforward to adapt the proof of Theorem
1.2
to the setting of metric grids. However, it appears that repeating the arguments of Theorem
1.4
on metric grids is non-trivial. This difficulty arises because the equality
\lVert\nabla\hat{A}f_{\varepsilon}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\varepsilon^{d-1}\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}^{2}
in (
4.4
) (see also
[
11
, Lemma 4.4]
) plays a crucial role in our arguments, while, on metric grids, by
[
11
, Eq. (39)]
, one only has

\lVert\nabla\mathcal{A}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\varepsilon^{d-1}\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}).

We conclude the presentation of our main results concerning the singular limit problem on lattice graphs with the following remark.

Remark 1.5
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
p\in(2,2^{*})
and
\omega>0
be fixed. For
R>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, let
B_{R}^{d}:=\{\mathrm{w}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}:\operatorname{dist}(\mathrm{w},0)\leq R\}
and

\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\varepsilon B_{R}^{d}}:=\{u\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:u(\mathrm{v})=0\,\,\text{ for every }\,\,\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\varepsilon B_{R}^{d}\}.

Then, it is easy to see that, there exists
u_{\varepsilon,R}
such that

\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u_{\varepsilon,R})=\inf_{v\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\varepsilon B_{R}^{d}}}\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(v)

and, for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\{\mathrm{v}_{\varepsilon,R}\}_{R>0}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
, such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{\varepsilon,R}(\cdot-\mathrm{v}_{\varepsilon,R})\to u_{\varepsilon}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
as
R\to+\infty
, where
u_{\varepsilon}
is a positive ground state of
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
in
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
. Then, by Lemmas
2.2
-
2.3
and
[
11
, Lemma 4.4]
, we know that, up to a subsequence,

\hat{\mathcal{A}}\left(u_{\varepsilon,R}(\cdot-\mathrm{v}_{\varepsilon,R})\right)\to\hat{\mathcal{A}}u_{\varepsilon}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
R\to+\infty
. Therefore, we conclude that, there exists
\{x_{\varepsilon_{n},R_{n}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and
R_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
, and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}u_{\varepsilon_{n},R_{n}}(\cdot-x_{\varepsilon_{n},R_{n}})\to\varphi_{\omega}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
n\to+\infty
, where
\varphi_{\omega}\in\mathcal{N}_{\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is the unique positive ground state of
J_{\omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
which attains its maximum at the origin. In conclusion, we show that, up to a subsequence, extensions of action ground states
u_{\varepsilon,R}
on the ball
B_{R}^{d}
(which also implies that
u_{\varepsilon,R}
has a finite support) converge to the action ground state on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
R\to+\infty
. The same conclusion holds for the energy ground states on lattice graphs under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
and we believe that this result will be very useful for numerical approximations of ground states on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

As a by-product of the arguments developed to prove Theorem
1.3
,
we obtain the following multiplicity result for mass constrained critical points of the energy at large masses on
\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
. For the sake of brevity, we present the result only for
\mathbb{V}_{1}^{d}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
p\in\left(2+\frac{4}{d},2^{*}\right)
. Then there exists
\{\mu_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\mu_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
, such that
\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}
has at least two distinct positive critical points
u_{n,1}
and
u_{n,2}
in
H^{1}_{\mu_{n}}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, where
u_{n,1}
is a positive ground state of
\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}
in
H^{1}_{\mu_{n}}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})
and
u_{n,2}
is a positive ground state of
\hat{J}_{\omega_{n},\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}
for some
\omega_{n}>0
. Moreover, we have

(1.11)

\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(u_{n,2})>\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(u_{n,1})=\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(\mu_{n}),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{+},

and

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(u_{n,1})\to+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(u_{n,2})\to 0,\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty.

The inequality (
1.11
) implies that, for every
d\geq 2
and
p\in\left(2+\frac{4}{d},2^{*}\right)
, there exists
\{\mu_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\mu_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
such that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there exists an action ground state that is not an energy ground state with mass
\mu_{n}
.
This result has to be compared with
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
and
[
28
, Theorem 1.6]
, which prove that energy ground states are necessarily action ground states. Thus, Theorem
1.6
shows that the equivalence between the action and energy ground state problems fails on lattice graphs. A similar result concerning the equivalence between action and energy ground states on metric graphs was established in
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
. There, the author considered the problem on compact metric graphs, noncompact metric graphs with finitely many edges, and
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic metric graphs, but did not cover the case of metric grids
\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1}
.

We now compare Theorem
1.6
with
[
11
, Proposition 2.4]
, which provides the multiplicity results for prescribed mass critical points of the energy functional on metric grids
\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1}
, without proving (
1.11
). On one hand, by repeating the argument in the proof of Theorem

, one can also prove that (
1.11
) holds on
\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1}
. On the other hand, since positive energy ground states exist for all

,

and

large enough (see

]
), Theorem

holds for all

and

, while
[

, Proposition 2.4]

1.2.2.
Optimal constants in Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities

Employing a strategy analogous to the analysis of the singular limit problem on lattice graphs, we now turn our attention to the Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequalities on lattice graphs (see (
2.7
) and (
2.10
)).
For
d\geq 1
and
q\in(2,2^{*})
, or
d\geq 3
and
q=2^{*}
, let

\displaystyle Q_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(u):=\frac{\lVert u\rVert^{q}_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}}{\lVert u\rVert^{d+(2-d)\frac{q}{2}}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}\lVert\nabla^{d}_{1}u\rVert^{(\frac{q}{2}-1)d}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}},\quad

\displaystyle u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})\setminus\{0\},

\displaystyle Q_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v):=\frac{\lVert v\rVert^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\lVert v\rVert^{d+(2-d)\frac{q}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{(\frac{q}{2}-1)d}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}},\quad

\displaystyle v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\},

and denote by

K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}:=\sup_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})\setminus\{0\}}Q_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(u),\quad K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}:=\sup_{v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}Q_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v)

the best constants in the
d
-dimensional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities on
\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}
(see (
2.7
) in Section 2) and on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see, e.g.,
[
11
, Eq. (4)]
). Then, we have the following results.

Theorem 1.7
.

If
d\geq 1
and
q\in(2,2^{*})
, or
d\geq 3
and
q=2^{*}
, then

(1.12)

K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}\geq K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

Furthermore, if
d\geq 1
and
q\in[2+\frac{4}{d},2^{*})
, or
d\geq 3
and
q=2^{*}
, then

(1.13)

K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}=K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}

and
K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}
is not attained for every
d\geq 1
and
q\in(2+\frac{4}{d},2^{*})
.

Since the map
q\to K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is continuous in
[2,2^{*}]
, Theorem
1.7
holds for
d\geq 3
and
q=2^{*}
, a case that was not considered on metric grids in
[
11
, Theorem 2.5]
.

For
d\geq 3
, as in
[
11
, Eq. (11)]
, let

K_{2^{*},\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1}}:=\sup_{v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lVert v\rVert^{2^{*}}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1})}}{\lVert v^{\prime}\rVert^{2^{*}}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1})}}.

By Theorem
1.7
, for
d\geq 3
, we have
K_{2^{*},\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}=K_{2^{*},\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. We note that this result can be easily extended to the best constant of
d
-dimensional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on metric grids (see
[
11
, Eq. (4)]
), i.e., for
d\geq 3
, we have

(1.14)

K_{2^{*},\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1}}=dK_{2^{*},\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

We note that this identity is somewhat surprising in view of the corresponding best constant of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
W^{1,1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1})
. More precisely, as in
[
11
, Section 2]
, for
d\geq 2
, letting

S_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:=\sup_{v\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\frac{d}{d-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}=\frac{(\Gamma(1+\frac{d}{2}))^{\frac{1}{d}}}{d\sqrt{\pi}}\text{ and }S_{\mathcal{G}_{1}^{d}}:=\sup_{v\in W^{1,1}(\mathcal{G}_{1}^{d})}\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\frac{d}{d-1}}(\mathcal{G}_{1}^{d})}}{\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{1}^{d})}}=\frac{1}{(2d)^{\frac{1}{d}}},

elementary computations show that,

dS_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\neq S_{\mathcal{G}_{1}^{d}}^{\frac{d}{d-1}},

which is totally different from (
1.14
).

As in
[
11
, Proposition 2.6]
, for
d\geq 1
and
q\in(2,2+\frac{4}{d}]
, it remains an open problem whether
K_{q,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1}}
is attained. The following proposition provides an existence criterion, which we believe to be the best result currently available in this direction.

Proposition 1.8
.

For every
d\geq 1
and
q\in(2,2+\frac{4}{d}]
, if there exists
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})
such that

Q_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(u)\geq K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}},

then
K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}
is attained.

and

2.
Connections between lattice graphs and metric grids

In this section, we establish connections between Sobolev spaces and ground states for (NLS) equation on lattice graphs and on metric grids. To this end, we first recall some basic settings of metric grids. For every
\varepsilon>0
, let
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}=(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
be the
d
-dimensional metric grid with edge length
\varepsilon
given by the subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with vertices on
\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and edges between every couple of vertices at distance
\varepsilon
.
Each edge
e\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
is identified with a closed and bounded interval
I_{e}=[0,\varepsilon]
.

Consistently, a function
u:\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is actually a family of functions
(u_{\mathrm{e}})
, where
u_{\mathrm{e}}:I_{\mathrm{e}}\to\mathbb{R}
is the restriction of
u
to the edge
\mathrm{e}\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
. The usual
L^{p}
space on the metric grid
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
is defined as follows

L^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}):=\bigoplus_{\mathrm{e}\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}L^{p}(I_{\mathrm{e}}),

endowed with the norm

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}:=\sum_{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lVert u_{\mathrm{e}}\rVert_{L^{p}(I_{\mathrm{e}})}^{p},\quad\text{ if }p\in[1,\infty)\quad\text{ and }\quad\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}:=\sup_{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lVert u_{\mathrm{e}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(I_{\mathrm{e}})}.

The inner product in
L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is

(u,v)_{2}=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}uv\,dx.

If
u:\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, then we write
u\in C(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
. The Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
consists of the set of continuous functions
u:\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
u_{\mathrm{e}}\in H^{1}([0,\varepsilon])
for every edge
\mathrm{e}\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
and
\underset{\mathrm{e}\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}{\sum}\left(\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{\mathrm{e}})}+\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{\mathrm{e}})}\right)<\infty
; the norm in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is defined as

\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}=\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}+\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})},

and the inner product in this space is

(u,v)=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u^{\prime}v^{\prime}+uv)\,dx.

For any
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, define
\hat{u}\in C(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
by
\hat{u}:=u|_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
.

For every
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, define

\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{\hat{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}=\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}+\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}.

For any
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, define
\tilde{f}\in C(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
by

\tilde{f}_{\mathrm{e}}(x):=\frac{f(\mathrm{w})-f(\mathrm{v})}{\varepsilon}x+f(\mathrm{v}),\quad\forall\mathrm{e}=(\mathrm{v},\mathrm{w})\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d},

where, for any edge
\mathrm{e}=(\mathrm{v},\mathrm{w})\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
,
e
is identified with interval
I_{e}=[0,\varepsilon]
,
\mathrm{v}
is identified with
0
, and
\mathrm{w}
is identified with
\varepsilon
.

By repeating the arguments in
[
6
, Lemma 2.3]
, we obtain, for all
d\geq 1
, the following estimates for concentrated nonlinearities on
\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exists a constant
S_{d,p}>0
, depending only on
d
and
p
such that

(2.1)

\left|d\varepsilon\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}|\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})|^{p}-\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}^{p}\right|\leq S_{d,p}\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^{2p-2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}^{p-1}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}).

To show that the extension
\tilde{f}
of
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
belongs to
H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
u\in C(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
satisfies
u_{\mathrm{e}}\in H^{1}([0,\varepsilon])
for every edge
\mathrm{e}\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
and
\lVert u\rVert_{\hat{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}<\infty
, then
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
and

(2.2)

\int_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq 2d\varepsilon\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}+2d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}\,dx.

In addition,
\lVert u\rVert_{\hat{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}
and
\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}
are equivalent on
H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in C(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
satisfy
u_{\mathrm{e}}\in H^{1}([0,\varepsilon])
for every edge
\mathrm{e}\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
and
\lVert u\rVert_{\hat{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}<\infty
. We first prove (
2.2
). For any
\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
and
R>0
, set
B_{R}(\mathrm{v}):=\{x\in\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}:\lvert x-\mathrm{v}\rvert\leq R\}
. For any fixed
\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}\,\max_{x\in\overline{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}\left(\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert+\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}\lvert u^{\prime}(y)\rvert\,dy\right)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}\left(\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}+\left(\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}\lvert u^{\prime}(y)\rvert\,dy\right)^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2d\varepsilon\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}+2d\varepsilon\left(\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}\lvert u^{\prime}(y)\rvert\,dy\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2d\varepsilon\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}+2d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}\,dx,

hence,

\int_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx=\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq 2d\varepsilon\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}+2d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}\,dx.

Since
\lVert u\rVert_{\hat{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}<\infty
, we conclude that
\int_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx<+\infty
, and thus,
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
. Moreover, by (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
, we know that
\lVert u\rVert_{\hat{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}
and
\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}
are equivalent on
H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})

∎

Now, we can establish connections between the Sobolev spaces on lattice graphs and on metric grids.
It is easy to see that, for any
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
,

(2.3)

\lVert\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}f\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\sum_{\mathrm{w}\sim\mathrm{v}}\lvert f(\mathrm{w})-f(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\tilde{f}^{\prime}\rvert^{2}\,dx=\lVert\tilde{f}^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}.

Thus, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, then
\hat{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
and

\lVert\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}\hat{u}\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}\leq\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}.

In addition, if
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, then
\tilde{f}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
and

\lVert\tilde{f}^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}=\lVert\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}f\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}.

Proof.

Firstly, let
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
. Then, by Lemma
2.1
and the equivalence of spaces
H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
and
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
(see, e.g.,
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
), we know that
\hat{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
. Moreover, by (
2.3
) and
[
11
, Eq. (26)]
, we obtain

\lVert\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}\hat{u}\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}=\lVert\tilde{\hat{u}}^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}\leq\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}.

Now, let
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
. Then, by (
2.3
), we know that
\lVert\tilde{f}^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}=\lVert\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}f\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}
and
\tilde{f}
satisfies
\tilde{f}_{\mathrm{e}}\in H^{1}([0,\ell_{\mathrm{e}}])
for every edge
\mathrm{e}\in\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}^{d}
and
\lVert\tilde{f}\rVert_{\hat{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}<\infty
. Hence, by Lemma
2.2
, we conclude that
\tilde{f}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
.
∎

As a direct consequence of Lemma
2.3
, we prove the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequalities for concentrated nonlinearities on
\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
, which play a key role in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. For any
d\geq 1
, the following inequality holds

(2.4)

\sup_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}{\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert}\leq\sqrt{2}(d\varepsilon)^{1/4}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/4},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}).

Moreover, for any
d\geq 1
and any
p\in[2,2^{*})
, there exists a constant
C_{d,p}>0
, depending only on
d
and
p
such that the following inequality holds

(2.5)

\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}\leq C_{d,p}(\varepsilon^{1/2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})})^{\beta_{p}p}\left(\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}\right)^{(1-\beta_{p})p/2},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}),

where
\beta_{p}:=d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to prove that the following inequalities hold

(2.6)

\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}\leq\sqrt{2}(d\varepsilon)^{1/4}\|\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{1/2}\|u\|^{1/2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}),

and there exists a constant
C_{d,p}>0
, depending only on
d
and
p
such that

(2.7)

\|u\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}\leq C_{d,p}(\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})})^{\beta_{p}p}\|u\|^{(1-\beta_{p})p}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}),

where
\beta_{p}:=d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)
.

We first prove (
2.6
). Recall that
C_{c}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is the set of all functions with finite support. For
u\in C_{c}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
, let
\mathrm{v}_{0},\mathrm{w}_{0}
be such that
\lvert u(\mathrm{v}_{0})\rvert=\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}
and
u(\mathrm{w}_{0})=0
. Denote the path connecting
\mathrm{v}_{0}
and
\mathrm{w}_{0}
by
\{\mathrm{v}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{k}
, where
\mathrm{v}_{k}=\mathrm{w}_{0}
. Then

\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}=\lvert u(\mathrm{v}_{0})\rvert\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lvert u(\mathrm{v}_{i})-u(\mathrm{v}_{i+1})\rvert\leq\sum_{(\mathrm{v},\mathrm{w})\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert u(\mathrm{v})-u(\mathrm{w})\rvert,

where we identify
(\mathrm{v},\mathrm{w})\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
and
(\mathrm{w},\mathrm{v})\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
as the same element in
\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
, thus, by Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(\mathrm{v},\mathrm{w})\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\left|\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}-\lvert u(\mathrm{w})\rvert^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\mathrm{v},\mathrm{w})\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\left|{u(\mathrm{v})}-{u(\mathrm{w})}\right|\left||{u(\mathrm{v})}|+|{u(\mathrm{w})}|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{(\mathrm{v},\mathrm{w})\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\left|{u(\mathrm{v})}-{u(\mathrm{w})}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{(\mathrm{v},\mathrm{w})\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\left(\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert+\lvert u(\mathrm{w})\rvert\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\sum_{y\sim x}\lvert u(y)-u(x)\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(2\sum_{(\mathrm{v},\mathrm{w})\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\left(\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}+\lvert u(\mathrm{w})\rvert^{2}\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}\cdot 2\sqrt{d}\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}=2\sqrt{d\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})},

which implies (
2.6
) since
H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is the completion of
C_{c}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
under the norm
\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}
.

By (
2.6
), we obtain (
2.7
) with
d=1
. For the proof of (
2.7
) with
d\geq 2
, one can refer to
[
32
, Eq. (4.11)]
or derive it using the discrete Sobolev inequality (see, e.g.,
[
25
, Theorem 3.6]
) and interpolation inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. For any
d\geq 3
, there exists a constant
C_{d,2^{*}}
, depending only on
d
such that the following inequalities hold

(2.8)

\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2^{*}}\leq C_{d,2^{*}}(\varepsilon^{1/2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})})^{2^{*}},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}),

and

(2.9)

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}\leq(C_{d,2^{*}}^{1/2^{*}}+2^{-1/2})\varepsilon^{1/2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}).

Proof.

Let
d\geq 3
. The Sobolev type inequality (
2.8
) follows directly from Lemma
2.3
and the following discrete Sobolev inequality (see, e.g.,
[
25
, Theorem 3.6]
)

(2.10)

\lVert f\rVert^{2^{*}}_{\ell^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}\leq C_{d,2^{*}}\lVert\nabla^{d}_{1}f\rVert^{2^{*}}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})},\quad\forall f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}).

Now, we prove (
2.9
). By (
2.8
), we have

(2.11)

\max_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert\leq\left(\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2^{*}}\right)^{1/2^{*}}\leq C_{d,2^{*}}^{1/2^{*}}\varepsilon^{1/2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}).

For any
x\in\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
, we know that, there exists
\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
such that
x\in B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})
, where
B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v}):=\{x\in\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}:\lvert x-\mathrm{v}\rvert\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\}
. Thus, by (
2.11
), for any
x\in\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
such that
x\in B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})
, we obtain that

\lvert u(x)\rvert\leq\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert+\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathrm{v})\cap\mathrm{e}}\lvert u^{\prime}(y)\rvert\,dy\leq\max_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert+\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}\leq(C_{d,2^{*}}^{1/2^{*}}+2^{-1/2})\varepsilon^{1/2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})},

where
\mathrm{e}\in\mathbb{E}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
such that
\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}
and
x
belonging to
\mathrm{e}
. By the above inequality, we complete the proof of Lemma
2.5
.
∎

For every
p>2
and every
\omega>0
, we introduce the action functional
\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}:H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}

\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon\omega}{2}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}

and the associated Nehari manifold

(2.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}:

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})\backslash\{0\}:\tilde{J}^{\prime}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u)u=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})\backslash\{0\}:\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}+\varepsilon\omega\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}=\varepsilon\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}\right\}.

Letting

(2.13)

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega):=\inf_{v\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}}\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(v)

be the corresponding minimization problem,
u\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
is called a ground state of
\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
if
\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u)=\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
d\geq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
,
p>2
and
\omega>0
,

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)=\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)>0.

In addition, if
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is a ground state of
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
, then
\tilde{f}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is a ground state of
\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
. Moreover, if
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
is a ground state of
\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
, then
\hat{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
is a ground state of
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
.

Proof.

Fix
d\geq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
,
p>2
and
\omega>0
. We first prove

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)=\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)>0.

By
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
, there exists a ground state
f
of
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
in
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
satisfying that
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(f)=(1/2-1/p)\varepsilon\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}>0
. Moreover, by (
2.3
), we know that
\tilde{f}\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
and

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)\leq\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\tilde{f})=\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(f)=\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega).

Therefore, to prove that
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)=\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)
, it suffices to show that

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)\geq\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)
.
For any
u\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
, by Lemma
2.2
, we know that

\varepsilon\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}=\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}+\varepsilon\omega\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}>0.

Thus,
\|\hat{u}\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}=\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}>0
. Define

\hat{t}_{\hat{u}}:=\left(\frac{\|\nabla_{\varepsilon}^{d}\hat{u}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}+\varepsilon\omega\|\hat{u}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}}{\varepsilon\|\hat{u}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}.

Then, we have that
\hat{t}_{\hat{u}}\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we know that
\hat{t}_{\hat{u}}\leq 1
, hence,

\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\hat{t}_{\hat{u}}\hat{u})=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon\hat{t}_{\hat{u}}^{p}\|\hat{u}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon\hat{t}_{\hat{u}}^{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}=\hat{t}_{\hat{u}}^{p}\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u)\leq\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u).

Since
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\hat{t}_{\hat{u}}\hat{u})\geq\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)
, we conclude that
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)\geq\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)=\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(f)>0
. Therefore,

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)=\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)>0.

It is clear that if
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is a ground state of
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
, then
\tilde{f}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is a ground state of
\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
.

Now, if
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
be a ground state of
\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
. Then, by repeating the arguments in
[
22
, Lemma 6.1]
, we have
\tilde{\hat{u}}=u
, hence, by Lemma
2.3
,
\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
and

\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(\hat{u})=\tilde{J}_{\omega,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u)=\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(\omega)=\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(\omega),

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

For every
p>2
and every
\mu>0
, we introduce the energy functional
\tilde{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}:H^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}

\tilde{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}

and the mass constraint

H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}):=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}):\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2}=\mu\right\}.

Due to the continuous embedding
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})\hookrightarrow\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
, the minimization problem

(2.14)

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\mu):=\inf_{v\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}\tilde{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(v)

is well defined. Moreover, a function
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}({\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}})
is called a ground state of
\tilde{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
at mass
\mu
if

\tilde{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(u)=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\mu).

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
d\geq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
,
p>2
and
\mu>0
,

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\mu)=\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\mu).

In addition, if
f\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is a ground state of
\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
at mass
\mu
, then
\tilde{f}\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})
is a ground state of
\tilde{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
at mass
\mu
. Moreover, if
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
is a ground state of
\tilde{E}_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
at mass
\mu
, then
\hat{u}\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
is a ground state of
\tilde{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
at mass
\mu
.

Proof.

Fix
d\geq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
,
p>2
and
\mu>0
. It follows from Lemma
2.3
that

\tilde{E}_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(\tilde{f})=\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(f)\quad\text{and }\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\left|\hat{\tilde{f}}(\mathrm{v})\right|^{2}=\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}\left|f(\mathrm{v})\right|^{2}=\mu,\quad\forall f\in H_{\mu}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})

and

\hat{E}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(\hat{u})\leq\tilde{E}_{\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u)\quad\text{and }\lVert\hat{u}\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}=\mu,\quad\forall u\in H_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}).

Therefore, we have

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\mu)=\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\mu)
, which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We conclude this section with the following lemma, which characterizes the asymptotic behavior of ground states for concentrated nonlinearities on
\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
p\in(2,2^{*})
, for every
\varepsilon>0
small enough, let
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
be such that

(2.15)

\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}\leq\varepsilon^{d-1}\|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2},\,\,\,\varepsilon^{d}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{2},\,\,\,\varepsilon^{d}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}\leq C^{\prime}

for a suitable constant
C^{\prime}>0
, independent of
\varepsilon

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{g}_{n}\rVert^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon_{n}})}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon_{n}d\lVert g_{n}\rVert^{q}_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon_{n}})}-C\varepsilon_{n}\|\tilde{g}_{n}\|_{L^{2q-2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon_{n}})}^{q-1}\|\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon_{n}})}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon_{n}d\lVert g_{n}\rVert^{q}_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon_{n}})}-C\varepsilon_{n}^{q/2+1}\lVert\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon_{n}})}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon_{n}d\lVert g_{n}\rVert^{q}_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon_{n}})}-C\varepsilon_{n}^{q+1},\quad\text{for}\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\,\,\text{large},

thus, by arguing as in (
2.22
), and using (
5.6
) and
[
11
, Lemma 4.4]
, we conclude that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\lVert\hat{\mathcal{A}}g_{n}\rVert^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{d-1}}{d}\lVert\tilde{g}_{n}\rVert^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon_{n}})}-C\varepsilon_{n}\lVert\hat{\mathcal{A}}g_{n}\rVert^{q-1}_{L^{2q-2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\nabla\hat{\mathcal{A}}g_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon_{n}^{d}\lVert g_{n}\rVert^{q}_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}-C\varepsilon_{n}^{q+d}-C\varepsilon_{n}^{1+d/2+{d/2}}\left(\varepsilon_{n}^{q-1}\lVert\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon_{n}})}^{2q-2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{n}^{d}\lVert f_{n}\rVert^{q}_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}-C\varepsilon_{n}^{q+d},\quad\text{for}\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\,\,\text{large}.

Since
\{\hat{\mathcal{A}}g_{n}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it follows from (
5.6
)-(
5.9
) that

\displaystyle K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}Q_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}g_{n})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert\hat{\mathcal{A}}g_{n}\rVert^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\lVert\hat{\mathcal{A}}g_{n}\rVert^{d+(2-d)\frac{q}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\nabla\hat{\mathcal{A}}g_{n}\rVert^{(\frac{q}{2}-1)d}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert f_{n}\rVert^{q}_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{(\frac{q}{2}-1)d}}=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert f_{n}\rVert^{q}_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}}{\lVert\nabla^{d}_{1}f_{n}\rVert^{(\frac{q}{2}-1)d}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}}=K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}},

which, together with (
1.12
), completes the proof of (
1.13
).
∎

Proof of proposition
1.8
.

From Theorem
1.7
, we know that for any
d\geq 1
and
q\in(2,2+\frac{4}{d}]
,
K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}\geq K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. If
K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}=K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and there exists
a function
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})
such that
Q_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(u)=K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, then conclusion holds immediately.

Now suppose that
K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}>K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
for any
d\geq 1
and
q\in(2,2+\frac{4}{d}]
. Choose
\{f_{n}\}\subset H_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})
such that
Q_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(f_{n})=K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}-\frac{1}{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
large. As in the proof of Theorem
1.7
, we know that
\{f_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})
, and, without loss of generality, we may assume that

\lvert f_{n}(0)\rvert=\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}.

Hence, up to a subsequence, there exists
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})
such that
f_{n}\rightharpoonup f
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})
as
n\to+\infty
, and
f_{n}(\mathrm{v})\to f(\mathrm{v})
for every
\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}
as
n\to+\infty
. By Fatou’s lemma, we have
m:=\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}\in[0,1]
. On one hand, if
m\in(0,1)
, then, by arguing as in the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 2.5]
, we will obtain
K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}<K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}
, which is a contradiction. On the other hand, if
m=0
, then, we have
f\equiv 0
, and thus

(5.10)

\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}=\lvert f_{n}(0)\rvert\to 0,\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty.

It follows from
Q_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(f_{n})=K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}-\frac{1}{n}
that

Q_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(f_{n})=\frac{\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}^{q}}{\lVert\nabla^{d}_{1}f_{n}\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}^{(\frac{q}{2}-1)d}}\leq\frac{\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}^{q-2}}{\lVert\nabla^{d}_{1}f_{n}\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}^{(\frac{q}{2}-1)d}},

thus, by (
5.10
), we obtain that

\lVert\nabla^{d}_{1}f_{n}\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})}\to 0,\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty.

Now, by repeating the arguments in the last part of the proof of Theorem
1.7
, we obtain

K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}\geq\lim_{n\to+\infty}Q_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(f_{n})=K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}

which is a contradiction since we assume that
K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}>K_{q,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. Therefore, we obtain that
m=1
, which, by
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
, implies that
f_{n}\to f
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1})
. Thus, we have that
Q_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}(f)=K_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}
, which completes the proof of Proposition
1.8
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The arguments in this section can be adapted directly to

\tilde{K}_{q,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1}}:=\sup_{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}\lvert\hat{u}(v)\rvert^{q}}{\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{(\frac{q}{2}-1)d}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1})}\left(\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}\lvert\hat{u}(v)\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}+(2-d)\frac{q}{4}}}.

Indeed, by Lemma
2.3
, if
u
is a maximizer of
\tilde{K}_{q,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1}}
, then
\hat{u}
is a maximizer of
\hat{K}_{q,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{1}}
and
\tilde{\hat{u}}
is a maximizer of
\tilde{K}_{q,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{1}}
, and the same conclusion holds for maximization sequences.

6.
Further remarks and open problems

In this section, we pose several interesting open problems about (NLS) equation on lattice graphs with Sobolev critical exponent. Firstly, we present a possibility to improve Theorem
1.6
for the Sobolev critical case. Then, due to the Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality (
2.7
), we introduce the Sobolev space
D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
on metric grids. On
D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
, for the Sobolev supercritical cases, on metric grids, we can obtain the existence and singular limit results of elliptic equations or the attainability of best constants in Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality, while, for the Sobolev critical case, these results remain unknown. Finally, we propose some interesting questions about singular limit of solutions to the (NLS) on lattice graphs with Sobolev critical exponent.

6.1.
A possibility to improve Theorem
1.6

The following lemma is the analogue of Lemma
3.2
for the case
d\geq 3
and
p\geq 2^{*}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For every
d\geq 3
,
p\geq 2^{*}
and
\omega>0
, there exist
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
and
C^{\prime}>0
, depending only on
p
,
\omega
and
d
, such that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\bar{\varepsilon})
, if
f\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
is a ground state of
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
, then

(6.1)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{d-1}\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2},\,\,\varepsilon^{d}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2},\,\,\varepsilon^{d}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}\leq C^{\prime},

and

(6.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{p+2}{p-2}}\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2},\,\,\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{p-2}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2},\,\,\varepsilon^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}\geq\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Let
d\geq 3
,
p\geq 2^{*}
and
\omega>0
. Let
f\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
be a ground state of
\hat{J}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
. By Lemma
2.6
and repeating the arguments in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we know that, for every
\varepsilon>0
small enough,

\varepsilon^{d-1}\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)=\varepsilon^{d-1}\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)\leq C.

Then, by the definition of
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
given in (
1.5
), we have that for
\varepsilon>0
small enough

\varepsilon^{d-1}\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2},\,\varepsilon^{d}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2},\,\varepsilon^{d}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}\leq C.

By Lemma
2.3
, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequalities (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), and the definition of
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
given in (
1.5
), one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}\leq\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}+\varepsilon\omega\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p-2^{*}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon(\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})})^{2^{*}}(\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})})^{p-2^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1+p/2}\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p},

that is,

\varepsilon^{\frac{p+2}{p-2}}\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}\geq C.

Thus, by repeating the arguments in the proof of
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have

\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}\geq C\varepsilon\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}\geq C\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{2-p}}.

Moreover, by the definition of
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\omega,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
given in (
1.5
), we obtain that

\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{p}\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}\geq C\varepsilon^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}.

∎

By Lemma
6.1
, we know that
\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{p-2}}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}
, where
f\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{1,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
is a ground state of
\hat{J}_{1,\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}
.
If, for
d\geq 3
and
p=2^{*}
, we can prove that
\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{2^{*}-2}}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon}^{d})}^{2}\to+\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, then, by repeating the arguments in the proof of Theorem
1.6
, we can improve the range of
p
in Theorem
1.6
from
(2+\frac{4}{d},2^{*})
to
(2+\frac{4}{d},2^{*}]
.

6.2.
Supercritical ground states in
D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})

For
d\geq 3
, let
D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
denote the completion of
H^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
with respect to the norm

\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}
. By Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality (
2.9
), we know that
D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})\hookrightarrow C(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
. Therefore, by
[
10
, Lemma 2.4]
or the Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality for
p=+\infty
(see
[
4
, Section 2]
), we have

\lim_{\lvert x\rvert\to+\infty}\lvert u(x)\rvert\to 0,\quad\forall u\in D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}),

where, for
x\in\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}
,
\lvert x\rvert:=\operatorname{dist}(x,0)
.
For
d\geq 3
,
p>2^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
, define

\mathcal{S}_{d,p,\varepsilon}:=\sup_{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}}{\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}}.

Then, by repeating the arguments in the proof of
[
24
, Theorem 1]
and using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{d}
(see, e.g.,
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
), we know that, for every
d\geq 3
,
p>2^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there always exists a positive maximizer for
\mathcal{S}_{d,p,\varepsilon}
.
For
d\geq 3
,
p\geq 2^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we introduce the following functional
\tilde{J}_{p,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})\to\mathbb{R}

\tilde{J}_{p,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}

and the associated Nehari manifold

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:=\left\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}):\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}=\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}\right\}.

Denote by

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{p,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:=\inf_{u\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}}\tilde{J}_{p,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u).

It is standard to prove that, for every
d\geq 3
,
p>2^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
,
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{p,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
is attained by some
u_{\varepsilon}\in D^{1,2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})
. Moreover, for every fixed
d\geq 3
and
p>2^{*}
, one can also repeat the arguments in the proof of Theorem
1.2
to show that
\varepsilon^{d-1}\lVert u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

However, the existence of maximizers for
\mathcal{S}_{d,2^{*},\varepsilon}
or minimizers of
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{p,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
remains unknown. Since the corresponding problem on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is attainable, we conjecture that both
\mathcal{S}_{d,2^{*},\varepsilon}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{p,\mathcal{G}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
are attainable.

6.3.
Singular limit problems in the Sobolev critical case

Let
d\geq 3
. We introduce the action functional involving a critical Sobolev exponent
J_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}

J_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\lVert u\rVert^{2^{*}}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and the associated Nehari manifold

\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:=\left\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\lVert u\rVert^{2^{*}}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\}.

Denote by

\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}J_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u)

the corresponding ground state problem.

Let
d\geq 3
and
\alpha>0
be fixed. For lattice graphs, we introduce the following functional involving a critical Sobolev exponent
\hat{J}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})\to\mathbb{R}

\hat{J}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha+1}}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{*}}\lVert u\rVert^{2^{*}}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}

and the associated Nehari manifold

\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}):\lVert\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon^{\alpha+1}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}=\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert^{2^{*}}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}\right\}.

Denote by

\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:=\inf_{u\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}}\hat{J}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u)

the corresponding ground state problem.

By cutting off the ground state of
J_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, it is easy to see that

\varepsilon^{d-1}\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\leq\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}+o(1)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

and, for the ground state
f_{\varepsilon}
of
\hat{J}_{\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}
, for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,

\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}\leq\varepsilon^{d-1}\lVert\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})},\,\,\varepsilon^{d}\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\rVert^{2^{*}}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}\leq C^{\prime}\quad\text{and }\varepsilon^{d+\alpha}\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}\leq C^{\prime}.

However, since Lemma
2.8
does not hold for
p=2^{*}
, for
t_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
v_{\varepsilon}:=t_{\varepsilon}\hat{\mathcal{A}}f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, it appears to be quite challenging to show

J_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v_{\varepsilon})\leq\varepsilon^{d-1}\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{d}}(\omega)+o(1)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

A similar problem could be stated as follows:

Let
d\geq 3
. For
p>2^{*}
, we introduce the following functional
\hat{J}_{p,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:D^{1,2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})\to\mathbb{R}

\hat{J}_{p,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}

and the associated Nehari manifold

\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{p,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:=\left\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}):\lVert\nabla^{d}_{\varepsilon}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}=\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon})}\right\}.

Denote by

\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{p,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}:=\inf_{u\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{p,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}}\hat{J}_{p,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}(u)

the corresponding ground state problem. It is an interesting problem to determine whether

\varepsilon^{d-1}\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{2^{*}+\varepsilon,\mathbb{V}^{d}_{\varepsilon}}\to\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.
