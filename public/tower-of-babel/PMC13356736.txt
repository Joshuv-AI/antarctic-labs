Selenium nanoparticle-modified probiotics attenuate deoxynivalenol-induced intestinal injury in chickens via glutathione-mediated metabolic pathway
Authors: Ke Li, Yebo Wang, Minjie Zhang, Xinyi Liu, Honglei Tian, Ying Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Deoxynivalenol (DON), one of the most prevalent mycotoxins, primarily targets the intestine causing oxidative stress and inflammatory storm. Meanwhile, Selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs) have been demonstrated to possess multiple physiological functions, including anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities. We successfully synthesized biogenic SeNPs with zero-valent and 142 ± 23.5 nm particle size using the intrinsic oxidoreductase enzymes and metabolic machinery of Lactobacillus rhamnosus CICC 20257 (Lr) to efficiently reduce sodium selenite through a biological process. This study intends to investigate the protective mechanism by which Lr-SeNPs alleviates DON-induced intestinal damage in chickens. Fifteen Hy-Line Variety Brown roosters were randomly divided into three groups: Control, DON (2.5 mg/kg), and DON (2.5 mg/kg) + Lr-SeNPs (0.5 mg Se/kg). The results showed that Lr-SeNPs exhibited excellent safety and antioxidant activity, and effectively attenuated DON-induced intestinal oxidative stress, inflammatory responses, and epithelial hyperpermeability. In addition, 16S rRNA sequencing and untargeted metabolomics revealed that Lr-SeNPs modulated microbial diversity, promoted the enrichment of beneficial bacteria such as Faecalibacterium, Agathobaculum, and Prevotellaceae, and reduced the abundance of Helicobacter. Furthermore, Lr-SeNPs enhanced the production of metabolites including glutamine, L-glutamic acid, and taurine. Integrative multi-omics analysis identified the glutathione metabolic pathway as a candidate mechanism, and subsequent L-BSO inhibition experiments confirmed the involvement in the protective effect of Lr‑SeNPs. These findings provide a theoretical basis for the application of Lr-SeNPs in modulating gut microbiota and metabolism, as well as its potential application in poultry nutrition and health.

Introduction

Deoxynivalenol (DON) is a prominent trichothecene mycotoxin synthesized by Fusarium species, notably Fusarium graminearum and Fusarium culmorum. DON commonly contaminates cereal grains including wheat, barley, and corn, among the most frequently mycotoxins detected in cereals and cereal-derived processed foods (Lee and Ryu, 2017; Panisson et al., 2023). 85% of corn samples and 98% of small grain samples in Poland had been contaminated with DON, with 24 samples above the EU's recommended mycotoxin limits (Kosicki et al., 2016). The inherent high-temperature and acid-base resistance of DON complicated its removal in conventional food processing, rendering them a significant danger to worldwide food safety and human health. Wheat, a primary cereal crop and significant feedstock for animal husbandry, is often contaminated by DON, leading to serious contamination of livestock feed. Exposure to elevated amounts could elicit detrimental consequences, including diarrhea, vomiting, leukocytosis, and gastrointestinal bleeding in animals, with humans, livestock and poultry being particularly susceptible to the toxic effects of DON (Faeste et al., 2019; Khoshal et al., 2019).

The intestine, as the primary site for nutrient digestion and absorption, as well as the first barrier against toxins, is highly vulnerable to DON exposure. DON can cause villous atrophy, oxidative stress, impaired barrier function, increased permeability, immune dysregulation, lymphocyte infiltration, and disruption of gut microbial diversity (Wang and Hogan, 2019; de Souza et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2024a). It promotes intracellular ROS accumulation and antioxidant deficiency, triggering inflammatory responses that lead to apoptosis and ferroptosis (Zhu et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025). Furthermore, DON activates the NF-kB signaling pathway, up-regulating pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β (Yu et al., 2021). Persistent intestinal inflammation will affect the growth performance of poultry, including reduced feed intake, abnormal food digestion and absorption, and low meat production, resulting in economic losses (Zhang et al., 2022).

Selenium (Se), an essential trace element that occurs naturally in forms such as sodium selenite and sodium selenate, which are not directly utilizable by body (González-Salitre et al., 2023). Studies indicate that sodium selenite can be converted into zero-valent selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs), a process that reduces its cytotoxicity and results in nanoparticles with higher bioavailability and lower toxicity than conventional selenium forms (Hosnedlova et al., 2018; Jiang et al., 2022). Preliminary studies suggest that SeNPs exhibit significant potential in antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-tumor applications. They demonstrate strong scavenging ability on O2• and ABTS free radicals and enhance the activity of antioxidant enzymes, including glutathione peroxidase, superoxide dismutase, and catalase in mice (Bai et al., 2017; Liu et al., 2021). SeNPs effectively suppress the production of IL-1β and IL-6 in LPS-induced RAW 264.7 macrophages (Sun et al., 2023). Moreover, Se deficiency leads to increased expression of inflammatory cytokines and gastrointestinal diseases. (Gao et al., 2016). The antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects of Se are mediated by the glutathione antioxidant system. Glutathione (GSH), a tripeptide containing glutamate, cysteine, and glycine with a reactive thiol group (-SH) and a γ-amide bond, is central to cellular antioxidant defense (Zhou et al., 2022; Xue et al., 2025). The glutathione metabolic pathway plays a pivotal role in mitigating oxidative stress, and maintaining redox homeostasis (represented by the GSH/GSSG ratio)(Zhou et al., 2022). Notably, selenium is essential for the activity of GSH-PX, a key enzyme in GSH metabolism. Li et al. demonstrated that selenium enhances antioxidant capacity and alleviates oxidative stress in chickens exposed to toxic pollutants (Li et al., 2024). Similarly, Ali et al. found that nano-selenium supplementation increased GSH levels and suppressed the elevation of TNF-α and IL-1β (Ali et al., 2020).

Biosynthesis has emerged as a promising approach for producing SeNPs, utilizing bacteria or fungi to convert inorganic selenium into biocompatible nanoparticles. This method offers advantages such as high biosafety, efficient utilization, environmental friendliness, and low cost (Zhong et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b). As a well-known probiotic, Lactobacillus rhamnosus (Lr) has been widely used and studied as a model probiotic strain, which has shown promising prospects in IBD treatment (Liao et al., 2025). Although Lr is a well-established probiotic capable of colonizing the gastrointestinal tract, modulating gut microbial communities, and reinforcing intestinal barrier integrity, its antioxidant efficacy is relatively limited. Conversely, while selenium can increase the activity of glutathione peroxidase, it lacks the ability to regulate gut microbiota composition or intestinal homeostasis. In this study, we harnessed the intrinsic redox enzyme system of Lr to synthesize zero-valent SeNPs, thereby constructing a SeNPs-modified Lr (Lr-SeNPs). Lr serves a dual function, acting both as a biofactory and a delivery vehicle for the biosynthesis of SeNPs. By controlling the culture duration, SeNPs were naturally loaded on the surface of Lr cells, forming a Se-enriched protective layer. Previous studies have demonstrated that Se embedded in the pericellular film significantly enhance the resistance of lactic acid bacteria and improved survival under the hostile microenvironment associated with intestinal inflammation (Guo et al., 2024). Consequently, Lr-SeNPs may simultaneously exert microbiota-regulatory, barrier-protective, and antioxidant activities, providing a promising strategy to overcome the individual limitations of probiotics or selenium supplementation alone. Furthermore, this study explored the mechanism of Lr-SeNPs alleviate the chicken intestinal damage in multiple aspects through synergistic regulation of gut microbiota and metabolites analysis.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

Deoxynivalenol (DON) was purchased from Pribolab company (Shandong, China). IPEC−J2 cells were purchased from Icell bioscience company (Shanghai, China). Hy-Line Variety Brown Rooster were purchased from from a local commercial hatchery. 1640 medium, fetal bovine serum (FBS), and antibiotic solution were purchased from Siji chun company (Zhejiang, China). DPPH and ABTS were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Darmstadt, Germany). CCK−8 assay kits and Annexin V−FITC/PI assay kits were purchased from Solarbio company (Beijing, China). L-Buthionine-(S,R)-sulfoximine (L-BSO) were purchased from TargetMol (Boston, USA). Superoxide Dismutase (SOD) assay kit, Catalase (CAT) assay kit, Reduced glutathione (GSH) assay kit and Malondialdehyde (MDA) assay kit were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Jiangsu, China).

Preparation of Lr-SeNPs, Lr, and SeNPs

The cryopreserved strain Lactobacillus rhamnosus CICC 20257 was activated in 100 mL of MRS broth under shaking at 37°C for 24 h. Lr was prepared through 36 h cultivation of 5% activated Lactobacillus rhamnosus CICC 20257 in 100 mL MRS broth. Activated inoculum was cultured into 100 mL of MRS medium containing 1mg/mL sodium selenite for 36 h under shaking, and Lr-SeNPs, which is the composite of Lr and SeNPs, was collected by centrifuging at 7000 rpm for 5 min subsequently. SeNPs was extracted after 72 h cultivation according to the reported method (Wang et al., 2025b). After cultivation, bacterial cells were collected from the broth and resuspended in deionized water. Typically, 100 µL of lysozyme solution was added to the bacterial suspension, followed by incubation at 37°C for 2 h and subsequent sonication. A mixture containing 1% SDS and 1.5 M Tris-HCl was then added to wash the sample, which was subsequently centrifuged at 6000 rpm for 10 min. After centrifugation, 1-octanol was added to the precipitate, and the mixture was left at 4°C for 24 h. The aqueous phase was then transferred to a clean tube and washed with ethanol. Finally, the precipitate was collected by centrifugation and washing, and the resulting SeNPs were obtained. All the three materials were washed and redispersed in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and stored in a dark place at 4°C for experiments.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis

The obtained Lr-SeNPs, Lr, and SeNPs were freeze-dried into powder form through Vacuum Freeze Dryer (FD8-6, SIM, USA). Subsequently, the powder samples were mounted on conductive adhesive, sputter-coated with gold and examined via FESEM (SU8220, Hitachi, Japan) to observe their microstructural features. Subsequently, the particle size of nano-selenium was analyzed using the ImageJ software.

Flow cytometry analyses

IPEC-J2 cells were grown in T25 flasks using 1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% antibiotics, maintained in incubator at 37°C in 5% CO2. After reaching stable growth, cells were divided into four groups: Control, Lr, and Lr-SeNPs and SeNPs. The Lr-SeNPs and SeNPs groups were treated with 6 mg Se/L for 24 h, while the Lr group was treated with Lr suspension diluted to approximately 3.83 × 10⁷ CFU/mL, prepared in the same volume as the Lr-SeNPs group. Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit was used to detect cell apoptosis by flow cytometry (cytoFLEX, S Beckman, USA) and the results were analyzed by FlowJo V10 software.

Zeta potential analysis

The Zeta potentials of Lr, Lr‑SeNPs and SeNPs were measured using a Zetasizer Nano ZS90 (Malvern Panalytical, UK) at 25°C. Each sample was diluted with deionized water and transferred to a disposable folded capillary cell (DTS1070) and and measured in triplicate.

Free radical scavenging capabilities

Lr-SeNPs, Lr, and SeNPs were determined for DPPH, ABTS, and hydroxyl radicals. The absorbance was measured using the microplate reader (Synergy H1, Agilent, USA) and radicals scavenging activity was calculated using the following equation:radicalscavengingactivity(%)=(1−A1−A2A0)×100%where A0 is the absorption of free radical ethanol solution, A1 is the absorption of the mixture of sample and free radical agent, and A2 is the absorption of the mixture of sample and ethanol.

Animals feed and observation

After one-week acclimatization, fifteen Hy-Line Variety Brown roosters (15-week-old) were divided into three groups with 5 replicates per group: control group (fed with basic diets (Table 1)), DON group (fed with 2.5 mg/kg DON-contaminated diets meeting the requirements for long-term low-dose exposure) and Lr-SeNPs+DON group (fed diets with 2.5 mg/kg DON and 0.5 mg Se/kg Lr-SeNPs, where “Se” refers to elemental selenium mass). During the experiment, the chickens were fed at 7:30, 14:00, 18:00, kept under a 12-hour light/dark cycle at a controlled temperature of 24-28 °C. The body weight of the roosters was monitored weekly. After 30 days of administration, chickens were sacrificed after 12 h of restricted food. The blood was collected and centrifuged to obtain serum. L-BSO inhibition experiment

Four experimental groups were established including control, DON, Lr-SeNPs + DON, and L-BSO + Lr-SeNPs +DON. In L-BSO + Lr-SeNPs +DON gourp, IPEC-J2 cells were treated with BSO at a concentration of 50 μM for 12 h prior to DON and Lr-SeNPs exposure. Subsequently, Lr‑SeNPs (6 mg Se/L) were added to the culture medium for an additional 12 h, followed by co‑treatment with DON (0.5 mg/L) for 24 h. After treatment, intracellular GSH, GSH-Px, and SOD contents were measured using a GSH assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng, China). The IL‑1β and IL-6 levels in the cell culture supernatant were determined by ELISA kit (Shanghai Jianglai Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China).

Gut microbiota analysis

Fecal samples were analyzed for 16 s rDNA sequencing by LC Bio Technology CO.,Ltd. DNA was extracted from fecal samples using the CTAB. After quality assessment, the V3-V4 hypervariable regions of bacterial 16S rRNA genes were amplified by PCR with the primers of 341F (5′-CCTACGGGNGGCWGCAG-3′) 805R(5′-GACTACHVGGGTATCTAATCC-3′). The amplicons were purified and then sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq platform. Based on the clean data, sequence denoising was performed using the DADA2 plugin in QIIME2 to remove potential PCR amplification and sequencing errors from high-throughput sequencing data, and to obtain representative amplicon sequence variants (ASVs). The average sequencing depth per sample was 83501. For alpha and beta diversity analyses, the ASV count table was rarefied to an even depth of 58,254 reads per sample. Differential microbial taxa were identified using Linear discriminant analysis Effect Size (LEfSe) with an LDA threshold of 2.0, combined with Wilcoxon rank‑sum test (P < 0.05). All graphs were drawn by OmicStudio created by LC Bio Technology CO.,Ltd.

Untargeted metabolomics sequencing and analysis

Fecal samples were analyzed for metabolomics by LC Bio Technology CO., Ltd. Metabolites were extracted from the samples via the organic solvent precipitation. The extracted samples were performed by liquid-mass spectrometry (LC-MS), with quality control (QC) samples inserted at the beginning, middle, and end of the sequence to assess technical reproducibility. Mass spectrometry detection was performed in both positive and negative ionization modes. Peak extraction and quality control were conducted using XCMS software, followed by metabolite identification with metaX. Metabolites were screened by VIP > 1.0, Wilcoxon test P < 0.05, FDR < 0.1, and fold change threshold (FC), ensuring biologically meaningful differences.

WGCNA analysis

To systematically investigate the coordinated variation patterns of metabolite expression in fecal samples and identify co-expression modules among metabolites, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was performed on the LC-Bio platform. Associations between phenotypic traits and metabolite modules were evaluated using a soft threshold of β = 0.85 to ensure scale‑free topology. Modules were defined by dynamic tree cutting (minimal module size = 30, merge threshold = 0.25), and the top 400 connected metabolites were visualized. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to identify modules correlated with phenotypes, with significance set at |cor| > 0.6 and P < 0.05.

Statistical analysis

The data results in vitro were generally performed in triplicate and presented as means ± standard deviations. All statistical tests in chicken were performed using independent biological replicates (individual chickens). Analyses and graphics were obtained using SPSS 24 software (SPSS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) and Prism software 8 (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, USA). Data normality was verified using the Shapiro-Wilk test prior to statistical analysis and and homogeneity of variances was evaluated using Levene's test. Statistical comparisons were conducted using one-way ANOVA when datasets satisfying both assumptions (P ≥ 0.05), followed by Tukey's multiple comparison test. Otherwise, the Kruskal-Wallis rank sum test was used. P < 0.05 means that the data is significantly different.

Results and discussion

Morphology and in vitro evaluation of Lr-SeNPs

Based on preliminary research and observations, Lr was identified as capable of reducing sodium selenite to form SeNPs through various intracellular enzymes and reducing substances (Fig. 1a). Due to its broad spectrum of probiotic properties and its capacity to adhere to and colonize the intestinal mucus barrier, this strain was selected as the model bacteria for subsequent research (Rao and Poonia, 2024; Wang et al., 2025b). A notable distinction was observed in the color of the solutions and the freeze-dried powders. The Lr samples appeared milky white, while the Lr-SeNPs exhibited a bright red color, and the SeNPs displayed a characteristic dark red hue (Fig. 1b). The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis revealed that the synthesized SeNPs, which exhibited a spherical morphology and a particle size of 142 ± 23.5 nm, aggregated and adhered to the bacterial surface in Lr-SeNPs group (Fig. 1c, d). The Se3d XPS spectrum displayed a characteristic peak at 55.24 eV, revealed two distinct peaks, Se3d3/2 and Se3d5/2, indicative of Se0 (Fig. 1e), with no signals corresponding to oxidized selenium species. The stability of Lr‑SeNPs was assessed by Zeta potential measurement. The Lr exhibited a Zeta potential of −29.47 ± 1.05 mV (Fig. 1f). The native negative charge on the bacterial surface provides moderate stability. The surface negativity increased to −41.27 ± 0.61 mV in Lr-SeNPs, confirming the successful loading of SeNPs transfer to the surface. Herein, toxicity and antioxidant capacity of Lr, Lr-SeNPs, and SeNPs were compered subsequently. The effects of the three materials on IPEC-J2 cell viability and apoptosis were evaluated using CCK-8 assay and flow cytometry (Fig. 1g, h, i). The results showed that there was no significant toxicity among different materials. Interestingly, Lr could promote the growth of IPEC-J2 cells, indicating excellent biocompatibility and negligible cytotoxicity of Lr-SeNPs. Selenium serves as an essential component of the active centers in various antioxidant enzymes within the human body, conferring a robust capacity to scavenge free radicals (Zhai et al., 2024). Low dose of Lr-SeNPs demonstrated a superior scavenging ability for ABTS and hydroxyl radicals, compared to both Lr and SeNPs groups in radical scavenging. The combination of Lr and SeNPs showed a synergistic effect in significantly improving free radical scavenging. (Fig. 1j-l). Lr-SeNPs ameliorates DON induced intestinal injury

DON causes severe intestinal damage through multiple synergistic mechanisms including oxidative stress induction, release of pro-inflammatory cytokine, and disruption of the intestinal barrier (Wang et al., 2024a; Hu et al., 2024b). In view of stability and free radical scavenging capacity of Lr-SeNPs, its therapeutic efficacy in DON-induced intestinal damage was further investigated in chickens according the protocol shown in Fig. 2a. Studies have shown that increasing antioxidant enzyme levels can effectively reduce oxidative stress, thereby serving as a protective agent against intoxication (Shaukat et al., 2026). The result showed that DON intake reduced SOD level and increased MDA level, implying the occurrence of oxidative stress. As expected, Lr-SeNPs significantly enhanced the SOD and GSH level, reduced the content of MDA, alleviating the oxidative damage (Fig. 2b-e). Next, RT-qPCR was used to quantify the expression of inflammatory cytokines and tight junction proteins in different treatment groups, evaluating the inflammation severity and intestinal barrier dysfunction. The results indicated that DON upregulated the gene expression of inflammatory cytokines, whereas Lr-SeNPs significantly downregulated these inflammatory markers in varying degrees (Fig. 2f-h). Tight junction proteins serve as the structural foundation of the intestinal epithelial barrier, directly influencing intestinal barrier function and liver health (Hu et al., 2024b). Toxin exposure, gut microbiota dysbiosis, and inflammatory conditions can all alter barrier homeostasis and increase permeability, leading to leaky gut syndrome (Matar et al., 2024). The Lr-SeNPs treatment marginally enhanced the expression of ZO-1, significantly upregulated the Occludin compared with the DON group, restoring their levels close to the control group and strengthening the intestinal barrier (Fig. 2i-j). Additionally, histological analysis by H&E staining further provided visual evidence that the control and Lr-SeNPs treatment groups showed a normal organizational structure but the DON group exhibited significant villous atrophy and detachment (Fig. 2n). Villi and crypts play an important role in nutrient absorption and intestinal health. A higher V/C ratio means more efficient nutrient absorption (Liu et al., 2020). The V/C were quantified by Image J and DON group exhibited shortened villi and deepened crypts while significant mitigative effect were noted with Lr-SeNPs (Fig. 2k-m). Lr-SeNPs ameliorates DON-induced gut microbiota dysbiosis

The maintenance of gut microbiota homeostasis is essential for animal health and physiological function (Hu et al., 2024a). Substantial evidence indicates that microbial dysbiosis is closely associated with the development of intestinal inflammatory conditions, such as inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) (Miyoshi and Chang, 2017). To systematically evaluate the impact of Lr-SeNPs on DON-induced microbial dysbiosis, we conducted 16S rDNA gene sequencing of fecal samples across experimental groups. Alpha diversity analysis, incorporating the Shannon index (species diversity), Chao 1 index (community richness), and Simpson index (dominance), revealed significant DON-induced alterations: the Chao1 index was markedly reduced while the Simpson index showed significant elevation (Fig. 3a-c). These metrics collectively reflected an altered community structure, where decreased Chao1 values indicate reduced microbial richness, and increased Simpson values reflect greater dominance of specific taxa within the disturbed microbial ecosystem. Comparative analysis revealed that Lr-SeNPs intervention significantly restored α-diversity, with the Chao1 index recovering to levels comparable with control group. β-diversity assessment through principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) and principal component analysis (PCA) demonstrated distinct clustering patterns among groups (Fig. 3d-e). suggesting that a shift in microbiota divergence was associated with DON exposure, while Lr-SeNPs tended to mitigate this loss of microbial diversity. At the phylum level, Proteobacteria, Firmicutes, Bacteroidota, Actinobacteriota, Synergistota, and Verrucomicrobiota had higher abundances (Fig. 3g). Notably, DON increased the relative abundances of Bacteroidota and Verrucomicrobiota, resulting in a low Firmicutes/Bacteroidota (F/B) ratio (Fig. 3k-m), this shift was particularly associated with enrichment of Barnesiella (Bacteroidota) and Clostridiales. While Bacteroidota participates in carbohydrate metabolism, its overgrowth, especially of Barnesiella, has been epidemiologically linked to necrotic enteritis in poultry models(Wan et al., 2022; Zhai et al., 2022). Heatmap analysis at the genus level revealed that Lr-SeNPs treated group exhibited more similar to the control group on abundance profiles and clustering patterns (Fig. 3h). Notably, Lr-SeNPs significantly reduced the abundance of Helicobacter, which has been reported to elicits intestinal inflammation in immunodeficient mice (Friedrich et al., 2021) and Clostridium, while increased the SCFA-producing genera Olsenella and Faecalibacterium. Subsequently, LEfSe analysis identified biomarkers in DON and Lr-SeNPs+DON group: Prevotellaceae, Anaerotruncus, Agathobaculum, and Acidovorax were identified as characteristic taxa in Lr-SeNPs group, whereas Bacteroidota_unclassified, Verrucomicrobiotaceae_unclassified, Odoribacter, and Roseburia were enriched in the DON group, reflecting fecal microbiota composition associated with Lr-SeNPs intervention (Fig. 3i, j). Notably, Akkermansia as degrading commensal bacterium, showed excessive enrichment in DON group (Fig. 3n). While typically considered beneficial, the excessive enrichment of Akkermansia observed in the DON group may not be a typical beneficial response. Researches showed that Akkermansia could intensify localized inflammation caused by epithelial barrier damage in the presence of commensal mucin degrader and a pathogen (Ganesh et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2022). Additionally, Lr-SeNPs restored the relative abundance of Bifidobacterium and Prevotellaceae (Fig. 3o-p). Bifidobacterium has been reported to alleviate DSS‑induced colitis and is often found to be deficient in patients with IBD (Mignini et al., 2023). It is also a key beneficial bacterial genus in the intestines of birds (Bastamy et al., 2024). Prevotellaceae as a potential probiotic in the gut, specialize in digesting complex polysaccharides in chicken (Yue et al., 2024). Lr-SeNPs intervention appeared to modulated the complex consortia, limiting the excessive proliferation of Akkermansia while restoring the relative abundance of Faecalibacterium, Bifidobacterium, and Prevotellaceae, confirming that Lr-SeNPs has the tendency to regulate dysbiosis.

The effect of Lr-SeNPs on intestinal metabolic response

The gut microbiota modulates intestinal health through the production of various small-molecule metabolites. To investigate how alterations in the microbiota affect host metabolic homeostasis, we conducted an untargeted metabolomic analysis of intestinal contents to explore potential associations with the gut microbiota. A total of 1222 metabolites were reliably identified, predominantly comprising lipids and lipid-like molecules, organic acids and their derivatives, and organoheterocyclic compounds. Partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) revealed a clear separation among three groups, and subsequent model validation confirmed that the model was robust, predictive, and not overfitting, with quality control samples forming a tight cluster, indicating high reproducibility. (Fig. 4a). Venn diagram analysis showed that the comparison between the Lr-SeNPs and DON groups yielded the highest number of differentially expressed metabolites, totaling 106 (Fig. 4b). Volcano plots and bar plots further indicated that DON exposure primarily resulted in metabolite downregulation (Fig. 4d). In contrast, Lr-SeNPs treatment led to the upregulation of 234 metabolites, reflecting a strong metabolic response to the intervention (Fig. 4c). These findings suggest that Lr-SeNPs may perturb metabolic homeostasis by inducing the production of novel metabolites, thereby shifting the overall metabolomic profile away from that of the DON group. Notably, Lr-SeNPs upregulated metabolites such as L-Tryptophan, 15(R)-HEDE, Cinnzeylanol, L-Glutamic acid, Taurine, Phenethylamine, and Glycine, while downregulating Alachlor ESA and 5-Methoxysalicylic acid. Interestingly, both L-Glutamic acid and Taurine were significantly reduced in DON group (Fig. 4e, f). Taurine has been shown to alleviate experimental colitis in mice and exert potent antioxidant effects by enhancing glutathione (GSH) activity (Qian et al., 2023). L-Tryptophan has been reported to modulate the gut microbiota, increases the abundance of tight junction proteins in the jejunum, and regulates oxidative stress, immune responses, and inflammation, thereby playing a critical role in maintaining the intestinal health (Liang et al., 2019). Additionally, L-Glutamate, glycine and 5-Oxo-L-Proline serve as intermediates in the glutathione cycle, contributing to ROS scavenging (Bachhawat and Yadav, 2018). Subsequently, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed using both MetaboAnalyst and the LC-Bio platform. The results indicated significant enrichment of classical pro-inflammatory pathways in the DON group, including necroptosis, ferroptosis, and the FoxO signaling pathway (Fig. 4g). In addition, GSEA identified three altered pathways: protein digestion and absorption (FDR.qval=0.0454), glycine, serine and threonine metabolism (FDR.qval=0.0849), and secondary bile acid biosynthesis (FDR.qval=0.169). Among these, protein digestion and absorption and glycine, serine and threonine metabolism showed significant negative enrichment scores, suggesting reduced activity of these pathways in the DON group. (Fig. 4h). In contrast, Lr-SeNPs intervention markedly influenced several key metabolic pathways compared to the DON group. These included glutathione metabolism, arginine biosynthesis, histidine metabolism, phenylalanine metabolism, and the metabolism of alanine, aspartate, and glutamate (Fig. 4i). Among these, glutathione metabolism emerged as a highly enriched target modulated by Lr-SeNPs. From a metabolic network perspective, glutamate serves as a key nodal metabolite overlapping between glutathione synthesis and arginine biosynthesis (Fig. 4j, k). Statistical analysis confirmed that the relative abundances of metabolites involved in glutathione, taurine, and arginine metabolism were significantly altered following Lr-SeNPs administration. Based on these metabolomic alterations, these interconnected pathways are speculated to potentially participate in preserving intestinal barrier integrity, enhancing antioxidant capacity, and regulating immune responses. The suppression of protein digestion and absorption suggests possible deficiencies in digestive enzyme and damage to intestinal mucosa, which may contribute to malnutrition and multi-system dysfunction - consistent with the known intestinal toxicity of DON (Li H.-Y. et al., 2022; S Chen. et al., 2022). Furthermore, the predicted upregulation of glutathione and taurine metabolism along with alterations in glycine levels, suggests a potential compensatory or therapeutic response aimed at neutralizing intestinal ROS and mitigating oxidative stress (Zhang et al., 2017; Han et al., 2020). Similarly, arginine-derived nitric oxide and histidine-derived histamine help potentiate immune responses, supporting the host’s ability to counteract inflammation (Yu et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024). Together, these findings demonstrate that glutathione-centered metabolic pathways as a potential mechanism through which Lr-SeNPs alleviates DON-induced injuries.

Synergistic mechanism of Lr-SeNPs to attenuate DON-induced intestinal injury

To explore the interaction between gut microbiota and metabolites, we performed a weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) integrating both metabolomic and gut microbiome data to identify co-expression modules and hub metabolites. The brown and turquoise modules exhibited the strongest associations with the DON group and the Lr-SeNPs+DON group, respectively (Fig. 5a). The brown module contained 83 metabolites, predominantly lipids (especially phospholipids and sphingolipids). The turquoise module consisted of 607 metabolites, among which taurine, glutamine, 5-oxo-L-proline, glycine, and glutamate displayed high intramodular connectivity (>100), suggesting their pivotal roles in the network. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed that the brown module was significantly associated with necroptosis, ferroptosis, and protein digestion and absorption, while the turquoise module was notably enriched in glutathione metabolism and histidine metabolism, findings that are fully consistent with previously identified metabolite functions (Fig. 5b-d). PICRUSt2 is a bioinformatic tool used to predict the functional composition and metabolic potential of microbial communities based on 16S rRNA gene sequencing data (Wang et al., 2025a). Using PICRUSt2, we predicted metabolic pathways associated with differentially abundant gut bacteria. The analysis identified several pathways including tryptophan metabolism, the TCA cycle, glutathione metabolism, and beta-alanine metabolism, that are closely linked to the regulation of oxidative stress and microbial energy homeostasis (Fig. 5e, f). Importantly, PICRUSt2 showed that Lr-SeNPs treatment reversed the downregulation of certain predicted functions observed in the DON group, restoring them to levels comparable to those in the control group. Helicobacter and Agathobaculum showed the strongest correlations with the predicted potential for glutathione metabolism (Fig. 5g-i). Spearman correlation analysis was conducted between 16 altered metabolites and the 10 differential bacterial genera to explore the interaction among gut microbiota, metabolites, and intestinal antioxidant enzyme activities (Fig. 5j-l). Bifidobacterium, Faecalibacterium, Agathobaculum, and Prevotellaceae showed positive correlations with GSH and SOD, and negative correlations with MDA, underscoring their potential role in mitigating intestinal oxidative stress. Glutamine, glycine, taurine, and cinnzeylanol were also positively correlated with key antioxidant enzymes. Glutamine serves as a major energy source for intestinal cells and a precursor for antioxidants synthesis (Wu et al., 2025). Liu et al. reported that glutamine promotes crypt cell proliferation and modulates intestinal architecture (Liu et al., 2020), which aligns with the reduced crypt depth observed in the Lr-SeNPs treatment group. Glycine contributes to restoring the reduced GSH to GSSG ratio under oxidative stress (Deng et al., 2023). These findings highlight the crucial role of these hub metabolites in intestinal repair and antioxidant mechanisms. Furthermore, glutamine, glycine, and taurine correlated negatively with DON-enriched genera such as Helicobacter, Bacteroidota, and Odoribacter, but positively correlated with beneficial genera predominant in the Lr-SeNPs group, including Bifidobacterium, Faecalibacterium, Agathobaculum, and Prevotellaceae, suggesting that metabolites profiles are likely influenced by microbial composition. Additionally, Faecalibacterium, a butyrate-producing genus, may further support intestinal barrier restoration (Bi et al., 2025). Collectively, these results indicate that Lr-SeNPs alleviates DON-induced oxidative stress, promotes intestinal barrier repair, and reduces intestinal inflammation by concurrently enriching beneficial gut microbiota and facilitating their associated antioxidant metabolites, ultimately attenuating intestinal inflammatory responses.

Integrated omics analyses repeatedly enriched the glutathione metabolism pathway. To simply verify this inference, we performed inhibition experiments using L‑BSO, a specific inhibitor of glutathione synthesis. As shown in Fig. 5g–h, L-BSO pretreatment (50 μM, 12 h) significantly reduced GSH‑Px and SOD activities (Fig. 5g) and reversed the suppressive effect of Lr-SeNPs on IL-6 levels (Fig. 5h). These results indicate that inhibition of glutathione synthesis attenuates the protective effects of Lr‑SeNPs against DON‑induced oxidative stress and inflammation, providing functional evidence for the involvement of the glutathione pathway in the underlying mechanism. This observation is consistent with the above metabolomic KEGG enrichment and gut microbiota PICRUSt2 predictions, supporting the notion that glutathione metabolism actively participates in the protective response. However, the experiments were conducted in vitro, and the relative contribution of this pathway in the chicken intestine remains to be determined. The causal role of the glutathione pathway in vivo merits further investigation. Overall, the integration of multi‑omics association data and functional validation strengthens the mechanistic inference that glutathione metabolism contributes to the protective effects of Lr‑SeNPs against DON‑induced intestinal injury.

Conclusion

To mitigate DON-induced intestinal injury in poultry, we developed a complex composed of SeNPs encapsulated in Lr. The present study demonstrated that Lr-SeNPs exhibits remarkable stability and free radical scavenging capacity. With high biosafety, Lr-SeNPs effectively alleviated oxidative stress in chickens by enhancing the activities of GSH-Px and SOD and reducing MDA content. Furthermore, it mitigated inflammatory responses and restored intestinal barrier integrity through downregulating of IL-6 and IL-1β and upregulating of ZO-1 expression. Histological analysis confirmed its protective effects on intestinal architecture. Lr-SeNPs also reversed DON-induced gut microbiota dysbiosis, significantly increasing microbial diversity and the abundance of beneficial probiotics. At the metabolic level, Lr-SeNPs treatment was associated with altered metabolic profiles, with enrichment of pathways including glutathione metabolism, taurine metabolism, and several amino acid metabolism pathways, thereby promoting intestinal and redox homeostasis and attenuating DON-triggered inflammation. Consequently, this study demonstrates that Lr-SeNPs alleviates DON-induced intestinal injury in a rooster model through mechanisms involving enhanced antioxidant capacity, restoration of gut barrier function, modulation of gut microbiota, and enrichment of glutathione-related metabolic pathways. These findings provide experimental evidence that Lr-SeNPs may represent a safe and effective nutritional supplement and a candidate intervention for mitigating mycotoxin-induced intestinal damage in poultry.
