Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{H}}

{{\mathbb{D}}}=\{z:\ |z|<1\}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}J_{CV}(f;z)&:=&1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)},\\
J_{ST}(f;z)&:=&\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}.\end{array}\right.

A function
f

is called
p
-valent in

w

f(z)=w
has at most
p
roots in

w_{0}

f(z)=w_{0}
has exactly
p
roots in

D
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}
the class of functions
f(z)
which

\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
,
the class of functions
f(z)\in\mathcal{H}
given by

f(z)=z^{p}+\sum_{n=p+1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the class
of all functions in
\mathcal{A}

which are univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Also let
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\ast}(\alpha)

\mathcal{C}_{p}(\alpha)
be the subclasses of

consisting of all

p
-valent functions which are strongly starlike and strongly convex

\alpha

, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\ast}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{f(z)\in\mathcal{A}(p):\left|\arg\left\{J_{ST}(f;z)\right\}\right|<\frac{\alpha\pi}{2},\ z\in\mathbb{D}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{p}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{f(z)\in\mathcal{A}(p):zf^{\prime}(z)/p\in\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\ast}(\alpha)\right\}.

Note that
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\ast}(1)=\mathcal{S}^{\ast}

, where

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f(z)=z^{p}+\sum_{n=p+1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. If

(2.1)

\left|\arg\{f^{(p)}(z)\}\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\alpha_{1}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\alpha_{1}\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}

for some
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1]
, then

(2.2)

\left|\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-1)}(z)}{z}\right\}\right|<\frac{\alpha_{1}\pi}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

Write

p(z)=\frac{f^{(p-1)}(z)}{p!z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
p(0)=1
and

f^{(p)}(z)=p!(p(z)+zp^{\prime}(z)).

From (
2.1
), we have

(2.3)

\left|\arg\{p(z)+zp^{\prime}(z)\}\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\alpha_{1}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\alpha_{1}\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

If there exists a point

z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
, such that

\displaystyle|\arg\{p(z)\}|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}\pi}{2},\quad|z|<|z_{0}|,

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\arg\{p(z_{0})\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}\pi}{2},

then from Lemma
1.1
, we have

\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}=\frac{2ik\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\right\}}{\pi}

for some
k\geq 1
. For the case

\arg\{p(z_{0})\}=\frac{\alpha_{1}\pi}{2}
, we have

\arg\left\{1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right\}=\arg\left\{1+i\alpha_{1}k\right\}\in[\tan^{-1}\alpha_{1},\pi/2)

because
k\geq 1
. Therefore, we have

(2.5)

\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\left(1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right)\right\}\\
=\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\right\}+\arg\left\{1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right\}.

This gives

\displaystyle\arg\left\{p(z_{0})+z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\left(1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\right\}+\arg\left\{1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}\pi}{2}+\arg\left\{1+i\alpha_{1}k\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}\pi}{2}+\tan^{-1}\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\alpha_{1}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\alpha_{1}\right).

This contradicts (
2.3
). For the case

\arg\{p(z_{0})\}=-\frac{\alpha_{1}\pi}{2}
, applying the same method as
the above we can obtain

\arg\left\{p(z_{0})+z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})\right\}\leq-\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\alpha_{1}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\alpha_{1}\right)

This contradicts (
2.3
) too. Therefore, we have

\left|\arg\left\{p(z)\right\}\right|<\frac{\alpha_{1}\pi}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}

which ends the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

\left|\arg\{f^{(p)}(z)\}\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\gamma_{0}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\gamma_{0}\right)=\frac{\pi\cdot 0.6\ldots}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f(z)=z^{p}+\sum_{n=p+1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. If

(2.11)

\left|\arg\{f^{(p)}(z)\}\right|<\frac{\pi\alpha_{0}}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

for some
\alpha_{0}\in(0,3/2]
then, we have

(2.12)

\left|\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-k)}(z)}{z^{k}}\right\}\right|<\frac{\pi\alpha_{k}}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where

(2.13)

\alpha_{0}\in(0,3/2],\quad\alpha_{k}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{k}=\alpha_{k-1}

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that the case
k=1
was considered in Theorem

2.1
with

\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\alpha_{1}.

Hence, we have

(2.14)

\left|\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-1)}(z)}{z}\right\}\right|<\frac{\pi\alpha_{1}}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

Next, let us put

q_{2}(z)=\frac{2f^{(p-2)}(z)}{p!z^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
q_{2}(0)=1

\frac{f^{(p-1)}(z)}{z}=\frac{p!}{2}q_{2}(z)\left(2+\frac{zq_{2}^{\prime}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}\right).

(2.15)

\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-1)}(z)}{z}\right\}=\arg\left\{q_{2}(z)\left(2+\frac{zq_{2}^{\prime}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}\right)\right\}

We apply the same method as in the case
k=1
. If there exists a
point
z_{2}\in\mathbb{D}
, such that

\displaystyle|\arg\{q_{2}(z)\}|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{2}\pi}{2},\quad|z|<|z_{2}|,

\displaystyle|\arg\{q_{2}(z_{2})\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{2}\pi}{2},

then from Lemma
1.1
, we have

\frac{z_{2}q_{2}^{\prime}(z_{2})}{q_{2}(z_{2})}=\frac{2ik\arg\left\{q_{2}(z_{2})\right\}}{\pi}

. For the case

\displaystyle\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-1)}(z_{2})}{z_{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\{q_{2}(z_{2})\}+\arg\left\{2+\frac{z_{2}q_{2}^{\prime}(z_{2})}{q_{2}(z_{2})}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{2}\pi}{2}+\arg\left\{2+i\alpha_{2}k\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{2}\pi}{2}+\tan^{-1}\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\alpha_{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\alpha_{1}}{2}.

This contradicts (
2.14
). For the case

\arg\{q_{2}(z_{2})\}=-\frac{\alpha_{2}\pi}{2}
, applying the same method
as the above we can obtain

\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-1)}(z_{2})}{z_{2}}\right\}\leq-\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\alpha_{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\right)

This contradicts (
2.14
) too. Therefore, we have

(2.16)

.

For the case

q_{3}(z)=\frac{2\cdot 3f^{(p-3)}(z)}{p!z^{3}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then

\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-2)}(z)}{z^{2}}\right\}=\arg\left\{q_{3}(z)\left(3+\frac{zq_{3}^{\prime}(z)}{q_{3}(z)}\right)\right\}

. If there exists a

z_{3}\in\mathbb{D}
, such that

\displaystyle|\arg\{q_{3}(z)\}|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{3}\pi}{2},\quad|z|<|z_{3}|,

\displaystyle|\arg\{q_{3}(z_{3})\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{3}\pi}{2},

then from Lemma
1.1
, we have

\frac{z_{3}q_{3}^{\prime}(z_{3})}{q_{3}(z_{3})}=\frac{2ik\arg\left\{q_{3}(z_{3})\right\}}{\pi}

. For the case

\displaystyle\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-2)}(z_{3})}{z^{2}_{3}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\{q_{3}(z_{3})\}+\arg\left\{3+\frac{z_{3}q_{3}^{\prime}(z_{3})}{q_{3}(z_{3})}\right\}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{3}\pi}{2}+\arg\left\{3+i\alpha_{3}k\right\}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{3}\pi}{2}+\tan^{-1}\frac{\alpha_{3}}{3}

Theorem 2.5
.

Let

. Assume that

\left|\arg\{f^{(p)}(z)\}\right|<\frac{\pi\alpha_{0}}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

for some
\alpha_{0}\in(0,3/2]
, and assume that the sequence

\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k=1}^{k=p}
is defined by

\alpha_{k}+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{k}=\alpha_{k-1}.

Then we have

(2.19)

\left|\arg\left\{\frac{zf^{(p-s+1)}(z)}{f^{(p-s)}(z)}\right\}\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}(\alpha_{s}+\alpha_{s-1})\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

for all
s\in\{2,\ldots,p\}
. Furthermore, if there exists a
positive integer
\sigma\in\{2,\ldots,p\}
such that

\alpha_{\sigma}+\alpha_{\sigma-1}\leq 1
, then

(2.20)

\left|\arg\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}\right|<\frac{\pi(\alpha_{p-1}+\alpha_{p})}{2}\leq\frac{\pi}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

is
p
-valently strongly starlike of order

\alpha_{p-1}+\alpha_{p}

Proof.

Applying (
2.12
) for

,

, we obtain

\left|\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-s+1)}(z)}{z^{s-1}}\right\}\right|<\frac{\alpha_{s-1}\pi}{2},\quad\left|\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-s)}(z)}{z^{s}}\right\}\right|<\frac{\alpha_{s}\pi}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

It is known that the number sequence

is
a decreasing sequence. If
\alpha_{0}=3/2
, then
\alpha_{1}=1
. If

\alpha_{0}<3/2
, then
\alpha_{1}<1
and
\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{1}<1
.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\left|\arg\left\{\frac{zf^{(p-s+1)}(z)}{f^{(p-s)}(z)}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\arg\left\{\frac{z^{s}}{f^{(p-s)}(z)}\frac{f^{(p-s+1)}(z)}{z^{s-1}}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-s)}(z)}{z^{s}}\right\}\right|+\left|\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(p-s+1)}(z)}{z^{s-1}}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}(\alpha_{s}+\alpha_{s-1}),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}

for all
s\in\{2,\ldots,p\}
. This gives (
2.19
). The sum

\alpha_{s}+\alpha_{s-1}
decreases when
s
runs over
\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Therefore, if there exists a positive integer

\sigma\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
such that

\alpha_{\sigma}+\alpha_{\sigma-1}\leq 1
, then

\left|\arg\left\{\frac{zf^{(p-s+1)}(z)}{f^{(p-s)}(z)}\right\}\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}(\alpha_{s}+\alpha_{s-1})\leq\frac{\pi}{2}

for all
s\in\{\sigma,\ldots,p\}
. Applying this for
s=p
gives
(
2.20
).
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume that

f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

If
a_{s-1}=0
and
a_{s}\neq 0
for some positive integer
s\geq 2

and

(2.21)

\left|\arg\{f^{(s)}(z)\}\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\delta+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\delta\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

for some
\delta>0
such that

2\delta+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\delta<2
. Then we have

(2.22)

\left|\arg\left\{\frac{zf^{(s)}(z)}{f^{(s-1)}(z)}\right\}\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}\left(2\delta+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\delta\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

We have

f^{(n)}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+k)!}{k!}a_{n+k}z^{k},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Therefore,

f^{(s)}(z)=s!a_{s}+(s+1)!a_{s+1}z+\cdots,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}

and

f^{(s-1)}(z)=s!a_{s}z+\frac{(s+1)!}{2!}a_{s+1}z^{2}+\cdots,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

because
a_{s-1}=0
. Write

p(z)=\frac{f^{(s-1)}(z)}{s!a_{s}z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
p(0)=1
and

f^{(s)}(z)=s!a_{s}(p(z)+zp^{\prime}(z)).

From (
2.1
), we have

(2.23)

\left|\arg\{p(z)+zp^{\prime}(z)\}\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\delta+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\delta\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

If there exists a point

z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
, such that

\displaystyle|\arg\{p(z)\}|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\pi}{2},\quad|z|<|z_{0}|,

\displaystyle|\arg\{p(z_{0})\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\pi}{2},

then from Lemma
1.1
, we have

\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}=\frac{2ik\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\right\}}{\pi}

for some
k\geq 1
. For the case

\arg\{p(z_{0})\}=\frac{\delta\pi}{2}
, we have

\tan^{-1}\delta\leq\arg\left\{1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right\}=\arg\left\{1+i\delta k\right\}<\pi/2

because
k\geq 1
. Therefore, we have

(2.24)

\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\left(1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right)\right\}\\
=\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\right\}+\arg\left\{1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right\}.

This gives

\displaystyle\arg\left\{p(z_{0})+z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\left(1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\left\{p(z_{0})\right\}+\arg\left\{1+\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\pi}{2}+\arg\left\{1+i\delta k\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\pi}{2}+\tan^{-1}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\delta+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\delta\right).

This contradicts (
2.23
). For the case

\arg\{p(z_{0})\}=-\frac{\delta\pi}{2}
, applying the same method as
the above we can obtain

\arg\left\{p(z_{0})+z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})\right\}\leq-\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\delta+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\delta\right)

This contradicts (
2.23
) too. Therefore, we have

\left|\arg\left\{p(z)\right\}\right|=\left|\arg\frac{f^{(s-1)}(z)}{s!a_{s}z}\right|=\left|\arg\frac{f^{(s-1)}(z)}{z}\right|<\frac{\delta\pi}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

From this we have

\displaystyle\left|\arg\left\{\frac{zf^{(s)}(z)}{f^{(s-1)}(z)}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\arg\left\{f^{(s)}(z)\right\}\right|+\left|\arg\left\{\frac{f^{(s-1)}(z)}{z}\right\}\right|\leq

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\delta+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\delta\right)+\frac{\delta\pi}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\left(2\delta+\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\delta\right)

which ends the proof.
∎
