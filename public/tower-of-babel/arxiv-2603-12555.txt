1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and Background

The stationary Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation

(1.1)

6uu^{\prime}-u^{(3)}=0

for
u:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
arises as the time-independent counterpart of the classical KdV equation, which models nonlinear dispersive phenomena such as shallow water waves and plasma oscillations. The KdV equation is a completely integrable system with a rich mathematical structure, including soliton solutions and an infinite hierarchy of conserved quantities, making it a central object in both applied and theoretical analysis of nonlinear dispersive partial differential equations.

Recent years have seen many exciting developments in the theory surrounding the KdV equation, mostly concerning questions of wellposedness. To give a very brief overview, in
[
28
]
and
[
29
]
Molinet shows that KdV is ill-posed for initial data lying in
H^{-s}(\mathbb{T})
and
H^{-s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>1
. Kappeler and Topalov
[
23
]
then showed that KdV is wellposed in
H^{-1}(\mathbb{T})
, and finally in
[
24
]
Killip and Vişan established the same threshold for KdV posed on the real line. We also mention the work of Christ
[
13
]
where he constructs a nonzero solution to KdV which has zero initial data and lies in
C_{t}H^{-\epsilon}_{x}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
,
\epsilon>0
. This does not conflict with the result of
[
23
]
since Christ’s notion of solution is weaker than what is considered in
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
28
]
, and
[
29
]
. This demonstrates the important role that low regularity solutions play in the study of KdV. In contrast, low regularity solutions of stationary KdV have to this point remained largely unexplored.

In this work, we investigate weak solutions of the stationary KdV equation in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
for
p<2
. Our main result establishes a sharp dichotomy:

•

for
p<2

•

for

[

[

for examples of the construction of such smooth solutions).

This result identifies the precise threshold in

regularity separating smooth from potentially singular solutions and demonstrates that stationary KdV admits a rich set of low-regularity solutions.

Previously, the method of convex integration was not able to be applied to the KdV equation or more general dispersive PDEs due to the loss of derivatives one would incur in what we call the

1.2.
Main Result

Our first task is to extend the definition of a weak solution to (
1.1
) to
u
lying in some Sobolev space with strictly negative regularity. To do this, we need to make sense of
u^{2}
for
u
potentially an arbitrary distribution. In general, there is not much that can be said about this product when both functions lie in a Sobolev space with negative regularity, but following
[
1
, Definition 1.1]
we may offer the following definition of the mean free product:

Definition 1.1

(
Paraproducts in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
)
.

Let
f,g
be distributions, so that
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}(f),\mathbb{P}_{2^{j^{\prime}}}(g)
are well-defined for
j,j^{\prime}\geq 0
. We say that
\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(fg)
is well-defined as a paraproduct in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for some
s\in\mathbb{R}
if

\sum_{j,j^{\prime}\geq 0}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}\left(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}(f)\mathbb{P}_{2^{j^{\prime}}}(g)\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}<\infty\,.

Then we define

\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(fg)=\sum_{j,j^{\prime}\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}\left(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}(f)\mathbb{P}_{2^{j^{\prime}}}(g)\right)\,,

since the right-hand side is an absolutely summable series in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
.

With this definition, adapting
[
1
, Definition 1.2]
, we may now define a weak solution to (
1.1
) which is valid for
u
belonging to a Sobolev space of arbitrary regularity.

Definition 1.2

(
Weak paraproduct solutions to (
1.1
)
)
.

If
u\in\dot{H}^{s}
,
s<0
, we say
u
is a weak paraproduct solution to the stationary KdV equation if there is
s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(u^{2})
is well defined as a paraproduct in
\dot{H}^{s^{\prime}}
in the sense of the previous definition and

-\left\langle u,\phi^{(3)}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s},\dot{H}^{-s}}+3\left\langle\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(u^{2}),\phi^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s^{\prime}},\dot{H}^{-s^{\prime}}}=0

for all smooth
\phi
.

With this, we may state our main results.

Theorem 1.3

(
Rigidity Result
)
.

If
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
solves (
1.1
) then
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.

Proof.

Suppose
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
satisfies

-\int_{\mathbb{T}}u\psi^{(3)}+3\int_{\mathbb{T}}u^{2}\psi^{\prime}=0

for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. In the sense of distributions we have

\left\langle\left(u^{\prime\prime}-3u^{2}\right)^{\prime},\psi\right\rangle=\left\langle u^{(3)}-3(u^{2})^{\prime},\psi\right\rangle=0.

And so, in the sense of distributions we have
u^{\prime\prime}-3u^{2}=C
for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, this implies
u\in W^{2,1}(\mathbb{T})
. From the Sobolev embedding
W^{2,1}(\mathbb{T})\subset C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
we conclude
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, and thus
u\in C^{3}(\mathbb{T})
. From here we may either appeal to bootstrapping or
[
18
, Lemma 1]
to deduce
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
∎

Theorem 1.4

(
Flexibility Result
)
.

There is

u\in\bigcap_{0<\epsilon<1}\left(\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{-\epsilon}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{2-\epsilon}(\mathbb{T})\right)\setminus L^{2}(\mathbb{T})

such that

(a)

\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(u^{2})
exists as a paraproduct in
\dot{H}^{-s}(\mathbb{T})
for all
s
large enough;

(b)

u
solves (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.2
.

Remark 1.5
.

In Theorem
1.4
it suffices to take
s>5/2
, but this exact value is unimportant for our purposes.

The rest of this paper is concerned with the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Background Theory and Technical Lemmas

The following on Littlewood-Paley theory, Fourier multiplier operators, and function spaces can be found in
[
2
]
and
[

]
. The exact formulation of Definition
2.1
is based off
[

, Definition 2.1]
.

Definition 2.1

(
Littlewood-Paley projectors
)
.

There exists
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
, smooth, radially symmetric, and compactly supported in
\{\xi:6/7\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
such that
\varphi(\xi)=1
on
\{\xi:1\leq|\xi|\leq 12/7\}
,

\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\hskip 7.11317pt\text{ for all }\hskip 7.11317pt|\xi|\geq 1,

and
\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{j}\cap\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{j^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for all
|j-j^{\prime}|\geq 2
, where
\varphi_{j}(\cdot)=\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)
. We define the projection of a function
f
on its
0
-mode by

\mathbb{P}_{=0}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x)\,dx,

and the projection on the
j^{\rm th}
shell by

\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}(f)(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{f}(\xi)\varphi_{j}(\xi)e^{2\pi i\xi x}\,.

We also define the frequency cutoff by

\mathbb{P}_{\leq 2^{j}}(f)=\mathbb{P}_{=0}(f)+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\mathbb{P}_{2^{k}}(f),

the projection onto high frequencies by
\mathbb{P}_{>2^{j}}(f)=(\operatorname{Id}-\mathbb{P}_{\leq 2^{j}})(f)
and the projection off the mean by
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}f:=(\operatorname{Id}-\mathbb{P}_{=0})f
.

The following lemma is standard, and can be deduced as a consequence of the Poisson summation formula.

Lemma 2.2

(
L^{p}
boundedness of projection operators
)
.

\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda}
is a bounded operator from
L^{p}
to
L^{p}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
with operator norm independent of
\lambda
.

Next we introduce the function spaces which will play a prominent role throughout.

Definition 2.3

(
\dot{H}^{s}
Sobolev spaces
)
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})=\left\{f:\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}|\xi|^{2s}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}<\infty\right\}

with the norm induced by the sum above.

Remark 2.4
.

For every
f\in\dot{H}^{s}
for some
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the Fourier coefficients

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\,dx,\hskip 7.11317pt\text{ where }\hskip 7.11317pt\mathbb{T}=[0,1],

and so we can define
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}f
for
j\geq 0
. Note that each
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}f
is smooth for
f\in\dot{H}^{s}
irrespective of the value of
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.5
.

(Homogeneous Besov spaces)

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p,q\leq\infty
, and
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, define the homogeneous Besov space to be

\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}=\left\{f:\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}=\left\|2^{j\alpha}\|\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\right\|_{\ell^{q}_{j\geq 0}}<\infty\right\}.

Remark 2.6
.

Note for
\alpha>0
the space
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}
is equivalent to
\{f\in C^{\alpha}:\hat{f}(0)=0\}
. In addition, the space
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{2,2}
is equivalent to the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.7

(
Intermittent slabs
)
.

Let
\lambda
be a large power of
2
and take
0<\epsilon<1
such that
\lambda^{\epsilon}
is also an integer. Then there exist smooth
\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}=0
;

(2)

\|\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\lambda^{(1-\epsilon)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}
;

(3)

\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}
is
\frac{\mathbb{T}}{\lambda^{\epsilon}}
-periodic.

Proof.

The construction is given in
[
19
, Lemma 2.12]
just with
d=1
. For the purposes of the proceeding proof though, we just state the function is of the form

(2.1)

\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda^{\frac{1-\epsilon_{q+1}}{2}}\phi\left(\lambda x+\lambda^{1-\epsilon}n\right).

where
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth, odd, supported in
(-1,1)
, mean-zero, and
L^{2}
normalized.
∎

Again following
[
19
, Lemma 2.13]
and
[
20
, Lemma 2.15]
Fourier series expansions of our intermittent building block will prove very useful, with the main application being the proof of Lemma

.

Lemma 2.8

(
Fourier series representation of the intermittent slab
)
.

The Fourier series representation of
\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}
is

(2.2)

\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{2}}\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)e^{2\pi i\lambda^{\epsilon}nx}.

Proof.

Applying the Poisson summation formula to (
2.1
) gives (
2.2
).
∎

Lemma 2.9

(
L^{2}
norm of intermittent slab
)
.

\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}
is
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
normalized to leading order. More precisely,

\|\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}=1+O\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}\right).

Proof.

From Lemma
2.8
and the Plancherel theorem we have the expression

(2.3)

\|\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda^{\epsilon-1}\left|\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)\right|^{2}.

We recognize the right hand side of (
2.3
) as being a Riemann sum approximation of
\|\hat{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}
with step size
\lambda^{\epsilon-1}
. Applying Plancherel one more time,
\|\hat{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1
and since
\phi
is smooth and compactly supported, we have that

(2.4)

\|\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}=1+O\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}\right).

Taking the square root of (
2.4
) and then using the Taylor expansion gives the result.
∎

The following lemma will show that in every norm that we will be concerned with, the projection of the product of a smooth function and our intermittent slabs can be made arbitrarily small simply by choosing the minimum active frequency of the intermittent slab large enough.

Lemma 2.10

(
High-low products in negative Sobolev norms
)
.

For any
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
,

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda^{2}}(a\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=0.

Proof.

For
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, we begin by expressing its Fourier series as

a(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{a}(k)e^{2\pi ikx}.

Together with (
2.2
), this allows us to write

(2.5)

(a\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon})(x)=\sum_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{a}(k)\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{2}}\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)e^{2\pi i\left(\lambda^{\epsilon}n+k\right)x}.

Recall
\lambda
was chosen to be a large power of
2
. So let us put
\mu=2\log_{2}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, from Definition
2.1
the high-frequency projection has the representation

\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda^{2}}(a\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon})(x)=\sum_{j=\mu}^{\infty}\sum_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{j}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon}n+k\right)\widehat{a}(k)\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{2}}\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)e^{2\pi i\left(\lambda^{\epsilon}n+k\right)x},

and this gives

\left\|\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda^{2}}(a\rho_{\lambda,\epsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,k\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\left|\lambda^{\epsilon}n+k\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{a}(k)|\left|\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)\right|\leq\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2},

where we put

\mathcal{S}_{1}\coloneq\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{2}}\sum_{|n|\geq\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{2-\epsilon}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\left|\lambda^{\epsilon}n+k\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{a}(k)|\left|\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)\right|

and

\mathcal{S}_{2}\coloneq\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{2}}\sum_{|n|\leq\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{2-\epsilon}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\left|\lambda^{\epsilon}n+k\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{a}(k)|\left|\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)\right|.

We will estimate each of these terms separately.

Estimate for
\mathcal{S}_{1}
:
Since
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, then
\hat{a}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
. Hence

(2.6)

\mathcal{S}_{1}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{2}}\sum_{|n|\gtrsim\lambda^{2-\epsilon}}\;\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{a}(k)|\left|\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{2}}\sum_{|n|\gtrsim\lambda^{2-\epsilon}}\;\left|\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)\right|.

Now since
\phi
was assumed to be Schwartz, we have the estimate

\left|\hat{\phi}(\xi)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|)^{N}}

for
N>100
. Hence we have

(2.7)

\sum_{|n|\gtrsim\lambda^{2-\epsilon}}\left|\hat{\phi}\left(\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right)\right|\lesssim\sum_{|n|\gtrsim\lambda^{2-\epsilon}}\frac{1}{(1+\left|\lambda^{\epsilon-1}n\right|)^{N}}\lesssim\lambda^{N(1-\epsilon)}\sum_{|n|\gtrsim\lambda^{2-\epsilon}}|n|^{-N}.

Now using the integral test we have that

(2.8)

\sum_{|n|\gtrsim\lambda^{2-\epsilon}}|n|^{-N}\simeq\int_{\lambda^{2-\epsilon}}^{\infty}x^{-N}\,dx\simeq\lambda^{(1-N)(2-\epsilon)}.

Combining (
2.6
), (
2.7
), and (
2.8
) we have the estimate

\mathcal{S}_{1}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{2}}\lambda^{N(1-\epsilon)}\lambda^{(1-N)(2-\epsilon)}

which clearly tends to
0
as we send
\lambda\to\infty
.

Estimate of
\mathcal{S}_{2}
:

In the regime where
|n|\leq\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{2-\epsilon}
, it follows that

\lambda^{\epsilon}|n|\leq\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{2}
.
Consequently, from the projection condition we deduce

|k|\geq\left|k+\lambda^{\epsilon}n\right|-\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{\epsilon}|n|\geq\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{2}.

Thus, in this region the frequency
k
is necessarily of high magnitude.

We now exploit the rapid decay of
\widehat{a}(k)
. Since
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, its Fourier coefficients decay faster than any polynomial rate. In particular, for

Lemma 2.12

(
Bernstein type estimates
)
.

Suppose
u,v:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
are smooth with the frequency support of
u
contained in
B(0,\mu)
and the frequency support of
v
contained in
B(0,2\mu)\setminus B(0,\mu)
,
\mu\gg 1
. Then we have the following estimates:

(a)

For
s\geq 0
we have

\|u\|_{C^{s}}\lesssim\mu^{s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}}\lesssim\mu^{s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(b)

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim\mu^{s}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}}\lesssim\mu^{s}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Proof.

Part (a).

For the Littlewood–Paley pieces we observe the following:

•

If
2^{j}\gg\mu
, then
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}u=0
since the Fourier support of
u
is contained in
B(0,\mu)
.

•

If
2^{j}\lesssim\mu
, then from Lemma
2.2
we have

\|\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}.

This give us

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}\lesssim\mu^{s}\|\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\mu^{s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
be a smooth radial cutoff at scale
1
and define
\chi_{\mu}(\xi)=\chi(\xi/\mu)
.
Since
u
has frequency support in
|\xi|\leq\mu
, we can write

u=\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\mu}(k)e^{2\pi ikx}\right)\ast u.

For
|\alpha|\leq s
, using Young’s convolution inequality we have

\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{\infty}}=\left\|\partial^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\mu}(k)e^{2\pi ikx}\right)\ast u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(2\pi ik)^{\alpha}\chi_{\mu}(k)e^{2\pi ikx}\right\|_{L^{1}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}.

So it suffices to show that

(2.10)

\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(2\pi ik)^{\alpha}\chi_{\mu}(k)e^{2\pi ikx}\right\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\mu^{\alpha}.

First, computing the Fourier transform of
x^{\alpha}\chi_{\mu}(x)
we have

\left(x^{\alpha}\chi_{\mu}(x)\right)^{\wedge}(\xi)=\mu^{\alpha+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{\alpha}\chi(x)e^{2\pi i\mu x\xi}\,dx

Now applying the Poisson summation formula yields

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(2\pi ik)^{\alpha}\chi_{\mu}(k)e^{-2\pi ikx}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(2\pi i)^{\alpha}\mu^{\alpha+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{\alpha}\chi(x)e^{2\pi i\mu(\xi+k)}\,dx.

Applying the
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
norm we have

\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(2\pi ik)^{\alpha}\chi_{\mu}(k)e^{2\pi ikx}\right\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu^{\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{\alpha}\chi(x)e^{2\pi i\mu x(\xi+k)}\,dx\right|\,d\xi.

Finally we perform the change of variables
\mu(\xi+k)\mapsto\xi
to get

\begin{split}\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(2\pi ik)^{\alpha}\chi_{\mu}(k)e^{2\pi ikx}\right\|_{L^{1}}&\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu^{\alpha}\int_{\mu k}^{\mu(k+1)}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{\alpha}\chi(x)e^{2\pi ix\xi}\,dx\right|\,d\xi\\
&=\mu^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{\alpha}\chi(x)e^{2\pi i\mu x\xi}\,dx\right|\,d\xi\\
&\simeq\mu^{\alpha}\|x^{\alpha}\chi(x)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\lesssim\mu^{\alpha}.\end{split}

Now summing over
\alpha\leq s
and using that
\mu\gg 1
gives (
2.10
). The modifications for the case
s\not\in\mathbb{N}
are obvious.

Part (b).

In this setup we observe
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}v\neq 0
only when
\mu\simeq 2^{j}
. Then we can write

\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}\simeq\mu^{s}\|\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\mu^{s}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}.

For the first estimate in part (b), we have on the support of
\widehat{v}
that
|k|\sim\mu
and thus we conclude

\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{\mu\leq|k|\leq 2\mu}|k|^{2s}|\hat{v}(k)|^{2}\lesssim\mu^{2s}\sum_{\mu\leq|k|\leq 2\mu}|\hat{v}(k)|^{2}\leq\mu^{2s}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

hence

\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim\mu^{s}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

&\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}\|\rho_{q+1}\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\\
&\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}\lambda_{q+1}^{\frac{{\bf 1}-\epsilon_{q+1}}{2}}.\end{split}

We have decay in (
5.10
) so long as

\frac{1}{2s+1}<\epsilon_{q+1}.

From our choices of
s>5/2
and
\epsilon_{q+1}=1-2^{-q-1001}
this is clearly satisfied. Hence we may take
\lambda_{q+1}
large enough to ensure that

(5.11)

\left\|2\|E_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left(\tilde{C}_{q+1}\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}+1\right)a_{q}^{2}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}({\rho_{q+1}^{2}})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}<2^{-q-1000}.

•

The second term we analyze is

\left[\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right]^{2},

and for this we invoke Lemma
2.10
to get

(5.12)

\begin{split}\left\|[\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})]^{2}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}(\mathbb{T})}&\lesssim\left\|[\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})]^{2}\right\|_{L^{1}}\\
&\lesssim\left\|\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\\
&<2^{-q-1000}\end{split}

where the final inequality in (
5.12
) holds for large enough
\lambda_{q+1}
.

•

For the last term again using Lemma
2.10
we have

(5.13)

\begin{split}\left\|a_{q}\rho_{q+1}\,\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}&\lesssim\left\|a_{q}\rho_{q+1}\,\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{L^{1}}\\
&\lesssim\|a_{q}\rho_{q+1}\|_{L^{2}}\;\|\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|a_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho_{q+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&<2^{-q-1000}.\end{split}

where again the final estimate in (
5.13
) requires
\lambda_{q+1}
to be chosen large enough.

Hence combining (
3.7
), (
5.6
), (
5.7
), (
5.9
), (
5.11
), (
5.12
), and (
5.13
) we arrive at

(5.14)

\|R_{O}\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}<4\left(2^{-q-1000}\right)<2^{-q-100}.

And so finally from (
5.4
), (
5.5
), and (
5.14
) we conclude

\|E_{q+1}\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}<3\left(2^{-q-100}\right)<2^{-q-1}

which completes the induction.

5.5.
Proof of Item
3

From Lemma (
5.2
) we have

(5.15)

\|u_{q+1}-u_{q}\|_{L^{p}}=\|w_{q+1}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}<2^{-C^{\prime}(p)q}

for
C^{\prime}(p)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
and large enough
\lambda_{q+1}
. Now suppose
q(\alpha)
is the final index such that

\alpha+\frac{1-\epsilon_{q(\alpha)}}{2}\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}.

We divide the analysis into two cases:

•

q+1\leq q(\alpha)
, then we have

(5.16)

\|u_{q+1}-u_{q}\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}=\|w_{q+1}\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}\leq\left(2^{q(\alpha)}\max_{0\leq n\leq q(\alpha)}\|w_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}\right)2^{-q+1}.

We note the constant in (
5.16
) depends solely on
\alpha
.

•

q+1>q(\alpha)
then using Lemma
2.12
we have

(5.17)

\|u_{q+1}-u_{q}\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}=\|w_{q+1}\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{\alpha}\|w_{q+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{\alpha+\frac{1-\epsilon_{q+1}}{2}}\leq 2^{-C^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)q}

for
C^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)=-\frac{\alpha}{2}
and
\lambda_{q+1}
large enough.

From (
5.15
), (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) we obtain

\|u_{q+1}-u_{q}\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}+\|u_{q+1}-u_{q}\|_{L^{p}}\leq 2^{-C^{\prime}(p)q}+\left(2^{q(\alpha)+1}\max_{0\leq n\leq q(\alpha)}\|w_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}\right)2^{-q}+2^{-C^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)q}

hence the result with a suitable choice of constant
C(\alpha,p)
.

5.6.
Proof of Item
4

We need to show that

(5.18)

\|w_{q+1}\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim 1.

From (
5.3
) and
1
we see that

0<\left(\|E_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{1/2}\leq|a_{q}|\leq\left(3\|E_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{1/2}

and so

(5.19)

|a_{q}|\simeq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\|a_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\simeq 1.

Now using the
L^{2}
normalization of
\rho_{q+1}
and (
5.19
) we have that

(5.20)

1=\|\rho_{q+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|a_{q}\rho_{q+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|a_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-1}\simeq\|a_{q}\rho_{q+1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Now clearly we have

\|a_{q}\rho_{q+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\geq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{L^{2}}

and from Lemma
2.10
we may choose
\lambda_{q+1}
large enough to ensure that

(5.21)

\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\geq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{L^{2}}<\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining (
5.20
) and (
5.21
) we see that

(5.22)

1\lesssim\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Now we compute

(5.23)

2\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\cos(2\pi\sigma_{q+1}x)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})^{2}\,\cos(4\pi\sigma_{q+1}x).

Now analyzing the final expression in (
5.23
) we see using the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma that for
\lambda_{q+1}
large enough we have that

(5.24)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})^{2}\,\cos(4\pi\sigma_{q+1}x)>-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining (
5.22
), (
5.23

5.24

\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\cos(2\pi\sigma_{q+1}x)\right\|_{L^{2}}>\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda_{q+1}^{2}}(a_{q}\rho_{q+1})\right\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim 1

5.7.
Proof of Item
5

Recall that multiplication by
\cos(2\pi\sigma_{q+1}x)
corresponds to a translation on the frequency side by
\pm\sigma_{q+1}
. Hence from Definition
2.1
and Definition
5.1
we see

\operatorname{supp}\widehat{w}_{q+1}\subset B(\sigma_{q+1},2\lambda_{q+1}^{2})\cup B(-\sigma_{q+1},2\lambda_{q+1}^{2}).

To ensure this support is contained in the portion of the smooth Littlewood-Paley frequency projection which is identity:

\{\,2^{j}\leq|\xi|\leq\tfrac{12}{7}2^{j}\,\},

(recall again Definition
2.1
) it suffices (assuming
\lambda_{q+1}^{3}=2^{j}
for large
j\geq 0
) that

2^{j}\leq\frac{5}{4}\lambda_{q+1}^{3}-2\lambda_{q+1}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{5}{4}\lambda_{q+1}^{3}+2\lambda_{q+1}^{2}\leq\tfrac{12}{7}2^{j}.

Dividing by
\lambda_{q+1}^{2}

(5.25)

2\leq\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{q+1}\quad\text{and}\quad 2\leq\frac{13}{28}\lambda_{q+1}.

By choosing
\lambda_{q+1}

5.25
) holds and thus

(5.26)

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda_{q+1}^{3}}(w_{q+1})=w_{q+1}.

(
5.26
) gives item
5

5.8.
Proof of Item
6

We start with induction hypothesis

(5.27)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq q\\
n\not=m\end{subarray}}\|(u_{n}-u_{n-1})(u_{m}-u_{m-1})\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}<C_{1}-2^{-q}.

From Lemma
5.2
, we may choose
\lambda_{q+1}
large enough such that

(5.28)

2\|w_{q+1}\|_{L^{1}}\sum_{n\leq q}\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}<2^{-q-100}

so then using (
5.27
) and (
5.28
) we may write

(5.29)

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq q+1\\
n\not=m\end{subarray}}\|(u_{n}-u_{n-1})(u_{m}-u_{m-1})\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}&<\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq q\\
n\not=m\end{subarray}}\|(u_{n}-u_{n-1})(u_{m}-u_{m-1})\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}\\
&\qquad+2\sum_{n\leq q}\|(u_{n}-u_{n-1})w_{q+1}\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}\\
&<C_{1}-2^{-q}+2\|w_{q+1}\|_{L^{1}}\sum_{n\leq q}\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&<C_{1}-2^{-q}+2^{-q-100}\\
&<C_{1}-2^{-q-1}.\end{split}

This gives the first portion of item
6
. For the next part we again use inductive hypothesis

(5.30)

\sum_{n\leq q}\|(u_{n}-u_{n-1})^{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}<C_{2}-2^{-q+100}

as well as item
2
and (
5.14
) to deduce

(5.31)

\begin{split}\sum_{n\leq q+1}\|(u_{n}-u_{n-1})^{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}&<C_{2}-2^{-q+100}+\|w_{q+1}^{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}\\
&=C_{2}-2^{-q+100}+\frac{1}{3}||E_{q}+3w_{q+1}^{2}-E_{q}||_{\dot{H}^{-s}}\\
&\leq C_{2}-2^{-q+100}+\frac{1}{3}||E_{q}+3w_{q+1}^{2}||_{\dot{H}^{-s}}+\frac{1}{3}||E_{q}||_{\dot{H}^{-s}}\\
&<C_{2}-2^{-q+100}+2^{-q-101}+2^{-q-1}\\
&<C_{2}-2^{-q+99}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of item
6
and thus Proposition
4.1
.
