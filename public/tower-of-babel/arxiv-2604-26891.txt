An improved cowaist inequality for line bundles and consequences
Aditya Kumar

Abstract
. We prove a refinement of Gromov’s cowaist inequality in the case of Hermitian line bundles. Combined with the cowaist-systole estimates in recent work of Stryker, this gives sharp stable two-systolic inequalities for odd-dimensional complex projective spaces. For products of n n two-dimensional spheres with scalar curvature at least 2 ​ n 2n , it improves the upper bound on the stable two systole from O ⁡ ( n 4 ​ log ⁡ n ) O(n^{4}\log n) to O ⁡ ( n 3 ​ log ⁡ n ) O(n^{3}\log n) . In particular, for S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2} with scalar curvature at least 4 4 , it improves the stable two systole upper bound from 36 ​ π 36\pi to 12 ​ π 12\pi .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Systolic inequalities and almost flat bundles

The basic idea behind Stryker’s recent work
[
5
]
on stable two systoles in positive scalar curvature may be summarized in two parts.

(1)

Large systole produces almost flat line bundles.

A large stable two systole means that the length of the shortest nonzero lattice vector in
H_{2}(M;\mathbf{Z})/\mathrm{tors}
, is large. By stable norm/comass duality and the relevant lattice estimates, this gives elements with small comass in the dual lattice
H^{2}(M;\mathbf{Z})
. Since elements of
H^{2}(M;\mathbf{Z})
occur as first Chern classes of complex line bundles, Chern-Weil theory realizes such classes as curvature forms for unitary connections; in particular a small comass class leads to a line bundle with small curvature. Thus a large stable two systole produces almost flat line bundles.

(2)

Positive scalar curvature obstructs almost flat line bundles.

Gromov points out in his Four Lectures
[
4
, section 3.14.1]
that almost flat bundles constrain positive scalar curvature when they are detected by the relevant index. Indeed if the twisting curvature is sufficiently small compared with the scalar curvature lower bound, then in the Lichnerowicz formula the scalar curvature dominates the twisting curvature. The twisted Dirac operator then has trivial kernel, and hence zero index. This contradicts the existence of a nonzero topological index.

Quantitatively, the argument consists of two inequalities. The first is Gromov’s cowaist-scalar curvature inequality, which gives an upper bound for cowaist under a positive scalar curvature lower bound; see
[
3
, §1/4]
and also
[
5
, Theorem 3.4]
. The second is a cowaist-systole inequality, which gives a lower bound for line bundle cowaist in terms of the stable two systole. The stable systolic estimates in
[
5
]
are obtained by combining these two bounds.

1.2.
Main results

In this note we observe that the cowaist-scalar curvature inequality can be sharpened for Hermitian line bundles, and we combine this refinement with the cowaist-systole inequality of
[
5
]
to improve the corresponding stable two systolic estimates.

We use the following conventions. If
E\to M
is a Hermitian vector bundle with metric connection, then
\left\lVert R^{E}\right\rVert_{\infty}
denotes the supremum of the comass norm in the two-form variables together with the operator norm in the bundle direction.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed spin Riemannian manifold. A Hermitian vector bundle
E\to M
is called
\widehat{A}
-admissible if

\left\langle\widehat{A}(TM)\operatorname{ch}(E),[M]\right\rangle\neq 0.

The
\widehat{A}
-cowaist of
M
is

\widehat{A}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}(M,g)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\left\lVert R^{E}\right\rVert_{\infty}}:E\text{ is an }\widehat{A}\text{-admissible Hermitian vector bundle with metric connection}\right\}.

Restricting
\widehat{A}
-cowaist to line bundles gives

\widehat{A}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(M,g)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\left\lVert R^{L}\right\rVert_{\infty}}:L\text{ is an }\widehat{A}\text{-admissible Hermitian line bundle with unitary connection}\right\}.

For a closed spin manifold of real dimension
2n
, the cowaist-scalar curvature inequality gives the following upper bound for cowaist under a positive scalar curvature lower bound; see
[
3
, §1/4]
, and also
[
5
, Theorem 3.4]
.

\widehat{A}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(M^{2n},g)\leq\frac{4n(2n-1)}{\min_{M}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}}.

We improve it to the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{2n},g)
be a closed spin Riemannian manifold with
\sigma=\min_{M}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}>0
. Then

\widehat{A}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(M,g)\leq\frac{4n}{\sigma}.

Equivalently, every
\widehat{A}
-admissible Hermitian line bundle
L\to M
with unitary connection satisfies

(1.1)

\left\lVert R^{L}\right\rVert_{\infty}\geq\frac{\sigma}{4n}.

Combining Theorem
1.2
with the cowaist-systole estimates of
[
5
]
gives the following consequences. First, for the product of
2
-spheres with scalar curvature atleast
2n
, it improves the
O(n^{4}\log n)
estimate proved in
[
5
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
(S^{2})^{n}
with
\operatorname{Scal}_{g}\geq 2n
. Then

\operatorname{stsys}_{2}((S^{2})^{n},g)\leq 4\pi V_{n}\Gamma_{n},

where
V_{n}=\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil
and
\Gamma_{n}
is the lattice constant of Goodwillie-Hebda-Katz
[
2
]
. In particular, this gives an
O(n^{3}\log n)
bound.

In the case of
S^{2}\times S^{2}
it improves the
36\pi
estimate proved in
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
. From the example of product of two round spheres one expects that the optimal constant is
4\pi
.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
g
is a Riemannian metric on
S^{2}\times S^{2}
with
\operatorname{Scal}_{g}\geq 4
, then
\operatorname{stsys}_{2}(S^{2}\times S^{2},g)\leq 12\pi.

Finally, it also leads to the sharp stable two-systolic inequalities for odd-dimensional complex projective spaces. This case is relatively easier because
b_{2}=1
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
N
be odd. If
g
is a Riemannian metric on
\mathbf{CP}^{N}
satisfying
\operatorname{Scal}_{g}\geq 4N(N+1)
then

\operatorname{stsys}_{2}(\mathbf{CP}^{N},g)\leq\pi.

Remark 1.6
.

In a very recent work
[
1
]
, Cecchini, Hirsch, and Zeidler have established the above inequality for all complex projective spaces using
spin^{c}
-line bundles. In fact they also prove a remarkable rigidity statement for the equality case: up to the stated normalization and pullback by a biholomorphism, the equality case is the Fubini–Study metric.

It seems that an extension of
\widehat{A}
-cowaist from Hermitian line bundles over spin manifolds to determinant line bundle over
spin^{c}
-manifolds will recover the sharp systolic inequality for all complex projective spaces. However, we are unable to obtain any rigidity result like
[
1
]
.

2.
Proof of the Theorem
1.2

Let
L\to M
be an
\widehat{A}
-admissible Hermitian line bundle. The twisted spin Dirac operator
D_{L}
has nonzero index, hence has a nonzero harmonic spinor. The Lichnerowicz formula is

(2.1)

D_{L}^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\frac{\operatorname{Scal}_{g}}{4}+\mathcal{R}^{L},

where, in a local orthonormal frame
e_{1},\ldots,e_{2n}
,

\mathcal{R}^{L}(\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p,q=1}^{2n}c(e_{p})c(e_{q})\psi\otimes R^{L}(e_{p},e_{q}).

Instead of applying the triangle inequality over all
\binom{2n}{2}=n(2n-1)
coordinate two-planes, we use the fact that
R^{L}
is a scalar-valued skew-Hermitian
2
-form because
L
is a line bundle. This allows us to use a normal form argument as follows.

Fix a point
x\in M
. Since
L_{x}
is one-dimensional and the connection is unitary, write
R^{L}=i\alpha
with
\alpha
a real two-form at
x
. Choose an orthonormal basis putting
\alpha
in skew-normal form:

(2.2)

\alpha=\lambda_{1}e^{1}\wedge e^{2}+\lambda_{2}e^{3}\wedge e^{4}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}e^{2n-1}\wedge e^{2n}.

For a two-form in this normal form, its comass is
\max_{j}|\lambda_{j}|
. With our convention for the line bundle curvature norm, this means
\left\lVert R^{L}\right\rVert_{x}=\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{\operatorname{comass}}=\max_{j}|\lambda_{j}|.

In the basis (
2.2
), the twisting term becomes

\mathcal{R}^{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}i\lambda_{j}c(e_{2j-1})c(e_{2j}).

Set
B_{j}=ic(e_{2j-1})c(e_{2j})
. The operators
B_{j}
are self-adjoint involutions, and they commute because they involve disjoint Clifford generators. Hence they can be diagonalized simultaneously, with eigenvalues
\pm 1
. Therefore the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{R}^{L}
are of the form
\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}
, where each
\epsilon_{j}
equals
\pm 1
. It follows that

(2.3)

\left\lVert\mathcal{R}^{L}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|\leq n\max_{j}|\lambda_{j}|=n\left\lVert R^{L}\right\rVert_{x}.

We now integrate (
2.1
) against the nonzero harmonic spinor
\psi
. Since
D_{L}\psi=0
, we get

0=\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\frac{\operatorname{Scal}_{g}}{4}|\psi|^{2}+\langle\mathcal{R}^{L}\psi,\psi\rangle\right).

Using (
2.3
),

\langle\mathcal{R}^{L}\psi,\psi\rangle\geq-n\left\lVert R^{L}\right\rVert_{\infty}|\psi|^{2}.

Thus

0\geq\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\left(\frac{\sigma}{4}-n\left\lVert R^{L}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)|\psi|^{2}\right).

If
\left\lVert R^{L}\right\rVert_{\infty}<\sigma/(4n)
, the integrand is positive wherever
\psi
is nonzero, a contradiction. Therefore every
\widehat{A}
-admissible line bundle satisfies (
1.1
). Taking reciprocals gives Theorem
1.2
.

3.
Proofs of Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
,
1.5

3.1.
Odd complex projective spaces

We now show a cowaist-systole inequality for odd-dimensional complex projective spaces. This is just a consequence of combining existing classical results. The
N=3
case is also explicitly covered by Stryker. We emphasize that since
b_{2}=1
for complex projective spaces so the stable norm/comass duality gives sharp information.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N
be odd, and let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
\mathbf{CP}^{N}
. Then

(3.1)

\widehat{A}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(\mathbf{CP}^{N},g)\geq\frac{\operatorname{stsys}_{2}(g)}{\pi(N+1)}.

Proof.

As
N
is odd,
(N+1)
is even. So we can take the line bundle
L
to be the square root of the anticanonical line bundle, i.e.,
L=K_{\mathbf{CP}^{N}}^{-1/2}=\mathcal{O}((N+1)/2)
. Since
c_{1}(L)=c_{1}(T\mathbf{CP}^{N})/2
, the Chern character of
L
satisfies
\operatorname{ch}(L)=e^{c_{1}(T\mathbf{CP}^{N})/2}
. Therefore the index pairing defining
\widehat{A}
-admissibility is

\langle\widehat{A}(T\mathbf{CP}^{N})\operatorname{ch}(L),[\mathbf{CP}^{N}]\rangle=\langle\operatorname{Td}(T\mathbf{CP}^{N}),[\mathbf{CP}^{N}]\rangle=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{CP}^{N}})=1.

The last equality follows from Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch. Hence
L
is
\widehat{A}
-admissible.

Now let,
H=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))
be the positive generator of
H^{2}(\mathbf{CP}^{N};\mathbf{Z})
, normalized by
H([\mathbf{CP}^{1}])=1
. Since
H_{2}(\mathbf{CP}^{N};\mathbf{Z})\cong\mathbf{Z}
, stable norm/comass duality gives
\|H\|_{*}=1/\operatorname{stsys}_{2}(g)
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose a closed two-form
\alpha
representing
H
such that

\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\leq\|H\|_{*}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi(N+1)}.

Then as
L=\mathcal{O}((N+1)/2)
,
\frac{N+1}{2}\alpha
represents
c_{1}(L)
, so
L
admits a unitary connection with curvature
R^{L}=-2\pi i\,\frac{N+1}{2}\alpha
. Therefore

\|R^{L}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\pi\frac{N+1}{2}\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\leq\pi(N+1)\|H\|_{*}+\varepsilon=\frac{\pi(N+1)}{\operatorname{stsys}_{2}(g)}+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary and
L
is
\widehat{A}
-admissible, Definition
1.1
gives the desired lower bound.

\widehat{A}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(\mathbf{CP}^{N},g)\geq\frac{\operatorname{stsys}_{2}(g)}{\pi(N+1)}.

∎

Now take
g
such that
\operatorname{Scal}_{g}\geq 4N(N+1)
. Since
\dim=2N
, Theorem
1.2
gives

(3.2)

\widehat{A}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(\mathbf{CP}^{N},g)\leq\frac{1}{N+1}.

Combining this with (
3.1
) one gets

(3.3)

\operatorname{stsys}_{2}(\mathbf{CP}^{N},g)\leq\pi.

3.2.
Product of spheres

Unlike the complex projective case, here
b_{2}=n>1
, as such we will just state the following non-trivial cowaist-systole inequality due to Stryker.

Lemma 3.2

(Lemma 6.3 in
[
5
]
)
.

If
g
is any Riemannian metric on
(S^{2})^{n}
. Then

(3.4)

\widehat{A}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}((S^{2})^{n},g)\geq\frac{\operatorname{stsys}_{2}((S^{2})^{n},g)}{2\pi V_{n}\Gamma_{n}}.

Here
V_{n}=\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rceil.

Further,
\Gamma_{2}=3/2

[
2
, Proposition 2.3]
and
\Gamma_{n}=O(n\log n)

[
2
, Section 2]
.

Now consider a metric
g
with
\operatorname{Scal}_{g}\geq 2n
. Since
\dim=2n
, Theorem
1.2
gives

(3.5)

\widehat{A}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}((S^{2})^{n},g)\leq 2.

Combining this with (
3.4
) one gets

(3.6)

\operatorname{stsys}_{2}((S^{2})^{n},g)\leq 4\pi V_{n}\Gamma_{n}.

In general one has
V_{n}=O(n^{2})
and
\Gamma_{n}=O(n\log n)

[
2
, Section 2]
. Therefore we get

\operatorname{stsys}_{2}((S^{2})^{n},g)\leq O(n^{3}\log n)

For
n=2
, one explicitly has
V_{2}=2
and
\Gamma_{2}=3/2

[
2
, Proposition 2.3]
, one obtains

(3.7)

\operatorname{stsys}_{2}(S^{2}\times S^{2},g)\leq 12\pi.
