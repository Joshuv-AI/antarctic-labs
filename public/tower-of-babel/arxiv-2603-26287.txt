A Galerkin Finite Element Method for the Fractional Calderón Problem
Mukul Dwivedi, Jesse Railo, Andreas Rupp

Abstract
. We study a numerical reconstruction strategy for the potential in the fractional Calderón problem from a single partial exterior measurement. The forward model is the fractional Schrödinger equation in a bounded domain, with prescribed exterior Dirichlet datum and corresponding measurement of the exterior flux in an open observation set. Motivated by single-measurement uniqueness results based on unique continuation [ 19 ] , we propose a decomposition strategy and a Galerkin–Tikhonov method to recover the potential by a stabilized least-squares quotient in a dedicated coefficient space. We prove the existence and uniqueness of the discrete reconstructor and establish conditional convergence under natural consistency and parameter choice assumptions. We further derive a priori error estimates for the reconstructed state and for the coefficient reconstruction, and combine the latter with logarithmic stability for the continuous inverse problem to obtain a total coefficient error bound. The framework cleanly separates the forward solver from the inverse reconstruction step and is compatible with practical truncation and quadrature schemes for the integral fractional Laplacian. Numerical experiments in one and two space dimensions illustrate stability with respect to noise and demonstrate reconstructions of both smooth and discontinuous potentials.

1.
Introduction

The classical Calderón problem asks whether one can determine the interior electrical conductivity of a medium from voltage and current measurements made only on the boundary.
In its conductivity form, one seeks to recover
\gamma
in

\nabla\!\cdot(\gamma\nabla u)=0,\qquad\text{in some domain }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},

from the Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DN) map that sends boundary voltages to boundary currents
[
12
]
.
After Calderón’s seminal observation, global uniqueness was established in many settings, including the foundational result of Sylvester–Uhlmann in
d\geq 3

[
44
]
, the two-dimensional theory of Astala–Päivärinta
[
5
]
, and partial data results such as
[
26
]
; see also
[
45
]
for a broader perspective. From the computational standpoint, Calderón–type inverse problems are a canonical example of instability: even when uniqueness holds, the reconstruction map is highly ill–posed and regularization is essential
[
17
,
25
]
.

In many applications, classical diffusion models are replaced by nonlocal operators that capture long-range interactions, anomalous transport, or jump processes.
A central example is the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
with
s\in(0,1)
, which admits several equivalent formulations (Fourier multipliers, singular integrals, extensions)
[
29
]
and appears as the generator of symmetric stable Lévy processes
[
4
]
.
Fractional and nonlocal PDEs are used in models of anomalous diffusion and transport
[
33
]
, in materials with nonlocal interactions, and in related areas; see, for instance,
[
10
,
32
,
36
]
.
In this setting, the natural boundary interaction is typically replaced by an exterior interaction, which leads to inverse problems driven by exterior data.

In this work, we consider the fractional Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u+qu=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=f&\text{in }\Omega_{e}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
s\in(0,1)
is fixed,
q
is an unknown potential supported in
\Omega
,
the solution
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
u-f\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
, and
f
is an exterior Dirichlet datum prescribed in
\Omega_{e}
. The corresponding DN map
\Lambda_{q}
assigns to
f
the exterior nonlocal flux of the solution and can be defined rigorously via the natural bilinear form associated with
(-\Delta)^{s}
; see
[
20
,
42
]
and equation (
2.4
).
We keep the analysis in general dimension
d\geq 1
, while the numerical experiments in this paper focus on
d\leq 2
.

A nonlocal analogue of the Calderón problem is to determine
q
from (partial) knowledge of
\Lambda_{q}
.
This was introduced for the fractional Schrödinger equation in
[
20
]
, where global uniqueness was proved even when exterior data are supported in one nonempty open set and observations are made on another (possibly disjoint) open set.
A key feature of the nonlocal setting is that
(-\Delta)^{s}
couples
\Omega
directly to
\Omega_{e}
, which leads to strong unique continuation and Runge approximation properties
[
20
,
40
]
.
These properties, in particular, prove that
q
can be recovered from a single non-zero exterior datum together with exterior observations on an arbitrary open set
[
19
]
.
The fractional Calderón theory has rapidly expanded to broader operators and geometries, including variable coefficient nonlocal operators
[
18
]
, lower order nonlocal perturbations
[
6
]
, higher order local perturbations
[
14
]
, time-dependent models
[
27
]
, and unbounded domains via suitable Poincaré inequalities
[
35
]
.

Despite its uniqueness, the problem remains severely ill-posed.
Logarithmic stability is essentially optimal, and exponential instability phenomena can occur
[
38
,
39
]
, while refined single-measurement stability estimates are studied in
[
41
]
.
Consequently, robust reconstruction from noisy data requires regularization and careful discretization that respects the nonlocal forward operator.

On the numerical side, two main difficulties interact: the nonlocality of the fractional Laplacian typically produces a dense system matrix, meaning that most degrees of freedom interact with many others, so assembly and linear algebra are substantially
more expensive than in local problems, while the inverse step amplifies noise and must be regularized.
For the fractional forward model, many discretization strategies exist, including finite difference–quadrature schemes
[
16
,
24
]
,
finite element methods for the integral fractional Laplacian
[
2
,
3
,
7
]
,
and approaches based on extensions or related operator-theoretic formulations
[
11
,
23
,
34
]
. For inverse problems, one may use optimization methods, regularization methods, or monotonicity-based techniques for suitable classes of potentials
[
21
,
22
]
.
In the single-measurement setting, Li
[
31
]
proposed a finite difference reconstruction method based on Tikhonov regularization and treats the simultaneous recovery of a potential and an internal source
[
30
]
. Very recently, a Bayesian formulation for the single-measurement fractional Calderón problem has been developed in
[
28
]
, further emphasizing the need for
more direct methods.

The purpose of the present work is to provide a finite element realization of the unique continuation based single-measurement reconstruction principle of
[
19
]
.
Our method separates the computational pipeline into a forward solve for (
1.1
) and a subsequent regularized state-recovery step that inverts an exterior restriction operator.
This separation is computationally advantageous: the discrete observation operator depends only on the geometry, the observation set, and the fractional order, so it can be assembled once and reused across reconstructions.
Moreover, because the reconstructed state vanishes in the exterior domain, the fractional Laplacian of the reconstructed state on the observation set can be evaluated through a nonsingular integral over
\Omega
, which improves quadrature robustness.
After recovering the state, we reconstruct
q
through a stabilized least-squares quotient that
avoids pointwise division by small values of
u
. On the analytical side, we prove convergence of the reconstructed state to the exact solution and derive an
a priori
error estimate for the state under natural consistency and parameter-coupling assumptions. We also quantify the coefficient reconstruction error at the discrete level and combine it with single-measurement logarithmic stability to obtain a total error estimate for the recovered potential. The numerical experiments, implemented in one and two space dimensions, illustrate stability with respect to noise, discretization, and regularization, and show that both smooth and discontinuous potentials can be recovered.
The contribution of the present paper is therefore numerical-analytical: we translate the existing single-measurement
theory into a reusable Galerkin framework and identify the main consistency, regularization, and coefficient-recovery issues that arise in its implementation.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
introduces the continuous forward problem, the DN map, and the reduction of the single-measurement inverse problem to an exterior unique continuation inversion. Section
3
develops the discrete reconstruction method, and Section
4
presents numerical experiments. We conclude with a discussion in Section
5
.

2.
Mathematical setting and single-measurement reconstruction

This section records the continuous model and the single-measurement reconstruction principle that motivates the discrete algorithm in Section
3
.
The forward variational framework for the fractional Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) is available in
general space dimensions
d\geq 1
, and the same is true for the single-measurement
reconstruction principle used below. More precisely, the weak formulation and DN map are
treated in the
d
-dimensional setting in
[
20
]
, while the single-measurement uniqueness and reconstruction result of
[
19
]
is stated for all
d\geq 1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain with exterior
\Omega_{e}
.
We write
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the standard fractional Sobolev space.
For an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we use the restriction space equipped with the quotient norm
[
39
, Sec. 2]
, i.e.,

H^{s}(U):=\{u|_{U}:\ u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\},\qquad\|u\|_{H^{s}(U)}:=\inf\{\|\mu\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\;:\;\mu|_{U}=u\}.

We also use the interior space

\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega):=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\,H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

whose elements can be identified with
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-functions supported in
\overline{\Omega}
.
For an open set
W\subset\Omega_{e}
, we define
\widetilde{H}^{s}(W)
analogously and denote its dual by
H^{-s}(W):=(\widetilde{H}^{s}(W))^{\ast}
. For sufficiently regular
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, the integral fractional Laplacian is given by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=c_{d,s}\,\mathrm{P.V.}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy,

(2.1)

with a normalization constant
c_{d,s}>0
, see
[
29
]
and references therein.
Associated with (
2.1
) is the bilinear energy form

a(u,v):=\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\big(v(x)-v(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy,

(2.2)

which is continuous on
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and satisfies

\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u,v\rangle=a(u,v)
in the distributional sense for suitable
u,v
; see
[
15
,
29
,
36
]
.

Let
q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be real-valued.
Given an exterior datum
f\in H^{s}(\Omega_{e})
, we consider the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
).
In weak form: find
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
u=f
in
\Omega_{e}
and

a(u,\phi)+\int_{\Omega}q\,u\,\phi\,dx=0\qquad\forall\phi\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega).

(2.3)

As in
[
19
]
, well-posedness is ensured by the following assumption.

Assumption 2.1

(No zero Dirichlet eigenvalue)
.

The only
w\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
satisfying

a(w,\phi)+\int_{\Omega}q\,w\,\phi\,dx=0
for all
\phi\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
is
w\equiv 0
.

If
q
satisfies Assumption
2.1
, then for each
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(up to extension), there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to (
2.3
) such that
u-f\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
; see
[
19
]
. Let
u
be the solution of (
1.1
) (equivalently, (
2.3
)) with exterior datum
f
.
The fractional DN map is the operator
\Lambda_{q}
that assigns to
f
the exterior nonlocal flux,

\Lambda_{q}f:=\big((-\Delta)^{s}u\big)\big|_{\Omega_{e}}\in H^{-s}(\Omega_{e}),

(2.4)

interpreted in the distributional sense.
This agrees with the standard weak definition via the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
; see
[
20
,
42
]
.

Fix nonempty open sets
W_{1},W_{2}\subset\Omega_{e}
.
In the partial data setting, one prescribes exterior inputs supported in
W_{1}
and measures DN data on
W_{2}
.
In the single-measurement problem, we choose one exterior datum

f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(W_{1}),

(2.5)

while the prescribed data is

g:=\Lambda_{q}f\big|_{W_{2}}.

(2.6)

To connect directly with the discretization, we impose the geometric separation that makes the measurement pointwise.
Throughout the reconstruction procedure, we assume that

W_{2}\subset\subset\Omega_{e},\qquad\operatorname{dist}(W_{2},\Omega)=:d_{0}>0.

(2.7)

Remark 2.2
.

For numerical purposes and simplicity, we set
W_{1}=W_{2}=:W
. This choice is made only to simplify notation. Nothing essential changes if
W_{1}\neq W_{2}
. This only introduces additional symbols in the notation and definitions; the arguments extend without essential change to the case
W_{1}\neq W_{2}
.

Since
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(W)
, we may extend
f
by
0
to a globally smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which we denote by
\bar{u}_{f}
.
We use the standard decomposition

u=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0},\qquad\iff\qquad u_{0}=u-\bar{u}_{f}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega).

(2.8)

Restricting (
2.4
) to
W
and using
u_{0}=0
in
\Omega_{e}
, we obtain on
W

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}=g-(-\Delta)^{s}\bar{u}_{f},

in the appropriate distributional sense.
This yields the preprocessed measurement

\mu:=g-\big((-\Delta)^{s}\bar{u}_{f}\big)\big|_{W}.

(2.9)

In the noiseless continuous model,
\mu=((-\Delta)^{s}u_{0})|_{W}
.

The unique continuation step recovers
u_{0}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
from
\mu
, see
[
19
]
. Once
u_{0}
is reconstructed, one recovers
u=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}
and then determines
q
from the interior identity
(-\Delta)^{s}u+qu=0
in
\Omega
.
In computations, the coefficient recovery is carried out in a stabilized (regularized) manner; see Section
3
.

Lemma 2.3

(Pointwise meaning of the preprocessed measurement)
.

Assume (
2.7
) and
W
is bounded.
If
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is extended by
0
outside
\Omega
, then for every
x\in W
the quantity

(\mathcal{L}v)(x):=(-\Delta)^{s}v(x)=-c_{d,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{v(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy

(2.10)

is well-defined and
\mathcal{L}v
is smooth on
W
.
Moreover, one has the bound

\|\mathcal{L}v\|_{L^{\infty}(W)}\leq C(d_{0},s,\Omega)\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(2.11)

and for every integer
m\geq 0
there exists
C_{m}>0
such that

\|\mathcal{L}v\|_{H^{m}(W)}\leq C_{m}\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall v\in L^{2}(\Omega).

(2.12)

In particular,
\mu
in (
2.9
) is a pointwise-defined function on
W
whenever
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in W
.
Since
\operatorname{dist}(x,\Omega)\geq d_{0}
, we have
|x-y|\geq d_{0}
for all
y\in\Omega
; hence,
|x-y|^{-d-2s}\leq d_{0}^{-d-2s}
and

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v(y)|}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\leq d_{0}^{-d-2s}\int_{\Omega}|v(y)|\,dy\leq d_{0}^{-d-2s}|\Omega|^{1/2}\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This proves absolute convergence and (
2.11
).
Continuity of
\mathcal{L}v
on
W
follows from dominated convergence since
x\mapsto|x-y|^{-d-2s}
is smooth on
W
for each fixed
y\in\Omega
and is uniformly dominated by
d_{0}^{-d-2s}
. For each multi-index
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\beta|\leq m
, there exists a constant

C_{\beta}>0
such that

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}k(x,y)|\leq C_{\beta}\,d_{0}^{-d-2s-|\beta|}\text{ for all }x\in W,\ y\in\Omega,

where
k(x,y):=|x-y|^{-d-2s}
. Differentiating under the integral sign gives

\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\mathcal{L}v)(x)=-c_{d,s}\int_{\Omega}v(y)\,\partial_{x}^{\beta}k(x,y)\,dy.

Using Cauchy–Schwarz in
y
, we obtain

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\mathcal{L}v)(x)|\leq c_{d,s}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|v(y)|^{2}\,dy\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}k(x,y)|^{2}\,dy\Big)^{1/2}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

uniformly in
x\in W
. Since
W
is bounded,

\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\mathcal{L}v)\in L^{2}(W)
and

\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\mathcal{L}v)\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
by Hölder’s inequality.
Summing over all
|\beta|\leq m
yields (
2.12
).
∎

The operator
\mathcal{L}
in (
2.10
) is injective and has a dense range in
H^{-s}(W)
by the exterior unique continuation property for the fractional Laplacian for any open
W\subset\Omega_{e}
, see
[
20
,
40
]
.
In our setting, we additionally assumed that
W
is bounded.
This boundedness is used in Lemma
2.3
to obtain the
L^{2}(W)
-based smoothing estimates and, following the cutoff argument in
[
19
, Lemma 2.2]
, it is also the natural hypothesis for compactness of
\mathcal{L}
.
Under the separation assumption (
2.7
),
\mathcal{L}
is also compact (indeed, smooth), which is the fundamental mechanism behind the ill-posedness of
\mathcal{L}u_{0}|_{W}=\mu
and the need for regularization
[
19
]
.
In Section
3
, we develop a fully discrete regularized inversion of
\mathcal{L}u_{0}|_{W}=\mu
and a stable coefficient recovery strategy.

3.
Discrete unique continuation and reconstruction of the potential

3.1.
Discrete setting: truncation domain, finite element spaces, and discrete norms

To perform computations, we truncate
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to a bounded domain

\Omega_{R}:=(-R,R)^{d},\qquad\Omega\cup W\subset\Omega_{R},

where
R>0
is chosen to be sufficiently large.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a quasi-uniform mesh of
\Omega_{R}
with mesh size
h
, and let

V_{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
be the continuous, piecewise-polynomial finite element space of degree
p\geq 1
, supported in
\Omega_{R}
.
We define the interior subspace:

V_{h,0}:=\{v_{h}\in V_{h}:\ v_{h}=0\ \text{on}\ \Omega_{R}\setminus\Omega\}.

(3.1)

Assumption 3.1

(Mesh fitted to
\Omega
)
.

Assume that
\Omega
is polygonal/polyhedral and that, for all sufficiently small
h
, the mesh

\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\Omega_{R}
is fitted to
\Omega
, i.e.
\partial\Omega
is a union of faces of

\mathcal{T}_{h}
. In particular, each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
satisfies either

K\subset\overline{\Omega}
or
K\subset\Omega_{R}\setminus\Omega
.
Then
V_{h,0}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
(identified with its zero extension to
\Omega_{R}
), and hence

V_{h,0}\subset\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
for every
0<s<1
.

Let
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{0}}
be the nodal basis of
V_{h,0}
.
Let
\bar{u}_{f}
denote the zero extension of
f
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and define

u_{h,f}:=I_{h}\bar{u}_{f}\in V_{h},

(3.2)

where
I_{h}
is the nodal interpolant on
\Omega_{R}
. Since
\bar{u}_{f}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{R})
, standard interpolation yields

\|\bar{u}_{f}-u_{h,f}\|_{H^{m}(\Omega_{R})}\leq Ch^{p+1-m}\|\bar{u}_{f}\|_{H^{p+1}(\Omega_{R})}\qquad(0\leq m\leq p+1).

(3.3)

In particular, for every
x\in W
the quantity
(-\Delta)^{s}u_{h,f}(x)
is pointwise well-defined
(see Lemma
2.3
).

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{W}
be a shape-regular mesh, so each element
K\subset W
satisfies

|K|\simeq h^{d}
and let

S_{h}^{W}\subset C(\overline{W})
be the continuous, piecewise-polynomial finite element space on

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{W}
. Denote by
\{\vartheta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{W}}
the nodal basis of
S_{h}^{W}
, and by

\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{W}}\subset\overline{W}
the associated nodal points. For a fixed polynomial degree,

N_{W}=\dim(S_{h}^{W})\simeq C\,h^{-d}.

We define the data space

Y:=\mathbb{R}^{N_{W}},\qquad\langle y,z\rangle_{Y}:=y^{\top}\mathbf{W}z,\qquad\|y\|_{Y}^{2}:=y^{\top}\mathbf{W}y,

where
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{W}\times N_{W}}
with

\mathbf{W}_{ij}:=\int_{W}\vartheta_{i}(x)\vartheta_{j}(x)\,dx
, and
\mathbf{W}
is symmetric positive definite (SPD).
For any continuous function
\psi
on
W
, we identify its sampled vector as follows:

\psi_{W}:=(\psi(x_{1}),\dots,\psi(x_{N_{W}}))^{\top}\in Y.

(3.4)

We also define the nodal interpolant

I_{h}^{W}\psi:=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{W}}\psi(x_{k})\vartheta_{k}\in S_{h}^{W}.

Define the discrete observation bilinear form by

Q_{h}^{W}(\phi,\psi):=\phi_{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}\psi_{W}=\int_{W}I_{h}^{W}\phi(x)\,I_{h}^{W}\psi(x)\,dx,\qquad\phi,\psi\in C(\overline{W}).

(3.5)

Thus, for any continuous
\psi
on
W
,

\|\psi_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}=Q_{h}^{W}(\psi,\psi)=\|I_{h}^{W}\psi\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}.

(3.6)

Assumption 3.2

(Order-
m
consistency of the discrete observation inner product on
W
)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{W}
be a shape-regular, quasi-uniform partition of
W
, and let

S_{h}^{W}
,
I_{h}^{W}
, and
\mathbf{W}
be defined as above.
Assume there exist an integer
m>d/2
and a constant
C>0
, independent of
h
, such that
for all
\phi\in H^{m}(W)
,

\|I_{h}^{W}\phi-\phi\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq Ch^{m}\|\phi\|_{H^{m}(W)}.

(3.7)

Since
m>d/2
, the nodal values
\phi(x_{k})
are well defined for
\phi\in H^{m}(W)
by
Sobolev embedding. Moreover, Assumption
3.2
implies

\left|\int_{W}\phi(x)\psi(x)\,dx-Q_{h}^{W}(\phi,\psi)\right|\leq Ch^{m}\|\phi\|_{H^{m}(W)}\|\psi\|_{H^{m}(W)}\qquad\forall\,\phi,\psi\in H^{m}(W).

(3.8)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{W}\phi\psi\,dx-Q_{h}^{W}(\phi,\psi)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{W}\phi\psi\,dx-\int_{W}I_{h}^{W}\phi\,I_{h}^{W}\psi\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{W}(\phi-I_{h}^{W}\phi)\psi\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{W}I_{h}^{W}\phi\,(\psi-I_{h}^{W}\psi)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi-I_{h}^{W}\phi\|_{L^{2}(W)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(W)}+\|I_{h}^{W}\phi\|_{L^{2}(W)}\|\psi-I_{h}^{W}\psi\|_{L^{2}(W)}.

Using Assumption
3.2
and

\|I_{h}^{W}\phi\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(W)}+\|I_{h}^{W}\phi-\phi\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{m}(W)},

we obtain (
3.8
).

Lemma 3.3

(Discrete data norm converges to
L^{2}(W)
)
.

Assume
\mathrm{dist}(W,\Omega)=d_{0}>0
and Assumption
3.2
for some integer
m>d/2
.
Then for every
v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
,

\|(\mathcal{L}v)_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}\longrightarrow\|\mathcal{L}v\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}\qquad\text{as }h\to 0,

(3.9)

and there exists
C>0
independent of
h
such that

\big|\,\|(\mathcal{L}v)_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}-\|\mathcal{L}v\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}\,\big|\leq C\,h^{m}\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\,h^{m}\,\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

(3.10)

Proof.

Fix
v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
. By Lemma
2.3
,
\mathcal{L}v\in H^{m}(W)
and

\|\mathcal{L}v\|_{H^{m}(W)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Using (
3.6
) and (
3.8
) with
\phi=\psi=\mathcal{L}v
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigl|\,\|(\mathcal{L}v)_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}-\|\mathcal{L}v\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}\,\bigr|

\displaystyle=\left|Q_{h}^{W}(\mathcal{L}v,\mathcal{L}v)-\int_{W}|\mathcal{L}v|^{2}\,dx\right|\leq C\,h^{m}\,\|\mathcal{L}v\|_{H^{m}(W)}^{2}\leq C\,h^{m}\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

This proves (
3.10
). The convergence (
3.9
)
follows by letting
h\to 0
. Finally,

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

gives the last inequality in (
3.10
).
∎

Define
L_{h}:V_{h,0}\to Y
by sampling
\mathcal{L}v_{h}
at
\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{W}}\subset W
:

(L_{h}v_{h})_{k}:=(\mathcal{L}v_{h})(x_{k})=-c_{d,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{h}(y)}{|x_{k}-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy.

Writing
v_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}}v_{i}\varphi_{i}
, we obtain the dense matrix representation

L_{h}v_{h}=B\mathbf{v},\qquad B\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{W}\times N_{0}},\qquad B_{ki}:=-c_{d,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{i}(y)}{|x_{k}-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy,\quad\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{N_{0}})^{\top}.

(3.11)

Because
|x_{k}-y|\geq d_{0}
and
\varphi_{i}
is polynomial on each element, the integrand is smooth on each element
and can be assembled by standard element-wise quadrature of sufficiently high order. Assume that a noisy measurement
g^{\delta}
on
W
is available with noise level
\delta
in the sampled norm, i.e.,

\|g^{\delta}_{W}-g_{W}\|_{Y}\leq\delta.

(3.12)

We define the sampled noisy preprocessed data vector by

\mu_{W}^{\delta}:=\bigl(g^{\delta}(x_{k})-(\mathcal{L}_{h,R}u_{h,f})_{k}\bigr)_{k=1}^{N_{W}}\in Y,

(3.13)

where the practical evaluation operator
\mathcal{L}_{h,R}
is given nodewise by

(\mathcal{L}_{h,R}v)_{k}:=c_{d,s}\,\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_{\Omega_{R}}\frac{v(x_{k})-v(y)}{|x_{k}-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy,\qquad k=1,\dots,N_{W}.

3.2.
Truncated fractional energy and truncation error

We define the truncated bilinear form for
u,v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose support is contained in
\overline{\Omega}_{R}
by

a_{R}(u,v):=\frac{c_{d,s}}{2}\iint\limits_{\Omega_{R}\times\Omega_{R}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy+c_{d,s}\int\limits_{\Omega_{R}}u(x)v(x)\Big(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{R}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\Big)\,dx.

For every
v_{h}\in V_{h,0}
, we have
a_{R}(v_{h},v_{h})=a(v_{h},v_{h})
. Therefore, by the standard norm equivalence on

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a constant
C=C(d,s)>0
such that

\|v_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C\Bigl(\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+a(v_{h},v_{h})\Bigr)=C\Bigl(\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}+a_{R}(v_{h},v_{h})\Bigr)=C\|v_{h}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}.

Lemma 3.4

(Tail integral bound)
.

Let
\Omega_{R}=(-R,R)^{d}
and let
x\in\Omega_{R}
. Set

r_{x}:=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega_{R})=R-|x|_{\infty}.
Let
\Omega\subset(-\rho,\rho)^{d}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{R}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\leq C_{d,s}(R-\rho)^{-2s}\qquad\forall x\in\Omega.

(3.14)

Consequently, for every
v
supported in
\Omega
,

\int_{\Omega_{R}}v(x)^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{R}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\right)\,dx\leq C_{d,s}(R-\rho)^{-2s}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(3.15)

Proof.

Note that we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{R}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r_{x})}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}.

Using spherical coordinates centered at
x
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r_{x})}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}=|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\int_{r_{x}}^{\infty}r^{-1-2s}\,dr=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{2s}\,r_{x}^{-2s}.

Then the estimate (
3.14
) follows from
r_{x}\geq R-\rho
for all

x\in\Omega\subset(-\rho,\rho)^{d}
. Multiplying by
v(x)^{2}
, integrating over
\Omega_{R}
, using
\mathrm{supp}(v)\subset(-\rho,\rho)^{d}
gives the estimate (
3.15
).
∎

3.2.1.
Deterministic consistency of the preprocessed data and total consistency error

The practical reconstruction uses the discrete noisy preprocessed data vector
\mu_{W}^{\delta}\in Y
from (
3.13
) and the exact sampled preprocessed data vector
\mu_{W}\in Y
.
Since
\|g_{W}^{\delta}-g_{W}\|_{Y}\leq\delta
, we obtain

\|\mu_{W}^{\delta}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}\leq\|g_{W}^{\delta}-g_{W}\|_{Y}+\bigl\|\bigl((-\Delta)^{s}\bar{u}_{f}\bigr)_{W}-\bigl((-\Delta)^{s}u_{h,f}\bigr)_{W}\bigr\|_{Y}+\bigl\|\bigl((-\Delta)^{s}u_{h,f}\bigr)_{W}-\mathcal{L}_{h,R}u_{h,f}\bigr\|_{Y}.

We therefore define

\eta_{I}(h):=\bigl\|\bigl((-\Delta)^{s}\bar{u}_{f}\bigr)_{W}-\bigl((-\Delta)^{s}u_{h,f}\bigr)_{W}\bigr\|_{Y},\quad\text{ and }\quad\eta_{t}(h,R):=\bigl\|\bigl((-\Delta)^{s}u_{h,f}\bigr)_{W}-\mathcal{L}_{h,R}u_{h,f}\bigr\|_{Y}.

Hence

\|\mu_{W}^{\delta}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}\leq\delta+\eta_{I}(h)+\eta_{t}(h,R).

By the interpolation estimate for
u_{h,f}
, we obtain
\eta_{I}(h)\to 0
as
h\to 0
whereas
\eta_{t}(h,R)
is the additional truncation error caused by replacing the full-space operator by its practical evaluation on
\Omega_{R}
. Moreover, since
u_{h,f}=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{R}
, for each
measurement point
x_{k}\in W
one has

\bigl((-\Delta)^{s}u_{h,f}\bigr)(x_{k})-(\mathcal{L}_{h,R}u_{h,f})_{k}=c_{d,s}\,u_{h,f}(x_{k})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{R}}\frac{dy}{|x_{k}-y|^{d+2s}}.

Thus, by the same tail estimate as in Lemma
3.4
,
\eta_{t}(h,R)\to 0
as

R\to\infty
.
Finally, we write

\|\mu_{W}^{\delta}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}\leq\delta+\eta(h,R),

where
\eta(h,R):=\eta_{I}(h)+\eta_{t}(h,R)
.

3.3.
Discrete Tikhonov functional and the normal equations

On
V_{h,0}
, we use the consistent discrete norm

\|v_{h}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}:=a_{R}(v_{h},v_{h})+\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}.

(3.16)

Let
A_{0},M_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}\times N_{0}}
be the interior stiffness and mass matrices defined by

(A_{0})_{ij}:=a_{R}(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}),\qquad(M_{0})_{ij}:=(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}.

(3.17)

Then, for
v_{h}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{0}}v_{i}\varphi_{i}
,

\|v_{h}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}=\mathbf{v}^{\top}S\mathbf{v},

where
S:=A_{0}+M_{0}.

For
\alpha>0
, define the discrete Tikhonov functional
J_{h,\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}
by

J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v}):=\|B\mathbf{v}-\mu^{\delta}_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}+\alpha\,\mathbf{v}^{\top}S\mathbf{v}=(B\mathbf{v}-\mu^{\delta}_{W})^{\top}\mathbf{W}(B\mathbf{v}-\mu^{\delta}_{W})+\alpha\mathbf{v}^{\top}S\mathbf{v}.

(3.18)

Let
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{0}}
be a basis of
V_{h,0}
.
For any coefficient vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
, we set

\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{v}:=v_{h}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}}v_{i}\,\varphi_{i}\in V_{h,0}.

Let
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}
denote the unique minimizer of
J_{h,\alpha}
and set

u_{0,h}^{\alpha}:=\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h,0},\qquad u_{h}^{\alpha}:=u_{h,f}+u_{0,h}^{\alpha}.

Theorem 3.5

(Existence, uniqueness, and normal equations)
.

For every
\alpha>0
, the discrete Tikhonov functional (
3.18
) admits a unique minimizer

\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
. Moreover,
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}
is the unique solution of the
SPD linear system

(B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B+\alpha S)\,\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}=B^{\top}\mathbf{W}\,\mu^{\delta}_{W}.

(3.19)

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{W}
is symmetric, expanding (
3.18
) gives

J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{v}^{\top}B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B\mathbf{v}-2(\mu^{\delta}_{W})^{\top}\mathbf{W}B\mathbf{v}+(\mu^{\delta}_{W})^{\top}\mathbf{W}\mu^{\delta}_{W}+\alpha\mathbf{v}^{\top}S\mathbf{v}.

Hence
J_{h,\alpha}
is a
C^{1}
quadratic on
\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
with gradient

\nabla J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v})=2B^{\top}\mathbf{W}(B\mathbf{v}-\mu^{\delta}_{W})+2\alpha S\mathbf{v}=2(B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B+\alpha S)\mathbf{v}-2B^{\top}\mathbf{W}\mu^{\delta}_{W}.

The first-order optimality condition
\nabla J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v})=0
yields the normal equation (
3.19
).

By construction,
\|y\|_{Y}^{2}:=y^{\top}\mathbf{W}y
is the squared norm induced by the discrete measurement
inner product. Since the measurement weights are strictly positive,
the inner product is nondegenerate and
\mathbf{W}
is SPD. Therefore, for any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
,

\mathbf{v}^{\top}B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B\,\mathbf{v}=(B\mathbf{v})^{\top}\mathbf{W}(B\mathbf{v})\geq 0,

Hence
B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B
is symmetric positive semidefinite.
Since
a_{R}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the symmetric bilinear form that defines
A_{0}
on
V_{h,0}
,
then for
v_{h}=\sum_{i}v_{i}\varphi_{i}
we have

\mathbf{v}^{\top}A_{0}\mathbf{v}=a_{R}(v_{h},v_{h})\geq 0,

because
a_{R}(\cdot,\cdot)
is an energy (nonnegative on the diagonal). Thus
A_{0}
is symmetric positive semidefinite.

M_{0}
is the mass matrix. Therefore, for
\mathbf{v}\neq 0
,
the corresponding
v_{h}\not\equiv 0
and

\mathbf{v}^{\top}M_{0}\mathbf{v}=\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}>0.

Hence
M_{0}
is SPD.
For
\alpha>0
, define
K_{\alpha}:=B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B+\alpha S
. Then for any
\mathbf{v}\neq 0
,

\mathbf{v}^{\top}K_{\alpha}\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{\top}B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B\,\mathbf{v}+\alpha\,\mathbf{v}^{\top}S\mathbf{v}\geq 0+\alpha\,\mathbf{v}^{\top}S\mathbf{v}>0,

hence
K_{\alpha}\succ 0
, and (
3.19
) has a unique solution.

Finally, since
J_{h,\alpha}
is quadratic with Hessian
2K_{\alpha}\succ 0
, it is strictly convex on
\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
.
Therefore, its unique critical point is the unique global minimizer, i.e.
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}
exists and is unique.
∎

3.4.
Convergence of the reconstructed state

We now prove that
u_{h}^{\alpha}
converges to the true solution
u
as
h\to 0
,
R\to\infty
and
\alpha\to 0
with a standard parameter coupling.

Consider the continuous Tikhonov problem.
Let us consider the (noise-free) continuous functional

J_{\alpha}(v):=\|\mathcal{L}v-\mu\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}+\alpha\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},\qquad v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega).

(3.20)

Since
\alpha>0
,
J_{\alpha}
is strictly convex and coercive; hence it has a unique minimizer, which we denote by

u_{0}^{\alpha}:=\operatorname*{argmin}_{v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)}J_{\alpha}(v).

(3.21)

Set
u^{\alpha}:=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}^{\alpha}
.

We use the unique continuation property: if
v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{L}v=0
on
W
,
then
v\equiv 0
. This is exactly the injectivity needed for Tikhonov regularization to recover
u_{0}

as
\alpha\to 0
(it is the analytic input from the fractional Calderón problem theory
[
19
]
).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\Pi_{h}:\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)\to V_{h,0}
be the
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
-orthogonal projection, i.e.

(\Pi_{h}v-v,\chi_{h})_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0\qquad\forall\chi_{h}\in V_{h,0}.

Then
\|\Pi_{h}v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for all
v\in{\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)}
, and

\|\Pi_{h}v-v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to 0\qquad\text{as }h\to 0

for every
v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The stability
\|\Pi_{h}v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is the standard contractivity of orthogonal projections.
To prove strong convergence, fix
v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and
\varepsilon>0
. Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in

\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
, choose
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\|v-\phi\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\varepsilon.

By Assumption
3.1
, for all sufficiently small
h
the nodal interpolant

I_{h}^{0}\phi
belongs to
V_{h,0}
. Standard fitted-mesh interpolation gives

\|I_{h}^{0}\phi-\phi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{R})}\to 0\qquad\text{as }h\to 0,

(see, e.g.,
[
9
, Ch. 4]
).
Since
0<s<1
and
\Omega_{R}=(-R,R)^{d}
, the embedding

H^{1}(\Omega_{R})\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\Omega_{R})
is continuous. Thus, we have

\|I_{h}^{0}\phi-\phi\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to 0.

By the best-approximation property of orthogonal projections,

\|\Pi_{h}v-v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|I_{h}^{0}\phi-v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|I_{h}^{0}\phi-\phi\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\phi-v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Taking
\limsup_{h\to 0}
and then letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields the claim.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

If one prefers a direct fractional-space interpolation estimate, the Scott–Zhang analysis of

[
13
, Thm. 3.3]
provides such a result for
s\in(1/2,1)
. We do not use it in the proof
of Theorem
3.8
, since the density argument above works for all
0<s<1
.

Theorem 3.8

(Convergence of the reconstructed state
u_{h}^{\alpha}
)
.

Assume (
2.5
)–(
2.7
) hold and that the operator
\mathcal{L}
is injective. Let the noisy sampled DN data
g^{\delta}_{W}
satisfy (
3.12
), let
\mu^{\delta}_{W}
be the corresponding preprocessed data vector defined by (
3.13
), and the deterministic consistency error is
\eta(h,R)
. Choose a parameter rule
\alpha=\alpha(h,\delta,R)>0
such that

\alpha(h,\delta,R)\to 0,\qquad\frac{(\delta+\eta(h,R))^{2}}{\alpha(h,\delta,R)}\to 0\quad\text{as}\qquad h\to 0,\ R\to\infty,\ \delta\to 0.

(3.22)

Then, after extending by
0
outside
\Omega_{R}
, the reconstructed states satisfy

u_{h}^{\alpha}\to u\quad\text{in }H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
u=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}
,
u_{0}=\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}u_{0}^{\alpha}
, and
u_{0}^{\alpha}
satisfies (
3.21
).

Proof.

Recall from (
3.18
) that

J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v})=\|B\mathbf{v}-\mu^{\delta}_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}+\alpha\,\mathbf{v}^{\top}S\mathbf{v}=\|(\mathcal{L}v_{h})_{W}-\mu^{\delta}_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}+\alpha\,\|v_{h}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}.

Since
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}
is the unique minimizer of
J_{h,\alpha}
, by minimality and testing with
\mathbf{v}=0
, we obtain

J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha})\leq J_{h,\alpha}(0)=\|\mu^{\delta}_{W}\|_{Y}^{2},

hence

\alpha\,\|u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}=\alpha\,(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha})^{\top}S\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}\leq\|\mu^{\delta}_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}.

(3.23)

Ideal noiseless case (
\delta=0
).

Fix
\alpha>0
and replace the discrete noisy measurement data with the exact sampled preprocessed data vector
\mu_{W}
in (
3.18
).
Set the competitor

u_{0,h}^{\alpha,*}:=\Pi_{h}u_{0}^{\alpha}\in V_{h,0},\qquad\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha,*}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}\text{ its coefficient vector (i.e. }u_{0,h}^{\alpha,*}=\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha,*}\text{)}.

By minimality of
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}
,

J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha})\leq J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha,*})=\|(\mathcal{L}\Pi_{h}u_{0}^{\alpha})_{W}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}+\alpha\|\Pi_{h}u_{0}^{\alpha}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}.

(3.24)

By (
3.9
), Lemma
2.3
, and Lemma
3.6
, we have
\|(\mathcal{L}(\Pi_{h}u_{0}^{\alpha}-u_{0}^{\alpha}))_{W}\|_{Y}\to 0
as
h\to 0
.
Hence, using the above convergence, we obtain

\limsup_{h\to 0,\,R\to\infty}J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha})\leq\lim_{h\to 0,\,R\to\infty}J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha,*})\leq\|\mathcal{L}u_{0}^{\alpha}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}+\alpha\|u_{0}^{\alpha}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=J_{\alpha}(u_{0}^{\alpha}),

(3.25)

where
J_{\alpha}
denotes the continuous functional in (
3.20
) and we have used Lemma
3.3
. The
a priori
estimate (
3.23
) implies that
\{u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\}
, extended by
0
outside
\Omega_{R}
, is bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Since each
u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h,0}
is supported in
\overline{\Omega}
, we may view
\{u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\}
as a bounded
sequence in
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
. Hence, along a subsequence,

u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}\quad\text{weakly in }\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega),\qquad u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\to\bar{u}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega),

(3.26)

where we used the compact embedding
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Set
g_{h}:=\mathcal{L}u_{0,h}^{\alpha}-\mu
and
\bar{g}:=\mathcal{L}\bar{u}-\mu
on
W
.
By Lemma
2.3
, the operator

\mathcal{L}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to H^{m}(W)
is bounded for any integer
m\geq 1
. In particular,

\|g_{h}-\bar{g}\|_{H^{m}(W)}=\|\mathcal{L}(u_{0,h}^{\alpha}-\bar{u})\|_{H^{m}(W)}\leq C\,\|u_{0,h}^{\alpha}-\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\qquad(h\to 0).

Therefore,
g_{h}\to\bar{g}
strongly in
H^{m}(W)
and hence also in
L^{2}(W)
.
We apply Lemma
3.3
to
g_{h}
, by noting that
g_{h}\in H^{m}(W)
and
m>\dim(W)/2
, and obtain

Q_{h}^{W}(|g_{h}|^{2})=\int_{W}|g_{h}|^{2}\,dx+\mathcal{O}(h^{m})\qquad(h\to 0).

(3.27)

Since
g_{h}\to\bar{g}
in
L^{2}(W)
, we have
\int_{W}|g_{h}|^{2}\to\int_{W}|\bar{g}|^{2}
. Combining with
(
3.27
) yields

\|(\mathcal{L}u_{0,h}^{\alpha})_{W}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}\longrightarrow\|\mathcal{L}\bar{u}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}.

(3.28)

Next, by the weak lower semicontinuity of the
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm and (
3.28
),

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}(\bar{u})

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{L}\bar{u}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}+\alpha\|\bar{u}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{h\to 0,\,R\to\infty}\Big(\|(\mathcal{L}u_{0,h}^{\alpha})_{W}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}+\alpha\|u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}\Big)=\liminf_{h\to 0,\,R\to\infty}J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}).

(3.29)

Together with the limsup inequality (
3.25
) we obtain

J_{\alpha}(\bar{u})\leq J_{\alpha}(u_{0}^{\alpha})
.
Since
u_{0}^{\alpha}
is the unique minimizer of
J_{\alpha}
, it follows that
\bar{u}=u_{0}^{\alpha}
.
As the limit is unique, the whole sequence satisfies

u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\rightharpoonup u_{0}^{\alpha}\quad\text{in }\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega).

(3.30)

From (
3.25
) and (
3.29
) with
\bar{u}=u_{0}^{\alpha}
, we get

\lim_{h\to 0,\,R\to\infty}J_{h,\alpha}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha})=J_{\alpha}(u_{0}^{\alpha}).

Using (
3.28
) with
\bar{u}=u_{0}^{\alpha}
yields

\lim_{h\to 0,\,R\to\infty}\|\mathcal{L}u_{0,h}^{\alpha}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}=\|\mathcal{L}u_{0}^{\alpha}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2},

and the regularization terms converge as well

\lim_{h\to 0,\,R\to\infty}\|u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}=\|u_{0}^{\alpha}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Since
u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\rightharpoonup u_{0}^{\alpha}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the norms converge,
we conclude
u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\to u_{0}^{\alpha}
strongly in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Consequently,

u_{h}^{\alpha}=u_{h,f}+u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\to\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}^{\alpha}=:u^{\alpha}\qquad\text{in }H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad(h\to 0,\ R\to\infty),

for fixed
\alpha>0
.

Practical noisy case.

Return now to the discrete noisy measurement data
\mu_{W}^{\delta}
for
\delta>0
.
Since
\mu_{W}\in Y
is the ideal preprocessed sampled data vector, by the definitions of
\delta
and
\eta(h,R)
we have

\|\mu^{\delta}_{W}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}\leq\delta+\eta(h,R).

(3.31)

Let
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}^{\alpha}
be the minimizer of
J_{h,\alpha}
with
\mu_{W}
in place of
\mu^{\delta}
,
and set
\widehat{u}_{0,h}^{\alpha}:=\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}^{\alpha}
.
Subtracting the normal equation (
3.19
) and noise free analogue normal equation
(B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B+\alpha S)\,\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}^{\alpha}=B^{\top}\mathbf{W}\,\mu_{W}
gives

K_{\alpha}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}^{\alpha})=B^{\top}\mathbf{W}(\mu^{\delta}_{W}-\mu_{W}),\qquad K_{\alpha}:=B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B+\alpha S.

Testing by
\mathbf{e}:=\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\alpha}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}^{\alpha}
yields the identity

\underbrace{\|B\mathbf{e}\|_{Y}^{2}}_{=\mathbf{e}^{\top}B^{\top}\mathbf{W}B\,\mathbf{e}}+\alpha\,\underbrace{\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{e}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}}_{=\mathbf{e}^{\top}S\mathbf{e}}=\langle B\mathbf{e},\ \mu^{\delta}_{W}-\mu_{W}\rangle_{Y}\leq\|B\mathbf{e}\|_{Y}\,\|\mu^{\delta}_{W}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}.

Hence
\|B\mathbf{e}\|_{Y}\leq\|\mu^{\delta}_{W}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}
, inserting this back gives

\alpha\,\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{e}\|_{s,R,h}^{2}\leq\|B\mathbf{e}\|_{Y}\,\|\mu^{\delta}_{W}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}\leq\|\mu^{\delta}_{W}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}^{2}.

Therefore,

\|u_{0,h}^{\alpha}-\widehat{u}_{0,h}^{\alpha}\|_{s,R,h}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\,\|\mu^{\delta}_{W}-\mu_{W}\|_{Y}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\big(\delta+\eta(h,R)\big).

(3.32)

For fixed
\alpha>0
, the previous part (ideal noiseless convergence) implies

\widehat{u}_{0,h}^{\alpha}\to u_{0}^{\alpha}
in
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
as
h\to 0
and
R\to\infty
.
Together with (
3.32
) and the parameter rule (
3.22
), this yields

u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\to u_{0}^{\alpha}\quad\text{in }\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)\qquad(h\to 0,\ R\to\infty,\ \delta\to 0),

and hence
u_{h}^{\alpha}=u_{h,f}+u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\to\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}^{\alpha}=:u^{\alpha}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

It remains to let
\alpha\to 0
. Since the preprocessed data are compatible in the exact model
(
\mathcal{L}u_{0}=\mu
with
u_{0}=u-\bar{u}_{f}
) and
\mathcal{L}
is injective, standard Tikhonov theory implies

u_{0}^{\alpha}\to u_{0}\quad\text{in }\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)\qquad(\alpha\to 0),

and therefore
u^{\alpha}=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}^{\alpha}\to\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}=u
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Combining the limits yields
u_{h}^{\alpha}\to u
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
after
0
-extension outside
\Omega_{R}
.
Since the forward problem (
1.1
) has a unique weak solution in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the limit is
exactly that weak solution.
∎

Theorem
3.8
gives the convergence of the reconstructed state, but it does not indicate how the error
depends on the noise level and the regularization parameter. For choosing
\alpha
in computations, it is useful
to keep an explicit error estimate. The result below is standard for linear Tikhonov regularization (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Ch. 4]
for more details).

By Lemma
2.3
and
[
19
, Lemma 2.2]
, the operator

\mathcal{L}:\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(W)
is bounded, injective, and compact.
Since
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and
L^{2}(W)
are Hilbert spaces,
\mathcal{L}
admits a unique Hilbert adjoint

\mathcal{L}^{*}:L^{2}(W)\to\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
satisfying

(\mathcal{L}v,y)_{L^{2}(W)}=(v,\mathcal{L}^{*}y)_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\qquad\forall v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega),\ \forall y\in L^{2}(W).

Define

A:=\mathcal{L}^{*}\mathcal{L}:\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)\to\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega).

Then
A
is bounded, self-adjoint, positive, and compact. Hence, the spectral theorem yields an orthonormal basis

\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
such that

Ae_{j}=\lambda_{j}e_{j},~j\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover
\ker(A)=\{0\}
as
\mathcal{L}
is injective, thus zero is not an eigenvalue of
A
, and therefore
\lambda_{j}>0
for all
j
. For
\nu>0
, we define

A^{\nu}v:=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{\nu}(v,e_{j})_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,e_{j},\qquad v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega),

so that
A^{\nu}
is again bounded, positive, and self-adjoint.

Assumption 3.9

(Source condition for the exact interior state)
.

Let
u_{0}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
. We assume that there exist

\nu\in(0,1]
,
w\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
, and

r_{u}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
such that

u_{0}=A^{\nu}w+r_{u},\qquad\|r_{u}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\epsilon_{u},\qquad\epsilon_{u}=\mathcal{O}((\delta+\eta(h,R))^{\frac{2\nu}{2\nu+1}}).

(3.33)

Since
\mathcal{L}
is injective,
\ker(A)=\{0\}
, hence

\ker(A^{\nu})=\ker(A)=\{0\}
for every
\nu>0
. As
A^{\nu}
is self-adjoint, we have
\overline{\operatorname{Range}(A^{\nu})}=(\ker(A^{\nu}))^{\perp}=\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
.
Thus
\operatorname{Range}(A^{\nu})
is dense in
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
, and for every
u_{0}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and every
\epsilon_{u}>0
one can choose
w\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and
r_{u}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
so that
(
3.33
) holds.
In particular, if
u_{0}
is in range of
A^{\nu}
,
then one may take
\epsilon_{u}=0
, and (
3.33
)
reduces to the exact source condition
u_{0}=A^{\nu}w
.

Also note that, if
u_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{0,j}e_{j}
with
u_{0,j}:=(u_{0},e_{j})_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, then

u_{0}\in\operatorname{Range}(A^{\nu})
if and only if

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{-2\nu}|u_{0,j}|^{2}<\infty
. In particular, every finite linear combination of eigenvectors of
A
belongs to
\operatorname{Range}(A^{\nu})
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the unique solution of (
1.1
) with
u=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}
where
u_{0}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{L}u_{0}=\mu
, and assume Assumption
3.9
holds. Let
u_{0}^{\alpha}
be the minimizer of the continuous functional

J_{\alpha}
in (
3.20

\|u_{0}^{\alpha}-u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\alpha^{\nu}\|w\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\epsilon_{u}.

(3.34)

In particular, if
u_{0}\in\operatorname{Range}(A^{\nu})
, then
r_{u}=0
and

\|u_{0}^{\alpha}-u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\alpha^{\nu}\|w\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

For every
\varphi\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
, the minimizer
u_{0}^{\alpha}
satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation

(\mathcal{L}u_{0}^{\alpha}-\mu,\mathcal{L}\varphi)_{L^{2}(W)}+\alpha(u_{0}^{\alpha},\varphi)_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

By the definition of the adjoint, this is equivalent to

(\mathcal{L}^{*}(\mathcal{L}u_{0}^{\alpha}-\mu)+\alpha u_{0}^{\alpha},\varphi)_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega).

Hence

(\mathcal{L}^{*}\mathcal{L}+\alpha I)u_{0}^{\alpha}=\mathcal{L}^{*}\mu.

(3.35)

Since
\mu=\mathcal{L}u_{0}
, we obtain

(A+\alpha I)u_{0}^{\alpha}=Au_{0}.

Moreover, for every
v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
,

((A+\alpha I)v,v)_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|\mathcal{L}v\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}+\alpha\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\geq\alpha\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

so
A+\alpha I
is invertible and bounded.
Using Assumption
3.9
,

u_{0}-u_{0}^{\alpha}=\alpha(A+\alpha I)^{-1}u_{0}=\alpha(A+\alpha I)^{-1}A^{\nu}w+\alpha(A+\alpha I)^{-1}r_{u}.

Writing
w=\sum_{j}w_{j}e_{j}
and
r_{u}=\sum_{j}r_{u,j}e_{j}
with
w_{j}:=(w,e_{j})_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and
r_{u,j}:=(r_{u},e_{j})_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, we have

\alpha(A+\alpha I)^{-1}A^{\nu}w=\sum_{j}\frac{\alpha\lambda_{j}^{\nu}}{\lambda_{j}+\alpha}\,w_{j}e_{j},\qquad\alpha(A+\alpha I)^{-1}r_{u}=\sum_{j}\frac{\alpha}{\lambda_{j}+\alpha}\,r_{u,j}e_{j}.

Therefore,

\|\alpha(A+\alpha I)^{-1}A^{\nu}w\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{j}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha\lambda_{j}^{\nu}}{\lambda_{j}+\alpha}\Bigr)^{2}|w_{j}|^{2}\leq\alpha^{2\nu}\|w\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

since
t^{\nu}\leq 1+t
for all
t\geq 0
when
0<\nu\leq 1
. Also,

\|\alpha(A+\alpha I)^{-1}r_{u}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{j}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda_{j}+\alpha}\Bigr)^{2}|r_{u,j}|^{2}\leq\|r_{u}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq\epsilon_{u}^{2}.

Combining the two bounds yields (
3.34
). The final statement is the special case

.
∎

3.5.
Reconstruction of
q

3.5.1.
Computing
(-\Delta)^{s}u_{h}^{\alpha}
in
\Omega

To recover
q
via (
1.1
), we need to first recover
(-\Delta)^{s}u^{\alpha}
inside
\Omega
. Discretely, we compute a consistent FE representation. Recall that the reconstructed state is decomposed as

u_{h}^{\alpha}=u_{0,h}^{\alpha}+u_{h,f},

where
u_{0,h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h,0}
is the unknown interior correction and
u_{h,f}
is the known exterior extension.
The auxiliary variable
w_{h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h,0}
is defined by

(w_{h}^{\alpha},\varphi_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=a_{R}(u_{h}^{\alpha},\varphi_{h})=a_{R}(u_{0,h}^{\alpha},\varphi_{h})+a_{R}(u_{h,f},\varphi_{h}),

(3.41)

for all
\varphi_{h}\in V_{h,0}
. Let
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{0}}
be a basis of
V_{h,0}
, and expand

w_{h}^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}}w_{j}^{\alpha}\,\phi_{j},\qquad u_{0,h}^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}}u_{j}^{\alpha}\,\phi_{j}.

Testing with
\varphi_{h}=\phi_{i}
yields the linear system

M_{0}\,\mathbf{w}^{\alpha}=A_{0}\,\mathbf{u}^{\alpha}+\mathbf{b}^{(\mathrm{ext})},

where the entries are given by

(M_{0})_{ij}:=(\phi_{j},\phi_{i})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\qquad(A_{0})_{ij}:=a_{R}(\phi_{j},\phi_{i}),\qquad b^{(\mathrm{ext})}_{i}:=a_{R}(u_{h,f},\phi_{i}).

Formally, from (
1.1
), in
\Omega
, the potential
q
satisfies

q=-\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}u}{u}.

(3.42)

However, direct pointwise division is numerically unstable if
u
becomes small. We therefore recover

q
through a regularized weighted least-squares problem that remains well-posed for all reconstructed
states. Since the nondegeneracy assumption is imposed only on an interior subdomain

\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
, we reconstruct the coefficient on
\Omega^{\prime}
and extend it by zero to

\Omega
only for visualization in Section
4
.

Let
Q_{h}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})\cap L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
be a finite element space for the coefficient
(e.g. elementwise polynomials, globally discontinuous), with basis
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{q}}
.
We seek
q_{h}^{\alpha}\in Q_{h}
in the form

q_{h}^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}q_{j}\psi_{j},\qquad\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{N_{q}})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{q}}.

Let
u_{h}^{\alpha}=u_{0,h}^{\alpha}+u_{h,f}\in V_{h}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega_{R})
be the reconstructed state,
and let
w_{h}^{\alpha}
denote the computable representation of
(-\Delta)^{s}u_{h}^{\alpha}
from
(
3.41
), restricted to
\Omega^{\prime}
. For fixed
\alpha_{q}>0
, we define
q_{h}^{\alpha}
by

q_{h}^{\alpha}:=\operatorname*{argmin}_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}}\Big(\|w_{h}^{\alpha}+q_{h}u_{h}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}+\alpha_{q}\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}\Big).

(3.43)

Theorem 3.12

(Existence and uniqueness of the discrete coefficient reconstruction)
.

Let
u_{h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h}
and
w_{h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h,0}
be given (recovered) quantities.
Then, for every fixed
\alpha_{q}>0
, the minimization problem (
3.43
) has a unique minimizer

q_{h}^{\alpha}\in Q_{h}
. Moreover,
q_{h}^{\alpha}
is characterized by the variational normal equations

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}+\alpha_{q}\big)\,q_{h}^{\alpha}\,\phi_{h}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}w_{h}^{\alpha}u_{h}^{\alpha}\,\phi_{h}\,dx\qquad\forall\phi_{h}\in Q_{h},

(3.44)

and the coefficient vector
\mathbf{q}
is the unique solution of the SPD linear system

G_{\alpha}\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{b},

(3.45a)

where

(G_{\alpha})_{ij}:=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}+\alpha_{q}\big)\psi_{i}\psi_{j}\,dx,\qquad b_{i}:=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}w_{h}^{\alpha}u_{h}^{\alpha}\psi_{i}\,dx.

(3.45b)

Proof.

Define

F(q_{h}):=\|w_{h}^{\alpha}+q_{h}\,u_{h}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}+\alpha_{q}\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2},\qquad q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

Since
Q_{h}
is finite-dimensional and
u_{h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h}
and
w_{h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h,0}
are fixed, the mapping
q_{h}\mapsto F(q_{h})

is a continuous quadratic functional on
Q_{h}
.
Coercivity follows from the regularization term

F(q_{h})\ \geq\ \alpha_{q}\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}\qquad\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

(3.46)

Hence
F(q_{h})\to\infty
as
\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\to\infty
in
Q_{h}
.
For any
q_{h},r_{h}\in Q_{h}
,

F(q_{h})-F(r_{h})-\langle F^{\prime}(r_{h}),q_{h}-r_{h}\rangle=\|(q_{h}-r_{h})u_{h}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}+\alpha_{q}\|q_{h}-r_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}\ \geq\ \alpha_{q}\|q_{h}-r_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2},

which is strictly positive whenever
q_{h}\neq r_{h}
since
\alpha_{q}>0
. Thus
F
is strictly convex.

Because
Q_{h}
is finite-dimensional, coercivity (
3.46
) implies that any minimizing sequence for
F

is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
, hence has a convergent subsequence in
Q_{h}
.
By continuity of
F
, the limit of this subsequence is a minimizer.
Uniqueness follows from strict convexity: if two minimizers existed, their midpoint would yield a strictly smaller value.

Let
q_{h}^{\alpha}
be the unique minimizer.
For any direction
\phi_{h}\in Q_{h}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
define
\Phi(t):=F(q_{h}^{\alpha}+t\phi_{h})
.
Since
F
is quadratic,
\Phi
is a differentiable convex function and
t=0
is its minimizer, so
\Phi^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Compute

\Phi^{\prime}(0)=2\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(w_{h}^{\alpha}+q_{h}^{\alpha}u_{h}^{\alpha})\,(u_{h}^{\alpha}\phi_{h})\,dx+2\alpha_{q}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}q_{h}^{\alpha}\phi_{h}\,dx.

Thus, this gives

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}\,q_{h}^{\alpha}\phi_{h}\,dx+\alpha_{q}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}q_{h}^{\alpha}\phi_{h}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}w_{h}^{\alpha}u_{h}^{\alpha}\phi_{h}\,dx,

which is exactly (
3.44
).

Write
q_{h}^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}q_{j}\psi_{j}
and choose
\phi_{h}=\psi_{i}
in (
3.44
).
This yields system (
3.45a
).
To prove
G_{\alpha}
is SPD, let
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{N_{q}})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{q}}\setminus\{0\}
and set
z_{h}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}\psi_{j}\in Q_{h}
.
Then

\mathbf{z}^{\top}G_{\alpha}\mathbf{z}=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}z_{h}^{2}\,dx+\alpha_{q}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}z_{h}^{2}\,dx\geq\alpha_{q}\|z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}>0,

since
\alpha_{q}>0
and
z_{h}\not\equiv 0
whenever
z\neq 0
. Hence
G_{\alpha}
is SPD.
∎

The matrix
G_{\alpha}
is sparse (the same stencil as the
Q_{h}
mass matrix) and SPD. Thus, it can be solved efficiently by the conjugate gradient method with standard preconditioning. The assembly of
G_{\alpha}
and
b
requires only local
element-wise quadrature of the integrals in (
3.45b
).

\|q-I_{h}^{Q}q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C\,h^{m}\,\|q\|_{H^{m}(\Omega^{\prime})},\qquad\|I_{h}^{Q}q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})},\qquad q\in H^{m}(\Omega^{\prime}).

(3.50)

Theorem 3.13

(Discretization error for the coefficient reconstruction on
\Omega^{\prime}
)
.

Fix
\alpha>0
and
\alpha_{q}>0
. Assume that
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
and
W\subset\Omega_{R}
such that
\operatorname{dist}(W,\Omega)=d_{0}>0
. In addition:

(1)

Let
u_{0}^{\alpha}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
be the minimizer of the state functional
(
3.47
), let
u^{\alpha}=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}^{\alpha}
, and let
w^{\alpha}\in L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
be defined by
(
3.48
). Let the coefficient reconstruction
q^{\alpha}\in L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
be the unique minimizer of (
3.49
).

(2)

Let
u_{h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h}
and
w_{h}^{\alpha}\in V_{h,0}
denote the corresponding discrete state and
interior fractional Laplacian reconstructions defined by (
3.18
) and (
3.41
)
respectively. Let
q_{h}^{\alpha}\in Q_{h}
be the discrete coefficient reconstruction, i.e. the unique
minimizer of (
3.43
). Equivalently,
q_{h}^{\alpha}
satisfies
(
3.44
).

(3)

Assume that there exists a constant
\kappa_{0}>0
such that

|u^{\alpha}(x)|\geq\kappa_{0}\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }x\in\Omega^{\prime}.

Define the discretization errors

E_{u}(h,\alpha):=\|u_{h}^{\alpha}-u^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})},\qquad E_{w}(h,\alpha):=\|w_{h}^{\alpha}-w^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}.

Assume
E_{u}(h,\alpha)\leq\kappa_{0}/2
. Then
|u_{h}^{\alpha}|\geq\kappa_{0}/2
a.e. on
\Omega^{\prime}
.

(4)

Assume additionally that
q^{\alpha}\in H^{m}(\Omega^{\prime})
for some
0<m\leq r+1
, where
r

is the polynomial degree of
Q_{h}
. Let
I_{h}^{Q}:H^{m}(\Omega^{\prime})\to Q_{h}
be an
L^{2}
-stable approximation operator satisfying (
3.50
).

Then there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
(but possibly depending on

\|u^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}
,
\|w^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}
,
\alpha_{q}
, and
\Omega^{\prime}
)
such that

\|q_{h}^{\alpha}-q^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\frac{C}{\kappa_{0}^{2}}\Big(E_{w}(h,\alpha)+E_{u}(h,\alpha)+h^{m}\|q^{\alpha}\|_{H^{m}(\Omega^{\prime})}\Big).

(3.51)

Proof.

By strict convexity of the functional in (
3.49
), the minimizer
q^{\alpha}

is characterized by

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u^{\alpha})^{2}+\alpha_{q}\big)\,q^{\alpha}\,\phi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}w^{\alpha}u^{\alpha}\,\phi\,dx\qquad\forall\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime}).

(3.52)

Let
q_{I}^{\alpha}:=I_{h}^{Q}q^{\alpha}\in Q_{h}
and set
e_{h}:=q_{h}^{\alpha}-q_{I}^{\alpha}\in Q_{h}
.
Then

\|q_{h}^{\alpha}-q^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\|q_{I}^{\alpha}-q^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}.

(3.53)

Testing (
3.44
) with
\phi_{h}=e_{h}
gives

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}+\alpha_{q}\big)\,q_{h}^{\alpha}\,e_{h}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}w_{h}^{\alpha}u_{h}^{\alpha}\,e_{h}\,dx,

and using
q_{h}^{\alpha}=e_{h}+q_{I}^{\alpha}
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}+\alpha_{q}\big)\,e_{h}^{2}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}w_{h}^{\alpha}u_{h}^{\alpha}\,e_{h}\,dx-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}+\alpha_{q}\big)\,q_{I}^{\alpha}\,e_{h}\,dx.

Add and subtract the continuous identity (
3.52
) (tested with
e_{h}
),
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}+\alpha_{q}\big)\,e_{h}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(w_{h}^{\alpha}u_{h}^{\alpha}-w^{\alpha}u^{\alpha})\,e_{h}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u^{\alpha})^{2}+\alpha_{q}\big)\,(q^{\alpha}-q_{I}^{\alpha})\,e_{h}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u^{\alpha})^{2}-(u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}\big)\,q_{I}^{\alpha}\,e_{h}\,dx.

By assumption
E_{u}(h,\alpha)\leq\kappa_{0}/2
and
|u_{h}^{\alpha}|\geq\kappa_{0}/2
a.e. on
\Omega^{\prime}
, we have

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big((u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}+\alpha_{q}\big)\,e_{h}^{2}\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}e_{h}^{2}\,dx\geq\frac{\kappa_{0}^{2}}{4}\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}.

Using Cauchy–Schwarz and the definitions of
E_{u},E_{w}
, we obtain

\|w_{h}^{\alpha}u_{h}^{\alpha}-w^{\alpha}u^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|u_{h}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}E_{w}(h,\alpha)+\|w^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}E_{u}(h,\alpha).

Moreover,

\|u_{h}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|u^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}+E_{u}(h,\alpha)\leq\|u^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\kappa_{0}/2,
so
\|u_{h}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}
is bounded independently of
h
. Also,

\|(u^{\alpha})^{2}-(u_{h}^{\alpha})^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\big(\|u^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\|u_{h}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\big)\,E_{u}(h,\alpha)\leq C\,E_{u}(h,\alpha).

Finally, (
3.50
) gives

\|q^{\alpha}-q_{I}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq Ch^{m}\|q^{\alpha}\|_{H^{m}(\Omega^{\prime})},

\|q_{I}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C\|q^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}.

Combining these estimates and dividing by the coercivity constant yields

\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\frac{C}{\kappa_{0}^{2}}\Big(E_{w}(h,\alpha)+E_{u}(h,\alpha)+h^{m}\|q^{\alpha}\|_{H^{m}(\Omega^{\prime})}\Big).

Insert this into (
3.53
) and use (
3.50
) once more to bound

\|q_{I}^{\alpha}-q^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}
, which proves (
3.51
).
∎

Remark 3.14

(Continuous noisy state error)
.

Let
u_{0}^{\alpha,\delta}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
be the minimizer of (
3.47
) with
u^{\alpha,\delta}:=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}^{\alpha,\delta}
and
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the exact solution with

. Then, from (

) and

, we have

(A+\alpha I)u_{0}^{\alpha,\delta}=Au_{0}+\mathcal{L}^{*}(\mu^{\delta}-\mu),

and therefore

The first term is estimated exactly as in Lemma

under Assumption

, while the second term is estimated by the standard resolvent bound

\|(A+\alpha I)^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{*}g\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(W)}\qquad\forall g\in L^{2}(W),

which follows by testing
(A+\alpha I)z=\mathcal{L}^{*}g
with

z=(A+\alpha I)^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{*}g\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
in the
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-inner product and using

, the adjoint identity, and Cauchy–Schwarz in

.
Hence

\|u^{\alpha,\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\Big(\alpha^{\nu}\|w\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\epsilon_{u}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\Big),

where

is the source element from Assumption
3.9
. In particular,
under the parameter choice from Theorem
3.11
and the assumptions of that
theorem, we have

\|u^{\alpha}-u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\mathcal{O}\!\Big((\delta+\eta(h,R))^{\frac{2\nu}{2\nu+1}}\Big)\qquad\text{as }\delta+\eta(h,R)\to 0.

Remark 3.15

(How
E_{u}
is related to the state reconstruction error)
.

If
s>d/2
, then the local Sobolev embedding yields

\|u_{h}^{\alpha,\delta}-u^{\alpha,\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C_{\Omega^{\prime}}\|u_{h}^{\alpha,\delta}-u^{\alpha,\delta}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By the triangle inequality,

\|u_{h}^{\alpha,\delta}-u^{\alpha,\delta}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|u_{h}^{\alpha,\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u^{\alpha,\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Therefore, under the hypothesis of Theorem
3.11
together with Remark
3.14
, we have

\|u_{h}^{\alpha,\delta}-u^{\alpha,\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}=\mathcal{O}\Big((\delta+\eta(h,R))^{\frac{2\nu}{2\nu+1}}\Big)\qquad\text{as }\delta+\eta(h,R)\to 0,

whenever
s>d/2
. Since
0<s<1
, the condition
s>d/2
can hold only in dimension
d=1
. For
d\geq 2
, or more generally without additional regularity
beyond the present
H^{s}
-framework, we keep
E_{u}(h,\alpha)
as a separate consistency
term. At present, no analogous estimate for
E_{w}(h,\alpha)
follows
from the state analysis alone, since
w^{\alpha}=((-\Delta)^{s}u^{\alpha})|_{\Omega^{\prime}}

involves one further application of the nonlocal operator. Hence,
E_{w}(h,\alpha)
is
kept as a separate consistency term in the coefficient reconstruction estimate.

3.7.
Logarithmic stability for the reconstructed potential (single measurement)

We now connect the reconstruction error (
3.51
) to the (single measurement) logarithmic stability estimate
for the fractional Calderón problem. Under the separation assumption (
2.7
), the exterior observation belongs to
L^{2}(W)
;
see
[
31
, Prop. 2.2]
. This justifies the
L^{2}(W)
-noise model used below.
To apply Rüland’s logarithmic stability theorem
[
41
, Thm. 1.1]
, we combine this with the continuous (indeed, compact) embedding
L^{2}(W)\hookrightarrow H^{-s}(W)
on bounded
W
. Once again, for the notational convenience, let us write
u^{\alpha}=u^{\alpha,\delta}
,
u_{0}^{\alpha}=u_{0}^{\alpha,\delta}
,
w^{\alpha}=w^{\alpha,\delta}
, and
q^{\alpha}=q^{\alpha,\delta}
in the next result.

Theorem 3.16

(Logarithmic stability for the reconstructed potential)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
W\subset\Omega_{e}
be bounded,
nonempty Lipschitz sets such that
\overline{\Omega}\cap\overline{W}=\emptyset
, and let
0<s<1
.
Assume that:

(1)

The exterior datum
f\in\widetilde{H}^{s+\epsilon}(W)\setminus\{0\}
satisfies

\frac{\|f\|_{H^{s}(W)}}{\|f\|_{L^{2}(W)}}\leq C_{f}

(3.54)

for some constant
C_{f}>0
.

(2)

The true potential
q\in C^{0,s}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies

\mathrm{supp}(q)\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega,\qquad\|q\|_{C^{0,s}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq M.

(3)

The true solution
u=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of (
1.1
) satisfies

\Lambda_{q}(f)=((-\Delta)^{s}u)\big|_{W},\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mu=((-\Delta)^{s}u_{0})|_{W}=\mathcal{L}u_{0}.

(4)

The noisy approximation
\mu^{\delta}\in L^{2}(W)
of
((-\Delta)^{s}u_{0})|_{W}
satisfies

\|\mu^{\delta}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq\delta.

(3.55)

(5)

For
\alpha>0
, let
u_{0}^{\alpha}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
be the unique minimizer of (
3.47
)
and

u^{\alpha}=\bar{u}_{f}+u_{0}^{\alpha}.

Assume that
\|w^{\alpha,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq M_{w},

and that there exists
\kappa_{0}>0
, independent of
\delta
, such that

|u^{\alpha}(x)|\geq\kappa_{0}\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }x\in\Omega^{\prime}

for all sufficiently small
\delta>0
.

(6)

The continuous coefficient reconstruction
q^{\alpha}\in L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
is the unique
minimizer of (
3.49
).
Assume moreover that

q^{\alpha}\in C^{0,s}(\overline{\Omega}),\qquad\mathrm{supp}(q^{\alpha})\subset\Omega^{\prime},\qquad\|q^{\alpha}\|_{C^{0,s}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq M,

and that the forward problem (
1.1
) with potential
q^{\alpha}
and exterior datum
f

is uniquely solvable.

Then there exist constants
\gamma>0
and
C,C_{1}>0
, depending only on

\Omega,W,s,C_{f},\epsilon,M,M_{w},\kappa_{0},d
, such that

\|q^{\alpha}-q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\,|\log(C(\delta+\sqrt{\alpha}+\alpha_{q}))|^{-\gamma},

(3.56)

for all sufficiently small
\delta>0
and
\alpha>0
. In particular, if
\alpha_{q}\sim\delta
and
\alpha=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{p})
for some
0<p<2
, then

\|q^{\alpha}-q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\,|\log(C\delta)|^{-\gamma},\qquad\text{for all sufficiently small }\delta>0.

(3.57)

Proof.

By the minimality of
u_{0}^{\alpha}
in (
3.47
),

J_{\alpha}^{\delta}(u_{0}^{\alpha})\leq J_{\alpha}^{\delta}(u_{0})=\|\mu^{\delta}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}+\alpha\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq\delta^{2}+\alpha\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Hence,

\|\mathcal{L}u_{0}^{\alpha}-\mu^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq\delta+\sqrt{\alpha}\,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Since
\Lambda_{q}(f)=((-\Delta)^{s}\bar{u}_{f})|_{W}+\mu
, we obtain

\|((-\Delta)^{s}u^{\alpha})|_{W}-\Lambda_{q}(f)\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq 2\delta+\sqrt{\alpha}\,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(3.58)

By the strict convexity of (
3.49
), we have (
3.52
), and the minimizer
q^{\alpha}
is characterized
pointwise a.e. on
\Omega^{\prime}
by

q^{\alpha}(x)=-\frac{u^{\alpha}(x)\,w^{\alpha}(x)}{|u^{\alpha}(x)|^{2}+\alpha_{q}}.

Therefore

r^{\alpha}:=w^{\alpha}+q^{\alpha}u^{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha_{q}}{|u^{\alpha}|^{2}+\alpha_{q}}\,w^{\alpha},

and using the lower bound on
u^{\alpha}
we get

\|r^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\frac{\alpha_{q}}{\kappa_{0}^{2}}\,\|w^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\frac{M_{w}}{\kappa_{0}^{2}}\,\alpha_{q}.

(3.59)

Let
\widetilde{u}^{\alpha}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the unique solution of the forward problem
(
1.1
) with potential
q^{\alpha}
and exterior datum
f
, and define

e^{\alpha}:=u^{\alpha}-\widetilde{u}^{\alpha}.

Then

((-\Delta)^{s}+q^{\alpha})e^{\alpha}=r^{\alpha}\quad\text{in }\Omega^{\prime},\qquad e^{\alpha}=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{e}.

The standard energy estimate for the fractional Schrödinger equation in
\Omega^{\prime}
and the assumption
\|q^{\alpha}\|_{C^{0,s}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})}
\leq M
implies

\|e^{\alpha}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|r^{\alpha}\|_{H^{-s}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C\|r^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}.

Since
e^{\alpha}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
, and
W
is separated from
\Omega
, Lemma
2.3

implies

\|((-\Delta)^{s}e^{\alpha})|_{W}\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq C\|e^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|e^{\alpha}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|r^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}.

Hence

\|\Lambda_{q^{\alpha}}(f)-((-\Delta)^{s}u^{\alpha})|_{W}\|_{L^{2}(W)}=\|((-\Delta)^{s}\widetilde{u}^{\alpha})|_{W}-((-\Delta)^{s}u^{\alpha})|_{W}\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq C\|r^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}.

(3.60)

Combining (
3.58
), (
3.59
), and
(
3.60
), we obtain

\|\Lambda_{q^{\alpha}}(f)-\Lambda_{q}(f)\|_{L^{2}(W)}\leq C(\delta+\sqrt{\alpha}+\alpha_{q}).

Since
W
is bounded, the embedding
L^{2}(W)\hookrightarrow H^{-s}(W)
is continuous, so

\|\Lambda_{q^{\alpha}}(f)-\Lambda_{q}(f)\|_{H^{-s}(W)}\leq C(\delta+\sqrt{\alpha}+\alpha_{q}).

Now all hypotheses of Rüland’s single-measurement logarithmic stability theorem

[
41
, Thm. 1]
are satisfied: the sets
\Omega
and
W
are bounded Lipschitz and disjoint,
the datum
f
satisfies (
3.54
), and both
q
and
q^{\alpha}
belong to

C^{0,s}(\overline{\Omega})
, are supported in
\Omega^{\prime}
, and obey the same
a priori
bound.
Therefore there exist
\gamma>0
and
C_{1}>0
such that

\|q^{\alpha}-q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\,|\log(C(\delta+\sqrt{\alpha}+\alpha_{q}))|^{-\gamma},

which is (
3.56
).

If
\alpha_{q}\sim\delta
and
\alpha=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{p})
with
0<p<2
, then

\delta+\sqrt{\alpha}+\alpha_{q}\leq C\delta^{\theta}
for some
\theta\in(0,1]
, and hence

|\log(C\delta^{\theta})|\simeq|\log(C\delta)|
as
\delta\downarrow 0
. This yields the bound (
3.57
).
∎

Note that from the triangle inequality, we have

\|q_{h}^{\alpha}-q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|q_{h}^{\alpha}-q^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}+|\Omega^{\prime}|^{1/2}\|q^{\alpha}-q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, combining Theorem
3.13
and Theorem
3.16
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.17

(Total coefficient error)
.

Assuming the hypotheses of Theorems
3.13
and Theorem
3.16

\|q_{h}^{\alpha}-q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\frac{C}{\kappa_{0}^{2}}\Big(E_{w}(h,\alpha)+E_{u}(h,\alpha)+h^{m}\|q^{\alpha}\|_{H^{m}(\Omega^{\prime})}\Big)+C_{1}|\Omega^{\prime}|^{1/2}|\log(C(\delta+\sqrt{\alpha}+\alpha_{q}))|^{-\gamma}.

We compare two coefficient reconstructions in the discontinuous space
Q_{h}=P_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h}|_{\Omega^{\prime}})

where

denotes the jump of the elementwise constant coefficient across
neighboring cells. The term

is the discrete TV seminorm on

Unlike the quadratic penalty in (

therefore promotes a sparse jump set, i.e. a reconstruction with few sharp interfaces rather than
many small oscillations; this is the basic edge-preserving mechanism of TV regularization

[
37
,
43
]
, and its use for discontinuous inverse problems is classical

[
1
]
. In the implementation, we use
\alpha=\delta^{3/2}

\alpha_{q}=\max\!\bigl(10^{6}\delta^{3/2},\,10^{-14}\bigr).

The TV parameter is chosen adaptively for each noise level.
More precisely, we first compute the baseline

5.
Conclusion

We presented a Galerkin finite element framework for single-measurement reconstruction in the fractional Calderón problem.
The method follows the unique continuation based single-measurement reconstruction principle: we first recover the interior correction
u_{0}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
from a partial exterior observation
\mathcal{L}u_{0}|_{W}
by Tikhonov regularization, and then reconstruct the potential
q
in a dedicated coefficient space through a stabilized least-squares quotient.
At the discrete level, we formulated the observation operator via nonsingular quadrature on the separated measurement set,
proved existence and uniqueness of the discrete state reconstructor and coefficient reconstructor, and established conditional convergence under natural consistency and parameter coupling assumptions.
We also connected the reconstruction error to known logarithmic-type stability bounds for the underlying single-measurement inverse problem.

The present work should be viewed as a first finite element realization and proof-of-concept
numerical study of the single-measurement reconstruction principle in the fractional Calderón problem. The numerical experiments in one and two space dimensions demonstrate the stable recovery of both smooth and discontinuous potentials. In the discontinuous case, a TV-type jump penalty in the coefficient step yields sharper interface recovery. The validation, automated parameter-choice rules for the TV-based
discontinuous reconstruction, together with sharper total-error estimates for the fully discrete coefficient reconstruction, remain important directions for future work.
