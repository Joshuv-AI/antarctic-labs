Enzyme-aware soil fertility prediction using dual optimization with improved SCSO
Authors: Pavithra Mahesh, Rajkumar Soundrapandiyan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Soil enzyme activity is a key determinant of crop productivity, as it regulates nutrient cycling, organic matter decomposition, and nitrogen transformation. The existing Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) approaches for soil fertility assessment often underutilize biochemical indicators owing to noise, missing data, and complex feature interactions. These models treat Feature Selection (FS) and Hyperparameter Tuning (HPT) as separate process. This limits the overall model performance. Conventional Sand Cat Swarm Optimization (SCO) method suffers from rigid exploration–exploitation transitions and premature convergence. To overcome these limitations, an Improved Sand Cat Swarm Optimization (Improved SCSO) based framework is implemented in this work. The proposed work sequentially performs FS and HPT within a unified optimization process and includes a stochastic escape-from-worst update mechanism. Cosine-modulated search behavior is incorporated in the model to enhance exploration. Exploitation and convergence stability are improved by Time-adaptive best-solution inheritance strategy. Enzyme-related soil attributes were explicitly incorporated into the optimization process, enabling the selection of biologically meaningful features. A correlation-based filtering step was applied to remove redundant features and improve the prediction consistency. The optimized feature subset was evaluated using multiple ML, DL and Hybrid models have also been assessed to understand the predictive performance of proposed framework. The model was evaluated using stratified k-fold cross-validation with Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-score. The experimental results show that the proposed framework consistently outperforms traditional SCO-based methods. Gradient Boost (GB) achieved the highest accuracy of 98.48%, followed by hybrid models such as Decision Tree (DT) + Random Forest (RF) (98.38%) and GB + RF (98.28%) respectively. In addition, a dynamic crop mapping strategy was developed to estimate crop suitability based on predicted fertility levels and enzyme activity, thereby improving its practical application. Overall, the proposed framework improves prediction accuracy and interpretability, providing an effective solution for soil fertility assessment and data-driven crop recommendation.

Introduction

Soil productivity is essential for sustainable agriculture and environmental conservation, as it directly influences crop yield, nutrient availability, and overall soil quality. In recent years, Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) techniques have been widely applied for soil fertility assessment and prediction.

They can also model complex interactions in the soil1–3. Most studies have focused on biological and chemical parameters. However, biochemical factors, such as soil enzyme activity, have not been studied sufficiently. These factors are important for nutrient cycling, organic matter breakdown, and microbial activity4,5. Soil enzyme activity provides important information about biological soil mechanisms and overall ecosystem performance. Enzymes, including urease, dehydrogenase, and phosphatase, are closely linked to nitrogen cycling, carbon transformation, and phosphorus accessibility4. However, integrating enzyme-based attributes into forecasting models is still complex because of high-dimensional data, redundancy, noise, and intricate interrelationships present in soil datasets1,6.

Traditional ML and DL models often do not perform well and it’s difficult to understand in biological terms. FS and HPT are important for improving these models. However, many studies handle FS and HPT separately, which can lead to poor results because features and model settings affect each other7–9. Most widely used techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and clustering often reduce the interpretability of biological datasets10,11. Metaheuristic optimization techniques are extensively used to handle complex problems by providing flexible and efficient search strategies. Techniques such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA), Genetic Algorithms (GA), and Sand Cat Swarm Optimization (SCO) have shown good performance in FS and hyperparameter optimization7–9. Enhanced SCO variants exhibit enhanced convergence and solution quality12–15. However, existing SCO methods still face issues such as poor exploration–exploitation balance and premature convergence in high-dimensional spaces12,14. In addition, most SCO-based studies handle FS and parameter optimization separately, whereas enzyme-based soil features are rarely considered in agricultural prediction models16–19.

In this study, an advanced framework based on the Improved SCSO was developed to provide a better evaluation of soil fertility. This framework integrates FS and HPT tuning processes through a unified adaptive optimization approach to ensure that both FS and model parameters cooperate optimally. Most existing studies perform FS and HPT independently, which may lead to suboptimal model configurations and increased complexities in computation. In contrast, the proposed framework integrates both processes within a unified Improved SCSO optimization strategy, enabling coordinated adaptation between feature subsets and classifier configurations. This approach differs from traditional SCO-based frameworks by incorporating a cosine-driven method that enhances global search capabilities and helps avoid poor solutions. This study makes another important contribution by explicitly incorporating enzyme-based soil characteristics into the optimization process, which produces biologically relevant patterns that are generally overlooked by traditional approaches. To eliminate redundant variables, a correlation-based redundancy filtering process was incorporated in the proposed study.

To evaluate the efficacy of the proposed method, various ML and DL algorithms were considered for experimentation. These include RF, DT, K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN), SVC (Support Vector Classifier), GB, CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost, Artificial Neural Network, Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. Furthermore, hybrid models have been considered to exploit the complementary advantages offered by various classifiers. In addition, DL architecture were included to provide a broader comparative evaluation of structured soil–enzyme datasets. A comprehensive series of experiments conducted using stratified k-fold cross-validation proved the superiority of the proposed method over existing SCO and baseline methods.

To enhance practical applicability, the research introduces a novel approach for dynamically mapping crops. This method integrates predicted soil fertility levels and enzymatic activity to assess crop suitability, effectively bridging the gap between predictive modelling outcomes and real-world agricultural practices. The structure of the paper is divided into four main sections. The first section provides a concise overview of related studies and key findings that have influenced the development of the proposed work, while also outlining its contributions. The second section details the proposed algorithm for predicting crop fertility. The third section focuses on presenting and analysing experimental results. Finally, the discussion and conclusion summarizing the findings and implications of the proposed work are provided in the concluding section.

Related work

In order to maximise yield, profitability, and sustainability, an efficient crop selection framework seeks to match the right crop to the correct enzyme with fertility levels in the ideal conditions. The framework must incorporate transdisciplinary data, intelligent decision-making models, and stakeholder needs in order to create a reliable and flexible system. The use of enzyme profiles to directly identify crop suitability has not yet been fully established in mainstream agricultural practices, despite the growing recognition of soil enzyme activity as a critical indication of soil health and fertility. Similar to5 and18, direct predictions of crop suitability are rarely given, even when ML is employed to analyze enzyme activities.20 noted that soil characteristics, flora, and terrain all have an impact on microbial communities, which in turn have an impact on enzyme activity. Enzyme profiles are not yet commonly used to assess crop compatibility, though other attempts have been made to use microbial markers to make useful agricultural decisions, such mapping soil fertility or identifying zones using dynamic zone management19. The complete potential of soil enzyme-based decision-support systems is still unknown, despite this being a promising research avenue.

Through predictive modeling, classification, and optimisation techniques, ML has become a game-changing tool in modern agriculture, enabling data-driven decision-making. ML is very useful for analysing complex agri-environmental data because of its capacity to represent intricate nonlinear interactions. Applications including agricultural yield prediction, soil fertility classification, nutrient assessment, and microbial dynamics modeling have found it very useful6,21–23.The application of analytical techniques in the agricultural sciences has been enhanced by the development of machine learning. For comprehensive yield prediction, soil categorisation, and nutrient mapping, a number of models are useful, such as RF, SVM, and Neural Networks24–27. For predicting soil organic carbon,24 examined the Support Vector Regressor (SVR), ANN, and RF and discovered that both RF and ensemble models performed better overall. Pejak et al.26 used Sentinel-2 data and ML models to forecast field-scale soybean yields, while12 showed how ML might be used to estimate nutrient status and manage crops on a site-specific basis. The scalability and reproducibility of ML pipelines in agriculture are supported by tools like Scikit-learn28. These evaluations did point out that very few studies have combined fertility indices with soil enzyme dynamics in crop advising systems.

The studies presented in16–19 detailed their earlier comparison work on ML performance in fertility classification; however, their interpretability and feature optimisation techniques were simplified. Additionally, it is clear that ML-based advisory systems rarely use dynamic modelling of soil health measures, such as enzyme activity indicators, indicating the need for integrated modeling techniques. In agriculture, at occasions high dimensionality and nonlinear interactions are beyond the capabilities of traditional statistical methods. Metaheuristic algorithms have proven invaluable in solving complicated optimisation problems29. There are many opportunities to investigate the application of metaheuristics30 in agriculture. While31 recognized the efficacy of a hybrid metaheuristic algorithm that combined Atom Search Optimisation and Simulated Annealing to improve FS effectiveness19, looked at how metaheuristic algorithms have been employed to help with land use optimisation. Frameworks for metaheuristic-based multiclass FS were provided by19, which showed that different FS techniques have comparable effects on classification performance.

The use of agricultural data in hybrid and adaptable techniques has received more attention in recent research. To assist FS,7 offered an adaptive PSO method with leadership learning. Significant global exploration–exploitation balances were also present in the Sand Cat Swarm Optimisation (SCO) method, as shown by13, which further produced more ideal feature subsets. The convergence time and problem quality were enhanced by the suggested multi-strategy SCO and elite decentralisation SCO15,23. The applicability of metaheuristic algorithms in various sectors has been investigated in a number of studies. The work12 highlighted the Social Cognitive Optimisation (SCO) algorithm’s transferability outside of its initial setting by demonstrating its efficacy in DL-based cybersecurity applications. In agricultural domain14, introduced an inertia-based Cat Swarm Optimization (CSO) method for crop yield prediction. But has not incorporated integration with enzyme-based modelling or a unified soil–crop pipeline. An adaptive PSO approach incorporating leadership learning was proposed by23, significantly improving both the FS and HPT processes. Similarly18, presented a hybrid model combining Atom Search Optimization and Simulated Annealing to enhance the efficiency of FS and hyperparameter configuration. In addition18, provides a comprehensive review of the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) and its various extensions. Notably,9 emphasized that hybrid models integrating FS and HPT tend to outperform traditional models in classification tasks.

Although agricultural modelling has been much enhanced by ML and metaheuristic algorithms, there is still little integration of biochemical soil indicators, especially enzyme activity, in optimization-driven crop recommendation systems. Furthermore, the majority of current SCO-based research places a little attention on unified optimisation techniques for soil-crop prediction tasks instead it concentrates on either FS or HPT separately. These drawbacks emphasise the necessity of biologically informed and integrated optimisation frameworks for applications in precision agriculture.

Methodology

Dataset description

The dataset used in this study was obtained from32 and comprises 969 observations aggregated from approximately 100 published studies until January 2021, covering global intercropping systems. The dataset contains soil-related attributes grouped into three domains: physical, chemical, and organic/biological domains. Physical attributes include clay, silt, sand, bulk density, texture descriptors, soil depth, and climatic variables such as Mean Annual Temperature (MAT), Mean Annual Precipitation (MAP), and agro-climatic zone, together with experimental metadata (e.g., location, author, land-use type, year, journal, and DOI). The chemical attributes included pH, Dissolved Organic Carbon (DOC), Soil Organic Carbon (SOC), Total Nitrogen (TN), and carbon-to-nitrogen ratios. Organic or biological attributes include enzyme type, microbial biomass carbon, extracellular enzyme activity, effect size indicators, and assay-related descriptors. This comprehensive feature set offers a suitable basis for optimization-based FS and predictive modeling.

After removing 387 observations with missing target labels, 582 labeled samples were retained for soil fertility classification. Missing values in the predictor variables were subsequently imputed using a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to preserve the statistical distribution of soil physicochemical and enzyme-related features. The target variable comprised three fertility classes: medium (253 samples, 43.47%), low (219 samples, 37.63%), and high (110 samples, 18.90%).

The pre-processed data were subjected to a two-stage optimization phase using the Improved SCSO, where Stage 1 performed binary FS to obtain an optimal subset of relevant soil and enzyme attributes. Next, a correlation-based redundancy reduction step is applied to the selected features to eliminate highly correlated variables and produce a compact non-redundant feature subset. Finally, Stage 2 performs continuous HPT using the reduced feature subset obtained after correlation filtering to maximize the classification performance. The resulting feature set is compact and ready for training using ML, DL and Hybrid models. During training, a k-fold stratified cross-validation mechanism was employed. Within each fold, FS and HPT using the Improved SCSO were performed exclusively on the training subset, whereas the test subset remained completely unseen, ensuring unbiased performance estimation and preventing data leakage. Then, the associated model performance such as accuracy, precision, recall, and f1 score, was computed. Finally, a dynamic crop mapping module was deployed to estimate the best-suited crops based on the estimated enzyme activity and fertility level.

Data preprocessing

To guarantee reliable performance in ML models, appropriate data preprocessing is crucial. First, observations with missing target labels that is 387 samples were removed because they could not be used for supervised classification and 582 labeled samples were retained for soil fertility classification. To handle missing values and standardize the data, a preprocessing step was applied to the soil dataset in this investigation, which included both numerical and categorical features30. Missing values in the predictor variables were then handled using Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)-based imputation for numerical soil physicochemical and enzyme-related attributes. This approach was adopted to preserve the underlying statistical distribution of heterogeneous soil features more effectively than simple mean imputation. The mean of the available observations was used to impute missing numerical values, and the mode of the relevant feature was used to replace missing categorical values. Following imputation, label encoding was used to transform categorical variables into numerical indices. The numerical features were then standardized to guarantee a consistent contribution across classifiers. After that, the pre-processed dataset was separated into the target variable for analysis and input features. Particularly, the target variable comprised three fertility classes: medium (253 samples, 43.47%), low (219 samples, 37.63%), and high (110 samples, 18.90%).

Experimental protocol and cross-validation strategy

All experiments were carried out utilizing stratified k-fold cross-validation with k = 5 in order to guarantee a trustworthy performance evaluation and prevent data leaking. The class distribution was maintained throughout the division of the dataset into five mutually exclusive folds. Four folds were used for training in each iteration, with one-fold set aside for testing and validation. To avoid information leaking from the validation samples into the optimization process, FS and HPT were only carried out during the training phase of each fold. To find the ideal feature subset, the suggested binary Improved SCSO-based FS was initially applied to the training subset. Only the chosen training features were then used for continual Improved SCSO-based HPT. The unseen validation fold was then used to assess the optimized model. During model creation, this hierarchical optimization approach made sure that FS, redundancy reduction, and HPT were completely segregated from the validation data. As a result, an objective prediction performance under practical evaluation conditions is reflected in the reported performance indicators. The suggested fold-wise optimization strategy made sure that FS and HPT remained completely isolated within each training fold prior to validation, significantly reducing data leakage and optimistic bias even though a strict nested cross-validation framework was not used.

Traditional SCO

The Traditional SCO algorithm is a recent population-based metaheuristic inspired by the predatory behavior and escape mechanisms of sand cats in arid regions. The algorithm proposed in33 adopts the behavior of sand cats to update individual solutions in a population to manage the trade-off between exploration and exploitation by simulating how sand cats move their position based on the best and worst individuals of the population.

Limitations of traditional SCO and motivation for improvement

Although Sand Cat Swarm Optimization (SCO) has exhibited efficiency in handling optimization problems, its straightforward application to complicated, multidimensional datasets, such as soil fertility prediction with enzyme-related features, reveals intrinsic constraints.

First, conventional SCO deal with a gradually decreasing control parameter to balance exploration and exploitation throughout the optimization process. However, this static strategy often results in an imbalanced search behaviour, causing more exploration during the early iterations and premature convergence in the last stages. This restriction becomes more significant when handling soil and enzyme datasets, as the complex nonlinear relationships and feature interactions require balanced and continuous exploration to improve the discovery of meaningful structures in data. Second, traditional SCO does not have a framework to manage weak solutions efficiently. The lack of method to avoid the least optimal solution reduces solution diversity and restricts the optimization capability to search unvisited areas of the solution space.

Furthermore, existing SCO implementations conventionally execute FS and HPT as separate problems, which overlooks the interplay between selected features and model parameters, often leading to poorly tuned settings in ML models, as selected features and parameter settings directly impact each other. These relationships and interaction effects must be explored and balanced continuously to enhance the finding of a meaningful structure.

Another aspect is the non-integration of domain-relevant information into the features. Traditional SCO-based FS does not consider domain knowledge-based indicators, such as the levels of soil enzyme activities, as optimization parameters. This implies that domain-specific biochemical features may not be appropriately weighted.

Improved SCSO framework consists of three major enhancements: the escape-from-worst strategy, cosine-modulated adaptive position update, and probabilistic best-solution inheritance. These mechanisms collectively improve exploration capability, reduce premature convergence, and strengthen the optimizer’s ability to select relevant and non-redundant features.

The convergence stability of the proposed Improved SCSO has been ensured through the combination of adaptive cosine modulation and probabilistic best-solution inheritance. As the iterations progress, the cosine modulation term gradually reduces the oscillatory exploration behavior, enabling a smoother transition toward exploitation. Simultaneously, probabilistic inheritance from the global best solution reinforces convergence around high-quality candidate regions while maintaining the stochastic diversity within the population. Compared with Traditional SCO, which relies on abrupt phase switching, the proposed method provides a smoother search adaptation and improved convergence stability for high-dimensional soil–enzyme optimization problems.

Although a strict mathematical convergence guarantee is difficult to establish for stochastic population-based metaheuristic algorithms, the proposed Improved SCSO is conceptually similar to Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) in that candidate solutions are guided toward the best solution found so far. However, unlike PSO, the proposed method incorporates an escape-from-worst mechanism that directs solutions away from poor-performing regions to preserve diversity and reduce premature convergence. Therefore, the proposed Improved SCSO was designed to improve practical convergence stability and search efficiency rather than provide a formal deterministic convergence guarantee.

Experimental analysis

This section deals with the systematic evaluation of the proposed framework, which includes a correlation analysis for FS, redundancy reduction, and HPT of various classifiers. The objective is to assess how the Improved SCSO selects the best features along with correlation for redundant feature reduction, which enhances classification accuracy, particularly in comparison to Traditional SCO and the Full Feature Set.

In addition to conventional ML models, advanced gradient boosting algorithms, such as XGBoost and LightGBM, were included as strong baselines owing to their proven effectiveness in tabular data classification tasks. All models were evaluated under the same experimental conditions, including an identical cross-validation strategy, preprocessing steps, and optimization framework, ensuring a fair comparison. FS and HPT were performed within each training fold to avoid data leakage, whereas the test fold remained unseen during optimization. Performance was evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, and f1-score.

Correlation matrix analysis

Correlation analysis and feature selection

To analyze the relationships among the input variables, a correlation matrix of the numerical features was generated (Fig. 3). The matrix revealed several strong positive and negative correlations among soil physiochemical properties, enzyme activity indicators, microbial biomass attributes and yield-related parameters. The presence of highly correlated variables indicates potential redundancy within the dataset, which may negatively affect model generalization and increase computational complexity.Fig. 3Correlation matrix of numerical features.

Correlation matrix of numerical features.

Several feature pairs exhibit strong positive correlations. For instance yield_lnrr and yield_ler show a high positive correlation of approximately 0.85, avg_mbc_trt and stddev_mbc_ctrl exhibit correlation values greater than 0.75, lnrr_eea and var_lnrr_eea demonstrate a correlation close to 0.80, avg_eea_ctrl and stddev_eea_ctrl also shows significant positive dependency.

Similarly, nutrient-related attributes such as av_p, som, and sand exhibited moderate-to-high correlations with other soil variables, indicating overlapping information content. These observations confirm that the direct utilization of all features may introduce multicollinearity and redundant representations, thereby motivating the need for an effective FS mechanism.

A correlation threshold of δ =0.95 was used to identify highly redundant features, as values above this level indicated strong multicollinearity. A binary threshold of τ =0.5 was used to convert continuous optimizer positions into feature-selection decisions, such that features with sigmoid activation values greater than or equal to 0.5 were selected.

Feature selection analysis

The feature subsets chosen by Traditional SCO and the proposed Improved SCSO show distinct differences in redundancy reduction and predictive relevance. As illustrated in Fig. 3, Traditional SCO retained several highly correlated variables including yield_lnrr and yield_ler, avg_mbc_trt and stddev_mbc_ctrl, and lnrr_eea and var_lnrr_eea, indicating limited ability to eliminate overlapping information. These observations suggest that Traditional SCO is more susceptible to premature convergence and may preserve redundant and weakly informative features.

In contrast, the proposed Improved SCSO removed highly correlated and low-contributing variables while retaining biologically and agronomically meaningful features such as enz_ec, avg_eea_trt, lnrr_mbc, tot_p, soc, duration, and inter_cat. These variables provide complementary information related to enzyme activity, nutrient availability, soil organic carbon, and temporal interactions. The improved FS performance is primarily attributed to the cosine-modulated exploration strategy, stochastic escape-from-worst updating, improved convergence stability, and correlation-based deduplication.

Overall, Improved SCSO produced a more compact and discriminative feature subset that reduced multicollinearity and dimensionality while enhancing interpretability and model generalization. The retention of enzyme-related attributes further confirms the critical role of biochemical indicators in intelligent soil fertility prediction.

Hyperparameter configuration

In this study, hyperparameter optimization was performed using the proposed Improved SCSO framework to enhance model performance and generalization capability. The optimization process jointly tunes the model-specific hyperparameters while simultaneously performing FS. For traditional ML models, important hyperparameters such as the number of estimators, maximum tree depth, learning rate, regularization parameters, and neighborhood size are optimized within predefined search ranges. For example, RF and GB models optimize the number of trees and maximum depth, whereas the SVC optimizes the regularization parameter C and kernel coefficient ϒ. Similarly, KNN optimizes the number of neighbors k.

For DL models, hyperparameters including learning rate, hidden units, number of filters, kernel size, dropout rate, and batch size, were optimized. The search ranges are selected based on literature recommendations and empirical analysis to balance convergence stability and computational complexity.

Discussion

The proposed hybrid ML scheme, which uses cosine-based Improved SCSO for both FS and HPT with ensemble classifiers, outperformed traditional and existing methods in soil classification and enzyme activity prediction. Unlike conventional dimensionality reduction techniques, such as PCA and fuzzy clustering10,11 or the early wrapper-based approaches, such as Extreme Learning Machines44, the proposed algorithm identifies a compact and highly informative feature set and significantly decreases the computational cost, improving the accuracy and interpretability. Moreover, by leveraging unbiased boosting with categorical features using CatBoost45, the framework enhances the classification performance in complex soil datasets. Previous studies on Ant Lion Optimization (ALO) and Atom Search Optimization (ASO) for FS have reported the usefulness of metaheuristics for model refinement; however, they have not been integrated with adaptive classifier tuning46,47. The algorithm proposed in this study unified the SCSO approach and optimized both the feature subset and model parameters, yielding superior performance, especially for hybrid models such as KNN + RF, and achieved an accuracy of 97.3%. This standardization is likely comparable to modern methods using metaheuristics, such as the CatBoost-Bayesian hybrid model for soil strength prediction48.

Compare with, PSO which uses velocity-based updates49; GA which applies crossover and mutation operators50; WOA which models spiral encircling behavior to the best solution51; and BO which develops surrogate probabilistic models for hyperparameter estimation52, are all well-known optimization techniques. In comparison, the introduced Improved SCSO uses stochastic escape-from-worst learning, cosine-modulated exploration, and probabilistic best-solution inheritance. Most importantly, these strategies not only enhance the diversity of the population but also minimize the risk of early convergence and produce optimization trajectories that are smoother and more stable than those of Traditional SCO. The proposed Improved SCSO method leads to higher predictive accuracy through combined FS and HPT. In addition, it reduces the complexity of the model by eliminating unnecessary soil and enzyme attributes. Therefore, the models that are developed as a result are computationally efficient and can be readily used in agricultural settings that are resource-limited, where the importance of both prediction accuracy and operational efficiency is recognized.

The experimental results further revealed that tree-based ML and hybrid ensemble models consistently outperformed DL models for structured soil–enzyme datasets. This behavior can be attributed to the relatively limited dataset size and tabular nature of soil physiochemical and enzyme-related features, which do not possess the strong spatial or sequential dependencies typically required for CNN and LSTM architectures. In contrast, ensemble-based ML models, such as GB, RF, and CatBoost, are more effective for modeling heterogeneous tabular data and nonlinear feature interactions with limited training samples. In addition, the proposed framework extends the analysis by evaluating the model performance at the enzyme level, thereby improving the biological interpretability of soil fertility prediction. For instance5, with the aid of MLR, RF, and ANN models per enzyme prediction for soil enzymes are analyzed comprising cellulase and N-acetyl-glucosaminidase. The proposed approach explicitly addresses predictions at the per-enzyme level, revealing consistent precision for key enzymes, such as β-glucosidase and dehydrogenase.

Finally, dynamic crop mapping based on predicted enzyme activity and fertility levels presents a novel paradigm in precision agriculture. Such efforts have been made by53, who used GBRT-DNN for crop suitability but did not incorporate biochemical indicators. By combining biological relevance, model reliability, and actionable crop recommendations, the proposed Improved SCSO-powered hybrid pipeline demonstrates competitive performance in soil and enzyme modeling, with practical applications in sustainable farm management.

Ablation study

To quantify the contribution of each module in the proposed two-stage Improved SCSO framework, an ablation study was performed by incrementally enabling the optimization stages. First, all classifier models were trained using the full feature set and default hyperparameters only. Next, in Stage 1, binary Improved SCSO-based FS was activated to evaluate the effect of reducing redundant soil and enzyme attributes. In Stage 2, continuous Improved SCSO-based HPT was performed. Finally, the complete pipeline (FS + HPT) was executed, where the hyperparameters were optimized on the reduced feature subset obtained from Stage 1. The results show that both stages individually improve performance, whereas the full Improved SCSO framework achieves the highest classification accuracy, confirming the necessity of both feature reduction and HPT.

Conclusion

This study used an Improved SCSO strategy to present a framework for soil fertility classification and a dynamic crop recommendation framework based on predicted fertility indicators and enzyme activity. The proposed methodology explicitly integrates soil enzyme activity indicators with physical and chemical soil attributes, addressing the limitation of conventional ML/DL models that often underutilize biochemical features owing to their complexity, redundancy, and noise. To overcome this, a two-stage Improved SCSO optimization pipeline was proposed. In Stage 1, an optimal subset of informative soil attributes was found using wrapper-based binary FS, and in Stage 2, a continuous Improved SCSO variant was applied to tune model hyperparameters on the reduced feature space. The improved convergence behavior of the Improved SCSO is achieved through a cosine-modulated escape-from-worst position update and adaptive inheritance from the global best solution, which effectively balances exploration and exploitation and reduces premature convergence. The proposed Improved SCSO was further improved using Correlation-Based Deduplication. Comprehensive experiments using stratified k-fold cross-validation demonstrated that the proposed Improved SCSO framework consistently improved the classification performance across multiple ML, DL, and hybrid models. Among all the evaluated classifiers, the GB model achieved the highest accuracy and was therefore selected as the final prediction engine. Finally, a dynamic crop mapping module was incorporated to translate fertility class predictions into crop suitability recommendations, thereby enabling adaptive and data-driven agricultural decision support. Overall, the Improved SCSO-based framework improves prediction accuracy, reduces feature dimensionality, improves prediction consistency, and increases agronomic relevance through the incorporation of enzyme-driven biological indicators, making it a reliable framework for intelligent soil fertility assessment and sustainable crop recommendation. Although the proposed framework achieved high classification accuracy, the dataset was relatively small and heterogeneous, as it was collected from various studies. Therefore, the performance obtained could partly reflect the specific characteristics of the dataset used. External validation was performed on independent soil datasets from different geographic regions to comprehensively evaluate the generalization ability and robustness of the proposed framework. Furthermore, future work will focus on incorporating remote sensing and IoT-based real-time monitoring systems to adopt a dynamic crop recommendation suitable for practical agricultural applications. Additionally, advanced structured feature representations, such as tensor-based or rolled feature transformations, can be explored to better align tabular soil variables with convolutional deep learning models.
