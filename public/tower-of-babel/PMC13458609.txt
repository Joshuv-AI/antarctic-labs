Adaptive texture and low-light feature learning in enhanced MobileNetV4 for industrial packaging quality inspection
Authors: Heng Xu, Jinmin Peng, Xianyin Lv, Xinyi Yu, Meishun Wu, Shituan Chi, Haoyu Zheng, Haotian Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSeal defect detection in food packaging integrity inspection presents substantial challenges, primarily owing to minimal textural disparities between defective and intact regions under industrial illumination. These challenges intensify in production environments where moderate class imbalance intersects with pronounced inter-class similarity. This study introduces an enhanced MobileNetV4 architecture incorporating adaptive feature learning mechanisms to overcome these limitations. A novel LocalAttention module, employing dynamically weighted rectangular sliding windows, augments the model’s texture discrimination capabilities by effectively isolating elongated structural anomalies characteristic of seal defects. To address suboptimal illumination conditions, we integrate Pinwheel-shaped Convolution (PConv), which leverages Gaussian-inspired distribution patterns to amplify weak signal detection in poorly lit environments. Additionally, an Adaptive Similarity-Modulated Loss function concurrently mitigates class imbalance while refining feature space organization for morphologically close categories. Evaluated on a grain packaging dataset from Zhangzhou, Fujian, the proposed framework achieves 99.54% accuracy and 99.75% F1-score with only 3.7M parameters and 25.27 FPS on Raspberry Pi 4B. To validate industrial reliability, robustness experiments under simulated LED degradation and sensor noise confirm consistent performance advantages over baseline architectures, and an 8-hour continuous stress test demonstrates stable edge deployment without thermal throttling. Cross-domain validation on NEU-CLS further achieves 99.72% accuracy with superior parameter efficiency, suggesting transferability to related industrial defect classification tasks.Keywords: Food packaging integrity, Defect detection, MobileNetV4, Edge computing, Computer visionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Food packaging serves as a cornerstone of modern supply chain management, ensuring product safety, extending shelf life, and mitigating waste across global distribution networks. Current statistics reveal that approximately 1.3 billion tons of food–representing one-third of total global production intended for human consumption–is lost or wasted annually, with compromised packaging integrity serving as a primary contributor to contamination and spoilage1. Against the backdrop of accelerating industrialization and escalating consumer expectations for premium-quality products, real-time assessment of packaging integrity has become paramount, particularly for moisture-sensitive commodities such as grains, where seal failures can precipitate microbial contamination and generate economic losses exceeding billions of dollars per annum2. This imperative is further intensified by stringent regulatory frameworks and the industry’s transition toward sustainable manufacturing paradigms,highlighting the importance of automated, precision-based detection systems in contemporary production environments.

Figure 1 depicts a granular vacuum packaging production line where particulate materials are first formed into hexahedral configurations through vacuum packaging machinery, then integrated into complete grain packages via automated bagging systems. Throughout this assembly sequence, deteriorating equipment conditions or operational irregularities can lead to absent exterior packaging elements or compromised seal integrity, consequently jeopardizing both food safety standards and institutional credibility. Present quality assurance protocols depend predominantly on manual inspection conducted by experienced personnel–an approach that proves labor-intensive, inefficient, and susceptible to human error. Therefore, exploring visual technology-based methodologies for evaluating packaging integrity has become imperative. Fundamentally, visual technology-driven assessment of packaging completeness constitutes a specialized variant of image classification tasks.

Vacuum packaging production line for granular products showing hexahedral forming and assembly stages. Seal integrity defects commonly emerge in outer packaging operations, requiring automated inspection systems to prevent quality failures in downstream distribution.

The domain of automated visual inspection has undergone profound transformation through advances in computer vision and deep learning methodologies, revolutionizing conventional manual assessment protocols into intelligent, data-driven systems. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) represent the predominant paradigm within this field, facilitating precise feature extraction from imagery to detect structural anomalies including cracks, seal breaches, and dimensional misalignments in packaging applications3. While early methodologies depended upon handcrafted feature descriptors coupled with classical machine learning algorithms, contemporary approaches have transitioned toward end-to-end deep learning architectures that demonstrate robust adaptation to complex environmental variations encompassing illumination conditions, spatial orientation, and material heterogeneity4. Lightweight network architectures, exemplified by the MobileNet series, have garnered significant attention due to their optimal balance between classification accuracy and computational efficiency, rendering them particularly suitable for deployment within resource-constrained edge computing environments5. Notably, MobileNetV3, introduced in 2019, incorporated depthwise separable convolutions and neural architecture search optimization to achieve superior performance metrics while maintaining reduced parameter counts, thereby enabling practical implementation across mobile and embedded platforms6. Subsequent iterations, including MobileNetV4, further enhance hybrid computational efficiency through the integration of universal inverted bottleneck structures and sophisticated attention mechanisms, demonstrating exceptional performance on standardized benchmarks such as ImageNet7. Within food packaging inspection applications, these architectures have been successfully deployed to identify anomalies across sealing interfaces, labeling adherence, and container integrity, frequently integrated with industrial imaging systems and programmable logic controllers to facilitate real-time process feedback8. Beyond their technical contributions, such systems establish critical intersections with interdisciplinary domains including food science and supply chain optimization, delivering substantial societal benefits through waste reduction, enhanced food security protocols, and diminished environmental impact via the prevention of defective product distribution9.

Despite these technological advances, substantial gaps remain in adapting lightweight architectures for real-time packaging inspection under operational constraints. While many contemporary approaches demonstrate high accuracy on curated datasets, they exhibit significant performance degradation when confronted with low-contrast imagery captured in suboptimal illumination conditions characteristic of industrial production environments, where subtle anomalies such as edge irregularities become obscured by ambient noise and shadow artifacts10. Previous investigations, including those utilizing GhostNet and EfficientNet variants, have successfully addressed parameter optimization but frequently compromise the capacity to capture long-range spatial dependencies or effectively manage imbalanced class distributions–prevalent conditions in industrial contexts where conforming packages dramatically outnumber defective specimens11,12. A representative 2022 investigation of seal defect detection employing YOLO-based architectures achieved superior recall metrics yet incurred prohibitive computational overhead, precluding deployment on cost-effective hardware platforms such as Raspberry Pi systems13. Furthermore, attention mechanisms incorporated within Transformer-hybrid architectures, while demonstrating efficacy for global contextual modeling, introduce processing latency that impedes real-time operation at production velocities exceeding 20 units per second14. These constraints not only undermine detection reliability but also neglect the requirement for adaptive feature learning algorithms that prioritize critical textural characteristics, particularly those manifested in grain packaging seal interfaces. Although benchmark datasets such as MVTEC AD have substantially advanced anomaly detection methodologies, they lack domain-specific relevance to food packaging applications, particularly those encountered in dynamic production environments across regions such as Southeast Asia, where environmental variability exacerbates imaging complexities15. Consequently, a significant research opportunity exists to augment lightweight network architectures with specialized modules that preserve computational efficiency while enhancing robustness to operational variations, thereby narrowing the performance gap between controlled laboratory conditions and industrial deployment scenarios.

This investigation seeks to address these deficiencies by proposing an enhanced MobileNetV4 architecture for real-time integrity assessment of food packaging seal systems. We introduce a LocalAttention module that employs dynamic weighted rectangular sliding windows to implement computationally efficient self-attention mechanisms, coupled with an edge attention decrement algorithm to prioritize texture-critical feature representations. Additionally, we incorporate Pinwheel-shaped partial convolutions (PConv) within the model’s foundational layers to approximate Gaussian-like receptive field characteristics, which may improve weak signal detection under suboptimal illumination conditions. To address class imbalance challenges, we propose an Adaptive Similarity-Modulated Loss (ASML) function that integrates similarity-aware weighting with feature space optimization.

Using a grain packaging dataset comprising high-resolution imagery from a production facility in Zhangzhou, Fujian Province (1,484 images), supplemented by cross-domain validation on the NEU-CLS industrial defect benchmark, we evaluate whether these architectural modifications can achieve favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-offs suitable for deployment on resource-constrained edge computing platforms. The proposed approach is tested on Raspberry Pi 4B hardware to assess its feasibility for real-time industrial applications. While our primary evaluation focuses on a specific grain packaging scenario, the cross-domain validation aims to provide preliminary evidence of potential transferability to similar industrial defect detection tasks. This work provides an alternative lightweight architecture approach for food packaging quality inspection and related industrial defect classification tasks.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 reviews related work; Section 3 details the proposed methodology; Section 4 presents experiments and analysis; and Section 5 concludes with discussions and future directions.

Related work

Automated defect detection in industrial environments, particularly for food packaging integrity assessment, has gained increasing attention with the rapid development of computer vision and deep learning technologies. These approaches play an important role in ensuring product safety, reducing material waste, and maintaining compliance with strict quality-control standards by identifying structural anomalies such as seal defects, contamination, and surface irregularities in real time. Early inspection systems mainly relied on traditional image processing techniques, including edge detection and intensity thresholding, but their performance was often limited by variations in illumination, surface reflectance, and production speed16. More recent studies have shifted toward convolutional neural network (CNN)-based methods, which can learn hierarchical feature representations and therefore provide more robust inspection performance across heterogeneous industrial scenarios10.

Lightweight CNN architectures have become a core foundation for real-time inspection systems because they emphasize computational efficiency and are well suited to edge devices with limited processing resources. The MobileNet family, beginning with MobileNetV1 in 2017, introduced depthwise separable convolutions to substantially reduce parameter count and computational cost while maintaining competitive classification accuracy, thereby establishing a practical basis for mobile and embedded vision applications5. Subsequent variants further improved this design. For example, MobileNetV3 incorporated neural architecture search and squeeze-and-excitation mechanisms to enhance efficiency and representation capacity6. More recently, MobileNetV4 extended this line of development through universal inverted bottleneck blocks and hybrid architectural designs, achieving state-of-the-art efficiency on benchmarks such as ImageNet and reporting latency reductions of up to 20% on mobile hardware platforms7. These lightweight architectures have also been adopted in defect detection applications. Abdelsattar et al17. investigated the MobileNet series for binary classification of contaminated and clean photovoltaic surfaces, showing that MobileNetV1 achieved favorable performance while remaining suitable for resource-constrained deployment. Similarly, the FedDyMNv4++ framework introduced a dynamic MobileNetV4-based model for remote sensing change detection, combining federated learning with reduced computational overhead for multi-modal data analysis18. Despite these advances, lightweight CNNs still often struggle in low-contrast or poorly illuminated environments, where subtle defect signals may be too weak to be captured effectively by general-purpose convolutional operations.

Attention mechanisms have emerged as an effective strategy for enhancing CNNs by allowing models to focus on informative features while capturing broader spatial dependencies. Inspired by the success of Transformer architectures, self-attention and related modules have been incorporated into lightweight networks to improve feature aggregation and defect localization without introducing excessive computational cost. For example, Ban et al.19 proposed an attention-enhanced model for cigarette package micro-defect detection, integrating channel and spatial attention to improve sensitivity to fine structural anomalies. Tang et al.20 developed a dual-domain attention framework for surface defect detection, in which channel and spatial pathways were jointly exploited to improve representation quality under limited computational budgets. In packaging inspection, Vijayakumar et al.21 employed coordinate attention in a real-time pharmaceutical blister inspection system, demonstrating that lightweight attention can improve focus on defect-prone regions while preserving low latency. However, many attention mechanisms, especially conventional multi-head self-attention designs, still incur quadratic computational complexity and are therefore difficult to deploy efficiently on edge devices. Although subsequent studies, such as hardware-oriented attention designs in GhostNetV2 and its extended variants, have alleviated part of this burden, challenges remain in capturing texture-sensitive information through dynamically adjusted local weighting under practical resource constraints11.

Imbalanced data distributions represent another persistent difficulty in industrial inspection, where defective samples are usually far less frequent than normal ones. Under such conditions, standard cross-entropy loss tends to bias training toward majority classes, which can substantially degrade minority class recognition. To address this issue, a large body of work has focused on specialized loss formulations. Focal loss reduces the contribution of easily classified samples and has been extended in later studies, such as the enhanced focal loss used in a 2024 imbalanced fault diagnosis framework to dynamically adjust decision boundaries for incremental learning scenarios22. Other approaches have introduced balanced loss formulations with class-aware weighting and truncation strategies to reduce label confusion in defect segmentation under uneven class distributions23. A recent review further emphasized hybrid strategies that combine data augmentation and cost-sensitive learning, including GAN-based methods that synthesize minority samples while jointly optimizing training objectives for rare-event recognition24. In computer vision, a 2025 survey of deep learning loss functions also highlighted the growing importance of adaptive criteria that consider semantic similarity in imbalanced settings, particularly for fine-grained anomaly segmentation tasks25. Nevertheless, most existing losses still focus primarily on sample reweighting or decision boundary adjustment, while paying less attention to improving feature-space discrimination when minority classes closely resemble normal samples, as is often the case in subtle packaging defect inspection.

Within food packaging integrity assessment, deep learning methods have been widely explored for tasks such as seal verification, label inspection, and contaminant detection. Diaz et al.26 applied CNN-based models to anomaly detection in sterile barrier packaging systems and reported promising accuracy, although their method required substantial annotated data. Tavasoli et al.27 introduced WIPE, a web-based evaluation framework that supports computer vision analysis in dynamic packaging workflows. For particulate material packaging, however, related studies remain limited. Sifnaios et al.28 proposed a hyperspectral imaging-based approach for real-time material classification, but the study did not address deployment on low-cost edge hardware. Overall, although existing methods have shown effectiveness in controlled settings, their performance often degrades in practical scenarios characterized by poor illumination, class imbalance, and strict real-time constraints on inexpensive platforms such as Raspberry Pi devices. These limitations are particularly problematic for subtle structural defects, which may be easily overlooked under weak visual contrast. Addressing these challenges requires not only task-adapted model design, but also end-to-end validation under realistic edge deployment conditions.

In summary, prior research has made substantial progress in lightweight model design, attention mechanisms, and imbalance-aware learning, yet three important gaps remain for practical packaging inspection on resource-constrained hardware. First, existing lightweight backbones such as MobileNetV4 provide strong efficiency but still rely on general-purpose convolutions not specifically tailored to extract weak defect signals under dim illumination. Second, many lightweight attention modules improve feature selection but do not explicitly model dynamic local weighting and edge-aware attenuation for texture-sensitive regions. Third, current loss functions can alleviate class imbalance but rarely enhance feature-space discrimination while accounting for inter-class similarity among subtle defect categories. Beyond these architectural gaps, recent work has highlighted the broader importance of end-to-end deployment validation for lightweight vision systems in resource-limited environments. You et al.29 deployed a pruned YOLO variant on the Jetson Orin Nano for apple detection under varying weather conditions, achieving over 100 FPS with fewer than 1M parameters. Weng et al.30 validated tomato stalk identification on the same platform at 26 FPS in real greenhouse environments, while Islam et al.31 demonstrated real-time weed segmentation on UAV companion computers under strict payload constraints. Although these studies address agricultural inspection tasks, they collectively underscore the importance of validating lightweight models under real deployment constraints – a principle that equally applies to industrial packaging inspection. Motivated by these observations, this work develops an integrated lightweight inspection framework based on MobileNetV4 for grain packaging defect detection. The proposed method combines a local attention mechanism for texture-sensitive feature enhancement, a pinwheel-shaped convolution module for weak-signal extraction under low-light conditions, and an adaptive similarity-aware loss for imbalanced classification. In addition to accuracy evaluation, we further validate the method on Raspberry Pi 4B to assess its practical suitability for edge-deployable industrial inspection.

Methods

MobileNetV4-Conv-Small is the smallest convolutional model variant in the MobileNetV4 family, designed for mobile devices with limited computing resources. The model achieves efficient inference on mobile devices, achieving extremely low latency while maintaining high accuracy. Compared with the previous MobileNet series, V4-Conv-Small adopts the Universal Inverted Bottleneck (UIB) search block, which is a unified and flexible structure that combines Inverted Bottleneck, ConvNext, Feed Forward Network, and a new Extra Depthwise variant.

The improved structure of this paper is shown in Fig. 2 and detailed in Table 1. As illustrated in the four-stage architecture, the study introduces three key innovations to address grain packaging seal defect classification challenges. First, the LocalAttention module is integrated in Stage 2 to enhance the model’s contextual attention ability and increase the focus on thin and long lines characteristic of seal defects. Second, the Pinwheel-shaped Convolution (PConv) is incorporated within the PIB module in Stage 3 to improve the model’s robustness under matte materials and low-light conditions. These architectural improvements enhance the model’s ability to recognize subtle textures and granularity differences in dark environments. Finally, the proposed ASML loss function, applied throughout the training process, improves the model’s ability to distinguish similar categories while alleviating the problem of uneven distribution of dataset samples.

LocalAttention

As a lightweight alternative to the standard Multi-Head Attention (MHA)14, the Multi-Query Attention (MQA) mechanism significantly reduces computational complexity and memory requirements by sharing key-value pairs across all attention heads. This parameter-sharing strategy provides MQA with a clear advantage in resource-constrained scenarios, particularly for edge device deployment and real-time processing applications.

However, the lightweight design of MQA also introduces limitations in representational capacity. Due to shared key-value pairs, different attention heads struggle to capture diverse feature information, which is particularly evident in tasks involving directional slender structures and texture anomalies, such as defect detection in packaging seals. Standard MQA often over-relies on global information, overlooking subtle local structural changes32.

The LocalAttention module proposed in this paper enhances MQA’s local perception through a direction-sensitive rectangular window mechanism.

As shown in Fig. 3, the key innovation is the application of an asymmetric rectangular average pooling of size (3,5) on the shared Key and Value projections, while maintaining the high-resolution spatial details of the Queries. This specific ratio is carefully chosen based on the physical characteristics of grain packaging seal defects. Typically, seal defects manifest as elongated, thin cracks or gaps, which exhibit greater ductility in the horizontal direction (width 5) compared to the vertical direction (height 3). This asymmetry allows the attention mechanism to efficiently capture horizontally dominant linear anomalies while maintaining sensitivity to vertical variations without sacrificing the fine-grained spatial information crucial for defect localization.

Structure of the proposed LocalAttention module. To preserve fine-grained details of defects, the Query (Q) features maintain the original resolution. The computational cost is reduced by applying a (3×5) asymmetric windowed pooling solely to the Key (K) and Value (V) branches. This design allows the module to capture directional linear features efficiently without sacrificing the spatial precision required for detecting thin cracks.

PConv Inverted Bottleneck (PIB)

The diversity of packaging materials poses a significant challenge to seal defect detection. Many commonly used materials, such as metalized films and matte-finish foils, exhibit low reflectance and weak surface contrast, making it difficult to distinguish subtle texture changes in the sealed area. As shown in Fig. 5, standard depthwise separable convolutions (DWConv), while computationally efficient, suffer from channel isolation that restricts inter-channel information flow, limiting the model’s capacity to capture such low-contrast texture variations.

Comparison of feature activation maps between PConv and DWConv. PConv produces stronger responses on low-contrast seal regions.

To address this limitation, we propose the PConv Inverted Bottleneck (PIB) module, which introduces the pinwheel-shaped convolution (PConv)33 as a spatial processing stage between the expansion and projection layers of the Fused Inverted Bottleneck (Fused IB) block in MobileNetV4. In the original Fused IB, a 3× 3 convolution directly expands the input channels and a 1× 1 convolution projects them back. Our PIB inserts a PConv module between these two layers, adding a multi-directional spatial mixing stage with minimal parameter overhead. The overall architecture is illustrated in Fig. 6.

Conceptual overview

ASML is designed to handle classification difficulty at three levels. At the class level, a similarity-aware weight encourages the model to allocate more attention to categories that are both underrepresented and semantically close to other categories. At the instance level, the focusing exponent adjusts how the loss treats easy versus hard samples depending on their category’s similarity profile. At the feature level, a contrastive term encourages same-class samples to cluster together while pushing different-class samples apart, which may help form clearer inter-class boundaries.

Dataset classification standards

Seal integrity assessment constitutes the primary quality control imperative in vacuum packaging operations, as seal failures directly compromise the fundamental preservation mechanism protecting granular food products from microbial contamination, oxidative degradation, and moisture infiltration. This criticality is codified in international food safety standards, particularly ISO 22000 (Food Safety Management Systems)36, which designates packaging seal verification as a mandatory Critical Control Point (CCP) requiring systematic monitoring throughout production. Unlike surface cosmetic defects such as printing irregularities, material wrinkles, or minor contamination–quality attributes that should be controlled by packaging material suppliers through proper material selection and pre-delivery quality inspection–seal integrity defects represent immediate food safety hazards necessitating real-time detection and rejection at the vacuum packaging stage. Consequently, this study focuses specifically on seal completeness assessment, the most safety-critical inspection task in the vacuum packaging workflow, where failure detection directly prevents compromised products from entering distribution channels.

The classification standard of the grain packaging dataset is based on the integrity of the packaging, and the defects are divided into three categories: , , and , as well as the normal category for complete packaging. This classification method can intuitively locate the specific location of the problem in the production line, thereby achieving targeted maintenance and repair.

Specifically, (all seal) means that only the vacuum inner bag enters the detection device, and the outer packaging is completely missing, which is mainly caused by problems in the bag feeding machine or failure of the pneumatic suction cup. (no seal) means that the outer packaging has been put on but a defect is detected due to the mismatch of the outer packaging bag shape or the plastic seal texture at the seal, which usually indicates that there may be a problem with the plastic sealing machine. (little seal) means that the seal is loose or incompletely sealed, which is generally related to heat sealing pressure or temperature control.

Evaluation indicators

Considering the class imbalance characteristics of the dataset, this study uses a variety of evaluation indicators to comprehensively evaluate the model performance. Although Accuracy is the most direct performance indicator, indicating the proportion of all correctly identified samples in the total samples, in the case of data imbalance, even if the model identifies all samples as the majority class, the Accuracy may be high and cannot truly reflect the model performance.

Therefore, in view of the characteristics of grain packaging defects, this paper introduces Precision, Recall and F1-score as the main evaluation indicators. Precision measures the proportion of samples predicted by the model as positive that are actually positive, reflecting false positives; Recall measures the proportion of samples that are correctly detected in the actual positive class, reflecting missed detections. Experimental configuration

Hardware and software configuration

The experiments in this study were conducted using GPU in the Windows operating system environment. The specific experimental configuration includes NVIDIA RTX 4090 graphics card, CUDA 11.7, PyTorch 1.13.1 deep learning framework, and Python 3.9.18 programming environment.

Training parameters

The dataset is divided into a training set and a test set in an 8:2 ratio. For model training, the number of iterations is set to 200 epochs, with a batch size of 128. The AdamW optimizer is adopted with initial learning rate of 0.005, weight decay of , and momentum parameters , . The cosine annealing learning rate scheduler is used with and . No learning rate warm-up is applied. Input images are resized to 224× 224 pixels and normalized using ImageNet mean and standard deviation values. No data augmentation is applied during training, as the industrial imaging system provides consistent capture conditions with controlled illumination and fixed camera positioning. All models are trained from scratch without pre-trained weights to ensure fair comparison across architectures.

Under severe brightness reduction, the dominant factor is parameter capacity: global illumination suppression compresses signal energy across all feature channels simultaneously, and MobileNetV4_Conv_Medium’s larger parameter count (8.43M) provides representational redundancy that tolerates this distributional shift — channels that lose discriminative signal are compensated by others51. Under extreme noise, the dominant factor shifts to frequency selectivity: PConv’s directional kernels amplify high-frequency textural signals, but Gaussian noise at occupies the same high-frequency band as the target features, causing our sensitivity mechanism to amplify noise and signal indiscriminately. The combined degradation results resolve both anomalies simultaneously: brightness reduction suppresses the high-frequency noise energy that undermines our model under isolated extreme noise, while noise simultaneously destabilizes MobileNetV4_Conv_Medium’s redundancy-dependent robustness, allowing our specialized design to prevail across all combined conditions.

The conditions under which baselines outperform our model represent isolated single-factor extremes outside the realistic industrial operating envelope, as evidenced by the combined degradation results where the proposed model consistently achieves the highest accuracy across all tested configurations.

Dynamic environmental robustness analysis

The hardware configuration of the proposed system was designed with explicit consideration of vibration-induced image degradation from the outset. Mechanical vibration has been shown to cause motion blur that measurably reduces the accuracy of AI-based visual perception systems in industrial settings, with degradation increasing with vibration amplitude and frequency53,54. To address this at the hardware level, two design decisions were adopted. First, the MV-CL042-91GC line-scan camera acquires images one pixel row at a time with a minimum per-line exposure time of 5 μs. Unlike area-scan cameras, which integrate scene information over a full-frame exposure window, line-scan cameras limit any within-row motion blur to the displacement occurring within a single microsecond-scale exposure interval, making them inherently more tolerant to vibration in dynamic environments55,56. Second, the production line employs a segmented belt-drive structure, which attenuates the mechanical coupling between the drive mechanism and the imaging zone.

Nevertheless, under sustained continuous operation, residual lateral vibration between successive scan lines cannot be fully excluded. Each row of the reconstructed image is acquired at a distinct time instant, separated by the line period . If lateral mechanical vibration is present, adjacent rows may be displaced horizontally relative to one another, and this accumulated inter-row offset manifests as horizontal distortion in the final image. This effect is directionally constrained: because the conveyor travels along a fixed horizontal axis, any vibration-induced displacement is predominantly aligned with that axis57. Accordingly, the appropriate simulation model is a horizontal linear motion blur kernel, in which each pixel in a given row is averaged with its horizontal neighbors over a span of k pixels, representing the smearing effect of lateral displacement across successive line acquisitions. The proposed model showed a maximum accuracy reduction of 0.47% under all blur conditions, which is consistent with the general stability observed across competing architectures. This outcome is in line with the hardware design rationale: the line-scan acquisition mechanism constrains inter-row displacement to a physically bounded range under typical industrial vibration conditions, and the segmented belt structure further limits vibration transmission to the imaging zone. Taken together, the hardware design choices and the simulation results provide a consistent account of why vibration does not substantially degrade system performance under normal operating conditions.

Long-term deployment stability on edge device

To validate sustained operational stability under continuous industrial deployment, a 8-hour stress test was conducted on the Raspberry Pi 4B under full system load. The device was equipped only with a standard aluminum heatsink on the CPU and the official Raspberry Pi 5 V cooling fan, with no additional thermal management measures applied, reflecting typical off-the-shelf deployment conditions. Inference speed, CPU temperature, and memory usage were logged every 0.5 seconds throughout the test. As shown in Fig. 16, the system achieved a stable mean throughput of 23.35 FPS (σ = 0.015) after an initial warm-up period, while CPU temperature stabilized at a mean of 50.6C, approximately 29C below the hardware throttle threshold of 80C. No thermal throttling events were observed during the entire 8-hour period, and memory usage remained stable without evidence of leakage. The FPS distribution was highly concentrated, with a P5–P95 range of 23.34–23.38 FPS, further confirming the consistency of long-term inference performance. Although FPS and temperature showed a statistically significant positive correlation (r = 0.646, p < 0.001), the absolute FPS variation across the observed temperature range of 48–54C remained below 0.07 FPS, indicating negligible practical impact for industrial deployment. It should be noted that this sustained system throughput (23.35 FPS) is slightly lower than the previously reported single-image inference benchmark (25.27 FPS), as the latter measures isolated forward-pass latency while the former reflects end-to-end pipeline overhead including preprocessing and PLC communication. Both values substantially exceed the production requirement of 40 bags/min, confirming the deployment reliability of the proposed system under prolonged operation.

System implementation and deployment

As shown in Figs. 17 and 18, the defect detection system based on the improved MobileNetV4 developed in this study adopts a layered architecture design. It realizes an end-to-end defect identification pipeline through the vertical collaboration of the physical layer, edge computing layer, and communication management layer.

Deployed inspection system. (Left) 3D structural schematic showing the spatial layout of dual LED light sources, line-scan camera, conveyor belt, and alarm light. (Right) Photograph of the production-line deployment and the three-tier communication architecture connecting the detection target, ONNX inference engine, and conveyor control subsystems.

Three-layer software architecture of the defect detection system. The Application layer manages camera acquisition and PLC interfacing; the Algorithm layer handles image preprocessing and ONNX model inference; the Detection System layer executes defect classification and generates quality reports.

The physical layer constructs the foundational hardware environment, where the PLC controller coordinates the synchronous operation of the conveyor belt, light source system, and industrial camera via industrial communication protocols. Specifically, the MV-CL042-91GC line scan camera and MV-LTDS-H-500-W light source utilize an optimized spatial layout to ensure stable, high-quality image acquisition at a conveying speed of 40 bags/min.

The edge computing layer is deployed on the Raspberry Pi 4B, implementing a lightweight processing model chain that comprises three key modules: the image preprocessing module is responsible for noise filtering and image enhancement; the improved MobileNetV4 network performs feature extraction and classification tasks; and the result processing module executes the statistics and analysis of the classification outputs. These three modules form a complete processing link driven by efficient data flow.

The communication and data management layer establishes a closed loop for detection tasks. It sequentially performs data storage, result analysis, and visual display, while enabling real-time anomaly alerts through the KRS-57(L)−2-R-R single-layer red alarm facility. The system layers achieve two-way data interaction via standardized interfaces: the physical layer transmits raw image data upward, the edge computing layer sends control instructions downward and transmits processing results upward, and the communication layer handles long-term data management and human-computer interaction.

This layered architecture not only guarantees the real-time performance of the system (with a single-image processing latency of merely 39 ms), but also supports flexible functional expansion. Ultimately, the system generates a visual detection report that meets industry standards and has been successfully integrated into actual packaging production lines.

Real-world deployment case study in Daqing factory

To verify the practical utility of the proposed model in engineering practice, this study presents a real-world case study deployed at the Daqing Grain Packaging Factory in Heilongjiang Province. The system achieves efficient and accurate defect detection through configurable deployment and modular design.

The operational workflow of the deployed system consists of four primary stages: initialization, image acquisition, model inference, and result analysis. In the initialization stage, the system automatically loads configuration parameters and establishes hardware connections. During image acquisition, the photoelectric sensor detects passing products and triggers the camera to capture the target image (as visualized in the host interface in Fig. 19). In the model inference stage, the captured image is transmitted to the pre-trained deep learning model for analysis. Finally, in the result analysis stage, the system calculates the probability of various defects based on the model’s output and presents the prediction results. The system subsequently communicates with the PLC via established transmission protocols to execute the automatic physical rejection of defective products.

Conclusion and discussion

This study introduces a comprehensive framework for vacuum-sealed grain packaging integrity assessment through three key innovations. First, a LocalAttention module employing dynamically weighted rectangular sliding windows enhances extraction of elongated textural features characteristic of packaging seals. Second, multi-directional convolutional branches construct Gaussian-distributed receptive field patterns, improving weak signal detection under low-light industrial conditions. Third, an adaptive loss optimization strategy integrates semantic similarity information with feature representation learning, simultaneously addressing class imbalance and high-similarity sample discrimination. Evaluated on both a grain packaging dataset and the NEU-CLS industrial benchmark, the framework achieves 99.54%,99.72% accuracy with 3.7M parameters while maintaining 25 FPS on Raspberry Pi 4B. Comprehensive sensitivity analysis across 39 hyperparameter configurations and feature space visualization confirm that performance gains stem from architectural design rather than dataset-specific shortcuts or meticulous tuning.

However, several critical limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, the framework addresses only seal integrity defects without unified detection of concurrent quality issues such as OCR errors, surface contamination, misalignment, or printing defects, limiting practical utility where operators require simultaneous multi-defect inspection. Second, while deployment succeeded in one factory and robustness experiments demonstrate resilience to simulated equipment degradation, the system lacks comprehensive long-term validation through systematic operational logging across diverse facilities. Simulated degradation cannot fully replicate the cumulative effects of months-long operation under varying ambient conditions and facility-specific interference patterns. In particular, while the dynamic environmental robustness analysis conducted in this work employs physically motivated horizontal motion blur kernels to simulate vibration-induced inter-row displacement, this simulation represents an idealized approximation of real conveyor vibration. Factors such as multi-directional vibration coupling, resonance effects at specific conveyor speeds, and progressive mechanical wear are not captured by the current simulation protocol. Systematic long-term deployment logging across multiple facilities would be necessary to fully characterize the system’s robustness under these conditions, and remains an important direction for future work. Third, although initial hardware costs appear favorable compared to the commercial solutions surveyed, the analysis does not quantify ongoing operational expenses including recalibration, component replacement, and maintenance costs over multi-year deployment cycles. Additionally, the dataset encompasses only three seal-related categories while industrial packaging suffers diverse failure modes including wrinkles, material contamination, and print quality issues. Deployment validation at a single facility provides insufficient evidence for generalization across varying packaging materials, production speeds, and environmental conditions.

Future research should address these limitations through several directions. Developing multi-task detection frameworks for simultaneous seal assessment, OCR verification, contamination detection, and alignment checking would enhance practical utility. Establishing multi-site deployment protocols with systematic performance logging and total cost of ownership tracking across 6–12 month periods would validate industrial robustness and provide actionable economic data. Expanding datasets to encompass diverse packaging materials, comprehensive defect taxonomies, and varied production environments would enable more generalizable models. Exploring semi-supervised learning to reduce annotation dependencies, coupled with neural architecture search for efficiency-accuracy optimization, would improve deployment feasibility and strengthen the foundation for intelligent quality inspection systems in manufacturing.
