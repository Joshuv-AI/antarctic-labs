1.
Introduction

The arithmetic of special values of zeta functions and
L
-functions lies at the heart of modern number theory, connecting modular forms, binary quadratic forms, and algebraic invariants of number fields. Since Hecke’s foundational work on modular forms and zeta functions of quadratic fields
[
6
]
, it has been clear that modular objects often provide a natural mechanism for producing explicit expressions for zeta values and for revealing their symmetries. A particularly effective tool in this direction is the theory of
period polynomials
, which encodes the critical
L
-values of cusp forms and reflects functional equations in a clear way
[
9
]
. When level is involved, it is natural to work with
vector-valued
period polynomials, which keep track of cosets in
\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and allow one to separate arithmetic contributions that are invisible in the scalar setting.

In parallel, binary quadratic forms provide a classical and remarkably concrete approach to quadratic fields and their zeta functions. Zagier’s work
[
12
]
illustrates how sums over quadratic forms of fixed discriminant give rise to modular forms and how such constructions can be used to obtain explicit formulas for arithmetic quantities. In the presence of congruence conditions, one is led to refined families of quadratic forms adapted to
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, and the associated orbit decompositions naturally produce Dirichlet-type zeta functions attached to
\Gamma_{0}(N)
-equivalence classes. Concretely, these are partial zeta-type sums obtained by summing suitable powers of
Q(x,y)
over lattice points (or equivalently over representatives in a
\Gamma_{0}(N)
-class), and they encode the arithmetic of the corresponding orbit in a way compatible with the level structure. On the modular side, level
N
also introduces Fricke/Atkin–Lehner involutions
[
1
]
, which interact with parity and with the decomposition of period polynomials into even and odd parts.

The purpose of this paper is to make these connections explicit by studying cusp forms built from
\Gamma_{0}(N)
-orbits of binary quadratic forms and by computing their vector-valued period polynomials in closed form.
Precise definitions of the objects and notation appearing below are collected in Section
2
.

Concretely, for integers
k\geq 2
,
N\geq 1
, and for
D>0
together with
\rho
satisfying
\rho^{2}\equiv D\pmod{4N}
, we consider explicit cusp forms in
S_{2k}(N)
defined by weighted sums over quadratic forms in
\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}
, where

\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}:=\{[Na,b,c]=Nax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}:a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z},\ b^{2}-4Nac=D,\ b\equiv\rho\pmod{2N}\}.

These forms admit natural combinations
f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{\pm}
adapted to the involution
b\mapsto-b
, and are well-suited to the Fricke operator
W_{N}
, enabling a clean period-polynomial analysis.

Our first main result (Theorem
2.1
) gives an explicit expression for the vector-valued period polynomial

r^{+}_{f^{+}_{k,N,D,\rho}}\;+\;r^{-}_{f^{-}_{k,N,D,\rho}}.

Here, for a cusp form
f\in S_{2k}(N)
,
r_{f}
denotes its vector-valued period polynomial and
r_{f}^{\pm}
its even/odd parts (see Section
2
).
The identity component of our formula is particularly explicit: it separates an
algebraic part
coming from finite sums over the ranges
a>0>c
and
a<0<c
of quadratic forms, and a
zeta part
involving the natural zeta functions
\zeta_{N,D,\rho}(s)
built from
\Gamma_{0}(N)
-equivalence classes (see Section
2
).
Conceptually, this can be viewed as a level-
N
period–zeta connection: period-polynomial coefficients are expressed in terms of quadratic-form sums together with explicitly normalized zeta values.

As a first application, when
k
is odd, we obtain a closed formula for the asymmetry

\zeta_{N,D,\rho}(k)\;-\;\zeta_{N,D,-\rho}(k)

(Theorem
2.2
). This result isolates the precise contribution of the congruence condition
\rho
and shows how it is detected by parity considerations in the period-polynomial setting. In particular, it exhibits how Fricke symmetry and the even/odd decomposition of periods translate into a nontrivial arithmetic identity between zeta values.

Finally, under a natural vanishing condition on Fricke-invariant cusp forms at the relevant lower levels, we apply the preceding period computations to deduce an explicit formula for the Dedekind zeta value
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})}(k)
at even integers
k
(Theorem
2.3
), expressed as a finite divisor-sum with congruence restrictions. This provides computable expressions for
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})}(k)
that refine the classical Hecke-type evaluations and illustrate the strength of period methods in the presence of level structure.

2.
Main Results

Let
k\geq 2
be a positive integer and let
N
be a positive integer.
We write
i:=\sqrt{-1}
and denote by
I
the
2\times 2
identity matrix. Additionally, we denote by
\mathbb{H}
the complex upper half-plane.
Let

\Gamma_{0}(N):=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\gamma\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\}.

Furthermore, let
D
be a positive integer which is congruent to a square modulo
4N
and let
\rho
be an integer with
\rho^{2}\equiv D\pmod{4N}
. Consider the set

\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}:=\{[Na,b,c]=Nax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}:a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z},\penalty\ b^{2}-4Nac=D,\penalty\ b\equiv\rho\pmod{2N}\}

on which the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
acts by

\left(Q\circ\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)(x,y)=Q(\alpha x+\beta y,\gamma x+\delta y)\quad\left(Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho},\penalty\ \left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N)\right).

Define

\displaystyle f_{k,N,D,\rho}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{D^{k-1/2}}{\pi\binom{2k-2}{k-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\sum_{[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}}(Naz^{2}+bz+c)^{-k},

\displaystyle f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{D^{k-1/2}}{\pi\binom{2k-2}{k-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\sum_{[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}^{\prime}}(Naz^{2}+bz+c)^{-k},

\displaystyle f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{+}

\displaystyle:=f_{k,N,D,\rho}+f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{\prime},\quad f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{-}:=i(f_{k,N,D,\rho}-f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{\prime}),

where

\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}^{\prime}:=\{[Na,-b,c]:[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}\}.

Then
f_{k,N,D,\rho},f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{\prime},f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{\pm}\in S_{2k}(N)
, where
S_{2k}(N)
denotes the space of cusp forms of weight
2k
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.

For each
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}/\Gamma_{0}(N)
, fix any representative
Q_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{C}
and write

\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}:=\{A\in\Gamma_{0}(N):Q_{\mathcal{C}}\circ A=Q_{\mathcal{C}}\}.

For
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
, we define

\zeta_{\mathcal{C}}^{(N)}(s):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(b,d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(b,d)>0\\
\gcd(d,N)=1\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(b,d)^{-s},

and set

\zeta_{N,D,\rho}(s):=\sum_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}/\Gamma_{0}(N)}\zeta_{\mathcal{C}}^{(N)}(s).

For
A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
and a holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, put

(f|_{2k}A)(z):=(\det A)^{k}(\gamma z+\delta)^{-2k}f\left(\frac{\alpha z+\beta}{\gamma z+\delta}\right).

We set
V_{2k-2}
to be the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of polynomials in
X
of degree at most
2k-2
. For simplicity, let
e_{N}:=[\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma_{0}(N)]
.
For
f\in S_{2k}(N)
, define the
vector-valued period polynomial

r_{f}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{e_{N}}
of
f
by the vector

r_{f}(X):=(r_{f}(A)(X))_{A\in\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}

where

r_{f}(A)(X):=\int_{0}^{i\infty}(f|_{2k}A)(z)(X-z)^{2k-2}dz\in V_{2k-2}.

We write
r_{f}^{+}(A)(X)
(resp.
r_{f}^{-}(A)(X)
) for the even (resp. odd) part of
r_{f}(A)(X)
i.e.

r_{f}^{\pm}(A)(X):=\frac{1}{2}(r_{f}(A)(X)\pm r_{f}(A)(-X)).

Our first main result gives an expression for each component of the vector-valued period polynomial
r_{f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{+}}^{+}+r_{f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{-}}^{-}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
k,N,D
and
\rho
be as above. Assume that
D
is not a perfect square. Set

c_{k,D}:=\binom{2k-2}{k-1}D^{1/2-k}\pi.

For
A\in\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, the
A
-th component of the vector-valued period polynomial
r_{f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{+}}^{+}+r_{f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{-}}^{-}
is given by

r_{f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{+}}^{+}(A)(X)+r_{f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{-}}^{-}(A)(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{2k-2}i^{-n+1}\binom{2k-2}{n}r_{n}(A)X^{2k-2-n},

where

r_{n}(A)=\frac{i^{n^{2}}}{2c_{k,D}}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1/\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{[a,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}\circ A}+(-1)^{n}\sum_{[a,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}^{\prime}\circ A}\frac{t^{n}}{(-at^{2}+bit+c)^{k}}\right)dt.

In particular,

\displaystyle r_{f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{+}}^{+}(I)(X)

\displaystyle+r_{f_{k,N,D,\rho}^{-}}^{-}(I)(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}\\
a>0>c\end{subarray}}(NaX^{2}-bX+c)^{k-1}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}(NaX^{2}-bX+c)^{k-1}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\pi}{N(2k-1)c_{k,D}}\cdot\frac{\zeta_{N,D,\rho}(k)+(-1)^{k}\zeta_{N,D,-\rho}(k)}{\zeta(2k)\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2k})}X^{2k-2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{N^{k}(2k-1)c_{k,D}}\cdot\frac{\zeta_{N,D,-\rho}(k)+(-1)^{k}\zeta_{N,D,\rho}(k)}{\zeta(2k)\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2k})}.

As an application of Theorem
2.1
, we obtain a closed formula for the difference
\zeta_{N,D,\rho}(k)-\zeta_{N,D,-\rho}(k)
when
k
is odd.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
k,N,D
and
\rho
be as in Theorem
2.1
. If
k
is odd, then

\displaystyle\zeta_{N,D,\rho}(k)-\zeta_{N,D,-\rho}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{2k-1}N^{k}(2k-1)D^{1/2-k}B_{2k}\pi^{2k}}{(2k)!}\binom{2k-2}{k-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2k})\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}\\
a>0>c\end{subarray}}c^{k-1}-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}c^{k-1}\right),

where
B_{n}
denotes the
n
th Bernoulli number.

For a positive integer
M
, let
W_{M}
denote the Fricke involution:

W_{M}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1/\sqrt{M}\\
\sqrt{M}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Next, given a positive divisor
d
of
N
, define

S_{2k}^{+}(N/d):=\{f\in S_{2k}(N/d):f|_{2k}W_{N/d}=f\}.

Using Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the following formula for the value of the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})}(s)
at
s=k
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
k
be an even positive integer, let
N
be a positive integer, and let
D>0
be a fundamental discriminant such that
D\equiv 1\pmod{4N}
. Assume that
S_{2k}^{+}(N/d)=\{0\}
for every positive divisor
d
of
N
.
Then

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})}(k)

\displaystyle=(2k-1)D^{1/2-k}\zeta(2k)N^{k}\binom{2k-2}{k-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-2k}\prod_{p\mid(N/d)}(1-p^{-2k})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{D}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2N/d}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{k-1}\left(\frac{d(D-b^{2})}{4N}\right).

In the following example, we utilize Theorem
2.3
to evaluate
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{17})}(2)
and
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{145})}(2)
.

Example 2.1
.

(i)

Let
N=2
and
D=17
. Then
D\equiv 1\pmod{4N}
. By
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
,

\dim S_{4}^{+}(2)=0,

and, by
[
7
, Corollary 8.8]
,

\dim S_{4}^{+}(2/2)=\dim S_{4}^{+}(1)=\dim S_{4}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))=0.

Applying Theorem
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{17})}(2)=

\displaystyle 24\times 17^{-3/2}\times\zeta(4)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{15}{16}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{17}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{4}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{17-b^{2}}{8}\right)+\frac{1}{16}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{17}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{17-b^{2}}{4}\right)\right).

Note that

\displaystyle\{b\in\mathbb{Z}:|b|<\sqrt{17},\penalty\ b\equiv 1\pmod{2}\}

\displaystyle=\{-3,-1,1,3\},

\displaystyle\{b\in\mathbb{Z}:|b|<\sqrt{17},\penalty\ b\equiv 1\pmod{4}\}

\displaystyle=\{1,-3\}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{17}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{4}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{17-b^{2}}{8}\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}(2)+\sigma_{1}(1)=4,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{17}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{17-b^{2}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sigma_{1}(4)+2\sigma_{1}(2)=20.

Meanwhile, it is known that
\zeta(4)=\pi^{4}/90
. Consequently,

\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{17})}(2)=24\times 17^{-3/2}\times\frac{\pi^{4}}{90}\times\left(\frac{15}{16}\times 4+\frac{1}{16}\times 20\right)=\frac{4\pi^{4}}{51\sqrt{17}}.

(ii)

Let
N=3
and
D=145
. Then
4N=12
and
D\equiv 1\pmod{12}
. Using
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[
7
, Corollary 8.8]
, one can check that

\dim S_{4}^{+}(3)=\dim S_{4}^{+}(3/3)=0.

Hence, by Theorem
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{145})}(2)=

\displaystyle 54\times 145^{-3/2}\times\zeta(4)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{80}{81}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{145}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{6}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{145-b^{2}}{12}\right)+\frac{1}{81}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{145}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{145-b^{2}}{4}\right)\right).

A similar computation to part (i) shows that

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{145}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{6}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{145-b^{2}}{12}\right)=64\quad\text{and}\quad\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{145}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{145-b^{2}}{4}\right)=640.

Therefore,

\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{145})}(2)=54\times 145^{-3/2}\times\frac{\pi^{4}}{90}\times\left(\frac{80}{81}\times 64+\frac{1}{81}\times 640\right)=\frac{128\pi^{4}}{435\sqrt{145}}.

Preliminaries

\displaystyle r_{f}:\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\rightarrow V_{2k-2}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto r_{f}(A)

\widetilde{V}_{2k-2}^{\Gamma_{0}(N)}
.

. Abusing notation, we also denote this polynomial by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{D}

\displaystyle:=\{[a,b,c]=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}:a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z},\penalty\ b^{2}-4ac=D\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{D}^{0}

For a positive integer

.

Then

Lemma 4.1

Fix

and
\varepsilon>0
. Put

Setting

, we get

(4.3)

Let

Nr&1\end{smallmatrix}\right):r\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Then

is a subgroup of

and acts on

by

.
A direct computation shows that, for

In particular,
c

varies,

runs through the congruence classes modulo

.
Fix, for each

.
Then, for each fixed

\displaystyle\{[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{C}:b\pmod{2Nc}\}\times\mathbb{Z}

b\pmod{2Nc}\end{subarray}}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{u^{n}}{(-Na_{r}\varepsilon^{2}u^{2}+i(b+2rNc)\varepsilon u+c)^{k}}du,

\displaystyle L_{\varepsilon}

The inner sum is the Riemann sum for the integral

c>0\\
b\pmod{2Nc}\end{subarray}}c^{-k}

(see

, p. 226]

Writing any

as

with

and therefore

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(b,d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\

\gcd(d,N)=1\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(b,d)^{-k}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gcd(\ell,N)=1\end{subarray}}\ell^{-2k}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(b,d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(b,d)>0\\
\gcd(d,N)=1\\
\gcd(b,d)=1\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(b,d)^{-k}

\displaystyle=\zeta(2k)\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2k})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{C}\\
c>0\\
b\pmod{2Nc}\end{subarray}}c^{-k}.

Note that

\displaystyle=\sum_{[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1/\varepsilon}\frac{t^{n}}{(-Nat^{2}-bit+c)^{k}}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,\rho}}\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{1/\varepsilon}+\int_{-1/\varepsilon}^{-\varepsilon}\right)\frac{t^{n}}{(-Nat^{2}+bit+c)^{k}}dt

Let

.
Put

Q^{(d)}:=\left[\frac{N}{d}a,b,cd\right]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}

and set

\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d):=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N/d):\beta\equiv 0\pmod{d}\right\}.

Then the map

\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{2},\quad(u,v)\mapsto(u,dv)

induces a one-to-one correspondence between

\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)_{Q^{(d)}}:Q^{(d)}(u,v)>0,\penalty\ (v,N/d)=1\}

and

\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q}:Q(u,v)>0,\penalty\ (v,N)=d\}.

The next lemma is a congruence property for stabilizers of primitive forms.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
Q=[A,B,C]\in\mathcal{Q}_{D}^{0}
. Then every
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{Q}
satisfies
\beta\equiv 0\pmod{C}
and
\gamma\equiv 0\pmod{A}
.

Proof.

Let
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{Q}
. It is known that

\alpha=\frac{t-Bu}{2},\quad\beta=-Cu,\quad\gamma=Au\quad\text{and}\quad\delta=\frac{t+Bu}{2}

for some integers
t,u
satisfying
t^{2}-Du^{2}=4
(see
[
2
, pp. 31–32]
). In particular,

\beta=-Cu\equiv 0\pmod{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=Au\equiv 0\pmod{A}.

∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
d
be any positive divisor of
N
and let
Q=[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}
. If we put

Q^{(d)}=\left[\frac{N}{d}a,b,cd\right],

then one has

\Gamma_{0}(N/d)_{Q^{(d)}}=\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)_{Q^{(d)}},

and hence

(6.2)

[\Gamma_{0}(N/d)_{Q^{(d)}}:\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)_{Q^{(d)}}]=1.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\Gamma_{0}(N/d)_{Q^{(d)}}\subset\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d).

Let
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N/d)_{Q^{(d)}}
. Then
M\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{Q^{(d)}}
. By Lemma
6.4
,

\beta\equiv 0\pmod{cd}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\equiv 0\pmod{Na/d}.

This implies that

\beta\equiv 0\pmod{d}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\equiv 0\pmod{N/d}.

∎

In the following lemma, we give a factorization of
\mathcal{a}_{Q}
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
Q=[A,B,C]\in\mathcal{Q}_{D}^{0}
and set

\mathfrak{a}_{Q}:=\left(A,\frac{-B+\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}.

Assume that
d\mid A
and
\gcd(B,d)=1
. Put

\omega_{B}:=\frac{-B+\sqrt{D}}{2}.

Then

(6.3)

\mathfrak{a}_{Q}=(A,\omega_{B})=\left(\frac{A}{d},\omega_{B}\right)\cdot(d,\omega_{B}).

In particular,

(6.4)

\left[\left(\frac{A}{d},\omega_{B}\right)\right]=[\mathfrak{a}_{Q}]\cdot[(d,\omega_{B})]^{-1}\quad\text{in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{K}).

Proof.

Set

I:=\left(\frac{A}{d},\omega_{B}\right),\qquad J:=(d,\omega_{B}).

First, we show
IJ\subset(A,\omega_{B})
.
Indeed,
IJ
is generated by

\frac{A}{d}\cdot d=A,\qquad\frac{A}{d}\omega_{B},\qquad d\omega_{B},\qquad\omega_{B}^{2}.

Clearly
A\in(A,\omega_{B})
,
\frac{A}{d}\omega_{B}\in(A,\omega_{B})
, and
d\omega_{B}\in(A,\omega_{B})
.
Moreover, since
D=B^{2}-4AC
, one has

\omega_{B}^{2}+B\omega_{B}+AC=0,

hence
\omega_{B}^{2}\in(A,\omega_{B})
.
Therefore
IJ\subset(A,\omega_{B})
.

Next, we show
(A,\omega_{B})\subset IJ
.
Since
A=\frac{A}{d}\cdot d\in IJ
, it remains to show
\omega_{B}\in IJ
.
Choose integers
x,y
such that
xB+yd=1
.
Then

(6.5)

\omega_{B}=xB\omega_{B}+yd\,\omega_{B}.

Here
d\omega_{B}\in IJ
by definition.
Also, using
\omega_{B}^{2}+B\omega_{B}+AC=0
, we obtain

B\omega_{B}=-\omega_{B}^{2}-AC.

Since
\omega_{B}^{2}\in IJ
(because
\omega_{B}\in I
and
\omega_{B}\in J
) and
AC\in IJ
(because
A\in IJ
),
we have
B\omega_{B}\in IJ
.
Thus, the right-hand side of (
6.5
) lies in
IJ
, and hence
\omega_{B}\in IJ
.
Therefore
(A,\omega_{B})\subset IJ
.

Combining the two inclusions gives (
6.3
), and (
6.4
) follows immediately.
∎

With these auxiliary results in place, we now proceed to the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

From the proposition given in
[
5
, p. 505]
, we see that the inclusion map
\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{D}^{0}
induces a bijection

(6.6)

\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{Q}_{D}^{0}/\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

Since
D\equiv 1\pmod{4N}
, we have
D\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, and hence

\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\right].

We denote by
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
the narrow ideal class group of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
It is known that the map

Q=[A,B,C]\mapsto\mathfrak{a}_{Q}:=\left(A,\frac{-B+\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}

induces a one-to-one correspondence

\mathcal{Q}_{D}^{0}/\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{K}).

(see, for example,
[
4
]
). Composing this with (
6.6
) gives a map

\Theta:\ \mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{K}).

Now let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)
, and fix a representative
Q_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{C}
. Then
Q_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{D}^{0}
. We define the
(narrow) partial Dedekind zeta function

attached to the narrow ideal class
[\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}]\in\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
by

(6.7)

\zeta_{[\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}]}(s):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
[\mathfrak{b}]=[\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}]\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{b})^{-s},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>1,

where the sum ranges over the integral ideals in the narrow class of
\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}
.

By Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}

\displaystyle=e_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}

(6.8)

\displaystyle=e_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
\mathfrak{b}\sim_{+}\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{b})^{-s}=e_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\cdot\zeta_{[\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}]}(s).

For a positive divisor
d
of
N
, set

T_{d}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),\quad\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d):=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N/d):\beta\equiv 0\pmod{d}\right\}.

Define

Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}:=\left[\frac{N}{d}a,b,cd\right]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}.

Since
\Gamma_{0}(N/d)
acts on
\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}
and
\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)
is a subgroup of
\Gamma_{0}(N/d)
, we get an action of
\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)
by restriction, and so the stabilizer
\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}
makes sense. Moreover, the conjugation by
T_{d}
identifies this stabilizer with
\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}
:

T_{d}\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}T_{d}^{-1}=\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}.

Note that

Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,dv)=d\cdot Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}(u,v)

for all integers
u,v
. This, together with Lemma
6.3
, shows that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid N}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\\
(v,N)=d\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid N}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}(u,v)>0\\
(v,N/d)=1\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,dv)^{-s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}(u,v)>0\\
(v,N/d)=1\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}(u,v)^{-s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid N}\frac{d^{-s}}{[\Gamma_{0}(N/d)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}:\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N/d)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}(u,v)>0\\
(v,N/d)=1\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}(u,v)^{-s}.

Denoting by
\mathcal{C}^{(d)}
the
\Gamma_{0}(N/d)
-orbit of
Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}
, we obtain

(6.9)

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}=\sum_{d\mid N}\frac{d^{-s}}{[\Gamma_{0}(N/d)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}:\Gamma_{0}^{0}(N/d,d)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}]}\zeta_{\mathcal{C}^{(d)}}^{(N/d)}(s).

By Corollary
6.5
, (
6.9
) becomes

(6.10)

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}=\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-s}\zeta_{\mathcal{C}^{(d)}}^{(N/d)}(s).

Again, let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)
and fix
Q_{\mathcal{C}}=[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{C}
.
For
d\mid N
, set

\mathfrak{d}_{d}:=(d,\omega)\subset\mathcal{O}_{K},

where

\omega:=\frac{-1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}.

Since
b\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
and
d\mid N
, we have
b\equiv 1\pmod{2d}
. Thus,

\frac{-b+\sqrt{D}}{2}-\omega=\frac{1-b}{2}\in d\mathbb{Z},

and hence

(6.11)

(d,\tfrac{-b+\sqrt{D}}{2})=(d,\omega)=\mathfrak{d}_{d}.

Recall that

Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}:=\left[\frac{N}{d}a,b,cd\right]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1},\qquad\mathcal{C}^{(d)}:=Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}\cdot\Gamma_{0}(N/d)\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N/d).

Since
b\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
, we have
b\equiv 1\pmod{d}
and hence
\gcd(b,d)=1
.
Applying Lemma
6.6
with
A=Na
and
B=b
, and using (
6.11
), we obtain

(6.12)

[\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{(d)}}]=[\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}]\cdot[\mathfrak{d}_{d}]^{-1}\quad\text{in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{K}).

In particular, the assignment
\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathcal{C}^{(d)}
is a bijection

(6.13)

\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N/d),\qquad\mathcal{C}\longmapsto\mathcal{C}^{(d)},

since it corresponds, under
\Theta
, to multiplication by the fixed class
[\mathfrak{d}_{d}]^{-1}
.

Summing (
6.10
) over
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-s}\,\zeta_{\mathcal{C}^{(d)}}^{(N/d)}(s)

(6.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-s}\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\zeta_{\mathcal{C}^{(d)}}^{(N/d)}(s).

Finally, by the bijection (
6.13
), we may re-index the inner sum:

\sum_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)}\zeta_{\mathcal{C}^{(d)}}^{(N/d)}(s)=\sum_{\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N/d)}\zeta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(N/d)}(s)=:\zeta_{N/d,D,1}(s).

Substituting this into (
6.14
), we obtain the explicit linear combination

(6.15)

\sum_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}=\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-s}\,\zeta_{N/d,D,1}(s).

Recall from (
6.8
) that

e_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\zeta_{[\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}]}(s)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s},

where
e_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}=[\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\cap\Gamma_{0}(N)]=[\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}:\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}]
. By Lemma
6.4
,
e_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}=1
. It follows that

\zeta_{[\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}]}(s)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}.

By Lemma
6.1
and (
6.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta_{K}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\mathfrak{a}]\in\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}\zeta_{[\mathfrak{a}]}(s)=\sum_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)}\zeta_{[\mathfrak{a}_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}]}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N,D,1}/\Gamma_{0}(N)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}(N)_{Q_{\mathcal{C}}}\\
Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)>0\end{subarray}}Q_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v)^{-s}=\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-s}\,\zeta_{N/d,D,1}(s).

Similarly,

\zeta_{K}(s)=\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-s}\,\zeta_{N/d,D,-1}(s).

Hence,

(6.16)

\zeta_{K}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-k}(\zeta_{N/d,D,1}(k)+\zeta_{N/d,D,-1}(k)).

Next we fix
1\leq d\mid N
and analyze the quantity
\zeta_{N/d,D,1}(k)+\zeta_{N/d,D,-1}(k)
. By Lemma
5.1
, the form
f_{k,N/d,D,1}^{+}
lies in
S_{2k}^{+}(\Gamma_{0}(N/d))
. However, by assumption,
S_{2k}^{+}(\Gamma_{0}(N/d))=\{0\}
. Thus,
f_{k,N/d,D,1}^{+}=0
, and hence
r_{f_{k,N/d,D,1}^{+}}(I)(X)=0
. In particular, the leading coefficient of
r_{f_{k,N/d,D,1}^{+}}
vanishes. As mentioned in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, the even-degree terms of
r_{f_{k,N/d,D,1}^{+}}^{+}(I)(X)+r_{f_{k,N/d,D,1}^{-}}^{-}(I)(X)
coincide with those of
r_{f_{k,N/d,D,1}}(I)(X)
. Using Theorem
2.1
and the assumption that
k
is even, we have

\begin{split}0=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a>0>c\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{Na}{d}\right)^{k-1}-&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{Na}{d}\right)^{k-1}\\
&-\frac{d\pi}{N(2k-1)c_{k,D}}\cdot\frac{\zeta_{N/d,D,1}(k)+\zeta_{N/d,D,-1}(k)}{\zeta(2k)\prod_{p\mid(N/d)}(1-p^{-2k})}.\end{split}

It follows that

\displaystyle\zeta_{N/d,D,1}(k)+\zeta_{N/d,D,-1}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{N(2k-1)c_{k,D}\zeta(2k)}{d\pi}\prod_{p\mid(N/d)}(1-p^{-2k})

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a>0>c\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{Na}{d}\right)^{k-1}-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{Na}{d}\right)^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=d^{-k}N^{k}(2k-1)\binom{2k-2}{k-1}D^{1/2-k}\zeta(2k)\prod_{p\mid(N/d)}(1-p^{-2k})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a>0>c\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}\right).

Inserting this into (
6.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta_{K}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-k}\cdot d^{-k}N^{k}(2k-1)\binom{2k-2}{k-1}D^{1/2-k}\zeta(2k)\prod_{p\mid(N/d)}(1-p^{-2k})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a>0>c\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(2k-1)D^{1/2-k}\zeta(2k)N^{k}\binom{2k-2}{k-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-2k}\prod_{p\mid(N/d)}(1-p^{-2k})\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a>0>c\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}\right).

It remains to rewrite the inner sums over
\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}
for each positive divisor
d
of
N
. To this end, fix
d\mid N
and define the following three sets:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle:=\{[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}:a>0>c\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\{(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:b^{2}-4Nac/d=D,\penalty\ b\equiv 1\pmod{2N/d},\penalty\ a>0>c\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(b,a)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:|b|<\sqrt{D},\penalty\ b\equiv 1\pmod{2N/d},\penalty\ a\penalty\ \bigg|\penalty\ \frac{d(D-b^{2})}{4N}\right\}.

Then the map
\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
,
[Na/d,b,c]\mapsto(a,b,c)
is a bijection. Furthermore, the map

\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}
,
(a,b,c)\mapsto(b,a)
is also a bijection. Consequently,

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a>0>c\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{D}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2N/d}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\big|\frac{d(D-b^{2})}{4N}\\
a>0\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{D}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2N/d}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{k-1}\left(\frac{d(D-b^{2})}{4N}\right).

A similar argument shows that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[Na/d,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{N/d,D,1}\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{D}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2N/d}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\big|\frac{d(D-b^{2})}{4N}\\
a<0\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{D}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2N/d}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\big|\frac{d(D-b^{2})}{4N}\\
a>0\end{subarray}}a^{k-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{D}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2N/d}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{k-1}\left(\frac{d(D-b^{2})}{4N}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\zeta_{K}(k)

\displaystyle=(2k-1)D^{1/2-k}\zeta(2k)N^{k}\binom{2k-2}{k-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{d\mid N}d^{-2k}\prod_{p\mid(N/d)}(1-p^{-2k})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|b|<\sqrt{D}\\
b\equiv 1\pmod{2N/d}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{k-1}\left(\frac{d(D-b^{2})}{4N}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎
