Baghdad Believes Ground War Has Begun

Baghdad radio today carried a statement indicating that the
ground battle has begun. Additional statements reported that
heavy attacks by coalition forces were underway in southern
Kuwait against the 7th and 14th Infantry Divisions--identified by
their honorifics as the Al-~Mansur and Al-Hamza forces, Baghdad
radio admitted that some Iraqi units were compelled to retreat.

Comment: Baghdad probably is responding to probing attacks,
intensified artillery raids, and reconnaissance in force

operations that coalition forces have undertaken over the past
few days. YS

Moscow Says Iraq Agrees to "New, Improved" Peace Plan

During a hastily called news conference this afternoon,
Gorbachev spokesman Ignatenko said Iraqi Foreign Minister Aziz
had agreed to several new provisions of the Soviet peace
proposal. Moscow is awaiting approval of the provisions from
Baghdad and then will send it to the UN. According to the new,
six-point plan:

o Iraq would implement UN Resolution 660, calling for an
immediate withdrawal from Kuwait, without delay and
without conditions.

© The withdrawal would begin one day after a ceasefire.

o The withdrawal would be completed 21 days after the
ceasefire.

o After withdrawal, all other Security Council resolutions

referring to the Gulf crisis would “lose their meaning and
be rescinded."

o Prisoners of war would be released within 72 hours after
the ceasefire begins.

o Supervision of the withdrawal would be conducted by a
peacekeeping force determined by the UN Security Council.

Comment: Saddam is looking for a way to get negotiations started
and to avoid a ground war, but he is not yet prepared to accept
US terms. He has made no major concession, and his latest
proposal retains his key demands to remove sanctions, the arms

embargo, and the threat of reparations. He
