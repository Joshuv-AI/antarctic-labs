A Decay Estimate For The Stability Operator Of The Helicoid
Stephen J. Kleene

Abstract
. We consider the Poisson Problem for the stability operator of the helicoid on a vertical strip, under the assumption that the source term is supported on a strip of fixed height. We prove that solutions decay at a definite rate away from the support assuming natural orthogonality conditions on the source term.



1.
introduction

In this article we consider the Poisson problem

(1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}u=E

for the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}+2\cosh^{-2}(s)
on domains of the form
R=[-\ell,\ell]\times\mathbb{R}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where we take
(s,z)
as coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and where the source term
E
is supported on the strip
[-\ell,\ell]\times[-2\pi,2\pi]
. The operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is, up to the conformal factor
\cosh^{2}(s)
, the stability operator of the helicoid in its standard conformal parametrization
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
given by

(2)

\displaystyle F(s,z)=\sinh(s)\mathbf{e}_{r}(z)+z\mathbf{e}_{z},\quad\mathbf{e}_{r}(z)=\cos(z)\mathbf{e}_{x}+\sin(z)\mathbf{e}_{y},

and problem (
1
) arises naturally in many geometric problems involving both the helicoid and the catenoid. In applications (such has
[
BK
]
,
[
BK2
]
), one frequently has symmetry assumptions which reduce the domain of (
1
) to long cylinders
[-\ell,\ell]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and where the invertibility properties of the operator
\mathcal{L}
are well understood. However, in many interesting problems one wishes to relax these assumptions, in which case one hopes symmetry can be replaced by decay estimates. This article records such a decay estimate for source terms
E
satisfying certain strong orthogonality conditions arising from the lower spectrum of the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
. Precisely, we assume that the source term
E
satisifies

(3)

\displaystyle\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}E(s,z)\cosh^{-1}(s)ds=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}E(s,z)\tanh(s)ds,\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

Our main theorem is then:

Theorem 1
.

For any
\xi\in(0,1/2)
, there is a constant
C=C(\xi)
such that: Given a
C^{0,\alpha}
function
E(s,z):[-\ell,\ell]\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
supported on the strip
[-\ell,\ell]\times[-2\pi,2\pi]
with
\|E\|_{0,\alpha}\leq\beta
and satisfying the strong orthogonality conditions (
3
), there is a unique locally
C^{2,\alpha}
function
u:[-\ell,\ell]\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
solving (
1
) and satisfying the orthogonality conditions (
3
) and such that:

(1)

The odd part
u^{-}(s,z):=\frac{1}{2}\left(u(s,z)-u(-s,z)\right)
of
u
satisfies the boundary condition

u^{-}_{,s}(\ell,z)\tanh(\ell)-u^{-}(\ell,z)\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)=0,\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}.

(2)

The even part
u^{+}(s,z):=\frac{1}{2}\left(u(s,z)+u(-s,z)\right)
of
u
satisfies the boundary condition

u^{+}_{,s}(\ell,z)\cosh^{-1}(\ell)-u^{+}(\ell,z)\left(\cosh^{-1}\right)^{\prime}(\ell)=0,\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover,
u
satisfies the weighted estimate

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{2,\alpha}\leq C\ell^{5/2}\beta\left(\frac{1}{1+|z|}\right)^{\xi}.

Above,
\|u\|_{k,\alpha}
denotes the localized
C^{k,\alpha}
holder norm for a function
u:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, so

\|u\|_{k,\alpha}(p):=\|u:C^{k,\alpha}(\Lambda\cap D_{p})\|

where
D_{p}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
centered at
p
.

1.1.
Structure of the proof of Theorem
1

The basic strategy for proving Theorem
1
is to search for solutions
u_{N}
on domains
[-\ell,\ell]\times[-N,N]
with appropriate boundary values, and to prove uniform integral estimates for
u_{N}
in
N
, which can then be improved to decay estimates. For fixed large
N
and
\ell
it can be shown that the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
has trivial dirichlet kernel on
[-\ell,\ell]\times[-N,N]
, however the gap at
0
decays exponentially in
\ell
. Thus, though solutions to (
1
) with Dirichlet boundary conditions on
[-\ell,\ell]\times[-N,N]
exist without assuming any orthogonality on the source term
E
, their bounds are poorly behaved in
\ell
. The bounded kernel of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is spanned by the functions
\tanh(s)
,
\cos(z)\cosh^{-1}(s)
, and
\sin(z)\cosh^{-1}(s)
, and these functions are the principal obstructions to obtaining uniform integral control of the solutions. The Poisson problem (
1
) can be solved with boundary conditions (
1
) and (
2
) in the Statement of Theorem
1
on the odd and even parts of
u_{N}
, respectively–as well as Neumann condition along the boundary component
z=\pm N
–and the strong orthogonality condition (
3
) on the source term is inherited by the solutions
u_{N}
. We can then apply a one-dimensional weighted Poincare Inequality to obtain
L^{2}
and this
C^{2,\alpha}
control on the solutions
u_{N}
, which thus converge on compact subsets of the strip
[-\ell,\ell]\times\mathbb{R}
to a limiting function
u
solving (
1
). The Poincare Inequality then implies convexity of the integrals
\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}u^{2}ds
which, combined with the uniform integral control implies decay. Schauder estimates and a bootstrapping argument then improves the integral decay to pointwise decay. We Remark that the arguments presented in this paper can likely be generalized to operators other than stability of the Helicoid, however we have not pursued this here.

Apriori estimates for solutions: Proposition

Proposition 1
.

There is a universal constant
C
so that: Given a function
u(s,z):[-\ell,\ell]\times[-N,N]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

The odd part
u^{-}
of

\displaystyle\partial_{s}u^{-}(\ell,z)\tanh(\ell)-u^{-}_{N}(\ell,z)/\cosh^{2}(\ell)=0.

(2)

The even part
u^{+}
of

satisfies the Robin boundary condition

\displaystyle\partial_{s}u^{+}(\ell,z)\cosh^{-1}(\ell)-u^{+}(\ell,z)\left(\cosh^{-1}\right)^{\prime}(\ell)=0.

\displaystyle\partial_{z}u(s,\pm N)=0.

\displaystyle\sup|u_{N}|,\quad\|u_{N}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\ell^{2}\left(\|\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{L^{2}}\right)

Definition 1
.

For an odd function
f
belonging to the space
W^{1,2}([-\ell,\ell])
we set

\displaystyle e^{-}_{\ell}(f):=\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds-f^{2}(\ell)\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)/\tanh(\ell).

Proposition 2

(One dimensional weighted Poincare Inequality, odd case)
.

There is a universal constant
P>0
independent of
\ell
so that: Let
f\in W^{1,2}([-\ell,\ell])
be an odd function satisfying the orthogonality condition:

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}f(s)\tanh(s)/\cosh^{2}(s)ds=0.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle e^{-}_{\ell}(f)

\displaystyle\geq P\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds

Before proving Proposition
2
, we characterize the bottom spectrum of the operator
\widetilde{L}=\partial_{ss}+2\cosh^{-2}(s)
.

Lemma 1

(Lower weighted eigenspace for
\widetilde{L}
)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a solution to the weighted eigenvalue problem for
\widetilde{L}
below:

\widetilde{L}\phi=\lambda\cosh^{-2}(s)\phi.

Assume that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi^{2}\cosh^{-2}(s)ds+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\phi^{\prime}(s)\right)^{2}ds<\infty

and
\lambda\leq 0
. Then
\lambda=-2
, in which case
\phi
is constant, or else
\lambda=0
and
\phi
is a multiple of
\tanh(s)
.

Proof.

The Gauss map
N:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
of the immersion
F
defined in (
2
) is (anti) conformal with conformal factor
\cosh^{-2}(s)
. Thus solutions to the weighted eigenvalue problem in the statement of the lemma correspond to rotationally invariant (about the
z
-axis)
W^{1,2}
solutions to

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi+2\phi=\lambda\phi

on the sphere. It is well-known that the eigenvalues of the laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is
\lambda_{k}=k(k+1)
with multiplicity
2k+1
. The eigenspace corresponding to
\lambda_{0}=0
is spanned by constants, while the eigenspace
\lambda_{1}=2
is spanned by the coordinate functions,
x,y,z
. The functions in these spaces that survive the rotational symmetry condition are
z
and
1
. Since the pullback
N^{*}z
of
z
under
N
is
\tanh(s)
, this completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Observe first that
e^{-}_{\ell}(f)
is bounded below for each
\ell
on
L^{2}
normalized functions: We have

f^{2}(\ell)=\left(\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime}(s)ds\right)^{2}\leq\ell\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds

and thus,

(5)

\displaystyle e^{-}_{\ell}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds-f^{2}(\ell)\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)/\tanh(\ell)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds-\ell\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)/\tanh(\ell)\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\left(1-\ell\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)/\tanh(\ell)\right)\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds.

Now, for each fixed
\ell
, we consider a minimizing sequence
f_{j}
for
e^{-}_{\ell}
with

\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}_{j}\cosh^{-2}(s)ds=1.

By (
5
), the sequence is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,2}\left([0,\ell]\right)
, and thus, after passing to a subsequence, we can assume that
f_{j}
strongly converges in
L^{2}
and weakly in
W^{1,2}
, to a limiting function
\phi_{\ell}
in
W^{1,2}([0,\ell])
. The function
\phi_{\ell}
is then a smooth solution to

(6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}\phi_{\ell}=\lambda_{\ell}\cosh^{-2}(s)\phi_{\ell},\quad\lambda_{\ell}=\inf_{f\in W^{1,2}[0,\ell]}e^{-}_{\ell}(f)

and satisfies the orthogonality condition

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi_{\ell}(s)\tanh(s)/\cosh^{2}(s)ds=0.

We now consider a sequence
\ell_{k}\rightarrow\infty
and set

\lambda:=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{k}

where
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{\ell_{k}}
. Let
\phi_{k}
denote the corresponding lowest eigenfunction
\phi_{k}
satisfying (
6
) with
\ell=\ell_{k}
, and assume that
\phi_{k}
is normalized so that

\int_{0}^{\ell_{k}}\phi^{2}_{k}\cosh^{-2}(s)ds=1.

The uniform energy bound on the sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}
gives that

(8)

\displaystyle|\phi_{k}(s)|\leq Cs^{1/2}

and thus the sequence
\phi_{k}
converges on compact subsets of
[0,\infty]
to a smooth limit
f
satisfying

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}f=\lambda\cosh^{-2}(s)f.

as well as the orthogonality condition (
7
) with
\ell=\infty
. Moreover, the uniform bound (
8
) and the dominated convergence therorem gives

\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds=1.

By Lemma
1
,
\lambda>0
since
f
is odd and satisfies (
7
). This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 2
.

For an even function
f
belonging to the space
W^{1,2}([-\ell,\ell])
we set

\displaystyle e^{+}_{\ell}(f)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds-f^{2}(\ell)(\cosh^{-1})^{\prime}(\ell)/\cosh^{-1}(\ell)

(9)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds+f^{2}(\ell)\tanh(\ell).

Proposition 3

(One dimensional weighted Poincare Inequality, even case)
.

There is a universal constant
P>0
independent of
\ell
so that: Let
f\in W^{1,2}([-\ell,\ell])
be an even function satisfying the orthogonality condition:

(10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}f(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds=0.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle e^{+}_{\ell}(f)

\displaystyle\geq P\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds.

Proof.

The proof of the Poincare inequality in the even case is essentially the same as in the odd case: As before we consider a minimizing sequence
f_{j}
for the energy
e^{+}_{\ell}(-)
. Although in this case, the boundary term of the energy integral is too large to be absorbed as in the odd case, it appears with the beneficial sign so that,

e^{+}(f)=\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds+f^{2}(\ell)\tanh(\ell)\geq-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds,

and thus as before
e^{+}(f)
is uniformly bounded below. Moreover, since
f_{j}
is a minimizing sequence, we have
e^{+}_{\ell}(f_{j})\leq K
for a fixed constant
K
and thus

\int_{0}^{\ell}f_{j}^{\prime 2}(s)ds\leq K+\int_{0}^{\ell}f_{j}^{2}(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds-f_{j}^{2}(\ell)\tanh(\ell)\leq K+2

where again we can discard the boundary term due to the beneficial sign. Thus, as before the sequence
f_{j}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,2}([0,\ell])
and thus weakly converges to a limit
\phi_{\ell}
satisfying the eigenvalue problem. Arguing as before we take a sequence
\ell_{k}\rightarrow\infty
and consider the corresponding sequence of eigenfunctions
\phi_{k}=\phi_{\ell_{k}}
. Passing to a subsequence gives smooth convergence on compact subsets of
[0,\ell]
to a normalized limit
\phi
satisfying

\widetilde{L}\phi=\lambda\cosh^{-2}(s)\phi,\quad\lambda=\liminf_{k}\lambda_{k}.

By Lemma
1
,
\lambda
must be positive since
\phi
is even–and thus not
\tanh(s)
–corresponding to
\lambda=0
–and satisfies the orthogonality condition

\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi\cosh^{-2}(s)ds=0

so that
\phi
cannot be a constant–corresponding to
\lambda=-2
.
∎

Definition 3
.

Given a function
f\in W^{1,2}([-\ell,\ell])
we set

e_{\ell}(f)=e^{-}_{\ell}(f^{-})+e^{+}_{\ell}(f^{+}),

where
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
denote the even and odd parts of
f
, respectively.

Proposition 4
.

Let
f\in W^{1,2}([-\ell,\ell])
be a function satisfying the orthogonality conditions

\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f(s)\tanh(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds=0.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2}{2+P}\right)\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)\leq 4e_{\ell}(f).

Proof.

The inequality in Proposition
2
can be written explicitly as:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{-})^{\prime 2}(s)ds-\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{-})^{2}(2+P)\cosh^{-2}(s)-(f^{-})^{2}(\ell)\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)/\tanh(\ell)\geq 0.

Equivalently:

(11)

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2}{2+P}\right)\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{-})^{\prime 2}(s)ds+\left(\frac{2}{2+P}-1\right)(f^{-})^{2}(\ell)\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)/\tanh(\ell)\leq e^{-}(f^{-}).

Since
(f^{-})^{2}(\ell)\leq\ell\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{-})^{\prime 2}(s)ds
we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2}{2+P}\right)\left(1-\ell\frac{\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)}{\tanh(\ell)}\right)\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{-})^{\prime 2}(s)ds\leq e^{-}(f^{-}).

Similarly, the inequality of Proposition
3
can be written

\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{+})^{\prime 2}(s)ds-\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{+})^{2}(2+P)\cosh^{-2}(s)-(f^{+})^{2}(\ell)(\cosh^{-1})^{\prime}(\ell)/\cosh^{-1}(\ell)\geq 0,

giving

(12)

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2}{2+P}\right)\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{+})^{\prime 2}(s)ds\leq e^{+}(f^{+}).

Summing (
11
) and (
12
) and assuming
\ell
is sufficiently large so that
\ell\frac{\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)}{\tanh(\ell)}<1/2
gives

\left(1-\frac{2}{2+P}\right)\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{+})^{\prime 2}(s)ds+\left(1-\frac{2}{2+P}\right)\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{-})^{\prime 2}(s)\leq 2e_{\ell}(f).

It is easy to verify that

\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{+})^{\prime 2}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{\ell}(f^{-})^{\prime 2}(s)ds=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f^{\prime 2}(s)ds,

from which the claim directly follows.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
f(s)
be a function in
L^{2}([-\ell,\ell])
satisfying the orthogonality conditions

\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f(s)\tanh(s)ds=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f(s)\cosh^{-1}(s)ds=0.

Then we can write

(13)

\displaystyle f(s)=\alpha\tanh(s)+\gamma\cosh^{-1}(s)+g(s)

where
g
satisfies the orthogonality conditions

\displaystyle\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}g(s)\tanh(s)/\cosh^{2}(s)=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}g(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)=0,

as well as the lower bound

\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{2}(s)ds\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}g^{2}(s)ds.

Proof.

Write

\displaystyle f=\alpha\tanh(s)+\gamma\cosh^{-1}(s)+(f-\alpha\tanh(s)-\gamma\cosh^{-1}(s)):=\alpha\tanh(s)+\gamma\cosh^{-1}(s)+g

Then choosing
\alpha
appropriately, it is clear that we can arrange for
g
to be orthogonal to
\tanh(s)/\cosh^{2}(s)
and
\cosh^{-2}(s)
. In particular, we choose
\alpha
so that

(14)

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f(s)\tanh(s)/\cosh^{2}(s)ds}{\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\tanh^{2}(s)/\cosh^{2}(s)ds},\quad\gamma=\frac{\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds}{\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\cosh^{-3}(s)ds}.

Then, the orthogonality conditions on
f
and Cauchy-Schwartz give

\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f^{2}ds=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}fg\leq\left(\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}f^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}g^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}.

∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
f
and
g
be as in the statement of Lemma
2
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle e_{\ell}(f)\geq e_{\ell}(g)\geq\left(1-\frac{2}{2+P}\right)\left(\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}g^{\prime 2}(s)ds\right).

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle e^{-}_{\ell}(f^{-})=B^{-}[f^{-},f^{-}]

for the bilinear form

B^{-}[f,g]:=\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime}(s)g^{\prime}(s)ds-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f(s)g(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds-f(\ell)g(\ell)\tanh^{\prime}(\ell)/\tanh(\ell).

Since
f^{-}=g^{-}+\alpha\tanh(s)
, we then have

\displaystyle e^{-}_{\ell}(f^{-})

\displaystyle=B^{-}[\alpha\tanh(s)+g^{-},\alpha\tanh(s)+g^{-}]

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}B^{-}[\tanh(s),\tanh(s)]+B[g^{-},g^{-}]+2\alpha B[g^{-},\tanh(s)]

\displaystyle=B[g^{-},g^{-}]

\displaystyle=e^{-}_{\ell}(g^{-}).

where the last equality above follows from the definition of
e^{-}_{\ell}(-)
and
B^{-}[-,-]
and where the third line follows from the second since
\tanh(s)
is in the kernel of
\tilde{L}
with the boundary conditions (
1
) and (
3
). A similar argument applies to the even part of
f
using the bilinear form

B^{+}[f,g]:=\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{\prime}(s)g^{\prime}(s)ds-2\int_{0}^{\ell}f(s)g(s)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds+f(\ell)g(\ell)\tanh(\ell).

Since
\widetilde{L}\cosh^{-1}(s)=\cosh^{-1}(s)
, we have

\displaystyle e_{\ell}^{+}(g^{+})

\displaystyle=B^{+}[g^{+},g^{+}]

\displaystyle=B^{+}[f^{+}+\gamma\cosh^{-1}(s),f^{+}+\gamma\cosh^{-1}(s)]

\displaystyle=B^{+}[f^{+},f^{+}]+2\gamma B^{+}[f^{+},\cosh^{-1}(s)]+\gamma^{2}B^{+}[\cosh^{-1}(s),\cosh^{-1}(s)]

\displaystyle=e_{\ell}^{+}(f^{+})-2\gamma\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{+}\widetilde{L}\cosh^{-1}(s)ds-\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}\cosh^{-1}(s)\widetilde{L}\cosh^{-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=e_{\ell}^{+}(f^{+})-2\gamma\int_{0}^{\ell}f^{+}\cosh^{-1}(s)ds-\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}\cosh^{-2}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq e_{\ell}^{+}(f^{+})

where above we have used the orthogonality of
f
to
\cosh^{-1}(s)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1
.

In the following, in order to conveniently reference Proposition
2
, Proposition
3
, Proposition
4
and Lemma
3
while using their notational conventions, we set

\displaystyle f(s,z)=u(s,z)=\alpha(z)\tanh(s)+\gamma(z)\cosh^{-1}(s)+g(s,z),

where the multiples
\alpha
and
\gamma
are chosen so that

\displaystyle\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}g(s,z)\tanh(s)/\cosh^{2}(s)ds=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}g(s,z)\cosh^{-2}(s)ds=0.

Additionally, we will throughout the proof abbreviate
R:=[-\ell,\ell]\times[-N,N]
. Let
f^{-}
and
f^{+}
denote the odd and even parts

, respectively, and similarly for
g
, so

f^{+}=\gamma\cosh^{-1}(s)+g^{+},\quad f^{-}=\gamma\cosh^{-1}(s)+g^{-}.

Since the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
preserves odd and even functions, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{R}f\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}f

\displaystyle=-\int_{R}(f^{+}+f^{-})\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(f^{+}+f^{-})

\displaystyle=-\int_{R}(f^{+})\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(f^{+})-\int_{R}(f^{-})\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(f^{-})

\displaystyle=-\int_{R}\left\{f^{+}\left(f^{+}\right)_{,zz}+f^{+}\widetilde{L}f^{+}\right\}-\int_{R}\left\{f^{-}\left(f^{-}\right)_{,zz}+f^{-}\widetilde{L}f^{-}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{R}\left(f^{+}_{z}\right)^{2}+2\int_{-N}^{N}e^{+}_{\ell}(f^{+})dz+2\int_{R}\left(f^{-}_{z}\right)^{2}+\int_{-N}^{N}e^{-}_{\ell}(f^{-})dz

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{-N}^{N}e^{+}_{\ell}(f^{+})dz+2\int_{-N}^{N}e^{-}_{\ell}(f^{-})dz

\displaystyle=2\int_{-N}^{N}e_{\ell}(f)dz.

Using Lemma
3
and the Cauchy Schwartz inequality we then have:

\displaystyle\int_{R}\left(g_{,s}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{-N}^{N}\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\left(g_{,s}\right)^{2}dsdz

\displaystyle\leq^{C}\int_{-N}^{N}e_{\ell}(g)dz

\displaystyle\leq^{C}\int_{-N}^{N}e_{\ell}(f)dz

\displaystyle\leq^{C}-\int_{R}f\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}f

\displaystyle\leq^{C}\left(\int_{R}f^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{R}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}f\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Applying the flat Poincare inequality for the interval
[0,l]
and Lemma
2
we have

\int_{R}f^{2}\leq\int_{R}g^{2}\leq\ell^{2}\int_{R}\left(g_{,s}\right)^{2}\leq^{C}\ell^{2}\left(\int_{R}f^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{R}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}f\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

This establishes the
L^{2}
estimates asserted in the proposition. To establish the uniform estimate on the supremum of
u
, pick a point
p_{0}=(s_{0},z_{0})
where the supremum of
u
is achieved and set
\overline{u}=|u(p_{0})|
. Let
r
denote the maximal radius such that
u
restricted to the ball
B_{r}(p_{0})
is at least
\frac{\overline{u}}{2}
. Schauder theory (see Remark
1
) then gives the bound
\|u\|_{2,\alpha}(p_{0})\leq C\overline{u}
on the localized holder norms, which then gives a uniform lower bound on
r
. To see this, pick
p\in B_{r}(p_{0})
such that
|u_{N}(p)|=\overline{u}/2
. Then

\frac{\overline{u}}{2}=\left|u(p_{0})-u(p)\right|\leq\sup_{B_{r}(p_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right||p-p_{0}|\leq^{C}r\overline{u}.

We then have

\ell^{4}\left(\left\|\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}u\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}\geq\int_{R}f^{2}\geq\int_{B_{r}(p_{0})\cap R}f^{2}\geq cr^{2}\overline{u}^{2}\geq c\overline{u}^{2},

or

\sup_{R}|u|\leq^{C}\ell^{2}\left\|\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}u\right\|_{L^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Solvability of the Poisson problem for strongly orthogonal source terms

We begin by characterizing the kernel of the stability operator on the rectangles
[-\ell,\ell]\times[-N,N]
with the imposed boundary conditions.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\phi:[-\ell,\ell]\times[-N,N]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an odd function satisfying:

(1)

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\phi=0
.

(2)

\partial_{s}\phi(\ell,z)\tanh(\ell)-\phi(\ell,z)/\cosh^{2}(\ell)=0
.

(3)

\partial_{z}\phi(s,\pm N)=0
.

Then
\phi
is a multiple of
\tanh(s)
.

Proof.

For
\phi
as in the hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi\tanh(s)ds\right)^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi_{,zz}\tanh(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\ell}\widetilde{L}\phi\tanh(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi\widetilde{L}\tanh(s)ds-\left.\phi^{\prime}\tanh(s)\right|_{0}^{\ell}+\left.\phi\tanh^{\prime}(s)\right|_{0}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=0.

where the last line follows from the fact that
\widetilde{L}\tanh(s)=0
and the imposed boundary values on
\phi
. Thus the integral
\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi\tanh(s)ds
is affine and the imposed boundary conditions at
z=\pm N
give that is constant. Thus, there is a constant
\alpha
such that
\widetilde{\phi}=\phi-\alpha\tanh(s)
satisfies
\widetilde{L}\widetilde{\phi}=0
and

\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi\tanh(s)ds=0,\quad\forall z.

Applying Proposition
1
with
E
= 0 then gives
\widetilde{\phi}\equiv 0
and gives the claim.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Let
\phi:[-\ell,\ell]\times[-N,N]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an even function satisfying:

(1)

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\phi=0
.

(2)

\partial_{s}\phi(\ell,z)\cosh^{-1}(\ell)-\phi(\ell,z)\partial_{s}\left(\cosh^{-1}\right)(\ell)=0
.

(3)

\partial_{z}\phi(s,\pm N)=0
.

Then
\phi
is in the span of
\sin(z)\cosh^{-1}(s)
and
\cos(z)\cosh^{-1}(s)
.

Proof.

Consider
\phi
as in the hypothesis and set

d=d(z)=\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi(s,z)\cosh^{-1}(s)ds.

Then

\displaystyle d^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi(s,z)\cosh^{-1}(s)ds\right)^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi_{,zz}(s,z)\cosh^{-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\ell}\widetilde{L}\phi\cosh^{-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi\widetilde{L}\cosh^{-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\ell}\phi\cosh^{-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-d.

Thus after adding a linear combination of
\sin(z)\cosh^{-1}(s)
and
\cos(z)\cosh^{-1}(z)
we obtain a function
\widetilde{\phi}
satisfying
\widetilde{L}\widetilde{\phi}=0
and

\int_{0}^{\ell}\widetilde{\phi}(s,z)\cosh^{-1}(s)ds=0.

Applying Proposition
1
with
E=0
gives that
\sup\widetilde{\phi}=0
, from which it follows that
\phi
is in the span of
\sin(z)\cosh^{-1}(s)
and
\cos(z)\cosh^{-1}(z)
.
∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
E(s,z)
be a class
C^{0,\alpha}
function supported on
[-\ell,\ell]\times[-2\pi,2\pi]
and satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}E(s,z)\tanh(s)ds=\int_{0}^{\ell}E(s,z)\cosh^{-1}(s)ds=0.

Then, given
N>2\pi
there is locally
C^{2,\alpha}
function
u_{N}(s,z):[-\ell,\ell]\times[-N,N]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) and (
4
) in the statement of Proposition
1
as well as the bounds

\sup\left|u_{N}\right|,\quad\left\|u_{N}:L^{2}\right\|\leq^{C}\ell^{5/2}\left(\sup\|E\|_{0,\alpha}\right).

Proof.

The existence of weak solutions and their higher regularity follows from standard theory (see
[
GT
]
Theorem 6.30 for regularity in the setting of the oblique derivative problem, and
[
Ev
]
, Chapter 6 for existence of weak solutions), and we need only show that the solutions
u_{N}
recover the strong orthogonality conditions (
3
). First consider the case that
u_{N}
is odd. Then strong orthogonality to
\cosh^{-1}(s)
is trivially satisfied. For the strong orthogonality to
\tanh(s)
, we have:

(15)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{zz}u_{N}+\partial^{2}_{ss}u_{N}+2\cosh^{-2}(s)u_{N}=\partial^{2}_{zz}u_{N}+\tilde{L}u_{N}=E,

where the operator
\widetilde{L}
is defined implicitly above. Multiplying both sides by
\tanh(s)
, integrating by parts and using the Robin boundary condition then gives

\displaystyle\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\left(\partial^{2}_{zz}u_{N}\right)(s,z)\tanh(s)ds=\partial^{2}_{zz}\left(\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}u_{N}(s,z)\tanh(s)ds\right)=0.

The boundary conditions then immediately imply
\left(\int_{0}^{\ell}u_{N}(s,z)\tanh(s)ds\right)
is constant in
z
. The conclusion then follows by adding a multiple of
\tanh(s)
to
u_{N}
if necessary.

The case that
u_{N}
is even is similar: The first orthogonality condition is again trivially satisfied since
\tanh(s)
is odd. Multiplying (
15
) by
\cosh^{-1}(s)
, using the imposed boundary conditions on the even part gives

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{zz}\left(\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}u_{N}(s,z)\cosh^{-1}(s)ds\right)+\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}u_{N}\cosh^{-1}(s)ds=0.

Thus, we have

\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}u_{N}\cosh^{-1}(s)ds=a\sin(z)+b\cos(z)

for constants
a
and
b
. Again, modifying
u_{N}
by adding multiples of the
\sin(z)\cosh^{-1}(s)
and
\cos(z)\cosh^{-1}(s)
, which lie in the kernel of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
we have that

\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}u(s,z)\cosh^{-1}(s)ds=0,\quad\forall z

We can now apply Proposition
1
to get

\displaystyle\sup\left|u_{N}\right|,\quad\left\|u_{N}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq^{C}\ell^{2}\left\|\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}u\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{2}\left\|E\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{5/2}\left(\sup\left\|E\right\|_{0,\alpha}\right)

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
E
is supported on
[-\ell,\ell]\times[-2\pi,2\pi]
.
∎
