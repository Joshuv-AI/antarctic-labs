Infinite-order multisoliton solutions to the Benjamin–Ono equation and soliton resolution
Louise Gassot, Patrick Gérard

Abstract
. We construct a class of infinite-order multisoliton solutions of the Benjamin-Ono equation on the line, for which the initial data exhibits slow spatial decay. We prove that in the long-time asymptotics, such a solution decouples as an infinite superposition of independent soliton solutions with different velocities and no radiation term. Dedicated to Yoshio Tsutsumi, on the occasion of his seventieth birthday



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
37K10, 35B40, 35Q51

1.
Introduction and main result

This paper is devoted to the long-time behavior of solutions of the Benjamin–Ono equation,

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}|D_{x}|u+\partial_{x}(u^{2})=0\ ,\ u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).

The function
u=u(t,x)
is real–valued, and
|D_{x}|
is the Fourier multiplier associated to the symbol
|\xi|
acting on functions on the real line. Equation (
1.1
) was introduced in 1967
[
3
]
in order to model long, unidirectional internal gravity waves
in a two-layer fluid with infinite depth, see e.g. the book
[
8
]
for more detail and
[
10
]
for a recent derivation.

We recall that an important class of solutions of the Benjamin-Ono equation (
1.1
) are the traveling wave solutions,

u(t,x)=u_{0}(x-ct).

These solutions were characterized in
[
1
]
, and have the form

(1.2)

u(t,x)=R_{p}(x-c_{p}t),\quad R_{p}(x)=\frac{2\mathrm{Im}(p)}{|x+p|^{2}},\quad c_{p}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}(p)},

where
p\in\mathbb{C}_{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\;\colon\;\mathrm{Im}(z)>0\}
is a complex number with positive imaginary part. Note that the
L^{2}
norm of such a solution is given by

\|R_{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=4\pi c_{p}.

In this paper, these solutions will be called soliton solutions.

It has been recently proved in
[
5
]
that, if the initial data
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is sufficiently decaying at infinity, the solution
u(t,\cdot)
can be written as
t\to\infty
as a finite superposition of soliton solutions with different velocities and a radiative remainder term. In this setting, the number of solitons involved in this soliton resolution is shown to be finite thanks to the sufficient spatial decay of the initial data, in particular
xu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. This includes the special case of multisoliton solutions, corresponding to initial data which are finite sums of functions
R_{p_{j}}
, and which was studied before in
[
9
]
and revisited in
[
11
]
. In the latter case, the radiative remainder term is going to
0
in any Sobolev space as
t\to\infty
.

The goal of this paper is to investigate a similar long-time asymptotics for a family of initial data for which the number of solitons is infinite. In particular, these initial data will satisfy
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
but
xu_{0}\not\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 1.1

(Infinite-order multisoliton)
.

We fix a sequence
(p_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of points in the upper half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. We say that
u_{0}
is an infinite-order multisoliton in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if
u_{0}
is given by the formula

(1.3)

u_{0}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\mathrm{Im}(p_{j})}{|x+p_{j}|^{2}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

and if the parameters
(p_{j})_{j\geq 1}
satisfy the condition

(1.4)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Im}(p_{j})}{|p_{j}-\overline{p_{k}}|^{2}}<\infty.

Our main result states that an infinite-order multisoliton in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
can be written as a superposition of solitons with different velocities in the long-time asymptotics, up to a small remainder term if the number of solitons is taken large enough.

Theorem 1.2

(Soliton resolution for the infinite-order multisoliton)
.

The initial data
u_{0}
given by (
1.3
) under the condition (
1.4
) belong to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, there is a sequence
(p_{j}^{\infty})_{j\geq 1}
of complex numbers in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
with increasing imaginary parts
0<\mathrm{Im}(p_{1}^{\infty})<\mathrm{Im}(p_{2}^{\infty})<\dots
, such that there holds

(1.5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\Big\|u(t,\cdot)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}\left(\cdot-c_{p_{k}^{\infty}}t\right)\Big\|_{L^{2}}=0.

Furthermore, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the formula

(1.6)

u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,x):=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}(x-c_{p_{j}^{\infty}}t),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

defines a continuous function
u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}
, tending to
0
as
x\to\infty
, and belonging to all the homogeneous Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\geq 1/2
. Finally, we have, for every
s\geq 1/2
,

(1.7)

\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}0.

The above statement calls for several comments. Firstly, the soliton resolution (
1.5
) in
L^{2}
has a different and slightly more complicated formulation compared to the soliton resolution
(
1.7
) in
L^{\infty}
and
\dot{H}^{s}
, because we do not know whether the function
u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)
defined by (
1.6
) belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Secondly, Theorem
1.2
clearly defines a map
(p_{j})_{j\geq 1}\mapsto(p_{j}^{\infty})_{j\geq 1}
from sequences of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
satisfying (
1.4
) into a subset of sequences of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
with strictly increasing imaginary parts. However, even if we know — see section 5— that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}(p_{j}^{\infty})}<\infty,

we do not know the exact range of this map: in view of section 5, describing this range would be equivalent to solving an inverse spectral problem for
the Lax operators associated to potentials
u_{0}
given by (
1.3
), which we think is an interesting open problem. See also Remark
4.4
for a related inverse problem.

The plan of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall the Lax pair structure associated to the Benjamin–Ono equation, and an explicit formula for the Benjamin-Ono equation derived in
[
6
]
. In Section
3
, we show that the eigenfunctions associated to the Lax operator
L_{u_{0}}
belong to the domain of the operator
X^{*}
occurring in the explicit formula. In Section
4
, we show that the infinite-order multisoliton
u_{0}
belongs to
H^{s}
for every
s\geq 0
, and we derive an identity between the
L^{2}
norm of
u_{0}
and the eigenvalues of the Lax operator
L_{u_{0}}
. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Explicit formula for the Benjamin-Ono equation

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on an explicit formula derived by the second author in
[
6
]
, which we recall in this section. Let us denote by

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

the inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

We denote by
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
the Hardy space of complex-valued functions
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
whose Fourier transform is supported in the nonnegative half-line:
\mathrm{supp}\widehat{f}\subseteq[0,\infty)
.
In view of the inverse Fourier formula,

f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}z\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

an element
f
in
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
extends as a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
satisfying

\sup_{y>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x+\mathrm{i}y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Let
\Pi
be the orthogonal projector from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
onto
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
. For
b\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote by
T_{b}
the Toeplitz operator on
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
with symbol
b
, given by

T_{b}f:=\Pi(bf),\quad f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R}).

The Lax operator for (
1.1
) associated to
u_{0}
is defined as

L_{u_{0}}:=-\mathrm{i}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}-T_{u_{0}}.

It is a self–adjoint operator on
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
, with the domain
H^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R}):=L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Next, we introduce the operator
X^{*}
defined as the adjoint in
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
of the multiplication by
x
. Alternatively, one can define
X^{*}
through the formula

\widehat{X^{*}f}(\xi):=\mathrm{i}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad\xi>0,

and the domain

\mathcal{D}(X^{*})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\;\colon\;\widehat{f}|_{(0,\infty)}\in H^{1}(0,\infty)\},

see for instance
[
7
]
.

Finally, for
f\in\mathcal{D}(X^{*})
, the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}(\xi)
admits a limit as
\xi\to 0^{+}
that we denote by

I_{+}(f):=\widehat{f}(0^{+}).

The explicit formula for the Benjamin-Ono equation reads as follows.

Theorem 2.1

(Explicit formula, see
[
6
]
)
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then the solution
u\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R},H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
of (
1.1
) is given by
u(t,x)=2\mathrm{Re}(\Pi u(t,x))
, where the holomorphic extension of
\Pi u
to the complex upper half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{+}
satisfies: for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

(2.1)

\Pi u(t,z)=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}I_{+}((X^{*}-2tL_{u_{0}}-z)^{-1}\Pi u_{0}).

3.
Eigenfunctions of the Lax operator

In this section, we prove a general property of the Lax eigenfunctions associated to a potential in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, which may be of independent interest.

Proposition 3.1

(Lax eigenfunctions belong to the domain of
X^{*}
)
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and let
\varphi
be an eigenfunction of
L_{u_{0}}
associated to a negative eigenvalue
\lambda<0
. Then
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(X^{*})
.
Moreover
X^{*}\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(L_{u_{0}})
and

(3.1)

(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda)(X^{*}\varphi)=-\mathrm{i}\varphi+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi|\lambda|}\langle\varphi,\Pi u_{0}\rangle\Pi u_{0}.

Proof.

Taking the Fourier transform of the equality
L_{u_{0}}\varphi=\lambda\varphi
, we find

(3.2)

\forall\xi>0,\quad\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=\frac{\widehat{u_{0}\varphi}(\xi)}{(\xi+|\lambda|)}.

Since
u_{0}\varphi\in L^{1}
, it is straightforward to check that the right-hand side of (
3.2
) belongs to
L^{2}(0,\infty)
and is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, so that

(3.3)

I_{+}(\varphi)=\widehat{\varphi}(0^{+})=\frac{\widehat{u_{0}\varphi}(0)}{|\lambda|}=\frac{\langle\varphi,\Pi u_{0}\rangle}{|\lambda|}.

In order to show that the right-hand side of (
3.2
) belongs to
H^{1}(0,\infty)
, we will show that the growth rate
\tau_{\eta}\widehat{\varphi}
, where
\tau_{\eta}
is defined for
\eta>0
as

\tau_{\eta}\psi:=\frac{\psi(\xi+\eta)-\psi(\xi)}{\eta},\quad\psi\in L^{2}(0,\infty),

admits a limit as
\eta\to 0
in
L^{2}(0,\infty)
.

First notice that the right hand side of (
3.2
) also reads
A_{u_{0}}(\widehat{\varphi})(\xi)
, where we have set

A_{v}\psi(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi(\xi+|\lambda|)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{v}(\xi-\zeta)\psi(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta,

for
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\psi\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
.
Similarly to what we already observed,
A_{v}
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}(0,\infty)
into itself, with a norm bounded by
C\|v\|_{L^{2}}
, for some constant
C
depending only on
\lambda
.

Let us fix some parameter
R>0
and introduce the truncated function
u_{0,R}
defined as

(3.4)

u_{0,R}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{|x|<R}u_{0}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

We decompose the eigenvalue equation (
3.2
) as

(3.5)

\forall\xi>0,\quad\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)-A_{u_{0}-u_{0,R}}(\widehat{\varphi})(\xi)=A_{u_{0,R}}(\widehat{\varphi})(\xi).

Since the norm of the operator
A_{u_{0}-u_{0,R}}
tends to
0
as
R\to\infty
, we can select
R>0
so that
\mathrm{Id}-A_{u_{0}-u_{0,R}}
is invertible as an operator of
L^{2}(0,\infty)
. Next we identify the commutator of
\tau_{\eta}
with
A_{v}
. A simple calculation leads to

(3.6)

[\tau_{\eta},A_{v}]\psi(\xi)=-\frac{A_{v}\psi(\xi+\eta)}{\xi+|\lambda|}+\frac{1}{2\pi(\xi+|\lambda|)}\int_{0}^{\eta}\widehat{v}(\xi+\eta-\zeta)\psi(\zeta)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\zeta}{\eta}.

If
\psi
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, we conclude that
[\tau_{\eta},A_{v}]\psi
has a limit
\Lambda_{v}\psi
in
L^{2}(0,\infty)
as
\eta\to 0^{+}
, given by

(3.7)

\Lambda_{v}\psi(\xi)=-\frac{A_{v}\psi(\xi)}{\xi+|\lambda|}+\frac{\widehat{v}(\xi)\psi(0^{+})}{2\pi(\xi+|\lambda|)}.

Coming back to (
3.5
), we have

\tau_{\eta}\widehat{\varphi}-A_{u_{0}-u_{0,R}}(\tau_{\eta}\widehat{\varphi})=\tau_{\eta}A_{u_{0,R}}(\widehat{\varphi})+[\tau_{\eta},A_{u_{0}-u_{0,R}}](\widehat{\varphi}),

and, since
xu_{0,R}\in L^{2}
and
\widehat{\varphi}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, the right hand side has a limit in
L^{2}(0,\infty)
as
\eta\to 0^{+}
, given by

-\mathrm{i}A_{xu_{0,R}}(\widehat{\varphi})+\Lambda_{u_{0}-u_{0,R}}(\widehat{\varphi}).

Consequently, the growth rate satisfies, as
\eta\to 0^{+}
,

\tau_{\eta}\widehat{\varphi}\longrightarrow(\mathrm{Id}-A_{u_{0}-u_{0,R}})^{-1}(-\mathrm{i}A_{xu_{0,R}}(\widehat{\varphi})+\Lambda_{u_{0}-u_{0,R}}(\widehat{\varphi})).

This completes the proof of the first statement. For the second statement, let us apply
\tau_{\eta}
directly to (
3.2
),

\tau_{\eta}\widehat{\varphi}=A_{u_{0}}\tau_{\eta}\widehat{\varphi}+[\tau_{\eta},A_{u_{0}}]\widehat{\varphi}.

Taking the limit as
\eta\to 0^{+}
, we obtain, in view of (
3.7
) and (
3.2
),

\quad\widehat{\varphi}^{\prime}(\xi)=A_{u_{0}}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\prime})(\xi)+\Lambda_{u_{0}}(\widehat{\varphi})(\xi)=A_{u_{0}}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\prime})(\xi)-(\xi+|\lambda|)^{-1}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)+(2\pi(\xi+|\lambda|))^{-1}\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi)I_{+}(\varphi).

This equation exactly means

(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda)(X^{*}\varphi)=-\mathrm{i}\varphi+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}I_{+}(\varphi)\Pi u_{0},

which, in view of (
3.3
), is (
3.1
).
∎

Using formula (
3.1
), one easily recovers Wu’s identity
[
12
, Lemma 2.5]
, see also
[
2
]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and let
\varphi
be an eigenfunction of
L_{u_{0}}
associated to a negative eigenvalue
\lambda<0
. Then

|\langle\Pi u_{0},\varphi\rangle|^{2}=2\pi|\lambda|\|\varphi\|^{2}.

Proof.

Take the inner product with
\varphi
of both sides of (
3.1
).
∎

4.
Construction of the infinite-order multisoliton

In this section, we justify that the initial data defined in (
1.3
) belongs to
H^{s}
for
s\geq 0
, and that it admits an infinite number of negative Lax eigenvalues.

Lemma 4.1

(Finite
H^{s}
norm for
s\geq 0
)
.

The function
u_{0}
given by (
1.3
) belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if the sum (
1.4
) is finite.
In this case,
u_{0}
belongs to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for every
s\geq 0
.

Proof.

We define the partial sums

(4.1)

u_{0}^{N}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{2\mathrm{Im}(p_{j})}{|x+p_{j}|^{2}}.

It is enough to show that the sequence
(\Pi u_{0}^{N})_{N\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Note that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\Pi u_{0}^{N}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{x+p_{j}}.

Let
1\leq M<N
. Applying the residue formula, we find

\|\Pi u_{0}^{N}-\Pi u_{0}^{M}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{j,k=M+1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x}{(x+p_{j})(x+\overline{p_{k}})}=\sum_{j,k=M+1}^{N}\frac{2\mathrm{i}\pi}{p_{j}-\overline{p_{k}}}.

Comparing the indices
j
and
k
, we conclude

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\Pi u_{0}^{N}-\Pi u_{0}^{M}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=M+1}^{N}\frac{\pi}{\mathrm{Im}(p_{j})}+\sum_{M<j<k\leq N}\frac{4\pi(\mathrm{Im}(p_{j})+\mathrm{Im}(p_{k}))}{|p_{j}-\overline{p_{k}}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=M+1}^{N}\frac{4\pi\mathrm{Im}(p_{j})}{|p_{j}-\overline{p_{k}}|^{2}}.

This implies that
(\Pi u_{0}^{N})_{N}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if the positive series (
1.4
) is convergent. In particular, note that thanks to (
4.2
), we have

(4.3)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}(p_{j})}<+\infty.

This implies that the sequence
(\mathrm{Im}(p_{j}))_{j\geq 1}
should go to infinity, hence it is bounded from below by some positive number
c_{0}>0
.

We finally fix
s\in(0,\infty)
and we calculate

\|R_{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|\Pi R_{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{2s}|\widehat{\Pi R_{p}}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where we recall that
R_{p}
is given by (
1.2
).
Since

\widehat{\Pi R_{p}}(\xi)=2\pi{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}p\xi},\quad\xi>0,

we infer

(4.4)

\|R_{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{2s}{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\mathrm{Im}(p)\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\frac{\pi\Gamma(2s+1)}{(2\mathrm{Im}(p))^{2s+1}}.

Using (
4.3
), we deduce that the series
\|R_{p_{j}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
is convergent for every
s\geq 1/2
, and therefore
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\geq 1/2
. Since
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that
u_{0}
does not belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and in particular
xu_{0}\not\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, we have

\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{1}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{2\mathrm{Im}(p_{j})}{|x+p_{j}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=2N\pi,

hence the sequence
(u_{0}^{N})_{N\geq 1}
does not converge in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 4.3

(Infinite number of negative Lax eigenvalues)
.

Consider the initial data
u_{0}
given by (
1.3
). The Lax operator
L_{u_{0}}
admits an infinite number of negative eigenvalues
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 1}
with
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\dots<0
. Moreover, there holds the distorted Plancherel formula

(4.5)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2\pi|\lambda_{k}|=\|\Pi u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

We know —see e.g.
[
12
]
— that the essential spectrum of
L_{u_{0}}
is the half–line
[0,\infty)
. Applying the max-min formula for the eigenvalues of
L_{u_{0}}
, we find that whenever the following maximum is negative, the operator
L_{u_{0}}
admits at least
N
negative eigenvalues, and

\lambda_{N}=\max_{\mathrm{dim}F=N-1}\min\{\langle L_{u_{0}}h|h\rangle\;\colon\;h\in H^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R})\cap F^{\perp},\;\|h\|_{L^{2}}=1\}.

Now, we fix
N\geq 1
and we show that
u_{0}
admits at least
N
eigenvalues by comparison with the truncated initial data
u_{0}^{N}
given by (
4.1
). Given that
u_{0}^{N}
is a
N
-soliton potential, it admits exactly
N
negative eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}^{N}<\dots<\lambda_{N}^{N}<0
, see e.g.
[
11
]
.

Moreover, since
u_{0}(x)\geq u_{0}^{N}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we know that
\langle L_{u_{0}}h|h\rangle\leq\langle L_{u_{0}^{N}}h|h\rangle
for every
h\in H^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
. Hence the max-min formula implies that
u_{0}
admits at least
N
negative eigenvalues, satisfying
\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k}^{N}
for
1\leq k\leq N
.

We now show equality (
4.5
). Since
u_{0}^{N}
is a multi–soliton of order
N
, we know — see e.g.
[
11
]
— that
\Pi u_{0}^{N}
is a linear combination of the
N
normalized eigenfunctions
\varphi_{1}^{N},\dots,\varphi_{N}^{N}
of
L_{u_{0}^{N}}
associated to
\lambda_{1}^{N},\dots,\lambda_{N}^{N}
, and Wu’s identity, recalled in Corollary
3.2
, implies that

\|\Pi u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\langle\Pi u_{0}^{N},\varphi_{j}^{N})|^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\pi|\lambda_{k}^{N}|,

so that

\|\Pi u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2\pi|\lambda_{k}|.

Passing to the limit
N\to\infty
, we deduce the inequality

\|\Pi u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2\pi|\lambda_{k}|.

In order to show the converse inequality, we apply Wu’s identity to
u_{0}
itself. Let
\varphi_{k}
be an eigenfunction of
L_{u_{0}}
associated to the negative eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
and satisfying
\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}=1
. Since the family
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is orthonormal, we deduce that

\|\Pi u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\Pi u_{0},\varphi_{k}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2\pi|\lambda_{k}|.

We conclude that (
4.5
) holds.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In view of the distorted Plancherel formula
[
4
, Theorem 26]
proven for initial data
u_{0}
with
xu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, it is natural to ask whether all initial data
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that the eigenvalues the Lax operator
L_{u_{0}}
satisfy (
4.5
) are of the form (
1.3
). This seems to be an open problem.

5.
Soliton resolution for the infinite-order multisoliton

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. We adapt the strategy implemented in
[
5
]
.

For
f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we introduce an operator
\Omega_{t}
on
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
as

\Omega_{t}f(z)=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}I_{+}((X^{*}-2tL_{u_{0}}-z)^{-1}f),\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

so that the explicit formula (
2.1
) reads
\Pi u(t,z)=\Omega_{t}(\Pi u_{0})(z)
.
We first note that
[
5
, Lemma 4.1]
still holds.

Lemma 5.1

(See
[
5
]
)
.

For
f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
, then
\Omega_{t}f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
and we have

|\Omega_{t}f(z)|\leq\frac{\|f\|_{L^{2}}}{2\sqrt{\pi\mathrm{Im}(z)}},\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

In what follows, we refine the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 4.2]
in order to remove the assumption
xu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
j\geq 1
and
f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
such that
f\perp\varphi_{j}
. Then

\Omega_{t}f(z-2t\lambda_{j})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\to\infty}0,\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Proof.

By density of
\mathcal{D}(X^{*})\cap\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
in
\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
and the uniform bound on
\Omega_{t}f
given in Lemma
5.1
, it is enough to prove the result when
f\in\mathcal{D}(X^{*})\cap\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
. In this case, by functional calculus for the selfadjoint operator
L_{u_{0}}
, there exists
h\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})h=f.

Let us show that
h\in\mathcal{D}(X^{*})
. By applying the Fourier transform to the above equation, we find

(5.1)

\forall\xi>0,\quad\widehat{h}(\xi)=\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi)}{\xi-\lambda_{j}}+\frac{\widehat{u_{0}h}(\xi)}{2\pi(\xi-\lambda_{j})}.

At this stage, we just adapt the proof of Proposition
3.1
. The only difference of (
5.1
) with respect to (
3.2
) is the additional term
(\xi-\lambda_{j})^{-1}\widehat{f}(\xi)
, which obviously belongs to
H^{1}(0,\infty)
since
f\in\mathcal{D}(X^{*})
. Hence the same proof leads to
\widehat{h}\in H^{1}(0,\infty)
, namely
h\in\mathcal{D}(X^{*}).

The conclusion now follows from the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 4.2]
: assuming
f\in\mathcal{D}(X^{*})\cap\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
so that
h\in\mathcal{D}(X^{*})
, we write for
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}

\Omega_{t}f(z-2t\lambda_{j})=-\frac{I_{+}(h)}{4\mathrm{i}\pi t}+\frac{\Omega_{t}(X^{*}h-zh)(z-2t\lambda_{j})}{2t},

which goes to zero as
t\to+\infty
thanks to Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Next, we adapt the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
and infer that
\Pi u(t,z-2t\lambda_{j})
admits a pointwise limit as
t\to+\infty
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
j\geq 1
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
. Then as
t\to\infty
, we have

\Pi u(t,z-2t\lambda_{j})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\to\infty}\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}(z),\quad p_{j}^{\infty}:=-\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle.

As we have already observed, applying the operator
\Pi
to (
1.2
) leads to
\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}(z)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{z+p_{j}^{\infty}}
. Moreover, from (
3.3
), we know that

(5.2)

\mathrm{Im}p_{j}^{\infty}=-\mathrm{Im}\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}\widehat{\varphi}_{j},\widehat{\varphi}_{j}\rangle=\frac{|I_{+}(\varphi_{j})|^{2}}{4\pi}=\frac{1}{2|\lambda_{j}|}.

Let us now recall the ideas of proof of this Lemma.

Proof.

We decompose
\Pi u_{0}=\langle\Pi u_{0},\varphi_{j}\rangle\varphi_{j}+f_{j}
, where
f_{j}\in\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
. Applying Lemma
5.2
, we know that
\Omega_{t}f_{j}(z-2t\lambda_{j})\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.
Next, set

\psi_{j}:=\frac{\varphi_{j}}{\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle-z},\quad g_{j}:=\frac{X^{*}\varphi_{j}-\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle\varphi_{j}}{\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle-z},

and observe that

(X^{*}-2t(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})-z)\psi_{j}=\varphi_{j}+g_{j},

so that

\Omega_{t}(\varphi_{j})(z-2t\lambda_{j})=\frac{I_{+}(\psi_{j})}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}-\Omega_{t}(g_{j})(z-2t\lambda_{j}).

Since
g_{j}\in\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
, Lemma
5.2
implies that
\Omega_{t}(g_{j})(z-2t\lambda_{j})
as
t\to+\infty
. Moreover,

I_{+}(\psi_{j})=\frac{I_{+}(\varphi_{j})}{\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle-z}.

Thanks to Corollary
3.2
and (
3.3
), we have
\langle\Pi u_{0},\varphi_{j}\rangle I_{+}(\varphi_{j})=2\pi
. Combining all the identities together, we conclude that

\Omega_{t}(\Pi u_{0})(z-2t\lambda_{j})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{z-\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle}.\qed

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us fix
N\geq 0
, and define the remainder term

r_{N}(t,x):=u(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}(x-c_{p_{j}^{\infty}}t),\quad c_{p_{j}^{\infty}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}p_{j}^{\infty}}=2|\lambda_{j}|.

We expand

\|\Pi r_{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\Pi u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{Re}\langle\Pi u(t,\cdot),\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}(\cdot+2t\lambda_{j})\rangle\\
-2\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\mathrm{Re}\langle\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}(\cdot+2t\lambda_{j}),\Pi R_{p_{k}^{\infty}}(\cdot+2t\lambda_{k})\rangle.

Given the weak convergence
\Pi u(t,\cdot-2t\lambda_{j})\rightharpoonup\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}
provided by Lemma
5.3
, we find that for every
j\geq 1
,

\langle\Pi u(t,\cdot),\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}(\cdot+2t\lambda_{j})\rangle=\langle\Pi u(t,\cdot-2t\lambda_{j}),\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}\rangle\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\to\infty}\langle\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}},\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}\rangle.

Moreover, when
j\neq k
, we have
\lambda_{j}\neq\lambda_{k}
, and in particular

\langle\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}(\cdot+2t\lambda_{j}),\Pi R_{p_{k}^{\infty}}(\cdot+2t\lambda_{k})\rangle\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\to\infty}0.

Combining these observation with the conservation of the
L^{2}
norm under the flow of (
1.1
), this leads to the following identity as
t\to+\infty
:

\|\Pi r_{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\Pi u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+o_{t\to\infty}(1).

Thanks to (
4.5
) and the identity
\|\Pi R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2\pi|\lambda_{j}|
, we find that

\|\Pi r_{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}2\pi|\lambda_{j}|+o_{t\to\infty}(1),

where the first term in the right-hand side is the remainder term of a convergent series in
N
. Letting
t\to\infty
then
N\to\infty
, we find (
1.5
).

Let us prove (
1.7
). First, in view of (
5.2
) and of Proposition (
4.5
), we have

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}(p_{j}^{\infty})}<\infty.

Since

\|R_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\frac{2}{\mathrm{Im}(p)},

we infer that the series (
1.6
) defining
u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,x)
is uniformly convergent, so that
u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
and tends to
0
at infinity. Furthermore, because of (
4.4
), the series (
1.6
) also converges normally in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for every
s\geq 1/2
. Hence
u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\in\dot{H}^{s}
for every
s\geq 1/2
.
On the other hand, due to the Benjamin–Ono conservation laws — see e.g.
[
7
]
—, we know that
u(t,\cdot)
is bounded in all the Sobolev spaces
H^{k}(\mathbb{R})
for
k\geq 0
.
Given
N\geq 1
, set

u_{\mathrm{sol}}^{N}(t,x):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{p_{j}^{\infty}}(x-c_{p_{j}^{\infty}}t).

Given
s\geq 1/2
, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t,\cdots)-u_{\mathrm{sol}}^{N}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\|u_{\mathrm{sol}}^{N}(t,\cdot)-u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t,\cdot)-u^{N}_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|u(t,\cdots)-u^{N}_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{2s}}^{1/2}+C_{s}\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{s+1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{s}(\|u(t,\cdot)-u^{N}_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}+\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{s+1/2}).

Similarly, since
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
, we have

\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|u(t,\cdot)-u^{N}_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}+\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|).

Passing to the upper limit as
t\to\infty
, we obtain, for every
N\geq 1
,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}(\|u(t,\cdots)-u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}})\lesssim\delta_{N}^{1/2}+\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|,

where
\delta_{N}:=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u(t,\cdot)-u^{N}_{\mathrm{sol}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}
.
Hence (
1.7
) follows by letting
N\to\infty
.
∎
