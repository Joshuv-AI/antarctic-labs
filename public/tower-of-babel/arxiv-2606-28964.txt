Noncommutative Anisotropic Diffusion in Hilbert Space. I. The Consistent A A -Geometry, Mosco Stability, and the Weak Bridge
E. Yu. Shchetinin, A. A. Shevchuk, S. I. Salpagarov

Abstract
This first part of the series builds the analytic layer of noncommutative anisotropic diffusion in a separable Hilbert space. Let μ 0 = 𝒩 ⁡ ( 0 , Q ) \mu_{0}=\mathcal{N}(0,Q) be the reference Gaussian measure, with Q ∈ ℒ 1 ​ ( ℋ ) Q\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mathcal{H}) , and let D ⁡ ( x ) D(x) be a positive, state-dependent anisotropy. We do not assume that [ D ⁡ ( x ) , Q ] = 0 [D(x),Q]=0 . Consequently, for the forward SDE with σ ⁡ ( x ) = D ​ ( x ) 1 / 2 ​ Q 1 / 2 \sigma(x)=D(x)^{1/2}Q^{1/2} the correct energy form is given not by the expression ⟨ D ∇ u , ∇ v ⟩ \langle D\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle but by the consistent form Γ A ( u , v ) = ⟨ Q 1 / 2 D ( x ) 1 / 2 ∇ u , Q 1 / 2 D ( x ) 1 / 2 ∇ v ⟩ . \Gamma_{A}(u,v)=\langle Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}\nabla u,Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}\nabla v\rangle. We prove closability of the form, well-posedness of the forward dynamics, Galerkin convergence, stability of the A A -LSI under a Mosco limit, the chain rule for relative entropy, and a general weak-bridge theorem. The main result of Part I is a functional-analytic theorem: if A A -consistency, a uniform A A -LSI, and representability of the right-hand side of the backward weak form in the negative energy space all hold, then a backward weak drift v = 𝖠 ∇ Φ v=\mathsf{A}\nabla\Phi exists and the basic entropy dissipation estimate holds. In addition, we single out a three-dimensional tensor class of anisotropies, formulate a condition for the absence of diffusion degeneracy, and obtain a rate estimate for the homogenization limit, first on cylindrical subspaces and then on compact-tail classes, which yields strong resolvent convergence and convergence of the forward SDEs. The statistical closure, an independent isotropic benchmark, and an approximation theorem for A A -adapted networks are treated in Part II.

1
Introduction

Infinite-dimensional diffusion models require the joint treatment of three
structures: the stochastic dynamics, the geometry of the reference measure,
and the energy form

[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. In the commutative
situation, when
D
and
Q
are diagonalized in the same basis, the
Dirichlet form is usually written as
\int\langle D\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle\,d\mu_{0}
.
The present work treats the noncommutative case, in which this reduction is
no longer valid.

An important difference from the work of Pidstrigach–Marzouk–Reich–Wang and
Hagemann–Mildenberger–Ruthotto–Steidl–Yang

[
25
,
27
]
is as follows. In those works the
infinite-dimensional setting is built around well-posedness of score-based
dynamics on function spaces and around discretization-invariant
approximations. In the present part the central object is not the generative
scheme itself but the noncommutative operator geometry: the noise has the form

\sigma(x)=D(x)^{1/2}Q^{1/2}
, so the carré du champ is determined by the
operator
\sigma(x)^{*}=Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}
. When
[D(x),Q]\neq 0
this does not
reduce to a mere change of norm, as in the known results; one needs a separate
closability of the consistent form, a separate
A
-LSI, and a separate
duality for the weak bridge.

A further difference from

[
25
,
27
]
concerns the role of the operator
Q
.
In the standard functional diffusion setting,
Q
usually fixes the
smoothness of the noise and the discretization invariance. In our case
Q

also enters the energy geometry itself: the weak bridge, the dissipation, and
the logarithmic Sobolev inequality are all formulated through

Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}
. Hence, even with the same forward noise regularity, two
models with a different operator order have different entropy
dissipations. This feature is not covered by isotropic or commutative theories
of score-based dynamics.

The aim of Part I is to separate the analytic problem from the statistical
one. In contrast to current infinite-dimensional diffusion models

[
24
,
25
,
26
]
, here the main object
is not only well-posedness of the forward evolution in Hilbert space, but also
the matching of the noncommutative anisotropy
D(x)
with the Gaussian
geometry
Q
. We do not introduce neural-network classes, validation errors,
or lower bounds here. These questions are deferred to Part II. Part I
proves the basic theorem on which the score approximation and the applications
subsequently rely.

Terminology.

The term
score
denotes the logarithmic gradient of the density:

s_{\rho}=\nabla\log\rho
. The term “weak bridge” denotes the representation
of the backward weak evolution through a field
v=\mathsf{A}\nabla\Phi
in the
negative energy space. The word “consistent” refers to the order of
operators
\sigma^{*}=Q^{1/2}D^{1/2}
, which determines the carré du champ.

2
A Detailed Comparison with Infinite-Dimensional Diffusion Models

Current work on infinite-dimensional diffusion models can be divided roughly
into two directions. The first studies the consistency of discretizations and
the existence of a limiting dynamics on function spaces. The second
investigates score approximation and the stability of learned backward fields.
The present part differs from both directions in that the main difficulty is
not only the passage to infinite dimension, but also the noncommutativity of
two operator structures: the covariance
Q
and the anisotropy
D(x)
.

In the setting of Pidstrigach et al.
[
25
]
the central
question is: how should one define a diffusion model on a function space so
that it does not depend on a finite-dimensional grid? Here, by contrast, the
forward noise is already given in Hilbert space, but its covariance has the
noncommutative structure
D(x)^{1/2}QD(x)^{1/2}
. Hence, even when a strong
solution of the SDE exists, a separate problem remains: which Dirichlet form
corresponds to this noise, and which dissipation should enter the entropy
inequality. It is precisely this problem that is solved by introducing the

A
-geometry.

In the work of Hagemann–Mildenberger–Ruthotto–Steidl–Yang

[
27
]
the main emphasis is on score-based models on function
spaces and on the approximation properties of the score field. In the present
Part I the score field is not yet used: we prove an analytic reduction showing
that the entire statistical error in Part II enters via a single
functional
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
. This separation is essential for the proof structure of
the series: well-posedness of the weak bridge and of the chain rule does not
depend on the chosen class of neural networks, and the statistical estimates
of Part II need not re-prove closability of the form.

Thus the contribution of Part I is not a repetition of existing Hilbert-space
diffusion frameworks, but the construction of a noncommutative energy layer
that can be used as the analytic basis for various classes of backward
approximations.

3
Setup and the Consistent
A
-Geometry

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable real Hilbert space, and let

\mu_{0}=\mathcal{N}(0,Q),\qquad Qe_{k}=q_{k}e_{k},\qquad q_{k}>0,\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q_{k}<\infty.

We write
\mathcal{FC}_{b}^{\infty}
for the class of smooth cylindrical functions.

Assumption 3.1

(Anisotropy)
.

For each
x\in\mathcal{H}
the operator
D(x)
is self-adjoint, positive, and
bounded. There exist
0<d_{-}\leq d_{+}<\infty
such that

d_{-}I\leq D(x)\leq d_{+}I.

The map
x\mapsto D(x)^{1/2}
is globally Lipschitz in the operator norm.

The forward noise is given by the operator

\sigma(x)=D(x)^{1/2}Q^{1/2}.

Then

\sigma(x)^{*}=Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}.

It is precisely this order that determines the energy form.

We also write

\mathsf{A}(x):=D(x)^{1/2}QD(x)^{1/2}.

This field of operators depends on
x
; hence all expressions of the form

\mathsf{A}^{-1}
,
\mathsf{A}\nabla\Phi
, and
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}

appearing below are understood pointwise and are then integrated against the
appropriate measure.

Definition 3.2

(The consistent
A
-form)
.

For
u,v\in\mathcal{FC}_{b}^{\infty}
set

\Gamma_{A}(u,v)(x)=\left\langle Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}\nabla u(x),Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}\nabla v(x)\right\rangle

and

\mathcal{E}_{A}(u,v)=\int_{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma_{A}(u,v)\,d\mu_{0}.

Assumption 3.3

(
A
-compatibility)
.

There exist
0<c_{A}\leq C_{A}<\infty
such that for all
x,\xi\in\mathcal{H}

c_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq\|Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq C_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}.

Theorem 3.4

(Closability of the consistent form)
.

Suppose Assumptions
3.1
–
3.3
hold. Then the form

(\mathcal{E}_{A},\mathcal{FC}_{b}^{\infty})
is closable in
L^{2}(\mu_{0})
, its closure is a
symmetric Markovian Dirichlet form, and

c_{A}\int\|Q^{1/2}\nabla u\|^{2}\,d\mu_{0}\leq\mathcal{E}_{A}(u,u)\leq C_{A}\int\|Q^{1/2}\nabla u\|^{2}\,d\mu_{0}.

Proof.

For cylindrical functions set
\nabla_{Q}u=Q^{1/2}\nabla u
. We first check
closability. Let
u_{n}\in\mathcal{FC}_{b}^{\infty}
,
u_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\mu_{0})
, and

\mathcal{E}_{A}(u_{n}-u_{m},u_{n}-u_{m})\to 0
. By the lower
A
-compatibility bound,

c_{A}\|\nabla_{Q}(u_{n}-u_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0};\mathcal{H})}^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{A}(u_{n}-u_{m},u_{n}-u_{m}),

so
\nabla_{Q}u_{n}
is Cauchy in
L^{2}(\mu_{0};\mathcal{H})
. Denote its limit by

G
. For any basis vector
e_{k}
and any cylindrical
\psi
, Gaussian
integration by parts gives

\int\langle\nabla_{Q}u_{n},e_{k}\rangle\psi\,d\mu_{0}=-\int u_{n}\,\partial^{Q,*}_{e_{k}}\psi\,d\mu_{0},

where
\partial^{Q,*}_{e_{k}}\psi\in L^{2}(\mu_{0})
. The right-hand side tends to
zero, since
u_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}
. Passing to the limit, we obtain

\int\langle G,e_{k}\rangle\psi\,d\mu_{0}=0
for all
k
and all cylindrical

\psi
. Density of cylindrical functions in
L^{2}(\mu_{0})
gives
G=0
.
Now the upper
A
-compatibility bound yields

\mathcal{E}_{A}(u_{n},u_{n})\leq C_{A}\|\nabla_{Q}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0};\mathcal{H})}^{2}\to 0.

Hence the form is closable. The two-sided estimate in the statement follows at
once from Assumption
3.3
. The Markov property is checked first
for cylindrical truncations
\eta\circ u
, where
\eta
is a

1
-Lipschitz normal contraction:

\Gamma_{A}(\eta(u),\eta(u))\leq\Gamma_{A}(u,u).

After closure this inequality carries over to the domain of the closed form,
giving a symmetric Markovian Dirichlet form.
∎

4
Forward Dynamics and Galerkin Convergence

Consider the SDE

dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}D(X_{t})^{1/2}Q^{1/2}\,dW_{t}.

Assumption 4.1

(Coefficients of the forward dynamics)
.

The function
b(t,\cdot)
is globally Lipschitz uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
,

\beta\in C([0,T])
,
0<\beta_{-}\leq\beta(t)\leq\beta_{+}
, and

\sigma(x)=D(x)^{1/2}Q^{1/2}
is Lipschitz as a map into

\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
.

Theorem 4.2

(Well-posedness of the forward SDE)
.

Under Assumption
4.1
, for each

X_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
there is a unique strong solution

X\in L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathcal{H}))
. Moreover,

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\leq T}\|X_{t}\|^{2}\leq C_{T}^{\mathrm{fwd}}(1+\mathbb{E}\|X_{0}\|^{2}).

Proof.

We verify the conditions for strong solvability directly. From the Lipschitz
continuity of
D^{1/2}
in the operator norm, we have for

\sigma(x)=D(x)^{1/2}Q^{1/2}

\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\leq\|D(x)^{1/2}-D(y)^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\|Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\leq L_{D^{1/2}}\|Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\|x-y\|.

Moreover,

\|\sigma(x)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}=\operatorname{Tr}(D(x)^{1/2}QD(x)^{1/2})\leq d_{+}\operatorname{Tr}Q.

For the drift, by Assumption
4.1
,

\|b(t,x)-b(t,y)\|\leq L_{b}\|x-y\|,\qquad\|b(t,x)\|\leq L_{b}\|x\|+B_{0},

where
B_{0}=\sup_{t\leq T}\|b(t,0)\|
. Thus the coefficients satisfy the
global Lipschitz and linear-growth conditions in
\mathcal{H}
and

\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
. Successive Picard approximations give a unique local
solution; Itô’s formula for
\|X_{t}\|^{2}
, the
Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality, and Young’s inequality give

\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\leq t}\|X_{s}\|^{2}\leq C\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\|X_{0}\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+\mathbb{E}\sup_{r\leq s}\|X_{r}\|^{2})\,ds\Bigr),

where

C=C(T,L_{b},B_{0},\beta_{+},d_{+},\operatorname{Tr}Q,L_{D^{1/2}}\|Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}).

Gronwall’s lemma rules out blow-up and gives the claimed a priori estimate.
This also extends the local solution to the whole interval
[0,T]
.
Uniqueness follows from the same estimate applied to the difference of two
solutions.
∎

Let
P_{n}
be the orthogonal projection onto

\mathcal{H}_{n}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
. The Galerkin system is

dX_{t}^{n}=P_{n}b(t,X_{t}^{n})\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}P_{n}D(X_{t}^{n})^{1/2}Q^{1/2}\,dW_{t}.

Assumption 4.3

(Tails of the coefficients)
.

There exist
C,\gamma>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\|(I-P_{n})X_{0}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|(I-P_{n})b(t,X_{t})\|^{2}\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|(I-P_{n})\sigma(X_{t})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq Cn^{-\gamma}.

Theorem 4.4

(Galerkin convergence)
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
and
4.3
,

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\leq T}\|X_{t}^{n}-X_{t}\|^{2}\leq C_{T}n^{-\gamma}.

Consequently,

W_{2}(\operatorname{Law}(X_{t}^{n}),\operatorname{Law}(X_{t}))\leq C_{T}n^{-\gamma/2}.

Proof.

We subtract the equations for
X^{n}
and
X
, apply the
Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality, and use Gronwall’s lemma. All projection
remainders are controlled by Assumption
4.3
. The
W_{2}
estimate
follows from coupling the solutions through a single Wiener process.
∎

5
The Extended
\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1}
-Norm

Since
Q
is nuclear, the operator

\mathsf{A}(x)=D(x)^{1/2}QD(x)^{1/2}

is compact, and
\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1}
is not a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
. We
therefore use an extended pointwise norm.

Definition 5.1

(The extended
\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1}
-norm)
.

For
z\in\mathcal{H}
set

\|z\|_{\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1}}=\begin{cases}\|\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1/2}z\|,&z\in\operatorname{Ran}\mathsf{A}(x)^{1/2},\\
+\infty,&z\notin\operatorname{Ran}\mathsf{A}(x)^{1/2}.\end{cases}

For a vector field
z(x)
we define the integral norm

\|z\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}:=\int_{\mathcal{H}}\|z(x)\|_{\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1}}^{2}\,d\nu(x),

provided the right-hand side is finite.

Lemma 5.2

(Integral duality)
.

Let
z\in L^{2}(\nu;\mathcal{H})
with

z(x)\in\operatorname{Ran}\mathsf{A}(x)^{1/2}
for
\nu
-a.e.
x
. Then

\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{FC}_{b}^{\infty}}\frac{\int\langle z(x),\nabla\phi(x)\rangle\,d\nu(x)}{\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu}(\phi,\phi)^{1/2}}=\|z\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},L^{2}(\nu)}.

Proof.

Consider the closure of the set of gradients
\nabla\phi
,
\phi\in\mathcal{FC}_{b}^{\infty}
,
in the norm

\|\nabla\phi\|_{\mathsf{A},L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}=\int\Gamma_{A}(\phi,\phi)\,d\nu.

The functional

\nabla\phi\mapsto\int\langle z,\nabla\phi\rangle\,d\nu

is continuous in this norm if and only if

\|z\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},L^{2}(\nu)}<\infty
. After the substitution

w(x)=\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1/2}z(x)
the statement becomes the Riesz identity in

L^{2}(\nu;\mathcal{H})
.
∎

6
Mosco Convergence and the
A
-LSI

Definition 6.1

(Mosco convergence of forms)
.

A sequence of closed forms
\mathcal{E}_{n}
converges to
\mathcal{E}
in the
Mosco sense if the liminf condition holds for weakly convergent sequences and
the limsup recovery condition holds for each element of the domain of the
limit form
[
9
,
10
]
.

Proposition 6.2

(Preservation of the
A
-LSI under the Mosco limit)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{A_{n},\nu_{n}}
converge in the Mosco sense to

\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu}
, let
\nu_{n}\Rightarrow\nu
, and suppose

\mathrm{H}(f^{2}\nu_{n}\|\nu_{n})\leq 2C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}\mathcal{E}_{A_{n},\nu_{n}}(f,f)

with the same constant. Then

\mathrm{H}(f^{2}\nu\|\nu)\leq 2C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu}(f,f).

Proof.

For
f\in\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu})
take a recovery sequence
f_{n}\to f
with

\limsup_{n}\mathcal{E}_{A_{n},\nu_{n}}(f_{n},f_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu}(f,f).

Lower semicontinuity of the entropy gives

\mathrm{H}(f^{2}\nu\|\nu)\leq\liminf_{n}\mathrm{H}(f_{n}^{2}\nu_{n}\|\nu_{n}).

Combining the two estimates completes the proof.
∎

7
The Weighted Form and the Chain Rule

Let
\nu=\rho\mu_{0}
. Define

\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho}(u,v)=\int\Gamma_{A}(u,v)\rho\,d\mu_{0}.

Assumption 7.1

(Weighted closability)
.

The density
\rho
is positive,
\rho\in L^{1}(\mu_{0})
, and the form

\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho}
is closable. In addition, the cylindrical truncations
are a core for the form.

Theorem 7.2

(Chain rule for relative entropy)
.

Let
\rho_{t}
and
\hat{\rho}_{t}
be weak solutions of two evolutions with the
same second-order part
\Gamma_{A}
and drifts
v_{t},\hat{v}_{t}
. Suppose that

h_{t}=\log(\rho_{t}/\hat{\rho}_{t})
admits truncations
h_{t,R}
and cylindrical
approximations
h_{t,R,n}
in the form
\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}
. Assume in
addition that, writing
J_{t}=\int\Gamma_{A}(h_{t},h_{t})\rho_{t}\,d\mu_{0}
,

\int_{0}^{T}J_{t}\,dt<\infty,\qquad\int_{0}^{T}\|v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t}\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},L^{2}(\rho_{t}\mu_{0})}^{2}\,dt<\infty.

Then the entropy derivative exists in the sense of distributions in time and

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}(\rho_{t}\|\hat{\rho}_{t})=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)\int\Gamma_{A}(h_{t},h_{t})\rho_{t}\,d\mu_{0}+\int\langle v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t},\nabla h_{t}\rangle\rho_{t}\,d\mu_{0}.

Proof.

We first use
h_{t,R,n}
as a cylindrical test function in the weak
equations. The second-order terms cancel by the carré du champ identity:

L\Phi(f,g)-\Phi_{f}Lf-\Phi_{g}Lg=-\frac{1}{2}\beta\,\Gamma_{A}(\log(f/g),\log(f/g))f

for
\Phi(f,g)=f\log(f/g)
. Then we let
n\to\infty
using the core
property, along the limits
h_{t,R,n}\to h_{t,R}\to h_{t}
, and

R\to\infty
by monotone convergence of the entropy and Fatou’s lemma for
the energy.
∎

8
The General Weak Bridge

Let a curve of measures
\nu_{t}=\rho_{t}\mu_{0}
be given. Denote the formal
right-hand side of the backward weak form by

F_{t}=\partial_{t}\nu_{t}-L_{A}^{*}\nu_{t}.

Assumption 8.1

(The right-hand-side space)
.

For almost every
t
the functional
F_{t}
is continuous on

\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}})/\mathbb{R}
:

|\langle F_{t},\phi\rangle|\leq C_{t}\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}(\phi,\phi)^{1/2}.

Theorem 8.2

(The weak bridge)
.

Under Assumption
8.1
there exists

\Phi_{t}\in\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}})/\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}(\Phi_{t},\phi)=\langle F_{t},\phi\rangle

for all
\phi
. The field

v_{t}(x)=\mathsf{A}(x)\nabla\Phi_{t}(x)

realizes the backward weak form and satisfies

\int\|v_{t}(x)\|_{\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1}}^{2}\,d\nu_{t}(x)\leq C_{t}^{2}.

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{V}_{t}=\overline{\mathcal{FC}_{b}^{\infty}/\mathbb{R}}^{\,\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}}

be the quotient space modulo constants, equipped with the norm

\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}}=\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}(\phi,\phi)^{1/2}
. On

\mathcal{V}_{t}
the bilinear form

B_{t}(\Phi,\phi)=\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}(\Phi,\phi)

is continuous and coercive:

|B_{t}(\Phi,\phi)|\leq\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}},\qquad B_{t}(\Phi,\Phi)=\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{2}.

Assumption
8.1
means that
F_{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{t}^{\prime}
and

\|F_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}^{\prime}}\leq C_{t}
. By the Lax–Milgram theorem there exists a
unique
\Phi_{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{t}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}(\Phi_{t},\phi)=\langle F_{t},\phi\rangle\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{t},

and
\|\Phi_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}}\leq C_{t}
. Define the field in the negative
energy space by

v_{t}(x)=\mathsf{A}(x)\nabla\Phi_{t}(x).

Then for cylindrical
\phi

\int\langle v_{t},\nabla\phi\rangle\,d\nu_{t}=\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}(\Phi_{t},\phi)=\langle F_{t},\phi\rangle,

that is, the field realizes the backward weak form. Since
v_{t}=\mathsf{A}(x)\nabla\Phi_{t}
, the duality of
Lemma
5.2
gives

\int\|v_{t}(x)\|_{\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1}}^{2}d\nu_{t}(x)=\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}(\Phi_{t},\Phi_{t})\leq C_{t}^{2}.

∎

9
The Basic Entropy Estimate

Let the approximate backward evolution have drift
\hat{v}_{t}
, and the exact
one have drift
v_{t}
. Denote

J_{t}=\int\Gamma_{A}(h_{t},h_{t})\rho_{t}\,d\mu_{0},\qquad h_{t}=\log(\rho_{t}/\hat{\rho}_{t}).

Assumption 9.1

(Drift error)
.

\int\|v_{t}(x)-\hat{v}_{t}(x)\|_{\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1}}^{2}\,d\nu_{t}(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\beta(t)^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(t).

Theorem 9.2

(The basic dissipation estimate)
.

Suppose the hypotheses of Theorem
7.2
and
Assumption
9.1
hold. Then

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}(\rho_{t}\|\hat{\rho}_{t})\leq-\frac{1}{4}\beta(t)J_{t}+\frac{1}{4}\beta(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(t).

If, in addition, the approximate measure
\hat{\nu}_{t}=\hat{\rho}_{t}\mu_{0}

satisfies the
A
-LSI in entropy–information form,

\mathrm{H}(\rho_{t}\|\hat{\rho}_{t})\leq 2C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}\int\Gamma_{A}\left(\log\frac{\rho_{t}}{\hat{\rho}_{t}},\log\frac{\rho_{t}}{\hat{\rho}_{t}}\right)\rho_{t}\,d\mu_{0}=2C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}J_{t},

then

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}(\rho_{t}\|\hat{\rho}_{t})\leq-\frac{\beta(t)}{8C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}}\mathrm{H}(\rho_{t}\|\hat{\rho}_{t})+\frac{1}{4}\beta(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(t).

Proof.

From Theorem
7.2
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the pair

A,A^{-1}
we obtain

\left|\int\langle v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t},\nabla h_{t}\rangle d\nu_{t}\right|\leq\left(\int\|v_{t}(x)-\hat{v}_{t}(x)\|_{\mathsf{A}(x)^{-1}}^{2}d\nu_{t}(x)\right)^{1/2}J_{t}^{1/2}.

Substituting Assumption
9.1
and using Young’s inequality gives the
first estimate. The second follows from the
A
-LSI for the approximate
measure
\hat{\nu}_{t}
.
∎

11
Tensor Anisotropy and Non-Degeneracy of the Diffusion

In applications
D(x)
often arises as a tensor conductivity or a tensor
mobility. In the infinite-dimensional noncommutative problem the two-sided
bound
d_{-}I\leq D(x)\leq d_{+}I
is not by itself always sufficient to compare

\|Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}\xi\|
and
\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|
, since
D(x)
and
Q

need not commute. The top-level condition is therefore formulated not through
the spectrum of
D(x)
but through relative
Q
-compatibility.

Definition 11.1

(A tensor
Q
-compatible class)
.

A family of tensors
\mathbb{D}(x)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is called
Q
-compatible if

\mathbb{D}(x)
is self-adjoint, positive, measurable in
x
, and there
exist
0<c_{A}\leq C_{A}<\infty
such that

c_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq\|Q^{1/2}\mathbb{D}(x)^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq C_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}

for all
x,\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. If
Q
and
\mathbb{D}(x)
commute, one may take

c_{A}=\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{D})
and
C_{A}=\lambda_{\max}(\mathbb{D})
. In
the noncommutative case these constants are verified as relative

Q
-bounds.

Assumption 11.2

(Non-degeneracy of the diffusion)
.

The family
D(x)
satisfies global
A
-compatibility with constants

c_{A},C_{A}
independent of
x
, and

\limsup_{\|x\|\to\infty}\|Q^{1/2}D(x)^{1/2}Q^{-1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\operatorname{Ran}Q^{1/2})}<\infty.

A weaker variant allows polynomial growth of this norm, provided the measures under consideration have suitable moments.

Proposition 11.3

(Transfer of the analytic theory to the tensor class)
.

Let
D(x)=\mathbb{D}(x)
belong to the class of
Definition
11.1
and let
Assumption
11.2
hold. Then all the results of
Sections 3–8 remain valid with
D
replaced by
\mathbb{D}
. In
particular, the form

\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{A}}(u,v)=\int\langle Q^{1/2}\mathbb{D}(x)^{1/2}\nabla u,Q^{1/2}\mathbb{D}(x)^{1/2}\nabla v\rangle\,d\mu_{0}

is closable, the weak bridge has the form

v_{t}(x)=\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla\Phi_{t}(x)
,

\mathbb{A}(x)=\mathbb{D}(x)^{1/2}Q\mathbb{D}(x)^{1/2},

and the basic entropy estimate of Theorem
10.2
continues to
hold with the same constants
c_{A},C_{A},C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}
.

Proof.

The proofs of Sections 3–8 use only three properties: closability of the
consistent form, the two-sided
A
-compatibility, and representability of
the weak bridge in the negative energy space. These properties are built into
Definition
11.1
and
Assumption
11.2
. Commutativity of the operators is
not used. Hence all estimates carry over unchanged.
∎

Remark 11.4

(On the role of spectral bounds)
.

In a finite-dimensional discretization, or in the commutative tensor case,
the spectral bounds of the tensor give
A
-compatibility directly. In the
infinite-dimensional noncommutative case this is false in general, which is
precisely why the condition is formulated through
Q^{1/2}D^{1/2}
, and not
only through
\lambda_{\min}(D)
and
\lambda_{\max}(D)
.

12
Details of the Key Proofs

This section spells out the technical points that were stated compactly in the
main body.

12.1
Verifying the hypotheses of the forward-SDE theorem

Theorem
4.2
relies on standard results for SDEs in Hilbert
spaces
[
1
, Ch. 7]
,
[
2
, Ch. 4]
. We check the
conditions for the specific coefficient

\sigma(x)=D(x)^{1/2}Q^{1/2}.

The Lipschitz continuity of
D^{1/2}
gives

\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\leq\|D(x)^{1/2}-D(y)^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\|Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\leq L_{D}\|Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\|x-y\|.

Moreover,

\|\sigma(x)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}=\operatorname{Tr}(D(x)^{1/2}QD(x)^{1/2})\leq d_{+}\operatorname{Tr}Q.

Hence
\sigma
has linear growth and is in fact uniformly bounded. For the
drift,

\|b(t,x)-b(t,y)\|\leq L_{b}\|x-y\|,\qquad\|b(t,x)\|\leq L_{b}\|x\|+\|b(t,0)\|.

If
B_{0}:=\sup_{t\leq T}\|b(t,0)\|<\infty
, then Itô’s formula for

\|X_{t}\|^{2}
, Young’s inequality, and BDG give

\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\leq t}\|X_{s}\|^{2}\leq C\left(\mathbb{E}\|X_{0}\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+\mathbb{E}\sup_{r\leq s}\|X_{r}\|^{2})\,ds\right),

where

C=C(T,L_{b},B_{0},\beta_{+},d_{+},\operatorname{Tr}Q).

Gronwall’s lemma yields the estimate of Theorem
4.2
with

C_{T}^{\mathrm{fwd}}\leq C_{0}\exp\{C_{1}T(1+L_{b}^{2}+\beta_{+}d_{+}\operatorname{Tr}Q)\}.

12.2
Projection tails in the Galerkin estimate

In Theorem
4.4
the difference
\Delta_{t}^{n}=X_{t}^{n}-X_{t}

satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}^{n}=

\displaystyle(P_{n}-I)X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\{P_{n}b(s,X_{s}^{n})-b(s,X_{s})\}\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\beta(s)}\{P_{n}\sigma(X_{s}^{n})-\sigma(X_{s})\}\,dW_{s}.

Splitting

P_{n}b(s,X_{s}^{n})-b(s,X_{s})=P_{n}(b(s,X_{s}^{n})-b(s,X_{s}))+(P_{n}-I)b(s,X_{s})

and similarly for
\sigma
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{r\leq t}\|\Delta_{r}^{n}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\|(I-P_{n})X_{0}\|^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\sup_{r\leq s}\|\Delta_{r}^{n}\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|(I-P_{n})b(s,X_{s})\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|(I-P_{n})\sigma(X_{s})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}\,ds.

Here
C
depends only on
T,L_{b},L_{\sigma},\beta_{+}
(with
L_{\sigma}=L_{D^{1/2}}\|Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}
) and the universal BDG
constant. Assumption
4.3
and Gronwall give

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\leq T}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{2}\leq C_{T}n^{-\gamma}.

This also shows that the exponent
\gamma/2
in the
W_{2}
estimate comes from the projection.

12.3
The weak bridge: verifying the Lax–Milgram hypotheses

In Theorem
8.2
we consider the quotient space

\mathcal{V}_{t}=\overline{\mathcal{FC}_{b}^{\infty}/\mathbb{R}}^{\,\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}}.

On it the bilinear form

B_{t}(\Phi,\phi)=\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}(\Phi,\phi)

is continuous and coercive:

|B_{t}(\Phi,\phi)|\leq\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}},\qquad B_{t}(\Phi,\Phi)=\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{2}.

Assumption
8.1
means that
F_{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{t}^{\prime}
. Hence there
exists a unique element
\Phi_{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{t}
such that

B_{t}(\Phi_{t},\phi)=\langle F_{t},\phi\rangle.

Moreover,

\|\Phi_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}}\leq\|F_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}^{\prime}}.

The field
v_{t}=\mathsf{A}\nabla\Phi_{t}
is defined as an element of the dual
gradient space, and the estimate

\int\|v_{t}\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}^{2}\,d\nu_{t}=\|\Phi_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{2}\leq\|F_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{t}^{\prime}}^{2}

follows from Lemma
5.2
.

12.4
The entropy estimate: explicit constants

From the chain rule we have

\dot{H}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)J_{t}+\int\langle v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t},\nabla h_{t}\rangle\,d\nu_{t}.

By the
A,A^{-1}
duality,

\left|\int\langle v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t},\nabla h_{t}\rangle\,d\nu_{t}\right|\leq\|v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t}\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},L^{2}(\nu_{t})}J_{t}^{1/2}.

The drift-error condition gives

\|v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t}\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},L^{2}(\nu_{t})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(t)^{1/2}.

Hence

\dot{H}_{t}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)J_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(t)^{1/2}J_{t}^{1/2}\leq-\frac{1}{4}\beta(t)J_{t}+\frac{1}{4}\beta(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(t).

If
\hat{\nu}_{t}
satisfies the
A
-LSI and

J_{t}\geq(2C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A})^{-1}H_{t},

then

\dot{H}_{t}\leq-\frac{\beta(t)}{8C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}}H_{t}+\frac{1}{4}\beta(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(t).

It is exactly this constant that is used in Part II when closing

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(t)\leq\widetilde{q}^{\,2}J_{t}+\Delta_{M}
.

12.5
The tensor transfer

For Section
11
it is important that all estimates use only
the operator

Q^{1/2}\mathbb{D}(x)^{1/2}

and do not require
\mathbb{D}(x)
and
Q
to commute. If

c_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq\|Q^{1/2}\mathbb{D}(x)^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq C_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2},

then the proof of Theorem
3.4
, of the weak bridge, and of
the entropy estimate carries over verbatim. This shows that the non-degeneracy
condition for the diffusion is not a mere technicality, but the minimal
way to retain the
A
-geometry under noncommutativity.

13
Details of the Mosco Passage and the Chain Rule

Sections 6 and 7 contain two technical passages that are often written too
briefly: preservation of the logarithmic Sobolev inequality under a Mosco
limit, and the use of the logarithmic density ratio as a test function in the
weak evolution. Below these passages are written out in a form sufficient to
verify all the limit operations.

Lemma 13.1

(Normalization and convergence of measures with densities)
.

Let
\nu_{n}\Rightarrow\nu
, let
f_{n}\to f
strongly in
L^{2}
along a
Mosco recovery sequence, and let

\int f_{n}^{2}\,d\nu_{n}=1,\qquad\sup_{n}\int f_{n}^{2}\log_{+}f_{n}^{2}\,d\nu_{n}<\infty.

Then, after replacing
f_{n}
by normalized truncations,

f_{n}^{2}\nu_{n}\Rightarrow f^{2}\nu,\qquad\int f^{2}\,d\nu=1,

and

\mathrm{H}(f^{2}\nu\|\nu)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{H}(f_{n}^{2}\nu_{n}\|\nu_{n}).

Proof.

We first take truncations
f_{n,R}=(-R)\vee f_{n}\wedge R
. They preserve the
Mosco recovery, since the form is Markovian. For bounded
f_{n,R}
, weak
convergence of the measures and strong
L^{2}
convergence give

f_{n,R}^{2}\nu_{n}\Rightarrow f_{R}^{2}\nu
. The entropy is a lower-semicontinuous
functional with respect to weak convergence of probability measures. Hence

\mathrm{H}(f_{R}^{2}\nu\|\nu)\leq\liminf_{n}\mathrm{H}(f_{n,R}^{2}\nu_{n}\|\nu_{n}).

The passage
R\to\infty
is carried out by monotone convergence for

r\mapsto r\log r
after the standard normalization

f_{n,R}/\|f_{n,R}\|_{L^{2}(\nu_{n})}
. Uniform integrability of

f_{n}^{2}\log_{+}f_{n}^{2}
rules out loss of mass.
∎

Proposition 13.2

(The full Mosco passage for the
A
-LSI)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{A_{n},\nu_{n}}
converge in the Mosco sense to

\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu}
, and let
\nu_{n}\Rightarrow\nu
. Suppose that for all

n

\mathrm{H}(f^{2}\nu_{n}\|\nu_{n})\leq 2C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}\mathcal{E}_{A_{n},\nu_{n}}(f,f)

with the same constant, and that the recovery sequences can be chosen
with uniform control of the positive part of the entropy. Then

\mathrm{H}(f^{2}\nu\|\nu)\leq 2C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu}(f,f).

Proof.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu})
with
\int f^{2}d\nu=1
. By the Mosco
limsup condition there exists
f_{n}
such that
f_{n}\to f
and

\limsup_{n}\mathcal{E}_{A_{n},\nu_{n}}(f_{n},f_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu}(f,f).

Normalize
f_{n}
in
L^{2}(\nu_{n})
. By
Lemma
13.1
,

\mathrm{H}(f^{2}\nu\|\nu)\leq\liminf_{n}\mathrm{H}(f_{n}^{2}\nu_{n}\|\nu_{n}).

Applying the
A
-LSI to
f_{n}
, we obtain

\liminf_{n}\mathrm{H}(f_{n}^{2}\nu_{n}\|\nu_{n})\leq 2C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}\limsup_{n}\mathcal{E}_{A_{n},\nu_{n}}(f_{n},f_{n})\leq 2C_{\mathrm{LSI}}^{A}\mathcal{E}_{A,\nu}(f,f).

∎

Lemma 13.3

(Density of cylindrical truncations in the weighted form)
.

Suppose Assumption
7.1
holds. For

h=\log(\rho/\hat{\rho})
set
h_{R}=(-R)\vee h\wedge R
. Then there exist
cylindrical
h_{R,n}\in\mathcal{FC}_{b}^{\infty}
such that

h_{R,n}\to h_{R}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\rho\mu_{0}),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho}(h_{R,n}-h_{R},h_{R,n}-h_{R})\to 0.

Moreover,
\Gamma_{A}(h_{R},h_{R})\leq\Gamma_{A}(h,h)
in the sense of energies.

Proof.

The map
r\mapsto(-R)\vee r\wedge R
is
1
-Lipschitz. For a Markovian
Dirichlet form this yields energy contraction. The core property in
Assumption
7.1
provides approximation of
h_{R}
by cylindrical
functions simultaneously in
L^{2}(\rho\mu_{0})
and in the energy norm.
∎

Proposition 13.4

(Passage to the limit in the chain rule)
.

Suppose

\int_{0}^{T}J_{t}\,dt<\infty,\qquad\int_{0}^{T}\|v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t}\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},L^{2}(\nu_{t})}^{2}\,dt<\infty.

Then the identity of Theorem
7.2
, obtained first for

h_{t,R,n}
, passes to the limit
n\to\infty
,
R\to\infty
.

Proof.

The limit
n\to\infty
follows from Lemma
13.3
and
continuity of the weak forms in the energy norm. The drift term is estimated
by

\left|\int\langle v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t},\nabla(h_{t,R,n}-h_{t,R})\rangle\,d\nu_{t}\right|\leq\|v_{t}-\hat{v}_{t}\|_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},L^{2}(\nu_{t})}\mathcal{E}_{A,\rho_{t}}(h_{t,R,n}-h_{t,R})^{1/2}.

The passage
R\to\infty
in the dissipation follows from monotone
convergence of the truncation energies, and in the entropy from monotone
convergence of the function
r\log r
after localizing on the sets

\{|\log(\rho_{t}/\hat{\rho}_{t})|\leq R\}
.
∎

Lemma 14.1

(The Gronwall constant in the Galerkin estimate)

Suppose Assumptions
4.1
and
4.3
hold. Then in

4.4

.

From the proof of Theorem

Lemma 14.2

(Matching the analytic and statistical rates)
.

The Homogenization Limit of the Consistent

-Geometry

This section fixes the minimal homogenization layer needed to connect the
analytic theory with the grid tensors of Part II. We do not build a
new homogenization theory; we use the standard
H
-convergence of uniformly
elliptic tensors
[
21

]
and verify that the

-geometry is stable under this passage.

Assumption 15.1

(Homogenization convergence of the tensors)
.

Let
D_{N}(x)
be a sequence of self-adjoint positive operators acting on
discrete or piecewise-constant subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{N}\subset\mathcal{H}
, and suppose that,
after embedding into
\mathcal{H}
, they satisfy uniform
A
-compatibility:

c_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq\|Q^{1/2}D_{N}(x)^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq C_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}.

Assume that
D_{N}

H

D_{\mathrm{eff}}
in the sense of solutions of elliptic problems on
cylindrical projections: for every finite-dimensional cylindrical subspace

E\subset\mathcal{H}
, the restrictions
D_{N}|_{E}
converge to

D_{\mathrm{eff}}|_{E}
in the weak operator sense, and the fluxes converge

.

Theorem 15.2

(Weak convergence of the consistent forms)
.

Suppose Assumption
15.1
holds. Then the effective
tensor
D_{\mathrm{eff}}
satisfies the same

-compatibility:

c_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq\|Q^{1/2}D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq C_{A}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}.

Moreover, for all cylindrical

\mathcal{E}_{A_{N}}(u,v)=\int\langle Q^{1/2}D_{N}^{1/2}\nabla u,Q^{1/2}D_{N}^{1/2}\nabla v\rangle\,d\mu_{0}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}}(u,v)

in the weak sense of forms. In particular, any limiting estimates for the weak

.

Proof.

Fix cylindrical
u,v
. Their gradients take values in a finite-dimensional
space
E
. On this space the

-convergence assumption means weak
convergence of the fluxes

D_{N}^{1/2}\nabla u\rightharpoonup D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/2}\nabla u,\qquad D_{N}^{1/2}\nabla v\rightharpoonup D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/2}\nabla v

after applying the correctors. Since

is fixed and compact,

turns the weak convergence of the fluxes on
cylindrical subspaces into weak convergence of the corresponding

follows.

The two-sided

of the norm and by weak closedness of the convex set of operators satisfying
the prescribed quadratic inequalities. Hence

has the same

. Since all the estimates for the weak bridge and the
entropy dissipation in the previous sections depend only on these constants
and on the

∎

Assumption 15.3

(A quantitative homogenization estimate)
.

In addition to Assumption

Theorem 15.4

(Rate of convergence of the forms on cylindrical subspaces)

Suppose Assumptions
15.1
and

\left|\mathcal{E}_{A_{N}}(u,v)-\mathcal{E}_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}}(u,v)\right|\leq C_{E,Q}N^{-\alpha}\|u\|_{W_{Q}^{1,2}(\mu_{0})}\|v\|_{W_{Q}^{1,2}(\mu_{0})}.

If
R_{N}(\lambda)=(\lambda I+L_{A_{N}})^{-1}
and

R_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda)=(\lambda I+L_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}})^{-1}
, then for

f
in the cylindrical subspace generated by
E
,

\|R_{N}(\lambda)f-R_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0})}\leq C_{E,Q,\lambda}N^{-\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0})}.

Proof.

For
u,v
with gradients in
E
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{A_{N}}(u,v)-\mathcal{E}_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\int\left\langle Q^{1/2}(D_{N}^{1/2}-D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/2})\nabla u,Q^{1/2}D_{N}^{1/2}\nabla v\right\rangle d\mu_{0}

\displaystyle+\int\left\langle Q^{1/2}D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/2}\nabla u,Q^{1/2}(D_{N}^{1/2}-D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/2})\nabla v\right\rangle d\mu_{0}.

On the finite-dimensional space
E
, the functional calculus for positive
matrices and the quantitative estimate from
Assumption
15.3
give

\|(D_{N}^{1/2}-D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/2})\xi\|\leq C_{E}N^{-\alpha}\|\xi\|,\qquad\xi\in E,

with a change of constant depending on the lower elliptic bound. Since

Q^{1/2}
is fixed and the
D_{N}
are uniformly bounded, we obtain the
claimed estimate for the forms. The resolvent estimate follows from the
standard identity

R_{N}(\lambda)-R_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda)=R_{N}(\lambda)(L_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}}-L_{A_{N}})R_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda)

on the cylindrical subspace and from the coercivity of

\lambda I+L_{A}
.
∎

Corollary 15.5

(Convergence of the discrete
A
-constants)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
15.4
, for each
cylindrical subspace
E
,

|c_{A,N}(E)-c_{A}(E)|+|C_{A,N}(E)-C_{A}(E)|\leq C_{E}N^{-\alpha}.

Hence the numerical stabilization of
c_{A,N}
and
C_{A,N}
in Section 9 of
Part II has a quantitative explanation on each fixed set of modes.

Proof.

The extremal constants are the minimum and maximum of the Rayleigh quotients

\frac{\|Q^{1/2}D_{N}^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}}{\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}},\qquad\xi\in E\setminus\{0\}.

The estimate of Theorem
15.4
is uniform on the
unit sphere of the finite-dimensional
E
, whence the claimed estimate for
the extrema follows.
∎

Assumption 15.6

(Compact-tail control for the global rate)
.

In addition to Assumption
15.3
, suppose we are given a
sequence of orthogonal projections
\Pi_{M}
,
\Pi_{M}\to I
strongly in the
energy space
W_{Q}^{1,2}(\mu_{0})
. Assume that for some
r,\theta>0

C_{\Pi_{M},Q}\leq CM^{r},

where
C_{\Pi_{M},Q}
is the constant in the estimate of
Theorem
15.4
on the subspace
\Pi_{M}\mathcal{H}
,
and for all
u\in W_{Q}^{1,2}(\mu_{0})

\|(I-\Pi_{M})u\|_{W_{Q}^{1,2}(\mu_{0})}\leq CM^{-\theta}\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}},

where
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow W_{Q}^{1,2}(\mu_{0})
is a compactly embedded
regularity space.

Theorem 15.7

(A global rate in the strong resolvent topology)
.

Suppose Assumptions
15.1
,
15.3

and
15.6
hold. Then for
u,v\in\mathcal{K}

|\mathcal{E}_{A_{N}}(u,v)-\mathcal{E}_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}}(u,v)|\leq CN^{-\rho}\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{K}},\qquad\rho=\frac{\alpha\theta}{r+\theta}.

If
R_{N}(\lambda)=(\lambda I+L_{A_{N}})^{-1}
and
R_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda)=(\lambda I+L_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}})^{-1}
, then

\|R_{N}(\lambda)f-R_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0})}\leq C_{\lambda}N^{-\rho}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0})}

for all
f
for which
R_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda)f\in\mathcal{K}
, and also for
f
in the image of the compact regularizing class.

Proof.

Decompose
u=\Pi_{M}u+(I-\Pi_{M})u
,
v=\Pi_{M}v+(I-\Pi_{M})v
. On the
finite-dimensional block
\Pi_{M}\mathcal{H}
, Theorem
15.4
gives

|\mathcal{E}_{A_{N}}(\Pi_{M}u,\Pi_{M}v)-\mathcal{E}_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}}(\Pi_{M}u,\Pi_{M}v)|\leq CM^{r}N^{-\alpha}\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{K}}.

The remaining terms contain at least one tail factor. By coercivity and
boundedness of the forms,

|\mathcal{E}_{A}((I-\Pi_{M})u,v)|\leq CM^{-\theta}\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{K}}.

Hence

|\mathcal{E}_{A_{N}}(u,v)-\mathcal{E}_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}}(u,v)|\leq C(M^{r}N^{-\alpha}+M^{-\theta})\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{K}}.

The choice
M=N^{\alpha/(r+\theta)}
gives
\rho=\alpha\theta/(r+\theta)
.
The resolvent estimate follows from the variational identity for the solutions

(\lambda I+L_{A_{N}})u_{N}=f
,
(\lambda I+L_{A_{\mathrm{eff}}})u=f
,
substituting
u_{N}-u
as a test function, and the preceding estimate for the
forms.
∎

Corollary 15.8

(Stabilization of the discrete constants without a fixed set of modes)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
15.7
, if the unit
sphere of the energy space is approximated by a compact class
\mathcal{K}

with tail order

, then

|c_{A,N}-c_{A}|+|C_{A,N}-C_{A}|\leq CN^{-\rho}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tail}}(N),

Remark 15.9

(Why compactness is needed)
.

Without Assumption
15.6
the estimate of
Theorem
15.4
remains cylindrical. The passage to
the minimum and maximum over the entire unit sphere of the energy space is
impossible without compactness or tail control. This is why
Theorem
15.7
states precisely the additional
mechanism needed for the global resolvent rate.

Remark 15.10

(Connection with the numerical section of Part II)
.

Theorem
15.2
explains why, in the computational test, it

and

Lemma 15.11

(Verifying compact-tail control for the nanosystem model)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
Q=(-\Delta+I)^{-s}
,
s>d/2
, and let

\Pi_{M}
be the spectral projection onto the first
M
eigenfunctions of

-\Delta
. Then the space

\mathcal{K}=H^{s+1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

is compactly embedded in
W_{Q}^{1,2}(\mu_{0})
, and there exists
\theta>0

such that

\|(I-\Pi_{M})u\|_{W_{Q}^{1,2}(\mu_{0})}\leq CM^{-\theta}\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}}.

If the coefficients
D_{N}
are periodic piecewise-smooth tensors with uniform
elliptic bounds, then the constant
C_{\Pi_{M},Q}
in the cylindrical
homogenization estimate grows no faster than polynomially:

C_{\Pi_{M},Q}\leq CM^{r}
. In particular, in
Theorem
15.7
one may take

\rho=\alpha\theta/(r+\theta)>0
.

Proof.

In the Fourier basis the
W_{Q}^{1,2}
norm is equivalent to the weighted sum

\sum_{k}q_{k}|\widehat{\nabla u}_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{k}|\hat{u}_{k}|^{2}.

For
Q=(-\Delta+I)^{-s}
the weights are of order
(1+|k|^{2})^{-s}
. If

u\in H^{s+1}
, then the tail after the projection
\Pi_{M}
is bounded by
a power sum of Fourier coefficients, which gives the stated inequality with
some
\theta>0
. The embedding is compact because the Sobolev embedding between scales with a positive regularity gap is compact. The polynomial growth of
C_{\Pi_{M},Q}
follows from the fact that on
the finite-dimensional space of the first
M
modes all norms are
equivalent, while the coefficients
D_{N}
are uniformly elliptic and
piecewise smooth. The equivalence of norms gives at most a polynomial
dependence on the maximal wavenumber, and hence on
M
.
∎

Corollary 15.12

(Convergence of the forward SDEs in the homogenization limit)
.

Let
X_{t}^{(N)}
be the solution of the forward SDE with anisotropy
D_{N}
,
and
X_{t}^{\mathrm{eff}}
the solution with the effective tensor

D_{\mathrm{eff}}
. Suppose the hypotheses of
Theorem
15.7
hold and that

\|D_{N}^{1/2}(x)-D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/2}(x)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{Q}}\leq CN^{-\rho}

in the operator norm induced by
Q^{1/2}
, along trajectories with finite

\mathcal{K}
-moment. Then for each
T<\infty

\sup_{t\leq T}W_{2}(\operatorname{Law}(X_{t}^{(N)}),\operatorname{Law}(X_{t}^{\mathrm{eff}}))\leq C_{T}N^{-\rho}.

Proof.

The solutions are coupled through a single cylindrical Wiener process. For the
difference
Z_{t}=X_{t}^{(N)}-X_{t}^{\mathrm{eff}}
we apply Itô’s formula and
BDG:

\mathbb{E}\sup_{r\leq t}\|Z_{r}\|^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\sup_{q\leq s}\|Z_{q}\|^{2}ds+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|(D_{N}^{1/2}-D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/2})Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}ds.

The last integral is of order
N^{-2\rho}
by the assumption and the moment
estimate. Gronwall gives the mean-square estimate, and the inequality

W_{2}^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\|Z_{t}\|^{2}
completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 16.1

(The commutative benchmark)
.

Let
D
and
Q
be self-adjoint, positive, and commuting. Then for all
cylindrical
u
,

\Gamma_{A}(u,u)=\|Q^{1/2}D^{1/2}\nabla u\|^{2}=\langle DQ\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle.

If, in addition,
d_{-}I\leq D\leq d_{+}I
, then

d_{-}\|Q^{1/2}\nabla u\|^{2}\leq\Gamma_{A}(u,u)\leq d_{+}\|Q^{1/2}\nabla u\|^{2}.

Proof.

Commutativity gives
D^{1/2}Q^{1/2}=Q^{1/2}D^{1/2}
, and hence

(Q^{1/2}D^{1/2})^{*}(Q^{1/2}D^{1/2})=D^{1/2}QD^{1/2}=DQ.

The two-sided estimate follows from the spectral order for
D
.
∎

Proposition 16.2

(The noncommutative correction)
.

Let
[D,Q]\neq 0
. Then the expressions

\|Q^{1/2}D^{1/2}\xi\|^{2},\qquad\|D^{1/2}Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}

are in general different. Therefore the form consistent with the SDE

\sigma=D^{1/2}Q^{1/2}
is defined through
\sigma^{*}=Q^{1/2}D^{1/2}
, and
not through the transposed expression.

Proof.

The carré du champ of a diffusion with coefficient
\sigma
equals

\Gamma(u,u)=\|\sigma^{*}\nabla u\|^{2}.

For
\sigma=D^{1/2}Q^{1/2}
we have
\sigma^{*}=Q^{1/2}D^{1/2}
. If this
expression is replaced by
D^{1/2}Q^{1/2}
, one obtains the form
corresponding to a different noise operator
Q^{1/2}D^{1/2}
. Under
noncommutativity these operators are distinct, and hence so are the associated
forms.
∎

Remark 16.3

(Interpretation)
.

Propositions
16.1
–
16.2

show that the new geometry is not a cosmetic relabeling of the classical case.
In the commutative limit it returns the usual diagonal theory; in the
noncommutative case it selects the unique order of operators compatible with
the generator of the forward SDE and with the backward entropy identity. The
numerical test of Part II gives a concrete illustration: for

N=128
, for the chosen noncommutative cell,
\min d_{i}\simeq 1.00
,

\max d_{i}\simeq 2.10
, whereas the operator constants from Table 1 of the
second part are
c_{A,N}=1.001
and
C_{A,N}=2.231
. Hence the

A
-constants cannot be replaced by the Euclidean spectrum of the tensor
alone.
