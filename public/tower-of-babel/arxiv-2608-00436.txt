Matter-Induced CPT Violation and Earth-Density Stratification Effects in Long-Baseline Neutrino Oscillation Experiments
Tia Pandit, Bipin Singh Koranga, Vivek Kumar Nautiyal

Abstract
We present a unified analysis of two matter-potential systematics in long-baseline (LBL) neutrino oscillation experiments, comprising—to our knowledge—one of the first systematic studies to treat matter-induced extrinsic CPT violation and Earth density stratification as correlated systematics within a single χ 2 \chi^{2} framework, including their joint impact on CP-phase reconstruction and hierarchy dependence. 2. Theoretical Framework

2.1. PMNS Mixing and Vacuum Hamiltonian

The three neutrino flavour states
|\nu_{\alpha}\rangle
are quantum
superpositions of mass eigenstates
|\nu_{i}\rangle
via the PMNS
matrix
[
14
]
:

|\nu_{\alpha}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}U_{\alpha i}\,|\nu_{i}\rangle,\quad\alpha=e,\mu,\tau,

(1)

parametrised by mixing angles
(\theta_{12},\theta_{23},\theta_{13})
,
Dirac phase
\delta_{CP}
, and Majorana phases
(a_{1},a_{2})
.
In the relativistic limit the vacuum Hamiltonian in the flavour basis is

H_{\rm vac}^{(f)}=\frac{1}{2E}\,U\,\mathrm{diag}\!\bigl(0,\Delta m^{2}_{21},\Delta m^{2}_{31}\bigr)\,U^{\dagger}.

(2)

2.2. MSW Matter Potential and Unified Hamiltonian

Coherent charged-current forward scattering of
\nu_{e}
off ambient
electrons induces the effective potential
[
5
]

V_{f}(x)=\sqrt{2}\,G_{F}\,N_{e}(x)\,\mathrm{diag}(1,0,0),\qquad N_{e}(x)=\frac{Y_{e}(x)\,\rho(x)}{m_{p}},

(3)

where
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant,
Y_{e}\approx 0.494
is the electron
fraction, and
\rho(x)
is the local Earth density from the
prem
profile.
For antineutrinos
V_{f}\to-V_{f}
. The total flavour Hamiltonian is

H_{f}(x)=H_{\rm vac}^{(f)}+V_{f}(x),

(4)

and the flavour state obeys
i\,d|\nu(x)\rangle/dx=H_{f}(x)|\nu(x)\rangle
.
The propagator over a spatial step
\Delta x
is computed via exact matrix
exponentiation

U_{\rm step}=\exp(-i\,H_{f}(x)\,\Delta x)

and accumulated piecewise along the neutrino chord, using
n=300
–
400

equally spaced steps per trajectory.

2.3. Extrinsic CPT-Violating Asymmetry

In vacuum, CPT invariance guarantees
P_{\mu e}=P_{\bar{e}\bar{\mu}}
exactly. In matter
the opposite sign of
V_{f}
for antineutrinos breaks this equality.
The extrinsic CPT asymmetry is defined as
[
7
]

A^{CPT}_{\mu e}=\frac{P_{\mu e}-P_{\bar{e}\bar{\mu}}}{P_{\mu e}+P_{\bar{e}\bar{\mu}}}.

(5)

The ratio form cancels many correlated experimental uncertainties (flux,
cross-section normalisation) at the level of event rates. By construction

A^{CPT}_{\mu e}\equiv 0
in vacuum, so any non-zero measurement is purely extrinsic.

4.3.
\chi^{2}
Statistic and
\Delta\chi^{2}
Profile Analysis

To quantify the bias introduced by the constant-density approximation
we define the Poisson log-likelihood ratio
\chi^{2}
statistic.
Let
N^{\mathrm{true}}_{i}
denote the true event rate in energy bin
i
,
generated with the full
prem
profile at
\delta_{CP}^{\rm true}=-90^{\circ}
,
and let
N^{\mathrm{test}}_{i}(\delta_{CP},\boldsymbol{\xi})
denote the test event rate
computed with the constant-density model at trial
\delta_{CP}
and nuisance
parameters
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
(flux
normalisation, cross-section uncertainty, and detector efficiency).
The event rates are obtained by weighting the appearance probability

P(\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e};E_{i})
by a quasi-realistic beam flux

\phi(E_{i})\propto e^{-E_{i}/3}
, a linear neutrino–nucleon cross
section
\sigma(E_{i})\propto E_{i}
, and a flat detector efficiency
of 80%. The
\chi^{2}
is then

\chi^{2}(\delta_{CP},\boldsymbol{\xi})=2\sum_{c\in\{\nu,\bar{\nu}\}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm bins}}\Bigl[N^{\mathrm{test}}_{i,c}(\delta_{CP},\boldsymbol{\xi})\,-\,N^{\mathrm{true}}_{i,c}\,-\,N^{\mathrm{true}}_{i,c}\,\ln\!\frac{N^{\mathrm{test}}_{i,c}(\delta_{CP},\boldsymbol{\xi})}{N^{\mathrm{true}}_{i,c}}\Bigr]\;+\;\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\xi_{j}^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}},

(8)

where the outer sum runs over both neutrino (
c=\nu
) and antineutrino
(
c=\bar{\nu}
) channels—including both is essential to break the

\delta_{CP}
–matter-effect degeneracy
[
19
]
—and the pull-term
sum imposes Gaussian priors
\sigma_{j}
on each nuisance parameter
(
\sigma_{\rm flux}=5\%
,
\sigma_{\rm xsec}=5\%
,

\sigma_{\rm eff}=2\%
). The inner sum runs over
N_{\rm bins}=9

energy bins in
[0.4,4.0]
GeV. This is the standard Poisson
log-likelihood ratio
[
18
]
; under the null hypothesis its
asymptotic distribution is
\chi^{2}
with
N_{\rm bins}-1-N_{\rm syst}

degrees of freedom. The profile statistic

\Delta\chi^{2}(\delta_{CP})=\chi^{2}(\delta_{CP},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\delta_{CP}))-\chi^{2}_{\min},

(9)

where
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\delta_{CP})
is the nuisance-parameter profiled
value at each trial
\delta_{CP}
, traces the bias introduced by the
constant-density approximation through the displacement of its minimum
from the true
\delta_{CP}^{\rm true}=-90^{\circ}
.

\Delta\chi^{2}(\delta_{CP})

profiles for T2K, NO
\nu
A, and

Both systematics enter the same Hamiltonian
H_{f}(x)
through the

, so an error in
\rho(x)

propagates simultaneously into the

5.1. Extended
\chi^{2}
with Density Pull Term

For completeness—and anticipating future very-long-baseline
analyses—we extend the
\chi^{2}
of Eq. (
8
) to include
an explicit density nuisance parameter
\xi_{\rho}
:

\chi^{2}_{\rm ext}(\delta_{CP},\boldsymbol{\xi},\xi_{\rho})=\chi^{2}(\delta_{CP},\boldsymbol{\xi})\;+\;\frac{\xi_{\rho}^{2}}{\sigma_{\rho}^{2}},

(10)

where
\sigma_{\rho}=2\%
(conservative geophysical prior) or

\sigma_{\rho}=5\%
(envelope for deep-mantle trajectories).
The test density used in
N^{\mathrm{test}}
is
\rho\to\rho(1+\xi_{\rho})
,
with the same shift applied simultaneously to the
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
computation,
ensuring neutrino–antineutrino consistency. Profiling over

\xi_{\rho}
widens the
\Delta\chi^{2}
well but does not shift
its minimum for current LBL baselines, as confirmed by the
quasi-linearity shown in Table
4
. For VLB
proposals this additional pull term is mandatory: the density
uncertainty couples into both the
\delta_{CP}
bias and the
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}

estimate simultaneously, and treating it as uncorrelated would
underestimate the total systematic.

For current LBL experiments
(T2K, NO
\nu
A,
dune
, Hyper-K):
the constant-density approximation is adequate for both analyses.
The
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
values of Table
3
should be subtracted
from the measured
\nu
–
\bar{\nu}
asymmetry using the path-averaged
density; the hierarchy dependence (Section
3.4
)
introduces an additional
\sim 5
–
9\%
uncertainty that should be
marginalised over in the fit. The density uncertainty contributes
an additional
\sim 1.3\%
systematic (Table
4
).

For proposed VLB facilities
(
L\gtrsim 5000
km): both
systematics become dominant simultaneously. The recommended procedure
is: (a) replace constant-density profiles in
globes
-based frameworks
with spatially resolved
prem
profiles; (b) compute
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
with
the same
prem
profile to ensure neutrino–antineutrino consistency;
and (c) propagate geophysical density uncertainties as correlated
systematics in both the
\delta_{CP}
fit (Eq.
10
) and the

A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
estimate jointly.

The present analysis complements and extends three threads in the
existing literature.

Matter-induced CPT violation.
Jacobson & Ohlsson
[
9
]

established the formalism for extrinsic CPT violation in the two-flavour
approximation and computed the asymmetry for atmospheric neutrinos;

LBL experiments and adds hierarchy dependence. Banuls, Barenboim &
Bernabéu
[
7
]
and Randhawa et al.
[
13
]

treated

without addressing density stratification; our

4
provides the missing link between their
CPT predictions and the geophysical uncertainty literature.

Earth density-profile effects.
Kelly & Parke
[
12
]

demonstrated that density-profile variations at DUNE shift

by a few percent at the oscillation maximum, using GLoBES-based

\chi^{2}
analysis recovers their conclusion that
the effect is sub-dominant at

result that the same density variations modify
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
at the 1–4%
level. Pandit & Koranga
[
11
]
quantified the
\delta_{CP}

reconstruction bias from the constant-density approximation across a
wide baseline range; the present paper identifies the correlated CPT
systematic that must be treated simultaneously in any VLB analysis.

DUNE sensitivity studies.
The DUNE Collaboration’s
conceptual design report
[
3
]
targets
|\Delta\delta_{CP}|<5^{\circ}

after ten years of running. Our results confirm that neither the CPT
asymmetry subtraction (requiring
\sim 9\%
hierarchy marginalisation
and
\sim 1.3\%
density marginalisation) nor the constant-density bias
(
0.3^{\circ}
) are limiting systematics for the baseline DUNE programme.
However, any proposed Phase III extension to longer baselines or

).

6. Conclusions

We have presented, to our knowledge, one of the first systematic
analyses treating matter-induced CPT violation and Earth density
stratification as
correlated
systematics in LBL neutrino
oscillation experiments. Our principal conclusions are:

1.

A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
at real experiments (Table
3
).

The extrinsic CPT asymmetry ranges from
0.018
(JUNO-TAO) to
0.180

(
dune
) at the respective peak energies under NH. Exact and analytic
approximate values agree to within
\lesssim 2\%
, validating the
second-order expansion in
\alpha
and
s_{13}
.

2.

3D CPT surface.
The surface
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}(E,\delta_{CP})
at

L=1285
km reveals two regimes: at
E\gtrsim 1.5
GeV (the
dune
operating region)
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
is insensitive to
\delta_{CP}
, enabling
straightforward subtraction; at
E\lesssim 0.5
GeV the solar term
couples
\delta_{CP}
into
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
, requiring a joint CP–CPT likelihood
analysis.

3.

Hierarchy dependence.
The NH–IH difference in
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}

is
\sim 9\%
at
dune
and
\lesssim 1\%
at T2K/Hyper-K. A
misidentified hierarchy introduces a
\sim 9\%
systematic in the

A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
subtraction at
dune

or marginalised over in precision fits.

4.

Matter-density uncertainty on
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
.
A fractional
density shift
\varepsilon=\pm 5\%
induces
|\Delta A_{CPT}|\lesssim 0.008
(
\sim 4\%
relative) at
dune

definition of
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
partially self-cancels density errors.
The geophysical prior
\sigma_{\rho}\approx 2\%
contributes

\sigma_{A_{CPT}}\approx 0.0023
, a
1.3\%
systematic on
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}
.

5.

PREM bias is negligible at current baselines.
For all
operating and approved experiments the constant-density bias is

|\Delta\delta_{CP}|<0.3^{\circ}
, well below current precision.

6.

PREM bias is catastrophic at very long baselines.

Beyond
L\approx 5000
km the bias grows to
17.8^{\circ}
at

L=7000
km (
3.6\times
the
dune
Phase II
1\sigma
target) and

172.2^{\circ}
at
L=12000

7.

Joint treatment via rigorous
\chi^{2}
.
The extended
Poisson log-likelihood ratio statistic of Eq. (
10
),
with nuisance pull terms for flux, cross-section, efficiency, and
density, summed over both
\nu
and
\bar{\nu}
channels, provides the
correct framework for future analyses.
For VLB proposals, both the test spectra
N^{\mathrm{test}}
and the
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}

estimate must use the same
prem
-resolved density profile, with
geophysical uncertainties propagated as correlated systematics.

In summary, for the current experimental programme (DUNE, T2K, Hyper-K)
the constant-density approximation is sufficient for both CP and CPT
analyses, and the matter-density uncertainty contributes only a

\sim 1.3\%
systematic on
A^{CPT}_{\mu e}

constraints. For future very-long-baseline proposals, Earth density
stratification and matter-induced CPT violation are not independent
refinements but two faces of the same dominant systematic and must be
addressed together using the rigorous correlated statistical framework
presented here. The formalism is directly applicable to proposed
next-generation facilities such as the P2O project (Protvino–ORCA,

L\approx 2600
km), any future Hyper-K extension, and envisioned
transcontinental baselines (
L>5000
km) where PREM-level simulation
