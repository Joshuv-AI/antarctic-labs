Rota–Baxter Operators on Truncated Polynomial Algebras
Azhar Farooq

Abstract
Let K K be a field of characteristic zero and let 𝔪 = ( x 1 , … , x n ) \mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}) be a maximal ideal of the polynomial ring R = K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] . We classify all Rota–Baxter operators of weight zero and weight one on the truncated polynomial algebra R = K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] / 𝔪 2 R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\mathfrak{m}^{2} . For weight zero we show that such operators are exactly the linear maps P P satisfying P 2 = 0 P^{2}=0 and Im ⁡ ( P ) ⊆ 𝔪 / 𝔪 2 \im(P)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2} . For non - zero weight a standard rescaling reduces the problem to weight one. Weight one operators split into two disjoint families determined by the scalar P ⁡ ( 1 ) ∈ { 0 , − 1 } P(1)\in\{0,-1\} ; on the maximal ideal they act as an endomorphism L L with L 2 + L = 0 L^{2}+L=0 , i.e. − L -L is an idempotent. Each family is isomorphic to the variety of idempotent matrices.



1
Introduction

Rota–Baxter operators were first introduced by Glen Baxter in the context of probability theory
[
1
]
and later studied by Gian-Carlo Rota and others as algebraic tools in combinatorics and mathematical physics
[
2
]
. Recall that for an associative
K
-algebra
A
and a scalar
\lambda\in K
, a
Rota–Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
is a
K
-linear map
P:A\to A
satisfying the identity

P(x)P(y)\;=\;P\bigl(xP(y)+P(x)y+\lambda\,xy\bigr)

(1)

for all
x,y\in A
. Classic examples include the integral operator (of weight 0) on continuous functions, whose integration-by-parts formula encodes the Rota–Baxter relation. The case
\lambda=0
and
\lambda=1
are of special interest, since any nonzero weight can be rescaled to 1
[
11
]
. Rota–Baxter algebras have rich connections to combinatorics and physics: for instance, the classical Spitzer identity in probability theory is naturally derived using a Rota–Baxter structure, and the Connes–Kreimer theory of renormalization in perturbative quantum field theory revealed that the decomposition of Feynman graphs is governed by a Rota–Baxter operator
[
3
,
5
,
6
]
. There are also links to dendriform and shuffle algebras
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, as well as to solutions of the classical Yang–Baxter equation.

The structure and classification of Rota–Baxter operators have been extensively studied on various algebras (see
[
11
]
for a survey). In particular, one encounters Rota–Baxter operators on polynomial algebras, free (noncommutative) algebras, and matrix or semigroup algebras, among others
[
16
,
12
,
4
,
15
,
13
,
14
,
10
]
. In this paper we initiate the study of Rota–Baxter operators on
truncated polynomial algebras
. Concretely, let

R\;=\;K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\mathfrak{m}^{2},\qquad\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

where
K
is a field of characteristic zero. Then
R
decomposes as
K\oplus V
with
V=\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}\cong K^{n}
and
V^{2}=0
. Algebras of the form
K\oplus V
with
V^{2}=0
play a fundamental role in deformation theory and geometry, encoding first-order neighbourhoods of points. We classify all Rota–Baxter operators of weight
0
and
1
on
R
.

Our main results can be summarized as follows:

1.

Weight 0.
We prove that a
K
-linear map
P:R\to R
is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
0
if and only if
P^{2}=0
and
\im(P)\subseteq V
(Theorem
3.4
). Equivalently, choosing the basis
(1,\bar{x}_{1},\dots,\bar{x}_{n})
of
R
, the matrix of
P
has the block form

\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
v_{0}&L\end{pmatrix},

where
v_{0}\in V
and
L\in\End_{K}(V)
satisfy
L^{2}=0
and
L\,v_{0}=0
. In other words,
P
projects into the nilpotent ideal
V
and is itself nilpotent.

2.

Weight 1.
For a nonzero weight operator we rescale to
\lambda=1
(by Lemma
4.1
). We show that any Rota–Baxter operator
P
of weight
1
on
R
must satisfy
P(1)\in\{0,-1\}
(Theorem
4.3
). Moreover, in either case
P
induces an endomorphism
L=P|_{V}
on
V
satisfying
L^{2}+L=0
(so
-L
is idempotent). Conversely, given any scalar
\lambda\in\{0,-1\}
and any idempotent
Q\in\End_{K}(V)
, the formula

P(a+u)\;=\;\lambda\,a\;-\;Q(u)\qquad(a\in K,\ u\in V)

defines a Rota–Baxter operator of weight 1 on
R
. Thus the set of weight 1 operators splits into two disjoint families (depending on
\lambda=P(1)
), each parametrized by idempotent matrices. In particular, the variety of weight-1 operators is isomorphic to the disjoint union of two copies of the variety of idempotent
n\times n
matrices (Corollary
4.4
).

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review basic properties of the truncated polynomial algebra
R
. Section
3
contains the proof of the weight 0 classification, and Section
4
treats the weight 1 case. Our arguments are essentially elementary linear algebra once one writes linear maps on
R
in the basis
(1,\bar{x}_{1},\dots,\bar{x}_{n})
.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout,
K
is a field of characteristic zero.
For
n\geq 1
we set
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, and

R=S/\mathfrak{m}^{2}.

Denote by
\bar{x}_{i}
the image of
x_{i}
in
R
. Every element of
R
can be written uniquely as

a+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\bar{x}_{i},\qquad a,b_{i}\in K.

Thus
R=K\oplus V
where
V=\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
is the
n
-dimensional vector space with basis
\bar{x}_{1},\dots,\bar{x}_{n}
. Multiplication satisfies

(a+u)(b+v)=ab+av+bu,\qquad a,b\in K,\;u,v\in V,

so
V\cdot V=0
. We identify
K
with the subring of scalar multiples of the unit
1
.

For a
K
-vector space
W
,
\End_{K}(W)
denotes the algebra of
K
-linear endomorphisms of
W
.
A projection on
V
is an idempotent
Q\in\End_{K}(V)
, i.e.
Q^{2}=Q
.

3
Weight zero Rota–Baxter operators

In this section we classify all Rota–Baxter operators of weight
\lambda=0
on
R
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
K
-linear map
P:R\to R
is a
Rota–Baxter operator of weight zero
if

P(x)P(y)=P\bigl(xP(y)+P(x)y\bigr)\qquad\forall x,y\in R.

(2)

Lemma 3.2
.

If
P
satisfies (
2
), then
\im(P)\subseteq V
. In particular
P(1)\in V
and
P(V)\subseteq V
.

Proof.

Write
P(1)=\alpha+v_{0}
with
\alpha\in K
,
v_{0}\in V
.
Using
x=y=1
in (
2
):

P(1)^{2}=P(2P(1))=2P(P(1)).

Expanding the left side gives
\alpha^{2}+2\alpha v_{0}
, while the right side is
2\alpha P(1)+2P(v_{0})=2\alpha^{2}+2\alpha v_{0}+2P(v_{0})
.
Cancelling
2\alpha v_{0}
we obtain

\alpha^{2}=2\alpha^{2}+2P(v_{0})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0=\alpha^{2}+2P(v_{0}).

(3)

Thus
2P(v_{0})=-\alpha^{2}\in K
, so
P(v_{0})
is a scalar.

Now use
x=v_{0},\;y=1
:

P(v_{0})P(1)=P\bigl(v_{0}P(1)+P(v_{0})\bigr).

Since
P(v_{0})=c\in K
, the left side is
c(\alpha+v_{0})=c\alpha+cv_{0}
.
On the right,
v_{0}P(1)+c=v_{0}(\alpha+v_{0})+c=\alpha v_{0}+c
because
v_{0}^{2}=0
.
Applying
P
yields
\alpha P(v_{0})+P(c)=\alpha c+cP(1)=\alpha c+c(\alpha+v_{0})=2\alpha c+cv_{0}
.
Equating the two sides gives
c\alpha+cv_{0}=2\alpha c+cv_{0}
, hence
c\alpha=2\alpha c
, i.e.
\alpha c=0
in
K
.
So either
\alpha=0
or
c=0
. If
c=0
, then from
c=-\alpha^{2}/2
(from
3
after dividing by
2
) we obtain
\alpha^{2}=0
, hence
\alpha=0
anyway.
Thus
\alpha=0
and then
3
gives
2P(v_{0})=0
, so
P(v_{0})=0
.
Therefore
P(1)=v_{0}\in V
and
P(P(1))=0
.

Now for any
u,v\in V
, write
P(u)=\beta+u^{\prime}
,
P(v)=\gamma+v^{\prime}
with
\beta,\gamma\in K
,
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in V
.
Using (
2
) with
x=u,y=v
:

P(u)P(v)=\beta\gamma+\beta v^{\prime}+\gamma u^{\prime},

while the right side is
P\bigl(uP(v)+P(u)v\bigr)=P(\beta v+\gamma u)=\beta P(v)+\gamma P(u)=2\beta\gamma+\beta v^{\prime}+\gamma u^{\prime}
.
Hence
\beta\gamma=2\beta\gamma
, so
\beta\gamma=0
. Taking
u=v
gives
\beta^{2}=0
, so
\beta=0
.
Thus
P(V)\subseteq V
.
Since every
x\in R
is
a+u
with
a\in K
,
u\in V
, we have
P(x)=aP(1)+P(u)\in V
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

If
P
satisfies (
2
), then
P^{2}=0
.

Proof.

We already know
P(P(1))=0
from the previous proof. For any
v\in V
, use
x=v
,
y=1
in (
2
):

P(v)P(1)=P\bigl(vP(1)+P(v)\bigr).

Since
P(1),P(v)\in V
and
V^{2}=0
, the left side is
0
. On the right,
vP(1)=0
, so the argument of
P
is just
P(v)
. Hence
0=P(P(v))
, so
P^{2}(v)=0
. Together with
P^{2}(1)=0
, linearity gives
P^{2}=0
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
R=S/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
be as above. A
K
-linear map
P:R\to R
is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight zero if and only if
\im(P)\subseteq V
and
P^{2}=0
.

Proof.

Necessity is Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
.
For sufficiency, assume
\im(P)\subseteq V
and
P^{2}=0
. For any
x,y\in R
, write them as
x=a+u
,
y=b+v
. Then
P(x),P(y)\in V
, so their product is
0
.
On the other hand,

xP(y)+P(x)y=(a+u)P(y)+P(x)(b+v)=aP(y)+bP(x)

because
uP(y)=P(x)v=0
(product in
V
).
Applying
P
gives
aP^{2}(y)+bP^{2}(x)=0
. Hence the Rota–Baxter identity holds.
∎

3.1
Matrix form and parameterisation

With respect to the basis
1,\bar{x}_{1},\dots,\bar{x}_{n}
of
R
, any linear map
P
with
\im(P)\subseteq V
has a matrix of the form

P=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\[2.0pt]
v_{0}&L\end{pmatrix},

(4)

where
v_{0}\in K^{n}
is the column vector
(p_{10},\dots,p_{n0})^{T}
and
L=(p_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in M_{n}(K)
.
The condition
P^{2}=0
translates to

L^{2}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad Lv_{0}=0.

Thus the set of weight
-
zero Rota–Baxter operators is in bijection with

\mathcal{RB}_{0}=\{(v_{0},L)\in V\times\End_{K}(V)\mid L^{2}=0,\;Lv_{0}=0\}.

Example 3.5
.

For
n=3
, the matrix of a weight
-
zero Rota–Baxter operator is

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
a_{1}&\ell_{11}&\ell_{12}&\ell_{13}\\
a_{2}&\ell_{21}&\ell_{22}&\ell_{23}\\
a_{3}&\ell_{31}&\ell_{32}&\ell_{33}\end{pmatrix}

with
L^{2}=0
and
L(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})^{T}=0
.

4
Weight one Rota–Baxter operators

For a Rota–Baxter operator with
\lambda\neq 0
, the following simple rescaling recovers the weight
-
one case.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
11
]

Let
P
be a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
\lambda\neq 0
on an algebra
A
. Then
Q=\lambda^{-1}P
is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
1
.

Hence the classification of Rota–Baxter operators of any non
-
zero weight immediately follows from that of weight one.
We now classify Rota–Baxter operators of weight
\lambda=1
on
R
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
P:R\to R
is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight one, then
P|_{V}
maps
V
to
V
, and
P(1)\in\{0,-1\}
. Moreover, for all
u\in V
,
P(u)=L(u)
with
L\in\End_{K}(V)
satisfying
L^{2}+L=0
.

Proof.

Write
P(1)=\alpha+v_{0}
with
\alpha\in K
,
v_{0}\in V
, and for
u\in V
write
P(u)=f(u)+L(u)
where
f:V\to K
is linear and
L\in\End_{K}(V)
.

First take
x,y\in V
. The weight
-
one identity (
1
) with
\lambda=1
gives

P(x)P(y)=P\bigl(xP(y)+P(x)y+xy\bigr).

Since
V^{2}=0
, we have
xy=0
,
xL(y)=0
, and
L(x)y=0
. Then

LHS

\displaystyle=(f(x)+L(x))(f(y)+L(y))=f(x)f(y)+f(x)L(y)+f(y)L(x),

RHS

\displaystyle=P\bigl(f(y)x+f(x)y\bigr)=f(y)P(x)+f(x)P(y)

\displaystyle=2f(x)f(y)+f(y)L(x)+f(x)L(y).

Equating scalar parts yields
f(x)f(y)=2f(x)f(y)
, hence
f(x)f(y)=0
for all
x,y
. A non
-
zero linear functional would have
f(u_{0})=1
for some
u_{0}
, then
f(u_{0})^{2}=1
, contradiction. Thus
f=0
and
P(V)\subseteq V
.

Now set
x=y=1
in (
1
):

P(1)^{2}=P(2P(1)+1).

Using
P(1)=\alpha+v_{0}
and
P|_{V}=L
,

LHS

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}+2\alpha v_{0},

RHS

\displaystyle=P(2\alpha+2v_{0}+1)=(2\alpha+1)P(1)+2P(v_{0})

\displaystyle=(2\alpha+1)(\alpha+v_{0})+2L(v_{0})=2\alpha^{2}+\alpha+(2\alpha+1)v_{0}+2L(v_{0}).

Comparing components gives

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}+\alpha\;\Longrightarrow\;\alpha(\alpha+1)=0,

\displaystyle 2\alpha v_{0}

\displaystyle=(2\alpha+1)v_{0}+2L(v_{0})\;\Longrightarrow\;L(v_{0})=-\tfrac{1}{2}v_{0}.

(B)

So
\alpha\in\{0,-1\}
.

Next take
x=u\in V
,
y=1
:

P(u)P(1)=P\bigl(uP(1)+P(u)+u\bigr).

Compute:

LHS

\displaystyle=L(u)(\alpha+v_{0})=\alpha L(u),

RHS

\displaystyle=P\bigl(u(\alpha+v_{0})+L(u)+u\bigr)=P\bigl(\alpha u+L(u)+u\bigr)

\displaystyle=(\alpha+1)L(u)+L^{2}(u).

Hence
\alpha L(u)=(\alpha+1)L(u)+L^{2}(u)
, i.e.
L^{2}+L=0
on
V
.

Finally, apply
L
to Equation (B):

L^{2}(v_{0})=-\tfrac{1}{2}L(v_{0})=\tfrac{1}{4}v_{0}.

But
L^{2}=-L
, so
L^{2}(v_{0})=-L(v_{0})=\tfrac{1}{2}v_{0}
. Thus
\tfrac{1}{4}v_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}v_{0}
, forcing
v_{0}=0
. Hence
P(1)=\alpha\in\{0,-1\}
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

A
K
-linear map
P:R\to R
is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight one if and only if there exist
\alpha\in\{0,-1\}
and an idempotent
Q\in\End_{K}(V)
(i.e.
Q^{2}=Q
) such that

P(a+u)=\alpha a-Q(u)\qquad\forall a\in K,\;u\in V.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.2
we have
P(1)=\alpha\in\{0,-1\}
and
P|_{V}=L
with
L^{2}+L=0
. Define
Q=-L
; then
Q^{2}=(-L)^{2}=L^{2}=-L=Q
, so
Q
is idempotent. Conversely, given
\alpha\in\{0,-1\}
and an idempotent
Q
, set
P(a+u)=\alpha a-Q(u)
. One verifies the weight
-
one identity by a direct computation similar to the weight
-
zero case, using that
V^{2}=0
and the property
Q^{2}=Q
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

The set
\mathcal{RB}_{1}
of weight
-
one Rota–Baxter operators on
R
is isomorphic to the disjoint union of two copies of the variety of idempotent matrices in
M_{n}(K)
:

\mathcal{RB}_{1}\cong\bigl(\{0\}\times\mathcal{P}\bigr)\sqcup\bigl(\{-1\}\times\mathcal{P}\bigr),

where
\mathcal{P}=\{Q\in M_{n}(K)\mid Q^{2}=Q\}
.

Proof.

The isomorphism is immediate from Theorem
4.3
.
∎
