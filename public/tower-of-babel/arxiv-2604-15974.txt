A note on a subclass of Bazilevič functions
Lokenath Thakur

Abstract
. In this artcle, we introduce and investigate a subclass of Bazilevič functions, denoted by ℬ φ A , B ​ ( α ( m ) ) \mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)}) . We determine the Hardy space to which this subclass of Bazilevič functions belong to. Additionally, we provide a necessary condition for a particular case of this subclass. Finally, we obtain a sharp coefficient estimate for the functions associated with ℬ 1 ​ ( α ) . \mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha).

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of all analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{~z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and let
\mathcal{A}
denote the subclass of
\mathcal{H}
with functions of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.

If
g(z)\in\mathcal{A}
is starlike (with respect to the origin) in
\mathbb{D}
,
P(z)\in\mathcal{A}
is regular with
\text{Re }P(z)>0
in
\mathbb{D}
,
\beta
is any real number and
\alpha>0
, then

(1.2)

\displaystyle f(z)=\left[(\alpha+i\beta)\int_{0}^{z}P(\zeta)g^{\alpha}(\zeta)\zeta^{i\beta-1}d\zeta\right]^{1/(\alpha+i\beta)}

has been shown by Bazilevič
[
1
]
to be a regular and univalent function in
\mathbb{D}
. The powers appearing in the formula are meant to be principal values. We denote by
\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{P},g)
the class of functions defined by (
1.2
). Let
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{S}^{*}
,
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{C}~~\text{and}~~\mathcal{P}
denote the subclasses of univalent, starlike, convex, close-to-convex and Caratheodory functions, respectively (For these classes, see
[
2
]
for instance). It is well known that the inclusion relations
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}\subset\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{P},g)\subset\mathcal{S}
are valid. So far, Bazilevič functions form the largest subclass of
\mathcal{S}
. For
\beta=0
, the class
\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{P},g)
is simply denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\mathcal{P},g)
.

For analytic functions
g
and
h
in
\mathbb{D}
,
g
is said to be subordinate to
h
if there exists an analytic function
\omega
such that

\omega(0)=0,\quad|\omega(z)|<1\quad\text{and}\quad g(z)=h(\omega(z))\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

This subordination will be denoted by
g\prec h
or, conventionally, by

g(z)\prec h(z).

In particular, when
h
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
,
g\prec h
if and only if

g(0)=h(0)\quad\text{and}\quad g(\mathbb{D})\subset h(\mathbb{D}).

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the class of zero-free analytic functions
\varphi
in
\mathbb{D}
with the normalization condition
\varphi(0)=1
. Then, following the earlier work by Ma and Minda
[
4
]
, Kim and Sugawa
[
3
]
introduced the subclasses
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{K}(\varphi)
of
\mathcal{A}
as the sets of functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying, respectively, the following subordination conditions:

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\varphi(z)

and

1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\varphi(z),

for each
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
. By definition, it is clear that

f\in\mathcal{K}(\varphi)\iff zf^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi).

We also note that

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{K}(\varphi)\subset\mathcal{K}(\psi)\quad(\varphi\prec\psi).

Next, for
A
and
B
such that
-1\leq B<A\leq 1
, let us define the Möbius transformation
\varphi_{A,B}
by

\varphi_{A,B}(z)=\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}.

Let
m
be a positive integer,
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in(0,+\infty)
and set
\gamma=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}+i\beta
. If
h\in\mathcal{P}
for some and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
, then Kim and Sugawa
[
5
]
proved that the function
f
defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle f(z)=\left[\gamma\int_{0}^{z}g_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(\zeta)\cdots g_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}(\zeta)h(\zeta)\zeta^{i\beta-1}d\zeta\right]^{1/\gamma},

belongs to
\mathcal{B}(\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m},\beta,P,g)
.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
, we denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)},\mathcal{P},g)
the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
which satisfy (
1.3
), where
g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{m}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi_{A,B})
(see, full details,
[
6
,
7
]
) and
\alpha^{(m)}=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
. In particular, if we put

\varphi(z)=\varphi_{1,-1}(z)=\frac{1+z}{1-z},

we easily see that

\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{1,-1}}(\alpha^{(1)},\mathcal{P},g)=\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\mathcal{P},g).

Throughout this paper, for the sake of brevity, we shall make use of the simplified notation
\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)})
for the class
\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)},\mathcal{P},g)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{p}(0<p\leq\infty)
denote the Hardy space of analytic functions
f(z)
in
\mathbb{D}
, and define the integral means by

M_{p}(r,f)=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{1/p}&(0<p<\infty)\\
\max\limits_{|z|\leq r}|f(z)|&(p=\infty).\end{cases}

Then, by definition, an analytic function
f(z)
in
\mathbb{D}
belongs to the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p}(0<p\leq\infty)
if

\|f\|_{p}:=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}M_{p}(r,f)<\infty.

For a function
f
in the class
\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\mathcal{P},g)
, Miller
[
8
]
investigated the Hardy spaces to which
f(z)
and
f^{\prime}(z)
belong. In the present paper, when
\varphi(z)=\varphi_{A,B}(z)
, we carry out the corresponding investigations for the function class
\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)})
. We also provide a necessary condition for
\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)})
when
m=1
and
g(z)=z
and we denote this subclass by
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha).

2.
The Hardy space for the class
\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)})

Eenigenburg and Keogh
[
10
]
have investigated the Hardy classes to which
f(z)
and
f^{\prime}(z)
belong for
f\in\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{B}(1,0,\mathcal{P},g)
. In this paper we carry out a similar investigation for the wider class
\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)})
. In what follows, we denote by
k_{\theta}(z)
any function of the form
z(1-e^{-i\theta}z)^{-2}
, where
\theta
a real constant. We require the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
9
]

If
f(z)
is univalent, then
f(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}
, for all
\lambda<1/2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
P(z)
is regular and
\operatorname{Re}P(z)>0
in
\mathbb{D}
, then
P(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}
for all
\lambda<1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
10
]

If
g\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
g(z)\neq k_{a,\tau}(z)
then there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(g)>0
such that
g(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{1/2+\epsilon}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
11
]

If
f^{\prime}(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}(0<\lambda<1)
, then
f(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{\lambda/(1-\lambda)}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)})
with
g(z)\neq k_{\theta}(z)
then there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(f)>0
such that
f(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{1/2m+\epsilon}
.

Proof.

Let
f(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{\varphi_{A,B}}(\alpha^{(m)})
. From (
1.3
) we have

f(z)=\left[\alpha\int_{0}^{z}g_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(\zeta)\cdots g_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}(\zeta)h(\zeta)\zeta^{i\beta-1}d\zeta\right]^{1/\alpha}

where
g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{m}\in S^{*}(\varphi_{A,B})
,
h\in\mathcal{P}
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
.

or

(2.1)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(z)=f^{1-\alpha}(z)g_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(z)\cdots g_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}(z)h(z)z^{-1}

If we let
F(z)=\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha},
then
F(z)
is regular in
\mathbb{D}
and satisfies

F^{\prime}(z)=\frac{\alpha f^{1-\alpha}(z)f^{\prime}(z)}{z^{\alpha}}-\frac{\alpha F(z)}{z}.

Using (
2.1
) this becomes,

F^{\prime}(z)=\frac{\alpha g_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(z)\cdots g_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}(z)h(z)}{z^{\alpha+1}}-\frac{\alpha F(z)}{z},

and if
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, then for
z=re^{i\theta}(0<r<1)
we obtain

I(r)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|F^{\prime}(z)\right|^{\lambda}d\theta\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\alpha\frac{g_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(z)\cdots g_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}(z)}{z^{\alpha+1}}P(z)\right|^{\lambda}d\theta

(2.2)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{\alpha F(z)}{z}\right|^{\lambda}d\theta\equiv I_{1}(r)+I_{2}(r).

Now

I_{2}(r)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{\alpha F(z)}{z}\right|^{\lambda}d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{\alpha}{z}\left[\frac{f(z)}{z}\right]^{\alpha}\right|^{\lambda}d\theta

=\frac{\alpha^{\lambda}}{r^{(\alpha+1)\lambda}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f(z)\right|^{\alpha\lambda}d\theta,

and since
f(z)
is univalent, by Lemma
2.1
,
\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}I_{2}(r)
exists provided that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\alpha\lambda<1/2.

To calculate
I_{1}(r)
, we apply Hölder’s inequality with conjugate indices
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{m+1}.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{r^{(\alpha+1)\lambda}}{\alpha^{\lambda}}I_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|g_{1}(z)|^{\alpha_{1}\lambda}\cdots|g_{m}(z)|^{\alpha_{m}\lambda}|P(z)|^{\lambda}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}|g_{1}(z)|^{\alpha\lambda p_{1}}d\theta\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\cdots\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}|g_{m}(z)|^{\alpha\lambda p_{m}}d\theta\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{m}}}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}|P(z)|^{\lambda p_{m+1}}d\theta\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{m+1}}}

\displaystyle=J_{1}(r)\cdots J_{m+1}(r).

By Lemma
2.3
,
\lim_{r\to 1^{-1}}J_{i}
exists if
\alpha\lambda p_{i}\leq 1/2+\epsilon(g_{i})=1/2+\epsilon_{i}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,m
. Again by Lemma
2.2
,
\lim_{r\to 1^{-1}}J_{m+1}
exists if
\lambda p_{m+1}<1.
Therefore, we see that
\lim_{r\to 1^{-1}}I_{r}
exists provided that
\lambda<1/2\alpha
,
2\alpha\lambda/(1+2\epsilon)\leq 1/p_{i}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,m
and
\lambda<1/p_{m+1}
, where
\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}1/p_{i}=1
and
p_{i}>1.
Let
\epsilon^{*}=\max\{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\cdots,\epsilon_{m}\}
.

Therefore,
2\alpha\lambda/(1+2\epsilon^{*})\leq 1/p_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\cdots,m
and
\lambda<1/p_{m+1}
. This system of inequalities gives
\lambda\leq(1+2\epsilon^{*})/(2\alpha m+1+2\epsilon^{*})
, where
\epsilon^{*}
is chosen sufficiently small so that
(1+2\epsilon^{*})/(2\alpha m+1+2\epsilon^{*})<1/2\alpha.

Hence, we have shown that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\lim_{r\to 1^{-1}}I_{r}
exists if
\lambda\leq(1+\epsilon)/(2\alpha m+1+\epsilon)
, i.e.

(2.4)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{(1+\epsilon)/(2\alpha m+1+\epsilon).}

For
\lambda=(1+\epsilon)/(2\alpha m+1+\epsilon),
we have
0<\lambda<1
and
\lambda/(1-\lambda)=1/2\alpha m
. By (
2.4
)
and Lemma
2.4
, we conclude that

(2.5)

\displaystyle F(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{(1+\epsilon)/2\alpha m.}

Since
f(z)=z[F(z)]^{1/\alpha}
, we have

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(z)|^{\lambda}d\theta=r^{\lambda}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|F(z)|^{\lambda/\alpha}d\theta,

and consequently from (
2.5
) we see that this integral will be bounded provided that
\lambda/\alpha\leq(1+\epsilon)/2\alpha m
, i.e.
\lambda\leq 1/2m+\epsilon/2
i.e.
f\in\mathcal{H}^{1/2m+\epsilon}
, and this completes the proof of the theorem.

The condition
g(z)\neq k_{\theta}(z)
in the theorem is essential. This may be seen by taking
g(z)=k_{\theta}(z)
and
h(z)=zk^{\prime}_{\theta}(z)/k_{\theta}(z)
. A simple calculation shows that for
f(z)\in\mathcal{B}(\alpha,0,zk^{\prime}_{\theta}/k_{\theta},k_{\theta})
we have
f(z)=k_{\theta}(z)
. Since

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|k_{\theta}(z)|^{1/2}d\theta=r^{1/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{|1-re^{i\theta}|}\geq\sqrt{2}r^{1/2}\log\frac{1}{1-r},

we see that
k_{\theta}(z)\notin\mathcal{H}^{1/2}
.

∎

Corollary 2.1
.

If
f\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
, with
g(z)\neq k_{\theta}(z)
, then there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(f)>0
such that

f(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.

3.
Necessary condition for the class
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)

Let
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}
be given by (
1.1
). We say that
f\in\mathcal{C}(\beta)
if and only if there exists a
\phi\in\mathcal{K}
such that,

\left|\arg\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{\phi^{\prime}(z)}\right|\leq\frac{\beta\pi}{2}\quad(0<\beta<1)

or equivalently,

f^{\prime}(z)=p^{\beta}(z)\phi^{\prime}(z)\quad\textrm{where}\quad p(z)\in\mathcal{P}

Now for
\phi(z)=z
the class
\mathcal{C}(\beta)
reduces to a subclass, namely,
\mathcal{C}_{I}(\beta).
We first notice that there exists a one-to-one corresponding between the classes
\mathcal{C}_{I}(\beta)
and
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
. Let
g(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)~~~~(\alpha>1)
then there exists a function
h(z)\in\mathcal{P}
such that

\displaystyle g(z)=\left[\alpha\int_{0}^{z}t^{\alpha-1}h(t)dt\right]^{1/\alpha}.

Now, consider the function
G(z)=\int_{0}^{z}(\frac{g(t)}{t})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}(g^{\prime}(t))^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}dt
. Then,

G^{\prime}(z)=\left(\left(\frac{g(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha-1}g^{\prime}(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=h^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(z),

which indicates that
G(z)\in\mathcal{C}_{I}(\beta)
.

Now let
f(z)\in\mathcal{C}_{I}(\beta)
, then there exists a function
p(z)\in\mathcal{P}
such that
f^{\prime}(z)=p^{\beta}(z)
. Now consider the function

F(z)=\left[\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{z}\left(tf^{\prime}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}t^{-1}dt\right]^{\alpha}=\left[\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{z}\left(t\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}f^{\prime\frac{1}{\beta}}(t)dt\right]^{\beta}=\left[\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{z}\left(t\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}p(z)dt\right]^{\beta},

which indicates that
F(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\frac{1}{\beta})
. Therefore, we have been able to a make bridge between the classes
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{C}_{I}(\beta)
when
\alpha>1~~\textrm{and}~~0<\beta<1
. In the following theorem, we provide an important necessary condition for
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
when
\alpha>1.
In order to state our result, we also introduce the notation

P[\alpha,f](z)=1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+(\alpha-1)\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}

for
\alpha>1
and
f\in\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
f(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
, then for
\alpha>1
we get

\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\operatorname{Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+(\alpha-1)\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)d\theta>-\pi~~i.e.~~\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\operatorname{Re}P[\alpha,f](z)d\theta>-\pi.

Proof.

If
f(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
with
\alpha>1
, then there exists a function
F(z)\in\mathcal{C}_{I}(\frac{1}{\alpha})
such that

F^{\prime}(z)=\left(\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha-1}f^{\prime}(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Let
T(F,re^{i\theta})
be the tangent to the curve
F(|z|=r)
at
F(re^{i\theta})
.

So,

\arg T(F,re^{i\theta})=\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\arg f(z)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\arg zf^{\prime}(z).

Therefore,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\arg T(F,re^{i\theta})=\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\operatorname{Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)

Since
F(z)\in\mathcal{C}_{z}(\frac{1}{\alpha})
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\arg T(F,re^{i\theta})>\frac{-\pi}{\alpha}.

Hence from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) we get,

\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\operatorname{Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+(\alpha-1)\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)d\theta>-\pi~~i.e.~~\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\operatorname{Re}P[\alpha,f](z)d\theta>-\pi.

∎

4.
a coefficient problem on
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)

The coefficient problem for the function class
B_{1}(\alpha)
was first investigated by Singh
[
12
]
, who established sharp bounds for the initial Taylor coefficients of functions in this class. For
f(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
of the form (
1.1
), Singh proved exact inequalities for
|a_{2}|
,
|a_{3}|
, and
|a_{4}|
, with
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
and
\alpha\geq 1
. Finding exact bounds for higher-order coefficients, particularly for
n\geq 5
, has proven more difficult. Recent progress includes the exact bound for
|a_{5}|
when
0\leq\alpha\leq 1/2
, obtained by Marjono et al.
[
13
]
. These results suggest the following obvious conjectures.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
. Suppose that
g\in S
and is given by
g(z)=z+b_{2}z^{2}+\cdots
and

(4.1)

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(z)\left[\frac{g(z)}{z}\right]^{\alpha-1}=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}.

If
f\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
of the form (
1.1
), then for
n\geq 2
,

|a_{n}|\leq b_{n}.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
and
f\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
be of the form (
1.1
). Then, for
n\geq 2
,

|a_{n}|\leq\frac{2}{n-1+\alpha}.

The conjectures stated above for the class
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
provide a natural starting point for further research and in light of these problems, we investigate the following coefficient problem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
be of the form (
1.1
), then for
\psi(z)=\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
, we have

|A_{n}|\leq\frac{2\alpha}{n+\alpha},~~n=1,2,\cdots,

where
\psi(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
and this estimate is sharp.

Proof.

Let
f(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\alpha)
. Then, we have,

\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha-1}f^{\prime}(z)>0,

which is equivalent to,

\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha-1}f^{\prime}(z)=p(z),

where
p(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}z^{n}
has positive real part.

Also we have,
\psi(z)=\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha}
and on differentiation we get,

\psi^{\prime}(z)=\frac{\alpha}{z}.\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha-1}f^{\prime}(z)-\frac{\alpha}{z}.\psi(z).

Thus,

\psi^{\prime}(z)=\frac{\alpha}{z}.p(z)-\frac{\alpha}{z}.\psi(z),

which simplifies to,

(4.2)

\displaystyle z\psi^{\prime}(z)+\alpha\psi(z)=\alpha p(z).

On comparing
n
-th coefficients in the above we get,

(n+\alpha)A_{n}=\alpha p_{n},

which implies the desired inequality using Caratheodory bound
|p_{n}|\leq 2
.

The inequality is sharp for the function

(4.3)

\displaystyle G(z)=\left(\frac{g(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{z^{\alpha}}.\int_{0}^{z}t^{\alpha-1}\frac{1+t}{1-t}~dt,

where
g(z)
is defined in (
4.1
).

Remark 4.1
.

The function
G(z)
defined in (
4.3
) satisfies the following differential subordination,

(4.4)

\displaystyle zG^{\prime}(z)+\alpha G(z)\prec\alpha\frac{1+z}{1-z},

and therefore combining (
4.4
) and (
4.2
) we get,

z\psi^{\prime}(z)+\alpha\psi(z)\prec zG^{\prime}(z)+\alpha G(z).

Remark 4.2
.

Theorem
4.1
implies the following coefficient domination relation,

\psi(z)=\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha}<<\left(\frac{g(z)}{z}\right)^{\alpha}=G(z).

i.e.

|a_{n}(\psi(z))|\leq\frac{2\alpha}{n+\alpha},~~~\alpha>0.

This establishes the validity of Conjecture
1
and Conjecture
2
for all integer values of
\alpha
. However, the validity of these conjectures for non-integer
\alpha>0
remains an open problem.
∎
