Determination of the ground state polarizability of 162 Dy near 530 nm 530\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
Alexandre Journeaux, Maxime Lecomte, Julie Veschambre, Maxence Lepers, Jean Dalibard, Raphael Lopes

Abstract
Open-shell lanthanide atoms, and dysprosium in particular, combine a large ground-state angular momentum with dense electronic spectra, making their dynamical polarizability strongly dependent on wavelength and internal state and therefore particularly challenging to characterize accurately. This issue has become especially relevant with the recent development of single-atom trapping of dysprosium in optical-tweezer arrays, where precise knowledge of the polarizability is needed to design optimized trapping architectures. Here, we exploit the strong spin-dependent light shift near the J ′ = J − 1 J^{\prime}=J-1 intercombination line at 530.306 nm 530.306\text{\,}\mathrm{nm} to determine the background scalar and vector polarizabilities of 162 Dy in its ground state near this wavelength. Our measurements quantitatively agree with atomic-structure calculations and provide new insight into the contributions of nearby transitions in a spectral region relevant to emerging dysprosium tweezer platforms.



The frequency response of an atom to an oscillating electric field is characterized by its dynamical polarizability
\alpha(\omega)
, which can be written as a sum over dipole-allowed transitions weighted by dipole matrix elements
Mitroy et al. 2010
. In the far-detuned regime,
\alpha(\omega)
sets the light shift of an internal state and thus the optical dipole potential in a laser field
Grimm et al. 2000
. Accurate determination of the polarizability is therefore required both for quantitative modeling of optical traps and for precision metrology, where differential polarizabilities enter the evaluation of laser-induced and blackbody-radiation shifts in optical frequency standards
Ludlow et al. 2015
.

Dysprosium combines a large magnetic dipole moment (
\mu\simeq 10\,\mu_{\rm B}
)
with a rich optical spectrum
Lu et al. 2010
;
Chomaz et al. 2022
;
Norcia and Ferlaino 2021
. These properties have enabled studies of dipolar quantum gases
Chomaz et al. 2022
, the emulation of artificial gauge fields in its large synthetic dimension
Chalopin et al. 2020
;
Bouhiron et al. 2024
, as well as controlled light scattering in dense ensembles
Hofer et al. 2025

and the trapping, imaging, and cooling of single Dy atoms in optical tweezer arrays at
532\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}

Bloch et al. 2023
;
Grün et al. 2024
;
Biagioni et al. 2025
.

In contrast to alkali atoms, whose ground-state polarizabilities are predominantly scalar, lanthanides such as erbium and dysprosium exhibit sizable vector and tensor contributions, leading to spin- and polarization-dependent optical potentials
Li et al. 2017
;
Chalopin et al. 2018
;
Norcia and Ferlaino 2021
;
Tsyganok et al. 2019
. Predicting
\alpha(\omega)
in the visible/near-infrared is particularly challenging for dysprosium because multiple transitions contribute appreciably, making theoretical values sensitive to the detailed atomic spectrum
Li et al. 2017
;
Dzuba et al. 2011
;
Lepers et al. 2014
. While benchmark measurements exist at
1064\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}

Ravensbergen et al. 2018
and near the
626\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
intercombination line
Kreyer et al. 2021
, recent measurements at
532\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
report a ground-state scalar polarizability about a factor of two below theoretical expectations and comparable to its
1064\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
value
Bloch et al. 2024
. These observations motivate a dedicated calibration of the polarizability of Dy around 530 nm, which is a key ingredient toward trapping and manipulating lanthanides in optical tweezers
Bloch et al. 2023
;
Grün et al. 2024
.

In contrast to the determination of the light shift, this approach does not require an absolute calibration of the beam intensity or mode profile, which is often a major source of uncertainty in experimental platforms.

In the following, we consider the role of the vector and tensor parts of the polarizability in the determination of the light shift.

We first focus on the resonant contribution. For a sufficiently large bias magnetic field, couplings between different Zeeman states can be neglected and
\hat{V}
is diagonal in the
\left|J,m_{J}\right\rangle
basis. In the case of a beam propagating along the quantization axis, with
\mathbf{e}\perp\mathbf{B}
, the light shift of a state
\left|J,m_{J}\right\rangle
then becomes

U_{\rm LS}=\frac{D^{2}}{\Delta}\frac{I}{2\epsilon_{0}c}\Biggl[\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\cos(2\theta)\frac{m_{J}}{2J}+\alpha_{3}\frac{J(J+1)-3m_{J}^{2}}{2J(2J-1)}\Biggr],

(4)

where
\alpha_{(s,v,t)}=(D^{2}/\Delta)\,\alpha_{(1,2,3)}
with
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})=(5/17,-15/17,-5/17)
. Here
D^{2}=d^{2}/\hbar=3\pi\epsilon_{0}(c/\omega_{0})^{3}\Gamma_{0}
, where
d
is the ordinary dipole matrix element. The angles (
\theta
,
\varphi
) define the polarization, such that

\mathbf{e}=\cos\theta\,{\bf e_{-}}+e^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi}\sin\theta\,{\bf e_{+}}

(5)

with
{\bf e_{\pm}}
corresponding to the
\sigma^{\pm}
polarizations. Equation. (
4
) illustrates the polarization dependence of the light shift. More generally, for a
J\to J-1
transition, the coupling between ground and excited manifolds has a two-dimensional null space, since the ground-state manifold has dimension
2J+1
while the excited-state manifold has dimension
2J-1
. As a result, there are always two dark states for arbitrary polarization. For
J=8
, pure
\sigma^{-}
polarization (
\theta=0
) cancels the light shift for
m_{J}=-7,-8
.

We now include the background contribution. Restricting to atoms prepared in the lowest-energy Zeeman sublevel
\left|J,-J\right\rangle
, it is convenient to introduce the normalized light shift
\tilde{U}_{\rm LS}~=~(4\epsilon_{0}c\,U_{\rm LS})/I
. One obtains

\tilde{U}_{\rm LS}=-\Bigl(2\alpha_{s}^{\rm bg}-\alpha_{t}^{\rm bg}-\frac{15}{17}\frac{D^{2}}{\Delta}\Bigr)-\cos(2\theta)\Bigl(\alpha_{v}^{\rm bg}+\frac{15}{17}\frac{D^{2}}{\Delta}\Bigr)\ ,

(6)

which can be expressed as the sum of two competing contributions: one involving the scalar and tensor polarizabilities,
\tilde{U}_{\rm LS}^{(st)}(\Delta)
, and one arising from the vector polarizability,
\cos(2\theta)\,\tilde{U}_{\rm LS}^{(v)}(\Delta)
. For a given detuning
\Delta
such that
\left|\tilde{U}_{\rm LS}^{(st)}/\tilde{U}_{\rm LS}^{(v)}\right|\leq 1
, there exists an angle
\theta_{\rm cancel}
for which the total light shift vanishes.

162
Dy near
530\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
.

Experimentally, we probe the sign and cancellation of the spin-dependent light shift (SLS) by monitoring the time-of-flight (ToF) expansion of a nondegenerate (

\left|J,-J\right\rangle

\mathbf{B}=B\,\mathbf{e}_{z}
with
B=$1.66\text{\,}\mathrm{G}$
. In the following, we simplify the notation and use
\left|J,m_{J}\right\rangle\equiv\left|m_{J}\right\rangle
and
\left|J^{\prime},m^{\prime}_{J}\right\rangle\equiv\left|m^{\prime}_{J}\right\rangle

\mathbf{e}_{z}

1

1/e^{2}

\Delta/(2\pi)

The polarization is controlled using a half-wave plate and a quarter-wave plate with fast-axis angles
\vartheta_{2}

\vartheta_{4}

To determine the cancellation angle
\theta_{\rm cancel}
at which the light shift of
\left|-J\right\rangle
vanishes, we compare the time-of-flight expansion in the presence of the SLS beam to a reference expansion without the SLS beam. We extract the peak radius
\tilde{r}_{\rm peak}
from the azimuthally integrated density profile
\tilde{n}(r)~=~\int{\rm d}\phi\,r\,n(r,\phi)

\tilde{n}(r)

2

(\vartheta_{2},\vartheta_{4})

\tilde{r}_{\rm rel}=\tilde{r}_{\rm peak}^{\rm(on)}/\tilde{r}_{\rm peak}^{\rm(off)},

(7)

and identify
\theta=\theta_{\rm cancel}
from the condition
\tilde{r}_{\rm rel}=1
, which signals the cancellation of the light shift in the regime where the density profile is not significantly distorted.

For a given detuning
\Delta

Experimentally, we prepare a nondegenerate sample at a temperature of

To conclude, we have measured the scalar plus tensor component and vector component of the dynamical polarizability of
162
Dy in the vicinity of the
530.306\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
intercombination line. Our approach exploits polarization-dependent cancellations of the light shift at various detunings from the
J^{\prime}=J-1
transition. By tracking these zero crossings, we extract the background polarizability without relying on an absolute intensity calibration, making the method both robust and experimentally straightforward.
A precise determination of the background polarizability near
530\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
is directly relevant for experiments employing optical tweezers
Biagioni et al. 2025
, where vector and tensor contributions generate sizable differential light shifts between different electronic levels. More generally, engineered differential light shifts constitute a resource for advanced cooling and evaporation protocols in optical dipole traps which rely sensitively on accurate polarizability values
Lopes 2021
. The improved precision reported here supports the implementation of such cooling strategies in cold dysprosium gases lanthanide systems.
