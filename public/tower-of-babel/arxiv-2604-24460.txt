Noise-robust 1-copy distillation protocol for all distillable Bell-diagonal qutrits
Tobias C. Sutter, Christopher Popp, Beatrix C. Hiesmayr

Abstract
Entanglement distillation is the process of converting noisy entangled states into maximally entangled pure states via local operations and classical communication. A long-standing, unresolved question is which entangled states are amenable to distillation, known as the distillability problem. We solve this for Bell-diagonal qutrits with Weyl structure, and present a noise-robust scheme for entanglement distillation. In particular, we find that violating the positive partial transposition (PPT) criterion is necessary and sufficient for the 1-distillability of these states. For this, we construct a Schmidt rank 2 eigenvector of the partially transposed density matrix associated with its unique, three-fold degenerate negative eigenvalue. This feature makes the derived entanglement distillation protocol resilient to white-noise effects on the quantum states. Our results thus make noisy entangled qutrit pairs more accessible for future quantum technologies.



I
Introduction

Entanglement is a fundamental resource for many quantum technologies, such as quantum computation
11
and cryptography
22
.
The performance of these technologies relies heavily on the amount of entanglement available
19
, and for bipartite applications, the gold standard is typically maximally entangled pure states.
However, experimental noise inevitably degrades the entanglement of any such pure states, yielding mixed states that are often less useful in practice, as the attainable quantum advantage may be lost
16
.

One practical solution to this problem is entanglement distillation, i.e., using local operations and classical communication (LOCC) to convert multiple copies of some input state into fewer copies of maximally entangled states.
The difficulty for entanglement distillation in practice and theory is twofold.
First, for the distillation task, one requires at least partial knowledge of the given quantum state
21
.
Thus, to design highly performant distillation protocols, suitable noise models leading to those expected mixed resource states need to be studied.
One important model concerns generalized Pauli errors (via the probabilistic application of Weyl-Heisenberg matrices) acting on one subsystem of the maximally entangled state, yielding Bell-diagonal states.
Second, to know whether some state can be distilled at all requires checking that, for some number
n
of copies, one can obtain a maximally entangled state by LOCC.
Ref.
20
shows that for every such
n>0
, there exists a state
\rho
that is
n
-copy-undistillable, but
(n+1)
-copy-distillable, illustrating one of the difficulties of the distillability question.

Adding to this difficulty is the fact that not all entangled states can be distilled.
A necessary criterion for distillability is the violation of the positive partial transposition (PPT) criterion
9
;
13
.
The problem is that while all separable states satisfy the criterion, not all entangled states violate it for bipartite systems with dimension
d_{A}\times d_{B}
with
d_{A}d_{B}>6
.
This means that such states are entangled but undistillable, and are called bound entangled
10
(for a recent review on bipartite bound entanglement, see
7
).
Nonetheless, for bipartite qubit-qudit systems with
d_{A}\geq 2
and
d_{B}=2
, a violation of the PPT criterion is necessary and sufficient for distillability
6
.
A long-standing open problem in this regard is whether a violation of the PPT criterion is also sufficient for distillability in general dimensions (with evidence against it presented, e.g., in Refs.
6
;
5
).

In this Letter, we answer this question in the affirmative for Bell-diagonal qutrit states with Weyl structure and present a corresponding noise-robust construction that can be used for entanglement distillation.
In particular, we explicitly construct a 1-distillability witness vector for all Bell-diagonal qutrit-qutrit states
\rho
that violate the PPT criterion.
This is based on an eigenvector of the partial transpose of
\rho
corresponding to its unique, three-fold degenerate negative eigenvalue.
It has the advantage that detecting the 1-distillability of
\rho
via the associated distillability witness and implementing the derived local projections as an initial step for entanglement distillation are more robust against white noise in real experiments than other approaches.

II
Entanglement distillation, partial transpose, and entanglement witnesses

Consider a bipartite Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
with
d_{A}:=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{A})
and
d_{B}:=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{B})
.
We denote linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
by
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, and the set of density matrices by
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})=\{\rho\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\rho\geq 0,\mytr(\rho)=1\}
.
A state
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
is called
separable
if it can be written as
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\,\rho_{i}^{(A)}\otimes\rho_{i}^{(B)}
with
\rho_{i}^{(A)}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
and
\rho_{i}^{(B)}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
, and
entangled
otherwise.
We denote the sets of separable and entangled states by
\mathrm{SEP}
and
\mathrm{ENT}
, respectively.
If unambiguous, we abbreviate product vectors such as
|\psi_{A}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{B}\rangle
by
|\psi_{A},\psi_{B}\rangle
.

Entanglement distillation is the task of converting
n
copies of a resource state
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
into
m\leq n
copies of a maximally entangled target state
\tilde{\rho}
using only local quantum operations and classical communication (LOCC).
If this is possible for some
n,m>0
, we say that
\rho
is
distillable
.
Without loss of generality, one can choose the maximally entangled qubit state
\tilde{\rho}=|\phi^{+}\rangle\langle\phi^{+}|
, where
|\phi^{+}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{1}|i,i\rangle

7
.

A valuable tool for investigating the distillability of quantum states is the partial transposition, which we denote by
\rho^{\Gamma}=(\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes T_{B})(\rho)
, where
T_{B}:|i\rangle\langle j|\mapsto|j\rangle\langle i|
is the transposition on
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
in the computational basis.
We define the disjoint sets
\mathrm{PPT}:=\{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\rho^{\Gamma}\geq 0\}
and
\mathrm{NPT}:=\{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\rho^{\Gamma}\ngeq 0\}
corresponding to states having a positive and a non-positive partial transposition, respectively.
This provides a partition of the set of density matrices
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})=\mathrm{PPT}\cup\mathrm{NPT}
.

A necessary condition for
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
to be distillable is that
\rho\in\mathrm{NPT}

10
, which is also sufficient if
\min(d_{A},d_{B})=2

6
.
Beyond that, the following gives an exact characterization of distillability for general dimensions:

Theorem 1

(
10
;
6
)
.

A bipartite state
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
is distillable if and only if there exists
n>0
and
|\varphi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
with Schmidt rank 2 such that

\displaystyle\langle\varphi|\;(\rho^{\Gamma})^{\otimes n}\;|\varphi\rangle<0\;.

(1)

W.l.o.g.,
|\varphi\rangle
can be chosen to be normalized, in which case its Schmidt decomposition is

\displaystyle|\varphi\rangle=\mu_{0}\,|a_{0}\rangle\otimes|b_{0}\rangle+\mu_{1}\,|a_{1}\rangle\otimes|b_{1}\rangle

(2)

with
\mu_{0}\geq\mu_{1}>0
,
\mu_{0}^{2}+\mu_{1}^{2}=1
,
\{|a_{0}\rangle,|a_{1}\rangle\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{A}^{\otimes n}
, and
\{|b_{0}\rangle,|b_{1}\rangle\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{B}^{\otimes n}
satisfying
\langle a_{i}|a_{j}\rangle=\langle b_{i}|b_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
.

We call a state
n
-distillable
if
n
is the smallest positive integer such that Theorem
1
holds, and the accompanying Schmidt rank 2 vector
|\varphi\rangle
is its
n
-distillability witness vector
.
If
\rho
is 1-distillable, we can rewrite (
1
) as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0>\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle&=\mytr(\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|)\\
&=\mytr(\rho\,(|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|)^{\Gamma})=\mytr(\rho\,W_{\varphi})\;,\end{aligned}

(3)

where we defined the
1-distillability witness

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} W_{\varphi}&:=(|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|)^{\Gamma}\\
&=\mu_{0}^{2}\,|a_{0},b_{0}^{\ast}\rangle\langle a_{0},b_{0}^{\ast}|+\mu_{1}^{2}\,|a_{1},b_{1}^{\ast}\rangle\langle a_{1},b_{1}^{\ast}|\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\mu_{0}\,\mu_{1}(|a_{0},b_{1}^{\ast}\rangle\langle a_{1},b_{0}^{\ast}|+|a_{1},b_{0}^{\ast}\rangle\langle a_{0},b_{1}^{\ast}|)\;.\end{aligned}

(4)

Here,
∗
denotes entry-wise complex conjugation in the computational basis.
From this, we see that
\mathrm{spec}(W_{\varphi})=\{\mu_{0}^{2},\mu_{1}^{2},\mu_{0}\mu_{1},-\mu_{0}\mu_{1},0\}
, and hence
W_{\varphi}\ngeq 0
.
Furthermore, for all product vectors
|a,b\rangle:=|a\rangle\otimes|b\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mytr(|a,b\rangle\langle a,b|W_{\varphi})&=\mytr(|a,b^{\ast}\rangle\langle a,b^{\ast}|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|)\\
&=|\langle a,b^{\ast}|\varphi\rangle|^{2}\geq 0\;,\end{aligned}

(5)

implying that
W_{\varphi}
is an entanglement witness.
This operator is also decomposable
12
and thus cannot detect PPT-entangled states.
Moreover, it is weakly optimal in the sense that there exist product vectors
|a,b\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\langle a,b|W_{\varphi}|a,b\rangle=0
.
In particular, this is achieved by
|a\rangle=|a_{0}\rangle
and
|b\rangle=|b_{1}^{\ast}\rangle
.
Finally, the mirrored operator to
W_{\varphi}
is
M_{\varphi}=\mu_{0}^{2}\,\mathbbm{1}_{d^{2}}-W_{\varphi}

2
, which is positive semidefinite and therefore not an entanglement witness.

For qutrit-qutrit systems, there is a sufficient condition for 1-distillability:

Theorem 2

(
3
)
.

If
\rho\in\mathrm{NPT}
with
d=d_{A}=d_{B}=3
and
\rho^{\Gamma}
has at least two non-positive eigenvalues counting multiplicities, then
\rho
is 1-distillable.

The proof of this theorem in Ref.
3
invokes Theorem
1
and constructs a Schmidt rank 2 vector
|\varphi\rangle
such that
\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle<0
.
However, it is not concerned with minimizing
\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle
over suitable
|\varphi\rangle
.
While this is sufficient for proving Theorem
2
, it hinders its experimental applicability in noisy conditions, as we discuss next.

II.1
Experimental limitations of Theorem
2

Since experimental realizations of qutrits are inevitably affected by noise, the main practical limitation for using
|\varphi\rangle
from Ref.
3
stems from the fact that
\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle<0
might be close to zero.
Below, we illustrate this for (a) the verification of 1-distillability using the witness
W_{\varphi}
and (b) entanglement distillation using local projections derived from
|\varphi\rangle
, and argue that for both tasks it may be beneficial to minimize
\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle
.

For (a), modeling experimental imperfections for
\rho
as white noise, we define

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{noisy}}:=(1-p)\,\rho+\frac{p}{d^{2}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{d^{2}}\;,

(6)

with noise parameter
p\in[0,1]
.
The expectation value of the 1-distillability witness (
4
) for this state is

\displaystyle\mytr\left(W_{\varphi}\rho_{\mathrm{noisy}}\right)=(1-p)\,\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle+\frac{p}{d^{2}}\;.

(7)

The state
\rho_{\mathrm{noisy}}
is detected as 1-distillable whenever
\mytr\left(W_{\varphi}\,\rho_{\mathrm{noisy}}\right)<0
, or equivalently, when

\displaystyle p<p_{\rho,\max}=\frac{-d^{2}\,\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle}{1-d^{2}\,\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle}\;,

(8)

where we used
d\geq 2
and
-\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})\leq\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle<0
for
\rho\in\mathrm{NPT}

17
.
As the right-hand side of (
8
) is monotonically decreasing for
-\frac{1}{2}\leq\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle<0
, the tolerable noise level
p
for witnessing 1-distillability of
\rho_{\mathrm{noisy}}
is maximized when
\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle
is minimized.

Concerning (b), we can construct local rank-2 projection operators

\displaystyle P_{A}=\sum_{i=0}^{1}|a_{i}\rangle\langle a_{i}|\;,\quad P_{B}=\sum_{i=0}^{1}|b_{i}^{\ast}\rangle\langle b_{i}^{\ast}|\;,

(9)

from the 1-distillability witness vector
|\varphi\rangle
in (
2
).
These can be used to filter the original state
\rho
to an effective entangled NPT qubit-qubit state

\displaystyle\sigma:=q^{-1}(P_{A}\otimes P_{B})\,\rho\,(P_{A}\otimes P_{B})\;,

(10)

where
q=\mytr((P_{A}\otimes P_{B})\,\rho)
is the success probability of the filtering process.
The minimal eigenvalue of
\sigma^{\Gamma}
is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{\Gamma})\leq\langle\varphi|\sigma^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle=q^{-1}\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle<0\;,

(11)

where we used
(P_{A}\otimes P_{B}^{T})|\varphi\rangle=|\varphi\rangle
.
Because all entangled bipartite qubit states can be distilled, this filtering can be used as the initial step of an entanglement distillation protocol for
\rho
.
However, experimental noise can degrade the entanglement of
\sigma
.
If we model this decoherence process as white noise on
\sigma
, we retain
\sigma\in\mathrm{NPT}
for higher noise levels if
\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{\Gamma})
is as negative as possible.
Hence, in light of (
11
), minimizing
\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle
over Schmidt rank 2 vectors
|\varphi\rangle
may be crucial for designing practical entanglement distillation protocols that function reliably in the presence of experimental noise.

In this regard, the minimum of
\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle
for any
|\varphi\rangle
is lower bounded by the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})
.
If this extreme value is reached,
|\varphi\rangle
is an eigenvector of
\rho^{\Gamma}
.
If, furthermore,
|\varphi\rangle
is a 1-distillability witness vector, the inequality in (
11
) becomes an equality because
|\varphi\rangle
is also an eigenvector of
\sigma^{\Gamma}
corresponding to its unique negative eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{\Gamma})=q^{-1}\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})
.
In this case, assuming white noise on
\sigma
, the noise threshold for maintaining
\sigma\in\mathrm{NPT}
is even larger than the one in (
8
) if
q<4/d^{2}
.
To see this, we consider white noise affecting
\sigma
(viewed as a bipartite qubit state), and define

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{noisy}}:=(1-p)\,\sigma+\frac{p}{4}\,\mathbbm{1}_{4}\;,

(12)

with
p\in[0,1]
.
We have
\sigma_{\mathrm{noisy}}\in\mathrm{NPT}
if and only if
\lambda_{\min}(\sigma_{\mathrm{noisy}}^{\Gamma})<0
, or equivalently

\displaystyle p<p_{\sigma,\max}=\frac{-4\,\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{\Gamma})}{1-4\,\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{\Gamma})}=\frac{-4\,\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})}{q-4\,\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})}\;,

(13)

where we used
\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{\Gamma})=q^{-1}\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})
.
For the qubit state
\sigma
to be more noise-resilient than
\rho
, we require
p_{\sigma,\max}>p_{\rho,\max}
, which is the case if and only if
q<4/d^{2}
, as claimed.

III
Bell-diagonal states with Weyl structure

The mixed states of interest in this contribution are Bell-diagonal states that inherit group structure via the projective Weyl-Heisenberg group
18
.
They occur naturally by considering generalized Pauli errors.
The
d
-dimensional Weyl-Heisenberg operators via which they are defined are given by

\displaystyle W_{k,l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{jk}\,|j\rangle\langle j+l|\;,

(14)

where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/d}
.
Throughout the paper, we denote the ring of integers modulo
d
by
\mathbbm{Z}_{d}:=\mathbbm{Z}/d\mathbbm{Z}
, and we take all indices and computational basis vector entries modulo
d
.
The operators (
14
) satisfy the so-called Weyl relations

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} W_{i,j}W_{k,l}&=\omega^{jk}\,W_{i+k,j+l}\;,\\
W_{k,l}^{\ast}&=W_{-k,l}\;,\\
W_{k,l}^{T}&=\omega^{-kl}W_{k,-l}\;.\end{aligned}

(15)

By defining the canonical maximally entangled state as

\displaystyle|\Omega_{0,0}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}|i,i\rangle\;,

(16)

we construct a basis of
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
with
d_{A}=d_{B}=d
using the
d^{2}
maximally entangled states

\displaystyle|\Omega_{k,l}\rangle=(W_{k,l}\otimes\mathbbm{1})|\Omega_{0,0}\rangle\;,

(17)

where
k,l\in\mathbbm{Z}_{d}
.
Convex combinations of the projectors
P_{k,l}:=|\Omega_{k,l}\rangle\langle\Omega_{k,l}|
are the so-called Bell-diagonal states, forming a “magic simplex”
1
, given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{d}

\displaystyle=\left\{\rho=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}c_{k,l}\,P_{k,l}\;\Big|\;\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}c_{k,l}=1,\;c_{k,l}\geq 0\right\}\;.

(18)

Operationally, states in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
can be obtained by locally applying the quantum channel
\mathcal{E}(X)=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}c_{k,l}\,W_{k,l}\,X\,W_{k,l}^{\dagger}
to the maximally entangled state
P_{0,0}
, i.e.,
(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d})(P_{0,0})\in\mathcal{M}_{d}
.
Any
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{d}
is diagonalized by the Bell-unitary

\displaystyle U=\sum_{r,s=0}^{d-1}|r,s\rangle\langle\Omega_{r,s}|\;.

(19)

A straightforward computation reveals a block-diagonal structure of the partially-transposed state

\displaystyle\rho^{\Gamma}=U^{\dagger}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}|m\rangle\langle m|\otimes B_{m}\right)U\;,

(20)

where we defined

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} B_{m}&:=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{k,l,x,y=0}^{d-1}\omega^{y(k-m)-xl}c_{k,l+y}W_{x,2y}\\
&=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,l,y=0}^{d-1}\omega^{y(k-m)}c_{k,l}|l-y\rangle\langle l+y|\;.\end{aligned}

(21)

For odd dimension
d
, the hermitian blocks
B_{m}
are unitarily equivalent; for even
d
, there are two classes of unitarily equivalent blocks, one for even and one for odd
m

1
.
Using (
21
) and (
15
), it can be checked that for all
d
we have

\displaystyle B_{m+2}=W_{1,0}B_{m}W_{1,0}^{\dagger}\;.

(22)

As shown in Ref.
18
, any Bell-diagonal NPT state
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{3}\cap\mathrm{NPT}
has three negative eigenvalues and hence satisfies the premise of Theorem
2
, leading to the corollary that every
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{3}\cap\mathrm{NPT}
is 1-distillable, i.e., there exist no NPT bound entangled states in
\mathcal{M}_{3}
.
In the next section, we give a constructive proof of our main theorem, which is the stronger statement

Theorem 3
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{3}\cap\mathrm{NPT}
and let
\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})<0
be the smallest eigenvalue of
\rho^{\Gamma}
.
Then there exists a normalized Schmidt rank 2 vector
|\varphi\rangle
such that

\displaystyle\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle=\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})|\varphi\rangle\;,

(23)

and hence

\displaystyle\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle=\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})<0\;.

(24)

Therefore, every
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{3}\cap\mathrm{NPT}
is 1-distillable.

IV
Proof of Theorem
3

To prove Thm.
3
, we start by noting that
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{3}\cap\mathrm{NPT}
implies
\rho^{\Gamma}\ngeq 0
, and hence
B_{m}\ngeq 0
for some
m\in\mathbbm{Z}_{3}
by (
20
).
As
d=3
is odd, the
B_{m}
are unitarily equivalent, and thus
B_{m}\ngeq 0
for all
m\in\mathbbm{Z}_{3}
.
Let
|u_{m}\rangle
be the normalized eigenvector of
B_{m}
with the smallest eigenvalue, i.e.,
B_{m}|u_{m}\rangle=\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})|u_{m}\rangle
.
Using (
22
), these eigenvectors satisfy

\displaystyle|u_{m+2}\rangle=W_{1,0}|u_{m}\rangle\;.

(25)

Next, we consider the normalized vector

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{2}\psi_{i}\;|i\rangle\otimes|u_{i}\rangle\;,

(26)

satisfying
\sum_{i=0}^{2}|\psi_{i}|^{2}=1
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\psi|U\rho^{\Gamma}U^{\dagger}|\psi\rangle&=\sum_{m=0}^{2}|\psi_{m}|^{2}\underbrace{\langle u_{m}|B_{m}|u_{m}\rangle}_{=\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})}\\
&=\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})<0\;.\end{aligned}

(27)

Next, we rewrite (
19
) as

\displaystyle U=(F\otimes\mathbbm{1})\,C_{s}\;,

(28)

with the discrete Fourier transform
F
and the controlled-sum gate
C_{s}
defined as

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{x,y=0}^{2}\omega^{-xy}|x\rangle\langle y|\;,

(29)

\displaystyle C_{s}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{z=0}^{2}|z\rangle\langle z|\otimes W_{0,z}\;.

(30)

Note that
C_{s}(|i,j\rangle)=|i,j-i\rangle
.
Furthermore, we define

\displaystyle|\alpha^{(m)}\rangle:=F^{\dagger}|u_{m}\rangle\;.

(31)

Using (
25
) and
F^{\dagger}W_{k,l}F=\omega^{-kl}W_{-l,k}
we find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\alpha^{(m+2)}\rangle&=F^{\dagger}|u_{m+2}\rangle=F^{\dagger}W_{1,0}|u_{m}\rangle\\
&=F^{\dagger}W_{1,0}F|\alpha^{(m)}\rangle=W_{0,1}|\alpha^{(m)}\rangle\;.\end{aligned}

(32)

Thus, writing
\alpha^{(m)}_{k}=\langle k|\alpha^{(m)}\rangle
, we have
\alpha^{(m+2)}_{i}=\alpha^{(m)}_{i+1}
.
Now we make the ansatz

\displaystyle|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle=\sum_{i,k=0}^{2}\,\psi_{i}\,\alpha^{(i)}_{k}\,|k,k+i\rangle\;.

(33)

A direct computation yields
U|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle=\mathbbm{F}|\psi\rangle
, with the flip operator
\mathbbm{F}=\sum_{r,s=0}^{2}|r,s\rangle\langle s,r|
.
Hence, defining

\displaystyle|\varphi\rangle:=U^{\dagger}\mathbbm{F}U|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\;,

(34)

we obtain with (
27
)

\displaystyle 0>\langle\psi|\,U\,\rho^{\Gamma}\,U^{\dagger}\,|\psi\rangle=\langle\varphi|\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle\;.

(35)

By construction, this
|\varphi\rangle
is a normalized eigenvector of
\rho^{\Gamma}\ngeq 0
with negative eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})<0
, i.e.,
\rho^{\Gamma}|\varphi\rangle=\lambda_{\min}(\rho^{\Gamma})|\varphi\rangle
.
To show that it is also a 1-distillability witness vector for
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{3}\cap\mathrm{NPT}
, and thereby prove Thm.
3
, we have to show that
|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle
has Schmidt rank 2 for some choice of
\psi_{i}
in (
26
), and that
U^{\dagger}\mathbbm{F}U
does not change the Schmidt rank.
First, we rewrite
U^{\dagger}\mathbbm{F}U
as

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}\mathbbm{F}U=(F^{\dagger}\otimes F)\mathbbm{F}\;,

(36)

which implies that it does not change the Schmidt rank.
Second, to show that
|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle
has Schmidt rank 2, we write
|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2}C_{i,j}|i,j\rangle
and investigate its coefficient matrix.
It suffices to only consider the case
\psi_{0}=1/\sqrt{2},\psi_{1}=0,\psi_{2}=-1/\sqrt{2}
, for which we obtain

\displaystyle C=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}^{(0)}&0&-\alpha_{0}^{(2)}\\
-\alpha_{1}^{(2)}&\alpha_{1}^{(0)}&0\\
0&-\alpha_{2}^{(2)}&\alpha_{2}^{(0)}\end{pmatrix}\;.

(37)

The vector
|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle
has Schmidt rank 2 if and only if
C
has rank 2, which is the case if and only if
\det(C)=0
and at least one
2\times 2
(principal) minor is nonzero
8
.
The quantities of interest are thus

\displaystyle\det(C)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(\alpha_{0}^{(0)}\alpha_{1}^{(0)}\alpha_{2}^{(0)}-\alpha_{0}^{(1)}\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\right),

(38)

\displaystyle M_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\det\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}^{(0)}&0\\
-\alpha_{2}^{(2)}&\alpha_{2}^{(0)}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha_{1}^{(0)}\alpha_{2}^{(0)}\right),

(39)

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\det\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}^{(0)}&-\alpha_{0}^{(2)}\\

V
Conclusion and outlook

We showed that all Bell-diagonal qutrit-qutrit states with non-positive partial transposition are 1-distillable, and explicitly constructed a Schmidt rank 2 vector
|\varphi\rangle
that serves as a 1-distillability witness vector for such states.
Consequently, there is no NPT bound entanglement for this subclass of states.
The crucial feature of our construction is that
|\varphi\rangle
is an eigenvector of the partially-transposed state
\rho^{\Gamma}
associated with its unique, three-fold degenerate negative eigenvalue.
Hence, it minimizes the expectation value of the 1-distillability witness
W_{\varphi}=|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|^{\Gamma}
, allowing for noise-resilient 1-distillability certification.
Furthermore, it enables projecting the original resource state onto an entangled bipartite qubit state whose negativity equals the negative eigenvalue of
\rho^{\Gamma}
divided by the success probability of the local filters constructed from
|\varphi\rangle
.
With this initial step, an effective entanglement distillation protocol is achieved.
In particular, the resulting qubit pair can be locally transformed to an NPT qubit Werner state
4
, which is LOCC-equivalent to an NPT qubit isotropic state.
Subsequently applying the stabilizer-based two-copy distillation protocol FIMAX
14
, which is numerically shown to be able to distill all such states
15
, provides a concrete route to maximally entangled qubit states.

In this regard, we note that directly applying FIMAX to an NPT Bell-diagonal qutrit pair can fail, even though the state is 1-distillable.
Indeed, Ref.
15
shows that FIMAX can distill only 83% of low-fidelity NPT Bell-diagonal qutrits, whereas the above-described strategy succeeds for all such states.
This is noteworthy insofar as FIMAX exploits the same group structure as is present in Bell-diagonal systems and is therefore tailor-made for this state family, for which it shows superior performance compared to other recurrent two-copy protocols.
Our results thus reveal the limitations of this stabilizer-based distillation protocol, leaving open the question of whether an algorithmic distillation protocol that works for all
n
-distillable states exists, or whether, in some cases, projecting onto an entangled qubit pair must be considered as an initial step.
Furthermore, it would be interesting to investigate the properties that distinguish FIMAX-distillable from FIMAX-undistillable states, as this could help develop a distillation protocol that applies to all Bell-diagonal states.

Regarding the limitations of the present results, the most striking one is the dimensionality: While our construction works for all
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{3}\cap\mathrm{NPT}
, it generally fails for
d>3
as the Schmidt rank of the constructed
|\varphi\rangle
is
d-1
(with slightly different
\psi_{i}
than above if
d
is even).
Even though this is not directly useful for distilling the
d
-dimensional Bell-diagonal state
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{d}\cap\mathrm{NPT}
, it can nonetheless be used as an initial step to LOCC-transform any NPT Bell-diagonal qudit to a
(d-1)
-dimensional NPT Werner state.

Finally, our results further strengthen the evidence that Bell-diagonal states are highly useful resource states for entanglement distillation compared with other state families, such as Werner states.
In this sense, Werner states appear comparatively hard to distill; indeed, they are often regarded as extremal in this respect, since any NPT state can be transformed by LOCC into an NPT Werner state.
Bell-diagonal states, by contrast, seem to be substantially more amenable to distillation.
Since they also arise naturally in experimental settings under generalized Pauli noise, our findings further support their relevance as practically accessible resource states for entanglement distillation.
