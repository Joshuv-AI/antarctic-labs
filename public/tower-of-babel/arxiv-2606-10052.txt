The span-squares adjunction
George Raptis, Wolfgang Steimle

Abstract
. We show a universal property of the span ∞ \infty -category that yields a description of functors defined on this category. For this, we view the span construction as a functor from double ∞ \infty -categories to ∞ \infty -categories, and show that this functor admits a right adjoint defined by the double ∞ \infty -categories of squares. Using this adjunction, we obtain new proofs of the equivalences between different models of algebraic K K -theory, given by the Q \operatorname{Q} -, the S \operatorname{S} -, the cobordism model, and the squares construction.



1.
Introduction

The span category
\operatorname{Span}(\mathcal{C})
of an (
\infty
-)category
\mathcal{C}
is an important and useful device for expressing combined covariant and contravariant functorialities that are related by base-change formulas. Such situations arise notably in the well-studied context of 6-functor formalisms (where the contravariant functor is given by pull-back of sheaves and the covariant functor extends proper pushforward), see e.g.
[
10
,
21
]
for recent treatments. They arise also in equivariant homotopy theory (where the contravariant functoriality is given by transfer and the base-change formula expresses the Mackey double coset formula), see e.g.
[
2
,
6
]
,
and in the context of higher semiadditivity (where the two functorialities combine to express norm maps), see e.g.
[
9
]
.

In such situations, a functor
\operatorname{Span}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{D}
into another
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}
should exactly integrate these combined covariant and contravariant data in a uniform and orderly way. As usual in higher category theory, the compatibilities between covariant and contravariant structures form a system of higher coherences. Thus, it becomes a complex problem to formulate in a useful way the precise data required for the definition of a functor out of the span
\infty
-category. Motivated by a theorem of Liu–Zheng
[
19
, Theorem 1.4.26]
, the first main goal of this note is to establish a simple such description, formulated in terms of an adjunction (
span-squares adjunction
) between
\infty
-categories and
double
\infty
-categories
. For this purpose, it is crucial to regard the span category as an
(\infty,1)
-category that is functorially associated to a double
\infty
-category (or even a bisimplicial space); this is not to be confused with enhancements of the span category itself to an
(\infty,2)
-category.

Span (
\infty
-)categories and their geometric realizations also arise in algebraic
K
-theory (via Quillen’s
\operatorname{Q}
-construction)
[
23
,
3
]
. Our double-categorical setup has the pleasant feature that one may take opposites in either of the two directions alone. It turns out that this procedure interchanges the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction with (an unpointed, double- and
\infty
-categorical extension of) the Segal-Waldhausen
\mathrm{s}
- and
\operatorname{S}
-construction
[
27
]
. The second main goal of this note is to
draw conclusions of the span-squares adjunction concerning different models of algebraic
K
-theory. Specifically, we obtain natural equivalences between the geometric realizations

|\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{D})|\simeq|\operatorname{S}(\mathcal{D})|\simeq|\mathcal{D}|

for any double
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}
. This gives new proofs and generalizations of the equivalences between various different models of algebraic
K
-theory
[
23
,
27
,
3
,
25
,
8
]
. The right-hand term can be considered as a double
\infty
-categorical version of squares
K
-theory (see
[
8
]
); this version of algebraic
K
-theory has been considered for the
K
-theory of varieties
[
7
]
and for the scissors congruence
K
-theory of manifolds
[
16
,
22
]
.

Conversely, we have a functor which sends a category to its
double category of squares
, defined via

\operatorname{Sq}\colon{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\to\mathrm{DSeg},\quad\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})_{m,n}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}([m]\times[n],\mathcal{C}).

Thus, both the horizontal and the vertical morphisms of
\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})

\mathcal{C}

\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})

\mathcal{C}

Theorem 1.1. (The span-squares adjunction)

The functor
\operatorname{Span}\colon\mathrm{DSeg}\to{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
is left adjoint to the functor
\mathcal{C}\mapsto\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{op}_{2}}
. Moreover,
\operatorname{Sq}(-)^{\mathrm{op}_{2}}\colon{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\to\mathrm{DSeg}
is fully faithful.

Here,
(-)^{\mathrm{op}_{2}}
denotes the opposite in the second/horizontal category direction. Through this adjunction, a functor
f\colon\operatorname{Span}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathcal{C}
is uniquely determined by an associated functor of double categories
\mathcal{D}\to\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{op}_{2}}
, that is, a structure that consists of a functor
f_{v}\colon\mathcal{D}_{v}\to\mathcal{C}
from the vertical category of
\mathcal{D}
to
\mathcal{C}
, a (contravariant) functor
f_{h}\colon\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
from the horizontal category of
\mathcal{D}
, and coherent choices of commutative squares in
\mathcal{C}
that are functorially associated (both vertically and horizontally) to the squares in
\mathcal{D}
. This is clearly related to the type of structure that emerges in the context of the six functors, however, we will not pursue this relation to formalisms or constructions of such structures in this note.

The span category can be defined similarly for arbitrary bisimplicial spaces. Our proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on an identification of the span category in this more general setting. Specifically, for any bisimplicial space
X\in\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
, there is a canonical comparison map of simplicial spaces

w_{X}\colon\delta(X)\to\operatorname{Q}(X^{\mathrm{op}_{2}})\cong\operatorname{S}(X)

where
\delta(X)\in\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
denotes the diagonal simplicial space of
X
, and
\operatorname{Q}(X)\in\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
(the
Q-construction
of
X
) is the simplicial space whose associated category defines the span category of
X
. We show in Theorem
2.9
that
w_{X}
induces an equivalence between the associated categories. Thus, the categorical realization
\mathrm{Diag}(X)\in{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
of
\delta(X)
(the
diagonal category
of
X
) agrees with the span category of
X^{\mathrm{op}_{2}}
. This allows us to interpret the span category as a concrete, often more computable, model for the diagonal category.

In the course of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we will also show that the functor
\operatorname{Span}
has a contractible space of endomorphisms.

Remark 1.2.

Theorem
1.1
remains true after passing from
\mathrm{DSeg}
to its full subcategory spanned by bicomplete double Segal spaces, and also after passing to double Segal spaces that are complete in only the first (or second) direction. This claim is a formal consequence of the fact that the functor
\operatorname{Sq}
takes values in bicomplete double Segal spaces. We mention that, conversely, the span-squares adjunction extends to an adjunction between
{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
and the category
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
of bisimplicial spaces and, in fact, to an adjunction between the category
\operatorname{Seg}
of Segal spaces and the category
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
, see Remark
2.10
below.

Notations and conventions

We denote by
\mathrm{An}
the category of spaces ( = animae) and consider it as a full subcategory of
{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
, the category of (small) categories. We also identify the latter category, through the Rezk nerve, with a full subcategory of the category
\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}=\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{An})
of simplicial spaces. For example, we will usually identify
[n]\in{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
with
\Delta^{n}\in\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
in the notation. We refer to the left adjoints

\mathrm{cat}\colon\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}\to{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad|-|\colon\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}\to\mathrm{An}

of the inclusions as
categorical
and
geometric realization
respectively; the latter functor is just the colimit over
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
and extends the geometric realization of categories. A map of simplicial spaces is called
categorical equivalence
if it becomes an equivalence after categorical realization (and therefore, after geometric realization).

As already explained above, we consider the category of (what we call here) double categories
\mathrm{DSeg}
as the full subcategory of the category
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}:=\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{An})
, consisting of the bisimplicial spaces that satisfy the Segal condition pointwise in both directions
(without any additional completeness assumptions). We refer to the left adjoint
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\to\mathrm{DSeg}
of the inclusion
\mathrm{DSeg}\subset\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
as
double-categorical realization
functor.
\mathrm{DSeg}
is also a full reflective subcategory of the full reflective subcategory of
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
that is spanned by the bisimplicial spaces which satisfy the Segal condition pointwise in only the first (or second) direction. Hence the double-categorical realization functor factors through the reflection into either of these two intermediate full subcategories. We write
[n]\otimes[m]
for the double category that is represented by
([n],[m])\in\Delta\times\Delta
.

Following the standard convention from matrix theory, we will refer to the first (resp., second) simplicial direction in a bisimplicial space as the
vertical
(resp.,
horizontal
) one and draw simplices in diagrams accordingly.

2.
Models of the diagonal category functor

2.1.
The diagonal category

The
diagonal simplicial space

\delta(X)
of a bisimplicial space
X\in\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
is defined as the restriction of
X
along the diagonal functor
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
, and the
diagonal category

\mathrm{Diag}(X)
is its categorical realization:

\mathrm{Diag}\colon\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{cat}}{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}.

Informally, say for
X=\mathcal{D}
a double category, the diagonal category
\mathrm{Diag}(\mathcal{D})
is the category whose morphisms are generated by the squares in
\mathcal{D}
. We will frequently use the computation

\mathrm{Diag}([m]\otimes[n])=[m]\times[n]

which stems from the equivalence of simplicial spaces
\delta([m]\otimes[n])=[m]\times[n]
.

Proposition 2.1.

The diagonal category functor is part of an adjunction

(
\mathrm{Diag}
)

\mathrm{Diag}\colon\mathrm{DSeg}\rightleftarrows{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\colon\operatorname{Sq}

where
\mathrm{Diag}
is the left adjoint. Moreover, the counit transformation

\mathrm{Diag}\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
is an equivalence (so that
\operatorname{Sq}
identifies
{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
with a Bousfield localization of
\mathrm{DSeg}
).

Remark 2.2.

Both functors in this adjunction preserve products and so we also obtain an induced adjunction between symmetric monoidal categories and symmetric monoidal double categories.

Proof.

We first observe that the functor
\delta
is part of an adjunction

\delta\colon\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}\colon\operatorname{Sq}

where the right adjoint
\operatorname{Sq}
is the obvious extension of
\operatorname{Sq}\colon{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\to\mathrm{DSeg}
, defined by the same rule for any
X\in\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
,

\operatorname{Sq}(X)\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{An},\ \operatorname{Sq}(X)_{m,n}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}}([m]\times[n],X).

As
\operatorname{Sq}
sends
{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
to
\mathrm{DSeg}
, we obtain an induced adjunction between
{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
and
\mathrm{DSeg}
, as claimed.

For the second statement, we note that the counit transformation of the adjunction
(\delta,\operatorname{Sq})
is given on
n
-simplices by the map

d^{*}\colon(\delta\operatorname{Sq}(X))_{n}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}}([n]\times[n],X)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}}([n],X)=X_{n}

that restricts along the diagonal
d\colon[n]\to[n]\times[n]
. A map in the other direction is given by precomposition with the minimum function
m\colon[n]\times[n]\to[n]
, thought of as a natural transformation of functors
\Delta\to\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
. Then
d^{*}\circ m^{*}=\mathrm{id}
since
m\circ d=\mathrm{id}
. On the other hand, the natural transformation
H\colon d\circ m\rightarrow\mathrm{id}
of endofunctors on
[n]\times[n]
, thought of as a natural transformation
[n]\times[n]\times[1]\to[n]\times[n]
of functors
\Delta\to\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
, gives rise to a natural transformation
m^{*}\circ d^{*}\rightarrow\mathrm{id}_{\delta\operatorname{Sq}(X)}
. Explicitly, this is defined by the simplicial map
\delta\operatorname{Sq}(X)\times[1]\to\delta\operatorname{Sq}(X)
that is given on
n
-simplices by the rule

(\sigma\colon[n]\times[n]\to X,\;\alpha\colon[n]\to[1])\mapsto([n]\times[n]\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{id},\alpha\circ m)}[n]\times[n]\times[1]\xrightarrow{H}[n]\times[n]\xrightarrow{\sigma}X).

After applying categorical realization (which commutes with products), and evaluating at
X=\mathcal{C}\in{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
, we obtain functors

\mathrm{cat}(d^{*})\colon\mathrm{Diag}\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})\rightleftarrows\mathcal{C}\colon\mathrm{cat}(m^{*})

with
\mathrm{cat}(d^{*})\circ\mathrm{cat}(m^{*})=\mathrm{id}
, and a natural transformation from
\mathrm{cat}(m^{*})\circ\mathrm{cat}(d^{*})
to the identity. It remains to check that the latter natural transformation is an equivalence at each object of
\mathrm{Diag}\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})
. But this is clear from the definition since the natural transformation
H
is stationary for
n=0
.
∎

Remark 2.4.

The essential image of
\operatorname{Sq}
, as part of a Bousfield localization, is given by all double Segal spaces
\mathcal{D}
which are local for the class of
\mathrm{Diag}
-equivalences, i.e., the maps that are inverted by
\mathrm{Diag}
, but it is also possible to identify smaller generating subsets of
\mathrm{Diag}
-equivalences in various ways. For example, consider the set of
\mathrm{Diag}
-equivalences:

S_{1}=\{[m]\otimes[n]\to\operatorname{Sq}([m]\times[n])\ |\ m,n\geq 0\},

given by unit maps of the adjunction
(\mathrm{Diag},\operatorname{Sq})
, and

S_{2}=\{[m]\times[n]_{|v=\mathrm{id}}\to\operatorname{Sq}([m]\times[n])\ |\ m,n\geq 0\},

given also by unit maps of the adjunction
(\mathrm{Diag},\operatorname{Sq})
, where
[m]\times[n]_{|v=\mathrm{id}}
denotes the horizontal category
[m]\times[n]
with identities vertically. Then a bicomplete double Segal space
\mathcal{D}
is in the essential image of
\operatorname{Sq}
if and only if it is
(S_{1}\cup S_{2})
-local. Indeed, in this case, the resulting equivalences

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{DSeg}}([\bullet]\otimes[\ast],\mathcal{D})\leftarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{DSeg}}(\operatorname{Sq}([\bullet]\times[\ast]),\mathcal{D})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{DSeg}}([\bullet]\times[\ast]_{|v=\mathrm{id}},\mathcal{D})=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}}([\bullet]\times[\ast],\mathcal{D}_{h})

identify
\mathcal{D}
with
\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{D}_{h})
, where
\mathcal{D}_{h}
denotes the horizontal subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
.

Despite its foundational importance, the diagonal category is difficult to access in general directly from its definition; the technical reason is that the simplicial space
\delta(X)
rarely is a Segal space, even if
X
is a double category (“squares do not compose diagonally”). Our main result can be viewed as providing a description of
\mathrm{Diag}(X)
in terms of another construction that is more familiar and often more accessible. Namely, in view of Proposition
2.1
, our main result may be reformulated as stating a natural equivalence between
\mathrm{Diag}(X)
and the span category
\operatorname{Span}(X^{\mathrm{op}_{2}})
to be defined below.

2.2.
The span category

The usual span category is the categorical realization of the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction
[
23
,
3
]
,
[
14
]
, which is defined in turn in terms of the twisted arrow category. Our double-categorical variant is defined by replacing the twisted arrow category by a double-categorical variant thereof.

For any
\mathcal{C}\in{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
, the
twisted arrow double category

\operatorname{TwArD}(\mathcal{C})
is the double category defined by

\operatorname{TwArD}(\mathcal{C})\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{An},\ \operatorname{TwArD}(\mathcal{C})_{p,q}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}}([p]\ast[q]^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{C});

the Segal condition in either direction follows from the fact that the join
\ast\colon{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\times{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\to{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
preserves colimits in each variable separately. Evidently, the construction
\operatorname{TwArD}
defines a functor
{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\to\mathrm{DSeg}
.

We define the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction functor
\operatorname{Q}\colon\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\to\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
by setting for any
X\in\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}

\operatorname{Q}(X)\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{An},\ \operatorname{Q}(X)_{n}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}}(\operatorname{TwArD}[n],X),

and then define the
span category
of
X\in\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
to be the category associated to the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction, i.e., it is given by the composite functor

\operatorname{Span}\colon\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Q}}\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{cat}}{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}.

\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{D})
turns out to be a Segal space for a double category
\mathcal{D}
more frequently than
\delta(\mathcal{D})
(see Corollary
3.2
), which makes the span category
\operatorname{Span}(\mathcal{D})
more accessible in general.

Remark 2.5.

The diagonal category of
\operatorname{TwArD}(\mathcal{C})
is the usual twisted arrow category of
\mathcal{C}
; in fact, this is already true for the diagonal simplicial space
\delta(\operatorname{TwArD}(\mathcal{C}))
, which is well known to be a category ( = complete Segal space) in this case.

Remark 2.6.

These definitions of the span category and of the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction generalize the usual ones, which take (single) categories as input. Indeed, for such
\mathcal{C}\in{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
, we have a natural equivalence of simplicial spaces

\operatorname{Q}(\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{C})

between the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction of the double category
\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})
and the usual
\operatorname{Q}
-construction of
\mathcal{C}
(considered as a simplicial space, given by the degreewise groupoid core of a simplicial category that is also often denoted by
\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{C})
). This equivalence is obtained by means of the adjunction
(\delta,\operatorname{Sq})
and Remark
2.5
. It follows that our
\operatorname{Span}(\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C}))
is identified with the usual span category of
\mathcal{C}
.

Moreover, for any (adequate) triple
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger})
in the sense of Barwick
[
6
]
, we may consider the bisimplicial subspace
\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger},\mathrm{cart})\subset\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})
which contains only the
(p,q)
-simplices
X\colon[p]\otimes[q]\to\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})
that send each horizontal morphism
\{i\}\times\{k\leq l\}
to
\mathcal{C}_{\dagger}
, each vertical morphism
\{i\leq j\}\times\{k\}
to
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}
, and each square
\{i<j\}\times\{k<l\}
to a cartesian square. Then the conditions for
\operatorname{TwArD}[n]\to\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})
to lie in
\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger},\mathrm{cart})
match exactly the conditions for the associated functor
\operatorname{TwAr}[n]\to\mathcal{C}
to lie in the usual
\operatorname{Q}
-construction of
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger})
, so that we obtain a natural equivalence of simplicial spaces

\operatorname{Q}(\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger},\mathrm{cart}))\simeq\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger})

with the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction (or effective Burnside
\infty
-category) of this (adequate) triple (see
[
6
, 5.8-5.10]
). Again, we obtain an identification of the corresponding span categories by passing to the associated categories.

2.3.
The step category

The step category of a bisimplicial space
X
is the category
\operatorname{Span}(X^{\mathrm{op}_{2}})
where
(-)^{\mathrm{op}_{2}}
indicates passing to the opposite in the second simplicial direction. Let us make this more explicit. For any
\mathcal{C}\in{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
, the
arrow double category

\operatorname{ArD}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
is given by the double Segal space

\operatorname{ArD}(\mathcal{C})\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{An},\ \operatorname{ArD}(\mathcal{C})_{p,q}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}}([p]\ast[q],\mathcal{C}).

In other words, we have an identification
\operatorname{ArD}(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{TwArD}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}_{2}})
.
By analogy with the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction, this construction also yields a functor
\operatorname{S}\colon\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\to\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
defined for any

by

\operatorname{S}(X)\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{An},\ \operatorname{S}(X)_{n}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}}(\operatorname{ArD}[n],X)

which we refer to as
\operatorname{S}
-construction
, because it is an unpointed variant of the
\operatorname{S}
-construction
[

]
extended to the double-categorical context (see also Section

).
Note that for any

, we have a canonical identification:

(
\operatorname{Q}
vs.
\operatorname{S}
)

\operatorname{S}(X)\simeq\operatorname{Q}(X^{\mathrm{op}_{2}}).

We then define the

of

as the category associated to the
\operatorname{S}
-construction, i.e., it is given by the composite functor

\mathrm{Step}\colon\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{S}}\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{cat}}{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}

\mathrm{Step}(X)\simeq\operatorname{Span}(X^{\mathrm{op}_{2}})
. Informally, the objects of
\mathrm{Step}(\mathcal{D})
, say for
\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{DSeg}
, are just the objects of
\mathcal{D}
, and each “step” in
\mathcal{D}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}

consisting of a horizontal followed by a vertical morphism of

, gives a morphism in
\mathrm{Step}(\mathcal{D})
. (In complete analogy to the span category, a general morphism in
\mathrm{Step}(\mathcal{D})
is a composite of such steps, where the length can be reduced to

\mathrm{Step}(\mathcal{D})
is

Remark 2.7.

Similarly to
\operatorname{TwArD}(\mathcal{C})

\operatorname{ArD}(\mathcal{C})
is the usual arrow category of
\mathcal{C}
, but this is less evident (because the simplicial space

usually fails to be a Segal space) and a consequence of Theorem

and Lemma

below.

Remark 2.8.

This version of the
\operatorname{S}
-construction has also been considered by Juran
[

]
(under the name Cnr) in the situation where it yields a complete Segal space – he has also obtained Corollary

In this context, he proved that the Step category (in our terminology) is canonically equipped with an orthogonal factorization system given by the images of the horizontal and the vertical morphisms, and that it defines an equivalence between a full subcategory of

2.4.
Reformulation of Theorem
1.1

The definition of the step category
\mathrm{Step}(\mathcal{D})=\mathrm{cat}(\operatorname{S}(\mathcal{D}))
was meant to emphasize the analogy with the span category
\operatorname{Span}(\mathcal{D})=\mathrm{cat}(\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{D}))
and unify the
\operatorname{Q}
- and
\operatorname{S}
-constructions in the double-categorical context, but our main result actually amounts to saying that the step category is (again) just the diagonal category.

There is a natural transformation
w\colon\delta\to\operatorname{S}
that is induced by precomposition with the canonical natural inclusion

\operatorname{ArD}[n]\to[n]\otimes[n],\quad(i\leq j)\mapsto(i,j).

Using (

Q

vs.

S

), the following Theorem shows the first part of Theorem
1.1
in a more general context where
\mathrm{DSeg}
is replaced by
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
. It will be shown in Section
4
. (The second part of Theorem
1.1
then follows from Proposition
2.1
.)

Theorem 2.9.

For each bisimplicial space
X
, the natural map

becomes an equivalence after categorical realization.

We remark that the inverse of
\mathrm{cat}(w_{X})\colon\mathrm{Diag}(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Step}(X)
does not arise from a simplicial map
\operatorname{S}(X)\to\delta(X)
before categorical realization.

When applied to a bisimplicial set

thought of as a discrete bisimplicial space, this theorem recovers
[

. In this case,

is a map of simplicial sets (= discrete simplicial spaces), and the composite functor
\mathrm{s}\mathrm{Set}\subset\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}\to{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}
is the localization at the Joyal equivalences (as follows from the work of Joyal–Tierney
[

, see also
[

).

Remark 2.10.

The proof of Theorem
2.9
also shows that

becomes an equivalence already after passing to the associated Segal spaces. In combination with Remark

3.
Analysis of the arrow double category

3.1.
A colimit decomposition of
\operatorname{ArD}[n]
.

The proof of Theorem
2.9
relies essentially on an analysis of the arrow double category of
[n]
. Note that
\operatorname{ArD}[n]\in\mathrm{DSeg}\subset\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
is a bisimplicial set, i.e., bi-levelwise discrete. We denote by
\mathrm{SpineArD}[n]\subseteq\operatorname{ArD}[n]
the bisimplicial subset which is spanned by all objects;
(0,1)
-simplices of the form

(i\leq j)\to(i\leq j+1);

(1,0)
-simplices of the form

(i\leq j)\to(i+1\leq j);

and
(1,1)
-simplices of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(i\leq j)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(i\leq j+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(i+1\leq j)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(i+1\leq j+1);}

\mathrm{SpineArD}[n]
can be depicted in the following way:

(DSpine)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(0\leq 0)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(0\leq 1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(0\leq 2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(0\leq n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(1\leq 1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(1\leq 2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(1\leq n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ddots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(n\leq n).}

We note that this picture is commonly also used to depict the category
\operatorname{Ar}[n]
. However, we distinguish here between the horizontal and vertical edges, and squares are understood in a bisimplicial sense. Moreover, there are no hidden composite morphisms
or squares in
\mathrm{SpineArD}[n]
.

Lemma 3.1.

The inclusion
\mathrm{SpineArD}[n]\hookrightarrow\operatorname{ArD}[n]
becomes an equivalence of double Segal spaces after double-categorical realization.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. The claim is obvious for
n=0
. Let
n>0
and write the bisimplicial set
\mathrm{SpineArD}[n]
as the union of two
subobjects
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
.
A_{1}
is spanned by
(i\leq j)
with
j\leq n-1
, and
A_{2}
is spanned by
(i\leq j)
with
j\geq n-1
, and their intersection
A_{0}
is spanned by
(i\leq j)
with
j=n-1
. This gives a pushout decomposition of
\mathrm{SpineArD}[n]
in
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{Set}
, and hence also in
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
.

By induction, the inclusion of bisimplicial sets

A_{1}\simeq\mathrm{SpineArD}[n-1]\to\operatorname{ArD}[n-1]

becomes an equivalence after double-categorical realization. Hence, given that double-categorical realization preserves colimits, it suffices to show that the canonical inclusion

(*)

\operatorname{ArD}[n-1]\cup_{A_{0}}A_{2}\to\operatorname{ArD}[n]

is an equivalence after double-categorical realization. This claim follows from the stronger assertion that the map (
*
‣
3.1
) is an equivalence of simplicial objects in Segal spaces, regarded levelwise in each vertical degree, i.e., we claim that for each
[p]\in\Delta
, the induced inclusion of simplicial (discrete) spaces

(**)

\operatorname{ArD}[n-1]_{p,\bullet}\cup_{(A_{0})_{p,\bullet}}(A_{2})_{p,\bullet}\to(\operatorname{ArD}[n])_{p,\bullet}

becomes an equivalence after Segal space reflection.

To see this, we observe that the target simplicial space of (
**
‣
3.1
) decomposes canonically into a disjoint union indexed by the maps
\alpha\colon[p]\to[n]
, according to the value of each
(p,q)
-bisimplex
[p]\ast[q]\to[n]
of
\operatorname{ArD}[n]
on the first join summand (which remains constant under the simplicial operations in the second direction). The summand corresponding to
\alpha\colon[p]\to[n]
is (the nerve of) the category

\{\alpha(p)\leq\dots\leq n\}=:\alpha(p)^{+}

since the restriction of a
(p,q)
-bisimplex
[p]\ast[q]\to[n]
on the second join summand can take arbitrary values
\geq\alpha(p)
. This decomposition induces a decomposition of all terms that arise in the map (
**
‣
3.1
), which is compatible with the map and with the pushout decomposition of the domain. Explicitly, the restriction of the map (
**
‣
3.1
) on each summand
\alpha\colon[p]\to[n]
becomes isomorphic to

(\alpha(p)^{+}\cap[n-1])\cup_{\alpha(p)^{+}\cap\{n-1\}}(\alpha(p)^{+}\cap\{n-1\leq n\})\to\alpha(p)^{+}

which is indeed an equivalence after Segal space reflection (also for
\alpha(p)=n
).
∎

To see that

\mathrm{SpineArD}[n]\hookrightarrow\operatorname{ArD}[n]

becomes an equivalence after categorical realization. (Indeed
\delta\colon\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\to\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
sends double-categorical equivalences to categorical equivalences because
\operatorname{Sq}
sends categories to double Segal spaces.) But on
\delta(\mathrm{SpineArD}[n])
the map

was defined to be the identity.

Thus, the functor
\mathrm{Diag}(\operatorname{ArD}[n])\to\operatorname{Ar}[n]
under consideration is indeed a map of posets which is a bijection on objects. It remains to show that it is full. For this, consider

\operatorname{Ar}[n]

i\leq i^{\prime}
and

; then this also defines a morphism
(i,j)\to(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
in

which gives, under the retraction
[n]\times[n]\to\mathrm{Diag}(\operatorname{ArD}[n])

\mathrm{Diag}(\operatorname{ArD}[n])

Remark 4.2.

Using Lemma
4.1
, we find that the colimit decomposition of
\operatorname{ArD}[n]
from Lemma

induces a colimit decomposition of
\operatorname{Ar}[n]
by passing to diagonals. Namely, the composite inclusion

\mathrm{Spine}\operatorname{Ar}[n]:=\delta(\mathrm{SpineArD}[n])\subset\delta(\operatorname{ArD}[n])\subset\operatorname{Ar}[n]

is a categorical equivalence of simplicial spaces. Explicitly,
\mathrm{Spine}\operatorname{Ar}[n]
is the subobject of the simplicial space (or set)
\operatorname{Ar}[n]
that is spanned by
1
-simplices of the form
(i\leq j)\to(i\leq j+1)
and
(i\leq j)\to(i+1\leq j)
, and
2
-simplices of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(i\leq j)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(i+1\leq j)}

It is also possible to first prove this result directly, by an inductive argument, and then to deduce Lemma

Proof of Theorem
2.9
.

We will more generally identify the spaces of natural transformations between functors
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\to{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\subset\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
:

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{Step},\mathrm{Diag}),\ \operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{Diag},\mathrm{Diag}),\ \text{ and }\operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{Step},\mathrm{Step})

and show that they are all contractible. This would readily imply the existence of an (essentially unique) natural transformation
\mathrm{Step}\to\mathrm{Diag}
which is an inverse to
\mathrm{cat}(w)
, as required.

Since
{\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}}\subset\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}
is a full reflective subcategory, the canonical natural transformation
\operatorname{S}\to\mathrm{Step}
induces an identification of spaces of natural transformations of functors
\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}\to\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{Step},\mathrm{Diag})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{S},\mathrm{Diag}).

The space of natural transformations
\operatorname{S}\to\mathrm{Diag}
(viewed as functors on
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
) is identified as follows:

\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{S},\mathrm{Diag})=\lim_{([m]\to[n])\in\operatorname{TwAr}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}})}\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{S}_{n},\mathrm{Diag}_{m})\simeq\lim_{([m]\to[n])\in\operatorname{TwAr}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}})}\mathrm{Diag}_{m}(\operatorname{ArD}[n])

where we have used that
\operatorname{S}_{n}
is represented by
\operatorname{ArD}[n]
and the Yoneda lemma. Moreover, since
X_{m}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{s}\mathrm{An}}([m],X)
naturally in
X
and
[m]\in\Delta
, we can rewrite this last expression as

\lim_{([n]\to[m])\in\operatorname{TwAr}(\Delta)}\operatorname{Hom}([m],\mathrm{Diag}(\operatorname{ArD}[n]))\simeq\operatorname{Hom}([-],\mathrm{Diag}(\operatorname{ArD}[-]))\simeq\operatorname{Hom}([-],\operatorname{Ar}[-])

where we have used Lemmas
4.1
and
3.3
in the last place; the latter
\operatorname{Hom}
is the space of natural transformations between cosimplicial objects in categories (in fact, posets).

Now we note that for any cosimplicial poset
P(-)
, natural transformations
\alpha\colon[-]\to P(-)
are determined by the individual maps
\alpha_{m}\colon[m]\to P(m)
, which in turn are determined by their values
\alpha_{m}(i)
, and the latter are simply determined by the single value of
\alpha
at
0\in[0]
.
In addition, the resulting injective map from
\operatorname{Hom}([-],P(-))
into (the underlying set of)
P(0)
is a bijection, since any choice of

x\in P(0)
extends to the natural transformation that sends
i\in[m]
to
P(i\colon[0]\to[m])(x)
. In summary, we conclude that

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{Step},\mathrm{Diag})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}([-],\operatorname{Ar}[-])\simeq\operatorname{Ar}[0]

is indeed contractible.
Entirely similar arguments also show canonical equivalences

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{Diag},\mathrm{Diag})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\delta,\mathrm{Diag})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}([-],\mathrm{Diag}([-]\otimes[-])\simeq\operatorname{Hom}([-],[-]\times[-])\simeq[0]\times[0]

and

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{Step},\mathrm{Step})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{S},\mathrm{Step})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}([-],\mathrm{Step}(\operatorname{ArD}[-])\simeq\operatorname{Hom}([-],\operatorname{Ar}[-])\simeq\operatorname{Ar}[0],

where we have used Lemma
3.3
again. So all these mapping spaces of natural transformations are again contractible as claimed.
(It would not be difficult to write down explicitly the transformation
\operatorname{S}\to\mathrm{Diag}
using the Yoneda lemma –pointwise–, and then demonstrate the commutativity of the triangles below more explicitly

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Diag}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Step}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cat}(w)}

using, again, the Yoneda lemma.)
∎

Combine Theorem

.

5.
Comparison of models for algebraic
K
-theory

5.1.
Comparison with the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction of exact categories

We now compare our version of the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction and of the span category with the usual
\operatorname{Q}
-construction of an exact category
[
23
,
3
]
.

For an exact (
\infty
-)category
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger})
in the sense of
[
3
,
4
]
, its
\operatorname{Q}
-construction is defined as the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction of this adequate triple. Thus, using Remark
2.6
, we conclude that this agrees with the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction of the double category

\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger},\mathrm{cart})\subset\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C}).

As a consequence of Theorem
2.9
, we deduce the following agreement of models for the algebraic
K
-theory of exact categories: via the
\operatorname{Q}
-construction and via
an
\infty
-categorical generalization of Thomason’s construction
[
27
, end of Section 1.3]
/squares
K
-theory (see
[
8
]
).

Corollary 5.1.

For any exact category
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger})
, we have

|\operatorname{Q}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger})|\simeq|\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{\dagger},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger},\mathrm{cart})|.

5.2.
Comparison with the
\operatorname{S}
-construction of Waldhausen categories

We now compare our version of the
\operatorname{S}
-construction and of the step category with usual
\operatorname{S}
-construction of (unpointed) Waldhausen categories
[
27
,
5
]
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
be an unpointed Waldhausen category, by which we mean an (
\infty
-)category
\mathcal{C}
equipped with a wide subcategory
\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C})
that is closed under cobase change in
\mathcal{C}
. We consider the double Segal subspace

\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{C},\mathrm{cocart})\subset\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})

which contains only the
(p,q)
-simplices
X\colon[p]\otimes[q]\to\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C})
that send each horizontal morphism to
\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C})
, and each square
\{i<j\}\times\{k<l\}
to a cocartesian square. This may be viewed as an
\infty
-categorical generalization of Thomason’s construction (see
[
27
, end of Section 1.3]
).

On the other hand, we also define an
unpointed

\operatorname{S}
-construction
\operatorname{S}^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
as follows.
\operatorname{S}^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
is a simplicial space whose
n
-simplices are given by the space of functors
A\colon\operatorname{Ar}[n]\to\mathcal{C}
required to satisfy the usual conditions of the
\operatorname{S}
-construction
[
27
, Section 1.3]
, except for the condition
A_{i\leq i}=*
. If
\mathcal{C}
has a zero object
\ast
, we denote by
\operatorname{S}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))\subset\operatorname{S}^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
the simplicial subspace of functors
A\colon\operatorname{Ar}[n]\to\mathcal{C}
,
n\geq 0
, such that, in addition,
A_{i\leq i}=\ast
holds.

Proposition 5.2.

The
\operatorname{S}
-construction of
\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{C},\mathrm{cocart})
agrees with the unpointed
\operatorname{S}
-construction
\operatorname{S}^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
.

Proof.

This is the same as the argument from Remark
2.6
, but using the identification
\mathrm{Diag}(\operatorname{ArD}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\operatorname{Ar}(\mathcal{C})
of Lemma
3.3
instead of the (trivial) identification
\mathrm{Diag}(\operatorname{TwArD}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\operatorname{TwAr}(\mathcal{C})
.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
2.9
, we deduce the following agreement of models for the algebraic
K
-theory of (unpointed) Waldhausen categories: via the (unpointed)
\operatorname{S}
-construction and the

\infty
-categorical generalization of Thomason’s construction/squares
K
-theory.

Corollary 5.3.

For any unpointed Waldhausen category
(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
, we have

|\operatorname{S}^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))|\simeq|\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{C},\mathrm{cocart})|.

In addition, we also obtain agreement with the usual (pointed)
\operatorname{S}
-construction.

Proposition 5.4.

For any (pointed) Waldhausen category
(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
, the inclusion of the usual (pointed)
\operatorname{S}
-construction is an equivalence after geometric realization:

|\operatorname{S}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))|\xrightarrow{\simeq}|\operatorname{S}^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))|.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.3
, it suffices to show that the geometric realization of the pointed
\operatorname{S}
-construction is canonically equivalent to the geometric realization of
\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{C},\mathrm{cocart})
. This is shown in
[
27
]
for ordinary Waldhausen categories, but the proof carries over to the
\infty
-categorical setting. Note that the space of
n
-simplices
\operatorname{S}_{n}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
is equivalent to the space of
n
-strings of cofibrations starting at
*
. The inclusion of this space into the geometric realization of the vertical direction in fixed horizontal degree
n
:

|\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{C},\mathrm{cocart})_{\bullet n}|

is an equivalence; the inverse functor is specified (on
0
-simplices) by the rule

(c_{0}\rightarrowtail\dots\rightarrowtail c_{n})\mapsto(*\rightarrowtail c_{1}/c_{0}\rightarrowtail\dots,\rightarrowtail c_{n}/c_{0});

this defines a functor
\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{C},\mathrm{cocart})_{\bullet n}\to\operatorname{S}_{n}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
that extends to the geometric realization.
∎

Remark 5.5.

After some evident modifications, the above results apply also to the case of Waldhausen categories with weak equivalences
(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}),w(\mathcal{C}))

[
5
]
, where
w(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
is a wide subcategory of weak equivalences (satisfying the glue axiom).

Remark 5.6.

Similar arguments apply to variations of the
\operatorname{S}
-construction for other choices of ‘distinguished squares’ instead of (co)cartesian ones. This is especially relevant for the case of the
K
-theory of higher homotopy categories where the distinguished squares are the respective higher weak pushouts (see
[
24
]
).

5.3.
Comparison with the cobordism model for Waldhausen categories

We also discuss the connection with the (
\infty
-categorical extension of the) cobordism category model for
\operatorname{K}
-theory
[
25
]
. This may defined using the language of the present note
as

\mathrm{Cob}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C})):=|\operatorname{Span}(\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{cocart}^{\mathrm{op}})|

where the last condition means that we allow only cartesian squares in
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
, i.e., cocartesian squares in
\mathcal{C}
. Thus, as a consequence of Theorem
2.9
, we obtain the following generalization of the main result of
[
25
]
(with a different proof).

Corollary 5.7.

For an unpointed Waldhausen
\infty
-category
(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))
we have

|\mathrm{Cob}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))|\simeq|\operatorname{S}^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}))|.

Proof.

In view of the previous corollary and Theorem
2.9
, it suffices to prove

|\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{cocart}^{\mathrm{op}})|\simeq|\operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{co}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{C},\mathrm{cocart})|.

Indeed the double Segal space on the left is just the opposite (in both directions) of the second, so their geometric realizations agree.
∎

5.4.
Squares
K
-theory

In summary, using the equivalences for any
X\in\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
(Theorem
2.9
)

\mathrm{Diag}(X)\simeq\mathrm{Step}(X)\simeq\operatorname{Span}(X^{\mathrm{op}_{2}}),

combined with
|X|\simeq|X^{{}^{\mathrm{op}_{2}}}|
, we conclude:

Corollary 5.8.

For any
X\in\mathrm{ss}\mathrm{An}
, there are natural equivalences of spaces

|X|\simeq|\delta(X)|\simeq|\operatorname{S}(X)|\simeq|\operatorname{Q}(X)|.

In view of the previous comparison results of models for algebraic
K
-theory, we may define
\operatorname{K}
-theory in the general setting of pointed double Segal spaces (or just bisimplicial spaces) as

\operatorname{K}=\Omega|-|\colon\mathrm{DSeg}_{*}\to\mathrm{An}.

Indeed, this definition is a double
\infty
-categorical generalization of squares
K
-theory
[
8
]
. Double
\infty
-categories seem to be a natural setting for
K
-theory because the abstract datum of a square enables us to formulate an inclusion-exclusion principle. Special instances of squares
K
-theory have recently been studied for ordinary (double) categories with weaker categorical properties than exact or Waldhausen categories or with different decomposition conditions, such as varieties (see e.g.
[
7
]
) and manifolds
[
16
,
22
]
.
