High expression of CD74 is associated with poor prognosis and tumor immune infiltration in Acute Myeloid Leukemia
Authors: Yixi Chen, Zihan Zhang, Ruotong Li, Shun Ding, Xinbao Hao, Yinglian Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the expression patterns of the CD74 gene in Acute Myeloid Leukemia (LAML), its clinical prognostic significance, and its association with the tumor microenvironment (TME).MethodsData from public databases, including TCGA and GTEx, were integrated to analyze the differences in CD74 expression between LAML tissues and normal controls; perform subgroup analysis based on clinical and pathological characteristics; evaluate its prognostic value using survival analysis and ROC curves; elucidate its potential immune regulatory mechanisms through the CIBERSORT algorithm and functional enrichment analysis.ResultsCD74 was significantly overexpressed in LAML tissues, with higher expression observed in the monocyte-derived subtypes (M4–M7) and in patients with high white blood cell counts (P < 0.05). High CD74 expression is significantly associated with poor overall survival in patients with LAML(P < 0.01). Immunohistochemical analysis revealed that CD74 expression was positively correlated with the abundance of memory B cells, monocytes, and M2 macrophages, and was primarily enriched in antigen-presenting and immune response pathways.ConclusionHigh CD74 expression is associated with poor prognosis in patients with LAML and may promote disease progression by reshaping the immune microenvironment; it holds promise as a new prognostic marker and immunotherapy target.Keywords: Acute Myeloid Leukemia, CD74, Prognosis, Biomarker, Immune infiltration

Introduction

LAML is a malignant blood disorder characterized by the proliferation of immature myeloid cells in the bone marrow. It has the second-highest incidence rate among all types of leukemia and is particularly common in the elderly. According to global statistics, the annual incidence of LAML is approximately 3 to 5 cases per 100,000 people, and the prognosis is poor, with a five-year survival rate of only 25% to 30% [1]. The pathogenesis of LAML is complex, involving multiple genetic and environmental factors, and its clinical manifestations and biological characteristics are highly heterogeneous. Although the introduction of new drugs in recent years has improved the prognosis for some patients, traditional treatment regimens, such as the 7 + 3 regimen (seven days of cytarabine followed by three days of anthracyclines), remain the primary treatment for most LAML patients. For elderly patients and those with comorbidities, low-intensity treatment regimens such as venetoclax combined with low-dose cytarabine have become the new standard of care [2]. However, the high recurrence rate and drug resistance of LAML remain major challenges in clinical treatment, particularly among patients with relapsed and refractory LAML, where new therapeutic strategies are urgently needed to improve survival rates [3].

CD74, a type I single-pass transmembrane protein, is a key chaperone for MHC class II molecules and plays a central role in mediating the processing and presentation of exogenous antigens. By occupying the MHC class II binding pocket, it prevents the peptide from binding abnormally. When antigen presentation is initiated, CD74 is degraded; the resulting class II-associated constant-chain peptide (CLIP) remains temporarily bound in the MHC class II binding pocket. Only after this fragment is completely removed can the target peptide bind to it, thereby initiating the subsequent antigen presentation process [4]. Consistent with its antigen-presenting function, this molecule is not only stably expressed in professional antigen-presenting cells, such as macrophages, dendritic cells, and B cells, but also detected in epithelial cells under inflammatory conditions [5]. It is worth noting that CD74 is overexpressed in a variety of malignant tumors, including hematologic malignancies such as B-cell lymphoma, T-cell lymphoma, LAML, multiple myeloma (MM), and chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), as well as gastrointestinal solid tumors [4, 6–12]. CD74 has emerged as a promising immunotherapy target; in preclinical models of B-cell and T-cell lymphomas, multiple myeloma, and solid tumors, anti-CD74 therapy has demonstrated clear antitumor activity [8, 10, 13–15]. Recently published preclinical data indicate that STRO-001, a novel targeted drug under development for B-cell lymphoma, has demonstrated significant cytotoxic effects against LAML and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) cells in both in vitro experiments and patient-derived xenograft (PDX) mouse models [16].

In this study, we investigated CD74 expression in various malignant tumors and compared its expression between the blood of LAML patients and that of healthy controls. Subsequently, based on the logic of hematopoietic differentiation, we divided the FAB subtypes into two groups and analyzed CD74 expression levels. We further analyzed CD74 expression levels in the blood of patients with diverse clinical characteristics and assessed the impact of CD74 gene expression on the prognosis of LAML. Finally, we investigated CD74 immune infiltration in LAML and performed GO functional and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses to identify the biological processes and key signaling pathways in which CD74 is involved.

Materials and methods

Bioinformatics analysis

Data sources and preprocessing

The LAML transcriptomic data and corresponding clinical and pathological information used in this study were primarily obtained from the UCSC XENA database (https://xena.ucsc.edu/). UCSC XENA is an open-source, scalable platform for genomic data visualization and analysis developed by the University of California, Santa Cruz (UCSC). It is designed to integrate and interactively display multi-omics data and clinical phenotype information from TCGA, GTEx, and user-defined sources [17]. Using the GEPIA 2 platform (https://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/)—an online tool that provides interactive analysis of gene expression profiles between cancerous and normal tissues based on TCGA and GTEx data—we compared gene expression differences between LAML tissues and normal peripheral blood/bone marrow tissues [18]. Data on immune cell infiltration levels were obtained using the TIMER database (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/) and the CIBERSORT algorithm. TIMER is a comprehensive resource for systematically evaluating immune cell infiltration levels across various cancer types. At the same time, CIBERSORT is a deconvolution algorithm that uses gene expression profiles to infer cell-type composition using support vector regression [19]. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network data were retrieved from the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org), which contains known and predicted protein interactions, including both direct physical binding and indirect functional associations [20]. All data cleaning, standardization, and subsequent statistical analyses were performed using R (v4.4.2) and its associated packages.

Analysis of CD74 gene expression differences

The limma package in R was used to standardize and perform differential expression analysis on the raw data downloaded from the XENA database. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare CD74 gene expression levels between LAML and normal control samples; simultaneously, the expression profiles of CD74 across various cancer types and their corresponding normal tissues were analyzed. Furthermore, the GEPIA 2 platform was used to further validate the expression patterns of CD74 across different tumor types. The threshold for statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Analysis of CD74 expression and baseline data

Based on clinical data from the TCGA-LAML cohort, including age, sex, ethnicity, white blood cell (WBC) count, proportion of blast cells in bone marrow and peripheral blood, cytogenetic risk stratification, and FAB classification, this study examines the relationship between CD74 expression levels and clinical characteristics to assess the correlation between CD74 expression and various clinical indicators.

Survival analysis and assessment of prognostic value

To evaluate the impact of CD74 expression on the prognosis of LAML patients, we analyzed data on overall survival (OS) and relapse-free survival (RFS). Patients were grouped based on the median CD74 expression level. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were plotted using the `survival` package in R, and the Log-Rank test was used to compare survival differences between groups; a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. To further quantify the predictive performance of CD74 for survival outcomes, Perl (v5.30.0) was used to extract relevant expression profile data. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed using the `timeROC` package in R, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to assess their diagnostic value.

Immune cell infiltration and correlation analysis

The CIBERSORT algorithm (https://cibersortx.stanford.edu/) was used to estimate the relative abundance of 22 immune cell types in LAML samples via deconvolution. Samples with CIBERSORT P < 0.05 were considered reliable and used for subsequent analysis. Correlations with |ρ| > 0.3 were regarded as biologically meaningful. The ggpubr and vioplot packages in R were used to visualize differences in immune cell infiltration levels between high- and low-CD74 expression groups. In addition, Spearman’s correlation analysis was used to assess the association between CD74 expression levels and the abundance of various immune cell subsets; a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Protein interaction networks and functional enrichment analysis

Construct an interaction network for the CD74 protein using the STRING database. The search parameters are set as follows: species is restricted to “Homo sapiens”; network type is set to “full STRING network”; confidence score is set to medium or higher (> 0.400); and the maximum number of interacting factors is limited to 20. Based on the selected interacting proteins, the Spearman correlation method is used to analyze their correlation with CD74. Subsequently, we performed Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis on CD74 and its significantly associated interacting proteins (https://www.kegg.jp/). The GO analysis covers three dimensions: molecular function (MF), cellular component (CC), and biological process (BP); the KEGG analysis aims to identify relevant signaling pathways. Enrichment analysis was performed using Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp) to elucidate potential biological mechanisms underlying CD74’s role in the development and progression of LAML.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) to detect the expression level of CD74

THP-1 cells and human peripheral blood CD14+monocytes were collected, and total RNA was extracted using the TRIzol method. The RNA was then reverse-transcribed into cDNA, followed by PCR amplification. The reaction conditions were as follows: 5-minute pre-denaturation at 95 °C, followed by 40 cycles of 30-second denaturation at 95 °C and 60-second annealing and extension at 60 °C. GAPDH was used as the internal control, and the relative expression levels of the target genes were calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT method.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0 and R 4.4.2 software. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. T-tests were used for comparisons between groups, and nonparametric tests were used for continuous variables that did not follow a normal distribution.

Discussion

Although several new drugs have been introduced for the treatment of acute myeloid leukemia, most patients’ treatment regimens currently still rely primarily on cytarabine and anthracyclines. Causes of death among patients with LAML typically include severe infections, life-threatening bleeding, and limited access to targeted therapies and CAR-T treatments [21, 22]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for more effective targeted therapies for LAML.

CD74 is highly expressed in various cancers and is highly expressed in some pediatric LAML patients, where it is associated with prognosis [23]. Different types of tumors exhibit significant differences in gene expression, and these abnormal expression patterns may lead to different clinical outcomes [24–27]. To determine CD74 expression levels across various cancers, we analyzed tumor samples from the TCGA and GETX databases. Analysis of overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) data revealed that CD74 can serve as a prognostic marker for LAML, with high CD74 expression indicating a poorer prognosis for patients.

Previous studies have demonstrated that different FAB subtypes of LAML exhibit significant heterogeneity in protein expression. Twenty-four signaling and apoptosis-related proteins show characteristic differences in expression between the pure myeloid subtypes (M0–M2) and the monocyte-associated subtypes (M4–M5), and can serve as molecular markers for subtype differentiation [28]. This study classified AML patients into M0–M3 and M4–M7 groups based on the hematopoietic differentiation pattern, cellular lineage characteristics, and the classical criteria of the FAB classification system. In the M0–M3 subgroups, leukemic cells were blocked at the early stage of myeloid hematopoiesis, predominantly consisting of primitive and promyelocytic cells with a single lineage and low differentiation maturity. In contrast, the M4–M7 subgroups were characterized by multilineage myeloid differentiation; besides myeloid primitive cells, leukemic blasts also exhibited differentiation toward monocytic, erythroid, and megakaryocytic lineages, accompanied by higher cellular heterogeneity and differentiation maturity [29]. This study further found that the CD74 gene also exhibited significant differences across different FAB subtypes of LAML (low expression in M0–M3 subtypes and high expression in M4–M7 subtypes, P < 0.001), This is consistent with the cellular differentiation status reflected by FAB classification (predominantly myeloid progenitor cells vs. mixed multilineage cells), suggesting that CD74 may be involved in the regulation of LAML cell differentiation and providing new evidence for the molecular mechanisms underlying FAB classification.

The TME is characterized by complex biological interactions that significantly modulate the body’s antitumor immune response and directly influence the efficacy of immunotherapy. Cytokines within the TME can mediate immune suppression, thereby hindering the effectiveness of immunotherapy. Relevant studies have demonstrated that elucidating the regulatory mechanisms of various TME components provides important theoretical support for enhancing the efficacy of immunotherapy [30–32]. Bioinformatics analysis based on the TCGA database in this study revealed that CD74 expression levels in AML patients are significantly positively correlated with the degree of M2 macrophage infiltration in the tumor microenvironment. This finding is highly consistent with the previously reported key role of the MIF-CD74 signaling pathway in regulating macrophage polarization. As a functional receptor for MIF, CD74 mediates MIF-induced polarization of macrophages toward the M2 phenotype and enhances their chemotaxis and infiltration capabilities [33]. Therefore, we hypothesize that CD74, which is highly expressed by AML cells, may promote the infiltration and polarization of M2 macrophages by binding to MIF in the tumor microenvironment, thereby establishing an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and driving the progression of AML. This is the first time that a positive correlation between CD74 expression and M2 macrophage infiltration has been demonstrated in LAML. Moving forward, we will conduct systematic experimental studies to further validate and elucidate the regulatory relationship between the MIF-CD74 signaling axis and M2 macrophages.

The binding of macrophage inhibitory factor (MIF) to its receptor CD74 is central to the regulation of inflammation and immunity. As a type II transmembrane glycoprotein, CD74 undergoes a conformational change upon binding to MIF and recruits co-receptors, thereby initiating downstream signaling cascades. This pathway primarily activates the Syk kinase, which in turn initiates the ERK/MAPK and PI3K/Akt pathways, regulating cytokine release and cell survival, respectively [34, 35]. Functionally, this signaling axis plays a decisive role in immune cell migration. Recent studies have shown that the interaction between MIF and CD74 promotes the binding of CD74 to CXCR4, thereby specifically regulating the directed migration of T cells, revealing its fine-tuning role in adaptive immune responses [36]. This study found a significant positive correlation between white blood cell count and CD74 gene expression; the CD74 expression level in the high white blood cell count group (> 20 × 10⁹/L) was significantly higher than that in the low white blood cell count group. These findings suggest that, under conditions of high leukocyte burden, the body may respond to potential infections or stress by upregulating CD74 expression to enhance antigen presentation or amplify MIF-mediated pro-inflammatory signaling pathways. In addition, high CD74 expression may also reflect the proliferation of immature myeloid cells during the active phase of bone marrow hematopoiesis; such cells are often associated with specific immunophenotypic characteristics [23]. This finding not only reveals the intrinsic link between changes in peripheral blood cell counts and the expression of specific immune molecules but also offers a new perspective on understanding the remodeling of the immune microenvironment in inflammatory diseases or hematologic malignancies.

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis in this study showed that high CD74 expression was significantly associated with shorter overall survival in AML patients, and time-dependent ROC curves also indicated that it possesses certain prognostic predictive value. To further clarify its independent prognostic value, we conducted a multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis. After adjusting for age, white blood cell count, cytogenetic risk, and FAB classification, the results showed that CD74 expression was not an independent prognostic factor for AML. However, we found that studies based on prospective multicenter cohorts have reported different results. Specifically, a study by Attar et al. on a cohort of young (age < 60 years) patients with cytogenetically normal acute myeloid leukemia (CN-AML) demonstrated that, after adjusting for potential confounding factors such as FLT3-TKD mutations, WT1 mutations, and BAALC expression levels using a multivariate Cox regression model, high CD74 expression remained an independent risk factor for reduced overall survival (OS) (HR = 1.58, 95% CI: 1.11–2.25; P = 0.01) [37]. Subsequently, Rassidakis et al. used reverse protein array (RPPA) technology to further validate the prognostic value of CD74 protein levels in 231 adult AML patients. The results showed that the median overall survival (OS) in the high-expression group was significantly shorter than that in the low-expression group (36.6 weeks vs. 48.6 weeks, P < 0.05), and multivariate analysis again confirmed its independent prognostic significance [38]. In the future, we will further investigate the prognostic value of CD74 by conducting analyses using an independent clinical sample.

To better understand the molecular function of CD74, we used STRING analysis to identify the top 20 proteins most closely associated with the protein encoded by the CD74 gene and performed GO and KEGG enrichment analyses. Functional enrichment analysis indicated that CD74 plays a central role in biological processes such as MHC class II-mediated antigen processing and presentation, immune cell activation, and the regulation of immune networks. KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that the top three pathways significantly enriched in AML all focus on immune recognition and autoimmune responses: allograft rejection, graft-versus-host disease, and type 1 diabetes pathways. The core functions of these pathways all involve MHC-mediated antigen presentation, T-cell activation, and inflammatory cascades. Among them, the first two pathways are highly correlated with the graft-versus-leukemia (GVL) effect following allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation for AML; their enrichment suggests abnormal activation of immune recognition pathways in AML patients. The type 1 diabetes pathway reflects an imbalance in the autoimmune-like microenvironment in AML and may be involved in regulating the immune evasion of leukemic cells. In summary, the results of this analysis provide important insights into the dysregulation of the AML immune microenvironment and the identification of targets for immunotherapeutic intervention.

Based on the TCGA database, this study confirmed that high expression of the CD74 gene is significantly associated with poor prognosis in patients with LAML. The AUC values at 1, 3, and 5 years were 0.632, 0.690, and 0.730, respectively, indicating a trend toward increasing predictive performance over time. Previous studies have demonstrated that CD74 influences the progression of LAML by regulating the MIF/ERK signaling pathway and participating in the polarization of leukemia-associated macrophages; furthermore, it has been reported to be closely associated with survival outcomes in pediatric LAML cohorts [23, 39]. This study further validated the association between CD74 expression and long-term prognosis in adult LAML; its predictive value for long-term outcomes (AUC = 0.730) was superior to that for short-term outcomes, suggesting that it may play a greater role in regulating long-term disease recurrence or drug resistance mechanisms rather than merely inducing an early response to chemotherapy. Although the AUC values in this study were moderate, due to the high heterogeneity of LAML, CD74, as a potential prognostic factor, offers value by providing a new molecular dimension for risk stratification. Although established biomarkers and prognostic models exist for LAML, the characterization of the immune microenvironment remains inadequate, and there is a lack of directly translatable targets. This study demonstrates that high CD74 expression is associated with poor prognosis, with a 5-year AUC of 0.730. It serves both as a prognostic stratification marker and an indicator of the immune microenvironment, acting as a single-gene hub linking immune infiltration and leukemia progression, similar to the WDR5-H3K4me3–TRMT6 regulatory axis in triple-negative breast cancer [40]. CD74 is expected to complement existing models to improve predictive accuracy, offering potential for both companion diagnostics and targeted therapy, thereby highlighting its clinical translational value.

This study has several limitations that must be carefully considered when interpreting the results and translating them into clinical practice. First, there is inherent bias in the sample comparison. CD74 is highly expressed in normal B cells and monocytes; however, since this study compared expression levels between bone marrow fluid from LAML patients and whole blood from healthy individuals, it cannot rule out interference from normal immune cells, which may introduce bias into the analysis of expression differences. Second, confounding factors exist in the subtype analysis. The M4 and M5 subtypes originate from granulomonocytes and monocytes, respectively, and CD74 is highly expressed in the monocyte lineage. The high expression of CD74 in the M4–M7 group may simply reflect an inherent expression characteristic of monocyte lineage cells rather than a leukemia-specific pathological regulatory mechanism; this confounding factor has not been fully ruled out. Third, the immune infiltration analysis has methodological flaws. The CIBERSORT algorithm was originally designed for the deconvolution of immune cells in solid tumors, whereas LAML tumor cells are inherently myeloid/ monocytic primitive immune cells. This algorithm cannot distinguish between normally infiltrating monocytes and leukemic monocytic progenitor cells; therefore, the positive correlation between CD74 and monocytes and M2-type macrophages may reflect the intrinsic expression characteristics of M4/M5 subtype leukemic cells rather than true differences in immune microenvironment infiltration. Fourth, the survival analysis was inadequately adjusted; it did not account for treatment regimens, FLT3-ITD, NPM1, and other key prognostic mutations, and its independent clinical predictive value remains unclear. Furthermore, this study is a retrospective bioinformatics analysis based solely on the single-center TCGA database, lacking validation in multicenter, large-sample external clinical cohorts; it only conducted experiments at the cellular level and thus cannot elucidate the specific molecular mechanisms by which CD74 regulates LAML progression.
