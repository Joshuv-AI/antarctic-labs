Asymptotic behavior of tropical rank functions
Ana Maria Botero, Alex Küronya, Eduardo Vital

Abstract
. We show that asymptotic behavior of the two main competing notions of rank of a linear series on a tropical curve is governed by a single invariant, closely paralleling the theory of volumes in algebraic geometry. In addition, we show that the tropical volume is compatible with the tropicalization of curves.



1.
Introduction

Divisor theory on graphs and tropical curves has developed into a powerful combinatorial counterpart of classical algebraic geometry. Since the foundational work of M. Baker and S. Norine in
[
3
]
, linear series on graphs have provided a discrete analog of the theory of divisors on algebraic curves, leading to tropical versions of the Riemann–Roch theorem and Brill–Noether theory
[
8
]
. Our purpose here is to initiate a study of the asymptotic point of view.

A central invariant of a divisor on a curve is the
rank
of the associated linear series. In the tropical setting, several notions of rank have been proposed. The
Baker–Norine rank
, defined originally for graphs and extended to metric graphs, captures the combinatorial structure of chip-firing, and has been fundamental for the development of Brill–Noether theory. Other notions of rank on a metric graph, such as the
independence rank
, introduced by D. Jensen and S. Payne in
[
19
]
, arise from considering a notion of tropical linear independence. Although these notions agree (up to
\pm 1
) in many cases, they do not coincide in general, and understanding their relationship remains an important open problem in tropical geometry; see e. g.
[
7
, § 9]
.

In classical algebraic geometry, an effective way to study linear series is through
asymptotic invariants
. Given a divisor
D
on a projective variety
X
, one studies the asymptotic growth of the spaces of sections
H^{0}\big((X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD)\big)
, as
m\to\infty
. The resulting invariant—called the
volume
of
D
—has remarkable formal properties: it depends only on the numerical class of
D
, it is homogeneous of degree the dimension of
X
, and it is log-concave on the cone of big divisors. These properties play an important role in modern birational geometry and the study of linear series; see for instance
[
21
]
or
[
9
]
, as well as in the theory of asymptotic cohomology developed in
[
20
]
. In situations with a strong combinatorial flavour, such as the case of toric varieties, the volume admits an especially precise description in terms of convex geometry
[
16
]
.

Motivated by these developments, we study asymptotic invariants of linear series on multigraphs and tropical curves, focusing on the growth rate of tropical ranks. More concretely, given a divisor
D
on a multigraph or tropical curve
\Gamma
, we consider the asymptotic behavior of the rank of the linear series associated to
mD
, as
m\to\infty
. This leads to a natural notion of the
tropical volume
.

The main result of this paper is that the asymptotic behavior is independent of the choice of the notion of rank one chooses. In particular, the associated tropical volume is determined by
\Gamma
and
D
only, and satisfies an asymptotic Riemann–Roch formula.

Theorem A

(
4.11
and
4.13
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a tropical curve, equivalently a metric multigraph, and let
D\in\Div(\Gamma)
a divisor. Then

\vol_{\mathrm{ind},\Gamma}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(D)=\vol_{\mathrm{BN},\Gamma}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(D)=\max\{\deg(D),0\},

where
\vol_{\mathrm{ind},\Gamma}^{{\mathbb{T}}}
and
\vol_{\mathrm{BN},\Gamma}^{{\mathbb{T}}}
denote the volume with respect to the independence and Baker–Norine ranks, respectively. Moreover, we obtain an asymptotic Riemann–Roch formula

\chi(\ell D)=\deg(D)\ell+o(1),

where
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\chi(\ell D)
is the Euler characteristic of the divisor
\ell D
.

One has a priori two Euler charactertistics
\chi_{\mathrm{ind}}
and
\chi_{\mathrm{BN}}
(corresponding to independence and Baker–Norine ranks) that do not agree in general. In fact,
Section 4
shows that Riemann–Roch is not satisfied for the independence rank in general. Even so, the above asymptotic result holds for both. In
Section 3
we comment on the relation between the combinatorial and the metric multigraph case.

Let us note here that the combinatorial statement in
Section 2
is not immediately implied by the metric multigraph statement in
Section 3
. Namely, given a multigraph
G
, we set the length of each edge
e\in E(G)
to be
1
, to define
\Gamma
, the metric multigraph associated with
G
. When
G
is not only a singleton, then both
\Div(\Gamma)
and
\Prin(\Gamma)
are strictly larger than
\Div(G)
and
\Prin(G)
, respectively. In
[
6
, pg. 618]
it was conjectured that the BN-rank in
G
and
\Gamma
would agree. It is easily verified that the conjecture is not true in full generality (take the example of a multigraph
G
that consists of just one loop). Nevertheless, it was shown in
[
17
, Thm. 1.3]
that the conjecture holds for loopless multigraphs.

If
\Gamma
is a multigraph, then
2.10
provides an asymptotic Riemann–Roch formula. We point out that there exists no Riemann–Roch theorem for multigraphs (i.e. allowing loops), in particular, the Riemann–Roch theorem of Baker–Norine is not valid for multigraphs. Indeed, consider the multigraph
G
which is a loop with one vertex. There have been various proposals to treat loops for the purpose of Riemann–Roch; e.g. by considering weights on the vertices; see
[
1
]
.
2.10
shows that an asymptotic version always holds (even without weights).

It follows directly from
Theorem A
that the resulting tropical volume
\vol_{\Gamma}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(\cdot)
shares several fundamental properties of the algebraic setting: it is homogeneous of degree one, and it is log-concave on the space of divisor classes. This provides some initial evidence that the tropical volume is the correct asymptotic invariant of linear series in this setting.
Finally, we show that the tropical volume function is well-behaved with respect to the tropicalization map; see
subsection 5.1
and
Equation 7
.

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section 2
, we study the asymptotic behavior of the Baker–Norine rank for divisors on multigraphs, introduce the notion of tropical volume, and prove
2.10
, showing that the asymptotic behavior of the
\mathrm{BN}
-rank depends only on the degree; we also establish an asymptotic Riemann–Roch theorem for multigraphs in
2.11
.
In
Section 3
, we extend this analysis to tropical curves, where the main result,
3.7
, shows that tropical volume admits the same simple description as in the combinatorial case. In
Section 4
, we introduce the independence rank of
{\mathbb{T}}
-submodules of
R(D)
and prove that it has the same asymptotic behavior as the Baker–Norine rank,
4.11
, leading to a unified notion of tropical volume and an asymptotic Riemann–Roch theorem,
4.13
. Finally, in
Section 5
, we relate these tropical results to classical algebraic geometry via tropicalization, showing that tropical volume is compatible with this process; see
subsection 5.1
.

2.
Asymptotics of the Baker–Norine rank of linear series on multigraphs

Here, we study the asymptotic behavior of the Baker–Norine rank for divisors on multigraphs i.e. we allow loops and multiple edges. We prove an asymptotic Riemann–Roch theorem for multigraphs. We also introduce the notion of tropical volume via the growth of ranks of multiples of a divisor, establish bounds for the rank, and prove that the asymptotic behavior depends only on the degree. This culminates in an explicit formula for the tropical volume in the combinatorial setting.

Let
S
be a finite set, and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We define
\binom{S}{n}\coloneqq\{S^{\prime}\subseteq S\mid\#S^{\prime}=n\}
.
A
multigraph

(V,E,\varepsilon)

is the set of
edges

\varepsilon:E\to\binom{V}{1}\cup\binom{V}{2}

In words, a multigraph is a graph that allows loops and multiple edges. We say that a multigraph
G=(V,E,\varepsilon)
is finite if both
V
and

are finite. Here, we consider only finite connected multigraphs.
Given a vertex

of

\val(v)
is the number of edges adjacent to it. If

is a loop, we consider that
\val\!\big(\varepsilon(e)\big)=2
; we refer the reader to
[
10

for more details concerning multigraphs.

Divisors on multigraphs

Let

be a multigraph. We define
\Div(G)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{v\in V(G)}{\mathbb{Z}}v
, the group of divisors on

, the free abelian group generated by

. An element

is called a

D=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_{v}v,\quad\text{ for }\quad d_{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

We use the definitions and notation of the theory of divisors on graphs from
[
3
]
, with the difference that here we allow loops.

If
G
is a loop at the vertex

D
at

. Moreover,
D\sim D^{\prime}
if and only if there exists a finite sequence of chip-firing that takes

As chip-firing is a commutative operation, for

Linear series on multigraphs

Let

Definition 2.1

The

|D|\coloneqq\{D^{\prime}\in\Div_{+}(G)\;|\;D^{\prime}\sim D\}.

The

r(D)\coloneqq\begin{cases}-1,\text{ if }|D|=\varnothing,\\
\max\{d\;|\;\forall E\in\Div_{+}^{d}(G),\;|D-E|\neq\varnothing\},\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Remark 2.2
.

If
D
has negative degree, then
r(D)=-1
.
In this case, it follows that
-1\leq r(\ell D)\leq 0
for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with
r(\ell D)=0
only if
\ell=0
.
Moreover, if
\deg(D)\geq-1

\deg(D)\geq r(D)
. Namely, suppose

E^{\prime}\in|D-E|

\deg(E^{\prime})=\deg(D)-r(D)<0,

a contradiction.

Definition 2.3
.

The

\displaystyle\vol_{G}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(D)\coloneqq\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{r(\ell D)}{\ell}.

We say that

As the space

\frac{\ell\deg(D)}{\ell}\geq\frac{r(\ell D)}{\ell}\geq\frac{\ell\deg(D)-C_{G}}{\ell},

Given

we define its

as

\chi(D)\coloneqq r(D)-r(K_{G}-D),

where
K_{G}
denotes the canonical divisor on

Since our multigraphs

does not satisfy the Riemann–Roch theorem in general. The following theorem shows that, asymptotically, it behaves as expected.

Theorem 2.11

(Asymptotic Riemann–Roch for multigraphs)
.

Let

, then

\chi(\ell D)=\deg(D)\ell+o(1).

Proof.

If
\deg(D)\geq 0
, then

is bounded. Thus, by

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{\chi(\ell D)-\deg(D)\ell}{\ell}=0.

If
\deg(D)<0
, then

for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For a large enough fixed
\ell^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, it follows that
\deg\!\big((K_{G}-\ell^{\prime}D)-\ell D\big)\geq-1
and

by

. Thus, by

and by

it follows that

\deg\!\big((K_{\Gamma}-\ell^{\prime}D)-\ell D\big)\geq r\big((K_{\Gamma}-\ell^{\prime}D)-\ell D\big)\geq r(K_{\Gamma}-\ell^{\prime}D)+r(-\ell D).

4.
Asymptotics of the independence rank of linear series on tropical curves

We recall the
independence rank
, a notion arising from tropical linear algebra, and compare it with the Baker–Norine rank. We study its algebraic and combinatorial properties, emphasizing key differences such as the failure of subadditivity. Despite these differences, we prove that both ranks have the same asymptotic behavior. This leads to a unified notion of tropical volume and an asymptotic Riemann–Roch theorem.

The
tropical semiring
is the triple
({\mathbb{T}},\oplus,\odot)
, where
{\mathbb{T}}\coloneqq{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\oplus:{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(a,b)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a+b}

and

and

for all

[
12
, § 1.2]

{\mathbb{T}}
and its different models.

-module

and all

(TM1)

(t\odot t^{\prime})m=t(t^{\prime}m)
;

(TM2)

5.2.
Extended tropicalization

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field, which is complete with respect to a nontrivial non-archimedean valuation
\nu\colon K^{*}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. The usual tropicalization map associates to a closed subvariety

X
of the torus
{\mathbb{G}}_{K}^{m}=(K^{*})^{m}

the underlying set
\trop(X)
of a finite polyhedral complex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of
dimension
\dim(X)
, which is the closure of the image of
X(K)
under the coordinatewise valuation
map.

Assume that
X=C
is of dimension
1
and let
\Gamma=\trop(X)
. Then
\Gamma
is a (non-compact) tropical curve in the sense of this article. The map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Div(C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Div(\Gamma),\quad\displaystyle\sum_{i}a_{i}p_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\sum_{i}a_{i}\trop(p_{i}),}

is clearly degree-preserving.

One can moreover define an
extended tropicalization map
of subvarieties of toric varieties; see
[
25
, § 3, 4]
. Indeed, let
X\subseteq Y_{\Delta}
be a subvariety of a toric variety
Y_{\Delta}
, defined over
K
, corresponding to a fan
\Delta
in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. In
loc. cit.
S. Payne constructs an extended tropicalization map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\trop}\colon X(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{\Delta},}

where
N_{\Delta}=\bigsqcup_{\sigma\in\Delta}N_{{\mathbb{R}}}/\langle\sigma\rangle

is the Kajiwara–Payne compactified tropical toric variety, a partial compactification of
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}

obtained by adding strata corresponding to cones of the fan. The
extended tropicalization of

X
is defined as the closure of the image
\overline{\trop}(X)
inside
N_{\Delta}
.

Assume
X=C\subset Y_{\Delta}
is of dimension one and let
\Gamma
be the extended tropicalization of
C
. The boundary
\partial\Gamma=\Gamma\smallsetminus\Gamma^{\circ}
consists of a finite set of points, called the
infinite vertices
. If all infinite vertices have valence one, or equivalently, if no two unbounded rays meet at infinity, then
\Gamma
is naturally endowed with a metric multigraph structure and hence can be identified with a compact tropical curve; see
[
18
, § 2]
.

In this case, the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Div(C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Div(\Gamma),\quad\displaystyle\sum_{i}a_{i}p_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\sum_{i}a_{i}\overline{\trop}(p_{i}),}

is again clearly degree preserving. Note that if
D
is a divisor on
C
which does not meet the toric boundary, then
\overline{\trop}(D)
is supported on
\Gamma^{\circ}
. Arguing as in the proof of
subsection 5.1
we conclude that

(7)

\vol_{C}(D)=\max\!\left\{\deg\!\big(\overline{\trop}(D)\big),0\right\}=\vol_{\Gamma}^{\mathbb{T}}\!\left(\overline{\trop}(D)\right)

for any
D\in\Div(C)
.

Remark 5.3
.

If
K
is discretely valued, then the (extended) tropicalization map factors through the specialization map, as defined in the previous Subsection. More generally, if
K
is not necessarily discretely valued, then the analog of the specialization map is the Berkovich retraction map
\tau\colon C^{\operatorname{an}}\to\Gamma
from the Berkovich analitification
C^{\operatorname{an}}
of
C
to an (extended) skeleton
\Gamma
, associated to any strongly semistable model
\mathfrak{C}
of
C
over the valuation ring of
K
. The (extended) tropicalization map extends continuously to
\overline{\trop}\colon C^{\operatorname{an}}\to N_{\Delta}

and there exists an integral, piecewise affine map
\gamma\colon\Gamma\to N_{\Delta}
such that
\overline{\trop}=\gamma\circ\tau
; see
[
4
, Prop. 5.4 (1)]
for the case that
C
is contained in a torus, and
[
18
, § 2.4]
for the extended version.
