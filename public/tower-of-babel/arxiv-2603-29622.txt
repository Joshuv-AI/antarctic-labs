A Finite-State Proof of the Well-Definedness of a Perturbed Hofstadter Sequence Finite constraint reduction and complete critical-core verification
Marco Mantovanelli

Abstract
We prove that the perturbed Hofstadter-type sequence defined by Q ⁡ ( 1 ) = 1 , Q ⁡ ( 2 ) = 1 , Q ⁡ ( n ) = Q ⁡ ( n − Q ⁡ ( n − 1 ) ) + Q ⁡ ( n − Q ⁡ ( n − 2 ) ) + ( − 1 ) n Q(1)=1,\quad Q(2)=1,\quad Q(n)=Q(n-Q(n-1))+Q(n-Q(n-2))+(-1)^{n} is well-defined for all n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , i.e. all recursive arguments remain positive —a property that remains open for the classical Hofstadter Q Q -sequence. The proof proceeds by reducing the infinite recursion to a finite combinatorial constraint system. We introduce a symbolic encoding of local configurations and enumerate a finite set of admissible contexts together with a compatibility relation capturing all valid local transitions. Global realizations of the recursion correspond to solutions of this finite system. A key structural ingredient is a decomposition of all valid assignments into two global modes, which allows the reduction of potential obstructions to a finite critical core. A complete finite verification then excludes all obstructions and establishes global well-definedness. More broadly, the argument shows that certain meta-Fibonacci recursions, despite their nonlocal definition, admit a finite-state description whose consistency can be decided by exhaustive combinatorial analysis.

1
Introduction

Meta-Fibonacci recursions form a class of sequences in which the arguments of the recursion depend on previously computed values. Among the most prominent examples is Hofstadter’s
Q
-sequence, introduced in
[
11
]
, defined by

Q(n)=Q(n-Q(n-1))+Q(n-Q(n-2)).

Despite its simple definition, the sequence exhibits highly nontrivial behavior,
and even basic structural properties remain difficult to analyze.
In particular, the question whether the sequence is well-defined for all
n

has remained notoriously difficult; see, for example,

[
16
,
17
,
3
,
1
,
18
]

for related discussions and variants.

Recent work has highlighted the diversity of behaviors in Hofstadter-type recursions. In particular, Fox
[
8
,
7
,
9
]

shows that such systems can exhibit structured, periodic, and chaotic regimes depending on the choice of initial conditions.

At the same time, it is known that, in general, questions about nested recurrence
relations can be undecidable
[
6
]
, which underscores
the inherent difficulty of establishing global properties such as well-definedness.

In this paper, we consider the perturbed Hofstadter-type recursion

Q(1)=1,\quad Q(2)=1,\quad Q(n)=Q(n-Q(n-1))+Q(n-Q(n-2))+(-1)^{n}.

The additional alternating term introduces a controlled perturbation of the classical recursion while preserving its essential nonlocal character.
At first sight, the result may appear unexpectedly strong, given the notorious difficulty of the classical
Q
-sequence. However, the alternating perturbation introduces a structural asymmetry that fundamentally alters the propagation behavior of the recursion. In particular, it enables a finite-state reduction that does not appear to be available in the unperturbed case.

This perturbed model was introduced in
[
14
]
, which provides strong empirical and structural evidence for regularity and self-similarity. In particular, the numerical and symbolic analysis suggests that the sequence remains globally well-behaved despite the presence of alternating forcing. The sequence has been recorded in the OEIS as A394051
[
12
]
.

A central problem for Hofstadter-type recursions is
well-definedness
, that is, whether the recursion produces a value for every
n\geq 1
, equivalently, whether all recursive arguments remain positive throughout the evolution. As noted above, even for the classical
Q
-sequence this question is notoriously difficult.

The main result of the present paper is a resolution of this question for the perturbed sequence.

Theorem 1
.

The perturbed Hofstadter-type sequence

Q(n)=Q(n-Q(n-1))+Q(n-Q(n-2))+(-1)^{n}

with initial values
Q(1)=Q(2)=1
is well-defined for all integers
n\geq 1
.

1.1
Method of proof

The proof proceeds by replacing the infinite recursive structure with a finite combinatorial model. In Section 2 we explain the reduction of the recursion to a finite symbolic system. Section 3 introduces the local contexts that encode the relevant neighborhood data, and Section 4 constructs the compatibility graph governing local propagation. In Section 5 we formulate the notion of global assignments and recast the problem as a finite constraint satisfaction problem.

The key structural ingredients appear in the next two sections. Section 6 proves the two-mode decomposition of valid assignments, beginning with a rigidity result for debt-
2
contexts. Section 7 introduces the associated support sets, which describe all locally admissible values in each mode.

The obstruction analysis occupies the final part of the paper. In Section 8 we reduce the obstruction problem to a finite critical core. Section 9 analyzes the induced structure on this core, and Section 10 carries out the finite verification showing that every subset of the critical core admits a valid assignment. Section 11 then combines all previous reductions to complete the proof of the main theorem.

The appendices contain the machine-checkable certificate for the critical-core verification, the full automaton data, and the compressed transition relation used in the computation.
A complete computational implementation of the finite verification is provided
in a publicly available repository
[
15
]
.

1.2
Position within the literature

The present argument combines ideas from the study of meta-Fibonacci recursions with methods that are more commonly associated with finite symbolic dynamics and finite constraint systems. In this sense, the proof shows that a nontrivial self-referential recursion can nevertheless admit a finite-state description at the level of local behavior; compare
[
13
,
10
]
for general background on symbolic systems and graph-homomorphism viewpoints.
Recent work on Hofstadter-type and related meta-Fibonacci structures also points toward a broad variety of emergent combinatorial behaviors; see, for example,
[
2
,
19
,
5
]
.

From a broader perspective, the result supports the idea that certain recursively defined sequences, although globally difficult to analyze directly, may be controlled through a finite local model together with an explicit obstruction analysis.

Scope and limitations.

The focus of the present work is exclusively on the question of well-definedness, namely, the existence of a globally consistent realization of the recursion. While the finite-state reduction developed here provides a complete solution to this problem, it is not intended to capture the full combinatorial complexity of the sequence. In particular, we do not address the precise asymptotic rate of convergence of
Q(n)/n
to
1/2
, nor do we claim that the finite symbolic model fully reflects the deeper combinatorial structures that may govern this convergence. It is conceivable that finer analyses involve richer objects (such as Catalan-type structures or lattice-path enumerations), which lie beyond the scope of the present approach.

2
Reduction to a Finite Symbolic System

The central difficulty in analyzing the recursion

Q(n)=Q(n-Q(n-1))+Q(n-Q(n-2))+(-1)^{n}

lies in its nonlocal nature: the arguments of the recursion depend on previously computed values and may range over a large portion of the past. As a result, direct inductive arguments are difficult to control.

The key idea of this paper is that, despite this nonlocal dependence, the recursion exhibits a strong form of
local regularity
. More precisely, while the numerical values of
Q(n)
are unbounded, the
relative structure
of the recursion in a bounded neighborhood of
n
can be encoded using finitely many symbolic configurations.

2.1
From recursion to symbolic data

Instead of working directly with the values of
Q(n)
, we introduce a finite set of symbolic states

S=\{S_{0},S_{1},\dots,S_{7}\},

which encode the local behavior of the recursion. Each symbolic state represents an equivalence class of local configurations that behave identically with respect to the recursive rule.
We write elements of
S\times\{0,2\}
in the shorthand form
S_{i}[d]
instead of
(S_{i},d)
.

In addition, we introduce a
debt parameter

d\in\{0,2\},

which accounts for the bounded discrepancy introduced by the alternating perturbation term
(-1)^{n}
. This parameter ensures closure of the symbolic system under propagation.

2.2
Local configurations and contexts

To capture the local structure of the recursion, we consider finite windows around a position
n
and encode them by
contexts
. Each context represents a class of local configurations that are indistinguishable with respect to future propagation.

The set of all admissible contexts will be denoted by
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
. It is finite and can be computed explicitly.

2.3
Compatibility constraints

The recursion induces local compatibility conditions between neighboring contexts. These constraints define a directed relation

\Psi\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\times\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}},

which specifies which contexts may follow each other in a valid configuration.
We identify
\Psi
with the edge set of a directed graph on
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
.

Thus, the infinite recursion is reduced to a finite combinatorial system consisting of:

•

a finite set of contexts
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
,

•

a finite set of symbolic states,

•

a finite compatibility relation
\Psi
.

2.4
Global consistency problem

A realization of the recursion corresponds to a global assignment of symbolic states to contexts that respects all compatibility constraints.

The problem of well-definedness is therefore equivalent to the following finite statement:

Every locally admissible configuration extends to a globally consistent assignment.

In the subsequent sections, we make this reduction precise and analyze the resulting finite system in detail.

3
Local Contexts

We now introduce the precise formal objects that encode local configurations of the recursion.

3.1
Definition of contexts

Definition 1
.

A
context
is a tuple

\lambda=(p,R,i,\pi,d),

where:

•

p
is a finite pattern describing symbolic states in a fixed local window,

•

R\in\{R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}\}
specifies the recursion regime,

•

i
is a distinguished position within the pattern,

•

\pi\in\{0,1\}
is the parity of the index,

•

d\in\{0,2\}
is the debt parameter.

We will also use an expanded notation in which the pattern
p
is written explicitly and the components are labeled:

(p,R,i,\pi,d)=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},R,\text{pos}=i,\text{parity}=\pi,\text{debt}=d).

Each context represents a local configuration centered at a position
n
, together with sufficient information to determine how the recursion propagates to neighboring positions.

3.2
Admissibility

Not every tuple
(p,R,i,\pi,d)
corresponds to a valid local configuration.

Definition 2
.

A context
\lambda
is
admissible
if there exists a realization of the recursion in which the symbolic data encoded by
\lambda
is consistent with the recursive rule.

Admissibility imposes local consistency conditions, including:

•

compatibility of symbolic states within the pattern,

•

consistency of recursive dependencies determined by
R
,

•

correct propagation of parity,

•

consistency of the debt parameter under recursion.

3.3
Finite enumeration

Proposition 1
.

The set of admissible contexts
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
is finite and has cardinality
28
.

We fix an enumeration

\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}=\{\lambda_{0},\dots,\lambda_{27}\},

and refer to contexts interchangeably by their index (“ctx
i
”) or by
\lambda_{i}
.

Proof.

Each component of the tuple
(p,R,i,\pi,d)
ranges over a finite set. The admissibility conditions impose only local constraints and therefore define a finite subset of the full parameter space. An explicit enumeration yields
|\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}|=28
.
∎

3.4
Role of contexts

Contexts serve as the vertices of the compatibility graph introduced in the next section. A transition between contexts corresponds to shifting the observation window by one position in the sequence.

Thus, sequences of contexts correspond to locally consistent configurations of the recursion.

3.5
Example: A concrete context and its interpretation

We illustrate the notion of a context with an explicit example taken from the critical core.

Consider the context

\lambda_{0}=(C,E,B,R_{1},\text{pos}=1,\text{parity}=1,\text{debt}=0).

Interpretation.

This context encodes a local configuration around some index
n
with the following properties:

•

The pattern
(C,E,B)
represents a symbolic description of the values of
Q
in a fixed window around
n
.

•

The rule type
R_{1}
specifies how the recursive calls

Q(n-Q(n-1)),\quad Q(n-Q(n-2))

interact in this configuration.

•

The position
\text{pos}=1
indicates that the current index
n
corresponds to the first entry of the pattern.

•

The parity
\text{parity}=1
means that
n
is odd, so the perturbation contributes
(-1)^{n}=-1
.

•

The debt parameter
d=0
indicates that no correction is currently carried over from previous steps.

Admissibility.

The fact that
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
means that there exists a realization of the recursion in which a local neighborhood of some index
n
exhibits exactly this symbolic structure, and all recursive dependencies are satisfied.

Propagation.

The compatibility graph
\Psi
determines which contexts may follow
\lambda_{0}
. In the induced structure on the critical core, we have the edge

\lambda_{0}\to\lambda_{2},

which means that after shifting the observation window by one position, the resulting local configuration must correspond to the context

\lambda_{2}=(E,B,E,R_{1},\text{pos}=2,\text{parity}=0,\text{debt}=0).

Symbolic states.

The support sets at
\lambda_{0}
are

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{0})=\{S1[0]\},\qquad\Sigma_{B}(\lambda_{0})=\{S2[0]\}.

Thus, depending on the global mode, the local configuration represented by
\lambda_{0}
can only realize one of two symbolic states.

Role in the global structure.

This example illustrates how a single context encodes:

•

local structural information about the recursion,

•

constraints on neighboring configurations via
\Psi
,

•

and mode-dependent admissible symbolic states via the support sets.

In particular, the interaction between
\lambda_{0}
and its successors is one of the key mechanisms through which global constraints propagate across the system.

4
The Compatibility Graph

The finite symbolic system introduced in the previous section is governed by a local compatibility relation between contexts. This relation encodes the propagation of the recursion across neighboring positions and forms the central combinatorial object of the proof.

4.1
Definition of the compatibility relation

Let
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
denote the set of admissible contexts. We define a directed relation

\Psi\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\times\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}

as follows.

Definition 3
.

A pair
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Psi
if and only if the context
\mu
can occur immediately to the right of
\lambda
in a locally consistent realization of the recursion.

More concretely, the relation
\Psi
is derived from the recursive rule

Q(n)=Q(n-Q(n-1))+Q(n-Q(n-2))+(-1)^{n},

by enforcing that the symbolic data encoded in
\lambda
and
\mu
are compatible with a common underlying configuration of the sequence.

This includes:

•

consistency of overlapping local patterns,

•

compatibility of recursive dependencies,

•

agreement of parity and debt parameters under propagation.

We call
\mu
a neighbor of
\lambda
if either
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Psi
or
(\mu,\lambda)\in\Psi
.

4.2
Local determinism and finite propagation

A crucial feature of the system is that compatibility is
local
: the admissibility of a transition
(\lambda,\mu)
depends only on the data contained in
\lambda
and
\mu
, and not on the global configuration.

This property places the system in the general framework of
finite constraint systems
or
shifts of finite type
in symbolic dynamics (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
). In this interpretation, contexts play the role of symbols, and the relation
\Psi
defines the allowed transitions between symbols.

Proposition 2
.

The compatibility relation
\Psi
is finite and effectively computable. In the present case, it consists of
34

4.3
Graph structure

We view
(\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}},\Psi)
as a directed graph, called the
compatibility graph
. Paths in this graph correspond to sequences of contexts that can appear consecutively in a valid configuration.

Definition 4
.

A path
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
is
admissible
if

(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i+1})\in\Psi\quad\text{for all }i.

Admissible paths represent locally consistent configurations of the recursion over finite intervals.

Proposition 3
.

The compatibility graph decomposes into two connected components.

Idea of proof.

This follows from an explicit computation of
\Psi
. The decomposition reflects a global dichotomy in the behavior of the recursion, which will later be identified with the two modes of global assignments.
∎

4.4
Global configurations as graph colorings

A global assignment

f:\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\to S\times\{0,2\}

can be interpreted as a labeling of the vertices of the compatibility graph.

The assignment is valid if and only if it respects all edges:

(\lambda,\mu)\in\Psi\;\Rightarrow\;(f(\lambda),f(\mu))\text{ is an admissible transition.}

Thus, valid assignments correspond to
graph homomorphisms
from the compatibility graph into a finite transition system on symbolic states.

This perspective connects the problem to classical constraint satisfaction problems (CSPs) and graph homomorphism problems (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
), where one seeks assignments satisfying local compatibility constraints.

4.5
Relation to meta-Fibonacci dynamics

The appearance of a finite compatibility graph is a nontrivial structural feature of the recursion. While meta-Fibonacci sequences are generally considered highly irregular (see
[
17
,
16
]
), the present construction shows that the perturbed sequence admits a finite-state description at the level of local behavior.

This phenomenon is closely related to the emergence of regularity patterns observed empirically in Hofstadter-type sequences and their variants
[
3
,
20
,
7
]
.

4.6
Role in the proof

The compatibility graph provides the bridge between the infinite recursion and the finite obstruction problem.

•

Local consistency is encoded by edges of
\Psi
.

•

Global consistency corresponds to the existence of valid assignments.

•

Failure of extendability corresponds to the existence of obstructions.

In the subsequent sections, we analyze the structure of this graph in detail. In particular, we show that all valid assignments fall into two global modes and that any obstruction must be supported on a small critical subset of contexts.

5
Global Assignments

Having introduced the finite set of contexts and the compatibility relation
\Psi
, we now formalize the notion of a global configuration of the system.

5.1
Assignments

Definition 5
.

A
global assignment
is a function

f:\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\to S\times\{0,2\},

which assigns to each admissible context a symbolic state together with a debt value.

Intuitively, an assignment specifies, for every possible local configuration, which symbolic state is realized in that configuration.

5.2
Compatibility constraints

Not every assignment corresponds to a valid configuration of the recursion. The assignments must respect the compatibility relation
\Psi
.

Definition 6
.

A global assignment
f
is
valid
if for every edge
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Psi
, the pair

(f(\lambda),f(\mu))

is an admissible transition of symbolic states.

This condition ensures that locally adjacent contexts are assigned compatible symbolic data.

5.3
Interpretation as a constraint system

The pair
(\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}},\Psi)
together with the symbolic states defines a finite constraint satisfaction problem (CSP). A valid assignment is precisely a solution to this CSP.

Equivalently, one may view the problem as a graph homomorphism problem: the compatibility graph
(\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}},\Psi)
must be mapped into a finite transition graph on symbolic states in a way that preserves edges (see
[
10
]
).

5.4
Relation to the recursion

The key link to the original recursion is the following principle:

Every realization of the recursion induces a valid assignment, and the finite symbolic system is constructed so that extendability of valid assignments is sufficient for global consistency of the recursion.

Indeed, given a realization of the sequence
Q(n)
, each position determines a context
\lambda
together with a symbolic state. The compatibility of neighboring positions implies that the induced assignment satisfies all
\Psi
constraints.

Thus, the existence of suitable valid assignments provides the finite consistency criterion used in the proof of well-definedness.

Lemma 1

(Context-determined symbolic state)
.

For every admissible context
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
, the symbolic
state assigned by the finite-state encoding is uniquely determined by

\lambda
. Equivalently, if the same admissible context occurs at two positions
in a realization of the recursion, then it induces the same element of

S\times\{0,2\}
at both positions.

Proof.

By construction, a context

\lambda=(p,R,i,\pi,d)\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}

contains exactly the local symbolic data used by the encoder: the local pattern

p
, the recursion regime
R
, the distinguished position
i
, the parity

\pi
, and the debt parameter
d
. The finite-state encoding was defined so
that the assigned symbolic state depends only on these local parameters and on
no additional global information.

Hence, whenever two occurrences of the recursion give rise to the same
admissible context
\lambda
, all inputs to the encoder coincide. Therefore the
output of the encoder must coincide as well. In other words, the symbolic state
associated with an admissible context is a well-defined function of the context
alone.

Thus the finite-state encoding determines a map

\mathrm{enc}:\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\to S\times\{0,2\},

and the symbolic state induced by any occurrence of
\lambda
is uniquely
determined by
\lambda
.
∎

The symbolic model is constructed so that local realizability in the recursion
coincides with local satisfiability in the associated finite constraint system.
In particular, a locally admissible configuration is extendable in the recursion
precisely when the corresponding partial assignment is extendable to a valid
global assignment in the finite model. Thus, failure of recursive
well-definedness gives rise to a non-extendable locally admissible
configuration, i.e. to an obstruction in the finite constraint system.

Theorem 2

(Correctness of the finite-state reduction)
.

The perturbed Hofstadter-type recursion

Q(1)=1,\qquad Q(2)=1,\qquad Q(n)=Q(n-Q(n-1))+Q(n-Q(n-2))+(-1)^{n}

is well-defined for all
n\geq 1
if and only if the associated finite
constraint system has no obstruction.

Here the associated finite constraint system is the symbolic model constructed
in Sections 2–4, with admissible context set
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
,
compatibility relation
\Psi
, and valid assignments

f:\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\to S\times\{0,2\}
.

Proof.

We prove both directions.

Soundness.

Assume that the recursion is well-defined for all
n\geq 1
.

Then every index
n
admits a genuine realization of the recursion, and hence determines a local configuration consistent with the recursive rule. By construction of the symbolic system, each such local configuration corresponds to an admissible context

\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}},

together with a symbolic state in
S\times\{0,2\}
describing the local behavior of the recursion.

By Lemma
1
, the symbolic state associated with an
admissible context depends only on the context itself and not on the particular
occurrence of that context in the realization.

This therefore defines a map

f:\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\to S\times\{0,2\},

obtained by assigning to each context the symbolic state realized in the
sequence.

Since consecutive positions in the sequence arise from a single globally consistent realization, overlapping local windows are compatible. Therefore, for every edge

(\lambda,\mu)\in\Psi,

the pair
(f(\lambda),f(\mu))
is an admissible symbolic transition. Hence
f
is a valid global assignment.

In particular, every locally admissible configuration occurring in the sequence extends to a valid global assignment.

Completeness.

Assume that the associated finite constraint system has no obstruction.
Equivalently, every partial assignment arising from a locally admissible
configuration extends to a valid global assignment.

Suppose, for contradiction, that the recursion is not well-defined. Then there exists a smallest index
n\geq 3
at which the recursion fails. By minimality, all values
Q(1),\dots,Q(n-1)
are defined.

The failure at
n
must therefore arise from a violation of the recursive rule, namely that at least one of the arguments

n-Q(n-1),\qquad n-Q(n-2)

is nonpositive or otherwise incompatible with the local consistency conditions imposed by the recursion.

By construction of the symbolic system, the local configuration around
n
determines an admissible context

\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}},

and the failure of the recursion corresponds to the fact that this configuration cannot be extended to a globally consistent realization. In the symbolic model, this means precisely that the corresponding local assignment does not extend to a valid global assignment, i.e. it forms an obstruction.

This contradicts the assumption that every locally admissible configuration extends to a valid global assignment. Therefore no such index
n
exists, and the recursion is well-defined for all
n\geq 1
.
∎

5.5
Extension problem

The central problem can now be reformulated purely in terms of assignments.

Definition 7
.

Let
H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
. A partial assignment

f_{H}:H\to S\times\{0,2\}

is
extendable
if it can be extended to a valid global assignment on all of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
.

Definition 8
.

An
obstruction
is a subset
H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
such that no assignment on
H
extends to a valid global assignment.

Thus, the problem of well-definedness reduces to showing that no obstruction exists.

5.6
Role in the proof

By Theorem
2
, it suffices to exclude obstructions
in the associated finite constraint system. In the subsequent sections, we
analyze the structure of valid assignments and prove that any potential
obstruction reduces to a finite critical core.

6
The Two-Mode Structure

A central structural property of the compatibility system is that all valid assignments fall into exactly two global classes. This dichotomy is the key mechanism that reduces the global extension problem to a finite obstruction problem.

We begin with a preliminary rigidity observation. In the debt-
2
sector, the symbolic system becomes completely rigid.

Lemma 2

(Debt rigidity)
.

If
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
has debt
d=2
, then

f(\lambda)=S6[2]

for every valid assignment
f
.

Proof.

This is an exact output of the propagation computation and appears in Appendix B.
∎

6.1
Statement of the result

Theorem 3

(Two-mode structure)
.

Let
f:\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\to S\times\{0,2\}
be a valid assignment. Then
f
belongs to exactly one of two classes:

•

Mode A
:
f(\lambda_{0})=S1[0]
,

•

Mode B
:
f(\lambda_{0})=S2[0]
.

Moreover, for each mode and every context
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
, the value
f(\lambda)
must lie in the corresponding support set
\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)
or
\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)
.

6.2
Root determination

We begin by analyzing the distinguished context
\lambda_{0}
.

Lemma 3

(Root restriction)
.

In any valid assignment
f
, the value
f(\lambda_{0})
is either
S1[0]
or
S2[0]
.

Proof.

From the computed support table, we have

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{0})=\{S1[0]\},\qquad\Sigma_{B}(\lambda_{0})=\{S2[0]\},

and no other symbolic states are compatible with
\lambda_{0}
under the constraints of
\Psi
. Hence the claim follows.
∎

6.3
Propagation along the compatibility graph

The key step is to show that fixing the value at
\lambda_{0}
propagates constraints throughout the entire connected component.

Lemma 4

(Root choice determines the global mode)
.

Let
f:\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\to S\times\{0,2\}
be a valid assignment. Then
the value of
f(\lambda_{0})
determines which of the two global modes
f

belongs to:

•

if
f(\lambda_{0})=S1[0]
, then
f
belongs to Mode A;

•

if
f(\lambda_{0})=S2[0]
, then
f
belongs to Mode B.

In particular, the root choice propagates through the finite compatibility
system and determines which family of support sets is relevant for
f
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3
, every valid assignment satisfies

f(\lambda_{0})\in\{S1[0],S2[0]\}.

The two possibilities are mutually exclusive by

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{0})=\{S1[0]\},\qquad\Sigma_{B}(\lambda_{0})=\{S2[0]\}.

Hence every valid assignment belongs to exactly one of the two classes singled
out by the root value. The corresponding support sets are then the ones
associated with that class, namely
\Sigma_{A}
in the first case and
\Sigma_{B}

in the second.
∎

The remaining connected component consists of debt-
2
contexts and is rigid by
Lemma
2
. Consequently, it does not produce an additional global mode beyond
the dichotomy determined at
\lambda_{0}
.

6.4
Mode decomposition

We now formalize the two global modes.

Definition 9
.

For each context
\lambda
, define the support sets:

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)=\{f(\lambda):f\text{ valid and }f(\lambda_{0})=S1[0]\},

\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)=\{f(\lambda):f\text{ valid and }f(\lambda_{0})=S2[0]\}.

These sets are computed explicitly by propagation of constraints.

Proposition 4
.

For every context
\lambda
, the sets
\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)
and
\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)
are nonempty and describe all possible values of
f(\lambda)
under the respective root conditions.

Proof.

Fix a mode (say Mode A). Starting from the root constraint
f(\lambda_{0})=S1[0]
, we propagate constraints along all edges of
\Psi
until a fixed point is reached. This yields the set of all values compatible with the root constraint.

Completeness follows because any valid assignment must satisfy the same local constraints and therefore cannot introduce additional values.
∎

6.5
Disjointness of modes

Lemma 5
.

The two modes are disjoint, i.e.,

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{0})\cap\Sigma_{B}(\lambda_{0})=\emptyset.

Proof.

Immediate from

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{0})=\{S1[0]\},\qquad\Sigma_{B}(\lambda_{0})=\{S2[0]\}.

∎

6.6
Global classification

We now complete the proof of Theorem
3
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

By Lemma
3
, every valid assignment must choose either
S1[0]
or
S2[0]
at
\lambda_{0}
.

By Lemma
4
, this choice determines which support family,
\Sigma_{A}
or
\Sigma_{B}
, governs the assignment.

By Proposition
4
, these support sets describe all possible assignments under each choice.

Finally, Lemma
5
shows that the two classes are disjoint.

Thus every valid assignment belongs to exactly one of the two modes.
∎

6.7
Interpretation

The two-mode structure reveals a hidden global rigidity of the recursion. Although the system is locally defined, the choice at a single context determines the entire global configuration up to finite ambiguity.

This phenomenon is analogous to phase separation in constraint systems and symbolic dynamics, where global configurations split into a small number of extremal classes (cf.
[
13
]
).

6.8
Role in the proof

The two-mode decomposition reduces the extension problem to the following:

Given a partial configuration, it suffices to show that at least one of the two modes extends it.

In particular, a simultaneous obstruction must block both modes. In the next section, we show that such an obstruction cannot exist.

7
Support Sets

The two-mode structure established in the previous section allows us to describe all valid assignments in terms of local admissible values at each context. These values are captured by the
support sets
.

7.1
Definition

Definition 10
.

For each context
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
, define:

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)=\{\,f(\lambda)\;:\;f\text{ is a valid assignment with }f(\lambda_{0})=S1[0]\,\},

\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)=\{\,f(\lambda)\;:\;f\text{ is a valid assignment with }f(\lambda_{0})=S2[0]\,\}.

Thus,
\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)
and
\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)
describe all possible symbolic states that can occur at
\lambda
under the two global modes.

7.2
Computation via constraint propagation

The support sets are obtained by propagating the root constraint along the compatibility graph.

Fix a mode (say Mode A). Starting from

f(\lambda_{0})=S1[0],

we iteratively enforce all compatibility constraints
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Psi
, removing values that are incompatible with already determined possibilities.

Since both
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
and the state space are finite, this process stabilizes after finitely many steps and yields the complete set
\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda
.

An analogous procedure yields
\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)
.

Proposition 5
.

The support sets
\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)
and
\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)
are finite, nonempty, and effectively computable for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
.

7.3
Completeness

The support sets describe all possible local values under each mode.

Proposition 6
.

Let
f
be a valid assignment.

•

If
f(\lambda_{0})=S1[0]
, then
f(\lambda)\in\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda
.

•

If
f(\lambda_{0})=S2[0]
, then
f(\lambda)\in\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the propagation procedure: any valid assignment must satisfy the same local constraints used to construct the support sets, and therefore cannot contain values outside them.
∎

7.4
Local flexibility vs. rigidity

The support sets quantify the degree of freedom at each context.

•

If
|\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)|=1
, then the value at
\lambda
is uniquely determined in Mode A.

•

If
|\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)|>1
, then multiple values are possible, reflecting local flexibility.

In particular, contexts with debt
d=2
are completely rigid:

Lemma 6

(Debt rigidity)
.

If
\lambda
has
d=2
, then

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)=\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)=\{S6[2]\}.

7.5
Sensitive contexts

The comparison of the two support sets leads to a key structural notion.

Definition 11
.

A context
\lambda
is called
sensitive
if

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)\neq\Sigma_{B}(\lambda).

Sensitive contexts are precisely those where the choice of mode has a genuine effect.

7.6
Examples from the critical core

For the four contexts in the critical core, the support sets are:

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{0})=\{S1[0]\},\quad\Sigma_{B}(\lambda_{0})=\{S2[0]\},

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{2})=\{S1[0],S4[0]\},\quad\Sigma_{B}(\lambda_{2})=\{S2[0]\},

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{14})=\{S1[0],S4[0],S7[0]\},\quad\Sigma_{B}(\lambda_{14})=\{S2[0],S3[0],S4[0],S5[0],S6[0]\},

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{24})=\{S1[0],S4[0],S7[0]\},\quad\Sigma_{B}(\lambda_{24})=\{S2[0],S3[0],S4[0],S5[0],S6[0]\}.

These differences are the source of all potential obstructions.

7.7
Role in the obstruction analysis

The support sets allow us to reformulate the extension problem purely locally:

An obstruction occurs if and only if, for some subset of contexts, no choice of values from the corresponding support sets satisfies all compatibility constraints.

In particular:

•

contexts with identical support in both modes are irrelevant for obstructions,

•

only sensitive contexts can contribute to contradictions,

•

the structure of
\Sigma_{A}
and
\Sigma_{B}
determines the critical core.

This observation forms the basis of the reduction carried out in the next section.

8
Reduction to the Critical Core

In this section we show that the obstruction problem reduces to a finite and explicitly identifiable subset of contexts. The argument proceeds by isolating the contexts where the two global modes differ and then proving that only a small subset of these contexts can support a minimal obstruction.

8.1
Obstructions

Recall that a subset
H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
is an
obstruction
if no assignment on
H
extends to a valid global assignment.

An obstruction
H
is
minimal
if:

•

H
is an obstruction,

•

every proper subset
H^{\prime}\subsetneq H
is extendable.

8.2
Sensitive contexts

Definition 12
.

The
sensitive set
is

\Lambda_{\mathrm{sens}}=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}:\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)\neq\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)\}.

These are precisely the contexts where the two global modes impose different local constraints.

8.3
Local extendability at insensitive contexts

We first isolate the key structural property used to eliminate insensitive contexts.

Lemma 7

(Computed local extendability at insensitive contexts)
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus\Lambda_{\mathrm{sens}}
be an insensitive context.
Let
N(\lambda)\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
denote the set of neighbors of
\lambda
in the compatibility graph, i.e.

N(\lambda)=\{\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}:(\lambda,\mu)\in\Psi\text{ or }(\mu,\lambda)\in\Psi\}.

Then every valid partial assignment on
N(\lambda)
extends across
\lambda
: more precisely, for every valid partial assignment

g:N(\lambda)\to S\times\{0,2\},

there exists a value

s\in\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)=\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)

such that the extended assignment on

N(\lambda)\cup\{\lambda\}

defined by
g(\lambda)=s
satisfies all compatibility constraints involving
\lambda
.

Proof.

This claim is established by exhaustive enumeration over the finite state space determined by the support sets and the compatibility relation
\Psi
. Since both the neighborhood configurations and the state sets are finite, the verification reduces to a finite check, whose complete output is available in the accompanying repository.
For each insensitive context
\lambda
, the checker enumerates all locally admissible assignments on the neighborhood
N(\lambda)
and confirms that at least one value

s\in\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)=\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)

extends the assignment across
\lambda
while preserving all incident
\Psi
-constraints.
Hence the stated extension property holds for every insensitive context.
∎

Lemma 8

(Mode-independent local extendability)
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
be such that

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)=\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)=:\Sigma(\lambda).

Let
H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
with
\lambda\notin H
, and let

f_{H}:H\to S\times\{0,2\}

be a valid partial assignment.

Assume that for every neighbor
\mu
of
\lambda
in the compatibility graph (i.e.
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Psi
or
(\mu,\lambda)\in\Psi
with
\mu\in H
), the value
f_{H}(\mu)
lies in the corresponding support set.

Then there exists
s\in\Sigma(\lambda)
such that extending
f_{H}
by
f(\lambda)=s
yields a valid assignment on
H\cup\{\lambda\}
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda\notin\Lambda_{\mathrm{sens}}
, we have

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)=\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)=:\Sigma(\lambda).

By restriction, the given valid partial assignment
f_{H}
on
H
induces a valid partial assignment on the neighborhood
N(\lambda)\cap H
.

By Lemma
7
, every valid partial assignment on the neighborhood of an insensitive context extends across that context. Therefore there exists

s\in\Sigma(\lambda)

such that extending
f_{H}
by
f(\lambda)=s
yields a valid assignment on
H\cup\{\lambda\}
.
∎

8.4
Elimination of insensitive contexts

Lemma 9

(Elimination of insensitive contexts)
.

Let
H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
be a minimal obstruction. Then

H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{sens}}.

Proof.

Suppose
\lambda\in H
is not sensitive, i.e.

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)=\Sigma_{B}(\lambda).

Let
H^{\prime}=H\setminus\{\lambda\}
. By minimality,
H^{\prime}
is extendable. Let

f_{H^{\prime}}:H^{\prime}\to S\times\{0,2\}

be a valid assignment.

We verify that the hypotheses of Lemma
8
are satisfied. Indeed, for every
\mu\in H^{\prime}
adjacent to
\lambda
, we have
f_{H^{\prime}}(\mu)\in\Sigma_{A}(\mu)
or
\Sigma_{B}(\mu)
depending on the mode, hence
f_{H^{\prime}}(\mu)
is locally admissible.

Since
\lambda
is insensitive, Lemma
8
applies and yields a value

s\in\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)=\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)

such that extending
f_{H^{\prime}}
by
f(\lambda)=s
yields a valid assignment on
H
.

This contradicts the assumption that
H
is an obstruction. Therefore, no such
\lambda
exists, and we conclude

H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{sens}}.

∎

8.5
Reduction to the critical core

We now isolate the remaining propagation property needed to reduce minimal obstructions from the full sensitive set to the four-context core.

Lemma 10

(Computed propagation to the critical core)
.

For every context

\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{sens}}\setminus\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}},

there exists a directed path

\lambda\to\lambda_{1}\to\cdots\to\lambda_{k}

in the compatibility graph
\Psi
such that:

•

\lambda_{k}\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
,

•

along this path, the admissible values at
\lambda
are determined by the admissible values at
\lambda_{k}
through successive local compatibility constraints,

•

and every valid partial assignment on the terminal critical context propagates backward to at least one admissible value at
\lambda
.

Proof.

This statement is verified computationally from the explicitly computed support sets and the compatibility relation
\Psi
.

More precisely, for each context

\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{sens}}\setminus\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}},

the checker enumerates directed paths in
\Psi
leading from
\lambda
into
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
, and confirms that at least one such path has the following property: every admissible value at the terminal critical context induces, by successive local propagation along the path, a nonempty set of admissible predecessor values at each earlier context, ending in a nonempty set of admissible values at
\lambda
.

Thus every such
\lambda
is controlled by a propagation chain terminating in the critical core.
∎

Proposition 7
.

Every minimal obstruction
H
is contained in

\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}=\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{14},\lambda_{24}\}.

Proof.

By Lemma
9
, any minimal obstruction satisfies

H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{sens}}.

Assume, for contradiction, that there exists

\lambda\in H\setminus\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}.

Let

H^{\prime}=H\setminus\{\lambda\}.

Since
H
is minimal, the set
H^{\prime}
is extendable. Let

f_{H^{\prime}}:H^{\prime}\to S\times\{0,2\}

be a valid assignment on
H^{\prime}
.

By Lemma
10
, there exists a directed path

\lambda\to\lambda_{1}\to\cdots\to\lambda_{k}

with terminal context
\lambda_{k}\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
, such that admissible values at
\lambda_{k}
propagate backward to at least one admissible value at
\lambda
.

If
\lambda_{k}\in H^{\prime}
, then the assigned value
f_{H^{\prime}}(\lambda_{k})
determines, via backward propagation along the path, a nonempty set of admissible values at
\lambda
. Choose one such value and denote it by
s
.

If
\lambda_{k}\notin H^{\prime}
, then since
H^{\prime}
is extendable, we may first extend
f_{H^{\prime}}
to a valid assignment on a set containing
\lambda_{k}
, and then propagate backward along the path to obtain a value
s
at
\lambda
.

In either case, the value
s
is compatible with all constraints imposed on
\lambda
by the path and, in particular, with all incident
\Psi
-constraints coming from contexts in
H^{\prime}
. Hence extending
f_{H^{\prime}}
by

f(\lambda)=s

yields a valid assignment on
H
.

This contradicts the assumption that
H
is an obstruction. Therefore no such
\lambda
exists, and we conclude

H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}.

∎

8.6
Conclusion

Combining Lemma
9
and Proposition
7
, we obtain:

Corollary 1
.

Every minimal obstruction
H
satisfies

H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}.

Thus, the obstruction problem reduces to checking subsets of the four-element set
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
.

The computational statements in Lemmas

and
10
are certified by exhaustive checks on the
computed support sets and the local compatibility graph
\Psi
; see
Appendices A–C and the accompanying source code.

9
The Critical Core

In this section we analyze the structure of the reduced system on the critical set

\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}=\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{14},\lambda_{24}\},

and explain why this structure already excludes the existence of obstructions.

9.1
Induced compatibility graph

The global compatibility relation
\Psi
induces a directed graph on
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
, obtained by restricting
\Psi
to pairs of contexts in
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
.

From the explicit computation, the induced edges are:

\lambda_{0}\to\lambda_{2},\qquad\lambda_{14}\to\lambda_{0},\qquad\lambda_{24}\to\lambda_{0}.

Proposition 8

(Core compatibility structure)
.

The induced compatibility graph on
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
consists of three directed edges:

\lambda_{14}\to\lambda_{0}\to\lambda_{2},\qquad\lambda_{24}\to\lambda_{0}.

In particular, it is acyclic and all constraints are mediated through the root context
\lambda_{0}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the computed induced
\Psi
-edges on the critical core (see Appendix A).
∎

9.2
Structural consequences

The induced graph has a simple hierarchical structure:

•

The contexts
\lambda_{14}
and
\lambda_{24}
feed into
\lambda_{0}
,

•

the context
\lambda_{0}
feeds into
\lambda_{2}
,

•

there are no cycles within the core.

Thus, any compatibility constraint between elements of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
factors through
\lambda_{0}
. In particular, once a value is fixed at
\lambda_{0}
, the admissible values at all other core contexts are constrained only locally and independently along this directed structure.

9.3
Mode A support on the core

We now examine the support sets in Mode A:

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{0})=\{S1[0]\},\qquad\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{2})=\{S1[0],S4[0]\},

\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{14})=\{S1[0],S4[0],S7[0]\},\qquad\Sigma_{A}(\lambda_{24})=\{S1[0],S4[0],S7[0]\}.

In particular, the symbolic state
S1[0]
belongs to all Mode A support sets:

S1[0]\in\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}.

9.4
Uniform assignment and compatibility

Consider the uniform assignment

f(\lambda)=S1[0]\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}.

We first record the elementary consistency principle needed below.

Lemma 11

(Constant-state CSP consistency)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite directed graph, and let
\mathcal{T}
be a binary
constraint relation on a finite state space
X
.
Assume that there exists a state
s\in X
such that

(s,s)\in\mathcal{T}.

Then the constant assignment

v\mapsto s\qquad(v\in V)

satisfies all edge constraints on
G
.

This is the simplest instance of a consistency principle familiar from the
constraint-satisfaction and graph-homomorphism viewpoint; see, for example,

[
10
,
4
]
.

Proof.

Let
(u,v)\in E
be any edge of
G
. Since both endpoints are assigned the same
state
s
, the corresponding constraint is exactly

(s,s)\in\mathcal{T},

which holds by assumption. Hence every edge constraint is satisfied.
∎

We now verify explicitly that the induced core constraints admit the constant value
S1[0]
.

The induced core graph has the three edges

\lambda_{0}\to\lambda_{2},\qquad\lambda_{14}\to\lambda_{0},\qquad\lambda_{24}\to\lambda_{0}.

For each of these edges, the constant symbolic transition

S1[0]\to S1[0]

is admissible.

More precisely:

•

for
\lambda_{0}\to\lambda_{2}
, the transition
S1[0]\to S1[0]
is admissible;

•

for
\lambda_{14}\to\lambda_{0}
, the transition
S1[0]\to S1[0]
is admissible;

•

for
\lambda_{24}\to\lambda_{0}
, the transition
S1[0]\to S1[0]
is admissible.

Equivalently, every edge of the induced core graph allows the same constant symbolic transition

S1[0]\to S1[0].

Since the induced compatibility graph on
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
is finite and acyclic, Lemma
11
applies. Therefore the uniform assignment

f(\lambda)=S1[0]\qquad(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}})

satisfies all compatibility constraints induced by
\Psi
.

9.5
Why Mode A suffices

By the previous subsection, the induced core graph admits the uniform valid assignment

f(\lambda)=S1[0]\qquad(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}).

Thus Mode A already provides a globally consistent assignment on the full critical core.

Because every induced constraint on a subset of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
is obtained by restriction from the full induced core graph, the same constant assignment remains valid on every subset.

In particular, no subset of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
can fail in Mode A. This is the structural reason why the finite verification in the next section succeeds uniformly across all
15
nonempty subsets of the critical core.

9.6
Conclusion

Proposition 9
.

There exists a valid assignment on
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
in Mode A.

Proof.

The uniform assignment
f(\lambda)=S1[0]
satisfies all compatibility constraints, as shown above.
∎

This identifies a natural candidate assignment in Mode A and reduces the remaining task to a finite verification over the nonempty subsets of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
.

The exhaustive verification of all subsets, together with a machine-checkable certificate, is presented in the next section.

10
Main Finite Verification

We now complete the proof by reducing the global consistency problem to a finite and explicitly verifiable statement.

10.1
Reduction to a finite family

By the reduction established in Section 8, any minimal obstruction

H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}

must satisfy

H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}.

Since the critical core consists of four contexts,

|\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}|=4,

there are exactly

2^{4}-1=15

nonempty subsets of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
.

Thus, the existence of an obstruction is equivalent to the existence of a subset

H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}},\quad H\neq\emptyset,

which admits no valid assignment.

10.2
Finite verification problem

For each subset
H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
, we must determine whether there exists a function

f:H\to S\times\{0,2\}

such that all compatibility constraints induced by
\Psi
on
H
are satisfied.

Equivalently, we must check whether every subset
H
is
extendable
to a valid global assignment.

10.3
Algorithmic verification

This problem is finite and can be solved by exhaustive enumeration.

The verification procedure proceeds as follows:

•

enumerate all
15
nonempty subsets
H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
,

•

for each
H
, enumerate all assignments

f:H\to S\times\{0,2\}

compatible with the support sets,

•

check whether at least one such assignment satisfies all induced compatibility constraints.

This procedure is implemented in the program
checkall()
, which operates entirely on the finite data extracted from the symbolic system (contexts, support sets, and
\Psi
-edges).

10.4
Verification result

The computation yields the following result:

For every nonempty subset
H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
, there exists a valid assignment satisfying all compatibility constraints.

In particular, no subset of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
is an obstruction.

10.5
Machine-checkable certificate

The complete output of the verification procedure is included in Appendix A (see Table

).

This certificate contains:

the support sets
\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)
and

and, for each of the

subsets

The data is presented both in human-readable tabular form and as raw program output, allowing independent verification.

10.6
Conclusion

Proposition 10
.

No subset of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
is an obstruction.

Proof.

The exhaustive computation shows that every subset admits a valid assignment. Hence no obstruction exists on the critical core.
∎

Corollary 2
.

No obstruction exists in
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
.

Proof.

By Section 8, any obstruction would be contained in

11
Completion of the Proof

We now combine the results of the previous sections to complete the proof of the main theorem.

11.1
Summary of the reduction

We have reduced the well-definedness of the recursion

Q(n)=Q(n-Q(n-1))+Q(n-Q(n-2))+(-1)^{n}

to a finite constraint problem as follows:

•

The local behavior of the recursion is encoded by a finite set of contexts
\Lambda_{\mathrm{adm}}
.

•

Compatibility between neighboring configurations is captured by the relation
\Psi
.

•

Valid configurations correspond to global assignments satisfying all
\Psi
-constraints.

Thus, the recursion is well-defined if and only if every locally admissible configuration extends to a valid global assignment.

11.2
Structural decomposition

We established several key structural results:

•

(
Two-mode structure
) Every valid assignment belongs to either Mode A or Mode B, determined by the value at the root context
\lambda_{0}
.

•

(
Support sets
) For each context
\lambda
, the sets
\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)
and
\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)
describe all possible local values under the two modes.

•

(
Sensitive reduction
) Any minimal obstruction must be contained in the sensitive set

\Lambda_{\mathrm{sens}}=\{\lambda:\Sigma_{A}(\lambda)\neq\Sigma_{B}(\lambda)\}.

•

(
Critical core
) Every minimal obstruction is contained in the finite set

\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}=\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{14},\lambda_{24}\}.

11.3
Elimination of obstructions

The remaining task was to verify that no subset of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
is an obstruction.

In Section 10, we showed that every subset
H\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}
admits a valid assignment. In particular, the uniform assignment

f(\lambda)=S1[0]

satisfies all compatibility constraints.

11.5
Final remark

The proof shows that the perturbed Hofstadter recursion admits a complete finite-state description at the level of local behavior. Despite the nonlocal nature of the recursion, all potential inconsistencies are confined to a finite combinatorial structure, which can be exhaustively analyzed.

11.6
Outlook

The finite-state reduction developed here suggests that similar techniques may apply to other meta-Fibonacci recursions with bounded perturbations. In particular, identifying finite symbolic models and critical cores may provide a general framework for proving well-definedness in broader classes of recursive sequences.
