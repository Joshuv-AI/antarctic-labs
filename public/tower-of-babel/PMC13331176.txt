Surgical management of intracerebral hemorrhage: A meta-analysis demonstrating improved functional outcome and survival with time-dependent functional benefit
Authors: Ali Hammed, Steffen Klaus Rosahl, Bilal Younes, Numier Alyosef, Sebastian Ertl, Salah Hammed, Moufid Mahfoud, Thomas Reithmeier
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: The role of surgical evacuation in spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) remains controversial. Previous trials have yielded inconclusive results, and uncertainty persists regarding how hemorrhage location, hematoma volume, surgical technique, and timing of intervention influence functional outcomes and mortality.Methods: We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of 29 randomized controlled trials comprising 5,531 patients comparing surgical evacuation with conservative medical management. Primary outcomes were a favorable functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale ≤3 at final follow-up) and all-cause mortality. Prespecified subgroup analyses explored potential effect modification by timing of intervention, hemorrhage location, hematoma volume, and surgical technique. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Absolute effects and numbers needed to treat (NNT) were derived from pooled estimates using representative control event rates.Results: Surgical evacuation was associated with improved functional outcome (OR 1.31, 95% CI 1.17–1.46) and reduced all-cause mortality (OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.73–0.93) compared with conservative management. Using representative control event rates, the pooled ORs correspond to an estimated absolute increase in favorable functional outcome of approximately 6–7% (NNT ≈15; range 11–26) and an absolute mortality reduction of approximately 4–5% (NNT ≈21; range 13–55), depending on baseline risk assumptions. In subgroup analysis, surgery performed within 24 hours showed greater absolute clinical benefit, corresponding to an estimated NNT of approximately 10 for favorable functional outcome and 12 for mortality reduction. Subgroup analysis demonstrated a statistically significant interaction between timing and functional outcome (p = 0.005), with greater functional benefit observed when surgery was performed within 24 h. In contrast, mortality reduction did not differ significantly according to timing (interaction p = 0.25). No statistically significant effect modification by hemorrhage location was detected. Although effect estimates varied numerically across hematoma volume categories, formal interaction testing did not consistently demonstrate definitive effect modification.Conclusion: Surgical evacuation in spontaneous ICH is associated with improved functional outcome and reduced mortality compared with conservative management. Greater functional benefit was observed with earlier intervention. No statistically significant effect modification was detected for anatomical location or surgical technique, and subgroup differences by hematoma volume should be interpreted cautiously. These findings support consideration of timely and individualized surgical management in appropriately selected patients.

INTRODUCTION

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is a devastating neurological emergency with profound clinical and societal impact. Although ICH accounts for only 10–15% of all strokes, it contributes disproportionately to stroke-related mortality and long-term disability. Despite advancements in acute medical care, patient outcomes remain poor: Nearly 40% of patients succumb within the 1st month, and fewer than one in five survivors achieve functional independence.[1-3] The current standard of care focuses on supportive medical interventions, such as aggressive blood pressure management, reversal of anticoagulation, and intracranial pressure regulation. Crucially, these measures do not directly address the mechanical mass effect or the cytotoxic burden imposed by the hematoma itself.

Surgical evacuation has long been proposed as a direct method to remove the clot, decompress surrounding brain tissue, and potentially interrupt the cascade of secondary injury. However, the role of operative intervention remains a subject of considerable controversy. Landmark randomized controlled trials (RCTs), including surgical trials in lobar intracerebral hemorrhage (STICH) and STICH II, failed to demonstrate a clear, overall benefit of early surgery compared to conservative medical management, leading to a perception of clinical equipoise regarding the procedure.[22,23] Subsequent patient-level analyses have suggested that this apparent neutrality may be due to treatment effect heterogeneity, indicating that certain patient subgroups, such as those with moderate neurological impairment (Glasgow coma scale 10–13) and large hematoma volumes, may, in fact, derive substantial benefit from surgery.

The field has been recently reinvigorated by the advent of minimally invasive surgical (MIS) techniques, including stereotactic aspiration and endoscopic evacuation. These approaches have shown encouraging results in terms of reduced complication rates and enhanced functional recovery in selected patients.[16,17,24,25,38] Nevertheless, significant uncertainties persist in the literature. Specifically, the influence of key variables such as hemorrhage location (lobar, deep, or mixed), the comparative efficacy of different surgical techniques (MIS vs. open craniotomy), and the critical timing of intervention remains to be fully elucidated. Many prior studies have either excluded certain locations, such as infratentorial bleeds, or failed to adequately stratify outcomes by these critical factors.

Despite multiple RCTs and prior meta-analyses, uncertainty persists regarding whether surgical evacuation provides a consistent benefit over conservative management in spontaneous ICH, and how this effect is modified by hemorrhage location, hematoma volume, surgical technique, and—critically—timing of intervention. To address these persistent gaps and the ongoing clinical debate, we conducted a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs comparing surgical and conservative management of spontaneous ICH. The primary objective was to quantify the effect of surgical evacuation on functional outcome and all-cause mortality, while the secondary objective was to determine whether treatment effects are modified by timing of intervention, hematoma volume, anatomical location, and surgical technique.

This systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines.[32] The protocol was prospectively registered in the PROSPERO database (CRD420251032932).

The search was conducted from the inception of the database to October, 2025. Assessment of surgical timing

Time to surgery was extracted as reported in each trial. When available, timing was defined as the interval from symptom onset to surgical evacuation; otherwise, the interval from hospital admission or randomization to surgery was used. Trials were categorized into ≤24 h or >24 h based on the reported time window. When timing definitions were unclear, classification followed the predominant recruitment criteria described in the study. Sensitivity analyses excluding trials with ambiguous timing definitions were performed to assess robustness.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes were:

Favourable functional outcome, defined as a mRS score of ≤3 at final follow-up; and

All-cause mortality at last available follow-up.

Secondary analyses examined the influence of hemorrhage location, surgical technique, timing of intervention, and hematoma volume on treatment effect.

Risk of bias (ROB) assessment

ROB was assessed using the revised Cochrane ROB tool for randomized trials (ROB-2). Two reviewers independently evaluated each included study across five domains: Randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of outcomes, and selection of reported results. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer. Domains were judged as low risk, some concerns, or high risk, and an overall judgment was assigned for each study based on ROB-2 guidance.

Certainty of evidence assessment (grading of recommendations assessment, development, and evaluation [GRADE])

Certainty of evidence was assessed using the GRADE framework. Two reviewers independently evaluated each outcome across five domains (ROB, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias), with discrepancies resolved by consensus. As all included studies were randomized trials, outcomes were initially rated as high certainty and downgraded only when domain-specific concerns were identified. In accordance with GRADE recommendations, seven clinically important outcomes were prioritized and summarized in a Summary of Findings table. GRADE ratings informed the interpretation of results but did not alter pooled effect estimates.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using RevMan (version 5•4•1). Pooled odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated on the log-OR scale using a fixed-effects model. When heterogeneity was present, pooled estimates were additionally examined using random-effects models, applying either the Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman or DerSimonian–Laird approach, as appropriate. Prespecified subgroup analyses were conducted to explore potential effect modification.

Statistical heterogeneity across studies was assessed using the χ2 test and quantified with the I2 statistic, with substantial heterogeneity defined as I2 > 50% or p < 0.10.

Calculation of absolute effects and numbers needed to treat (NNT)

Absolute effects were derived from pooled ORs by converting them to absolute risk differences using control-group event rates from the included trials. For each outcome, the baseline risk was defined as the median control-group event rate across trials (with the pooled control event rate reported as a secondary summary). The time horizon corresponded to the final available follow-up in each study. Expected event rates in the surgical group were calculated from the pooled OR and baseline risk using standard transformations, and NNT values were computed as the reciprocal of the absolute risk difference. Uncertainty in NNT was expressed as a range derived from the 95% CI of the pooled OR applied to the same baseline risk. These absolute effect estimates and NNT values were derived from pooled ORs and should be interpreted as approximate and dependent on baseline risk assumptions.

RESULTS

A total of 2472 records were identified across five databases, of which 1539 duplicates were removed before screening. The remaining 933 records underwent title and abstract screening, yielding 60 full-text articles for eligibility assessment. Following review, 31 studies were excluded due to insufficient quantitative data (n = 15), duplicate cohorts (n = 5), unclear or inconsistent outcome definitions (n = 6). In addition, five studies were excluded because they did not meet the predefined methodological quality criteria. The final meta-analysis therefore included 29 studies[4-7,11-15,18-21,23-30,33-36], selected through a rigorous and transparent screening process to ensure data quality and methodological consistency [Figure 1].

Characteristics of the included studies

The dataset comprises 29 clinical trials including 5,531 patients, representing a broad international effort to evaluate surgical versus conservative management strategies for spontaneous ICH. The included studies span more than three decades and originate from Europe, North America, and Asia, underscoring the global clinical relevance of this condition.

Study designs ranged from small single-center trials with limited sample sizes (as low as 10–21 participants) to large, pivotal multicenter randomized trials, most notably the STICH trials (n = 1,033 and n = 601), as well as several contemporary multinational studies.

Interventions were heterogeneous but predominantly involved surgical hematoma evacuation compared with conservative medical treatment. Surgical techniques included open craniotomy, minimally invasive procedures such as stereotactic aspiration, endoscopic evacuation, and minimally invasive parafascicular surgery -based approaches, as well as decompressive craniectomy in selected patients with significant mass effect. Conservative medical therapy served as the control arm in the majority of trials.

Across studies, patients typically presented with clinically relevant hematoma volumes, most commonly ranging between 50 and 75 mL, although some trials included smaller (<30 mL) or very large (>80–90 mL) hemorrhages. Hematoma location varied, with most studies enrolling patients with lobar, deep (basal ganglia or thalamic), or mixed supratentorial hemorrhages, while several large trials explicitly included both anatomical sites. Follow-up duration varied across studies, ranging from short-term assessments (≤24–48 h) to long-term follow-up of up to 120 months, allowing evaluation of both early mortality and longer-term functional outcomes [Table 1].

Best functional outcome

A total of 27 studies were included in the meta-analysis, evaluating functional outcomes following surgical versus conservative treatment. The pooled analysis demonstrated a statistically significant benefit of surgical intervention, with higher odds of achieving a favorable functional outcome compared with conservative management (OR = 1.31, 95% CI 1.17–1.46; p < 0.00001) [Figure 3]. Between-study heterogeneity was low to moderate (I2 = 24%). Using a control-group favorable outcome rate of 46.6% at final follow-up for absolute risk translation, the pooled effect (OR 1.31, 95% CI 1.17–1.46) corresponds to an estimated favorable outcome rate of 53.3% in the surgical group, representing an absolute increase of 6.7%. This translates into a NNT of approximately 15, with a corresponding range of approximately 11–26 based on the 95% CI of the pooled OR.

Mortality outcome

Across 18 studies, surgical management was associated with a statistically significant reduction in mortality compared with conservative treatment (pooled OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.73–0.93; p = 0.002). Between-study heterogeneity was low (I2 = 9%), indicating consistent effects across studies. Using a control-group mortality rate of 52.3% at final follow-up for absolute risk translation, the pooled effect (OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.73–0.93) corresponds to an estimated mortality rate of 47.6% in the surgical group, representing an absolute reduction of 4.7%. This translates into a NNT of approximately 21, with a corresponding range of approximately 13–55 based on the 95% CI [Figure 8].

Certainty of evidence

Using the GRADE framework, certainty of evidence was rated as high for both favorable functional outcome and mortality, indicating a reliable and clinically meaningful treatment effect of surgical evacuation compared with conservative care. Evidence regarding modifiers such as timing, surgical technique, hematoma volume, and hemorrhage location ranged from moderate to very low certainty, mainly due to indirectness and imprecision inherent to subgroup analyses. Overall, the evidence supports a high-certainty benefit of surgery in improving outcomes, while emphasizing the importance of individualized selection based on hemorrhage characteristics and timing [Table 2].

DISCUSSION

Summary of findings

This meta-analysis demonstrates that surgical evacuation is associated with a statistically significant improvement in functional outcomes and a reduction in all-cause mortality when compared with conservative management in spontaneous ICH. Nonetheless, the magnitude of benefit varied across prespecified clinical strata, reflecting the complex interaction between patient-, disease-, and treatment-specific factors.

Temporal factors emerged as an important determinant of functional efficacy. Early intervention (≤24 h) yielded a significantly larger improvement in functional recovery than delayed surgery, whereas delayed intervention (>24 h) remained beneficial, albeit with a smaller effect size. In contrast, subgroup analyses of mortality revealed no statistically significant interaction with timing, indicating that the survival advantage associated with surgical evacuation is not dependent on the temporal interval between symptom onset and treatment, despite directional differences in point estimates. These data suggest that functional recovery is more sensitive to treatment latency than survival.

Subgroup-specific estimates varied according to anatomical location; however, formal interaction testing did not demonstrate statistically significant effect modification. Functional improvement appeared more consistent in deep ICH, whereas lobar hemorrhage showed a non-significant trend toward benefit. Mortality reduction did not differ significantly across anatomical locations, and no statistically significant effect modification by location was detected.

Hematoma volume was a salient modifier of functional outcome. Patients with small and medium-sized hematomas experienced the greatest functional gains, whereas those with large hematomas showed a smaller but statistically significant improvement. Conversely, mortality benefit was most pronounced in large-volume hemorrhage, with no evidence of statistically significant interaction, suggesting preserved survival benefit across volume strata.

Regarding surgical modality, both minimally invasive and open approaches were associated with improved functional outcomes, with no statistically significant difference between techniques. For mortality, open surgery demonstrated a statistically significant survival benefit, whereas minimally invasive approaches showed a non-significant trend toward benefit. These findings suggest that mortality reduction is primarily driven by effective hematoma evacuation rather than the specific surgical approach.

In aggregate, surgical evacuation provides clinically meaningful benefits in both survival and functional recovery across diverse clinical contexts. Variation in treatment effects was more pronounced for functional outcomes than for mortality, underscoring the importance of early intervention and individualized patient selection when functional optimization is the primary therapeutic objective. Importantly, absolute treatment effects demonstrated greater clinical benefit with early surgical intervention. Compared with the pooled analysis (NNT ≈15 for favorable functional outcome and ≈21 for mortality reduction), surgery performed within 24 hours improved estimated NNT values to approximately 10 and 12, respectively.

COMPARISON WITH PREVIOUS EVIDENCE

Previous meta-analyses and systematic reviews have consistently suggested a benefit of surgical intervention in spontaneous ICH; however, the majority were significantly limited by a narrow scope, often focusing on comparing various surgical modalities (e.g., MIS vs. endoscopy) and establishing their inherent efficacy hierarchy, as detailed in reports by Alkhiri et al.,[3] Guo et al.,[14] Waseem et al.,[37] and Huan et al.[18] Consequently, these earlier works offered limited direct insight into the fundamental core clinical decision of surgery versus conservative therapy. Furthermore, while foundational analyses by Sondag et al.[30] and Zhou et al.[40] confirmed an overall direction of benefit, and earlier RCTs cited by Akhigbe et al.,[1] and Gregson et al.[13] were essential, they were often constrained by small sample sizes or limited anatomical diversity. This lack of statistical power in previous studies prevented robust and detailed subgroup analyses necessary to identify which specific patient characteristics or procedural factors might modify the treatment effect. More recent contributions, such as the location-specific analysis by Al-Ajlan et al.[2] (focused exclusively on deep ICH and demonstrated substantial heterogeneity), added valuable, but ultimately incomplete insights. A recent network meta-analysis by Wang et al. [34] (2025) evaluated a range of surgical techniques and suggested that endoscopic surgery and minimally invasive puncture surgery may reduce mortality and increase functional independence. However, the certainty of these findings was limited, as the underlying evidence was graded as low to very low due to considerable heterogeneity.

The present meta-analysis addresses key limitations of prior studies by pooling a substantially larger evidence base, encompassing twenty-nine RCTs with 5,531 patients, to comprehensively compare surgical evacuation with conservative medical management. Our findings demonstrate a statistically significant benefit of surgical intervention for both functional outcome and all-cause mortality. Importantly, prespecified subgroup analyses revealed clinically relevant effect modification: timing significantly influenced functional recovery, with the greatest benefit observed following early intervention, and functional improvement appeared greater in small-to-medium hematomas, although subgroup findings remain exploratory. In contrast, the survival benefit of surgery was observed across hematoma volume strata, without evidence of significant interaction. Finally, the consistent benefit of surgery compared with conservative management across both minimally invasive and open approaches suggests that the principal therapeutic effect is driven by effective hematoma evacuation rather than the specific surgical technique employed.

COMPARISON WITH ESO GUIDELINES AND IMPLICATIONS FOR FUTURE PRACTICE

The robust findings of this meta-analysis provide strong evidence that broadly supports, and in several aspects extends, the 2025 European Stroke Organization (ESO) guidelines for the surgical management of spontaneous ICH. The ESO guidelines recommend consideration of surgery in lobar hemorrhage and adopt a more selective approach for deep hemorrhages, particularly in patients with clinical deterioration.[41] Our analysis is consistent with this individualized framework, demonstrating a statistically significant overall benefit of surgical evacuation, without evidence of statistically significant effect modification by anatomical location. Although the anatomical site did not significantly modify the survival effect, functional improvement appeared more pronounced in deep ICH; however, subgroup findings should be interpreted cautiously. These results align with the ESO emphasis on patient-specific rather than purely anatomical selection criteria.

A key contribution of the present analysis relates to surgical timing, an aspect acknowledged by the ESO guidelines but not defined by a specific temporal threshold. Our findings demonstrate that earlier intervention, particularly within 24 h, is associated with a substantially greater magnitude of functional improvement compared with delayed surgery. Importantly, although delayed surgical evacuation remained beneficial, these results suggest that prioritization of early intervention may be critical for optimizing functional recovery.

In addition, our stratified analyses provide clinically relevant insights regarding hematoma volume and surgical technique. Functional improvement appeared greater in small-to-medium hematomas, although subgroup findings remain exploratory, reinforcing the ESO recommendation for individualized decision-making based on prognostic factors. Furthermore, by directly comparing surgical evacuation with conservative management, our results indicate that both minimally invasive and open surgical approaches are associated with improved functional outcomes. This supports the interpretation that the principal therapeutic benefit arises from effective hematoma removal rather than the specific operative technique employed.

In summary, this meta-analysis not only corroborates the overarching direction of the ESO 2025 guidelines but also contributes high-level evidence highlighting the critical role of early timing and hemorrhage characteristics in achieving optimal outcomes following surgical evacuation in spontaneous ICH.

CLINICAL AND RESEARCH IMPLICATIONS

These findings have important clinical implications for the triage and management of patients with spontaneous ICH. Early identification and rapid referral of surgical candidates are crucial, as functional benefit is maximized when evacuation is initiated within 24 h of onset, highlighting the need for time-based treatment targets similar to reperfusion paradigms in ischemic stroke. Efficient system organization, including standardized triage protocols and expedited access to neurosurgical capability, may therefore improve outcomes at a population level. Patient selection should incorporate hemorrhage volume, neurological status, and anticipated surgical accessibility, as functional benefit was greater in small-to-medium hematomas, whereas mortality benefit was most pronounced in large-volume lesions. These results support a more proactive and individualized surgical strategy rather than routine conservative management.

Future research should prioritize randomized trials that stratify patients by time-to-intervention, particularly within ultra-early windows (<12 h), to determine whether accelerated evacuation yields additional functional and survival benefits. Individual patient-level meta-analyses are needed to validate modifiers such as volume, location, and baseline clinical severity, and to better characterize treatment-effect heterogeneity. Moreover, outcomes beyond the mRS—including cognitive recovery, quality of life, and long-term functional reintegration—should be systematically evaluated to capture the full therapeutic impact of surgical intervention. Research efforts that integrate imaging biomarkers, standardized surgical techniques, and health-systems implementation strategies may further optimize outcomes and reduce treatment variability.

STRENGTHS AND LIMITATIONS

Strengths

The primary strength of this meta-analysis lies in its substantially large and robust evidence base, encompassing 29 RCTs with 5,531 patients. This scale provides high statistical power, allowing us to definitively confirm the overall benefit of surgical evacuation over conservative management for both functional outcome and mortality. Crucially, the size and comprehensive scope enabled detailed, high-powered subgroup analyses that were previously unattainable. We were able to quantify the influence of critical clinical modifiers such as timing (demonstrating the superiority of intervention within 24 h), hematoma volume (showing volume-dependence for functional gains), and surgical technique, thereby providing specific, actionable insights that resolve previous uncertainties and enhance clinical decision-making.

Limitations

Despite its strengths, the analysis is subject to several limitations. First, while we included only RCTs, the ROB assessment revealed that a proportion of studies had “some concerns,” which may introduce methodological heterogeneity, although no study was rated at high risk. Second, the heterogeneity of surgical techniques across the included trials (ranging from open craniotomy to various minimally invasive approaches) makes it challenging to isolate the effect of one specific technique, though we did find that the overall benefit likely stems from hematoma evacuation itself. Finally, the analysis relied on published data, precluding a more granular, patient-level meta-analysis, which would allow for simultaneous adjustment of multiple interacting prognostic factors.

CONCLUSION

Surgical evacuation for spontaneous ICH is associated with clinically meaningful improvements in functional outcome and reductions in all-cause mortality compared with conservative management. In absolute terms, the estimated NNT is approximately 15 to achieve one additional favorable functional outcome and approximately 21 to prevent one death, depending on baseline risk assumptions. Greater functional benefit was observed when surgery was performed within 24 h, whereas mortality reduction did not differ significantly according to timing. Although effect estimates varied numerically across hematoma volume categories and anatomical locations, formal interaction testing did not demonstrate consistent statistically significant effect modification. These findings support consideration of timely and individualized surgical strategies in appropriately selected patients.
