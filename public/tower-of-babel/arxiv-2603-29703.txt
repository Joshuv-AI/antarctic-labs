On Lipschitzian properties of multifunctions defined implicitly by “split” feasibility problems Journal: Noname
Amos Uderzo

Abstract
In the present paper, a systematic study is made of quantitative semicontinuity (a.k.a. Lipschitzian) properties of certain multifunctions, which are defined as a solution map associated to a family of parameterized “split” feasibility problems. The latter are a particular class of convex feasibility problems with well recognized applications to several areas of engineering and systems biology. As a part of a perturbation analysis of variational systems, this study falls within the framework of a line of research pursued by several authors. It is performed by means of techniques of variational analysis, which lead to establish sufficient conditions for the Lipschitz lower semicontinuity, calmness, isolated calmness, Lipschitz upper semicontinuity and Aubin property of the solution map. Along with each of these properties, a quantitative estimate of the related exact bound is also provided. The key elements emerging on the way to achieving the main results are dual regularity conditions qualifying the problem behaviour, which are expressed in terms of convex analysis constructions involving problem data. The approach here proposed tries to unify the study of the aforementioned properties.

Introduction

Let
(\mathbb{X},\|\cdot\|)
and
(\mathbb{Y},\|\cdot\|)
be real normed (vector) spaces.
Given a linear bounded operator
A:\mathbb{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Y}
, and two nonempty,
closed and convex sets
C\subseteq\mathbb{X}
and
Q\subset\mathbb{Y}
, the following problem

\hbox{find }x\in C:\ A(x)\in Q.

[formula]

is an abstract formulation of the “split” feasibility problem, which was
originally introduced in
(
CenElf94
, Section 6.1)
in a finite-dimensional setting.
Since its solution set can be clearly represented as
\Sigma=C\cap A^{-1}(Q)
,
one sees that (SFP) falls in the class of the convex feasibility problems, which
consist in finding a common point to several given convex sets
(see
BauBor93
;
BauBor96
;
Breg67
;
Comb93
;
GuPoRa67
).
Having found relevant applications in signal processing and in image
reconstruction (see
CeElKoBo05
;
Hurt89
), (SFP) has been widely studied,
especially from the point of view of solution algorithms
(see
CenElf94
;
HuXuYe25
;
HuXuYe25b
;
ReTrMa20
;
ReiTuy21
;
WaHuLiYa17
;
WaGaLiYa24
).

In the present paper, a parameterized version of “split” feasibility problems
will be considered in the following terms. Let
(P,d)
be
a metric space representing the set of all parameter perturbations.
Given a mapping
A:P\times\mathbb{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Y}
and two
set-valued maps
C:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
and
Q:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
,
any element
p\in P
determines the “split” feasibility problem

\hbox{find }x\in C(p):\ A(p,x)\in Q(p).

[formula]

The solution set-valued map
\Sigma:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
associated to the resulting family of perturbed
“split” feasibility problems
({\rm SFP}_{p})
is therefore given by

\Sigma(p)=\left\{x\in C(p):\ A(p,x)\in Q(p)\right\}=C(p)\cap A(p,\cdot)^{-1}(Q(p)).

(1)

The subject of this paper are so-called Lipschitzian
properties of the multifunction
\Sigma
.
The study here exposed follows a research line recently
initiated by
HuXuYe25
;
HuXuYe25b
and should be regarded
as a part of a more comprehensive perturbation analysis of

({\rm SFP})
. This term denotes an apparatus of systematic investigations
about effects produced on the solution set by changes of problem data,
which may be caused by different reasons. These investigations
may be qualitative, e.g. when dealing with existence of solutions (solvability)
or their topological properties under changes of data, or quantitative,
when focusing on the measurement of variations of the solution set
as a consequence of measured changes of the perturbation parameter
(sensitivity).
The perturbation analysis is a crucial issue in understanding the
deep nature of many mathematical problems. In the specific case of

({\rm SFP})
, some reasons for conducting a perturbation analysis are
well explained in
HuXuYe25
;
HuXuYe25b
. To them, some further
motivations can be added, which come from the topics described below:

\bullet

mathematical programming with
({\rm SFP})
constraints
.
Given
\vartheta:P\times\mathbb{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
,

\Omega\subseteq P
and a family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
, consider the optimization
problem

\min\vartheta(p,x)\ \hbox{ subject to }\ p\in\Omega,\ x\in\Sigma(p),

(2)

where
\Sigma
is as in (
1
).
The definition of the feasible region of such an optimization problem
involves lower level “split” feasibility problems to be solved for each
value of
p
. This may be not always possible or convenient for the
analysis of the given optimization problem, so the behaviour of
\Sigma

as an implicit multifunction becomes an important issue.
Indeed, the problem (
2
) can be reformulated as a mathematical
program with equilibrium constraints (for short,
{\rm MPEC}
). A successful approach to
derive optimality conditions for
{\rm MPEC}
is known to rely on the
Lipschitzian stability of parameterized variational systems
(see
(
Mord06b
, Section 5.2)
).

\bullet

bi-level
({\rm SFP})
. Given two maps
defined on normed spaces
A_{1},\,A_{2}:\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Y}
,
with
A_{1}(\cdot,x)\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Y})
for each
x\in\mathbb{X}
and

A_{2}(p,\cdot)\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
,
two subsets
C_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
and
Q_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
, and two set-valued maps

C_{2}:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
and
Q_{2}:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
, the problem

\hbox{find }p\in C_{1}:\ A_{1}(p,x)\in Q_{1}\hbox{ and }x\in\Sigma(p),

where
\Sigma:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
is the solution map of the lower
level problem

\hbox{find }x\in C_{2}(p):\ A_{2}(p,x)\in Q_{2}(p),

can be referred to as a bi-level “split” feasibility problem. Also in this context,
without solving each lower level problem, one can
take profit from indirect information about the behaviour of the
multifunction
\Sigma
.

\bullet

approximated solution methods
. It is well known
that there are some classes of
({\rm SFP})
significantly more tractable,
from both the theoretical and the computational viewpoint, than others.
An instance of this is provided by
({\rm SFP})
with polyhedral data:
advantages in computing projections (on which CQ algorithms rely) and
the possibility of exploiting linear algebra tools make them preferable
to
({\rm SFP})
with general convex data. Thus, one could consider to perturb
a given
({\rm SFP})
in such a way to generate a sequence
({\rm SFP}_{p_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
with each problem of the sequence (approximated problem) having polyhedral data.
Such an approach opens the question whether sequences of solutions to
approximated problems do and, if so, how converge to a solution of the
original
({\rm SFP})
. Meaningful insights into this question can be afforded
from a knowledge of the behaviour of the multifunction
\Sigma
.

1.1
Outline, methodology and contributions

The contents of the paper are arranged as follows.
In the next subsection the basic notations employed in the paper
are listed.
In Section
2
, the definition of the main properties under investigation
is recalled and needed technical preliminaries are gathered.
In Section
3
, the main results are exposed. They are sufficient conditions
for
\Sigma
to satisfy each of the Lipschitzian properties recalled
in Section
2
, with the perturbation parameter
p
varying in
a metric space. Each of them is complemented with an estimate of the related
exact bound, quantifying “how much” the property is fulfilled.
In Section
4
, a specialized sufficient condition for the Aubin
property of
\Sigma
is established under a convexity assumption, with the
perturbation parameter now varying in a normed space.

A feature of the present study to be remarked is that all of the presented
sufficient conditions, except one, are derived from the same preliminary
result, which states a condition for local solvability and error bounds.
Such a feature reflects the methodology chosen to accomplish the task
of the present study. Instead of performing one independent analysis for
each of the Lipschitzian properties, according to the approach here adopted
all the results are achieved as a consequence of a unique qualification condition,
and its nonlocal counterpart in the case of upper Lipschitz continuity.
The price to be paid for getting a unifying scheme of analysis is that, in general, such
qualification condition turns out to be too strong to obtain full
characterizations.

In fact,
({\rm SFP}_{p})
can be regarded as a class of parametric variational systems,
namely a parameterized version of generalized equations. A well developed
literature devoted to the study of the Lipschitzian properties
of the related solution map is nowadays at disposal within variational
analysis (see, among others,
DonRoc14
;
LevRoc94
;
Mord06
;
Robi79
;
Robi80
).
A characterization of the Aubin peorperty of
\Sigma
has been established
in
HuXuYe25
, in the particular case of a finite-dimensional setting,
in which
C
and
Q
are supposed to be constant (given sets) and the map
A

is perturbed in the Euclidean space of all the matrices with real entries.
This result was achieved by exploiting a finite-dimensional characterization
of the Aubin property of solution map to variational systems due to B.S.
Mordukhovich, which relies on the coderivative calculus.
In contrast, to perform a comprehensive analysis allowing perturbations
to vary in a metric space, a different approach has been followed in the
present paper. Whereas some constructions based on subdifferential calculus
are still used, solvability and a key error bound estimate, on which the main
results are based, are obtained by means of elementary tools of variational analysis
in metric spaces (partial strong slope), via a scalarization method
in the spirit of
BorZhu05
.

Tools of variational analysis in metric spaces

The main properties, investigated in the present paper with

Definition 1
(Lipschitzian properties)

Let

be a set-valued mapping between
metric spaces and let
(p_{0},x_{0})\in{\rm gph}\,F
. The map
F
is said:

(i)

to be Lipschitz lower semicontinuous at

if

\ell
such that

F(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[x_{0};\ell d(p,p_{0})\right]\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[p_{0};\delta\right];

(5)

the value

{\rm Liplsc}(F;p_{0},x_{0})=\inf\{\ell>0:\ \exists\delta>0\hbox{ for which (\ref{in:defLiplsc}) holds}\}

is called exact Lipschitz lower semicontinuity bound of
F

at
(p_{0},x_{0})
.

(ii)

to be calm at
(p_{0},x_{0})
if there exist
positive constants
\delta
,
\zeta
and
\ell
such that

F(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[x_{0};\zeta\right]\subseteq{\rm B}\left[F(p_{0});\ell d(p,p_{0})\right],\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[p_{0};\delta\right];

(6)

the value

{\rm clm}(F;p_{0},x_{0})=\inf\{\ell>0:\ \exists\delta,\,\zeta>0\hbox{ for which (\ref{in:defsvcalm}) holds}\}

is called exact calmness bound of
F
at
(p_{0},x_{0})
.

(iii)

to be Lipschitz upper semicontinuous (or
outer Lipschitz continuous/upper Lipschitzian) at

if
there exist positive constants
\delta
and
\ell
such that

F(p)\subseteq{\rm B}\left[F(p_{0});\ell d(p,p_{0})\right],\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[p_{0};\delta\right];

(7)

the value

{\rm Lipusc}(F;p_{0})=\inf\{\ell>0:\ \exists\delta>0\hbox{ for which (\ref{in:defLipusc}) holds}\}

is called exact Lipschitz upper semicontinuity bound of

(iv)

to satisfy the Aubin property (or, equivalently,

,

(8)

the value

{\rm Lip}(F;p_{0},x_{0})=\inf\{\ell>0:\ \exists\delta,\,\eta>0\hbox{ for which (\ref{in:defAubin}) holds}\}

Remark 2

(i) In view of a subsequent use, let us recall that if

F:P\rightrightarrows X
is Lipschitz l.s.c.
at
(p_{0},x_{0})\in{\rm gph}\,F
, then it is also l.s.c. in the sense
of
Borw81
at the same point, namely for every
\epsilon>0

there must exist
\delta_{\epsilon}>0
such that

F(p)\cap{\rm B}\left(x_{0};\epsilon\right)\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[p_{0};\delta_{\epsilon}\right].

The latter is a localized counterpart of the classic
notion of lower semicontinuity for set-valued maps acting in
metric spaces.

(ii) Following
(
DonRoc14
, Section 3.2)
,
a set-valued map
F:P\rightrightarrows X
between metric spaces
is said to be inner semicontinuous (for short, i.s.c.) at

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to p_{0}}{\rm exc}(F(p_{0});F(p))=0
. In connection with the present analysis,
it is useful to notice that if
F
is i.s.c. at
p_{0}
, then it is

(p_{0},x_{0})
, for every
x_{0}\in F(p_{0})
.
Indeed, fixed
\epsilon>0

existence of

So, taken an arbitrary
x_{0}\in F(p_{0})
, from the last inequality
one obtains in particular

{\rm dist}\left(x_{0};F(p)\right)<\epsilon,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[p_{0};r_{\epsilon}\right],

which implies

F(p)\cap{\rm B}\left(x_{0};\epsilon\right)\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[p_{0};r_{\epsilon}\right].

Moreover, it is known that

is i.s.c. at

iff

So, if

is i.s.c. at

, then the

(p_{0},x_{0})
.

d(F(p),F(p_{0}))\leq\ell d(p,p_{0}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[p_{0};\delta\right],

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to x_{0}}{\varphi(p_{0},x_{0})-\varphi(p_{0},x)\over d(x,x_{0})},&\hbox{\ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The fundamental fact that will be employed for ensuring solution existence and for providing
useful error bounds via a strong slope condition is the following Basic Lemma.
For its proof in a metric space setting, which is based on the
Ekeland variational principle, the reader is referred to
(

, Lemma 2.3)
.
Another (earlier) formulation in a more structured setting can be found in

(

, Theorem 3.6.3)
.

Lemma 1
(Basic Lemma)

Let
P
and
X
be metric spaces, let
(\bar{p},\bar{x})\in P\times X

and let
\varphi:P\times X\longrightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a given function.
Suppose that:

(i)

(X,d)
is metrically complete;

(ii)

\varphi(\bar{p},\bar{x})=0
;

(iii)

the function
p\mapsto\varphi(p,\bar{x})
is
(u.s.) continuous at
\bar{p}
;

(iv)

\exists r>0
such that each function
x\mapsto\varphi(p,x)

is l.s.c. on
X
, for every

;

(v)

\exists\delta>0
such that

\tau_{\nabla}(\delta)=\inf\{|\nabla_{x}\varphi|(p,x):\ (p,x)\in[{\rm B}\left(\bar{p};\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(\bar{x};\delta\right)]\cap[\varphi>0]\}>0.

Then, there exist
\eta,\,\zeta>0
such that:

(t)

[\varphi(p,\cdot)\leq 0]\cap{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta\right]\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\zeta\right]
;

(tt)

the following estimate holds

{\rm dist}\left(x;[\varphi(p,\cdot)\leq 0]\right)\leq\tau_{\nabla}^{-1}(\delta)\varphi(p,x),\quad\forall(p,x)\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\zeta\right]\times{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta\right].

(11)

The next lemma provides a sufficient condition for the continuity of
the function

),

Lemma 2

With reference to a family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
, let
(\bar{p},\bar{x})\in{\rm gph}\,\Sigma
.
Suppose that:

(i)

C:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
is l.s.c. at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
;

(ii)

Q:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
is l.s.c. at
\left(\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x})\right)
;

(iii)

A(\cdot,\bar{x}):P\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
is continuous at
\bar{p}
.

Then, the function
p\mapsto\nu(p,\bar{x})
is continuous at
\bar{p}
.

Proof

Fix an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
. According to hypothesis (i), corresponding to

\epsilon/3
there exists
\delta_{C,\epsilon}>0
such that

C(p)\cap{\rm B}\left(\bar{x};\epsilon/3\right)\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{C,\epsilon}\right],

and hence

{\rm dist}\left(\bar{x};C(p)\right)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3},\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{C,\epsilon}\right].

(12)

Similarly, by hypothesis (ii), corresponding to

\epsilon/3
there exists
\delta_{Q,\epsilon}>0
such that

Q(p)\cap{\rm B}\left(A(\bar{p},\bar{x});\epsilon/3\right)\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{Q,\epsilon}\right],

and hence

{\rm dist}\left(A(\bar{p},\bar{x});C(p)\right)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3},\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{Q,\epsilon}\right].

(13)

According to hypothesis (iii), corresponding to

\epsilon/3
there exists
\delta_{A,\epsilon}>0
such that

\|A(p,\bar{x})-A(\bar{p},\bar{x})\|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3},\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{A,\epsilon}\right].

(14)

Thus, by taking
\delta_{\epsilon}=\min\{\delta_{C,\epsilon},\,\delta_{Q,\epsilon},\,\delta_{A,\epsilon}\}
, as a consequence of inequalities (
12
),
(
13
), and (
14
), one obtains

\displaystyle\nu(p,\bar{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(A(p,\bar{x});Q(p)\right)+{\rm dist}\left(\bar{x};C(p)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A(p,\bar{x})-A(\bar{p},\bar{x})\|+{\rm dist}\left(A(\bar{p},\bar{x});Q(p)\right)+{\rm dist}\left(\bar{x};C(p)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{3}+\frac{\epsilon}{3}+\frac{\epsilon}{3}=\nu(\bar{p},\bar{x})+\epsilon,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{\epsilon}\right].

The thesis follows from the last inequality, as
\nu
is nonnegative
by its definition.

2.3
Tools from convex analysis

The regularity conditions, upon which the main results will be established,
are expressed in terms of some basic constructions of convex analysis,
dealing with the approximation of sets and functions. In what follows,
their definitions and the needed calculus rules are briefly recalled.
Given a convex set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{X}
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega
, the set

{\rm N}(\bar{x};\Omega)=\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}\ :\ \langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq 0,\quad\forall x\in\Omega\}

is the normal cone to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
. By its very definition, it is clear that
the normal cone is always a closed, convex cone in
\mathbb{X}^{*}
, containing
\mathbf{0}^{*}
. Moreover,
whenever
\bar{x}\in{\rm int}\,\Omega
, one has
{\rm N}(\bar{x};\Omega)=\{\mathbf{0}^{*}\}
.

Given a convex function
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}

and
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}\,\varphi
, the (Moreau-Rockafellar) subdifferential of
\varphi

at
\bar{x}
is the set

\partial\varphi(\bar{x})=\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}\ :\ \langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{X}\}.

This derivative-like object is known to enjoy a rich apparatus of calculus.
Among the subdifferential calculus rules, the following ones will be employed in the sequel:

sum rule: if
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
and

\vartheta:\mathbb{X}\longrightarrow\cup\{\pm\infty\}
are convex functions, with at least one
of them being continuous at
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}\,\varphi\cap{\rm dom}\,\vartheta
, then the following equality holds

\partial\left(\varphi+\vartheta\right)(\bar{x})=\partial\varphi(\bar{x})+\partial\vartheta(\bar{x})

(15)

(see
(
MorNam22
, Theorem 3.48)
).

chain rule: if
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
and
\varphi:\mathbb{Y}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}

is a convex function, continuous at
\Lambda(\bar{x})
, with
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
, then it holds

\partial(\varphi\circ\Lambda)(\bar{x})=\Lambda^{*}\left(\partial\varphi(\Lambda\bar{x})\right)

(16)

(see
(
MorNam22
, Theorem 3.55)
).

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{X}
be a nonempty, closed, convex set and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
. In this case, the function
x\mapsto{\rm dist}\left(x;\Omega\right)
turns out to be
(Lipschitz) continuous and convex on
\mathbb{X}
, with
{\rm dom}\,{\rm dist}\left(\cdot;\Omega\right)=\mathbb{X}
.
The generalized differentiation of this function leads to a connection between the
two above constructions:

\partial{\rm dist}\left(\cdot;\Omega\right)(\bar{x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm N}(\bar{x};\Omega)\cap{\mathbb{B}}^{*},&\quad\hbox{ if }\bar{x}\in\Omega,\\
\\
{\rm N}(\bar{x};{\rm B}\left[\Omega;d_{\Omega}\right])\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{*},&\quad\hbox{ if }\bar{x}\not\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(17)

where
d_{\Omega}={\rm dist}\left(\bar{x};\Omega\right)

(see
(
MorNam22
, Proposition 3.77(a), Theorem 3.82)
).

A further element to be recalled enables to estimate the strong slope
of a convex function by means of its subdifferential: if

\varphi:\mathbb{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is a convex
function and
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}\,\varphi
, then

|\nabla\varphi|(\bar{x})=\inf\{\|x^{*}\|:\ x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\}={\rm dist}\left(\mathbf{0}^{*};\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\right).

(18)

(see
(
FaHeKrOu10
, Theorem 5(i))
).

By combining the recalled subdifferential estimate with
(
AzeCor06
, Theorem 2.8)
,
on account of the metric completeness of
\mathbb{X}
(assumption
(a_{0})
),
it is possible to establish the following global error bound result,
which will be employed in the sequel:

Lemma 3

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex function continuous on
the Banach space
\mathbb{X}
.
Assume that
[\varphi>0]\neq\varnothing
and that

\tau_{\partial}=\inf_{x\in[\varphi>0]}{\rm dist}\left(\mathbf{0}^{*};\partial\varphi(x)\right)=\inf\{\|x^{*}\|\ :\ x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x),\quad x\in[\varphi>0]\}>0.

(19)

Then, it is
[\varphi\leq 0]\neq\varnothing
and

{\rm dist}\left(x;[\varphi\leq 0]\right)\leq{\varphi(x)\over\tau_{\partial}},\quad\forall x\in[\varphi>0].

Recall that a set-valued map between normed spaces
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}

is said to be convex if
{\rm gph}\,F
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
.

Lemma 4

If
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
is convex, then the function
(x,y)\mapsto{\rm dist}\left(y;F(x)\right)

is convex on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
.

For a finite-dimensional setting, its proof can be found e.g. in
(
Uder23
, Lemma 2.7)
.
Nonetheless, the arguments employed there can be reproduced without any change in a normed
space setting.

3.1
The dual regularity condition, local solvability and error bounds

Given any
\delta>0
and any pair
(p,x)\in P\times\mathbb{X}
, define by convenience
\nu_{A,Q}(p,x)={\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(p)\right)
,

\nu_{C}(p,x)={\rm dist}\left(x;C(p)\right)
,
d_{A,Q}={\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(p)\right)
and
d_{C}={\rm dist}\left(x;C(p)\right)
.
On the base of these notations, let us introduce the following quantities

\displaystyle\tau_{A,Q}(\delta)=\inf\bigg\{\|x^{*}\|:\

\displaystyle x^{*}\in A(p,\cdot)^{*}\left({\rm N}(A(p,x);{\rm B}\left[Q(p);d_{A,Q}\right]))\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{*}\right)

(20)

\displaystyle+[{\rm N}(x;{\rm B}\left[C(p);d_{C}\right])\cap{\mathbb{B}}^{*}],

\displaystyle(p,x)\in[\nu_{A,Q}>0]\cap\left[{\rm B}\left(\bar{p};\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(\bar{x};\delta\right)\right]\bigg\}

and

\displaystyle\tau_{C}(\delta)=\inf\bigg\{\|x^{*}\|:\

\displaystyle x^{*}\in A(p,\cdot)^{*}\left({\rm N}(A(p,x);Q(p))\cap{\mathbb{B}}^{*}\right)+[{\rm N}(x;{\rm B}\left[C(p);d_{C}\right])\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{*}],

(21)

\displaystyle(p,x)\in[\nu_{A,Q}=0]\cap[\nu_{C}>0]\cap\left[{\rm B}\left(\bar{p};\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(\bar{x};\delta\right)\right]\bigg\}.

Notice that in the formula (
20
), whenever
x\in C(p)
, so

,
then it results in
{\rm N}(x;{\rm B}\left[C(p);d_{C}\right])={\rm N}(x;C(p))

In what follows, some conditions for the quantitative semicontinuity
properties of
\Sigma
are established under a qualification of the
behaviour of a family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
near
(\bar{p},\bar{x})

in terms of the constructions introduced above. More precisely,

is said to satisfy the

(for short, DRC) near

\exists\delta>0:\ \tau(\delta)=\min\{\tau_{A,Q}(\delta),\,\tau_{C}(\delta)\}>0.

Theorem 3.1

With reference to a family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
, let
(\bar{p},\bar{x})\in{\rm gph}\,\Sigma
. Suppose that:

(i)

C:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}

(\bar{p},\bar{x})

(ii)

\bar{p}

Then, there exist

(t)

(tt)

the following estimate holds

{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(p)\right)\leq\tau(\delta)^{-1}\nu(p,x),\quad\forall(p,x)\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\zeta\right]\times{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta\right].

Proof

In the light of the scalar characterization of
\Sigma
provided by
Proposition
1
, both the assertions in the thesis
follow from Lemma
1
(the Basic Lemma).
Thus, it suffices to show that all the hypotheses of Lemma
1

are satisfied, upon the current set of assumptions.
To this aim, observe that assumption
(a_{0})
ensures the metric

(\mathbb{X},\|\cdot\|)
.
The fact that
(\bar{p},\bar{x})\in{\rm gph}\,\Sigma

\nu(\bar{p},\bar{x})=0

By virtue of hypotheses (i)-(iii), Lemma

allows to state that

is continuous at
\bar{p}
.
Moreover, as observed in Remark

is (Lipschitz) continuous, and hence, l.s.c. on
\mathbb{X}
,
for every

So, it remains to check the validity of the hypothesis (v) in Lemma

It is at this point that the condition
({\rm DRC})
comes into play.
Take an arbitrary
(p,x)\in[\nu>0]\cap\left[{\rm B}\left(\bar{p};\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(\bar{x};\delta\right)\right]

is as in the assumption (iv).

Consider first the case
{\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(p)\right)>0
.
Notice that, for every
p\in P

x\mapsto\nu(p,x)
is convex
and real-valued on
\mathbb{X}

By taking into account the subdifferential

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\nu|(p,x)

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(\mathbf{0}^{*};\partial\nu(p,\cdot)(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(\mathbf{0}^{*};\partial{\rm dist}\left(A(p,\cdot);Q(p)\right)(x)+\partial{\rm dist}\left(\cdot;C(p)\right)(x)\right).

17

\displaystyle\partial{\rm dist}\left(A(p,\cdot);Q(p)\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(p,\cdot)^{*}(\partial{\rm dist}\left(\cdot;Q(p)\right)(A(p,x)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(p,\cdot)^{*}\left({\rm N}(A(p,x);{\rm B}\left[Q(p);d_{A,Q}\right]))\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{*}\right).

On the other hand, if

(

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt(p,x)

Remark 4

Whenever
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
are Hilbert spaces (in particular,

formulation of
\tau_{A,Q}(\delta)

(

\partial{\rm dist}\left(\cdot;\Omega\right)(\bar{x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm N}(\bar{x};\Omega)\cap{\mathbb{B}}&\quad\hbox{ if }\bar{x}\in\Omega,\\
\\
\left\{\displaystyle{\bar{x}-\prod(\bar{x};\Omega)\over\|\bar{x}-\prod(\bar{x};\Omega)\|}\right\}&\quad\hbox{ if }\bar{x}\not\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\prod(\bar{x};\Omega)
denotes the (uniquely defined) metric
projection of
\bar{x}
onto
\Omega
(see
(

, Corollary 3.79)
).
This fact yields

\displaystyle\tau_{A,Q}(\delta)=\inf\bigg\{\|v\|:\

\displaystyle v\in A(p,\cdot)^{*}\left({A(p,x)-\prod(A(p,x);Q(p))\over\|A(p,x)-\prod(A(p,x);Q(p))\|}\right)+[{\rm N}(x;{\rm B}\left[C(p);d_{C}\right])\cap{\mathbb{B}}],

\displaystyle(p,x)\in[\nu_{A,Q}>0]\cap\left[{\rm B}\left(\bar{p};\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(\bar{x};\delta\right)\right]\bigg\}

and

\displaystyle\tau_{C}(\delta)=\inf\bigg\{\|v\|:\

\displaystyle v\in A(p,\cdot)^{*}\left({\rm N}(A(p,x);Q(p))\cap{\mathbb{B}}\right)+\left\{{x-\prod(x;C(p))\over\|x-\prod(x;C(p))\|}\right\},

\displaystyle(p,x)\in[\nu_{A,Q}=0]\cap[\nu_{C}>0]\cap\left[{\rm B}\left(\bar{p};\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(\bar{x};\delta\right)\right]\bigg\}.

In order to clarify the role of
({\rm DRC})
, some examples
are presented, which should discourage from its removal.
The first one aims at showing that the violation of the condition

({\rm DRC})
can yield dramatic effects already in terms of solution
existence.

Example 1

Let
P=[0,+\infty)
and
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Y}=\mathbb{R}
be equipped with their standard metric
and normed structure, respectively. Consider the family
({\rm SFP}_{p})

defined by the following data:

C(p)=(-\infty,-p],\qquad Q(p)=[p,+\infty),\qquad A(p,x)=\left(p+\frac{1}{2}\right)x,

and set
\bar{p}=\bar{x}=0
. Clearly, the solution map
\Sigma:[0,+\infty)\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}
associated with the family defined above is given by

\Sigma(p)=C(p)\cap A(\cdot,p)^{-1}(Q(p))=(-\infty,-p]\cap\left[\frac{p}{p+\frac{1}{2}},+\infty\right),\quad p\in[0,+\infty).

Therefore, one finds

\displaystyle\Sigma(p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{0\},&\qquad\hbox{\ if }p=0,\\
\\
\varnothing,&\qquad\hbox{\ if }p\in(0,+\infty).\\
\end{array}\right.

In other words, such an instance of
({\rm SFP}_{p})
fails to be solvable
if
p\neq\bar{p}=0
, no matter how close is
p
to
\bar{p}
.
Through the definition, one checks that
C
and
Q
are l.s.c. at
(0,0)

and at
(0,A(0,0))=(0,0)
, respectively.
The map
A(\cdot,0)
is continuous everywhere, as it is constant.
Let us check that the condition
({\rm DRC})
in this case is violated.
Fix an arbitrary
\delta>0
and choose
p_{\delta}\in(0,\min\{\delta,\frac{1}{2}\})

and
x_{\delta}
such that

-\delta<x_{\delta}=-p_{\delta}<0<\frac{p_{\delta}}{p_{\delta}+\frac{1}{2}}.

With the above choices, it is true that

(p_{\delta},x_{\delta})\in{\rm B}\left(0;\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(0;\delta\right)\hbox{ and }A(p_{\delta},x_{\delta})\not\in[p_{\delta},+\infty)=Q(p_{\delta}),

and hence
(p_{\delta},x_{\delta})\in[\nu_{A,Q}>0]\cap[{\rm B}\left(0;\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(0;\delta\right)]
.
Nevertheless, one has

{\rm N}(A(p_{\delta},x_{\delta});{\rm B}\left[Q(p_{\delta});d_{A,Q}\right])\cap{\mathbb{S}}=\{-1\},

so that

A(p_{\delta},\cdot)^{*}\left({\rm N}(A(p_{\delta},x_{\delta});{\rm B}\left[Q(p_{\delta});d_{A,Q}\right])\cap{\mathbb{S}}\right)=\left\{-\left(p_{\delta}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\}.

On the other hand, since
x_{\delta}\in{\rm bd}\,(-\infty,-p_{\delta}]
, one has

{\rm N}(x_{\delta};{\rm B}\left[C(p_{\delta});d_{C}\right])\cap{\mathbb{B}}={\rm N}(x_{\delta};C(p_{\delta}))\cap{\mathbb{B}}=[0,1].

Thus, for an element such as
(x_{\delta},p_{\delta})
one finds

\displaystyle A(p_{\delta},\cdot)^{*}\left({\rm N}(A(p_{\delta},x_{\delta});{\rm B}\left[Q(p_{\delta});d_{A,Q}\right])\cap{\mathbb{S}}\right)+[{\rm N}(x_{\delta};{\rm B}\left[C(p_{\delta});d_{C}\right])\cap{\mathbb{B}}]

\displaystyle=\left\{-\left(p_{\delta}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\}+[0,1]=\left[-\left(p_{\delta}+\frac{1}{2}\right),\frac{1}{2}-p_{\delta}\right].

Since
p_{\delta}<\frac{1}{2}
, one has
0\in\left[-\left(p_{\delta}+\frac{1}{2}\right),\frac{1}{2}-p_{\delta}\right]
,
what implies that
\tau(\delta)=\tau_{A,Q}=0
.
As the same happens for every
\delta>0
, the condition (
DRC
) is
violated.

The next example shows that, even if all the problems
({\rm SFP}_{p})
are solvable all around

\bar{p}
, yet in the absence of the condition
({\rm DRC})
the error bound estimate
in (
22
) may fail to hold true.

Example 2

Let
P
,
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be as in Example
1
.
Consider now the family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
defined by the following data:

C(p)=\mathbb{R},\qquad Q(p)=[p^{2},+\infty),\qquad A(p,x)=px,

and set again
\bar{p}=\bar{x}=0
. The related solution map
\Sigma

is given by

\displaystyle\Sigma(p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{R},&\qquad\hbox{\ if }p=0,\\
\\
\hbox{$[p,+\infty)$},&\qquad\hbox{\ if }p\in(0,+\infty).\end{array}\right.

Consequently,

{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(p)\right)=\max\{p-x,0\},\quad\forall(p,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R},

while

{\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(p)\right)=\max\{p^{2}-px,0\},\quad\forall(p,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.

In particular, once fixed arbitrarily positive
\eta,\,\zeta
and
\sigma
, if taking

p\in(0,\min\{\sigma^{-1},\,\zeta\})
, one finds

{\rm dist}\left(0;\Sigma(p)\right)=p>\sigma p^{2}=\sigma{\rm dist}\left(A(p,0);Q(p)\right),

which contradicts the assertion (tt) in Theorem
3.1
.
Notice that, whereas hypotheses (i)–(iii) of that theorem are fulfilled,
the condition
({\rm DRC})
is violated. To see this, fix an arbitrary
\delta>0

and consider the sequence of points
\{(1/n,x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
P\times\mathbb{X}=[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
,
with
x_{n}
such that

0<x_{n}<\frac{1}{n^{2}}<\frac{1}{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0,\,1\}.

For
n>1/\delta
, one has
(1/n,x_{n})\in{\rm B}\left(0;\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(0;\delta\right)

and
A(1/n,x_{n})\not\in Q(1/n)
, and hence
{\rm N}(A(1/n,\cdot);{\rm B}\left[Q(1/n);d_{A,Q}\right])\cap{\mathbb{S}}=\{-1\}
,
so that

A(1/n,\cdot)^{*}\left({\rm N}(A(1/n,\cdot);{\rm B}\left[Q(1/n);d_{A,Q}\right])\cap{\mathbb{S}}\right)=\left\{-\frac{1}{n}\right\},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0,\,1\},

while
{\rm N}(x_{n};{\rm B}\left[C(1/n);d_{C}\right])={\rm N}(x_{n};C(1/n))=\{0\}
, as
x_{n}\in{\rm int}\,C(1/n)
. Since
for this family of problems
[\nu_{C}>0]=\varnothing
, one has
\tau_{C}=+\infty
. Thus, one obtains

\tau(\delta)=\tau_{A,Q}(\delta)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|-\frac{1}{n}\right|=0.

In contrast to the preceding examples, the next one illustrates a simple case, to
which Theorem
3.1
can be successfully applied thanks to the fulfilment
of the condition
({\rm DRC})
.

Example 3

Let
P
,
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be as in Example
1
.
Consider the family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
defined by the following data:

C(p)=\mathbb{R},\qquad Q(p)=[p,+\infty),\qquad A(p,x)=(p+1)x,

and set again
\bar{p}=\bar{x}=0
. Associated to such a family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
,
one finds the following solution map:

\Sigma(p)=\left[\frac{p}{p+1},+\infty\right),\quad\forall p\in[0,+\infty).

It follows

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(p)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{p}{p+1}-x,\,0\right\}\leq\max\{p-(p+1)x,\,0\}

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{A,Q}(p,x)=\nu(p,x),\quad\forall(p,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.

Indeed, if
x<\frac{p}{p+1}
, as
p\in[0,+\infty)
, it is true that

\frac{p}{p+1}-x\leq p-(p+1)x.

The above inequality shows that the error bound estimate in (
22
)
holds with
\tau(\delta)=1
and arbitrary
\zeta
and
\eta
.
Let us check the fulfilment of the condition
({\rm DRC})
.
If
(p,x)
is an arbitrary element in
[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
, such that
\nu_{A,Q}(p,x)>0
, that is

x<\frac{p}{p+1}
, one has
{\rm N}(A(p,x);{\rm B}\left[Q(p);d_{A,Q}\right])\cap{\mathbb{S}}=\{-1\}
, and hence

A(p,\cdot)^{*}\left({\rm N}(A(p,x);{\rm B}\left[Q(p);d_{A,Q}\right])\cap{\mathbb{S}}\right)=\{-(p+1)\},

while
{\rm N}(x;C(p))\cap{\mathbb{B}}=\{0\}
, as
x\in{\rm int}\,C(p)
. Consequently,
fixed any
\delta>0
, one obtains

\tau_{A,Q}(\delta)=\inf\{|-(p+1)|:\ (p,x)\in[\nu_{A,Q}>0]\cap[{\rm B}\left(0;\delta\right)\times{\rm B}\left(0;\delta\right)]\}=1.

The fact that
[\nu_{C}>0]=\varnothing
so
\tau_{C}(\delta)=+\infty
, allows
one to conclude that
\tau(\delta)=1
.

In order to afford further insights into the role played by
({\rm DRC})
, it is relevant to
note that the error bound estimate in (
22
) can be achieved also
under an alternative qualification condition based on the concept of
subtransversality/metric subregularity.
Recall that two intersecting subsets, say
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
, of the same
metric space are said to be subtransversal at
x_{0}\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}

if there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\delta>0
such that

\alpha{\rm dist}\left(x;\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}\right)\leq\max\{{\rm dist}\left(x;\Omega_{1}\right),\,{\rm dist}\left(x;\Omega_{2}\right)\},\quad\forall x\in{\rm B}\left[x_{0};\delta\right].

A set-valued map between metric spaces
F:X\rightrightarrows Y
is said to be
subregular at
(x_{0},y_{0})\in{\rm gph}\,F
if there exists positive
\kappa
and
\delta

such that

{\rm dist}\left(x;F^{-1}(y_{0})\right)\leq\kappa{\rm dist}\left(y_{0};F(x)\right),\quad\forall x\in{\rm B}\left[x_{0};\delta\right].

Now, with reference to a family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
, notice that if, for any fixed
p\in P
,
the set-valued map
F_{A,Q}(p,\cdot):\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
, defined as

F_{A,Q}(p,x)=A(p,x)-Q(p),

is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\mathbf{0})
one has

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(x;A(p,\cdot)^{-1}(Q(p))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(x;[A(p,\cdot)-Q(p)]^{-1}(\mathbf{0})\right)={\rm dist}\left(x;F_{A,Q}(p,\cdot)^{-1}(\mathbf{0})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa{\rm dist}\left(\mathbf{0};F_{A,Q}(p,x)\right)=\kappa{\rm dist}\left(\mathbf{0};A(p,x)-Q(p)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa{\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(p)\right),\quad\forall x\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\delta\right].

Thus, if assuming that there exist positive constants
\zeta
and
\eta

such that both the following condition hold:

(i)

for every
p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\zeta\right]
, each pair of sets
C(p)
and

A(p,\cdot)^{-1}(Q(p))
do intersect in
{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta\right]
and
are subtransversal at
\bar{x}\in C(\bar{p})\cap A(\bar{p},\cdot)^{-1}(Q(\bar{p}))
,
with uniform subtransversality constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\delta>0
;

(ii)

for every
p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\zeta\right]
, each set-valued map

F_{A,Q}(p,\cdot)
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\mathbf{0})
, with a uniform
metric subregularity bound
\kappa>0
,

one finds

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(p)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(x;C(p)\cap[A(p,\cdot)^{-1}(Q(p))]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\max\{{\rm dist}\left(x;C(p)\right),\,{\rm dist}\left(x;A(p,\cdot)^{-1}(Q(p))\right)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\left[{\rm dist}\left(x;C(p)\right)+{\rm dist}\left(x;A(p,\cdot)^{-1}(Q(p))\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\left[{\rm dist}\left(x;C(p)\right)+\kappa{\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(p)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\max\{1,\,\kappa\}\nu(p,x),\quad\forall(p,x)\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\zeta\right]\times{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta\right].

Since a set-valued map
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
between normed
spaces is known to be metrically subregular at
(x_{0},y_{0})\in{\rm gph}\,F
iff
the pair
{\rm gph}\,F
and
\mathbb{X}\times\{y_{0}\}
is subtransversal at
(x_{0},y_{0})
,
one sees that the alternative qualification condition presented above can be expressed
purely in terms of subtransversality (for a thorough study of subtransversality
in the context of variational analysis, the reader can refer to
(
Ioff17
, Section 7.1)

and
KrLuTh17
).

3.2
A condition for Lipschitz lower semicontinuity

On the base of the elements discussed above, one is in a
position to establish the following sufficient condition for the first of the
properties appearing in Definition
1
.

Theorem 3.2
(Lipschitz lower semicontinuity of
\Sigma
)

With reference to a family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
, let
(\bar{p},\bar{x})\in{\rm gph}\,\Sigma
. Suppose that:

(i)

C:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
is Lipschitz l.s.c. at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
;

(ii)

Q:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
is Lipschitz l.s.c. at

\left(\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x})\right)
;

(iii)

A(\cdot,\bar{x}):P\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
is calm at
\bar{p}
;

(iv)

the condition
({\rm DRC})
holds.

Then,
\Sigma
is Lipschitz l.s.c. at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
and it holds

{\rm Liplsc}(\Sigma;\bar{p},\bar{x})\leq{{\rm Liplsc}(C;\bar{p},\bar{x})+{\rm clm}(A(\cdot,\bar{x});\bar{p})+{\rm Liplsc}(Q;\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x}))\over\tau(\delta)}.

(29)

Proof

Let us start with observing that, since Lipschitz lower
semicontinuity implies mere lower semicontinuity for any set-valued
mapping (recall Remark
2
(i)), as well as calmness
for a single-valued mapping implies continuity (recall Remark
2
(iv)),
the assumption set of Theorem
3.2
enables one to apply Theorem

3.1
, according to which there exist positive
constants
\zeta
and
\eta
such that the estimate in (
22
)
is true. The latter, in particular, implies

{\rm dist}\left(\bar{x};\Sigma(p)\right)\leq\tau(\delta)^{-1}\nu(p,\bar{x}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\zeta\right].

(30)

Take
\ell_{C}>\tilde{\ell_{C}}>{\rm Liplsc}(C;\bar{p},\bar{x})
. According to hypothesis (i)
there exists
r_{C}>0
such that

C(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\tilde{\ell_{C}}d(p,\bar{p})\right]\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{C}\right].

(31)

Take
\ell_{Q}>\tilde{\ell_{Q}}>{\rm Liplsc}(Q;\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x}))
. Owing to hypothesis (ii)
there exists
r_{Q}>0
such that

Q(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[A(\bar{p},\bar{x});\tilde{\ell_{Q}}d(p,\bar{p})\right]\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{Q}\right].

(32)

Take
\gamma_{A}>{\rm clm}(A(\cdot,\bar{x});\bar{p})
. According to hypothesis (iii)
there exists
r_{A}>0
such that

\|A(p,\bar{x})-A(\bar{p},\bar{x})\|\leq\gamma_{A}d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{A}\right].

(33)

Now, define
r_{\Sigma}=\min\{\zeta,\,r_{C},\,r_{Q},\,r_{A}\}
.
On account of the nonemptiness in (
31
), one has

{\rm dist}\left(\bar{x};C(p)\right)\leq\tilde{\ell_{C}}d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{\Sigma}\right].

(34)

On account of the nonemptiness in (
32
) and of inequality
(
33
), one has

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(A(p,\bar{x});Q(p)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A(p,\bar{x})-A(\bar{p},\bar{x})\|+{\rm dist}\left(A(\bar{p},\bar{x});Q(p)\right)

(35)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\gamma_{A}+\tilde{\ell_{Q}})d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{\Sigma}\right].

Thus, by combining the inequalities (
30
), (
34
)
and (
35
), one obtains

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(\bar{x};\Sigma(p)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\tilde{\ell_{C}}+\gamma_{A}+\tilde{\ell_{Q}}\over\tau(\delta)}d(p,\bar{p})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle{\ell_{C}+\gamma_{A}+\ell_{Q}\over\tau(\delta)}d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{\Sigma}\right]\backslash\{\bar{p}\}.

The last inequality means that

\Sigma(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\frac{\ell_{C}+\gamma_{A}+\ell_{Q}}{\tau(\delta)}d(p,\bar{p})\right]\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{\Sigma}\right].

This amounts to say that
\Sigma
is Lipschitz l.s.c. at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})

with

{\rm Liplsc}(\Sigma;\bar{p},\bar{x})\leq\frac{\ell_{C}+\gamma_{A}+\ell_{Q}}{\tau(\delta)}.

Since the above inequality holds true for every
\ell_{C}>{\rm Liplsc}(C;\bar{p},\bar{x})
, for
every
\gamma_{A}>{\rm clm}(A(\cdot,\bar{x});\bar{p})
and for every
\ell_{Q}>{\rm Liplsc}(Q;\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x}))
,
from it one can derive the estimate (
29
) in the thesis.
This completes the proof.
∎∎

Remark 5

With respect to Theorem
3.1
, the gain obtained thanks to Theorem
3.2

consists in ensuring the existence of a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of solutions to approximated problems
which converges to the solution
\bar{x}
linearly with respect to the perturbation
parameter, with a linear convergence rate estimated as
in (
29
). Of course, the price to be paid for such an improvement
is a stricter set of assumptions, replacing mere semicontinuity properties of
the data with their quantitative counterparts.

3.3
A condition for calmness

As Lipschitz lower semicontinuity and calmness are, in general,
independent of each other, a condition devoted to the latter property
is worth being established too.

Theorem 3.3
(Calmness of
\Sigma
)

With reference to a class
({\rm SFP}_{p})
, let
(\bar{p},\bar{x})\in{\rm gph}\,\Sigma
.
Suppose that:

(i)

C:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
is l.s.c. and calm at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
;

(ii)

Q:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
is l.s.c. and calm at

\left(\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x})\right)
;

(iii)

A:P\times\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
is Lipschitz around
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
;

(iv)

the condition
({\rm DRC})
holds.

Then,
\Sigma
is calm at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
and it holds

{\rm clm}(\Sigma;\bar{p},\bar{x})\leq{{\rm clm}(C;\bar{p},\bar{x})+{\rm lip}(A;(\bar{p},\bar{x}))+{\rm clm}(Q;\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x}))\over\tau(\delta)}.

(36)

Proof

Under the above set of assumptions it is possible to apply Theorem
3.1
.
Thus, one gets the existence of
\zeta,\,\eta>0
for which the estimate in (
22
)
holds.
Take
\gamma_{C}>{\rm clm}(C;\bar{p},\bar{x})
.
According to hypothesis (i), there exist positive
r_{C}
and
\eta_{C}
such that

C(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta_{C}\right]\subseteq{\rm B}\left[C(\bar{p});\gamma_{C}d(p,\bar{p})\right],\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{C}\right].

(37)

Take
\gamma_{Q}>{\rm clm}(Q;\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x}))
.
According to hypothesis (ii), there exist positive
r_{Q}
and
\eta_{Q}
such that

Q(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[A(\bar{p},\bar{x});\eta_{Q}\right]\subseteq{\rm B}\left[Q(\bar{p});\gamma_{Q}d(p,\bar{p})\right],\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{Q}\right].

(38)

Take
\ell_{A}>{\rm lip}(A;(\bar{p},\bar{x}))
. Then, by hypothesis (iii) there exists

r_{A}>0
such that

\|A(p,x)-A(\bar{p},\bar{x})\|\leq\ell_{A}\left[d(p,\bar{p})+\|x-\bar{x}\|\right],\quad\forall(p,x)\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{A}\right]\times{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};r_{A}\right].

(39)

Define

\eta_{\Sigma}=\min\{\eta,\,\eta_{C},\,\eta_{Q}\}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad r_{\Sigma}=\min\{\zeta,\,r_{C},\,r_{Q},\,r_{A},\,(2\ell_{A})^{-1}\eta_{Q}\}.

Now, take an arbitrary
x\in\Sigma(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta_{\Sigma}\right]
.
Since, in particular,
x\in C(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta_{C}\right]
, then, on account of
inclusion (
37
), one has

{\rm dist}\left(x;C(\bar{p})\right)\leq\gamma_{C}d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{\Sigma}\right].

Moreover, as
x\in\Sigma(p)
, it must be
A(p,x)\in Q(p)
.
From inequality (
39
), provided that
(p,x)\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{\Sigma}\right]\times{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};r_{\Sigma}\right]
, it is possible to deduce

\|A(p,x)-A(\bar{p},\bar{x})\|\leq\ell_{A}\cdot 2r_{\Sigma}\leq\eta_{Q},

so it is true that
A(p,x)\in Q(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[A(\bar{p},\bar{x});\eta_{Q}\right]
.
Consequently, by inequality (
38
) one finds

{\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(\bar{p})\right)\leq\gamma_{Q}d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{\Sigma}\right].

Thus, for every
x\in\Sigma(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta_{\Sigma}\right]
,
by recalling the estimate in (
22
) one obtains

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(\bar{p})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau(\delta)^{-1}\nu(x,\bar{p})=\tau(\delta)^{-1}\left[{\rm dist}\left(A(\bar{p},x);Q(\bar{p})\right)+{\rm dist}\left(x;C(\bar{p})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau(\delta)^{-1}\left[\|A(\bar{p},x)-A(p,x)\|+{\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(\bar{p})\right)+{\rm dist}\left(x;C(\bar{p})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau(\delta)^{-1}\left(\ell_{A}+\gamma_{Q}+\gamma_{C}\right)d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};r_{\Sigma}\right].

By arbitrariness of
x\in\Sigma(p)\cap{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta_{\Sigma}\right]

∎∎

which means that for every
x\in\Sigma(p)\subseteq C(p)

{\rm dist}\left(x;C(\bar{p})\right)\leq\ell_{C}d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{C}\right].

(54)

Analogously, by hypothesis (ii), fixed any
\ell_{Q}>{\rm Lipusc}(Q;\bar{p})
, there exists
\delta_{Q}>0

such that

Q(p)\subseteq{\rm B}\left[Q(\bar{p});\ell_{Q}d(p,\bar{p})\right],\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{Q}\right],

which means that for every
y\in Q(p)

{\rm dist}\left(y;Q(\bar{p})\right)\leq\ell_{Q}d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{Q}\right].

(55)

By hypothesis (iii), fixed any
\gamma_{A}>{\rm clm}(A(\cdot,x);\bar{p})
, there exists
\delta_{A}>0

such that

\|A(p,x)-A(\bar{p},x)\|\leq\gamma_{A}d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{A}\right],\ \forall x\in\mathbb{X}.

(56)

Now, define
\delta_{\Sigma}=\min\{\delta_{C},\,\delta_{Q},\,\delta_{A}\}
.
From (
55
) and (
56
), whenever
x\in\Sigma(p)
, so that
A(p,x)\in Q(p)
,
it follows

{\rm dist}\left(A(\bar{p},x);Q(\bar{p})\right)\leq\|A(\bar{p},x)-A(p,x)\|+{\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(\bar{p})\right)\leq(\ell_{A}+\ell_{Q})d(p,\bar{p}).

Thus, by recalling the inequalities in (
54
) and (
53
),
one obtains that if
x\in\Sigma(p)
, then it holds

\displaystyle{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(\bar{p})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm dist}\left(A(\bar{p},x);Q(\bar{p})\right)+{\rm dist}\left(x;C(\bar{p})\right)}{\tau(\bar{p})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\gamma_{A}+\ell_{Q}+\ell_{C}\right)}{\tau(\bar{p})}d(p,\bar{p}),\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{\Sigma}\right].

By arbitrariness of
x\in\Sigma(p)
, the above inequality results in

\Sigma(p)\subseteq{\rm B}\left[\Sigma(\bar{p});\frac{\left(\gamma_{A}+\ell_{Q}+\ell_{C}\right)}{\tau(\bar{p})}d(p,\bar{p})\right],\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\delta_{\Sigma}\right],

which shows that
\Sigma
is Lipschitz u.s.c. at
\bar{p}
with

{\rm Lipusc}(\Sigma;\bar{p})\leq\frac{\left(\gamma_{A}+\ell_{Q}+\ell_{C}\right)}{\tau(\bar{p})}.

From such inequality, by reasoning as already made in estimating the bound
associated to a quantitative semicontinuity property, one can deduce the
inequality in (
52
), thereby completing the proof.
∎∎

4
Some special results with perturbations in a normed space

Throughout the current section, the metric space
P

is assumed to have, in particular, the structure of a normed vector space.
To mark the difference of such a specific setting,
(P,d)
will be henceforth denoted
by
(\mathbb{P},\|\cdot\|)
. Of course, such a specialization results in allowing
less general perturbations.
The next proposition states that the solution map associated to a
family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
with data depending “convexly” from the perturbation
parameter, inherits the convex dependence.
This result extends to parameterized families of “split” feasibility problems
a phenomenon , which has been already observed for some of their
specific manifestations.

Proposition 3
(Convexity of
\Sigma
)

With reference to a family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
, with
p\in\mathbb{P}
, suppose that:

(i)

C:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
is convex on
\mathbb{P}
;

(ii)

Q:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
is convex on
\mathbb{P}
;

(iii)

A\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
.

Then, the multifunction
\Sigma:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
is convex on
\mathbb{P}
.

Proof

Take arbitrary
p_{1},\,p_{2}\in{\rm dom}\,\Sigma
,
x_{1}\in\Sigma(p_{1})
,
x_{2}\in\Sigma(p_{2})
,
and
t\in[0,1]
. This means that
x_{1}\in C(p_{1})\cap A(p_{1},\cdot)^{-1}(Q(p_{1}))

and
x_{2}\in C(p_{2})\cap A(p_{2},\cdot)^{-1}(Q(p_{2}))
. Since
C
is convex, from the
above inclusions it follows

tx_{1}+(1-t)x_{2}\in tC(p_{1})+(1-t)C(p_{2})\subseteq C(tp_{1}+(1-t)p_{2}).

(57)

Moreover, by the joint linearity of
A
and the convexity of
Q
, one has

\displaystyle A(tp_{1}+(1-t)p_{2},tx_{1}+(1-t)x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tA(p_{1},x_{1})+(1-t)A(p_{2},x_{2})

(58)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle tQ(p_{1})+(1-t)Q(p_{2})\subseteq Q(tp_{1}+(1-t)p_{2}).

The inclusions in (
57
) and (
58
) result
in

\displaystyle tx_{1}+(1-t)x_{2}\in C(tp_{1}+(1-t)p_{2})\cap\ A(tp_{1}+(1-t)p_{2},\cdot)^{-1}\left(Q(tp_{1}+(1-t)p_{2})\right),

which entails
tx_{1}+(1-t)x_{2}\in\Sigma(tp_{1}+(1-t)p_{2})
. By arbitrariness
of
x_{1}\in\Sigma(p_{1})
and
x_{2}\in\Sigma(p_{2})
, the last inclusion enables to
conclude that

t\Sigma(p_{1})+(1-t)\Sigma(p_{2})\subseteq\Sigma(tp_{1}+(1-t)p_{2}),\quad\forall p_{1},\,p_{2}\in{\rm dom}\,\Sigma.

If at least one among
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
does not belong to
{\rm dom}\,\Sigma
,
the last inclusion becomes trivially true, according to the conventions

t\cdot\varnothing=\varnothing
and
\varnothing+\Omega=\varnothing
.
∎∎

Elementary counterexamples show that, if one of the hypotheses of
Proposition
3
is dropped out, then
\Sigma
may fail to be convex.
As it happens for scalar functions, the combination of convexity and
continuity properties yields Lipschitzian behaviour of multifunction,
provided that a proper qualification conditions is in force.

Theorem 4.1
(Aubin property of
\Sigma
under convexity)

Given a family
({\rm SFP}_{p})
and
\bar{p}\in\mathbb{P}
, let
(\bar{p},\bar{x})\in{\rm gph}\,\Sigma
.
Suppose that:

(i)

C:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
is convex and i.s.c. at
\bar{p}
;

(ii)

Q:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
is convex and i.s.c. at
\bar{p}
;

(iii)

A\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
;

(iv)

the condition
({\rm DRC})
holds.

Then,
\Sigma:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}
has the Aubin property around
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
.

Proof

Observe that, as noticed in Remark
2
(ii), since

C
is i.s.c. at
\bar{p}
, then it is also l.s.c. at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})\in{\rm gph}\,C
.
Analogously, since
Q
is i.s.c. at
\bar{p}
, then it is also l.s.c.
at
(\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x}))\in{\rm gph}\,Q
.
The hypothesis (iii) entails that
A(\cdot,\bar{x})
is continuous at
\bar{p}
.
Thus, under the condition
({\rm DRC})
, it is possible to apply Theorem
3.1
,
which ensures that, for some positive
\zeta
and
\eta
,

\Sigma(p)\neq\varnothing,\quad\forall p\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\zeta\right],

(59)

and the error bound estimate in (
22
) holds.
Notice that (
59
) implies that the function
(p,x)\mapsto{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(p)\right)

is real-valued around
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
. Moreover, by the convexity of
C
and
Q
,
being
A\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
,
according to Proposition
3
, such function is convex on
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{X}
.
On the other hand, by recalling Remark
2
(ii), since
C
is i.s.c.
at
\bar{p}
, one can say that the function
(p,x)\mapsto{\rm dist}\left(x;C(p)\right)
is
u.s.c. at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
.
Similarly, since
Q
is i.s.c. at
\bar{p}
, one can say that the function
(p,y)\mapsto{\rm dist}\left(y;Q(p)\right)
is u.s.c. at
(\bar{p},y)
, for every
y\in Q(\bar{p})
. In turn,
by continuity of
A
, this implies that the function
(p,x)\mapsto{\rm dist}\left(A(p,x);Q(p)\right)

is u.s.c. at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
. Consequently, as a sum of two functions u.s.c. at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
,

\nu
is u.s.c. at the same point.
Thus, as it is

{\rm dist}\left(\bar{x};\Sigma(\bar{p})\right)=0\leq{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(p)\right)\leq\tau(\delta)^{-1}\nu(p,x),\quad\forall(p,x)\in{\rm B}\left[\bar{p};\zeta\right]\times{\rm B}\left[\bar{x};\eta\right],

the function
(p,x)\mapsto{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(p)\right)
turns out to be continuous
at the point
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
interior to its domain.
By convexity, this fact is known to imply the Lipschitz continuity of that
function around
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
(see, for instance,
(
MorNam22
, Theorem 2.149)
).
In the light of the scalarization criterion formulated in
Proposition
2
, what has been obtained
is sufficient for
\Sigma
to have the Aubin property around
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
.
This completes the proof.
∎∎

Remark 6

(i) Whenever
(\mathbb{P},\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach space, a shortcut for
the proof of Theorem
4.1
can be devised, which relies on the
Robinson-Ursescu Theorem (see
(
DonRoc14
, Theorem 5B.4)
).
Indeed, the nonemptiness in (
59
) says that
\bar{p}\in{\rm int}\,{\rm dom}\,\Sigma={\rm int}\,\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbb{X})
, namely
\bar{p}
is
an interior point of the range of
\Sigma^{-1}
. In consideration of the convexity
of
\Sigma
, and hence of
\Sigma^{-1}
, by the Robinson-Ursescu Theorem this fact is equivalent to
the metric regularity of inverse multifunction
\Sigma^{-1}
around
(\bar{x},\bar{p})
,
which is known to be an equivalent reformulation of the Aubin property of
\Sigma

around
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
.

(ii) Whenever
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{X}
are finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces,
the hypotheses (i) and (ii) in Theorem
4.1
can be
weakened, by replacing the inner semicontinuity of
C
and
Q
with their mere
lower semicontinuity at
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
and
(\bar{p},A(\bar{p},\bar{x}))
,
respectively. Indeed, such weaker assumptions still enable to apply
Theorem
3.1
, so as to get that
(\bar{p},\bar{x})
belongs
to the interior of the domain of the function
(p,x)\mapsto{\rm dist}\left(x;\Sigma(p)\right)
.
As convex functions are locally Lipschitz on the interior of finite-dimensional
domains (see
(
MorNam22
, Corollary 2.152)
), in such a circumstance one can
directly apply Proposition
2
.

5
Conclusions

The findings emerging from the exploration of well-known Lipschitzian properties
for those maps implicitly defined by a parameterized family
of “split” feasibility problems tell us that the validity of each of these
properties for the problem data multifunctions can be inherited by the solution
map, provided that an adequate qualification condition is assumed to hold.
According to the proposed approach, such a qualification condition can
be read as a subdifferential slope condition, tying the behaviour
of all the problem data. In fact, it yields local solvability and
metric estimates, as if stemming from subtransversality assumptions.
The findings of the present study can serve as a starting point for investigating
further aspects of the perturbation analysis of “split” feasibility problems
and as a solid base for elaborating approximated methods for their resolution.
