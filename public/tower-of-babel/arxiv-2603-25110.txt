On a generalization of Shmel’kin’s theorem This work was supported by the Russian Science Foundation, project no. 22-11-00075
Mikhail A. Mikheenko

Abstract
It is known that every nilpotent group contains solution of every finite unimodular system of equatiuons over itself. This statement, however, is not true for infinite systems. Moreover, there are abelian groups which disprove the infinite system analogue of the statement. It has already been researched which periodic abelian groups contain solutions of all infinite unimodular systems of equations over themselves. The present article covers the same question for periodic nilpotent groups and for torsion-free nilpotent groups.

Introduction

Let us adopt the following convention on commutators:

[g,h]=g^{-1}h^{-1}gh
.

\mathfrak{Z}_{p}

This article is devoted to solvability of equations over groups.

Definition
.

Let
G
be a group.
An equation in variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}

over
G
is an expression (which may have coefficients from
G
)

w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=1
, where
w
is an element of the free product
G*F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
and
F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a free group with basis
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.

An equation
w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=1
is
solvable in the group

\widetilde{G}
if
G\subset\widetilde{G}

and
\widetilde{G}
contains a solution to said equation (i.e. there are elements

\widetilde{g}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{g}_{n}\in\widetilde{G}

).
This is the same as having a homomorphism
G*F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to\widetilde{G}
,
which is injective on

to

.
In this case the group
\widetilde{G}
is called the

.

If an equation

is solvable in some group
\widetilde{G}
,
the equation

G
is called

.

Solvability of a (finite or infinite) system of equations
(possibly in an infinite set of variables) is defined likewise.

Definition
.

Let
\{w_{i}=1\}_{i\in I}
be a system of equations
in a set of variables
\{x_{j}\}_{k\in J}
over a group
G

For each equation

Any nonsingular system of equations over a finite group

Theorem

Any nonsingular system of equations over a locally indicable

The last theorem has also an analogue for prime numbers.
Recall that a

Theorem

(

Let

Is it still remains unknown if there is a nonsingular system of equations over
some group which is not solvable.
Howie conjecture
[
10
]
, which states
that any nonsingular system of equations over any group is solvable,
still has yet to be either proved or disproved.

Apart from searching solutions of a system of equations in
any overgroup, one can try to find solutions in groups
similar to the initial group (in the best case, to find a solution
in the initial group itself).
The present paper is concerned with finding solutions of
nonsingular systems of equations in nilpotent groups.

The question is well-researched in the case of finite systems.

Recall that for a set of (not necessarily all) prime numbers
\pi
a group
G
is called

•

\pi
-divisible
if for each prime
p\in\pi
and each element
g\in G

there is an element
h\in G
such that
h^{p}=g
;
in particular any group is
\varnothing
-divisible;

•

\pi
-torsion free
if for each prime
p\in\pi

the group
G
has no elements of order
p
;
in particular, any group is a
\varnothing
-torsion free group.

Note that a divisible group is a
\Pi
-divisible groups,
while a torsion-free group is a
\Pi
-torsion free group,
where
\Pi
is the set of all prime numbers.

There is a following theorem.

Shmel’kin’s theorem

(
[
26
]
)
.

Suppose that
G
is a
\pi
-divisible
locally nilpotent

\pi
-torsion free
group.

Then every finite

\pi^{\prime}
-nonsingular
system of equations over

G
is solvable in
G
.
Moreover, if there are as many equations in the system
as there are variables, then the solution is unique.

Particularly, a divisible torsion-free nilpotent group contains
a solution of any finite nonsingular system of equations.
For finite unimodular systems the situation is even better,
as every nilpotent group contains a solution of any finite
unimodular system of equations over itself.

The last statement is not true for infinite systems:
abelian groups

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{3}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{5}\oplus\ldots,

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{4}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{8}\oplus\ldots

fail to have solutions to some infinite unimodular systems over themselves.
In
[
22
]
it was studied which periodic abelian groups
contain solutions of all infinite unimodular systems of equations over themselves.

Theorem

(
[
22
]
)
.

Suppose that
A
is a periodic abelian group.
Then
A
contains a solution of any unimoldular
system of equations over itself
if and only if
the reduced part of
A
has bounded period.

Some positive results for nilpotent groups,
partially extending Shmel’kin’s theorem to
infinite systems,
were also achieved.

Theorem

(
[
22
]
)
.

Suppose that
G
is a nilpotent group of bounded period.
Then any (not necessarily finite) unimodular system
of equations over
G
is solvable in
G
itself.

Theorem

(
[
22
]
)
.

Suppose that
G
is a divisible nilpotent group.
Then any (not necessarily finite) nonsingular
system of equations over
G

has a solution in
G
itself.

However, the question remained unanswered for
torsion-free abelian groups,
as well as it remained unclear how to
extend the criterion from abelian groups
to nilpotent ones.

The following results provide answers to these questions.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
G
is a periodic nilpotent group.

Then
G
contains a solution of any unimodular system of
equations over itself
if and only if
only a finite number of
p
-components of

G
has non-trivial first Ulm factor and
all these factors have bounded period.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
G
is a torsion-free nilpotent group.

Then
G
contains a solution of any unimodular system
over itself
if and only if

G
is divisible.

In Section
2
we prove some auxiliary statements
on the connection between divisible subgroups of a nilpotent group
G

G/Z(G)

In Section

In Section

In Section

2
Divisible subgroups of nilpotent groups

Recall that the upper central series of a group
G

is a normal series

Z_{0}(G)\triangleleft Z_{1}(G)\triangleleft Z_{2}(G)\triangleleft Z_{3}(G)\triangleleft\ldots

where
Z_{0}(G)=\{1\}
and

Z_{i}(G)
is a set of elements
h\in G

such that
\left[h,g\right]\in Z_{i-1}(G)
for any
g\in G
.
In other words,
Z_{i}(G)\subset G
is a preimage of the center of the quotient group

G/Z_{i-1}(G)
under taking the quotient
G\to G/Z_{i-1}(G)
.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that
G
is a nilpotent group such that

G/Z(G)
contains a nontrivial
divisible subgroup.
Then
G
contains a nontrivial divisible subgroup as well.

Proof.

Denote the nilpotency class of
G
by
s
.
If
G/Z(G)
contains a nontrivial divisible subgroup,
then consider the preimage
H\subset G
of this divisible
subgroup under taking the quotient
G\to G/Z(G)
.
The quotient group

H\Bigl/\bigl(Z(G)\cap H\bigr)
is divisible.
Suppose that

Z_{k}(G)\not\supset H\subset Z_{k+1}(G).

Then

[G,H]\not\subset Z_{k-1}(G),[G,[G,H]]\not\subset Z_{k-2}(G)

and so on.
In particular, we have

w:=[g_{1},[g_{2},\ldots,[g_{k},h]\ldots]]\in Z(G)\setminus\{1\}

for some
g_{i}\in G,h\in H
.
Let
n
be a natural number.
Since
H\Bigl/\bigl(Z(G)\cap H\bigr)
is divisible, we have

h=r^{n}z
, where
r\in H,z\in Z(G)

and

[g_{k},h]=[g_{k},r^{n}z]=[g_{k},r^{n}]

as
z
is a central element.
Using the commutator indentity

[a,bc]=[a,c][a,b][[a,b],c],

we get

[g_{k},r^{n}]=[g_{k},r^{n-1}][g_{k},r][[g_{k},r],r^{n-1}].

Since
H\subset Z_{k+1}(G)
, we get

[[g_{k},r],r^{n-1}]\in Z_{k-1}(G),

therefore

[g_{k},r^{n}]=[g_{k},r^{n-1}][g_{k},r]\mod{Z_{k-1}(G)}.

After several iterations we arrive to

[g_{k},r^{n}]=[g_{k},r]^{n}\mod{Z_{k-1}(G)}.

Now consider
[g_{k-1},[g_{k},r^{n}]]
.
As

[g_{k},r^{n}]=[g_{k},r]^{n}\mod{Z_{k-1}(G)},

we have

[g_{k-1},[g_{k},r^{n}]]=[g_{k-1},[g_{k},r]^{n}]\mod{Z_{k-2}(G)}.

Also, similarly to written above,

[g_{k-1},[g_{k},r]^{n}]=[g_{k-1},[g_{k},r]]^{n}\mod{Z_{k-2}(G)}.

Thus,

[g_{k-1},[g_{k},r^{n}]]=[g_{k-1},[g_{k},r]]^{n}\mod{Z_{k-2}(G)}.

Likewise we get

[g_{i},\ldots,[g_{k},r^{n}]\ldots]=[g_{i},\ldots,[g_{k},r]\ldots]^{n}\mod{Z_{i-1}(G)},

and particularly,

w=[g_{1},[g_{2},\ldots,[g_{k},r^{n}]\ldots]]=[g_{1},[g_{2},\ldots,[g_{k},r]\ldots]]^{n}\mod{Z_{0}(G)}=\{1\}.

In other words,
we can take a root of
w
of any natural power.
Moreover, since
r\in H
, we can take
roots of
[g_{1},[g_{2},\ldots,[g_{k},r]\ldots]]

of any power as well.
So we have

w=w_{1}=w_{2}^{2},\quad w_{2}=w_{3}^{3},\quad w_{3}=w_{4}^{4}=\ldots,

where
w_{i}\in G
, and these elements generate a divisible subgroup of
G
.

∎

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
G
is a reduced nilpotent group.
Then
G/Z(G)
is reduced as well.

Proof.

If
G/Z(G)
contains a nontrivial divisible subgroup,
then
G
contains a nontrivial divisible subgroup as well,
which is forbidden by hypothesis.

∎

3
Periodic nilpotent groups

In this section we consider nilpotent
p
-groups.
Recall the definition of the height of an element
in such group.

Definition
.

Let
G
be a nilpotent
p
-group.
The
height
of an element
g\in G
is
the greatest number
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

g=h^{p^{n}}
for some
h\in G
.
If there is no greatest number among these, it is said that

g
has
infinite height
.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose that
G
is a nilpotent
p
-group.
Then the set
G_{0}
of elements of infinite height is
a normal subgroup such that
the quotient group
G/G_{0}
has no elements of infinite height.

Proof.

The normality of the subset
G_{0}
is obvious.
Now let
g,h\in G_{0},k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Denote the nilpotency class of
G
by
s
.
Then
g=r^{p^{k\cdot s}},h=t^{p^{k\cdot s}}
.
Therefore
gh=r^{p^{k\cdot s}}t^{p^{k\cdot s}}
.
Since for a nilpotent group
G
of class
s

it is true that

\left\langle G^{n^{s}}\right\rangle\subset G^{n}
,
we get that
gh\in G^{p^{k}}
for any
k
.
So a product of two elements of
G_{0}

has an infinite height as well, so

G_{0}
is a subgroup (as an inverse of an element
of infinite height has an infinite height as well).

Now suppose that
\bar{a}\in G/G_{0}
is a nontrivial element of infinite height
in
G/G_{0}
.
Consider an element
a
of the preimage of
\bar{a}
under
taking the quotient
G\to G/G_{0}
.
For every
k
there is an element
b\in G

such that
b^{p^{k\cdot s}}=ac
, where
c\in G_{0}
.
For
c
there is an element
d\in G
such that

d^{p^{k\cdot s}}=c^{-1}
.
Hence
a=b^{p^{k\cdot s}}d^{p^{k\cdot s}}
.
Again, we get that

a\in\left\langle G^{p^{k\cdot s}}\right\rangle\subset G^{p^{k}}

for any
k
, therefore
a\in G_{0}
.
We arrive at a contradiction with the non-triviality of
\bar{a}
.
That is why there are no nontrivial
elements of infinite height in
G/G_{0}
.

∎

Definition
.

The quotient group
G/G_{0}

is called the
first Ulm factor

of
G
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
G
is a nilpotent
p
-group with
the first Ulm factor of bounded period.
Then
G_{0}
is divisible.

Proof.

Since the order of any element of
G_{0}
is a power of
the prime number
p
,
it suffices to prove that for each
g\in G_{0}

there is
t\in G_{0}
such that
t^{p}=g
.

Let
g\in G_{0}
.
Suppose that the period of
G/G_{0}
is
p^{l}
.
Then take an element
r\in G
,
such that
r^{p^{l+1}}=g
. As
r^{p^{l}}\in G_{0}
,
we conclude that for any element
g\in G_{0}

there is an element
t\in G_{0}
such that

t^{p}=g
, as needed.

∎

Lemma 3
.

Suppose that
G
is a reduced nilpotent

p
-group of unbounded period.
Then there is a unimodular system of equations over
G

which has no solutions in
G
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the nilpotency class of
G
.

The base of induction (i.e. the case when
G
is abelian) is proved in
[
22
]
.

Suppose that the statement of the lemma is proved
for nilpotent groups of class
s-1
.
Now we prove the statement for nilpotent groups
of class
s
.
Note that the quotient group
G/G_{0}

is a nilpotent
p
-group with no elements
of infinite height which has unbounded period by Lemma

2
, since
G_{0}
is not divisible unless
G_{0}

is trivial.
Hence
G
can be assumed to have no elements of infinite height.

Now consider
G/Z(G)
. By Lemma

1
, it is a reduced group.
If it has unbounded period, then by induction
there is a unimodular system of equations over
G/Z(G)

which has no solutions in
G/Z(G)
.
Therefore, there is an analogous system of equations over
G
as well.

Suppose, on the contrary, that
G/Z(G)
has a bounded period
p^{m}
.
Then
Z(G)
has unbounded period.
So
Z(G)
is an abelian
p
-group of unbounded period with no
elements of infinite height.
Thus, it is separable, i.e.
for any
r\in\mathbb{N}

there is a cyclic direct multiplier of
Z(G)
:

Z(G)=\langle a\rangle_{\widetilde{r}}\times B,

such that
\widetilde{r}>r
.
Hence there are following decompositions:

Z(G)=\langle a_{i}\rangle_{k_{i}}\times B_{i},

such that
k_{i+1}-k_{i}>k_{i}
, i.e.
k_{i+1}>2k_{i}
,
and
k_{1}>m
.

Consider then the following system of equations:

\begin{cases}x_{1}x_{2}^{-p^{k_{1}}}&=a_{1},\\
x_{2}x_{3}^{-p^{k_{2}-k_{1}}}&=a_{2},\\
x_{3}x_{4}^{-p^{k_{3}-k_{2}}}&=a_{3},\\
&\vdots\end{cases}.

Note that this system is unimodular, since the matrix of exponent sums of variables
in equations is as follows.

\begin{pmatrix}1&-p^{k_{1}}&0&0&\ldots\\
0&1&-p^{k_{2}-k_{1}}&0&\ldots\\
0&0&1&-p^{k_{3}-k_{2}}&\ldots\\
0&0&0&1&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}

Suppose that the group
G
contains a solution
\{y_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of the system above.
In that case the elements
y_{2}^{-p^{k_{1}}},y_{3}^{p^{k_{2}-k_{1}}},y_{4}^{-p^{k_{3}-k_{2}}}
, and so on,
lie in the center of
G
, therefore, in particular, we get that

y_{1}\cdot y_{n+1}^{-p^{k_{n}}}=a_{1}\cdot a_{2}^{p^{k_{1}}}\cdot\ldots\cdot a_{n}^{p^{k_{n-1}}}.

Since the order of
a_{i}^{p_{i-1}}
equals
p^{k_{i}-k_{i-1}}
,
the order of the right-hand side of this equation equals the order of

a_{n}^{p^{k_{n-1}}}
(as the other elements have smaller orders),
that is
p^{k_{n}-k_{n-1}}
.

Also note that
y_{n+1}^{p^{k_{n}}}=z^{p^{k_{n}-m}}

for some
z\in Z(G)
.
In the decomposition

Z(G)=\langle a_{n}\rangle_{p^{k_{n}}}\times B_{n}

the first component of
z^{p_{k_{n}}-m}

has order at most
p^{m}
.
Therefore, if
k_{n}-k_{n-1}>m
, then
the order of
y_{1}
is at least
p^{k_{n}-k_{n-1}}
,
as the order of
y_{n+1}^{p^{k_{n}}}=z^{p^{k_{n}-m}}

in that case is less than
p^{k_{n}-k_{n-1}}
.

Since

k_{i}-k_{i-1}>k_{i-1}-k_{i-2}>\ldots>k_{2}-k_{1}>k_{1}>m,

the condition

k_{i}-k_{i-1}>m

is indeed satisfied for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
On the other hand,

k_{i}-k_{i-1}>k_{i-1}>k_{i-2}\cdot 2>\ldots>k_{1}\cdot 2^{i-2}>m\cdot 2^{i-2}.

That is why the order of
y_{1}
is at least
p^{m\cdot 2^{n-2}}

for any sufficiently big number
n
.
In other words,
y_{1}
has infinite order, which is impossible
in the periodic group
G
. Thus, the system above has no solutions in
G
.

As a conclusion, a unimodular system of equations over
G
,
which has no solutions in
G
itself,
is found both in case when
G/Z(G)
has bounded period
and in case when the period of
G/Z(G)
is unbounded,
hence the proof of the lemma is complete.

∎

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that
G
is a nilpotent

p
-group with the first Ulm
factor of bounded period.
Then every
p
-nonsingular
system of equations over
G

has a solution in
G
.

Proof.

Note that the subgroup
G_{0}\subset G
is divisible by Lemma
2
.
We argue by induction on nilpotency class of
G
.
For abelian groups we have a decomposition of
G

into a direct product of
G_{0}

and the first Ulm factor of
G
, which has bounded period.
Such groups contain solutions of all
p
-nonsingular systems of equations over themselves

[
22
]
.

Suppose that the statement of the lemma is proved for
nilpotent groups of nilpotency class
s-1
.
Now we prove the statement for nilpotent groups of class
s
.
Consider
G/\gamma_{s}(G)
. As
(G/\gamma_{s}(G))_{0}

contains the image of
G_{0}
under taking the quotient

G\to G/\gamma_{s}(G)
,
the first Ulm factor of

G/\gamma_{s}(G)
has bounded period as well.
Hence, by induction,
G/\gamma_{s}(G)
contains a solution of any

p
-nonsingular system of equations over itself.
This means that any
p
-nonsingular system of equations over
G
,
after a fitting substitution of variables,
can be made such that
the product of coefficients of any equation lies in
\gamma_{s}(G)
.
So from now we consider only systems with this property.

Assume that the variables in the system commute with each other as well as with the coefficients of
W
.
In that case the system
W
is equivalent to a system
W^{\prime}
, in which every equation
has no coefficients apart from one coefficient lying in
\gamma_{s}(G)
.
Note that a set of elements from
\gamma_{l+1}(G)
is a solution of
W

if and only if this set is a solution of
W^{\prime}
.
Indeed, the subgroup
\gamma_{l+1}(G)

is central in
G
, therefore the elements of such solution
actually commute with each other and with coefficients of
W
,
so in this case the systems
W
and
W^{\prime}
are indeed equivalent.
As the system
W
is
p
-nonsingular,
such is the system
W^{\prime}
as well. Thus,
to prove the lemma, it suffices to prove that
\gamma_{l+1}(G)

satisfies the conditions of the lemma.

We prove that there is a number
t

such that
\left(\gamma_{s}(G)\right)^{t}
is divisible
(from this fact follows that the reduced part of

\gamma_{s}(G)
has bounded period, as needed).
Take
[g,h]
, where
g\in G,h\in\gamma_{s-1}(G)
.
Suppose that the period of the first Ulm factor of
G

equals
m
. Then
g^{m}\in G_{0}
. So for any natural number
k

there is an element
r\in G
such that
g=r^{k}
.
It follows that

[g^{m},h]=[r^{km},h]=[r,h]^{km}\mod{\gamma_{s+1}=\{1\}}.

Also note that
[g^{m},h]=[g,h]^{m}\mod{\gamma_{s+1}(G)=\{1\}}
.
As a conclusion,

[g,h]^{m}=[r,h]^{km}.

In other words, for any element
c
of the form
[g,h]^{m}\left(\gamma_{s}(G)\right)^{m}
,
where
g\in G,h\in\gamma_{s-1}(G)
,
and any integer
k
there is an element
b:=[r,h]^{m}\in\left(\gamma_{s}(G)\right)^{m}

such that
c=b^{k}
.
As these elements generate the abelian subgroup

\left(\gamma_{s}(G)\right)^{m}
, we conclude that this subgroup is divisible.

∎

The proved lemmas allow us to prove Theorem

1
, which was formulated in the introduction.
Recall its statement.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
G
is a periodic nilpotent group.

Then
G
contains a solution of any unimodular system of
equations over itself
if and only if
only a finite number of
p
-components of

G
has non-trivial first Ulm factor and
all these factors have bounded period.

Proof.

Denote the
p
-component of
G
by
G_{p}
.

If there is component
G_{p}

of
G
with unbounded period of its first Ulm factor,
then, by Lemma
3
, there is a unimodular
system over
G_{p}
with no solutions in
G_{p}

(and, consequently, there is an analogous system over
G
).

If, nonetheless, all of the first Ulm factors of
p
-components have bounded periods,
but there is an infinite number of nontrivial ones among them,
then
G
contains no solutions of the system

\begin{cases}xy_{1}^{-p_{1}^{m_{1}}}&=a_{1},\\
xy_{2}^{-p_{2}^{m_{2}}}&=a_{2},\\
&\vdots\end{cases}

where
p_{i}^{m_{i}}
is a period of
G_{p_{i}}/\left(G_{p_{i}}\right)_{0}
,
while
a_{i}\in G_{p_{i}}\setminus(G_{p_{i}})_{0}
.
Indeed, having a solution of this system assumes that the element
x

of this solution has a nontrivial
p_{i}
-component for every
i

(since the corresponding component of
y_{i}^{p_{i}^{m_{i}}}

lies in
(G_{p_{i}})_{0}\not\ni a_{i}
),
thus
x
has infinite order, which is impossible in a periodic group.

Now note that the matrix of exponent sums of variables in the equations of the system is

\begin{pmatrix}1&-p_{1}^{m_{1}}&0&0&0&\ldots\\
1&0&-p_{2}^{m_{2}}&0&0&\ldots\\
1&0&0&-p_{3}^{m_{3}}&0&\ldots\\
1&0&0&0&-p_{4}^{m_{4}}&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.

It follows that the system above is
p
-nonsingular
both for prime numbers
p_{i}
, which are included in this matrix,
(since a power of each of such prime numbers is included in the matrix only once)
and for the remaining prime numbers
p
.
In other words, this system, which has no solutions in
G
, is unimodular.

If, on the contrary, there is only a finite number of
p
-components
G_{p}

of
G
with nontrivial first Ulm factors, and all these factors have bounded period,
then
G
is decomposed into a direct product of
a divisible nilpotent group
and a finite number of
groups, which satisfy Lemma
4
.
Such product contains a solution to every
unimodular system of equations over itself.

∎

4
Torsion-free groups

Similarly with the previous section we introduce the notion of

p
-height.

Definition
.

The
p
-height
of an element
g\in G

is the greatest number
n
shuch that
g=h^{p^{n}}
for some

h\in G
. If there is no greatest number among these, we call

g
an element of
infinite
p
-height
.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that
A
is a reduced torsion-free abelian group.
Then there is a unimodular system of equations over
A

which has no solutions in
A
.

Proof.

For the length of this proof we adopt
the additive notation for abelian groups.
Furthermore, since we seek solutions of systems of equations
in abelian groups, the systems can also
be written additively, assuming that the variables
commute with each other as well as with the coefficients of
the equations.

Since
A
is reduced, there is a prime number

p
such that
A
is not
p
-divisible.
Embed
A
into its divisible hull
\widehat{A}

and take the subgroup
A_{p}\subset\widehat{A}
generated by the set

\left\{\dfrac{a}{n}\mid a\in A,(n,p)=1\right\}
.
Suppose that some element
b\notin pA
lies in
pA_{p}
.
Then
b
is equal to a finite linear combination
of elements from
pA
with rational coefficients whose denominators
are coprime with
p
.
In other words,
kb\in pA
for some number
k
coprime with
p
.
On the other hand,
pb\in pA
.
Therefore
b\in pA
. We arrive at a contradiction, hence

b\notin pA_{p}
.
It follows that
A_{p}
is not
p
-divisible as well, and
A_{p}\setminus pA_{p}

contains
A\setminus pA
.

The group
A_{p}
is a direct sum of a divisible group and a reduced one.
Denote the reduced direct summand by
B_{p}
. The reduced group
B_{p}
is

p^{\prime}
-divisible, however, all its elements have finite
p
-height.
Choose a maximal linearly independent set
\{b_{j}\mid j\in J\}

over
\mathbb{Z}
,
where
b_{j}\notin pB_{p}
.
Multiplying the elements
b_{j}
by numbers coprime with
p
,
we can assume that each
b_{j}
lies in
the image of
A\subset A_{p}
under projection onto
B_{p}
.
Thus we get a free
\mathcal{R}_{p}
-module
B_{p}
with the basis

\{b_{j}\mid j\in J\}
, where
\mathcal{R}_{p}
is the ring
of rational numbers with denominators coprime with
p
.
Now consider a free module
\mathfrak{B}_{p}

with the same basis, but over the ring

\mathfrak{Z}_{p}
of integer
p
-adic numbers.
Since
\mathcal{R}_{p}\subset\mathfrak{Z}_{p}
,
we can assume inclusion
B_{p}\subset\mathfrak{B}_{p}
.
Note now that
\mathfrak{B}_{p}
admits the
p
-adic topology in which
neighborhoods of
0
are
p^{n}\mathfrak{B}_{p}
.
Also note that since for any non-zero integer
p
-adic number
\mathfrak{a}

there is a number
n
such that
\mathfrak{a}\notin p^{n}\mathfrak{Z}_{p}
,
for any nontrivial element
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathfrak{B}_{p}

there is also a number
n
such that
\mathfrak{b}\notin p^{n}\mathfrak{B}_{p}
.
It follows that

\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}p^{n}\mathfrak{B}_{p}=\{0\},

i.e. the topology on
\mathfrak{B}_{p}
is Hausdorff,
and the limit of any sequence of elements of
\mathfrak{B}_{p}

is unique (provided the limit exists).

Consider, for a start, the following system
of equations over
\mathfrak{Z}_{p}
:

\begin{cases}x_{1}-px_{2}&=c_{1};\\
x_{2}-px_{3}&=c_{2};\\
x_{3}-px_{4}&=c_{3};\\
&\vdots\end{cases}

where
c_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
.
Suppose that this systems has a solution in
\mathfrak{Z}_{p}
.
Then

x_{1}-p^{k}x_{k+1}=c_{1}+pc_{2}+p^{2}c_{3}+\ldots+p^{k-1}c_{k}.

As
x_{k+1}
are integer
p
-adic numbers, we have

p^{k}x_{k+1}\to 0
for
k\to\infty
,
hence

x_{1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}p^{i-1}c_{i}.

In particular,
x_{1}
belongs to
R_{p}
if and only if
the sequence
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\ldots)
is periodic.
Then, for a non-periodic sequence
(1,0,1,0,0,1,0,0,0,1,\ldots)
,
we get a unimodular system of equations over
\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathfrak{Z}_{p}

with no rational solutions in
\mathfrak{Z}_{p}
.

Now, taking the same system for the sequence of the elements

(b_{1},0,b_{1},0,0,b_{1},0,0,0,\ldots),

we get a system with no solutions contained solely in
B_{p}\subset\mathfrak{B}_{p}

(as in this case
x_{1}
is the limits of the sequence

\left\{\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}p^{i-1}c_{i}\right)b_{1}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},

which converges to
\zeta b_{1}
, where
\zeta\notin\mathbb{Q}
).
As a conclusion, we have found a unimodular system of equations over
B_{p}

with no solutions in
B_{p}
.
As
b_{1}
is an image of some element of
A
under a homomorphism
A\to B_{p}
,
there is, as well, a unimodular system of equations over
A

with no solutions in
A
.

∎

Proposition 3
.

Suppose that
G
is a nilpotent torsion-free group.
Then there exists the largest divisible subgroup

H\subset G
, and
G/H
is a reduced torsion-free group.

Proof.

Suppose that
A,B\subset G
are two divisible subgroups.
Take the subgroup
\langle A,B\rangle\subset G
.
Note that if the nilpotency class of

G
is
s
, and
n
is some natural number, then

\langle A,B\rangle=\langle A^{n^{s}},B^{n^{s}}\rangle\subseteq\langle A,B\rangle^{n}\subseteq\langle A,B\rangle.

As the start and the end of this sequence are the same, all inclusions in the
sequence are equalities.
In particular,

\langle A,B\rangle^{n}\subseteq\langle A,B\rangle

for any natural
n
.
In other words,
\langle A,B\rangle
is divisible as well.
Therefore, a subgroup of
G
, generated by a finite number
of divisible subgroup, is divisible as well.
The same is true for a family of an arbitrary cardinality,
as each element of a subgroup, generated by such family,
belongs to a subgroup generated by only a finite
number of subgroups from the family, which is divisible.

Then the largest divisible group
H
is a subgroup
generated by all the divisible subgroups of
G
.
As
H
contains every divisible subgroups of
G
,

H
is the largest of these.

Assume that
G/H
has a nontrivial divisible subgroup
\overline{D}
.
Consider its preimage
D\subset G

under taking the quotient
G\to G/H
.
Let
d\in D
and
\overline{d}\in\overline{D}
be an image of
d
under taking the quotient
G\to G/H
.
Also let
n
be a natural number.
Since the divisibility of
\overline{D}
there is an element

\overline{f}\in\overline{D}
such that
\overline{d}=\overline{f}^{n^{s}}
.
Let
f\in D
be a preimage of
\overline{f}\in\overline{D}
.
Then we have

d\in f^{n^{s}}\cdot H\subset f^{n^{s}}\cdot\left\langle H^{n^{s}}\right\rangle\subset\left\langle D^{n^{s}}\right\rangle\subset D^{n}

for any
d\in D,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In other words, we get a divisible subgroup
D\subset G
,
which is larger than
H
. We arrive at a contradiction with the fact that

H
is the largest one.
Thus
G/H
is reduced.

Now suppose that there is an element
\bar{g}\in G/H

of finite order
m
.
Then, for its preimage
g\in G
,
we have
g^{m}\in H
, and, since
H
is divisible,
g^{m}=h^{m}

for some
h\in H
.
However,
m
th roots in a nilpotent trosion-free group are unique,
hence
h=g
, i.e.
g\in H
.
It follows that
G/H
is torsion-free.

∎

Lemma 6
.

Suppose that
G
is a reduced torsion-free nilpotent group.
Then
G/Z_{s-1}(G)
is a reduced torsion-free abelian group,
where
s
is the nilpotency class of
G
.

Proof.

In
[
20
]
it is proved that

G/Z_{s-1}(G)
is actually torsion-free.

All
G/Z_{i}(G)
are reduced by Lemma
1
.
Indeed, this lemma states that

G/Z(G)=G/Z_{1}(G)
is reduced. Since

\Bigl(G/Z_{i}(G)\Bigr)\biggl/Z\Bigl(G/Z_{i}(G)\Bigr)\cong G/Z_{i+1}(G),

the fact that
G/Z_{i}(G)
is reduced implies,
by Lemma
1
,
that
G/Z_{i+1}(G)
is reduced as well.

∎

Lemma 7
.

Suppose that
G
is a non-divisible torsion-free nilpotent group.
Then there is a unimodular system of equations over
G

with no solutions in
G
.

Proof.

After taking the quotient by the largest divisible subgroup

H\subset G
, we can assume that

G
is reduced.
By Lemma
6
, the group

G
has a reduced torsion-free abelian quotient group,
namely
G/Z_{s-1}(G)
.
By Lemma
5
,
there is a unimodular system of equations over
G/Z_{s-1}(G)

which has no solutions in the
G/Z_{s-1}(G)
.
Consequently, there is a unimodular system of equations over
G

with no solutions in
G
.

∎

Having proved the last lemma, we can prove Theorem

2

Theorem 2

Suppose that

over itself

Proof.

The case when

The statement for a non-divisible group
