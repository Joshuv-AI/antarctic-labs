Sex- and age-dependent physiological adaptation of captive père david’s deer revealed by multi-omics analysis
Authors: Rongji Yu, Shumiao Zhang, Yiping Li, Gang Niu, Zhibin Cheng, Junfang Li, Jiade Bai, Shubing Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUnderstanding how age and sex influence molecular and physiological changes is essential for studying endangered species, particularly Père David’s deer, which is extinct in the wild. In this study, multi-omics analyses were performed to investigate transcriptomic, metabolomic, and proteomic dynamics in male and female Père David’s deer across different developmental stages under captive conditions.ResultsThe results revealed sex- and stage-specific molecular trajectories, with males showing enhanced ion metabolism during early life and females exhibiting increased lipid metabolism during the subadult stage. A substantial proportion of differentially abundant metabolites and differentially expressed proteins were associated with immune and inflammatory processes. Transcriptome-based age estimation indicated that individuals at the Fawn stage exhibited younger transcriptomic profiles, and enrichment analysis of highly weighted genes highlighted immune-related pathways. In addition, transcriptomic deconvolution analysis revealed coordinated alterations in innate and adaptive immune cell populations during development.ConclusionsThese findings provide a comprehensive multi-omics characterization of molecular and immune dynamics in captive Père David’s deer and improve understanding of developmental and sex-associated biological variation in this endangered species.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-026-13021-1.Keywords: Elaphurus davidianus, Multi-omics Analysis, Age Dependency, Sex-Specific Differences, Evolution

Background

Père David’s Deer (Elaphurus davidianus) is the sole extant species of the genus Elaphurus within the Cervidae family, order Artiodactyla, and class Mammalia. By the late 19th century, this species had become extinct in the wild in China. Since 1985, efforts to reintroduce Père David’s Deer to China have been ongoing [1]. Despite these efforts, it remains classified as “Extinct in the Wild” on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species [2]. Today, Père David’s Deer remains listed as “Extinct in the Wild” on the IUCN Red List, making it the world’s only extant mammalian species currently classified under this category. Unlike other cervid species, such as sika deer(Cervus nippon) [3, 4], which have been extensively studied, Père David’s Deer has received less attention due to the rarity of available samples.

Although reintroduction efforts began in 1985 following the successful return of founder individuals from Europe to China, the species has undergone several severe bottlenecks since the 19th century, including the historic bottleneck in Woburn Abbey and subsequent intermittent founder effects during captive breeding and translocations. These events drastically reduced genetic diversity, leading to high inbreeding coefficients and limited evolutionary potential within contemporary populations. Additionally, Chai et al. conducted a comparative analysis of blood transcriptomes across multiple species, uncovering unique characteristic genes in Père David’s Deer that distinguish them from closely related species such as sika deer and reindeer [5]. Although the transcriptomic characteristics of Pere David’s deer at different stages of development have significant implications for the breeding and release of deers into the wild, the area remains largely unexplored.

In addition to the blood transcriptome, the physiological and biochemical indicators of Père David’s deer at a broader level are also important metrics for investigating the differences between individuals with different traits [6]. Research has demonstrated significant differences in most physiological and biochemical parameters between fawns and subadults, as well as between fawns and adults. However, the physiological and biochemical differences between subadults and adults are minimal [7]. These physiological and biochemical indicators primarily reflect protein and small molecule levels. They highlight the importance of investigating the blood proteome and metabolome for the healthy management and breeding of Père David’s Deer.

Analyzing the physiological, biochemical, and transcriptomic characteristics of animals from a blood perspective is a commonly employed research method in animal studies [8–10]. The differences in blood composition between sexes and age groups are also crucial phenotypes in these studies [11, 12]. For example, red deer(Cervus elaphus) show a higher susceptibility to ion-related diseases shortly after birth, while in elderly female deer, the levels of related ions and oxidative stress biomarkers in the blood are significantly lower [13]. In roe deer(Capreolus capreolus), blood oxidative stress indicators were also shown to vary with age and sampling conditions, emphasizing the importance of blood biochemical homeostasis in ecological and physiological studies [14]. Moreover, ionomic analyses in other cervid species, such as fallow deer (Dama dama), have demonstrated significant tissue and blood elemental variations associated with sex and age, suggesting conserved physiological regulatory mechanisms among cervids [15]. However, in Père David’s deer, the age or sex dependent blood transcriptome remains unclear, and the identification of relevant proteins and metabolites is still an unknown area of research.

This study represents the first multi-omics analysis of the transcriptome, proteome, and metabolome of Père David’s Deer, addressing a critical gap in the field. The results indicated that juvenile development and adult aging corresponded to periods of pronounced transcriptomic fluctuations in male and female individuals, respectively. Sex- and stage-specific metabolic patterns were observed, with lipid metabolism elevated in developing females and ion metabolism prominent in juvenile males. In addition, a substantial fraction of differentially expressed metabolites and proteins were associated with immune and inflammatory processes. Transcriptomic age analysis revealed lower transcriptomic age in Fawn-stage individuals, and pathway enrichment of high-weight genes at this stage highlighted immune-related pathways. Deconvolution of transcriptomic data further revealed coordinated changes in innate and adaptive immune cell populations across development. Collectively, these findings provide a comprehensive characterization of molecular and immune dynamics in captive Père David’s Deer, while emphasizing that extrapolation to wild populations or physiological adaptation should be interpreted cautiously.

Methods

Sample collection

Blood samples were collected from 33 individual Père David’s deer (male: female = 19:14), categorized into four developmental stages: Fawn, Sub-adult, Adult, and Old (Table 1). Specifically, Fawns (< 1 year) represent the rapid growth stage, Sub-adults (1–3 years) correspond to developmental and reproductive maturation, Adults (3–7 years) represent the stable reproductive stage, and Old individuals (> 7 years) reflect aging-associated physiological changes. Previous studies in cervids have reported age-related differences in metabolism, reproduction, immune regulation, and feeding behavior across these stages.All blood samples were collected between 08:00 and 11:00 a.m. on August 25–27, 2020, by the same veterinary team following a standardized sampling protocol. To minimize circadian and handling-related variation, all animals were sampled under similar physiological and environmental conditions. Whole blood samples were collected into vacuum blood collection tubes containing heparin anticoagulant, temporarily stored on ice, and processed within 2 h after collection before long-term storage at − 80 °C. Blood transcriptome sequencing was performed for all 33 individuals (Fig. 1).

Collection and analysis workflow for père david’s deer blood samples

Animal health status and housing conditions

All Père David’s deer included in this study were clinically healthy at the time of sampling, with no observable signs of disease or abnormal behavior. The animals were maintained at the Beijing Milu Park, located in the former Nanhaizi Royal Hunting Garden in the southern suburbs of Beijing, an area characterized by favorable climatic, topographical, and ecological conditions for Père David’s deer habitation (39°42′–39°52′ N, 116°20′–116°32′ E).

The park is situated in a warm temperate semi-humid climate zone at an average elevation of approximately 31.5 m. The landscape comprises natural wetlands, grasslands, ponds, and woodland areas, providing abundant vegetation and water resources. The deer were managed under a semi-free-ranging (semi-captive) system, allowing free movement within large enclosed natural habitats while receiving standardized daily care.

Dedicated deer shelters and research facilities are available on site to support routine husbandry and scientific monitoring. Animal maintenance and care were conducted by trained husbandry staff in collaboration with a professional research team. Health status was monitored regularly through routine physical examinations and disease prevention programs, and animal behavior and physiological conditions were continuously observed and recorded to ensure animal welfare throughout the study period.

Transcriptome library construction and quality control

mRNA was enriched from the total RNA using magnetic beads, followed by fragmentation to obtain mRNA segments. cDNA was synthesized through a reverse transcription reaction, and cDNA fragments approximately 370–420 bp in length were selected, amplified by PCR, and purified. The final library was prepared and sequenced. Raw data were processed by removing adapter-containing reads, reads with N bases, and low-quality reads to obtain clean data. HISAT2 v2.0.5 was used to align the sequences to the reference genome (GCA_002443075.1), and novel gene prediction was performed using StringTie (1.3.3b) [16]. FeatureCounts (1.5.0-p3) was used for quantification, generating the gene expression matrix.

Protein extraction and quality control

For proteomic analysis, plasma samples separated from anticoagulated blood were used for protein extraction. High-abundance proteins, including hemoglobin and albumin, were depleted prior to protein extraction to improve the detection sensitivity of low-abundance proteins. Cell lysis was performed using a lysis buffer combined with ultrasonic disruption. After protein precipitation, proteins were extracted and purified. Proteomic analysis was performed using the Q Exactive™ HF-X mass spectrometer with the Nanospray Flex™ (ESI) ion source, setting the spray voltage at 2.3 kV and the ion transfer tube temperature at 320 °C. Data-dependent acquisition mode was used for mass spectrometry analysis of protein samples. The search software Proteome Discoverer 2.4 (PD2.4) was employed to search the spectra results against a customized UniProt FASTA database for each run. Because a fully annotated Père David’s deer protein database is currently unavailable, the customized database was constructed using all available protein sequences from cervid-related species, including Muntiacus reevesi, Muntiacus muntjak, and Cervus elaphus, retrieved from UniProt on September 27, 2020, comprising a total of 50,980 protein entries. Only credible peptide and protein hits were retained, and false discovery rate (FDR) validation was performed to exclude peptides and proteins with an FDR greater than 1%. High-abundance blood proteins such as HB were retained.

Metabolite extraction and identification

Methanol of analytical grade was added to the blood samples to precipitate proteins. After vortexing, ice-bath treatment, and centrifugation, the supernatant was collected and subjected to LC-MS analysis [17, 18]. Equal volumes from each experimental sample were pooled to create a QC sample, and an equal volume of background solution was used as a blank sample. The raw data were imported into the CD search software for preliminary screening. Metabolite identification was performed by matching molecular ion peaks and MS/MS fragmentation spectra. Molecular formulas were predicted based on accurate mass and fragment ion information. Metabolite annotation was conducted using mzVault software (Thermo Fisher Scientific) through spectral matching against the mzCloud database (accessed on September 28, 2020) and the MassList reference library. mzVault served as a spectral matching platform, while mzCloud was used as the primary public spectral database.Background ions were removed using blank samples, and signal normalization was performed prior to downstream analysis to obtain final metabolite identification and quantitative data.

Chromatographic separation

Chromatographic separation was performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Germany) coupled to a Q Exactive™ HF-X mass spectrometer. Samples were separated on a Hypesil Gold C18 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and the flow rate was set at 0.2 mL/min. For positive ion mode, mobile phase A consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of methanol. For negative ion mode, mobile phase A consisted of 5 mM ammonium acetate (pH 9.0), and mobile phase B consisted of methanol. The gradient elution program was as follows: 98% A (0–1.5 min), 98%–0% A (1.5–12 min), 0% A (12–14 min), and re-equilibration at 98% A (14.1–17 min).

Differential gene/protein analysis

Differential gene or protein analysis was performed using DESeq2 version 1.40.2 [19]. The DESeqDataSet object was constructed using the default parameters. Differential genes or proteins were selected based on the criteria of an absolute Log₂FoldChange > 1 and a corrected p-value < 0.05. Differential metabolites were identified using variable importance in projection (VIP) > 1.0, absolute Log₂FoldChange > 0.5, and p-value < 0.05 based on PLS-DA and Student’s t-test.They were subsequently annotated as Up, Down, or None.

Pathway enrichment analysis

Pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using clusterProfiler version 4.10.0 [20], utilizing the GO and KEGG databases for biological process enrichment. Because clusterProfiler does not provide built-in annotation libraries for Cervidae species, Bos taurus was used as the reference species based on the org.Bt.eg.db annotation package (version 3.22.0). Multiple testing correction was performed using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, and pathways with an FDR-corrected p-value < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

Weighted correlation network analysis

WGCNA was used to identify gene modules associated with different developmental stages [21, 22]. During the module merging stage, a mergeCutHeight of 0.35 was applied, corresponding to merging modules with eigengene correlations greater than 0.65, in order to reduce redundancy among highly similar co-expression modules. Gene clusters with a correlation > 0.5 and p < 0.05 were considered as gene modules associated with the respective developmental stage.

Time series analysis

Mfuzz was used for time series analysis to identify gene clusters associated with age-related changes in the differentially expressed genes [23, 24]. The input data consisted of a normalized gene expression matrix and the corresponding age information for each sample. The fuzzification parameter was calculated using the mestimate function. The number of clusters was set to 9 based on the gene count and clustering results.

Protein interaction network analysis

The online database STRING v12.0 (https://string-db.org/) was used to construct the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network. Proteins with higher connectivity were analyzed for key features. PPI network analysis was performed on the target protein nodes of interest and key proteins predicted to be involved in signaling pathways to identify potential interactions.

Transcriptomic Age Analysis (TAI).

Transcriptomic age analysis was performed using the myTAI framework, which integrates transcriptome expression data with evolutionary origin information of genes to quantify the “age” of a transcriptome [25].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R 4.4.0. Student’s t-test was used for comparisons between two groups, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant unless otherwise specified. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted based on hypergeometric testing with adjusted p-values/FDR used to assess significance. WGCNA was performed using correlation-based network analysis. Transcriptomic age analysis was conducted using the myTAI framework with permutation-based statistical testing.

Use of Artificial Intelligence Tools.

During the preparation of this manuscript, the authors used an artificial intelligence (AI)-based language model (e.g., ChatGPT) solely for language editing and improvement of readability. The AI tool was not used for data analysis, interpretation, or generation of scientific content. All scientific conclusions and interpretations were independently developed and verified by the authors.

Results

Transcriptomic changes in male père david’s deer across developmental stages

Blood samples from 19 male Père David’s Deer were subjected to transcriptomic analysis to identify gene expression differences between adjacent developmental stages. The results revealed that the largest number of differentially expressed genes occurred between the Fawn and Subadult stages, followed by the Adult and Old stages, with the fewest differences observed between the Subadult and Adult stages (Fig. 2A). Notably, the transition from the Fawn to Subadult stage in male Père David’s Deer showed the most substantial changes in gene expression, with the number of differentially expressed genes being 5 to 10 times higher than in other adjacent developmental stages.

Further weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) revealed that major gene integrations were found in the Blue (4806), Turquoise (4832), and Brown (3227) modules (Fig. 2B). Correlation analysis between gene modules and developmental stages identified the Blue and Pink modules as significantly associated with the Fawn and Old stages(Supplementary Table 1), respectively (Fig. 2C, Supplementary Figure S1A). Pathway enrichment analysis of the Blue module, which was most significantly associated with the Fawn stage, highlighted key pathways related to developmental growth and homeostasis maintenance, such as morphogenesis, DNA damage repair, and telomere maintenance (Fig. 2D, Supplementary Table 2). These results indicate that the blood transcriptomic landscape of male Père David’s Deer fluctuates most significantly during the Fawn stage, suggesting its importance in male developmental growth. Special attention should be given to this stage, possibly due to unique physiological activities, such as antler growth during sexual maturation.

The blood transcriptome of male Père David’s Deer exhibits the most significant changes during the Fawn stage, with the number of differentially expressed genes far exceeding that of other stages. WGCNA analysis revealed that gene modules significantly associated with the Fawn stage are primarily involved in pathways related to developmental growth and homeostasis maintenance. These findings suggest that the Fawn stage represents a critical period in the development of male Père David’s Deer, warranting special attention to their unique physiological activities.

Transcriptomic changes in female père david’s deer across aging stages

Analysis of the transcriptional differences between adjacent developmental stages of 14 female Père David’s Deer revealed that, unlike males, the greatest number of differentially expressed genes was observed between the Adult and Old stages, followed by Fawn and Sub, with the fewest differences found between Sub and Adult (Fig. 3A). Notably, the number of differentially expressed genes between Adult and Old was 10 to 35 times higher than in other adjacent stages. These results suggest that, unlike male Père David’s Deer, the most significant fluctuations in the blood transcriptome of females occur between the Adult and Old stages.

Further WGCNA analysis revealed that the major gene integrations were found in the turquoise module (4994), blue module (3489), green module (1764), and yellow module (1767) (Fig. 3B). Correlation analysis between gene modules and developmental stages identified the turquoise module as significantly associated with the Old stage, the tan and pink modules with the Sub stage, the brown, cyan, and black modules with the Adult stage, and the green module with the Fawn stage (Fig. 3C, Supplementary Figure S1B). The most prominent change in the blood transcriptome background occurred in the turquoise module during the Old stage, with a high correlation of 0.83(Supplementary Table 3). Pathway enrichment analysis revealed that the major pathway changes were concentrated in the oxidative metabolism domain, including aerobic respiration, ATP synthesis, and protein catabolism (Fig. 3D, Supplementary Table 4). These results indicate that the blood transcriptomic background in female Père David’s Deer experiences the most significant fluctuations during the Old stage, with major genes focusing on metabolic processes. This suggests that the metabolic background in female Père David’s Deer may undergo substantial fluctuations during the Old stage.

Overall, integrative analyses of differential gene expression and WGCNA consistently indicate that the most pronounced transcriptomic remodeling in female Père David’s Deer occurs during the transition from the Adult to Old stage. This remodeling is characterized by extensive gene expression shifts and strong stage-specific module associations, particularly the Old stage–related turquoise module. Functional enrichment further suggests that these changes are predominantly driven by pathways involved in energy metabolism and protein turnover, highlighting a coordinated reprogramming of metabolic homeostasis during late-life development.

Ion homeostasis drives development in male père david’s deer

Transcriptomic changes in male Père David’s deer vary across developmental stages. While the transcriptomic background characteristics of male Père David’s deer are primarily established during the Fawn stage, the functional changes between developmental stages remain unclear. To investigate transcriptomic changes across developmental stages, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed on transcriptomic data from adjacent stages. The results revealed that during the Fawn-to-Sub stage, upregulated pathways included starch and sucrose metabolism, while downregulated pathways were primarily associated with vitamin digestion and absorption, as well as chlorophyll metabolism (Fig. 4A-B, Supplementary Table 5).

Ion homeostasis drives development in male père david’s deer. A Changes in signaling pathway expression across developmental stages; (B) Enrichment changes of related signaling pathways; (C) Expression changes of genes related to ion metabolism and DNA damage repair; (D) Iron content in artificial feed.

In the Sub-to-Adult stage, upregulated pathways encompassed metal ion homeostasis, cellular metal ion homeostasis, and cellular chemical homeostasis, whereas downregulated pathways were related to DNA replication, DNA metabolic processes, and cellular responses to DNA damage stimuli (Fig. 4A, B). During the Adult-to-Old stage, upregulated pathways included extracellular matrix, extracellular region part, and extracellular region, while downregulated pathways involved cellular responses to stress, cellular responses to DNA damage stimuli, and DNA metabolic processes (Fig. 4A, B).

The transcriptomic changes in male Père David’s deer across developmental stages reveal distinct pathway dynamics. During the transition from the Sub-adult to Adult stage, pathways related to ion homeostasis and metal ion metabolism are continuously upregulated, suggesting their important role in developmental progression. In contrast, pathways involved in DNA damage repair show a progressive downregulation from the Sub-adult to Adult stage and further into the Adult-to-Old transition, indicating a gradual decline in genomic maintenance capacity during aging (Fig. 4C).

The developmental changes observed in male Père David’s deer may provide preliminary insights that could be helpful for optimizing artificial breeding management and informing future reintroduction strategies. The artificial dry feed for Père David’s deer primarily consists of a mixture of corn, soybean meal, wheat bran, barley, calcium hydrogen phosphate, and salt. Using data from the Chinese Feed Database (www.chinafeeddata.org.cn), the iron content of the main components was compared. Our results suggest that during the juvenile development stage of male Père David’s Deer, the addition of alfalfa meal and attention to the supplementation of calcium hydrogen phosphate and mineral salts may be necessary, although this recommendation requires further validation (Fig. 4D).

Lipid metabolism influences developmental growth in female père david’s deer

Previous results indicated significant differences in the developmental stages of transcriptomic background fluctuations between female and male Père David’s deer. Therefore, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was also performed on transcriptomic data from adjacent developmental stages in female deer. The results revealed that during the Fawn-to-Sub stage, upregulated pathways included lipid biosynthesis, phospholipid biosynthesis, and protein tyrosine kinase activity, while downregulated pathways were primarily associated with RNA modification, ribosome biogenesis, and tRNA processing (Fig. 5A-B, Supplementary Table 6).

In the Sub-to-Adult stage, upregulated pathways encompassed growth, cellular responses to stress, and cellular responses to DNA damage stimuli, whereas downregulated pathways were related to phospholipid binding, lipid binding, and phosphatidylinositol binding (Fig. 5A, B). During the Adult-to-Old stage, upregulated pathways included cellular ion homeostasis, cellular chemical homeostasis, and cellular cation homeostasis, while downregulated pathways involved protein deubiquitination, ubiquitin-like protein-specific protease activity, and removal of small protein modifications (Fig. 5A, B).

During the transition from the Fawn to Sub stage, lipid metabolism-related processes were upregulated in female deer. In contrast, during the maturation from Sub to Adult stage, lipid metabolism-related processes were downregulated, with upregulation primarily observed in pathways related to growth, methylation, and DNA damage response (Fig. 5C).

Changes in the MAPK-FOXO axis in the père david’s deer time series model

Previous results have shown that metabolic pathways, such as lipid metabolism and ion metabolism, play a crucial role in the growth and development of Père David’s Deer. Furthermore, an integrated analysis of transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics was conducted. Initially, the samples were categorized into sub-adult and adult groups(Subadult : Adult = 5 : 5). Blood proteomic and untargeted metabolomic analyses were conducted on a subset of 10 individuals, resulting in the identification of 358 proteins and 458 metabolites. A time-series analysis of differentially expressed genes was conducted to identify gene sets that change with age. Among the nine identified gene sets, Gene Set 4 (containing 20 genes) showed a decreasing trend with growth, while Gene Set 9 (containing 21 genes) exhibited an increasing trend with age (Fig. 6A-B, Supplementary Table 7).

Changes in the MAPK-FOXO axis in the père david’s deer time series model. A Clustering results from the time-series analysis of differentially expressed genes (9 clusters); (B) Clusters 4 and 9 showing downregulation and upregulation, respectively, across the time-series progression; (C) Pathway enrichment results for differentially expressed genes between sub-adult and adult Père David’s Deer; (D) Gene expression heatmaps for Clusters 4 and 9 from the time-series analysis.

In addition to the changes observed at the gene level, a key focus of this study was the functional analysis of the transcriptome, particularly the signaling pathways. Pathway enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes revealed that, in adult Père David’s Deer, the MAPK signaling pathway was activated, while the FOXO signaling pathway, regulated by MAPK, was downregulated. In contrast, this pattern was reversed in sub-adult Père David’s Deer (Fig. 6C). Similarly, within Gene Set 4 and Gene Set 9, genes associated with the MAPK-FOXO axis, such as NOXO1, SOD3, and FGG, were identified (Fig. 6D, Supplementary Figure S2A).

Time-series analysis of differentially expressed genes identified two gene sets with distinct age-related expression trends: Gene Set 4 (decreasing with age) and Gene Set 9 (increasing with age). Pathway enrichment analysis revealed activation of the MAPK signaling pathway and downregulation of the FOXO signaling pathway in adult Père David’s Deer, with an opposite pattern in sub-adult individuals. These findings highlight the critical role of the MAPK-FOXO axis in the metabolic regulation of Père David’s Deer during development.

Transcriptional-proteomic-metabolic regulation of père david’s deer development

Blood samples from Père David’s deer were analyzed using mass spectrometry and quality control procedures, leading to the identification and quantification of 358 proteins. Differential expression analysis revealed 5 upregulated proteins and 29 downregulated proteins between groups (Fig. 7A-B).Principal component analysis (PCA) demonstrated clear separation among different developmental stages, indicating stage-dependent proteomic differences, whereas samples grouped by sex showed relatively low dispersion and no obvious clustering pattern (Supplementary Figure S3A–B).

Correlation analysis between genes from Gene Set 4 and Gene Set 9 and differentially expressed proteins identified 40 genes and 33 strongly correlated proteins (|r| > 0.5, p < 0.05) (Fig. 7C). In the chord diagram, the width of the connecting bands represents the strength of the correlations between genes and proteins. Given the direct connection between metabolite production and protein expression, we further analyzed the correlation between these 33 proteins and 142 differentially expressed small-molecule metabolites (Supplementary Table 8, Supplementary Fig. 3), revealing 131 highly correlated metabolites (Fig. 7D). Based on the filtered time-series genes, differential proteins, and strongly correlated metabolites, we constructed a transcriptome-proteome-metabolome regulatory network, which revealed expression correlations between blood metabolites and genes (Fig. 7E). In this three-dimensional regulatory network, several proteins at the proteomic level directly interact with the MAPK-FOXO axis (Supplementary Figure S2B), further supporting the age-dependent changes in the MAPK-FOXO axis observed at the transcriptional level. This co-regulatory network provides valuable multi-omics data for time-series studies of Père David’s deer (Supplementary Table 9).

In summary, the integration of transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic data revealed significant correlations between differentially expressed genes, proteins, and metabolites in Père David’s Deer. The constructed regulatory network highlights key molecular interactions, including the involvement of the MAPK-FOXO axis, providing comprehensive insights into age-dependent metabolic and signaling pathway changes. These findings offer a valuable framework for understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying growth and aging in Père David’s Deer.

Molecular and cellular remodeling across developmental stages revealed by multi-omics integration

Integrated metabolomic and proteomic analyses revealed that a substantial proportion of differentially expressed metabolites and proteins were associated with immune and inflammatory processes, highlighting immune regulation as a major molecular feature across developmental stages (Fig. 8A). At the metabolite level, immune-related lipid mediators, particularly arachidonic acid–derived eicosanoids such as prostaglandins and thromboxane B1, were prominently altered, indicating modulation of inflammatory signaling pathways [26, 27]. In parallel, dysregulation of lysophospholipid and sphingolipid species further supported changes in immune-related lipid signaling [28, 29].

Multi-omics profiling uncovers coordinated immune alterations across developmental stages. A Immune-related differentially expressed metabolites and proteins identified by integrated metabolomic and proteomic analyses; (B) Transcriptomic age index calculated from transcriptome data across four developmental stages; (C) Pathway enrichment analysis of high-weight genes contributing to transcriptomic age in the Fawn stage; (D) Inferred immune cell composition across developmental stages based on transcriptomic deconvolution analysis.

Consistently, proteomic profiling identified multiple immune-associated proteins with differential expression, including regulators of cytokine signaling (IL1R2), complement activation (MASP1), innate immune recognition (PGLYRP2), and antigen processing machinery (proteasome subunits PSMA and PSMB family members), collectively suggesting coordinated alterations in immune effector and surveillance pathways (Fig. 8A) [30–33].

To determine whether these immune-associated molecular changes were linked to developmental and aging-related transcriptional programs, we next assessed transcriptomic age across the four stages. Notably, individuals at the Fawn stage exhibited a significantly lower transcriptomic age than all other stages, whereas transcriptomic age values remained relatively stable from Sub to Old stages (Fig. 8B).

Importantly, pathway enrichment analysis of high-weight genes contributing to the transcriptomic age signature in the Fawn stage revealed significant enrichment of immune- and inflammation-related pathways (Fig. 8C), indicating that immune-associated transcriptional programs are a defining feature of early development.

Therefore further examined whether these molecular and transcriptional immune signatures were reflected at the cellular level. Transcriptome-based immune deconvolution analysis demonstrated pronounced, stage-dependent remodeling of immune cell composition (Fig. 8D). In particular, innate immune cells such as neutrophils and macrophage subsets exhibited age-associated shifts, while adaptive immune populations showed a decline in naïve T cells and an accumulation of regulatory and cytotoxic subsets with aging, consistent with progressive immune remodeling across development.

Collectively, these results demonstrate that immune-related alterations emerge coherently across metabolites, proteins, transcriptomic aging signatures, and immune cell populations, establishing immune remodeling as a central biological feature underlying developmental stage transitions.

Discussion

Père David’s Deer were historically widely distributed in high-salinity regions, including coastal areas of China [34]. Today, the largest nature reserve for Père David’s Deer is located in Dafeng, Jiangsu Province, within the shallow coastal zones of eastern China. Previous studies have indicated that genes associated with high-salt metabolism are under positive selection in the evolution of Père David’s Deer [35]. Our findings show gender and age-related differences in ion metabolism signaling pathways in this species. Specifically, during the developmental and growth stages of male Père David’s Deer calves, there is a progressive increase in ion metabolism-related signaling pathways. Similar studies on other deer species, such as white-tailed and sika deer, have reported higher frequencies of salt-licking behavior throughout the growth stages of males, with peaks occurring during the rutting season and antler growth periods [36–38]. The distinctive physiological activities of male Père David’s Deer, such as the significant increase in bone mass and ion deposition in antlers, further support these findings.

Female Père David’s Deer experience an upregulation of ion metabolism signaling pathways only after reaching adulthood. Studies have shown that female sika deer increase their salt intake by 40% during lactation [39], with a corresponding increase in salt-licking behavior specifically during this period [36]. Given that the link between ion drive and physiological needs, which influences dietary preferences, has been demonstrated in ungulates at an early stage [40], many studies have explored the gender- and stage-dependent differences in salt-licking behavior in these animals. However, the molecular-level impact of this dietary preference on activity fluctuations remains largely unexplored, particularly in Père David’s Deer, where research on salt-licking behavior is scarce. Our results suggest that the deviations in ion metabolism across different genders and stages indicate that, during the juvenile development stage, male Père David’s Deer should prioritize salt intake to meet their needs. In contrast, for female Père David’s Deer, the priority for salt intake should increase only after they reach adulthood.

Fat, as one of the primary energy sources, can be metabolized to produce heat, which helps maintain body temperature and provides energy for the functioning of various organs [41]. For herbivores, crude fat from plants plays a crucial role in energy acquisition [42]. Crude protein and crude fat are key nutrients found in the tender leaves of plants preferred by Père David’s Deer [43]. Our findings indicate that lipid metabolism increases during the juvenile stage of female Père David’s Deer and becomes a prominent feature in the subadult (Sub) phase. Given that the plants favored by Père David’s Deer, such as Spartina alterniflora and Phragmites australis, are not particularly high in fat content, this suggests that the observed lipid metabolism characteristics in the subadult phase are not attributable to dietary preferences but rather to physiological fluctuations related to the deer’s activity cycle. Based on these results, it is recommended to introduce oilseed crops like legumes into the activity areas of female Père David’s Deer to better support their physiological needs during the juvenile developmental stage.

In addition to ion and lipid metabolism fluctuations, our integrated multi-omics analysis revealed substantial immune-related molecular changes. Specifically, a significant portion of differentially expressed metabolites and proteins were associated with immune and inflammatory processes, including eicosanoids derived from arachidonic acid metabolism such as prostaglandins (Prostaglandin A1, Prostaglandin A2, Prostaglandin J2, and 15-keto Prostaglandin A1) and thromboxane B1, which are established regulators of inflammation and immune cell recruitment [26, 27]. Dysregulation of lysophospholipids and sphingolipid-related metabolites (LPAs, LPS, sphingosine) was also observed, indicating potential alterations in immune cell signaling and migration [28]. Correspondingly, immune-related proteins such as IL1R2, MASP1, and PGLYRP2, as well as proteasome subunits involved in antigen processing (PSMA2, PSMA6, PSMB2, PSMB3), exhibited differential expression, suggesting coordinated changes in immune surveillance and inflammatory regulation.

At the transcriptomic level, analysis of transcriptomic age revealed that Fawn-stage individuals exhibited significantly lower transcriptomic ages compared to other stages, while later stages (Sub, Adult, Old) were relatively stable. Pathway enrichment analysis of high-weight Fawn-stage genes further indicated prominent activation of immune and inflammation-related transcriptional programs. Moreover, transcriptome deconvolution revealed dynamic shifts in immune cell populations across development, including progressive increases in neutrophils and CD8 T cells with age, a decline in naïve CD4 T cells, and accumulation of regulatory T cells in older individuals, reflecting coordinated remodeling of both innate and adaptive immunity during growth and aging [44]. These findings highlight that immune modulation is a key feature during developmental transitions in Père David’s Deer, likely supporting physiological adaptation and homeostasis.

In addition, the results also revealed a downregulation of the classic FOXO-related signaling axis during the aging process. In mammals, FOXO is the homolog of daf-16 in Caenorhabditis elegans and is linked to stress adaptation. Under stress conditions, FOXO is activated and initiates a transcriptional program to counteract stress and restore homeostasis [45]. Furthermore, FOXO plays a key role in maintaining stem cell function, supporting stable tissue turnover [46]. FOXO enables organisms to cope with adverse conditions, ensuring that cells, organs, and ultimately the organism can recover and maintain internal balance [47]. As an upstream regulator of FOXO, MAPK was also observed to accumulate with age. These findings suggest that the downregulation of the MAPK-FOXO signaling axis contributes to the decline in Père David’s Deer’s ability to withstand stress, triggering aging-related physiological changes.

To date, there have been no publicly available proteomics or metabolomics data for Père David’s Deer. However, changes at the protein level and the subsequent fluctuations in metabolite levels are crucial for understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying physiological activities [48–50]. In this study, we constructed an integrative regulatory network based on correlations among age-dependent genes, differentially expressed proteins, and highly correlated metabolites derived from multi-omics datasets. This network analysis, together with proteomic evidence, suggests that MAPK-related molecules, including CD1, CD34, and CD44, are associated with the MAPK–FOXO signaling axis, indicating its potential role in regulating aging-related processes in Père David’s deer.

Several limitations should also be acknowledged. Proteomic and metabolomic datasets were available only for the Adult and Sub-adult groups, which may limit the resolution of molecular dynamics across the full developmental trajectory. Nevertheless, transcriptomic analyses and PCA results consistently indicate that developmental stage is the primary source of variation in the dataset. Future studies incorporating full-stage multi-omics profiling and larger sample sizes will be essential to further validate and refine these findings.

Conclusions

Long-term studies have characterized relatively stable physiological patterns in Père David’s deer; however, seasonal variation within a single year remains insufficiently explored, despite its potential influence on metabolic and immune regulation. This represents an important knowledge gap, highlighting the need for temporally resolved studies to better support breeding management and conservation strategies. Future work should also extend beyond age and sex dimensions to incorporate environmental and ecological variables, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of physiological adaptation in this species.

Ecological diversity underpins biodiversity, and the reintroduction of extinct species essentially reflects the restoration of ecological structure and function. In this context, genes, proteins, and metabolites serve as molecular links between biodiversity and ecological adaptation. Based on integrative analyses, this study highlights several ecological factors potentially relevant to Père David’s deer habitat, including mineral salt availability, wetland environments, and leguminous forage such as alfalfa, which together may represent key components of their ecological requirements.

This study represents the first integrated multi-omics analysis of transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic profiles in Père David’s deer, providing a systematic view of molecular alterations across developmental stages, sexes, and ages. Our findings reveal coordinated changes in ion metabolism, lipid metabolism, immune regulation, and transcriptomic aging signatures, suggesting tightly coupled regulation of metabolic and immune processes during development and aging. Although these results provide important insights into stage- and sex-dependent molecular dynamics, the study is based on captive individuals; therefore, extrapolation to wild populations should be made cautiously.
