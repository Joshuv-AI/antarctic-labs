Superspecial plane quintics with large automorphism groups
Ryo Ohashi

Abstract
In this paper, we study plane quintic curves whose automorphism groups have order greater than 10, as well as those with cyclic automorphism groups of order 8 and 10. The latter two cases are represented as one-parameter families, where their superspeciality can be explicitly described in terms of a truncation of certain Gaussian hypergeometric series. Applying this characterization, we determine the exact number of isomorphism classes of superspecial plane quintic curves with automorphism groups ≅ ℤ / 10 ​ ℤ \cong\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z} . We also provide an efficient algorithm to enumerate such curves with automorphism groups ≅ ℤ / 8 ​ ℤ \cong\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z} , and provide the computational results for the range 13 < p < 10000 13<p<10000 .

Introduction

Throughout this paper, a
curve
always means a non-singular projective variety of dimension one defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
.
A curve is called
superspecial
if its Jacobian is isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves.
Superspecial curves remain an important topic in number theory

and algebraic geometry, with many open problems regarding their existence and the number of isomorphism classes.
In recent years, they have also attracted attention because of their applications in cryptography and coding theory, since they often have many rational points relative to their genus.

It is well known that every plane quartic curve has genus
3
and is non-hyperelliptic, and conversely, every non-hyperelliptic curve of genus
3
can be realized as a plane quartic curve.
Several studies have investigated the superspeciality of plane quartic curves.
Oort
[
16
, Theorem 5.12(1)]
showed that there exists a superspecial plane quartic curve in any characteristic
p\geq 3
.
His proof focused on a special family of plane quartic curves called
Ciani curves
, whose Jacobians decompose as products of three elliptic curves.
Also, Moriya-Kudo
[
11
]
proposed an algorithm for enumerating superspecial Ciani curves.
Another characterization of a Ciani curve is as a plane quartic curve with an automorphism group containing a subgroup isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
.
The possible automorphism groups of plane quartic curves in characteristic
p\geq 5
are classified into
13
types (the explicit list of these groups is found in
[
3
, Theorem 6.5.2]
).
Building on this classification, Brock
[
2
]
studied superspecial plane quartic curves for each of these types. In particular, the superspeciality for the types with
0
-dimensional families is determined as follows:

•

The Klein quartic
X^{3}Y+Y^{3}Z+Z^{3}X=0
with an automorphism group of order
168
is superspecial if and only if
p\equiv 3,5,6\pmod{7}
.

•

The Fermat quartic
X^{4}+Y^{4}+Z^{4}=0
with an automorphism group of order
96
is superspecial if and only if
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.

•

The plane quartic
X^{4}+Y^{4}+YZ^{3}=0
with an automorphism group of order
48
is superspecial if and only if
p\equiv 11\pmod{12}
.

•

The plane quartic
X^{4}+XY^{3}+YZ^{3}=0
with cyclic automorphism group of order
9
is superspecial if and only if
p\equiv 8\pmod{9}
.

Also, there are
3
types which admit 1-dimensional families.
Among them, those whose automorphism groups have order
24
or
16
are Ciani curves;  their superspeciality can be completely described by the supersingularity of elliptic curves (the explicit equations of them are found in
[
10
, Sections 4–5]
).
The remaining type consists of curves with cyclic automorphism groups of order
6
, for which it is known (cf.
[
15
]
) that there exist exactly
\lfloor p/12\rfloor
superspecial curves if
p\equiv 5\pmod{6}
, whereas no such curves exist if
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
.

This paper focuses on
plane quintic curves
, all of which have genus
6
and are non-hyperelliptic.
Note that, in contrast to plane quartic curves, there exist non-hyperelliptic curves of genus
6
that are not isomorphic to any plane quintic curve (cf.
[
5
, Example IV.5.6]
).
Badr-Bars
[
1
]
classified the possible automorphism groups of plane quintic curves in characteristic
p>13
into
14
types, and gave an explicit defining equation for each type.
Among these
14
types, those with
0
-dimensional families are as follows:

•

The Fermat quintic
X^{5}+Y^{5}+Z^{5}=0
with an automorphism group of order
150
.

•

The Hurwitz quintic
X^{4}Y+Y^{4}Z+Z^{4}X=0
with an automorphism group of order
39
.

•

The plane quintic
X^{5}+Y^{4}Z+YZ^{4}=0
with an automorphism group of order
30
.

•

The plane quintic
X^{5}+Y^{5}+XZ^{4}=0
with cyclic automorphism group of order
20
.

•

The plane quintic
X^{5}+Y^{4}Z+XZ^{4}=0
with cyclic automorphism group of order
16
.

In Section
3.1
, we provide explicit necessary and sufficient conditions on
p
for these curves to be superspecial.
In addition, there are
2
types which admit
1
-dimensional families.
One of them is the family of plane quintics defined by the equation

X^{5}+Y^{5}+XZ^{4}+rX^{3}Z^{2}=0\ \text{ with }\,r\neq 0,\pm 2,\hskip 1.99168ptr^{2}\neq 20\vskip-0.56905pt

which has a cyclic automorphism group of order
10
.
In Section
3.2
, we investigate several properties of such a curve.
In particular, we show that its superspeciality can be described by (a truncation of) a single Gaussian hypergeometric series, which leads to our first main theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

The number of isomorphism classes of superspecial plane quintic curves with cyclic automorphism group of order
10
equals

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(3p-27)/20&\text{ if }p\equiv 9\!\!\pmod{20},\\[-0.56905pt]
(3p-37)/20&\text{ if }p\equiv 19\!\!\pmod{20},\\[-0.56905pt]
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.\vskip-3.41432pt

in characteristic
p>13
.

In Section
3.3
, we study another 1-dimensional family of plane quintics by the equation

X^{5}+Y^{4}Z+XZ^{4}+rX^{3}Z^{2}=0\ \text{ with }\,r\neq 0,\pm 2,\vskip 1.42262pt

which has a cyclic automorphism group of order
8
;  however, unlike the case of order
10
, its superspeciality is characterized by multiple Gaussian hypergeometric series.
Consequently, the number of isomorphism classes of such superspecial curves cannot be expressed by a simple explicit formula like Theorem
1.1
.
We therefore propose an efficient algorithm (Theorem
3.12
) to determine this number in small characteristic
p
.
Executing our algorithm in the range
13<p<10000
, we obtain our second main theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

For each characteristic
p
in the range
13<p<10000
, there are no superspecial plane quintic curves with a cyclic automorphism group of order
8
if
p\equiv 1,3,5\pmod{8}
.
Also, the numbers of isomorphism classes of such curves for
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
are listed in Table
3
.

Our implementation code is available at the following URL:

https://github.com/Ryo-Ohashi/SSpZ8quintic
.

We remark that it took only approximately 36.2 seconds to obtain the result of Theorem
1.2
(see Section
3.3
for details on the computing environment), which suggests that the upper bound for
p
can be easily updated.
The proof of (non)-existence and the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial plane quintic curves with a cyclic automorphism group of order
8
for general characteristics is left for future work.

Organization.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we review the relationship between the superspeciality of specific superelliptic curves and Gaussian hypergeometric series.
In Section
3
, we discuss the superspeciality of plane quintic curves with automorphism groups of order greater than
10
, or cyclic automorphism groups of order

10
.
Finally, we give concluding remarks in Section

.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall several properties of superelliptic curves that will be used in the subsequent section.
Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>2
.
A
superelliptic curve
over
K
is a curve defined by a projective equation

Y^{n}Z^{m-n}=F(X,Z)\ \text{ with }\,n\hskip-0.28453pt\geq 2\,\text{ and }\,p\nmid n,\vskip 1.13809pt

(2.1)

where
F(X,Z)\in K[X,Z]
is a binary form of degree
m\geq 3
with no multiple factors.
The Riemann-Hurwitz formula tells us that the genus of the superelliptic curve (
2.1
) is given by

g=\frac{(m-1)(n-1)-{\rm gcd}(m,n)+1}{2}.

Also, the following is a fundamental proposition:

Proposition 2.1

(cf.
[
10
, Section 8]
)
.

The superelliptic curves
Y^{n}Z^{m-n}=F(X,Z)
and
Y^{n}Z^{m-n}=G(X,Z)
over
K
are isomorphic to each other if and only if
F(X,Z)
and
G(X,Z)
are equivalent under
{\rm GL}_{2}(K)
.

In what follows, by setting
x=X/Z
and
y=Y/Z
, we identify the superelliptic curve (
2.1
) with its affine model
y^{n}=f(x)
, where
f(x)\coloneqq F(x,1)\in K[x]
is a square-free polynomial of degree
m\geq 3
.
Let us describe a necessary and sufficient condition for superelliptic curves to be superspecial:

Theorem 2.2

(cf.
[
2
, Theorem 2.15]
)
.

The superelliptic curve given by
y^{n}=f(x)
is superspecial if and only if the
x^{hp-k}
-coefficients in
f(x)^{(ip-j)/n}
are equal to
0
for all integers
(i,j,h,k)
satisfying

1\leq i,j<n,\quad 1\leq h<mi/n,\quad 1\leq k<mj/n,\ \text{ and }\ n\mid\hskip-0.56905pt(ip-j),\vskip-0.28453pt

where
m\geq 3
is the degree of
f(x)
.

As a corollary of Theorem
2.2
, the superelliptic curve
y^{n}=x^{m}-x
is superspecial if and only if

S\coloneqq\left\{\,(i,j,h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}\ \middle|\begin{array}[]{l}1\leq i,j<n,\ n\mid(ip-j),\\[-0.56905pt]
1\leq h<mi/n,\ 1\leq k<mj/n,\ (m-1)\mid\bigl(hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}\bigr),\\
0\leq hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}\leq(m-1)\frac{ip-j}{n}\end{array}\hskip-2.84526pt\right\}\vskip-0.28453pt

(2.2)

is empty.
Indeed, it follows from the binomial theorem that

\displaystyle(x^{m}-x)^{(ip-j)/n}

\displaystyle=x^{(ip-j)/n}(x^{m-1}-1)^{(ip-j)/n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{(ip-j)/n}\binom{\frac{ip-j}{n}}{\ell}(-1)^{(ip-j)/n-\ell}x^{(ip-j)/n+(m-1)\ell}.

Since
0\leq(ip-j)/n<p
, one has

\binom{\frac{ip-j}{n}}{\ell}(-1)^{(ip-j)/n-\ell}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}\vskip-0.85358pt

for every
0\leq\ell\leq(ip-j)/n
.
Hence, the
x^{hp-k}
-coefficient in
(x^{m}-x)^{(ip-j)/n}
is non-zero if and only if there exists an integer
\ell
such that

hp-k=(ip-j)/n+(m-1)\ell\ \text{ with }\ 0\leq\ell\leq(ip-j)/n.\vskip 1.13809pt

This condition is equivalent to

(m-1)\mid\Bigl(hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}\Bigr)\ \text{ and }\ 0\leq hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}\leq(m-1)\frac{ip-j}{n},

as desired.
Here, the following sufficient conditions for
S
to be empty were given in
[
2
, Corollary 2.16]
.

Corollary 2.3

(cf.
[
2
, Corollary 2.16]
)
.

The set
S
in (
2.2
) is empty if one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

p\equiv-1\pmod{(m-1)n}
, or

(ii)

p\equiv m\pmod{(m-1)n}
when
n
is a divisor of
m+1
.

Consequently, the superelliptic curve
y^{n}=x^{m}-x
is superspecial in each of these cases.

Next, we fix a pair
(n,r)
of positive integers where
n\geq 2
and
p\nmid n
.
Consider the following 1-dimensional family of superelliptic curves defined by the equation

C_{\lambda}:y^{n}=x(x^{r}-1)(x^{r}-\lambda)\ \text{ with }\,\lambda\neq 0,1,\vskip-1.42262pt

(2.3)

and the following set

T\coloneqq\left\{\,(i,j,h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}\ \middle|\begin{array}[]{l}1\leq i,j<n,\ n\mid(ip-j),\\[-0.56905pt]
1\leq h<(2r+1)i/n,\ 1\leq k<(2r+1)j/n,\ r\mid\bigl(hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}\bigr),\\
0\leq hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}\leq 2r\frac{ip-j}{n}\end{array}\hskip-2.84526pt\right\}.\vskip-1.13809pt

(2.4)

Then, the superspeciality of
C_{\lambda}
can be described in terms of Gaussian hypergeometric series as follows:

Theorem 2.4
.

The superelliptic curve
C_{\lambda}
in (
2.3
) is superspecial if and only if the following conditions hold for every
4
-tuple
(i,j,h,k)\in T
, where
T
is the set defined in (
2.4
):

•

If
hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}\leq r\frac{ip-j}{n}
, then

G^{((hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n})/r)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{j}{n},\hskip 0.85358pt\frac{k}{r}-\frac{j}{nr},\hskip 1.42262pt1-\frac{j}{n}+\frac{k}{r}-\frac{j}{nr}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0.\vskip-4.2679pt

•

If
hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}>r\frac{ip-j}{n}
, then

G^{(2\frac{ip-j}{n}-(hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n})/r)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{j}{n},-\frac{k}{r}+\frac{2j}{n}+\frac{j}{nr},\hskip 1.42262pt1+\frac{j}{n}-\frac{k}{r}+\frac{j}{nr}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0.\vskip-6.25958pt

Here,
G^{(d)}(a,b,c\mid z)
denotes the truncated Gaussian hypergeometric series defined in
[
14
, Definition 2.1.2]
, namely, we write

G^{(d)}(a,b,c\mid z)\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}\frac{(a\,;\ell)(b\,;\ell)}{(c\,;\ell)(1\,;\ell)}z^{\ell}\vskip 4.83694pt

(2.5)

where
(x\,;0)=1
and
(x\,;\ell)=x(x+1)\cdots(x+\ell-1)
for an integer
\ell\geq 1
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
2.2
that
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if, for all integers
(i,j,h,k)
satisfying the conditions

1\leq i,j<n,\quad 1\leq h<(2r+1)i/n,\quad 1\leq k<(2r+1)j/n,\ \text{ and }\ n\mid\hskip-0.56905pt(ip-j),\vskip 2.27621pt

(2.6)

the
x^{hp-k}
-coefficients in
\{x(x^{r}-1)(x^{r}-\lambda)\}^{(ip-j)/n}
are equal to
0
.
Since

\displaystyle(x^{r}-1)^{(ip-j)/n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{(ip-j)/n}\binom{(ip-j)/n}{\ell_{1}}(-1)^{(ip-j)/n-\ell_{1}}x^{r\ell_{1}},\,\text{ and}

\displaystyle(x^{r}-\lambda)^{(ip-j)/n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell_{2}=0}^{(ip-j)/n}\binom{(ip-j)/n}{\ell_{2}}(-\lambda)^{(ip-j)/n-\ell_{2}}x^{r\ell_{2}}

by the binomial theorem, one can expand
\{x(x^{r}-1)(x^{r}-\lambda)\}^{(ip-j)/n}
as

\sum_{\ell=0}^{2(ip-j)/n}\hskip-1.13809pt\Biggl(\hskip 1.42262pt\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell}\binom{(ip-j)/n}{\ell_{1}}\!\binom{(ip-j)/n}{\ell_{2}}(-1)^{(ip-j)/n-\ell_{1}}(-\lambda)^{(ip-j)/n-\ell_{2}}\hskip-1.42262pt\Biggr)x^{(ip-j)/n+r\ell}.\vskip-0.85358pt

(2.7)

Hence, for integers
(i,j,h,k)
satisfying (
2.6
), the
x^{hp-k}
-coefficient in
\{x(x^{r}-1)(x^{r}-\lambda)\}^{(ip-j)/n}
is non-zero
only if
there exists an integer
\ell
such that

hp-k=(ip-j)/n+r\ell\ \text{ with }\ 0\leq\ell\leq 2(ip-j)/n,\vskip-0.85358pt

which is equivalent to

r\mid\Bigl(hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}\Bigr)\text{ and }0\leq hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}\leq 2r\frac{ip-j}{n}.\vskip 1.13809pt

This is precisely the condition appearing in the definition of
T
.

For each
(i,j,h,k)\in T
, up to a sign, the
x^{hp-k}
-coefficient in (
2.7
) is given by

\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=(hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n})/r}\!\binom{(ip-j)/n}{\ell_{1}}\!\binom{(ip-j)/n}{\ell_{2}}\lambda^{(ip-j)/n-\ell_{2}}=\sum_{\ell_{1}=\max(0,b-a)}^{\min(a,b)}\!\binom{a}{\ell_{1}}\!\binom{a}{b-\ell_{1}}\lambda^{a-b+\ell_{1}},\vskip-1.70717pt

where we define

a\coloneqq\frac{ip-j}{n}\ \text{ and }\ b\coloneqq\frac{hp-k-\frac{ip-j}{n}}{r}.\vskip 2.27621pt

We divide the proof into two cases according to whether
b\leq a
or
b>a
:

•

If
b\leq a
, then a straightforward computation (cf.
[
14
, Lemma A.1]
) leads to

\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{b}\!\binom{a}{\ell_{1}}\!\binom{a}{b-\ell_{1}}\lambda^{a-b+\ell_{1}}=\lambda^{a-b}\cdot\binom{a}{b}G^{(b)}(-a,-b,1+a-b\mid\lambda).\vskip-3.1298pt

Also, we have

-a\equiv\frac{j}{n},\quad-b\equiv\frac{k}{r}-\frac{j}{nr},\ \text{ and }\ 1+a-b\equiv 1-\frac{j}{n}+\frac{k}{r}-\frac{j}{nr}\vskip 2.56073pt

modulo
p
, and thus the
x^{hp-k}
-coefficient in (
2.7
) vanishes if and only if

G^{(b)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{j}{n},\hskip 0.85358pt\frac{k}{r}-\frac{j}{nr},\hskip 1.42262pt1-\frac{j}{n}+\frac{k}{r}-\frac{j}{nr}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0\vskip-1.13809pt

since
b\leq a<p
and
\lambda\neq 0
.

•

If
b>a
, then a straightforward computation (cf.
[
14
, Lemma A.1]
) leads to

\sum_{\ell_{1}=b-a}^{a}\!\binom{a}{\ell_{1}}\!\binom{a}{b-\ell_{1}}\lambda^{a-b+\ell_{1}}=\binom{a}{2a-b}G^{(2a-b)}(-a,b-2a,1+b-a\mid\lambda).\vskip-2.27621pt

Also, we have

-a\equiv\frac{j}{n},\quad b-2a\equiv-\frac{k}{r}+\frac{2j}{n}+\frac{j}{nr},\ \text{ and }\ 1+b-a\equiv 1+\frac{j}{n}-\frac{k}{r}+\frac{j}{nr}

modulo
p
, and thus the
x^{hp-k}
-coefficient in (
2.7
) vanishes if and only if

G^{(2a-b)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{j}{n},-\frac{k}{r}+\frac{2j}{n}+\frac{j}{nr},\hskip 1.42262pt1+\frac{j}{n}-\frac{k}{r}+\frac{j}{nr}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0\vskip-2.56073pt

since
2a-b<a<p
.

Combining these two cases, we obtain the desired assertion.

∎

Proposition 3.1
.

The following statements hold:

(1)

The Fermat quintic

Proof.

3.2

In this subsection, let us consider a plane quintic curve with an automorphism group containing a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}
.
Such a curve can be written as

C:X^{5}+Y^{5}+XZ^{4}+rX^{3}Z^{2}=0\ \text{ with }\ r\neq\pm 2.

(3.2)

by
[
1
, Proposition 8]
.
We note that the constraint
r\neq\pm 2
is imposed so that
C
is a curve (i.e., non-singular).
In addition, the automorphism group of
C
can be described as follows:

•

If
r^{2}=20
, then the automorphism group of
C
has order
150
.

•

If
r=0
, then the automorphism group of
C
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/20\mathbb{Z}
.

•

Otherwise, the automorphism group of
C
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}
.

We begin by summarizing some basic properties of such curves:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
C^{\prime}
be another plane quintic curve defined by
X^{5}+Y^{5}+XZ^{4}+r^{\prime}X^{3}Z^{2}=0
with
r^{\prime}\neq\pm 2
.
Then, the curve
C
in (
3.2
) is isomorphic to
C^{\prime}
if and only if
r^{2}=r^{\prime 2}
.

Proof.

Let

F(X,Z)\coloneqq X^{5}+rX^{3}Z^{2}+XZ^{4}\ \text{ and }\ G(X,Z)\coloneqq X^{5}+r^{\prime}X^{3}Z^{2}+XZ^{4}\vskip 1.99168pt

be binary forms of degree
5
in
K[X,Z]
.
It is clear that
C
is isomorphic to the curve defined by
Y^{5}=F(X,Z)
and that
C^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the curve defined by
Y^{5}=G(X,Z)
.
By Proposition
2.1
, they are isomorphic to

each other if and only if
F(X,Z)
and
G(X,Z)
are equivalent under
{\rm GL}_{2}(K)
.
Rewriting

\displaystyle F(X,Z)

\displaystyle=X(X^{2}-\alpha Z^{2})(X^{2}-\beta Z^{2}),

\displaystyle\alpha+\beta=-r,

\displaystyle\alpha\beta=1,

\displaystyle G(X,Z)

\displaystyle=X(X^{2}-\alpha^{\prime}\hskip-0.85358ptZ^{2})(X^{2}-\beta^{\prime}\hskip-0.85358ptZ^{2}),

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}\hskip-0.85358pt+\beta^{\prime}\hskip-0.85358pt=-r^{\prime},

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}\hskip-0.85358pt\beta^{\prime}\hskip-0.85358pt=1,

it follows from
[
7
, Corollary 2.1.1]
that
F(X,Z)
and
G(X,Z)
are equivalent under
{\rm GL}_{2}(K)
if and only if

\frac{\alpha}{\beta}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\beta^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\alpha^{\prime}}.\vskip-1.99168pt

By using the relations
\alpha+\beta=1,\alpha\beta=1
, this condition is equivalent to

r^{2}-2=\frac{(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta}=\frac{(\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime})^{2}-2\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}=r^{\prime 2}-2,

which simplifies to
r^{2}=r^{\prime 2}
, as desired.

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

If the plane quintic curve
C
in (
3.2
) is superspecial, then
r^{2}
belongs to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}

Proof.

Suppose that

is superspecial.
As is well known (cf.

), any superspecial curve admits a model defined over

Hence

is isomorphic to its

By Lemma
3.3
, this implies that
r^{2}=(r^{p^{2}})^{2}
.
It follows that
(r^{2})^{p^{2}}=r^{2}
, and thus

.
∎

Now, fix a root

The following lemma collects some properties of the polynomial

Lemma 3.6

If

(1) By definition, the degree of

•

r=0
, then the automorphism group of
C
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}
.

•

Otherwise, the automorphism group of
C
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}
.

A necessary and sufficient condition for such curves to be isomorphic is given as follows:

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
C^{\prime}
be another plane quintic curve defined by
\hskip-0.56905ptX^{5}+Y^{4}Z+XZ^{4}+r^{\prime}\hskip-0.56905ptX^{3}Z^{2}=0
with
r^{\prime}\neq\pm 2
.
Then, the curve
C
in (
3.7
) is isomorphic to
C^{\prime}
if and only if
r^{2}=r^{\prime 2}
.

Proof.

We see that
C
(resp.
C^{\prime}
) is isomorphic to the curve defined by
Y^{4}Z=F(X,Z)
(resp.
Y^{4}Z=G(X,Z)
) where

F(X,Z)\coloneqq X^{5}+rX^{3}Z^{2}+XZ^{4}\ \text{ and }\ G(X,Z)\coloneqq X^{5}+r^{\prime}X^{3}Z^{2}+XZ^{4}\vskip 2.56073pt

are binary forms of degree
5
in
K[X,Z]
.
Hence, it follows from Proposition
2.1
that
C
and
C^{\prime}
are isomorphic to each other if and only if
F(X,Z)
and
G(X,Z)
are equivalent under
{\rm GL}_{2}(K)
.
The remainder of the proof is exactly the same as that of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

By the above lemma, one can show, similarly to Corollary
3.4
, that if the curve
C
in (
3.7
) is superspecial, then
r^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
On the other hand, we can prove the following stronger statement:

Proposition 3.9
.

If the plane quintic curve
C
in (
3.7
) is superspecial, then
r+2
and
r-2
are both squares in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. In particular,
r
belongs to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Proof.

We fix a root
t\neq 0
of the equation
t^{16}+rt^{8}+1=0
.
Then, the curve
C
in (
3.7
) is isomorphic to the superelliptic curve

C_{\lambda}:y^{4}=x(x^{2}-1)(x^{2}-\lambda)\ \text{ with }\,\lambda\coloneqq t^{16}\vskip 3.41432pt

(3.8)

via the map
(X:Y:Z)=(-tx:y:t^{5})
.
This curve admits the involution
(x,y)\mapsto\hskip-0.85358pt(x,-y)
, whose quotient curve is the genus-2 curve

H:v^{2}=u(u^{2}-1)(u^{2}-\lambda)\vskip 2.84526pt

(3.9)

where
u\coloneqq x,\,v\coloneqq y^{2}
.
It follows from
[
8
, Corollary 2.8]
that, if
C
is superspecial, then
H
is also superspecial.
By
[
12
, Main Theorem A(1)]
, the superspeciality of
H
implies that
\hskip-0.56905pt\sqrt{\lambda}=t^{8}
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.56905pt
, which means that
t^{4}
belongs to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
Therefore, the values

r\pm 2=-(t^{8}\mp 2+t^{-8})=-(t^{4}\mp t^{-4})^{2}

are squares in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, since
-1
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
∎

In the following, instead of working with the plane quintic curve
C
in (
3.7
), we consider the superelliptic curve
C_{\lambda}
, which was constructed in the proof of Proposition
3.9
.
Since
r=-(t^{8}+t^{-8})
and
\lambda=t^{16}
, we have the important relation

\lambda+\frac{1}{\lambda}=r^{2}\hskip-0.85358pt-2.

(3.10)

Note that
\lambda\neq 0
since
t\neq 0
, and that
\lambda\neq 1
follows from the assumption
r\neq\pm 2
.
The following proposition characterizes the superspeciality of
C_{\lambda}
in terms of Gaussian hypergeometric series:

Proposition 3.10
.

For the superelliptic curve
C_{\lambda}
in (
3.8
), the following statements hold:

(1)

If
p\equiv 1\pmod{8}
, then
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if

\displaystyle G^{((p-1)/4)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-1)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{7}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((p-1)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{3}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((3p-3)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{8},\frac{5}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-9)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{9}{8},\frac{11}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((3p-11)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{11}{8},\frac{13}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0.

(2)

If
p\equiv 3\pmod{8}
, then
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if

\displaystyle G^{((p-3)/4)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((3p-1)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{7}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((p-3)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-3)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{8},\frac{5}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-11)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{11}{8},\frac{13}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0.

(3)

If
p\equiv 5\pmod{8}
, then
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if

\displaystyle G^{((p-1)/4)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((5p-1)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{3}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((p-5)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{8},\frac{7}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((3p-7)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{7}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((5p-9)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{9}{8},\frac{11}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0.

(4)

If
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
, then
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if

G^{((p-3)/4)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((p-7)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{7}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0.\vskip-6.54413pt

Proof.

(1) If
p\equiv 1\pmod{8}
, then we see that the set
T
in (
2.4
) is given by

T=\{(1,1,1,1),(2,2,1,1),(2,2,2,2),(3,3,1,1),(3,3,1,3),(3,3,2,2),(3,3,3,1),(3,3,3,3)\}.

Therefore, it follows from Theorem
2.4
that
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if

\displaystyle G^{((p-1)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{7}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-1)/4)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-1)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{3}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-9)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{9}{8},\frac{11}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((5p-5)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{8},\frac{7}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((3p-11)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{11}{8},\frac{13}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((3p-3)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{8},\frac{5}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0.

Note that both
(2,2,1,1)
and
(2,2,2,2)
in
T
determine the second polynomial of the above equalities.
Also, a straightforward computation with Euler’s transformation formula yields

G^{((5p-5)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{8},\frac{7}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=(1-\lambda)^{(p-1)/2}G^{((p-1)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{7}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$},\vskip-3.1298pt

and hence, the first and fifth polynomial of the above equalities have the same roots since
\lambda\neq 1
.

(2) If
p\equiv 3\pmod{8}
, then we see that the set
T
in (
2.4
) is given by

T=\{(1,3,1,1),(1,3,1,3),(2,2,1,2),(2,2,2,1),(3,1,1,1),(3,1,3,1)\}.\vskip-0.28453pt

Therefore, it follows from Theorem
2.4
that
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if

\displaystyle G^{((p-11)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{11}{8},\frac{13}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-3)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{8},\frac{5}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((p-3)/4)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-3)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((3p-1)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{7}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0.

Note that both
(2,2,1,2)
and
(2,2,2,1)
in
T
determine the third polynomial of the above equalities.

(3) If
p\equiv 5\pmod{8}
, then we see that the set
T
in (
2.4
) is given by

T=\{(2,2,1,1),(2,2,2,2),(3,3,1,2),(3,3,2,1),(3,3,2,3),(3,3,3,2)\}.\vskip-0.56905pt

Therefore, it follows from Theorem
2.4
that
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if

\displaystyle G^{((p-1)/4)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((p-5)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{8},\frac{7}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((5p-1)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{1}{8},\frac{3}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((5p-9)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{9}{8},\frac{11}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}

\displaystyle=G^{((3p-7)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{7}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=0.

Note that both
(2,2,1,1)
and
(2,2,2,2)
in
T
determine the first polynomial of the above equalities.

(4) If
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
, then we see that the set
T
in (
2.4
) is given by

T=\{(1,3,1,2),(2,2,1,2),(2,2,2,1),(3,1,2,1)\}.\vskip-0.28453pt

Therefore, it follows from Theorem
2.4
that
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if

G^{((p-7)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{7}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((p-3)/4)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=G^{((5p-3)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}.\vskip-2.84526pt

Note that both
(2,2,1,2)
and
(2,2,2,1)
in
T
determine the second polynomial of the above equalities.
Also, a straightforward computation with Euler’s transformation formula yields

G^{((5p-3)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}=(1-\lambda)^{(p+1)/2}G^{((p-7)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{7}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$},\vskip-3.1298pt

and hence, the first and third polynomial of the above equalities have the same roots since
\lambda\neq 1
.

∎

Remark 3.11
.

Applying Proposition
3.10
for the case
(n,r)=(2,2)
, we see that the hyperelliptic curve
H
in (
3.9
) is superspecial if and only if
h(\lambda)=0
, where

h(\lambda)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}G^{((p-1)/4)}(1/2,1/4,3/4\mid\lambda)&\text{if }\,p\equiv 1\!\!\pmod{4},\\
G^{((p-3)/4)}(1/2,3/4,5/4\mid\lambda)&\text{if }\,p\equiv 3\!\!\pmod{4}.\end{array}\right.\vskip-1.99168pt

(3.11)

This coincides with the condition that the first polynomial vanishes in Proposition
3.10
for each case.

Let
G(\lambda)
be the gcd of polynomials in Proposition
3.10
for each case. For example, we define

G(\lambda)\coloneqq{\rm gcd}\biggl(G^{((p-3)/4)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$},\,G^{((p-7)/8)}\scalebox{0.95}{$\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{7}{8},\frac{9}{8}\ \middle|\ \lambda\right)$}\biggr)\vskip-0.56905pt

if
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
.
This polynomial is separable and has no roots equal to
0
or
1
, since
h(\lambda)
defined in (
3.11
) is separable and has no roots equal to
0
or
1
by
[
6
, Proposition 1.14]
.
Hence, we have the following theorem as an analogy of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
G(\lambda)
be the gcd of polynomials in Proposition
3.10
for each case.
Then, the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial plane quintic curves with cyclic automorphism group of order
8
equals

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hskip-0.85358pt\frac{1}{2}(\deg{G(\lambda)}-1)&\text{ if }p\equiv 15\!\!\pmod{16},\\[1.42262pt]
\hskip-0.85358pt\frac{1}{2}\hskip-0.85358pt\deg{G(\lambda)}&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.\vskip-2.84526pt

in characteristic
p>13
.

Proof.

Recall that every plane quintic curve with an automorphism group containing a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}
is isomorphic to
C_{\lambda}
in (
3.8
) for some
\lambda\neq 0,1
.
By the definition of
G(\lambda)
, the curve
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if
G(\lambda)=0
.
Since
G(\lambda)
is separable and has no roots equal to
0
or
1
, the number of
\lambda\neq 0,1
for which
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial and
{\rm Aut}(C_{\lambda})\cong\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}
is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hskip-0.85358pt\deg{G(\lambda)}-1&\text{ if }p\equiv 15\!\!\pmod{16},\\[-0.56905pt]
\hskip-0.85358pt\deg{G(\lambda)}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.\vskip-2.84526pt

(3.12)

Indeed, by the discussion at the beginning of Section
3.3
and the relation (
3.8
),

•

If
\lambda=-1
, then the automorphism group of
C_{\lambda}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}
.
In this case
C_{\lambda}
corresponds to the plane quintic of Type
5
, which is superspecial if and only if
p\equiv 15\pmod{16}
by Proposition
3.2
(3).

•

Otherwise, the automorphism group of
C_{\lambda}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}
.

By Lemma
3.8
and the relation (
3.8
), we have
C_{\lambda}\cong C_{\lambda^{\prime}}
if and only if
\lambda^{\prime}\in\{\lambda,1/\lambda\}
.
Therefore, the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial curves
C_{\lambda}
with
{\rm Aut}(C_{\lambda})\cong\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}
is half of (
3.12
), as desired.
∎
