tropical covers, tropical abelian varieties and Prym varieties
Abolfazl Mohajer

Abstract
. We define and investigate the tropical Prym varieties associated to unramified Galois cyclic covers of tropical curves (or equivalently metric graphs) Γ ~ → Γ \tilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma . Our approach here is to study the tropical Prym varieties using group actions on tropical abelian varieties induced by the cyclic Galois group of the cover of tropical curves. We also define and consider the Abel-Prym map for tropical cyclic covers extending that for double covers. As a special case we consider free ℤ 3 {\mathbb{Z}}_{3} -covers of tropical curves and their associated tropical Prym variety and compute its volume generalizing the case of double covers.

1.
introduction

It is well-known that if
X
is a Riemann surface and
B\subset X
is a finite set, then there is a unique cover
Y\to X
ramified exactly over
B
with prescribed monodromy. GIven a finite group
G

X

G

for some

[
7
]
.

The tropical counterpart of a non-constant holomorphic map of Riemann surfaces is a finite harmonic map of metric graphs (equivalently tropical curves). This need not be a topological cover, indeed harmonic maps can dilate the edges of the graph, namely restricted to an edge
e
identified with an interval
[0,a]
, it is given by a map
x\mapsto dx
. Associated to a cover of tropical curves
Y\to X
, there is a Prym variety analogous to the Prym variety obtained by covers of Riemann surfaces. The tropical Prym variety is an abelian tropical variety. The classical Prym variety is an isogeny factor of the Jacobian variety of
Y
and has many properties that can be controlled through the covering map. Our aim in this paper is to establish similar properties for the Prym variety associated to tropical curves or equivalently metric graphs, the so-called tropical Prym variety of the tropical cover.

In the next section, we introduce harmonic morphisms of metric graphs and explore properties of harmonic morphisms and consider Galois covers of tropical curves (metric graphs).

In Section 3, we consider tropical abelian varieties and group actions on them. This section paves the way for Section 4 in which we introduce the tropical Prym variety by using the group action of the Galois group of tropical Galois cover
\varphi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
on the tropical Jacobian
\jac(\widetilde{\Gamma})

\widetilde{\Gamma}\to P
for
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
-Galois cover of tropical covers. This generalizes the Abel-Prym map fro double covers considered in
[
8
]

In particular we consider triple covers

Let

A continuous map

\varphi

d_{\widetilde{e}}(\varphi)>0

\widetilde{e}

\widetilde{\Gamma}

Let

Further let

d_{\widetilde{x},v}(\varphi)=\displaystyle\sum_{\widetilde{v}\in\varphi^{-1}(v)\cap T_{\widetilde{x}}(\widetilde{\Gamma})}d_{\widetilde{v}}(\varphi)

A finite morphism

A free harmonic morphism is a covering space in the topological sense that preserves adjacency meaning that it is an isomorphism in the neighbourhood of every vertex of

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

be an unramified

-cover of metris graphs with

(1)

(2)

If

consists of points of genus
\geq\frac{1}{2}\deg_{\gamma(\varphi)}-(d_{\widetilde{x}}-1)
.

Here

\gamma(\varphi)
.

Proof.

By the fact that the genus function of

is trivial, using the ramification formula we obtain

2g(\widetilde{x})+2(s-1)=\displaystyle\sum_{\widetilde{v}\in T_{\widetilde{x}}(\widetilde{\Gamma})}(d_{\widetilde{v}}(\varphi)-1),

where

. Therefore if

, then the above equality implies that

which is not possible. Therefore if

One of the most useful properties of Galois covers of algebraic curves is that in such covers, the ramification indices of ramification points above a given branch point are equal. The next definition gives such condition for covers of metric graphs.

Definition 2.7
.

An unramified
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
-cover of metric graphs

is called strongly unramified if for every

\widetilde{x}_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2}\in\varphi^{-1}(x)

. We therefore denote this degree by

. If
\varphi

\deg(\varphi)
is a multiple of

. In particular, if
\deg(\varphi)=p
is a prime number, then each

is equal to 1 or

.

Remark 2.8
.

Note that by Lemma 5.4 of
[

]
, any harmonic double cover is strongly unramified.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\varphi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a strongly unramified
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
-cover of metris graphs with

unaugmented, then

(1)

If

then
\varphi^{-1}(x)
consists of precisely
n
points of genus 0.

(2)

If

then
\varphi^{-1}(x)
consists of

points of genus

\geq\frac{1}{2}\deg_{\gamma(\varphi)}-(d_{x}-1)

3.
tropical abelian varieties with group action

In this section we consider tropical abelian varieties and group actions on them. This will be used in the next section to introduce the tropical Prym variety of tropical Galois covers
\varphi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
by considering the group action on the tropical Jacobian
\jac(\widetilde{\Gamma})
.

Definition 3.1
.

A real torus is a quotient
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}/\Lambda
, where
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a lattice in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of rank
n
. A polarization
Q
on a reall torus is a
\Lambda\to({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})^{*}
. A reall torus is called a tropical abelian variety if
Q
induces a positive definite and symmetric bilinear form. The polarization is called principal if it induces an isomorphism from
\Lambda
to
({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})^{*}
. We sometimes denote a principally polarized tropical abelian variety (pptav) as a pair
({\mathbb{R}}^{n}/\Lambda,Q)
.

Definition 3.2
.

Two tropical abelian varieties
({\mathbb{R}}^{n}/\Lambda,Q)
and
({\mathbb{R}}^{n}/\Lambda^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
are isomorphic if there exists
h\in\gl(n,{\mathbb{R}})
such that
h(\Lambda)=\Lambda^{\prime}
and
hQh^{t}=Q^{\prime}
. An automorphism of a pptav
A=({\mathbb{R}}^{n}/\Lambda,Q)
is given by an element
h\in\gl(n,{\mathbb{R}})
such that
h(\Lambda)=\Lambda
and
hQh^{t}=Q
, i.e., an element
h
which fixes the lattice and the positive definite quadratic form
Q
. The set of all automorphisms of a pptav forms a group under composition (equivalently matrix multiplication) which we denote by
\aut(A)
.

Remark 3.3
.

Every tropical abelian variety
({\mathbb{R}}^{n}/\Lambda,Q)
can be written in the form
({\mathbb{R}}^{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},Q^{\prime})
, where we set
Q^{\prime}=hQh^{t}
, and
h\in\gl(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is such that
h(\Lambda)={\mathbb{Z}}^{g}
. From now on we therefore consider the tropical abelian varieties in the form
({\mathbb{R}}^{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},Q)
, where
Q
is uniquely determined up to arithmetic equivalence i.e., up to the identification
Q\sim hQh^{t}
for some
h\in\gl(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
A=({\mathbb{R}}^{g}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{g},Q)
be a pptav and
G
a finite gorpup. We say that
G
acts on
A
if
G
admits a group monomorphism
G\hookrightarrow\aut(A)
.

The Dedekind zeta function of a number field is defined for complex numbers
s

by the Dirichlet series. The gtraph-theoretic counterpart for graphs
\Gamma
using an Euler product over equivalence classes of closed paths on
\Gamma
. Here we recall some elementary defintions and properties that we will use in this section.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph with
n

m
edges and genus
g
. A path
P
of length
k
is a sequence
P=e_{1}\cdots e_{k}
of oriented edges of
\Gamma
such that
t(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for
i=1,\ldots,k-1
. The path
P
is called

if
t(e_{k})=s(e_{1})
and

if
e_{i+1}\neq\overline{e}_{i}
for

and
e_{1}\neq\overline{e}_{k}
. The inetegr powers of closed paths are defined by concatenation, and a closed reduces path
P
is called
primitive
if it cannot be written in the form
P=Q^{k}
for some closed path
Q
and
k\geq 2
. Two reduced paths are considered equivalent if they differ by a choice of starting point; that is, we set
e_{1}\cdots e_{k}\equiv e_{j}\cdots e_{k}e_{1}\cdots e_{k}{j-1}
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
. A
prime

P
of
\Gamma
is an equivalent class of primitve paths and has a well-defined length
\ell(P)
. The Ihara zeta function
\zeta(s,\Gamma)
of a graph
\Gamma
is the product

\zeta(s,\Gamma)=\displaystyle\prod_{p}(1-s^{\ell(P)})^{-1}

We have the following three-term formula due to Bass

\zeta(s,\Gamma)^{-1}=(1-s^{2})^{g-1}\det(I_{n}-As+(Q-I_{n})s^{2})

This function vanishes at
s=1

and it follows from the main result of
[

]

\Gamma

\zeta(s,\Gamma)^{-1}=(-1)^{g-1}2^{g}(g-1)|\jac(\Gamma)|(s-1)^{g}+O((s-1)^{g+1})

Now let
\varphi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
