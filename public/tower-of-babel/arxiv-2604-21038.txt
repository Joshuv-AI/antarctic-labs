Two-Way Feedback Mechanisms between the Madden-Julian Oscillation and Mesoscale Convective Systems


Abstract
The Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO) is a planetary-scale convective system characterized by large-scale envelopes of enhanced and suppressed convection that contain numerous mesoscale convective systems (MCSs). While MCSs are widely recognized as the fundamental convective elements embedded within the MJO, their relationship with the MJO is intrinsically two-way: the MJO modulates the large-scale dynamical and thermodynamic environment that organizes MCS activity, while the collective upscale impacts of MCSs feed back onto the MJO through the transport of momentum and heat. However, the nature of this bidirectional interaction remains insufficiently quantified from an observational perspective. In this study, we use satellite-based MJO indices together with a long-term, objectively tracked MCS dataset to investigate the two-way feedback mechanisms between the MJO and MCSs. By compositing MCS activity across different MJO phases and analyzing their environmental conditions, we quantify how the evolving MJO circulation regulates MCS frequency, intensity, and organization. At the same time, we diagnose the aggregate influence of MCS populations on the large-scale MJO circulation through their associated momentum and thermodynamic anomalies. Our results reveal a robust two-way coupling between the MJO and MCSs. Enhanced MCS activity preferentially occurs in specific MJO phases associated with favorable moisture, instability, and vertical shear, indicating strong MJO control on MCS organization. Conversely, periods of enhanced MCS activity are associated with coherent large-scale circulation anomalies consistent with upscale transport of momentum and moisture that reinforce the MJO convective envelope and support its eastward propagation. This feedback suggests that MCSs are not merely passive responses to the MJO environment, but actively contribute to its maintenance and evolution. These findings highlight the importance of treating the MJO–MCS relationship as a coupled, two-way interaction across scales, providing an observational basis for understanding how mesoscale convective organization contributes to intraseasonal variability in the tropics.

The Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO) strongly influences global weather and climate variability, yet current understanding of how it interacts with embedded mesoscale convective systems (MCSs) remains incomplete. Using long-term satellite-based MJO indices and objectively tracked MCS data, this study quantifies the two-way feedback between the MJO and MCSs from observations. We show that the MJO systematically organizes MCS activity through large-scale moisture, instability, and wind shear conditions, while aggregated MCS activity in turn feeds back onto the MJO circulation through coherent large-scale anomalies consistent with upscale transport processes. These results demonstrate that MCSs are not merely passive responses to the MJO but actively participate in its evolution. The findings provide an observational basis for improving our conceptual understanding of multiscale tropical convection and its role in intraseasonal variability.

We systematically examine the modulation of MCS properties by the MJO and diagnose the corresponding large-scale circulation anomalies associated with MCS activity. By analyzing these coupled relationships across MJO phases, we aim to quantify the two-way feedback mechanisms between the MJO and MCSs from an observational perspective and to provide a clearer picture of their multiscale interaction.

2
Methodology

We use observational datasets to independently characterize the evolution of the Madden Julian Oscillation and mesoscale convective systems and then examine their statistical relationships. The MJO is identified using a standard real time multivariate MJO index based on large scale circulation and outgoing longwave radiation anomalies, which provides a phase based description of its propagation and amplitude. MCSs are tracked using a satellite based infrared brightness temperature tracking algorithm that objectively identifies convective clusters and follows their life cycles in time and space. This dataset provides information on MCS frequency, intensity, and spatial organization across the tropics.

To quantify MJO MCS interactions, we construct phase composites of MCS properties as a function of MJO phase and amplitude, including MCS occurrence, precipitation characteristics, and spatial distribution. We further examine associated large scale circulation and thermodynamic anomalies conditioned on MCS activity to assess the feedback from aggregated MCS populations onto the MJO environment. All analyses are performed on daily to intraseasonal time scales, with filtering applied to isolate variability associated with the MJO. This framework provides a consistent observational basis for diagnosing two way statistical relationships between the MJO and MCSs.

We first examine the large scale structure and phase evolution of the Madden Julian Oscillation using composite analysis of the MJO index. As shown in Fig.

The horizontal structure evolves systematically across phases, with convective anomalies initiating over the Indian Ocean and propagating into the Maritime Continent and western Pacific. The accompanying circulation fields show coherent transitions in zonal wind and moisture convergence, consistent with the canonical view of the MJO as a coupled convective dynamical system. The phase resolved horizontal profiles further highlight the gradual reorganization of convection and large scale circulation, emphasizing the continuous nature of MJO propagation rather than discrete events.

We next quantify the contribution of mesoscale convective systems to total MJO precipitation. Figure
2
shows that MCSs account for a dominant fraction of tropical precipitation within the MJO envelope. This fraction is not constant but varies systematically with MJO phase, reaching its maximum during active convective phases and decreasing substantially during suppressed phases. This result indicates that the MJO does not merely modulate total precipitation but also reorganizes the relative importance of organized mesoscale systems within the convective spectrum. The enhanced contribution of MCSs during active phases suggests that organized convection becomes the primary rainfall mode when large scale environmental conditions are favorable, reinforcing the hierarchical nature of tropical convection.

To further explore this modulation, Fig.

compares key MCS characteristics between active and suppressed MJO phases. A robust contrast is evident across multiple metrics. MCS frequency increases markedly during active phases, indicating more frequent initiation of organized convective systems. In addition, MCS intensity and size also increase, suggesting that the MJO environment not only triggers more systems but also supports their growth and longevity. These changes point to a systematic enhancement of convective organization under favorable large scale conditions, likely associated with increased moisture, instability, and vertical shear within the active MJO envelope.

Figure

The pattern reveals a coherent large scale structure, with enhanced MCS activity concentrated within the convectively active region of the MJO and suppressed activity in surrounding regions. This dipole like structure highlights the role of the MJO as a large scale organizing agent that selectively enhances mesoscale convective development in specific longitudinal sectors. The spatial coherence of these anomalies further suggests that MCS modulation is tightly linked to the propagating MJO circulation rather than being purely local.

Finally, Fig.

All variables show consistent enhancement during active MJO phases across large regions of the tropics, particularly over the Indo Pacific warm pool. Longer durations and larger cloud shields indicate that MCSs become more organized and sustained under active MJO conditions, while increased total precipitation reflects their enhanced hydrological impact. Variations in propagation speed further suggest that the MJO environment may influence the dynamical evolution and lifecycle of MCSs, potentially through modulation of steering flow and vertical shear.

Taken together, these results provide a coherent observational picture of strong MJO control on MCS statistics across multiple dimensions, including frequency, intensity, spatial organization, and dynamical behavior. The consistent phase dependence across all diagnostics highlights the hierarchical coupling between large scale intraseasonal variability and mesoscale convective organization, and sets the stage for examining the complementary feedback from aggregated MCS activity onto the MJO circulation.

4
Concluding Discussion

This study provides an observationally based assessment of the two way feedback mechanisms between the Madden Julian Oscillation and mesoscale convective systems. By combining a phase resolved MJO framework with an objectively tracked long term MCS dataset, we document how convection is organized across scales and how mesoscale systems both respond to and actively shape intraseasonal variability.

The results demonstrate a clear hierarchical organization of tropical convection within the MJO envelope. The MJO systematically modulates MCS activity, controlling their frequency, intensity, spatial distribution, and structural properties. Active MJO phases are characterized by significantly enhanced MCS occurrence and more organized convective systems, while suppressed phases exhibit reduced and weaker mesoscale activity. Importantly, MCSs contribute a substantial and phase dependent fraction of total tropical precipitation, indicating that they are not simply embedded features but a dominant component of the MJO precipitation budget.

Beyond this downward control, the analysis also provides observational evidence for an upward feedback from MCS populations to the MJO scale circulation. Spatially coherent anomalies in MCS activity are aligned with the MJO convective envelope and exhibit organized large scale patterns consistent with their potential to reinforce intraseasonal variability through aggregated effects. The modulation of MCS properties, including duration, size, precipitation, and propagation characteristics, further suggests that MCSs actively participate in regulating the effective structure and evolution of the MJO.

Together, these findings support a conceptual framework in which the MJO and MCSs form a coupled multiscale system rather than a one way hierarchical cascade. The MJO provides the slowly varying environment that organizes mesoscale convection, while the collective behavior of MCSs contributes back to the maintenance and modulation of the MJO circulation. This two way interaction emphasizes that accurate representation of tropical intraseasonal variability requires understanding not only how large scale dynamics controls convection, but also how organized mesoscale convection feeds back onto planetary scale variability.
