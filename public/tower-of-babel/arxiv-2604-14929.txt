1.
Introduction

When considering the homotopical structure of locally complicated spaces such as fractals and Peano continua, the existence of a “wild point” limits the applicability of traditional methods. Ironically, it is precisely these points and their relationship to infinite products in homotopy groups that have been used to distinguish and classify homotopy types of Peano continua
[
12
,
8
,
13
]
. For
n\geq 0
, the
\pi_{n}
-wild set

\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
of a topological space
X
is the subspace of
X
consisting of points
x\in X
at where there exists a sequence
f_{k}:(S^{n},\ast)\to(X,x)
of essential based maps that converges to the constant map at
x
(in the compact-open topology). When
X
is first countable and
n=1
,
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
is the set of points at which
X
fails to be semilocally simply connected. In particular, the
\pi_{1}
-wild set plays a key role in the homotopy theory of one-dimensional
[
14
,
22
]
and planar spaces
[
21
]
due to its invariance under homotopy equivalences
[
4
, Section 9]
.

Higher dimensional wild sets
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
,
n\geq 2
have recently been shown to enjoy similar invariance properties
[
5
]
and are expected to play an important role as progress is made in higher dimensions. The main problem of interest here beyond computing higher homotopy groups is whether or not weakly homotopy equivalent finite dimensional Peano continua must be homotopy equivalent
[
5
, Problem 1.1]
(see
[
15
, Problem 4.5]
and
[
19
, Problem 5.1]
for a weaker question).

Since
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
is a space in its own right,
\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{n}(X))
may be non-empty, i.e. a wild set may have its own internal wildness. This possibility allows one to recursively define the iterated wild sets
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{k}(X)=\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{n}^{k-1}(X))
to obtain a descending sequence of subspaces
X\supseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)\supseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{2}(X)\supseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{3}(X)\supseteq
. Motivated by an early version of the current paper, the initial sequence
X,\mathbf{w}_{1}(X),\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}(X),\mathbf{w}_{1}^{3}(X),\dots
in dimension
n=1
was applied successfully in
[
6
]
to compute the topological complexity of many one-dimensional Peano continua. Furthermore, it is possible that the intersection
\bigcap_{k\in\omega}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{k}(X)
of the sequence of iterated wild sets is non-empty and itself has a non-trivial
\pi_{n}
-wild set. By defining
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\omega}(X)=\bigcap_{k\in\omega}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{k}(X)
and continuing the definition with transfinite recursion, we define
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
for any ordinal
\kappa
. Basic set-theoretic considerations ensure that the transfinite sequence
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
stabilizes at some smallest ordinal
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\kappa
, which we call the
\pi_{n}
-wild rank of
X
.

It is instructive to compare
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)
with the classical Cantor-Bendixson rank of a space
X
. Recall that the
Cantor-Bendixson derivative
of a space
X
is the subspace
L(X)
of
X
consisting of all limit points (i.e. non-isolated points) of
X
. Define
L^{0}(X)=X
,
L^{\kappa+1}(X)=L(L^{\kappa}(X))
for all ordinals
\kappa
and
L^{\kappa}(X)=\bigcap_{\lambda<\kappa}L^{\lambda}(X)
when
\kappa
is a limit ordinal. The transfinite sequence
\{L^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
stabilizes at either an empty space or a perfect set and the smallest ordinal
\kappa
for which
L^{\kappa+1}(X)=L^{\kappa}(X)
is the
Cantor-Bendixson rank
of
X
. In comparison, the points of
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
are “limit points” of
X
in a homotopical sense and the transfinite sequence
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
will stabilize either at an empty space or a perfectly
\pi_{n}
-wild space, i.e. one which is wild at all of its points (Definition
2.16
).

In this paper, we show that a homotopy equivalence
X\simeq Y
, restricts to a homotopy equivalence
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\simeq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
for all ordinals
\kappa
(Theorem
3.16
). Hence, if
ho(X)
denotes the homotopy type of a space
X
, then the entire transfinite sequence
\{ho(\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X))\}_{\kappa}
of homotopy types is itself a homotopy invariant of
X
(Corollary
3.17
). Our main result is the following theorem, which shows that for a Peano continuum
X
, the transfinite sequence
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
may continue non-trivially into arbitrarily large countable ordinals.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any countable ordinal
\lambda\geq 1
, there exists a based
n
-dimensional Peano continuum
(X_{\lambda},x_{\lambda})
such that
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X_{\lambda})=\lambda
and
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X_{\lambda})=\emptyset
. Moreover, when
\lambda\geq 2
is a successor ordinal, we may arrange for
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda-1}(X_{\lambda})=\{x_{\lambda}\}
.

Some technical issues are created by the following combination of facts: If
X
is not locally path-connected, then
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
need not be closed in
X

[
5
, Example 2.12]
. Additionally, if
X
is a Peano continuum, then
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
can be an arbitrary continuum (compact metrizable space)
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
. When
X
is a non-locally path-connected continuum,
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
need not be closed in
X
and it becomes unclear if
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
stabilizes at a countable ordinal.

Hence,
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
need not be closed in
X
for
\kappa\geq 2
. These difficulties highlight the non-triviality of the following open problems.

Problem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
. Characterize the class of spaces which are homeomorphic to
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
for a continuum
X
.

Problem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
. Is there a continuum
X
(equivalently, a Peano continuum) such that
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)
is uncountable?

It seems likely that the answer to both of these problems is independent of
n
. The equivalence in Problem
1.3
is due to
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
, which is stated as Theorem
2.14
in the current paper.

In
[
18
]
, an important difference between the unbased and based versions of the
semilocally simply connected
property is considered. This same difference is to blame for the difficulty in answering Problems
1.2
and
1.3
. While
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
is the notion of “wild set” that is most relevant to the algebra of homotopy groups we are motivated to also consider a natural basepoint-free analogue of
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
. For
n\geq 0
,
free
\pi_{n}
-wild set

\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)
of a topological space
X
is the subspace of
X
consisting of points
x\in X
at where there exists a sequence
f_{k}:S^{n}\to X
of essential
unbased
maps that converges to the constant map at
x
. We obtain an analogous transfinite sequence
\{\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\lambda}
and rank
\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X)
that even more closely behaves like the classical Cantor-Bendixson construction. In particular, for a continuum
X
,
\{\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\lambda}
is a descending sequence of
closed
subspaces of
X
and
\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X)
must be countable for elementary reasons. Our main result regarding free
\pi_{n}
-wild sets, which is analogous to Theorem
1.1
, shows that every countable ordinal is a free
\pi_{n}
-wild rank.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
X
is a continuum, then
\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X)
is a countable ordinal. Conversely, for any countable ordinal
\lambda\geq 0
, there exists a continuum
X_{\lambda}
such that
\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X_{\lambda})=\lambda
and such that
\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\lambda-1}(X_{\lambda})=S^{n}
whenever
\lambda
is a successor ordinal.

In both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
, the respective condition for the successor ordinal case is present to aid in the proof, which is by transfinite induction.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

All topological spaces are assumed to be Hausdorff and a “map” is a continuous function. A continuum is a compact metrizable space and a Peano continuum is a path-connected and locally path-connected continuum. We use Lebesgue covering dimension for topological dimension and refer to
[
17
]
for standard dimension theory.

For
n\geq 0
,
S^{n}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\mid\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}=1\}
will be the unit
n
-sphere with basepoint
s_{0}=(1,0,\dots,0)
. A map
f:S^{n}\to X
is said to be
inessential
if it is null-homotopic and
essential
otherwise. When
X
is an unbased space, we let
X^{+}=X\sqcup\{x_{0}\}
denote the space with an additional isolated point.

When
X
and
Y
are topological spaces and we write
ho(X)
to denote the homotopy type of
X
and
Y^{X}
will denote the space of continuous functions
X\to Y
with the compact-open topology. If
A\subseteq X
and
B\subseteq Y
, then
(Y,B)^{(X,A)}
denotes the subspace of
Y^{X}
consisting of relative maps
(X,A)\to(Y,B)
. When
y\in Y
,
c_{y}:X\to Y
will denote the constant map at
y
. For a based topological space
(X,x_{0})
, we write
\Omega^{n}(X,x_{0})
to denote the
n
-loop space
(X,x_{0})^{(S^{n},s_{0})}
and
\pi_{n}(X,x_{0})=\{[f]\mid f\in\Omega^{n}(X,x_{0})\}
to denote the
n
-th homotopy group. If
f\in\Omega^{n}(X,x_{1})
and
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
is a path from
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
, then
\alpha\ast f\in\Omega^{n}(X,x_{0})
will denote the standard path-conjugate of
f
by
\alpha
.

We say that a sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of maps
f_{k}:X\to Y

converges to

if
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\to c_{y}
in

, that is, if for every neighborhood

of

such that

for all

.

.

Let

.

(1)

A point

is a
\pi_{n}
-wild point of

. Let
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
denote the subspace of

\pi_{n}
-wild points of

(2)

A point

-wild points of

.

Definition 2.2
.

The
shrinking wedge
of countable set

of based spaces is the space
\bigcurlyvee_{j\in J}(A_{j},a_{j})
whose underlying set is the usual one-point union

•

•

When the basepoints and/or indexing set are clear from context, we may write the shrinking wedge as

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
x\in X
and
n\geq 0
. Then

(1)

x\in\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
if and only if there exists a fully essential based map
f:(\mathbb{E}_{n},b_{0})\to(X,x)
.

(2)

x\in\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)
if and only if there exists a fully essential based map
f:(\mathbb{FE}_{n},a_{0})\to(X,x)
.

Remark 2.7
.

The inclusion
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)
holds for an arbitrary space
X
. Indeed, the canonical map
\iota:\mathbb{FE}_{n}\to\mathbb{E}_{n}
, which maps
a_{0}
to
b_{0}
and which maps the
j
-th spheres of
\mathbb{FE}_{n}
homeomorphically to the
j
-th sphere of
\mathbb{E}_{n}
is fully essential. Therefore, if
f:(\mathbb{E}_{n},b_{0})\to(X,x)
is fully, essential, then so is
f\circ\iota:(\mathbb{FE}_{n},a_{0})\to(X,x)
.

In general,
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
can be a proper subset of
\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)
. For example, we have
\mathbf{fw}_{n}(\mathbb{FE}_{n})=\{a_{0}\}
and
\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbb{FE}_{n})=\emptyset
since the path components of
\mathbb{FE}_{n}
are locally contractible.

Proposition 2.8
.

For any space
X
,
\mathbf{w}_{0}(X)=\mathbf{fw}_{0}(X)
.

Proof.

One inclusion follows from the previous remark. Suppose
x\in\mathbf{fw}_{0}(X)
. Then there exists a fully essential map
f:(\mathbb{FE}_{0},a_{0})\to(X,x)
. Let
P_{j}=\{a_{j},b_{j}\}
denote the
j
-th copy of
S^{0}
in
\mathbb{FE}_{0}
. Then
f(a_{j})
and

lie in distinct path-components of

. In particular, for each

f(a_{j})
or

does not lie in the path-component of
x
. Relabeling some

and

, if necessary, we may assume that
f(a_{j})
does not lie in the path-component of
x
for all
j\geq 1
. Define

so that
g(0)=x
,

for each

. Then

is fully essential, proving

.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
n\geq 0
. If
X
is first countable, then
\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)
is closed in
X
. Moreover, if

and

is also locally path-connected, then
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)=\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)

Proof.

Suppose

for all

. Let

. Now

is a sequence of essential maps that converges to

. For the second statement, recall that

be the image of

under the

∎

\mathbf{fw}_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{n}(X))
and
\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X))
are not comparable. Using Theorem
2.14
, we can construct a Peano continuum
X_{1}
for which
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X_{1})=\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X_{1})=\mathbb{FE}_{n}
. In this case,
\mathbf{fw}_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{n}(X_{1}))=\{a_{0}\}
and
\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X_{1}))=\emptyset
. Next, let
Y
be a countably infinite disjoint union of
n
-spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\backslash\mathbb{E}_{n}
of null-diameter such that
\overline{Y}=Y\cup\mathbb{E}_{n}
. Set
X_{2}=\overline{Y}
. In this case, we have
\mathbf{fw}_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{n}(X_{2}))=\emptyset
and
\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X_{2}))=\{b_{0}\}
. If we define
X=X_{1}\sqcup X_{2}
, then
\mathbf{fw}_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{n}(X))=\{a_{0}\}
and
\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X))=\{b_{0}\}
.

Definition 2.16
.

A space
X
is
perfectly (resp. free)
\pi_{n}
-wild
if
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)=X
(resp.
\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)=X
).

The ternary Cantor set is perfectly
\pi_{0}
-wild. Well-known examples of perfectly
\pi_{1}
-wild spaces include the Sierpinski Carpet, Menger Curve, and others
[
10
,
11
]
. Examples of perfectly
\pi_{n}
-wild spaces for
n\geq 2
are constructed in
[
5
]
. Since
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)
holds in general, every perfectly
\pi_{n}
-wild space is perfectly free
\pi_{n}
-wild.

3.
Iterating the
\pi_{n}
-wild set operator

For any space
X
, we recursively define a descending transfinite sequence of subspaces of
X
: Let
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{0}(X)=\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{0}(X)=X
. Set
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa+1}(X)=\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X))
and
\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa+1}(X)=\mathbf{fw}_{n}(\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X))
for each ordinal
\kappa\geq 0
. Define
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)=\bigcap_{\lambda<\kappa}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
and
\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)=\bigcap_{\lambda<\kappa}\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
if
\kappa
is a limit ordinal. We refer to
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
as the
\kappa
-th iterated
\pi_{n}
-wild set of
X
and
\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
as the
\kappa
-th iterated free-
\pi_{n}
-wild set of
X
.

Note that the transfinite sequences
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
and
\{\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
are “descending” in the sense that
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)
and
\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)
if
\kappa\geq\lambda
. Since
X
is a fixed space both of these transfinite sequences must stabilize, i.e. there must be an ordinal
\kappa_{0}
for which
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa_{0}}(X)=\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa_{0}+1}(X)
and an ordinal
\kappa_{1}
for which
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa_{1}}(X)=\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa_{1}+1}(X)
(otherwise, one could inject an arbitrarily large ordinals into the power set of
X
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a space.

(1)

The smallest ordinal
\kappa_{0}
such that
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa_{0}}(X)=\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
for all
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
is the
(based)
\pi_{n}
-wild rank of
X
and is denoted
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)
.

(2)

The smallest ordinal
\kappa_{1}
such that
\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa_{1}}(X)=\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
for all
\kappa\geq\kappa_{1}
is the
free
\pi_{n}
-wild rank of
X
and is denoted
\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X)
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\kappa_{0}
(resp.
\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X)=\kappa_{1}
) is the smallest ordinal for which the inclusion
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa_{0}+1}(X)\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa_{0}}(X)
(resp.
\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa_{1}+1}(X)\to\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa_{1}}(X)
) is the identity map. In particular, the transfinite sequence
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
either stabilizes in the empty set or at a perfectly
\pi_{n}
-wild set and
\{\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
either stabilizes in the empty set or at a perfectly free
\pi_{n}
-wild set.

Remark 3.3
.

In dimension
n=0
, it follows from Proposition
2.8
that for any space
X
,
\mathbf{w}_{0}^{\kappa}(X)=\mathbf{fw}_{0}^{\kappa}(X)
for all ordinals
\kappa
. Thus
\mathbf{wrk}_{0}(X)=\mathbf{fwrk}_{0}(X)
.

Example 3.4
.

If
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)
is finite and non-empty, then
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=2
. For example, since
\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{n})=\{b_{0}\}
, we have
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{n})=2
.

Example 3.5

(The wild circle and its variants)
.

Consider the
wild circle

X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
from
[
5
]
, where a shrinking sequence of circles are attached to
S^{1}
in a dense fashion so that

is a one-dimensional planar Peano continuum with
\mathbf{w}_{1}(X)=S^{1}
. Take the basepoint of
X
to be a point
x_{0}
that lies in
S^{1}
and let

y_{0}
. Then

\mathbf{w}_{1}(Y)\cong\mathbb{E}_{1}

\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}(Y)=\{y_{0}\}
, and
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{3}(Y)=\emptyset
giving
\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(Y)=3
. By replacing
S^{1}
with

and
\mathbb{E}_{1}
with
\mathbb{E}_{n}
, one obtains an analogous example for the higher-dimensional ranks.

Extending, the previous example, repeated application of Theorem
2.14
implies that for any
n
-dimensional compact metric space

n
-dimensional Peano continuum

and finite ordinal

and such that
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)
is a Peano continuum for all

to be a one-point space, we have the following.

Corollary 3.6
.

For every
\kappa\in\omega
, there exists an
n
-dimensional Peano continuum

with
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\kappa
.

Example 3.7

(A rank
\omega+1
example)
.

Let
X_{1}=\{\ast\}
and
X_{2}=\mathbb{E}_{n}
. Using Theorem
2.14
, we can construct an
n
-dimensional Peano continuum
X_{3}
where
X_{3}\backslash X_{2}
is a countable union of open
n

\mathbf{w}_{n}(X_{3})=X_{2}

-dimensional Peano continua
\mathbb{E}_{n}=X_{2}\subseteq X_{3}\subseteq X_{4}\subseteq X_{5}\subseteq\cdots
where
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X_{k+1})=X_{k}

\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X_{k})=k

along a dense set. Let

and note that
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)=\bigcurlyvee_{m\geq 2}X_{m}

\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)\cong X
although the inclusion
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X)\to X

Example 3.8

(A rank
\omega
example)
.

We can modify the previous example slightly to obtain an
n
-dimensional Peano continuum with
\pi_{n}
-wild rank
\omega
and whose sequence of iterated wild sets terminates at the empty set. As before, take
X_{1}=\{\ast\}
and recursively apply Theorem
2.14
to construct
n
-dimensional Peano continua
X_{2},X_{3},X_{4},\dots
so that
\mathbf{w}_{n}(X_{k})=X_{k-1}
for all

x_{k}
be a given basepoint in
X_{k}
and let

naturally as a subspace of

and let
y_{k}
denote the image of

(end of the whisker) in

Remark 3.9

(Cardinal Bounds)
.

For any space
X
and
n\geq 0
, the axiom of choice ensures that
|\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)|\leq|X|
and
|\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X)|\leq|X|
: if
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\kappa_{0}
, choose
x_{\lambda}\in\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)\backslash\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda+1}(X)
for each
\lambda\in\kappa_{0}
and define injection
\kappa_{0}\to X
by
\lambda\mapsto x_{\lambda}
. The same argument applies for free
\pi_{n}
-wild rank. For example, if
X
is a separable metrizable space, then
\max\{|\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)|,|\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X)|\}\leq 2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

An advantage of the free
\pi_{n}
-wild rank is the following.

Theorem 3.10
.

If
X
is second countable, then
\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X)
is a countable ordinal.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.9
that
\{\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)\}_{\lambda}
is a descending transfinite sequence of closed subspaces of
X
. It is well-known that any such sequence in a second countable space must stabilize at a countable ordinal
[
20
]
.
∎

In dimension
n=0
, Theorem
3.10
and Remark
3.3
combine to give the following.

Corollary 3.11
.

If
X
is second countable, then
\mathbf{wrk}_{0}(X)
is countable.

Proposition 3.12
.

If
X
is one-dimensional, then
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)
for all
\kappa
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction and the general inclusion
\mathbf{w}_{1}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{1}(X)
serves as the initial step. The limit ordinal case is clear so we focus on the successor ordinal case. Suppose that
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)
for some ordinal
\kappa
. If
B
is a one-dimensional Hausdorff space and
A\subseteq B
then the inclusion
A\to B
is
\pi_{1}
-injective
[
7
, Corollary 3.3]
and thus
\mathbf{w}_{1}(A)\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{1}(B)
. Applying
\mathbf{w}_{1}
to the inclusion
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)
gives
\mathbf{w}_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\kappa}(X))\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{1}(\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\kappa}(X))
. Since
\mathbf{w}_{1}(Z)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{1}(Z)
holds in general, we have
\mathbf{w}_{1}(\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\kappa}(X))\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{1}(\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\kappa}(X))
. Combining these inclusions gives
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\kappa+1}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\kappa+1}(X)
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

When
X
is a one-dimensional Hausdorff space, Proposition
3.12
does not imply a general relationship between the
\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(X)
and
\mathbf{fwrk}_{1}(X)
. However, if
\{\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
stabilizes at the empty space, then so does
\{\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
and we have
\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(X)\leq\mathbf{fwrk}_{1}(X)
.

Remark 3.14

(Rank is not homotopy invariant)
.

Neither the based
\pi_{n}
-wild rank nor the free
\pi_{n}
-wild rank is an invariant of homotopy type. For instance, let
X
be any perfectly
\pi_{n}
-wild set, e.g. the Menger curve if
n=1
. Let
Y=X\vee[0,1]
consists of
X
with a “whisker” attached. Then
\mathbf{w}_{n}(Y)=X
and the inclusion
X\to Y
is a homotopy equivalence. Hence,
X\simeq Y
but
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(Y)=\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(Y)=1\neq 0=\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X)
.

Although we do not study it in detail here, it is possible to define a potentially useful version of the
\pi_{n}
-wild rank that does happen to be homotopy invariant. One might define the
homotopy
\pi_{n}
-wild rank of
X
to be the smallest ordinal
\mathbf{hwrk}_{n}(X)=\kappa_{0}
for which the inclusion
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa_{0}}(X)
is a homotopy equivalence for all
\lambda>\kappa_{0}
. It is always the case that
\mathbf{hwrk}_{n}(X)\leq\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)
and it is possible that
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\mathbf{hwrk}_{n}(X)+\lambda
for an arbitrarily large ordinal
\lambda
.

Lemma 3.15
.

If
X
is a space and
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)\cong A
for some ordinal
\lambda<\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)
, then
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\lambda+\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(A)
.

Proof.

Identify
A
with
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)
. The transfinite sequence
\{\mathbf{w}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa}
consists of the initial segment
\{\mathbf{w}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa<\lambda}
followed by
\{\mathbf{w}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\lambda\leq\kappa\leq\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)}
(whose first term is
A
), which is then followed by the constant terminal segment
\{\mathbf{w}^{\kappa}(X)\}_{\kappa>\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)}
. If
\mu
is an ordinal that is order isomorphic to the ordinal segment
[\lambda,\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)]
, then
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(A)=\mu
and
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\lambda+\mu
.
∎

Theorem 3.16
.

If
f:X\to Y
and
g:Y\to X
are homotopy inverses, then, for any ordinal
\kappa
, so are the restrictions
f_{\kappa}:\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
and
g_{\kappa}:\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)

Proof.

We proceed by transfinite induction. Let
f_{0}=f
,
g_{0}=g
, and fix homotopies
H_{0}:X\times I\to X
from
id_{X}
to
g\circ f
and
G_{0}:Y\times I\to Y
from
id_{Y}
to
f\circ g
. Note that all of the maps
f_{0},g_{0},H_{0},G_{0}
are
\pi_{n}
-injective. Suppose that for all
\lambda<\kappa
, the respective restrictions of
f_{0}
,
g_{0}
,
H_{0}
, and
G_{0}
give homotopy inverses
f_{\lambda}:\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)
and
g_{\lambda}:\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)
with homotopies
H_{\lambda}
from
id_{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)}
to
g_{\lambda}\circ f_{\lambda}
and
G_{\lambda}
from
id_{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)}
to
f_{\lambda}\circ g_{\lambda}
.

If
\kappa=\lambda+1
is a successor ordinal, we apply Lemma
2.11
: let
f_{\kappa}:\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
be the restriction of
f_{\lambda}
to
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
and
g_{\kappa}:\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
be the restriction of
g_{\lambda}
to
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
. Similarly, let
H_{\kappa}
be the restriction of
H_{\lambda}
to a map
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)\times I\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)
and
G_{\kappa}
be the restriction of
G_{\lambda}
to
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\times I\to\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
. Now Lemma
2.11
ensures that the homotopies
H_{\kappa}
and
G_{\kappa}
give

and
id_{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)}\simeq f_{\kappa}\circ g_{\kappa}

If
\kappa
is a limit ordinal, then
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X)=\bigcap_{\lambda<\kappa}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(X)
and
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)=\bigcap_{\lambda<\kappa}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)

and

for all
\lambda<\kappa
. Therefore, if we take

from

to

∎

Corollary 3.17
.

The transfinite sequence
\{ho(\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X))\}_{\kappa}
of homotopy types is a homotopy invariant of
X
.

Corollary 3.18
.

If
n\geq 0
and

(1)

and
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\}

(2)

-wild spaces.

If

implies that the restricted functions

4.
Every countable ordinal is a
\pi_{n}
-wild rank

In this section, we give constructions to show that for every countable ordinal
\lambda
, there is an
n
-dimensional Peano continuum with
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\lambda
. The next lemma is straightforward to prove using Proposition
2.13
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
n\geq 0
and
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a collection of spaces, then
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(\coprod_{i\in I}X_{i})=\coprod_{i\in I}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(X_{i})
for all ordinals
\kappa
. Moreover,
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}\left(\coprod_{i\in I}X_{i}\right)=\sup\{\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X_{i})\mid i\in I\}
.

The construction used to prove Theorem
2.14
is the following “shrinking” modification of adjunction spaces, which we will use heavily in the remainder of the paper.

Definition 4.2

(Shrinking Point-Attachment Spaces)
.

Let
X
be a compact space,
A=\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence (of not necessarily distinct points) in
X
and let
\mathscr{B}=\{(B_{j},b_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of based spaces. Let
\mathscr{S}(X,A,\mathscr{B})=X\sqcup\coprod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}B_{j}/\mathord{\sim}
where
a_{j}\sim b_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. That is
\mathscr{S}(X,A,\mathscr{B})
is obtained by attaching each
B_{j}
to
X
by identifying the basepoint of
B_{j}
with
a_{j}
. We give
\mathscr{S}(X,A,\mathscr{B})
the following topology:
U\subseteq\mathscr{S}(X,A,\mathscr{V})
is open if and only if

(1)

X\cap U
is open in
X

(2)

X\cap B_{j}
is open in
B_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3)

whenever
x\in U\cap X
and
j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}<\cdots
is such that
\{a_{j_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\to x
in
X
, then
B_{j_{i}}\subseteq U
for all but finitely many
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

When
\mathscr{B}=\{B,B,B,\dots\}
is constant, we write
\mathscr{S}(X,A,B)
for the shrinking point-attachment.

Example 4.3
.

If
X=\{x_{0}\}
contains a single point, then
\mathscr{S}(X,A,\mathscr{B})=\bigcurlyvee_{j\in\mathbb{N}}B_{j}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

[
5
, Proposition 5.11]

If
X
and each
B_{j}
is separable (resp. path-connected, path connected and locally path-connected), then so is
\mathscr{S}(X,A,\mathscr{B})
.

Proposition 4.5
.

[
5
, Proposition 5.12]

If
X
and each
B_{j}\in\mathscr{B}

Theorem 4.6
.

If

\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)
is a continuum. Conversely, if
Y
is a continuum, then there exists a continuum

(1)

Y\subseteq X
and

(2)

\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)=Y

(3)

When
X

\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)
is closed in

\mathbf{fw}_{n}(X)
is a continuum. Given a continuum
Y

be a sequence with dense image in

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose

In the next lemma, we extend the previous lemma to higher iterations of the
\pi_{n}
-wild set.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
Y=\mathscr{S}(X,A,\mathscr{B})
is a shrinking point-attachment with
\mathscr{B}=\{(B_{j},b_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, then for all ordinals
\lambda
, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)

(2)

and
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)\backslash X=\coprod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})\backslash\{b_{j}\}
.

Proof.

We prove (1) and (2) simultaneously by transfinite induction on
\lambda
. Certainly both are true for
\lambda=0
. Suppose the two statements hold for all
\kappa<\lambda
.

If
\lambda
is a limit ordinal, then for given
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})=\bigcap_{\kappa<\lambda}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\subseteq\bigcap_{\kappa<\lambda}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)=\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y).

Thus
\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)
holds. This inclusion already implies the direction
\supseteq
in the second statement of the lemma. If
x\in\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)\backslash X
, then
x\in\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\backslash X=\coprod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\backslash\{b_{j}\}
for all
\kappa<\lambda
. Find
i
with
x\in B_{i}\backslash\{b_{i}\}
. Then
x\in\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{i})\backslash\{b_{i}\}
for all
\kappa<\lambda
. Thus

x\in\bigcap_{\kappa<\lambda}\left(\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{i})\backslash\{b_{i}\}\right)=\left(\bigcap_{\kappa<\lambda}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{i})\right)\backslash\{b_{i}\}=\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{i})\backslash\{b_{i}\}

proving the inclusion
\subseteq
of (2).

Suppose
\lambda=\kappa+1
. For the inclusion, fix
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We consider two cases:

Case I:
Suppose
b_{j}\in\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
. Since we have assumed
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
and
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\backslash X=\coprod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\backslash\{b_{j}\}
, we have
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\cap B_{j}=\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\cup\{b_{j}\}
. Thus we may write
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
uniquely as a one-point union
Z\vee\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})
with wedgepoint
b_{j}
. In particular,
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})
is a retract of
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
and it follows from Lemma
2.11
that
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)
. Thus (1) holds. For (2), the inclusion
\supseteq
follows from (1). Suppose
x\in\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)\cap B_{j}\backslash X
. Find a fully essential map
f:(\mathbb{E}_{n},b_{0})\to(\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y),x)
. Since
B_{j}\backslash\{b_{j}\}
is an open neighborhood of
x
in
Y
, we may disregard finitely many spheres in
\mathbb{E}_{n}
and assume that
f
has image in
(B_{j}\backslash\{b_{j}\})\cap\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
. Since
f
has image in
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\backslash X=\coprod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\backslash\{b_{j}\}
,
f
must have image in
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\backslash\{b_{j}\}
. If the
k
-th restriction of
f
is not null-homotopic in
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
it is not null-homotopic in the subspace
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})
. Since
f:(\mathbb{E}_{n},b_{0})\to(\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j}),x)
is fully essential, we have
x\in\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})
. Thus
x\in\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})\backslash\{b_{j}\}
, proving the inclusion
\subseteq
for (2).

Case II:
Suppose
b_{j}\notin\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
. Now, the induction hypothesis
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
implies that
b_{j}\notin\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})
and

\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)=\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\sqcup\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\backslash\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j}).

It follows that

\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)=\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})\sqcup\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\backslash\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})).

In particular,
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)
, proving (1). The inclusion
\supseteq
for (2) follows again from (1). Let
x\in\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)\backslash X
. Find
i
with
x\in B_{i}\backslash\{b_{i}\}
. Since

\mathbf{w}_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)\backslash\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{i}))\subseteq Y\backslash B_{i},

we must have

. This proves the inclusion
\subseteq
for (2).

In Cases I and II, we have established both (1) and (2) in the statement of the lemma. This completes the induction.
∎

Lemma 4.10

Suppose

\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)=X\cup\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})

By Corollary

for all

.. Therefore, it suffices to show that

\lambda

\lambda=0

for all ordinals
\kappa<\lambda
. If
\lambda

Suppose

. We have assumed

and will show that

\im(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{k}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(B_{j})\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\lambda}(Y)

. Suppose

. Since
\im(A)
is dense in

, find a sequence

of integers such that
\{a_{j_{i}}\}\to x
in

. For all

, we have

\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j_{i}})
contains

,
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j_{i}})
is a retract of
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)=X\cup\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})
. Thus the
p
-th restriction

of

is not null-homotopic in
\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(Y)
. Since

is first countable and locally path connected, we may replace

with a cofinal subsequence so that there exists a path

from

∎

Lemma 4.11

\lambda=\sup\{\lambda_{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{N}\}

(B_{j},b_{j})

\bigcurlyvee_{j\in\mathbb{N}}B_{j}

Proof.

We will apply Lemma

with

and
\mathscr{B}=\{(B_{j},b_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
.

By Part (1) of Lemma

, we have
\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(B_{j})\subseteq\mathbf{w}_{n}^{\kappa}(\bigcurlyvee_{j\in\mathbb{N}}B_{j})

. In the case where

, we have

. Fix continuum
X_{\kappa_{j}}
with
\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(X_{\kappa_{j}})=\kappa_{j}
and let
X_{\kappa}^{+}
be the space with an additional isolated basepoint. Let
W=\bigcurlyvee_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(X_{\kappa_{j}}^{+})
with wedgepoint

Let
A=\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

∎

Example 4.12
.

With Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
established, we show that it is possible for
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(Z)=\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(Z)+\lambda
to occur where the difference
\lambda
is an arbitrary infinite countable ordinal.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional Peano continuum with
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=\lambda
. By standard embedding theorems, we may view
K
as a subspace of
[0,1]^{k}
for some
k\leq 2n+1
. Let
A=\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
[0,1]^{k}
where
\{a_{2i-1}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a dense subset of
K
and
\{a_{2i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a countable dense subset of
[0,1]^{k}
. Let
\mathscr{B}
be the alternating sequence of based spaces
(\mathbb{E}_{n},b_{0}),(\mathbb{FE}_{n},a_{0}),(\mathbb{E}_{n},b_{0}),(\mathbb{FE}_{n},a_{0}),\dots
and set
Y=\mathscr{S}(X,A,\mathscr{B})
. Then
Y
is an
n
-dimensional continuum with
\mathbf{w}_{n}(Y)=K
and
\mathbf{fw}_{n}(Y)=[0,1]^{k}
. By Theorem
2.14
, there exists an
n
-dimensional Peano continuum
Z
such that
\mathbf{w}_{n}(Z)=Y
(we also have
\mathbf{fw}_{n}(Z)=Y
by Lemma
2.9
).

We have
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(Z)=2+\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(X)=2+\lambda=\lambda
since
\lambda
is infinite. On the other hand,
\mathbf{fw}_{n}^{2}(Z)=[0,1]^{k}
and thus
\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(Z)=3
. Hence,
\mathbf{wrk}_{n}(Z)=\lambda=3+\lambda=\mathbf{fwrk}_{n}(Z)+\lambda
.

We conclude with an observation regarding Problem
1.3
. The following proposition suggests that, even in the one-dimensional setting, a (Peano) continuum with uncountable
\pi_{1}
-wild rank will be fairly complicated. In particular, one should only attempt to construct a perfectly free
\pi_{1}
-wild example. The authors acknowledge that the existence of such a space may be independent of ZFC.

Proposition 4.13
.

If there exists a one-dimensional continuum
X
with
\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(X)\geq\omega_{1}
, then there exists a perfectly free
\pi_{1}
-wild subcontinuum
Y\subseteq X
with
\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(Y)\geq\omega_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
X
is a one-dimensional continuum,
\mathbf{fwrk}_{1}(X)=\lambda
is countable by Theorem
3.10
. Let
Y=\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\lambda}(X)
. By Proposition
3.12
, we have
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\kappa}(X)
for all ordinals
\kappa
. Thus
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\lambda}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{fw}_{1}^{\lambda}(X)
. Since
\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(X)
is not countable,
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\lambda}(X)\neq\emptyset
. It follows that
Y
is a non-empty perfectly free
\pi_{1}
-wild space. By Lemma
3.15
, we have
\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(X)=\lambda+\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(Y)
. Since
\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(X)
is uncountable, so is
\mathbf{wrk}_{1}(Y)
.
∎
