A note on computable étale spaces
Matthew de Brecht

Abstract
An étale space over a topological space Y Y is defined as a local homeomorphism from a topological space X X into Y Y . They often come up in topos theory because of the equivalence between sheaves and étale spaces over a space. In this note, we define computable étale spaces over a computable topological space Y Y within the TTE framework of computable topology, and show they are naturally equivalent to computable functions from Y Y to 𝖮𝖣𝖲 {\mathsf{ODS}} , the effective quasi-Polish category of overt-discrete quasi-Polish spaces. More generally, if 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} is a computable category (or groupoid), then there is an equivalence between computable functors from 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} to 𝖮𝖣𝖲 {\mathsf{ODS}} , and computable étale spaces equipped with a computable action by 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} .

2.2
Computable categories

A

is an internal category
[
14
]
in the category of computable topological spaces and computable functions. More concretely, a computable category is a tuple
{\mathcal{C}}=({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}},{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}},{\sigma},{\tau},{\iota},\circ)

•

{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}

(morphisms) are computable topological spaces.

•

{\sigma}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
(source),

(target), and
{\iota}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}

•

(composition) is a partial computable function with domain

dom(\circ)=\{\langle g,f\rangle\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}\times{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}\mid{\sigma}(g)={\tau}(f)\}

subject to the following:

•

{\sigma}(g\circ f)={\sigma}(f)
and
{\tau}(g\circ f)={\tau}(g)
,

•

{\sigma}({\iota}(c))=c
and
{\tau}({\iota}(c))=c
,

•

(h\circ g)\circ f=h\circ(g\circ f)
when the compositions
h\circ g
and
g\circ f
are defined,

•

if
{\sigma}(f)=c
and
{\tau}(f)=d
then
f\circ{\iota}(c)=f={\iota}(d)\circ f
.

We write
f\colon c\to d
to denote that

is a morphism with
{\sigma}(f)=c
and
{\tau}(f)=d
.

{\mathcal{C}}
an

is an internal category in the category of effective quasi-Polish spaces and computable functions, because effective quasi-Polish spaces are closed under finite limits (in particular, the pullback

is an effective quasi-Polish space).
{\mathcal{C}}
is

As a useful example, note that every computable topological space

Computable functors

Let

Functor categories

Computable étale spaces

An

is equivalent to the category of sheaves of sets over

18
]

We only consider countably based étale spaces in this note. Having a countable basis forces the fibers to be countable, hence these can be viewed as sheaves of countable sets over a space. Note that a countably based étale space over a quasi-Polish space is automatically quasi-Polish (Lemma 2.2 of
[
1
]

Let
X
and

be computable topological spaces. A

consists of a computable function
p\colon X\to Y
along with the following:

1.

a computable enumeration of c.e. open subsets
(U_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
that cover

,

2.

a computable enumeration of c.e. open subsets
(V_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
Y
,

3.

a computable enumeration of computable functions (sections)

(

•

In this case, we call

Computable actions

The definitions in this section are a minor generalization of the continuous groupoid actions in

1

to continuous actions by topological categories.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a computable category, and

by

1.

2.

3.

(g\circ f)\cdot x=g\cdot(f\cdot x)
.

Intuitively,
\alpha

in
{\mathcal{C}}
as a function

A

from

to

(both equipped with computable actions by

such that

and
f(g\cdot x)=g\cdot f(x)
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a computable category, then a computable étale space over

is called a

{\mathcal{C}}
-set

These definitions relativize to countably based étale spaces, continuous actions, and continuous equivariant functions. We write

2.7
The category of overt discrete quasi-Polish spaces

Let
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{N}})
be the powerset of the natural numbers with the Scott-topology. If

A symmetric transitive relation is called a

{\sigma}(\langle G,\equiv_{\sigma},\equiv_{\tau}\rangle)=\equiv_{\sigma}

, the space of ideals of
\equiv
. This is quasi-Polish by definition, and discrete because if
I,J\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv)
then
I=J
if and only if
(\exists a\in{\mathbb{N}})[a\in I\,\&\,a\in J]
. Finally,
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv)
is overt (relative to
\equiv
) because
[a]_{\prec}\not=\emptyset
if and only if
a\equiv a
. Clearly, if
\equiv
is a computable point then
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv)
is a computably overt computably discrete effective quasi-Polish space.

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
maps
\langle G,\equiv_{\sigma},\equiv_{\tau}\rangle
in
{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
to the function
f_{G}\colon{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv_{\sigma})\to{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv_{\tau})
defined as

f_{G}(I)=\{b\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid(\exists a\in I)\,G(a,b)\}

for each
I\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv_{\sigma})
. It follows from the definition of ideals and the five axioms defining elements of
{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
that
f_{G}(I)
is an ideal of
\equiv_{\tau}
, hence
f_{G}
is a well-defined function. Clearly,
f_{G}
is computable from
\langle G,\equiv_{\sigma},\equiv_{\tau}\rangle
. Note that a computable point of
{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
has computable source and target because
{\sigma}
and
{\tau}
are computable.

It is easy to see that
{\mathcal{E}}
is a full and faithful functor. Next, we show that each computably overt computably discrete effective quasi-Polish space is computably homeomorphic to
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv)
for some computable point
\equiv
of
{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
.

Assume
\prec
is a c.e. transitive relation, that
E_{\prec}=\{a\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid[a]_{\prec}\not=\emptyset\}
is c.e., and that
D_{\prec}\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
is c.e. and such that
I=J
in
{{\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}}(\prec)}
if and only if there is
\langle a,b\rangle\in D_{\prec}
with
a\in I
and
b\in J
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\langle a,b\rangle\in D_{\prec}
implies
a\in E_{\prec}
and
b\in E_{\prec}
.

Define

S=\{c\in E_{\prec}\mid(\exists\langle a,b\rangle\in D_{\prec})[a\prec c\,\&\,b\prec c]\}.

Note that for each
c\in S
there is a unique
I\in{{\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}}(\prec)}
such that
\{I\}=[c]_{\prec}
, and conversely for each
I\in{{\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}}(\prec)}
there is at least one
c\in S
with
\{I\}=[c]_{\prec}
. If
[a]_{\prec}=[b]_{\prec}=\{I\}
then
a
and
b
must have a
\prec
-upper bound in
I
, so by defining

a\equiv b\iff a,b\in S\,\&\,(\exists c\in S)[a\prec c\,\&\,b\prec c],

we obtain that
a\equiv b
if and only if
a,b\in S
and
[a]_{\prec}=[b]_{\prec}=\{I\}
for a unique
I\in{{\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}}(\prec)}
. Now define
g\colon{{\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}}(\prec)}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv)
and
h\colon{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv)\to{{\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}}(\prec)}
as

\displaystyle g(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{a\in I\mid a\equiv a\}

\displaystyle h(J)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{b\in S\mid(\exists a\in J)\,b\prec a\}.

Then
g(I)
is the
\equiv
-ideal composed of all
a\in S
with
[a]_{\prec}=\{I\}
. Furthermore,
h(J)
is a
\prec
-ideal because each
\equiv
-ideal is
\prec
-directed. Therefore,
g
and
h
are well-defined, and easily seen to be computable inverses of each other. Therefore,
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(\equiv)
is computably homeomorphic to
{{\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}}(\prec)}
. Since the proof relativizes, it follows that
{\mathcal{E}}
is essentially surjective.

3
Main result

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 3.1

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a computable category. The category
{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})
of countably based
{\mathcal{C}}
-sets and continuous equivariant functions and the category
{\mathsf{ODS}}^{\mathcal{C}}
of (topologically) continuous functors from
{\mathcal{C}}
to
{\mathsf{ODS}}
are equivalent. The equivalence preserves the computability of both objects and morphisms.

\sqcap
\sqcup

As a special case, if
X
is a computable topological space then there is an equivalence between computable étale spaces over
X
and computable functions from
X
to
{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
. Thus, the functor category
{\mathsf{ODS}}^{{\mathcal{C}}_{X}}
is equivalent to the category of countably based étale spaces over
X
.

We prove our main result in the next four sections by constructing functors
{\mathcal{F}}\colon{\mathsf{ODS}}^{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})
and
{\mathcal{G}}\colon{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathsf{ODS}}^{\mathcal{C}}
that preserve computable objects and morphisms, and then show that that
{\mathcal{G}}\circ{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}\circ{\mathcal{G}}
are naturally isomorphic to the identity functors.

3.1
From
{\mathsf{ODS}}^{\mathcal{C}}
to
{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})

The next two subsections define the functor
{\mathcal{F}}\colon{\mathsf{ODS}}^{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})
.

Objects (functors to
{\mathcal{C}}
-sets)

{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
maps a functor
F\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}
to an étale space
p_{F}\colon X_{F}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
equipped with an action
\alpha_{F}
defined as follows.

Assume
F\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}
is a computable functor. For
c\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
we write
\equiv_{c}
for the partial equivalence relation
F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)
, and for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we write
[n]_{c}=\{m\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid n\equiv_{c}m\}
for the equivalence class of
n
with respect to
\equiv_{c}
. Let
X_{F}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{N}})
be the subspace defined as

X_{F}=\{\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{N}})\mid n\equiv_{c}n\}.

Define
p_{F}\colon X_{F}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
to be the projection on to the first coordinate. Define open sets

\displaystyle U_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle\in X_{F}\mid n\equiv_{c}n\},\text{ and}

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{c\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\mid n\equiv_{c}n\}.

Define
s_{n}\colon V_{n}\to U_{n}
as
s_{n}(c)=\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle
. Then
s_{n}
is the inverse for
p|_{U_{n}}
, which shows that
p
is a computable local homeomorphism.

Define the action
\alpha_{F}:\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}\times X_{F}\to X_{F}
as

\alpha_{F}(f,\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle)=\langle{\tau}(f),F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)(n)\rangle

where for
F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)=\langle G_{f},\equiv_{\sigma},\equiv_{\tau}\rangle
we set
F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)(n)=\{m\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid G_{f}(n,m)\}
. Using the assumption that
F
is a computable functor, it is straightforward to check that
\alpha_{F}
is a computable action.

Morphisms (natural transformations to equivariant functions)

{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
maps a natural transformation
\eta
to an equivariant function
h
as follows.

Assume
F,F^{\prime}\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}
are computable functors, and that
\eta\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
corresponds to a computable natural transformation from
F
to
F^{\prime}
. Let
p\colon X\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
and
q\colon Y\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
be the
{\mathcal{C}}
-sets
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(F)
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(F^{\prime})
, respectively (we omit the subscripts). Define
h\colon X\to Y
as

h(\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle)=\langle c,\eta(c)(n)\rangle

where for
\eta(c)=\langle G,F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c),F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)\rangle
we set
\eta(c)(n)=\{m\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid G(n,m)\}
.

It is clear that
h
is computable. We check that it is equivariant. Since
p
and
q
project the first coordinate and
h
does not change it, it is clear that
p=q\circ h
. For
f\colon c\to d
in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
, we have (with a slight abuse of notation in the second and third lines)

\displaystyle h(f\cdot\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(\langle d,F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)(n)\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle d,\eta(d)(F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)(n))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle d,F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)(\eta(c)(n))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\cdot\langle c,\eta(c)(n)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\cdot h(\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle),

hence
h
is equivariant.

3.2
From
{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})
to
{\mathsf{ODS}}^{\mathcal{C}}

The next two subsections define the functor
{\mathcal{G}}\colon{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathsf{ODS}}^{\mathcal{C}}
.

Objects (
{\mathcal{C}}
-sets to functors)

{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
maps an étale space
p\colon X\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
equipped with an action
\alpha
to a functor
F\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}
as follows.

Assume
p\colon X\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
is a computable étale space with sections
s_{n}\colon V_{n}\to U_{n}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
), and computable action
\alpha:\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}\times X\to X
. Define
F_{\mathsf{Obj}}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
as

F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)=\{\langle n,m\rangle\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\mid c\in V_{n}\cap V_{m}\,\&\,s_{n}(c)=s_{m}(c)\}

and define
F_{\mathsf{Mor}}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
as

F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)=\langle G_{f},\equiv_{\sigma},\equiv_{\tau}\rangle,

where

\displaystyle G_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\langle n,m\rangle\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\mid n\equiv_{\sigma}n\,\&\,m\equiv_{\tau}m\,\&\,\alpha(f,s_{n}({\sigma}(f)))=s_{m}({\tau}(f))\}

\displaystyle\equiv_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{\mathsf{Obj}}({\sigma}(f))

\displaystyle\equiv_{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{\mathsf{Obj}}({\tau}(f)).

We show that
F\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}
is a computable functor. It is clear that
F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(x)
is a partial equivalence relation, hence
F_{\mathsf{Obj}}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
is well-defined and easily seen to be computable by using Lemma
1
.

F_{\mathsf{Mor}}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
is also easily seen to be computable by using Lemma
1
and the fact that
\alpha
being an action implies
p(\alpha(f,s_{n}({\sigma}(f))))={\tau}(f)
, but we must check that
F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)
satisfies the 5 conditions to be in
{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
. Condition 1 holds by definition. For condition 2, if
G_{f}(n,m)
and
n\equiv_{\sigma}n^{\prime}
, then
s_{n}({\sigma}(f))=s_{n^{\prime}}({\sigma}(f))
by definition of
\equiv_{\sigma}
, hence
G_{f}(n^{\prime},m)
follows. Condition 3 is similar. For condition 4, if
G_{f}(n,m)
and
G_{f}(n,m^{\prime})
then
{\tau}(f)\in V_{m}\cap V_{m^{\prime}}
and
s_{m}({\tau}(f))=s_{m^{\prime}}({\tau}(f))
because
\alpha
is single-valued, hence
m\equiv_{\tau}m^{\prime}
. For condition 5, if
n\equiv_{\sigma}n
, then
{\sigma}(f)\in V_{n}
, hence
p(s_{n}({\sigma}(f)))={\sigma}(f)
, thus
\langle f,s_{n}({\sigma}(f))\rangle\in dom(\alpha)
. Choose
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\alpha(f,s_{n}({\sigma}(f)))\in U_{m}
. Then
{\tau}(f)=p(\alpha(f,s_{n}({\sigma}(f))))
because
\alpha
is an action. Thus
{\tau}(f)\in V_{m}
, which implies
m\equiv_{\tau}m
, and it follows from Lemma
1
that
\alpha(f,s_{n}({\sigma}(f)))=s_{m}({\tau}(f))
. Therefore,
G_{f}(n,m)
, and condition 5 is satisfied.

Finally, it is straightforward to verify that
F_{\mathsf{Obj}}
and
F_{\mathsf{Mor}}
together satisfy the 4 conditions required to be a functor: the first two conditions hold by definition, and the last two follow from the properties of actions.

Morphisms (equivariant functions to natural transformations)

{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
maps an equivariant function
h
to a natural transformation
\eta_{h}
as follows.

Assume
h\colon X\to Y
is a computable equivariant function from
p\colon X\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
to
q\colon Y\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
. Let
s_{n}\colon V_{n}\to U_{n}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
) be the sections of
p
, and
s^{\prime}_{n}\colon V^{\prime}_{n}\to U^{\prime}_{n}
the sections of
q
. Let
F,F^{\prime}\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}
be the computable functors corresponding to
p
and
q
, respectively.

Define
\eta_{h}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
as

\eta_{h}(c)=\langle G,\equiv_{\sigma},\equiv_{\tau}\rangle

where

•

G=\{\langle n,m\rangle\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\mid n\equiv_{\sigma}n\,\&\,m\equiv_{\tau}m\,\&\,h(s_{n}(c))=s^{\prime}_{m}(c)\}

•

\equiv_{\sigma}=F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)

•

\equiv_{\tau}=F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)

It is straightforward to see that
\eta_{h}
is well-defined, and since
q(h(s_{n}(c)))=p(s_{n}(c))=c
, Lemma
1
implies
h(s_{n}(c))=s^{\prime}_{m}(c)
if and only if
h(s_{n}(c))\in U^{\prime}_{m}
, hence
\eta_{h}
is computable.

We check that it is in fact a natural transformation
\eta_{h}\colon F\to F^{\prime}
. For
f\colon c\to d
in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
,

\displaystyle\eta_{h}(d)\circ F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\{\langle n,m\rangle\mid(\exists k)[\cdots]\,\&\,f\cdot s_{n}(c)=s_{k}(d)\,\&\,h(s_{k}(d))=s^{\prime}_{m}(d)\},F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c),F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(d)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\{\langle n,m\rangle\mid[\cdots]\,\&\,h(f\cdot s_{n}(c))=s^{\prime}_{m}(d)\},F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c),F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(d)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\{\langle n,m\rangle\mid[\cdots]\,\&\,f\cdot h(s_{n}(c))=s^{\prime}_{m}(d)\},F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c),F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(d)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\{\langle n,m\rangle\mid(\exists k)[\cdots]\,\&\,h(s_{n}(c))=s^{\prime}_{k}(c)\,\&\,f\cdot s^{\prime}_{k}(c)=s^{\prime}_{m}(d)\},F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c),F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(d)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)\circ\eta_{h}(c)

where
[\cdots]
abbreviates
n\equiv n
,
m\equiv m
,
k\equiv k
(when relevant, and with the appropriate subscripts). The other condition for being a natural transformation is trivial.

3.3
Natural isomorphism (from
{\mathsf{ODS}}^{\mathcal{C}}
and back)

We show that
{\mathcal{G}}\circ{\mathcal{F}}
is actually equal to the identity functor on
{\mathsf{ODS}}^{\mathcal{C}}
.

Assume
F\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}
is a computable functor, and let
\widehat{F}={\mathcal{G}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}({\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(F))
. Then
\widehat{F}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
is as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\langle n,m\rangle\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\mid c\in V_{n}\cap V_{m}\,\&\,s_{n}(c)=s_{m}(c)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\langle n,m\rangle\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\mid n\equiv_{c}n\,\&\,m\equiv_{c}m\,\&\,\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle=\langle c,[m]_{c}\rangle\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\langle n,m\rangle\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\mid n\equiv_{c}m\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c),

where
\equiv_{c}
and
[\cdot]_{c}
refer to
F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c),
and
s_{n}\colon V_{n}\to U_{n}
are the sections of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(F)
.

For morphisms, assuming
F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)=\langle G_{f},\equiv_{\sigma},\equiv_{\tau}\rangle
, we have that
\widehat{F}_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)=\langle\widehat{G}_{f},\equiv_{\sigma},\equiv_{\tau}\rangle
, where
\equiv_{\sigma}=F_{\mathsf{Obj}}({\sigma}(f))=\widehat{F}_{\mathsf{Obj}}({\sigma}(f))
, and
\equiv_{\tau}=F_{\mathsf{Obj}}({\tau}(f))=\widehat{F}_{\mathsf{Obj}}({\tau}(f))
. Furthermore, assuming
n\equiv_{\sigma}n
and
m\equiv_{\tau}m
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{G}_{f}(n,m)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}(f,s_{n}({\sigma}(f)))=s_{m}({\tau}(f))

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}(f,\langle{\sigma}(f),[n]_{{\sigma}(f)}\rangle)=\langle{\tau}(f),[m]_{{\tau}(f)}\rangle

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\langle{\tau}(f),F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)(n)\rangle=\langle{\tau}(f),[m]_{{\tau}(f)}\rangle

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle F_{\mathsf{Mor}}(f)(n)=[m]_{{\tau}(f)}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle G_{f}(n,m),

where
\alpha_{F}
is the action of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(F)
. Therefore,
\widehat{F}=F
.

Next, assume
F,F^{\prime}\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}
are functors and that
\eta\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}
corresponds to a natural transformation from

to

. Let
\widehat{\eta}={\mathcal{G}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}({\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}(\eta))
. Fix

and assume

and

. Then
\widehat{\eta}(c)=\langle\widehat{G},F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c),F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)\rangle
, where for
\langle n,n\rangle\in F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)
and
\langle m,m\rangle\in F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{G}(n,m)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle h(s_{n}(c))=s^{\prime}_{m}(c)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle h(\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle)=\langle c,[m]^{\prime}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\langle c,\eta(c)(n)\rangle=\langle c,[m]^{\prime}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\eta(c)(n)=[m]^{\prime}_{c}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\{b\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid G(n,b)\}=\{b\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid b\equiv^{\prime}_{c}m\}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle G(n,m),

where
s_{n}
refers to
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(F)
, and
\equiv^{\prime}_{c}
,
[\cdot]^{\prime}_{c}
, and

refer to
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(F^{\prime})

.

3.4
Natural isomorphism (from
{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})
and back)

We show that
{\mathcal{F}}\circ{\mathcal{G}}

Assume
p\colon X\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
is a computable étale space with sections
s_{n}\colon V_{n}\to U_{n}
(

), and computable action
\alpha:\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}\times X\to X

F\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{ODS}}
be the functor obtained from applying
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
to

(see Section

).

We calculate the étale space
p_{F}\colon X_{F}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
with action
\alpha_{F}
corresponding to
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(F)
(see Section
3.1
). We have

\displaystyle X_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{N}})\mid n\equiv_{c}n\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{N}})\mid c\in V_{n}\},

where for
c\in V_{n}

\displaystyle[n]_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{m\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid n\equiv_{c}m\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{m\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid c\in V_{m}\,\&\,s_{n}(c)=s_{m}(c)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{m\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid s_{n}(c)\in U_{m}\}

and
p_{F}\colon X_{F}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
is the projection on to the first coordinate.

After unwinding the definitions, the action
\alpha_{F}:\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}\times X_{F}\to X_{F}

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}(f,\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\tau}(f),\{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid{\tau}(f)\in V_{m^{\prime}}\,\&\,\alpha(f,s_{n}(c))=s_{m^{\prime}}({\tau}(f))\}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle{\tau}(f),\{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid\alpha(f,s_{n}(c))\in U_{m^{\prime}}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\tau}(f),\{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid s_{m}(p(\alpha(f,s_{n}(c))))\in U_{m^{\prime}}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\tau}(f),\{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid s_{m}({\tau}(f))\in U_{m^{\prime}}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\tau}(f),[m]_{{\tau}(f)}\}\rangle

where by assumption

and

Define
\theta_{X}\colon X\to X_{F}

(if

does not depend on the choice of

\theta_{X}
is computable, and it is equivariant because

\displaystyle\theta_{X}(\alpha(f,x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle p(\alpha(f,x)),[m]_{p(\alpha(f,x))}\rangle\quad\text{ (where $m\in{\mathbb{N}}$ satisfies $\alpha(f,x)\in U_{m}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\tau(f),[m]_{\tau(f)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}(f,\langle p(x),[n]_{p(x)}\rangle)

(as shown above, because
{\sigma}(f)=p(x)
and
p(x)\in V_{n}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}(f,\theta_{X}(x)).

Next, define
\theta_{X}^{\prime}\colon X_{F}\to X

\theta_{X}^{\prime}
is well-defined because
s_{n}(c)=s_{m}(c)
for each
m\in[n]_{c}
. It is clear that
\theta_{X}^{\prime}
is computable, and it is equivariant because:

\displaystyle\theta_{X}^{\prime}(\alpha_{F}(f,\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{X}^{\prime}(\langle{\tau}(f),[m]_{{\tau}(f)}\rangle)

(where
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies
\alpha(f,s_{n}(c))\in U_{m}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{m}({\tau}(f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(f,s_{n}(c))\quad\text{ (by Lemma~\ref{lem:sections} because $\alpha(f,s_{m}(c))\in U_{m}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(f,\theta_{X}^{\prime}(\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle)).

Furthermore,
\theta_{X}
and
\theta_{X}^{\prime}
are inverses because:

\displaystyle\theta_{X}^{\prime}(\theta_{X}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{X}^{\prime}(\langle p(x),[n]_{p(x)}\rangle)\quad\text{ (where $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ satisfies $x\in U_{n}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{n}(p(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x,

and

\displaystyle\theta_{X}(\theta_{X}^{\prime}(\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{X}(s_{n}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle p(s_{n}(c)),[n]_{p(s_{n}(c))}\rangle\quad\text{ (because $s_{n}(c)\in U_{n}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle.

Therefore,
X
and
X_{F}
are computably isomorphic
{\mathcal{C}}
-sets.

Finally, we prove that
\theta\colon 1_{{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})}\to{\mathcal{F}}\circ{\mathcal{G}}
is a natural transformation (it automatically follows that the inverse
\theta^{\prime}
is also natural). Assume
h\colon X\to Y
is a continuous equivariant function from
p\colon X\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
to
q\colon Y\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
. Let
s_{n}\colon V_{n}\to U_{n}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
) be the sections of
p
, and
s^{\prime}_{n}\colon V^{\prime}_{n}\to U^{\prime}_{n}
the sections of
q
. We write
\eta_{h}\colon F\to F^{\prime}
for
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}(h)
and
h^{\prime}\colon X_{F}\to Y_{F^{\prime}}
for
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathsf{Mor}}(\eta_{h})
. Then for
c\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
with
c\in V_{n}
we have

h^{\prime}(\langle c,[n]_{c}\rangle)=\langle c,\eta_{h}(c)(n)\rangle

where

\displaystyle m\in\eta_{h}(c)(n)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\langle n,n\rangle\in F_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)\,\&\,\langle m,m\rangle\in F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}(c)\,\&\,h(s_{n}(c))=s^{\prime}_{m}(c)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle c\in V_{n}\cap V^{\prime}_{m}\,\&\,h(s_{n}(c))=s^{\prime}_{m}(c)

(by definition of
F_{\mathsf{Obj}}
and
F^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Obj}}
)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle c\in V_{n}\cap V^{\prime}_{m}\,\&\,h(s_{n}(c))\in U^{\prime}_{m}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{ (by Lemma~\ref{lem:sections} because $q(h(s_{n}(c)))=c$)}.

Assume
x\in X
, and fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
x\in U_{n}
. Since
s_{n}(p(x))=x
, if
m\in\eta_{h}(p(x))(n)
then
h(x)=h(s_{n}(p(x)))\in U^{\prime}_{m}
. Conversely, if
h(x)\in U^{\prime}_{m}
, then since
p(x)\in V_{n}
and
p(x)=q(h(x))\in V^{\prime}_{m}
and
h(s_{n}(p(x)))=h(x)\in U^{\prime}_{m}
, it follows that
m\in\eta_{h}(p(x))(n)
. Therefore,
m\in\eta_{h}(p(x))(n)
if and only if
h(x)\in U^{\prime}_{m}
.

Now fix any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
h(x)\in U^{\prime}_{m}
. Since
s^{\prime}_{m}(p(x))=s^{\prime}_{m}(q(h(x)))=h(x)
, we have
m^{\prime}\in[m]^{\prime}_{p(x)}
if and only if
h(x)=s^{\prime}_{m}(p(x))\in U^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}
.

It follows that
\eta_{h}(p(x))(n)=[m]^{\prime}_{p(x)}
, hence

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(\theta_{X}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(\langle p(x),[n]_{p(x)}\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle p(x),\eta_{h}(p(x))(n)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle p(x),[m]^{\prime}_{p(x)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle q(h(x)),[m]^{\prime}_{q(h(x))}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{Y}(h(x)).

Therefore,
\theta\colon 1_{{\mathrm{\mathbb{Act}}}({\mathcal{C}})}\to{\mathcal{F}}\circ{\mathcal{G}}
is a natural isomorphism.
