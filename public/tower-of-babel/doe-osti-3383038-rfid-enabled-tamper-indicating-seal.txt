RFID-Enabled Tamper-Indicating Seal
A smart adhesive seal that wirelessly verifies whether a protected asset has remained secure.

Value Proposition
Tamper-indicating seals protect sensitive materials, valuable equipment, and critical shipments.
Most conventional seals must be checked through manual visual inspection and do not provide a
digital record of their condition. Some attempts to remove, alter, or bypass a seal may also leave
little visible damage, making tampering difficult to identify during routine inspections.

Los Alamos National Laboratory has developed a smart tamper-indicating seal that converts
even subtle tampering into a persistent digital record. The seal communicates its identity and
status wirelessly through standard radio-frequency identification (RFID) readers, giving
organizations a faster, more reliable way to verify that an asset has remained secure.

Overview
Tamper-indicating seals are designed to provide evidence of unauthorized access, tampering, or
counterfeiting. They are commonly used to protect shipping containers, equipment, hazardous
materials, and other high-value items that must remain secure between inspections.

Conventional seals typically rely on manual visual inspection, but signs of tampering are not
always obvious, and different inspectors may reach different conclusions. In nuclear facilities
and other hazardous environments, manual seal inspections may require personnel to work near
hazardous materials, increasing inspection burden and potential occupational exposure.

Los Alamos National Laboratory has developed a flexible, adhesive RFID-enabled tamper-
indicating seal that records a persistent change when tampering occurs. Built-in electronics
detect the event and store the seal’s tamper status so that both its identity and condition can be
retrieved wirelessly in real time or during subsequent interrogation.

In prototype testing, the seal has been interrogated from distances greater than 15 feet using
standard RFID readers, without physical contact, opening the container, or handling the
protected item. This provides organizations with a faster, more reliable, and more auditable way
to verify whether an asset has remained secure while creating a digital record of the seal’s
condition. The technology is intended for government and commercial applications requiring
secure, remote verification of high-value assets.

Technology Description
The technology is a flexible, adhesive tamper-indicating seal that combines two primary
elements: a tamper-sensitive feature and an electronic circuit. The tamper-sensitive feature is
designed to respond when someone attempts to remove, alter, or damage the seal. It functions as

a built-in tripwire, converting physical disturbance into a persistent change that can be detected
electronically.

When the tamper-sensitive feature is disturbed, measurable electrical characteristics of the seal
change relative to its established intact condition. The electronic circuit measures these
characteristics and compares them with the seal’s baseline state. If the measured change exceeds
a defined threshold, the system records a persistent tamper status in memory. Depending on the
application, the feature can also respond to extreme temperatures, chemicals, vibration, or other
environmental conditions.

The circuit is integrated with an RFID tag that communicates the seal’s unique identity and
stored tamper status to a standard UHF RFID reader. RFID uses radio waves to exchange
information between the seal and the reader, allowing inspectors to verify the seal remotely
without opening the container or physically handling the protected asset. This capability can
reduce inspection time and limit the need for personnel to approach hazardous or sensitive
materials.

The current technology is implemented as a flexible adhesive seal designed for application to
containers and other high-value assets. It is also compatible with other wireless communication
technologies, including ultra-wideband (UWB), which can provide longer communication
distances or more precise location information for specialized applications.

Advantages
  • Replaces subjective visual inspection with objective, electronic verification.
  • Reports seal identity and tamper status wirelessly without handling the protected asset.
  • Records tampering as a persistent, machine-readable status.
  • Designed to detect multiple forms of attack, including mechanical, chemical, and thermal
 tampering.
  • Works with standard RFID infrastructure, reducing deployment costs and simplifying
 adoption.
  • Combines tamper detection, wireless communication, and unique identification in a
 single seal.
  • Uses a flexible, adhesive form factor that can be sized for different assets and containers.
  • Designed for scalable manufacturing for commercial and government applications.

Market Applications
  • Nuclear safeguards and nuclear material accountability.
  • Secure components for mission-critical systems, including defense and aerospace.
  • Pharmaceutical packaging and cold-chain security.
  • Supply chain and logistics.
  • Anti-counterfeiting and product authentication.
  • Hazardous material storage and transportation.
  • Industrial asset tracking.
  • Secure evidence and chain-of-custody systems.
  • High-value equipment and electronics protection.
