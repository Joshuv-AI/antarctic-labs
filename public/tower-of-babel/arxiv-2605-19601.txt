First Chen Inequality for CR-Warped Product Submanifolds of a Complex Space Form and Applications
Abdulqader Mustafa, Monika Sati, Uday Chand De, Cenap Özel, Alexander Pigazzini

Abstract
In this paper, the first Chen inequality is proved for CR-warped product submanifolds in complex space forms. This inequality involves intrinsic invariants (a leaf-wise δ \delta -invariant and the sectional curvature) controlled by an extrinsic one (the mean curvature vector), which provides an answer to Problem 1 . We carefully distinguish the leaf-wise δ \delta -invariant of a factor (used in the bound) from the intrinsic Chen invariant of the same factor, the two being related, on the totally real factor, by the Bishop–O’Neill formula. The bound is sharp and is uniform in the sign of the holomorphic sectional curvature c c . As a geometric application, we derive necessary conditions for the immersed CR-warped product submanifold to be minimal in a complex space form, providing a partial answer to a well-known problem proposed by S. S. Chern (Problem 2 ). For further research directions, we address a couple of open problems (Problem 3 and Problem 4 ). AMS Subject Classification (2010) : 53C15; 53C40; 53C42; 53B25. Keywords : Mean curvature vector; δ \delta -invariant; scalar curvature; C ​ R CR -warped products; minimal submanifolds; complex space forms.

Introduction

Warped products play important roles in general relativity, where they provide useful mathematical models of spacetime: the Robertson–Walker spacetime, the Friedmann cosmological models and the standard static spacetime are all warped products. Warped products also provide natural settings to model spacetime near black holes; e.g. the Schwarzschild spacetime admits a warped product construction
[
18
]
.

Extrinsic and intrinsic Riemannian invariants have wide applications in many fields of science other than differential geometry; in particular they are of considerable significance in general relativity
[
19
]
. Among extrinsic invariants, the shape operator and the squared mean curvature are central; among intrinsic invariants, sectional, Ricci and scalar curvatures together with Chen’s
\delta
-invariant are the most prominent. Motivated by the Nash embedding theorem, the program of B.–Y. Chen is to search for sharp control of extrinsic quantities by intrinsic ones, and vice versa
[
6
,
10
]
. This was the original motivation behind the following problem.

Problem 1
.

[
5
]
Establish simple relationships between the main extrinsic invariants and the main intrinsic invariants of a submanifold.

Several famous results in differential geometry, such as the isoperimetric inequality, Chern–Lashof’s inequality and the Gauss–Bonnet theorem, can be regarded as results in this respect.

It is well known
[
4
]
that the following two conditions are necessary for the immersion to be minimal in Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}^{m}
:

Condition 1:
If
\varphi:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{m}
is a minimal immersion from a manifold of positive dimension into a Euclidean
m
-space, then
M^{n}
is non-compact.

Condition 2:
If
\varphi:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{m}
is a minimal immersion from a manifold of positive dimension into a Euclidean
m
-space, then the Ricci tensor of
M^{n}
is negative semi-definite.

S. S. Chern asked on page 13 of
[
13
]
to search for further necessary conditions on the Riemannian metric of a submanifold
M^{n}
in order to admit an isometric minimal immersion into a Euclidean space. Later, Chen materialized this goal for warped product submanifolds in the following form.

Problem 2
.

[
6
]
Given a warped product
N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}
, what are the necessary conditions for the warped product to admit a minimal isometric immersion in a Euclidean
m
-space
\mathbb{E}^{m}
(or in
\tilde{M}^{2m}(c)
)?

In
[
4
]
, Chen initiated a significant inequality in terms of the intrinsic
\delta
-invariant. This celebrated inequality drew the attention of several authors
[
10
]
. Motivated by Chen’s results, we construct a new general inequality in terms of the
\delta
-invariant, this time for CR-warped product submanifolds of complex space forms. As we will see, the natural quantity controlled by the inequality is the
leaf-wise

\delta
-invariant of the factor (Definition
2.2
), which for the holomorphic factor
N_{T}
coincides with its intrinsic Chen invariant, while for the totally real factor
N_{\perp}
is related to its intrinsic Chen invariant by the Bishop–O’Neill formula (Proposition
2.3
).

We first recall the following algebraic lemma from
[
4
]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta
be
(n+1)
real numbers (
n\geq 2
) such that

\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\Bigr)^{\!2}\;=\;(n-1)\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha^{2}_{i}+\beta\Bigr).

Then
2\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\geq\beta
, with equality if and only if
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=\dots=\alpha_{n}
.

We also recall the definition of the Chen first invariant, which is the main intrinsic invariant in our inequality:

\delta_{\tilde{M}^{m}}(x)\;=\;\tilde{\tau}(T_{x}\tilde{M}^{m})-\inf\bigl\{\tilde{K}(\pi):\pi\subset T_{x}\tilde{M}^{m},\ \dim\pi=2\bigr\},\qquad x\in\tilde{M}^{m}.

(1.1)

The following theorem was first proved for Riemannian submanifolds in real space forms by Chen in
[
4
]
. It is known as the
Chen first inequality
. Since its publication, it has been extended to Riemannian submanifolds in various ambient spaces
[
11
]
; however, it had not been proved for CR-warped product submanifolds in any complex ambient manifold. Thus, this work is devoted to proving such an inequality in the setting of CR-warped product submanifolds of complex space forms.

The classical Chen first inequality reads as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Chen 1993,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
M^{n}
(
n\geq 3
) be an
n
-dimensional submanifold of a real space form
\tilde{M}^{m}(c)
of constant sectional curvature
c
. Then

\delta_{M}(x)\;\leq\;\frac{n^{2}(n-2)}{2(n-1)}\|\vec{H}\|^{2}+\frac{(n+1)(n-2)}{2}\,c,

(1.2)

where
\delta_{M}(x)=\tau(T_{x}M^{n})-\inf K(\pi)
. Equivalently,

\inf K\;\geq\;\tau(T_{x}M^{n})\;-\;\frac{n^{2}(n-2)}{2(n-1)}\|\vec{H}\|^{2}\;-\;\frac{(n+1)(n-2)}{2}\,c.

Equality holds if and only if, with respect to a suitable orthonormal frame
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},e_{n+1},\ldots,e_{m}
, the shape operators of
M^{n}
in
\tilde{M}^{m}(c)
take the forms

A_{e_{n+1}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\mu_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\mu&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\mu\end{pmatrix},\quad\mu=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2},

A_{e_{r}}=\begin{pmatrix}h^{r}_{11}&h^{r}_{12}&0&\cdots&0\\
h^{r}_{12}&-h^{r}_{11}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad r=n+2,\ldots,m.

5.1
and
5.2

2
Preliminaries

Let
\tilde{M}^{m}
be a smooth Riemannian manifold (real dimension
m
). If
X,Y\in T_{x}\tilde{M}^{m}
are linearly independent, the
sectional curvature
of the
2
-plane
\pi
spanned by
X
and
Y
is

\tilde{K}(X\wedge Y)\;=\;\frac{\tilde{g}(\tilde{R}(X,Y)Y,X)}{\tilde{g}(X,X)\tilde{g}(Y,Y)-(\tilde{g}(X,Y))^{2}},

(2.1)

where
\tilde{g}
is the Riemannian metric on
\tilde{M}^{m}
. If
\pi
is spanned by orthonormal vectors
X,Y
at
x
, the previous formula reduces to

\tilde{K}(\pi)\;=\;\tilde{K}_{\tilde{M}^{m}}(X\wedge Y)\;=\;\tilde{g}(\tilde{R}(X,Y)Y,X).

(2.2)

The
scalar curvature
of
\tilde{M}^{m}
at
x
is

\tilde{\tau}(T_{x}\tilde{M}^{m})\;=\;\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\tilde{K}_{ij},

(2.3)

where
\tilde{K}_{ij}=\tilde{K}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})
. Equivalently,

2\tilde{\tau}(T_{x}\tilde{M}^{m})\;=\;\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq m}\tilde{K}_{ij}.

(2.4)

Partial scalar curvature and Chen invariants on subspaces.

For a linear subspace
V\subseteq T_{x}\tilde{M}^{m}
of dimension
k\geq 2
, the
partial scalar curvature of
\tilde{M}^{m}
on
V
at
x
is

\tilde{\tau}(V)\;:=\;\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\tilde{K}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j}),

(2.5)

where
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
is any orthonormal basis of
V
(the sum is independent of the basis). The
leaf-wise Chen first invariant of
\tilde{M}^{m}
on
V
is

\delta(V)\;:=\;\tilde{\tau}(V)\;-\;\inf\bigl\{\tilde{K}(\pi):\pi\subseteq V,\ \dim\pi=2\bigr\}.

(2.6)

When
V=T_{x}\tilde{M}^{m}
,
\delta(V)=\delta_{\tilde{M}^{m}}(x)
recovers (
1.1
).

Gradient and Laplacian.

For a smooth function
\psi
on
\tilde{M}^{m}
, the gradient
\tilde{\nabla}\psi
and Laplacian
\Delta\psi
are

\tilde{g}(\tilde{\nabla}\psi,X)\;=\;X(\psi),\qquad\Delta\psi\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl((\tilde{\nabla}_{e_{i}}e_{i})\psi-e_{i}e_{i}\psi\bigr),

(2.7)

i.e. the geometer’s sign convention (
\Delta=-\mathrm{div}\,\mathrm{grad}
).

Warped products.

In an attempt to construct manifolds of negative curvature, R. L. Bishop and B. O’Neill
[
3
]
introduced warped product manifolds. Let
N_{1},N_{2}
be two Riemannian manifolds with metrics
g_{N_{1}},g_{N_{2}}
, and let
f>0
be a smooth function on
N_{1}
. The warped product
M:=N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}
is the product manifold
N_{1}\times N_{2}
equipped with the metric
g_{M}=g_{N_{1}}+f^{2}g_{N_{2}}
. The warped product is
trivial
if
f
is constant. For a non-trivial warped product,
\mathfrak{D}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{2}
denote the distributions tangent to the leaves and fibers respectively.

Bishop–O’Neill formulas.

For
X,Y
tangent to
N_{1}
and
V,W
tangent to
N_{2}
, the curvature of
M
satisfies

R^{M}(X,Y)Z\;=\;R^{N_{1}}(X,Y)Z\quad\text{if }X,Y,Z\in TN_{1},

(2.8)

and, for unit
V,W\in TN_{2}
(i.e.
g_{M}
-orthonormal in
TN_{2}
, equivalently
\tilde{V}:=fV,\ \tilde{W}:=fW
are
g_{N_{2}}
-orthonormal at the corresponding point of
N_{2}
),

K^{M}(V\wedge W)\;=\;\frac{1}{f^{2}}\,K^{N_{2}}(\tilde{V}\wedge\tilde{W})\;-\;\frac{\|\nabla f\|^{2}}{f^{2}}.

(2.9)

The leaves
N_{1}\times\{q\}
are totally geodesic in
M
, while the fibers
\{p\}\times N_{2}
are totally umbilical in
M
with mean curvature vector
-\nabla\ln f
.

Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n_{1}},e_{n_{1}+1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a local orthonormal frame of
TM
with
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n_{1}}\}
tangent to
N_{1}
and
\{e_{n_{1}+1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
tangent to
N_{2}
. The fundamental warped-product identity (see e.g.
[
6
,
10
,
11
]
) is

\sum_{a=1}^{n_{1}}\sum_{A=n_{1}+1}^{n}K^{M}(e_{a}\wedge e_{A})\;=\;\frac{n_{2}\,\Delta f}{f}.

(2.10)

Second fundamental form of an isometric immersion.

For an isometric immersion
\varphi:M^{n}\to\tilde{M}^{m}
with second fundamental form
h
and shape operator
A_{\xi}
,

g(A_{\xi}X,Y)=g(h(X,Y),\xi),\qquad X,Y\in\Gamma(TM^{n}),\ \xi\in\Gamma(T^{\perp}M^{n}).

(2.11)

Choose a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},e_{n+1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
adapted to
\varphi
, with
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
tangent and
\{e_{n+1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
normal to
M^{n}
. The mean curvature vector is

\vec{H}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(e_{i},e_{i}).

(2.12)

M^{n}
is
minimal
if
\vec{H}\equiv 0
and
totally umbilical
if
h(X,Y)=g(X,Y)\vec{H}
for all
X,Y

[
9
]
.

The Gauss equation reads

R(X,Y,Z,W)=\tilde{R}(X,Y,Z,W)+g(h(X,W),h(Y,Z))-g(h(X,Z),h(Y,W)).

(2.13)

We denote the coefficients of
h
in the chosen frame by

h^{r}_{ij}=g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r}),\qquad i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\ r\in\{n+1,\ldots,m\}.

(2.14)

From (
2.14
), (
2.2
), (
2.13
) we obtain

K(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=\tilde{K}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})+\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\bigl(h^{r}_{ii}h^{r}_{jj}-(h^{r}_{ij})^{2}\bigr).

(2.15)

Summing over the tangent frame and using (
2.4
),

2\tau(T_{x}M^{n})\;=\;2\tilde{\tau}(T_{x}M^{n})+n^{2}\|\vec{H}\|^{2}-\|h\|^{2},

(2.16)

where
\tilde{\tau}(T_{x}M^{n})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\tilde{K}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})
is the partial scalar curvature of
\tilde{M}^{m}
on
T_{x}M^{n}
. More generally, for any subspace
V\subseteq T_{x}M^{n}
,

2\tau^{M}(V)\;=\;2\tilde{\tau}(V)+\sum_{r}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{tr}_{V}A^{r}\bigr)^{2}-\|A^{r}|_{V}\|^{2}\Bigr],

(2.17)

where
\tau^{M}(V)=\sum_{e_{i},e_{j}\in V,\ i<j}K^{M}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})
,
A^{r}=A_{e_{r}}
, and the trace and squared norm of
A^{r}|_{V}
are taken over an orthonormal basis of
V
.

Complex space forms.

Let
\tilde{M}^{2m}
be a smooth manifold (real dimension
2m
) with almost complex structure
J
(
J^{2}=-I
). If
J
is integrable (the Nijenhuis tensor vanishes),
(\tilde{M}^{2m},J)
is a complex manifold. If
\tilde{M}^{2m}
also carries a Hermitian metric
\tilde{g}
(so
\tilde{g}(JX,JY)=\tilde{g}(X,Y)
) and the Kähler form
\omega(X,Y)=\tilde{g}(JX,Y)
is closed, then
(\tilde{M}^{2m},J,\tilde{g})
is Kähler. Equivalently,
\tilde{\nabla}J=0
. A Kähler manifold of constant holomorphic sectional curvature
c\in\mathbb{R}
is a
complex space form

\tilde{M}^{2m}(c)
. Its Riemannian curvature tensor is

\tilde{R}(X,Y,Z,W)=\frac{c}{4}\Bigl\{\tilde{g}(X,W)\tilde{g}(Y,Z)-\tilde{g}(X,Z)\tilde{g}(Y,W)\\
+\tilde{g}(JX,W)\tilde{g}(JY,Z)-\tilde{g}(JX,Z)\tilde{g}(JY,W)+2\,\tilde{g}(X,JY)\tilde{g}(JZ,W)\Bigr\}.

(2.18)

From (
2.18
) one computes the sectional curvature of a 2-plane spanned by orthonormal
X,Y
:

\tilde{K}(X\wedge Y)\;=\;\frac{c}{4}\bigl(1+3\,\tilde{g}(JX,Y)^{2}\bigr).

(2.19)

In particular, a
holomorphic
2-plane (i.e.
Y=JX
) has
\tilde{K}=c
, while a 2-plane on which
J
acts as zero (i.e.
JX\perp Y
) has
\tilde{K}=c/4
. For any 2-plane
\pi
in
\tilde{M}^{2m}(c)
,

\min\bigl(\tfrac{c}{4},\,c\bigr)\;\leq\;\tilde{K}(\pi)\;\leq\;\max\bigl(\tfrac{c}{4},\,c\bigr).

(2.20)

For a
J
-invariant subspace
V
of even real dimension
\geq 2
, both bounds are attained: the minimum on a non-
J
-invariant 2-plane (which always exists as soon as
\dim V\geq 4
, and for
\dim V=2
if and only if
V
is not itself
J
-invariant) and the maximum on a holomorphic 2-plane.

CR submanifolds.

Following
[
1
]
, a submanifold
M^{n}
of an almost Hermitian manifold
\tilde{M}^{2m}
is called a
CR-submanifold
if
TM^{n}
admits an orthogonal decomposition into a holomorphic distribution
\mathfrak{D}_{T}
(
J\mathfrak{D}_{T}\subseteq TM^{n}
) and a totally real distribution
\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}
(
J\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}\subseteq T^{\perp}M^{n}
):

(i)

TM^{n}=\mathfrak{D}_{T}\oplus\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}
;

(ii)

\mathfrak{D}_{T}
is
J
-invariant:
J\mathfrak{D}_{T}=\mathfrak{D}_{T}
;

(iii)

\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}
is anti-invariant:
J\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}\subseteq T^{\perp}M^{n}
.

Denoting by
\nu
the maximal
J
-invariant subbundle of
T^{\perp}M^{n}
,

T^{\perp}M^{n}=F\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}\oplus\nu.

(2.21)

A
CR-warped product submanifold
is a warped product
M^{n}=N_{T}\times_{f}N_{\perp}
, where
N_{T}
is holomorphic (
TN_{T}=\mathfrak{D}_{T}
) and
N_{\perp}
is totally real (
TN_{\perp}=\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}
). Note that, since
J
acts as a complex structure on
\mathfrak{D}_{T}
, the dimension
n_{1}=\dim N_{T}
is necessarily even.

Ambient scalar curvature of a CR submanifold.

For a CR submanifold
M^{n}
of
\tilde{M}^{2m}(c)
with
n_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{D}_{T}
and
n_{2}=\dim\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}
(
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
), a direct computation from (
2.19
) gives

2\tilde{\tau}(T_{x}M^{n})\;=\;\frac{c}{4}\bigl[n(n-1)+3n_{1}\bigr].

(2.22)

Indeed, choose an orthonormal basis of
T_{x}M^{n}
such that
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n_{1}}\}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{T}
with
Je_{2a-1}=e_{2a}
for
a=1,\ldots,n_{1}/2
, and
\{e_{n_{1}+1},\ldots,e_{n}\}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}
. Then
\tilde{g}(Je_{i},e_{j})^{2}=1
exactly for the
n_{1}
ordered pairs
(2a-1,2a)
and
(2a,2a-1)
, and vanishes otherwise. Summing (
2.19
) over
i\neq j
yields (
2.22
).

Remark 2.1
.

Equivalently, for the holomorphic factor
T_{x}N_{T}
alone (which is
J
-invariant of dimension
n_{1}
and contains
n_{1}/2
holomorphic 2-planes from the chosen basis):

2\tilde{\tau}(T_{x}N_{T})\;=\;\frac{c}{4}\bigl[n_{1}(n_{1}-1)+3n_{1}\bigr]\;=\;\frac{c}{4}\,n_{1}(n_{1}+2),

(2.23)

while for the totally real factor
T_{x}N_{\perp}
, since
J\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}\perp\mathfrak{D}_{\perp}
,

2\tilde{\tau}(T_{x}N_{\perp})\;=\;\frac{c}{4}\,n_{2}(n_{2}-1).

(2.24)

Combining (
2.16
) with (
2.22
) yields the fundamental identity for a CR submanifold of
\tilde{M}^{2m}(c)
:

\;n^{2}\|\vec{H}\|^{2}\;=\;2\tau(T_{x}M^{n})+\|h\|^{2}-\frac{c}{4}\bigl[n(n-1)+3n_{1}\bigr].\;

(2.25)

Leaf-wise
\delta
-invariant of a CR-warped product factor.

Throughout the rest of the paper, fix a CR-warped product
M^{n}=N_{T}\times_{f}N_{\perp}
isometrically immersed in a complex space form
\tilde{M}^{2m}(c)
. The natural quantity controlled by the inequalities we prove is the following.

Definition 2.2
.

For
x\in M^{n}
and
V\in\{T_{x}N_{T},\,T_{x}N_{\perp}\}
with
\dim V\geq 2
, set

\hat{\delta}(V)(x)\;:=\;\tau^{M}(V)\;-\;\inf\bigl\{K^{M}(\pi):\pi\subseteq V,\ \dim\pi=2\bigr\}.

(2.26)

We call
\hat{\delta}(T_{x}N_{T})
and
\hat{\delta}(T_{x}N_{\perp})
the
leaf-wise first Chen invariants
of
N_{T}
and
N_{\perp}
at
x
. Throughout,
K^{M}
and
\tau^{M}
denote sectional and partial scalar curvatures of
M^{n}
(
not
of the factors).

We now relate
\hat{\delta}(\cdot)
to the genuinely intrinsic Chen invariants of the factor manifolds.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
x=(p,q)\in M^{n}=N_{T}\times_{f}N_{\perp}
. Then:

(a)

Since the leaf
N_{T}\times\{q\}
is totally geodesic in
M
and the metric induced by
g_{M}
on it is
g_{N_{T}}
,

K^{M}|_{T_{x}N_{T}}=K^{N_{T}}\bigr|_{T_{p}N_{T}},\qquad\tau^{M}(T_{x}N_{T})=\tau^{N_{T}}(p),\qquad\hat{\delta}(T_{x}N_{T})(x)=\delta_{N_{T}}(p).

(2.27)

(b)

Since the fiber
\{p\}\times N_{\perp}
carries the metric
f(p)^{2}\,g_{N_{\perp}}
in
g_{M}
, the Bishop–O’Neill formula (
2.9
) gives, for any orthonormal
V,W\in T_{x}N_{\perp}
,

K^{M}(V\wedge W)\;=\;\frac{1}{f(p)^{2}}\,K^{N_{\perp}}(\tilde{V}\wedge\tilde{W})\;-\;\frac{\|\nabla f(p)\|^{2}}{f(p)^{2}},

(2.28)

where
\tilde{V}=f(p)\,V
,
\tilde{W}=f(p)\,W
are
g_{N_{\perp}}
-orthonormal. Hence

\tau^{M}(T_{x}N_{\perp})\;=\;\frac{1}{f(p)^{2}}\,\tau^{N_{\perp}}(q)\;-\;\binom{n_{2}}{2}\frac{\|\nabla f(p)\|^{2}}{f(p)^{2}},

(2.29)

and, taking infima of (
2.28
) over 2-planes in
T_{x}N_{\perp}
,

\hat{\delta}(T_{x}N_{\perp})(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{f(p)^{2}}\,\delta_{N_{\perp}}(q)\;-\;\Bigl[\binom{n_{2}}{2}-1\Bigr]\frac{\|\nabla f(p)\|^{2}}{f(p)^{2}}.

(2.30)

Proof.

Part (a) follows from the standard fact that for an isometric, totally geodesic embedding
\iota:L\hookrightarrow(M,g_{M})
, the intrinsic curvature of
L
(in the induced metric) coincides with the restriction of
K^{M}
to 2-planes tangent to
L
(Gauss equation with
h=0
). For the warped product, the leaf
N_{T}\times\{q\}
is totally geodesic by Bishop–O’Neill
[
3
,
18
]
, and the induced metric on it is
g_{N_{T}}
.

For (b), (
2.28
) is the Bishop–O’Neill formula (
2.9
) restricted to a 2-plane in
T_{x}N_{\perp}
. Summing over a
g_{M}
-orthonormal basis of
T_{x}N_{\perp}
yields (
2.29
). Subtracting an infimum (which corresponds, via the bijection
\pi\mapsto\tilde{\pi}
between
g_{M}
-orthonormal and
g_{N_{\perp}}
-orthonormal 2-planes, to an infimum on
N_{\perp}
) gives

3
Basic Lemmas

This section collects three computational lemmas. The first is the warped-product identity, already stated as (
2.10
): for
M^{n}=N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}
,

\sum_{a=1}^{n_{1}}\sum_{A=n_{1}+1}^{n}K^{M}(e_{a}\wedge e_{A})=\frac{n_{2}\Delta f}{f}.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi
be an isometric immersion of a warped product
M^{n}=N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}
into a Riemannian manifold
\tilde{M}^{m}
. Then

\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\!(h_{ij}^{n+1})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(h_{ij}^{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}h_{11}^{r}h_{22}^{r}-\!\!\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\!\!(h_{12}^{r})^{2}\\
=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=3\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\!(h_{ij}^{n+1})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{i,j=3}^{n}(h_{ij}^{r})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}(h_{11}^{r}+h_{22}^{r})^{2}\\
+\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\sum_{j=3}^{n}\bigl((h_{1j}^{r})^{2}+(h_{2j}^{r})^{2}\bigr).

(3.2)

Proof.

Split the index sets
\{1,\ldots,n\}=\{1,2\}\cup\{3,\ldots,n\}
. The first two summands on the left expand as

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\!(h_{ij}^{n+1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=3\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\!(h_{ij}^{n+1})^{2}+(h_{12}^{n+1})^{2}+\sum_{j=3}^{n}\bigl((h_{1j}^{n+1})^{2}+(h_{2j}^{n+1})^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(h_{ij}^{r})^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{i,j=3}^{n}(h_{ij}^{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{j=3}^{n}\bigl((h_{1j}^{r})^{2}+(h_{2j}^{r})^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}(h_{12}^{r})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\bigl((h_{11}^{r})^{2}+(h_{22}^{r})^{2}\bigr).

Using

\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}h_{11}^{r}h_{22}^{r}+\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\!\!\bigl((h_{11}^{r})^{2}+(h_{22}^{r})^{2}\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\!\!\bigl(h_{11}^{r}+h_{22}^{r}\bigr)^{2},

the identity follows by direct substitution; the term
(h_{12}^{n+1})^{2}+\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}(h_{12}^{r})^{2}-\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}(h_{12}^{r})^{2}=0
cancels.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi
be an isometric immersion of a warped product
M^{n}=N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}
into a Riemannian manifold
\tilde{M}^{m}
. Then

\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=3\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\!(h_{ij}^{n+1})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{i,j=3}^{n}(h_{ij}^{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\sum_{j=3}^{n}\bigl((h_{1j}^{r})^{2}+(h_{2j}^{r})^{2}\bigr)\\
=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=3\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}^{n_{1}}\!(h_{ab}^{n+1})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A,B=n_{1}+1\\
A\neq B\end{subarray}}^{n}\!\!\!(h_{AB}^{n+1})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{a,b=3}^{n_{1}}(h_{ab}^{r})^{2}\\
+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{A,B=n_{1}+1}^{n}(h_{AB}^{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\sum_{a=3}^{n_{1}}\bigl((h_{1a}^{r})^{2}+(h_{2a}^{r})^{2}\bigr)\\
+\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\sum_{a=3}^{n_{1}}\sum_{A=n_{1}+1}^{n}(h_{aA}^{r})^{2}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Split
\{3,\ldots,n\}=\{3,\ldots,n_{1}\}\cup\{n_{1}+1,\ldots,n\}
, so that

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=3\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\!(h_{ij}^{n+1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=3\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}^{n_{1}}\!(h_{ab}^{n+1})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A,B=n_{1}+1\\
A\neq B\end{subarray}}^{n}\!\!(h_{AB}^{n+1})^{2}+\!\!\sum_{a=3}^{n_{1}}\sum_{A=n_{1}+1}^{n}\!\!(h_{aA}^{n+1})^{2},

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{i,j=3}^{n}(h_{ij}^{r})^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{a,b=3}^{n_{1}}(h_{ab}^{r})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{A,B=n_{1}+1}^{n}(h_{AB}^{r})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{a=3}^{n_{1}}\sum_{A=n_{1}+1}^{n}(h_{aA}^{r})^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\sum_{j=3}^{n}\bigl((h_{1j}^{r})^{2}+(h_{2j}^{r})^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\sum_{a=3}^{n_{1}}\bigl((h_{1a}^{r})^{2}+(h_{2a}^{r})^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\sum_{A=n_{1}+1}^{n}\bigl((h_{1A}^{r})^{2}+(h_{2A}^{r})^{2}\bigr).

Combining and using the identity

\sum_{a=3}^{n_{1}}\!\sum_{A=n_{1}+1}^{n}\!(h_{aA}^{n+1})^{2}+\!\!\sum_{r=n+2}^{m}\sum_{a=3}^{n_{1}}\sum_{A=n_{1}+1}^{n}\!\!(h_{aA}^{r})^{2}\;=\;\sum_{r=n+1}^{m}\sum_{a=3}^{n_{1}}\sum_{A=n_{1}+1}^{n}(h_{aA}^{r})^{2}

yields (
3.3
).
∎

4

For the statement of the main theorem we abbreviate

\tilde{K}_{\min}(V):=\inf\bigl\{\tilde{K}(\pi):\pi\subseteq V,\ \dim\pi=2\bigr\}

(4.1)

for a subspace

\tilde{K}_{\min}(T_{x}N_{T})\;=\;\min\bigl(\tfrac{c}{4},\,c\bigr),\qquad\tilde{K}_{\min}(T_{x}N_{\perp})\;=\;\tfrac{c}{4}.

(4.4)

(ii)

5
Answer to Chern’s Problem: Necessary Conditions for
CR
-Warped Products to be Minimal

Setting
\vec{H}=0
in Theorem
4.1
yields the following necessary conditions for a CR-warped product to admit a minimal isometric immersion into a complex space form.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\varphi:M^{n}=N_{T}\times_{f}N_{\perp}\to\tilde{M}^{2m}(c)
be a minimal isometric immersion of a CR-warped product. Then for each
x\in M^{n}
, denoting
p=\mathrm{pr}_{N_{T}}(x)
:

\;\delta_{N_{T}}(p)+\frac{n_{2}\Delta f}{f}\;\leq\;\frac{n_{1}(n_{1}+2n_{2}+2)}{2}\cdot\frac{c}{4}-\tilde{K}_{\min}(T_{x}N_{T}).\;

(5.1)

Equivalently:

•

if
c\geq 0
,
\;\delta_{N_{T}}(p)+\dfrac{n_{2}\Delta f}{f}\leq\dfrac{n_{1}(n_{1}+2n_{2}+2)-2}{2}\cdot\dfrac{c}{4};

•

if
c<0
,
\;\delta_{N_{T}}(p)+\dfrac{n_{2}\Delta f}{f}\leq\dfrac{n_{1}(n_{1}+2n_{2}+2)}{2}\cdot\dfrac{c}{4}-c=\dfrac{n_{1}(n_{1}+2n_{2}+2)-8}{8}\cdot c.

Corollary 5.2
.

Under the same hypotheses, for each
x\in M^{n}
, denoting
q=\mathrm{pr}_{N_{\perp}}(x)
:

\;\hat{\delta}(T_{x}N_{\perp})(x)+\frac{n_{2}\Delta f}{f}\;\leq\;\frac{n_{2}(n_{2}+2n_{1}-1)}{2}\cdot\frac{c}{4}-\frac{c}{4}\;=\;\frac{n_{2}(n_{2}+2n_{1}-1)-2}{2}\cdot\frac{c}{4}.\;

(5.2)

Equivalently, in intrinsic form (Remark
4.4
),

\delta_{N_{\perp}}(q)+f^{2}\,\frac{n_{2}\Delta f}{f}\;\leq\;f^{2}\cdot\frac{n_{2}(n_{2}+2n_{1}-1)-2}{2}\cdot\frac{c}{4}+\Bigl[\binom{n_{2}}{2}-1\Bigr]\|\nabla f\|^{2}.

(5.3)

Remark 5.3
.

For the flat ambient
c=0
(Euclidean case), both corollaries reduce to

\delta_{N_{T}}(p)+\frac{n_{2}\Delta f}{f}\leq 0,\qquad\hat{\delta}(T_{x}N_{\perp})(x)+\frac{n_{2}\Delta f}{f}\leq 0,

which provides the announced answer to Chern’s problem in
\mathbb{E}^{m}
: the sum of any leaf-wise
\delta
-invariant of either factor and
n_{2}\Delta f/f
must be non-positive. The intrinsic form of the second inequality is, by (
5.3
),

\delta_{N_{\perp}}(q)+n_{2}f\Delta f\;\leq\;\Bigl[\binom{n_{2}}{2}-1\Bigr]\|\nabla f\|^{2}.
