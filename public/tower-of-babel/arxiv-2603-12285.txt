The Fisher Paradox: Dissipation Interference in Information-Regularized Gradient Flows
Michael Farmer, Abhinav Kochar, Yugyung Lee

Abstract
We show that Fisher-regularized Wasserstein gradient flows exhibit a previously unrecognized interference mechanism in their dissipation identity: a cross-dissipation term whose sign becomes positive when the state width falls below a critical scale. In this regime the geometric Fisher channel transiently opposes descent of the baseline free-energy functional, producing what we term the Fisher Paradox . Restricting the flow to the Gaussian manifold yields an exact Riccati-type variance equation with a closed-form trajectory, exposing three dynamical regimes separated by two critical scales: σ = 1 \sigma=1 (cross-dissipation sign flip) and σ = ε \sigma=\sqrt{\varepsilon} (Fisher takeover). The variance potential V ⁡ ( u ) = u 2 − 2 ​ u − ε ​ ln ⁡ u V(u)=u^{2}-2u-\varepsilon\ln u contains a logarithmic centrifugal barrier that shifts the equilibrium attractor by Δ ​ σ ≈ ε / 4 \Delta\sigma\approx\varepsilon/4 . The interference persists for a duration t cross ∼ D KL t_{\mathrm{cross}}\sim D_{\mathrm{KL}} , linking the dissipation delay directly to initial information distance. Finite-difference simulations on a 512-point grid confirm all analytical predictions to within 5.21 × 10 − 4 5.21\times 10^{-4} mean relative error. Numerical experiments with bimodal and Laplace initial conditions confirm the effect persists beyond Gaussian closure, with direct implications for information-geometric dissipative dynamics.



I
Introduction

Wasserstein gradient flows provide the geometric foundation for a large class of
dissipative systems
4
;
8
;
10
:

\partial_{t}\rho=\nabla\cdot(\rho\,\nabla\mu),\qquad\mu=\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\rho}.

(1)

A common modification introduces Fisher information regularization.
We use the convention

\Phi_{F}(\rho)=\int|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}\,dx=\tfrac{1}{4}I(\rho)
,
where
I(\rho)=\int\rho|\nabla\log\rho|^{2}\,dx
is the standard Fisher
information
1
;
6
. The regularized functional is

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\mathcal{F}_{0}(\rho)+\varepsilon\,\Phi_{F}(\rho).

(2)

Although
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
decreases monotonically, we show that its
dissipation identity contains a cross-term whose sign depends on the state width

\sigma
: when
\sigma<1
the geometric Fisher channel transiently opposes
free-energy descent of the
baseline
functional
\mathcal{F}_{0}
.
We call this the
Fisher Paradox
: additive geometric regularization
retards free-energy descent during a well-defined transient window.
The effect is not simply a consequence of changing the objective:
it appears explicitly in the dissipation identity for
\mathcal{F}_{0}
,
the regularized system settles to a
permanently
shifted attractor

\sigma_{\infty}=1+\varepsilon/4\neq\sigma_{\infty}^{(0)}=1
, and the retardation
duration is set by the initial information distance—all absent from
unregularized flow.

II
Riccati Reduction and Exact Trajectory

Adding Fisher regularization modifies the chemical potential to

\mu_{\varepsilon}=\mu_{0}+\varepsilon\mu_{F}
with

\mu_{0}=\ln\rho+x^{2}/2
and

\mu_{F}=\frac{\delta\Phi_{F}}{\delta\rho}=-\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}.

(3)

The complete derivation is given in Supplemental Sec. A; the two factors of 2
cancel, yielding no prefactor.
For Gaussian states
\rho_{\sigma}(x)=(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-1/2}\exp(-x^{2}/2\sigma^{2})
,
one computes
\mu_{F}=(2\sigma^{2}-x^{2})/(4\sigma^{4})
,
and the variance moment equation yields the
\sigma
-ODE

\dot{\sigma}=\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{\sigma}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\sigma^{3}}.

(4)

Setting
u=\sigma^{2}
removes the singularity:

\dot{u}=2(1-u)+\frac{\varepsilon}{u}.

(5)

Equation (
5
) is separable with roots

u_{\pm}=\bigl(1\pm\sqrt{1+2\varepsilon}\bigr)/2
,
giving the implicit closed-form trajectory

t=\frac{u_{-}\ln|u-u_{-}|-u_{+}\ln|u-u_{+}|}{2(u_{+}-u_{-})}+C.

(6)

For
\varepsilon\to 0
, Eq. (
6
) reduces to

t\approx-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(1-u)+\tfrac{1}{2}u
, recovering standard OU
relaxation. For
u_{0}\ll 1
, the Fisher-dominant limit gives

t\approx(u^{-1}-u_{0}^{-1})/(2\varepsilon)
, confirming stiff dynamics
near the origin.

III
Three-Scale Phase Structure

The reduced system is a gradient flow in the variance potential

V(u)=u^{2}-2u-\varepsilon\ln u,

(7)

encoding OU confinement, entropy, and Fisher curvature respectively.
The
-\varepsilon\ln u
term acts as a
logarithmic centrifugal barrier
,
analogous to the centrifugal term in polar-coordinate quantum mechanics and to
the Coulomb-gas entropy repulsion in random matrix theory
7
:
it prevents variance collapse
but forces a mandatory macroscopic overshoot.
Two critical scales separate three regimes.

Regime I—Fisher dominance
(
\sigma<\sqrt{\varepsilon}
).
The Fisher drift
\varepsilon/(2\sigma^{3})
overwhelms the OU drift
1/\sigma
,
giving
\dot{\sigma}\approx\varepsilon/(2\sigma^{3})
. This is the
second
critical threshold
: below
\sigma\sim\sqrt{\varepsilon}
the system is numerically
stiff, with drift and sensitivity scaling as
\sigma^{-3}
and
\sigma^{-4}
.
Discretized implementations therefore encounter a practical
uncertainty floor

at
\sigma\sim\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, below which curvature regularization governs
the effective step size.

Regime II—Competition
(
\sqrt{\varepsilon}<\sigma<1
).
The
\mathcal{F}_{0}
dissipation rate splits into

\frac{d\mathcal{F}_{0}}{dt}=-\int\rho\|\nabla\mu_{0}\|^{2}-\varepsilon\int\rho\,\nabla\mu_{0}\cdot\nabla\mu_{F}.

(8)

The second term is positive here, producing the Fisher Paradox.

Regime III—Shifted equilibrium
(
\sigma>1
).
The fixed point
2u^{2}-2u-\varepsilon=0
gives

\sigma_{\infty}=\sqrt{u^{*}}\approx 1+\frac{\varepsilon}{4},\qquad\Delta\sigma\approx\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

(9)

The full energy decomposition (
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
,
\mathcal{F}_{0}
, and

\varepsilon\Phi_{F}
separately) is shown in Supplemental Fig. S2, where

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
decreases monotonically while
\mathcal{F}_{0}

experiences transient retardation at
-2.06\times
the unregularized
baseline rate during the competition window.

IV
KL Relaxation Law

The paradox persists until
\sigma=1
; the crossing time is

t_{\mathrm{cross}}=\int_{u_{0}}^{1}\frac{u\,du}{2(u-u^{2})+\varepsilon}.

(10)

For small
\varepsilon
and
\sigma_{0}
sufficiently below 1,

t_{\mathrm{cross}}\approx\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(1/\sigma_{0}^{2})
.
Since
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho_{0}\|\rho^{*})\approx-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln\sigma_{0}^{2}

for
\sigma_{0}\ll 1
,

\boxed{t_{\mathrm{cross}}\sim D_{\mathrm{KL}}.}

(11)

The paradox duration equals precisely the initial information distance from equilibrium.

VI
Discussion

The Fisher Paradox emerges from competition between the
-\varepsilon\ln u

centrifugal barrier and OU transport, producing stiff anti-collapse below

\sigma\sim\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, the paradox window
(\sqrt{\varepsilon},1)
,
and the permanently shifted attractor
\sigma_{\infty}=1+\varepsilon/4
.
The equilibrium shift is a permanent consequence of the regularization, not
merely a transient one: the regularized system settles to a state with
strictly higher
\mathcal{F}_{0}
than the unregularized fixed point, a
distinction measurable independently of the paradox window.
These results are exact on the Gaussian manifold.

Generality of the critical scale.

The threshold
\sigma_{c}=1
is specific to
V(x)=x^{2}/2
.
For a general confining potential
V(x)
, the cross-term changes sign where

\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{\sigma}}[xV^{\prime}(x)]=1
, giving

\sigma_{c}^{2}=1/\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{\sigma_{c}}}[V^{\prime\prime}(X)]
—the
reciprocal of the average curvature of
V
under the state.
For
V=x^{2}/(2L^{2})
,
\sigma_{c}=L
: the paradox window is set by the ratio of
state width to the natural equilibrium scale, not by any absolute threshold.

Non-Gaussian universality of
t_{\mathrm{cross}}
.

To verify that the mechanism is not restricted to Gaussian closure, we
repeated the numerical experiment with non-Gaussian initial conditions matched
to the same effective width
\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.5
: a symmetric bimodal
mixture
\rho_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{N}(-0.4,0.09)+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{N}(0.4,0.09)

and a Laplace distribution
\rho_{0}\propto e^{-|x|/b}
with
b=\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}/\sqrt{2}
.
In both cases the cross-dissipation term
C(\rho(t))=\int\rho\,\nabla\mu_{0}\!\cdot\!\nabla\mu_{F}\,dx

remains positive throughout
\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}<1
(Fig.
4
),
producing the same paradox duration
t_{\mathrm{cross}}\approx 1.32
.
While non-Gaussian structure modifies the initial magnitude of the cross-term
(
{\approx}0.56\times
for the bimodal mixture,
{\approx}4\times
for the Laplace cusp
relative to the Gaussian value
C(0)=6.0
; see Supplemental Table S3),
all trajectories converge to the same shifted equilibrium, consistent with a
common attractor structure.
The preserved
t_{\mathrm{cross}}
is not a coincidence; it follows from a
perturbative bound.
The variance ODE is
\dot{u}=2(1-u)+\varepsilon K(\rho)
, where

K(\rho)=-2\int x\rho\,\partial_{x}\mu_{F}\,dx
is the Fisher variance moment
(distinct from the free-energy cross-term
C
; for Gaussians
K_{G}=1/u
).
Writing
K=K_{G}+\delta K
, integrating against the shape-relaxation decay

|\delta K(\rho(t))|\lesssim|\delta K_{0}|e^{-2t}
(OU second eigenvalue), and
evaluating at the initial rate
\dot{u}_{0}=2(1-u_{0})+\varepsilon/u_{0}
gives

|\Delta t_{\mathrm{cross}}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon\,|\delta K_{0}|}{2\,\dot{u}_{0}}.

(12)

For the Laplace IC,
|\delta K_{0}/K_{G}|\sim\kappa_{4}/\sigma^{4}\approx 1.5

(
\kappa_{4}=6b^{4}
,
b=0.354
), giving

|\Delta t_{\mathrm{cross}}|\leq\frac{0.1\times(1.5/0.25)}{2\times 1.90}=0.079\approx 6\%\ \text{of}\ t_{\mathrm{cross}}.

The bound is conservative: near
u\to 1
where the integral weight concentrates,

\dot{u}\to 0^{+}
but the OU shape-relaxation rate (
\lambda_{2}=2
) already
dominates, so
\rho
is near-Gaussian by the time it matters.
Full derivation in Supplemental Sec. S6.
This strongly indicates that the paradox arises from the sign structure
of the dissipation identity, not from Gaussian closure
(see Supplemental Secs. S1–S6).

) gives a thermodynamic interpretation

Connections to existing literature.

The functional derivative
\mu_{F}=-\Delta\sqrt{\rho}/\sqrt{\rho}
is precisely
the quantum pressure (Bohm potential) of Madelung’s hydrodynamic formulation
of quantum mechanics
5
;
2
; retardation effects of the
quantum pressure on classical probability flows have been noted in that context.
The present analysis provides a clean dissipation-identity mechanism for such
retardation that was previously unavailable.
The Fisher-Rao gradient flow—which uses Fisher information as the

metric
rather than as an additive objective
3
—does
not exhibit this interference, reinforcing the design principle below.
In score-based diffusion
9
, Fisher geometry similarly enters the
score metric rather than the free energy, avoiding the paradox by construction.

In geometric frameworks where Fisher information enters the metric of the update
rule rather than the objective functional, this interference does not
arise
1
;
6
;
3
.
This establishes a structural design principle:
geometric regularizers
must be isolated to the update operator (metric tensor) rather than conflated
with the global transport objective.
Conflating the two channels induces the
thermodynamic delay and equilibrium shift quantified here.

The Fisher Paradox therefore reveals a previously unrecognized constraint on
information-geometric gradient flows: stabilizing information channels can transiently
oppose free-energy descent when curvature exceeds a critical scale.

This Supplemental Material provides complete derivations (Secs. A–F),
and full details of the non-Gaussian robustness study including numerical
method, initial conditions, cross-term diagnostics, paradox duration, and
extended figures (Secs. S1–S6). Throughout,

\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(t)=\int x^{2}\rho(x,t)\,dx
denotes the
second moment of the distribution; for Gaussian states it coincides with the
variance parameter
\sigma^{2}
.

A.; First variation of the Fisher functional

We use the convention

\Phi_{F}(\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}\,dx=\tfrac{1}{4}\,I(\rho),

(S1)

where
I(\rho)=\int\rho|\partial_{x}\log\rho|^{2}\,dx
is the standard
Fisher information.

Set
f=\sqrt{\rho}
, so
\rho=f^{2}
and
\Phi_{F}=\int f_{x}^{2}\,dx
.
For a mass-preserving variation
\rho_{\eta}=\rho+\eta h

(
\int h\,dx=0
,
h
smooth with rapid decay),

\delta f=\frac{d}{d\eta}\bigg|_{\eta=0}\sqrt{\rho+\eta h}=\frac{h}{2f}.

(S2)

Hence

\displaystyle\delta\Phi_{F}

\displaystyle=2\int f_{x}\,(\delta f)_{x}\,dx=2\int f_{x}\,\partial_{x}\!\left(\frac{h}{2f}\right)\!dx

\displaystyle=-2\int f_{xx}\,\frac{h}{2f}\,dx=-\int\frac{f_{xx}}{f}\,h\,dx,

(S3)

where integration by parts was applied in the last step.
Therefore

\boxed{\frac{\delta\Phi_{F}}{\delta\rho}=-\frac{\partial_{xx}\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\equiv\mu_{F}.}

(S4)

Note that both factors of 2 cancel: the
2
in
2\int f_{x}(\delta f)_{x}

and the
\tfrac{1}{2}
in
\delta f=h/(2f)
.
This is the definition of
\mu_{F}
used as Eq. (3) of the main letter.

For reference, the OU free energy gives

\mu_{0}=\frac{\delta F_{0}}{\delta\rho}=\ln\rho+\frac{x^{2}}{2}+1.

(S5)

The additive constant is irrelevant because only
\nabla\mu_{0}
enters
the flow; we write
\mu_{0}=\ln\rho+x^{2}/2
.

The regularized Fokker–Planck equation is therefore

\partial_{t}\rho=\partial_{x}\!\left[\rho\,\partial_{x}\!\left(\ln\rho+\frac{x^{2}}{2}-\varepsilon\,\frac{\partial_{xx}\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)\right].

(S6)

B.; Gaussian evaluation

Restrict to the Gaussian family

\rho_{\sigma}(x)=(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-1/2}\exp(-x^{2}/2\sigma^{2})
.
Then

\partial_{xx}\sqrt{\rho_{\sigma}}=\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}+\frac{x^{2}}{4\sigma^{4}}\right)\sqrt{\rho_{\sigma}},

(S7)

so

\boxed{\mu_{F}(x)=\frac{2\sigma^{2}-x^{2}}{4\sigma^{4}}.}

(S8)

The spatial gradients needed below are

\partial_{x}\mu_{0}=x\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right),\qquad\partial_{x}\mu_{F}=-\frac{x}{2\sigma^{4}}.

(S9)

C.; Exact variance equation on the Gaussian manifold

Let
u(t)=\int x^{2}\rho(x,t)\,dx=\sigma^{2}(t)
. Then

\dot{u}=-2\int x\,\rho\,\partial_{x}\mu\,dx=-2\int x\rho\,\partial_{x}\mu_{0}\,dx-2\varepsilon\!\int x\rho\,\partial_{x}\mu_{F}\,dx.

(S10)

OU contribution.

Using Eq. (
S9
) and
\int x^{2}\rho_{\sigma}\,dx=\sigma^{2}
,

-2\int x\rho\,\partial_{x}\mu_{0}\,dx=-2\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\sigma^{2}=2(1-\sigma^{2}).

(S11)

Fisher contribution.

-2\varepsilon\int x\rho\,\partial_{x}\mu_{F}\,dx=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma^{4}}\int x^{2}\rho_{\sigma}\,dx=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma^{2}}.

(S12)

Combining, with
u=\sigma^{2}
,

\boxed{\dot{u}=2(1-u)+\frac{\varepsilon}{u},\qquad\dot{\sigma}=\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{\sigma}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\sigma^{3}}.}

(S13)

These are Eqs. (4)–(5) of the main text.

D.; Effective potential and equilibrium

Equation (
S13
) is a gradient flow:

\dot{u}=-V^{\prime}(u),\qquad V(u)=u^{2}-2u-\varepsilon\ln u.

(S14)

The equilibrium
\dot{u}=0
satisfies
2u^{2}-2u-\varepsilon=0
, giving

u_{*}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+2\varepsilon}}{2},\qquad\sigma_{*}=\sqrt{u_{*}}=1+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

(S15)

E.; Dissipation identity and exact cross-term

The baseline free-energy time derivative is

\displaystyle\frac{dF_{0}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\int\mu_{0}\,\partial_{t}\rho\,dx=-\int\rho\,|\partial_{x}\mu_{0}|^{2}\,dx-\varepsilon\!\int\!\rho\,\partial_{x}\mu_{0}\cdot\partial_{x}\mu_{F}\,dx.

(S16)

Define the Gaussian cross-term

C(\sigma)=\int\rho_{\sigma}\,\partial_{x}\mu_{0}\cdot\partial_{x}\mu_{F}\,dx.

(S17)

Substituting Eq. (
S9
),

\displaystyle C(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\int\rho_{\sigma}\cdot x\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\cdot\left(-\frac{x}{2\sigma^{4}}\right)\!dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{4}}\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\sigma^{2}=-\frac{\sigma^{2}-1}{2\sigma^{4}}.

(S18)

Therefore

\boxed{C(\sigma)=\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{2\sigma^{4}},}

(S19)

and the full dissipation identity is

\frac{dF_{0}}{dt}=-\frac{(\sigma^{2}-1)^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}-\varepsilon\,\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{2\sigma^{4}}.

(S20)

In particular
C(\sigma)>0
for
\sigma\in(0,1)
,
C(1)=0
, and

C(\sigma)<0
for
\sigma>1
, establishing the paradox window

0<\sigma<1
.

F.; Crossing-time representation and KL scaling

Separating variables in Eq. (
S13
),

\boxed{t_{\mathrm{cross}}=\int_{u_{0}}^{1}\frac{u\,du}{2(u-u^{2})+\varepsilon}.}

(S21)

For
\varepsilon\to 0
,

t_{\mathrm{cross}}\approx\frac{1}{2}\int_{u_{0}}^{1}\frac{du}{1-u}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}.

(S22)

The KL divergence between two zero-mean Gaussians is

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho_{\sigma_{0}}\|\rho_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\!\left(\sigma_{0}^{2}-1-\ln\sigma_{0}^{2}\right)\approx-\frac{1}{2}\ln\sigma_{0}^{2},\quad\sigma_{0}\ll 1,

(S23)

giving the asymptotic scaling

t_{\mathrm{cross}}\sim D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho_{\sigma_{0}}\|\rho_{1})

(S24)

at leading order in
\varepsilon
and
\sigma_{0}\ll 1
.

S1.; Numerical method

We solve the regularized Fokker–Planck equation

\partial_{t}\rho=\partial_{x}\!\left[\rho\,\partial_{x}\!\left(\ln\rho+\frac{x^{2}}{2}-\varepsilon\,\frac{\partial_{xx}\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)\right]

(S25)

on a uniform grid of
N=512
points on
[-8,8]
with periodic boundary
conditions and spatial resolution
\Delta x=16/512\approx 0.031

Time integration uses a semi-implicit operator-splitting scheme with
step size
\Delta t=10^{-4}
: the linear diffusion and drift terms are
handled implicitly via tridiagonal solve, while the nonlinear Fisher term
is treated explicitly. The cross-term
C(\rho(t))
and effective variance

by direct quadrature. All three initial conditions used
\varepsilon=0.1

The mean relative error is
5.21\times 10^{-4}
, and the
pointwise maximum relative error is
0.2\%

initial conditions.

S6. Asymptotic bound on non-Gaussian correction to
t_{\mathrm{cross}}

The variance moment equation for an arbitrary (non-Gaussian)
\rho
is

\dot{u}=2(1-u)+\varepsilon K(\rho),\qquad u=\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2},

(S28)

where the Fisher variance moment is

K(\rho)=-2\int x\,\rho\,\partial_{x}\mu_{F}\,dx.

(S29)

This is distinct from the free-energy cross-term

C(\rho)=\int\rho\,\partial_{x}\mu_{0}\cdot\partial_{x}\mu_{F}\,dx
appearing in
the dissipation identity.
For a Gaussian state
K_{G}(\sigma)=1/\sigma^{2}=1/u
, recovering Eq. (5) of
the main text.

Write
K(\rho)=K_{G}(u)+\delta K(\rho)
, where
\delta K
captures
shape-dependent deviations.
Expanding
t_{\mathrm{cross}}
in
\varepsilon
,
the non-Gaussian correction satisfies

|\Delta t_{\mathrm{cross}}|\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{cross}}}\frac{|\delta K(\rho(t))|}{(\dot{u})^{2}}\,|\dot{u}|\,dt.

(S30)

For the leading-order estimate,
|\delta K_{0}/K_{G}|\sim\kappa_{4}/\sigma^{4}

where
\kappa_{4}
is the fourth cumulant of
\rho_{0}
.
For the Laplace initial condition (
b=0.354
):

\kappa_{4}=6b^{4}\approx 0.094
,
\sigma^{4}=0.0625
, giving
|\delta K_{0}/K_{G}|\approx 1.5
.

The shape-dependent correction
\delta K(\rho(t))
decays on the OU
shape-relaxation timescale.
The leading OU eigenvalue for shape relaxation is
\lambda_{2}=2
(the second
Hermite mode), giving
|\delta K(\rho(t))|\lesssim|\delta K_{0}|e^{-2t}
.
Inserting this into the bound (
S30
) and evaluating at
the initial rate
\dot{u}|_{t=0}=2(1-u_{0})+\varepsilon/u_{0}
,

\displaystyle|\Delta t_{\mathrm{cross}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon\,|\delta K_{0}|}{\dot{u}|_{t=0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2t}\,dt=\frac{\varepsilon\,|\delta K_{0}|}{2\,\dot{u}|_{t=0}}.

(S31)

For the Laplace case with
u_{0}=0.25
,
\varepsilon=0.1
:

|\Delta t_{\mathrm{cross}}|\leq\frac{0.1\times 1.5\times(1/0.25)}{2\times 1.90}=0.079,

(S32)

which is
{\approx}6\%
of
t_{\mathrm{cross}}\approx 1.32
.
This is an upper bound; near
u\to 1
where the integrand in

t_{\mathrm{cross}}=\int_{u_{0}}^{1}u\,du/[2(u-u^{2})+\varepsilon]
is
concentrated,
\dot{u}\to 0^{+}
and
\rho
has already relaxed toward
Gaussian (shape-relaxation rate 2 exceeds variance rate
\dot{u}
throughout
most of
[0,t_{\mathrm{cross}}]
), so the actual correction is smaller than
the bound.

The bound implies
|\Delta t_{\mathrm{cross}}|\lesssim 0.08
, or roughly
6\%

of
t_{\mathrm{cross}}\approx 1.32
, consistent with the numerical observations
in Table S4 (Sec. S4).
