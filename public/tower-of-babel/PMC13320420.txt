BACI-VI-Bench: A dataset of variational inequality benchmark instances for multi-agent trade-network equilibrium
Authors: Pham Thanh Hieu, Nguyen Kieu Linh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This article presents BACI-VI-Bench, a processed benchmark dataset and reproducible construction pipeline that transforms CEPII-BACI product-level international trade records into finite-dimensional variational inequality instances for multi-commodity trade-network equilibrium. The source data are CEPII-BACI HS17 bilateral trade files, version V202601, covering annual exporter-importer-product flows from 2017 to 2024. The pipeline filters positive bilateral trade flows, maps six-digit Harmonized System products to commodity sectors, selects leading exporting and importing economies, aggregates values and quantities into structured flow tensors, normalizes observed trade volumes, and calibrates a Nagurney-style benchmark cost-and-price operator. The dataset provides year-level instances of dimension 500 (five commodity sectors, ten exporters, ten importers, one route) and sector-level instances of dimension 100 for five HS commodity groups: Machinery, Minerals, Chemicals, Transport Equipment, and Metals. Each variational inequality instance contains a feasible set, a calibrated operator, observed and normalized flow tensors, exporter and importer identifiers, sector labels, operator parameters, and projection residual diagnostics. The repository includes Python construction and validation scripts, benchmark characterization data for extragradient and self-adaptive inertial solvers, figures, and metadata files enabling reuse with projection, extragradient, self-adaptive variational inequality algorithms, and multi-agent reinforcement learning frameworks. The benchmark dataset was originally developed in the context of a broader study on trade-network equilibrium modeling. Nevertheless, the present data article provides a complete and self-contained description of the dataset, benchmark construction pipeline, instance format, metadata, validation procedures, and reproducibility resources, enabling independent reuse of the benchmark.

Background

Variational inequalities provide a unifying mathematical framework or equilibrium problems in optimization, network economics, and multi-agent systems [3,4]. International trade networks can be represented as commodity flows between supply and demand nodes subject to feasibility constraints and equilibrium conditions, making them natural instances for variational inequality formulations [5]. A companion research article [2] introduced a self-adaptive inertial extragradient method for trade-network equilibrium computation and conducted experiments on BACI-derived network instances, motivating the need for a standalone, reusable benchmark dataset.

The CEPII-BACI database provides harmonized bilateral trade flows at the HS 6-digit product level for a comprehensive set of countries and years [6]. BACI-VI-Bench transforms product-level BACI records into finite-dimensional variational inequality instances with explicit feasible sets, calibrated operators, residual diagnostics, and solver-ready files. The benchmark construction is independent of any specific algorithm: the dataset is designed for reuse across projection, extragradient, inertial, self-adaptive, and multi-agent methods. The MARL-VIP connection interprets equilibrium-seeking agents as solvers, with trade-flow adjustment policies corresponding to VI projection steps, providing a structured empirical environment for reinforcement learning research in network economics.

Benchmark characterization data

The four CSV files in benchmark_outputs/ contain diagnostic data characterizing each instance’s computational difficulty. These data are generated by running solvers on each instance and recording convergence behavior. The all-years CSV (benchmark_BACI_HS17_all_years.csv) records for each solver–instance combination: year, method label, last iteration, iteration counts to convergence at thresholds 10⁻⁶ and 10⁻³ (DNF if not reached within 3000 iterations), final projection residual G(xk), a secondary distance diagnostic (FinalDist), CPU time in seconds, backtrack counts, function evaluation counts, step-size λ, and hyperparameter values (θ,σ,μ). The sector CSV records analogous columns for two solvers on sector-level instances. The tuning CSV records hyperparameter combinations explored during a grid search on the year 2022 instance together with a summary convergence score.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Source data and inclusion criteria

The raw data source is the CEPII-BACI HS17 bilateral trade database, version V202601 [1,6]. BACI provides annual bilateral trade flows at the HS 6-digit product level. The columns used are: t (year), i (exporter country code), j (importer country code), k (HS6 product code), v(trade value in thousand USD), and q (trade quantity in tonnes, where available). Only records with strictly positive trade value (v>0) and valid HS6 codes are retained. BACI raw files are not included in the Zenodo archive; the file raw_source_manifest/how_to_obtain_baci_hs17_v202601.md provides instructions for downloading from CEPII. Eight annual datasets are used: 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, and 2024, with 2022 as the primary reference year.

Network construction: country and sector selection

For each annual dataset, the top m=10 exporting economies and top n=10 importing economies are selected by aggregate bilateral trade value. The top K=5 HS commodity sectors by total trade value are retained. The number of routes is fixed at L=1 per exporter-importer pair because BACI does not contain route-level transport data. The year-level network dimension is d=K×m×n×L=500. For sector-level instances, K=1and d=100.

The set of selected exporting and importing economies is determined independently for each year by aggregate trade value, so the composition of the ten selected countries may differ across annual instances. In contrast, the five commodity sectors are defined by fixed HS2 chapter mappings (Machinery: chapters 84–85; Minerals: chapters 25–27; Chemicals: chapters 28–38; Transport Equipment: chapters 86–89; Base Metals: chapters 72–83), which are consistent across all year-level and sector-level instances. As a result, the eight year-level instances share a common sector structure but are not constrained to include the same set of trading economies.

HS6-to-sector aggregation

HS 6-digit product codes are mapped to commodity sectors via their HS2 chapter codes. The mapping is provided in metadata/hs2_to_sector_mapping.csv. The five commodity sector groups are: Machinery and Electrical Equipment (HS2 chapters 84–85), Mineral Products (chapters 25–27), Chemical Products (chapters 28–38), Transport Equipment (chapters 86–89), and Base Metals (chapters 72–83). Within each sector, trade values and quantities are aggregated across all retained HS6 codes for each exporter-importer pair.

Flow-value processing and normalization

Trade values in thousand USD are converted to million USD. The normalization scale is the maximum retained flow value across the full tensor. The normalized observed flow tensor is:xobs[k,i,j,l]=flow_value_musd[k,i,j,l]normalization_scale_musd.

All entries of xobs lie in [0, 1]. The normalization scale is stored in each NPZ file under the key normalization_scale_musd.

Feasible set and capacity construction

The feasible set is the nonnegative exporter-wise capacity set:C={x≥0:∑k,j,ℓx[k,i,j,ℓ]≤supply_cap[i],i=1,…,m}.

The capacity vector supply_cap[i] is set to 1.5 times the total normalized outgoing flow of exporter iin the observed data, making xobsstrictly interior to C.

Variational inequality operator calibration

The benchmark operator is a Nagurney-style trade-network excess-cost-minus-incentive operator [5]. For sector k, exporter i, importerj, and route l:Fk,i,j,ℓ(x)=aik(1+b∑jx[k,i,j,ℓ])+τaik−pjk1+djk∑ix[k,i,j,ℓ]·where ∑j(...) denotes summation over importers and ∑i(...) over exporters.

Operator parameter calibration: aik is proportional to the exporter-sector normalized unit value; pjk is proportional to the importer-sector normalized unit value; djk is calibrated from importer demand totals; b=0.25 and τ=0.05 are fixed benchmark defaults recorded in metadata/benchmark_config_default.json. All calibrated parameters are stored in each NPZ file and verified in metadata/calibration_audit.csv, which records zero absolute error across all 13 instances. The exact calibration formulas implemented in code/build_baci_vi_bench.py are: a_cost[k,i] = 0.5 * clip(uv_exporter_norm[k,i], 0.2, 5.0); d_demand[k,j] = 0.4 / max(import_total[k,j], 0.01), where import_total[k,j] = sum_{i,l} x_obs[k,i,j,l]; and p_price[k,j] = max(avg_cost[k,j] * (1 + d_demand[k,j] * import_total[k,j]), 0.8 * avg_cost[k,j]), where avg_cost[k,j] is the mean production-plus-transport cost over active exporters to importer j in sector k.

Operator properties

Under the benchmark calibration, F is Lipschitz continuous on C and pseudomonotone on C in the sense of network equilibrium theory [5]. These properties guarantee existence of at least one solution by the Hartman–Stampacchia theorem [3]. Strict monotonicity need not hold in general. A high-accuracy approximate reference solution can be obtained by running the included solvers from xobs with tolerance 10−8; pre-computed reference solutions will be added in a future dataset release.

Projection onto the feasible set

Projection PC(z) onto C is performed in two steps: (a) truncate all negative entries to zero; (b) for each exporteri, if ∑k,j,ℓz[k,i,j,ℓ]>supply_cap[i], rescale outgoing flows proportionally to satisfy the capacity constraint exactly. This procedure is equivalent to the Euclidean projection when exporter constraint sets are independent. The implementation in code/trade_vi_instance.py is verified to be idempotent to machine precision.

Residual and gap diagnostics

The benchmark residual is the natural projection residual:G(x)=‖x−PC(x−F(x))‖,where ‖·‖is the Euclidean norm. G(x)=0 if and only if xsolves the variational inequality. For each instance, G(xobs) is computed and recorded as a baseline diagnostic. The convergence threshold for the characterization solvers is G(xk) < 10–6.

Limitations

BACI provides annual bilateral aggregate trade flows but does not contain route-level transport data. The benchmark therefore uses L=1 route per exporter-importer pair by default. Extending to multiple routes would require supplementary transport data not present in BACI.

The variational inequality operator is calibrated from aggregated trade values and quantities. It serves as a benchmark construction, not a structurally estimated econometric model. Researchers requiring a structurally identified equilibrium model should treat this operator as an initialization or benchmark reference.

Selection of the top ten exporters, top ten importers, and top five commodity sectors keeps the benchmark computationally tractable and yields instances of controlled size. However, the resulting benchmark should not be interpreted as a complete representation of the global trade system. Smaller economies, less-traded sectors, and long-tail trading relationships are intentionally excluded. Therefore, conclusions drawn from these benchmark instances should be interpreted with caution when extrapolating to the broader global trade system. The year-level instances are intended primarily for comparative benchmarking across annual trade environments rather than for strict longitudinal analysis on an identical network topology.

Some BACI quantity records are missing or expressed in heterogeneous units; the benchmark prioritizes value-based flow construction. The dataset is derived from BACI HS17 V202601; later BACI releases may yield different numerical instances due to data revisions.

The current version does not include pre-computed high-accuracy reference solutions. Users requiring solution accuracy metrics can generate approximate reference solutions by running the included solvers to a tight tolerance G(x)<10−8. Pre-computed reference solutions are planned for a future dataset release.
