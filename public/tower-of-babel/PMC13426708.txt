Time to modernize the traditional professional reference in the appointment process for staff surgeons
Authors: Adarsh Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary Professional recommendations are subjective and inherently biased. When singing a candidate’s praises, references can lead to successful appointments; however, their content can also negatively influence outcomes and, in extreme cases, damage a candidate’s reputation and career. This commentary discusses the need for a more modern, standardized approach to professional references to address favouritism and discrimination in the process of appointing staff surgeons.

A professional surgical reference is a testimony to qualifying for a surgical job based on insight into a candidate’s credentials, work ethic, skills, knowledge, experience, strengths, and achievements.1 Normally, professional referees are former employers, colleagues, clients, or supervisors, and traditionally such professional surgical referees vouch for the above attributes and varied skills and work experience.1 Arguably, traditional single-source professional surgical references are no longer justified in modern clinical practice. Let me explain:

What constitutes a good professional reference?

A good reference is truthful, objective, and accurate.2 Referees should not include close friends or family members unless they are professional colleagues for a protracted period. References carry more weight if they are written by individuals with appropriate credentials; for example, academic references are valid if written by instructors or vocational counsellors, whereas professional references should come from individuals who know the candidate in a professional capacity.2 Testimony of employment should include a coworker or past employer, who can attest to specific qualifications, credentials, and employment experience.

Why is a change needed?

There are many problems with the traditional professional reference, which comes from a single source and often lacks objectivity, transparency, and details on a candidate’s credentials, achievements, and clinical performance. Such testimony may not provide first-hand information on interactions with clinical staff from different areas in an organization and may lack an important objective component: patient perspective. A traditional reference also carries a risk of discrimination or bias (e.g., “code words” in the content), especially if the same person is writing references for more than 1 candidate competing for the same job.3 Professional conflicts and differences in clinical opinions can lead to bias when vouching for a candidate.

A traditional professional referee may be influenced by other coworkers and colleagues, which raises questions on the legality of negative content in references and, more importantly, the consequences and long-term potentially damaging impact of a bad, unrequested reference.4 Misleading unrequested references can be challenged in the court of law.4 Unfortunately, screening potential negative references may be inadequate to protect a candidate’s reputation with the hiring team, because many human resource departments will ask to speak to former employers, regardless of whether they are formally listed as referees on a candidate’s job application.3 Although professional testimony has been considered an important aspect of the candidate selection process, input from such testimonies is difficult to quantify as a measure for evaluating candidates.5 Although inherent bias exists in most professional references because they are written by the candidate’s best advocates, authoritative recommendations from professionals with strong reputations, compared with those written by others who are less known nationally, may lead to selection bias.3

Often, references are requested at the last minute, just before or after the interview, leaving little time for the referee to respond. Furthermore, many employers may not bother to chase or even review references in cases where there is already a preferred candidate. Therefore, all hospitals, institutes, and governing bodies should be mandated to post all staff surgeon jobs, regardless of whether they are hospital, academic, or community-based posts, in a clear and transparent manner. Institutions and governing bodies should exercise due diligence when hiring staff surgeons by relying on a more modern process for obtaining professional surgical references.

What constitutes a modern professional reference?

A modern reference is an objective, transparent, and professional document reflective of the candidate. Such a document should include information on the candidate’s credentials, clinical performance indicators (e.g., operative log book and endoscopy procedure data, emergency surgical performances, specialist and complex clinic consultations, hospital stay and complication rates from the institute dashboard, records of patient complaints), feedback from colleagues in multiple clinical areas, professional and academic achievements, and patient perspectives, all in a standardized format. A standardized format allows the quality of a reference to be measured.

It should be mandatory to request and submit such a standardized reference in a timely manner, allowing it to be an important part of the discussion during interviews for a staff surgeon role. Hopefully, such a robust mechanism will provide a detailed, accurate, and fair assessment of a candidate’s credentials, work life, experience, and achievements. Moreover, such a system can eliminate favouritism or discrimination associated with professional networking and, in extreme cases, slander that damages a candidate’s professional reputation and career, which could be subjected to legal proceedings.4 Essentially, this more modern reference process would allow a competing candidate to generate their own professional testimony to support their candidacy, reducing bias with feedback from multiple clinical areas (360° evaluation) as well as patient perspectives, but the crucial aspect remains the candidate’s credentials and evidence of performance.

Conclusion

While it is difficult to imagine the process of selection without a recommendation in a traditional job market, there is a great need to modernize the reference process in surgical practice and beyond. However, more work may be needed to measure the economic impact and other benefits of a new professional reference system in the recruitment process. In future, traditional subjective praise or discriminatory notes or phone calls (i.e., word of mouth) could be replaced by modern, objectively structured, and transparent testimony hinged on personal credentials and evidence of work performance.
