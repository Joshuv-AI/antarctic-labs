Relations between different definitions of the quantum Wasserstein distance for qubits
Géza Tóth, József Pitrik

Abstract
The quantum Wasserstein distances defined by Golse, Mouhot, Paul, and Caglioti and by De Palma and Trevisan coincide for qubits when a single operator appears in the cost function. As a consequence, the self-distance equals the Wigner–Yanase skew information in this case.

IV
Consequences for spin chain calculations

Let us point out one of the consequences of our main result.

Based on Definition 1 and Eq. (
4
), we can relate the ground state energy of spin chains to the Wigner-Yanase skew information as

\displaystyle\min_{\varrho_{12}}\;

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[-\sum_{n=1}^{N}(H_{n}^{T}\otimes H_{n})\varrho_{12}\right]=\sum_{n}I_{\varrho}(H_{n})-\langle{H_{n}^{2}}\rangle_{\varrho},

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{D},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho^{T},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{1}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho.

(29)

Here, on the left-hand side there is the ground state energy for a quantum state with given marginals.

Due to the results of our paper, we can also obtain for qubits that

\displaystyle\min_{\varrho_{12}}\;

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[-(H_{1}\otimes H_{1})\varrho_{12}\right]=I_{\varrho}(H_{1})-\langle{H_{1}^{2}}\rangle_{\varrho},

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{D},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{1}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho.

(30)

Thus, we can relate energy minimum of the ferromagnetic model for an
H_{1}
with given marginals to the Wigner-Yanase skew information.
Note that transposes do not appear in the formula in Eq. (
30

V
Conclusions

We showed that the quantum Wasserstein distances defined by Golse, Mouhot, Paul, and Caglioti, and by De Palma and Trevisan are equal for qubits when a single operator appears in the cost function.
