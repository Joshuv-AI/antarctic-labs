Introduction
This final report describes the research work pursued under the Iowa State University (ISU) Medium
Energy Nuclear Physics grant for the period from September 2010 – September 2025.

One of the primary goals of the RHIC spin program is to understand the quark-gluon structure of the
nucleon, the fundamental bound state of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). The experimental group at
ISU is involved in collider-based probes of nuclear matter, notably through major collaborations like
PHENIX, STAR and sPHENIX experiments at Brookhaven’s RHIC and preparatory work for the ePIC
detector at the Electron-Ion Collider. Experimental efforts include spin physics measurements with
polarized proton collisions and detector development — methods that help reveal how quark
and gluon dynamics contribute to nucleon structure at intermediate momentum transfers.

Studies of transverse single spin asymmetries (SSA’s) in polarized proton-proton collisions at RHIC offer a
potential window into the parton dynamics inside the proton. The work included a measurement of the
transverse SSA AN for Drell-Yan (DY) using the STAR Forward Meson Spectrometer in Run2017 data taken
at a center of mass energy of 510 GeV. This measurement is key to establishing the non-universality of
the Sivers function through the relative sign difference of the asymmetry between Drell-Yan and Semi
Inclusive Deep Inelastic Scattering (SIDIS) measured at CERN.

In addition we studied modifications to the fragmentation of partons in the presence of a nuclear
medium. That fragmentation is modified in the presence of a nuclear medium has been observed for
quite some time, but is a poorly understood phenomenon. Measurements of jet yields alone depend on
both nuclear modifications to the underlying parton distribution function (nPDFs) as well as interactions
of the scattered parton with cold nuclear matter, and cannot properly distinguish between these two
effects. Recent advances in jet substructure measurements, as well as new data to be taken at RHIC with
sPHENIX, a purpose-built jet detector, offer new opportunities to study modifications to vacuum
fragmentation even if they are relatively subtle at moderate momentum transfer Q2. While the EIC will

 2

probe hadronization in the nuclear medium with unprecedented reach and accuracy in e+A collisions,
interpretation of these results in a universal framework will require complementary measurements in
hadron-nucleus collisions as well. The data taken in 200 GeV p+p/p+Au collisions at RHIC will provide a
comparison sample at center-of-mass energies comparable to those at the EIC, enabling important
comparisons to elucidate factorization-breaking effects that may be present in the p+Au data, for
example.

The original goal of this work was to study modifications to the fragmentation of partons in the presence
of a nuclear medium using sPHENIX p+Au data that was originally anticipated to be taken in 2024.
However, modifications to the sPHENIX run plan necessitated by sPHENIX commissioning and the
cryogenic failure at RHIC in 2023 have eliminated p+Au running from the sPHENIX run plan.
Nevertheless, recent advances in jet substructure measurements, as well as data to be taken at with
sPHENIX, a purpose-built jet detector, offer new opportunities to study the fragmentation process in
p+p collisions in unprecedented detail. These measurements will be essential to provide a comparison
with e+p collisions at the EIC. During the remaining period covered by this grant we propose to complete
an analysis of jet substructure in spin-polarized p+p collisions using sPHENIX data in lieu of the originally
planned study of fragmentation in p+Au collisions.

While the EIC will probe hadronization in the nuclear medium with unprecedented reach and accuracy
in e+p/e+A collisions, interpretation of these results in a universal framework will require
complementary measurements in hadron-nucleus collisions as well. The data taken in 200 GeV p+p
collisions at RHIC will provide a comparison sample at center-of-mass energies comparable to those at
the EIC, enabling important comparisons to elucidate factorization-breaking effects that may be present
in the p+Au data, for example. We are supporting the development of the reconstruction software and
simulations for the ePIC detector at the EIC with this long-term research objective in mind.

Analysis of π0 and η meson in d+Au Collisions with the PHENIX MPC-EX
Forward instrumentation in the PHENIX detector consists of the Muon Piston Calorimeter (MPC), which
covers a pseudorapidity range 3.1<η<3.9 with an array of PbW04 crystals as an electromagnetic
calorimeter. The MPC-EX preshower detector [1] was added to the MPC to enable π0/photon separation.
The MPC-EX is a Si+W preshower detector, which uses eight layers of 2 mm tungsten absorber and Si
minipad (1.8mm x 15mm) detectors to identify the two separate centers of gravity of electromagnetic
showers originating from π0 decay. The primary scientific goal of the MPC-EX is the study of parton
distributions in nuclei at low-x through prompt photon production. Previous studies using pion
production at forward rapidities in d+Au collisions average over a broad range in parton momentum
fraction x, and theoretical fits using this data are limited by averaging over multiple production
processes. Neutral pions in the pseudorapidity range 3.1<η<3.9 begin to merge in the PHENIX MPC for
E>20 GeV, or pT~1 GeV (the MPC has a 2.2cm tower size). The fine resolution of the MPC-EX minipads is
used to identify the merged entry photons to measure the opening angle; the combined energy of the
MPC-EX and MPC is split evenly between the two photons and combined with the opening angle to
calculate an invariant mass. In this way the MPC-EX enables the reconstruction of π0 mesons up to very
high energies (E>65 GeV) in the forward direction in PHENIX. After extracting the raw π0 counts and be
correcting for the reconstruction efficiency, as well as the efficiency of cuts used to reject backgrounds,

the invariant multiplicities and RCP can be calculated. The RCP is the ratio of central to most peripheral
(60-88) centrality, and was calculated as:.

Development of an Energy-Energy Correlations Framework for sPHENIX
In addition to the approaches to jet substructure currently popular at the LHC, which are mainly
focused on shape variables, we have also been exploring a new approach, the n-point energy
correlators (ENC), which are designed to make optimal use of experimental data in a framework
that allows detailed theoretical comparisons to QCD calculations [4]. The use of correlation
functions to characterize jets has been proposed as a reframing of jet substructure in terms of a
universal scaling behavior that can be directly calculated in perturbative QCD. In [4], the authors
use CMS open data to demonstrate their approach, as outlined in Figure 2.

 5

 Figure 2: This is Figure 1 from [4], showing the 2-point energy correlator between hadrons for high pT jets in CMS open data
 from the LHC. The correlations show three distinct regions as a function of the angle between hadrons. Just below the jet anti-
 kT radius the correlations are driven by the evolution of the parton shower leading to an approximately flat scaling region,
 followed by a change of slope in a transition region, and finally a universal slope corresponding to free hadrons.

The authors in [4] note that this approach is enabled both by the fine angular resolution of modern
particle physics detectors as well as the high constituent multiplicity in LHC jets. The sPHENIX
detector, with its fine angular resolution of modern and high data-taking rate, will be an ideal
experiment to measure these ENC.

Ahead of Run 24, significant progress was made towards implementing a framework to measure
ENC in the sPHENIX software stack. This will be crucial for analyzing the Run 24 p+p data as soon
as calibrated data become available. The basic components of the framework necessary for
measuring the raw, uncorrected two-point energy-energy correlators (EEC) and the projected
three-point energy-energy correlators (E3C) have been successfully implemented.

 6

This framework was exercised on both p+p and p+Au sPHENIX simulations. Figure 3 shows the EEC
spectra for R = 0.4 anti-kT charged jets from sPHENIX p+p simulations at both the truth and
reconstructed level. Of particular note is how small the relative differences are between the truth and
reconstructed ENC spectra. While the detector effects present in these simulations are only estimates of
what they will be in actual running conditions, this study suggests that these observables are fairly
resilient to the anticipated detector effects, and so we expect that corrections to the measured data will
be comparatively small.

sPHENIX, Derek’s focused on the development of sPHENIX jet reconstruction software and supporting

For example, critical to all future jet analyses at sPHENIX will be the ability to mitigate beam-induced
backgrounds. These backgrounds can significantly dilute and distort measured jet spectra on the level of
event-averaged ensembles. Towards this, Derek worked with students from Columbia University and the
University of Colorado to develop a component of the sPHENIX software framework Fun4All (F4A) to
filter such beam background events and analyze their impact both during data taking and during offline
analysis. This component is modular, extensible, and will be able to evolve with sPHENIX analyses.

reconstruction for (1) p+p analyses, and for (2) data taking operations during Run 3. Contributions
towards the former focused on ensuring that there were p+p equivalents to the existing jet
reconstruction software components which were developed for Au+Au analyses.

PHENIX Spin-Dependent EEC Analysis

Initial feasibility studies were carried out on Run 15 p+p and p+Au PHENIX data, building on the recent
Run 15 PHENIX jet analyses led by ISU. Derek also led the development of an analysis framework to carry
out a novel spin-dependent Energy-Energy Correlator (EEC) measurement on Run 15 PHENIX p+p and

analyze (1) Dihadron Interference Fragmentation Functions, and (2) the modification of fragmentation in

Reconstruction Development for the ePIC Detector at the EIC

As we have already emphasized, measurements of jet substructure in hadronic collisions will be
essential complements to corresponding measurements in leptonic collisions. Both are necessary to
understand fragmentation in a truly universal framework. Thus, to prepare for jet substructure
measurements at the Electron-Ion Collider, Dr. Anderson has continued to contribute to the
development of the ePIC software stack.

These contributions include improvements to the jet reconstruction software he helped implement last
year, as well as taking on the role of co-convener of the Reconstruction Working Group. This group is
charged with coordinating development efforts on the reconstruction component of the software stack,
and promoting efforts to develop holistic reconstruction techniques, i.e. techniques which combine
information across different subsystems. As part of this role, Dr. Anderson has served as the coordinator
of the effort to implement a baseline Particle Flow algorithm in the reconstruction software. Such an
algorithm will be critical for future measurements of jet substructure in Deep-Inelastic Scattering and for
fully exploiting the potential of the ePIC detector.

The sPHENIX Outer Hadronic Calorimeter (OHCal) will be reused in ePIC as the Barrel Hadronic
Calorimeter (BHCal). This system will be crucial to measuring the neutral component of jets in DIS events
and the identification of muons. Derek built on ISU’s experience in designing, constructing, and
operating the sPHENIX OHCal to contribute to assessing the performance of the ePIC BHCal design.
Derek led the development of a suite of analysis tools for carrying out BHCal performance studies in
single particle events and made contributions to the development of the EIC reconstruction framework,
EICrecon, that enable such BHCal performance studies. One example of a contribution is the upgrade of
an algorithm to allow users to easily vary the segmentation of the BHCal. He then worked with students
from New Hampshire University and Georgia State University to carry out and analyze these
performance studies, the results of which formed the basis for the initial drafts of the BHCal chapter of
the ePIC pre-Technical Design Report (pTDR).

Furthermore, state-of-the-art reconstruction is necessary to fully exploit the scientific potential of the
ePIC detector and to realize ambitious medium energy jet substructure analyses. Therefore, during this
time Derek continued his role as a co-convenor the ePIC Reconstruction Working Group.

This group coordinates the development of EICrecon to ensure that it is modular and holistic, meaning
that all information from the detector is used to maximize the breadth and accuracy of reconstruction.

For example, Derek led the planning and implementation of a development program to implement a
baseline Particle Flow Algorithm (PFA) in EICrecon. A PFA uses information from tracking, particle
identification (PID), and calorimeter systems for precision reconstruction of individual particles. Such
algorithms will be critical to future jet and jet substructure analyses. An example is shown in Figure 6
where the energy of single neutrons reconstructed in the ePIC BHCal corrected to the hadronic scale
using a Linear Discriminant machine learning model trained on the energy of clusters in the BHCal and
Barrel Imaging Calorimeter (BIC).

medium energy prospects with RHIC, and preparations for and commissioning of the EIC.
