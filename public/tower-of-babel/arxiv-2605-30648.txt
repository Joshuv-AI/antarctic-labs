Convergence of Steepest Descent and Adam under Non-Uniform Smoothness
Sharan Vaswani, Yifan Sun, Reza Babanezhad

Abstract
Recent work has analyzed the convergence of first-order methods under non-uniform smoothness assumptions that better model the loss landscape in machine learning tasks. We generalize this assumption to objectives whose curvature is an affine function of the objective value. This property is satisfied by a broad class of problems, including logistic regression, generalized linear models with a logistic link function, softmax policy gradient in reinforcement learning, and a class of neural networks. Under this assumption and gradient domination conditions, we establish a general convergence rate for the steepest descent method, and deterministic, diagonal variants of RMSProp and Adam. Our results imply that for logistic regression on separable data and the softmax policy gradient objective, sign GD converges linearly and is provably faster than GD. Furthermore, we show that for a class of two-layer neural networks on separable data, RMSProp and Adam can converge at a linear rate with a constant step-size and momentum parameter. Finally, we present a lower bound demonstrating that, under our assumption, RMSProp and Adam are provably faster than AdaGrad, AMSGrad, gradient descent, and heavy-ball momentum.

We analyzed the convergence of steepest descent, and deterministic

Clarke subdifferential
(
8
)
.

For a nonconvex locally Lipschitz function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the Clarke subdifferential is defined as

\partial f(x):=\mathrm{cl}\,\mathrm{co}\left\{\lim_{x_{k}\to x}\nabla f(x_{k})\right\}

where
\mathrm{cl}\,\mathrm{co}
is the closed convex hull of the set, and
x_{k}
forms a trajectory of points for which
\nabla f(x_{k})
exists. If additionally
f
is convex, then
\partial f(x)
is the standard convex subdifferential.

Lemma 12

(Chain rule for Clarke subdifferentials)
.

Suppose
h(x)=f(g(x))
, and both
f
and
g
are locally Lipschitz. Then,

1.

if
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
smooth and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
convex but not smooth, then

\partial h(x)=f^{\prime}(g(x))\cdot\partial g(x)

2.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
nonsmooth but continuous, and
g:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
smooth
then

\partial h(x)=J(x)^{T}y,\qquad u\in\partial f(u),\;u=g(x)

where
J(x)=[\nabla g_{1}(x),...,\nabla g_{n}(x)]^{T}
the Jacobian of the mapping
g
.

Proof.

The first statement is given exactly in
(
8
)
. Both are the result of the following statement, which is follows from the standard definition of lim sup:

Consider
A(t)\to a
and
\limsup_{t\to 0}B(t)=\mathcal{B}
where
a
is a point and
\mathcal{B}
is a closed set.
Then if
a\in\mathbb{R}
or
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}
, then

\limsup_{t\to 0}A(t)\cdot B(t)\subset a\cdot\mathcal{B}=\{ab:b\in\mathcal{B}\}.

If
h(x)
is smooth at
x
, then

\partial h(x)=\{\nabla h(x)\}=\begin{cases}\{f^{\prime}(g(x))\cdot\nabla g(x)\},&g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\\
\{J(x)^{T}\nabla f(g(x))\},&g:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{cases}

If
h(x)
is not smooth at
x
, then in the first case,

\limsup_{z\to x}\nabla h(z)=\limsup_{z\to x}f^{\prime}(g(z))\cdot\nabla g(z)\subseteq f^{\prime}(g(x))\cdot\partial g(x).

Since subdifferentials are closed and convex, then

\partial h(x)=\mathrm{co\,cl}\limsup_{z\to x}\nabla h(z)=f^{\prime}(g(x))\cdot\partial g(x).

By similar logic, in the second case, for
w=g(z)
,
u=g(x)

\limsup_{z\to x}\nabla h(z)=\limsup_{z\to x}J(z)^{T}\nabla f(w)\subset J(x)^{T}\limsup_{w\to u}\nabla f(w)=J(x)^{T}\partial f(u)

which are also closed and convex sets. So,

\partial h(x)=\{J(x)^{T}y:y\in\partial f(u),\,u=g(x)\}.

\left\|\theta_{t}-\theta_{t+1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{L_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{1}\,\left\|m_{t-1}-\nabla_{t-1}\right\|_{1}+\beta_{1}\,(L_{0}+L_{1}\,\left\|\nabla f(\theta_{t})\right\|_{1})\,e\,\left\|\theta_{t}-\theta_{t-1}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{1}\,\left\|m_{t-1}-\nabla_{t-1}\right\|_{1}+\beta_{1}\,\underbrace{(L_{0}+L_{1}\,\left\|\nabla f(\theta_{t})\right\|_{1})}_{:=\bar{L}_{t}}\,e\,\eta_{t-1}\,B

\displaystyle\implies\frac{\left\|m_{t}-g_{t}\right\|_{1}}{\bar{L_{t}}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{1}\,\frac{\left\|m_{t-1}-\nabla_{t-1}\right\|_{1}}{\bar{L}_{t}}+\beta_{1}\,e\,\eta_{t-1}\,B

(90)

We now write
\bar{L}_{t}
in terms of
\bar{L}_{t-1}
. In particular, using
Lemma

21
with
p=\infty
,
q=1
and
y=\theta_{t-1}
,
x=\theta_{t}
,

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{t}=L_{0}+L_{1}\,\left\|\nabla f(\theta_{t})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\left(L_{0}+L_{1}\,\left\|\nabla f(\theta_{t-1})\right\|_{1}\right)\,\exp\left(-L_{1}\,\left\|\theta_{t}-\theta_{t-1}\right\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{L}_{t-1}\,\exp\left(-L_{1}\,\left\|\theta_{t}-\theta_{t-1}\right\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{L}_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\bar{L}_{t-1}}\,\exp\left(L_{1}\,\left\|\theta_{t}-\theta_{t-1}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\bar{L}_{t-1}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta_{1}}}

(Using

86
and ensuring that
\eta_{t-1}\leq\frac{1}{B\,L_{1}}\,\ln(1/\sqrt{\beta_{1}}
)

Combining the above inequality with
Equation

90
,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|m_{t}-g_{t}\right\|_{1}}{\bar{L_{t}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\beta_{1}}\,\frac{\left\|m_{t-1}-\nabla_{t-1}\right\|_{1}}{\bar{L}_{t-1}}+\beta_{1}\,e\,\eta_{t-1}\,B

(91)

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{1}\,e\,B\,\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}(\sqrt{\beta_{1}})^{t-1-s}\,\eta_{s}

(Recursing and using that
m_{0,i}=g_{1,i}
)

\displaystyle\implies\left\|m_{t}-g_{t}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq(L_{0}+L_{1}\,\left\|f(\theta_{t})\right\|_{1})\,\beta_{1}\,e\,B\,\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}(\sqrt{\beta_{1}})^{t-1-s}\,\eta_{s}

∎

Appendix I
Simplifying and Generalizing the result in
32

The update for steepest descent with Armijo line-search can be written as:

\displaystyle\theta_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\theta_{t}-{\eta_{t}}\,\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|_{q}\,d_{t}\quad\text{where,}\quad d_{t}:=\argmax_{\left\|d\right\|_{p}\leq 1}\langle d,\nabla_{t}\rangle\,.

(93)

Given
c\in(0,1)
and
\eta_{\max}>0
,
{\eta_{t}}
is the largest step-size that satisfies the Armijo condition at iteration
t
, i.e.,

\displaystyle f(\theta_{t}-\eta\,d_{t})

\displaystyle\leq f(\theta_{t})-c\,\eta\,\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|^{2}_{q}\quad\text{;}\quad\eta\leq\eta_{\max}

(94)

We will now prove the following lemma that lower-bounds the step-size returned by the Armijo line-search.

Lemma 27
.

If
f
satisfies
1
and
2
, at iteration
t
, the update in
Equations

93
and
94
returns a step-size
{\eta_{t}}\geq\min\left\{\eta_{\max},\frac{1-c}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}\right\}
.

Proof.

We will show that any
\eta\leq\frac{1-c}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}
will satisfy the Armijo condition, and hence the back-tracking Armijo line-search will return a step-size larger than
\min\left\{\eta_{\max},\frac{1-c}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}\right\}
.

First, note that for
\eta\leq\frac{1}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}
,

\displaystyle\eta\,\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|_{q}}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2H_{0}\,f(\theta_{t})+H_{1}\,[f(\theta_{t})]^{2}}}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}

(Using
Lemma

3
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{H_{1}}}\,\frac{\sqrt{2H_{0}\,H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})+H_{1}^{2}\,[f(\theta_{t})]^{2}+H_{0}^{2}}}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}

(Since
H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})>0
)

\displaystyle\implies\eta\,\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{H_{1}}}

Hence, for any
\eta\leq\frac{1-c}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}<\frac{1}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}
, the condition required for
Lemma

7
is satisfied for
y=\theta_{t+1}
,
x=\theta_{t}
. Using
Lemma

7
,

\displaystyle f(\theta_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(\theta_{t})-\eta\,\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|_{q}\,\langle\nabla_{t},d_{t}\rangle+\eta^{2}\,(H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t}))\,\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|^{2}_{q}

(Since
\left\|d_{t}\right\|_{p}=1
)

\displaystyle=f(\theta_{t})-\eta\,\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|^{2}_{q}+\eta^{2}\,(H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t}))\,\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|^{2}_{q}

(By definition of the dual norm)

\displaystyle\leq f(\theta_{t})-c\,\eta\,\left\|\nabla_{t}\right\|^{2}_{q}

(By choice of
\eta
)

Hence, the Armijo condition is satisfied for any
\eta\leq\frac{1-c}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}
. Since the back-tracking line-search returns the largest step-size (smaller than
\eta_{\max}
) that satisfies the Armijo condition, the returned step-size
{\eta_{t}}\geq\max\left\{\eta_{\max},\frac{1-c}{H_{0}+H_{1}\,f(\theta_{t})}\right\}
.
∎

The above lower-bound on
{\eta_{t}}
holds for steepest descent and is tighter than the one derived in
32
. Given this lower-bound, the subsequent results in
32
can be derived analogously.
