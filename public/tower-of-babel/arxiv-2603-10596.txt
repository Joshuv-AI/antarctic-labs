M-Polynomial of Product Graphs
El-Mehdi Mehiri, Sandi Klavžar

Abstract
The M-polynomial provides a unifying framework for a wide class of degree-based topological indices. Despite its structural importance, general methods for computing the M-polynomial under graph constructions remain limited. In this paper, explicit formulas, and compact ones whenever possible, for the M-polynomial under different graph products whose vertex sets are the Cartesian product of the factors are developed. The products studied are the direct, the Cartesian, the strong, the lexicographic, the symmetric-difference, the disjunction, and the Sierpiński product. The obtained formulas yield a unified structural description of how vertex-degree interactions propagate under graph constructions and extend existing results for degree-based indices at the polynomial level.

Introduction

Topological indices are numerical graph invariants designed to encode structural
information in a compact algebraic form.
Originally motivated by chemical graph theory, where molecular structures are
modeled by graphs whose vertices represent atoms and edges represent bonds,
these indices have found applications in quantitative structure–property and
structure–activity relationships (QSPR/QSAR); see

[
14
,
32
]
.
Among the numerous classes of such invariants,
degree-based
topological indices occupy
a central position due to their simplicity, interpretability, and strong empirical
correlations with a wide range of physicochemical properties
[
10
]
.

The last decades have witnessed a growing emphasis on unifying frameworks that
encode entire families of topological indices within a single polynomial.
In the distance-based setting, this role is played by the Wiener polynomial
introduced by Sagan, Yeh and Zhang
[
29
]
,
whose derivative recovers the classical Wiener index.
The closely related Hosoya polynomial, which generalizes the Wiener number,
has also proven to be a powerful tool in studying composite graphs

[
30
]
.
Similarly, extensions such as the hyper-Wiener index

[
21
]
and the
y
-Wiener index

[
16
]
have led to systematic investigations of
graph operations under distance-based invariants.
Parallel developments exist for degree-based indices,
including the Zagreb indices under graph operations

[
22
]
.

In the degree-based setting, Deutsch and Klavžar introduced in 2015 the

M-polynomial

[
12
]
.
This polynomial serves as a unifying framework for expressing many
degree-based topological indices in closed form, much like the Hosoya
polynomial does for distance-based indices
[
13
]
.
Once the bivariate M-polynomial
M(G;x,y)
is known, a broad spectrum of classical indices—including
the Zagreb, Randić, harmonic, symmetric division, forgotten, and inverse sum
indegree indices—can be obtained by applying differential and integral
operators to
M(G;x,y)

[
12
]
. The recent papers
[
8
,
24
]
shows how this approach can also be applied to a large variety of Sombor-type indices, while in
[
26
]
the M-polynomial has been used to derive machine learning models for predicting physicochemical properties of antibiotics. Thus, the computation of the M-polynomial for structured graph families becomes a central task.

Despite its importance, only a limited number of general methods for
computing the M-polynomial are currently available.
Early systematic approaches were developed for restricted graph classes

[
13
,
11
]
,
while numerous recent papers compute explicit expressions for specific
families of graphs, for a selection see
[
2
,
1
,
19
,
7
,
25
,
28
]
.
However, comparatively less attention has been given to a structural
analysis of how the M-polynomial behaves under general graph
constructions.

In this paper, we consider graph products in which the vertex set is the Cartesian product of the vertex sets of the factors. The products in question are the four standard graph products (the Cartesian, the direct, the strong, and the lexicographic), the symmetric-difference product, the disjunction product, and the Sierpiński product. Classical works on Boolean operations

[
17
]

and systematic treatments of composite graphs

[
33
,
30
]

demonstrate the fundamental role of such constructions in graph theory.
More recently, generalized product-like operations such as the
Sierpiński product
[
23
]

have further expanded this structural landscape. For a systematic treatment of graph products, including Boolean product graphs, we refer the reader to the monograph
[
15
]
, while for the Sierpiński product, in addition to the seminal paper
[
23
]
, see also
[
18
,
31
]
.

The motivation of the present work is both structural and computational.
Our goal is to represent the M-polynomial of a product

G\ast H
in terms of the M-polynomials of the factors
G
and
H

and their degree polynomials.
This objective is natural from a computational standpoint.
If
|V(G)|=n_{G}
and
|V(H)|=n_{H}
, then the product graph
has
n_{G}n_{H}
vertices.
Even moderate factor sizes lead to a quadratic explosion in the number
of vertices, and consequently in the number of edges.
Since the M-polynomial requires enumerating edges according to
the degree types of their endpoints, a direct computation on

G\ast H
quickly becomes expensive.
In contrast, computing
M(G;x,y)
and
M(H;x,y)
separately is
considerably cheaper.
If one can derive a structural formula expressing

M(G\ast H;x,y)
in terms of invariants of the factors,
then the global computation reduces to algebraic manipulations
of smaller polynomials.

Such factorizations parallel classical formulas obtained for distance-based indices under graph operations
[
6
,
33
,
30
,
21
,
16
]
. Similar developments have been pursued for Hosoya and Wiener type polynomials under graph operations
[
27
]
, for degree polynomial and its behavior under products
[
20
]
, as well as the Zagreb indices
[
22
]
, the forgotten F-index
[
4
,
9
]
, and related degree-based descriptors under various constructions
[
12
,
5
,
3
]
. Together, these results emphasize the importance of deriving structural formulas at the level of graph operations rather than recomputing invariants directly on large composite graphs.

The main contribution of this paper is the derivation of
explicit and compact formulas for the M-polynomial for the listed seven graph products. Whenever possible, we express the resulting formulas in terms of the M-polynomials and degree polynomials of the factors,
thereby reducing global complexity to local algebra.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we first introduce the notation and definitions used throughout the work. Then the graph products considered are introduced and their degree formulas stated, which serve as the structural foundation for the subsequent derivations. The main results are stated and proved in Section
3
, each product considered being treated in a separate subsection. In Section
4
the obtained formulas are illustrated using the example of products of paths. Section
5
summarizes the obtained formulas and discusses perspectives for future research.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, all graphs are finite, simple, and undirected.
For a graph
G
, we denote by
V(G)
and
E(G)
its vertex set and edge set, respectively. We write
n_{G}=|V(G)|
for the order of
G
. The complement of a graph
G
is denoted by
\overline{G}
. For a vertex
g\in V(G)
, the (open) neighborhood of
g
is
N_{G}(g)=\{g^{\prime}\in V(G):\ gg^{\prime}\in E(G)\}
. The degree of
g
is denoted by
\deg_{G}(g)
, that is,
\deg_{G}(g)=|N_{G}(g)|
. The minimum and the maximum degrees of
G
are denoted by
\delta(G)
and
\Delta(G)
, respectively. For
i\geq 0
, let

n_{i}(G)=|\{g\in V(G):\ \deg_{G}(g)=i\}|\,,

and for
i\leq j
, let

m_{i,j}(G)=|\{\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G):\ \{\deg_{G}(g),\deg_{G}(g^{\prime})\}=\{i,j\}\}|\,.

In Section
3.5
we will also use ordered nonadjacent degree-type numbers, which are defined by

\widehat{m}_{i,j}(G)=|\{(g,g^{\prime})\in V(G)^{2}:\ gg^{\prime}\notin E(G),\;(\deg_{G}(g),\deg_{G}(g^{\prime}))=(i,j)\}|\,.

Note that in the definition of
\widehat{m}_{i,j}(G)
we allow
g=g^{\prime}
.

The
degree polynomial
and the
M-polynomial
of a graph
G
are defined respectively by

\displaystyle D_{G}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{{D}}(G)}n_{i}(G)\,t^{i}\,,

\displaystyle M(G;x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\leq j}m_{i,j}(G)x^{i}y^{j}\,.

2.1
Graph Products

We now present the definitions of the graph products considered in this work. The products of our interest are the four standard graph products (the Cartesian, the direct, the strong, and the lexicographic), the disjunction product (OR), the symmetric-difference product (XOR), and the Sierpiński product. If
G
and
H
are graphs, and
\ast
is an arbitrary of the listed products, then we have
V(G\ast H)=V(G)\times V(H)
. The edges in these products are determined in the following way.

•

In the
Cartesian product

G\cp H
, vertices
(g_{1},h_{1})
and
(g_{2},h_{2})
are adjacent if
g_{1}=g_{2}
and
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
, or
h_{1}=h_{2}
and
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
.

•

In the
direct product

G\times H
, vertices
(g_{1},h_{1})
and
(g_{2},h_{2})
are adjacent if
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
and
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
.

•

In the
strong product

G\boxtimes H
, vertices
(g_{1},h_{1})
and
(g_{2},h_{2})
are adjacent if
g_{1}=g_{2}
and
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
, or
h_{1}=h_{2}
and
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
, or
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
and
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
.

•

In the
lexicographic product

G[H]
, vertices
(g_{1},h_{1})
and
(g_{2},h_{2})
are adjacent if
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
, or
g_{1}=g_{2}
and
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
.

•

In the
symmetric difference product
(XOR)
G\oplus H
, vertices
(g_{1},h_{1})
and
(g_{2},h_{2})
are adjacent if either
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
or
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
(that is, exactly one of these).

•

In the
disjunction product
(OR)
G\vee H
, vertices
(g_{1},h_{1})
and
(g_{2},h_{2})
are adjacent if
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
or
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
.

•

In the
Sierpiński product

G\otimes_{f}H
with respect to
f:V(G)\to V(H)
, the edge set consists of the following two types of edges.

–

Inner edges
: for every
g\in V(G)
and every
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
we have
(g,h)(g,h^{\prime})\in E(G\otimes_{f}H)
.

–

Connecting edges
: for every
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
, we have
(g,f(g^{\prime}))(g^{\prime},f(g))\in E(G\otimes_{f}H)
.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs, and let
\ast
be an arbitrary graph product from the seven graph products just introduced. For a given
h\in V(H)
, the set of vertices
\{(g,h):\ g\in V(G)\}
is called a
G
-layer
. Analogously, for a given vertex
g\in V(G)
, the set of vertices
\{(g,h):\ h\in V(H)\}
is called an
H
-layer
. When speaking of a
G
-layer (resp.
H
-layer) we may also interpret it as the subgraph of
G\ast H
induced by the vertices from the layer. With this interpretation we may infer that in most of the cases, layers are isomorphic to the corresponding factors.

In the derivation of the M-polynomial for graph products, the degree of vertices in the resulting graphs play a central role. For convenience and later reference we list degree formulas for the products considered. These formulas are well-known for most of the products considered here, they can be deduced directly from definitions. So let
(g,h)
be an arbitrary vertex in any of these products. Then we have:

\displaystyle\deg_{G\square H}(g,h)

\displaystyle=\deg_{G}(g)+\deg_{H}(h)\,,

(1)

\displaystyle\deg_{G\times H}(g,h)

\displaystyle=\deg_{G}(g)\deg_{H}(h)\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\deg_{G\boxtimes H}(g,h)

\displaystyle=\deg_{G}(g)+\deg_{H}(h)+\deg_{G}(g)\deg_{H}(h)\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\deg_{G[H]}(g,h)

\displaystyle=n_{H}\deg_{G}(g)+\deg_{H}(h)\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\deg_{G\oplus H}(g,h)

\displaystyle=n_{H}\deg_{G}(g)+n_{G}\deg_{H}(h)-2\deg_{G}(g)\deg_{H}(h)\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\deg_{G\vee H}(g,h)

\displaystyle=n_{H}\deg_{G}(g)+n_{G}\deg_{H}(h)-\deg_{G}(g)\deg_{H}(h)\,.

(6)

Consider finally the Sierpiński product
G\otimes_{f}H
with respect to
f:V(G)\to V(H)
. Then setting

c(g,h)=\bigl|\{g^{\prime}\in N_{G}(g):\ f(g^{\prime})=h\}\bigr|\,,

we infer that

\displaystyle\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(g,h)

\displaystyle=\deg_{H}(h)+c(g,h)\,.

(7)

3
Formulas

In this section we present formulas for the M-polynomial of all the graph products considered in this paper. Each of the products is discussed in a separate subsection.

3.1
Cartesian Product

Theorem 3.1
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, then

M(G\cp H;x,y)=D_{G}(xy)\,M(H;x,y)+D_{H}(xy)\,M(G;x,y).

(8)

Proof.

Let
g\in V(G)
and let
e=(g,h_{1})(g,h_{2})
be an edge from an
H
-layer. Then
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
and by (
1
), the endpoints of
e
have respective degrees
\deg_{G}(g)+\deg_{H}(h_{1})
and
\deg_{G}(g)+\deg_{H}(h_{2})
.
Fix
i,j_{1},j_{2}
and consider edges
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
with

(\deg_{H}(h_{1}),\deg_{H}(h_{2}))=(j_{1},j_{2})
.
For each such edge, every choice of
g\in V(G)
with
\deg_{G}(g)=i

yields an edge in
G\cp H
whose endpoint degrees are

(i+j_{1},i+j_{2})
.
Hence, for each triple
(i,j_{1},j_{2})
we obtain

n_{i}(G)\,m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)
edges contributing the monomial

x^{i+j_{1}}y^{i+j_{2}}
.
Summing over all
i,j_{1},j_{2}
gives
\sum_{i}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}n_{i}(G)\,m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\,x^{i+j_{1}}\,y^{i+j_{2}}
.

By the commutativity of the Cartesian product, we get that the contribution of the edges from
H
-layers is
\sum_{j}\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}n_{j}(H)\,m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\,x^{i_{1}+j}\,y^{i_{2}+j}
, hence
M(G\cp H;x,y)
is equal to

\sum_{i}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}n_{i}(G)\,m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\,x^{i+j_{1}}\,y^{i+j_{2}}+\sum_{j}\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}n_{j}(H)\,m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\,x^{i_{1}+j}\,y^{i_{2}+j}.

Using
x^{i+j_{1}}y^{i+j_{2}}=(xy)^{i}x^{j_{1}}y^{j_{2}}
, we can factor the first sum as

\sum_{i}n_{i}(G)(xy)^{i}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{j_{1}}y^{j_{2}}=D_{G}(xy)\,M(H;x,y)\,.

The second sum factors analogously as
D_{H}(xy)\,M(G;x,y)
.
∎

3.2
Direct Product

Theorem 3.2
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, then

\displaystyle M(G\times H;x,y)=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)

\displaystyle\Bigl(x^{\min\{i_{1}j_{1},i_{2}j_{2}\}}y^{\max\{i_{1}j_{1},i_{2}j_{2}\}}

(9)

\displaystyle+x^{\min\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}}y^{\max\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}}\Bigr)\,.

Proof.

Let
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
, where
\deg_{G}(g_{1})=i_{1}\leq i_{2}=\deg_{G}(g_{2})
, and let
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
, where
\deg_{H}(h_{1})=j_{1}\leq j_{2}=\deg_{H}(h_{2})
. By the definition of the direct product, the edges
g_{1}g_{2}
and
h_{1}h_{2}
produce edges
(g_{1},h_{1})(g_{2},h_{2})
and
(g_{1},h_{2})(g_{2},h_{1})

in
G\times H
. By (
2
), the endpoint degrees of the first one have degrees
(i_{1}j_{1},i_{2}j_{2})
. This edge thus contributes to the degree-type given by the unordered pair

\{i_{1}j_{1},i_{2}j_{2}\}
, which we encode as
(a_{1},b_{1})
with
a_{1}\leq b_{1}
. Analogously, the endpoint degrees of the second edge have degrees
(i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1})
, so it contributes to the degree-type encoded by the unordered pair
\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}
, i.e.,
(a_{2},b_{2})
with
a_{2}\leq b_{2}
.

Now fix
(i_{1},i_{2})
and
(j_{1},j_{2})
.
There are
m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)
edges in
G
with endpoint degrees
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
, and

m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)
edges in
H
with endpoint degrees
\{j_{1},j_{2}\}
. Hence, for fixed
(i_{1},i_{2})
and
(j_{1},j_{2})
the total contribution to the
M-polynomial is

m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\bigl(x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}}+x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}}\bigr)\,,

where
a_{1}=\min\{i_{1}j_{1},i_{2}j_{2}\}
,
b_{1}=\max\{i_{1}j_{1},i_{2}j_{2}\}
,
a_{2}=\min\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}
, and
b_{2}=\max\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}
. Summing over all possible degree-types
(i_{1},i_{2})
of edges in
G
and

(j_{1},j_{2})
of edges in
H
yields the stated formula.
∎

Since,
i_{1}\leq i_{2}
and
j_{1}\leq j_{2}
, we can rewrite
M=M(G\times H;x,y)
as follows

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\Bigl(x^{i_{1}j_{1}}y^{i_{2}j_{2}}+x^{\min\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}}y^{\max\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\Bigl(\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{i_{1}j_{1}}y^{i_{2}j_{2}}+\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{\min\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}}y^{\max\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\Bigl(M(H;x^{i_{1}},y^{i_{2}})+\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{\min\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}}y^{\max\{i_{1}j_{2},i_{2}j_{1}\}}\Bigr)\,.

3.3
Strong Product

To formulate a formula for the M-polynomial of strong products, some preparation is needed. Let
G
and
H
be graphs. Then we can partition the set of edges of
G\boxtimes H
into those that lie in
G
-layers, those that lie in
H
-layers, and the other edges (direct-type ones). We respectively treat them as follows.

(1)

For edges
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
and vertices
h\in V(H)
, where
\deg_{G}(g_{1})=i_{1}
,
\deg_{G}(g_{2})=i_{2}
, and
\deg_{H}(h)=j
, set
d_{1}=i_{1}+j+i_{1}j
,
d_{2}=i_{2}+j+i_{2}j
, and

M^{(G)}(x,y)=\sum_{j}\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}n_{j}(H)m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)x^{\min(d_{1},d_{2})}y^{\max(d_{1},d_{2})}\,.

(10)

(2)

For edges
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
and vertices
g\in V(G)
, where
\deg_{H}(h_{1})=j_{1}
,
\deg_{H}(h_{2})=j_{2}
, and
\deg_{G}(g)=i
, set
e_{1}=i+j_{1}+ij_{1}
,
e_{2}=i+j_{2}+ij_{2}
, and

M^{(H)}(x,y)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}n_{i}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{\min(e_{1},e_{2})}y^{\max(e_{1},e_{2})}\,.

(11)

(3)

For edges
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
and
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
, where
\deg_{G}(g_{1})=i_{1}
,
\deg_{G}(g_{2})=i_{2}
,
\deg_{H}(h_{1})=j_{1}
, and
\deg_{H}(h_{2})=j_{2}
, with
i_{1}\leq i_{2}
and
j_{1}\leq j_{2}
, set
f_{11}=i_{1}+j_{1}+i_{1}j_{1}
,
f_{12}=i_{1}+j_{2}+i_{1}j_{2}
,
f_{21}=i_{2}+j_{1}+i_{2}j_{1}
,
f_{22}=i_{2}+j_{2}+i_{2}j_{2}
, and

M^{(\times)}(x,y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leq i_{2}\\
j_{1}\leq j_{2}\end{subarray}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\Big(x^{\min(f_{11},f_{22})}y^{\max(f_{11},f_{22})}+x^{\min(f_{12},f_{21})}y^{\max(f_{12},f_{21})}\Big)\,.

(12)

The above sums are taken over unordered degree pairs, so that
i_{1}\leq i_{2}
and
j_{1}\leq j_{2}
, and the powers of
x
and
y
are always ordered using
\min
and
\max
, in accordance with the M-polynomial convention
i\leq j
. With these preparations the main result for the strong product reads as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, then

M(G\boxtimes H;x,y)=M^{(G)}(x,y)+M^{(H)}(x,y)+M^{(\times)}(x,y)\,.

(13)

Proof.

If
(g,h)\in V(G\boxtimes H)
, then by (
3
),
\deg_{G\boxtimes H}(g,h)=\deg_{G}(g)+\deg_{H}(h)+\deg_{G}(g)\deg_{H}(h)
. As also already mentioned, we have a partition of
E(G\boxtimes H)
into three pairwise disjoint sets

E(G\boxtimes H)=E_{G}\,\dot{\cup}\,E_{H}\,\dot{\cup}\,E_{\times}\,,

(14)

where
E_{G}
is the set of edges lying in
G
-layers,
E_{H}
is the set of edges from
H
-layers, and
E_{\times}
is the set of the other edges (the direct-type ones). We examine now the contribution of each of the three parts to the M-polynomial.

Consider an edge
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
with
\deg_{G}(g_{1})=i_{1}
and
\deg_{G}(g_{2})=i_{2}
where
i_{1}\leq i_{2}
, and a vertex
h\in V(H)
with
\deg_{H}(h)=j
. Then, using (
3
), the edge
(g_{1},h)(g_{2},h)
contributes the unordered degree-pair
\{\min(d_{1},d_{2}),\max(d_{1},d_{2})\}
, where
d_{1}=i_{1}+j+i_{1}j
and
d_{2}=i_{2}+j+i_{2}j
. There are
m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)
edges
g_{1}g_{2}
in
G
whose endpoint degrees are
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
, and there are
n_{j}(H)
vertices
h
in
H
with degree
j
. Therefore, the number of edges in
E_{G}
corresponding to the triple
(i_{1},i_{2},j)
is
m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)n_{j}(H)
, and each of them contributes the same monomial
x^{\min(d_{1},d_{2})}y^{\max(d_{1},d_{2})}
in the M-polynomial. Summing over all
i_{1}\leq i_{2}
and all degrees
j
of
H
, we obtain the contribution (
10
) to
M^{(G)}(x,y)
.

By the commutativity of the strong product, we analogously infer that the edges from
H
-layers contribute (
11
) to
M^{(H)}(x,y)
.

Consider next the edges from
E_{\times}
. Let
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
and
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
, where
\deg_{G}(g_{1})=i_{1}
and
\deg_{G}(g_{2})=i_{2}
with
i_{1}\leq i_{2}
, and
\deg_{H}(h_{1})=j_{1}
and
\deg_{H}(h_{2})=j_{2}
with
j_{1}\leq j_{2}
. By (
3
),

\displaystyle\deg(g_{1},h_{1})

\displaystyle=i_{1}+j_{1}+i_{1}j_{1}=f_{11},\quad\deg(g_{1},h_{2})=i_{1}+j_{2}+i_{1}j_{2}=f_{12},

\displaystyle\deg(g_{2},h_{1})

\displaystyle=i_{2}+j_{1}+i_{2}j_{1}=f_{21},\quad\deg(g_{2},h_{2})=i_{2}+j_{2}+i_{2}j_{2}=f_{22}.

Thus, the edge
(g_{1},h_{1})(g_{2},h_{2})
has endpoint degrees
(f_{11},f_{22})
and contributes the unordered pair
\{\min(f_{11},f_{22}),\max(f_{11},f_{22})\}
, while the edge
(g_{1},h_{2})(g_{2},h_{1})
has endpoint degrees
(f_{12},f_{21})
and contributes the unordered pair
\{\min(f_{12},f_{21}),\max(f_{12},f_{21})\}
.

For fixed
i_{1}\leq i_{2}
and
j_{1}\leq j_{2}
, there are
m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)
choices of
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
with endpoint degrees
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
, and there are
m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)
choices of
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
with endpoint degrees
\{j_{1},j_{2}\}
. Hence the total contribution of the edges from
E_{\times}
is

\displaystyle M^{(\times)}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}\Big[m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{\min(f_{11},f_{22})}y^{\max(f_{11},f_{22})}

\displaystyle+m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{\min(f_{12},f_{21})}y^{\max(f_{12},f_{21})}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\Big(x^{\min(f_{11},f_{22})}y^{\max(f_{11},f_{22})}+x^{\min(f_{12},f_{21})}y^{\max(f_{12},f_{21})}\Big)\,.

Combining the contributions from
E_{G}
,
E_{H}
, and
E_{\times}
, the result follows.
∎

The terms
M^{(G)}(x,y)
,
M^{(H)}(x,y)
, and
M^{(\times)}(x,y)
can be formulated also in a more compact form as we demonstrate next. Since
i_{1}\leq i_{2}
and
j_{1}\leq j_{2}
, we have
i_{1}+j+i_{1}j=d_{1}\leq d_{2}=i_{2}+j+i_{2}j
,
i+j_{1}+ij_{1}=e_{1}\leq e_{2}=i+j_{2}+ij_{2}
, and
i_{1}+j_{1}+i_{1}j_{1}=f_{11}\leq f_{22}=i_{2}+j_{2}+i_{2}j_{2}
. Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle M^{(G)}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}n_{j}(H)m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)x^{\min(d_{1},d_{2})}y^{\max(d_{1},d_{2})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}n_{j}(H)\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)x^{d_{1}}y^{d_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}n_{j}(H)\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)x^{i_{1}+j+i_{1}j}y^{i_{2}+j+i_{2}j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}n_{j}(H)x^{j}y^{j}\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)x^{i_{1}(1+j)}y^{i_{2}(1+j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}n_{j}(H)x^{j}y^{j}M(G;x^{1+j},y^{1+j}).

Similarly,

\displaystyle M^{(H)}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}n_{i}(G)x^{i}y^{i}M(H;x^{1+i},y^{1+i}).

Moreover, setting
Z=M^{(\times)}(x,y)
, we can compute as follows:

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\Big(x^{\min(f_{11},f_{22})}y^{\max(f_{11},f_{22})}+x^{\min(f_{12},f_{21})}y^{\max(f_{12},f_{21})}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\Big(x^{f_{11}}y^{f_{22}}+x^{\min(f_{12},f_{21})}y^{\max(f_{12},f_{21})}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\Big(\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{i_{1}+j_{1}+i_{1}j_{1}}y^{i_{2}+j_{2}+i_{2}j_{2}}+\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{\min(f_{12},f_{21})}y^{\max(f_{12},f_{21})}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\Big(x^{i_{1}}y^{i_{2}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{j_{1}(1+i_{1})}y^{j_{2}(1+i_{2})}+\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{\min(f_{12},f_{21})}y^{\max(f_{12},f_{21})}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\Big(x^{i_{1}}y^{i_{2}}M(H;x^{1+i_{1}},y^{1+i_{2}})+\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{\min(f_{12},f_{21})}y^{\max(f_{12},f_{21})}\Big).

The Cartesian degree identity (
1
) can be obtained from the strong-product degree identity (
3
) by dropping the multiplicative term, hence
M(G\cp H;x,y)
can be recovered from
M(G\boxtimes H;x,y)
as follows.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, then

M(G\cp H;x,y)=M^{(G)}_{\square}(x,y)+M^{(H)}_{\square}(x,y),

where

M^{(G)}_{\square}(x,y)=\sum_{j}\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}n_{j}(H)m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)x^{\min(i_{1}+j,i_{2}+j)}y^{\max(i_{1}+j,i_{2}+j)}\,,

M^{(H)}_{\square}(x,y)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}n_{i}(G)m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)x^{\min(j_{1}+i,j_{2}+i)}y^{\max(j_{1}+i,j_{2}+i)}\,.

Proof.

Start with the proof of Theorem
3.3
and remove the direct adjacency condition.
Vertex degrees now satisfy (
1
) with no multiplicative term.
Substituting this degree into the M-polynomial summation formulas yields exactly the two
expressions displayed above. No direct edges appear, so there is no
M^{(\times)}
term.
∎

In contrast, the direct product
G\times H
cannot be obtained by
a direct specialization of Theorem
3.3
. Although
G\times H

uses only the direct adjacency mechanism (i.e., only the edge class

E_{\times}
survives), the vertex degrees in the direct product satisfy the
different identity (
2
) rather than (
3
).
Since the M-polynomial depends on the degrees of the endpoints in the
final graph, one must recompute the endpoint degrees under the
direct-product degree rule; therefore, a direct-product corollary does not
follow from Theorem
3.3
by merely discarding terms.

3.4
Lexicographic Product

Theorem 3.5
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, then

\displaystyle M(G[H];x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}n_{i}(G)\,m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\,x^{\,j_{1}+i\cdot n_{H}}\,y^{\,j_{2}+i\cdot n_{H}}

(15)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\,n_{j_{1}}(H)\,n_{j_{2}}(H)\,x^{\,j_{1}+i_{1}\cdot n_{H}}\,y^{\,j_{2}+i_{2}\cdot n_{H}}\,.

Proof.

Consider first the edges from
G
-layers. Fix a vertex
g\in G
, where
i=\deg_{G}(g)
.
Each edge
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
with degrees
(j_{1},j_{2})
gives the edge
(g,h_{1})(g,h_{2})
whose endpoint degrees are
(j_{1}+i\cdot n_{H},j_{2}+i\cdot n_{H})
. Hence the edges from the
H
-layers altogether contribute

\sum_{i}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}n_{i}(G)\,m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\,x^{j_{1}+i\cdot n_{H}}y^{j_{2}+i\cdot n_{H}}\,.

The other edges of
G[H]
connect vertices from different
H
-layers. Let
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
, where
i_{1}=\deg_{G}(g_{1})
and
i_{2}=\deg_{G}(g_{2})
, and let
h_{1},h_{2}\in V(H)
. Then the degrees of the end-vertices of the edge
(g_{1},h_{1})(g_{2},h_{2})
are
j_{1}+i_{1}\cdot n_{H}
and
j_{2}+i_{2}\cdot n_{H}
. Hence the contribution of such edges is

\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\,n_{j_{1}}(H)n_{j_{2}}(H)\,x^{j_{1}+i_{1}\cdot n_{H}}y^{j_{2}+i_{2}\cdot n_{H}}.

Adding the two contributions yields the stated formula.
∎

The M-polynomial of lexicographic products can also be expressed in the following compact form.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, then

M(G[H];x,y)=M(H;x,y)\,D_{G}\bigl((xy)^{n_{H}}\bigr)\;+\;M\bigl(G;x^{n_{H}},y^{n_{H}}\bigr)\,D_{H}(x)\,D_{H}(y)\,.

(16)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}n_{i}(G)\,m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\,x^{j_{1}+i\cdot n_{H}}y^{j_{2}+i\cdot n_{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}n_{i}(G)(xy)^{i\cdot n_{H}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\,x^{j_{1}}y^{j_{2}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(H)\,x^{j_{1}}y^{j_{2}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{i}n_{i}(G)\,(xy)^{i\cdot n_{H}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=M(H;x,y)\,D_{G}\bigl((xy)^{n_{H}}\bigr).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}\sum_{j_{1}\leq j_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\,n_{j_{1}}(H)n_{j_{2}}(H)\,x^{j_{1}+i_{1}n_{H}}y^{j_{2}+i_{2}n_{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\,x^{i_{1}n_{H}}y^{i_{2}n_{H}}\sum_{j_{1}}n_{j_{1}}(H)x^{j_{1}}\sum_{j_{2}}n_{j_{2}}(H)y^{j_{2}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{i_{1}\leq i_{2}}m_{i_{1},i_{2}}(G)\,(x^{n_{H}})^{i_{1}}(y^{n_{H}})^{i_{2}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{j_{1}}n_{j_{1}}(H)x^{j_{1}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{j_{2}}n_{j_{2}}(H)y^{j_{2}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=M\bigl(G;x^{n_{H}},y^{n_{H}}\bigr)\,D_{H}(x)\,D_{H}(y).

Adding the two contributions yields the claimed formula.
∎

3.5
Symmetric-Difference Product

Consider next the symmetric-difference product
G\oplus H
of graphs
G
and
H
. If
g\in V(G)
and
h\in V(H)
, where
\deg_{G}(g)=i
and
\deg_{H}(h)=j
, then by (
5
),

\deg_{G\oplus H}(g,h)=i(n_{H}-j)+j(n_{G}-i)=i\cdot n_{H}+j\cdot n_{G}-2ij.

(17)

Since this value is independent of the selection of a vertex of
G
of degree
i
and a vertex of
H
of degree
j
, we set

\delta_{\oplus}^{G\oplus H}(i,j)\;=\;\deg_{G\oplus H}(g,h)\,.

If
G
and
H
are clear from the context, we may simplify the notation
\delta_{\oplus}^{G\oplus H}(i,j)
to
\delta_{\oplus}(i,j)
.

Theorem 3.7
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, then

\displaystyle M(G\oplus H;x,y)={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\leq i^{\prime}}\ \sum_{j,j^{\prime}}m_{i,i^{\prime}}(G)\widehat{m}_{j,j^{\prime}}(H)x^{\min\{\delta_{\oplus}(i,j),\delta_{\oplus}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}y^{\max\{\delta_{\oplus}(i,j),\delta_{\oplus}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}

(18)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq j^{\prime}}\ \sum_{i,i^{\prime}}m_{j,j^{\prime}}(H)\widehat{m}_{i,i^{\prime}}(G)x^{\min\{\delta_{\oplus}(i,j),\delta_{\oplus}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}y^{\max\{\delta_{\oplus}(i,j),\delta_{\oplus}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}\,.

Proof.

In
G\oplus H
, two distinct vertices
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are adjacent if exactly one of
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
and
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
holds.

Assume first that
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
and
hh^{\prime}\notin E(H)
, where the endpoints of the edge
gg^{\prime}
have degrees
(i,i^{\prime})
, and the endpoints of
hh^{\prime}
have degrees
(j,j^{\prime})
. The endpoints of the edge
(g,h)(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
of
G\oplus H
have degrees
\delta_{\oplus}(i,j)
and
\delta_{\oplus}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
. The number of such ordered pairs
(h,h^{\prime})
is
\widehat{m}_{j,j^{\prime}}(H)
, and there are
m_{i,i^{\prime}}(G)
choices for
gg^{\prime}
. This gives the first summation of (
18
). By the symmetry, the second summation is obtained for the case when
gg^{\prime}\notin E(G)
and
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
. Summing
x^{\min}y^{\max}
over all edges gives the stated
formula.
∎

We next demonstrate that
\widehat{m}_{i,j}(G)
can be expressed in terms of the classical coefficients of the M-polynomial of the complement. This relation allows a reformulation of Theorem
3.7
purely in terms of classical degree-type numbers. To state the reformulation, we first define the indicator function by

\mathbf{1}_{\{i=j\}}:=\begin{cases}1;&i=j,\\
0;&i\neq j.\end{cases}

Proposition 3.8
.

If
G
is a graph, and
i
and
j
are degrees of some vertices of
G
, then

\widehat{m}_{i,j}(G)=2m_{n_{G}-1-i,\;n_{G}-1-j}(\overline{G})+\mathbf{1}_{\{i=j\}}n_{i}(G).

(19)

Proof.

Fix
i
and
j
, and consider the set

S_{i,j}=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in V(G)^{2}:\ g_{1}g_{2}\notin E(G),\ (\deg_{G}(g_{1}),\deg_{G}(g_{2}))=(i,j)\}.

Split
S_{i,j}
into disjoint subsets
S_{i,j}^{\neq}=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in S_{i,j}:g_{1}\neq g_{2}\}
and
S_{i,j}^{=}=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in S_{i,j}\}
.

Consider first the case when
g_{1}\neq g_{2}
. For
(g_{1},g_{2})\in S_{i,j}^{\neq}
, the condition
g_{1}g_{2}\notin E(G)
is equivalent to

g_{1}g_{2}\in E(\overline{G})
. Moreover, for every vertex
g_{1}
,

\deg_{\overline{G}}(g_{1})=n_{G}-1-\deg_{G}(g_{1}),

so
(\deg_{G}(g_{1}),\deg_{G}(g_{2}))=(i,j)
is equivalent to

(\deg_{\overline{G}}(g_{1}),\deg_{\overline{G}}(g_{2}))=(n_{G}-1-i,\;n_{G}-1-j)
.
Hence unordered edges
\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\in E(\overline{G})
of degree-type

\{n_{G}-1-i,\;n_{G}-1-j\}
correspond bijectively to pairs of ordered
elements
(g_{1},g_{2})
and
(g_{2},g_{1})
in
S_{i,j}^{\neq}
. Therefore,

|S_{i,j}^{\neq}|=2m_{n_{G}-1-i,\;n_{G}-1-j}(\overline{G}).

Consider second the case
g_{1}=g_{2}
. We have
(g_{1},g_{2})\in S_{i,j}^{=}
if and only if
i=j=\deg_{G}(g_{1})
.
Thus
|S_{i,j}^{=}|=\mathbf{1}_{\{i=j\}}n_{i}(G)
.

Summing the two cases yields (
19
) as claimed.
∎

3.6
Disjunction Product

In the disjunction product
G\vee H
of graphs
G
and
H
, if
g\in V(G)
has
\deg_{G}(g)=i
, and
h\in V(H)
has
\deg_{H}(h)=j
, then by (
6
),

\deg_{G\vee H}(g,h)=i\cdot n_{H}+j\cdot n_{G}-ij.

We set

\delta_{\vee}^{G\vee H}(i,j)\;=\;\deg_{G\vee H}(g,h)\,.

and when
G
and
H
are clear from the context, we may simplify the notation
\delta_{\vee}^{G\vee H}(i,j)
to
\delta_{\vee}(i,j)
.

Theorem 3.9
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, then

\displaystyle M(G\vee H;x,y)={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\leq i^{\prime}}\ \sum_{j,j^{\prime}}m_{i,i^{\prime}}(G)n_{j}(H)n_{j^{\prime}}(H)x^{\min\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}y^{\max\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}

(20)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\leq j^{\prime}}\ \sum_{i,i^{\prime}}m_{j,j^{\prime}}(H)n_{i}(G)n_{i^{\prime}}(G)x^{\min\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}y^{\max\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\leq i^{\prime}}\ \sum_{j\leq j^{\prime}}m_{i,i^{\prime}}(G)m_{j,j^{\prime}}(H)\Bigl(x^{\min\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}y^{\max\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}

\displaystyle+x^{\min\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j^{\prime}),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j)\}}y^{\max\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j^{\prime}),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j)\}}\Bigr).

Proof.

By the definition of
G\vee H
we have
E(G\vee H)=E_{A}\cup E_{B}
,
where

\displaystyle E_{A}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\}:\ gg^{\prime}\in E(G)\bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle E_{B}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\}:\ hh^{\prime}\in E(H)\bigr\}\,.

Moreover,

E_{A}\cap E_{B}=\bigl\{\{(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\}:\ gg^{\prime}\in E(G),hh^{\prime}\in E(H)\bigr\}\,.

Fix now an edge
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
whose endpoint degrees in
G
are
(i,i^{\prime})
(with
i\leq i^{\prime}
).
In
E_{A}
, the second coordinates are arbitrary, so for any choice of

h,h^{\prime}\in V(H)
with
\deg_{H}(h)=j
and
\deg_{H}(h^{\prime})=j^{\prime}
, the unordered pair

\{(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\}
is an edge of
E_{A}
.
The number of such choices is
n_{j}(H)n_{j^{\prime}}(H)
.
For each such edge, the endpoint degrees in
G\vee H
are

\delta_{\vee}(i,j)
and
\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
, hence its M-monomial is

x^{\min\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}y^{\max\{\delta_{\vee}(i,j),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}}.

Summing over all edges of
G
of type
(i,i^{\prime})
, of which there are
m_{i,i^{\prime}}(G)
,
and over all
(j,j^{\prime})
, gives exactly the first double sum in the statement, which is

M_{A}=\sum_{e\in E_{A}}x^{\min}y^{\max}
. By the symmetry, fixing an edge
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
yields the second sum.

Consider next the contribution of the intersection
E_{A}\cap E_{B}
.
Fix an edge
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
of type
(i,i^{\prime})
and an edge
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
of type
(j,j^{\prime})
.
There are
exactly two
distinct edges in
E_{A}\cap E_{B}
generated by these two edges:

\{(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{(g,h^{\prime}),(g^{\prime},h)\}.

(They are distinct because
h\neq h^{\prime}
and
g\neq g^{\prime}
.)
Their endpoint degree pairs in
G\vee H
are

\bigl(\delta_{\vee}(i,j),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad\bigl(\delta_{\vee}(i,j^{\prime}),\delta_{\vee}(i^{\prime},j)\bigr),

hence their
M
-monomials are the two terms inside the parentheses in the third sum.
There are
m_{i,i^{\prime}}(G)m_{j,j^{\prime}}(H)
ways to pick the underlying edges
gg^{\prime}
and
hh^{\prime}
,
so summing over all
(i,i^{\prime})
and
(j,j^{\prime})
yields

M_{I}=\sum_{e\in E_{A}\cap E_{B}}x^{\min}y^{\max}\,,

which is equal to the third (subtracted) sum in the statement.

Since
E(G\vee H)=E_{A}\cup E_{B}
and
E_{A},E_{B}
are finite,

\sum_{e\in E(G\vee H)}x^{\min}y^{\max}=\sum_{e\in E_{A}}x^{\min}y^{\max}+\sum_{e\in E_{B}}x^{\min}y^{\max}-\sum_{e\in E_{A}\cap E_{B}}x^{\min}y^{\max},

that is,
M(G\vee H;x,y)=M_{A}+M_{B}-M_{I}
, which is precisely the formula claimed.
∎

3.7
Sierpiński Product

Theorem 3.10
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs and
f:V(G)\to V(H)
, then

M(G\otimes_{f}H;x,y)=M^{(\mathrm{inner})}(x,y)+M^{(\mathrm{conn})}(x,y)\,,

(21)

where

\displaystyle M^{(\mathrm{inner})}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in V(G)}\ \sum_{\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(H)}x^{\min\{d_{g,h},\,d_{g,h^{\prime}}\}}\,y^{\max\{d_{g,h},\,d_{g,h^{\prime}}\}}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle M^{(\mathrm{conn})}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)}x^{\min\{d_{g,f(g^{\prime})},\,d_{g^{\prime},f(g)}\}}\,y^{\max\{d_{g,f(g^{\prime})},\,d_{g^{\prime},f(g)}\}},

(23)

and
d_{u,v}=\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(u,v)
for all
(u,v)\in V(G\otimes_{f}H)
.

Proof.

By the definition of the Sierpiński product,
E(G\otimes_{f}H)
is the disjoint union of
E_{\mathrm{inner}}
and
E_{\mathrm{conn}}
, where

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{inner}}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{(g,h),(g,h^{\prime})\}:g\in V(G),\ \{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(H)\bigr\},

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{conn}}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{(g,f(g^{\prime})),(g^{\prime},f(g))\}:\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)\bigr\}.

Using the standard symmetric definition,

M(G\otimes_{f}H;x,y)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E(G\otimes_{f}H)}x^{\min\{\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(u),\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(v)\}}\,y^{\max\{\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(u),\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(v)\}}\,.

(24)

Since
E(G\otimes_{f}H)
is a disjoint union of
E_{\mathrm{inner}}
and
E_{\mathrm{conn}}
,
we have

\displaystyle M(G\otimes_{f}H;x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E_{\mathrm{inner}}}x^{\min\{\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(u),\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(v)\}}y^{\max\{\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(u),\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(v)\}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E_{\mathrm{conn}}}x^{\min\{\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(u),\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(v)\}}y^{\max\{\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(u),\deg_{G\otimes_{f}H}(v)\}}.

Recall that degrees in
G\otimes_{f}H
are given in (
7
).
An inner edge has the form
\{(g,h),(g,h^{\prime})\}
with
\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(H)
. Hence its monomial
contribution equals

x^{\min\{d_{g,h},d_{g,h^{\prime}}\}}\,y^{\max\{d_{g,h},d_{g,h^{\prime}}\}}.

Summing over all
g\in V(G)
and all
\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(H)
gives
M^{(\mathrm{inner})}(x,y)
.

A connecting edge corresponding to
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)
has the form

\{(g,f(g^{\prime})),(g^{\prime},f(g))\}
. Hence
the monomial contribution equals

x^{\min\{d_{g,f(g^{\prime})},\,d_{g^{\prime},f(g)}\}}\,y^{\max\{d_{g,f(g^{\prime})},\,d_{g^{\prime},f(g)}\}}.

Summing over all
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)
gives
M^{(\mathrm{conn})}(x,y)
.

Therefore,
M(G\otimes_{f}H;x,y)=M^{(\mathrm{inner})}(x,y)+M^{(\mathrm{conn})}(x,y)
.
∎

To illustrate formulas from the previous section, we consider here products of paths. Unless stated otherwise, we assume that paths considered are of order at least three. For the path

\displaystyle D_{P_{n}}(t)

\displaystyle=2t+(n-2)t^{2}\,,

\displaystyle M(P_{n};x,y)

\displaystyle=2xy^{2}+(n-3)x^{2}y^{2}\,.

We consider products of paths in the same order as in the previous section.

\displaystyle=\bigl(2xy+(m-2)(xy)^{2}\bigr)\bigl(2xy^{2}+(n-3)x^{2}y^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle+\bigl(2xy+(n-2)(xy)^{2}\bigr)\bigl(2xy^{2}+(m-3)x^{2}y^{2}\bigr)\,.

Strong product

5
Conclusion and Perspectives

In this paper, we developed a systematic framework for computing the M-polynomial under graph products in which the vertex set of a product is the Cartesian product of the vertex sets of its factors. Using formulas for vertex degrees, we derived explicit expressions for the corresponding M-polynomials
M(G\ast H;x,y)
and, whenever possible, obtained compact factorizations in terms of
M(G;x,y)
,
M(H;x,y)
, and the degree polynomials
D_{G}
and
D_{H}
. In particular, the Cartesian and the lexicographic product admit elegant compact forms that reduce the global computation to substitutions and products of lower-dimensional polynomials.

As already mentioned, most papers on the M-polynomial to date focus on specific classes of graphs and specific chemical molecules. However, further research is certainly needed to gain a better understanding of this polynomial in more general terms. This includes investigations of coefficients, roots, and recursive structures.

From the perspective of our research, it certainly makes sense to further investigate other graph operations. Local operations appear less promising from this perspective, since they typically change the M-polynomial only slightly. On the other hand, there are “non-product" global operations, such as joins, hierarchical products, corona products, and blow-up graphs, that are interesting from the perspective of the M-polynomial.
