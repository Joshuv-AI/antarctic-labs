CACNA2D1 promotes ovulation by regulating ovarian immune cell infiltration
Authors: Conghui Guo, Jie Liu, Kaihao Chen, Guangbin Liu, Dewu Liu, Yaokun Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn the previous single-cell transcriptome analysis of goat ovulation, we identified that CACNA2D1 may play a critical role in immune cell infiltration during the ovulation process. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the function of CACNA2D1 in ovulation.MethodsCACNA2D1-associated signaling was pharmacologically inhibited in a mouse ovulation model to evaluate ovarian immune cell infiltration, ovulatory output, and transcriptomic changes. In vitro, CACNA2D1 knockdown and overexpression were performed in ovarian granulosa cells to assess chemokine production, intracellular calcium signaling, CaMKII activation, and cell proliferation. Granulosa cell-mediated macrophage recruitment was functionally examined using a RAW264.7 transwell migration assay.ResultsCACNA2D1 showed increased expression during the periovulatory period, and pharmacological inhibition of CACNA2D1-associated signaling in mice significantly reduced ovarian immune cell infiltration, particularly macrophages, accompanied by decreased ovulatory output. Ovarian transcriptomic analysis after inhibition revealed broad alterations in genes related to immune regulation, signal transduction, and cellular functional states. In granulosa cells, CACNA2D1 knockdown significantly reduced intracellular Ca2+ levels, decreased CaMKII phosphorylation, and suppressed the expression and secretion of immune recruitment-related factors, including IL6, CCL2, and CXCL12. Restoration of calcium signaling with A-23,187 rescued the reduced expression of these chemokines. Functionally, CACNA2D1 knockdown impaired granulosa cell-mediated RAW264.7 macrophage migration in a transwell co-culture system. CACNA2D1 also influenced granulosa cell proliferative activity, whereas its effect on macrophage-related responses appeared to be more closely associated with chemotactic signaling than with macrophage proliferation.ConclusionsThis study identifies CACNA2D1 as a periovulatory granulosa cell-associated regulator involved in calcium-dependent chemokine production and macrophage recruitment. These findings provide new insight into ovarian immune remodeling during ovulation and offer a potential molecular basis for future studies of follicular development, ovulation efficiency, and fertility regulation in livestock species.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12917-026-05617-2.

Background

In the reproductive biology of mammals, ovulation is not only a complex endocrine event but also a physiological process involving extensive immune regulation [1]. The immune cell populations inherent in the ovarian microenvironment, including macrophages, T cells, and neutrophils [2–4], vary in number and activity in response to fluctuations in hormone levels throughout the reproductive cycle [5]. Particularly following the LH (Luteinizing hormone) surge, these immune cells migrate from the bloodstream into the ovarian tissue and participate in the regulation of the ovulation process [6]. These immune cells contribute to follicle wall remodeling and rupture by producing cytokines and proteolytic enzymes, including interleukins, TNF-related mediators, and matrix metalloproteinases, thereby facilitating ovulation [7–9] .The role of immune cells in ovulation resembles a finely regulated inflammatory response [10, 11]. The biological nature of this process reveals the complex interplay between hormonal signaling and immune regulation networks. However, while it is known that the surge of LH increases the number of immune cells within the follicle, the specific mechanisms by which these cells are precisely regulated to key locations remain unclear.

In recent years, there has been an increasing amount of research on the migration, localization, and function of immune cells during ovulation. Studies have shown that ovarian granulosa cells not only play a role in follicular development, maturation, and rupture during ovulation, but also contribute significantly to immune regulation [12]. Granulosa cells may regulate the migration and activation of immune cells by secreting various cytokines and chemokines, such as CXCL1, CXCL8, and CCL20 [13, 14], thereby promoting follicular rupture and oocyte release. The precise regulation of immune cell infiltration is essential for efficient ovulation. Although previous studies have established the importance of immune cell infiltration in the ovulatory process, the molecular signals by which ovarian somatic cells coordinate immune cell recruitment remain incompletely understood.

In previous studies, we found that the main cell types in the ovarian follicular fluid during ovulation are granulosa cells and immune cells, and there is an interaction between these two cell types. Therefore, we further explored and discovered that CACNA2D1 may promote the proliferation of granulosa cells and have a recruiting effect on immune cell infiltration. This finding is crucial for understanding the cellular and molecular basis of ovulation and may provide new strategies for treating related reproductive disorders.

Results

CACNA2D1 expression is elevated in ovulating follicles

Previous studies have identified that the main cell types in goat ovarian follicular fluid during the ovulatory phase are granulosa cells and immune cells, and that there is an interaction between these two cell types [15]. Based on this finding, we hypothesize that granulosa cells may play a role in recruiting immune cells to infiltrate the ovarian environment. Given that immune cell infiltration primarily occurs within 6 h after the LH surge, we performed differential gene GO analysis of granulosa cells from single-cell sequencing samples at FL (Follicular fluid, FL) 0 h and FL 6 h. Thirteen transport-related terms were found to be enriched. Among these, seven terms all included CACNA2D1, which exhibited the highest association frequency (Fig. 1A). Subsequently, We analyzed the expression pattern of CACNA2D1 during the periovulatory in granulosa cells using pseudotime trajectory analysis. The results showed that CACNA2D1 expression was low at the initial stage of pseudotime, then progressively increased as pseudotime advanced, reaching its peak at the late pseudotime stage (Fig. 1B). This indicates that CACNA2D1 is continuously upregulated during ovulation, suggesting its potential involvement in the regulation of this process. Quantitative analysis of CACNA2D1 expression in follicles at different developmental stages revealed that the expression level of CACNA2D1 in large follicles was significantly higher than in medium-sized follicles (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1C), further confirming that CACNA2D1 expression is elevated during ovulation. Interestingly, we analyzed spatial transcriptome data from mouse follicles at different time points after hCG (human Chorionic Gonadotropin, hCG) injection [16] and found that CACNA2D1 was highly expressed within 1–6 h after ovulation induction, similar to the trend observed in this study (Fig. 1D). Collectively, these results demonstrate a conserved periovulatory upregulation of CACNA2D1 in ovarian granulosa cells from both goats and mice, supporting the hypothesis that CACNA2D1 may participate in immune cell recruitment within the ovulatory follicular microenvironment.

Effects of CACNA2D1 on immune cell infiltration and ovulation

Prior to exploring the functional role of CACNA2D1 in ovulation, we first investigated the dynamic changes of immune cells during the ovulatory process in mice as a model organism. The experimental results showed that 3 h after hCG injection, the number of myeloid cells in the ovaries significantly increased, predominantly consisting of M1 macrophages (Fig. 2ABCD). At 6 h, the proportion of M1 cells began to decrease (Fig. 2D), while at 9 h, the number of M2 macrophages significantly increased (Fig. 2E). These findings are consistent with previous observations in goats, particularly regarding the trend of myeloid cell numbers and the transition between M1 and M2 macrophages [15]. These results suggest that goats and mice share comparable immune dynamics during ovulation. Based on this similarity and the experimental advantages of the mouse model, mice were used for subsequent functional studies of CACNA2D1.

Next, we examined the role of CACNA2D1 in ovulation by locally inhibiting its function in the mouse ovary. Gabapentin, an inhibitor of CACNA2D1/α2δ-1-associated signaling, was injected into the ovary (Fig. 3A), and ovarian cells were collected 6 h later for flow cytometric analysis. Compared with the PBS group, gabapentin treatment significantly reduced the total number of ovarian immune cells (P < 0.05) (Figure. 3B), with prominent reductions in myeloid cells, especially macrophages (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3CD). In contrast, no significant difference in immune cell abundance was observed between the gabapentin-treated group and the blank group representing the non-ovulatory phase, including the luteal phase or follicular developmental phase (P > 0.05). These findings indicate that inhibition of CACNA2D1/α2δ-1-associated signaling markedly reduced periovulatory immune cell infiltration, resulting in ovarian immune cell levels comparable to those observed in the non-ovulatory state. To further evaluate ovulatory outcome, mouse oviducts were collected 12 h after the LH surge. Compared with the PBS group, gabapentin-treated mice exhibited a smaller oviductal ampulla and a significantly reduced number of oocytes recovered from the oviducts (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3E). Together, these results suggest that CACNA2D1/α2δ-1-associated signaling contributes to periovulatory immune cell infiltration and ovulatory output in mice.

Gabapentin is a clinically used drug for the treatment of epilepsy, neuropathic pain, and related disorders. We therefore further evaluated whether systemic gabapentin administration affects ovarian immune remodeling and ovulatory function. To mimic systemic drug exposure, mice were intraperitoneally administered gabapentin, and ovarian cells and oviducts were collected at 6 h and 12 h after the LH surge, respectively. Compared with the PBS group, gabapentin treatment significantly reduced the total number of ovarian immune cells in a dose-dependent manner (P < 0.05) (Figure. S1A), with a particularly pronounced reduction in macrophages (P < 0.05) (Figure. S1BC). Consistently, gabapentin dose-dependently decreased the size of the oviductal ampulla and significantly reduced the number of oocytes recovered from the oviducts (P < 0.05) (Figure. S1DE).

Transcriptome analysis of ovaries following CACNA2D1 inhibition

To investigate the regulatory effect of CACNA2D1 on the gene expression of ovarian granulosa cells, we performed transcriptome sequencing on ovaries after inhibiting CACNA2D1. After quality control, the sequencing data showed that the Q20 value was above 98% and the Q30 value was above 94%, indicating high data quality. Additionally, clustering analysis revealed significant differences in gene expression patterns between samples, further confirming the high quality of the sequencing data (Fig. 4A). Differential analysis showed that a total of 702 differentially expressed genes were identified compared to the control group, including 580 downregulated genes and 122 upregulated genes (Fig. 4B). Among the significantly downregulated genes, representative genes potentially related to inflammatory regulation, signal transduction, and cell adhesion included Serpinb2, Il33, Prkg2, and Nrcam. To investigate the biological functions of differentially expressed genes, enrichment analysis was conducted. KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that a total of 8 terms were significantly enriched, including pathways such as Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, cAMP signaling pathway, and Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction (Fig. 4C). GO enrichment analysis identified 559 significantly enriched terms, including signaling receptor binding, regulation of cell population proliferation, and extracellular space (Fig. 4D). Therefore, we hypothesize that CACNA2D1 may play an important regulatory role in ovarian follicular development, immune regulation, and cell proliferation and differentiation.

CACNA2D1 influences the expression of granulosa cell chemokines

Given the classical theory that immune cell migration is regulated by chemokines and the enrichment of the chemokine signaling pathway in the transcriptome, we hypothesize that CACNA2D1 may be involved in the dynamic regulation of immune cells by modulating chemokines. Therefore, we examined the expression of factors related to immune cell chemotaxis in granulosa cells. The results indicated that inhibition of CACNA2D1significantly suppressed the mRNA expression of IL6, CCL2, CCL5, CCL7, CXCL10, CXCL12, and CXCL16 in granulosa cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5AB), and significantly reduced the extracellular secretion levels of IL6, CCL2, and CXCL12 (Fig. 5CDE). Overexpression of CACNA2D1 exhibited the opposite trend, with a significant increase in the mRNA expression levels of IL6, CCL2, CCL5, CCL7, and CXCL12 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5FG), and a significant elevation in the secretion level of the chemokine IL6 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5H). However, there were no significant differences in the mRNA levels of CXCL10 and CXCL16, or in the secretion levels of CCL2 and CXCL12 (P > 0.05) (Fig. 5GIJ). Therefore, CACNA2D1 may influence immune cell recruitment by regulating the secretion of chemokines.

CACNA2D1 influences chemokine secretion by regulating intracellular calcium signaling in granulosa cells

CACNA2D1, as a key auxiliary subunit of the voltage-gated calcium channel, plays a crucial role in the regulation of intracellular calcium homeostasis. Given that calcium signaling pathways are widely involved in fundamental biological processes such as transcription factor activation and vesicular transport, and that the calcium signaling pathway was enriched in the transcriptome, this study hypothesizes that CACNA2D1 may influence the secretion of chemokines through the regulation of calcium signaling. To test this hypothesis, we measured intracellular calcium concentrations. The results showed that overexpression of CACNA2D1 significantly increased calcium levels in granulosa cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6A), while siRNA-mediated silencing of CACNA2D1 significantly decreased calcium levels in granulosa cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6B).

To further investigate the regulatory role of calcium signaling in chemokine secretion, calcium ionophore A-23,187 was introduced 24 h after siRNA transfection to rescue calcium signaling. The results showed that, compared to the siRNA-only interference group, the intracellular calcium fluorescence intensity in the A-23,187 treatment group was significantly restored to the level of the normal control group (siRNA + A-23187 vs. siRNA, P < 0.05; siRNA + A-23187 vs. NC, P > 0.05) (Fig. 6C). Notably, the reactivation of calcium signaling significantly restored the mRNA expression levels of IL6, CCL2, and CXCL12 (Fig. 6D), indicating that A-23,187 treatment effectively reversed the downregulation of chemokine expression caused by CACNA2D1 inhibition.

CACNA2D1 promotes the recruitment of RAW264.7 macrophages by ovarian granulosa cells

CACNA2D1 regulates the calcium ion concentration and chemokine secretion in granulosa cells, prompting us to urgently investigate whether it truly exerts a recruitment effect on macrophages. Subsequently, co-culturing granulosa cells with RAW264.7 cells (Fig. 7A) revealed that when CACNA2D1 gene expression was suppressed, the number of RAW264.7 cells in the transwell chamber was significantly reduced, with a relative migration rate decrease of 23% (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7B). This indicates that, under non-direct contact co-culture conditions, the suppression of CACNA2D1 gene expression in granulosa cells significantly weakens the migratory ability of RAW264.7 cells.

To investigate the downstream signaling mechanisms of CACNA2D1, this study employed western blotting to detect CaMKII [17], as well as commonly associated NFκB and JAK-STAT3 pathway proteins related to chemokine secretion. The results revealed that inhibition of CACNA2D1 significantly reduced the expression of CACNA2D1 protein in granulosa cells (Fig. 7C). The relative expression of p- CaMKII/ CaMKII was significantly lower in the experimental group compared to the control group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7D). However, no significant differences were observed in the relative expression of p-STAT3/STAT3 and pP65/P65 proteins (P > 0.05) (Figure S2AB). These findings suggest that CACNA2D1 may influence calcium signaling and chemokine secretion by affecting the CaMKII pathway.

CACNA2D1 promotes the proliferation of ovarian granulosa cells, and ovarian granulosa cells promote the proliferation of RAW264.7 cells

In the previous experiments involving CACNA2D1, we found that this gene may influence granulosa cell proliferation. Therefore, we assessed the effect of CACNA2D1 on granulosa cells using CCK8, EdU, and flow cytometry. The results showed that knockdown of CACNA2D1 in granulosa cells significantly decreased their proliferative activity (Fig. 8AB), with a marked reduction in the percentage of cells in the G2/M phase (P < 0.05), while no significant differences were observed in the G1 and S phases (P > 0.05) (Fig. 8C). Conversely, overexpression of CACNA2D1 in granulosa cells significantly increased their proliferative activity (Figure S3AB), with a significant decrease in the G1 phase proportion (P < 0.05) and a significant increase in the G2/M phase proportion (P < 0.05), while no statistical differences were observed in the S phase proportion (P > 0.05) (Figure S3C).

Subsequently, the effect of granulosa cell secretions on the proliferation of RAW264.7 cells was investigated. An indirect co-culture method was employed, where the granulosa cell culture supernatant was added to the culture medium of RAW264.7 cells. CCK8 assays revealed that the proliferation activity of RAW264.7 cells were significantly increased at 24, 48, and 72 h after the addition of the supernatant, compared to both the control and blank groups (P < 0.01) (Fig. 9A). EdU staining further indicated that the addition of the supernatant significantly promoted RAW264.7 cell proliferation (Fig. 9B). Cell cycle analysis showed that the proportion of RAW264.7 cells in the G1 phase was significantly reduced (P < 0.05) after the addition of granulosa cell supernatant, while the proportion in the S phase was significantly increased (P < 0.05), with no significant difference observed in the G2 phase (P > 0.05) (Fig. 9C). These findings suggest that granulosa cell secretions may enhance RAW264.7 cell proliferation by promoting the transition from the G1 to the S phase.

Furthermore, we investigated the effect of granulosa cell culture supernatants with overexpression or suppression of CACNA2D1 on the proliferation of RAW264.7 cells. The results showed that at 24 h and 48 h, RAW264.7 cells treated with different granulosa cell supernatants exhibited higher proliferative activity (P < 0.01). At 72 h, the proliferative activity of RAW264.7 cells treated with siRNA-NC granulosa cell supernatants was significantly increased compared to the control group (P < 0.05). Moreover, there was no significant difference between the RAW264.7 cells treated with supernatants from granulosa cells overexpressing CACNA2D1 and the control group (P > 0.05), and similarly, no significant difference was observed between RAW264.7 cells treated with siRNA-CACNA2D1 granulosa cell supernatants and the control group (P > 0.05) (Fig. 9D). In conclusion, these results suggest that CACNA2D1 has no significant effect on the proliferation of RAW264.7 cells.

Discussion

Ovulation is a highly coordinated inflammatory-like physiological process involving multiple events, including local vascular responses, extracellular matrix degradation, chemokine release, and immune cell recruitment [18]. Although immune cell infiltration is considered an important component of periovulatory follicular microenvironment remodeling [19], the molecular mechanisms governing immune cell recruitment remain poorly understood. In the present study, CACNA2D1 emerged as a candidate regulatory molecule that is markedly upregulated in granulosa cells during the periovulatory period. Functional analyses further suggest that CACNA2D1 may contribute to ovarian immune cell recruitment by coordinating calcium signaling, CaMKII activation, and chemokine secretion in granulosa cells. Thus, CACNA2D1 may serve as a molecular link connecting endocrine stimulation, granulosa cell responses, and the establishment of the periovulatory ovarian immune microenvironment.

Granulosa cells exhibit marked functional plasticity during follicular development. Beyond their established roles in steroidogenesis, oocyte support, and follicular structural maintenance, accumulating evidence indicates that periovulatory granulosa cells actively shape the local ovulatory microenvironment by producing cytokines, chemokines, and extracellular matrix-remodeling factors [14, 20, 21]. In the present study, CACNA2D1 expression gradually increased during ovulation in goats, suggesting that CACNA2D1 may not merely participate in general follicular growth, but may be more closely related to the functional transition of mature follicles after they enter the ovulatory state. Together with the similar expression pattern observed in mouse spatial transcriptomic data after hCG-induced ovulation, these findings suggest that CACNA2D1-associated regulation may represent a relatively conserved feature of ovulatory follicles across species and that CACNA2D1 may serve as a candidate molecular indicator of periovulatory granulosa cell activation.

Immune cell infiltration is a key event during ovulation, yet this process requires precise temporal and spatial regulation. Classical studies have shown that leukocytes, including macrophages and neutrophils, accumulate within the follicle during the periovulatory period [10], and leukocyte supplementation increases LH-induced ovulation rates in the in vitro perfused rat ovary, supporting their contributory role in ovulatory efficiency [22]. Consistent with these observations, inhibition of CACNA2D1-associated signaling in the present study markedly reduced periovulatory immune cell infiltration and was accompanied by decreased ovulatory output, supporting a functional association between immune cell recruitment and ovulatory outcome. These findings further indicate that immune cell infiltration represents an important component of the ovulatory microenvironment. Notably, gabapentin is a pharmacological ligand of the voltage-gated calcium channel α2δ subunit and is clinically used for the treatment of neuropathic pain, epilepsy, and related disorders [23–25]. In this study, gabapentin treatment reduced ovarian immune cell infiltration during the periovulatory period and impaired ovulatory output, suggesting that α2δ subunit-associated calcium channel signaling may be relevant to ovarian function. This finding also raises the need to systematically evaluate the reproductive effects of gabapentin and related α2δ-modulating drugs. Such evaluation may be particularly important in veterinary reproductive management, where ovulation efficiency directly affects fertility in livestock species, especially during estrus synchronization, superovulation, artificial insemination, and embryo transfer programs.

CACNA2D1 encodes the α2δ-1 auxiliary subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels and is closely associated with calcium channel trafficking, membrane localization, and calcium signal regulation [23, 26]. Calcium signaling serves as a key intracellular interface linking extracellular stimuli to transcriptional, secretory, and functional responses. During ovulation, LH/hCG stimulation induces rapid molecular and cellular remodeling [27], in which calcium-dependent pathways may connect endocrine activation with the local inflammatory-like follicular response. In this study, altered CACNA2D1 expression affected intracellular Ca2+ levels in granulosa cells, while the calcium ionophore A-23,187 restored the expression of IL6, CCL2, and CXCL12 under CACNA2D1 knockdown conditions. These findings suggest that CACNA2D1 may regulate periovulatory immune cell recruitment by maintaining calcium-dependent chemokine production in granulosa cells. Periovulatory chemokine secretion has traditionally been considered part of the LH/hCG-induced inflammatory-like response, yet the upstream signals that precisely regulate this process remain incompletely understood. Previous studies have shown that LH stimulation induces chemokine-related molecules in granulosa cells, including CXCL8, CXCL16, CCL20, and CXCR4 [13, 14, 20, 21, 28]. Building on these findings, our data suggest that CACNA2D1 may provide a calcium-dependent signaling basis required for periovulatory chemokine expression.

The involvement of the CaMKII pathway further supports the importance of calcium-dependent signaling in this process. CaMKII is a canonical calcium/calmodulin-dependent kinase that translates intracellular Ca2+ fluctuations into downstream transcriptional, secretory, and functional responses [17, 29]. In this study, CACNA2D1 knockdown decreased the p- CaMKII/ CaMKII ratio, whereas no obvious changes were observed in the STAT3 or NF-κB p65 pathways, suggesting that Ca2+/CaMKII-related signaling may participate in CACNA2D1-associated chemokine regulation. Biologically, ovulation requires a tightly controlled inflammatory state: insufficient inflammation may impair follicle wall rupture, whereas excessive inflammation may disrupt ovarian tissue homeostasis. Therefore, CACNA2D1-associated calcium signaling may help modulate chemokine secretion and immune cell recruitment, thereby contributing to the maintenance of a balanced periovulatory microenvironment. However, because CaMKII inhibition or rescue experiments were not performed, the causal relationship remains to be established. Future studies using CaMKII inhibitors, activators, or rescue strategies are needed to determine whether CaMKII is required for CACNA2D1-dependent chemokine production and macrophage recruitment.

In addition to its role in immune chemotaxis, CACNA2D1 may also participate in the regulation of granulosa cell proliferation. Our results showed that modulation of CACNA2D1 expression affected granulosa cell proliferative activity and cell-cycle distribution, consistent with previous reports implicating CACNA2D1 in cell proliferation [30, 31]. In mature follicles, the maintenance of granulosa cell proliferation and function may contribute to local secretory activity, follicular structural stability, and the establishment of a microenvironment permissive for oocyte release. However, the relationship between granulosa cell proliferation and immune cell recruitment requires further clarification. Although granulosa cell-conditioned medium promoted RAW264.7 cell proliferation, altering CACNA2D1 expression in granulosa cells did not further affect RAW264.7 proliferation. These observations suggest that the overall granulosa cell secretome may support macrophage expansion or survival, whereas CACNA2D1 appears to act primarily by regulating directional macrophage migration rather than directly promoting macrophage proliferation. Therefore, within the ovulatory immune microenvironment, CACNA2D1 may contribute more prominently to immune cell infiltration through chemotactic regulation than through macrophage expansion.

In summary, this study shows that CACNA2D1 is upregulated in granulosa cells during the LH/hCG-induced periovulatory period and may help maintain intracellular Ca2+ signaling and CaMKII-related responses. This, in turn, promotes the production of chemokines such as IL6, CCL2, and CXCL12, thereby facilitating macrophage recruitment to the ovary and contributing to follicular rupture and oocyte release. These findings provide new insight into the relationship among endocrine stimulation, granulosa cell calcium signaling, and the local immune microenvironment during ovulation. CACNA2D1 may serve as a candidate molecular indicator of periovulatory granulosa cell activation and follicular ovulatory competence. However, further validation in goat granulosa cells or ovarian tissue is required to confirm the protein expression, spatial localization, and functional relevance of CACNA2D1 in small ruminants. These findings may provide a molecular basis for future studies aimed at improving ovulation efficiency in small ruminants and for evaluating the potential reproductive effects of clinically used gabapentin and related α2δ-modulating drugs.

Conclusions

This study found that injection of a specific CACNA2D1 inhibitor into the ovaries of mice reduced immune cell infiltration during ovulation, thereby decreasing the number of ovulations. At the cellular level, inhibition of CACNA2D1 expression suppressed granulosa cell proliferation, weakened calcium signaling in granulosa cells, and reduced the secretion of chemokines such as IL6, CCL2, and CXCL12, consequently inhibiting the migration ability of RAW264.7 cells (Fig. 10). In conclusion, this study demonstrates that CACNA2D1 can serve as a target for regulating ovulation in goats and mice, providing new theoretical insights for improving mammalian fertility and offering novel approaches for the treatment of related ovulatory disorders and ovarian diseases.

Methods

Experimental animals and cells

The female Kunming mice (6 weeks old, weighing approximately 25 g) used in this experiment were purchased from Zhuhai BesTest Bio-Tech Co,.Ltd., with animal license number (SCXK Yue) 2025-0051 and batch number 20,251,103. The experimental protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of South China Agricultural University (2025f423), and all procedures involving the animals strictly adhered to the regulations set forth by the university’s Animal Ethics Committee. Immortalized mouse ovarian granulosa cells (catalog number: iCell-0114a) were purchased from ICell Bioscience Inc.

Collection of mouse ovaries

Mice were deeply anesthetized with 5% isoflurane for 3–5 min until loss of the righting reflex, followed by euthanasia via potassium chloride injection at a dose of 0.1 g/kg. Death was confirmed by the absence of spontaneous breathing, heartbeat, pedal withdrawal reflex, and corneal reflex for ≥ 2 min. All animal procedures were performed in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines. Subsequently, the mouse was positioned with its dorsal side facing up, and an incision approximately 1 cm in length was made along both sides of the spine, approximately at the midpoint of the back, to expose a mass of white adipose tissue. The adipose layer was gently displaced to reveal the mulberry-shaped ovaries. Using a stereomicroscope, the surrounding adipose tissue and oviduct were carefully separated, and the clean ovaries were placed into a centrifuge tube for cell collection.

Drug administration to experimental animals

The mice were housed under a 12-hour light/dark cycle with free access to food and water. After a 2-week acclimation period, each mouse was intraperitoneally injected with 10 IU pregnant mare serum gonadotropin (PMSG), followed by an ovarian injection of gabapentin (MCE, USA) 48 h later. Initially, the mice were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection with tribromoethanol (Avertin) at a dose of 250 mg/kg (10 µL/g). (Melun, China), and their backs were disinfected with 75% ethanol. After locating the right ovarian bursa, 2 µL of 100 µg/mL gabapentin was slowly injected into the right ovary using a microinjection syringe, while an equal volume of PBS was injected into the left ovary. Then sutured, and the surgical sites were disinfected with iodine tincture, followed by an intraperitoneal injection of 10 IU hCG. Mice were sacrificed by anesthesia 6–12 h later, and the ovaries or fallopian tubes were harvested for subsequent experiments.

For the gabapentin intraperitoneal injection experiment, the mice were randomly divided into four groups (six mice per group). After two weeks of acclimation, the mice were injected with PMSG, followed by intraperitoneal injection of 0.1 mL of either 0 (PBS), 10, 50, or 100 mg/kg gabapentin 36 h later. After 12 h, the same dose of gabapentin was administered, followed by 10 IU hCG injection. Mice were anesthetized and sacrificed 6–12 h later, and ovaries or fallopian tubes were collected for further analysis.

Transcriptome analysis

After drug treatment, ovarian tissues were collected from the mice, and total RNA was extracted using the Trizol method. The RNA concentration was ensured to be no less than 30 ng/µL, with a RIN value greater than 6.5, and an OD260/280 ratio close to 2.0. mRNA was then enriched using Oligo(dT) magnetic beads for library construction, followed by high-throughput sequencing using the NovaSeq Reagent Kit (NovaSeq X Plus). After quality control of the raw data, gene and transcript expression levels were quantitatively analyzed using RSEM software, and differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2 software. The criteria for selecting differentially expressed genes were set as: FDR < 0.05 and |log2FC| ≥ 1. For the selected differential genes, GO enrichment analysis was conducted using the Goatools software, and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed using the Python scipy library. A corrected p-value (Corrected P-Value) less than 0.05 was considered to indicate significant enrichment of the corresponding GO terms or KEGG pathways.

Overexpression vector construction and siRNA design

The lentiviral vector LVO11-pHBLV-CMV-MCS-3flag-EF1-T2A-Zsgreen-Puro (abbreviated as LVO11) was purchased from Hanbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Primers were initially designed using the online primer design tool CE Design (https://tool.vazyme.com:18002/cetool/singlefragment.htmL) provided by Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd. The target fragment was then ligated to the vector using seamless cloning technology. The ligated vector was transformed into DH5α cells, and after overnight incubation at 37 °C, single colonies were selected for colony PCR and sequencing verification. The plasmid containing the correct CACNA2D1 fragment was named LVO11-CACNA2D1, while the control plasmid without the inserted target gene was named LVO11-NC. The SiRNA sequence, CAAACAAGATTGATCTATA, was synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.

Elisa

The determination of IL-6 concentration was performed according to the instructions provided in the Elabscience Mouse IL-6 (Interleukin 6) ELISA Kit manual. The CCL2 concentration was measured following the instructions in the MENMIAN Mouse CCL2 ELISA Kit manual. The CXCL12 concentration was determined based on the guidelines outlined in the Elabscience Mouse SDF-1/CXCL12 (Stromal Cell Derived Factor 1) ELISA Kit manual.

Detection of cellular calcium ion concentration

The intracellular calcium ion concentration was detected using fluorescence probe technology 48 h after transfection with particles. The specific procedure was carried out according to the instructions of the Fluo-4 AM Calcium Ion Detection Kit (Beyotime). For the rescue experiment, after transfection of siRNA into the particle cells for 24 h, the calcium ion agonist A-23,187 (prepared in DMSO, final DMSO concentration ≤ 0.1%) was added to the culture medium to achieve a final concentration of 2 µM [32, 33]. After continued incubation for 24 h, the intracellular calcium ion concentration was measured again using the aforementioned method.

Co-culture and cell migration

Ovarian granulosa cells were seeded in a 24-well culture plate, and RAW264.7 cells were inoculated into adjacent culture chambers. After interference treatment of CACNA2D1 in granulosa cells, RAW264.7 cells were transferred to the chamber opposite the granulosa cells for co-culture for 48 h. At the end of the co-culture period, cotton swabs were used to gently wipe the inner surface of the chamber to remove the cells, and the chambers were then fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 20 min. Following fixation, the cells were stained with crystal violet for 10 min. Finally, images were captured under a microscope to count the number of cells on the outer side of the chamber membrane. Five fields from the top, middle, bottom, left, and right of each chamber were selected for cell counting, and the average cell number was calculated. The number of migrating cells in the control group was used as a baseline for normalization, and the relative migration rate was calculated.

Indirect co-culture

Transfer the culture supernatant from ovarian granulosa cells to the medium of RAW264.7 cells for indirect co-culture. The design of experiment 1 is as follows: (1) Blank Control Group: RAW264.7 cells were cultured under normal conditions; (2) Experimental Group: RAW + GC supernatant. When the confluence of RAW264.7 cells reached 50%, 5% granulosa cells culture supernatant was added to the culture medium; (3) Control Group: RAW + DMEM/F12. When the confluence of RAW264.7 cells reached 50%, 5% complete DMEM/F12 medium was added to the culture medium.

Groupings for Experiment 2 are as follows: (1) The blank control group, where RAW264.7 cells are cultured under normal conditions; (2)The overexpression experimental group (RAW + OE-CACNA2D1), where granulosa cells are transfected with CACNA2D1 vector, and after 24 h, the supernatant is collected and added to the RAW264.7 cell culture medium; (3)The overexpression control group (RAW+empty vector), where granulosa cells are transfected with an empty vector, and after 24 h, the supernatant is collected and added to the RAW264.7 cell culture medium; (4) The inhibition experimental group (RAW+siRNA), where granulosa cells are transfected with siRNA targeting CACNA2D1, and after 24 h, the supernatant is collected and added to the RAW264.7 cell culture medium; (5) The inhibition control group (RAW + NC), where granulosa cells are transfected with non-targeting siRNA, and after 24 h, the supernatant is collected and added to the RAW264.7 cell culture medium. The granulosa cell supernatants added to the RAW264.7 cell culture medium in the above groups account for 5% of the total volume.

Western blot

Cell transfection was performed in 6-well plates, and after culturing for 24 h, 250 µL of lysis buffer was added to lyse the cells. After collecting the supernatant from the lysate, loading buffer was added, and the samples were boiled at 100 °C for 10 min to denature the proteins. Based on the protein concentration, an appropriate amount of sample was loaded and electrophoresed at 160 V for 45 min. Following electrophoresis, the proteins were transferred to a membrane at 0.4 mA for 55 min. After transfer, the membrane was blocked with BSA, and then incubated with the primary antibody overnight. The following day, the membrane was incubated with the corresponding secondary antibody for 2 h. Finally, the membrane was exposed to ECL chemiluminescence reagent for a few seconds, and protein bands were visualized using a TANON exposure system. Protein band intensities were analyzed using ImageJ software. The primary antibodies used for western blotting were as follows: anti-Dihydropyridine Receptor α2 Subunit mouse monoclonal antibody (D219; Merck, USA), anti-phospho-CaMKII-T286 rabbit monoclonal antibody (AP1386; ABclonal, China), anti-CaMKII Gamma rabbit polyclonal antibody (12666-2-AP; ProteinTech, China), anti-phospho-STAT3 (Tyr705) rabbit monoclonal antibody (9145; Cell Signaling Technology, USA), anti-STAT3 rabbit monoclonal antibody (12640; Cell Signaling Technology, USA), anti-NF-κB p65 rabbit monoclonal antibody (8242; Cell Signaling Technology, USA), and anti-phospho-NF-κB p65 (Ser536) rabbit monoclonal antibody (3033; Cell Signaling Technology, USA).

Cell cycle detection

The cells were seeded in a 6-well plate and treated when they reached 60% confluence, according to the experimental protocol. After 48 h of further incubation, cell cycle analysis was performed using the Elabscience Cell Cycle Assay Kit (Red Fluorescence). Following the incubation, the samples were immediately analyzed using the CYTOFLEX flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter).

CCK-8

Well-grown cells were seeded in a 96-well plate and allowed to adhere and grow stably. After treatment according to the experimental protocol, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well at 24, 48, and 72 h, respectively. Following a 1-hour incubation, the absorbance of the culture medium at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader to assess cell proliferation.

EdU

The experiment was conducted according to the Cell-Light EdU Apollo567 In Vitro Kit operation manual provided by RIBOBIO. Finally, the ratio of EdU-positive cells to Hoechst-stained cells was calculated using ImageJ software to determine the cell proliferation rate.

Flow cytometry sorting

Ovaries from mice treated with different drugs were collected and subjected to mechanical disruption using the handle of a 1 mL syringe to gently press and grind the ovaries. The resulting ovarian tissue cell suspension was then filtered through a 70 μm cell strainer to prepare a single-cell suspension, which was transferred into a flow cytometry tube. The cells were sequentially stained with Live/Dead dye, followed by blocking and incubation with antibodies (CD11b for myeloid cells, CD45 for immune cells, CD206 for M2 macrophages, and CD86 for M1 macrophages). After staining, the samples were analyzed using an LSRFortessa™ X-20 flow cytometer, and the data were processed using FlowJo V10.8.1 software.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data in this study are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), with each experiment conducted in at least three independent replicates. Data were analyzed and plotted using GraphPad Prism 9 software, with statistical analysis performed using t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) to assess group differences. In the figures, “*” indicates P < 0.05, “**” indicates P < 0.01, and “ns” indicates P ≥ 0.05. P < 0.05 represents statistically significant differences, while P ≥ 0.05 indicates no significant difference.
