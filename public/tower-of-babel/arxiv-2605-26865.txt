A palindromicity criterion for the h h -polynomials of bipartite edge rings
Yuta Hatasa

Abstract
. We study a symmetry problem for the h h -polynomials of edge rings of bipartite graphs. Let G G be a bipartite graph and write h ⁡ ( 𝕜 ⁡ [ G ] , t ) = h 0 + h 1 ​ t + ⋯ + h s ​ t s h(\Bbbk[G];t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s} . We prove that if 𝕜 ⁡ [ G ] \Bbbk[G] is pseudo-Gorenstein and h 1 = h s − 1 h_{1}=h_{s-1} , then 𝕜 ⁡ [ G ] \Bbbk[G] is Gorenstein. Equivalently, under these assumptions the h h -polynomial of 𝕜 ⁡ [ G ] \Bbbk[G] is palindromic. The proof treats the 2 2 -connected case first by translating the numerical condition h 1 = h s − 1 h_{1}=h_{s-1} into a tight-separation condition for non-edges, and then passes to arbitrary bipartite graphs using the block decomposition. We also construct a blockwise minimal Gorenstein closure, obtained by adjoining all non-edges not separated by tight acceptable sets, and show that this construction preserves the next-to-leading coefficient of the h h -polynomial.

1.
Introduction

Let
R=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}R_{k}
be a Cohen–Macaulay homogeneous domain of dimension
d
over a field
R_{0}=\Bbbk
.
The Hilbert series of
R
has the form

\sum_{k\geq 0}(\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{k})t^{k}=\frac{h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}}{(1-t)^{d}},\quad h_{s}\neq 0.

The numerator
h(R;t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}
is called the
h
-polynomial
of
R
.
The ring
R
is called
pseudo-Gorenstein
if
h_{s}=1
.
When
R
is a Cohen–Macaulay homogeneous domain, Stanley’s theorem states that
R
is Gorenstein if and only if the
h
-vector is symmetric, that is,
h_{i}=h_{s-i}
for all
i

[
5
]
.

We study this question for edge rings of bipartite graphs.
For a finite simple graph
G
on the vertex set
V(G)=[d]
, let
E(G)
denote its edge set.
The
edge ring
of
G
is

\Bbbk[G]=\Bbbk[t_{i}t_{j}:\{i,j\}\in E(G)]\subset\Bbbk[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}].

The edge rings of bipartite graphs are normal, hence Cohen–Macaulay, by the theorem of Ohsugi–Hibi and Simis–Vasconcelos–Villarreal
[
2
,
4
]
.

For bipartite graphs, Hatasa, Kowaki, and Matsushita proved that
\Bbbk[G]
is pseudo-Gorenstein if and only if every block of
G
is matching-covered
[
1
]
.
In particular, if
G
is
2
-connected and bipartite, the pseudo-Gorenstein property is equivalent to
G
being matching-covered.
The natural next question is whether a small amount of additional symmetry of the
h
-vector already forces full symmetry.
Our main result gives an affirmative answer for bipartite graphs.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph and write

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}.

If
h_{s}=1
and
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
, then
\Bbbk[G]
is Gorenstein.
In particular,
h(\Bbbk[G];t)
is palindromic.

The same method also gives a blockwise Gorenstein closure for pseudo-Gorenstein bipartite edge rings.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph and write

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}.

Suppose that
h_{s}=1
.
For each block
B
of
G
, choose a bipartition
X_{B}\sqcup Y_{B}
.
Let
\Fill(B)
be the set of non-edges
\{x,y\}
of
B
, with
x\in X_{B}
and
y\in Y_{B}
, that are not separated by any tight acceptable set
T\subset X_{B}
, in the sense that no such
T
satisfies

x\in T,\qquad y\notin N_{B}(T).

For each block
B
, let
\widehat{B}
be the graph on the same vertex set as
B
with

E(\widehat{B})=E(B)\cup\Fill(B).

Let
\widehat{G}
be the graph obtained from
G
by replacing each block
B
by
\widehat{B}
.
Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

\Bbbk[\widehat{G}]
is Gorenstein.

(ii)

Writing

h(\Bbbk[\widehat{G}];t)=\widehat{h}_{0}+\widehat{h}_{1}t+\cdots+\widehat{h}_{\widehat{s}}t^{\widehat{s}},

one has
\widehat{s}=s
and
\widehat{h}_{s-1}=h_{s-1}
.

(iii)

The construction is blockwise minimal: if
B
is a block of
G
with at least one edge and
K_{B}
is a bipartite graph with bipartition
X_{B}\sqcup Y_{B}
such that
E(B)\subset E(K_{B})
and
\Bbbk[K_{B}]
is Gorenstein, then

E(\widehat{B})\subset E(K_{B}).

The proof first handles the
2
-connected case.
In that case, we express the difference
h_{s-1}-h_{1}
as the number of specific interior lattice points of the edge polytope arising from non-edges of
G
.
Thus
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
is equivalent to the assertion that every non-edge is separated by a tight acceptable set on the
X
-side, and symmetrically on the
Y
-side.
We then prove a purely graph-theoretic statement: in a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph, this
X
-side separation condition is equivalent to the tightness of every acceptable set.
By the Ohsugi–Hibi criterion, this is exactly the Gorenstein condition.
Finally, for a bipartite graph, the edge ring decomposes as a tensor product over its blocks, so its
h
-polynomial is the product of the
h
-polynomials of the blocks.
This reduces the general bipartite case to the
2
-connected one.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Edge polytopes and facets

Throughout this paper, all graphs are finite and simple.
Let
G
be a graph on the vertex set
V(G)=[d]
.
For an edge
e=\{i,j\}
, set
\rho(e)=\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The
edge polytope
of
G
is

P_{G}=\conv\{\rho(e):e\in E(G)\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

If
G
is connected and bipartite, then
\dim P_{G}=d-2
, and
\dim\Bbbk[G]=d-1
.
Moreover, since
\Bbbk[G]
is normal, the Hilbert function of
\Bbbk[G]
is the Ehrhart function of
P_{G}
:

\dim_{\Bbbk}\Bbbk[G]_{k}=\lvert kP_{G}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\rvert.

Let
G
be bipartite with bipartition
V(G)=X\sqcup Y
.
For
T\subset X
, set

N_{G}(T)=\left\{y\in Y:\{x,y\}\in E(G)\text{ for some }x\in T\right\},\qquad\delta_{G}(T)=\lvert N_{G}(T)\rvert-\lvert T\rvert.

We use the same notation for subsets of
Y
.
Following Ohsugi–Hibi
[
2
]
, a non-empty set
T\subset X
is called
acceptable
if the bipartite graph
B(T)
induced by the edges between
T
and
N_{G}(T)
is connected and the induced graph on

(X\setminus T)\sqcup(Y\setminus N_{G}(T))

is connected with at least one edge.
Acceptable subsets of
Y
are defined in the same way.
In this paper, we call a subset
T

tight
if
\delta_{G}(T)=1
.

We also use the terminology of Ohsugi–Hibi
[
2
]
: a vertex
v
of a connected graph is called
ordinary
if
G\setminus v
, the induced graph obtained by deleting
v
, is connected.
With this terminology, a connected graph is called
2
-connected
if all of its vertices are ordinary.
The facet description of Ohsugi–Hibi states that, for a connected bipartite graph, the coordinate facets of
P_{G}
are precisely the facets supported by

z_{v}=0

for ordinary vertices
v
.
The remaining facets are supported by the hyperplanes

\sum_{y\in N_{G}(T)}z_{y}-\sum_{x\in T}z_{x}=0

for acceptable sets
T\subset X
; equivalently, one may use the symmetric formulation with acceptable subsets of
Y
.
In particular, if
G
is
2
-connected, then every vertex is ordinary, so every coordinate hyperplane
z_{v}=0
supports a facet of
P_{G}
.

2.2.
Blocks

A
block
of
G
is a maximal
2
-connected subgraph.

We use the following decomposition of bipartite edge rings along blocks.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
6
, Proposition 10.1.48]
)
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph, and let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
be the blocks of
G
.
Then

\Bbbk[G]\cong\Bbbk[B_{1}]\otimes_{\Bbbk}\cdots\otimes_{\Bbbk}\Bbbk[B_{m}]

as standard graded
\Bbbk
-algebras.
In particular,

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}h(\Bbbk[B_{i}];t).

2.3.
Matching-covered bipartite graphs

A connected graph
G
is
matching-covered
if every edge of
G
is contained in a perfect matching.
The proof of the main theorem uses the following elementary strict Hall property for the
2
-connected matching-covered blocks that occur there.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
.
Then
\lvert X\rvert=\lvert Y\rvert
and, for every non-empty proper subset
A\subsetneq X
,

\lvert N_{G}(A)\rvert\geq\lvert A\rvert+1.

The analogous statement holds for non-empty proper subsets of
Y
.

Proof.

The equality
\lvert X\rvert=\lvert Y\rvert
follows from the existence of a perfect matching.
Hall’s theorem gives
\lvert N_{G}(A)\rvert\geq\lvert A\rvert
.
Suppose that
\lvert N_{G}(A)\rvert=\lvert A\rvert
for some non-empty proper subset
A\subsetneq X
.
Then every perfect matching matches all vertices of
A
with the vertices of
N_{G}(A)
.
Hence no edge from
X\setminus A
to
N_{G}(A)
can be contained in a perfect matching.
Since
G
is connected and
A
is a non-empty proper subset, such an edge exists, contradicting the matching-covered property.
∎

3.
The coefficient condition

We first record a general formula for the linear coefficient of the
h
-polynomial.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph.
Put
d=\lvert V(G)\rvert
and write

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}.

Then

h_{1}=\lvert E(G)\rvert-d+1.

Proof.

Since
G
is connected and bipartite,
\dim\Bbbk[G]=d-1
.
The lattice points of
P_{G}
are precisely the points
\rho(e)
for
e\in E(G)
, so

\lvert P_{G}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\rvert=\lvert E(G)\rvert.

Comparing the coefficient of degree one in the Ehrhart series

\sum_{k\geq 0}\lvert kP_{G}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\rvert t^{k}=\frac{h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}}{(1-t)^{d-1}}

gives
\lvert E(G)\rvert=(d-1)+h_{1}
.
∎

For the rest of this section, let
G
be a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
.
By
Section

2.3
,
\lvert X\rvert=\lvert Y\rvert
; write
\lvert X\rvert=\lvert Y\rvert=n
and

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}.

Then
\dim P_{G}=2n-2
,
\dim\Bbbk[G]=2n-1
, and the vector

\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}

is the unique lattice point in
\int(nP_{G})
.
In particular,
h_{s}=1
and
s=n-1
.
The remaining results in this section use these assumptions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
, and use the notation above.
Then

h_{s-1}=\lvert\int((n+1)P_{G})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\rvert-2n+1.

Proof.

Ehrhart reciprocity gives

\sum_{k\geq 1}\lvert\int(kP_{G})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\rvert t^{k}=\frac{t^{2n-1}h(\Bbbk[G];t^{-1})}{(1-t)^{2n-1}}.

Taking the coefficient of
t^{n+1}
and using
s=n-1
and
h_{s}=1
, we obtain

\lvert\int((n+1)P_{G})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\rvert=h_{s-1}+(2n-1)h_{s}=h_{s-1}+2n-1.\qed

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
, and use the notation above.
Let

\mathcal{N}_{G}=\left\{(x,y)\in X\times Y:\{x,y\}\notin E(G)\right\}

be the set of non-edges across the bipartition.
Then

h_{s-1}-h_{1}=\lvert\left\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{N}_{G}:\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y}\in\int((n+1)P_{G})\right\}\rvert.

Proof.

Every lattice point of
(n+1)P_{G}
has non-negative integral coordinates, and the sums of its
X
-coordinates and
Y
-coordinates are both
n+1
.
If such a point is in the interior, then all its coordinates are positive.
Indeed, for each vertex
v
, the coordinate hyperplane
z_{v}=0
supports a facet of
P_{G}
by
Section

2.1
, while
P_{G}
is contained in the half-space
z_{v}\geq 0
.
Thus an interior point cannot lie on any coordinate hyperplane.
Hence every lattice point of
\int((n+1)P_{G})
has the form

\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y}

for some
x\in X
and
y\in Y
.
If
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
, then

\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y}=\mathbf{1}+\rho(\{x,y\})

lies in
\int((n+1)P_{G})
, since
\mathbf{1}\in\int(nP_{G})
.
Therefore,

\lvert\int((n+1)P_{G})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\rvert=\lvert E(G)\rvert+\lvert\left\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{N}_{G}:\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y}\in\int((n+1)P_{G})\right\}\rvert.

The assertion follows from
Sections

3
and
3
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
, and use the notation above.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

h_{1}=h_{s-1}
;

(ii)

for every non-edge
(x,y)\in\mathcal{N}_{G}
, there exists a tight acceptable set
T\subset X
such that

x\in T,\quad y\notin N_{G}(T);

(iii)

for every non-edge
(x,y)\in\mathcal{N}_{G}
, there exists a tight acceptable set
S\subset Y
such that

y\in S,\quad x\notin N_{G}(S).

Proof.

We first prove that (i) implies (ii).
By
Section

3
, the equality
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
means precisely that none of the points
\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y}
corresponding to non-edges lies in
\int((n+1)P_{G})
.
Fix a non-edge
(x,y)\in\mathcal{N}_{G}
and put

p=\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y}.

The point
p
belongs to the affine span of
(n+1)P_{G}
, since the sum of its
X
-coordinates and the sum of its
Y
-coordinates are both
n+1
.
Moreover,
p
has positive coordinates, so it satisfies all strict inequalities defined by the coordinate facets.
Therefore, if
p
is not in
\int((n+1)P_{G})
, then, by the Ohsugi–Hibi facet description in the
X
-side form,
p
must fail the strict inequality corresponding to an acceptable set
T\subset X
.
We evaluate the linear form defining this facet at
p
.
The vector
\mathbf{1}
contributes
\lvert N_{G}(T)\rvert-\lvert T\rvert=\delta_{G}(T)
.
Since
x\in X
and
y\in Y
, the additional vector
\mathbf{e}_{y}
contributes
1
exactly when
y\in N_{G}(T)
, while the additional vector
\mathbf{e}_{x}
is subtracted and contributes
-1
exactly when
x\in T
.
Equivalently, if

\epsilon_{y}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }y\in N_{G}(T),\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\epsilon_{x}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in T,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

then the contribution of
\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y}
is
\epsilon_{y}-\epsilon_{x}
.
Hence

\sum_{v\in N_{G}(T)}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y})_{v}-\sum_{u\in T}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y})_{u}=\delta_{G}(T)+\epsilon_{y}-\epsilon_{x}.

By
Section

2.3
,
\delta_{G}(T)\geq 1
.
Hence the above quantity is non-positive if and only if

\delta_{G}(T)=1,\qquad x\in T,\qquad y\notin N_{G}(T).

Thus (ii) holds.

Conversely, assume (ii).
For each non-edge
(x,y)\in\mathcal{N}_{G}
, choose a tight acceptable set
T\subset X
such that
x\in T
and
y\notin N_{G}(T)
.
The same computation gives

\sum_{v\in N_{G}(T)}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y})_{v}-\sum_{u\in T}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y})_{u}=0.

Hence
\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{e}_{x}+\mathbf{e}_{y}
lies on the corresponding acceptable facet and is not in
\int((n+1)P_{G})
.
Therefore the set counted in
Section

3
is empty, and
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
.
This proves the equivalence of (i) and (ii).
The same argument, using the
Y
-side form of the Ohsugi–Hibi facet description, gives the direct equivalence of (i) and (iii).

It remains to relate (ii) and (iii) directly.
Assume (ii).
Let
(x,y)\in\mathcal{N}_{G}
, and choose
T\subset X
as in (ii).
Put
S=Y\setminus N_{G}(T)
.
Then
y\in S
.
Since
T
is acceptable, the graph on
(X\setminus T)\sqcup S
is connected, and hence
N_{G}(S)=X\setminus T
.
Thus
x\notin N_{G}(S)
.
Moreover, since
T
is tight,

\lvert N_{G}(S)\rvert=\lvert X\setminus T\rvert=n-\lvert T\rvert=n-\lvert N_{G}(T)\rvert+1=\lvert S\rvert+1.

Hence
S
is tight.
The two connected graphs in the acceptable condition are interchanged, so
S
is acceptable.
The implication from (iii) to (ii) is symmetric.
∎

4.
A graph-theoretic criterion

In this section we prove the graph-theoretic implication needed in the
2
-connected case of the main theorem.
The key point is that, in the graph-theoretic setting, the
X
-side tight acceptable separation condition in
Section

3
is equivalent to the tightness of every acceptable set.
Throughout this section, the statements are formulated for
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graphs, the situation supplied by the pseudo-Gorenstein criterion in the
2
-connected case.
We first record a simple uncrossing lemma for tight subsets.

Lemma 4.1

(Uncrossing)
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
.
Suppose that
U,V\subset X
are tight,
U\cup V\subsetneq X
, and

N_{G}(U)\cap N_{G}(V)\neq\emptyset.

Then
U\cup V
is tight.
The same assertion holds after exchanging
X
and
Y
.

Proof.

Since
U
and
V
are tight, we have
\delta_{G}(U)=\delta_{G}(V)=1
.
Since
N_{G}(U\cup V)=N_{G}(U)\cup N_{G}(V)
, inclusion–exclusion gives

\delta_{G}(U\cup V)=\delta_{G}(U)+\delta_{G}(V)-\left(\lvert N_{G}(U)\cap N_{G}(V)\rvert-\lvert U\cap V\rvert\right).

If
U\cap V=\emptyset
, then the hypothesis gives

\lvert N_{G}(U)\cap N_{G}(V)\rvert-\lvert U\cap V\rvert\geq 1.

If
U\cap V\neq\emptyset
, then
N_{G}(U\cap V)\subset N_{G}(U)\cap N_{G}(V)
, and
Section

2.3
gives

\lvert N_{G}(U)\cap N_{G}(V)\rvert-\lvert U\cap V\rvert\geq\lvert N_{G}(U\cap V)\rvert-\lvert U\cap V\rvert\geq 1.

Therefore
\delta_{G}(U\cup V)\leq 1
.
On the other hand,
U\cup V
is a proper subset of
X
, and
Section

2.3
gives
\delta_{G}(U\cup V)\geq 1
.
Thus
\delta_{G}(U\cup V)=1
, as desired.
The proof of the symmetric assertion is identical.
∎

The next lemma isolates the local construction that produces tight subsets inside an acceptable set.

Lemma 4.2

(Internal tight cover)
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
.
Assume that for every non-edge
(x,y)\in X\times Y
there exists a tight subset
S\subset Y
such that

y\in S,\qquad x\notin N_{G}(S).

If
A\subset X
is an acceptable set, then, for every
x\in A
, there exists a tight subset
R_{x}\subset A
of
X
such that
x\in R_{x}
.

Proof.

Put

D=N_{G}(A),\qquad B=X\setminus A,\qquad C=Y\setminus D.

Since
A
is acceptable, the induced graphs on
A\sqcup D
and
B\sqcup C
are connected, and the latter graph has at least one edge.
There is no edge between
A
and
C
.
Since the graph on
B\sqcup C
is connected, this gives
N_{G}(C)=B
.
Thus
C
is an acceptable subset of
Y
.

Put

r=\delta_{G}(C)=\lvert B\rvert-\lvert C\rvert.

Since
\lvert X\rvert=\lvert Y\rvert
, we have

\lvert D\rvert=\lvert Y\rvert-\lvert C\rvert=\lvert X\rvert-\lvert B\rvert+r=\lvert A\rvert+r.

For each
x\in A
, we construct such a set
R_{x}
as follows.
For every
y\in C
, the pair
(x,y)
is a non-edge.
Hence there is a tight set
S_{y}\subset Y
such that
y\in S_{y}
and
x\notin N_{G}(S_{y})
.

The graph on
B\sqcup C
is connected.
Moreover, all intermediate unions avoid
x
in their neighborhoods, and hence are proper subsets of
Y
.
Repeated use of the symmetric form of
Section

4
yields a tight set
U_{x}\subset Y
such that

C\subset U_{x},\qquad x\notin N_{G}(U_{x}).

Put

P_{x}=U_{x}\setminus C\subset D,\qquad Q_{x}=N_{G}(P_{x})\cap A.

Then
N_{G}(U_{x})=B\sqcup Q_{x}
, and the tightness of
U_{x}
gives

1=\lvert B\rvert+\lvert Q_{x}\rvert-\lvert C\rvert-\lvert P_{x}\rvert.

Hence

\lvert P_{x}\rvert-\lvert Q_{x}\rvert=r-1.

Let
R_{x}=A\setminus Q_{x}
.
Since
x\notin N_{G}(U_{x})
, we have
x\in R_{x}
.
Also, no vertex of
C
is adjacent to
R_{x}\subset A
, and no vertex of
P_{x}
is adjacent to
R_{x}
by the definition of
Q_{x}
.
Therefore
N_{G}(R_{x})\subset D\setminus P_{x}
.
Moreover,

\lvert D\setminus P_{x}\rvert=\lvert D\rvert-\lvert P_{x}\rvert=\lvert A\rvert+r-\left(\lvert Q_{x}\rvert+r-1\right)=\lvert R_{x}\rvert+1.

Since
x\in R_{x}\subset A
and
A
is acceptable,
R_{x}
is a non-empty proper subset of
X
.
Hence
Section

2.3
gives

\lvert N_{G}(R_{x})\rvert\geq\lvert R_{x}\rvert+1.

Thus

N_{G}(R_{x})=D\setminus P_{x},\qquad\lvert N_{G}(R_{x})\rvert=\lvert R_{x}\rvert+1.

Hence
R_{x}
is tight.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

for every non-edge
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, there exists a tight acceptable set
T\subset X
such that

x\in T,\quad y\notin N_{G}(T);

(ii)

every acceptable set is tight.

Proof.

Assume (i).
By the equivalence of the
X
-side and
Y
-side separation conditions in
Section

3
, for every non-edge
(x,y)\in X\times Y
there exists a tight acceptable set
S\subset Y
such that
y\in S
and
x\notin N_{G}(S)
.
In particular, the hypothesis of
Section

4
is satisfied.

Let
A\subset X
be an acceptable set.
By
Section

4
, every
x\in A
belongs to a tight subset
R_{x}\subset A
.
Since the graph induced by the edges between
A
and
N_{G}(A)
is connected, the sets
R_{x}
cover
A
, and all intermediate unions remain contained in the proper subset
A\subsetneq X
, repeated use of
Section

4
shows that their union, namely
A
, is tight.
Hence every acceptable set in
X
is tight.

For acceptable subsets of
Y
, the symmetric argument uses the original
X
-side condition in (i) and gives the same conclusion.
Thus (ii) holds.

Conversely, assume (ii), and let
(x,y)\in X\times Y
be a non-edge.
Since
G
is
2
-connected,
G\setminus y
is connected.
Let
H
be the induced subgraph on

(X\setminus N_{G}(y))\sqcup(Y\setminus\left\{y\right\}),

and let
T
be the set of vertices in
X
lying in the connected component of
H
that contains
x
.
Then
x\in T
and
y\notin N_{G}(T)
.
Moreover, the graph induced by the edges between
T
and
N_{G}(T)
is connected by construction.
Since
G\setminus y
is connected, every other connected component of
H
is adjacent to a vertex of
N_{G}(y)
.
It follows that the graph induced by the edges between
X\setminus T
and
Y\setminus N_{G}(T)
is connected; it contains the edges from
y
to
N_{G}(y)
and all other components of
H
attach to these vertices.
This graph has at least one edge.
Hence
T
is acceptable.
By (ii), the set
T
is tight, proving (i).
∎

5.
Proof of the main theorem

We now combine the coefficient interpretation from
Section

3
with the graph-theoretic criterion from
Section

4
.
This completes the passage from the numerical condition
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
to the Ohsugi–Hibi Gorenstein criterion in the
2
-connected case.
The block decomposition then gives the stated result for arbitrary bipartite graphs.

We use the following form of the Ohsugi–Hibi criterion, rewritten in the terminology of acceptable sets.
In their notation the statement is formulated for the toric ring of
P_{G}
, which is naturally isomorphic to our edge ring
\Bbbk[G]
as a standard graded algebra.

Theorem 5.1

(Ohsugi–Hibi Gorenstein Criterion
[
3
, Theorem 2.1(a
′
)]
)
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
.
Then
\Bbbk[G]
is Gorenstein if and only if
G
has a perfect matching and

\lvert N_{G}(T)\rvert=\lvert T\rvert+1

for every acceptable set
T\subset X
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected bipartite graph and write

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}.

If
h_{s}=1
and
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
, then
\Bbbk[G]
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

Since
G
is
2
-connected and bipartite, the assumption
h_{s}=1
implies that
G
is matching-covered by the pseudo-Gorenstein criterion for bipartite edge rings
[
1
]
.
In this setting,
Sections

3
and
4
show that
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
is equivalent to the tightness of every acceptable set.
Hence every acceptable set is tight.
Since a matching-covered graph has a perfect matching,
5.1
implies that
\Bbbk[G]
is Gorenstein.
∎

Proof of
1.1
.

Isolated vertices do not appear in the generators of
\Bbbk[G]
, so they may be ignored.
If
G
has no edges, then
\Bbbk[G]=\Bbbk
and the assertion is clear.
Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
be the blocks of
G
, and write

h(\Bbbk[B_{i}];t)=h_{i,0}+h_{i,1}t+\cdots+h_{i,s_{i}}t^{s_{i}}.

By
Section

2.2
,

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}h(\Bbbk[B_{i}];t).

Hence

h_{s}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}h_{i,s_{i}}.

Since
h_{s}=1
and all coefficients are non-negative integers, we have
h_{i,s_{i}}=1
for every
i
.
If
s=0
, then
h(\Bbbk[G];t)=1
, so
\Bbbk[G]
is Gorenstein by Stanley’s theorem
[
5
]
.
Thus we may assume
s>0
.

Blocks with
s_{i}=0
have
h(\Bbbk[B_{i}];t)=1
, and hence their edge rings are Gorenstein by Stanley’s theorem.
For any block with
s_{i}>0
, the graph
B_{i}
is
2
-connected and bipartite, and
\Bbbk[B_{i}]
is pseudo-Gorenstein.
By the pseudo-Gorenstein criterion for bipartite edge rings
[
1
]
, this block is matching-covered.
Applying
Section

3
to
B_{i}
gives

h_{i,s_{i}-1}-h_{i,1}\geq 0.

On the other hand, comparing the coefficients of
t
and of the next-to-leading term in the product formula gives

h_{1}=\sum_{s_{i}>0}h_{i,1},\qquad h_{s-1}=\sum_{s_{i}>0}h_{i,s_{i}-1}.

Since
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
, all the non-negative differences

h_{i,s_{i}-1}-h_{i,1}

are zero.
Thus, for each block with
s_{i}>0
,
Section

5
shows that
\Bbbk[B_{i}]
is Gorenstein.

Therefore every block has a palindromic
h
-polynomial.
Their product
h(\Bbbk[G];t)
is palindromic, and Stanley’s theorem again implies that
\Bbbk[G]
is Gorenstein.
The palindromicity assertion follows at the same time.
∎

The same argument gives a Gorenstein closure obtained by adjoining all non-edges that are not separated by tight acceptable sets.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected matching-covered bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
.
Write

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}.

Let
\Fill(G)
be the set of non-edges
\{x,y\}
, with
x\in X
and
y\in Y
, that are not separated by any tight acceptable set
T\subset X
, in the sense that no such
T
satisfies

x\in T,\qquad y\notin N_{G}(T).

Let
\widehat{G}
be the bipartite graph on the same bipartition with

E(\widehat{G})=E(G)\cup\Fill(G).

Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

\widehat{G}
is again
2
-connected and matching-covered, and
\Bbbk[\widehat{G}]
is Gorenstein.

(ii)

Writing

h(\Bbbk[\widehat{G}];t)=\widehat{h}_{0}+\widehat{h}_{1}t+\cdots+\widehat{h}_{\widehat{s}}t^{\widehat{s}},

one has
\widehat{s}=s
and
\widehat{h}_{s-1}=h_{s-1}
.

(iii)

\widehat{G}
is minimal among Gorenstein bipartite supergraphs with this bipartition: if
K
is a bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
such that
E(G)\subset E(K)
and
\Bbbk[K]
is Gorenstein, then

E(\widehat{G})\subset E(K).

Proof.

We first prove (i).
Adding edges preserves
2
-connectedness, so
\widehat{G}
is
2
-connected.
By
Section

2.3
,
\lvert X\rvert=\lvert Y\rvert
.
We show that
\widehat{G}
is matching-covered.
Every edge of
G
is contained in a perfect matching of
G
, and hence also in a perfect matching of
\widehat{G}
.
Let
\{x,y\}\in\Fill(G)
be a new edge of
\widehat{G}
.
For every non-empty subset
A\subset X\setminus\left\{x\right\}
,
Section

2.3
gives

\lvert N_{G}(A)\rvert\geq\lvert A\rvert+1,

and therefore

\lvert N_{G}(A)\setminus\left\{y\right\}\rvert\geq\lvert A\rvert.

Since
\lvert X\setminus\left\{x\right\}\rvert=\lvert Y\setminus\left\{y\right\}\rvert
, Hall’s theorem gives a perfect matching of the induced graph
G\setminus\left\{x,y\right\}
.
Together with the edge
\{x,y\}
, this gives a perfect matching of
\widehat{G}
containing
\{x,y\}
.

We next verify the
X
-side condition in
Section

4
for
\widehat{G}
.
Let
(x,y)
be a non-edge of
\widehat{G}
.
Then
\{x,y\}\notin\Fill(G)
, so there exists a tight acceptable set
T\subset X
for
G
such that

x\in T,\qquad y\notin N_{G}(T).

We claim that no added edge of
\widehat{G}
joins a vertex of
T
to a vertex of
Y\setminus N_{G}(T)
.
Indeed, if
a\in T
and
b\notin N_{G}(T)
, then the same set
T
separates the non-edge
\{a,b\}
, so
\{a,b\}\notin\Fill(G)
.
It follows that

N_{\widehat{G}}(T)=N_{G}(T).

Consequently,
T
remains tight for
\widehat{G}
.
The two connected graphs in the acceptable condition for
T
can only gain edges when passing from
G
to
\widehat{G}
, so
T
is also acceptable for
\widehat{G}
.

Thus every non-edge of
\widehat{G}
is separated by a tight acceptable subset of
X
.
By
Section

4
, every acceptable set of
\widehat{G}
is tight.
Since
\widehat{G}
has a perfect matching,
5.1
implies that
\Bbbk[\widehat{G}]
is Gorenstein.

We next prove (ii).
The argument in the proof of
Section

3
identifies
\Fill(G)
with the set counted in
Section

3
, so

\lvert\Fill(G)\rvert=h_{s-1}-h_{1}.

By
Section

3
, the first coefficient of
h(\Bbbk[\widehat{G}];t)
is

\widehat{h}_{1}=\lvert E(\widehat{G})\rvert-\lvert V(G)\rvert+1=h_{1}+\lvert\Fill(G)\rvert=h_{s-1}.

Since
\widehat{G}
is again
2
-connected and matching-covered with the same bipartition as
G
, its
h
-polynomial has degree
\widehat{s}=s
.
Since
\Bbbk[\widehat{G}]
is Gorenstein, its
h
-polynomial is palindromic; hence
\widehat{h}_{s}=1
and
\widehat{h}_{s-1}=\widehat{h}_{1}=h_{s-1}
.

Finally, we prove (iii).
Let
K
be a bipartite graph with bipartition
X\sqcup Y
such that
E(G)\subset E(K)
and
\Bbbk[K]
is Gorenstein.
As
G
is
2
-connected, the supergraph
K
is also
2
-connected.
Since
\Bbbk[K]
is Gorenstein, it is pseudo-Gorenstein; by the pseudo-Gorenstein criterion for bipartite edge rings
[
1
]
,
K
is matching-covered.
The
h
-polynomial of
\Bbbk[K]
is palindromic, so its first and next-to-leading coefficients are equal.
Let
\{x,y\}
be a non-edge of
K
, with
x\in X
and
y\in Y
.
Then
\{x,y\}
is also a non-edge of
G
.
Applying
Section

3
to the non-edge
(x,y)
of
K
, there exists a tight acceptable set
T\subset X
for
K
such that

x\in T,\qquad y\notin N_{K}(T).

We show that
T
is also a tight acceptable set for
G
.
Since
E(G)\subset E(K)
, we have
N_{G}(T)\subset N_{K}(T)
.
The tightness of
T
in
K
gives
\lvert N_{K}(T)\rvert=\lvert T\rvert+1
, while
Section

2.3
applied to
G
gives

\lvert N_{G}(T)\rvert\geq\lvert T\rvert+1.

Thus
N_{G}(T)=N_{K}(T)
.
In particular,
T
is tight for
G
and
y\notin N_{G}(T)
.

It remains to check acceptability in
G
.
Put
D=N_{G}(T)
.
If the graph induced by the edges of
G
between
T
and
D
had
r
connected components, and if
T_{1},\ldots,T_{r}
were their
X
-parts, then each
T_{i}
would be a non-empty proper subset of
X
.
Hence
Section

2.3
would give

\lvert D\rvert=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lvert N_{G}(T_{i})\rvert\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\lvert T_{i}\rvert+1\right)=\lvert T\rvert+r.

Since
\lvert D\rvert=\lvert T\rvert+1
, we get
r=1
.
Hence the graph induced by the edges between
T
and
D
is connected.

Next put
C=Y\setminus D
.
The set
C
is non-empty because
y\in C
.
Since
T
is non-empty and
G
is connected,
D
is non-empty, so
C
is a proper subset of
Y
.
Moreover, no vertex of
C
is adjacent to a vertex of
T
, so
N_{G}(C)\subset X\setminus T
.
The symmetric form of
Section

2.3
, together with the equality

\lvert X\setminus T\rvert=\lvert X\rvert-\lvert T\rvert=\lvert Y\rvert-\lvert D\rvert+1=\lvert C\rvert+1,

gives
N_{G}(C)=X\setminus T
.
The same component-count argument, now applied to the subset
C\subset Y
, shows that the graph induced by the edges between
X\setminus T
and
C
is connected.
Thus the complementary graph in the acceptable condition is connected and has an edge.
Therefore
T
is acceptable for
G
.

This tight acceptable set satisfies
x\in T
and
y\notin N_{G}(T)
.
Hence
\{x,y\}\notin\Fill(G)
.
Therefore every non-edge of
K
is not in
\Fill(G)
, and so every edge in
\Fill(G)
belongs to
E(K)
.
Thus
E(\widehat{G})\subset E(K)
.
∎

Proof of
1.2
.

Isolated vertices do not affect the edge ring, so we ignore them.
If
G
has no edges, then
\widehat{G}=G
and
\Bbbk[G]=\Bbbk
, so the assertion is clear.
Hence assume that
G
has at least one edge.
Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
be the blocks of
G
with at least one edge.
Write

h(\Bbbk[B_{i}];t)=h_{i,0}+h_{i,1}t+\cdots+h_{i,s_{i}}t^{s_{i}}.

By
Section

2.2
,

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}h(\Bbbk[B_{i}];t).

Since
h_{s}=1
and all coefficients are non-negative integers, the leading coefficient of each factor is one.
Thus every block
B_{i}
is pseudo-Gorenstein.

Let
\widehat{B}_{i}
be the graph obtained from
B_{i}
by adjoining the edges in
\Fill(B_{i})
, and write

h(\Bbbk[\widehat{B}_{i}];t)=\widehat{h}_{i,0}+\widehat{h}_{i,1}t+\cdots+\widehat{h}_{i,\widehat{s}_{i}}t^{\widehat{s}_{i}}.

If
B_{i}
is a single-edge block, then
\Fill(B_{i})=\emptyset
,
\widehat{B}_{i}=B_{i}
,
\Bbbk[B_{i}]
is a polynomial ring, and
\widehat{s}_{i}=s_{i}=0
.
Otherwise,
B_{i}
is
2
-connected and bipartite.
By the pseudo-Gorenstein criterion for bipartite edge rings
[
1
]
, the block
B_{i}
is matching-covered.
Hence
Section

5
applies to it, so
\widehat{B}_{i}
is
2
-connected and matching-covered and
\Bbbk[\widehat{B}_{i}]
is Gorenstein; moreover,
\widehat{h}_{i,\widehat{s}_{i}-1}=h_{i,s_{i}-1}
.
Since
B_{i}
and
\widehat{B}_{i}
have the same bipartition, their
h
-polynomials have the same degree; hence
\widehat{s}_{i}=s_{i}
.

We first prove (i).
All added edges lie inside the original blocks, so the blocks of
\widehat{G}
are precisely these filled blocks together with the unchanged single-edge blocks.
Therefore, by
Section

2.2
,

h(\Bbbk[\widehat{G}];t)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}h(\Bbbk[\widehat{B}_{i}];t).

Each factor is palindromic; hence this product is palindromic, and its degree is
\widehat{s}=\sum_{i}\widehat{s}_{i}=\sum_{i}s_{i}=s
.
In particular,
\widehat{h}_{\widehat{s}}=1
.
Since
\widehat{G}
is bipartite,
\Bbbk[\widehat{G}]
is a Cohen–Macaulay domain.
Stanley’s theorem
[
5
]
implies that
\Bbbk[\widehat{G}]
is Gorenstein.

We next prove (ii).
Comparison of next-to-leading coefficients in the two product formulas gives

\widehat{h}_{s-1}=\sum_{s_{i}>0}\widehat{h}_{i,\widehat{s}_{i}-1}=\sum_{s_{i}>0}h_{i,s_{i}-1}=h_{s-1}.

Finally, we prove (iii).
The asserted blockwise minimality is trivial for single-edge blocks and follows from the minimality assertion in
Section

5
for all other blocks.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The argument shows that possible counterexamples to palindromicity with
h_{s}=1
and
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
cannot occur among bipartite graphs.
Without the bipartite assumption, such counterexamples do exist.
For instance, as observed in
[
1
]
, the Petersen graph has

h(\Bbbk[G];t)=1+5t+15t^{2}+25t^{3}+5t^{4}+t^{5},

so
h_{s}=1
and
h_{1}=h_{s-1}
, but the
h
-polynomial is not palindromic.
