A novel belief-degree-based uncertain Tchebycheff norm DEA model for the case study of risks prioritizing in e-business projects
Authors: Masoomeh Shahbazi, Hamidreza Taghizadeh Rajaei, Kazem Askarifar, Ali Mahmoodirad, Sadegh Niroomand
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractE-business projects may face several risks that are potential to affect the success of such projects. As an important issue for a project manager in this field, the potential risks should be prioritized according to their potential impact on the success of the project. In this study, the potential risks of the e-business sector are to be evaluated and ranked based on the success criteria of such projects. For this aim a complete set of risks compared to the literature is considered and their impact on the project success criteria are determined linguistically by the experts of the field as a case study. To respect the uncertain nature of the evaluations, the linguistic evaluations are converted to belief-degree-based uncertain values. Then for the first time, a data envelopment analysis (DEA) model is used to evaluate and rank such risks. For this aim, the classical Tchebycheff norm DEA model is extended to a belief-degree-based uncertain environment for the first time. An extensive computational study including sensitivity analysis and comparative study is performed by the uncertain Tchebycheff norm model to evaluate and rank the risks of the case study. Based on the obtained results “introducing new technology” and “cultural risk” are the most and least important risks respectively.Keywords: E-business project, Risk evaluation and ranking, Uncertain data envelopment analysis, Tchebycheff norm model, Belief-degree-based uncertaintySubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Risk factors

Introduction

Conceptual background

E-business, or even called electronic business, is a type of business to perform commercial activities and transactions over the internet. It consists of a wide range of online operations such as online retail, electronic payments, digital marketing, etc. Bai et al.1, Dogan2. E-business has transformed classical business methods by enabling companies to easily access global markets, decrease operational costs, and increase customer experiences3. In existence of digital platforms, businesses can simplify processes, increase efficiency, and provide personalized services. E-business also provides challenges like cybersecurity threats, data privacy problems, and the need for continuous technological conformity. Effective and good e-business strategies need robust infrastructure, secure payment methods and systems, and a strong online presence to make trust with customers4. As the digital economy is growing, e-business is becoming an important part of modern commerce, providing competitive and dynamic businesses to adapt to today’s changing market dynamics. By admitting digital transformation, companies can discover new opportunities, optimize and improve operations, and deliver more value to customers in today’s world.

E-business project management includes the planning, execution, and control of projects which are developing or enhancing online business operations5. It needs a strategic method to align digital inventions with business goals, ensuring projects are completed on time, with the pre-determined budget, and to the preferred quality standards. Effective and acceptable e-business project management starts with a clear understanding of the project scope, goals, and stakeholder preferences. This involves determining key deliverables, like website development, e-commerce platform integration, or digital marketing teams. Project managers also should direct challenges to the digital environment such as quickly changing technologies, cybersecurity problems, and the need for continuous user experiences. Collaboration among cross-functional teams such as IT, marketing, and operations, is necessary to be sure that all aspects of the project are coordinated. Agile methodologies are usually used to adjust to evolving needs and market conditions. In addition, robust risk management practices are important to relieve potential disruptions such as technical failures or data violations. Effective e-business project management not only considers innovation and efficiency but also elevates customer satisfaction and competitive advantage of a project.

E-business projects are naturally potential for a variety of risks that may significantly affect their success6,7. One of the initial risks is technical failure, which involves system crashes, software bugs, integration issues, etc. These can disturb and interrupt operations, cause downtime, and decrease revenue. Cybersecurity threats are also a risk as e-business projects usually work with sensitive customer data that can be attractive targets for hackers. Data violations can cause financial losses, legal penalties, and harm to the company’s reputation. Financial risks are also popular which are including budget overruns and unexpected costs, that may pressure resources and extend project timelines. Market risks such as changes in consumer preferences, extreme competition, and economic fluctuations, may decrease demand and profitability. Operational risks like inadequate resource allocation, poor team expertise, and ineffective processes, can negatively affect project execution and results in weak outcomes. In addition, supply chain interruptions that can be because of geopolitical issues, natural disasters, or supplier failures, may affect the availability of main resources. In order to manage these risks, e-business projects need suitable planning, correct risk management strategies, and continuous controlling and monitoring. Project success measures such as project time, project cost, project quality, etc. can be used to address challenges, evaluate risks, and ensure the successful implementation of e-business projects. By introducing potential risks, prioritizing them, and developing possible plans, project managers could improve resilience, optimize resource allocation, and improve the likelihood of achieving project goals.

About the problem of risk evaluation and ranking, multi-criteria decision making (MCDM) and data envelopment analysis (DEA) approaches propose philosophically distinct and complement methodologies. MCDM methods like AHP8 or TOPSIS9, are typically subjective and rely on expert opinion to determine weight values for various risk factors and also produce a comprehensive rank of each entity according to their perceived weakness or threat degree. On the other hand, DEA is a non-parametric and data-driven method that determines the relative efficiency of decision-making units (shown as DMUs) by simultaneously considering multiple inputs and outputs for the DMUs. For the case of risk assessment, where risk factors behave as undesirable inputs or outputs, typical advanced DEA approaches provide some benefits. For example, super-efficiency DEA model10 allows to rank efficient DMUs or all DMUs or cross-efficiency DEA model11 enhances distinction by evaluating each DMU considering the optimal weights of other DMUs and promotes a peer-evaluation structure. In such area, network DEA model12 offers an internal viewpoint to assess the efficiency of sub-processes or stages of a system. The non-radial DEA models like the slack-based measure (SBM) mainly address input excesses and output shortness to provide a more accurate reflection of risk degrees in the cases that improvements are not proportional. To address uncertain nature of real-world data, advanced DEA models may have some critical methodological robustness. For example, fuzzy DEA13 considers possibility theory and allows for the incorporation of imprecise or vague data (e.g. linguistic risk assessments) by considering fuzzy values for inputs and outputs. On the other hand, stochastic DEA models14 apply probability theory to manage randomness of data. In cases with distributional ambiguity, various robust DEA models15 provide worst-case-oriented approaches.

Literature review

There are many studies in the literature focusing on evaluation and prioritizing the risks of e-business projects. Here, some of these studies are mentioned.

Evangelidis et al.16 introduced risk assessment factors and success factors for e-government in a UK establishment. Kleist17, proposed an approach for evaluating e-business information systems projects. Stevens and Fowell6, performed a study to discover some perspectives on e-business software project risks. Currie18, proposed a knowledge-based risk assessment framework in order to evaluate web-enabled application outsourcing projects. Dimitrakos19, proposed the CORAS approach that integrates model-based security risk management into eBusiness systems development. Hunter et al.20 proposed a framework based on risk importance and probability for classification of business-to-business buying decisions for e-business benefits. Scott21, performed a study to measure the dimensions of perceived e-business risks. Lesjak and Vehovar22, introduced some factors that are important to evaluate e-business projects. Khokhar et al.23 performed an evidential reasoning based study for risk assessment of e-commerce projects. Sutton et al.24 performed a study on risk analysis in extended enterprise environments. Fritz and Canavari25, developed a management model for perceived e-business risks in food‐supply networks. They applied e‐trust as prerequisite for supply‐chain system innovation. In this study they identified some critical risk factors in B2B e-commerce relationships. Kefallinos et al.26 proposed an extended risk assessment model for secure E-government projects. Hussain et al.27 introduced a transactional risk-based decision-making system in e-business interactions. Zirakja and Samizadeh28, applied a fuzzy logic based approach for risk analysis in e-commerce. Hussain et al.29 proposed a probability based assessment method for financial risks in e-business associations. Wang et al.30 introduced a method using big data analytics concept to evaluate enterprise credit risk of e-Business platform. Eltweri et al.31 performed a study and discovered the important role of risk management in auditing e-business.

Further than the above-mentioned studies of literature, some recent studies have proposed some methods of risk evaluation for not only e-business projects. Koohathongsumrit and Meethom32, developed an integrated approach involving fuzzy risk assessment model and DEA for route selection in multimodal transportation networks. Ildarabadi and Alamatian33, proposed a new function for evaluating the financial risk of construction projects. Their proposed function is based on the Monte Carlo method. They applied the proposed risk evaluation approach to the construction industry of Iran. Song et al.34 performed a study on risk evaluation of information technology outsourcing projects. They proposed an integrated approach which considers both risk strength and interactions among risk factors. Chen35, performed a study on the risk evaluation of urban wastewater treatment projects. They proposed a study based on an improved fuzzy cognitive map. As an advantage of the proposed method, the information conflict of fuzzy evaluation among experts was solved. The study of Zhang et al.36 also is about risk evaluation of projects. They proposed an approach based on improved failure mode and effect analysis method and group decision-making technique for risk evaluation of utility tunnels construction projects.

In addition to the above-mentioned approaches the data envelopment analysis models also have many applications in real-world problems but still have not been applied for risk evaluation purposes particularly for case of e-business projects seriously. For example, their applications or evaluating rural comprehensive health service centers37, performance evaluation and ranking of personnels in organizations38,39, performance evaluation of hospitals13, selection problems of energy sector40,41, etc. can be of the instances.

Motivation and novelties

Based on the previous explanations in this section, evaluation and prioritizing the potential risks of an e-business project is an important problem and has gained attention in literature. This study presents a novel extension of data envelopment analysis to an uncertain environment in order to evaluate and prioritize the potential risks of e-business projects. Several pre-determined potential risks of e-business projects are determined and their effects on project success factors are assessed by experts in the field. Equivalent chance-constrained model (CCM)

Here the most and least important risks are discussed. According to the ranking by the average score values, the most important risk is “RS1: Introducing new technology”. This risk is a popular risk in most of the e-business projects. This happens when a new competitor arrives at the market with a newer and more complete online platform. In such cases, the project owners should be ready to respond to such risk. This means that the responses such as updating the project, adding some new features, etc. can be useful to respond to the risk of introducing new technology. The second important risk of the ranking of Table 15 is “RS3: Unauthorized access by hackers”. This risk has a high impact on the success of an e-business project. Unauthorized access by hackers has many negative effects on an e-business project such as interrupting the project procedure, losing the information, etc. Therefore, the project owners and managers should be able to secure the project platform and prevent any unauthorized access by hackers. Also, the project should be ready for the event that unauthorized access by hackers happens. The least important risk according to the ranking of Table 15 is “RS8: Cultural risk”. As an e-business is performed on an online platform and the customers with different cultures can access the platform easily without facing the project owners and the business employees, the cultural risk may have a minor effect on the success of the project. Therefore, this risk may need less attention from the project owner and managers.

Conclusion

In this study an important problem from the e-business sector was considered. E-business projects may face several risks that can affect success of such projects. As an important issue for a project manager in this field, the potential risks should be prioritized according to their impact on the success of the project. Based on the prioritized risks, the manager can develop an effective response plan for the potential risks. For this aim a complete set of risks compared to the literature was considered and their impact on the project success criteria were determined by the experts of the field as a case study. As the potential risks of e-business projects and their impact on the success criteria of such sector have high degree of uncertainty, the evaluations obtained by the experts were converted to belief-degree-based uncertain values. Then in order to evaluate and rank the potential risks, for the first time, a DEA model was applied. For this aim, the Tchebycheff norm model for the first time was extended to a belief-degree-based uncertain environment to evaluate and rank the risks of the case study. Based on the obtained results “introducing new technology” and “cultural risk” are the most and least important risks respectively.

In summary, the proposed approach has advantages such as working with belief-degree-based uncertain data which can be very useful for the situations where experts’ evaluations are important. On the other hand, the proposed framework can be extended to fuzzy theory and its various numbers to reflect the uncertainty of experts’ evaluations.
