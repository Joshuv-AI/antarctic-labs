Knowledge, attitude and practice of labor neuraxial analgesia in pregnant women and their partners: a cross-sectional survey in China
Authors: Min Du, Linjia Zhong, Ping Hu, Minli Huang, Shuzhen Wu, Hongfei Dai, Zijing Zhang, Zhenyan Han, Lingling Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLabor neuraxial analgesia (NA) is considered the gold standard for labor pain management worldwide, yet its use remains low in China. This study aimed to evaluate the barriers and facilitators regarding the use of NA from the perspectives of pregnant women and their partners.MethodsA cross-sectional survey using a knowledge-attitude-practice (KAP) questionnaire was conducted in 13 hospitals from April to December 2022. A total of 1,164 pairs of pregnant women and their partners attending prenatal care clinics were recruited using a convenience sampling technique. Data were analyzed using univariate analysis and multivariable linear regression.ResultsBoth pregnant women and their partners exhibited a lack of knowledge about NA, and online information and communicating with others were the primary ways to learn about NA. Multivariable regression indicated education level (B = 1.33, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.94–1.73), gestational age (B = 1.29, 95% CI: 0.96–1.63), hospital level 2 (B = 1.73, 95% CI: 0.73–2.73), level 3 (B = 2.11, 95% CI: 1.25–2.97), and parity (B = 1.32, 95% CI: 0.68–1.97) influenced pregnant women’s knowledge. Similarly, education level (B = 1.04, 95% CI: 0.66–1.42) and gestational age (B = 0.68, 95% CI:0.33–1.03) were positively associated with their partners’ knowledge. For pregnant women, annual income (B = 1.39, 95% CI: 0.72–2.05) and parity (B = -1.43, 95% CI: -2.71– -0.15) affected attitudes, while gestational age (B = 0.67, 95% CI: 0.23–1.11), annual income (B = 1.18, 95% CI: 0.57–1.79) and parity (B = -1.43, 95% CI: -2.61– -0.26) affected in practice. For partners, hospital level and age positively influenced attitudes and practice towards NA. Most concerns regarding NA were attributed to inadequate awareness and misconception about the safety of NA.ConclusionsKnowledge regarding NA was found to be inadequate among both pregnant women and their partners, while annual income, parity, hospital level and age were the influencing factors regarding attitudes and practice. Online hospital-based educational programs incorporating tailored information about NA could potentially improve the decision-making process of NA in China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-026-09514-2.Keywords: Labor neuraxial analgesia; Pregnant women; Partners; Knowledge, attitude and practice

Background

Labor pain is considered the most painful experience in life by a majority of women. Maternal requests for pain relief and rest during labor should be addressed properly [1, 2], and are recognized as a fundamental human right [3]. Neuraxial analgesia (NA) can relieve labor pain effectively [1, 2], increase mother-infant intimacy [4], reduce the rate of intrapartum Cesarean section by maternal request [5], and improve obstetric outcomes [5, 6]. Therefore, NA is widely used and considered the gold standard for labor pain management.

In the United States, more than 65% of pregnant women select epidural analgesia during the labor process, and the popularity has steadily increased over time [7]. In China, the percentage of women using NA increased from 8.4% in 2012 to 16.6% in 2019 [8], but the use of labor analgesia across the entire country remains significantly lower compared to many developed nations [7, 8]. In order to increase the use of epidural analgesia for labor pain management, it is important to investigate the barriers and facilitators influencing its use in labor from the perspectives of pregnant women and their partners.

Previous studies noted that insufficient awareness of labor analgesia and concerns about risks for the mother and child were important reasons for not choosing NA during labor [9–13]. A recent review [14] involving 14,803 pregnant women from 42 studies in multiple countries assessed the factors influencing the request for epidural analgesia reported that the main overall barrier was concern about maternal and fetal safety, and that social support and patient counseling would facilitate its use. Furthermore, some studies also noted that education level, parity, race, partners’ attitude, and economic status were also related to the use of NA [15–18], whereas other studies have observed fewer correlations between these factors and labor analgesia rates [19, 20]. The varying outcomes of these studies could be attributed to the complexity and individual perception of pain. Therefore, assessing the awareness and attitudes of pregnant women and their partners towards epidural analgesia in a certain country or region will facilitate better communication with patients and help them to choose pain relief during labor. However, there are few published studies on this topic in China.

The knowledge-attitude-practice (KAP) survey is a useful tool for systematically evaluating a population’s understanding, beliefs, attitudes, and behaviors concerning a particular health issue, enabling the design of targeted interventions and creation of effective health education [21, 22]. To address the above issue, we designed a KAP questionnaire about epidural analgesia for pregnant women and their partners, to assess their awareness, perceptions, and preferences regarding labor analgesia, as well as the relevant influencing factors.

Methods

Study design and population

Pregnant women and their partners attending an antenatal clinic were recruited to complete a cross-sectional survey. The survey was conducted by face-to-face interviews using a KAP questionnaire. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (approval number: [2022] 02-051-01). In January 2022, a preliminary survey was conducted at the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University, and 54 prenatal pregnant women and their partners completed the survey. Based on the findings of the preliminary survey and feedback from obstetrics and anesthesia experts, modifications were made to the content and sequence of the questions. Subsequently, from April to December 2022, investigators were trained to distribute the formal questionnaire and collected responses from 13 healthcare institutions of different levels of care in Guangzhou city, Guangdong Province, China.

Pregnant women and their partners attending prenatal care clinics at these 13 target hospitals were recruited using a convenience sampling technique. The inclusion criteria for pregnant women and their partners were: (1) Age ≥ 18 years; (2) Singleton pregnancy; (3) Without mental illness or communication disorder; (4) Voluntarily chose to participate in the study. All the participants provided written informed consents before participating in the study.

Questionnaire development

A comprehensive literature review was conducted to develop a questionnaire based on the KAP theory model. The questionnaire was comprised of 4 parts: (1) General information of the participants including name, age, hospital level, marital status, educational level, occupation, annual income, insurance type, gestational age, and parity. (2) Knowledge of NA, which consisted of 33 rating questions and 2 non-rating questions for sources of knowledge. A correct answer was scored as 1 point, and an incorrect answer was given a score of 0 points. A total knowledge score < 17 points was categorized as poor knowledge, a score from 17 to 24 points was considered moderate knowledge, and a score > 24 points was considered knowledgeable. (3) Attitudes towards NA was comprised of 9 items including fear, necessity, and recognition of labor pain. Participants were required to respond on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = completely disagree, 2 = partially disagree, 3 = neutral, 4 = partially agree, 5 = fully agree). Scores ranged from 9 to 45 with scores ≤ 21 indicating negative attitudes, scores from 21 to 33 indicating neutral attitudes, and a score > 33 indicating positive attitudes. (4) Practice included 8 rating items related to willingness to choose NA, availability of NA at the hospital, and participating a prenatal course about NA, as well as a non-rating question for concerns regarding NA. Participants answered using a 5-point Likert scale scored the same way as attitudes towards NA. Scores range from 8 to 40, with scores ≤ 18 indicating negative practice, 18 to 30 indicating neutral practice, and > 30 indicating positive practice.

Reliability and validity of questionnaire

The internal consistency of the KAP questionnaire was assessed by Cronbach’s α, and a value of Cronbach’s α closer to 1 indicates excellent consistency and reliability. Validity analysis was performed with SPSS version 25 software (IBM, Chicago IL, USA) using the Meyer-Olkin (KMO) and Barlett’s spherical test. A KMO value approaching 1 with P < 0.05 for Barlett’s test indicates better validity of the questionnaire.

Statistical analysis

The sample size was estimated according to a published reference [11] to accommodate potential sample loss and invalid questionnaires, resulting in a 20% increase in the number of questionnaires that needed to be distributed. Therefore, a minimum of 456 pregnant women and their partners were required. Demographic continuous data were analyzed using the paired Student’s t test, while categorical data were assessed using the χ2 test. Multiple linear regression was used to identify the factors influencing the participants knowledge-attitude-practice. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 25 software (IBM, Chicago IL, USA).

Results

During study period, a total of 2,520 questionnaires were administered, and 2,328 were considered valid. Thus, the study included 1,164 pairs of pregnant women and their partners (Fig. 1).

Flow chart of the study population

The Cronbach’s α coefficients for each section of the questionnaire during the formal survey were 0.93 for pregnant women and 0.94 for their partners, indicating excellent consistency and reliability. The KMO test values for all parts of the questionnaire were above 0.9. Bartlett’s test yielded a significance value approaching 0, and the cumulative variance of the questionnaires for pregnant women and their partners were 65% and 67%, respectively.

The majority of the pregnant women were 26 to 35 years old, and with respect to education levels, the largest group had a graduate level education. Employees were the primary occupation group; most reported an annual household income exceeding 100,000 yuan (RMB); the majority had urban medical insurance; and level 3 hospitals were the most frequently reported. Most pregnant women were in their first trimester, and the proportions of primiparous and multiparous women were similar (Table 1).

Discussion

This study enrolled 1,164 pairs of pregnant women and their partners from 13 public hospitals, and assessed their awareness, attitudes, and preferences towards epidural analgesia using the KAP questionnaire, which demonstrated high reliability and validity. The findings revealed that both pregnant women and their partners had minimal knowledge about pain relief during labor. Furthermore, regarding factors shaping attitudes and behavior towards epidural analgesia, annual income and number of deliveries were particularly significant for pregnant women, whereas hospital level and age affected their partners.

Our results showed that nearly 90% of pregnant women and their partners exhibited poor knowledge regarding NA, with 10% of pregnant women and 16% of their partners completely unaware about epidural analgesia. This finding aligns with many studies conducted in low- and middle-income countries [10, 13, 19, 20, 23]. Moreover, consistent with previous reports [24–26], our multivariable regression revealed that education level and gestational stage positively impacted knowledge of epidural analgesia among both pregnant women and their partners. This could be attributed to individuals with higher educational level having more opportunity to obtain information about epidural analgesia, allowing them to better understand its benefits and true risks from the myriads of unfiltered knowledges from various sources. However, despite more than 70% of individuals in this study holding at least a bachelor’s degree, the overall knowledge score regarding epidural analgesia was remarkably low. This discrepancy may be explained by several factors. First, knowledge of neuraxial analgesia is highly specialized and may not necessarily correlated with general educational attainment [27] Second, more than half of the participants were aged 26 to 35 years, and this age group was more likely to acquire information online through streaming platforms [28]. However, the quality of online health information related to pregnancy often falls short of optimal standards [29]. Therefore, addressing this challenge in the current digital era necessitates policy authorities and healthcare institutions to carry out more online educational projects using well-received tools, such as short videos, audio broadcasts, infographics, and interactive quizzes [30, 31]. These online interventions can be delivered through diverse platforms, including mobile websites, WeChat mini-programs, YouTube, and AI-powered intelligent platforms [32–34], allowing for revisiting and sharing of content, engaging individuals in decision-making processes, and also relieving medical staff from repetitive explanations [35, 36].

Regarding the positive correlation between gestational age and knowledge, it is widely recognized that expectant parents tend to increasingly focus more on information concerning the labor process and labor pain management as the pregnancy progresses. This may explain the knowledge gap identified in this study, since a significant portion of pregnant women were in their first trimester. This highlights that labor pain management is an important topic throughout the entire pregnancy. We also observed that the hospital level was positively related with pregnant women’s knowledge score, suggesting that the perspectives of prenatal caregivers are an important source of reliable information regarding epidural analgesia. In China, most of the maternity medical care providers are obstetricians, whose recommendations and viewpoints on NA are inevitably shaped by their hospitals’ methods of labor pain relief. For instance, level 3 hospitals typically possess more advanced medical equipment and a relatively larger number of anesthesiologists compared to level 1 and 2 hospitals, and usually have a higher NA rate [8]. In addition, hospitals at different levels have different anesthesia services, such as specialized obstetric anesthesiologists with 24-hour NA services and shared anesthesia teams with pain relief during labor availability on an ad-hoc basis, which also indirectly underscore the impact of hospital-level on labor analgesia use. Moreover, the awareness of epidural analgesia was significantly higher in multiparous women compared with primiparous women (B = 1.32), which is consistent with the study by Workie et al. [20]. This may be attributed to the fact that multiparous women have experienced labor pain, which may enhance their understanding of epidural analgesia. Conversely, primiparous women, lacking childbirth experience, often exhibit greater anxious and may consequently focus more on labor pain relief influencing their subsequent attitudes and practices. Therefore, improving obstetricians’ awareness and advocating the use of NA during labor across different hospital levels could potentially enhance primiparous women’s knowledge of epidural analgesia in China.

Although both pregnant women and their partners demonstrated generally limited knowledge of labor analgesia, consistent trends were observed in their overall attitudes and behavioral intentions. Meanwhile, our findings showed that pregnant women exhibited a significantly higher level of willingness compared to their partners, aligning with a previous report from Israel [26]. This discrepancy can be theoretically linked to heightened maternal concern about the impending labor pain. Another possible explanation could be the partners’ relatively limited knowledge of epidural analgesia [37]. These findings highlight the importance of including spouses in perinatal education to improve overall concordance and acceptance of labor analgesia. Moreover, our multivariate analysis identified a positive association between annual household income and maternal (not paternal) attitudes and practice towards epidural analgesia, even when NA costs were covered by medical insurance. This trend is consistent with findings from previous studies conducted in several high-income countries [24, 38]. The reasons for this correlation are intricate due to the mutual interaction between income, education, and healthcare literacy [37, 38]. Finaly, the only negative factor related to maternal attitudes and practice towards NA in this study was parity, a consistent finding in most previous studies [16, 18]. This observation could be attributed to multiparous women being less anxious about labor pain, more confident about childbirth, and typically undergoing shorter labor process compared with primiparous women.

Furthermore, we found that pregnant women and their partners shared remarkably similar concerns regarding NA, with the top 5 concerns revolving around inadequate awareness and misconception about the safety of epidural analgesia. For instance, nearly half of pregnant women and their partners cited maternal sequela and the impact of anesthesia on infant safety as reasons for declining labor analgesia. Despite these concerns having been refuted by substantial clinical data and international guidelines years ago [1, 2, 39], this outcome highlights the difficulty and limitation of disseminating correct medical knowledge to the general public. A previous study [40] found that fears and concerns about NA could be effectively addressed by patient-provider communication and education. Therefore, routine prenatal care should incorporate tailored consultations focusing on patient concerns about epidural analgesia to improve decision-making process before labor, and ultimately achieve a satisfactory delivery experience.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the participants recruited may differ from non-respondents in terms of their knowledge, attitudes, or practices, and the demographic characteristics (e.g., well-educated urban, predominantly primiparas) of our sample may introduce response and selection bias. Second, the data were collected using self-reported KAP questionnaires, which are inherently susceptible to social desirability bias. Finally, as a cross-sectional survey lacking follow-up data on actual NA utilization during labor, this study may be limited in its ability to link prenatal attitudes with real-world decision-making.

Conclusion

In summary, our study revealed insufficient knowledge about epidural analgesia among both pregnant women and their partners. For pregnant women, factors such as education level, gestational age, parity, and hospital level were found to positively influence their knowledge of NA, while a higher annual income was the only factor influencing attitudes and behavior. Additionally, hospital level and age positively affected the partner’s attitudes and behavior towards epidural analgesia. The primary concerns regarding NA are predominantly attributed to knowledge gaps and misconceptions about its safety. Implementing online hospital-based educational programs incorporating tailored information about epidural analgesia could potentially improve the decision-making process for pregnant women and their partners.
