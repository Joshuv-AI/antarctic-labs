Nonuniversal beyond-LHY corrections to thermodynamic properties of a weakly interacting Bose gas
Pham Duy Thanh, Nguyen Van Thu

Abstract
We investigate the effects of finite-range interatomic interactions on the equation of state (EoS) of a weakly interacting Bose gas. Within the Cornwall–Jackiw–Tomboulis effective action approach, we show that finite-range effects influence not only the EoS but also the thermodynamic properties of the system at zero temperature, leading to nonuniversal behavior.



I
Introduction

The study of thermodynamic properties of ultracold Bose gases dates back to the 1950s, initiated by the seminal works of Lee, Huang, and Yang (LHY)
lee1957eigenvalues
;
lee1957many
. An important result of these studies is the LHY correction, which shows that the ground-state energy at zero temperature density acquires a contribution scaling as
(128/15\sqrt{\pi})\sqrt{\rho a_{s}^{3}}
, where
a_{s}
is the
s
-wave scattering length and
\rho
is the atomic density. Numerous subsequent studies have confirmed this finding
brueckner1957bose
;
beliaev1958energy
;
lieb1963exact
;
haugset1998thermodynamics
. Recent years have witnessed a resurgence of interest in beyond–LHY correction in weakly interacting Bose systems, largely motivated by advances in the experimental realization of highly controllable quantum gas mixtures with tunable interactions
mordini2020measurement
;
skov2021observation
;
busley2022compressibility
;
cominotti2023ferromagnetism
. Within the framework of the Cornwall–Jackiw–Tomboulis (CJT) effective action formalism at the two-loop level, corrections to the energy density up to order
(\rho a_{s}^{3})^{3/2}
have recently been reported
van2022condensed
.

The CJT effective action approach developed in Refs.
van2022condensed
;
zhang2024cornwall
has been shown to be a powerful and versatile framework for describing ultracold quantum gases. In particular, its application to Bose-condensed systems offers two major advantages: (i) it provides a set of self-consistent equations whose solutions are intimately connected to the mechanism of spontaneous symmetry breaking; and (ii) it enables a direct and systematic determination of thermodynamic quantities through the effective potential density. Importantly, Ref.
zhang2024cornwall
demonstrates that this framework provides a natural and systematic approach to nonuniversal effects arising from finite-range interatomic interactions
braaten2001nonuniversal
;
cappellaro2017thermal
;
tononi2018condensation
;
lorenzi2023shell
;
ye2024nonuniversal
;
yu2024interaction
.

As a continuation of previous studies employing the CJT effective action approach at two-loop level, in this work we investigate the nonuniversal beyond-LHY corrections to thermodynamic properties of a weakly interacting Bose gas. The paper is organized as follows: In Section
II
, after briefly reviewing the determination of the effective mass by using the CJT theory within improved Hartree–Fock (HF) approximation, we analyze several thermodynamic quantities in Section
III
. Finally, conclusions and perspectives for future work are presented in Section
IV
.

II
CJT effective potential density and effective mass

Let us begin with the Lagrangian density of a Bose-condensed gas,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[\psi,\psi^{*}]=

\displaystyle\ \psi(\textbf{r},t)\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\right)\psi(\textbf{r},t)-\mu|\psi(\textbf{r},t)|^{2}+\frac{g}{2}|\psi(\textbf{r},t)|^{4}

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{2}|\psi(\textbf{r},t)|^{2}\nabla^{2}|\psi(\textbf{r},t)|^{2},

(1)

where
\mu
denotes the chemical potential, and
\psi(\mathbf{r},t)
is the bosonic field depending on space
\mathbf{r}
and time
t
;
m
is the atomic mass. The coupling constant
g
characterizes the interatomic interaction. Within the contact-interaction model, it is expressed in terms of the atomic mass and the
s
-wave scattering length. In the first Born approximation,
g
is given by
g=4\pi\hbar^{2}a_{s}/m
. The parameter
\lambda
accounts for finite-range effects and is defined as
\lambda=2\pi\hbar^{2}a_{s}^{2}r_{s}/m
, where
r_{s}
characterizes the effective range of the interatomic interaction
fu2003beyond
and may take either positive or negative values.

Then, we introduce the CJT effective potential density in the HF approximation that can be derived from Lagrangian density (
II
) in the manner that was pointed out in
phat2009bose
,

V_{\rm eff}[\psi_{0},G]=-\mu\psi_{0}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}\psi_{0}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\beta}\operatorname{Tr}[\ln G^{-1}(k)+G_{0}^{-1}(k)G(k)-1\!\!\!1]+\frac{3g}{8}\left(P_{11}^{2}+P_{22}^{2}\right)+\frac{g}{4}P_{11}P_{22}.

(2)

Here,
\psi_{0}=\sqrt{\mu/g}
denotes the expectation value of the field
\psi
at the tree level. The quantities
P_{aa}
are defined as the momentum integrals

P_{aa}=\int_{\beta}G_{aa}(k)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}G_{aa}(\omega_{n},k),\ a=(1,2),

(3)

where
\beta=1/(k_{B}T)
and
\omega_{n}=2\pi n\beta^{-1}
are the bosonic Matsubara frequencies at temperature
T
with
k_{B}
the Boltzmann constant. The propagators
G_{0}(k)
and
G(k)
correspond to the tree-level and HF approximations, respectively, and are functions of the wave vector
\bf k
.

Subsequently, physical solutions are obtained by imposing the stationarity of the generalized effective potential, which is expressed through the conditions:
\delta V_{\rm eff}/\delta\psi(x)=0
,
\delta V_{\rm eff}/\delta G(x,y)=0
. It can be seen that central to the CJT theoretical framework is the treatment of the full propagator
G(x,y)
as an independent variable, which allows one to derive self-consistent equations for both
\psi(x)
and
G(x,y)
from the CJT effective potential density. In particular, the variation
\delta V_{\rm eff}/\delta G(x,y)=0
of the CJT effective potential
V_{\rm eff}
in Eq. (
2
) leads to a self-consistent Schwinger-Dyson equation of the form

G^{-1}(k)=G_{0}^{-1}(k)+\Sigma.

(4)

On the right-hand side of Eq. (
4
),
G_{0}^{-1}(k)
is the inverse propagator within one-loop approximation and take the form

G_{0}^{-1}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{k}-\mu+3g\psi_{0}^{2}+2\lambda\psi_{0}^{2}k^{2}&-\omega_{n}\\
\omega_{n}&\varepsilon_{k}-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

(5)

where
\varepsilon_{k}=\hbar^{2}k^{2}/(2m)
. Meanwhile,
\Sigma
denotes the matrix containing the self-energies, namely

\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{1}&0\\
0&\Sigma_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(6)

with the self-energies
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{3g}{2}P_{11}+\frac{g}{2}P_{22},

(7a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{2}P_{11}+\frac{3g}{2}P_{22}.

(7b)

It’s easily to see that the propagator
G(k)
can be obtain when turned out
G^{-1}(k)
, namely

G(k)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}^{2}+E^{2}(k)}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{k}-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Sigma_{2}&\omega_{n}\\
-\omega_{n}&\varepsilon^{*}_{k}-\mu+3g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Sigma_{1}\end{pmatrix}

(8)

where
\varepsilon^{*}_{k}=\hbar^{2}k^{2}/(2m^{*})
, with modified mass
m^{*}
is defined as

m^{*}=\frac{m}{1+\dfrac{4m\lambda\psi_{0}^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}}\approx\frac{m}{1+8\pi\rho a_{s}^{2}r_{s}}.

(9)

In Eq. (
8
),
E(k)
is the dispersion relation or the excitations spectrum, which is determined by examining poles of the inverse propagator,

E(k)=\sqrt{(\varepsilon^{*}_{k}-\mu+3g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Sigma_{1})(\varepsilon_{k}-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Sigma_{2})}.

(10)

In the HF approximation, the excitations spectrum (
10
) exhibits an energy gap, in violation of Goldstone’s theorem
goldstone1962broken
in the context of spontaneous symmetry breaking. To remedy this issue and restore the Nambu-Goldstone boson, previous studies have supplemented the CJT effective potential density with a additional term, chosen such that the results in the symmetry-restored phase remain unchanged. As a consequence, the effective potential density takes the modified form

V_{\rm eff}[\psi_{0},G]=-\mu\psi_{0}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}\psi_{0}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\beta}\operatorname{Tr}[\ln G^{-1}(k)+G_{0}^{-1}(k)G(k)-1\!\!1]+\frac{g}{8}\left(P_{11}^{2}+P_{22}^{2}\right)+\frac{3g}{4}P_{11}P_{22}.

(11)

Simultaneously, this modification results in an interchange of the self-energy definitions,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{2}P_{11}+\frac{3g}{2}P_{22},

(12a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{3g}{2}P_{11}+\frac{g}{2}P_{22}.

(12b)

Analogous to the procedure used to determine the propagator in Eq. (
8
), the propagator within the improved HF approximation is given by

G(k)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}^{2}+E^{2}(k)}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{k}&\omega_{n}\\
-\omega_{n}&\varepsilon_{k}^{*}+M^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(13)

Clearly, the corresponding excitations spectrum is gapless and reads

E(k)=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}\left(\varepsilon_{k}^{*}+M^{2}\right)}.

(14)

By minimizing the CJT effective potential density in (
11
) with respect to the expectation value of the field operator, we derive the gap equation

-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Sigma_{2}=0.

(15)

In addition, the SD equation that the effective mass
M^{2}
satisfies can be reformulated as

-\mu+3g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Sigma_{1}=M^{2}.

(16)

It is important to note that, the chemical potential can be obtained by taking the first-order derivative of the
V_{\rm eff}
in Eq. (
11
) with respect to total density
\rho
,

\mu=-\frac{\partial V_{\rm eff}}{\partial\rho}=g\rho+gP_{11}.

(17)

Combining Eqs. (
12
) and (
15
)-(
17
), we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm ex}=\frac{1}{2}(P_{11}+P_{22}),

(18)

\displaystyle M^{2}=2g\rho-3g\rho_{ex}-\frac{g}{2}(P_{11}-3P_{22}).

(19)

Here,
\rho_{\rm ex}=\rho-\psi_{0}^{2}
denotes the non-condensed density, also referred to as the quantum depletion.

To proceed, we must determine the self-energies; in other words, this amounts to evaluating the momentum integrals

\displaystyle P_{11}=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\frac{d\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\omega_{n}^{2}+E^{2}(k)},

(20a)

\displaystyle P_{22}=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\frac{d\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}^{*}+M^{2}}{\omega_{n}^{2}+E^{2}(k)}.

(20b)

By using the following formula

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\omega_{n}^{2}+E^{2}(k)}=\frac{\beta}{2E(k)}\left(1+\frac{2}{e^{\beta E(k)}-1}\right),

(21)

we have

\displaystyle P_{11}=\int\frac{d\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2E(k)}\left(1+\frac{2}{e^{\beta E(k)}-1}\right),

(22a)

\displaystyle P_{22}=\int\frac{d\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}^{*}+M^{2}}{2E(k)}\left(1+\frac{2}{e^{\beta E(k)}-1}\right).

(22b)

At zero temperature, the integrals (
22a
) and (
22b
) take the form

\displaystyle P_{11}=\frac{(2m^{*})^{3/2}M^{3}}{6\pi^{2}\hbar^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{m^{*}}{m}},

(23a)

\displaystyle P_{22}=-\frac{(2m^{*})^{3/2}M^{3}}{12\pi^{2}\hbar^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{m}{m^{*}}}.

(23b)

To determine the parameter
M
, we first combine Eqs. (
18
) and (
19

P_{11}
given in in Eq. (

). Finally, we readily find that

M^{2}=2g\rho-\frac{(2m^{*})^{3/2}g}{3\pi^{2}\hbar^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{m^{*}}{m}}M^{3}.

(24)

We now briefly review the key steps involved in solving Eq. (
24
) using perturbation theory. First, we introduce a small parameter
\epsilon
such that

M^{2}=2g\rho-\epsilon\frac{(2m^{*})^{3/2}g}{3\pi^{2}\hbar^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{m^{*}}{m}}M^{3}.

(25)

Meanwhile,
M
is expressed as a polynomial expansion in terms of the parameter
\epsilon

M\to M_{0}+\epsilon M_{1}+\epsilon^{2}M_{2}.

(26)

Next, the expression for

given in Eq. (
26

25

up to

\displaystyle M_{0}=

\displaystyle\ \sqrt{2g\rho},

(27a)

\displaystyle M_{1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{(2m^{*})^{3/2}g}{6\pi^{2}\hbar^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{m^{*}}{m}}M_{0}^{2},

(27b)

\displaystyle M_{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{2M_{0}}-\frac{(2m^{*})^{3/2}g}{2\pi^{2}\hbar^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{m^{*}}{m}}M_{0}M_{1}.

(27c)

Finally, we obtain the expression for

IV
Conclusions

In this paper, we have theoretically investigated nonuniversal quantum effects in an ultracold Bose-condensed gas with finite-range interatomic interactions within an improved HF approximation based on the CJT effective field theory. Analytical expressions for several thermodynamic quantities have been derived as functions of the interaction parameters. Our results reveal the emergence of nonuniversal beyond-LHY corrections arising from quantum fluctuations and finite-range effects.

Importantly, we have shown that finite-range contributions can lead to measurable deviations in thermodynamic quantities, particularly in the ground-state energy density, thereby providing a feasible route for experimental detection of beyond-LHY effects.

Overall, our work offers a complementary perspective on ultracold quantum systems within the CJT formalism. The present approach can be further extended to investigate strongly correlated regimes and novel quantum phases, including quantum droplets in Bose mixtures as well as other ultracold Fermi systems.
