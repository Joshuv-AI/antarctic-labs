The five-sequence of adjoints for combinatorial simplicial complexes
Gunnar Fløystad

Abstract
. For a set A A let 𝐒𝐂 A {{\mathbf{SC}}_{A}} be the poset of simplicial complexes whose vertices are in A A . For a function f : A → B f:A\rightarrow B there are functors f ! ! , f ∗ ⁣ ∗ , f ! ! : 𝐒𝐂 A → 𝐒𝐂 B , f ! ∗ , f ! ∗ : 𝐒𝐂 B → 𝐒𝐂 A , f^{{!}{!}},f^{**},f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}:{{\mathbf{SC}}_{A}}\rightarrow{{\mathbf{SC}}_{B}},\quad f^{{!}*},f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}*}:{{\mathbf{SC}}_{B}}\rightarrow{{\mathbf{SC}}_{A}}, forming a five sequence of adjoints f ! ! ⊣ f ∗ ! ⊣ f ∗ ⁣ ∗ ⊣ f ∗ ! ⊣ f ! ! . f^{{!}{!}}\dashv f^{*{!}}\dashv f^{**}\dashv f^{*{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}\dashv f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}. Introduction

A (combinatorial) simplicial complex
X
on a set
A
is a family of
subsets of
A
such that if
F\in X
and
G\subseteq F
then
G\in X
.
Let
{{\mathbf{SC}}_{A}}
be the poset (ordered by inclusion) of simplicial
complexes on
A
. Given a function
f:A\rightarrow B
,
there are functors (order-preserving maps)

f^{{!}{!}},f^{**},f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}:{{\mathbf{SC}}_{A}}\rightarrow{{\mathbf{SC}}_{B}},\quad f^{{!}*},f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}*}:{{\mathbf{SC}}_{B}}\rightarrow{{\mathbf{SC}}_{A}},

such that these form a five sequence of adjoint functors

f^{{!}{!}}\dashv f^{*{!}}\dashv f^{**}\dashv f^{*{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}\dashv f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}.

We investigate these functors, and how they transform
simplicial complexes. In particular we do this in detail for

f
injective and for
f
surjective.

Let
k
be a commutative ring (usually a field), and
k[x_{A}]
the
polynomial ring with
x_{a},a\in A
as variables. To a simplicial complex

X
on a finite set
A
is associated the
face ring
or
Stanley-Reisner ring

k[x_{A}]/I_{X}
where
I_{X}\subseteq k[x_{A}]
is the ideal generated by monomials

\prod_{i\in N}x_{i}
where
N\subseteq A
ranges over the subsets not in
X
.

Since their introduction in the mid-seventies,
Stanley-Reisner rings have been massively studied, see standard
textbooks
[
7
,
10
,
17
,
18
]
. The important
notions of Cohen-Macaulay
and Gorenstein simplicial complexes are defined via their Stanley-Reisner
rings. Major achievements of this correspondence was R.Stanley’s
proof in 1975 of the Upper Bound Conjecture for simplicial spheres

[
15
]
,
and some years later the characterization of
f
-vectors of simplicial
polytopes,
[
2
,
16
]
.

Normally in mathematics a
correspondence between geometry and algebra is functorial. This also
holds for the Stanley-Reisner correspondence. But this has received little
(if any) attention, perhaps due to a certain unnaturality: the morphisms of
rings do not respect multigradings.
Using the above functors we give two new categorical structures on
simplicial complexes on finite sets, and define three categorical
structures on Stanley-Reisner rings. We get three dualities
between categories of simplicial complexes and categories of
Stanley-Reisner rings. The two new dualities introduced respect multigradings,
as the ring homomorphisms send homogeneous elements to homogeneous elements.

Let us mention another recent direction,
[
4
]
, considering
simplicial complexes and Stanley-Reisner theory
from a more principled setting. It
relates to algebraic geometry and sheaf theory. In particular
the set of subsets of a finite
n
-set is considered as the affine scheme

{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{{\mathsf{F}}_{1}}
over the “field with one element”.
An early contribution in this direction is
[
19
]
.

The organization of this article is as follows. Section
1

contains preliminaries on posets, Galois correspondences and adjoint
functors. Section
2
details the notion of simplicial complexes

gives explicit descriptions of these functors. Section

gives two new categorical structures on simplicial complexes making

In Section

we show how the functors interact with
Alexander duality. The short Section

recalls some
terminology for simplicial complexes. Section
7
gives more
detail on the functors when

is injective, and Section

8
does the same when
f:A\rightarrow B
is surjective.
In the last Section

we use the functors to define
several products of simplicial complexes
X,Y
on disjoint
sets
A
and
B
.

Note.
Simplicial complexes
are assumed to be defined on arbitrary
sets, usually denoted
A
or
B
. But in statements related to
polynomial rings
k[x_{A}]
or
k[y_{B}]
, we always assume

1.
Preliminaries on posets

Posets give rise to distributive lattices, and we recall this. We
also see how an order-preserving morphism between posets
f:P\rightarrow Q
,
gives rise to three adjoint functors
f^{!}\dashv f^{*}\dashv f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}

between the associated distributive lattices.

1.1.
Galois correspondences
and minimal and maximal elements

Given posets
P,Q
. Recall that a Galois correspondence between
them is two order-preserving maps

P\overset{F}{\underset{G}{\rightleftarrows}}Q
such that
F(p)\leq q
if and only if

p\leq G(q)
.
The following is immediate:

Lemma 1.1
.

Given such a Galois correspondence.
Let
p_{0}\in P
and
q_{0}\in Q
.

a.

The unique maximal
p\in P
such that
F(p)\leq q_{0}
,
is
p=G(q_{0})
.

b.

The unique minimal
q\in Q
such
that
G(q)\geq p_{0}
is
q=F(p_{0})
.

A Galois correspondence is a special case of the much more
general notion of an adjunction between categories.
We will use the adjunction terminology instead of Galois correspondence.

1.2.
The lattice of cuts in a posets

For posets
P,Q
, denote by
\text{Hom}(P,Q)
the set of order-preserving
maps between them. This is itself a poset by
\phi\leq\psi
if

\phi(p)\leq\psi(p)
for every
p\in P
. Denote
{\mathbf{2}}:=\{0<1\}
.

A
down-set

D
in a poset
P

is a subset of
P
such that if
q\in D
and
p\leq q
, then
p\in D
.
For a poset
P
, denote by
P^{{{\text{op}}}}

the
opposite poset
with order
p\leq_{{{\text{op}}}}q
if
p\geq q
.
The lattice of down-sets of
P
identifies as
\text{Hom}(P^{{{\text{op}}}},{\mathbf{2}})
.
A down-set
D
gives a map

\phi:P^{{{\text{op}}}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{2}},\quad\phi(p)=\begin{cases}1,&p\in D\\
0,&p\not\in D\end{cases}.

For short, denote by
\hat{P}
the set of order-preserving maps

\text{Hom}(P^{{{\text{op}}}},{\mathbf{2}})
,
which we equally well identify as the down-sets of
P
.
It is the distributive lattice associated to
P
. (Birkhoff’s
representation theorem says that any distributive lattice is of this
form,
[
3
]
.)

If
D
is a down-set, the complement
U
of
D
in
P
is an
up-set
.
We may consider the elements of
\hat{P}
to be
cuts

(D,U)
.
We sometimes write this as
(D,-)
or
(-,U)
according to which part
we focus on.

Given an order-preserving map
f:P\rightarrow Q
. This induces an
order-preserving map

f^{*}:\text{Hom}(Q^{{{\text{op}}}},{\mathbf{2}})\rightarrow\text{Hom}(P^{{{\text{op}}}},{\mathbf{2}}),\,\text{ or for short }f^{*}:\hat{Q}\rightarrow\hat{P},

sending a cut
(D,U)
of
Q
to the cut
(f^{-1}(D),f^{-1}(U)
of
P
.
This map
f^{*}
has both a left and a right adjoint

f^{!},f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}:\hat{P}\rightarrow\hat{Q},\quad f^{!}\dashv f^{*}\dashv f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}},

as we now explain.
For a subset
S
of the poset
P
, denote by
S^{\downarrow}
the down-set
of
P
generated by
S
, so

S^{\downarrow}=\{p\in P\,|\,p\leq s\text{ for some }s\in S\}.

Similarly we have
S^{\uparrow}
, the up-set generated by
S
.
The adjoints are then given by

f^{!}(D,U)=(f(D)^{\downarrow},-),\quad f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}(D,U)=(-,f(U)^{\uparrow}).

Example 1.2
.

Consider
A
to be a set of apples and
B
buckets. The function
f:A\rightarrow B

puts apples into buckets. Then:

•

f^{*}:\hat{B}\rightarrow\hat{A}
sends a set of buckets

C\subseteq B
to the set of apples contained in these buckets,
f^{-1}(C)\subseteq A
.

•

f^{!}:\hat{A}\rightarrow\hat{B}
sends a subset of apples
D\subseteq A
to the
buckets containing
some
element from
D
(the “exist-a-
D
apple” buckets)

•

f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}:\hat{A}\rightarrow\hat{B}
sends the subset
D
to the buckets
which
only
contain apples from
D
(the “all-
D
apple” buckets)

Given a set map
f:A\rightarrow B
and
D\subseteq A
, the
f
-core
of
D

is the largest
C\subseteq B
such that
f^{-1}(C)\subseteq D
. We write

{\text{{\it core}}}_{f}(D)
for this
C
.
Note that
{\text{{\it core}}}_{f}(D)
is the set of all
b
such that the full fiber

f^{-1}(b)
is contained in
D
. In particular if
E=B\backslash f(A)
,
then
E
is contained in every
{\text{{\it core}}}_{f}(D)
.
For
(D,U)\in\hat{A}
we observe that:

•

f^{!}(D,U)=(f(D),{\text{{\it core}}}_{f}(U))
,

•

f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}(D,U)=({\text{{\it core}}}_{f}(D),f(U))
.

We get the following from Lemma
1.1

concerning fibers when we have both
a left and right adjoint.

Corollary 1.3
.

Given adjoints

f^{!}\dashv f^{*}\dashv f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}
of posets:

f^{!},f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}:P\rightarrow Q,\quad f^{*}:Q\rightarrow P.

Suppose
f^{*}(q)=p
, then such
q
giving value
p
,
are precisely those in the interval

f^{!}(p)\leq q\leq f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}(p)
(note that the interval may be empty).

Proof.

If
f^{*}(q)=p
, by Lemma
1.1

f^{!}(p)\leq q\leq f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}(p)
. Furthermore

p\leq f^{*}\circ f^{{!}}(p)\leq f^{*}(q)\leq f^{*}\circ f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}(p)\leq p,

so all give value
p
.
∎

1.3.
The general setting

Before proceeding we note how this fits in a very general setting.
For categories
\mathcal{C}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
denote by
\text{Hom}(\mathcal{C},{\mathcal{D}})
the functors
from
\mathcal{C}
to
{\mathcal{D}}
. This becomes itself a category with morphisms
between functors
F
and
G
the natural transformations
F\Rightarrow G
.
Denote by
{\mathbf{Set}}
the category of sets. The category of presheaves on

\mathcal{C}
has objects the functors
\text{Hom}(\mathcal{C}^{{{\text{op}}}},{\mathbf{Set}})
.
Given a functor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
, let
\mathcal{Z}
be another category.
This induces a functor

F^{*}:\text{Hom}({\mathcal{D}}^{{{\text{op}}}},\mathcal{Z})\rightarrow\text{Hom}(\mathcal{C}^{{{\text{op}}}},\mathcal{Z}).

One may ask if this functor has a left or right adjoint. Adjoints
in this setting are called Kan extensions. We have the following

[
13
, Cor.6.2.6]
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
are small categories and

F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
a functor. If
\mathcal{Z}
is complete and co-complete
(i.e. has all limits and colimits), then

F^{*}:({\mathcal{D}}^{{{\text{op}}}},\mathcal{Z})\rightarrow(\mathcal{C}^{{{\text{op}}}},\mathcal{Z})
has a left adjoint

F^{!}
and a right adjoint
F^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}
.

Note that
{\mathbf{Set}}
is complete and co-complete. We are applying this
in the setting of enriched categories,
[
9
]
or
[
6
, Sec.4.4]
,
where
{\mathbf{Set}}
is replaced by the category
{\mathbf{2}}
.
A poset
P
is then a
{\mathbf{2}}
-category,
since if
p,q\in P
, then
\text{Hom}(p,q)
is an object
in
{\mathbf{2}}
: This Hom-set is
1
if
p\leq q
and
0
if we do not have

p\leq q
. Generally, in an enriched category
\mathcal{C}
, one
replaces

Set
with a symmetric monoidal closed category
\mathcal{M}
such that for objects

P,Q
in
\mathcal{C}
the morphisms
\text{Hom}(P,Q)
is an object in
\mathcal{M}
.

2.
Simplicial complexes and the five-sequence
of adjoints

We develop the conceptual setting of (combinatorial) simplicial complexes
on a set, and we give the five-sequence of adjoints.

2.1.
Simplicial complexes

For a set
A
let
P(A)
be the power set of
A
, the set of
all subsets of
A
. This is a (Boolean) lattice ordered by
inclusion of subsets. A
(combinatorial) simplicial complex
on
A
is a set
X

of subsets of
A
such that if
F\in X
and
G\subseteq F
, then
G\in X
.
Such
X
are precisely sets of subsets of
P(A)
closed under taking smaller
subsets, or a
down-set
of
P(A)
. For basics on combinatorial
simplicial complexes, we refer to
[
7
,
10
,
17
]
.

The power set
P(A)
may be identified with maps (set functions)

\phi:A\rightarrow{\mathbf{2}}
. A subset
D\subseteq A
identifies with the function

\phi:A\rightarrow{\mathbf{2}},\quad\phi(a)=\begin{cases}1,&a\in D\\
0,&a\not\in D\end{cases}.

Thus subsets
D\subseteq A
corresponds to elements of

\hat{A}=\text{Hom}(A^{{{\text{op}}}},{\mathbf{2}})=\text{Hom}(A,{\mathbf{2}}).

Iterating the construction of Subsection
1.2
, for
a poset
P
:

\hat{\hat{P}}=\text{Hom}((\hat{P})^{{\text{op}}},{\mathbf{2}})=\text{Hom}(\widehat{P^{{\text{op}}}},{\mathbf{2}}).

An element here is a cut
({\mathcal{D}},\mathcal{U})
where
{\mathcal{D}}
is a down-set
of
\hat{P}
and
\mathcal{U}
is the complementary up-set.
A simplicial complex
X
on
A
, corresponds to a down-set
in
\hat{A}
. These correspond again precisely to order-preserving maps
(
D
is a subset of
A
)

\Psi:(\hat{A})^{{{\text{op}}}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{2}},\quad\phi(D)=\begin{cases}1,&D\in X\\
0,&D\not\in X\end{cases}.

Thus simplicial complexes correspond precisely to elements of

\hat{\hat{A}}=\text{Hom}((\hat{A})^{{{\text{op}}}},{\mathbf{2}})
.

An element in
\hat{\hat{A}}
is a cut
({\mathcal{D}},\mathcal{U})
,
where
{\mathcal{D}}
is a down-set in the boolean
lattice
\hat{A}
which identifies as the Boolean lattice
P(A)

of subsets of
A
. Hence elements of
\hat{\hat{A}}
identifies as a
simplicial complex
X
.
Precisely this is as follows:
The elements of
{\mathcal{D}}
are cuts
(D,U)
. The elements of
X
are
then the subsets
D
of
A
. We shall henceforth
use
\hat{\hat{A}}
as representing simplicial complexes
{{\mathbf{SC}}_{A}}
.
It will be convenient, especially in
Section
9
, to have a full
arsenal of terminology:

1.

When
(D,U)\in{\mathcal{D}}
, then
D
is a
face
of
X
, and

U
is a
face complement
of
X
.

2.

When
(D,U)\in\mathcal{U}
, then
D
is a
non-face
of
X

and
U
is a

non-face complement
of
X
.

The dimension of a face is
D
is
|D|-1
. The dimension of
X

is the maximal dimension of a face.

2.2.
The five-sequence of adjoints

Now consider an order-preserving map
f:P\rightarrow Q
of posets. By Proposition

1.4
we get left and right adjoints

(1)

f^{{!}},f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}:\hat{P}\overset{}{\underset{}{\rightleftarrows}}\hat{Q}:f^{*}.

We may repeat this process and get further maps between

\hat{\hat{P}}
and
\hat{\hat{Q}}
. If
p,q\in\{{!},*,{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}\}
denote

(f^{p})^{q}
as
f^{pq}
. Each of the maps
f^{p}
in (
1
)
gives three maps
f^{p{!}},f^{p*}
and
f^{p{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}
. In all one gets
nine such maps, as
p={!},*,{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}
, but not all of these are distinct.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f:P\rightarrow Q
be order-preserving maps of posets.
Then

\text{a. }\,f^{{!}*}=f^{*{!}},\quad\text{b. }\,f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}*}=f^{*{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}},\quad\text{c. }\,f^{**}=f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{!}}=f^{{!}{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}.

Thus we have three distinct functors

f^{{!}{!}},f^{**},f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}:\hat{\hat{P}}\rightarrow\hat{\hat{Q}},

and two distinct functors

f^{{!}*},f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}*}:\hat{\hat{Q}}\rightarrow\hat{\hat{P}}.

Proof.

a. We have
f^{!}\dashv f^{*}
. By
[
13
, Prop.4.4.6]

f^{{!}*}\dashv f^{**}
. Since
f^{*{!}}\dashv f^{**}
, by uniqueness
of adjoints,
[
13
, Prop.4.4.1]
,
we get
f^{*{!}}=f^{{!}*}
. Part b. is similar.

c. We have
f^{*}\dashv f^{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}
. Again by
[
13
, Prop.4.4.6]

f^{**}\dashv f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}*}
. Also
f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{!}}\dashv f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}*}
. Again by
uniqueness of adjunctions
f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{!}}=f^{**}
. Similarly

f^{{!}{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}=f^{**}
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

This five-sequence of adjoints for posets is well-known to category
theorists. It is described explicitly in the book
[
14
, Section 6]
.
But we are unaware of any detailed study of this five-sequence, at
least from the perspective of simplicial complexes, as we now undertake.

Corollary 2.3
.

For a map
f:A\rightarrow B
of sets we get
a five-sequence of adjoints

f^{{!}{!}}\dashv f^{*{!}}\dashv f^{**}\dashv f^{*{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}\dashv f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}},

which are functors between
simplicial complexes on
A
and
B
:

(2)

\displaystyle f^{{!}{!}},f^{**},f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\hat{\hat{A}}\rightarrow\hat{\hat{B}}

(3)

\displaystyle f^{{!}*},f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}*}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\hat{\hat{B}}\rightarrow\hat{\hat{A}}.

Our main objective is to describe and investigate these five functors.

3.
General statement and proofs

We give the explicit description of the functors (
2
)
and (
3
). We first note the immediate:

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a function
f:A\rightarrow B
.
Let
X
be a simplicial complex on
A
, and fix
C\subseteq B
.

a.

Every
D\subseteq A
with
f(D)=C
is a non-face of
X
if
and only if no face
D
of

has
C\subseteq f(D)
.

b.

Every
D\subseteq A
with

is a face of

if
and only if no non-face
D
of
X
has
C\geq{\text{{\it core}}}_{f}(D)
.

Proof.

Part a is obvious. As for part b, taking negations on each side
the statement is that some non-face
D
has
{\text{{\it core}}}_{f}(D)=C
iff
some non-face
D
has
C\geq{\text{{\it core}}}_{f}(D)
, which is clear.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Given a function
f:A\rightarrow B
.
Let

be a simplicial complex on

, and let

.

a.

is a face of

for some face

.

Proof.

Both statements are immediate.

Theorem 3.3
.

Given a function

.

a.

The map

sends

to the simplicial complex

is a face of

. This is the largest

.

b.

The map
f^{{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}*}:\hat{\hat{B}}\rightarrow\hat{\hat{A}}
sends

4.
Functoriality of Stanley-Reisner correspondence

To a combinatorial simplicial complex
X
there is an associated ring,
its Stanley-Reisner ring. This is a central object in combinatorial
commutative algebra.
In mathematics, natural and
fruitful correspondences between geometry and algebra are normally functorial.
The Stanley-Reisner correspondence is also functorial. But, perhaps due
to a certain unnaturality (the morphisms on algebras do not respect
multigradings), there has hardly been attention on this.

With the greater arsenal of maps
between simplicial complexes we now have,
it is possible to define three functorial correspondence between
simplicial complexes and rings, giving dualities. And in fact two of the three do
respect multigradings.

4.1.
Stanley-Reisner ring

For a finite set
A
let
k[x_{A}]
be the polynomial ring
whose variables are
x_{a}
for
a\in A
. If
E\subseteq A
write

x^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{E}
for the monomial
\prod_{e\in E}x_{e}
(called a square-free monomial
since it is a product of distinct variables).

When
X
is a simplicial complex on the finite set
A
,
the
Stanley-Reisner ideal

I_{X}
of
X
is the ideal in
k[x_{A}]
generated by monomials
x^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{E}

where
E
is
not
in
X
. The
Stanley-Reisner
ring is

k[X]:=k[x_{A}]/I_{X}
.
A squarefree monomial
x^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{E}
is then non-zero in
k[x_{A}]/I_{X}
iff

E\in X
. Note that the ring
k[X]
will be
{\mathbb{Z}}^{A}
-graded.

4.2.
Categories of simplicial complexes

Simplicial complexes can be made into a category. Given simplicial
complexes
X
on
A
and
Y
on
B
(
A,B
not necessarily finite),
the traditional notion for a
morphism from
X
to
Y
is a function
f:A\rightarrow B
such that
for each face
F
of
X
, the image
f(F)
is a face of
Y
.
With our notation here, it says that a morphism from

X
to
Y
is a function
f:A\rightarrow B
such
f^{{!}{!}}(X)\subseteq Y
.
Denote this category as
{\mathbf{SC_{0}}}
.

We shall give two other ways to make simplicial complexes into a category.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f:A\rightarrow B
be a set function, and
X
and
Y
simplicial
complexes on respectively
A
and
B
.

a.

If
Y=f^{**}(X)
, the map
f
(or really
f^{!}
) sends
non-faces of
X
to non-faces of
Y
.

b.

If
X=f^{*{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}(Y)
, then
f^{-1}
(or really
f^{*}
) sends
non-faces of
Y
to non-faces of
X
.

Proof.

For part a. let
N
be a non-face of
X
. If
f(N)
was a face of
Y
,
it would have to be a core of some face
F
of
X
.
But this contradicts that
N
is a non-face.

For part b. let
Y
correspond to a cut
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V})
of
\hat{B}
.
Then
f^{*{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}(Y)
is
(-,f^{*\uparrow}(\mathcal{V}))
, and so non-faces of
Y
,
(elements of
\mathcal{V}
) map to non-faces of
X
.
∎

In addition to
{\mathbf{SC_{0}}}
,
we now define two other categories of simplicial complexes.

Definition 4.2
.

The categories
{\mathbf{SC_{1}}}
and
{\mathbf{SC_{2}}}
have
simplicial complexes on finite sets as
objects. Let
X
be a simplicial complex on the set
A
, and
Y

a simplicial complex on the set
B
.

A morphism
F:X\rightarrow Y
in
{\mathbf{SC_{1}}}

is a set map
f:A\rightarrow B
such that
f^{**}(X)\subseteq Y
, or equivalently
(due to the adjunction
f^{**}\dashv f^{*{\scalebox{0.78}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\rm{!}}}}}
)

.

A morphism
G:X\rightarrow Y
in
{\mathbf{SC_{2}}}

is a set map
g:B\rightarrow A
such that
X\subseteq g^{**}(Y)
, or equivalently
(due to the adjunction
g^{*{!}}\dashv g^{**}
)

.

More explicitly a morphism
F:X\rightarrow Y
in
{\mathbf{SC_{1}}}
is a map
f:A\rightarrow B

such that:

•

If
C\subseteq B
and
f^{-1}(C)
is a face of
X
,
then
C
is a face of
Y
.

If we think of
f:A\rightarrow B
as giving
a partition of the vertices of
A
into color classes

, one for each
color
b\in B
, then
such a partition gives a morphism if whenever a union of color classes
A_{b_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup A_{b_{m}}
is a face of
X
,
then
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
is a face of
Y
.

On the other hand a morphism
G:X\rightarrow Y
in
{\mathbf{SC_{2}}}
given by

is such that:

•

If
D\subseteq A
and
A
is a face of
X
, then
g^{-1}(A)
is
a face of
Y
.

We can think of
g
as “blowing up” each vertex

to a set
B_{a}
.
It gives a morphism if whenever
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is a face of
X
,
then the “blow up”
B_{a_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup B_{a_{n}}
is a face of

.

Example 4.3
.

Let
X
be the discrete simplicial complex on
A
, so that each vertex

a
is a facet
\{a\}
. Then a morphism

in
{\mathbf{SC_{2}}}
is equivalent to give a matching in
Y
, indexed by
A
,
i.e. a partition

B=\cup_{a\in A}B_{a}
such that each
B_{a}
is a face of
Y
.

Example 4.4
.

Let
Y=\{\emptyset\}
. Then a morphism
X\rightarrow Y

in
{\mathbf{SC_{1}}}
is equivalent to give a partition
A=\cup_{b\in A}A_{b}

such that no monochromatic set

is a face of

.
See Example

for more detail.
In particular, there are
many morphisms

in
{\mathbf{SC_{1}}}
such that

is non-empty
and

. So there is not a direct geometric

.

4.3.
Categories of rings

Fix a commutative ring
k
.
We define three categories of rings

{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{0}_{k},\quad{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{1}_{k},\quad{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{2}_{k}.

These categories all have the same
objects
: Quotients of
polynomial rings
k[x_{A}]/I

where
A
is a finite set and

a squarefree monomial ideal.
So each generator of

is a squarefree monomial
x_{E}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}
where
E\subseteq A
. This is
a category of

-algebras but the objects come with additional data,
a set of variables.
The categories are distinguished by their
morphisms
:

In each category a morphism is a
k
-algebra morphism

k[y_{B}]/I_{2}\rightarrow k[x_{A}]/I_{1}
induced by a
k
-algebra homomorphism
of polynomial rings (which is part of the data):
\phi:k[y_{B}]\rightarrow k[x_{A}]
.
The homomorphism
\phi
has the following form:

•

In
{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{2}_{k}
, it
sends each variable
y_{b}
to some variable
x_{a}
.
So this morphism comes with a set map
g:B\rightarrow A
, and
a=g(b)
.

•

In
{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{1}_{k}
, it sends each squarefree monomial

y^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{C}
(with
C\subseteq B
) to a
squarefree
monomial

x^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{D}
(with
D\subseteq A
). Such a morphism is readily seen to
come from a set map
f:A\rightarrow B
,
and each variable
y_{b}
is sent to
x^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{A_{b}}
(note that if
b
is
not in the image of
f
, then
y_{b}\mapsto 1
).

•

In
{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{0}_{k}
, it sends each variable
y_{b}

to a sum of variables
x_{a_{1}}+x_{a_{2}}+\cdots+x_{a_{r}}
where
the sets
A_{b}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}
are disjoint for distinct
b
’s,
Again such a morphism comes from a map
f:A\rightarrow B
,
and the variable
y_{b}
is sent to
\sum_{a\in A_{b}}x_{a}
(note that if
b
is
not in the image of
f
, then
y_{b}\mapsto 0
).

Note that in the last category
{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{0}_{k}
, the maps do not
correspond to a homomorphism of multigradings
{\mathbb{Z}}^{B}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}^{A}
,
since a sum of two or more variables is not homogeneous.
However, morphisms in the
two other categories do send homogeneous elements to
homogeneous elements.

4.4.
Functors from simplicial complexes to
commutative rings

Now let
{\mathbf{sc_{1}}}
be the subcategory of
{\mathbf{SC_{1}}}
consisting of
simplicial complexes

on
finite
sets. Similarly we
have
{\mathbf{sc_{0}}}
and
{\mathbf{sc_{2}}}
.
We define a functor:
{\mathbf{sc_{1}}}^{{{\text{op}}}}\rightarrow{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{1}_{k}
(the

the functor is contravariant), by sending a simplicial complex
to its Stanley-Reisner ring. If
F:X\rightarrow Y
is a morphism in
{\mathbf{sc_{1}}}
,
let

. We then get a
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebra homomorphism:

{\mathbf{k}}[y_{B}]/I_{Y}\rightarrow{\mathbf{k}}[x_{A}]/I_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{k}}[x_{A}]/I_{X}.

The second map above is due to

being a subcomplex of

X^{\prime}
. The first map sends a variable
y_{b}
to
x^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{A_{b}}
. By
Proposition
4.1
b. it is well defined.

We define a functor:
{\mathbf{sc_{2}}}^{{{\text{op}}}}\rightarrow{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{2}_{k}
,
by sending a simplicial complex
to its Stanley-Reisner ring. If
G:X\rightarrow Y
is a morphism in
{\mathbf{sc_{2}}}
,
let
X\subseteq X^{\prime}=g^{**}(Y)
. We then have maps
of Stanley-Reisner rings:

{\mathbf{k}}[y_{B}]/I_{Y}\rightarrow{\mathbf{k}}[x_{A}]/I_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{k}}[x_{A}]/I_{X}.

The second map comes from the inclusion
X\subseteq X^{\prime}
. The first
map sends a variable
y_{b}
to
x_{f(b)}
.
By Proposition
4.1
a. it is well defined
(for its application switch
X
and
Y
).

We define a functor:
{\mathbf{sc_{0}}}^{{{\text{op}}}}\rightarrow{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{0}_{k}
,
by sending a simplicial complex
to its Stanley-Reisner ring. If
F:X\rightarrow Y
is a morphism in
{\mathbf{sc_{0}}}
,
let
X\subseteq X^{\prime}=f^{*{!}}(Y)
. We then have maps
of Stanley-Reisner rings:

{\mathbf{k}}[y_{B}]/I_{Y}\rightarrow{\mathbf{k}}[x_{A}]/I_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{k}}[x_{A}]/I_{X}.

The second map comes from the inclusion
X\subseteq X^{\prime}
. The first
map sends a variable
y_{b}
to the sum
\sum_{a\in A_{b}}x_{a}
.
This is well-defined, since if
N
is a nonface of
Y
,
then for any choices
a_{n}\in A_{n}
for

, the
set

is a nonface of

(it cannot
be a face as

is a face of

of

).

Theorem 4.5
.

The Stanley-Reisner correspondence gives dualities

{\mathbf{sc_{0}}}^{{{\text{op}}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{0}_{k},\quad{\mathbf{sc_{1}}}^{{{\text{op}}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{1}_{k},\quad{\mathbf{sc_{2}}}^{{{\text{op}}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{2}_{k}

Proof.

Functoriality:
Given morphisms in
{\mathbf{sc_{1}}}
:

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z,\quad A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}C.

The maps on Stanley-Reisner rings are

k[z_{C}]/I_{Z}\rightarrow k[y_{B}]/I_{Y}\rightarrow k[x_{A}]/I_{X},\quad z_{c}\mapsto y^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{B_{c}}\mapsto\prod_{b\in B_{c}}x^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{A_{b}}.

But the latter is
x^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}_{A_{c}}
for the composition
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{2}\circ f_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}C
.

Given morphisms in
{\mathbf{sc_{2}}}
:

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z,\quad A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{2}}}{{\longleftarrow}}B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{1}}}{{\longleftarrow}}C.

The maps on Stanley-Reisner rings are

k[z_{C}]/I_{Z}\rightarrow k[y_{B}]/I_{Y}\rightarrow k[x_{A}]/I_{X},\quad z_{c}\mapsto y_{g_{1}(c)}\mapsto x_{g_{2}\circ g_{1}(c)},

which shows the functoriality.
The composition in the
{\mathbf{sc_{0}}}
-case is similar to the first.

Duality:

Let
\phi:k[x_{B}]/I_{2}\rightarrow k[x_{A}]/I_{1}
be
a homomorphism in one of the
{\text{\bf sqfRing}}_{k}
-categories,
and let

and

correspond to the
simplicial complexes
Y
and

respectively.

Let
\phi\in{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{1}_{k}
. If

is a non-face of
Y
,
since
\phi
is a homomorphism,
f^{-1}(C)
a non-face.
But then if
C\subseteq B
is such
that
f^{-1}(C)
is a face, then
C
must be face of
Y
. So

f^{**}(X)\subseteq Y
and we have a morphism in
{\mathbf{sc_{1}}}
.

If
\phi\in{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{2}_{k}
, then
when
C\subseteq B
is a nonface of
Y
,
g(C)
is a non-face of

X
. If

is a face of

, then

must be
face, since otherwise

is a non-face.
Thus we get a morphism

in
{\mathbf{sc_{2}}}
.

Now let
\phi\in{\text{\bf sqfRing}}^{0}_{k}
.
Let
D\subseteq A
be a face, and
C=f(D)\subseteq B
.
Then there is a section

of

.
So

and so

is
face. If

was a non-face then for every section

,

is a term in

and so

would be a non-face, giving
a contradiction. Thus

is a face and we have a morphism

in
{\mathbf{sc_{0}}}
.
∎
