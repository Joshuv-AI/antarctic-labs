Executive Summary of CRADA Work:
 • The engineering of microbes for biomanufacturing (e.g. of fuels, chemicals, materials)
 applications has advanced to a stage where researchers screen genetic libraries with millions of
 variations each for those with enhanced productivity. This screening, however, can be slow and
 expensive, as screening individual variants in a high-throughput yet cost-effective manner is
 challenging. In this project, we aimed to reduce by 3-fold costs associated with the sequencing

 aspects of the screening process (to determine which genetic variant is responsible for an
 observed change in productivity), while being able to process over 1,000 samples per batch.
 • As part of the project, we verified the compatibility of seqWell kits with our liquid-handling
 instruments, and provided suggestions for how the buffer delivery format could be further
 improved for high-throughput workflows. We established a SYBR Green-based PCR monitoring
 method, which allows researchers to assess amplification success and PCR efficiency. We
 successfully reduced library preparation reaction volumes by 87.5%, and combined with the
 successful development of a DNA extraction–free library preparation method, this translated
 into reductions in our publicly-funded research costs. Our analysis identified additional
 prospective opportunities for improvements that could further enhance sequencing results.

Summary of Research Results:
 • Phase 1, Task 1: System Compatibility Testing
 o The compatibilities of the sequencing and genome engineering reagents with the Echo
 and Mantis systems were evaluated. Compatible reagents and parameters for the Echo
 and Mantis systems were identified. Reagent transfer accuracy and reproducibility for
 high-throughput screening workflows were tested. It was confirmed that the dispensing
 systems can handle the volumes required for using the seqWell kits. System
 compatibility testing was completed and suggestions were provided to seqWell for
 improving their buffer delivery format for high-throughput workflows.
 • Phase 2, Task 1: Library Generation Testing
 o The seqWell kit was tested in the generation of libraries from E. coli strains carrying
 high-copy-number plasmids without requiring DNA extraction. Initial library quality and
 sequencing compatibility was assessed by conducting pilot tests using the seqWell kit
 as-is. The seqWell kit was used to generate Illumina libraries without DNA extraction or
 purification, and conditions were optimized as necessary. The library quality from DNA-
 extraction-free material was determined.
 o The results indicated that the seqWell kit generated different library yields for each
 sample (Figure 1).
 Figure 1: Cq values and RFU values from the library preparation of different bacteria
 strains showed significant differences (n=24 for each
 strain).

• Phase 2, Task 2: Collaborative Optimization
 o A PCR monitoring method using SYBR Green was established, allowing researchers to
 assess amplification success and PCR efficiency. Library preparation reaction volumes
 were successfully reduced by 87.5%. A DNA extraction–free library preparation method
 was developed, and the resulting libraries were successfully sequenced on the MiSeq
 platform. Data analysis indicated that the seqWell team may want to further optimize
 the enzyme and buffer composition to achieve more even coverage and consistent
 library yields across samples. For further improvement, the ABF team would need to
 perform additional R&D for the library preparation method to reduce fragmentation size
 towards improved sequencing results. It was not required to modify or optimize kit
 components to improve performance without DNA extraction, but the seqWell team did
 modify their kit to make it more compatible with high throughput workflows.
• Phase 2, Task 3: Data analysis
 o Sequencing data was analyzed and evaluated. The results indicated that > 50 % of the
 reads were mapped to the plasmid references (Figure 2), suggesting that the seqWell kit
 can replace the Illumina Nextera Kit for high throughput plasmid validation.
 Figure 2: Sequencing reads mapped to the plasmid reference.

• Phase 3, Task 1: Whole Genome Sequencing Workflow Testing
 o The seqWell kit was used in high-throughput format to generate whole genome
 sequencing libraries. Sequencing efforts were determined based on the genome size.
 Sequencing results were evaluated for sequencing bias potentially due to the
 fragmentation process and bias potentially due to the design of the seqWell technology.
 High-throughput sequencing without DNA extraction was successfully demonstrated. An
 optimized high-throughput protocol suitable for large-scale workflows to screen
 insertion location in the genome was developed.
• Phase 3, Task 2: Condition Optimization
 o Reaction conditions were adjusted and optimized to improve sequencing accuracy and
 consistency. We successfully reduced the reaction volume from 16 uL to 2 uL. Reads
 were successfully mapped to the E. coli genome (Figure 3). Additional R&D from both
 ABF and seqWell team would be necessary to further improve data quality.

 Figure 3: Sequencing reads mapped to the bacterial
 genome.

• Phase 3, Task 3: seqWell Kit Performance Evaluation
 o Index hopping, fragmentation and pooling biases, and the maximum sample capacity of
 the seqWell kit, were evaluated. Index hopping was observed but the frequency was
 low.
• Phase 4, Task 1: Targeted Region Pull-Down Protocol and Task 2: Data analysis
 o Time and resources did not permit efforts supporting these tasks.
 o While they were not pursued, what was originally proposed was to develop a protocol
 to selectively enrich targeted regions for insertion verification, and collaborate on the
 potential development of a new kit specifically for targeted pull-down applications. The
 resulting data was to be sequenced and analyzed.
 o Should resources and mutual interest again arise, these tasks could be re-instated in
 future projects.
