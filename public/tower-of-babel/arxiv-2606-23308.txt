Kohn-–Luttinger like superconductivity in twisted bilayer graphene at large twist angles
A.O. Sboychakov

Abstract
We predict that twisted bilayer graphene with large twist angle and small superlattice cell can be superconducting. Such a bilayer graphene can have a gap in the spectrum. This gap appears due to the hybridization of electrons moving in different layers with Fermi momenta close to the Dirac points which are equivalent to each other in the superlattice Brillouin zone. Small doping of the bilayer introduces charge carries having large density of states. We show that the screened Coulomb interaction is enough to stabilize superconducting state in the material. The symmetry of the order parameter is of the d d -wave type. Application of the bias voltage increases the superconducting transition temperature. For realistic values of the model parameters the transition temperature can be as large as several hundreds of milikelvin.



I
Introduction

Discovery of the superconductivity in magic angle twisted bilayer graphene
Cao et al. 2018
(MAtBLG) in 2018 stimulated the search of the superconducting states in different types of the graphene-based materials. Besides MAtBLG
Lu et al. 2019
, in recent years the superconductivity has been observed in non-moiré graphene multi-layers, such as Bernal stacked bilayer graphene
Zhou et al. 2022
, rhombohedral trilayer
Zhou et al. 2021
, tetralayer
Choi et al. 2025
;
Han et al. 2025
, pentalayer
Han et al. 2025
, and even hexalayer
Morissette et al. 2025

In non-moiré graphene multi-layers the large density of states is achieved by shifting the chemical potential close to the low-energy van Hove singularity existed in this materials. The large density of states favors the transition to the superconducting state with experimentally observable values. However, the transition temperatures in graphene-based materials are quite small. In MAtBLG the transition temperature is about
1.7
K, in Bernal bilayer graphene it is about
26

In this paper we consider one more candidate to the list of graphene-based material where the superconductivity is observed. Namely, we study twisted bilayer graphene (tBLG) with large twist angle
\theta
and small superlattice cell (LAtBLG). The low-energy spectrum of the tBLG depends substantially on the twist angle (for details, see review paper, Ref.
Rozhkov et al. 2016

\theta\cong 1^{\circ}

\theta

Mele 2010
;
Shallcross et al. 2008
;
Rozhkov et al. 2017
. A small doping (when chemical potential is closed to the band edge) of gapped LAtBLG introduces extra charge carries into the system making the material a metal with large density of states. This leads to the large screening of the Coulomb interaction at small transfer momenta. We show that in such a situation the screened Coulomb interaction is enough to stabilize the superconducting state in LAtBLG. The leading instability is the spin-singlet inter-valley superconducting order parameter of the
d
-wave type. However, the spin-singlet and spin-triplet inter-valley as well as spin-triplet intra-valley superconducting states are almost three-fold degenerate. We show also that applied bias voltage increases the transition temperature. We predict that for experimentally reachable doping concentrations

and displacement fields

n\sim 0.2\cdot 10^{-12}
cm

,
D\sim 0.5

II
Single-particle Hamiltonian of LAtBLG

Before proceed to the main part of this Section let us recap several basic facts about the geometry of the tBLG important for further consideration (for more details, see, e.g., review paper Ref.
Rozhkov et al. 2016
). In twisted bilayer graphene, the top layer
2
is rotated relative to the bottom layer
1
by an angle
\theta
. This rotation breaks the translation symmetry on the lattice period. On the same time, for commensurate twist angles satisfying the equation
Rozhkov et al. 2016

\cos\theta=(3m_{0}^{2}+3m_{0}r+r^{2}/2)(3m_{0}^{2}+3m_{0}r+r^{2})
, where
m_{0}
and
r
are mutually prime integers, the system has a superstructure. The number of unit cells of each graphene layer in the supercell is
N_{sc}=(3m_{0}^{2}+3m_{0}r+r^{2})/g
, where
g=1
if
r\neq 3n
(
n
is an integer) or
g=3
otherwise. The number of carbon atoms in the supercell is
4N_{sc}
. In this paper, for the reasons described below, we consider the case
r\neq 3n
.

The Brillouin zone of each graphene layer is of the hexagonal shape. The Brillouin zone of layer
2
is rotated relative to the Brillouin zone of layer
1
by angle
\theta
around the
\Gamma
point. The Brillouin zone of the superlattice also has a shape of hexagon, but smaller in size. Two nonequivalent Dirac points of layer
1
are equal to
\mathbf{K}^{1}_{+1}=\{4\pi/(3a),\,0\}
and
\mathbf{K}^{1}_{-1}=-\mathbf{K}^{1}_{+1}
, where
a=2.46
Å is the graphene lattice constant. Nonequivalent Dirac points of layer
2
, which we denote as
\mathbf{K}^{2}_{\xi}
(
\xi=\pm 1
), are obtained from the points
\mathbf{K}^{1}_{-\xi}
by the rotation on the angle
\theta
. For commensurate
\theta
, the Dirac points
\mathbf{K}^{1}_{\xi}
,
\mathbf{K}^{2}_{\xi}
are pairwise equivalent, i.e. they differ from each other by some vector
\mathbf{G}
of the reciprocal superlattice. For superstructures with
r\neq 3n
, considered in this paper, we have
\mathbf{K}^{1}_{\xi}\sim\mathbf{K}^{2}_{\xi}\sim\mathbf{k}_{\xi}
, where
\mathbf{k}_{\xi}
are nonequivalent Dirac points of the superlattice Brillouin zone.

Let us consider now the single-particle electronic properties of tBLG at large twist angles (the condition for the angle whcih can be considered to be large will be given in the Discussion section). At low energies electrons in graphene have momenta close to the Dirac points. Neglecting the interlayer hopping, electrons in each layer are described by the massless Dirac equation. Let’s consider an electron moving in layer
1
with momentum
\mathbf{K}_{\xi}^{1}+\mathbf{p}_{1}
, and an electron moving in layer
2
with momentum
\mathbf{K}_{\xi}^{2}+\mathbf{p}_{2}
, where
\mathbf{p}_{1}
and
\mathbf{p}_{2}
are small enough. We will call such electrons as belonging to valley
\xi
. Since Dirac points
\mathbf{K}_{\xi}^{1}
and
\mathbf{K}_{\xi}^{2}
are equivalent to each other, there exists a nonzero matrix element of the electron hopping from layer
1
to layer
2
, with
\mathbf{p}_{1}=\mathbf{p}_{2}\equiv\mathbf{p}
. In the approach, first proposed by E.J. Mele in Ref.
Mele 2010
, only such interlayer hoppings are taken into account. Let
d^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}i\alpha\sigma}
(
d^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\mathbf{k}i\alpha\sigma}
) being the creation (annihilation) operator of the electron with momentum
\mathbf{k}
(measured from
\bm{\Gamma}
point) in the layer
i
(
=1,\,2
) in the sublattice
\alpha
(
=A,\,B
) with spin projection
\sigma
. Creation (annihilation) operators, describing the electrons in valley
\xi
are equal to
\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}=d^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{K}^{i}_{\xi}+\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}
(
\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}=d^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\mathbf{K}^{i}_{\xi}+\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}
). Next, we introduce four component spinor

\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}\sigma}=(\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}1A\sigma},\,\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}1B\sigma},\,\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}2A\sigma},\,\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}2B\sigma}).

(1)

We also denote
\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{+1\mathbf{p}\sigma}
as
\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{p}\sigma}
. Taking all aforementioned into account, we can write the low-energy single-particle Hamiltonian of the LAtBLG in the form

H_{0}=\sum_{\xi\mathbf{p}\sigma}\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}\sigma}\left(\hat{H}_{\xi\mathbf{p}}-\mu\right)\Psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}\sigma},

(2)

where
\mu
is the chemical potential. The
4\times 4
matrix
\hat{H}_{+1\mathbf{p}}
for valley
\xi=+1
(which we call also
\hat{H}_{\mathbf{p}}
) can be written in the block-matrix form as
Mele 2010

\hat{H}_{+1\mathbf{p}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{\rm F}\bm{\sigma}\mathbf{p}+\frac{V}{2}&\hat{T}^{\prime}_{12}\\
\hat{T}_{12}^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}}&-v_{\rm F}\bm{\sigma}_{\theta}^{*}\mathbf{p}-\frac{V}{2}\end{array}\right)\equiv\hat{H}_{\mathbf{p}}.

(3)

In this expression,
v_{\rm F}
is the Fermi velocity of electrons in graphene,
\bm{\sigma}
are the Pauli matrices acting in the sublattice space, while
\bm{\sigma}_{\theta}
are the “rotated” Pauli matrices defined as

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}_{\theta}=e^{i\sigma_{z}\theta/2}\bm{\sigma}e^{-i\sigma_{z}\theta/2}.

(4)

In Eq. (
3
) we also take into account the biased voltage,
V/e
, applied between two layers. The Hamiltonian (
3
) is written for valley
\xi=+1
. Since
\mathbf{K}^{i}_{-1}=-\mathbf{K}^{i}_{+1}
, due to time-reversal symmetry, we have

\hat{H}_{-1\mathbf{p}}=\hat{H}^{*}_{+1-\mathbf{p}}\,.

(5)

The
2\times 2
matrix
\hat{T}^{\prime}_{12}
in Eq. (
3
) describes the interlayer hopping. In Ref.
Mele 2010
within the framework of the continuum model, it was shown from symmetry considerations that for the structures with
r\neq 3n
, the matrix
\hat{T}^{\prime}_{12}
should have the form

\hat{T}^{\prime}_{12}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&me^{i\phi^{\prime}}\\
me^{i\psi^{\prime}}&0\end{array}\right),

(6)

where
m
,
\phi^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime}
are real parameters that depend on the superstructure. Numerical calculations of the elements of the matrix
\hat{T}^{\prime}_{12}
in the framework of the tight-binding model, performed in Ref.
Rozhkov et al. 2017
for different superstructures, fully confirmed
Not
the formula (
6
).

For further consideration it will be convenient for us to perform two subsequent unitary transformations
\Psi_{\xi\mathbf{p}\sigma}\to\hat{U}_{\xi}\Psi_{\xi\mathbf{p}\sigma}
, where
\hat{U}_{\xi}=\hat{U}_{2}\hat{U}_{1\xi}
, and matrices
\hat{U}_{1\xi}
and
U_{2}
have the form

\hat{U}_{1\xi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sigma_{0}&0\\
0&e^{-i\xi\sigma_{z}\theta/2}\end{array}\right),

(7)

\hat{U}_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sigma_{0}e^{i\frac{\phi^{\prime}+\psi^{\prime}}{4}}&0\\
0&\sigma_{0}e^{-i\frac{\phi^{\prime}+\psi^{\prime}}{4}}\end{array}\right).

(8)

In the latter expression
\sigma_{0}
is identity
2\times 2
matrix. After such a unitary transformation, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{+1\mathbf{p}}
takes the form

\hat{H}_{+1\mathbf{p}}\to\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}_{\xi}\hat{H}_{+1\mathbf{p}}\hat{U}_{\xi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{\rm F}\bm{\sigma}\mathbf{p}+\frac{V}{2}&\hat{T}_{12}\\
\hat{T}_{12}^{{\dagger}}&-v_{\rm F}\bm{\sigma}^{*}\mathbf{p}-\frac{V}{2}\end{array}\right),

(9)

where

\hat{T}_{12}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\Delta\\
\Delta^{*}&0\end{array}\right),\;\;\Delta=me^{i\phi},\;\;\phi=\frac{\phi^{\prime}-\psi^{\prime}+\theta}{2}\,.

(10)

After these unitary transformations, the matrix
\hat{H}_{-1\mathbf{p}}
still satisfies Eq. (
5
).

The eigenvalues,
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{p}}^{(S)}
(
S=1,\,\dots,\,4
), of the Hamiltonian (
9
) can be calculated analytically. The energy spectrum is given by the expressions

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathbf{p}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\Delta_{R}^{2}+\left(v_{\rm F}p+\Delta_{V}\right)^{2}}=-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{p}}^{(4)},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathbf{p}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\Delta_{R}^{2}+\left(v_{\rm F}p-\Delta_{V}\right)^{2}}=-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{p}}^{(3)},

where

In this paper we study slightly doped LAtBLG. Namely, we consider the case, when the chemical potential
\mu
crosses the band
S=3
leaving the band
S=4
empty of charge carriers. This happens when
\mu
lays in the range
\Delta_{R}<\mu<|m|
forming two circular Fermi surface sheets (in each valley) with opposite Fermi velocities. Fermi momenta of these sheets are equal to

p_{\text{F}1,2}=\frac{1}{v_{\text{F}}}\left(\Delta_{V}\mp\sqrt{\mu^{2}-\Delta_{R}^{2}}\right)\,.

(12)

The doping level per one cite,
x
, is given by the following relation

x=2\pi\!\!\int\limits_{p_{\text{F}1}}^{p_{\text{F}2}}\!\!\frac{pdp}{v_{\text{BZ}}}=\frac{4\pi\Delta_{V}}{v_{\text{BZ}}v_{\text{F}}^{2}}\sqrt{\mu^{2}-\Delta_{R}^{2}}\,,

(13)

where
v_{\text{BZ}}=8\pi^{2}/(a^{2}\sqrt{3})
is the graphene’s Brillouin zone area. The extra electron density is
n=4x/v_{c}
, where
v_{c}=a^{2}\sqrt{3}/2
is the graphene’s unit cell area. The factor
4
in the latter formula comes from the fact that the unit cell of each graphene layer contains two cites. The density of states (per one cite) is equal to

\rho(E)=\frac{4\pi\Delta_{V}E}{v_{\text{BZ}}v_{\text{F}}^{2}\sqrt{E^{2}-\Delta_{R}^{2}}}\,.

(14)

The density of states diverges when
E
tends to the band edge. In the next Section we will show that this leads to the large screening of the Coulomb interaction at small transfer momenta, which, in turn, can stabilize the superconducting state.

III
Polarization operator and screened Coulomb interaction

We add now a term describing the electron-electron interaction to the full Hamiltonian of the system. In this paper, we take into account only the long-range Coulomb repulsion. The interaction Hamiltonian has the form

H_{\text{int}}=\frac{1}{2\cal N}\!\sum_{\mathbf{kk}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}}\sum_{ij\alpha\beta\atop\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}d^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}i\alpha\sigma}d^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\mathbf{k}i\alpha\sigma}V^{(0)ij}_{\mathbf{q}}d^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}d^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}},

(15)

where
{\cal N}
is the number of unit cells of each graphene layer in the sample. The function
V^{(0)ij}_{\mathbf{q}}
is the Fourier transformed bare Coulomb interaction, where we distinguish intra-layer and inter-layer interaction. For small transfer momentum the function
V^{(0)ij}_{\mathbf{q}}
can be written in the matrix form as

\hat{V}^{(0)}_{\mathbf{q}}=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\epsilon q}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&e^{-qd}\\
e^{-qd}&1\end{array}\right),

(16)

where
d=3.35
Å is the inter-layer distance and
\epsilon
is the dielectric constant of the media surrounded the graphene sample. Since the doped LAtBLG is a metal with large density of state, the Coulomb interaction at small transfer momenta experiences a large screening. We calculate the screened Coulomb interaction within RPA. It is commonly believed that for graphene-based systems the RPA is appropriate approach due to the large degeneracy factor
N_{d}=4
coming from spin and valley degeneracies. The low-energy Coulomb interaction can be rewritten in terms of
\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}
operators as

\displaystyle H_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\cal N}\!\sum_{\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}}\sum_{ij\alpha\beta\atop\xi\xi^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}i\alpha\sigma}\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}V^{ij}_{\mathbf{q}}\times

(17)

\displaystyle\times\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi^{\prime}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\xi^{\prime}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}},

where
V^{ij}_{\mathbf{q}}
is the screened Coulomb interaction. The unitary transformations (
7
) and (
8
) do not change the form of the interaction Hamiltonian (
17
). The screened Coulomb interaction is found from the matrix equation

\hat{V}_{\mathbf{q}}=\left[\left(\hat{V}^{(0)}_{\mathbf{q}}\right)^{-1}-\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{q}}\right]^{-1}\!\!\!\!,

(18)

where
\Pi^{ij}_{\mathbf{q}}
is the static polarization operator of the bilayer. It can be written as

\displaystyle\Pi^{ij}_{\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{d}\sum_{SS^{\prime}}\int\!\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{n_{F}\left(\varepsilon^{(S)}_{\mathbf{p}}\right)-n_{F}\left(\varepsilon^{(S^{\prime})}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}}\right)}{\varepsilon^{(S)}_{\mathbf{p}}-\varepsilon^{(S^{\prime})}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}}}\times

(19)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i\alpha}\Phi^{(S)}_{+1\mathbf{p}i\alpha}\Phi^{(S^{\prime})*}_{+1\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}i\alpha}\Big)\Big(\sum_{j\beta}\Phi^{(S)*}_{+1\mathbf{p}j\beta}\Phi^{(S^{\prime})}_{+1\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}j\beta}\Big),

where
n_{F}(E)
is the Fermi function and
\Phi^{(S)}_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha}
are the eigenfunctions of the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\xi\mathbf{p}}
. We also denote
\Phi^{(S)}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha}=\Phi^{(S)}_{+1\mathbf{p}i\alpha}
. From Eq. (
5
) it follows that

\Phi^{(S)}_{-1\mathbf{p}i\alpha}=\Phi^{(S)*}_{-\mathbf{p}i\alpha}.

(20)

The analytical expressions for the wave functions
\Phi^{(S)}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha}
at non-zero
V
are unknown for us. For this reason, we evaluate them numerically while calculating polarization operator.

\mathbf{p}

), is shown in Fig.

IV
Inter-valley superconductivity

IV.1
Inter-valley interaction in Cooper channel

In this Section we consider the inter-valley superconducting pairing. For such type of order, some of the expectation values of the type

\langle\psi_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}\psi_{\bar{\xi}-\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle
are non-zero (here
\bar{\xi}=-\xi
). Note that for
i=j
the total momentum of such a pair of electrons is zero, while for different layers it is equal to
K^{i}_{\xi}-K^{j}_{\xi}
, which is the certain reciprocal superlattice vector. From Eq. (
15
) one can show that the interaction Hamiltonian, responsible for generation of such type of pairs, is equal to

H_{\text{int}}^{\text{inter}}=\frac{1}{2\cal N}\!\sum_{\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}}\sum_{ij\alpha\beta\atop\xi\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{\xi}-\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}V^{ij}_{\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\bar{\xi}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}^{\prime}i\alpha\sigma}+\frac{1}{2\cal N}\!\sum_{\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}}\sum_{i\alpha\beta\atop\xi\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{\xi}-\mathbf{p}i\beta\sigma^{\prime}}V^{ii}_{2\mathbf{K}^{i}_{\xi}+\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\xi-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}i\beta\sigma^{\prime}}\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\bar{\xi}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}i\alpha\sigma}.

(21)

The second term in this formula includes only intra-layer interaction due to the momentum conservation low. The transfer momentum in the second term is much larger than that of the first one. Since
V^{ij}_{\mathbf{q}}
decreases at large transfer momentum, the contribution to the total interaction from the last term in Eq. (
21
) will be much smaller than that from the first term. For this reason, we omit the last term of Eq. (
21
) (the role of this term will be discussed below).

For further consideration it is convenient to introduce new electronic operators,
a_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}=\psi_{+1\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}
and
b_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}=\psi_{-1\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}
. In terms of these operators, the total Hamiltonian can be written as

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\sigma}\sum_{ij\alpha\beta}\left[a^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}\left(H^{i\alpha;j\beta}_{\mathbf{p}}-\mu\right)a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma}\right.-

(22)

\displaystyle-\left.b^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{-\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}\left(H^{i\alpha;j\beta}_{\mathbf{p}}-\mu\right)b^{{\dagger}}_{-\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma}\right]-8\mu{\cal N}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\cal N}\sum_{\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\sum_{ij\alpha\beta}a^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}b^{{\dagger}}_{-\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}V^{ij}_{\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}b^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}i\alpha\sigma}\,,

where
H^{i\alpha;j\beta}_{\mathbf{p}}=(\hat{H}_{\mathbf{p}})^{i\alpha;j\beta}
. Deriving this equation we took into account the symmetry relation (
5
) and the fact that
V^{ij}_{-\mathbf{q}}=V^{ji}_{\mathbf{q}}
.

IV.2
Self-consistency equations for the order parameter

As a next step we introduce normal and anomalous Matsubara Green’s functions

\displaystyle\left[\hat{G}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime},\,\mathbf{p})\right]^{i\alpha;j\beta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle G^{i\alpha;j\beta}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime},\,\mathbf{p})=

\displaystyle-\left\langle T_{\tau}a_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}(\tau)\bar{a}_{\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\right\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{F}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime},\,\mathbf{p})\right]^{i\alpha;j\beta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle F^{i\alpha;j\beta}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime},\,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle-\left\langle T_{\tau}\bar{b}_{-\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}(\tau)\bar{a}_{\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\right\rangle\,,

where
a_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}(\tau)
[
b_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}(\tau)
] and
\bar{a}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}(\tau)
[
\bar{b}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}(\tau)
] are the Matsubara electronic operators corresponding to
a_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}
(
b_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}
) and
a^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}
(
b^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma})
, respectively.

The spin structure of anomalous Green’s function is different for spin-singlet and spin-triplet superconducting states. Let us consider first the spin-singlet state. In this case we have
\hat{G}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\,\mathbf{p})=\hat{G}(\tau,\,\mathbf{p})\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
,
\hat{F}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\,\mathbf{p})=\hat{F}(\tau,\,\mathbf{p})[i\sigma_{y}]_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
. Next we write down the Gor’kov’s equations for the matrix functions
\hat{G}
and
\hat{F}
. From Hamiltonian (
22
), in frequency representation, we derive

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(i\omega_{n}-\hat{H}_{\mathbf{p}}+\mu\right)\hat{G}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})-\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}^{+}\,\hat{F}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})=1\,,\\
\left(i\omega_{n}+\hat{H}_{\mathbf{p}}-\mu\right)\hat{F}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})-\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}\,\hat{G}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})=0\,,\end{array}\right.

(24)

where the order parameter is equal to

\left[\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}\right]^{i\alpha;j\beta}\equiv\Delta_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}=\frac{T}{\cal N}\sum_{n}\sum_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}V^{ij}_{\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}F^{i\alpha;j\beta}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,.

(25)

Let us notice, that since the momentum
\mathbf{p}
is counted from the Dirac point and not from
\Gamma
point, there is no any restriction for the inter-valley order parameter connecting
\Delta_{-\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}
and
\Delta_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}
. Equations (
24
) and (
25
) form a closed system of equations for finding the superconducting order parameter and superconducting transition temperature,
T_{c}
. We solve these equations in the limit
T\to T_{c}
. In this case, the equation for the order parameter can be linearized. From second equation in (
24
) we can write

\hat{F}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})\cong-\hat{G}_{0}(-i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}\hat{G}_{0}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})\,,

(26)

where

\hat{G}_{0}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})=\left[i\omega_{n}+\mu-\hat{H}_{\mathbf{p}}\right]^{-1}

(27)

is the Green’s function of the normal state. As a result, the self-consistency equation (
25
) becomes

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{T}{\cal N}\sum_{n}\sum_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\sum_{lm\mu\nu}V^{ij}_{\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}G_{0}^{i\alpha;l\mu}(-i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\times

(28)

\displaystyle\Delta^{l\mu;m\nu}_{\mathbf{p^{\prime}}}G_{0}^{m\nu;j\beta}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,.

To go further we will use the technique described, e.g., in Ref.
Cea et al. 2022
. Namely, we write down the normal Green’s function in the form

G^{i\alpha;j\beta}_{0}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})=\sum_{S=1}^{4}\frac{\Phi^{(S)}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha}\Phi^{(S)*}_{\mathbf{p}j\beta}}{i\omega_{n}+\mu-\varepsilon^{(S)}_{\mathbf{p}}}\,.

(29)

We also proceed from the order parameter defined in the layer-sublattice space to that defined in the band space. It is equal to

\Delta_{SS^{\prime}\mathbf{p}}=\sum_{ij\alpha\beta}\Phi^{(S)*}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha}\Delta_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}\Phi^{(S^{\prime})}_{\mathbf{p}j\beta}.

(30)

Using Eqs. (
29
) and (
30
) and performing the frequency summation in Eq. (
28
), we obtain the linearized equation for the order parameter
\Delta_{SS^{\prime}\mathbf{p}}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{S_{1}S_{2}\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\Gamma^{(S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime})}_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\Delta_{S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\times

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{n_{F}\left(-\varepsilon^{S_{1}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right)-n_{F}\left(\varepsilon^{S_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right)}{\varepsilon^{S_{1}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}+\varepsilon^{S_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}}\,,

where

\Gamma^{(S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime})}_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}=\sum_{ij\alpha\beta}\Phi^{(S_{1})*}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha}\Phi^{(S_{1}^{\prime})}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}i\alpha}V^{ij}_{\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\Phi^{(S_{2})}_{\mathbf{p}j\beta}\Phi^{(S_{2}^{\prime})*}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}j\beta}\,.

(32)

Equation (
31
) can be simplified if we observe that due to the symmetry of the problem the following relation holds true:

\Gamma^{(S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime})}_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}=\Gamma^{(S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime})}(p,\,p^{\prime},\,\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}-\varphi_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}})\,,

(33)

where
\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}
(
\varphi_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
) is the polar angle of the vector
\mathbf{p}
(
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
). As a result, we can seek a solution to Eq. (
31
) in the form
\Delta_{S_{1}S_{2}\mathbf{p}}=e^{-i\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}\ell}\Delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2}p}
, where
\ell=0,\,\pm 1,\dots
. For the reason described above we can consider both odd and even
\ell
for the spin-singlet (and spin-triplet) inter-valley order parameter. We also introduce the following quantity:
\delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2}p}=\Delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2}p}f_{S_{1}S_{2}p}
, where

f_{S_{1}S_{2}p}=\sqrt{p\frac{n_{F}\left(-\varepsilon^{S_{1}}_{\mathbf{p}}\right)-n_{F}\left(\varepsilon^{S_{2}}_{\mathbf{p}}\right)}{\varepsilon^{S_{1}}_{\mathbf{p}}+\varepsilon^{S_{2}}_{\mathbf{p}}}}\,.

(34)

The equation for
\delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2}p}
is

\delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2}p}=\sum_{S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!dp\,w^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}}(p,\,p^{\prime})\delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}p^{\prime}}\,,

(35)

where the kernel
w^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}}(p,\,p^{\prime})
is

\displaystyle w^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}}(p,\,p^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{S_{1}S_{2}p}f_{S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}p^{\prime}}\times

(36)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Gamma^{(S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime})}(p,\,p^{\prime},\,\varphi)e^{i\varphi\ell}\,.

Kernel
w^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}}(p,\,p^{\prime})
is hermitian one and its eigenvalues are real. For given
\ell
the transition temperature
T^{(\ell)}_{c}
is found as temperature when the largest eigenvalue of
w^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}}(p,\,p^{\prime})
is equal to
1
. Corresponding eigenfunction gives the momentum dependence of the order parameter. From Eqs. (
32
) and (
36
) it follows that

w^{(-\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2};S_{1}^{\prime}S_{2}^{\prime}}(p,\,p^{\prime})=w^{(\ell)}_{S_{2}S_{1};S_{2}^{\prime}S_{1}^{\prime}}(p,\,p^{\prime})^{*}.

(37)

From this relation, we obtain
T^{(-\ell)}_{c}=T^{(\ell)}_{c}
and
\Delta^{(-\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2}p}=\Delta^{(\ell)*}_{S_{2}S_{1}p}
. Thus, we can consider only positive
\ell
(the state with
\ell=0
is absolutely unstable since interaction is repulsive). The components of the order parameter
\Delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2}p}
, responsible to the gap opening at the Fermi level, are equal to
\Delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{0}S_{0}p}
, where
S_{0}=3
. For given
\ell>0
, the order parameter, which opens the gap at the Fermi level, is

\Delta^{(\ell)}_{\mathbf{p}}=2\left|\Delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{0}S_{0}p}\right|\cos(\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}\ell-\chi^{(\ell)}_{p})\,,

(38)

where
\chi^{(\ell)}_{p}=\arg(\Delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{0}S_{0}p})
. The order parameter
\Delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{1}S_{2}p}
is defined up to arbitrary phase. We define it such that
\chi^{(\ell)}_{p_{0}}=0
, where
p_{0}
corresponds to the maximum of
|\Delta^{(\ell)}_{S_{0}S_{0}p}|
.

At the end of this Section let us consider the triplet state. In this case the anomalous Green’s function has the form
\hat{F}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\,\mathbf{p})=\hat{\mathbf{F}}(\tau,\,\mathbf{p})[i\sigma_{y}\bm{\sigma}]_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
, where

V
Intra-valley superconductivity

Besides inter-valley superconducting state, the intra-valley one is also possible in our model. In this state the Cooper pairs are located in the same valley in the momentum space. Thus, in the intra-valley superconducting state some of the following expectation values are non-zero:

\langle\psi_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}\psi_{\xi-\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle
. The total momentum of the pair is equal to
K^{i}_{\xi}+K^{j}_{\xi}
. It is non-zero for any
i
and
j
. Thus, the intra-valley superconducting state is a kind of pair density wave. The interaction Hamiltonian, responsible for the formation of such type of pairs, is:

\displaystyle H_{\text{int}}^{\text{intra}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\cal N}\!\sum_{\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}}\sum_{ij\alpha\beta\atop\xi\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi-\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}V^{ij}_{\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\times

(39)

\displaystyle\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\xi-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}\psi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\xi\mathbf{p}^{\prime}i\alpha\sigma}\,.

This interaction Hamiltonian contains only terms coupling electrons in the same valley. The term, describing the inter-valley coupling [analog of the second term in Eq. (
21
)] is absent due to the momentum conservation low. In this case, the total Hamiltonian splits into to terms,
H=\sum_{\xi}H_{\xi}
. Let us consider the pairing in the valley
\xi=+1
(the pairing in another valley is studied in similar manner). In terms of operators
a_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}=\psi_{+1\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}
, the Hamiltonian
H_{+1}
can be written as

\displaystyle H_{+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{p}\sigma}\sum_{ij\alpha\beta}\left[a^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}\left(H^{i\alpha;j\beta}_{\mathbf{p}}-\mu\right)a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma}\right.-

(40)

\displaystyle-\left.a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{-\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}\left(H^{i\alpha;j\beta*}_{-\mathbf{p}}-\mu\right)a^{{\dagger}}_{-\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma}\right]-4\mu{\cal N}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\cal N}\sum_{\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\sum_{ij\alpha\beta}a^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}a^{{\dagger}}_{-\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}V^{ij}_{\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}i\alpha\sigma}\,.

We introduce the anomalous Green’s function for the intra-valley pairing:

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\tilde{F}}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime},\,\mathbf{p})\right]^{i\alpha;j\beta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\tilde{F}^{i\alpha;j\beta}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime},\,\mathbf{p})

(41)

\displaystyle-\left\langle T_{\tau}\bar{a}_{-\mathbf{p}i\alpha\sigma}(\tau)\bar{a}_{\mathbf{p}j\beta\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\right\rangle\,.

The Gor’kov’s equations now read [c.f. with Eq. (
24
)]:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(i\omega_{n}-\hat{H}_{\mathbf{p}}+\mu\right)\hat{G}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})-\\
-\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\hat{\tilde{\Delta}}_{\mathbf{p}\sigma\sigma^{\prime\prime}}^{+}\,\hat{\tilde{F}}_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\,,\\
\left(i\omega_{n}+\hat{H}^{*}_{-\mathbf{p}}-\mu\right)\hat{\tilde{F}}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})-\\
-\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\hat{\tilde{\Delta}}_{\mathbf{p}\sigma\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\,\hat{G}_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p})=0\,,\end{array}\right.

(42)

where the intra-valley order parameter is equal to

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\tilde{\Delta}}_{\mathbf{p}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\right]^{i\alpha;j\beta}\equiv\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}=

\displaystyle\frac{T}{\cal N}\sum_{n}\sum_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}V^{ij}_{\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\tilde{F}^{i\alpha;j\beta}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(i\omega_{n},\,\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,.

(43)

For the spin-singlet state we have
\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}=\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}[i\sigma_{y}]_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
. In contrast to the inter-valley case, the
\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}
should satisfy the relation

\tilde{\Delta}_{-\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}=\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}^{j\beta;i\alpha}\,.

(44)

In spin-triplet state the order parameter can be written in the form
\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}=\tilde{\bm{\Delta}}_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}[i\bm{\sigma}\sigma_{y}]_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
. Below we assume the following ansatz for spin-triplet order parameter:
\tilde{\bm{\Delta}}_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}=\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}\mathbf{n}
, where
\mathbf{n}
is arbitrary unit vector. The order parameter
\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}
in the latter formula should satisfy the equation

\tilde{\Delta}_{-\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}=-\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}^{j\beta;i\alpha}\,.

(45)

Thus, for the ansatz chosen, both spin singlet and spin-triplet intra-valley states can be considered in similar manner when accounting for different symmetry relations.

We also introduce the order parameter in the band space according to [c.f. with Eq. (
30
)]

\tilde{\Delta}_{SS^{\prime}\mathbf{p}}=\sum_{ij\alpha\beta}\Phi^{(S)}_{-\mathbf{p}i\alpha}\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}^{i\alpha;j\beta}\Phi^{(S^{\prime})}_{\mathbf{p}j\beta}.

(46)

It should satisfy the relation

VI
Discussion and conclusions

VI.1
Comparison with other graphene bilayers

Thus, our calculations demonstrate that the biased and slightly doped LAtBLG should be superconducting. The transition temperature can be as large as several hundreds of milikelvin. This is several times smaller than the transition temperature of the magic angle twisted bilayer graphene
Cao et al. 2018
;
Lu et al. 2019
,
T_{c}=1.7
K. At the same time it is by one order of magnitude larger that of the AB stacked bilayer graphene
Zhou et al. 2022
,
T_{c}=26
mK. Our calculations showed that in order to obtain an experimentally observable transition temperature, the bias voltage between two graphene layers should be applied. The larger bias voltage is the larger is the transition temperature. This is because Fermi momenta are larger for large bias voltage [see Eq. (
12
)]. In our calculations the largest bias voltage used is
V/e=0.22
V, which corresponds to the displacement field
D=0.64
V/nm. This value is experimentally achievable. For example, the phase diagram shown in Fig. 1 of Ref.
Zhou et al. 2022
presents data up to about
D=1
V/nm. Note, however, that we do not take into account the effect of screening of the bias voltage due to the inter-layer long-range Coulomb repulsion. This effect renormalizes parameter
V
making it smaller than the external bias.

Our calculations show that at largest bias voltage used the transition temperature becomes negligible at doping concentration
n_{c}\approx 0.44\times 10^{12}
cm
-2
. The superconductivity is observable at smaller dopings. These values of doping are several times smaller than the characteristic doping concentration
n\approx 1.5\times 10^{12}
cm
-2
, where the superconductivity was observed in magic angle twisted bilayer graphene (see, e.g., Fig. 2 of Ref.
Cao et al. 2018
). However,
n_{c}
is comparable to the doping concentration
n\approx 0.57\times 10^{12}
cm
-2
, where the superconductivity was observed in AB stacked bilayer graphene (see, e.g., Fig. 2 of Ref.
Zhou et al. 2022
).

Thus, both doping concentrations and biased voltages used lay in the experimentally achievable ranges and are comparable to the values characteristic for observation of the superconductivity in other graphene bilayers. In our study we consider only one superstructure with
m_{0}=1
,
r=1
having twist angle
\theta=21.79^{\circ}
. This bilayer has the largest single-particle gap
\Delta_{R}
among all superstructures. The superconducting transition temperature is very sensitive to the value of
\Delta_{R}
: the larger
\Delta_{R}
is, the larger is
T_{c}
. Thus, this bilayer is the best candidate for observation of the superconductivity in LAtBLG.

VI.2
RPA validity

In contrast to original work by W. Kohn and J. M. Luttinger, Ref.
Kohn and Luttinger 1965
, we do not study here the electron-electron interaction mediated superconductivity by perturbation theory. This is because the electron-electron interaction in graphene-based materials cannot be considered to be small. Indeed, the strength of the electron-electron interaction in graphene is described by the ”fine structure“ constant
\alpha=e^{2}/(\epsilon v_{F})
. For
\epsilon=1
, we have
\alpha\approx 2
, that is, the electron-electron interaction is not small. In our study we use RPA, which is uncontrollable approximation. However, it is commonly accepted that RPA is applicable to the graphene-based materials since each bubble diagram enters with weight
N_{d}=4
due to the spin and valley degeneracies. Moreover, calculations performed in the framework of RPA for AB stacked graphene bilayer
Sboychakov et al. 2023
give the transition temperature in quantitative agreement with experimental value.

VI.3
Where is
p
-wave?

Our calculations show that the most stable order parameter corresponds to the
d
-wave state. This result does not look obvious. From the dependence
V^{ij}_{\mathbf{q}}
vs
q
, shown in Fig.
3
, one could expect that the
p
-wave should be the most stable state. Indeed, since
V^{ij}_{\mathbf{q}}
growths with
q
at small
q
, the integral
\int d\varphi V^{ij}(\sqrt{p^{2}+p^{\prime 2}-2pp^{\prime}\cos\varphi})\cos\varphi
is negative as soon as
p
and
p^{\prime}
are small enough. Thus, one would expect that the
p
-wave will be the most stable state. Our calculations demonstrate, however, that the
p
-wave state is either unstable at all, or loses against the
d
-wave. Analysis shows that the solution to this puzzle lays in the angular dependence of the wave functions. To show this let us analyze the angular dependence of the function
\Gamma_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\equiv\Gamma^{(S_{0}S_{0};S_{0}S_{0})}_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
– the key component for the stabilization of the superconductivity. According to Eq. (
33
) it depends on the difference
\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}-\varphi_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
and on the absolute values of
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
. Figure shows the dependence of
\Gamma_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
on
\phi=\varphi_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
calculated at
p=p^{\prime}=p_{\text{F}2}
and
\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}=0
. We see that
\Gamma_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
has minimum at
\phi=\pi
instead of maximum, as it would be expected from the dependence of
V^{ij}_{\mathbf{q}}
on
q
. This is because the sum
\sum_{\alpha}\Phi^{(S_{0})}_{\mathbf{p}i\alpha}\Phi^{(S_{0})*}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}i\alpha}
, entered into definition (
32
), is equal to zero at
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=-\mathbf{p}
. From the dependence of
\Gamma_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
on
\varphi_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
, shown in Fig.
9
, it is clear that the most stable state should be the
d
-wave state. This result is in contrast to that obtained in Ref.
Sboychakov et al. 2023
for the AB stacked bilayer graphene, where it was shown that the
p
-wave state should be the most stable one. This discrepancy is explained by the difference of the angular dependence of the wave functions of the LAtBLG and AB stacked bilayer graphene.

VI.4
Criterion for the twist angle

Before conclude, let us consider the condition of applicability of the single-particle Hamiltonian (
3
). As already mentioned above, the approximation used is valid for large twist angles. Quantitatively, this condition can be written as
Rozhkov et al. 2016

v_{\rm F}\Delta K\gg t_{\perp}\,,\;\;\Delta K=\frac{8\pi}{3a}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\,,

(52)

where
t_{\perp}\approx 0.4t_{0}
and
t_{0}
is the largest inter-layer hopping amplitude (see, Ref.
Lopes dos Santos et al. 2012
). The value of
\Delta K
decreases when twist angle decreases. When the condition (
52
) is no more valid, it is necessary to take into account the hybridization of electrons in layer
1
with momenta near the Dirac point
\mathbf{K}^{1}_{\xi}
and electrons in layer
2
with momenta near the Dirac point
\mathbf{K}^{2}_{-\xi}
. This can be done in continuum media models
Bistritzer and MacDonald 2011
;
Lopes dos Santos et al. 2012
or in the tight-binding approximation
Trambly de Laissardière
et al. 2010
;
Sboychakov et al. 2015
. Taking
Rozhkov et al. 2016
;
Lopes dos Santos et al. 2012

t_{\perp}=0.14
eV, we obtain that
v_{\rm F}\Delta K>10t_{\perp}
for
\theta\gtrsim 10^{\circ}
. The superstructure
m_{0}=1
,
r=1
with
\theta=21.79^{\circ}
, studied in this paper, satisfies this criterion.

VI.5
Conclusions

In conclusion, we have shown that the screened Coulomb interaction in doped twisted bilayer graphene with large twist angle can stabilize the superconductivity. The superconducting transition temperature can be as large as several hundreds of milikelvin. Application of the bias voltage between layers increases the transition temperature. The symmetry of the order parameter is of the
d
-wave type. In the framework of our model, the spin-singlet and spin triplet inter-valley and spin-triplet intra-valley order parameters are almost three-fold degenerate. We studied the bilayer, characterized by the superstructure with
m_{0}=1
,
r=1
and twist angle
\theta=21.79^{\circ}
. Such a bilayer should have the largest transition temperature among other bilayers with large twist angles since it is characterized by the largest hybridization of the Dirac cones, which increases the density of states in slightly doped bilayer.
