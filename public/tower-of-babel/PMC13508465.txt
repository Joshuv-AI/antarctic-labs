Bibliometric analysis of research hotspots in coronary artery CT fractional flow reserve for diagnosing myocardial ischemia in coronary artery disease
Authors: Abudounaibi Abudoueryimu, Aibibiguli Xirefu, Zulifeiya Maihemuti, Maimaitiming Mahemuti, Chen Qiaohui, Guzailinuer Keremu, Wang Xiaomei, M. A. Haihong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric analysis to map the global research landscape, identify evolving hotspots, and forecast future trends in the application of coronary computed tomography-derived fractional flow reserve (CT-FFR) for diagnosing myocardial ischemia in coronary artery disease (CAD).MethodsA systematic literature search was performed in the Web of Science Core Collection for publications from 2015 to 2025, using an expanded strategy combining CT-FFR terms with synonyms for myocardial ischemia and CAD. Bibliometric data visualization and analysis were conducted using CiteSpace and VOSviewer to examine publication trends, national/institutional contributions, collaborative networks, core journals, keyword co-occurrence, and citation bursts.ResultsFrom an initial retrieval of 730 records, 582 eligible publications (405 articles, 117 reviews) were included. Annual publication output showed sustained growth with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 6.4%. The United States led in publication volume (n = 231) and total citations, followed by China in output (n = 143), though with lower average citation impact. International collaboration was prominent, with the US, China, and Italy forming central hubs. JACC: Cardiovascular Imaging was the leading journal in both output and influence. Keyword analysis confirmed "Fractional Flow Reserve," "Coronary Artery Disease," and "Diagnostic Performance" as core themes. Burst detection revealed a clear evolution: early research (2015–2018) focused on technical validation (e.g., "dual source ct"), while recent trends (2022–2025) shifted towards clinical integration, with strong bursts for "stable chest pain," "diagnosis," and "coronary computed tomography angiography."ConclusionThis bibliometric analysis delineates a dynamic and growing CT-FFR research field, marked by a transition from technological exploration to clinical guideline-directed application. The findings highlight distinct geographic patterns of productivity and influence, and clarify the evolution of research priorities towards personalized, ischemia-guided patient management. Future research is anticipated to focus on multimodal integration with plaque and perfusion imaging, artificial intelligence-driven workflow solutions, and evidence generation in complex patient cohorts to solidify clinical adoption.

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) remains a leading global cause of morbidity and mortality. Accurate diagnosis of hemodynamically significant stenosis, which induces myocardial ischemia, is crucial for guiding appropriate revascularization therapy and improving patient outcomes [1, 2]. While invasive fractional flow reserve (FFR) is the reference standard for assessing lesion-specific ischemia, its need for catheterization has driven the development of non-invasive alternatives [3, 4].

Coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) excels at ruling out obstructive CAD but has limited specificity for identifying ischemia-causing lesions [5]. CT-derived fractional flow reserve (CT-FFR) addresses this gap by applying computational fluid dynamics to standard CCTA images, providing a non-invasive estimate of coronary pressure gradients [6, 7]. Since its introduction, CT-FFR has been extensively studied for its diagnostic accuracy, prognostic value, and potential to streamline clinical pathways [8, 9]. A decade of research has generated a substantial body of literature, creating a need to synthesize the intellectual landscape, identify key contributors, and trace the evolution of research themes. While systematic reviews address clinical questions, a bibliometric analysis can reveal macroscopic trends, collaboration networks, and emerging frontiers from a meta-perspective [10]. Therefore, this study aims to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of the CT-FFR literature focused on diagnosing myocardial ischemia in CAD. We seek to delineate global research patterns, map collaborative networks, identify seminal works and journals, analyze keyword evolution, and ultimately inform researchers and clinicians about the past development and future directions of this transformative technology.

Analysis of the data

Bibliometrics is a method for analyzing the publication output and status of a specific research field from both quantitative and qualitative perspectives. This method applies mathematical and statistical techniques to quantitatively analyze scientific literature. By examining data such as publication volume, citation patterns, and the distribution of authors and institutions, bibliometrics can reveal the scientific activities and development trends within a specific research area [11]. In the field of CT-FFR research, bibliometric analysis allows us to identify key journals, major research institutions, leading scientists, as well as research hotspots and emerging technologies.

Network analysis parameters

For the bibliometric network maps generated in this study the following conventions are used:Node size represents the volume of publication (or the frequency of occurrence for keywords).Node color indicates distinct clusters of closely related items (e.g., collaborating countries, co-citing journals, or thematic groups of keywords), as automatically determined by the VOSviewer/CiteSpace clustering algorithm. Edge thickness (the line between nodes) represents the strength of the connection, such as collaboration strength between countries or co-occurrence frequency between keywords.

Current status of global research in the field of CT-FFR

Based on the updated annual publication data from 2015 to 2025 (Fig. 2), the research output in the field of CT-FFR for diagnosing coronary artery disease and myocardial ischemia demonstrates a generally upward trajectory with notable fluctuations. The number of publications increased from 34 in 2015 to 42 in 2016, followed by a significant rise to 59 in 2017. After a slight decline to 50 in 2018 and stabilization at 50 in 2019, a marked peak occurred in 2020 with 81 publications. Subsequently, the output experienced variations: 61 in 2021, 68 in 2022, 53 in 2023, 61 in 2024, and 63 in 2025.The overall trend can be divided into three phases: Growth Phase (2015–2017): The average annual growth rate during this period was approximately 31.7%, reflecting rapid expansion in research interest, likely driven by technological advancements and initial clinical validation of CT-FFR. Fluctuation Phase (2018–2021): Publication numbers showed variability, with an average annual growth rate of about 5.1%. The peak in 2020 may correlate with increased research focus during the global health crisis, while subsequent dips suggest shifts in research priorities or methodological refinements. Stabilization Phase (2022–2025): Output remained relatively steady, averaging 61 publications per year, indicating maturation of the field and consolidation of research themes. The compound annual growth rate (CAGR) from 2015 to 2025 is approximately 6.4%, underscoring sustained scholarly interest and continuous investment in CT-FFR research. Fluctuations in annual output may be influenced by factors such as technological evolution, updates in clinical guidelines, allocation of research funding, and the frequency of academic exchanges. Despite periodic variations, the overall rising trend highlights the growing importance of CT-FFR as a non-invasive tool for functional assessment of coronary artery disease.

Discussion

Global research status and evolving hotspots

This bibliometric analysis elucidates the dynamic landscape of CT-FFR research from 2015 to 2025. The sustained growth in annual publications (CAGR: 6.4%) underscores its transition from a novel technological innovation to an established pillar of clinical cardiovascular investigation. Our findings reveal distinct geographical and qualitative disparities. The United States maintains a dominant position in both research volume and cumulative impact, a leadership that aligns with its role in pioneering and validating the technology through major multicenter trials. Several European nations and Canada, while producing fewer total articles, achieve exceptional per-article influence, indicative of high research quality and international visibility. Notably, China ranks second in publication output, reflecting vigorous national research activity. However, its comparatively lower average citation rate suggests an opportunity to enhance global impact through strengthened international collaboration, a focus on guideline-influencing studies, and targeting high-impact journals. This observation is contextualized by the broader challenges of clinical adoption; as noted in a recent authoritative review, the real-world integration of CT-FFR can be hindered by technical constraints like image quality requirements and cost, factors that may also influence the global reach and citation impact of research from all regions [12, 14].

The keyword burst detection analysis provides critical insights into the evolution of research priorities. The early phase (2015–2018) was characterized by bursts in technical methodologies ("dual source ct", "transluminal attenuation gradient"), aligning with the period of initial validation and refinement of the CT-FFR technique itself. Subsequently, the focus shifted markedly towards clinical integration and application. The strong, recent burst of "stable chest pain" (2022–2025) directly corresponds to the central role now accorded to CT-FFR in contemporary diagnostic pathways. This shift mirrors the conclusions of Kasaeian et al., who highlight that the combined use of CCTA and CT-FFR is now pivotal in evaluating stable chest pain, positioning these tools as primary gatekeepers to the catheterization laboratory [15]. The burst of "committee" (2023–2025) likely coincides with the dissemination of major international guidelines, further highlighting the field’s maturation. The most intense burst associated with "coronary computed tomography angiography" reaffirms that CT-FFR fundamentally enhances the value of anatomical CCTA.

The diagnostic value of CT-FFR for myocardial ischemia in CAD

The persistent co-occurrence of the core keywords "Fractional Flow Reserve," "Coronary Artery Disease," and "Myocardial Ischemia" underscores the primary clinical question addressed by CT-FFR: determining the hemodynamic significance of coronary stenosis. Our bibliometric data confirms that research is intensely focused on validating and refining this diagnostic capability. This focus is well-placed, as multiple large-scale trials (e.g., DISCOVER-FLOW, NXT) validated CT-FFR’s diagnostic accuracy against invasive FFR, establishing it as a highly accurate, non-invasive alternative for identifying functionally significant stenoses [16]. The technology addresses a critical diagnostic gap, as not all anatomically moderate stenoses cause ischemia. Recent research extends beyond validation to proving clinical utility, demonstrating that CT-FFR effectively guides treatment by identifying patients likely to benefit from revascularization while reducing unnecessary invasive procedures, a finding strongly supported by registries like ADVANCE [17].

Future directions informed by bibliometric trends

The identified trends point toward several concrete future research avenues: Personalized Diagnosis via Multimodal Integration: The strong link between "stable chest pain" and CT-FFR research will likely evolve towards more personalized risk stratification. This involves combining CT-FFR with other CT-based markers like plaque characterization and perfusion imaging. This aligns with the emerging research hotspot of integrating advanced plaque imaging (e.g., low-attenuation plaque) with physiological assessment. As highlighted by Dupouy P et al., patients with high-risk plaque features have a significantly increased likelihood of ischemia on CT-FFR, suggesting a combined approach offers superior risk stratification [18]. Artificial Intelligence and Workflow Revolution: The future trajectory includes efforts to enhance accessibility and efficiency through fully automated, on-site AI-based CT-FFR solutions. This is a recognized major advancement. The review notes that on-site machine learning-based CT-FFR (CT-FFRML), while having comparable diagnostic performance to traditional putational fluid dynamics (CFD)-based methods, can optimize workflow, reduce turnaround time, and improve cost-effectiveness, potentially enhancing equitable access [19].

Expansion into Therapeutic Planning and Complex Cohorts: Research is extending the application of CT-FFR into pre-procedural planning for PCI and CABG. This represents one of the most promising advancements. Tools like the HeartFlow® Planner enable simulation of revascularization strategies, with early studies showing virtual stenting results correlate closely with post-PCI invasive FFR [20]. Furthermore, validating CT-FFR in specific patient cohorts (e.g., those with valvular heart disease or coronary anomalies) and generating robust real-world cost-effectiveness evidence will be crucial for solidifying its role in routine care.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, while our expanded search strategy aimed for comprehensiveness by including numerous synonyms for myocardial ischemia and CAD, it is possible that some relevant studies using unconventional terminology were missed. Second, the analysis is based solely on the Web of Science Core Collection; incorporating data from Scopus or PubMed might yield a slightly different sample. Third, bibliometric analysis reflects publication and citation trends, which can be influenced by factors beyond pure scientific merit, such as journal popularity, geographic biases in citation practices, and the inherent time lag for recent publications to accrue citations. Finally, this study describes the research landscape but does not critically appraise the methodological quality of the included studies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this bibliometric analysis maps the evolving research landscape of CT-FFR for diagnosing myocardial ischemia in patients with CAD. The field has demonstrated sustained growth, transitioning from early technical validation to a focus on clinical integration and guideline-directed application. Key findings include the dominant research output and influence of the United States, the high-volume yet lower-impact profile of Chinese research, and the pivotal role of specialized cardiovascular imaging journals. The keyword evolution reveals a clear shift from methodological exploration to addressing core clinical problems like stable chest pain evaluation.

The analysis confirms that CT-FFR research is centrally focused on solving the critical clinical problem of linking coronary anatomy to ischemic pathophysiology. Future directions, as indicated by emerging trends, will likely involve the multimodal integration of CT-FFR with plaque and perfusion imaging, the adoption of AI for workflow optimization, and the generation of evidence in complex patient populations to guide broader clinical implementation.
