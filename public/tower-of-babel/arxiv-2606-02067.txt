Search for a leptoquark in events with a hadronically decaying τ \tau -lepton and missing transverse momentum using p ​ p pp collisions at s = 13 ​ TeV \sqrt{s}=13\penalty\ \text{TeV} with the ATLAS detector


Abstract
A search for leptoquark signals is performed in final states with a hadronically decaying τ \tau -lepton and missing transverse momentum, using data from proton–proton collisions at a centre-of-mass energy of s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV recorded by the ATLAS detector at the Large Hadron Collider during Run 2 (2015–18), corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 140 fb -1 . The analysis is designed to probe both resonant production and non-resonant t t -channel exchange of the leptoquark, covering a wide range of coupling scenarios. No excess above the Standard Model background prediction is observed. Limits are set on the couplings in the benchmark U 1 U_{1} vector-leptoquark model at 95% confidence level for masses between 1.5 TeV and 3.0 TeV.

ATLAS detector

The ATLAS detector
[
27
,
30
]
is a multipurpose apparatus with nearly
4\pi
coverage in
solid angle.
1
1

1

ATLAS uses a right-handed coordinate system with its origin at the nominal interaction point (IP)
in the centre of the detector and the
z
-axis along the beam pipe.
The
x
-axis points from the IP to the centre of the LHC ring, and the
y
-axis points upwards.
Polar coordinates
(r,\phi)
are used in the transverse plane,
\phi
being the azimuthal angle around the
z
-axis.
The pseudorapidity is defined in terms of the polar angle
\theta
as
\eta=-\ln\tan(\theta/2)
and is equal to the rapidity

y=(1/2)\ln[(E+p_{z})/(E-p_{z})]
in the relativistic limit.
Angular distance is measured in units of
\Delta R\equiv\sqrt{(\Delta y)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}
.

It consists of an inner tracking detector (ID) inside a
2\text{\,}\mathrm{T}
superconducting solenoid, electromagnetic and hadronic calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer.
The inner tracking detector covers the pseudorapidity range
|\eta|<2.5
.
It consists of silicon pixel, silicon microstrip, and transition radiation tracking detectors.
Lead/liquid-argon (LAr) sampling calorimeters provide electromagnetic (EM) energy measurements
with high granularity within the region
|\eta|<3.2
.
A steel/scintillator-tile hadronic calorimeter covers the central pseudorapidity range (
|\eta|<1.7
).
The endcap and forward regions are instrumented with LAr calorimeters
for EM and hadronic energy measurements up to
|\eta|=4.9
.
The muon spectrometer (MS) surrounds the calorimeters and is based on
three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets with eight coils each.
The field integral of the toroids ranges between
2.0
and
6.0\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\text{\,}\mathrm{m}

across most of the detector.
The MS includes a system of precision tracking chambers up to
|\eta|=2.7
and fast detectors for triggering up to
|\eta|=2.4
.
A two-level trigger system is used to select events
[
31
]
.
The first-level trigger is implemented in hardware and uses a subset of the detector information
to accept events at a rate close to
100\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
.
This is followed by a software-based trigger that
reduces the accepted rate of complete events to
1.25\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
on average in Run 2,
depending on the data-taking conditions.
A software suite
[
32
]
is used in data simulation, in the reconstruction
and analysis of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and data acquisition systems of the experiment.

Signal model

This search focuses on the production of a vector leptoquark
U_{1}
with quantum numbers
(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{1},{2/3})
, which correspond to the colour, weak, and hypercharge representations, respectively.
The effective Lagrangian for
U_{1}
LQ interactions
[
33
]
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{U_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}U^{\dagger}_{1\mu\nu}U^{\mu\nu}_{1}+m_{\textit{U}_{1}}^{2}U^{\dagger}_{1\mu}U^{\mu}_{1}-ig_{\text{s}}(1-\kappa)U^{\dagger}_{1\mu}T^{a}U_{1\nu}G^{a\mu\nu}

\displaystyle-ig_{\text{Y}}\frac{2}{3}(1-\tilde{\kappa})U^{\dagger}_{1\mu}U_{1\nu}B^{\mu\nu}+\frac{g_{U}}{\sqrt{2}}\left[U^{\mu}_{1}(\beta_{\text{L}}^{ij}\bar{q}_{\text{L}}^{i}\gamma_{\mu}\ell_{\text{L}}^{j}+\beta_{\text{R}}^{ij}\bar{d}_{\text{R}}^{i}\gamma_{\mu}e_{\text{R}}^{j})+h.c.\right],

where
U_{1\mu}
is a vector LQ field,
U_{1\mu\nu}\equiv D_{\mu}U_{1\nu}-D_{\nu}U_{1\mu}
is its field-strength tensor,
m_{\textit{U}_{1}}
is the LQ mass,
G^{a}
and
B
denote the field-strength tensors of the SU(3)
{}_{\text{c}}
and U(1)
{}_{\text{Y}}
gauge bosons,
g_{\text{s}}
and
g_{\text{Y}}
the corresponding gauge coupling constants, and
T^{a}=\lambda^{a}/2
(Gell-Mann matrices).
Here
q_{\text{L}}
(
\ell_{\text{L}}
) denotes the left-handed quark (lepton) doublets and
d_{\text{R}}
(
e_{\text{R}}
) denotes the right-handed down-type quark (charged-lepton) singlets,

i\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}
and

j\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}
are flavour indices,
and
a\in\left\{1,\ldots,8\right\}
is a SU(3)
{}_{\text{c}}
index.
The CKM matrix elements
V_{ji}
are included in
q_{\text{L}}^{i}
as

q_{\text{L}}^{i}=\begin{pmatrix}V^{*}_{ji}u^{j}_{\text{L}}\\
d^{i}_{\text{L}}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The flavour basis of the quark fields is identified with the mass basis of the down-type quarks.
The couplings
\beta_{\text{L}}
and
\beta_{\text{R}}
are complex
3\times 3
matrices in flavour space.
In this analysis, motivated by the discussion in Refs.
[
34
,
35
]
, they are defined as

\beta_{\text{L}}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&\beta_{\text{L}}^{13}\\
0&0&\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}\\
0&0&\beta_{\text{L}}^{33}\\
\end{matrix}\right),\hskip 1.42262pt\beta_{\text{R}}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&\beta_{\text{R}}^{33}\\
\end{matrix}\right).

Conventionally, the normalisation factor
g_{U}
is introduced to set
\beta_{\text{L}}^{33}=1
.
There are two benchmark scenarios, corresponding to
\left|\beta_{\text{R}}^{33}\right|=0
and
\left|\beta_{\text{R}}^{33}\right|=1
. In this analysis, the interpretation is performed in the purely left-handed scenario (
\left|\beta_{\text{R}}^{33}\right|=0
).
The parameters
\kappa
and
\tilde{\kappa}
introduce the LQ couplings to the SM gauge-boson fields.
They are set to
\kappa=\tilde{\kappa}=0
in this analysis (Yang–Mills coupling scenario).

Integrating out the
U_{1}
field at low energy induces semileptonic four-fermion operators.
The left-handed coefficient for the
b\to c\tau\nu
transition scales as

C_{\text{LL}}^{c}\sim\frac{g_{U}^{2}v^{2}}{4m_{\textit{U}_{1}}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{V_{cs}}{V_{cb}}\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}\right),

(1)

where
v
is the electroweak vacuum expectation value.
The term proportional to
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}
is enhanced by the ratio
V_{cs}/V_{cb}
, implying that even moderate values of
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}
can generate sizeable contributions to the
R(D^{(*)})
anomalies.
2
2

2

The parameter
\beta_{\text{L}}^{13}
is set to
V_{td}^{*}/V_{ts}^{*}\ \beta_{\text{L}}^{23}\sim 0.2\ \beta_{\text{L}}^{23}
following the convention of Ref.
[
33
]
.
Its contribution is smaller than the
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}
term and is ignored here for simplicity.

Although
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}\sim 0.1
is often assumed in UV-complete models
[
34
,
35
]
, low-energy charged-current observables alone do not impose stringent constraints on this parameter.
Additional constraints arise from flavour-changing neutral-current observables, particularly
B_{s(d)}
–
\bar{B}_{s(d)}
mixing, as well as from LFU tests in
\tau
-lepton decays.
However, these bounds are model dependent and may be affected by additional states in the UV theory
[
35
]
.
This search therefore explores the high-
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}
region
[
33
]
, which was not thoroughly covered by previous ATLAS and CMS searches.

The signal is primarily characterised by three parameters:

m_{\textit{U}_{1}}
,
g_{U}
and
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}
.
The relative contributions of resonant and non-resonant production depend strongly on
m_{\textit{U}_{1}}
.
The resonant production cross-section decreases rapidly around
m_{\textit{U}_{1}}\sim 1.5\penalty\ \text{TeV}
,
mainly due to the suppression of the parton distribution functions (PDFs) at high Bjorken-
x
.
As a result, the non-resonant contribution becomes dominant at higher masses.
The coupling
g_{U}
controls the overall signal normalisation.
The parameter
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}
influences the flavour structure of the production process and changes the effective parton luminosity contributing to the signal.
Larger values of
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}
also reduce the relative fraction of signal events containing a
b
-quark in the final state.
The decay width of the LQ scales approximately as
\Gamma/m_{\textit{U}_{1}}\propto g_{U}^{2}\times\left[1+(\beta_{\text{L}}^{23})^{2}\right]
.
For example, at
m_{\textit{U}_{1}}=1.5\penalty\ \text{TeV}
,
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}=0.6
, and
g_{U}=2
, the intrinsic width is about 7% of the LQ mass.

Event reconstruction and preselection

Hadronically decaying
\tau
-leptons are reconstructed from seed jets formed by the anti-

algorithm
[

]
with a radius parameter
R=0.4

[

]

All
\tau_{\text{had}}

and

The vertex of a

Geometric and impact-parameter criteria are then applied to all tracks associated with the seed jet to select those well matched to the

candidate,
after which a track classifier based on a recurrent neural network (RNN)
[
68

distinguishes
\tau_{\text{had}}
tracks from other tracks.
Each
\tau_{\text{had}}
candidate is required to have one or three tracks classified as
\tau_{\text{had}}
tracks, with the sum of their charges equal to
\pm 1
.
Another RNN-based classifier is used to separate
\tau_{\text{had}}
candidates from the hadron-jet background
[
68
]

Background estimation

The SM backgrounds are estimated using simulated samples.
The dominant SM background in the SRs comes from
W\to\tau\nu
production in association with jets.
Subdominant contributions arise from top-quark decays producing
\tau
-leptons (

\tau

b

The normalisations of

+ ​jets and top-quark samples are constrained by the data in high-purity control regions (CRs), where single-lepton triggers are used instead of the
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}

Events with a jet misidentified as a
\tau_{\text{had}}

Z\to\ell\ell
events.
Multijet background is found to be negligible after the preselection.
Other SM backgrounds, including diboson and
Z\to\tau\tau
contributions, as well as minor contributions from
W\to\ell\nu
and
Z\to\ell\ell
processes, are also estimated by simulation.

The

8
Systematic uncertainties

Experimental and modelling systematic uncertainties are included as normalisation and shape variations in the fit and are smaller than the statistical uncertainty from the limited SR event yields.

The uncertainty in the combined 2015–2018 integrated luminosity is 0.83%
[
78
]
, obtained using the LUCID-2 detector
[
79
]
for the primary luminosity measurements, complemented by measurements using the inner detector and calorimeters.

The
\tau
-lepton energy scale and its uncertainties are measured in situ using
Z\to\tau\tau
events, combined with test beam and MC simulation comparison in the high-
p_{\text{T}}
region
[
80
,
81
,
68
]
.
The correction applied to the simulated
\tau
energy scale is typically below 1% of the reconstructed
\tau_{\text{had}}
energy.
The associated uncertainty in the absolute
\tau
energy scale is smaller than 1.5%.
The efficiencies of
\tau
-lepton reconstruction and identification in the simulation samples are corrected to agree with data, depending on
p_{\text{T}}
, using
t\bar{t}
events.
The central value of the correction factor is close to unity in all
p_{\text{T}}
ranges, with uncertainties of 5%–8% (6%–11%) for 1-prong (3-prong)
\tau_{\text{had}}
at the

?
Tight
?
identification working point.
The uncertainties in the electron and muon reconstruction and identification efficiencies and their energy and momentum scales and resolutions are considered, and they are significantly smaller than those affecting
\tau
-leptons.

The jet energy scale and resolution and
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
soft-term uncertainties are estimated using in situ methods
[
82
,
76
]
.
The efficiency of
b
-jet identification in the simulation is corrected to match that measured in
t\bar{t}
data events, and uncertainties in the correction factor are estimated
[
83
,
84
,
85
]
. For signal samples using AtlFast3 detector simulation, an additional uncertainty taking into account how it differs from the full
Geant4
simulation is considered, and its impact on the signal yield is around 1%.

Uncertainties in the theoretical modelling of simulated events are also considered.
For simulated
W
+ ​jets events,
variations of the renormalisation and factorisation scales, by factors of 2 and
1/2
, and the PDFs are considered
[
86
]
.
Additionally, approximate NLO electroweak corrections are applied to the nominal
W
+ ​jets samples,
and the corresponding effect is taken as an additional systematic uncertainty.
Since the normalisation of
W\to\tau\nu
events is estimated in the
\mathrm{CRW}
regions with a
b
-jet veto and applied to SRs with a
b
-jet, a 30% uncertainty is assigned in those regions to the ratio of
W
-boson production in association with heavy-flavour hadrons (

W

[
87
,
88
,
89
,
90
]
.

For simulated top-quark events,
variations of the ME calculation, renormalisation and factorisation scales, and PDFs are considered, similarly to the
W
+ ​jets samples, along with variations related to initial- and final-state radiation and the
h_{\textrm{damp}}
parameter
[
91
]
. The difference in the top-quark
p_{\text{T}}
distribution’s shape between the nominal NLO prediction and the NNLO prediction
[
92
]
is also considered as an additional uncertainty.
Uncertainties in the interference between
t\bar{t}
and single-
t
events are estimated by comparing simulations using the diagram removal and diagram subtraction schemes
[
93
]
.

The background containing a jet misidentified as a
\tau_{\text{had}}
is a minor contribution in the SRs and is mainly due to
Z\to\nu\nu
production.
The modelling of the probability of misidentifying a jet as a
\tau_{\text{had}}
in simulated events is checked in a control sample requiring

\tau_{\text{had}}
candidate, providing a sample with more than 90% purity in events containing
Z

\tau_{\text{had}}
candidate.
The kinematic distributions are generally well modelled by the simulation, while up to 60% differences are observed in the overall event yield between data and simulation.
These differences include effects from both the modelling of
Z
+ ​jets and the probability for jets to be misidentified as
\tau_{\text{had}}
candidates, with the latter expected to dominate.
Since this background contributes only a small fraction of the total background in the SRs,
a conservative 100% uncertainty is assigned to the normalisation of those simulated events.
For the signal samples, variations of the renormalisation and factorisation scales and PDF are taken into account.

Conclusion

This Letter presents the first ATLAS search targeting high-mass
U_{1}
leptoquark signals in the
\tau\nu(+b)
final state with a hadronically decaying

\tau
-lepton and missing transverse momentum. The search uses 140 fb
-1
of data from 13 TeV proton–proton collisions at the LHC.
The analysis is designed to probe both resonant production and non-resonant
t
-channel exchange of the leptoquark, covering a wide range of coupling scenarios.
No significant excess is observed over the SM background.
Upper limits on the
U_{1}
vector-leptoquark couplings are set at 95% CL for masses between 1.5 TeV and 3.0 TeV.
The results probe part of the parameter space at high
\beta_{\text{L}}^{23}
values, which is not covered by the previous
\tau\tau
searches.
