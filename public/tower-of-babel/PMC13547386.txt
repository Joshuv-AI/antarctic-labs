Short-term load forecasting using a two-stage CPO-PSO hyperparameter optimization of LSSVM
Authors: XinHao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate short-term electricity load forecasting is critical for ensuring reliable power system operation, particularly in the face of increasing grid complexity and the nonlinear, time-varying nature of load data. This study proposes a novel hybrid model that integrates Crested Porcupine Optimization (CPO), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Least Squares Support Vector Machine (LSSVM). Initially, CPO is employed to perform global hyperparameter optimization, effectively exploring the parameter space and preventing premature convergence. Subsequently, PSO performs local refinement of the parameters to enhance the model’s predictive accuracy and generalization capability. Finally, the optimized LSSVM model captures complex nonlinear relationships between load and external factors such as temperature, date, and holidays. To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed CPO-PSO-LSSVM model, comparative experiments were conducted against baseline LSSVM, PSO-LSSVM, and CPO-LSSVM models using two real-world datasets. On the Jiangsu dataset (hourly resolution), the proposed model achieved reductions in MAE and RMSE of 40.2% and 50.3%, respectively, compared to LSSVM, while improving NSE from 0.604 to 0.902. On the Australian dataset (half-hourly resolution), it achieved reductions in MAE and RMSE of 25.3% and 50.8%, with an NSE increase from 0.985 to 0.996. These results confirm that the CPO-PSO-LSSVM model offers superior forecasting accuracy, robustness across regions and time granularities, and practical applicability for modern power system management.

Introduction

Load forecasting plays a crucial role in maintaining the balance between electricity supply and demand since the emergence of modern power grids. In particular, short-term load forecasting (STLF) is vital for the reliable operation of power systems and meeting market requirements1. With the increasing penetration of renewable energy sources, demand-side management, and market liberalization, the complexity and importance of accurate load forecasting have grown significantly2–4. Accurate STLF not only improves power system reliability and economic dispatch efficiency, but also supports renewable energy integration and intelligent grid management under increasingly uncertain operating conditions.

Short-term load forecasting methods are generally divided into mathematical-statistical approaches and machine learning techniques5. Traditional methods, such as multiple linear regression6, Kalman filters7, and autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) models8, are theoretically sound and easy to implement, with interpretable parameters. They consider historical loads, relevant factors, and temporal dependencies9. However, these approaches generally rely on linear assumptions and often struggle to capture the highly nonlinear, nonstationary, and dynamic characteristics of modern electricity load data. In addition, model complexity and parameter instability tend to increase significantly with higher model orders and complex operating conditions. These limitations have motivated the adoption of machine learning methods to improve forecasting performance.

Artificial intelligence-based load forecasting methods leverage machine learning and deep learning techniques to automatically extract features and patterns from large-scale historical electricity data, enabling accurate prediction of future power loads. Common approaches include Support Vector Regression (SVR)10, Radial Basis Function (RBF) neural networks11, and Extreme Learning Machines (ELM)12, which enhance the ability to model nonlinear relationships. With the advancement of deep learning, architectures such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks13, and Least Squares Support Vector Machine (LSSVM)14 have been widely applied to load forecasting, significantly improving temporal modeling capabilities and predictive accuracy. Despite these advancements, existing machine learning and deep learning methods still face several limitations in practical applications.

Recent studies have increasingly focused on hybrid forecasting frameworks that integrate decomposition techniques, intelligent optimization algorithms, and machine learning models to improve forecasting accuracy and robustness. Zhang and Hong15 proposed a CEEMDAN-SVR framework optimized by a quantum-based dragonfly algorithm, demonstrating that decomposition and metaheuristic optimization can effectively improve electric load forecasting performance. Zhang et al.16 further developed a VMD-SR-SVR model combined with an improved cuckoo search strategy to alleviate premature convergence and enhance forecasting stability. More recently, Zhang et al.17 proposed a BiGRU-enhanced twin support vector regression model with seasonal mechanisms, which improved the capability of capturing nonlinear and seasonal load patterns. Peng et al.18 introduced a multimodal fusion forecasting framework integrating optimization algorithms and deep learning techniques to further enhance feature extraction and forecasting performance. Although these hybrid approaches have achieved promising forecasting results, several limitations still remain. Many existing methods rely on single-stage optimization strategies, which may suffer from insufficient balance between global exploration and local exploitation during hyperparameter tuning. Consequently, optimization stability, convergence quality, and generalization capability may deteriorate when handling highly nonlinear and time-varying STLF tasks.

As an extension of Support Vector Machine (SVM), the LSSVM replaces the inequality constraints and quadratic programming of standard SVM with equality constraints and a linear system, significantly reducing computational complexity19. This reformulation retains the strong nonlinear modeling capability of SVM while improving convergence speed and scalability. LSSVM has demonstrated excellent performance in STLF, offering high accuracy, robustness to small datasets, and efficient computation20,21. However, the predictive performance of LSSVM is highly sensitive to its hyperparameters, including the regularization parameter and kernel parameters, which directly influence generalization and accuracy. Conventional optimization methods, such as grid search and metaheuristic algorithms like Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Genetic Algorithms (GA), have been widely adopted to tune these parameters.

Recent studies have demonstrated the potential of PSO-LSSVM models in various applications: Ge et al. proposed a reinforcement learning and PSO-LSSVM hybrid method for industrial power load forecasting, achieving high accuracy across diverse production patterns22; Xu et al. applied PSO-LSSVM to air-conditioning load forecasting in GSHP systems, realizing up to 11.76% energy savings23; and Ji et al. developed a dynamic PSO-LSSVM model that reduced mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) to 1.91%, significantly outperforming other models in short-term power load forecasting24. Nevertheless, existing PSO-LSSVM frameworks still exhibit certain limitations when applied to STLF tasks. The standard PSO algorithm often suffers from premature convergence and insufficient exploration in high-dimensional parameter spaces, leading to suboptimal hyperparameter tuning of LSSVM. Moreover, the dynamic and highly nonlinear characteristics of short-term power load data further exacerbate these challenges.

Compared with conventional single-stage optimization methods, a two-stage optimization framework can better balance global exploration and local exploitation during hyperparameter tuning. The Crested Porcupine Optimizer (CPO) exhibits strong exploration capability and population diversity, which helps avoid premature convergence in the early optimization stage. Meanwhile, PSO demonstrates fast convergence and efficient local refinement ability during later-stage optimization. By integrating the complementary strengths of CPO and PSO, the proposed framework aims to achieve more stable and accurate hyperparameter optimization for LSSVM in STLF applications.

To address these challenges of nonlinearity, dynamic patterns, and hyperparameter sensitivity in short-term load forecasting, this paper proposes a CPO-PSO-LSSVM hybrid model. The first two (visual and auditory) drive global exploration, while the latter two (olfactory and physical attack) enhance local exploitation.

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) mimics the collective behavior of animals during migration and foraging26. A swarm of particles, each with a position x and velocity v, iteratively searches for the optimal solution. Each particle adjusts its trajectory based on its own best-found position pbest and the global best position gbest identified by the swarm27. The velocity and position updates are defined as:11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$v_{i + 1} = w \cdot v_{i} + c_{1} \cdot rand_{1} \cdot (pbest_{i} - x_{i} ) + c_{2} \cdot rand_{2} \cdot (gbest_{i} - x_{i} )$$\end{document}12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x_{i + 1} = x_{i} + v_{i + 1}$$\end{document}where w is the inertia weight controlling exploration–exploitation balance; c1 and c2 are cognitive and social coefficients; rand1 and rand2 are random numbers between zero and one; vi and xi represent the velocity and position of the ith dimension of a particle, respectively; pbesti and gbesti are the local and global optimal solutions of the ith dimension of the optimal particle position, respectively.

Results

To validate the accuracy of the model proposed in this paper, electricity load data from a specific city in Jiangsu, China, were used as the case study dataset. The simulation was conducted using hourly load data from February 3, 2003, to March 16, 2003, which totals 1000 h. In addition to load information, this dataset also includes details on dates, times, holidays, and temperature.

CPO global search and PSO local refinement

In the proposed two-stage optimization framework, the Crested Porcupine Optimizer (CPO) and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) are integrated to achieve robust and efficient hyperparameter tuning of LSSVM for short-term electricity load forecasting.

In the first stage, CPO was deployed to perform a global search over the LSSVM hyperparameter space. The population size was set to 20, and the maximum iteration count to 10, as determined empirically for a balance between exploration capability and computational efficiency. The search targeted two key hyperparameters of LSSVM: the regularization parameter γ (gamma) and the kernel parameter σ2 (sig2). The fitness function was defined as the average mean squared error (MSE) obtained from tenfold cross-validation on the training dataset, enabling CPO to explore a broad solution space and avoid premature convergence. The convergence rate α was set to 0.2 to balance exploration and exploitation, and the CPR minimum population size was defined as 80% of N (i.e., 16) to maintain adequate population diversity during late-stage iterations.

In the second stage, the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm was used to perform local refinement of the hyperparameters obtained from CPO. PSO was configured with a smaller population size of 20 and a maximum of 50 iterations, focusing on fine-tuning to minimize overfitting and enhance generalization. Unlike the first stage, the PSO fitness function evaluated the average MSE across tenfold cross-validation, ensuring stability and robustness of the optimized hyperparameters under varying data subsets.

Discussion

Comparison with other methods

From Table 3, it can be observed that the proposed CPO-PSO-LSSVM model exhibits significant improvements across all evaluation metrics when compared to the baseline LSSVM, PSO-LSSVM, and CPO-LSSVM models. Specifically, its MAE of 7.148 represents a reduction of 40.2% compared to LSSVM, and decreases of 28.4% and 15.1% compared to PSO-LSSVM and CPO-LSSVM, respectively. Similarly, its RMSE of 9.537 reflects a reduction of 50.3% relative to LSSVM, and improvements of 34.5% and 19.7% compared to PSO-LSSVM and CPO-LSSVM. Moreover, its NSE of 0.902 indicates an improvement in predictive efficiency relative to the other methods, being closer to the ideal value of 1 and reflecting enhanced predictive reliability.

These results indicate that the CPO-PSO-LSSVM model successfully leverages the complementary strengths of CPO and PSO. The superior forecasting performance of the proposed framework can be attributed to the complementary optimization characteristics of CPO and PSO. Specifically, CPO enhances population diversity and broadens the exploration of the hyperparameter space during the early optimization stage, thereby alleviating premature convergence. Subsequently, PSO performs efficient local refinement within promising parameter regions, improving convergence precision and model generalization capability. This collaborative optimization mechanism enables the LSSVM model to better capture nonlinear and dynamic load variations under different operating conditions. The two-stage optimization strategy significantly enhances both model precision and stability. Previous studies have also demonstrated that optimization-based neural network training algorithms, including LM, BR, and SCG, can significantly improve forecasting performance, further supporting the effectiveness of optimization-enhanced forecasting frameworks24.

From Fig. 3 and Table 4, it is further evident that the CPO-PSO-LSSVM model maintains excellent performance on the auxiliary Australian dataset. Here, its MAE of 8.117 demonstrates reductions of 25.3%, 12.9%, and 8.8% compared to LSSVM, PSO-LSSVM, and CPO-LSSVM, respectively. Its RMSE of 10.605 reflects a reduction of 50.8% relative to LSSVM, and improvements of 31.0% and 19.2% compared to PSO-LSSVM and CPO-LSSVM. The NSE of 0.996, approaching the ideal value, highlights its strong generalization ability across datasets with different temporal resolutions and geographical characteristics.

In summary, the CPO-PSO-LSSVM model consistently surpasses the other three models across all performance indicators, particularly excelling in reducing MAE and RMSE. The auxiliary validation with Australian electricity load data reaffirms its exceptional performance and adaptability. Across different time intervals (1 h and 0.5 h) and regions, the CPO-PSO-LSSVM model demonstrates robust stability and high predictive accuracy, confirming its effectiveness for short-term electricity load forecasting. The improved forecasting accuracy of the proposed model may contribute to more reliable power dispatching, reduced operational uncertainty, and enhanced energy management efficiency in modern smart grid systems.

Critical analysis

Synergistic effects of global–local optimization

The experimental results clearly demonstrate that the proposed CPO-PSO-LSSVM model achieves superior forecasting performance compared to LSSVM, PSO-LSSVM, and CPO-LSSVM across all evaluation metrics. This improvement is primarily attributed to the synergistic integration of CPO’s global search and PSO’s local refinement. The CPO algorithm effectively explores the broader hyperparameter space, avoiding premature convergence, while PSO fine-tunes within a narrowed parameter region, enhancing the model’s ability to generalize across unseen data. This global–local optimization strategy not only improves predictive accuracy but also reduces the computational burden compared to exhaustive grid searches or single-stage metaheuristic optimization. Compared with deep learning-based forecasting frameworks, the proposed LSSVM-based model requires fewer training samples and lower computational resources while still maintaining competitive forecasting accuracy. This characteristic makes the proposed framework more suitable for practical STLF scenarios with limited data availability and computational constraints.

Model robustness across datasets

The CPO-PSO-LSSVM model maintains robust performance across different datasets and time granularities (hourly in Jiangsu and half-hourly in Australia). In both cases, it consistently outperforms the baseline models in MAE, RMSE, and NSE metrics. The relatively smaller improvement observed on the Australian dataset may be associated with its higher baseline forecasting stability and lower fluctuation intensity compared with the Jiangsu dataset. Since the Australian dataset already exhibits relatively smooth load patterns and high baseline NSE values, the optimization space for further performance enhancement becomes comparatively limited. Nevertheless, the minimal degradation in performance on the auxiliary dataset highlights the framework’s adaptability to different grid conditions and data resolutions, which is crucial for practical deployment in diverse energy systems.

Limitations and future perspectives

Although the model has been validated using datasets from Jiangsu (China) and Australia, both regions have relatively stable power grids and moderate climatic variability. The generalizability of the CPO-PSO-LSSVM model to regions with more volatile electricity demand, such as grids with high renewable energy integration (e.g., solar or wind-dominated systems) or developing areas with frequent power outages, remains to be established.

The current model includes basic external factors such as temperature and holiday indicators. However, other influential variables—such as real-time weather conditions (e.g., humidity, wind speed), electricity market dynamics (e.g., demand response events, price spikes), and socioeconomic factors—are not incorporated. The absence of these features may limit forecasting accuracy under atypical load patterns or extreme events. In addition, although the proposed two-stage optimization framework improves forecasting accuracy and optimization stability, the hybrid optimization process inevitably increases computational complexity compared with single-stage optimization methods. This may limit real-time deployment efficiency in extremely large-scale forecasting scenarios or resource-constrained environments.

Future research should focus on validating the model in diverse geographical contexts and integrating additional external features to enhance predictive performance under complex real-world scenarios. Moreover, exploring hybridization with deep learning architectures could further improve the model’s ability to capture nonlinear temporal dependencies in highly dynamic power systems.
