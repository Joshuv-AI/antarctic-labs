Data‑driven stress problem under purely normal homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions
Cristian G. Gebhardt, Kundan Kumar, Florin A. Radu

Abstract
Data‑Driven Continuum Mechanics – the continuous counterpart of Data-Driven Computational Mechanics – is a modern paradigm that enhances classical continuum mechanics by incorporating finite sets of experimental material data directly, avoiding any form of constitutive modeling. Despite recent progress, its analytical foundations remain at an early stage. In this work, we establish a rigorous functional‑analytic framework for the data-driven stress problem under purely homogeneous normal Neumann boundary conditions. The problem is formulated as finding a stress field (satisfying the balance of linear and angular momenta and the boundary conditions) that is closest, in an L p L^{p} -sense, to an auxiliary stress field that is simultaneously sought and locally resembles a finite discrete set of experimental stress states. Our analysis relies on two key ingredients. First, the divergence operator induces a topological isomorphism between the space of symmetric stress fields modulo its kernel and the space of loads balanced by rigid-body motions, ensuring the existence of an equilibrated response. Second, the finiteness of the material data set guarantees proximinality in the stress space, which in turn yields a complete existence and uniquenes theory for solution equivalence classes. Together, these two properties provide a rigorous mathematical foundation for the data‑driven stress problem under purely homogeneous normal Neumann boundary conditions. In this contribution, we develop a rigorous functional‑analytic foundation for, perhaps, the simplest specialization of the general Data‑Driven Continuous Mechanics framework, focusing exclusively on symmetric stress fields under purely normal homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions. Working within Sobolev and Lebesgue spaces, we establish the conditions required to ensure both the existence and uniqueness of the resulting equilibrated stress field, as well as the existence of an auxiliary stress field that locally resembles the available experimental data. To this end, we begin by introducing the essential analytical ingredients needed to formulate the problem under consideration. We then proceed to define the problem precisely and present the corresponding existence theorem, thereby establishing the mathematical framework in which the remainder of this contribution is developed.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) be a bounded Lipschitz domain with boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
. Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
be Hölder conjugate exponents, i.e.,

1/p+1/q=1.

Let
\mathbb{M}^{N}
be the space of real
N\times N
matrices and consider its canonical direct-sum decomposition
\mathbb{M}^{N}=\mathbb{S}^{N}\oplus\mathbb{A}^{N}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{N}
and
\mathbb{A}^{N}
are mutually orthogonal subspaces of symmetric and anti-symmetric matrices, respectively. The orthogonality is taken with respect to the Frobenius inner product. Moreover,

\dim(\mathbb{M}^{N})=N^{2}
,
\dim(\mathbb{S}^{N})=N(N+1)/2
and
\dim(\mathbb{A}^{N})=N(N-1)/2.

Let

W^{\Div,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}):=\{s\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}):\Div s\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\}

and

W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}):=\{s\in W^{\Div,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}):s\nu=0\in W^{-1/p,p}(\Gamma;\mathbb{R}^{N})\},

where
\nu\in S^{N-1}
is the outward unit normal on
\Gamma
.

Let

{\mathscr{S}}:=\{\tilde{s}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}):\tilde{s}(\omega)=\mathfrak{s}_{m}\in\mathbb{S}^{N}~\text{for a.e.}~\omega\in\Omega,~\mathrm{for~some}~\mathfrak{s}_{m}\in\mathfrak{S}\}

and
\mathfrak{S}=\{\mathfrak{s}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\subset\mathbb{S}^{N}
is a given finite set of symmetric stress states.

Introduce the space of infinitesimal rigid motions

R_{q}:=\{\ w:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}:w(\omega)=A\omega+b,\ A\in\mathbb{A}^{N},\ b\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\}\cap L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and its annihilator with respect to the
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})-L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
dual pairing

R^{\circ}_{q}:=\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}):\langle f,w\rangle_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0~\forall~w\in R_{q}\}.

Assuming a Helmholz-Weyl decomposition (to be elaborated later), let
\Pi
denote the projector that maps any
v\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
to its divergence-free component such that
v=\Pi v+\Grads u
for some
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, understood up to infinitesimal rigid body motions.

We now define the data-driven stress problem under purely homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions (
DDSPN_{0}
).

Problem 1

(
DDSPN_{0}
)
.

Given
f\in R^{\circ}_{p}
,
consider the variational problem

\smash[b]{\inf_{(s,\tilde{s})\in W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})\times L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}}\quad\frac{1}{p}\|s-\tilde{s}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}~~\st~~\Div s+f=0,\quad\Pi s=0,\quad\tilde{s}\in{\mathscr{S}}.

The constraint
\Div s+f=0
enforces the balance of linear and angular momenta, while the constraint
\Pi s=0
suppresses the divergence-free component of the stress field, thereby selecting the canonical representative within the corresponding equivalence class. The auxiliary field
\tilde{s}
is restricted to the set
\mathscr{S}
. The objective functional measures the distance between these stress fields in the
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
-norm. The interplay between the equilibrium constraint and the discrete nature of the data requires establishing both existence of admissible stress fields and the well-posedness of the closest‑point projection onto
\mathscr{S}
.

Theorem 1

(
DDSPN_{0}
)
.

Let
f\in R^{\circ}_{p}
. Then
DDSPN_{0}
admits at least one solution

(s_{f},\tilde{s})\in W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})\times\mathscr{S}

satisfying

\Div s_{f}+f=0,\quad\Pi s_{f}=0.

Moreover, the equilibrated stress field
s_{f}
is uniquely determined, whereas the auxiliary field
\tilde{s}
not need to be unique.

The proof of this main theorem rests on two fundamental ingredients. First, the divergence operator induces a topological isomorphism between the quotient space
W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})/\Kernel\left(\Div|_{W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\right)
and the space
R^{\circ}_{p}
, which guarantees the existence of admissible stress fields for every balanced loading field
f
. The additional constraint
\Pi s=0
then selects the unique representative of minimal norm within the corresponding solution equivalence class. Second, the finiteness of the stress data set
\mathfrak{S}
guarantees that the associated admissible auxiliary stress fields
\mathscr{S}
are proximinal in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
. Consequently, the distance-minimization problem admits solutions.

The organization of this manuscript is as follows. Section 2 introduces the mathematical machinery required for the analysis, and Section 3 presents the proof of the main result.

Theorem 6

(Helmholtz-Weyl decomposition)
.

Introduce the subspaces

M:=\{\Grads u:u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\}=\Image(\Grads|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})})

and

N:=\{s\in W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}):\Div s=0\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\}=\Kernel\left(\Div|_{W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\right).

Then,
M
and
N
are closed in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
, and
M\oplus N=L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
.

Proof.

Part I – Closedness of
M
.

Let
\{s_{n}\}\subset M
with
s_{n}\to s
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
.
For each
n
, choose
u_{n}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

s_{n}=\Grads u_{n}.

Replace
u_{n}
by its equivalence class
[u_{n}]
in the quotient space
\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})/R_{p}
,
so that symmetric gradients are unchanged.
In this quotient setting, Korn’s second inequality applies directly.
Hence, there exists
C>0
such that

\|\Grad u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{N})}\leq C\|\Grads u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}=C\|s_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}.

Since
s_{n}\to s
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
, the sequence
\{\Grads u_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{N})
.
By Poincaré’s inequality,
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Because
1<p<\infty
, the space
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is reflexive.
Hence there exists
\{u_{n_{k}}\}
and
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

u_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

In particular,
\Grads u_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup\Grads u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
.
But since
s_{n}=\Grads u_{n}\to s
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
, the weak limit must coincide with the strong limit. Thus

\Grads u=s.

Hence
s\in M
, proving that
M
is closed in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
.

Part II – Closedness of
N
.

The space
W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
is a Banach space when equipped with the graph norm

\|s\|_{\Div}=\|s\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}+\|\Div s\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}~\forall~s\in W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}).

Since
N=\Kernel\left(\Div|_{W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\right)
is the kernel of a bounded linear operator between Banach spaces, it follows that
N
is closed.

Part III – The decomposition.
To show that
M
and
N
span
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
, we observe that if
s\in M\cap N
, then

\langle\Grads\varphi,s\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}),L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}=-\langle\varphi,\Div s\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0~\forall~\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Since
s\in M
, there exists a unique class
[u]\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})/R_{p}
such that
s=\Grads[u]
. By the density of
\mathcal{D}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the above identity extends to

\langle\Grads\varphi,\Grads u\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}),L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}=0~\forall~\varphi\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Applying Korn’s first inequality combined with Poincaré’s inequality, we have

\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{q}\|\Grads\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}~\forall~\varphi\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Applying Korn’s second inequality on the quotient space, we have

\|[u]\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{p}\|\Grads[u]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}~\forall~[u]\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})/R_{p}.

Then, for any nonzero
\varphi\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have the following progression

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq[u]\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})/R_{p}}\frac{\langle\Grads\varphi,\Grads[u]\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}),L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}}{\|[u]\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0\neq[u]\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})/R_{p}}\frac{|\langle\Grads\varphi,\Grads[u]\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}),L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}|}{C_{p}\|\Grads[u]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0\neq s\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\frac{|\langle\Grads\varphi,s\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}),L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}|}{C_{p}\|s\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C_{p}}\|\Grads\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C_{p}C_{q}}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})},

which yields the
\inf-\sup
condition

\inf_{0\neq\varphi\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\sup_{0\neq[u]\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})/R_{p}}\frac{\langle\Grads\varphi,\Grads[u]\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}),L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}}{\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|[u]\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\geq\frac{1}{C_{p}C_{q}}>0.

This result establishes weak coercivity and injectivity for the gradient operator; consequently, since the pairing is zero for all
\varphi\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the
\inf-\sup
bound forces
\Grads[u]=0
. Hence
s=\Grads[u]=0
, proving
M\cap N=\{0\}
.

In addition, since
M
and
N
are closed, the Closed Range Theorem establishes duality through the annihilator, i.e.,
M=N^{\circ}
and
N^{\circ}=M
. It follows that
\{0\}=M\cap N=N^{\circ}\cap M^{\circ}=(M+N)^{\circ}
.

To show that
M+N
is closed, let
\{s_{n}\}\in M+N
be such that
s_{n}\to s\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
. Writing
s_{n}=s_{M_{n}}+s_{N_{n}}
(where
s_{M_{n}}\in M
and
s_{N_{n}}\in N
) and noting that
\langle s_{N_{n}},\Grad\varphi\rangle_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}),L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}=0
for any
\varphi\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Observe that

\displaystyle|\langle s_{M_{n}},\Grad\varphi\rangle_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}),L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}|

\displaystyle=|\langle s_{n},\Grad\varphi\rangle_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N}),L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}|

\displaystyle\leq\|s_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\|\Grads\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\|s_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}

implies
\|s_{M_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\leq C\|s_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}
.
Since
\{s_{n}\}
is convergent, it is bounded. Thus,
\{s_{M_{n}}\}
and
\{s_{N_{n}}=s_{n}-s_{M_{n}}\}
are bounded in the reflexive space
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
(
1<p<\infty
). By closedness (and weak closedness) of
M
and
N
, there exist limits
s_{M}\in M
and
s_{N}\in N
such that
s=s_{M}+s_{N}\in M+N
. Consequently, the sum
M+N
is closed.

Due to the triviality of the annihilator
(M+N)^{\circ}
and the closedness of
M+N
, we conclude that
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})=M\oplus N
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
f\in R^{\circ}_{p}
. Then,

\Div s+f=0

in
\Omega
and
s\nu=0
on
\Gamma
has a unique solution equivalent class
[s]\in W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})/\Kernel(\Div|_{W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})})
and this equilibrated stress can be represented as
[s]=\Grads[v]
for a unique equivalence class
[v]\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})/R_{p}
. Moreover, the representative element
s_{f}\in[s]
satisfying
\Pi s_{f}=0
, is the unique element of
[s]
with minimal
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
-norm.

Proof.

Part I – Existence.

The range of
-\Div:W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})\to L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the annihilator of the kernel of its adjoint. Due to the reflexivity of Lebesgue spaces for
1<p<\infty
, we have
(L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}\cong L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

-\Div|_{W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}^{*}=\Grads|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{**}=\Grads|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Since
\Kernel\left(\Grads|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)=R_{p}
, the Closed Range Theorem implies

\Image\left(-\Div|_{W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\right)=R^{\circ}_{p}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Since
f\in R^{\circ}_{p}
, there exists
s\in W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
such that
\Div s+f=0
.

Part II – The solution as a gradient.

Since
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})=M\oplus N
, the solution from the previous part decomposes
s=s_{M}+s_{N}
, where
s_{M}\in M
and
s_{N}\in N
. By the definition of annihilator
N=\Kernel\left(\Div|_{W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\right)
, we observe

-f=\Div s=\Div(s_{M}+s_{N})=\Div(s_{M}).

Since
s_{M}\in M
, there exists a unique class
[v]\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})/R_{p}
such that
s_{M}=\Grads[v].

Part III – Uniqueness.

If there exists another solution
s^{\prime}\in M\cap W^{\Div,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
,
s_{M}-s^{\prime}
satisfies
\Div(s_{M}-s^{\prime})=0
, which implies
s_{M}-s^{\prime}\in N
. Since
s_{M}-s^{\prime}
is also in
M
and
M\cap N={0}
,
s_{M}-s^{\prime}
vanishes, ensuring solution’s uniqueness.

Part IV – Minimal norm.
Let
s_{f}\in M
(
\Pi s_{f}=0
) and
s\in[s]
be such that
s-s_{f}=\Pi s
. Due to the strict convexity of the norm, we have

\|s\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}=\|s_{f}+\Pi s\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}\geq\|s_{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})}.

Theorem 8
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)

\tilde{s}^{*}(\omega)\in\arg\left\{\min_{\mathfrak{s}\in\mathfrak{S}}\|s(\omega)-\mathfrak{s}\|_{F}\right\}.

Moreover, if the tie-set, i.e.,

\Omega_{T}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{s}_{i},\mathfrak{s}_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}\\
\mathfrak{s}_{i}\neq\mathfrak{s}_{j}\end{subarray}}\{\omega\in\Omega:\|s(\omega)-\mathfrak{s}_{i}\|_{F}=\|s(\omega)-\mathfrak{s}_{j}\|_{F}\},

satisfies
\mu(\Omega_{T})=0
, then
\tilde{s}^{*}
is unique
\mu
-almost everywhere.

Proof.

Part I – Pointwise minimization.

Since
\mathfrak{S}
is finite, the minimization problem at each point
\omega\in\Omega
is a search over a discrete set. For a fixed
\omega
, we define the function
g_{\omega}(\mathfrak{s})=\|s(\omega)-\mathfrak{s}\|_{F}
.
On a finite set, the “minimization” simply reduces to

\min\left\{g_{\omega}(\mathfrak{s}_{1}),...,g_{\omega}(\mathfrak{s}_{|\mathfrak{S}|})\right\}.

The minimum of finitely many real numbers always exists and is attained (not necessarily unique).

Part II – Existence.

Recall first that
\mathbb{S}^{N}
is a separable finite-dimensional normed space (hence Polish) and with its well-defined Borel
\sigma
-algebra . Since a minimizer exists for each
\omega\in\Omega
, we can define a mapping
\tilde{s}^{*}:\Omega\to\mathfrak{S}
. For each
\omega
, choose
\mathfrak{s}_{i}
with the smallest index among all elements of
\arg\left\{\min_{\mathfrak{s}\in\mathfrak{S}}\|s(\omega)-\mathfrak{s}\|_{F}\right\}
. Because
s
is a measurable function (as it belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
) and
\mathfrak{S}
is finite, the selection of the nearest point in
\mathfrak{S}
results in a measurable simple function. Thus, the measurable selection
\tilde{s}^{*}
exists and belongs to
L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
, and by extension to
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{N})
.

Part III – Uniqueness.

For distinct
\mathfrak{s}_{i},\mathfrak{s}_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}
, define the measurable function
f_{ij}:=\|s(\omega)-\mathfrak{s}_{i}\|_{F}-\|s(\omega)-\mathfrak{s}_{j}\|_{F}
. Then the set of points where
\mathfrak{s}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{j}
tie is
\Omega_{T_{ij}}=f_{ij}^{-1}\{0\}
. Because
\{0\}
is a Borel set on
\mathbb{R}
and
f_{ij}
is measurable, each
\Omega_{T_{ij}}\in\mathcal{F}
is measurable. Thus, the full tie-set is measurable. The minimizer fails to be unique exactly on
\Omega_{T}
. If
\mu(\Omega_{T})=0
, the set of points where the
\arg\left\{\min_{\mathfrak{s}\in\mathfrak{S}}\|s(\omega)-\mathfrak{s}\|_{F}\right\}
is not a singleton is negligible. Therefore, the selection
\tilde{s}^{*}
is unique
\mu
-almost everywhere.

Part IV – Voronoi interpretation and measurable partition.
Because
\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathbb{S}^{N}
is finite, it induces a Voronoi tessellation of
\mathbb{S}^{N}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{F}
. For each
\mathfrak{s}_{i}\in\mathfrak{S}
, define its Frobenius-Voronoi cell

V_{i}:=\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{N}:\|\sigma-\mathfrak{s}_{i}\|_{F}\leq\|\sigma-\mathfrak{s}_{j}\|_{F}~\forall~\mathfrak{s}_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}\right\}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{s}_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}}\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{N}:\|\sigma-\mathfrak{s}_{i}\|_{F}-\|\sigma-\mathfrak{s}_{j}\|_{F}\leq 0\right\}.

Each
V_{i}
is a closed Borel set in
\mathbb{S}^{N}
, since it is a finite intersection of preimages of
(-\infty,0]
under continuous maps
\sigma\mapsto\|\sigma-\mathfrak{s}_{i}\|_{F}-\|\sigma-\mathfrak{s}_{j}\|_{F}
. Consequently, the preimage
\Omega_{i}:=s^{-1}(V_{i})=\{\omega\in\Omega:s(\omega)\in V_{i}\}
is measurable for every
i
. On
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{T}
, the cells are disjoint, so
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\mathfrak{S}|}

form a measurable partition (up to null set) of
\Omega
: for
\mu
-almost every
\omega
, the selector equals the unique label of the Frobenius-Voronoi cell containing
s(\omega)
, i.e.,

\tilde{s}^{*}(\omega)=\mathfrak{s}_{i}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\omega\in\Omega_{i}\mathrm{~and~}\omega\notin\Omega_{T}.

∎
