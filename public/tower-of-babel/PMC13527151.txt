Enhanced human pose estimation via self-distilled and token-pruned transformer
Authors: Jundu Zhang, Zhengjie Deng, Xiyan Li, Sijian Yan, Zhirui Li, Chang Liu, Weidong Ke
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Human pose estimation (HPE) is a fundamental challenge in computer vision, aiming to detect anatomical keypoints in images. Traditional methods rely on CNN models, but recent advancements in Vision Transformer (ViT) models have shown superior performance. However, ViTs often require substantial computational resources. This paper introduces SPTPose, a method that employs self-distillation and token pruning to reduce computational costs while maintaining high performance. Our SPTPose-B achieves a mAP of 74.8% on the MSCOCO validation set with only 13.2 million parameters and 4.7 GFLOPs. The source code is available at https://github.com/duduxx123/SPTPose.

Introduction

2D Human Pose Estimation (HPE) is a fundamental problem in computer vision that aims to detect human anatomical keypoints in images. Traditional pose estimation methods are based on CNN models. For example, HRNet1 performs pose estimation while maintaining high-resolution images, while SimpleBaseLines2 uses transposed convolution to upsample feature maps and achieves good results.

In recent years, Vision Transformer (ViT) models have demonstrated remarkable performance across a variety of visual tasks, motivating an increasing number of researchers to explore Transformer-based approaches3,4 for human pose estimation. The Transformer implements a large number of self-attention mechanisms in the model, making these methods more powerful than traditional CNN-based methods in capturing global information. However, pure transformer models often require a large number of parameters and computational resources in order to achieve high performance. When the scale of ViT is insufficient, transformer-based models struggle to effectively learn the relationships between keypoints and visual elements, leading to performance degradation. Although stacking more transformer encoder layers can improve the performance, it increases the network size.

One effective strategy to improve the performance of compact models involves leveraging knowledge from larger models5,6. However, previous knowledge distillation methods have two shortcomings: (1) Aligning vector spaces during the feature extraction, which may lead to performance degradation; (2) Training a separate teacher network, which requires a significant additional cost.

SDPose7 introduces a cyclic forwarding scheme and designs a self-distillation method to alleviate the contradiction between the scale and the performance in human pose estimation7. It designed the Multi-Cyclic Transformer (MCT) module to increase potential depth without adding extra parameters. To reduce training overhead, SDPose adopts an efficient and lightweight self-distillation strategy, in which tokenized features are fed into the MCT module during training. Since both the input and output of each iteration share the same vector space, knowledge from later iterations can be directly transferred to earlier ones without additional processing. During inference, only one pass is performed to maintain the original inference computational complexity.

When performing self-distillation, SDPose focuses more on global information, which can achieve higher performance. However, this also leads the model to pay attention to the image’s background unrelated to pose estimation, which interferes with the model’s recognition of global human information and limits further performance improvement. Inspired by PPT8, this paper designed a human token recognition module based on the MCT module of SDPose to trim off the background information. It also added a Channel Attention Module (CAM) to both CNN and transformer to extract global features, thereby helping the transformer focus on human token information.

Based on SDPose, this paper designs a series of models: SPTPose-S-V1, SPTPose-S-V2, and SPTPose-B. Our models achieve higher performance while reducing inference computational complexity.

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

We design a Token Cropping Module that assists the model during self-distillation by focusing on global human body information rather than irrelevant background information. This module effectively improves performance while simultaneously reducing inference computational complexity.

We introduce a Channel Attention Module (CAM) to facilitate the model’s extraction of more discriminative global features.

A series of tests have been conducted to demonstrate the performance and broad utility of the proposed method across different tasks.

Related works

Human pose estimation

Currently, deep learning approaches represent the most effective solutions for Human Pose Estimation (HPE), and they are typically classified into heatmap-based and regression-based methods.

By directly regressing keypoint locations, regression-based techniques provide coordinate-level predictions. Toshev et al.9 were the first to introduce CNNs for estimating 2D joint positions in images. Subsequently, cascaded networks10,11 have been widely used in human keypoint prediction tasks. Notably, models built upon transformer structures—such as Poseur12—have shown high-quality prediction results. In addition to top-down and heatmap-based methods, HigherHRNet13 uses a bottom-up approach to first estimate all human keypoints and then perform matching. By modeling the uncertainty in coordinate regression, RLE14 and colleagues enhanced the regression-based learning framework.

Heatmap-based methods use probabilistic maps to predict the likelihood of keypoints appearing in the image and decode them into coordinates. Tompson et al.15 were the first to introduce heatmaps into pose estimation. Heatmaps have now become the main output representation because as their dimensions closely match the structure of the input image, this alignment reduces the burden of representation learning in neural networks.

The transformer module has been validated as an effective tool for feature extraction and pose estimation in both types of output representations. In particular, Yang et al.6 employed transformer encoders to convert CNN-produced feature maps into representations of keypoints. Li et al.3 designed token representations for keypoints and feature maps, and provided learnable keypoint tokens and visual tokens extracted by a shallow CNN network as inputs to the transformer module.

Lightweight CNN-based networks have already achieved good performance with a significant reduction in parameters and inference overhead, such as Lite-HRNet16 and Dite-HRNet17. Some studies leave the network architecture untouched and instead exploit sample differences for speed-up. For instance, DynPose18 uses a light router to pick a small or large network per sample, speeding up without retraining.

Several recent works have introduced lightweight human pose estimation models built upon transformer architectures. Ye et al.8 proposed DistilPose, which facilitates distillation to a regression-based student from a heatmap-based teacher by leveraging pseudo-heatmaps for loss computation. Although DistilPose achieved good performance in regression methods, The distillation approach relies on separately pre-training the teacher network. In contrast, PPT8 by Ma et al. reduces computational cost by removing low-attention tokens in TokenPose3. Although computational complexity is reduced by PPT without the introduction of new parameters, its performance cost was somewhat reduced compared to TokenPose. Chen et al.‘s SDPose7 improved performance without increasing additional parameters or training overhead, but it did not reduce the inference computational load. Previous approaches did not manage to reduce computation costs while achieving SOTA results via optimization, whereas our approach attains this balance.

Knowledge distillation

Knowledge distillation can train more powerful models with fewer resources. Hinton et al.19 first proposed knowledge distillation to compress models by facilitating the transfer of knowledge from the teacher network to the student one. Recently, the concept of self-distillation within a single model has been explored. The “Be your own teacher” method20 distills knowledge from deeper to shallower layers inside the same network. Reborn neural networks21 extend this idea temporally by cycling through the model multiple times, using previous iteration outputs to supervise current learning. Our work applies a human token recognizer to a transformer-based self-distillation HPE model, aiming to enhance the potential depth of the model while reducing its computational complexity.

Method

During training, the feature maps output by the CNN first pass through the Channel Attention Module (CAM) to capture the correlation of spatial information within the feature maps. Subsequently, they enter the Human Token Identification (HIT) module for pruning. Finally, self-distillation is performed on the MCT module. We conclude by introducing the loss functions utilized in the self-distillation architecture. The overall model architecture is illustrated in Fig. 1.

MCT module and self-distillation

Using the MCT module often results in a substantial increase in computational overhead. Therefore, we apply self-distillation to the MCT module, enabling it to enhance model performance without incurring additional computational costs. We treat the output of a latter MCT cycle as the teacher and extract its knowledge into the output of the earlier cycle, which acts as the student. Hybrid-Pose22 demonstrates that combining multiple proven modules significantly boosts performance. In contrast, SPTPose performs self-distillation between two successive passes, using the later pass as the teacher for the earlier one—an implicit cascade of knowledge rather than extra parameters. Within transformer layers, the input and output tokens reside in an identical embedding space, output tokens from various cycles can be extracted with negligible information degradation.

Specifically, if the number of cycles in the MCT module is set to N, we use the outputs \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:V{T}_{i}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:K{T}_{i}$$\end{document} to distill knowledge into the outputs \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:V{T}_{i-1}$$\end{document}​ and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:K{T}_{i-1}$$\end{document} of the previous cycle. During training, we extract the outputs from all cycles and progressively distill knowledge into the first cycle’s output. In inference, we perform only a single pass to ensure that the computational cost remains unchanged. According to the SDPose7 paper, the model’s output in later cycles focuses more on global information. Therefore, we enable the HTI module to prune tokens and remove irrelevant background information during the first run of the MCT module, ensuring that the model focuses more on global human information during subsequent cycles.

Finally, we use a single prediction head to predict all keypoint tokens and visual tokens obtained through N cycles. We calculate the loss using the ground truth for all predictions to constrain the correctness of all tokens.

Channel attention module

Context plays a vital role in inferring human poses. To enhance contextual understanding, SDPose7 employs a shallow CNN network for initial feature extraction. However, this operation focuses only on local receptive fields and fails to account for the relationships among multiple locations in the global view. The structure of CAM is shown in Fig. 3.

To model spatial dependencies across the feature map, the CAM module from DANet23 is integrated into the network. It refines the representation at each location by aggregating features from all positions, weighted by their pairwise similarity. Similar features at any two positions, regardless of distance, can promote each other.

Loss Function

For the output tokens of the (i + 1)-th cycle, we calculate the loss with respect to the outputs of the i-th cycle. Specifically, our loss is designed as:6\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{L}_{kt}=\sum\:_{i=1}^{N-1}\:MSE\left(K{T}_{i},K{T}_{i+1}\right)$$\end{document}7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{L}_{vt}=\sum\:_{i=1}^{N-1}\:MSE\left(V{T}_{i},V{T}_{i+1}\right)$$\end{document}

To measure the token-level differences, the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is adopted due to its proven reliability. Specifically, we employ MSE with mean reduction, which normalizes the loss by the total number of elements. This implies that both visual tokens and keypoint tokens are weighted equally during the distillation process, encouraging the student cycle to learn the complete feature representation from the teacher cycle regardless of token type. Experiments

Experiments and ablation analysis were carried out on the MSCOCO dataset24 to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed SPTPose model. All experiments were conducted using the MMPose25 platform to maintain fairness across evaluations.

Datasets

Experiments were carried out on the MSCOCO dataset24. MSCOCO contains over 200,000 images of humans, each annotated with 17 keypoints (e.g., nose, left ear, etc.). We used the MSCOCO train2017 set, which comprising 57 K images, this dataset is used for training our models and evaluating different approaches. Among them, 5 K images were used for the validation set, and 20 K images were used for the test-dev set.

The evaluation employs bounding boxes and metrics that align with those adopted in prior studies1,3,6,8. This method uses Average Precision (AP) and Average Recall (AR) as evaluation criteria.

Settings and training

Our approach was implemented on SDPose-S-V1, SDPose-S-V2, and SDPose-B models. We incorporated our HTI module into the MCT module and added the CAM after the shallow CNN. The remaining model settings were kept identical to those described in the SDPose7 paper.

We named our models SPTPose-S-V1, SPTPose-S-V2, and SPTPose-B. The models were trained on a machine equipped with a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, with a batch size set to 64. The training was performed for 300 epochs using the Adam optimizer, with an initial learning rate of 1e-3 that was decreased tenfold at epochs 200 and 260. For the retention rate, we set ω = 0.7. For the hyperparameters in the loss function, we set α1 = α2 = 5e-6.

Validation results

This section evaluates the evaluation of SPTPose on the COCO val2017 and COCO test-dev2017 datasets for human keypoint detection, as shown in Tables 1 and 2. We primarily compare our method with TokenPose3, SDPose1, and PPT8. As shown in the tables, SPTPose-S-v1 achieves 73.1% AP on the Val set and 72.3% AP on the test-dev set with 2.0 GFLOPs and 6.6 M parameters, setting a new state-of-the-art (SOTA) effectiveness in small models. Compared to SDPose-S-v1, our method improves AP by 0.8% while reducing the computational cost (GFLOPs) by 16.6%. SPTPose-T, compared to SDPose-T, achieves a 0.4% increase in AP while significantly reducing the computational cost by 5.5%. For the larger model variant, SPTPose-B outperforms SDPose-B by achieving 74.8% AP with a 9.6% reduction in GFLOPs, demonstrating the effectiveness of our approach in improving performance while reducing computational overhead.

Failure case analysis

To further investigate the limitations of SPTPose, we conducted an exhaustive analysis of failure cases based on the experimental results. Our analysis reveals that the model still encounters challenges in scenarios characterized by extreme occlusion or highly complex backgrounds.

Specifically, in crowded scenes, the high similarity between tokens of adjacent individuals may mislead the Human Token Identification (HTI) module, causing it to inadvertently prune tokens that contain essential limb information. Furthermore, in cases of severe occlusion, the absence of sufficient visual cues makes it difficult for the self-distillation mechanism to accurately infer keypoint positions from the remaining sparse tokens.

Additionally, in extremely ambiguous scenarios, we observed that the fixed pruning rate may prove too aggressive, leading to the loss of subtle contextual cues necessary for localization. Future work will focus on developing adaptive pruning rates to enhance.

Conclusion

We propose SPTPose, an innovative architecture designed for human pose estimation, which includes a self-distillation module with a human token recognition module and a CAM module. Through our designed pose estimation framework, we can greatly enhance the capability of small models by reducing inference computational complexity without changing the parameter count, while achieving state-of-the-art performance among models of comparable scale.
