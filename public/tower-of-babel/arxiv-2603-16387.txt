B ​ C BC Toda chain II: symmetries. Dual picture


Abstract
In the previous paper we derived Gauss–Givental integral representation for the wave functions of quantum B ​ C BC Toda chain and also introduced Baxter operators for this model. In the present paper we prove commutativity of Baxter operators, as well as show that the constructed wave functions are symmetric with respect to signed permutations of spectral parameters and diagonalize Baxter operators. Furthermore, we derive Mellin–Barnes integral representation for the wave functions. With its help we show that wave functions satisfy dual system of difference equations with respect to spectral parameters and coincide with hyperoctahedral Whittaker functions. Finally, we give heuristic proofs of orthogonality and completeness of the wave functions.

1
Introduction

The quantum Toda chain of
BC_{n}
type is governed by the Hamiltonian
[
Skl2
,
I
]

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{BC}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e^{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}+2\alpha\,e^{-x_{1}}+\beta^{2}e^{-2x_{1}},

(1.1)

which acts on functions of
n
spatial variables
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and contains two parameters
\alpha,\beta
.
In
[
BDK
]
we found the reflection operator, which acts on functions of one variable and can be used to diagonalize
BC_{1}
Toda Hamiltonian. Furthermore, combining reflection operator with Sklyanin’s intertwining operators
[
Skl3
]
we constructed the monodromy operators satisfying reflection equation associated with
BC_{n}
system. Reductions of monodromy operators produce the so-called raising and Baxter operators. With the help of raising operators we derived the explicit expression for the wave function of
BC_{n}
system in a form of Gauss–Givental iterated integrals. Besides, we proved the commutativity of Baxter operators with
BC_{n}
Toda Hamiltonians and Baxter equation characterising their spectra.

The same technique was used in
[
ADV1
,
DKM1
,
BKP
]
for the analysis of
q
-Toda chain and Heisenberg spin chains, while for
GL_{n}
Toda chain (
\alpha=\beta=0
) the analogous construction reproduces the known results of
[
GLO2
]
obtained using representation theory of classical Lie groups
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
.

In the present paper we further study the wave functions of
BC_{n}
Toda chain using other tools known in the theory of quantum integrable systems. First, we prove commutativity and exchange relations for Baxter and raising operators constructed in
[
BDK
]
. These relations correspond to certain integral identities, which can be described and
derived in a graphical language using a few basic transformations called star-triangle and flip relations. In addition, we show that our raising and Baxter operators, as well as their products, are well defined on the spaces of polynomially bounded continuous functions.

Second, using the relations between the Baxter and raising operators, we diagonalize the Baxter operators and establish the symmetry of the wave functions with respect to signed permutations of the spectral parameters.

Next, we compute the scalar product between
GL_{n}
and
BC_{n}
wave functions. This computation leads us to the Mellin–Barnes integral representation of
BC_{n}
wave functions, generalizing Iorgov and Shadura results
[
IS
]
for
B_{n}
system (
\beta=0
). Further, we show that Gauss–Givental and Mellin–Barnes integral representations are essentially sufficient to prove the orthogonality and completeness relations for the
BC_{n}
wave functions.

It is known that properly normalized wave functions of
BC_{n}
Toda chain, also called hyperoctahedral Whittaker functions, enjoy difference equations in spectral parameters
[
DE2
]
. To verify the dual equations for our wave functions we note that the kernel of Mellin–Barnes representation can be regarded as Ruijsenaars
BC_{n}
—
GL_{n}
kernel function, which satisfies corresponding difference equations. In addition, we found the second kernel function and another Mellin–Barnes integral representation for
BC_{n}
wave functions.

Finally, following the strategy of
[
HR
,
BCDK
]
we establish asymptotics of our wave function and identify it with the hyperoctahedral Whittaker function by van Diejen and Emsiz. The Mellin–Barnes iterative integral can be calculated by residues and the result is the precise formula for the corresponding Harish-Chandra series.

In the following subsections we briefly recall the construction of commuting Hamiltonians and their eigenfunctions for both
GL_{n}
and
BC_{n}
Toda chains (see
[
BDK
]
and references therein for details), and then state the main results of this paper.

1.1
Hamiltonians

For the description of quantum Toda chains of
GL
and
BC
types we need two basic blocks: Lax matrix associated with the
j
-th particle
[
F
,
G
,
Skl1
]

\displaystyle L_{j}(u)=\begin{pmatrix}u+\mathrm{i}\partial_{x_{j}}&e^{-x_{j}}\\[4.0pt]
-e^{x_{j}}&0\end{pmatrix}

(1.2)

and the reflection (boundary) matrix

\displaystyle K(u)=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha&u-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\\[6.0pt]
-\beta^{2}\bigl(u-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\bigr)&-\alpha\end{pmatrix}.

(1.3)

With their help we construct monodromy matrix
T_{n}(u)

\displaystyle T_{n}(u)=L_{n}(u)\cdots L_{1}(u)=\begin{pmatrix}A_{n}(u)&B_{n}(u)\\[4.0pt]
C_{n}(u)&D_{n}(u)\end{pmatrix},

(1.4)

which serves for
GL
Toda chain, and monodromy matrix
\mathbb{T}_{n}(u)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{T}_{n}(u)=T_{n}(u)\,K(u)\,\sigma_{2}\,T_{n}^{t}(-u)\,\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{A}_{n}(u)&\mathbb{B}_{n}(u)\\[4.0pt]
\mathbb{C}_{n}(u)&\mathbb{D}_{n}(u)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mathrm{i}\\[4.0pt]
\mathrm{i}&0\end{pmatrix}\end{aligned},

(1.5)

which desribes
BC
Toda system (to distinguish objects related to
BC
system we often use the
mathbb
font). The elements
A_{n}(u)
and
\mathbb{B}_{n}(u)
are generating functions for commuting Hamiltonians of
GL
and
BC
Toda chains correspondingly

\displaystyle A_{n}(u)=u^{n}+\sum_{s=1}^{n}u^{n-s}\,\mathrm{H}_{s},

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt[\mathrm{H}_{s},\mathrm{H}_{r}]=0,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{n}(u)=(-1)^{n}\biggl(u-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\biggr)\biggl(u^{2n}+\sum_{s=1}^{n}u^{2(n-s)}\,\mathbb{H}_{s}\biggr),

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt[\mathbb{H}_{s},\mathbb{H}_{r}]=0.

(1.7)

The first coefficients are related to the quadratic Hamiltonian (
1.1
), namely,
\mathbb{H}_{1}=-\mathbb{H}_{BC}
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{i}\partial_{x_{j}},\qquad\mathrm{H}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\mathrm{H}_{1}^{2}-\mathrm{H}_{GL}\bigr),

(1.8)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{GL}=\mathbb{H}_{BC}\bigr|_{\alpha=\beta=0}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e^{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}.

(1.9)

Two other special cases of
BC
Toda system are
B
Toda chain (
\alpha\not=0
,
\beta=0
) and
C
Toda chain (
\alpha=0
,
\beta\not=0
).

1.2
Wave functions and Baxter operators

Denote tuples of
n
variables and sums of their components

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{n}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),\qquad\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}=x_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}.

(1.10)

The wave functions of
GL
and
BC
systems are parametrized by zeroes of the generating functions eigenvalues

\displaystyle A_{n}(u)\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(u-\lambda_{j})\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{n}(u)\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=(-1)^{n}\biggl(u-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\biggr)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(u^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(1.12)

As shown in
[
BDK
]
, they can be constructed using the so-called raising operators. To introduce these operators denote

\displaystyle g=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}.

(1.13)

Besides, in what follows we always assume conditions

\displaystyle g>0,\qquad\beta>0.

(1.14)

The raising operators are integral operators acting on functions
\varphi(\bm{x}_{n-1})
by the formulas

\displaystyle\bigl[\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\;\Lambda_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,\varphi(\bm{y}_{n-1}),

(1.15)

\displaystyle\bigl[\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\;\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,\varphi(\bm{y}_{n-1}),

(1.16)

where the kernels are given by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})=\exp\biggl(\mathrm{i}\lambda\bigl(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}\bigr)-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(e^{x_{j}-y_{j}}+e^{y_{j}-x_{j+1}})\biggr),

(1.17)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})=\frac{(2\beta)^{\mathrm{i}\lambda}}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{z}_{n}\;(1+\beta e^{-z_{1}})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda-g}\;(1-\beta e^{-z_{1}})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\,\theta(z_{1}-\ln\beta)\\[6.0pt]
&\quad\times\exp\biggl(\mathrm{i}\lambda\bigl(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}+\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}-2\underline{\bm{z}}_{n}\bigr)-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(e^{z_{j}-x_{j}}+e^{z_{j}-y_{j}}+e^{x_{j}-z_{j+1}}+e^{y_{j}-z_{j+1}})-e^{z_{n}-x_{n}}\biggr).\end{aligned}

(1.18)

Here and in what follows by
\theta(x)
we denote the Heaviside step function

\displaystyle\theta(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&x\geq 0,\\[4.0pt]
&0,&&x<0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.19)

The wave functions are defined by iterative integrals

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\cdots\Lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1})\cdot 1,

(1.20)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda_{n})\cdots\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}(\lambda_{1})\cdot 1,

(1.21)

which by tradition are called Gauss–Givental representations. Note that from the explicit formula (
1.17
) we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(\lambda+\rho)=e^{\mathrm{i}\rho\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}}\,\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\rho\underline{\bm{x}}_{n-1}},

(1.22)

and consequently,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}+\rho\bm{e}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{\mathrm{i}\rho\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}}\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),\qquad\bm{e}_{n}=(1,\dots,1).

(1.23)

Example 1
.

In the case
n=1
we have
\Phi_{\lambda}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Psi_{\lambda}(x)&=\frac{e^{\frac{x}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\beta}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2}-g,-\mathrm{i}\lambda}(2\beta e^{-x})\\[6.0pt]
&=\frac{(2\beta)^{\mathrm{i}\lambda}}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\int_{\ln\beta}^{\infty}dz\;e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(x-2z)-e^{z-x}}\,(1+\beta e^{-z})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda-g}\,(1-\beta e^{-z})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1},\end{aligned}

(1.24)

where
W_{\kappa,\mu}(z)
is the Whittaker function
[
DLMF
,
Chapter 13
]
. The above integral representation for it is well known
[
DLMF
,
(13.16.5)
]
.

The close relatives of the raising operators are Baxter operators. These are integral operators acting on functions
\varphi(\bm{x}_{n})
by the formulas

\displaystyle\bigl[Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;Q_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})\,\varphi(\bm{y}_{n}),

(1.25)

\displaystyle\bigl[\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})\,\varphi(\bm{y}_{n}),

(1.26)

where the kernels are given by

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})=\exp\biggl(\mathrm{i}\lambda\bigl(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n}\bigr)-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(e^{x_{j}-y_{j}}+e^{y_{j}-x_{j+1}})-e^{x_{n}-y_{n}}\biggr),

(1.27)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{Q}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})&=\frac{(2\beta)^{\mathrm{i}\lambda}}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}d\bm{z}_{n+1}\;(1+\beta e^{-z_{1}})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda-g}\;(1-\beta e^{-z_{1}})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\,\theta(z_{1}-\ln\beta)\\[6.0pt]
&\times\exp\biggl(\mathrm{i}\lambda\bigl(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}+\underline{\bm{y}}_{n}-2\underline{\bm{z}}_{n+1}\bigr)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e^{z_{j}-x_{j}}+e^{z_{j}-y_{j}}+e^{x_{j}-z_{j+1}}+e^{y_{j}-z_{j+1}})\biggr).\end{aligned}

(1.28)

These operators commute with the Hamiltonians of the corresponding systems

\displaystyle\bigl[Q_{n}(\lambda),A_{n}(u)\bigr]=0,\qquad\bigl[\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda),\mathbb{B}_{n}(u)\bigr]=0,

(1.29)

and also satisfy Baxter equations

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\lambda)\,A_{n}(\lambda)=\mathrm{i}^{-n}\,Q_{n}(\lambda-\mathrm{i}),\quad\quad\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{B}_{n}(\lambda)=-\frac{\beta(g+\mathrm{i}\lambda)}{2\lambda}\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda-\mathrm{i}),

(1.30)

see
[
BDK
]
.

As shown in Appendix
B
, all of the above integral operators are well defined on the spaces of polynomially bounded continuous functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}=\bigl\{\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon\quad|\varphi(\bm{x}_{n})|\leq P(|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|),\quad P\text{ --- polynomial}\bigr\}.

(1.31)

Namely, by Propositions
B.1
,
B.2
we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)\colon\;\mathcal{P}_{n-1}\;\to\;\mathcal{P}_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

(1.32)

\displaystyle\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda)\colon\;\mathcal{P}_{n-1}\;\to\;\mathcal{P}_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

(1.33)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\lambda)\colon\;\mathcal{P}_{n}\;\to\;\mathcal{P}_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\Im\lambda<0,

(1.34)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\colon\;\mathcal{P}_{n}\;\to\;\mathcal{P}_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\Im\lambda\in(-g,0).

(1.35)

As a result, the products of these operators are well defined. In particular, the Gauss–Givental representations (
1.20
), (
1.21
) converge and wave functions
\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
are polynomially bounded.

In the previous paper
[
BDK
]
we also proved that the wave function
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
decays rapidly in the classically forbidden regions
x_{j+1}\ll x_{j}
, as well as
x_{1}\ll 0
, see Proposition
B.3
here for the precise statement.

In

all relations with operators hold on the spaces of exponentially tempered smooth functions, which are suited for the action of monodromy matrix entries. In this paper we work with the spaces

that are more suitable for the products of integral operators: note that the Baxter operators do not preserve the space

Main results

In what follows we use shorthand notation for the products of gamma functions

\displaystyle\Gamma(a\pm b)=\Gamma(a+b)\,\Gamma(a-b),\qquad\Gamma(\pm a\pm b)=\Gamma(a+b)\,\Gamma(a-b)\,\Gamma(-a+b)\,\Gamma(-a-b).

(1.36)

In Section
2

BC

Propositions
2.1
,
2.3
.

The relations

\displaystyle\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda)=\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(-\lambda),

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n+1}(\lambda)\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\rho)=\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n+1}(\rho)\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda),

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\lambda,\rho\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\rho)=\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\rho)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda),

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\Im\lambda,\,\Im\rho\in(-g,0),

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\rho)=\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}\rho)\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\rho)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n-1}(\lambda),

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\Im\lambda\in(-g,0),\,\rho\in\mathbb{R},

hold on the spaces
\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
correspondingly.

In the last identity for

we denote

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda}\,\Gamma(2\mathrm{i}\lambda)}{\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\mathrm{Id}.

(1.37)

The above relations imply that the

Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
,
\bm{\varepsilon}_{n}\in\{1,-1\}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Psi_{\varepsilon_{1}\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\lambda_{\sigma(n)}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(1.38)

Theorem
2.2
.

Let
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Im\lambda\in(-g,0)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda}\,\Gamma(2\mathrm{i}\lambda)}{\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

Note that the above symmetry implies non-degeneracy of the Hamiltonians eigenvalues (

), while the last formula is in accordance with the Baxter equation (

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n+1}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n}(\rho)=\Lambda_{n+1}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n}(\rho),

(1.39)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\lambda)\,Q_{n}(\rho)=Q_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n}(\lambda),

(1.40)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n}(\rho)=\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda-\mathrm{i}\rho)\,\Lambda_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda),

(1.41)

which lead to the analogous statements for the

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Phi_{\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\lambda_{\sigma(n)}}(\bm{x}_{n}),\qquad\sigma\in S_{n},

(1.42)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

Proposition
2.5
.

Let
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\bm{\gamma}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\Im\gamma_{1}=\ldots=\Im\gamma_{n}<0
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n}\;\Phi_{-\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}\prod\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}.

It is known that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n}\;\overline{\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})}\,\Phi_{\bm{\rho}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\frac{1}{\hat{\mu}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})}\,\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\delta(\lambda_{1}-\rho_{\sigma(1)})\cdots\delta(\lambda_{n}-\rho_{\sigma(n)}),

(1.44)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{\lambda}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,\overline{\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})}\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{y}_{n})=\delta(x_{1}-y_{1})\cdots\delta(x_{n}-y_{n})

(1.45)

with the spectral measure

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!\,(2\pi)^{n}}\prod_{1\leq j\not=k\leq n}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})},

(1.46)

see

,
K
]

Theorem
2.3
.

For
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)^{n}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}\prod\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(1.47)

Note that the symmetry with respect to signed permutations of spectral parameters (
1.38
) becomes evident from this representation.

The formula (
1.47
) generalizes Iorgov and Shadura result for
B
Toda chain
[
IS
, (26)]
, which can be obtained in the limit
\beta\to 0
. In this limit the integrand simplifies

\displaystyle\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\bigr)}\sim\biggl[\frac{(e\beta/\alpha)^{\alpha/\beta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\biggr]^{n}\,(2\alpha)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}},\qquad\beta\to 0,

(1.48)

due to Stirling formula.

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{e^{\frac{x}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\beta}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2}-g,-\mathrm{i}\lambda}(2\beta e^{-x})=e^{\beta e^{-x}}\int_{\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}\frac{d\gamma}{2\pi}\;\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma}\,\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma+\mathrm{i}\lambda)\,\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}{\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\gamma)}\;e^{\mathrm{i}\gamma x},

(1.49)

which is well-known formula for Whittaker function
[

,
(13.16.12)
]
.

The obtained Mellin–Barnes representation for

2.7

The following proposition is needed to derive Mellin–Barnes representation of the

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\bm{\rho}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\Im\rho_{1}=\ldots=\Im\rho_{n}<0
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n}\;\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\rho}}_{n}}\prod\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{j}+\mathrm{i}\rho_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\rho_{j})}.

(2.44)

The proof is by induction over

. Denote the above integral as

\displaystyle I(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n}\;\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}}).

(2.45)

The following lemmas represent base case and induction step.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

,
\Im\rho<0

\displaystyle I(\lambda,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\;\Phi_{-\rho}(x)\,\Psi_{\lambda}(x)\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x})=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\rho}\,\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda)}{\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\rho)}.

(2.46)

Proof.

Using Gauss–Givental representations for wave functions (see Example

) we rewrite the above integral as

(2.47)

Then it can be calculated with the help of diagrams, see Figure

. On the first step one uses chain relation (Figure
5
), and on the second step the identity from Figure

.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

such that

. Then the integral (

) satisfies the recurrence

\displaystyle I(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}}\,\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n})\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})\,\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n})}{\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\rho_{n})\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{j}+\mathrm{i}\rho_{n})}\,I(\bm{\lambda}_{n-1},\bm{\rho}_{n-1}).

Proof.

Let us present main steps of the proof, postponing the justification of interchanging integrals and other convergence issues to the end.

First, rewrite the Gauss–Givental representation for

1.20
)

\displaystyle\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Lambda_{n}(-\rho_{n})\,\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})

(2.49)

using the corresponding Baxter operator (
1.43
)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})&=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}+\mathrm{i}\rho_{j})}\,\Lambda_{n}(-\rho_{n})\,Q_{n-1}(\rho_{n})\,\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}+\mathrm{i}\rho_{j})}\\[6.0pt]
&\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{z}_{n-1}\;\Lambda_{-\rho_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,Q_{\rho_{n}}(\bm{y}_{n-1}|\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n-1}}(\bm{z}_{n-1}).\end{aligned}

(2.50)

Besides, recall the Gauss–Givental representation of
BC
wave function (
2.30
)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{s}_{n-1}\;\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{\lambda_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{s}_{n-1})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{s}_{n-1}).

(2.51)

Inserting these formulas into the integral (
2.45
) we obtain

I(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}+\mathrm{i}\rho_{j})}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{z}_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{s}_{n-1}\;\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\\[6.0pt]
\times\Lambda_{-\rho_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,Q_{\rho_{n}}(\bm{y}_{n-1}|\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{\lambda_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{s}_{n-1})\,\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n-1}}(\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{s}_{n-1}).

(2.52)

The integral over
\bm{x}_{n}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n}\;\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\,\Lambda_{-\rho_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,Q_{\rho_{n}}(\bm{y}_{n-1}|\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{\lambda_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{s}_{n-1})

(2.53)

represents kernel of the operator product

\displaystyle Q^{t}_{n-1}(\rho_{n})\,\Lambda^{t}_{n}(-\rho_{n})\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda_{n}).

(2.54)

The definition of transposed operators is given in (
2.42
).

By Proposition
2.4
, the relation

Q^{t}_{n-1}(\rho_{n})\,\Lambda^{t}_{n}(-\rho_{n})\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda_{n})\\[6.0pt]
=\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n})\;Q^{t}_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\,Q^{t}_{n-1}(-\lambda_{n})\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\,\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{n-1}(\rho_{n})

(2.55)

holds as the equality between corresponding kernels. Applying it to the integral (
2.52
) we arrive at

I(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})=\frac{\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n})}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}+\mathrm{i}\rho_{j})}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{z}_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{s}_{n-1}\;\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\\[6.0pt]
\times Q_{-\lambda_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,Q_{\lambda_{n}}(\bm{y}_{n-1}|\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{\rho_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}|\bm{s}_{n-1})\,\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n-1}}(\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{s}_{n-1}).

(2.56)

Now the integral over
\bm{s}_{n-1}
can be calculated, since it represents action of
BC
Baxter operator on its eigenfunction (
2.37
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{s}_{n-1}\;\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{\rho_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}|\bm{s}_{n-1})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{s}_{n-1})=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}}}{\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\rho_{n})}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).

(2.57)

Analogously for the integrals over
\bm{y}_{n-1}
and
\bm{z}_{n-1}
we use (
1.43
)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{z}_{n-1}\;Q_{-\lambda_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,Q_{\lambda_{n}}(\bm{y}_{n-1}|\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n-1}}(\bm{z}_{n-1})\\
=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n})\,\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).

(2.58)

Inserting these results into (
2.56
) we are left with the integral over
\bm{x}_{n-1}
, which gives the claimed relation (
2.48
).

It remain to justify all of the above manipulations. Denote

\displaystyle\Re\rho_{j}=\gamma_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\Im\rho_{j}=-\varepsilon<0.

(2.59)

Then due to the property (
1.23
)

\displaystyle\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}}\,\Phi_{-\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(2.60)

First, let us prove that the initial integral (
2.52
) is absolutely convergent. Combining Corollaries
B.1
,
B.2
we have the estimate

\displaystyle\bigl|e^{-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\,\Phi_{-\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\bigr|\leq C(1+|\bm{x}_{n}|)^{-d}

(2.61)

for any
d\geq 0
, which implies convergence.

Next, notice that the representation (
2.50
) can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho_{n}+\mathrm{i}\rho_{j})}\,e^{-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}}\,\Lambda_{n}(-\gamma_{n})\,Q_{n-1}(\gamma_{n}-2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)\,\Phi_{-\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).

(2.62)

Since
\Phi_{-\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
(Corollary
B.1
) and operators
Q_{n-1}(\gamma_{n}-2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)
,
\Lambda_{n}(-\gamma_{n})
are well defined on
\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
(Proposition
B.1
), the above representation is convergent.

Now consider the multiple integral (
2.52
). The integrand is estimated as

\bigl|\Lambda_{-\rho_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,Q_{\rho_{n}}(\bm{y}_{n-1}|\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{\lambda_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{s}_{n-1})\,\Phi_{-\bm{\rho}_{n-1}}(\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{s}_{n-1})\bigr|\\[6.0pt]
\leq C(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,e^{-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}}\,\Lambda_{0}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,Q_{-2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}(\bm{y}_{n-1}|\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{0}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{s}_{n-1})\,\Phi_{0,\dots,0}(\bm{z}_{n-1})\,\Psi_{0,\dots,0}(\bm{s}_{n-1}).

(2.63)

Hence, the integral is absolutely convergent at least in initial order, when it equals the original expression (
2.45
) with
\bm{\lambda}_{n}=(0,\dots,0)
and
\bm{\rho}_{n}=-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(1,\dots,1)
, whose convergence is already established. Therefore, by Fubini–Tonelli theorem it converges in any order, and we can integrate over
\bm{x}_{n}
first.

Analogous arguments can be applied to the integral (
2.56
): it is absolutely convergent in the order, when we evaluate integrals (
2.57
), (
2.58
) first, since in that case it boils down to the initial expression (
2.45
) with decreased number of variables (
n\to n-1
).
∎

2.8
From Gauss–Givental to Mellin–Barnes

In this section we prove that
BC
Toda wave function defined by means of Gauss–Givental integral also admits Mellin–Barnes representation, which generalizes Iorgov–Shadura formula
[
IS
, (26)]
.

Recall
GL
Toda spectral measure (
1.46
)

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!\,(2\pi)^{n}}\prod_{j\not=k}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}

(2.64)

and define the function

\displaystyle K_{1}(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n})=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}\prod\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}.

(2.65)

Theorem 2.3
.

For
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)^{n}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,K_{1}(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(2.66)

Proof.

The proof relies on Proposition
2.5
and inversion formula for
GL
Toda wave function. Namely, it is known
[
W1
]
that smooth and sufficiently fast decaying functions
\varphi(\bm{x}_{n})
, which belong to the so called
Whittaker Schwartz space
, can be expanded in terms of
GL
Toda wave functions

\displaystyle\varphi(\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;\overline{\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{y}_{n})}\,\varphi(\bm{y}_{n}).

(2.67)

Equivalently,
GL
wave functions satisfy completeness relation (
1.45
). The definition of Whittaker Schwartz space is given in Section
B.5
.

The formula (
2.66
) can be written in the same spirit. Namely, using the relation (
1.23
)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\bm{e}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}}\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),\qquad\bm{e}_{n}=(1,\dots,1),

(2.68)

and shifting integration variables
\gamma_{j}\to\gamma_{j}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon
we arrive at

\displaystyle e^{-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,K_{1}(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\bm{e}_{n})\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(2.69)

By Corollary
B.2
, the function
e^{-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
belongs to Whittaker Schwartz space, and hence we are allowed to apply inversion formula (
2.67
) to it.

It is left to recall Proposition
2.5
, which states that the scalar product of this function with
GL
Toda wave function coincides with the kernel (
2.65
). Namely, for
\bm{\gamma}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})}=\Phi_{-\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})

(2.70)

see (
1.17
), so that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n}\;\overline{\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})}\,e^{-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n}\;\Phi_{-\bm{\gamma}_{n}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\bm{e}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\,e^{-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\\[6.0pt]
&=K_{1}(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\bm{e}_{n}).\end{aligned}

(2.71)

∎

2.9
Orthogonality

The Hamiltonians of
BC
Toda chain are formally self-adjoint with respect to the standard scalar product

\displaystyle\langle\psi|\varphi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{x}_{n}\;\overline{\psi(\bm{x}_{n})}\,\varphi(\bm{x}_{n}).

(2.72)

In this section we present heuristic calculation of the scalar product between
BC
wave functions

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}|\Psi_{\bm{\rho}_{n}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\hat{{\mu}}_{BC}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})}\,\delta_{\mathrm{sym}}(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n}),\qquad\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.73)

where we denote the measure

\displaystyle\hat{{\mu}}_{BC}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!\,(4\pi)^{n}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(g\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}{\Gamma(\pm 2\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}

(2.74)

and delta function symmetric with respect to signed permutations

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{sym}}(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!\,2^{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{n}\\[2.0pt]
\bm{\varepsilon}_{n}\in\{1,-1\}^{n}\end{subarray}}\delta\bigl(\lambda_{1}-\varepsilon_{1}\rho_{\sigma(1)}\bigr)\cdots\delta\bigl(\lambda_{n}-\varepsilon_{n}\rho_{\sigma(n)}\bigr).

(2.75)

The calculation is very similar to the one for
GL
Toda chain, see
[
Sil
, Section 4]
. Mind that most of manipulations below are formal, since we work with divergent integrals. Yet, in the case of
GL
model the analogous derivation can be made rigorous by regularizing the corresponding integrals and working with suitable space of test functions, see
[
K
, Sections 1, 2]
. We expect that the same justification can be made for the calculations below.

One particle.

In the simplest case of one particle the scalar product can be calculated in a straightforward way using Gauss–Givental representation

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Psi_{\lambda}(x)&=\frac{(2\beta)^{\mathrm{i}\lambda}}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\int_{\ln\beta}^{\infty}dy\;e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(x-2y)-e^{y-x}}\,(1+\beta e^{-y})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda-g}\,(1-\beta e^{-y})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\\[6.0pt]
&=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda}}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\beta(2t+1)e^{-x}}\,(t+1)^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda-g}\,t^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1},\end{aligned}

(2.76)

where passing to the second line we changed variable
e^{y}=\beta(2t+1)
. Inserting the last expression into the scalar product we have

\langle\Psi_{\lambda}|\Psi_{\rho}\rangle=\frac{(2\beta)^{\mathrm{i}\lambda}}{\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\rho}}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\rho)}\\[6.0pt]
\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\;e^{\mathrm{i}(\rho-\lambda)x-2\beta(t+s+1)e^{-x}}\,(t+1)^{\mathrm{i}\lambda-g}\,t^{\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\,(s+1)^{-\mathrm{i}\rho-g}\,s^{-\mathrm{i}\rho+g-1}.

(2.77)

Changing the variable
x\to x+\ln(2\beta(t+s+1))
we calculate the integral over
x

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\;e^{\mathrm{i}(\rho-\lambda)x-2\beta(t+s+1)e^{-x}}=(2\beta(t+s+1))^{\mathrm{i}(\rho-\lambda)}\,\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda-\mathrm{i}\rho),

(2.78)

where for the right hand side to make sense we assume
\lambda\not=\rho
. In the remaining double integral we change the integration variable
s\to(t+1)s

\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\;(t+s+1)^{\mathrm{i}(\rho-\lambda)}\,(t+1)^{\mathrm{i}\lambda-g}\,t^{\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\,(s+1)^{-\mathrm{i}\rho-g}\,s^{-\mathrm{i}\rho+g-1}\\[6.0pt]
=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\;t^{\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\,(s+1)^{\mathrm{i}(\rho-\lambda)}\,(st+s+1)^{-\mathrm{i}\rho-g}\,s^{-\mathrm{i}\rho+g-1}.

(2.79)

After another change of integration variable
t\to(s+1)t/s
the integrals separate and can be evaluated explicitly

\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;t^{\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\,(s+1)^{\mathrm{i}(\rho-\lambda)}\,(st+s+1)^{-\mathrm{i}\rho-g}\,s^{-\mathrm{i}\rho+g-1}\\[6.0pt]
=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;t^{\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\,(t+1)^{-\mathrm{i}\rho-g}\,s^{-\mathrm{i}(\lambda+\rho)-1}=\frac{\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\lambda)\,\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\rho-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}{\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\rho)}\,2\pi\delta(\lambda+\rho),

(2.80)

where we used

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,s^{-\mathrm{i}(\lambda+\rho)-1}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,e^{-\mathrm{i}p(\lambda+\rho)}=2\pi\delta(\lambda+\rho).

(2.81)

Collecting everything together we arrive at

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{\lambda}|\Psi_{\rho}\rangle=2\pi\frac{\Gamma(\pm 2\mathrm{i}\lambda)}{\Gamma(g\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\delta(\lambda+\rho),

(2.82)

where we assume
\lambda\not=\rho
. Since wave functions are even in spectral parameters, the general formula can be restored by symmetry

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{\lambda}|\Psi_{\rho}\rangle=4\pi\frac{\Gamma(\pm 2\mathrm{i}\lambda)}{\Gamma(g\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\frac{\delta(\lambda+\rho)+\delta(\lambda-\rho)}{2}.

(2.83)

It coincides with the claimed expression (
2.73
).

Many particles.

Using Gauss–Givental recursive formula we can rewrite the scalar product in the form

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}|\Psi_{\bm{\rho}_{n}}\rangle=\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}^{t}(\lambda_{n})\cdots\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}^{t}(\lambda_{1})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\rho_{n})\cdots\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}(\rho_{1})\cdot 1,

(2.84)

the transposed operators are defined in (
2.42
). The calculation is based on the local relation

\displaystyle\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}^{t}(\lambda)\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\rho)=\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}\rho)\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n-1}(\rho)\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n-1}^{t}(\lambda),\qquad n\geq 2.

(2.85)

Let us postpone its proof to the end of this section. Applying it many times we have

\displaystyle\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}^{t}(\lambda_{1})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\rho_{n})\cdots\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}(\rho_{1})\cdot 1=\prod_{k=2}^{n}\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{1}\pm\mathrm{i}\rho_{k})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n-1}(\rho_{n})\cdots\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}(\rho_{2})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}^{t}(\lambda_{1})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}(\rho_{1})\cdot 1.

(2.86)

Here from the right we have the scalar product of one particle wave functions

\displaystyle\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}^{t}(\lambda_{1})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}(\rho_{1})\cdot 1=\langle\Psi_{\lambda_{1}}|\Psi_{\rho_{1}}\rangle,

(2.87)

while the rest part represents wave function for
n-1
particles

\displaystyle\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}^{t}(\lambda_{1})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\rho_{n})\cdots\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}(\rho_{1})\cdot 1=\langle\Psi_{\lambda_{1}}|\Psi_{\rho_{1}}\rangle\prod_{k=2}^{n}\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{1}\pm\mathrm{i}\rho_{k})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n-1}(\rho_{n})\cdots\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{1}(\rho_{2})\cdot 1.

(2.88)

Therefore, the whole scalar product (
2.84
) breaks into the simplest ones

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}|\Psi_{\bm{\rho}_{n}}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\biggl[\langle\Psi_{\lambda_{j}}|\Psi_{\rho_{j}}\rangle\prod_{k=j+1}^{n}\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\rho_{k})\biggr].

(2.89)

For the right hand side to make sense we assume
\lambda_{j}\not=\pm\rho_{k}
for
j\not=k
. Inserting one particle scalar products (
2.83
) we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}|\Psi_{\bm{\rho}_{n}}\rangle&=(4\pi)^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(\pm 2\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}{\Gamma(g\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})\\[6.0pt]
&\times\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\bm{\varepsilon}_{n}\in\{1,-1\}^{n}}\delta(\lambda_{1}-\varepsilon_{1}\rho_{1})\cdots\delta(\lambda_{n}-\varepsilon_{n}\rho_{n}).\end{aligned}

(2.90)

The general formula (
2.73
) without restrictions on
\lambda_{j},\rho_{k}
is restored using symmetry of wave functions with respect to permutations of spectral parameters (Theorem
2.1
).

3
Dual system

In the previous sections we dealt with operators acting in the space of spatial variables
x_{j}
. On the other hand,
GL
and
BC
Toda wave functions can be also studied as functions of spectral variables
\lambda_{j}
, which involves analogous objects, such as Hamiltonians and integral operators, acting in the spectral space.

3.1
GL
system

In this section we recall the known facts about
GL
Toda chain: dual Hamiltonians, dual Baxter and raising operators, Mellin–Barnes representation of wave function.

3.1.1
Dual Hamiltonians, raising and Baxter operators

Dual Hamiltonians acting on functions of spectral variables
\varphi(\bm{\lambda}_{n})
are given by
[
B
]

\displaystyle\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{s}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\[1.0pt]
|J|=s\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
k\not\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k}}\,\prod_{j\in J}e^{-\mathrm{i}\partial_{\lambda_{j}}},

(3.1)

the simplest ones are

\displaystyle\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\,\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k}}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\partial_{\lambda_{j}}},\qquad\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{n}=e^{-\mathrm{i}(\partial_{\lambda_{1}}+\ldots+\partial_{\lambda_{n}})}.

(3.2)

Here
e^{-\mathrm{i}\partial_{\lambda}}\varphi(\lambda)=\varphi(\lambda-\mathrm{i})
, so these operators are well defined on the functions analytic in
\lambda_{j}
in the strips
\Im\lambda_{j}\in[-1,0]
.
The Hamiltonians are formally self-adjoint with respect to the scalar product

\displaystyle\langle\psi|\varphi\rangle_{\hat{\mu}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{\lambda}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,\overline{\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{n})}\,\varphi(\bm{\lambda}_{n})

(3.3)

with the measure

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!\,(2\pi)^{n}}\prod_{1\leq j\not=k\leq n}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}.

(3.4)

Note that this function appears in the orthogonality relation for
GL
wave functions (
1.44
).

Dual raising operator
\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(x)
acts on functions
\varphi(\bm{\lambda}_{n-1})
by the formula

\displaystyle\bigl[\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(x)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}0)^{n-1}}\!d\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\,e^{\mathrm{i}(\underline{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n-1})x}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\varphi(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}).

(3.5)

Dual Baxter operator
\hat{Q}_{n}(x)
acts on functions
\varphi(\bm{\lambda}_{n})
by the formula

\displaystyle\bigl[\hat{Q}_{n}(x)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}0)^{n}}\!d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,e^{\mathrm{i}(\underline{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n})x}\,\prod_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\varphi(\bm{\gamma}_{n}).

(3.6)

These operators are defined in
[
GLO1
, Section 3]
, although essentially the raising operator appears in the earlier work
[
KL2
, (4.3)]
.

Denote the reflection operator

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}\colon\;\varphi(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\;\mapsto\;\varphi(-\bm{\lambda}_{n}).

(3.7)

The family of Hamiltonians is preserved under its action

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}\,\hat{H}_{s}\,\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}=\hat{H}_{n}^{-1}\,\hat{H}_{n-s}.

(3.8)

Define reflected raising and Baxter operators

\displaystyle\hat{\Lambda}^{\prime}(x)=\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}\,\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(-x)\,\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{n},\qquad\hat{Q}^{\prime}_{n}(x)=\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}\,\hat{Q}_{n}(-x)\,\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{n},

(3.9)

so that in particular,

\displaystyle\bigl[\hat{Q}^{\prime}_{n}(x)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}0)^{n}}\!d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,e^{\mathrm{i}(\underline{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n})x}\,\prod_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k}-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})\,\varphi(\bm{\gamma}_{n}).

(3.10)

3.1.2
Local relations and eigenfunctions

The above operators satisfy local relations

\displaystyle\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{s}\,\hat{Q}_{n}(x)=\hat{Q}_{n}(x)\,\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{s},

(3.11)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{s}\,\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(x)=e^{x}\,\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(x)\,\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{s-1},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{n}(x)\,\hat{Q}_{n}(y)=\hat{Q}_{n}\bigl(-\ln(e^{-x}+e^{-y})\bigr)=\hat{Q}_{n}(y)\,\hat{Q}_{n}(x),

(3.13)

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{n}(x)\,\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(y)=\exp(-e^{y-x})\,\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(y),

(3.14)

where in the second identity for
s=1
we denote
\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{0}=\mathrm{Id}
.

The first two can be proved using symmetry of Hamiltonians with respect to the scalar product (
3.3
) and simple kernel function type of identity
[
B
]

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|I|=s\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\not\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
a=1,\ldots,n\end{subarray}}(x_{i}-y_{a})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|I|=s\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in I\\
b\not\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{y_{b}-y_{a}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in I\\
i=1,\ldots,n\end{subarray}}(x_{i}-y_{a}),

(3.15)

or its degeneration as
y_{n}\to\infty
. The last two relations follow from degenerations of Gustafson identity (
D.1
).

In fact, in the present paper we won’t need the above relations, but only some of their corollaries. Notice that the Mellin–Barnes representation of
GL
Toda wave functions, mentioned in the introduction (
1.50
), is given by iterative formula

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(x_{n})\cdots\hat{\Lambda}_{1}(x_{1})\cdot 1.

(3.16)

Moreover, using Gauss–Givental representation (
1.20
) one can easily prove the symmetry

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Phi_{-\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(-x_{n},\dots,-x_{1}),

(3.17)

which in terms of dual operators reads

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\hat{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{n}(x_{1})\cdots\hat{\Lambda}_{1}^{\prime}(x_{n})\cdot 1.

(3.18)

Thus, from the above relations and definitions
GL
Toda wave functions diagonalize dual Hamiltonians and Baxter operators

\displaystyle\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{s}\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{x_{n-s+1}+\ldots+x_{n}}\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(3.19)

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{n}(x)\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\exp(-e^{x_{n}-x})\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(3.20)

\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{\prime}_{n}(x)\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\exp(-e^{x-x_{1}})\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(3.21)

Diagonalization of dual Hamiltonians is proven in
[
B
, Section 4]
, while diagonalization of Baxter operators is shown in
[
GLO1
, Proposition 3.2]
. Note that by Corollary
C.1
the Mellin–Barnes representation (
3.16
) can be analytically continued to
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, so that the action of dual Hamiltonians (
3.1
) is well defined.

Orthogonality relation for wave functions in the space of spectral parameters is equivalent to completeness relation in the space of spatial parameters

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{\lambda}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,\overline{\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})}\,\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{y}_{n})=\delta(x_{1}-y_{1})\cdots\delta(x_{n}-y_{n}).

(3.22)

It is proved by different methods in
[
W1
]
,
[
K
, Section 3]
,
[
DKM2
, Section 2]
.

3.2
BC
system

3.2.1
Dual Hamiltonians

In this section we describe the dual Hamiltonians derived in
[
DE2
, Theorem 3]
. Introduce rational functions

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &B(u_{1},u_{2})=\frac{1}{u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2}},\qquad C(u_{1},u_{2})=\frac{1}{(u_{1}+u_{2})(u_{1}+u_{2}+1)}\end{aligned}

(3.23)

and their products for any subsets of indices
I,J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
(such that
I\cap J=\varnothing
)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &B_{I,J}(\bm{u}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\in J\end{subarray}}B(u_{i},u_{j}),\\
&C_{I}(\bm{u}_{n})=\prod_{i\in I}(u_{i}+g)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in I\\
i\leq j\end{subarray}}C(u_{i},u_{j}),\\
&C^{\prime}_{I}(\bm{u}_{n})=\prod_{i\in I}(u_{i}+g)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in I\\
i\leq j\end{subarray}}(-C(u_{i},u_{j})).\end{aligned}

(3.24)

Besides, for any subset
I
define symmetrizer

\mathrm{Refl}_{I}=\prod_{i\in I}\mathrm{Refl}_{i},\qquad\mathrm{Refl}_{i}\bigl[\varphi(u_{i})\bigr]=\varphi(u_{i})+\varphi(-u_{i}).

(3.25)

Dual van Diejen–Emsiz Hamiltonians are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbb{H}}_{s}=(2\beta)^{s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J,K:\ |K|=n-s,\\
I\sqcup J\sqcup K=\{1,\dots,n\}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\mathrm{Refl}_{I\sqcup J}\biggl[B_{I,J}(\mathrm{i}\bm{\lambda}_{n})B_{I,K}(\mathrm{i}\bm{\lambda}_{n})B_{J,K}(\mathrm{i}\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,C_{I}(\mathrm{i}\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,C^{\prime}_{J}(\mathrm{i}\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,\,\prod_{i\in I}e^{-\mathrm{i}\partial_{\lambda_{i}}}\biggr].

(3.26)

In particular, for
s=1

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbb{H}}_{1}=2\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\not=i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2}}\Biggl[\,\frac{\mathrm{i}\lambda_{i}+g}{2\lambda_{i}(2\lambda_{i}-\mathrm{i})}\,\bigl(e^{-\mathrm{i}\partial_{\lambda_{i}}}-1\bigr)+\frac{-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{i}+g}{2\lambda_{i}(2\lambda_{i}+\mathrm{i})}\,\bigl(e^{\mathrm{i}\partial_{\lambda_{i}}}-1\bigr)\Biggr].

(3.27)

Dual Hamiltonians are formally self-adjoint with respect to the scalar product

\displaystyle\langle\psi|\varphi\rangle_{\hat{{\mu}}_{BC}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{\lambda}_{n}\;\hat{{\mu}}_{BC}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,\overline{\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{n})}\,\varphi(\bm{\lambda}_{n})

(3.28)

with the measure (
2.74
)

\displaystyle\hat{{\mu}}_{BC}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!\,(4\pi)^{n}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(g\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}{\Gamma(\pm 2\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}.

(3.29)

Moreover, each summand of the operator (
3.26
) is formally self-adjoint. Due to factorized form of coefficients, it is sufficient to check the identities

\displaystyle\frac{m(\lambda+\mathrm{i})}{m(\lambda)}=\frac{\overline{c(\lambda)}}{c(\lambda+\mathrm{i})},\qquad\frac{m(\lambda+\mathrm{i},\rho+\mathrm{i})}{m(\lambda,\rho)}=\frac{\overline{c(\lambda,\rho)}}{c(\lambda+\mathrm{i},\rho+\mathrm{i})}

(3.30)

where for all real
\lambda,\rho
and
a

\displaystyle c(\lambda)=\frac{\mathrm{i}\lambda+g}{2\lambda(2\lambda-\mathrm{i})(\lambda^{2}-a^{2})},

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptm(\lambda)=\frac{\Gamma(g\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda)}{\Gamma(\pm 2\mathrm{i}\lambda)\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}a)},

(3.31)

\displaystyle{c}(\lambda,\rho)=\frac{1}{(\lambda+\rho)(\lambda+\rho-\mathrm{i})},

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptm(\lambda,\rho)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}\rho)}.

(3.32)

3.2.2
GL
–
BC
intertwiners and Mellin–Barnes integrals

In Section
2.8
we derived Mellin–Barnes representation for the
BC
wave functions. The kernel of this representation is given by the function (
2.65
)

\displaystyle K_{1}(\bm{\lambda}_{m},\bm{\gamma}_{n})=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}

(3.33)

with
m=n
. Consider the similar kernel function

\displaystyle K_{2}(\bm{\lambda}_{m},\bm{\gamma}_{n})=\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(g\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}.

(3.34)

To prove that
BC
wave functions diagonalize dual Hamiltonians (
3.26
) we need the following identity.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
n\leq m

kernel functions
K_{1}(\bm{\lambda}_{m},\bm{\gamma}_{n})
and
K_{2}(\bm{\lambda}_{m},\bm{\gamma}_{n})
intertwine dual Hamiltonians of
GL
and
BC
types

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbb{H}}_{s}(\bm{\lambda}_{m})\,K_{i}(\bm{\lambda}_{m},\bm{\gamma}_{n})=\hat{\mathrm{H}}_{s}(-\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,K_{i}(\bm{\lambda}_{m},\bm{\gamma}_{n}),\qquad i=1,2,\ \ s=1,\ldots,n.

(3.35)

Proof.

By straightforward calculation, the relation (
3.35
) for the function
K_{1}(\bm{\lambda}_{m},\bm{\gamma}_{n})
is equivalent to the following identity on rational functions

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J,K:\ |K|=m-s,\\
I\sqcup J\sqcup K=\{1,\dots,m\}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\mathrm{Refl}_{I\sqcup J}\biggl[B_{I,J}(\bm{x}_{m})B_{I,K}(\bm{x}_{m})B_{J,K}(\bm{x}_{m})\,C_{I}(\bm{x}_{m})\,C^{\prime}_{J}(\bm{x}_{m})D_{I}^{n}(\bm{x}_{m},\bm{y}_{n})\biggr]\\
=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L,M:\ |L|=s,\\
L\sqcup M=\{1,\dots,n\}\end{subarray}}\tilde{B}_{L,M}(\bm{y}_{n})\tilde{C}_{L}(\bm{y}_{n})\tilde{D}_{L}^{m}(\bm{x}_{m},\bm{y}_{n}),

(3.36)

where we denote

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\tilde{B}_{L,M}(\bm{y}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in L\\
b\in M\end{subarray}}\frac{y_{a}+y_{b}+1}{y_{b}-y_{a}},&&\qquad\tilde{C}_{L}(\bm{y}_{n})=\prod_{a\in L}(y_{a}+g)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in L\\
a<b\end{subarray}}(y_{a}+y_{b})(y_{a}+y_{b}+1),\\[6.0pt]
&\tilde{D}^{m}_{L}(\bm{x}_{m},\bm{y}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in L,\\
i=1,\ldots,m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{y_{a}^{2}-x_{i}^{2}},&&\qquad D_{I}^{n}(\bm{x}_{m},\bm{y}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I,\\
a=1,\ldots,n\end{subarray}}\frac{y_{a}+x_{i}+1}{y_{a}-x_{i}}.\end{aligned}

(3.37)

Let us prove the identity (
3.36
). Its both sides are rational functions of
\bm{x}_{n}
and
\bm{y}_{n}
, symmetric with respect to permutations of the variables
\bm{y}_{n}
and with respect to permutations and sign changes of variables
\bm{x}_{m}
. Besides, they are of degree less then zero with respect to each variable
x_{i}
. Thus, it is sufficient to prove that the difference between two sides has no poles with respect to each
x_{i}
. All the poles are simple. Symmetry implies that there are no poles of the form
x_{i}=x_{j}
and
x_{i}=0
. The only poles to check are

x_{i}=\pm\frac{1}{2},\qquad x_{i}=\pm x_{j}\pm 1,\qquad x_{i}=\pm y_{j}.

Again, due to symmetry of our functions it is sufficient to check three possibilities

x_{m}=-\frac{1}{2},\qquad x_{1}=-x_{2}-1,\qquad x_{m}=y_{n}.

Let us prove that residues at these points from different terms cancel each other.

Pole at
x_{m}=-1/2
.

Clearly, the right hand side of (
3.36
) does not have such a pole. Denote by
b_{I,J,K}
the summand of the left hand side of (
3.36
), which corresponds to the decomposition
\{1,\ldots,m\}=I\sqcup J\sqcup K
, and assume that
I
contains
m
. Denote
I^{\prime}=I\setminus\{m\}
and
J^{\prime}=J\sqcup\{m\}
. Then

\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{x_{m}=-1/2}b_{I,J,K}=-\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{x_{m}=-1/2}b_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime},K}\\[6.0pt]
=-\frac{1}{2}\biggl(g-\frac{1}{2}\biggr)\prod_{i\in I^{\prime}}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}-1/4}\prod_{j\in J}\frac{1}{1/4-x_{j}^{2}}\prod_{k\in K}\frac{1}{1/4-x_{k}^{2}}B_{I^{\prime},J}B_{I^{\prime},K}B_{J,K}C_{I^{\prime}}C^{\prime}_{J}D_{I^{\prime}}^{n}.

(3.38)

Thus, two residues cancel each other, which verifies that the total residue of the left hand side vanishes.

Pole at
x_{1}=-x_{2}-1
.

Again, the right hand side of (
3.36
) does note have such a pole. In the left hand side there are contributions from the summands
b_{I,J,K}
when either
I
or
J
contains both
1
and
2
. Let
1,2\in I
. Denote
I^{\prime\prime}=I\setminus\{1,2\}
,
J^{\prime\prime}=J\sqcup\{1,2\}
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{x_{1}=-x_{2}-1}b_{I,J,K}=-\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{x_{1}=-x_{2}-1}b_{I^{\prime\prime},J^{\prime\prime},K}\\[6.0pt]
&\quad\quad=\prod_{i\in I^{\prime\prime}}\frac{1}{(x_{i}^{2}-x_{1}^{2})(x_{i}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})}\prod_{j\in J}\frac{1}{(x_{1}^{2}-x_{j}^{2})(x_{2}^{2}-x_{j}^{2})}\prod_{k\in K}\frac{1}{(x_{1}^{2}-x_{k}^{2})(x_{2}^{2}-x_{k}^{2})}\\[6.0pt]
&\quad\quad\times\frac{1}{x_{1}+x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}+g}{2x_{1}(2x_{1}+1)}\frac{x_{2}+g}{2x_{2}(2x_{2}+1)}B_{I^{\prime\prime},J}B_{I^{\prime\prime},K}B_{J,K}C_{I^{\prime\prime}}C^{\prime}_{J}D_{I^{\prime\prime}}^{n}\Big|_{x_{1}=-x_{2}-1}.\end{aligned}

(3.39)

Again, two residues cancel each other.

Pole at
x_{m}=y_{n}
.

In the right hand side of (
3.36
) we count residues of the summands
\tilde{b}_{L,M}
for
L
that contain
n
. Denote
L^{\prime}=L\setminus\{n\}
. Then

\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{x_{m}=y_{n}}\tilde{b}_{L,M}=-\frac{y_{n}+g}{2y_{n}}\prod_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{y_{n}^{2}-x_{l}^{2}}\prod_{c=1}^{n-1}\frac{y_{c}+y_{n}+1}{y_{c}-y_{n}}\tilde{B}_{L^{\prime},M}\tilde{C}_{L^{\prime}}\tilde{D}_{L^{\prime}}^{m-1}.

(3.40)

Similarly, in the left hand side of (
3.36
) we count residues of the summands
b_{I,J,K}
for
I
containing
m
. Again denote
I^{\prime}=I\setminus\{m\}
. Then

\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{x_{m}=y_{n}}{b}_{I,J,K}=-\frac{y_{n}+g}{2y_{n}}\prod_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{y_{n}^{2}-x_{l}^{2}}\prod_{c=1}^{n-1}\frac{y_{c}+y_{n}+1}{y_{c}-y_{n}}{B}_{I^{\prime},J}B_{I^{\prime},K}B_{J.K}C_{I^{\prime}}C^{\prime}_{J}{D}_{I^{\prime}}^{n-1}.

(3.41)

Summing up (
3.41
) over all partitions appearing in (
3.36
) we conclude that the residue
\operatorname{Res}_{x_{m}=y_{n}}
of the left hand side of (
3.36
) equals to the left hand side of (
3.36
) evaluated in variables
\bm{x}_{m-1}
,
\bm{y}_{n-1}
and multiplied by the function

-\frac{y_{n}+g}{2y_{n}}\prod_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{y_{n}^{2}-x_{l}^{2}}\prod_{c=1}^{n-1}\frac{y_{c}+y_{n}+1}{y_{c}-y_{n}}.

The same holds for the right hand side of (
3.36
). This proves that the difference of two sides residues vanishes by induction over
n
. Note that in the base case
n=0
the right hand side vanishes and the left hand side is a rational function without poles of degree less then zero, so it also equals zero.

The relation (

Proposition 3.2
.

For

and

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)^{n}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,K_{2}(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

Proof.

We have to prove the equality

\displaystyle e^{\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\hskip-8.5359pt\int\limits_{(\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)^{n}}\!\!d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,\frac{\prod\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}\,(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})

\displaystyle=e^{-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\hskip-8.5359pt\int\limits_{(\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)^{n}}\!\!d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,\frac{\prod\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}\,(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

For that we need two ingredients. The first one is the action of

Here

3.2.4
Asymptotics and hyperoctahedral Whittaker function

In this section we calculate Mellin–Barnes integrals for
GL
and
BC
wave functions by residues. This gives us Harish-Chandra series representations and asymptotics of wave functions.

GL
system.

It is known that the wave function
\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
fast decreases in all asymptotical zones, except

x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{n},

(3.102)

e.g. see the bound given in
[
BC
, Proposition 4.1.3]
. In the latter zone it can be represented by a convergent series, which comes from residue calculation of Mellin–Barnes integral (
3.16
). Note that for
x_{1}\ll\ldots\ll x_{n}
the interaction between particles disappears (
1.9
).

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the set of all lower triangular matrices with zeroes on diagonal, whose elements are nonnegative integers

\mathcal{A}=\{A=(a_{ij})\in M_{n}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\,|\,a_{ij}=0\text{ for all }i\leq j\}.

(3.103)

Each matrix
A\in\mathcal{A}
determines another lower triangular matrix
P=(p_{ij})
by the rule

p_{ij}=a_{ij}+a_{i+1,j}+\ldots+a_{nj},\qquad 1\leq j<i\leq n.

(3.104)

For a given
A\in\mathcal{A}
define the meromorphic function

c_{A}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\prod\limits_{k=2}^{n}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{(-1)^{a_{ki}}}{a_{ki}!}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1,j\not=i}^{k}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})-p_{ki}+p_{k+1,j})}{\prod\limits_{j=1,\,j\not=i}^{k-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})-p_{ki}+p_{kj})},

(3.105)

where we set
p_{n+1,i}=0.

For example, for
n=3

c_{A}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})=\frac{(-1)^{a_{31}+a_{32}+a_{21}}}{a_{31}!a_{32}!a_{21}!}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})-a_{21}-a_{31}+a_{32})\\[6.0pt]
\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1})-a_{31}\right)\Gamma\left(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})-a_{31}\right)\Gamma\left(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2})-a_{32}\right)\Gamma\left(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})-a_{32}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})-a_{31}+a_{32}\right)\Gamma\left(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})-a_{32}+a_{31}\right)}.

(3.106)

Denote by
\varphi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
the series

\varphi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{\mathrm{i}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}}\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}}c_{A}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,e^{-\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j})}.

(3.107)

Then the wave function
\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
can be represented (actually for any
\bm{x}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

[
DE2
, Proposition 1]
) as the symmetrization of
\varphi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
over the spectral parameters

\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\varphi_{\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{\sigma(n)}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(3.108)

which follows from straightforward calculation of Mellin–Barnes integral (
3.16
) by residues.

Remark 4
.

For
n=2
the decomposition (
3.108
) expresses Macdonald function via modified Bessel functions
[
DLMF
,
(10.27.4)
]
,

K_{\nu}(z)=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{I_{-\nu}(z)-I_{\nu}(z)}{\sin\pi\nu}.

The nearest poles (
a_{ij}=0
) determine the asymptotics of the Mellin–Barnes integral for
x_{1}\ll\ldots\ll x_{n}
. Set

\Phi^{as}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\prod_{i<j}\Gamma\left(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{\sigma(j)}-\lambda_{\sigma(i)})\right)e^{\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{\sigma(k)}x_{k}}

(3.109)

Following the arguments presented in
[
HR
]
(see also
[
BCDK
, Section 5.1]
) one can show that in the region (
3.102
) under condition

\min_{i=1,\dots,n-1}(x_{i+1}-x_{i})=\varepsilon>0

(3.110)

we have a bound

|\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})-\Phi^{as}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})|<C(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\exp(-\varepsilon/2)

(3.111)

for any
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that
\lambda_{i}\not=\lambda_{j}
.

The eigenfunction of
GL
Toda Hamiltonians analytical in
the region

with plane wave asymptotic

is usually called the Harish-Chandra function

[

]

It can be represented in a form of convergent series over
variables

[

,

,

]

Thus,

\varphi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{i<j}\Gamma\left(\mathrm{i}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})\right)\varphi^{HC}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(3.112)

and the relation (

.

BC

Denote by

Let

(3.113)

Then the complement of

and converge uniformly in
x_{j},x_{j+1}
. This gives the needed polynomial estimate

\displaystyle\Bigl|\bigl[\Lambda^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})\Bigr|\leq P(|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|),

(B.14)

while uniformity ensures that
\bigl[\Lambda^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})
is continuous in
\bm{x}_{n}
.

Now consider the third statement (
B.11
). Due to definition

\Bigl|\bigl[Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})\Bigr|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;P(|y_{1}|,\dots,|y_{n}|)\,e^{-\Im\lambda\,(x_{n}-y_{n})-e^{x_{n}-y_{n}}}\\
\times\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}e^{-\Im\lambda\,(x_{j}-y_{j})-e^{x_{j}-y_{j}}-e^{y_{j}-x_{j+1}}}.

(B.15)

Again writing polynomial in terms of monomials we obtain the sum with terms factorised into integrals of two types

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy_{j}\;|y_{j}|^{m_{j}}\,e^{-\Im\lambda\,(x_{j}-y_{j})-e^{x_{j}-y_{j}}-e^{y_{j}-x_{j+1}}}\qquad(j=1,\dots,n-1),

(B.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy_{n}\;|y_{n}|^{m_{n}}\,e^{-\Im\lambda\,(x_{n}-y_{n})-e^{x_{n}-y_{n}}},

(B.17)

where
\Im\lambda<0
. All of them are polynomially bounded and converge uniformly in
x_{j}
by Lemmas
B.1
,
B.2
.
∎

As a by-product, from the above proposition we get the following estimate for
GL
Toda wave function

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\cdots\Lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1})\cdot 1.

(B.18)

Corollary B.1
.

Let
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, and the corresponding integral is absolutely convergent.

Proof.

The fact that wave function belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is clear from (
B.10
). The absolute convergence follows from the property of the raising operators kernels

\displaystyle\bigl|\Lambda_{\lambda_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y}_{k-1})\bigr|\leq\Lambda_{0}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{y}_{k-1}),\qquad\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{R},

(B.19)

see (
B.7
).
∎

B.2
BC
system

Introduce two axillary operators

\displaystyle\bigl[V_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\frac{(2\beta)^{\mathrm{i}\lambda}}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda-g}\,(1-\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\,\theta(y_{1}-\ln\beta)

\displaystyle\hskip 66.86414pt\times\exp\biggl(\mathrm{i}\lambda\bigl(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n}\bigr)-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(e^{y_{j}-x_{j}}+e^{x_{j}-y_{j+1}})-e^{y_{n}-x_{n}}\biggr)\;\varphi(\bm{y}_{n}),

(B.20)

\displaystyle\bigl[W_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\frac{(2\beta)^{\mathrm{i}\lambda}}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}d\bm{y}_{n+1}\;(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda-g}\,(1-\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda+g-1}\,\theta(y_{1}-\ln\beta)

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt\times\exp\biggl(\mathrm{i}\lambda\bigl(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n+1}-y_{1}\bigr)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e^{y_{j}-x_{j}}+e^{x_{j}-y_{j+1}})\biggr)\;\varphi(\bm{y}_{n+1}).

(B.21)

Then
BC
Toda raising and Baxter operators from Sections
2.2
and
2.3
can be written as the products of
GL
Toda operators with axillary ones

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda)=V_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n}(-\lambda),\\[6.0pt]
&\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)=W_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda^{\prime}_{n+1}(-\lambda),\\[6.0pt]
&\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)=V_{n}(-\lambda)\,Q_{n}(\lambda).\end{aligned}

(B.22)

This splitting is easily visualized in the language of diagrams from Section
2.1
.

By Proposition
B.1

GL
Toda operators act within the space of polynomially bounded continuous functions, so it is left to establish the same thing about axillary operators.

Lemma B.4
.

We have

\displaystyle V_{n}(\lambda)\colon\;\mathcal{P}_{n}\;\to\;\mathcal{P}_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\Im\lambda\geq 0,

(B.23)

\displaystyle W_{n}(\lambda)\colon\;\mathcal{P}_{n+1}\;\to\;\mathcal{P}_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\Im\lambda\in(-g,0).

(B.24)

Before we prove this lemma let us remark that together with Proposition
B.1
and formulas (
B.22
) it gives the following statement.

Proposition B.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\lambda)\colon\;\mathcal{P}_{n-1}\;\to\;\mathcal{P}_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

(B.25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\colon\;\mathcal{P}_{n}\;\to\;\mathcal{P}_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\Im\lambda\in(-g,0),

(B.26)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\colon\;\mathcal{P}_{n}\;\to\;\mathcal{P}_{n},

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\Im\lambda<0.

(B.27)

Proof of Lemma
B.4
.

First, consider the operator
V_{n}(\lambda)
. By definition (
B.20
) we have

\Bigl|\bigl[V_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})\Bigr|\leq\frac{(2\beta)^{-\Im\lambda}}{|\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)|}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;P(|y_{1}|,\dots,|y_{n}|)\,\prod_{j=2}^{n}\,e^{\Im\lambda\,(y_{j}-x_{j})-e^{x_{j-1}-y_{j}}-e^{y_{j}-x_{j}}}\\[6.0pt]
\times e^{\Im\lambda\,(y_{1}-x_{1})-e^{y_{1}-x_{1}}}\,(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda-g}\,(1-\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda+g-1}\,\theta(y_{1}-\ln\beta).

(B.28)

Expanding polynomial in terms of monomials
|y_{1}|^{m_{1}}\cdots|y_{n}|^{m_{n}}
we obtain sum with terms factorised into integrals of two types

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy_{j}\;|y_{j}|^{m_{j}}\,e^{\Im\lambda\,(y_{j}-x_{j})-e^{x_{j-1}-y_{j}}-e^{y_{j}-x_{j}}}\qquad(j=2,\dots,n),

(B.29)

\displaystyle\int_{\ln\beta}^{\infty}dy_{1}\;|y_{1}|^{m_{1}}\,e^{\Im\lambda\,(y_{1}-x_{1})-e^{y_{1}-x_{1}}}\,(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda-g}\,(1-\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda+g-1},

(B.30)

where
\Im\lambda\geq 0
. Integrals from the first line are polynomially bounded and converge uniformly in
x_{j-1},x_{j}
due to Lemma
B.2
. In the integral from the second line we can use inequality

\displaystyle(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda-g}=(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{2\Im\lambda}\,(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{-\Im\lambda-g}\leq 4^{\Im\lambda}\,(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{-\Im\lambda-g},

(B.31)

and change integration variable to
z=y_{1}-\ln\beta
, so that

\int_{\ln\beta}^{\infty}dy_{1}\;|y_{1}|^{m_{1}}\,e^{\Im\lambda\,(y_{1}-x_{1})-e^{y_{1}-x_{1}}}\,(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda-g}\,(1-\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda+g-1}\\[6.0pt]
\leq 4^{\Im\lambda}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\;(|z|+|\ln\beta|)^{m_{1}}\,e^{\Im\lambda\,(z+\ln\beta-x_{1})-e^{z+\ln\beta-x_{1}}}\,(1+e^{-z})^{-g^{\prime}}\,(1-e^{-z})^{g^{\prime}-1},

(B.32)

where we also denoted
g^{\prime}=\Im\lambda+g>0
. Then Lemma
B.3
says that the last integral is bounded polynomially and converges uniformly in
x
. Hence, the whole right hand side (
B.28
) is bounded by polynomial in
\bm{x}_{n}
and
\bigl[V_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})
is continuous.

Now consider the operator
W_{n}(\lambda)
. By definition (
B.21
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Bigl|\bigl[W_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})&\Bigr|\leq\frac{(2\beta)^{-\Im\lambda}}{|\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)|}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}d\bm{y}_{n+1}\;P(|y_{1}|,\dots,|y_{n+1}|)\\[6.0pt]
&\times e^{-\Im\lambda\,(x_{n}-y_{n+1})-e^{x_{n}-y_{n+1}}}\,\prod_{j=2}^{n}\,e^{-\Im\lambda\,(x_{j-1}-y_{j})-e^{x_{j-1}-y_{j}}-e^{y_{j}-x_{j}}}\\[6.0pt]
&\times e^{2\Im\lambda\,y_{1}-e^{y_{1}-x_{1}}}\,(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda-g}\,(1-\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda+g-1}\,\theta(y_{1}-\ln\beta).\end{aligned}

(B.33)

Again writing polynomial in terms of monomials we obtain sum with terms factorised into integrals of three types

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy_{n+1}\;|y_{n+1}|^{m_{n+1}}\,e^{-\Im\lambda\,(x_{n}-y_{n+1})-e^{x_{n}-y_{n+1}}},

(B.34)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy_{j}\;|y_{j}|^{m_{j}}\,e^{-\Im\lambda\,(x_{j-1}-y_{j})-e^{x_{j-1}-y_{j}}-e^{y_{j}-x_{j}}}\qquad(j=2,\dots,n),

(B.35)

\displaystyle\int_{\ln\beta}^{\infty}dy_{1}\;|y_{1}|^{m_{1}}\,e^{2\Im\lambda\,y_{1}-e^{y_{1}-x_{1}}}\,(1+\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda-g}\,(1-\beta e^{-y_{1}})^{\Im\lambda+g-1},

(B.36)

where
\Im\lambda<0
. The first two are polynomially bounded due to Lemmas
B.1
and
B.2
correspondingly. In the last integral we can use inequality

\displaystyle e^{2\Im\lambda\,y_{1}}=\beta^{2\Im\lambda}\,e^{2\Im\lambda\,(y_{1}-\ln\beta)}\leq\beta^{2\Im\lambda}

(B.37)

to bound it by the integral of the same type, as we already encountered (
B.30
). The remaining steps are the same, as before, so that we obtain polynomial bound for the whole expression (
B.33
).
∎

B.3
Kernels of operator products

Raising and Baxter operators of
BC
Toda split into products of two simpler operators (
B.22
). Besides, in the paper we consider local relations between various products of the above operators, such as

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\lambda)\,Q_{n}(\rho)=Q_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n}(\lambda),\qquad\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\rho)=\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\rho)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda),

(B.38)

and others. Let us prove that the kernels of all these products are given by absolutely convergent integrals.

For example, consider the
BC
Baxter operator (
B.22
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)=W_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda^{\prime}_{n+1}(-\lambda),

(B.39)

with
\Im\lambda\in(-g,0)
. By Proposition
B.2
the following integral is convergent for
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}

\displaystyle\bigl[\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}d\bm{y}_{n+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{z}_{n}\;W_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n+1})\,\Lambda^{\prime}_{-\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n+1}|\bm{z}_{n})\,\varphi(\bm{z}_{n}),

(B.40)

where in the integrand we have kernels of operators
W_{n}(\lambda)
,
\Lambda^{\prime}_{n+1}(-\lambda)
. Moreover, since

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bigl|W_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n+1})\bigr|=\biggl|\frac{\Gamma(g+\Im\lambda)}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\biggr|\;W_{\mathrm{i}\Im\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n+1}),\\[6.0pt]
&\bigl|\Lambda^{\prime}_{-\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n+1}|\bm{z}_{n})\bigr|=\Lambda^{\prime}_{-\mathrm{i}\Im\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n+1}|\bm{z}_{n})\end{aligned}

(B.41)

and
|\varphi|
is still in
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, the above integral is absolutely convergent. By Fubini–Tonelli theorem this means that we can interchange the order of integrals and write

\displaystyle\bigl[\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{z}_{n}\;\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{z}_{n})\,\varphi(\bm{z}_{n}),

(B.42)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{z}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}d\bm{y}_{n+1}\;W_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n+1})\,\Lambda^{\prime}_{-\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n+1}|\bm{z}_{n}).

(B.43)

Now notice that the kernel

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}_{-\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n+1}|\bm{z}_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\exp\bigl(\mathrm{i}\lambda(z_{j}-y_{j+1})-e^{y_{j}-z_{j}}-e^{z_{j}-y_{j+1}}\bigr),

(B.44)

as a function of
\bm{y}_{n+1}
, belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{n+1}
. Indeed, it is continuous and bounded by constant

\displaystyle\bigl|\Lambda^{\prime}_{-\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n+1}|\bm{z}_{n})\bigr|\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n}\exp\bigl(-\Im\lambda(z_{j}-y_{j+1})-e^{z_{j}-y_{j+1}}\bigr)\leq C(\lambda),

(B.45)

since
\Im\lambda<0
. By Lemma
B.4
, the operator
W_{n}(\lambda)
is well-defined on
\mathcal{P}_{n+1}
, hence, the integral (
B.43
) is convergent for
\Im\lambda\in(-g,0)
. Furthermore, because of (
B.41
) it is absolutely convergent.

The same arguments can be applied to all other kernels of operator products appearing in the paper. For example, the kernel of
Q
-operators’ product

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\rho)=W_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda^{\prime}_{n+1}(-\lambda)\,W_{n}(\rho)\,\Lambda^{\prime}_{n+1}(-\rho)

(B.46)

is given by convergent integral, since again the kernel of the last operator
\Lambda^{\prime}_{-\rho}(\bm{y}_{n+1}|\bm{z}_{n})
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{n+1}
(as a function of
\bm{y}_{n+1}
).

Let us also consider the operator product from Section
2.6

Q^{t}_{n-1}(\lambda)\,\Lambda^{t}_{n}(-\lambda)\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\,\reflectbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\mathbb V$}}}_{n}(\rho)\\[6.0pt]
=Q^{t}_{n-1}(\lambda)\,\Lambda^{t}_{n}(-\lambda)\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\,V_{n}(\rho)\,\Lambda_{n}(-\rho),\qquad\Im\lambda<0,\quad\rho\in\mathbb{R},

(B.47)

since it is slightly trickier. Here the transposed operators are defined as follows

\displaystyle\bigl[Q^{t}_{n-1}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n-1})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\;Q_{\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n-1}|\bm{x}_{n-1})\,\varphi(\bm{y}_{n-1}),

(B.48)

\displaystyle\bigl[\Lambda^{t}_{n}(-\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n-1})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;\Lambda_{-\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n}|\bm{x}_{n-1})\,\varphi(\bm{y}_{n}).

(B.49)

The kernel of the above operator product equals

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{z}_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{s}_{n}\;Q_{\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n-1}|\bm{x}_{n-1})\,\Lambda_{-\lambda}(\bm{z}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n-1})\,\exp(-\beta e^{-z_{1}})\\[6.0pt]
\times V_{\rho}(\bm{z}_{n}|\bm{s}_{n})\,\Lambda_{-\rho}(\bm{s}_{n}|\bm{t}_{n-1}).

(B.50)

Note that
\Lambda_{-\rho}(\bm{s}_{n}|\bm{t}_{n-1})
, as a function of
\bm{s}_{n}
, belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. So, again to prove that the last integral is absolutely convergent it is enough to show that the product

\displaystyle Q^{t}_{n-1}(\lambda)\,\Lambda^{t}_{n}(-\lambda)\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\,V_{n}(\rho)

(B.51)

is well defined on the space
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

From the explicit formulas (
B.7
) we have

\displaystyle\Lambda^{t}_{n}(-\lambda)\,\exp(-\beta e^{-x_{1}})\colon\;\varphi(\bm{x}_{n})\;\mapsto\;\bigl[Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\varphi\bigr](\ln\beta,\bm{x}_{n-1}).

(B.52)

Besides,

\displaystyle Q_{n-1}^{t}(\lambda)=\mathcal{I}_{n-1}\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda)\,\mathcal{I}_{n-1},\qquad\mathcal{I}_{n-1}\colon\;\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\mapsto\varphi(-x_{n-1},\dots,-x_{1}).

(B.53)

Therefore by Proposition
B.1
and Lemma
B.4
the product (
B.51
) is well defined on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

B.4
From equality of kernels to equality of operators

All local relations between
GL
and
BC
Toda operators hold on the space of polynomially bounded continuous functions. In this section we demonstrate that to establish the equality of operators on this space it is sufficient to prove the equality of kernels.

For example, consider the commutativity

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\rho)=\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\rho)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda),\qquad\Im\lambda,\,\Im\rho\in(-g,0).

(B.54)

By Proposition
B.2
both sides of this relation are well defined on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and, as we argued in Section
B.3
, the kernels of both products are given by absolutely convergent integrals.
In Section
2.4
using diagram technique we prove that these kernels are equal to each other

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})\,\mathbb{Q}_{\rho}(\bm{y}_{n}|\bm{z}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;\mathbb{Q}_{\rho}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})\,\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda}(\bm{y}_{n}|\bm{z}_{n}).

(B.55)

Let us show that, as a consequence, the identity (
B.54
) holds on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

Acting on
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
from the left we have

\displaystyle\bigl[\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\rho)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{z}_{n}\;\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})\,\mathbb{Q}_{\rho}(\bm{y}_{n}|\bm{z}_{n})\,\varphi(\bm{z}_{n}).

(B.56)

From definition

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})\bigr|=\biggl|\frac{\Gamma(g+\Im\lambda)}{\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda)}\biggr|\;\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{i}\Im\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n}),

(B.57)

and clearly
|\varphi|\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
. Hence, by Proposition
B.2
the integral (
B.56
) is absolutely convergent (in the initial order). Therefore, by Fubini–Tonelli theorem we can interchange the order of integrals

\displaystyle\bigl[\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}(\rho)\,\varphi\bigr](\bm{x}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{z}_{n}\biggl[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\;\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})\,\mathbb{Q}_{\rho}(\bm{y}_{n}|\bm{z}_{n})\biggr]\,\varphi(\bm{z}_{n}).

(B.58)

The same steps are applied to the right hand side of (
B.54
). As a result, this identity follows from the equality of kernels (
B.55
) in square brackets.

All other local identities considered in the paper hold on the space of polynomially bounded continuous functions by the same arguments.

B.5
Bounds on
BC
Toda wave function

For
\bm{k}_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
denote standardly

\displaystyle\partial_{\bm{x}_{n}}^{\bm{k}_{n}}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{k_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{n}}^{k_{n}}.

(B.59)

Also, for brevity, we introduce

\displaystyle C(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{|\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})|}.

(B.60)

In previous paper
[
BDK
]
we proved the following bound for
BC
Toda wave function.

Proposition B.3
.

[
BDK
, Proposition 1]

Let
\bm{k}_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and
\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
is smooth in
\bm{x}_{n}
and admits the bound

\Bigl|\partial_{\bm{x}_{n}}^{\bm{k}_{n}}\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\Bigr|\leq C(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,P(|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|,|\lambda_{n}|)\,\exp\Biggl(\bigl[k_{1}(\ln\beta-x_{1})-\beta e^{-x_{1}}\bigr]\,\theta(\ln\beta-x_{1})\\[2.0pt]
+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\biggl[(k_{j}+k_{j+1})\,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}{2}-e^{\frac{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}{2}}\biggr]\,\theta(x_{j}-x_{j+1})\Biggr),

(B.61)

where
P
is polynomial, whose coefficients depend on
\bm{k}_{n},\beta,g
. In particular, for
\bm{k}_{n}=(0,\dots,0)
it doesn’t depend on
\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bigl|\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\bigr|&\leq C(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,P(|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|)\\[6.0pt]
&\times\exp\Biggl(-\beta e^{-x_{1}}\,\theta(\ln\beta-x_{1})-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e^{\frac{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}{2}}\,\theta(x_{j}-x_{j+1})\Biggr).\end{aligned}

(B.62)

In Section
2.8
to prove the equivalence of Gauss–Givental and Mellin–Barnes representations for
BC
Toda wave function we use the inversion formula for
GL
Toda wave function.
According to Wallach
[
W1
]
(see also
[
W2
, Section 7]
), it holds for the functions from
Whittaker Schwartz space

\mathcal{T}
, which consists of
\psi(\bm{x}_{n})\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{x}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\exp\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}m_{j}\,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}{2}\Biggr)\,(1+|\bm{x}_{n}|)^{d}\,|\mathcal{D}\,\psi(\bm{x}_{n})|<\infty,

(B.63)

for all
m_{j},d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and constant coefficient differential operators
\mathcal{D}
. From the above proposition we have the following statement.

Corollary B.2
.

Let
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\displaystyle e^{-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\in\mathcal{T}.

(B.64)

Proof.

For brevity, denote function in question

\displaystyle\psi(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(B.65)

First, by Proposition
B.3
it is smooth. Second, let us calculate its derivatives. For any
\bm{k}_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\bm{x}_{n}}^{\bm{k}_{n}}\,\psi(\bm{x}_{n})=\sum_{i}c_{i}(\varepsilon)\,e^{\ell^{(i)}(\ln\beta-x_{1})-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\,\partial_{\bm{x}_{n}}^{\bm{s}_{n}^{(i)}}\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(B.66)

with some coefficients
c_{i}
and integers
\ell^{(i)}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\bm{s}_{n}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, such that
\ell^{(i)}\leq k_{1}
,
s_{j}^{(i)}\leq k_{j}
. Since
\ell^{(i)}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle e^{\ell^{(i)}(\ln\beta-x_{1})-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\leq e^{[\ell^{(i)}(\ln\beta-x_{1})-\beta e^{-x_{1}}]\,\theta(\ln\beta-x_{1})}.

(B.67)

Hence, using Proposition
B.3
and the fact that
\ell^{(i)}\leq k_{1}
,
s_{j}^{(i)}\leq k_{j}
we derive estimate

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bigl|\partial_{\bm{x}_{n}}^{\bm{k}_{n}}\,\psi(\bm{x}_{n})\bigr|&\leq P(|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|)\,\exp\Biggl(-\varepsilon\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}+2\bigl[k_{1}(\ln\beta-x_{1})-\beta e^{-x_{1}}\bigr]\,\theta(\ln\beta-x_{1})\\[2.0pt]
&+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\biggl[(k_{j}+k_{j+1})\,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}{2}-e^{\frac{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}{2}}\biggr]\,\theta(x_{j}-x_{j+1})\Biggr),\end{aligned}

(B.68)

which is sufficient to prove (
B.63
). Notice that function
(1+|\bm{x}_{n}|)^{d}
from (
B.63
) can be absorbed into polynomial
P

\displaystyle(1+|\bm{x}_{n}|)^{d}\,P(|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|)\leq(1+|\bm{x}_{n}|^{2})^{d}\,P(|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|),

(B.69)

while in the exponent from (
B.63
) we can add step functions

\displaystyle\exp\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}m_{j}\,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}{2}\Biggr)\leq\exp\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}m_{j}\,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}{2}\,\theta(x_{j}-x_{j+1})\Biggr),

(B.70)

so that it has the same form, as exponents from the third line (
B.68
). So, these two factors doesn’t essentially change estimate from the right hand side (
B.68
). To see that this estimate is bounded for all
\bm{x}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we pass to the variables

\displaystyle y_{1}=\ln\beta-x_{1},\qquad y_{2}=\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{2},\qquad\dots\qquad y_{n}=\frac{x_{n-1}-x_{n}}{2}.

(B.71)

Expanding polynomial
P
in monomials
|y_{1}|^{d_{1}}\cdots|y_{n}|^{d_{n}}
we can rewrite the estimate (
B.68
) as the sum of factorised functions

\displaystyle|y_{1}|^{d_{1}}\,e^{n\varepsilon\,y_{1}+2[k_{1}y_{1}-e^{y_{1}}]\,\theta(y_{1})}\,\prod_{j=2}^{n}|y_{j}|^{d_{j}}\,e^{2(n+1-j)\varepsilon y_{j}+[(k_{j}+k_{j+1})y_{j}-e^{y_{j}}]\,\theta(y_{j})},

(B.72)

Since
\varepsilon>0
, these functions are bounded for all
\bm{y}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Appendix C
Mellin–Barnes bounds and analyticity

In this section we prove that Mellin–Barnes representations for
GL
and
BC
wave functions are absolutely convergent and entire in spectral parameters. For this we use bounds on gamma functions

\displaystyle|\Gamma(a+\mathrm{i}b)|\leq\sqrt{2\pi}\,e^{\frac{1}{6a}}\,|a+\mathrm{i}b|^{a-\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|b|},

(C.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma(a+\mathrm{i}b)|}\leq\frac{e^{a+\frac{1}{6a}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,|a+\mathrm{i}b|^{-a+\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{\frac{\pi}{2}|b|},

(C.2)

where
a>0
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
. These bounds follow from
[
PK
, p. 34]
.

C.1
GL
system

The Mellin–Barnes representation for
GL
Toda wave function is defined by recursive formula

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\int\limits_{(\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}r)^{n-1}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\,e^{\mathrm{i}(\underline{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n-1})x_{n}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})

(C.3)

with one particle wave function
\Phi_{\lambda_{1}}(x_{1})=e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}
and measure

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!\,(2\pi)^{n}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\
j\not=k\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}=\frac{1}{n!\,(2\pi)^{n}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\frac{(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})\,\sh\pi(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})}{\pi}.

(C.4)

The parameter
r\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen so that poles of gamma functions

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\lambda_{j}-\mathrm{i}m,\qquad m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(C.5)

lie below the integration contours, that is
r>\Im\lambda_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,n
.

The analytic continuation of wave function in spectral variables is achieved by shifting integration contours. To prove that the above integral is absolutely convergent and, as a consequence, justify such shifts, we need the following inequality.

Lemma C.1
.

[
BDKK
, Lemma 2]

Let
(y_{m1},\dots,y_{mm})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
for
m=1,\dots n
. Then for any
\delta>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

2\sum_{m=2}^{n-1}\,\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}|y_{mj}-y_{mk}|-\sum_{m=2}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|y_{mj}-y_{m-1,k}|\\
\leq-\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|y_{nj}-y_{nk}|+\delta\sum_{j=1}^{n}|y_{nj}|-\varepsilon\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|y_{mj}|.

(C.6)

Proposition C.1
.

Let
\Im\lambda_{j}<r
for
j=1,\dots,n
. Then the integral (
C.3
) is absolutely convergent uniformly in
\lambda_{j}
from compact subsets. In particular, for
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\delta>0
we have

\displaystyle\bigl|\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\bigr|\leq P_{\delta}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,\exp\biggl(-\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}|+\delta\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|\biggr),

(C.7)

where
P_{\delta}
is polynomial.

Corollary C.1
.

The function
\Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
can be analytically continued to
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof of Proposition
C.1
.

Denote
\bm{\gamma}_{m}=(\gamma_{m1},\dots,\gamma_{mm})
with identification
\bm{\gamma}_{0}\equiv 0
and
\bm{\gamma}_{n}\equiv\bm{\lambda}_{n}
. Then Mellin–Barnes representation in its full form is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Phi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Biggl(\prod_{m=1}^{n-1}\,\int\limits_{(\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}r_{m})^{m}}d\bm{\gamma}_{m}\Biggr)&\;e^{\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}(\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{m}-\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{m-1})x_{m}}\,\prod_{m=2}^{n-1}\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{m})\\
&\times\prod_{m=2}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{mj}-\mathrm{i}\gamma_{m-1,k}),\end{aligned}

(C.8)

where
r_{1}>r_{2}>\ldots>r_{n-1}\equiv r>\Im\lambda_{j}
.
From definition (
C.4
) we have bound on the measure

\displaystyle\bigl|\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{m})\bigr|\leq P(\bm{\gamma}_{m})\,\exp\biggl(\pi\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}|\Re\gamma_{mj}-\Re\gamma_{mk}|\biggr).

(C.9)

Here and in what follows by
P
we denote polynomials. Combining this with inequality (
C.1
) we bound absolute value of the whole integrand (
C.8
) by the function

P(\Re\bm{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\Re\bm{\gamma}_{n-1},\Re\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,\exp\biggl(\,\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(x_{m+1}-x_{m})mr_{m}-x_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Im\lambda_{j}\\
+\pi\sum_{m=2}^{n-1}\,\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}|\Re\gamma_{mj}-\Re\gamma_{mk}|-\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{m=2}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|\Re\gamma_{mj}-\Re\gamma_{m-1,k}|\biggr)

(C.10)

Due to (
C.6
) for any
\delta>0
this function is bounded by

P(\Re\bm{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\Re\bm{\gamma}_{n-1},\Re\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,\exp\biggl(\,\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(x_{m+1}-x_{m})mr_{m}-x_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Im\lambda_{j}\\
-\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|\Re\lambda_{j}-\Re\lambda_{k}|+\delta\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\Re\lambda_{j}|-\varepsilon\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\Re\gamma_{mj}|\biggr)

(C.11)

where
\varepsilon>0
. This gives convergence uniform in
\lambda_{j}
from compact subsets. Besides, this estimate ensures that we can shift integration contours. Hence, for
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we can send
r_{m}\to 0
(one by one), which gives the claimed bound (
C.7
).
∎

C.2
BC
system

The first Mellin–Barnes representation for
BC
wave function is given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\hskip-8.5359pt\int\limits_{(\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}r)^{n}}\!\!d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}\prod\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}\;\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(C.12)

where
r>0
is chosen so that poles of the integrand

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\pm\lambda_{k}+\mathrm{i}m,\qquad m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(C.13)

are above the integration contours, that is
r>|\Im\lambda_{j}|
for
j=1,\dots n
. As before, to analytically continue this function in spectral parameters we shift contours, which is justified by the following proposition.

Proposition C.2
.

Let
|\Im\lambda_{j}|<r
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then the integral (
C.12
) is absolutely convergent uniformly in
\lambda_{j}
from compact subsets.

Corollary C.2
.

The function
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
can be analytically continued to
\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof of Proposition
C.2
.

First, change integration variables
\gamma_{j}=\rho_{j}-\mathrm{i}r
, so that
\rho_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, and use the relation

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{r\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}}\,\Phi_{\bm{\rho}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(C.14)

Then with the help of inequalities (
C.1
), (
C.2
), (
C.7
) and (
C.9
) we estimate the absolute value of the integrand in (
C.12
) by the function

f(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})\,\exp\biggl(\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(|\rho_{j}-\rho_{k}|+|\rho_{j}+\rho_{k}|)\\
-\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}(|\rho_{j}-\Re\lambda_{k}|+|\rho_{j}+\Re\lambda_{k}|)+\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\rho_{j}|\biggr),

(C.15)

where
f
is regular (for
|\Im\lambda_{j}|<r
,
\rho_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and polynomially bounded. Since

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|\rho_{j}\pm\rho_{k}|\leq\sum_{1\leq j\not=k\leq n}|\rho_{j}|=(n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\rho_{j}|,

(C.16)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}|\rho_{j}\pm\Re\lambda_{k}|\leq\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}(|\Re\lambda_{k}|-|\rho_{j}|)=n\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\Re\lambda_{j}|-n\sum_{j=1}|\rho_{j}|,

(C.17)

the exponential part is bounded by

\displaystyle\exp\biggl(\Bigl(\delta-\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\rho_{j}|\biggr),

(C.18)

which gives the stated convergence. This bound also allows to shift integration contours and clearly uniform in
\lambda_{j}
from compact sets.
∎

The second Mellin–Barnes representation is given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{-\beta e^{-x_{1}}}\hskip-8.5359pt\int\limits_{(\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}r)^{n}}\!\!d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\;\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\,\frac{(2\beta)^{-\mathrm{i}\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n}}\,\prod\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g-\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(g\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}\,\Phi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(C.19)

where
r
is chosen so that integration contours pass between upward and downward series of integrand poles

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}=\pm\lambda_{k}+\mathrm{i}m,\qquad\gamma_{j}=-\mathrm{i}g-\mathrm{i}m,\qquad m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(C.20)

that is
r>|\Im\lambda_{j}|
and
r<g
. Its convergence can be proven in analogous way, but this time we have analytic continuation only to the strip
|\Im\lambda_{j}|<g
.

Proposition C.3
.

Let
|\Im\lambda_{j}|<r<g
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then the integral (
C.19
) is absolutely convergent uniformly in
\lambda_{j}
from compact subsets.

Appendix D
Gustafson integral reduction

The following identity is proved in
[
G
, Theorem 9.3]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n}\,n!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{\lambda}_{n}\;\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2n+2}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma(z_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\pm 2\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}=\frac{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq 2n+2}\Gamma(z_{j}+z_{k})}{\Gamma(z_{1}+\ldots+z_{2n+2})},

(D.1)

where it is assumed that
\Re z_{j}>0
for
j=1,\dots,2n+2
. Let us prove that in the limit
z_{2n+2}\to\infty
it reduces to the formula

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n}\,n!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{\lambda}_{n}\;\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2n+1}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma(z_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\Gamma(\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j}\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\pm 2\mathrm{i}\lambda_{j})}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq 2n+1}\Gamma(z_{j}+z_{k}).

(D.2)

The absolute convergence of both integrals can be easily shown using inequality (
C.1
) and reflection formula for the gamma function.

To take the limit we use the asymptotic formula
[
DLMF
,
(5.11.12)
]

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(a+L)}{\Gamma(b+L)}\sim L^{a-b},\qquad L\to\infty.

(D.3)

Namely, denote
z_{2n+2}=L
and divide both sides of the identity (
D.1
) by
\Gamma^{2n}(L)
. Then the right hand side tends to the desired expression

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq 2n+1}\Gamma(z_{j}+z_{k})\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2n+1}\Gamma(z_{j}+L)}{\Gamma(z_{1}+\ldots+z_{2n+1}+L)\,\Gamma^{2n}(L)}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq 2n+1}\Gamma(z_{j}+z_{k}).

(D.4)

The integrand of the left hand side also tends pointwise to the integrand in the reduced formula (
D.2
), because

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma(L\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\Gamma^{2n}(L)}=1.

(D.5)

Moreover, since
|\Gamma(z)|\leq|\Gamma(\Re z)|
we have inequality

\displaystyle\Biggl|\frac{\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma(L\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda_{k})}{\Gamma^{2n}(L)}\Biggr|\leq 1.

(D.6)

Hence, we can use dominated convergence theorem to interchange limit
L\to\infty
and integration, which concludes the proof of (
D.2
).
