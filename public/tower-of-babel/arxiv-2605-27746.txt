Logarithmic oscillatory multipliers and log-subdyadic square functions
Vicente Vergara

Abstract
We study Fourier multipliers with logarithmic oscillation at high frequency. The guiding example is the radial symbol m γ , β ​ ( ξ ) = ( log ⁡ ( e + | ξ | ) ) − β ​ e i ​ ( log ⁡ ( e + | ξ | ) ) γ , γ > 1 , m_{\gamma,\beta}(\xi)=\bigl(\log(e+|\xi|)\bigr)^{-\beta}e^{i(\log(e+|\xi|))^{\gamma}},\qquad\gamma>1, whose natural frequency scale is smaller than dyadic but larger than every fixed power-subdyadic scale. We develop a square-function theory adapted to this logarithmic scale. The main square-function result is a pointwise estimate for Fourier multiplier operators whose symbols satisfy a localized logarithmic Miyachi condition. We prove the corresponding log-subdyadic frequency decomposition, the associated decoupling and recoupling estimates, and the local multiplier estimate needed to control the operator. We also establish a high-frequency weighted L 2 L^{2} multiplier estimate and derive unweighted L p L^{p} -boundedness for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty under the sufficient logarithmic decay condition β > d ⁡ ( γ − 1 ) ​ | 1 2 − 1 p | . \beta>d(\gamma-1)\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|. The logarithmic model multiplier above satisfies the localized hypothesis in the high-frequency region.

Introduction

The starting point of this paper is the observation that the subdyadic
geometry in the square-function theory of Beltran–Bennett

[
1
]
is dictated by a local oscillation scale.
This viewpoint is rooted in the earlier work of Miyachi on oscillatory
Fourier multipliers
[
5
,
6
]

and in the classical power-phase examples related to Wainger’s work

[
11
]
. For the Miyachi model
multiplier

m_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\beta}e^{i|\xi|^{\alpha}},

the radial phase is
\Phi(R)=R^{\alpha}
, and the scale on which the phase
changes by an amount comparable to one is

|\Phi^{\prime}(R)|^{-1}\sim R^{1-\alpha}.

This is precisely the radius of the
\alpha
-subdyadic balls used in the
Beltran–Bennett decomposition.

This places the argument in the broader Littlewood–Paley tradition of
Stein’s square-function method

[
8
,
9
]
. It is also related in
spirit to square functions associated with non-dyadic frequency
decompositions, such as Rubio de Francia’s square function for arbitrary
intervals
[
7
]
, and to square-function
and maximal estimates for radial Fourier multipliers

[
4
]
.

The logarithmic model considered here belongs to the broader family of
oscillatory Fourier multipliers, but it has a different geometric scale from
the power-phase examples above. Related contemporary work includes rapidly
oscillating angular symbols

[
10
]
, bilinear oscillatory
Fourier multipliers
[
3
]
,
and oscillating spectral multipliers in non-Euclidean settings

[
2
]
. These results have
different geometric hypotheses from those used below; they are cited to place the present logarithmic construction within the
broader literature on oscillatory multiplier phenomena.

The purpose of the present work is to develop the corresponding
logarithmic theory. We fix

\Phi(R)=(\log R)^{\gamma},\qquad\gamma>1,

for
R
sufficiently large. The induced frequency scale is

\rho(R)=\frac{R}{(\log R)^{\gamma-1}}.

(1)

The spatial aperture dual to this frequency scale is

a_{\gamma}(t)=\rho(t^{-1})^{-1}=t(\log(1/t))^{\gamma-1}.

(2)

Thus the logarithmic approach region is wider than the classical cone by a
logarithmic factor, while remaining much closer to dyadic geometry than the
power-subdyadic regimes.

The model multiplier is

m_{\gamma,\beta}(\xi)=\bigl(\log(e+|\xi|)\bigr)^{-\beta}e^{i(\log(e+|\xi|))^{\gamma}}.

(3)

The parameter
\beta
measures logarithmic decay. Since frequencies

|\xi|\sim t^{-1}
correspond to the logarithmic size

\log(1/t)
, the output square function carries the compensating factor

(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}.

This is the logarithmic analogue of the Beltran–Bennett normalization in
which
g_{\alpha,\beta}
carries the factor
t^{-2\beta}
for symbols
with power decay
|\xi|^{-\beta}
.

The logarithmic model considered here is motivated by, but not reducible to,
the Miyachi–Beltran–Bennett framework for oscillatory multipliers with
power phases, where the adapted frequency scale is dictated by the inverse
derivative of the radial phase. Indeed, in the high-frequency region the
first radial derivative has size

|m^{\prime}_{\gamma,\beta}(R)|\simeq(\log R)^{-\beta+\gamma-1}R^{-1},\qquad R\gg 1,

so the model lies outside the classical dyadic Mikhlin regime with
logarithmic decay. At the same time, the oscillation scale forced by the
phase is

|\Phi^{\prime}(R)|^{-1}\sim\frac{R}{(\log R)^{\gamma-1}},

which lies strictly between the dyadic scale
R
and every fixed
power-subdyadic scale
R^{1-\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, as
R\to\infty
.
Thus the model is too oscillatory for a purely dyadic Littlewood–Paley
analysis, while its natural scale is not one of the fixed power-subdyadic
scales covered by Beltran–Bennett. The present decomposition is therefore
adapted to a genuine logarithmic regime, rather than a formal reformulation of the power-subdyadic theory. The corresponding geometry and
multiplier hypothesis are formalized in Definitions

2.2
,
2.3
and Hypothesis

2.6
; the model verification is given in
Proposition
6.1
.

Equivalently, the logarithmic model can be compared with the formal
propagators behind the power-phase Beltran–Bennett examples. In the
power case one has

U_{\alpha}(t)=e^{it|D|^{\alpha}},

and the corresponding time-one smoothed multiplier is

|D|^{-\beta}U_{\alpha}(1)=|D|^{-\beta}e^{i|D|^{\alpha}}.

In the present logarithmic regime the analogous formal propagator is

U_{\log,\gamma}(t)=e^{it(\log(e+|D|))^{\gamma}},

and the model multiplier (
3
) is the logarithmically
smoothed time-one operator

(\log(e+|D|))^{-\beta}U_{\log,\gamma}(1).

The respective inverse derivative scales are
R^{1-\alpha}
and

R(\log R)^{1-\gamma}
. This comparison is only at the level of Fourier
multipliers. To the best of our knowledge, there is no systematic dispersive
or Strichartz theory for this specific logarithmic propagator. No such
estimates are claimed here.

The corresponding logarithmic square function is

g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{a_{\gamma}(t)^{d}}(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/2}.

We also use the robust square function

g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,\beta,\lambda}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}K_{t}^{\lambda}(x-y)(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/2},

where

K_{t}^{\lambda}(z)=a_{\gamma}(t)^{-d}\left(1+\frac{|z|}{a_{\gamma}(t)}\right)^{-d\lambda}.

These objects are defined precisely in Definition

2.5
.

Our first main result is a logarithmic Beltran–Bennett pointwise estimate
for the Fourier multiplier operator
T_{m}
. It says that, under the
localized logarithmic Miyachi condition
2.6
, the
logarithmic square function of
T_{m}f
is controlled pointwise by a robust
logarithmic square function of
f
:

g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}(T_{m}f)(x)\leq Cg^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}(f)(x).

(4)

More precisely, if the localized Sobolev order is
\sigma>d/2
, then one
may take

\lambda=\frac{2\sigma}{d}>1.

The constant in (
4
) may depend on

d,\gamma,\beta,\sigma,\lambda
, on the constants in the localized
Miyachi condition, and on the fixed cutoffs, but not on
f
,
x
, the
scale
t
, or the particular log-subdyadic ball. The full statement is
Theorem
4.2
.

The proof follows the Beltran–Bennett architecture

[
1
]
, but at the logarithmic scale

\rho(R)
. The log-subdyadic lattice decomposition is constructed in
Section
3
. The two geometric estimates are the
decoupling and recoupling inequalities, Propositions

3.2
and
3.4
. These are combined
with the local multiplier estimate on a single adapted ball,
Proposition
4.1
, to obtain Theorem

4.2
.

Our second main result is a weighted
L^{2}
estimate for the
high-frequency Fourier multiplier operator
T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}
. The
logarithmic replacement for the Beltran–Bennett geometric maximal operator
is

M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}w(x)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t<t_{0}\\
|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)\end{subarray}}(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta}A_{t}w(y),

where
A_{t}w=\eta_{t}*w
,
\eta_{t}(x)=t^{-d}\eta(x/t)
, and
\eta
is a
fixed nonnegative Schwartz function with integral one. Thus
A_{t}w
is a
smooth average of the weight at the physical Littlewood–Paley scale
t
,
while the supremum is taken over centers
y
lying in the larger
logarithmic tube
|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)
.
The factor
(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta}
is not a normalization convention: it
is the exact inverse of the logarithmic square-function weight appearing in

g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}
. The precise definition is given in Definition

5.1
.

The weighted theorem states that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}M^{2}\bigl(M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w\bigr)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(5)

This is the logarithmic analogue of the weighted estimate in the
Beltran–Bennett theory. It is proved by combining the pointwise estimate
(
4
), the logarithmic reverse square-function
inequality, and the forward weighted estimate for

g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}
. The full high-frequency statement is
Theorem
5.8
. A global inhomogeneous estimate
follows after adding a standard smooth compactly supported low-frequency
multiplier.

The weighted part of the argument is in the tradition of weighted
Littlewood–Paley theory, in particular Wilson’s continuous square-function
estimates
[
12
]
.

Our third main result is the corresponding unweighted
L^{p}
boundedness of
the Fourier multiplier operator
T_{m}
. For
1<p<\infty
, one has

\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

provided

\beta>d(\gamma-1)\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|.

This holds under the high-frequency localized logarithmic Miyachi hypothesis
for
m_{\mathrm{hi}}
, after writing

m=m_{\mathrm{lo}}+m_{\mathrm{hi}},

where
m_{\mathrm{lo}}
is a smooth compactly supported multiplier. For

p<2
, the high-frequency adjoint symbol
\overline{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}

is assumed to satisfy the same localized logarithmic Miyachi condition.
The proof uses the
L^{r}
-boundedness of
M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}
, Lemma

7.1
, and the final duality argument in Corollary

7.3
.

2
fixes the
logarithmic geometry, the square functions, and the localized Miyachi

4

6

(

2
Preliminaries and logarithmic geometry

Throughout the paper
d\geq 1
,
\gamma>1
, and
R_{0}\geq e^{2}
is chosen
sufficiently large, depending only on the structural constants under
discussion. Constants denoted by
C
may change from line to line.
Non-universal constants have their dependencies stated explicitly.

2.1
The logarithmic scale

For
R\geq R_{0}
, define

\rho(R)=\frac{R}{(\log R)^{\gamma-1}}.

The scale
\rho(R)
is comparable to
|\Phi^{\prime}(R)|^{-1}
for the phase
\Phi(R)=(\log R)^{\gamma}
. We also define

a_{\gamma}(t)=\rho(t^{-1})^{-1}=t(\log(1/t))^{\gamma-1},\qquad 0<t<t_{0}.

The parameter
t_{0}
is chosen sufficiently small so that the Fourier support of
\phi_{t}
lies inside the high-frequency region covered by the logarithmic partition. More precisely, after fixing
k_{0}
with
2^{k_{0}}\geq R_{0}
, we require

\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\phi_{t}}\subset\{\xi:|\xi|\geq 2^{k_{0}+1}\}\qquad\text{for all }0<t<t_{0}.

This ensures that the square functions below do not detect the low-frequency portion of a multiplier.

Lemma 2.1

(Local stability of the logarithmic scale)
.

Let
A>0
. If
R_{0}
is sufficiently large depending on
A
and
\gamma
, then

|R^{\prime}-R|\leq A\rho(R),\qquad R\geq R_{0},

implies

C_{A}^{-1}\rho(R)\leq\rho(R^{\prime})\leq C_{A}\rho(R).

Moreover, the ratio
\rho(R^{\prime})/\rho(R)
tends to
1
uniformly under this constraint as
R\to\infty
.

Proof.

Since

\rho^{\prime}(R)=(\log R)^{1-\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\gamma-1}{\log R}\right),

we have
|\rho^{\prime}(R)|\leq C\rho(R)/R
for
R\geq R_{0}
. The assumption gives

\frac{|R^{\prime}-R|}{R}\leq A(\log R)^{1-\gamma},

which tends to zero. Hence
R^{\prime}\sim R
and
\log R^{\prime}\sim\log R
, with constants depending only on
A
and
\gamma
. Substitution in the definition of
\rho
gives the claim.
∎

2.2
Log-subdyadic balls

Definition 2.2

(Log-subdyadic ball)
.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Euclidean ball. Write
r(B)
for its radius and

R_{B}=\operatorname{dist}(B,0).

The ball
B
is called log-subdyadic of order
\gamma
if
R_{B}\geq R_{0}
and

c_{0}\rho(R_{B})\leq r(B)\leq C_{0}\rho(R_{B}),

where
0<c_{0}<C_{0}<\infty
are fixed structural constants.

Definition 2.3

(Adapted normalized bump)
.

Let
N
be a fixed integer, chosen sufficiently large compared with the Sobolev exponents used below. A normalized bump adapted to a ball
B
is a function
\Psi_{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\operatorname{supp}\Psi_{B}\subset C_{1}B

for a fixed dilation constant
C_{1}
, and

|D^{\nu}\Psi_{B}(\xi)|\leq C_{\nu}r(B)^{-|\nu|},\qquad|\nu|\leq N.

All constants in statements involving adapted bumps are required to be uniform over this class.

Lemma 2.4

(Stability inside adapted balls)
.

If
B
is log-subdyadic and
\xi,\eta\in 2B
, then

|\xi|\sim|\eta|\sim R_{B},\qquad\rho(|\xi|)\sim\rho(|\eta|)\sim\rho(R_{B})\sim r(B).

The implicit constants depend only on
d
,
\gamma
, and on the structural constants in Definition
2.2
.

Proof.

For
\xi\in 2B
, the distance from
|\xi|
to
R_{B}
is bounded by a fixed multiple of
r(B)
, hence by a fixed multiple of
\rho(R_{B})
. Lemma
2.1
gives
\rho(|\xi|)\sim\rho(R_{B})
, and the remaining comparabilities follow in the same way.
∎

2.3
Fourier multipliers and maximal operators

For a bounded measurable symbol
m
, we write

T_{m}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\widehat{f})=:(m\widehat{f})^{\vee}

initially on Schwartz functions. We write
M
for the Hardy–Littlewood
maximal operator,

Mf(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y,

and
M^{j}
for its
j
-fold composition.

2.4
Square functions

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy

\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\phi}\subset\{\xi:1/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2\},

and set
\phi_{t}(x)=t^{-d}\phi(x/t)
.

Definition 2.5

(Log-subdyadic square functions)
.

For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, define

g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{a_{\gamma}(t)^{d}}(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/2}.

For
\lambda>1
, define

g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,\beta,\lambda}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{a_{\gamma}(t)}\right)^{-d\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{a_{\gamma}(t)^{d}}(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/2}.

Define the normalized kernel

K_{t}^{\lambda}(z)=a_{\gamma}(t)^{-d}\left(1+\frac{|z|}{a_{\gamma}(t)}\right)^{-d\lambda}.

(6)

For
\lambda>1
,
\|K_{t}^{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C
. We shall also use the
elementary convolution stability

K_{t}^{\lambda}*K_{t}^{\lambda}(z)\leq CK_{t}^{\lambda}(z),

(7)

uniformly in
0<t<t_{0}
. Indeed, after the change of variables

u=a_{\gamma}(t)^{-1}y
, this reduces to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|u|)^{-d\lambda}(1+|v-u|)^{-d\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}u\lesssim(1+|v|)^{-d\lambda}.

To prove this, cover the domain of integration by the two regions

|u|\geq|v|/2
and
|v-u|\geq|v|/2
. On the first region,

(1+|u|)^{-d\lambda}\lesssim(1+|v|)^{-d\lambda},

and the remaining factor is integrable; the second region is identical. The
condition
\lambda>1
is exactly what gives integrability in dimension

d
.

2.5
Logarithmic Miyachi conditions

Hypothesis 2.6

(Localized logarithmic Miyachi condition)
.

Let
\sigma>d/2
. A multiplier
m
satisfies the localized logarithmic Miyachi condition of order
(\gamma,\beta,\sigma)
if, for every log-subdyadic ball
B
, every adapted normalized bump
\Psi_{B}
, and every
0\leq\theta\leq\sigma
,

(\log R_{B})^{\beta}\rho(R_{B})^{\theta}|B|^{-1/2}\|m\Psi_{B}\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta}}\leq C_{m}.

The constant
C_{m}
is independent of
B
and
\Psi_{B}
.

Remark 2.7

(Comparison with classical symbol classes)
.

The logarithmic Miyachi condition contains the usual Mikhlin condition with
logarithmic decay. Indeed, if

|D^{\nu}m(\xi)|\lesssim_{\nu}(\log|\xi|)^{-\beta}|\xi|^{-|\nu|},

then
m
satisfies Hypothesis
2.6
, since

|\xi|^{-1}\leq\rho(|\xi|)^{-1}=\frac{(\log|\xi|)^{\gamma-1}}{|\xi|}

for
|\xi|
large. The inclusion is strict, as shown for the model
multiplier in Proposition
6.1
: the first radial
derivative of

(\log(e+|\xi|))^{-\beta}e^{i(\log(e+|\xi|))^{\gamma}}

has size

(\log|\xi|)^{-\beta}\frac{(\log|\xi|)^{\gamma-1}}{|\xi|},

and therefore the model is not a classical Mikhlin symbol with logarithmic
decay when
\gamma>1
.

Lemma 2.8

(Pointwise derivative condition implies localized Sobolev control)
.

Assume that, for every multi-index
\nu
with
|\nu|\leq N
, where
N>\sigma
,

|D^{\nu}m(\xi)|\leq C_{\nu}(\log|\xi|)^{-\beta}\rho(|\xi|)^{-|\nu|},\qquad|\xi|\geq R_{0}.

Then
m
satisfies Hypothesis
2.6
.

Proof.

For integer
\theta=n
, Leibniz’ rule and Lemma
2.4
give, for
|\nu|=n
,

\|D^{\nu}(m\Psi_{B})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\nu}(\log R_{B})^{-\beta}\rho(R_{B})^{-n}|B|^{1/2}.

This yields the required estimate for integer
n
. Fractional orders follow by interpolation between adjacent integer Sobolev estimates. The constants depend on finitely many derivative bounds for
m
and on the bump normalization.
∎

3
Log-subdyadic decompositions

3.1
Lattice partitions

Let
A_{k}=\{\xi:2^{k}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
, and set

r_{k}=\rho(2^{k})\sim\frac{2^{k}}{k^{\gamma-1}}

for
k\geq k_{0}
, where
2^{k_{0}}\geq R_{0}
. Choose a smooth dyadic partition
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq k_{0}}
on high frequencies with
\operatorname{supp}\eta_{k}\subset\{\xi:|\xi|\sim 2^{k}\}
. Choose
\nu\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\nu(\zeta-\ell)=1,\qquad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Set

\nu_{k,\ell}(\xi)=\nu(r_{k}^{-1}\xi-\ell),\qquad\psi_{k,\ell}(\xi)=\eta_{k}(\xi)\nu_{k,\ell}(\xi).

Then

\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\psi_{k,\ell}(\xi)=1,\qquad|\xi|\geq 2^{k_{0}+1},

and each
\psi_{k,\ell}
is supported in a ball of radius comparable to
r_{k}
. The number of relevant balls in
A_{k}
is
O(k^{d(\gamma-1)})
.

Throughout this section
\psi_{k,\ell}
,
\eta_{k}
, and
\nu_{k,\ell}
denote frequency cutoffs, and
\check{\psi}_{k,\ell}
,
\check{\eta}_{k}
, and
\check{\nu}_{k,\ell}
denote their inverse Fourier transforms. Thus
f*\check{\psi}_{k,\ell}
is the Fourier projection associated with the cutoff
\psi_{k,\ell}
.

Remark 3.1

(Why the lattice is retained)
.

The decoupling estimate only uses bounded overlap and scale stability. The recoupling estimate below uses the lattice structure through a local Bessel inequality, so it is stated for this particular partition.

3.2
Decoupling

Proposition 3.2

(Log-subdyadic decoupling)
.

Let
\lambda>1
. If
f_{k,\ell}
has Fourier support contained in
\operatorname{supp}\psi_{k,\ell}
, then

g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}\left(\sum_{k,\ell}f_{k,\ell}\right)(x)^{2}\leq C\sum_{k,\ell}g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,\beta,\lambda}(f_{k,\ell})(x)^{2}.

The constant depends on
d
,
\gamma
,
\beta
,
\lambda
, and on the fixed cutoffs.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the estimate for Schwartz functions and finite sums of
frequency pieces; the general case follows by the usual density and limiting
arguments. We use a smooth majorant of the indicator of the unit ball. Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy
\Phi\geq c>0
on
|z|\leq 1
and
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\Phi}\subset\{|\zeta|\leq C_{0}\}
. The corresponding smoothed square function majorizes
g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}
.

After expanding the square, the spatial cutoff

a_{\gamma}(t)^{-d}\Phi\left(\frac{x-y}{a_{\gamma}(t)}\right)

has Fourier support, in the
y
-variable, contained in a ball of radius

O(a_{\gamma}(t)^{-1})=O(\rho(t^{-1}))
. On the other hand,

\widehat{\phi_{t}}
restricts the frequency variable to
|\xi|\sim t^{-1}
.
Thus only indices
(k,\ell)
with
2^{k}\sim t^{-1}
contribute.

For each fixed
t
, let
\mathcal{N}(k,\ell)
denote the uniformly finite
set of indices
(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
whose frequency supports can interact with

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{k,\ell}
after multiplication by the smoothed
spatial cutoff. The preceding support discussion gives

\#\mathcal{N}(k,\ell)\leq C

uniformly in
k,\ell,t
. Hence Cauchy’s inequality over this finite
neighbor set yields, after integration against the smoothed spatial cutoff,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\sum_{k,\ell}f_{k,\ell}*\phi_{t}(y)\right|^{2}a_{\gamma}(t)^{-d}\Phi\left(\frac{x-y}{a_{\gamma}(t)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k,\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f_{k,\ell}*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}a_{\gamma}(t)^{-d}\widetilde{\Phi}\left(\frac{x-y}{a_{\gamma}(t)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
\widetilde{\Phi}
is another fixed Schwartz majorant. Choosing

\widetilde{\Phi}
with sufficiently rapid decay, the corresponding kernel
is dominated by
K_{t}^{\lambda}
. Integrating in
t
gives the asserted
estimate.
∎

3.3
Recoupling

Lemma 3.3

(Local lattice Bessel inequality)
.

For the lattice partition above,

\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|F*\check{\nu}_{k,\ell}(y)|^{2}\leq C|F|^{2}*|\check{\nu}_{k}|(y),

where
\check{\nu}_{k}
is a nonnegative Schwartz majorant at spatial scale
r_{k}^{-1}
. The constant
C
is independent of
k
,
F
, and
y
.

Proof.

By dilation it suffices to prove the estimate for
r_{k}=1
. In that case write
\nu_{\ell}(\xi)=\nu(\xi-\ell)
. For fixed
y
, the quantity
F*\check{\nu}_{\ell}(y)
can be written, up to harmless normalizing constants, as

e^{2\pi i\ell\cdot y}\widehat{H_{y}}(\ell),\qquad H_{y}(u)=F(u)\check{\nu}(y-u).

Parseval’s identity on the torus, followed by the Poisson summation formula, gives

\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|F*\check{\nu}_{\ell}(y)|^{2}\leq C\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|F(u+m)|^{2}|\check{\nu}(y-u-m)|\,\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\check{\nu}(y-u-m^{\prime})|\,\mathrm{d}u.

Since
\check{\nu}
is Schwartz,

\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\check{\nu}(v-m)|<\infty.

Therefore

\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|F*\check{\nu}_{\ell}(y)|^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(u)|^{2}|\check{\nu}(y-u)|\,\mathrm{d}u.

Rescaling gives the stated estimate, with
\check{\nu}_{k}
denoting the corresponding scale
r_{k}^{-1}
majorant.
∎

Proposition 3.4

(Log-subdyadic recoupling)
.

Let
\lambda>1
. For the lattice partition
\{\psi_{k,\ell}\}
,

\sum_{k,\ell}g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,\beta,\lambda}(f*\check{\psi}_{k,\ell})(x)^{2}\leq Cg^{*}_{\log,\gamma,\beta,\lambda}(f)(x)^{2}.

The constant depends only on the structural parameters and on the fixed cutoffs.

Proof.

As before, it suffices to argue first for Schwartz functions and finite partial sums. By definition and Fubini’s theorem, the left-hand side is

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{k,\ell}|f*\phi_{t}*\check{\eta}_{k}*\check{\nu}_{k,\ell}(y)|^{2}K_{t}^{\lambda}(x-y)(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}\,\mathrm{d}y\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

Only
k
with
2^{k}\sim t^{-1}
contribute. Applying Lemma
3.3
with

F=f*\phi_{t}*\check{\eta}_{k}
gives

\sum_{\ell}|f*\phi_{t}*\check{\eta}_{k}*\check{\nu}_{k,\ell}|^{2}\leq C|f*\phi_{t}*\check{\eta}_{k}|^{2}*|\check{\nu}_{k}|.

Since
\|\check{\eta}_{k}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 1
, Cauchy’s inequality gives

|f*\phi_{t}*\check{\eta}_{k}|^{2}\lesssim|f*\phi_{t}|^{2}*|\check{\eta}_{k}|.

Consequently,

\sum_{\ell}|f*\phi_{t}*\check{\eta}_{k}*\check{\nu}_{k,\ell}|^{2}\leq C|f*\phi_{t}|^{2}*|\check{\eta}_{k}|*|\check{\nu}_{k}|.

The kernels
|\check{\nu}_{k}|
are Schwartz majorants at spatial scale

r_{k}^{-1}\sim a_{\gamma}(t)
, while
|\check{\eta}_{k}|
is at spatial scale

2^{-k}\sim t\leq a_{\gamma}(t)
. Since only
O(1)
indices
k
satisfy

2^{k}\sim t^{-1}
, and since
K_{t}^{\lambda}
is stable under convolution
with uniformly
L^{1}
-normalized Schwartz kernels at scales bounded by

Ca_{\gamma}(t)
, we have

\sum_{2^{k}\sim t^{-1}}|\check{\eta}_{k}|*|\check{\nu}_{k}|*K_{t}^{\lambda}\leq CK_{t}^{\lambda}.

Here the constant depends on
d,\gamma,\lambda
and on the fixed cutoffs.
Substitution gives the desired estimate.
∎

4.1
The local multiplier estimate

Proposition 4.1

(Local logarithmic multiplier estimate)
.

Let
\sigma>d/2
, set
\lambda=2\sigma/d
, and assume that
m

satisfies Hypothesis
2.6
. Let
B
be a
log-subdyadic ball, and let
\psi_{B}
be a normalized frequency cutoff
adapted to
B
, with adaptation constants uniform in
B
. Then

g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,\beta,\lambda}\bigl(T_{m}(f*\check{\psi}_{B})\bigr)(x)\leq Cg^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}(f*\check{\psi}_{B})(x).

The constant depends on
d
,
\gamma
,
\beta
,
\sigma
, the
localized Miyachi constant of
m
, and the fixed cutoff constants, but is
uniform in
B
,
f
, and
x
.

Proof.

Choose a normalized bump
\varphi_{B}
adapted to
B
, equal to one on

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{B}
, supported in a fixed dilation of
B
, and
satisfying

|D^{\nu}\varphi_{B}(\xi)|\leq C_{\nu}r(B)^{-|\nu|}

with constants uniform in
B
. Since
\varphi_{B}\equiv 1
on

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{B}
, the localized multiplier relevant to

f*\check{\psi}_{B}
is

K_{B}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\varphi_{B}),

and

T_{m}(f*\check{\psi}_{B})=K_{B}*(f*\check{\psi}_{B}).

Hypothesis
2.6
with
\theta=0
, together with Plancherel, gives

\|K_{B}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\log R_{B})^{-\beta}r(B)^{d/2}.

Hypothesis
2.6
with
\theta=\sigma
gives the corresponding weighted kernel estimate

\||z|^{\sigma}K_{B}(z)\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\leq C(\log R_{B})^{-\beta}r(B)^{d/2-\sigma}.

Here we use the Plancherel correspondence between Sobolev regularity in
frequency and spatial moments of the inverse Fourier transform:

\||z|^{\sigma}K_{B}\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\lesssim\|m\varphi_{B}\|_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}.

For integer
\sigma
this follows by differentiating
m\varphi_{B}
in
frequency. Fractional
\sigma
follows from the usual fractional Sobolev
form of Plancherel.

Fix
t
for which
(f*\check{\psi}_{B})*\phi_{t}
is nonzero. By the support of
\widehat{\phi_{t}}
and the support of
\psi_{B}
, we have

t^{-1}\sim R_{B},\qquad r(B)\sim\rho(t^{-1})=a_{\gamma}(t)^{-1},\qquad\log R_{B}\sim\log(1/t).

Set

F_{t}=(f*\check{\psi}_{B})*\phi_{t}.

Split

K_{B}*F_{t}(y)=I_{\mathrm{near}}(y)+I_{\mathrm{far}}(y),

where

I_{\mathrm{near}}(y)=\int_{|z|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}K_{B}(z)F_{t}(y-z)\,\mathrm{d}z

and

I_{\mathrm{far}}(y)=\int_{|z|>a_{\gamma}(t)}K_{B}(z)F_{t}(y-z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

For the near term, Cauchy’s inequality and the
L^{2}
bound for
K_{B}
give

|I_{\mathrm{near}}(y)|^{2}\leq C(\log R_{B})^{-2\beta}r(B)^{d}\int_{|z|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}|F_{t}(y-z)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Since
r(B)^{d}\sim a_{\gamma}(t)^{-d}
, and
K_{t}^{\lambda}(z)\sim a_{\gamma}(t)^{-d}
for
|z|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)
, this yields

|I_{\mathrm{near}}(y)|^{2}\leq C(\log R_{B})^{-2\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F_{t}(y-z)|^{2}K_{t}^{\lambda}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

For the far term, Cauchy’s inequality with weight
|z|^{\sigma}
gives

|I_{\mathrm{far}}(y)|^{2}\leq\||z|^{\sigma}K_{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{|z|>a_{\gamma}(t)}|z|^{-2\sigma}|F_{t}(y-z)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Using the weighted kernel estimate and
r(B)\sim a_{\gamma}(t)^{-1}
, the right-hand side is bounded by

C(\log R_{B})^{-2\beta}\int_{|z|>a_{\gamma}(t)}|F_{t}(y-z)|^{2}a_{\gamma}(t)^{-d}\left(\frac{|z|}{a_{\gamma}(t)}\right)^{-2\sigma}\mathrm{d}z.

Since
2\sigma=d\lambda
, this is bounded by

C(\log R_{B})^{-2\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F_{t}(y-z)|^{2}K_{t}^{\lambda}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

Combining the near and far estimates, and multiplying by the output weight
(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}
, gives

(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}|K_{B}*F_{t}(y)|^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F_{t}(y-z)|^{2}K_{t}^{\lambda}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

Integrating against
K_{t}^{\lambda}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y
, using (
7
), and then integrating in
t
, gives the result.
∎

Theorem 4.2

Let

,
\lambda=2\sigma/d
, and assume that

,

,

,

,

, the localized Miyachi constant of

, and the fixed cutoffs, but not on

or

5
Weighted estimates

We now record the weighted estimates needed to pass from the pointwise
square-function estimate of Theorem
4.2
to weighted

L^{2}
bounds. The forward estimate for the robust square function is
standard. The reverse estimate is the logarithmic analogue of the
Beltran–Bennett reverse square-function inequality

[
1
]
.

Throughout this section we fix a nonnegative radial function

\eta\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1,\qquad\eta(x)\geq c\,\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}(x),

chosen so that the Peetre-type smoothing estimate in Lemma

5.5
holds for functions with Fourier support in a
fixed annulus. We set

\eta_{t}(x)=t^{-d}\eta(x/t).

For a locally integrable function
w
, define the smooth averaging
operator

A_{t}w(y)=\eta_{t}*w(y).

Definition 5.1

(Logarithmic geometric maximal operator)
.

Let

a_{\gamma}(t)=t(\log(1/t))^{\gamma-1}.

For a locally integrable weight
w
, define

M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}w(x)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t<t_{0}\\
|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)\end{subarray}}(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta}A_{t}w(y).

The operator is local in scale, consistently with the high-frequency nature
of the logarithmic square functions. The displacement of the center is
measured at the log-subdyadic aperture
a_{\gamma}(t)
, while the averaging
takes place at the underlying Littlewood–Paley scale
t
.

Remark 5.2

(Normalization)
.

The logarithmic square function
g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}
contains the
factor

(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}.

The corresponding weighted maximal operator contains the inverse factor

(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta}.

This is the logarithmic counterpart of the Beltran–Bennett power
normalization:
g_{\alpha,\beta}
carries the factor
t^{-2\beta}
,
whereas the associated maximal operator carries the compensating factor

t^{2\beta}
.

Remark 5.3

(Smooth and hard averages)
.

Since
\eta\geq c\,\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}
, the smooth average
A_{t}w(y)

dominates the normalized average over
B(y,t)
, up to a dimensional
constant. Thus the smooth maximal operator in Definition

5.1
is stronger than the corresponding
hard-ball version. The smooth formulation is convenient because it is
stable under the Littlewood–Paley smoothing step used below.

Proposition 5.4

(Forward weighted estimate for
g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}
)
.

Let
\gamma>1
and
\lambda>1
. Then, for every nonnegative locally
integrable weight
w
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}(f)(x)^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}M^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The implicit constant depends only on
d,\gamma,\lambda
, and on the
fixed Littlewood–Paley cutoff.

Proof.

By the definition of
g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}
and Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}(f)(x)^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}(K_{t}^{\lambda}*w)(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

Since
\lambda>1
, the kernels
K_{t}^{\lambda}
are uniformly integrable
and are dominated, after dyadic annular decomposition, by averages over
balls centered at
y
. Hence

K_{t}^{\lambda}*w(y)\lesssim Mw(y)

uniformly in
0<t<t_{0}
. Therefore

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}(f)(x)^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}Mw(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

The classical weighted Littlewood–Paley estimate gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}W(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{2}MW(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

for every nonnegative locally integrable weight
W
; see, for instance,

[
8
]
. Applying this with
W=Mw
, and
discarding the harmless restriction
0<t<t_{0}
, yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}(f)(x)^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{2}M^{2}w(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

∎

Lemma 5.5

(Littlewood–Paley smoothing of the weight)
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be fixed with

\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\phi}\subset\{\xi:1/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}.

Then, for every nonnegative locally integrable weight
w
, every
Schwartz function
f
, and every
t>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}M^{3}w(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}A_{t}M^{4}w(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

The implicit constant depends only on
d
, on the fixed cutoff
\phi
,
and on the fixed averaging kernel
\eta
.

Proof.

Let

F_{t}=f*\phi_{t}.

Since
F_{t}
is frequency localized at scale
t^{-1}
, the fixed choice
of
\eta
and the standard Peetre-type smoothing estimate give

|F_{t}(y)|^{2}\lesssim(|F_{t}|^{2}*\eta_{t})(y).

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F_{t}(y)|^{2}M^{3}w(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|F_{t}|^{2}*\eta_{t})(y)M^{3}w(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F_{t}(z)|^{2}(\eta_{t}*M^{3}w)(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

Since the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator dominates its input almost
everywhere,

M^{3}w\leq M^{4}w\qquad\text{a.e.}

Therefore

\eta_{t}*M^{3}w(z)\leq\eta_{t}*M^{4}w(z)=A_{t}M^{4}w(z).

Combining the preceding inequalities gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 5.6

(Geometric averaging over logarithmic approach regions)
.

For every nonnegative measurable function
F
, every nonnegative locally
integrable weight
W
, and every
0<t<t_{0}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(y)A_{t}W(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}F(y)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{a_{\gamma}(t)^{d}}\sup_{|z-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}A_{t}W(z)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The implicit constant depends only on
d
.

Proof.

For each fixed
y
,

A_{t}W(y)\leq\sup_{|z-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}A_{t}W(z)

whenever
|x-y|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)
. Hence

F(y)A_{t}W(y)\lesssim F(y)\frac{1}{a_{\gamma}(t)^{d}}\int_{|x-y|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}\sup_{|z-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}A_{t}W(z)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Integrating in
y
and applying Fubini gives the claim.
∎

Proposition 5.7

(Reverse weighted estimate for
g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}
)
.

Let
\gamma>1
, let
\beta\geq 0
, and let
P_{\mathrm{hi}}
denote
the high-frequency projection associated with the Littlewood–Paley
resolution on
0<t<t_{0}
. Then, for every nonnegative locally integrable
weight
w
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|P_{\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x)^{2}M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The implicit constant depends only on
d,\gamma,\beta
, and on the fixed
Littlewood–Paley cutoff.

Proof.

By the classical reverse weighted Littlewood–Paley inequality applied to
the high-frequency projection
P_{\mathrm{hi}}f
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|P_{\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}M^{3}w(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

Applying Lemma
5.5
at each scale
t
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|P_{\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}A_{t}M^{4}w(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

We now apply Lemma
5.6
with

F(y)=|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2},\qquad W=M^{4}w.

This gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|P_{\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{a_{\gamma}(t)^{d}}\sup_{|z-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}A_{t}M^{4}w(z)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

Insert

1=(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta}.

By Definition
5.1
,

\sup_{|z-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta}A_{t}M^{4}w(z)\leq M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w(x).

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|P_{\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)}|f*\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{a_{\gamma}(t)^{d}}(\log(1/t))^{2\beta}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The expression in parentheses is exactly

g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x)^{2}
, and the proof follows.
∎

Theorem 5.8

(Weighted logarithmic multiplier estimate)
.

Let
\gamma>1
, let
\beta\geq 0
, and let
\sigma>d/2
. Set

\lambda=\frac{2\sigma}{d}.

Assume that
m
satisfies the localized logarithmic Miyachi condition of
Hypothesis
2.6
, and let
T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}

denote the corresponding high-frequency multiplier. Then, for every
nonnegative locally integrable weight
w
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}M^{2}\bigl(M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w\bigr)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The implicit constant depends on the constants in Theorem

4.2
, on
d,\gamma,\beta,\sigma
, and on the fixed
Littlewood–Paley cutoff.

Proof.

Since
T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f
is supported in the high-frequency region
detected by the Littlewood–Paley resolution, we have

P_{\mathrm{hi}}T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f=T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f.

Applying Proposition
5.7
to
T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f

therefore gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}(T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f)(x)^{2}M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

By the pointwise estimate of Theorem
4.2
,

g_{\log,\gamma,\beta}(T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f)(x)\lesssim g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}(f)(x).

Therefore

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{*}_{\log,\gamma,0,\lambda}(f)(x)^{2}M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Finally apply Proposition
5.4
with the weight

W=M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w.

This gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m,\mathrm{hi}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}M^{2}W(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

∎

Remark 5.9

(Low frequencies)
.

Theorem
5.8
is stated for the high-frequency
piece governed by the logarithmic square functions. If

, where

is a smooth

6
The logarithmic model multiplier

We verify that the logarithmic model multiplier belongs to the localized
logarithmic Miyachi class introduced above, and record the resulting

L^{p}
consequences.

Let

m_{\gamma,\beta}(\xi)=\bigl(\log(e+|\xi|)\bigr)^{-\beta}e^{i(\log(e+|\xi|))^{\gamma}},\qquad\gamma>1.

The regularization near the origin is harmless. All estimates below concern
the high-frequency region
|\xi|\gg 1
, where

\log(e+|\xi|)\sim\log|\xi|.

Proposition 6.1

(The model satisfies the logarithmic Miyachi condition)
.

Let
\gamma>1
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and let

m_{\gamma,\beta}(\xi)=\bigl(\log(e+|\xi|)\bigr)^{-\beta}e^{i(\log(e+|\xi|))^{\gamma}}.

Then, in the high-frequency region,
m_{\gamma,\beta}
satisfies the
pointwise logarithmic Miyachi estimates

|D^{\nu}m_{\gamma,\beta}(\xi)|\lesssim_{\nu}(\log|\xi|)^{-\beta}\left(\frac{(\log|\xi|)^{\gamma-1}}{|\xi|}\right)^{|\nu|}

for every multi-index
\nu
. Consequently,

m_{\gamma,\beta}
satisfies Hypothesis

2.6
for every finite Sobolev order

\sigma
.

Proof.

Write, for
R\gg 1
,

a(R)=(\log R)^{-\beta},\qquad\Phi(R)=(\log R)^{\gamma}.

Then

m_{\gamma,\beta}(\xi)=a(|\xi|)e^{i\Phi(|\xi|)}

up to the harmless low-frequency regularization.

For every integer
j\geq 1
,

|\Phi^{(j)}(R)|\lesssim_{j}\frac{(\log R)^{\gamma-1}}{R^{j}},

while

|a^{(j)}(R)|\lesssim_{j}(\log R)^{-\beta}R^{-j}.

By Faà di Bruno’s formula,

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{j}}{\mathrm{d}R^{j}}e^{i\Phi(R)}\right|\lesssim_{j}\left(\frac{(\log R)^{\gamma-1}}{R}\right)^{j}.

Indeed, each term is a product of derivatives of
\Phi
, and the worst
term is the
j
-fold product of
\Phi^{\prime}(R)
.

Therefore, by Leibniz’ rule,

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{j}}{\mathrm{d}R^{j}}\left(a(R)e^{i\Phi(R)}\right)\right|\lesssim_{j}(\log R)^{-\beta}\left(\frac{(\log R)^{\gamma-1}}{R}\right)^{j}.

The standard formulae for derivatives of radial functions then give, for

|\xi|\gg 1
,

|D^{\nu}m_{\gamma,\beta}(\xi)|\lesssim_{\nu}(\log|\xi|)^{-\beta}\left(\frac{(\log|\xi|)^{\gamma-1}}{|\xi|}\right)^{|\nu|}.

Since

\rho(R)^{-1}=\frac{(\log R)^{\gamma-1}}{R},

this is precisely the pointwise logarithmic Miyachi condition. The localized
Sobolev condition follows by applying these derivative estimates on each
log-subdyadic ball
B
, using
r(B)\sim\rho(R_{B})
and the derivative
bounds for the normalized cutoffs adapted to
B
.
∎

Remark 6.2

(Comparison with classical Mikhlin classes)
.

The logarithmic Miyachi class contains the usual Mikhlin class with
logarithmic decay. Indeed, if

|D^{\nu}m(\xi)|\lesssim_{\nu}(\log|\xi|)^{-\beta}|\xi|^{-|\nu|}

for
|\xi|\gg 1
, then

|D^{\nu}m(\xi)|\lesssim_{\nu}(\log|\xi|)^{-\beta}\rho(|\xi|)^{-|\nu|},

because

|\xi|^{-1}\leq\rho(|\xi|)^{-1}=\frac{(\log|\xi|)^{\gamma-1}}{|\xi|}

for
|\xi|
large. Hence the logarithmic Mikhlin class with decay

(\log|\xi|)^{-\beta}
is contained in the logarithmic Miyachi class.

The inclusion is strict. Indeed, the radial model

m_{\gamma,\beta}(R)=(\log R)^{-\beta}e^{i(\log R)^{\gamma}},\qquad R\gg 1,

satisfies the logarithmic Miyachi condition, but it does not satisfy the
classical Mikhlin bound with logarithmic decay. Differentiating gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}R}m_{\gamma,\beta}(R)=e^{i(\log R)^{\gamma}}\left[-\beta(\log R)^{-\beta-1}\frac{1}{R}+i\gamma(\log R)^{-\beta+\gamma-1}\frac{1}{R}\right].

The two terms in brackets are real and purely imaginary, respectively.
Hence there is no cancellation between them, and therefore

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}R}m_{\gamma,\beta}(R)\right|\geq c_{\gamma,\beta}(\log R)^{-\beta+\gamma-1}\frac{1}{R}

for all sufficiently large
R
. Since
\gamma>1
, this cannot be bounded
by

C(\log R)^{-\beta}\frac{1}{R}.

Thus the model belongs to the logarithmic Miyachi class but is not a
classical Mikhlin symbol with logarithmic decay.

Corollary 6.3

(Weighted estimate for the model)
.

Let
\gamma>1
,
\beta\geq 0
, and let

m_{\gamma,\beta}(\xi)=\bigl(\log(e+|\xi|)\bigr)^{-\beta}e^{i(\log(e+|\xi|))^{\gamma}}.

Let

m_{\gamma,\beta}=m_{\mathrm{lo}}+m_{\mathrm{hi}}

be an inhomogeneous decomposition, where
m_{\mathrm{lo}}
is smooth and
compactly supported and
m_{\mathrm{hi}}
is supported in the
high-frequency region. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}M^{2}\bigl(M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w\bigr)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

for every nonnegative locally integrable weight
w
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
, the high-frequency symbol

m_{\mathrm{hi}}
satisfies Hypothesis

2.6
. The result therefore follows directly from
Theorem
5.8
.
∎

Corollary 6.4

(
L^{p}
boundedness for the model)
.

Let
\gamma>1
,
1<p<\infty
, and
\beta\geq 0
. Let

m_{\gamma,\beta}(\xi)=\bigl(\log(e+|\xi|)\bigr)^{-\beta}e^{i(\log(e+|\xi|))^{\gamma}}.

If
p\neq 2
, assume

\beta>d(\gamma-1)\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|.

Then

\|T_{m_{\gamma,\beta}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

For
p=2
, the same conclusion holds for every
\beta\geq 0
.

Proof.

Choose an inhomogeneous decomposition

m_{\gamma,\beta}=m_{\mathrm{lo}}+m_{\mathrm{hi}},

where
m_{\mathrm{lo}}
is smooth and compactly supported and

m_{\mathrm{hi}}
is supported in the high-frequency region. The
low-frequency operator
T_{m_{\mathrm{lo}}}
is bounded on
L^{p}
by the
standard Mikhlin theorem.

By Proposition
6.1
, the high-frequency part

m_{\mathrm{hi}}
satisfies Hypothesis

2.6
. Moreover, the adjoint high-frequency
symbol
\overline{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}
satisfies the same condition, since
complex conjugation preserves the pointwise derivative bounds above. The
claim follows from Corollary
7.3
.
∎

Remark 6.5

(Interpretation of the threshold)
.

For the logarithmic model, the
L^{p}
condition

\beta>d(\gamma-1)\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|

comes from the logarithmic aperture

\frac{a_{\gamma}(t)}{t}=(\log(1/t))^{\gamma-1}.

At frequency scale
t^{-1}
, this produces an effective logarithmic
geometric loss of order

(\log(1/t))^{d(\gamma-1)|1/2-1/p|},

which is compensated by the logarithmic decay

(\log|\xi|)^{-\beta}\sim(\log(1/t))^{-\beta}.

The endpoint case

\beta=d(\gamma-1)\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|

is not addressed here.

7
Lebesgue space consequences

We now derive unweighted
L^{p}
consequences from the weighted estimate
of Theorem
5.8
. The bounds obtained in this
section are consequences of the weighted theorem and of the logarithmic
maximal estimate below.

The relevant point is that the logarithmic geometric maximal operator is
controlled by a Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator of order
s
. This
avoids summing over logarithmic scales and gives the natural logarithmic
threshold.

Lemma 7.1

(
L^{r}
bound for
M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}
)
.

Let
\gamma>1
,
1<r<\infty
, and suppose that

2\beta>\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{r}.

Then

\|M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}w\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

For
r=\infty
, the same conclusion holds whenever
\beta\geq 0
.
The implicit constant depends on
d,\gamma,\beta,r
, on
t_{0}
, and on
the fixed averaging kernel
\eta
.

Proof.

We first treat the case
1<r<\infty
. Choose
s
such that

1<s<r\qquad\text{and}\qquad 2\beta>\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{s}.

This is possible because
2\beta>d(\gamma-1)/r
.

Let

M_{s}w(x)=\bigl(M(|w|^{s})(x)\bigr)^{1/s}.

We claim that

M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}w(x)\lesssim M_{s}w(x).

Fix
0<t<t_{0}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t).

Since
\eta\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
N>d
we have

|\eta_{t}(z)|\lesssim_{N}t^{-d}\left(1+\frac{|z|}{t}\right)^{-N}.

Thus

A_{t}w(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta_{t}(y-z)w(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\lesssim_{N}\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-jN}t^{-d}\int_{|z-y|\leq 2^{j+1}t}|w(z)|\,\mathrm{d}z.

By Hölder’s inequality,

t^{-d}\int_{|z-y|\leq 2^{j+1}t}|w(z)|\,\mathrm{d}z\lesssim 2^{jd/s^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{(2^{j}t)^{d}}\int_{|z-y|\leq 2^{j+1}t}|w(z)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/s}.

Since
|y-x|\leq a_{\gamma}(t)
, the ball
B(y,2^{j+1}t)
is contained in
a ball centered at
x
of radius comparable to

a_{\gamma}(t)+2^{j}t.

Therefore

\left(\frac{1}{(2^{j}t)^{d}}\int_{|z-y|\leq 2^{j+1}t}|w(z)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/s}\lesssim\left(\frac{a_{\gamma}(t)+2^{j}t}{2^{j}t}\right)^{d/s}M_{s}w(x).

Combining the last two estimates gives

A_{t}w(y)\lesssim M_{s}w(x)\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-jN}2^{jd/s^{\prime}}\left(1+\frac{a_{\gamma}(t)}{2^{j}t}\right)^{d/s}.

Choose
N>d+d/s^{\prime}
. Then the sum is bounded by

C\left(\frac{a_{\gamma}(t)}{t}\right)^{d/s}.

Consequently,

A_{t}w(y)\lesssim\left(\frac{a_{\gamma}(t)}{t}\right)^{d/s}M_{s}w(x).

Since

\frac{a_{\gamma}(t)}{t}=(\log(1/t))^{\gamma-1},

we obtain

A_{t}w(y)\lesssim(\log(1/t))^{d(\gamma-1)/s}M_{s}w(x).

Multiplying by the logarithmic factor in the definition of

M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}
, we get

(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta}A_{t}w(y)\lesssim(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta+d(\gamma-1)/s}M_{s}w(x).

By the choice of
s
, the exponent

-2\beta+\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{s}

is negative. Since
0<t<t_{0}
, the logarithmic factor is uniformly bounded.
Taking the supremum over
t
and
y
gives

M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}w(x)\lesssim M_{s}w(x).

Finally,
M_{s}
is bounded on
L^{r}
because
s<r
. Hence

\|M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}w\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|M_{s}w\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{r}}.

For
r=\infty
, the conclusion follows directly from

A_{t}w(y)\leq\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}

and the boundedness of
(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta}
on
0<t<t_{0}
when

\beta\geq 0
.
∎

Theorem 7.2

(
L^{p}
boundedness above
L^{2}
)
.

Let
\gamma>1
, let
2\leq p<\infty
, and let
\beta\geq 0
. If

p>2
, assume in addition that

\beta>d(\gamma-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right).

Let

m=m_{\mathrm{lo}}+m_{\mathrm{hi}},

where
m_{\mathrm{hi}}
satisfies the localized logarithmic Miyachi
condition of Hypothesis
2.6
, and where

m_{\mathrm{lo}}
is a smooth compactly supported multiplier. Then

\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The implicit constant depends on the constants in the localized Miyachi
condition, on
d,\gamma,\beta,p
, and on the fixed cutoffs.

Proof.

The low-frequency term
T_{m_{\mathrm{lo}}}
is bounded on
L^{p}
by the
standard Mikhlin theorem, since
m_{\mathrm{lo}}
is smooth and compactly
supported. It remains to treat
T_{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}
.

The case
p=2
follows from Theorem

5.8
with
w\equiv 1
, or directly from
Plancherel and the
\theta=0
part of Hypothesis

2.6
.

Assume now that
p>2
, and set

r=\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-2}.

By duality,

\|T_{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}=\bigl\||T_{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}f|^{2}\bigr\|_{L^{p/2}}=\sup_{\|w\|_{L^{r}}=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where the supremum is taken over nonnegative
w\in L^{r}
.

Applying Theorem
5.8
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}M^{2}\bigl(M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w\bigr)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
M
is bounded on
L^{r}
and, by Lemma

7.1
,
M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}
is bounded on
L^{r}

whenever

2\beta>\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{r},

we have

\bigl\|M^{2}(M_{\log,\gamma,\beta}M^{4}w)\bigr\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{r}}.

Hölder’s inequality therefore gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}f(x)|^{2}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\|\,|f|^{2}\,\|_{L^{p/2}}\|w\|_{L^{r}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\|w\|_{L^{r}}.

Taking the supremum over
\|w\|_{L^{r}}=1
yields

\|T_{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Combining this estimate with the
L^{p}
-boundedness of

T_{m_{\mathrm{lo}}}
gives the asserted bound for
T_{m}
.

Finally,

2\beta>\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{r}

is equivalent to

\beta>\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{2r}=d(\gamma-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right),

which is the stated condition for
p>2
.
∎

Corollary 7.3

(
L^{p}
boundedness by duality)
.

Let
\gamma>1
,
1<p<\infty
, and let
\beta\geq 0
. Let

m=m_{\mathrm{lo}}+m_{\mathrm{hi}},

where
m_{\mathrm{lo}}
is a smooth compactly supported multiplier and

m_{\mathrm{hi}}
satisfies Hypothesis

2.6
. If
1<p<2
, assume also that the
high-frequency adjoint symbol
\overline{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}
satisfies the
same condition. If
p\neq 2
, assume in addition that

\beta>d(\gamma-1)\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|.

Then

\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

For
2\leq p<\infty
, this is Theorem

7.2
.

Assume
1<p<2
. Then
p^{\prime}>2
. The adjoint multiplier decomposes as

\overline{m}=\overline{m_{\mathrm{lo}}}+\overline{m_{\mathrm{hi}}},

where
\overline{m_{\mathrm{lo}}}
is again smooth and compactly
supported, while
\overline{m_{\mathrm{hi}}}
satisfies the localized
logarithmic Miyachi condition by hypothesis. Applying Theorem

7.2
to
T_{\overline{m}}=T_{m}^{*}
on

L^{p^{\prime}}
, and then dualizing, gives the
L^{p}
bound for
T_{m}
.
Since

\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2},

the stated condition is exactly the condition needed for the adjoint bound.
∎

Remark 7.4

(On the threshold)
.

The condition in Corollary
7.3
is the
natural threshold obtained from the logarithmic maximal theorem. Indeed, the
factor

\frac{a_{\gamma}(t)}{t}=(\log(1/t))^{\gamma-1}

produces an effective logarithmic displacement of order

(\log(1/t))^{d(\gamma-1)/r}
on
L^{r}
, while the maximal operator
contains the compensating factor

(\log(1/t))^{-2\beta}.

Balancing these two quantities gives

2\beta>\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{r}.

The endpoint case

2\beta=\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{r}

is not addressed here.

Remark 7.5

(No power-type Sobolev gain)
.

Unlike the power subdyadic setting, the logarithmic geometry does not give
a genuine power improvement from
L^{p}
to
L^{q}
with
q>p
. The
admissible displacement satisfies

\frac{a_{\gamma}(t)}{t}=(\log(1/t))^{\gamma-1},

so the loss in the associated maximal operator is logarithmic rather than
polynomial in
t^{-1}
. Accordingly, the natural consequence of the
weighted estimate is an
L^{p}\to L^{p}
theorem with a logarithmic smoothness
threshold.
