Many-body dynamical localization in Fock space
N. Dupont, B. Peaudecerf, D. Guéry-Odelin, G. Lemarié, B. Georgeot, Ch. Miniatura, N. Goldman

Abstract
We investigate the emergence of many-body dynamical localization (MBDL) in the Fock space of an interacting two-mode bosonic system subject to periodic driving. Using a mapping to the paradigmatic kicked-top model, we analyze the interplay between classical chaotic diffusion and quantum interference effects. While the mean-field (classical) dynamics exhibits bounded ergodic diffusion along the population imbalance axis, the quantum dynamics reveals strong suppression of transport in Fock space, in close analogy with Anderson localization in disordered lattices. We characterize the localization length, its scaling with particle number and driving parameters, and reveal the spectral crossover from random-matrix to Poisson statistics as the many-body ensemble localizes. We highlight the connection between MBDL and discrete time crystals. Our findings offer a promising avenue to study the Anderson transition in Fock space.

The classical map for the kicked top (Eq. 4 for the main text) is derived from the Floquet operator

(S1)

\hat{J}_{\mu}(m+1)=\hat{U}_{\text{F}}^{\dagger}\hat{J}_{\mu}(m)\hat{U}_{\text{F}}

\mu=x,y,z

\text{e}^{A}B\text{e}^{-A}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}[A,B]_{k}/k!

[A,B]_{k}=[\underbrace{A,...[A,[A}_{k\;\text{times}},B]]...]

[\hat{\mu},\hat{\nu}]=\text{i}\hbar_{\text{eff}}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda}\hat{\lambda}

\displaystyle x_{m+1}\!

\displaystyle=\cos(b z_m)x_{m}-\sin(b z_m)y_{m},

The effective 1D Anderson Hamiltonian (Eq. (6) of the main text) is obtained by following Refs.
42

49

\ket{\phi_j^-}
(resp.
\ket{\phi_j^+}

\hat{R}_{x}(a)=\text{e}^{-\text{i}a\hat{J}_{x}}

\ket{\phi_j}\equiv\ket{\phi_j^-}

\ket{\phi_j^\pm}

\begin{split}\hat{S}_{z}(b)\hat{R}_{x}(a)\ket{\phi_j^-}&=\text{e}^{-\text{i}\varepsilon_{j}T/\hbar}\ket{\phi_j^-},\\
\hat{R}_{x}(a)\hat{S}_{z}(b)\ket{\phi_j^+}&=\text{e}^{-\text{i}\varepsilon_{j}T/\hbar}\ket{\phi_j^+},\\
\ket{\phi_j^+}&=\hat{R}_{x}(a)\ket{\phi_j^-},\end{split}

(S10)

These states are expanded over the Fock basis

\ket{\Phi_j}\equiv(\ket{\phi_j^+}+\ket{\phi_j^-})/2=\sum_{n}c_{n}\ket{n},

(S13)

III. Quantum resonances and the random quantum kicked top

Figures 3 and 4 of the main text present results obtained with the random quantum kicked top (RQKT), in which the torsion operator
\hat{S}_{z}(b)=\text{e}^{\text{i}\hbar_{\text{eff}}b\hat{J}_{z}^{2}}
is replaced by random phases multiplying each Fock coefficient (an approach commonly employed to model the quantum kicked rotor
7
). The RQKT is introduced in order to avoid quantum resonances
63
;
42
, which suppress the effective disorder produced by the kicking (see Eqs. (
S17
) and (
S18
)). Indeed, the torsion operator is diagonal over the Fock basis, with matrix elements
\langle n^{\prime}_{1}|\hat{S}_{z}(b)|n_{1}\rangle=\text{e}^{-\text{i}\theta_{n_{1}}}\delta_{n^{\prime}_{1},n_{1}}
, where
\theta_{n_{1}}=(2b/N)(n_{1}-N/2)^{2}
. When
b=p/q\times\pi N
for integers
p,q
, these phases become rational multiples of
\pi
, thereby restoring order in the system (a dramatic instance being
b=\pi N
, where the torsion reduces to the identity). For a given
N
, such resonances can be avoided by sampling
b
away from these special values, as done in Figs. 1 and 2 of the main text. In the regime
b\gg\pi N
,
the phases
\theta_{n_{1}}
are well approximated by uniformly distributed random variables on
[0,2\pi]
, yielding essentially identical results, as illustrated in Fig.
S2
.

In our study, the Floquet operator possesses the rotational symmetry
\hat{U}_{\text{F}}=\hat{R}_{x}(\pi)^{\dagger}\hat{U}_{\text{F}}\hat{R}_{x}(\pi)
, which partitions the Hilbert space into two subspaces of opposite parity. This symmetry is generically broken by the RQKT, but can be restored by symmetrizing the random phases
\theta^{sym}_{n_{1}}\equiv\theta_{n_{1}}+\theta_{N-n_{1}}\mod 2\pi
. In Fig. 3 of the main text, the phases are left unsymmetrized, since the absence of Hilbert space partition simplifies the spectral analysis (without loss of generality for our purposes)
52
;
69
;
58
. For the results of Fig. 4, however, the rotational symmetry is enforced. Indeed, we observe that breaking this symmetry accelerates
the melting of the DTC, as an initial Fock state then projects onto eigenstates with no
clean pairing structure, resulting in a greater variety of quasienergy
splittings contributing to
\langle\hat{J}_{z}\rangle_{\psi(t)}
, and thus
faster dephasing upon averaging over disorder realizations.

IV. Kullback-Leibler divergence

In Fig. 3 of the main text, we employ the Kullback-Leibler divergence
D_{\text{KL}}
to quantify the distance between the quasienergy-difference distribution
P(s)
obtained numerically and the Poisson distribution
P_{\text{Poisson}}=\text{e}^{-s}
, expected for integrable systems. This quantity is defined as

D_{\text{KL}}(P|P_{\text{Poisson}})=\int_{0}^{s_{\text{max}}}P(s)\ln\left(\dfrac{P(s)}{P_{\text{Poisson}}(s)}\right)\differential s,

(S19)

where
s_{\text{max}}
is a cutoff introduced to ensure that the numerical values
P(s\leq s_{\text{max}})>0
. We used
s_{\text{max}}=2
for the data of Fig. 3.
