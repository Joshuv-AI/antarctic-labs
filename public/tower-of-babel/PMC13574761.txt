ABSTRACT
AbstractMicro-Electro-Mechanical Systems (MEMS) are extensively utilized in many different applications because of its compact design, low power consumption and greater sensitivity. Compared to piezoresistive alternatives, a MEMS- based Touch Mode Capacitive Pressure Sensor (TMCPS) is designed and simulated to achieve better sensitivity, stability and linearity. This work presents a Double Touch Mode MEMS-based Capacitive Pressure Sensor (DTMCPS) with a flexible circular diaphragm integrated into an M-shaped silicon substrate. By increasing the diaphragm-substrate contact area, the proposed M-shaped structural arrangement improves sensor linearity and sensitivity. The diaphragms deflection characteristics are modeled using small deflection theory to minimize nonlinearity. A comprehensive analysis of the sensors capacitive behavior is carried out. The analytical formulations for capacitance, capacitive sensitivity and mechanical sensitivity are derived and generated using MATLAB based simulations. The diaphragm deformation is further assessed through structural analysis using COMSOL Multiphysics. The goal is to improve the performance of the conventional DTMCPS by integrating a silicon (Si) circular diaphragm with an M-shaped silicon substrate. The position of the touch point is crucial in defining the overall sensitivity, hence the notch size have a considerable impact on the sensors operational properties. Small deflection theory is used to model the diaphragm behavior in order to minimize nonlinear effects. These results show the potential use in cutting edge medical, automotive, aerospace and industrial sensing applications by demonstrating the higher sensitivity, accuracy and durability of the preferred TMCPS design.Keywords: MEMS pressure sensor, Capacitive sensing, M-shaped diaphragm design, Geometric optimization, Multiphysics simulationSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

MEMS stands for Micro-Electro-Mechanical systems. It is miniaturized device with the integration of mechanical micro structures like microsensors, microactuators and microelectronic devices into a single silicon substrate. MEMS technology offers several advantages like compact size, lightweight and cost effective sensor design1. The continuous evolution of MEMS technology has given rise to various innovative devices like strain gauges, gyroscopes, accelerometers, flow sensors and pressure sensors2. Recent advancements in MEMS capacitive pressure sensors have resulted in greater sensitivity, reduced power consumption and compact design. MEMS sensor offers advantages over piezoresistive sensors, especially in terms of design difficulties, modeling and simulation elements3. Silicon is a popular material for high-performance, small sensors because of its exceptional mechanical qualities and ease of micro fabrication.Its growing application in micromechanical systems is revolutionizing the creation of highly integrated sensors used for low temperature ranges4. In order to overcome the drawbacks of traditional silicon sensors, the author analyzed a unique Silicon Carbide (SiC) capacitive pressure sensor made for exceptionally demanding settings (high pressure/temperature). The simulation findings demonstrate outstanding performance with a low nonlinearity of the sensor and better stability5. In this design the material compatibility of tungsten and molybdenum with and without packaging is analyzed for MEMS capacitive pressure sensor using COMSOL simulations. The Molybdenum Tungsten design demonstrated remarkable linearity6. This work suggests a novel wing-like mechanism to greatly improve the sensitivity and responsiveness of a MEMS Capacitive Pressure Sensor (CPS). By using insulating layers that expand when pressure is applied, the mechanism successfully reduces the diaphragms deflection distance prior to contact7. The author examined a less cost straight forward utilizing Polymer Optical Fiber (POF) and intensity variation technique to alleviate the mechanical limitations of silica fiber under high pressure applications8. In order to increase the sensitivity of flexible capacitive pressure sensors, the author examines current developments with an emphasis on designs, materials and performance assessment techniques. In addition, it contrast efficient improvement methods and talks about upcoming developments for high-sensitivity applications9. Using FEA-optimized double-comb designs and corrugated edges this design has improved capacitive membrane type pressure sensor devices with high linearity10.

The author examined, the non-linearity of silicon CPS featuring a single sided island diaphragm. Two linearization techniques are put forth one uses a push-pull capacitive arrangement, while the other uses output feedback to modify the excitation voltage11. A multi-step notch design quadruple touch mode capacitive pressure sensor (TMCPS) is presented in this work to improve linearity, sensitivity and robustness for industrial applications. Better capacitance behavior and diaphragm performance are shown using COMSOL and MATLAB simulation12. A capacitive pressure with a linkage film proposed in this study, has improved linearity and increases the linear response range. Compared to designs with flat substrates, the suggested concave substrate Double Touch Mode Capacitive Pressure Sensor (DTMCPS) maintains higher saturation pressure and improves sensitivity. Strong industrial applications can benefit greatly from its enhanced performance which is confirmed by MATLAB simulations14. Supported by analytical modeling, MATLAB simulation and numerical analysis, a unique convex substrate square diaphragm TMCPS with improved sensitivity, extended linear operating range and superior performance for harsh-environment altimeter applications15–17. Conclusion

The Proposed M-shaped Double Touch Mode Capacitive Pressure Sensor (DTMCPS) increases linearity, sensitivity and capacitance response over the pressure range of (0–2) MPa which improves the diaphragm substrate contact area.The suggested structure circular diaphragm design is responsible for a noticeable increase in sensitivity. This structure significantly achieves a maximum capacitance of 19.9 pf, a sensitivity of 1.63 and a resolution of 3.25 Pa throughout a pressure range of (0–2) MPa. Consistency with small deflection theory is confirmed by the greatest central deflection of 1.81 from COMSOL, which closely matches MATLAB estimates of 1.62 . Both values stay below one-fifth of the diaphragm thickness. COMSOL simulations are used to validate the theoretical model. Due to its improved structural stability, the M-shaped design performs better than other geometrical configurations. The improved performance of the circular diaphragm is confirmed by both analytical and simulated results, which emphasize its enhanced sensitivity, better capacitance response and strengthened structural robustness. Future research will concentrate on temperature impacts, residual stress reduction.
