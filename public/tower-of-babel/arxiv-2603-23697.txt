Logarithmic purity and logarithmic Nori fundamental group
Sara Mehidi

Abstract
. We generalize the logarithmic purity theorem [ 4 , 18 ] to torsors which arise in the Kummer log flat topology under finite flat linearly reductive group schemes. As an application, we construct the logarithmic Nori fundamental group of a log regular log scheme classifying those torsors, and compare it to classical Nori fundamental group and tame fundamental group.



1.
Introduction

Logarithmic geometry in its current form was introduced in the 1980s by Fontaine-Illusie and was further developed systematically by Kato. It provides a natural framework for studying schemes with boundary singularities. In analogy with the classical étale coverings developed by Grothendieck, Fujiwara and Kato defined the notion of Kummer log étale coverings and introduced the logarithmic étale fundamental group
[
4
]
. This group classifies finite Kummer log étale covers and plays a key role in arithmetic geometry, particularly in the study of tame ramification along boundary divisors.

An important structural result was established in
[
4
]
and
[
18
]
, where a logarithmic analog of the
Zariski–Nagata purity theorem
was proved. Recall that the classical statement asserts that a finite flat morphism that is étale outside a subset of
codimension at least two is in fact étale everywhere. In the logarithmic setting, they established an analogous purity statement by interpreting finite generically étale morphisms that are tamely ramified along a boundary divisor as Kummer log étale covers. This perspective also enables a comparison between the logarithmic étale fundamental group and the tame fundamental group of Grothendieck-Murre introduced in
[
9
]
.

On the other hand, Nori introduced in
[
22
]
a fundamental group scheme attached to a scheme over a field, defined by means of the Tannakian formalism. In positive characteristic, this construction refines the classical étale fundamental group of Grothendieck by incorporating torsors under non-étale finite group schemes, such as
\mu_{p}
. Nori’s fundamental group scheme has since become an important tool in the study of essentially finite vector bundles and torsors in characteristic
p
.

The purpose of this article is to bring together these two perspectives. More precisely, we first extend the logarithmic purity theorem to a setting where we allow covers that are not necessarily log étale. In particular, we consider torsors under finite flat linearly reductive group schemes in the log setting. We then construct a logarithmic Nori fundamental group which classifies those log torsors.

1.1.
Logarithmic purity for linearly reductive torsors

A natural variant of the purity theorem arises when considering morphisms obtained as quotients of schemes by the action of finite flat group schemes. In particular, when the action is free and under suitable conditions ensuring that such quotients exist as schemes, the resulting morphism is an fppf torsor. There is a natural weakening of the freeness condition leading to the notion of logarithmic torsors.

Let
S
be a log regular log scheme (for example, a toric variety, resp. a regular scheme with log structure induced by a normal crossing divisor), and denote by
U\subset S
the dense open locus where the log structure is trivial (the dense torus, resp. the complement of the divisor). We are concerned with the following question: when does an fppf
G
-torsor on
U
extend to all of
S
? Our first result answers this question when
G
is linearly reductive by providing a solution in the category of log torsors.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
3.6
)
Let
S
be a log regular log scheme with integral underlying scheme, and let
U\subset S
be the dense open locus of trivial log structure. Let
G
be a finite flat linearly reductive group scheme over
S
. Then the restriction functor

\mathrm{Tor}_{\mathrm{klf}}(S,G)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Tor}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(U,G)

is an equivalence of categories.

This result can be viewed as a generalization of the logarithmic purity theorem to the log flat setting. It also extends
[
6
, Theorem 2.8]
where the same statement is proved in the case where
X
is regular and endowed with the log structure induced by a normal crossing divisor.

The problem of extending torsors in the logarithmic setting has been studied in
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
15
]
for torsors on curves. In the latter reference, it is shown that when
X/S
is a family of nodal curves endowed with a log structure making it log smooth, fppf
G
-torsors on the smooth locus
X_{U}
extend uniquely to logarithmic
G
-torsors on
X
when
G
is diagonalizable. In Corollary
3.8
, we extend this result to the case where
G
is linearly reductive.

1.2.
Logarithmic Nori fundamental group

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
, and let
S
be a log regular
k
-log scheme with integral underlying scheme. Fix a
k
-log point
s_{0}\to S
, i.e. the underlying scheme of
s_{0}
is
\Spec k
and it is endowed with a log structure inducing a morphism of log schemes
s_{0}\to S
. We consider the category of Kummer log flat torsors over
S
under finite linearly reductive
k
-group schemes, pointed over
s_{0}
.

Using Theorem
1.1
, we show that this category is cofiltered and admits a universal pro-object. This leads to our second main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem
4.5
and Corollary
4.7
)
Let
S
be a log regular
k
-log scheme with integral underlying scheme and let
s_{0}
be a
k
-log point. Then there exists a profinite pro-linearly reductive
k
-group scheme

\pi^{N,\log}_{1}(S,s_{0})

classifying Kummer log flat torsors over
S
(pointed over
s_{0}
) under finite linearly reductive group schemes. In addition, if
s_{0}
has the trivial log structure, then

\pi^{N,\log}_{1}(S,s_{0})\simeq\pi^{N}_{1}(U,s_{0})

where
\pi_{1}^{N}
denotes the maximal pro-linearly reductive quotient of the (classical) Nori fundamental group on
U
.

Naturally, when the log structures are all trivial, this recovers the maximal pro-linearly reductive quotient of the (classical) Nori fundamental group scheme of
S
. If
k
is algebraically closed of characteristic
0
, then finite linearly reductive group schemes coincide with constant finite group schemes. Hence, in this case, the construction recovers the logarithmic étale fundamental group.

1.3.
Future Directions

As a natural generalization, we aim to construct a logarithmic Nori fundamental group that classifies
all
Kummer log flat torsors, not only those under finite linearly reductive group schemes. Such a construction would provide a more complete picture of coverings in the logarithmic setting.

Nori showed that for a finite group scheme
G
over a field
k
, the data of an fppf
G
-torsor over a
k
-scheme
X
is equivalent to that of a tensor exact functor
\mathrm{Rep}_{k}(G)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Vect}(X)
, from the category of finite-dimensional representations of
G
to the category of vector bundles on
X
. The Nori fundamental group scheme is then obtained as the Tannaka dual of a suitable subcategory of vector bundles on
X
.
In the logarithmic setting, vector bundles are replaced by parabolic bundles (see, for example,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
), reflecting the additional boundary structure. A fundamental question is then whether the logarithmic Nori fundamental group can likewise be reconstructed from an appropriate Tannakian category and what applications it could have.

2.
Log torsors

We assume basic knowledge on log schemes and refer to
[
23
]
and
[
14
]
for reference. All the log schemes considered here are fine and saturated. We denote
(fs)
(resp.
(fs/X)
) the category of fine saturated log schemes (resp. fine saturated log schemes over
X
). In particular, all fiber products of log schemes considered here are understood in the category of fs log schemes. We also assume that our schemes here are locally noetherian.

We briefly recall the definitions of the Kummer log flat topology and Kummer log flat torsors and refer to
[
14
, Section 2]
for further details.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a log scheme. A family of morphisms
\{f_{i}:X_{i}\to X\}_{i}
of log schemes is called a covering of
X
for the Kummer log flat (resp. Kummer log étale) topology if the conditions (1) (resp. (1)
′
) and (2) are satisfied:

(1)

The morphisms
f_{i}
are log flat and of Kummer type, and the underlying morphisms of schemes are locally of finite presentation.

(1)
′

The morphisms
f_{i}
are log étale and of Kummer type.

(2)

X=\bigcup_{i}f_{i}(X_{i})
(set-theoretically).

We define the Grothendieck topology, called the Kummer log flat (resp. Kummer log étale) topology (denoted klf for simplification) on
(\mathrm{fs}/X)
by taking coverings as above, and we use the induced site to define log torsors.

Definition 2.2

(Logarithmic torsor)
.

Let
Y
,
X
be log schemes and
G
a group scheme over
X
such that
G\to X
is strict. Assume that
G
acts on
Y\to X
in the category of log schemes. We say that
Y\to X
is a klf
G
-torsor (or simply a log
G
-torsor) if there exists a klf covering
X_{i}\to X
and
G
-equivariant isomorphisms
Y\times_{X}X_{i}\simeq G_{X_{i}}
over
X_{i}
, where
G_{X_{i}}
is equipped with the left translation action of itself.

Example 2.3
.

Let
R
be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer
\pi
. Let
X=\Spec R
with log structure given by the chart

\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow R,\qquad 1\longmapsto\pi.

Set

Y=\Spec R[T]/(T^{r}-\pi)

with log structure defined by

\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow R[T]/(T^{r}-\pi),\qquad 1\longmapsto T.

The morphism of log schemes
Y\to X
is induced by the Kummer map of monoids

\mathbb{N}\xrightarrow{\times r}\mathbb{N}.

There is a natural
\mu_{r}
action on
Y
(which is not free) making
Y\to X
a klf
\mu_{r}
-torsor. If
r
is invertible in
R
, then it is a Kummer log étale
\mu_{r}
-torsor.

Log Purity Theorem

3.1.
Log regular base

The main result of this section (Theorem

[

]
,
[

, Theorem 7.6]
and independently by Mochizuki
[

Theorem 3.1

(
[
6

)
.

Let

be a regular integral scheme and let

be a normal crossing divisor. Endow

with the log structure induced by

and set

. Let

be a finite flat linearly reductive group scheme over

\mathrm{Tor}_{\mathrm{klf}}(X,G)\;\longrightarrow\;\mathrm{Tor}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(U,G)

In this section, we will extend this result to the case where

is log regular and

the dense locus of trivial log structure.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a log scheme. We denote the functor

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{m,log}:(fs/X)

\displaystyle\to(Ab)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\mapsto\Gamma(T,M_{T}^{gp})

It is a sheaf for the Kummer log flat topology by
[
12
, Theorem 3.2]
.

In what follows, we also denote
\mathbb{G}_{m,log}
the induced sheaves on the sites
(Sch/X)_{fppf}
and
(Sch/X)_{\acute{e}t}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a logarithmic scheme. Then the natural map

H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(X,\mathbf{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\log})\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(X,\mathbf{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\log})

is an isomorphism. Equivalently, every fppf
\mathbf{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\log}
-torsor on

X
descends uniquely to an étale
\mathbf{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\log}
-torsor on

X
.

Proof.

Let

\epsilon:(Sch/X)_{fppf}\to(Sch/X)_{\acute{e}t}

be the morphism of sites.

Given an abelian sheaf
F
on
(Sch/X)_{fppf}
, the Leray spectral sequence gives

H^{p}_{\acute{e}t}(X,R^{q}\epsilon_{*}F)\implies H^{p+q}_{fppf}(X,F).

It yields an exact sequence

0\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(X,\epsilon_{*}F)\to H^{1}_{fppf}(X,F)\to H^{0}_{\acute{e}t}(X,R^{1}\epsilon_{*}F).

We apply this to
F:=\epsilon^{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,log}=\mathbb{G}_{m,log}
. We have that
\mathbb{G}_{m,log}\to\epsilon_{*}\epsilon^{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,log}
is an isomorphism. We then get

0\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m,log})\to H^{1}_{fppf}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m,log})\to H^{0}_{\acute{e}t}(X,R^{1}\epsilon_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,log}).

It is enough to prove that
R^{1}\epsilon_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,log}=0
. For that, since it’s a sheaf on
(Sch/X)_{\acute{e}t}
, it is enough to show that its geometric stalks vanish. If
X
is local strictly henselian, then
H^{1}_{fppf}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m,log})=H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m,log})=0
by Hilbert 90 (
[
21
, Corollary 3.21]
). This concludes the proof.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S
be a log regular log scheme. Let
U
be the dense open subset of
S
with trivial log structure. Then the restriction morphism

H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(S,\mathbb{G}_{m,log})\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(U,\mathbb{G}_{m})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Write
j:U\hookrightarrow S
for the open immersion. For

an abelian sheaf on
(Sch/U)_{\acute{e}t}
, the Leray sequence gives

H_{\acute{e}t}^{p}(S,R^{q}j_{*}F)\implies H_{\acute{e}t}^{p+q}(U,F)

yielding when applied to

0\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(S,j_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,U})\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(U,\mathbb{G}_{m,U})\to H^{0}_{\acute{e}t}(S,R^{1}j_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,U}).

By
[
13
, Theorem 11.6]
,
M_{X}^{\mathrm{gp}}\simeq j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\times}
. It follows that

0\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(S,\mathbb{G}_{m,log})\to H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(U,\mathbb{G}_{m,U})\to H^{0}_{\acute{e}t}(S,R^{1}j_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,U}).

By
[

R^{1}j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\times}=0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
S
be a log regular log scheme, with integral underlying scheme and let
U
be the dense open subset of
S
with trivial log structure. Let
G
be a finite flat linearly reductive

-group scheme. Then the restriction functor

\mathrm{Tor}_{\mathrm{klf}}(S,G)\;\longrightarrow\;\mathrm{Tor}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(U,G).

is an equivalence of categories.

Proof.

In
[
6
, Proposition 4.3]
, it was proved that when
G
is finite flat over
S
, the functor

\mathrm{Tor}_{\mathrm{klf}}(S,G)\;\longrightarrow\;\mathrm{Tor}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(U,G).

is fully faithful when
S
is regular. The proof relies on
[
7
, Corollary 6.1.14]
which only needs
S
to be normal and integral (as a scheme). But if
S
is log regular, then it is normal by
[
13
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Essential surjectivity relies on Lemma
3.5
and follows the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 2.8]
. We summarize it here. By étale descent, we may assume that

is local and strictly henselian. By
[

, Lemma 2.20]
, there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Delta\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow 0

with

a diagonalizable group scheme and
H
a constant group scheme of invertible order.

An fppf

-torsor

decomposes as an fppf

-torsor

and an étale

-torsor

. By
[
6

, to extend the fppf

over

Since

is log regular, the étale

-torsor

extends uniquely to a Kummer log étale

-torsor

by
[

, Theorem 7.6]
. Then

is log étale and hence

is log regular. By Theorem

, the

extends uniquely to a Kummer log flat
\Delta
-torsor
T^{\prime\prime}\to T^{\prime}
. Finally, to conclude that the composition

is a klf

[
6
, Theorem 2.8]
.

4.
Log Nori fundamental group

4.1.
Log étale fundamental group

The construction below of the logarithmic étale fundamental group appears for example in
[
11
, Section 4]
and
[

, Section 10]
.

Let
X
be a log scheme with a connected underlying scheme. Let

be a log geometric point of

as defined in
[

, Definition 4.1]
. We denote by
(\mathrm{kl\acute{e}}(X))
the category consisting of finite Kummer log étale covers of

and
(\mathrm{fsets})
the category of finite sets. The fiber functor

is pro-representable by a pro-object

of

(\mathrm{kl\acute{e}}(X))

\pi_{1}^{\log,\acute{e}t}(X,\overline{x})
denotes the profinite group
\mathrm{Aut}(F)
, then

induces an equivalence of categories

(\mathrm{kl\acute{e}}(X))\;\xrightarrow{\sim}\;{\pi_{1}^{\log,\acute{e}t}(X,\overline{x})}-(\mathrm{fsets}),

where the right-hand side denotes the category of finite sets endowed with a
continuous action of
\pi_{1}^{\log,\acute{e}t}(X,\overline{x})
. The group
\pi_{1}^{\log,\acute{e}t}(X,\overline{x})
is called the
logarithmic étale fundamental group

of

at

, and

is called a

of

If

is a finite (abstract) group, Kummer étale Galois covers of

with group

endowed with an action of

by

-torsor over

along continuous homomorphisms

Now, we fix an algebraically closed field

of characteristic

.
Then every

-group scheme is étale, and hence every fppf

-torsor is étale by descent. In particular, any construction of
an analogue of the Nori fundamental group of a

In the next section, our goal is to construct an analogue of the Nori
fundamental group which classifies Kummer log flat torsors in the case

x_{0}
. Then the triple

\bigl(T_{1}\times_{T_{0}}T_{2},\;G_{1}\times_{G_{0}}G_{2},(t_{1},t_{2})\bigr)=:\bigl(T^{\times},G^{\times},t^{\times}\bigr)

is a klf torsor over
X
, pointed over
x_{0}
.

Proof.

We claim that
T^{\times}\to X
is surjective on sets. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, if
p_{i}:T_{1}\times_{X}T_{2}\to T_{i}\to T_{0}
, there exists a (unique) morphism
g:T_{1}\times_{X}T_{2}\to G_{0}
such that
gp_{1}=p_{2}
(because
T_{0}\to X
is a klf
G_{0}
-torsor). In particular, considering the natural map
T^{\times}\to T_{1}\times_{X}T_{2}
and if
\epsilon:\Spec k\to G_{0}
is the identity section, we have that
T^{\times}
is the closed subscheme
g^{-1}(\epsilon)\subset T_{1}\times_{X}T_{2}
. Now, we know that
\pi:T_{1}\times_{X}T_{2}\to X
is a klf
(G_{1}\times G_{2})
-torsor. By
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
,
\pi
has a finite underlying morphism of schemes, and since
X
is locally noetherian,
\pi
is locally of finite presentation. It follows from
[
14
, Proposition 2.5]
that
\pi
is open. But it is also closed as it is finite. On the other hand, since
G_{0}
is finite over
k
, the connected components of
G_{0}
have a single element each, hence
\epsilon=G^{0}_{0}
as sets, where
G^{0}_{0}
is the identity component of
G_{0}
. Since
G^{0}_{0}
is open and closed,
g^{-1}(G^{0}_{0})
is open and closed, and therefore,
\pi(T^{\times})=\pi(g^{-1}(\epsilon))=\pi(g^{-1}(G^{0}_{0}))
(as sets) is open and closed in
X
. But since
X
is connected,
\pi(T^{\times})=X
, hence the claim.

Since
T^{\times}\to X
is surjective on sets,
T^{\times}_{U}
(where
T^{\times}_{U}=T_{1,U}\times_{T_{0,U}}T_{2,U}=T^{\times}\times_{X}U
) is not empty. Since the action of
G^{\times}
on
T^{\times}_{U}
is free and
T^{\times}_{U}\to U
is affine, it follows that there exists a closed subscheme
Z\subset U
such that
T^{\times}_{U}\to Z
is an fppf
G^{\times}
-torsor. Since
U
is reduced and connected,
Z=U
by
[
22
, Chapter II]
. Therefore,

T^{\times}_{U}\simeq T_{1,U}\times_{T_{0,U}}T_{2,U}\to U

is an fppf
G^{\times}
-torsor. By Lemma
4.3
,
G^{\times}
is linearly reductive. Hence, by Theorem
3.6
,
T^{\times}_{U}
extends uniquely to

\widetilde{T}\to X

a klf
G^{\times}
-torsor (note that if
G\to\Spec k
is finite linearly reductive, then
G_{X}\to X
is finite flat linearly reductive by
[
1
, Proposition 2.6]
).

Denote the projections

\rho_{i}:G^{\times}=G_{1}\times_{G_{0}}G_{2}\to G_{i},~~~~~~i\in\{0,1,2\}.

Set
\widetilde{T}_{i}:=\widetilde{T}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{i}\to X
to be the contracted product corresponding to the klf
G_{i}
-torsor obtained by push-out of
\widetilde{T}\to X
along
\rho_{i}
. We claim that

\widetilde{T}_{i,U}\simeq T_{i,U}

as fppf
G_{i}
-torsors over
U
. We consider the projections

\psi_{i,U}:\widetilde{T}_{U}=T_{1,U}\times_{T_{0,U}}T_{2,U}\to T_{i,U}

which are equivariant with respect to
\rho_{i}
. We now construct

\displaystyle\Phi_{i,U}:\widetilde{T}_{U}\times G_{i}=(T_{1,U}\times_{T_{0,U}}T_{2,U})\times G_{i}

\displaystyle\to T_{i,U}

\displaystyle(t,g)

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi_{i,U}(t).g

Let
h\in G^{\times}
. Consider the action of
G^{\times}
on
\widetilde{T}_{U}\times G_{i}
given by
(t,g).h=(t.h,\rho_{i}(h)^{-1}g)
. We have that

\Phi_{i,U}((t,g).h)=\Phi_{i,U}(t.h,\rho_{i}(h)^{-1}g)=\psi_{i,U}(t.h)\rho_{i}(h)^{-1}g=\psi_{i,U}(t)\rho_{i}(h)\rho_{i}(h)^{-1}g=\Phi_{i,U}(t,g)

where the next-to-last equality follows from the fact that
\psi_{i,U}
is
\rho_{i}
-equivariant. Therefore, we get a map

\overline{\Phi}_{i,U}:\widetilde{T}_{U}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{i}\to T_{i,U}

which is
G_{i}
-equivariant. Since both sides are
G_{i}
-fppf torsors over
U
, it is an isomorphism of
G_{i}
-torsors. By Theorem
3.6
(uniqueness of extension), we deduce an isomorphism of klf
G_{i}
-torsors

\widetilde{T}_{i}=\widetilde{T}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{i}\xrightarrow[h_{i}]{\simeq}T_{i}.

We claim that there exists an isomorphism

\widetilde{T}\simeq T_{1}\times_{T_{0}}T_{2}

over
X
which is
G^{\times}
-equivariant.
We notice that for
i\in\{1,2\}
, we have by construction that the composition
\widetilde{T}_{i,U}\xrightarrow[h_{i}]{\simeq}T_{i,U}\xrightarrow{f_{i}}T_{0,U}\xrightarrow[h_{0}^{-1}]{\simeq}\widetilde{T}_{U,0}
is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{U}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{i}

\displaystyle\to\widetilde{T}_{U}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{0},

\displaystyle(t,g_{i})

\displaystyle\mapsto(t,\pi_{i}(g_{i})).

We would like to prove that the global
\pi_{i}
-morphism

\widetilde{T}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{i}\xrightarrow[h_{i}]{\simeq}T_{i}\xrightarrow{f_{i}}T_{0}\xrightarrow[h_{0}^{-1}]{\simeq}\widetilde{T}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{0}

is given by
(t,g_{i})\mapsto(t,\pi_{i}(g_{i}))
as well. For this, it is enough to show that if
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are finite flat
X
-group schemes with a map
H_{1}\to H_{2}
, and if
Y_{1}\to X
is a klf
H_{1}
-torsor and
Y_{2}\to X
a klf
H_{2}
-torsor, then the restriction map of morphisms of torsors

\mathrm{Hom}_{X}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{X}(Y_{1,U},Y_{2,U})

is injective. Let
f,g:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
be two morphisms of torsors over
X
such that
f_{U}=g_{U}
. Consider the Cartesian diagram

\begin{matrix}E&\longrightarrow&Y_{2}\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
Y_{1}&\longrightarrow&Y_{2}\times_{X}Y_{2}\end{matrix}

where the bottom horizontal map is the map
(f,g)
and the right vertical map is the diagonal
\Delta_{2}
(we take the fs fiber product). The diagonal
\Delta_{2}
is closed. Indeed, if we compose it with the isomorphism
Y_{2}\times_{X}Y_{2}\simeq H_{2}\times Y_{2}
(it is an isomorphism because
Y_{2}\to X
is a klf
H_{2}
-torsor), we get the morphism induced by the section
X\to H_{2}
, which is a closed immersion since
H_{2}\to X
is separated (since it is finite). Therefore,
E\to Y_{1}
is a closed immersion. But it contains a dense open subset of
Y_{1}
, hence
E=Y_{1}
and therefore
f=g
.

Now, it remains to notice that given how the maps
\widetilde{T}_{i}\to\widetilde{T}_{0}
are defined, we have

T_{1}\times_{T_{0}}T_{2}\simeq\widetilde{T}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{1}\times_{\widetilde{T}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{0}}\widetilde{T}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{2}\simeq\widetilde{T}\times^{G^{\times}}G_{1}\times_{G_{0}}G_{2}\simeq\widetilde{T}.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
and
G
be finite linearly reductive
k
-group schemes. Assume that we have group homomorphisms
G_{1}\to G_{0}
and
G_{2}\to G_{0}
. Then
G_{1}\times_{G}G_{2}
is a finite linearly reductive
k
-group scheme.

Proof.

G_{1}\times_{k}G_{2}
is finite linearly reductive by
[
1
, Proposition 2.6]
. Furthermore, if
\phi_{i}:G_{i}\to G_{0}
, then
G_{1}\times_{G_{0}}G_{2}
is the equalizer of
\phi_{i}\circ p_{i}:G_{1}\times_{k}G_{2}\to G_{0}
, hence a (closed) subgroup scheme of
G_{1}\times_{k}G_{2}
, hence finite and linearly reductive by
[
1
, Proposition 2.7]
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

The category
FT(X)_{x_{0}}
is a cofiltered category.

Proof.

Indeed, we have that

•

FT(X)_{x_{0}}
is nonempty:
X\to X
is a klf torsor.

•

If
T_{1},T_{2}
are klf torsors,
T_{1}\times_{X}T_{2}
is a klf torsor too, and we have
morphisms
T_{1}\times_{X}T_{2}\to T_{1},T_{1}\times_{X}T_{2}\to T_{2}
.

•

Given two morphisms of klf-torsors
f,g:T^{\prime}\to T
over
X
, there exists by Lemma
4.2
a klf torsor
T^{\prime}\times_{T}T^{\prime}
over
X
such that the two compositions
f\circ p_{1},g\circ p_{2}:T^{\prime}\times_{T}T^{\prime}\to T^{\prime}\to T
are equal.

∎
