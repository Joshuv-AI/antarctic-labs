Valley-Controlled Viscosity of Two-Dimensional Dirac Fluids Preprint: APS/123-QED
Alexey Ermakov, Alessandro Principi

Abstract
Motivated by recent experiments in weakly hybridized small-angle twisted bilayer graphene, we investigate how valley imbalance affects the viscosity of two-dimensional Dirac fluids. We show that shifting the two low-energy Dirac cones relative to one another provides a direct knob to control the viscosity of the electron fluid. As the splitting is increased, the system passes through distinct transport regimes associated with valley depletion, charge-neutrality crossover, and the onset of electron-hole scattering, producing a pronounced nonmonotonic response. To place this result in context, we also analyze the viscosity in monolayer graphene (MLG) and two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG). We show that, due to the strong dependence of its inertial mass density on temperature, the kinematic viscosity of MLG is a monotonically decreasing function of temperature. Our results identify valley control as a route to tuning hydrodynamic transport in Dirac materials and clarify the interplay between band structure, scattering phase space, and screening in setting the viscous response.

Introduction

The hydrodynamic regime of electronic transport emerges when electron-electron collisions dominate over momentum-relaxing processes due to disorder, phonons, or boundaries

2

;
26
. In this regime, the shear viscosity governs momentum diffusion and leaves direct signatures in nonlocal transport

Small-angle (away from the magic angle) twisted bilayer graphene (SA-TBG)

Because it redistributes carriers between the two Dirac cones, and can even drive one valley across charge neutrality, it naturally changes the balance between intraband and electron-hole scattering channels and therefore need not affect the viscosity monotonically.

Recent advances in nonlocal transport measurements have begun to probe related valley-dependent thermodynamic and transport effects

30

7

20

In particular, viscosity changes induced by valley splitting should manifest directly in tunable nonlocal resistance profiles, in the flow pattern of the current, and in the critical thresholds for current-vortex formation

;

;

In this paper we use a linearized semiclassical Boltzmann approach to study how electron-electron scattering controls the shear and kinematic viscosities in this valley-imbalanced setting. The main result is that the viscosity of the two-cone Dirac system is a strongly nonmonotonic function of the valley splitting
\Delta
. As
\Delta
is increased, the viscosity first rises, then develops a maximum and a second minimum, before increasing again in the strongly degenerate regime.

We also study the role of carrier density and thermal effects. We explicitly focus on momentum-conserving electron-electron interaction. We assume, as it is appropriate in the hydrodynamic regime of graphene-based systems
4
;
22
;
26
, that Umklapp processes, acoustic phonons, and static disorder play a subdominant role
14
;
10
;
8
. We can then isolate the intrinsic viscous response driven purely by Coulomb interactions.

We also study monolayer graphene (MLG) and a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) as benchmarks and control cases. They serve two purposes: to test the kinetic framework against simpler limits and to isolate which features are specific to the SA-TBG problem. In particular, the MLG benchmark shows both the known features of electron viscosity and a subtle screening effect in the shear channel. Meanwhile, the parabolic-band model provides the conventional Fermi-liquid control case.

In MLG and the 2DEG, we also discuss the behavior of the shear and kinematic viscosities,
\eta
and
\nu
, with particular emphasis on the viscosity minimum. In simple fluids,
\eta
often exhibits a minimum at the crossover between liquid-like and gas-like behavior. When the mass density varies only weakly,
\nu
does as well. Here we show that the shear viscosity of both MLG and the 2DEG likewise exhibits a minimum at the crossover between degenerate Fermi-liquid and nondegenerate Fermi-gas regimes. In MLG, however, the strong temperature dependence of the inertial mass density prevents a corresponding minimum in
\nu
, which instead decreases monotonically with temperature. This unusual behavior is a distinctive consequence of graphene’s linear band structure and semimetallic character.

Section IV collects the benchmark systems: monolayer graphene and the parabolic-band 2DEG. Section V contains conclusions and discussions. Technical derivations are collected in the Appendices.

II
General kinetic framework

In this section we introduce the kinetic framework used throughout the paper to compute the shear viscosity of the electron fluids considered below. The starting point is the semiclassical Boltzmann equation for the quasiparticle distribution function
f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})
, where
\lambda
denotes a generic composite quasiparticle label collecting all discrete internal degrees of freedom. In the model-specific sections this general label will be specialized further, but throughout Sec.
II
it is kept abstract.
Since our focus is the hydrodynamic regime, we retain only electron-electron scattering in the collision integral, which controls momentum-conserving relaxation of a nonuniform flow.
Within this approximation,

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}_{\bm{r}}f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})-e{\bm{E}}\cdot\nabla_{{\bm{k}}}f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})={\cal I}_{\rm ee}[f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})]~,

(1)

where
{\bm{v}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}=\nabla_{\bm{k}}\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}
. Here,
\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}
is the band energy, while the collision integral is

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\rm ee}[f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bm{q}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}W_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{q}})\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}+\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}})

(2)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\big[f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}(1-f_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime}})(1-f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}})-(1-f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda})(1-f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})f_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}\big]~.

The scattering kernel
W_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}
contains the dynamically screened Coulomb interaction
V_{\rm ee}({\bm{q}},\omega)=v_{\bm{q}}/\epsilon({\bm{q}},\omega)
, with
v_{\bm{q}}=2\pi e^{2}/(\kappa q)
the bare Coulomb interaction and
\epsilon({\bm{q}},\omega)=1-v_{\bm{q}}\chi_{nn}({\bm{q}},\omega)
the dielectric function. Here
\kappa
is the dielectric constant of the surrounding medium and
\chi_{nn}
is the density-density response function. The explicit formulas for
W_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}
and the overlap factors are collected in App.
B
. Although Eq. (
1
) is written in a form that allows for external fields, all viscosity calculations below are performed at
{\bm{E}}=0
. For brevity, we set
\hbar=1
in the intermediate kinetic derivations of Sec.
II
and the Appendices, but restore factors of
\hbar
in all final physical results and thermodynamic quantities.

Figure
2
shows a representative momentum-conserving process contributing to the collision integral.

To calculate the shear viscosity, we consider a weakly nonuniform flow described by a local velocity field
{\bm{u}}({\bm{r}})
.
As a reference state we use a locally drifting distribution,

\displaystyle f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{\rm F}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}-\mu-{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{u}}({\bm{r}})}{k_{\rm B}T}\right)+\delta f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})

(3)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle f^{(0)}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})+\delta f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})~,

where
n_{\rm F}(x)=(e^{x}+1)^{-1}
is the Fermi distribution and
\delta f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})
is the nonequilibrium correction induced by spatial gradients of the flow.
Linearizing Eq. (
1
) in gradients of
{\bm{u}}({\bm{r}})
and restricting to incompressible flow,
\partial_{\rho}u_{\rho}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\partial f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda})}{\partial\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}}\right)A^{\alpha\beta}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\partial_{\alpha}u_{\beta}={\cal I}_{\rm ee}^{({\rm lin})}[\delta f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})],

(4)

where
{\cal I}_{\rm ee}^{({\rm lin})}
is the linearized collision integral and
A^{\alpha\beta}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}~=~\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}(k^{\alpha}k^{\beta}/k^{2}-\delta^{\alpha\beta}/2)
is the traceless stress vertex that projects the kinetic equation onto the shear channel.
We approximate the nonequilibrium correction by the standard shear-mode Ansatz

\displaystyle\delta f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{v}\left(-\frac{\partial f^{(0)}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}}{\partial\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}}\right)A^{\alpha\beta}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\partial_{\alpha}u_{\beta},

(5)

where
\tau_{v}
is the viscosity transport time.
In Eq. (
5
) we approximate the perturbation to the equilibrium distribution by the leading shear harmonic and take
\tau_{v}
to be independent of momentum and discrete index.

The dissipative stress tensor is proportional to the symmetrized traceless velocity gradient,
\Pi^{(\rm diss)}_{\alpha\beta}=2\eta u_{\alpha\beta}
.
Comparing this relation with the kinetic stress tensor obtained from the shear vertex and the correction
\delta f_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}
gives

\displaystyle\eta={\cal D}\tau_{v},\qquad\nu=\frac{\eta}{\rho_{\rm eff}},

(6)

where
{\cal D}
plays a role of viscosity Drude weight and
\rho_{\rm eff}
is the effective inertial density appropriate to the model under consideration.

Substituting the Ansatz (
5
) into Eq. (
2
) and projecting onto the shear channel yields a model-dependent expression for the collision rate
1/\tau_{v}
.
For the Dirac systems studied below, the final result is quoted in Sec.
III
, while the full reduction of the collision integral, including the explicit forms of the all the quantities introduced earlier, is given in App.
B
.

III
Valley-polarized two-cone Dirac system

We now turn to an effective two-cone Dirac system in which an energy offset
\Delta
separates the two low-energy cones. The setup is motivated by weakly hybridized SA-TBG, where (mini)valley-dependent energy shifts can be induced by the application of an interlayer potential difference (
i.e.
by a displacement field), while the low-energy spectrum still resembles two displaced Dirac cones. In the explicit model below, the index
\Lambda=0,1
labels these two displaced cones within a mini-Brillouin zone (mBZ).
Note, however, that the two valleys of the graphene Brillouin zone remain degenerate. Unless otherwise stated, all results are reported for a single valley in the Brillouin zone. The full valley multiplicity is obtained by multiplying by a factor of two. Throughout, the true valley index is suppressed, and the term “valley” is used as shorthand for “mini-valley”.
Throughout this section we use the two-cone model as an effective description of the mini-valley-imbalanced viscous fluid introduced schematically in Fig.
1
.

III.1
Model and density constraint

We denote the band index by
s=\pm 1
and the displaced-valley index by
\Lambda=\{0,1\}
. When both labels need to be treated together, we group them into a composite index
a=(s,\Lambda)
and write
\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},a}\equiv\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},s,\Lambda}
.
The single-particle energy is taken to be

\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},s,\Lambda}=s\hbar v_{\rm F}k+\Lambda\Delta,

(7)

We allow scattering between electrons belonging to the same valley
as well as to different ones. However, we assume that each electron preserves its valley index during the scattering process. That is, no intervalley scattering occurs, and the population of each valley is conserved.
This is appropriate when disorder is smooth on the lattice scale and the interaction is sufficiently long ranged, so that the large momentum transfer needed to switch valleys is suppressed. The corresponding collision-integral formulas are obtained by extending the framework of Sec. II and are given in App.
E
.

In equilibrium, the chemical potential
\mu
is common to both displaced valleys, so the energy offset is accounted for by the effective chemical potential of each valley

\mu_{\Lambda}\equiv\mu-\Lambda\,\Delta,

(8)

so that

f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},s,\Lambda}-\mu)=f^{(0)}(s\hbar v_{\rm F}k-\mu_{\Lambda})
. Here
v_{\rm F}=10^{6}
m/s is the Fermi velocity.
Because the valley index is kept explicit throughout this section, the remaining internal degeneracy factor is the spin degeneracy
N=g_{s}=2
.

For a two-dimensional Dirac fluid with internal degeneracy
N
, the net carrier density is

\displaystyle n_{\rm D}(T,\mu)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\varepsilon\,\frac{N\varepsilon}{2\pi(\hbar v_{\rm F})^{2}}\left[n_{\rm F}\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon-\mu}{k_{\rm B}T}\right)-n_{\rm F}\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon+\mu}{k_{\rm B}T}\right)\right]

(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2\pi}\left(\frac{k_{\rm B}T}{\hbar v_{\rm F}}\right)^{2}\left[{\rm Li}_{2}\!\left(-e^{-\mu/(k_{\rm B}T)}\right)-{\rm Li}_{2}\!\left(-e^{\mu/(k_{\rm B}T)}\right)\right],

while the corresponding energy density is

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\rm D}(T,\mu)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\varepsilon\,\frac{N\varepsilon^{2}}{2\pi(\hbar v_{\rm F})^{2}}\left[n_{\rm F}\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon-\mu}{k_{\rm B}T}\right)+n_{\rm F}\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon+\mu}{k_{\rm B}T}\right)\right]

(10)

\displaystyle=-\frac{N(k_{\rm B}T)^{3}}{\pi(\hbar v_{\rm F})^{2}}\left[{\rm Li}_{3}\!\left(-e^{-\mu/(k_{\rm B}T)}\right)+{\rm Li}_{3}\!\left(-e^{\mu/(k_{\rm B}T)}\right)\right].

For a scale-invariant Dirac fluid, the effective inertial density entering
\nu
is naturally related to the enthalpy density
w_{\rm D}

24
;
22
,

\rho_{\rm D}(T,\mu)=\frac{w_{\rm D}(T,\mu)}{v_{\rm F}^{2}}=\frac{d+1}{d}\frac{\varepsilon_{\rm D}(T,\mu)}{v_{\rm F}^{2}},

(11)

where
d=2
is the number of spatial dimensions.

For later use, we define the single-valley carrier and inertial-mass densities by

n_{v}(\mu,T)\equiv n_{\rm D}(T,\mu)\big|_{N=2},\quad\rho_{v}(\mu,T)\equiv\rho_{\rm D}(T,\mu)\big|_{N=2}.

(12)

The fixed gate-controlled density then becomes

n_{0}=n_{v}(\mu_{0},T)+n_{v}(\mu_{1},T)=n_{v}(\mu,T)+n_{v}(\mu-\Delta,T),

(13)

which implicitly defines the global chemical potential
\mu(T,\Delta)
for a given set of parameters
(n_{0},T,\Delta)
.
The corresponding two-valley inertial density is

\rho_{\rm eff}(T,\Delta)=\rho_{v}(\mu,T)+\rho_{v}(\mu-\Delta,T),

(14)

and all kinematic and shear viscosities shown below are computed from Eq. (
6
).

The corresponding viscosity Drude weight is

\displaystyle{\cal D}=N\sum_{a}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{v_{\rm F}^{2}k^{3}}{16k_{\rm B}T\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},a}-\mu}{2k_{\rm B}T}\right)},

(15)

where the composite index
a=(s,\Lambda)
collects the band and valley labels.

The inverse viscosity transport time then assumes the following form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi v_{\rm F}^{2}}{8{\cal D}}\sum_{a,b,c,d}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\int dq\int dk\int dk^{\prime}\frac{q}{2\pi}\frac{|V_{\rm ee}(q,\omega)|^{2}}{4k_{\rm B}T\sinh^{2}(\frac{\omega}{2k_{\rm B}T})}\frac{k}{2\pi}\frac{k^{\prime}}{2\pi}{\cal I}_{a}(k,\omega,q){\cal I}^{\prime}_{b}(k^{\prime},\omega,q)

(16)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle[f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},a})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},a}+\omega)][f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},b})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},b}-\omega)]{\cal M}_{ab}(k,k^{\prime},\omega,q),

where
{\cal I}_{a}(k,\omega,q)
and
{\cal I}^{\prime}_{b}(k^{\prime},\omega,q)
are the angular phase-space factors generated by the two energy-conserving
\delta
-function integrals, while
{\cal M}_{ab}(k,k^{\prime},\omega,q)
is the corresponding shear-mode matrix element in the shear channel. The explicit expressions for
{\cal I}_{a}
,
{\cal I}^{\prime}_{b}
, and
{\cal M}_{ab}
, together with the full reduction of the collision integral, are given in App.
B
. The corresponding valley-imbalance extension used in this section is summarized in App.
E
.

III.3
Physical interpretation

For temperatures below
T_{c}\approx 260
K the curve
\nu(\Delta)
has a clear maximum away from
\Delta=0
.
As temperature increases, the maximum of
\nu(\Delta,T)
broadens and moves toward
\Delta=0
, so that its location becomes increasingly difficult to determine numerically.
Within our resolution,
\Delta_{\max}=0
for all
T>T_{c}
.
Above this temperature we do not resolve a robust maximum at finite
\Delta
.
This behavior is consistent with a simple physical picture. For small
\Delta
, both valleys are electron doped and contribute comparable phase space for shear relaxation. Near
\Delta_{\max}
, the shifted valley is driven close to neutrality while the other valley remains electron doped; this suppresses the net contribution of the shifted cone and produces a peak in
\nu
. For
\Delta>\Delta_{\max}
, the shifted valley becomes hole doped and electron-hole scattering channels open, lowering the viscosity toward a second minimum at
\Delta_{\min}
. At still larger splitting, both valleys move deeper into the degenerate regime, phase-space restrictions and Pauli blocking dominate, and
\nu
rises again.

The termination of the peak line at
T_{c}
has the same origin. Once thermal broadening is already strong enough at
\Delta=0
to wash out the distinction between a clearly electron-doped cone and a nearly neutral one, tuning
\Delta
no longer creates a separate weak-relaxation regime. The finite-
\Delta
maximum therefore broadens and disappears rather than persisting to arbitrarily high temperature.

III.4
Analytic estimates for
\Delta_{\max}
and
\Delta_{\min}

The numerical structure described above can be understood analytically in the degenerate regime. For the single-cone density
n_{v}(\mu,T)
defined in Eq. (
12
), the exact polylog expression (
9
) reduces, for
\mu\gg k_{\rm B}T
(
\mu>0
), to the Sommerfeld expansion

n_{v}(\mu,T)\simeq\frac{g_{s}}{2\pi(\hbar v_{\rm F})^{2}}\left[\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}\right],

(17)

with corrections exponentially small in
-\mu/k_{\rm B}T
.
This approximation breaks down when
\mu\sim k_{\rm B}T
. A useful crossover marker is the intrinsic thermal density of electrons and holes at neutrality
11
;
23
,

n_{\rm th}(T)\equiv\frac{g_{s}\pi}{6}\left(\frac{k_{\rm B}T}{\hbar v_{\rm F}}\right)^{2},

(18)

and the corresponding temperature scale
T^{\ast}
defined by
n_{\rm th}(T^{\ast})\approx n_{0}
.

III.4.1
Evolution of the maximum
\Delta_{\max}(T)
.

Numerically we find a pronounced maximum of
\nu(\Delta,T)
at
\Delta=\Delta_{\max}(T)
.
Physically, this occurs when the shifted valley
\Lambda=1
is tuned close to charge neutrality,

\mu_{1}=\mu-\Delta\simeq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Delta_{\max}(T)\simeq\mu(T,\Delta_{\max}).

(19)

Under the condition
\mu_{1}=0
, the
\Lambda=1
valley carries no net charge,
n_{v}(0,T)=0
, and the density constraint (
13
) reduces to

n_{0}=n_{v}(\Delta_{\max},T).

(20)

Together with the exact polylog form (
9
), Eq. (
20
) provides an implicit definition of
\Delta_{\max}(T)
.
In the degenerate regime
\Delta_{\max}\gg k_{\rm B}T
, using the Sommerfeld expansion (
17
) yields

n_{0}\simeq\frac{g_{s}}{2\pi(\hbar v_{\rm F})^{2}}\left[\frac{\Delta_{\max}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}\right].

(21)

Defining the fully polarized
T=0
scale

\Delta_{0}\equiv\hbar v_{\rm F}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi n_{0}}{g_{s}}},

(22)

we obtain the compact estimate

\Delta_{\max}(T)\simeq\sqrt{\Delta_{0}^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}},\qquad(\Delta_{\max}\gg k_{\rm B}T).

(23)

Equation (
23
) is therefore a degenerate-regime approximation. Once
T
approaches
T^{\ast}
, or equivalently
\Delta_{\max}\sim k_{\rm B}T
, the full polylog relation (
9
) should be used instead. Figure
4
(a) compares the numerically extracted maxima (dots) with the low-
T
analytic result (red solid line) and with the phenomenological fit
\Delta^{\star}\sqrt{1-(T/T_{c})^{2}}
(black dashed line). For
T>T_{c}
the maximum is in fact at
\Delta=0
, but the peak at the origin becomes very broad and the direct numerical extraction of a separate finite-
\Delta
maximum becomes noisy; this is still visible in the full curves collected in Fig.
3
, where the higher-
T
profiles have their maxima at
\Delta=0
.

III.4.2
Evolution of the local minimum
\Delta_{\min}(T)
.

At larger valley splitting the shifted valley becomes hole doped,
\mu_{1}<0
, and strong electron-hole scattering channels become available. We empirically find a second minimum of
\nu(\Delta,T)
at
\Delta=\Delta_{\min}(T)
. To obtain a simple analytic estimate, we assume that the minimum is reached once a moderately developed hole pocket has formed in the shifted valley. We therefore define the hole chemical potential

\mu_{h}\equiv-\mu_{1}=\Delta-\mu,

(24)

and parameterize the onset of the electron-hole regime as

\mu_{h}\simeq\xi\,k_{\rm B}T,

(25)

where
\xi
is a non-universal constant extracted from a fit to the numerical data. Writing
\mu_{0}\equiv\mu>0
for the electron-doped valley and using Eq. (
17
) for the single-cone density, the degenerate electron-hole configuration gives

n_{0}\simeq\frac{g_{s}}{4\pi(\hbar v_{\rm F})^{2}}\left(\mu_{0}^{2}-\mu_{h}^{2}\right),

(26)

so that the
T^{2}
corrections cancel in the net charge and the density constraint becomes algebraic:

n_{0}=\frac{g_{s}}{4\pi(\hbar v_{\rm F})^{2}}\Big[\mu^{2}-(\Delta-\mu)^{2}\Big]=\frac{g_{s}}{4\pi(\hbar v_{\rm F})^{2}}\left(2\Delta\mu-\Delta^{2}\right).

(27)

Solving for
\mu_{h}
in terms of
\Delta
and imposing the minimum condition (
25
) gives

\frac{\Delta_{\min}^{2}-\Delta_{0}^{2}}{2\Delta_{\min}}=\xi k_{\rm B}T,

(28)

with solution

IV
Monolayer graphene, parabolic 2DEG, and the role of screening

To demonstrate that the nonmonotonic viscosity of the valley-imbalanced system is a unique consequence of intervalley carrier redistribution, we contrast our findings with the standard single-cone and parabolic-band limits. In this section, we analyze both
MLG

2DEG.
We use these model systems to also isolate

IV.1
Monolayer Graphene: Monotonicity and Screening

In MLG, the low-energy spectrum is described by valley-degenerate massless Dirac fermions,

\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},s}=s\hbar v_{\rm F}k,

(30)

Furthermore, extending our kinetic framework to the MLG shear channel reveals a subtle but essential physical feature. While projecting the collision integral onto the shear mode successfully removes the collinear divergence associated with Dirac chirality, the bare Coulomb interaction still leaves a residual logarithmic divergence. As we show below, introducing static screening is not a secondary quantitative correction, but a strict physical requirement to restore the correct low-temperature Fermi-liquid-like asymptotics in the shear transport time.

IV.1.3
Screening and low-
T
asymptotics in the shear channel

The role of screening in the shear channel deserves to be stated separately. First, we introduce the Thomas-Fermi wavevector

q_{\rm TF}=2\pi\alpha\frac{\partial n}{\partial\mu},

(42)

so the statically screened Coulomb interaction can be written as

\displaystyle V_{\rm scr}(q)=\frac{2\pi\alpha}{q+q_{\rm TF}}.

(43)

The shear mode removes the collinear divergence associated with the Dirac-wavefunction chirality factor, but this is not yet the full low-temperature story. Our calculation shows that with a bare Coulomb interaction a residual logarithmic divergence survives, so that the low-
T
behavior remains quasiparticle-lifetime-like,
\sim 1/[T^{2}\ln T]
(Fig.
7

43

T^{-2}
form. In practice, even a very small fraction of the full Thomas-Fermi screening (red points in Fig.
7
) already drives the numerical result close to the screened asymptote.

IV.2
Parabolic-Band 2DEG: The Fermi-Liquid Contrast

To isolate the features of the shear and kinematic viscosities that are unique to the Dirac dispersion, we contrast our results with a parabolic-band model, given by the Hamiltonian

H=\frac{k^{2}}{2m},

(44)

which is appropriate for a conventional 2DEG. Unlike the Dirac fluid, where the inertial density is intrinsically temperature-dependent, the 2DEG provides a standard Fermi-liquid baseline where the mass density is strictly fixed by the carrier concentration as
\rho=nm
.

Furthermore, analyzing the 2DEG collision integral reveals a divergence at small momentum transfers for the bare Coulomb interaction. Consequently, static screening is an essential regularization step to obtain a finite transport time. The results show that, unlike in the Dirac systems, both kinematic and shear viscosities develop a minimum as functions of temperature.

The kinetic calculation follows the same steps as in Sec. II: one linearizes the Boltzmann equation in the shear channel, projects onto the corresponding tensor structure, and extracts the viscosity transport time from the linearized collision integral.
Since the algebra is analogous but technically lengthy, we defer the derivation to App.
C
and quote here the final result.
We denote quantities referring to the parabolic-band 2DEG by the subscript “p”:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm p}}=\frac{-2\pi}{8m^{2}k_{\rm B}T{\cal D}_{\rm p}}

\displaystyle\int dqdkdk^{\prime}\int d\omega\frac{q}{2\pi}\frac{k}{2\pi}\frac{k^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{|V_{\rm ee}({\bm{q}},\omega)|^{2}}{4\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{\omega}{2k_{\rm B}T}\right)}[f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+\omega)][f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}-\omega)]

(45)

\displaystyle\times{\cal I}_{\rm p}(k,q,\omega){\cal I}_{\rm p}(k^{\prime},q,-\omega){\cal M}_{\rm p},

where

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\rm p}=2(k^{2}+k^{\prime 2})q^{2}-q^{4}-4m^{2}\omega^{2},

(46)

and the corresponding Drude
weight is

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{\rm p}(\mu,T)=-\frac{m(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}}{4\pi}{\rm Li}_{2}\left(-e^{\mu/k_{\rm B}T}\right),

(47)

The angular integral entering Eq. (
45
) is

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\rm p}(k,q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2m}{\pi}\frac{\theta\left(k-\left|\frac{q^{2}-2m\omega}{2q}\right|\right)}{\sqrt{4k^{2}q^{2}-\left(q^{2}-2m\omega\right)^{2}}}.

(48)

Equation (
45
) provides the parabolic-band analogue of the monolayer result.
The structure is similar. In the doped regime, the
q
integral generated by the collision kernel is divergent at its lower bound for the bare Coulomb interaction.
For this reason, screening is not a secondary quantitative correction here, but an essential part of the calculation.
We use the same static screening as defined in Eq. (
43
). This screened form controls the magnitude of the transport time and makes the low-temperature asymptotics well defined.

The low-temperature limit
\mu\gg k_{\rm B}T
can be obtained analytically by restricting the momenta to the Fermi surface, as shown in App.
D
. In this regime Eq. (
45
) reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm p}}=\frac{2\pi\alpha^{2}}{3}\frac{m}{\varepsilon_{F}}\frac{(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}}{\varepsilon_{F}}\int_{0}^{2k_{F}}\frac{q\,dq}{(q+q_{\rm TF})^{2}},

(49)

which makes the two main features of the parabolic-band problem explicit. First, the viscosity relaxation rate follows the conventional Fermi-liquid scaling
1/\tau_{\rm p}\propto T^{2}
at low temperature. Second, the remaining momentum integral depends explicitly on the screening wavevector
q_{\rm TF}
, showing that forward scattering must be regularized at the level of the transport time.

V
Conclusions

The central result of this paper is that a valley-imbalanced two-cone Dirac fluid, motivated by weakly hybridized small-angle twisted graphene, exhibits a strongly nonmonotonic viscosity as a function of valley splitting
\Delta
. As the latter is increased, the kinematic viscosity first rises, then develops a local maximum and a second minimum, and finally increases again in the strongly degenerate regime. The characteristic lines
\Delta_{\max}(T)
and
\Delta_{\min}(T)
admit simple analytic estimates: the maximum occurs when the shifted valley is driven close to charge neutrality, whereas the local minimum appears once that valley becomes hole doped and electron-hole scattering channels become effective.

We also calculate the viscosity of monolayer graphene and a parabolic-band 2DEG. In monolayer graphene at finite density, the shear viscosity develops a minimum while the kinematic viscosity remains monotonic. This behavior is unusual and reflects the pronounced temperature dependence of the inertial mass density at fixed carrier density. By contrast, in many simple fluids, both shear and kinematic viscosity exhibit a minimum near the crossover between dense liquid-like and dilute gas-like regimes. In the liquid regime, the viscosity typically rises rapidly as the temperature is lowered and may approximately follow an activated form, while in the dilute-gas regime kinetic theory gives
\eta\propto T^{1/2}
and, at fixed pressure,
\nu\propto T^{3/2}

33
;
18
.

In a 2DEG, by contrast, the mass density is fixed by the product of carrier density and effective mass, so the shear and kinematic viscosities have the same temperature dependence and both exhibit a minimum. Although this superficially resembles the behavior of simple fluids, the low-temperature viscosity of a Fermi liquid is qualitatively different: rather than increasing exponentially, it grows algebraically,
\eta\propto T^{-2}
. The underlying reason is the presence of a Fermi surface: Pauli blocking severely restricts the phase space for low-energy scattering and, correspondingly, renders generic quasiparticle interactions irrelevant in the renormalization-group sense
29
. As a result, the electron-electron scattering rate vanishes algebraically as
T\to 0
, leading in turn to an algebraic divergence of the viscosity.

Finally, we emphasized a technical aspect related to screening. In monolayer graphene, the projection onto shear modes suppresses the collinear singularity that would otherwise arise in the Dirac collision integral, but the bare Coulomb interaction still produces a residual logarithmic divergence. Screening is therefore necessary to recover the correct low-temperature asymptotics. In the parabolic-band 2DEG, by contrast, the low-temperature transport is Fermi-liquid-like, and static screening is required to regularize the transport integral and obtain a finite viscosity relaxation time.

\displaystyle f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}^{(0)}(1-f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}^{(0)})=-n(\omega)[f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}-\omega)],

(56)

we rewrite Eq.
54
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi v_{\rm F}^{2}}{8{\cal D}}\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{|V_{\rm ee}({\bm{q}},\omega)|^{2}}{4k_{\rm B}T\sinh^{2}(\frac{\omega}{2k_{\rm B}T})}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{k}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\delta(\omega+\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}-\omega){\cal F}({{\bm{k}},\lambda};{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime}){\cal F}({{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}};{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime})[f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}+\omega)]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle[f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}-\omega)]\big[\lambda k\cos(2\varphi_{{\bm{k}}})+\lambda^{\prime}k^{\prime}\cos(2\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})-\lambda^{\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}|\cos(2\varphi_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}})-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}|\cos(2\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}})\big]^{2}.

In what follows we proceed to prepare this equation for numerical implementation.

B.2
The integration boundaries

We shift
\varphi_{\bm{k}},\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\to\varphi_{\bm{k}}+\varphi_{\bm{q}},\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}+\varphi_{\bm{q}}
.
The delta function
\delta(\omega+\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime}})
is solved by

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\omega+\lambda v_{\rm F}k)-v_{\rm F}\sqrt{k^{2}+q^{2}+2kq\cos(\varphi_{\bm{k}})}=0.

(57)

The solution of the delta function
\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}-\omega)
can be found from the previous one by setting
k\to k^{\prime},\lambda\to\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}\to\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}
and
q,\omega\to-q,-\omega
.
Thus, we will not discuss it in detail, but directly quote the boundaries of integrations it produces at the end of this section.
The equation above has solution, when
\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\omega+\lambda v_{\rm F}k)>0
,

\displaystyle\cos(\varphi_{\bm{k}}^{(0)})=\frac{\omega^{2}+2\lambda\omega v_{\rm F}k-v_{\rm F}^{2}q^{2}}{2v_{\rm F}^{2}kq}.

(58)

Note that there are two solutions for
\varphi_{\bm{k}}^{(0)}
, one between
0
and
\pi
and one between
-\pi
and
0
.
The integrand is symmetric in
\varphi_{\bm{k}}
, so one can consider only one of them and multiply the result by
2
.
The cosine must lie between
-1
and
1
. Therefore, we must impose

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\omega+\lambda v_{\rm F}k)>0\\
(\lambda\omega-v_{\rm F}q)(\lambda\omega+v_{\rm F}q+2v_{\rm F}k)<0\\
(\lambda\omega+v_{\rm F}q)(\lambda\omega-v_{\rm F}q+2v_{\rm F}k)>0\end{array}\right..

We can then distinguish
2
cases for each of the last two equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle q<\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}}\\
{\displaystyle 0<k<-\frac{\lambda\omega+v_{\rm F}q}{2v_{\rm F}}}\end{array}\right.\quad\text{or}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle q>\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}}\\
{\displaystyle k>-\frac{\lambda\omega+v_{\rm F}q}{2v_{\rm F}}}\end{array}\right.,

and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle q>-\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}}\\
{\displaystyle k>\frac{v_{\rm F}q-\lambda\omega}{2v_{\rm F}}}\end{array}\right.\quad\text{or}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle q<-\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}}\\
{\displaystyle 0<k<\frac{v_{\rm F}q-\lambda\omega}{2v_{\rm F}}}\end{array}\right..

There are therefore
4
cases (
\ell=1,\ldots,4
), which can be summarised as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\max\left(0,q_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(\lambda\omega)\right)<q<q_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(\lambda\omega)}\\
{\displaystyle k_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(q,\lambda\omega)<k<k_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(q,\lambda\omega)}\end{array}\right.,

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&\ell=1&\ell=2&\ell=3&\ell=4\\
\hline\cr q_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(\lambda\omega)&{\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}}&{\displaystyle 0}&{\displaystyle\max\left(-\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}},\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}\right)}&{\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}}\\
\hline\cr q_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(\lambda\omega)&{\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}}&{\displaystyle\min\left(-\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}},\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}\right)}&{\displaystyle\infty}&{\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}}}\\
\hline\cr k_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(q,\lambda\omega)&{\displaystyle\max\left(0,k_{0}\right)}&0&{\displaystyle\max\left(k_{0},k_{1}\right)}&{\displaystyle\max\left(0,k_{1}\right)}\\
\hline\cr k_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(q,\lambda\omega)&{\displaystyle k_{1}}&{\displaystyle\min\left(k_{0},k_{1}\right)}&{\displaystyle\infty}&{\displaystyle k_{0}}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Here we defined
k_{0}=(v_{\rm F}q-\lambda\omega)/(2v_{\rm F})
and
k_{1}=-(v_{\rm F}q+\lambda\omega)/(2v_{\rm F})
.
Obviously, one has to require that
q_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(\lambda\omega)>0
and
k_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(q,\lambda\omega)>0
, as well as account for the first inequality in Eq.
B.2
.
We find, for each of the cases above,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime\prime}=\lambda}\\
{\displaystyle\max\left(0,q_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(\lambda\omega)\right)<q<q_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(\lambda\omega)}\\
{\displaystyle\max\left(-\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}},k_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(q,\lambda\omega)\right)<k^{\prime}<k_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(q,\lambda\omega)}\end{array}\right.\quad\text{or}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime\prime}=-\lambda}\\
{\displaystyle\max\left(0,q_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(\lambda\omega)\right)<q<q_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(\lambda\omega)}\\
{\displaystyle k_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(q,\lambda\omega)<k^{\prime}<\min\left(-\frac{\lambda\omega}{v_{\rm F}},k_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(q,\lambda\omega)\right)}\end{array}\right..

For the
\delta
-function
\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}-\omega)
we find instead

\displaystyle\cos(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{(0)})=-\frac{\omega^{2}-2\lambda^{\prime}\omega v_{\rm F}k^{\prime}-v_{\rm F}^{2}q^{2}}{2v_{\rm F}^{2}k^{\prime}q},

(89)

and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}}\\
{\displaystyle\max\left(0,-q_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(-\lambda^{\prime}\omega)\right)<q<-q_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(-\lambda^{\prime}\omega)}\\
{\displaystyle\max\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\omega}{v_{\rm F}},k_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(-q,-\lambda^{\prime}\omega)\right)<k<k_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(-q,-\lambda^{\prime}\omega)}\end{array}\right.\quad\text{or}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}=-\lambda^{\prime}}\\
{\displaystyle\max\left(0,-q_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(-\lambda^{\prime}\omega)\right)<q<-q_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(-\lambda^{\prime}\omega)}\\
{\displaystyle k_{\rm min}^{(\ell)}(-q,-\lambda^{\prime}\omega)<k<\min\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\omega}{v_{\rm F}},k_{\rm max}^{(\ell)}(-q,-\lambda^{\prime}\omega)\right)}\end{array}\right..

B.3
The matrix element of the viscosity

The matrix element on the last line of Eq.
B.1
reads

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime},\omega,q)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\big[\lambda k\cos(2\varphi_{{\bm{k}}})+\lambda^{\prime}k^{\prime}\cos(2\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})-\lambda^{\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}|\cos(2\varphi_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}})-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}|\cos(2\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}})\big]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\big[\lambda k\cos^{2}(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}})+\lambda^{\prime}k^{\prime}\cos^{2}(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})-\lambda^{\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}|\cos^{2}(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}})-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}|\cos^{2}(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}})\big]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\left[\lambda k\cos^{2}(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}})-\frac{\big[k\cos(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}})+q\cos(\varphi_{{\bm{q}}})\big]^{2}}{\omega/v_{\rm F}+\lambda k}+\lambda^{\prime}k^{\prime}\cos^{2}(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})-\frac{\big[k^{\prime}\cos(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})-q\cos(\varphi_{{\bm{q}}})\big]^{2}}{\lambda^{\prime}k^{\prime}-\omega/v_{\rm F}}\right]^{2},

where we used the
\delta
-functions that impose energy conservation to cancel a term proportional to

\displaystyle\lambda k+\lambda^{\prime}k^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}|-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}|=0,

(97)

and to replace

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}|\to\omega/v_{\rm F}+\lambda k,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}|{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}|\to\lambda^{\prime}k^{\prime}-\omega/v_{\rm F}.

(98)

If we shift
\varphi_{\bm{k}},\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\to\varphi_{\bm{k}}+\varphi_{\bm{q}},\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}+\varphi_{\bm{q}}
, the matrix element in Eq.
B.3
is the only term that depends on
\varphi_{\bm{q}}
.
We can then expand sines and cosines, as well as the brackets, and perform the integration.
Finally, all odd terms in either
\varphi_{\bm{k}}
or
\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}
vanish under angular integration, and we can use Eqs.
58
and
89
to replace the remaining trigonometric functions.
The final result is quite lengthy and is not reported here.

B.4
The integral of the delta function

We now evaluate

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\lambda}(k,\omega,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\varphi_{\bm{k}}}{2\pi}\delta(\omega+\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}){\cal F}({{\bm{k}},\lambda};{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime})

(99)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\varphi_{\bm{k}}}{2\pi}\delta(\omega+\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime}})\left(1+\lambda\frac{k+q\cos(\varphi_{\bm{k}}-\varphi_{\bm{q}})}{\lambda k+\omega/v_{\rm F}}\right).

We shift
\varphi_{\bm{k}}\to\varphi_{\bm{k}}+\varphi_{\bm{q}}
, and using Eq.
58
we get

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\lambda}(k,\omega,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\lambda\big[(\omega+2\lambda v_{\rm F}k)^{2}-v_{\rm F}^{2}q^{2}\big]}{2v_{\rm F}k(\lambda v_{\rm F}k+\omega)}\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{|\omega+\lambda v_{\rm F}k|}{v_{\rm F}^{2}kq\sqrt{1-\cos^{2}(\varphi_{\bm{k}}^{(0)})}}.

(100)

Finally, using Eq.
58
we get

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\lambda}(k,\omega,q)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{2\lambda\big[(\omega+2\lambda v_{\rm F}k)^{2}-v_{\rm F}^{2}q^{2}\big]}{2v_{\rm F}k(\lambda v_{\rm F}k+\omega)}\frac{2|\omega+\lambda v_{\rm F}k|}{\sqrt{(2v_{\rm F}^{2}kq)^{2}-(\omega^{2}+2\lambda\omega v_{\rm F}k-v_{\rm F}^{2}q^{2})^{2}}}.

(101)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle{\cal I}^{\prime}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(k^{\prime},\omega,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\varphi_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}-\omega){\cal F}({{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}};{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime})

(102)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{2\lambda^{\prime}\big[(\omega-2\lambda^{\prime}v_{\rm F}k^{\prime})^{2}-v_{\rm F}^{2}q^{2}\big]}{2v_{\rm F}k^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}v_{\rm F}k^{\prime}-\omega)}\frac{2|\omega-\lambda^{\prime}v_{\rm F}k^{\prime}|}{\sqrt{(2v_{\rm F}^{2}k^{\prime}q)^{2}-(\omega^{2}-2\lambda^{\prime}\omega v_{\rm F}k^{\prime}-v_{\rm F}^{2}q^{2})^{2}}}.

B.5
Final integral for the viscosity transport time

The final integral takes the form of Eq.
16
.
To find the temperature dependence of Eq.
16
, we scale the various quantities as follows

\displaystyle\omega=k_{\rm B}T\bar{\omega},\quad q=k_{\rm B}T\bar{q}/v_{\rm F},\quad k=k_{\rm B}T\bar{k}/v_{\rm F},\quad k^{\prime}=k_{\rm B}T\bar{k}^{\prime}/v_{\rm F},\quad\mu=k_{\rm B}T\bar{\mu}.

(103)

The delta-function integrals are then

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\lambda}(k,\omega,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{2\lambda(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}\big[(\bar{\omega}+2\lambda\bar{k})^{2}-\bar{q}^{2}\big]}{(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}2\bar{k}(\lambda\bar{k}+\bar{\omega})}\frac{2k_{\rm B}T|\bar{\omega}+\lambda\bar{k}|}{(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}\sqrt{(2\bar{k}\bar{q})^{2}-(\bar{\omega}^{2}+2\lambda\bar{\omega}\bar{k}-\bar{q}^{2})^{2}}}

(104)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k_{\rm B}T}\bar{\cal I}_{\lambda}(\bar{k},\bar{\omega},\bar{q}),

and same for
{\cal I}^{\prime}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(k^{\prime},\omega,q)=(k_{\rm B}T)^{-1}\bar{\cal I}^{\prime}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bar{k}^{\prime},\bar{\omega},\bar{q})
.

The matrix element (
B.3
) scales as

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime},\omega,q)=(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}\bar{\cal M}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(\bar{k},\bar{k}^{\prime},\bar{\omega},\bar{q}).

(105)

{\cal D}
from (
15
) is a function of
\mu
and scales the following way

\displaystyle{\cal D(\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k_{\rm B}T)^{3}\cal\bar{D}(\bar{\mu}).

(106)

The electron-electron potential
V_{\rm ee}
contains the dielectric function
\epsilon({\bm{q}},\omega)=1-v_{\bm{q}}\chi_{nn}({\bm{q}},\omega)
.
The density-density response can be estimated as

\displaystyle\Re e\chi^{(0)}_{nn}({\bm{q}},\omega)=-\frac{q^{2}}{16}\frac{\theta(v_{\rm F}^{2}q^{2}-\omega^{2})}{\sqrt{v_{\rm F}^{2}q^{2}-\omega^{2}}}=-k_{\rm B}T\frac{\bar{q}^{2}}{16}\frac{\theta(\bar{q}^{2}-\bar{\omega}^{2})}{\sqrt{\bar{q}^{2}-\bar{\omega}^{2}}}=k_{\rm B}T\Re e\bar{\chi}^{(0)}_{nn}({\bm{\bar{}}q},\bar{\omega}).

(107)

Therefore, the electron-electron potential scales as

\displaystyle|V_{\rm ee}(q,\omega)|^{2}=\frac{1}{(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}}|\bar{V}_{\rm ee}(\bar{q},\bar{\omega})|^{2},

(108)

where
{\bar{V}}_{\rm ee}(\bar{q},\bar{\omega})={\bar{v}}_{\bm{q}}/\epsilon({\bm{q}},\omega)
with
{\bar{v}}_{\bm{q}}=2\pi\alpha_{\rm ee}/\bar{q}
.
Here,
\alpha_{\rm ee}=e^{2}/(\hbar v_{\rm F}\kappa)
is the graphene’s fine structure constant.
The final expression for the viscosity transport time is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{\rm B}TC_{v},

(109)

where
C_{v}
is a dimensionless quantity defined as the integral

\displaystyle C_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi}{8{\cal\bar{D}}}\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\bar{\omega}\int d\bar{q}\int d\bar{k}\int d\bar{k}^{\prime}\frac{\bar{q}}{2\pi}\frac{\bar{k}}{2\pi}\frac{\bar{k}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{|V_{\rm ee}(\bar{q},\bar{\omega})|^{2}}{4\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{\bar{\omega}}{2}\right)}\bar{\cal I}_{\lambda}(\bar{k},\bar{\omega},\bar{q})\bar{\cal I}^{\prime}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bar{k}^{\prime},\bar{\omega},\bar{q})

(110)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle[f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{\bar{}}k},\lambda})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{\bar{}}k},\lambda}+\bar{\omega})][f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{\bar{}}k}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{{\bm{\bar{}}k}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}-\bar{\omega})]\bar{\cal M}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(\bar{k},\bar{k}^{\prime},\bar{\omega},\bar{q}).

Appendix C
Viscosity in a parabolic-band 2DEG

The single-particle Hamiltonian introduced in Sec. IV.B is

H=\frac{k^{2}}{2m}.

(111)

The corresponding eigenstates and eigenvalues are

\displaystyle\ket{\bm{k}}=e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}},\qquad\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}=\frac{k^{2}}{2m}.

(112)

Starting from the general kinetic framework of Sec.
II
, we specialize Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) to a single parabolic band.
Because the form factors are unity for a 2DEG, the scattering kernel entering Eq. (
2
) reduces to

\displaystyle W({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\left|V_{\rm ee}\big({\bm{q}},\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\big)\right|^{2}.

(113)

The collision integral therefore becomes

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\rm ee}[f_{\bm{k}}({\bm{r}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bm{q}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}W({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{q}})\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}})

(114)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big[f_{\bm{k}}f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}(1-f_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}})(1-f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}})-(1-f_{\bm{k}})(1-f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})f_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}}\Big].

As in the main text, we write

\displaystyle f_{\bm{k}}({\bm{r}})=f^{(0)}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{r}})+\delta f_{\bm{k}}({\bm{r}}).

(115)

Linearizing the left-hand side of the Boltzmann equation in the velocity gradients gives

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{\bm{k}}^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}f^{(0)}_{\bm{k}}=\frac{k^{\alpha}k^{\beta}}{2m}\left(-\frac{\partial f^{(0)}_{\bm{k}}}{\partial\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}}\right)(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+\partial_{\beta}u_{\alpha}).

(116)

For incompressible flow,
\partial_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}=0
, this becomes

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\partial f^{(0)}_{\bm{k}}}{\partial\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}}\right)A_{\bm{k}}^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha\beta},

(117)

where

\displaystyle A_{\bm{k}}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}}{2m}\left(\sigma_{z}^{\alpha\beta}\cos 2\phi_{\bm{k}}+\sigma_{x}^{\alpha\beta}\sin 2\phi_{\bm{k}}\right),

(118)

\displaystyle u_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+\partial_{\beta}u_{\alpha}-\delta_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\rho}u_{\rho}\right).

(119)

We use the same single-mode ansatz as in the main text,

\displaystyle\delta f_{\bm{k}}=\tau_{\rm p}\left(-\frac{\partial f^{(0)}_{\bm{k}}}{\partial\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}}\right)A_{\bm{k}}^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha\beta}.

(120)

Projecting the linearized kinetic equation onto the shear tensor structure is cleaner if the left- and right-hand sides are treated separately.
We therefore define

\displaystyle I_{\rm LHS}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(-\frac{\partial f^{(0)}_{\bm{k}}}{\partial\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}}\right)A_{\bm{k}}^{\mu\nu}A_{\bm{k}}^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha\beta},

(121)

\displaystyle I_{\rm RHS}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\tau_{\rm p}}{4k_{\rm B}T}\sum_{{\bm{q}},{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\left|V_{\rm ee}\big({\bm{q}},\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}\big)\right|^{2}\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}})

(122)

\displaystyle\times f_{\bm{k}}f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}(1-f_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}})(1-f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}})\Big[A_{\bm{k}}^{\alpha\beta}+A_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{\alpha\beta}-A_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}^{\alpha\beta}-A_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}}^{\alpha\beta}\Big]

\displaystyle\times\Big[A_{\bm{k}}^{\mu\nu}+A_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{\mu\nu}-A_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}^{\mu\nu}-A_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}}^{\mu\nu}\Big]u_{\alpha\beta}.

The angular average on the left-hand side satisfies

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\phi_{\bm{k}}}{2\pi}A_{\bm{k}}^{\mu\nu}A_{\bm{k}}^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2m}\right)^{2}u_{\mu\nu}.

(123)

Using Eq. (
123
), Eq. (
121
) becomes

\displaystyle I_{\rm LHS}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{\rm p}u_{\mu\nu},

(124)

where

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{\rm p}(\mu,T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\phi\,dk}{(2\pi)^{2}}k\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2m}\right)^{2}\left(-\frac{\partial f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}-\mu)}{\partial\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}}\right)

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{Nm(k_{\rm B}T)^{2}}{4\pi}{\rm Li}_{2}\!\left(-e^{\mu/k_{\rm B}T}\right).

After symmetrizing Eq. (
122
) in the standard way, the right-hand side can be written as

\displaystyle I_{\rm RHS}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\tau_{\rm p}}{8m^{2}k_{\rm B}T}\sum_{{\bm{q}},{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\left|V_{\rm ee}\big({\bm{q}},\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}\big)\right|^{2}\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}})

(126)

\displaystyle\times f_{\bm{k}}f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}(1-f_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}})(1-f_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}}){\cal M}_{\rm p}({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{q}})u_{\mu\nu},

where

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\rm p}({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[k^{2}\cos(2\phi_{\bm{k}})+k^{\prime 2}\cos(2\phi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})-({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}})^{2}\cos(2\phi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{q}}})-({\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}})^{2}\cos(2\phi_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}})\Big]^{2}

(127)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16q^{2}\cos^{2}\phi_{\bm{q}}\Big[q\cos\phi_{\bm{q}}+k\cos\phi_{\bm{k}}-k^{\prime}\cos\phi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\Big]^{2}.

To evaluate the angular delta-functions, we define

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\rm p}(k,q,\omega)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\phi_{\bm{k}}}{2\pi}\delta(\omega+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}).

(128)

The argument of the delta-function vanishes when

\displaystyle\omega-\frac{q^{2}}{2m}-\frac{kq}{m}\cos(\phi_{\bm{k}}-\phi_{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(129)

After shifting
\phi_{\bm{k}}\to\phi_{\bm{k}}-\phi_{\bm{q}}
, Eq. (

2DEG in the doped regime

In the doped regime, Eq. (

\displaystyle|\bm{k}|=|\bm{k}^{\prime}|=|\bm{k}+\bm{q}|=|\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{q}|=k_{F}.

The differences of Fermi functions become

\displaystyle f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}})-f^{(0)}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+\omega)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\omega\frac{\partial f^{(0)}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}{\partial\varepsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}=-\frac{m\omega}{k_{F}}\delta(k^{\prime}-k_{F}).

\displaystyle\phi_{\bm{k}}=\pi+\phi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}},\qquad\phi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}=\pi+\phi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{q}},
