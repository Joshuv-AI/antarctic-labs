Strongly continuous and locally equicontinuous families of operators and their relation to bi-continuity
Karsten Kruse, Christian Seifert

Abstract
. We study strongly continuous and locally equicontinuous families of operators on sequentially complete Hausdorff locally convex spaces. In case of Saks spaces, we relate the general notions to bi-continuity as well as equitightness. In this way, we recover and also generalise known results for special classes of operator families such as bi-continuous ( C C -)semigroups and ( C C -)cosine families by well-known results for the corresponding families in Hausdorff locally convex spaces.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to investigate strongly continuous and (locally) equicontinuous families of operators on Hausdorff locally convex spaces and to relate many different variants—particular classes of operators on particular spaces—present in the literature to this situation. In this way, on the one hand, we analyse and structure the various notions available. On the other hand, we are able to recover existing, as well as to obtain new, results in special situations by means of the corresponding results for operator families in Hausdorff locally convex spaces.

Strongly continuous and (locally) equicontinuous families of operators on Hausdorff locally convex spaces are classical objects in functional analysis and operator theory. Such families appear, for example, as fundamental solutions for abstract Cauchy problems (ACPs). Let us mention Stone’s theorem for strongly continuous groups of unitary operators on Hilbert spaces
[
112
,
116
]
and the celebrated Hille–Yosida theorem for strongly continuous semigroups on Banach spaces
[
47
,
125
]
, for first order ACPs. Furthermore, we also mention the corresponding generation theorems for strongly continuous cosine families in Banach spaces
[
110
,
55
]
for second order ACPs, as well as the analogs for higher order ACPs in Banach spaces
[
95
]
. Standard monographs for semigroups and/or cosine families, also including applications, are e.g.
[
46
,
99
,
44
,
32
]
.
Since the late 1950s, various generalisations have appeared in the literature. The theory for special classes of operator families on Banach or Hilbert spaces has been generalised to operator families in Hausdorff locally convex spaces, see
[
103
,
56
,
57
,
126
]
for strongly continuous and (locally) equicontinuous semigroups on sequentially complete, even locally complete
[
113
]
, Hausdorff locally convex spaces,
[
38
,
39
,
58
]
for strongly continuous and (locally) equicontinuous cosine families on sequentially complete Hausdorff locally convex spaces. Another generalisation arises from so-called
C
-semigroups and
C
-cosine families, both on Banach spaces
[
25
,
77
,
78
]
as well as on sequentially complete Hausdorff locally convex spaces
[
124
]
. Further, the adjoints of semigroups
[
115
]
and cosine families
[
108
,
12
,
13
]
on Banach spaces have also been studied.
Moreover, in the 1980s, integrated semigroups and integrated cosine families on Banach spaces have been introduced and studied
[
8
,
96
,
54
]
(to name a few). Starting from the 2000s, two further variants have been studied, namely bi-continuous semigroups and cosine families, where the operator families defined on Banach spaces are only strongly continuous with respect to a weaker Hausdorff locally convex topology, see
[
74
,
75
,
36
,
5
,
37
,
15
]
for semigroups and
[
30
,
19
,
17
,
18
]
for cosine families. Moreover, so-called integrable semigroups on norming dual pairs have been introduced by Kunze
[
76
]
. Recently, also locally equicontinuous semigroup on Hausdorff locally convex spaces which can also be equipped with an auxiliary norm (so the concept is closely related to Saks spaces and bi-continuous semigroups)
[
63
]
as well as sequentially locally equicontinuous semigroups
[
40
]
have been considered.

From the point of view of the underlying spaces, the most general situation treated is the (sequentially complete) Hausdorff locally convex case. This gives rise to the question of whether the above-mentioned variants are special situations of this general setup.
Our aim is to actually show that this is indeed the case. More precisely, we will relate bi-continuous and locally (exponentially) bounded operator families in a one-to-one fashion with strongly continuous and locally (exponentially) equicontinuous families with respect to the mixed topology, as long as the mixed topology is C-sequential. Note that the assumption that a space is
C-sequential
(i.e. all convex sequentially open subsets are open) is very mild and is fulfilled in all relevant applications treated in the literature. In this way, we can recover as well as generalise (large parts of) the theory of bi-continuous operator families, say, by means of the well-established theory of operator families on locally convex spaces.
Further, it refutes
[
75
, Remark 5 (b)]
(cf.
[
74
, p. 1 & 54]
)
where it is said that it seems that the theory of locally equicontinuous semigroups on sequentially complete Hausdorff
locally convex spaces has only few applications in contrast to the theory of locally bi-continuous semigroups.

We first review equicontinuity and sequential equicontinuity of operator families in Section
2
. Indeed, on C-sequential spaces these two notions are equivalent. We then turn to special classes of operator families such as
C
-semigroups or
C
-cosine families as well as strongly continuous and (locally) equicontinuous families of operators in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we focus on bi-continuous operator families, in particular semigroups and cosine families. We then have all the necessary objects and tools available to relate the various notions with each other in Section
5

The final Section
6
is devoted to applications where we show that we can recover existing results for, say, bi-continuous operator semigroups by means of their counterparts in Hausdorff locally convex spaces in a standard manner, as well as obtain new results by the same strategy. Here, we focus on (already existing) generation theorems, (existing as well as new) approximation theorems, and (new) representation formulas.

2.
Sequential equicontinuity

For a Hausdorff locally convex space
(X,\tau)
we denote by
\mathcal{P}
a system of
seminorms generating the topology
\tau
. W.l.o.g.
\mathcal{P}
is directed. If no confusion about the topology
\tau
seems
to be likely, we just write
X
instead of
(X,\tau)
.
On the other hand, if we want to emphasise the dependency of
\mathcal{P}
on
X
or
\tau
, we write
\mathcal{P}_{X}
or
\mathcal{P}_{\tau}

instead of just
\mathcal{P}
, respectively.
We denote by
\mathcal{L}(X,\tau)
the space of continuous linear maps from
(X,\tau)
to
(X,\tau)
.
If
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)
is a normed space, then
\tau_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
denotes the topology induced by

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
and we write
\mathcal{L}(X)\coloneq\mathcal{L}(X,\tau_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert})
which we equip with operator norm

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X)}
. Further, we denote by
(X,\tau)^{\prime}
the topological dual space of
(X,\tau)
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
40
, pp. 1135, 1166]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hausdorff locally convex spaces and
\mathcal{S}
be a set of linear maps
from
X
to
Y
. Then
\mathcal{S}
is called

(a)

equicontinuous
if for all
p\in\mathcal{P}_{Y}
there exist
q\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
and
K\geq 0
such that

\sup_{T\in\mathcal{S}}p(Tx)\leq Kq(x)
for all
x\in X
.

(b)

sequentially equicontinuous
if for all
p\in\mathcal{P}_{Y}
and all sequences
(x_{n})
in
X
such that
x_{n}\to 0

we have that
\sup_{T\in\mathcal{S}}p(Tx_{n})\to 0
.

We note that the definition of (sequential) equicontinuity
does not depend on the choice of the systems
of seminorms
\mathcal{P}_{X}
and
\mathcal{P}_{Y}
that generate the Hausdorff locally convex topologies on
X
and
Y
, respectively.
Clearly, if
\mathcal{S}
is equicontinuous, then it is also sequentially equicontinuous.
We note the following characterisation of sequential equicontinuity whose proof we include for the sake of completeness.
We recall that a subset
U
of a topological space
X
is called
sequentially open

if every sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
converging to a point
x\in U
is eventually in
U
, see
[
42
, p. 108]
.
In particular, open sets are sequentially open but the converse might not hold.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hausdorff locally convex spaces and
\mathcal{S}
a set of linear maps from
X
to
Y
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

\mathcal{S}
is sequentially equicontinuous.

(b)

For all absolutely convex open neighbourhoods
V
of zero in
Y

there is an absolutely convex sequentially open neighbourhood
U
of zero in
X
such that
T(U)\subseteq V

for all
T\in\mathcal{S}
.

(c)

For all
p\in\mathcal{P}_{Y}
there are a sequentially continuous seminorm
q
on
X
and
K\geq 0
such that
\sup_{T\in\mathcal{S}}p(T(x))\leq Kq(x)
for all
x\in X
.

Proof.

“
(a)
\Rightarrow
(b)
” Let
V
be an absolutely convex open neighbourhood
of zero in
Y
. Then the Minkowski functional
p_{V}\colon Y\to\mathds{R}
,
p_{V}(y)\coloneq\inf\{\lambda>0\;|\;y\in\lambda V\}
,
is continuous and
V=\{y\in Y\;|\;p_{V}(y)<1\}
by
[
92
, Lemma 22.3]
. It follows that

q(x)\coloneq\sup_{T\in\mathcal{S}}p_{V}(Tx)
,
x\in X
, defines a seminorm on
X
which is sequentially continuous since

\mathcal{S}
is sequentially equicontinuous by
(a)
. The set
U\coloneq\{x\in X\;|\;q(x)<1\}
is an absolutely convex sequentially open neighbourhood of zero in
X
because
q
is a sequentially continuous seminorm.
Furthermore, for
T\in\mathcal{S}
and
x\in U
, we have
p_{V}(Tx)\leq q(x)<1
and so
T(x)\in V
.

“
(b)
\Rightarrow
(c)
” Let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{Y}

and define the set
V\coloneq\{y\in Y\;|\;p(y)<1\}
, which is an absolutely convex open neighbourhood
of zero in
Y
. Then there is an absolutely convex sequentially open neighbourhood
U
of zero in
X
such that
T(U)\subseteq V

for all
T\in\mathcal{S}
by
(b)
. We define the Minkowski functional
q_{U}\colon X\to\mathds{R}

by
q_{U}(x)\coloneq\inf\{\lambda>0\;|\;x\in\lambda U\}
for
x\in X
. By the proof of

[
109
, Theorem 1, (3)
\Rightarrow
(4)]
,
q_{U}
is a sequentially continuous seminorm on
X
.
Let
x\in X
and
T\in\mathcal{S}
. Let
\lambda>0
such that
x\in\lambda U
. Then we have

Tx\in\lambda T(U)\subseteq\lambda V

and thus
p(Tx)<\lambda
, which implies
p(Tx)\leq q_{U}(x)
by the definition of
q_{U}
.

“
(c)
\Rightarrow
(a)
” Let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{Y}
and
(x_{n})
in
X

such that
x_{n}\to 0
. By
(c)
, there are a sequentially continuous seminorm
q
on
X
and
K\geq 0
such that
\sup_{T\in\mathcal{S}}p(Tx)\leq Kq(x)
for all
x\in X
. Since
q
is sequentially continuous,
we obtain that
q(x_{n})\to 0
, implying
\sup_{T\in\mathcal{S}}p(Tx_{n})\to 0
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{S}
is sequentially equicontinuous.
∎

The equivalence “
(a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b)
” of
Proposition
2.2
is mentioned in
[
48
, p. 419]
without a proof.
Next, we show that sequential equicontinuity and equicontinuity are equivalent if
X
is C-sequential.
Recall that a Hausdorff locally convex space
X
is called
C-sequential
if every convex
sequentially open subset of
X
is already open, see
[
109
, p. 273]
.
For instance, every bornological Hausdorff locally convex space is C-sequential by
[
109
, Theorem 8]
.
However, the spaces we are mainly interested in, namely Saks spaces
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert,\tau)
equipped with their mixed topology

\gamma
, see Definition
4.2
and Definition
4.6
, are not bornological
by
[
22
, I.1.15 Proposition]
unless the mixed topology already coincides with the norm-topology.
Nevertheless, many Saks spaces are still C-sequential (w.r.t. the mixed topology),
see
[
66
, Remarks 3.19, 3.20]
and
[
72
, 3.5 Corollary & Section 4]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hausdorff locally convex spaces,
X
C-sequential and
\mathcal{S}
a set of linear maps from
X
to
Y
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

\mathcal{S}
is sequentially equicontinuous.

(b)

\mathcal{S}
is equicontinuous.

Proof.

We only need to prove the implication “
(a)
\Rightarrow
(b)
”.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be sequentially equicontinuous and
p\in\mathcal{P}_{Y}
. Due to Proposition
2.2

there are a sequentially continuous seminorm
q
on
X
and
K\geq 0
such that
\sup_{T\in\mathcal{S}}p(T(x))\leq Kq(x)

for all
x\in X
. Since
X
is C-sequential,
q
is continuous by
[
109
, Theorem 1]
.
Hence,
\mathcal{S}
is equicontinuous.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let

(sequentially)

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff locally convex space,
C\colon X\to X
linear and
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
a
C
-cosine family on
X
.

(a)

If
x\in X
is such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}S(t)x=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}S(t)Cx=0

and
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is sequentially equicontinuous, then
C^{2}x=0
where
C^{2}x\coloneq CCx
.

(b)

If there is
k\colon[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}k(t)=\infty
,

\inf_{t\geq 0}k(t)>0
and
(k(t)S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is sequentially equicontinuous, then
C^{2}x=0
for all
x\in X
.

(c)

If
X\neq\{0\}
,
C^{2}
is injective and there is
\alpha\in\mathds{R}
such
(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha t}S(t))_{t\geq 0}

is sequentially equicontinuous, then
\alpha\geq 0
.

Proof.

(a)
We have
2S(t)^{2}x=S(2t)Cx+C^{2}x
for all
t\geq 0
where
S(t)^{2}x\coloneq S(t)S(t)x
.
Let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
. Since
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is sequentially equicontinuous, there are a sequentially continuous seminorm
q
on
X
and

K\geq 0
such that
p(S(t)^{2}x)\leq Kq(S(t)x)
for all
t\geq 0
by Proposition
2.2
,
which implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}S(t)^{2}x=0
as
\lim_{t\to\infty}S(t)x=0
.
Hence, we get

C^{2}x=\lim_{t\to\infty}S(2t)Cx+C^{2}x=2\lim_{t\to\infty}S(t)^{2}x=0

since
\lim_{t\to\infty}S(t)Cx=0
.

(b)
Since
\lim_{t\to\infty}k(t)=\infty
, there is
t_{0}\geq 0
such that
k(t)\geq 1
for all
t\geq t_{0}
.
Further, we note that
K_{1}\coloneq\inf_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}k(t)\geq\inf_{t\geq 0}k(t)>0
. Let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
. Then we have

p(S(t)x)=\frac{1}{k(t)}p(k(t)S(t)x)\leq\frac{1}{K_{1}}p(k(t)S(t)x)\quad(t\in[0,t_{0}],\,x\in X)

and
p(S(t)x)\leq p(k(t)S(t)x)
for all
t\geq t_{0}
and
x\in X
. Thus, the sequential equicontinuity of
(k(t)S(t))_{t\geq 0}
implies
that
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is sequentially equicontinuous. Moreover, by Proposition
2.2
and the sequential equicontinuity
of
(k(t)S(t))_{t\geq 0}
, there are also a sequentially continuous seminorm
q
on
X
and
K\geq 0
such that

p(S(t)x)\leq\frac{K}{k(t)}q(x)\quad(t\geq 0,\,x\in X),

yielding that
\lim_{t\to\infty}S(t)x=0
for all
x\in X
because
\lim_{t\to\infty}k(t)=\infty
.
In particular,
\lim_{t\to\infty}S(t)Cx=0
for all
x\in X
as well. We conclude that
C^{2}x=0
for all
x\in X
by part
(a)
.

(c)
Suppose there is
\alpha\in\mathds{R}
with
\alpha<0
such that
(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha t}S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is
sequentially equicontinuous. Then it follows from part
(b)
applied to
k(t)\coloneq\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha t}
for
t\geq 0

that
C^{2}x=0
for all
x\in X
. Since
C^{2}
is injective, we obtain that
X=\{0\}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff locally convex space and
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
a semigroup or cosine family on
X
.
If
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a cosine family, then we assume in addition that

Proof.

We only need to prove the implication “
\Leftarrow

(S(t))_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}
is
(sequentially) equicontinuous.
We claim that

, which we prove by induction.
For

this is clear.

(i) Let
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a semigroup. Assume that
n\in\mathds{N}
is such that
(S(t))_{t\in[0,nt_{0}]}
is
(sequentially) equicontinuous.
Let

. By the (sequential) equicontinuity of

(S(t))_{t\in[0,nt_{0}]}
and Proposition
2.2
,
there are a (sequentially) continuous seminorm
q
on
X

and
K\geq 0
such that for all
t\in((n-1)t_{0},nt_{0}]
and
x\in X
it holds that

p(S(t+t_{0})x)=p(S(t)S(t_{0})x)\leq Kq(S(t_{0})x),

which proves our claim by the (sequential) continuity of
S(t_{0})
.

(ii) Let
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a cosine family. Let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
.
First, we show that
(S(t))_{t\in[0,2t_{0}]}
is (sequentially) equicontinuous.
By the (sequential) equicontinuity of
(S(t))_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}
and Proposition
2.2
,
there are a (sequentially) continuous seminorm
q
on
X

and
K\geq 0
such that for all
t\in(0,t_{0}]
and
x\in X

p(S(t+t_{0})x)=p(2S(t_{0})S(t)x-S(t_{0}-t)x)=p(2S(t)S(t_{0})x-S(t_{0}-t)x)\leq 2Kq(S(t_{0})x)+Kq(x)

.
Assume that

,
n\geq 2

(S(t))_{t\in[0,nt_{0}]}

The proof is now quite similar to case (i).
Again, by the (sequential) equicontinuity of
(S(t))_{t\in[0,nt_{0}]}

and Proposition
2.2
, there are a (sequentially) continuous seminorm
q
on
X

and
K\geq 0
such that for all
t\in((n-1)t_{0},nt_{0}]
and
x\in X
it holds that

p(S(t+t_{0})x)=p(2S(t)S(t_{0})-S(t-t_{0})x)\leq 2Kq(S(t_{0})x)+Kq(x),

∎
