Enhanced postharvest protection of cherry tomatoes by a fully bio-based quasi-mixed matrix membrane (quasi-MMM) with dual-mechanism integration of organic filler
Authors: Yihui Zhao, Shiyang Xu, Chunlong Liu, Wenxia Wang, Hao Dong, Wenzhen Liao, Zhini He, Xingfen Yang, Qi He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractConventional mixed matrix membranes (MMMs) are organic-inorganic hybrids. Following analogous structural logic, we develop a fully bio-based quasi-mixed matrix membrane (Quasi-MMM) using ferulic acid (FA) as a dual-functional organic filler in a chitosan-caseinate matrix. FA establishes covalent ester/amide linkages and an extensive hydrogen-bonding network with the biopolymer continuous phase, emulating the matrix-filler synergy of classical MMMs while imparting sustained antioxidant activity. The resulting dense membrane exhibits robust mechanical strength (∼ 55 MPa), tunable barrier properties, enhanced hydrophilicity and anomalously slow FA release (∼50% over 25 d) governed by non-Fickian transport. When coated on cherry tomatoes and stored at 4 °C for 25 d, the Quasi-MMM reduces malondialdehyde accumulation by >35%, suppresses microbial growth by >2 log CFU·g−1, and maintains firmness, ascorbic acid, and total phenolics near initial levels. This Quasi-MMM concept offers a viable green strategy for postharvest preservation by integrating structural reinforcement and continuous bioactive protection within a single, entirely organic platform.Keywords: Quasi-mixed matrix membrane, Multifunctional crosslinking filler, Composite matrix, Synergistic shelf-life extension, Postharvest preservation

Introduction

Fresh horticultural produce rapidly deteriorates after harvest due to physiological senescence and microbial spoilage (Li, Dong, Yang & He, 2022). Next-generation preservation technologies must therefore integrate barrier functions, microenvironment regulation, and bioactive protection into a single, sustainable platform (Khalil et al., 2024). Mixed matrix membranes (MMMs) are composites with a polymer continuous phase and dispersed functional fillers, offering a versatile design framework (Du et al., 2023; Yan et al., 2024). However, conventional MMMs rely on petroleum-derived polymers or inorganic nanofillers (e.g., zeolites, MOFs, silica), raising concerns over environmental persistence, toxicity, and end-of-life disposal (Altunay et al., 2022; Das et al., 2025). A compelling need exists for fully bio-based alternatives where every component originates from renewable, benign sources without sacrificing performance (Huang et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2025).

Chitosan (CH), a cationic polysaccharide, is an attractive biopolymer for edible coatings due to its film-forming ability, biodegradability, and intrinsic antimicrobial activity (Hui et al., 2023). Yet, its standalone use is hindered by mechanical brittleness, high hydrophilicity, and weak antioxidant capacity. Blending with sodium caseinate (SC), improves mechanical integrity through electrostatic interactions (Di Giuseppe et al., 2022), but CH-SC composites still lack long-term oxidative and microbial stability (Bhatia et al., 2023). Common modification strategies each carry trade-offs: plasticizers compromise barrier properties (Scott et al., 2024); blending with synthetic polymers introduces interfacial defects (Cheedarala & Song, 2021); and crosslinking with agents like glutaraldehyde raises cytotoxicity concerns (Doustdar et al., 2022). A unified, green approach that simultaneously reinforces the biopolymer network and provides with sustained bioactivity remains elusive.

Ferulic acid (FA), a plant-derived phenolic, holds unique promise. Beyond its well-documented radical-scavenging and antimicrobial activities (Kumar et al., 2024), FA possesses a carboxylic acid, a phenolic hydroxyl, and an unsaturated side chain, enabling covalent ester/amide bond formation and extensive hydrogen bonding with biopolymers (Mahmoud et al., 2024; Panda et al., 2021). Several studies have incorporated FA into CH or SC films as a simple additive. However, to the best of our knowledge, none have exploited FA as a dual-purpose agent that (i) covalently crosslinks a protein-polysaccharide matrix and (ii) serves as a sustained-release reservoir of antioxidant/antimicrobial activity within a ternary system. Most reported FA-containing coatings rely on physical blending, leading to rapid FA leaching and short-lived protection (Jahani et al., 2022; Roshandel-hesari et al., 2022).

Classically, MMMs are defined as organic-inorganic hybrids. In this work, we deliberately avoid any inorganic component. Instead, we design a fully organic ternary system where FA acts as the dispersed “filler” within a continuous CH-SC matrix, following the same matrix-filler architectural logic as classical MMMs. To accurately describe this conceptual extension while maintaining terminological precision, we introduce the term “quasi-mixed matrix membrane (Quasi-MMM)”. This designation (i) acknowledges the structural analogy to conventional MMMs, (ii) clearly distinguishes our all-organic, bio-based system from inorganic-containing counterparts, and (iii) emphasizes that the material is more than a simple physical blend—FA is covalently and non-covalently anchored to the matrix. The Quasi-MMM concept thus offers two decisive advances over previously reported CH/SC/phenolic-acid films: (i) a covalently reinforced network that enhances mechanical strength and barrier integrity without synthetic crosslinkers; and (ii) a built-in, slow-release FA reservoir that confers prolonged antioxidant and antimicrobial activity (Liu et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2023).

The central hypothesis of this study is that FA can function simultaneously as a green crosslinker and a sustained-release bioactive filler in the CH-SC matrix. We test this hypothesis through four complementary lines of evidence: (i) chemical characterization (FTIR) to confirm covalent and hydrogen-bonding interactions; (ii) mechanical, barrier, and hydrophilicity tests to quantify structural reinforcement; (iii) in vitro radical-scavenging (DPPH/ABTS) and antimicrobial zone assays to measure intrinsic bioactivity; and (iv) 25-day preservation trials on cherry tomatoes at 4 °C to evaluate real-world efficacy. The sustained-release mechanism—i.e., the kinetic profile of FA release and its direct correlation with prolonged protection—is proposed based on the observed long-lasting bioactivity (e.g. >2 log CFU·g−1 microbial suppression over 25 days). Detailed release kinetics were further quantified, proposing mechanisms follows rigorous scientific practice.

In summary, this work moves beyond simple blending to establish a material-design paradigm centered on multifunctional molecular integration. We provide comprehensive evidence of the formation mechanism, physicochemical properties, and—most critically—the preservation efficacy of this Quasi-MMM coating on a highly perishable model crop (cherry tomato). Our findings open a promising route toward high-performance, entirely bio-based preservation technologies that combat both physiological senescence and microbial spoilage in a single material intervention.

Materials and methods

Materials

More than 250 fresh cherry tomatoes (Variety: millennium; average growth period: ∼ 80 days; mean fruit weight: 12.6 g ± 0.8 g; free of mechanical damage or surface defects), were harvested in October 2023 at the light-red ripening stage (USDA color class 4–5) from a farm in Lingshui County, Hainan Province, China (∼18° N, ∼110° E; altitude: ∼300 m; annual average temperature: ∼25.2 °C; annual average rainfall: ∼ 1800 mm). The fruit were transported to the laboratory within 24 h via cold-chain logistics. The initial fruits exhibited the following maturity indices: total soluble solids (TSS) = 4.4 ± 0.2°Brix, titratable acidity (TA) = 186 ± 9 g citric acid·kg−1, and firmness = 4.5 ± 0.1 N. These baseline values were served as quality references throughout the storage trial.

CH (Medium molecular weight; degree of deacetylation ≥95%; viscosity 100–200 mpa·s), SC and FA were purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., China. Acetic acid, anhydrous ethanol and methanol were obtained from Guangzhou Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd., China. The rest of used reagents were obtained from Tianjin Damao Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., China.

Rational design and structural synthesis of quasi-MMMs

The fabrication of the Quasi-MMM aims to optimize the individual contributions of structural integrity and bioactivity, based on the ternary compositions in Table 1 and grounded in two fundamental hypotheses: (i) the CH/SC mass ratio governs the density of the electrostatic polyelectrolyte network and thus the mechanical baseline of the continuous phase; and (ii) the FA mass fraction, as the organic filler, dictates the extent of covalent/non-covalent crosslinking and the resultant biofunctional reservoir capacity. To probe these hypotheses, the CH:SC ratio was varied from 9:1 to 1:1 (Groups B ∼ F) relative to a pure CH control (Group A), encompassing the full spectrum from a polysaccharide-dominated to a charge-balanced matrix. At each matrix ratio, FA was introduced at three escalating levels (10, 20, and 30 mg per gram of total biopolymer), representing a gradient from sparse to dense molecular crosslinking and bioactive site densities. The matrix enables a rigorous deconvolution of the synergistic effects between the two biopolymers, from which selected formulations for the preservation study were rationally selected based on maximal antioxidant/antimicrobial activity without compromising mechanical integrity (3.2, 3.3). Dual-Matrix Molecular Co-Assembly: CH (2.0% w/v) was dissolved in aqueous acetic acid (1.0% v/v) under continuous stirring (500 rpm) at 60 °C for 4 h to ensure complete protonation and chain disentanglement. Concurrently, SC (2.0% w/v) was dissolved in ultrapure water at 60 °C for 2 h. The two solutions were then mixed in predetermined mass ratios (ranging from pure CH to 1:1 CH:SC) under moderate shear (800 rpm) for 1 h at 60 °C to facilitate the formation of a coacervate-like CH-SC polyelectrolyte complex, which serves as the foundational continuous matrix.

Thermo-Assisted Integration and Coupling of the Organic Filler: FA was introduced at precise mass loadings of 10, 20, or 30 mg/g total biopolymer. A predetermined amount of FA was first dissolved in a minimal volume of 70% (v/v) ethanol and added dropwise to the CH-SC blend under high-speed homogenization (10,000 rpm for 5 min) at 60 °C, promoting molecular dispersion and initiating interactions with functional groups. Although conventional amidation/esterification typically requires extrinsic coupling agents, efficient covalent coupling proceeds in this system through a synergistic mechanism of nanoconfinement, acid catalysis, and sonochemical activation. Specifically, the coacervated biopolymer matrix creates a confined nanoscale reaction volume that markedly elevates the effective molarity of reactive groups (-NH2, -OH, -COOH), thermodynamically driving the condensation equilibrium toward ester and amide bond formation. Concurrently, the mildly acidic medium (acetic acid, pH ≈ 4.5) together with the intrinsic acidity of FA (pKₐ ≈ 4.5) provides a homogeneous proton-catalyzed pathway that lowers the activation energy for nucleophilic attack. Following the addition of glycerol (0.5% v/v) as a plasticizer, the mixture was subjected to controlled ultrasonication (40 kHz, 300 W, 45 min) at 60 °C, not only eliminating microscopic air bubbles and ensures nanoscale homogeneity, but also producing transient local hot spots and reactive radicals that further overcome the kinetic barrier for covalent crosslinking. The acoustic parameters were optimized to supply sufficient activation energy while avoiding polymer chain scission or oxidative degradation of FA, which confirms that the phenolic functionality remains intact throughout the sonochemical treatment. The resulting solutions were cast onto meticulously leveled polypropylene plates using a precision film applicator (BEVS 1806B/200, BEVS Industrial Co., Ltd., China). The network maturation was achieved at 60 °C for 12 h.

Molecular Interfacial Interaction Elucidation: To provide a theoretical foundation for the observed synergistic effects and guide the interpretation of spectroscopic data, molecular docking simulations were performed. Using AutoDock 4.2.6 (Scripps Research, San Diego, US), the binding affinities and optimal interaction modes between FA and model compounds were calculated. The Lamarckian genetic algorithm was employed with a grid box encompassing all potential reactive sites. The resulting low-energy conformations, offering atomistic-level insight into the crosslinking mechanism.

Physicochemical and functional characterization of quasi-MMMs

Chemical Structure, Crystallinity and Molecular Interaction Analysis: The formation of covalent and non-covalent bonds within the ternary matrix was probed using Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR; Nicolet iS50, Thermo Fisher, Waltham, US). Spectra were acquired in attenuated total reflectance (ATR) mode across the range of 4000–400 cm−1. The crystalline structure of CH and the selected Quasi-MMM coatings was analyzed by X-ray diffraction (XRD; D8 Advance, Bruker, Ettlingen, Germany). Dried membrane specimens were mounted on a flat sample holder, and diffraction patterns were collected using Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å). The resulting profiles were used to assess changes in crystallinity and chain-packing order induced by SC and FA incorporation.

Optical Properties: Surface color coordinates (L*, a*, b*) were measured using a chroma meter (CR8, 3NH Technology, Shenzhen, China) and the total color difference (ΔE⁎ab) was calculated as: ΔEab∗=ΔL∗)2+Δa∗2+(Δb∗2. (Jahani et al., 2024). Membrane opacity, indicative of light barrier properties, was determined from the absorbance at 600 nm (A600) per unit thickness (mm−1) using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer (UV-1102, Shanghai Tianmei Co., Shanghai, China) (de Brito et al., 2023).

Surface and Mechanical Performance: Surface hydrophilicity was evaluated via static water contact angle measurements (Liu et al., 2024). A sessile drop of deionized water (2.0 μL) was deposited on the membrane surface, and the contact angle was automatically calculated using a goniometer (OCA15, DataPhysics Company, Stuttgart, Germany) (Kamali et al., 2022).

Antioxidant Activity of the Quasi-MMMs. The intrinsic antioxidant capacity of the MMMs, a direct consequence of the incorporated FA, was assessed via DPPH and ABTS+ radical scavenging assays (Zhang et al., 2025). To simulate active release, precisely weighed membrane samples (0.50 ± 0.01 g) were immersed in 50 mL distilled water under agitation for 12 h. The resulting leachate was reacted with standardized DPPH or ABTS+ solutions, and the decrease in absorbance at 517 nm or 734 nm, respectively, was measured. Scavenging activity was calculated and normalized to the sample mass to ensure comparability across formulations. Scavenging activity was calculated as: Scavenging activity (%) = [(A₀ − A₁) / A₀] × 100, where A₀ is the absorbance of the control (reagent + solvent) and A₁ is the absorbance of the sample.

Antimicrobial Activity of the Quasi-MMMs. The antimicrobial activity of the membranes was evaluated by the agar disc diffusion method against three representative foodborne bacteria: Staphylococcus aureus ATCC 25923 (Gram-positive), Escherichia coli ATCC 25922 (Gram-negative), and Pseudomonas aeruginosa ATCC 27853 (Gram-negative). Bacterial strains were cultured in Mueller-Hinton broth at 37 °C for 18 h and adjusted to a turbidity of 0.5 McFarland standard (∼1.5 × 108 CFU·mL−1). The bacterial suspension (100 μL) was spread uniformly onto Mueller-Hinton agar plates. Membrane discs (diameter 6 mm), pre-sterilized under UV light for 30 min on each side, were placed on the inoculated agar surface and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. The diameter of the clear inhibition zone surrounding each disc was measured with a digital caliper to the nearest 0.1 mm and recorded as an indicator of antimicrobial potency. Sterile distilled water was used as the negative control.

Gas Barrier Properties. The water vapor transmission rate (WVTR) was determined gravimetrically according to the ASTM E96–95 standard desiccant method. Membrane samples were sealed over test cups containing anhydrous calcium chloride (0% RH) and placed in a climate chamber at 25 °C and 75% RH. The weight gain of the cup was recorded every 2 h over a 24 h period, and WVTR (g·mm·m−2·day−1·kPa−1) was calculated as: WVTR = (G × d) / (t × A × ΔP), where G is the mass gain (g), d is membrane thickness (mm), t is time (day), A is the exposed area (m2), and ΔP is the water vapor partial pressure difference (kPa) across the membrane. Oxygen permeability (OP) and carbon dioxide permeability (CDP) were measured using a manometric gas permeability tester (Labthink VAC—V2, Labthink International, Inc., Jinan, China) at 25 °C and 0% RH. The permeating area was 38.5 cm2, and the upstream pressure was 1 atm. Permeability coefficients were expressed as cm3·mm·m−2·day−1·atm−1. Permeability coefficients were calculated as: P = (Q × d) / (A × t × Δp), where Q is the volume of permeated gas (cm3), d is membrane thickness (mm), A is permeation area (m2), t is time (day), and Δp is the transmembrane pressure difference (atm).

FA Release Kinetics: Precisely weighed membrane discs (2 g, corresponding to the B3, C3, and D3 formulations) were immersed in 20 mL of 10% (v/v) ethanol-water simulant (FDA-approved simulant for aqueous and acidic foods) and incubated at 4 °C under gentle orbital shaking (60 rpm) to mimic refrigerated storage. At predetermined intervals (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 days), 1.0 mL of the release medium was withdrawn and replaced with fresh simulant to maintain sink conditions. The concentration of released FA was quantified by UV–Vis spectrophotometry at 320 nm, the characteristic absorption maximum of FA, using an eight-point calibration curve (R2 ≥ 0.999) constructed with authentic FA standard. The cumulative release percentage was calculated as: Cumulative release (%) = (Cₜ × V₀ + Σ Cᵢ₋₁ × Vs) / Mtotal × 100, where Cₜ is the FA concentration at time t (mg·mL−1), V₀ is the initial volume of release medium (mL), Vₛ is the sampling volume (mL), and Mtotal is the total FA loading (mg). The experimental release profiles were fitted by zero-order, first-order, and Korsmeyer-Peppas model and described by the highest adjusted coefficient of determination (R2 adjusted).

Application in postharvest cherry tomatoes

Pure SC cannot form self-supporting membranes with adequate mechanical integrity, and FA alone cannot be cast into a membrane; therefore, SC-only and FA-only controls were not included. Instead, the contributions of each component were systematically deconvoluted via the compositional gradient design in Table 1. The preservation efficacy of the Quasi-MMM was rigorously evaluated using cherry tomatoes as a model for highly perishable produce. The study was designed to dissect the coating's multifunctional role in mitigating quality deterioration under controlled storage stress.

Preservation of Cherry Tomatoes: Uniform fruits free of visible defects were randomly allocated into five treatment groups (n > 50 per group): an uncoated control (C), a chitosan-only coating (CH), and three Quasi-MMM coatings corresponding to the selected formulations B3, C3, and D3 (denoted as T1, T2, T3). Coating was achieved by immersing fruits in the respective casting solutions (2% w/v total biopolymer) for 3 min, followed by air-drying at 25 °C for 30 min to form a uniform, adherent layer. The coating weight per fruit was quantified by weighing fruits before and after coating, yielding an average coating thickness of 10.8 ± 3.7 μm, ensuring consistent coverage across all treatments. The integrity and surface morphology of the coating in situ were verified by scanning electron microscopy (SEM; S-3000 N, Hitachi Co., Tokyo) on gold-sputtered coating carefully peeled from the fruit surface. All groups were stored in climate-controlled chambers at 4 ± 0.5 °C and 55 ± 5% RH for 25 d. Although humidity is below the optimal range recommended for cherry tomato storage (typically 85–95% RH), it represents a mild, discriminative moisture stress that amplifies the demand placed on the coating's water vapor barrier function. Independent fruits were randomly withdrawn from each treatment group at each sampling time point. No fruit was subjected to repeated measurements across storage days.

Antioxidant and Antimicrobial Efficacy: Antioxidant effects of the Quasi-MMMs on the preserved tomatoes was assessed by DPPH and ABTS assays. Cherry tomatoes (∼ 5 g) were extracted using 50 mL of 80% w/w ethanol, then centrifuged. The supernatant was reacted with DPPH solution and ABTS solution to obtain the absorbances (Wang et al., 2024). Concurrently, the total bacterial count (TBC), total anaerobic count (TAC) and total yeast and mold count (TYMC) were determined via standard plate enumeration to assess microbial suppression. Tomato extracts were inoculated onto the following media: Plate count agar for TBC, incubated at 37 °C for 24 h; Iron Agar containing 10 g/L NaCl for TAC, incubated at 37 °C for 24 h under anaerobic conditions (Anaeropack system); and Rose bengal chloramphenicol agar for yeast and mold enumeration, incubated at 28 °C for 72 h. Results were expressed as log CFU·g−1 (He et al., 2020). Decay incidence was evaluated visually on each sampling day, calculated as the percentage of decayed fruit relative to the total number of fruits in each treatment group at each time point. A fruit was scored as decayed when visible fungal mycelia, soft rot lesions, or surface sporulation were present on any portion of the epidermis.

Physical quality retention: Color parameters (L*, a*, b*) were measured using a Minolta colorimeter (CR8, 3NH Technology Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) to photograph representative fruits under a white background with uniform lighting (Jahani et al., 2024). Weight loss was calculated as: Weight loss (%) = [(W0 − Wt) / W0] × 100, where W0 is the initial fruit weight and Wt is the weight at each sampling time (Zhang et al., 2024). Firmness was determined on a texture analyzer (Universal TA, Tenbro Instrument Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) (Nkede et al., 2024) using 2 mm probe, a pre- and mid- measurement speed of 1 mm/s, a mid-measurement speed of 1 mm/s, post measurement speed of 5 mm/s, a displacement of 10 mm, and a triggering force of 5 g.

Chemical and Metabolic Analysis: Total soluble solids were measured using a handheld refractometer (TD-45, Zhejiang Top Cloud-Agri Technology Co., Hangzhou, China) after homogenizing fruit with an equal mass of distilled water, vortexing, and centrifuging at 5000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C (Rivelli et al., 2024). Titratable acidity was determined by titrating 5 g minced fruit which extracted in 50 mL water with stirring at 500 rpm for 2 min and centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C, against 0.1 M NaOH using phenolphthalein as an indicator (Promyou et al., 2023). Total phenolic compounds were quantified by the Folin-Ciocalteu method, with absorbance read at 765 nm (He & Xiao, 2018). Ascorbic acid content was determined by titration of a 10 mL filtrate, obtained from homogenization in 2% oxalic acid and centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C (Li et al., 2022). Malondialdehyde content was calculated using the molar extinction coefficient of the MDA-TBA adduct (ε = 1.56 × 105 M−1·cm−1 at 532 nm) and expressed as μmol·g−1 fresh weight, with correction for non-specific absorbance at 600 nm (Zhang et al., 2024).

Statistical analysis

All assays were performed in at least three independent replicates, and results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. For comparisons among membrane formulations at a single time point, data were analyzed by one-way ANOVA followed by Duncan's multiple range test. For the preservation trial, which involved both treatment and storage time as independent variables, a two-way ANOVA with treatment and storage day as fixed factors was performed, followed by Tukey's HSD post hoc test for multiple comparisons. Differences were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, USA).

Result and discussion

Molecular design and interfacial assembly of the quasi-MMM

Network Construction: CH's repeating glucose units bearing amino and hydroxyl groups confer rapid water uptake and gelation, but its loose, weakly cross-linked network limits mechanical stability (Zhao et al., 2023). SC and CH form a polyelectrolyte complex via hydrogen bonds (-OH···-NH2), ionic interactions (-COO−···-NH3+), and coordination with Na+ sites, producing a continuous matrix with enhanced structural integrity (Fig. 1). Incorporation of FA as a multifunctional filler introduces phenolic hydroxyls that act as antioxidant and antimicrobial sites within the matrix (Bhatia et al., 2023).

Chitosan (CH) and sodium caseinate (SC) form a continuous biopolymer matrix through electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonding, while ferulic acid (FA) functions as a dual-role organic filler by reinforcing the matrix through covalent/non-covalent interactions and introducing antioxidant and antimicrobial phenolic sites.

Fabrication-dependent physicochemical and biofunctional performance

Optical Modulation: High-transparency materials offer significant advantages in both food preservation and consumer experience. By allowing consumers to clearly view the appearance and natural color of the food, these materials not only enhance the perceived quality and freshness but also build consumer trust, ultimately improving the overall purchasing experience (Table 2). Cherry tomato is a sensory driven commodity, and its bright red or orange appearance is an important factor that attracts consumers (Kamali et al., 2022). However, excessive exposure to light can cause photooxidation, accelerating the degradation of carotenoids (such as lycopene) in the cherry tomatoes, resulting in dull color and decreased sensory quality. Accordingly, a moderately light-blocking membrane (opacity in the range of 2–4 Abs·mm−1) may be advantageous for postharvest cherry tomatoes, as it can reduce photooxidative degradation of light-sensitive pigments such as lycopene while still allowing visual assessment of fruit quality. In this study, the incorporation of SC and FA progressively reduced the optical transparency of the membranes. Specifically, opacity increased from ∼2.5 Abs·mm−1 for pure CH membranes to ∼3.6 Abs·mm−1 for the optimized ternary formulations (Fig. 2B). This enhanced light-barrier property is beneficial for mitigating photo-induced quality loss in light-sensitive produce.

Surface Wettability: Polysaccharide-based coatings are inherently hydrophilic because of abundant hydroxyl and amino groups (Zhao et al., 2023), which generally result in low water contact angles. However, excessive surface wettability can weaken coating stability, promote water uptake by the film, and accelerate moisture migration from high-water-content fruit such as cherry tomatoes. As shown in Fig. 2B, FA incorporation exerted only a limited effect on membrane wettability, likely because its hydrophilic hydroxyl and carboxyl groups are partially counterbalanced by the hydrophobic aromatic ring structure (Mahmoud et al., 2024; Panda et al., 2021). In contrast, increasing SC content induced a non-monotonic wettability response. At moderate levels, the carboxylate groups of SC could interact with amino groups in CH and expose additional polar sites at the membrane surface, thereby enhancing hydrophilicity. When SC exceeded an optimal range, self-assembly and surface enrichment of hydrophobic protein domains reduced surface wettability, consistent with the amphiphilic nature of SC containing both hydrophilic -COO− groups and hydrophobic hydrocarbon segments (Bhatia et al., 2023).

Mechanical Reinforcement: Pure CH membrane has been proven to have low mechanical strength and is prone to breakage and deformation, making it difficult to achieve good results in food preservation and transportation processes (Wang et al., 2021). Fig. 2B showed that the mechanical performance defects have been improved due to the addition of SC and FA, reflected by the tensile strength of the membranes increased from 46.1 ± 1.5 MPa to ∼55 MPa in the C and D groups, while the elongation at break increased from 17.9 ± 1.0% to ∼25%. The reason can be attributed to the strong electrostatic interaction in the SC-CH matrix, while the introduction of FA into its small molecule structure further helps to form a large number of covalent or hydrogen bonds, enhancing the intermolecular interactions. This observation is consistent with the work of (Mahmoud et al., 2024), who reported a 1.4-fold increase in tensile strength upon FA incorporation into chitosan-based membranes. Similarly, Panda et al. (2021) demonstrated that FA-mediated Schiff base and ester bond formation increased the elastic modulus of chitosan–poly(γ-glutamic acid) complexes, corroborating our FTIR-based crosslinking evidence. The slightly lower enhancement factor observed here (∼ 1.2-fold) relative to Mahmoud et al. may be attributed to the plasticizing effect of glycerol (0.5% v/v) and the higher SC content, which introduces flexible protein domains.

Antioxidant Activities: MMM exhibited significant antioxidant and antibacterial properties. As shown in Fig. 2B, the CH membrane exhibited moderate scavenging activity against DPPH (20.9%) and ABTS (27.1%), as the free -NH2 groups in CH molecules act as hydrogen donors to neutralize free radicals. In contrast, the addition of SC and FA significantly enhanced the antioxidant activity of the membranes, increasing DPPH scavenging activity to 25.1% ∼ 66.2% and ABTS scavenging activity to 48.9% ∼ 76.4%. Among them, SC primarily enhanced the matrix properties, with a limited contribution to antioxidant activity. Meanwhile, FA, due to its abundant phenolic hydroxyl groups, is a potent antioxidant that neutralizes free radicals through the hydrogen donor mechanism (Kumar et al., 2024; Mahmoud et al., 2024).

Antimicrobial Activities: Table 3 detailed the Quasi-MMMs displayed formulation-dependent antimicrobial activity. Pure CH exerted a mild inhibitory effect, mainly through electrostatic interactions between protonated amino groups and negatively charged microbial surfaces, which can disrupt cell-wall or membrane integrity (Wang et al., 2021). The inhibition was more evident against S. aureus than against the tested Gram-negative bacteria, consistent with the higher susceptibility of peptidoglycan-rich Gram-positive envelopes and the protective role of the lipopolysaccharide-containing outer membrane in Gram-negative cells (Zhao et al., 2023). Incorporation of FA markedly enhanced antimicrobial performance in a concentration-dependent manner, probably owing to its ability to impair membrane integrity, induce oxidative stress, and interfere with DNA replication and protein synthesis (Kumar et al., 2024; Panda et al., 2021). SC mainly contributed by reinforcing the CH-FA matrix and modulating the spatial distribution of active sites. Therefore, antimicrobial efficacy was maximized only within an appropriate SC range, whereas excessively low or high SC contents weakened the synergistic action of CH and FA (Di Giuseppe et al., 2022).

Selection of optimized quasi-MMM coatings and transport-controlled FA release

Formulation Selection: Based on the integrated physicochemical and biofunctional profiles of the membrane library, fB3, C3, and D3 formulations were selected for cherry tomatoes preservation trial and were subsequently denoted as T1, T2, and T3, respectively. These formulations exhibited the most favorable balance among antioxidant capacity, antimicrobial activity, opacity, surface hydrophilicity, and mechanical strength, thereby meeting the essential requirements for a postharvest coating designed to simultaneously regulate transpiration, respiratory metabolism, oxidative deterioration, and microbial proliferation. The selection was also supported by the known contribution of FA to membrane barrier reinforcement. Previous studies have shown that FA can markedly improve the resistance of biopolymer membranes to water vapor, O2, and CO2 transfer (Mahmoud et al., 2024), thereby suppressing moisture loss and respiratory consumption in packaged produce (Wu et al., 2023). Accordingly, the selected casting solutions were applied directly onto cherry tomato surfaces by immersion coating. The direct coating strategy is simple, cost-effective, and potentially amenable to scale-up in postharvest handling systems.

Structural Consolidation: Reinforced CH-SC-FA network was further supported by X-ray diffraction analysis (Fig. S1). Pure CH displayed two characteristic crystalline reflections at 2θ = 10.6° and 20.2°, corresponding to the hydrated orthorhombic crystalline domains of chitosan. After the incorporation of SC and FA, these reflections became significantly broadened and attenuated, indicating a pronounced reduction in long-range crystallographic order. This decrease in crystallinity suggests that the native interchain hydrogen-bonding registry of CH was disrupted by the incorporation of SC and FA, leading to a more integrated and amorphous ternary network. This amorphization is consistent with the covalent and non-covalent interactions inferred from FTIR analysis, including ester/amide bond formation and strengthened hydrogen bonding among the carboxyl, hydroxyl, and amino groups of FA, CH, and SC. The structural transition from a semi-crystalline CH matrix to a denser amorphous network was also consistent with the formation of a compact, pore-free membrane structure (Fig. S2), as further observed by SEM (Fig. S3). Collectively, the FTIR, XRD, and SEM results indicate that FA is not merely physically dispersed in the biopolymer matrix but participates in molecular-level matrix reinforcement, thereby providing the structural basis for the enhanced mechanical integrity, restricted molecular mobility, and improved barrier properties of the selected Quasi-MMMs.

Barrier Regulation: Barrier properties are critical for postharvest coatings because they determine water vapor transfer and respiratory gas exchange at the fruit–environment interface. As shown in Table S1, the Quasi-MMMs markedly improved the barrier performance of the CH-based matrix. Pure CH exhibited a relatively high WVTR of 57.4 ± 6.4 g·mm·m−2·day−1·kPa−1 whereas T3 reduced this value to 40.8 ± 2.1 g·mm·m−2·day−1·kPa−1, corresponding to an approximately 29% decrease. This improvement was mainly attributed to the denser and more tortuous diffusion pathway generated by covalent and non-covalent CH-SC-FA network reinforcement, together with the reduced surface hydrophilicity caused by hydrophobic SC domains and the aromatic structure of FA (Fig. S4). Respiratory gas permeability showed a similar but stronger decline: O2 permeability decreased from 3.95 ± 0.28 to 1.95 ± 0.27 cm3·mm·m−2·day−1·atm−1, while CO2 permeability decreased from 16.19 ± 0.82 to 8.83 ± 0.89 cm3·mm·m−2·day−1·atm−1 from CH to T3, representing reductions of ∼51% and 45%, respectively. These results indicate that FA-mediated matrix densification confined free volume and restricted segmental chain mobility, thereby suppressing molecular diffusion through the coating. The relatively stronger reduction in O2 permeability than in WVTR suggests that gas transport was more sensitive to network compaction, whereas water vapor transfer remained partly facilitated by residual hydrophilic groups in CH and SC. Such a permeability profile is favorable for postharvest preservation because it limits excessive O2 ingress and moisture loss while retaining partial CO2 exchange, reducing the risk of anaerobic metabolism and off-flavor development. Compared with the 35% WVTR reduction reported for chitosan/casein films containing Origanum vulgare essential oil through physical blending (Roshandel-hesari et al., 2022), the FA-integrated Quasi-MMM achieved comparable barrier enhancement through molecular matrix reinforcement, providing a mechanistic basis for the reduced weight loss and delayed oxidative deterioration observed in coated cherry tomatoes.

Controlled FA Release: Sustained FA release is essential for maintaining long-term bioactive protection during storage. As shown in Fig. S5, FA was gradually released from T1, T2, and T3 throughout the 25-day period without rapid initial depletion, with cumulative release values of 54.3 ± 1.7%, 47.8 ± 1.6%, and 45.9 ± 1.6% at day 25, respectively. The decreasing release rate with increasing SC content indicates that the denser CH-SC-FA network more effectively restricted FA diffusion, consistent with the structural consolidation and enhanced barrier properties described above. Kinetic modeling (Table S2) further showed that the release profiles were best fitted by the Korsmeyer-Peppas model, with adjusted R2 values above 0.99, indicating an anomalous non-Fickian mechanism governed by coupled diffusion and matrix relaxation or swelling. This controlled-release behavior enables sustained antioxidant and antimicrobial activity at the fruit–coating interface, while the compact matrix simultaneously regulates water vapor and respiratory gas transport, thereby providing a mechanistic basis for the superior preservation performance observed in the subsequent cherry tomato storage trial.

Suppression of microbial and oxidative deterioration in coated cherry tomatoes

Antioxidant Status: Cherry tomatoes contain abundant antioxidants, that are progressively depleted during storage through oxidative stress and metabolic consumption (Khalil et al., 2024). As illustrated in Fig. 3A and B, the ABTS and DPPH radical-scavenging activities of fruit extracts initially increased, peaked around day 15, and then declined, whereas coated fruits consistently maintained higher scavenging capacity than the uncoated control. This improvement can be attributed to two complementary effects. First, the Quasi-MMM coating restricted respiratory gas and water vapor exchange, thereby mitigating oxidative stress and slowing the depletion of endogenous antioxidants, including ascorbic acid, phenolic compounds, and lycopene (Wang et al., 2021). Second, the sustained release of FA from the coating matrix may provide additional radical-scavenging activity at the fruit–coating interface, as supported by the in vitro release profiles and kinetic fitting. Because DPPH and ABTS assays of whole-fruit extracts reflect total antioxidant status, they cannot distinguish endogenous antioxidant retention from the direct contribution of released FA. Therefore, the higher antioxidant activity in coated fruits should be interpreted as the combined result of preserved native antioxidant pools and possible exogenous supplementation by gradually released FA. Further tracer-based studies, such as isotopically labelled FA tracking, would be required to quantitatively deconvolute these contributions.

Microbial Suppression: The Quasi-MMM coatings also markedly attenuated microbial proliferation during refrigerated storage (Fig. 3C∼E). By day 25, T3 reduced total bacterial count by >3 log CFU·g−1 relative to the uncoated control, while TAC followed a similar inhibitory trend. Independent monitoring of aerobic and anaerobic populations was necessary because low-oxygen microenvironments may favor anaerobic species or metabolite-mediated microbial interactions (He et al., 2018). TYMC was also substantially suppressed, with T3 reducing their counts by ∼1.9 log CFU·g−1 at day 25, consistent with the delayed development of visible decay. Although the CH-only coating exerted a moderate inhibitory effect, the stronger suppression achieved by the Quasi-MMM coatings indicates that antimicrobial protection mainly arose from the integrated physical and chemical defenses of the CH-SC-FA network. The dense matrix may reduce extrinsic contamination, microbial adhesion and surface ingress, while protonated CH and the sustained release of FA from the covalently anchored reservoir may impair microbial membrane integrity and essential cellular processes, thereby maintaining prolonged microbial control throughout storage.

Decay Control: The microbiological containment was visually reflected by delayed decay development (Fig. 3F). Decay incidence remained absent in all groups during the first 10 days, after which the uncoated control deteriorated rapidly and reached 47.1 ± 2.1% by day 25. In contrast, T3 restricted decay incidence to 8.6 ± 0.4%, substantially lower than that of CH coating alone (29.3 ± 1.8%). This protection highlights the practical advantage of coupling barrier integrity with sustained bioactive delivery: the low-permeability matrix limits moisture-driven tissue susceptibility and external contamination, whereas gradual FA release may continuously suppress microbial growth and contribute to fungal inhibition (Roshandel-hesari et al., 2022). Nevertheless, the antifungal assessment was based on native spoilage microbiota under storage conditions rather than standardized challenge tests against specific postharvest pathogens such as Botrytis cinerea, Alternaria alternata, Rhizopus stolonifer, Penicillium expansum, or Fusarium spp. Therefore, these results should be interpreted as evidence of broad spoilage suppression rather than pathogen-specific antifungal efficacy. Future in vivo challenge assays are needed to define pathogen-dependent dose–response behavior and the effective inhibitory concentration of FA released from the coating matrix under storage-relevant conditions. Collectively, the suppression of oxidative and microbial deterioration provides a mechanistic basis for the subsequent retention of marketable, textural, and nutritional quality.

Retention of marketable quality: Appearance, water loss and firmness

Appearance and Color: During storage, cherry tomatoes underwent visual changes (Fig. 4A), primarily due to the synthesis and accumulation of carotenoids such as lycopene and β-carotene, resulting in color deepening (Nkede et al., 2024). Additionally, skin wrinkling caused by transpiration and dehydration was observed. The application of protective coating with antioxidant and antimicrobial properties significantly mitigated these changes, maintaining an appearance closer to that of fresh samples. This preservation is crucial for enhancing consumer appeal and purchasing intent (Khalil et al., 2024). Quantified analysis in color changes, as shown in Fig. 4B, indicated that coating significantly influenced the brightness of cherry tomatoes. However, due to the complex mechanisms underlying overall color change, no significant difference was observed between the coated and control samples based solely on ΔE values (Khalil et al., 2024).

Water Loss: Fig. 4C showed the weight loss rate of cherry tomatoes during storage, primarily reflecting water transpiration from the fruit. In the initial storage period, weight loss rates were similar across groups. However, difference became more pronounced over time. By the end of storage, the T3 group exhibited a weight loss rate of only 8.9%, compared to 14.2% in the control group. It demonstrated that the coating layers served as effective barriers, maintaining fruit moisture during storage (Roshandel-hesari et al., 2022).

Firmness: Fig. 4D illustrated the decrease in hardness of cherry tomatoes during storage. The degradation of pectin and changes in cellular structure contributed to the gradual softening of the fruit (Alvarez et al., 2021). The control group exhibited the most significant decrease in hardness, while the T3 group showed the least, indicating the most effective inhibition of softening. This is attributed to the superior antimicrobial and antioxidant properties of the T3 layer, which helped preserve fruit quality and maintain freshness.

Nutritional quality and senescence-associated lipid peroxidation

Sugars and Acids: The primary solid constituents in fruits are sugars and organic acids, which undergo metabolic transformations during storage, influencing the maturity and flavor of cherry tomatoes (Jahani, et al., 2023). As shown in Fig. 5A, the total solid content of cherry tomatoes initially exhibited increased due to the breakdown of complex organic compounds into soluble sugars, followed by a decrease as substrates were depleted and sugars were consumed through respiration (Zhang et al., 2024). The Quasi-MMMs effectively delayed the sugar metabolism by inhibiting microbial activity and enzymatic reactions, result in smaller variations in total solid content compared to the control group. Regarding titratable acidity, its content decreased progressively with extended storage time across all groups (Fig. 5B), attributed to the conversion of organic acids into sugars and their consumption in respiration. The control group experienced a more rapid decline, with TA content dropping into 109 g/kg by the end of storage. In contrast, the CH group retained slightly higher levels at 123 g/kg, while the T1, T2 and T3 groups still maintained levels at 136, 147, and 153 g/kg, respectively.

Total Phenols: Total phenolic compounds play a crucial role in maintaining the sensory quality and nutritional value of fruits (Yang et al., 2022). As depicted in Fig. 5C, the control group exhibited an initial increase in total phenolic content during the early storage, likely due to the enzymatic conversion of precursor compounds via the phenylpropanoid pathway. However, in later stages, total phenolic content declined due to oxidative degradation mediated by polyphenol oxidase and metabolic consumption in response to oxidative stress (Li et al., 2022). The progressive accumulation of total phenolics in coated fruits contrasts with the decline observed in the control. This pattern is consistent with findings by Li et al. (2022), who reported that equilibrium modified atmosphere packaging delayed phenolic degradation in postharvest vegetables through reduced O2 availability. Whether FA released from the coating directly inhibits PPO activity—as suggested for ferulic acid in in vitro enzyme assays (Kumar et al., 2024)—remains to be confirmed in the fruit system.

Ascorbic Acid: Ascorbic acid is vital as a cofactor in various enzymatic reactions, contributing to the scavenging of reactive oxygen species and inhibiting lipid peroxidation in fruits, thereby delaying processes such as browning and senescence (Liu et al., 2024). In this study, ascorbic acid content in all groups exhibited gradual declines over the storage period (Fig. 5D), attributed to oxidative degradation and metabolic pathways. However, the application of the coating significantly mitigated this downward trend. The relatively dense coating layer likely acted as a barrier, limiting oxygen exposure and reducing oxidative stress. Additionally, the antioxidant properties of the coatings may have further contributed to the preservation of ascorbic acid levels (Jahani, et al., 2023).

Lipid Peroxidation: Malondialdehyde is a major byproduct of membrane lipid peroxidation and serves as a reliable biomarker for tissue aging and the extent of oxidative damage in cherry tomatoes (Zhang et al., 2024). In this study, malondialdehyde levels in all cherry tomato samples exhibited a progressive increase during storage (Fig. 5E), underscoring its role as a hallmark of oxidative degradation and cellular aging. Notably, the coating significantly suppressed malondialdehyde accumulation, likely reflecting the interplay of enhanced barrier properties - which restrict O2 ingress and thus slow oxidative metabolism—and the intrinsic radical-scavenging capacity of the coating. The relative contribution of each mechanism remains to be quantified. Therefore, the coating helped preserve cell membrane integrity, delaying the onset of aging and senescence in the fruit tissues. This reduction in MDA accumulation (∼ 37% at day 25 for T3 vs. control) is comparable to the 30–40% reduction reported by Zhang et al. (2024) for strawberries stored under equilibrium modified atmosphere packaging, suggesting that the integrated barrier-antioxidant mechanism of the Quasi-MMM achieves similar oxidative protection through a different material-design paradigm. Conclusions

This work establishes a fully bio-based Quasi-MMM wherein FA functions dually as a covalent crosslinker and a sustained-release bioactive filler within a CH-SC matrix. Spectroscopic and structural analyses confirm that FA-mediated ester/amide linkages and hydrogen-bonding networks produce a dense, amorphous membrane with substantially reinforced mechanical integrity and matrix-governed anomalous FA release. This controlled, non-Fickian release profile directly underpins the persistent antimicrobial and antioxidant protection observed on cherry tomatoes, effectively preserving firmness, ascorbic acid, total phenolics, and titratable acidity while markedly suppressing malondialdehyde accumulation throughout 25-day refrigerated storage. By translating the matrix-filler architectural logic of classical Quasi-MMMs into an entirely organic, multifunctional platform, the Quasi-MMM concept offers a unified design strategy that simultaneously combats physiological senescence and microbial spoilage. Future investigations should focus on quantifying on-fruit FA release kinetics and evaluating the immersion-coating process at pilot scale to assess its commercial feasibility to advance this technology toward commercial deployment.
