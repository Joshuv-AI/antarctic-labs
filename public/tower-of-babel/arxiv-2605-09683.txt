Rook theory, normal ordering in the q q -deformed Ore algebra and the polynomial generalization
Matthias Schork 1

Abstract
. For words in the variables X X and Y Y satisfying the commutation relation of the q q -deformed generalized Ore algebra, X ​ Y − q ​ Y ​ X = μ ​ I + ν ​ Y XY-qYX=\mu I+\nu Y , we show that the corresponding normal ordering coefficients can be given an interpretation in terms of mixed placements of rooks and files. In particular, the associated q q -deformed Ore-Stirling and Ore-Lah numbers are treated in detail. We show that the q q -deformed Ore-Stirling numbers (resp., q q -deformed Ore-Lah numbers) are given as mixed placement numbers of rooks and files on the staircase board (resp., Lah board). Using this combinatorial interpretation, their recurrence relations are derived. In addition, the normal ordered form of the binomial ( X + Y ) m (X+Y)^{m} in the q q -deformed generalized Ore algebra is determined. These considerations are then extended to the q q -deformed polynomial Weyl algebra generated by X X and Y Y satisfying X ​ Y − q ​ Y ​ X = f ⁡ ( Y ) XY-qYX=f(Y) for some polynomial f ∈ ℂ ⁡ [ Y ] f\in\mathbb{C}[Y] . In particular, associated q q -deformed polynomial Stirling and Lah numbers are introduced and their properties studied. The normal ordered form of the binomial is also extended to the q q -deformed polynomial Weyl algebra.

1.
Introduction

The
q
-deformed generalized Ore algebra

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu,\nu}(q)
is defined as the complex unital algebra generated by two variables
X
and
Y
satisfying the commutation relation

(1)

XY-qYX=\mu I+\nu Y

for some complex parameters
\mu,\nu
. Letting
\mu=\nu=1
, we obtain the
q
-deformed Ore algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(q)={\mathcal{O}}_{1,1}(q)
. Letting instead
q=1
, we obtain the undeformed generalized Ore algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu,\nu}={\mathcal{O}}_{\mu,\nu}(1)
. Letting furthermore
\mu=-1
and
\nu=-1
, the defining commutation relation becomes
YX-XY=I+Y
which coincides with the one of Patrias and Pylyavskyy
[
30
,
31
]
who coined the name
Ore algebra
. They observed that by an appropriate scaling of the generators it suffices to consider this reduced form of the commutation relation but for us it will be convenient to keep the parameters
\mu,\nu
. Patrias and Pylyavskyy
[
30
,
31
]
studied this commutation relation in the corresponding theory of
dual filtered graphs
which is a K-theoretic analogue of Fomin’s
dual graded graphs

[
12
,
13
]
and their predecessors, Stanley’s
differential posets

[
38
]
. One way to view this theory is as a study of certain combinatorial representations of the first Weyl algebra
{\mathcal{W}}
. In fact, many results of
[
12
,
13
,
38
]
can be viewed as normal ordering results in the Weyl algebra. Starting with
[
30
,
31
]
, some results on normal ordering in the Ore algebra have been derived recently. For
X
and
Y
satisfying
YX-XY=I+Y
, Yeliussizov
[
41
]
derived the normal ordered form of
Y^{n}X^{m}
and showed that it involves the Eulerian numbers. Kodsueb and Lytvynov
[
19
]
considered ordering
(XY)^{n}
in the parametrized situation
YX-XY=bI+aY
studied also in
[
27
]
where Ore-Stirling numbers were introduced as normal ordering coefficients of
(YX)^{n}
. Recently, considering the
q
-deformed version,
YX-qXY=I+Y
, normal ordering
(XY)^{n}
was studied by Briand
[
6
]
. Note that Levandovskyy et al.
[
20
]
also considered (
1
) with respect to normal ordering.

The Ore algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu,\nu}(q)
can also be considered as a common generalization of the Weyl algebra and the shift algebra. Indeed, for
\nu=0
and
\mu=1
, one obtains from (
1
) with
q=1
the defining relation of the
Weyl algebra

{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{O}}_{1,0}(1)
,

(2)

XY-YX=I,

while, for
\mu=0
and
\nu=1
, one obtains from (
1
) with
q=1
the defining relation of the
shift algebra

{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{O}}_{0,1}(1)
,

(3)

XY-YX=Y.

Letting
\mu=\nu=0
, one obtains the
quantum plane

{\mathcal{O}}_{0,0}(q)
where
XY=qYX
.

We say a word
\omega
in the letters
X
and
Y
is in
normal ordered form
if all the letters
Y
stand to the left of all the letters
X
, i.e.,
\omega
is written in the form
\omega=\sum_{j,k}c_{j,k}Y^{j}X^{k}
for some complex coefficients
c_{j,k}
, the
normal ordering coefficients
. For example, it is a famous result that one has in the Weyl algebra
{\mathcal{W}}
the normal ordering result

(4)

(YX)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)Y^{k}X^{k},

where the Stirling numbers of the second kind appear as normal ordering coefficients. The Weyl algebra has a concrete representation in terms of the derivative operator
D
and the multiplication operator
M_{x}
, acting on functions of a real variable as
(Df)(x)=f^{\prime}(x)
and
(M_{x}f)(x)=xf(x)
, i.e.,
DM_{x}-M_{x}D=I
. In this context,
M_{x}
is also written as
X
, and (
4
) translates into
(XD)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)X^{k}D^{k}
. In this form it was already known to Scherk in 1823, and he also considered
(X^{r}D)^{n}
and the corresponding normal ordering coefficients. The generalization to other words, e.g.,
(X^{p}D^{q})^{n}
was considered already in the early 1930s, see
[
25
]
for a comprehensive history of early results and
[
7
]
for some new results. See also the beautiful survey of Blasiak and Flajolet
[
5
]
for the many ramifications of normal ordering in the Weyl algebra (in the appendix one can find a detailed discussion of Scherk’s dissertation from 1823). For the shift algebra
{\mathcal{S}}
one has analogous results, and the normal ordering coefficients of
(YX)^{n}
in
{\mathcal{S}}
are given by the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind
|s(n,k)|
. Normal ordering other words in the shift algebra has also been considered, see, e.g.,
[
36
]
and the references given therein. The
q
-deformation of the Weyl algebra and the shift algebra has also been considered with respect to normal ordering, see
[
25
]
. In the undeformed case, one may consider, for
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the complex unital algebra
\mathcal{A}_{s;h}
generated by
X
and
Y
satisfying

(5)

XY-YX=hY^{s}.

Clearly, for
s=0
and
h=1
, one recovers the commutation relation of the Weyl algebra, while for
s=1
and
h=1
the one of the shift algebra. The case
s=2
is also known and describes the
Jordan plane
, see
[
25
]
and the references given therein. For arbitrary
s
the study of this relation – and normal ordering words in
X
and
Y
satisfying (
5
) – was begun by Burde
[
8
]
and Varvak
[
39
]
. It is natural to introduce
generalized Stirling numbers

{\mathfrak{S}}_{s;h}(n,k)
as normal ordering coefficients of
(YX)^{n}
in
\mathcal{A}_{s;h}
, see
[
23
,
24
]
. In the generalized Ore algebra (where
q=1
) analogues of the Stirling numbers have also been introduced as normal ordering coefficients of
(YX)^{n}

[
27
]
.

As a common generalization of the
q
-deformed Ore algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu,\nu}(q)
and
\mathcal{A}_{s;h}(q)
where (
5
) is
q
-deformed to
XY-qYX=hY^{s}
, one may consider the
q
-deformed polynomial Weyl algebra

\mathcal{A}_{f}(q)
which is defined as the complex unital algebra generated by
X
and
Y
satisfying

(6)

XY-qYX=f(Y),\,\,f(Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\alpha_{j}Y^{j},

where
f
is a polynomial in
Y
and
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
. Normal ordering in
\mathcal{A}_{f}(q)
was started in
[
22
]
but for
q=1
Viskov
[
40
]
considered some aspects already in 1996 (and, even earlier, in 1979 Irving
[
16
]
derived some ordering results en route). Benkart et al.
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
considered
\mathcal{A}_{f}(1)
in depth (including some ordering results) and this algebra was considered subsequently by several authors, see the recent survey of Lopes
[
21
]
on different generalizations of the Weyl algebra and related structures (from a more algebraic perspective). In another direction, normal ordering was studied very recently by Rubiano and Reyes
[
33
]
for 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings with 3 generators satisfying 3 commutation relations.

Let us turn to the connection to rook theory. Rook theory began in 1946 with the publication of
[
18
]
. Since then, the original problem of counting the number of possibilities of placing (non-attacking) rooks on certain boards, in particular
Ferrers boards
, has been extended by many authors in many different directions. In our context, the first hint for a connection between normal ordering coefficients and rook numbers seems to be by Navon
[
29
]
, but the precise connection was given by Varvak in the fundamental paper
[
39
]
(see also Fomin
[
13
]
). Briefly, to a word in
X
and
Y
satisfying (
2
), one associates a Ferrers board, and the normal ordering coefficients are given by the rook numbers of the board. For the particular word
(YX)^{n}
, the board associated is the staircase board
J_{n}
, and it is well known that the rook numbers of
J_{n}
are given by
S(n,k)
, thereby recovering (
4
). Varvak also showed that normal ordering words in
X
and
Y
satisfying (
5
) involves the
s
-rook placements introduced by Goldman and Haglund
[
15
]
. In particular, choosing
s=0
, the
0
-rook placements correspond to rook placements, and it is equally well known that the file numbers of the staircase board
J_{n}
are given by
|s(n,k)|
, thereby recovering the analogue of (
4
) in the shift algebra. An extension to the
q
-deformed case of (
5
) was given by Celeste et al.
[
11
]
.

In
[
27
]
, analogues of the Stirling numbers of the second kind were defined in the Ore algebra as normal ordering coefficients of
(YX)^{n}
, and it was shown these
Ore-Stirling numbers
can be expressed in terms of Stirling numbers of the second kind and the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind. This was shown in an algebraic fashion, but a combinatorial interpretation was lacking. In the present paper, we will give a combinatorial interpretation for normal ordering arbitrary words in
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu,\nu}(q)
defined by (
1
). We also show how this interpretation can be extended to the
q
-deformed polynomial Weyl algebra
\mathcal{A}_{f}(q)
defined by (
6
).

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we recall rook and file placements and their connection to normal ordering. We then introduce a
q
-statistic on mixed rook and file placements and show that this describes the normal ordering coefficients in the
q
-deformed Ore algebra. As an example, we consider the
q
-deformed Ore-Stirling and Ore-Lah numbers in this context. In Section
3
, we investigate the binomial formula for
(X+Y)^{m}
in the
q
-deformed generalized Ore algebra. All these considerations are extended to the
q
-deformed polynomial Weyl algebra in Section
4
. Some conclusions are presented in Section
5
.

\omega
in the letters
X

Example 2.1
.

Let us consider the staircase board
J_{n}
. It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
25
, Section 2.4.4]
and the references given therein) that

(7)

r_{n-k}(J_{n})=S(n,k),\,\,\,\,f_{n-k}(J_{n})=|s(n,k)|.

Now, let us draw the connection to normal ordering. For this, we have to consider the concrete commutation relation between
X
and
Y
at hand and use its implications for placing rooks (or files).

Let us start with the Weyl algebra
{\mathcal{W}}
where
XY=YX+\mu I
. Recall that the process of normal ordering a word
\omega
in
{\mathcal{W}}
consists of selecting the rightmost corner of
B_{\omega}
(i.e., corresponding to the rightmost subword
XY
) and either placing a rook (corresponding to a contraction
XY\rightsquigarrow\mu I
) or leaving it empty (corresponding to the commutation
XY\rightsquigarrow YX
). By iterating over all possibilities of placing the rooks (or not), one obtains that the normal ordering coefficients are given as sum over all possible rook placements. Let
\omega
be a word containing
m
letters
X
and
n
letters
Y
. Each placement of
k
rooks on the board – representing one particular sequence to normal order – yields a summand in the normal ordered form proportional to
\mu^{k}Y^{n-k}X^{m-k}
since
k
pairs of
X
and
Y
are contracted. In total, one obtains the normal ordering result
[
13
,
39
]

(8)

\omega=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m,n)}\mu^{k}r_{k}(B_{\omega})Y^{n-k}X^{m-k}.

For example, when
\omega=(YX)^{n}
, the corresponding Ferrers board is the staircase board
J_{n}
, and (
8
) together with (
7
) recovers (
4
). The study of the normal ordering coefficients of arbitrary words in the Weyl algebra was started by McCoy in 1934
[
28
]
and several different combinatorial interpretations have been given, see
[
5
]
and
[
25
]
for a survey.

For the
q
-deformed Weyl algebra, the procedure is similar, but we have to introduce certain
q
-weights for the rook placements to capture the factor
q
when not placing a rook (corresponding to the
q
-commutation
XY\rightsquigarrow qYX
). For this, let
B
be a Ferrers board and let
\phi\in\mathcal{R}_{k}(B)
. We partition the boxes of
B
as follows into classes:

(1)

A box is called a
rook box
if a rook is placed in it,

(2)

A box is called a
cancelled box
, if it is not a rook box and lying in the same column above a rook or in the same row to the left of a rook,

(3)

All remaining boxes are
empty boxes
.

In the following figures, a rook boxed will be marked by a filled square, a cancelled box by an
X
.

The
q
-weight of the placement

\phi\in\mathcal{R}_{k}(B)
is defined to be

(9)

w_{\mu;q}(B,\phi)=\mu^{\#\mbox{rook boxes}}q^{\#\mbox{empty boxes}}=\mu^{k}q^{\#\mbox{empty boxes}}.

The
q
-deformed rook numbers
are defined by

(10)

r_{k}(B;q)=\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{R}_{k}(B)}w_{\mu;q}(B,\phi),

and the
q
-deformed analogue of (

) holds true for the

-deformed Weyl algebra
[

]

(11)

\omega=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m,n)}r_{k}(B_{\omega};q)Y^{n-k}X^{m-k}.

Clearly, for

r_{k}(B;1)=\mu^{k}|\mathcal{R}_{k}(B)|=\mu^{k}r_{k}(B)
, recovering the undeformed situation. The

-weights above go back to Garsia and Remmel
[
14
]
.

Let us turn to the
q
-deformed generalized Ore algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu,\nu}(q)

Definition 2.3
.

Let a Ferrers board
B
be given. A placement of
k
rooks and
\ell
files on the board
B
will be called non-attacking, if

•

No two (or more) rooks lie in the same row or column, and

•

No two (or more) files lie in the same column, and

•

No file and rook lie in the same column, and no file lies to the left of a rook in the same row.

The set of all non-attacking placements of
k
rooks and
\ell
files on the board
B
will be denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(B)
(
\mathcal{M}
for “mixed”).

The first condition is the rule for placing rooks, the second condition is the rule for placing files, and the third conditon is the rule for their “interaction”. As an example, in Figure
4

B

. Each such placement

of

j

\phi

.

The

For

Now, let us turn to the basic word

, for

m

and it remains to determine

t

(r-t)
files on

. The rook and file numbers of

are given by

Let us recall the simple argument: To place
k

we first choose
k
of the

\binom{m}{k}
. In each of the
k

possibilities to place the file, hence the factor

, yielding in total

possibilities. For the placement of
k

(n-1)
possible rows to place the second rook,
(n-2)
possible rows to place the third rook, hence,

possibilities to place the
k

possibilities.

To determine the number of mixed placements of
k

\ell

k

possibilities to choose from, when placing it between the

-th and

-th rook from the right (with

) we have in the column
(n-j)

(n-k)
possibilities to place it. Let us introduce some terminology. We denote by

from right to left, starting with 1 for the right-most column. Let us place the
k

k

\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(m)
. To distribute
\ell

colums, we consider the (at most)
(k+1)

blocks

k

into

.

Let

. The number of mixed placements of

\ell

Proof.

We continue the argument from above and note that we have
k!\binom{n}{k}|{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(m)|
possibilities to place the
k
rooks. However, for each
\pi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(m)
we have to determine the possible placements of
\ell

\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k+1})\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{k+1}(\ell||\pi)

-th block, for

. Since the

-th block has

-th block has

∎

For

Remark 2.20

Special rectangular boards have been considered in literature. For example, the Abel board

[

As mentioned in the introduction, Yeliussizov
[

, Lemma 10.2]
gave a formula for

where

involving the Eulerian numbers. We adapt the proof given in
[
41
]
to the present case and obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
X
and
Y
satisfy the commutation relation
XY-YX=I+Y
of the Ore algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{1,1}(1)
. Then one has, for
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

X^{m}Y^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{m}\sum_{t=0}^{n}\binom{m}{r}\binom{n}{t}\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\binom{r-t+\ell}{\ell}A(r,n-\ell)\right\}Y^{n-t}X^{m-r},

where the
A(n,k)
denotes the Eulerian numbers.

Proof.

First observe that
XY-YX=I+Y
implies
X(Y+1)=(Y+1)(X+1)
; here and in the following we identify
kI
with
k
. An induction over
m
and then over
k
shows
X^{m}(Y+1)^{k}=(Y+1)^{k}(X+k)^{m}
. Writing
Y=(Y+1)-1
, one has

\displaystyle X^{m}Y^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}(Y+1)^{k}(X+k)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=i}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{m}{j}k^{m-j}Y^{i}X^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{n}{i}\binom{m}{j}Y^{i}X^{j}\left\{\sum_{k=i}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n-i}{k-i}k^{m-j}\right\},

where we expanded in the second equation the binomials and switched the order of summation, and used in the third equation that
\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{i}=\binom{n}{i}\binom{n-i}{k-i}
. Letting
r=m-j
and
t=n-i
, and then
\binom{t}{k-n+t}=\binom{t}{n-k}
, we obtain

X^{m}Y^{n}=\sum_{t=0}^{n}\sum_{r=0}^{m}\binom{n}{t}\binom{m}{r}Y^{n-t}X^{m-r}\left\{\sum_{k=n-t}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{t}{n-k}k^{r}\right\}.

The sum
\sum_{k=n-t}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{t}{n-k}k^{r}
equals the expression
q_{n}(r,t)
considered by Yeliussizov
[
41
]
who showed that
q_{n}(r,t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\binom{r-t+\ell}{\ell}A(r,n-\ell)
with the Eulerian numbers
A(r,n-\ell)
counting the number of permutations of
(1,\ldots,r)
with
(n-\ell)
descents.
∎

Comparing the two expressions for the normal ordered form of
X^{m}Y^{n}
, we obtain the identity

\sum_{\pi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{t}(m)}\sum_{\rho\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{t+1}(r-t||\pi)}\prod_{j=1}^{t+1}\binom{m_{j}(\pi)}{\rho_{j}}(n+1-j)^{\rho_{j}}=\frac{1}{t!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\binom{m}{r}\binom{r-t+\ell}{\ell}A(r,n-\ell).

In this section, we consider the binomial

We will expand the binomial into a sum over all binary words over the alphabet

Let us introduce some basic notations for partitions following

\lambda_{i}

{\mathcal{I}}_{m}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{m}{\mathcal{I}}_{m-k,k}

{\mathcal{I}}_{m-k,k}
contains

elements, hence

. Let us recall the following result
[

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists a bijection between the Young diagrams in the set
\mathcal{I}_{m}
and the binary words over the alphabet

of length

.

We recall the construction of this bijection. Let
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{m}
be any word over the alphabet
\{X,Y\}
of length
m
. Let
\ell
be the number of occurrences of the letter

in
\pi
. Then the number of occurrences of the letter

in

is given by (

). The numbers
\lambda_{j}
, for

(38)

\displaystyle\pi=Y^{m-\ell-\lambda_{1}}XY^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\cdots XY^{\lambda_{\ell-1}-\lambda_{\ell}}XY^{\lambda_{\ell}}.

By construction,
\lambda_{\ell}\geq 0
,
\lambda_{j}\geq\lambda_{j+1}
(for

), and
\lambda_{1}\leq(m-\ell
), thus
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})\equiv\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{\ell}
is a partition with

. On the other hand, if
Y_{\lambda}
is a Young diagram in
\mathcal{I}_{m-\ell,\ell}\subset\mathcal{I}_{m}
, then we define the word
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{m}
by (

is a binary word over the alphabet
\{X,Y\}
of length

with exactly

letters of

and exactly (

of length

with exactly
\ell
letters of

and exactly (
m-\ell
) letters of

bijectively to the set

. Clearly, this is a bijection between the set of all binary words over the alphabet
\{X,Y\}
of length

and the set
\mathcal{I}_{m}
.

Now, by expanding the binomial

, we get a sum over all binary words of length

in

and

and, in particular (

where we write here – and in the following – with a slight abuse of notation

\omega_{\lambda}=XY^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\cdots XY^{\lambda_{\ell-1}-\lambda_{\ell}}XY^{\lambda_{\ell}}.

Then we can write (

(X+Y)^{m}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{m-\ell,\ell}}Y^{m-\ell-\lambda_{1}}\omega_{\lambda},

and

Now, we use the commutation relation. The words

Following Goldman and Haglund

Definition 4.1
.

Let

. Given a Ferrers board

For

A rook satisfying the

Recall the procedure from Section

-deformed generalized Ore algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}}(q)
where
XY=qYX+\alpha_{0}I+\alpha_{1}Y
one has in the process of normal ordering for each subword

to decide between the following three possibilities, corresponding to the terms on the right-hand side of the commutation relation:

•

leave it empty (corresponding to
XY\rightsquigarrow qYX
), or

•

place a rook of weight 0 (corresponding to
XY\rightsquigarrow\alpha_{0}I
), or

•

place a rook of weight 1 (corresponding to
XY\rightsquigarrow\alpha_{1}Y
).

The above-mentioned result of Varvak shows that the term
\alpha_{s}Y^{s}
on the right-hand side of the commutation relation corresponds to the placement of a rook of weight
s
(corresponding to
XY\rightsquigarrow\alpha_{s}Y^{s}
). Thus, to describe the normal ordering in
\mathcal{A}_{f}(q)
where one has

(44)

XY=qYX+\alpha_{0}I+\alpha_{1}Y+\alpha_{2}Y^{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{s}Y^{s},

we have to bring these observations together. Roughly speaking, each time we come across a subword
XY
we can choose between
(s+2)
possibilities: Either do not place a rook (corresponding to
XY\rightsquigarrow qYX
), or place a rook of weight
k
(corresponding to
XY\rightsquigarrow\alpha_{k}Y^{k}
), where
k=0,1,\ldots,s
. Then one has to sum over all possibilities of placing rooks of weights
k
with
k=0,\ldots,s
(or not). We now formalize these notions. Let us introduce, following Goldman and Haglund
[
15
]
, the following notion.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
B
be a Ferrers board. Let
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and let
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, for
j=0,\ldots,s
. A mixed rook placement of type
{\bf k}\equiv(k_{0},\ldots,k_{s})
on
B
is a non-attacking placement
\phi
of
k_{j}
rooks with weights
j
,
j=0,\ldots,s
, on
B
, going from right to left, creating
j
rows to the left of each rook of weight
j
. The set of all mixed rook placements of type
{\bf k}
on
B
will be denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf k}}(B)
or
{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{0},\ldots,k_{s}}(B)
.

Note that in the definition of a mixed rook placement no particular order of the rooks of different weights is assumed. A mixed rook placement of type
{\bf k}=(0,\ldots,0,k_{\ell},0,\ldots,0)
is a non-attacking placement of
k_{\ell}
rooks of weight
\ell
and will also be called a
pure rook placement of degree
\ell
. Thus,

(45)

|{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ldots,0,k_{\ell},0,\ldots,0}(B)|=r_{k_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}(B).

By construction, the above placements generalize the mixed placements of rooks and files we considered in Section
2
. Namely, a mixed rook placement of type
(k_{0},k_{1},0,\ldots,0)
is a non-attacking placement of
k_{0}
rooks and
k_{1}
files, i.e.,

(46)

{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{0},k_{1},0,\ldots,0}(B)={\mathcal{M}}_{k_{0},k_{1}}(B).

Let
B
be a Ferrers board and let
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{0},\ldots,k_{s}}(B)
. We partition the boxes of
B
into
(s+3)
classes:

(1)

A box is called a
rook box of weight
j
if a rook of weight
j
is placed in it, for
j=0,\ldots,s
,

(2)

A box is called a
cancelled box
, if it is neither a rook box (irrespective of weight), and it is lying above a rook in the same column or to the left of a rook in the same row.

(3)

All remaining boxes are
empty boxes
.

Let us abbreviate the set of parameters by
{\bf\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{s})
and use multi-index notation,

{\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}}=\prod_{j=0}^{s}\alpha_{j}^{k_{j}}.

Definition 4.5
.

The
q
-weight of the mixed placement
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}(B)
is defined to be

(47)

w_{{\bf\alpha};q}(B,\phi)=q^{\#\mbox{empty boxes}}\prod_{j=0}^{s}\alpha_{j}^{\#\mbox{rook boxes of weight $j$}}={\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}}q^{\#\mbox{empty boxes}}.

Remark 4.6
.

The definition of the
q
-weights reduces for
\phi
of type
(k_{0}=\mu,k_{1}=\nu,0,\ldots,0)
to the definition of
w_{\mu,\nu;q}(B,\phi)
for the
q
-deformed generalized Ore algebra. However, it seems that our definition of the classes of the boxes does not coincide with the one given there. This impression is false and the reason is that we use here for a placement of a file the 1-rule creation rule, where we add a new row to the left of the file and interpret the row of the file as filled. In Section
2
, we did not create a new row, but allowed placements of files and rooks to the left of the file in that row.

For example, the mixed rook placement of type
(1,1,0,1)
shown in Figure

has
q
-weight
q^{7}\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}
. Let us turn to the connection to normal ordering. The staircase board
J_{5}
is derived from the word
(YX)^{5}
. The particular sequence of contractions and commutations described by the mixed rook placement shown in Figure

contains one placement of a rook of weight 0 (
XY\rightsquigarrow\alpha_{0}I
), one placement of a rook of weight 1 (
XY\rightsquigarrow\alpha_{1}Y
), and one placement of a rook of weight 3 (
XY\rightsquigarrow\alpha_{3}Y^{3}
). Thus, the resulting normal ordered expression is
q^{7}\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}Y^{5-1+0+2}X^{5-1-1-1}=q^{7}\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}Y^{6}X^{2}
. In general, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let a word
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}=Y^{n_{r}}X^{m_{r}}\cdots Y^{n_{1}}X^{m_{1}}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{f}(q)
be given where
f(Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\alpha_{j}Y^{j}
. Let
\phi\in{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf k}}(B_{\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}})
be a mixed rook placement of type
{\bf k}=(k_{0},\ldots,k_{s})
. Then the corresponding summand has the normal ordered form

(48)

q^{\ell}{\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}}\,Y^{|{\bf n}|+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}}X^{|{\bf m}|-|{\bf k}|},

where
|{\bf k}|=k_{0}+\cdots+k_{s}
and the exponent
\ell
depends on the concrete placement of the rooks on
B_{\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}}
.

Proof.

Each placement of a rook of weight
j
gives a factor of
\alpha_{j}
and reduces the number of letters
X
by one and changes the number of letters
Y
by
(j-1)
. Thus, the placement of
k_{j}
rooks of weight
j
gives the factor
\alpha_{j}^{k_{j}}
, reduces the number of letters
X
by
k_{j}
and changes the number of letters
Y
by
(j-1)k_{j}
. Considering all different weights yields the right-hand side up to the power of
q
. This comes from the number of empty boxes and depends on the concrete placement of the rooks.
∎

Note that if
\phi
is pure of degree
s
, i.e., one has a placement of
j
rooks of weight
s
, the expression on the right-hand side of (
48
) reduces for
q=1
to
\alpha_{s}^{j}Y^{|{\bf n}|+(s-1)j}X^{|{\bf m}|-j}
, recovering the structure of the summands on the right-hand side of (
43
).

Before we can collect all summands over all possibilities of non-attacking mixed rook placements, we extend the
q
-deformed mixed placement numbers from Definition
2.5
to the present situation.

Definition 4.8
.

The
q
-deformed mixed placement numbers of type
{\bf k}=(k_{0},\ldots,k_{s})
are defined by

(49)

m_{{\bf k}}(B;q)\equiv m_{{\bf k}}^{\bf\alpha}(B;q)=\sum_{\phi\in{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf k}}(B)}w_{{\bf\alpha};q}(B,\phi)={\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}}\sum_{\phi\in{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf k}}(B)}q^{\#\mbox{empty boxes}}.

For
q=1
, this reduces to
m_{{\bf k}}(B;1)={\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}}|{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf k}}(B)|
.
Now, we have to sum over all possible types of placements. For
k,s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we let

{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(k)=\left\{{\bf k}\equiv(k_{0},\ldots,k_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s+1}\,|\,|{\bf k}|\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{s}k_{j}=k\right\},

i.e., the set of weak compositions of
k
with
(s+1)
parts.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}=Y^{n_{r}}X^{m_{r}}\cdots Y^{n_{1}}X^{m_{1}}
be a word in
{\mathcal{A}}_{f}(q)
where
f(Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\alpha_{j}Y^{j}
. Then the normal ordered form of
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}
is given by

(50)

\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{|{\bf m}|-m_{1}}\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(k)}m_{{\bf k}}(B_{\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}};q)\,Y^{|{\bf n}|+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}}X^{|{\bf m}|-k}.

Proof.

The normal ordered form of
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}
is given as the sum over all possible non-attacking mixed rook placements, where for each placement we have a normal ordered summand. To take care of all such mixed placements, we sum over all possible types of placements, explaining the double sum in (
50
). For each placement of a fixed type, the structure of the normal ordered summand was determined in (
48
). The sum over all placements of a fixed type gives the combinatorial prefactor
m_{{\bf k}}(B_{\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}};q)
.
∎

Let us consider the case
s=1
of (
50
). Then
{\bf k}=(k_{0},k_{1})\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{2}(k)
means that
k_{0}+k_{1}=k
. Letting
t\equiv k_{0}
, one has
{\bf k}=(t,k-t)
and
\sum_{j=0}^{1}(j-1)k_{j}=-k_{0}=-t
. Hence, (
50
) reduces to

\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{|{\bf m}|-m_{1}}\sum_{t=0}^{\min(|{\bf n}|,k)}m_{t,k-t}(B_{\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}};q)\,Y^{|{\bf n}|-t}X^{|{\bf m}|-k},

recovering (
21
).

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
XY=qYX+\alpha_{\ell}Y^{\ell}
. Then (
50
) reduces to the
q
-deformed version of (

) given by Celeste et al.

.

Proof.

In the case under consideration, all rook placements are pure of degree

{\bf k}=(0,\ldots,0,k_{\ell},0,\ldots,0)
. From the set of weak compositions of

exactly one member remains,
(0,\ldots,0,k_{\ell}=k,0,\ldots,0)

reducing for the pure case to

\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{|{\bf m}|-m_{1}}\alpha_{\ell}^{k}r_{k}^{(\ell)}(B_{\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}})Y^{|{\bf n}|+(\ell-1)k}X^{|{\bf m}|-k},

which is exactly (

).
A pure placement of degree

of

Remark 4.11
.

As mentioned in the introduction, some basic normal ordering results for
{\mathcal{A}}_{f}(q)
were derived in

. For

Recall that the normal ordering coefficients of the word

have been considered in different cases:

•

): Stirling numbers of the second kind,
S(n,k)
, see (

•

Shift algebra (

, see (

•

Generalized Weyl algebra (
\alpha_{s}=1
): Generalized Stirling numbers

[

,

]
.

•

Ore algebra (
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=1
): Ore-Stirling numbers, see (

).

For all these numbers, a
q
-deformed version has been considered as well. In particular, the definition of the
q
-deformed Ore-Stirling numbers in (
28
) motivates the following definition.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
X
and
Y
satisfy the commutation relation (
44
) of the
q
-deformed polynomial Weyl algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{f}(q)
. Then the
q
-deformed polynomial Stirling numbers of degree
s
,
{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j,k)
, are defined as normal ordering coefficients of
(YX)^{n}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{f}(q)
,

(YX)^{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{ns}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j,k)Y^{j}X^{k}.

To see that
j
is at most
ns
note that the maximum is reached when, starting from
Y^{n}
, there are
(n-1)
placements of a rook of weight
s
, giving
Y^{n+(n-1)s}=Y^{ns}
. Before we can give a description of the
{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j,k)
, we introduce a partition of
{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(k)
into smaller pieces. Let us introduce, for
t=-k,\ldots,0,\ldots,(s-1)k
, the sets

{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(k|t)=\left\{{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(k)\,|\,\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}=t\right\}.

The minimal value
t=-k
is reached by the placement
{\bf k}=(k_{0}=k,0,\ldots,0)
while the maximal value
t=(s-1)k
is reached by the placement
{\bf k}=(0,0,\ldots,0,k_{s}=k)
. Thus, we have a partition

(52)

{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(k)=\bigcup_{t=-k}^{(s-1)k}{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(k|t).

Now, we can formulate the following result.

Proposition 4.13
.

The
q
-deformed polynomial Stirling numbers of degree
s
are given by

(53)

{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j,k)=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n-k|j-n)}m_{{\bf k}}(J_{n};q).

Proof.

Specializing (
50
) for the word
(YX)^{n}
and switching to
\ell=n-k
, one finds

(YX)^{n}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n-\ell)}m_{{\bf k}}(J_{n};q)\,Y^{n+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}}X^{\ell}.

Using the partition (
52
) and letting
j=n+t
, this yields

(YX)^{n}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{j=\ell}^{n+(s-1)(n-\ell)}\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n-\ell|j-n)}m_{{\bf k}}(J_{n};q)\,Y^{j}X^{\ell}.

Comparing with the definition of
{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j,k)
shows the assertion.
∎

Example 4.14
.

Let
s=1
. In (
53
),
{\bf k}\equiv(k_{0},k_{1})\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{2}(n-k|j-n)
means that
k_{0}+k_{1}=n-k
and
-k_{0}=j-n
. Thus, the sum reduces to one summand
{\bf k}=(n-j,j-k)
. Hence,

{\mathscr{S}}_{(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1});q}(n;j,k)=m_{n-j,j-k}(J_{n};q)=S_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1};q}(n;j,k),

recovering the
q
-deformed Ore-Stirling numbers
S_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1};q}(n;j,k)
according to (
29
).

One can extend Proposition
2.13
to the
q
-deformed polynomial Stirling numbers as follows.

Proposition 4.15
.

The
q
-deformed polynomial Stirling numbers of degree
s
satisfy the recurrence relation

(54)

{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n+1;j,k)=q^{j-1}{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j-1,k-1)+\sum_{r=0}^{s}\alpha_{r}[j-r]_{q}{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j-r,k).

Proof.

The proof is combinatorial and very similar to the one of Proposition
2.13
. Using (
53
) and the definition of the
m_{{\bf k}}(B;q)
, one has

{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n+1;j,k)=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n+1-k|j-n-1)}{\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}}\sum_{\phi\in{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf k}}(J_{n+1})}q^{\#\mbox{empty boxes}}.

Thus, we have to consider all mixed placements
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}(J_{n+1})
. As above, we write in an informal fashion
J_{n+1}=C_{n}\oplus J_{n}
. For each type
{\bf k}
, there are
(s+2)
types of placements of the rooks of the different weights on
J_{n+1}
:

•

Type
I
: Place all rooks on
J_{n}
and leave
C_{n}
empty.

•

Type
II_{r}
: Place one rook of weight
r
in
C_{n}
and the remaining rooks on
J_{n}
, for
r=0,\ldots,s
.

Thus, we can write

\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}(J_{n+1})=\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}^{I}(J_{n+1})\cup\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}^{II_{0}}(J_{n+1})\cup\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}^{II_{1}}(J_{n+1})\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}^{II_{s}}(J_{n+1}).

Let us start with placements of type
I
. Each such placement
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}^{I}(J_{n+1})
corresponds to a placement
\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}(J_{n})
. In
C_{n}
, one has for a placement of type
{\bf k}=(k_{0},\ldots,k_{s})
in total
n+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}
empty boxes. Here
{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n+1-k|j-n-1)
, i.e.,
\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}=(j-n-1)
. Hence, there are
n+(j-n-1)=j-1
empty boxes in
C_{n}
. If
\phi^{\prime}
has weight
\omega_{{\bf\alpha};q}(J_{n},\phi^{\prime})
then
\omega_{{\bf\alpha};q}(J_{n+1},\phi)=q^{j-1}\omega_{{\bf\alpha}}(J_{n},\phi^{\prime})
. Thus, the contribution of all placements of type
I
is given by

\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n-(k-1)|(j-1)-n)}{\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}}q^{j-1}\sum_{\phi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf k}}(J_{n})}q^{\#\mbox{empty boxes}}=q^{j-1}{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j-1,k-1).

Let us turn to placements of type
II_{r}
(for
r=0,\ldots,s
). For
{\bf k}=(k_{0},\ldots,k_{s})
, let us denote

{\bf k}^{(r)}\equiv(k_{0}^{(r)},\ldots,k_{s}^{(r)})=(k_{0}-\delta_{r,0},\ldots,k_{s}-\delta_{r,s}),

i.e., the
r
-th entry is reduced by one. One has
\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}^{(r)}=\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}-(r-1)
. Thus,

{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n+1-k|j-n-1)\Longrightarrow{\bf k}^{(r)}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n-k|j-n-r),

and one has
{\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}}=\alpha_{r}{\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}^{(r)}}
. If
\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}^{(r)}}(J_{n})
, then there are
n+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}^{(r)}=j-r
possible boxes to place the rook of weight
r
on
C_{n}
to get a placement
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}^{II_{r}}(J_{n+1})
. Placing the rook in the
\ell
-th possible row from above (where
\ell=1,\ldots,j-r
) will result in
(j-r-\ell)
additional empty boxes. Thus, the sum of the weights of these
j-r
placements
\phi
is given by
\alpha_{r}(1+q+\cdots+q^{j-r-1})\omega_{{\bf\alpha};q}(J_{n},\phi^{\prime})=\alpha_{r}[j-r]_{q}\omega_{{\bf\alpha};q}(J_{n},\phi^{\prime})
. The contribution of all placements of type
II_{r}
is, therefore, given by

\sum_{{\bf k}^{(r)}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n-k|j-r-n)}\alpha_{r}{\bf\alpha}^{{\bf k}^{(r)}}[j-r]_{q}\sum_{\phi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf k}^{(r)}}(J_{n})}q^{\#\mbox{empty boxes}}=\alpha_{r}[j-r]_{q}{\mathscr{S}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j-r,k).

Summing over the types
I
and
II_{r}
, for
r=0,\ldots,s
, yields the assertion.
∎

As extension of the
q
-deformed Ore-Lah numbers defined in(
32
), we define the
q
-deformed polynomial Lah numbers of degree

,
{\mathscr{L}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j,k)
, as normal ordering coefficients of
(Y^{2}X)^{n}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{f}(q)
,

(Y^{2}X)^{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{(s+1)n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\mathscr{L}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j,k)Y^{j}X^{k}.

Recalling that the word
(Y^{2}X)^{n}
outlines the Lah board
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
, one can show in the same way the following analogues of (

) and (

).

Proposition 4.16
.

The
q
-deformed polynomial Lah numbers of degree
s
are given by

(55)

{\mathscr{L}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j,k)=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n-k|j-2n)}m_{{\bf k}}({\mathcal{L}}_{n};q).

They satisfy the recurrence relation

(56)

{\mathscr{L}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n+1;j,k)=q^{j-2}{\mathscr{L}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j-2,k-1)+\sum_{r=0}^{s}\alpha_{r}[j-r-1]_{q}{\mathscr{L}}_{{\bf\alpha};q}(n;j-r-1,k).

Proof.

Since the proofs are so similar to the ones for the polynomial Stirling numbers, we give only a sketch. To show (
55
), we specialize (
50
) for the word

(Y^{2}X)^{n}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathcal{W}C}_{s+1}(n-\ell)}m_{{\bf k}}({\mathcal{L}}_{n};q)\,Y^{2n+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}}X^{\ell}.

Using the partition (

Using the same arguments as above for deriving the recurrence relation of the
q
-deformed polynomial Stirling numbers, we can give derive an extension of the result given by Celeste et al.
[
11

for the

-deformed rook numbers
R_{s,h;q}[B_{n}(\lambda),k]
to the recurrence relation for the
q
-deformed mixed placement numbers. For this, we consider the Ferrers board
B_{\lambda_{n-1}\lambda_{n-2}\cdots\lambda_{1}\lambda_{0}}
, i.e., we label the columns from right to left, and the first column from the right has
\lambda_{0}
boxes, while the first column to the left has
\lambda_{n-1}
boxes. If we let
\lambda=\lambda_{n-1}\lambda_{n-2}\cdots\lambda_{1}\lambda_{0}
be the associated partition, we denote the above board also as
B_{n}(\lambda)
, and
B_{n-1}(\lambda)=B_{\lambda_{n-2}\cdots\lambda_{0}}
.

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
{\bf k}\equiv(k_{0},\ldots,k_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s+1}
. The
q
-deformed mixed placement numbers
m_{{\bf k}}(B_{n}(\lambda);q)
satisfy the following recursive formula

\displaystyle m_{{\bf k}}(B_{n}(\lambda);q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}}m_{{\bf k}}(B_{n-1}(\lambda);q)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=0}^{s}\alpha_{r}\left[\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}-(r-1)\right]_{q}m_{{\bf k}^{(r)}}(B_{n-1}(\lambda);q),

where
{\bf k}^{(r)}=(k_{0}-\delta_{r,0},\ldots,k_{s}-\delta_{r,s})
and only summands with
{\bf k}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s+1}
contribute. For
{\bf k}={\bf 0}
, one has
m_{{\bf 0}}(B_{n}(\lambda);q)=q^{|\lambda|}
, where
|\lambda|=\lambda_{n-1}+\cdots+\lambda_{0}
denotes the length of the partition
\lambda
.

Proof.

Exactly as in the proof of Proposition
4.15
, we write
B_{n}(\lambda)=C_{\lambda_{n-1}}\oplus B_{n-1}(\lambda)
and we partition the set of placement of rooks on
B_{n}(\lambda)
into those of type
I
(all rooks are placed in
B_{n-1}(\lambda)
and the column
C_{\lambda_{n-1}}
is left empty), and those of type
II_{r}
(for
r=0,\ldots,s
) where one rook of weight
r
is placed in
C_{\lambda_{n-1}}
and the remaining rooks are placed in
B_{n-1}(\lambda)
. Since for each placement of type
I
there are
(\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j})
empty boxes in
C_{\lambda_{n-1}}
the contribution of all placements of type
I
is given by

q^{\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}}m_{{\bf k}}(B_{n-1}(\lambda);q).

Consider a placement of type
II_{r}
(for
r=0,\ldots,s
). For
{\bf k}=(k_{0},\ldots,k_{s})
, we write as above
{\bf k}^{(r)}\equiv(k_{0}^{(r)},\ldots,k_{s}^{(r)})=(k_{0}-\delta_{r,0},\ldots,k_{s}-\delta_{r,s})
. If
\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}^{(r)}}(B_{n-1}(\lambda))
, then there are
\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}^{(r)}=\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}-(r-1)
possible boxes to place the rook of weight
r
on
C_{\lambda_{n-1}}
to get a placement
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bf k}}^{II_{r}}(B_{n}(\lambda))
. Placing the rook in the
\ell
-th possible row from above (where
\ell=1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}-(r-1))
will result in
(\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}-(r-1)-\ell)
additional empty boxes. Thus, the sum of the weights of these
\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}-(r-1)
placements
\phi
is given by
\alpha_{r}[\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}-(r-1)]_{q}\omega_{{\bf\alpha};q}(B_{n-1}(\lambda),\phi^{\prime})
. The contribution of all placements of type
II_{r}
is, therefore, given by

\alpha_{r}[\lambda_{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{s}(j-1)k_{j}-(r-1)]_{q}\,m_{{\bf k}^{(r)}}(B_{n-1}(\lambda);q).

Summing over the types
I
and
II_{r}
, for
r=0,\ldots,s
gives the asserted recurrence. The initial value
m_{{\bf 0}}(B_{n}(\lambda);q)=q^{|\lambda|}
follows directly from Definition
4.8
.
∎

For a pure type
{\bf k}=(0,\ldots,0,k_{\ell},0,\ldots,0)
, one has
m_{0,\ldots,0,k_{\ell},0,\ldots,0}(B_{n}(\lambda);q)=R_{\ell,\alpha_{\ell};q}[B_{n}(\lambda),k_{\ell}]

4.19
reduces to

\displaystyle R_{\ell,\alpha_{\ell};q}[B_{n}(\lambda),k_{\ell}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\lambda_{n-1}+(\ell-1)k_{\ell}}R_{\ell,\alpha_{\ell};q}[B_{n-1}(\lambda),k_{\ell}]

\displaystyle+\alpha_{\ell}[\lambda_{n-1}+(\ell-1)(k_{\ell}-1)]_{q}R_{\ell,\alpha_{\ell};q}[B_{n-1}(\lambda),k_{\ell}-1],

which is exactly the result given by Celeste et al.
[
11
, Theorem 7]
. Specializing to
q=1

[
15
, Equation (3)]
.

5.
Conclusion

In this paper, we considered normal ordering in the
q
-deformed Ore algebra. Mixed placements of rooks and files were defined and it was shown that the normal ordering coefficients of arbitrary words are given as mixed placement numbers of rooks and files. The corresponding
q
-deformed Ore-Stirling and Ore-Lah numbers were introduced and studied. Furthermore, we gave an expression for the normal ordered binomial formula in the
q
-deformed Ore algebra. The
q
-deformed Ore algebra can be considered as the case
s=1
of the
q
-deformed polynomial Weyl algebra (while the case
s=0
corresponds to the conventional
q
-deformed Weyl algebra). We discussed how the definitions and results can be extended from the
q
-deformed Ore algebra to the
q
-deformed polynomial Weyl algebra. For this, we introduced
q
-deformed mixed placement numbers and showed that the normal ordering coefficients of arbitrary words in the
q
-deformed polynomial algebra are given in terms of these
q
-deformed mixed placement numbers. We introduced and studied the associated
q
-deformed polynomial Stirling and Lah numbers in detail. Since the cases
s=0
(Weyl algebra) and
s=1
(Ore algebra) have been studied explicitly, it might be interesting to consider the case
s=2
– where
XY-qYX=\alpha_{0}I+\alpha_{1}Y+\alpha_{2}Y^{2}
– in more detail, since the relation
XY-qYX=\alpha_{2}Y^{2}
is the defining relation for the
q
-deformed Jordan plane, a well-known object. This will be the subject of further study.
