Entropy stable finite difference schemes for One-Fluid Two-Temperature Euler Non-equilibrium Hydrodynamics
Chetan Singh, Harish Kumar

Abstract
In this work, we consider the One-Fluid Two-Temperature Euler (OFTT-Euler) equations used for modeling non-equilibrium hydrodynamics. The model comprises a system of nonlinear hyperbolic partial differential equations with non-conservative products. The model decomposed the total pressure into two scalar components: one for electrons and one for ions. Our aim in this work is to design entropy-stable finite difference numerical schemes for the model. This is achieved by reformulating the equations such that the reformulated non-conservative part does not contribute to the entropy. Then, we design higher-order entropy-conservative numerical schemes by using Tadmor’s relation for the conservative part and higher-order central differences for the non-conservative parts. Finally, we design the entropy-dissipation terms using the entropy-scaled right eigenvectors of the conservative part, thereby deriving the entropy inequality for the entire system. We present several test cases in one and two dimensions to demonstrate the accuracy and stability of the proposed schemes.

3
Entropy analysis and reformulation

The entropy
\mathcal{E}
and the entropy flux
\mathbf{q}_{d}
for the OFTT-Euler system (
4
), are given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=-\rho s,\qquad\mathbf{q}_{d}=-\rho v_{d}s,\qquad~~s=\frac{1}{(\gamma_{e}-1)}\ln\left(\dfrac{p_{e}}{\rho^{\gamma_{e}}}\right)+\frac{1}{(\gamma_{i}-1)}\ln\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{\rho^{\gamma_{i}}}\right),

(7)

where,
d\in\left\{x,y\right\}
. We now have the following result:

Lemma 3.1
.

For a smooth solution of the OFTT-Euler system, we have the following equality,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}s+v_{x}\partial_{x}s=0,

which results in the entropy equality,

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}+\partial_{x}\mathbf{q}_{x}=0.

(8)

Proof.

From system (
1
), we have,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+v_{x}\partial_{x}\rho+\rho\partial_{x}v_{x}=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{e}+v_{x}\partial_{x}p_{e}+p_{e}\partial_{x}v_{x}+(\gamma_{e}-1)p_{e}\partial_{x}v_{x}=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{i}+v_{x}\partial_{x}p_{i}+p_{i}\partial_{x}v_{x}+(\gamma_{i}-1)p_{i}\partial_{x}v_{x}=0.

Using these, we get,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}s=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(\gamma_{e}-1)p_{e}}\left\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,1}v_{x}\partial_{x}p_{e}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}p_{e}\partial_{x}v_{x}+(\gamma_{e}-1)p_{e}\partial_{x}v_{x}}\right\}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(\gamma_{i}-1)p_{i}}\left\{{\color[rgb]{0.01,1,0.48}v_{x}\partial_{x}p_{i}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}p_{i}\partial_{x}v_{x}+(\gamma_{i}-1)p_{i}\partial_{x}v_{x}}\right\}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\gamma_{e}}{(\gamma_{e}-1)\rho}+\frac{\gamma_{i}}{(\gamma_{i}-1)\rho}\right)\left\{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0,0}v_{x}\partial_{x}\rho}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\rho\partial_{x}v_{x}}\right\},

and

\displaystyle v_{x}\partial_{x}s=

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,1}\frac{v_{x}\partial_{x}p_{e}}{(\gamma_{e}-1)p_{e}}}+{\color[rgb]{0.01,1,0.48}\frac{v_{x}\partial_{x}p_{i}}{(\gamma_{i}-1)p_{i}}}-{\color[rgb]{0.68,0,0}\left(\frac{\gamma_{e}v_{x}\partial_{x}\rho}{(\gamma_{e}-1)\rho}+\frac{\gamma_{i}v_{x}\partial_{x}\rho}{(\gamma_{i}-1)\rho}\right)}.

Adding them results in,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}s+v_{x}\partial_{x}s=0

Combining this with mass conservation results in (
8
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

In two dimensions, the above entropy equality (
8
) is,

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}+\partial_{x}\mathbf{q}_{x}+\partial_{y}\mathbf{q}_{y}=0,

(9)

and for non-smooth solutions, we have the entropy inequality,

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}+\partial_{x}\mathbf{q}_{x}+\partial_{y}\mathbf{q}_{y}\leq 0.

(10)

4
Semi-discrete numerical schemes

The reformulated OFTT-Euler equation (
12
) are suitable for designing entropy stable finite difference numerical schemes. We start by considering a uniform mesh of size
(\Delta x,\Delta y)
with cell centers at the grid points,
(x_{i},y_{j}),\;\;0\leq i\leq n_{x}
and
0\leq j\leq n_{y}.
The vertices of the cells are given by
(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}},y_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
with
x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{x_{i}+x_{i+1}}{2}
and
y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{y_{j}+y_{j+1}}{2}.

A semi-discrete finite difference numerical scheme for the system (

\displaystyle\frac{d\textbf{U}_{i,j}}{dt}=-\frac{\boldsymbol{f}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\boldsymbol{f}_{x,i-\frac{1}{2},j}}{\Delta x}-\frac{\boldsymbol{f}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{f}_{y,i,j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta y}-\textbf{C}_{x}(\textbf{U}_{i,j})\left(\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial x}\right)_{i,j}-\textbf{C}_{y}(\textbf{U}_{i,j})\left(\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial y}\right)_{i,j}.

(16)

Here, the numerical fluxes
\boldsymbol{f}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}

\boldsymbol{f}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
are consistent with the continuous fluxes
\textbf{f}_{x}
and

, respectively. Additionally,
\left(\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial x}\right)_{i,j}
and
\left(\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial y}\right)_{i,j}
are discretized using central difference approximations of appropriate order. We begin by developing higher-order entropy conservative schemes for system (

).

\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}_{x}(\textbf{U}_{l},\textbf{U}_{r})=\left[\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)},\tilde{f}_{x}^{(2)},\tilde{f}_{x}^{(3)},\tilde{f}_{x}^{(4)},\tilde{f}_{x}^{(5)}\right]^{\top}.

Our aim to construct an entropy conservative flux
\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}_{x}
that satisfied the identity

[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!]\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}_{x}=[\![\mathcal{F}_{x}]\!].

(19)

We first change the expression for jump in
[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!],
to expresses it in jumps
[\![\rho]\!],[\![v_{x}]\!],[\![v_{y}]\!],[\![\beta_{e}]\!],[\![\beta_{i}]\!]
as

[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!]=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2}{\rho^{\ln}}[\![\rho]\!]+\frac{1}{(\gamma_{i}-1)}\dfrac{[\![\beta_{i}]\!]}{{\beta}^{\ln}_{i}}-\bar{\beta}_{e}\left(\bar{v}_{x}[\![v_{x}]\!]+\bar{v}_{y}[\![v_{y}]\!]\right)+\left[\frac{1}{(\gamma_{e}-1){\beta}^{\ln}_{e}}-\frac{\overline{|v|^{2}}}{2}\right][\![\beta_{e}]\!]\\
\bar{\beta}_{e}[\![v_{x}]\!]+\bar{v}_{x}[\![\beta_{e}]\!]\\
\bar{\beta}_{e}[\![v_{y}]\!]+\bar{v}_{y}[\![\beta_{e}]\!]\\
-[\![\beta_{e}]\!]\\
\frac{[\![\beta_{e}]\!]-[\![\beta_{i}]\!]}{\gamma_{i}-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Then
[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!]\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}_{x}
can be written as,

\displaystyle[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!]\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}_{x}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)}}{{\rho}^{\ln}}[\![\rho]\!]+\left(-\bar{v}_{x}\bar{\beta}_{e}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)}+\bar{\beta}_{e}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(2)}\right)[\![v_{x}]\!]

\displaystyle+\left(-\bar{v}_{y}\bar{\beta}_{e}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)}+\bar{\beta}_{e}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(3)}\right)[\![v_{y}]\!]+\left(\frac{\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)}}{(\gamma_{i}-1){\beta}^{\ln}_{i}}-\frac{\tilde{f}_{x}^{(5)}}{(\gamma_{i}-1)}\right)[\![\beta_{i}]\!]

\displaystyle+\Bigg[\left(\frac{1}{(\gamma_{e}-1){\beta}^{\ln}_{e}}-\frac{\overline{|v|^{2}}}{2}\right)\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)}+\bar{v}_{x}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(2)}+\bar{v}_{y}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(3)}-\tilde{f}_{x}^{(4)}+\frac{\tilde{f}_{x}^{(5)}}{(\gamma_{i}-1)}\Bigg][\![\beta_{e}]\!].

(20)

Also, the right hand side of (
19
), simplifies to,

\displaystyle[\![\mathcal{F}_{x}]\!]=

\displaystyle[\![2\rho v_{x}]\!]=2\bar{v}_{x}[\![\rho]\!]+2\bar{\rho}[\![v_{x}]\!].

(21)

By equating to jumps
[\![\rho]\!],[\![v_{x}]\!],[\![v_{y}]\!],[\![\beta_{e}]\!],
and
[\![\beta_{i}]\!]

20
) and (

), we get,

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)}=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\rho}^{\ln}\bar{v}_{x},~~\tilde{f}_{x}^{(2)}=\frac{2\bar{\rho}}{\bar{\beta}_{e}}+\bar{v}_{x}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)},~~\tilde{f}_{x}^{(3)}=\bar{v}_{y}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)},~~\tilde{f}_{x}^{(5)}=\frac{\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)}}{{\beta}^{\ln}_{i}},

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{x}^{(4)}=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{(\gamma_{e}-1){\beta}^{\ln}_{e}}-\frac{\overline{|v|^{2}}}{2}\right]\tilde{f}_{x}^{(1)}+\bar{v}_{x}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(2)}+\bar{v}_{y}\tilde{f}_{x}^{(3)}+\frac{\tilde{f}_{x}^{(5)}}{{\beta}^{\ln}_{i}}.

4.2
Higher-order entropy-stable schemes

The schemes defined in Section
4.1
are designed to conserve entropy. However, in the presence of the discontinuities, it is necessary to have appropriate entropy decay; otherwise, the numerical solution will contain undesirable numerical oscillations. Following
52
, we modify the numerical flux to include dissipation terms as,

\displaystyle{\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\frac{1}{2}\textbf{D}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j},~\quad{\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\textbf{D}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!]_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}.

(26)

The diffusion matrices
\textbf{D}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}
and
\textbf{D}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
are symmetric positive definite and are based on Rusanov’s type diffusion operators, given by,

\textbf{D}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\tilde{R}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\Lambda_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\tilde{R}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{\top}~\text{and}~\textbf{D}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\tilde{R}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{R}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\top}.

(27)

Here,
\tilde{R}_{d},~d\in\left\{x,y\right\}
are the matrices of entropy-scaled right eigenvectors (see
5
) presented in Appendix
C
. Also, the matrices are
{\Lambda_{d}}
are diagonal matrices, given by,

{\Lambda_{d}}=\left(\max_{\lambda\in\tilde{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{d}}|\lambda|\right)\mathbf{I}_{5\times 5},

where,
\tilde{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{d}
is the set of eigenvalues of the flux Jacobian
\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{d}}{\partial\textbf{U}}
given in Appendix
C
.

Thus, the numerical scheme (
16
) with the modified flux (
26
) is entropy stable. However, the first-order jump terms
[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}
and
[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!]_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
restricts the overall accuracy to first order. Replacing these jumps with higher-order polynomial reconstructions improves accuracy but does not guarantee entropy stability. To address this issue, we follow the approach proposed in
26
and describe the process in
x-
direction. Same process is followed for reconstruction in
y-
direction. Let us introduce
scaled entropy variables

\mathcal{W}^{\pm}_{x,i,j}=\tilde{R}^{{\top}}_{x,i\pm\frac{1}{2},j}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{i,j}.

Using ENO reconstruction procedure, we reconstruct
\mathcal{W}^{\pm}_{x,i,j}
with the
k^{th}
-degree polynomials
\mathcal{P}^{\pm}_{x,i,j}(x)
and define,

\hat{\mathcal{W}}_{x,i,j}^{\pm}=\mathcal{P}^{\pm}_{x,i,j}(x_{i\pm\frac{1}{2}}).

Using these values, the reconstructed
[\![\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}
of
k
-th order is defined as,

[\![\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}]\!]^{k}_{x,i,j}=\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}^{-}_{x,i+1,j}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}^{+}_{x,i,j}.

where

\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}^{\pm}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\left\{\tilde{R}^{{\top}}_{x,i\pm\frac{1}{2},j}\right\}^{(-1)}\hat{\mathcal{W}}_{x,i,j}^{\pm}.

Following
26
, we define the higher-order entropy stable numerical flux in the
x
-direction as follows:

{\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}}^{k}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\,=\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{2p}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\textbf{D}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}[\![\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}]\!]_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{k},

(28)

where,
k
denotes the order of the scheme and
p\in\mathbb{N}
is given by

p=\begin{cases}\frac{k}{2},&\textrm{if }k~\text{is even,}\\
\frac{k+1}{2},&\textrm{if }k~\text{is odd}.\end{cases}

To ensure the entropy stability of the modified flux, the reconstruction procedure for
\mathcal{W}
must satisfy the sign-preserving property, which is a sufficient condition for ensuring that the numerical flux (
28
) is entropy-stable. For the
2^{nd}
-order scheme, the MinMod reconstruction is used to ensure this property. For higher-order schemes i.e.
3^{rd}
-order
(k=3)
and
4^{th}
-order
(k=4)
schemes, we employ ENO-based reconstruction
27
, which ensures
sign-preserving property
of the reconstruction procedure. Following the same procedure in
y
-direction, we can define a higher numerical flux in the
y
-direction as follows:

{\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}}^{k}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\,=\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{2p}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\textbf{D}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}[\![\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}]\!]_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{k}.

(29)

Now we have the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

The semi-discrete scheme (
16
) with entropy stable fluxes (
28
), (
29
) and with
2^{nd}
-order (for
k=2
) and
4^{th}
-order (for
k=3,4
) central difference approximations to approximate
\left(\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial x}\right)_{i,j}
and
\left(\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial y}\right)_{i,j},
is
k^{th}
-order accurate and entropy stable, i.e. it satisfies,

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(\textbf{U}_{i,j})+\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{k}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{k}_{x,i-\frac{1}{2},j}\right)+\frac{1}{\Delta y}\left(\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{k}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{k}_{y,i,j-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq 0,

(30)

where
\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{k}_{x}
and
\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{k}_{y}
are given by,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{k}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\tilde{\mathbf{q}}^{2p}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{\top}\textbf{D}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}[\![\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}]\!]^{k}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}

and

\mathbf{q}_{x}

\mathbf{q}_{y},

7
Conclusion

In this work, we have designed higher-order entropy-stable finite difference schemes for the One-Fluid Two-Temperature Euler Non-equilibrium Hydrodynamics system, which are a set of hyperbolic PDEs with non-conservative terms. We show that the presence of the non-conservative terms results in non-symmetrizability of the systems. To design entropy-stable numerical schemes, we present a novel reformulation of the equations so that the conservative part is symmetrizable and the non-conservative part does not contribute to the entropy evolution. Finally, an entropy-stable discretization for the new conservative part is proposed, which results in the entropy stability of the complete discretization. We have presented extensive numerical results to demonstrate the accuracy and entropy stability of the proposed numerical schemes.

) do not contribute to the entropy evolution, i.e., the coefficient matrices
\textbf{C}_{x}(\textbf{U})
and
\textbf{C}_{y}(\textbf{U})
satisfy,

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\textbf{C}_{x}(\textbf{U})=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\textbf{C}_{y}(\textbf{U})=0.

Proof.

We compute
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot C_{i}
for
i=\{1,2,3,4,5\}
, where
C_{i}
are the columns of the matrix
\textbf{C}_{x}(\textbf{U})
. We get,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot C_{1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot\left\{0,-\frac{(\gamma_{e}-1)|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}}{2},0,-\left(\frac{(\gamma_{e}-1)|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{p_{i}-p_{e}}{\rho}\right)v_{x},-\frac{(\gamma_{i}-1)p_{i}v_{x}}{\rho}\right\}

\displaystyle=-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{(\gamma_{e}-1)|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}}{2}\left(\beta_{e}v_{x}\right)}-\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{(\gamma_{e}-1)|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}}{2}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{p_{i}-p_{e}}{\rho}}\right)v_{x}\left(-\beta_{e}\right)-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{(\gamma_{i}-1)p_{i}v_{x}}{\rho}\left(\frac{\beta_{e}-\beta_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot C_{2}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot\left\{0,(\gamma_{e}-1)v_{x},0,(\gamma_{e}-1)v_{x}^{2}+\frac{p_{i}-p_{e}}{\rho},\frac{(\gamma_{i}-1)p_{i}}{\rho}\right\}

\displaystyle={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\gamma_{e}-1)v_{x}\left(\beta_{e}v_{x}\right)}+\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\gamma_{e}-1)v_{x}^{2}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{p_{i}-p_{e}}{\rho}}\right)\left(-\beta_{e}\right)+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{(\gamma_{i}-1)p_{i}}{\rho}\left(\frac{\beta_{e}-\beta_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot C_{3}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot\left\{0,(\gamma_{e}-1)v_{y},0,(\gamma_{e}-1)v_{x}v_{y},0\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot C_{4}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot\left\{0,-(\gamma_{e}-1),0,-(\gamma_{e}-1)v_{x},0\right\}=0,

and

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot C_{5}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\cdot\left\{0,\left(\frac{\gamma_{e}-1}{\gamma_{i}-1}+1\right),0,\left(\frac{\gamma_{e}-1}{\gamma_{i}-1}+1\right)v_{x},0\right\}=0.

Hence
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\textbf{C}_{x}(\textbf{U})=0
. Similarly, we can easily prove that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}^{\top}\textbf{C}_{y}(\textbf{U})=0
.
∎

Appendix B
Non-symmetrizability of OFTT-Euler system

Here, we investigate symmetrization of the OFTT-Euler system. Let us recall,

Definition B.1
.

The conservation law

\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{x}}{\partial x}=0,

(34)

is said to be symmetrizable if there exists a change of variable
\textbf{U}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}
which symmetrizes it, i.e., (
34
) with a change of variable can be written as,

\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}{\partial{t}}+\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{x}}{\partial\textbf{U}}\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}{\partial{x}}=0.

where
\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}
is a symmetric positive definite matrix. Furthermore,
\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{x}}{\partial\textbf{U}}\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}
is symmetric matrices.

We also have the following results from
[
28
]
:

Theorem B.1
.

The conservative system have a strictly convex entropy
\mathcal{E}(\textbf{U})
if and only if the system is symmetrizable.

Remark B.1
.

The conservative part of the OFTT-Euler system is symmetrizable because it satisfies,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{x}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}=\bigg(\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{x}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}}\bigg)^{\top}.

We now consider the complete OFTT-Euler system in one dimension, i.e.

\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{x}}{\partial x}+\textbf{C}_{x}(\textbf{U})\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial x}=0.

(35)

where
\textbf{U},~\textbf{f}_{x}
and
\textbf{C}_{x}(\textbf{U})
are defined in (
3.1
). Following
[
56
]
, to prove the symmetrizability of the system, we need to prove that the matrix

\mathcal{H}(\textbf{U})=\left(\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{x}}{\partial\textbf{U}}+\textbf{C}_{x}(\textbf{U})\right)\frac{\partial\textbf{U}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}},

is symmetric. However, a lengthy calculation shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\textbf{U})-\mathcal{H}(\textbf{U})^{\top}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{p_{e}-p_{i}}{2}&0&\frac{(p_{e}-p_{i})v_{x}}{2}&0\\
\frac{p_{i}-p_{e}}{2}&0&\frac{(p_{i}-p_{e})v_{y}}{2}&\Gamma&-\frac{(p_{e}-p_{i})p_{i}(-1+2\gamma_{i})}{2\rho}\\
0&\frac{(p_{e}-p_{i})v_{y}}{2}&0&\frac{(p_{e}-p_{i})v_{x}v_{y}}{2}&0\\
\frac{(p_{i}-p_{e})v_{x}}{2}&-\Gamma&\frac{(p_{i}-p_{e})v_{x}v_{y}}{2}&0&-\frac{(p_{e}-p_{i})p_{i}v_{x}(-1+2\gamma_{i})}{2\rho}\\
0&\frac{(p_{e}-p_{i})p_{i}(-1+2\gamma_{i})}{2\rho}&0&\frac{(p_{e}-p_{i})p_{i}v_{x}(-1+2\gamma_{i})}{2\rho}&0\end{pmatrix}.

with
\Gamma=\frac{(p_{e}-p_{i})\left(-2p_{e}\left(\gamma_{i}-1\right)+\left(\gamma_{e}-1\right)\left(2p_{i}\left(1-2\gamma_{i}\right)+\rho\left(v_{x}^{2}-v_{y}^{2}\right)\left(\gamma_{i}-1\right)\right)\right)}{4\rho\left(\gamma_{e}-1\right)\left(\gamma_{i}-1\right)}
. Which is not zero. Hence, the system in not symmetriable. However, we note that when
p_{e}=p_{i}
the matrix
\mathcal{H}(\textbf{U})
is symmetric, which is expected as in that case, the OFFT-Euler system reduces to compressible Euler equations.
