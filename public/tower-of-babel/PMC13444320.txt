Modal contrast engineering in ultraviolet and visible metalenses enabled by material-selective hybridization
Authors: Junhwa Seong, Youngsun Jeon, Geongu Han, Sangwon Lee, Younghwan Yang, Seokwoo Kim, Chihun Lee, Joohoon Kim, Jaekyung Kim, Minsu Jeong, Jaebum Noh, Jihwan An, Junsuk Rho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMetasurfaces enable ultrathin optics through nanoscale light control, yet extending high-efficiency operation from the ultraviolet (UV) to visible has been constrained by material and design limitations. Here, we present a material-selective hybrid metalens platform in which a structurally invariant SiO2 skeleton is conformally coated with nanometer-thick oxide layers, producing high-efficiency metalenses in distinct spectral windows without geometric modification. Low-temperature atomic layer annealing at 80 °C crystallizes TiO2 to raise its refractive index for visible operation while preserving amorphous ZrO2 for UV transparency. Ultrathin coatings establish the modal birefringence required for polarization conversion, yielding average efficiencies of 69% (250–400 nm) and 75% (400–700 nm), with diffraction-limited focusing across the bandwidth. We demonstrate a compact real-scene UV camera revealing sunscreen coverage, authentication markers, and biological structures for dermatology, materials inspection, and forensics, alongside a lightweight full-color virtual-reality eyepiece. These results establish hybrid metasurfaces as scalable, manufacturing-compatible alternatives to bulky refractive optics.Subject terms: Composites, Polymers, Metamaterials, Nanophotonics and plasmonics, Surface patterning

Introduction

Metasurfaces provide nanoscale control over optical wavefronts through sub-wavelength structuring, enabling ultra-compact alternatives to conventional refractive and diffractive optics1,2. By encoding phase, amplitude, and polarization into planar arrays of scatterers, these ultrathin optical elements have advanced applications ranging from hyperspectral imaging and polarimetry3–8 to emerging consumer technologies, such as augmented and virtual reality (AR/VR) displays9–15. However, translating metasurfaces into practical devices requires simultaneously addressing three critical challenges: broadband operation, high optical efficiency, and scalable fabrication compatible with existing lithographic infrastructure16–18.

Visible-spectrum metalenses have achieved high efficiencies using high-refractive-index dielectrics, such as titanium dioxide (TiO2)19–22 and gallium nitride (GaN)23–25. However, these materials require thick ( > 500 nm) films deposited via atomic layer deposition (ALD) and high-aspect-ratio, multi-step etching, complicating fabrication and limiting yield26,27. Extension to the ultraviolet (UV) regime, essential for sterilization, photolithography, and materials characterization, confronts a fundamental materials trade-off. While high refractive index (n > 2) enables efficient phase modulation, high-index dielectrics exhibit prohibitive absorption at UV wavelengths due to band-edge effects and electronic transitions28–30. Amorphous oxides, such as hafnium oxide (HfO2) and zirconium oxide (ZrO2), offer reduced absorption31–33 and have enabled ALD-based metalenses34,35 operating in the UV. Nevertheless, these approaches retain fully high-index architectures, for which achieving the large, uniform film thicknesses required for efficient UV operation remains fabrication-intensive. Consequently, a practical challenge persists in realizing scalable, high-efficiency meta-optics that can be adapted across both UV and visible spectral bands within a single, unified fabrication strategy.

Silicon dioxide (SiO2) enables a fundamentally different strategy. Rather than optimizing refractive index, SiO2 structures serve dual roles: as functional optical elements and as master templates for nanoimprint replication or material infiltration36. This approach leverages deep-UV to infrared transparency of SiO2, allowing optical operation concurrent with its use as a scalable fabrication intermediate37. The trade-off is explicit: SiO2’s low refractive index (n ≈ 1.46) induces weak optical contrast, limiting phase accumulation per unit height. Recent studies have demonstrated that pure SiO2 metasurfaces can achieve full 2π phase coverage under suitably optimized geometries38,39; nevertheless, the intrinsically weak optical contrast of SiO2 imposes practical trade-offs on achievable strong birefringent response. Consequently, SiO2-only metalenses have largely been restricted to spectral regimes, vacuum-UV ( < 200 nm)40, where SiO2 benefits from an exceptionally low extinction coefficient, and the red-visible ( > 600 nm)41,42, where lithographic resolution requirements are less stringent. Hybrid designs combining low-index materials with high-index coatings have demonstrated improved performance43–46, yet remain spectrally narrow due to the competing requirements on structural and material optimization across broad wavelength ranges.

Here, we overcome these limitations with hybrid metalenses that combine the fabrication simplicity of SiO2 metasurfaces with the optical performance of high-index dielectrics. The platform is built on a single, structurally invariant SiO2 nanopillar etched into a fused silica substrate as the skeleton. These structures are then coated conformally with nanometer-thick ZrO2 (UV operation) or TiO2 (visible operation) via ALD (Fig. 1a). Crucially, we employ atomic layer annealing (ALA) at the exceptionally low temperature of 80 °C during deposition to crystallize these coatings in situ without high-temperature post-annealing. This low-temperature process not only increases the refractive index but also maintains compatibility with temperature-sensitive substrates and large-area processing. The central design principle is that the conformal coating material functions as an independent spectral-tuning parameter: the identical SiO2 skeleton supports high-efficiency operation in either the UV or visible regime purely through the choice of coating, with no modification to the underlying structural geometry. We demonstrate two 5-mm-aperture metalenses on this basis: a ZrO2-coated (10 nm) UV device and a TiO2-coated (20 nm) visible device, exhibiting average polarization conversion efficiencies of 69% over 250–400 nm and 75% over 400–700 nm, respectively. These devices are further applied to two representative real-world demonstrations: a compact real-scene UV camera and a full-color near-eye VR display. These demonstrations establish hybrid metalenses as a scalable platform for both scientific instrumentation and consumer electronics.

Results

Material-selective crystallization for wavelength-tailored optimization

High-efficiency hybrid metalenses require high-refractive-index coatings to achieve higher birefringence of meta-atoms for broadband operation. Post-deposition annealing at > 400 °C can crystallize these films and increase their refractive index, but such temperatures exceed the thermal stability limits of electron-beam resists (Tg ≈ 90 °C for ZEP520A) and nanoimprint resins (Tg <100 °C), precluding the use of high-temperature processing in scalable manufacturing workflows47–49. Low-temperature ALA addresses this constraint while enabling in-situ crystallization at 80 °C through argon/oxygen plasma exposure after each deposition cycle (Methods and Fig. 1b), while simultaneously providing a route to wavelength-specific material optimization within a unified, low-temperature process. For TiO2, ALA increases the refractive index from n = 2.2 (amorphous) to n = 2.4 (crystalline) at 532 nm (mid-visible), approaching the performance of films annealed at 400 °C (Fig. 1c and Supplementary Figs. 1–3). X-ray diffraction (XRD) reveals rutile-phase reflections near 2θ ≈ 27.45° and 54.32°, demonstrating successful low-temperature crystallization (Fig. 1d). The resulting increase in refractive index strengthens the optical contrast of TiO2 coatings in the visible regime. ZrO2, by contrast, remains predominantly amorphous under the same ALA conditions, with minimal refractive index change (Δn < 0.05). This behavior is specifically advantageous for UV operation: amorphous ZrO2 exhibits sufficiently high refractive index for effective phase modulation while maintaining comparatively low absorption loss at UV wavelengths.

We note that crystalline TiO2 can exhibit intrinsic birefringence depending on crystal orientation. However, in the present thin film coatings, the film is poly-crystalline rather than a single oriented crystal, so any intrinsic crystal-axis birefringence is expected to be spatially averaged. Accordingly, the birefringence discussed here is dominated by the geometry-dependent modal response of the hybrid meta-atom.

Design of single-geometry hybrid meta-atoms

Rather than engineering broadband operation through the multi-pixel strategy, requiring large meta-atom libraries21, we achieve it through material hybridization of a single, fixed geometry. To achieve this, we construct the skeleton meta-atom from SiO2, a low-loss, UV-visible transparent dielectric that is directly compatible with standard complementary metal-oxide semiconductor (CMOS) fabrication. SiO2 enables high-throughput, wafer-scale processing; however, its low refractive index ( ~ 1.46) limits phase accumulation, restricting optical efficiency. To overcome this, we introduce a hybrid architecture: SiO2 skeleton conformally coated with nanometer-thick high-index layers via ALD (Fig. 2a). We employ ZrO2 coatings for UV operation and TiO2 for visible wavelengths. In each case, the hybrid structure functions as an efficient half-wave plate element within its corresponding spectral window, enabling geometric-phase control50 through a structurally invariant SiO2-based design.

We performed parametric simulations of cross-polarization transmittance (CPT), a metric for polarization conversion efficiency, across structural dimensions (width: 40–140 nm, length: 105–220 nm, height: 400–1000 nm) and coating thickness (0–25 nm) to identify optimal geometries (Methods). From this design space, we identify a single common geometry (width: 50 nm, length: 200 nm, height: 900 nm) that supports high efficiency at both 320 nm (76.0% with 10 nm ZrO2) and 532 nm (81.9% with 20 nm TiO2) (Fig. 2b, c). For the visible device, 532 nm was selected as the primary design wavelength because it lies near the center of the visible regime and provides a balanced starting point for multi-wavelength operation. CPT maps were further examined at 450 nm and 635 nm to confirm that the chosen geometry maintains robust efficiency across all three RGB wavelengths (Supplementary Fig. 4); the final geometry represents a practical compromise that preserves multi-wavelength performance rather than maximizing efficiency at a single wavelength37. Without coating, the same SiO2 structures achieve only below 15% efficiencies, confirming that nanometer-thick high-index layers alone are sufficient to enable high-performance operation. Efficiency scales monotonically with coating thickness, saturating near 10 nm at 320 nm and 20 nm at 532 nm (Fig. 2d). Notably, the superior visible-regime performance reflects the higher refractive index of crystalline TiO2 compared to as-deposited amorphous films (Supplementary Fig. 5).

Broadband simulations across the full design space reveal average conversion efficiencies of approximately 69% in the UV (250–400 nm) and 75% in the visible (400–700 nm), with a peak of 95.7% at 490 nm for the optimized structure (Supplementary Figs. 6, 7). Simulated electric field distributions reveal significantly enhanced light confinement within the high-index coating of hybrid structures compared to bare SiO2 (Fig. 2e, f), directly explaining the improved polarization conversion efficiency through increased optical path length in the high-index material. The shared SiO2 skeleton simplifies the overall fabrication workflow by preserving a common structural backbone across both device implementations, with spectral adaptation achieved entirely through the choice of conformal coating material.

Enhanced modal contrast driven by sidewall coatings

To elucidate the physical mechanism underlying the conversion efficiency enhancement, we isolated the contributions of sidewall versus top/bottom coatings. We compared two configurations: a fully coated entire structure (ALD on all surfaces) and a sidewall-only structure (ALD confined to vertical facets) (Fig. 3a). Remarkably, across all coating thicknesses, the sidewall-only structure matches or exceeds the efficiency of the fully coated design for both ZrO2 at 320 nm and TiO2 at 532 nm (Fig. 3b, c). These results demonstrate that the sidewall coatings are the dominant contributor to efficiency enhancement, functioning by selectively modulating the effective indices (neff) of orthogonal polarization modes. Subsequently, we computed the effective refractive indices for transverse electric (TE) and transverse magnetic (TM) modes via rigorous eigenmode analysis (Methods). The resulting Δneff values of 0.140 at 320 nm and 0.250 at 532 nm are close to the ideal retardation conditions, but do not fully satisfy the requirement for maximum efficiency (Supplementary Fig. 8). This discrepancy reflects the fact that the present geometry was selected to balance birefringent retardation, broadband transmission, and fabrication compatibility rather than to maximize Δneff alone; global optimization strategies, such as particle swarm optimization (PSO)51 could further close this gap. In a geometric-phase, however, such non-ideal birefringence primarily reduces the polarization conversion efficiency, because the encoded phase profile itself is set by the in-plane rotation of each meta-atom. In contrast, the same-geometry bare SiO2 meta-atoms exhibit Δneff values of only 0.05 at 320 nm and 0.04 at 532 nm, directly explaining their low conversion efficiencies of 14.5 and 3.2% at the respective wavelengths. This case serves as a fixed-geometry reference to isolate the coating contribution, rather than as a fully optimized SiO2-only benchmark. When SiO2 structures are independently optimized over a broader parameter space, performance improves substantially, reaching 76.2% at 320 nm and 57.6% at 532 nm, but only with significantly larger periods and taller pillars (period = 350 nm, height = 3.0 µm) (Supplementary Figs. 9, 10). While pure SiO2 can therefore approach moderate-to-high efficiency under such conditions38, the hybrid approach achieves comparable or superior performance within a far more compact geometry.

Electric field simulations reveal how side wall coatings create this modal index contrast (Fig. 3d–g). In hybrid structures, TM-polarized modes concentrate within the high-index sidewall coatings, while TE modes distribute across the x-oriented surfaces, creating strong spatial separation between orthogonal polarizations (Fig. 3d, e). Quantitatively, this selective confinement increases the mode-averaged optical intensity within high-index regions by 4–5× relative to bare SiO2, where fields leak into the surrounding air due to weak index contrast (Fig. 3f, g). This enhanced mode confinement directly accounts for the >7-fold efficiency improvement observed in coated versus uncoated structures, establishing the sidewall coating geometry as the critical design parameter.

Diffraction-limited metalenses across the UV-visible spectrum

To validate the hybrid meta-atom design experimentally, we fabricated two 5-mm-diameter metalenses, each comprising approximately 400 million elements: a ZrO2-coated (10 nm) UV metalens and a TiO2-coated (20 nm) visible metalens. In both cases, the underlying SiO2 skeletons were patterned via electron-beam lithography and reactive ion etching, then conformally coated with high-index layers (Methods and Supplementary Fig. 11). To demonstrate scalability, we also replicated the master template via nanoimprint lithography and achieved comparable efficiencies across the UV-visible range (Supplementary Figs. 12-14), confirming manufacturability for high-volume production. We designed 5-mm-diameter metalenses with a numerical aperture (NA) of 0.2 and a design wavelength of 532 nm, yielding a focal length of 12.25 mm (Supplementary Fig. 15). The required hyperbolic phase profile52 was encoded through geometric phase modulation, where each meta-atom is rotated (0–180°) to impart the target phase shift via the geometric phase. Due to chromatic dispersion inherent to geometric phase metalenses, the effective focal lengths and effective NA shift to 20.4 mm, 0.12 (320 nm), 14.5 mm, 0.17 (450 nm), and 10.2 mm, 0.24 (635 nm). We characterized focal spot quality using back focal plane imaging at λ = 320, 450, 532, and 635 nm (Methods and Supplementary Fig. 16). The 320 nm focal measurement was obtained using the ZrO2-coated UV metalens, while the 450, 532, and 635 nm measurements were obtained using the TiO2-coated visible metalens. At all measured wavelengths, each device produces diffraction-limited or near-diffraction-limited focal intensity distributions at its respective design wavelength (Fig. 4a, b). The measured full-width-at-half-maximum (FWHM) values are 1.482, 1.516, 1.455, and 1.813 µm, respectively, compared to the diffraction limit of 1.37 µm (0.514λ/NA). The Strehl ratios range from 0.70 (635 nm) to >0.85 (320–532 nm), confirming high-quality focusing in the UV and visible demonstrations. The slight broadening at longer wavelengths likely arises from residual aberrations and non-uniform illumination, addressable through further optimization of the design and fabrication. While UV wavelengths typically pose sampling challenges due to Nyquist limitations, the 250 nm period used here is sufficiently small to satisfy the Nyquist criterion for the demonstrated metalens parameters, including NA = 0.12 at the shortest measured wavelength of 320 nm53.

We further quantified imaging performance through modulation transfer function (MTF) analysis (Methods). The cut-off frequency remains consistently at 752 lp/mm across all wavelengths, with measured MTF curves closely matching theoretical predictions (Supplementary Fig. 17). Resolution target imaging confirms that the metalens resolves 2.19 µm line pairs at both UV and visible wavelengths (Fig. 4c), consistent with the diffraction limit for NA = 0.2. Measured focusing efficiencies, defined as the fraction of incident optical power collected within the focal region of the fabricated metalens, range from 68.8% to 74.9% across 320–635 nm, in close agreement with simulations (Supplementary Figs. 6, 7) and confirming that the hybrid coating architecture maintains high performance across the entire UV-visible bandwidth.

Compact UV camera and near-eye VR display

We demonstrate the platform’s versatility through two applications spanning UV to visible wavelengths: a compact camera for real-scene UV imaging and a lightweight eyepiece for near-eye VR displays. For the UV camera, a 5-mm ZrO2-coated metalens is integrated with a compact CMOS sensor (Fig. 5a). Images of flowers, balls, and human skin exhibit high contrast and reveal UV-specific features invisible to standard RGB cameras (Fig. 5b). Direct comparison with smartphone images shows UV-specific contrast: flower petals display nectar guides, and sunscreen coverage on balls and skin appears as dark regions. The demonstrated imaging performance is directly relevant to dermatology (sunscreen efficacy testing), materials inspection (polymer identification), and forensic analysis (trace detection). In parallel, the visible-regime TiO2-coated metalens functions as an eyepiece for near-eye VR projection (Fig. 5c). Paired with a full-color organic light-emitting diode (OLED) microdisplay, the system renders text and complex images across blue, green, and red channels without auxiliary chromatic correction optics (Fig. 5d and Supplementary Fig. 18). The RGB images were acquired at their respective focal distances, demonstrating multi-wavelength visible image formation with the TiO2-coated metalens. The primary advantage of metalenses for VR applications lies in their compact and lightweight form factor. Conventional VR headsets require substantial focal distances between the display panel and imaging optics, resulting in bulky enclosures consisting largely of space. The transmissive metalens eyepiece presented here circumvents these spatial constraints, enabling a significantly reduced device footprint.

These demonstrations illustrate the practical versatility of the hybrid platform. The identical SiO2 skeleton geometry, when conformally coated with ZrO2, enables efficient UV imaging; when coated with crystalline TiO2, it supports broadband visible operation, both achieving focusing efficiencies of approximately 70% without any modification to the underlying structure. This material-selective strategy provides a scalable manufacturing pathway for wavelength-agnostic metasurface optics in consumer electronics, wearables, and optical instrumentation.

Discussion

We have demonstrated a material-selective hybrid metalens platform built on a structurally invariant SiO2 skeleton, realized through two device implementations targeting the UV and visible regimes. The UV and visible devices exhibit average polarization conversion efficiencies of 69% (250–400 nm) and 75% (400–700 nm), respectively, sharing a common geometric design and remaining compatible with scalable nanoimprint-based fabrication. Our scalable fabrication strategy compares favorably with conventional metalens fabrication approaches across key evaluation metrics (Supplementary Table 1). By combining low-temperature ALA at 80 °C with wavelength-tailored material selection, this platform achieves spectral adaptation across distinct wavelength regimes through coating choice alone, without redesigning the underlying structural geometry. Critically, the efficiency enhancement originates primarily from nanoscale sidewall coatings that create the modal birefringence required for polarization conversion, a mechanism we validated through rigorous eigenmode analysis and direct field visualization. The scalably manufactured metalenses exhibit focusing efficiencies of 69.9%, 70.9%, 74.9%, and 68.8% at 320, 450, 532, and 635 nm, respectively. Beyond the fundamental advances in metasurface design, this work demonstrates a compact real-scene UV imaging system based on meta-optics. While previous metasurface studies have reported UV focusing and resolution-target imaging54, real-scene UV imaging in a compact meta-optical format remains comparatively unexplored. Our UV camera, constructed with a 5-mm ZrO2-coated metalens, captures images of flowers, balls, and human skin that reveal features invisible to conventional visible-light cameras, including sunscreen coverage patterns critical for dermatological assessment and invisible authentication markers used in security applications. This establishes metalenses as viable components for portable UV instrumentation in materials inspection, forensic analysis, and biomedical diagnostics. In parallel, the TiO2-coated metalens functions as a lightweight eyepiece for VR displays, maintaining diffraction-limited image quality across RGB channels. Together, these demonstrations span applications from scientific instrumentation to consumer electronics, illustrating the breadth of the platform.

The material-selective design principle introduced here extends beyond the UV-visible range demonstrated in this work. The approach is directly applicable to other spectral regimes where conformal high-index coatings are available, including the near-infrared (silicon coatings on silica skeletons) and mid-infrared (germanium or chalcogenide coatings), suggesting a general framework for designing wavelength-agnostic metasurface optics. Near-term improvements include PSO or inverse design methods to further minimize the deviation between achieved and theoretical modal birefringence, potentially increasing efficiencies55–57. The fabrication workflow, based on a single electron-beam lithography master followed by nanoimprint replication and selective coating, is compatible with high-volume manufacturing and could enable mass-producible flat optics for smartphones, wearable devices, and AR/VR systems. Looking forward, integrating the hybrid platform with active tuning mechanisms, such as phase-change materials or liquid crystals, could yield dynamically reconfigurable metalenses that adapt their focal properties in real time58–60. Furthermore, this approach could be extended to 3D nanostructures, offering additional degrees of freedom for advanced wavefront control and multifunctional optical responses61,62. We believe that these directions collectively position hybrid metasurfaces as a scalable and versatile foundation for addressing longstanding challenges in compact, broadband optical systems across diverse wavelength ranges and application domains.

Methods

Plasma-induced ALA process

For the deposition of TiO2 and ZrO2 using ALD, a capacitively coupled plasma-type ALD system (iOV n100, iSAC research) was utilized. Tetrakis(dimethylamido) titanium(IV) (TDMA-Ti, Ti(NMe2)4) and Tetrakis(ethylmethylamido) zirconium(IV) (TEMA-Zr, Zr(NEtMe)4) served as the precursors for the TiO2 and ZrO2 deposition, with the precursor canister maintained at a temperature of 60 °C and 70 °C. The chamber temperature during the deposition process was set at 80 °C, and the oxidation process pressure was maintained at 600 mTorr. The PEALD process ALA involved a sequence of precursor pulses for 1.5 s, followed by a 6 s Ar precursor purge, 3 s O2 plasma exposure, and a 3 s Ar oxidant purge. For the process with ALA, after completing the ALD cycle, there was a 20 s pressure stabilization followed by a 30 s ALA step. In the ALA process, the gas flow rates were set at 90 sccm for Ar and 50 sccm for O2, with the process gas pressure fixed at 400 mTorr, and the exposure time consistently set at 30 s.

Numerical simulations

Cross-polarization transmittance and electric field distributions were computed using finite element method simulations (COMSOL Multiphysics 6.1) with periodic boundary conditions and perfectly matched layers. Refractive index values for simulations were obtained from ellipsometry measurements of deposited films. Additional field distribution calculations employed rigorous coupled-wave analysis implemented in MATLAB. Unit-cell simulations in Figs. 2 and 3 were performed under periodic boundary conditions to extract the local electromagnetic response of the hybrid meta-atom within the local periodic approximation (LPA). Although the fabricated geometric-phase metalens is not strictly periodic, as adjacent meta-atoms carry spatially varying in-plane rotation angles, the LPA is well justified for the present device, which operates at modest numerical apertures (NA = 0.12 at 320 nm; NA = 0.20 at 532 nm) with a deeply subwavelength lattice period (250 nm). To explicitly quantify the effect of neighboring-orientation variation, we performed additional supercell simulations comprising eight meta-atoms uniformly sampling the full 0°–180° rotation range. These show that inter-element coupling reduces the conversion efficiency by only 3–4% in absolute terms (73.0% at 320 nm; 78.1% at 532 nm) while preserving continuous and accurate phase modulation, confirming the practical validity of the LPA-based analysis for the demonstrated device geometry.

Fabrication method for 5-mm-diameter metalens

The fabrication begins with spin-coating an electron-beam resist (ZEP-520A, Zeon) onto a fused silica substrate, followed by high-speed electron-beam lithography (ELS-BODEN, ELIONIX) to define the metasurface pattern. The exposed resist serves as a mask for sequential deposition (100 nm thick Cr mask, KVT, KVE-ENS4004) and dry etching steps. The Cr mask was removed in Cr etchant (ETCR-400, APCT), yielding the core SiO2 nanostructures of the hybrid meta-atoms. Subsequently, a high-refractive-index thin film is conformally deposited onto the metasurface skeletons using plasma-enhanced ALD, integrated with a low-temperature ALA process. In parallel, the pre-ALD patterned SiO2 skeleton was also used to fabricate a soft mold for nanoimprint lithography (NIL).

Optical characterization

For visible, RGB laser sources (CPS635, CPS532, CPS450, Thorlabs) are fiber-coupled and subsequently collimated. The collimated beams are passed through a linear polarizer (LP) (LPVISE050-A, Thorlabs), followed by a quarter-wave plate (QWP) (AQWP05M-600, Thorlabs), converting the initially unpolarized light into circularly polarized light. An aperture is subsequently used to spatially confine the beam, matching the beam size to the diameter of the metalens. After passing through the metalens, the converted circularly polarized light is focused on its focal plane. The focused lights are collected using a 20× objective lens (LUCPLFLN20X, Olympus). Also, the non-converted polarized lights are analyzed using QWP and LP placed in the optical path. Only focused lights are directed through a tube lens (TTL180-A, Thorlabs) and captured by an sCMOS camera (pco.panda 4.2 bi UV, Excelitas Technologies). The optical components positioned behind the metalens are aligned and mounted on a motorized translation stage, allowing high-precision control. To characterize the imaging performance, all other optical components remained fixed, while the positions of the objective and tube lens were exchanged. A USAF 1951 resolution target is placed between them, serving as the object to evaluate the imaging performance of the metalens.

For UV, a 320 nm laser source is collimated by a 4 f system with two plano-convex lenses (LA4052-UV-ML, LA4380-UV-ML, Thorlabs). The same configuration as used in the visible is employed, with all components replaced by UV-compatible optical components. The UV-compatible optical components used in the modified setup are as follows: LP (GLB10-UV, Thorlabs), QWP (AHWP05M-340, Thorlabs), objective lens (LMU-15X-UVB, Thorlabs), Tube lens (LA4855-UV-ML, Thorlabs).
