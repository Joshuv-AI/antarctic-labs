Regularized Brascamp-Lieb inequalities via Optimal Transport and Study of Equality Cases
Bader Ammari

Abstract
. We consider regularized Brascamp-Lieb inequalities using the theory of optimal transportation, more precisely an anisotropic version of Caffarelli’s contraction theorem. Furthermore, we provide a full picture concerning the issues of finiteness of the Brascamp-Lieb constant and of the existence of Gaussian extremizers. Finally, we find all optimizers for these regularized Brascamp-Lieb inequalities by employing heat flow methods that were already used to settle this question for the non-regularized Brascamp-Lieb inequality, and give some interesting applications.



Mathematics Subject Classification:
26D15, 52A40, 49Q20, 35K05

Keywords:
Brascamp-Lieb inequality, Log-convexity/concavity, Gaussian extremizer, Caffarelli’s contraction, Heat flow

1.
Introduction

Forward Brascamp-Lieb inequalities provide a natural generalization to many important inequalities as the inequalities of Hölder, Young convolution or Loomis-Whitney and are nowadays very well-understood. In addition, applications given by Brascamp-Lieb (BL) inequalities may be found in harmonic analysis (see
[
6
]
in the context of Fourier restriction theory), analytic number theory (see
[
23
]
), convex geometry (see
[
1
,
2
]
where the author studies estimates for the volume of sections of the unit ball of
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\|\cdot\|_{p})
as well as the volume ratio), probability, and much more.

The first crucial result that made the study of Brascamp-Lieb inequalities flourish is the following observation of Gaussian saturation by Lieb in
[
25
]
:

Theorem 1.1

(Lieb
[
25
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\ni\mathcal{Q}\geq 0
,
C_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
surjective linear transformations for
i=1,\cdots,m
and
p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}>0.
Then, the largest constant
C
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(C_{i}x)^{p_{i}}\,dx\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}f_{i}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}

holds for all non-negative functions
f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}
is also the largest constant such that the inequality holds for centered Gaussian functions and is usually called the Brascamp-Lieb constant.

From this work, two main approaches have emerged:

The first is based on heat flow methods and was first introduced in
[
14
]
. Using a monotonicity formula for positive solutions to heat equations in multilinear settings, which was inspired by
[
14
]
,
[
5
]
established the necessary conditions for the Brascamp-Lieb constant to be finite or for its value to be attained. In addition, Valdimarsson in
[
34
]
settled the question of the form of functions that give equality when it is known that the Brascamp-Lieb constant is attained.

The second method has its roots in optimal transport. The pioneering papers of Brenier and McCann (see
[
12
,
26
]
) led to the well-known Brenier’s theorem which enabled identifying the optimal transport map between absolutely continuous measures in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the gradient of a convex function. These works also hinted at the connection between optimal transportation theory and functional/geometric inequalities. In Euclidean spaces, many classical inequalities can then be derived or redemonstrated using Brenier maps combined with the change of variables formula for convex gradients. The forward Brascamp-Lieb inequality was one of the first inequalities to which this strategy was applied in
[
4
]
and, surprisingly, one even gets a dual inequality which is now known as the reverse Brascamp-Lieb inequality.

Our goal in this work is to give a complete study of a regularized version of the forward Brascamp-Lieb inequalities in the sense that a log-convexity/log-concavity condition will be imposed on the inputs. Regularizing functional inequalities by restricting the inputs to a smaller subclass of functions is a natural procedure whose main advantage consists in obtaining an improved form of the inequality. This viewpoint is often adopted in areas such as convex geometry, differential geometry, or information theory. Among them, let us mention three examples (for more references, see
[
8
]
). In
[
21
]
, the constant of the log-Sobolev inequality is improved thanks to a regularization in terms of the Poincaré constant. In
[
7
]
, deficit estimates for Nelson’s hypercontractivity, the logarithmic Sobolev inequality and Talagrand’s cost inequality are given under the assumptions of log-subharmonicity, log-convexity and log-concavity. Notice that the hypercontractivity is proven via Fokker-Planck flows which behave well with respect to log-subharmonicity while Talagrand’s cost inequality is proven by an optimal transport argument inspired by
[
19
]
.
Finally in the works of
[
31
,
30
]
(see also
[
18
,
28
,
29
]
), the authors manage to first prove an inverse Brascamp-Lieb inequality under centering, log-concavity and log-convexity assumptions. At the limit, it then retrieves the inverse Brascamp-Lieb inequality of
[
3
]
without non-degeneracy conditions, for arbitrary centered log-concave inputs, which allows proving the Gaussian correlation inequality for centered convex sets. In addition, we refer the reader to
[
27
]
for the beautiful resolution of the strong Gaussian correlation conjecture by the Forward-Reverse Brascamp-Lieb inequality for centered log-concave functions.

Let us explain the main results in this work. We first manage to prove regularized Brascamp-Lieb inequalities and their dual (see
Proposition

2.4
) using an anisotropic extension of Caffarelli’s contraction theorem and a strategy similar to the proof in
[
4
]
of the classical forward Brascamp-Lieb inequality.
This generalizes the works of
[
33
]
and
[
5
]
where type
G
functions,
i.e.
convolutions with
N(0,G^{-1})
, are considered.
Then, we find necessary and sufficient conditions for the finiteness of a regularized Brascamp-Lieb constant (see
Theorem

3.2
) and show that extremizable Brascamp-Lieb data are also Gaussian extremizable (see
Theorem

3.12
). Finally, using the heat flow approach employed in
[
5
]
, we define a notion of generalized geometric Brascamp-Lieb data to which any extremizable Brascamp-Lieb datum is equivalent (see
Proposition

3.21
) and prove that extremizers of such data have a very specific form.

This work is organized as follows. In the first section, we prove two regularized versions of Brascamp-Lieb inequalities along with their dual reverse inequalities using arguments from the optimal transportation theory. In the second section, we focus on establishing finiteness conditions on the Brascamp-Lieb constant and a satisfactory description of the Brascamp-Lieb datum for which extremizers or Gaussian extremizers exist, thus generalizing the main results of
[
5
]
. The third section is devoted to the study of equality cases for extremizable Brascamp-Lieb data and generalizes the work of
[
34
]
. Finally, some applications are provided in the last section and a general Caffarelli contraction is proven in the appendix, where an extension of the results of
[
20
]
is also included, as it is an important tool for the proof of the regularized Brascamp-Lieb inequalities.

2.
Proof of Regularized Brascamp-Lieb Inequalities

We will consider regularized Brascamp-Lieb inequalities on Euclidean spaces,
i.e.
a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space
H
endowed with the usual Lebesgue measure
dx.

All functions below will be assumed to be non-negative.

Given a positive definite transformation
A:H\rightarrow H
, we denote

g_{A}(x)=\exp(-\pi\langle Ax,x\rangle)

for
x\in H.
We say
0<A\leq B
for
A,B:H\rightarrow H
two positive definite transformations iff
B-A
is positive semi-definite.
Moreover, let
C_{i}:H\rightarrow H_{i}
surjective linear transformations for
i=1,\cdots,m
and
p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}>0.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
f:H\rightarrow[0;+\infty)
is log-concave (resp. log-convex) if
\log(f):H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is concave (resp. convex). Given a positive semi-definite
Q:H\rightarrow H
, we say that
f
is more log-concave (resp. more log-convex) than
g_{Q}
if
f=g_{Q}h
for some log-concave (resp. log-convex) function
h.

Remark 2.2
.

For a smooth function
f
,
f
is more log-concave (resp. more log-convex) than
g_{Q}
iff

\mathrm{Hess}(-\log f)\geq 2\pi Q\;(\text{resp. }\leq).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}:H\rightarrow H
be a positive semi-definite transformation,
I\subset\{1,\cdots,m\},Q_{i}:H_{i}\rightarrow H_{i}
be positive definite transformations for any
1\leq i\leq m.
If
f_{i}\in L^{1}(H_{i})
is more log-convex than
g_{Q_{i}}
for any
i\in I
and
f_{j}\in L^{1}(H_{j})
is more log-concave than
g_{Q_{j}}
for any
j\in I^{c}
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(C_{i}x)^{p_{i}}\,dx}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{i}}f_{i}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}}\\
\leq\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<B_{i}\leq Q_{i}\text{ for all }i\in I\\
B_{j}\geq Q_{j}\text{ for all }j\in I^{c}\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}g_{B_{i}}(C_{i}x)^{p_{i}}\,dx}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{i}}g_{B_{i}}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<B_{i}\leq Q_{i}\text{ for all }i\in I\\
B_{j}\geq Q_{j}\text{ for all }j\in I^{c}\end{subarray}}\Bigl\{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\mathcal{Q}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}C_{i})}\Bigl\}^{1/2}.\end{array}

By duality, if
g_{i}
is more log-concave than
g_{Q_{i}^{-1}}
for any
i\in I
and
g_{j}
is more log-convex than
g_{Q_{j}^{-1}}
for any
j\in I^{c}
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{H}g_{\mathcal{Q}^{-1}}*\sup\Bigl\{\prod_{j=1}^{m}g_{j}(y_{j})^{p_{j}}:\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}y_{j}=x,y_{j}\in H_{j}\Bigl\}\,dx}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{i}}g_{i}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\geq\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{i}\geq Q_{i}^{-1}\text{ for all }i\in I\\
0<B_{j}\leq Q_{j}^{-1}\text{ for all }j\in I^{c}\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{H}g_{\mathcal{Q}^{-1}}*\sup\Bigl\{\prod_{j=1}^{m}g_{B_{j}}(y_{j})^{p_{j}}:\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}y_{j}=x,y_{j}\in H_{j}\Bigl\}\,dx}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{i}}g_{B_{i}}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\hskip 23.49976pt\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<B_{i}\leq Q_{i}\text{ for all }i\in I\\
B_{j}\geq Q_{j}\text{ for all }j\in I^{c}\end{subarray}}\Bigl\{\frac{\det(\mathcal{Q}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}C_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i})^{p_{i}}}\Bigl\}^{1/2}\end{array}

where

f*g(x)=\sup_{y\in H}f(y)g(x-y)

and
g_{\mathcal{Q}^{-1}}
is understood for
\mathcal{Q}=0
to be equal to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle 1\text{ if }y=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr 0\text{ if }y\neq 0.\end{array}\right.

Considering
I=\varnothing
and
I=\{1,\cdots,m\}
gives the following proposition.

.

g_{j}

, then

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{H}g_{\mathcal{Q}^{-1}}*\sup\Bigl\{\prod_{j=1}^{m}g_{j}(y_{j})^{p_{j}}:\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}y_{j}=x,y_{j}\in H_{j}\Bigl\}\,dx}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{i}}g_{i}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}}\\

If
f_{i}\in L^{1}(H_{i})

for any

(2.3)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(C_{i}x)^{p_{i}}\,dx}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{i}}f_{i}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\leq\displaystyle\sup_{B_{i}\geq Q_{i}}\frac{\displaystyle\int e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}g_{B_{i}}(C_{i}x)^{p_{i}}\,dx}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{i}}g_{B_{i}}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}}=\sup_{B_{i}\geq Q_{i}}\Bigl\{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\mathcal{Q}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}C_{i})}\Bigl\}^{1/2}.\end{array}

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\geq\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{i}\geq Q_{i}^{-1}\text{ for all }i\in I\\

By approximation for matrices, the previous statements are proven for any

We follow the strategy of Valdimarsson’s proof (see

]
).

Proof of

.

3.
Finiteness and Structure of Regularized Brascamp-Lieb Inequalities

Now that the desired inequalities have been proved, it is natural to ask when the regularized Brascamp-Lieb constants are finite or even whether they are attained by a family of transformations
B_{i}:H_{i}\rightarrow H_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,m.

For simplicity, we will only write the conditions of finiteness (or existence of Gaussian extremizers) for the Brascamp-Lieb constant of
Equation

2.1
. In fact, it suffices to flip the signs to get the equivalent conditions for
Equation

2.3
. By convention, we will denote
n=\dim(H),n_{i}=\dim(H_{i})
for any
1\leq i\leq m.

Definition 3.1
.

We define

\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})=\sup_{0<B_{i}\leq Q_{i}}\Bigl\{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}C_{i})}\Bigl\}^{1/2}

where

\textbf{C}=\Bigl(H,(H_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m},(C_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}\Bigl)

with
H,H_{1},\cdots,H_{m}
Euclidean spaces and for each
j
,
C_{j}:H\rightarrow H_{j}
linear transformations (with
C
non-degenerate
i.e.

C_{i}
surjective and
\bigcap\ker(C_{i})=\{0\}
),
p=(p_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
and
\textbf{Q}=(Q_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
with
Q_{i}:H_{i}\rightarrow H_{i},Q_{i}\geq 0.
Similarly, one may define

\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})=\sup_{0<B_{i}\leq Q_{i}}\Bigl\{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\mathcal{Q}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}C_{i})}\Bigl\}^{1/2}.

In the latter,
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})
(if
\mathcal{Q}=0
, we will write
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
) will be said to be a generalized Brascamp-Lieb datum and
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})
a generalized Brascamp-Lieb constant.

The conditions of finiteness can immediately be treated for the case
\mathcal{Q}>0
. The supremum may be extended continuously to semi-definite
B_{i}
and as a supremum over a compact interval, it is finite and attained. More precisely,

\sup_{0<B_{i}\leq Q_{i}}\Bigl\{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\mathcal{Q}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}C_{i})}\Bigl\}^{1/2}=\max_{0\leq B_{i}\leq Q_{i}}\Bigl\{\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\mathcal{Q}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}C_{i})}\Bigl\}^{1/2}<+\infty.

Thus, an extremiser exists in the range
0\leq B_{i}\leq Q_{i}
but the expression vanishes when
\det(B_{i})=0
for some
i
and hence at the extremum, one has
B_{i}>0
for all
1\leq i\leq m.

If
\mathcal{Q}=0
, the sufficient and necessary conditions for the supremum in
Equation

2.1
to be finite can be formulated as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

The regularized BL constant
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is finite if and only if for any subspace
V\subset H
,

(3.1)

\dim(V)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}V).

Remark 3.3
.

In fact, if
\mathcal{Q}\geq 0
and is not positive definite,

\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})<+\infty

if and only if for any subspace
V\subset\ker(\mathcal{Q}),

\dim(V)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}V).

Indeed, the supremum may be extended to
0\leq B_{i}\leq Q_{i}
with
B_{i}|_{H_{i}/(C_{i}\ker(\mathcal{Q}))}>0
and then, we can apply the same arguments that will be presented on
\ker(\mathcal{Q})
where obviously
\mathcal{Q}=0.

Proof.

We can prove easily the direction
\Longrightarrow.
Indeed, take
V
to be any subspace of
H,0<\varepsilon<1.
Let us define
A^{(\varepsilon)}=(A^{(\varepsilon)}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
with

A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\mathrm{id}_{C_{j}V}\,\oplus\,\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathrm{id}_{(C_{j}V)^{\perp}}\hskip 11.74988pt\text{ with }\lambda>\frac{1}{\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\lambda_{\text{min}}(Q_{j})}.

By the choice of
\lambda
and as
0<\varepsilon<1
,
0<A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}\leq Q_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq m.
It is clear that
\det(A_{j})
decays like
\lambda^{-n_{j}}\varepsilon^{\dim(C_{j}V)}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and

\det\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}^{*}A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}C_{j}\Bigl)\text{ decays at least as fast as }\lambda^{-n}\varepsilon^{\dim(V)}

since
\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}^{*}A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}C_{j}
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
and when restricted to
V
, decays linearly in
\varepsilon
. Thus, as
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})<+\infty
, we conclude that

\lambda^{(n-\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j})/2}\varepsilon^{(\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}V)-\dim(V))/2}<+\infty\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

which means that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}V)\geq\dim(V).

∎

To prove the opposite direction, some additional work needs to be done. We recall a very important definition to be able to study finiteness and extremizers.

Definition 3.4
.

We say that a subspace
V
of
H
is critical for a generalized Brascamp-Lieb datum
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
if

\dim(V)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}V).

For the following lemma,
[
5
, Definition 4.2]
is employed. In addition, when writing
\textbf{Q}|_{V}
, we mean that
\textbf{Q}|_{V}=(Q_{i}|_{C_{i}V})_{1\leq i\leq m}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum and let
V_{c}
be a critical subspace. Then,
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
satisfies
Equation

3.1
if and only if both
(\textbf{C}_{V_{c}},p,\textbf{Q}|_{V_{c}})
and
(\textbf{C}_{H/V_{c}},p,\textbf{Q}|_{H/V_{c}})
obey
Equation

3.1
.

Proof.

On the one hand, suppose that
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
satisfies
Equation

3.1
. Then, it is clear that
(\textbf{C}_{V_{c}},p,\textbf{Q}|_{V_{c}})
obeys
Equation

3.1
by restricting the spaces
V\subseteq H
to subspaces of
V_{c}.
Let now
V
be a subspace of
H/V_{c}.
We notice that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\dim(V)&=&\dim(V+V_{c})-\dim(V_{c})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}(V+V_{c}))-\dim(V_{c})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}(\dim(C_{j}V+C_{j}V_{c})-\dim(C_{j}V_{c}))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j,H/V_{c}}V)\end{array}

as desired. On the other hand, assume that
(\textbf{C}_{V_{c}},p,\textbf{Q}|_{V_{c}})
and
(\textbf{C}_{H/V_{c}},p,\textbf{Q}|_{H/V_{c}})
obey
Equation

3.1
. For any
V\subseteq H
, writing
U=V\cap V_{c},W=(V+V_{c})/V_{c}
, one has

\begin{array}[]{lll}\dim(V)&=&\dim(V\cap V_{c})+\dim(V+V_{c})-\dim(V_{c})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\dim(U)+\dim(W)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j,V}U)+p_{j}\dim(C_{j,H/V}W)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}U)+p_{j}(\dim(C_{j}W+C_{j}V_{c})-\dim(C_{j}V_{c}))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\Bigl[\dim(C_{j}U)+\dim(C_{j}V+C_{j}V_{c})-\dim(C_{j}V_{c})\Bigl]\end{array}

since
W+V_{c}=V+V_{c}.
As

\dim(C_{j}U)=\dim(C_{j}(V\cap V_{c}))\leq\dim(C_{j}V\cap C_{j}V_{c})

and

\dim(C_{j}V\cap C_{j}V_{c})+\dim(C_{j}V+C_{j}V_{c})-\dim(C_{j}V_{c})=\dim(C_{j}V),

we see that
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
satisfies
Equation

3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is a generalized Brascamp-Lieb datum, then

\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})\leq\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{V})\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{H/V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{H/V}).

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{V})
and
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{H/V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{H\setminus V})
are finite. Take
f=(f_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
with
f_{i}
more log-convex than
g_{Q_{i}}
for any
1\leq i\leq m.
We may normalize
\int_{H_{j}}f_{j}=1.
The goal is to show that

\int_{H}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(f_{j}\circ C_{j})^{p_{j}}\,dx\leq\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{V})\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{H/V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{H/V}).

By Fubini-Tonelli, the left-hand side can be rewritten as

\int_{V^{\perp}}\Bigl(\int_{V}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(f_{j}\circ C_{j})^{p_{j}}(v+w)\,dv\Bigl)\,dw.

However, one can write
f_{j}\circ C_{j}(v+w)=f_{j,w}\circ C_{j,V}(v)
where
f_{j,w}:C_{j}V\rightarrow[0;+\infty)
is the function

f_{j,w}(v_{j})=f_{j}(v_{j}+C_{j}w)\hskip 11.74988pt\forall v_{j}\in C_{j}V.

We claim that
f_{j,w}
(respectively
f_{j,H/V}
, see the definition below) is more log-convex than
g_{Q_{j}|_{C_{j}V}}
(resp.
g_{Q_{j}|_{C_{j}(H/V)}}.
) Suppose the claim is proven.

Then, since
f_{j,w}
is more-log convex than
g_{Q_{j}|_{V}}
, we conclude that

\int_{V}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(f_{j}\circ C_{j})^{p_{j}}(v+w)\,dv\leq\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{V})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{C_{j}V}f_{j,w}\Bigl)^{p_{j}}.

Therefore, it suffices to show that

(3.2)

\int_{V^{\perp}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{C_{j}V}f_{j,w}\Bigl)^{p_{j}}\,dw\leq\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{H/V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{H/V}).

Identifying
V^{\perp}
with
H/V
, we observe that
\displaystyle\int_{C_{j}V}f_{j,w}=f_{j,H/V}\circ C_{j,H/V}(w)
where
f_{j,H/V}:H_{j}/(C_{j}V)\ \longrightarrow[0;+\infty)
is the function

f_{j,H/V}(w_{j})=\int_{w_{j}+C_{j}V}f_{j}.

Since
f_{j,{H/V}}
is more log-convex than
g_{Q_{j}|_{H/V}}
for every
1\leq j\leq n
, by Fubini-Tonelli and the normalization
\int_{H_{j}}f_{j}=1
,
Equation

3.2
holds true.

Let us now prove the claim. In fact, setting
\widetilde{w}=(1-\alpha)w_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{w}_{j},\widetilde{v}=(1-\alpha)v_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{v}_{j}
, one has using that
\log(f_{j})+\pi\langle Q_{j}\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is convex, the following for
\alpha\in(0;1)
:

(3.3)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\\
\log(f_{j}(\widetilde{v}+\widetilde{w}))=\log(f_{j}((1-\alpha)(v_{j}+w_{j})+\alpha(\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}))\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr=\log[f_{j}((1-\alpha)(v_{j}+w_{j})+\alpha(\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}))]+\pi\langle Q_{j}(\widetilde{v}+\widetilde{w}),\widetilde{v}+\widetilde{w}\rangle\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr-\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}\langle Q_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j}),v_{j}+w_{j}\rangle-2\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\langle Q_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j}),\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr-\pi\alpha^{2}\langle Q_{j}(\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}),\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\leq(1-\alpha)\Bigl[\log f_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j})+\pi\langle Q_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j}),v_{j}+w_{j}\rangle\Bigl]\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr+\alpha\Bigl[\log f_{j}(\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j})+\pi\langle Q_{j}(\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}),\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\Bigl]-\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}\langle Q_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j}),v_{j}+w_{j}\rangle\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr-2\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\langle Q_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j}),\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle-\pi\alpha^{2}\langle Q_{j}(\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}),\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle=(1-\alpha)\log f_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j})\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr+\alpha\log f_{j}(\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j})+\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\Bigl[\langle Q_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j}),v_{j}+w_{j}\rangle-2\langle Q_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j}),\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr+\langle Q_{j}(\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}),\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\Bigl]=(1-\alpha)\log f_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j})+\alpha\log f_{j}(\widetilde{v}_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j})\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr+\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\langle Q_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j}-\widetilde{v}_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}),v_{j}+w_{j}-\widetilde{v}_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle.\end{array}

In particular, taking
v_{j}=\widetilde{v}_{j}
in
Equation

3.3
, one obtains

\begin{array}[]{lll}\log f_{j}(v_{j}+(1-\alpha)w_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{w}_{j})&\leq&(1-\alpha)\log f_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j})+\alpha\log f_{j}(v_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&&+\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\langle Q_{j}(w_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}),w_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\end{array}

and taking the exponential,

f_{j}(v_{j}+(1-\alpha)w_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{w}_{j})\leq f_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j})^{1-\alpha}f_{j}(v_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j})^{\alpha}e^{\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\langle Q_{j}(w_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}),w_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle}.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{C_{j}V}f_{j}(v_{j}+(1-\alpha)w_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{w}_{j})\,dv_{j}&\leq&\displaystyle e^{\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\langle Q_{j}(w_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}),w_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle}\Bigl(\int_{C_{j}V}f_{j}(v_{j}+w_{j})\,dv_{j}\Bigl)^{1-\alpha}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&&\displaystyle\Bigl(\int_{C_{j}V}f_{j}(v_{j}+\widetilde{w}_{j})\,dv_{j}\Bigl)^{\alpha}.\end{array}

This implies that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\log f_{j,H/V}((1-\alpha)w_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{w}_{j})+\pi\langle Q_{j}((1-\alpha)w_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{w}_{j}),(1-\alpha)w_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\leq(1-\alpha)\log f_{j,H/V}(w_{j})+\alpha\log f_{j,H/V}(\widetilde{w}_{j})+\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\langle Q_{j}(w_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}),w_{j}-\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr+\pi(1-\alpha)^{2}\langle Q_{j}w_{j},w_{j}\rangle+2\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\langle Q_{j}w_{j},\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle+\pi\alpha^{2}\langle Q_{j}\widetilde{w}_{j},\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr=(1-\alpha)\Bigl[\log f_{j,H/V}(w_{j})+\pi\langle Q_{j}w_{j},w_{j}\rangle\Bigl]+\alpha\Bigl[\log f_{j,H/V}(\widetilde{w}_{j})+\pi\langle Q_{j}\widetilde{w}_{j},\widetilde{w}_{j}\rangle\Bigl].\end{array}

Setting
\widetilde{w}=w_{j}=\widetilde{w}_{j}=C_{j}w
in
Equation

3.3
, one gets

\begin{array}[]{lll}\log f_{j,w}((1-\alpha)v_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{v}_{j})&=&\log f_{j}((1-\alpha)v_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{v}_{j}+C_{j}w)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&(1-\alpha)\log f_{j}(v_{j}+C_{j}w)+\alpha\log f_{j}(\widetilde{v}_{j}+C_{j}w)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&&+\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)\langle Q_{j}(v_{j}-\widetilde{v}_{j}),v_{j}-\widetilde{v}_{j}\rangle\end{array}

which implies that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\log f_{j,w}((1-\alpha)v_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{v}_{j})+\pi\langle Q_{j}[(1-\alpha)v_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{v}_{j}],(1-\alpha)v_{j}+\alpha\widetilde{v}_{j}\rangle\leq\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\leq(1-\alpha)\Bigl[\log f_{j,w}(v_{j})+\pi\langle Q_{j}v_{j},v_{j}\rangle\Bigl]+\alpha\Bigl[\log f_{j,w}(\widetilde{v}_{j})+\pi\langle Q_{j}\widetilde{v}_{j},\widetilde{v}_{j}\rangle\Bigl]\end{array}

and concludes the proof of the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

In fact, if
V
is critical and
Q
splits with respect to
V
, i.e.

\hskip 11.74988ptQ_{j}(C_{j}V)\subset C_{j}V,\hskip 11.74988ptQ_{j}(H_{j}/(C_{j}V))\subset H_{j}/(C_{j}V)\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq j\leq m,

then

\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})=\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{V})\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{H/V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{H/V}).

Proof.

By the previous lemma, we only need to show that
\geq
holds.
If
Equation

3.1
does not hold for
(\textbf{C},p)
, there is nothing to show as
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})=+\infty.

Let
0<C_{V}<\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{V}),0<C_{H/V}<\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C}_{H/V},p,\textbf{Q}|_{H/V})
be arbitrary constants. By
Proposition

2.4
, there exist
0<B_{i}\leq Q_{i}|_{C_{i}V}
and
0<B_{i}^{\prime}\leq Q_{i}|_{H_{i}/(C_{i}V)}
for any
1\leq i\leq m
such that

\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}C_{i})}\geq C_{V}^{2},\hskip 11.74988pt\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i}^{\prime})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}^{\prime}C_{i})}\geq C_{H/V}^{2}.

Identifying
H_{i}/(C_{i}V)
with
(C_{i}V)^{\perp}
as a subset of
H_{i}
, we define
P_{i}:H_{i}\rightarrow C_{i}V
orthogonal projection and
I-P_{i}:H_{i}\rightarrow H_{i}/(C_{i}V)
, which leads to considering

\widetilde{B}_{i}=P_{i}^{*}B_{i}P_{i}+(I-P_{i})^{*}B_{i}^{\prime}(I-P_{i})\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq i\leq m.

Then,
0<\widetilde{B}_{i}\leq Q_{i}
as
Q
splits with respect to
V
and thus
\langle Q_{i}P_{i}x,(I-P_{i})x\rangle=0
for any

which implies that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\langle\widetilde{B}_{i}x,x\rangle&=&\langle B_{i}P_{i}x,P_{i}x\rangle+\langle B_{i}^{\prime}(I-P_{i})x,(I-P_{i})x\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&\langle Q_{i}P_{i}x,P_{i}x\rangle+\langle Q_{i}(I-P_{i})x,(I-P_{i})x\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\langle Q_{i}x,x\rangle\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x\in H_{i}.\end{array}

Furthermore,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})^{2}&\geq&\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det\widetilde{B}_{i})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}\widetilde{B}_{i}C_{i})}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}C_{i})}\times\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\det B_{i}^{\prime})^{p_{i}}}{\det(\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}B_{i}^{\prime}C_{i})}\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\geq&C_{V}^{2}C_{H/V}^{2}.\end{array}

∎

Remark 3.8
.

We do not expect this to hold if there exists
1\leq j\leq m
such that
Q_{j}
does not split with respect to
V.
As one is imposing a quite strong condition on the structure of
Q_{j}
for any
1\leq j\leq m
,
i.e.

Q_{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ c | c }C_{j}V&0\\
\hline\cr 0&(C_{j}V)^{\perp}\end{array}\right),

the property of multiplicativity of Brascamp-Lieb constants is not completely preserved by regularizing.

Lemma 3.9
.

If

\dim(V)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}V)\text{ for all subspaces }V\text{ of }H,

then there exists a positive real number
c>0
such that for every orthonormal basis
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
of
H
, there exists a set
I_{j}\subseteq\{1,\cdots,n\}
for each
1\leq j\leq m
with
|I_{j}|=\dim(H_{j})
so that

(3.4)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}|I_{j}\,\cap\{k+1,\cdots,n\}|\geq n-k\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 0\leq k\leq n

and

(3.5)

\Bigl\|\bigwedge_{i\in I_{j}}C_{j}e_{i}\Bigl\|_{H}\geq c\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq j\leq m.

If there are no critical subspaces, we can enforce strict inequality in
Equation

3.4
for all
0\leq k<n.

Proof.

Recall that the space of all orthonormal bases is compact and the number of possible
I_{j}
is finite. By continuity and compactness, it suffices to show that

\bigwedge_{i\in I_{j}}C_{j}e_{i}\neq 0\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq m

to deduce
Equation

3.5
. In other words, we want the vectors
(C_{j}e_{i})_{i\in I_{j}}
to be linearly independent in
H_{j}
for each
j.

In fact, take
I_{j}
to be the set of indices
i
for which
C_{j}e_{i}
is not in the linear span of
\{C_{j}e_{i^{\prime}}:i<i^{\prime}\leq n\}.
As
C_{j}
is surjective,
|I_{j}|=\dim(H_{j})
for every
1\leq j\leq m.
Applying the assumption with
V
equal to the span of
\{e_{k+1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
, one has

\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}V)\geq n-k.

But by construction of
I_{j}
,
\dim(C_{j}V)=|I_{j}\cap\{k+1,\cdots,n\}|.
If there are no critical subspaces, one can use that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(C_{j}V)>\dim(V)\text{ for any non-zero subspace }V\text{ of }H.

∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum such that
Equation

3.1
holds. Then,
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is finite.
Furthermore, if
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is simple (in other words, there is no critical subspace), then
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is Gaussian-extremizable.

Proof.

Fix
A=(A_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
with
0<A_{j}\leq Q_{j}.
Set
M=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}A_{j}C_{j}>0
as a positive definite self-adjoint transformation. By choosing an appropriate orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
of
H
, we can assume that

M=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\text{ with }\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}>0.

Moreover,
M\leq\widetilde{M}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}Q_{j}C_{j}
and in particular,

\lambda_{1}=\langle Me_{1},e_{1}\rangle\leq\langle\widetilde{M}e_{1},e_{1}\rangle\leq\sup_{\|e\|=1}\langle\widetilde{M}e,e\rangle=\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde{M}).

By the previous lemma, we find
I_{j}\subset\{1,\cdots,n\}
for each
1\leq j\leq m
of cardinality
\dim(H_{j})
obeying
Equation

3.4
and
Equation

3.5
. Thus, for
i\in I_{j}
,

\langle A_{j}C_{j}e_{i},C_{j}e_{i}\rangle=\langle e_{i},C_{j}^{*}A_{j}C_{j}e_{i}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{p_{j}}\langle e_{i},Me_{i}\rangle=\frac{\lambda_{i}}{p_{j}}.

As
(C_{j}e_{i})_{i\in I_{j}}
is a basis of
H_{j}
with a lower bound on the degeneracy,

\det(A_{j})\leq C\prod_{i\in I_{j}}\lambda_{i}

for some constant
C>0
depending on
(\textbf{C},p).
This implies that

\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(A_{j})^{p_{j}}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}|I_{j}\cap\{i\}|}.

Telescoping the right hand side, one gets

\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\det A_{j})^{p_{j}}\leq C\lambda_{1}^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}}\;\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(\lambda_{k+1}/\lambda_{k})^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}|I_{j}\cap\{k+1,\cdots,n\}|}

with the convention
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}.
As
\lambda_{k+1}\leq\lambda_{k}
for any
0\leq k\leq n-1
and

\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}|I_{j}\cap\{k+1,\cdots,n\}|\geq n-k,

one concludes that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\det A_{j})^{p_{j}}&\leq&\displaystyle C\lambda_{1}^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(\lambda_{k+1}/\lambda_{k})^{n-k}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&\displaystyle C\lambda_{1}^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}-n}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}.\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&\displaystyle C\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde{M})^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}-n}\det(M)=\widetilde{C}\det(M)\end{array}

for some constant
\widetilde{C}>0
depending on
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
, using that
\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}\geq n.

Let us assume that
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is simple. We then have a strict inequality in
Equation

3.4
which gives that

\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\det A_{j})^{p_{j}}\leq C\det(M)\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(\lambda_{k+1}/\lambda_{k})^{c}=C\det(M)(\lambda_{n}/\lambda_{1})^{c}

for some
c>0
depending on the Brascamp-Lieb data. Thus,
\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\det A_{j})^{p_{j}}/\det(M)
converges to
0
whenever
\lambda_{n}/\lambda_{1}
goes to
0.
This proves that it suffices to evaluate the supremum in the region
\lambda_{1}(M)\leq C\lambda_{n}(M).

Assume by contradiction that
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is not Gaussian-extremizable. This means there exists a sequence
(A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)})_{1\leq j\leq m}
such that
0<A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}\leq Q_{j}\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq j\leq m
, so that

\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)})^{p_{j}}}{\det(\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}C_{j})}\rightarrow_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})>\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(B_{j})^{p_{j}}}{\det(\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}B_{j}C_{j})}

for any
0<B_{j}\leq Q_{j}
and the determinant of
M^{(\varepsilon)}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}C_{j}
converges to
0.
Since
M^{(\varepsilon)}
is symmetric, one can write

M^{(\varepsilon)}=U^{(\varepsilon)}\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1}^{(\varepsilon)},\cdots,\lambda_{n}^{(\varepsilon)})(U^{(\varepsilon)})^{T}\text{ for }U^{(\varepsilon)}\in O(n),\text{ with }\lambda_{1}^{(\varepsilon)}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}^{(\varepsilon)}>0.

Then,
\lambda_{n}^{(\varepsilon)}\rightarrow_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}0
and by what was previously shown,
C\lambda_{n}^{(\varepsilon)}\geq\lambda_{1}^{(\varepsilon)}
. This means that
\lambda_{1}^{(\varepsilon)}\rightarrow_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}0
with the same rate of convergence than
\lambda_{n}^{(\varepsilon)}
, which will be denoted by
f(\varepsilon).
Consequently,
\lambda_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}\rightarrow_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}0
for any
1\leq k\leq n
with rate of convergence
f(\varepsilon).

Moreover, by compactness of
O(n)
, up to a subsequence,
U^{(\varepsilon)}\rightarrow U\in O(n)
which proves that
M^{(\varepsilon)}\rightarrow_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}0.
Hence,

\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\langle A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}C_{j}x,C_{j}y\rangle=\langle M^{(\varepsilon)}x,y\rangle\rightarrow 0\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x,y\in H

and by surjectivity of
C_{j}
and
p_{j}>0
for
j=1,\cdots,m
,

\langle A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}v_{j},w_{j}\rangle\rightarrow_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}0\hskip 11.74988pt\forall v_{j},w_{j}\in H_{j}\Longrightarrow A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}\rightarrow 0\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq j\leq m.

Now, write

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\det A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)})^{p_{j}}}{\det(M^{(\varepsilon)})}=f(\varepsilon)^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}-n}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det\Bigl(\frac{A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}}{f(\varepsilon)}\Bigl)^{p_{j}}}{\det\Bigl(\frac{M^{(\varepsilon)}}{f(\varepsilon)}\Bigl)}

and notice that
M^{(\varepsilon)}/f(\varepsilon)\rightarrow_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}U\mathrm{diag}(\widetilde{\lambda}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{\lambda}_{n})U^{T}=\widetilde{M}>0
up to a subsequence. Furthermore, for
v_{j}^{(k,\varepsilon)}
a unit norm eigenvector of
A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}
associated to its
k
-th eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)})
,

\frac{\lambda_{k}(A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)})}{f(\varepsilon)}=\Bigl\langle\frac{A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}v_{j}^{(k,\varepsilon)}}{f(\varepsilon)},v_{j}^{(k,\varepsilon)}\Bigl\rangle\leq\frac{1}{p_{j}}\Bigl\langle\frac{M^{(\varepsilon)}}{f(\varepsilon)}v^{(\varepsilon)},v^{(\varepsilon)}\Bigl\rangle

where
v^{(\varepsilon)}=D_{j}v_{j}^{(k,\varepsilon)}
for
D_{j}
a right-inverse of
C_{j}
, which exists since
C_{j}
is surjective (
i.e.

C_{j}D_{j}=\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}
so that
C_{j}v^{(\varepsilon)}=v_{j}^{(k,\varepsilon)}
). As
v^{(\varepsilon)}
has a bounded norm and consequently has a convergent subsequence by compactness,
\frac{\lambda_{k}(A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)})}{f(\varepsilon)}
is bounded in
\varepsilon
up to a subsequence. Thus, we can conclude with a contradiction since
\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}-n>0
,
f(\varepsilon)\rightarrow_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}0
and

\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det\Bigl(\frac{A_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}}{f(\varepsilon)}\Bigl)^{p_{j}}}{\det\Bigl(\frac{M^{(\varepsilon)}}{f(\varepsilon)}\Bigl)}

is uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon
, which would mean that
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})=0
(which is, of course, impossible).
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Recall the following classical fact. When
f
is more log-convex than
g_{A}
, g more log-convex than
g_{B}
for some
A,B>0
,
f\star g
is more log-convex than
g_{C}
for
C=A-A(A+B)^{-1}A
. Indeed, since
f=g_{A}h
and
g=g_{B}\widetilde{h}
for log-convex functions
h,\widetilde{h}
, one has

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle e^{\pi\langle Cx,x\rangle}(f\star g)(x)&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x-y)\widetilde{h}(y)e^{\pi\langle Cx,x\rangle}e^{-\pi\langle A(x-y),x-y\rangle}e^{-\pi\langle By,y\rangle}\,dy\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x-y)\widetilde{h}(y)e^{-\pi\langle M(x,y),(x,y)\rangle}\,dy\end{array}

where

M=\begin{pmatrix}A(A+B)^{-1}A&-A\\
-A&A+B\end{pmatrix}.

The log-convexity of
h
and
\widetilde{h}
implies the log-convexity of
(x,y)\mapsto h(x-y)\widetilde{h}(y)
, which gives, by
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
, that
f\star g
is more log-convex than
g_{C}.

Define

\mathrm{Conv}(f,g)=2^{n/2}(f\star g)(\sqrt{2}\;\cdot)

for
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}_{+}).
From what was proven, if
f
and
g
are more log-convex than
g_{A}
,
\mathrm{Conv}(f,g)
is also more log-convex than
g_{A}.

Using convolutions and the central limit theorem allows to prove the following:

Theorem 3.12
.

If the Brascamp-Lieb constant
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is extremizable (i.e. there is equality for some functions
f_{i}
that are more log-concave than
g_{Q_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq m
), it is Gaussian extremizable (i.e. the supremum in the definition of
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is attained).

Proof.

The computation will first start by considering the more general
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q}).
One can assume as usual that
H=\mathbb{R}^{n},H_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
for some
n_{i}\leq n,\;1\leq i\leq m.
Let
f_{i},g_{i}
be more log-convex than
g_{Q_{i}}
for any
1\leq i\leq m
and
\mathcal{Q}\geq 0.
Assume without loss of generality that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}f_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}g_{i}=1
and set

F(x)=e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(C_{i}x)^{p_{i}},G(y)=e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}y,y\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}(C_{i}y)^{p_{i}}.

Then,

(3.6)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F\star G(x)\,dx\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle 2^{n/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}y,y\rangle}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}(\sqrt{2}x-y),\sqrt{2}x-y\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(f_{i}(\sqrt{2}C_{i}x-C_{i}y)g_{i}(C_{i}y)\Bigl)^{p_{i}}\,dxdy\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2}}\rangle}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}}\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(f_{i}\bigl(\frac{C_{i}x-C_{i}y}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl)g_{i}\bigl(\frac{C_{i}x+C_{i}y}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl)\Bigl)^{p_{i}}\,dxdy\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}y,y\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}(C_{i}y)^{p_{i}}\,dy\Bigl)\,dx\end{array}

where
F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}(y_{i})=f_{i}(\frac{C_{i}x-y_{i}}{\sqrt{2}})g_{i}(\frac{C_{i}x+y_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}).

In the third line, the change of variables is
y\mapsto\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2}}
and in the last line, it was used that

\Bigl\langle\mathcal{Q}\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl\rangle+\Bigl\langle\mathcal{Q}\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl\rangle=\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle+\langle\mathcal{Q}y,y\rangle\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

As
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
are more log-convex than
g_{Q_{i}}
, there exist log-convex functions
h_{i},\widetilde{h}_{i}
such that
f_{i}(x_{i})=e^{-\pi\langle Q_{i}x_{i},x_{i}\rangle}h_{i}(x_{i}),g_{i}(x_{i})=e^{-\pi\langle Q_{i}x_{i},x_{i}\rangle}\widetilde{h}_{i}(x_{i}).
Hence, we deduce that
F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}
is also more log-convex than
g_{Q_{i}}
as

F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}(y_{i})=e^{-\pi\langle Q_{i}C_{i}x,C_{i}x\rangle}e^{-\pi\langle Q_{i}y_{i},y_{i}\rangle}h_{i}\bigl(\frac{C_{i}x-y_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl)\widetilde{h}_{i}\bigl(\frac{C_{i}x+y_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl)

and notice that

\mathrm{Conv}(f_{i},g_{i})(C_{i}x)=2^{n_{i}/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}f_{i}(\sqrt{2}C_{i}x_{i}-y_{i})g_{i}(y_{i})\,dy_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}(y_{i})\,dy_{i}

by the change of variables from
y_{i}
to
\frac{C_{i}x_{i}+y_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}.

Thus, denoting
\mathrm{Conv}(f,g)=(\mathrm{Conv}(f_{i},g_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq m}
,

(3.7)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{Q}}(f)J_{\mathcal{Q}}(g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G&\leq&\displaystyle\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}(y_{i})\,dy_{i}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})J_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathrm{Conv}(f,g)).\end{array}

Here, we set

J_{\mathcal{Q}}((h_{i})_{i=1}^{m})=\frac{\displaystyle\int e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}(C_{i}x)^{p_{i}}\,dx}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int h_{i}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}}.

For the rest of the argument, we can take
\mathcal{Q}=0
(and we will write
J=J_{0}
) as we already know that
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})
is Gaussian extremizable when
\mathcal{Q}>0
. Now, from the previous inequality, if
f
is an extremizer for
J
with
\int f_{i}=1
for any
1\leq i\leq m
,
\mathrm{Conv}(f):=\mathrm{Conv}(f,f)
is also an extremizer as

\begin{array}[]{lll}J(\mathrm{Conv}(f))&\geq&\displaystyle\frac{J(f)^{2}}{\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})}=\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int\mathrm{Conv}(f_{i})\Bigl)^{p_{i}}\end{array}

with
\mathrm{Conv}(f_{i})
being more log-convex than
g_{Q_{i}}
for any
1\leq i\leq m
according to the previous remark. Iterating this process, we deduce that
\mathrm{Conv}^{k}(f)
is also an extremizer for all
k\geq 1.
By the central limit theorem (for example, see
[
9
, Thm 1.1]
), each
\mathrm{Conv}^{k}(f_{i})
converges in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}})
and almost everywhere as
k\rightarrow+\infty
to a centered Gaussian
\frac{g_{B_{i}}}{Z_{i}}
, which is still more log-convex than
g_{Q_{i}}.

By Fatou’s lemma and
Lemma

3.13
(ii),(iv) with
g=(g_{B_{1}},\cdots,g_{B_{m}})
and
0<B_{i}\leq Q_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
,

J(g)\geq\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}g_{B_{i}}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}

and this proves that the BL constant is Gaussian-extremizable, as desired.
∎

Thanks to the previous lemma, we may easily obtain the following closure properties of extremizers of
J_{Q}
:

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})
with
\mathcal{Q}\geq 0
be a generalized Brascamp-Lieb datum with
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})
finite.

(i)

If
f=(f_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
is an extremizer, then so is
(f_{j}(-\cdot))_{1\leq j\leq m}.

(ii)

If
f=(f_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
is an extremizer, then so is
(c_{j}f_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
for any positive real numbers
c_{1},\cdots,c_{m}.

(iii)

If
f=(f_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
is an extremizer, then so is
(f_{j}(\cdot-C_{j}x_{0}))_{1\leq j\leq m}
for any
x_{0}\in H.

(iv)

If
f^{(k)}=(f_{j}^{(k)})_{1\leq j\leq n}
is a sequence of extremizers converging in
L^{1}
to
f
, i.e.
\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\|f_{j}^{(k)}-f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(H_{j})}=0
for all
1\leq j\leq m
and such that
f_{j}
is more log-convex than
g_{Q_{j}}
for all
j=1,\cdots,m
, then
f
is also an extremizer.

(v)

If
f
and
g
are extremizers, then
\mathrm{Conv}(f,g)
is also extremizer.

(vi)

If
f
and
g
are extremizers, then

\Bigl(f_{j}\Bigl(\frac{\cdot-C_{j}x_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl)g_{j}\Bigl(\frac{\cdot+C_{j}x_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl)\Bigl)_{1\leq j\leq m}

is also an extremizer for almost every
x_{0}\in H.
In addition, if
f
and
g
are bounded, then the latter holds for every
x_{0}\in H.

Proof.

The first three statements are trivial.
Statement (iv) follows from standard arguments since one can use the definition of
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})
and the assumption that the Brascamp-Lieb constant is finite to show that

\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(f_{j}^{(k)}\circ C_{j})^{p_{j}}\rightarrow_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(f_{j}\circ C_{j})^{p_{j}}.

Statement (v) is a consequence of
Equation

3.7
and

\int_{H_{i}}\mathrm{Conv}(f_{i},g_{i})=\int_{H_{i}}f_{i}\int_{H_{i}}g_{i}.

Finally for statement (vi), we use
Equation

3.6
and the pairs
(\widetilde{f}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m},(g_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
where

\widetilde{f}_{j}(x)=f_{j}(-x)
for any
1\leq j\leq m.
As usual, we can assume that
\int f_{i}=\int g_{i}=1
for any
1\leq i\leq m
by (ii). Thus,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})^{2}=\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\Bigl(\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}y,y\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}(C_{i}y))^{p_{i}}\,dy\Bigl)\,dx\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\leq\displaystyle\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{i}}F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}\,dx\leq\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})^{2}\end{array}

which means that there must be equality

\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\mathcal{Q}x,x\rangle}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}\circ C_{i})^{p_{i}}\,dx=\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{i}}F_{i}^{(C_{i}x)}\Bigl)^{p_{i}}

for almost every
x.
To remove the almost in case
f
and
g
are bounded, the argument is the same as in
[
5
]
.
∎

Specializing the above lemma to centered Gaussian extremizers, we conclude:

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})
with
\mathcal{Q}\geq 0
be a generalized Brascamp-Lieb datum with
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q},\mathcal{Q})
finite.

(i)

If
A^{(n)}
is a sequence of extremizing Gaussian inputs which converge to a Gaussian input
A=(A_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
with
0<A_{i}\leq Q_{i}
for any
i=1,\cdots,m
, then
A
is also an extremizer.

(ii)

If
A=(A_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m},A^{\prime}=(A_{j}^{\prime})_{1\leq j\leq m}
are Gaussian extremizers, then so is
(2(A_{j}^{-1}+(A_{j}^{\prime})^{-1})^{-1})_{1\leq j\leq m}.

(iii)

If
A
and
A^{\prime}
are Gaussian extremizers, then so is
\frac{A+A^{\prime}}{2}.

Proof.

(i) is clear by
Lemma

3.13
(iv); (ii) follows from
Lemma

3.13
(v), the computation of the convolution of two Gaussians and the fact that

0<A_{i},A_{i}^{\prime}\leq Q_{i}\Longrightarrow A_{i}^{-1}+(A_{i}^{\prime})^{-1}\geq 2Q_{i}^{-1}\Longrightarrow 0<2(A_{i}^{-1}+(A_{i}^{\prime})^{-1})^{-1}\leq Q_{i}.

Finally, (iii) follows from

3.13
(vi).
∎

Now that we have shown that extremizable is equivalent to Gaussian extremizable, one would like to understand what are the necessary and sufficient conditions for Gaussian extremizers/extremizers to exist.
We start by defining a notion of equivalence between generalized Brascamp-Lieb data.

Theorem 3.15
.

Two Brascamp-Lieb data
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
and
(\textbf{C'},p^{\prime},\textbf{Q'})
are equivalent if there exist invertible linear transformations
D:H^{\prime}\rightarrow H
and
D_{j}:H_{j}^{\prime}\rightarrow H_{j}
(called intertwining transformations) such that
C^{\prime}_{j}=D_{j}^{-1}C_{j}D
and
Q_{j}^{\prime}=D_{j}^{*}Q_{j}D_{j}
for all
j
and
p=p^{\prime}.

The Brascamp-Lieb constants of equivalent data are closely related.

Lemma 3.16
.

Suppose that
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q}),(\textbf{C'},p^{\prime},\textbf{Q'})
are two equivalent BL data with intertwining maps
D:H^{\prime}\rightarrow H,D_{j}:H_{j}^{\prime}\rightarrow H_{j}.
Then,

\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C'},p^{\prime},\textbf{Q'})=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(D_{j})^{p_{j}}}{\det(D)}\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q}).

Proof.

This lemma is a consequence of the following simple computation:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C'},p^{\prime},\textbf{Q'})&=&\displaystyle\sup_{0<A_{j}\leq Q_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(A_{j})^{p_{j}/2}}{\det\Bigl(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}(C_{j}^{\prime})^{*}A_{j}C_{j}^{\prime}\Bigl)^{1/2}}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\sup_{0<A_{j}\leq Q_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(A_{j})^{p_{j}/2}}{\det\Bigl(D^{*}\Bigl[\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}\bigl(D_{j}^{-*}A_{j}D_{j}^{-1}\bigl)C_{j}\Bigl]D\Bigl)^{1/2}}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(D_{j})^{p_{j}}}{\det(D)}\sup_{0<A_{j}\leq Q_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(D_{j}^{-*}A_{j}D_{j}^{-1})^{p_{j}/2}}{\det\Bigl(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}\bigl(D_{j}^{-*}A_{j}D_{j}^{-1}\bigl)C_{j}\Bigl)^{1/2}}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(D_{j})^{p_{j}}}{\det(D)}\sup_{0<A_{j}\leq(D_{j})^{-*}Q_{j}^{\prime}D_{j}^{-1}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(A_{j})^{p_{j}/2}}{\det\Bigl(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}A_{j}C_{j}\Bigl)^{1/2}}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(D_{j})^{p_{j}}}{\det(D)}\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q}).\end{array}

∎

Definition 3.17
.

A Brascamp-Lieb datum
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is a generalized geometric BL datum if
C_{j}C_{j}^{*}\leq\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}},Q_{j}\geq\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}},(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})(Q_{j}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}})=0
for any
1\leq j\leq m
and

(3.8)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}=\mathrm{id}_{H}.

Remark 3.18
.

Let
G>0
be positive definite and let
u\in C^{2}
be a positive function that is more log-convex than
g_{G}
and
A\geq 0.
Then,

\mathrm{div}(A\nabla\log u)=\mathrm{Tr}(A^{1/2}D^{2}(\log u)A^{1/2})\geq-2\pi\mathrm{Tr}(A^{1/2}GA^{1/2})=-2\pi\mathrm{Tr}(AG).

Theorem 3.19
.

A generalized geometric Brascamp-Lieb datum has BL constant equal to
1.

From the proof of
Proposition

2.4
, one may see that

1\leq\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})=\mathrm{BL}_{g}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})\leq\mathrm{BL}_{\text{type Q fcts}}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})=1

where the last equality is shown in
[
5
, Prop 8.9]
and the first inequality holds by testing
A_{j}=\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}
for any
1\leq j\leq m.
However, we repeat the proof to be able to treat the equality cases.

Proof.

Let
f_{j}
be a Schwarz function that is more log-convex than
g_{Q_{j}}
for all
1\leq j\leq m
. This implies that
\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j}(x,t)
grows at most polynomially in space, locally uniformly on
(1;+\infty)
by
[
34
]
and the assumptions of
[
5
, Lemma 2.6]
are then satisfied.
Let us define the following function:

\widetilde{Q}(x,t)=t^{(\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(H_{j})-\dim(H))/2}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{f}_{j}(C_{j}x,t)^{p_{j}}

with
\widetilde{f}_{j}:H_{j}\times[1;+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
solution to the heat equation

\widetilde{f}_{j}(x_{j},1)=f_{j}(x_{j})\text{ and }\partial_{t}\widetilde{f}_{j}(x_{j},t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\Delta\widetilde{f}_{j}(x_{j},t)\text{ for }t>1.

Then
\widetilde{Q}(x,1)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(C_{j}x)^{p_{j}}
and since

\widetilde{f}_{j}(x,t)=\int_{H_{j}}\frac{1}{(t-1)^{n_{j}/2}}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\pi|x-y|^{2}}{t-1}\Bigl)f_{j}(y)\,dy=\int_{H_{j}}\exp(-\pi|z|^{2})f_{j}(x+\sqrt{t-1}z)\,dz,

we have that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{H}\widetilde{Q}(x,t)\,dx&=&\displaystyle t^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(H_{j})/2}\int_{H}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{f}_{j}^{p_{j}}(\sqrt{t}C_{j}x,t)\,dx\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\frac{t^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}/2}}{(t-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}/2}}\int_{H}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{j}}e^{-\pi\frac{|\sqrt{t}C_{j}x-y|^{2}}{t-1}}f_{j}(y)\,dy\Bigl)^{p_{j}}\,dx\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\frac{t^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}/2}}{(t-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}n_{j}/2}}\int_{H}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(e^{-\pi\frac{t}{t-1}p_{j}|C_{j}x|^{2}}\Bigl(\int_{H_{j}}e^{\frac{2\sqrt{t}}{t-1}\langle C_{j}x,y\rangle-\frac{|y|^{2}}{t-1}}f_{j}(y)\,dy\Bigl)^{p_{j}}\Bigl)\,dx\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&&\displaystyle\rightarrow_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{H}e^{-\pi\langle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}x,x\rangle}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{j}}f_{j}\Bigl)^{p_{j}}\,dx=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int f_{j}\Bigl)^{p_{j}}.\end{array}

In the last line, dominated convergence was used as well as the fact that
\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}=\mathrm{id}_{H}
since the BL datum is generalized geometric.
The goal is to show that

\int_{H}\widetilde{Q}(x,t)\,dx

is non-decreasing in
t
(which suffices by density of Schwarz functions) and the proof employs
[
5
, Lemma 2.6]
(multilinear monotonicity for transport equations).
First, let us show that
\widetilde{f}_{j}(\cdot,t)
is more log-convex than
g_{Q_{j}/t}.
Indeed, setting
h_{j}(x)=e^{\pi\langle Q_{j}x,x\rangle}f_{j}(x)
, one has

\begin{array}[]{lll}\widetilde{f}_{j}(x,t)&=&\displaystyle\int_{H_{j}}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}f_{j}(x+\sqrt{t-1}z)\,dz\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\int_{H_{j}}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}e^{-\pi\langle Q_{j}(x+\sqrt{t-1}z),x+\sqrt{t-1}z\rangle}h_{j}(x+\sqrt{t-1}z)\,dz\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\int_{H_{j}}e^{-\pi\langle Q_{j}x,x\rangle}e^{-2\pi\sqrt{t-1}\langle Q_{j}x,z\rangle}e^{-\pi(t-1)\langle Q_{j}z,z\rangle}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}h_{j}(x+\sqrt{t-1}z)\,dz.\end{array}

Using the notation of
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
,

\begin{pmatrix}A\hskip 11.74988ptB\\
B^{*}\hskip 11.74988ptC\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi Q_{j}\hskip 11.74988pt\pi\sqrt{t-1}Q_{j}\\
\pi\sqrt{t-1}Q_{j}\hskip 11.74988pt\pi(t-1)Q_{j}+\pi\mathrm{Id}\end{pmatrix},

one has

\begin{array}[]{lll}D&=&A-B^{*}C^{-1}B\\
&=&\pi Q_{j}-\pi^{2}(t-1)Q_{j}^{2}\bigl[\pi(t-1)Q_{j}+\pi\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}\bigl]^{-1}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\pi Q_{j}\Bigl(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-(t-1)Q_{j}[(t-1)Q_{j}+\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}]^{-1}\Bigl)=\pi Q_{j}[(t-1)Q_{j}+\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}]^{-1}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\pi[(t-1)\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}+Q_{j}^{-1}]^{-1}\leq\pi Q_{j}/t,\end{array}

since
\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}\geq Q_{j}^{-1}\Longrightarrow(t-1)\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}+Q_{j}^{-1}\geq tQ_{j}^{-1}.
Applying
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
, it follows that
\widetilde{f}_{j}(\cdot,t)
is more log-convex than
g_{D/\pi}
, in particular more log-convex than
g_{Q_{j}/t}.

Consider the functions

u_{j}=\widetilde{f}_{j}\circ C_{j},\overrightarrow{v_{j}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}D_{j}(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})\circ C_{j}

where
D_{j}
is a right-inverse of
C_{j}
,

\overrightarrow{v}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}v_{j},\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\dim(H)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(H_{j})\Bigl).

As
\partial_{t}\widetilde{f}_{j}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\Delta\widetilde{f}_{j}
,

\partial_{t}u_{j}=\frac{1}{4\pi}(\Delta\widetilde{f}_{j})\circ C_{j}

and

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathrm{div}(\overrightarrow{v_{j}}u_{j})&=&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}\mathrm{div}([D_{j}(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})\widetilde{f}_{j}]\circ C_{j})\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}\mathrm{div}(C_{j}D_{j}\nabla\widetilde{f}_{j})\circ C_{j}\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}(\Delta\widetilde{f}_{j})\circ C_{j}.\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\end{array}

Thus,
\mathrm{div}(\overrightarrow{v_{j}}u_{j})+\partial_{t}u_{j}=0
for any
1\leq j\leq m.
Moreover, since
C_{j}(C_{j}^{*}C_{j}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}})D_{j}=C_{j}C_{j}^{*}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\Bigl(\overrightarrow{v}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\overrightarrow{v_{j}}\Bigl)&=&\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\mathrm{div}((C_{j}^{*}C_{j}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}})\overrightarrow{v_{j}})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\mathrm{div}((C_{j}^{*}C_{j}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}})D_{j}(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})\circ C_{j})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\mathrm{div}(C_{j}(C_{j}^{*}C_{j}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}})D_{j}(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j}))\circ C_{j}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\mathrm{div}((\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})\circ C_{j}.\end{array}

By the previous remark,

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}((\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})\geq\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi}{t}\mathrm{Tr}((\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})Q_{j})=-\frac{2\pi}{t}\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})

which implies by summing over
j=1,\cdots,m
, that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\mathrm{div}((\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})&\geq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2t}\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\mathrm{Tr}(C_{j}^{*}C_{j})-\frac{1}{2t}\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(H_{j})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{t}.\end{array}

Finally,

\nabla\log u_{j}=C_{j}^{*}(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})\circ C_{j}\Longrightarrow-C_{j}^{*}C_{j}\overrightarrow{v_{j}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}D_{j}(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})\circ C_{j}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\nabla\log u_{j}

which gives that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\langle\overrightarrow{v}-\overrightarrow{v_{j}},\nabla\log u_{j}\rangle_{H}=4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\langle C_{j}^{*}C_{j}(\overrightarrow{v}-\overrightarrow{v_{j}}),-\overrightarrow{v_{j}}\rangle_{H}.

As
\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}(\overrightarrow{v}-\overrightarrow{v_{j}})=\overrightarrow{v}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}\overrightarrow{v_{j}}=0
, one has

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\langle\overrightarrow{v}-\overrightarrow{v_{j}},\nabla\log u_{j}\rangle_{H}&=&\displaystyle 4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\langle C_{j}^{*}C_{j}(\overrightarrow{v}-\overrightarrow{v_{j}}),\overrightarrow{v}-\overrightarrow{v_{j}})\geq 0\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle 4\pi\Bigl[\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\langle C_{j}\overrightarrow{v_{j}},C_{j}\overrightarrow{v_{j}}\rangle-\langle\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{v}\rangle\Bigl]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&4\pi\langle(I-P)A(x),A(x)\rangle\geq 0\end{array}

where

A(x)=\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}^{1/2}C_{1}\overrightarrow{v_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
p_{m}^{1/2}C_{m}\overrightarrow{v_{m}}\end{pmatrix},\;T=\begin{pmatrix}\cdots p_{1}^{1/2}C_{1}\cdots\\
\vdots\\
\cdots p_{m}^{1/2}C_{m}\cdots\end{pmatrix}

and
P=TT^{*}=T(T^{*}T)^{-1}T^{*}
is a projection (recall by assumption,
T^{*}T=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}=\mathrm{id}_{H}
). The conditions to apply Lemma
2.6
are all fulfilled and hence we get the desired claim.
∎

Remark 3.20
.

There would be equality

\int_{H}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(f_{j}\circ C_{j})^{p_{j}}\,=\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{j}}f_{j}\Bigl)^{p_{j}}

if and only if

\mathrm{Tr}((\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})(D^{2}\log\widetilde{f}_{j}+\frac{2\pi}{t}Q_{j}))=0\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq j\leq m,

as the trace of a product of two positive semi-definite matrices and

\langle(I-P)A(x),A(x)\rangle=0.

One can give a satisfactory algebraic characterization of Gaussian extremizable Brascamp-Lieb data using the previously introduced definitions.

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum with
p_{j}>0
for all
j
,
B=(B_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
such that
0<B_{j}\leq Q_{j}.
Denote by
M=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}B_{j}C_{j}.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(a)

B
is a global extremizer, i.e.

\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\det(B_{j})^{p_{j}/2}}{\det(M)^{1/2}},

(b)

B
is a local extremizer,

(c)

M
is invertible with

B_{j}^{-1}\geq C_{j}M^{-1}C_{j}^{*},\,(B_{j}^{-1}-C_{j}M^{-1}C_{j}^{*})(Q_{j}-B_{j})=0\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq j\leq m,

(d)

(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is equivalent to a generalized geometric BL datum
(\textbf{C'},p^{\prime},\textbf{Q'})
with intertwining operators
D=M^{-1/2},D_{j}=B_{j}^{-1/2}.

Proof.

The implication from
(a)
to
(b)
is trivial. Let us verify that
(b)
implies
(c)
.
By assumption,
B
is a local extremizer for the function

f(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\log\det A_{j}-\log\det\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}A_{j}C_{j}\Bigl)

under the constraint
0<A_{j}\leq Q_{j}.
Fix some
1\leq j\leq m
and denote by
V_{j}
the kernel of the positive semi-definite operator
Q_{j}-B_{j}.
We also consider
\textit{i}_{j}:V_{j}\rightarrow H_{j}
the inclusion map and
G_{j}
a self-adjoint operator that is negative semi-definite on
V_{j}
(in other words,
\textit{i}_{j}^{*}G_{j}\textit{i}_{j}\leq 0
). Hence,
0<B_{j}+\varepsilon G_{j}\leq Q_{j}
for all
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and as
B
is a local extremizer,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{f(B_{1},\cdots,B_{j-1},B_{j}+\varepsilon G_{j},B_{j+1},\cdots,B_{m})-f(B_{1},\cdots,B_{m})}{\varepsilon}\leq 0

which gives that

\mathrm{Tr}((B_{j}^{-1}-C_{j}M^{-1}C_{j}^{*})G_{j})\leq 0

whenever
G_{j}
is negative semi-definite on
V_{j}.

In particular, as the previous inequality holds whenever
G_{j}
is negative semi-definite on
H_{j}
, one obtains that

B_{j}^{-1}\geq C_{j}M^{-1}C_{j}^{*}.

Furthermore,

\mathrm{Tr}((B_{j}^{-1}-C_{j}M^{-1}C_{j}^{*})G_{j})=0\text{ if }\textit{i}_{j}^{*}G_{j}\textit{i}_{j}=0,G_{j}^{*}=G_{j}.

By positive semi-definiteness of
B_{j}^{-1}-C_{j}M^{-1}C_{j}^{*}
, one deduces that it must be equal to
\textit{i}_{j}N_{j}\textit{i}_{j}^{*}
for a self-adjoint transformation
N_{j}:V_{j}\rightarrow V_{j}.
But
(Q_{j}-B_{j})\textit{i}_{j}=0
by definition of
V_{j}
which implies that
\textit{i}_{j}^{*}(Q_{j}-B_{j})=0
and so

(B_{j}^{-1}-C_{j}M^{-1}C_{j}^{*})(Q_{j}-B_{j})=0.

We prove that
(c)
implies
(d)
. Let us define
D=M^{-1/2},D_{j}=B_{j}^{-1/2},Q_{j}^{\prime}=B_{j}^{-1/2}Q_{j}B_{j}^{-1/2}
and
C_{j}^{\prime}=B_{j}^{1/2}C_{j}M^{-1/2}
for any
1\leq j\leq m.
We claim that
(\textbf{C'},p,\textbf{Q'})
is a generalized geometric BL datum. Indeed, as
B_{j}\leq Q_{j},
one has
Q_{j}^{\prime}\geq\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}
and

C_{j}^{\prime}(C_{j}^{\prime})^{*}=B_{j}^{1/2}C_{j}M^{-1}C_{j}^{*}B_{j}^{1/2}\leq B_{j}^{1/2}B_{j}^{-1}B_{j}^{1/2}=\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}.

Moreover,

\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}(C_{j}^{\prime})^{*}C_{j}^{\prime}=M^{-1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}B_{j}C_{j}\Bigl)M^{-1/2}=\mathrm{id}_{H}

by definition of
M
and

(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}^{\prime}(C_{j}^{\prime})^{*})(Q_{j}^{\prime}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}})=B_{j}^{1/2}(B_{j}^{-1}-C_{j}M^{-1}C_{j}^{*})B_{j}^{1/2}B_{j}^{-1/2}(Q_{j}-B_{j})B_{j}^{-1/2}=0

by the second assumption of
(c).

Finally,
(d)
implies
(a)
as a consequence of
Lemmas

3.16
and
3.19
.
∎

Example 3.23

(Some examples on Gaussian extremisability)
.

(i)

Consider
n=4,n_{i}=1,q_{i}=1
for all
1\leq i\leq 5.
Moreover, take
C_{1}=(1,-1,0,0),C_{2}=(0,1,0,0),C_{3}=(1,0,0,0),C_{4}=(0,0,1,0),C_{5}=(0,0,0,1)
and
p_{1}=p_{4}=p_{5}=2,p_{2}=p_{3}=\frac{1}{2}.

Then, one can check that

\sum_{i=1}^{5}p_{i}\dim(C_{i}V)=\sum_{V\not\subset\ker(C_{i})}p_{i}\geq\dim(V)\text{ for any linear subspace }V\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}.

Notice that

\sum_{i=1}^{5}p_{i}b_{i}C_{i}^{*}C_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}2b_{1}+\frac{1}{2}b_{3}&-2b_{1}&0&0\\
-2b_{1}&2b_{1}+\frac{1}{2}b_{2}&0&0\\
0&0&2b_{4}&0\\
0&0&0&2b_{5}\end{pmatrix}

which implies that

f(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4},b_{5})=\frac{b_{1}^{2}b_{2}^{1/2}b_{3}^{1/2}b_{4}^{2}b_{5}^{2}}{(b_{1}b_{2}+b_{1}b_{3}+\frac{1}{4}b_{2}b_{3})4b_{4}b_{5}}=\frac{b_{1}b_{4}b_{5}}{4}\frac{b_{2}^{1/2}b_{3}^{1/2}}{b_{2}+b_{3}+\frac{1}{4b_{1}}b_{2}b_{3}}.

To determine

\sup_{0<b_{i}\leq 1}f(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4},b_{5}),

it is clear that we need to take
b_{1}=b_{4}=b_{5}=1
so we are left with the computation of

\sup_{0<b_{2},b_{3}\leq 1}\frac{b_{2}^{1/2}b_{3}^{1/2}}{b_{2}+b_{3}+\frac{1}{4}b_{2}b_{3}}.

Since
b_{2}^{1/2}b_{3}^{1/2}\leq\frac{1}{2}(b_{2}+b_{3})
, the supremum is less than
\frac{1}{2}.
Moreover, if we take
b_{2}=b_{3}=\varepsilon
, then

\frac{b_{2}^{1/2}b_{3}^{1/2}}{b_{2}+b_{3}+\frac{1}{4}b_{2}b_{3}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\varepsilon+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}}=\frac{1}{2+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon}\rightarrow_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}.

So the supremum is
1/2
but can never be attained. This shows that not every non-degenerate Brascamp-Lieb data is extremizable.

(ii)

Let us instead consider a Gaussian extremizable Brascamp-Lieb datum.
Set
n=2=n_{1},n_{2}=1,Q_{1}=I_{2},Q_{2}=1
and
C_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\;1\\
0\;1\end{pmatrix},C_{2}=(1,2),p_{1}=1,p_{2}=2.
This datum satisfies the desired conditions for finiteness with a critical subspace
V=\mathrm{span}\{(2,-1)\}=\ker(C_{2})
. Writing
B_{1}
as
\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}^{(1)}\;b_{1}^{(2)}\\
b_{1}^{(2)}\;b_{1}^{(3)}\end{pmatrix}
, we find that

p_{1}C_{1}^{*}B_{1}C_{1}+p_{2}C_{2}^{*}B_{2}C_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}^{(1)}+2b_{2}\hskip 11.74988ptb_{1}^{(1)}+b_{1}^{(2)}+4b_{2}\\
b_{1}^{(1)}+b_{1}^{(2)}+4b_{2}\hskip 11.74988ptb_{1}^{(1)}+2b_{1}^{(2)}+b_{1}^{(3)}+8b_{2}\end{pmatrix}

and computing the determinant gives

b_{1}^{(1)}b_{1}^{(3)}-(b_{1}^{(2)})^{2}+2b_{2}(b_{1}^{(1)}+b_{1}^{(3)}-2b_{1}^{(2)}).

So, we need to maximize

\frac{\det(B)b_{2}^{2}}{\det(B)+2b_{2}\langle B(1,-1)^{T},(1,-1)^{T}\rangle}=\frac{b_{2}^{2}}{1+2b_{2}\langle\frac{B}{\det(B)}(1,-1)^{T},(1,-1)^{T}\rangle}

or in other words, to minimize
\langle\frac{B}{\det(B)}(1,-1)^{T},(1,-1)^{T}\rangle.

Notice that any
0<B\leq I_{2}
can be written as

B=U\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})U^{T},0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq 1,U\in O(n)

and by Rayleigh’s principle,

\Bigl\langle\frac{B}{\det(B)}\frac{(1,-1)^{T}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{(1,-1)^{T}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl\rangle\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}\geq 1.

Taking

B_{\varepsilon}=\begin{pmatrix}1\;1\\
-1\;1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon\;0\\
0\;1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\;1\\
-1\;1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+\varepsilon\hskip 11.74988pt1-\varepsilon\\
1-\varepsilon\hskip 11.74988pt1+\varepsilon\end{pmatrix},

we see that
\det(B_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon,B_{\varepsilon}(1,-1)^{T}=\varepsilon(1,-1)^{T}
which gives

\frac{b_{2}^{2}}{1+4b_{2}}

maximized at
b_{2}=1
. So, the supremum is equal to
\frac{1}{5}
and is achieved at the family
(B_{\varepsilon},1)
for any
0<\varepsilon<1
.

4.
Optimizers of Regularized Brascamp-Lieb Inequalities

We would like to turn to the study of equality cases for Gaussian extremizable Brascamp-Lieb data. Indeed, investigating equality cases is only meaningful when the datum
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
has a Brascamp-Lieb constant
\mathrm{BL}(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
that is extremizable. But, by
Theorem

3.12
, this implies that the Brascamp-Lieb constant must be Gaussian extremizable.

Moreover, by the argument of change of variables in
Proposition

3.21
, one may first reduce to considering the general extremizers of a generalized geometric BL.

Suppose that the inputs
(f_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
are Schwarz functions such that
f_{j}
is more log-convex than
g_{Q_{j}}
for any
1\leq j\leq m
and attain equality for the generalized geometric Brascamp-Lieb datum
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
,
i.e.

\int_{H}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(f_{j}\circ C_{j})^{p_{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{H_{j}}f_{j}\Bigl)^{p_{j}}.

Fix some time
t>1.
Let us first derive from the second condition obtained in
Remark

3.20
an equality between the gradients of
\log\widetilde{Q}
and
\log\widetilde{f}_{j}
, which will be fundamental for the rest of the argument.
Since
\langle(I-P)A(x),A(x)\rangle=0
with
P=TT^{*}
, one has

A(x)=T\beta(x)

for almost every
x\in H
where
\beta:H\rightarrow H.
This implies that
p_{j}^{1/2}C_{j}\overrightarrow{v_{j}}=p_{j}^{1/2}C_{j}\beta(x)
for any
1\leq j\leq m
by equality of the rows. In other words,

-\frac{1}{4\pi}(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x)=C_{j}\beta(x)\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq j\leq m.

This shows that
\beta
is smooth and
A(x)=T\beta(x)
must hold for all
x\in H.

Then, as

\log\widetilde{Q}(x)=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\dim(H_{j})-\dim(H)}{2}\log(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\log(\widetilde{f}_{j}(C_{j}x)),

one has, using
\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}=\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}
,

\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x)=-4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}^{*}C_{j}\beta(x)=-4\pi\beta(x).

Thus,

(4.1)

C_{j}\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x)=(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x)

for all
x\in H.

It is now time to extract as much information as possible from the first condition of
Remark

3.20
.
Setting
B_{j}=\bigl[(t-1)\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}+Q_{j}^{-1}\bigl]^{-1},
note that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{Q_{j}}{t}-B_{j}&=&\displaystyle\frac{Q_{j}}{t}-\bigl[(t-1)\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}+Q_{j}^{-1}\bigl]^{-1}\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{Q_{j}}{t}\bigl[(t-1)\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}+Q_{j}^{-1}\bigl]-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}\Bigl)\bigl[(t-1)\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}+Q_{j}^{-1}\bigl]^{-1}\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle\frac{t-1}{t}(Q_{j}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}})\bigl[(t-1)\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}+Q_{j}^{-1}\bigl]^{-1}.\end{array}

By assumption,

\mathrm{Tr}((\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})(D^{2}\log\widetilde{f}_{j}+\frac{2\pi}{t}Q_{j}))=0

and
(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})(Q_{j}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}})=0
, which shows that

\mathrm{Tr}((\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})(D^{2}\log\widetilde{f}_{j}+2\pi B_{j}))=0.

Further, since
\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*}
and
D^{2}\log\widetilde{f}_{j}+2\pi B_{j}
are both positive semi-definite, the previous trace equality actually means that

(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})^{1/2}(D^{2}\log\widetilde{f}_{j}+2\pi B_{j})(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})^{1/2}=0.

For notational convenience, set
A_{j}=\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*}<\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}.
As
A_{j},Q_{j}
commute, they are simultaneously diagonalizable or in other words, there exists an orthonormal basis of
H_{j}
,
\{v_{1}^{(j)},\cdots,v_{n_{j}}^{(j)}\}
, such that

A_{j}v_{i}^{(j)}=a_{i}^{(j)}v_{i}^{(j)},Q_{j}v_{i}^{(j)}=q_{i}^{(j)}v_{i}^{(j)}\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq i\leq n_{j}

with
1>a_{1}^{(j)}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n_{j}}^{(j)}\geq 0,\;q_{i}^{(j)}\geq 1
for any
1\leq i\leq n_{j}
(we can only ask for one set of eigenvalues to be ordered). Set
0\leq k_{j}\leq n_{j}
such that
a_{i}^{(j)}>0
for any
1\leq i\leq k_{j},a_{i}^{(j)}=0
for all
k_{j}+1\leq i\leq n_{j}.
Denoting
V_{j}\in O(n_{j})
the change of basis matrix,

Q_{j}=V_{j}\,\mathrm{diag}(q_{1}^{(j)},\cdots,q_{n_{j}}^{(j)})V_{j}^{T}\Longrightarrow B_{j}=V_{j}\,\mathrm{diag}\Bigl(\frac{1}{t-1+\frac{1}{q_{1}^{(j)}}},\cdots,\frac{1}{t-1+\frac{1}{q_{n_{j}}^{(j)}}}\Bigl)V_{j}^{T}.

Moreover, as
(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})(Q_{j}-\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}})=0
,
q_{i}^{(j)}=1
for any
1\leq i\leq k_{j}.
In particular, the
k_{j}
-th first eigenvalues of
B_{j}
are
t^{-1}.
Applying the eigenvectors of
A_{j}
that are not associated to a zero eigenvalue to the equality
A_{j}^{1/2}(D^{2}\log\widetilde{f}_{j}+2\pi B_{j})A_{j}^{1/2}=0
, one obtains that

A_{j}^{1/2}((D^{2}\log\widetilde{f}_{j})v_{i}^{(j)}+2\pi B_{j}v_{i}^{(j)})=0\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq i\leq k_{j}

which implies that

(D^{2}\log\widetilde{f}_{j})v_{i}^{(j)}=-\frac{2\pi}{t}v_{i}^{(j)}+\sum_{l=k_{j}+1}^{n_{j}}\alpha_{l}v_{l}^{(j)}.

This means that decomposing
H_{j}
as
\mathrm{Ran}(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})=\mathrm{span}\{v_{1}^{(j)},\cdots,v_{k_{j}}^{(j)}\}
in direct sum with
\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})=\mathrm{span}\{v_{k_{j}+1}^{(j)},\cdots,v_{n_{j}}^{(j)}\}
, one gets that

D^{2}(\log\widetilde{f}_{j})=V_{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\mathrm{diag}(-\frac{2\pi}{t},\cdots,-\frac{2\pi}{t})&\cdot_{j}\\
\hline\cr\cdot_{j}&\bullet_{j}\end{array}\right)V_{j}^{T}.

This uses completely the first condition of
Remark

3.20
in the sense that if
D^{2}(\log\widetilde{f}_{j})
is of this form, then it satisfies the trace equality.
Recall the two remaining conditions that are available to us:

(4.2)

(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x)=C_{j}\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x)\text{ and }D^{2}\log\widetilde{f}_{j}\geq-2\pi B_{j}\geq-2\pi\frac{Q_{j}}{t}.

It will be useful to decompose

as a direct sum containing
\ker(C_{j})
. More precisely, one may write

C_{j}x=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\beta_{i}v_{i}^{(j)},C_{j}(C_{j}^{*}v_{i}^{(j)})=(1-a_{i}^{(j)})v_{i}^{(j)}

Unless
Q_{j}>\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}
(in which case

[
34
]
), we have

0\leq D^{2}(\log\widetilde{f}_{j})+2\pi\frac{Q_{j}}{t}=V_{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\mathrm{diag}(0,\cdots,0)&\cdot_{j}\\
\hline\cr\cdot_{j}&\bullet_{j}+\mathrm{diag}(\frac{2\pi}{t}q_{k_{j}+1},\cdots,\frac{2\pi}{t}q_{n_{j}})\end{array}\right)V_{j}^{T},

which means that

By differentiating the first equality in

4.2

D^{2}(\log\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x)C_{j}=C_{j}D^{2}\log\widetilde{Q}(x)

and in particular, for

,

and so,

D^{2}(\log\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x)=V_{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\mathrm{diag}(-\frac{2\pi}{t},\cdots,-\frac{2\pi}{t})&0\\
\hline\cr 0&C_{j}D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x)C_{j}^{*}\bigl|_{\ker(A_{j})}\end{array}\right)V_{j}^{T}.

Thus, further investigation of

is needed.

Notice that for
c_{j}\in\mathrm{Ran}(C_{j}^{*}),c_{j}=C_{j}^{*}e_{j}
,

\langle\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x),c_{j}\rangle=-4\pi\langle\beta(x),C_{j}^{*}e_{j}\rangle=\langle\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j}(C_{j}x),e_{j}\rangle.

Differentiating in a direction of
\ker(C_{j})
, one obtains that for
a_{j}\in\ker(C_{j})
,

\langle D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x)a_{j},C_{j}^{*}e_{j}\rangle=0.

Moreover, for any
1\leq i\leq k_{j}
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}C_{j}D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x)C_{j}^{*}v_{i}^{(j)}&=&D^{2}(\log\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x)C_{j}C_{j}^{*}v_{i}^{(j)}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle(1-a_{i}^{(j)})D^{2}(\log\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x)v_{i}^{(j)}=-\frac{2\pi}{t}(1-a_{i}^{(j)})v_{i}^{(j)}\end{array}

which implies that

D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x)C_{j}^{*}v_{i}^{(j)}=-\frac{2\pi}{t}C_{j}^{*}v_{i}^{(j)}\text{ since }D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x)C_{j}^{*}v_{i}^{(j)}\perp\ker(C_{j}).

Hence,

D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\ker(C_{j})&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left.\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\mathrm{diag}(-\frac{2\pi}{t},\cdots,-\frac{2\pi}{t})&0\\
\hline\cr 0&W_{j}\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right)

where
W_{j}=C_{j}^{*}\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})
and the second block is with respect to the basis
C_{j}^{*}\mathrm{Ran}(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})=:\widetilde{W}_{j}.

Set

K=\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}\bigl(\ker(C_{j})\oplus C_{j}^{*}\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})\bigl).

As we already know the form of
D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})
on
C_{j}^{*}\mathrm{Ran}(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})
for any
1\leq j\leq m,
what is left to determine is the form of
D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})
on
K.
In fact,
K
might not be critical so we will further decompose
D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})
with respect to the largest critical subspace that is invariant under
C_{i}C_{i}^{*}
(it will be seen why this invariance property is crucial for the argument to hold) and its orthogonal complement.

We introduce the following notation: if
A\subset B\subset H
, then the perpendicular of
A
with respect to
B
will be denoted by

A^{\perp,B}=A^{\perp}\cap B.

Let us remark that

C_{j}^{*}\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})=\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H}-C_{j}^{*}C_{j})\text{ for any }1\leq j\leq m.

On the one hand,
x\in C_{j}^{*}\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})\Longrightarrow x=C_{j}^{*}z
where
z\in\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})
and

(C_{j}^{*}C_{j})x=C_{j}^{*}(C_{j}C_{j}^{*}z)=C_{j}^{*}z=x

so
x\in\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*}).
On the other hand,
x\in\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H}-C_{j}^{*}C_{j})
implies that
x=C_{j}^{*}(C_{j}x)
. But

C_{j}C_{j}^{*}(C_{j}x)=C_{j}x\Longrightarrow C_{j}x\in\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*}).

To have a better grasp on the strategy of the proof, let us compute two examples where the behavior of the data with respect to

.

\begin{array}[]{lll}\ker(C_{1})=\{0\},C_{1}^{*}\ker(I_{2}-C_{1}C_{1}^{*})=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1}\};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\ker(C_{2})=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1}\},C_{2}^{*}\ker(1-C_{2}C_{2}^{*})=\{0\};\\

First, we write the Brascamp-Lieb inequality with such a datum: for
f_{i}
more log-convex than
g_{Q_{i}}
for any

,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{1}\Bigl(x,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}y\Bigl)^{1/2}f_{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}y\Bigl)^{1/2}f_{3}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}x+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}y\Bigl)^{1/2}f_{4}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}x-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}y\Bigl)^{1/2}\,dx\,dy\leq\prod_{i=1}^{4}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}f_{i}\Bigl)^{1/2}.

One can directly take
t=1
as it does not change anything to the argument. Using the characterization of
D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})
when in terms of special eigenspaces of
C_{1}
or
C_{2}
, one gets

D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{11}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x,y)\;0\\
0\hskip 23.49976pt\hskip 23.49976pt-2\pi\end{pmatrix}

with
\partial_{11}(\log\widetilde{Q})\geq-4\pi.
Then,
\partial_{1}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x,y)
is constant in

and so

\log\widetilde{Q}(x,y)=\log Q(x)-\pi y^{2}-by\text{ and }\nabla(\log\widetilde{Q})(x,y)=((\log Q)^{\prime}(x),-2\pi y-b).

We may forget about the constants since, by
Lemma

3.13
, the extremizers that will be derived can be multiplied by any positive constants.
Now, let us use
Equation

4.1
for
j=1,2,3,4.
First,

(\log f_{3})^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}x+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}y\Bigl)=C_{3}\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\log Q)^{\prime}(x)-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{2}}y-\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}},

(\log f_{4})^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}x-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}y\Bigl)=C_{4}\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\log Q)^{\prime}(x)+\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{2}}y+\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}

and taking

(\log f_{3})^{\prime}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\log Q)^{\prime}(x)-\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}},(\log f_{4})^{\prime}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\log Q)^{\prime}(x)+\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}.

This implies that

\log f_{3}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\log Q)(\sqrt{2}x)-\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}x,\log f_{4}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\log Q)(\sqrt{2}x)+\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}x.

For
j=1,2
, we have

\nabla(\log f_{1})\Bigl(x,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}y\Bigl)=C_{1}\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x,y)=\Bigl((\log Q)^{\prime}(x),-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{2}}y-\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl)

and

which implies that

\log f_{1}(x,y)=\log Q(x)-\pi y^{2}-\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}y,\log f_{2}(y)=-\pi y^{2}-\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}y.

To find the final form of

, we use the equation of

to get

(\log Q)^{\prime}(y)=(\log Q)^{\prime}(0)-2\pi y,

which means that

and consequently,

\begin{array}[]{lll}f_{1}(x,y)=c_{1}e^{-\pi x^{2}-ax}e^{-\pi y^{2}-by},f_{2}(y)=c_{2}e^{-\pi y^{2}-by},\\
\\
f_{3}(x)=c_{3}Q(\sqrt{2}x)^{1/2}e^{-bx}=c_{3}e^{-\pi x^{2}-(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}+b)x},\\
\\
f_{4}(x)=c_{4}Q(\sqrt{2}x)^{1/2}e^{bx}=c_{4}e^{-\pi x^{2}-(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}-b)x}\end{array}

for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }a,b\in\mathbb{R}
arbitrary. Let us show that if
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}
are of this form, then they are extremizers. Indeed, first notice that
f_{i}
are more log-convex than
g_{Q_{i}}
for any

Second, let us compute

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{4}f_{j}(C_{j}(x,y))^{1/2}\,dx\,dy&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\pi x^{2}-ax}e^{-\pi y^{2}-\sqrt{2}by}\,dx\,dy\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle e^{a^{2}/4\pi}e^{b^{2}/2\pi}\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{4}\Bigl(\int f_{j}\Bigl)^{1/2}&=&\displaystyle e^{a^{2}/8\pi}e^{b^{2}/8\pi}e^{b^{2}/8\pi}e^{(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}+b)^{2}/8\pi}e^{(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}-b)^{2}/8\pi}\\
&=&e^{a^{2}/4\pi}e^{b^{2}/2\pi},\end{array}

since
\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\pi x^{2}-cx}\,dx=e^{c^{2}/4\pi}
for any
c\in\mathbb{R}.

Recall that

D^{2}(\log\widetilde{Q})(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\ker(C_{j})&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left.\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\mathrm{diag}(-\frac{2\pi}{t},\cdots,-\frac{2\pi}{t})&0\\
\hline\cr 0&W_{j}\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right)

where
W_{j}=C_{j}^{*}\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})
and the second block is with respect to the basis
\{C_{j}^{*}v_{i}^{(j)}:1\leq i\leq k_{j}\}=C_{j}^{*}\mathrm{Ran}(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})=:\widetilde{W}_{j}.

We introduce the definition of generalized independent subspace that plays a crucial role in
[
34
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

A subspace
\widetilde{H}
of
K
is generalized independent with respect to the generalized geometric datum if it is not
\{0\}
and has the form

\widetilde{H}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{H}_{j}^{a}

where for each
j,\widetilde{H}_{j}^{a}
is either
\ker(C_{j})
or
W_{j}=C_{j}^{*}\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{j}}-C_{j}C_{j}^{*})=\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H}-C_{j}^{*}C_{j}).

Note that for
x\in\widetilde{H}
a generalized independent subspace of
K
, either
C_{i}x=0
or
C_{i}^{*}C_{i}x=x
for any
1\leq i\leq m
. This implies that
C_{i}^{*}C_{i}\widetilde{H}\subset\widetilde{H}
for all
1\leq i\leq m.
By
Lemma

4.5
, any generalized independent subspace is critical.

Moreover, define
\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}
as the orthogonal complement of
\bigoplus_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\widetilde{K}_{k}
as subspace of
K
where
\{\widetilde{K}_{k}:k=1,\cdots,k_{0}\}
is an enumeration of the generalized independent subspaces of
K
. Then, arguing exactly as in
[
34
, Lemma 13]
, one obtains

D^{2}\log\widetilde{Q}(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\mathrm{diag}(-\frac{2\pi}{t},\cdots,-\frac{2\pi}{t})&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left.\begin{array}[]{ c | c }\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\widetilde{K}_{1}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left.\begin{array}[]{c | c}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\ddots&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left.\begin{array}[]{c | c}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\widetilde{K}_{k_{0}}&0\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\hline\cr 0&\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right.\par\end{array}\right).

Intuitively, we will decompose further
\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}
into critical subspaces and a non-critical part,
i.e.

\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}=\bigoplus_{k=k_{0}+1}^{k_{1}}\widetilde{K}_{k}\oplus\text{Remaining non-critical part}.

On
\bigoplus_{k=1}^{k_{1}}\widetilde{K}_{k}
, the Brascamp-Lieb data will be reduced to a geometric Brascamp-Lieb data with
\bigoplus_{k=k_{0}+1}^{k_{1}}\widetilde{K}_{k}
being the dependent part of the independent decomposition using the terminology of
[
34
]
. Moreover, on
\widetilde{K}_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq k_{0}
, we have an arbitrary Jacobian as in the situation of Hölder’s inequality and on
\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}
, we have a constant Jacobian, which means that the functions are Gaussians as in the case of Young’s inequality.

Thus, for any
1\leq j\leq m
, we have

\log\widetilde{Q}(x)=-\frac{\pi}{t}\langle P_{\widetilde{W}_{j}}x,P_{\widetilde{W}_{j}}(x+b_{j})\rangle+u_{j}^{\parallel}(P_{\ker(C_{j})}x)+u_{j}^{\perp}(P_{W_{j}}x)

and

\log\widetilde{Q}(x)=-\frac{\pi}{t}\langle P_{K^{\perp}}x,P_{K^{\perp}}(x+b_{\perp})\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}u_{\widetilde{K}_{k}}(P_{\widetilde{K}_{k}}x)+u_{\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}}({P_{\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}}}x).

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
K
has no generalized independent subspace. Then any optimizer must be Gaussian.

Proof.

Recall that

\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x)=-\frac{\pi}{t}P_{\widetilde{W}_{j}}^{*}P_{\widetilde{W}_{j}}(2x+b_{j})+P_{\ker(C_{j})}^{*}\nabla u_{j}^{||}(P_{\ker(C_{j})}x)+P_{W_{j}}^{*}\nabla u_{j}^{\perp}(P_{W_{j}}x).

As it is well-known that the Fourier transform of a distribution supported at the origin is a polynomial (and reversely, the Fourier transform of a polynomial is a distribution supported at the origin), the Fourier transform of
\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x)
is supported on
\ker(C_{j})\cup W_{j}
for any
1\leq j\leq m.
As this holds for any
1\leq j\leq m
, it is in fact supported on

\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}(\ker(C_{j})\cup W_{j})=\{0\},

since
K
has no generalized independent subspace. Hence, using growth estimates,
\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x)
is a linear polynomial. More precisely, as we are assuming that
f_{j}
is a Schwartz function for any
1\leq j\leq m
, one may show that
\nabla\log(\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x)
has linear growth in
x
(see
[
34
, Lemma 5]
) which shows the previous claim as

\nabla\log\widetilde{Q}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}C_{j}(\nabla\log\widetilde{f}_{j})(C_{j}x).

This means that all
\widetilde{f}_{j}
are Gaussians.
∎

We can consider the largest subspace of
\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}
such that it is invariant under
C_{i}^{*}C_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and denote its orthogonal complement in
\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}
by
H_{\mathrm{dep}}^{\perp,K}.
Moreover, by the next lemma, this subspace is critical.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
V\subset K
satisfies
C_{i}^{*}C_{i}V\subset V
for all
1\leq i\leq m
where
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is a generalized geometric BL, then
V
is critical.

Proof.

By assumption,
C_{i}^{*}C_{i}V\subset V
for any
1\leq i\leq m.
Thus for any
x\in C_{i}V,y\in V,

\langle C_{i}^{*}x-(C_{i}|_{V})^{*}x,y\rangle=0\Longrightarrow(C_{i}|_{V})^{*}x=C_{i}^{*}x\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq i\leq m\text{ since }C_{i}^{*}x\in V.

This shows that
(C_{i}|_{V})^{*}=C_{i}^{*}|_{C_{i}V}
for any
1\leq i\leq m.

Moreover, for
x\in C_{i}V
, there exists
z\in V\subset K
such that
x=C_{i}z.
As
z=C_{i}^{*}z_{i}+\widetilde{z}_{i}
where
z_{i}\in\ker(\mathrm{id}_{H_{i}}-C_{i}C_{i}^{*}),\widetilde{z}_{i}\in\ker(C_{i}),

x=C_{i}C_{i}^{*}z_{i}=z_{i}

so
C_{i}|_{V}(C_{i}|_{V})^{*}x=C_{i}C_{i}^{*}x=x.
In other words, one has

C_{i}|_{V}(C_{i}|_{V})^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{C_{i}V}\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq i\leq m.

Moreover,

\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}(C_{i}|_{V})^{*}C_{i}|_{V}=\mathrm{id}_{V}

by restricting the equality
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{i}^{*}C_{i}=\mathrm{id}_{H}
on
V
, which holds since
(\textbf{C},p,\textbf{Q})
is a generalized geometric BL. Taking traces, the claim follows.
∎

This shows that there exists a generalized maximum critical decomposition of
\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}

\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{dep}}=\bigoplus_{k=k_{0}+1}^{k_{1}}\widetilde{K}_{k}\oplus H_{\mathrm{dep}}^{\perp,K},

which implies that

H_{\mathrm{dep}}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{k_{1}}\widetilde{K}_{k},

such that the purely quadratic term in this Gaussian is the tensor product of multiples of the identity operator on each
\widetilde{K}_{k}
for
k_{0}+1\leq k\leq k_{1}
,
H_{\mathrm{dep}}^{\perp,K}
appearing in this decomposition. Furthermore, by definition of
\bigoplus_{k=k_{0}+1}^{k_{1}}\widetilde{K}_{k}
and as
\widetilde{K}_{k}
for any
1\leq k\leq k_{0}
is invariant under all
C_{i}^{*}C_{i}
, this is also the case for
H_{\mathrm{dep}}
, which means that
H_{\mathrm{dep}}
is critical by the previous lemma. Hence, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\log\widetilde{Q}(x)&=&\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{t}\langle P_{K^{\perp}}x,P_{K^{\perp}}(x+b_{\perp})\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}u_{\widetilde{K}_{k}}(P_{\widetilde{K}_{k}}x)\\
&&\displaystyle-\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{k_{1}}d_{k}\langle P_{\widetilde{K}_{k}}x,P_{\widetilde{K}_{k}}(x+b_{k})\rangle-c\langle P_{H_{\mathrm{dep}}^{\perp,K}}x,P_{H_{\mathrm{dep}}^{\perp,K}}(x+\widetilde{b}_{\perp})\rangle.\end{array}

where
b_{\perp}\in K^{\perp},b_{k}\in\widetilde{K}_{k}
and
\widetilde{b}_{\perp}\in H_{\mathrm{dep}}^{\perp,K}.

The final step to be able to determine the extremizers is to understand the special form of the Gaussian constant
c
on the block

Appendix A
A general Caffarelli’s Contraction Theorem

A celebrated result of Caffarelli in
[
13
]
concerns the Lipschitz property of the optimal transportation map under log-convexity (resp. log-concavity) assumptions on the source measure (resp. the target measure). The following statement was shown in
[
13
]
and subsequently extended in
[
24
]
: if
d\mu=e^{-V}\,dx
and
d\nu=e^{-W}\,dx
are probability measures in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\mu
supported in all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that there exist
\lambda,\Lambda>0
with

\nabla^{2}V\leq\lambda I_{n}\text{ and }\nabla^{2}W\geq\Lambda I_{n}

and
\nabla\Phi
is the Brenier map transporting
\mu
to
\nu
, then

\nabla^{2}\Phi\leq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda}}I_{n}.

For our purposes, we would like to prove a similar statement with slightly more general assumptions, replacing
\lambda I_{n},\Lambda I_{n}
by positive definite matrices.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
d\mu=e^{-V}\,dx
and
d\nu=e^{-W}\,dx
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\mu
supported on all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that there exist
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\ni A,B>0
with

(A.1)

\nabla^{2}V\leq A\text{ and }\nabla^{2}W\geq B.

Let
\nabla\Phi
be the Brenier map transporting
\mu
to
\nu.
Then,

\nabla^{2}\Phi\leq A^{1/2}(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{-1/2}A^{1/2}.

This result can actually be deduced from the work of Chewi and Pooladian (see
[
16
]
) who proved the theorem for two commuting matrices
A
and
B
under mild regularity assumptions on
V
and
W.
It was also proved directly by Gozlan and Sylvestre in
[
22
]
.
However, the proof we would like to present does not use any advanced tools such as the entropic regularization, which the aforementioned authors brought into play to deduce an extension of Caffarelli’s theorem.

Remark A.2
.

Recall that the Hessian of the Brenier potential between the Gaussian distributions
N(0,A^{-1})
and
N(0,B^{-1})
, which trivially satisfy the constraint
Equation

A.1
, is the matrix

B^{-1/2}(B^{-1/2}A^{-1}B^{-1/2})^{-1/2}B^{-1/2}=B^{-1/2}(B^{1/2}AB^{1/2})^{1/2}B^{-1/2}

by
[
32
, Lemma 2.3]
. But setting
C=B^{1/2}A^{1/2}(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{-1/2}A^{1/2}B^{1/2}
, one has

\begin{array}[]{lll}C^{2}&=&B^{1/2}A^{1/2}(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{-1/2}A^{1/2}BA^{1/2}(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{-1/2}A^{1/2}B^{1/2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&B^{1/2}A^{1/2}A^{1/2}B^{1/2}=B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}.\end{array}

This proves that

A^{1/2}(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{-1/2}A^{1/2}=B^{-1/2}(B^{1/2}AB^{1/2})^{1/2}B^{-1/2}.

This observation shows that the previous theorem is sharp for every pair of matrices and implies that the Brenier potential between Gaussians achieves the largest possible Hessian among all source and target measures obeying the constraint
Equation

A.1
.

The proof strategy is based on the maximum principle and is very similar to
[
33
]
.

Proof.

Set

\widetilde{V}=V(A^{-1/2}\cdot)+\frac{1}{2}\log(\det A),\widetilde{W}=W(A^{1/2}\cdot)-\frac{1}{2}\log(\det A)

and
\widetilde{\mu}=e^{-\widetilde{V}}\,dx=(A^{1/2})_{\#}\mu,\widetilde{\nu}=e^{-\widetilde{W}}\,dx=(A^{-1/2})_{\#}\nu
. Then, defining
\widetilde{\Phi}(x)=\Phi(A^{-1/2}x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\nabla\Phi
being the Brenier map that transports
\mu
to
\nu
,
\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}
is the Brenier map that transports
\widetilde{\mu}
to
\widetilde{\nu}
. Moreover,

\nabla^{2}\widetilde{V}=A^{-1/2}\nabla^{2}V(A^{-1/2}\cdot)A^{-1/2}\leq I_{n},\nabla^{2}\widetilde{W}\geq A^{1/2}BA^{1/2}.

Since

\nabla^{2}\Phi=A^{1/2}\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}(A^{1/2}\cdot)A^{1/2},

we see that it suffices to show that

\nabla^{2}\widetilde{\Phi}\leq(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{-1/2}.

In other words, we reduced the claim to showing that:

\text{ If }D^{2}V\leq I_{n}\text{ and }D^{2}W\geq A>0,\text{ then }\nabla^{2}\Phi\leq A^{-1/2}.

First of all, we replace
e^{-W}
by
e^{-W_{r}}
with
W_{r}:\overline{B}_{r}(0)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
W_{r}(x)=W(x)+C^{\prime}
where
C^{\prime}
is a normalizing constant chosen so that
\int e^{-W_{r}}\,dx=1.
Let us write the Brenier map transporting
e^{-V}\,dx
to
e^{-W_{r}}\,dx
as
\nabla\Phi^{r}
with
\Phi^{r}
convex.

We will be interested in the function

K(x,\alpha)=\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle-\frac{\Phi^{r}(x+h\alpha)+\Phi^{r}(x-h\alpha)-2\Phi^{r}(x)}{h^{2}}\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\forall\alpha\in S^{n-1}

for some fixed
h>0.
One may assume by approximation that
V,W\in C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Hence, by Caffarelli’s regularity theory,
K
is
C^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S^{n-1}
and the goal is to show that
K
is non-negative.
By the convexity of
\Phi^{r}
,

K(x,\alpha)\leq\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle\hskip 11.74988pt\forall\alpha\in S^{n-1}.

As in the limit as
x
tends to infinity, this inequality becomes an equality (by
[
33
, Lemma 2.1]
or by
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
). This implies that
K
has a global minimum that we assume to be attained at
(x_{0},\alpha_{0})
.

Then,

\partial_{x_{i}}K(x_{0},\alpha_{0})=0\Longrightarrow\Phi_{i}^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0})+\Phi_{i}^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0})-2\Phi_{i}^{r}(x_{0})=0\hskip 11.74988pt\forall 1\leq i\leq n

which gives

\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0})+\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0})-2\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0})=0.

In addition, for any vector
v\in\mathrm{span}\{\alpha\}^{\perp}
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{v}K(x_{0},\alpha_{0})&=&\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{K\bigl(x_{0},\frac{\alpha_{0}+tv}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\bigl)-K(x_{0},\alpha_{0})}{t}\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\displaystyle 2\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,v\rangle-\frac{\langle\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0}),hv\rangle-\langle\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0}),hv\rangle}{h^{2}}=0\end{array}

since
(x_{0},\alpha_{0})
is a local minimum. Hence, there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0}=\frac{\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0})-\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0})}{2h}-\lambda\alpha_{0}

and so

\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}\pm h\alpha_{0})=\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0})\pm h(A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0}+\lambda\alpha_{0}).

Taking the logarithm of the Monge-Ampère equation, one has

(A.2)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\log(\det\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0}))+\log(\det\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0}))-2\log(\det\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0}))\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr=W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0}))+W_{r}(\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0}))-2W_{r}(\Phi^{r}(x_{0}))\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr-[V(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0})+V(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0})-2V(x_{0})].\end{array}

Since
A\mapsto\det(A)
is a log-concave function, the left-hand side is less than

D(\log\det)(\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0}))E=\mathrm{Tr}(\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0})^{-1}E)=\mathrm{Tr}(\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0})^{-1/2}\,E\;\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0})^{-1/2})

where
E=\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0})+\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0})-2\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x_{0}).
As
E
is the Hessian of the function

x\mapsto\Phi^{r}(x+h\alpha_{0})+\Phi^{r}(x-h\alpha_{0})-2\Phi^{r}(x)

which attains a local maximum at
x_{0}
,
E
is negative semi-definite. Hence, the left-hand side of
Equation

A.2
is non-positive.

On the right-hand side of
Equation

A.2
, we notice that
\nabla^{2}V\leq I_{n}
gives

V(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0})+V(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0})-2V(x_{0})\leq h^{2}|\alpha_{0}|^{2}.

Moreover, denoting
v=\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0})-\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0})=\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0})-\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0})
, one has

\begin{array}[]{lll}W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0}))+W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0}))-2W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}))\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr=\displaystyle W_{r}(\nabla\Phi_{r}(x_{0})+v)-W_{r}(\nabla\Phi_{r}(x_{0}))+W_{r}(\nabla\Phi_{r}(x_{0})-v)-W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}))\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\langle v,\nabla W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0})+sv)-\nabla W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0})-sv)\rangle\,ds\\
\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-s}^{s}\langle v,\nabla^{2}W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0})+tv)v\rangle\,dt\,ds.\end{array}

As
\nabla^{2}W_{r}\geq A
, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{lll}W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0}))+W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0}))-2W_{r}(\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\geq h^{2}\langle A(A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0}+\lambda\alpha_{0}),A^{-1/2}\alpha+\lambda\alpha_{0}\rangle.\end{array}

since
v=h(A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0}+\lambda\alpha_{0}).
Hence,

2\lambda\langle A^{1/2}\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\rangle+\lambda^{2}\langle A\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\rangle=-\langle\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\rangle+\langle A(A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0}+\lambda\alpha_{0}),A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0}+\lambda\alpha_{0}\rangle\leq 0

and so
\lambda\leq 0.
Thus,

\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\rangle\geq\frac{\langle\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0}),\alpha_{0}\rangle-\langle\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0}),\alpha_{0}\rangle}{2h}.

This implies by the convexity of
\Phi^{r}
that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0})+\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0})-2\Phi^{r}(x_{0})}{h^{2}}&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{\langle\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha_{0}),\alpha_{0}\rangle-\langle\nabla\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha_{0}),\alpha_{0}\rangle}{h}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&2\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\rangle\end{array}

which allows us to conclude that

K(x,\alpha)\geq K(x_{0},\alpha_{0})\geq-\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\rangle\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\alpha\in S^{n-1}

since
(x_{0},\alpha_{0})
is a global minimum. This is equivalent to

\frac{\Phi^{r}(x+h\alpha)+\Phi^{r}(x-h\alpha)-2\Phi^{r}(x)}{h^{2}}\leq\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle+\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\rangle.

Taking
M
to be the ratio of the largest and smallest eigenvalues of
A^{-1/2}
, one obtains

\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\rangle\leq M\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle

by Rayleigh’s principle,
i.e.
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni B>0
,

\langle Bx,x\rangle\geq\lambda_{\min}(B)\text{ and }\langle Bx,x\rangle\leq\lambda_{\max}(B)\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x\in S^{n-1}.

Therefore,

\frac{\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha)+\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha)-2\Phi^{r}(x)}{h^{2}}\leq(M+1)\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle.

Using the fact that the estimate is uniform in

, we apply the same strategy of bootstrapping as in
[

]
to get that

\frac{\Phi^{r}(x_{0}+h\alpha)+\Phi^{r}(x_{0}-h\alpha)-2\Phi^{r}(x)}{h^{2}}\leq\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle

which asserts that
K(x_{0},\alpha_{0})\geq 0
and proves that

K(x,\alpha)\geq 0\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ and }\alpha\in S^{n-1}.

Letting

, this shows that

\langle\nabla^{2}\Phi^{r}(x)\alpha,\alpha\rangle\leq\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle

which translates to

\frac{\langle\nabla\Phi^{r}(x+h\alpha)-\nabla\Phi^{r}(x-h\alpha),\alpha\rangle}{2h}\leq\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle

by integration. Since
e^{-W_{r}}\,dx

weakly, the stability of the transport map gives that

\frac{\langle\nabla\Phi(x+h\alpha)-\nabla\Phi(x-h\alpha),\alpha\rangle}{2h}\leq\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle.

Taking the limit as

, we get

\langle\nabla^{2}\Phi(x)\alpha,\alpha\rangle\leq\langle A^{-1/2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle\hskip 11.74988pt\forall\alpha\in S^{n-1}

which concludes the proof.

First, let us establish the following simple trace inequality:

Lemma A.4
.

For
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\ni B>0,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\ni C,D\geq 0,C\neq 0
,

\mathrm{Tr}(B^{-1}CDC)\geq\mathrm{Tr}(DB)^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(DC)^{2}.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathrm{Tr}(DC)^{2}&=&\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}((D^{1/2}CB^{-1/2})(D^{1/2}B^{1/2})^{T})^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(B^{-1/2}CDCB^{-1/2})\mathrm{Tr}(D^{1/2}BD^{1/2})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(B^{-1}CDC)\mathrm{Tr}(DB).\end{array}

∎

Proof.

We only sketch a formal proof.
Let us first assume that
A=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

For a given unit vector
e\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a function
\xi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\xi_{e}
(resp.
\xi_{ee}
) the first (resp. second) directional derivative of
\xi
in the direction
e.
Taking the logarithm in the Monge-Ampère equation and differentiating twice in the direction
e
gives

V_{ee}=\langle\nabla^{2}W(\nabla\Phi)\nabla^{2}\Phi e,\nabla^{2}\Phi e\rangle+\langle\nabla W,\nabla\Phi_{ee}\rangle-\mathrm{Tr}((\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{-1}\nabla^{2}\Phi_{ee})+\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(((\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{-1}\nabla^{2}\Phi_{e})^{2}\bigl).

Using
\nabla^{2}W\geq G^{-1}
, the previous inequality leads to

V_{ee}\geq\langle G^{-1}\nabla^{2}\Phi e,\nabla^{2}\Phi e\rangle+\langle\nabla W,\nabla\Phi_{ee}\rangle-\mathrm{Tr}((\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{-1}\nabla^{2}\Phi_{ee})+\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(((\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{-1}\nabla^{2}\Phi_{e})^{2}\bigl).

Summing on both sides over the standard basis
\{e_{i}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
yields

\Delta V\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\nabla^{2}\Phi\,G^{-1}\,\nabla^{2}\Phi e_{i},e_{i}\rangle+\langle\nabla W,\nabla\Delta\Phi\rangle-\mathrm{Tr}((\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{-1}\nabla^{2}\Delta\Phi)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(((\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{-1}\nabla^{2}\Phi_{e_{i}})^{2}\bigl).

Assume that
\Delta\Phi
attains its maximum at
x_{0}.
On the one hand,
\nabla\Delta\Phi(x_{0})=0
and
\nabla^{2}\Delta\Phi(x_{0})\leq 0
and so

\langle\nabla W(x_{0}),\nabla\Delta\Phi(x_{0})\rangle-\mathrm{Tr}[(\nabla^{2}\Phi(x_{0}))^{-1}\nabla^{2}\Delta\Phi(x_{0})]\geq 0.

On the other hand,

\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(((\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{-1}\nabla^{2}\Phi_{e})^{2}\bigl)=\mathrm{Tr}(A^{2})\geq 0\text{ with }A=(\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{-1/2}\nabla^{2}\Phi_{e}(\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{-1/2}.

By
Lemma

A.4
,

\alpha\geq\Delta V(x_{0})\geq\mathrm{Tr}(G^{-1}(\nabla^{2}\Phi(x_{0}))^{2})\geq\mathrm{Tr}(G)^{-1}(\Delta\Phi(x_{0}))^{2}

and therefore,

\Delta\Phi(x_{0})\leq\sqrt{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{Tr}(G)}

using that
\Delta\Phi(x_{0})\geq 0
as
\Phi
is convex. Since
x_{0}
is a point where
\Delta\Phi
attains its maximum, we obtain the desired claim.

Let us now prove the case with
A>0
. We consider
\widetilde{V}=V(A^{1/2}\cdot)-\frac{1}{2}\log(\det A)
and
\widetilde{\mu}=e^{-\widetilde{V}}\,dx.
Notice that for any Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(A^{-1/2})_{\#}\mu(B)=\int_{A^{1/2}B}e^{-V(x)}\,dx=\int_{B}e^{-V(A^{1/2}y)}\det(A)^{1/2}\,dy

i.e.

(A^{-1/2})_{\#}\mu=\widetilde{\mu}.
Consider
\widetilde{\nu}=(A^{1/2})_{\#}\nu=e^{-\widetilde{W}}\,dx
with
\widetilde{W}=W(A^{-1/2}\cdot)+\frac{1}{2}\log(\det A).
Set
\widetilde{\Phi}(x)=\Phi(A^{1/2}x)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\nabla\Phi
is the Brenier map pushing
\mu
forward to
\nu.
Then,
\widetilde{\Phi}
is also convex and
\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}(x)=A^{1/2}\nabla\Phi(A^{1/2}x)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
This implies that

\begin{array}[]{lll}(\nabla\widetilde{\Phi})_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}&=&(A^{1/2}\circ\nabla\Phi\circ A^{1/2})_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}=(A^{1/2}\circ\nabla\Phi)_{\#}(A^{1/2})_{\#}((A^{-1/2})_{\#}\mu)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&(A^{1/2})_{\#}((\nabla\Phi)_{\#}\mu)=(A^{1/2})_{\#}\nu\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&=&\widetilde{\nu}.\end{array}

Hence, by the uniqueness part of Brenier’s theorem,
\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}
is the optimal transport map from
\widetilde{\mu}
to
\widetilde{\nu}.
Moreover,

\alpha\geq\mathrm{div}(A\nabla V)(A^{1/2}x)=\mathrm{Tr}(A^{1/2}D^{2}V(A^{1/2}x)A^{1/2})=\mathrm{Tr}(D^{2}\widetilde{V}(x))=\Delta\widetilde{V}(x)\text{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and similarly,

D^{2}\widetilde{W}(x)=A^{-1/2}D^{2}W(A^{-1/2}x)A^{-1/2}\geq A^{-1/2}G^{-1}A^{-1/2}=(A^{1/2}GA^{1/2})^{-1}\text{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Thus,

\|\Delta\widetilde{\Phi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{\alpha\mathrm{Tr}(A^{1/2}GA^{1/2})}=\sqrt{\alpha\mathrm{Tr}(AG)}.

The claim is proven if we notice that, as
\widetilde{\Phi}(x)=\Phi(A^{1/2}x)
,

\Delta\widetilde{\Phi}(x)=\mathrm{Tr}(D^{2}\widetilde{\Phi}(x))=\mathrm{Tr}(A^{1/2}D^{2}\Phi(A^{1/2}x)A^{1/2})=\mathrm{div}(A\nabla\Phi(A^{1/2}x))

which shows that

\|\mathrm{div}(A\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{\alpha\mathrm{Tr}(AG)}.

To treat the case where

d\mu_{\beta,N,Q}(z)=\frac{e^{-Q_{\beta,N}(z)-G_{\beta,N}(z)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta,N}}\text{ with }\mathcal{Z}_{\beta,N}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}}e^{-Q_{\beta,N}(z)-G_{\beta,N}(z)}\,dz

Theorem A.5
.

Suppose that there exists
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\ni G_{i},A_{i}>0,
and
\alpha_{i}>0
such that

\mathrm{div}(A_{i}\nabla Q_{i}(x))\leq\alpha_{i},\nabla^{2}Q_{i}(x)\geq G_{i}^{-1}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq N

and for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
\nabla^{2}Q_{i}
is the distributional derivative on
\mathbb{R}^{2}.
Let
\nabla\Phi_{\beta,N,Q}
be the optimal transport map between
\mu_{\beta,N,Q}
and
\nu_{\beta,N,Q}.
Then,

\|\mathrm{div}(A\nabla\Phi_{\beta,N,Q})\|_{L^{\infty}(dx)}\leq\sqrt{\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{Tr}(A_{i}G_{i})}

where

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}.

Proof.

Denote
d\mu_{\beta,N,Q}=e^{-V_{\beta,N,Q}}\,dz.
The assumption
\nabla^{2}Q_{i}\geq G_{i}^{-1}
implies that
\nabla^{2}Q_{\beta,N}\geq\beta N\mathrm{diag}(G_{1}^{-1},\cdots,G_{N}^{-1}).
Since
\mathrm{div}(A\nabla G_{\beta,N})\leq 0
, it follows that

\mathrm{div}(A\nabla V_{\beta,N,Q})=\mathrm{div}(A\nabla Q_{\beta,N})+\mathrm{div}(A\nabla G_{\beta,N})\leq\beta N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{div}(A_{i}\nabla Q_{i})\leq\beta N\alpha

where
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}.
Hence, we can apply
Theorem

A.3
with

n=2N,\mu=\mu_{\beta,N,Q},\nu=\nu_{\beta,N,Q},A=\mathrm{diag}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{N}),G=(\beta N)^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(G_{1},\cdots,G_{N}).

This shows that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\|\mathrm{div}(A\nabla\Phi_{\beta,N,Q})\|_{L^{\infty}(dx)}&\leq&\displaystyle\sqrt{\beta N\alpha\,\mathrm{Tr}(AG)}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\leq&\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{Tr}(A_{i}G_{i})}.\end{array}

∎
