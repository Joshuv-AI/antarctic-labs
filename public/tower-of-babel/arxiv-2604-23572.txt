Mean Waiting Times in Discrete-Time Priority Queues
with Geometrically Distributed Idle Periods

Abstract




Abstract

This paper considers the mean waiting times in discrete-time
preemptive-resume and nonpreemptive priority single-server queues fed
by
K
independent batch Markovian arrival streams with geometrically
distributed idle periods. While being active, the
k
th
(
k=1,2,\ldots,K
) arrival stream feeds at least one customer to the
queue, where the number of arriving customers depends on the state of
the underlying Markov chain. Service times of class
k
customers are
independent and identically distributed according to a general
distribution. For these queues, we derive explicit formulae for the
mean waiting times of customers in each class.

Keywords

discrete-time queue, batch Markovian arrival streams, conservation
law, preemptive-resume priority, nonpreemptive priority, mean waiting
time

Mathematical Subject Classification

60K25, 60K37

1
Introduction

Priority queues have been studied extensively in the literature

[
3
,
6
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. See
[
19
]
and
references therein also. When arrivals are memoryless (i.e., the
arrival process has independent and stationary increment properties),
explicit formulae for the mean waiting time can be obtained

[
9
,
11
,
12
]
. Here we use the word “explicit” to
imply that there is no need to solve a system of equations or a
fixed-point equation to evaluate the formula, which arise typically in
queueing analysis. When arrivals are correlated in time, however, we
cannot obtain such an explicit formula in general. See

[
4
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
20
]
for example.

This paper considers discrete-time priority single-server queues with
a superposition of
K
independent batch Markovian arrival streams of
customers. Customers from the
k
th arrival stream are referred to as
class
k
customers. Time is divided into slots of equal length. Each
arrival stream becomes idle and active alternately, and idle and
active periods form a discrete-time alternating renewal process. The
lengths of idle periods are geometrically distributed and no customers
arrive during idle periods. On the other hand, the lengths of active
periods follow a discrete phase-type distribution, which is equivalent
to the distribution of first passage times to the absorbing state in
an underlying absorbing Markov chain, and at least one customer
arrives at each slot in active periods. Note that
the number of customers arriving at each slot in active periods
can depend on the state of the underlying absorbing Markov
chain. Service times of customers in each class are independent and
identically distributed (i.i.d.) according to a general distribution.

The main purpose of this paper is the derivation of explicit formulae
for the mean waiting times in discrete-time preemptive-resume and
nonpreemptive priority single-server queues with the above-mentioned
arrival streams. The advantage of discrete-time queues over the
continuous-time counterparts is that even when arrivals are correlated
in time, we may be able to derive explicit formulae for performance
measures of interest. For example, Takine derived the mean
amount of unfinished work
in the discrete-time work-conserving single-server queue with
the above-mentioned arrival streams
[
17
]
. See
[
8
]

and
[
18
]
also. To the best of our knowledge, however, there
are no reports on an explicit formula for the mean waiting time in

K
-class priority queues with arrivals correlated in time.

The essential assumption in the model is that (i) arrival streams are
independent of each other, (ii) idle periods in each stream follow a
geometric distribution, and (iii) each stream feeds at least one
customer at each slot in active periods. These assumptions yield an
i.i.d. sequence of busy cycle during which customers are served
successively. We first derive the conservation law for mean waiting
times in work-conserving, nonpreemptive-service single-server queues,
using the general formula for the mean amount of unfinished work in

[
17
]
. Note that the mean waiting times in the nonpreemptive
priority queue satisfy this conservation law. Next, we consider the
preemptive-resume priority queue and derive an explicit formula for
the mean waiting time. We then consider the relation between mean
waiting times in nonpreemptive and preemptive-resume priority queues
and derive the mean waiting time in the nonpreemptive priority queue
in terms of that in the preemptive-resume priority queue.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section

2
describes the mathematical model. In
Section
3
, we first establish the conservation law in
work-conserving single-server queues with nonpreemptive services. We
then discuss the first two moments of busy cycle lengths. In
Section
4
, we consider the preemptive-resume
priority queue and in Section
5
, we consider the
nonpreemptive priority queue. Finally, we provide the results for
some important special cases in Section
6
.

Throughout this paper, we use the following conventions.
For any function
f(z)
of
z
, let
f^{(n)}
denote the
n
th
derivative of
f(z)
evaluated at
z=1-
, i.e.,

f^{(n)}=\lim_{z\to 1-}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}f(z).

This convention applies to vector and matrix functions, too. Furthermore,
for any nonnegative integer random variable

\tilde{F}

denote an equilibrium random variable.

\Pr(\tilde{F}=n)=\frac{\Pr(F>n)}{\mathrm{E}[F]},\quad n=0,1,\ldots,

\mathrm{E}[\tilde{F}]=\frac{\mathrm{E}[F(F-1)]}{2\mathrm{E}[F]}.

Note that the forward recurrence time of

2
Model

We consider discrete-time work-conserving single-server queues with

K
independent batch Markovian arrival streams of customers. Let
\mathcal{K}=\{1,2,\ldots,K\}
. Customers arriving from the
k
th (
k\in\mathcal{K}
)
arrival stream are referred to as class
k
customers. Let
A_{k,n}

(
k\in\mathcal{K}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
) denote the number of class
k
customers
arriving at the
n
th slot. The sequence
\{A_{k,n}\}_{n=1,2,\ldots}

is characterized as follows. For each
k
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
), we assume
that there exists an irreducible underlying Markov chain with finite
state space
\mathcal{M}_{k}=\{0,1,\ldots,M_{k}\}
, which governs class
k

arrivals. To avoid triviality, we assume
M_{k}\geq 1
. Let
S_{k,n}

(
k\in\mathcal{K}
,
n=0,1,\ldots
) denote the state of the underlying Markov
chain for class
k
arrivals at the
n
th slot.

Assumption 1
.

A_{k,n}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
) depends only on
S_{k,n-1}
and

S_{k,n}
.

Assumption 2
.

The following relationships hold for all
k
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) and for all

n
(
n=1,2,\ldots
).

S_{k,n}=0\,\Leftrightarrow\,A_{k,n}=0,\qquad S_{k,n}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}\,\Leftrightarrow\,A_{k,n}\geq 1,

where
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}=\{1,2,\ldots M_{k}\}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
).

Accordingly, we assume that the irreducible transition probability
matrix
\bm{P}_{k}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) of the underlying Markov chain for
class
k
is given by

\bm{P}_{k}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p_{k}&\left(1-p_{k}\right)\bm{\alpha}_{k}\\
(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})\bm{e}_{k}&\bm{T}_{k}\end{array}\right),\quad k\in\mathcal{K},

(1)

where
\bm{\alpha}_{k}
and
\bm{T}_{k}
denote a
1\times M_{k}

probability vector and an
M_{k}\times M_{k}
substochastic matrix,

\bm{I}_{k}
denotes an
M_{k}\times M_{k}
unit matrix, and
\bm{e}_{k}
denotes an
M_{k}\times 1

vector whose elements are all equal to one. Because
\bm{P}_{k}
is
assumed to be irreducible,
0\leq p_{k}<1
,
(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})\bm{e}_{k}\neq\bf{0}
, and the stochastic matrix
\bm{T}_{k}+(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})\bm{e}_{k}\bm{\alpha}_{k}
is irreducible.

All arriving customers are accommodated in the buffer of infinite
capacity and they are served by a single server. Let
B_{k,n}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
) denote the amount of class
k
work arriving at
the
n
th slot. We then have

B_{k,n}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{A_{k,n}}H_{k,n,l},\qquad k\in\mathcal{K},\ n=1,2,\ldots,

where
H_{k,n,l}
denotes the service time of the
l
th customer in
class
k
who arrives at the
n
th slot. We assume that service times
of class
k
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) customers are i.i.d. according to a general
distribution. Let
H_{k}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) denote a generic random variable
for
H_{k,n,l}
. For simplicity, we assume that
H_{k}
is positive.
The probability generating function of
H_{k}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) is denoted
by
H_{k}(z)
:

H_{k}(z)=\mathrm{E}\left[z^{H_{k}}\right]=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Pr(H_{k}=m)z^{m},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}.

Let
X_{n}
(
n=0,1,\ldots
) denote the total amount of unfinished work
at the
n
th slot. We assume that
\{X_{n}\}_{n=0,1,\ldots}
satisfies

X_{n}=\max(X_{n-1}-1,0)+B_{n},\qquad n=1,2,\ldots,

(2)

where

B_{n}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}B_{k,n},\qquad n=1,2,\ldots.

Equation (
2
) implies that the service discipline is
work-conserving and that
B_{n}=0
if
X_{n}=0
. Because
B_{n}=0
is
equivalent to
A_{k,n}=0
for all
k
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
), we have

\Pr(S_{n}=0\mid X_{n}=0)=\prod_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\Pr(S_{k,n}=0\mid X_{n}=0)=1,

(3)

where the first equality follows from the independence of arrival
streams and Assumption
2
.

Within the above settings, we consider the preemptive-resume and
nonpreemptive priority disciplines, where priority is assigned in a
descending order of class indices. Specifically, class 1 customers
have the highest priority and class
K
customers the lowest. In
these priority disciplines, services of class
k
customers start only
when there are no customers in classes 1 to
k-1
. Once a
service starts in the nonpreemptive priority queue, it continues to
the completion. In the preemptive-resume priority queue, however,
services are interrupted when customers in higher priority classes
arrive, and they restart from the interrupted points again when all
customers in higher priority classes complete their services. The order
of services within each class is arbitrary, provided that it is
independent of service times of customers and once a service starts,
it completes before other services in the same class start.

We define
\bm{A}_{k}(z)
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) as an
(M_{k}+1)\times(M_{k}+1)

matrix whose (
i,j
)th (
i,j\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
) element
A_{k,i,j}(z)
is
given by

A_{k,i,j}(z)=P_{k,i,j}a_{k,i,j}(z),

where
P_{k,i,j}
denotes the (
i,j
)th element of
\bm{P}_{k}
and

a_{k,i,j}(z)=\mathrm{E}\left[z^{A_{k,n}}\mid S_{k,n-1}=i,S_{k,n}=j\right].

Let
\alpha_{k,j}
and
T_{k,i,j}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
,
i,j\in\mathcal{M}_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}
) denote the
j
th element of
\bm{\alpha}_{k}
and the
(
i,j
)th element of
\bm{T}_{k}
. It then follows that

\bm{A}_{k}(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p_{k}&(1-p_{k})\bm{\alpha}_{k}(z)\\
(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})\bm{e}_{k}&\bm{T}_{k}(z)\end{array}\right),\qquad k\in\mathcal{K},

(4)

where
\bm{\alpha}_{k}(z)
denotes a
1\times M_{k}
vector whose
j
th
(
j\in\mathcal{M}_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}
)
element
\alpha_{k,j}(z)
is given by

\alpha_{k,j}(z)=\alpha_{k,j}a_{k,0,j}(z),

and
\bm{T}_{k}(z)
denotes an
M_{k}\times M_{k}
matrix whose
(i,j)
th
(
i,j\in\mathcal{M}_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}
) element
T_{k,i,j}(z)
is given by

T_{k,i,j}(z)=T_{k,i,j}a_{k,i,j}(z).

Therefore
\{A_{k,n}\}_{n=1,2,\ldots}
is characterized fully by

p_{k}
,
\bm{\alpha}_{k}(z)
, and
\bm{T}_{k}(z)
. We also define

\bm{B}_{k}(z)
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) as

\bm{B}_{k}(z)=\bm{A}_{k}(H_{k}(z)).

Note that the (
i,j
)th (
i,j\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
) element
B_{k,i,j}(z)
of

\bm{B}_{k}(z)
is given by

B_{k,i,j}(z)=\mathrm{E}\left[z^{B_{k,n}}\mbox{$\mathbb{1}$}(S_{k,n}=j)\mid S_{k,n-1}=i\right],

where
\mbox{$\mathbb{1}$}(\chi)
denotes the indicator function of event
\chi
.

Let
\bm{\pi}_{k}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) denote the steady-state probability
vector of the underlying Markov chain for class
k
. Because the
underlying Markov chain for each class is assumed to be
irreducible,
\bm{\pi}_{k}
is determined uniquely by
\bm{\pi}_{k}=\bm{\pi}_{k}\bm{P}_{k}
and
\bm{\pi}_{k}\check{\bm{e}}_{k}=1
, i.e.,

\bm{\pi}_{k}=\left(\pi_{k,0},\ \bm{\pi}_{k,1}\right),\quad k\in\mathcal{K},

where
\check{\bm{e}}_{k}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) denotes an
(M_{k}+1)\times 1
vector
whose elements are all equal to one, and

\pi_{k,0}=\frac{(1-p_{k})^{-1}}{(1-p_{k})^{-1}+\bm{\alpha}_{k}(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})^{-1}\bm{e}_{k}},\qquad\bm{\pi}_{k,1}=\frac{\bm{\alpha}_{k}(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})^{-1}}{(1-p_{k})^{-1}+\bm{\alpha}_{k}(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})^{-1}\bm{e}_{k}},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}.

Let
\lambda_{k}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) denote the arrival rate of class
k

customers.

\lambda_{k}=\bm{\pi}_{k}\bm{A}_{k}^{(1)}\bm{e}_{k}=\frac{\bm{\alpha}_{k}^{(1)}\bm{e}_{k}+\bm{\alpha}_{k}(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})^{-1}\bm{T}_{k}^{(1)}\bm{e}_{k}}{(1-p_{k})^{-1}+\bm{\alpha}_{k}(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})^{-1}\bm{e}_{k}},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}.

We define
\lambda
as

\lambda=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\lambda_{k}.

Let
\rho_{k}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) denote the traffic intensity of class
k

customers. We then have

\rho_{k}=\bm{\pi}_{k}\bm{B}_{k}^{(1)}\check{\bm{e}}_{k}=\lambda_{k}\mathrm{E}[H_{k}],\quad k\in\mathcal{K}.

Assumption 3
.

\rho=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}<1,

(5)

and the system is in steady state.

3
Conservation law and busy cycle

The priority queues described in Section
2
can be
viewed as special cases of the model studied in
[
17
]
, where
the mean amount of unfinished work in the discrete-time
work-conserving single-server queue with multiple, independent arrival

establish the conservation law for nonpreemptive services.
Furthermore, we derive explicit expressions for the first two
factorial moments of busy cycle lengths.

Moments of busy cycle lengths

We consider the length of a randomly chosen busy cycle in the

Lemma 12

\rho_{0}^{+}=0

\mathcal{K}_{K+1}^{-}=\emptyset
.

Remark 19
.

It is interesting to observe that the difference between

\mathrm{E}\big[W_{k}^{\mathrm{PR}}\big]

\rho_{j}
(

6
Special cases

The queueing model in this paper is capable of representing various
arrival processes by setting
p_{k}
,
\bm{\alpha}_{k}(z)
,
\bm{T}_{k}(z)

(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) in (
4
) appropriately. In this section, we
provide the results in three important special cases: the queue with
constant service times of one slot, the queue with i.i.d. arrivals,
and the queue with i.i.d. arrivals during active periods.

6.1
Queues with constant service times of one slot

Suppose
H_{k}=1
(
k\in K
) with probability one. In this case,
any service discipline is nonpreemptive and

\mathrm{E}[H_{k}]=1,\qquad\mathrm{E}[\tilde{H}_{k}]=0,\quad k\in\mathcal{K}.

The conservation law is then given by

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}\mathrm{E}[W_{k}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(1-\rho)}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}(\rho-\rho_{k})+\frac{1}{1-\rho}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}\left(\mathrm{E}[\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}]-\pi_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}\frac{\mathrm{E}[C_{k}\Lambda_{k}]}{\mathrm{E}[C_{k}]}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\pi_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}\rho_{k}\left(1+\frac{\rho_{k}}{1-\rho}\right)\left(1+\mathrm{E}[\tilde{C}_{k}]\right)-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\pi_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}\mathrm{E}[\Lambda_{k}(\tilde{C}_{k})].

Furthermore, the mean waiting times
\mathrm{E}[W_{k}^{\mathrm{PR}}]
and
\mathrm{E}[W_{k}^{\mathrm{NP}}]

(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) in the preemptive-resume and nonpreemptive priority queues
are identical and given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left[W_{k}^{\mathrm{PR}}\right]=\mathrm{E}\left[W_{k}^{\mathrm{NP}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{K}_{k}^{+}}\rho_{\ell}\left[\pi_{\ell}^{({\mathrm{on}})}\rho_{\ell}\left(1+\mathrm{E}[\tilde{C}_{\ell}]\right)+\mathrm{E}[\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ell}]-\pi_{\ell}^{({\mathrm{on}})}\frac{\mathrm{E}[C_{\ell}\Lambda_{\ell}]}{\mathrm{E}[C_{\ell}]}\right]}{(1-\rho_{k}^{+})(1-\rho_{k-1}^{+})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{K}_{k}^{+}}\rho_{\ell}(\rho_{k}^{+}-\rho_{\ell})}{2(1-\rho_{k}^{+})(1-\rho_{k-1}^{+})}+\frac{\rho_{k-1}^{+}+\mathrm{E}[\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}]-\pi_{k}^{({\mathrm{on}})}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{E}[C_{k}\Lambda_{k}]}{\mathrm{E}[C_{k}]}}{1-\rho_{k}^{+}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{}+\pi_{k}^{({\mathrm{on}})}\left[\left(1+\frac{\rho_{k}}{1-\rho_{k-1}^{+}}\right)\left(1+\mathrm{E}[\tilde{C}_{k}]\right)-\frac{\mathrm{E}[\Lambda_{k}(\tilde{C}_{k})]}{\lambda_{k}}\right].

6.2
Queues with i.i.d. arrivals

Suppose
\{A_{k,n}\}_{n=1,2,\ldots}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) is a sequence of
i.i.d. random variables with probability mass function
a_{k}(m)=\Pr(A_{k,n}=m)
(
m=0,1,\ldots
), i.e.,

\bm{A}_{k}(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{k}(0)&E[z^{A_{k}}]-a_{k}(0)\\
a_{k}(0)&E[z^{A_{k}}]-a_{k}(0)\end{array}\right),\qquad k\in\mathcal{K},

where
A_{k}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) denotes a generic random variable for

A_{k,n}
. Note that
\lambda_{k}=E[A_{k}]
,
\rho_{k}=\lambda_{k}E[H_{k}]
, and
\pi_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}=1-a_{k}(0)
. Furthermore, it is easy to
see that

\displaystyle E[\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}]=E[\tilde{A}_{k}]+\frac{\lambda_{k}}{a_{k}(0)},

\displaystyle\qquad E[\Lambda_{k}(\tilde{C}_{k})]=\frac{\lambda_{k}}{a_{k}(0)},

\displaystyle E[\tilde{C}_{k}]=\frac{1-a_{k}(0)}{a_{k}(0)},

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{E[C_{k}\Lambda_{k}]}{E[C_{k}]}=\frac{\lambda_{k}(2-a_{k}(0))}{a_{k}(0)(1-a_{k}(0))}.

The conservation law for nonpreemptive services is then given by

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}\mathrm{E}[W_{k}]=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}\mathrm{E}[\tilde{H}_{k}]+\frac{1}{2(1-\rho)}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}(\rho-\rho_{k})+\frac{1}{1-\rho}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}\mathrm{E}[H_{k}]\mathrm{E}[\tilde{A}_{k}],

and the mean waiting time
E[W_{k}^{\mathrm{PR}}]

(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) in the preemptive-resume priority queue is given by

\mathrm{E}\left[W_{k}^{\mathrm{PR}}\right]=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{K}_{k}^{+}}\rho_{\ell}\mathrm{E}[\tilde{H}_{\ell}]}{(1-\rho_{k}^{+})(1-\rho_{k-1}^{+})}+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{K}_{k}^{+}}\rho_{\ell}\left(\rho_{k}^{+}-\rho_{\ell}+2\mathrm{E}[H_{\ell}]\mathrm{E}[\tilde{A}_{\ell}]\right)}{2(1-\rho_{k}^{+})(1-\rho_{k-1}^{+})}+\frac{\rho_{k-1}^{+}+\mathrm{E}[H_{k}]\mathrm{E}[\tilde{A}_{k}]}{1-\rho_{k-1}^{+}}.

For
\mathrm{E}[W_{k}^{\mathrm{NP}}]
, see Theorem
18
.
These results agree with the well-known results
(cf. Section 6.6 in
[
12
]
).

6.3
Queues with i.i.d. arrivals during active periods

Suppose the number of class
k
customers arriving at each slot during
active periods is independent of the state of the underlying Markov
chain. Let
A_{k}^{+}
(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) denote the conditional random
variable of
A_{k}
given
A_{k}\geq 1
. We then have

\bm{A}_{k}(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p_{k}&(1-p_{k})a_{k}^{+}(z)\bm{\alpha}_{k}\\
(\bm{I}_{k}-\bm{T}_{k})\bm{e}_{k}&a_{k}^{+}(z)\bm{T}_{k}\end{array}\right),\qquad k\in\mathcal{K},

where
a_{k}^{+}(z)=E[z^{A_{k}^{+}}]=E[z^{A_{k}}\mid A_{k}\geq 1]
. Note that

\displaystyle\pi_{k}^{[\mathrm{on}]}=\frac{\lambda_{k}}{E[A_{k}^{+}]},

\displaystyle\qquad E[\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}]=E[\tilde{A}_{k}^{+}]+E[A_{k}^{+}]E[\tilde{C}_{k}],

\displaystyle E[\Lambda_{k}(\tilde{C}_{k})]=E[A_{k}^{+}]E[\tilde{C}_{k}],

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{E[C_{k}\Lambda_{k}]}{E[C_{k}]}=2E[A_{k}^{+}]E[\tilde{C}_{k}]+E[A_{k}^{+}].

The conservation law for nonpreemptive services is then given by

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}\mathrm{E}[W_{k}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}\mathrm{E}[\tilde{H}_{k}]}{1-\rho}+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}\left[2\mathrm{E}[H_{k}]\left(\mathrm{E}[\tilde{A}_{k}^{+}]-\mathrm{E}[A_{k}^{+}]\right)+2-\rho+\rho_{k}\right]}{2(1-\rho)}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\rho_{k}\left(\mathrm{E}[H_{k}]\mathrm{E}[A_{k}^{+}]-1+\rho-\rho_{k}\right)\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\mathrm{E}[A_{k}^{+}]}\right)\left(1+\mathrm{E}[\tilde{C}_{k}]\right)}{1-\rho},

and the mean waiting time
E[W_{k}^{\mathrm{PR}}]

(
k\in\mathcal{K}
) in the preemptive-resume priority queue is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left[W_{k}^{\mathrm{PR}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{K}_{k}^{+}}\rho_{\ell}\mathrm{E}[\tilde{H}_{\ell}]}{(1-\rho_{k}^{+})(1-\rho_{k-1}^{+})}+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{K}_{k}^{+}}\rho_{\ell}\left[2\mathrm{E}[H_{\ell}]\left(\mathrm{E}[\tilde{A}_{\ell}^{+}]-\mathrm{E}[A_{\ell}^{+}]\right)+\rho_{k}^{+}+\rho_{\ell}\right]}{2(1-\rho_{k}^{+})(1-\rho_{k-1}^{+})}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{K}_{k}^{+}}\rho_{\ell}\left(\mathrm{E}[H_{\ell}]\mathrm{E}[A_{\ell}^{+}]-\rho_{\ell}\right)\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{\ell}}{\mathrm{E}[A_{\ell}^{+}]}\right)\left(1+\mathrm{E}[\tilde{C}_{\ell}]\right)}{(1-\rho_{k}^{+})(1-\rho_{k-1}^{+})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\frac{\mathrm{E}[H_{k}]\left(\mathrm{E}[\tilde{A}_{k}^{+}]-\mathrm{E}[A_{k}^{+}]\right)+1+\rho_{k}}{1-\rho_{k-1}^{+}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\frac{\left(\mathrm{E}[H_{k}]\mathrm{E}[A_{k}^{+}]-1+\rho_{k-1}^{+}-\rho_{k}\right)\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\mathrm{E}[A_{k}^{+}]}\right)\left(1+\mathrm{E}[\tilde{C}_{k}]\right)}{1-\rho_{k-1}^{+}}.

For
\mathrm{E}[W_{k}^{\mathrm{NP}}]
, see Theorem
18
.
