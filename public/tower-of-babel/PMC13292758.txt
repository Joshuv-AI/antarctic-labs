Integrated three-dimensional genomic analysis reveals that dynamic changes in chromatin structure are associated with differences in egg production performance between White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens☆
Authors: Shenao Wang, Yuedi Cao, Geng G. Tian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Egg-laying performance varies significantly among different chicken breeds. The liver, as the central organ responsible for yolk precursor synthesis, largely determines the egg production performance of poultry. However, the underlying genetic basis of these phenotypic differences remains poorly understood. In this study, we systematically compared the transcriptomic differences and dynamic changes in three-dimensional (3D) genome organization in the livers of a high-producing commercial breed (White Leghorn) and a low-producing indigenous breed (Wenchang chicken) during the laying period. Transcriptomic analysis revealed that significantly upregulated genes in White Leghorns were highly enriched in lipid metabolism-related pathways, including CPT1A, ACSL1, ACACB, ACOX1, EHHADH, DHCR7, and SQLE, whereas highly expressed genes in Wenchang chickens were mainly enriched in pathways such as the ribosome. 3D genomic analysis demonstrated that although the global chromatin framework of the liver was relatively conserved between the two breeds, significant breed-specific remodeling occurred in local spatial organization. At the compartment level, key metabolic genes such as BBOX1 and GRB14 underwent B-to-A compartment switching and transcriptional activation in White Leghorns, which may be associated with their high laying trait. At the topologically associating domain (TAD) level, the livers of White Leghorns exhibited relatively weaker boundary insulation and a looser intra-domain folding state. Furthermore, the functional enrichment results of breed-specific TAD boundary genes were relatively consistent with the transcriptomic profiles. At the chromatin loop level, White Leghorns displayed stronger intra-loop spatial interactions. Critically, White Leghorn-specific chromatin loops precisely anchored and upregulated some key genes involved in lipid metabolism, such as ACSL1, ACOX1, and EHHADH, whereas Wenchang chicken-specific loops primarily drove the expression of ribosome-related genes. In summary, this study reveals a close correlation between the dynamic changes in the 3D chromatin structure and the transcription differences in the livers of the two chicken breeds, and these differences may be linked to egg production performance. These findings provide new insights into the genetic basis underlying the liver's role in regulating poultry laying performance.

Introduction

As one of the most important livestock species worldwide, chickens provide egg products that play an irreplaceable role in meeting human demands for high-quality animal protein and in promoting agricultural economic development (Liu et al., 2018; Huang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026). With the continuous growth of the global population and the ongoing upgrading of dietary patterns, improving egg production efficiency has become a core objective of modern poultry breeding programs (Zhang et al., 2026). However, egg laying is a complex physiological process, and its underlying genetic mechanisms remain unclear (Wang et al., 2024a). This restricts the improvement of breeding efficiency. Therefore, an in-depth elucidation of the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying egg production holds significant theoretical and practical importance.

The liver is the central organ responsible for the synthesis of yolk precursor substances during egg laying in chickens. During the laying period, the liver synthesizes large quantities of yolk precursors, including vitellogenin (VTG) and very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL), which are subsequently transported via the bloodstream to developing oocytes, thereby driving rapid yolk accumulation (Li et al., 2015; Huang et al., 2022, 2025b). Studies have demonstrated that a decline in the liver capacity for yolk precursor synthesis leads to reduced egg production rate (Huang et al., 2022). In recent years, multiple transcriptomic and metabolomic studies have further revealed that genes related to lipid synthesis, transport, and metabolism in the liver play important roles in regulating egg-laying performance (Li et al., 2015; Huang et al., 2022, 2025b). However, most current research focuses on changes in expression profiles. The deeper regulatory mechanisms, such as how higher-order chromatin structure regulates gene expression, remain to be systematically elucidated.

The development of high-throughput chromosome conformation capture (Hi-C) has greatly advanced our understanding of the spatial organization of chromatin within the cell nucleus and its functional implications (Lieberman-Aiden et al., 2009). Studies have demonstrated that three-dimensional (3D) genomic features, including A/B compartments, topologically associating domains (TADs), and chromatin loops, are closely associated with the regulation of gene expression (Dixon et al., 2012; Nora et al., 2012; Rao et al., 2014). In recent years, 3D genome technologies have been progressively applied to poultry research. For instance, the first pan-3D genome atlas of the chicken was recently constructed, providing a novel perspective for functional genomics and molecular breeding research in chickens (Zhou et al., 2026). One study reconstructed the 3D chromatin regulatory networks of abdominal fat deposition in fat and lean broiler lines, revealing that the rs734209466 variant functions as an allele-specific enhancer that remotely regulates the transcription of IGFBP2 and IGFBP5 by recruiting the transcription factor IRF4, thereby influencing the abdominal fat deposition phenotype in chickens (Shen et al., 2024). A study focusing on fast-growing commercial broilers and indigenous chicken breeds found that dynamic changes in chromatin compartments and TADs may regulate the expression of genes associated with breed-specific breast muscle traits, consequently contributing to phenotypic differences between the two breeds (Wang et al., 2024b). However, systematic comparative studies on the 3D genome architecture of the liver in chicken breeds with different laying performances are currently lacking.

The White Leghorn is one of the most commercialized layer breeds in the world, known for its high egg production. In contrast, the Wenchang chicken is a local breed famous for its meat quality and flavor, but its laying performance is relatively lower (Gu et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025). Based on this background, this study combined liver Hi-C and RNA-seq datasets from White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens during the laying period (Foissac et al., 2019; Shao et al., 2022). By comparing 3D genomic features such as A/B compartments, TADs, and chromatin loops, we described the differences in the 3D chromatin structure of the liver between the two breeds. At the same time, we combined these findings with transcriptome data to systematically explore how liver functions might affect the egg-laying phenotypes of both breeds. The results showed a close relationship between the dynamic changes in 3D chromatin structure and the transcription differences in the livers of the two breeds, and these differences may be related to laying performance. These findings provide new molecular targets and a theoretical basis for epigenetic breeding strategies in poultry based on 3D genomic information.

Materials and methods

Data collection

Through literature and database searches, we collected Hi-C data (PRJEB27364 and PRJNA817871) and RNA-seq data (PRJEB27455 and PRJNA800119) for White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens (Foissac et al., 2019; Shao et al., 2022). The datasets for both breeds were collected from liver tissues, and the selected samples were all hens in the laying period. Wenchang chickens were sampled at 43 weeks of age and White Leghorn chickens were sampled at 55 weeks of age.

RNA-seq data pre-processing

The raw sequencing data were subjected to quality control and filtering using FastQC (v0.12.1) and fastp (v0.24.1) (Andrews, 2010; Chen, 2025). High-quality clean reads were aligned to the reference genome (GRCg7b) using HISAT2 (v2.2.1), followed by format conversion and sorting with samtools (v1.21) (Kim et al., 2019; Danecek et al., 2021). Gene-level counting was performed using featureCounts (v2.1.1) (Liao et al., 2014). Finally, to eliminate the influence of gene length and sequencing depth, the expression matrix was obtained by calculating Fragments Per Kilobase of exon model per Million mapped fragments (FPKM) values.

Differentially expressed genes analysis

Differential gene expression analysis was performed on the liver RNA-seq data of White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens using the limma (v3.58.1) package (Ritchie et al., 2015). In this study, taking the Wenchang chicken as the baseline, we identified genes that were significantly upregulated or downregulated in the livers of White Leghorn chickens compared to Wenchang chickens. The criteria for screening significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were set as: adjusted P-value < 0.05 and |log₂FC| > 1.

Functional enrichment analysis

In this study, functional enrichment analysis of the relevant genes was performed using the online biological analysis tool KOBAS 3.0 (Bu et al., 2021). Through this analysis, relevant Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways and Gene Ontology (GO) terms were identified. The significance threshold was set at P-value < 0.05.

Hi-C data processing

The Hi-C sequencing data from the liver tissues of White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens were input into the HiC-Pro (v3.1.0) analysis pipeline, with the chicken reference genome (GRCg7b) used as a template for alignment (Servant et al., 2015). During this process, invalid data, including singleton, multiple-mapped, and unmapped reads, as well as dangling end pairs, religation pairs, and self-cycle pairs, were automatically filtered to construct high-quality valid chromatin interaction matrices. To eliminate systematic biases and enable unbiased comparisons of 3D genomic features across different datasets, the generated Hi-C matrices were normalized using the iterative correction and eigenvector decomposition (ICE) algorithm via the cooler balance module (Imakaev et al., 2012; Abdennur and Mirny, 2020). To evaluate the correlation of the 3D chromatin architecture between the livers of the two chicken breeds, we used the hicCorrelate tool from HiCExplorer (v3.7.6) to calculate the Pearson correlation coefficient between them at a 100 kb resolution (Wolff et al., 2020).

Simulation of chromatin three-dimensional structure

The 3D chromatin architecture of liver tissues from White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens was reconstructed using the multidimensional scaling (MDS) algorithm implemented in PASTIS (v0.4.0), based on Hi-C contact matrices binned at 100 kb resolution (Varoquaux et al., 2014). The resulting structural models were subsequently rendered and visualized using the Mayavi (v4.7.3) software package (Ramachandran and Varoquaux, 2011).

Contact frequency analysis as a function of genomic distance

Based on the 100 kb resolution Hi-C matrices, the expected_cis function of the cooltools (v0.7.0) software was utilized to calculate the P(s) curves, representing the change in contact frequency with genomic distance in the liver Hi-C data of White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens (Open2C et al., 2024). This method exploits the characteristic that each diagonal in a Hi-C map records contact information between loci at the same genomic distance; thus, the average contact frequency P(s) at a given genomic distance s is defined as the mean value of all pixels on the corresponding diagonal.

Chromatin compartment status analysis

To investigate the compartmentalization characteristics of chromatin, this study performed A/B compartment identification on 100 kb resolution Hi-C data using the cooltools (v0.7.0) analysis framework (Open2C et al., 2024). In accordance with the recommendation from a previous study, only the 25 longest chromosomes were selected for A/B compartment calling to ensure the robustness of the statistical analysis (Foissac et al., 2019). Specifically, eigenvalue decomposition was conducted on the cis-interaction matrices via the eigs_cis module, and the first eigenvector (E1) was extracted as the basis for classification: regions with positive E1 values were mapped to active A compartments, while regions with negative E1 values were mapped to inactive B compartments. To further characterize the spatial contact preferences between different compartments, we sequentially ranked the genomic bins based on E1 values and constructed saddle plots combined with the distance-corrected Observed/Expected (O/E) matrices. Finally, the contact frequencies from the four corners of the saddle plot matrices were extracted, and the compartment strength was quantitatively characterized by the enrichment ratio of intra-compartment contacts (A-A and B-B) to inter-compartment contacts (A-B and B-A), calculated using the formula (AA+BB)/(AB+BA). Venn diagrams were generated using the online biological tool EVenn (Yang et al., 2024).

Identification of topologically associating domains

In this study, TAD identification and characterization were conducted based on Hi-C contact maps at a 20 kb resolution. Consistent with the chromosome selection criteria used for compartment analysis, only the 25 longest chromosomes were included. The specific analysis relied on the insulation score method provided by the cooltools (v0.7.0) framework (Open2C et al., 2024). During the calculation, a sliding window of 200 kb was set to scan along the main diagonal of the Hi-C matrices to evaluate the local spatial contact intensity between adjacent genomic regions. Regions with lower insulation scores reflect a decrease in contact frequency between upstream and downstream genomic loci, corresponding to potential TAD boundary positions. Subsequently, to eliminate false-positive signals resulting from data sparsity or experimental background, the preliminary results were threshold-filtered based on boundary strength to establish a high-reliability set of TAD boundaries for further exploration. To assess the robustness of TAD boundary identification, valid read pairs were randomly downsampled to 60%, 70%, 80%, and 90%, followed by repeated boundary calling. For cross-resolution validation, TAD boundaries were also identified at 40 kb and 50 kb resolutions using the same insulation score framework, and their overlap with the 20 kb boundaries was evaluated. Finally, the genomic coordinates of TAD boundaries from the two breeds were clustered using a spatial tolerance threshold of 20 kb. Boundaries present in both breeds were classified as shared boundaries, whereas those found in only one breed were defined as breed-specific boundaries.

Identification of chromatin loops

In this study, FitHiC2 software (v2.0.8) was utilized to identify chromatin loops from the 20 kb resolution Hi-C data (Kaul et al., 2020). To prevent interference from very-short-range random contacts, the lower genomic distance bound was set to 40 kb, as officially recommended. Significant chromatin interaction locus pairs were retained using a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.01 as the significance threshold. Subsequently, spatial merging was performed on significant interaction pairs located in proximal genomic intervals, finally establishing a list of high-confidence chromatin loops for subsequent biological interpretation. For chromatin loops, pairwise comparisons between breeds were performed using a 20 kb positional tolerance for both loop anchors. A loop was classified as a shared loop only if both anchors met the matching criteria simultaneously; otherwise, it was identified as a breed-specific loop. To further explore the regulatory mechanisms underlying the breed-specific chromatin loops, motif enrichment analysis of their anchor regions was performed using Analysis of Motif Enrichment (AME) (v5.5.7) against the JASPAR2022 CORE vertebrates non-redundant motif database (McLeay and Bailey, 2010; Bailey et al., 2015; Castro-Mondragon et al., 2022). Motifs with adjusted P-value < 0.01 were considered significant. Sequence logos of significantly enriched motifs were visualized using WebLogo (Crooks et al., 2004).

Aggregate pile-up analysis of local three-dimensional chromatin structures

In this study, the coolpup.py (v1.1.0) tool was employed to perform pile-up analysis on the identified core 3D genomic elements (TADs and their boundaries, and chromatin loops) to reveal their common spatial characteristics (Flyamer et al., 2020). For TADs of varying lengths, a rescaling method was applied, utilizing the pile-up of O/E matrices to visualize the contact enrichment within the domains. When evaluating TAD boundaries, the matrices were centered on the boundary coordinates and extended 800 kb on both sides for pile-up, demonstrating the insulation effect of boundaries on chromatin interactions. Furthermore, for chromatin loops, the identified loop anchor coordinates were used as input to present the local enrichment signals of chromatin loops through aggregate analysis.

Results

Quality control and overview of RNA-seq and Hi-C sequencing data

To ensure the reliability of downstream comparative analyses, we first evaluated the quality of all RNA-seq and Hi-C datasets. For the RNA-seq data, quality control and mapping statistics are summarized in Supplementary Table S1. After adapter trimming and quality filtering, mapping rates to the GRCg7b reference genome ranged from 95.22% to 95.23% for White Leghorn samples and from 87.84% to 89.68% for Wenchang chicken samples, indicating high-quality alignments across all samples.

For the Hi-C data, key sequencing and mapping statistics are summarized in Supplementary Table S2. After filtering invalid read pairs and removing PCR duplicates, the numbers of high-confidence non-redundant valid interaction pairs (valid_rmdup) were 32,510,162 and 51,240,755 for the two White Leghorn samples, and 207,223,570 for the Wenchang chicken sample. The proportions of cis interactions among valid_rmdup pairs were 73.76%, 76.13%, and 81.38% for the two White Leghorn samples and the Wenchang chicken sample, respectively, indicating that all datasets are of good quality for 3D genomic analysis.

Using thresholds of adjusted P-value < 0.05 and | log₂FC | > 1, we identified a total of 2,444 DEGs. Among these, 963 genes were significantly upregulated and 1,481 genes were significantly downregulated in White Leghorns (Fig. 1A). We also performed GO enrichment analysis on the significantly upregulated and downregulated gene sets. Among the significantly upregulated genes, we found enriched terms related to lipid metabolism, including sterol biosynthetic process, fatty acid beta-oxidation, lipid homeostasis, and lipid droplet (Fig. 1E). We also found enriched terms related to chromatin organization and epigenetic regulation, such as chromosome organization, regulation of gene expression, epigenetic, and histone acetyltransferase activity (Fig. 1E). Among the significantly downregulated genes, we identified enriched terms related to the ribosome, including structural constituent of ribosome, cytosolic small ribosomal subunit, polysomal ribosome, and ribosomal large subunit biogenesis (Fig. 1F). Additionally, we found terms associated with chromatin organization, such as chromatin binding, chromatin-mediated maintenance of transcription, histone deacetylase binding, histone deacetylase complex, and condensed nuclear chromosome (Fig. 1F).

In summary, these results show widespread transcriptional differences in the livers between White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens. Genes related to lipid metabolism were significantly upregulated in White Leghorn livers, while ribosome-related genes were relatively downregulated. At the same time, the significant enrichment of terms related to chromatin structure and epigenetics suggests that there may be differences in the liver chromatin structure between the two breeds.

Comparative analysis of three-dimensional chromatin conformations in the livers of White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens

To evaluate the differences in the 3D chromatin conformation of the liver between the two chicken breeds, we performed a comparative analysis of the global contact patterns. We first constructed models of the 3D chromatin structure based on the Hi-C data. It can be observed that different chromosomes do not undergo disordered random entangling in space; instead, they each occupy relatively independent spatial territories (Fig. 2A and B). We calculated the Pearson correlation coefficients for the Hi-C contact matrices of both breeds at a 100 kb resolution. The results showed a genome-wide contact correlation coefficient of 0.86 between White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens (Fig. 2C and D), indicating that the overall 3D organizational framework of liver chromatin is relatively conserved, while also exhibiting breed-specific differences. This provides a foundation for the subsequent in-depth analysis of local conformational changes. The relationship between contact frequency and genomic distance (P(s) curve) showed that Wenchang chickens had a higher contact probability than White Leghorns in the short-range interval (below 10⁶ bp) (Fig. 2E). This suggests that the local chromatin folding in White Leghorns exists in a relatively loose physical state. However, White Leghorns exhibited higher contact probabilities in the medium-to-long-range interval (approximately 2 × 10⁶ to 107 bp), while Wenchang chickens were higher in the long-range interval (approximately 2 × 107 to 9 × 107 bp) (Fig. 2E). In the ultra-long-range interval exceeding 10⁸ bp, the contact probability of White Leghorns was again higher (Fig. 2E). Taking Chromosomes 1 and 2 as representatives, the differential contact maps showed results similar to those of the P(s) curves (Fig. 2F and G). Despite these similarities, a bin-by-bin analysis of compartment switching events revealed local differences. Defining the direction of compartment switching from Wenchang to White Leghorn, we detected that 3.3% of the genomic regions underwent a B-to-A transition, while 1.9% underwent an A-to-B transition (Fig. 3C). We further integrated the genes located in these switching regions with the DEGs. The results showed that among the genes located in the repressive B compartment in Wenchang chickens but transitioning to the active A compartment in White Leghorns, 34 genes were significantly upregulated in the White Leghorn liver (Fig. 3D). Conversely, 21 genes transitioning from the A to the B compartment were significantly downregulated (Fig. 3E), such as peptidyl arginine deiminase, type II (PADI2) and peptidyl arginine deiminase 3 (PADI3). Among the significantly upregulated genes, gamma-butyrobetaine hydroxylase 1 (BBOX1) and growth factor receptor bound protein 14 (GRB14) each showed high consistency between their compartment status and expression changes (Fig. 3F and G). Their upregulation may also be associated with high egg-laying traits.

Comparative analysis of topologically associating domains in the livers of White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens

We identified TADs based on the Hi-C data and performed a quantitative comparative analysis across five levels: reproducibility validation, regional visualization, insulation score, intra-TAD contact enrichment, and the insulation effect of TAD boundaries. First, downsampling analyses and cross-resolution validations demonstrated that the TAD boundaries identified at the 20 kb resolution possess excellent reproducibility and structural robustness (Fig. S3 and S4). Taking the 9.5–20.5 Mb region of Chromosome 1 as an example, the Hi-C heatmaps of both breeds showed clear TAD structures; however, the contact frequency within TADs was higher in Wenchang chickens, which also exhibited larger fluctuations in insulation scores (Fig. 4A). To quantify this global difference, we extracted the insulation scores at all TAD boundaries and performed an aggregate analysis. The results showed that Wenchang chickens had a deeper "valley" in insulation scores at TAD boundaries, indicating that Wenchang chickens maintain stronger TAD boundaries (Fig. 4B). To further assess the compactness of the TAD structures, we used the rescaling method to perform an O/E matrix pile-up analysis on TADs of varying lengths. The central enrichment value of intra-TAD contacts in Wenchang chickens (1.25) was higher than that in White Leghorns (1.15), suggesting that intra-domain chromatin folding is more compact and the TAD structure is stronger in Wenchang chickens (Fig. 4C and D). Pile-up analysis anchored at TAD boundaries further quantified the insulation effect of boundaries on chromatin interactions. Wenchang chicken TAD boundaries displayed a stronger spatial insulation capability than those of White Leghorns (1.10 vs. 1.08), with cross-boundary interactions being more strongly inhibited (Fig. 4E and F). Discussion

The liver is a crucial organ influencing egg-laying performance in chickens. In this study, transcriptome analysis identified a total of 2,444 genes significantly differentially expressed between the livers of White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens. Functional enrichment analysis of the 963 genes significantly upregulated in White Leghorns revealed that most pathways were related to lipid metabolism, and genes such as CPT1A, ACSL1, ACACB, ACOX1, and EHHADH were identified within these pathways.CPT1A encodes a key rate-limiting enzyme in mitochondrial fatty acid β-oxidation (Liu et al., 2024). Research has reported that the upregulation of CPT1A in the liver is accompanied by significantly increased levels of total polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA), ω−3 PUFA, and ω−6 PUFA in the yolk (Liu et al., 2024). ACSL1 is responsible for converting fatty acids into acyl-CoAs (Cruvinel et al., 2025), while the core function of ACACB is to generate malonyl-CoAs (Li et al., 2025b). A study has found that their upregulation in the liver indicates promoted fatty acid synthesis and triglyceride accumulation; the activation of this metabolic pattern provides sufficient lipid precursors for yolk formation (Li et al., 2025b). ACOX1 and EHHADH have been found to be downregulated in the liver alongside a decrease in average egg weight (Hu et al., 2024). In the physiological laying cycle of poultry, an efficient supply of cholesterol is crucial (Naber, 1976; Li et al., 2025b). Cholesterol deposited in the egg yolk is an essential nutrient for supporting normal embryonic development, and limiting cholesterol biosynthesis affects reproductive capacity (Naber, 1976; Li et al., 2025b). The liver is the primary site of cholesterol synthesis for eggs (Naber, 1976). In this study, we found that upregulated genes such as DHCR7 and SQLE were significantly enriched in Steroid biosynthesis. DHCR7 catalyzes the final step of cholesterol biosynthesis, and research has found that its significant downregulation in the liver due to infection may be associated with decreased egg production (James et al., 2025). SQLE is a rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol synthesis, and its increased mRNA expression level enhances the cholesterol content in the liver of laying hens (Zhang et al., 2020). Functional enrichment analysis of the 1,481 genes significantly downregulated in White Leghorns (i.e., significantly upregulated in Wenchang chickens) revealed significant enrichment in the Ribosome pathway. Similar results were also reported in a transcriptomic study of another indigenous Chinese chicken breed known for its meat quality, the Nandan-Yao (Xiao et al., 2021). Researchers performed functional enrichment analysis on liver DEGs from high-fat and low-fat Nandan-Yao chickens and found significant enrichment in the ribosome pathway. They proposed that these ribosome-related genes might play an essential role in body fat deposition (Xiao et al., 2021). Therefore, these distinct gene expression patterns may be closely related to the specific phenotypes of these two chicken breeds. Furthermore, we found that terms related to chromatin organization and epigenetic regulation, such as chromosome organization, regulation of gene expression, epigenetic, histone acetyltransferase activity, chromatin binding, chromatin-mediated maintenance of transcription, and condensed nuclear chromosome, were also significantly enriched. This suggests that differences may exist in the liver chromatin structure between the two breeds.

Chromatin compartments are one of the core units of 3D genomic organization in eukaryotes. Based on chromatin activity, they can be classified into A and B compartments, which partition the genome into functionally distinct regions (Lieberman-Aiden et al., 2009). Interactions between the same type of chromatin compartments are stronger than those between different types; specifically, the intensity of A-A or B-B interactions is higher than that of A-B interactions. In this study, we found that liver chromatin in both White Leghorns and Wenchang chickens exhibited clear A/B compartment segregation, with no significant difference in overall compartment strength between the two breeds. This suggests that the 3D genomic differences between the strains do not stem from global changes in the degree of chromatin compartmentalization, but may instead be reflected in local compartment remodeling at specific genomic loci. The partitioning of A/B compartments is highly dynamic and can be remodeled in response to the internal state of the cell. For example, extensive A/B compartment transitions occur during embryonic stem cell differentiation; the conversion of genomic regions from compartment A to B is often accompanied by gene silencing, whereas the transition from compartment B to A typically activates gene expression (Liu et al., 2023; Dixon et al., 2015). In this study, we found that 34 genes, including BBOX1 and GRB14, simultaneously underwent B-to-A compartment transition and transcriptional activation in White Leghorns, while 21 genes, such as PADI2 and PADI3, concurrently experienced A-to-B compartment transition and transcriptional silencing. BBOX1 encodes the enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of γ-butyrobetaine (γ-BB) to l-carnitine (Li et al., 2025a), and research has found that dietary l-carnitine supplementation can increase ovarian follicle yolk deposition (Peebles et al., 2007). Grb14 is an intracellular adaptor protein, and its liver-specific knockdown has been shown to reduce the expression of lipogenic genes, inhibit de novo fatty acid synthesis, and decrease liver triglyceride content (Popineau et al., 2016; Ding et al., 2020). We speculate that these genes, which transitioned to compartment A and were significantly upregulated in the liver of White Leghorns, may be associated with the characteristic of high egg production. Protein arginine deiminases (PADIs) mediate histone citrullination, and as important members of this family, Padi2 and Padi3 have been confirmed as key regulators of epigenomic organization in mouse trophoblast stem cells (Ballasy et al., 2022). Their double knockout can lead to DNA hypomethylation of CpG islands in certain genes, thereby inducing the transcriptional upregulation of the corresponding genes (Ballasy et al., 2022). We speculate that their B-compartmentalization and transcriptional silencing in the White Leghorn liver may also participate in regulating the expression of related genes. However, research on PADI2 and PADI3 in chickens is currently very limited, and their specific functions and targets in the liver remain unclear, requiring further exploration.

As "molecular walls" for regulating gene expression, TAD boundaries effectively restrict the scope of enhancer-promoter interactions, preventing unnecessary cross-regulation. The disruption of TADs allows enhancers to interact with genes outside their normal TAD, leading to the aberrant expression of these genes and subsequent phenotypic changes (Lupiáñez et al., 2015). A study on fast-growing commercial broilers and indigenous chicken breeds has revealed that breed-specific TAD reprogramming regulates the expression of genes involved in myogenesis and adipogenesis, which is correlated with differences in muscle mass and intramuscular fat deposition between the two breeds (Wang et al., 2024b). In this study, we found that compared to Wenchang chickens, the TAD structures in the White Leghorn liver overall exhibited weaker boundary insulation and a more relaxed intra-domain folding state. This more dynamic and open high-order chromatin architecture may provide White Leghorn liver cells with greater transcriptional regulatory flexibility to meet the demands of continuous and high-intensity yolk precursor synthesis. Furthermore, the functional enrichment of breed-specific TAD boundary genes was highly consistent with the transcriptomic profiles. Specific genes located at White Leghorn TAD boundaries were significantly enriched in lipid metabolism pathways, such as Glycerophospholipid metabolism and Glycerolipid metabolism, while specific genes at Wenchang TAD boundaries showed significant enrichment in pathways like Ribosome. This multi-level consistency indicates that breed-specific TAD reprogramming may be associated with the phenotypic differences between the two breeds.

Chromatin loop formation is a key feature of higher-order genomic organization and is essential for the regulation of gene expression. It can bring distant enhancers and target gene promoters into close spatial proximity, which is associated with transcriptional activation (Rao et al., 2014). A study found that the similarity of chromatin loops between fast-growing commercial broilers and indigenous chicken breeds is extremely low, and breed-specific chromatin loops regulate the expression of genes with different functions, contributing to the phenotypic differences between the two breeds (Wang et al., 2024b). Our analysis indicates that White Leghorns exhibit stronger chromatin loop interactions, which may provide the physical structural basis for high-intensity transcriptional activity at specific gene loci. In White Leghorns, breed-specific chromatin loops precisely anchor and upregulate some lipid metabolism-related genes, such as ACSL1, ACOX1, and EHHADH. In contrast, in the Wenchang chicken liver, highly expressed genes driven by breed-specific chromatin loops are primarily enriched in pathways such as the ribosome and oxidative phosphorylation. The high consistency across multi-omics results indicates a close correlation between the dynamic changes in 3D liver chromatin architecture and transcriptional differences between the two breeds, suggesting that these differences may be associated with egg-laying performance.

Furthermore, we found that the binding motifs of transcription factors including CTCF, ESR2, and PPARG::RXRA were significantly enriched at White Leghorn-specific loop anchors. CTCF is a key insulator protein that plays a central role in mediating chromatin loop formation and maintaining high-order genomic architecture (Rao et al., 2014). Previous studies have demonstrated that chromatin loop anchor regions are highly enriched for CTCF binding sites (Rao et al., 2014), providing biological support for the breed-specific chromatin loops identified in this study and indicating that these loops represent functional chromatin conformations with genuine structural foundations rather than random genomic contacts. ESR2, as an estrogen receptor, binds estradiol to regulate the synthesis and secretion of major yolk precursors in the liver, playing an important role in yolk deposition and egg-laying performance (Dai et al., 2021; Qin et al., 2024). PPARG heterodimerizes with RXR to regulate gene expression (Brooks et al., 2015), and studies have shown that this complex can activate lipid metabolism-related genes to promote triglyceride synthesis (Lim et al., 2015). Notably, binding motifs for a large number of Kruppel-like factor (KLF) family members were also identified as significantly enriched, including KLF4, KLF10, KLF5, KLF7, KLF15, KLF12, KLF2, KLF11, and KLF3. The KLF family represents a class of transcription factors that play key roles in lipid metabolism and fat deposition (Ling et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024). However, research on the KLF gene family in poultry remains relatively limited, and their specific biological functions and transcriptional regulatory mechanisms require further investigation.

Although this study provides novel insights into the correlation between liver chromatin structure and egg-laying performance in chickens, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, differences in absolute sampling age and breed background should be considered when interpreting the findings. Future studies employing perfectly matched sampling time points or intra-breed comparisons will further strengthen the inference regarding the relationship between the identified regulatory features and egg-laying performance. Second, the precise functional mechanisms by which spatial chromatin architecture regulates transcription remain to be fully elucidated. Future studies should integrate additional epigenomic analyses (such as ATAC-seq and ChIP-seq) to conduct more in-depth multi-omics functional investigations. This is crucial for fully revealing how specific higher-order 3D chromatin structures mediate cis-regulatory elements to influence gene expression, thereby regulating egg-laying performance.

Conclusions

This study constructed the first integrated "transcriptome-3D genome" regulatory map for the livers of high- and low-yielding egg-laying chicken breeds. Our research indicates that differences exist between the livers of White Leghorn and Wenchang chickens in terms of global chromatin folding patterns, A/B compartment status, TAD structures, and chromatin loops. Furthermore, the high egg-laying performance of White Leghorns may be associated with the systematic remodeling of high-order chromatin architecture in the liver. This study provides novel insights into the epigenetic regulatory mechanisms of laying traits in poultry and offers potential key targets for future molecular-assisted breeding based on 3D genomic information.
