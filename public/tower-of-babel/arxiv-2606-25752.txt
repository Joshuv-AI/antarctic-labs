Quantity-Dependent Bulk-to-Wall Observability of Surface Loading in Rarefied Hypersonic Flow over Triangular Protrusions
Elyas Lekzian, Ehsan Roohi

Abstract
Localized protrusions on hypersonic vehicles generate pressure, heat-transfer, and shear loads whose rarefied response can depend on gas beyond the immediate wall neighborhood. This work quantifies that bulk-to-wall dependence for triangular protrusions and tests whether coordinate-conditioned surrogates preserve it. Geometry-consistent surrogates are trained for direct simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) velocity, temperature, pressure, and wall-load profiles over Mach numbers 4–8, Knudsen numbers (Kn) 0.1–0.8, and three protrusion orientations. The central analysis is performed on raw DSMC fields. Around each wall point, circular neighborhoods of increasing radius are summarized by weighted statistics, extrema, nearest-point values, and tangent/normal gradients of velocity, temperature, and pressure. A fixed region-to-point diagnostic predicts the pressure coefficient ( C p C_{p} ), heat-transfer coefficient ( C q C_{q} ), and shear-stress magnitude ( | τ | |\tau| ). We define R 95 R_{95} as the smallest tested radius whose complete wall-profile error lies within 5% of the full-domain descriptor error. The principal physical result is that rarefied surface loading has no single information length. Full-domain descriptors reduce errors from 45.5% to 13.8% for C p C_{p} and from 72.6% to 12.9% for C q C_{q} , whereas shear improves only from 49.1% to 31.9%. Heat transfer exhibits the clearest order- h s h_{s} nonlocal support, where h s h_{s} is the protrusion-base length. Pressure is frequently right-censored beyond 3 ​ h s 3h_{s} , and shear saturates at shorter radii but remains least identifiable. Ridge-regression and threshold controls preserve this hierarchy, while a closed-loop audit shows partial surrogate preservation, with the largest degradation in forward-facing heat transfer and shear.

Antennas, sensor windows, wiring ducts, flanges, thermal-protection-system tile offsets, inspection ports, and manufacturing imperfections all behave as localized protrusions that can reorganize the near-wall flow. The present problem asks the reverse but physically related question: how much of the surrounding bulk field is required to determine a lower-dimensional wall-load profile? Rather than assuming a sensor layout in advance, the radius-controlled experiment first identifies the spatial domain in which informative pressure, velocity, and temperature measurements can plausibly reside. Throughout this paper,
observability
is used in this predictive sense: recovery of a wall-load profile from a prescribed bulk-field neighborhood by a fixed diagnostic. It is neither a control-theoretic observability-rank condition nor a Shannon-information or mutual-information measure.

This question is especially relevant in a rarefied gas because stress and heat flux need not be local functions of strain rate and temperature gradient. Li, Hu, and Wu recently proposed a non-localized spatial–temporal constitutive relation in which stress and heat flux are convolutional functionals of gradients over a finite neighborhood, with nonlocal parameters depending on Knudsen number.
LiHuWu2024
The present information horizon is complementary to, but distinct from, such a constitutive kernel. We use it as an empirical, model-conditional predictive-support measure: it identifies the spatial extent of DSMC-resolved bulk information required by a fixed diagnostic model to recover wall pressure, heat transfer, or shear. This formulation connects sparse observability, nonlocal rarefied transport, and engineering surface loading while keeping the physical interpretation tied to the sampled variables, diagnostic family, and validation protocol.
The central physical issue is therefore not only whether a surrogate can reproduce DSMC contours, but whether the surface response has a single local support length at all. If pressure, heat transfer, and shear were governed by the same near-wall information, a common radius-controlled saturation would be expected. Conversely, different saturation behavior would indicate that the three wall loads sample different parts of the rarefied bulk flow: pressure through the extended compression and deflection field, heat transfer through the thermal and velocity history feeding the windward face, and shear through near-wall momentum exchange. The present study uses this distinction as the physical test behind the data-driven diagnostic.
The main outcome is a load-dependent classification of bulk-to-wall predictive support in a rarefied hypersonic protrusion flow, rather than a universal information length or a purely surrogate-accuracy benchmark. The contribution of this work is threefold. First, geometry-consistent, output-specialized coordinate-conditioned surrogates reconstruct DSMC-resolved velocity, temperature, pressure, and protrusion-wall loads along interpolation directions supported by the database. The corrected base/apex interpretation is treated as a data-integrity prerequisite. Second, the complete 27-case Mach-number–Knudsen-number–orientation block supports a radius-controlled observability experiment whose neighborhoods are extracted directly from raw DSMC fields. Parameter-only, wall-local, finite-radius, and full-domain descriptors are compared under leave-one-physical-case-out validation, and a right-censored, model-conditional predictive-support radius
R_{95}
is extracted for
C_{p}
,
C_{q}
, and
|\tau|
. Third, a closed-loop preservation audit replaces only the held-out raw bulk field by its surrogate reconstruction while leaving the diagnostic model and DSMC wall targets unchanged; tree-bootstrap resampling quantifies diagnostic-regressor variability. The resulting analysis separates surrogate accuracy, observability in the raw kinetic fields, and preservation of that observability after reconstruction, so that each conclusion is supported by the appropriate experiment.

III
Output-specialized surrogate and observability framework

The surrogate is designed around the physical character of the outputs rather than around code convenience. Figure
2
separates the two computational roles of the study. Panel (a) follows the 57-condition database and corrected geometry through three specialized coordinate-conditioned surrogates to reconstructed bulk fields and direct wall-load profiles. Panel (b) begins instead from nested raw-DSMC neighborhoods centered at a wall point, maps their descriptors through a fixed region-to-point regressor, and returns the model-conditional predictive-support radius
R_{95}
. This separation keeps the reference observability result independent of surrogate reconstruction error while permitting a subsequent preservation audit. A single network trained on all fields and all wall quantities with one loss is statistically attractive but physically mismatched: bulk temperature and velocity occupy the entire gas region and vary relatively smoothly, pressure is localized and positive in the compression layer, and wall coefficients are one-dimensional functions with sharp windward-face and apex peaks.

The smooth-field and pressure surrogates use multiplicative branch–trunk multilayer perceptrons: case and coordinate features are encoded separately, multiplied in a latent space, and decoded through fully connected layers with SiLU (Sigmoid Linear Unit) activations. The smooth-field model uses 160 latent channels, four hidden layers of width 224, dropout 0.03, and AdamW with initial learning rate
7\times 10^{-4}

4\times 10^{-4}

8\times 10^{-4}
, and peak/apex weighting to preserve localized wall extrema. All models use weight decay, Huber-type losses, gradient clipping, plateau-based learning-rate reduction, and early stopping. The reported widths, depths, latent dimensions, dropout rates, and initial learning rates were selected from compact pilot sweeps that compared validation relative-
L_{2}

Smaller models under-resolved the apex and compression structures, whereas further increases in capacity produced negligible validation improvement and greater run-to-run variability. The selected settings were then fixed for all reported holdouts rather than retuned case by case. The training scripts, random seeds, processed split tables, and post-processing code are included with the submission package.

The orientation dependence is physically interpretable but not reducible to a single monotone law. Pressure responds to the spatially extended compression and deflection pattern, which explains the frequent domain-scale censoring. Heat-transfer loading requires the thermal and velocity history feeding the windward face, so it exhibits the clearest systematic benefit from increasing radius. Shear is dominated by near-wall momentum transfer and therefore reaches its full-domain accuracy at shorter radii, but its absolute predictive error remains larger because the target is sharp, orientation-sensitive, and statistically noisy. These findings identify a predictive-support footprint rather than a universal material length. Because the present descriptors contain neighborhood statistics, extrema, and estimated gradients rather than direct point-sensor readings, the footprint should be viewed as a candidate computational or measurement domain. A subsequent study must still define the observation type and solve a formal sensor-placement problem within that domain.

VII
Physics-aware post-processing

Field contours alone are not sufficient for evaluating a surrogate for rarefied hypersonic protrusion flow. A visually smooth field can still miss wall loads, peak locations, or thermodynamic consistency. We therefore compute engineering quantities of interest (QoIs), region-conditioned errors, and an entropy-index diagnostic. The available DSMC files contain macroscopic fields but not full velocity distribution functions, heat-flux vectors, stress tensors, or higher-order moments. Consequently, we do not claim to compute Boltzmann entropy production. Instead, for a monatomic gas we define a local-equilibrium entropy-rise proxy,

\frac{\Delta s_{\rm eq}}{R_{g}}=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\ln\left(\frac{T}{T_{\rm ref}}\right)-\ln\left(\frac{P}{P_{\rm ref}}\right),\qquad\gamma=\frac{5}{3},

(7)

where
R_{g}
is the specific gas constant of argon.
T_{\rm ref}
and
P_{\rm ref}
are estimated from an upstream/outer DSMC region for each case. This diagnostic uses the temperature from the smooth-field surrogate and the pressure from the pressure-focused surrogate, so it checks whether two separately trained decoders remain thermodynamically compatible.

and

\xi
and
\eta
, and the triangular solid is masked. This layout tests both the spatial entropy-rise pattern and compatibility of the separately trained decoders. The DSMC and reconstructed maps contain the same high-entropy band associated with compression and thermalization, while the largest residuals occur near the apex and near-wall recovery region where temperature and pressure gradients are steep. ,
where

is the location of the maximum pressure in the gas region.

VIII
Physical interpretation and implications

The bulk-to-wall analysis distinguishes geometric proximity from predictive locality. A wall point can be close to a gas cell while its pressure, heat-transfer, or shear response remains tied to a wider compression, thermal, or momentum-exchange region. The principal physical result is that the three wall loads do not share a single information length. Heat transfer shows the clearest order-
h_{s}
nonlocal support, consistent with its dependence on the thermal and velocity history feeding the windward face and apex. Pressure often retains domain-scale support within the tested window, indicating sensitivity to the broader compression and deflection field rather than only the nearest gas state. Shear reaches shorter radii, but its larger absolute error shows that short apparent support should not be interpreted as accurate local closure. Thus, the nonmonotonic finite-radius curves and censored pressure cases are part of the physical result: rarefied protrusion loading is quantity-dependent, orientation-sensitive, and not reducible to one universal mean-free-path scaling.

A consistency check between the two parts of the study deserves explicit comment, because the heat-transfer footprint that is clearest in the raw-DSMC diagnostic is also the one least faithfully reproduced when the bulk field is replaced by its surrogate reconstruction. In the closed-loop test (Table
6
),
C_{q}
footprint preservation is strong for the backward-facing orientation (censoring agreement
1.00
) but degrades for the symmetric and forward-facing orientations (
0.60
and
0.65
), and the forward-facing case carries the largest heat-transfer full-domain error inflation (
8.11
percentage points), exceeded only by forward-facing shear (
12.55
percentage points). This shared behavior follows from the same physical mechanism. The forward-facing protrusion presents the steepest windward face and the most spatially concentrated apex compression, which is simultaneously the region where the field surrogate incurs its largest reconstruction error and the structure that the bulk-to-wall diagnostic must resolve to fix the wall load. The order-
h_{s}
heat-transfer footprint is therefore a property of the kinetic fields themselves, established on the raw-DSMC reference, rather than a guarantee that a deployed surrogate will recover that footprint in every orientation. In practical terms, surrogate-based footprint estimates are most reliable for the backward-facing geometry but should defer to the raw-DSMC diagnostic for forward-facing heat transfer and shear, where apex-compression sharpness limits both field reconstruction and footprint transfer.

The connection to sparse sensing is prospective rather than direct. Existing reconstruction studies optimize point sensors to recover a complete field from limited measurements, whereas the present descriptors are engineered neighborhood summaries used to recover a lower-dimensional engineering output.

The validation results are consistent with the DSMC physics of triangular protrusions. FWD protrusions are the most demanding orientation because the incoming flow meets a steeper windward face earlier, producing stronger compression and larger pressure and heat-transfer peaks. In a continuum interpretation this would be described as a strong obstacle-induced compression layer; in the rarefied setting the same effect is mediated by molecular trajectories and wall accommodation. The surrogate captures the dominant response but still shows its largest errors where the compression layer impinges on the apex, which is the correct physical location for difficulty.

Height changes the effective obstruction and the size of the separated or recirculating region. The reference DSMC study already characterized the associated orientation-dependent vortices: forward-facing cases concentrate recirculation on the windward side, backward-facing cases emphasize the leeward wake, and the symmetric protrusion develops a stronger recirculating region as height increases. We therefore use vortex topology as physical context for the load footprints rather than duplicating that established classification with another contour set. The height-sweep validation at
h_{p}/h_{s}=1.25
lies inside the sampled height range and tests whether the model can interpolate the growth of the thermal and pressure footprints. The model performs well in this case because, over the sampled interval, the original DSMC study reports nearly monotone and approximately low-order sensitivity of the peak quantities to height. The model should not be interpreted as learning arbitrary height-Mach interaction, because such combinations were not sampled. Wall temperature affects energy accommodation and near-wall thermal exchange. Increasing wall temperature raises the local gas temperature while reducing the heat-transfer coefficient when the wall becomes closer to the gas temperature. The wall-temperature holdout at
T_{w}/T_{\infty}=2
confirms that the surrogate captures this supported thermal direction, with low errors in temperature and pressure and moderate errors in heat-transfer coefficient.

Rarefaction changes the balance between collision-mediated transport and ballistic molecular motion. Increasing Knudsen number reduces pressure loading and drag in the DSMC database while changing the thickness and location of thermal structures. The Mach-rarefaction-orientation block is therefore the strongest and most stringent validation block, because it contains the full factorial interaction of Mach number, Knudsen number, and orientation. The pressure error is largest there because pressure responds nonlinearly to both compression and rarefaction. This observation is not a weakness of the framework; it explains why pressure was separated into its own specialized head.

The ablation summary in Table

documents the design decisions. The most important result is that geometry correction is not optional. The wrong base interpretation creates artificial solid regions and corrupts both field and surface representations. The second result is that pressure and surface loads require dedicated decoders. These choices follow from the physics of localized compression and wall loading, rather than from arbitrary neural-network tuning.

IX
Scope of validity

The framework provides a verified surrogate and a model-conditional observability diagnostic for the sampled rarefied protrusion database. The primary
R_{95}
values are extracted from raw DSMC bulk fields, and the coordinate-conditioned surrogates are evaluated separately through a preservation audit on the three independent Mach-number 6, Knudsen-number 0.33 orientation holdouts. The largest preservation losses occur for forward-facing heat transfer and shear. Tree-bootstrap intervals quantify diagnostic-regressor ensemble variability; independent DSMC repeats remain the appropriate next step for separating particle-sampling uncertainty from model variability. The numerical value of the information horizon is conditional on the variables
\{u,v,T,\log P\}
, patch statistics, regressor family, tolerance relative to the reference descriptor, and right-censoring at
R/h_{s}=3
. The ridge, threshold, and reference controls show that the load-dependent ordering is stable, while the exact numerical horizon shifts with the learner and descriptor construction. Within those definitions, the robust result is a quantity-dependent hierarchy of predictive support from wall-local to domain-scale, rather than a universal mean-free-path power law inferred from only three Knudsen numbers. The sampled height and wall-temperature sweeps are restricted to Mach number 6 and do not include simultaneous nonbaseline combinations. The available field files contain macroscopic variables rather than velocity distributions, heat-flux vectors, stress tensors, or higher moments; accordingly, the entropy quantity is a local-equilibrium compatibility diagnostic. The present conclusions apply to two-dimensional monatomic argon flow, while three-dimensional protrusions, internal molecular modes, chemistry, and real-gas effects define natural extensions.

X
Conclusions

This work combines geometry-consistent, output-specialized coordinate-conditioned surrogates with a raw-DSMC bulk-to-wall observability analysis for rarefied hypersonic flow over triangular protrusions. Separate field, pressure, and wall-surface models respect the distinct localization of velocity, temperature, compression, and wall loading. On supported interpolation holdouts, errors are 2.0–3.3% for temperature, 1.3–2.2% for streamwise velocity, 3.1–6.4% for pressure, and approximately 4–5% for direct wall-load prediction. The corrected geometry is essential for data integrity, while the central physical result comes from the independent 27-case Mach-number–Knudsen-number–orientation block.

The central physical conclusion is that operating parameters alone do not determine the wall profiles, and neither does a single universal near-wall information length. Replacing parameter-only inputs by full-domain raw-DSMC descriptors reduces mean leave-one-physical-case-out error from 45.47% to 13.76% for
C_{p}
and from 72.59% to 12.89% for
C_{q}
; the reduction for
|\tau|
is smaller, from 49.11% to 31.85%. The information-horizon analysis reveals three different observability regimes rather than one universal length: heat transfer has the clearest order-
h_{s}
nonlocal footprint, pressure is frequently domain-scale and right-censored at
R/h_{s}=3
, and shear reaches shorter radii but remains the least identifiable profile. The corresponding Knudsen-number trends are orientation-dependent and nonmonotonic in several groups, so they are not promoted to a universal mean-free-path scaling.

The closed-loop experiment connects the two computational parts of the study without conflating them. Raw DSMC fields define the reference predictive support, while surrogate-reconstructed fields test preservation under deployment. The broad radius dependence is retained for most pressure and heat-transfer cases, with measurable degradation for forward-facing
C_{q}
and especially forward-facing shear. Tree-bootstrap resampling supports an order-
h_{s}
heat-transfer footprint and shows that the current data resolve the scale of the support more reliably than fine monotonic variation with Knudsen number. A ridge-regression control, a 2.5–10% tolerance sweep, and a full-ring versus maximum-cumulative reference comparison preserve the central quantity ordering while confirming that exact horizon values remain model-conditional. Together, the experiments distinguish predictive support in the kinetic fields, preservation by the surrogate, and uncertainty of the diagnostic regressor. Future work should add independent DSMC realizations, intermediate Knudsen numbers, exported heat-flux and stress moments, and an explicit point-sensor model before load-specific sensor placement is attempted.
