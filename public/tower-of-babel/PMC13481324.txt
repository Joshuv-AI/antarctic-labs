Triple-coaxial Balloon-occluded n-butyl Cyanoacrylate Embolization for a Renal Arteriovenous Fistula: A Case Report
Authors: Yohei Takeshita, Junji Moriya, Yo Matsui, Toshinori Seto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRenal arteriovenous fistula is a rare vascular lesion. We report a man in his 40s with a right renal arteriovenous fistula consisting of a single feeding artery, a single draining vein, and a venous aneurysmal component with outflow stenosis, corresponding to a type I lesion in the Cho/Yakes-based angio-architectural classification. To achieve occlusion extending from the shunt point toward the venous aneurysmal component while preserving the surrounding renal parenchyma, balloon-occluded embolization with warmed 33% n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate using a triple-coaxial system was performed, followed by proximal coil reinforcement. The shunt disappeared completely without renal dysfunction or procedure-related complications. This case suggests that triple-coaxial balloon-occluded n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate embolization may be a useful option for selected renal arteriovenous fistulas.Keywords: renal arteriovenous fistula, balloon-occluded glue embolization, n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate, triple-coaxial catheter system, endovascular therapy

Introduction

Renal AVF is a rare vascular lesion that requires evaluation from both diagnostic and therapeutic perspectives [1, 2]. In lesions with a direct arteriovenous communication, corresponding to type I in the Cho/Yakes-based angio-architectural classification, embolization of the shunt using coils or a vascular plug is generally regarded as the most effective treatment strategy [2]. However, renal AVFs have also been treated successfully with NBCA, indicating that liquid embolization may be a reasonable option in selected cases [3].

Uncontrolled injection of NBCA may result in loss of embolic control, which can lead to unintended venous migration or other non-target embolic complications, including pulmonary embolization [4]. In addition, excessive or inappropriate proximal venous occlusion may cause renal congestion or hemorrhagic complications. Microballoon-assisted endovascular treatment offers technical advantages such as flow control and improved selectivity [5]. Furthermore, the combination of a triaxial microballoon system and warmed NBCA provides stable back-up, a reduced risk of glue adhesion, and improved distal penetration capability [6].

Here, we report a case of renal AVF treated with balloon-occluded NBCA embolization using a triple-coaxial system through a slender 3.0-Fr guiding sheath to achieve occlusion extending from the shunt point toward the venous aneurysmal component while preserving the surrounding renal parenchyma.

Case Reports

A 41-year-old man was referred to our institution after screening ultrasonography revealed abnormal vascular signals in the right kidney. He was asymptomatic, without gross hematuria, flank pain, or signs of heart failure. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated a right renal AVF in the dorsal portion of the kidney, consisting of a single feeding artery, a single draining vein, and a venous aneurysmal component (Figure 1). Notably, a stenotic segment was present on the proximal venous side between the shunt point and the venous aneurysmal component, and another stenotic segment was present on the distal outflow side (Figure 2a and b). Early venous enhancement during the arterial phase indicated the presence of an arteriovenous shunt. According to the Cho/Yakes-based angio-architectural classification, this configuration corresponded to a type I lesion.

(b) Schematic illustration of the AVF configuration, showing the shunt point (arrow), proximal stenotic segment (arrowhead), venous aneurysmal component (asterisk), and distal outflow stenosis (double arrowheads).

There was no history of renal trauma, renal biopsy, or previous renal intervention; however, the exact etiology of the lesion remained uncertain. His medical history included diabetes mellitus and hypertension, both of which were well-controlled with medication. Baseline renal function was preserved, with normal serum creatinine levels and no proteinuria.

Although the patient was asymptomatic, intervention was considered reasonable because the lesion had a venous aneurysmal component and venous stenotic segments, findings that raised concern for future progression of the shunt and possible hemorrhagic or venous hypertensive complications. The lesion was also considered suitable for selective embolization with preservation of the surrounding renal parenchyma.

This case report was approved by the institutional review board of our institution. Written informed consent for publication of the case report and accompanying images was obtained from the patient.

Procedure

An initial transvenous approach was attempted because embolization of the venous aneurysmal component was considered an acceptable initial therapeutic goal, and the venous route appeared to provide the most direct access to that component. If transvenous access had been feasible, retrograde coil embolization was planned to occlude the venous aneurysmal component and the venous segment adjacent to the shunt point, rather than the distal outflow vein alone. However, catheter advancement through the draining vein was not possible because of its small caliber and focal stenosis, and the procedure was therefore converted to a transarterial approach.

A right femoral artery puncture was performed, and a Parent Plus 30 guiding sheath (Medikit Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) was introduced directly into the right renal artery without the use of an outer sheath. Renal arteriography demonstrated a right renal AVF consisting of a single feeding artery, a single draining vein, and a venous aneurysmal component, consistent with the CT findings (Figure 3). Through the Parent Plus 30 guiding sheath, a Pinnacle Blue 27 microballoon catheter (Tokai Medical Products, Aichi, Japan) was advanced selectively into the feeding artery. Subsequently, a Marvel 1.9-Fr microcatheter (Tokai Medical Products, Aichi, Japan) was passed coaxially through the balloon catheter and positioned distal to the balloon tip, forming a stable triple-coaxial system (Figure 4).

Balloon-occluded angiography demonstrated the shunt point and venous aneurysmal component without visualization of normal renal arterial branches or renal parenchymal enhancement (Figure 4). These findings were considered sufficient for procedural decision-making in this case, and a separate balloon occlusion test was not performed.

Although the shunt point itself was accessible, catheterization up to the venous aneurysmal component was difficult because of the intervening stenosis. Coil embolization alone was therefore considered less suitable for treating the distal shunt compartment, including the venous aneurysmal component. Balloon-occluded NBCA embolization was selected to achieve occlusion extending from the shunt point toward the venous aneurysmal component while preserving the surrounding renal parenchyma.

Before injection, the 33% NBCA-Lipiodol mixture was warmed to 40°C in a water bath. Because a proximal stenotic segment was present between the shunt point and the venous aneurysmal component, warming was considered important to allow the embolic material to traverse this narrowed segment and reach the aneurysmal sac, rather than forming a premature proximal cast. Under balloon occlusion, the warmed NBCA-Lipiodol mixture was slowly injected through the Marvel microcatheter with careful attention to avoid migration into the normal renal vein (Figure 5). Injection was terminated after the embolic material reached the distal venous aneurysmal component and satisfactory filling of the aneurysmal sac was confirmed. A total of 1.8 mL of the warmed 33% NBCA-Lipiodol mixture was administered. The angiography immediately after the injection confirmed disappearance of both the shunt and the venous aneurysmal component.

Balloon-occluded NBCA embolization through the Marvel 1.9-Fr microcatheter.

The embolic cast extends from the shunt point toward the venous aneurysmal component (asterisk).

Additional proximal coil embolization was then performed as reinforcement of the embolized segment. Using an ASAHI Meister 0.016-inch guidewire (ASAHI INTECC, Aichi, Japan), a Veloute 19 DM microcatheter (ASAHI INTECC, Aichi, Japan) was advanced to the vicinity of the NBCA cast, and two Target 360 Soft coils (4 mm × 12 cm; Stryker, Kalamazoo, MI, USA) and two C-stopper coils (2.8 mm × 10 cm; Piolax Medical Devices, Inc., Kanagawa, Japan) were deployed (Figure 6). Systemic heparinization was not performed because the procedure was performed through a small-caliber guiding sheath and was expected to be completed within a short time, and the risk of thrombotic complications was considered low.

Post-embolization digital subtraction angiography confirming disappearance of the shunt and venous aneurysmal component. Additional proximal coil reinforcement (arrow) is indicated.

The patient tolerated the procedure well and experienced no procedure-related complications. There was no evidence of glue migration, access-site complications, or post-embolization syndrome. Renal function remained stable throughout the postoperative period. He was discharged uneventfully, and follow-up imaging confirmed durable occlusion of the AVF (Figure 7).

Discussion

In the present case, the lesion was considered consistent with a renal AVF with a single feeding artery, a single draining vein, and a venous aneurysmal component associated with both proximal and distal venous stenotic segments. According to the Cho/Yakes-based angio-architectural classification, this configuration corresponded to a type I lesion. In general, embolization of the shunt using coils or a vascular plug is regarded as the most effective treatment strategy for type I lesions with a direct arteriovenous communication [2]. However, renal AVFs have also been treated successfully with NBCA, indicating that liquid embolization may be a reasonable option in selected cases [3].

Although the shunt point itself was accessible in this case, coil embolization alone was considered less suitable for treating the distal shunt compartment, including the venous aneurysmal component. In particular, focal mechanical occlusion limited to the immediate vicinity of the shunt point might have left residual communication toward the venous aneurysmal component. The presence of a proximal stenotic segment between the shunt point and the aneurysmal sac was considered an important anatomical feature, because it was relevant both to the difficulty of catheter advancement into the venous aneurysmal component and to the possibility that NBCA might not adequately reach the aneurysmal sac because of the stenosis. Because the therapeutic aim was to obtain occlusion extending from the shunt point toward the venous aneurysmal component while preserving the surrounding renal parenchyma, balloon-occluded NBCA embolization was selected. The angiography immediately after the injection confirmed disappearance of both the shunt and the venous aneurysmal component, and proximal coil embolization was subsequently added as reinforcement of the treated segment. The absolute necessity of additional coil reinforcement is difficult to determine in a single case; however, it was intended to provide supplementary mechanical support to the proximal treated segment. In addition, initial occlusion with NBCA may reduce the number of coils required compared with coil embolization alone, although this potential benefit cannot be definitively evaluated in a single case.

The technical advantages of this triple-coaxial balloon-occluded NBCA embolization technique are consistent with those previously described for a triaxial microballoon system. Specifically, stable back-up and adequate flow control can be achieved during glue embolization, while injection through a distally advanced inner microcatheter may reduce the risk of glue adhesion to the balloon surface. In addition, warming of NBCA reduces viscosity and may improve distal penetration capability [6]. In the present case, the 33% NBCA-Lipiodol mixture was warmed to 40°C in a water bath to facilitate traversal of the proximal stenotic segment and penetration into the venous aneurysmal component. Balloon-occluded angiography also demonstrated no visualization of normal renal arterial branches or renal parenchymal enhancement, supporting selective embolization while preserving the surrounding renal parenchyma. This finding may also have diagnostic value, because selective balloon-occluded angiography can help identify the final normal renal arterial branch and the shunt point more precisely. This information may serve as a useful landmark for determining the appropriate embolization site in renal AVF.

This report has several limitations. First, the conceptual framework of warmed NBCA embolization using a triaxial microballoon system has already been described, and the novelty of the present case lies not in the device concept itself but in its disease-specific application to renal AVF [6]. Specifically, the system was used through a slender 3.0-Fr guiding sheath to achieve occlusion extending from the shunt point toward the venous aneurysmal component in a type I lesion. Second, this approach should not be interpreted as a universal first-line strategy for all type I renal AVFs, se coil or plug embolization remains the standard treatment option in many such lesions [2]. In addition, as an alternative transarterial strategy for a direct shunt, coaxial embolization using a 5-Fr balloon catheter under systemic heparinization may also be considered; however, in the present case, the triple-coaxial system was selected because it allowed selective injection through the inner microcatheter while maintaining slender access. Third, triple-coaxial glue embolization carries a potential risk of glue adhesion to the inner microcatheter, which may secondarily affect the outer microballoon catheter during withdrawal, although this problem was not encountered in the present case. Finally, because this is a single case report, no definitive conclusion can be drawn regarding superiority over coil embolization alone.

Conclusion

Triple-coaxial balloon-occluded NBCA embolization using a Parent Plus 30 guiding sheath, Pinnacle Blue 27 microballoon catheter, and Marvel microcatheter enables selective occlusion extending from the shunt point toward the venous aneurysmal component while preserving the surrounding renal parenchyma. This technique may represent a useful treatment option for selected renal AVFs.
