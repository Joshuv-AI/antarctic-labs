Sectionally indecomposable groups
Andrea Lucchini, Nowras Otmen

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of sectional indecomposability and study it for finite groups: a group H H is sectionally indecomposable if, whenever H H is a section of a direct product A × B A\times B , then H H is already a section of A A or of B B . We show that the study of sectionally indecomposable finite groups reduces to the monolithic case. Our main result is a complete characterisation of sectional indecomposability for monolithic primitive groups: such a group G G with N = soc ⁡ ( G ) N=\mathrm{soc}(G) is sectionally indecomposable if and only if either N N is non-abelian, or N N is a p p -group and O p ′ ​ ( G / N ) ≠ 1 O_{p^{\prime}}(G/N)\neq 1 . The proof relies on the introduction of the notion of an H H -Frattini module and on the theory of the universal p p -Frattini cover, together with a result of Griess–Schmid. As a corollary, every monolithic primitive solvable group is sectionally indecomposable. We also discuss the non-primitive case, which appears significantly harder, and highlight open questions concerning monolithic p p -groups.



1.
Introduction

There exist various notions of ‘indecomposability’ in the theory of groups. Two illustrations of this are as follows.

Let
G
be a group. Then
G
is
directly indecomposable
if it is not isomorphic to a non-trivial direct product of groups. One way to rephrase this is that, if

, then, without loss of generality,

and

is trivial. One can also speak of free indecomposability: we say that

is

The purpose of this note is to explore another concept which falls into this type of framework. Let

and

be two groups. Then

is a

of

if it is the quotient of a subgroup of

and we denote it by

. We define a group

to be

if, whenever

and

or

.

Although this definition makes sense in general, the bulk of this work concerns itself solely with finite groups, so this will be tacitly assumed until the last subsection, where we briefly comment on this property for infinite groups. As such, one can very quickly see that the study of sectionally indecomposable finite groups is reduced to the monolithic case, as shown in

2.3

Let

This result is surprising for a couple of reasons. The first criterion to detect sectional indecomposability that we proved, which is the content of

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Sectionally indecomposable groups

Here, we will define the notion of sectional indecomposability and prove some basic results. Let
G
and
H
be finite groups. One says that
G
is a
section
of
H
if there exist
L\unlhd K\leq H
such that
K/L\cong G
, and we denote this by
G\leq_{s}H
.

Definition 2.1
.

A finite group
G
is
sectionally indecomposable
if, for finite groups
A
and
B
, the condition
G\leq_{s}A\times B
implies
G\leq_{s}A
or
G\leq_{s}B
.

The following easy characterisation of sectional indecomposability will be useful in a few proofs and in finding examples of groups which do not satisfy this property.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Then
G
is sectionally indecomposable if, and only if, whenever a subdirect product
X\leq A\times B
is such that
G
is an epimorphic image of
X
, then
G\leq_{s}A
or
G\leq_{s}B
.

Proof.

The direction
(\Rightarrow)
is clear. If
G\leq_{s}A\times B
, then there exist
K\unlhd H\leq A\times B
such that
H/K\cong G
. If we denote by
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
the projections of
A\times B
onto
A
and
B
, respectively, then
H\leq\pi_{A}(H)\times\pi_{B}(H)
is subdirect and surjects onto
G
, so either
G\leq_{s}\pi_{A}(H)
or
G\leq_{s}\pi_{B}(H)
, and we are done.
∎

Notice that, by induction, our definition of sectionally indecomposable is equivalent to the following statement:
G\leq_{s}A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n}
implies
G\leq_{s}A_{i}
for some
i
. Recall that a finite group
G
is
monolithic
if it contains a unique minimal normal subgroup.

Lemma 2.3
.

If a finite group
G
is sectionally indecomposable, then
G
is monolithic.

Proof.

If
G
possesses two distinct minimal normal subgroups
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
, then
G
embeds in
G/N_{1}\times G/N_{2}
, contradicting the sectional indecomposability of
G
.
∎

The proof of the previous lemma suggests a further definition: a finite group
G
is
subdirectly indecomposable
if, for finite groups
A
and
B
, the condition
G\leq A\times B
implies
G\leq_{s}A
or
G\leq_{s}B
. It is easy to see that the following result holds.

Proposition 2.4
.

A finite group
G
is subdirectly indecomposable if and only if it is monolithic.

Proof.

As we noticed in the proof of
Lemma

2.3
, if
G
possesses two distinct minimal normal subgroups
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
, then
G
embeds in
G/N_{1}\times G/N_{2}
, hence it is not subdirectly indecomposable. Conversely, assume that
G\leq H_{1}\times H_{2}
, but neither
G\leq_{s}H_{1}
nor
G\leq_{s}H_{2}.
For
1\leq i\leq 2,
consider the projection
\pi_{i}\colon G\leq H_{1}\times H_{2}\to H_{i}.
Since
G/\ker\pi_{i}\cong G^{\pi_{i}}\leq H_{i},
it follows from
G\not\leq_{s}H_{i}
that
\ker\pi_{i}\neq 1
. In particular,
\ker\pi_{1}
and
\ker\pi_{2}
are distinct non-trivial normal subgroups of

with trivial intersection. Hence

is not monolithic, as required.
∎

Lemma

2.3
immediately reduces the problem of determining whether a group

is sectionally indecomposable to the case where

is a monolithic group, which will be taken up in

.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose a finite group

can be written as

with

abelian and let

be the second cohomology group associated to this action. If there exists a non-split extension

by

and

, then

is not sectionally indecomposable.

Proof.

Let

and, for any subgroup

, define

Consider the subgroups

and

of

X/K\cong(M\times M)/K\rtimes\mathrm{diag}(E)/K\cong M\rtimes H,

where the action of

Corollary 2.6

If

If

Suppose that

Lemma 2.7

Let

We note that the previous result does not hold in general if one does not assume

2.2.
Crowns in finite groups

The notion of crowns was introduced by Gaschütz in

]
in the case of finite soluble groups and generalised in

]
to arbitrary finite groups.
A detailed exposition of the theory is also given in

Let

It turns out that

has a complemented chief factor

Let

be the smallest normal subgroup contained in

and isomorphic as a

It turns out that

where

such that

The

such that

is called the

and coincides with the number of

-isomorphic to

Notice in particular that if

then

The

We recall a result from Gaschütz

. Let

are isomorphic as

Suppose

3.
Monolithic groups

First, we deal with the easy case of monolithic groups with a non-abelian socle.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group with a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
. If

is non-abelian, then

is sectionally indecomposable.

Proof.

Let
A
and
B
be finite groups and let

be a subdirect product such that
X/K\cong G
for some
K\unlhd X
. Denote by
\pi_{A}
and by
\pi_{B}
the projections of
X
onto
A
and
B
, respectively, and let
Y_{A}=\ker\pi_{A}
and
Y_{B}=\ker\pi_{B}
. It is not restrictive to assume that both
Y_{A}
and
Y_{B}
are non-trivial. Denote by
T/K
the minimal normal subgroup of
X/K
and suppose that
Y_{A}\not\leq K
. In that case, it follows that
T/K\leq Y_{A}K/K
. Since
Y_{A}
and
Y_{B}
commute in

, we have
Y_{B}K/K\leq C_{X/K}(Y_{A}K/K)
. Since
Y_{B}K/K\leq C_{X/K}(Y_{A}K/K)
, which implies that

and thus that

. Thus

is sectionally indecomposable by

2.2
.
∎

Henceforth, let

be a monolithic primitive group, and assume that

is abelian. Then

where

is an irreducible subgroup of

We want to determine under which conditions

is sectionally indecomposable.

If

then

is cyclic of prime order and

is clearly sectionally indecomposable. So we may assume

To simplify our arguments, the following definition will be useful.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

and

be finite groups and let

and

be an
H
-module and a

-module, respectively. We say that

is

-equivalent

if there exist group isomorphisms
\alpha:B\to A
and
\beta:G/C_{G}(B)\to H
such that
(b^{g})^{\alpha}=(b^{\alpha})^{(gC_{G}(B))^{\beta}}
for every

and

In that way,

can be seen as an

-module which is

-isomorphic to

.

The motivation for

3.2
is clarified by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X=A\rtimes H
, with
A
a faithful irreducible
H
-module, let
G
be a finite group and let
B
be an irreducible
G
-module. Then
L_{B}\cong X
if and only if
B
is
H
-equivalent to
A
. In particular,
G
admits an epimorphic image isomorphic to
X
if and only if
G
has a non-Frattini chief factor
H
-equivalent to
A
.

Proof.

First, assume that
B
is
H
-equivalent to
A
. Given
\alpha\colon B\to A
and

\beta\colon G/C_{G}(B)\to H
as in
Definition

3.2
, we can define an isomorphism

\gamma\colon L_{B}=B\rtimes G/C_{G}(B)\to X
by setting
(b,gC_{G}(B))^{\gamma}=b^{\alpha}\cdot(gC_{G}(B))^{\beta}.

Conversely, if
\gamma\colon L_{B}\to X
is an isomorphism, then the restriction
\alpha
of
\gamma
to
B
induces an isomorphism between
B=\soc(L_{B})
and
A=\soc(X)
. Moreover, for every
g\in G,
there exists a unique

such that
(B,gC_{G}(B))^{\gamma}=(A,h)
. If we set
(gC_{G}(B))^{\beta}=h
, we obtain an isomorphism
\beta\colon G/C_{G}(B)\to H
with the property that
(b^{g})^{\alpha}=(b^{\alpha})^{(gC_{G}(B))^{\beta}}
for every

and

∎

A
proper section
of a group
G
is either a proper quotient of
G
or a quotient of any of its proper subgroups.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose we are under the hypotheses of
Lemma

3.3
. Assume that
G
has a minimal normal subgroup
N
that is
H
-equivalent to
A
, but no proper section of
G
admits a chief factor
H
-equivalent to
A.

Then either
G\cong X
or
C_{G}(N)\leq\Phi(G).

Proof.

First, we claim that
N
is the unique minimal normal subgroup of
G
. Indeed, suppose that
M
is another minimal normal subgroup of
G
; then
[M,N]=1
, so
M\leq C_{G}(N)
. Now, let
\alpha\colon N\to A
and
\beta:G/C_{G}(N)\to H
be as in
Definition

3.2
. Define
\tilde{\alpha}\colon NM/M\to A
by
(nM)^{\tilde{\alpha}}=n^{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\beta}\colon(G/M)/(C_{G/M}(NM/M))\to H
by
(gM\cdot C_{G/M}(NM/M))^{\tilde{\beta}}=(gC_{G}(N))^{\beta}
. Then it can be easily verified that

is a chief factor of

which is
H
-equivalent to
A
.

If

, then, by

3.3
,

is an epimorphic image of

. Since no proper section of

admits a chief factor

-equivalent to

, the kernel of the epimorphism

.

It remains to prove that if

, then

.
Assume, by contradiction, that

. Then there exists a non-Frattini chief factor

of

with
N\leq V<U\leq C_{G}(N)
. There exists a supplement

of

in

. Then

is a minimal normal subgroup of

, and we may define an isomorphism

by setting

. Then

for all

. Thus,

is a chief factor of

, which is

-equivalent to

, in contradiction with the assumption that no proper section of

admits a chief factor

-equivalent to

.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

We say that an
H
-module

is

-Frattini
if there exist a finite group

and a normal subgroup

of

with the following properties:

(1)

is
H
-equivalent to

;

(2)

C_{G}(N)\leq\Phi(G).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
X=A\rtimes H
be a monolithic primitive group with abelian socle
A
, and assume

Then

is sectionally indecomposable if and only if
A
is not a Frattini
H
-module.

Proof.

First, we prove that if
A
is a Frattini
H
-module, then
X
is not sectionally indecomposable. Take
G
as in
Definition

3.5
and let
C=C_{G}(N)
. For every
U\leq G,

we define

{\rm{diag}}(U)=\{(u,u)\mid u\in U\}\leq G^{2}.

Consider

Y=\frac{N^{2}{\rm{diag}}(G)}{{\rm{diag}}(C)}.

Let

M=\frac{{\rm{diag}}(C)N^{2}}{{\rm{diag}}(C)},\quad K=\frac{{\rm{diag}}(G)}{{\rm{diag}}(C)}.

Clearly
K\cong G/C\cong H
,
M\cong N
,

and
M\cap K=1.
Moreover, if

and

then
m^{k}=(n^{g},1){\rm{diag}}(C),
so

is
H
-equivalent to
A

and therefore

We want to prove that no section of

can be isomorphic to

. Assume by contradiction that

is a section of
G
with

. In particular

is a chief factor of
R
and
R/C_{R}(U/T)\cong H.
Since

is nilpotent,

and therefore, by
[

, 5.2.9]
,

C\cap R\leq C_{R}(U/T).

This implies

|H|=\left|\frac{R}{C_{R}(U/T)}\right|\leq\left|\frac{R}{R\cap C}\right|=\left|\frac{RC}{C}\right|\leq\left|\frac{G}{C}\right|=|H|.

In particular

and
C_{R}(U/T)=C\cap R
. However, since

from

we deduce that
G=R
and
C_{G}(U/T)=C.
Since
U\leq C
and
|G/U|=|G/C|=|H|,
we deduce
U=C\leq\Phi(G)
in contradiction with the fact that
U/T
is complemented in
G/T.
We have thus proved that

is not sectionally indecomposable.

Conversely, assume that
X
is not sectionally indecomposable.
So there exist
Y_{1},Y_{2}
such that
X
is neither a section
of
Y_{1}
nor of
Y_{2},
yet there exists a section
U/V
of

Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
with
U/V\cong X.
For
i\in\{1,2\},

consider the projection
\pi_{i}\colon Y_{1}\times Y_{2}\to Y_{i}.

We choose
U\leq Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
minimal with respect to
the property that
U/V\cong X
for some normal subgroup

V
of
U.
Without loss of generality we may assume

U^{\pi_{1}}=Y_{1}
and
U^{\pi_{2}}=Y_{2}.
Let
K_{1}=\ker\pi_{1}\cap U

and
K_{2}=\ker\pi_{2}\cap U.
It must be that
K_{i}\neq 1
, for

otherwise

would be a subgroup of
Y_{i}
with a section isomorphic to
X
.
We may identify
A
with a chief
factor of
U
. It follows that
V
contains
R=R_{U}(A).

In fact, the minimality of
U
ensures that

and consequently

with
C=C_{U}(A).

Since
(K_{1}\cap R)\times(K_{2}\cap R)
is a normal subgroup
of

contained in

without loss of
generality we may assume
K_{1}\cap R=K_{2}\cap R=1.
It follows that, for
i\in\{1,2\},

K_{i}R/R
is a nontrivial normal subgroup of

, and therefore
C\leq K_{i}R.
Since
[K_{1},K_{2}]=1,

K_{2}\leq C_{U}(RK_{1}/R)\leq C_{U}(C/R)=C.

Similarly
K_{1}\leq C.

In other words,
C=R\times K_{1}=R\times K_{2},
and
K_{1}\cong_{U}K_{2}\cong_{U}A

(indeed
A\cong_{U}C/R\cong_{U}RK_{i}/R\cong_{U}K_{i},
for

.
Recall that

for a suitable subgroup

of

and

L/R\cong H.
Now consider
J:=(K_{1}L)^{\pi_{2}}.
First, notice that
\tilde{K}:=K_{1}^{\pi_{2}}\cong K_{1}\cong_{U}A
(indeed
K_{1}^{\pi_{2}}\cong_{U}K_{1}K_{2}/K_{2}\cong_{U}K_{2}
). Moreover

and

Therefore we have shown that

has an epimorphic image containing a minimal normal subgroup which is
H
-equivalent to

. We can consider an epimorphic image of

that is as small as possible with respect to this property. This epimorphic image satisfies the assumptions of

, so it is either a split extension of

by

or

is

-Frattini.
∎

4.
The main theorem

Let
H
be a finite group. While the definition of
H
-Frattini modules is needed to prove the main technical theorem of the last section, it is unclear how to check whether, for a given
H
-module
A
, there exists a finite group
G
which satisfies the conditions of
Definition

3.5
. The aim of this section is to address this difficulty, and we will do so by means of the
p
-Frattini modules. The notation for this section follows the one defined in
Section

2.3
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
A
is a faithful irreducible non-trivial
H
-Frattini module. If
A
is a
p
-group, then
O_{p^{\prime}}(H)=1.
In particular,
H
is not solvable.

Proof.

Let
G
be a finite group satisfying the conditions of
Definition

3.5
. While the extension of
C_{G}(N)
by
H
is not a
p
-Frattini extension, the fact that
N
is a
p
-group contained in the Frattini of
G
means that we can quotient by
O_{p^{\prime}}(\Phi(G))
. Thus, it is not restrictive to assume that
C_{G}(N)
is a
p
-group. The properties of the universal Frattini
p
-cover of
H
imply the existence of an integer
n
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{O_{p}(H_{n})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{G}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

commutes; here,

because

. This implies
[(O_{p^{\prime}}(H_{n}))^{\delta},C_{G}(N)]=1
and thus
(O_{p^{\prime}}(H_{n}))^{\delta}\subseteq C_{G}(N)

.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

be a faithful irreducible

-module. Assume that

is a

-group. Then

is

-Frattini if and only if
O_{p^{\prime}}(H)=1.

Proof.

By

, if

is

-Frattini, then

Conversely, assume that

Recall that a group

is

has a normal

Combined with

that

This yields a concrete criterion to establish whether a monolithic primitive group with abelian socle is sectionally indecomposable.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let

be a monolithic primitive group with abelian socle

, and assume

is sectionally indecomposable if and only if

5.
Concluding remarks and open questions

5.1.
Cohomology and irreducible modules

Let
A
be a faithful irreducible
H
-module. Notice that, if
O_{p^{\prime}}(H)\neq 1
, it can be seen from the five-term exact sequence that
H^{2}(H,A)=0
, while
Corollary

2.6
indicates that if the second cohomology group is non-trivial, then the group

is not sectionally indecomposable as long as there exists a non-split extension of
A
by
H
in the cohomology group which is not isomorphic to
A\rtimes H
. The following example shows that the second cohomology group being trivial is not a sufficient condition for sectional indecomposability.

We use the notation in
[
10
, Example 1]
. Let
H=A_{5}
be the alternating group of degree 5 and let
M_{0}=A_{2}(H)
. There exist 3 irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{2}H
-modules up to isomorphism:
\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
X
and
P
of dimensions 1, 4 and 4, respectively. The group
G=X\rtimes H
is a monolithic primitive group with abelian socle and
O_{p^{\prime}}(H)=1
, so
G
is not sectionally indecomposable by
Corollary

4.3
. However, from the discussion in
[
10
, Example 1]
, the head of
A_{2}(H)
(i.e., its quotient by the radical) is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, whereas the socle is isomorphic to
X
. Then
[
10
, Lemma 3]
on one hand implies
H^{1}(H,X)
and
H^{2}(H,\mathbb{F}_{2})
are non-trivial, and on the other that
H^{1}(H,\mathbb{F}_{2})
and

are trivial. Furthermore, if we had considered instead

, then
L
is not sectionally indecomposable and
H^{1}(H,P)=H^{2}(H,P)=0
; this illustrates the need for
Proposition

2.10
, since
P
is not isomorphic to any composition factors of
M_{0}
, but it will be so for some
M_{n}
with

.

5.2.
On non-split extensions and
p
-groups

The methods presented here fall short in the attempt of proving any general statement in the context of non-primitive monolithic groups. In general, if
G
is a monolithic group with abelian socle
A
which does not split over
A
, one could still hope that it splits over some other abelian normal subgroup, say,
G=M\rtimes H
, in the hopes of applying
Lemma

2.5
. However, even if
H^{2}(H,M)\neq 0
, it is not clear whether there is at least one 2-cocycle which defines a non-split extension
E
that is not isomorphic to
G
. This prompts the following natural question, which is open to the best of our knowledge.

Question 5.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and suppose
G=M\rtimes H
with
M
abelian. If
H^{2}(H,M)\neq 0
, does there exist a non-split extension
E
of
M
by
H
corresponding to a non-trivial 2-cocycle in

which is not isomorphic to
G
?

A particular manifestation of the above is the case of
p
-groups. A
p
-group is monolithic if, and only if, its center is cyclic, and furthermore it is clearly not split over its socle. Even though we can make no general statement about the sectional indecomposability of
p
-groups, we offer some examples of
p
-groups which do not enjoy this property.

For a positive integer

, denote by
D_{2^{n}}
and by

the dihedral and the generalised quaternion groups of order
2^{n}
, respectively. Let

. Since
D_{2^{n}}\cong C_{2^{n-1}}\rtimes C_{2},
and
Q_{2^{n}}
is a non-split extension of
C_{2^{n-1}}
by
C_{2},
it follows from
Lemma

2.5
that
D_{2^{n}}
is not sectionally indecomposable.
For
G=Q_{2^{n}}
, consider the following construction. If

H=D_{2^{n}}=\langle x,y\mid x^{2^{n-1}}=1=y^{2},\,x^{y}=x^{-1}\rangle\,\text{ and }X=C_{4}=\langle a\mid a^{4}=1\rangle

then

is neither a section of
H
nor a section of
X.
Take
U\leq H\times X
generated by
(x,1)
and
(y,a)
and

. One can see that

is non-abelian of order
2^{n}
and
(x^{2^{n-2}},1)K
is the unique element of order 2 in
U/K
, hence
U/K\cong Q_{2^{n}}.

Next, consider the non-abelian groups of order
p^{3}
for an odd prime
p
given by

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=(C_{p}\times C_{p})\rtimes C_{p}=\langle x,y,z\mid x^{p}=y^{p}=z^{p}=1,[x,y]=1,x^{z}=xy,y^{z}=y\rangle,

\displaystyle G_{2}

\displaystyle=C_{p^{2}}\rtimes C_{p}=\langle u,v\mid u^{p^{2}}=1,v^{p}=1,u^{v}=u^{p+1}\rangle.

Note that
G_{2}
contains a normal subgroup
N=\langle u^{p},v\rangle\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}
, which is neither complemented nor central, so
G_{2}
is a non-split extension of
N
by
C_{p}
which gives a non-trivial class in
H^{2}(C_{p},C_{p}\times C_{p})
, so
Lemma

2.5
applies and
G_{1}
is not sectionally indecomposable. For
G_{2}
, we resort to a specific construction. Let
A=C_{p^{2}}=\langle a\rangle
, and consider the subgroup
U
of
A\times G_{1}
generated by the 3 elements
\alpha=(a^{p},y)
,
\beta=(a,x)
,
\gamma=(1,z)
. One can see that
\langle\alpha\rangle\leq Z(U)
and
U/\langle\alpha\rangle
is a non-abelian
p
-group of order
p^{3}
containing an element of order
p^{2}
; it follows thus that
G_{2}\cong U/\langle\alpha\rangle
, so
G_{2}
is not sectionally indecomposable.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
k
be a positive integer. We will denote the group
C_{p}\wr C_{p^{k}}
by
W_{p,k}
; it can be equivalently rewritten as
\mathbb{F}_{p}C_{p^{k}}\rtimes C_{p^{k}}
. By
[
2
, Theorem 8.5]
, it holds that
H^{2}(C_{p^{k}},\mathbb{F}_{p}C_{p^{k}})=0
, and one can prove that the only splitting of
W_{p,k}
as a semidirect product of a normal abelian subgroup and a complement is the one given above, so there is no hope of applying
Lemma

2.5
.

Theorem 5.2
.

For each be prime number
p
and each positive integer
k
, the group
W_{p,k}
is not sectionally indecomposable.

Proof.

Let
p
be a prime number. Define the following groups:

\displaystyle G_{1}=\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{p^{k}+1}\mid\;

\displaystyle f_{1}^{p^{k}}=1,\ f_{i}^{p}=1,\ \forall\,i>1

\displaystyle[f_{2},f_{1}]=f_{3},\ [f_{3},f_{1}]=f_{4},\ \ldots,\ [f_{p^{k}},f_{1}]=f_{p^{k}+1},

\displaystyle[f_{p^{k}+1},f_{1}]=1,

\displaystyle[f_{i},f_{j}]=1,\ \forall\,i>j>1\rangle

\displaystyle G_{2}=\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{p^{k}+1}\mid\;

\displaystyle f_{2}^{p}=f_{p^{k}+1},\ f_{1}^{p^{k}}=1,\ f_{i}^{p}=1\ \text{ for }i\notin\{1,2\},

\displaystyle[f_{2},f_{1}]=f_{3},\ [f_{3},f_{1}]=f_{4},\ \ldots,\ [f_{p^{k}},f_{1}]=f_{p^{k}+1},

\displaystyle[f_{p^{k}+1},f_{1}]=1,

\displaystyle[f_{i},f_{j}]=1,\ \forall\,i>j>1\rangle

and let
X=\langle a\mid a^{p^{2}}=1\rangle\cong C_{p^{2}}
.

In the direct product
G_{2}\times X
, let
H
be the subgroup generated by:

h_{1}=(f_{1},1),\quad h_{2}=(f_{2},a),\quad h_{3}=(f_{3},1),\quad\ldots,\quad h_{p^{k}+1}=(f_{p^{k}+1},1)

and let
K=\langle(f_{2}^{p},\,a^{p})\rangle=\langle(f_{p^{k}+1},\,a^{p})\rangle
.

Our aim is to show the following claims:

(1)

K\cong C_{p}
,

(2)

K\leq Z(H)
,

(3)

H/K\cong G_{1}
.

Observe first that
G_{1}\simeq C_{p}\wr C_{p^{k}}=C_{p}^{p^{k}}\rtimes C_{p^{k}}
and
G_{2}\simeq(C_{p^{2}}\oplus C_{p}^{p^{k}-2})\rtimes C_{p^{k}}
, so
|G_{1}|=|G_{2}|=p^{p^{k}+k}
. Then one observes that
G_{2}
will have no normal abelian subgroups of index
p^{k}
of exponent
p
, so
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
will not be isomorphic. Claim (1) and Claim (2) follow directly upon inspection of the presentation of
G_{2}
.

To prove Claim (3), we proceed as follows. First, the order of
H
is
p^{p^{k}+k+1}
; indeed, on one hand,
H
is a proper subgroup of
G_{2}\times X
, which is of order
p^{p^{k}+k+2}
, and on the other, we have
|H|>|G_{2}|=p^{p^{k}+k}
, so the assertion holds. Second, let
\bar{h}_{i}
be the images of the
h_{i}
in
H/K
. We want to check that the relations in the presentation of
G_{1}
hold for the
\bar{h}_{i}
. All but one of the relations are straightforward consequences of the relations of
G_{2}
, which we highlight below. One has

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{2}^{p}=\overline{(f_{2},a)^{p}}=\overline{(f_{2}^{p},a^{p})}

which is the identity in
H/K
. Thus, we get a surjective homomorphism
G_{1}\to H/K
given by
f_{i}\mapsto\bar{h}_{i}
, which is an isomorphism in light of the fact that
|H|/|K|=p^{p^{k}+k+1}/p=|G_{1}|
.
∎

All the examples of non-sectionally indecomposable
p
-groups given above follow a similar blueprint: we find another group with equal order and a “similar” presentation, we take the direct product with a convenient cyclic group and then we quotient by a cyclic central subgroup. We suspect that there does
not
exist a non-cyclic monolithic sectionally indecomposable
p
-group, but we do not know how to generalise the above mentioned strategy to any
p
-group.

Question 5.3
.

Do non-cyclic monolithic sectionally indecomposable
p
-groups exist?

5.3.
Infinite groups

We briefly discuss the notion of sectional indecomposability for infinite groups. The following gives a plethora of examples of sectional indecomposability.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G
be a infinite group.

(i)

If
G
is has a non-abelian minimal normal subgroup and its centraliser in
G
is trivial, then
G
is sectionally indecomposable.

(ii)

If
G
is countable and contains a non-abelian free subgroup, then
G
is sectionally indecomposable.

Proof.

For (i), if
G
is contains a unique non-abelian minimal normal subgroup
N
with
C_{G}(N)=1
, then we can simply mimic the proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

For (ii), first let
F
be a non-abelian free group and suppose
F\leq_{s}A\times B
. Since
F
is projective, it follows that
F
is a subgroup of
A\times B
, thus we may assume that
A
and
B
are quotients of
F
. Consider the projections
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
of
F
onto
A
and
B
, respectively. If
\ker\pi_{1}
and
\ker\pi_{2}
were both non-trivial, this would mean one can find two commuting elements in
F
that generate a free abelian group of rank 2, a contradiction. So either
A
or
B
is isomorphic to
F
.

Second, suppose a countable group
G
contains a non-abelian free subgroup
F
and suppose
G\leq_{s}L\times K
. This implies that
F\leq_{s}L\times K
, so by the previous paragraph we may assume without loss of generality that
F\leq L
. The group
[F,F]
is free on countably many generators, so it admits
G
as an epimorphic image. It then follows that
G\leq_{s}L
.
∎

For instance, it follows that Thompson’s groups
F
,
T
and
V
are sectionally indecomposable. Indeed,
T
and
V
are simple, and
[F,F]
is a simple, normal subgroup of
F
which is contained in every other non-trivial normal subgroup of
F
and such that
C_{F}([F,F])=1
.

In contrast with the finite case, where the only sectionally indecomposable abelian groups are the cyclic ones, there do exist infinite abelian groups which are sectionally indecomposable. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}
be the
p
-Prüfer group for a prime
p
.

Proposition 5.5
.

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}
is sectionally indecomposable.

Proof.

For (i), assume
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}\leq_{s}Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
. So there exist
K\unlhd H\leq Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
with
H/K\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}
. Let
\rho

be the restriction to
H
of the projection
\pi_{1}:Y_{1}\times Y_{2}\to Y_{1}.
Consider the normal subgroup
N=K\ker\rho
of
H
. The factor group
H/N
is an epimorphic image of
H/K\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}
so we have only two possibilities:

(1)

H/N\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}.
In this case
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}
is an epimorphic images of
H/\ker\rho\leq Y_{1},
so
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}\leq_{s}Y_{1}.

(2)

H=N.
In this case
H/K=K\ker\rho/K\cong\ker\rho/(K\cap\ker\rho).
Since
\ker\rho\leq Y_{2},
we conclude
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}\leq_{s}Y_{2}.
∎

As far as divisible groups go, the Prüfer groups are the only sectionally indecomposable ones. For a directly indecomposable divisible group
A
, if it is not isomorphic to the a Prüfer group, it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}
, which is an epimorphic image of
\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}\times\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
via the map
(x,y)\mapsto x+y
, where the first factor is the additive group of the localisation of
\mathbb{Z}
on the prime ideal
(2)
and the second factor is the additive group of the subring of
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
1/2
. More generally, if
\pi
is any set of primes with
|\pi|\geq 2
, a similar argument shows that the additive subgroup of the subring of
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
1/p
for
p\in\pi
is not sectionally indecomposable. However, when
|\pi|=1
, we do not know if sectional indecomposability holds or not.

We also provide virtually abelian examples. Let
D_{p^{\infty}}
denote the semidirect product
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}\rtimes C_{2}
, where
C_{2}
acts by inversion.

Proposition 5.6
.

D_{p^{\infty}}
is sectionally indecomposable if and only if
p
is odd.

Proof.

First, assume that
p
is odd and
D_{p^{\infty}}\leq_{s}Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
. As such, there exist
K\unlhd H\leq Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
with
H/K\cong D_{p^{\infty}}
. We use the same notation as in the proof of
Proposition

5.5
and we recall that
\ker\rho=H\cap Y_{2}
. We have three possibilities for the factor group
H/N
:
H=N,

H/N\cong D_{p^{\infty}},

H/N\cong C_{2}.
In the first two cases we can conclude, as in
Proposition

5.5
, that either
D_{p^{\infty}}\leq_{s}Y_{1}
or
D_{p^{\infty}}\leq_{s}Y_{2}.
Assume
H/N\cong C_{2}.

In this case
N/K\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}
and
there exists
h=(y_{1},y_{2})\in H
such that
n^{h}\equiv n^{-1}\pmod{K}
for every
n\in N.
Since
N/K=K\ker\rho/K\cong_{H}\ker\rho/(K\cap\ker\rho)
the element
y_{2}
acts on
\ker\rho
as the inversion modulo
K\cap\ker\rho.
Consider
X=\langle y_{2},\ker\rho\rangle\leq Y_{2}
, let
\overline{X}=X/(K\cap\ker\rho)

and
x=y_{2}(K\cap\ker\rho)\in\overline{X}.
Notice that
\overline{X}=A\langle x\rangle,
where
A=\ker\rho/(K\cap\ker\rho)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}

and
a^{x}=-a
for every
a\in A.
Let
Z=\langle x^{2}\rangle.
If
Z
is infinite, then
Z\cap A=1,

and
X/Z\cong D_{p^{\infty}}.
Otherwise
|Z\cap A|=p^{t}
for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
|x|=2p^{t}
, so
X=A\langle x^{p^{t}}\rangle\cong D_{p^{\infty}}.

If
p=2,
we may consider the direct limit
Q_{2^{\infty}}
of the direct system of the quaternion groups
Q_{2^{n}}
. Indeed
Q_{2^{\infty}}=A\langle x\rangle,
where
A\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2^{\infty}},

a^{x}=-a
for every
a\in A
and
x^{2}
coincides with the unique element of order 2 in
A.
By the same argument used to see that
D_{2^{n}}\leq_{s}Q_{2^{n}}\times Q_{2^{n}},
we can deduce that
D_{2^{\infty}}\leq_{s}Q_{2^{\infty}}\times Q_{2^{\infty}}.

∎

A last remark on the non-abelian case is that there are many interesting test cases which are not covered by any of the above results. For instance, periodic branch groups, which are far from being simple since they are residually finite and do not contain non-abelian free subgroups. However, it is not clear if there should be any sort of general criterion to prove or disprove sectional indecomposability for them, so we do not explore this direction in this work.
