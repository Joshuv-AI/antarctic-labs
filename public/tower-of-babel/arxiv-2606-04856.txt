Spectral bounds for distance coloring and packing parameters of graphs via semidefinite programming
Aida Abiad, Yue Yang, Jiang Zhou

Abstract
Using methods from spectral graph theory and semidefinite programming, we obtain sharp spectral bounds for several graph parameters related to distance colorings and packing, including the injective chromatic number, the open packing number, the injective chromatic index, and the strong chromatic index. The new spectral bounds improve several existing combinatorial bounds. Furthermore, we apply the obtained eigenvalue bounds on the first two parameters to estimate the code covering number and the open packing number of hypercubes, obtaining new exact values and strengthened bounds regarding existing literature results. The obtained results illustrate the power of combining spectral and semidefinite programming tools for tackling coloring and packing problems in graph theory and coding theory. Keywords: Graphs, Eigenvalues; Semidefinite programming; Injective chromatic number; Open packing number; Injective chromatic index; Strong chromatic index; Code covering number AMS classification: 05C50, 05C69, 05C15, 15A18

Introduction

The interplay between structural properties of graphs and the spectra of their associated matrices is a central theme in discrete mathematics, and one that has driven much of the development of spectral graph theory. Classical work in the area has focused on fundamental graph invariants such as the independence number and the chromatic number, following the seminal work of Hoffman and Lovász, among others. In recent years, however, more refined colouring and packing parameters have been introduced and investigated in the literature.

In this work we investigate the following graph parameters. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with
|V|=n
vertices and
|E|=m
edges.

•

In an
injective coloring
, for every vertex
v\in V
, all the neighbors of
v
are assigned with distinct colors. The
injective chromatic number

\chi_{i}(G)
is the minimum number of colors needed for an injective coloring.

•

An
open packing
of
G
is a vertex subset
S
if any two vertices in
S
do not have a common neighbor in
G
. The
open packing number

\rho^{o}(G)
is the maximum cardinality among all open packings of
G
.

•

An edge coloring of
G
is an
injective edge coloring
if for any two distinct edges
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, the colors of
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are distinct if they are at distance
1
in
G
or in a common triangle. The
injective chromatic index

\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)
is the minimum number of colors needed for an injective edge coloring of
G
.

•

A
strong edge coloring
of
G
is an assignment of colors to the edges of
G
such that two distinct edges are colored differently if their distance is at most
1
. The
strong chromatic index

\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)
is the smallest number of colors in any strong edge coloring.

These four parameters exhibit quite a different behavior from their classical counterparts. Thus, specific tools to estimate them have been proposed in the literature – for a survey paper on graph coloring parameters, we refer the reader to
[
19
]
. The above parameters have become important in modeling constraints arising in areas such as communication networks (where interference constraints extend to vertices at distance two), scheduling, steganography and coding theory (where distance constraints on codes translate into colouring and packing problems on associated graphs). These more refined parameters can be viewed as instances of coloring problems on graph powers or line graphs, modeling constraints that propagate beyond immediate adjacency.

Despite their relevance for multiple applications, the above parameters are computationally intractable (see e.g.
[
29
,
47
,
39
,
25
,
43
]
), making the development of sharp and easily computable bounds essential; this motivates the search for eigenvalue bounds. Existing bounds for quantities such as
\chi_{i}(G)
and
\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)
are typically expressed in terms of local parameters like the maximum degree
\Delta
, order, or size
[
10
,
29
,
41
]
, and therefore often fail to capture the global graph symmetries and regularity (which are encoded in the graph spectrum).

Motivated by the above, in this paper we derive eigenvalue bounds for the aforementioned parameters by combining techniques from spectral graph theory and positive semidefinite programming. In particular, we encode combinatorial distance constraints as semidefinite conditions, allowing us to optimise over suitably structured matrix families rather than only the adjacency spectrum. We also show that our bounds are tight and can outperform the following known results:

•

Lower bound on the
injective chromatic number
that can improve a known result by Brešar, Samadi and Yero
[
10
, Theorem 6]
. As an application to hypercubes of our spectral bound for the injective chromatic number (in particular, to estimate the code covering number in error correction), we obtain new sufficient and necessary conditions for its exact value, and new lower bounds, extending the results by Hahn, Kratochvıíl, Širáň and Sotteau
[
29
]
.

•

Upper bounds on the
open packing number
of hypercubes that, when applied to hypercubes, improves upon
previous results by Brešar et al.
[
11
]
and Brimkov et al.
[
12
, Corollary 3.12]
.

•

Extension of a lower bound on the
injective chromatic index
by Kostochka et al.
[
35

which holds for
d
-regular bipartite graphs to general graphs. For some graphs, our lower bounds are better than the bound of Cardoso et al.
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
.

•

Lower bound on the
strong chromatic index
that can improve a known result by Abiad and Reijnders
[
2
, Corollary 9]
.

Preliminaries

Let

. The maximum and minimum degrees in

of

Lemma 2.1

(Hoffman bound)
.

[
34

Let

and
\lambda_{n}
be the maximum and minimum eigenvalues of

, respectively. Then

\displaystyle\chi(G)\geq 1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{n}}.

A coloring that meets the Hoffman bound above is called a

.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
8
, Proposition 2.3]

If
G
is an regular graph with a Hoffman coloring, then all color classes of the Hoffman coloring have equal size.

The

of

Lemma 2.3

.

[

G
be an

-vertex graph with minimum degree

\displaystyle\alpha(G)\leq n\frac{-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{n}}{\delta^{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{n}}.

.

[

Let

n

\displaystyle nd_{1}-n_{1}d\geq(n-n_{1})\lambda_{n}.

.

3
Injective chromatic number

Injective colorings were introduced by Hahn, Kratochvıíl, Širáň and Sotteau in
[
29
]
, in which they proved the inequality
\Delta\leq\chi_{i}(G)\leq\Delta^{2}-\Delta+1
, where
\Delta
is the maximum degree of
G
. In the same paper the authors showed applications of the injective chromatic number of the hypercube in the theory of error-correcting codes. Injective colorings have received a lot of attention in the literature since their introduction, see e.g.
[
16
,
21
,
17
,
24
,
31
,
36
,
18
]
.

Note that an injective colouring is not necessarily a proper colouring, i.e., it is possible for two adjacent vertices to receive the same colour. Also, observe that

\chi(G)\leq\chi_{i}(G)\leq\chi(G^{2})
where
G^{2}
is the
square power
of
G
(a graph with the same vertex set as
G
and where two vertices are adjacent if they are at distance at most
2
in
G
).

The relation between
\chi_{i}(G)
and
\Delta(G)
has been widely investigated, see e.g.
[
14
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
29
,
44
]
. Doyon, Hahn and Raspaud
[
21
]
established an interesting relation between the injective chromatic number and the maximum average degree of graphs. Also the injective chromatic number of some graph operations (including direct product, Cartesian product and lexicographic product) have been studied in
[
46
,
48
]
.

In
[
10
]
the following lower bound for
\chi_{i}(G)
was obtained in terms of the order, size and open packing number of
G
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
10
, Theorem 6]

If
G
is a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
and size
m
, then

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{2m-n}{\rho^{o}(G)}}.

We are now ready to derive the first spectral bound for
\chi_{i}(G)
, which later we will also use to estimate
\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph without isolated vertices, and let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{G}
be a matrix associated with
G
such that all the row vectors of
A
are unit vectors and
AA^{\top}\neq I
. Then

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(G)\geq\sup_{k}\sqrt[k]{\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})}\geq\inf_{k}\sqrt[k]{\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}}{1-\lambda_{n}},

where
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}(AA^{\top})
,
\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{n}(AA^{\top})
.

Proof.

Since
AA^{\top}-\lambda_{n}I
is positive semidefinite,
AA^{\top}\neq I
and
(AA^{\top})_{uu}=1
for all
u
, we have
1-\lambda_{n}>0
. Let
M=(1-\lambda_{n})^{-1}(AA^{\top}-\lambda_{n}I)
. Then
M
is positive semidefinite and
(M)_{uu}=1
for all
u
. From the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{G}
, we have

\displaystyle(M)_{uv}=(1-\lambda_{n})^{-1}(AA^{\top})_{uv}=0

when
u,v
are two distinct vertices satisfying
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)=\emptyset
.

According to Lemma
2.6
, we get

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(G)\geq\sup_{k}\sqrt[k]{\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})}.

So we only need to prove that
\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})\geq(\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}}{1-\lambda_{n}})^{k}
for any positive integer
k
. Let
H_{k}
be the two-step graph of the direct product graph
G^{\otimes k}
. Using the sandwich inequality, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})=\chi(H_{k})\geq\vartheta(\overline{H_{k}}).

If two distinct vertices
u=u_{1}\cdots u_{k}
and
v=v_{1}\cdots v_{k}
are nonadjacent in the two-step graph
H_{k}
, then there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that
N_{G}(u_{i})\cap N_{G}(v_{i})=\emptyset
in
G
. In this case, we have
(M)_{u_{i}v_{i}}=0
and

\displaystyle(M^{\otimes k})_{uv}=(M)_{u_{1}v_{1}}\cdots(M)_{u_{k}v_{k}}=0.

Since
M
is a positive semidefinite matrix and
(M)_{uu}=1
for all
u\in V(G)
,
M^{\otimes k}
is also positive semidefinite matrix and each diagonal entry of
M^{\otimes k}
is
1
. By Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})\geq\vartheta(\overline{H_{k}})\geq\lambda_{1}(M^{\otimes k})=\lambda_{1}(M)^{k}=\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}}{1-\lambda_{n}}\right)^{k},

which implies

\displaystyle\inf_{k}\sqrt[k]{\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}}{1-\lambda_{n}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(G)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{k}\sqrt[k]{\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})}\geq\inf_{k}\sqrt[k]{\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}}{1-\lambda_{n}}.

∎

As a direct application of Theorem
3.2
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

For an
n
-vertex graph
G
without isolated vertices, let
A=D_{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A_{G}
, where

is the diagonal degree matrix of

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(G)\geq\sup_{k}\sqrt[k]{\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})}\geq\inf_{k}\sqrt[k]{\chi_{i}(G^{\otimes k})}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}}{1-\lambda_{n}},

where
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}(D_{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A_{G}^{2}D_{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})
,
\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{n}(D_{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A_{G}^{2}D_{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})
.

4
Open packing number

Injective colorings of graphs are related to the so-called open packings, cf.
[
42
]
More precisely, an injective coloring of a graph is exactly a partition of its vertex set into open packings. A vertex subset
S\subseteq V(G)
is called an
open packing
of
G
if any two vertices in
S
do not have a common neighbor in
G
, that is,
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)=\emptyset
for any distinct vertices
u,v\in S
. As mentioned, there is a one-to-one correspondence between injective
k
-colorings of
G
and
k
-partitions of
V(G)
into
k
open packings. So the injective chromatic number
\chi_{i}(G)
is the minimum
k
such that
G
has a partition
V(G)=S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{k}
, where
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
are open packings of
G
. The
open packing number
of
G
, introduced in
[
33
]
and denoted
\rho^{o}(G)
, is the maximum cardinality among all open packings of
G
. Previous work on the open packing number can be found, for instance, in
[
30
,
32
,
45
,
11
]
.

We start this section by showing an upper bound for the open packing number in terms of the eigenvalues of an associated matrix to a graph.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{G}
be a matrix associated with a graph
G
, and let
\lambda
be a positive eigenvalue of
A
with a positive unit eigenvector
x
. Then, the open packing number of
G
satisfies

\displaystyle\rho^{o}(G)\leq\lambda^{-2}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}},

with equality if and only if there is a constant
c
and there exists an open packing
S
such that

cx_{u}=\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)\cap S}(A)_{vu}x_{v}^{-1}~~~~~~(u\in V(G))

(4.1)

and

\frac{(AA^{\top})_{vv}}{x^{2}_{v}}=\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x^{2}_{u}}

(4.2)

for all
v\in S
.

Proof.

By
Ax=\lambda x
and
\lambda>0
, we obtain
x=\lambda^{-1}Ax
. For any open packing
S
of
G
, let
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})^{\top}
be the vector such that

\displaystyle y_{u}=\begin{cases}x_{u}^{-1}~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\in S,\\
0~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\notin S.\end{cases}

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle|S|^{2}=(y^{\top}x)^{2}=(\lambda^{-1}y^{\top}Ax)^{2}\leq\lambda^{-2}y^{\top}AA^{\top}y.

For any two distinct vertices
u,v
in
S
, we have
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)=\emptyset
. So
(AA^{\top})_{uv}=0
if
u,v\in S
and
u\neq v
. Then we get

\displaystyle|S|^{2}\leq\lambda^{-2}y^{\top}AA^{\top}y=\lambda^{-2}\sum_{u\in S}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}.

To show the equality characterization, suppose that
S
is a maximum open packing of
G
. Then
\rho^{o}(G)=|S|
and

\displaystyle\rho^{o}(G)\leq\lambda^{-2}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}},

with equality if and only if there is a constant
c
such that
A^{\top}y=cx
and

\frac{(AA^{\top})_{vv}}{x^{2}_{v}}=\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x^{2}_{u}}

for all
v\in S
.
∎

If
A\in\mathcal{A}_{G}
is the adjacency matrix of a graph
G
, then we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\lambda
be the largest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix
A_{G}
with a positive unit eigenvector
x
, and let
d_{u}
be the degree of vertex
u
. Then

\rho^{o}(G)\leq\lambda^{-2}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{d_{u}}{x_{u}^{2}},

with equality if and only if there exists an open packing
S
that satisfies conditions (4.1) and
\frac{d_{v}}{x^{2}_{v}}=\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{d_{u}}{x^{2}_{u}}
for all
v\in S
.

Proof.

We know
(AA^{\top})_{uu}=d_{u}
. By Theorem
4.1
, we get

\rho^{o}(G)\leq\lambda^{-2}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{d_{u}}{x_{u}^{2}},

with equality if and only if there exists an open packing
S
that satisfies conditions (4.1) and
\frac{d_{v}}{x^{2}_{v}}=\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{d_{u}}{x^{2}_{u}}
for all
v\in S
.
∎

Using Theorem
4.1
, next we show spectral upper bounds for the open packing number of the direct product graph
G^{\otimes k}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{G}
be a matrix associated with a graph
G
, and let
\lambda
be a positive eigenvalue of
A
with a positive unit eigenvector
x
. Then

\rho^{o}(G)\leq\sup_{k}\sqrt[k]{\rho^{o}(G^{\otimes k})}\leq\lambda^{-2}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}.

Proof.

Since
A^{\otimes k}\in\mathcal{A}_{G^{\otimes k}}
is the matrix associated with the direct product graph
G^{\otimes k}
. Based on the characteristic equation
Ax=\lambda x
, we have

A^{\otimes k}x^{\otimes k}=\lambda^{k}x^{\otimes k}.

So
x^{\otimes k}
is the positive unit eigenvector of matrix
A^{\otimes k}
associated with the positive eigenvalue
\lambda^{k}
.
Now using Theorem
4.1
, we have

\rho^{o}(G^{\otimes k})\leq\lambda^{-2k}\bigg(\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}\bigg)^{k}.

Next, suppose that
S
is the maximum open packing of
G
such that
|S|=\rho^{o}(G)
. Then
S^{k}=\{u_{1}\cdots u_{k}:u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in S\}
is an open packing of direct product graph
G^{\otimes k}
. So we have
{\rho^{o}(G)}^{k}\leq\rho^{o}(G^{\otimes k})
.
Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\rho^{o}(G)\leq\sup_{k}\sqrt[k]{\rho^{o}(G^{\otimes k})}\leq\lambda^{-2}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}.

∎

Below we present a family of direct product graphs attaining the bound in Theorem
4.3
.

Example 4.4
.

For a complete bipartite graph
G=K_{m,n}(m\leq n)
, we know that
\rho^{o}(G)=2
. Let
A=A_{G}
be the adjacency matrix of graph
G
, and let
\lambda=\sqrt{mn}
be the largest eigenvalue of
A
with a positive unit eigenvector
x
. Then
x_{u}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m}}
if
u
is a vertex belongs to the color class of size
m
, and
x_{u}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}
if
u
is a vertex belongs to the color class of size
n
. Then

\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}=2mn

and

\rho^{o}(G)=\lambda^{-2}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(AA^{\top})_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}=2.

By Theorem
4.3
, we have

\rho^{o}(G^{\otimes k})=\rho^{o}(G)^{k}=\bigg(\lambda^{-2}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{d_{u}}{x_{u}^{2}}\bigg)^{k}=2^{k}

for any positive integer
k
.

5
Injective chromatic index

Injective edge colorings were introduced in
[
15
]
motivated by the socalled Packet Radio Network problem. The injective chromatic index is also called induced star arboricity in
[
4
]
. Bounds for
\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)
have been obtained in the literature, see e.g.
[
9
,
15
,
35
]
.

For an induced subgraph
H
of a graph
G
, we say that
H
is an
induced star forest
if each component of
H
is a star. To derive our bounds in this section, we will use the following known result.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
15
, Proposition 2.2]

Let
G
be a graph. Then
\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)=k
if and only if
k
is the minimum positive integer for which
E(G)
can be partitioned into non-empty subsets
E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}
such that
E_{i}
induces an induced star forest for every
i
.

In
[
35
, Proposition 8]
, Kostochka et al. proved that
\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)\geq d
when
G
is a
d
-regular bipartite graph. For a
d
-regular bipartite graph
G
, the minimum eigenvalue of
A_{G}
is
-d
. We extend
[
35
, Proposition 8]
as follows.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
m\geq 1
edges and minimum degree
\delta
, and let
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
be eigenvalues of
A_{G}
. Then

\displaystyle\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)\geq\frac{m(\delta^{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{n})}{-n\lambda_{1}\lambda_{n}}.

Moreover, if
G
is
d
-regular, then

\displaystyle\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)\geq\frac{d(d-\lambda_{n})}{-2\lambda_{n}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, we know that there exists a partition
E(G)=E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{k}
such that
k=\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)
and
E_{i}
induces an induced star forest for every
i
. Hence
\max_{1\leq i\leq k}|E_{i}|\leq\alpha(G)
, where
\alpha(G)
is independence number of
G
. Then

\displaystyle\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)\alpha(G)\geq m.

By Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)\geq\frac{m(\delta^{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{n})}{-n\lambda_{1}\lambda_{n}}.

If
G
is
d
-regular, then
\lambda_{1}=d
,
m=nd/2
and

\displaystyle\frac{m(\delta^{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{n})}{-n\lambda_{1}\lambda_{n}}=\frac{d(d-\lambda_{n})}{-2\lambda_{n}}.

∎

Next we show a class of graphs attaining the bound in Theorem
5.2
.

Example 5.3
.

Let
G=K_{n,\ldots,n}
be the complete
p
-partite graph with
pn
vertices. Then
G
is regular of degree
(p-1)n
and the minimum eigenvalue of
A_{G}
is
-n
. By Theorem
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)\geq\frac{p(p-1)n}{2}.

Since
E(G)
can be partitioned into
\frac{p(p-1)n}{2}
induced star forests, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)=\frac{p(p-1)n}{2}.

In
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
the following lower bound on the injective chromatic index of
G
in terms of clique number
\omega(G)
was obtained. Later, we will compare it with Theorem
5.2
.

Theorem 5.4
.

[
15
, Proposition 3.1]

For any connected graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)\geq\frac{\omega(G)(\omega(G)-1)}{2}.

In Table
3
in the Appendix we compare Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
5.4
(
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
) for some Sagemath named graphs and for some graph classes. The computational experiments show that our spectral bound on the injective chromatic index is also tight, and that for some graphs, our bound is better than
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
.

6
Strong chromatic index

Given a graph
G
, the square of the line graph is denoted
L(G)^{2}
. Alternatively, the strong chromatic index of
G
is the chromatic number of
L(G)^{2}
, and according to (
1
), we have
\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)=\chi{(L(G)^{2})}\geq\chi_{i}(L(G))
. Also, note that from the definitions, it holds
\chi_{i}^{\prime}(G)\leq\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)
for any graph
G
, where
\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)
is strong chromatic index of
G
.

A trivial upper bound of the strong chromatic index is
\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)\leq 2\Delta^{2}-2\Delta+1
. More refined upper bounds on the strong chromatic index have also been obtained in the literature, see e.g.
[
23
,
41
,
7
]
. Erdős and Nešetřil
[
23
]
posed the following conjecture on the parameter of interest.

Conjecture 6.1
.

[
23
, Section1]

\begin{cases}\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)\leq\frac{5}{4}\Delta^{2},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~if~\Delta~is~even,\\
\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)\leq\frac{5\Delta^{2}-2\Delta+1}{4},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~if~\Delta~is~odd.\end{cases}

The conjecture above has triggered quite some work, see e.g.
[
7
,
3
,
27
,
38
]
.

Most existing results on the strong chromatic index consist of upper bounds. In
[
2
, Corollary 9]
, a spectral lower bound for the strong chromatic index of a
k
-regular graph was shown using the adjacency eigenvalues of the line graph
L(G)
.

Corollary 6.2
.

[
2
, Corollary 9]

Let
G
be a
k
-regular graph. Let
2(k-1)=\theta^{\prime}_{0}>\cdots>\theta^{\prime}_{d^{\prime}}
be
the distinct adjacency eigenvalues of
L(G)
, with
d^{\prime}\geq 2
. Let
\theta^{\prime}_{i}
be the largest eigenvalue such that
\theta^{\prime}_{i}\leq-1
. Then

\displaystyle\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)\geq\frac{\mid E\mid}{\big\lfloor\mid E\mid\frac{\theta^{\prime}_{0}+\theta^{\prime}_{i}\theta^{\prime}_{i-1}}{(\theta^{\prime}_{0}-\theta^{\prime}_{i})(\theta^{\prime}_{0}-\theta^{\prime}_{i-1})}\big\rfloor}.

In this section we show two alternative spectral lower bounds on the strong chromatic index. Furthermore, we give some graph classes for which these bounds are tight.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
m
edges and adjacency eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)\geq\frac{m}{\min\{|\{i:\lambda_{i}\geq 1\}|,|\{i:\lambda_{i}\leq-1\}|\}}.

Proof.

There exists a partition
E(G)=E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{k}
such that
k=\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)
and each
E_{i}
is an induced matching of
G
. By the Cauchy interlacing theorem, we have

\displaystyle|E_{i}|\leq\min\{|\{i:\lambda_{i}\geq 1\}|,|\{i:\lambda_{i}\leq-1\}|\}

for
i=1,\ldots,k
. Hence

\displaystyle m=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|E_{i}|\leq k\min\{|\{i:\lambda_{i}\geq 1\}|,|\{i:\lambda_{i}\leq-1\}|\}.

∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph with adjacency eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)\geq\frac{nd}{2\left\lfloor\frac{n(1-\lambda_{n})}{2(d-\lambda_{n})}\right\rfloor}\geq\frac{d(d-\lambda_{n})}{1-\lambda_{n}}.

Proof.

There exists a partition
E(G)=E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{k}
such that
k=\chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)
and each
E_{i}
is an induced matching of
G
. Let
r=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}|E_{i}|
. Then
k\geq\frac{nd}{2r}
and
G
has a
1
-regular induced subgraph with
2r
vertices. By Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle n-2rd

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(n-2r)\lambda_{n},

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{n(1-\lambda_{n})}{2(d-\lambda_{n})}\right\rfloor.

Hence

\displaystyle k\geq\frac{nd}{2r}\geq\frac{nd}{2\left\lfloor\frac{n(1-\lambda_{n})}{2(d-\lambda_{n})}\right\rfloor}\geq\frac{d(d-\lambda_{n})}{1-\lambda_{n}}.

∎

It is easy to see that for complete graphs and complete bipartite graphs, the bound in Theorem
6.3
is tight. For complete graphs, the bound in Theorem
6.4
is also achieved.

In Table
4
we compare Conjecture
6.1
(
[

), Theorem
6.3
, Theorem
6.4
and Corollary
6.2
(
[

) for the Sagemath named graphs. The computational results show that our spectral lower bounds on the strong chromatic index can be tight and, in some specific instances, can bring improvements upon the spectral lower bound from

.

7
Applications

Hypercubes appear throughout theoretical computer science and combinatorics for a variety of reasons, making their structure an essential subject of study. Despite their seemingly simple form, they often give rise to surprisingly complex and challenging problems; we will see two of them in this section.

7.1
The code covering number of hypercubes

In coding theory, there is an interest in the problem of determining the injective
chromatic number of the
n
-dimensional hypercube, see e.g.
[
6
,
22
,
40
]
. Recall that the
n
-dimensional cube

Q_{n}
is the graph defined on the vertex set
\{0,1\}
by
[a,b]\in E(Q_{n})
if and only if
a
and
b
differ in exactly one coordinate. A (binary)
code
of length
n
is an arbitrary subset
S
of vertices of the
n
-cube
Q_{n}
. The code
S
is
single-error-correcting
if the Hamming distance of any two distinct vertices of
S
is at least
3
. The
code covering number
of the
n
-cube, denoted
\gamma(Q_{n})
, is the minimum number
t
of single-error-correcting codes
S_{1},\dots,S_{t}
such that
V(Q_{n})=S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{t}
.

Let us first give a small overview of the some known results on the code covering number of the
n
-cube, obtained by Hahn, Kratochvıíl, Širáň and Sotteau
[
29
]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

[
29
]

\gamma(Q_{n})=\chi_{i}(Q_{n+1})
.

Lemma 7.2
.

[
29
]

\chi_{i}(Q_{n})=n
if and only if
n=2^{r}
for some integer
r
.

Lemma 7.3
.

[
29
]

\chi_{i}(Q_{2^{r}-j})=2^{r}
for
0\leq j\leq 3
.

Lemma 7.4
.

[
29
]

\chi_{i}(Q_{2n+1})\leq 2\chi_{i}(Q_{n+1})
.

Thus, for any positive integer
n
, it is known that
\chi_{i}(Q_{n})\geq n
, with equality if and only if
n=2^{r}
for some integer
r
(see Lemma
7.2
). For an odd integer
n>1
, we prove a sufficient and necessary conditions for
\chi_{i}(Q_{n})=n+1
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
n>1
be an odd integer. Then
\chi_{i}(Q_{n})\geq n+1
, with equality if and only if
n=2^{r}-1
for some integer
r
.

Proof.

Recall that
\chi_{i}(Q_{n})
equals to the chromatic number
\chi(Q_{n}^{(2)})
of the two-step graph
Q_{n}^{(2)}
. Notice that there are no triangles in
Q_{n}
, and any two vertices with distance
2
in
Q_{n}
have exactly
2
common neighbors. So the adjacency matrices of
Q_{n}
and
Q_{n}^{(2)}
satisfy

\displaystyle A_{Q_{n}^{(2)}}=\frac{1}{2}(A_{Q_{n}}^{2}-nI).

Since
n
is odd, the minimum eigenvalue of
A_{Q_{n}}^{2}
is
1
. Then
Q_{n}^{(2)}
is an regular graph with degree
\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}
, and the minimum adjacency eigenvalue of
Q_{n}^{(2)}
is
\frac{1-n}{2}
. By Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(Q_{n})=\chi(Q_{n}^{(2)})\geq 1+\frac{n^{2}-n}{n-1}=n+1.

If
\chi_{i}(Q_{n})=\chi(Q_{n}^{(2)})=n+1
, then by Lemma
2.2
, we know that
Q_{n}^{(2)}
has a proper vertex coloring such that all color classes have equal size. In this case,
2^{n}
is divisible by
n+1
, i.e.,
n=2^{r}-1
for some integer
r
. Conversely, if
n=2^{r}-1
for some integer
r
, then by Lemma
7.3
, we have
\chi_{i}(Q_{n})=n+1
.
∎

Theorem 7.6
.

Suppose that
n=2^{r}-j
for
r\geq 3
and
3\leq j\leq 4
. Then

\displaystyle 2n+3\leq\chi_{i}(Q_{2n+1})\leq 2n+2j.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.4
and Lemma
7.3
, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(Q_{2n+1})\leq 2\chi_{i}(Q_{n+1})=2^{r+1}=2n+2j.

Since
2n+1=2^{r+1}-(2j-1)
, using Theorem
7.5
we obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(Q_{2n+1})\geq 2n+3.

∎

Now we are ready to show the following result concerning the code covering number of the
n
-cube
\gamma(Q_{n})
, which provides sufficient and necessary conditions for
\gamma(Q_{n})=n+2
, and new lower bounds for the case of
n=2^{r}-j
(
r\geq 3
,
3\leq j\leq 4
).

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
Q_{n}
denote the
n
-dimensional cube.

(i)

If
n
is odd, then

\gamma(Q_{n})\geq n+1
, with equality if and only if
n=2^{r}-1
for some integer
r
.

(ii)

If
n
is even, then

\gamma(Q_{n})\geq n+2
, with equality if and only if
n=2^{r}-2
for some integer
r
.

(iii)

If
n=2^{r}-j
for
r\geq 3
and
3\leq j\leq 4
, then

\displaystyle 2n+3\leq\gamma(Q_{2n})\leq 2n+2j.

Proof.

For a graph
G
, let
\lambda(G)
denote the minimum absolute value among all eigenvalues of
A_{G}
.

(i)

If
n
is odd, then
\lambda(Q_{n+1})=0
. By Lemma
7.1
and Corollary
3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma(Q_{n})=\chi_{i}(Q_{n+1})\geq\frac{(n+1)^{2}-0}{(n+1)-0}=n+1.

Lemma
7.2
implies that the equality holds if and only if
n+1=2^{r}
for some integer
r
.

(ii)

If
n
is even, then
\lambda(Q_{n+1})=1
. By Lemma
7.1
and Corollary
3.6
, we get

\displaystyle\gamma(Q_{n})=\chi_{i}(Q_{n+1})\geq\frac{(n+1)^{2}-1}{(n+1)-1}=n+2.

Theorem
7.5
implies that the equality holds if and only if
n+1=2^{r}-1
for some integer
r
.

(iii)

According to Lemma
7.1
and Theorem
7.6
, we have

\displaystyle 2n+3\leq\gamma(Q_{2n})=\chi_{i}(Q_{2n+1})\leq 2n+2j.

∎

7.2
The open packing number of bipartite prisms

Recall that, given two graphs
G
and
H
, their
Cartesian product

G\Box H
is the graph with vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
, and two vertices
(u_{1},v_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2})
are adjacent in
G\Box H
if and only if
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\in E(G),v_{1}=v_{2}
or
u_{1}=u_{2},\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in E(H)
. The
square power
of a graph
G
, is the graph with the same vertex set as
G
, and where two edges are adjacent if they are at distance at most
2
in
G
. Let
\alpha_{2}(G)
denote the independence number of the square power of
G
.

In this section we consider a generalization of hypercube graphs. The
bipartite prism
of a bipartite graph
G
is defined as
G\Box K_{2}
, that is, the Cartesian product of the bipartite graph
G
with the complete graph on 2 vertices. Since
Q_{n}=Q_{n-1}\Box K_{2}
, the hypercube
Q_{n}
is a special bipartite prism.

The goal is to investigate the open packing number of bipartite prisms, with a particular focus on hypercubes. In this direction, Brešar et al.
[
11
]
showed the following results.

Theorem 7.8
.

[
11
, Theorem 2.2]

If
G
is a bipartite graph, then
\rho^{o}(G\Box K_{2})=2\alpha_{2}(G)
.

Theorem 7.9
.

[
11
, Theorem 3.2]

If
n\geq 2
, then
\rho^{o}(Q_{n})\geq 2^{n-\lfloor\log(n-1)\rfloor-1}
.

A graph is said to be
perfect injectively colorable
if it has an injective coloring in which every color class forms an open packing of largest cardinality. It is known that the
n
-dimensional hypercubes
Q_{n}
, for
n\leq 8
, are perfect injectively colorable graphs
[
11
, Section 4]
. So
Q_{9}
is the first instance of a hypercube, which is not in this class of graphs, for which bounds have been derived in the literature. In particular, Brešar et al. (see
[
11
, Table 1]
) showed that

\displaystyle 34\leq\rho^{o}(Q_{9})=2\alpha_{2}(Q_{8})\leq 60.

The following lemma can be directly derived from
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
, and will be used to show our last main result.

Lemma 7.10
.

[
1
]

For a graph
G
, let
\rho>0
be the maximum eigenvalue of
A_{G}
with a positive unit eigenvector
y
. Let
p(x)=x^{2}+c_{1}x+c_{0}
be a polynomial such that
p(\rho)>0
and
p(A_{G})
is positive semidefinite. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(G)\leq\frac{1}{p(\rho)}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(p(A_{G}))_{uu}}{y_{u}^{2}}.

Let
d_{u}
denote the degree of a vertex
u
. We first show the following spectral upper bound for the open packing number of general bipartite prisms.

Proposition 7.11
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph, and let
\rho>0
be the maximum eigenvalue of
A_{G}
with a positive unit eigenvector
y
. Then

\displaystyle\rho^{o}(G\Box K_{2})=2\alpha_{2}(G)\leq\frac{2}{\rho(\rho+\lambda)}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{d_{u}}{y_{u}^{2}},

where
\lambda
is the minimum positive eigenvalue of
A_{G}
. Moreover, if
G
is
d
-regular, then

\displaystyle\rho^{o}(G\Box K_{2})=2\alpha_{2}(G)\leq\frac{2|V(G)|}{(d+\lambda)}.

Proof.

Since
G
be a bipartite,
-\lambda
is the maximum negative eigenvalue of
A_{G}
. Let
p(x)=x^{2}+\lambda x
. Then
p(\rho)>0
and
p(A_{G})
is positive semidefinite. By Lemma
7.10
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(G)\leq\frac{1}{\rho(\rho+\lambda)}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{d_{u}}{y_{u}^{2}}.

∎

From Proposition
7.11
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 7.12
.

\alpha_{2}(Q_{n})\leq\Big(n+\frac{3+(-1)^{n}}{2}\Big)^{-1}2^{n}
.

In
[
11
