Introduction
The Texas A&M Transportation Institute (TTI) and the Los Alamos National Laboratory -
Parking and Transportation Services (LANL) have been working in close collaboration to seek
solutions for parking, traffic operations, traffic safety, and transit issues facing LANL. NM 502
is a main route for employee traffic entering the lab as well as traffic in and out of the Los
Alamos Townsite. The section of roadway in the vicinity of Camino Entrada is of particular
interest as it was the site of two very serious crashes involving the extended LANL family, and it
is also the entry point onto NM 502 for Los Alamos County Operations to include the school
district and transit vehicles.
TTI is very experienced in this area and was requested to perform this effort as part of our
standing agreement. The focus of this effort occurred on 16-17 July 2025 onsite. We greatly
appreciate the collaborative engagement of participants from the New Mexico Department of
Transportation, Los Alamos County (Public Works and Police), Los Alamos School District,
Atomic City Transit, and LANL.
Three overall goals were established and successfully achieved for this Road Safety Audit (RSA)
effort:
 1. Apply the RSA process in this specific section of NM 502 and develop actionable
 recommendations.
 2. Understand if specific recommendations can be drawn from the high profile crashes that
 occurred in this area.
 3. Increase awareness of issues and the processes to improve traffic safety to a broader
 audience at LANL and within the greater Los Alamos County community.
An essential element of this project is the linkage back to the following Parking/Transportation
Efforts now underway:
 1. Better employee utilization of bus, carpool, and vanpool options will lessen the number
 of single occupant vehicles on the road (less risk of crashes and lower emissions) while
 also meeting the mission requirements of LANL to use limited available space for
 production facilities/offices versus building more parking.
 1. Predictable parking availability will make staggering peak arrivals reasonable. Those
 arriving later to LANL will be able to find a parking space. This will improve traffic
 operations and demand during peak arrivals.
 2. Commuting to the lab is a pressure point for new and existing employees. Predictable
 parking and commute options will also contribute to recruiting and retention efforts.
The following sections provide the results of the internal safety survey, background for the RSA
effort, observations/recommendations from the RSA team, consolidated recommendations for

implementation, and additional information for consideration and further analysis. Specific
details will be included in the appendices (to include a listing of participants).

LANL Lab-Wide Safety Survey
Traffic safety affects every employee at LANL and the broader communities. Whether it is
direct involvement in a crash, seeing a colleague recover from injuries, or knowing a family is
suffering - it is important to all of us. The first goal of this RSA effort is to increase awareness
of traffic safety and possible solutions. The survey is a means to both increase this awareness
and gather information on concerns. This information can be used to not only inform our efforts
on East Jemez, but it will also inform the pursuit of future efforts. This survey was published on
25 November 2024 with closure on 13 December 2024. More than 4,700 responses were
received and evaluated. NM 502 was identified as a key corridor of concern for employees.
The detailed results are included in Appendix A. The summary findings are as follows:
Consistent Themes Across Survey
 • Speeding, aggressive driving, and lack of enforcement are recurring concerns across
 all questions.
 • Congestion during peak hours is a stressor for commuters to and from the lab.
 • Construction zones and merging areas require better planning and monitoring.
 • Infrastructure upgrades, including dividers and improved signage, are widely
 recommended.
 • Wildlife hazards add unpredictability to driving conditions, especially on less-
 illuminated roads.
Overarching Actionable Suggestions:
 • Use technology (e.g., cameras) to enhance enforcement.
 • Prioritize audits and improvements in high-risk areas like Truck Route, NM 502, and
 E. Jemez Road.
 • Leadership and individuals alike need to engage in promoting safer commuting
 practices.

Key Locations Highlighted for Conducting the Next Audit
 1. Jemez Road:
 • A significant number of mentions suggest concerns with the entire stretch or parts
 of Jemez Road, due to traffic, speed, or road conditions.
 2. Truck Route:
 • Issues include heavy vehicle traffic or road design challenges.
 3. NM-502:
 • Specific focus on NM-502 indicates issues with merging, speed, and
 infrastructure.
 4. Diamond Drive:
 • Problematic intersections, pedestrian crossings, and traffic flow concerns.
 5. Pajarito Road:
 • Highlights specific safety risks involving high traffic volumes or poor road
 conditions.
 6. Main Hill:
 • Commonly referenced as an area with steep grades and visibility issues.

Background
NM 502 is the main arterial entering the Townsite and is the traditional main entry point for
LANL. Main Gate Park is located just east of the intersection with Camino Entrada. The facility
is owned by NMDOT with cooperative improvements in the corridor by Los Alamos County.
The 2024 traffic count on NM 502 was 13, 527 (See Figure 1) and turning movement counts
were provided by Los Alamos County (see Appendix B). The intersection has left and right turn
lanes along with acceleration lanes. The approach from the east is a steep section of roadway
with tight curves and limited speed. As the WB traffic gets past this section (near the Santa Fe
County line) the terrain opens, and the roadway is generally level with a curve to the west of the
Camino Entrada intersection (site of one of the high-profile crashes) and then proceeds past the
airport. The area studied was approximately one mile in length.
Land use in the area includes a hotel, grocery store, office buildings, retail, and the LA County
operations facility. All driveways and intersections are stop controlled with no stop signs or
traffic signals for traffic on NM 502. The study area is shown in Figure 2.
The field work was completed on 16 July 2025 during the morning and evening peaks, and a
session to review the findings and recommendations was conducted at the LA County facilities
on 17 July 2025. Traffic observations and radar speed data collection were completed.

NM 502 @ Camino Entrada
The intersection at NM 502/Camino Entrada is stop controlled (southbound traffic only). Key
traffic generators included in the LA Operations facility (including LA Schools and ACT). It
should be noted that with the schools not being in session, there was no bus traffic. Buses
turning left to go to White Rock conflict with the morning peak traffic. It was reported that the
buses have a difficult time safely executing the left turn out of Camino Entrada. AM peak
traffic is dominated by westbound movement (entering LANL) and the opposite trend exists in
the PM Peak (leaving LANL).

Crash/Speed Data
Speed observations hovered around the speed limit and appeared to be metered somewhat by the
platooning of vehicles in both directions. Speeds are reported (LAPD) to increase in off-peak
hours. See Appendix C.
Crash data did not appear to be excessive. There were two high-profile crashes within the last 18
months that drew attention to the area and are described with the crash data in Appendix D.

Observations During RSA
 • Numerous rolling stops SB on Camino Entrada turning right
 • WB Right Turn Lane occasionally used for passing
 • Driveways into landscape business add confusion
 • ACT buses - some had to turn left to serve White Rock; same for school buses
 • When this area becomes the detour route for the Truck Route, the congestion is reported
 to be severe.
 • WB Right turns directly from main lanes – across right turn lane

Options for Consideration
Short Term (next 12 months)
 • Review Median Treatments
 o Two-way left turn lane
 o Raised medians
 o Better definition of lane purpose (turn lane or acceleration lane) and resolve
 confusion with wide open area
 o Traffic calming/Buffer for eastbound
 o Consider cross-hatching medians west of Camino Entrada
 • Camina Entrada – revised striping
 • Vegetation blocking view to the east from Camino Entrada (depending on where drivers
 stop)
 • Driver Education – Poor behavior, fatigue management, distracted driving, gap
 acceptance
 • Verify off-peak speeds – reports that speeds increase as volumes decrease off-peak

 13

Intermediate Term (1-4 years)
 • Provide sufficient shoulders for bikes

Long Term (5 years +)
 • Develop alternative intersection control with a roundabout. A roundabout is located west
 of this location at the Trinity/Central intersection. Detailed modelling revealed no
 change/slight improvement in delay, and greatly simplified turning movements into and
 out of Camino Entrada (See Appendix F)

NM 502 (East and West of Camino Entrada)
The area east of the intersection includes the entrance to a landscaping company and the
intersection with East Gate Drive (not studied in detail). The area west of the intersection curve
slightly to the north. It should be noted that high-profile fatality occurred in this area.

Crash/Speed Data
Speed observations hovered around the speed limit and appeared to be metered somewhat by the
platooning of vehicles in both directions. Speeds are reported (LAPD) to increase in off-peak
hours. See Appendix C.
Crash data did not appear to be excessive. There were two high-profile crashes within the last 18
months that drew attention to the area and are described with the crash data in Appendix D.

Observations During RSA
 • Not clear as to the function of the right turn lane and its transition from an acceleration
 lane
 • No WB left turn lane into Canyon Rim trailhead or EB Right turn lane into Canyon Rim
 trailhead
 • Pedestrian Accommodations - no sidewalk on north side of NM 502 from Camino
 Entrada to East Gate. ACT bus stop near hotel/grocery store appears to serve East Gate
 area. Passengers heading to/from East Gate have no sidewalk
 • Bike Accommodations – NM DOT has designated this area a Tier One Bike Route

Options for Consideration
Short Term (next 12 months)
 • Chevrons in curve west of Camino Entrada
 • Consider impact of winter weather conditions/snow-plowing

Intermediate Term (1-4 years)
 • Extension of rumble strip from Santa Fe County Line west to at least curve – maybe past
 airport
 • Add sidewalk on north side of NM 502 from Camino Entrada to East Gate

Additional Observations/Considerations
While NM DOT and Los Alamos County are the primary entities controlling the engineering
improvements to NM 502 and the approaching cross streets, there are steps that can be taken in
the human factors area to be considered when possible. Appendix E includes a number of
resources available to all entities for use in employee training and in young driver programs
(Teens in the Driver Seat Program may be of value to the Los Alamos School District).
Critical to solving the broader issues we face with traffic safety, the three “Es” of traffic safety
must be considered concurrently – Engineering, Educations, and Enforcement. Without proper
attention to all three, it will be difficult to approach our goal of zero fatalities on our highways.

Pedestrian/Bicycle Safety

include the following:
