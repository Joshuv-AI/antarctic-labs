Deformation mechanism and mechanical response of coal mine roadway roof in the unsupported area during rapid tunneling
Authors: Shuai Wang, Sen Yang, Wenbing Guo, Liqiang Ma, Zaijiang Yu, Ping Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAiming at the prominent problems in the rapid excavation of coal roadways, namely the complex factors affecting the stability of composite roofs and the restricted excavation efficiency caused by an overly small unsupported roof distance, this study takes the 1211(1) transportation roadway of Guqiao Coal Mine in Huainan Mining Area as the research object. Through numerical simulation, the influence of unsupported roof distance on roof stability is quantitatively analyzed. Considering the actual stress characteristics of the composite roof in the unsupported area of coal roadways, a thick plate mechanical model with three fixed edges and one simply supported edge is established. Based on elasticity theory, the deflection expression for any point on the composite roof is derived, the sensitivity of deflection and subsidence of the immediate roof in the unsupported area is analyzed, and the response surface methodology is used to determine the key factors dominating the deformation of the unsupported roof and their coupling relationships. The results show that under constant geological and engineering conditions, the unsupported roof distance is the most critical factor governing the deformation and failure of the unsupported roof; when it exceeds 2 m, the roof subsidence rate increases sharply and the difficulty of roof control increases significantly. Combined with the engineering geological conditions of the roadway, the optimal unsupported roof distance is determined as 1 m. After adopting the “one-cycle excavation and one-cycle support” protocol and high-prestressed bolt-cable combined support, the average roadway excavation speed is increased from 6 to 15–18 m/d, and the roof remains stable and controllable throughout the excavation process, verifying the rationality and engineering practicability of the research findings.Keywords: Composite roof, Rapid excavation, Thick plate theory, Unsupported roof distance, Surrounding rock controlSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

Coal mine roadway excavation is a complex construction procedure involving the alternation of multiple processes, and each operational link exerts a direct impact on the overall excavation efficiency. The roadway excavation rate is not only restricted by the mechanization level of excavation equipment and the coordination degree between different construction processes, but also closely correlated with multiple factors including engineering geological conditions, professional skills and quality of construction workers, as well as on-site organization and management1–3. Additionally, the uncoordinated mechanization matching between fully mechanized excavation and fully mechanized coal mining has led to a long-standing imbalance between mining and excavation activities nationwide. The mining-excavation ratio has remained at approximately 1:3 for a long period, and the slow coal roadway excavation speed has failed to keep pace with the coal mining speed, thus becoming a critical constraint restricting the high-yield and high-efficiency production of coal mines4,5.

The geological conditions of coal-bearing strata are complex and variable. Compared with shallow coal seams, the surrounding rock of deep-buried roadways frequently exhibits distinctive nonlinear deformation characteristics, which poses a severe threat to the construction safety and progress of deep-buried coal roadways with large cross-Sects6–8. The essence of rapid excavation technology for coal roadways lies in adopting excavation and support equipment suitable for on-site engineering geological conditions to form an integrated and efficient excavation operation line, and optimizing construction organization and management schemes, so as to maximize the roadway excavation speed. Nevertheless, according to extensive field statistical data, the majority of coal mines currently adopt a sequential construction mode combining boom-type roadheaders with hydraulic anchor drilling rigs and single-boom anchor drilling machines.

To control the deformation and instability of the roof in the unsupported area, these mines mainly rely on shortening the unsupported roof distance and implementing one-time high-strength support measures, which consequently leads to frequent advance and retreat of the tunneling equipment, low cyclic operation efficiency, and slow support speed that cannot match the cutting speed of the roadheader. This situation greatly restricts the improvement of coal roadway excavation efficiency and further intensifies the tense mining-excavation relationship in coal mines9,10.

Numerous experts and scholars worldwide have conducted in-depth investigations on the deformation and failure mechanism of coal mine roadway roofs within unsupported areas, and have obtained numerous valuable research findings. Kang et al. analyzed the stress distribution and deformation-failure characteristics of surrounding rock at the excavation face, and further explored the distribution features of the stress field within the head-on surrounding rock support structure, as well as the control effect of bolt support on the roof in the unsupported area11. Chu et al. established a beam structure model to tackle the issues of complex influencing factors governing the stability of the roof near the excavation face and the restricted excavation efficiency induced by unreasonable unsupported roof distance, and subsequently derived a theoretical calculation formula for the allowable unsupported roof distance at the excavation working face12. Wu developed a fully fixed-supported elastic thin plate mechanical model, deduced the deflection and stress calculation formulas of the roadway roof based on elasticity theory, and determined the ultimate unsupported roof distance in accordance with the roof instability criterion13.

Ma investigated the deformation evolution characteristics of the roof in the unsupported area by establishing a roof rock beam model, and obtained a calculation formula for the ultimate unsupported roof distance during roadway excavation14. Li treated the immediate roof of the heading area as a thin plate with three fixed edges and one simply supported edge, and derived the rational unsupported roof distance formula for the heading section via elastic thin plate theory15. Zhao constructed a coupled mechanical model of the composite roof covering both the unsupported area and the supported area, solved the deflection and stress equations at any point of the composite roof in the unsupported area using elasticity theory, and further analyzed the deformation-failure features and key stability-influencing factors of the composite roof in both regions16.

The aforementioned research outcomes provide crucial insights into accurately comprehending the deformation characteristics and failure mechanism of roofs in unsupported areas of coal mine roadways. However, regarding the immediate roof of coal mine roadways, its thickness is typically substantial, often exceeding one-fifth of the roadway width. When establishing the equilibrium equations, the contribution of transverse shear forces must be explicitly incorporated. If classical thin-plate theory is still employed, it would artificially impose an infinitely large shear modulus along the thickness direction, which is inconsistent with the actual mechanical behavior of the rock mass.

To address this limitation, this study proposes a novel mechanical model consisting of an elastic plate with three fixed edges and one simply supported edge. This model is developed based on systematic numerical simulations investigating the influence of varying unsupported roof distances on the stress distribution and deformation characteristics of the surrounding rock at the excavation face. Utilizing this model, this research comprehensively analyzes the deformation behavior of roofs in unsupported areas and identifies the key factors governing their stability. Consequently, a rational unsupported roof distance for excavation is determined. Furthermore, the engineering applicability of the proposed model and optimal parameters is validated through numerical simulation analysis. The findings of this study are expected to provide valuable theoretical and technical references for the rapid excavation of similar coal mine roadways.

Engineering background

The 1211(1) working face of Guqiao Coal Mine primarily mines the No. 11 − 2 coal seam, with an average burial depth of 610 m, an average coal seam thickness of 3.4 m, and a coal seam dip angle of approximately 3°, indicating a nearly horizontal coal seam suitable for conventional mining. Affected by geological structures such as faults, the dip angle of the local coal seam fluctuates considerably, impairing the continuity and integrity of both the coal seam and its overlying roof.

Main roof consists of fine sandstone with thickness of 2.9–5.0 m and thick layered structure. Quartz dominates its mineral composition, followed by feldspar and rock debris. Thin siltstone interlayers can be seen with deformed bedding developed.

Immediate roof is composed of mudstone and No.11-3 coal seam, ranging 0.6–2.9 m in thickness. The thick-layered mudstone contains plant root fossils in upper part and stem-leaf fossils in lower part. The No.11-3 coal seam is powdery and fragmented, mainly bright coal with minor dull coal, classified as semi-bright coal featuring asphalt to glass luster.

As illustrated in Fig. 1, the immediate roof constitutes a composite roof structure with poor overall stability, which is susceptible to interlayer separation. This separation behavior can further induce roof deformation and instability, exacerbating the safety control challenges for the coal roadway roof. A large unsupported roof distance or delayed support operation can easily trigger roof collapse accidents at the excavation face; conversely, an overly small unsupported roof distance or excessive support density will result in low cyclic operation efficiency and prolonged support duration, thereby hindering the rapid excavation and forming of the roadway.

Comprehensive columnar section of coal seam roof and floor.

The 1211(1) transportation roadway adopts a rectangular cross-section, with a width of 5.6 m and a height of 3.9 m. Given the weak roof conditions and complex geological structures of the coal seam, a combined support system is implemented for the coal roadway, dominated by rock bolt support and reinforced by bolt-net-cable support with additional anchor cables. Specifically, left-handed threaded steel pretensioned rock bolts are utilized for the roof support, with a specification of Φ22 × 2500 mm and a row spacing of 950 × 1000 mm. Six roof bolts are installed per row, with a pretightening torque of no less than 200 N m and an anchoring force of at least 150 kN.

For the rib support, left-handed threaded steel pretensioned rock bolts are also adopted, with a row spacing of 880 × 900 mm. Five rock bolts are arranged on each rib, featuring a pretightening torque of no less than 200 N·m and an anchoring force of at least 125 kN. High-prestressed anchor cables at the roof are installed in a staggered layout using T-shaped steel beams in a “3–3” arrangement. The anchor cables have a specification of Φ22 × 6200 mm, with a row spacing of 1500 × 1000 mm, a pretension force of no less than 180 kN, and an anchoring force of at least 350 kN. The layout of the roadway support cross-section is depicted in Fig. 2.

Roadway support parameters.

Fully mechanized construction is adopted for the excavation of the 1211(1) transportation roadway, and a typical “excavate one cycle, support one cycle” sequential operation scheme is implemented throughout the excavation process, as illustrated in Fig. 3.

Typical process flow of comprehensive excavator.

During roadway excavation, multi-cycle operation is carried out in accordance with standardized cyclic construction procedures, following a three-shift work system with eight working hours per shift. Excavation operations are conducted during the morning and evening shifts, while routine maintenance is performed during the middle shift. Each shift is scheduled to complete at least three standard excavation cycles, with a minimum cyclic footage of 1.0 m; a total of six cycles per day yields a daily footage of 6 m. In the actual field construction, however, the limited unsupported roof distance of only 0.5 m leads to extremely low cyclic operation efficiency. Based on the statistical analysis of roadway construction logs provided by the mine, the average monthly excavation footage is merely 110 m. Accordingly, the overall excavation efficiency of the coal roadway at Guqiao Coal Mine remains relatively low, further exacerbating the severe imbalance between mining and excavation activities.

Mechanical model and deformation law of the roof in the unsupported area

Construction of mechanical model for the roof of the unsupported area

During coal roadway excavation, the structural characteristics and support status of the roadway roof undergo dynamic variations with the cyclic forward advancement of the excavation face. Nevertheless, at any stage within the excavation cycle, the spatial structure of the excavated roadway can be systematically divided into three distinct zones: the anchorage zone, the unsupported roof zone, and the zone to be excavated, as depicted in Fig. 4. Specifically, the anchorage zone denotes the section of the roadway where permanent support measures have been installed; the unsupported roof zone refers to the unsupportive section between the anchorage zone and the excavation face; and the zone to be excavated is the rock mass section located immediately in front of the unsupported roof zone.

Structure and morphology of coal roadway excavation space.

Following the excavation of coal mine roadways, the stress state of the surrounding rock shifts from a triaxial stress state to a biaxial stress state, leading to a significant reduction in the strength of the superficial rock mass and gradual failure propagation from the shallow region to the deep strata17. Restrained by the support of the excavation face, the elastoplastic deformation of the roadway roof cannot be fully released, and the corresponding stress redistribution process cannot be completed rapidly. As a result, the surrounding rock deformation of the roadway is mainly concentrated in the shallow strata, the duration of roof exposure in the unsupported zone is relatively short, and the roof at the excavation face remains in the elastic deformation stage.

When conducting mechanical analysis on the stability of the heading face roof during cyclic excavation, the heading roof can be simplified into an elastic plate model with three fixed edges and one simply supported edge in accordance with the spatial structural division of the excavated roadway. In the process of coal roadway excavation, the immediate roof generally maintains favorable integrity and can be regarded as a continuous medium for mechanical analysis; meanwhile, no significant interlayer bonding force exists between the immediate roof, so the interlayer physical bonding strength is neglected in the modeling process. For the composite roof in the unsupported zone of a large-cross-section coal roadway selected as the research object, its thickness-to-span ratio is generally greater than 1/5, and such a roof structure is classified as a thick plate18,19. Accordingly, the transverse shear effect must be fully taken into account when establishing the equilibrium differential equations for the composite roof.

Based on the aforementioned analysis, a mechanical model for the roof at the heading face is established utilizing the thick plate theory, featuring three fixed edges and one simply supported edge, as illustrated in Fig. 5. In this model, a denotes the excavation width of the coal roadway, b represents the length of the unsupported roof zone (i.e., the unsupported roof distance), and h signifies the thickness of the immediate roof.

Distribution pattern of roof deflection in the unsupported roof area.

As observed from the analysis of Fig. 6, under the self-weight of the immediate roof and overlying loads, the immediate roof within the unsupported zone experiences flexural deformation of varying magnitudes, with the maximum roof subsidence occurring near the central and rear portions of the unsupported area. Deformation law of composite roof in unsupported zones under variable operating conditions

The deflection deformation of the composite roof under varying influencing factors is analyzed in accordance with Eq. (13), with the corresponding results illustrated in Fig. 7. Four key variable effects on roof subsidence are systematically revealed as follows:

The deflection curve of the roof in the unsupported roof area under different variables.

Firstly, with the increase in overlying strata load, the roof subsidence increases drastically. The maximum roof subsidence corresponding to an overlying strata load of 15 MPa is approximately seven times that under a 5 MPa load, demonstrating that roof deformation is highly sensitive to the magnitude of overlying rock load.

Secondly, the roof subsidence decreases significantly with the increase in immediate roof thickness. The maximum subsidence at an immediate roof thickness of 0.7 m is nearly five times that at 1.1 m, which further confirms the high sensitivity of roof deformation to immediate roof thickness.

Thirdly, a notable reduction in roof deformation occurs as the elastic modulus of the immediate roof rises. The maximum roof subsidence at an elastic modulus of 5000 MPa is roughly four times that at 20,000 MPa, indicating that roof deformation is also strongly sensitive to the elastic modulus of the immediate roof layer.

Fourthly, the roof subsidence increases markedly with the enlargement of the unsupported roof span. The maximum subsidence at an unsupported roof span of 6.0 m is about three times that at 2.0 m, verifying the high sensitivity of roof deformation to the unsupported roof span.

Based on the above analysis, it can be concluded that the deformation of the composite roof in coal roadways exhibits high sensitivity to four key parameters: overlying strata load, immediate roof thickness, elastic modulus, and unsupported roof span. Once the magnitude of the overlying rock load is determined, the deformation of the roof within the unsupported zone is predominantly governed by the structural properties, thickness, and span of the roof rock strata. Restricted by the support effect of the anchorage zone, the roof deformation in this region is notably restrained and slowed down, which further forces the maximum roof subsidence point to shift toward the unsupported roof zone. With the increase in unsupported roof span, the location of the maximum roof subsidence in the unsupported area gradually migrates toward the rear portion of this zone. In essence, the increasing unsupported roof span and the corresponding bending subsidence of the roof in the unsupported zone will further accelerate the subsidence development of the roof within the adjacent anchorage zone.

Numerical calculation model

FLAC3D is a continuum mechanics numerical software developed to address diverse complicated problems in geotechnical engineering, which enables efficient numerical simulation of coal roadway excavation construction and various mechanical responses of surrounding rock during excavation. Accordingly, FLAC3D 5.0 is adopted in this section to simulate the fully-mechanized excavation of coal roadways with composite roof. By analyzing the mechanical response characteristics of composite roof, the dynamic deformation law and instability mechanism of composite roof in the process of fully-mechanized roadway excavation are investigated22.

During tunnel excavation, the initially balanced in-situ stress regime is disrupted, triggering stress redistribution within the surrounding rock mass. This stress disturbance zone typically extends to a width of approximately five times the tunnel span. Furthermore, the coal seam features a relatively gentle dip angle, which exerts negligible influence on the mechanical response induced by coal roadway excavation; accordingly, the coal seam is classified as a near-horizontal mining seam. To account for the impact of boundary constraints on numerical simulation results, the computational model is dimensioned at 80 m (length) × 60 m (width) × 40 m (height), with the detailed numerical calculation model illustrated in Fig. 12.

Numerical calculation model.

To balance computational efficiency and numerical accuracy, a graded mesh refinement strategy was adopted for the numerical model. Local mesh densification was performed on the roof and floor strata adjacent to the roadway. Specifically, the mesh size was refined to 0.5 m×0.5 m for the immediate surrounding rock within the roadway cross-section, while a relatively coarse mesh of 1.0 m×1.0 m was assigned to the far-field rock strata. The final optimized model consists of 305,760 elements in total. For boundary conditions, a vertical stress of 15.25 MPa was applied to the top boundary to simulate the actual roadway burial depth of 610 m. Normal displacement constraints were imposed on the left, right, front, and rear vertical boundaries, whereas both normal and tangential displacement constraints were set for the bottom boundary. The Mohr-Coulomb yield criterion was employed as the constitutive model for all numerical calculations, and the mechanical parameters of coal and rock masses used in the model are summarized in Table 2.

In accordance with field in-situ stress measurements, the stress field of the 1211(1) transportation roadway in the Guqiao Mine is dominated by horizontal tectonic stress, exhibiting prominent directionality and regional variability. The maximum horizontal principal stress is measured as 21.65 MPa, with a stable orientation ranging from NE56.8° to NE76.3°.

Characteristics of stress and displacement distribution in surrounding rock

Based on the surrounding rock support conditions along the roadway, the entire excavation section can be categorized into three distinct zones: the excavation face, the unsupported roof zone, and the supported zone, as illustrated in Fig. 13.

Contour map of vertical stress distribution near the heading of the roadway.

The supported zone is defined as the roadway section that has been fully reinforced with support structures behind the excavation face, whereas the unsupported roof zone refers to the unprotected roadway segment between the supported zone and the excavation head. Given the entirely unconstrained state of the immediate roof within this unsupported zone, its overall stability is predominantly governed by three key factors: the excavation face conditions, the length of the unsupported roof zone, and the load-bearing strength of the rear support system.

Subject to roadway excavation-induced unloading, the stress state of the surrounding rock shifts from three-dimensional in-situ stress to plane stress, accompanied by a notable reduction in the mechanical strength of the superficial surrounding rock mass. Consequently, its load-bearing capacity deteriorates sharply, and deformation as well as failure will occur once the rock mass can no longer withstand the initial in-situ stress. This process further weakens the spatial confinement effect on the shallow surrounding rock, forcing the shallow strata to undertake additional loads and thus elevating its bearing stress level. As the stress-bearing capacity of the superficial and shallow rock masses gradually transfers to the deep surrounding rock, a distinct high-stress confinement zone forms in the deep strata; this is followed by elastic deformation-induced stress transfer, with stress values declining gradually until the original in-situ stress level is restored. Upon the installation of support structures, the surrounding rock stress release coefficient decreases significantly, which in turn optimizes the overall stress distribution and mechanical state of the coal roadway surrounding rock, as depicted in Fig. 14.

Vertical stress distribution at different cross-sections of roadway.

As illustrated in Fig. 15, the mechanical properties of the bolt-anchored rock mass—including the elastic modulus E, cohesion c, and internal friction angle φ—are significantly enhanced upon the implementation of rock bolt support. Correspondingly, the overall load-bearing capacity and deformation resistance of the reinforced rock mass are substantially improved. Consequently, the distribution range of high stress within the anchored rock mass expands markedly, whereas the overall deformation of the rock mass decreases synchronously.

Displacement of the roof and two ribs in the support area and unsupported roof area.

Following rock bolt installation, the axial displacement constraint effect of the bolts can fully mobilize the confinement potential of the deep surrounding rock (in both displacement and stress aspects), so as to compensate for the insufficient confinement capacity of the shallow surrounding rock and effectively suppress the deformation tendency of the unconsolidated rock mass. The synergistic effect of enhanced surrounding rock bearing capacity and mitigated deformation trend further reduces the overall rock mass displacement, decreases the proportion of in-situ stress released through rock deformation, and ultimately optimizes the overall stress state of the entire surrounding rock system.

Influence of unsupported roof span changes on vertical stress and displacement distribution of composite roof

After tunnel excavation, the superficial roof rock mass undergoes fragmentation and crack propagation, placing the shallow immediate roof in a pseudo-triaxial confining pressure state. This reduces the upper strength limit of the immediate roof and correspondingly weakens its overall load-bearing capacity. As depicted in Fig. 16, the roof within the unsupported roof zone remains in a shallow tensile state while subjected to lateral compression on both sides, owing to the complete absence of support measures. As the unsupported roof span increases from 1.0 m to 3.0 m, the vertical stress decreases marginally from 20.01 MPa to 19.98 MPa, with a reduction rate of less than 2‰. Accordingly, variations in the unsupported roof span exert a negligible influence on the vertical stress distribution of the roof within the unsupported zone.

Cloud map of vertical stress of the roof at different unsupported roof distance.

Figure 17 illustrates the axial distribution of roof vertical displacement corresponding to three different unsupported roof spans: 1.0 m, 2.0 m, and 3.0 m. At an unsupported span of 1.0 m, the axial roof subsidence along the roadway shows a monotonically increasing pattern, with no obvious roof deflection detected. As the unsupported roof span increases to 2.0 m, a peak subsidence value appears within the unsupported region, and this maximum point occurs behind the supported zone. Moreover, roof deflection becomes increasingly prominent with a further increase in the unsupported roof span.

Application effect evaluation

Circular footage and support parameters for roadway excavation

To verify the rationality of the allowable unsupported roof distance derived from the aforementioned theoretical analysis, the 1211(1) transportation roadway in Guqiao Coal Mine was selected as the field test roadway, with its specific layout illustrated in Fig. 18. Geologically and mining-wise, the north side of the 1211(1) working face is adjacent to the protective coal pillar line along the 11 − 2 coal fault within the Gugui graben fault zone; the south side borders the ventilation airway of the 11 − 2 upper panel; the east side abuts the 1212(1) working face; and the west side is set against the boundary protective coal pillar line separating Guqiao Mine and Gubei Mine.

Layout plan of 1211(1) working face.

Based on the aforementioned theoretical and numerical analysis of roof deformation and stability characteristics, combined with the actual geological conditions of the 1211(1) transportation roadway in Guqiao Coal Mine-namely an immediate roof thickness of 1.6 m and overlying strata composed mainly of thick, dense, hard fine sandstone with high strength and poor fissure development-the maximum allowable unsupported roof distance during roadway excavation construction is quantitatively determined to be 1.0 m. Correspondingly, to ensure timely and effective roof control and avoid excessive roof subsidence or local deflection within the unsupported zone, the maximum support row spacing for bolt-anchor support must not exceed 1.0 m.

Such a parameter design fully matches the “excavate one section, anchor one section” mechanized excavation and support process, which is conducive to realizing synchronous advancement of excavation and support, as well as continuous and stable control of the roadway surrounding rock. the specific process layout and zoning are illustrated in Fig. 19. In this figure, the red dashed line distinctly denotes the temporary unsupported roof zone formed after roadway excavation, while the yellow dashed line marks the pending support area that requires immediate bolt-anchor construction to restrict roof deformation.

Original process.

3.Process improvement measures.

Process improvement measures.

Based on the investigation, analysis and summary of existing underground construction technologies, anchor excavators are used for cutting, temporary support and bolt construction with temporary support equipment. Each process and personnel configuration are refined to improve excavation efficiency, accelerate cycle progress and reduce workers’ labor intensity. The processes to be completed in each cycle are as follows: in the small cycle, 4 roof bolts are completed at one time, and 2 bolts are constructed simultaneously on each upper part of the coal rib support; in the middle cycle, first install the remaining roof bolts and the bottom corner bolts of the coal ribs, and add the bottom corner bolts within the middle cycle range; in the large cycle, single anchor cables are added to strengthen the roof support, as shown in Fig. 21.

Monitoring and effect evaluation of surrounding rock deformation in roadways

To verify the engineering reliability of the excavation and support schemes, field industrial tests were carried out on the 1211(1) transportation roadway of Guqiao Mine. A set of surface displacement monitoring stations was arranged in the roadway, and the cross-point method was adopted to monitor displacements at the roof center and coal wall waistline. The monitoring was conducted once a day for a total duration of 90 days. After sorting and analyzing the measured data, the convergence deformation curve of roadway surrounding rock was plotted, as shown in Fig. 22(a). The monitoring results indicate that the deformation of roadway surrounding rock undergoes three evolutionary stages: severe deformation, slow deformation, and stable deformation, with the most significant deformation and fastest deformation rate occurring in the initial excavation stage. The maximum deformation rates of the roadway ribs and roof are 4.5 mm/d and 3.2 mm/d, respectively, and the overall surrounding rock deformation remains controllable.

Evaluation of the roadway excavation and support effect.

Accordingly, the reasonable maximum unsupported roof distance of the roadway is determined to be approximately 1.0 m. Adopting the one-cycle excavation and one-cycle support combined with high-prestressed bolt-cable combined support to strengthen the surrounding rock support, the average daily excavation speed of the roadway increased from 6 m/d to 15–18 m/d. Meanwhile, the overall stability of roadway surrounding rock was greatly improved, which effectively verifies the engineering applicability of the proposed excavation and support schemes.

Conclusions

A mechanical model of a thick plate with three fixed supports and one simple support was constructed, and the deflection formula for any point in the unsupported roof area was derived using elastic mechanics theory. Theoretical analysis indicates that the roof deflection distribution along the radial direction of the roadway is completely symmetric, with the maximum subsidence point located at the center of the roadway. In contrast, along the axial direction of the roadway, the roof deflection distribution within the unsupported roof range is asymmetric-that is, the maximum subsidence point is offset toward the supported area.

By analyzing the distribution law of roof deflection and subsidence under different variables, it is found that the deformation of the coal roadway composite roof is highly sensitive to the overlying load, immediate roof thickness, elastic modulus, and unsupported roof distance. The degrees of influence of these factors on roof deflection and subsidence, in descending order, are immediate roof thickness, elastic modulus, unsupported roof distance, and overlying load. Based on the actual engineering situation, three key influencing factors were identified: immediate roof thickness, elastic modulus, and unsupported roof distance.
