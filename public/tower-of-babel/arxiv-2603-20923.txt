k-graph algebras are iterated Cuntz–Pimsner algebras — from the bottom up
Valentin Deaconu, Menevşe Eryüzlü Paulovicks, S. Kaliszewski, John Quigg

Abstract
. We introduce a new method of expressing a k k -graph C ∗ C^{*} -algebra as a Cuntz–Pimsner algebra. Kumjian, Pask, and Sims have done this directly, using a linking algebra approach and a ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -graph algebra. This can be iterated downward. Our process, on the other hand, starts at the bottom, with Pimsner’s theorem for graph algebras, and iterates upward. We actually work with product systems over ℕ k \mathbb{N}^{k} , and the result for k k -graphs is a special case. Our iteration step involves a “decategorization” of a recent theorem showing that the Cuntz–Pimsner construction is functorial at the level of “enchilada categories”.

1.
Introduction

This paper combines
the following topics:
directed graph
C^{*}
-algebras
and their higher-rank generalizations,

C^{*}
-correspondences
and product systems of these,
and their Cuntz–Pimsner algebras.
And a bit of category theory —
at least in spirit if not in body.

It is tempting to imagine that one of Pimsner’s primary motivations for inventing (what are now called) Cuntz–Pimsner algebras (see
[
Pim97
]
) was his realization that the
C^{*}
-algebra of a directed graph is naturally isomorphic to the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra of an associated
C^{*}
–correspondence formed from the edges and vertices.
The
k
-graphs introduced by Kumjian and Pask in
[
KP00
]
are rank-
k
generalizations of directed graphs, and have
C^{*}
-algebras formed by similar generators and relations. So it’s tempting to ask whether these
k
-graph algebras are also Cuntz–Pimsner algebras. It’s certainly too naïve to try for a correspondence over functions on the vertices — any such correspondence would be isomorphic to one associated to a graph, as shown in
[
KPQ12
]
.

But this is not a serious obstacle;
Raeburn and Sims showed in
[
RS05
]
that there is a product system, over
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, of correspondences, and Fowler showed us in
[
Fow02
]
how to construct an associated
C^{*}
-algebra.
This product system is constructed from the edges in the
k
dimensions.

It would still be good to have a correspondence whose Cuntz–Pimsner algebra is isomorphic to the
k
-graph algebra.
Kumjian, Pask, and Sims (see
[
KPS11
]
) gave one solution to this problem: use the edges in dimension
k
to give a correspondence over the
C^{*}
-algebra of the
(k-1)
-graph formed by the edges in the first
k-1
dimensions.
They then used a linking algebra to prove that the associated Cuntz–Pimsner algebra is isomorphic to the
k
-graph algebra. This process could clearly be iterated downward
k
times.
Something along the
same lines (but with fewer restrictions on the
k
-graphs) is done in
[
Fle19
]
.

Our purpose in this paper is to introduce a new method, which proceeds from the bottom up rather than the top down. We begin with Pimsner’s theorem to get the algebra of the graph in dimension 1 as a Cuntz–Pimsner algebra. To continue, we apply a technique derived from a functor developed by the second author (see
[
Ery22
]
), using an inductive process
k
times with the aim of arriving at the
k
-graph algebra.
There is an extra step required each time: the construction derived from the functor produces a correspondence that is not directly applicable to
k
-graphs. To fix this, we must apply a tool introduced by the first author (see
[
Dea07
]
) to reformulate the correspondence in terms of product systems. Actually, in its original form, this tool is applicable only for 2-graphs. To complete our iterative process we generalize this tool to
k
-graph algebras.

There is a subtlety here: what our iterative process produces are
C^{*}
-algebras of product systems over powers of
\mathbb{N}
.
To get the higher-rank graph algebras
we apply the Raeburn–Sims theorem.
However, the initial output of the process gives us something more general.
In fact, our inductive process can be regarded as producing the algebra of a given product system over
\mathbb{N}^{k}
in
k
steps.
The product systems associated to
k
-graphs are really only special cases.
In fact, this can be seen already when
k=2
:
the third and fourth authors, in collaboration with Patani (see
[
KPQ13
]
), have shown that
there is an obstruction preventing arbitrary product systems over
\mathbb{N}^{2}
from being 2-graph product systems.
In this way, our method is somewhat more general than
k
-graphs.

In the above remarks we glossed over the connection with category theory. The functor introduced by the second author could, in principle, be used to give a categorical procedure. However, due to the nature of the relevant categories, this would involve a lot of passing back and forth between correspondences and their isomorphism classes. So in the end we reluctantly decided to forego the functorial approach. Nevertheless, the iterative process itself is most clearly formulated categorically, and in Section
3
we present the abstract procedure;
however, we stress that this categorical formulation is not used in the main body of our paper, and is included purely for illustrative purposes.

In Section
2

we state our conventions regarding
k
-graphs, correspondences,
product systems, and the associated
C^{*}
-algebras.
We emphasize that we impose fairly stringent conditions on our
k
-graphs and correspondences: we require them to be nondegenerate, and also

regular
in the sense
that the
k
-graphs must be row-finite and source-free,
and the correspondences must have left module homomorphism
that is injective into the compacts.
Presumably these assumptions could be relaxed,
but we believe that the clearest exposition of our methods requires the regularity assumption.

Section
3
contains (as we mentioned above) a quite brief illustration of our method in the abstract realm of category theory, which we again emphasize is not used in the formulation of our main results.
In Section
4
we give a precise description of our iterative process, using what we call

generating systems
,
by which we mean that we work exclusively with the correspondences associated to the generators
e_{i}
of
\mathbb{N}^{k}
.
The main technical tool we need involves the
“tensor groupoids” of Fowler–Sims, although we use the more traditional terminology “monoidal categories”.
Our main result is Corollary
4.4
, showing that the
k
-graph algebra is a Cuntz–Pimsner algebra. As mentioned above, we deduce this as a special case of the general version for product systems (Theorem
4.3
).
The details of the proof of this proposition are somewhat messy, and to avoid interrupting the flow we relegate many of them to Section
5
.
We further relegate the proofs of a few technical lemmas to an appendix.

A recent preprint

[
aHNS
]

uses two iterations of a version of this Cuntz–Pimsner procedure (they refer to it as the “Deaconu–Fletcher constructions”) to prove K-theory results for 2-graphs.

A concluding remark:
given the wide interest in Leavitt path algebras,
which are purely algebraic versions of graph
C^{*}
-algebras,
it seems natural to try to do something along the lines of the present paper
for Kumjian–Pask algebras
[
CFaH14
]
,
which are higher-rank versions of Leavitt path algebras.
We leave this to the experts in the Leavitt path algebra world.

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(a)=\pi_{\mathscr{E}{Y_{1}}}(\pi_{Y_{1}}(a)).
