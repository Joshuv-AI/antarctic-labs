Unveiling the transcriptomic landscape of coronavirus disease 2019 and psoriasis via systems biology-based analysis
Authors: Yuanyuan Zhang, Xi Xie, Tingting Dai, Wei Li, Wen Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIncreasing evidence shows that, compared with the general population, the prevalence of coronavirus disease 2019 is significantly higher in patients with psoriasis treated with immunosuppressive therapy. However, the underlying mechanisms have not yet been clarified.Materials and methodsThe aim of this study was to further investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying these two diseases. Gene expression profiles for coronavirus disease 2019 and psoriasis were downloaded from the gene expression omnibus database (GSE150316 and GSE30999). After identifying the common differentially expressed genes between coronavirus disease 2019 and psoriasis, functional annotation, protein-protein interaction network analysis, module construction, and hub gene identification were performed. Finally, transcription factor-gene regulatory and transcription factor-miRNA regulatory networks of the hub genes were constructed.ResultsA total of 306 common differentially expressed genes, including 168 upregulated genes and 138 downregulated genes, were identified and used for subsequent analysis. According to Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes enrichment analysis, the Rap1 signaling pathway, axon guidance, and focal adhesion contribute to the occurrence and development of coronavirus disease 2019 and psoriasis. Finally, five hub genes, namely EGF, IL1B, SERPINA1, CD8A, and WNT5A, were identified using the CytoHubba plug-in. Among them, three hub genes (EGF, SERPINA1, and CD8A) showed good diagnostic marker value for coronavirus disease 2019 and psoriasis.ConclusionOur findings suggest that coronavirus disease 2019 and psoriasis share common molecular mechanisms that are driven by several specific hub genes. This study provides new insights into the relationship between coronavirus disease 2019 and psoriasis.Keywords: COVID-19, psoriasis, differentially expressed genes, hub genes, pathogenic mechanisms

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory skin disease mediated by the immune system, which is characterized by scaly, erythematous, well-defined plaques on the skin. It is associated with many other medical conditions and affects nearly 125 million people worldwide. 1 Many factors contribute to the development of psoriasis, with genetic factors playing an important role in its pathogenesis. Psoriasis susceptibility gene 1 (PSORS1) is located within an approximately 220 kb fragment of the major histocompatibility complex on chromosome 6p21 and is a major susceptibility locus for psoriasis.2–4 In genetically susceptible individuals, various triggers can induce the disease. Approximately one-sixth of patients with psoriasis have moderate to severe disease requiring systemic treatment, such as biologics. 5 The efficacy of biologic agents in the treatment of psoriasis has been widely recognized. Unlike conventional systemic therapies, biologic agents target specific inflammatory pathways associated with the disease. Currently, two major pathways are targeted by biologic therapies for psoriasis: the IL-23/Th17 axis and TNF-α signaling. 6 Infliximab, adalimumab, and certolizumab pegol are used to treat psoriasis by inhibiting TNF-α. 7 Ustekinumab, guselkumab, risankizumab, and tildrakizumab are used to treat psoriasis by inhibiting IL-23.

Since 2019, the outbreak of novel coronavirus pneumonia (COVID-19), caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), has had catastrophic consequences for global health and the economy. 8 Because COVID-19 is characterized by a highly inflammatory state, it is necessary to explore its relationship with psoriasis. Although several studies have reported an increased risk of SARS-CoV-2 infection and a more severe disease course in patients with immune-mediated and autoimmune diseases,8–10 others have not supported these findings.11–16 During the pandemic, there were significant uncertainties regarding the management of patients requiring systemic therapies. Because immunosuppressive therapy may interfere with antiviral immunity, 17 patients receiving immunomodulatory therapy, particularly those with severe comorbidities, may be more likely to experience adverse outcomes from COVID-19. Conversely, excessive activation of the immune system contributes to the lung injury caused by SARS-CoV-2, and some patients may benefit from immunosuppressive therapy. 18

Genetic factors, including polymorphisms in cytokine genes such as IL19, IL20, and IL24, play crucial roles in psoriasis susceptibility. Association analyses have linked single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in these genes to palmoplantar pustulosis and plaque-type psoriasis, suggesting shared inflammatory pathways.19,20 Additionally, seasonal variations influence physiology, even in laboratory animals, with studies showing elevated cholecystokinin (CCK) receptor density during summer that affects stress responses, which may be relevant to the seasonal patterns of COVID-19. 21 Hematological markers such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) also aid in detecting inflammation in COVID-19, highlighting diagnostic gaps in combined analyses. 22

Therefore, investigating the shared transcriptional profiles of psoriasis and COVID-19 may provide new insights into their relationship. However, existing studies have often overlooked the comprehensive integration of known psoriasis-related genes (e.g. IL19/IL20/IL24), seasonal influences on disease patterns, and hematological inflammatory markers such as NLR/PLR. In this study, we analyzed the GSE150316 and GSE30999 datasets from the gene expression omnibus (GEO) database to identify common differentially expressed genes (DEGs), screen hub genes using bioinformatics approaches, and construct transcription factor (TF)-gene and TF-miRNA regulatory networks while discussing known psoriasis-related genes, seasonal effects, and previous literature. This study provides new insights for further research into the relationship between COVID-19 and psoriasis.

Materials and methods

Raw data collection

GEO (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo) 23 is a public database that contains a large number of high-throughput sequencing and microarray datasets submitted by research groups worldwide. To investigate the genetic interaction between COVID-19 and psoriasis, two RNA sequencing datasets were downloaded from GEO, representing COVID-19 and psoriasis samples, respectively. The registration number of COVID-19 dataset is GSE150316, 24 which contains 52 COVID-19 samples and 5 negative control samples through high-throughput sequencing Illumina MiSeq and Illumina NextSeq 500 platform for extracting RNA sequence. The psoriasis dataset with the registration number GSE30999 25 contains 85 psoriasis lesions (LS) samples and 85 nonlesional skin samples, which is based on the GPL570 (HG-U133_Plus_2) Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array platform. Detailed information on sample collection, processing, quality control, and sequencing preparation has been published elsewhere by the data initiator.24,25

Identification of DEGs

We used the Limma package (version 3.40.6, https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/limma.html) of R software (version 4.3.1, https://www.r-project.org/) to observe the differential expression of mRNAs. To correct for false-positive results in the GEO datasets, we performed an analysis of the p-values. The thresholds for differentially expressed of mRNAs were set as “p <0.05 and Fold Change (FC) ≥ 2.” We removed probe sets which without corresponding gene symbols or genes with more than one probe set. Then we used the online Venn diagram tool to get the overlapping DEGs.

Enrichment analyses of DEGs

To elucidate the underlying mechanisms shared between COVID-19 and psoriasis, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed. KEGG is a widely used database for gene function analysis that links genomic information with higher-order functional information. GO provides information on gene function in terms of biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular component (CC). The ClusterProfiler package (version 3.14.3, http://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/clusterProfiler.html) 26 was used to present the above enrichment analysis results. p value <0.05 was considered valuable.

Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network construction and module analysis

By using a search tool for Interacting Genes (STRING; http://string-db.org) (version 11.5) 27 was used to explore interactions among the target proteins. The composite score of the interaction greater than 0.4 was used as an indicator to evaluate whether there was statistical significance. We used Cytoscape (version 3.9.0; http://www.cytoscape.org) 28 to visualize this PPI network. Cytoscape's plug-in molecular complex detection technology (MCODE) was used to analyze key functional modules. Set the selection criteria as: K-core = 2, degree cutoff = 2, max depth = 100, and node score cutoff = 0.2.

Selection and analysis of hub genes

Hub genes were identified using the CytoHubba plug-in in Cytoscape. Five commonly used algorithms (maximal neighborhood component (MNC), Degree, Closeness, Radiality, and edge percolation component (EPC)) were applied to evaluate and select the hub genes. Subsequently, a coexpression network of the hub genes was constructed using GeneMANIA (http://www.genemania.org/), 29 which is a set of reliable identification of a gene within the associated tools.

Construction of TF-gene interactions

TF is a set of protein molecules which can specifically bind to the specific sequence upstream of the 5′ end of the gene, so that the target gene can be expressed in a particular time and space with a particular intensity. We analyzed the interaction between hub genes and TFs through NetworkAnalyst 3.0 (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/) 30 to evaluate the influence of TFs on the expression and functional pathways of the hub genes. TF-target interactions were obtained from the JASPAR TF-binding profile database (http://jaspar.genereg.net/). 31

TF-miRNA coregulatory network analysis

MiRNA is a set of small endogenous RNA of about 20 to 24 nucleotides, which can regulate the target mRNA by disrupting its stability and inhibiting its translation. By analyzing the transcriptional regulatory network between TF and miRNA, we can understand the gene expression imbalance under different physiological and disease conditions. We used NetworkAnalyst 3.0 to build the TF-miRNA coregulatory network, and from RegNetwork (http://www.regnetworkweb.org/) access to literature retrieval to the regulation of information interaction. 32

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves of hub genes

To evaluate the diagnostic performance of the hub genes for COVID-19 and psoriasis, two independent validation datasets (GSE171110 and GSE13355) were selected for ROC curve analysis. The analysis was performed using the pROC package (version 1.17.0.1, http://expasy.org/tools/pROC). 33

Results

Identification of DEGs

The research flowchart of this research was shown in Figure 1. After standardization of the microarray results, DEGs (5781 in GSE150316 and 2082 in GSE30999) were identified (Figure 2(a) and (b)). Through Venn diagram calculation, we obtained 168 overlapping upregulated genes and 138 overlapping downregulated genes shared between GSE150316 and GSE30999 (Figure 2(c) and (d)).

Analysis of the functional characteristics of common DEGs

GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were performed on these overlapping genes to analyze their biological functions and pathways associated with the common DEGs. GO analysis results showed that, for BP, the DEGs were significantly enriched in synapse assembly, regulation of synapse assembly, and excitatory synapse assembly (Figure 3(a)). For CC, the DEGs were mainly enriched in the glutamatergic synapse, synaptic membrane, and collagen-containing extracellular matrix (Figure 3(a)). For MF, the DEGs were primarily enriched in ephrin receptor activity, transmembrane ephrin receptor activity, and transmembrane receptor protein tyrosine kinase activity (Figure 3(a)). KEGG pathway analysis revealed that the DEGs were mainly enriched in the Rap1 signaling pathway, axon guidance, and focal adhesion (Figure 3(b)).

DEGs: differentially expressed genes; GO: gene ontology; KEGG: Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes.

PPI network construction and module analysis

A PPI network was constructed using the overlapping DEGs with a combined interaction score >0.4 using Cytoscape. The network consisted of 199 nodes and 301 pairs work against each other (Figure 4). Three tightly connected gene modules, including 24 common DEGs and 47 interaction pairs, were obtained by Cytoscape's MCODE plug-in (Figure 5(a) to (c)). GO analysis indicated that these genes were associated with fatty acid transport, positive regulation of the immune effector process, platelet alpha granule, secretory granule lumen, low-density lipoprotein particle binding, and transforming growth factor-beta binding (Figure 5(d)). KEGG pathway analysis showed that these genes were mainly involved in Malaria, PPAR signaling pathway, and hematopoietic cell lineage (Figure 5(e)).

Selection and analysis of hub genes

Through the five algorithms of plug-in CytoHubba, we have calculated the top 10 hub genes (Table 1). After taking the intersection of the Venn diagrams, we found five overlapping hub genes, including EGF, IL1B, SERPINA1, CD8A, and WNT5A (Figure 6(a)). Table 2 illustrates their full names and associated functions. The coexpression networks and related functions of these genes were analyzed by GeneMANIA database. These genes showed the complex PPI. The resulting network consisted of 77.64% physical interactions, 8.01% coexpression, 5.37% predicted interactions, 3.63% colocalization, 2.87% genetic interactions, 1.88% pathway associations, and 0.60% shared protein domains (Figure 6(b)). These genes were mainly associated with the response to interleukin-1, cellular response to interleukin-1, and clathrin-coated vesicles (Figure 6(b)).

ROC curves of hub genes

To evaluate the diagnostic performance of the five hub genes, ROC curves. In the COVID-19 dataset, EGF (AUC = 0.936), SERPINA1 (AUC = 0.864), and CD8A (AUC = 0.936) demonstrated good diagnostic performance in distinguishing patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection from healthy controls (Figure 9(a)). In the psoriasis dataset, EGF (AUC = 0.876), IL1B (AUC = 0.959), SERPINA1 (AUC = 0.981), CD8A (AUC = 0.869), and WNT5A (AUC = 1.000) demonstrated good diagnostic performance (Figure 9(b)).

Discussion

Our analysis identified shared transcriptomic mechanisms between COVID-19 and psoriasis that are centered on the hub genes EGF, IL1B, SERPINA1, CD8A, and WNT5A. These findings are consistent with previous reports of genetic associations involving the IL-19 subfamily, in which IL20 polymorphisms increase the risk of psoriasis, 20 and haplotypes in IL19, IL20, and IL24 increase susceptibility to related conditions such as palmoplantar pustulosis. 19 However, our cross-disease analysis extends previous psoriasis-focused studies by highlighting overlapping inflammatory and interferon-related responses, suggesting shared molecular mechanisms that may contribute to the comorbidity between COVID-19 and psoriasis.

Compared with previous bioinformatics studies, 34 our KEGG pathways (e.g. Rap1 signaling pathway and focal adhesion) extend beyond broad overviews in existing GEO-based research. For example, although an integrated analysis of 23 psoriasis datasets identified dysregulation of housekeeping genes, 35 our study challenges this by incorporating COVID-19 transcriptomic data and identified shared hub genes, including IL1B in shared inflammation, potentially explaining increased COVID-19 severity in psoriasis patients. Similarly, single-cell sequencing in psoriasis identified hub genes for drug targeting,36,37 whereas our systems biology framework questions the generalizability by linking to viral responses, suggesting repurposed therapies.

Seasonal fluctuations, as demonstrated in rat models with elevated CCK and 5-HT2 receptors in summer leading to reduced exploration, 21 may parallel COVID-19' seasonal peaks, challenging nonseasonal models and implying stress-related immune interactions exacerbate psoriasis flares during pandemics. 38 This contrasts with transcriptomic studies ignoring seasonality.

In addition, hematological markers such as the NLR and PLR, elevated in severe COVID-19 and predictive of thrombosis, 22 complement our hub genes’ roles in inflammation and coagulation. Unlike COVID-only reviews,39,40 our work challenges the field by integrating these with psoriasis inflammasome activation and WNT5A signaling. However, there are some limitations in this study. First, this is analysis research based on sequencing data and public database. Second, although some previous studies have confirmed the roles of these hub genes in COVID-19 and psoriasis, they should be further verified in experimental animal model. Finally, the regulatory network we identified needs further functional experiments to verify.

Conclusions

In summary, we identified common DEGs shared between COVID-19 and psoriasis and conduct the enrichment and PPI network analyses. Our research finds that there are some common pathogenic mechanisms between COVID-19 and psoriasis, which are mediated by specific five hub genes including EGF, IL1B, SERPINA1, CD8A, and WNT5A. This study provides a potential idea for further research on the relationship between COVID-19 and psoriasis.
