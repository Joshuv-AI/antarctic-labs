New Solutions of RG Equations for α s \alpha_{s} and y t ​ o ​ p y_{top}


Abstract
We construct simple analytical solutions of the RG equations for the running couplings α s \alpha_{s} and y t ​ o ​ p y_{top} in the asymptotic regime. These solutions have an explicit form, contain only logarithms and no special functions, and subsequently sum up the leading, subleading, etc logarithms in all orders of PT. While the effect of y t ​ o ​ p y_{top} on the running of α s \alpha_{s} happens to be negligible, the role of α s \alpha_{s} on the running of y t ​ o ​ p y_{top} is essential, the account of higher orders gives a noticeable contribution.

1
Introduction

In a recent paper
[
1
]
we proposed a strategy that allows one to sum up the leading, NL, NNL, etc logs for the running coupling, resulting in a relatively simple scheme that provides an explicit analytical form at each stage. We demonstrated how this strategy works in a theory with a single coupling like QCD. In this note, we extend the proposed approach to the case of two couplings, namely we consider the real case of the Standard Model with the strong and top Yukawa couplings. All the other couplings are relatively small and we ignore them in our analysis.

Remind the main issues of the advocated approach. Our main point is not to solve the RG equations written in a given order of PT exactly but first to linearise them and then solve the
linear equations in subsequent orders. These new explicit solutions provide a summation of the corresponding logs in the Leading, Nex-to-Leading, etc approximation. In a theory with a single coupling, this results in a regular expansion containing only elementary logs and no other special functions. Moreover, further summation and improvement are possible, leading to the same logs but with more complex arguments
[
1
]
.
It should be stressed that the usual approach to solving the non-linear RG equations in higher orders does not allow for analytical solutions and sums not only the corresponding log terms but also includes some amount of subleading logs (see e.g. Appendix in
[
2
]
). The advantage of our approach is that at each stage we sum just the needed logs and do it analytically.

The case of two couplings happens to be more complicated since the one-loop RG equation for the Yukawa coupling has a tricky solution even though it contains only logarithms. As a result, the solutions of our linearised equations in higher orders contain, in addition to the logarithmic part, a relatively small addendum described by a special function. In what follows, we ignore this part in the asymptotic regime due to its numerical suppression. A relatively simple analytic form allows for further summation of the leading terms, just like in the one-coupling case, which results in more stable asymptotic solutions.

Perturbative Expansion and New Solutions

Consider the Standard Model where only the strong gauge and top Yukawa interaction are taken into account and all the other couplings are ignored. Then the RG equations for the running couplings have the form

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha(L)}{dL}

3
RG Invariant Parametrization

It is useful to rewrite the obtained solutions expressing them via RG invariants. In the case of a single coupling theory like QCD, it is the well-known invariant
\Lambda

[
7
,
8
]
. In the case of two couplings, one has the other invariant
K
which characterises the curve in the phase space of these couplings. Both parameters vary with the number of loops.

3.1
\ell=1:\hat{L}_{1},K_{1}

The one-loop solutions can be rewritten in terms of scale invariants as

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{-\beta_{0}\hat{L}_{1}},

(21)

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0}}{\beta_{0}\gamma_{01}\hat{L}_{1}+K_{1}(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})\hat{L}_{1}^{\nu}},

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L-\dfrac{1}{\beta_{0}\alpha_{0}}=\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda_{1}^{2}),\ \ \ K_{1}=\dfrac{\alpha_{0}(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})+y_{0}\gamma_{01}}{\alpha_{0}y_{0}(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})}(-\beta_{0}\alpha_{0})^{\nu}.

(23)

Practical significance of such formulation if that now
\Lambda
and
K

do not depend on the renomalization scale

\mu
along the RG curve and actually parametrise the curve itself.

One can see that all curves merge at high energies but deviate closer to the pole where the perturbation theory fails. At the same time, the more terms of expansion are taken into account the smoother is the behaviour of the curve.

4
Vertical Summation

So far, we have obtained the following “reduced” form of our solutions:

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{-\beta_{0}\hat{L}},

(36)

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{2}\zeta_{1},

(37)

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{0}^{2}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{3}\zeta_{1}^{2}+\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{2}(\beta_{10}\hat{\alpha}_{1}+\beta_{01}\hat{y}_{1})\zeta_{1}+\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}(c_{5}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{2}+c_{6}\hat{\alpha}_{1}\hat{y}_{1}+c_{7}\hat{y}_{1}^{2}),

(38)

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0}}{\beta_{0}\gamma_{01}\hat{L}+K(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})\hat{L}^{\nu}},

(39)

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{1}(\gamma_{10}\hat{\alpha}_{1}+\gamma_{01}\hat{y}_{1})\zeta_{1}+\hat{y}_{1}(c_{3}\hat{\alpha}_{1}+c_{4}\hat{y}_{1}),

(40)

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{1}(\gamma_{01}\hat{y}_{1}^{2}+\dfrac{3}{2}\gamma_{10}\hat{y}_{1}\hat{\alpha}_{1}+\dfrac{\gamma_{10}(\gamma_{10}+\beta_{0})}{2\gamma_{01}}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{2})\zeta_{1}^{2}

(41)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{1}(c_{8}\hat{y}_{1}^{2}+c_{9}\hat{y}_{1}\hat{\alpha}_{1}+c_{10}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{2})\zeta_{1}+\hat{y}_{1}(c_{11}\hat{y}_{1}^{2}+c_{12}\hat{y}_{1}\hat{\alpha}_{1}+c_{13}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{2}),

\displaystyle\dots

with

\zeta_{1}=c_{1}\ln\hat{\alpha}_{1}+c_{2}\ln\hat{y}_{1}.

We now try to sum the ”vertical” infinite series of
\ln\hat{\alpha}_{1}
and
\ln\hat{y}_{1}
. We denote the resulting expressions as
\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(1)}
and
\hat{y}_{\ell}^{(1)}
. It turns out that they are given by the same functions with a replacement of arguments.
It fact,
\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(1)}
and
\hat{y}_{\ell}^{(1)}
repeat the form of
\alpha_{\ell}^{R}
and
y_{\ell}^{R}
with the replacement:

\displaystyle\alpha_{\ell}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{\ell}^{R}(L\to\zeta_{1},\alpha_{0}\to\hat{\alpha}_{1},y_{0}\to\hat{y}_{1}),

(42)

\displaystyle y_{\ell}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y_{\ell}^{R}(L\to\zeta_{1},\alpha_{0}\to\hat{\alpha}_{1},y_{0}\to\hat{y}_{1}),

(43)

which can be checked directly by expanding
\alpha_{\ell}^{R}
and
y_{\ell}^{R}
in a series of
L,\alpha_{0},y_{0}
. Alternatively, we can write equations (
42
,
43
) in terms of
\hat{\alpha}_{1},\hat{y}_{1}
:

\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}=\dfrac{\hat{\alpha}_{1}}{1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}\zeta_{1}},\\[10.0pt]
\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}=\dfrac{(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})\hat{\alpha}_{1}{\hat{y}_{1}}}{-\gamma_{01}{\hat{y}_{1}}(1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}\zeta_{1})+(\hat{\alpha}_{1}(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})+\gamma_{01}\hat{y}_{1})(1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}\zeta_{1})^{\scalebox{1.}{$\nu$}}},\\[20.0pt]
\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(1)}=\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\left[\hat{\alpha}_{1}\to\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)},\,\hat{y}_{1}\to\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)},\,\ln\hat{\alpha}_{1}\to\ln\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1},\,\ln\hat{y}_{1}\to\ln\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{y}_{1}\right],\\[8.0pt]
\hat{y}_{\ell}^{(1)}=\hat{y}_{\ell}\left[\hat{\alpha}_{1}\to\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)},\,\hat{y}_{1}\to\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)},\,\ln\hat{\alpha}_{1}\to\ln\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1},\,\ln\hat{y}_{1}\to\ln\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{y}_{1}\right],\end{array}

(44)

which can be formally rewritten as

\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}=\dfrac{\hat{\alpha}_{1}}{1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}\zeta_{1}},\\[10.0pt]
\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}=\dfrac{(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})\hat{\alpha}_{1}{\hat{y}_{1}}}{-\gamma_{01}{\hat{y}_{1}}(1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}\zeta_{1})+(\hat{\alpha}_{1}(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})+\gamma_{01}\hat{y}_{1})(1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}\zeta_{1})^{\scalebox{1.}{$\nu$}}},\\[10.0pt]
\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(1)}=\Xi_{1}^{\ell}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\left[\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\Xi_{1},\,\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\Xi_{1}\right],\\[8.0pt]
\hat{y}_{\ell}^{(1)}=\Xi_{1}^{\ell}\hat{y}_{\ell}\left[\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\Xi_{1},\,\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\Xi_{1}\right],\\[10.0pt]
\Xi_{1}=\left(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{c_{1}}\hat{y}_{1}^{c_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{c_{1}+c_{2}}}.\end{array}

(45)

One should not be confused by the appearance of the object
\Xi_{1}
with non-integer powers of couplings, since they disappear when substituted inside the logarithms. Indeed,
\ln\Xi_{1}=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{1}+c_{2}}\ln\hat{\alpha}_{1}+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}+c_{2}}\ln\hat{y}_{1}
and powers of
\Xi_{1}
in (
45
) just cancel. This can be seen from the explicit expressions in the lowest orders:

\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\alpha}_{2}^{(1)}=\beta_{0}(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)})^{2}(c_{1}\ln(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1})+c_{2}\ln(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{y}_{1})),\\[10.0pt]
\hat{y}_{2}^{(1)}=\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}(\gamma_{10}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}+\gamma_{01}\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)})(c_{1}\ln(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1})+c_{2}\ln(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{y}_{1}))+\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}(c_{3}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}+c_{4}\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}),\\[10.0pt]
\hat{\alpha}_{3}^{(1)}=\beta_{0}^{2}(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)})^{3}(c_{1}\ln(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1})+c_{2}\ln(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{y}_{1})^{2}\\[8.0pt]
\qquad+(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)})^{2}(\beta_{10}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}+\beta_{01}\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)})(c_{1}\ln(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1})+c_{2}\ln(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{y}_{1}))\\[8.0pt]
\qquad+\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}(c_{5}(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)})^{2}+c_{6}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}+c_{7}(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)})^{2}),\\[10.0pt]
\hat{y}_{3}^{(1)}=\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}(\gamma_{01}(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)})^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\gamma_{10}\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}+\frac{\gamma_{10}(\gamma_{10}+\beta_{0})}{2\gamma_{01}}(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)})^{2})(c_{1}\ln(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1})+c_{2}\ln(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{y}_{1}))^{2}\\[10.0pt]
\qquad+\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}\left(c_{8}(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)})^{2}+c_{9}\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}+c_{10}(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)})^{2}\right)(c_{1}\ln(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1})+c_{2}\ln(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{y}_{1}))\\[10.0pt]
\qquad+\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}(c_{11}(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)})^{2}+c_{12}\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}+c_{13}(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)})^{2}).\end{array}

(46)

Since the functions appearing at the level ”1” are the same as the original solutions, the logarithmic terms inside
\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(1)}
and
\hat{y}_{\ell}^{(1)}
can once again be vertically summated. One has, respectively,

\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(2)}=\dfrac{\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}}{1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}\zeta_{2}},\\[10.0pt]
\hat{y}_{1}^{(2)}=\dfrac{(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}{\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}}}{-\gamma_{01}{\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}}(1-\beta\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}\zeta_{2})+\left(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})+\gamma_{01}\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}\right)(1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}\zeta_{2})^{\scalebox{1.}{$\nu$}}},\\[10.0pt]
\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(2)}=\Xi_{2}^{\ell}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\left[\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(2)}/\Xi_{2},\,\hat{y}_{1}^{(2)}/\Xi_{2}\right],\\[8.0pt]
\hat{y}_{\ell}^{(2)}=\Xi_{2}^{\ell}\hat{y}_{\ell}\left[\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(2)}/\Xi_{2},\,\hat{y}_{1}^{(2)}/\Xi_{2}\right],\\[10.0pt]
\zeta_{2}=c_{1}\ln(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1})+c_{2}\ln(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)}/\hat{y}_{1}),\ \ \ \Xi_{2}=\left((\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(1)})^{c_{1}}(\hat{y}_{1}^{(1)})^{c_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{c_{1}+c_{2}}}.\end{array}

(47)

Moreover, we can perform this as many times as needed (here
n>2
):

\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n)}=\dfrac{\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-1)}}{1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-1)}\zeta_{n}},\\[10.0pt]
\hat{y}_{1}^{(n)}=\dfrac{(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-1)}{\hat{y}_{1}^{(n-1)}}}{-\gamma_{01}{\hat{y}_{1}^{(n-1)}}(1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-1)}\zeta_{n})+(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-1)}(\gamma_{10}-\beta_{0})+\gamma_{01}\hat{y}_{1}^{(n-1)})(1-\beta_{0}\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-1)}\zeta_{n})^{\scalebox{1.}{$\nu$}}},\\[10.0pt]
\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(n)}=\Xi_{n}^{\ell}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}\left[\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n)}/\Xi_{n},\,\hat{y}_{1}^{(n)}/\Xi_{n}\right],\\[8.0pt]
\hat{y}_{\ell}^{(n)}=\Xi_{n}^{\ell}\hat{y}_{\ell}\left[\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n)}/\Xi_{n},\,\hat{y}_{1}^{(n)}/\Xi_{n}\right],\\[10.0pt]
\zeta_{n}=c_{1}\ln(\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-1)}/\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-2)})+c_{2}\ln(\hat{y}_{1}^{(n-1)}/\hat{y}_{1}^{(n-2)}),\ \ \ \Xi_{n}=\left((\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-1)})^{c_{1}}(\hat{y}_{1}^{(n-1)})^{c_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{c_{1}+c_{2}}}.\end{array}

(48)

One can notice the similarity between
\Xi_{n}
and
\zeta_{n}
. Indeed, the connection between the two can be expressed as follows:

\zeta_{n}=\left(c_{1}+c_{2}\right)\ln\left(\dfrac{\Xi_{n}}{\Xi_{n-1}}\right)\quad<=>\quad\Xi_{n}=\exp\left(\dfrac{\zeta_{1}+\ldots+\zeta_{n}}{c_{1}+c_{2}}\right).

(49)

Note that if we go back to the case of the absence of
y
in the beta-function for
\alpha
, we get

\beta_{01}=0\,=>\,c_{1}=\beta_{10}/\beta_{0}^{2},\,c_{2}=0\,=>\,\Xi_{n}=\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{(n-1)}.

These formulas allow further improvement of the approximation due to further summation of the vertical infinite series, and this procedure continues endlessly. At each step, one has the same functions but with different arguments and one can cut this process at a point when a new term of the beta-function appears. For the calculated N-loop beta-functions one has for the best approximation

\bar{\alpha}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(N-1)},\ \ \bar{y}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\hat{y}_{\ell}^{(N-1)},

(50)

where
\hat{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(n)}
and
\hat{y}_{\ell}^{(n)}
are given by equations (
48
).

One can notice the unusual bending of

5
Conclusion

To conclude, we have demonstrated that choosing the right strategy, one can get relatively simple explicit formulas for the running coupling expansion in the asymptotic regime. These formulas contain only logarithms and no special functions, and correspond to the summation of infinite series of leading, next-to-leading, etc logarithms.
The case of two couplings appeared to be more complicated, and one has to neglect certain terms that do not allow a description in terms of elementary functions. However, in the asymptotic regime that we consider, these terms give an inessential contribution. The strategy allows further improvement of the approximation by summing the ”vertical” series of terms, which are described by the same functions with a change of arguments.
