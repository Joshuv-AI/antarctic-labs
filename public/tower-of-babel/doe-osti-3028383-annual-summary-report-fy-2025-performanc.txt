 Executive Summary
Beginning in 1996, the Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility (ERDF) started
accepting low-level radioactive, hazardous, and mixed wastes that were generated during
cleanup activities at the Hanford Site. ERDF is composed of a series of cells or disposal
areas and can accommodate future design expansions as needed. Currently, there are
eight cells and two supercells in ERDF. Each supercell is the equivalent of two cells.
During this reporting period, fiscal year (FY) 2025, extending from October 1, 2024,
through September 30, 2025, approximately 50,653 metric tons (55,835 U.S. tons) of
waste was disposed at ERDF. From ERDF inception through September 30, 2025,
approximately 17.3 million metric tons (19.1 million U.S. tons) of waste has been
disposed at ERDF, which equates to consumption of approximately 91.0% of
the currently constructed disposal volume. According to the design of ERDF, the facility
has the ability to be expanded as needed. In 2023, the Department of Energy approved
the construction of supercell 11 to provide additional disposal capacity to meet
the cleanup mission at the Hanford Site. The construction of supercell 11 began in
FY 2025, and it is expected to take up to two years. Construction of the new cell will not
affect ongoing waste disposal activities at ERDF.

DOE O 435.1, Radioactive Waste Management, and DOE M 435.1-1, Radioactive Waste
Management Manual, require that a determination of continued adequacy of
the performance assessment (PA) (CP-60089, Performance Assessment for the
Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility, Hanford, Washington, formerly WCH 520
Rev. 1), composite analysis (CA), and disposal authorization statement (DAS) be made
on an annual basis, and that the determination must consider the results of data
collection and analysis from research, field studies, and monitoring as well as the need to
update any Radioactive Waste Management Basis (RWMB) documents. As a condition
of the DAS, disposal operations within ERDF must be in accordance with the waste
acceptance criteria (ERDF-00011, Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility Waste
Acceptance Criteria) that provide specific radionuclide disposal limits, waste form
restrictions, and descriptions of acceptable waste packages in compliance with
the requirements of DOE M 435-1.1. The ERDF waste acceptance criteria stipulate that
waste destined for disposal at ERDF be controlled based on source, physical form, and
contaminant concentration and activity levels.

During this reporting period, no Unreviewed Disposal Question Evaluation (UDQE) or
Special Analysis was conducted and no significant changes were found.

Sum-of-fractions analysis shows that the disposed inventory meets the concentration
threshold limits based on the waste acceptance criteria. The disposed waste inventory for
primary dose-contributing radionuclides remains well under the PA-imposed limits, as
shown in Table 4 and Table 5 in the main text of this report.

Required monitoring was satisfactorily completed during the FY reporting period.

Overall, there are no substantive changes to primary PA assumptions or changes to
the PA analysis conclusion; therefore, compliance with DOE O 435.1 and the DAS
is maintained.

 1 Changes Potentially Affecting the Performance Assessment
This Annual Summary Report (ASR) outlines all potential or actual changes, discoveries, proposed
actions, and new information identified during the reporting period of fiscal year (FY) 2025 (October 1,
2024, through September 30, 2025) for the Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility (ERDF)
Performance Assessment (PA) (CP-60089, Performance Assessment for the Environmental Restoration
Disposal Facility, Hanford, Washington, formerly WCH-520 Rev. 1 [hereinafter called the ERDF PA]).

During this reporting period, no Unreviewed Disposal Question (UDQ) or Special Analysis was conducted
and no significant changes were found, as summarized in Table 1. While no significant changes were
found during this reporting period, the following aspects are noted that affect the current ERDF PA:

1. In FY 2024, a Special Analysis was performed to evaluate the effect of the additional disposal
 capacity from the planned construction of supercell 11. The analysis considered updated inventory
 estimates at closure due to additional capacity along with larger footprint over which the source
 release would occur. Additionally, the analysis used the latest available dose coefficients. The
 calculations were performed using the system model approved for ERDF PA. The All-Pathways peak
 dose during the 1,000 year compliance period was calculated to be 0.87 mrem/yr and for the
 postcompliance period was calculated to be 0.47 mrem/yr. Based on the results of the Special
 Analysis and the recommendations of the Low-Level Waste Disposal Facility Federal Review Group
 (LFRG) co-chairs, the Disposal Authorization Statement for ERDF was revised to authorize
 construction of Cell 11 (Ellis, 2024, Revised Disposal Authorization Statement for the Environmental
 Restoration Disposal Facility for Construction of Cell 11).
2. In early 2025, a complete revision of the ERDF PA was initiated because the original PA (CP-60089)
 was completed in 2013 and several changes have occurred since then with respect to the disposed
 inventory and waste volume estimates, dose coefficients, institutional control time period, hydraulic
 properties, waste area footprint (e.g., new supercell 11), etc. Significant progress has been achieved
 in developing the updated PA in 2025, and the effort will continue in FY 2026.

 2 Cumulative Effects of Changes
In accordance with DOE M 435.1-1, Radioactive Waste Management Manual, the purpose of this chapter
is to identify any cumulative effects of changes in facility operations, waste receipts, waste form behavior,
monitoring data, research and development (R&D) data, or land-use decisions during the reporting period
that have affected PA assumptions and conclusions, collectively representing the Radioactive Waste
Management Basis (RWMB).

The RWMB for ERDF is provided in CPCC-MP-WM-52872, Waste Management Basis. Appendix E of
CPCC-MP-WM-52872 provides the facility-specific information and documents.

No substantive changes have occurred in disposal facility operations, disposal facility performance, and
PA assumptions or results in this reporting period other than the planned start of construction of
supercell 11. The ERDF PA continues to be maintained on an annual basis. Appendix A provides a
maintenance history for this PA since its approval.

The composite analysis (CA) supporting this PA is reported in DOE/RL-2019-52, Composite Analysis for
Low-Level Waste Disposal in the Hanford Site Central Plateau (FY2020). The CA is maintained
separately under its own maintenance plan (DOE/RL-2000-29, Maintenance Plan for the Updated
Composite Analysis of the Hanford Site, Southeast Washington), and the concurrent ASR for the CA will
be provided in the forthcoming FY 2025 ASR for the CA.

 3 Waste Receipts
ERDF is a landfill authorized under the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and
Liability Act of 1980 (CERCLA) for disposal of wastes. Disposed waste is generated from remediation
of waste sites within the Hanford Site (EPA/ROD/R10-95/100, Record of Decision: U.S. DOE Hanford
Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility, Hanford Site, Benton County, Washington [hereinafter called
the ERDF Record of Decision (ROD)] (Figure 1)).

ERDF is constructed in a modular fashion so that added disposal capacity can be built on the eastern
side as needed 1 (Figure 2). The existing 10-cell configuration is evaluated in the ERDF PA (CP-60089).
ERDF is currently being expanded and the construction of supercell 11 (TPA-M-16-22-02, Establish New
Negotiated M-016 Series Milestones in TPA, Appendix D) is ongoing.

ERDF is operated for the disposal of CERCLA waste and specific non-CERCLA and Resource
Conservation and Recovery Act of 1976 (RCRA) past-practice waste, as identified in the ERDF ROD
(EPA/ROD/R10-95/100), originating from within the boundaries of the Hanford Site. Waste types received
at ERDF include contaminated soil from the CERCLA waste site cleanup activities, debris generated from
decontamination and decommissioning activities, and activated metal from solid waste burial grounds and
other locations.

The Waste Management Information System (WMIS) database provides waste disposal information for
the ERDF landfill. According to records in the WMIS database, during FY 2025 a total of 50,653 metric
tons (55,835 U.S. tons) of waste containing 1.12E+04Ci of inventory (decay-corrected to assumed
closure date of Year 2035) was disposed. Records also indicate that from ERDF inception through
the end of FY 2025, a total of 17.3 million metric tons (19.0 million U.S. tons) of waste has been disposed.
Table 2 presents a summary of the compiled waste receipts from inception through FY 2025, which
shows the continuous adequacy of the PA.

 4 Monitoring
Monitoring of water and air for contaminants (both radiological and chemical) is an ongoing program
across the Hanford Site. Groundwater monitoring wells and air sampling stations located around ERDF
are routinely monitored for contaminants. Monitoring associated with ERDF that meets the needs to
satisfy the DOE O 435.1, Radioactive Waste Management, requirements is specified in CP-60152,
Performance Assessment Monitoring Plan for the Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility, formerly
WCH-579 Rev. 1. No changes to the monitoring plan are required at present.

The latest summary of groundwater monitoring information (DOE/HFO-2024-41, Hanford Site
Groundwater Monitoring Report for 2024) describes data collected during calendar year (CY) 2024
(January 1 through December 31, 2024). It represents the latest available information for the purposes of
this ASR. Groundwater monitoring at ERDF is performed in accordance with CP-60092, Groundwater
Protection Plan for the Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility, formerly WCH-198 Rev. 1. To
evaluate the potential impacts from ERDF to groundwater quality, sampling results were compared to
the baseline conditions established when monitoring began in 1996 using the data from pre-operational
sampling events and a tolerance interval approach (Appendix B of CP-60092).

Table 6 summarizes the compliance monitoring results and comparisons against the drinking water
standard. One upgradient well (699-36-70A) and five downgradient wells (699-35-66A, 699-36-65,
699-36-66B, 699-37-66, and 699-37-67) were sampled semiannually in 2024. Several constituents
(tritium, iodine-129, nitrate, and carbon tetrachloride) are present in groundwater near or above

4.1 Air Monitoring
Air sampling in support of ERDF operations was conducted at five locations at or near the ERDF. These
locations included three project-specific stations and two upwind stations that are part of

years. Uranium-233/234 and uranium-238 were detected in approximately 10 percent and 20 percent of
the samples, respectively. All other radionuclides of concern were below analytical detection limits.

4.2 Leachate Monitoring
Leachate liquids are primarily generated from natural precipitation and the application of dust control
water that percolates downward through the disposed waste materials. Each of the ERDF cells were
constructed with a double-liner system designed to deliver leachate to sump areas. Leachate is collected
from the primary (upper) leachate collection layer sump and the secondary (lower) leak detection sump
for each of the ERDF cells. The leachate collected in the primary and secondary sumps is routinely
transferred from the sumps to on-site leachate storage tanks prior to transfer to the 200 West Pump
and Treat Facility. The leachate volumes for each cell (combined primary and secondary volumes),
the total leachate volumes, and precipitation are summarized annually in the sampling and monitoring
report (ERDF-00202).

Leachate generated at ERDF is collected from the cells and stored in two storage tanks. The leachate is
sampled to (1) provide data to maintain leachate delisting status, (2) provide confirmation that
the approved treatment facility acceptance criteria are continuing to be met, and (3) assess whether
additional contaminants of concern should be added to the routine ERDF groundwater monitoring
program. Leachate samples are collected from the holding tanks in accordance with CP-60070,
Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility Leachate Sampling and Analysis Plan, formerly
WCH-173 Rev. 2, and represent composite samples of leachate collected from all 10 cells. The sampled
constituents did not present a significant challenge to maintaining their delisting value.

As discussed in ERDF-00202, during CY 2024, the total leachate volume collected from each cell ranged
from 193,548 to 2,272,988 L (51,130 to 600,460 gal), and the total volume collected from all 10 cells was
8,195,920 L (2,165,133 gal). Small proportions of this leachate were collected from the secondary leak
detection sumps during CY 2024, ranging from 0 to 33,766 L (0 to 8,920 gal), with cell leakage rates
ranging from 0 to 1.56 gal/acre/day.

Leachate samples were collected in CY 2024 with a reduced frequency of twice a year (semiannual) with
concurrence of the U.S. Department of Energy and U.S. Environmental Protection Agency on October 27,
2021 (ERDF-00202). The sample analysis results show that leachate concentrations did not exceed
delisting levels for any detected constituents in CY 2024 (see Figure 5). One-tenth of the nitrate delisting
level (1,062 mg/L) was exceeded, with nitrate concentrations being between 253 and 275 mg/L. There

4.3 Groundwater Monitoring
The sitewide annual groundwater monitoring reports provide information on the groundwater
concentrations in and around ERDF. The latest available report is DOE/HFO-2024-41. Information from
this report is presented below.

Groundwater monitoring at ERDF is performed in accordance with CP-60092. The groundwater flow
direction is toward the east-northeast. One upgradient well (699-36-70A) and five downgradient wells
(699-35-66A 2, 699-36-65, 699-36-66B, 699-37-66, and 699-37-67; see Figure 6) were sampled
semiannually in 2024. To detect potential impacts to groundwater quality, sample results were compared
to baseline conditions established when monitoring began in 1996 using a tolerance interval approach
(Appendix B of CP-60092).

The results of ERDF groundwater monitoring continue to indicate that the facility has not affected
groundwater quality. Several constituents (tritium, iodine-129, nitrate, and carbon tetrachloride) are
present in groundwater near or above the 200-UP-1 Operable Unit cleanup levels, but these constituents
