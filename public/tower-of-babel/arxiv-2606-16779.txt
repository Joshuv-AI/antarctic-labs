Abstract
Using more than 600 thousand K S 0 → π + ​ π − K_{S}^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-} decays from the e + ​ e − → ϕ ⁡ ( 1020 ) → K S 0 ​ K L 0 e^{+}e^{-}\to\phi(1020)\to K_{S}^{0}K_{L}^{0} reaction, the neutral kaon mass has been measured with the CMD-3 detector at the VEPP-2000 collider. Using the beam energies control by the back-scattering laser light photons, and a calibration to the world average ϕ \phi -meson mass, the neutral kaon mass is determind to be m( K 0 K^{0} ) = 497.587 ± \pm 0.004(stat.) ± \pm 0.008(syst.) ± \pm 0.009(calibr.) MeV / c 2 \rm MeV/c^{2} , where the first uncertainty is statistical, the second is systematic, and the third is the uncertainty from the energy calibration.



1
Introduction

The neutral kaon mass was first measured in the 1960s with the optical spark
counters and the bubble chambers with about 0.3
\div
0.5
\rm MeV/c^{2}

accuracy
pdg
using neutral kaon beams or kaons from the
proton-anti-proton annihilation. A real breakthrough happened in the 1980s
when the neutral kaons became available at the
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders
with a precision measurement of the beam energies by the
resonance depolarization method
rdm
.
A clean kinematics in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\phi(1020)\to K_{S}^{0}K_{L}^{0}
reaction
allows one to develop a method to determine a kaon momentum by
measuring an opening angle between pions from the
K_{S}^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decay.
In the specific case when the pions in the kaon rest frame
move perpendicular to the kaon momentum direction, the opening angle has
a minimum value.
In this paper we call it "edge angle”,
\psi_{c}
, and the kaon mass is
calculated as:

{\rm m}(K_{S}^{0})=\sqrt{{\rm E}^{2}_{K_{S}^{0}}{\rm sin^{2}}\frac{\psi_{c}}{2}+4{\rm m}^{2}_{\pi}{\rm cos^{2}}\frac{\psi_{c}}{2}}.

(1)

The equation includes only the kaon energy (
{\rm E}_{K_{S}^{0}}
well measured), the pion
mass value (
{\rm m}_{\pi}
very precisely known), and the edge angle.
This method was used in the first collider
experiments by CMD collaboration
barkov85
;
barkov87
, and allowed to
get the kaon mass with a few tens of keV/c
2
.
The precision of these measurements was limited by the low
collider luminosity and by beam energy drift during the data taking.

A more general kinematical expression, suggested in
ref.
Zaitsev
, allows one to use pions with any decay angles to calculate the kaon mass.
If the pion 3-momenta,
{\bf p}_{+},{\bf p}_{-}
, from the
K_{S}^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decay are reconstructed,
the kaon mass value,
{\rm m}_{K_{S}^{0}}
, can be extracted from the equation:

\beta^{2}_{K_{S}^{0}}=\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}(1+{\rm cos}\psi\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}})(1-\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}_{\pi}\eta^{2}}),

(2)

where
\eta=(1-Y^{2})/(1+Y^{2}),Y=|{\bf p}_{+}|/|{\bf p}_{-}|
,

\beta^{2}_{K_{S}^{0}}=1-({\rm m}_{K_{S}^{0}}/{\rm E}_{K_{S}^{0}})^{2},~~~\beta^{2}_{\pi}=1-(2{\rm m}_{\pi}/{\rm E}_{K_{S}^{0}})^{2}
,

{\rm E}_{K_{S}^{0}}
is the kaon energy, and
\psi
is the opening angle
between the pions. The mass value in this equation depends on the kaon
energy (assumed to be measured precisely), opening angle between pions,
and the pion momenta ratio,
Y
; in the ratio it is reasonable to
assume that a significant part of the systematic
uncertainties related to the momentum measurement canceles out.
This approach allows one to use all decay events, and has been demonstrated
by the CMD-2 Collaboration, reaching an overall systematic
uncertainty of 24 keV/c
2
.
The result was reported and appeared in a conference
proceedings
Zaitsev
, but had not been published in a regular peer-reviewed journal.

If the decay pions in the kaon rest frame move perpendicular to the kaon direction, the value

Y=1
and the eq.
2
becomes very simple:

\beta^{2}_{K_{S}^{0}}=\beta^{2}_{\pi}{\rm cos}^{2}\frac{\psi_{c}}{2},

(3)

which is equivalent to eq.
1
.

Later on, the high-statistics measurements of the neutral kaon mass were
published by few experimental groups
NA48
;
KLOE
;
KLEO
, which are
in a tension (scale factor 1.2
pdg
) with the first measurements
by the CMD Collaboration, a further clarification is desirable.

In this paper we present a new measurement of the neutral kaon mass with
the CMD-3 detector at the VEPP-2000 collider
vepp
. The neutral
kaons are produced in the energy scan of the
\phi(1020)
resonance with
the integrated luminosity of about 14 pb
-1
, collected at 15 energy
points.
The kinematical expression given by eq.
2
is used for a
modification of the measured opening angle between pions what allows us to increase
statistics at the edge angle.

The third generation of the detector and the machine allows us to collect and analyze a large number
of the neutral kaons with a continuous beam energy monitoring during the
data taking. The large statistics allow us to perform many cross checks
and reduce systematic uncertainties.

2
CMD-3 detector

The general-purpose detector CMD-3 has been described in
detail elsewhere
sndcmd3
. Its tracking system consists of a
cylindrical drift chamber (DC)
dc
placed inside a thin
(0.2 X
0
) superconducting solenoid with a field of 1.3 T.

The tracking system allows to detect charged particle tracks with a minimum polar
angle about 0.5 radians relative to the beam axis (about 90% of 4
\pi
).
The DC information allows us to measure the charged particle momenta
with 1.5-4.5% accuracy in the
100-1000 MeV/c range, and provides the measurement of the polar and azimuth
angles with 15–20 mrad and 3.5-8.0 mrad accuracies, respectively. An
amplitude information from the DC wires is used to measure the ionization losses,
dE/dX
, of charged
particles with
\sigma_{dE/dX}\approx
11-14% accuracy for the minimun ionization particles.
It is important for the analysis
to reconstruct a decay vertex position (the decay length of
K_{S}^{0}
is
about 6 mm for the considered energies), and DC provides about 0.3 mm resolution
for the decay point radius.

The barrel liquid-xenon (LXe) calorimeter with a 5.4 X
0
thickness has
fine electrode structure, providing a 1
\div
2 mm spatial resolution
for photons
lxe
, and
shares the cryostat vacuum volume with the superconducting solenoid.
The barrel CsI-crystal calorimeter is placed outside the LXe calorimeter,
and increases the total thickness to 13.5 X
0
. The end cap BGO
calorimeter with a thickness of 13.4 X
0
is placed inside the
solenoid
cal
.
The luminosity is measured using events of Bhabha scattering
at large angles with about 1% accuracy
lum
.

To understand the detector response to the processes under study we use Monte Carlo (MC)
simulation of our detector based on the GEANT4
geant4
package,
in which all simulated events pass the whole reconstruction and selection
procedure. The MC simulation uses primary generator with matrix elements
for the studied process, including soft photon radiation by the initial
electron or positron
kur_fad
;
mcgpj
. For each energy point we
simulate 500 thousand signal events, and include experimental conditions
(efficiency, calibrations, beam position and energy etc.) for the
detector elements. The neutral kaon mass in the simulation is set to
the PDG
pdg
value: m(
K^{0}
) = 497.611
\rm MeV/c^{2}
.

3
Kaon energy

3.1
Beam energy control

The VEPP-2000 collider has one ring with the same orbit for the electrons
and positrons.
Because of the two-body
\phi
decay
each neutral kaon has energy, close to the beam one,
\rm E_{\rm beam}
, with some corrections,
described below.
The beam energy is monitored using
the Back-Scattering-Laser-Light system
laser
. During the data
taking for this experiment the beam energy measurements were performed every 20 minutes
with the statistical uncertainty
\sigma\approx
30 keV.
The collider operators monitored the energy measurements
and ensured that the machine operated within
\pm 2\sigma
of the
nominal energy value.

Figure
1
shows a screenshot from the energy monitor
presenting the data-taking history for the
\rm E_{\rm beam}
=509.5 MeV.
Each point corresponds to the beam energy and the energy
spread values, obtained from the back-scattered-photon energy spectrum fit as described
in ref.
laser
. The average values for the energy and
its spread are also shown. If the measured energy deviates by more than
2
\sigma
from the average, the corresponding data are not taken into
analysis, like the first point in figure
1
:
less than one percent of the events are removed.
As a result, each experimental point has
the energy obtained by the averaging of the individual measurements with
much smaller statistical accuracy compared to the 30 keV systematic
uncertainty, declared in ref.
laser
. The average also takes into
account a weight of each point, related to the luminosity integral,
shown at the bottom of figure
1
.

Another approach is presented in
the SND experiment for the
\phi(1020)
meson parameters
study
sndphi
. It is the most precise study of the
\phi

resonance. The data in the the SND experiment were collected
simultaneously with CMD-3 using another collision section of the
collider, hence they have the same energies
and have luminosity integrals almost identical to those of CMD-3 for each energy
point. The average values for
the beam energy and the energy spread are obtained in SND study by the fitting of the
combined back-scattering-photon energy spectra for each experimental
point. It allows them to obtain much better statistical uncertainties for the energy spread. These
energies,
\rm E_{\rm beam}
, and the
\rm E_{\rm c.m.}
energy spreads,
\delta\rm E_{\rm c.m.}
, derived
from the SND paper
sndphi
, are listed in table
1
and are used in our analysis.

Also we should repeat a statement from the SND paper:

"The uncertainty of PDG
pdg

\phi
-meson mass 1019.461
\pm

0.016 MeV is significantly smaller than the systematic uncertainty of
the collider energy measurement (60 keV). Therefore, the PDG mass can
be used for calibration of the c.m. energy scale. To do this we introduce
into the fit an additional parameter
\Delta\rm E_{\rm c.m.}
(common shift
of all energy points). Its fitted value

\Delta\rm E_{\rm c.m.}
= 0.017
\pm
0.018 MeV”

The half of this value and uncertainty,
\Delta E_{SND}=0.0085\pm 0.0090
MeV, is used to correct the beam energies in the first column of
table
1
.

The energies obtained by averaging the
individual measurements (figure

listed in table

, and are used for the systematic uncertainty study.

3.2
Kaon energy corrections

Current VEPP-2000 optics with the round beams at the interaction
region results in a relatively large collision energy spread, which accounts
for almost 10% of the
\phi
-resonance width. At the sharp resonance slopes the
kaon pairs are produced with a larger cross section at one side of the
energy spread distribution and with a smaller cross section at another
side. Therefore the average c.m. energy of the kaons,
weighted with the resonance curve,
is shifted from the measured
\rm E_{\rm c.m.}
energy.

Using the
\rm E_{\rm c.m.}
energy spread from table
1
and the

\phi
-meson parameters we convolute the resonance curve with the
Gaussian distribution of the beam energy around nominal
\rm E_{\rm c.m.}
, and
calculate the weighted c.m. energy for the kaon pair at each
experimental point. The weighted c.m. energy is shifted up to
\pm
60 keV at the slopes, and
the shift is zero on top of the resonance.
We check also how large is the energy shift if we vary the

\phi
-meson parameters or the energy spread value.

Figure
2
shows a difference,
\Delta E_{\phi}
, of the weighted energy from the measured
\rm E_{\rm c.m.}

energy, obtained for the different conditions. The error bars
show the uncertainty which comes from the accuracy of the energy spread measurements.
The squared markers show the
obtained corrections if we use the
\phi
width value from the
PDG
pdg
(open) or
from the SND measurement
sndphi
(filled). The circular markers are for the
set of the energy spreads, obtained by averaging the individual fits, as
shown in figure
1
. The
\Delta E_{\phi}
values for the
described conditions don’t change by more
than 0.002–0.003 keV, taken as an estimate of the systematic uncertainty.

The kaon energy is shifted by a half the presented difference,

\Delta E_{\phi}
, and the
used corrections are listed in table
1
.

4
Signal events selection

We select events of the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\phi\to K_{S}^{0}K_{L}^{0}
reaction, where the neutral kaon candidate
is reconstructed from the
K_{S}^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decay to obtain
a clean event sample for the mass measurement.

At the first stage, our reconstruction procedure provides momentum,
angles, and dE/dX for each charged track in the DC.
We require exactly two oppositely charged
"good" tracks, each track has more than 10 hits in DC, its polar angle is in the
range 1.0
\div
(
\pi
-1) radians (tracks cross all
wires in DC), and the dE/dX values corresponds to that of the charged pions.
We also require each track to be originated from the interaction region
along the beams within
\pm
10 cm from the center of the interaction region.

At the second stage, the pair of the tracks is tested for the presence of a common vertex within
the DC resolution, and the invariant mass of these tracks, assuming them
to be pions, is required to be in the |m(
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
) -
m(
K^{0}
)|<25
\rm MeV/c^{2}
range (about eight standard deviations from average). We
require the radius of the vertex position to be inside the vacuum beam
pipe of the collider.

Figure
3
(a) shows the invariant mass for the pair of the charged
pions with found common vertex for data (points) and simulation
(shaded histogram). Vertical lines show selections of events for
further analysis. In total we select 2039879 events corresponding to
the
K_{S}^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decays with a practically negligible background.
As shown in our previous paper on the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\phi\to K_{S}^{0}K_{L}^{0}
cross

Kozyrev
, the contribution from other decays of the

\phi
resonance does not exceed a few per mil level, and do not provide a
peaking background under the
K_{S}^{0}
mass.

5
Edge angle approach using full two-body reconstruction

As discussed in the Introduction, the kaon mass can be calculated by
using the kinematical relation in eq.
2
.
Figure
4
(a) shows the kaon mass vs log(Y), calculated for
the reconstructed events according to
eq.
2
for MC with
\rm E_{\rm beam}
=509.5 MeV. The log(Y) = log(
p_{+}/p_{-}
), denoted as
LogY
, is
a convenient parameter: LogY=0 if
p_{+}=p_{-}
, and has a positive
value if
p_{+}
moves forward relative to the kaon direction
(
p_{+}>p_{-}
), and has a negative value in the reverse situation.
The line shows the kaon mass value used in the simulation.
To extract the measured kaon mass a procedure of averaging in small bins in
the LogY value is used, as suggested in ref.
Zaitsev
.
A correction on the systematic
shift in each bin, reflecting influence of the resolution in the
\psi
value should
be applied. Unfortunately,
only a central part of the distribution can be practically used for
the mass determination because of
a distortion in kinematics and other possible distortions, for example
in the Y value if not all systematic uncertainties are canceled out
in the
p_{+}/p_{-}
ratio.

We use another approach based on the same kinematical relation as
eq.
2
.
If the kaon energy and mass values are used as input,
the opening angle
\psi
can be calculated vs LogY, and
the resulting curve is shown in figure
4
(b).
The edge angle,
\psi_{c}^{0}
, is the lowest point on the curve. The
experimental points, shown in figure
4
(b), are distributed
around this "base line", reflecting the uncertainties in the
\psi

and LogY values after reconstruction.
The idea is to use the "base line" behaviour to compensate the deviations of
the experimental points from the
\psi_{c}^{0}
value for all measured
angles.

We develop a procedure which minimizes the mean square deviation (using
weights according to the
\psi
and LogY resolutions) of a given experimental point
on the two-dimentional distribution in figure
4
(b) from the point on the
curve, and calculates a length, L
LogY
, of the curve segment from LogY=0 to the
determined optimal point (with sign) in the units of LogY.

We determine a "modified opening angle" for each measurement as a sum of
the deviation value and the
\psi_{c}^{0}
value. The deviation
can be positive and negative depending on the side from the curve.
Figure
4
(c) shows a scatter plot of the modified
opening angles vs L
LogY
.

It is important that the procedure does not shift the positions of the points
close to the edge angle, where
LogY=0, even if the input mass in eq.
2
is
shifted from the PDG value; however, it moves all the other points closer to the edge angle value.

We apply the procedure to the angles of the pions from the primary
generator, and the dark points in figure
4
(c) form a
sharp narrow structure at the
\psi_{c}^{0}
edge angle value.
Note that for the generator angles there are no
points below the
\psi_{c}^{0}
value, but large angles are allowed, because
the radiative effects decrease the kaon energy.

Edge angles in MC

6.1
Edge angle extraction procedure and corrections

After the described transformation, the modified angle distribution of
figure
4
(c) is
subjected to a profile procedure to get the average edge angle
value vs LogY, as shown in figure
5
(a). The "standard” profile, which
calculates the average values of points in a
\pm
0.2 radians window, is much more reliable
than that for the distribution in figure
4
(a). But an
expanded view of this plot, figure
5
(b), shows a significant deviation of the
profile values from the input edge angle, corresponding to the
horizontal line. As shown below, 1 mrad in the egde angle value
corresponds to about 50 keV/c
2
in the kaon mass.

This deviation is caused by a rapid change of the ionization energy
loss of the pion vs its momentum (see figure
3
(b) in the
detector material, resulting a non-linearity of the Y value.
A relative difference of the
generated, P
true
, and the reconstructed, P
meas
, pion momentum vs the generated pion
momentum is shown in figure
6
(a), demonstrating the
energy loss effect.
Note that a similar comparison of the generated and the reconstructed angles
of the pions does not show a significant difference.
We correct the reconstructed
pion momentum in each event according
to the second order polynomial fit function, shown in
figure
6
(a). The resulting
"standard” profile is shown in figure
6
(b) by the black
points: the deviations are smaller but still not uniform.

As already pointed above, the result of the "standard” profile very much depends on
the region of averaging, asymmetry in resolution, and on background
distribution tails.
The profile result depends also on the distribution tails from the
emission of the the soft photons by the initial particles.
To minimize these effects in the average value, we use a Gaussian fit
of the modified angle distribution for each 0.1 interval of the LogY value.

We perform the fit in the angular range from the level of 0.1 of the
peak height on the left slope of the distribution up to the level of 0.5 of
the peak height on the right slope of the distribution.
All Gaussian parameters are free.
The average values of the Gaussian functions are shown by the connected crosses in figure
6
(b), and they
exhibit a nearly uniform behaviour in a region around
\rm L_{\rm LogY}
=0.

7
Edge angles in data

7.1
Edge angle corrections: pion momenta

The expanded view of the scatter plot for the modified angles vs length for the
experimental events at
\rm E_{\rm beam}
=509.5 MeV with "standard" profile
points is shown in figure
9
(a) before any
corrections. The profile demonstrates a relatively large
deviation from the uniform behaviour, simular to that from the
simulation, shown in figure
5
(b).

The correction of each pion momentum according to the function shown in
figure
6
(a) almost completely removes the
observed large deviations.

The profile in figure
9
(a) is also somehow asymmetric relative to the center at LogY=0.
We investigate the influence of other uncertainties in the momentum
measurement on the determination of the edge angle.
The present DC calibration procedure
dc
, based on the high energy
cosmic muons, has a limited precision,
which leads to a small difference in the

p_{+}
and
p_{-}
values, and the edge angle is not at LogY=0.
In addition to the momentum correction we introduce a shift to the
LogY value at the level of 0.001, (one-per-mil for the difference in the momenta) which
visually removes the asymmetry.

The events from figure

7.2
Edge angle corrections: pion angles

As noted in Sec.
6.1
the reconstructed angles of the pions
in simulation do not
deviate (on average) from the initial generator values. We cannot
perform this test for our signal
K_{S}^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
events in data.

To investigate possible deviations in data, we develop a procedure with
test events from the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\phi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
reaction.
The pions from the
\phi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
decay originate from the
collision region and have a
momentum range similar to that from the
K_{S}^{0}
decay.

The position of the collision point in a transverse plane is known with a few
microns accuracy, and the colliding beams have a transverse size of
about 10 microns: ten times better than the DC resolution.
For each track our reconstruction procedure fits the hits in the DC to
obtain the production angles
and momentum. In addition, we perform a fit which uses the
interaction point as an additional hit on the track: weights of
the DC hits, starting at the radius of 4 cm, are significantly reduced, therefore
the reconstructed angle is closer to the true one. The
difference in the azimuthal angles for these two fits provides an estimate
of the systematic angle shift.

A relatively clean
sample of the
\phi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
events is selected by requiring
energy-momentum conservation for the detected two oppositely-charged
pion tracks, and two
photons in the calorimeter with an invariant mass close to that for
\pi^{0}
.
We make a scatter plot of the difference between the two azimuthal
angles vs the transverse momentum, separately for the positive and the
negative pions. An expanded view of these scatter plots with
the Gaussian profiles for a few energy points is
shown in figure
10
. The azimuth angular differences,
\Delta\phi
, for the positive
and the negative pions have different behaviours with the transverse
momentum, and the difference is not completely canceled when we
calculate the opening angle for the two tracks.

The
K_{S}^{0}
decay length is about 6 mm, and the pions from the decay are
somehow shifted from the collision point. These shifts are relatively
small, therefore we assume similar angular differences
\Delta\phi

for the decay pions, and use them for corrections.

9.1
Energy calibration

The uncertainty in the calibration constant

sndphi
of the

\phi
-meson mass relative to the PDG world average value
pdg
, is the
major systematic uncertainty in our kaon mass measurement. A work on the better
calibration of the laser system at the VEPP-2000 collider using the
resonance depolarization method is in progress, and could further
improve the result.

The different approach to the average-over-point energy calculations,
discussed in Sec.
3.1
, gives
a different result because of a possible machine energy drift during the
data taking. For example, the average beam energy in figure

=509.528
\pm

\rm E_{\rm beam}
=509.539
\pm

\pm

\rm MeV/c^{2}

\rm MeV/c^{2}

\rm MeV/c^{2}

0.001
\rm MeV/c^{2}

10
Conclusion

Table
2
summarises the systematic uncertainties discussed
in Sec.
9
. The largest uncertainty comes from the beam energy
calibration using the world average
\phi
-meson mass.
The
K_{S}^{0}
mass differs from the
K^{0}
mass by a negligible value, therefor
our result is:

m(K^{0})=497.587\pm 0.004(stat.)\pm 0.008(syst.)\pm 0.009(calibr.)~\rm MeV/c^{2},

where the first uncertainty is statistical, the second is systematic,
and the third is the unsertainty from the energy calibration.
Assuming no
correlations between uncertainty sources, the overall
uncertainty in our measurement is estimated as 0.0124
\rm MeV/c^{2}
.
Figure
17
shows our result in comparison with the other
K^{0}

mass measurements.
Our result is in good agreement with all high statistical experiments
and has better accuracy.
