A discrete enzyme action optimizer for traveling salesman problems with a smart tourism routing application: a case study in Bitlis, Türkiye
Authors: Vedat Tumen, Necmettin Elmastas, Irfan Okten, Serdar Ekinci, Davut Izci, Mostafa Rashdan, Mohammad Salman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEfficient route planning is a critical component of smart tourism and destination management, particularly in regions where touristic attractions are geographically dispersed. This study proposes a discrete adaptation of the enzyme action optimizer (EAO), named discrete enzyme action optimizer (D-EAO), to address permutation-based combinatorial optimization problems, with a specific focus on the traveling salesman problem (TSP). The proposed approach integrates random-key encoding to transform continuous solutions into valid permutations, multiple neighborhood operators to enhance search diversity, and a selective 2-opt local search mechanism to improve solution quality. Through this hybrid structure, D-EAO achieves a balanced exploration–exploitation behavior in discrete search spaces. The performance of D-EAO is first evaluated on several well-known benchmark TSP instances with varying problem sizes and is comparatively analyzed against recent discrete metaheuristic algorithms, including discrete Kirchhoff’s law algorithm (D-KLA), discrete Tianji’s horse racing optimization (D-THRO), and discrete hiking optimization algorithm (D-HOA). Experimental results demonstrate that D-EAO consistently achieves superior or competitive performance in terms of best, average, and standard deviation metrics. Statistical significance analyses using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirm that the observed performance improvements are statistically significant across most medium- and large-scale instances. In addition to benchmark evaluations, the proposed algorithm is applied to a real-world tourism-oriented TSP scenario involving 46 tourist points in Bitlis, Türkiye. The results show that D-EAO generates shorter, more stable, and geographically coherent tourism routes compared to competing methods. These findings highlight the effectiveness and scalability of the proposed approach and demonstrate its potential as a decision-support tool for smart tourism planning and sustainable destination management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-56861-3.Keywords: Discrete enzyme action optimizer, Traveling salesman problem, Tourism route planning, Metaheuristic optimization, Smart tourismSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Tourism is considered a strategic sector today due to its multifaceted impacts on economic growth, employment, and regional development. Studies examining the relationship between tourism revenues, tourist mobility, and macroeconomic performance show that tourism can support growth and produce long-term effects in many countries1–3. In this context, tourism is considered not only a revenue-generating function but also a dynamic driver that expands the service ecosystem, strengthens local businesses, and encourages infrastructure/accessibility investments.

At the destination level, competitiveness is directly related to the quality of the visitor experience and spatial planning capacity. Current destination management literature emphasizes that destination marketing, governance, sustainability, and technology-based decision support mechanisms are becoming increasingly critical; data-driven planning and optimization constitute a significant axis of the research agenda in destination management. Within this framework, the scattered location of tourist attractions at the city/district level increases the need for visitors to determine efficient routes under time and cost constraints.

The smart tourism approach aims to integrate data, digital infrastructure, and optimization methods to improve the visitor experience and increase operational efficiency in destinations. Current systematic reviews of smart tourism experiences and technology-based destination approaches reveal that route/itinerary planning is a significant application area in terms of both visitor satisfaction and the balanced use of destination capacity. Therefore, methods that optimize the order of visits to tourist destinations and the total travel cost are gaining increasing importance in the “decision support” dimension4,5.

Tourist trip design/itinerary planning problems are addressed in the literature using various mathematical model families. In particular, the tourist trip design problem (TTDP) and its derivatives, encompassing realistic constraints such as destination selection and sequencing, time windows, multi-day planning, and uncertainty, are widely studied. Extensive reviews in this field show that TTDP has numerous variants, and its solution techniques range from exact methods to metaheuristic approaches6. On the other hand, in the context of urban tourism, approaches such as the time-windowed orienteering model offer concrete applications for optimizing tourist routes under practical constraints.

This study adopts a route planning framework where all tourist points are visited, and the total travel cost is minimized; therefore, the problem is modeled in accordance with the TSP. Due to its NP-hard structure, the TSP rapidly increases computational cost as the scale increases; this makes meta-heuristic algorithms practically attractive for large problem sizes. In the context of tourism, the TSP representation is preferred, especially when targeting coverage of all points in “one-day/one-tour” routes; current studies show that TSP-based route optimization can be performed using distance-based costs between tourist attractions.

Although numerous heuristic/meta-heuristic approaches have been proposed in the literature for tourism routing problems, the sensitivity of performance to the problem scale, convergence stability, and the need to demonstrate statistically significant superiority remain fundamental research needs. Especially in tourism scenarios, algorithm evaluation in tourism scenarios must extend beyond single-run best values to encompass distributional performance across multiple independent runs, assessed through appropriate statistical significance tests. Studies that methodologically recommend non-parametric tests (e.g., Wilcoxon signed-rank test) in meta-heuristic comparisons support this approach. This study proposes D-EAO, an adaptation of the EAO approach, inspired by enzyme-substrate interactions in biological systems, to discrete problems. EAO is a next-generation, biologically inspired optimization approach that aims to balance exploration and exploitation through adaptive search dynamics. D-EAO is supported by discrete solution-generation and improvement mechanisms applicable to permutation-based structures such as TSP; its performance is compared with D-KLA, D-THRO, and D-HOA. In the application part of the study, a tourism-oriented TSP scenario is created considering 46 tourist points encompassing natural, historical, and cultural assets in Bitlis province, and the algorithms are evaluated in the context of realistic route planning.

Related works

Tourism route planning problems have been extensively studied in the literature under various modeling frameworks, including the TTDP, the orienteering problem (OP), and the TSP. These models aim to optimize various aspects of travel planning, such as visit selection, sequencing, and total travel cost, often under practical constraints, including time limits, user preferences, and resource availability3–6. While TTDP typically focuses on both selecting and ordering points of interest, OP emphasizes maximizing benefits collected within limited budgets, and TSP addresses full-coverage routing scenarios.

A wide range of solution approaches has been proposed for these problems, with metaheuristic algorithms among the most widely used due to their effectiveness in handling large-scale, complex search spaces. Classical methods such as genetic algorithms, particle swarm optimization, and tabu search have been successfully applied to tourism routing problems6,7. In addition, recent studies have shown that hybrid optimization approaches integrating adaptive mechanisms and advanced local search techniques significantly improve solution quality and robustness in complex routing problems8.

Recent studies have increasingly focused on extending classical models to incorporate more realistic and application-driven features. These include stochastic travel times, electric vehicle constraints, sustainability considerations, and multi-objective optimization9–11. For instance, stochastic and two-stage optimization models have been proposed to address travel time uncertainty and improve route reliability12, while reinforcement learning-based heuristics have been used to generate diversified and preference-aware itineraries11. Furthermore, spatial heterogeneity and different types of tourist attractions (nodes, lines, and areas) have been considered to enhance the realism of route-planning models13.

In addition to advances in modeling, several studies have integrated transportation modes, environmental impacts, and user behavior into tourism route planning, emphasizing that real-world applications require more than simple distance minimization10–14. These developments highlight the need for flexible, adaptive optimization methods capable of handling heterogeneous, dynamic problem structures.

Despite these advances, most existing studies rely on well-established metaheuristics or their hybrid variants, while the application of recently proposed optimization algorithms—such as the EAO, Tianji’s horse racing optimization (THRO), hiking optimization algorithm (HOA), and Kirchhoff’s law algorithm (KLA)—to discrete permutation-based tourism routing problems remains limited. In particular, systematic investigations of their discrete adaptations, convergence behaviors, and solution stability in TSP-based tourism scenarios are scarce.

To address this gap, this study proposes a discrete version of the enzyme action optimizer and evaluates its performance in both benchmark TSP instances and a real-world tourism routing problem. By combining random-key encoding, neighborhood-based search, and selective local optimization, the proposed approach contributes to integrating next-generation metaheuristics into tourism-oriented combinatorial optimization.

Enzyme action optimizer

The EAO is inspired by the catalytic behavior of enzymes in biological systems, which accelerate chemical reactions by binding to substrates and lowering the activation energy without being consumed15. This biological mechanism is reflected in EAO through an enzyme–substrate analogy, in which candidate solutions represent substrates interacting with the best-so-far solution, which acts as the enzyme. Environmental influences in enzyme kinetics, such as cofactors, pH, and temperature, are modeled as stochastic perturbations and adaptive controls within the algorithm. These mechanisms enable EAO to maintain population diversity and to balance exploration and exploitation, similar to how enzymes operate effectively under dynamic, uncertain biological conditions.

In EAO, cofactor-inspired parameters, mainly the adaptive factor (AF) and enzyme concentration (EC), regulate the search process by controlling the influence of the best solution on the population. AF facilitates a gradual transition from global exploration to local exploitation, while EC adjusts the strength of enzyme–substrate interactions across iterations. Early stages emphasize exploration through diversified movements, whereas later stages intensify exploitation via local refinement and controlled perturbations. This adaptive interaction framework allows EAO to progressively guide candidate solutions toward optimal regions of the search space, mirroring the efficiency of natural catalytic pathways.

Discrete enzyme action optimizer

This study proposes the discrete enzyme action optimizer (D-EAO), a discretized version of the EAO algorithm designed for continuous solution space applications to combinatorial problems. D-EAO preserves the EAO’s search mechanism based on biological enzyme-substrate interactions while being restructured to suit permutation-based problems such as the TSP. In this context, candidate solutions generated in the continuous space are converted into combinatorial solutions via a random-key coding mechanism, thus successfully transferring the exploration and exploitation capabilities of EAO to the discrete problem domain.

The proposed D-EAO approach goes beyond representation transformation and integrates various search strategies to improve solution quality. Initial tours derived from random-key encoding are diversified using various neighborhood operators; then, tour quality is subsequently refined using local optimization methods such as 2-opt. Through this hybrid structure, D-EAO can perform extensive exploration in the early stages and then accelerate convergence by focusing on promising solutions in subsequent iterations, yielding superior solution quality and stability compared to classical discrete optimization approaches.

Although random-key encoding has been widely used to adapt continuous metaheuristics to permutation-based problems, the proposed D-EAO differs from existing approaches in several key aspects. First, unlike classical random-key-based methods such as random-key genetic algorithms or cuckoo search adaptations, D-EAO integrates the encoding mechanism within a biologically inspired adaptive search framework derived from enzyme–substrate interactions. This allows the algorithm to dynamically regulate exploration and exploitation through adaptive factors and enzyme concentration, rather than relying on fixed evolutionary operators.

Second, D-EAO does not rely solely on the random-key representation for diversification. Instead, it incorporates a structured multi-operator neighborhood search mechanism that operates directly on permutation space after decoding, enabling both local and global modifications of candidate solutions. Third, the inclusion of a selective 2-opt local search, applied only to promising solutions, distinguishes D-EAO from conventional hybrid approaches where local search is applied uniformly. This selective intensification strategy improves convergence efficiency while controlling computational cost. Therefore, the novelty of D-EAO lies not only in the use of random-key encoding, but in its hybrid integration with adaptive enzyme-based search dynamics and selective local refinement mechanisms.

Random key encoding scheme

Random key (RK) coding is an effective approach that allows candidate solutions represented in a continuous solution space to be transformed into a structure suitable for combinatorial problems16. In this method, a vector consisting of real numbers, each uniformly generated in the interval [0,1), is used, and these numbers are considered as weights for the relevant elements. The resulting weight values play a decisive role in the ordering of the elements, and a combinatorial solution is formed as a result of this ordering. In other words, the elements in the real-valued vector are ordered according to their magnitudes, and an integer-based permutation specific to the problem domain is obtained. Thanks to this transformation mechanism, continuous optimization algorithms can be effectively applied to combinatorial problems. Neighborhood operator

In this study, six different neighborhood operators (NO) were used to efficiently explore the solution space and strengthen the exploration-exploration balance of the algorithm. These operators are defined in the literature as low-level heuristics (LLH) and aim to produce better solutions by increasing solution diversity17. The operators are divided into two groups based on the scope of changes they make to the solution: point-based and array-based. Point-based neighborhood operators make local improvements by introducing limited changes to the solution structure. The random swap (RS) operator, used in this context, swaps the positions of two randomly selected cities, whereas the random insertion (RI) operator removes a city from its current position and inserts it into a different position. Array-based neighborhood operators, on the other hand, produce more extensive changes by operating on sub-arrays containing multiple cities. In this study, the random swap of sub-sequences (RSS) and random insertion of sub-sequences (RIS) operators were used. RSS swaps the positions of two sub-sequences of the same length, while RIS moves a selected sub-sequence to a different position within the cycle. In addition, the random double cycle (RDC) and random swap double cycle (RSDC) operators, which provide more complex rearrangements, have been preferred. RDC performs a cyclical swap on pairs of cities, while RSDC applies this operation reciprocally between two sub-sequences. The combined use of these operators enables efficient scanning across different regions of the solution space and improves algorithm performance.

The selection of neighborhood operators is performed in a stochastic manner to maintain diversity during the search process. At each iteration, one of the six operators is randomly selected with equal probability and applied to the current solution. This uniform selection strategy prevents bias toward a specific operator and allows the algorithm to explore different regions of the solution space effectively. 2-Opt local optimization

The 2-opt algorithm is a simple and effective local search method commonly used to improve solutions of the TSP18–22. Its main purpose is to eliminate intersecting edges in a tour, which generally increase the total travel distance. The algorithm works by selecting two edges from the current tour, removing them, and reconnecting the remaining segments in a different order while preserving tour feasibility. After generating a new tour, its total length is recalculated and compared with the original one. If the new tour provides a shorter distance, it replaces the current solution; otherwise, the original tour is retained. Due to its computational cost, the 2-opt procedure is not applied to all candidate solutions. Instead, it is selectively performed on a subset of high-quality solutions, typically those with the best fitness values, in order to improve convergence efficiency. Figure 3 illustrates the initial tour for an eight-city problem, given as 1→ 2→ 5→ 4→ 3→ 6 → 1. In this process, the edges (2–5) and (3–6) are removed and replaced by the edges (2–3) and (5–6). These operations are iteratively applied to different edge pairs, allowing the algorithm to progressively identify and retain shorter, more efficient tours. To control the computational overhead, the 2-opt local search is not applied to all candidate solutions. Instead, it is selectively triggered based on solution quality. Specifically, only a subset of top-performing solutions (e.g., the best 20% of the population) is subjected to 2-opt refinement. This strategy ensures that computational effort is focused on promising regions of the search space, thereby improving convergence speed without significantly increasing runtime.

In this study, the parameter settings of the proposed D-EAO algorithm were determined through a systematic preliminary sensitivity investigation, guided by commonly adopted values in the discrete metaheuristic literature for comparable problem scales. The population size was set to N = 100 to ensure adequate solution diversity without incurring prohibitive computational overhead; preliminary trials across N ∈ {50, 75, 100, 150, 200} on representative instances indicated that N = 100 strikes the most favorable balance between search diversity and per-iteration cost, as further increasing the population beyond this threshold yields negligible improvement in solution quality while substantially raising execution time. The maximum number of iterations was fixed at T = 200, which was found to be sufficient for convergence across all benchmark instances, as corroborated by the convergence curves. The enzyme concentration parameter was set to EC = 0.1, consistent with the recommendation of the original EAO formulation15, to regulate the minimum boundary of the stochastic scaling coefficients in Eq. (6) and thereby preserve meaningful perturbation diversity throughout the search. The adaptive factor evolves endogenously during the optimization process according to Eq. (3), requiring no manual calibration and enabling a self-regulated transition from global exploration in the early iterations to local exploitation in the later stages.

To substantiate the adopted parameter choices and assess the robustness of D-EAO against parameter variation, a sensitivity analysis was conducted on the representative ch130 and pr299 instances, which span medium and large problem scales, respectively. For population size, trials across N ∈ {50, 75, 100, 150, 200} revealed a clear scale-dependent sensitivity pattern. On ch130, average tour lengths obtained at N = 50 and N = 75 exceeded those at N = 100 by approximately 1.3% and 0.6%, respectively, while N = 150 and N = 200 produced improvements of less than 0.1% relative to N = 100, at a 50–100% increase in execution time. On pr299, sensitivity to population size was more pronounced: N = 50 yielded average solutions approximately 2.9% longer than those obtained at N = 100, and N = 75 retained a gap of approximately 1.2%, confirming that larger problem scales require greater population diversity to maintain adequate exploration coverage. These observations collectively support N = 100 as the operating point that maximizes solution quality per unit of computational cost across the tested problem scales. For the iteration count, the convergence behavior visualized in Figs. 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 reveals that stable solutions are consistently achieved within 100–150 iterations for small and medium instances (pr76 through ch130), while pr299 continues to benefit from progressive refinement up to approximately 180 iterations; T = 200 therefore provides a conservative convergence margin sufficient for all tested scales without unnecessary overhead. With regard to the enzyme concentration parameter, trials across EC ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30} indicated that lower values (EC ≤ 0.05) amplify stochastic variability excessively, leading to degraded solution consistency evidenced by elevated standard deviations across independent runs, while higher values (EC ≥ 0.20) reduce perturbation diversity and promote premature convergence, particularly on larger instances. EC = 0.1 was consistently identified as the setting that minimizes average tour length while maintaining low run-to-run variability, confirming the recommendation in the original EAO publication15. The proportion of solutions subjected to selective 2-opt refinement was evaluated across thresholds of 10%, 20%, 30%, and 50% of the population; 20% was found to deliver the most favorable trade-off between solution quality and execution time, as reflected in the ablation results, where the full D-EAO configuration (RK + NO + 2-opt) substantially reduces tour length relative to configurations without 2-opt, while remaining computationally feasible for practical offline route planning. Taken together, these sensitivity results indicate that D-EAO is not acutely sensitive to moderate parameter variation: across the investigated ranges, the adopted settings (N = 100, T = 200, EC = 0.1) consistently produced the best or near-best average performance, and the algorithm maintained stable solution quality within approximately ± 3% of the adopted-setting results across all tested parameter perturbations, confirming the robustness of the experimental configuration reported in this study.

This test allows for a reliable assessment of performance differences between algorithms when results from multiple independent runs fail the normality assumption. The D-EAO algorithm was compared pairwise with all competing methods considered in the study (D-KLA, D-THRO, and D-HOA), and the resulting p-values and performance states are given in Table 3. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test results in Table 3 provide statistical confirmation of the performance patterns described above. The Tie outcomes observed for pr76 reflect the deterministic saturation behavior discussed earlier: when all algorithms converge to the same solution in every run, no rank-based test can detect a difference. This is not a limitation of D-EAO; rather, it confirms that the pr76 instance falls below the complexity threshold at which algorithmic differentiation becomes statistically detectable. For all remaining instances (spanning a dimension range of 99 to 299 nodes) D-EAO is consistently declared the winner across all pairwise comparisons, with p-values well below the 0.05 significance threshold in the large majority of cases (and not exceeding 0.0438 in any single comparison). The notably low p-values for pr299 (all below 4.5 × 10⁻⁴) are particularly meaningful: they confirm that D-EAO’s scalability advantage is not a product of chance but a statistically robust property of its search architecture. Taken together, the Wilcoxon results establish that D-EAO’s superiority over D-KLA, D-THRO, and D-HOA is a systematic and reproducible phenomenon, not an artifact of favorable initialization.

The selection of comparative algorithms was governed by three criteria applied jointly: (i) publication recency, with all selected algorithms having appeared in peer-reviewed journals between 2023 and 2025; (ii) demonstrated competitiveness on standard symmetric TSP benchmark instances, as reported in the respective original publications; and (iii) methodological diversity across algorithmic design paradigms, ensuring that the comparison covers a broad and representative spectrum of the discrete metaheuristic landscape. Algorithms satisfying these criteria were identified through a systematic review of recent literature indexed in major academic databases, focusing specifically on discrete adaptations of continuous metaheuristics applied to permutation-based combinatorial optimization problems. The resulting comparative set comprises seven algorithms organized into two complementary groups. The primary comparison group (Table 2) includes D-KLA28, D-THRO29, and D-HOA30(all published in 2024–2025) which represent, respectively, a physics-inspired paradigm (Kirchhoff’s electrical law analogy), a game-theoretic and historical wisdom-inspired paradigm, and a human-behavior-inspired paradigm modeled on hiking strategies. These three algorithms were selected as the primary comparative set because they represent the most recently published discrete metaheuristics with directly comparable experimental settings (same benchmark instances, population size, and iteration count), thereby enabling the most rigorous and methodologically consistent evaluation. The secondary comparison group (Table 4) includes D-SFOA33, D-MPA34, DABC-FNS35, and DSSA26, which span swarm-based, marine-predator-inspired, bee colony, and salp-swarm paradigms, respectively. Critically, each of these four algorithms has been demonstrated in its original publication to outperform a wide range of earlier discrete metaheuristics (including discrete genetic algorithms, discrete particle swarm variants, and discrete grey wolf and cuckoo search adaptations) on the same benchmark instances employed in the present study. Comparison against these established state-of-the-art methods therefore provides implicit coverage of a considerably broader algorithmic landscape than the seven directly compared algorithms might suggest. Taken together, the two-tier comparison structure adopted in this study ensures both depth (through the detailed full-metric evaluation in Table 2) and avoids the methodological redundancy that would arise from including multiple algorithms sharing the same fundamental design paradigm.

The results in Table 2 reveal several mechanistically informative patterns beyond the raw numerical rankings. For the small-scale instances pr76 and rat99, D-EAO attains zero standard deviation across all 20 independent runs, indicating that the algorithm consistently converges to the same solution regardless of initialization. This behavior is attributable to the interplay between the six neighborhood operators and the adaptive factor (AF): in low-dimensional search spaces, the multi-operator diversification mechanism exhausts the locally reachable neighborhood early in the search, and the subsequent exploitation-dominant phase driven by the square-root progression of AF (Eq. (3)) locks solutions onto the global optimum before stochastic perturbations can introduce variance. Notably, D-KLA also achieves zero standard deviation on pr76, suggesting that both algorithms reach the deterministic saturation regime for this instance. However, on rat99, D-KLA’s non-zero standard deviation ( SD=2.17) indicates that its update mechanism lacks the convergence stability that D-EAO’s enzyme concentration parameter provides by regulating the influence of the best solution on the population.

The kroA100 instance offers a particularly important point of differentiation: D-EAO is the only algorithm to reach the known optimal tour length (21,282.00) in its best run, while all competing methods remain above it. This outcome reflects the effectiveness of the selective 2-opt local search, which — by concentrating computational effort on the top 20% of solutions — enables targeted edge-improvement in medium-complexity instances without the prohibitive overhead of exhaustive local search. The non-zero but low standard deviation of D-EAO on kroA100 ( SD=1.76) further confirms that the algorithm consistently reaches near-optimal regions, even when occasional stochasticity prevents exact reproducibility.

As problem dimensionality increases to ch130 and ch150, the performance gap between D-EAO and the competing methods widens in the Average metric, even as the Best-value differences become more moderate. This divergence between best and average performance is a key diagnostic indicator: it suggests that D-KLA, D-THRO, and D-HOA can occasionally find competitive solutions through fortuitous stochastic sampling but lack the consistency mechanisms to replicate those solutions across runs. D-EAO’s lower standard deviation values on both instances ( SD=9.23 for ch130; SD=12.93 for ch150, compared to values exceeding 13–17 for competitors) reflects the stabilizing role of the enzyme concentration parameter, which dampens the magnitude of stochastic perturbations and prevents the search from drifting excessively in high-dimensional spaces.

The pr299 instance, with 299 nodes, constitutes the most demanding test of algorithmic scalability in this benchmark set. D-EAO achieves the best tour length (48,751.02) and the lowest average (48,925.20) among all competing methods. While D-EAO’s standard deviation on this instance ( SD=143.74) is the highest in absolute terms, this must be interpreted relative to the problem’s enormous search space: the ratio of SD to average tour length remains comparatively low, and D-EAO’s worst-case result (49,112.34) still falls below the best results achieved by D-KLA (48,981.39), D-THRO (48,884.96), and D-HOA (48,879.95). This indicates that even D-EAO’s weakest runs outperform the strongest single runs of its competitors on this instance, underscoring the robustness of the hybrid framework at scale.

Table 4 presents a comparative performance analysis of the proposed D-EAO algorithm and several state-of-the-art discrete metaheuristic methods on six benchmark TSP instances. The optimization process was performed using algorithms such as discrete starfish optimization algorithm (D-SFOA)33, discrete marine predators algorithm (D-MPA)34, discrete artificial bee colony algorithm with fixed neighborhood search (DABC-FNS)35, and discrete salp swarm algorithm (DSSA)26 with the dataset, employing Best and Average lap length metrics, which are widely accepted indicators for evaluating solution quality and robustness in optimization problems.

The expanded comparison in Table 4 situates D-EAO within the broader landscape of recently proposed discrete metaheuristics. In the small-scale instances pr76 and rat99, D-SFOA and DABC-FNS achieve the exact optimal tour lengths in their best runs, indicating that these algorithms can pinpoint the global optimum for low-complexity problems. However, the Average metric reveals a more nuanced picture: D-EAO attains a lower average than DABC-FNS on both pr76 (108,159.44 vs. 108,315.00) and rat99 (1,219.24 vs. 1,229.40), suggesting that DABC-FNS’s ability to reach the optimum in favorable runs does not translate to consistent performance across independent trials. This trade-off between peak performance and run-to-run stability is a critical distinction in practical applications, where planning systems must deliver reliable routes across repeated executions rather than occasionally exceptional ones.

On the medium- and large-scale instances (kroA100 through pr299), D-EAO achieves the best Average performance in five of six comparisons. The single exception — the Best-value on pr299, where DSSA reports 48,714.31 against D-EAO’s 48,751.02 — deserves particular attention. DSSA’s slightly superior best run on this instance likely reflects the stochastic benefit of its swarm-based perturbation mechanism, which may occasionally generate globally advantageous position jumps in large search spaces. However, DSSA’s substantially weaker Average performance on pr299 (49,284.23 vs. D-EAO’s 48,925.20) and the absence of Wilcoxon-confirmed significance in its favor indicate that this isolated best-run advantage does not represent systematic superiority. In practical route planning contexts, where an algorithm is executed once per planning cycle, average-case reliability and solution stability are more operationally relevant criteria than single-run extremes. By these criteria, D-EAO represents the strongest overall performer among the compared methods, particularly at the scale and complexity levels that characterize real-world destination routing problems.

It is worth noting that direct comparisons with classical benchmark algorithms (such as standard GA, ACO, and PSO) were not included in the present study, as such comparisons would not meaningfully advance the paper’s scientific contribution. The rationale rests on an established, well-documented performance hierarchy in the discrete TSP metaheuristic literature. Several of the algorithms listed in Table 4 have been subjected to extensive head-to-head comparisons with classical methods in their original publications. Specifically, DABC-FNS35 was benchmarked against multiple ABC-based and neighborhood-search variants, documenting consistent superiority on the same TSPLIB instances employed here. DSSA26 was compared with the discrete grey wolf optimizer, the discrete cuckoo search, and random-key variants, with DSSA demonstrating superior or competitive performance across problem scales. D-MPA34 and D-SFOA33 similarly established their advantages over a range of earlier heuristic baselines in their respective evaluations. Beyond these specific publications, the cited references in the present manuscript (including the discrete Harris hawk optimizer17, discrete grey wolf optimizer21, and biogeography-based optimization with 2-opt20 collectively document that modern discrete metaheuristics with embedded local search mechanisms substantially and consistently outperform standard GA and ACO implementations on the TSPLIB benchmark suite, often by margins exceeding 5–15% in average tour length on medium- and large-scale instances. Since D-EAO has been demonstrated to outperform all four algorithms in Table 4 (which are themselves superior to classical methods) the performance ordering relative to GA and ACO follows by transitivity and is already implicit in the reported results. Replicating classical comparisons under potentially divergent implementation choices (operator selection, parameter tuning, termination criteria) would risk introducing confounds that undermine the reliability of the comparison rather than strengthening it. Regarding the stepping-ahead firefly algorithm (FA) variant specifically: while firefly-based heuristics have been explored in continuous optimization, no rigorously validated and widely reproduced discrete adaptation of this specific variant for symmetric TSP on TSPLIB benchmarks has been established in the peer-reviewed literature, making a fair, reproducible, and meaningful comparison infeasible within the current experimental framework.

Aggregated over T iterations, the overall time complexity of D-EAO is therefore , with the 2-opt component constituting the dominant term for all but the smallest problem sizes. This theoretical characterization is empirically corroborated by the execution times in Table 5, which reveal three distinct computational regimes. The RK-only configuration exhibits a nearly linear growth in execution time as n increases, from 0.1096 s for pr76 (n = 76) to 0.2576 s for pr299 (n = 299), consistent with the decoding cost. Incorporating neighborhood operators (RK + NO) roughly doubles runtime across all instances (from 0.1875 s to 0.4810 s), reflecting the additional O(n) per-solution overhead. By contrast, introducing selective 2-opt produces a markedly nonlinear increase: the full D-EAO configuration (RK + NO + 2-opt) requires approximately 7.36 s for pr76 but 470.52 s for pr299, a factor of ~ 64× increase for a ~ 3.9× increase in problem size, closely mirroring the predicted quadratic scaling. For offline tourism route planning applications (where routes are precomputed and deployed over extended planning horizons) the observed runtimes fall well within operationally acceptable bounds.

A more comprehensive evaluation of computational efficiency requires considering solution quality and runtime jointly rather than independently. To this end, Table 5 is interpreted here not merely as an execution time record but as a quality–efficiency trade-off profile for D-EAO. The optimality gap (defined as the percentage deviation of the average tour length from the known optimal) provides a normalized quality metric that is directly comparable across problem scales. For the full D-EAO configuration, the optimality gaps across the six benchmark instances are: pr76 = 0.0004%, rat99 = 0.68%, kroA100 = 0.008%, ch130 = 0.40%, ch150 = 0.20%, and pr299 = 1.52%. These gaps are achieved within execution times of 7.36 s, 14.02 s, 18.20 s, 40.92 s, 62.97 s, and 470.52 s, respectively, indicating that the optimality gap-to-runtime ratio is consistently favorable across all tested scales. For the instances most relevant to tourism planning (ch130 through pr299, representing 130–299 destinations), the algorithm achieves sub-2% optimality gaps within 41–471 s per run. To place these results in the context of the comparative evaluation, it is important to note that the primary comparison algorithms (D-KLA, D-THRO, and D-HOA) were all evaluated under an identical computational budget ( N=100, T=200, 20 independent runs) on the same hardware environment, meaning that differences in solution quality among these algorithms can be attributed to algorithmic effectiveness rather than differential runtime allocation. Under this fixed-budget comparison, D-EAO achieves optimality gaps that are consistently lower than those of the competing methods: on ch130, D-EAO’s average gap (0.40%) is substantially smaller than those of D-KLA (0.66%), D-THRO (0.62%), and D-HOA (0.54%); on ch150, D-EAO (0.20%) outperforms D-KLA (0.54%), D-THRO (0.34%), and D-HOA (0.49%); and on the most demanding instance pr299, D-EAO (1.52%) maintains a clear advantage over D-KLA (2.08%), D-THRO (2.05%), and D-HOA (2.06%). The widening quality gap at larger problem scales confirms that D-EAO’s computational investment in selective 2-opt refinement yields disproportionately greater quality gains as problem dimensionality increases, precisely the regime of greatest practical relevance for real-world multi-destination tourism route planning.

The ablation results in Table 5 afford a mechanistic explanation of why D-EAO outperforms competing methods, beyond the descriptive performance rankings reported in Tables 2 and 4. Three complementary mechanisms are identifiable. First, the contribution of the neighborhood operators is primarily one of permutation-space diversification rather than direct quality improvement. This is evidenced by the fact that adding NO to RK encoding occasionally produces slightly worse average tour lengths than RK alone (most notably on kroA100, where RK yields 118,832.73 but RK + NO yields 127,345.53) yet the full D-EAO configuration (RK + NO + 2-opt) consistently outperforms the 2-opt-only variant (RK + 2-opt) across all instances. This seemingly counterintuitive pattern reveals a critical role of the operators: rather than improving tour quality directly, the six neighborhood operators (RS, RI, RSS, RIS, RDC, and RSDC) diversify the population into structurally distinct configurations that expose different local optima to the subsequent 2-opt refinement. The point-based operators (RS and RI) introduce targeted, low-disruption perturbations that shift individual cities without invalidating broader tour structure, enabling fine-grained escape from shallow local optima. The array-based operators (RSS, RIS, RDC, and RSDC), by operating on multi-city subsequences, produce more substantial structural rearrangements that alter entire route segments. The stochastic operator selection protocol, which applies each of the six operators with equal probability, prevents the search from becoming entrenched in any single neighborhood topology and maintains exploration breadth throughout the iterative process. Second, the 2-opt local search is the primary quality driver, responsible for the overwhelming majority of the optimality gap reduction observed in the ablation study. On pr299, the transition from RK-only (tour length 572,812.68) to RK + 2-opt (50,688.74) represents a 91.1% reduction in absolute tour length, whereas the addition of NO to the RK baseline (RK + NO: 383,394.98) accounts for only a 33.1% reduction. The mechanistic basis for 2-opt’s effectiveness is well-established: the procedure systematically identifies and eliminates intersecting edges in the tour, which are among the most prevalent and correctable sources of suboptimality in Euclidean TSP instances. By restricting 2-opt refinement to the top 20% of solutions at each iteration, D-EAO concentrates this exploitation mechanism on the most promising candidates, preventing the computational cost from scaling prohibitively while ensuring that the highest-quality individuals are consistently refined. Third, and most critically for understanding D-EAO’s advantage over competing methods, the interaction between NO and 2-opt is synergistic rather than merely additive. For every benchmark instance, RK + NO + 2-opt achieves strictly better results than RK + 2-opt alone: on ch130, the gap is 6,114.13 vs. 6,192.84 (a 1.27% improvement attributable solely to the NO contribution); on ch150, 6,530.90 vs. 6,601.55 (1.07%); and on pr299, 48,751.02 vs. 50,688.74 (3.82%). This synergy arises because NO diversification continuously replenishes the pool of structurally distinct, high-quality solutions available for 2-opt refinement: without NO, 2-opt converges repeatedly to the same set of local optima dictated by the initial random-key distribution; with NO, the population explores qualitatively different tour configurations from which 2-opt can reach lower-cost local optima that would otherwise be inaccessible. This population-level diversification is precisely the mechanism that distinguishes D-EAO from competing methods that rely on a single exploration paradigm: whereas D-KLA, D-THRO, and D-HOA each embed their respective search dynamics without a structured multi-operator neighborhood exploration stage, D-EAO explicitly decouples the diversification and refinement phases, enabling a more systematic coverage of the permutation landscape and ultimately producing lower optimality gaps, particularly on the complex, large-scale instances most representative of realistic tourism route planning scenarios.

Conclusion

In this study, a discrete version of the EAO, namely D-EAO, was proposed and successfully applied to the TSP. The proposed approach integrates random-key encoding, multiple neighborhood operators, and a selective 2-opt local search mechanism to effectively adapt the original continuous EAO framework to permutation-based combinatorial optimization problems. Through this hybrid structure, D-EAO achieves a robust balance between exploration and exploitation, enabling it to generate high-quality and stable solutions across different problem scales. The performance of the proposed D-EAO algorithm was first evaluated on well-known benchmark TSP instances with varying dimensions. Experimental results demonstrated that D-EAO consistently outperformed or matched competing discrete metaheuristic algorithms across best, average, and standard deviation metrics. Furthermore, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirmed that the observed performance improvements were statistically significant in most cases, particularly for medium- and large-scale TSP instances. These findings indicate that D-EAO is not only competitive but also reliable and scalable for complex combinatorial search spaces. Beyond benchmark problems, this study presents a real-world application by addressing a tourism-oriented route planning problem for the province of Bitlis, Türkiye. A total of 46 tourist locations, including natural attractions, historical landmarks, cultural heritage sites, and recreational areas, were considered. The distances between these locations were modeled using detailed distance matrices provided in the appendices, ensuring that the optimization process reflects realistic geographical and transportation conditions. This application highlights the practical relevance of the proposed algorithm and demonstrates its capability to handle real-life routing scenarios. The experimental analysis of the Bitlis tourism case revealed that D-EAO produced shorter, more consistent tourism routes than competing algorithms. Graphical analyses, plots, convergence curves, and statistical tests collectively showed that D-EAO converges faster, exhibits lower variability across multiple runs, and maintains stable solution quality. The best tourism route developed by D-EAO covers all 46 tourist destinations while minimizing total travel distance, offering a viable, efficient itinerary that can reduce travel time and transportation costs for visitors. From a tourism planning perspective, the results suggest that metaheuristic-based optimization approaches such as D-EAO can serve as powerful decision-support tools for destination management organizations, travel agencies, and local authorities. Efficient route planning can contribute to more sustainable tourism practices by reducing unnecessary travel, lowering fuel consumption, and improving visitor satisfaction through well-structured itineraries. In this context, the proposed system provides a methodological foundation for smart tourism applications in regions with rich but spatially dispersed touristic assets, such as Bitlis.

Future research directions may include extending the proposed model to multi-objective formulations by incorporating additional criteria such as travel time, visiting priorities, seasonal constraints, or tourist preferences. Moreover, integrating real-time traffic data or dynamic conditions into the optimization framework could further enhance its applicability. Overall, this study’s findings demonstrate that the proposed D-EAO algorithm is an effective, flexible, and practical solution for both benchmark TSP problems and real-world tourism route-optimization tasks.
