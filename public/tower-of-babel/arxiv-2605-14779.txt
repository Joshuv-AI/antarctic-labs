Peng’s Q( λ \lambda ) for Conservative Value Estimation in Offline Reinforcement Learning
Byeongchan Kim, Min-hwan Oh

Abstract
We propose a model-free offline multi-step reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm, Conservative Peng’s Q( λ \lambda ) (CPQL). Our algorithm adapts the Peng’s Q( λ \lambda ) (PQL) operator for conservative value estimation as an alternative to the Bellman operator. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work in offline RL to theoretically and empirically demonstrate the effectiveness of conservative value estimation with a multi-step operator by fully leveraging offline trajectories. The fixed point of the PQL operator in offline RL lies closer to the value function of the behavior policy, thereby naturally inducing implicit behavior regularization. CPQL simultaneously mitigates over-pessimistic value estimation, achieves performance greater than (or equal to) that of the behavior policy, and provides near-optimal performance guarantees — a milestone that previous conservative approaches could not achieve. Extensive numerical experiments on the D4RL benchmark demonstrate that CPQL consistently and significantly outperforms existing offline single-step baselines. In addition to the contributions of CPQL in offline RL, our proposed method also contributes to the offline-to-online learning framework. Using the Q-function pre-trained by CPQL in offline settings enables the online PQL agent to avoid the performance drop typically observed at the start of fine-tuning and to attain robust performance improvements. Our code is available at https://github.com/oh-lab/CPQL .

However, offline RL faces a major challenge known as distributional shift

This issue is exacerbated when the application of the Bellman updates to value functions requires querying values of out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs,
which can lead to an accumulation of extrapolation errors and ultimately result in poor performance of learned policies.

To tackle OOD actions in policy evaluation, conservative Q-learning (CQL)

Building on CQL, subsequent algorithms

Intuitively, leveraging offline trajectories that span multiple timesteps, rather than individual single-timestep transitions,
provides more information about the behavior policy and can potentially prevent the selection of OOD actions for offline datasets.

Preliminaries

3.1
Markov Decision Process

We consider a Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by a tuple
\mathcal{M}:=\left({\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{R},d_{0},\gamma\right)
, where
{\mathcal{S}}
is the state space,
\mathcal{A}
is the action space,
\mathcal{P}:{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\Delta_{{\mathcal{S}}}
represents the state transition probability kernel,
\mathcal{R}
is the reward distribution,
d_{0}\in\Delta_{{\mathcal{S}}}
is the initial state distribution, and
\gamma\in\left[0,1\right)
is the discount factor.
We let the reward function
r\in\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}}
be defined as
r(s,a):=\int r^{\prime}\mathcal{R}\left(dr^{\prime}|s,a\right)
, and assume that
\left|r(s,a)\right|\leq R_{\mathrm{max}}
,
\forall\left(s,a\right)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}
.
Let a policy
\pi:{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
be a mapping from states to actions (deterministic) or a probability distribution over actions (stochastic).
Given any policy
\pi
, an agent starts from an initial state
s_{0}
and interacts with
\mathcal{M}
, repeatedly taking actions, receiving rewards, and observing subsequent states.
This process generates a trajectory
\tau=\left\{\left(s_{t},a_{t},r\left(s_{t},a_{t}\right)\right)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
, where
a_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|s_{t})
and
s_{t+1}\sim\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s_{t},a_{t})
.
The state-value function and action-value function (Q-function) for the policy
\pi
are defined as

V^{\pi}(s):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r(s_{t},a_{t})\mid s_{0}=s\right]
and
Q^{\pi}(s,a):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r(s_{t},a_{t})\mid s_{0}=s,a_{0}=a\right]
, respectively.
We define the discounted state visitation distribution of a policy
\pi
under the environment
\mathcal{M}
as

d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\pi}\left(s\right):=\left(1-\gamma\right)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\mathrm{Pr}^{\pi}\left(s_{t}=s\mid s_{0}\sim d_{0},\mathcal{P}\right)
,
where
\mathrm{Pr}^{\pi}\left(s_{t}=s\mid s_{0}\sim d_{0},\mathcal{P}\right)
denotes the probability of reaching state
s
at time-step
t
under
\pi
and
\mathcal{P}
, starting from initial state
s_{0}
distributed according to the initial state distribution
d_{0}
.
Similarly, we define the discounted state-action visitation distribution as

d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\pi}\left(s,a\right):=d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\pi}\left(s\right)\pi\left(a|s\right)
.

3.2
Off-policy Operators

Off-policy RL consists of two main tasks: evaluation and improvement.
The evaluation process is to learn the Q-function of a fixed policy.
In the improvement setting, the goal is to obtain an optimal policy
\pi^{*}
that maximizes the expected discounted return under
d_{0}
, represented as
\max_{\pi}J_{\mathcal{M}}(\pi):=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{0}}\left[V^{\pi}\left(s\right)\right]=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s,a\sim d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\pi}}\left[r\left(s,a\right)\right]
.
Operators are a fundamental concept in RL because all value-based RL algorithms update the Q-function using a recursion
Q_{k+1}:=\mathcal{O}_{k}Q_{k}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}}
is an operator that specifies the update rule of the algorithm.
We define
\mathcal{P}^{\pi}
as the transition matrix coupled with the policy, given by

\mathcal{P}^{\pi}Q(s,a):=\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s,a),\,a^{\prime}\sim\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime})}\left[Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\right].

Bellman Operator.

The Bellman operator
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}:\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}}
is defined as
{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}Q:=r+\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi}Q
.
We denote the set of all greedy policies with respect to
Q
as
\mathbf{G}(Q)
.
The Bellman optimality operator
{\mathcal{T}}^{*}
is defined by
{\mathcal{T}}^{*}Q:={\mathcal{T}}^{\pi_{Q}}Q
, where
\pi_{Q}\in\mathbf{G}(Q)
.

Peng’s Q(
\lambda
) (PQL).

For
\lambda\in[0,1)
,
PQL updates the Q-function using the recursion
Q_{k+1}:={\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pi_{\beta,k},\pi_{k}}Q_{k}

(
51
;
33
)
, where
\pi_{k}\in\mathbf{G}(Q_{k})
.
The PQL operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pi_{\beta},\pi}:\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}}
is defined as

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\pi_{\beta},\pi}Q:=\left(1-\lambda\right){\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}}\lambda^{n-1}{\mathcal{T}}_{n}^{\pi_{\beta},\pi}Q
, where
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}^{\pi_{\beta},\pi}Q:=\left({\mathcal{T}}^{\pi_{\beta}}\right)^{n-1}{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}Q
is the uncorrected
n
-step return operator
(
67
;
9
;
61
;
23
)
.

3.3
Offline RL

In offline RL, the learned policy is constrained to a static dataset without additional interactions with the environment during the control process.
The offline dataset
\mathcal{D}
consists of either trajectories
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

gathered by unknown behavior policies
\pi_{\beta}
.
On all states

, we denote the empirical behavior policy as
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right):=\frac{\sum_{s,a\in\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{1}[s=s,a=a]}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{1}[s=s]}

We define the state space induced by
\mathcal{D}
as
S(\mathcal{D})
, consisting of all states in
\mathcal{D}

Since

typically covers a subset of the tuple space, offline RL algorithms based on the Bellman operator suffer from action distribution shift.
Because the learning policy is updated to maximize Q-values, cumulative extrapolation errors in unseen actions can drive it toward OOD actions with erroneously high Q-values
(
38
)
.

To address the overestimated Q-value problem,

(
35

penalizes the learned Q-function for OOD actions induced by the learning policy.
The objective function of CQL with a non-negative conservatism parameter
\alpha
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{s,a,s^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\left(Q\left(s,a\right)-{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}Q\left(s,a\right)\right)^{2}\right]+\alpha\left(\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D},a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[Q\left(s,a\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{s,a\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[Q\left(s,a\right)\right]\right).

4
Conservative PQL

In this section, we develop the CPQL algorithm,
where the learned Q-function mitigates overestimation bias in value estimation.
We provide several novel theoretical results that
include guarantees for the sub-optimality gap between the optimal policy and the mixture policy learned via CPQL.
It is important to note that PQL has not been studied under
offline
RL settings.
Hence, we first present how previous findings on online PQL can be adapted to offline PQL, addressing fundamental challenges of Q-learning in offline RL.

4.1
Towards Offline PQL

Prior works
(
51
;
61
;
33
)
have investigated the PQL operator only in
online
RL.
In this work, we focus on constructing the PQL operator in
offline
RL for the first time.
Adapting PQL to offline RL not only facilitates faster convergence to the fixed point but also mitigates the effects of extrapolation errors and over-pessimistic value estimation, which are key issues in offline RL.
We begin by recalling the fixed-point characterization of the PQL operator and reinterpreting it from an
offline
RL perspective.
We consider the exact case where no update errors exist in the value functions.

Proposition 1
(
22
)

The fixed point of the PQL operator,
Q^{\pi_{\beta},\pi}
, satisfies:

\displaystyle Q^{\pi_{\beta},\pi}=\left(\lambda{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi_{\beta}}+\left(1-\lambda\right){\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}\right)Q^{\pi_{\beta},\pi}.

Proposition
1
states that a fixed point of the PQL operator coincides with the fixed point of
\lambda{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi_{\beta}}+\left(1-\lambda\right){\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}
for the target policy
\pi
.
Since
\lambda{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi_{\beta}}+\left(1-\lambda\right){\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}
is a contraction with modulus
\gamma
under
L^{\infty}
-norm,
the existence and uniqueness of this fixed point are guaranteed.
However, this fixed point does not ensure the convergence of the optimal Q-function in online RL
unless
\pi_{\beta}
is sufficiently close to
\pi

(
22
;
33
)
.
In contrast, when we use the
fixed
empirical behavior policy
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}
from
\mathcal{D}
,
the Q-function updated by the PQL operator converges to
Q^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}
.

Proposition 2
(
33
)

Let
\pi
be a policy such that
Q^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\geq Q^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\bar{\pi}}
holds pointwise for any policy
\bar{\pi}
.
Then,

Q_{k}
for the
k
-th iteration, updated by the PQL operator, uniformly converges to
Q^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}

with a contraction rate of
\beta^{k}
, where
\beta:=\frac{\gamma\left(1-\lambda\right)}{1-\gamma\lambda}
.

Proposition
2
states a trade-off between bias and contraction rate, that is, PQL with the fixed behavior policy converges to a biased fixed point that differs from
Q^{*}
, with a contraction rate
\beta
.

Interpretation to offline RL.

Prior work
(
33
)
focused on online RL, particularly on how updating the behavior policy is necessary for this fixed point to converge to
Q^{*}
.
However, the fixed point
Q^{\pi}
with
\lambda=0
, corresponding to the value derived from the Bellman operator, can still deviate from
Q^{*}
due to distribution shift in offline RL
(
16
;
34
;
38
)
.
Thus, one of our main points is that we should focus on

how an appropriately chosen
\lambda
mitigates Q-value overestimation for the learned policy
by shifting the fixed point closer to
Q^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}
,
rather than focusing on increasing the bias introduced by a large
\lambda
.
Because the fixed point lies closer to the behavioral value, it naturally induces implicit behavior regularization.
A carefully chosen
\lambda
can effectively address the over-pessimism problem in conservative value estimation methods and yield a more robust learned Q-function, as it mitigates the influence of the learned policy.

4.2
Theoretical Analysis

We aim to mitigate the over-pessimistic estimation of Q-values for the learned policy induced by conservatism.
We integrate the PQL operator into the CQL loss, as it provides a simple and effective way to alleviate the over-pessimism of Q-values.
We replace the standard Bellman operator in Equation
1
with the PQL operator.
This leads to the following iterative Q-value update in CPQL:

\widehat{Q}_{k+1}\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{Q}\biggl\{\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{s,a,s^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\left(Q\left(s,a\right)-{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta},\pi_{k}}\widehat{Q}_{k}\left(s,a\right)\right)^{2}\right]\\
+\alpha\left(\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D},a\sim\pi_{k}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[Q\left(s,a\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{s,a\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[Q\left(s,a\right)\right]\right)\biggl\}.

(2)

The following theorem shows that the expectation of the learned Q-function obtained by iterating Equation
2
lower bounds the expectation of the true Q-function.
This result is an adaptation of Theorem 3.2 in
35
.
For readability, we state the main results without sampling-error terms.
The corresponding finite-sample high-probability bounds are provided in Appendix
B.1
.

Theorem 1
(Lower Bound on the State Value Function of CPQL)

Let
\widehat{Q}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}
denote the Q-function derived from
CPQL as defined in Equation
2
.
Then,
the state value of
\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi
,

\widehat{V}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi\right)(\cdot|s)}\left[\widehat{Q}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}(s,a)\right]
,
lower bounds the true state value of the policy obtained via exact policy evaluation,
V^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}(s)
,
for sufficiently large
\alpha
.
Formally,
for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{D})
,

\widehat{V}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}(s)\leq V^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}(s).

The next two theorems show that the mixture policy learned by CPQL is guaranteed to achieve the performance greater than (or equal to) that of
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}
(Theorem

Theorem 2
(Comparison to the Behavior Policy)

Let
\hat{\pi}:=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{0}}\left[\widehat{V}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}(s)\right]
.

\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}
achieves a policy improvement over
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}
in the actual MDP

as follows:

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)\geq

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)+\frac{\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}(\cdot|s)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}-1\right]\right].

Theorem 3
(Sub-Optimality Gap)

The gap of the expected discounted return between the optimal policy
\pi^{*}
and the mixture policy
\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}
under the actual MDP
\mathcal{M}
satisfies

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\pi^{*}\right)-J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\lambda R_{\mathrm{max}}}{\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}}\Bigl[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi^{*},\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)(s)\Bigl]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}(s)}\!\left[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi^{*},\hat{\pi}\right)\!(s)\!\left(\xi\!\left(\hat{\pi}\right)\!(s)+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\!\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right)\right],

where

\xi\!\left(\hat{\pi}\right)\!(s)\!:=\!\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\!\frac{\pi^{*}(a|s)+\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}
and

d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\right)
is the total variation distance of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
.

Discussion of Theorems
2
and
3

In Theorem
2

\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+(1-\lambda)\hat{\pi}
achieves at least the performance of
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}
under the actual MDP
\mathcal{M}
.
When accounting for sampling error,
\alpha
is chosen such that the conservative term exceeds the sum of the sampling error terms.
However, an excessively large
\alpha
does not guarantee that the sub-optimality gap decreases.
In
Theorem

3
, increasing
\alpha
significantly can lead to larger influence of
\xi(\hat{\pi})
and
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}(\cdot|s)}\!\left[\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)/\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)\right]
on the RHS.
To reduce these gaps, it is crucial to control the two unbounded terms, since their reduction has a greater effect than reducing the total variation distance.
Thus,
\hat{\pi}
approaches
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}
when
\alpha
takes on a large value by Theorem
2
.
However, the bound suggests that CPQL can reduce the sub-optimality bound relative to the
\lambda=0
case under suitable behavior-policy quality and coverage conditions.
If
\hat{\pi}
deviates from
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}
, then
\xi(\hat{\pi})
and
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}(\cdot|s)}\!\left[\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)/\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)\right]
can grow large to infinity.
While CQL lacks a mechanism to directly mitigate this divergence, CPQL addresses it through
\lambda
, which balances between the first and second terms on the RHS.
For example,
since
\pi^{*}
and
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}
are fixed policies,
if
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}
is similar to
\pi^{*}
, choosing a large value of
\lambda
further reduces the sub-optimality gap.
Conversely, if
\hat{\pi}_{\beta}
differs from
\pi^{*}
, adjusting
\lambda
appropriately is more effective than CQL at reducing the sub-optimality gap.

6
Conclusion

CPQL proposes the first approach to a model-free offline multi-step RL algorithm by incorporating the PQL operator for conservative value estimation, mitigating over-pessimism in the Q-function, and reducing the sub-optimality gap.
A key insight of CPQL is that the fixed point of the PQL operator lies closer to the value function of the behavior policy, thereby inducing implicit behavior regularization.
CPQL outperforms existing offline RL algorithms, and its pre-trained Q-function enables PQL to avoid the performance drop at the start of fine-tuning and achieve robust performance improvements in the online phase.
There are two limitations of CPQL.
First, CPQL incurs additional computational cost due to multi-step backups, but in practice, the overhead and the increase in running time are small.
Second, on low-quality datasets, performance may degrade, and single-step updates can be preferable to multi-step operators.
However, CPQL can reproduce the single-step TD learning.

Appendix A
Proof of Technical Lemmas for Theorems

First, we provide a Lemma and a proof for the sampling error bound of the PQL operator.
We assume the concentration properties of the reward function and the transition dynamics:

Assumption 1

Given a state-action pair
\left(s,a\right)\in\mathcal{D}
, the following relationships hold with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\left|r\left(s,a\right)-\hat{r}\left(s,a\right)\right|\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}}{\sqrt{N\left(s,a\right)}},\quad\left\|\mathcal{P}\left(\cdot\mid s,a\right)-\widehat{\mathcal{P}}\left(\cdot\mid s,a\right)\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{\sqrt{N\left(s,a\right)}},

where
C_{r}^{\delta}
and
C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}
are constants that depend on
\delta\in(0,1)
,
N\left(s,a\right)
is the number of samples for
\left(s,a\right)
, and the concentration properties of
r
and
{\mathcal{T}}
, respectively.

Under Assumption
1
and
Proposition

1
, the sampling error between the empirical PQL operator and the actual PQL operator can be bounded, as shown in the following proof:

Lemma 1
(Sampling Error Bound of the PQL operator)

Given a state-action pair
\left(s,a\right)\in\mathcal{D}
,
with probability at least
1-\delta
,
the sampling error between the empirical PQL operator and the actual PQL operator for
(s,a)
satisfies the following inequality:

\left|\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta},\pi}Q(s,a)-\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta},\pi}Q(s,a)\right|\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}R_{\mathrm{max}}/(1-\gamma)}{(1-\gamma\lambda)\sqrt{N(s,a)}},

where
C_{r,\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}
is a constant dependent on the concentration properties
r
and
\mathcal{P}
, with
\delta\in\left(0,1\right)
.

Proof

For
\left(s,a\right)
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta},\pi}Q(s,a)-\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta},\pi}Q(s,a)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\left(r+\gamma(1-\lambda)\mathcal{P}^{\pi}Q(s,a)\right)-\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\left(\hat{r}+\gamma(1-\lambda)\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\pi}Q(s,a)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\left(r+\gamma(1-\lambda)\mathcal{P}^{\pi}Q(s,a)\right)-\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\left(\hat{r}+\gamma(1-\lambda)\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\pi}Q(s,a)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\left(\hat{r}+\gamma(1-\lambda)\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\pi}Q(s,a)\right)-\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\left(\hat{r}+\gamma(1-\lambda)\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\pi}Q(s,a)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\right|\left(\left|r(s,a)-\hat{r}(s,a)\right|+\gamma(1-\lambda)\left\|\mathcal{P}^{\pi}(\cdot|s,a)-\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\pi}(\cdot|s,a)\right\|_{1}Q(s,a)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}-\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\right|\left|\hat{r}+\gamma(1-\lambda)\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\pi}Q(s,a)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\right|\left(\left|r(s,a)-\hat{r}(s,a)\right|+\gamma(1-\lambda)\left\|\mathcal{P}^{\pi}(\cdot|s,a)-\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\pi}(\cdot|s,a)\right\|_{1}Q(s,a)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\gamma\left|\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\right|\left\|\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}(\cdot|s,a)-\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}(\cdot|s,a)\right\|_{1}\left|\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\lambda\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}\right)^{-1}\right|\frac{(1-\gamma\lambda)R_{\mathrm{max}}}{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma(1-\lambda)C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}R_{\mathrm{max}}/(1-\gamma)}{(1-\gamma\lambda)\sqrt{N(s,a)}}+\frac{\gamma\lambda C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}R_{\mathrm{max}}/(1-\gamma)}{(1-\gamma\lambda)\sqrt{N(s,a)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}R_{\mathrm{max}}/(1-\gamma)}{(1-\gamma\lambda)\sqrt{N(s,a)}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
1
.

Based on the interpretation of the sampling error of the PQL operator,
if
\lambda
is zero, the sampling error of the PQL operator is equivalent to that of the Bellman operator.
For example, when
\lambda=0
, the sampling error between the empirical PQL operator and the actual PQL operator for
\left(s,a\right)
is bounded by
\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}R_{\mathrm{max}}/\left(1-\gamma\right)}{\sqrt{N\left(s,a\right)}}
.
This result aligns with the sampling error between the empirical Bellman operator and the actual Bellman operator (Section D.3 in
35
).

In Lemma

\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}-1\right]

In other words,
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]

Lemma 2

For any state
s
and any two policies
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, the following inequality holds:

\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{1}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi_{1}(a|s)}{\pi_{2}(a|s)}-1\right]\geq 0.

with equality if and only if
\pi_{1}
=
\pi_{2}
.

Proof

For any state
s
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{1}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi_{1}(a|s)}{\pi_{2}(a|s)}-1\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi_{1}(a|s)\left(\frac{\pi_{1}(a|s)}{\pi_{2}(a|s)}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\left(\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)+\pi_{2}(a|s)\right)\left(\frac{\pi_{1}(a|s)}{\pi_{2}(a|s)}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\left(\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\right)\left(\frac{\pi_{1}(a|s)}{\pi_{2}(a|s)}-1\right)+\sum_{a}\pi_{2}(a|s)\left(\frac{\pi_{1}(a|s)}{\pi_{2}(a|s)}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\left(\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\right)\left(\frac{\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)}{\pi_{2}(a|s)}\right)+\sum_{a}\left(\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\frac{\left(\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\right)^{2}}{\pi_{2}(a|s)}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\pi_{1}(a|s)
and
\pi_{2}(a|s)
are positive values for all actions and
\sum_{a}\pi_{1}(a|s)=\sum_{a}\pi_{2}(a|s)=1
.
This concludes the proof.

Next, two lemmas are adaptations of Lemma 3 from

Lemma 3

For any two policies
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
,
the vector difference of the discounted future state visitation distributions on two different policies holds:

d^{\pi_{1}}-d^{\pi_{2}}=\gamma\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)d^{\pi_{2}}.

Proof

Recall that the discounted state visitation distribution of a policy
\pi
,
d^{\pi}
, which is defined as

d^{\pi}\left(s\right)=\left(1-\gamma\right)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\mathrm{Pr}\left(s_{t}=s\mid\pi,\mathcal{P}\right).

For finite state spaces,
d^{\pi}
can be expressed in vector form as follows:

d^{\pi}=\left(1-\gamma\right)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi}\right)^{t}d_{0}=\left(1-\gamma\right)\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi}\right)^{-1}d_{0},

where
d_{0}
is the initial state distribution.
Then, we obtain

\displaystyle d^{\pi_{1}}-d^{\pi_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\gamma\right)\left[\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)^{-1}-\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)^{-1}\right]d_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\gamma\right)\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)^{-1}\Bigl[\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)-\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)\Bigl]\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)^{-1}d_{0}

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(1-\gamma\right)\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)^{-1}d_{0}

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)d^{\pi_{2}}.

This concludes the proof.

Lemma 4

The divergence between discounted state visitation distributions,
||d^{\pi_{1}}-d^{\pi_{2}}||
, is bounded by an average divergence of the policies
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
:

\displaystyle||d^{\pi_{1}}-d^{\pi_{2}}||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{\pi_{2}}}\left[\sum_{a}\Bigl|\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\Bigl|\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{\pi_{2}}}\left[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\right)(s)\right],

where
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\right)(s)=\left(1/2\right)\sum_{a}\left|\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\right|
.

Proof

First, from Lemma
3
, we obtain

\displaystyle||d^{\pi_{1}}-d^{\pi_{2}}||_{1}

\displaystyle=\gamma||\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)d^{\pi_{2}}||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma||\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)^{-1}||_{1}||\left(\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)d^{\pi_{2}}||_{1}.

||\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)^{-1}||_{1}
is bounded by:

||\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right)^{-1}||_{1}\leq\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left(||\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}||_{1}\right)^{t}=\left(1-\gamma\right)^{-1}.

To conclude the lemma, we bound
||\left(\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)d^{\pi_{2}}||_{1}
.

\displaystyle||\left(\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)d^{\pi_{2}}||_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}}\left|\sum_{s}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right)d^{\pi_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\left|\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathcal{P}^{\pi_{2}}\right|d^{\pi_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\left|\sum_{a}\mathcal{P}\left(s^{\prime}|s,a\right)\left(\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\right)d^{\pi_{2}}\left(s\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s,a,s^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}\left(s^{\prime}|s,a\right)\left|\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\right|d^{\pi_{2}}\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s,a}\left|\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\right|d^{\pi_{2}}\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{\pi_{2}}}\left[\sum_{a}\Bigl|\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\Bigl|\right]

Therefore, we obtain that:

\displaystyle||d^{\pi_{1}}-d^{\pi_{2}}||_{1}\leq\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{\pi_{2}}}\left[\sum_{a}\Bigl|\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\Bigl|\right].

If we express this inequality in terms of the total variation distance, it becomes the following inequality:

\displaystyle||d^{\pi_{1}}-d^{\pi_{2}}||_{1}\leq\frac{2\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{\pi_{2}}}\left[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\right)(s)\right].

This concludes the proof.

We prove the following lemma, which bounds the difference between the expected discounted return under
\mathcal{M}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
.

Lemma 5

Given any policy
\pi
,
for any MDP
\mathcal{M}
and the empirical MDP
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
,
the following holds with probability at least
1-\delta
:

\displaystyle\left|J_{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi)-J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma R_{\mathrm{max}}C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}/(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\lambda+\left(1-\lambda\right)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right)\right].

Proof

To prove this inequality, we use the triangle inequality to separate the gap in the expected discounted return into differences in rewards and transition dynamics, as follows:

\displaystyle\left|J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi\right)-J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\Biggl|\sum_{s,a}d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)+(1-\lambda)\pi(a|s)\right)r_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(s,a\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\sum_{s,a}d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi(a|s)}\left(s\right)\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)+(1-\lambda)\pi(a|s)\right)r_{\mathcal{M}}\left(s,a\right)\Biggl|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left|\sum_{s,a}d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)\underbrace{\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)+(1-\lambda)\pi(a|s)\right)\left(r_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(s,a\right)-r_{\mathcal{M}}\left(s,a\right)\right)}_{=:\Delta_{r}\left(s\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left|\sum_{s,a}\underbrace{\left(d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)-d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)\right)}_{=:\Delta_{d}\left(s\right)}\pi(a|s)r_{\mathcal{M}}\left(s,a\right)\right|.

We first bound the term that includes the difference between the actual rewards and the estimated rewards by applying concentration inequalities to derive an upper bound for
\Delta_{r}\left(s\right)
.
Note that under concentration assumptions, and using the fact that
\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{r}\left(s\right)\right]=0
in the limit of infinite data, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{r}\left(s\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)+(1-\lambda)\pi(a|s)\right)\left|r_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(s,a\right)-r_{\mathcal{M}}\left(s,a\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)+(1-\lambda)\pi(a|s)\right)\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)\cdot\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\sum_{a}\left(\lambda\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}+(1-\lambda)\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\lambda\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}+(1-\lambda)\sum_{a}\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}\right).

(3)

Next, we bound the term that involves the difference between the actual and estimated transition dynamics by applying concentration inequalities to derive an upper bound for
\Delta_{d}\left(s\right)
.
By Lemma
3
, we obtain the following equation:

\Delta_{d}=\gamma\left(\mathcal{I}-\gamma\mathcal{P}_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\right)^{-1}\underbrace{\left(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}-\mathcal{P}_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\right)}_{=:\Delta_{P}}d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}.

We know that
\gamma
is positive and
||\left(I-\gamma\mathcal{P}^{\pi}\right)^{-1}||_{1}\leq\left(1-\gamma\right)^{-1}
for any policy
\pi
,
we only need to bound the remaining terms.

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg|\Delta_{P}~d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\bigg|\bigg|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}}\left|\sum_{s}\Delta_{P}\left(s^{\prime}|s\right)d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime},s}\left|\Delta_{P}\left(s^{\prime}|s\right)\right|d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime},s}\left|\sum_{a}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(s^{\prime}|s,a\right)-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\left(s^{\prime}|s,a\right)\right)\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi(a|s)\right)\right|d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime},s}\bigg|\bigg|\mathcal{P}_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\cdot|s,a\right)-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\cdot|s,a\right)\bigg|\bigg|_{1}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi(a|s)\right)d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)\frac{C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\sum_{a}\frac{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)\frac{C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\sum_{a}\left(\lambda\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)\frac{C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\lambda\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\sum_{a}\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}\right)

where the last inequality is derived from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
Hence, we can bound
\Delta_{d}\left(s\right)
as follows:

\left|\Delta_{d}(s)\right|\leq\frac{\gamma C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}\left(s\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\lambda\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\sum_{a}\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}\right).

(4)

To derive the final upper bound of the objective function, it is necessary to bound
\sum_{a}\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}\right]=\sum_{a}\frac{\left(\pi(a|s)\right)^{2}}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\left(\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{a}\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}\right)^{2}\leq\left|\mathcal{A}\right|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}\right]

Then we obtain

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}\right]}\leq\sum_{a}\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}\leq\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}\right]}.

(5)

By Equation
3
, Equation
4
, and Equation
5
, we have that:

\displaystyle\bigl|J_{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi)-J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi)\bigl|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma R_{\mathrm{max}}C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}/(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\lambda+\left(1-\lambda\right)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right)\right]

(6)

Equation
6
reflects the trade-off between
1
and
\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}
(
\geq 1
), by weighting them with
\lambda
and
1-\lambda
, respectively.
This bound can be tighter than the single-step counterpart when the density-ratio term is controlled.
This completes the proof of Lemma
5
.

The proof of this statement is divided into three parts:

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)-J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)-J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)}_{=:\Delta_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)-J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)}_{=:\Delta_{2}}+\underbrace{J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)-J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)}_{=:\Delta_{3}}.

By Lemma
5
, we obtain the upper bound of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{3}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{1}\right|\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma R_{\mathrm{max}}C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}/(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\lambda+\left(1-\lambda\right)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{3}\right|\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma R_{\mathrm{max}}C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}/(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\right].

Next, we obtain the lower bound of
\Delta_{2}
by the definition of
\hat{\pi}
and Equation
7
:

\displaystyle J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)-\frac{\alpha\left(1-\lambda\right)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right]\geq J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)-0.

Thus, we have that:

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}\geq\frac{\alpha\left(1-\lambda\right)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right]

Therefore, by integrating the bound of
\Delta_{1}
,
\Delta_{2}
, and
\Delta_{3}
,
we obtain that:

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\geq J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)+\frac{\alpha\left(1-\lambda\right)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{C_{r,\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(1+\lambda+\left(1-\lambda\right)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right)\right],

where
C_{r,\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}=C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma R_{\mathrm{max}}C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}/(1-\gamma)
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

When
\lambda=0
, we obtain the following lower bound:

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\hat{\pi}\right)-J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right]-\frac{2C_{r,\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N(s)}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}(\cdot|s)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right].

This result coincides with Theorem 3.6 from CQL
(
35
)
.
Our theorem converges under the same conditions, thereby ensuring consistency with the CQL framework.

B.3
Theorem
3

We first present, to the best of our knowledge, theoretical guarantees concerning the sub-optimality gap between the optimal policy and the mixture policy.

Theorem 3
(Sub-Optimality Gap)

With probability at least
1-\delta
, the gap of the expected discounted return between the optimal policy
\pi^{*}
and the mixture policy
\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+(1-\lambda)\hat{\pi}
under the actual MDP
\mathcal{M}
satisfies

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\pi^{*}\right)-J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\lambda R_{\mathrm{max}}}{\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}}\Bigl[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi^{*},\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)(s)\Bigl]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}(s)}\!\left[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi^{*},\hat{\pi}\right)\!(s)\!\left(\xi\!\left(\hat{\pi}\right)\!(s)+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\!\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C_{r,\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\lambda+\left(1-\lambda\right)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C_{r,\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\pi^{*}}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{*}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi^{*}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right],

where

\xi\!\left(\hat{\pi}\right)\!(s)\!:=\!\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\!\frac{\pi^{*}(a|s)+\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}
and

d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\right)
is the total variation distance of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
.

Proof

The proof of this statement is divided into four parts:

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\pi^{*}\right)-J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\pi^{*}\right)-J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\pi^{*}\right)}_{=:\Delta_{1}}+\underbrace{J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\pi^{*}\right)-J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}\right)}_{=:\Delta_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}\right)-J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)}_{=:\Delta_{3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{J_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)-J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)}_{=:\Delta_{4}}.

By Lemma
5
, we obtain the upper bound of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{4}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{1}\right|\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma R_{\mathrm{max}}C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}/(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\pi^{*}}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{*}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi^{*}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{4}\right|\leq\frac{C_{r}^{\delta}+\gamma R_{\mathrm{max}}C_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}/(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\lambda+\left(1-\lambda\right)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right)\right]

Next, we derive the upper bound of
\Delta_{2}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left|\sum_{s,a}\left(d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\pi^{*}}\left(s\right)\pi^{*}(a|s)-d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}\left(s\right)\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+(1-\lambda)\pi^{*}\right)(a|s)\right)r_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}\left(s,a\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R_{\mathrm{max}}}{1-\gamma}\left|\sum_{s}\left(d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\pi^{*}}\left(s\right)-d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}\left(s\right)\right)\right|+\frac{\lambda R_{\mathrm{max}}}{1-\gamma}\left|\sum_{s,a}d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}\left(s\right)\left(\pi^{*}-\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)(s,a)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma\lambda R_{\mathrm{max}}}{\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}}\left[\sum_{a}\Bigl|\pi^{*}(a|s)-\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)\Bigl|\right]+\frac{\lambda R_{\mathrm{max}}}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}}\left[\sum_{a}\Bigl|\pi^{*}(a|s)-\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)\Bigl|\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\lambda R_{\mathrm{max}}}{\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}}\left[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi^{*},\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)(s)\right],

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
4
and the last equality holds with the definition of total variation distance,
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\right)(s)=\sum_{a}\left|\pi_{1}(a|s)-\pi_{2}(a|s)\right|/2
.

By the definition of
\hat{\pi}
and Equation
7
, we derive the upper bound of
\Delta_{3}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}\Biggl|\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}(s)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*})(\cdot|s)}\left[\frac{\pi^{*}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi})(\cdot|s)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right]\Biggl|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\left|\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}(s)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{*}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\pi^{*}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}\right]\right]\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}\left|d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}\left(s\right)-d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}\left(s\right)\right|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}(s)}\left[\sum_{a}\frac{\left(\pi^{*}(a|s)+\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)\right)\left|\pi^{*}(a|s)-\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)\right|}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}\left|d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}\left(s\right)-d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}\left(s\right)\right|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}(s)}\left[\underbrace{\sum_{a}\frac{\pi^{*}(a|s)+\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}}_{:=\xi(\hat{\pi})(s)}\sum_{a}\left|\pi^{*}(a|s)-\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha\gamma\left(1-\lambda\right)}{\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}(s)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\sum_{a}\Bigl|\hat{\pi}(a|s)-\pi^{*}(a|s)\Bigl|\right],

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}(s)}\left[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi^{*},\hat{\pi}\right)(s)\left(\xi\left(\hat{\pi}\right)(s)+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right)\right],

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
\xi\left(\hat{\pi}\right)
and Lemma
4
.

Therefore, by integrating the bound of
\Delta_{1}
,
\Delta_{2}
,
\Delta_{3}
and
\Delta_{4}
,
we have that:

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\pi^{*}\right)-J_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\lambda R_{\mathrm{max}}}{\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}}\Bigl[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi^{*},\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\right)(s)\Bigl]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\alpha(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\pi^{*}}(s)}\!\left[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\pi^{*},\hat{\pi}\right)\!(s)\!\left(\xi\!\left(\hat{\pi}\right)\!(s)+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\!\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}(a|s)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}(a|s)}-1\right]\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C_{r,\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}^{\lambda\hat{\pi}_{\beta}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\hat{\pi}}(s)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}}{\sqrt{N\left(s\right)}}\left(\lambda+\left(1-\lambda\right)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\hat{\pi}\left(\cdot|s\right)}\left[\frac{\hat{\pi}\left(a|s\right)}{\hat{\pi}_{\beta}\left(a|s\right)}\right]}\right)\right]

\text{Normalized score}=\frac{\text{average return - return of the random policy}}{\text{return of the expert policy - return of the random policy}}\times 100.

0
represents the performance of a random policy, and

represents the performance of an expert policy.

If the ant successfully reaches the goal location, it is rewarded with
1.0
, indicating a successful episode.
Conversely, if the ant fails to reach the goal, it receives a reward of
0.0
, reflecting an unsuccessful attempt.

Additional Related Works

Model-based Offline RL.

Model-based offline RL methods build dynamics and reward models from the offline dataset,
leveraging state transitions and rewards of estimated model outputs for planning and policy improvements.
They typically achieve this by
penalizing the reward function with the error between the ground truth and estimated models
(
77
;
28
;
54
;
40
;
29
;
60
)
,
learning conservative Q-function within the model-based regime
(
76
)
,
and training the policy and the dynamics model adversarially
(
56
)
.
Algorithms that learn by planning synthetic trajectories under estimated dynamics typically perform policy evaluation using a single-step approach.
However, applying CPQL in model-based offline RL settings can be particularly beneficial, similar to COMBO
(
76
)
.
It enables more conservative learning of the Q-function, mitigating overestimation issues.
This suggests that CPQL has broad applicability and can enhance various aspects of model-based reinforcement learning.

Recently,
50
considered a model-based offline RL approach, computing the target Q-function by applying lower expectile regression to
\lambda
-returns on synthetic trajectories planned from the estimated dynamics.
This method differs from ours: we take a model-free offline RL approach, leveraging offline trajectories collected from the
actual
environment.
Our method effectively enhances performance by utilizing real trajectories rather than relying on synthetic trajectories, which are subject to model estimation uncertainty.
Furthermore, while they additionally employ lower expectile regression to obtain a conservative return estimate,
CPQL derives a conservative value estimate solely by integrating the multi-step operator with a conservative estimation mechanism.

36
proposed Uni-O4, an offline policy evaluation method for safe multi-step policy improvement based on an approximate model and fitted Q evaluation.
However, CPQL differs conceptually from Uni-O4.
First, CPQL applies the PQL operator directly to offline trajectories from the real environment, whereas Uni-O4 computes its multi-step objective by rolling out an approximate transition model
\hat{T}
trained on the offline dataset and aggregating fitted-Q estimates along simulated trajectories (AM-Q).
Second, CPQL uses these multi-step returns in the critic update to obtain conservative value estimates for control, while Uni-O4 uses AM-Q only as an offline policy evaluation oracle that gates PPO-style policy updates.
Finally, Uni-O4 trains an ensemble behavior-cloning policy to stay close to the data-support region and stabilize AM-Q, whereas CPQL does not require learning the behavior policy or a transition model.

Offline Trajectory.

Several works
(
79
;
39
;
73
)
have attempted to handle offline trajectories in different ways to adaptively utilize information from past observations, where rewards have already been realized.
They propose several methods, such as return-based data rebalancing in
79
, priority assignment based on trajectory quality using average, minimum, maximum, and quantile rewards in
39
, as well as a least-squares-based reward redistribution method for reward estimation in
73
.
However, these methods are not applicable in sparse reward settings, such as AntMaze tasks, and were not empirically tested in such environments.
In contrast, we show that CPQL achieves superior performance in sparse reward settings.
