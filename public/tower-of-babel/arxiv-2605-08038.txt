Invariant domain preserving limiting of time explicit and time implicit discretizations for systems of conservation laws
Bartolomeo Fanizza, Florent Renac

Abstract
This work concerns the design and analysis of a limiting technique that allows the preservation of invariant domains for high-order numerical approximations of nonlinear hyperbolic systems of conservation laws. The method can be applied to any conservative discretization method in space as well as to a wide range of explicit and implicit time integration schemes. The method limits the high-order solution around a low-order accurate solution that is known to preserve all the invariant domains. It generalizes the flux-corrected transport limiter [J. P. Boris and D. L. Book, J. Comput. Phys., 11, 1973; S. T. Zalesak, J. Comput. Phys., 31, 1979] to systems of conservation laws and relies on the limitation of antidiffusive fluxes, but defines the limiting coefficients so as to express the limited solution as a convex combination of invariant domain preserving quantities similarly to the convex limiting framework [Guermond et al., Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Engrg., 347, 2019]. We give details on the derivation of this limiting technique and provide some illustration with finite volume or discontinuous Galerkin (DG) space discretizations associated to explicit or implicit Runge-Kutta methods as well as to time DG integrations. The limiter is applied iteratively to refine the limited solution around the high-order one, while preserving the invariant domains, and a heuristic is proposed to accelerate its convergence. Numerical experiments solving one- and two-dimensional problems involving scalar hyperbolic equations and the compressible Euler equations are presented to illustrate the properties of these schemes.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf u}+\nabla\cdot{\bf f}({\bf u})

\displaystyle=0,\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1a)

\displaystyle{\bf u}(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle={\bf u}_{0}(\cdot),\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.

\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\prime}({\bf u})^{\top}{\bf f}_{i}^{\prime}({\bf u})={\bf q}_{i}^{\prime}({\bf u})^{\top},\quad 1\leq i\leq d,\quad\forall{\bf u}\in\Omega^{a}.

(3)

Example 1.1
(Scalar conservation laws)

In the scalar case,

Example 1.2
(Compressible Euler equations)

For the compressible Euler equations for gas dynamics, the conservative variables and fluxes in

1a

{\bf u}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\
\rho{\bf v}\\
\rho E\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf f}({\bf u})=\begin{pmatrix}\rho{\bf v}^{\top}\\
\rho{\bf v}{\bf v}^{\top}+\mathrm{p}{\bf I}_{d}\\
(\rho E+\mathrm{p}){\bf v}^{\top}\end{pmatrix},

(4)

where
\rho

\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{p}(\frac{1}{\rho},e)
with

\partial_{\tau}\mathrm{p}(\tau,e)<0

\Omega^{a}=\{{\bf u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+2}:\;\rho>0,{\bf v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},e>0\}

1a
and

\eta({\bf u})=-\rho\mathrm{s}
and
{\bf q}({\bf u})=-\rho\mathrm{s}{\bf v}

\mathrm{s}=\mathrm{s}(\frac{1}{\rho},e)

\mathrm{T}d\mathrm{s}=de+\mathrm{p}d\big(\tfrac{1}{\rho}\big)
with
\mathrm{T}
the temperature.
\mathcal{D}=\{{\bf u}\in\Omega^{a}:\;\mathrm{s}({\bf u})\geq\mathrm{s}_{0}\}
, with
\mathrm{s}_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}
, is an invariant domain.

We are here interested in the approximation of
eq.

1
with a high-order (HO) discretization. These methods accurately capture smooth solutions, but may not satisfy the above properties of invariant domain preservation at the discrete level. In contrast, low-order (LO) numerical schemes such as the Godunov scheme
[
20
]
, the Lax-Friedrichs and flux vector splitting schemes
[
12
]
, or the guaranteed maximum speed graph viscosity scheme
[
17
]
have been shown to be invariant domain preserving (IDP). The flux-corrected transport (FCT) limiter
[
30
,
4
,
50
,
26
,
41
,
32
]
and the convex limiter
[
16
,
15
,
36
]
make use of these LO IDP schemes to limit the HO solution around the LO solution so as to preserve the invariant domains, while keeping conservation thanks to the introduction of antidiffusive fluxes. The FCT limiter can be applied to both explicit and implicit time stepping in contrast to convex limiting that is restricted to explicit time stepping. The FCT limiter is, however, designed to impose bounds on the components of the solution, while it is often necessary to impose bounds on nonlinear functions of the solution, such as the internal energy in the compressible Euler equations, or the void and mass fractions in multiphase and multispecies models. For this reason, the FCT limiter is mainly restricted to scalar equations. Monolithic convex limiting was then proposed that is based on adding a graph Laplacian to the HO scheme with a coefficient large enough to ensure that the so-called bar states preserve invariant domains
[
28
]
. The antidiffusive fluxes are then limited in order to reduce the effect of the graph viscosity, while preserving invariant domains and keeping conservation. It was then extended to time implicit integration of the compressible Euler equations in
[
33
]
by exploiting the homogeneity property of the flux Jacobian. The introduction of bar states in finite volume (FV) and discontinuous Galerkin (DG) schemes was possible in
[
16
]
through the use of the Rusanov numerical flux at interfaces. Even though HO approximations may not be affected by the choice of the numerical flux for smooth solutions or large scale oscillations around discontinuities
[
38
,
40
]
, this is no longer the case when small scale flow features are involved and the numerical flux has a strong effect on their resolution
[
34
,
8
]
.

In this work, we introduce a fairly general limiter that may be applied to any hyperbolic conservation laws with any conservative discretization method on general meshes and a large range of explicit and implicit time integration schemes. Our approach also relies on the limitation of antidiffusive fluxes as in the FCT framework, but the limiting coefficients are defined in order to express the limited solution as a convex combination of IDP quantities similarly to convex limiting. We depart from the latter approach in the definition of the limiting coefficients and the convex combinations that do not use bar states and thus allow the use of any consistent and conservative numerical flux in the HO scheme. The coefficients of the convex combination here define the limited solution as closely as possible to local convex combinations of the LO and HO solutions, while maintaining conservation. We embed the limiter in an iterative procedure
[
43
]
that refines the limited solution as closely as possible to the HO solution, while preserving invariant domains. This iterative procedure also allows to recover positive HO solutions and we propose a heuristic to accelerate its convergence. We focus on finite volume (FV) and discontinuous Gakerkin (DG) schemes, as well as the DG spectral element method (DGSEM) as illustrative examples, but the present approach may be applied to any conservative discretization. The space discretizations may be associated to explicit and implicit Runge-Kutta methods, explicit and implicit multistep methods, as well as to time DG integrations among others.

2
Invariant domain limiting

We here introduce the present limiter in the context of a generic scheme in space and restrict to a first-order time discretization without loss of generality. The application to high-order explicit and implicit schemes will be described in
section

3
, while examples of space discretization methods will be given in
section

4
.

2.1
Generic discretization method

The space domain is discretized with a shape-regular mesh
\Omega_{h}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
consisting of nonoverlapping and nonempty open elements
\kappa
and we assume that it forms a partition of
\Omega
. By
p+1\geq 1
we usually refer to the formal order of space accuracy of the scheme and
N_{p}\geq 1
denotes the number of DOFs per equation and per element
\kappa
in
\Omega_{h}
.

Using a method of lines, we consider conservative space discretization schemes for
eq.

1a
in the general following form

{\bf M}d_{t}{\bf u}_{h}+{\bf R}_{h}({\bf u}_{h})=0,

(5)

where
{\bf u}_{h}
denotes the global numerical solution represented by the general ansatz

{\bf u}_{h}({\bf x},t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i}(t)\phi_{\kappa}^{i}({\bf x})\quad\forall{\bf x}\in\kappa\in\Omega_{h},t\geq 0,

(6)

where
{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{eq}}
are the DOFs of the discrete problem to be solved. Canonical examples we will use in the numerical experiments of
section

5
are the finite volume (FV) schemes where
N_{p}=1
and
\phi_{\kappa}^{1}\equiv 1_{\kappa}
the indicator function of the cell
\kappa
; or the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method where the
(\phi_{\kappa}^{1\leq i\leq N_{p}})
are some basis functions spanning a given space of polynomials of degree
p\geq 0
restricted to the element
\kappa
and
N_{p}
is its dimension.

By
{\bf R}_{h}
we denote the discretization of the space derivatives in
eq.

1a
, while
{\bf M}
denotes the global mass matrix. To avoid technical complexity, we assume here that
{\bf M}
is diagonal:
{\bf M}={\bf I}_{n_{eq}}\otimes\diag(M_{\kappa}^{i}:\;\kappa\in\Omega_{h},1\leq i\leq N_{p})
with
{\bf I}_{n_{eq}}
the identity matrix of size
n_{eq}
. However, it is possible to handle general mass matrices in the same way as similar limiters such as FCT and convex limiting
[
26
,
27
]
.

The time is now discretized into time steps
\Delta t^{(n)}>0
and we set
t^{(n+1)}=t^{(n)}+\Delta t^{(n)}
for
n\geq 0
. We restrict ourselves to a first-order time discretization in this section for the sake of clarity. Given a global numerical solution
{\bf u}_{h}^{(n)}\in\mathcal{D}
at time
t^{(n)}
that is assumed to preserve all invariant domains of the PDE, here we consider a high-order space discretization scheme in the general following form

\frac{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\Delta t^{(n)}}\big({\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}-{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n}\big)+{\bf R}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i}({\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{n+\theta})=0\quad\forall\kappa\in\Omega_{h},1\leq i\leq N_{p},

(7)

where
{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{n+\theta}
denotes the HO solution at time
t^{n+\theta}
with DOFs
{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+\theta}
, while
\theta=0
corresponds to the explicit forward-Euler scheme and
\theta=1
to the implicit backward-Euler scheme. In the explicit case
\theta=0
, we have
{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{n}={\bf u}_{h}^{n}
. Here,
M_{\kappa}^{i}>0
refers to the
i
th entry of the mass matrix in the element
\kappa
.

Scheme
eq.

7
is HO accurate in space, but may fail to preserve the invariant domains. Following the FCT
[
26
,
27
]
and convex limiting
[
15
]
frameworks, among others, we aim at limiting the HO solution around a LO IDP solution computed from the LO scheme

\frac{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\Delta t^{(n)}}\big({\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}-{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n}\big)+{\bf R}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i}({\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+\theta})=0\quad\forall\kappa\in\Omega_{h},1\leq i\leq N_{p},

(8)

where again
{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n}={\bf u}_{h}^{n}
for
\theta=0
. The comparison of the HO and LO schemes
eqs.

7
and
8
will allow us to introduce the antidiffusive fluxes in the next section that commonly constitute an essential aspect to keep conservation of the present limiter.

2.2
Invariant domain preserving limiter

2.2.1
Antidiffusive fluxes

Subtracting scheme
eq.

8
for the LO solution from
eq.

7
for the HO solution gives

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\Delta t^{(n)}}\big({\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}-{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}\big)

\displaystyle={\bf R}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i}({\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+\theta})-{\bf R}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i}({\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{n+\theta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}\quad\forall\kappa\in\Omega_{h},1\leq i\leq N_{p},

(9)

where
{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}
correspond to the so-called antidiffusive fluxes
[
50
,
26
]
and satisfy the conservation property:
{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}=-{\bf A}_{\kappa^{\prime},\kappa}^{j,i,n+\theta}
(hence
{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa}^{i,i,n+\theta}=0
). In the usual terminology,
{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}
denotes the set of antidiffusive element contributions needed to recover the HO accuracy from the LO scheme
[
26
, Sec. 2]
. This corresponds to the faces of the element
\kappa

[
4
,
50
]
in the original FCT framework, or to the stencil of the scheme in the convex limiting framework
[
15
]
. The antidiffusive fluxes will be defined in
section

4
.

The limited solution
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1}
is then defined by

\frac{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\Delta t^{(n)}}\big({\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1}-{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}\big)=\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}\quad\forall\kappa\in\Omega_{h},1\leq i\leq N_{p},

(10)

where the limiting coefficients
0\leq l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}\leq 1
should be defined to guarantee that
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1}\in\mathcal{D}
. Note that setting all the
l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}=1
gives the HO solution, while
l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}=0
gives the LO solution. We further require
l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}=l_{\kappa^{\prime},\kappa}^{j,i}
to maintain the global conservation of the limited scheme:

\sum_{\kappa\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}M_{\kappa}^{i}{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1}=\sum_{\kappa\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}M_{\kappa}^{i}{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}=\sum_{\kappa\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}M_{\kappa}^{i}{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}=\sum_{\kappa\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}M_{\kappa}^{i}{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n}

in the case of compactly supported solutions.

2.2.2
Limiting coefficients

The limiting coefficients
l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}
in
eq.

10
are defined as follows. Let us introduce coefficients
\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}>0
such that
\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}=1
, then rewrite
eq.

10
as

{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1}={\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}+\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}=\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}\Big({\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}+\frac{l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}}{\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}}\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}\Big).

(11)

Now we look for the largest coefficients
0\leq k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}\leq 1
such that

{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}+k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}\in\mathcal{D}

(12)

and then set

l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}=l_{\kappa^{\prime},\kappa}^{j,i}:=\min(\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j},\alpha_{\kappa}^{i}k_{\kappa}^{i})

(13)

to keep conservation. Note that the coefficients
k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}
in
eq.

12
are well defined by convexity of
\mathcal{D}
and the fact that
{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}\in\mathcal{D}
.

The optimal choices for the
\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}
are the ones that balance the effects of the limiter from every contribution
(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}
to the
i
th DOF. We therefore impose
\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}=\alpha_{\kappa}^{i}k_{\kappa}^{i}
for all
(\kappa^{\prime},j)
in
{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}
, so using
\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}=1
gives

\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}=\frac{1}{k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}}\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{(\kappa^{\prime\prime},l)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}\frac{1}{k_{\kappa^{\prime\prime}}^{l}}}\quad\forall(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}.

(14)

Let us propose an interpretation of the present limiter. Assuming that
eq.

13
results in
l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}=\alpha_{\kappa}^{i}k_{\kappa}^{i}
for all
(\kappa^{\prime},j)
in
{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}
and using
eq.

11
gives

\displaystyle{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1}

\displaystyle={\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}+\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}\frac{1}{k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}}}\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}\sum\limits_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}\frac{1}{k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}}}\Big){\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}+\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}\frac{1}{k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}}}{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1},

which would define
{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1}
as a convex combination of
{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}
and the HO update
{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}
.

Note that the above relation may not hold when it happens that
l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}<\alpha_{\kappa}^{i}k_{\kappa}^{i}
for some
(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}
due to
eq.

13
; however, the choice
eqs.

12
,
13
, and
14
is an attempt to keep the limited solution as close as possible to local convex combinations of the LO and HO solutions, while maintaining conservation.

As a consequence of the limiter
eq.

10
, the limited solution is a convex combination of quantities that are in
\mathcal{D}
and therefore satisfies all the invariant domains:
{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1}\in\mathcal{D}
. Indeed, rewriting again
eq.

10
as in
eq.

11
and using the fact that
l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}\leq\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}
by
eq.

13
, we have

{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}+\frac{l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}}{\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}}\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}=\Big(1-\frac{l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}}{\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}}\Big){\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}+\frac{l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}}{\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}}\Big({\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}+k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j}\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}\Big),

which is a convex combination of quantities in
\mathcal{D}
from
eq.

12
and we conclude that
{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1}\in\mathcal{D}
from
eq.

11
.

2.3
Iterative limiter

Once the limiter is applied to produce a limited solution
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1}\in\mathcal{D}
, this solution may be reused to again limit the HO solution
{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{n+1}
around this new IDP solution
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1}
. This defines an iterative limiting procedure that has also been proposed in the context of the FCT limiter
[
43
]
. We here describe this procedure in the context of the present limiter.

Let
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1,(k)}\in\mathcal{D}
be an IDP solution after iteration
k\geq 0
, the objective being to apply the limiter introduced above in
section

2.2
to define a new conservative IDP solution
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1,(k+1)}\in\mathcal{D}
closer to
{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{n+1}
. The unlimited and limited schemes read

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\Delta t^{(n)}}\big({\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}-{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k)},

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\Delta t^{(n)}}\big({\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k+1)}-{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k)},

(15)

where we look for coefficients
0\leq l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}=l_{\kappa^{\prime},\kappa}^{j,i,(k)}\leq 1
such that
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1,(k+1)}\in\mathcal{D}
.

Using the notations in
section

2.2
, we have
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1,(0)}\equiv{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+1}
defined from
eq.

8
and
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1,(1)}\equiv{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1}
the first limited solution defined from
eq.

10
, while
l_{\kappa^{\prime},\kappa}^{j,i,(0)}=l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j}
and
{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(0)}={\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}
.

Subtracting the two above equations, we get the relation between the HO and limited solutions:

\frac{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\Delta t^{(n)}}\big({\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}-{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k+1)}\big)=\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}(1-l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}){\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k)}=\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k+1)},

where the
{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k+1)}:=(1-l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}){\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k)}
constitute the antidiffusive fluxes between the new LO solution,
{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1,(k+1)}
, and the HO solution,
{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{n+1}
that may be reused for the next step and further limited with
l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k+1)}
coefficients. By induction, we obtain an explicit form of the antidiffusive fluxes
{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k+1)}=\Pi_{l=0}^{k}(1-l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(l)}){\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}
. This defines an iterative algorithm that does not require one to apply the numerical scheme: at a given iteration
k\geq 0
we only need to compute the new limiting coefficients
l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}
and scale the antidiffusive fluxes by
(1-l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)})
to obtain the next ones.

The algorithm flowchart of the iterative limiter is described in
Algorithm

1
, where the iterative loop is stopped when a maximum number of iterations or a given tolerance on the relative distance between two consecutive limited solutions is reached. The following results provide some information about convergence of the present iterative limiter.

Proposition 2.1
(Existence of a fixed point)

The iterative limiter
eq.

15
has at least one fixed point.

Proof 1

Every iteration of the limiter satisfies
\|{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}-{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}\|\leq\tfrac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)}\|{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}\|
and therefore remains in a finite dimensional ball equipped with the Euclidean norm and centered on
{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}
. By continuity of the mapping
eq.

15
between two successive limited solutions, there exists at least one fixed point according to Brouwer’s fixed point theorem in finite dimension.

Proposition 2.2
(Recovering IDP HO solutions)

Assume that
{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}\in\mathcal{D}
and that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that the ball
{\cal B}({\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1},\epsilon)\subset\mathcal{D}
and that all
k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}
in
eq.

12
are chosen to satisfy
{\cal B}({\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}+k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta},\epsilon)\subset\mathcal{D}
at every iteration
k\geq 0
of the limiter. Then, the iterative limiter
eq.

15
converges to a unique fixed point satisfying
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}={\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}
.

Proof 2

First, we proceed by induction to show that
{\cal B}({\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)},\epsilon)\subset\mathcal{D}
holds for every
k\geq 0
. This is true for
k=0
by assumption:
{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(0)}={\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{i,n+1}
. Then, using
eqs.

11
and
13
at iteration
k
we rewrite
eq.

15
as

\displaystyle{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k+1)}=

\displaystyle{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}+\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}\Big({\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}+\frac{l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}}{\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}}\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(\kappa^{\prime},j)\in{\cal N}_{\kappa}^{i}}\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}\bigg(\Big(1-\frac{l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}}{\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}}\Big){\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}

\displaystyle\hskip 64.01869pt+\frac{l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}}{\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}}\Big({\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}+k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k)}\Big)\bigg)

which is indeed a convex combination due to
eq.

and confirms the induction. Therefore, there exists
\epsilon_{k}
such that
0<\epsilon_{k}\leq k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}
, from which we deduce
\tfrac{\epsilon_{k}}{{\cal N}_{max}}\leq l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(k)}<1
by
eqs.

13
and
14
, where
{\cal N}_{max}:=\max(\#{\cal N}_{\kappa\in\Omega_{h}}^{1\leq i\leq N_{p}})
. We therefore have
0<\Pi_{l=0}^{k-1}(1-l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(l)})\leq(1-\tfrac{\epsilon_{k}}{{\cal N}_{max}})^{k+1}
and we conclude by noting that
{\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta,(k+1)}=\Pi_{l=0}^{k-1}(1-l_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,(l)}){\bf A}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}^{i,j,n+\theta}\rightarrow_{k}0
.

Corollary 2.1
(Maximum convergence speed)

Under the assumptions of
proposition

2.2
and further assuming that no limiting is needed, i.e. every
k_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{j,(k)}=1

\#{\cal N}_{\kappa\in\Omega_{h}}^{1\leq i\leq N_{p}}={\cal N}_{max}

1-\tfrac{1}{{\cal N}_{max}}
as Lipschitz coefficient:

\|{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}-{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k+1)}\|=\big(1-\tfrac{1}{{\cal N}_{max}}\big)\|{\bf U}_{\kappa,\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{i,n+1}-{\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n+1,(k)}\|.

2.5
IDP constraints

Here we discuss constraints in
eq.

12
and how to compute the
k_{\kappa}^{i}
coefficients. The constraints we discuss are not new and we recall them for the sake of completeness of the description of the limiter and of the numerical results. We, however, emphasize that we leverage the iterative character of the limiter to avoid line-searches to evaluate the
k_{\kappa}^{i}
and rather use explicit formulas that may be less accurate, but also less expansive. For the sake of clarity, we here consider a generic constraint and look for
0\leq k\leq 1
such that

{\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+k{\bf A}=(1-k){\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+k({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+{\bf A})\in\mathcal{D}.

By
m
and
M
in
\mathbb{R}
, we denote some generic lower and upper bounds. We will consider the following constraints in the numerical experiments in
section

5
:

•

Bounds on some component of
{\bf u}
:
m<u<M
. Examples are the maximum principle for nonlinear scalar equations (see
example

1.1
) with
m=\essinf_{{\bf x}\in\Omega}u_{0}({\bf x})
and
M=\esssup_{{\bf x}\in\Omega}u_{0}({\bf x})
, or positivity of the density
u=\rho
with
M=\infty
and
0<m\ll 1
for the compressible Euler equations (see
example

1.2
, we typically use
m=10^{-8}
in the numerical experiments of
section

5
). In this case, we impose

k=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{m-U_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}}{A_{u}}&\text{if }U_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+A_{u}<m,\\
\frac{M-U_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}}{A_{u}}&\text{if }M<U_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+A_{u},\\
1&\text{else,}\end{array}\right.

with
A_{u}
the component in
{\bf A}
corresponding to
u
in
{\bf u}
.

•

Lower bound on concave functions of
{\bf u}
:
f({\bf u})>m
, where
\partial_{{\bf u}{\bf u}}^{2}f({\bf u})
is negative semi-definite. An example is the internal energy for the compressible Euler equations (see
example

1.2
). Using concavity of
f(\cdot)
, it is enough to impose
f({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+k{\bf A})\geq(1-k)f({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})+kf({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+{\bf A})=m
for what we obtain

k=\max\Big(\min\Big(\frac{f({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})-m}{f({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})-f({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+{\bf A})},1\Big),0\Big),

and proceed the same way to impose an upper bound to a convex function,
f({\bf u})<M
.

•

Bounds on specific entropies: given a convex entropy in
eq.

2
of the form
\eta({\bf u})
with
{\bf u}=(\rho,\rho{\bf y}^{\top})^{\top}
and
\rho>0
a positive state variable (e.g., the density for the compressible Euler or traffic flow equations, the water height in the shallow water equations), it is a classical matter that
\mathrm{s}({\bf w})=\mathrm{s}(\tau,{\bf y}^{\top})=-\tau\eta(\tfrac{1}{\tau},\tfrac{1}{\tau}{\bf y}^{\top})
is a concave function. Likewise, the mapping
{\bf u}={\bf u}({\bf w})
is one-to-one and for any given
0\leq\vartheta\leq 1
, there exists a unique
0\leq k\leq 1
such that
{\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+k{\bf A}={\bf u}\big((1-\vartheta){\bf w}({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})+\vartheta{\bf w}({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+{\bf A})\big)
. An example is the specific entropy
(\tfrac{1}{\rho},{\bf w}^{\top},E)\mapsto\mathrm{s}(\tfrac{1}{\rho},E-\tfrac{1}{2}{\bf w}\cdot{\bf w})
for the compressible Euler equations
[
14
, Sec. II]
(see
example

1.2
), thus giving
{\bf w}({\bf u})=(\tfrac{1}{\rho},{\bf v}^{\top},E)^{\top}
with
{\bf u}
defined in
eq.

4
. Assuming that the LO scheme satisfies a minimum entropy principle,
\mathrm{s}\circ{\bf w}({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})\geq m
, we compute
0\leq\vartheta\leq 1
from

\vartheta=\max\Big(\min\Big(\frac{\mathrm{s}\circ{\bf w}({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})-m}{\mathrm{s}\circ{\bf w}({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})-\mathrm{s}\circ{\bf w}({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+{\bf A})},1\Big),0\Big),

and then set
k=\tfrac{\vartheta\rho_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}}{\rho_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+(1-\vartheta)A_{\rho}}
with
A_{\rho}
the first component in
{\bf A}
corresponding to
\rho
in
{\bf u}
. Note that the same results hold for scalar equations with
\rho\equiv\tau\equiv 1
and
{\bf u}\equiv{\bf v}\equiv u
.

•

Discrete entropy inequalities: assume that the LO scheme satisfies some discrete entropy inequality for a given convex entropy
\eta({\bf u})
of the form

\eta({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})\leq\eta({\bf U}_{\kappa}^{i,n})-\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}}{M_{\kappa}^{i}}{\bf Q}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}({\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})=:m\quad\forall\kappa\in\Omega_{h},1\leq i\leq N_{p},

where
{\bf Q}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}
corresponds to some conservative discretization of
\nabla\cdot{\bf q}({\bf u})
in
eq.

2
. If the HO scheme fails to satisfy the same kind of entropy inequality, it is possible to use the RHS in the above equation as a bound by defining:

k=\max\Big(\min\Big(\frac{\eta({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})-m}{\eta({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})-\eta({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+{\bf A})},1\Big),0\Big),

which guarantees that
\eta({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}+k{\bf A})\leq m
and is always possible since
\eta({\bf U}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}})\leq m
.

•

Multiple bounds: it is sometimes beneficial to impose multiple bounds to the HO solution. This is done in a sequential way so as to ensure that all bounds can be effectively imposed. For instance, for the compressible Euler equations, one may first limit density, then limit the internal energy and finally impose the maximum principle on entropy. In this sequence, one needs a positive density for the internal energy to be concave, then one needs positive density and internal energy for the physical entropy to be well defined.

3
Time integration schemes

We here describe how the IDP limiter introduced in
section

2
can be adapted to HO explicit and implicit time integration schemes. We pay attention to lower the cost of the limiter by limiting the computation of the LO scheme to a time integration with a single step. The precise definition of the antidiffusive fluxes depends on the choice of the space discretization and will be given in
section

4
.

3.1
Runge-Kutta schemes

A general
{\cal S}
-stage RK method in Butcher form for the HO time integration of
eq.

5
reads

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n,k)}

\displaystyle={\bf u}_{h}^{n}-\Delta t^{(n)}{\bf M}^{-1}\sum_{l=1}^{k+\theta-1}a_{kl}{\bf R}_{h}({\bf u}_{h}^{(n,l)})\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq{\cal S},

(16a)

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{h}^{n+1}

\displaystyle={\bf u}_{h}^{n}-\Delta t^{(n)}{\bf M}^{-1}\sum_{l=1}^{{\cal S}}b_{l}{\bf R}_{h}({\bf u}_{h}^{(n,l)}),

(16b)

with
\sum_{l=1}^{{\cal S}}b_{l}=1
for time consistency. We here consider the cases of explicit RK methods for
\theta=0
and diagonally implicit Runge-Kutta (DIRK) methods for
\theta=1
. Note that by
eq.

16a
we have

in the explicit case
\theta=0
. We define

(17)

and assume that they are nonnegative without loss of generality, this property being satisfied by most of the RK methods.

Let us consider the limitation of the
k
th stage
eq.

16a
, the limitation of
eq.

16b
being similar. Given
{\bf u}_{h}^{n}
and
{\bf u}_{h}^{(n,l)}
in
\mathcal{D}
for

, we aim at limiting the solution
{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{(n,k)}
of the HO scheme

{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{(n,k)}={\bf u}_{h}^{n}-\Delta t^{(n)}{\bf M}^{-1}\sum_{l=1}^{k+\theta-1}a_{kl}{\bf R}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}({\bf u}_{h}^{(n,l)}).

(18)

with
{\bf u}_{h}^{(n,k)}={\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm HO$}}^{(n,k)}
when

.

\theta=0

\theta=1
, to define the quantity

{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+1}={\bf u}_{h}^{n}-c_{\infty}\Delta t^{(n)}{\bf M}^{-1}{\bf R}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}({\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+\theta})

(19)

with
c_{\infty}:=\max(c_{1\leq k\leq{\cal S}},1)
and
{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n}={\bf u}_{h}^{n}
when

. We assume that the LO scheme satisfies

. Note that the time step should be limited in the explicit case and must satisfy

where
\Delta t^{(n)}_{FE}
corresponds to the CFL condition for
eq.

19

c_{\infty}=1
and
\theta=0
. This may also be the case in the implicit case and depends on the choice of LO scheme and on the PDEs as discussed in

We then use the quantity
{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+1}
in

19
to evaluate the LO solution at the

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{(n,k)}

\displaystyle:={\bf u}_{h}^{n}-\Delta t^{(n)}{\bf M}^{-1}\sum_{l=1}^{k+\theta-1}a_{kl}{\bf R}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}({\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+\theta})

(20)

\displaystyle\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:def-ck-coeff-in-RKM}}{=}{\bf u}_{h}^{n}-c_{k}\Delta t^{(n)}{\bf M}^{-1}{\bf R}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}({\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+\theta})

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{c_{k}}{c_{\infty}}\Big){\bf u}_{h}^{n}+\frac{c_{k}}{c_{\infty}}\Big({\bf u}_{h}^{n}-c_{\infty}\Delta t^{(n)}{\bf M}^{-1}{\bf R}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}({\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+\theta})\Big)

\displaystyle\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:def-global-uLO-RKM}}{=}\Big(1-\frac{c_{k}}{c_{\infty}}\Big){\bf u}_{h}^{n}+\frac{c_{k}}{c_{\infty}}{\bf u}_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\rm LO$}}^{n+1},

6
Concluding remarks

We propose and analyze an IDP limiter for HO discretizations of hyperbolic systems of conservation laws. The limiter generalizes the FCT limiter to systems of conservation laws by limiting the HO solution around a given IDP LO solution. It limits antidiffusive fluxes as in the FCT framework, but defines the limiting coefficients so as to express the limited solution as a convex combination of IDP quantities similarly to convex limiting. The limiter preserves conservation and can be applied to every conservative discretization and to a wide range of explicit and implicit time integration schemes. It can be applied iteratively to improve the accuracy of the limited solution, while preserving the invariant domains and keeping conservation. A heuristic is finally proposed to accelerate the convergence of the iterative limiter.

As an illustration, the limiter is applied to four different schemes: FV schemes with either ERK, or DIRK time stepping; DGSEM with DIRK time stepping; and to a space-time DGSEM. Implementation details are provided and we propose formulations of the antidiffusive fluxes for DG schemes that do not satisfy partition of unity. Numerical experiments on 1D and 2D scalar hyperbolic equations and the compressible Euler equations illustrate the stability and robustness of the limited solution and its ability to preserve the accuracy of the HO solution in smooth regions. Future work will consider the extension of the present limiter to linearized backward-Euler time stepping for the approximation of steady-state solutions of hyperbolic systems of conservation laws.
