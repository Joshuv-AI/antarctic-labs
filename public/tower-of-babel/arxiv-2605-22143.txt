Holographic Dark Energy with Hubble Radius as an Infrared Cutoff in Einstein-Cartan Gravity
Yongjun Yun, and Jungjai Lee

Abstract
In this work, we investigate non-interacting holographic dark energy (HDE) with the Hubble radius as the infrared cutoff in Einstein-Cartan gravity. We derive the Einstein-Cartan equations from the action principle and obtain Friedmann-like equations by introducing a torsion scalar. Considering a Weyssenhoff spin fluid, we determine the scaling behavior of the torsion scalar as Φ ∼ a − 3 \Phi\sim a^{-3} without introducing an ad hoc ansatz, resolving the ansatz problem of previous torsion scalar scenarios. In the absence of interactions between dark matter and dark energy, the torsion scalar shifts the equation of state for holographic dark energy toward negative values from the dust-like value obtained in HDE without torsion, making cosmic acceleration possible. In particular, the resulting equation of state can approach ω X ≃ − 1 \omega_{X}\simeq-1 and cross the phantom divide within the weak torsion regime | Φ / H | < 1 |\Phi/H|<1 . The model predicts a dynamical equation of state in which cosmic acceleration gradually weakens, potentially consistent with recent DESI observations. In spacetimes with torsion, the cosmic distance duality relation between the luminosity distance d L d_{L} and the angular diameter distance d A d_{A} is modified as d L = d A ​ ( 1 + z ) 2 ​ ( 1 + η ) d_{L}=d_{A}(1+z)^{2}(1+\eta) . In the presence of the torsion scalar, we show that the standard relation between redshift and the scale factor is preserved, while the deviation parameter arising from torsion effects is determined as η ∼ ∫ t S t O d ​ t ​ a − 3 \eta\sim\int_{t_{S}}^{t_{O}}dta^{-3} , where t S t_{S} and t O t_{O} denote the emission time at the source and the observation time at the observer, respectively. Overall, our results support the feasibility of the model and provide a theoretical framework for preparing likelihood analyses.

2.2
Weak Equivalence Principle

In spacetimes with torsion, there exist three distinct types of curves, namely geodesics, autoparallels, and null curves.
While the geodesics are curves of extremal length, the autoparallels represent the straightest possible trajectories.
These two curves no longer coincide due to the presence of torsion.
This implies that the notion of free fall must be carefully defined.
The properties of the null curve are discussed in the last section.

We consider a curve
x^{\mu}=x^{\mu}(s)
parameterized by an affine parameter
s
and the corresponding tangent vector
u^{\mu}=dx^{\mu}/ds
.
The condition of parallel transport
u^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}u^{\mu}=0
is transformed into the autoparallel equations

\frac{d^{2}x^{\mu}}{ds^{2}}+\Gamma^{\mu}_{(\nu\rho)}\frac{dx^{\nu}}{ds}\frac{dx^{\rho}}{ds}=0.

(16)

Here, the symmetric part of the affine connection (
4
) is given by
\Gamma^{\mu}_{(\nu\rho)}=\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}-K_{(\nu\rho)}{}^{\mu}
.
When the torsion tensor is totally antisymmetric, the symmetric part of the contorsion tensor (
5
) vanishes,
K_{(\nu\rho)}{}^{\mu}=0
.
Hence, the autoparallel equations reduce to the geodesic equations

\frac{d^{2}x^{\mu}}{ds^{2}}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}\frac{dx^{\nu}}{ds}\frac{dx^{\rho}}{ds}=0.

(17)

Thus, the totally antisymmetric torsion tensor does not modify the geodesic equations.

We now turn to the weak equivalence principle in the context of torsion.
Following
43

P
such that

g_{\mu\nu}\big|_{P}=\eta_{\mu\nu},\quad\partial_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}\big|_{P}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma^{\rho}_{(\mu\nu)}\big|_{P}=0.

(18)

Under these conditions, the autoparallel equations (
16
) become
d^{2}x^{\mu}/ds^{2}|_{P}=0
.
This reflects that the gravitational effect is locally eliminated for the observer.
As discussed, for the totally antisymmetric torsion tensor, one finds

\Gamma^{\rho}_{(\mu\nu)}\big|_{P}=\tilde{\Gamma}^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}\big|_{P}=0.

(19)

This shows that the weak equivalence principle remains valid despite the presence of totally antisymmetric torsion.
In contrast, for a general torsion tensor, the symmetric part of the contorsion tensor does not vanish, leading to

\Gamma^{\rho}_{(\mu\nu)}\big|_{P}=K_{(\mu\nu)}{}^{\rho}\big|_{P}\neq 0.

(20)

This prevents the existence of a local inertial frame.
Consequently, the weak equivalence principle is no longer satisfied in the presence of general torsion.
In the Einstein-Cartan theory, spacetime is endowed with a more general geometric structure in which the spin of matter acts as the source of torsion.
This suggests that the weak equivalence principle could be violated in microscopic regimes where spin effects become dominant.

4.3
Cross-section

We examine the rate of change of a cross-sectional area in spacetimes with torsion.
To do this, the metric orthogonal to the null vector
k^{\alpha}
must be separated, which is not straightforward.
For instance, the naive choice
\hat{h}_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}+k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}
is not suitable because
\hat{h}_{\alpha\beta}k^{\alpha}\neq 0
.
For this reason, we introduce the null coordinates
u=t-x
and
v=t+x
in the local Lorentz frame.
Here,
u
and
v
represent the outgoing null direction and the ingoing null direction, respectively.
We assume that the null vector
k^{\alpha}
is tangent to curves
u=\text{constant}
, and another null vector
N^{\alpha}
is tangent to curves
v=\text{constant}
.
The null vector
N^{\alpha}
satisfies
N^{\alpha}N_{\alpha}=0
and
N^{\alpha}k_{\alpha}\neq 0
.
For
k_{\alpha}\sim\partial_{\alpha}u=(1,-1,0,0)/\sqrt{2}
and
N_{\alpha}\sim\partial_{\alpha}v=(1,1,0,0)/\sqrt{2}
,
the normalization condition
k^{\alpha}N_{\alpha}=-1
can be imposed.
A projection metric tensor orthogonal to these null vectors is given by

\hat{h}_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}+k_{\alpha}N_{\beta}+N_{\alpha}k_{\beta},

(148)

which satisfies
\hat{h}_{\alpha\beta}k^{\alpha}=0
,
\hat{h}_{\alpha\beta}N^{\alpha}=0
and
\hat{h}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}=2

68
.
Therefore,
\hat{h}_{\alpha\beta}
is identified as the metric tensor on the two-dimensional screen space.
Let
\hat{\Theta}
be an expansion scalar on the screen space.
It is defined by
\hat{\Theta}=g^{\alpha\beta}\hat{h}^{\mu}_{\alpha}\hat{h}^{\nu}_{\beta}\nabla_{\nu}k_{\mu}
.
Using the eikonal equation (
106
) and the null equations (
107
), one finds

\hat{\Theta}=\hat{h}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}k_{\mu}=\Theta+2S_{\mu\nu\rho}k^{\mu}N^{\nu}k^{\rho},

(149)

where
\Theta=\nabla_{\mu}k^{\mu}
is the expansion scalar in the four-dimensional spacetime.
In the torsion-free limit, these expansion scalars coincide,
\hat{\Theta}=\Theta
.
In the presence of torsion, however, these expansion scalars are generally distinct.
For the torsion scalar (
64
), the relation
\hat{\Theta}=\Theta
still holds.

To define a cross-section of the null bundle, a reference null curve
\gamma(\lambda)
is selected within the bundle.
At a point
P
on this null curve, the parameter takes the value
\lambda_{P}
.
We introduce auxiliary curves with tangent vector
N^{\alpha}
and assign a parameter
\mu
to each auxiliary curve.
Furthermore, the parameter
\mu

The auxiliary curve passing through

and its parameter at

\beta(\mu)

\mu_{\gamma}

A cross-section in a neighborhood of

is defined as the set of points
\{Q\}

Q
is given by the intersection of a null curve from the bundle and an auxiliary curve.
At each point
Q
, the parameters
\lambda
and
\mu
take the values
\lambda_{P}
and
\mu_{\gamma}
, respectively.
The two-dimensional cross-section
\delta S(\lambda_{P},\mu_{\gamma})
is spanned by the intersection of the two three-dimensional hypersurfaces
\lambda=\lambda_{P}
and
\mu=\mu_{\gamma}
.
The curves
\gamma
and
\beta
are chosen to be orthogonal to the cross section.
Since
\mu
remains constant along the null curve
\gamma
, the cross-section can be simply denoted by
\delta S(\lambda_{P})
, as shown in Fig.
4
.

As discussed in

, we assign coordinates
\theta^{A}
to each point on the cross-section, where

.
Since exactly one null curve passes through each point, the coordinates
\theta^{A}

Requiring the coordinates to remain constant along each null curve, points on another cross section
\delta S(\lambda)

\theta^{A}

Based on this setup, a local coordinate system
(\lambda,\mu,\theta^{A})

\gamma

x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}(\lambda,\mu,\theta^{A})

Since

(150)

Basis vectors tangent to the cross section are defined by

(151)

Since

(152)

Therefore, the basis vectors

In terms of the covariant derivative, this condition

(153)

(154)

A cross-sectional area is given by

\sigma=\det(\sigma_{AB})

\frac{1}{dA}\frac{D(dA)}{d\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{AB}\frac{D\sigma_{AB}}{d\lambda}.

(155)

Using Eqs. (
149
) and (

\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{AB}\frac{D\sigma_{AB}}{d\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{AB}k^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\sigma_{AB}=\nabla_{\alpha}k^{\alpha}+2S_{\rho\alpha}{}^{\alpha}k^{\rho}+4S_{\mu\nu\rho}k^{\mu}N^{\nu}k^{\rho}.

(156)

Substituting Eq. (
156

\nabla_{\alpha}k^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{dA}\frac{D(dA)}{d\lambda}-2S_{\rho\alpha}{}^{\alpha}k^{\rho}-4S_{\mu\nu\rho}k^{\mu}N^{\nu}k^{\rho}.

(157)

In the torsion-free limit, the divergence of the null vector coincides with the rate of change of the cross-sectional area, recovering the well-established relation in general relativity.
In the presence of torsion, however, this relation is modified, as shown above.

4.4
Cosmic Distance Duality Relation

We now turn to the relation between the luminosity distance
d_{L}
and the angular diameter distance

In general relativity, these cosmological distances satisfy the cosmic distance duality relation
d_{L}=d_{A}(1+z)^{2}

In the presence of torsion, however, this relation is modified through the generalized reciprocity theorem (

A previous study investigated how torsion modifies the cosmic distance duality relation using assumed Einstein-Cartan equations that are formally analogous to the Einstein equations, in which the energy-momentum tensor is identified as the physical source
\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}

43

5
Conclusions

We have demonstrated the feasibility of the non-interacting holographic dark energy with the Hubble radius as the IR cutoff within the framework of Einstein-Cartan gravity.
The torsion scalar plays an important role in the equation of state of holographic dark energy, enabling cosmic acceleration even in the weak torsion regime, while remaining consistent with the cosmological principle.
Furthermore, we found that the torsion scalar preserves the standard relation between redshift and the scale factor but modifies the relation between the luminosity distance and the angular diameter distance.
This suggests that the subtle effect of the torsion scalar could be encoded in direct observations such as SNIa and BAO.

In spacetimes with torsion, the weak equivalence principle is not always satisfied because geodesics, autoparallels, and null curves are generally distinct.
Totally antisymmetric torsion preserves the weak equivalence principle, whereas general torsion violates it.
Since the physical origin of torsion is the spin of matter, which is intrinsically quantum in nature, this violation may be inevitable.
Torsion does not propagate and is directly encoded in the spin of matter through the algebraic Cartan equations.
In this work, we have adopted the Einstein-like form of the Einstein-Cartan equations because the canonical energy-momentum tensor naturally incorporates the spin density of matter.

To utilize the results of the standard Friedmann equations, the Einstein-like form can be recast into the effective form, from which Friedmann-like equations follow under the cosmological principle.
The torsion scalar is linked to the spin density of matter through the Cartan equations.
Assuming the Weyssenhoff fluid of fermions without spin polarization, together with adiabatic expansion and particle number conservation, the behavior of the torsion scalar is determined as
\Phi\sim a^{-3}
.
Interestingly, the torsion scalar exhibits matter-like scaling behavior, which is attributed to the non-propagating nature of torsion.
This shows that the ansatz problem for the torsion scalar has been resolved.
For the non-interacting holographic dark energy with the Hubble radius as the IR cutoff, the torsion scalar shifts the equation of state
\omega_{X}
toward the negative values from the dust-like value obtained in the non-interacting holographic dark without torsion, making cosmic acceleration possible even in the weak torsion regime
|\Phi/H|<1
.
Specifically, the phantom divide
\omega_{X}=-1
is reached at
|\Phi/H|\simeq 0.667
for
d=0.837
and at
|\Phi/H|\simeq 0.381
for
d=0.95
.
Furthermore, we have found that
\dot{\omega}_{X}>0
, which indicates that cosmic acceleration is gradually weakening.
This suggests that our model may provide a feasible cosmological scenario consistent with recent DESI observations.

To prepare for the likelihood analysis, we have investigated light propagation in spacetimes with torsion under the geometric optics approximation.
Considering both a bundle of null curves diverging from the source and a bundle converging to the observer, we have shown that torsion affects light propagation, modifying the standard relation between redshift and the scale factor and the reciprocity theorem.
As a result, the relation between the luminosity distance
d_{L}
and the angular diameter distance
d_{A}
is modified as
d_{L}=d_{A}(1+z)^{2}(1+\eta)
, where
\eta
is the deviation parameter arising from torsion effects.
Based on the derived the scaling behavior of the torsion scalar,
\Phi\sim a^{-3}
, the scaling relation of the deviation parameter is determined as
\eta\sim\int_{t_{S}}^{t_{O}}dta^{-3}
, where
t_{S}
and
t_{O}
denote the emission time at the source and the observation time at the observer, respectively.
This shows that the deviation parameter is predicted to increase in the early universe but remains significantly small at late times.

An important feature of this work is that it avoids the causality problem and circular logic problem of holographic dark energy models with the future event horizon as the IR cutoff.
Moreover, a non-interacting limit exists.
Overall, our results support the feasibility of the model and provide a theoretical framework for preparing likelihood analyses.
