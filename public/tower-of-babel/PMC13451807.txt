Coupled carrier-message engineering for low-dose mRNA vaccination
Authors: Junchao Xu, Jung Gi Kim, Jeongah Song, Hyunjoo Cha-Molstad, Michael J Mitchell, full study team
Published: 2026

Main text

In our recent ACS Nano study,1 we asked a practical question for next-generation mRNA vaccines: can dose, delivery route, antigen expression, and immune quality be improved by engineering the carrier and the message as one system? mRNA vaccines have already shown how rapidly a nucleic acid medicine can be designed and deployed.2,3,4 However, the field still faces constraints that matter beyond COVID-19: protection can wane, reactogenicity often rises with dose, and only a small fraction of injected material reaches antigen-presenting cells. We therefore framed the work as a multistage, coupled design problem. As summarized in Figure 1, the lipid nanoparticle (LNP) surface, mRNA untranslated regions (UTRs), lymphatic transport, cellular release, and immune output were treated as linked determinants of functional delivery rather than separate optimization steps.

Coupled carrier-message engineering for low-dose mRNA vaccination

The display item links two engineered inputs: an H9T6/DOPG, low-PEG LNP surface selected through carrier optimization and a 5B-8 mRNA scaffold derived from large-scale 3′ and 5′ UTR screening. Their integration links successive stages of functional delivery—albumin-associated lymphatic routing, dendritic-cell uptake, endosomal release, localized antigen expression, and Th1-biased humoral and cellular immune output.

We first built the carrier layer. From a 96-member biodegradable ionizable lipid library, we identified the stereodefined lipid H9T6 as a lead candidate for dendritic-cell mRNA delivery. The lead lipid was necessary but not sufficient: its in vivo behavior emerged from formulation context. We therefore used design-of-experiments optimization to vary helper lipid identity, PEG (polyethylene glycol)-lipid content, cholesterol, and ionizable lipid ratio. The lead composition, H9T6:DOPG (1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phospho-(1'-rac-glycerol)):cholesterol:PEG at 40:16:43.5:0.5 mol %, improved dendritic-cell transfection, increased endosomal escape, and produced a more localized expression profile after intramuscular injection than benchmark formulations.

This result changed how we interpreted the LNP. Rather than viewing an ionizable lipid as the dominant functional unit, our data supported a formulation-level view in which the particle surface becomes the biological interface. The low-PEG, DOPG-containing formulation appeared better matched to albumin-associated lymphatic transport and dendritic-cell uptake. For a field that often ranks lipid structures as if they were portable parts, this was a useful caution: helper lipids, PEG density, and the resulting protein corona can decide whether a promising lipid becomes a useful vaccine delivery system.5,6 Figure 1 therefore depicts H9T6 within a formulated particle context, not as an isolated molecule.

In parallel, we engineered the mRNA message. We treated UTRs as tunable regulatory elements rather than fixed background sequences. In this framework, the message itself was an active design layer, not merely a payload. We ranked more than 630,000 randomized 3′ UTR variants by transcript decay in primary human nasal epithelial cells and more than 68,000 randomized 5′ UTR variants by ribosome loading through polysome profiling. After sequence filtering for features consistent with efficient scanning and start codon recognition, we combined the strongest 5′ and 3′ UTR candidates and identified the 5B-8 scaffold. 5B-8 increased peak and cumulative antigen expression compared with benchmark UTR architectures derived from authorized mRNA vaccines.

The key result emerged when the optimized carrier and message were combined. Encapsulating 5B-8 mRNA in H9T6 LNPs enhanced lymph-node delivery and shifted immune output. In mouse SARS-CoV-2 spike mRNA vaccination studies, 5B-8 primarily increased humoral responses, whereas H9T6 contributed strongly to cellular immunity. Together, H9T6/5B-8 generated Th1-biased responses, increased IFN-γ-secreting T cells, activated dendritic cells in draining lymph nodes, and increased memory B cell frequencies. The platform did not simply add two improvements; it aligned successive bottlenecks in the functional delivery cascade: lymphatic routing, dendritic-cell access, endosomal release, antigen expression, and immune programming.

Mechanistic experiments supported an albumin-associated transport model. H9T6/5B-8 accumulated more strongly in inguinal lymph nodes than ALC-0315 comparator formulations. In serum-free conditions, the advantage of the DOPG/low-PEG H9T6 LNP was reduced, whereas adding albumin restored it selectively. Receptor-blocking experiments further supported the involvement of albumin-handling pathways. Together, the data are consistent with a model in which the DOPG/low-PEG surface promotes albumin association, thereby facilitating lymphatic transport and dendritic-cell uptake, followed by productive endosomal escape. Accordingly, the display item treats lymph-node accumulation not as an endpoint by itself, but as one stage in a functional delivery cascade linking tissue distribution, target-cell uptake, intracellular release, antigen expression, and immune output.

The broader message for MTNA readers is that mRNA vaccine design should be multidimensional but mechanistically ordered. A potent delivery system will not reach its full value if mRNA stability and translation are limiting; a strong mRNA scaffold will not compensate for poor cellular delivery or endosomal escape. Repeat-dose rat studies up to 100 μg showed only transient, reversible findings without additional safety signals under the tested conditions, supporting further benchmarking. We now see three priorities: quantify the albumin-LNP interaction more directly, define the sequence-level mechanisms that make 5B-8 effective, and test whether coupled carrier-message engineering generalizes across antigens and disease models. Although demonstrated here in vaccination, this design logic may extend to extrahepatic RNA medicines, for which particle interface, biological transport, intracellular release, and RNA program must be engineered together. For us, the practical conclusion is that low-dose mRNA vaccination will likely come from aligning transport route, particle interface, intracellular release, and message design—not from maximizing any single component in isolation.
