3. Executive Summary

The SmartGrid Advanced Load Management & Optimized Neighborhood (SALMON) project
aimed to demonstrate and documentthe value of Distributed Energy Resources (DERs) as
a grid resource for both bulk system and distribution operations. Through retrofitting
approximately 580 buildings with smart thermostats, smart water heaters, solar with smart
inverters, storage, and managed EV charging, the project sought to develop a 1.4- megawatt
flexible load resource while achieving a minimum 10% averageefficiency improvement
acrossall treated buildings.

The project successfully developed a digital twin of the local grid at National Renewable
Energy Laboratory (NREL, now National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR)) with a real-time
model of the study area, operating within 5% accuracy compared to SCADA(Supervisory
Control and Data Acquisition) recordings. This model included explicit representations of
approximately 4,100 residential housing units and ran on NREL's high-performance
computer, Kestrel, enabling real-time simulation. The team also successfully deployed
Porland General Electric's (PGE) Advanced Distribution Management System (ADMS)and
Distributed Energy Resource Management System (DERMS)platform at NREL, which
helped derisk PGE's field deploymentthrough laboratory evaluation.

Customer engagementefforts resulted in the developmentand regulatory approval of
seven residential and three commercial programs designed to lower energy costs. These
programs offered customerssignificant incentives, including bill credits, cash back, and
free upgrades to make homes moreefficient, reliable, and smart. The project team also
conducted extensive marketing and outreach activities, including targeted emails, social
media ads, physical billboards, and neighborhood events.

Technical progress included the development of the community model,verification of data
exchange between the DERMS and the community model, and completion of functional
developmentfor battery aggregator code. The project team also documented DERMS
functions mapping to use casesin a gap analysis document and began preparation to test
battery aggregator with DERMS and the community model.

Despite the early termination of the award on October 2, 2025, the project successfully
met its BP1 and BP2 go/no-gocriteria and made significant progress toward understanding
how grid behavior evolves as projected DERs reshape load and operational parameters.
The project's findings are informing PGE's product developmentprocessand being
disseminated acrossthe Pacific Northwest through existing regional stakeholder networks.

5. Background

The electric utility industry is undergoing significant transformation as it adapts to
increasing penetration of distributed energy resources (DERs) and the need for greater grid
flexibility. Utilities across the United States are exploring how to effectively integrate and
managethese resources to support grid operations while providing value to customers. The
SALMON project builds on this growing body of work by focusing on the practical
implementation and testing of DER management systems in a real-world setting.

Recentliterature has highlighted the potential of DERs to provide grid services, including
voltage support, congestion relief, and peak load reduction. Studies’? by Lawrence
Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL) and Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL)
have demonstrated the technical feasibility of using residential and commercial DERsfor
grid services, but real-world implementation and scaling remain challenging.

The SALMON project leverages NREL's AdvancedDistribution Management System (ADMS)
Test Bed to evaluate DER controlstrategies before field deployment. This approach aligns

Unlike previous work that often focused on either technical integration or customer
engagementin isolation, the SALMON projecttakes a holistic approach that addresses

practical, scalable solutions for DER integration that consider both technical performance
and customer acceptance.

6. Project Objectives

The SALMON project aimed to demonstrate and documentthe value of Distributed Energy
Resources (DERs)as a grid resource for bulk system and distribution operations. The
specific goals included:

 1. Develop a 1.4-megawatt (MW)flexible load resource byretrofitting approximately
 580 buildings with smart thermostats, smart water heaters, solar with smart
 inverters, storage, and managed EV charging.

 2. Achieve a minimum 10% average efficiency improvementacrossall treated
 buildings.

 3. Reduce customer bills and enhance occupant comfort.

 4. Improve the thermal profile of buildings to support load shifting.

 5. Model and evaluate dispatch and control of the flexible load resource in NREL's
 ADMS Test Bed before field testing on PGE's enterprise ADMS/DERMS system.

 6. Explore co-optimization of bulk services (energy, capacity, frequency response) with
 distribution services (congestion relief, power quality support).

 7. Assess novel use cases and co-optimize solar and storage with flexible loads to
 minimize grid exports.

Additional learning objectives included:

 e Enhanceregional flex load program design anddelivery

 e Understand occupant preferences and perception of flexible load programs

 e Bolster regional partnership models for energy planning and coordination

 e Explore the combined value and co-benefits of flexibility and energy efficiency

 e Assess ADMS/DERMS capabilities and integration requirements

Subtask 4.3: DER Benefits Analysis
NREL and PGE collaborated to define performance metrics for evaluating the simulation
outputs. These metrics were used to quantify the benefits of dispatching different DERs for
the distribution voltage support use case, as well as customer-level outcomes.

The team was able to demonstrate that the different DERs are being controlled in a way
that supports voltage. The DERMS was set up with an objective to minimize the standard
variation in voltages over an hour. The plots below from a simulation with only the
residential battery energy storage system (BESS) aggregator show the total BESS power and
how batteries 1) discharge when voltages are low, which reducesthe total load, and 2)
charge when voltages are high, which increases total load. Therefore, the BESS act as
expected to reducevoltage variation.

The team usedthe difference in the standard deviation in voltages over the entire
simulated day as the metric and the table below summarizes the results of all the
scenarios that we simulated. A positive metric indicates that the standard deviation was
lower in the controlled case than in the baseline case.

 Metric     0.74          0.18           0.39           0.61             0.04           0.50

Results showed:

 e Small positive impact using battery energy storage systems (BESS), Electric Vehicles
 (EVs), and thermostats

 e Very small impact using heat pump water heaters (HPWH)

 e Limited impact on primary voltage due to small resource size

 e Potential for greater impact with higher DER levels

 e Possibility for more significant impact in other use cases (e.g., congestion relief)

Task 7: Engagement, Enrollment and Commissioning

The team finalized measure-level enrollment forecasts, defined customer-facing offers,
and received OPUC approval for enhancedincentives. Community Energy Project (CEP)

When residential customers responded to marketing activities by visiting the project
website, they were invited to schedule a free home energy score assessment. The team
delivered targeted communications highlighting special offers available to customers,
including:

 e Targeted emails and social media ads for Low or
 Moderate Income (LMI) customers

 e Free contractor-installed smart thermostats for
 residential and small-to-medium sized businesses

 e EV charging options for eligible customers

Over 100 unique DERs were enrolled in SALMON programs by
the end of the project.

8. Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions

The SALMON project achieved several significant accomplishments beforeits early
termination:

. Digital Twin Development: Successfully created a digital twin of the local grid with
 a real-time model of the study area, operating within 5% accuracy compared to
 SCADArecordings. This model included explicit representations of approximately
 4,100 residential housing units and ran on NREL's high-performance computer,
 enabling real-time simulation.

. DERMS Deploymentat NREL: Successfully deployed PGE's ADMS and DERMS
 platform at NREL, which helped derisk PGE's field deployment through laboratory
 evaluation.

. Regulatory Approval: Obtained OPUC approval for enhanced incentives specific to
 this project, enabling the developmentof seven residential and three commercial
 programs designed to lower energy costs.

. Customer Engagement: Developed and implemented a comprehensive marketing
 and outreach strategy that included targeted emails, social media ads, physical
 billboards, and neighborhood events. Over 100 unique DERs were enrolled in
 SALMON programs.

. DER Benefits Analysis: Demonstrated the use of DERsfor voltage support using
 advanced controls and quantified the impact. Results showed that while individual
 DER impacts were small, the approach could be effective at higher DER penetration
 levels.

. Workforce Development: Initiated a workforce development program through
 Energy Trust of Oregon to train new Heat Pump Water Heater Installers, contributing
 to the developmentof skilled labor in the clean energy sector.

 Knowledge Dissemination: Shared project findings through conference
 presentations, workshops, and publications
