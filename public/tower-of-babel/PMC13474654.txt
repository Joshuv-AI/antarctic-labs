Standardized ileal digestible linoleic acid requirement of modern lactating sows: A dose–response study
Authors: Yuansen Yang, Yifan Chen, Wenjun Gao, Yu Wei, Nuo Xiao, Xiaokang Zhang, Lu Wang, Changhua Lai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractContinued genetics and management improvement necessitates the verification of nutrient requirements for prolific and high-producing modern sow. Therefore, the objective of this study was to determine the standardized ileal digestible (SID) linoleic acid (LA) requirement of lactating sows using dose–response estimation methods. A total of 100 healthy Yorkshire × Landrace sows (parity two to four; initial body weight 245.0 ± 15.8 kg) were randomly assigned to five treatment groups with 20 replicates per group. The five diets contained 1.47%, 1.91%, 2.32%, 2.74%, or 3.15% SID LA. The experimental period lasted 28 d from d 107 of gestation until weaning. Reproductive performance of sows were not affected by dietary SID LA levels (P > 0.05); however, piglet weaning weight linearly increased with increasing SID LA levels (P = 0.044). A linear–broken relationship between litter weight and SID LA concentration was observed, litter weight on d 14 increased with SID LA until reaching a plateau at 2.36% of SID LA (P = 0.005), reaching a plateau at 2.15% of SID LA on d 21 (P = 0.083). The contents of immunoglobulin A in colostrum (P = 0.061) and immunoglobulin M in milk (P = 0.088) showed a trend towards a quadratic polynomial relationship with SID LA levels, immunoglobulin M content in milk peaked at 2.41% of SID LA (P = 0.038). In addition, the tumor necrosis factor-α content in plasma of piglets on d 7 of lactation showed a quadratic polynomial relationship with SID LA levels (P = 0.041). The content of immunoglobulin G on d 21 of lactation in sows linearly increased with higher SID LA levels (P < 0.001). The level of total antioxidant capacity of piglets on d 21 showed a quadratic polynomial relationship with SID LA levels (P = 0.001), which peaked at 2.23 g/kg of SID LA (P = 0.002). The glutathione peroxidase activity of piglets on d 7 increased linearly with the increase of SID LA, and reached a plateau at 2.07% of SID LA (P = 0.002). In conclusion, using broken line and quadratic regression model, the SID LA requirement for modern lactating sows ranged from 2.07% to 2.43% (133-156 g/d) to achieve optimal litter weight, milk immunoglobulin, and antioxidant capacity of sows and piglets.Keywords: Antioxidant capacity, Lactating sow, Linoleic acid, Milk composition, Reproductive performance, Requirement

Introduction

Linoleic acid (LA, C18:2n-6) and α-linolenic acid (ALA, C18:3n-3) are essential fatty acids (EFA), which cannot be synthesized in the body, and must be provided through the diet (Becker et al., 2023). Insufficient or excess dietary intake of EFA has been showed to be associated with various disorders and diseases, such as oxidative stress, tissue damage, and mitochondrial dysfunction (Burr and Burr, 1929; Mercola and D'Adamo, 2023; Reinwald et al., 2004; Saini and Keum, 2018). The potential benefits of EFA for sows and piglets include enhanced litter performance, improved milk quality, neural development, and a strengthened immune response (Innis, 2007). Shipman et al. (2025) reported supplementation of high levels of EFA in lactation diets benefits the subsequent reproduction of sows. However, most of these studies have been conducted to increase the EFA content of lactating sow diets by adding single fat or oil. It is difficult to distinguish the effects of single fatty acids (LA or ALA) on the reproductive performance of lactating sows.

The risk of a negative LA balance is the highest during lactation, because LA secretion in milk would far surpass the daily intake, thereby requiring tissue mobilization. Rosero et al. (2016a) estimated that the apparent balance of LA during lactation was −25.49 g/d when sows were fed diets without supplemental LA. Additionally, LA play a key role in follicular and embryonic development and serve as precursors to reproductive hormones involved in pregnancy maintenance through cell signaling pathways (Thatcher et al., 2010). Consequently, a negative LA balance is expected to disrupt sow's subsequent reproduction and litter performance of sows (Rosero et al., 2016a). The National Research Council (NRC, 2012) recommends a daily intake of 6.0 g of LA for sows, and this requirement estimate based on a study in 1985 (Christensen, 1985). Although a significant advancement in swine genetic breeding and feeding practices, the EFA requirement has remained unchanged for decades. Furthermore, Rosero et al. (2016b) observed that for sows fed diets without supplemental lipids, the amount of LA secreted in milk was 90 g/d, compared to this result, the recommended amount of 6 g/d is far from adequate. Rosero et al. (2016a) suggested that dietary EFA intake should exceed 125 g/d of LA to optimize reproductive performance of sows. These estimated values were derived from three studies, the data were obtained through multiple comparisons, and indicators only consider the number of weaned piglets (Rosero et al., 2012a, 2012b, 2015). Dose-response often used assess nutrient requirements; however, there are few studies that used a dose-response approach to validate the LA requirement of lactating sows.

To further enhance the accuracy with which lactation sow diets are formulated with respect to LA supply, the digestibility of LA, rather than the total amount of LA intake, better reflects the degree of LA absorption. Therefore, in the present study, standardized ileal digestible (SID) LA is used to assess the requirement. However, due to insufficient data on the SID of fat and fatty acid in feed ingredients, the concept of digestible fat and fatty acid is currently lacking in feed formulations. Derived from previous research in authors’ laboratory, the SID of fat and fatty acid can be assessed using the fat-free method (Wang et al., 2019). Therefore, this study aimed to: 1) explore the effects of SID LA on reproductive performance, colostrum and milk composition, immune and antioxidant capacity in lactating sows, diet maintain a constant level of dietary ALA; and 2) determine the SID LA requirement of lactating sows fed corn-soybean meal-based diets using dose–response approach. These findings provide fundamental data for the establishment of a standardized database on fatty acid requirements, and improve the precision of EFA formulation in sow diets.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

The experimental protocol was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of China Agricultural University (Beijing, China). Protocols were based on the National Research Council's Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (AW60305202-1-05).

Animals, diets and experimental design

The experiment was conducted in the FengNing Swine Research Unit of China Agricultural University (Academician Workstation in Chengde Jiuyun Agricultural and Livestock Co., Ltd., Chengde, Hebei, China). On d 107 of gestation, 100 healthy sows Yorkshire × Landrace (parity of two to four; initial body weight of 245.0 ± 15.8 kg) were randomly divided into five treatment groups with 20 replicated pigs per treatment based on body weight, parity, and back fat thickness. The SID LA levels of the dietary treatments were formulated as 1.47%, 1.91%, 2.32%, 2.74%, and 3.15%, respectively. This variation ranges of LA content basically covered the LA levels reported in the literature (Fig. S1). The LA of the diet consists of two parts, the corn-soybean meal-wheat bran and the supplemental fat as beef tallow or corn oil. Prior to the start of the sow experiment, the SID of ALA and LA in corn-soybean meal-bran basal diets for growing pigs were determined using the fat-free method (Wang et al., 2020). In addition, the SID for beef tallow and corn oil were derived from previous research (Wang et al., 2019). Each treatment was formulated to meet nutritional requirements of lactating sows according to the recommendations by the NRC (2012). Calculated nutrient levels were calculated according to NRC (2012) except for SID LA and ALA, SID LA and ALA which was calculated using analyzed ALA and LA concentrations in ingredient and the measured of SID of ALA and LA.

In addition, mixing corn oil with beef tallow can establish a gradient of dietary LA levels while maintaining consistent contents of ALA and net energy. This is because, compared with corn oil, beef tallow has a very low LA content (53.5% vs. 3.1%) and a similar net energy value (31.58 vs. 28.85 MJ/kg). The levels of SID ALA were consistent across all diets. The composition and nutrient levels of experimental diets is presented in Table 1. Sows were assigned to begin consumption of experimental diets on d 107 of gestation and fed until the end of lactation (d 21 of lactation).

The ingredients and nutrient compositions of late gestation (d 85-107 of gestation) diets are shown in Table S1. Sows were moved to farrowing stalls on d 107 of gestation and ingested 2.5 kg of daily diet (09:00 and 15:00) from d 107 of gestation to parturition. Sows were offered approximately 0.5 kg on the first day after farrowing and then fed daily feed allotment was increased by 1.0 kg every day until maximum feed intake was reached. When maximum feed intake was achieved, sows were allowed ad libitum access to feed. Throughout the experiment, sows and piglets were allowed ad libitum access to water. On the day of farrowing (d 0), litter size was adjusted to 14 ± 1 piglets by cross-fostering within dietary treatment. The ambient temperature in the farrowing room for sows was maintained at 20 to 23 °C. Heat lamps were used to provide heat to neonatal pigs. The suckling piglets underwent routine tooth clipping, tail removal, subcutaneous iron dextran injections, and immunization. During the experiment, sow's milk was the sole food source for suckling piglets.

Feed intake of the sows was recorded daily to allow calculation of average daily feed intake (ADFI) during the entire lactation period, and piglets were weighed at birth and d 21 of lactation. Back fat thickness (P2, 6 cm from the mid-line at the head of the last rib) of sows at d 107 of gestation, d 1 and 21 of lactation was measured using an ultrasonic device (Piglog 105, version 3.1, SFK Technology A/S, Herlev, Denmark) to assess the body condition of sows. Within 48 h of parturition, pig litters were cross-fostered among sows receiving the same treatment to standardize litter size. Counts of pigs born alive, stillborn, and mummified were recorded for each sow. Each piglet was individually weighed and litter weights were calculated on d 7, 14, and 21 of lactation. All instances and reasons for piglet mortalities were recorded. After weaning, sows were moved to individual gestation stalls and checked daily for signs of estrus to determine weaning to the first service interval.

Collection of colostrum and milk from sows

Six sows from each group were randomly selected by injection of 10 IU oxytocin via the ear vein to induce milk letdown into the teat canal on d 0 (colostrum, within 2 h after starting delivery of the sows) and d 21 (milk) of lactation. Milk samples were collected from different mammary glands (front, middle, and rear), and equal volumes (15 mL) of milk from three glands of a sow were mixed, and the samples were stored at −20 °C for subsequent analysis.

Collection of blood samples from sows and piglets

Blood samples of 5 mL (six samples per treatment) were obtained from the sows' auricular vein, and blood samples of 5 mL (six samples per treatment) were obtained from the piglets’ anterior vein cava on d 7 and 21 of lactation. Plasma samples were harvested for each blood sample after centrifugation at 3000 × g and 4 °C for 15 min, and stored at −80 °C for later analysis.

Chemical composition of diets

Diet nutrient composition was determined according to AOAC (2000) methods. Crude protein was analyzed by the Kjeldahl method (method 954.01) using an automatic Kjeldahl apparatus (Kjeltec 8400, FOSS Analytical AB, Höganäs, Skåne County, Sweden). Calcium was determined by the ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) titrimetric method (method 984.04) using a digital burette (Titrette, BRAND GmbH + Co. KG, Wertheim, Baden-Württemberg, Germany). Phosphorus was determined by the photometric method (method 965.17) using a UV-VIS spectrophotometer (UV-1800, Shimadzu Corporation, Kyoto, Japan). Acid-hydrolyzed ether extract was determined according to method 954.02AOAC (2007) using automated fat extractor (Soxtec, 2050, FOSS North America, Inc., Eden Prairie, MN, USA). The LA and ALA of the diets were determined using a gas chromatograph (Agilent 6890 Series, Agilent Technologies, Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA) following a modification of the procedures of Sukhija and Palmquist (1988). The values of net energy, crude protein, calcium, total phosphorus, SID Lys, Met, Thr, Trp, and Val were calculated using data provided by the Nutrient Requirements of Swine in China (Li et al., 2020). The SID values of LA and ALA were calculated using analyzed LA and ALA concentrations in feed ingredients and measured SID values for LA and ALA.

Analysis of immunoglobulin and components in colostrum and milk

The concentrations of immunoglobulin A (IgA), immunoglobulin G (IgG), and immunoglobulin M (IgM) in colostrum and milk were determined according to Che et al. (2012). Briefly, the optical density values of IgA, IgG and IgM standards were measured at 340, 700, and 340 nm using a UV-VIS recording spectrophotometer (UV–2401PC, Shimadzu Corporation, Kyoto, Japan), and the content of each immunoglobulin was calculated by standard curve. The content of milk fat, milk protein and lactose in colostrum and regular milk samples was measured by infrared spectrometer (MilkoScan FT2, FOSS Analytical A/S, Hillerød, Denmark).

Analysis of immunoglobulin in serum

The levels of IgG, superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), malondialdehyde (MDA), tumor nuclear factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β) of plasma of sows and piglets were analyzed by spectrophotometric methods using a spectrophotometer (SFZ1606017568, Shanghai Lengguang Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) following the instructions of the kit's manufacturer (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). Product codes of the test kits are H106-1-1, A001-1-2, A005-1-2, A015-1-2, A003-1-2, H052-1-2, H007-1-2, and H002-1-2, respectively.

Results

Standardized ileal digestibility of LA in ingredient

Based on previous laboratory studies, the LA content in corn-soybean meal-wheat bran, beef tallow, and corn oil were 1.78, 2.42, and 56.45 g/kg, respectively. The apparent ileal digestibility of LA in these ingredients for growing pigs was 80.35%, 89.94%, and 98.12%, respectively. The estimated basal endogenous loss of LA was 0.41 g/kg of dry matter intake. Consequently, the SID of LA was 82.85%, 94.19%, and 99.49%, respectively, according to the fat-free diet method.

Performance of lactating sows and piglets

As shown in Table 2, there were no differences in lactational sow ADFI, lactational backfat loss, weaning estrus interval, total piglets born, or piglets born alive among dietary treatments (P > 0.05). However, there was a trend toward a linearly increased total piglets born in the subsequent breeding cycle when sows were fed 2.74% SID LA (P = 0.079). There were no differences in litter weights at birth or weaning, nor in piglet ADG (P > 0.05; Table 3). However, piglet weight on d 21 of lactation linearly increased with the increasing SID LA levels (P = 0.044). Quadratic model showed that when SID LA level was 2.43%, the litter weight of piglets at d 7 reached the maximum (P = 0.083; Fig. 1B). Moreover, a linear–broken relationship between litter weight and SID LA concentration was observed (Fig. 1). Litter weight on d 14 of lactation was maximized when sows were fed 2.36% SID LA (P = 0.005), while at d 21 of lactation, the optimal SID LA level was 2.15% (P = 0.083).

Inflammatory cytokines in plasma of sows and piglets

The SID LA level exhibited a trend toward increased TNF-α contents on d 21 of lactation in sows (P = 0.088; Table 5). The content of IL-1β (P = 0.044) and IgG (P < 0.001) on d 21 of lactation in sows linearly increased with the increasing SID LA levels. The content of IL-6 on d 7 of lactation in piglets linearly increased with the increasing SID LA levels (P = 0.008). The plasma TNF-α content on d 7 of lactation in piglets (P = 0.041) and IL-6 content on d 21 of lactation in sows (P = 0.094) followed a quadratic polynomial relationship.

Discussion

An experimental design of dose-response studies is determined by the choice of dose levels, this is the key to determining optimal requirements (Holland-Letz and Kopp-Schneider, 2015). Therefore, before the start of this study, 114 articles retrieved from Web of Science and PubMed were used to evaluate the EFA content of conventional diets for lactating sows (Table S2 and Fig. S1). Where the range of variation in LA level is 1.4%–4.0%, which provided a theoretical basis for the formulation of the LA gradient in this study.

Lactation is a crucial phase in a sow's reproductive cycle (Li et al., 2023). The development of modern prolific and high-producing sows has resulted in animals with reduced body fat reserves, and a lower appetite (Hermesch et al., 2008). Dietary lipids are extensively used in sow diets as sources of efficient energy supply and EFA. Consistent with previous research (Holen et al., 2022; Holen et al., 2022; Rosero et al., 2016a), dietary LA levels had no significant effects on ADFI, back fat loss, and the weaning-to-estrus interval. A plausible explanation for this finding is the consistent energy content of the diets, which supports the idea that pigs regulate feed consumption to meet their energy requirements (Yang et al., 2024). These diets met the nutrient requirements for sows, supporting milk production and thereby reducing the mobilization of body fat. Additionally, although not statistically different, sows fed 2.32% SID LA had numerically lower backfat loss during lactation. Increasing the intake of EFA is a key strategy to enhance reproductive performance and extend the productive lifespan of sows (Rosero et al., 2016a). Since LA levels were increased for only a single lactation period in the present study, the relatively short duration of LA supplementation was likely insufficient to affect litter size. Weeden et al. (1994) and observed similar results in sows fed diets supplemented with soybean oil. In addition, a dietary SID LA level of 2.32% tended to increase the total number of piglets born in the subsequent reproductive cycle. Holen et al. (2022), Rosero et al. (2016b), and Shipman et al., (2025) reported that dietary supplemental EFA in the lactation period could influence subsequent reproductive performance. Reproductive performance of sows can be directly influenced by EFA incorporation into oocyte cell membranes, ovarian follicle and embryonic development, cell signaling for pregnancy recognition and maintenance, and modulation of prostaglandin expression patterns (Thatcher et al., 2010; Weems et al., 2006; Wathes et al., 2007). These mechanisms may explain the improved subsequent reproductive performance.

Growth performance is a key economic indicator for assessing the production capacity and breeding value of livestock. Weaning weight, in particular, directly influences the health and fertility of piglets. In this study, SID LA levels had no significant effect on piglet ADG or body weight at any stage during lactation. These findings agree with previous studies (Chang, 2016; Holen et al., 2022, 2023). An LA balance table was established based on estimated LA intake, milk LA output, and body fat mobilization and results are provided in the supplementary material (Table S3). The results showed that the lowest LA group (1.47%), total LA supply (diet + body fat), almost matched LA output in milk, resulting in only a small negative LA balance (−12.05 g/d). In contrast, at ≥ 1.91% SID LA, the LA balance became clearly positive and increased with higher dietary LA. Meanwhile, sows in the 1.47% SID LA group were able to cover this small LA deficit through limited mobilization of maternal fat reserves without compromising body condition. This could be a reasonable explanation for the lack of difference in growth performance among piglets. However, the body weight of weaned piglets increased linearly with higher levels of SID LA, furthermore, litter weight on d 14 and 21 showed a broken-line response to SID LA levels, indicating that SID LA may be limiting for litter weight before the breakpoint, whereas additional SID LA beyond this point may provide limited further benefit. The EFA are primarily secreted in the milk of lactating sows to support litter growth and development (Innis, 2007; Odle et al., 2014). Rosero et al. (2015) observed that the amount of LA secreted in milk was 90 g/d when sows fed diets without supplemented lipids, and this value could be even greater for modern prolific and high-producing sows. These findings suggest that, before the estimated breakpoint is reached, SID LA supply may be insufficient to meet the requirement of lactating sows, potentially resulting in a negative LA balance, increased mobilization of body fat, and impaired lactational performance. Based on the estimated breakpoint derived from the d 21 broken-line model for litter weight, the minimum LA requirement of modern lactating sows was estimated to be 138 g/d. As LA levels increase, improvements may occur in the nutrient composition of colostrum and milk. Tummaruk et al. (2014) observed that adding microencapsulated fat increased the fat content in the sow's milk, improved the piglet's daily weight gain, and reduced piglet mortality. In this study, sows fed an SID LA level of 2.32% exhibited a trend toward increased milk fat content. Sows are under catabolic and oxidative stress status during lactation and these can potentially impair litter performance (Kim et al., 2013). The results of the present study indicate that LA supplementation may enhance immune function and antioxidant capacity in both sows and piglets. Collectively, these responses may partly explain the observed variation in litter weigh. The statistical model used to describe the response is of great importance. Single-slope line models and quadratic curve models are the most commonly used approaches for studying nutrient requirements (Kielland et al., 2015). Among the candidate models fitted to litter weight at each sampling time point, the quadratic model yielded the best fit on d 7, indicating that litter weight peaked at an SID LA level of 2.43%. The broken-line model performed better for d 14 and d 21. The plateau occurred at 2.36% SID LA on d 14, whereas a plateau was observed at 2.15% SID LA on d 21. Therefore, these findings indicate that the optimal dietary SID LA level for supporting sow lactation performance and litter performance may range from 2.15% to 2.43%. Considering that the optimal values at different stages are different, to optimize litter performance, dietary SID LA should be adjusted based on the stage of lactation rather than applying a single fixed value across the entire period, this should be as a potential direction for future studies.

Some researchers have observed the greater colostrum and milk fat output when lactating sows consumed diets with increased energy density provided by supplemental lipids (Holen et al., 2023; Rosero et al., 2016a). The absence of significant effects of LA levels on colostrum and milk fat content may be attributed to the similar dietary energy levels maintained across treatment groups. These results were consistent with the findings of Holen et al. (2022). Immunoglobulins in milk are critical for maintaining pig health and production (Kielland et al., 2015). Passive immune protection in suckling pigs begins with elevated colostrum immunoglobulin concentrations during the initial hours of life. In the present study, no significant differences were observed among dietary treatments in immunoglobulin levels in colostrum and milk. Similarly, Jin et al. (2017) and Bai et al. (2017) reported that dietary oils had no effect on IgG and IgM levels in colostrum and milk. However, the content of IgA in colostrum and the content of IgM in milk exhibited a trend following a quadratic polynomial relationship with SID LA levels. Due to the poor model fit for colostrum IgA, the quadratic model was selected to describe the response of milk IgM to SID LA levels. Milk IgM levels reached their peak at an SID LA level of 2.41%. To date, limited information is available regarding the relationship between LA supplementation and milk IgM content in lactating sows. The mechanism underlying the LA-associated increase in milk IgM content remains unclear and warrants further investigation.

In this experiment, inflammatory status and antioxidant capacity were assessed to evaluate the effects of dietary LA on sow and piglet health. Previous studies have indicated that high-grade LA increases the release of pro-inflammatory factors (Du et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2020). In this study, the content of IL-6 at d 21 of lactation in sows and the content of TNF-α at d 7 of lactation in piglets exhibited a trend following a quadratic polynomial relationship with SID LA levels. This is because as the LA content gradually increases, it enhances the synthesis of arachidonic acid and its inflammatory mediators, leading to increased pro-inflammatory factor levels (Dyall et al., 2022). However, at higher LA concentrations, metabolic regulation and feedback inhibition, along with adjustments in fatty acid utilization and storage by tissues, may down-regulate pro-inflammatory factor synthesis or secretion (Calder, 2008; Funk, 2001). This may be the reason for the quadratic polynomial effect. In this experiment, only the dietary LA concentration was altered, whereas the ALA concentration remained constant, which represents a limitation of this study. The ALA can increase the release of anti-inflammatory factors, thus n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) and n-6 PUFA play significant roles in regulating the balance between inflammation and anti-inflammation in the body (Dyall et al., 2022; Gabbs et al., 2015). Therefore, SID ALA requirements should be considered in production practices to achieve an optimal balance between LA and ALA, thereby helping to regulate inflammatory and immune status. Future studies should further clarify the interactive effects of LA and ALA in lactating sows.

During the perinatal period and lactation, rapid fetal growth and high lactation demands elevate the sow's metabolic activity, resulting in an overproduction of free radicals and inducing oxidative stress. This oxidative burden can lead to reduced litter size, impaired lactation, and lower piglet survival rates (Berchieri-Ronchi et al., 2011; Kim et al., 2013; Zhao et al., 2013). The PUFA is highly unstable, susceptible to attack by free radicals, and prone to lipid peroxidation (Catalá, 2009). Therefore, the content of EFA in the diet of lactating sows is not as high as possible. The levels of SOD, GSH-Px, and T-AOC serve as key biomarkers reflecting an organism's oxidative defense capacity, while MDA levels indicate the extent of lipid peroxidation (Kuang et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2009). The appropriate amount of conjugated LA can enhance the activity of antioxidant enzymes and reduce oxidative damage (Jiang et al., 2014; Putera et al., 2023). In the present study, SID LA levels were associated with a linear trend of decreasing MDA content in sow plasma on lactation d 7, suggesting that moderate LA supplementation can mitigate lipid peroxidation. Additionally, both linear and quadratic effects were detected for SOD activity in sow plasma on lactation d 21, and a quadratic trend in T-AOC was observed in piglet plasma at the same time point. The peak value at 2.23% SID LA suggests that it is an optimal LA concentration for maintaining overall antioxidant balance, thereby supporting a favorable redox state during late lactation. Moreover, a breakpoint value of 2.07% SID LA was identified for GSH-Px activity in piglet plasma on lactation d 7, indicating that this level optimally supports GSH-Px production. Excessive LA intake may modulate the arachidonic acid metabolic pathway, reducing its peroxidation rate and consequently lowering antioxidant demand, leading GSH-Px activity to stabilize (Brigelius-Flohé and Maiorino, 2013; Calder, 2008; Funk, 2001). Therefore, these results indicate that moderate LA supplementation (2.07% to 2.23% SID LA) may contribute to improved antioxidant capacity in both sows and piglets and help maintain redox homeostasis during lactation.

Based on the findings of this study, dietary LA supplementation was shown to enhance piglet growth performance, improve milk quality, and promote both immune and antioxidant capacities in lactating sows and their offspring. The NRC (2012) recommends an LA requirement of 0.1% for all stages of pig, based on a study conducted in 1985 (Christensen, 1985). This recommendation has remained unchanged for decades despite significant advancements in swine genetic selection and feeding conditions. Rosero et al. (2015) concluded that sows experiencing a negative EFA balance could enter a deficiency state that impairs subsequent reproductive function. Later, through modeling approaches, Rosero et al. (2016a) suggested that dietary EFA intake should exceed 125 g/d of LA to maximize reproductive performance. These key studies identified regarding LA requirements in sows. These results suggest that SID LA supplementation within the range of 2.07% to 2.43% (133–156 g/d) may contribute to optimized litter weight, increased milk immunoglobulin levels, and improved immune function and antioxidant capacity in both sows and piglets. Moreover, estimating the requirement based on SID LA provides a more physiologically relevant assessment of the actual LA requirement of lactating sows. Future research should be considered that the requirement for ALA and its potential adjustments based on different parity and lactation stages. To date, limited studies have used a dose-response approach to assess the LA requirement of modern lactating sows. Therefore, the present study may provide a valuable reference for future research on EFA requirements.

It should be noted that, dietary LA levels were adjusted by exchanging corn oil with tallow at a constant fat inclusion level, which inevitably altered the overall fatty acid profile. However, LA approximately doubled from 17.31 to 34.24 mg/g diet, whereas changes in saturated, monounsaturated and ALA were comparatively modest, indicating LA was the main driver of treatment differences (Table S4). Therefore, the responses observed here should be interpreted as effects of modifying the essential fatty acid supply within the whole dietary fatty acid profile, with LA as the primary factor, and future studies using low-LA basal diets or purified fat blends are warranted to isolate specific fatty acid effects more clearly. A limitation of this study is that the corn-soybean meal-wheat bran basal diet, although representative of commercial practice, contained inherent LA, thereby preventing the formulation of a completely LA-deficient control diet. Future studies should consider the use of low-LA basal diets (e.g., tapioca- or sorghum-based diets combined with saturated fat sources) to better establish LA-deficient conditions and to more clearly isolate the role of LA.

Conclusion

In conclusion, supplemental SID LA had no influence on the reproductive performance of lactating sows. However, it improved litter performance and milk fat concentration, and enhanced immune function and antioxidant capacity in both sows and piglets. Based on broken-line and quadratic regression models, the estimated SID LA requirement for modern lactating sows ranged from 2.07% to 2.43% to achieve optimal litter weight, milk immunoglobulin content, and antioxidant status in sows and piglets. Using the average feed intake observed in this trial (6.426 kg/d), the SID LA requirement for modern lactating sows ranged from 133 to 156 g/d when expressed on a daily basis.
