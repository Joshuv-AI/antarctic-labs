Period-Aware Asymptotic Gain with Application to a Periodically Forced Synchronization Circuit
Anton Ponomarev, Lutz Gröll, Veit Hagenmeyer

Abstract
The classical asymptotic gain (AG) is a concept known from the input-to-state stability theory. Given a uniform input bound, AG estimates the asymptotic bound of the output. Sometimes, however, more information is known about the input than just a bound. In this paper we consider the case of a periodic input. Under the assumption that the system converges to a periodic solution, we introduce a new gain, called period-aware asymptotic gain (PAG), which employs periodicity to enable a sharper asymptotic estimation of the output. Since the PAG can distinguish between short-period (“high-frequency”) and long-period (“low-frequency”) signals, it is able to rigorously quantify such properties as bandwidth, resonant behavior, and high-frequency damping. We discuss how the PAG can be computed and illustrate it with a numerical example from the field of power electronics.



I
Introduction

Asymptotic gain
(AG) is a convenient characteristic of nonlinear control systems. It relates the uniform input bound and the asymptotic bound of the output. AG is a part of the
input-to-state stability
(ISS) theory
[
15
]
.

One application of the AG is in the
small-gain
stability analysis of dynamical networks
[
4
]
. Based on the way each subsystem damps or amplifies the input, conclusions can be made about whether the signals eventually vanish while looping through the network. On a very high level, the small-gain idea can be compared to the
passivity
and
dissipativity
conditions which operate with flows of energy rather than signal bounds
[
16
]
.

If the input is affected by a bounded persistent perturbation, both AG and energy-based approaches can bound the output. An important distinction is that AG deals with uniform (supremum) bounds whereas energy is a more delicate measure of an “effective” magnitude. In practice, input perturbation often
oscillates
inside the supremum bounds which makes the AG approach very conservative. In the present contribution we tweak the AG concept to try and capture some aspects of averaging and oscillatory behavior, at the same time preserving the magnitude bounds that are characteristic of ISS and not of the energy-based tools.

Consider a linear system and its Bode diagram, particularly its magnitude part. At face value, it shows the gain of pure sine signals as they go through the system. To carry this notion over to nonlinear systems, interaction between different frequencies has to be taken into account. This gives rise to such methods as
harmonic balance
,
Volterra series
,
nonlinear frequency response

[
12
]
, etc. One may also read from the Bode diagram that the system blocks inputs in a certain frequency band, or that there is resonance at a certain frequency. Our variation of AG indicates similar properties of a nonlinear system forced by a
periodic input
. Note the difference: whereas methods like harmonic balance assume
harmonic
inputs, we consider
arbitrary
periodic signals.

The proposed AG is called a
period-aware asymptotic gain
(PAG) and applies to the case of periodic inputs. Such inputs or disturbances do appear in some applications – e.g., in Section
VI
we consider an example from power electronics where disturbance is often dominated by the harmonics of the main grid frequency. The idea is to treat the input as the sum of a constant signal (
DC component
) and a periodic signal with zero average (
AC component
). Knowing the magnitude bounds of each component, we can estimate the asymptotic bounds of the DC and AC components of the output – this essentially defines the PAG.

PAG must be compared to the recently introduced ISS variations that bound the output magnitude by the moving average of the input
[
5
,
9
]
. PAG is different in two ways:

•

PAG depends on the
magnitudes
of the AC/DC components of the input, not on the moving average which would be an energy-like approach;

•

PAG is a
vectorial
function – it maps two arguments (the bounds on the AC/DC components of the input) to two bounds on the AC/DC components of the output.

However, PAG does not replace ISS: it is invoked
after
convergence to a periodic solution is established – for instance, by means of ISS, see Section
III-B
.

If, looking at the PAG plot, one sees that the gain of short-period inputs is far below 1, then it can be interpreted similarly to the high-frequency attenuation property of linear systems. It is readily assumed and widely used in the engineering practice that such frequency-dependent properties transfer from linearization to nonlinear systems under the small-signal approximation
[
17
]
. The PAG analysis may help quantify this linearization-based point of view.

In Section
III
we define the PAG and outline the framework for its use. The formula for PAG in the linear case is given in Section
IV
. For nonlinear systems, we derive a linearization-based approximation in Section
V
. The estimations are tried out in Section
VI
in a nonlinear example. The results show that the PAG bounds are tighter than the classical period-agnostic AG.

III
Period-Aware Asymptotic Gain

III-A

Definition

Consider the system

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=Ax+Bu+Ff(x,u),

(8a)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=Cx+g(x)

(8b)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, matrix
A
is Hurwitz, and functions
f
and
g
are globally smooth.

It is often necessary to limit the admissible initial states and inputs. To this end, we introduce:

•

\mathcal{X}_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
– an open set of initial states with
0\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
;

•

\mathcal{U}=\{u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})\colon\|u\|_{\infty}<u_{\mathrm{max}}\}
– a set of inputs bounded by some
u_{\mathrm{max}}\in(0,\infty]
.

Recall the following classical definition.

Definition 1

A non-decreasing
\gamma\colon[0,u_{\mathrm{max}})\to[0,\infty)
is called a (conservative)
asymptotic gain
(AG) of (
8
) if for every
u\in\mathcal{U}
and
x(0)\in\mathcal{X}_{0}

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|y(t)\|\leq\gamma(\|u\|_{\infty}).

(9)

The minorant of conservative AGs is called the
exact
AG.

Given an input magnitude bound, AG provides an asymptotic bound of the output magnitude. If, in addition to the magnitude bound,
periodicity
of the input is assumed, then it is natural to expect that the output bound can be tightened. This tighter bound is provided by the
period-aware
AG defined as follows.

In the notation of Section
II
, let

\mathcal{U}_{T}=\{u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})\colon u\text{\ is\ $T$-periodic,}\\
\|\rho_{T}(u)\|_{1}<u_{\mathrm{max}}\}.

(10)

Note that
\mathcal{U}_{T}\subset\mathcal{U}
because for a
T
-periodic
u

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\|u_{\mathrm{dc}}\|+\|u_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}\equiv\|\rho_{T}(u)\|_{1}.

(11)

Definition 2

With the notation of Section
II
, a
\preceq
-non-decreasing function
\gamma_{T}\colon[0,u_{\mathrm{max}})^{2}\to[0,\infty)^{2}
is called a (conservative)
period-aware asymptotic gain
(PAG) of (
8
) if for every
u\in\mathcal{U}_{T}
and
x(0)\in\mathcal{X}_{0}

\rho_{T}(y)\preceq\gamma_{T}\big(\rho_{T}(u)\big).

(12)

The
\preceq
-minorant of conservative PAGs is the
exact
PAG.

The following claim is obvious since
\mathcal{U}_{T}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{kT}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 1

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
T>0
, PAG
\gamma_{T}
satisfies
\gamma_{T}\big(\rho_{T}(\cdot)\big)\preceq\gamma_{kT}\big(\rho_{kT}(\cdot)\big)
.

Once
\gamma_{T}
is found for
T\in[T_{0},2T_{0}]
with some
T_{0}>0
, Proposition
1
can be used to lower-bound
\gamma_{T}
for
T>2T_{0}
.

III-B

Typical Scenario

We view the PAG concept as an
add-on
to the classical
input-to-state stability
(ISS) analysis rather than a proper refinement thereof. We have in mind a practical scenario of its use that shall be illustrated by an example in Section
VI
. The scenario starts with validating the following two assumptions by any of the existing methods.

Assumption 1

Every solution
x(t)
of (
8
) with
u\in\mathcal{U}
and
x(0)\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
after some time enters a compact forward-invariant set
\mathcal{S}(\|u\|_{\infty})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Assumption
1
can be established, e.g., by showing that system (
8
) is
locally input-to-state stable
(LISS)
[
14
]
. It implies the conservative AG

\gamma(\|u\|_{\infty})=\max_{x\in\mathcal{S}(\|u\|_{\infty})}\|Cx+g(x)\|.

(13)

Assumption 2

System (
8
) with every
u\in\mathcal{U}
is
uniformly exponentially contractive

[
11
]
in
\mathcal{S}(\|u\|_{\infty})
, i.e., for every pair of its solutions
\bar{x},\tilde{x}
residing in
\mathcal{S}(\|u\|_{\infty})

\|\bar{x}(t)-\tilde{x}(t)\|\leq c\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma t}\|\bar{x}(0)-\tilde{x}(0)\|

(14)

for all
t\geq 0
and some constants
c\geq 1
and
\sigma>0
independent of
u
,
\bar{x}(0)
, and
\tilde{x}(0)
.

With Assumptions
1
and
2
established, we proceed with the PAG analysis by assuming that
u\in\mathcal{U}_{T}\subset\mathcal{U}
. Then the stroboscopic map
x(0)\mapsto x(T)
is a contraction that maps
\mathcal{S}(\|\rho_{T}(u)\|_{1})
into itself, and by Banach’s theorem the map has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{S}(\|\rho_{T}(u)\|_{1})
. Thus, all solutions of (
8
) with
u\in\mathcal{U}_{T}
and
x(0)\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
converge to a unique periodic solution
\hat{x}(t;u)
contained in
\mathcal{S}(\|\rho_{T}(u)\|_{1})
. Suppose that the AC/DC components of the output
\hat{y}
produced on
\hat{x}
can be estimated as

\rho_{T}(\hat{y})=\begin{bmatrix}[l]\|\hat{y}_{\mathrm{dc}}\|\\
\|\hat{y}_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}\end{bmatrix}\preceq\gamma_{T}\big(\rho_{T}(u)\big)

(15)

with some function
\gamma_{T}
. Then
\gamma_{T}
is a PAG.

Is PAG sharper than AG?

With the AG
\gamma
from (

) using (

) we could have immediately had for

One might say that PAG

is

It is possible that, on the contrary,

turns out sharper than

, perhaps in large part due to the crudeness of (

). Nevertheless, even in that case the

estimations of the output’s AC/DC components provided by

IV
Linear Case

In the linear case, PAG can be found using the basic linear system theory.

IV-A

Calculating the PAG

Consider system (
8
) with
f=0
and
g=0
. Let
G(s)
be its transfer matrix and
H(t)
its impulse response matrix:

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle:=C(sI-A)^{-1}B,\quad

\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{C},

(19)

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle:=C\mathrm{e}^{At}B,

\displaystyle t\geq 0.

(20)

Recall that
y(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}H(\tau)u(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau
and thus the exact AG is the linear function

\gamma(\|u\|_{\infty})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|H(t)\|\,\mathrm{d}t\>\|u\|_{\infty}.

(21)

Let
H_{T}(t)
be the
T
-periodic-impulse response:

H_{T}(t):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}H(t+kT)\equiv C\mathrm{e}^{At}(I-\mathrm{e}^{AT})^{-1}B.

(22)

Theorem 1

The exact PAG of a stable linear system with transfer matrix
G
and
T
-periodic-impulse response
H_{T}
is

\gamma_{T}\big(\rho_{T}(u)\big)=\Gamma_{T}\rho_{T}(u)

(23a)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{T}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\gamma_{\mathrm{dc}}&0\\
0&\gamma_{\mathrm{ac}}(T)\end{bmatrix},

(23b)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{dc}}

\displaystyle=\|G(0)\|,

(23c)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{ac}}(T)

\displaystyle=T\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\\
\|v\|=1\end{subarray}}D_{\median}(v\cdot H_{T}).

(23d)

Proof:

Since
A
is Hurwitz, the system’s response to a
T
-periodic input
u(t)=u_{\mathrm{dc}}+u_{\mathrm{ac}}(t)
globally converges to the
T
-periodic output
\hat{y}(t)=\hat{y}_{\mathrm{dc}}+\hat{y}_{\mathrm{ac}}(t)
with

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{\mathrm{dc}}

\displaystyle=G(0)u_{\mathrm{dc}},

(24a)

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{\mathrm{ac}}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}H(\tau)u_{\mathrm{ac}}(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(24b)

From (
24a
) follows (
23c
).

Since
u_{\mathrm{ac}}
is
T
-periodic, (
24b
) can be rearranged into the
periodic convolution

\hat{y}_{\mathrm{ac}}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{T}H_{T}(\tau)u_{\mathrm{ac}}(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(25)

Thus,

\|\hat{y}_{\mathrm{ac}}(t)\|=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\\
\|v\|=1\end{subarray}}\left\|\int_{0}^{T}v\cdot H_{T}(\tau)u_{\mathrm{ac}}(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|.

(26)

If
\langle u_{\mathrm{ac}}\rangle_{T}=0
then for every constant
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

\|\hat{y}_{\mathrm{ac}}(t)\|\equiv\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\\
\|v\|=1\end{subarray}}\left\|\int_{0}^{T}\big(v\cdot H_{T}(\tau)-\mu^{\mathsf{T}}\big)u_{\mathrm{ac}}(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|.

(27)

The exact maximum of

\left\|\int_{0}^{T}\big(v\cdot H_{T}(\tau)-\mu^{\mathsf{T}}\big)u_{\mathrm{ac}}(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|

(28)

with respect to
u_{\mathrm{ac}}(\cdot)
bounded by
\|u_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}
is delivered by

u_{\mathrm{ac}}(t-\tau)=\frac{v\cdot H_{T}(\tau)-\mu^{\mathsf{T}}}{\|v\cdot H_{T}(\tau)-\mu^{\mathsf{T}}\|}\|u_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}.

(29)

In order to satisfy the requirement
\langle u_{\mathrm{ac}}\rangle_{T}=0
, it is necessary to take
\mu^{\mathsf{T}}=\median(v\cdot H_{T})
according to (
6
). With this
\mu
, the value of (
28
) using (
29
) is
TD_{\median}(v\cdot H_{T})\|u_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}
which leads to (
23d
).
∎

IV-B

Comparison of Three Gains

For linear systems, we have the following asymptotic input-output gains:

1.

\|G(j\omega)\|
– relevant if the input is a pure sine wave with angular frequency
\omega
;

2.

PAG
\gamma_{T}
– relevant for bounded
T
-periodic inputs;

3.

AG
\gamma
– relevant for all uniformly bounded inputs.

The set of
T
-periodic signals is a superset of sine waves with the corresponding
fundamental
angular frequency
\omega=2\pi/T
. Under this correspondence, each gain on the list above provides an output bound for a more general class of inputs than the previous one. The following relations therefore hold.

Proposition 2

If
G
is the transfer matrix of a stable linear system,
\gamma_{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{dc}}
are the AC/DC components of the exact PAG from Theorem
1
, and
\gamma^{\prime}
is the derivative of the exact AG
\gamma
from (
21
), then for all
T>0

\displaystyle\|G(0)\|

\displaystyle=\hskip 7.11317pt\gamma_{\mathrm{dc}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{\prime},

(31a)

\displaystyle\big\|G(j2\pi/T)\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{ac}}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{\prime}.

(31b)

Figure
1
shows qualitatively the three gains for an example two-dimensional SISO LTI system. The AC component
\gamma_{\mathrm{ac}}
of the PAG captures the high-frequency attenuation feature of the system and converges to the classical AG for long-period signals. At the same time, the low-frequency gain is captured by the DC component
\gamma_{\mathrm{dc}}
. In this sense, PAG occupies a “middle ground” between the classical AG and the frequency response.

We also note that, in agreement with intuition and Proposition
1
, the peaks of
\gamma_{\mathrm{ac}}
occur at the periods
2\pi k/\omega_{0}
where
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega_{0}
is the resonant frequency of the system.

Finally, observe that for the exact PAG

\displaystyle\big\|\gamma_{T}\big(\rho_{T}(u)\big)\big\|_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\gamma_{\mathrm{dc}}\|u_{\mathrm{dc}}\|+\gamma_{\mathrm{ac}}(T)\|u_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\gamma_{\mathrm{dc}},\gamma_{\mathrm{ac}}(T)\}\|\rho_{T}(u)\|_{1}

(32)

and by Proposition
2
inequality (
18
) holds, at least as a non-strict inequality. Therefore, in the linear case PAG is
at least as sharp
as the classical AG (in the sense of Section
III-C
).

V
Nonlinear Case

V-A

General System

Consider system (
8
) and suppose that we are in the setting of Section
III-B
, i.e., Assumptions
1
and
2
hold and guarantee that for every
u\in\mathcal{U}_{T}
there exists a unique periodic solution
\hat{x}
in a compact set
\mathcal{S}(\|u\|_{\infty})
that attracts all other solutions starting in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. Our goal is to attain a PAG by estimating
\rho_{T}(\hat{y})
of the periodic output
\hat{y}
. The estimation uses the following quadratic bounds on the nonlinear part of the system. They hold, e.g., if
f
and
g
have continuous second derivatives.

Assumption 3

For all arguments
x,\bar{x}\in\mathcal{S}(u_{\mathrm{max}})
and
\|u\|,\|\bar{u}\|\leq u_{\mathrm{max}}
functions
f
and
g
in (
8
) satisfy

\Big\|f(x,u)-f(\bar{x},\bar{u})\\
-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(\bar{x},\bar{u})(x-\bar{x})-\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(\bar{x},\bar{u})(u-\bar{u})\Big\|\\
\leq M_{f}\big(\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2}\big),

(33a)

\Big\|g(x)-g(\bar{x})-\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x})\Big\|\leq M_{g}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2},

(33b)

\displaystyle f(0,0)=0,\quad\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(0,0)=0,\quad\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(0,0)=0,

(33c)

\displaystyle g(0)=0,\quad\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(0)=0

(33d)

where
\partial f/\partial x
,
\partial f/\partial u
, and
\partial g/\partial x
are the Jacobian matrices and
M_{f},M_{g}>0
are some constants.

We start by estimating
f(\hat{x},u)
along the periodic solution
\hat{x}
based on presupposed knowledge of
\rho_{T}(\hat{x})
.

Lemma 1

Let
\hat{f}(t):=f(\hat{x}(t),u(t))
with any
T
-periodic
\hat{x}
and
u
. Then under Assumption
3

\rho_{T}(\hat{f})\preceq M_{f}\begin{bmatrix}\|\rho_{T}(\hat{x})\|^{2}+\|\rho_{T}(u)\|^{2}\\
2\big(\|\hat{x}_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|u_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\big)\end{bmatrix}.

(34)

Proof:

From (
33a
) with
(\bar{x},\bar{u})=(0,0)
we obtain

\big\|f(\hat{x}_{\mathrm{dc}},u_{\mathrm{dc}})\big\|\leq M_{f}\big(\|\hat{x}_{\mathrm{dc}}\|^{2}+\|u_{\mathrm{dc}}\|^{2}\big)

(35)

and with
(\bar{x},\bar{u})=(\hat{x}_{\mathrm{dc}},u_{\mathrm{dc}})
– the “Jensen gap” estimation

\displaystyle\big\|\hat{f}_{\mathrm{dc}}-f(\hat{x}_{\mathrm{dc}},u_{\mathrm{dc}})\big\|

\displaystyle\equiv\big\|\big\langle f(\hat{x},u)\big\rangle_{T}-f\big(\langle\hat{x}\rangle_{T},\langle u\rangle_{T}\big)\big\|

\displaystyle\leq M_{f}\big(\langle\hat{x}_{\mathrm{ac}}^{2}\rangle_{T}+\langle u_{\mathrm{ac}}^{2}\rangle_{T}\big)

\displaystyle\leq M_{f}\big(\|\hat{x}_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|u_{\mathrm{ac}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\big)

(36)

which together produce the first component of the vector inequality (
34

VII
Conclusions

We introduce the period-aware asymptotic gain (PAG) – an input-output gain for nonlinear systems with arbitrary bounded periodic input. Similarly to the classical asymptotic gain, it relates supremum norms of the input and output. However, it also takes into account the input periodicity, resembling a linear system’s frequency response. The gain allows one to speak of bandwidth, low-pass behavior, etc. for nonlinear systems in a quantifiable manner. Future analysis will include inputs that are sums of signals with commensurable or incommensurable periods and almost periodic signals. Application of the PAG to the analysis of periodically forced networks is of interest as well.
