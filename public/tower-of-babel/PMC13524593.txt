Immunopathogenesis of itch: an integrative framework for chronic pruritus
Authors: Agnieszka Kaczmarska-Such, Adam Reich
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Chronic pruritus is increasingly recognized as a complex neuroimmune disorder rather than a purely sensory symptom. Historically, itch research has progressed through successive mechanistic paradigms—from histamine and mast cells, through dedicated neuronal circuits, to cytokine-driven and intracellular signaling pathways. Yet, each advance revealed that chronic pruritus cannot be explained by a single mediator or pathway. In this review, we propose the immunopathogenic spiral of itch as an integrative framework that describes both the evolution of pruritus research and the interconnected biology of itch pathogenesis. It reflects not only the cumulative history of the field but also the biological progression within an individual patient: from acute, single-mediator itch to a self-sustaining neuroimmune network in which multiple mechanistic layers operate simultaneously. In contrast to the traditional itch–scratch cycle, the spiral model captures how barrier dysfunction, immune activation, and neuroimmune remodeling link successive mechanistic layers into self-reinforcing networks. We discuss how histaminergic signaling, sensory neuron specialization, type 2 immune polarization, IL-17-dependent pathways, and JAK-signal transducer and activator of transcription convergence represent interconnected rather than competing layers of itch biology. This framework may help explain why single-target therapies achieve meaningful but incomplete itch control, supporting the rationale for multi-target therapeutic strategies.

Introduction

Dermatology is primarily a visual discipline in which skin conditions can be seen, felt, and, if needed, analyzed under a microscope. However, pruritus escapes direct observation. It cannot be identified during clinical examinations or detected in histological samples. Understanding itch requires a shift from visible structures to the cellular and molecular level, where immune cells, epithelial cells, and peripheral sensory neurons interact through bidirectional (and sometimes even multidirectional) communication (Cevikbas and Lerner, 2020).

Pruritus, also known as itch, is an unpleasant sensation that evokes the desire to scratch (Ständer et al, 2025). Acute itch functions as a protective response, warning the organism of potentially harmful environmental factors (Sutaria et al, 2022). Chronic pruritus (CP), defined as itch lasting longer than 6 weeks, is a highly prevalent and burdensome condition and 1 of the most common reasons for dermatological consultation (Ständer et al, 2007). Although pruritus is a hallmark of inflammatory skin diseases, particularly atopic dermatitis (AD), prurigo nodularis (PN), lichen planus, and urticaria, it also accompanies systemic, neurological, and metabolic disorders (Hashimoto and Yosipovitch, 2019; Zeidler et al, 2019). When itch becomes persistent and dissociated from the course of the underlying disorder, it is increasingly recognized as a distinct pathological entity (Ständer et al, 2025). Despite significant advances achieved in recent years, the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying CP remain incompletely understood, and no single framework has yet integrated the growing number of immune and neuronal pathways involved in its pathogenesis (Butler et al, 2024).

In clinical practice, CP is commonly explained by the “itch-scratch-itch cycle” paradigm, a self-perpetuating process in which scratching intensifies the inflammatory and neuronal pathways responsible for itch (Cevikbas and Lerner, 2020) (Figure 1). In this review, we propose that this “itch-scratch-itch cycle” is not a simple circle returning to its starting point, and scratching does not merely sustain inflammation; rather, it may contribute to the remodeling of the itch pathway itself (Mochizuki et al, 2015; Ulzii et al, 2019). Rather than returning to baseline, each turn amplifies the underlying pathology, forming not a cycle but a spiral. This spiral metaphor also extends to the history of pruritus research itself. Over the past century, successive frameworks, from histaminergic signaling through dedicated neuronal circuits to type 2 cytokine-mediated neuroimmune communication, have each illuminated 1 layer of the pruritogenic network, only to reveal the next (Dale and Laidlaw, 1910; Ikoma et al, 2006; Oetjen et al, 2017) (Figure 2).Figure 1The itch-scratch cycle. The organizing principle of this review is that the turns of the spiral are not sequential replacements but cumulative layers. Histamine did not disappear when IL-31 was discovered, it became 1 component of a multilayered network. Neurons were not superseded by cytokines, they became the integrators upon which immune signals converge. However, the spiral metaphor is not only a map of research history. It also reflects the biological progression within an individual patient. A mosquito bite or contact with an allergen engages simple mechanisms: histamine release, pruriceptor activation, and a transient itch-scratch response. If the stimulus persists, each new layer may become self-sustaining and begin to drive the next, until the network operates independently of the trigger that initiated it.

Immunopathological mechanisms represent the connecting arrows that run between the turns of the spiral, connecting each mechanistic layer to the next. The chronicity of pruritus arises from this layering: overlapping loops linking barrier disruption, immune activation, and neuronal sensitization operate simultaneously, each reinforcing the others (Kabashima, 2013).

Peripheral Itch Transmission: The Neuroimmune Interface

The skin is among the most densely innervated organs, serving as the primary interface for somatosensory perception. Cutaneous nerve endings originate from somatosensory neurons located in the dorsal root ganglia (DRG), which transmit sensory signals from the skin to the central nervous system (Cevikbas and Lerner, 2020; Ikoma et al, 2006). These neurons give rise to 3 classes of fibers, distinguished by conduction velocity and degree of myelination. Heavily myelinated Aβ fibers terminate in the dermis, where they primarily function as mechanoreceptors, sensing vibration, pressure, and hair movement (Abraira and Ginty, 2013). In contrast, thinly myelinated Aδ fibers and unmyelinated C fibers terminate as free nerve endings within the epidermis, where they function as nociceptors, thermoreceptors, and pruriceptors (Potenzieri and Undem, 2012). This superficial localization exposes them to a wide range of pruritogens, chemical mediators that activate sensory pathways involved in itch signaling (Bautista et al, 2014). These pruritogens can be classified by their origin. Endogenous pruritogens are produced locally by keratinocytes and resident or infiltrating immune cells (including mast cells, T lymphocytes, eosinophils, and other granulocytes) or reach the skin as circulating factors via the local vasculature (Steinhoff et al, 2022). Exogenous pruritogens originate outside the organism and encompass environmental, chemical, microbial, or parasitic factors that contact the skin directly or arrive via the circulation (Szöllősi et al, 2022). Sensory neurons do not operate in isolation but participate in continuous bidirectional communication with the surrounding tissue. Activated C fibers release neuropeptides, most notably substance P (SP) and calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), which act on keratinocytes, endothelial cells, and immune cells to promote neurogenic inflammation (Ruppenstein et al, 2021). Keratinocytes in turn produce alarmins, such as thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP) and IL-33, neurotrophic factors, including nerve growth factor (NGF), as well as cytokines and chemokines that modulate neuronal excitability and survival (Balaji et al, 2025; Imai, 2019; Lin et al, 2024; Talagas et al, 2018). This bidirectional signaling positions the skin as an active neuroimmune interface in which epithelial, immune, and neuronal compartments continuously modulate each other's function.

Turn 1: The Histamine Era And Its Limits

Histamine, released from IgE-activated mast cells, was the first mediator identified as a driver of itch. By binding histamine receptors 1(H1Rs) on cutaneous sensory nerve endings, it initiates pruriceptive signaling and generates the itch sensation (Dale and Laidlaw, 1910). Although histamine may also be produced by basophils and macrophages, mast cells are considered the primary cellular source linking immune activation to itch (Iwasaki et al, 2021; Karasuyama et al, 2009). This single-mediator paradigm dominated pruritus research for much of the 20th century (Ikoma et al, 2006). Four histamine receptor subtypes (H1R, histamine receptor 2 [H2R], histamine receptor 3 [H3R], and histamine receptor 4 [H4R]), all belonging to the G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) family, are currently recognized (Akdis and Simons, 2006). Each is characterized by distinct tissue distribution, downstream signaling pathways, and different roles in the modulation of immune responses and itch transmission (Gutzmer et al, 2009).

The H1R is coupled to the Gq/11 protein and is expressed on sensory neurons, keratinocytes, mast cells, T lymphocytes, and vascular endothelial cells (Haas et al, 2008; Thurmond et al, 2008). Such widespread distribution allows it to participate in both itch signaling and in the regulation of inflammatory responses. In inflammatory skin diseases associated with pruritus, H1R expression is upregulated, which amplifies the response to histamine and contributes to the persistence of itch (Ohsawa and Hirasawa, 2014). Upon binding to H1R on sensory neurons, histamine activates phospholipase C beta 3/Ca2+ signaling and triggers itch transmission (Han et al, 2006). In keratinocytes, H1R activation modulates the production of 2 opposing regulators of cutaneous innervation: NGF, which promotes nerve branching, and semaphorin 3A, which inhibits it (Fukamachi et al, 2011; Kanda and Watanabe, 2003). This dual regulation links histamine signaling to the structural remodeling of epidermal innervation observed in chronic pruritic dermatoses (Agelopoulos et al, 2023). At the level of adaptive immunity, H1R signaling on dendritic cells (DCs) supports T helper cell (Th) type 1 polarization by promoting IL-12 production and IFN-γ+ effector T-cell priming (Vanbervliet et al, 2011). Consistent with these observations, in H1R-deficient mice, IFN-γ production is impaired, and the immune response shifts toward a Th2-dominant phenotype, characterized by elevated IL-4, IL-13, and IgE levels (Jutel et al, 2001, 2006). Clinical observations in AD have reported reduced serum IL-31 levels in patients receiving H1R antagonists, although the cellular source of this reduction remains unclear (Otsuka et al, 2011). In clinical practice, however, H1 antihistamines—despite being first-line therapy in urticaria—fail to control itch in the vast majority of chronic pruritic dermatoses, including AD, PN, psoriasis, and chronic pruritus of unknown origin (CPUO) (Zuberbier et al, 2022; Yosipovitch and Bernhard, 2013; Matterne et al, 2019; Kawashima et al, 2003; Hawro et al, 2022). The limited therapeutic efficacy of H1R antagonism in most chronic pruritic conditions has shifted interest toward other histamine receptor subtypes as possible mediators of persistent itch.

H2R and H3R: immunomodulatory but not pruritogenic

In contrast to H1R, neither H2R nor H3R appears to contribute directly to pruriceptive signaling. H2R is expressed on gastric parietal cells and multiple immune cell populations, including monocytes and DCs. Its activation suppresses IL-12 and enhances IL-10 production, thereby attenuating proinflammatory signaling (Elenkov et al, 1998; Idzko et al, 2002). Despite these immunomodulatory properties, pharmacological blockade of H2R with ranitidine does not suppress scratching behavior in experimental models (Rossbach et al, 2011). This is consistent with clinical evidence showing limited benefits of H2R antagonism in chronic urticaria (Fedorowicz et al, 2012; Zuberbier et al, 2022). A notable exception is mastocytosis, in which combined H1R/H2R antagonism remains clinically effective for pruritus relief (Kwiatkowska and Reich, 2021; Theoharides et al, 2015).

The H3 receptor (H3R) was originally characterized as a presynaptic autoreceptor on central histaminergic neurons (Arrang et al, 1983). However, Rossbach et al (2011) demonstrated that H3R is also expressed on skin-innervating DRG neurons alongside H1R and H4R. Pharmacological blockade of H3R elicited scratching behavior in mice, an effect that was abolished by combined H1R and H4R antagonism and lowered the activation threshold of histamine-sensitive sensory neurons in calcium imaging experiments (Rossbach et al, 2011). These findings suggest that H3R functions as an inhibitory modulator of peripheral pruriceptors, restraining their sensitivity to histamine.

H4R: expanding histaminergic itch beyond H1 signaling

The limited efficacy of H1R- and H2R-targeted treatments and the unclear role of H3R in pruritus underscore a gap in histamine-dependent itch signaling. The discovery of H4R in the early 2000s positioned it as a potential missing link (Thangam et al, 2018; Thurmond, 2015). H4R is expressed on multiple immune cell populations involved in allergic inflammation, including mast cells, eosinophils, basophils, and T lymphocytes, allowing histamine signaling to amplify immune activation across several cellular compartments (Ling et al, 2004; Jemima et al, 2014; Buckland et al, 2003). At the adaptive level, H4R activation enhances IL-5 and IL-13 production in Th2 cells (Nikolouli et al, 2024) and promotes IL-17 secretion in Th17 cells (Han et al, 2017; Mommert et al, 2012). This dual capacity extends the immunological reach of histamine beyond Th2-driven atopic inflammation into Th17-mediated diseases such as psoriasis (Han et al, 2017). H4R-deficient mice display both reduced scratching and attenuated inflammation in experimental AD (Dunford et al, 2007; Rossbach et al, 2016).

The preclinical findings generated therapeutic optimism, but clinical results were disappointing (Uluckan et al, 2023). JNJ-39758979, the first H4R antagonist tested clinically, showed significant pruritus improvement in phase 2a trial in moderate AD, but was terminated due to adverse events (Murata et al, 2015). Adriforant (ZPL-3893787) demonstrated significant antiinflammatory activity in a phase 2a trial in moderate-to-severe AD yet failed to produce a meaningful antipruritic effect (Werfel et al, 2019). Izuforant (LEO 152020), the first H4R antagonist tested in urticaria, also failed to demonstrate significant improvement in cholinergic urticaria (Grekowitz et al, 2024).

In summary, histamine, derived from the Greek word histos ('tissue'), was the first mediator placed at the center of pruritus pathophysiology. However, the clinical insufficiency of antihistamines did not invalidate the histamine framework, it revealed that histamine represents 1 component of a broader pruritogenic network. As the focus shifted from the mediator to its target, the sensory neuron emerged as an active integrator of converging immune inputs rather than a passive receiver of histamine signals.

Turn 2: The Neuron as Integrator

For much of the 20th century, itch was considered a low-intensity variant of pain rather than an independent sensory modality. Itch transmission requires a complete signaling chain: a pruritogen, a receptor on the sensory nerve endings, activation of intracellular transduction channels, propagation of the signal through the DRG, and a dedicated spinal circuit that relays it to the brain. Over the past 2 decades, each component of this chain has been molecularly defined. The first evidence came from electrophysiological studies in human skin, which identified a subset of unmyelinated, mechano-insensitive C fibers that respond selectively to histamine but not to mechanical or thermal stimuli (Schmelz et al, 1997).

Dedicated pruriceptors

At the receptor level, Mas-related G protein-coupled receptors (Mrgprs), first characterized in murine models, were identified as neuronal sensors dedicated to non-histaminergic itch. MrgprA3-expressing neurons respond exclusively to itch-inducing stimuli, and their ablation eliminates itch without affecting pain (Han et al, 2013). Two human family members have been characterized: human Mas-related G protein-coupled receptor X (MRGPRX)1, which mediates chloroquine- and peptide-induced itch (Solinski et al, 2013), and MRGPRX4, which is selectively activated by bile acids and bilirubin, providing a molecular basis for cholestatic pruritus (Yu et al, 2019). A second class of pruriceptive receptors, protease-activated receptors (PARs), links extracellular protease activity directly to neuronal activation. PAR-2 was the first component of the pathway implicated in non-histaminergic itch; it is activated by tryptase from mast cells and kallikreins produced by keratinocytes (Steinhoff et al, 2003), with expression upregulated in lesional skin of patients with CP (Kitayama et al, 2025). PAR-1 was subsequently shown to mediate itch evoked by Staphylococcus (S) aureus protease V8 independently of inflammation (Deng et al, 2023b). Since S aureus colonizes lesional skin across multiple inflammatory dermatoses, particularly AD (Totté et al, 2016), this mechanism may be clinically relevant.

Despite their distinct ligands, signals from histamine receptors, Mrgprs, and PARs all converge on transient receptor potential (TRP) ion channels—specifically transient receptor potential vanilloid (TRPV)1 and transient receptor potential ankyrin 1 (TRPA1)—which serve as final common effectors that convert chemical stimulation into the generation of action potential (Cevikbas et al, 2014; Wilson et al, 2011).

Spinal transmission

The molecular diversity of pruriceptive neurons was further resolved by single-cell RNA sequencing of murine DRG, which classified them into 3 distinct clusters—nociceptor populations 1/2/3 (DRG clusters) (NP1, NP2, and NP3)—each responding to different classes of pruritogens (Usoskin et al, 2015). Functionally analogous populations have been identified in human DRG, although direct cross-species translation requires caution (Tavares-Ferreira et al, 2022). Among these, the NP3 cluster is considered the principal transducer of inflammatory itch, given its expression of natriuretic peptide B (Nppb) and IL-31 receptors (Wang and Kim, 2020).

This circuit has been defined primarily in murine models; its precise organization in the human spinal cord remains to be fully characterized. These interneurons engage downstream gastrin-releasing peptide receptor+ (GRPR+) neurons that are selectively required for itch transmission (Sun and Chen, 2007; Mishra and Hoon, 2013). GRPR+ interneurons relay the signal to projection neurons in lamina I, which transmit it via the spinothalamic tract to the thalamus (Andrew and Craig, 2001; Chen and Sun, 2020).

Clinical translation of the neuronal framework

Within the spinal dorsal horn, μ-opioid receptors (MOR) and κ-opioid receptors (KOR) exert opposing effects: MOR activation promotes itch by suppressing inhibitory interneurons, whereas KOR signaling directly inhibits spinal itch transmission (Wang et al, 2021). This balance explains why opioid analgesics, such as epidural morphine, can provoke pruritus. In chronic pruritic conditions, this axis appears disrupted: reduced cutaneous KOR expression correlates with itch intensity in chronic kidney disease-associated pruritus (CKD-aP) (Wieczorek et al, 2020), AD (Tominaga et al, 2007), and psoriasis (Kupczyk et al, 2017), suggesting that KOR downregulation is a shared feature across immunologically distinct pruritic diseases.

KOR agonists have demonstrated clinical efficacy in specific settings. Nalfurafine is approved in Japan for uremic pruritus (Kumagai et al, 2010), and intravenous difelikefalin is approved for CKD-aP (Fishbane et al, 2020). However, oral difelikefalin failed to achieve significant itch reduction in AD, despite biomarker evidence of IL-31 and type 2 pathway modulation (Guttman-Yassky et al, 2023). MOR antagonists, such as naltrexone, are recommended for cholestatic pruritus (Lindor et al, 2019).

Gabapentinoids

Pregabalin and gabapentin reduce neuronal excitability by binding to the α2δ-1 subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels. They are effective in neuropathic itch (brachioradial pruritus, notalgia paresthetica) and uremic pruritus (Hercz et al, 2020). Clinical benefit has also been reported in PN (Wang et al, 2026) and cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL) (Misery, 2014).

Neurokinin 1 receptor antagonists

SP, released from activated C-fiber endings, signals through the neurokinin 1 receptor (NK1R) on keratinocytes, immune cells, and spinal interneurons (Ständer and Yosipovitch, 2019). Increased SP levels have been reported in AD, PN, and bullous pemphigoid (BP), correlating with itch intensity (Hashimoto et al., 2020; Ohanyan et al, 2018). However, Azimi et al (2017) demonstrated that SP-induced scratching in mice depends not on NK1R but on MrgprA1 (Azimi et al, 2017). SP was also identified as a potent agonist of MRGPRX2 on human mast cells, triggering IgE-independent degranulation (Subramanian et al, 2016). This dual action positions SP at the center of a neuroimmune amplification loop: sensory neurons release SP, which activates mast cells via MRGPRX2, which in turn release tryptase and histamine that further sensitize sensory neurons (Nagamine et al, 2024).

Despite strong preclinical rationale, NK1R antagonists have produced disappointing clinical results. Serlopitant significantly reduced pruritus in phase 2 trials in CP and PN (Ständer et al, 2019; Yosipovitch et al, 2018), but the phase 3 program was terminated. A meta-analysis has confirmed that NK1R antagonists as a class produce only modest antipruritic effects (Ho et al, 2025).

Limits of the neuronal framework

The neuron-centric era established that sensory neurons are essential integrators of itch signaling, yet targeting neuronal pathways alone proved insufficient in most chronic pruritic conditions. KOR agonists achieved approval in CKD-aP, and gabapentinoids achieved approval in neuropathic itch, but NK1R antagonists failed in phase 3 development. Even the clinical success of difelikefalin carried an immunological signature: biomarker analyses showed that baseline itch intensity correlated with IL-31, TSLP, C-C motif chemokine ligand (CCL) 17, and NGF and that treatment responders exhibited selective reductions in these markers (Spencer et al, 2026). These findings indicate that chronic itch is sustained by neuroimmune interactions rather than by neuronal activation alone.

Turn 3: The Immune Polarization of Itch

The pruritogenic potential of cutaneous inflammation varies across inflammatory skin diseases and correlates with the dominant Th polarization (Garcovich et al, 2021). AD is the prototypical Th2-driven dermatosis, in which pruritus represents a cardinal and often the most debilitating symptom (Kabashima, 2013). Psoriasis, by contrast, was historically regarded as a non-pruritic dermatosis, yet is now recognized to be associated with pruritus in the majority of patients (Jaworecka et al, 2023; Elewski et al, 2019). Although itch is common in psoriasis, it is typically less intense and more variable than in AD (Kaczmarska et al, 2023).

The conceptual framework to explain this difference was provided by the recognition that adaptive immunity is organized into functionally distinct Th subsets, each producing a characteristic cytokine profile (Mosmann et al, 1986; Harrington et al, 2005; Park et al, 2005). The mutual antagonism between Th1/Th17 and Th2 responses within the same skin was first demonstrated by Eyerich et al (2011) in patients with cooccurring psoriasis and AD (Eyerich et al, 2011). Building on this principle, Eyerich and Eyerich subsequently proposed an integrated classification of non-communicable inflammatory skin diseases based on dominant immune response patterns—type 1 (Th1/ innate lymphoid cell type 1 [ILC1], IFN-γ, TNF-α), type 2 (Th2/ILC2, IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, IL-31), and type 3 (Th17/ILC3, IL-17A, IL-17F, IL-22)—each associated with distinct cutaneous pathology and a different pruritogenic profile (Eyerich and Eyerich, 2018). Among these patterns, Th2-dominated conditions are consistently associated with the most intense pruritus, a finding that points to the Th2 cytokine network as the principal immunological driver of itch. These axes exist in reciprocal regulation: IFN-γ inhibits Th2 differentiation, while IL-4 suppresses Th1 and Th17 development (Gajewski et al, 1988; Miossec, 2009). This cross-regulation is clinically relevant: dupilumab-treated patients with AD may develop psoriasiform eruptions (Lin et al, 2025). Transcriptomic analyses of these lesions have confirmed a shift from Th2 to Th17 polarization (Grolleau et al, 2023; Su and Zeng, 2023). These observations suggest that shifts in Th polarization may also alter the pruritogenic profile of the disease.

Cytokines as direct pruritogens

Barrier disruption activates keratinocytes to release alarmins and proinflammatory mediators, including TSLP, IL-33, endothelin-1, prostaglandins, leukotrienes, and proteases such as kallikreins and cathepsin S. These mediators act on DCs, ILC2s, and Th2 lymphocytes, amplifying type 2 immune responses (Figure 3). A key determinant of pruritogenic potential is whether cytokines can directly engage sensory neurons. IL-4 and IL-13 signal through IL-4 receptor alpha (IL-4Rα) expressed on DRG neurons, activating JAK1–— signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) 6 signaling and lowering neuronal activation thresholds (Oetjen et al, 2017). IL-13 exerts additional pruritogenic effects through IL-13 receptor alpha (IL-13Rα) 1, which forms a type II receptor complex with IL-4Rα. In sensory neurons, IL-13Rα1 activation enhances GPCR- and TRP-dependent pruriceptive circuits. At the tissue level, IL-13Rα1 signaling induces periostin, an IL-4/IL-13-driven matricellular protein that promotes extracellular matrix remodeling. Through this mechanism, IL-4/IL-13 signaling sustains neuronal sensitization beyond the duration of acute cytokine exposure (Hashimoto et al, 2021a; Izuhara et al, 2019).

The Th17 and Th1 axes also contribute to pruriceptive signaling through distinct mechanisms. IL-17A has recently been shown to directly activate DRG neurons via IL-17 receptor (IL-17R) A. Two distinct mechanisms have been identified: upregulation of the pruritic ion channel TRPV4 through extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) signaling (Zhang et al, 2025) and induction of neuronal IL-6 expression, which triggers spinal astrocyte activation and amplifies chronic itch signaling (Liu et al, 2026). These findings provide a molecular basis for pruritus in Th17-dominated conditions, such as psoriasis. In contrast, IFN-γ does not directly activate pruriceptive neurons but modulates itch through indirect mechanisms, including impairment of epidermal barrier function and upregulation of IL-31 receptor alpha (IL-31RA) on dermal endothelial cells (Oetjen et al, 2017). This gradient of pruritogenic directness—from IL-4/IL-13 and IL-31 (direct neuronal activation), through IL-17A (recently demonstrated direct activation), to IFN-γ (indirect modulation)—may partly explain why Th2-dominated conditions produce the most intense pruritus, while itch in psoriasis is more variable (Elewski et al, 2019).

Eosinophils: amplifiers, not initiators of itch

Beyond cytokine-producing lymphocytes, the Th2 response also recruits eosinophils into inflamed skin. Under physiological conditions, eosinophils are absent in the skin. IL-5 promotes their maturation and mobilization from the bone marrow, while eotaxins (CCL11, CCL24, CCL26), released by keratinocytes and fibroblasts under IL-4/IL-13 stimulation, direct their migration into the dermis (Radonjic-Hoesli et al, 2021). Once recruited, eosinophils do not directly activate pruriceptive neurons. Instead, they amplify itch through indirect mechanisms: promoting sensory nerve branching and hyperinnervation, increasing SP content in cutaneous nerves, and serving as a major cellular source of IL-31 (Leiferman, 2015; Nakashima et al, 2019; Rüdrich et al, 2018). In murine models, eosinophil depletion reduces not only eosinophilic infiltration but also lymphocyte and mast cell accumulation, epidermal thickening, and collagen deposition (Lee et al, 2015). Circulating eosinophil counts have also been identified as a biomarker of clinical presentation and therapeutic response in CPUO (Roh et al, 2021).

Clinical trial data consistently support this interpretation. Benralizumab (antiIL-5Rα) achieved near-complete eosinophil depletion in moderate-to-severe AD (the HILLIER trial) and in chronic spontaneous urticaria (the ARROYO trial), yet neither trial demonstrated improvement in pruritus; the HILLIER trial was terminated early for lack of efficacy (Guttman-Yassky et al, 2024; Altrichter et al, 2024). Mepolizumab (antiIL-5) was similarly ineffective in AD, failing to reduce pruritus despite significantly depleting eosinophils (Oldhoff et al, 2005). These findings indicate that eosinophils function as downstream amplifiers within a pruritogenic program driven by cytokines that act directly on sensory neurons, and that eosinophil depletion alone is insufficient to achieve meaningful itch control.

IL-31: the neuroimmune cytokine

Although IL-4 and IL-13 lower neuronal activation thresholds through JAK1-STAT6 signaling, intradermal injection of either cytokine fails to induce scratching behavior comparable to histamine or cowhage (Oetjen et al, 2017). Their primary pruritogenic effect is, therefore, sensitization rather than direct neuronal activation. This distinction has clinical correlates: while dupilumab achieves substantial skin clearance in AD, a subset of patients exhibits an incomplete itch response, suggesting that IL-4/IL-13 blockade alone does not fully address the pruritogenic complexity of type 2 inflammation (Simpson et al, 2016).

IL-31, discovered in 2004 as a Th2-derived cytokine, provides a direct mechanistic link between adaptive immunity and itch. Transgenic overexpression of IL-31 in mice produced severe pruritus, alopecia, and dermatitis, demonstrating that a single cytokine could drive itch behavior (Dillon et al, 2004). IL-31 signals through the heterodimeric IL-31RA/ oncostatin M receptor (OSMR) complex expressed on TRPV1+/TRPA1+ DRG neurons (Cevikbas et al, 2014), a population corresponding to the NP3 cluster identified by single-cell RNA sequencing as the principal transducer of inflammatory itch (Usoskin et al, 2015). Unlike histamine, which triggers immediate but short-lived itch, IL-31 induces a delayed and sustained scratching response that more closely reflects the temporal pattern of CP (Datsi et al, 2021).

The cellular sources of IL-31 extend beyond Th2 lymphocytes to include macrophages, basophils, and DCs. In AD lesions, CD163+ M2 macrophages constitute a significant IL-31-producing population, regulated by a TSLP-periostin-basophil network, the disruption of which attenuates scratching behavior (Hashimoto et al, 2023). Beyond direct neuronal activation, IL-31 upregulates Nppb expression in DRG neurons and modulates cytokine release from keratinocytes, engaging both neuronal and epithelial components of the itch circuit (Meng et al, 2018). IL-31 also enhances neuronal responses to other pruritogens, functioning as a sensitizer in addition to being a direct activator (Takahashi et al, 2023).

The classical distinction between IL-4/IL-13 as sensitizers and IL-31 as a direct pruritogen has recently been challenged. Jha et al (2025) demonstrated that IL-4 and IL-13 promote sensory neuron growth and enhance itch responses at a magnitude comparable to that of IL-31, suggesting that these cytokines constitute a coordinated Th2 pruritogenic program rather than a hierarchy of sensitizers and activators (Jha et al, 2025). The clinical relevance of the IL-31 axis is supported by the robust and rapid antipruritic efficacy of IL-31RA blockade across multiple pruritic conditions, including PN, prurigo nodularis (BP), and CKD-aP (Ständer et al, 2025).

Type 2 itch beyond AD

The pruritogenic reach of type 2 immunity extends beyond AD, providing some of the strongest evidence that Th2 cytokines represent a shared pruritogenic mechanism across immunologically distinct conditions.

PN

PN is characterized by the highest itch intensity scores among chronic pruritic dermatoses and is now recognized as a distinct neuroimmunological entity (Williams et al, 2020; Kwatra et al, 2025). Single-cell RNA sequencing reveals a shared type 2 immune skewing between PN and AD, but PN diverges in its stromal compartment, showing upregulated collagen synthesis, fibrosis, and unique fibroblast populations absent in AD (Alkon et al, 2023; Ma et al, 2024).

The immune profile of PN is not purely Th2 but involves mixed responses. Th2 cytokines (IL-4, IL-13, IL-31) dominate, with IL-31 consistently elevated in lesional skin and correlated with itch intensity (Kwatra et al, 2023; Liao et al, 2024). However, Th17 and Th22 pathways also contribute: Belzberg et al (2021) demonstrated systemic and cutaneous Th22 polarization with increased IL-22 expression from both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells (Belzberg et al, 2021). Recent endotyping has separated PN into subgroups based on Th2 gene expression signatures, with differing relationships to the Th17/Th22 axes (Liao et al, 2024; Ständer et al, 2025).

Dupilumab became the first approved systemic therapy for PN (Kim et al, 2026; Yosipovitch et al, 2024), and nemolizumab the second, based on the OLYMPIA phase 3 trials (Robertson and Rohan, 2026). With both agents, itch reduction preceded visible improvement in skin lesions, consistent with IL-31’s dual role in direct neuronal activation and immune-mediated inflammation (Kwatra et al, 2023; Ständer et al, 2025). Transcriptomic analyses confirmed that IL-31RA blockade suppresses not only the Th2/IL-13 but also the Th17/IL-17 pathways and modulates neuroimmune and epithelial dysregulation, including normalization of neuronal architecture (Deng et al, 2023a; Tsoi et al, 2022). However, the success of Th2-targeted therapies does not extend uniformly across all patient populations. African American patients with PN exhibit a less Th2-polarized immune profile and lower response rates to dupilumab (Cornman et al, 2024). Genome-wide association analyses identified the IL31 locus as the first genome-wide significant genetic risk factor for PN, with the risk variant occurring more frequently in European than in African individuals, potentially contributing to these differences in therapeutic response (Vasavda et al, 2023; Patrick et al, 2025).

BP

BP illustrates how type 2-driven pruritus operates outside the classical spectrum of atopic disease. Pruritus is often the earliest and most persistent manifestation of BP, and its severity does not consistently correlate with autoantibody levels or disease activity scores, suggesting that itch is mediated through mechanisms that are partly distinct from the autoimmune blistering process (Osuoji et al, 2025). Although BP is initiated by IgG and IgE autoantibodies targeting BP180 and BP230, the inflammatory cascade sustaining itch is predominantly type 2-driven (Cozzani et al, 2015). Hashimoto et al (2020) demonstrated that pruritus severity correlates with eosinophil infiltration, IL-31RA/OSMRβ expression, IL-13, periostin, and basophil density, but not with mast cells, IL-4, or TSLP (Hashimoto et al., 2020). Eosinophils, rather than T lymphocytes, appear to be the principal source of IL-31 in BP lesions, with markedly elevated IL-31 concentrations in blister fluid compared with serum (Rüdrich et al, 2018). This distinguishes BP from AD, where Th2 lymphocytes and macrophages are the predominant IL-31-producing cells, indicating that distinct diseases may converge on a shared IL-31-driven itch pathway through different cellular architectures.

The contrast with pemphigus vulgaris reinforces this point. IL-31 and IL-31RA expression are significantly increased in BP skin and peripheral nerves but are minimal in pemphigus vulgaris, a blistering disease associated with pain rather than itch (Ergun et al, 2023). This suggests that IL-31 axis activation, rather than autoimmunity per se, determines pruritus generation. At the transcriptomic level, single-cell RNA sequencing identified IL-13/IL-13Rα1 as the dominant mediator of immune-stromal crosstalk in BP, with IL-13-secreting Th2 cells activating fibroblasts and DCs in a positive feedback loop (Liu et al, 2024). Therapeutic data further support this framework. Dupilumab, the first biologic approved for moderate-to-severe BP, selectively suppresses IL-4- and IL-13-producing T-cell populations, while Th1 responses remain largely unchanged (Murrell et al, 2024; Takamura and Teraki, 2022). These findings indicate that although autoantibodies initiate BP, the pruritic phenotype is largely sustained by type 2 inflammatory pathways, particularly eosinophil-derived IL-31 signaling.

Senile pruritus

Senile pruritus is defined as chronic itch in older adults (≥65 years) without a primary dermatological or systemic cause. It remains poorly defined and is often classified within the heterogeneous category of CPUO (Chen et al, 2022a; Clerc and Misery, 2017; Kim et al, 2019). Whether it represents a distinct entity or reflects undiagnosed conditions in the elderly remains debated (Valdes-Rodriguez et al, 2015).

Although historically attributed to xerosis and peripheral nerve degeneration, emerging evidence implicates immunosenescence, age-related immune remodeling characterized by declining Th1 responses and a progressive Th2 bias (Sandmand et al, 2002; Chen et al, 2022b). In aged skin, these shifts, combined with barrier dysfunction and chronic low-grade inflammation, may promote Th2-skewed environments that are conducive to CP. Skin biopsies from patients with CPUO show increased GATA3 expression in dermal T cells compared with healthy controls, confirming Th2 polarization (Dehner et al, 2021). Elderly patients with CP similarly demonstrate elevated serum IgE, peripheral eosinophilia, and reduced CD8+ T-cell counts, features consistent with a progressive loss of Th1 counterregulation and age-related Th2 skewing (Berger and Steinhoff, 2011; Ingrasci et al, 2021).

Preliminary therapeutic data support this framework. In a systematic review, all 21 patients with chronic idiopathic pruritus treated with dupilumab achieved clinically meaningful itch reduction (mean numeric rating scale decrease from 8.33 to 0.95), with improvement evident within 2 weeks (Gael et al, 2022). Abrocitinib, a selective JAK1 inhibitor, similarly reduced pruritus in patients with CPUO by 53.7% over 12 weeks (Kwatra et al, 2024). These responses to type 2-targeted therapies suggest that CP in older adults may represent a manifestation of age-related immune remodeling rather than a distinct disease entity (Ingrasci et al, 2021).

Pruritus in systemic diseases

CKD-aP has historically been attributed to uremic toxin accumulation. However, multiple studies have demonstrated elevated serum IL-31 levels in patients with pruritus undergoing hemodialysis compared with non-pruritic controls (Ko et al, 2014; Oweis et al, 2021). Spencer et al (2026) confirmed this association in over 850 patients undergoing hemodialysis: itch intensity correlated with IL-31, CCL17, and NGF at baseline, and IL-31, TSLP, and TNFα were selectively reduced in responders to difelikefalin but not in non-responders (Spencer et al, 2026). These biomarker changes occurred independently of uremic toxin levels, suggesting that the antipruritic effect is mediated, at least in part, through type 2 immune pathways.

Cholestatic pruritus has long been attributed to bile acid accumulation and lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) signaling, with serum autotaxin activity correlating with itch intensity (Kremer et al, 2010; Beuers et al, 2014). However, total bile acid levels do not directly correlate with pruritus severity (Beuers et al, 2023; Vander Does et al, 2022). The identification of MRGPRX4 as a bile acid receptor on human DRG neurons provided a molecular explanation: MRGPRX4 activation depends on specific bile acid species rather than their total concentration (Yu et al, 2019). Emerging evidence implicates IL-31 as an additional pruritogenic mediator in cholestasis. Serum IL-31 levels are elevated in primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) and primary biliary cholangitis (PBC), and baseline IL-31 levels correlate with pruritus scores in both conditions (Xu et al, 2023). IL-31 mRNA is also elevated in hepatocytes from patients with PSC and NASH; in a humanized liver murine model, the farnesoid X receptor agonist obeticholic acid increased hepatocyte IL-31 expression (Xu et al, 2023). In the ENHANCE trial, seladelpar (a peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor delta agonist) dose-dependently reduced both serum IL-31 and bile acids in PBC, with reductions in IL-31 correlating with pruritus improvement (Kremer et al, 2024). Complete resolution of refractory cholestatic pruritus has also been reported with nemolizumab in a patient with PSC following 2 liver transplants, despite prior failure of IL-4/IL-13 blockade (Soto-Canetti and Talia, 2025).

In mastocytosis, neoplastic mast cells produce IL-31 at levels that correlate with pruritus severity, supporting a pruritogenic role that is independent of IgE-mediated pathways (Ishii et al, 2009; Lange et al, 2017). The resolution of pruritus with nemolizumab has been reported in indolent systemic mastocytosis that is refractory to omalizumab and dupilumab (Minbaeva and Tyring, 2026).

CTCL

CTCL is a lymphoproliferative disorder with complex and incompletely understood mechanisms underlying its initiation, progression, and symptom generation (Dippel et al, 2022). Pruritus affects the majority of patients across all stages of CTCL and represents the primary driver of QOL impairment. It frequently remains refractory to conventional antipruritic therapy (Ahern et al, 2012; Lewis et al, 2018). The mechanisms underlying pruritus in CTCL are closely linked to the immunobiology of disease progression (Misery, 2014). CTCL encompasses a spectrum from indolent mycosis fungoides (MF) to the aggressive, leukemic Sézary syndrome (SS). SS is now considered to arise from central memory T cells, distinct from the skin-resident effector memory T cells that give rise to MF (Zhang and Sokol, 2026). CTCL is particularly informative for studying itch immunopathogenesis, as the malignant cell is itself a T lymphocyte, whose cytokine output evolves with disease progression (Papadavid et al, 2003; Campbell et al, 2010).

In early-stage MF, the tumor microenvironment retains a Th1-dominant character, with IFN-γ and IL-2 production, and pruritus is typically moderate (Chong et al, 2008; Saed et al, 1994). As MF progresses, transcriptomic profiling reveals a gradual shift toward Th2/Th9 polarization, with progressive upregulation of STAT3/STAT5/STAT6 signaling (Stadler et al, 2020; Netchiporouk et al, 2014; Xiao et al, 2022).

In advanced SS, this transition is largely complete: malignant T cells produce IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, and IL-31 and actively suppress Th1 function in surrounding non-malignant lymphocytes, an immunosuppressive effect reversible by neutralizing IL-4 and IL-13 in coculture experiments (Guenova et al, 2013). Integrated single-cell transcriptomic analyses across multiple cohorts have demonstrated that malignant clones in CTCL progressively adopt Th2 differentiation programs and are enriched within a B cell-rich tumor microenvironment whose expansion correlates with advanced disease stage (Jiang et al, 2024; Li et al, 2024).

The molecular link between this evolving immune polarization and the intensification of itch seems to be IL-31. Nattkemper et al (2016) demonstrated elevated epidermal and dermal IL-31 expression in patients with CTCL with severe pruritus, with epidermal levels showing a direct correlation with itch intensity (Nattkemper et al, 2016). The principal cellular source is the C-C chemokine receptor (CCR) type 4-positive malignant T cell itself; accordingly, therapies that reduce the malignant T-cell burden have been associated with decreased IL-31 levels and improved pruritus (Cedeno-Laurent et al, 2015). Mogamulizumab, an antiCCR4 antibody approved for relapsed/refractory MF/SS, directly depletes CCR4+ malignant T cells and has been demonstrated to cause significant improvement in pruritus, particularly in SS, where responses in the blood compartment are highest (Kim et al, 2018; Ottevanger et al, 2022).

In a murine CTCL model, Chen et al (2023) provided direct mechanistic evidence: IL-31 was overexpressed in the tumor microenvironment, IL-31RA was persistently upregulated on TRPV1+ sensory neurons innervating the tumor, and intratumoral antiIL-31 suppressed both scratching behavior and alloknesis, demonstrating that tumor-derived IL-31 drives itch through direct neuronal sensitization rather than through secondary inflammation (Chen et al., 2023). However, the use of type 2-targeted biologics in CTCL raises important immunological concerns. While dupilumab has produced rapid pruritus reduction in individual patients with SS, (Steck et al, 2021) IL-4/IL-13 blockade may paradoxically release Th1-mediated antitumor immunity or, conversely, disrupt the immunological balance that constrains malignant T-cell expansion (Guglielmo et al, 2025). A systematic review identified new or worsening CTCL lesions after dupilumab initiation, and expert consensus recommends avoiding dupilumab when MF/SS is suspected or confirmed (Amatore et al, 2026; Calvão et al, 2026). Geskin et al (2026) demonstrated ex vivo that dupilumab inhibits malignant T-cell proliferation and reverses the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment in SS, suggesting that reported cases may reflect diagnostic misclassification rather than drug-induced oncogenesis. Epidemiological data remain conflicting: while some database studies report increased CTCL risk after dupilumab initiation, (Hasan et al, 2024) a large retrospective cohort study found no increased lymphoma risk and suggested a possible protective trend (Kridin et al, 2026).

This clinical dilemma illustrates a broader challenge in the immunopathogenesis of itch: the same Th2 cytokines that drive pruritus may simultaneously serve immunoregulatory functions within the tumor microenvironment, and their therapeutic modulation carries consequences that extend beyond symptom control (Cabrera-Perez et al, 2025).

Turn 4: Beyond TH2 - IL-17A, IFN-Γ, and the Pruritogenic Potential of TYPE 1/3 Immunity

Psoriasis, a prototypical Th1/Th17-driven dermatosis, was historically classified as a non-pruritic dermatosis, a view so entrenched that itch was excluded from diagnostic criteria and rarely assessed in clinical trials (Elewski et al, 2019). Yet psoriasis epidemiological studies consistently report pruritus as the most bothersome symptom (Jaworecka et al, 2023; Szepietowski and Reich, 2016). For decades, this discrepancy had no mechanistic explanation: the Th17/Th1 cytokines driving psoriasis were not known to activate sensory neurons, and the type 2 paradigm offered no framework for understanding itch outside its own domain (Komiya et al, 2020). As outlined above (Section [Cytokines as direct pruritogens]), recent studies have identified IL-17A as a direct activator of sensory neurons and IFN-γ as an indirect modulator of itch. The following sections examine the specific molecular circuits underlying these effects and their clinical validation.

IFN-γ: indirect but consequential

IFN-γ, the signature cytokine of Th1 immunity, does not directly activate pruriceptive neurons (Oetjen et al, 2017). Instead, it modulates itch indirectly by impairing epidermal barrier function and upregulating IL-31RA on dermal endothelial cells (Kanoh et al, 2019; Feld et al, 2010). These indirect mechanisms may explain why Th1-dominant conditions produce less intense and more variable itch than Th2-driven diseases. Early clinical trials of recombinant IFN-γ in severe AD demonstrated overall clinical improvement, with therapeutic benefit correlating with eosinophil reduction rather than IgE suppression (Chang and Stevens, 2002); however, a subsequent meta-analysis found only a small and non-significant antipruritic effect, and the approach was not pursued further (Tan et al, 2022).

IL-17A as a direct pruritogen

Recent mechanistic studies published in 2025 support the concept that IL-17A may directly modulate sensory neuronal signaling through distinct neuroimmune pathways. Zhang et al (2025) showed that IL-17RA and IL-17RC on DRG neurons are upregulated in imiquimod-induced psoriasis. Conditional knockout of either receptor subunit in sensory neurons potently attenuated itch. The underlying mechanism involves ERK-dependent upregulation of TRPV4, a channel not previously implicated in psoriatic itch, establishing a complete IL-17R/ERK/TRPV4 signaling axis (Zhang et al, 2025).

uncovered a complementary spinal circuit: IL-17A promotes neuronal IL-6 expression in DRG, and this neuron-derived IL-6 triggers astrocyte activation and IL-1β secretion in the spinal dorsal horn, amplifying chronic itch signaling from the skin to the spinal cord (Liu et al, 2026). Delaleu et al (2025) added a structural dimension using a model of S aureus skin colonization preceding experimental psoriasis. In this model, IL-17A/IL-17RA signaling on TRPV1+ neurons drove CGRPα+ sensory nerve hyperinnervation, linking dysbiotic microbiota-driven immunity to long-term remodeling of cutaneous innervation. These findings are derived from murine models of psoriasis-like inflammation; direct demonstration of IL-17A-driven pruriceptor activation in human DRG neurons has not yet been reported. Together, these studies identify IL-17A as a direct pruritogen operating through circuits entirely distinct from the IL-4/IL-13/IL-31 pathways. In contrast to histamine or IL-31, no study has demonstrated that a single intradermal injection of IL-17A evokes acute scratching behavior. Rather, the pruritogenic effects of IL-17A appear to operate through chronic receptor upregulation and sustained neuronal sensitization (Zhang et al, 2025; Liu et al, 2026).

IL-23: upstream regulator, not a direct pruritogen

IL-23, the master regulator of Th17 differentiation, occupies a more complex position in itch biology. Pavlenko et al (2020) demonstrated that IL-23 activates only 5% of DRG neurons and, unexpectedly, does not induce scratching behavior. Instead, IL-23 pretreatment significantly reduced histamine-evoked scratching and calcium responses to histamine and capsaicin, suggesting an inhibitory effect on histaminergic itch via modulation of TRPV1 activity (Pavlenko et al, 2020). Immunohistochemical studies of psoriatic scalp skin have shown that IL-23 expression correlates significantly with itch intensity, alongside PAR-2, SP, and TRPV3 (Nattkemper et al, 2023).

Clinical validation of the IL-17 axis

AntiIL-17 therapy provided strong clinical confirmation that IL-17A functions as a direct pruritogen. Ixekizumab, secukinumab, and brodalumab all achieve rapid antipruritic effects in psoriasis. Notably, itch reduction often precedes skin clearance, a temporal dissociation consistent with direct neuronal effects rather than secondary antiinflammatory benefit (Gottlieb et al, 2015; Kimball et al, 2016; Strober et al, 2016). The PSORITUS trial demonstrated that secukinumab not only reduced pruritus but also reversed cutaneous neuroanatomical abnormalities. Upon treatment withdrawal, pruritus relapsed while neuroanatomical recovery persisted, suggesting that IL-17A blockade induces structural nerve remodeling that outlasts its antipruritic effect (Renkhold et al, 2024). Bimekizumab, which dually inhibits IL-17A and IL-17F, achieved higher rates of complete itch resolution than secukinumab, with responses sustained through 3 years, suggesting that IL-17F contributes independently to psoriatic pruritus (Augustin et al, 2026).

A meta-analysis of 13 placebo-controlled trials confirmed that antiIL-17 agents achieved the largest antipruritic effect size among systemic psoriasis therapies, followed by JAK inhibitors and adalimumab; antiIL-23 agents were not yet included due to the absence of published pruritus data at that time (Théréné et al, 2018). AntiIL-23 agents also reduce psoriatic pruritus, though with a slower onset than antiIL-17 therapy, consistent with IL-23's upstream regulatory role. Risankizumab achieved superior itch resolution compared with ustekinumab in the UltIMMa trials (Augustin et al, 2020; Gordon et al, 2018); guselkumab demonstrated clinically meaningful itch improvement in 66.7% versus 16.7% on placebo in the VISIBLE trial (Alexis et al, 2025).

The pruritogenic limits of type 1/3 immunity

The identification of IL-17A as a direct pruritogen resolved a long-standing paradox but did not eliminate the fundamental asymmetry between immune axes. Th2-dominated conditions consistently produce the most intense pruritus, whereas Th1/Th17-driven diseases generate less severe and more variable itch, an asymmetry that reflects the molecular architecture of each axis: IL-4, IL-13, and IL-31 each act directly on pruriceptive neurons, and their effects are additive, whereas the Th1/Th17 axis engages sensory neurons through fewer and partially indirect routes (Hawro et al, 2022; Kaczmarska et al, 2023). Despite robust antipruritic efficacy, IL-17 blockade does not fully resolve psoriatic itch in all patients. Residual pruritus persists even after near-complete skin clearance, and a substantial proportion of patients on systemic therapy report inadequate itch control (Théréné et al, 2018).

Moreover, psoriatic itch can persist in clinically uninvolved skin, suggesting that neuronal sensitization may outlast the inflammatory stimulus that initiates it (Ayasse et al, 2020). These observations mirror the pattern seen in Turn 3: just as IL-4/IL-13 blockade leaves residual itch in AD, IL-17A blockade leaves residual itch in psoriasis; each therapy silences 1 pruritogenic circuit while others continue to operate. Diseases with mixed immune profiles, or those undergoing immune polarization shifts during treatment, often exhibit the most treatment-resistant pruritus, precisely because multiple pruritogenic circuits operate simultaneously.

Turn 5: JAK-Stat Convergence—Where All Pathways Meet

The JAK-STAT pathway was identified in the early 1990s as a universal mechanism through which extracellular cytokine signals are translated into changes in gene expression. Receptor-associated JAKs (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, TYK2) phosphorylate latent STAT transcription factors, which then translocate to the nucleus to regulate target genes. For decades, this pathway was studied primarily in immune and hematopoietic cells (O'Shea et al, 2013; Philips et al, 2022). Its relevance to pruritus emerged when the cytokines described in Turns 3 and 4 were shown to signal through JAK-dependent receptors. Each receptor recruits a different JAK combination: IL-4Rα signals through JAK1/JAK3 (type I) or JAK1/TYK2 via IL-13Rα1 (type II), IL-31RA/OSMR through JAK1/JAK2, and the TSLP receptor through JAK1/JAK2 (Keegan et al, 1995; Russell et al, 1994). Despite this diversity, JAK1 is shared by all major pruritogenic type 2 cytokine receptors, positioning it as the intracellular node at which distinct itch pathways converge. This introduced a fundamentally different therapeutic principle: rather than blocking 1 cytokine at a time, a single intervention could interrupt signaling downstream of multiple pruritogenic receptors simultaneously (Mikhaylov et al, 2023).

As discussed in Section “Turn 3: The immune polarization of itch,” Oetjen et al (2017) demonstrated that type 2 cytokines activate JAK1 directly within sensory neurons and that genetic deletion of neuronal JAK1 reduces chronic itch with minimal effect on skin inflammation, dissociating the antipruritic from the antiinflammatory effect (Oetjen et al, 2017). Takahashi et al (2023) further showed that IL-31-induced itch requires neuronal STAT3, which propagates across spinal itch circuits (Takahashi et al, 2023). These findings establish that JAK-STAT signaling operates not only in immune cells but also within the neurons that transmit itch.

Clinical trials rapidly confirmed this prediction. In head-to-head comparisons, oral JAK1 inhibitors consistently achieved faster itch reduction than dupilumab. Upadacitinib (JAK1-preferential) was superior for itch response as early as week 1 in the Heads Up trial (Blauvelt et al, 2021) and produced clinically meaningful improvement by day 2 in the Measure Up trials (Guttman-Yassky et al, 2021). Abrocitinib (JAK1-selective) showed a comparable advantage in JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE, with faster itch reduction at week 2, though dupilumab gradually approached similar efficacy at later time points (Bieber et al, 2021; Reich et al, 2022). Topical ruxolitinib (JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor) produced significant itch reduction within 12 hours of the first application in a phase 3 trial, with measurable improvement reported as early as 15 minutes (Bissonnette et al, 2026; Papp et al, 2021). This timeline—minutes for topical, days for oral JAK inhibitors—precedes measurable skin clearance, supporting direct neuromodulation rather than secondary antiinflammatory benefit.

Beyond AD, abrocitinib achieved rapid itch improvement in PN (80% clinically meaningful response at week 12) and CPUO (60%) (Kwatra et al, 2024). The lower response rate in CPUO may reflect greater heterogeneity in the underlying pruritogenic mechanisms, not all of which are JAK1-dependent.

JAK inhibitors carry a broader risk profile than cytokine-targeted biologics. The most common adverse events include acne, herpes zoster reactivation, and elevated creatine phosphokinase (Yoon et al, 2024). A meta-analysis of over 20,000 dermatological patients found no significant increase in major adverse cardiovascular events or venous thromboembolism versus placebo, and 5-year upadacitinib data in AD confirmed a stable safety profile (Ingrassia et al, 2024). Caution remains warranted in elderly patients—the European Medicines Agency recommends JAK inhibitors in those aged ≥65 only if no suitable alternatives are available (Wollenberg et al, 2023)—a restriction particularly relevant for PN, which predominantly affects older adults (Lauletta et al, 2025).

The convergence of multiple pruritogenic pathways on JAK1 initially suggested that JAK inhibition might represent a universal antipruritic strategy. Clinical experience has shown otherwise. Residual pruritus persists even with optimal skin clearance, indicating that significant pruritogenic mechanisms operate entirely outside the JAK-STAT cascade, including IgE-independent mast cell activation via MRGPRX2, protease-PAR signaling, and central sensitization (Corbière et al, 2021). These JAK-independent mechanisms mark the boundaries of what intracellular convergence can achieve and point toward the final turn of the spiral.

Turn 6: The Spiral Widens—Old Players, New Rules

Each preceding turn introduced a dominant mechanism—histamine, neuronal circuits, type 2 cytokines, IL-17A, JAK-STAT convergence—and each revealed its own limits. Turn 6 does not introduce a new dominant pathway. Instead, it returns to elements already encountered in the spiral and shows how recent discoveries have transformed their roles. Unlike the preceding turns, turn 6 has no endpoint; it represents the expanding frontier of pruritus research, where each new discovery widens the spiral further. Old players reappear in new roles, unexpected cell types enter the network, and mechanisms once considered resolved reveal additional layers of complexity. The spiral does not close; it continues to widen.

The return of the mast cell

Turn 1 defined the mast cell through a single activation pathway: IgE- high-affinity IgE receptor (FcεRI)-histamine. The discovery of MRGPRX2 revealed that mast cells can be activated independently of IgE by SP, host defense peptides, and S aureus δ-toxin, releasing tryptase rather than histamine (Meixiong and Dong, 2017; Subramanian et al, 2016). In chronic prurigo, cortistatin—a neuropeptide released by sensory neurons—activates skin mast cells via MRGPRX2, and the number of MRGPRX2-expressing cells in lesional skin correlates with disease severity (Kolkhir et al, 2022).

This IgE-independent pathway explains why antihistamines fail in conditions where MRGPRX2-driven degranulation predominates. It also generates a self-amplifying loop: tryptase activates neuronal PAR-2, neurons release SP, and SP reengages MRGPRX2 on mast cells, a circuit independent of both IgE and JAK-STAT signaling (Nagamine et al, 2024). Because this mechanism bypasses both IgE and histamine, therapeutic strategies have shifted from blocking individual mediators to eliminating the cell itself. Barzolvolimab (antiKIT) tested this principle: in chronic inducible urticaria, a single dose produced complete responses in 95% of patients; in chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU), 38–51% achieved a complete response, including those who had failed omalizumab (Metz et al, 2026; Terhorst-Molawi et al, 2023). A complementary approach targets mast cell signaling rather than survival: remibrutinib, a Bruton's tyrosine kinase inhibitor, was recently approved for antihistamine-refractory CSU, confirming that mast cell activation can be therapeutically intercepted at multiple levels (Metz et al, 2025).

A parallel discovery expanded the MRGPR family beyond the mast cell. MRGPRX4, expressed on human DRG neurons and activated by bile acids and bilirubin, provided the first molecular explanation for cholestatic pruritus (Yu et al, 2019). A selective MRGPRX4 inverse agonist (HEP-50768) has demonstrated robust suppression of bile acid-induced pruritic behavior in humanized animal models, with preclinical profiling supporting its advancement to clinical evaluation (Yang et al, 2026).

Schwann cells: from support to sensory transduction

Turn 2 established the sensory neuron as the integrator of itch signaling. The neuronal turn returns through an unexpected cell: the Schwann cell (SC). SC have traditionally been regarded as supportive glial cells of the peripheral nervous system, responsible for axonal myelination and maintenance of nerve integrity. Specialized sensory SC located at the dermal-epidermal junction form close contacts with Aδ- and C-fiber endings (Abdo et al, 2019; Bray et al, 2020). Abdo et al (2019) demonstrated that activation of these cells can directly initiate sensory responses, redefining their role from passive support cells to active sensory transducers (Abdo et al, 2019). The concept is not new. As early as the 1990s, hydroxyethyl starch-induced pruritus was associated with long-term starch deposition within cutaneous nerves and SC, accompanied by persistent severe itch (Ständer et al, 2014). In AD, cutaneous SC exhibit morphologic changes and reduced contact with sensory nerve endings, making AD a valuable model for studying glial involvement in pruritus. SC secrete inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-4, and IL-6), neurotrophic factors (NGF and neurotrophin 3), and express itch-related receptors (TRPA1, TRPV1, and LPA receptor 1) (Bray et al, 2020).

More direct mechanistic evidence was provided by Zhang et al (2023) who showed that human SC express IL-13 receptors (IL-13Rα1 and IL-13Rα2). Upon IL-13 stimulation, these cells selectively release CCL7, a pruritogenic chemokine that is elevated in lesional AD skin. This response was not observed in keratinocytes, fibroblasts, or mononuclear cells, suggesting a SC-specific pruritogenic function (Zhang et al, 2023).

In addition, lysophosphatidic acid promoted SC migration and the upregulation of itch-related mediators, indicating that multiple inflammatory pathways may converge on SC activation (Zhang et al, 2023). These discoveries are connected to broader evidence of neuroimmune remodeling in CP. Jha et al (2025) demonstrated that IL-4 and IL-13 promote sensory neuron growth, whereas dupilumab-mediated IL-4Rα blockade reduces neurite outgrowth (Jha et al, 2025). Clinically, Wiegmann et al (2026) showed that dupilumab treatment improves intraepidermal nerve fiber density, reduces alloknesis in patients with AD, and normalizes IL-13 receptor expression (Wiegmann et al, 2026). Although these effects are not SC-specific, they reinforce the role of the neuroimmune axis in the structural remodeling of cutaneous innervation.

The sympathetic-eosinophil axis: stress as a pruritogenic circuit

Turn 3 positioned eosinophils primarily as downstream amplifiers rather than primary initiators of itch, recruited by type 2 cytokines and contributing to inflammatory amplification rather than directly activating pruriceptive neurons. Tian et al (2026) described an alternative neuroimmune pathway in murine models in which prodynorphin-positive noradrenergic sympathetic neurons innervating hairy skin recruit eosinophils through the CCL11-CCR3 axis and activate them via β2-adrenergic signaling. Genetic ablation of these neurons or eosinophil depletion attenuates stress-exacerbated type 2 inflammation, whereas selective neuronal stimulation is sufficient to intensify it (Tian et al, 2026). These findings support the concept of a direct sympathetic-eosinophil neuroimmune circuit linking psychological stress to cutaneous type 2 inflammation in experimental models and suggest a potential mechanistic explanation for the clinically recognized association between stress and worsening itch in AD and other pruritic dermatoses, although its relevance to human CP remains to be fully established (Gaudenzio and Basso, 2026).

OSMRβ: beyond IL-31 alone

OSMRβ is the shared receptor subunit for IL-31 and oncostatin M (OSM). While IL-31 directly activates sensory neurons, OSM sensitizes them, potentiating responses to other pruritogens without independently triggering itch (Tseng and Hoon, 2021). In PN, dermal OSM expression correlates with itch intensity (Hashimoto et al, 2021b). Recent data show that OSM also induces sensory nerve elongation exceeding that of IL-31 and NGF through JAK/STAT3, ERK, and p38/MAPK pathways. Notably, OSM production by CD4+ T cells is enhanced by IL-4 and TNF-α (Ishikawa et al, 2025). Vixarelimab, a monoclonal antibody targeting OSMRβ, achieved dose-dependent itch reduction with separation from placebo as early as week 1 in a phase 2b trial, with sustained improvement through 52 weeks in the open-label extension (Ständer et al., 2026, Ständer et al., 2026, Ständer et al., 2026).

Somatic mutations: beyond the spiral

Rajeh et al (2026) performed whole-exome sequencing of PN lesional skin and discovered recurrent somatic alterations in NOTCH1 that were present in lesional but absent in nonlesional skin and were entirely absent in AD. PN fibroblasts showed increased NOTCH1 expression and enhanced Notch signaling, alongside somatic deletions in axonal guidance genes (Rajeh et al, 2026). Separately, GWAS identified the IL31 locus as the first genome-wide significant genetic risk factor for PN (Patrick et al, 2025). These findings introduce a dimension that no cytokine-targeted or JAK-directed therapy can address: somatic genetic changes within the tissue itself. If NOTCH1-driven fibrosis sustains nodular architecture independently of the inflammatory signals that initiated it, even complete suppression of pruritogenic cytokines may not fully resolve established lesions.

Conclusion

The immunopathogenic spiral proposed in this review reframes CP as a multilayered neuroimmune process rather than a single-pathway disorder. Each turn of the spiral—from histamine through dedicated neuronal circuits, type 2 immune polarization, IL-17A-driven pruriception, to JAK-STAT convergence—introduces a dominant paradigm that advances both mechanistic understanding and therapeutic development yet simultaneously reveals its own limits. The recurring pattern across all turns is consistent: single-target therapies achieve meaningful but incomplete itch control. Antihistamines fail beyond urticaria. IL-4/IL-13 blockade leaves residual itch in AD. IL-17 blockade does not fully resolve psoriatic pruritus. Even JAK inhibitors, which intercept multiple cytokine pathways at a shared intracellular node, cannot address pruritogenic mechanisms operating outside the JAK-STAT cascade. Future therapeutic advances will likely require combination or multi-target strategies capable of simultaneously addressing neuronal sensitization, immune activation, and tissue-level changes to achieve complete itch control in refractory disease.
