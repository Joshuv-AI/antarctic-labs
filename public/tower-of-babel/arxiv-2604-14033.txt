On the divergence of the composition of irregular fields with BV functions
Graziano Crasta, Virginia De Cicco, Annalisa Malusa

Abstract
. We introduce a family of (nonlinear) pairing measures that ensure the validity of the divergence rule for composite functions 𝑩 ⁡ ( x , u ⁡ ( x ) ) \boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x)) , where 𝑩 ⁡ ( ⋅ , t ) \boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t) is a bounded divergence-measure vector field, and u u is a scalar function of bounded variation. The elements of the family depend on the choice of the pointwise representative of u u on its jump set. Beyond the standard properties, such as the Coarea and Gauss-Green formulas on sets of finite perimeter, this flexibility allows us to characterize the pairings that ensure the lower semicontinuity of the corresponding functionals along sequences converging in L 1 L^{1} with controlled precise values. We show that these lower semicontinuous pairings arise as the relaxation of integral functionals defined in Sobolev spaces.

1.
Introduction

This paper establishes a chain rule formula for the distributional divergence of the composition

{\bf v}(x)=\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))
defined in an open subset
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, extending the classical one

(1.1)

\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u))=(\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B})(x,u)+\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u)\cdot\nabla u\,,\ \ x\in\Omega

that holds in the pointwise sense if
\boldsymbol{B}(x,t)\in C^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
.

In the present setting, the scalar function
u
is assumed to be bounded and of bounded variation in an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and

\boldsymbol{B}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a family of bounded divergence measure vector fields.
Formulas of the type (
1.1
) are unifying,
including the following two special cases interesting in themselves: the chain rule formulas, if
\boldsymbol{B}(x,t)=\boldsymbol{B}(t)
is a Lipschitz function, and
the Leibniz formulas, if
\boldsymbol{B}(x,t)=G(t)\boldsymbol{A}(x)
for
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\boldsymbol{A}\in C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

The purpose of our study.

The main challenge in this framework is identifying a natural substitute for the scalar product

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u)\cdot\nabla u
featured in (
1.1
), usually called
pairing
between
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u)
and
Du
, enabling the extension of the chain rule identity in the sense of measures.

We introduce a novel, broader family of pairing measures that depend on the choice of the pointwise representative on the jump set. These pairings not only ensure the validity of the chain rule and preserve all the classical properties of known pairings (such as the coarea and Gauss–Green formulas), but they also provide a framework to study variational problems related to the semicontinuity and relaxation of integral functionals in the
BV
setting.

The choice of this substitute is also closely
connected with the validity of a Gauss–Green formula of the type

(1.2)

\int_{\Omega}(\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B})(x,u)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u)\cdot\nabla u\,dx=-\int_{\partial\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u)\cdot\nu_{\partial\Omega}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\,,

valid for
\boldsymbol{B}
,
u
as before, and
\Omega
Lipschitz domain, with inward normal
\nu_{\partial\Omega}
.

Much effort has been made in recent years in order to extend and generalize (
1.1
),
and so (
1.2
), in irregular frameworks
(see
[
10
,
4
,
16
,
27
,
29
,
21
,
22
,
28
]
) where a suitable notion of weak normal traces
of a vector field on countably
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-rectifiable sets is available (see
[
11
,
3
,
24
]
).

The strategy to extend (
1.1
) is to reinterpret this formula as an equality in the sense of measures, instead of in a pointwise sense, and to introduce a
pairing measure

(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)

between the field
\boldsymbol{b}(x,u(x))
(where
\boldsymbol{b}(x,t):=\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{B}(x,t))
) and the measure derivative
Du
of a
BV
bounded function
u
,
defined on smooth functions as

(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du):=\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u))-(\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B})(x,u).

The assumptions on
\boldsymbol{b}
ensuring that
(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)
is well-defined as a Radon measure in
\Omega
are stated in Section
3
, and include the requirement that
\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, is a family of bounded vector fields whose distributional divergence is a finite Radon measure (briefly
\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
), and such that the least upper bound of the total variations
|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)|
, denoted by
\sigma
, is also a Radon measure. Clearly, the same property is shared by the family
\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t)
, since
\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,s)ds
implies that
\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,s)ds
.
Under these weak assumptions on the vector field, but requiring that
u
is still a regular function, the natural definition in the sense of distributions for
(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)
is

\langle(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du),\varphi\rangle:=-\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\,\varphi\,d\sigma,\qquad\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),

where
F(x,t)
is the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the measure
(\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B})(x,t)
with respect to
\sigma
.
In
[
28
]
it is proved that this distribution is, in fact, a finite Radon measure in
\Omega
.
Nevertheless, if
u
is a
BV
function, the second term of the right hand side
is meaningless, until a pointwise value of
u
, defined
\sigma
-a.e., is given.

The literature on the pairing.

In the linear case
\boldsymbol{B}(x,t)=t\boldsymbol{A}(x)
it is well established (see
[
10
,
16
]
) that the extension to
BV
functions can be done whenever
\boldsymbol{A}\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
by choosing on
J_{u}
the precise representative

defined
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-a.e. and hence
|\Div\boldsymbol{A}|
-a.e. (see Proposition

), i.e.

(\boldsymbol{A},Du):=\Div(u\boldsymbol{A})-{u^{*}}\Div\boldsymbol{A}.

This is usually called
standard (linear) pairing
. All the main properties and features
as coarea, Leibniz and Gauss–Green formulas for this pairing are proved in
[
29
]
.

A nonlinear pairing was proposed in
[
28
]
, where, under the assumptions on
\boldsymbol{b}
stated in Section

, it is shown that the divergence rule extends to

and
{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)

\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)

(1.3)

\langle(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du),\varphi\rangle:=-\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}[F(x,u^{+})+F(x,u^{-})]\,\varphi\,d\sigma.

Hence, some very general Gauss–Green formulas are proved, which extend to the nonlinear pairing the analogous result proved in
[
29

The standard pairings are very useful technical tools in many situations,
for instance for optimality conditions in plasticity, which is the original motivation behind their introduction (see, e.g.,

,

), for the study of

(see

,

,

,
32
]
), for the study of transport equation and of
conservation laws with discontinuous flux of the form

(see
[

,
3
,
4
,

,

,

,
19
,

,
18
,

]
),
in the setting of weak formulations of PDEs such as Euler–Lagrange equations associated
with integral functionals defined in
BV

[
6
,
43
,
44
]
,
Dirichlet problems for equations involving the
1
–Laplace operator

[
7
,

]
,

[

,

]
, and

,

,

]
.

In contrast, the standard pairing
proves inadequate when dealing with semicontinuity properties.
This becomes apparent in
[
26
]
, where
we characterize the Borel selections
\lambda:\Omega\to[0,1]
making the functionals
corresponding to
(linear)
\lambda
–pairings

\left(\boldsymbol{A},Du\right)_{{\lambda}}=\Div(u\boldsymbol{A})-u^{\lambda}\Div\boldsymbol{A},

semicontinuous with respect to the strict convergence in
BV
. Here
u^{\lambda}:=(1-\lambda)u^{-}+\lambda u^{+}
is a Borel
choice of the representative of
u
on the jump set, between the approximate lower and upper limits
u^{-}
and
u^{+}
.
We show that, in general,
the standard pairing, corresponding to
\lambda=1/2
, does not share this property.
In
[
22
]
(see also
[
29
]
and
[

]
)
a detailed description of the density of the pairing measure

with respect to

is obtained.

Furthermore, in
[

the authors prove that, while the original definition is given in a distributional sense, the pairing measure introduced in

can be regarded in a variational sense as a relaxed functional with respect to the weak

, under the crucial assumption that the measure

has an

density with respect to the Lebesgue measure. More precisely,
for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
,
the functional

(1.4)

{\overline{F}_{\varphi}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\ d(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)\,,\qquad u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)

is the relaxation of

F_{\varphi}(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi\ \boldsymbol{b}(x,u)\cdot\nabla u\,dx&\text{if $u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)$}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\\
+\infty,&\text{if}\ u\in(BV(\Omega)\ \setminus W^{1,1}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\,,\end{cases}

with respect to weak
∗
convergence in
BV
.

Explanation of our results.

In the present paper we extend these results to general bounded fields with measure divergence. To this aim, we introduce the family of
nonlinear
\lambda
-pairings
which includes all those studied in the literature so far. We show that a further very general Gauss–Green formula, extending all the previous ones, holds, so that these pairings can be used for the weak formulation of PDEs. In addition, the nonlinear
\lambda
-pairings allow us to treat the lower semicontinuity and relaxation for integral functionals defined in
BV(\Omega)
, in the spirit of
[
26
,
30
]
.

The most obvious way to generalize (
1.3
) (the standard nonlinear pairing
(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)_{*}
) is to associate with every
Borel function
\lambda\colon\Omega\to[0,1]
,
an
external nonlinear
\lambda
–pairing

(1.5)

\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}=-\left((1-\lambda)F(x,u^{-})+\lambda F(x,u^{+})\right)\,\sigma+\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u))\,.

Nevertheless, we show that the external nonlinear pairing is not the right notion for the semicontinuity issue.
In order to identify a good selection that guarantees lower semicontinuity properties, we need to enlarge the class of these pairings.
Indeed, we define the

nonlinear
\lambda
–pairings

as the measure

(1.6)

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}=-F(x,u^{\lambda})\,\sigma+\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u))\,.

We prove that for every Borel
selection
\Lambda\colon\Omega\to[0,1]
there exists a Borel selection
\lambda\colon\Omega\to[0,1]
such that

\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\Lambda}}=\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}

(see Proposition
4.10
).

On the other hand, the nonlinear pairings differ from the external ones only on the jump set of
u
, and hence they share many of the same properties. In many cases these pairings coincide.
If, for instance,
t\mapsto F(x,t)
is a nondecreasing function on
\mathbb{R}
,
then there is a canonical identification between all external nonlinear pairings and nonlinear pairings
(see Proposition
4.10
and Remark
4.12
).
In particular, this is the case if
\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)
can be split as
\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)=g(t)\boldsymbol{A}(x)
,
g\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
,
g\geq 0
, and
\boldsymbol{A}\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}

(see Example
3.11
). The linear case
\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)=\boldsymbol{A}(x)
is recovered for
g(t)=1
, and

(\boldsymbol{A},Du)_{\lambda}=[\boldsymbol{A},Du]_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
, with the same
\lambda
.
Both pairings admit similar structure theorems.
We prove that the pairings of both families (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) are absolutely continuous with respect to the measure
|Du|
(see Theorem
4.7
). Their corresponding densities with respect to
|Du|
can be explicitly written in
J_{u}
in terms of the weak normal traces of
\boldsymbol{b}
on
J_{u}

(see Proposition
4.6
).
On the other hand, in
\Omega\setminus J_{u}
all the pairing measures coincide and the density of their diffuse parts
(which is independent of
\lambda
) can be described by using the weak normal traces of
\boldsymbol{b}
on the reduced boundary of the level set
\{u>\widetilde{u}(x)\}
(Theorem
6.1
).
This representation of the diffuse part is based on the use of the coarea formula (see Theorem
5.1
) valid for the pairings (
1.5
).
Finally, in Theorem
7.1
, Gauss–Green formulas is obtained, for both the families (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), by generalizing together Theorem 6.1 in
[
28
]
and Theorem 6.3 in
[
26
]
.

In the last part of the paper, we address lower semicontinuity and relaxation results.
As in
[
26
]
, we exhibit pairings
(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)_{L}
lower semicontinuous with respect to a suitable notion of convergence
(see Definition
8.2
),
weaker than strict convergence in
BV
.
Finally, a relaxation formula (
1.4
) for integral functionals defined in
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
can be established by using approximation sequences considered in
[
23
, Theorem 3.1]
(see Theorem
9.1
).

Outline of the paper.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
and Section
3
contain the necessary notation, the standing assumptions on the vector fields, and some preliminary results. In Section
4
, we define the families of nonlinear pairings and establish their representations outside the Cantor part of
Du
. Building on this, Sections
5
and
6
present a generalized Coarea formula and the representation of the Cantor part of the pairings, respectively. These tools are applied in Section 7 to derive generalized Gauss-Green formulas. Finally, the variational applications are discussed in the last two sections: Section
8
addresses the lower semicontinuity of the pairings, and Section
9
proves the related relaxation formula.

As a rule, we follow the notation established in

In the sequel,

We write

For

, respectively.
We denote by

the Lebesgue measure in

and by

(N-1)

Given a

-measurable set

,

the set of points of density

, namely

E^{t}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\ \lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{{\mathcal{L}}^{N}(E\cap B_{\rho}(x))}{{\mathcal{L}}^{N}(B_{\rho}(x))}=t\right\}\,.

The sets
E^{0}
,

,
\partial^{e}E:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(E^{0}\cup E^{1})
are called,

,
the

and
the

of

.

Let

be a Borel function.
We denote by
u^{-}
and
u^{+}

the

and the

of

,
defined respectively by

\displaystyle u^{+}(x):=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\ \{u>t\}\ \text{has density $0$ at $x$}\},

\displaystyle u^{-}(x):=\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\ \{u>t\}\ \text{has density $1$ at $x$}\}.

The function

is

at

if
u^{+}(x)=u^{-}(x)
; in this case, we denote their common value
by
\widetilde{u}(x)
.

For

,
a point

is a
Lebesgue point

of

(with respect to
{\mathcal{L}}^{N}
)
if there exists

such that

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\left(B_{r}(x)\right)}\int_{B_{r}\left(x\right)}\left|u(y)-z\right|\,dy=0.

In this case,

is a point of approximate

(see
[
36
, Proposition 1.163]
).
We denote by

the set of points where

We say that

is an

of

if

and a unit vector

and

(2.1)

\begin{gathered}\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{{\mathcal{L}}^{N}(B_{r}^{i}(x,\nu))}\int_{B_{r}^{i}(x,\nu)}|u(y)-a|\,dy=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{{\mathcal{L}}^{N}(B_{r}^{e}(x,\nu))}\int_{B_{r}^{e}(x,\nu)}|u(y)-b|\,dy=0,\end{gathered}

\begin{split}B_{r}^{i}(x,\nu)&:=\{y\in B_{r}(x):\ (y-x)\cdot\nu_{u}(x)>0\},\\

The triplet

, uniquely determined by (

)

2.1.
Measures

We denote by

2.2.
Functions of bounded variation

We say that

The vector space of all functions of bounded variation in

If

The jump set

Moreover, the singular part

,
and

D^{j}u:=D^{s}u\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}=(u^{i}-u^{e})\,\nu_{u}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}.

We denote by
D^{d}u:=D^{a}u+D^{c}u
the diffuse part of the measure

Du
.

At every point
x\in J_{u}
we have

-\infty<u^{-}(x)<u^{+}(x)<+\infty
and

u^{-}(x)=\min\{{u^{i}}(x),{u^{e}}(x)\},\qquad u^{+}(x)=\max\{{u^{i}}(x),{u^{e}}(x)\},\qquad x\in J_{u}.

In few occasions, to simplify the notation,
we will choose the orientation on
J_{u}
so that
{u^{i}}=u^{+}
on
J_{u}

(see
[
37
, §4.1.4, Theorem 2]
).
In this case,
\nu_{u}
coincides
{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}
-a.e. on
J_{u}
with the density of the polar decomposition
of
Du
, that, with some abuse of notation, we denote with
\nu_{u}
, i.e.,
Du=\nu_{u}|Du|
.
In what follows, we extend the functions
u^{-},u^{+}
to

\Omega\setminus(S_{u}\setminus J_{u})
by setting

u^{-}=u^{+}=\widetilde{u}\quad\text{in}\ \Omega\setminus S_{u}.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a Borel function
\lambda\colon\Omega\to[0,1]
(briefly
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
),
the

\lambda
–representative
of
u\in BV(\Omega)
is defined by

(2.3)

u^{\lambda}(x):=\begin{cases}\widetilde{u}(x),&x\in\Omega\setminus S_{u},\\
(1-\lambda(x))u^{-}(x)+\lambda(x)u^{+}(x),&x\in J_{u}.\end{cases}

Remark 2.3
.

When
\lambda(x)=1/2
for every
x\in\Omega
,
the
\lambda
–representative coincides with the
precise representative

u^{*}:=(u^{+}+u^{-})/2
of
u
.
Since
{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}(S_{u}\setminus J_{u})=0
for
u\in BV(\Omega)
,
the function
u^{\lambda}
is well defined
{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}
–a.e. in
\Omega
.

The following lemma relates the pointwise behavior of
\chi_{\{u>t\}}
to that of
u
, see
[
32
, Lemma 2.2]
for the proof.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
u\in BV(\Omega)
.
Then, for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
the function
\chi_{\{u>t\}}
belongs to
BV(\Omega)
.
Moreover, there
exists a Borel set
N_{t}\subset\Omega
, with
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(N_{t})=0
, such that
the following relation holds:

\chi_{\{u^{\pm}>t\}}(x)=\chi_{\{u>t\}}^{\pm}(x),\qquad\forall x\in\Omega\setminus N_{t}.

A
{\mathcal{L}}^{N}
-measurable set
E
compactly contained in
\Omega
is said to be a
set of finite perimeter
in
\Omega
if
its characteristic function
\chi_{E}
belongs to
BV(\Omega)
.
The
reduced boundary

\partial^{*}E
of a set of finite perimeter
E
is the set of all points

x\in\Omega
in the support of
|D\chi_{E}|
such that the limit

\widetilde{\nu}_{E}(x):=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{D\chi_{E}(B_{\rho}(x))}{|D\chi_{E}|(B_{\rho}(x))}

exists in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and satisfies
|\widetilde{\nu}_{E}(x)|=1
.
The function
\widetilde{\nu}_{E}\colon\partial^{*}E\to S^{N-1}
is called
the
measure-theoretic interior unit normal
to
E
.

A fundamental result of De Giorgi (see
[
5
, Theorem 3.59]
) states that

\partial^{*}E
is countably
(N-1)
-rectifiable
and
|D\chi_{E}|={\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\partial^{*}E
.
If
E
has finite perimeter in
\Omega
, Federer’s structure theorem
states that

\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega\subset E^{1/2}\subset\partial^{e}E

and
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\Omega\setminus(E^{0}\cup\partial^{e}E\cup E^{1}))=0

(see
[
5
, Theorem 3.61]
).

2.3.
Divergence–measure fields

We denote by
{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
the space of all
vector fields

\boldsymbol{A}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})

whose distributional divergence, defined by

\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,d\Div\boldsymbol{A}=-\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega),

is a finite Radon measure in
\Omega
.

The basic properties of such vector fields are collected in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\boldsymbol{A}\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
, and define

(2.4)

\Theta_{\Div\boldsymbol{A}}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \limsup_{r\to 0+}\frac{|\Div\boldsymbol{A}|(B_{r}(x))}{r^{N-1}}>0\right\}.

Then:

(i)

\Div\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
;

(ii)

\Theta_{\Div\boldsymbol{A}}
is a Borel set,
\sigma
-finite with respect to

{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}
;

(iii)

\Div\boldsymbol{A}=\Div^{a}\boldsymbol{A}+\Div^{c}\boldsymbol{A}+\Div^{j}\boldsymbol{A},

where
\Div^{a}\boldsymbol{A}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
{\mathcal{L}}^{N}
,

\Div^{c}\boldsymbol{A}(B)=0
for every set
B
with
{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}(B)<+\infty
,
and there exists
f\in L^{1}(\Theta_{\Div\boldsymbol{A}};{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Theta_{\Div\boldsymbol{A}})

such that

\Div^{j}\boldsymbol{A}=f\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Theta_{\Div\boldsymbol{A}}
.

Proof.

The main property (i) is proved in
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
.
The decomposition then follows from
[
4
, Proposition 2.3]
.
∎

The measures
\Div^{j}\boldsymbol{A}
and
\Div^{c}\boldsymbol{A}
are called, respectively,
the
jump part
and the
Cantor part

of the measure
\Div\boldsymbol{A}
, while
\Theta_{\Div\boldsymbol{A}}
is referred to
as the
jump set
of
\Div\boldsymbol{A}
.

It is proved in
[
3
]
that every
\boldsymbol{A}\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
admits weak normal traces on a countably
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–rectifiable set
\Sigma\subset\Omega
.
To fix notation, we briefly recall how the weak normal traces are defined.

Let
(\Sigma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a covering of
\Sigma
,
and let
N_{i}\subseteq\Sigma_{i}
be
Borel sets satisfying the following properties:

(R1)

Each
\Sigma_{i}
is an
oriented

C^{1}
hypersurface, equipped
with its classical normal vector field
\nu_{\Sigma_{i}}
.

(R2)

The sets
N_{i}\subseteq\Sigma_{i}
are pairwise disjoint, and
{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}(\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{i}N_{i})=0
.

(R3)

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist two open, bounded sets
\Omega_{i},\Omega^{\prime}_{i}
with
C^{1}
boundary,
whose exterior normal vectors we denote by
\nu_{\Omega_{i}}
and
\nu_{\Omega_{i}^{\prime}}
, such that

N_{i}\subseteq\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\partial\Omega^{\prime}_{i}
,
and

\nu_{\Sigma_{i}}(x)=\nu_{\Omega_{i}}(x)=-\nu_{\Omega^{\prime}_{i}}(x)\qquad\forall x\in N_{i}.

For every open set
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
of class
C^{1}
,
the trace of the normal component of
\boldsymbol{A}
on
\partial\Omega^{\prime}

can be defined in the sense of distributions by

\left\langle\Tr(\boldsymbol{A},\partial\Omega^{\prime})\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle:=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{A}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\varphi\,d\Div\boldsymbol{A},\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

It can be proved (see
[
3
]
) that this distribution is
induced by an
L^{\infty}
function on

\partial\Omega^{\prime}
, still denoted by
\Tr(\boldsymbol{A},\partial\Omega^{\prime})
, satisfying

(2.5)

\|\Tr(\boldsymbol{A},\partial\Omega^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega^{\prime};{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\partial\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|\boldsymbol{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}.

We fix an orientation on
\Sigma
by setting

\nu_{\Sigma}(x):=\nu_{\Sigma_{i}}(x),\qquad{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}-\text{a.e. on}\ N_{i}

and we define the weak normal traces of
\boldsymbol{A}
on
\Sigma
by

\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{A},\Sigma):=\Tr(\boldsymbol{A},\partial\Omega_{i}),\quad\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{A},\Sigma):=-\Tr(\boldsymbol{A},\partial\Omega^{\prime}_{i}),\qquad{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}-\text{a.e.\ on}\ N_{i}.

By
[
3
, Proposition 3.2]
,
these definitions are independent of the choice
of
\Sigma_{i}
and
N_{i}
.

Moreover, the normal traces belong to

L^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Sigma)

and it holds that

(2.6)

\Div\boldsymbol{A}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Sigma=\left[\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{A},\Sigma)-\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{A},\Sigma)\right]\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Sigma

(see
[
3
, Proposition 3.4]
).
In particular, by (
2.5
),

|\Div\boldsymbol{A}|(\Sigma)\leq 2\|\boldsymbol{A}\|_{\infty}{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}(\Sigma)
.

In what follows, we use the notation

(2.7)

\Tr^{*}(\boldsymbol{A},\Sigma):=\frac{\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{A},\Sigma)+\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{A},\Sigma)}{2}.

Remark 2.6
.

A simple example that helps illustrate the above notation is the following.
Let
E
be an open, bounded set of class
C^{1}
, and assume that
its boundary
\Sigma:=\partial E
is oriented by
the
inward
unit normal vector
\widetilde{\nu}_{E}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{A}
be a piecewise
C^{1}
vector field
that extends continuously to vector fields
\boldsymbol{A}_{i}
and
\boldsymbol{A}_{e}

in
\overline{E}
and
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E
respectively.
Then

\Tr^{e}(A,\partial E)=\boldsymbol{A}_{e}\cdot\widetilde{\nu}_{E},\qquad\Tr^{i}(A,\partial E)=\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\cdot\widetilde{\nu}_{E},

and

\Div\boldsymbol{A}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Sigma=(\boldsymbol{A}_{i}-\boldsymbol{A}_{e})\cdot\widetilde{\nu}_{E}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Sigma.

3.
Assumptions on the vector fields and known results

In line with
[
28
]
, this section details the assumptions on the vector fields and reviews the results required in what follows. Specifically, we consider
\boldsymbol{b}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
fulfilling the following requirements:

(b1)

\boldsymbol{b}
is a bounded Borel function in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
,
that is,
\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}:=\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})}<+\infty
.

(b2)

For
{\mathcal{L}}^{N}
–a.e.
x\in\Omega
, the map

t\mapsto\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)
is continuous in
\mathbb{R}
.

(b3)

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the map

x\mapsto\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)
belongs to
{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
.

(b4)

The least upper bound of the measures

|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)|
,

\sigma:=\bigvee_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)|\,,

is a Radon measure.

Recall that (see
[
5
, Definition 1.68]
)

\bigvee_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)|(E)=\sup\left\{\sum_{t\in T}|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)|(E_{t})\right\}

where
T
is a finite or countable subset of
\mathbb{R}
, and
(E_{t})_{t\in T}
is a partition of
E
.

Remark 3.1
.

In (b1), it is sufficient to assume that

\boldsymbol{b}
is a
locally
bounded Borel function,
but at the price of some additional technicalities in the proofs.

Remark 3.2
.

By (b3) and Proposition
2.5
(i), we have

\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Consequently, the measure
\sigma
also belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
.

Remark 3.3
.

From (b1) and Proposition 2.3 in
[
28
]
, it follows that
there exists a set
Z_{1}\subset\Omega
such that
{\mathcal{L}}^{N}(Z_{1})=0
and,
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
x\mapsto\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)

is approximately continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus Z_{1}
.

By the definition of the least upper bound of measures, we have

\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)\ll\sigma
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
We denote by
f(\cdot,t)
the Radon–Nikodým derivative
of
\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)
with respect to
\sigma
, so that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
it follows that

\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)=f(x,t)\,\sigma\ \text{for $\sigma$-a.e. }x\in\Omega,\qquad f(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}(\Omega;\sigma).

Furthermore, since
|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)|\leq\sigma
, it follows that

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}:\quad|f(x,t)|\leq 1\quad\text{for $\sigma$-a.e.}\ x\in\Omega.

We extend
\boldsymbol{b}
by setting
\boldsymbol{b}=0
in
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}
,
so that the vector field

(3.1)

\boldsymbol{B}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{b}(x,s)\,ds,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ t\in\mathbb{R},

is defined for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
and

\boldsymbol{B}(x,0)=0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
By (b2), for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

we have
\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)=\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{B}(x,t)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, by (b1) and
\boldsymbol{B}(x,0)=0
,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(3.2)

|\boldsymbol{B}(x,t)|\leq C|t|\ \ \ \qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 3.4
.

In what follows, when we wish to emphasize the fact that we are dealing with a family of
{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}
fields parametrized by
t
,
we will use the notation

\boldsymbol{b}_{t}:=\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t),\quad\boldsymbol{B}_{t}:=\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

The following properties of
\boldsymbol{B}
are established in
[
28
, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t)\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
and
\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t)\ll\sigma
.
If
F(\cdot,t)
denotes the Radon–Nikodým derivative
of
\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t)
with respect to
\sigma
, we have that

(3.3)

\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t)=F(\cdot,t)\,\sigma,\qquad F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)\,ds,\qquad\text{for $\sigma$--a.e.}\ x\in\Omega.

In particular, for
\sigma
–a.e.
x\in\Omega
,

(3.4)

|F(x,t)-F(x,s)|\leq|t-s|,\qquad\forall t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists a set
Z_{2}\subset\Omega

with
\sigma(Z_{2})=0
and
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(Z_{2})=0
such that
every
x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus Z_{2}

is a Lebesgue point of
F(\cdot,t)

with respect to
\sigma
,
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 3.7
.

Since both
\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)
and
\sigma
are finite Radon measures, we may assume that
f(\cdot,t)
, and hence
F(\cdot,t)
,
is a Borel function in
\Omega
(see, e.g.
[
42

).

One of the key properties of the fields considered here is that the composition
{\bf v}(x):=\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))
, with

, is itself a
{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}

[

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)-(b4)
,
and let
\boldsymbol{B}
be defined by (

).
For every

Remark 3.9
.

In what follows, we will use the notation

\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x)):=(\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B})(x,u(x))=\left.\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}(x,t)\right|_{t=u(x)}.

We introduce here three classes of fields satisfying (b1)–(b4), for which the subsequent results simplify.

Example 3.10

If
\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)=\boldsymbol{b}_{0}(t)

,

\boldsymbol{B}

Example 3.11

(Separated fields)

If

and
\boldsymbol{B}(x,t)=\boldsymbol{A}(x)G(t)

The linear case treated in earlier works

Example 3.12

(Separable fields)
.

Another interesting case, which we refer to as the

\Omega=\Omega^{+}\cup\Omega^{-}
,
with
\Omega^{+}\cap\Omega^{-}=\emptyset
, such that, for all

\begin{split}\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)\geq 0\quad\text{in}\ \Omega^{+},\qquad\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,t)\leq 0\quad\text{in}\ \Omega^{-},\end{split}

equivalently,

(3.5)

\begin{split}f(\cdot,t)\geq 0\quad\sigma-\text{a.e.\ in}\ \Omega^{+},\qquad f(\cdot,t)&\leq 0\quad\sigma-\text{a.e.\ in}\ \Omega^{-},\end{split}

and, for

–a.e.

is nondecreasing,
while for

–a.e.

Remark 3.13
.

Clearly, if
\boldsymbol{b}
is separated and

\boldsymbol{b}
is separable with

given by the Hahn’s decomposition of

Nevertheless, it is easy to construct separable fields that are not separated.
For example, take

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\boldsymbol{b}(x_{1},x_{2},t)=\left(g_{1}(t),h(x_{1},x_{2})\,g_{2}(t)\right)
, with

,
g_{1}\neq g_{2}
,
g_{2}\geq 0
.
In this case,

\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}(x_{1},x_{2},t)=h_{x_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})g_{2}(t)
, and
\Omega^{\pm}
are determined by the sign of

.

By Lemma
3.5
and the results recalled in subsection

,
it follows that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
the traces of the normal component of the vector field
\boldsymbol{B}_{t}

on an oriented countably
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–rectifiable set

can be defined in the sense of distributions, denoted by

\Tr^{i,e}(\boldsymbol{B}_{t},\Sigma)
.
These distributions are induced by
L^{\infty}
functions on
\Sigma
,
still denoted by
\Tr^{i,e}(\boldsymbol{B}_{t},\Sigma)
, and satisfy

\|\Tr^{i,e}(\boldsymbol{B}_{t},\Sigma)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mathcal{H}^{N-1})}\leq\|\boldsymbol{B}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

In what follows we use the notation

(3.6)

\beta^{i}(\cdot,t):=\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{B}_{t},\Sigma),\quad\beta^{e}(\cdot,t):=\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{B}_{t},\Sigma),

so that, by (

),

(3.7)

\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Sigma=\left[\beta^{i}(\cdot,t)-\beta^{e}(\cdot,t)\right]\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Sigma\,.

The basic estimates for the weak normal traces are established in
[

, Lemma 3.3 and Proposition 3.4]
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\Omega
be an oriented countably
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–rectifiable set.
There exists a subset
\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma

with
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime})=0
,
and representatives
\overline{\beta}^{i,e}
of

\beta^{i,e}

(in the sense that, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
the chosen
\overline{\beta}^{i,e}(\cdot,t)

agrees with

\beta^{i,e}(\cdot,t)

\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–a.e. in
\Sigma
)
such that

|\overline{\beta}^{i,e}(x,t)-\overline{\beta}^{i,e}(x,s)|\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}\,|t-s|\qquad\forall x\in\Sigma^{\prime},\ t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 3.15
.

In what follows, we always identify
\beta^{i,e}
with
the representatives
\overline{\beta}^{i,e}

provided by Proposition
3.14
.

Remark 3.16
.

Since
\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the normal traces

\gamma^{i,e}(\cdot,t):=\Tr^{i,e}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},\Sigma)

belongs to
L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
.
Moreover, one easily checks that

\beta^{i,e}(\cdot,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\gamma^{i,e}(\cdot,s)\,ds.

Finally, we recall the representation formula for the weak normal traces of the composite function

\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))
, as given in
[
28
, Proposition 4.5]
.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)
-
(b4)
,
let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)

and let
\Sigma\subset\Omega
be an oriented countably
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-rectifiable set.
Then the weak normal traces on
\Sigma
of the composite function
\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
,
as defined in Lemma
3.8
, are given by

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,u),\Sigma)(x)=\begin{cases}\beta^{i}(x,u^{i}(x)),&\text{for $\mathcal{H}^{N-1}$-a.e.}\ x\in J_{u},\\
\beta^{i}(x,\widetilde{u}(x)),&\text{for $\mathcal{H}^{N-1}$-a.e.}\ x\in\Sigma\setminus J_{u},\end{cases}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,u),\Sigma)(x)=\begin{cases}\beta^{e}(x,u^{e}(x)),&\text{for $\mathcal{H}^{N-1}$-a.e.}\ x\in J_{u},\\
\beta^{e}(x,\widetilde{u}(x)),&\text{for $\mathcal{H}^{N-1}$-a.e.}\ x\in\Sigma\setminus J_{u}.\end{cases}

With the convention
u^{i,e}(x)=\widetilde{u}(x)
for

, we obtain the compact expression

\Tr^{i,e}(\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,u),\Sigma)(x)=\beta^{i,e}(x,u^{i,e}(x))\qquad\text{for $\mathcal{H}^{N-1}$-a.e.\ $x\in\Sigma$}.

4.
Definition and basic properties of nonlinear pairings

We introduce a new definition of pairing between a
BV
function
u
and the field
\boldsymbol{b}(x,u(x))
, depending on the selected representative
u^{\lambda}
(see (
2.3
)).
Relying on Lemmas
3.5
and
3.8
, this pairing extends the divergence chain rule (
1.1
) to the present setting, providing a well–defined measure–theoretic interpretation.

Definition 4.1

(Nonlinear
\lambda
-pairing)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)-(b4)
,
let
\boldsymbol{B}
be defined by (
3.1
), and let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
For

\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,
the nonlinear
\lambda
–pairing
between
\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u)
and
Du
is the
finite Radon measure in
\Omega
defined by

(4.1)

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}:=-F(x,u^{\lambda})\,\sigma+\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u))\,.

By Lemma
3.5
, the definition of
\sigma
, and Lemma
3.8
, this measure belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Using (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), we obtain,
for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
,

(4.2)

\langle(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)_{\lambda},\varphi\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\int_{0}^{u^{\lambda}(x)}f(x,t)\,dt\,d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{u(x)}\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\,dt\,dx.

In
[
28
]
, the first two authors introduced another nonlinear pairing, defined by

(4.3)

\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{*}}:=-\frac{1}{2}\left[F(x,u^{+}(x))+F(x,u^{-}(x))\right]\,\sigma+\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u))\,.

We refer to
\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{*}}
as the

standard (external) nonlinear pairing
.
Its extension to a general

\lambda\not\equiv 1/2
is given next.

Definition 4.3

(External nonlinear
\lambda
-pairing)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)-(b4)
,
let
\boldsymbol{B}
be defined by (
3.1
), and let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
For
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,
the external nonlinear
\lambda
–pairing
between
\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u)
and
Du
is the measure

(4.4)

\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}:=-\left((1-\lambda)F(x,u^{-})+\lambda F(x,u^{+})\right)\,\sigma+\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u))\,.

Remark 4.4
.

Since the nonlinear pairings differ from the external ones only on the jump set
J_{u}
, they share many of the same fundamental properties.
In particular, for every
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,

(4.5)

\begin{split}\left(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus J_{u})&=-F(x,\widetilde{u})\,\sigma\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus J_{u})+\Div\boldsymbol{B}(x,u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus J_{u})\\
&=\left[\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus J_{u}).\end{split}

However, the external pairing is not suitable for addressing semicontinuity issues (see Section
8
), while the Coarea Formula seems to hold in general only for this specific choice (see Remark
5.2
). Hence we develop the theory for both pairings.

Remark 4.5
.

In the autonomous case
\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)=\boldsymbol{b}(t)
, the pairing measure is independent of
\lambda
, since

\sigma=0
, and

(4.6)

(\boldsymbol{b}(u),Du)_{\lambda}=[\boldsymbol{b}(u),Du]_{\lambda}=\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(u)).

The following result exhibits an explicit representation of
\lambda
–pairings on the jump set
J_{u}
of
u
, in terms of the weak normal traces of
\boldsymbol{B}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be given, and assume
that the jump set
J_{u}
is oriented so that
u^{i}=u^{+}
and
u^{e}=u^{-}
. Then

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&\phantom{bbbbbbb}=[(\beta^{i}(x,u^{+})-\beta^{i}(x,u^{\lambda}))+(\beta^{e}(x,u^{\lambda})-\beta^{e}(x,u^{-}))]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&\phantom{bbbbbbb}=\left[\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{u^{-}(x)}^{u^{+}(x)}\Tr_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},J_{u})(x)\,dt\right]|D^{j}u|\,.\end{split}

Here

\Tr_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},J_{u}):=\chi_{[u^{-},u^{\lambda}]}(t)\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},J_{u})+\chi_{[u^{\lambda},u^{+}]}(t)\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},J_{u})
.
Moreover,

(4.8)

\begin{split}&\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&\phantom{bbbbbbb}=\left[(1-\lambda)[\beta^{i}(x,u^{+})-\beta^{i}(x,u^{-})]+\lambda[\beta^{e}(x,u^{+})-\beta^{e}(x,u^{-})]\right]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&\phantom{bbbbbbb}=\left[\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{u^{-}(x)}^{u^{+}(x)}[(1-\lambda)\gamma^{i}(x,t)+\lambda\gamma^{e}(x,t)]\,dt\right]|D^{j}u|.\end{split}

Proof.

Recalling that
F(x,u^{\lambda}(x))\,\sigma=\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u^{\lambda}(x))
and using (
3.7
), we get

F(x,u^{\lambda}(x))\,\sigma\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}=\left[\beta^{i}(x,u^{\lambda}(x))-\beta^{e}(x,u^{\lambda}(x))\right]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}.

On the other hand, since
\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))
belongs to
{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
, by (
2.6
)
we have that

(4.9)

\begin{split}\Div\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}&=\left[\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u),J_{u})-\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u),J_{u})\right]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&=\left[\beta^{i}(x,u^{+}(x))-\beta^{e}(x,u^{-}(x))\right]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}.\end{split}

where in the last equality we have used (
3.8
), (
3.9
), and the orientation chosen for
J_{u}
.

The first equality in (
4.7
) then follows from the definition of
\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}
.
Moreover, by Remark
3.16
we infer that

\begin{split}&\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&\phantom{bbbbbbb}=[\beta^{e}(x,u^{\lambda}(x))-\beta^{e}(x,u^{-}(x))+\beta^{i}(x,u^{+}(x))-\beta^{i}(x,u^{\lambda}(x))]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&\phantom{bbbbbbb}=\left[\int_{u^{-}}^{u^{\lambda}}\gamma^{e}(x,t)\,dt+\int_{u^{\lambda}}^{u^{+}}\gamma^{i}(x,t)\,dt\right]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&\phantom{bbbbbbb}=\left[\int_{u^{-}}^{u^{+}}[\chi_{[u^{-},u^{\lambda}]}\gamma^{e}(x,t)+\chi_{[u^{\lambda},u^{+}]}\gamma^{i}(x,t)]\,dt\right]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&\phantom{bbbbbbb}=\left[\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{u^{-}}^{u^{+}}[\chi_{[u^{-},u^{\lambda}]}\gamma^{e}(x,t)+\chi_{[u^{\lambda},u^{+}]}\gamma^{i}(x,t)]\,dt\right]|D^{j}u|.\end{split}

Similarly, (
4.8
) follows from (
4.9
) and the fact that

\begin{split}[(1&-\lambda)F(x,u^{-})+\lambda F(x,u^{+})]\,\sigma\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&=\left[(1-\lambda)\left[\beta^{i}(x,u^{-})-\beta^{e}(x,u^{-})\right]+\lambda\left[\beta^{i}(x,u^{+})-\beta^{e}(x,u^{+})\right]\right]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}.\qed\end{split}

A key property of the pairings
\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}
and
\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}
is that they are absolutely continuous with respect to the measure
|Du|
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)-(b4)
and let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Then, for every

\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])

and for every Borel set
E\subset\Omega
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}|(E)\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}\,|Du|(E),

(4.11)

\displaystyle|\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}|(E)\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}\,|Du|(E)\,.

Proof.

To prove (
4.10
), we first establish the inequality for the standard nonlinear pairing (
4.3
), giving an argument independent of the proof in
[
28
]
.
We use a regularization method similar to that in
[
32
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Let

\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t):=\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast\boldsymbol{B}_{t}

and define
{\bf v}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u(x))
.
Since
\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}
is
C^{1}
in
(x,t)
,
the
BV
chain rule
(see
[
5
, Theorem 3.96]
) yields

(4.12)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi(x)&\cdot{\bf v}_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u(x))\,dx\\
&-\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\,\boldsymbol{b}_{\varepsilon}(x,\widetilde{u}(x))\,dD^{d}u\\
&-\int_{J_{u}}\varphi(x)\left(\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u^{+}(x))-\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u^{-}(x))\right)\cdot\nu_{u}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1},\end{split}

for every
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
.
By Lemma
3.8
,

{\bf v}_{\varepsilon}(x)\to{\bf v}(x)
for
\mathcal{L}^{N}
–a.e.
x\in\Omega
, and by (
3.2
) we have

\|{\bf v}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Hence, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot{\bf v}_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot{\bf v}(x)\,dx\,,

which means that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\Div[\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u(x))]\,\mathcal{L}^{N}=\Div{\bf v}(x)\,,\quad\hbox{in the weak${}^{*}$ sense of measures. }

We now claim that

(4.13)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}I_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\left[F(x,u^{+}(x))+F(x,u^{-}(x))\right]\,d\sigma(x),

where

I_{\varepsilon}:=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u(x))\,dx,

so that, passing to the limit in (
4.12
), we obtain

(4.14)

\begin{split}\langle\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{*}}\varphi\rangle&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\Big[\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\,\boldsymbol{b}_{\varepsilon}(x,\widetilde{u}(x))\,dD^{d}u\\
&+\int_{J_{u}}\varphi(x)\left(\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u^{+}(x))-\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u^{-}(x))\right)\cdot\nu_{u}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\Big].\end{split}

To prove (
4.13
),
assume for simplicity that
u\geq 0
, and choose
C>\|u\|_{\infty}
.
By Fubini’s Theorem, we have that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u(x))\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\int_{0}^{u(x)}\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\,dt\,dx\,.

Since
0\leq u(x)\leq C
, we can rewrite the inner integral as

\int_{0}^{u(x)}\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\,dt\,dx=\int_{0}^{C}\chi_{\{u>t\}}(x)\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\,dt\,.

Thus

\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u(x))\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\int_{0}^{C}\chi_{\{u>t\}}(x)\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\,dt\,dx\,.

Using the representation

\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,f(y,t)\,d\sigma(y)

we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u(x))\,dx&=\int_{\Omega}d\sigma(y)\,\int_{0}^{C}\,dt\,f(y,t)\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,\chi_{\{u>t\}}(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{C}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast(\varphi\chi_{\{u>t\}})(y)f(y,t)\,d\sigma(y)\,dt\,.\end{split}

It is well known that for a.e.
t\in[0,C]
the convolutions

\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast(\varphi\chi_{\{u>t\}})
converge pointwise to

\varphi\chi^{*}_{\{u>t\}}

\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-a.e., and hence
\sigma
-a.e., in
\Omega

(see
[
5
, Corollary 3.80]
).
By Lemma
2.4
,

(4.15)

\chi^{*}_{\{u>t\}}(y)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\chi_{\{u^{+}>t\}}(y)+\chi_{\{u^{-}>t\}}(y)\right)\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-a.e.\ y\in\Omega.

Passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and using (
4.15
) we obtain

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u(x))\,dx=\int_{0}^{C}\,dt\int_{\Omega}\varphi(y)\chi^{*}_{\{u>t\}}(y)\,f(y,t)\,d\sigma(y)\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(y)\,d\sigma(y)\int_{0}^{C}\chi^{*}_{\{u>t\}}(y)\,f(y,t)\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)(F(x,u^{+}(x))+F(x,u^{-}(x)))\,d\sigma(x),\end{split}

which completes the proof of (
4.13
)
and hence (
4.14
) holds true.

Finally,
let
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
with support contained in a compact set
K
,
let
r_{0}>0
be such that
K_{r_{0}}:=K+\overline{B_{r_{0}}}\subset\Omega

and pick
r\in(0,r_{0})
so that
|Du|(\partial K_{r})=0
.
Using the bound

\left|\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u^{+}(x))-\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u^{-}(x))\right|=\left|\int_{u^{-}(x)}^{u^{+}(x)}\boldsymbol{b}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\,dt\right|\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}\left|u^{+}(x)-u^{-}(x)\right|

we obtain the estimate

(4.16)

\begin{split}\Big|&\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\boldsymbol{b}_{\varepsilon}(x,\widetilde{u})\,dD^{d}u+\int_{J_{u}}\varphi(x)\left(\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u^{+})-\boldsymbol{B}_{\varepsilon}(x,u^{-})\right)\cdot\nu_{u}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\Big|\\
&\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}|D^{d}u|(K_{r})+\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}\int_{J_{u}\cap K_{r}}\left|u^{+}(x)-u^{-}(x)\right|d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\\
&=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}|Du|(K_{r}).\end{split}

Estimates (
4.14
) and (
4.16
) give

|\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{*}}|(E)\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}|Du|(E),\qquad\text{for every Borel set}\ E\Subset\Omega\,.

This is exactly estimate (
4.10
) for the standard pairing.

Using (
4.5
), we obtain

(4.17)

|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}|\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus J_{u})=|\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{*}}|\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus J_{u})\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}|Du|\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus J_{u}).

Next, by Proposition
4.6
, Proposition
3.14
, choosing the orientation of
J_{u}
such that
{u^{i}}=u^{+}

and
{u^{e}}=u^{-}
,
we deduce

(4.18)

\begin{split}\left|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}\right|\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}&\leq\left|\beta^{i}(x,u^{+}(x))-\beta^{i}(x,u^{\lambda}(x))\right|\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&+\left|\beta^{e}(x,u^{\lambda}(x))-\beta^{e}(x,u^{-}(x))\right|\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N})}(|u^{+}(x)-u^{\lambda}(x)|+|u^{\lambda}(x)-u^{-}(x)|)\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&=\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N})}(u^{+}(x)-u^{-}(x))\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&=\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N})}|Du|\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}.\end{split}

Combining (
4.17
) and (
4.18
),
we obtain estimate (
4.10
).

The proof of (
4.11
) is analogous, using (
4.17
) together with (
4.8
).
∎

Remark 4.8
.

From (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) it follows that there exist two Borel functions

\theta_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)
and
\theta^{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)\in L^{1}(\Omega;|Du|)

such that

\theta_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)\,|Du|=\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}\,,\quad\theta^{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)\,|Du|=\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}\qquad\text{$|Du|$--a.e.\ in $\Omega$.}

By Proposition
4.6
, we already know that

\displaystyle\theta_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;x)=\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{u^{-}(x)}^{u^{+}(x)}\Tr_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},J_{u})(x)\,dt,\qquad|D^{j}u|-\text{a.e. in}\ \Omega,

\displaystyle\theta^{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;x)=\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{u^{-}(x)}^{u^{+}(x)}[(1-\lambda)\gamma^{i}(x,t)+\lambda\gamma^{e}(x,t)]\,dt,\qquad|D^{j}u|-\text{a.e. in}\ \Omega.

Moreover, by (
4.5
),
the two pairing measures
\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}
and
(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)_{\lambda}

have the same diffuse part, independent of
\lambda
, that we simply denote by
(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)^{d}
:

(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)^{d}:=\theta_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)\,|D^{d}u|=\theta^{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)\,|D^{d}u|,\quad\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1]).

Hence, also the corresponding densities coincide
|D^{d}u|
-a.e. in
\Omega
and are independent of
\lambda
;
we denote by
\theta(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)\in L^{1}(\Omega;|D^{d}u|)
their common value:

(4.19)

\theta(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot):=\theta^{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)=\theta_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot),\qquad\text{$|D^{d}u|$--a.e.\ in $\Omega$.}

In
[
28
, Theorem 4.6]
, a representation is provided of the density of the standard nonlinear pairing:

(4.20)

\theta(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)\,|D^{a}u|=\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{*}}^{a}=\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,\widetilde{u})\cdot\nabla u\right){\mathcal{L}}^{N},

and hence it remains to represent the Cantor part
\theta(\boldsymbol{b},u;\cdot)\,|Du^{c}|
of the pairing.
This requires some additional effort, and it is proved in Theorem
6.3
below.

We conclude this section showing that, in fact, the external
\lambda
–pairings can be identified with a subclass of the nonlinear pairings.
We start by proving a simple selection lemma, that will be useful also in the next sections.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a Borel set, and let
\psi\colon A\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}

be a Carathéodory function, that is

(i)

for every
s\in[0,1]
, the map
x\mapsto\psi(x,s)
is Borel-measurable;

(ii)

for every
x\in A
, the map
s\mapsto\psi(x,s)
is continuous.

Moreover, assume that, for every
x\in A
, the section

\Gamma_{x}:=\{s\in[0,1]\colon\psi(x,s)=0\}

is not empty.

Then, there exists a Borel-measurable function
\lambda\colon A\to[0,1]
such that

\psi(x,\lambda(x))=0
for every
x\in A
.

Proof.

By
[
12
, Lemma 6.4.6]
, the function
\psi
is Borel-measurable in the
product space
A\times[0,1]
,
hence its zero-level set

\Gamma:=\{(x,s)\in A\times[0,1]\colon\psi(x,s)=0\}

is a Borel subset of
A\times[0,1]
.
For every
x\in A
,
by assumption the section
\Gamma_{x}
is not empty,
and, by the continuity of the function
s\mapsto\psi(x,s)
,
it is compact.

Hence, by
[
12
, Theorem 6.9.6]
,

\Gamma
contains the graph of some
Borel-measurable function
\lambda\colon A\to[0,1]
.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be given.
Then, for every

\Lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
there exists
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,
depending on
u
and
\Lambda
,
such that

(4.21)

\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\Lambda}}=(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)_{\lambda}.

Conversely, for every

\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
, there exists

\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,
depending on
u
and
\lambda
,
such that

(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)_{\lambda}=\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\widetilde{\Lambda}}}+R(u)\,\sigma\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\,,

where

R(u)(x)=0
if
F(x,u^{\lambda(x)})
lies between
F(x,u^{-}(x))
and
F(x,u^{+}(x))

and, in general,

|R(u)|\leq u^{+}-u^{-}\qquad\text{$\sigma$-a.e.\ on $J_{u}$}\,.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,
let
A:=\Omega\setminus(S_{u}\setminus J_{u})

and let

\psi\colon A\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by

\psi(x,s):=F(x,(1-s)u^{-}(x)+su^{+}(x))-(1-\Lambda(x))F(x,u^{-}(x))-\Lambda(x)F(x,u^{+}(x))\,.

The function
\psi
satisfies the assumptions (i)–(ii) of Lemma
4.9
.
Moreover, by (ii) and the intermediate value theorem,
for every
x\in A
the section
\Gamma_{x}
is a non-empty subset of
[0,1]
.
Hence, by Lemma
4.9
, there exists

\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(A;[0,1])
,
that can be extended to a function
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,
so that

\Lambda(x)F(x,u^{+}(x))+(1-\Lambda(x))F(x,u^{-}(x))=F(x,u^{\lambda(x)})\qquad\forall x\in\Omega^{\prime},

and (
4.21
) follows.

To prove the second part of the theorem,
it is enough to define

\Lambda(x):=\begin{cases}\frac{F(x,u^{\lambda}(x))-F(x,u^{-}(x))}{F(x,u^{+}(x))-F(x,u^{-}(x))}&\text{if}\ F(x,u^{-}(x))\neq F(x,u^{+}(x)),\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and
\widetilde{\Lambda}(x):=\Lambda(x)\wedge 1\vee 0
.
(The choice
\Lambda(x)=0
when
F(x,u^{-}(x))=F(x,u^{+}(x))
is arbitrary and can be
replaced by any other Borel-measurable choice in
[0,1]
, since its value in this region
does not affect the pairing measure.)
Clearly, if
F(x,u^{\lambda}(x))
lies between
F(x,u^{-}(x))
and
F(x,u^{+}(x))
, then
\Lambda(x)\in[0,1]
and
R(u)(x)=0
.
Otherwise, it is enough to observe that
F
is
1
-Lipschitz with respect to
u
, so that

|R(u)(x)|\leq u^{+}(x)-u^{-}(x)
.
Specifically, consider for example a point
x\in J_{u}
where

F(x,u^{-}(x))<F(x,u^{+}(x))<F(x,{u}^{\lambda}(x))
, so that

\Lambda(x)>1
and
\widetilde{\Lambda}(x)=1
. Then

|R(u)(x)|=|F(x,{u}^{\lambda}(x))-F(x,u^{+}(x))|\leq|u^{\lambda}(x)-u^{+}(x)|\leq u^{+}(x)-u^{-}(x)\,.

Similarly, we can handle the other cases.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

,

In particular, this is the case

with

in

A nonlinear pairing for separated fields

): for
v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}

it holds that

\displaystyle\left(v\boldsymbol{A},Du\right)_{{\lambda}}^{d}=v^{\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{A},Du\right)_{{\lambda}}^{d}=v^{*}\left[\boldsymbol{A},Du\right]_{{*}}^{d},

\displaystyle\left(v\boldsymbol{A},Du\right)_{{\lambda}}^{j}=[(1-\lambda)\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{A},J_{u}){v^{i}}+\lambda\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{A},J_{u}){v^{e}}]\,(u^{+}-u^{-})\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\,.

For

\displaystyle\left(g(u)\boldsymbol{A},Du\right)_{{\lambda}}^{d}=g(u)^{*}\left[\boldsymbol{A},Du\right]_{{*}}^{d},

\displaystyle\left(g(u)\boldsymbol{A},Du\right)_{{\lambda}}^{j}=[(1-\lambda)\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{A},J_{u})g(u)^{i}+\lambda\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{A},J_{u})g(u)^{e}]\,(u^{+}-u^{-})\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\,.

5.
Coarea formula and slicing

This section is devoted to the proof of the coarea formula
for the external nonlinear
\lambda
-pairing
\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}
,
and a related slicing result for the density
\theta^{\lambda}
of its diffuse part.

We recall the notation
\boldsymbol{b}_{t}(x):=\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)
, so that

(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},Du)_{\lambda}=[\boldsymbol{b}_{t},Du]_{\lambda}
is the linear
\lambda
–pairing between the field

\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\Omega)
and the function
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
.

Theorem 5.1

(Coarea formula)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}
satisfy assumptions
(b1)-(b4)
. For
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and

\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,
it holds that

(5.1)

\left\langle\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle\left[\boldsymbol{b}_{t},D\chi_{\{u>t\}}\right]_{{\lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\,dt,\qquad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega).

Proof.

By considering the level sets
\{u>t\}
separately for

t\geq 0
and
t\leq 0
,
we may assume without loss of generality that
u\geq 0
.
Fixed
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, we know that the distributional divergence of
\varphi\boldsymbol{b}_{t}
is a finite Radon measure in
\Omega
, and

\Div(\varphi\boldsymbol{b}_{t})=\varphi f(\cdot,t)\sigma+\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathcal{L}^{N}.

Moreover
\chi_{\{u>t\}}\in BV(\Omega)
for
{\mathcal{L}}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
hence for every
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
its precise representative
\chi_{\{u>t\}}^{\lambda}
belongs to

L^{1}(\Omega;|\Div(\varphi\boldsymbol{b}_{t})|)
, and

\left\langle\left[\boldsymbol{b}_{t},D\chi_{\{u>t\}}\right]_{{\lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}^{\lambda}\,d\Div(\varphi\boldsymbol{b}_{t}).

By Fubini’s Theorem and recalling that, by Lemma
2.4
, for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(5.2)

\chi^{\lambda}_{\{u>t\}}=\lambda\chi_{\{u^{+}>t\}}+(1-\lambda)\chi_{\{u^{-}>t\}}\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\text{a.e.\ in}\ \Omega,

we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}

\displaystyle\left\langle\left[\boldsymbol{b}_{t},D\chi_{\{u>t\}}\right]_{{\lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\,dt=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}^{\lambda}\,d\Div(\varphi\boldsymbol{b}_{t})\right)\,dt

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-\lambda)\chi_{\{u^{-}>t\}}\varphi f(x,t)\,dt\,d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-\lambda)\chi_{\{u^{-}>t\}}\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dt\,dx\,

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda\chi_{\{u^{+}>t\}}\,\varphi f(x,t)\,dt\,\,d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda\chi_{\{u^{+}>t\}}\,\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dt\,dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(1-\lambda)\varphi F(x,u^{-})+\lambda\varphi F(x,u^{+})\,d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,d\left[\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}.\qed

Remark 5.2
.

By inspection of the proof, the validity of the coarea formula for the external pairing
depends on the linearity of
\chi^{\lambda}_{\{u>t\}}
with respect to
\lambda

(see (
5.2
)).
It is then apparent that the coarea formula does not in general hold for the nonlinear pairing,
due to its intrinsic nonlinearity with respect to
\lambda
on the jump set.

Remark 5.3
.

Localizing (
5.1
) in
\Omega\setminus J_{u}
, and recalling that

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}^{d}
and
\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}^{d}
coincide and do not depend on
\lambda
in
\Omega\setminus J_{u}
,
we obtain that

(5.3)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,&d\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}^{d}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,d\left[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{\lambda}}^{d}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\Omega\setminus J_{u}}\varphi\,d\left[\boldsymbol{b}_{t},D\chi_{\{u>t\}}\right]_{{\lambda}}\right)\,dt\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\Omega\setminus J_{u}}\chi_{\{\widetilde{u}>t\}}\,\varphi\,f(x,t)\,d\sigma\,dt-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\Omega\setminus J_{u}}\chi_{\{\widetilde{u}>t\}}\,\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

In order to provide a representation of the Cantor part of the
\lambda
–pairings,
we need some preliminary results.
We start by showing that the standard external pairing can be approximated by external pairings
with smooth vector fields.

Theorem 5.4

(Approximation by
C^{\infty}
fields)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)-(b4)
,
and let
M>0
be a given constant.
Then there exists a sequence of vector fields
\boldsymbol{b}^{k}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

satisfying, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

(c1)

\boldsymbol{b}^{k}\in C_{b}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
with
\|\boldsymbol{b}^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}
.

(c2)

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the map
x\mapsto\boldsymbol{b}^{k}(x,t)

belongs to
C^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and

\int_{\Omega}|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}(x)|\,dx\leq|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{t}|(\Omega)+\frac{1}{k}\,,\qquad\forall t\in[-M,M].

(c3)

The least upper bound

\sigma^{k}_{M}:=\bigvee_{t\in[-M,M]}|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}|\mathcal{L}^{N}

is a Radon measure,
with
\sigma^{k}_{M}(\Omega)\leq\sigma(\Omega)+\frac{1}{k}
.

(c4)

For every
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
with
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq M
, it holds that

\left[\boldsymbol{b}^{k}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{*}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\left[\boldsymbol{b}^{\phantom{k}}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{*}},

locally in the weak
∗
sense of measures in
\Omega
.

Remark 5.5
.

It is clear that a vector field satisfying (c1)–(c2)
also satisfies assumptions (b1)–(b3) given in Section
3
.
Property (c3) is a localized version of (b4),
with respect to the
t
variable.

Proof.

The main arguments of the proof are taken
from
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
26
, Proposition 4.11]
.
In our setting some care is needed in order to handle
the
t
dependence of the vector field.

We briefly recall the construction given in
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
which, in turn, is similar to the analogous
construction for
BV
functions
(see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 3.9]
).

Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 1
,
since
\sigma
is a finite Radon measure,
there exists
R>0
such that

\sigma(\Omega\setminus D_{i})<\frac{1}{k}\,,\qquad i=0,1,2,\ldots,

where

D_{i}:=\left\{x\in\Omega\colon\dist(x,\partial\Omega)>\frac{1}{R+i}\right\}\cap B_{R+i}(0)\,.

Consider the sets
\Omega_{1}:=D_{2}
and

\Omega_{i}:=D_{i+1}\setminus\overline{D}_{i-1}
,

i\geq 2
, and
let
(\varphi_{i})
be a partition of unity subordinate
to the covering
\{\Omega_{i}\}
.
Let
(\rho_{\varepsilon})
denote the standard family of mollifiers.
For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose
\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}>0

such that

\spt\rho_{\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}}\ast(\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\,\varphi_{i})\subset D_{i+2}\setminus\overline{D}_{i-2}\,,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\qquad(D_{-1}:=\emptyset).

Let us consider the map

(0,\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}]\times[-M,M]\ni(\varepsilon,t)\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast(\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\,\varphi_{i})-\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\,\varphi_{i}|\,dx,

extended to
0
for
\varepsilon=0
.
Since
\boldsymbol{b}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
by the basic properties of convolution it is continuous
in
[0,\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}]\times[-M,M]
.
Hence, there exists
\varepsilon_{i}\in(0,\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}]

such that

(5.4)

\int_{\Omega}\left|\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\varphi_{i})-\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\varphi_{i}\right|\,dx\leq\frac{1}{4k\,2^{i}}\,,\qquad\forall t\in[-M,M].

Using a similar argument, by possibly choosing a smaller
value of
\varepsilon_{i}
, it also holds that

(5.5)

\int_{\Omega}\left|\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{i})-\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{i}\right|\,dx\leq\frac{1}{4k\,2^{i}}\,,\qquad\forall t\in[-M,M].

Finally, let us define

\boldsymbol{b}^{k}(x,t):=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\,\varphi_{i})\,.

By repeating verbatim the same arguments as in the proof
of
[
16
, Theorem 1.2, p. 93]
,
it follows that

(5.6)

\int_{\Omega}|\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}-\boldsymbol{b}_{t}|\,dx<\frac{1}{4k}\,,\quad\int_{\Omega}|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}|\,dx\leq|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{t}|(\Omega)+\frac{1}{k}\qquad\forall t\in[-M,M],

so that properties (c1) and (c2) hold.
By the definition of
\sigma
we have that

|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{t}|(\Omega)\leq\sigma(\Omega)
,
so that also (c3) follows.

The first estimate in (
5.6
),
together with
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
,
implies that

|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{t}|(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}|\,dx,\qquad\forall t\in[-M,M].

Taking into account the second estimate in (
5.6
),
we thus conclude that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}|\,dx=|\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{t}|(\Omega),\qquad\forall t\in[-M,M].

Let us prove (c4).
By repeating the same argument as in the proof of

[
26
, Proposition 4.11]
,
for every
v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
, and for every
t\in[-M,M]
,
it holds that

(5.7)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}v\,\varphi\,\Div\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}v^{*}\,\varphi\,d\Div\boldsymbol{b}_{t}=\int_{\Omega}v^{*}\,\varphi f(x,t)\,d\sigma\qquad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}(\Omega)

(see
[
26
]
, formula (4.8)).

Let us fix
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
with
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq M
, and
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
.
To simplify the notation, we assume without loss of generality
that
u\geq 0
.
By the representation formula (
4.2
) and Fubini’s Theorem, we have that

\begin{split}&\left\langle\left[\boldsymbol{b}^{k}(\cdot,u),Du\right]_{{*}},\varphi\right\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}\varphi\int_{0}^{u(x)}\,\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}\,dt\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{u(x)}\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dt\,dx\\
&\phantom{xx}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}\,\varphi\,\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}\,\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\,dt\\
&\phantom{xx}=:-I^{k}_{1}-I^{k}_{2}.\end{split}

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\chi_{\{u>t\}}\in BV(\Omega)
, by (
5.7
) with
v=\chi_{\{u>t\}}
we
deduce that, as
k\to\infty
,

\zeta^{k}(t):=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}\,\varphi\,\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}^{*}\,\varphi f(x,t)\,d\sigma\,.

Let
K\Subset\Omega
denote the support of
\varphi

and let
a:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}
.
Since, by (c2) and (c3),

|\zeta^{k}(t)|\leq\chi_{[0,a]}(t)\,\|\varphi\|_{\infty}(\sigma(K)+1)\,,

by the Dominated Convergence Theorem and by Lemma
2.4
we deduce that

(5.8)

\begin{split}&\lim_{k\to\infty}I^{k}_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}^{*}\,\varphi f(x,t)\,d\sigma=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\chi_{\{u^{+}>t\}}+\chi_{\{u^{-}>t\}}}{2}\,\varphi f(x,t)\,d\sigma\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{0}^{u^{+}}f(x,t)\,dt+\int_{0}^{u^{-}}f(x,t)\,dt\right]\,d\sigma=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\frac{F(x,u^{-})+F(x,u^{+})}{2}\,d\sigma\,.\end{split}

Let us compute the limit of
I^{k}_{2}
.
Since, for every
t\in[-M,M]
,
\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}\to\boldsymbol{b}_{t}
in
L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
it holds that

\psi^{k}(t):=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}\,\boldsymbol{b}^{k}_{t}(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}\,\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\qquad(k\to+\infty).

Since
\|\boldsymbol{b}^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

|\psi^{k}(t)|\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\infty}\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|\,dx\,,\qquad\forall t\geq 0,\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N},

and hence, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem,

(5.9)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}I^{k}_{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u>t\}}\,\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\,dt=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{u}\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dt\,dx\,.

The conclusion now follows from (
5.8
) and (
5.9
).
∎

6.
Integral representation of the pairing measure

We now employ the results of the previous section to obtain the representation of the diffuse

Recall that we already know the density of the absolutely continuous part of the

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Then there exists a Borel set
B\subset\Omega
with the following properties:

(i)

|D^{d}u|(\Omega\setminus B)=0
;

(ii)

E_{t}:=\{u>t\}

\Omega

x\in\partial^{*}E_{\widetilde{u}(x)}

|D^{d}u|
-a.e.
x\in\Omega

(iii)

for every

\theta(\boldsymbol{b},u;x)=\theta(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},u;x)=\theta(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},\chi_{E_{t}};x)=\Tr^{*}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},\partial^{*}E_{t})(x)

(recall definition (
2.7

Proof.

The line of proof is similar to the one of
[
22
, Theorem 3.12]
.
For the sake of completeness we report here some details.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{R}
be the set such that
for every

the following hold:

(a)

E_{t}:=\{u>t\}
is a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega

(b)

(c)

\displaystyle\theta(\boldsymbol{b},u;x)=\theta(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},u;x)=\theta(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},\chi_{E_{t}};x)=\Tr^{*}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},\partial^{*}E_{t})(x)\quad\text{for ${\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}$-a.e.}\ x\in\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap(\Omega\setminus J_{u}).

By the coarea formula in
BV
,
by Theorem
5.6
,
Corollary 3.7 and Theorem 3.12 in
[
22
]
(with
\boldsymbol{A}=\boldsymbol{b}_{t}
),
we have that
{\mathcal{L}}^{1}(Z)=0
.

Since
{\mathcal{L}}^{1}(Z)=0

[

, we have that

\nabla u=0\quad\text{${\mathcal{L}}^{N}$-a.e.\ in}\ u^{-1}(Z)\,\text{ and }\,|D^{c}u|(\widetilde{u}^{-1}(Z))=0.

As a consequence,
for
|D^{d}u|
-a.e.

we have that

\widetilde{u}(x)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus Z
,
i.e.
|D^{d}u|(\widetilde{u}^{-1}(Z))=0
.

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus Z
, let

be a set such that
the following hold:

(d)

\widetilde{u}(x)=t
for every
x\in(\Omega\setminus S_{u})\cap(\partial^{*}E_{t}\setminus N_{t})
;

(e)

equality in (c) holds for every

By (b) and (c), the set

can be chosen of zero

{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}

We claim that

(6.1)

|D^{d}u|(\Omega\setminus B)=0,\qquad\text{where}\ B:=\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus Z}(\partial^{*}E_{t}\setminus N_{t})\,.

Specifically,
since the sets

[

),
we have that

hence, by the coarea formula for

\displaystyle|D^{d}u|(\Omega\setminus B)=|Du|((\Omega\setminus S_{u})\setminus B)

(hence, by (

-a.e.

7.
The Gauss–Green formula

Let
E\Subset\Omega
be a set of finite perimeter, and denote by
\partial^{*}E
its reduced boundary.
We will assume that the generalized normal vector on
\partial^{*}E
coincides

{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}
-a.e. on
\partial^{*}E
with the measure–theoretic
inward unit normal vector
\widetilde{\nu}_{E}
to
E
.

The following result generalizes the one proved in
[
26
, Theorem 6.3]
for linear pairings.

Theorem 7.1

(Gauss–Green formula)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)-(b4)
, let
\boldsymbol{B}
be defined by (
3.1
), and let
F
be as in (
3.3
). Given a bounded set
E
with finite perimeter and compactly contained in
\Omega
,
let
\beta^{i}(\cdot,t)
and
\beta^{e}(\cdot,t)
denote the normal traces of the field
\boldsymbol{B}(\cdot,t)

on
\partial^{*}E
defined in (
3.6
).
Let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and

\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
.
Then the following Gauss–Green formulas hold:

(7.1)

\int_{E^{1}}\big(\lambda F(x,u^{+})+(1-\lambda)F(x,u^{-})\big)\,d\sigma+\int_{E^{1}}[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du]_{\lambda}=-\int_{\partial^{*}E}\beta^{i}(x,u^{i})\ d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

(7.2)

\int_{E^{1}\cup\partial^{*}E}\big(\lambda F(x,u^{+})+(1-\lambda)F(x,u^{-})\big)\,d\sigma+\int_{E^{1}\cup\partial^{*}E}[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du]_{\lambda}=-\int_{\partial^{*}E}\beta^{e}(x,u^{e})\ d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

(7.3)

\int_{E^{1}}F(x,u^{\lambda})\,d\sigma+\int_{E^{1}}(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)_{\lambda}=-\int_{\partial^{*}E}\beta^{i}(x,u^{i})\ d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

(7.4)

\int_{E^{1}\cup\partial^{*}E}F(x,u^{\lambda})\,d\sigma+\int_{E^{1}\cup\partial^{*}E}(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)_{\lambda}=-\int_{\partial^{*}E}\beta^{e}(x,u^{e})\ d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

where
E^{1}
is the measure theoretic interior of
E
.

The first two formulas concern the external pairings, whereas the following two concern the nonlinear pairings.
Moreover, formulas (
7.1
) and (
7.3
) are related to the measure-theoretic interior
E^{1}
of the set
E
,
whereas formulas (
7.2
) and (
7.4
) are related to its measure-theoretic closure

.

Proof.

We shall prove formulas (
7.1
) and (
7.2
)
following the lines of the proof of
[
28
, Theorem 6.1]
, where
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
.

Since
E
is compactly contained in
\Omega
, we may assume without loss of generality
that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
By Theorem
4.7
, the composite function
{\bf v}(x):=\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))

belongs to
{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Since
E
is a bounded set of finite perimeter, the characteristic function

\chi_{E}
is a compactly supported
BV
function, so that

\Div(\chi_{E}{\bf v})(\mathbb{R}^{N})=0

(see
[
24
, Lemma 3.1]
).

Applying the Leibniz formula for the divergence

\Div(w\boldsymbol{A})=w^{\lambda}\Div\boldsymbol{A}+(\boldsymbol{A},Dw)_{\lambda}.

to
w=\chi_{E}\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\boldsymbol{A}={\bf v}\in{\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
it follows that

0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Div(\chi_{E}{\bf v})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}({\bf v},D\chi_{E})_{\lambda}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi_{E}^{\lambda}\,d\Div{\bf v}.

From Proposition
3.17
and the representation formula (
6.2
) we get

(7.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi_{E}^{\lambda}\,d\Div{\bf v}&=-({\bf v},D\chi_{E})_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\\
&=-\int_{\partial^{*}E}\big((1-\lambda)\beta^{i}(x,u^{i})+\lambda\beta^{e}(x,u^{e})\big)\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.\end{split}

On the other hand, we notice that

\chi_{E}^{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)\chi_{E^{1}}+\lambda\chi_{E^{1}\cup\partial^{*}E}=\chi_{E^{1}}+\lambda\chi_{\partial^{*}E},

since
\chi_{E}^{-}=\chi_{E^{1}}
and
\chi_{E}^{+}=\chi_{E^{1}\cup\partial^{*}E}
.
Using again
Proposition
3.17

and
(
4.4
)
we obtain

(7.6)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi_{E}^{\lambda}\,d\Div{\bf v}={}&\int_{E^{1}}\,d\Div{\bf v}+\lambda\int_{\partial^{*}E}\,d\Div{\bf v}\\
={}&\int_{E^{1}}\,d\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u))+\lambda\int_{\partial^{*}E}\,[\beta^{i}(x,u^{i})-\beta^{e}(x,u^{e})]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\\
={}&\int_{E^{1}}[(1-\lambda)F(x,u^{-})+\lambda F(x,u^{+})]\,d\sigma+\int_{E^{1}}\,d[\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du]_{\lambda}\\
{}&+\lambda\int_{\partial^{*}E}\,[\beta^{i}(x,u^{i})-\beta^{e}(x,u^{e})]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\,.\end{split}

Formula (
7.1
) now follows from (
7.5
) and (
7.6
).
The proof of (
7.2
) is entirely similar.

In order to prove (
7.3
) and (
7.4
), it is sufficient to observe that by (
4.1
)
we obtain that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi_{E}^{\lambda}\,d\Div{\bf v}={}&\int_{E^{1}}\,d\Div{\bf v}+\lambda\int_{\partial^{*}E}\,d\Div{\bf v}\\
={}&\int_{E^{1}}\,d\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u))+\lambda\int_{\partial^{*}E}\,[\beta^{i}(x,u^{i})-\beta^{e}(x,u^{e})]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\\
={}&\int_{E^{1}}F(x,u^{\lambda})\,d\sigma+\int_{E^{1}}\,d(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)_{\lambda}\\
{}&+\lambda\int_{\partial^{*}E}\,[\beta^{e}(x,u^{e})-\beta^{i}(x,u^{i})]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\,.\qed\end{split}

8.
Semicontinuity results

In this section we consider the pairing
as a function in
BV
:

BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\ni u\mapsto\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega),

and we characterize the selections
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
making this function lower semicontinuous
along bounded sequences converging in
L^{1}
with controlled pointwise values (see Definition
8.2
).

In order to find the correct selections giving the lower semicontinuity of this function, we consider

D:=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon a\leq b\}
, and we define

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a,b):=\max_{t\in[a,b]}F(x,t)\qquad(a,b)\in D.

Recalling the definition (
3.3
) of
F
,
we immediately deduce that
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,0,0)=F(x,0)=0
.
Moreover, using the Lipschitz property (
3.4
),
the following elementary facts can be easily checked.

(i)

For every
x\in\Omega
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
, the map
a\mapsto\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a,b)
is monotone non-increasing in
(-\infty,b]
.
More precisely,
0\leq\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a,b)-\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a^{\prime},b)\leq a^{\prime}-a
for every
a\leq a^{\prime}\leq b
.

(ii)

For every
x\in\Omega
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the map
b\mapsto\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a,b)
is monotone non-decreasing in
[a,+\infty)
.
More precisely,
0\leq\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a,b^{\prime})-\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a,b)\leq b^{\prime}-b
for every
a\leq b\leq b^{\prime}
.

(iii)

For every
x\in\Omega
, the map
(a,b)\mapsto\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a,b)
is Lipschitz continuous in
D
.
More precisely,
|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})-\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,a,b)|\leq|a^{\prime}-a|+|b^{\prime}-b|

for every
(a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in D
.

For a fixed
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
, we consider the measure

(8.1)

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}:=-\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x))\,\sigma+\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))),

where the subscript
L
in the notation stands for
Lower
semicontinuous.
Since
F(x,\cdot)
is a Lipschitz function for
\sigma
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
,
for every
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
the set

\mathcal{L}_{M}[u]:=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])\colon\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x))=F(x,u^{\lambda}(x))\ \text{for}\ \sigma\text{-a.e.}\ x\in\Omega\}

is not empty.
Specifically, let us consider the Borel set
A:=\Omega\setminus(S_{u}\setminus J_{u})
and
let us define the function

g(x):=\max_{s\in[0,1]}F(x,(1-s)u^{-}(x)+s\,u^{+}(x)),\qquad x\in A.

Since
F
is continuous with respect to the second variable, we have that

g(x)=\sup_{q\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]}F(x,(1-q)u^{-}(x)+q\,u^{+}(x)),\qquad x\in A,

is a Borel-measurable function,
being the supremum of a countable family of Borel-measurable functions.
Hence, the function

\psi(x,s):=g(x)-F(x,(1-s)u^{-}(x)+s\,u^{+}(x)),\qquad(x,s)\in A\times[0,1],

satisfies all the assumptions of Lemma
4.9
,
so that there exists
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(A;[0,1])
,
that can be extended to a function
\widehat{\lambda}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
, such that

\psi(x,\widehat{\lambda}(x))=0
for every
x\in A
, and hence for

\sigma
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
.

By the definition of
\psi
, this is equivalent to say that

(8.2)

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x))=F(x,\widehat{u}(x)),\qquad\widehat{u}:=u^{\widehat{\lambda}}\,,\qquad\text{for $\sigma$-a.e.}\ x\in\Omega.

In particular,
\widehat{u}
is a Borel measurable function. Moreover,

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}=\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}},\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{M}[u],

so that Proposition
4.6
and Theorem
4.7
are in force.

We remark that, for every
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,

(8.3)

\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\leq\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle,\qquad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega),\ \varphi\geq 0.

Remark 8.1
.

We can give an explicit representation of
\mathcal{L}_{M}[u]
for separable fields, that is when
there exists a decomposition
\Omega=\Omega^{+}\cup\Omega^{-}
, with
\Omega^{+}\cap\Omega^{-}=\emptyset
,
such that (
3.5
) holds.
In this case,

F(x,\cdot)
is a nondecreasing function on
\mathbb{R}
for
\sigma
–a.e.
x\in\Omega^{+}
, and it is
a nonincreasing function on
\mathbb{R}
for
\sigma
–a.e.
x\in\Omega^{-}
,
so that

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{M}[u]&=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])\colon\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x))=F(x,u^{\lambda}(x))\ \text{for $\sigma$-a.e. $x$\ in}\ \Omega\}\\
&=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])\colon\lambda=0\ \text{$\sigma$-a.e.\ in }\ \Omega^{+}\cap J_{u},\ \lambda=1\ \text{$\sigma$-a.e.\ in}\ \Omega^{-}\cap J_{u}\}.\end{split}

In particular, in the autonomous case
\boldsymbol{b}(x,t)\equiv\boldsymbol{A}(x)
we recover the results proved in
[
26
]
.

We introduce the sequences along which we will compute our functionals. We add to the usual
L^{1}
-convergence a very mild version of convergence of the precise representatives of
BV
functions,
and an equi-boundedness condition in
L^{1}(\Omega;\sigma)
.

Definition 8.2
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n}
be a sequence in
BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}

and let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
.
We say that
(u_{n})\in\mathcal{A}(u)
if the following properties hold.

(i)

u_{n}\to u
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

The Pointwise Representative property (PR-property for short) holds, meaning that

(8.4)

u^{-}(x)\leq\liminf_{n}u_{n}^{-}(x)\leq\limsup_{n}u_{n}^{+}(x)\leq u^{+}(x)\,,\ \ \ {\text{$\sigma$-a.e. in}}\ \Omega.

(iii)

There exists
g\in L^{1}(\Omega;\sigma)
such that

|u_{n}^{\pm}|\leq g
,
\sigma
-a.e. in
\Omega
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 8.3
.

We recall that by
[
39
, Theorem 3.2, and Corollary 3.3]
,
if a sequence
u_{n}
strictly converges in
BV
to
u
,
then it admits a subsequence satisfying the PR-property
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-a.e. in
\Omega
, and hence
\sigma
-a.e. in
\Omega
.

Remark 8.4
.

If
\sigma\ll\mathcal{L}^{N}
,
then any sequence
(u_{n})
converging to
u
in
L^{1}(\Omega)

admits a subsequence satisfying (ii) and (iii).
Hence, if
(u_{n})
and
u
belong to
BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
and
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
,
then there exists a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})\in\mathcal{A}(u)
.

Remark 8.5
.

Condition (iii) is needed to apply dominated convergence in
L^{1}(\Omega;\sigma)

when passing to the limit in the pairing
along sequences.

The following essential tool, which guarantees the existence of approximating sequences with well-behaved pointwise values, is proved in
[
23
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem 8.6

(Comi–Leonardi
\lambda
-approximation)
.

Fixed
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
, for every

u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
there exists a sequence
(u_{n}^{(\lambda)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap BV(\Omega)
such that the following hold:

(i)

u_{n}^{(\lambda)}\to u
in
BV(\Omega)
-strict as
n\to+\infty
;

(ii)

u_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x)\to u^{\lambda}(x)
for
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–a.e.
x\in\Omega
as
n\to+\infty
;

(iii)

\|u_{n}^{(\lambda)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.

In particular,
(u_{n}^{(\lambda)})\in\mathcal{A}(u)
.

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}
satisfy assumptions
(b1)
-
(b4)
.
Given
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
, and
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
, let
(u_{n}^{(\lambda)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence given by Theorem
8.6
. Then for every
\Lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])

(8.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u_{n}^{(\lambda)}),Du_{n}^{(\lambda)}\right)_{{\Lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle=\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle,\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega).

Proof.

For every
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
we have

\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u_{n}^{(\lambda)}),Du_{n}^{(\lambda)}\right)_{{\Lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u_{n}^{(\lambda)})\nabla\varphi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u_{n}^{(\lambda)})\varphi\,d\sigma,

with right–hand side not depending on
\Lambda
and with both terms that pass to the limit by (generalized) dominated convergence, due to (
3.2
), (
3.4
), and Theorem
8.6
.
∎

We first prove that, fixed
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
, then the
\lambda
-pairings for
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{M}[u]
are lower semicontinuous along the sequences in
\mathcal{A}(u)
.

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}
satisfy assumptions
(b1)
-
(b4)
.
Given
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
, let
\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}
be the pairing defined in (
8.1
).
Then, for every
(u_{n})\in\mathcal{A}(u)
,
it holds that

(8.6)

\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u_{n}),Du_{n})\right)_{L}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\ \varphi\geq 0.

Proof.

Since
|\boldsymbol{B}(x,u_{n}(x)|\leq C|u_{n}(x)|
(see (
3.2
)),
by the (generalized) Dominated Convergence Theorem we have that

(8.7)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u_{n}(x))\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx.

Let
x\in\Omega
be such that (
8.4
) holds true. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

u^{-}(x)-\varepsilon\leq u_{n}^{-}(x)\leq u_{n}^{+}(x)\leq u^{+}(x)+\varepsilon,\qquad\quad\forall n\geq n_{\varepsilon}.

Using the monotonicity properties and the 1-lipschitzianity of
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
, we have

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u_{n}^{-}(x),u_{n}^{+}(x))\leq\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x)-\varepsilon,u^{+}(x)+\varepsilon)\leq\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x))+2\varepsilon,\quad\forall n\geq n_{\varepsilon},

which implies that

(8.8)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u_{n}^{-}(x),u_{n}^{+}(x))\leq\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x)),\qquad\text{for $\sigma$-a.e.}\ x\in\Omega.

Moreover, by Definition
8.2
(iii), we also infer that

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u_{n}^{-}(x),u_{n}^{+}(x))\leq|u_{n}^{+}(x)|+|u_{n}^{-}(x)|\leq 2g(x)\in L^{1}(\Omega;\sigma).

and hence, by Fatou’s Lemma and (
8.8
), we get

(8.9)

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u_{n}^{-}(x),u_{n}^{+}(x))\,\varphi(x)\,d\sigma\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u_{n}^{-}(x),u_{n}^{+}(x))\right]\varphi(x)\,d\sigma\leq\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x))\varphi(x)\,d\sigma.\end{split}

Finally,
from (
8.7
) and (
8.9
)
we conclude that

\begin{split}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}&\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u_{n}),Du_{n})\right)_{L}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\\
&=-\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u_{n}(x))\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u_{n}^{-}(x),u_{n}^{+}(x))\,\varphi(x)\,d\sigma\right)\\
&\geq-\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x))\,\varphi(x)\,d\sigma\\
&=\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)\right)_{L}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\end{split}

i.e., (
8.6
) holds true.
∎

Taking into account Remark
8.3
the following result holds.

Corollary 8.9
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)
-
(b4)
.
Then,
for every
u_{n},u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
such that
(u_{n})
strictly converges to
u
in
BV
,
and satisfies property (iii) in Definition
8.2
,
the lower semicontinuity property (
8.6
) holds.

We now prove the converse of Theorem
8.8
.

Theorem 8.10
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)
-
(b4)
.
Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
, and
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
.
Assume that,
for every sequence
(u_{n})\in\mathcal{A}(u)
,
it holds that

(8.10)

\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du)\right)_{{\lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u_{n}),Du_{n}\right)_{{\lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\ \varphi\geq 0.

Then we have that
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{M}[u]
, i.e.

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}=\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
satisfy (
8.10
).
Let
\widehat{u}
be the function defined in (
8.2
),
and let
u_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{A}(\widehat{u})
be the sequence considered in Theorem
8.6
.
By Lemma
8.7
we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u_{n}^{(\lambda)}),Du_{n}^{(\lambda)}\right)_{{\lambda}}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle=\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle,\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega).

Hence (
8.10
) gives

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}\leq\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L},

and the conclusion now follows recalling (
8.3
).
∎

Remark 8.11
.

In the autonomous case (
4.6
),
considered in Remark
6.5
, it is known that, in fact,
for every
u_{n},u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
such that

(u_{n})
converges to
u
in
L^{1}

it holds that

\left\langle(\boldsymbol{b}(u),Du))\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\left\langle(\boldsymbol{b}(u_{n}),Du_{n})\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega),\ \varphi\geq 0

and

\int_{\Omega}|(\boldsymbol{b}(u),Du)|\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|(\boldsymbol{b}(u_{n}),Du_{n})|\,,

(see
[
34
, Lemma 9]
).

In the following proposition we give an explicit representation of the jump part
of the lower-semicontinuous pairing.

Proposition 8.12
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}
satisfy assumptions
(b1)
-
(b4)
,
let
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and assume
J_{u}
oriented in such a way that
u^{i}=u^{+}
and
u^{e}=u^{-}
. Then

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}=\Tr_{L}(\boldsymbol{b},J_{u})\,|D^{j}u|,

where

\begin{split}\Tr_{L}(\boldsymbol{b},J_{u})(x):=\min_{s\in[u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x)]}\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{u^{-}(x)}^{u^{+}(x)}&\Big[\chi_{[u^{-}(x),s]}(t)\,\Tr^{e}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},J_{u})(x)\\
&+\chi_{[s,u^{+}(x)]}(t)\,\Tr^{i}(\boldsymbol{b}_{t},J_{u})(x)\Big]\,dt.\end{split}

Proof.

The result follows from a straightforward computation:

\begin{split}\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}&=\Big[\beta^{i}(x,u^{+}(x))-\beta^{e}(x,u^{-}(x))-\max_{s\in[u^{-},u^{+}]}[\beta^{i}(x,s)-\beta^{e}(x,s)]\Big]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&=\min_{s\in[u^{-},u^{+}]}\Big[\beta^{i}(x,u^{+}(x))-\beta^{e}(x,u^{-}(x))-\beta^{i}(x,s)+\beta^{e}(x,s)\Big]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&=\min_{s\in[u^{-},u^{+}]}\left[\int_{u^{-}}^{s}\gamma^{e}(x,t)\,dt+\int_{s}^{u^{+}}\gamma^{i}(x,t)\,dt\right]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&=\min_{s\in[u^{-},u^{+}]}\left[\int_{u^{-}}^{u^{+}}[\chi_{[u^{-},s]}(t)\,\gamma^{e}(x,t)+\chi_{[s,u^{+}]}(t)\,\gamma^{i}(x,t)]\,dt\right]\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits J_{u}\\
&=\min_{s\in[u^{-},u^{+}]}\left[\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{u^{-}}^{u^{+}}[\chi_{[u^{-},s]}(t)\,\gamma^{e}(x,t)+\chi_{[s,u^{+}]}(t)\,\gamma^{i}(x,t)]\,dt\right]|D^{j}u|.\end{split}

∎

The total variation of the pairing measure

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}
is not, in general,
lower semicontinuous along sequences

(u_{n})\in\mathcal{A}(u)
.
Therefore, we proceed to characterize the selections

\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
that guarantee it.
More precisely,
given
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
,
for every
x\in A:=\Omega\setminus(S_{u}\setminus J_{u})

let us define the function

(8.11)

\zeta(x,t):=\int_{u^{-}(x)}^{t}\gamma^{e}(x,\ell)\,d\ell+\int_{t}^{u^{+}(x)}\gamma^{i}(x,\ell)\,d\ell,\qquad t\in[u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x)]

(see, for comparison, Proposition
8.12
),
and let

g(x):=\min_{s\in[0,1]}|\zeta(x,(1-s)u^{-}(x)+s\,u^{+}(x))|\,,\qquad x\in A.

The function
g
is Borel-measurable, since it can be written as the infimum
of a countable family of Borel-measurable functions:

g(x)=\inf_{q\in[0,1]}|\zeta(x,(1-q)u^{-}(x)+q\,u^{+}(x)),\qquad x\in A.

Hence, the function

\psi(x,s):=|\zeta(x,(1-s)u^{-}(x)+s\,u^{+}(x))|-g(x),\qquad(x,s)\in A\times[0,1],

satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.9
,
so that there exists a function
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(A;[0,1])
,
that can be extended to a function
\lambda^{V}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])
,
such that
\psi(x,\lambda^{V}(x))=0
for every
x\in A
.

By construction, the function
u^{V}:=u^{\lambda^{V}}
satisfies

|\zeta(x,u^{V}(x))|=\min_{t\in[u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x)]}|\zeta(x,t)|\,,\qquad\text{for $\sigma$-a.e.}\ x\in\Omega.

The class of selections
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])

for which
|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{V}|
is lower semicontinuous along sequences

(u_{n})\in\mathcal{A}(u)
is

\mathcal{L}_{V}[u]:=\Big\{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1])\colon\lambda(x)=\dfrac{u^{V}(x)-u^{-}(x)}{u^{+}(x)-u^{-}(x)}\ \text{for $\sigma$-a.e.}\ x\in J_{u}\Big\}.

This class is not empty since, by construction,
\lambda^{V}\in\mathcal{L}_{V}[u]
.
We can then define the following pairing measure,

(8.12)

\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{V}=\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}},\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{V}[u],

which is clearly independent of the choice of

\lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{V}[u]
.
Moreover, it is straightforward to check that,

|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{V}|\leq|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{{\lambda}}|\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega;[0,1]).

The following result,
analogous to Theorem
8.8
, holds.

Theorem 8.13
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}
satisfy assumptions
(b1)
-
(b4)
.
Fixed
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
, let
\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{V}
be the pairing defined in (
8.12
).
Then, for every
(u_{n})\in\mathcal{A}(u)
,
it holds that

(8.13)

\int_{\Omega}|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{V}|\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u_{n}),Du_{n})\right)_{V}|\,.

Proof.

We can partition

J_{u}
into three disjoint Borel sets
J_{u}^{m}
,
J_{u}^{M}
, and
J_{u}^{0}

such that

\begin{cases}m_{x}:=\min_{t\in[u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x)]}\zeta(x,t)>0&\forall x\in J_{u}^{m},\\
M_{x}:=\max_{t\in[u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x)]}\zeta(x,t)<0&\forall x\in J_{u}^{M},\\
m_{x}\leq 0\leq M_{x}&\forall x\in J_{u}^{0},\end{cases}

where
\zeta
is the function defined in (
8.11
).
Given
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{V}[u]
, the density
\Theta_{V}

of
|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{V}|
with respect to
|Du|

satisfies

(8.14)

{\Theta_{V}}(x)=|\zeta(x,u^{V}(x))|=\begin{cases}m_{x},&\text{for $\sigma$-a.e.\ $x\in J_{u}^{m}$},\\
-M_{x},&\text{for $\sigma$-a.e.\ $x\in J_{u}^{M}$},\\
0,&\text{for $\sigma$-a.e.\ $x\in J_{u}^{0}$}.\end{cases}

Hence, if we denote by
\Theta_{G}

and
\Theta_{H}
the densities of the measures

\left((\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)_{L}\right)^{+}

and

\left((-\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)_{L}\right)^{+}
respectively,
then it holds that

\displaystyle\Theta_{G}=\Theta_{V},\quad\text{$\sigma$-a.e.\ on $J^{m}_{u}$},\quad\Theta_{G}=0\quad\text{$\sigma$-a.e.\ on $J^{M}_{u}\cup J^{0}_{u}$},

\displaystyle\Theta_{H}=\Theta_{V},\quad\text{$\sigma$-a.e.\ on $J_{u}^{M}$},\quad\Theta_{H}=0\quad\text{$\sigma$-a.e.\ on $J^{m}_{u}\cup J^{0}_{u}$}.

whereas
\Theta_{V}=\Theta_{G}+\Theta_{H}

\sigma
-a.e. on
\Omega\setminus J_{u}
.

Then

F(u):=\int_{\Omega}|(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)_{V}|=G^{+}(u)+H^{+}(u),

where

G^{+},H^{+}\colon BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}\to[0,+\infty[
are defined by

\quad G^{+}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\left((\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)_{L}\right)^{+}\,\qquad\qquad H^{+}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\left((-\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)_{L}\right)^{+}\,.

The conclusion then follows once one proves that the functionals
G^{+}(u)
and
H^{+}(u)
are lower semicontinuous along sequences
(u_{n})\in\mathcal{A}(u)
.
Since

G^{+}(u)=\sup_{\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),0\leq\varphi\leq 1\}}\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{L}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle,

this is a consequence of Theorem
8.8
.
The proof for
H^{+}
is similar.
∎

9.
Pairing as relaxed functional

For every function
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)

let us consider the functional
defined by

\mathcal{F}^{\varphi}(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\boldsymbol{b}(x,u)\cdot\nabla u\,dx&\text{$u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)$}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\\
+\infty,&u\in(BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))\setminus W^{1,1}(\Omega).\end{cases}

Moreover, for every
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}

we define the relaxation

\overline{\mathcal{F}^{\varphi}}(u):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{\varphi}(u_{n})\colon(u_{n})\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{A}(u)\right\}\,,

where
\mathcal{A}(u)
is the class introduced in Definition
8.2
.

The following result was proved in
[
30
, Theorem 7.1]

under the additional assumption
\Div_{x}\boldsymbol{b}_{t}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.
Here we provide a full generalization to the case of
divergence-measure vector fields.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{b}

satisfy assumptions
(b1)
-
(b4)
.
Then,
for every
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
, it holds that

(9.1)

\overline{\mathcal{F}^{\varphi}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,d(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)_{L}\,,\qquad u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}\,.

Proof.

Firstly, we notice that Theorem
8.8
guarantees the inequality

(9.2)

\overline{\mathcal{F}^{\varphi}}(u)\geq\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,d(\boldsymbol{b}(x,u),Du)_{L}.

On the other hand,
for every
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}
, we obtain the recovery sequence as follows.
For
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{M}[u]
be such that
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x,u^{-}(x),u^{+}(x))=F(x,u^{\lambda}(x))
, let
(u_{n}^{(\lambda)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{A}(u^{\lambda})
be as in Theorem
8.6
.
By Lemma
8.7
we have that

(9.3)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{\varphi}(u_{n}^{(\lambda)})=\overline{\mathcal{F}^{\varphi}}(u)\,,

which, combined with (
9.2
), leads to (
9.1
).

(9.4)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{\varphi}(u_{n}^{(\lambda)})=&\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\boldsymbol{b}(x,u_{n}^{(\lambda)})\cdot\nabla u_{n}^{(\lambda)}\,dx\\
=&-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u_{n}^{(\lambda)})\varphi\,d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{B}(x,u_{n}^{(\lambda)})\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx.\end{split}

which, combined with (
9.2
), leads to (
9.1
).
∎

The above relaxation result is clearly related to the semicontinuity results
proved in Theorems
8.8
and
8.10
.

On the other hand,
in view of the semicontinuity result stated in Theorem
8.13
,
we conjecture that an analogous relaxation result holds true also for

|\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{V}|
,
where
\left(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,u),Du\right)_{V}
is the pairing measure introduced in (
8.12
).

Indeed, this relaxation result, with respect to the
L^{1}
convergence,
has been already proved in the linear case

\boldsymbol{B}(x,t)=\boldsymbol{A}(x)\,t
,
with
\boldsymbol{A}\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Specifically, in this setting the function
f(x,\xi):=|\boldsymbol{A}(x)\cdot\xi|

satisfies the assumptions of Theorem 4.2 in
[
1
]
,
where it is proved that the relaxation of the functional

\mathcal{F}(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\boldsymbol{A}(x)\cdot\nabla u|\,dx&\text{$u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)$}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\\
+\infty,&u\in(BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))\setminus W^{1,1}(\Omega),\end{cases}

with respect to the
L^{1}
convergence,
is in fact

\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u)=|\left(\boldsymbol{A},Du\right)_{V}|(\Omega),\qquad\forall u\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}{(\Omega)}.

In
[
1
, formula (1.7)]
the relaxed functional
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is expressed
in terms of the function

f^{-}(x,\xi)=\min\{|\boldsymbol{A}^{+}(x)\cdot\xi|,\,|\boldsymbol{A}^{-}(x)\cdot\xi|\}.

From the explicit representation given in (
8.14
), we have that

f^{-}(x,\nu_{u})
coincides with the density
\Theta_{V}

of
|\left(\boldsymbol{A},Du\right)_{V}|
with respect to
|Du|
.

The main obstruction in proving the relaxation result
in the general case is to pass to the limit along the recovering sequence,
as in (
9.4
).
Indeed, due to the presence of the absolute value, we cannot integrate by parts
the term containing
\Div(\boldsymbol{B}(x,u(x))

and entirely different techniques should be used.
