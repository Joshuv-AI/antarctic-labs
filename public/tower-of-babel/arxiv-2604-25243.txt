B ′ B^{\prime} -orbits on flag varieties and symmetry breaking
Valentin MASSICOT

Abstract
Motivated by branching problems for principal series representations of the Lie group G = G ​ L ​ ( n , ℝ ) G=GL(n,\mathbb{R}) , we consider all pairs ( G ′ , P ) (G^{\prime},P) with G ′ G^{\prime} being the Levy factor of a parabolic subgroup of G G and P P a parabolic subgroup of G G for which a Borel subgroup B ′ B^{\prime} of G ′ G^{\prime} has finitely many orbits on G / P G/P . We classify all such pairs ( G ′ , P ) (G^{\prime},P) for which B ′ B^{\prime} -orbits on the generalized flag variety G / P G/P are determined by invariant functions inspired from the Bruhat decomposition. We also describe explicitly the double coset space B ′ \ G / P B^{\prime}\backslash G/P as well as the closed B ′ B^{\prime} -orbits on G / P G/P whenever B ′ B^{\prime} -orbits are computed by these invariant functions.

Introduction

Let
G^{\prime}\subset G
be a pair of groups and
(V,\pi)
be an irreducible representation of
G
. Branching problems ask about the behaviour of the restriction
\pi_{|G^{\prime}}
. If
\pi
happens to be unitary and
G
and
G^{\prime}
locally compact, the Mautner theorem (see
[
21
, Theorem 1.2.]
) implies that
\pi_{|G^{\prime}}
decomposes (uniquely if
G
is a type I group) as a direct integral of irreducible unitary representations of
G^{\prime}
. When
\pi
is no longer unitary (for instance a smooth representation of a Lie group), there is no universal notion of irreducible decomposition. In this broader context, one may study restrictions of representations through the space of symmetry breaking operators (SBOs for short) which are elements of
\mathrm{Hom}_{G^{\prime}}(\pi_{|G^{\prime}},\tau)
where
\tau
ranges over an appropriate class of representations of
G^{\prime}
.

We are interested in the setting where
G^{\prime}\subset G
is a pair of real reductive groups. In this configuration, a theorem of Casselman (see
[
9
, Theorem 8.37]
) asserts that every irreducible admissible representation of
G
may be embedded in some principal series representation. Moreover, these principal series representations are known to be generically irreducible (see
[
2
, Theorem 7.2]
for unitary principal series) so they will constitute our ”appropriate class of representations”. These representations of
G
are obtained by induction with respect to parabolic subgroups and can thus be realized on sections of vector bundles over some generalized flag varieties. This geometric realization allows us to use functional analysis techniques to tackle down branching problems. More concretely, if
P
is a parabolic subgroup of
G
and
\chi
is a finite dimensional representation of
P
, the quotient space
\mathcal{V}
of
G\times V
by the equivalence relation
(g,v)\sim(gp,\chi^{-1}(p)v)
is a homogeneous vector bundle over
G/P
and the induced representation
\operatorname{ind}_{P}^{G}\chi
may be realized on the space of smooth sections
C^{\infty}(G/P,\mathcal{V})
. If in addition we consider a parabolic subgroup
P^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
and an homogeneous vector bundle
\mathcal{W}
over
G^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
, the Schwartz kernel theorem (
[
22
, Proposition 25]
) implies that
G^{\prime}
-intertwining operators
T:C^{\infty}(G/P,\mathcal{V})\to C^{\infty}(G^{\prime}/P^{\prime},\mathcal{W})
correspond to
P^{\prime}
-equivariant distribution kernels
K_{T}\in(D^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}^{*})\otimes W)^{P^{\prime}}
where
W
is the fiber of
\mathcal{W}
(see
[
15
, Proposition 3.2]
). Note that the support of such a distribution kernel is a
P^{\prime}
-invariant closed subset of
G/P
.

This fact leads us to the following strategy: the topological analysis of
P^{\prime}
-orbits of
G/P
provides an information on
K_{T}
, hence on
T
. More concretely, one may translate the equivariance of
K_{T}
as a system of PDEs and use the knowledge of the support of
K_{T}
as ”boundary conditions” in order to solve this system (see
[
16
, Chapter 3]
).

One way to describe the topological structure of
P^{\prime}\backslash G/P
is through the partial order defined as

\forall X_{1},X_{2}\in P^{\prime}\backslash G/P,\hskip 8.53581ptX_{1}\prec X_{2}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\iff}X_{1}\subset\overline{X_{2}}

and the corresponding Hasse diagram (see

In this work, we are interested in the setting where

An extreme case is given by

and

the Borel subgroup of

is in bijection with the symmetric group

\sigma&\mapsto&\operatorname{Card}\{s\in\{1,\dots,j\}\mid\sigma(s)\geqslant n-i+1\}\end{array}

in the following sense:

Fact 1

(Combinatorial formulation)
.

The map

r:\begin{array}[]{ccc}B\backslash G/B&\to&\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 0}^{\frac{(n-2)(n-1)}{2}}\\
BM_{\sigma}B&\mapsto&(r_{ij}(\sigma))_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1}\end{array}

is injective and induces a poset isomorphism onto its image.

Note that for a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, one has
r_{ij}(\sigma)=\operatorname{rank}(M_{\sigma}^{ij})
where given a matrix
g
,

is the submatrix obtained from
g
by intersecting the last
i
rows with the first
j
columns.
Since the rank of such submatrices is a
(B\times B)
-invariant (i.e. invariant under both right and left translation by
B
), we may rephrase
1
as

Fact 1
′

(Invariant-theoretic formulation)
.

The map

r:\begin{array}[]{ccc}B\backslash G/B&\to&\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 0}^{\frac{(n-2)(n-1)}{2}}\\
BgB&\mapsto&(\operatorname{rank}(g^{ij}))_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1}\end{array}

is a well-defined injection and induces a poset isomorphism onto its image.

This fact may be formulated more algebraicly. Indeed, if we denote by
P_{i}
the subgroup

P_{i}\coloneq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
0&z\end{pmatrix}x\in GL(i,\mathbb{R}),z\in GL(n-i,\mathbb{R}),y\in M_{i,n-i}(\mathbb{R})\right\}\subset GL(n,\mathbb{R}),

then
(P_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}
is exactly the set of all (both standard and non-standard) maximal parabolic subgroups of
G
containing
B
and the map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}GL(n,\mathbb{R})&\to&\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 0}^{\frac{(n-2)(n-1)}{2}}\\
g&\mapsto&(\operatorname{rank}(g^{ij}))_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1}\end{array}

induces a bijection between
P_{i}\backslash G/P_{j}
and its image. Consequently, we may state
1
as

Fact 1
′′

(Algebraic formulation)
.

The map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}B\backslash G/B&\to&\displaystyle\prod_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1}P_{i}\backslash G/P_{j}\\
BgB&\mapsto&(P_{i}gP_{j})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1}\end{array}

is injective and induces a poset isomorphism onto its image.

Let us return to the case where
G^{\prime}=GL(n_{1},\mathbb{R})\times\cdots\times GL(n_{k},\mathbb{R})
and
P
is a general parabolic subgroup of

. We ask the following question:

Question 2

(Invariant-theoretic formulation)
.

When are
B^{\prime}
-orbits on
G/P
determined by the
(B^{\prime}\times P)
-invariant maps

?

Again, we may formulate it more algebraically using the sets

of (possibly non-standard) maximal parabolic subgroups of

containing

and

respectively.

Question 2
′

(Algebraic formulation)
.

When is the map

\Psi:\begin{array}[]{ccc}B^{\prime}\backslash G/P&\to&\displaystyle\prod_{Q_{1}\in\mathcal{Q}_{B^{\prime}},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}_{P}}Q_{1}\backslash G/Q_{2}\\
B^{\prime}gP&\mapsto&(Q_{1}gQ_{2})_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{1}\in\mathcal{Q}_{B^{\prime}}\\
Q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}_{P}\end{subarray}}\end{array}

Considering there is only finitely many possible choices of a submatrix, hence of rank functions (and finitely many values for each rank), the codomain of
\Psi
is finite.
In particular,
\Psi
can only be injective if
B^{\prime}\backslash G/P
is finite.
The classification of the pairs
(G^{\prime},P)
for which

(

The pairs

\begin{array}[]{rcccc}0)&k=2,&\text{any }N,&l=2,&\text{any }M,\\

Our main result is the following theorem (see

Theorem 1
.

Assume that

is finite.

i)

If condition
\mathrm{I}^{\prime})
of
3

,

ii)

In all remaining cases,

As explained previously, the injectivity of the map

This gives a rather explicit way to compute the

2.1
Parabolic subgroups and flag varieties

Let

of positive integers satisfying

with

(m_{1},\dots,m_{l})
of length

of
n

\underline{n}
if there exists a composition
(i_{1},\dots,i_{l})
of
k
such that

\forall j\in\{1,\dots,l\},\hskip 8.53581ptm_{j}=\sum_{s=1}^{i_{j}}n_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j-1}+s}.

(2.1)

If
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
is a composition of
n
, define

to be the standard parabolic subgroup of
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
given by

\left(\vbox{\hrule height=59.15178pt,depth=59.15178pt,width=0.0pt}\right.
\left.\vbox{\hrule height=59.15178pt,depth=59.15178pt,width=0.0pt}\right)
n_{1}
n_{2}
n_{k}

\ddots

P_{\underline{n}}=
.

In this work, the term ”parabolic subgroup” will refer to any subgroup of
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
which is conjugate to a standard parabolic subgroup by a permutation matrix. These are exactly the parabolic subgroups corresponding to the standard Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})
equipped with a non-standard basis of its root system.

Recall (see for instance
[
1
, Section
1.2
]
) that given a composition
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
of
n
, a flag of type
\underline{n}
is a sequence

V_{1}\subset\cdots\subset V_{k-1}\subset V_{k}=\mathbb{R}^{n}

of subspaces satisfying
\dim V_{i}=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i}
. The group
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
acts transitively on the set
\mathcal{D}_{\underline{n}}
of flags of type
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
. Moreover, if
(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the isotropy subgroup of the flag

\mathrm{Span}(e_{1},\dots,e_{n_{1}})\subset\mathrm{Span}(e_{1},\dots,e_{n_{1}+n_{2}})\subset\cdots\subset\mathrm{Span}(e_{1},\dots,e_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}})=\mathbb{R}^{n}

is the standard parabolic subgroup
P_{\underline{n}}
. This induces a bijection
\mathcal{\mathcal{D}}_{\underline{n}}\simeq GL(n,\mathbb{R})/P_{\underline{n}}
from which we define a topology on
\mathcal{\mathcal{D}}_{\underline{n}}
, turning the bijection into a homeomorphism.

If
A\in GL(n,\mathbb{R})
is decomposed as

A=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\cdots&A_{k}\end{pmatrix}

with each
A_{i}\in M_{n,n_{i}}(\mathbb{R})
, we will write the corresponding flag
D=(V_{0},\dots,V_{k})\in\mathcal{D}_{\underline{n}}\simeq G/P_{\underline{n}}
as

D=\left[\begin{tabular}[]{c|c|c}$A_{1}$&$\cdots$&$A_{k}$\end{tabular}\right].

Since the block
A_{k}
corresponds to the subspace
V_{k}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, changing it has no effect on
D
and
D
is entirely determined by
A_{1},\dots,A_{k-1}
. The invertibility of
A
implies that

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\cdots&A_{k-1}\end{pmatrix}

has maximal rank. In particular, we can also realize the flag variety
\mathcal{D}_{\underline{n}}
as the quotient of the space of maximal rank matrices
M_{n,n-n_{k}}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathrm{reg}}
under the right action of
P_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k-1})}\subset GL(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k-1})
.

The third description of
\mathcal{D}_{\underline{n}}
(as a quotient of
M_{n,n-n_{k}}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathrm{reg}}
)is more convenient for explicit computations as it contains less redundancy but the second one (as a quotient of
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
) may allow us to describe specific flags via permutation matrices (see
B

Remark 2.1
.

Since we realize flags as equivalence classes of invertible (resp. maximal rank) matrices, there is no unique way of representing a specific flag. More precisely, the action of the Levi part of
P_{\underline{n}}
(resp.
P_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k-1})}

A_{i}
and its unipotent radical allows us to add any linear combination of columns of a block
A_{i}
to any column of a different block

as long as

.

2.2
Ranks of flags

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
be a composition of
n
,
J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
,
s\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\underline{n}}
be the flag variety associated to
\underline{n}
(see
Section

2.1

\operatorname{rank}_{J,s}:\mathcal{D}_{\underline{n}}\to\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 0}
is defined by

\forall D\in\mathcal{D}_{\underline{n}},\hskip 8.53581pt\operatorname{rank}_{J,s}(D)=\operatorname{rank}\Big((a_{ij})_{(i,j)\in J\times\{1,\dots,n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}\}}\Big)

where
(a_{ij})\in M_{n,n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathrm{reg}}
is a representative for
D_{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s},n-n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s})}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Note that these maps are well defined since
\operatorname{rank}_{J,s}(D)
coincides with
\dim p_{J}(D_{s})
where

is the projection onto
\mathrm{Span}(e_{j},j\in J)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

Remark 2.8
.

These maps are lower semi-continuous because the usual rank of matrices is.
In particular, if
X\subset\mathcal{D}_{\underline{n}}
is a set of matrices satisfying
\operatorname{rank}_{J,s}D=r

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\underline{m}=(m_{1},m_{2})
be a composition of

-orbits on

B_{1}&\cdots&B_{m_{1}}\end{bmatrix}

with

and

and a change of basis allowed by the right action of

Theorem B
.

Let
P_{\underline{m}}
be any parabolic subgroup of
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
and
B^{\prime}
be the standard Borel subgroup of
GL(n-1,\mathbb{R})\times GL(1,\mathbb{R})
. We denote by
(e_{1},\dots,e_{n-1})
the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Then, any
B^{\prime}
-orbit of
\mathcal{D}_{\underline{m}}\simeq G/P_{\underline{m}}
admits a representative given by

A=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&\cdots&A_{l}\end{bmatrix}

where each block

is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}e_{i_{j,1}}&\dots&e_{i_{j,m_{j}}}\\
0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}

(3.2)

except exactly one of them which is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{k}e_{i_{s}}&e_{i_{j,2}}&\dots&e_{i_{j,m_{j}}}\\
1&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}

(3.3)

for some
k\in\{0,\dots,l-1\}
. Moreover,

–

all basis vectors
e_{i_{j,j^{\prime}}}
involved are distinct,

–

if the block
A_{j_{0}}
is of the form (
3.3
), then there exists an increasing sequence
j_{0}<j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}
such that
e_{i_{s}}
appears in the block
A_{j_{s}}
for all
s\in\{1,\dots,k\}
.

This normal form is unique up to permutations of the
e_{j,j^{\prime}}
vectors in each block
A_{j}
.

Corollary C
.

In the setting of
B
, the closed
B^{\prime}
-orbits coincide with the
B^{\prime}
-fixed points. More precisely, they are given by the normal forms satisfying

(i)

k=0
,

(ii)

i_{j_{1},j_{1}^{\prime}}<i_{j_{2},j_{2}^{\prime}}
whenever
j_{1}<j_{2}
.

Proof.

First note that the normal forms satisfying both conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
do correspond to
B^{\prime}
-fixed points, hence to closed orbits.

On the other hand, if some normal form has
k\geqslant 1
, we may choose elements in the same orbit for which the block of type (
3.3
) is replaced by

\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{k}\frac{1}{N}e_{i_{s}}&e_{i_{j,2}}&\dots&e_{i_{j,m_{j}}}\\
1&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}

and then let
N\to+\infty
to see that the orbit is not closed. In particular, the normal form of a closed orbit must satisfy condition
(i)
.

Now assume that some normal form satisfies condition
(i)
but not condition
(ii)
. Then, if
j_{1},j_{1}^{\prime},j_{2},j_{2}^{\prime}
are indices satisfying

i_{j_{1},j_{1}^{\prime}}>i_{j_{2},j_{2}^{\prime}},\hskip 28.45274ptj_{1}<j_{2},

we may replace the columns

\begin{pmatrix}e_{i_{j_{1},j_{1}^{\prime}}}&e_{i_{j_{2},j_{2}^{\prime}}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

by

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{N}e_{i_{j_{1},j_{1}^{\prime}}}-e_{i_{j_{2},j_{2}^{\prime}}}&e_{i_{j_{1},j_{1}^{\prime}}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

using both the left
B^{\prime}
-action and the right
P_{\underline{m}}
-action, then let
N\to+\infty
to find again that the orbit is not closed. Thus, any closed orbit must satisfy both conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
.
∎

Remark 3.5

Corollary D
.

In the setting of
B
, one has

\left|B^{\prime}\backslash G/P_{\underline{m}}\right|=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{m_{1},\dots,m_{l}}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sigma_{k}(m_{1},\dots,m_{l})

(3.4)

where
\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-th symmetric function in
l
-variables and

\binom{n}{m_{1},\dots,m_{l}}=\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{l}!}

is a multinomial coefficient.

Proof.

Let us consider all the normal forms for which
k=0
. These are in bijection with the quotient of the symmetric group
S_{n}
by its subgroup
S_{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{m_{l}}
which is of cardinality equal to

\binom{n}{m_{1},\dots,m_{l}}=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{m_{1},\dots,m_{l}}\sigma_{1}(m_{1},\dots,m_{l}).

We now consider all the normal forms for a specific value of

. Such a normal form is determined by the set
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
, the sequence
j_{0}<\cdots<j_{k}
and the sets

(or

=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{m_{1},\dots,m_{l}}\frac{1}{k!}\sigma_{k+1}(m_{1},\dots,m_{l}).

Thus, the formula (
3.4

∎

Corollary C
′
.

In the setting of

, the closed
B^{\prime}
-orbits coincide with the
B^{\prime}
-fixed points. More explicitly, they are given by the normal forms

\begin{bmatrix}0&\cdots&0&e_{1}&\cdots&e_{m_{1}-r}\\
f_{1}&\cdots&f_{r}&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}.

for
r\in\{\max(0,m_{1}-n_{1}),\dots,\min(m_{1},n_{1})\}

Proof.

First observe that any flag of the form

\begin{bmatrix}0&\cdots&0&e_{1}&\cdots&e_{m_{1}-r}\\

is fixed under the

-action. In particular, its orbit is a singleton, hence closed.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{O}
be a closed
P^{\prime}
-orbit having the following normal form

D=\begin{bmatrix}0&\cdots&0&e_{j_{r+1}}&\cdots&e_{j_{s}}&e_{j_{s+1}}&\cdots&e_{j_{m_{1}}}\\
f_{i_{1}}&\cdots&f_{i_{r}}&f_{i_{r+1}}&\cdots&f_{i_{s}}&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}.

If

, one has

\mathcal{O}\ni\begin{bmatrix}0&\cdots&0&\frac{1}{N}e_{j_{r+1}}&e_{j_{r+2}}&\cdots&e_{j_{s}}&e_{j_{s+1}}&\cdots&e_{j_{m_{1}}}\\

f_{i_{1}}&\cdots&f_{i_{r}}&f_{i_{r+1}}&f_{i_{r+2}}&\cdots&f_{i_{s}}&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}.

Since this is a normal form different from the one defining

, this contradict
\mathcal{O}
being closed. We conclude that we must have

D=\begin{bmatrix}0&\cdots&0&e_{j_{r+1}}&\cdots&e_{j_{m_{1}}}\\
f_{i_{1}}&\cdots&f_{i_{r}}&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}.

Similarly,

.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
(e_{1},\dots,e_{q})
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
. Consider the
T(p,\mathbb{R})\times GL(q,\mathbb{R})

M_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
by

(A,B)\cdot C=ACB^{-1}.

Then each element of
M_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
belongs to a
(T(p,\mathbb{R})\times GL(q,\mathbb{R}))
-orbit of the form

(T(p,\mathbb{R})\times GL(q,\mathbb{R}))\cdot\begin{pmatrix}e_{i_{1}}&\cdots&e_{i_{r}}&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}

for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}
.

Proof.

Let
A=(a_{ij})\in M_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
. Since the
GL(q,\mathbb{R})
-orbits on
M_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
are determined by the linear span of columns of matrices, we may assume that
a_{ij}=0
if
j>\operatorname{rank}A\eqcolon r
.

Next, we define
i_{r}
as

i_{r}=\max\{i\in\{1,\dots,p\}\mid\exists j\in\{1,\dots,q\},\,a_{ij}\neq 0\}.

Acting on the right by a permutation matrix if needed, we may assume that
a_{i_{r},r}\neq 0
.
Then, multiplying on the left by a suitable element of
T(p,\mathbb{R})
and on the right by a suitable element of
GL(q,\mathbb{R})
, we find
B=(b_{ij})
in the same orbit as
A
which satisfies

b_{ij}=\begin{cases}0\text{ if }j>r\text{ or }\Big(j=r\text{ and }i\neq i_{r}\Big)\text{ or }\Big(i=i_{r}\text{ and }j\neq r\Big),\\
1\text{ if }(i,j)=(i_{r},r).\end{cases}

We now proceed by finite induction on
r
by defining

i_{r-1}=\max\{i\in\{1,\dots,i_{r-1}\}\mid\exists j\in\{1,\dots,r-1\},\,a_{ij}\neq 0\}

and applying the same process.
∎
