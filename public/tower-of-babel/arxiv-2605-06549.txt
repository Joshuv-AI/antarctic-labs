Stochastic Non-Smooth Non-Convex Optimization with Decision-Dependent Distributions
Chengchang Liu, Zongqi Wan, Haishan Ye, John C.S. Lui

Abstract
We study stochastic zeroth-order optimization with decision-dependent distributions, where the sampling law depends on the current decision and only noisy function values are available. For the non-smooth non-convex setting, we establish an explicit convergence guarantee for finding a ( δ , ϵ ) (\delta,\epsilon) -Goldstein stationary point with stochastic zeroth-order oracle (SZO) complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( d 2 ​ δ − 3 ​ ϵ − 3 ) {\mathcal{O}}(d^{2}\delta^{-3}\epsilon^{-3}) . In addition, we show that the above complexity can be achieved with single SZO feedback per iteration. We further extend the analysis to smooth and Hessian-Lipschitz objectives, obtaining complexities 𝒪 ⁡ ( d 2 ​ ϵ − 6 ) {\mathcal{O}}(d^{2}\epsilon^{-6}) and 𝒪 ( d 2 ϵ − 9 / 2 ) {\mathcal{O}}(d^{2}\epsilon^{-9/2}) , respectively. In the Hessian-Lipschitz case, this improves the best-known dependence on ϵ \epsilon for decision-dependent zeroth-order methods by a factor of ϵ − 1 / 2 \epsilon^{-1/2} .

Introduction

This paper studies the stochastic optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f({\bm{x}})\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\Xi({\bm{x}})}[F({\bm{x}};\xi)],

(1)

where the sampling distribution
\Xi({\bm{x}})
depends on the decision
{\bm{x}}
, and only stochastic function values are available. We allow the expected objective
f
to be non-smooth and non-convex.
Decision-dependent models arise naturally when deployed decisions change the future data-generating process
[
2
,
25
,
23
,
4
,
7
]
, as in price optimization
[
26
]
and strategic classification
[
14
,
18
]
.
In these settings, each stochastic sample is observed only after querying the system at the current decision.

The main difficulty in solving (
1
) is the
distribution drift
caused by the dependence of
\Xi({\bm{x}})
. If
\Xi({\bm{x}})
admits a density
p_{{\bm{x}}}
with respect to a reference measure, then

\displaystyle\nabla f({\bm{x}})=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\Xi({\bm{x}})}[\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}F({\bm{x}};\xi)]+\int F({\bm{x}};\xi)\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}p_{{\bm{x}}}(\xi)\,d\xi.

The second term depends on the derivative of the unknown sampling law and cannot be recovered from stochastic first-order information alone.
Therefore, it is difficult, and in general even impossible, to construct a reliable estimator of
\nabla f({\bm{x}})
from the stochastic first-order oracle
\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}F({\bm{x}};\xi)
.
This leads us to consider stochastic zeroth-order methods, where we assume access to the following stochastic zeroth-order oracle (SZO).

Assumption 1.1
.

We assume the stochastic zeroth-order oracle
F({\bm{x}};\xi)
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\Xi({\bm{x}})}[F({\bm{x}};\xi)]=f({\bm{x}})~~\text{and}~~\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\Xi({\bm{x}})}\big[|F({\bm{x}};\xi)-f({\bm{x}})|^{2}\big]\leq\sigma^{2}~~~\text{for all}~~{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Several zeroth-order methods have been proposed to find an
\epsilon
-stationary point of (
1
).

20

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}(\ell^{6}d^{2}\epsilon^{-6})
, where

This rate is improved to

{\mathcal{O}}(d^{2}\epsilon^{-5})

However, all of the above decision-dependent results assume that the objective function is smooth.
It remains unclear whether one can provide an explicit convergence guarantee for stochastic non-smooth non-convex problems.
This motivates us to ask the following question.

Can one design efficient zeroth-order methods for stochastic non-smooth non-convex optimization with decision-dependent distributions?

For non-smooth non-convex problems, it is natural to seek a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point, following
11
and
33

In the decision-independent setting, where the sampling distribution is fixed (i.e.,
\Xi({\bm{x}})\equiv\Xi

This complexity is further improved to

[

However, all these results assume that

Moreover, the above methods for decision-independent non-smooth non-convex optimization require at least two-point feedback per iteration to obtain a good approximation of
\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}})
.
This can be restrictive in settings where only one function evaluation is available at each iteration
[
9

27

In addition, existing one-point methods for smooth non-convex optimization with decision-dependent distributions often have strong dependence on either the magnitude of the SZO values

or the dimension

This motivates us to further consider the following question.

Can one-point-feedback zeroth-order methods achieve comparable SZO complexity for (non)-smooth non-convex problems?

In this paper, we answer the above two questions by studying how the two-point and one-point estimators approximate
\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}})

Preliminaries

Throughout,
\left\|\cdot\right\|
denotes the Euclidean norm. For
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r>0
, define

\displaystyle{\mathbb{B}}({\bm{x}},r)\triangleq\{{\bm{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\left\|{\bm{y}}-{\bm{x}}\right\|\leq r\},\qquad{\mathbb{B}}^{d}\triangleq{\mathbb{B}}({\bm{0}},1),\qquad{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\triangleq\{{\bm{u}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\left\|{\bm{u}}\right\|=1\}.

For a nonempty set
S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, let

{\rm dist}({\bm{x}},S)\triangleq\inf_{{\bm{y}}\in S}\left\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\right\|.

We also let
\Pi_{S}
denote the Euclidean projection onto a closed convex set
S
.
All expectations are denoted by
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
, with subscripts added when the underlying randomness needs to be emphasized. For a locally Lipschitz function
h
,
\partial h({\bm{x}})
denotes its Clarke subdifferential.

We make the following assumptions on the objective function (
1
).

Assumption 2.1
.

The objective function is lower bounded such that
f^{*}\triangleq\inf_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f({\bm{x}})>-\infty
.

Assumption 2.2
.

The objective function is
L
-Lipschitz continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, i.e., for all
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, it holds that

\displaystyle|f({\bm{x}})-f({\bm{y}})|\leq L\left\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\right\|.

We then introduce the definition of Clarke and Goldstein subdifferentials
[
5
,
11
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Clarke and Goldstein subdifferentials)
.

For a locally Lipschitz function
f
and
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the Clarke subdifferential is

\displaystyle\partial f({\bm{x}})\triangleq\operatorname{conv}\left\{{\bm{g}}:\exists{\bm{x}}_{n}\to{\bm{x}},\;\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{n})\;\text{exists},\;\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{n})\to{\bm{g}}\right\}.

For
\delta>0
, the Goldstein
\delta
-subdifferential is

\displaystyle\partial_{\delta}f({\bm{x}})\triangleq\operatorname{conv}\Big(\cup_{{\bm{y}}\in{\mathbb{B}}({\bm{x}},\delta)}\partial f({\bm{y}})\Big).

We aim to find an approximate Goldstein stationary point
[
33
]
of the non-smooth non-convex objective.

Definition 2.2

(
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point)
.

A point
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point of
f
if

{\rm dist}({\bm{0}},\partial_{\delta}f({\bm{x}}))\leq\epsilon.

Zeroth-Order O2NC for Non-Smooth Non-Convex Decision Dependent Problems

Although the O2NC framework provides explicit convergence guarantee for non-smooth non-convex problems, it requires a good unbiased estimator of
\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}})
.
When
\xi\sim\Xi
is decision-independent, the prior zeroth-order methods
[
10
,
24
,
19
,
3
,
16
,
1
]
use

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bm{g}}}_{t}\triangleq\frac{d}{2\delta}\left(F({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t})-F({\bm{y}}_{t}-\delta{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t})\right){\bm{u}}_{t}

(3)

which satisfies Condition
2
with
\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{{\bm{g}}}_{t}\|^{2}]\leq dL^{2}
and thus leads to the complexity of
\mathcal{O}(dL^{2}\delta^{-1}\epsilon^{-3})
through the O2NC framework.
However,
\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{{\bm{g}}}_{t}\|^{2}]\leq dL^{2}
requires the Lipschitz continuity assumption on
F({\bm{x}};\xi)
,
which does not hold in our setting in general.
In addition, for the decision-dependent problems, one cannot access
F({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t})
and
F({\bm{y}}_{t}-\delta{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t})

under the same
\xi_{t}
.

In this section, we study two types of zeroth-order estimators for approximating
\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}})
(see Section
3.1
).
We also equip these zeroth-order estimators in the O2NC framework and show the explicit SZO complexity for the non-smooth non-convex decision-dependent problems (Section
3.2
).

3.1
Zeroth-Order Gradient Estimators

We first construct
{\bm{g}}_{t}
by the two-points randomized estimator as in
[
14
]
:

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}_{\text{2pt}}({\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t}^{+},\xi_{t}^{-})\triangleq\frac{d}{2\delta}\left(F({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t}^{+})-F({\bm{y}}_{t}-\delta{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t}^{-})\right){\bm{u}}_{t},

(4)

where
{\bm{u}}_{t}\sim{\rm Unif}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})
,
\xi_{t}^{+}\sim\Xi({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t})
, and
\xi_{t}^{-}\sim\Xi({\bm{y}}_{t}-\delta{\bm{u}}_{t})

The following lemma shows that the two-point estimator can be considered an unbiased stochastic gradient estimator of
\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})
without smooth conditions or mini-batch requirements.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
1.1
and
2.2
hold. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\bm{g}}_{\text{\rm 2pt}}({\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t}^{+},\xi_{t}^{-})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{y}}_{t}),

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\left\|{\bm{g}}_{\text{\rm 2pt}}({\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t}^{+},\xi_{t}^{-})\right\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\big]\leq\frac{d^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2\delta^{2}}+16\sqrt{2\pi}dL^{2}.

We then construct
{\bm{g}}_{t}
by the one-point estimator.
The standard one-point estimator used in prior work
[
20
,
9
]

Inspired by the residual-feedback ideas of

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}_{\text{1pt}}({\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{u}}_{t},\xi_{t};{\bm{y}}_{t-1},{\bm{u}}_{t-1},\xi_{t-1})\triangleq\frac{d}{\delta}\left(F({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t})-F({\bm{y}}_{t-1}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t-1};\xi_{t-1})\right){\bm{u}}_{t},

6
Conclusions and Future Works

We studied stochastic zeroth-order optimization with decision-dependent distributions in the non-smooth non-convex setting.
We established an explicit convergence guaranty for finding a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point with an SZO complexity of
{\mathcal{O}}(d^{2}\delta^{-3}\epsilon^{-3})
.
In addition, we showed that such an SZO complexity can be achieved by single-point zeroth-order estimator.
We also extended the analysis to smooth and Hessian-Lipschitz objectives.
In the smooth case, our framework achieves the state-of-the-art complexity
{\mathcal{O}}(d^{2}\epsilon^{-6})
.
In the Hessian-Lipschitz case, it improves the best known result from
{\mathcal{O}}(d^{2}\epsilon^{-5})
to
{\mathcal{O}}(d^{2}\epsilon^{-9/2})
.
These results show that the proposed ZO-O2NC method provides a unified guarantee to handle both non-smooth and smooth decision-dependent problems under zeroth-order feedback.

For the future work, it would be interesting to establish lower bounds for decision-dependent zeroth-order optimization, to understand whether the rates obtained here are optimal.
It is also possible to consider stochastic composite or constraint non-smooth non-convex problems with decision dependent distribution
[
22
,
21
]
.

{\bm{u}}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
.
Then
\psi_{t}
is odd, so
\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{u}}\sim{\rm Unif}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})}[\psi_{t}({\bm{u}})]=0
. Moreover, Assumption
2.2
implies

\displaystyle|\psi_{t}({\bm{u}})-\psi_{t}({\bm{v}})|\leq|f({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{u}})-f({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{v}})|+|f({\bm{y}}_{t}-\delta{\bm{u}})-f({\bm{y}}_{t}-\delta{\bm{v}})|\leq 2\delta L\left\|{\bm{u}}-{\bm{v}}\right\|,

so
\psi_{t}
is
(2\delta L)
-Lipschitz on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
. Therefore, Lemma
B.1
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|S_{t}|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\big]=\mathrm{Var}(\psi_{t}({\bm{u}}_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})\leq\frac{64\sqrt{2\pi}\delta^{2}L^{2}}{d}.

B.2
The Proof of Lemma
3.2

Proof.

We write

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}_{\text{1pt},t}\triangleq{\bm{g}}_{\text{1pt}}({\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{u}}_{t},\xi_{t};{\bm{y}}_{t-1},{\bm{u}}_{t-1},\xi_{t-1}),

and let

\displaystyle F_{t}^{+}\triangleq F({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t};\xi_{t}),\qquad F_{t-1}^{+}\triangleq F({\bm{y}}_{t-1}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t-1};\xi_{t-1}),

\displaystyle f_{t}^{+}\triangleq f({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t}),\qquad f_{t-1}^{+}\triangleq f({\bm{y}}_{t-1}+\delta{\bm{u}}_{t-1}).

Since
{\bm{u}}_{t}
and
\xi_{t}
are independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
, with
\mathbb{E}[{\bm{u}}_{t}]={\bm{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\bm{g}}_{\text{1pt},t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{t}^{+}{\bm{u}}_{t}-F_{t-1}^{+}{\bm{u}}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[f_{t}^{+}{\bm{u}}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{\delta}\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{u}}\sim{\rm Unif}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})}\left[f({\bm{y}}_{t}+\delta{\bm{u}}){\bm{u}}\right]=\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{y}}_{t}),

where the last identity is the standard randomized-smoothing gradient formula.

We define

\displaystyle Z_{t}\triangleq F_{t}^{+}-f_{t}^{+},\qquad Z_{t-1}\triangleq F_{t-1}^{+}-f_{t-1}^{+}.

Since
\left\|{\bm{u}}_{t}\right\|=1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\left\|{\bm{g}}_{\text{1pt},t}\right\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{t}-Z_{t-1}+f_{t}^{+}-f_{t-1}^{+}|^{2}\right].

Let

\displaystyle A_{t}\triangleq f_{t}^{+}-f_{t-1}^{+}.

Using
(a+b+c)^{2}\leq 3a^{2}+3b^{2}+3c^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\left\|{\bm{g}}_{\text{1pt},t}\right\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{t}-Z_{t-1}+A_{t}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\left(3\mathbb{E}[|Z_{t}|^{2}]+3\mathbb{E}[|Z_{t-1}|^{2}]+3\mathbb{E}[|A_{t}|^{2}]\right).

Assumption
1.1
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|Z_{t}|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2},\qquad\mathbb{E}[|Z_{t-1}|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\left\|{\bm{g}}_{\text{1pt},t}\right\|^{2}]\leq\frac{6d^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\delta^{2}}+\frac{3d^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[|f_{t}^{+}-f_{t-1}^{+}|^{2}\right].

Define the sphere-smoothed mean

\displaystyle f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}})\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{u}}\sim{\rm Unif}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})}\big[f({\bm{y}}+\delta{\bm{u}})\big].

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|f_{t}^{+}-f_{t-1}^{+}|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(f_{t}^{+}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})+\big(f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t})-f_{t-1}^{+}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Var}(f_{t}^{+}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})+2\big(f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t})-f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t-1})\big)^{2}+2\big(f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t-1})-f_{t-1}^{+}\big)^{2}.

Taking expectations gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|f_{t}^{+}-f_{t-1}^{+}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Var}(f_{t}^{+}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})\big]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\big(f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t})-f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t-1})\big)^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\big(f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t-1})-f_{t-1}^{+}\big)^{2}\right].

Now, for a fixed
{\bm{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the map

\displaystyle\phi_{{\bm{y}}}({\bm{u}})\triangleq f({\bm{y}}+\delta{\bm{u}}),\qquad{\bm{u}}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1},

is
(\delta L)
-Lipschitz on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
. Hence, Lemma
B.1
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\phi_{{\bm{y}}}({\bm{u}}))\leq\frac{16\sqrt{2\pi}\delta^{2}L^{2}}{d}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Var}(f_{t}^{+}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})\big]\leq\frac{16\sqrt{2\pi}\delta^{2}L^{2}}{d},\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[\big(f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t-1})-f_{t-1}^{+}\big)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{16\sqrt{2\pi}\delta^{2}L^{2}}{d}.

Moreover, Assumption
2.2
implies that
f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}
is
L
-Lipschitz such that

\displaystyle\big|f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t})-f_{\delta}^{{\mathbb{S}}}({\bm{y}}_{t-1})\big|\leq L\left\|{\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t-1}\right\|.

Substituting these bounds into the preceding display yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|f_{t}^{+}-f_{t-1}^{+}|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{48\sqrt{2\pi}\delta^{2}L^{2}}{d}+2L^{2}\mathbb{E}[\left\|{\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t-1}\right\|^{2}].

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\left\|{\bm{g}}_{\text{1pt},t}\right\|^{2}]\leq\frac{6d^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\delta^{2}}+144\sqrt{2\pi}dL^{2}+\frac{6d^{2}L^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\left\|{\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t-1}\right\|^{2}].

∎

\displaystyle\nabla f_{\delta}(\bar{{\bm{y}}})=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{u}}\sim{\rm Unif}({\mathbb{B}}^{d})}[\nabla f(\bar{{\bm{y}}}+\delta{\bm{u}})].

4.2

\displaystyle N_{\rm SZO}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\gamma+\delta L}{\delta(\epsilon/2)^{3}}\left(\frac{d^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\delta^{2}}+d^{2}L^{2}\right)\right).

Substituting

shows that both bounds reduce to

\gamma,\sigma,L,
and
L_{H}
are absorbed into the

∎

Theorem E.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
1.1
,

, and
2.2
hold,
then Algorithm
4
can find a
(\delta,\epsilon)

f

\displaystyle N_{\rm SZO}=\mathcal{O}\left((\gamma+\delta L)\left(d^{5/2}L\sigma^{2}\delta^{-3}+d^{3/2}L^{3}\delta^{-1}\right)\epsilon^{-4}\right)

stochastic zeroth-order oracle calls.

Proof.

Since
f_{\delta}
is
\beta_{\delta}
-smooth, for every
t
,

\displaystyle f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t+1})\leq f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})+\langle\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\rangle+\frac{\beta_{\delta}}{2}\left\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\|^{2}.

Substituting
{\bm{x}}_{t+1}={\bm{x}}_{t}-\eta\widehat{{\bm{g}}}_{t}
gives

\displaystyle f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t+1})\leq f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})-\eta\langle\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t}),\widehat{{\bm{g}}}_{t}\rangle+\frac{\beta_{\delta}\eta^{2}}{2}\left\|\widehat{{\bm{g}}}_{t}\right\|^{2}.

Taking conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
and using Lemma
E.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t+1})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle\leq f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})-\eta\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\|^{2}+\frac{\beta_{\delta}\eta^{2}}{2}\left(\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\|^{2}+\frac{V_{\delta}}{B}\right)

\displaystyle=f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})-\eta\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\delta}\eta}{2}\right)\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\|^{2}+\frac{\beta_{\delta}\eta^{2}V_{\delta}}{2B}.

Since
\eta\leq 1/\beta_{\delta}
, we have
1-\beta_{\delta}\eta/2\geq 1/2
, and hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t+1})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})-\frac{\eta}{2}\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\|^{2}+\frac{\beta_{\delta}\eta^{2}V_{\delta}}{2B}.

Taking total expectation and summing over
t=0,\ldots,T-1
yield

\displaystyle\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\|^{2}\big]\leq f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{0})-f_{\delta}^{*}+\frac{\beta_{\delta}\eta^{2}TV_{\delta}}{2B}.

By Proposition
2.1
(a) and Assumption
2.1

\displaystyle f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{0})-f_{\delta}^{*}\leq f({\bm{x}}_{0})-f^{*}+L\delta\leq\gamma+\delta L.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{2(\gamma+\delta L)}{\eta T}+\frac{\beta_{\delta}\eta V_{\delta}}{B}.

Since
t_{\rm out}
is sampled uniformly from
\{0,\ldots,T-1\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t_{\rm out}})\right\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{2(\gamma+\delta L)}{\eta T}+\frac{\beta_{\delta}\eta V_{\delta}}{B}.

Now choose

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{1}{\beta_{\delta}},\qquad B=\left\lceil\frac{2V_{\delta}}{\epsilon^{2}}\right\rceil,\qquad T=\left\lceil\frac{4\beta_{\delta}(\gamma+\delta L)}{\epsilon^{2}}\right\rceil.

\displaystyle\frac{2(\gamma+\delta L)}{\eta T}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2},\qquad\frac{\beta_{\delta}\eta V_{\delta}}{B}=\frac{V_{\delta}}{B}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2},

and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t_{\rm out}})\right\|^{2}\big]\leq\epsilon^{2}.

By Proposition
2.1
(e),

\displaystyle\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t_{\rm out}})\in\partial_{\delta}f({\bm{x}}_{t_{\rm out}}),

and therefore

\displaystyle{\rm dist}({\bm{0}},\partial_{\delta}f({\bm{x}}_{t_{\rm out}}))\leq\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t_{\rm out}})\right\|.

Taking expectations and applying Jensen’s inequality give

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[{\rm dist}({\bm{0}},\partial_{\delta}f({\bm{x}}_{t_{\rm out}}))\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t_{\rm out}})\right\|\big]\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\big[\left\|\nabla f_{\delta}({\bm{x}}_{t_{\rm out}})\right\|^{2}\big]\right)^{1/2}\leq\epsilon.

Finally, since each iteration uses
2B
stochastic zeroth-order oracle calls,

\displaystyle N_{\rm SZO}=2BT=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{(\gamma+\delta L)\beta_{\delta}V_{\delta}}{\epsilon^{4}}\right).

Substituting

\beta_{\delta}=\frac{c\sqrt{d}L}{\delta},\qquad V_{\delta}=\frac{d^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2\delta^{2}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}16\sqrt{2\pi}dL^{2}}

yields

\displaystyle N_{\rm SZO}=\mathcal{O}\left((\gamma+\delta L)\left(d^{5/2}L\sigma^{2}\delta^{-3}+d^{3/2}L^{3}\delta^{-1}\right)\epsilon^{-4}\right),

as claimed.

\tau

{\bm{x}}

In closed form, if
s={\bm{x}}_{\rm feat}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{\rm true}+b<0

{\bm{w}}_{\rm proj}={\bm{w}}_{\rm true}-\frac{s}{\|{\bm{x}}_{\rm feat}\|_{2}^{2}}{\bm{x}}_{\rm feat}.

\tilde{{\bm{w}}}={\bm{w}}_{\rm true}

or
\|{\bm{x}}_{\rm feat}\|_{2}=0

\tilde{{\bm{w}}}={\bm{w}}_{\rm true}
.

f({\bm{x}})=\mathbb{E}_{({\bm{w}}_{\rm true},y)\sim D_{\rm train}}\left[\max\left\{0,1-y\big({\bm{x}}_{\rm feat}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}({\bm{x}};{\bm{w}}_{\rm true})+b\big)\right\}\right].

F.2
Multi-product Pricing

Problem setting.

The pricing experiment uses the multi-product pricing model of
15
. There are
n=30
substitutable products and
m_{\rm buyer}=120
potential buyers. The decision variable is the price vector
{\bm{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, so the optimization dimension is
d=30
. We use four fixed weekly instances, corresponding to KSP-SP weeks 202610, 202611, 202612, and 202613. For each weekly instance, we take the top 30 recorded average confectionery prices and min-max normalize them into
[0.1,0.9]
to obtain the reference price vector
{\bm{\theta}}=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n})
.

Given a queried price vector
{\bm{x}}
, each buyer independently either purchases one product or chooses the outside option. Conditional on
{\bm{x}}
, the product probabilities follow a multinomial-logit model:

p_{i}({\bm{x}})=\frac{\exp\{\gamma_{i}(\theta_{i}-x_{i})\}}{a_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp\{\gamma_{j}(\theta_{j}-x_{j})\}},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

and the no-purchase probability is

p_{0}({\bm{x}})=\frac{a_{0}}{a_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp\{\gamma_{j}(\theta_{j}-x_{j})\}}.

We set
a_{0}=0.1n=3
and
\gamma_{i}=2\pi/(\sqrt{6}\theta_{i})
. The demand vector
{\bm{\xi}}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
is obtained by drawing
m_{\rm buyer}
multinomial choices with probabilities
(p_{1}({\bm{x}}),\ldots,p_{n}({\bm{x}}),p_{0}({\bm{x}}))
and retaining the product counts. Thus
\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\leq m_{\rm buyer}
.

For a realized demand vector
{\bm{\xi}}
, the seller’s revenue is

s({\bm{x}},{\bm{\xi}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\xi_{i},

and the production cost is separable,
c({\bm{\xi}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}(\xi_{i})
. As in
15
, each product has thresholds
l_{i}=0.5m_{\rm buyer}/n=2
and
u_{i}=1.5m_{\rm buyer}/n=6
. The per-product cost is piecewise linear, with a larger marginal cost in the high-demand regime:

c_{i}(z)=\begin{cases}2w_{i}z,&z\leq l_{i},\\
w_{i}(z-l_{i})+2w_{i}l_{i},&l_{i}<z\leq u_{i},\\
3w_{i}(z-u_{i})+w_{i}(u_{i}-l_{i})+2w_{i}l_{i},&z>u_{i},\end{cases}

where
w_{i}=\rho_{i}\theta_{i}
and
\rho_{i}\sim{\rm Unif}[0.25,0.5]
is drawn once and fixed within the instance. The induced demand distribution depends on
{\bm{x}}
, and the stochastic oracle returns the negative profit

F({\bm{x}};{\bm{\xi}})=-s({\bm{x}},{\bm{\xi}})+c({\bm{\xi}}),

where
{\bm{\xi}}\sim D({\bm{x}})
is the multinomial demand vector generated by the MNL model. The objective is

f({\bm{x}})=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}\sim D({\bm{x}})}[F({\bm{x}};{\bm{\xi}})].

For final evaluation and plots, we estimate
f({\bm{x}})
with an independent 1000-sample Monte Carlo average at the reported point. These evaluation samples are not counted as optimization oracle queries.
