Metric perturbations and deformations of k → \vec{k} -nondegenerate ℤ / 2 \mathbb{Z}/2 -harmonic 1-forms
Siqi He and Willem Adriaan Salm

Abstract
. We study metric perturbations and deformation theory for degenerate ℤ / 2 \mathbb{Z}/2 -harmonic 1 1 -forms. For a natural class of degenerate examples, we prove that after a suitable perturbation of the ambient Riemannian metric, the form can be deformed to a nearby non-degenerate ℤ / 2 \mathbb{Z}/2 -harmonic 1 1 -form. Our argument combines analysis of the leading coefficients in the local expansion under metric perturbations with a quantitative Nash–Moser implicit function theorem.



1.
Introduction

\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms have emerged as a natural analytic object in compactification problems in gauge theory.
Starting from Taubes’ analytic compactification of moduli spaces of flat
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
connections on
3
-manifolds, these forms appear as ideal boundary data describing the limiting behaviour of sequences of connections; see
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
.
They have since played a role in related analytic and geometric questions, providing a useful bridge between gauge theory, low-dimensional topology, and geometric analysis; see for instance
[
2
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
13
,
14
,
10
,
12
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
26
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold.
Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a co-oriented codimension-two submanifold, and let
\mathcal{I}
be a flat real line bundle over
M\setminus\Sigma
with monodromy
-1
along small loops linking
\Sigma
.
A
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form is a triple
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega)
, where
\omega
is a smooth
\mathcal{I}
-valued harmonic
1
-form on
M\setminus\Sigma
such that
|\omega|
extends continuously across
\Sigma
.
Equivalently,
\omega
may be viewed locally as a
\pm
-valued harmonic
1
-form with singular set
\Sigma
, reflecting the nontrivial monodromy of
\mathcal{I}
.

Assume
\Sigma
is smooth.
Results of
[
6
,
16
]
imply that
\omega
admits a local expansion. Namely, if
N
is the normal bundle of
\Sigma
, then
\mathcal{I}
determines a square root
N^{\frac{1}{2}}
in the appropriate sense. Given a complex normal coordinate
z
on
N
and a local coordinate
t
on
\Sigma
, one has

\omega=\operatorname{d}\operatorname{Re}\bigl(f_{k}(t)\,z^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)+o\!\left(|z|^{k}\right),

for some integer
k\geq 1
and a local section
f_{k}
of
N^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}
.

Definition 1.1

(
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate)
.

Given a connected component
\Sigma_{i}
of
\Sigma
, we say that
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega)
is
k
-nondegenerate along
\Sigma_{i}
, if
k\geq 1
is the smallest index for which
f_{k}\not\equiv 0
and
f_{k}
is nowhere vanishing along
\Sigma_{i}
.
Moreover, given a multi-index
\vec{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{b_{0}(\Sigma)})
, we say that
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega)
is
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
if for each connected component
\Sigma_{i}\subset\Sigma
, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-harmonic 1-form
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega)
is
k_{i}
-nondegenerate
along
\Sigma_{i}
.

The case
\vec{k}=(1,\ldots,1)
is the classical non-degeneracy condition used in the literature (see for example
[
6
,
18
]
), while
k_{i}\geq 1
describes a controlled finite-order degeneracy.
For classically non-degenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms, the resulting deformation theory leads to a well-posed local moduli problem and supports transversality and gluing arguments, with applications ranging from gauge-theoretic compactifications to calibrated geometry; see
[
6
,
18
,
26
]
.

From an analytic point of view, the non-degeneracy condition is crucial: It provides uniform control of the linearized deformation problem near the singular locus.
When non-degeneracy fails, this uniform control breaks down and the standard deformation theory no longer applies.
Despite the progress above, degeneracies are common and often unavoidable.
This paper addresses the following question.

Question 1.2
.

Given a degenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form, can one perturb the ambient metric and deform the form to remove the degeneracy?

A basic obstruction is topological: the relevant leading coefficient naturally lives in a fractional power
N^{-3/2}
of the complex normal bundle
N\to\Sigma
. If
N^{-3/2}
is nontrivial then any section must vanish somewhere, forcing degeneracy.
Thus, one cannot hope to avoid degenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms in general, and the deformation theory in the degenerate regime requires new ideas.

In this paper we give an affirmative answer to Question
1.2
for
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms. Namely,

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega_{0})
be a
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form on
(M,g_{0})
, such that
N^{-\frac{3}{2}}
is trivial.
Then there exists a one-parameter family of metrics
g_{s}
extending
g_{0}
, such that for each
s\in(0,1)
there exist non-degenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega_{s})
with respect to
g_{s}
, satisfying the following:

(i)

In the cohomology class of
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic 1-forms,
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{s}
represent the same element.

(ii)

Given any compact set
K\subset M\setminus\Sigma_{0}
, the family
g_{s}
can be chosen so that
g_{s}|_{K}=g_{0}|_{K}
.

The proof has two main steps.
First, after a small perturbation of the ambient metric, we deform a
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate form to one with improved leading behaviour (allowing a controlled additional singular term).
Second, we apply a refined version of the Nash–Moser implicit function theorem to carry out a Donaldson-type deformation argument for the coupled metric–form problem.

A main difficulty here is quantitative rather than conceptual. Nash–Moser techniques play a central role in the deformation theory of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms and have been used extensively in the literature, see for example
[
6
,
5
,
18
,
20
]
.
In our setting, however, the standard Nash–Moser framework is not sufficiently precise. We need a finer version in which all constants are tracked at different small scales, so that the admissible solvability radius can be determined explicitly.

In our case, there are two main competing scales we need to keep track of. First, for a generic perturbation of
\Sigma
, the norm
|\omega|
will not extend continuously over
\Sigma
. For the perturbation argument to succeed we need to measure the failure of this extension. Secondly, we need to estimate the size of the right inverse of the linearized operator. Because
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega_{0})
is degenerate, this estimate will worsen when
g_{s}
converges to
g_{0}
.

Only after keeping track of this balance can one determine an explicit neighbourhood in the target space for which invertibility holds.
In our setting this requires tracking the dependence of all Nash–Moser constants on the small scale dictated by the vanishing order, which cannot be obtained from a black-box choice of weighted Sobolev spaces.
This is the reason we work with a Nash–Moser scheme, where all tame estimates are formulated with explicit control of the small parameter at each step. For this we give an explicit proof in Appendix
A
.

2.
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic 1-form over a closed manifold

In this section we introduce the analytic foundations of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms,
as developed in
[
6
,
24
]
and refined in subsequent work. See also
[
2
,
9
]
for further background and additional references. We first revisit
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms and recall their
L^{2}
-Hodge theoretic interpretation via the canonical double branched covering. Secondly, we explain their local expansions and decay behaviour near the singular set. Finally, we finish this sections with a few examples.

2.1.
Hodge theory for
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic 1-forms

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold, and let
\Sigma\subset M
be a co-oriented
embedded submanifold of codimension two. Let
\mathcal{I}
be a flat real line bundle over

M\setminus\Sigma
with monodromy
-1
around small loops linking
\Sigma
.
An
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form
is a triple
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega)
, where

\omega\in\Omega^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma;\>\mathcal{I})
satisfies:

(1)

\operatorname{d}\omega=0
and
\operatorname{d}^{\ast}\!\omega=0
(Here
\operatorname{d}
is computed using the flat connection on

\mathcal{I}
and
d^{\ast}
is the formal adjoint with respect to
g
), and

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\omega|^{2}<\infty
.

The notion of
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms is weaker than that of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms
since it does not require
|\omega|
to extend continuously across
\Sigma
. Nevertheless, there is a useful Hodge theory in this setting.

Let
\pi\colon\widehat{M}\to M\setminus\Sigma
be the unique double cover that trivializes
\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{I}
,
and let
\hat{g}:=\pi^{\ast}g
be the induced cone metric on
\widehat{M}
. Denote by
H^{1}_{-}(\widehat{M})
the
anti-invariant part of the de Rham cohomology under the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
action of the deck transformation. For an
L^{2}
-bounded

\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega)
, the pullback
\hat{\omega}:=\pi^{\ast}\omega
is an
ordinary
1
-form on
\widehat{M}
, anti-invariant under the deck transformation, and satisfies

\operatorname{d}\hat{\omega}=0,\qquad\operatorname{d}^{\ast}_{\hat{g}}\hat{\omega}=0.

By the
L^{2}
Hodge theorem for Lipschitz metrics,
\hat{\omega}
determines a cohomology class

[\hat{\omega}]\in H^{1}_{-}(\widehat{M})
.

Conversely, for every
\sigma\in H^{1}_{-}(\widehat{M})
there exists a unique
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic

1
-form
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega)
such that
[\pi^{\ast}\omega]=\sigma

[
6
,
15
]
. A convenient way to see existence is as follows: Let
U
be a tubular neighbourhood of
\Sigma
in
\widehat{M}
. Using the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-antisymmetry one has
H^{1}_{-}(U)=0
, and
so
\sigma
admits a representative
\omega_{\mathrm{cpt}}\in\Omega^{1}(\widehat{M})
that is compactly
supported away from
\Sigma
. To obtain the harmonic representative, solve

(1)

\Delta_{\hat{g}}u=\operatorname{d}^{\ast}\omega_{\mathrm{cpt}}

for an anti-invariant function
u\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{M})
. Then

\widehat{\omega}:=\omega_{\mathrm{cpt}}-\operatorname{d}u

is
L^{2}
-harmonic on
(\widehat{M},\hat{g})
representing
\sigma
.
The push forward
\pi_{*}(\hat{\omega})
is an
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form on
M
.

2.2.
Local expansions of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic 1-forms

A basic analytic input in this subject is that
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic objects admit precise asymptotic
expansions near the singular set; see

[
2
,
6
,
9
,
16
]
.
We briefly recall the form of these expansions and the associated leading coefficients.

We first set up some local coordinates.
Let
t=(t_{i})
be local coordinates on an open set in
\Sigma
, and let
U
be the corresponding
tubular neighbourhood in
M
. On
U\setminus\Sigma
we use polar normal coordinates

(r,\phi,t)
, where
r
is the distance to
\Sigma
and
\phi
is the angular variable in the
oriented normal plane. Setting
z=re^{i\phi}
identifies the oriented normal bundle
N
with a
complex line bundle. Using
\mathcal{I}
, we can also consider the bundle
N^{1/2}
and equip it with the local coordinate
z^{1/2}=r^{1/2}e^{i\phi/2}
.

In these coordinates we can write down the asymptotic expansion near
\Sigma
.
Explicitly, if
u\in\Gamma(\mathcal{I})
is harmonic in a neighbourhood of
\Sigma
, then
u
admits a
polyhomogeneous expansion of the form

u\sim\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{q=0}^{p}u_{pq}(t,\phi)\,r^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\,(\log r)^{q}.

Concretely, if

u_{k}:=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\sum_{q=0}^{p}u_{pq}(t,\phi)\,r^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\,(\log r)^{q},

then
u-u_{k}=o(r^{k+1})
as
r\to 0
, i.e.

\lim_{r\to 0}\,\bigl\|r^{-k-1}(u-u_{k})\bigr\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma\times\{r\}\times S^{1})}=0.

Donaldson
[
6
]
also gave a description of the leading order terms of an
L^{2}
-bounded

\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form
\omega
. Namely, on
U
there exists a complex-valued function
A(t)
such that

\omega=\operatorname{d}\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A(t)\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)+o(1).

Although this expression uses local coordinates, the coefficient
A
is intrinsically a section of

N^{-1/2}
. Moreover,
A\equiv 0
if and only if
\omega
is a genuine
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form (i.e. when
|\omega|
extends
continuously across
\Sigma
). In that case there exists a global section

B\in\Gamma(N^{-3/2})
such that

\omega=\operatorname{d}\operatorname{Re}\bigl(B(t)\,z^{\frac{3}{2}}\bigr)+o(r).

A
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form is nondegenerate in the classical sense if
B
is nowhere vanishing.

Remark 2.2
.

Even when
A\not\equiv 0
, the next coefficient
B
can still be defined
[
6
]
.
In general there is a section
\mu\in\Gamma(N^{-1})
, determined by the mean curvature of
\Sigma
,
such that the expansion takes the form

(2)

\omega=d\,\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(A(t)\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}+B(t)\,z^{\frac{3}{2}}\bigr)-\frac{1}{2}\,d\,\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(A(t)\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\,\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(\mu(t)\,z\bigr)+o(r).

We refer to
A
and
B
as the
A
–coefficient
and
B
–coefficient
of
\omega
.

The coefficients
A
and
B
depend on the cohomology class of
\omega
and on the metric
g
.
When it is helpful to emphasize this, we write
A(g,\cdot)
and
B(g,\cdot)
, or

A(g,[\omega],\cdot)
and
B(g,[\omega],\cdot)
respectively.

Finally, to compare these coefficients under perturbations of
(g,\Sigma)
, one needs a
canonical identification of the normal data. Donaldson showed that, if
g
is sufficiently close
to
g_{0}
and
\Sigma
is sufficiently close to
\Sigma_{0}
, then there exists a diffeomorphism
identifying the singular sets and their normal structures
[
6
, Proposition 4.2]
.
This allows one to view the expansions on a fixed normal bundle (and with respect to a fixed
reference metric) when studying variations.

2.3.
Decay behaviour and topological constraints.

From the asymptotic expansion, one can read off the decay behaviour of
\omega
near its singular set

\Sigma
. Namely, if
\omega
is a genuine
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form then
|\omega|^{-1}=\mathcal{O}(r^{-1/2})

near
\Sigma
, i.e. there exists a constant
C>0
such that
|\omega|>C\,r^{1/2}
near
\Sigma
.
Therefore, in this case
\Sigma
is an isolated zero of
\omega
.

The same behaviour holds for
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms. In this case there
exists an integer
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
|\omega|^{-1}=\mathcal{O}(r^{\,l})
near
\Sigma
. Moreover, this decay
behaviour is the only feature of
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms that we will use in
this article.

However, such a decay behaviour does not occur for all
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms. For
example:

Example 2.3
.

On
\mathbb{C}_{z=re^{i\theta}}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}
, consider
\omega:=\operatorname{d}\operatorname{Re}\bigl(t\,z^{\frac{3}{2}}\bigr).

This is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form with singular set
\Sigma=\{(0,t)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}\}
. However,
the zero set of
\omega
is not isolated; more precisely,

|\omega|^{-1}(0)=\{(0,t)\}\cup\{(r=0,\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{3}+\tfrac{2m\pi}{3})\},

for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
\omega
is not
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate.

\vec{k}
-nondegeneracy imposes not only an analytic condition, but also a mild and useful
restriction on the topology of the normal bundle. Namely, if
\omega
is
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
along
\Sigma
, then
N^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}
admits a nowhere vanishing section and hence is a trivial
complex line bundle. Equivalently,
(2k+1)c_{1}(N)=0\in H^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}).

Moreover, if a
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form can be deformed to a

1
-nondegenerate one, then we additionally require
3c_{1}(N)=0
. Consequently,
\gcd(3,2k+1)\,c_{1}(N)=0,

and note that
\gcd(3,2k+1)\in\{1,3\}
. In particular, if
\dim M=3,4
, then for a

\vec{k}
-nondegenerate solution,
N
must be a trivial bundle.

2.4.
Examples of
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic 1-forms.

Finally, we present some known examples of
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms.

Example 2.4

(On a Riemann surface)
.

Let
M
be a Riemann surface with a complex structure induced by
g
and let
q\in H^{0}(M,K_{M}^{2})
be a holomorphic quadratic differential.
Let
\Sigma
be the set of zeros of
q
of odd vanishing order.
The real part of a local holomorphic square root
\sqrt{q}
, which is well-defined up to sign, defines a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form
\omega
on
M\setminus\Sigma
.

For any zero
p\in M
of
q
of odd vanishing order, and any choice of local holomorphic coordinate
z
centred at
p
, one can write

q=a_{p}\,z^{2k+1}\,dz\otimes dz+\text{higher order terms},

where
a_{p}\neq 0
. This implies
\omega
is
k
-nondegenerate along
p
.

Example 2.5

(Ellipsoid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
)
.

There exists a
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
whose singular set
\Sigma

is an ellipsoid
[
3
]
.

Example 2.6

(On a Seifert-fibered
3
-manifold)
.

Let
\pi:Y\to\Sigma
be a Seifert-fibered
3
-manifold, where
\Sigma
is a
2
-dimensional orbifold
equipped with an orbifold metric
g_{\Sigma}
. Let
i\eta
be the connection
1
-form of a
constant-curvature
U(1)
connection on
Y
, and consider the Riemannian metric

g_{Y}=\eta^{2}+\pi^{*}g_{\Sigma}

on

. Given an orbifold quadratic differential
q
on

, the construction in

[
11
, Section 4.3]
produces a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic

-form on

,
which is
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate along the singular set.

3.
Metric perturbations of
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic 1-forms

In this section we study how the
A
– and
B
–coefficients of an
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic

1
-form vary under perturbations of the ambient metric. We show that suitable metric
perturbations allow one to prescribe the Taylor jet of the
B
–coefficient along
\Sigma
,
while forcing the
A
–coefficient to vanish to arbitrarily high order.

Before stating this precisely, we recall the notion of
Schwartz functions
. In what follows,
we will repeatedly encounter remainder terms that are negligible to all orders as
r\to 0
near

\Sigma
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
U
be a neighbourhood of
\Sigma
, and let
r
denote the distance function to
\Sigma
.
A section
f\in\Gamma(\mathcal{I}|_{U\setminus\Sigma})
is called
Schwartz
on

U\setminus\Sigma
if
f
is smooth on
U\setminus\Sigma
and, for every pair of
nonnegative integers
p,q
, one has

\sup_{U\setminus\Sigma}\,\bigl|\,r^{-p}\nabla^{q}f\,\bigr|<\infty,

where
\nabla
denotes the covariant derivative induced by
g
together with the flat
connection on
\mathcal{I}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{S}
the space of Schwartz sections of
\mathcal{I}
on
U\setminus\Sigma
.

In this section we prove the following metric-variation statement.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(M,g_{0})
be a closed Riemannian manifold, and let
\Sigma\subset M
be a smoothly embedded
codimension-two submanifold with oriented normal bundle. Let
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega_{0})
be a

\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form. Assume:

(i)

|\omega_{0}|>0
on
U\setminus\Sigma
for some tubular neighbourhood
U
of
\Sigma
;

(ii)

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}
, one has
f/|\omega_{0}|\in\mathcal{S}
.

Then, for any integer
m\geq 1
and any prescribed smooth sections

\tilde{B}_{i}\in\Gamma\!\bigl(N^{-(i+\frac{1}{2})}\bigr),\qquad 1\leq i\leq m,

there exists a one-parameter family of smooth metrics
\{g_{s}\}_{s\in(0,1)}
with
g_{s=0}=g_{0}

together with a family of
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega_{s})
on

(M,g_{s})
representing the same cohomology class
[\omega_{s}]=[\omega_{0}]\in H^{1}_{-}(\hat{M})
, such that
the corresponding leading coefficients
A_{s},B_{s}
in (
2
) satisfy

(3)

\left.\partial_{s}^{i}A_{s}\right|_{s=0}=0,\qquad\left.\partial_{s}^{i}B_{s}\right|_{s=0}=\tilde{B}_{i},

for all
1\leq i\leq m
and all
t\in\Sigma
. Moreover, for any fixed compact set
K\subset M\setminus\Sigma
, the family can be chosen so that
g_{s}|_{K}=g_{0}|_{K}
for all
s
.

The assumptions in Proposition
3.2
are only needed to justify
division by
|\omega_{0}|
in the inductive step; in particular, they are automatic in the

\vec{k}
-nondegenerate situation considered later. The remainder of this section is devoted to
the proof of Proposition
3.2
. The proof proceeds by induction on
the jet order and combines linearized variation formulas, a local exactness statement for

\mathbb{Z}/2
-equivariant closed forms near
\Sigma
, and the construction of local models with prescribed
leading terms and Schwartz Laplacian.

3.1.
General Variation formulas

As a start, we will compute the variation formula under metric perturbations.
For this we first need to set up some notation.
Fix a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega_{0})
on a closed Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{0})
of dimension
n
.
Let
g_{s}
be a 1-parameter family of Riemann metric, that begins at
g_{0}
.
Let
*_{s}
be the Hodge star operator and
\Delta_{g_{s}}
be the Laplace operator defined by
g_{s}
. Like before, let
\widehat{M}
be the double cover over
M
branched along
\Sigma
that trivializes
\mathcal{I}
.

Fix
\omega_{cpt}\in\Omega^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma;\>\mathcal{I})
such that
[\omega_{0}]=[\omega_{cpt}]\in H^{1}_{-}(\widehat{M})
and
\omega_{cpt}=0

Let

-harmonic 1-form on

To denote the derivatives, write

Also, let

.
Finally, consider the 2-tensor

\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{s}:=g_{s}^{(m)}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{Tr}_{g_{s}}(g_{s}^{(m)})\,g_{s}\;\in\;\Gamma(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}M)).

Proposition 3.3
.

The first derivative
u_{s}^{(1)}
satisfies

(6)

\operatorname{d}^{*_{s}}\!\big(\!\operatorname{d}u_{s}^{(1)}-\widehat{T}^{\langle 1\rangle}_{s}(\omega_{s}^{\sharp},\cdot)\big)=0.

More generally, for each

(7)

d^{*_{s}}\big(\!\operatorname{d}u_{s}^{(m)}-\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{s}(\omega_{s}^{\sharp},\cdot)\;+\;L^{m}_{s}\big)=0,

is a linear combination of lower-order terms depending on

g_{s},\;g_{s}^{(1)},\ldots,g_{s}^{(m-1)},\qquad\nabla u_{s}^{(1)},\ldots,\nabla u_{s}^{(m-1)},\qquad\omega_{s}^{\sharp},

and every term in

contains at least one factor of

for some

Proof.

Recall that for any
1
-form

\partial_{s}(*_{s})v=*_{s}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{Tr}_{g_{s}}(g_{s}^{(1)})\,v\;-\;g_{s}^{(1)}\!\big(v_{s}^{\sharp},\cdot\big)\right),

By (

\operatorname{d}*_{s}\>\omega_{s}=\operatorname{d}*_{s}(du_{s}+\omega_{\mathrm{cpt}})=0

Differentiating with respect to

\operatorname{d}*_{s}\operatorname{d}u_{s}^{(1)}=-\operatorname{d}(\partial_{s}*_{s})\>\omega_{s}=\operatorname{d}*_{s}\!\left(g_{s}^{(1)}(\omega^{\sharp},\cdot)-\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{Tr}_{g_{s}}(g_{s}^{(1)})\,\omega_{s}\right),

where the last equality follows from (

For higher derivatives, differentiate (

The term

All remaining terms arise from differentiating

The unique term containing

\Delta_{g_{s}}u_{s}^{(m)}=\operatorname{d}^{*_{s}}\!\left(\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{s}(\omega_{s}^{\sharp},\cdot)+L^{m}_{s}\right),

∎

Notice that

\operatorname{Tr}(\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{s})=\operatorname{Tr}(g^{(m)}_{s})-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(g^{(m)}_{s})\operatorname{Tr}(g_{s})=\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)\operatorname{Tr}(g^{(m)}_{s}).

Hence, if

g^{(m)}_{s}=\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{s}+\frac{1}{2-n}\operatorname{Tr}(\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{s})g_{s}.

Therefore, if we find a satisfying solution of (
7

\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{0}

10
).

Instead of solving for
u^{(m)}_{0}
in terms of
g^{(m)}_{0}
, we will find
g^{(m)}_{0}
in terms of
u^{(m)}_{0}
. Using the De Rham cohomology on

, one can show there exists a family
\{\sigma^{m}_{0}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{I})
, such that
\operatorname{d}^{*}\!\sigma^{m}_{0}=0
and

(11)

\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{0}(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},\cdot)=\operatorname{d}u_{0}^{(m)}+\sigma^{m}_{0}+L^{m}_{0}.

We claim that wherever
\omega_{0}\not=0
, (
11
) can always be solved for
g^{(m)}_{0}
using the following canonical choice:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\alpha\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{I})
and define the symmetric
2
-tensor

T\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}T^{*}(M\setminus\Sigma))
by

T:=\frac{\omega_{0}\otimes\alpha+\alpha\otimes\omega_{0}}{|\omega_{0}|^{2}}.

Let
\widehat{T}:=T-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(T)\,g
. Then, wherever
\omega_{0}\not=0
,

\widehat{T}(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},\cdot)=\alpha.

Proof.

For any vector field
X
we have

T(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},X)=\frac{\omega_{0}(\omega_{0}^{\sharp})\alpha(X)+\alpha(\omega_{0}^{\sharp})\omega_{0}(X)}{|\omega_{0}|^{2}}=\alpha(X)+\frac{\langle\alpha,\omega_{0}\rangle}{|\omega_{0}|^{2}}\,\omega_{0}(X).

Since
\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(\omega_{0}\otimes\alpha)=\langle\omega_{0},\alpha\rangle
and
\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(\alpha\otimes\omega_{0})=\langle\alpha,\omega_{0}\rangle
,
we have
\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(T)=\frac{2\langle\alpha,\omega_{0}\rangle}{|\omega_{0}|^{2}}
, hence

\widehat{T}(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},X)=T(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},X)-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(T)\,g(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},X)=\alpha(X).

∎

Our goal is to find a solution for
\widehat{T}_{0}^{\langle m\rangle}
that smoothly extend over the whole of
M
. Away from
\Sigma
, we can use bump functions to make
\widehat{T}_{0}^{\langle m\rangle}
well-defined. Near
\Sigma
this is not trivial. For example, a pointwise bound of (
11
) for the first derivative yields,

\left|\operatorname{d}u_{0}^{(1)}+\sigma^{1}_{0}\right|\leq|\widehat{T}^{\langle 1\rangle}_{0}|\cdot|\omega_{0}|

Because we want to control
B_{s}
in our variation,
u^{(1)}_{0}=\mathcal{O}(r^{3/2})
. Also, we want that
\widehat{T}^{\langle 1\rangle}_{0}
extends to a smooth section over a compact manifold, and thus
\widehat{T}^{\langle 1\rangle}_{0}=\mathcal{O}(1)
. Finally, we need an estimate on
|\omega_{0}|^{-1}
near
\Sigma
. For a degenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic 1-form there is no restriction on
|\omega_{0}|^{-1}
, but for
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic 1-forms, we have

|\omega_{0}|^{-1}=\mathcal{O}\left(r^{\frac{1}{2}-\lceil\vec{k}\rceil}\right).

Therefore, for the first derivative we have to balance
\sigma^{1}_{0}
such that

\left|\mathcal{O}(r^{\frac{1}{2}})+\sigma^{1}_{0}\right|=\mathcal{O}(1)\cdot\mathcal{O}\left(r^{\lceil\vec{k}\rceil-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

To determine the freedom we have in
\sigma^{1}_{0}
, we first need to consider the cohomology of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant forms.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
U\subset\widehat{M}
be a tubular neighbourhood of the singular set
\Sigma
. Any closed form
\eta
on
U
that is anti-invariant under the deck transformation is exact.

Proof.

Retracting
U
to the unit circle bundle
\pi:S\to\Sigma
, we consider the Gysin exact sequence:

\cdots\longrightarrow H^{k}(\Sigma)\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}}H^{k}(U)\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}}H^{k-1}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow\cdots,

where
\pi_{*}
denotes integration along the fibre. Let
\tau:U\to U
be the non-trivial deck transformation. Since
\tau
acts on the
S^{1}
-fibers by rotation by
\pi
, it preserves the orientation of the fibres. Consequently, the integration map satisfies
\pi_{*}\circ\tau^{*}=\pi_{*}
.

Let
[\eta]\in H^{k}(U)
be an anti-invariant class, satisfying
\tau^{*}[\eta]=-[\eta]
. Applying the integration map, we find:

\pi_{*}([\eta])=\pi_{*}(\tau^{*}[\eta])=-\pi_{*}([\eta]).

Thus,
\pi_{*}([\eta])=0
. By exactness,
[\eta]=\pi^{*}(\alpha)
for some
\alpha\in H^{k}(\Sigma)
. Since the projection
\pi
is
\tau
-invariant (i.e.,
\pi\circ\tau=\pi
), the image of
\pi^{*}
lies entirely in the invariant subspace:

\tau^{*}[\eta]=\tau^{*}(\pi^{*}\alpha)=(\pi\circ\tau)^{*}\alpha=\pi^{*}\alpha=[\eta].

Comparing this with the assumption
\tau^{*}[\eta]=-[\eta]
, we conclude that
[\eta]=0
.
∎

This lemma shows that
\sigma^{m}_{0}
is co-exact on a tubular neighbourhood of
\Sigma
. Therefore, on this tubular neighbourhood, there exists a family
\{\tau^{m}_{0}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{I})
, such that

\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{0}(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},\cdot)=\operatorname{d}u_{0}^{(m)}+\operatorname{d}^{*}\!\tau^{m}_{0}+L^{m}_{0}.

3.2.
Perturbation using locally harmonic function

Before proving Proposition
3.2
, we first consider a simpler situation, closely related to
[

]
, in which the metric variation is constant near the singular set. More precisely, let

be a small
neighbourhood of
\Sigma

on
U
.
Assume in addition that
|\omega_{0}|>0
on

. Under these hypotheses, the
deformation problem reduces to a local analytic question whether there exist

\mathcal{I}
-valued harmonic functions near
\Sigma
with prescribed leading asymptotics. Explicitly,

Proposition 3.6
.

Pick
\widetilde{A}\in\Gamma(N^{-1/2})
and
\widetilde{B}\in\Gamma(N^{-3/2})
.
There exists a family
g_{s}
such that for (
4
) the leading expansion satisfies

u_{0}^{(1)}=\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{A}\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}+\widetilde{B}\,z^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{A}\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\operatorname{Re}\left(\mu\,z\right)+o(r^{2})

if and only if there exist a neighbourhood
U
of
\Sigma
and a harmonic section
f\in\Gamma(\mathcal{I}_{U})
with

f=\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{A}\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}+\widetilde{B}\,z^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{A}\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\operatorname{Re}\left(\mu\,z\right)+o(r^{2}).

Proof.

Only if:

Since
g_{s}
is supported away from
\Sigma
, differentiating (
4
) at
s=0
gives

\Delta_{g_{0}}\dot{u}_{0}=0
on a neighbourhood of
\Sigma

Thus,
\dot{u}_{0}
is locally harmonic near
\Sigma
, and its leading expansion is of the required form.

If:

Conversely, assume we are given such a harmonic
f
on
U
.
Since
\operatorname{d}\>(*\operatorname{d}f)=0
, the
(n-1)
-form
*\operatorname{d}f
is closed.
By Lemma
3.5
, there exists
h\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{U})
such that

\operatorname{d}f+\operatorname{d}^{*}h=0.

Let
\chi
be a cutoff function supported in
U
with
\chi\equiv 1
near
\Sigma
.
Let
g_{s}=g_{0}+s\>T^{\langle 1\rangle}_{0}
with

(12)

T^{\langle 1\rangle}_{0}:=\frac{2\,\omega_{0}\cdot\bigl(\operatorname{d}(\chi f)+\operatorname{d}^{*}(\chi h)\bigr)}{|\omega_{0}|^{2}}.

By construction,

is supported in an annular region (where

) and so it extends smoothly over

.
Moreover, Lemma
3.4
applied on (

\operatorname{d}^{*}\widehat{T}_{0}^{\langle 1\rangle}(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},\ldots)=\Delta_{g_{0}}(\chi f).

Then Proposition

\Delta_{g_{0}}(\dot{u}_{0})=\operatorname{d}^{*}\widehat{T}_{0}^{\langle 1\rangle}(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},\ldots)=\Delta_{g_{0}}(\chi f).

Hence,
\Delta_{g_{0}}(\dot{u}_{0}-\chi f)=0

By

, any bounded

-valued harmonic section on

.
Therefore, they have the same asymptotic expansion.
∎

One can also prescribe higher-order derivatives of the leading coefficients, provided one has enough locally harmonic sections. For this we use that the error
L^{m}_{s}
is compactly supported away from
\sigma
whenever

near
\Sigma
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and pick
\widetilde{A}^{1},\ldots\widetilde{A}^{m}\in\Gamma(N^{-1/2})
and
\widetilde{B}^{1},\ldots\widetilde{B}^{m}\in\Gamma(N^{-3/2})
. There exists a family

such that for (
4
) the leading expansion satisfies

u_{0}^{(i)}=\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{A}^{i}\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}+\widetilde{B}^{i}\,z^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{A}^{i}\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\operatorname{Re}\left(\mu\,z\right)+o(r^{2})

for all

if and only if there exist a neighbourhood

of
\Sigma
and a family of harmonic sections

with

f^{i}=\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{A}^{i}\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}+\widetilde{B}^{i}\,z^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{A}^{i}\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\operatorname{Re}\left(\mu\,z\right)+o(r^{2}).

We construct tensors
T_{i}
so that
g_{s}=g_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{s^{i}}{i!}\,T_{i}
, and we proceed by induction.

The case

is given by Proposition
3.6
.
Assume
T_{i}
has been constructed for all

.
Then in (
7
) the term
L_{0}^{m}
is well-defined, supported away from

and smooth.

Define

(13)

\begin{split}T^{\langle m\rangle}_{0}:=\frac{2\,\omega_{0}\cdot\bigl(\operatorname{d}(\chi f_{m})+\operatorname{d}^{*}(\chi h_{m})-L_{0}^{m}\bigr)}{|\omega_{0}|^{2}}.\end{split}

By construction this is a smooth section of
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}M)
.
Moreover, Lemma
3.4
implies

\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{0}(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},\ldots)=\operatorname{d}(\chi f)+\operatorname{d}^{*}(\chi h)-L_{0}^{m}.

Therefore,

\Delta_{g_{0}}\left(u^{(m)}_{0}\right)=\operatorname{d}^{*}\!\left(\widehat{T}^{\langle m\rangle}_{0}(\omega_{0}^{\sharp},\ldots)+L_{0}^{m}\right)=\Delta_{g_{0}}(\chi f).

Since

is bounded, the same argument as above implies

, which gives the desired identities for
\partial_{s}^{m}A_{s}
and
\partial_{s}^{m}B_{s}
.
∎

3.3.
Schwartz approximation and deformations

Given
B\in\Gamma(N^{-3/2})
, one should not expect
B
to arise as the leading coefficient of
an
exact
locally harmonic section near
\Sigma
for an arbitrary metric
g
; this is a
genuinely metric-dependent constraint. For instance, when
g
is real-analytic, one expects
(though we are not aware of a proof) that locally harmonic sections have real-analytic leading
coefficients along
\Sigma
. In particular, a non-real-analytic section
B
should not occur as
the leading term of an exact harmonic expansion. For this reason, the natural goal is to solve the
harmonic equation
up to infinite order
near
\Sigma
: we prescribe the leading behaviour while
allowing an error that is Schwartz along
\Sigma
.

In this subsection, working in a neighbourhood
U
of
\Sigma\subset M
, we construct a

Schwartz approximation
with prescribed leading expansion which solves the harmonic equation
to infinite order near
\Sigma
. The construction is standard in the edge calculus and relies on
a Borel-type extension; see
[
9
,
16
]
. Moreover, this Schwartz approximation will serve as an input for the metric deformations constructed later.

Proposition 3.8
.

Given
\widetilde{B}\in\Gamma(N^{-3/2})
, there exists a polyhomogeneous section

u\in\Gamma(\mathcal{I}|_{U\setminus\Sigma})
such that
u=\widetilde{B}\,z^{3/2}+o(r^{2})
and

\Delta u\in\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

The construction is standard in the edge calculus: we first build a formal polyhomogeneous approximation and then use the Borel lemma to obtain an actual polyhomogeneous section with the desired asymptotics.

In local Fermi coordinates
(r,\theta,t)
near
\Sigma
, we write the Laplacian as
\Delta=\Delta_{0}+E
, where

\Delta_{0}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\partial_{\theta}^{2}+r^{2}\Delta_{\Sigma}
, and
E
is an error term which can be expressed schematically as a linear combination of

\mathcal{O}(r^{-1})\{(r\partial)^{2},\partial_{\theta}^{2},r^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}\}
.

Let
L_{0}=(r\partial_{r})^{2}+\partial_{\theta}^{2}
.
For
h=r^{k_{1}}e^{ik_{2}\theta}
, the equation
L_{0}f=h
is solvable after allowing suitable
\log r
terms:
if
k_{1}\neq|k_{2}|
, one may take
f=\frac{1}{k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}h
, while if
k_{1}=|k_{2}|
, one may take
f=\frac{1}{2k_{1}}(\log r)\,h
.
More generally, if
h=P_{k_{0}}(\log r)\,r^{k_{1}}e^{ik_{2}\theta}
with
P_{k_{0}}
a polynomial of degree
k_{0}
, then one can find a polynomial
Q
of degree
\leq k_{0}
(and of degree
\leq k_{0}+1
in the resonant case
k_{1}=|k_{2}|
) such that

f=Q(\log r)\,r^{k_{1}}e^{ik_{2}\theta}
satisfies
L_{0}f=h
.
Since solutions are not unique, we fix one choice and denote it by
L_{0}^{-1}h
.

We write
r^{2}\Delta=L_{0}+R
, where
R=r^{2}\Delta_{\Sigma}+r^{2}E
; note that
R
contributes higher order terms in
r
compared with
L_{0}
.
Set
u_{0}:=Bz^{3/2}
.
Then
L_{0}u_{0}=0
and
R(u_{0})\sim r^{5/2}h+\mathcal{O}(r^{7/2})
for some
h
.
Define
u_{1}:=L_{0}^{-1}(r^{5/2}h)
; then
\Delta(u_{0}+u_{1})=\mathcal{O}(r^{7/2})
.
Iterating this procedure, we obtain a formal polyhomogeneous series
\hat{u}\sim u_{0}+u_{1}+\cdots
with

u_{i}\sim r^{3/2+i}
(possibly with
\log r
terms), such that
\Delta\hat{u}
vanishes to infinite order as
r\to 0
.

Finally, we take a Borel sum to obtain the desired
u
.
Let
\chi
be a cutoff function supported in
U
with
\chi\equiv 1
near
\Sigma
, and set
\varepsilon_{i}:=e^{-i^{2}}
.
Define
u:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\chi\!\left(\frac{r}{\varepsilon_{i}}\right)u_{i},
then
u
is polyhomogeneous and satisfies
\Delta u\in\mathcal{S}
, as claimed.
∎

In this rest of this subsection, we will show Proposition
3.2
by repeating the argument in Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
3.7
up to Schwartz error. As these propositions heavily depend on Lemma
3.5
, we first need to generalize Lemma
3.5
so that it allows a Schwartz error.

Lemma 3.9
.

For
\eta\in\Omega^{k}(\mathcal{I}|_{U})
, if
\eta|_{\Sigma}=0
and
d\eta\in\mathcal{S}
, then for
\zeta:=\int_{s\in(0,r)}\iota_{\partial_{r}}\eta\in\Omega^{k-1}(\mathcal{I}|_{U})
, we have
\eta-d\zeta\in\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

By the Cartan formula
\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{r}}\eta=d(\iota_{\partial_{r}}\eta)+\iota_{\partial_{r}}d\eta
, hence

\eta(r)-\eta(0)=\int_{0}^{r}\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{s}}\eta(s)\,ds=d\zeta+\int_{0}^{r}\iota_{\partial_{s}}d\eta(s)\,ds.

Since
\eta|_{\Sigma}=0
we have
\eta(0)=0
, so we conclude

\eta-d\zeta=\int_{0}^{r}\iota_{\partial_{s}}d\eta(s)\,ds\in\mathcal{S}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

The argument is identical to that of Proposition
3.6
and
Proposition
3.7
, with locally harmonic functions replaced by their
Schwartz approximations.

For
m\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, let
T^{\langle m\rangle}\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}M))
and set
g_{s}:=g_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{s^{m}}{m!}\,T^{\langle m\rangle}.
We determine
T^{\langle m\rangle}
inductively. Given
B_{m}
, Proposition
3.8
provides a polyhomogeneous
function
f_{m}
on
U
such that
\Delta f_{m}\in\mathcal{S}
and
f_{m}\sim\operatorname{Re}(\widetilde{B}_{m}\,z^{\frac{3}{2}})+\cdots.

By Lemma
3.9
, there exists a
polyhomogeneous
2
-form
h_{m}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{I}|_{U})
such that
df_{m}+d^{*}h_{m}\in\mathcal{S}
.
Define
T^{\langle m\rangle}
by (
13
). By construction,

|\omega_{0}|^{2}\,T^{\langle m\rangle}
is Schwartz; hence
T^{\langle m\rangle}
is Schwartz as well and
therefore extends smoothly across
\Sigma
to a smooth tensor on
M
.

Repeating the argument of Proposition
3.7
, we conclude
\left.\partial_{s}^{m}A_{s}\right|_{s=0}=0,\;\left.\partial_{s}^{m}B_{s}\right|_{s=0}=\widetilde{B}_{m},
as desired.
∎

3.4.
Deformations
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate

In this subsection we explain why the linear deformation result of

\vec{k}

Lemma 3.10
.

Let

\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2

.
Then the two technical assumptions in Proposition

.

(2)

For any function

Proof.

(1) By

(2) Since

is polyhomogeneous, all derivatives of
\omega_{0}
have at most polynomial growth
near
\Sigma
.
If

is rapidly decaying along
\Sigma
, then

is also rapidly decaying by (1),
and differentiating

produces only sums of products of derivatives of

(still rapidly decaying) with derivatives of
|\omega_{0}|^{-1}
(at most polynomially growing).
Therefore, every derivative of
f/|\omega_{0}|
is rapidly decaying, i.e.
f/|\omega_{0}|\in\mathcal{S}
.
∎

By Lemma

,
the assumptions of Proposition

are satisfied for
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic 1-forms. Therefore, applying Proposition

on these 1-forms gives us the following corollary.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
(M,g_{0})
be a closed Riemannian manifold and let

be a smoothly embedded
codimension-two submanifold such that the normal bundle

is oriented and

is trivial.
Let

be a
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic

-form.
Then, for any fixed integer

and any prescribed smooth sections

\widetilde{B}_{i}\in\Gamma\!\big(N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big),\qquad 1\leq i\leq m,

there exists a one-parameter family of smooth metrics

with

such that (

) holds for all

.
Moreover, for any fixed compact subset

.

4.
Deformations of k-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic 1-forms

In this section we prove the main existence result of the paper. The aim is to show that under certain conditions and after a suitable perturbation of the ambient metric, a degenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form can be deformed to a nondegenerate one.

Recall from Section
3
that we constructed a one-parameter family of metrics
g_{s}
which gives precise control of the Taylor expansions of the leading coefficients
A
and
B
along the singular set
\Sigma
. Following Donaldson
[
6
]
, the remaining step is to deform the singular set itself. We parameterize nearby singular sets by normal sections
v\in\Gamma(N)
and consider the corresponding map
A(g,v)
whose zeros encode solutions.

The main analytic input is a non-uniform Nash–Moser implicit function theorem. In our situation the relevant tame constants depend on the parameter
s
(in particular, the inverse of the linearized operator and the quadratic error deteriorate as
s\to 0
), so we must work with a parameter-dependent Nash–Moser scheme and keep track of the
s
-dependence throughout.

We now state the main theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\omega_{0})
be a degenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-form on a closed Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{0})
.
Let
U
be a tubular neighbourhood of
\Sigma
and fix a compact set
K\subset M\setminus\Sigma
.
Assume that

(i)

|\omega_{0}|>0
on
U\setminus\Sigma
,

(ii)

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}
, one has
f/|\omega_{0}|\in\mathcal{S}
, and

(iii)

there exist a section
\widetilde{B}\in\Gamma(N^{-3/2})
and a constant
C>0
such that for all
s\in(0,1)
and all
t\in\Sigma
,

\bigl|B(g_{0})(t)+s\,\widetilde{B}(t)\bigr|\geq Cs.

There exist a one-parameter family of smooth metrics
\{g_{s}\}_{s\in(0,1)}

a deformation
\{\Sigma_{s}\}_{s\in(0,1)}
of
\Sigma
, and a family of nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic
1
-forms

(\Sigma_{s},\mathcal{I},\omega_{s})\quad\text{on}\quad(M,g_{s}),

such that
g_{s}|_{s=0}=g_{0}
,
g_{s}|_{K}=g_{0}|_{K}
, and

\omega_{s}
represents the same cohomology class as
\omega_{0}
in
H^{1}_{-}(\widehat{M})
.

4.1.
Donaldson’s Deformation Theory

We briefly recall the analytic structure behind Donaldson’s deformation argument in
[
6
]
. Let
M
be a compact
n
-manifold. Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the space of smooth Riemannian metrics on
M
, and let
\mathcal{S}
denote the space of smooth, codimension-2, co-oriented submanifolds of
M
. Both
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{S}
are tame Fréchet manifolds.

Fix a reference submanifold
\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}
and let
N
denote its normal bundle. As discussed in
[
6
, Section 4]
, a neighbourhood of
\Sigma
in
\mathcal{S}
can be identified with a neighbourhood of the zero section in the space of sections
\Gamma(N)
. Specifically, using the normal exponential map, any section
v\in\Gamma(N)
that is sufficiently small determines a nearby submanifold
\Sigma_{v}
. This identification allows us to parameterize local deformations of
\Sigma
directly by sections of its normal bundle.

Let
(\Sigma_{0},\mathcal{I}_{0},\omega_{0})
be an
L^{2}
-bounded
\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic 1-form over
(M,g_{0})
. Let
U_{1}\subset\mathcal{M}
be a neighbourhood of
g_{0}
and let
U_{2}\subset\Gamma(N)
be a neighbourhood of the zero section.
For any pair
(g,v)\in U_{1}\times U_{2}
, let
\Sigma_{v}
be the submanifold determined by
v
. There exists a unique
L^{2}
-harmonic 1-form
\omega
on
(M,g)
representing the same cohomology as
\omega_{0}
in
H^{1}_{-}(\widehat{M})
. As explained in Section
2.2
,
\omega
can be written as

(14)

\omega=d\,\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A(t)\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}+B(t)\,z^{\frac{3}{2}}\bigr)-\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{d}\,\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A(t)\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\,\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\mu(t)\,z\bigr)+o(r)

using some suitable normal complex coordinate
z
.

Now, we fixed a cohomology class. Note that the coefficients
A
and
B
are originally defined over the perturbed submanifold
\Sigma_{v}
. To work within a fixed function space, we use the diffeomorphism induced by the normal exponential map to identify
\Sigma_{v}
with
\Sigma_{0}
. This allows us to view
A
and
B
as sections over
\Sigma_{0}
; for technical details on this identification and the corresponding bundle maps, we refer to
[
6
, Section 4]
. Thus, we define the obstruction map

A:U_{1}\times U_{2}\to\Gamma(\Sigma_{0},N^{-1/2}),\quad(g,v)\mapsto A(g,v),

and the secondary map

B:U_{1}\times U_{2}\to\Gamma(\Sigma_{0},N^{-3/2}),\quad(g,v)\mapsto B(g,v).

Moreover, the maps
A
and
B
are smooth tame maps in the sense of Hamilton
[
8
]
. This means that they satisfy linear growth estimates with respect to the graded norms of the Fréchet spaces. Specifically, there exists an integer
d\geq 0
such that for every
i\geq 0
, we have a tame estimate of the form:

(15)

\|A(g,v)\|_{C^{i,\alpha}}\leq C_{i}\left(1+\|g\|_{C^{i+d,\alpha}}+\|v\|_{C^{i+d,\alpha}}\right).

An analogous estimate holds for the secondary map
B
.

Donaldson computes the linearization of
A
with respect to the normal variation
w\in\Gamma(N)
. He shows that the partial derivative takes the form:

(16)

D_{v}A(g,v)\cdot w=\frac{3}{2}Bw+\mathcal{R}(g,v,A,w),

where
\mathcal{R}
is a remainder term that depends linearly on
A
and
w
.

Equation (
16
) highlights the critical role of the secondary coefficient. If
A(g,v)=0
, the remainder term
\mathcal{R}
vanishes, and the linearization reduces to the multiplication map
w\mapsto\frac{3}{2}Bw
. If
B
is nowhere vanishing, this map is an algebraic isomorphism from
\Gamma(N)
to
\Gamma(N^{-1/2})
.

However, the obstruction map
A
is defined via the solution of an elliptic operator on the manifold, which introduces a ‘loss of derivatives’ in the Fréchet topology. Standard Banach space methods fail here. Instead, Donaldson applies the Nash-Moser implicit function theorem. The key requirement is the existence of an ‘approximate inverse’ with quadratic error.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
6
]
, Theorem 3)
.

If
B
is nowhere vanishing, then the map
D_{v}A
admits a right inverse with quadratic error. Specifically, the map
\Psi(\eta)=\frac{2}{3}B^{-1}\eta
serves as an approximate inverse. This means that for any
\eta\in\Gamma(N^{-1/2})
, we have

(17)

D_{v}A(g,v)\left(\Psi(\eta)\right)=\eta+Q(g,v,A,\eta),

where
Q
is a smooth tame map that is bilinear in
A
and
\eta
.

The local solvability in the non-degenerate case follows directly from this result. Since
B(g_{0},0)
is assumed to be nowhere vanishing, it admits a uniform lower bound, which ensures that the multiplication operator
B^{-1}
is bounded. Consequently, the approximate inverse
\Psi
satisfies the necessary tame estimates to drive the convergence of the Newton iteration, yielding the following theorem:

Theorem 4.3

(
[
6
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Suppose that
A(g_{0},0)=0
and that the non-degeneracy condition holds, i.e.,
B(g_{0},0)
is nowhere vanishing on
\Sigma
. Then there exist a neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{M}
of
g_{0}
and a neighbourhood
\mathcal{V}\subset\Gamma(N)
of the zero section such that for every metric
g\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists a unique normal section
v_{g}\in\mathcal{V}
satisfying

A(g,v_{g})=0.

Moreover, the map
g\mapsto v_{g}
is a smooth tame map from
\mathcal{U}
to
\mathcal{V}
.

The central difficulty we address in this paper is the failure of the non-degeneracy condition. In our setting, the coefficient
B
approaches zero as
s\to 0
, causing the uniform bound on
B^{-1}
to fail. Specifically, under the family of metrics
g_{s}
constructed in Theorem
4.1
, the coefficient
B_{s}
behaves asymptotically like
s\,\widetilde{B}
. Consequently, the norm of the inverse scales as
\|B_{s}^{-1}\|\sim s^{-1}
.

This creates a critical competition: the error term
A_{s}
(which we control via Section 3) must vanish at a sufficient rate to counteract the blow-up of the linearized operator as
s\to 0
. Standard perturbation arguments are insufficient here, as we must ensure that the solution
\Sigma_{s}
remains within the domain of validity of the iteration scheme for every
s
. Therefore, our proof relies on a precise, quantitative version of the Nash-Moser theorem—carried out in the following subsection and Appendix A—where the dependence of all analytic estimates on the degeneration parameter
s
is explicitly tracked.

4.2.
Quantitative Estimates for the Quantitative Nash–Moser

In this subsection, we establish the quantitative estimates required for the Nash–Moser scheme in the degenerating regime
s\to 0
. To apply the scale-dependent implicit function theorem (Proposition A.2), we must carefully track the dependence of all relevant analytic constants on the parameter
s
. Specifically, we estimate the norm of the approximate inverse
\Psi_{s}
(which diverges as
B_{s}\to 0
), the tame estimates for the error term
A_{s}
, and the quadratic error term.

Given a
\vec{k}
-nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic 1-form
(\omega,\mathcal{I},\Sigma)
. Recall from Proposition
3.2
that for any fixed integer
m\geq 1
, we have constructed a one-parameter family of smooth metrics
\{g_{s}\}_{s\in(0,1)}
extending
g_{0}
. In the notation of our obstruction map, the reference submanifold
\Sigma
corresponds to the zero section
0\in\Gamma(N)
. The construction in ensures that the Taylor coefficients at the zero section satisfy:

(18)

\left.\partial_{s}B(g_{s},0)\right|_{s=0}=\widetilde{B},\qquad\left.\partial_{s}^{i}A(g_{s},0)\right|_{s=0}=0,\quad\left.\partial_{s}^{i+1}B(g_{s},0)\right|_{s=0}=0\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq m.

In particular, the coefficients admit the following asymptotic expansions:

\displaystyle A(g_{s},0)=\mathcal{O}(s^{m+1})\quad\text{and}\quad B(g_{s},0)=B_{0}+s\,\widetilde{B}+\mathcal{O}(s^{m+1}).

We now consider variations of the singular set parameterized by sections
v\in\Gamma(N)
. By restricting the general maps
A(g,v)
and
B(g,v)
to our specific family of metrics, we define the parameter-dependent operators:

A_{s}(v):=A(g_{s},v),\qquad B_{s}(v):=B(g_{s},v).

Since the bundle
N^{-3/2}
is trivial, we shall regard
B_{s}(v)
simply as a complex-valued function on
\Sigma
. With this notation, our goal is to find a small section
v
such that
A_{s}(v)=0
. The term
A_{s}(0)
represents the initial error at the reference submanifold, which is small at
\mathcal{O}(s^{m+1})
, but non-zero.

We now record the quantitative estimates needed for the Nash–Moser scheme in the degenerating
regime
s\to 0
. The key point is to make the
s
–dependence of all constants explicit.
We first estimate the decay rate of the linearized operator
B_{s}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
in (
18
). There exist
s_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that for all
0<s<s_{0}
,
the function
B_{s}(0)\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{C}
is nowhere vanishing, hence invertible, and

\|B_{s}(0)^{-1}\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma)}\leq C\,s^{-1}.

Proof.

Using the integral remainder,

(19)

B_{s}(0)=B_{0}(0)+s\,\widetilde{B}+R_{m+1}(s),\text{ where }R_{m+1}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{(s-\sigma)^{m}}{m!}\,\left.\partial_{s}^{m+1}B_{s}(0)\right|_{s=\sigma}\,d\sigma.

Since the map
s\mapsto g_{s}
is smooth and
B(\,\cdot\,,\Sigma)
is tame, the family
\{\partial_{s}^{m+1}B_{s}(0)\}_{s\in[0,1]}
is bounded in
C^{0}(\Sigma)
. Hence, there exists

C_{1}>0
such that

\|R_{m+1}(s)\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma)}\leq C_{1}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\sigma)^{m}\,d\sigma=\frac{C_{1}}{(m+1)!}\,s^{m+1}.

On the other hand, assumption (iii) in Theorem
4.1
provides a constant
c_{0}>0
such that for all
t\in\Sigma

and all
s\in(0,1)
,

|B_{0}(0)(t)+s\,\widetilde{B}(t)|\geq c_{0}\,s.

Choose
s_{0}>0
so that
\frac{C_{1}}{(m+1)!}\,s^{m}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{2}
whenever
0<s<s_{0}
.
Then for all
t\in\Sigma
and
0<s<s_{0}
,

|B_{s}(0)(t)|\geq|B_{0}(0)(t)+s\,\widetilde{B}(t)|-|R_{m+1}(s)(t)|\geq c_{0}s-\frac{C_{1}}{(m+1)!}s^{m+1}\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2}\,s.

Thus,
B_{s}(0)
is nowhere vanishing and
\|B_{s}(0)^{-1}\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma)}\leq\frac{2}{c_{0}}\,s^{-1}.

∎

Next, we prove uniform tame bounds for
A_{s}
and its first two derivatives. These estimates are needed for Equation (

Lemma 4.5
.

Fix

Since

Similarly, smooth tameness gives tame bounds for the first and second derivatives in the

∎

Tame bounds for

\displaystyle|\psi(u)v|_{s}
