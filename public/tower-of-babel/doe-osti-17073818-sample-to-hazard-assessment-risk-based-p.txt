Sample to Hazard Assessment, Risk-based Platform (SHARP)
Formerly the Integrated Biothreat Assessment Platform
Principal Investigators: Thomas W. Bates and Crystal Jaing
Laboratory Directed Research and Development Project Tracking Number: 24-SI-006

Project Overview
Biological threats arising from natural outbreaks, accidental releases, or deliberate misuse
require detection capabilities that extend beyond predefined lists of organisms. Existing methods
can miss unknown biological material, particularly when small quantities are obscured by
complex clinical or environmental backgrounds. Identifying an organism or protein also does not
establish its biological activity or potential health effects. This project developed complementary
experimental and computational capabilities to address these challenges, combining sample
separation and enrichment, genetic and protein-sequence analysis, a structure-based pipeline to
predict biological activity, an experimental system for laboratory testing of activity, and
computational prediction of adverse effects.
Under the preparation and enrichment conditions evaluated, sequencing approaches improved
pathogen detection by up to 1,000-fold. A broad-screening workflow was applied to test clinical
nasal-swab samples, and complementary software recovered expected toxin peptide-related test
sequences across a set of controlled samples. A structure-based pipeline further evaluated test
sequences for functional activity, identifying candidates predicted to be experimentally active.
Our team then established laboratory methods to produce and validate peptide activity on human
proteins involved in nerve signaling. Finally, in a separate computational pipeline, the peptide
sequences, combined with chemical information and additional biological context, were further
evaluated to predict adverse health effects. Together, these accomplishments establish important
components for assessing unknown biological material while identifying remaining limitations.

Mission Impact
This research strengthened Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) capabilities in
biological surveillance, unfamiliar-sequence assessment, and experimental characterization of
biological activity. These advances support the Department of Energy and National Nuclear
Security Administration missions to develop science and technology for emerging national-
security challenges, including counterproliferation and emergency response. Clinical and
wastewater workflows also support public-health and defense applications. Collaboration among
national laboratories, universities, public-health agencies, and industry established a foundation
for continued integration and independent evaluation. The investment contributed to the Forge
initiative and generated three newly funded independent verification and validation efforts, two
sponsored by the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency and one by the Advanced
Research Projects Agency for Health. These transitions provide opportunities to improve
pipeline performance, reproducibility, and turnaround time for biosurveillance and threat
characterization.
