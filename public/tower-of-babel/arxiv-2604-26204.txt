Introduction

The study of moduli spaces of stable sheaves on elliptic surfaces are started by fundamental works of
Friedman (see
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
).
He obtained a birational description of the moduli spaces of rank 2 stable sheaves.
In particular it is proved that the moduli spaces are birationally equivalent to the
Hilbert scheme of points, if the relative degree of the first Chern class
is odd.
Later it was generalized to higher rank cases by Bridgeland
[
5
]
and Yoshioka
[
21
]
.
For the construction of the birational map,
Bridgeland’s method
[
5
]
is notable, that is,
he developed a powerful method called relative Fourier-Mukai transforms.
They are equivalences of the derived category of coherent sheaves on elliptic surfaces by using moduli spaces of stable fiber sheaves on the elliptic fibration.
Then the birational maps for higher rank cases are easily follow.
Since then,
many properties of moduli spaces are found by Bridgeland’s method (cf.
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
).

In
[
29
]
, we introduced a notion of stability for coherent sheaves
associated to relative Fourier-Mukai transforms
and studied the wall crossing behaviors.
In this paper, we shall generalize the notion to the case where the relative degree and the rank is not
relatively prime, and study some properties.

Let
\pi:X\to C
be a minimal elliptic surface over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let

H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}:=H^{0}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus H^{4}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

be the algebraic part of the cohomology ring
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
, where

\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}=\operatorname{NS}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We define
\varrho_{X}\in H^{4}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
by

\int_{X}\varrho_{X}=1
.
Then we have

H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}={\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}\varrho_{X}.

For
u=x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2}\varrho_{X}
(
x_{0},x_{2}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,
x_{1}\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
),
we set
u^{\vee}:=x_{0}-x_{1}+x_{2}\varrho_{X}
.
As in
[
30
]
, we introduce Mukai pairing and Mukai vector.

(1)

We define the Mukai pairing
\langle\;\;,\;\;\rangle
on

H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}

by

(0.1)

\langle u,v\rangle=-\int_{X}u^{\vee}v=(x_{1}\cdot y_{1})-x_{0}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{0},\;u=x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2}\varrho_{X},v=y_{0}+y_{1}+y_{2}\varrho_{X},

where
(x_{1}\cdot y_{1})
is the intersection pairing of
\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(2)

For
E\in{\bf D}(X)
, we define the Mukai vector
v(E)
by

(0.2)

v(E):=\operatorname{ch}E(1+\tfrac{\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})}{2}\varrho_{X})\in H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}.

(3)

If
v\in v({\bf D}(X))
, then we also call
v
a Mukai vector.

For an object
E\in{\bf D}(X)
,
we define
(f,G,H)
-semistability in Definition
2.5
and
2.7
,
where
H
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor which is
\pi
-ample with

(H^{2})=0
and
(H\cdot f)=1
.
Roughly speaking, it means that

E
or the derived dual
E^{\vee}
is a coherent sheaf with a possible torsion fiber sheaf,
where
G\in K(X)
with the rank
\operatorname{rk}G>0
.

We shall study the moduli stack
{\mathcal{M}}_{(f,H)}^{G}(v)

of
(f,G,H)
-semistable sheaves
E
with
v(E)=v
.
The
(f,G,H)
-semistability depends on
c_{1}(G)/\operatorname{rk}G\mod{\mathbb{Q}}f
.
Thus we have a parameter space

{\mathcal{G}}(v)\subset\{(s,\alpha)\mid s\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\cap f^{\perp}\cap H^{\perp}\}

for
(f,G,H)
-semistability.
As in slope-semistability or Bridgeland semistability,
we have a wall/chamber structure in

(Definition
3.10
).
We shall prove that there is a chamber parameterizing Gieseker semistable sheaves
with respect to
H_{f}:=H_{f}

(n\gg 0)
(Proposition
3.19
).
Thus our stability is a generalization of Gieseker semistability.
If
-K_{X}
is nef, we can study wall crossing behaviors. In particular we
can compare
(f,G,H)
-semistability with Gieseker stability.

Let

\Phi_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}}:{\bf D}(X)\to{\bf D}(X^{\prime})
be
a relative Fourier-Mukai transform, that is,

X^{\prime}
is a moduli space of stable sheaves supported on fibers of
\pi

and
{\mathcal{P}}\in\operatorname{Coh}(X\times X^{\prime})
the universal family.
Then there is an elliptic fibration
\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to C
and we can associate a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
H^{\prime}
which is

\pi^{\prime}
-ample with
({H^{\prime}}^{2})=0
and
(H^{\prime}\cdot f^{\prime})=1
.
Let
\overline{\Phi}_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}}
be its cohomological Fourier-Mukai
transform (see Lemma
1.7
).
For
E\in{\bf D}(X)
, we note that

\operatorname{rk}\Phi_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}[k]}(E)=(-1)^{k+1}\langle v(E),v({\mathcal{P}}_{|X\times\{x^{\prime}\}})\rangle
(Lemma
1.5
).
Then we have the following result which explains that our semistability behaves nicely under relative Fourier-Mukai transforms.

Theorem 0.1

(Theorem
5.12
)
.

Let
E
be a
(f,G,H)
-semistable object.
Assume that
G
is general and

\operatorname{rk}\Phi_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}}(E)\neq 0
. Then there is a triple
(f^{\prime},G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
such that

\Phi_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}}(E)
is
(f^{\prime},G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
-semistable up to shift.

Theorem
0.1
shows that
\Phi_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}}
induces a bijective
correspondence of the set of chambers.
Hence by studying chamber structure,
we can study the behavior of Gieseker semistable objects
under relative Fourier-Mukai transforms.
In particular we get the following result.

Theorem 0.2

(cf. Theorem
5.16
)
.

Let
E
be a Gieseker semistable sheaf with respect to
H_{f}
.
Then

\Phi_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}[1]}(E(mH))
is Gieseker semistable with respect to

H^{\prime}_{f^{\prime}}

if

is sufficiently large.

By studying the wall crossing behavior, we can show that the topological invariants
do not depend on the choice of the chamber if
(K_{X}\cdot H)\leq 0
.
Thus we get the following result.

Proposition 0.3

(cf. Proposition
4.6
)
.

Assume that
(K_{X}\cdot H)\leq 0
.

(1)

The vertual Hodge number
e({\mathcal{M}}_{(f,H)}^{G}(v))

does not depend on the choice of chambers.

(2)

\Phi_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}}
preserves virtual Hodge polynomials

e({\mathcal{M}}_{H_{f}}(v))

of the moduli of Gieseker semistable sheaves.

We remark that (2) is a consequence of (1) and Theorem
0.1
.
In particular,

\Phi_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}}
preserves virtual Hodge polynomials

for a rational elliptic surface and an elliptic ruled surface.

Let

(i)

-semistable.

(ii)

-twisted semistable 1-dimensional

Thus wall-crossings for

In
[
30
]
, we studied Bridgeland stability conditions on elliptic surfaces and their relations with
relative Fourier-Mukai transforms.
For a chamber
{\mathcal{C}}
of
(f,G,H)
-semistability, there is a
relative Fourier-Mukai transform under which
{\mathcal{C}}
is transformed to a chamber
parameterizing Gieseker semistable sheaves (Corollary

Since Gieseker semistable objects are examples of stable objects in the sense of Bridgeland
[

,

-semistability can be understood by using Bridgeland stability conditions.
On the other hand, a Bridgeland stability
\sigma
(
[
6
]

consists of a collection of semistable objects
{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\phi)\;(\phi\in{\mathbb{R}})
with
a stability function
Z_{\sigma}:{\bf D}(X)\to{\mathbb{C}}
which
has much more information, and the parameter space
(that is, the space of stability conditions) is too big for our study of wall crossing
for
(f,G,H)
-semistability.
Therefore we do not use Bridgeland stability conditions to study our
(f,G,H)
-semistability.
For relations with Bridgeland stability, we just mention some references
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
.

Let us explain the organization of this paper.
In section

In particular we prove a Bogomolov type inequality for semistable objects.
In section

In section

In section

1.2.
Fourier-Mukai transforms

For smooth projective varieties
X,Y
and
{\bf P}\in{\bf D}(X\times Y)
,

\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\bf P}}:{\bf D}(X)\to{\bf D}(Y)
is an integral functor defined by

(1.1)

\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\bf P}}(E):={\bf R}p_{Y*}({\bf P}\otimes p_{X}^{*}(E)),\;E\in{\bf D}(X),

where
p_{X}:X\times Y\to X
and
p_{Y}:X\times Y\to Y
are projections.
If
\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\bf P}}
is an equivalence, then it is called a Fourier-Mukai transform.
For the equivalence
\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\bf P}}
,

(1.2)

\begin{split}\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\bf P}}\circ\Phi_{Y\to X}^{{\bf P}^{\vee}}=&\otimes p_{Y}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(-K_{Y}))[-n],\\
\Phi_{Y\to X}^{{\bf P}^{\vee}}\circ\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\bf P}}=&\otimes p_{X}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{X}))[-n],\end{split}

where
n=\dim X=\dim Y
.

For a spherical object
E_{0}\in{\bf D}(X)
,
let
R_{E_{0}}:{\bf D}(X)\to{\bf D}(X)
be the spherical twist defined by

(1.3)

R_{E_{0}}(E):=\mathrm{cone}({\bf R}\operatorname{Hom}(E_{0},E)\otimes E_{0}\to E).

If
E_{0},E\in\operatorname{Coh}(X)
satisfies
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E_{0},E)=0
,
then we have an exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Hom}(E_{0},E)\otimes E_{0}\to E\to R_{E_{0}}(E)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E_{0},E)\otimes E_{0}\to 0.

Assume that
X
is an Enriques surface.
For an exceptional object
E_{0}\in{\bf D}(X)
,
we also have an equivalence

R_{E_{0}}:{\bf D}(X)\to{\bf D}(X)
such that

(1.4)

R_{E_{0}}(E):=\mathrm{cone}({\bf R}\operatorname{Hom}(E_{0},E)\otimes E_{0}\oplus{\bf R}\operatorname{Hom}(E_{0}(K_{X}),E)\otimes E_{0}(K_{X})\to E).

Since
\langle v(E_{0})^{2}\rangle=-\chi(E_{0},E_{0})=-1
and

v(R_{E_{0}}(E))=v(E)+2\langle v(E),v(E_{0})\rangle v(E_{0})
,

R_{E_{0}}
indices a
(-1)
-reflection on the Mukai lattice.

For
E\in{\bf D}(X)
, we set

(1.5)

D_{X}(E):={\bf R}{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})\in{\bf D}(X).

Then
D_{X}
defines a contravariant functor
{\bf D}(X)\to{\bf D}(X)
.
For simplicity, we introduce the following notation.

(1.6)

\begin{split}E^{*}:=&H^{0}(D_{X}(E))={\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}),\\
E^{\vee}:=&D_{X}(E).\end{split}

In particular the double dual
E^{**}
of
E
is a locally free sheaf.

E^{*}
is the usual dual of
E
and
E^{\vee}
is the derived dual of
E
.

Remark 1.2
.

For

(\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}[1]}(E))^{\vee}(-K_{Y})={\bf R}\operatorname{Hom}_{p_{Y}}(p_{X}^{*}(E),{\mathcal{P}}[1])

Let

In order to define our stability,

Definition 2.1

(1)

We set

(2.1)

N_{H}:=\{D\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid(D\cdot f)=(D\cdot H)=0\}.

(2)

We have a orthogonal decomposition

For

denotes the

-component of

Remark 2.2

We take a positive integer
\mu
such that
\mu H\in\operatorname{NS}(X)

For
\xi\in\operatorname{NS}(X)
,
we set

\xi=dH+kf+D,D\in N_{H}.

Then

\mu k=(\xi\cdot\mu H)\in{\mathbb{Z}}

Hence
\mu D=\mu\xi-d\mu H-k\mu f\in\operatorname{NS}(X)

We take

\operatorname{rk}G>0

Definition 2.3

For

with
\operatorname{rk}E>0

\mu_{f}(E):=\frac{(c_{1}(E)\cdot f)}{\operatorname{rk}E},\;\mu_{\beta,f}(E):=\mu_{f}(E(-\beta))=\frac{((c_{1}(E)-\operatorname{rk}E\beta)\cdot f)}{\operatorname{rk}E},

where
\beta\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}

Definition 2.4
.

(i)

Lemma 2.9

Proof.

Let

We shall prove that

\begin{split}\langle v(E/T_{1})^{2}\rangle=&\langle v(E)^{2}\rangle-2n\langle v(A),v(E)\rangle-2n^{2}\\
\leq&\langle v(E)^{2}\rangle.\end{split}
