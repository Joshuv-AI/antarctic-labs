Role of anisotropic electronic friction in laser-driven hydrogen recombination on copper
Alexander Spears, Wojciech G. Stark, Reinhard J. Maurer

Abstract
Ultrafast light-driven chemical dynamics at surfaces are governed by energy transfer from excited electrons to vibrational degrees of freedom. When this nonadiabatic energy transfer is anisotropic, it can lead to dynamical steering effects that affect reaction probabilities or non-thermal final energy distributions in molecules. Here, we use a machine-learning-enabled simulation framework to compare isotropic and anisotropic models of electronic friction during laser-driven hydrogen evolution on the (111) facet of copper. While anisotropic friction strongly determines the rate of energy transfer into the adsorbate and the fluence dependence of reaction probabilities, it has little effect on final translational, vibrational and rotational energy distributions as these are mainly governed by the potential energy landscape at the barrier.

Adsorption and desorption of molecular hydrogen from metal surfaces are important steps in heterogeneous catalysis and electrochemical processes
Langmuir 1912
.
Although these steps are thoroughly studied in thermally activated catalysis, many open questions remain for photocatalytic processes on metal interfaces
Lee 2023
.
When a metal surface or nanoparticle is exposed to light, electron-hole pair (EHP) excitations are generated efficiently
Petek et al. 2023
.
These non-thermal excitations rapidly thermalise into a ”hot” electron distribution through electron-electron scattering with an effective temperature above the surrounding lattice
Frischkorn and Wolf 2006
.
Equilibration with the lattice through electron-phonon scattering occurs at a picosecond scale during which chemical dynamics at the surface can occur
Frischkorn and Wolf 2006
;
Rethfeld et al. 2004
.
As a result, hot electrons can transfer energy into adsorbed atoms or molecules at a faster rate than into the lattice, driving chemical transformations
Frischkorn 2008
such as the recombinative desorption of H
2
from Ru(0001)
Denzler et al. 2004
.

Laser-desorbed H
2
molecules generally have a non-thermal partitioning of energy across vibrational, rotational and translational degrees of freedom
Frischkorn 2008
.
It is still not clear if this non-equipartition of energy in the molecule results from non-thermal heating of chemisorbed hydrogen or from details of the barrier landscape during recombinative desorption.
Ultrafast pump-probe experiments report a transient change to adsorbate vibrational modes, e.g. the red-shift of the CO stretch vibration frequency on a Cu(100) substrate,
Inoue et al. 2016
which has been interpreted as a consequence of non-thermal and mode selective EHP-mediated energy transfer into the adsorbate.
Laser-induced dynamical effects can also enhance isotope effects
Lindner et al. 2023
, promote trapping in precursor states
Mladineo et al. 2025
, or induce direction-specific diffusion
Gonzalez et al. 2025
.
The vibrational anisotropy of the coupling between hot electrons and adsorbate vibrations has been shown to affect dynamics in measured and calculated vibrational linewidths
Hertl et al. 2026
;
Morin et al. 1992
;
Gadzuk and Luntz 1984
of adsorbed hydrogen, scattering of atomic hydrogen
Box et al. 2024
, and dissociative chemisorption of H
2

Maurer et al. 2017
;
Stark et al. 2025
.

Classical molecular dynamics simulations impose the Born-Oppenheimer approximation and are unsuitable to describe non-adiabatic electron-nuclear coupling effects, as they neglect the Born-Oppenheimer approximation
González and Lindh 2021
.
While a fully
ab-initio
description of electron dynamics triggered by a laser pulse is possible
Schäfer and Johansson 2022
, simulations of the ensuing coupled-electron nuclear dynamics of realistic interfaces are still computationally challenging
Shi et al. 2025
.
A commonly employed mixed quantum-classical approach to study light-driven dynamics at surfaces is the Molecular Dynamics with Electronic Friction (MDEF) method

;
Füchsel et al. 2011
;
Luntz et al. 2006
;
Alducin et al. 2019

Light-driven surface dynamics can be simulated by coupling MDEF with the Two-Temperature Model (TTM)

\mathrm{T_{el}}
) and phononic (

While previous dynamics studies were able to quantify desorption yields and non-thermal energy partitioning for hydrogen evolution on Ru(0001) using low-dimensional PES with static surfaces constructed from Density Functional Theory (DFT)
Füchsel et al. 2011
, the emergence of high-dimensional machine learning (ML) potentials has enabled studies that consider all degrees of freedom.
Simulations of laser-activated CO diffusion on Cu(110)
Gonzalez et al. 2025
and O diffusion on Pd(111)
Wang et al. 2025
have demonstrated how electronic and phononic excitations cooperatively activate diffusion processes multiple ps after an incident laser pulse. In addition, a study by Lindner
et al
on laser-driven H
2
evolution from Ru(0001)
Lindner et al. 2023
recently showed how finite size effects alter desorption yields, calling the accuracy of low-dimensional surrogate models into question.

Virtually all light-driven dynamics simulations so far have evaluated the electronic friction tensor (EFT) via the isotropic Local Density Friction Approximation (LDFA), where friction coefficients are calculated from ion scattering shifts in a homogeneous electron gas that matches the electron density of the substrate at the position of the adsorbate atom
Juaristi et al. 2008
.
While the non-equipartition of energy in desorbed molecules has been successfully predicted for H
2
evolution on Ru(0001)
Füchsel et al. 2011
, previously predicted laser fluence dependences do not agree with experiment
Lindner et al. 2023
;
Frischkorn 2008
.

In this Letter, we perform full-dimensional TTM-MDEF simulations of laser-driven H
2
evolution from a Cu(111) surface using a tensorial and anisotropic description of orbital-dependent friction (ODF) that is directly calculated from first-principles electron-phonon coupling
Hellsing and Persson 1984
;
Persson and Hellsing 1982
;
Maurer et al. 2016
;
Box et al. 2023
.
ODF has been shown to accurately reproduce the mode dependence of vibrational lifetimes
Maurer et al. 2016
;
Box et al. 2024
and spatially anisotropic energy dissipation effects in reactive scattering of N
2
on Ru(0001)
Shakouri et al. 2017
and H
2
on Cu
Luntz et al. 2009
.
H
2
/Cu(111) exhibits a highly activated late barrier (0.592 eV desorption barrier,
fig.

1
c) and strong anisotropy of friction across cartesian coordinates of chemisorbed hydrogen and internal modes of molecular hydrogen during recombination (
fig.

1
b).
We compare desorption probabilities, surface diffusion behaviour and energy partitioning for isotropic friction as predicted by LDFA and anisotropic, mode-dependent friction as predicted by ODF.
We find that the topology of the PES dominates the final energy distributions of desorbed molecules, but the two different EFT models differ significantly in the on-surface diffusion that they induce, yielding drastically different fluence dependence.
The results indicate that the mechanism of H
2
recombination is primarily affected by the shape of the PES, while the rate of recombination and fluence dependence is determined by the level of theoretical description of non-adiabatic coupling.

The TTM-MDEF simulations include surface degrees of freedom and the effect of laser-heating on the phonons.
After energy input from the laser pulse (
fig.

2
a), the electronic temperature
\mathrm{T_{el}}
rises rapidly to over 5000 K within approximately 0.3 ps, while the phononic temperature
\mathrm{T_{ph}}
rises slowly.
The rapid increase of
\mathrm{T_{el}}
leads to activation of hydrogen atom diffusion already within the time span of the laser
Lindner et al. 2023
.
The rate of kinetic energy transfer is determined by the magnitude of the EFT.

On average, adsorbed hydrogen atoms gain kinetic energy roughly twice as quickly in LDFA simulations compared to ODF (
fig.

2
b).
The increased kinetic energy of the adsorbed hydrogen atoms raises the frequency of collisions between adsorbates, which in turn can lead to recombinative desorption if enough energy has been absorbed to cross the recombination barrier.
As a result, the rate of desorption (
fig.

2
c) reaches a maximum shortly after the maximum kinetic energy transfer into the hydrogen atoms is reached (about 0.5 ps for LDFA, 0.75 ps for ODF).
While the desorption rate decreases more rapidly for LDFA simulations, the distribution of desorption events over time is broader and decays more slowly for ODF simulations, indicating a slower relaxation after laser excitation.

To analyse the diffusion behaviour of adsorbed hydrogen atoms, we partition the primitive unit cell of the Cu(111) surface into areas associated with high-symmetry sites which are commonly used for the discussion of reaction energetics in
ab-initio
studies
Wijzenbroek et al. 2015
(
fig.

3
a).
For a subset of about 200 trajectories per electronic friction approximation, we follow the motion of the adsorbed hydrogen atoms, measuring the number of transitions between sites.
Based on the different site-to-site hopping rates, the diffusion rate of hydrogen atoms is roughly 20% slower on average in ODF simulations as a result of the weaker energy transfer (
fig.

3
b).
The site hopping rate in LDFA simulations reaches a higher peak value, but decays slightly faster than observed in ODF simulations.
We also compare the population of the different surface areas by H atoms over time for LDFA and ODF (
fig.

3

For both friction approximations, hydrogen atoms initially reside in the
hollow
sites, the global energy minimum, and begin to populate higher energy
bridge

top

Since the minimum energy barrier for H
2
recombination from Cu(111) involves the approach of two hydrogen atoms through a
bridge
site, the reaction rate for H
2
recombination will most strongly be influenced by diffusion to the
bridge
sites.
In ODF simulations, the population shift to bridge sites is slightly less pronounced than for LDFA simulations, and the population of
top
sites is almost negligible between 0 to 2 ps after laser excitation.
Along the MEP for desorption, the diagonal

-2

-2

The overestimation of friction coefficients for perpendicular motion in LDFA has been reported in previous studies

;
Maurer et al. 2016
;
Box et al. 2024

The desorption probability follows a power law relationship of the form

The mode-selective ODF based on a first-principles description with DFT response theory has proven to affect reaction dynamics of some adsorbates on metal surfaces

For example, in quantum-state resolved scattering

Analysis of the translational, vibrational and rotational energy of desorbed molecules reveal subtle differences in the non-thermal energy distributions of molecules (
fig.

4

In general, desorbing H
2
molecules in ODF simulations have less translational, vibrational, and rotational energy than in LDFA simulations. However, the absolute vibrational energy remains nearly as high as its LDFA counterpart.
This is the result of stronger non-adiabatic coupling along the bond axis (

1

Spiering and Meyer 2018
;
Stark et al. 2025
.
It is likely that the total vibrational energy is slightly overestimated in both sets of simulations due to the semi-classical Einstein-Brillouin-Keller

Due to the high symmetry and lack of steps or edges on the Cu(111) surface, collision events between hydrogen atoms are most likely to occur when both atoms are at similar heights over the surface, and the MEP favours a concerted motion away from the surface.
These two factors likely discourage cartwheel-like rotations on desorption, leading to desorption with, on average, low rotation energy.
This is particularly so for ODF where the component of friction perpendicular to the surface is significantly lower than in LDFA.

After diffusion around local energetic minima, a collision with sufficient energy to cross the barrier to desorption (

The potential energy term

From the beginning of the trajectory until the onset of desorption (

The high electronic temperature, combined with a higher magnitude of electronic friction experienced by hydrogen atoms close to the surface results in a continuous overall energy uptake.

\Lambda\dot{\mathbf{R}}

However, during the tens of femtoseconds before and after the onset of desorption (

While there is some evidence of pre-organisation of the two hydrogen atoms prior to desorption (at approximately

Based on similar observations for LDFA and ODF desorption trajectories, we can conclude that the ultrafast laser pulse drives reactivity through photothermal activation of hydrogen diffusion over several hundred femtoseconds.
However, since desorption takes place on a time scale of tens of femtoseconds, electronic friction does not strongly affect the energy distribution during the recombination process and therefore also does not significantly steer the mechanism of desorption. Instead, the desorption is mainly governed by the shape of the PES.
These conclusions are consistent with observations from theoretical simulations and experiments on H
2
scattering.
For example, Spiering
et al

Spiering and Meyer 2018
and Stark
et al

Stark et al. 2025
determined that frictional coupling along the vibrational axis of H
2
is stronger for ODF compared to LDFA, however, the choice of LDFA compared to ODF did not significantly affect sticking probabilities.
Since scattering H
2
molecules are only close to the surface for a short time, EFT values, even though they might be strongly anisotropic along different modes, do not strongly affect the dynamics, despite significantly affecting the final energy distributions.

Prior to dissociation, in the diffusion regime, where the distance between H atoms is large, off-diagonal elements of the ODF EFT are significantly lower than diagonal components, so differences between LDFA and ODF mostly depend on the magnitude of diagonal EFT components.
ODF coefficients are significantly lower than LDFA perpendicular to the surface, resulting in lower energy gain (
fig.

2
) and reduced efficiency in mobilising hydrogen atoms at the surface (
fig.

3
b).
However, at higher surface coverages (here presented simulations were performed at 2/9 ML coverage), the shorter average H-H distances during diffusion lead to increased off-diagonal ODF EFT components
Hertl et al. 2026
.
This means that frictional steering effects along specific collective adsorbate vibrational modes, an effect that LDFA cannot capture, are more likely to be observed at high coverage.

In summary, we have applied a machine-learning-based workflow to the simulation of laser-driven H
2
recombination from a Cu(111) surface. The simulation approach enables the study of anisotropic orbital-dependent friction effects and includes all degrees of freedom of the system.
By comparing simulations using ODF and LDFA, we show that the main role of electronic friction lies in mobilising adsorbed hydrogen atoms towards the desorption barrier.
While the magnitude of non-adiabatic coupling differs substantially between LDFA and ODF, this does not translate into dramatic differences in energy partitioning of desorbed H
2
.
As LDFA is known to underestimate vibrational lifetimes of adsorbed H atoms
Box et al. 2024
, we expect that overestimation of non-adiabatic coupling for motion perpendicular to the surface leads to an overestimation of desorption probability in LDFA.
The presented simulation workflow is fully scalable, enabling the future study of steering effects in high coverage scenarios and surface facet dependence of desorption probability.
