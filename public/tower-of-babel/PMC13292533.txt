Clinico-biological trajectories stratified by combined tau biomarkers in preclinical Alzheimer’s disease
Authors: Miguel A Labrador-Espinosa, Nicolai Franzmeier, Stamatia Karagianni, Alexis Moscoso, Michael Schöll
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTau pathology biomarkers provide prognostic indicators of neurodegeneration and cognitive decline in Alzheimer’s disease (AD), making them crucial to early patient stratification for disease-modifying interventions. Plasma p-tau217 indexes early tau pathophysiology, whereas FDA/EMA-approved [18F]flortaucipir PET (tau-PET) visual assessments indicate advanced neurofibrillary tangle pathology. Here, we identified concordant and discordant profiles based on combined plasma p-tau217 status and tau-PET visual assessments in cognitively unimpaired amyloid-β (Aβ)-positive older adults and investigated whether these profiles delineate distinct longitudinal trajectories of Aβ and tau accumulation, neurodegeneration, and cognitive decline.MethodsWe included 330 cognitively unimpaired Aβ-PET-positive participants (72.2 ± 4.8 years; 58% female; 48% randomized to receive solanezumab) from the A4 Study, who underwent plasma p-tau217 and tau-PET at baseline. Plasma positivity (T1 +) followed A4 core criteria as previously reported (≥ 0.28 U/mL), while tau-PET positivity (T2 +) was determined by three expert visual readers with strong inter-rater agreement (κ = 0.954). Participants were stratified as T1[− / +]T2[− / +] at baseline and were followed for 5.0 ± 1.7 years. Baseline and longitudinal differences in regional Aβ- and tau-PET SUVR, MRI-measured gray-matter (GM) volume, and cognitive performance were examined using ANCOVA and mixed-effects models. All models accounted for treatment, with additional sensitivity analyses excluding treated participants.ResultsAt baseline, 57% were negative-concordant (T1 − T2 − : n = 187), 24% were discordant (T1 + T2 − : n = 53; T1 − T2 + : n = 27), and 19% were positive-concordant (T1 + T2 + : n = 63). T1 + T2 + profile constituted the highest-risk state, showing the greatest baseline cortical Aβ burden, the strongest neocortical tau progression (fronto-temporo-parietal pattern; p(FDR) < 0.05), marked baseline atrophy with accelerated longitudinal GM loss in overlapping regions, and the fastest cognitive decline (PACC: d = -1.84, p < 0.001). Consistently, T1 + T2 + exhibited the highest hazard of progression to more advanced clinico-biological stages over follow-up (HRClinical = 3.03, HRBiological = 9.98; p < 0.001). Discordant profiles showed comparatively limited progression, suggesting earlier or low-tau states. Results were essentially unchanged after excluding treated participants.ConclusionsIntegrating plasma p-tau217 with tau-PET visual assessment reveals clinically meaningful tau-biomarker heterogeneity in preclinical AD. Our findings highlight the value of combining these biomarkers to refine early risk prediction and support prioritization strategies for prevention trials. The frequency of discordance also motivates refining tau-PET visual assessments beyond binary classification (e.g., ordinal/semi-quantitative staging) to better capture subtle early tau signal.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13195-026-02123-3.Keywords: Alzheimer’s disease, Preclinical AD, Amyloid-β, Tau, P-tau217, PET, MRI, Cognitive decline

Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is characterized by the progressive accumulation of amyloid-β (Aβ) plaques and neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs) of tau protein [1], which play a crucial role in a neurodegenerative cascade that disrupts neural function and culminates in irreversible dementia [2, 3]. An accurate understanding of AD prognosis through pathology-specific biomarkers is essential for identifying at-risk patients who may benefit from early disease-modifying therapies prior to the onset of neurodegeneration and cognitive decline [4–7].

Positron emission tomography (PET) imaging of Aβ has demonstrated clinical utility in the diagnostic evaluation of patients with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or dementia of uncertain etiology [8]. However, the prognostic implications of Aβ-positivity at earlier AD stages remain limited, as Aβ-positivity can be observed in older individuals who may remain cognitively asymptomatic over their lifetime [9, 10] and exhibit no in vivo evidence of regional AD-related neurodegeneration on structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) [11]. In contrast, NFT pathology is widely recognized to be more closely associated with neurodegeneration and cognitive decline [12, 13], establishing tau proteinopathy as a pivotal target for early prognostic assessment in AD [4]. Among emerging biomarkers, plasma phosphorylated mid-region tau (p-tau) fragments show cost-effective promise for detecting early AD-related pathophysiological changes, as abnormal plasma p-tau levels reflect a pathophysiological response to Aβ deposition that mechanistically links Aβ and tau pathologies [14–16]. Plasma p-tau217 has been established as a primary biomarker for detection of Aβ pathology by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and has also demonstrated remarkable performance in detecting early tau aggregation and predicting clinical progression [17, 18]. However, plasma p-tau217 may be limited for accurate risk stratification as it provides no regional information on pathological brain changes and its signal represents a mix of Aβ and tau pathology, therefore making it less tau-specific biomarker [16, 19]. These limitations highlight the need for clinical biomarkers that can directly capture the regional burden of tau pathology in vivo. In this context, tau-PET imaging has demonstrated strong potential in detecting regional tau pathology across the AD continuum [20, 21]. Recently, tau-PET with [18F]flortaucipir received approval by the FDA and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for clinical use, providing a standardized approach for detecting NFT pathology [22, 23]. Under the FDA/EMA-approved visual interpretation framework [24, 25], a positive assessment of [18F]flortaucipir PET scans confirms the presence of neuropathologically established NFT and indicates intermediate-to-high AD neuropathologic change (ADNC) [24]. Consistent with its clinical relevance, individuals with visually assessed tau-PET positivity exhibit a markedly higher risk of progression to MCI or dementia even from preclinical AD stages [25, 26].

New revised diagnostic criteria for AD emphasize the integration of fluid-based and neuroimaging-based biomarkers to stage the clinico-biological severity of the disease and to enhance prognostic accuracy [27]. Consistent with this framework, prior studies demonstrated a close, yet diverse relationship between plasma p-tau217 and tau-PET across the AD clinical spectrum, while also suggesting that tau-PET may be more robustly associated with downstream neurodegeneration and longitudinal cognitive decline than plasma p-tau217 [14, 19, 28]. These findings indicate that plasma p-tau217 and tau-PET capture related but non-identical aspects of AD-related tau pathophysiology, supporting their complementary value for disease staging and prognostic stratification. A recent study demonstrated that incorporating both plasma p-tau217 levels and tau-PET measures as predictors in combined-biomarker models consistently led to a more accurate forecast of cognitive decline compared to single-biomarker models [29]. In this context, staging may go beyond simply cross-modal biomarkers mixture by positioning individuals along the temporal course of tau-related biomarker abnormalities and refining prognostic trajectories of downstream AD hallmarks through complementary pathophysiological information. However, the prognostic value of plasma p-tau217 status in combination with FDA/EMA-approved tau-PET visual assessments to predict the trajectories of neuroimaging-detectable regional AD hallmarks (i.e., Aβ and tau burden, and neurodegeneration) in asymptomatic individuals remains to be explored. Furthermore, the recently proposed clinico-biological staging framework has not yet been examined in prospective cohorts of asymptomatic AD individuals stratified by combined plasma p-tau217 and visual tau-PET assessments.

In this study, we investigated the prognostic value of combining plasma p-tau217 (T1) with FDA/EMA-approved tau-PET visual assessments (T2) in preclinical AD. Specifically, we stratify a well-characterized sample of cognitively unimpaired individuals with Aβ-PET–confirmed AD enrolled on the Anti-Amyloid Treatment in Asymptomatic Alzheimer’s (A4) Study into combined tau-status groups (T1[–/+]T2[–/+]), and examine their biomarker-stratified trajectories of (i) longitudinal regional Aβ and tau accumulation on PET, (ii) longitudinal regional neurodegeneration on MRI, and (iii) cognitive decline. To further enhance the clinical interpretability, we applied the recently defined AD staging framework to characterize longitudinal trajectories of the clinico-biological stages and quantify progression risk across the combined tau biomarker groups.

Methods

Participants

Our study included a total of 330 cognitively unimpaired AD individuals as confirmed by Aβ-PET positivity enrolled in the A4 study. The A4 study is a multicenter clinical trial specifically designed to ensure a high prevalence of Aβ pathology through enrichment with individuals in asymptomatic AD—Stage 1 of the NIA-AA criteria and to test solanezumab in the preclinical stages (48% of our sample received solanezumab during the first 240 weeks). Details of the screening process have been described previously [30] and briefly summarized in Supplementary Methods 1.

All asymptomatic AD individuals included in our current study were selected based on described screening criteria and the availability of core ADNC biomarkers at the baseline study visit. These included Aβ-PET imaging to determine Aβ positivity (A +), plasma p-tau217 levels as a marker of tau phosphorylation (T1), and tau-PET imaging to assess the presence of NTFs (T2). Flowchart of the participants inclusion are detailed in the Supplementary Methods 2. We also used normative tau-PET imaging data from 52 cognitively normal controls (70 ± 5 years old, 61% female) enrolled in the A4-LEARN Study as a healthy reference for clinico-biological stage classification. These healthy participants were negative for Aβ-PET, plasma p-tau217 and tau-PET (Supplementary Methods 3), and included Aβ-PET-negative (A −) A4 participants according to the criteria described below (n = 19, 37%) and Aβ-PET-negative (A −) LEARN participants who were A4 screen-fails (n = 33, 63%). For up-to-date information on the A4-LEARN study visit https://www.a4studydata.org/.

Core AD biomarkers at baseline

Aβ-PET scans positivity was determined using the method established by the A4 Study Imaging Researchers, as previously described [31]. Briefly, Aβ-PET scans were obtained using the [18F]florbetapir tracer and cortical standardized uptake value ratios (SUVR) were calculated using a cerebellar reference. Baseline mean SUVR values were converted to the standard Centiloid scale [32] and positivity for Aβ-PET scans was defined as a Centiloid value higher than 24.4 [33]. By design, all individuals included in the study were confirmed to be Aβ-positive (A +) at baseline.

Plasma p-tau217 levels were analyzed on a validated ECL immunoassay using a MesoScale (MSD) Sector SImager 600 MM at the CAP-accredited, CLIA-certified Lilly Clinical Diagnostics Laboratory. Detailed procedures for blood sample collection and plasma p-tau217 analysis have been described previously [34]. According to recent reports by Sperling et al. [35], abnormally high plasma p-tau217 levels at baseline were defined as those in the highest tertile of the full A4 Study cohort to identify individuals at greatest risk of tau pathology and cognitive decline. Importantly, this p-tau217 positivity threshold was not intended to define Aβ-positivity, as all participants were Aβ-PET positive by design. Rather, p-tau217 positivity was used as a marker of increased risk of tau pathology. Individuals with plasma p-tau217 levels above the established cut-off of 0.28 were classified as p-tau217 positive (T1 +) at baseline. Consistently, this threshold was subsequently validated in our sample of asymptomatic AD individuals using complementary analyses to estimate a plasma p-tau217 cutoff corresponding to tau-PET visual positivity (Supplementary Methods 4, Supplementary Table 1).

Tau-PET scans were obtained using [18F]flortaucipir tracer and were classified as either positive or negative using the FDA/EMA-approved visual interpretation method [25]. This visual-read approach was selected to closely approximate a clinically interpretable assessment used in routine diagnostic settings. This method defines a scan as positive if it exhibits increased signal in posterolateral temporal, occipital, and/or parietal/precuneus regions, with or without elevated signal in frontal areas. Tau-PET scans were assessed by a team of three readers, including nuclear medicine physicians and neuroimaging researchers, who were trained using materials provided by Avid Radiopharmaceuticals to interpret [18F]flortaucipir PET scans. The robustness of our visual interpretation procedure was validated in inter-rater agreement analyses using bootstrap resampling (n = 1000 iterations; Fleiss’s κ = 0.954, 95% CI: [0.919–0.981]). Individuals with positivity for tau-PET visual assessments were classified as tau-PET positive (T2 +) at baseline.

Groups stratified by tau biomarkers

Participants were classified into four groups based on combination of plasma p-tau217 and tau-PET positivity at baseline, following the new nomenclature proposed by the revised criteria for diagnosis and staging of AD [27] for core tau biomarkers: T1− T2− (negative-concordant group), T1+ T2− and T1− T2+ (discordant groups), and T1+ T2+ (positive-concordant group).

Longitudinal multimodal neuroimaging

Longitudinal multimodal neuroimaging data were collected within a flexible follow-up design across imaging modalities to maximize the use of available data. All participants had complete multimodal neuroimaging data at baseline, while the number of follow-up scans varied by participant and modality over a mean follow-up period of 4.5 ± 1.6 years: Aβ-PET scans (median of 2 scans per participant; max follow-up: 240 weeks), tau-PET scans (median of 4 scans per participant; max follow-up: 312 weeks), and structural MRI scans (median of 6 scans per participant; max follow-up: 312 weeks). The proportion of participants with baseline-only data for each specific modality was 24% for Aβ-PET (n = 81), 10% for tau-PET (n = 34), and 0% for structural MRI (n = 0), with detailed follow-up distributions reported in Supplementary Table 2.

High-resolution structural MRI scans were acquired using a three-dimensional (3D) magnetization-prepared rapid acquisition gradient echo (MPRAGE) T1-weighted sequence on 3 T MRI scanners from Siemens, GE, and Philips systems, following a harmonized MRI acquisition protocol across A4/LEARN sites [36]. Detailed scanner information and acquisition metadata are available through the A4/LEARN imaging data repository (https://www.a4studydata.org/). T1-weighted structural MRI scans were used to assess regional cortical volume using established longitudinal processing pipelines for voxel-based morphometry implemented in the Computational Anatomy Toolbox (CAT12, Jena University Hospital) running under the Statistical Parametric Mapping package (SPM12, Wellcome Trust Center for Neuroimaging) in Matlab R2024a (MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA) [37, 38]. Briefly, MRI scans were segmented into gray matter (GM), white matter (WM), and CSF partitions and high-dimensionally registered to Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) standard space. Regional GM volumes were then automatically extracted by summing up the modulated GM voxel values within bilateral regions of interest (ROI) encompassing the entire cortical GM map as defined in the Harvard–Oxford atlas [39]. The total intracranial volume (TIV), as a measure of head-size, was calculated as the sum of total GM, WM, and CSF partitions and averaged across the longitudinal measurements.

Processing of Aβ- and tau-PET scans similarly followed our established in-house pipeline for molecular imaging [40]. Each subject’ PET scan was rigidly co-registered to the corresponding structural MRI scan and corrected for partial volume effects (PVEs) using the Müller–Gärtner method implemented in the PETPVE12 toolbox in SPM12 [41]. PVE-corrected PET scans were warped to the MNI reference space using the deformation flow fields derived from the normalization of the corresponding MRI scans. Normalized PET scans were intensity-scaled using the cerebellum as the reference region to generate SUVR maps. Mean SUVR values within the cortical ROIs was also extracted automatically by calculating the mean signal within the bilateral ROIs as defined in the Harvard–Oxford atlas [39, 42].

Longitudinal cognitive assessments

Longitudinal cognitive assessments were collected over a mean follow-up of 5.0 ± 1.7 years (median of 12 assessments per participant; max follow-up: 384 weeks), with only 1.5% of participants having baseline-only cognitive data (n = 5; detailed follow-up distributions are reported in Supplementary Table 3). Cognitive performance was assessed using the four-component Preclinical Alzheimer’s Cognitive Composite (PACC) [43], which consists of tests that capture episodic memory by Free and Cued Selective Reminding Test (FCSRT) and delayed recall on Logical Memory IIa (LMIIa) test, executive function by Digit-Symbol Substitution Test (DSST), and the global cognition by Mini Mental Status Examination (MMSE). Additional cognitive outcomes included the Clinical Dementia Rating – Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) [44], as well as participant– and study partner–reported assessments of the Cognitive Function Index (CFI) [45] and the Activities of Daily Living Prevention Instrument (ADL) [46].

Clinico-biological stages

Longitudinal clinico-biological stages were categorized in alphanumeric segments by combining biological and clinical stages classifications, following the framework described by Jack et al. [27]. Clinical stages were determined using serial CDR-SB assessments according to established criteria [44], as follows: asymptomatic (Stage 1: score = 0), subtle cognitive deficits (Stage 2: score ≥ 0.5 to < 2.5), mild cognitive impairment (Stage 3: ≥ 2.5 to < 4.0), and dementia (Stage 4: ≥ 4.0). Biological stages were defined using age- and sex-adjusted tau-PET w-scores derived from the healthy control sample, with abnormal tau-PET signal defined as w-score ≥ 2 within previously reported stage-specific composite regions [47]: the medial temporal lobe (MTL) composite region (including amygdala, parahippocampal and entorhinal cortex) for initial and early stages (stage A if below and stage B if above the 2-SD threshold), the temporal lobe composite region (including inferior and middle temporal gyri) for the intermediate stage (stage C), and the neocortical composite region for the advanced stage (stage D) (see Supplementary Methods 5). Stages C and D were distinguished using predefined tau-PET composite ROIs designed to capture increasing topographical extent of tau involvement, which differs slightly from the AA workgroup framework, where these stages are described as differing by the level of tau burden within a neocortical ROI.

Statistical analyses

Baseline differences in regional Aβ and tau burden, regional GM volume, and cognitive performance between groups were examined using contrasts of estimated marginal means from ANCOVA models controlling for sex, age, years of education, treatment, and TIV (when using MRI-derived volume measures). Longitudinal changes in these clinical outcomes were examined using linear mixed-effects models controlling for the same covariates. Mixed-effects models included follow-up time (weeks) and all covariates as fixed-effects, and participant-specific intercepts and their effects over time as random effects. Mixed-effects models were fitted using maximum likelihood estimators. Standardized effect sizes for baseline differences and longitudinal changes were expressed as Cohen’s d to facilitate direct comparability across all tests. Specifically, effect sizes between groups were computed for baseline contrasts using the estimated marginal mean difference (d = [ 1– 2]/σ) and for longitudinal contrasts using the estimated marginal mean difference in slopes (d = [β1–β2]/σ), where σ defined as the pooled residual standard deviation from the corresponding model. All statistical effects were corrected for multiple comparisons using the false discovery rate (FDR) and results were assessed at a threshold of PFDR < 0.05. Additional sensitivity analyses excluding individuals who received solanezumab were conducted to minimize potential treatment-related effects.

Complementary analyses evaluated within-group longitudinal associations between cognitive decline (PACC scores), regional Aβ and tau accumulation (SUVR change), and cortical GM loss (volume) using Pearson’s correlations of individual slopes (rates of change) for these outcomes. Individual slopes in PET-based SUVR values and MRI-based GM volumes were summarized as global weighted-average slopes across ROIs that showed significant longitudinal change (PFDR < 0.05) over time, using t-values for the time effect as weights. Mediation effects were further examined using path analyses with non-parametric bootstrap with 1000 resamples (see Supplementary Methods 6).

Clinico-biological staging trajectories were determined for each group using longitudinal estimates of CDR-SB scores and tau-PET SUVR values in the composite MTL, temporal lobe, and neocortical regions (Supplementary Methods 5). Longitudinal estimates were obtained from linear mixed-effects models controlled for sex, age, years of education, and treatment. Specifically, longitudinal estimates of tau-PET SUVR values were modelled separately for each stage-specific composite region. Estimated SUVR values were then converted into w-scores relative to the healthy control sample using age- and sex-adjusted normative standardization. Clinico-biological stages were subsequently assigned according to the criteria described above. This procedure allowed sequencing of longitudinal clinico-biological staging progression across the follow-up period (0–400 weeks). Estimated staging trajectories were further characterized by the observed longitudinal distribution across groups at each visit with concurrent tau-PET scans and CDR-SB assessments (follow-up weeks 0, 84, 180, 240, and 312). Stratified risks of progressing to intermediate/advanced biological stages (stages C and D) and to clinical stages of cognitive impairment (stages ≥ 2) was estimated across groups using Cox regression models.

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Overall, 35% (n = 116) of participants were plasma p-tau217 positive (T1 +), and 27% (n = 90) exhibited visually positive tau-PET scans (T2 +). Stratification based on tau biomarkers revealed heterogeneity within the sample: 187 participants (57%) were classified as T1 − T2 −, 53 (16%) as T1 + T2 −, 27 (8%) as T1 − T2 +, and 63 (19%) as T1 + T2 +. APOE-ε4 positivity was present in 60% of the total sample (n = 197), with a higher prevalence in T1 + T2 + individuals (71%) than in T1 − T2 − (57%, p = 0.05) and T1 + T2 − individuals (51%, p = 0.03).

Concordant-T1/T2-positive individuals show highest fibrillar Aβ/tau burden at baseline and fastest tau accumulation

Baseline differences and longitudinal changes in brain-wide regional Aβ accumulation between groups are shown in Fig. 1 and detailed statistical results are reported in Supplementary Tables 4 and 5. At baseline, T1 + T2 + individuals showed significantly higher Aβ burden than the T1 − T2 − group in widespread cortical areas, with pronounced differences in lateral temporal areas (notably superior temporal gyrus) and fronto-parietal cortices (peak-effect in the precuneus cortex: d = 1.50; =1.28 [1.04, 1.53]; t = 10.23; p < 0.001). In contrast, both T1 + T2 − and T1 − T2 + differed from T1 − T2 − in cortical Aβ-PET signal, but with smaller effect sizes than T1 + T2 + and no significant differences between the two groups (see Supplementary Fig. 1). A similar pattern was observed for baseline Centiloid values (Table 1). Longitudinally, T1 + T2 + individuals showed no cortical changes in Aβ accumulation compared with the T1 − T2 − group, and no significant changes remained in the T1 + T2 − or T1 − T2 + groups after correction for multiple comparisons. Similar baseline and longitudinal differences in regional Aβ accumulation were observed after excluding participants who received treatment (Supplementary Fig. 2). Overall, group differences in Aβ were most evident at baseline, while longitudinal Aβ changes were minimal.

Analogously, baseline differences and longitudinal changes in brain-wide regional tau accumulation between groups are shown in Fig. 2 and detailed statistical results are reported in Supplementary Tables 6 and 7. At baseline, as expected and consistent with the study design, T1 + T2 + individuals confirmed significantly greater tau burden than T1 − T2 − in temporo-parietal cortices, with particularly pronounced differences in the parahippocampal cortex and inferior–middle temporal regions (peak effect in the temporo-occipital part of inferior temporal gyrus: d = 1.96; =1.56 [1.33, 1.79]; t = 13.38; p < 0.001). In contrast, T1 − T2 + showed only modest, widespread increase in cortical burden, whereas no significant differences remained in T1 + T2 − individuals after correction for multiple comparisons; both groups exhibited significantly lower tau burden than T1 + T2 + (Supplementary Fig. 3). Longitudinally, T1 + T2 + individuals showed a notable increase in tau accumulation compared to T1 − T2 − in fronto-temporo-parietal cortices, with pronounced increases in the lateral temporal lobe, precuneus, and parietal regions (peak effect in the angular gyrus: d = 3.01; β = 0.60 [0.51, 0.68]; t = 13.66; p < 0.001). In comparison, T1 + T2 − and T1 − T2 + exhibited slight cortical increases in cortical tau accumulation compared to T1 − T2 −, but both were significantly lower than in T1 + T2 +. Similar baseline and longitudinal differences in regional tau accumulation were observed after excluding participants who received treatment (Supplementary Fig. 4). Taken together, T1 + T2 + showed the highest baseline tau burden and the strongest cortical tau progression over time.

Concordant-T1/T2-positive individuals exhibit pronounced baseline atrophy and the fastest neurodegeneration

Baseline differences and longitudinal changes in brain-wide regional GM volume between groups are shown in Fig. 3 and detailed statistical results are reported in Supplementary Tables 8 and 9. At baseline, T1 + T2 + individuals exhibited significantly lower GM volume than T1 − T2 − in temporo-parietal cortices, with particularly pronounced differences in the lateral inferior–middle temporal regions (peak effect in the posterior division of middle temporal gyrus: d = −0.86; =−0.56 [−0.75, −0.37]; t = −5.84; p < 0.001). In other groups, only T1 − T2 + group showed modest widespread reduction in cortical GM volume compared to T1 − T2 −. Longitudinally, T1 + T2 + individuals showed significantly greater GM loss compared with T1 − T2 − in the cortical temporo-parietal areas, with pronounced changes in lateral parietal areas and lateral inferior temporal lobe (peak effect in the posterior division of middle temporal gyrus: d = −1.60; β = −0.20 [−0.24, −0.17]; t = −10.77; p < 0.001). In comparison, T1 + T2 − and T1 − T2 + groups exhibited slight cortical volume changes compared to T1 − T2 −, but no significant differences between them were observed and both were significantly weaker than T1 + T2 + group (see Supplementary Fig. 5). Similar baseline and longitudinal differences in regional GM loss were observed after excluding participants who received treatment (Supplementary Fig. 6). Together, T1 + T2 + showed the strongest pattern of baseline atrophy and accelerated GM loss.

Discussion

In this multimodal AD biomarker study, we investigated the prognostic value of combining plasma p-tau217 levels with FDA/EMA-approved tau-PET visual assessments in a well-characterized sample of asymptomatic individuals with Aβ-PET-confirmed AD. Our findings showed that a considerable 43% of individuals exhibited evidence of emerging tau pathophysiology in asymptomatic stages, as indicated by either visual tau-PET positivity or markedly elevated plasma p-tau217 levels, whereas 57% remained negative for both measures. Particularly, 23% were discordant between tau biomarkers (T1 + T2– or T1–T2 +) and only approximately 20% of participants were positive-concordant for both elevated plasma p-tau217 levels and tau-PET visual assessment (T1 + T2 +), representing the highest-risk group. Compared to the rest of the sample, these positive-concordant individuals demonstrated the greatest Aβ burden across widespread cortical regions and accelerated tau accumulation within fronto-temporo-parietal association areas. They further exhibited pronounced baseline cortical atrophy in overlapping regions, with accelerated neurodegeneration in these areas over time and the most pronounced cognitive decline. When contextualizing our findings relative to healthy controls to delineate clinico-biological trajectories, we observed that individuals with positivity for tau-PET visual assessment exhibited and progressed to advanced biological stages of AD, although only those stratified as T1 + T2 + ultimately reached the estimated clinical stage of dementia during the follow-up. Together, these findings identify the T1 + T2 + profile as the highest-risk state in preclinical AD, linking concordant tau-biomarker positivity to accelerated clinico-biological progression, including tau accumulation, neurodegeneration, and cognitive decline.

Previous neuropathological studies have reported that the FDA/EMA-approved criteria for tau-PET visual positivity correspond to neuropathologically advanced tau pathology (Braak stages V–VI) [24] and that high levels of plasma p-tau217 are associated with neurofibrillary tangle burden [16]. Interestingly, recent in vivo findings have shown that both tau-PET visual positivity and markedly elevated plasma p-tau217 are prevalent (20% − 40%) in asymptomatic Aβ-positive older adults [25, 48]. Consistent with these previous reports, our findings revealed a similar prevalence of asymptomatic older individuals exhibiting elevated plasma p-tau217 levels and visually assessed tau-PET positivity. However, among those with evidence of emerging tau pathology in asymptomatic stages, about half (56%) of individuals showed discrepancies between these tau biomarkers (24% of the total sample; T1 + T2 − : 16% and T1 − T2 + : 8%), whereas the remaining 44% were positive-concordant (T1 + T2 + : 19% of the total sample). Similar discrepancies between these tau biomarkers in Aβ-positive individuals have been reported across the AD continuum [49]. These data may denote that T1 + T2 − individuals reflect an earlier transitional state in which elevations of soluble p-tau217 precede PET-detectable fibrillar tau [50, 51]. However, the T1 + T2 − profile should not be interpreted solely as reflecting an early soluble tau-related state, as it may also include individuals with higher Aβ burden without detectable tau-PET signal and/or low-level tau-PET signal below the sensitivity of the visual-read threshold. Conversely, T1 − T2 + (a less frequent profile) most plausibly reflects focally elevated tau on visual tau-PET near current regional sensitivity limits of plasma assays, interindividual variability in tau dynamics, and/or potential false-positive visual assessments. These interpretations are further supported by complementary quantitative data in stage-specific tau-PET composite regions, where T1 + T2 − individuals showed only a subtle MTL increase relative to healthy controls (Supplementary Fig. 10). Consistently, individual SUVR projection maps from representative T1 + T2 − cases did not show visually detectable neocortical tau-PET positivity, whereas T1 − T2 + cases showed visual tau-PET positivity ranging from focal uptake to a more widespread temporo-parietal pattern (Supplementary Fig. 11, 12, 13 and 14). Taken together, these findings likely indicate that plasma p-tau217 status and visual tau-PET assessments capture partially overlapping but non-identical aspects of tau pathology in asymptomatic Aβ-positive individuals, and that integrating both biomarkers may improve the characterization of early tau states and reduce false-positive classification of tau pathology [29, 52].

As a key contribution, our findings demonstrated that the positive-concordant group represents the highest-risk state within the canonical regional Aβ–tau–neurodegeneration cascade in asymptomatic stages. Compared to discordant and negative-concordant profiles, positive-concordant individuals showed the highest—and most spatially extensive—cortical Aβ burden at baseline, which persisted cortically over time [53]. In parallel, the positive-concordant group showed the greatest longitudinal tau accumulation across neocortical fronto-temporo-parietal regions. These findings support that combined positivity for elevated plasma p-tau217 and visual tau-PET assessments captures the most advanced state of Aβ and tau pathology in preclinical stages and accurately identifies asymptomatic AD individuals at high risk of further tau accumulation, which is consistent with a model in which prominent cortical Aβ accelerates the emergence and spread of tau pathology [20, 51, 54]. Moreover, the positive-concordant group exhibited the lowest GM volume in overlapping tau-related areas and notably fastest cortical volume loss, supporting tau as the principal driver of downstream neurodegeneration [55–57]. In contrast, the discordant groups showed lower baseline Aβ and tau burdens, only light longitudinal accumulation, and correspondingly less cortical neurodegeneration at both baseline and follow-up. These findings should be considered by the fact that a visual T2 + classification is dichotomous and does not capture the continuum of tau burden; therefore, T2 + can range from focal/low-level uptake to widespread/high tau pathology (Supplementary Fig. 14). This likely explains why the discordant T1 − T2 + group shows substantially lower baseline tau than T1 + T2 +, suggesting discordant T2 + cases may represent low-tau visual positives near threshold (Supplementary Fig. 15). This interpretation supports the potential value of complementing FDA/EMA visual reads with semi-quantitative measures of tau burden or spatial extent to better characterize focal/low-level tau-PET positivity within visually T2 + individuals. As a clinically feasible refinement, T2 + could be further stratified into low/intermediate/high tau levels based on the extent of visually positive regions (as implemented in the TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 Trial on donanemab and consistent with our regional patterns) [58], to better distinguish early from advanced tau and improve staging and risk interpretation. Additional investigations are needed to refine visual tau-PET stratification and its integration with plasma tau assays, thereby improving staging and risk prediction.

Our regional neuroimaging findings were consistent with cognitive trajectories. While asymptomatic individuals in discordant groups already showed subtle baseline deficits and measurable decline, the positive-concordant group constituted the highest-risk stratum, with the lowest baseline performance and the steepest subsequent decline. These results are consistent with recent reports indicating that elevated plasma p-tau217 and quantitative tau-PET positivity may be used to identify individuals at greatest risk of progression in the preclinical stage [59] and across the AD continuum [49]. Furthermore, in our study, both tau accumulation and cortical neurodegeneration in overlapping regions were associated with cognitive decline in positive-concordant individuals, and path analyses demonstrated that the link between tau accumulation and cognitive decline was mediated by cortical atrophy exclusively in those with positive-concordant tau biomarkers. Recent studies have similarly reported cross-sectional and longitudinal mediation effects of cortical atrophy in the association between tau burden and cognitive decline across the Aβ-positive AD continuum [60, 61]. Our findings extend to existing literature by demonstrating that the longitudinal mediation of the tau–neurodegeneration–cognition is detectable even in the asymptomatic stages of individuals with advanced tau pathology, as identified by combined tau biomarkers. Nevertheless, this pathway interpretation relies on observational mediation models, from which causality cannot be inferred and unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded. In addition, as these complementary analyses were based on individually derived longitudinal slopes, they do not preserve the full temporal ordering of repeated measurements, and future studies using time-resolved mediation approaches are needed to formally test the temporal sequence linking tau accumulation, cortical atrophy, and cognitive decline across biomarker-defined profiles.

Distinct clinico-biological progression across groups was confirmed by applying the recent AD staging framework [27]. The positive-concordant group involved the highest-risk individuals and exhibited the most pronounced progression from intermediate to advanced biological stages, spanning the full clinical continuum from asymptomatic to dementia during follow-up. In line with other recent evidence on combined tau biomarkers in AD [29, 49], these findings support the simultaneous use of plasma p-tau217 and visual tau-PET to identify asymptomatic individuals at highest risk of progression and to help define accurate prioritization criteria for early intervention in disease-modifying therapy trials. Moreover, our findings may open new avenues for hypothesis generation regarding the therapeutic effects that can realistically be expected within each stratified group. Individuals within the spectrum from T1 − T2 − to T1 + T2 −, who appear biologically less advanced and only subtly affected clinically [47], may preferentially benefit from early Aβ-directed monotherapy and may be best suited for close preventive monitoring of tau emergence before widespread neurodegeneration ensues [58, 62]. Future studies incorporating early risk stratification are required to establish whether this approach enhances the clinical efficacy of disease-modifying therapies.

Further refinement is also needed to determine how plasma p-tau217 and visual tau-PET assessments are integrated for early AD staging. Two-cutoff approaches may provide additional granularity for interpreting intermediate plasma p-tau217 levels within Aβ-positive preclinical AD, particularly among individuals with discordant or borderline biomarker profiles [63]. In addition, future studies with repeated plasma p-tau217 measures and longitudinal tau-PET visual-reads should evaluate the temporal stability of these T1/T2 profiles and transitions between discordant and concordant tau-biomarker states over time [50, 59]. Together, these approaches may help determine whether discordant profiles represent stable biomarker states, transitional phases, or stage-dependent patterns across preclinical and symptomatic AD.

Our study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the participants in this study were volunteers who met eligibility criteria for a prevention trial targeting cognitive decline in preclinical stages, and thus may not represent a generalizable population. They were generally in good health, highly motivated to participate in repeated assessments over several years and had a higher prevalence of family history of dementia compared with the general older adult population. Nonetheless, this scenario may also be a strength as it partially mirrors the clinical context in which asymptomatic individuals are enrolled for early risk stratification and potential disease-modifying interventions [64]. Second, approximately half of the participants in our sample received solanezumab during the initial weeks of the A4 clinical trial, which could have influenced the observed effects. However, all primary analyses accounted for treatment, and sensitivity analyses excluding treated participants yielded essentially unchanged results, indicating that any treatment-related impact on our findings was minimal. Third, although our findings are informed by the controlled research environment of the A4 cohort, reliance on a single cohort may have introduced bias in the estimated frequencies of plasma p-tau217 and tau-PET positivity. The absence of an independent validation cohort limits the generalizability of these frequency estimates and overall findings, highlighting the need for replication in external samples in future studies. Nevertheless, similar positivity rates for these tau biomarkers have been reported in other preclinical cohorts (e.g., TRIAD, ADNI, and K-ROAD), suggesting that any such bias is likely modest [49]. Notably, the unequal size of the T1/T2 biomarker groups, particularly the relatively small size of the T1 − T2 + group, may limit the statistical power and precision for discordant-group comparisons. Although linear mixed-effects models can accommodate unbalanced repeated-measures data and were adjusted for relevant covariates, estimates for this less frequent subgroup remain inherently more uncertain than those from larger groups. Fourth, tau-PET visual positivity is a dichotomous classification and does not capture the full spectrum of tau burden; consequently, it may classify positive individuals with markedly different levels and spatial distributions of tau pathology, introducing clinical and biomarker heterogeneity within T2 + groups. In addition, regional tau-PET variability may contribute to discrepancies between visual T2 positivity and clinico-biological staging, particularly when posterior/occipital-predominant patterns are not fully captured by the stage-specific composites used for biological staging [65, 66]. At the same time, the FDA/EMA visual assessment and the quantitative clinico-biological staging framework differ methodologically in terms of thresholding, hemispheric assessment, and inclusion of MTL signal. These methodological differences should be considered when interpreting cross-framework characterizations. Importantly, the quantitative staging framework was not intended to replicate the FDA/EMA visual read, but rather to provide broader complementary measures of biological tau progression. Fifth, the FDA/EMA visual-read criteria for [1⁸F]flortaucipir were originally developed and validated primarily in symptomatic MCI and dementia populations, and their specificity in cognitively unimpaired individuals remains less well established. Although our excellent inter-rater agreement indicates reliable application of the criteria, it does not by itself confirm biological specificity or exclude systematic overclassification. Nevertheless, applying this clinically validated visual-read framework to asymptomatic Aβ-positive individuals is one of the novel aspects of the present study, and recent evidence using the FDA/EMA-approved method supports that visual tau-PET positivity can be detected and is clinically meaningful in cognitively unimpaired Aβ-positive individuals, with a reported prevalence of 25%, closely matching the 27% visual T2 + prevalence observed in our sample [25]. Sixth, although plasma p-tau217 is a highly sensitive and specific biomarker for AD pathology, the discordance observed between the tau biomarkers in preclinical AD may partly reflect heterogeneity in plasma p-tau217 assay cut-points used to detect tau pathology across previous studies [67]. However, abnormal plasma p-tau217 levels at baseline were defined as the highest tertile of the full A4-LEARN cohort, consistent with Sperling et al. (p-tau217 ≥ 0.28 U/mL) [35], thereby enriching for individuals at greatest risk of tau pathology and cognitive decline. Furthermore, to align biomarkers more directly—and because FDA/EMA-approved tau-PET visual assessments standardize detection of anatomically advanced tau pathology—we also conducted complementary analyses in our well-characterized asymptomatic AD sample to estimate a plasma p-tau217 threshold corresponding to tau-PET visual positivity; the resulting cut-point was practically identical (0.274 U/mL; Supplementary Methods 4 and Supplementary Table 1). Finally, blood-based tau measures can be influenced by chronic renal dysfunction, which represents a limitation and potentially may increase false-positive classification near established cut-points, highlighting the importance of considering kidney disease when interpreting plasma results [68].

Conclusions

Our study reveals clinically meaningful tau-biomarker heterogeneity in preclinical AD by integrating plasma p-tau217 status with visual tau-PET assessments in Aβ-positive asymptomatic older adults. Concordant positivity identifies a distinct highest-risk state characterized by the greatest baseline amyloidosis, the strongest neocortical tau progression, accelerated neurodegeneration, and the fastest cognitive decline, translating into progression toward advanced clinico-biological stages over follow-up. In contrast, discordant profiles show comparatively limited progression, consistent with earlier or low-tau states. Importantly, our findings also motivate refinement of tau-PET visual assessments beyond binary classification—by incorporating ordinal/semi-quantitative staging and regional pattern evaluation—to better capture subtle early tau signal. Overall, combined tau biomarkers can refine early risk prediction and support prioritization strategies for disease-modifying prevention trials in preclinical AD.
