Sarcomatoid adrenocortical carcinoma with aggressive disease course: a rare case report and literature review
Authors: Xin-Xin Xu, Guang-Fu Zhou, Cheng-Zhou Du, Xiao-Xin Gao, Xiao-Wei Guo, Ya-Long Li, Meng-Yuan Lu, Tian-Xiao He, Peng Chen, Hong-Tao Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSarcomatoid Adrenocortical Carcinoma (SAC) is an extremely rare, highly aggressive, and poorly differentiated subtype of Adrenocortical Carcinoma (ACC). To date, only 38 cases have been reported worldwide. No standardized clinical diagnosis and treatment protocols have been established, and the prognosis of affected patients is extremely poor. This study reports a case of SAC in a 60-year-old female patient presenting with an abdominal mass, aiming to analyze the clinical characteristics, diagnosis, and treatment features of this rare tumor.Case presentationA 60-year-old female was admitted to our hospital due to an upper abdominal mass. Imaging examinations revealed a cystic-solid space-occupying lesion in the left retroperitoneum, and the tumor was highly suspected to be of adrenal cortical origin preoperatively. Given the rapid disease progression and the patient’s strong demand for urgent surgery, preoperative endocrine function examinations including cortisol, aldosterone, sex hormones and catecholamines were not performed. Laparotomy with complete tumor resection was Sarcomatoid Adrenocortical Carcinomasubsequently performed, and the final pathological diagnosis was SAC. The tumor was composed of 60% carcinomatous components and 40% high-grade sarcomatous components, classified as Fuhrman nuclear grade IV, with extensive parenchymal necrosis and vascular tumor thrombi observed in the lesion. One month after surgery, the patient developed extensive peritoneal metastasis and ascites, and eventually died on the 42nd day postoperatively.ConclusionSAC has non-specific manifestations and high diagnostic difficulty, with histopathology plus immunohistochemistry as the diagnostic gold standard. Highly invasive and rapidly progressive, it is mainly treated with surgical resection by following ACC protocols, yet their pathological features differ significantly. Given the extremely limited number of reported cases, current evidence is insufficient to either support or refute the efficacy of mitotane for SAC, and more multicenter large-sample studies are urgently needed.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s12672-026-05500-4.Keywords: Sarcomatoid adrenocortical carcinoma, Immunohistochemical, Mitotane, Adrenocortical carcinoma staging systems, case report

Background

Sarcomatoid Adrenocortical Carcinoma (SAC) are an extremely rare and aggressive subtype of adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC), is an extremely rare, highly aggressive, and poorly differentiated subtype of ACC. Its diagnosis requires meticulous histological and immunohistochemical examinations [1]. The first known case was described by Okazumi et al. in 1987 [2]. A distinctive feature of these tumors is that they exhibit both carcinomatous and sarcomatous differentiation [3].

Case presentation

A 60-year-old female patient was admitted on June 18, 2025, with a chief complaint of “discovery of an upper abdominal mass for over one month”. One month prior, the patient without obvious cause, which progressively enlarged and was accompanied by distending pain and discomfort after eating. Upper abdominal ultrasound revealed an intra-abdominal space-occupying lesion, considered to be a retroperitoneal tumor, and the patient was admitted as an outpatient with “intra-abdominal tumor”. Post-admission contrast-enhanced abdominal CT revealed: 1. A left retroperitoneal cystic-solid mass with locally unclear boundaries with the pancreatic tail, mostly considered as (1) solid pseudopapillary tumor, (2) mesenchymal tumor; left kidney and renal vein were compressed, and the left ovarian vein was significantly thickened and tortuous. 2. Enhanced nodule in the right posterior lobe of the liver during the arterial phase, mostly considered as hemangioma (Fig. 1). Preoperative tumor marker levels were as follows: AFP 2.40 µg/L, CEA 1.49ng/mL, CA-125 8.9U/mL, CA-153 4.5U/mL, CA-199 5.3U/mL. Based on the relevant examination results, the tumor was most likely of adrenocortical origin. After evaluating the patient’s general condition and excluding surgical contraindications, exploratory laparotomy was performed on June 23, 2025. During the operation, the mass was found to be located in front of the left kidney; the entire mass was meticulously dissected and completely resected, with careful protection of adjacent organs and blood vessels. Gross pathological examination revealed an irregular grayish-white to grayish-red tissue specimen measuring 11.0 cm × 9.0 cm × 9.0 cm; the cut surface was grayish-red and soft in texture. (Supplementary Fig. 1). Immunohistochemical results of the mass yielded that CKp (±), EMA (±), Inhibin-A (Focal+), Melan-A (+), Calretinin (−), Vimentin (+), Desmin (partial sarcomatous component+), SMA (partial sarcomatous component+), CD56 (+), Ki67 (sarcomatous component index ≈ 40%). Pathological diagnosis demonstrated that morphological changes combined with immunohistochemical staining results were consistent with adrenal cortical carcinosarcoma. The carcinomatous component corresponded to the conventional subtype of adrenal cortical carcinoma, accounting for approximately 60% of the tumor; the sarcomatous component was identified as high-grade sarcoma, comprising roughly 40%. The tumor was assigned a Fuhrman nuclear grade IV. Extensive parenchymal necrosis and vascular tumor thrombi were observed in the lesion. (Table 1; Fig. 2). In accordance with the 2022 WHO classification of adrenal cortical tumors, the final diagnosis was SAC (ICD-10: C74.00; ICD-11: 2D11.0) [4], a rare aggressive subtype of adrenocortical carcinoma composed of mixed epithelial and sarcomatoid components. Retrospective analysis of published literature indicated that the average survival of affected patients was around seven months. The patient was followed up postoperatively. Abdominal distension developed one month after surgery and the patient subsequently sought medical care locally. Imaging examination confirmed massive ascites, and the patient passed away on postoperative day 42.

Discussion

A comprehensive search of the PubMed database identified 38 case reports on SAC, including the case presented herein (Table 2). Analysis of these cases revealed a patient age range at diagnosis from 23 to 80 years and an exceedingly poor overall prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate below 15% [5]. Published literature indicated a range of postoperative survival periods from a mere 2 days [6] to 30 months [7], with an average of 7.2 months. We posit that this dismal prognosis may be intrinsically linked to delays in diagnosis. Fine-needle aspiration biopsy in the diagnostic workup of SAC carries a potential risk of tumor dissemination; consequently, histopathological examination of the resected tumor remains the diagnostic “gold standard” [8]. The immunohistochemical staining results of the tissue specimen from this case provided critical evidence for the diagnosis and pathological classification of the disease. The results demonstrated that the molecular markers associated with the carcinomatous component in the tumor tissue, including CKp, EMA, inhibin-A, and Melan-A, all exhibited positive expression, indicating the presence of an epithelial-derived carcinomatous component in the tumor. Meanwhile, the mesenchymal markers related to the sarcomatous component, namely vimentin, desmin, and SMA, also showed positive expression, which confirmed the existence of a mesenchymal-derived sarcomatous component in the tumor and was consistent with the typical pathological feature of carcinosarcoma characterized by the coexistence of dual components. In addition, the positive expression of neuroendocrine-related markers CD56 and Syn suggested that the tumor might have neuroendocrine differentiation characteristics, thus providing supporting evidence for the heterogeneity of the disease.

Studies have indicated that only a small fraction (3%) of cases present with a maximum tumor diameter of ≤4 cm at diagnosis; therefore, clinicians should maintain a high index of suspicion for malignant lesions such as ACC in patients presenting with adrenal tumors exceeding 5 cm in diameter [9]. In adult primary SAC, the main somatic genetic alterations occur in genes that encode proteins involved in the WNT-β-catenin pathway, cell cycle and p53 apoptosis pathway, chromatin remodelling and telomere maintenance pathway, cAMP–protein kinase A pathway or DNA transcription and RNA translation pathways [10]. Given the extreme rarity of SAC, dedicated research remains limited, and standardized treatment protocols are lacking. Current therapeutic approaches often extrapolate from the diagnostic and therapeutic principles established for ACC, where surgical resection combined with postoperative adjuvant therapy represents the cornerstone of treatment [11]. Specifically, radical surgical resection offers the only potentially curative option for patients with stage I, II, and select stage III disease (Table 3) [12]. Based on the established diagnostic and staging criteria for ACC, the present case was assigned a pathological stage of pT4N0M0, corresponding to Stage IV disease. In the realm of postoperative adjuvant therapy, oral mitotane has historically been considered the first-line agent, although its clinical value remains a subject of ongoing debate [12].

Mitotane exerts its primary mechanism of action within the mitochondria, both by interfering with mitochondrial respiratory chain activity through the induction of cytochrome c oxidase deficiency in ACC cells and by inhibiting the function of Sterol-O-Acyl Transferase 1 (SOAT1). The latter leads to intracellular accumulation of free cholesterol and fatty acids, subsequently triggering endoplasmic reticulum stress and ultimately promoting ACC cell apoptosis [13, 14]. A meta-analysis encompassing 1249 ACC patients undergoing adjuvant mitotane therapy demonstrated that mitotane significantly reduced postoperative tumor recurrence and mortality in patients without distant metastasis [15]. However, a more recent randomized controlled trial (RCT) involving 91 patients with low-grade localized ACC compared adjuvant mitotane therapy (for at least 2 years) against observation alone. The study concluded that adjuvant mitotane therapy did not yield a statistically significant improvement in recurrence-free survival (RFS) for patients with low to moderate postoperative recurrence risk [16], thereby tempering clinical enthusiasm for this adjuvant treatment strategy.

Conclusion

After analyzing the included SAC cases, we found that due to the lack of a clear standardized treatment regimen for SAC, its current treatment mostly refers to the diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for ACC; however, there are significant differences between the two in pathological essence. Statistical results showed that four patients received mitotane, a chemotherapeutic drug commonly used in ACC treatment, after surgery; the other two patients who received chemotherapy were treated with an etoposide-platinum-based chemotherapy regimen. Among all patients who received chemotherapy, the one with the longest survival time was a patient treated with mitotane, with a recorded overall survival (OS) of 16 months after diagnosis. Additionally, thirty patients received only surgical treatment, with a postoperative survival time ranging from 1 day to 2.5 years. Due to the extremely low incidence of SAC and limited relevant case reports, the available data are insufficient to confirm that “surgery combined with adjuvant mitotane therapy” has a definite therapeutic effect in the treatment of SAC. Furthermore, most SAC patients have non-functional carcinosarcomas, and their symptoms at the time of consultation are caused by the tumor’s space-occupying effect, rather than specific manifestations of tumor biological progression.
