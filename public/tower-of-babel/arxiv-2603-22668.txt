Fixed-level calibration of the Cauchy combination test
Hirofumi Ota

Abstract
. The Cauchy combination test (CCT) is widely used because it gives a closed-form combined p p -value and is known to be asymptotically valid as the nominal level α ↓ 0 \alpha\downarrow 0 under broad dependence structures. We study a different asymptotic question: whether the usual Cauchy cutoff remains accurate at an ordinary fixed level when the number K K of combined p p -values grows under dependence. Under a canonical one-factor equicorrelated Gaussian copula model, we show that the raw CCT is generally not asymptotically exact at fixed α \alpha . With fixed positive correlation, the statistic converges to a random latent-factor limit, so there is no universal fixed-level reference law. When the common correlation ρ K \rho_{K} weakens with K K , fixed-level behaviour is governed by the boundary-layer scale s K = ρ K ​ ( log ⁡ K ) 3 / 2 s_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2} , and the raw CCT is asymptotically exact if and only if ρ K ​ ( log ⁡ K ) 3 → 0 \rho_{K}(\log K)^{3}\to 0 . Because the size distortion arises entirely from the reference law and not from the statistic, it can be corrected without modifying the test statistic itself. We propose the boundary-layer calibrated CCT (BL-CCT), which replaces the standard Cauchy reference by a one-parameter Gaussian-smoothed Cauchy family while keeping the statistic unchanged. This reference-law correction is fundamentally different from existing approaches that modify the test statistic. BL-CCT is asymptotically exact under the weaker condition ρ K ​ log ⁡ K → 0 \rho_{K}\log K\to 0 and provides a useful finite- K K approximation on bounded boundary layers. Numerical experiments support the theory.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Combining many
p
-values into a single global test is a classical problem in mathematical statistics. Among analytic combination rules, the Cauchy combination test (CCT) of Liu and Xie
9
is attractive because it yields a closed-form combined
p
-value and remains numerically stable at very small significance levels. Its standard asymptotic justification, however, is a vanishing-level result: under broad conditions on the null dependence structure, the CCT statistic is asymptotically standard Cauchy as the nominal level
\alpha\downarrow 0
. In practice the test is routinely applied at ordinary levels such as
0.05
or
0.01
with a large number
K
of component tests; see, for example,
7
;
19
. This creates a gap between theory and practice: does the usual Cauchy cutoff remain asymptotically exact when
\alpha
is fixed and
K\to\infty
under dependence? Recent numerical work indicates that the answer can be negative
10
;
11
;
6
;
1
.

We study this question under the one-factor equicorrelated Gaussian copula model, in which all dependence among the
p
-values is generated by a single shared Gaussian factor. This model is widely adopted in genomic applications and is analytically tractable because conditioning on the latent factor renders the component tests independent. The key finding is that, although the conditional Cauchy limit familiar from vanishing-level theory still holds, a deterministic centring term induced by the latent factor grows with
K
and
\rho_{K}
and displaces the null distribution of the statistic. This centring is the source of the fixed-level size distortion. It is of lower order than the statistic itself and therefore vanishes under the rescaling implicit in
\alpha\downarrow 0
, which is why vanishing-level analysis does not detect it.

1.2.
Main contributions

Our first result concerns the raw CCT developed by
9
. When the common correlation
\rho
is fixed and positive, the CCT statistic converges to a random latent-factor limit, so there is no universal fixed-level reference law. When instead
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
as
K\to\infty
, the transition between exact and inexact behaviour is governed by the boundary-layer scale

s_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2}.

When
s_{K}
is bounded, the conditional
1
-stable fluctuation reduces to a standard Cauchy law, but a deterministic centring term of order
s_{K}
persists in the null distribution. The raw CCT is therefore asymptotically exact at fixed
\alpha

if and only if

\rho_{K}(\log K)^{3}\to 0
.

A key consequence is that the size distortion originates from a mismatch between the standard Cauchy reference law and the actual null distribution of
T_{K}
, not from the heavy-tailed fluctuation of the statistic. The statistic
T_{K}
itself does not need to be modified. We exploit this by introducing the boundary-layer calibrated CCT (BL-CCT), which replaces only the standard Cauchy cutoff by a one-parameter Gaussian-smoothed Cauchy family indexed by
s_{K}
. Under this correction, exactness holds under the weaker condition
\rho_{K}\log K\to 0
, and on bounded boundary layers the reference family already provides a useful finite-
K
approximation. On the broader scale
c_{K}=\rho_{K}\log K
, the remaining size distortion is explicit and strictly conservative at conventional levels such as
0.05
and
0.1
.

1.3.
Related works

Existing asymptotic theory for the CCT and its extensions
9
;
10
;
5
;
6
operates in the regime
\alpha\downarrow 0
with
K
fixed or growing. The present paper instead fixes
\alpha
at a conventional level and lets
K\to\infty
, which reveals a latent-factor drift mechanism that vanishing-level analysis does not detect.

Several recent studies address the finite-
K
size distortion of the CCT by modifying the test statistic: a positive adjustment to the combination weights
11
, a right-tail-weighted combination encompassing Cauchy-type statistics
8
, a truncated variant
3
, and a stepwise procedure
2
. In contrast, BL-CCT keeps the statistic unchanged and corrects only the reference law, preserving the computational simplicity of the original CCT. The present paper gives a precise characterisation of the fixed-level transition and identifies the boundary-layer scale.

Beyond the genomic applications mentioned above
7
;
19
, the CCT has been adopted for microbiome studies
17
and high-dimensional regression
18
. For general background on
p
-value combination, see
16
;
15
;
14
;
4
. The connection between Cauchy averages and
1
-stable laws, identified by Pillai and Meng
12
, underpins the conditional independence structure exploited throughout this paper.

Notation

\varphi
and

2.
Setup and the canonical Gaussian model

This section defines the one-factor equicorrelated Gaussian copula model used throughout the paper and formulates the fixed-level calibration problem.

For
\rho\in[0,1)
, let

Z=(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{K})^{\top}\sim N(0,\Sigma_{\rho}),

where
\Sigma_{\rho}
is the equicorrelated covariance matrix given by

(\Sigma_{\rho})_{ii}=1,\qquad(\Sigma_{\rho})_{ij}=\rho\quad(i\neq j).

Since
\Sigma_{\rho}=(1-\rho)I_{K}+\rho\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
, this model admits the one-factor representation

Z_{i}=\sqrt{\rho}\,V+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,\varepsilon_{i},\qquad i=1,\dots,K,

(1)

where
V\sim N(0,1)
, the
\varepsilon_{i}
are i.i.d.
N(0,1)
, and
V
is independent of
(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{K})
. Thus the null dependence is generated by a single latent Gaussian factor. Conditional on
V
, the coordinates are independent and satisfy

Z_{i}\mid V\sim N(\sqrt{\rho}\,V,\;1-\rho),\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

(2)

We work with one-sided Gaussian
p
-values

p_{i}=\bar{\Phi}(Z_{i})=1-\Phi(Z_{i}),

and with the standard Cauchy quantile transform

f(z)=\tan\{\pi(\Phi(z)-1/2)\}.

(3)

The equal-weight Cauchy combination statistic is given by the average

T_{K}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}f(Z_{i}).

(4)

At level
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, the usual Cauchy combination test rejects when

T_{K}>t_{\alpha},\qquad t_{\alpha}:=\cot(\pi\alpha)

(5)

holds.

We work throughout under the one-factor equicorrelated Gaussian copula model. This model is chosen for its analytic tractability: the representation (
1
) renders the coordinates conditionally independent given
V
, so that the rejection probability can be analysed by first conditioning on
V
and then integrating over the latent factor. The case of independence, corresponding to
\rho=0
, serves as the reference point. The transition of interest arises when
\rho>0
, especially in triangular regimes with
\rho=\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
as
K\to\infty
. We work with one-sided Gaussian
p
-values because they retain the effect of the common Gaussian factor in the most direct form.

3.
Raw CCT at fixed levels

This section analyses the fixed-level behaviour of the raw CCT. We first consider the case of fixed positive correlation and then the triangular regime
\rho=\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
.

3.1.
Fixed positive correlation: a latent-factor limit

When
\rho>0
is fixed, the loading
\sqrt{\rho}
on the latent factor
V
remains constant as
K\to\infty
. Conditional on
V
, the Cauchy-transformed scores are i.i.d., so by the law of large numbers
T_{K}
converges to a limit that depends on
V
.

For
\rho>0
, define the conditional mean

\mu_{\rho}(v):=\mathbb{E}\left[f(\sqrt{\rho}v+\sqrt{1-\rho}\varepsilon)\right],\qquad\varepsilon\sim N(0,1).

(6)

Theorem 3.1

(Random latent-factor limit at fixed positive correlation)
.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
. Then we have

T_{K}\xrightarrow[K\to\infty]{a.s.}\mu_{\rho}(V).

(7)

Moreover, we have

\mathbb{P}(T_{K}>t_{\alpha})\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}\left(\mu_{\rho}(V)>t_{\alpha}\right).

(8)

Because
\rho>0
is fixed, the law of large numbers yields a conditional limit
\mu_{\rho}(v)
that varies with the realization of
V
. The unconditional limit
\mu_{\rho}(V)
is therefore a nondegenerate random variable, and the fixed-level rejection probability is determined by the distribution of
\mu_{\rho}(V)
rather than by a universal Cauchy reference.

To connect this regime with independence, we next examine
\mu_{\rho}(v)
as
\rho\downarrow 0
. The two iterated limits
\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}\lim_{K}
and
\lim_{K}\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}
differ, so the triangular regime
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
with
K\to\infty
must be analysed directly.

Proposition 3.2
.

For each fixed
v\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\rho\mu_{\rho}(v)\longrightarrow\sqrt{2/\pi}\,ve^{v^{2}/2}\qquad(\rho\downarrow 0).

(9)

In particular,
\mathbb{P}\left(\mu_{\rho}(V)>t_{\alpha}\right)\longrightarrow\frac{1}{2}
, so

\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}\lim_{K\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(T_{K}>t_{\alpha})=\frac{1}{2},\qquad\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}\mathbb{P}(T_{K}>t_{\alpha})=\alpha.

(10)

The non-commutativity (
10
) confirms that the fixed-
\rho
regime and the independent case
\rho=0
are separated by a nontrivial phase transition: taking
K\to\infty
first preserves the latent-factor effect, whereas taking
\rho\downarrow 0
first recovers the standard Cauchy reference.

3.2.
Weakening dependence and the fixed-level boundary layer

We now consider the triangular regime
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
, which interpolates between the random-limit regime above and the independent Cauchy limit at
\rho=0
. The fixed-level behaviour turns out to depend on two logarithmic scales: the broader scale
c_{K}=\rho_{K}\log K
and the finer boundary-layer scale
s_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2}
.

Both scales arise from the same source: a nonzero latent factor
V
shifts each
Z_{i}
by
\sqrt{\rho_{K}}\,V
, but this shift affects the CCT statistic only through the extreme upper tail of the
Z_{i}
, where values near
\sqrt{2\log K}
produce Cauchy scores of order
K
. The parameter
c_{K}
measures whether the shift is large enough to change the density of such extreme values, while
s_{K}
captures the resulting net effect on the mean of the statistic. Theorem
3.3
below makes this decomposition precise.

Now let
\rho=\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
with
K
, and write

c_{K}:=\rho_{K}\log K,\qquad s_{K}:=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2}.

(11)

For each fixed
v\in\mathbb{R}
, let

Z_{i,K}^{(v)}:=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}v+\sqrt{1-\rho_{K}}\varepsilon_{i},\qquad X_{i,K}^{(v)}:=f\left(Z_{i,K}^{(v)}\right),\qquad T_{K}^{(v)}:=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}X_{i,K}^{(v)}.

(12)

The truncated-mean centring term is defined by

b_{K}(v):=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1,K}^{(v)}\mathbf{1}\left\{|X_{1,K}^{(v)}|\leq K\right\}\right]=K\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\right|\leq 1\right\}\right].

(13)

If
c_{K}\to c\in[0,\infty)
, define

\lambda^{+}(v):=\exp\left(-c+\sqrt{2c}v\right),\qquad\lambda^{-}(v):=\exp\left(-c-\sqrt{2c}v\right),

(14)

and let

\Lambda_{v}(dx):=\frac{\lambda^{+}(v)}{\pi x^{2}}\mathbf{1}\{x>0\}dx+\frac{\lambda^{-}(v)}{\pi x^{2}}\mathbf{1}\{x<0\}dx.

(15)

The next theorem decomposes the conditional null distribution of
T_{K}
into a
1
-stable fluctuation and a deterministic centring term, and gives the small-
c_{K}
expansion of the latter.

Theorem 3.3

(Conditional decomposition into stable fluctuation and centring)
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
and
c_{K}\to c\in[0,\infty)
. Fix
v\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, conditionally on
V=v
, we have

T_{K}-b_{K}(v)\xrightarrow{d}S_{c,v},

(16)

where
S_{c,v}
is the
1
-stable law determined by the Lévy measure
\Lambda_{v}
in (
15
). Moreover,

\rho_{K}b_{K}(v)\longrightarrow B_{c}(v):=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2c}}te^{-t^{2}/2}\sinh(vt)dt.

(17)

If, in addition,
c_{K}\to 0
, then

b_{K}(v)=\kappa vs_{K}+o(s_{K}),\qquad\kappa:=\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\pi}.

(18)

The stable law
S_{c,v}
in (
16
) is characterized by

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itS_{c,v}}\right]=\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\left(e^{itx}-1-itx\mathbf{1}\{|x|\leq 1\}\right)\Lambda_{v}(dx)\right\},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

(19)

with
\Lambda_{v}
built from
\lambda^{\pm}(v)
in (
14
).

Because the stable law
S_{c,v}
is symmetric and does not depend on
K
, the fixed-level size distortion is entirely due to the deterministic centring
b_{K}(v)
. Taking the expectation over
V
yields the unconditional rejection probability. The first corollary specialises to
c_{K}\to 0
, where
S_{c,v}
reduces to the standard Cauchy law and
b_{K}(v)\approx\kappa v\,s_{K}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
be fixed and assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
.

(1)

If
s_{K}\to s\in[0,\infty)
, then we have

\mathbb{P}(T_{K}>t_{\alpha})\longrightarrow\Psi_{\alpha}(s):=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\left(t_{\alpha}-\kappa sV\right)\right].

(20)

(2)

If
s_{K}\to\infty
, then we have

\mathbb{P}(T_{K}>t_{\alpha})\longrightarrow\frac{1}{2}.

(21)

Thus
s_{K}
is the sole parameter governing the fixed-level size of the raw CCT in this regime:
s_{K}\to 0
yields exactness, bounded
s_{K}
yields the explicit distortion
\Psi_{\alpha}(s)
, and
s_{K}\to\infty
drives the rejection probability to
1/2
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
. Then, for every fixed
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, we have

\mathbb{P}(T_{K}>t_{\alpha})\longrightarrow\alpha\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho_{K}(\log K)^{3}\longrightarrow 0.

(22)

The exponent
3
on
\log K
comes from the small-
c
behaviour of
B_{c}(v)
.
Expanding
\sinh(vt)\approx vt
in (
17
) gives

B_{c}(v)=\frac{2v}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2c}}t^{2}e^{-t^{2}/2}\,dt+O(c^{5/2})=\kappa v\,c^{3/2}+O(c^{5/2}),

so
b_{K}(v)\approx\kappa v\,s_{K}
with
s_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2}
, and the exactness condition
s_{K}\to 0
reduces to
\rho_{K}(\log K)^{3}\to 0
.
Because
b_{K}(v)
is a centering term rather than a component of the stable limit
S_{c,v}
, this distortion does not appear in the vanishing-level regime
\alpha_{K}\to 0
studied by
9
.

4.
Boundary-layer calibration

This section introduces a calibrated version of the CCT that corrects the fixed-level size distortion identified in Section
3
. The statistic
T_{K}
is kept unchanged; only the reference law is replaced so that the cutoff accounts for the leading boundary-layer centring.

4.1.
Calibrated reference family

Specifically, we replace the standard Cauchy tail probability by the boundary-layer family

p_{\mathrm{BL}}(t;s):=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\left(t-\kappa sV\right)\right],\qquad s\geq 0.

(23)

For
s=0
this reduces to the standard Cauchy tail probability used by the raw CCT. The parameter
s
is the boundary-layer scale defined in (
11
), so the reference family is parametrised by the same quantity that governs the size distortion of the raw test.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{C}\sim\mathrm{Cauchy}(0,1)
and
V\sim N(0,1)
be independent. Then we have

T_{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathbb{C}+\kappa sV,\qquad\phi_{T_{s}}(u)=\exp\left(-|u|-\frac{\kappa^{2}s^{2}u^{2}}{2}\right).

(24)

Consequently, for each
\alpha\in(0,1)
the calibrated cutoff

q_{\alpha}(s):=\inf\{t:\ p_{\mathrm{BL}}(t;s)\leq\alpha\}

(25)

is uniquely defined. If
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, then the map
s\mapsto q_{\alpha}(s)
is strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
and satisfies

q_{\alpha}(0)=t_{\alpha},\qquad q_{\alpha}(s)=t_{\alpha}+\frac{\kappa^{2}t_{\alpha}}{1+t_{\alpha}^{2}}s^{2}+O_{\alpha}(s^{4})\quad(s\downarrow 0),

(26)

while

q_{\alpha}(s)=\kappa z_{1-\alpha}s+O_{\alpha}(1)\quad(s\to\infty),

(27)

where
z_{1-\alpha}:=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)
.

The calibrated law
T_{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathbb{C}+\kappa sV
is a location mixture of the Cauchy distribution with Gaussian mixing. The cutoff
q_{\alpha}(s)
departs from the standard Cauchy cutoff
t_{\alpha}
by
O(s^{2})
for small
s
and grows as
\kappa z_{1-\alpha}\,s
for large
s
.

4.2.
Uniform validity on bounded boundary layers

The next result establishes a uniform distributional approximation of
T_{K}
by the calibrated family (
23
), valid whenever
s_{K}
remains bounded.

Theorem 4.2

(Boundary-layer approximation)
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
and
\sup_{K}s_{K}<\infty
. Let
F_{s}(t)=1-p_{\mathrm{BL}}(t;s)
. Then we have

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}(T_{K}\leq t)-F_{s_{K}}(t)\right|\longrightarrow 0.

(28)

Consequently, we have

\sup_{u\in[0,1]}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(p_{\mathrm{BL}}(T_{K};s_{K})\leq u\right)-u\right|\longrightarrow 0.

(29)

If
\widehat{s}_{K}-s_{K}\to 0
in probability, then the same uniformity conclusion holds with
\widehat{s}_{K}
in place of
s_{K}
.

Since BL-CCT modifies only the cutoff and not the statistic, the approximation (
28
) immediately yields calibrated rejection probabilities. The next corollary records the resulting exactness criterion.

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
and
c_{K}=\rho_{K}\log K\to 0
. Then we have

\sup_{u\in[0,1]}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(p_{\mathrm{BL}}(T_{K};s_{K})\leq u\right)-u\right|\longrightarrow 0.

Equivalently, for every fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(T_{K}>q_{\alpha}(s_{K})\right)\longrightarrow\alpha.

(30)

Comparing with Corollary
3.5
, the exactness threshold improves from
\rho_{K}=o((\log K)^{-3})
for the raw CCT to
\rho_{K}=o((\log K)^{-1})
for BL-CCT, because the boundary-layer centring has been absorbed into the reference family. The next subsection examines the residual size distortion when
c_{K}=\rho_{K}\log K
remains bounded away from zero.

4.3.
Behaviour on the broader scale

When
c_{K}=\rho_{K}\log K
does not vanish, the boundary-layer correction no longer eliminates all size distortion. The remaining discrepancy is governed by
c_{K}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
and assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
.
If
c_{K}=\rho_{K}\log K\to c\in(0,\infty)
, then we have

\mathbb{P}\left(T_{K}>q_{\alpha}(s_{K})\right)\longrightarrow\Xi_{\alpha}(c):=\mathbb{P}\left(M_{c}>\kappa z_{1-\alpha}\sqrt{c}\right),

(31)

where
M_{c}=B_{c}(V)/c
.
If
c_{K}\to\infty
, then we have

\mathbb{P}\left(T_{K}>q_{\alpha}(s_{K})\right)\longrightarrow 0.

The limiting size
\Xi_{\alpha}(c)
is thus determined by the distribution of
M_{c}=B_{c}(V)/c
, a univariate transformation of the latent factor. The next result extends this to a finite-
K
uniform approximation on compact positive
c
-windows.

Proposition 4.5
.

Fix
0<c_{-}<c_{+}<\infty
and assume

c_{K}\in[c_{-},c_{+}]

for all sufficiently large
K
. Let
H_{c}
denote the distribution function of
M_{c}
. Then we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{T_{K}}{\log K}\leq x\right)-H_{c_{K}}(x)\right|\longrightarrow 0.

(32)

Consequently, for every fixed
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(T_{K}>q_{\alpha}(s_{K})\right)=\Xi_{\alpha}(c_{K})+o(1).

(33)

Together with Proposition
4.4
, this shows that
c_{K}
fully determines the calibrated size beyond the boundary layer. At conventional levels such as
\alpha=0.05
and
0.1
, the size function
\Xi_{\alpha}(c)
is strictly below
\alpha
for all
c>0
, so the residual distortion is conservative.

Corollary 4.6
.

If
\alpha\in[\overline{\Phi}(\sqrt{3}),1/2)
, then
\Xi_{\alpha}(c)<\alpha
for every
c>0
, and therefore

\mathbb{P}\left(T_{K}>q_{\alpha}(s_{K})\right)\longrightarrow\alpha\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho_{K}\log K\longrightarrow 0.

(34)

In particular, this covers standard levels such as
\alpha=0.05
and
\alpha=0.1
.

Corollary
4.6
establishes that
\rho_{K}\log K\to 0
is both necessary and sufficient for exactness of BL-CCT at conventional levels, paralleling Corollary
3.5
for the raw CCT. When
c_{K}
is bounded away from zero, the calibrated size converges to
\Xi_{\alpha}(c)<\alpha
, which can be computed numerically. Section
5
illustrates the finite-sample behaviour.

5.
Numerical experiments

This section describes the computation of BL-CCT and presents several Monte Carlo experiments to validate the theoretical results of Sections
3
and
4
.

5.1.
Implementation

The calibrated
p
-value
p_{\mathrm{BL}}(t;s)
is a one-dimensional integral with respect to the standard normal density of
V
, which can be evaluated by standard numerical integration. Under the equicorrelated Gaussian copula, a natural plug-in estimator of
\rho_{K}
is given by

\widehat{\rho}:=\max\left\{0,1-\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}(z_{i}-\bar{z})^{2}\right\},

(35)

with

z_{i}=\Phi^{-1}(1-p_{i}),\qquad\bar{z}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}z_{i},\qquad\widehat{s}_{K}=\sqrt{\widehat{\rho}}(\log K)^{3/2},\qquad\widehat{c}_{K}=\widehat{\rho}\log K.

(36)

Proposition
E.2
in the Appendix shows that
\widehat{\rho}-\rho_{K}=O_{p}(K^{-1/2})
under the null model, so
\widehat{s}_{K}-s_{K}\to 0
on compact boundary layers and the plug-in BL-CCT has the same asymptotic calibration as the oracle procedure (Theorem
4.2
).

5.2.
Monte Carlo experiments

All simulations are conducted under the equicorrelated Gaussian null with one-sided
p
-values at nominal level
\alpha=0.05
. Each configuration uses
5000
Monte Carlo repetitions. The scale parameters are held fixed while
K
varies, so the correlation
\rho_{K}
is determined by the identity
s_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2}
or
c_{K}=\rho_{K}\log K
: specifically, we set
\rho_{K}=s^{2}/(\log K)^{3}
so that
s_{K}\equiv s
in panels (a) and (b), and
\rho_{K}=c/\log K
so that
c_{K}\equiv c
in panels (c) and (d). Since
\rho_{K}
decreases with
K
for each fixed
s
or
c
, larger
K
corresponds to weaker dependence.

0.05

6.
Conclusion

This paper has studied the fixed-level calibration of the Cauchy combination test under dependence. In the one-factor Gaussian copula model, the size distortion of the raw CCT is driven by a latent-factor centring term, yielding the sharp threshold
\rho_{K}(\log K)^{3}\to 0
. Because this distortion arises from the inadequacy of the standard Cauchy reference law rather than from the statistic itself, it can be corrected by replacing the Cauchy reference with a Gaussian-smoothed Cauchy family, leading to BL-CCT with the weaker exactness condition
\rho_{K}\log K\to 0
.

Several directions remain open. The equicorrelated model relies on a single scalar
\rho_{K}
. Under a multi-factor model, each latent factor contributes its own centring term, and whether the one-parameter BL-CCT family suffices or a multi-parameter reference is needed remains unclear. Heterogeneous or block-structured correlations would require a different decomposition of the truncated conditional mean. The present analysis is confined to null calibration; the power properties of BL-CCT under specific alternatives remain to be investigated.

The fixed-level mechanism is also not specific to the Cauchy transform. Any combination rule based on a regularly varying score function with tail index
1
, including the harmonic mean
p
-value
15
and the classes studied by
5
and
6
, will produce a similar centring effect under the same copula model. A unified treatment covering general regularly varying scores is a natural next step.

\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\rho}^{2}e^{Z_{\rho}^{2}/2}\right]=\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{1-\sigma^{2}}+\frac{m^{2}}{(1-\sigma^{2})^{2}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[e^{Z_{\rho}^{2}/2}\right]=\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}+\frac{\rho v^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}e^{v^{2}/2}=\frac{1-\rho+v^{2}}{\rho^{3/2}}e^{v^{2}/2}.

Together with (
42
), this gives

\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{\rho}|e^{Z_{\rho}^{2}/2}\right]\leq\frac{\sqrt{1-\rho+v^{2}}}{\rho}e^{v^{2}/2}.

Therefore, we have

\limsup_{\rho\downarrow 0}\ \rho|\mathbb{E}[r(Z_{\rho})]|\leq\epsilon\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\sqrt{1+v^{2}}\,e^{v^{2}/2}.

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude
\rho\mathbb{E}[r(Z_{\rho})]\to 0
as
\rho\downarrow 0
.

Combining the leading-term and remainder estimates yields (
41
) for each fixed
v
.

Uniformity on compact
v
-sets.

Let
|v|\leq V_{0}
.
The bound
\rho\mathbb{E}[|Z_{\rho}|e^{Z_{\rho}^{2}/2}]\leq\sqrt{1+V_{0}^{2}}e^{V_{0}^{2}/2}
is uniform over
|v|\leq V_{0}
by the preceding calculation.
Hence the same
\epsilon
–
R_{\epsilon}
argument gives uniform convergence on compact sets.

Finally, if
v\neq 0
, (
41
) implies

|\mu_{\rho}(v)|\sim\frac{\sqrt{2/\pi}}{\rho}\,|v|e^{v^{2}/2}\to\infty,

and the sign matches
\mathrm{sign}(v)
because the leading constant is positive.
∎

.

Fix

\mathbb{R}

\mathbb{R}^{K}

Finally, because the standard Cauchy law is continuous,

\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}\mathbb{P}_{\rho}(T_{K}>t_{\alpha})=\mathbb{P}(T_{K}^{(0)}>t_{\alpha})=\alpha.

C.1.
Deviation of the statistic from its conditional mean

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
and recall that, conditional on
V=v
, the
X_{i}=f(Z_{i})
are i.i.d. and

T_{K}-\mu_{\rho}(v)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left(X_{i}-\mu_{\rho}(v)\right)\qquad\text{under }(V=v).

Write
Y_{i}:=X_{i}-\mu_{\rho}(v)
for the centered variables under
(V=v)
.

Proposition C.1
.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
and
v\in\mathbb{R}
.
Fix any
q
satisfying

1<q<\min\left\{2,\frac{1}{1-\rho}\right\}.

Then
\mathbb{E}[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v]<\infty
and, for every
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|T_{K}-\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}\mid V=v\right]

\displaystyle\leq K^{1-q}\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right],

(44)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|T_{K}-\mu_{\rho}(v)|>\delta\mid V=v\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{-q}K^{1-q}\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right].

(45)

Proof.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
,
v\in\mathbb{R}
, and

1<q<\min\left\{2,\frac{1}{1-\rho}\right\}.

Apply Proposition
C.2
with any
M>|v|
.
Its first display implies
\mathbb{E}[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v]<\infty
, and Proposition
B.1
gives
\mu_{\rho}(v)=\mathbb{E}[X_{1}\mid V=v]\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus
Y_{1}=X_{1}-\mu_{\rho}(v)
satisfies
\mathbb{E}[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v]<\infty
by the elementary bound

|Y_{1}|^{q}\leq 2^{q-1}\left(|X_{1}|^{q}+|\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}\right),

together with Jensen’s inequality
|\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}\leq\mathbb{E}[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v]
.

Now consider the i.i.d. centered sequence
(Y_{i})_{i=1}^{K}
under
(V=v)
.
Since
1<q\leq 2
, the von Bahr–Esseen inequality
[
13
]
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{i=1}^{K}Y_{i}\right|^{q}\mid V=v\right]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{i}|^{q}\mid V=v\right]=K\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right].

Divide both sides by
K^{q}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|T_{K}-\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}\mid V=v\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}Y_{i}\right|^{q}\mid V=v\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K^{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{i=1}^{K}Y_{i}\right|^{q}\mid V=v\right]

\displaystyle\leq K^{1-q}\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right].

which is (
44
).

Finally, apply Markov’s inequality to the nonnegative random variable
|T_{K}-\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}
under
(V=v)
:

\mathbb{P}\left(|T_{K}-\mu_{\rho}(v)|>\delta\mid V=v\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(|T_{K}-\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}>\delta^{q}\mid V=v\right)\leq\delta^{-q}\mathbb{E}\left[|T_{K}-\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}\mid V=v\right].

Substituting (
44
) yields (
45
).
∎

C.2.
Uniform control on bounded factor events

To convert the conditional bound in Proposition
C.1
into an unconditional statement, we bound the conditional
q
-moment
\mathbb{E}[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v]
uniformly over
|v|\leq M
.

For
q>0
and
\eta>0
, write

A_{q,\eta}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}(1+|z|)^{q}e^{-\eta z^{2}}.

Since the Gaussian factor dominates polynomial growth, we have
A_{q,\eta}<\infty
and hence

(1+|z|)^{q}\leq A_{q,\eta}e^{\eta z^{2}}\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}.

Proposition C.2
.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
, set
\sigma^{2}=1-\rho\in(0,1)
and
\beta=1/\sigma^{2}
.
Let
q\in[1,\beta)
and
M>0
.
Then we have

\sup_{|v|\leq M}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right]<\infty,\qquad\sup_{|v|\leq M}\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right]<\infty,

where
X_{1}=f(Z_{1})
and
Y_{1}=X_{1}-\mu_{\rho}(v)
under
(V=v)
.
More explicitly, letting
C_{f}
be the constant in Lemma
A.3
(
39
) and setting

\eta:=\frac{\beta-q}{4}>0,\qquad\lambda:=\frac{q}{2}+\eta=\frac{q+\beta}{4},

we have for all
|v|\leq M
,

\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right]\leq C_{f}^{q}A_{q,\eta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2\lambda\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(\frac{\lambda\rho v^{2}}{1-2\lambda\sigma^{2}}\right),

(46)

and consequently

\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right]\leq 2^{q}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right]\leq 2^{q}C_{f}^{q}A_{q,\eta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2\lambda\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(\frac{\lambda\rho M^{2}}{1-2\lambda\sigma^{2}}\right).

(47)

Proof.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
,
q\in[1,\beta)
and
M>0
.
Let
v\in[-M,M]
and consider the conditional law
(V=v)
.
Then
Z_{1}\sim N(m,\sigma^{2})
with
m=\sqrt{\rho}v
and
\sigma^{2}=1-\rho
.
By Lemma
A.3
(
39
),

|X_{1}|^{q}=|f(Z_{1})|^{q}\leq C_{f}^{q}(1+|Z_{1}|)^{q}e^{qZ_{1}^{2}/2}.

By the definition of
A_{q,\eta}
,

(1+|Z_{1}|)^{q}\leq A_{q,\eta}e^{\eta Z_{1}^{2}}.

Therefore,

|X_{1}|^{q}\leq C_{f}^{q}A_{q,\eta}e^{(q/2+\eta)Z_{1}^{2}}=C_{f}^{q}A_{q,\eta}e^{\lambda Z_{1}^{2}}.

Taking conditional expectation and using the standard Gaussian identity

\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z_{1}^{2}}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2\lambda\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(\frac{\lambda m^{2}}{1-2\lambda\sigma^{2}}\right),\qquad 2\lambda\sigma^{2}<1,

we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v\right]\leq C_{f}^{q}A_{q,\eta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2\lambda\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(\frac{\lambda m^{2}}{1-2\lambda\sigma^{2}}\right).

Since
m^{2}=\rho v^{2}
, this is exactly (
46
).
Moreover, the right-hand side is finite because
2\lambda\sigma^{2}<1
:
indeed,

2\lambda\sigma^{2}=2\cdot\frac{q+\beta}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{q+\beta}{2\beta}<1\qquad\text{since }q<\beta.

Taking the supremum over
|v|\leq M
yields the first claimed uniform bound.

For the centered variable
Y_{1}=X_{1}-\mu_{\rho}(v)
, the restriction
q\geq 1
is important: it lets us use the standard inequality

|Y_{1}|^{q}=|X_{1}-\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}\leq 2^{q-1}\left(|X_{1}|^{q}+|\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}\right).

By Jensen,
|\mu_{\rho}(v)|^{q}\leq\mathbb{E}[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v]
.
Hence

\mathbb{E}[|Y_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v]\leq 2^{q-1}\left(\mathbb{E}[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v]+\mathbb{E}[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v]\right)=2^{q}\mathbb{E}[|X_{1}|^{q}\mid V=v],

which gives (
47
) after applying (
46
) and bounding
v^{2}\leq M^{2}
.
∎

C.3.
A finite-K sandwich bound for the size

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
and recall that the one-sided CCT rejects when
T_{K}>t_{\alpha}=\cot(\pi\alpha)
.
Define the size

s_{\alpha,K}(\rho):=\mathbb{P}(T_{K}>t_{\alpha}).

The factor
\lambda_{K}(v)
defined below accounts for the multiplicative distortion of the conditional p-value tail
\mathbb{P}(P_{i,K}^{(v)}\leq u)
relative to
u
at the scale
u\asymp 1/K
.

Fix
v\in\mathbb{R}
throughout this section.
Conditionally on
V=v
, define

Z_{i,K}^{(v)}:=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}v+\sqrt{1-\rho_{K}}\varepsilon_{i},\qquad P_{i,K}^{(v)}:=\overline{\Phi}\left(Z_{i,K}^{(v)}\right),\qquad i=1,\dots,K,

where
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\dots
are i.i.d.
N(0,1)
.
Set

\lambda_{K}(v):=\exp\left(-c_{K}+\sqrt{2c_{K}}v\right).

(48)

Since
c_{K}\to c
, we have

\lambda_{K}(v)\to\lambda(v):=\exp\left(-c+\sqrt{2c}v\right)\in(0,\infty).

(49)

Proposition C.3
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
and
c_{K}:=\rho_{K}\log K\to c\in[0,\infty)
.
Fix
v\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\lambda_{K}(v)
be defined by (
48
).
For
x>0
, define

u_{K,x}(v):=\frac{x}{\lambda_{K}(v)K},\qquad q_{K,x}(v):=\overline{\Phi}^{-1}\left(u_{K,x}(v)\right).

Then, for every compact interval
I=[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)
,

\sup_{x\in I}\left|K\mathbb{P}\left(P_{1,K}^{(v)}\leq\frac{x}{\lambda_{K}(v)K}\mid V=v\right)-x\right|\longrightarrow 0.

(50)

Proof.

Fix a compact interval
I=[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)
.
For each
x\in I
, set

u_{K,x}:=u_{K,x}(v)=\frac{x}{\lambda_{K}(v)K},\qquad q_{K,x}:=q_{K,x}(v)=\overline{\Phi}^{-1}(u_{K,x}).

Since
\lambda_{K}(v)\to\lambda(v)\in(0,\infty)
by (
49
), there exist constants
0<\lambda_{-}<\lambda_{+}<\infty
and
K_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\lambda_{-}\leq\lambda_{K}(v)\leq\lambda_{+}

for all
K\geq K_{0}
.
Consequently, we have

\frac{a}{\lambda_{+}K}\leq u_{K,x}\leq\frac{b}{\lambda_{-}K}

for all
x\in I
and
K\geq K_{0}
.
In particular,
u_{K,x}\downarrow 0
uniformly in
x\in I
as
K\to\infty
.
Since
\overline{\Phi}
is strictly decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, it follows that

q_{K,x}\to\infty

uniformly in
x\in I
.

We first show that

\sup_{x\in I}\left|\frac{q_{K,x}}{\sqrt{2\log K}}-1\right|\longrightarrow 0.

(51)

By the standard Mills ratio inequalities, for every
x\in I
and every
K\geq K_{0}
,

\frac{\varphi(q_{K,x})}{q_{K,x}+q_{K,x}^{-1}}\leq u_{K,x}\leq\frac{\varphi(q_{K,x})}{q_{K,x}}.

Using
\varphi(t)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-t^{2}/2}
, this becomes

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{e^{-q_{K,x}^{2}/2}}{q_{K,x}+q_{K,x}^{-1}}\leq u_{K,x}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{e^{-q_{K,x}^{2}/2}}{q_{K,x}}.

(52)

For the upper bound in (
51
), use the left inequality in (
52
):

u_{K,x}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{e^{-q_{K,x}^{2}/2}}{q_{K,x}+q_{K,x}^{-1}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-q_{K,x}^{2}/2}\frac{1}{q_{K,x}+1},

because
q_{K,x}^{-1}\leq 1
once
K
is large enough.
Taking reciprocals and logarithms gives

\frac{q_{K,x}^{2}}{2}\leq\log\frac{1}{u_{K,x}}+\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)+\log(q_{K,x}+1).

Since
u_{K,x}\geq a/(\lambda_{+}K)
, we have

\log\frac{1}{u_{K,x}}\leq\log K+\log\frac{\lambda_{+}}{a}.

Moreover, because
q_{K,x}\to\infty
uniformly and (
52
) implies
q_{K,x}=O(\sqrt{\log K})
uniformly, we have
\log(q_{K,x}+1)=O(\log\log K)
uniformly in
x\in I
.
Therefore, we have

\sup_{x\in I}q_{K,x}^{2}\leq 2\log K+O(\log\log K).

This yields

\sup_{x\in I}\frac{q_{K,x}^{2}}{2\log K}\leq 1+o(1).

(53)

For the lower bound in (
51
), use the right inequality in (
52
):

u_{K,x}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{e^{-q_{K,x}^{2}/2}}{q_{K,x}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-q_{K,x}^{2}/2},

hence

\frac{q_{K,x}^{2}}{2}\geq\log\frac{1}{u_{K,x}}-\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi).

Since
u_{K,x}\leq b/(\lambda_{-}K)
,

\log\frac{1}{u_{K,x}}\geq\log K+\log\frac{\lambda_{-}}{b}.

Thus

\inf_{x\in I}q_{K,x}^{2}\geq 2\log K+O(1),

which implies

\inf_{x\in I}\frac{q_{K,x}^{2}}{2\log K}\geq 1+o(1).

(54)

Combining (
53
) and (
54
) proves (
51
).

Now define

m_{K}:=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}v,\qquad\sigma_{K}:=\sqrt{1-\rho_{K}},\qquad y_{K,x}:=\frac{q_{K,x}-m_{K}}{\sigma_{K}}.

Since
Z_{1,K}^{(v)}\mid V=v\sim N(m_{K},\sigma_{K}^{2})
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(P_{1,K}^{(v)}\leq\frac{x}{\lambda_{K}(v)K}\mid V=v\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{1,K}^{(v)}\geq q_{K,x}\mid V=v\right)=\overline{\Phi}(y_{K,x}).

(55)

We next compare
y_{K,x}
with
q_{K,x}
.
Since

\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}}-1=\frac{1-\sigma_{K}}{\sigma_{K}}=\frac{\rho_{K}}{\sigma_{K}(1+\sigma_{K})},

we obtain

y_{K,x}-q_{K,x}=q_{K,x}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}}-1\right)-\frac{m_{K}}{\sigma_{K}}=\frac{\rho_{K}q_{K,x}}{\sigma_{K}(1+\sigma_{K})}-\frac{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}v}{\sigma_{K}}.

Because
\sigma_{K}\to 1
,
q_{K,x}=O(\sqrt{\log K})
uniformly in
x
, and
\rho_{K}\log K=c_{K}\to c
, it follows that

\sup_{x\in I}\left|\frac{\rho_{K}q_{K,x}}{\sigma_{K}(1+\sigma_{K})}\right|\leq C\rho_{K}\sqrt{\log K}=C\frac{c_{K}}{\sqrt{\log K}}\longrightarrow 0,

while

\left|\frac{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}v}{\sigma_{K}}\right|\leq C_{v}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore,

\sup_{x\in I}|y_{K,x}-q_{K,x}|\longrightarrow 0.

(56)

Since
q_{K,x}\to\infty
uniformly, (
56
) implies

\sup_{x\in I}\left|\frac{q_{K,x}}{y_{K,x}}-1\right|\longrightarrow 0.

(57)

We now derive the exponential factor.
A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle y_{K,x}^{2}-q_{K,x}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q_{K,x}-m_{K})^{2}}{1-\rho_{K}}-q_{K,x}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{K,x}^{2}-2m_{K}q_{K,x}+m_{K}^{2}-(1-\rho_{K})q_{K,x}^{2}}{1-\rho_{K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{K}q_{K,x}^{2}-2m_{K}q_{K,x}+m_{K}^{2}}{1-\rho_{K}}.

(58)

We claim that

\sup_{x\in I}\left|-\frac{y_{K,x}^{2}-q_{K,x}^{2}}{2}-\log\lambda_{K}(v)\right|\longrightarrow 0.

(59)

Indeed, by (
51
),

\sup_{x\in I}\left|\rho_{K}q_{K,x}^{2}-2c_{K}\right|=\rho_{K}\sup_{x\in I}\left|q_{K,x}^{2}-2\log K\right|\leq\rho_{K}O(\log\log K)=c_{K}\frac{O(\log\log K)}{\log K}\longrightarrow 0.

Next,

m_{K}q_{K,x}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}vq_{K,x}=v\sqrt{\rho_{K}q_{K,x}^{2}}.

Since
\rho_{K}q_{K,x}^{2}\to 2c
uniformly in
x\in I
, continuity of the square root yields

\sup_{x\in I}\left|m_{K}q_{K,x}-\sqrt{2c_{K}}v\right|\longrightarrow 0.

Also,

m_{K}^{2}=\rho_{K}v^{2}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad\frac{1}{1-\rho_{K}}\longrightarrow 1.

Substituting these facts into (
58
), we obtain

\sup_{x\in I}\left|\frac{y_{K,x}^{2}-q_{K,x}^{2}}{2}-\left(c_{K}-\sqrt{2c_{K}}v\right)\right|\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\log\lambda_{K}(v)=-c_{K}+\sqrt{2c_{K}}v
, this proves (
59
).

Finally, apply the standard Mills ratio inequalities both at
q_{K,x}
and at
y_{K,x}
.
Define the Mills-ratio remainder

R(z):=\frac{\overline{\Phi}(z)z}{\varphi(z)},\qquad z>0.

These inequalities imply

\frac{z^{2}}{1+z^{2}}\leq R(z)\leq 1,

so in particular

\sup_{z\geq m}|R(z)-1|\leq\frac{1}{1+m^{2}}\qquad(m>0).

Because
q_{K,x}\to\infty
and
y_{K,x}\to\infty
uniformly on
I
, the quantity

m_{K}:=\inf_{x\in I}\min\{q_{K,x},y_{K,x}\}

satisfies
m_{K}\to\infty
. Hence

\sup_{x\in I}\left|\frac{R(y_{K,x})}{R(q_{K,x})}-1\right|\longrightarrow 0.

Using the identity
\overline{\Phi}(z)=\varphi(z)R(z)/z
, we therefore obtain

\frac{\overline{\Phi}(y_{K,x})}{\overline{\Phi}(q_{K,x})}=\frac{\varphi(y_{K,x})}{\varphi(q_{K,x})}\frac{q_{K,x}}{y_{K,x}}\frac{R(y_{K,x})}{R(q_{K,x})}=\exp\left(-\frac{y_{K,x}^{2}-q_{K,x}^{2}}{2}\right)\frac{q_{K,x}}{y_{K,x}}(1+o(1))

uniformly in
x\in I
.
Using (
57
) and (
59
), we conclude that

\sup_{x\in I}\left|\frac{\overline{\Phi}(y_{K,x})}{\overline{\Phi}(q_{K,x})}-\lambda_{K}(v)\right|\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\overline{\Phi}(q_{K,x})=u_{K,x}=x/(\lambda_{K}(v)K)
, we obtain

\overline{\Phi}(y_{K,x})=\frac{x}{K}(1+o(1))

uniformly in
x\in I
.
Combining this with (
55
) proves (
50
).
∎

C.4.
Point-process approximation for Theorem 3.3

Proposition
C.3
controls the upper tail of
P_{i,K}^{(v)}
, which governs large positive scores. To describe the full

-stable limit, we also need the lower tail, which governs large negative scores.
For this purpose write

\lambda_{K}^{+}(v):=\lambda_{K}(v)=\exp\left(-c_{K}+\sqrt{2c_{K}}v\right),\qquad\lambda_{K}^{-}(v):=\exp\left(-c_{K}-\sqrt{2c_{K}}v\right),

and

\lambda^{+}(v):=\exp\left(-c+\sqrt{2c}v\right),\qquad\lambda^{-}(v):=\exp\left(-c-\sqrt{2c}v\right).

Also write

Q_{i,K}^{(v)}:=\Phi\left(Z_{i,K}^{(v)}\right)=1-P_{i,K}^{(v)}.

Large positive CCT scores correspond to small values of
P_{i,K}^{(v)}
, whereas large negative CCT scores correspond to small values of
Q_{i,K}^{(v)}
.

The lower-tail estimates follow by symmetry, because

Q_{1,K}^{(v)}=\overline{\Phi}\left(-Z_{1,K}^{(v)}\right)

and
-Z_{1,K}^{(v)}
has the same law as
Z_{1,K}^{(-v)}
under
(V=v)
.

Recall that

X_{i,K}^{(v)}:=f\left(Z_{i,K}^{(v)}\right)=\tan\left(\pi(\Phi(Z_{i,K}^{(v)})-1/2)\right).

When
X_{i,K}^{(v)}>0
we have, by (
37

X_{i,K}^{(v)}=\cot\left(\pi P_{i,K}^{(v)}\right),

whereas when
X_{i,K}^{(v)}<0
,

X_{i,K}^{(v)}=\tan\left(\pi(Q_{i,K}^{(v)}-1/2)\right)=-\cot\left(\pi Q_{i,K}^{(v)}\right).

Lemma C.4
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
and
c_{K}:=\rho_{K}\log K\to c\in[0,\infty)
.

.
Let

Then, conditionally on
V=v

K\mathbb{P}\left(r<\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\leq s\mid V=v\right)\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda^{+}(v)}{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right),

(60)

and

K\mathbb{P}\left(-s\leq\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}<-r\mid V=v\right)\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda^{-}(v)}{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right).

(61)

In addition,

K\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}>M\mid V=v\right)\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda^{+}(v)}{\pi M},

(62)

and

K\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}<-M\mid V=v\right)\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda^{-}(v)}{\pi M}

(63)

for every
M>0
.

Proof.

We first prove (
60
).
Because
Z_{1,K}^{(v)}\mid V=v
has a Gaussian density and
\bar{\Phi}
,
\Phi
are smooth monotone maps,
the conditional laws of
P_{1,K}^{(v)}
and
Q_{1,K}^{(v)}
are continuous. In particular,
all threshold events below have the same conditional probability whether written with
<
or
\leq

On the event
\{X_{1,K}^{(v)}>0\}
we have

X_{1,K}^{(v)}=\cot\left(\pi P_{1,K}^{(v)}\right),

and the function
u\mapsto\cot(\pi u)
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1/2)

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\{r<\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\leq s\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{Kr<\cot\left(\pi P_{1,K}^{(v)}\right)\leq Ks\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(Ks)\leq P_{1,K}^{(v)}<\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(Kr)\right\}.

Multiplying by
\lambda_{K}^{+}(v)K
gives

\displaystyle\left\{r<\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\leq s\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left\{a_{K}^{+}(s)\leq\lambda_{K}^{+}(v)KP_{1,K}^{(v)}<a_{K}^{+}(r)\right\},

(64)

where

a_{K}^{+}(x):=\frac{\lambda_{K}^{+}(v)K}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(Kx),\qquad x>0.

We claim that

(65)

Indeed, for

\operatorname{arccot}(y)=\arctan(1/y)=\frac{1}{y}+O\left(\frac{1}{y^{3}}\right),

because

as

.
Substituting

yields

K\operatorname{arccot}(Kx)=\frac{1}{x}+O\left(\frac{1}{K^{2}}\right),

which proves (
65
).

Fix

.
Since
a_{K}^{+}(x)\to\lambda^{+}(v)/(\pi x)\in(0,\infty)
, there exists a compact interval

such that
a_{K}^{+}(x)\in I_{x}
for all sufficiently large
K
.
Applying Proposition
C.3
at the point

gives

K\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{K}^{+}(v)KP_{1,K}^{(v)}\leq a_{K}^{+}(x)\mid V=v\right)=a_{K}^{+}(x)+o(1).

By (

\displaystyle K\mathbb{P}\left(r<\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\leq s\mid V=v\right)

\displaystyle=K\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{K}^{+}(v)KP_{1,K}^{(v)}<a_{K}^{+}(r)\mid V=v\right)

\displaystyle\quad-K\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{K}^{+}(v)KP_{1,K}^{(v)}<a_{K}^{+}(s)\mid V=v\right).

Using the previous display at
x=r
and
x=s
, we obtain

K\mathbb{P}\left(r<\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\leq s\mid V=v\right)=a_{K}^{+}(r)-a_{K}^{+}(s)+o(1).

Taking the limit and using (
65
) proves (
60
).

The negative interval statement follows from the same calculation applied to

Q_{1,K}^{(v)}
and Proposition
C.3
with
-v
in place of
v
.
Indeed, on
\{X_{1,K}^{(v)}<0\}
we have

X_{1,K}^{(v)}=-\cot\left(\pi Q_{1,K}^{(v)}\right),

so the same arccot transformation yields (
61
) with

\lambda^{-}(v)=\lambda(-v)
.

The one-sided tail limits (
62
) and (
63
)
follow in exactly the same way by replacing the interval
(r,s]
with a single threshold event.
∎

For
\eta>0
, let

Define the finite measure on
B_{\eta}
by

\Lambda_{v}(dx):=\frac{\lambda^{+}(v)}{\pi x^{2}}\mathbf{1}\{x>0\}dx+\frac{\lambda^{-}(v)}{\pi x^{2}}\mathbf{1}\{x<0\}dx.

(66)

For
\eta>0
, define the signed extreme-score sum

S_{K,\eta}(v):=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{X_{i,K}^{(v)}}{K}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|\frac{X_{i,K}^{(v)}}{K}\right|>\eta\right\}.

(67)

This sum keeps only the score contributions whose size is at least
\eta
after the natural

Proposition C.5
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
and
c_{K}:=\rho_{K}\log K\to c\in[0,\infty)
.
Fix

and
\eta>0
.
Then, conditionally on
V=v
,

S_{K,\eta}(v)\xrightarrow{d}S_{\eta,v},

where
S_{\eta,v}
is an infinitely divisible random variable with characteristic function

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itS_{\eta,v}}\right]=\exp\left\{\int_{|x|>\eta}(e^{itx}-1)\Lambda_{v}(dx)\right\},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(68)

Equivalently,
S_{\eta,v}
is the compound-Poisson sum generated by a Poisson point process on
B_{\eta}
with intensity measure
\Lambda_{v}
.

Proof.

Fix
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
For
M>\eta
, define the additionally truncated sum

S_{K,\eta,M}(v):=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{X_{i,K}^{(v)}}{K}\mathbf{1}\left\{\eta<\left|\frac{X_{i,K}^{(v)}}{K}\right|\leq M\right\}.

We first identify the limit of
S_{K,\eta,M}(v)
for fixed
M
, and then remove the upper cutoff
M
.

Bounded truncation.

Let

C_{\eta,M}:=[-M,-\eta]\cup[\eta,M],\qquad\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x):=e^{itx}-1,\quad x\in C_{\eta,M}.

Because the conditional law of
X_{1,K}^{(v)}/K
is absolutely continuous and
\Lambda_{v}
has a density, both
\mu_{K,v}
and
\Lambda_{v}
assign zero mass to every singleton in
C_{\eta,M}
. In particular, changing endpoint values at
\{\pm\eta,\pm M\}
does not alter any of the integrals below, so the original cutoff integrand
x\mapsto(e^{itx}-1)\mathbf{1}\{\eta<|x|\leq M\}
may be replaced by the continuous representative
\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}
on
C_{\eta,M}
.
The function
\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}
is bounded and uniformly continuous on the compact set
C_{\eta,M}
.
Let

\mu_{K,v}(A):=K\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\in A\mid V=v\right),\qquad A\subset C_{\eta,M}\ \text{Borel}.

By Lemma
C.4
, the interval asymptotics are already known for intervals of the form
(r,s]\subset(0,\infty)
and
[-s,-r)\subset(-\infty,0)
. Since singleton boundary masses vanish for both
\mu_{K,v}
and
\Lambda_{v}
, the same limits hold for any interval
B\subset C_{\eta,M}
whose endpoints lie in
[-M,-\eta]\cup[\eta,M]
; changing endpoint inclusions affects only finitely many massless boundary points.
We now prove that

\int_{C_{\eta,M}}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x)\mu_{K,v}(dx)\longrightarrow\int_{C_{\eta,M}}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x)\Lambda_{v}(dx).

(69)

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Because
\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}
is uniformly continuous on
C_{\eta,M}
, there exists a finite partition of
C_{\eta,M}
into pairwise disjoint half-open intervals

C_{\eta,M}=B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{L}

such that

\sup_{x,y\in B_{\ell}}|\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x)-\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(y)|\leq\varepsilon\qquad\text{for every }\ell=1,\dots,L.

Choose
x_{\ell}\in B_{\ell}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{C_{\eta,M}}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x)\mu_{K,v}(dx)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x_{\ell})\mu_{K,v}(B_{\ell})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\int_{B_{\ell}}|\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x)-\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x_{\ell})|\mu_{K,v}(dx)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\mu_{K,v}(C_{\eta,M}).

By Lemma
C.4
,

\mu_{K,v}(C_{\eta,M})=K\mathbb{P}\left(\eta<\left|\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\right|\leq M\mid V=v\right)\longrightarrow\Lambda_{v}(C_{\eta,M}),

so
\sup_{K}\mu_{K,v}(C_{\eta,M})<\infty
.
Hence

\limsup_{K\to\infty}\left|\int_{C_{\eta,M}}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x)\mu_{K,v}(dx)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x_{\ell})\mu_{K,v}(B_{\ell})\right|\leq C_{\eta,M,v}\varepsilon

(70)

for some finite constant
C_{\eta,M,v}
.

The same argument with
\Lambda_{v}
in place of
\mu_{K,v}
gives

\left|\int_{C_{\eta,M}}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x)\Lambda_{v}(dx)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x_{\ell})\Lambda_{v}(B_{\ell})\right|\leq\varepsilon\Lambda_{v}(C_{\eta,M}).

(71)

Because
\mu_{K,v}(B_{\ell})\to\Lambda_{v}(B_{\ell})
for each
\ell
, we have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x_{\ell})\mu_{K,v}(B_{\ell})\longrightarrow\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\widetilde{h}_{t,\eta,M}(x_{\ell})\Lambda_{v}(B_{\ell}).

Combining this convergence with (
70
) and (
71
), and then letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, proves (
69
).

Next define

W_{i,K}^{(\eta,M)}(v):=\frac{X_{i,K}^{(v)}}{K}\mathbf{1}\left\{\eta<\left|\frac{X_{i,K}^{(v)}}{K}\right|\leq M\right\}.

Conditionally on
V=v
, the variables
W_{1,K}^{(\eta,M)}(v),\dots,W_{K,K}^{(\eta,M)}(v)
are i.i.d., and

S_{K,\eta,M}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}W_{i,K}^{(\eta,M)}(v).

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itS_{K,\eta,M}(v)}\mid V=v\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itW_{1,K}^{(\eta,M)}(v)}\mid V=v\right]\right)^{K}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[(e^{itX_{1,K}^{(v)}/K}-1)\mathbf{1}\left\{\eta<\left|\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\right|\leq M\right\}\mid V=v\right]\right)^{K}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{a_{K,\eta,M}(t,v)}{K}\right)^{K},

(72)

where

a_{K,\eta,M}(t,v):=K\mathbb{E}\left[(e^{itX_{1,K}^{(v)}/K}-1)\mathbf{1}\left\{\eta<\left|\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\right|\leq M\right\}\mid V=v\right].

By (
69
) and the boundary-mass observation at the start of the bounded-truncation step,

a_{K,\eta,M}(t,v)\longrightarrow A_{\eta,M}(t,v):=\int_{\eta<|x|\leq M}(e^{itx}-1)\Lambda_{v}(dx).

Substituting into (
72
) and using

\left(1+\frac{u_{K}}{K}\right)^{K}\to e^{u}\qquad\text{whenever }u_{K}\to u,

we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itS_{K,\eta,M}(v)}\mid V=v\right]\longrightarrow\exp\left(A_{\eta,M}(t,v)\right).

(73)

Removing the upper cutoff.

We compare
S_{K,\eta}(v)
and
S_{K,\eta,M}(v)
.
If these two sums differ, then there exists at least one index
i
such that

\left|\frac{X_{i,K}^{(v)}}{K}\right|>M.

Hence, by the union bound,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(S_{K,\eta}(v)\neq S_{K,\eta,M}(v)\mid V=v\right)

\displaystyle\leq K\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}\right|>M\mid V=v\right)

\displaystyle=K\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}>M\mid V=v\right)+K\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{X_{1,K}^{(v)}}{K}<-M\mid V=v\right).

(74)

By (
62
) and (
63
),

\limsup_{K\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{K,\eta}(v)\neq S_{K,\eta,M}(v)\mid V=v\right)\leq\frac{\lambda^{+}(v)+\lambda^{-}(v)}{\pi M}.

(75)

Therefore, for every fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{K\to\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itS_{K,\eta}(v)}\mid V=v\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itS_{K,\eta,M}(v)}\mid V=v\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\limsup_{K\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{K,\eta}(v)\neq S_{K,\eta,M}(v)\mid V=v\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{2(\lambda^{+}(v)+\lambda^{-}(v))}{\pi M}.

(76)

On the other hand, since
|e^{itx}-1|\leq 2
and
\Lambda_{v}(|x|>\eta)<\infty
,

A_{\eta,M}(t,v)=\int_{\eta<|x|\leq M}(e^{itx}-1)\Lambda_{v}(dx)\longrightarrow A_{\eta}(t,v):=\int_{|x|>\eta}(e^{itx}-1)\Lambda_{v}(dx)

as

by dominated convergence.
Therefore

\exp\left(A_{\eta,M}(t,v)\right)\longrightarrow\exp\left(A_{\eta}(t,v)\right).

Now let
\varepsilon>0
.
Choose
M
large enough that both

\frac{2(\lambda^{+}(v)+\lambda^{-}(v))}{\pi M}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

and

\left|\exp\left(A_{\eta,M}(t,v)\right)-\exp\left(A_{\eta}(t,v)\right)\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

hold.
Then choose
K
large enough that, by (
73
),

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itS_{K,\eta,M}(v)}\mid V=v\right]-\exp\left(A_{\eta,M}(t,v)\right)\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Using (
76
), we conclude that

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itS_{K,\eta}(v)}\mid V=v\right]-\exp\left(A_{\eta}(t,v)\right)\right|<\varepsilon

for all sufficiently large
K
.
This proves

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itS_{K,\eta}(v)}\mid V=v\right]\longrightarrow\exp\left\{\int_{|x|>\eta}(e^{itx}-1)\Lambda_{v}(dx)\right\}.

Since the right-hand side is a characteristic function, Lévy’s continuity theorem gives the conditional convergence in distribution of
S_{K,\eta}(v)
to a random variable
S_{\eta,v}
with characteristic function (
68
).
Because
\Lambda_{v}(B_{\eta})<\infty
, this law is compound Poisson.
∎

Lemma C.7

K<1/x
, so

K\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{1,K}^{(v)}>x\mid V=v\right)\leq\frac{1}{x}.

Thus (
81
) holds in this range.

Range
1/K\leq x\leq 1
.

Set

u:=Kx\in[1,K].

Since
f
is strictly increasing by Lemma
A.1
, the event
\{Y_{1,K}^{(v)}>x\}
is equivalent to

\left\{X_{1,K}^{(v)}>u\right\}=\left\{f\left(Z_{1,K}^{(v)}\right)>u\right\}=\left\{Z_{1,K}^{(v)}\geq q_{u}\right\},

where

q_{u}:=f^{-1}(u)=\bar{\Phi}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(u)\right).

Define

a_{u}:=\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(u)=\overline{\Phi}(q_{u}),\qquad y_{u}:=\frac{q_{u}-m_{K}}{\sigma_{K}}.

Then, conditionally on
V=v
,

\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{1,K}^{(v)}>x\mid V=v\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{1,K}^{(v)}\geq q_{u}\mid V=v\right)=\overline{\Phi}(y_{u}).

We first obtain deterministic bounds on
q_{u}
.
Since
u\in[1,K]
and
\operatorname{arccot}
is decreasing on
(0,\infty)
,

\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(K)\leq a_{u}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(1)=\frac{1}{4}.

Therefore

q_{u}\geq\bar{\Phi}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)=:q_{\star}>0.

Moreover, because
a_{u}\geq\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(K)
and
\operatorname{arccot}(K)\sim 1/K
, the standard Mills ratio bounds imply
q_{u}=O(\sqrt{\log K})
uniformly in
u\in[1,K]
.
More explicitly, there exists a constant
C_{1}<\infty
such that

q_{u}^{2}\leq C_{1}\log K\qquad\text{for all }u\in[1,K]\text{ and all sufficiently large }K.

(83)

Next we compare
y_{u}
with
q_{u}
.
Since

y_{u}-q_{u}=q_{u}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}}-1\right)-\frac{m_{K}}{\sigma_{K}},

and

\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}}-1=\frac{1-\sigma_{K}}{\sigma_{K}}=\frac{\rho_{K}}{\sigma_{K}(1+\sigma_{K})},

we obtain

|y_{u}-q_{u}|\leq\frac{\rho_{K}q_{u}}{\sigma_{K}(1+\sigma_{K})}+\frac{|m_{K}|}{\sigma_{K}}.

Because
\sigma_{K}\to 1
,
q_{u}=O(\sqrt{\log K})
uniformly by (
83
),
\rho_{K}\log K=c_{K}\leq c+1
, and
m_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}v
, it follows that

\sup_{u\in[1,K]}|y_{u}-q_{u}|\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }K\to\infty.

Hence, for all sufficiently large
K
,

y_{u}\geq\frac{q_{\star}}{2}>0\qquad\text{for all }u\in[1,K].

Therefore Mills’ inequality applies to both
q_{u}
and
y_{u}
, and yields

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\Phi}(y_{u})}{\overline{\Phi}(q_{u})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\phi(y_{u})}{y_{u}}\frac{q_{u}+q_{u}^{-1}}{\phi(q_{u})}

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{u}+q_{u}^{-1}}{y_{u}}\exp\left(-\frac{y_{u}^{2}-q_{u}^{2}}{2}\right).

(84)

We now bound the right-hand side of (
84
).
Since
\sup_{u\in[1,K]}|y_{u}-q_{u}|\to 0
and
q_{u}\geq q_{\star}
, we have
y_{u}\geq q_{u}/2
for all sufficiently large
K
.
Therefore

\frac{q_{u}+q_{u}^{-1}}{y_{u}}\leq\frac{q_{u}}{y_{u}}+\frac{q_{u}^{-1}}{y_{u}}\leq 2+\frac{2}{q_{u}^{2}}\leq 2+\frac{2}{q_{\star}^{2}}=:C_{2}.

Next,

y_{u}^{2}-q_{u}^{2}=\frac{\rho_{K}q_{u}^{2}-2m_{K}q_{u}+m_{K}^{2}}{1-\rho_{K}},

exactly as in (
58
).
Therefore

\displaystyle-\frac{y_{u}^{2}-q_{u}^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho_{K}q_{u}^{2}}{2(1-\rho_{K})}+\frac{m_{K}q_{u}}{1-\rho_{K}}-\frac{m_{K}^{2}}{2(1-\rho_{K})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|m_{K}|q_{u}}{1-\rho_{K}},

because the first and third terms on the right-hand side are nonpositive.
Using (
83
),

|m_{K}|q_{u}=|v|\sqrt{\rho_{K}}q_{u}\leq|v|\sqrt{\rho_{K}C_{1}\log K}\leq|v|\sqrt{C_{1}(c+1)}.

Also
1-\rho_{K}\to 1
, so for all sufficiently large
K

\exp\left(-\frac{y_{u}^{2}-q_{u}^{2}}{2}\right)\leq C_{3}

for some finite constant
C_{3}
depending only on
v
and the limit
c
.
Combining this with (
84
) yields

\frac{\overline{\Phi}(y_{u})}{\overline{\Phi}(q_{u})}\leq C_{2}C_{3}=:C_{4}.

Recalling that
\overline{\Phi}(q_{u})=a_{u}=\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(u)
, we obtain

\overline{\Phi}(y_{u})\leq C_{4}a_{u}.

Since
\operatorname{arccot}(u)\leq 1/u
for all
u>0
,

a_{u}=\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{arccot}(u)\leq\frac{1}{\pi u}.

Therefore

\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{1,K}^{(v)}>x\mid V=v\right)=\overline{\Phi}(y_{u})\leq\frac{C_{4}}{\pi u}=\frac{C_{4}}{\pi Kx}.

Multiplying by
K
gives

K\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{1,K}^{(v)}>x\mid V=v\right)\leq\frac{C_{4}/\pi}{x}.

Combining Steps 1 and 2 proves (
81
) after enlarging the constant if necessary.
The proof of (
82
) is identical.

Theorem C.9

.

\int_{0<|x|\leq 1}\left|e^{itx}-1-itx\right|\Lambda_{v}(dx)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{0<|x|\leq 1}x^{2}\Lambda_{v}(dx)<\infty,

Corollary C.10
.

Assume the setting of Theorem
C.9
with
c=0
. Then, for every fixed
v\in\mathbb{R}
,

S_{0,v}\sim\mathrm{Cauchy}(0,1).

Proof.

When
c=0
, we have
\lambda^{+}(v)=\lambda^{-}(v)=1
, so (
66
) reduces to

\Lambda_{v}(dx)=\frac{1}{\pi x^{2}}dx,\qquad x\neq 0.

Hence the exponent in (
92
) becomes

\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\left(e^{itx}-1-itx\mathbf{1}\{|x|\leq 1\}\right)\frac{dx}{\pi x^{2}}.

The odd part cancels, and the remaining even part equals

C.6.
Asymptotics of the centering sequence

Theorem
C.9
identifies the centred conditional limit
T_{K}^{(v)}-b_{K}(v)\xrightarrow{d}S_{c,v}
. To complete the proof of Theorem

b_{K}(v)=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1,K}^{(v)}\mathbf{1}\{|X_{1,K}^{(v)}|\leq K\}\mid V=v\right].

We show that
\rho_{K}b_{K}(v)
converges to the explicit limit
B_{c}(v)
whenever
c_{K}\to c\in[0,\infty)

c=0
yields the boundary-layer scale
s_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2}

Throughout this section we condition on
V=v
, write

m_{K}:=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}v,\qquad\sigma_{K}^{2}:=1-\rho_{K},

and denote by

g_{K}(z):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{(z-m_{K})^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R},

the conditional density of
Z_{1,K}^{(v)}
.
We also write

a_{K}:=t(K)=f^{-1}(K)>0,

so that, by monotonicity and oddness of
f

|X_{1,K}^{(v)}|\leq K\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad|Z_{1,K}^{(v)}|\leq a_{K}.

Lemma C.11

For every
K

(103)

Consequently, if

u_{K}:=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}a_{K},

then we have

\rho_{K}b_{K}(v)=\int_{0}^{u_{K}}H_{K}(t,v)dt,

(104)

where

H_{K}(t,v):=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}f\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\left[g_{K}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)-g_{K}\left(-\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\right].

(105)

Proof.

By definition of
b_{K}(v)
and the relation
|X_{1,K}^{(v)}|\leq K\Longleftrightarrow|Z_{1,K}^{(v)}|\leq a_{K}

\displaystyle b_{K}(v)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(Z_{1,K}^{(v)}\right)\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|Z_{1,K}^{(v)}\right|\leq a_{K}\right\}\mid V=v\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{-a_{K}}^{a_{K}}f(z)g_{K}(z)dz.

(106)

Split the integral at

\int_{-a_{K}}^{a_{K}}f(z)g_{K}(z)dz=\int_{-a_{K}}^{0}f(z)g_{K}(z)dz+\int_{0}^{a_{K}}f(z)g_{K}(z)dz.

In the first integral, perform the substitution
z=-y
.
Since
dz=-dy
, when
z=-a_{K}
we have
y=a_{K}
, and when
z=0
we have
y=0
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{-a_{K}}^{0}f(z)g_{K}(z)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{a_{K}}^{0}f(-y)g_{K}(-y)(-dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{K}}f(-y)g_{K}(-y)dy.

By Lemma
A.1
,
f
is odd, so
f(-y)=-f(y)
.
Hence

\int_{-a_{K}}^{0}f(z)g_{K}(z)dz=-\int_{0}^{a_{K}}f(y)g_{K}(-y)dy.

Substituting this into (
106
) yields

b_{K}(v)=-\int_{0}^{a_{K}}f(y)g_{K}(-y)dy+\int_{0}^{a_{K}}f(y)g_{K}(y)dy=\int_{0}^{a_{K}}f(y)\left(g_{K}(y)-g_{K}(-y)\right)dy.

Renaming
y
back to
z
gives (
102
).

We now prove (
103
).
By definition of
g_{K}
,

g_{K}(z)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{(z-m_{K})^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right),

and

g_{K}(-z)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{(-z-m_{K})^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{(z+m_{K})^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle g_{K}(z)-g_{K}(-z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}\sqrt{2\pi}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(z-m_{K})^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{(z+m_{K})^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\right].

Expand the squares:

(z-m_{K})^{2}=z^{2}-2m_{K}z+m_{K}^{2},\qquad(z+m_{K})^{2}=z^{2}+2m_{K}z+m_{K}^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle g_{K}(z)-g_{K}(-z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}+m_{K}^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\left[\exp\left(\frac{m_{K}z}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{m_{K}z}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\right].

Using
e^{x}-e^{-x}=2\sinh(x)
, we obtain (
103
).

Finally, multiply (
102
) by
\rho_{K}
and substitute
t=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}z
.
Then
z=t/\sqrt{\rho_{K}}
and
dz=dt/\sqrt{\rho_{K}}
.
When
z=0
,
t=0
.
When
z=a_{K}
,
t=u_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}a_{K}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\rho_{K}b_{K}(v)

\displaystyle=\rho_{K}\int_{0}^{a_{K}}f(z)\left(g_{K}(z)-g_{K}(-z)\right)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u_{K}}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}f\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\left[g_{K}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)-g_{K}\left(-\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u_{K}}H_{K}(t,v)dt,

which is (
104
).
∎

Theorem C.12

(Asymptotic formula for the centering sequence)
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
and
c_{K}:=\rho_{K}\log K\to c\in[0,\infty)
.
Fix
v\in\mathbb{R}
and define

B_{c}(v):=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2c}}te^{-t^{2}/2}\sinh(vt)dt.

(107)

Then we have

\rho_{K}b_{K}(v)\longrightarrow B_{c}(v).

(108)

Proof.

Let

u:=\sqrt{2c}.

We first prove that

u_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}a_{K}\longrightarrow u.

(109)

By Lemma
A.2
, with
a_{K}=t(K)
,

\frac{a_{K}}{\sqrt{2\log K}}\longrightarrow 1.

Squaring both sides gives

\frac{a_{K}^{2}}{2\log K}\longrightarrow 1.

Multiplying by
\rho_{K}
yields

\rho_{K}a_{K}^{2}=2c_{K}\cdot\frac{a_{K}^{2}}{2\log K}\longrightarrow 2c.

Because both sides are nonnegative, taking square roots gives (
109
).

We next identify the pointwise limit of
H_{K}(t,v)
for each fixed
t>0
.
By Lemma
A.3
, for
z>0
we may write

f(z)=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,ze^{z^{2}/2}\left(1+r(z)\right),

where
r(z)\to 0
as
z\to\infty
.
Fix
t>0
.
Since
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
, we have
t/\sqrt{\rho_{K}}\to\infty
, hence

r\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\longrightarrow 0.

Substitute the asymptotic representation of
f
and the exact formula (
103
) into (
105
).
Writing
z=t/\sqrt{\rho_{K}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{K}(t,v)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}}{2\rho_{K}}\right)\left[1+r\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{2}{\sigma_{K}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}/\rho_{K}+m_{K}^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\sinh\left(\frac{m_{K}t}{\sigma_{K}^{2}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right).

Because
m_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}v
, the argument of the hyperbolic sine simplifies to

\frac{m_{K}t}{\sigma_{K}^{2}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}=\frac{vt}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle H_{K}(t,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi\sigma_{K}}t\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}}{2\rho_{K}}-\frac{t^{2}/\rho_{K}+\rho_{K}v^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\sinh\left(\frac{vt}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\left[1+r\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\right].

(110)

We now simplify the exponent.
Since
\sigma_{K}^{2}=1-\rho_{K}
,

1-\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}=1-\frac{1}{1-\rho_{K}}=-\frac{\rho_{K}}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}.

Hence

\frac{t^{2}}{2\rho_{K}}-\frac{t^{2}/\rho_{K}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}=\frac{t^{2}}{2\rho_{K}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)=-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}.

Substituting this into (
110
) yields

H_{K}(t,v)=\frac{2}{\pi\sigma_{K}}t\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}-\frac{\rho_{K}v^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\sinh\left(\frac{vt}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\left[1+r\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\right].

(111)

Because
\sigma_{K}\to 1
,
\rho_{K}\to 0
, and
r(t/\sqrt{\rho_{K}})\to 0
, the right-hand side converges to

\frac{2}{\pi}te^{-t^{2}/2}\sinh(vt).

Thus, for every fixed
t>0
,

H_{K}(t,v)\longrightarrow h(t,v):=\frac{2}{\pi}te^{-t^{2}/2}\sinh(vt).

(112)

We now prove an integrable domination on a common compact interval.
Choose
M>u+1
.
By (
109
), we have
u_{K}\leq M
for all sufficiently large
K
.
For
t\in[0,M]
, set
z=t/\sqrt{\rho_{K}}
.
By Lemma
A.3
,

\left|f\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\right|\leq C\left(1+\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}}{2\rho_{K}}\right)

for some absolute constant
C
.
Combining this with (
103
) gives

\displaystyle|H_{K}(t,v)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\rho_{K}}\left(1+\frac{t}{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}}{2\rho_{K}}\right)\frac{2}{\sigma_{K}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}/\rho_{K}+\rho_{K}v^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\left|\sinh\left(\frac{vt}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}\frac{\sqrt{\rho_{K}}+t}{\sigma_{K}}\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}-\frac{\rho_{K}v^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\left|\sinh\left(\frac{vt}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\right|.

Since
\sigma_{K}\to 1
, there exists
K_{0}
such that

\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma_{K}\leq 1

for all
K\geq K_{0}
.
Then, for
t\in[0,M]
and
K\geq K_{0}
,

\left|\sinh\left(\frac{vt}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\right|\leq C_{M,v}t,

because the function
x\mapsto\sinh(x)/x
is continuous on bounded sets.
Therefore, for
K\geq K_{0}
and
t\in[0,M]
,

|H_{K}(t,v)|\leq C_{M,v}(\sqrt{\rho_{K}}+t)t\leq C_{M,v}(t+t^{2}).

The function
t\mapsto C_{M,v}(t+t^{2})
is integrable on
[0,M]
.

Define

\widetilde{H}_{K}(t,v):=H_{K}(t,v)\mathbf{1}\{t\leq u_{K}\},\qquad\widetilde{h}(t,v):=h(t,v)\mathbf{1}\{t\leq u\}.

For almost every
t\in[0,M]
, (
112
) and (
109
) imply

\widetilde{H}_{K}(t,v)\longrightarrow\widetilde{h}(t,v).

Because
|\widetilde{H}_{K}(t,v)|\leq C_{M,v}(t+t^{2})
, dominated convergence yields

\int_{0}^{M}\widetilde{H}_{K}(t,v)dt\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{M}\widetilde{h}(t,v)dt.

Since
u_{K}\leq M
for all large
K
and
u\leq M
, the left-hand side is

\int_{0}^{u_{K}}H_{K}(t,v)dt=\rho_{K}b_{K}(v)

by (
104
), whereas the right-hand side is

\int_{0}^{u}h(t,v)dt=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2c}}te^{-t^{2}/2}\sinh(vt)dt=B_{c}(v).

This proves (
108
).
∎

Theorem C.13

(Sharp small-
c_{K}
asymptotic of the centering sequence)
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
and
c_{K}=\rho_{K}\log K\to 0
.
Define

s_{K}:=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2}.

Then, for every fixed
v\in\mathbb{R}
,

b_{K}(v)=\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\pi}vs_{K}+o(s_{K}).

(113)

Proof.

We keep the notation of Lemma
C.11
.
By Lemma
A.2
, we have

\frac{a_{K}}{\sqrt{2\log K}}\longrightarrow 1.

Hence

u_{K}^{2}=\rho_{K}a_{K}^{2}=2c_{K}\cdot\frac{a_{K}^{2}}{2\log K}\longrightarrow 0,

so

u_{K}\longrightarrow 0.

Bounded-
z
region.

Fix
M\geq 1
.
Write

b_{K}(v)=I_{1,K}(M)+I_{2,K}(M),

where

I_{1,K}(M):=\int_{0}^{M}f(z)\left(g_{K}(z)-g_{K}(-z)\right)dz,

and

I_{2,K}(M):=\int_{M}^{a_{K}}f(z)\left(g_{K}(z)-g_{K}(-z)\right)dz.

(If
a_{K}<M
, then
I_{2,K}(M)=0
; however, since
a_{K}=t(K)\to\infty
, for every fixed
M
we have
a_{K}>M
for all sufficiently large
K
.)

We first bound
I_{1,K}(M)
.
For
z\in[0,M]
, formula (
103
) gives

\left|g_{K}(z)-g_{K}(-z)\right|\leq\frac{2}{\sigma_{K}\sqrt{2\pi}}\left|\sinh\left(\frac{m_{K}z}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\right|.

Because
m_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}v\to 0
, the quantity

\frac{m_{K}z}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}

tends to
0
uniformly in
z\in[0,M]
.
Hence, for all sufficiently large
K
and all
z\in[0,M]
,

\left|\frac{m_{K}z}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right|\leq 1.

For
|x|\leq 1
, the Taylor formula for
\sinh
implies
|\sinh(x)|\leq 2|x|
.
Therefore, for all large
K
and all
z\in[0,M]
,

\left|g_{K}(z)-g_{K}(-z)\right|\leq C_{M,v}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}.

Since
f
is continuous on
[0,M]
, it is bounded there:

\sup_{0\leq z\leq M}|f(z)|<\infty.

Consequently, we have

|I_{1,K}(M)|\leq C_{M,v}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}.

Multiplying by
\rho_{K}
, we obtain

\rho_{K}|I_{1,K}(M)|\leq C_{M,v}\rho_{K}^{3/2}.

(114)

Large-
z
region.

Define

r(z):=\frac{f(z)}{\sqrt{2/\pi}\,ze^{z^{2}/2}}-1,\qquad z>0.

By Lemma
A.3
,
r(z)\to 0
as
z\to\infty
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Choose
M_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
so large that

|r(z)|\leq\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }z\geq M_{\varepsilon}.

(115)

Fix this
M_{\varepsilon}
and consider
I_{2,K}(M_{\varepsilon})
.
Multiplying by
\rho_{K}
and substituting
t=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}z
yields

\rho_{K}I_{2,K}(M_{\varepsilon})=\int_{M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}^{u_{K}}H_{K}(t,v)dt.

For
t\in[M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}},u_{K}]
we have
z=t/\sqrt{\rho_{K}}\geq M_{\varepsilon}
, so (
115
) applies.
Repeating the computation leading to (
111
), we obtain

\displaystyle H_{K}(t,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi\sigma_{K}}t\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}-\frac{\rho_{K}v^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\sinh\left(\frac{vt}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right)\left(1+\theta_{K}(t)\right),

(116)

where

|\theta_{K}(t)|\leq\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }t\in[M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}},u_{K}].

(117)

Small-
t
expansion.

Because
u_{K}\to 0
and
\sigma_{K}\to 1
, the interval
[M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}},u_{K}]
eventually lies in a neighborhood of
0
.
We now record two uniform expansions on that interval.

First, define

A_{K}(t):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}}\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}-\frac{\rho_{K}v^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right).

Since
\sigma_{K}\to 1
, we have

\frac{1}{\sigma_{K}}=1+O(\rho_{K}).

Also, because
t\in[0,u_{K}]
and
u_{K}\to 0
,

\left|-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}-\frac{\rho_{K}v^{2}}{2\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right|\leq C_{v}(t^{2}+\rho_{K})

for all large
K
.
The Taylor formula
e^{x}=1+x+O(x^{2})
at
x=0
therefore implies

A_{K}(t)=1+O(t^{2}+\rho_{K})

uniformly for
t\in[0,u_{K}]
.

Second, define

B_{K}(t):=\sinh\left(\frac{vt}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}\right).

Since
vt/\sigma_{K}^{2}\to 0
uniformly for
t\in[0,u_{K}]
, the Taylor formula for
\sinh
gives

B_{K}(t)=\frac{vt}{\sigma_{K}^{2}}+O(t^{3})=vt+O(\rho_{K}t)+O(t^{3})

uniformly for
t\in[0,u_{K}]
.
Combining the expansions of
A_{K}
and
B_{K}
yields

\displaystyle A_{K}(t)B_{K}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(1+O(t^{2}+\rho_{K})\right)\left(vt+O(\rho_{K}t+t^{3})\right)

\displaystyle=vt+O(\rho_{K}t+t^{3})+O(t^{2})vt+O(\rho_{K})vt

\displaystyle=vt+O(\rho_{K}t+t^{3})

(118)

uniformly for
t\in[0,u_{K}]
.
Multiplying (
118
) by
1+\theta_{K}(t)
and using (
117
) gives

\displaystyle A_{K}(t)B_{K}(t)(1+\theta_{K}(t))

\displaystyle=vt+O(\varepsilon t)+O(\rho_{K}t+t^{3})

(119)

uniformly for
t\in[M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}},u_{K}]
.
Substituting (
119
) into (
116
) yields

H_{K}(t,v)=\frac{2v}{\pi}t^{2}+O(\varepsilon t^{2})+O(\rho_{K}t^{2}+t^{4})

(120)

uniformly for
t\in[M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}},u_{K}]
.

Integration.

Integrating (
120
) over
[M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}},u_{K}]
gives

\displaystyle\rho_{K}I_{2,K}(M_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{2v}{\pi}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}^{u_{K}}t^{2}dt+O\left(\varepsilon\int_{M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}^{u_{K}}t^{2}dt\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+O\left(\rho_{K}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}^{u_{K}}t^{2}dt\right)+O\left(\int_{M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}^{u_{K}}t^{4}dt\right).

(121)

Now

\int_{M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}^{u_{K}}t^{2}dt=\frac{u_{K}^{3}}{3}-\frac{M_{\varepsilon}^{3}\rho_{K}^{3/2}}{3},

so

\int_{M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}^{u_{K}}t^{2}dt=\frac{u_{K}^{3}}{3}+O(\rho_{K}^{3/2}).

(122)

Also,

\int_{M_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}}^{u_{K}}t^{4}dt\leq u_{K}^{5}.

Because
u_{K}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}a_{K}
,

u_{K}^{3}=\rho_{K}^{3/2}a_{K}^{3}.

Since
a_{K}=t(K)\to\infty
, we have

\rho_{K}^{3/2}=o(u_{K}^{3}).

(123)

Moreover,
u_{K}\to 0
, hence

u_{K}^{5}=o(u_{K}^{3}),\qquad\rho_{K}u_{K}^{3}=o(u_{K}^{3}).

(124)

Substituting (
122
), (
123
), and (
124
) into (
121
), we obtain

\rho_{K}I_{2,K}(M_{\varepsilon})=\frac{2v}{3\pi}u_{K}^{3}+O(\varepsilon u_{K}^{3})+o(u_{K}^{3}).

(125)

Combination.

By (
114
) and (
123
),

\rho_{K}I_{1,K}(M_{\varepsilon})=o(u_{K}^{3}).

Since

\rho_{K}b_{K}(v)=\rho_{K}I_{1,K}(M_{\varepsilon})+\rho_{K}I_{2,K}(M_{\varepsilon}),

equation (
125
) yields

\rho_{K}b_{K}(v)=\frac{2v}{3\pi}u_{K}^{3}+O(\varepsilon u_{K}^{3})+o(u_{K}^{3}).

Because
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
gives

\rho_{K}b_{K}(v)=\frac{2v}{3\pi}u_{K}^{3}+o(u_{K}^{3}).

(126)

It remains to rewrite the result in terms of
s_{K}
.
By Lemma
A.2
,

a_{K}=\sqrt{2\log K}(1+o(1)).

Therefore

a_{K}^{3}=(2\log K)^{3/2}(1+o(1))=2\sqrt{2}(\log K)^{3/2}(1+o(1)).

Since
u_{K}^{3}=\rho_{K}^{3/2}a_{K}^{3}
,

\frac{u_{K}^{3}}{\rho_{K}}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}a_{K}^{3}=2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\rho_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2}(1+o(1))=2\sqrt{2}s_{K}(1+o(1)).

Dividing (
126
) by
\rho_{K}
and substituting the last display gives

b_{K}(v)=\frac{2v}{3\pi}\cdot 2\sqrt{2}s_{K}(1+o(1))+o(s_{K})=\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\pi}vs_{K}+o(s_{K}),

which is (
113
).
∎

Proposition C.16
.

Assume
\rho_{K}\downarrow 0
and
c_{K}=\rho_{K}\log K\to\infty
.
Fix
v\neq 0
and write

\mu_{K}(v):=\mu_{\rho_{K}}(v).

Then we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|T_{K}^{(v)}-\mu_{K}(v)\right|>\frac{1}{2}|\mu_{K}(v)|\mid V=v\right)\longrightarrow 0.

Consequently, for every fixed
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(T_{K}>t_{\alpha}\mid V=v\right)\longrightarrow\mathbf{1}\{v>0\}.

Proof.

Fix
v\neq 0
.
For all sufficiently large
K
, we have
\rho_{K}<1/2
.
Set

\beta_{K}:=\frac{1}{1-\rho_{K}},\qquad q_{K}:=\frac{1+\beta_{K}}{2}\in(1,\beta_{K}).

-moment.

E.5.
Plug-in consistency under the null model

Proposition E.2
.

Under the equicorrelated Gaussian null with possibly
K
-dependent correlation parameter
\rho_{K}\in[0,1)
, let

\widehat{\rho}_{K}:=\max\left\{0,1-\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}(z_{i}-\bar{z})^{2}\right\},\qquad\widehat{s}_{K}:=\sqrt{\widehat{\rho}_{K}}(\log K)^{3/2},

where
z_{i}=\Phi^{-1}(1-p_{i})=Z_{i}
and
\bar{z}=K^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}z_{i}
.
Then we have

\widehat{\rho}_{K}-\rho_{K}=O_{p}(K^{-1/2}).

In particular, if
\sup_{K}s_{K}<\infty
, then
\widehat{s}_{K}-s_{K}\to 0
in probability and therefore we have

\sup_{u\in[0,1]}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(p_{\mathrm{BL}}(T_{K};\widehat{s}_{K})\leq u\right)-u\right|\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

Under the null model we have
z_{i}=Z_{i}
, so by the one-factor representation

z_{i}=\sqrt{\rho_{K}}V+\sqrt{1-\rho_{K}}\varepsilon_{i}.

Subtracting the sample mean removes the common factor:

z_{i}-\bar{z}=\sqrt{1-\rho_{K}}(\varepsilon_{i}-\bar{\varepsilon}),\qquad\bar{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\varepsilon_{i}.

Hence

\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}(z_{i}-\bar{z})^{2}=(1-\rho_{K})\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}(\varepsilon_{i}-\bar{\varepsilon})^{2}.

The Gaussian sample variance satisfies

\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}(\varepsilon_{i}-\bar{\varepsilon})^{2}\sim\frac{\chi^{2}_{K-1}}{K-1}=1+O_{p}(K^{-1/2}).

Therefore

1-\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}(z_{i}-\bar{z})^{2}=\rho_{K}+O_{p}(K^{-1/2}).

Because
x\mapsto\max\{0,x\}
is
1
-Lipschitz and
\rho_{K}\geq 0
, this implies

\widehat{\rho}_{K}-\rho_{K}=O_{p}(K^{-1/2}).

Now assume
\sup_{K}s_{K}<\infty
. Then we have

|\widehat{s}_{K}-s_{K}|=(\log K)^{3/2}|\sqrt{\widehat{\rho}_{K}}-\sqrt{\rho_{K}}|\leq(\log K)^{3/2}\sqrt{|\widehat{\rho}_{K}-\rho_{K}|},

so

|\widehat{s}_{K}-s_{K}|=O_{p}\left((\log K)^{3/2}K^{-1/4}\right)=o_{p}(1).

The final uniformity statement now follows directly from Theorem
4.2
.
∎
