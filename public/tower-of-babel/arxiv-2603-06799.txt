to an edge.

One can think of
R
as a “
(k,k-1)
-system blow-up” of the
k
-graph
G
(Definition
3.1
) in the sense that it contains many unordered copies of
G
, while
R
itself remains a
(k,k-1)
-system. Hence, one might expect that a random ordering of the vertices of
R
contains, with high probability, an ordered copy of an element of
\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)
. This is confirmed in the next lemma.

Note that any ordering of the vertices of
V(R)
can be viewed as a bijection
\psi:V(R)\rightarrow[N]
by the correspondence sending the bijection
\psi
to an ordering
<_{\psi}
such that
x<_{\psi}y
if and only if
\psi(x)<\psi(y)
. We say that a bijection
\psi
is
good
if
(R,<_{\psi})
contains a copy of
(G,<)
for every
(G,<)\in\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)
. Otherwise, we say that
\psi
is
bad
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\psi:V(R)\rightarrow[N]
be an bijection chosen uniformly at random. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\psi\text{ is bad})=\exp(-\Omega(n^{2})).

Proof.

Fix integers
n,k\geq 3
, and define

(31)

\displaystyle\alpha:=\frac{1}{4n^{k-1}},\quad\beta:=1-\frac{1}{2n^{k-1}},\quad\gamma:=\frac{1}{4n^{k}}

to be real numbers that will be used throughout the proof. Consider the partition

\displaystyle[N]=X_{I}\cup X_{\star}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},

where the sets are ordered by
X_{I}<X_{\star}<X_{1}<\ldots<X_{n}
and have sizes

\displaystyle|X_{I}|=\alpha N,\quad|X_{\star}|=\beta N,\quad|X_{1}|=\ldots=|X_{n}|=\gamma N.

We expose the random bijection
\psi:V(R)\rightarrow[N]
in three rounds:

Round 1: We expose the labels of
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
.

Round 2: We expose the labels of
\bigcup_{J\neq I}V_{J}
.

Round 3: We expose the labels of
V_{I}
.

In each round, we will show that a desired event
\mathcal{E}_{i}
holds with high probability for
1\leq i\leq 3
(to be described later in the proof). Finally, we will conclude by showing that any bijection satisfying all
\mathcal{E}_{i}
is a good one. We now proceed with the details.

Round 1
: We first expose the labels of the vertices in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
. Note that for
1\leq i\leq n
, the random variable
|\psi(V_{i})\cap X_{i}|
follows the distribution of a hypergeometric random variable
\Hyp(N,|X_{i}|,|V_{i}|)
. By Theorem
3.3
, together with (
30
) and (
31
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\Big||\psi(V_{i})\cap X_{i}|-\gamma m\Big|>\frac{\gamma m}{2}\right)=\exp\left(-\Omega(\gamma m)\right)=\exp(-\Omega(n^{3})).

Therefore, by a union bound, with probability at least
1-\exp(-\Omega(n^{3}))
we have the event

(32)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}:\,|\psi(V_{i})\cap X_{i}|=\left(1\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\gamma m,\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq n.

Round 2
: Consider all possible bijections conditioned on the event
\mathcal{E}_{1}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
W_{i}\subseteq\psi(V_{i})\cap X_{i}
be a subset of size

(33)

\displaystyle t:=\frac{\gamma m}{2}=\Theta(n^{3}).

For each
J\in\Omega_{I}\setminus\{I\}
, define

\displaystyle W_{J}=\{v_{z}:\>z=(w_{j})_{j\in J}\in\prod_{j\in J}W_{j}\}\subseteq V_{J},

the set of vertices extending the transversals
\{w_{j}\}_{j\in J}
to an edge in
R
.

We now expose the labels of
\bigcup_{J\neq I}V_{J}
. Let

\displaystyle X_{I}^{2}=X_{I}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\psi(V_{i})\right),\quad X_{\star}^{2}=X_{\star}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\psi(V_{i})\right)

denote the remaining available labels in
X_{I}
and
X_{\star}
after Round 1. By (
30
) and (
31
),

\displaystyle|X_{I}^{2}|

\displaystyle\geq|X_{I}|-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|V_{i}|=\alpha N-mn>\alpha N/2,

(34)

\displaystyle|X_{\star}^{2}|

\displaystyle\geq|X_{\star}|-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|V_{i}|=\beta N-mn>\left(\beta-\frac{1}{n^{k}}\right)N.

For each
J\in\Omega_{I}\setminus\{I\}
, the random variable
|\psi(W_{J})\cap X_{I}^{2}|
follows a hypergeometric distribution
\Hyp(N-mn,|X_{I}^{2}|,|W_{J}|)
with mean

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(|\psi(W_{J})\cap X_{I}^{2}|\,\Big|\,\mathcal{E}_{1}\right)=\frac{|X_{I}^{2}|\cdot|W_{J}|}{N-mn}\leq 2\alpha t^{k-1}.

Hence, Theorem
3.3
, together with (
30
), (
31
) and (
33
) gives us that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|\psi(W_{J})\cap X_{I}^{2}|>3\alpha t^{k-1}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{E}_{1}\right)=\exp\left(-\Omega(\alpha t^{k-1})\right)=\exp(-\Omega(n^{2k-2}))=\exp(-\Omega(n^{4})),

for
k\geq 3
.

Similarly,
|\psi(W_{J})\cap X_{\star}^{(2)}|\sim\Hyp(N-mn,|X_{\star}^{(2)}|,|W_{J}|)
, and by (
3.1
),

\displaystyle t^{k-1}\geq\mathbb{E}\left(|\psi(W_{J})\cap X_{\star}^{(2)}|\,\Big|\,\mathcal{E}_{1}\right)=\frac{|X_{\star}^{2}|\cdot|W_{J}|}{N-mn}\geq\left(\beta-\frac{1}{n^{k}}\right)t^{k-1}.

Therefore, Theorem
3.3
applied with
s=\left(\frac{1}{2n^{k-1}}-\frac{1}{n^{k}}\right)t^{k-1}
, (
30
), (
31
) and (
33
) gives us that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|\psi(W_{J})\cap X_{\star}^{2}|<\left(\beta-\frac{1}{2n^{k-1}}\right)t^{k-1}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{E}_{1}\right)=\exp\left(-\Omega\!\left(\tfrac{t^{k-1}}{n^{2k-2}}\right)\right)=\exp(-\Omega(n^{2})),

for
k\geq 3
. Thus, by a union bound, with probability at least
1-\exp(-\Omega(n^{2}))
, the event

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}:\>|\psi(W_{J})\cap X_{I}^{2}|\leq 3\alpha t^{k-1}\text{ and }|\psi(W_{J})\cap X_{\star}^{2}|\geq\left(\beta-\frac{1}{2n^{k-1}}\right)t^{k-1},\quad\forall J\in\Omega_{I}\setminus\{I\},

holds.

Round 3
: Consider all bijections of
V_{I}
conditioned on
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
. A transversal
z=(w_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}
is
consistent
if for every
J\in\Omega_{I}\setminus\{I\}
, the vertex
w_{z}\in W_{J}
lies in
X_{\star}^{(2)}
. In particular, for a consistent trasnversal, the vertex set

\displaystyle\{v_{z}:\>z=(w_{j})_{j\in J},\,J\in\Omega_{I}\setminus\{I\}\}\cup\{w_{i}:\>1\leq i\leq n\}

forms a partial copy of an element of
\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)
. To complete the copy, it suffices to find a consistent transversal
(w_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with vertex
v_{z}\in X_{I}\cap V_{I}
for
z=(w_{i})_{i\in I}
.

For each
J\in\Omega_{I}\setminus\{I\}
, the event
\mathcal{E}_{2}
guarantees that at most
(1-\beta+\frac{1}{2n^{k-1}})t^{k-1}
tuples in
\prod_{j\in J}W_{j}
correspond to vertices in
W_{J}
outside
X_{\star}^{(2)}
. Thus, by (
30
), (
31
), and
k\geq 3
, the total number of non-consistent transversals is at most

\displaystyle\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(1-\beta+\frac{1}{2n^{k-1}}\right)t^{n}\leq\frac{t^{n}}{2}.

In other words, at least
t^{n}/2
transversals are consistent. By an averaging argument, there exists a set
Z\subseteq\prod_{i\in I}W_{i}
of size
t^{k-1}/2
such that each element can be extended to a consistent transversal. Let

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}:=\{v_{z}\in V_{I}:\>z=(w_{i})_{i\in I}\in Z\}\subseteq V_{I}

be the set of vertices in
V_{I}
corresponding to the
(k-1)
-tuples in
Z
, i.e., those satisfying property
(

⋆

)
‣
3.1
of
R
. In particular,
|\widetilde{Z}|\geq t^{k-1}/2
.

We now expose the labeling of
V_{I}
. Let
X_{I}^{3}:=X_{I}^{2}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{J\neq I}V_{J}\right)
be the set of remaining labels available in
X_{I}
. By (
30
), (
31
), (
3.1
), and (
35
), we have

\displaystyle|X_{I}^{3}|\geq|X_{I}^{2}|-\sum_{J\neq I}|\psi(W_{J})\cap X_{I}^{2}|>\frac{\alpha N}{2}-\binom{n-1}{k-1}\cdot 3\alpha t^{k-1}\geq\frac{\alpha N}{4}.

As in the previous two rounds, note that
|\psi(\widetilde{Z})\cap X_{I}^{3}|\sim\Hyp(|V_{I}|,|X_{I}^{3}|,|\widetilde{Z}|)
with mean

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(|\psi(\widetilde{Z})\cap X_{I}^{3}|\,\big|\,\mathcal{E}_{1},\,\mathcal{E}_{2}\right)=\frac{|X_{I}^{3}|\cdot|\widetilde{Z}|}{|V_{I}|}\geq\frac{(\alpha N/4)\cdot t^{k-1}/2}{m^{k}}=\Omega(n^{3k-3}).

Then by Theorem
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\psi(\widetilde{Z})\cap X_{I}^{3}|=0)=\exp(-\Omega(n^{3k-3}))=\exp(-\Omega(n^{5}))

for
k\geq 3
. This implies that, with probability at least
1-\exp(-\Omega(n^{5}))
, the event

(36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{3}:\>\psi(\widetilde{Z})\cap X_{I}^{3}\neq\emptyset

holds.

Finishing the proof
: To finish the proof, note that a bijection satisfying events
\mathcal{E}_{1}
,
\mathcal{E}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{3}
contains a copy
\widetilde{G}
of
G
with vertices

\displaystyle V(\widetilde{G})=\{v_{i}\in V_{i}:\>1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\left\{v_{z_{J}}\in V_{J}:\>z_{J}=(v_{j})_{j\in J}\in\prod_{j\in J}V_{j},\,J\in\Omega_{I}\right\}

satisfying

\displaystyle v_{i}\in X_{i},\quad v_{z_{I}}\in X_{I},\quad\text{and}\quad v_{z_{J}}\in X_{\star},

for all
J\in\Omega_{I}\setminus\{I\}
. Hence
(\widetilde{G},<_{\psi})\in\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)
. Recall that a bijection
\psi
is bad if
(R,<_{\psi})
does not contain a member of
\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)
. Therefore, if
\psi
is bad, then at least one of the events
\mathcal{E}_{1}
,
\mathcal{E}_{2}
, or
\mathcal{E}_{3}
does not hold, and by (
32
), (
35
), and (
36
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\psi\text{ is bad})=\exp(-\Omega(n^{2})).

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We remark that a similar statement in Theorem
3.2
holds if the map
\psi
is injective or if we replace
\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)
by
\rev\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)
.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.2

Let
p
be the smallest prime greater or equal to
N
. By Betrand’s postulate, one can always have

(37)

\displaystyle N\leq p\leq 2N.

A projective plane
\mathcal{L}_{p}
of order
p
is a
(p+1)
-graph satisfying the following properties

(P1)

v(\mathcal{L}_{p})=e(\mathcal{L}_{p})=p^{2}+p+1
,

(P2)

\mathcal{L}_{p}
is a full Steiner
(p+1,2)
-system,

(P3)

If
L_{1},L_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
, then
|L_{1}\cap L_{2}|=1
.

It is well-known that the projective plane
\mathcal{L}_{p}
exists for every prime
p
.

We construct the graph
H
as follows: Let
R
be the
k
-graph defined in the previous subsection (Subsection
3.1
) and let
\widetilde{R}
the hypergraph on
p
vertices obtained by adding
p-N
isolated vertices to
R
. Fix an arbitrary bijection
f:V(\widetilde{R})\rightarrow[p]
. We define the vertex set of
H
to be the vertices of
\mathcal{L}_{P}
, i.e.,
V(H)=V(\mathcal{L}_{p})
. For each
L\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
consider a bijection
\psi_{L}:V(L)\rightarrow[p]
chosen uniformly at random. Let
\widetilde{R}_{L}
be the hypergraph with vertex set
V(L)
induced by the map
f^{-1}\circ\psi_{L}
. That is,
e\in\widetilde{R}_{L}
if and only if
f^{-1}\circ\psi_{L}(e)\in\widetilde{R}
. We set

\displaystyle H=\bigcup_{L\in\mathcal{L}_{p}}\widetilde{R}_{L}.

Note that since every
\widetilde{R}_{L}
is a
(k,k-1)
-system, by
(P2)
we obtain that
H
is a
(k,k-1)
-system. Moreover, by (
30
) and (
37
) we have

(38)

\displaystyle v(H)=p^{2}+p+1\leq 4N^{2}+2N+1=O(n^{2k^{2}+8k-2}).

Therefore, it only remains to show that with high probability

\displaystyle H\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[1.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ord}}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)\quad\text{and}\quad H\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[1.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ord}}}{\longrightarrow}}\rev\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)

holds. Fix a ordering
<
of
V(H)
. For each
L\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
, the ordering
<
induces a bijection
g:V(L)\rightarrow[p]
. Thus, the fact that
\widetilde{R}_{L}
contains an element of
\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)
is equivalent to the composition
\varphi_{L}:=g\circ f^{-1}\circ\psi_{L}
being bad. By Theorem
3.2
and the fact that
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is a linear graph, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left((H,<)\text{ does not contain a member of $\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)$}\right)=\prod_{L\in\mathcal{L}_{p}}\mathbb{P}(\varphi_{L}\text{ is bad})=\exp(-\Omega(n^{2}\cdot e(\mathcal{L}_{P})))

Similarly, by the remark in Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left((H,<)\text{ does not contain a member of $\rev\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)$}\right)=\exp(-\Omega(n^{2}\cdot e(\mathcal{L}_{P}))).

Taking an union bound over all possible
v(H)!
orderings, we obtain by
(P1)
, (
38
) and the definition of
V(H)
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(H\not\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[1.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ord}}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n)\text{ or }H\not\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[1.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ord}}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G}_{I}^{(k)}(n))=2v(H)!\cdot\exp(-\Omega(n^{2}\cdot v(H)))=o(1).

This concludes the proof of the theorem. ∎

5.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we showed that there exist
(k,k-1)
-systems whose Ramsey numbers admit lower bounds of the same order of magnitude as those of the complete
k
-uniform hypergraph. One caveat of our proof is that we require at least
4
colors. This is unlike in
[
9
,
6
]
, where one can still apply the step-up method for
2
colors and uniformity at least
k\geq 4
. More precisely, the authors of
[
9
,
6
]
proved that

\displaystyle R(K_{n}^{(k)};2)\geq t_{k-2}(cn^{2})

for some absolute positive constant
c
. Note that this result is only one power away from the upper bound described in the introduction. It would be interesting to know whether one can obtain similar lower bounds for
(k,k-1)
-systems with
2
colors.

Perhaps the most interesting question concerns the Ramsey numbers of other
(k,\ell)
-systems.

Problem 5.1
.

Determine for which
2\leq\ell\leq k
there exist an integer
r\geq 2
and a constant
c>0
such that, for all sufficiently large
n
, one can find a
(k,\ell)
-system
H
on
n
vertices satisfying

R(H;r)\;\geq\;t_{k-1}(n^{c}).

Another classical parameter for measuring sparsity in a hypergraph is its girth. A cycle of length
t
in a
k
-graph is a sequence of vertices and edges
v_{0},e_{0},v_{1},e_{1},\ldots,v_{t-1},e_{t-1}
such that
v_{i}
belongs to both
e_{i-1}
and
e_{i}
, where the indices are taken modulo
t
. A
k
-graph
H
has girth
g
if it does not contain cycles of length smaller than
g
. Note that a
k
-graph of girth
g\geq 3
must be linear. It would be interesting to determine the Ramsey number of linear
3
-graphs of large girth.

Problem 5.2
.

Determine whether there exists an integer
r\geq 2
, such that for every
g
and all sufficiently large
n
, one can find a linear
3
-graph
H
of girth
g
on
n
vertices with
R(H;r)
doubly exponential.

We remark that an argument similar to that of Theorem
1.1
yields a triangle-free linear
H
with doubly exponential Ramsey number. However, we were not able to find such a hypergraph for
g=5
.
