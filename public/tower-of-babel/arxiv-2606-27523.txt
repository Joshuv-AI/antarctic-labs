Coordinate projections of c c -vectors of cluster algebras from the annulus
Sarah B. Brodsky

Abstract
. For an acyclic cluster algebra, the c c -vectors are, up to sign, the real Schur roots of the associated root system. We study the two-coordinate projections ( c v , c w ) (c_{v},c_{w}) of this configuration: when the difference c v − c w c_{v}-c_{w} is bounded the image lies in a band of lattice lines, and we ask when the projection fills that band. A band-existence dichotomy, valid in every acyclic type, shows the difference is bounded if and only if the null root satisfies δ v = δ w \delta_{v}=\delta_{w} . For affine type A ~ n \widetilde{A}_{n} (the annulus), in the source-sink orientation, we resolve the filling question completely: every coordinate projection fills its band except along the source-sink diagonal, which carries only the finite regular part. The obstruction is the Auslander–Reiten defect, which a projection sees on its diagonal exactly when the defect is a coordinate difference; the only such pair is the source-sink pair of A ~ n \widetilde{A}_{n} , so the pattern depends on the chosen seed. More generally, every banded pair of null-root coefficient one fills, except these diagonals. Off the diagonal a banded pair in E ~ 7 \widetilde{E}_{7} fails to fill, so non-filling is not confined to type A ~ n \widetilde{A}_{n} ; a computation classifies the pairs of coefficient at least two over a range of affine types, where this E ~ 7 \widetilde{E}_{7} pair is the only further failure, and the general classification remains open.

1.
Introduction

Let
Q
be an acyclic quiver on vertex set
Q_{0}
, and let
\mathcal{A}(Q)
be the
cluster algebra with principal coefficients at the seed associated with

Q
. The
c
-vectors of
\mathcal{A}(Q)
, collected over all seeds, form a finite
or infinite subset
C(Q)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
; by sign-coherence

[
5
]
each
c
-vector is either nonnegative or nonpositive, and
by Nájera Chávez
[
9
]
the positive
c
-vectors are
exactly the real Schur roots of
Q
, the dimension vectors of the
exceptional indecomposable representations. Thus

C(Q)\;=\;\{\,\pm\beta:\beta\text{ a real Schur root of }Q\,\}.

For a pair of vertices
v,w\in Q_{0}
we consider the coordinate
projection

\pi_{vw}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\to\mathbb{Z}^{2},\qquad\pi_{vw}(c)=(c_{v},c_{w}).

When the difference
c_{v}-c_{w}
is bounded over
C(Q)
, the image

\pi_{vw}(C(Q))
is contained in a finite union of lattice lines, the

band

\{(x,y):|x-y|\leq b_{vw}\}
with
b_{vw}=\sup_{c}|c_{v}-c_{w}|
. The present paper answers two questions: for which pairs is the
difference bounded, and, when it is, when does
\pi_{vw}
surject onto
the band.

Two things make these questions natural. The
c
-vector set is a basic
mutation invariant, sign-coherent and equal to the signed real Schur roots;
its coordinate projections are the simplest two-dimensional shadows of this
configuration, and the band is exactly the region a bounded-difference shadow
can occupy, so filling is the natural completeness question. Moreover the
answer is not merely combinatorial: we show that the sole obstruction to
filling is the Auslander–Reiten defect, so the question detects a homological
invariant. In the annulus this acquires a topological reading, with the

c
-vectors the arcs of the surface

[
2
]
and the source-sink difference their
signed crossing number against the core curve (Section
9
).

The first question has a uniform answer in every acyclic type, governed
by the null root.

Theorem 1.1

(Band-existence dichotomy)
.

Let
Q
be an acyclic quiver. If
Q
is of finite type, then
C(Q)
is
finite, so every coordinate difference is bounded. If
Q
is of affine
type with null root
\delta
, then for
v,w\in Q_{0}
,

\sup_{c\in C(Q)}|c_{v}-c_{w}|<\infty\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\delta_{v}=\delta_{w},

and when
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}
one has

b_{vw}=\max_{\alpha}|\alpha_{v}-\alpha_{w}|
over the real roots

\alpha
of the underlying finite root system.

In type
\widetilde{A}_{n}
the null root is
\delta=(1,\dots,1)
, so

every
pair is banded, with
b_{vw}=1
. These are the cluster
algebras of the annulus
[
2
]
. We work throughout
with the
source-sink orientation
of the
(n+1)
-cycle: an acyclic
orientation with a
unique
source

and a
unique
sink
t

which divide the cycle into two oriented paths

and

q

.) In the surface model the two arcs
are the two boundary components, carrying
p
and
q
marked points, and the
two non-homogeneous tubes have ranks
p
and
q
. Our main result resolves
the surjectivity question completely for this seed.

Theorem 1.2

(Projection-filling)
.

Let
Q
be a source-sink orientation of the
(n+1)
-cycle, with unique source

s
and unique sink
t
. Then:

(1)

every coordinate difference satisfies
|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq 1
, so the
band is
\{|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq 1\}
;

(2)

for every pair
(v,w)
other than
(s,t)
, the projection

\pi_{vw}
surjects onto the full band;

(3)

for the source-sink pair,
\pi_{st}(C(Q))
is the band with the
diagonal
\{c_{s}=c_{t}\}
removed except for a bounded segment; the
diagonal carries only the regular part of
C(Q)
.

In particular the source-sink diagonal
\{c_{s}=c_{t}\}
is the unique
non-filled band line.

The mechanism is transparent once the right invariant is identified. The
defect form of the tame hereditary algebra
kQ
, which separates
preprojective, regular, and preinjective modules, turns out to be the

coordinate difference of source and sink
,

\partial(x)\;=\;x_{s}-x_{t},

a one-line consequence of the source having in-degree
0
and the sink
in-degree
2
in the cycle (Lemma

). Consequently an arc
is transjective exactly when it contains exactly one of
\{s,t\}
, and
the source-sink diagonal
\{c_{s}=c_{t}\}
is exactly the defect-zero
locus, where only the finitely many regular real Schur roots live. Every
other line is reached by a transjective root, whose
\delta
-shifts sweep
the entire line; the realizers are supplied by two short constructions
(Section
5
).

This gives the result a meaning beyond the combinatorics
(Section
7
). The defect is the canonical bounded
invariant whose zero-level is the finite set of exceptional regular roots
(Proposition
7.2
). On the
diagonal
this is
sharp: a banded pair has a non-filling diagonal exactly when the defect is
the coordinate difference
x_{v}-x_{w}
, which across all acyclic affine
types happens only for the source-sink pair of
\widetilde{A}_{n}
in its
source-sink orientation (Corollary
8.3
); there
the three band lines
of
\pi_{st}
are the preprojective, regular, and preinjective strata
(Theorem
7.3
). Off the diagonal the picture is
richer. In
\widetilde{D}_{4}
the defect
x_{1}+x_{2}-x_{3}-x_{4}
is not a
coordinate difference and every coordinate projection still fills
(Example
7.4
); but in
\widetilde{E}_{7}
a banded pair fails
to fill at its
outer
band lines, which the regular exceptional

c
-vectors reach but the transjective ones do not
(Proposition
8.9
). So non-filling pairs are not
confined to
\widetilde{A}_{n}
, and arise from two distinct mechanisms: an
interior gap at the defect diagonal, and a boundary extension by regular
roots. The first we control completely: every banded pair whose null-root
coefficient equals
1
fills, except the source-sink diagonal of

\widetilde{A}_{n}
(Theorem
8.7
), so non-filling at coefficient

1
is exactly that diagonal. The second can occur only at coefficient at
least
2
(Lemma
8.5
); a direct computation classifies it
across a substantial range of types, finding the lone
\widetilde{E}_{7}
pair,
and the general classification we leave open.

The squarefree Prüfer
4
-cycle on
\{1,2,3,6\}
, an acyclic
orientation of the
4
-cycle of affine type
\widetilde{A}_{3}
, is the
smallest nontrivial instance; there the source-sink pair is
(1,6)
,
and Theorem
1.2
recovers by hand the projection structure we
work out in Section
6
.

Conventions.

All quivers are finite, connected, and without loops or
2
-cycles. We
work over an algebraically closed field

, and identify a real root

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the facts about acyclic cluster algebras and
tame hereditary algebras that we use. We refer to

[
4
]
for cluster algebras with principal
coefficients and
c
-vectors, to
[

,

]

c
-vectors and real Schur roots

The
c
-vectors of
\mathcal{A}(Q)
at a seed

are the columns of the
c
-matrix

C^{t}
, which tracks the principal coefficients. By sign-coherence
(
[
5
, Theorem 1.7]
; for the skew-symmetric case,
Derksen–Weyman–Zelevinsky
[
1
]
) each
c
-vector is
nonnegative or nonpositive, and by Nájera Chávez

[
9
, Theorem 1.3]
the positive

-vectors of an acyclic
cluster algebra are exactly the real Schur roots of
Q
, that is, the
dimension vectors of the exceptional (rigid, indecomposable)
kQ
-modules.
Hence
C(Q)
is the set of signed real Schur roots. The
c
-vectors of affine
and tame type are thus governed by the tame hereditary representation theory
of
Q

[
10
]
; we use this dictionary throughout. To the best of
our knowledge the coordinate projections of the configuration
C(Q)
, and the
filling question studied here, have not been considered before.

Type classification

By the Fomin–Zelevinsky classification
[
3
]
, an
acyclic cluster algebra is of finite type if and only if the underlying
graph of
Q
is a Dynkin diagram, and of affine (tame) type if and only
if that graph is a Euclidean diagram. The acyclic orientations of the

(n+1)
-cycle are exactly the connected quivers of affine type

\widetilde{A}_{n}
.

Roots of affine type

Since
Q
is acyclic its Euler form is unitriangular and its symmetrized
Tits form is the affine quadratic form, which is positive semidefinite
with one-dimensional radical
\mathbb{Z}\delta
spanned by the null root

\delta
. The real roots of a simply-laced affine root system are exactly

\{\,\alpha+m\delta:\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathrm{fin}},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}\,\},

where
\Delta_{\mathrm{fin}}
is the underlying finite root system

[
7
, Ch. 6]
. In type
\widetilde{A}_{n}
one has

\delta=(1,\dots,1)
, and
\Delta_{\mathrm{fin}}=A_{n}
; the positive
roots of
A_{n}
obtained by deleting a vertex of the cycle are the
indicators of cyclic intervals, so the real roots of
\widetilde{A}_{n}

are

\{\,\pm\alpha_{I}+m\delta:I\text{ a cyclic interval},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}\,\}.

Preprojective, regular, preinjective

For a tame hereditary algebra the indecomposable modules split into three
families: preprojective, regular, and preinjective. The

transjective
modules are the preprojective and the preinjective
ones; the regular modules lie in tubes, of which all but finitely many
are homogeneous (rank
1
) and the rest are non-homogeneous. In type

\widetilde{A}_{n}
there are two non-homogeneous tubes, of ranks
p
and

q
; in types
\widetilde{D}
and
\widetilde{E}
there are three. The
defect
\partial
is the linear form on the
Grothendieck group with
\partial(M)<0
,
=0
,
>0
according as
M

is preprojective, regular, or preinjective; a real root is regular if and
only if its defect vanishes
[
10
]
. Preprojective and
preinjective modules are directing, hence exceptional, so every
transjective real root is a real Schur root. In a tube of rank
r
the
exceptional regular modules are exactly those of quasi-length less than

r
, finitely many.

3.
The band-existence dichotomy

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
, valid in
every acyclic type.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
Q
is of finite type, then
C(Q)
is a finite set of roots, so every
coordinate difference is bounded.

Suppose
Q
is of affine type. By the preliminaries every
c
-vector is

\pm(\alpha+m\delta)
with
\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathrm{fin}}
and

m\in\mathbb{Z}
, whence

c_{v}-c_{w}\;=\;\pm(\alpha_{v}-\alpha_{w})+m\,(\delta_{v}-\delta_{w}).

The set
\{\alpha_{v}-\alpha_{w}:\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathrm{fin}}\}
is
finite. If
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}
, the term in
m
vanishes, so

|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq\max_{\alpha}|\alpha_{v}-\alpha_{w}|<\infty
. If

\delta_{v}\neq\delta_{w}
, recall that the transjective real roots are
real Schur roots, hence
c
-vectors, and that there are infinitely many of
them, the preprojective and preinjective components each being infinite
(
[
10
, §3]
). Each such root is

\alpha+m\delta
with
\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathrm{fin}}
, and

\Delta_{\mathrm{fin}}
is finite, so
|m|
is unbounded over them. As

\delta_{v}\neq\delta_{w}
, the difference
c_{v}-c_{w}=\pm(\alpha_{v}-\alpha_{w})+m(\delta_{v}-\delta_{w})
has bounded
\alpha
-part and unbounded

m
-term, hence is unbounded. This proves the equivalence, and
the value of
b_{vw}
when
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}
, as desired.
∎

Example 3.1

(The dichotomy in type
\widetilde{D}_{4}
)
.

Let
Q
be the
4
-subspace quiver: a central vertex
0
and four leaves

1,2,3,4
, with any acyclic orientation of the four edges. The null
root is
\delta=(2,1,1,1,1)
, with the central coordinate equal to

2
. By Theorem
1.1
, the six leaf-leaf pairs are
banded, while the four center-leaf pairs are not. A direct enumeration of

c
-vectors confirms this: the leaf-leaf differences never exceed
1
,
whereas each center-leaf difference takes arbitrarily large values.

4.
The defect form in type
\widetilde{A}_{n}

In this section, we identify the defect form of an acyclic cycle quiver
and deduce the transjective criterion. Fix a source-sink orientation
Q
of
the
(n+1)
-cycle, with unique source
s
and unique sink
t
.

Lemma 4.1

(Defect and transjective criterion)
.

For any acyclic orientation of the
(n+1)
-cycle, with sources
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
and sinks
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
, the defect form of
kQ
is, up to sign,

\partial(x)\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{s_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{t_{i}};

this is a coordinate difference if and only if
k=1
, that is, the source
and sink are unique, in which case
\partial(x)=x_{s}-x_{t}
. Under that

(
standing
)
hypothesis a cyclic interval indicator

\alpha_{I}
is regular if and only
if
I
contains both or neither of
\{s,t\}
, and transjective if and
only if
I
contains exactly one of
\{s,t\}
. Moreover, if
\alpha_{I}
is
transjective, then
\alpha_{I}+m\delta
is a
c
-vector for every

m\in\mathbb{Z}
, whereas the regular part of
C(Q)
is finite.

Proof.

The defect is
\partial(x)=\langle\delta,x\rangle
, where

\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i}x_{i}y_{i}-\sum_{i\to j}x_{i}y_{j}
is the
Euler form. Since
\delta=(1,\dots,1)
,

\partial(x)\;=\;\sum_{i}x_{i}-\sum_{i\to j}x_{j}\;=\;\sum_{i}x_{i}-\sum_{j}\indeg(j)\,x_{j}.

In the
(n+1)
-cycle every vertex has degree
2
, so a source has in-degree

0
, a sink in-degree
2
, and every other vertex in-degree
1
; hence

\sum_{j}\indeg(j)\,x_{j}=\sum_{j}x_{j}+\sum_{i}x_{t_{i}}-\sum_{i}x_{s_{i}}
, and

\partial(x)\;=\;\sum_{i}x_{i}-\Bigl(\sum_{j}x_{j}+\sum_{i}x_{t_{i}}-\sum_{i}x_{s_{i}}\Bigr)\;=\;\sum_{i}x_{s_{i}}-\sum_{i}x_{t_{i}}.

This has
2k
nonzero coefficients, so it is a coordinate difference exactly
when
k=1
, giving
\partial(x)=x_{s}-x_{t}
; we assume this from now on.
A real root is regular if and only if its defect vanishes

[
10
]
; for a
0/1
indicator
\alpha_{I}
this means

(\alpha_{I})_{s}=(\alpha_{I})_{t}
, that is,
I
contains both or neither of

\{s,t\}
. Otherwise
\partial(\alpha_{I})=\pm 1
and
\alpha_{I}
is
transjective, which happens exactly when
I
contains one of
\{s,t\}
.

For the last statement, the defect is unchanged by adding
\delta
, since

\partial(\delta)=\delta_{s}-\delta_{t}=0
. Thus if
\alpha_{I}
is
transjective, then
\alpha_{I}+m\delta
has nonzero defect for every
m
,
so it is the dimension vector of a preprojective or preinjective
indecomposable, hence exceptional, hence a
c
-vector. The regular real
Schur roots are the exceptional regular modules; each of the two
non-homogeneous tubes contributes finitely many, and the homogeneous
tubes contain no exceptional modules, so the regular part of
C(Q)
is
finite, as desired.
∎

5.
The projection-filling theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. We keep
Q
,
s
,
t

as above, and we say a band line
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=d\}
is
filled
if

\pi_{vw}(C(Q))
contains every integer point of it.

We first record the elementary but decisive observation that a single
transjective root fills its whole line.

Lemma 5.1

(Transjective roots fill)
.

Let
\alpha_{I}
be a transjective cyclic interval and set

d=(\alpha_{I})_{v}-(\alpha_{I})_{w}
. Then the line
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=d\}
is
filled.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
,
\alpha_{I}+m\delta\in C(Q)
for every

m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Its projection is

\pi_{vw}(\alpha_{I}+m\delta)=((\alpha_{I})_{v}+m,\,(\alpha_{I})_{w}+m)
,
which runs over every integer point of
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=d\}
as
m
varies, as
desired.
∎

Observe that, by applying the sign
-1
, a transjective root with
difference
d
also fills the line
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=-d\}
. We now produce
transjective realizers for every line except the source-sink diagonal.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

(1)
By Theorem
1.1
with

\delta=(1,\dots,1)
, every difference is bounded, and the bound is

\max_{\alpha}|\alpha_{v}-\alpha_{w}|=1
since the finite roots of
A_{n}

are signed interval indicators.

(2) and the filled lines.
Fix a pair
(v,w)
and a value

d\in\{-1,0,1\}
. By Lemma
5.1
it suffices to exhibit a
transjective cyclic interval
I
with
(\alpha_{I})_{v}-(\alpha_{I})_{w}=d
,
that is, by Lemma
4.1
, an interval containing exactly one of

\{s,t\}
with the prescribed relation to
v,w
.

The lines
d=\pm 1
.
We treat
d=+1
, where the interval must
contain
v
and not
w
; the case
d=-1
is symmetric. If

v\in\{s,t\}
, the singleton
\{v\}
contains exactly one of

\{s,t\}
and excludes
w\neq v
, as required. If
v\notin\{s,t\}
,
let
I_{s}
be the unique arc from
v
to
s
avoiding
t
, and
I_{t}
the
unique arc from
v
to
t
avoiding
s
; each exists because deleting the
avoided vertex opens the cycle into a path containing both endpoints.
Then
I_{s}
contains
s
but not
t
, and
I_{t}
contains
t
but not
s
,
so both are transjective, and both contain
v
. They leave
v
toward
different neighbors: if they shared a first edge, then walking from
v

along it we would meet one of
s,t
before the other, and the arc avoiding
that vertex could not have used the edge. Hence
I_{s}\cap I_{t}=\{v\}
.
Since
w\neq v
, at least one of
I_{s},I_{t}
excludes

, and that interval
is the desired realizer.

The lines
d=0
.
Here the interval must contain both or neither
of
\{v,w\}
. The singleton
\{s\}
is transjective, and it contains
neither
v
nor

precisely when
s\notin\{v,w\}
; likewise
\{t\}

works precisely when
t\notin\{v,w\}
. If
\{v,w\}\neq\{s,t\}
,
then at least one of

lies outside
\{v,w\}
, so the corresponding

.

Thus for every pair
(v,w)\neq(s,t)
all three lines

(s,t)
the

are filled by
\{s\}
and
\{t\}
.

(3) The source-sink diagonal.
For the pair
(s,t)
and

d=0
, the condition
(\alpha_{I})_{s}=(\alpha_{I})_{t}
is the defect-zero
condition, so by Lemma
4.1
no transjective interval realizes
it; only regular roots lie on the diagonal
\{c_{s}=c_{t}\}
. The regular
part of

is finite (Lemma

), so the diagonal
carries only finitely many

∎

Remark 5.2

(Invariant phrasing)
.

The exceptional locus is intrinsic: it is the defect-zero line of the
source-sink pair,

\{c_{s}=c_{t}\}\;=\;\{\partial=0\}.

Equivalently, it is the projection direction along which the two tubes,
and only the tubes, are seen. Theorem
1.2
thus reads:
the

c
-vector set of an annulus cluster algebra surjects onto the full
bounded-difference band in every coordinate pair, except along the
defect-zero line of the source-sink pair, where only its regular part
lives.

(

Let

7.
The defect as the universal obstruction

In this section, we explain what projection-filling means: the obstruction
to surjectivity is, in every acyclic affine type, the Auslander–Reiten
defect, and a coordinate projection exhibits it precisely when the defect
happens to be a coordinate difference. In type
\widetilde{A}_{n}
it is,
namely
\partial=(\cdot)_{s}-(\cdot)_{t}
, and this is why a non-filling
coordinate pair exists there; in other affine types the defect is not a
coordinate difference, and the obstruction is invisible to coordinate
projections although it persists for the defect form itself.

We first isolate the filling criterion underlying
Theorem
1.2
, in a form valid in every acyclic affine type.

Lemma 7.1

(Filling criterion)
.

Let
Q
be acyclic of affine type and
(v,w)
a banded pair, that is,

\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}
. A band line
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=d\}
is filled if and
only if some
transjective
finite root
\alpha

(
defect

\partial(\alpha)\neq 0
)
satisfies
\alpha_{v}-\alpha_{w}=d
;
otherwise it carries only finitely many
c
-vectors.

Proof.

If such
\alpha
exists, then
\partial(\alpha+m\delta)=\partial(\alpha)\neq 0
for every
m
, so
\alpha+m\delta
is a transjective real root,
hence the dimension vector of a preprojective or preinjective
indecomposable, hence exceptional, hence a
c
-vector. Its projection

\pi_{vw}(\alpha+m\delta)=(\alpha_{v}+m,\alpha_{w}+m)
runs over every
integer point of the line as
m
varies, since
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}
. So
the line is filled. Conversely, every
c
-vector is
\pm(\alpha+m\delta)
;
if no transjective
\alpha
realizes the difference
d
, then every

c
-vector on the line has regular finite-root part (
\partial=0
), and
the regular real Schur roots are finite in number, so the line carries
only finitely many
c
-vectors, as desired.
∎

The role of the defect is now visible: a line is unfilled exactly when its
difference value is achieved only on the regular part, and the regular
part is finite. We record this finiteness intrinsically.

Proposition 7.2

(The defect is bounded with finite zero-level)
.

Let
Q
be acyclic of affine type. The defect
\partial
is bounded on

C(Q)
. Its zero-level
\{c\in C(Q):\partial(c)=0\}
is finite,
consisting of the signed exceptional regular roots, while every nonzero
level of
\partial
on
C(Q)
is infinite.

Proof.

The defect is invariant under the Auslander–Reiten translate and is
constant on each of the finitely many transjective
\tau
-orbits, so it
takes finitely many values on
C(Q)
. Its zero-level is the set of regular

c
-vectors, that is, the signed exceptional regular roots; these lie in the
finitely many non-homogeneous tubes and are finite in number, the
homogeneous tubes containing no exceptional modules. Each nonzero value of
\partial
is
attained on a full transjective
\tau
-orbit, which is infinite, so the
corresponding level is infinite, as desired.
∎

Thus
\partial
is the canonical bounded invariant whose vanishing cuts
out the finite regular part; it is the universal obstruction to filling. A
coordinate projection sees this obstruction exactly when its difference is
the defect.

Theorem 7.3

(Projection-filling detects the defect)
.

Let
Q
be acyclic of affine type, and let
(v,w)
be a banded pair. If

x_{v}-x_{w}=\pm\partial
as linear forms, then
\pi_{vw}
fails to fill,
and its non-filled line is exactly the defect-zero line
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=0\}
,
which carries only the finite regular part of
C(Q)
. In type

\widetilde{A}_{n}
the defect is the coordinate difference

\partial=(\cdot)_{s}-(\cdot)_{t}
, so this occurs precisely for the
source-sink pair
(s,t)
; there
\partial
takes only the values

-1,0,+1
, and the three band lines
c_{s}-c_{t}=-1,0,+1
are exactly
the preprojective, regular, and preinjective strata of
C(Q)
. This is the
content of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

If
x_{v}-x_{w}=\pm\partial
, then the line
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=0\}
is the
defect-zero locus, which by Proposition
7.2
is
finite, hence unfilled; the off-diagonal lines
c_{v}-c_{w}=\pm 1
are the
nonzero defect levels, infinite and filled by
Lemma
7.1
(the simples
S_{s},S_{t}
at the source
and sink are preinjective and preprojective, of defect
\pm 1
). In type

\widetilde{A}_{n}
, Lemma
4.1
gives
\partial=(\cdot)_{s}-(\cdot)_{t}
, and
x_{v}-x_{w}=\pm\partial
holds for a coordinate pair if
and only if
\{v,w\}=\{s,t\}
. Finally, every indecomposable of

\widetilde{A}_{n}
has dimension vector
\alpha_{I}+m\delta
with
\alpha_{I}

a
0/1
indicator, so
\partial(\alpha_{I}+m\delta)=(\alpha_{I})_{s}-(\alpha_{I})_{t}\in\{-1,0,1\}
; the three values are the preprojective,
regular, and preinjective defects, as desired.
∎

The point of Theorem
7.3
is that the elementary
combinatorial question of projection-surjectivity recovers a homological
invariant: in the annulus
\widetilde{A}_{n}
the unique non-filling
coordinate pair is the one computing the Auslander–Reiten defect
(Theorem
1.2
). Off the diagonal, and in other affine types, the
relationship is looser. The next example shows a type where the defect is
not a coordinate difference and every coordinate projection nonetheless
fills; by contrast, Proposition
8.9
exhibits a
type (
\widetilde{E}_{7}
) where a projection fails to fill off the diagonal,
so the defect being a coordinate difference is
not
necessary for
non-filling.

Example 7.4

(The obstruction without a non-filling pair:
\widetilde{D}_{4}
)
.

Let
Q
be the affine quiver
\widetilde{D}_{4}
with central vertex
0
and
leaves
1,2,3,4
, in the orientation
1\to 0
,
2\to 0
,
0\to 3
,

0\to 4
. The null root is
\delta=(2,1,1,1,1)
, and the defect,
computed as in Lemma
4.1
, is

\partial\;=\;x_{1}+x_{2}-x_{3}-x_{4},

which is
not
a coordinate difference. By
Theorem
1.1
the banded pairs are the six leaf-leaf
pairs, each with bound
1
. A direct enumeration of
c
-vectors shows that

every
leaf-leaf projection fills its band: there is no non-filling
coordinate pair. Yet the defect remains the obstruction in the sense of
Proposition
7.2
: on
C(Q)
it takes the values

-2,-1,0,1,2
, with the zero-level finite (twelve signed
exceptional regular roots) and each nonzero level infinite. The
obstruction has simply migrated off the coordinate axes: it lives in the
linear form
\partial
, which no single coordinate projection isolates
because
\partial
is not a coordinate difference.

Remark 7.5

(The defect, the Coxeter–Jordan invariant, and the divisor lattice)
.

In type
\widetilde{A}_{n}
the defect
\partial=(\cdot)_{s}-(\cdot)_{t}

also has a Coxeter-theoretic reading: it is, up to sign, the unique
linear invariant of the Coxeter transformation’s size-two Jordan block at
eigenvalue
1
(the partner of the null root
\delta
).
For the divisor lattice of Example
6.1
, where the
vertices are the divisors of a product of two primes, the source is the
bottom
\hat{0}=1
and the sink is the top
\hat{1}=6
of the divisor
lattice, so the defect is the difference of the extreme coordinates,

\partial=x_{\hat{0}}-x_{\hat{1}}
, of multiplicative weight
\log 6=\log(2\cdot 3)
. The maximally composite direction of the lattice and the
Auslander–Reiten defect of the cluster category thus coincide.

8.
Non-filling pairs across affine types

In this section, we go beyond the annulus and ask how non-filling pairs
behave in the other affine types. We settle the diagonal lines in every type
(Corollary
8.3
) and, more generally, every
banded pair of null-root coefficient one
(Theorem
8.7
): such a pair fills unless it is the
source-sink diagonal of
\widetilde{A}_{n}
. The remaining pairs have
coefficient at least
2
; there we reduce the off-diagonal lines to a
root-string condition (Lemma
8.8
) and classify the
non-filling pairs by computer across a substantial range of classical and
exceptional types (Proposition
8.10
),
where the only failure beyond the
\widetilde{A}_{n}
diagonals is a single
pair in
\widetilde{E}_{7}
.

Throughout, we write
\ell=x_{v}-x_{w}
for a banded pair
(v,w)
. The
arguments rest on the following spanning property of the hyperplane

\ker\ell=\{x_{v}=x_{w}\}
.

Lemma 8.1

(Spanning)
.

Let
Q
be a connected acyclic quiver of affine type, and let
(v,w)
be a
pair of distinct vertices. Then the real roots lying on
\ker\ell
span it.

Proof.

The hyperplane
\ker\ell
has dimension
|Q_{0}|-1
, and contains the

|Q_{0}|-2
linearly independent simple roots
e_{i}
with
i\neq v,w
. It
therefore suffices to exhibit one real root on
\ker\ell
outside their span,
that is, a real root
\alpha
with
\alpha_{v}=\alpha_{w}\neq 0
. The
underlying graph of
Q
is a tree or a cycle, so it contains a geodesic

v=u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{k}=w
whose vertices induce a subquiver of type

A_{k+1}
(an induced path: a tree has no chords, and a proper arc
of a cycle is a path). The sum of its simple roots,

\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{k}e_{u_{i}}
, is the highest root of that subsystem,
hence a real root of
Q
, and satisfies
\alpha_{v}=\alpha_{w}=1
. Together
with the
e_{i}
,
i\neq v,w
, it spans
\ker\ell
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 8.2

(The diagonal detects the defect)
.

The diagonal line
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=0\}
fills if and only if

\ell\neq\pm\partial
. Equivalently, the diagonal fails to fill if and only
if the defect is the coordinate difference
\ell
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.1
the diagonal fills if and only if some
transjective root lies on
\ker\ell
. If
\ell=\pm\partial
, then

\ker\ell=\ker\partial
contains no transjective root (those
have
\partial\neq 0
), so the diagonal does not fill. Suppose
instead
\ell\neq\pm\partial
. Were every real root on
\ker\ell
regular,
then by Lemma
8.1
the real roots spanning
\ker\ell
would all
lie in
\ker\partial
, giving
\ker\ell\subseteq\ker\partial
; as both are
hyperplanes this is an equality, so
\partial=c\,\ell
for a scalar
c
. But

\ell
is primitive, and
\partial=\langle\delta,-\rangle
is primitive as
well (the unimodular Euler form sends the primitive null root

\delta
to a primitive coefficient vector), so
c=\pm 1
and

\ell=\pm\partial
, a contradiction. Hence some real root on
\ker\ell
is
transjective, and the diagonal fills, as desired.
∎

Corollary 8.3

(The diagonal classification, all affine types)
.

A banded coordinate pair
(v,w)
of a connected acyclic affine quiver
Q

has a non-filling diagonal if and only if
Q
is a source-sink orientation of
the
(n+1)
-cycle
(
type
\widetilde{A}_{n}
)
and

\{v,w\}=\{s,t\}
is its source-sink pair. In particular such a pair is
unique when it exists, and no diagonal fails to fill in types

\widetilde{D}
,
\widetilde{E}
, or in any cycle orientation with more than
one source.

Proof.

By Proposition
8.2
a diagonal fails iff

\ell=\pm\partial
; i.e. iff
\partial
is a coordinate difference. Now

\partial=\sum_{j}\partial_{j}\,x_{j}
with

\partial_{j}=\delta_{j}-\sum_{i\to j}\delta_{i}
is a coordinate difference
precisely when exactly two coefficients
\partial_{j}
are nonzero, equal to

+1
and
-1
. For the cycle (
\widetilde{A}_{n}
), Lemma
4.1

gives
\partial=\sum_{i}x_{s_{i}}-\sum_{i}x_{t_{i}}
over the
k
sources and

k
sinks; this is a coordinate difference iff
k=1
, in which case

\partial=x_{s}-x_{t}
is realized by the source-sink pair, while an
orientation with
k\geq 2
has
2k\geq 4
nonzero coefficients and no
failing diagonal. For trees we count nonzero coefficients by leaves: at a
leaf
j
with unique neighbor
k
, the null-root relation
2\delta_{j}=\sum_{i\sim j}\delta_{i}
gives
\delta_{k}=2\delta_{j}
, so
\partial_{j}=\delta_{j}
if
j
is a source and
\partial_{j}=\delta_{j}-\delta_{k}=-\delta_{j}
if
j
is a sink; thus
\partial_{j}=\pm\delta_{j}\neq 0
at

every
leaf, in any orientation. Types
\widetilde{D}
and

\widetilde{E}
are trees with at least three leaves (four for

\widetilde{D}_{n}
, three for
\widetilde{E}_{n}
), so
\partial
has
at least three nonzero coefficients and is not a coordinate difference. The
stated classification follows, as desired.
∎

Remark 8.4

(Seed-dependence of the filling pattern)
.

The
c
-vector set
C(Q)
, and with it the filling pattern, depends on the
chosen acyclic seed, not merely on its mutation type. On the
4
-cycle with
vertices
\{0,1,2,3\}
the source-sink orientation
0\to 1\to 2
,

0\to 3\to 2
has defect
x_{0}-x_{2}
and the unique non-filling pair

(0,2)
of Theorem
1.2
, whereas the alternating orientation

0\to 1\leftarrow 2\to 3\leftarrow 0
has two sources
\{0,2\}
and two
sinks
\{1,3\}
, defect
\partial=x_{0}-x_{1}+x_{2}-x_{3}
(not a
coordinate difference), and hence no non-filling pair at all, by
Corollary
8.3
. Both are of cluster type

\widetilde{A}_{2,2}
, the type of the Prüfer example
(Example
6.1
); so non-filling is a property of
the source-sink seed, not of the annulus cluster algebra in the abstract.
This is why we fix the source-sink orientation throughout.

We next bound the regular contribution to any band; it confines all further
non-filling to pairs of null-root coefficient at least
2
.

Lemma 8.5

(Regular band bound)
.

Let
(v,w)
be a banded pair, so
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}
. Every regular

c
-vector
c
satisfies
|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq\delta_{v}
. In particular a band line

\{c_{v}-c_{w}=d\}
with
|d|\geq 2
that is met by a regular
c
-vector
forces
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}\geq 2
.

Proof.

A regular
c
-vector is, up to sign, the dimension vector of an exceptional
regular module: the positive
c
-vectors are the real Schur roots
(§
2
), a regular one is a real Schur root of vanishing defect,
and such a root is the dimension vector of an exceptional regular
indecomposable. Write the
c
-vector as
\pm\beta
with
\beta=\underline{\dim}\,M
;
it suffices to prove
0\leq\beta\leq\delta
componentwise, since then both

\beta_{v}
and
\beta_{w}
lie in
[0,\delta_{v}]
(using
\delta_{w}=\delta_{v}
),
so
|c_{v}-c_{w}|=|\beta_{v}-\beta_{w}|\leq\delta_{v}
.

We recall the structure of the regular components
(
[
10
, §3]
). The regular indecomposables lie
in tubes; in a tube of rank
r
the quasi-simple objects

S_{1},\dots,S_{r}
, cyclically ordered, are its regular simple modules, and
their dimension vectors satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\underline{\dim}\,S_{i}=\delta
. Every indecomposable of the tube is uniserial for the regular
composition series, determined by its quasi-socle
S_{j}
and quasi-length

m\geq 1
, with quasi-composition factors the
m
consecutive quasi-simples

S_{j},S_{j+1},\dots,S_{j+m-1}
(indices modulo
r
); hence

\beta\;=\;\underline{\dim}\,M\;=\;\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\underline{\dim}\,S_{\,j+i}.

By the preliminaries the exceptional regular indecomposables are exactly
those of quasi-length
m\leq r-1
; since
M
is exceptional we have

1\leq m\leq r-1
, so in particular
r\geq 2
and the tube is
non-homogeneous. The
m
summands are then pairwise distinct quasi-simples
(being fewer than a full cycle of length
r
), so the
complementary
r-m\geq 1
quasi-simples form the disjoint consecutive arc

S_{j+m},\dots,S_{j+r-1}
, and

\delta-\beta\;=\;\sum_{i=m}^{r-1}\underline{\dim}\,S_{\,j+i}

is again a sum of dimension vectors of modules, hence nonnegative. Both
\beta
and
\delta-\beta
are therefore nonnegative in every coordinate, that is,

0\leq\beta\leq\delta
, as required.

For the last assertion, if a regular
c
-vector
c
meets the line
c_{v}-c_{w}=d
, then
|d|=|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}
by the bound just
proved, so
|d|\geq 2
forces
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}\geq 2
, as desired.
∎

A second ingredient identifies the coefficient-one vertices outside the
cycle.

Lemma 8.6

(Coefficient one forces a leaf)
.

Let
Q
be a connected acyclic quiver of affine type whose underlying graph
is a tree. If
\delta_{v}=1
, then
v
is a leaf.

Proof.

The null root spans the radical of the symmetric Tits form, so it satisfies
the balance relation
2\delta_{v}=\sum_{u\sim v}\delta_{u}
at every vertex

v
. Let
T=\{u:\delta_{u}=1,\ \deg u\geq 2\}
; we show
T=\varnothing
, which is the claim. Suppose
v\in T
. Since
\sum_{u\sim v}\delta_{u}=2\delta_{v}=2
and each
\delta_{u}\geq 1
, the vertex
v
has
exactly two neighbors, both of coefficient
1
. For such a neighbor
u
one
has
\delta_{u}=1
; were
\deg u=1
, its unique neighbor would be
v
and
the balance relation would read
2=2\delta_{u}=\delta_{v}=1
, which is
absurd. So
\deg u\geq 2
and
u\in T
. Thus every vertex of
T
has both
of its neighbors in
T
, so the subgraph induced on
T
is
2
-regular and
contains a cycle. As
Q
is a tree this is impossible, so
T=\varnothing

and every coefficient-one vertex is a leaf, as desired.
∎

With these in hand we settle every banded pair of coefficient one.

Theorem 8.7

(Coefficient-one pairs fill)
.

Let
Q
be a connected acyclic quiver of affine type, and let
(v,w)
be a
banded pair with
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}=1
. Then
\pi_{vw}
surjects onto the
whole band, unless
Q
is a source-sink orientation of the
(n+1)
-cycle and

(v,w)
is its source-sink pair, in which case the diagonal
\{c_{v}=c_{w}\}

is the only band line that fails to fill.

Proof.

If
Q
is a source-sink orientation of the
(n+1)
-cycle, this is
Theorem
1.2
. If
Q
is any other orientation of the cycle, it has

k\geq 2
sources, so by Lemma
4.1
its defect has
2k\geq 4

nonzero coefficients and is not a coordinate difference; the diagonal

then fills by Corollary
8.3
. For

we
exhibit a transjective cyclic interval containing
v
but not

: if
v
is a
source or a sink, the singleton
\{v\}
is such an interval
(defect
\pm 1
); otherwise
v
is interior to a maximal
directed arc bounded by a source
s
and a sink
t
, and the sub-arcs from
s

to
v
and from
v
to
t
each contain exactly one of
\{s,t\}
, hence are
transjective, and meet only in
v
, so the one avoiding

is the desired
interval. Its
\delta
-shifts fill
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=1\}
, and the sign
-1

fills
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=-1\}
. Since
\delta=(1,\dots,1)
, the band is

\{|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq 1\}
by Theorem

, so these are all
its lines and every one fills. Finally suppose
Q
is a tree. A
band line carrying a

c
-vector is filled, since the
\delta
-shifts of that

-vector
sweep it (Lemma
7.1
). By
Lemma
8.5
a regular
c
-vector satisfies

|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq\delta_{v}=1
, so every line
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=d\}
with

|d|\geq 2
carries only transjective
c
-vectors and is filled; it remains to
treat

.

By Lemma
8.6
both

and

are leaves. A leaf is a
source or a sink of
Q
, so its simple module is injective or projective,
hence preinjective or preprojective; in particular the simple root
e_{v}
is
transjective (
\partial(e_{v})=\pm 1\neq 0
), and likewise

e_{w}
. The transjective
c
-vectors
e_{v}+m\delta
(

) project onto every integer point of
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=1\}
, and
the
e_{w}+m\delta
onto every point of
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=-1\}
, so both lines
fill. Finally, by the computation in the proof of
Corollary

the defect
\partial
is a
coordinate difference only for the source-sink pair of the cycle; as

is a

\partial\neq\pm(x_{v}-x_{w})
, so the diagonal

Proposition

. Hence the whole band fills, as
desired.
∎

In particular, among banded pairs with
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}=1
the only
failure is the source-sink diagonal of
\widetilde{A}_{n}
; this resolves all
of type
\widetilde{A}_{n}
together with every leaf-leaf pair of

\widetilde{D}
and
\widetilde{E}
. The remaining banded pairs have

\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}\geq 2
, where Lemma

permits the

-vectors to push the band beyond the transjective range. We treat
these next; a root-string argument first reduces the off-diagonal lines to a
non-orthogonality condition.

Lemma 8.8

(Off-diagonal reduction)
.

Let
d\neq 0
be a band value of a banded pair
(v,w)
. If some regular
root
\alpha
with
\ell(\alpha)=d
is non-orthogonal to some transjective
root
\mu
with
\ell(\mu)=0
, then the line
\{c_{v}-c_{w}=d\}
fills.

Proof.

Since
(\alpha,\mu)\neq 0
in a simply-laced root system,
\alpha+\mu

or
\alpha-\mu
is a root; call it
\beta
. Then
\ell(\beta)=\ell(\alpha)\pm\ell(\mu)=d
and
\partial(\beta)=\partial(\alpha)\pm\partial(\mu)=\pm\partial(\mu)\neq 0
, so
\beta
is a transjective root realizing the
difference
d
, and the line fills by Lemma
7.1
, as
desired.
∎

The sufficient condition of Lemma
8.8
can fail, and when
it does the line can genuinely fail to fill. This happens already in

\widetilde{E}_{7}
, and shows that non-filling pairs are
not
confined
to type
\widetilde{A}_{n}
.

Proposition 8.9

(An off-diagonal non-filling pair in
\widetilde{E}_{7}
)
.

Let
Q
be the affine quiver
\widetilde{E}_{7}
, drawn as the star with three
arms from a central vertex
0
: the short arm
1
, the first long arm

2,3,4
, and the second long arm
5,6,7

(
each long arm listed
from the center outward
)
. Its null root is then
\delta=(\delta_{0};\delta_{1};\delta_{2},\delta_{3},\delta_{4};\delta_{5},\delta_{6},\delta_{7})=(4;2;3,2,1;3,2,1)
. The pair
(v,w)=(3,6)
of the two
arm-interior vertices of null-root coefficient
2
is banded, and its band
has
b_{vw}=2
: the regular exceptional roots

(2,1,1,0,0,2,2,1)
and
(2,1,2,2,1,1,0,0)
are
c
-vectors with

c_{v}-c_{w}=\mp 2
, whereas every
transjective

c
-vector has

|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq 1
. Hence the outer lines
c_{v}-c_{w}=\pm 2
carry only
finitely many
(
regular
)

c
-vectors and do not fill: the
pair is non-filling, off the diagonal.

Proof.

Both displayed vectors are real roots (norm
2
for the Tits
form) of defect
0
, hence regular, and the mutation enumeration of
the ancillary scripts (Remark
8.11
) produces them as
c
-vectors;
their
(v,w)
-projections lie on

c_{v}-c_{w}=\mp 2
. Every transjective
c
-vector, on the other hand,
satisfies
|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq 1
: writing it as
\alpha+m\delta
with

\partial(\alpha)\neq 0
, the
\delta
-shift cancels in

c_{v}-c_{w}=\alpha_{v}-\alpha_{w}
(since
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}
),
and the computation of Remark
8.11
bounds

|\alpha_{v}-\alpha_{w}|
by
1
over the transjective roots. So no transjective
root realizes
d=\pm 2
, and by
Lemma
7.1
those lines do not fill, while the
finitely many regular
c
-vectors leave them with only finitely many points,
as desired.
∎

So there are
two
distinct sources of non-filling, both consistent
with Lemma
7.1
. In
\widetilde{A}_{n}
the gap is

interior
: the transjective roots skip the defect-zero diagonal,
which only the regular roots meet (Theorems
1.2

and
8.7
). In

\widetilde{E}_{7}
the failure is at the
boundary
: the regular
exceptional
c
-vectors reach further than the transjective ones, extending
the band past the transjective range and leaving its outer lines unfilled.
The diagonal classification (Corollary
8.3
)
is unaffected; it concerns only
d=0
, while the
\widetilde{E}_{7}
failure
is at
d=\pm 2
, in line with the bound
|c_{v}-c_{w}|\leq\delta_{v}
of
Lemma
8.5
.

A direct computation, carried out by two independent methods, classifies the
non-filling pairs across a substantial range of simply-laced affine types.

Proposition 8.10

(Classification by computer)
.

For the simply-laced affine types
\widetilde{A}_{p,q}

(
p+q\leq 6
)
,
\widetilde{D}_{n}

(
4\leq n\leq 8
)
, and
\widetilde{E}_{6},\widetilde{E}_{7},\widetilde{E}_{8}
, the
non-filling banded pairs are exactly the following:

(1)

in each
\widetilde{A}_{p,q}
, the unique source-sink pair, whose only
non-filling line is the defect diagonal
d=0

(
the interior-gap
mechanism of Corollary
8.3
)
;

(2)

in
\widetilde{E}_{7}
, the pair of
Proposition
8.9
, whose non-filling lines are

d=\pm 2

(
the boundary-extension mechanism
)
.

Every banded pair in
\widetilde{D}_{n}

(
4\leq n\leq 8
)
,

\widetilde{E}_{6}
, and
\widetilde{E}_{8}
fills. In particular,

\widetilde{E}_{7}
is the only exceptional type in this range with a
non-filling pair.

Proof.

This is a finite computation. The
c
-vectors are the signed real Schur roots
(§
2
); their transjective part is generated exactly through
integer powers of the Coxeter transformation acting on the projective
dimension vectors, and the regular exceptional part is enumerated exactly as
the positive real roots
\beta\leq\delta
of defect
0

(Lemma
8.5
). For each banded pair we record the band and,
by the defect criterion of Lemma
7.1
, the set of
filled lines. The same data is produced independently by mutation of the

(B,C)
-seeds. The two methods agree, and the integer arithmetic
(
q(\beta)=1
and
\partial(\beta)=\langle\delta,\beta\rangle
exact)
admits no numerical artifact. The full computation is provided as the
ancillary scripts described in Remark
8.11
, which finishes
the proof.
∎

Remark 8.11

(Computational verification)
.

The classifications of Propositions
8.9

and
8.10
are reproducible from the
ancillary files accompanying this paper (directory

anc/
). Two
independent enumerations are run.
Root-theoretic:
the Euler matrix of
an acyclic quiver is unitriangular, so the Coxeter matrix
\Phi=-E^{-1}E^{T}

is integral; the transjective
c
-vectors are generated exactly as

\Phi^{\pm m}
applied to the projective dimension vectors for
m
up to two
Coxeter periods (
2n+8
steps, beyond which the difference values
on a banded pair have stabilized), and the regular exceptional

c
-vectors are the finitely many positive real roots
\beta\leq\delta
of
defect
0
(an exact box enumeration, justified by
Lemma
8.5
).
Mutation:
a breadth-first
mutation of the
(B,C)
-seeds, keyed on the full seed, collects
c
-vectors
until the per-pair band data stabilizes (with an
L^{1}
-norm cap on
the seeds enqueued, which does not affect difference values). Every
test is exact integer arithmetic: a vector is a real root iff
q(\beta)=1

and its defect is
\langle\delta,\beta\rangle
, both computed over
\mathbb{Z}
.
The two enumerations agree on every banded pair throughout the stated range.
The scripts are short, dependency-free (Python 3 with NumPy for
integer matrix algebra, no floating point in any test), and the
ancillary
anc/
directory includes a README with the exact commands
and the expected output.

In summary, non-filling falls under exactly two headings. For

\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}=1
it occurs only at the source-sink diagonal of

\widetilde{A}_{n}
, by Theorem
8.7
; this is the interior gap,
and it is settled in
all
affine types, not merely the computed range.
For
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}\geq 2
, Lemma
8.5
permits a
regular
c
-vector to reach beyond the transjective range, and
Proposition
8.9
shows this does happen; the
computation locates it, in the present range, only at the single

\widetilde{E}_{7}
pair.

Remark 8.12

(The boundary mechanism)
.

The computation locates the boundary mechanism more precisely. For a banded
pair, let
m_{t}
and
m_{r}
denote the largest value of
|c_{v}-c_{w}|
attained
by a transjective, respectively a regular,
c
-vector; then
m_{r}\leq\delta_{v}

by Lemma
8.5
, and the band is
[-B,B]
with

B=\max(m_{t},m_{r})
. The quantity
m_{r}
is a tube invariant: it is the
largest separation of
v
and
w
achieved by a consecutive run of
quasi-simples in a non-homogeneous tube. Across the computed range the
transjective differences fill the contiguous interval
[-m_{t},m_{t}]
, so the
pair fails to fill exactly when
m_{r}>m_{t}
, the non-filled lines being

m_{t}<|d|\leq m_{r}
. Every computed pair with
\delta_{v}\geq 2
has

m_{r}=1
, with the sole exception of the
\widetilde{E}_{7}
pair of
Proposition
8.9
, where a single non-homogeneous tube
separates
v
and
w
by
m_{r}=2
while the transjective reach is only

m_{t}=1
. We conjecture that the transjective differences are contiguous in
every affine type, so that filling fails precisely when
m_{r}>m_{t}
; the
determination of
m_{t}
and
m_{r}
from the tube ranks and the position of

v,w
we leave open.

A complete classification of the non-filling pairs in
all
affine types
remains open beyond this range. By Remark
8.12
it
amounts to reading the transjective and regular reaches
m_{t},m_{r}
off the
tube data; the evidence is that
m_{r}>m_{t}
is rare, occurring only at the

\widetilde{E}_{7}
pair among the types above.

9.
Further directions

In this section, we collect two directions for further work: a topological
reading of the defect in the annulus, and the general classification problem
that Proposition
8.10
leaves open.

A surface reading of the defect.

This places the present discussion in the line of geometric descriptions of

c
-vectors by non-self-crossing curves on a surface
(
[
6
]
; conjecturally in general

[
8
]
), specialized here to the annulus.
In the annulus model of
\widetilde{A}_{n}
, the indecomposable modules are
the arcs, and the trichotomy has a topological form: the transjective arcs
are those joining the
two
boundary components, and the regular ones
are confined to a single boundary component or are closed loops around the
annulus. The defect
\partial=c_{s}-c_{t}
is then the net transverse
crossing of the arc; it is
\pm 1
for an arc that joins the two
boundaries (according to orientation) and
0
for a confined arc. Read this
way, the three band lines of the source-sink projection are the two
transverse orientations and the confined locus: the off-diagonal lines are
filled because boundary-joining arcs occur at every winding offset (the

\delta
-shift adds a loop around the annulus), while the diagonal carries
only the finitely many exceptional confined arcs. Making this precise
(identifying
\partial
with the transverse intersection number against the
core curve, and the
\delta
-shift with Dehn twisting) would recast
Theorem
7.3
as a statement in surface topology.
Observe that this clean dichotomy is special to the annulus; the orbifold
surface models of
\widetilde{D}
and
\widetilde{E}
are more intricate,
consistent with the richer non-filling behavior found in
Proposition
8.9
.

Classifying the non-filling pairs.

The main problem left open is the full classification of non-filling
banded coordinate pairs across all affine types. The coefficient-one pairs
are settled in every type by Theorem
8.7
, so the question is
confined to
\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}\geq 2
, where by
Remark
8.12
it amounts to comparing the transjective
reach
m_{t}
with the regular reach
m_{r}
. The computational classification
(Proposition
8.10
) settles a substantial
finite range: the only off-diagonal failure there is the single

\widetilde{E}_{7}
pair, with
\widetilde{D}_{n}
(
n\leq 8
),

\widetilde{E}_{6}
, and
\widetilde{E}_{8}
filling completely. A general answer
would express
m_{t}
and
m_{r}
, and hence the failure locus, in terms of the
ranks of the non-homogeneous tubes and the position of
v,w
relative to
them.
