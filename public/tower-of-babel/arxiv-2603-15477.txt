Mean Field Games with Reflected Dynamics: Penalization and Relaxed Control Approach


Abstract
In this paper, we investigate a class of Mean Field Games (MFGs) in which the state dynamics are governed by multidimensional reflected stochastic differential equations (SDEs). We establish the existence of an equilibrium and show that it can be approximated by the equilibrium of MFGs with non-reflected SDE. This approximation is constructed via a penalization method combined with the relaxed control approach introduced in [ 21 ] . Under a uniform ellipticity condition, and by applying the penalization method together with the mimicking theorem, we prove the existence of a Markovian MFG. Furthermore, under an additional convexity assumption, we demonstrate the existence of a strict-Markovian MFG. In the general case, we prove that relaxed MFG solutions with reflected dynamics can be approximated by strict controls whose dynamics are governed by penalized SDEs.

Introduction

Mean Field Games offer an optimization-based framework to address the complexity of large-population decision problems by approximating the collective behavior of many interacting agents through ideas from statistical physics. Introduced independently by Lasry and Lions
[
24
,
23
,
22
]
and by Huang et al.
[
14
,
13
]
, MFGs arise as the limiting case of symmetric stochastic differential
N
-player games when
N\to\infty
, where each agent interacts weakly with the population via its empirical distribution. In this limit, the analysis simplifies while still yielding approximate Nash equilibria for the original finite game. The framework relies on the representative agent approach, where the empirical distribution of states is approximated by a deterministic, measure-valued process, leading to an optimization problem for a single player whose optimal strategy must be consistent, in a fixed-point sense, with the collective distribution of all agents.

In this paper, we study a class of MFGs where the state dynamics are governed by a multidimensional reflected SDE. The formulation proceeds as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{(1) Fix a flow of measures }\mu_{t}:[0,T]\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\ \\[4.30554pt]
&\text{(2) solve the corresponding stochastic control problem}\\[4.30554pt]
&\quad\inf_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X_{t},\mu_{t},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}h(t,X_{t},\mu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}K_{t}+g(X_{T},\mu_{T})\right),\\[4.30554pt]
&\text{subject to the reflected SDE}\\[4.30554pt]
&\quad\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X_{t}+\mathrm{d}K_{t}=b(t,X_{t},\mu_{t},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t,X_{t},\mu_{t},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t},\\[2.15277pt]
\text{Law}(X_{0})=\lambda,\quad X_{t}\in\bar{D}\ \text{a.s.},\\[2.15277pt]
K_{t}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\eta(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}|K|_{s},\quad|K|_{t}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{1}_{\{X_{s}\in\partial D\}}\,\mathrm{d}|K|_{s}<\infty;\end{cases}\\[8.61108pt]
&\text{(3) solve the fixed point problem }\text{Law}(X)=\mu,\text{where X is the optimal state process from 2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

A flow
(\mu_{t})
that satisfies the consistency condition in point 3 is referred to as a MFG solution.

The MFG problem has been investigated through three main approaches. The first, proposed by Lasry and Lions
[
24
]
, is an analytical method based on solving a coupled system of forward–backward partial differential equations. The backward equation corresponds to the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation derived from the individual optimization problem, while the forward equation is a Kolmogorov–Fokker–Planck equation describing the evolution of the population distribution. The second approach is probabilistic, relying on the stochastic Pontryagin maximum principle to formulate the fixed-point problem as a system of McKean–Vlasov forward–backward stochastic differential equations; see, for example,
[
5
,
8
,
9
]
. The third approach, introduced by Lacker
[
21
]
, is based on the weak formulation of stochastic control, employing relaxed controls and martingale problems. Using compactness arguments and Berge’s maximum theorem, Lacker established the upper hemi-continuity of the best-response correspondence and applied the Kakutani–Fan–Glicksberg fixed-point theorem to prove the existence of a measure-valued process
\mu
consistent with the agents’ optimal responses. More recently, this weak formulation has been extended to the study of MFGs with reflected dynamics; see
[
7
,
27
,
17
]
.

To analyze the MFG system in (
1.1
), we consider a penalized version without reflection, denoted by
MFG_{n}
, defined as follows for each
n\geq 1
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{(i) Fix a flow of measures }\mu_{t}^{n}:[0,T]\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\\[4.30554pt]
&\text{(ii) Solve the associated stochastic control problem:}\\[4.30554pt]
&\quad\inf_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},\alpha_{t})+n\,h(t,X_{t},\mu_{t})\,(X_{t}^{n}-\pi_{\bar{D}}(X_{t}^{n}))\,\mathrm{d}t+g(X_{T}^{n},\mu_{T}^{n})\right);\\[4.30554pt]
&\text{subject to the penalized (non-reflected) dynamics:}\\[4.30554pt]
&\quad\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{n}=b(t,X_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t-n\big(X_{t}^{n}-\pi_{\bar{D}}(X_{t}^{n})\big)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t,X_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t},\\[2.15277pt]
\text{Law}(X_{0}^{n})=\lambda;\end{cases}\\[8.61108pt]
&\text{(iii) Solve the fixed-point problem: }\text{Law}(X_{t}^{n})=\mu_{t}^{n},\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T].\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

When there is no control
\alpha
and the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
do not depend on the measure flow
\mu
, it is well known that the penalized processes
X^{n}
in point (ii) of (
1.2
) converge in law to the weak solution
X
of the reflected SDE described in point 2 of (
1.1
). Under Lipschitz conditions on
b
and
\sigma
, Lions et al.
[
31
]
showed that

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{n}-X_{t}|^{2}\Big)\to 0.

Bahlali et al.
[
3
]
later proved that when
b
and
\sigma
are only continuous, the pair

\left(X^{n},\;K^{n}:=\int_{0}^{\cdot}n\big(X_{s}^{n}-\pi_{\bar{D}}(X_{s}^{n})\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

still converges in law to
(X,K)
with respect to the uniform topology.

For the controlled setting, without the interaction term
\mu
, the penalized boundary term
h
, and the terminal cost
g
, Menaldi
[
29
]
proved that

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{n}-X_{t}|^{p}\Big)\to 0,\qquad 1\leq p<\infty,

where
X^{n}
is the optimal state for the penalized control problem in point (ii) of (
1.2
) and
X
is the optimal state for the reflected problem in point 2 of (
1.1
). The convergence holds uniformly in the control
\alpha
. He also showed that the optimal cost for the penalized problem converges to that of the reflected control problem.

In our work, we consider a MFG involving both control and mean field interactions, where the dynamics are reflected on the boundary of a convex open subset
D
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We extend the penalization ideas from
[
31
,
29
]
to build a solution to (
1.1
). For each
n
, we use Lacker’s method
[
21
]
to prove the existence of an
MFG_{n}
solution
\mu^{n}
to the penalized problem (
1.2
). From these solutions, we construct a solution to the reflected problem (
1.1
) and show that
\mu^{n}\to\mu
, where
\mu
is an MFG solution of (
1.1
). The argument relies on tools such as tightness and convergence of the associated processes.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the notion of the canonical space and introduce relaxed controls for stochastic control problems governed by systems of reflected SDEs and their penalized counterparts. In the same section, we present the formulation of MFGs for both the reflected and penalized SDE frameworks, state the assumptions on the model coefficients, and establish our first main result (Theorem
2.2
).
Section
3
is devoted to the proof of this theorem.
Section
4
presents our second main result: the existence of Markovian and strict Markovian MFG solutions under a convexity assumption (Theorem
4.1
).
Finally, in Section
5
, we show that, in the general setting without the convexity assumption, a relaxed MFG solution with reflected dynamics can be approximated by strict controls with penalized SDE dynamics. This constitutes our third main result (Theorem
5.1
).

2
Assumptions and Existence of MFG Solutions

Throughout this paper, for any set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}^{d}(\bar{A})
the space of continuous functions from
[0,T]
to
\bar{A}
, and by
\mathcal{A}^{d}(\bar{A})\subset\mathcal{C}^{d}(\bar{A})
the subset of functions with bounded variation. Both spaces are endowed with the topology of uniform convergence. In addition, we write
\mathcal{C}^{d}:=\mathcal{C}^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let
(\mathcal{Z},\rho)
be a metric space. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})
the set of all probability measures on the measurable space
(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Z}))
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Z})
stands for the Borel
\sigma
-field of
\mathcal{Z}
. The space
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})
is equipped with the topology of weak convergence of measures.

For any
p\geq 1
, define

\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{Z}):=\left\{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z}):\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\,\mathbb{P}(dx)<\infty\text{ for some (hence all) }x_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}.

The space
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{Z})
is endowed with the
p
-Wasserstein metric

W_{\mathcal{Z},p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\left(\int_{\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}}\rho(x,y)^{p}\,\pi(dx,dy)\right)^{1/p},\quad\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{Z}),

where

\Pi(\mu,\nu):=\{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}):\pi\text{ has marginals }\mu\text{ and }\nu\}.

We denote by
\mathcal{U}
the set of all probability measures on
[0,T]\times U
whose first marginal coincides with the Lebesgue measure on
[0,T]
and whose second marginal is a probability measure on
U
. The space
\mathcal{U}
is equipped with a metric

d_{\mathcal{U}}(q_{1},q_{2}):=W_{[0,T]\times U,2}\left(\frac{q_{1}}{T},\frac{q_{2}}{T}\right),

where
W_{[0,T]\times U,2}
denotes the 2-Wasserstein distance.
Since
U
is compact under assumption
(A.6)
, it follows that the metric space
(\mathcal{U},d_{\mathcal{U}})
is compact and therefore complete and separable.

Let
(X,K)
be a solution of the reflected SDE

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X_{t}+\mathrm{d}K_{t}=B(t,X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+A(t,X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t,\\[11.00008pt]
\mathrm{Law}(X_{0})=\lambda,\quad X_{t}\in\bar{D},\ \forall\,t\geq 0,\\[3.99994pt]
|K|_{t}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{s}\in\partial D\}}\,\mathrm{d}|K|_{s}<\infty,\quad K_{t}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\eta(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}|K|_{s},\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\eta(x)
denotes the unit inward normal to
\partial D
at
x\in\partial D
, and
K
is a bounded variation process that ensures
X_{t}\in\bar{D}
.

For simplicity, we will often use the compact notation

\mathrm{d}X_{t}+\mathrm{d}K_{t}=B(t,X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+A(t,X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t,

with the reflection conditions on
K
implicitly understood.

We introduce the following notation for any set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

•

Canonical space:

\Omega[\bar{A}]:=\mathcal{C}^{d}(\bar{A})\times\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{A}^{d}(\bar{A}).

•

\sigma
-algebra:

\mathcal{F}[\bar{A}]:=\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{C}^{d}(\bar{A})}\times\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{U}}\times\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{C}^{d}(\bar{A})},

where
\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{C}^{d}(\bar{A})}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{U}}
denote the Borel
\sigma
-algebras of
\mathcal{C}^{d}(\bar{A})
and
\mathcal{U}
, respectively.

•

Coordinate projections
X
,
Q
, and
K
:
For each
\omega=(x,q,k)\in\Omega[\bar{A}]
,

X(\omega)=x,\quad Q(\omega)=q,\quad K(\omega)=k.

According to
[
21
, Lemma 3.2]
, the measure
Q
admits a predictable disintegration:

Q(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}u)=Q_{t}(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mathrm{d}t.

•

Filtration:

\mathbb{F}:=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
with

\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\mathcal{F}_{t}^{X}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Q}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{t}^{K},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}^{X}

\displaystyle=\sigma(X_{s}:s\leq t),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\sigma(Q(F):F\in\mathcal{B}([0,t]\times U)),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}^{K}

\displaystyle=\sigma(K_{s}:s\leq t).

Assumption
(A)
.

(A.1)

The functions
b,\sigma,f,
and
g
of
(t,x,\mu,u)
are measurable in
t
and continuous in
(x,\mu,u)
.

(A.2)

The function
h
is continuous in
(t,x,\mu)
.

(A.3)

There exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that, for all
(t,u)\in[0,T]\times U
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

|b(t,x,\mu,u)-b(t,y,\nu,u)|+|\sigma(t,x,\mu,u)-\sigma(t,y,\nu,u)|\leq C_{1}\big(|x-y|+W_{\mathbb{R}^{d},2}(\mu,\nu)\big),

|b(t,x,\mu,u)|\leq C_{2}\Big(1+|x|+\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\Big)^{1/2}\Big),

and

|\sigma\sigma^{\top}(t,x,\mu,u)|\leq C_{2}\Big(1+|x|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\Big).

Here,
\sigma^{\top}
denotes the transpose of

.

(A.4)

There exist strictly positive constants
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
such that, for all
(t,x,\mu,u)
,

|g(x,\mu)|\leq C_{4}\left(1+|x|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\right),

|h(t,x,\mu)|\leq C_{4}\left(1+|x|+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\right)^{1/2}\right),

and

|f(t,x,\mu,u)|\leq C_{4}\left(1+|x|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\right).

(A.5)

The initial law
\lambda
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{q^{\prime}}(\bar{D})
for some
q^{\prime}>2
.

(A.6)

The control space
U
is compact subset
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
.

(A.7)

The state space
D
is a convex open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
0\in D
.

Remark 2.1
.

For
\Psi\in\{\sigma\sigma^{\top},f\}
and corresponding constants
C^{\prime}\in\{C_{2},C_{4}\}
, the growth bounds in Assumptions
(A.3)
and
(A.4)
can be relaxed to allow an additional dependence on the control variable

as follows:

\displaystyle|\Psi(t,x,\mu,u)|\leq C^{\prime}\left(1+|x|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}z)+\phi(u)\right)

\displaystyle|b(t,x,\mu,u)|\leq C_{2}\left(1+|x|+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\right)^{1/2}+\phi(u)\right)

for some continuous function
(\phi^{1},\phi^{2},\phi^{3})\ni\phi:U\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
However, since
U
is compact by Assumption
(A.6)
, the function
\phi
is bounded on

. Consequently, the bounds in Assumptions
(A.3)
and
(A.4)
can be taken to hold uniformly in

.

2.1
Relaxed Formulation of the MFG with Penalized Dynamics

Following the relaxed control framework developed in
[
21
]
, we define, for each
n\geq 1
, the relaxed control associated with the penalized dynamics. The corresponding control problem for the MFG with penalized dynamics is given by

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\inf_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}f^{n}(t,X_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+g(X_{T}^{n},\mu_{T}^{n})\right),\\
\text{subject to:}\\
\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{n}=b^{n}(t,X_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t,X_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t},\\
\text{Law}(X_{0}^{n})=\lambda,\end{cases}

(2.2)

where the penalized coefficients are defined by

b^{n}(t,x,\mu,\alpha):=b(t,x,\mu,\alpha)-n(x-\pi_{\bar{D}}(x)),\quad f^{n}(t,x,\mu,\alpha):=f(t,x,\mu,\alpha)+nh(t,x,\mu)(x-\pi_{\bar{D}}(x)).

Remark 2.2
.

For each
n\geq 1
, the penalized drift
b^{n}
is Lipschitz continuous and satisfies the same growth condition as in Assumption
(A.3)
, with a constant
C^{1}_{n}
. Likewise, the penalized running cost
f^{n}
is continuous in
(x,\mu,u)
and satisfies the same condition as in Assumption
(A.4)
, with a constant
C^{2}_{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For each
n\geq 1
and
\mu^{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{C}^{d})
, we define the set of admissible relaxed controls:

\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mu^{n}):=\left\{P^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega[\mathbb{R}^{d}])\,:\,\text{conditions (i)\textendash(ii) hold}\right\},

where:

i)

P^{n}\circ X_{0}^{-1}=\lambda
;

ii)

For every
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the process
\mathcal{M}^{\mu,\phi,n}
defined by

\mathcal{M}^{\mu,\phi,n}_{t}:=\phi(X_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U}\mathcal{L}^{n}\phi(s,X_{s},\mu^{n}_{s},u)Q_{s}(\mathrm{d}u)\mathrm{d}s

is an
(\mathbb{F},P^{n})
-martingale, with the operator

\mathcal{L}^{n}\phi(t,x,\mu,u)=b^{n}(t,x,\mu,u)^{\top}D\phi(x)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma\sigma^{\top}(t,x,\mu,u)D^{2}\phi(x)\right).

The cost functional associated with
P^{n}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mu^{n})
is defined as

J^{n}(\mu^{n},P^{n}):=\mathbb{E}^{P^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}f^{n}(t,X_{t},\mu^{n}_{t},u)Q_{t}(\mathrm{d}u)\mathrm{d}t+g(X_{T},\mu^{n}_{T})\right).

We also define the (possibly empty) set of optimal admissible laws

\mathcal{R}_{n}^{*}(\mu^{n}):=\arg\min_{P^{n}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mu^{n})}J^{n}(\mu,P^{n}).

By Proposition 3.5 in
[
21
]
, for each
n\geq 1
and corresponding measure
\mu^{n}
,
the set
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mu^{n})
coincides with the collection of laws of weak solutions
to a SDE driven by martingale measures.
We adopt this representation throughout our framework.

Proposition 2.1
.

A probability measure
P^{n}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mu^{n})
if and only if there exists a filtered probability space
(\Omega^{n},\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t},\mathbb{Q}^{n})
supporting:

•

a
d
-dimensional
\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t}
-adapted process
X^{n}
,

•

a collection
M^{n}=(M^{n}_{1},\ldots,M^{n}_{m})
of
m
orthogonal martingale measures on
U\times[0,T]
, with intensity
Q^{n}_{t}(\mathrm{d}a)\,\mathrm{d}t
,

such that
\mathbb{Q}^{n}\circ(X^{n},Q^{n})^{-1}=P^{n}
, and the following state equation is satisfied:

\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{n}=\int_{U}b^{n}(t,X_{t}^{n},\mu^{n}_{t},u)\,Q_{t}^{n}(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{U}\sigma(t,X_{t}^{n},\mu^{n}_{t},u)\,M^{n}(\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}t).

Under standard Lipschitz and growth assumptions, this equation admits a unique strong solution on any such filtered probability space.

Definition 2.2
.

For each
n\geq 1
, a relaxed MFG solution is a probability measure
P^{n}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}^{*}(P^{n}\circ X^{-1})
.

If, in addition,
P^{n}
-almost surely

Q(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}u)=\delta_{u_{t}}(\mathrm{d}u)\mathrm{d}t

for some progressively measurable process
u
, then
P^{n}
is called a strict solution.

The following theorem is a direct consequence of Theorem 4.1 in
[
21
]
.
For each
n\geq 1
, the coefficients
(b^{n},\sigma,f^{n},g)
satisfy Assumption
(A)
of
[
21
]
(with
p=p_{\sigma}=2
and
p^{\prime}=q^{\prime}
), as ensured by Remark
2.2
together with Assumption
(A)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For each
n\geq 1
, there exists a probability measure
P^{n}
satisfying

P^{n}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}^{*}(\mu^{n}),\qquad\mu^{n}=P^{n}\circ X^{-1}.

2.2
Relaxed MFGs with Reflected Dynamics

We introduce the concept of relaxed controls, following the framework used for controlled SDEs without reflection. To formulate the martingale problem for our controlled system, we adopt the method of Ikeda and Watanabe
[
15
]
. The existence of optimal relaxed controls for reflected SDEs has been established in
[
19
,
20
]
using the compactification method. We employ a similar approach to address MFGs with reflected dynamics. The notion of relaxed controls used here is based on
[
19
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mu
be a given probability measure in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{C}^{d}(\bar{D}))
.
We denote by
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{ref}(D)}(\mu)
the set of all probability measures

P\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega[\bar{D}])
satisfying the following conditions:

1)

P\circ X_{0}^{-1}=\lambda
.

2)

For each
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the process

\mathcal{M}^{\mu,\phi}
defined by

\mathcal{M}^{\mu,\phi}_{t}:=\phi(X_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U}\mathcal{L}\phi(s,X_{s},\mu_{s},u)\,Q_{s}(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}D\phi(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}K_{s}

(2.3)

is an
(\mathbb{F},P)
-martingale, where the operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}\phi(t,x,\mu,u):=b(t,x,\mu,u)^{\top}D\phi(x)+\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\sigma\sigma^{\top}(t,x,\mu,u)\,D^{2}\phi(x)\right).

3)

P
-a.s., for every
t\in[0,T]
,

|K|_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{s}\in\partial D\}}\,\mathrm{d}|K|_{s}<\infty,\qquad K_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\eta(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}|K|_{s}.

Remark 2.3
.

Under Assumption
(A.3)
, the set
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{ref}(D)}(\mu)
is nonempty for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{C}^{d}(\bar{D}))
.
Indeed, fixing a constant control
\alpha_{0}
, it follows from
[
34
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
36
, Theorem 4.1]
) that there exists a probability measure

P\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{ref}(D)}(\mu)
such that

P\left(Q_{t}=\delta_{\alpha_{0}}\text{ for almost every }t\in[0,T]\right)=1.

The cost functional associated with an admissible law
P\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{ref}(D)}(\mu)
is defined by

J(\mu,P):=\mathbb{E}^{P}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}f(t,X_{t},\mu_{t},u)\,Q_{t}(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}h(t,X_{t},\mu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}K_{t}+g(X_{T},\mu_{T})\right),

We then define the (possibly empty) set of optimal admissible laws by

The following proposition provides a characterization of the set

in terms of reflected SDEs driven by martingale measures. Since each element of

Proposition 2.2

Let

and the reflected dynamics satisfy

\mathrm{d}X^{\prime}_{t}+\mathrm{d}K^{\prime}_{t}=\displaystyle\int_{U}b(t,X^{\prime}_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t},u)\,Q^{\prime}_{t}(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mathrm{d}t+\displaystyle\int_{U}\sigma(t,X^{\prime}_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t},u)\,M^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}t).

The following provides the formal definition of a MFG solution with reflected dynamics.

Definition 2.4
.

A relaxed MFG solution is a probability measure

P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega[\bar{D}])

such that

P\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{ref}(D)}^{*}\bigl(P\circ X^{-1}\bigr),

that is,
P
is a fixed point of the correspondence

we also refer to the induced flow of measures
\mu=P\circ X^{-1}
as a relaxed MFG solution.

A
strict MFG solution
is a relaxed solution
P
for which there exists a progressively measurable control process

such that

P\bigl(Q(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}u)=\delta_{u_{t}}(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mathrm{d}t\bigr)=1.

A relaxed solution
P
is called

if its control measure
Q
satisfies

P\bigl(Q(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}u)=\mathrm{d}t\,q(t,X_{t})(\mathrm{d}u)\bigr)=1

for some measurable mapping

.

It is called
strict Markovian
if there exists a measurable feedback control

\alpha:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to U
such that

P\bigl(Q(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}u)=\mathrm{d}t\,\delta_{\alpha(t,X_{t})}(\mathrm{d}u)\bigr)=1.

Proof.

Applying Itô’s formula to

\displaystyle|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}+2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle X^{n}_{s},\mathrm{d}K^{n}_{s}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\left(|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle X^{n}_{s},\mathrm{d}K^{n}_{s}\rangle\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(|X_{0}|^{2q}+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U}\langle X^{n}_{s},b^{n}(s,X^{n}_{s},\mu^{n}_{s},u)\rangle Q^{n}_{s}(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U}\big\langle X^{n}_{s},\sigma(s,X^{n}_{s},\mu^{n}_{s},u)\big\rangle M^{n}(\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}s)\right|^{q}

4
Existence of Markovian and Strict Markovian MFG Solutions

In this section, we establish the existence of a strict Markovian MFG solution with reflected dynamics. Throughout this section, we maintain Assumption
(A)
, which guarantees the existence of a relaxed MFG solution by Theorem
2.2
.

To construct a Markovian relaxed MFG solution, we employ a penalization approach. Specifically, we first introduce a penalized reflected SDE , and then apply a mimicking theorem to the penalized dynamics in order to obtain a relaxed Markovian MFG formulation. Subsequently, by letting the penalization parameter tend to infinity, we recover a relaxed Markovian MFG corresponding to the original reflected system. Finally, a measurable selection argument allows us to derive the existence of a strict-Markovian MFG solution.

Applying the mimicking theorem to the penalized system yields a model whose coefficients are only measurable, due to the regularity of the mapping
(t,x)\mapsto q(t,x)
. This limited smoothness necessitates additional analytical arguments. To address these difficulties, we draw on methods developed for penalization schemes in reflected SDEs with merely measurable coefficients (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
35
]
). In order to ensure the convergence of the penalization procedure, we introduce the following structural assumption.

Assumption
(M)
.

The diffusion coefficient
\sigma
is uniformly elliptic: there exists a constant
\beta>0
such that

\sigma(t,x,\mu,u)\sigma(t,x,\mu,u)^{\top}\geq\beta I_{d},\quad\forall(t,x,\mu,u)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times U.

Assumption

.

For all
(t,x,\mu)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

5
Approximation of Relaxed MFGs with Reflected Dynamics by Strict Controls with Penalized Dynamics

In this section, we show that relaxed MFG solutions with reflection, without assuming Assumption
(C)
, can be approximated by strict controls whose dynamics are governed by penalized SDEs.
The analysis relies on two main ingredients: the chattering lemma, which links relaxed and strict controls, and a penalization method that replaces reflected SDEs with standard SDEs augmented by a penalization term enforcing the boundary constraint.

Let’s begin with the following lemma, which includes two results: the first is the chattering lemma, and the second is based on the stability theorem for martingale measures. Both results are proved in
[
28
, p. 196]

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a relaxed control,

a martingale measure on

a Brownian motion. All processes are defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathbb{F}^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
.

i)

There exists a sequence of
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}
-adapted processes
\alpha^{n}
taking values in
U
, such that the sequence of random measures
\delta_{\alpha^{n}_{t}}(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mathrm{d}t
converges to
q_{t}(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mathrm{d}t
in
\mathcal{U}

\mathbb{P}^{\prime}

ii)

For any continuous and bounded function

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{T}\phi(s,\alpha^{n}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}\phi(s,u)\,M(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u)\right|\right)=0.
