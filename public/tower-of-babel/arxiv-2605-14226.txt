Kodaira-Neron statistics for rational elliptic curves with j j -invariant 0 and 1728
John Cullinan, Sebastian Sargenti

Abstract
. Elliptic curves over 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} with j j -invariant 0 or 1728 have additive reduction at all primes of bad reduction. In addition, all elliptic curves with j j -invariant 0 have bad reduction at p = 3 p=3 and all elliptic curves with j j -invariant 1728 have bad reduction at p = 2 p=2 . In this paper we count elliptic curves with j j -invariant 0 and 1728 by height and determine asymptotics for the various Kodaira-Néron types at 3 and 2, respectively. We also give related statistics by holding the torsion subgoup and isogeny-torsion graph constant.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
. If
p

Kodaira-Néron types have been studied for certain families of rational elliptic curves. For example, fix a nontrivial torsion group
T
and write
E_{T}
for a parameterized family of minimal integral models of elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
with torsion subgroup

[
1
]
the authors determined, for each prime

of bad reduction, and for each

family. As an illustration of their results, we present the following example.

Example 1.1
.

Let

. By
[
1
, Prop. 2.4, Lem. 2.5]
every minimal elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
with a 5-torsion point is a specialization of the model

where
a
and

are coprime integers with

positive. If

has multiplicative reduction at

with

given explicitly in
[
2
, Thm. 2]
. By
[

, Thm. 3]

has additive reduction at

(the converse is not necessarily true). If

is additive at 5 then

\III&\text{ if $v_{5}(a+18b)>1.$}\end{cases}

The main question that we address in this paper is

each Kodaira-Néron type occurs within specified families of elliptic curves. In order to make interesting comparisons, we choose families such that every member has a common prime of bad reduction. In this paper we study elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}

-invariant 0 and

-invariant 1728, which always have additive reduction at 3 and 2, respectively, and determine the exact proportion with which each Kodaira-Néron type occurs.

Organization of the Paper

2.

2.1.

Every elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}

is nontrivial, then the Kodaira-Néron types have been determined by the work in
[
1
]

or

]

3.
The Proportion of Some Sets of Integers

In this section we will determine the proportions of certain sets of integers with a view toward counting elliptic curves with specified Kodaira-Néron symbol. Recall that the proportion of
k
th-power-free integers has proportion
1/\zeta(k)
among all integers and

\#\{1\leq n\leq X\mid n\text{ is $k$th-power-free}\}=\frac{x}{\zeta(k)}+O(X^{1/k}).

We record the following lemma here for ease of reference later in the paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

The proportion of
k
th-power-free positive integers
n
such that:

(1)

k=2
and
n
is odd is
(2/3)\cdot(1/\zeta(2))
;

(2)

k=2
and
n
is coprime to 3, is
(3/4)\cdot(1/\zeta(2))
;

(3)

k=3
and
n
is odd is
(4/7)\cdot(1/\zeta(3))
;

(4)

k=3
and
v_{2}(n)=1
is
(2/7)\cdot(1/\zeta(3))
;

(5)

k=4
and
n
is odd is
(8/15)\cdot(1/\zeta(4))
;

These proportions are easily derived from the main theorem of
[
3
]
. However, we do not need the full strength of that result for Lemma
3.1
. We need only augment certain local Euler factors of
\zeta(k)
. For example, the proportion of Lemma
3.1
.
1
is given by

\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\cdot\frac{\left(1-1/4\right)^{-1}}{\left(1-1/2\right)^{-1}}=\frac{2}{3\zeta(2)}

since we remove the condition that

be squarefree at 2 and replace it with the condition that
