Backward Coherence and Hidden-State Stability in Recurrent Neural Networks: A Quasi-Reverse-Martingale Theory
Yuan-chin Ivan Chang

Abstract
Recurrent neural networks maintain a hidden state h t h_{t} whose probabilistic meaning has remained largely uncharacterised. We develop a theory of hidden-state stability via backward coherence : the degree to which h t h_{t} can be recovered from its successor h t + 1 h_{t+1} through a learned backward projector g ϕ g_{\phi} . Under a contraction condition and summable backward drift, the hidden-state sequence is shown to be a quasi-reverse-martingale , implying almost-sure convergence with a rate under mixing, an interpretable limit representation, and pathwise stopping rules with finite stopping times. A framework for time-uniform confidence sequences is also established theoretically. Simulation experiments confirm the core predictions: backward-coherence regularisation reduces the empirical quasi-martingale total Q ^ \hat{Q} by 43 43 – 58 % 58\% , reaches the stability threshold 28 28 – 44 % 44\% earlier than the unregularised baseline, and produces tracking-error recovery consistent with the geometric bounds. Two further experiments probe additional predictions: a proxy-free echo-state forgetting test confirms empirical rates are bounded above by ρ \rho , and a direct computation of the increment-sum tube R t R_{t} confirms the pathwise enclosure of Proposition 3.18 (ii) at 100 % 100\% simultaneous coverage; R t R_{t} is valid but loose in practice (median inflation 17 17 – 30 30 ), making the defect-tail proxy Q ^ t \hat{Q}_{t} the practically useful monitoring instrument. Scope and limitations are discussed in Section 5. These guarantees apply when the stated conditions hold; universality is not claimed. Minimising the backward-coherence loss is equivalent to minimising a Kullback–Leibler divergence in a Gaussian backward model, connecting to variational inference. Extensions cover ϕ \phi -mixing inputs, geometric tracking-error bounds at change points, and finite-sample concentration inequalities. Three real-data studies validate the theory: on the PhysioNet 2012 ICU Challenge, the Reverse Martingale RNN (RMRNN) matches RNN mortality-prediction AUC whilst reaching a stable representation 13 hours earlier in a 48-hour stay; on FRED-MD macroeconomic data, it reduces one-month-ahead forecast error approximately fourfold relative to the unregularised RNN under concept drift; and on UCI Human Activity Recognition, post-transition tracking error decays geometrically and RMRNN maintains a consistently lower tracking error than the unregularised baseline, consistent with the theoretical bound.

Introduction

Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are the canonical tool for sequential
learning. Given an input sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, an RNN
maintains a hidden state
h_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
updated by
h_{t}=f_{\theta}(h_{t-1},x_{t})
, accumulating information about the past and providing a running
representation used for prediction or control. Gated variants—the Long
Short-Term Memory
(
Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997
)
and the Gated Recurrent Unit

(
Cho et al. 2014
)
—use multiplicative gates to selectively retain or discard
information, enabling practical learning over long sequences. Despite
widespread empirical success, a fundamental probabilistic question has
remained unanswered:
does the hidden state
h_{t}
converge, and if so,
to what?
Without an answer, the practitioner cannot know when to trust the
network’s representation, how to set stopping rules, or what statistical
meaning to attach to the accumulated summary. The question arises in
clinical sequential prediction, where
h_{t}
encodes a patient’s evolving
physiological profile and an early stable representation enables earlier
clinical decisions
(
Rajpurkar et al. 2022
;
Reyna et al. 2020
;
Wiens et al. 2019
)
; in
macroeconomic forecasting, where structural breaks and slow concept drift
require the hidden state to track a shifting target distribution; and in
activity and regime recognition, where abrupt discrete switches demand
fast recovery of the hidden state to its new steady-state mean.

A complete theory of hidden-state dynamics must simultaneously guarantee
almost-sure pathwise convergence of
h_{t}
, provide a statistically
interpretable limit identifiable as a conditional expectation, automatically
control the first moment without auxiliary assumptions, and yield anytime-valid
sequential confidence sets without a pre-specified sample size. Standard
analytical tools each address part of this problem but leave at least one
requirement unmet. Lyapunov stability
(
Khalil 2002
)
certifies
boundedness of the deterministic map but provides no statistical
interpretation of the limit. Ergodic theory and Markov chain methods

(
Meyn and Tweedie 2009
)
guarantee convergence of time-averages but not of individual
sample paths, and do not identify the limit as a conditional expectation.
Forward martingale convergence requires uniform integrability for both
L^{1}

convergence and conditional-expectation identification of the limit, a
condition that cannot in general be verified without prior knowledge of the
limit itself. Bayesian state-space models
(
Särkkä 2013
;
Shumway and Stoffer 2000
)

satisfy all four requirements but only under correct parametric
specification—a strong and often unverifiable assumption for modern deep
networks.

We argue that
reverse martingales

(
Doob 1953
;
Neveu 1975
)
provide
the correct framework. A reverse martingale is adapted to a

decreasing
filtration
\mathcal{F}_{1}\supseteq\mathcal{F}_{2}\supseteq\cdots
and
satisfies
\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{n}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\right]=M_{n+1}
almost surely. As the
conditioning set contracts, the sequence converges almost surely and in
L^{1}

to a limit identified as a conditional expectation by Lévy’s backward
theorem—with no uniform integrability assumption required. Rather than
imposing a Gaussian or linear model on backward dynamics, as in the Kalman
smoother
(
Särkkä 2013
)
or Bayesian state-space methods

(
Shumway and Stoffer 2000
)
, we train a backward projector
g_{\phi}
as an empirical
risk minimiser for the conditional mean
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
. The
resulting loss
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
is a distribution-free quasi-likelihood objective that
requires only first-moment structure of the backward dynamics, replacing
brittle parametric assumptions with an approach valid under model
misspecification.

The paper makes five contributions. We first establish that the
running-average linear RNN on i.i.d. inputs is an exact reverse martingale,
so its almost-sure convergence to the population mean is the Strong Law of
Large Numbers in disguise (Proposition
2.5
). For general
nonlinear RNNs, we prove that the drift-summability condition

D_{\infty}+\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t}<\infty
, where

D_{\infty}=\sum_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})-h_{t+1}\right\|]

measures the observable reverse-martingale drift, implies almost-sure
convergence of
h_{t}
,
L^{1}
convergence under an additional
uniform-integrability condition, and an explicit
O(\alpha^{t})
geometric
convergence rate under exponential
\phi
-mixing, with an interpretable
conditional-expectation representation of the limit in the exact
reverse-martingale case
(Theorem
3.2
, Corollary
3.4
).
We extend the theory to dependent inputs via a

\phi
-mixing conditional-mean bound and to piecewise-stationary inputs via
geometric tracking-error bounds at change points
(Propositions
3.12
–
3.16
). We show that
minimising
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
is equivalent to minimising the Kullback–Leibler
divergence in a Gaussian backward model, connecting the framework to
variational inference (Proposition
3.17
). Finally, a
McDiarmid-type concentration inequality
(
McDiarmid 1989
)
quantifies finite-sample stability
of
h_{T}
, and the quasi-martingale structure yields pathwise confidence tubes
that become time-uniform confidence sequences after an observable calibration
(Propositions
3.19
–
3.20
).

Section
2
develops the reverse-martingale framework,
introduces RNN backward coherence, and analyses the linear case.
Section
3
develops the convergence theory, extensions to
dependent and non-stationary inputs, and statistical inference.
Section
4
reports numerical validation on synthetic
benchmarks and three real-data domains. Section
5

discusses connections to related work and open problems. All proofs are
in the Supplementary Material.

2
Reverse Martingales and RNN Backward Coherence

2.1
Reverse martingale theory

Definition 2.1

(Reverse martingale)
.

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a
decreasing
filtration,

\mathcal{F}_{1}\supseteq\mathcal{F}_{2}\supseteq\cdots
, on a probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
. A real-valued sequence
(M_{n})_{n\geq 1}
with

M_{n}\in L^{1}
is a
reverse martingale
with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{n})
if

M_{n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-measurable and

\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{n}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\right]=M_{n+1}\quad\text{a.s.\ for all }n\geq 1.

(1)

The canonical construction: fix
Z\in L^{1}
and set

M_{n}:=\mathbb{E}\!\left[Z\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]
. The tower property immediately yields (
1
) since

\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{n}
. Every reverse martingale arises this way.

Proposition 2.1

(Canonical form)
.

If
(M_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a reverse martingale, then

M_{n}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]
almost surely for every
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Induction:
M_{n+1}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{n}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\right]
by the tower law.
∎

Proposition
2.1
has an important interpretation: every
reverse martingale is a
progressive reduction of uncertainty about a
latent quantity
. As
n
increases,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
contracts, and
M_{n}
becomes a
more constrained conditional expectation of
M_{1}
—precisely the structure
a well-designed RNN hidden state should exhibit.

Theorem 2.2

(Doob’s reverse martingale convergence theorem)
.

Let
(M_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a reverse martingale with

\mathbb{E}[\left|M_{1}\right|]<\infty
and

\mathcal{F}_{\infty}:=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{F}_{n}

(the tail
\sigma
-algebra of the reverse filtration

(
Neveu 1975
;
Williams 1991
)
).
Then:

(i)

\sup_{n\geq 1}\,\mathbb{E}[\left|M_{n}\right|]\leq\mathbb{E}[\left|M_{1}\right|]<\infty
.

(ii)

M_{n}\to M_{\infty}:=\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\right]
almost surely
and in
L^{1}
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material, §A.1.
∎

In contrast to forward martingales, where
L^{1}
convergence requires
uniform integrability as an additional hypothesis, for a reverse martingale

L^{1}
control is automatic from part (i) and the limit is identified
as
\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\right]
by Proposition
2.1

without further conditions.

The Strong Law of Large Numbers is, at root, a theorem about reverse
martingales.

Proposition 2.3

(Strong Law via reverse martingales)
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{iid}}}{{\sim}}F
with
\mu=\mathbb{E}[x_{1}]
and

\mathbb{E}[\left|x_{1}\right|]<\infty
. Set
\bar{x}_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
and

\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(S_{n},\,x_{n+1},x_{n+2},\ldots)
, where
S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
. Then
(\bar{x}_{n},\,\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a reverse
martingale and
\bar{x}_{n}\to\mu
almost surely and in
L^{1}
.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material. The reverse martingale property follows
from the symmetry of i.i.d. summands given their sum; convergence is
Theorem
2.2
with
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
trivial by the Hewitt–Savage
zero-one law
(
Durrett 2019
)
.
∎

The tail sigma-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}=\bigcap_{n}\mathcal{F}_{n}
encodes the irreducible
long-run uncertainty that cannot be removed by finite conditioning;
Lévy’s backward theorem
(
Neveu 1975
;
Williams 1991
)
identifies

M_{\infty}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\right]
as a constant when
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
is
trivial (as in the i.i.d. case).

2.2
RNN architectures and backward coherence

Three canonical RNN architectures are considered. The
Elman RNN

(
Elman 1990
)
:

h_{t}=\sigma_{h}(W_{h}\,h_{t-1}+W_{x}\,x_{t}+b),

(2)

where
W_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
,
W_{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
and
\sigma_{h}
is an elementwise nonlinearity. The
LSTM

(
Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997
)
:

c_{t}=f_{t}\odot c_{t-1}+i_{t}\odot\tilde{c}_{t},\quad h_{t}=o_{t}\odot\tanh(c_{t}),

(3)

with effective forgetting factor
\rho^{*}:=\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|f_{t}\right\|_{\infty}]
.
The
GRU

(
Cho et al. 2014
)
:

h_{t}=(1-z_{t})\odot h_{t-1}+z_{t}\odot\tilde{h}_{t},

(4)

with contraction factor
\rho^{*}=\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|1-z_{t}\right\|_{\infty}]
.

Define the
backward filtration
:

\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}}:=\sigma(h_{t},\,h_{t+1},\,\ldots,\,h_{T}).

(5)

The filtration is decreasing in
t
by simple generator inclusion:

\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}}
is generated by
\{h_{t},h_{t+1},\ldots,h_{T}\}
,
a strict superset of the generators of
\mathcal{F}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}
,
so
\mathcal{F}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}}
:

\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{T-1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}
.
The backward filtration is therefore
decreasing
in
t
, in exact
correspondence with Definition
2.1
.
For the infinite-horizon theory (convergence as
T\to\infty
), we extend
the definition to
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}}:=\sigma(h_{s}:s\geq t)
, the
sigma-algebra generated by all hidden states from time
t
onward; the
decreasing property
\mathcal{F}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}}

continues to hold by the same generator-inclusion argument.
The tail sigma-algebra

\mathcal{F}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{bwd}}=\bigcap_{t}\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}}

(the tail
\sigma
-algebra of the backward reverse filtration,
in exact analogy with
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
in Theorem
2.2
)
encodes the irreducible long-run uncertainty in the hidden-state sequence.

We formalise
backward coherence
as the property that

h_{t}\approx\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
for all
t
. In a perfect reverse
martingale this holds with equality; for a general nonlinear RNN it holds
approximately, with the discrepancy measured by the defect

\delta_{t}:=h_{t}-\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
.

Since
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
is not directly accessible, we approximate
it with a
backward projector

g_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
trained
jointly with the forward RNN. We use a residual architecture,

g_{\phi}(h)=h+W_{2}\,\mathrm{ReLU}(W_{1}\,h+b_{1})+b_{2},

(6)

zero-initialised so that
g_{\phi}=\mathrm{Id}
at the start of training.
The
reverse martingale regularisation loss
is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}(\theta,\phi):=\frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\left\|h_{t}-g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})\right\|^{2}.

(7)

The total training objective is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
, with
\lambda>0
a regularisation weight. Minimising
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}

drives the hidden states towards a quasi-reverse-martingale.

Remark 2.4

(Relation to bidirectional RNNs)
.

Bidirectional RNNs
(
Schuster and Paliwal 1997
)
process the sequence in both
directions but do not impose the probabilistic backward model that is
central here. The backward projector
g_{\phi}
is a discriminatively
trained approximation to the conditional backward mean

\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
, analogous to the Kalman smoother backward pass
(
Särkkä 2013
)

but without requiring a parametric distributional assumption.

2.3
The linear case and counterexample

We begin with the simplest nontrivial RNN: a linear running average with
decaying learning rate
\alpha_{t}=1/t
,

h_{t}=\Bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{t}\Bigr)h_{t-1}+\tfrac{1}{t}\,x_{t},\quad h_{0}=0,\quad t=1,2,\ldots,

(8)

with
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{iid}}}{{\sim}}F
,
\mu=\mathbb{E}[x_{1}]
,
\mathbb{E}[\left|x_{1}\right|]<\infty
.

Proposition 2.5

(Linear RNN as an exact reverse martingale)
.

Under (
8
):

(i)

h_{t}=\bar{x}_{t}
(the running sample mean) for all
t\geq 1
.

(ii)

(h_{t},\,\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}})_{t\geq 1}
is a reverse martingale.

(iii)

h_{t}\to\mu
almost surely and in
L^{1}
.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material.
∎

Proposition
2.5
reveals the Strong Law as a special case
of Theorem
2.2
. The backward conditional mean is exact:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]=h_{t+1}
(since
h_{t+1}=\bar{x}_{t+1}

is a sufficient statistic for
\mu
and
h_{t}=\bar{x}_{t}
is a coarser
summary of the same data). Consequently the reverse-martingale drift is
identically zero:
\rho_{t}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]-h_{t+1}=0

and
D=0
, so the sequence is an exact reverse martingale. Note that
the backward prediction residual
\delta_{t}=h_{t}-g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})=\bar{x}_{t}-\bar{x}_{t+1}\neq 0
in general; the exact reverse-martingale
property is characterised by zero drift
D=0
, not by zero prediction
residual.

The condition
\alpha_{t}=1/t
is essential. A constant learning rate

\alpha\in(0,1)
yields an exponentially weighted moving average that
converges in distribution but is not a reverse martingale: the backward
property
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]=h_{t+1}
fails because
h_{t+1}
does
not contain sufficient information to recover
h_{t}
, and almost-sure
convergence therefore fails. By contrast, if
h_{t}=\tanh(W_{h}h_{t-1}+W_{x}x_{t})
with
\left\|W_{h}\right\|>1
, the spectral radius violates
Assumption
3.1
below; the hidden state can exhibit
exponentially growing oscillations
(
Bengio et al. 1994
;
Pascanu et al. 2013
)
, and in
this regime
D=\infty
almost surely, so neither almost-sure convergence
nor
L^{1}
control obtains.

3
Convergence Theory and Statistical Inference

3.1
Quasi-reverse-martingale convergence

Definition 3.1

(Backward prediction residual, reverse-martingale drift, and diagnostics)
.

The learned one-step
backward prediction residual
is

\delta_{t}:=h_{t}-g_{\phi}(h_{t+1}).

(9)

The
observable reverse-martingale drift
—the empirical proxy for
the quasi-martingale defect
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]-h_{t+1}
—is

r_{t}^{\phi}:=g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})-h_{t+1}.

(10)

For a finite observed sequence of length
T
, the
empirical
backward-prediction total
and
empirical drift total
are

Q_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|],\qquad D_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|].

(11)

For infinite sequences:
Q_{\infty}:=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|]

and
D_{\infty}:=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|]
, each finite
whenever summable.

Three quantities play distinct roles. The backward prediction residual

\delta_{t}
is
observable
and enters the training loss
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
;

Q_{T}
measures accumulated prediction error and serves as a practical
stability diagnostic over the observed horizon. The reverse-martingale
drift
r_{t}^{\phi}=g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})-h_{t+1}
is also observable: it
measures how far
g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})
departs from
h_{t+1}
, which is
precisely the quasi-martingale defect that must be small for
h_{t+1}
to
function as the backward conditional mean of
h_{t}
. The drift total

D_{T}
is the observable proxy for the true quasi-martingale defect

\sum_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\mathbb{E}[h_{t}\mid h_{t+1}]-h_{t+1}\right\|]
and is the quantity
that appears in the convergence theorem. The ideal quasi-martingale
drift
\rho_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]-h_{t+1}

(the population counterpart of
r_{t}^{\phi}
, introduced in the theorem
below) is a
population quantity
unobservable without knowledge of
the data-generating process; Assumption
3.4
bounds the gap

\mathbb{E}[\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}-\rho_{t}\right\|]\leq\varepsilon_{t}
through the approximation
error. Minimising
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
drives
\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|
downward, which
partially controls
\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|
through the triangle inequality

\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|\leq\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|+\left\|h_{t}-h_{t+1}\right\|
.
The second term
\left\|h_{t}-h_{t+1}\right\|
is not controlled by
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}

alone; hence
D_{\infty}=\sum_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|]
must be verified
directly from the observable sequence
(r_{t}^{\phi})
rather than inferred
solely from the training loss. In practice the empirical total

\hat{D}_{T}=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|
serves as the primary
stability diagnostic: a non-stabilising or growing
\hat{D}_{T}
signals
that the summability condition of Theorem
3.2
(iv) may fail,
even when
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
has converged.

Assumption 3.1

(Contraction)
.

The recurrent weight matrix satisfies
\left\|W_{h}\right\|\leq\rho<1
in operator
norm. For the Elman architecture (
2
) with a
1
-Lipschitz
activation (e.g., tanh), this implies that
h\mapsto f_{\theta}(h,x)
is
a
\rho
-contraction in
h
for each fixed
x
. For general
f_{\theta}
,
we assume this Lipschitz property holds directly:

\left\|f_{\theta}(h,x)-f_{\theta}(h^{\prime},x)\right\|\leq\rho\left\|h-h^{\prime}\right\|
for all
h,h^{\prime},x
.

Assumption 3.2

(Finite first moment)
.

The inputs satisfy
\mathbb{E}[\left\|x_{1}\right\|]<\infty
.

Assumption 3.3

(Backward Markov sufficiency)
.

\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]

a.s. for all
t
. Equivalently,
h_{t+1}
is sufficient for the
backward conditional mean.

Assumption 3.4

(Backward approximation)
.

Define the backward-projector error

\varepsilon_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl\|g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})-\mathbb{E}[h_{t}\mid h_{t+1}]\bigr\|\right]
.
For finite-horizon diagnostics:
\sup_{1\leq t<T}\varepsilon_{t}\leq\varepsilon<\infty
.
For infinite-horizon convergence:
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{t}<\infty
.

Remark 3.1

(On the assumptions)
.

Assumption
3.1
is the echo-state stability condition

(
Jaeger 2001
)
, achievable by spectral normalisation
(
Miyato et al. 2018
)

or weight clipping. In gated architectures the effective contraction factor
varies with input and gate state; the empirical total
\hat{Q}_{T}
remains a
valid finite-horizon diagnostic of backward incoherence even when the global
spectral bound cannot be directly verified.

Assumption
3.3
is a modelling condition, not a consequence
of contraction. Jointly minimising
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
alongside the task loss actively
incentivises
h_{t+1}
to be a sufficient statistic for the backward prediction
of
h_{t}
, providing the mechanism through which the condition is approximately
enforced in practice. It holds exactly for linear dynamical systems
(
h_{t}=Ah_{t-1}+Bx_{t}+\epsilon_{t}
with
\epsilon_{t}
independent of

\{h_{s},x_{s}\}_{s\neq t}
, i.e. the standard state-space Markov property,
which implies backward sufficiency independently of whether
A
is invertible)
and coordinatewise for AR(1) dynamics; for general nonlinear RNNs it is an approximation that
improves as
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}\to 0
. It can be verified empirically by comparing the
one-step error of
g_{\phi}
against a post-hoc full-horizon LSTM smoother
on frozen hidden states: in all three real-data experiments in
Section
4.2
,
g_{\phi}
achieves equal or lower backward prediction
error than the LSTM smoother, confirming that
h_{t+1}
is an adequate
sufficient statistic.

Assumption
3.4
separates finite training error from the summable
error required by the infinite-horizon theorem. The summability

\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t}<\infty
holds when the projector error decays geometrically
(stationary training distribution) or when inputs are exponentially

\phi
-mixing
(
Bradley 2007
)
—the class that covers stationary ARMA
processes, geometrically ergodic Markov chains, and the three application
domains studied here. Violation (persistent non-stationary drift with no
recurrent pattern) is detectable via an unbounded or non-stabilising
\hat{Q}_{T}
.

Define the ideal quasi-martingale drift

\rho_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\right]-h_{t+1}

(the population analogue of
r_{t}^{\phi}
; this is zero for an exact reverse
martingale). Under Assumption
3.3
,
h_{t+1}
is a
sufficient statistic for
\mathcal{F}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}
, so

\mathbb{E}[h_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}]=\mathbb{E}[h_{t}\mid h_{t+1}]
and the
two formulations of
\rho_{t}
in Definition
3.1
and above
coincide. Hence

\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|]\leq\mathbb{E}[\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|]+\varepsilon_{t}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Quasi-reverse-martingale convergence)
.

Assumptions
3.1
–
3.4
are standing
conditions throughout. Part (i) and part (v) hold under these assumptions
alone. Parts (ii)–(iv) additionally require summability of the
quasi-martingale drift
\sum_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|]<\infty
; the observable
sufficient condition in part (iv) implies this summability but is not itself
one of Assumptions
3.1
–
3.4
.
Under these conditions:

(i)

Moment control.

\sup_{t\geq 1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|h_{t}\right\|]\leq C/(1-\rho)<\infty
,
where
C=\left\|W_{x}\right\|\mathbb{E}[\left\|x_{1}\right\|]+\left\|b\right\|
.

(ii)

Almost-sure convergence.
If

\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|]<\infty
,
then
h_{t}\to h_{\infty}
almost surely.

(iii)

L^{1}
convergence.
If additionally
\{h_{t}\}
is uniformly
integrable—in particular, if
\left\|h_{t}\right\|\leq H
a.s. (bounded
activations such as
\tanh
) or
\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|h_{t}\right\|^{1+\eta}]<\infty

for some
\eta>0
—then
h_{t}\to h_{\infty}
in
L^{1}
as well.

(iv)

Observable sufficient condition.

D_{\infty}+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{t}<\infty
implies (ii) and,
under the uniform integrability condition of (iii), also (iii).

(v)

Finite-horizon approximation.

\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|]\leq D_{T}+(T-1)\varepsilon<\infty
,
justifying finite-horizon diagnostics.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material. The five steps are: (1) contraction gives
uniform
L^{1}
control; (2) Assumption
3.3
and the triangle
inequality give
\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|]\leq\mathbb{E}[\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|]+\varepsilon_{t}
,
so
D_{\infty}+\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t}<\infty
implies

\sum_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|]<\infty
; (3) this summability of the
quasi-martingale drift
\rho_{t}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]-h_{t+1}

verifies the quasi-martingale criterion for the reverse filtration;
the reverse-filtration analogue of Rao’s criterion is covered by

Neveu 1975
§ V-3 (reverse sub/supermartingales under a decreasing
filtration) combined with the directed-index-set framework of

Krickeberg 1956
, which encompasses decreasing filtrations as a special
case of his general directed-set theory (see Supplementary Material § A.3
for the explicit adaptation);
(4) coordinatewise application of this reverse quasi-martingale convergence,
using the Krickeberg decomposition into non-negative reverse supermartingales

(
Krickeberg 1956
;
Neveu 1975
)
, establishes pathwise almost-sure convergence
of each coordinate
h_{t}^{(j)}\to h_{\infty}^{(j)}
,
j=1,\ldots,p
,
without requiring a uniform integrability assumption on the raw innovations;
for fixed finite
p
, joint almost-sure convergence
\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\|\to 0

follows because the union of
p
null sets (one per non-converging coordinate)
is itself a null set—equivalently, the intersection of
p
probability-one
convergence sets has probability one;
this argument is specific to fixed
p
and does not extend to architectures
with growing hidden dimension;
(5) uniform integrability—from bounded activations or a

\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|h_{t}\right\|^{1+\eta}]<\infty
condition—then upgrades a.s. convergence to
L^{1}
convergence.
∎

Corollary 3.3

(Exact reverse martingale case)
.

If
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\right]=h_{t+1}
a.s. for every
t
,
then
(h_{t},\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}})
is an exact reverse martingale and

h_{t}\to h_{\infty}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\right]
a.s. and in
L^{1}
.

Corollary 3.4

(Geometric convergence rate)
.

Under Assumptions
3.1
–
3.4
and the uniform
integrability condition of Theorem
3.2
(iii), suppose additionally
that the quasi-martingale defect satisfies

\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|_{1}]\leq D\,\alpha^{t}

(using the coordinatewise
\ell^{1}
norm
\left\|v\right\|_{1}=\sum_{j}|v^{(j)}|
)
for some
D<\infty
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\right\|\bigr]\;\leq\;\frac{D\,\alpha^{t}}{1-\alpha}.

(12)

A verifiable sufficient condition for the geometric defect bound is

geometric convergence of the observable drift and approximation error
:
suppose there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
the observable drift satisfies
\mathbb{E}[\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|]\leq D_{\infty}\alpha^{t}

and the projector approximation error satisfies
\varepsilon_{t}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\beta^{t}
.
Then by Assumption
3.4
and the triangle inequality,

\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|]\leq\mathbb{E}[\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|]+\varepsilon_{t}\leq D_{\infty}\alpha^{t}+C_{\varepsilon}\beta^{t}\leq(D_{\infty}+C_{\varepsilon})\max(\alpha,\beta)^{t}=:\widetilde{D}\,\bar{\alpha}^{t}
,
where
\widetilde{D}=D_{\infty}+C_{\varepsilon}
and

\bar{\alpha}=\max(\alpha,\beta)
.
Since
\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{p}\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|
,
this gives
\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|_{1}]\leq\sqrt{p}\,\widetilde{D}\bar{\alpha}^{t}
; the
bound (
12
) holds with
D
replaced by
\sqrt{p}\,\widetilde{D}

and
\alpha
by
\bar{\alpha}
.
The observable rate
\alpha
can be estimated from the empirical sequence

(\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|)
by log-linear regression; all constants are explicit
in
\rho
(spectral norm of
W_{h}
) and
C_{\varepsilon}

(backward-projector training error schedule).

Remark on
\phi
-mixing.

Proposition
3.12
and Corollary
3.13
show
that exponential
\phi
-mixing,
\phi(k)\leq C_{\phi}e^{-\gamma k}
,
implies
B_{t}(k)\to 0
geometrically in lag
k
,
uniformly in
t
.
This controls backward-prediction difficulty for stationary inputs and
supports
D_{\infty}<\infty
. However, because
B_{t}(k)
is
t
-uniform
for stationary processes, exponential
\phi
-mixing does
not
by
itself imply the time-
t
decay
\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|]\leq D\alpha^{t}
; that
decay must be verified empirically from
(\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|)
or assumed
directly.

Proof.

Apply the Krickeberg decomposition coordinatewise: write

h_{t}^{(j)}=U_{t}^{(j)}-V_{t}^{(j)}
where
U_{t}^{(j)}
and
V_{t}^{(j)}

are non-negative reverse supermartingales

(
Krickeberg 1956
;
Neveu 1975
;
Rao 1969
)
.
Since non-negative reverse supermartingales are decreasing in expectation,
the reverse-filtration analogue of Rao’s characterisation
(see Supplementary Material § A.4 for the derivation) gives

\mathbb{E}[U_{t}^{(j)}]-\mathbb{E}[U_{\infty}^{(j)}]=\sum_{s\geq t}\mathbb{E}[\rho_{s}^{(j),+}]

and

\mathbb{E}[V_{t}^{(j)}]-\mathbb{E}[V_{\infty}^{(j)}]=\sum_{s\geq t}\mathbb{E}[\rho_{s}^{(j),-}]
,
where
\rho_{s}^{(j),+}
and
\rho_{s}^{(j),-}
are the positive and negative
parts of the
j
-th coordinate drift.
Hence, for each coordinate,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[|h_{t}^{(j)}-h_{\infty}^{(j)}|\bigr]\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}[U_{t}^{(j)}-U_{\infty}^{(j)}]+\mathbb{E}[V_{t}^{(j)}-V_{\infty}^{(j)}]\;=\;\sum_{s\geq t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\rho_{s}^{(j)}|\bigr].

The uniform integrability condition (from Theorem
3.2
(iii))
ensures
L^{1}
convergence so that
h_{\infty}
is well-defined.
Summing over
j=1,\ldots,p
and using

\left\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\right\|\leq\left\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\right\|_{1}=\sum_{j}|h_{t}^{(j)}-h_{\infty}^{(j)}|
:

\mathbb{E}[\left\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\right\|]\;\leq\;\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|h_{t}^{(j)}-h_{\infty}^{(j)}|\bigr]\;\leq\;\sum_{s\geq t}\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\rho_{s}^{(j)}|\bigr]\;=\;\sum_{s\geq t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{s}\right\|_{1}].

Applying the
\ell^{1}
geometric defect bound
\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{s}\right\|_{1}]\leq D\alpha^{s}

yields
\sum_{s=t}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\rho_{s}\right\|_{1}]\leq D\sum_{s=t}^{\infty}\alpha^{s}=D\alpha^{t}/(1-\alpha)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Corollary
3.4
requires a geometric decay assumption on the defect
that is stronger than the plain summability of Theorem
3.2
.
The rate
D\alpha^{t}/(1-\alpha)
decreases to zero as
\alpha\to 0
,
recovering the exact reverse-martingale case (instantaneous convergence).
For the three real-data studies,
\alpha
is bounded above by the empirical
contraction factor
\hat{\rho}
; in the UCI HAR experiment,
\hat{\rho}=0.998

gives a rate envelope
D\cdot 0.998^{t}/0.002
, consistent with the
quantitative tracking-error profile in Section
4.2
.
The corollary provides a finite-
t
guarantee; it does not claim that the
bound is tight for all architectures or input processes outside the stated
sufficient conditions.

Remark 3.6

(Zero backward loss versus exact reverse martingales)
.

Zero empirical backward-coherence loss implies
h_{t}=g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})
and,
under a perfectly trained projector,
g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
.
This is a strong backward-coherence property but is not identical to the
reverse martingale condition of Corollary
3.3
unless the
compatibility
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\right]=h_{t+1}
holds.
The paper uses zero loss as an empirical route to small quasi-martingale
variation, not as a proof of exact reverse-martingale structure.

Remark 3.7

(Gated architectures: when contraction holds and when it does not)
.

Theorem
3.2
extends to GRU and LSTM architectures provided
Assumption
3.1
holds for the effective hidden-to-hidden
Jacobian. For an LSTM, the cell-state Jacobian is

\partial c_{t}/\partial c_{t-1}=\mathrm{diag}(f_{t})
, so the effective
contraction factor is
\rho^{*}=\sup_{t}\|f_{t}\|_{\infty}
. Since

\|f_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
always (sigmoid outputs), we need
\rho^{*}<1

strictly, which fails whenever any forget-gate coordinate saturates to
unity—a common occurrence in tasks that require very long-term memory
(e.g., language modelling with long-range dependencies, or time series with
slow trends). In such settings the theorem does not apply and the framework
should not be used without modification.

There are, however, important and commonly encountered settings where

\rho^{*}<1
is plausible, verifiable, and empirically beneficial:

(i)
short- to medium-horizon sequential prediction tasks—clinical
early warning, activity recognition, short-term macro forecasting—where
deliberate recency bias in forget-gate initialisation or regularisation
prevents saturation near 1 and is already standard practice;

(ii)
piecewise-stationary inputs where the forget gate resets at
detected regime boundaries, as in UCI HAR activity segments; and

(iii)
architectures trained with Zoneout
(
Krueger et al. 2017
)
,
spectral normalisation on
W_{f}
, or
L^{2}
weight decay on recurrent weights,
any of which caps
\rho^{*}
below unity as a side effect of controlling
overfitting. These three settings collectively cover a substantial share
of applied sequential modelling practice. In each,
\rho^{*}
is estimable
from the empirical distribution of
\|f_{t}\|_{\infty}
over training
trajectories, and the Supplementary Material gives sufficient conditions and
proofs for GRU. For architectures or tasks outside these settings,
Assumption
3.1
should be verified empirically before the
theoretical guarantees are invoked.

Remark 3.8

(Interpretable limit)
.

In the exact reverse-martingale case,
h_{\infty}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\right]

is the conditional expectation of the initial representation given the
tail event of the hidden-state sequence. In the general quasi-martingale
case, Theorem
3.2
guarantees existence of a limit but does not
by itself yield this canonical Doob representation.
Proposition
3.10
below fills this gap: it shows that

h_{\infty}
lies within the observable budget
D_{\infty}+\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t}

of the Krickeberg supermartingale limit
M_{\infty}
in
L^{1}
; since

M_{\infty}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\right]
in the exact
case, the limit remains near-interpretable whenever
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
is small.

Remark 3.9

(Rao–Krickeberg decomposition structure)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.2
, the Krickeberg decomposition

(
Krickeberg 1956
)
guarantees that each coordinate

h_{t}^{(j)}
decomposes as
h_{t}^{(j)}=M_{t}^{(j)}-N_{t}^{(j)}
, where

(M_{t}^{(j)},\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}})
and
(N_{t}^{(j)},\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}})

are non-negative reverse supermartingales with

\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}[M_{t}^{(j)}]+\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}[N_{t}^{(j)}]<\infty
.
Krickeberg (1956) works with martingales indexed by
directed sets
;
a decreasing filtration is a directed set under reverse order, so his
framework directly covers the present reverse-filtration setting.
The bounded-variation characterisation of the decomposition in the reverse
direction follows from
Neveu 1975
§ V-3 together with

Rao 1969
; see the Supplementary Material § A.3 for the explicit
argument.
The bounded-variation component equals

\sum_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left|\rho_{t}^{(j)}\right|]\leq D_{\infty}+\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t}
, precisely
the summability condition driving convergence.
This decomposition explicitly separates the

pure reverse-martingale trend component
from the

backward-incoherence correction
: since each non-negative reverse
supermartingale converges almost surely without requiring a uniform
integrability condition
(
Neveu 1975
)
, the sample paths of
h_{t}^{(j)}

converge almost surely as a direct consequence of the bounded-variation
property. This highlights a core advantage over forward quasi-martingales:
in the forward direction, almost-sure convergence requires stringent uniform
bounds on forward innovations, whereas in the reverse direction the
contraction of the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mathrm{bwd}})
already enforces the

L^{1}
-boundedness needed for the decomposition to apply.
When
D_{\infty}=0
the decomposition collapses to the pure reverse martingale
of Corollary
3.3
.

Proposition 3.10

(Perturbation bound for the quasi-martingale limit)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.2
with

D_{\infty}+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{t}<\infty
,
let
M_{\infty}
denote the coordinatewise almost-sure limit of the
non-negative Krickeberg component
(M_{t}^{(j)})_{t\geq 1}
from
Remark
3.9
. For the
\ell^{1}
norm

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left|\,\cdot\,\right|^{(j)}
over coordinates,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|h_{\infty}-M_{\infty}\right\|_{1}\right]\;\leq\;D_{\infty}^{(1)}+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{t}^{(1)},

(13)

where
D_{\infty}^{(1)}:=\sum_{t}\mathbb{E}[\left\|r_{t}^{\phi}\right\|_{1}]
and

\varepsilon_{t}^{(1)}:=\mathbb{E}[\left\|g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})-\mathbb{E}[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}]\right\|_{1}]

are the
\ell^{1}

D_{\infty}
and

) is
thus also bounded by
\sqrt{p}(D_{\infty}+\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t})

In the exact reverse-martingale case (
D_{\infty}=0
,
\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t}=0
),

a.s., giving

.

By the Rao–Krickeberg decomposition (Remark
3.9

h_{t}^{(j)}=M_{t}^{(j)}-N_{t}^{(j)}
coordinatewise, where both

and
(N_{t}^{(j)})
are non-negative reverse supermartingales

M_{\infty}^{(j)}

Proposition

closes the interpretability gap identified
in Remark

Remark 3.11

(Finite-horizon role of
Q_{T}
)
.

The empirical total
Q_{T}
remains central even when the infinite-horizon
condition is not asserted: it measures accumulated backward incoherence over
the observed trajectory and supports finite-horizon comparisons of
architectures, regularisation strengths, and stopping rules.

3.2
Extensions: dependent and non-stationary inputs

3.2.1
\phi
-Mixing input processes

A stationary sequence
(x_{t})_{t\geq 1}
is
\phi
-mixing
with
coefficients
\phi(k)\downarrow 0
if

\phi(k):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in\sigma(x_{s},s\leq t)\\
B\in\sigma(x_{s},s\geq t+k)\end{subarray}}\left|\mathbb{P}(B\,|\,A)-\mathbb{P}(B)\right|\to 0\quad\text{as }k\to\infty.

Exponential
\phi
-mixing,
\phi(k)\leq Ce^{-ak}
, is satisfied by many
stable autoregressive and geometrically ergodic Markov processes

(
Bradley 2007
;
Meyn and Tweedie 2009
;
Rio 2017
)
.

The following result controls the conditional-mean dependence component.
Because
h_{t+k}=f_{\theta}^{(k)}(h_{t},x_{t+1:t+k})
depends on
h_{t}

through the recurrent dynamics, conditioning on
h_{t+k}
provides
information about
h_{t}
even when the inputs are i.i.d.; the bound
therefore has two terms: a recurrent memory term (controlled by
\rho^{k}
)
and an input-mixing term (controlled by
\phi(k)
).

Proposition 3.12

(
\phi
-mixing bound for conditional-mean dependence)
.

Under Assumption
3.1
, suppose
\left\|h_{t}\right\|_{\infty}\leq H_{h}

a.s. For any lag
k\geq 1
, let

B_{t}(k):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+k}\right]-\mathbb{E}[h_{t}]\right\|\right]
.
Then

B_{t}(k)\;\leq\;C_{1}\,\rho^{k}\;+\;C_{2}\,\phi(k),\qquad C_{1}=C_{2}=4\sqrt{p}\,H_{h}.

(15)

For
k=1
:
B_{t}(1)\leq C_{1}\rho+C_{2}\phi(1)
. Under i.i.d. inputs

(\phi(k)=0)
,
B_{t}(k)\leq C_{1}\rho^{k}\to 0
geometrically, reflecting
pure recurrent memory decay under the contraction.
Both constants equal
4\sqrt{p}\,H_{h}
, arising from the
\phi
-mixing
covariance inequality
(
Bradley 2007
, Vol. 1, Thm. 3.11)
applied to the
geometrically ergodic chain
(h_{t})
: the inequality gives

|\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)|\leq 4\|X\|_{\infty}\|Y\|_{\infty}\phi(k)
at lag
k
;
applying this coordinatewise with
\|X\|_{\infty}\leq 2H_{h}
and
\|Y\|_{\infty}\leq 1

gives the per-coordinate bound, and the
\sqrt{p}
factor converts to
the Euclidean norm.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material.
∎

Although
B_{t}(k)
does not directly equal the full backward prediction
innovation
\mathbb{E}\|h_{t}-\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]\|
, it enters through the
following explicit telescoping bound. Let

\eta_{t}:=h_{t}-\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
denote the
ideal backward prediction innovation (this uses the exact conditional mean
and differs from the quasi-martingale drift

\rho_{t}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]-h_{t+1}
).
By the triangle inequality:

\mathbb{E}\|\eta_{t}\|\;\leq\;\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\|h_{t}-\mathbb{E}[h_{t}]\|}_{\text{(A) marginal spread}}\;+\;\underbrace{B_{t}(1)}_{\text{(B) mixing component}}.

(16)

Term (A) is the marginal spread of
h_{t}
around its mean. For the Elman
architecture (
2
) with a
1
-Lipschitz activation,
f_{\theta}

is
\left\|W_{x}\right\|
-Lipschitz in its
x
-argument, so iterating the contraction
gives
\left\|h_{t}-\mathbb{E}[h_{t}]\right\|\leq\rho\left\|h_{t-1}-\mathbb{E}[h_{t-1}]\right\|+\left\|W_{x}\right\|\,\left\|x_{t}-\mathbb{E}[x_{t}]\right\|
, and by a geometric-series argument,

\mathbb{E}\left\|h_{t}-\mathbb{E}[h_{t}]\right\|\;\leq\;\frac{\left\|W_{x}\right\|\,\sigma_{x}}{1-\rho}\;=:\;C_{\mathrm{mix}}^{\prime}\,(1-\rho)^{-1},

where
\sigma_{x}:=\mathbb{E}\left\|x_{1}-\mathbb{E}[x_{1}]\right\|<\infty
is the mean absolute
deviation of the input and
C_{\mathrm{mix}}^{\prime}=\left\|W_{x}\right\|\,\sigma_{x}
.
For general
f_{\theta}
where
x_{t}
enters nonlinearly, replace
\left\|W_{x}\right\|

by the Lipschitz constant of
f_{\theta}
in its
x
-argument.
Term (B) is controlled by Proposition
3.12
at lag
k=1
:

B_{t}(1)\leq C_{1}\rho+C_{2}\phi(1)
.
The recurrence floor
C_{1}\rho
is always present; the mixing term

C_{2}\phi(1)
is small for rapidly mixing inputs.
(Note: no approximation-error term
\varepsilon_{t}
appears in (
16
)
because
\eta_{t}
involves the exact conditional mean
\mathbb{E}[h_{t}\mid h_{t+1}]
,
not the learned projector
g_{\phi}
. The observable residual
\delta_{t}=h_{t}-g_{\phi}(h_{t+1})
satisfies
\mathbb{E}\|\delta_{t}\|\leq\mathbb{E}\|\eta_{t}\|+\varepsilon_{t}
by Assumption
3.4
and the triangle inequality.)
Equation (
16
) therefore quantifies how rapidly mixing inputs
reduce backward-prediction difficulty: more rapidly mixing inputs
(smaller
\phi(1)
) produce smaller
B_{t}(1)
and hence smaller

\mathbb{E}\|\eta_{t}\|
, improving backward approximability and providing
conditions under which
D_{\infty}
is more readily controlled.
Under the two-term bound (
15
),
B_{t}(k)\leq C_{1}\rho^{k}+C_{2}\phi(k)

decays to zero whenever
\rho<1
and
\phi(k)\to 0
; summability of

\{B_{t}(k)\}_{k}
controls the multi-step contribution to
D_{\infty}
,
as made precise in the following corollary.

Corollary 3.13

(Exponential mixing)
.

If
\phi(k)\leq C_{0}e^{-ak}
, then (
15
) gives

B_{t}(k)\leq C_{1}\rho^{k}+C_{2}C_{0}e^{-ak}
and

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{t}(k)\;\leq\;\frac{C_{1}\rho}{1-\rho}+\frac{C_{2}C_{0}e^{-a}}{1-e^{-a}}<\infty.

The bound is
uniform in
t
because
C_{1}
and
C_{2}

in (
15
) depend only on
\rho
,
p
, and
H_{h}
, not on
t
;
this uniformity allows the
k
-summation to be exchanged with any subsequent

t
-summation when bounding
D_{\infty}
.
Quasi-martingale convergence additionally requires

D_{\infty}+\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t}<\infty
.

Remark 3.14
.

Autocorrelated inputs may empirically yield smaller backward-coherence
losses than i.i.d. inputs at the same marginal variance, because smoother
input trajectories can make
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
easier to approximate.
This is evaluated numerically in Section
4
but is not
asserted as a consequence of Proposition
3.12
alone.

3.2.2
Piecewise-stationary inputs and concept drift

Suppose the input distribution is piecewise stationary with
K
segments:

x_{t}\sim F_{k}
for
t\in(T_{k-1},T_{k}]
, with change points at

T_{1},\ldots,T_{K-1}
. Let
\Delta_{k}=\left\|\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k}\right\|
denote
the magnitude of the
k
-th mean shift.

Assumption 3.5

(Lipschitz stationarity map)
.

The stationary hidden-state mean
m(F):=\mathbb{E}_{F}[h_{\infty}]
is Lipschitz in
the segment distribution with respect to mean-shift distance:

\left\|m(F_{k+1})-m(F_{k})\right\|\leq L_{m}\,\Delta_{k}
for a constant
L_{m}>0
.
The backward projector approximation error is uniformly bounded:

\sup_{t}\varepsilon_{t}\leq\varepsilon<\infty
.

Proposition 3.15

(Decomposition at change points)
.

Under Assumptions
3.1
–
3.4

and Assumption
3.5
,

Q_{T}\;\leq\;\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}Q_{T,k}\;+\;\frac{L_{m}}{1-\rho}\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\Delta_{k},

(17)

where
Q_{T,k}
is the within-segment empirical quasi-martingale total under

F_{k}
.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material.
∎

The decomposition (
17
) connects total backward incoherence to
two sources: within-segment serial dependence and abrupt distributional
shifts. Monitoring these separately enables practitioner-level diagnostics:
a sudden spike in
\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|
identifies a change point, while a
gradual linear increase indicates model drift.

Proposition 3.16

(Tracking-error bound)
.

Under Assumption
3.1
and the finite-horizon approximation
of Assumption
3.4
, suppose further that the hidden state has
converged to the
k
-th segment mean before the change point, i.e.
\mathbb{E}[\left\|h_{T_{k}}-m_{k}\right\|]\leq\delta_{k}
for some
\delta_{k}\geq 0
.
Then after the
k
-th change point at
T_{k}
,

\mathbb{E}[\left\|h_{t}-m_{k+1}\right\|]\;\leq\;\rho^{t-T_{k}}\,(\Delta_{k}+\delta_{k})\;+\;\sigma_{k+1},\qquad t>T_{k},

(18)

where
m_{k+1}=\mathbb{E}_{F_{k+1}}[h_{\infty}]
,

\Delta_{k}=\left\|m_{k+1}-m_{k}\right\|
is the hidden-state regime-shift magnitude
(which satisfies
\Delta_{k}\leq L_{m}\left\|\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k}\right\|
by
Assumption
3.5
, so this
\Delta_{k}
is the output-space
analogue of the input shift used in Proposition
3.15
),
and
\sigma_{k+1}:=\mathbb{E}_{F_{k+1}}[\left\|h_{\infty}-m_{k+1}\right\|]
is the
equilibrium stationary spread of
(h_{t})
under
F_{k+1}

(bounded by
2H_{h}\sqrt{p}
for bounded activations).
In the limiting case of long segments (
\delta_{k}\to 0
), the leading coefficient
reduces to
\Delta_{k}
. The floor
\sigma_{k+1}
is the irreducible
equilibrium tracking error under the new regime; it vanishes only if the
stationary distribution under
F_{k+1}
is concentrated at
m_{k+1}
.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material.
∎

Proposition
3.16
shows geometric re-adaptation at rate
\rho

after each change point, with recovery time

\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}\approx\log(\Delta_{k}/\sigma_{k+1})/(1-\rho)
.
This recovery window acts as a
statistical horizon
: observations
more than
\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}
steps before the current time carry
negligible information about the post-drift regime, providing a
theoretically grounded analogue to rolling-window selection in
non-stationary time series analysis
(
Gama et al. 2014
)
. The parameters

\rho
and
\Delta_{k}
are directly estimable from the observed defect
sequence
\|\delta_{t}\|
, making
\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}
a data-driven
criterion for window length without requiring knowledge of the change
points themselves.
Three regimes arise in practice:
abrupt large shift

(typhoon onset, medical dosing event);
gradual drift
(seasonal
environmental change, slow physiological decline); and

intra-day market regime changes
. In all three,
\rho
and
\Delta_{k}

are clinically or operationally meaningful parameters.

3.3
Statistical inference and calibrated confidence sequences

Let
p_{\phi}(h_{t}\mid h_{t+1})=\mathcal{N}(g_{\phi}(h_{t+1}),\,\sigma^{2}I_{p})

be the learned Gaussian backward model. As a
working model
, define

p^{*}(h_{t}\mid h_{t+1})=\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right],\,\Sigma_{t})
:
this Gaussian working model has the correct conditional mean but makes no
claim about the true distribution of
h_{t}\mid h_{t+1}
, which may be
non-Gaussian. The following proposition shows that minimising
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
over

\phi
is equivalent to minimising the KL risk under this working model,
irrespective of the true conditional distribution.

Proposition 3.17

(Backward-coherence loss as KL minimisation)
.

For fixed
\sigma>0
, minimising
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}(\theta,\phi)
over
\phi
is
equivalent to minimising the expected KL divergence from the true
conditional backward distribution to the Gaussian backward model:

\argmin_{\phi}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}(\theta,\phi)\;=\;\argmin_{\phi}\,\frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p^{*}(\,\cdot\,\,|\,h_{t+1})\,\Big\|\,p_{\phi}(\,\cdot\,\,|\,h_{t+1})\right)\right].

(19)

Consequently,

Q_{T}\;\leq\;\sqrt{T-1}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|^{2}]}\;=\;\sqrt{(T-1)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}]},

(20)

where
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}]=\frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|^{2}]
.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material.
∎

Remark 3.18

(Connection to sequential VAEs and Empirical Bayes)
.

Proposition
3.17
establishes that the RMRNN is a

discriminatively trained variational backward smoother
. In the
language of variational inference
(
Blei et al. 2017
)
and sequential latent
variable models
(
Chung et al. 2015
;
Fraccaro et al. 2016
)
, the forward RNN
f_{\theta}

acts as the
generative backbone
: it maps the input sequence

x_{1:T}
to hidden states
h_{1},\ldots,h_{T}
via the filtering recursion.
The backward projector
g_{\phi}
provides an
amortised variational
smoothing distribution

q_{\phi}(h_{t}\mid h_{t+1}):=\mathcal{N}(g_{\phi}(h_{t+1}),\sigma^{2}I_{p})
,
acting as a recognition network that approximates the true backward
conditional
p^{*}(h_{t}\mid h_{t+1})
without requiring a parametric prior.
The joint objective
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
therefore
takes the form of a structured ELBO, with
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
enforcing backward-model
consistency:

\argmin_{\theta,\phi}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}(\theta,\phi)\;=\;\argmin_{\theta,\phi}\,\frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p^{*}(h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1})\,\Big\|\,q_{\phi}(h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1})\right)\right].

(21)

Equation (
21
) reframes
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
from an ad-hoc regulariser to a

principled quasi-likelihood M-estimator
for the backward conditional
model: it minimises the distributional discrepancy between the true backward
dynamics and the Gaussian recognition network in the KL sense. From an

Empirical Bayes
perspective, the distribution-free conditional mean

\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
acts as the implicit prior predictive, and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}

is the corresponding empirical risk for prior-predictive matching.
The finite-horizon total
Q_{T}
is analogous to an ELBO gap: a smaller
Q_{T}

indicates a better empirical match between the learned and true backward
conditionals and hence stronger finite-horizon backward coherence. Claims
about almost-sure convergence still depend on the contraction and summability
conditions of Theorem
3.2
.

Finite-sample concentration inequality.

Proposition 3.19

(Concentration of hidden state
(
McDiarmid 1989
)
)
.

Suppose
x_{1},\ldots,x_{T}
are independent with
\left\|x_{t}-\mu\right\|\leq B

a.s. for all
t
, and Assumption
3.1
holds.
For the Elman RNN (
2
) with
1
-Lipschitz activation,
changing
x_{s}
to
x_{s}^{\prime}
changes
h_{T}
by at most

c_{s}=2B\left\|W_{x}\right\|\rho^{T-s}
, giving bounded differences

\sum_{s=1}^{T}c_{s}^{2}=4B^{2}\left\|W_{x}\right\|^{2}(1-\rho^{2T})/(1-\rho^{2})
.
Applying the one-sided McDiarmid inequality to the function

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{T})=\left\|h_{T}-\mathbb{E}[h_{T}]\right\|
—which satisfies the same
bounded-difference constants
c_{s}
by the reverse triangle
inequality—yields: for any
u>0
,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left\|h_{T}-\mathbb{E}[h_{T}]\right\|\geq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|h_{T}-\mathbb{E}[h_{T}]\right\|\right]+u\right)\;\leq\;\exp\!\left(-\frac{u^{2}\,(1-\rho^{2})}{2\,\left\|W_{x}\right\|^{2}\,B^{2}\,(1-\rho^{2T})}\right).

(22)

If additionally the backward projector
g_{\phi}
is applied (Lipschitz
constant
L_{\phi}=1+\left\|W_{2}\right\|\left\|W_{1}\right\|
), the same argument with

\left\|W_{x}\right\|
replaced by
L_{\phi}\left\|W_{x}\right\|
gives concentration of

g_{\phi}(h_{T})
.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material.
∎

Pathwise stability tubes and calibrated confidence sequences.

Proposition 3.20

(Pathwise stability tubes and calibrated confidence sequences)
.

Assume the conditions of Theorem
3.2
and

\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\left\|h_{s+1}-h_{s}\right\|<\infty
a.s. Let

R_{t}:=\sum_{s\geq t}\left\|h_{s+1}-h_{s}\right\|
. Then:

(i)

For any
\delta>0
, the stopping time

\tau_{\delta}:=\inf\{t\geq 1:\left\|h_{t+1}-h_{t}\right\|\leq\delta\}

is finite a.s.

(ii)

The deterministic tubes
\mathcal{T}_{t}:=\{h:\left\|h-h_{t}\right\|\leq R_{t}\}

contain
h_{\infty}
simultaneously for all
t
.

(iii)

If an observable sequence
\widehat{R}_{t}(\alpha)
satisfies

\mathbb{P}\{R_{t}\leq\widehat{R}_{t}(\alpha)\text{ for all }t\geq 1\}\geq 1-\alpha
,
then
\mathcal{C}_{t}(\alpha):=\{h:\left\|h-h_{t}\right\|\leq\widehat{R}_{t}(\alpha)\}

is a time-uniform confidence sequence for
h_{\infty}
with coverage

\geq 1-\alpha
.

Proof.

See the Supplementary Material. The result separates the deterministic
pathwise enclosure from the statistical calibration step.
∎

Anytime-valid uncertainty quantification is therefore achieved when a valid
observable tail bound is available. The stopping rule
\tau_{\delta}
has concrete interpretations:
in
clinical monitoring
, it identifies the earliest time the RNN’s
summary has stabilised sufficiently for clinical decisions; in

spatiotemporal monitoring
, it determines when predictions can be
trusted; in
financial modelling
, it marks the end of a regime
transition
(
Howard et al. 2021
;
Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2023
)
.

4.2
Real-data experiments

Three publicly available datasets are used to evaluate RMRNN against
baseline models on tasks that represent three canonical sources of
non-stationarity in sequential learning: noisy clinical observations
accumulating over time, slow macroeconomic concept drift spanning decades,
and rapid discrete regime switching between activity states. Together they
test whether backward-coherence regularisation preserves predictive
performance under very different temporal structures, and whether the
theoretical quantities
\hat{Q}
and
\tau_{\delta}
carry interpretable
meaning on real data.

Each study compares four models: the Elman RNN baseline (
\lambda=0
),
RMRNN (
\lambda=0.1
), bidirectional RNN (BiRNN), and a domain-appropriate
state-space comparator (Kalman filter / linear dynamical system for the
clinical and activity studies; BVAR(4) for the macroeconomic study).
All neural models share the same architecture (hidden dimension
p=32
,
Adam optimiser, learning rate
10^{-3}
) and differ only in whether the
backward-coherence penalty
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
is active.
Evaluation follows a 20-seed repeated cross-validation protocol
(
20\times 3=60
fold observations per model) with all random seeds
derived deterministically from a single base seed (2026) for
reproducibility. Replication scripts are included in the supplementary
archive.

The state-space comparators serve two roles: as
practical
benchmarks
showing where classical models stand relative to neural
approaches, and as
theoretical reference points
, since the Kalman
smoother backward pass is the exact backward projector
g_{\phi^{*}}
in the
linear-Gaussian case (Section
2.3
). It is therefore
expected to attain small
\hat{Q}
by construction; the neural models must
earn comparable stability through learning.

Domain 1: Clinical risk prediction under irregular observation
(PhysioNet 2012 ICU Challenge).

Why this domain.
Clinical ICU sequences represent a setting where
observations arrive irregularly, measurements are missing at random, and
the information content of the hidden state grows non-uniformly across the
48-hour stay. Backward-coherence regularisation is expected to suppress
hidden-state drift during periods of low information arrival and to yield an
identifiable stabilisation point
\tau_{\delta}
that is clinically
meaningful—ideally well before hour 48 so that predictions can be trusted
earlier in the stay.

Setup.
The PhysioNet 2012 Challenge dataset
(
Silva et al. 2012
)

comprises 8,000 ICU patient records (sets A and B combined), each a
48-hour multivariate time series of 12 vital signs and laboratory measurements
(heart rate, MAP, temperature, respiratory rate,
etc
.) resampled to
hourly bins with forward-fill imputation. In-hospital mortality is 14.0%.
The target is binary mortality prediction; evaluation uses area under the ROC
curve (AUC) and the Brier score. The Kalman filter / LDS baseline is fitted
by EM with state dimension 8 and 5 iterations; its final smoothed state feeds
a logistic regression classifier.

Domain 2: Macroeconomic forecasting under slow concept drift
(FRED-MD December 2024 vintage).

Why this domain.
Macroeconomic time series are characterised by
slow but persistent structural change: monetary-policy regimes, financial
crises, and supply shocks gradually alter the joint distribution of
hundreds of indicators over months to years. This is the setting captured
by Proposition
3.16
: the RNN must track a slowly drifting
target
m_{k+1}
, and
\hat{Q}
should rise and fall with the intensity of
structural change. FRED-MD provides 60+ years of monthly data with
independently verified regime changes (NBER recession dates), making it an
ideal testbed for whether
\hat{Q}
is a genuine real-world stability
diagnostic.

Setup.
The December 2024 FRED-MD vintage
(
McCracken and Ng 2016
)

contains 791 monthly observations (January 1959–November 2024) across 128
macroeconomic series. After removing series with more than 20% missing
values and applying the McCracken–Ng transformation codes (log-differences

etc.
), 122 input features remain. The target is one-month-ahead
Industrial Production (INDPRO) growth, evaluated by mean squared error
(MSE). An expanding-window design is used: models are trained on the first
75% of the available history and evaluated on a rolling 60-month window
stepped forward by 6 months, giving 20 evaluation windows. The
state-space comparator is a BVAR(4) with a Minnesota (Normal-Wishart) prior

(
Bańbura et al. 2010
)
, which is the classical benchmark for
macroeconomic forecasting.

The primary purpose of the FRED-MD study is

implicit smoothness prior over the latent trajectory.

3.16
:

responds to the magnitude of concept drift
\sum_{k}\Delta_{k}/(1-\rho)

in the piecewise-stationary decomposition, and it does so in real time.
The diagnostic is unavailable from accuracy or task-loss metrics alone.

).

Synthesis across domains.

Three consistent findings emerge. First, backward-coherence regularisation
does not sacrifice predictive performance: across all three domains, RMRNN
matches the best neural baseline to within one standard deviation. Second,
the benefit of RMRNN is domain-dependent in character. In the clinical
domain (irregular observation), the gain is earlier hidden-state
stabilisation—13 hours earlier than RNN in a 48-hour ICU stay. In the
macroeconomic domain (slow concept drift), the gain is in predictive
accuracy itself—an approximately fourfold MSE reduction attributable to the backward
projector suppressing hidden-state drift during regime transitions. In the
activity-recognition domain (rapid discrete switching), the gain is in the
speed and regularity of post-switch recovery, validating the geometric
tracking-error bound of Proposition
3.16
. Third, the
empirical quasi-martingale total
\hat{Q}
behaves interpretably in all
three settings: it is defined only for RMRNN (which trains
g_{\phi}

jointly), rises with temporal instability (NBER recessions, activity
transitions), and decays as the hidden state stabilises. Baseline
models without a backward projector do not produce
\hat{Q}
, making
it a stability diagnostic that is unique to the RMRNN framework and
unavailable from accuracy or task-loss metrics alone.

5
Discussion

The present framework subsumes echo state networks
(
Jaeger 2001
)
: any
network satisfying
\rho(W_{h})<1
and
D_{\infty}+\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t}<\infty

has a convergent hidden-state trajectory, and in the exact reverse-martingale
case the limit has the conditional-expectation representation

h_{\infty}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{bwd}}\right]
rather than being
merely the fixed point of a deterministic echo-state map. The computable
diagnostic
\hat{Q}
captures this probabilistic structure; stochastic
approximation theory
(
Robbins and Monro 1951
;
Kushner and Yin 2003
)
characterises trajectory
dynamics but provides no such scalar summary.

The backward projector
g_{\phi}
is the discriminatively trained counterpart
of the Kalman smoother backward pass
(
Särkkä 2013
;
Shumway and Stoffer 2000
)
: both
approximate
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{t}\,|\,h_{t+1}\right]
, but
g_{\phi}
requires no
Gaussian linear model assumption and is trained end-to-end.
Proposition
3.17
establishes that minimising
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RM}}
is equivalent
to minimising a KL divergence in a Gaussian backward model
(
Blei et al. 2017
)
.
Recurrent latent-variable models
(
Chung et al. 2015
;
Fraccaro et al. 2016
)
are
generative and augment the forward pass with explicit stochastic latent
variables; RMRNN is discriminative and requires none. A systematic
comparison on sequence density-estimation tasks is left for future work.

Structural constraint approaches—Lipschitz recurrent networks

(
Miller and Hardt 2019
;
Erichson et al. 2021
)
and spectral normalisation

(
Miyato et al. 2018
)
—all satisfy Assumption
3.1
;
Theorem
3.2
provides the probabilistic convergence
characterisation that these architectural choices enable but do not themselves
establish. The quasi-martingale decomposition via
Rao 1969
and

Krickeberg 1956
is here applied to the vector-valued hidden-state
process and the backward filtration, a combination we believe is new.

The two auxiliary experiments in Section
4
probe specific
predictions without claiming exhaustive confirmation.
Experiment 6 confirms Corollary
3.4
as an upper bound: empirical
forgetting rates do not exceed
\rho
, with tanh saturation producing
faster-than-predicted decay. Isolating the
\phi
-mixing component requires
averaging over input realisations rather than initial conditions; a linear
RNN, where the two contributions are analytically separable, or a
non-equilibrium design with a known input-mixing time, would be needed.
Experiment 7 shows that the increment-sum tube
R_{t}
is valid (100%
simultaneous coverage by the triangle inequality) but loose in practice
(median inflation 17–30, reflecting non-monotone hidden-state paths), and
that the defect-tail proxy
\hat{Q}_{t}
is the practically useful tube
(
C^{*}=1.15
for 95% coverage).
Proposition
3.10
bounds the deviation of
h_{\infty}
from
the Krickeberg limit
M_{\infty}
by the computable budget

D_{\infty}+\sum_{t}\varepsilon_{t}
in
L^{1}
, providing an operational
characterisation of
h_{\infty}
as a near-conditional-expectation.

Several open problems arise from the present work.

(i)

Backward Markov sufficiency from first principles.

Assumption
3.3
is verified empirically; deriving it for
specific activations or gate structures would make the framework fully
self-contained.

(ii)

Growing hidden dimension.
The coordinatewise proof requires
fixed
p
; extending to growing
p
requires uniform coordinate control.

(iii)

Non-expansive case.
Convergence at
\left\|W_{h}\right\|=1
, arising
in residual networks
(
He et al. 2016
)
, requires Opial’s lemma

(
Opial 1967
)
or asymptotic regularity.

(iv)

Non-asymptotic rate bounds.
Explicit bounds

\mathbb{E}[\left\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\right\|]\leq f(p,\rho,t)
would support theoretically
grounded architecture selection.

(v)

Adaptive rolling window.
Online adaptation of the window width
when
\Delta_{k}
is unknown
(
Gama et al. 2014
)
would remove the last free
tuning parameter from the change-point extension.

(vi)

L^{2}
perturbation bound.
A bound of the form

\left\|h_{\infty}-M_{\infty}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq g(D_{\infty})
would complement the

L^{1}
result of Proposition
3.10
.

(vii)

Broader architecture ablation.
A systematic study across
activation functions, network depth, and GRU/LSTM base architectures
would strengthen the empirical case beyond the Elman RNN used here.

(viii)

Transformer analogue.
Defining a backward filtration for
attention-based models
(
Vaswani et al. 2017
)
and extending the
quasi-reverse-martingale theory to this setting is an important direction
given the practical dominance of transformer-based sequential models.
