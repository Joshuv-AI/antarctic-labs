Dithiocarbamate derivatives: modern synthetic methods, mechanistic insights, and future perspectives
Authors: Saumy Sharma, Soumya Tripathi, Subhash Banerjee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Organic dithiocarbamates are an important class of sulfur-containing compounds with widespread applications in various fields including, medicinal chemistry, agrochemicals, materials science, and catalysis, etc. The present review article summarizes recent advancements in the synthesis of organic dithiocarbamates, highlighting diverse synthetic strategies, including multicomponent reactions, catalyst-free as well as catalyst-assisted protocols, green and sustainable approaches, and solvent-free methodologies. The scope of these methods is critically discussed with respect to substrate compatibility, reaction efficiency, and functional group tolerance. Furthermore, key challenges such as catalyst recyclability, selectivity, scalability, and environmental concerns are discussed, providing insights into future directions for the development of efficient and sustainable dithiocarbamate synthesis.

Introduction

Dithiocarbamates are an important group of sulfur-containing organic compounds with the general structure (R2N–C(S)–S). Their unique functionalization, comprising nitrogen and sulphur atoms with different alkyl or (hetero)aryl side groups, not only enhances their biological and pharmaceutical activities but also enables numerous possibilities for the fabrication of structurally diverse organic dithiocarbamate moieties.1 Interestingly, in pharmaceutics, various functionalized carbamates play various roles, such as anti-infectives,2 antifungals,3–5 antibacterials,6 antioxidants,7–9 antivirals,10–14 anti-Alzheimer's,15–17 anti-glaucoma,18 anti-tuberculosis,19–22 and anticancer agents,23,24 among others. The structures of a few representative biologically active dithiocarbamate compounds have been presented in Fig. 1. Apart from biomedical applications, they have also shown their efficiency in different fields, such as organic synthesis,25,26 environmental remediation,27–30 linkers,31,32 and accelerating agents in the vulcanization of rubber, etc.,33,34 The unique arrangements of carbon and sulphur make them great chelating agents; as a result, many reports have been made on their chelating properties with various transition and non-transition metals. These complexes have been used in applications in drug design and sensing.35 One of their key features is the ability to distribute electron density across the nitrogen and sulphur atoms, which makes them both reactive and stable. This property allows them to act as useful intermediates in organic synthesis and as strong metal-binding molecules in coordination chemistry.

A few representatives of biologically active dithiocarbamate molecules.

In recent years, the dithiocarbamate moieties have gained renewed interest in the research community because of their wide applications and the need for more sustainable and efficient synthetic methods. Majorly, the dithiocarbamate derivatives are prepared either by the reaction of alkali salts of dithiocarbamic acid with suitable electrophiles such as alkyl/aryl halides, epoxides, α,β-unsaturated compounds, aryl boronic acids, styrenes, etc., or by a single-step multicomponent approach consisting of the reaction of amines with carbon disulfide and the previously mentioned electrophiles.36–38 However, the two-step method has several practical difficulties. It requires the formation of alkali metal dithiocarbamate salts using strong bases such as NaOH or KOH, which can be challenging to handle. In addition, the resulting dithiocarbamate salts are not very stable and must be stored in dry, moisture-free conditions. As a result, factors such as solvent choice, reaction temperature, and sensitivity to air, moisture, and acidic environments become important challenges in this method.39–43

On the other hand, multicomponent reactions offer a more convenient and straightforward route for synthesizing a wide range of dithiocarbamates. These methods allow multiple reactants to combine in a single step, reducing the number of reaction steps, saving time, and improving overall efficiency. Such approaches are particularly useful for rapidly generating a wide range of compounds, which is important in fields such as drug discovery.

Despite this advantage, many methods still rely on transition metal catalysts, toxic reagents, harmful solvents, and harsh reaction conditions. They may also suffer from low yields, limited substrate scope, higher costs, and longer reaction times. Furthermore, the synthesis of dithiocarbamates often requires metal catalysts, adding another level of complexity.44–46 Moreover, several methodologies have been developed that involve reactions of amines with expensive and hazardous reagents, such as thiophosgene, perchlorophenyl carbonochloridodithioate, sulfamidate, and isothiocyanate, etc.42,47,48 As a result, researchers have been actively developing improved synthetic strategies that are safer, more efficient, and environmentally friendly. In summary, the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives has gained considerable attention due to their broad applicability in pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, materials science, and organic synthesis. Recent advancements in synthetic methodologies, by innovations in catalysis, green chemistry, and mechanistic understanding, have significantly improved the efficiency, selectivity, and sustainability of dithiocarbamate synthesis. These developments have expanded the scope of accessible dithiocarbamate derivatives while addressing several challenges associated with conventional synthetic approaches.

Motivated by the growing interest and rapid progress in this field, the present review provides a comprehensive overview on evolution of methodologies in the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives. Particular focus is placed on the progression of synthetic protocols, including their reaction scope, mechanistic features, advantages, and limitations. Furthermore, the utility of these methodologies in the preparation of structurally diverse and biologically relevant dithiocarbamate-containing compounds is discussed. In contrast to previously published reviews,36–38 this work offers a critical and up-to-date perspective on dithiocarbamate synthesis by integrating recent developments. It further identifies existing knowledge gaps and proposes future research directions for the development of more efficient and environmentally benign synthetic strategies. Overall, this review aims to serve as a valuable resource for researchers seeking to develop novel and sustainable approaches for the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives.

Literature review on the recent advancements in the synthesis of organic dithiocarbamate derivatives

As mentioned earlier, there is various kinds of strategies have been reported for the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives; however, one of the most common approach is three component reaction of amines, and carbon disulphide with appropriate electrophiles. On the basis of different electrophiles, the synthesis of various dithiocarbamate derivatives has been discussed.

Synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives by using epoxide as electrophile

There are many reports are available for the three-component synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives by the reaction of amines, carbon disulfide, and epoxides. In this regard, Li and coworkers (2003) developed a simple and highly efficient method for the preparation of 2-hydroxyalkyl dithiocarbamate derivatives by the reaction of amines, carbon disulfide, and epoxide derivatives using anhydrous K3PO4 as a base in acetone at 0 °C at room temperature via regioselective ring opening of epoxides (product 1; Scheme 2). The desired products were obtained in moderate to good yields (62–85%). This method was found to be suitable for different classes of epoxides, bearing ether and thioether functionalities side chains, and the mild reaction conditions allow the use of substrates containing delicate functional groups, as mentioned. These advantages make the present methodology more applicable. However, its failure to produce the desired products with aromatic amines and the use of acetone as a solvent are the major limitations.49

Synthesis of 2-hydroxyalkyl dithiocarbamate derivatives by amines, carbon disulfide, and epoxides.

Chaturvedi (2007) and his co-workers have developed an efficient, mild, and regioselective one-pot protocol for the synthesis of 2-hydroxy alkyl dithiocarbamates via the reaction of amines, carbon disulfide, and epoxides using Amberlite IRA 400 (basic resin) in dry DMSO at room temperature, affording high yields (78–98%) of the desired products within a reaction time of 1–1.5 h (product 2; Scheme 3). In these reactions, firstly, the amine attacks the carbon disulfide, leading to the formation of the dithiocarbamate anion, which then attacks the corresponding epoxides. The ring opening of the epoxide leads to the formation of the desired products. The advantages of the proposed method exhibit substrate versatility, mild reaction conditions, and experimental convenience. As compared to Li's method, this protocol produced high yields of the same products. However, this method was found unsuitable for the formation of N-aryl dithiocarbamate derivatives. Moreover, using dry DMSO as a solvent serves as its major drawback.50

One-pot synthesis of 2-hydroxy alkyl dithiocarbamates using Amberlite IRA 400 as a catalyst.

Ranu et al. (2008) have reported an efficient ionic liquid-catalyzed three-component protocol for the synthesis of functionalized dithiocarbamate derivatives through the reaction of various aliphatic amines, carbon disulfide (CS2), and epoxides using 1-methyl-3-pentylimidazolium bromide ([pmIm]Br), which served as both catalyst and reaction medium, at room temperature (product 3; Scheme 4). A wide range of cyclic and acyclic amines was successfully employed, affording the desired dithiocarbamates in excellent yields of 86–93% within 30 minutes. The major advantages of this protocol include very short reaction times, mild and solvent-free reaction conditions, broad substrate scope, and excellent regioselectivity. However, the aromatic amines could not be employed, representing a minor limitation of the methodology.51

[pmImBr]-catalyzed synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives by using amines, carbon disulfide and epoxides.

Movassagh and group (2012) developed a facile, convenient, and efficient protocol for the one-pot reaction of various aliphatic/aromatic amines and CS2 with various symmetric and asymmetric epoxides in ethanol at room temperature to produce the dithiocarbamate derivatives with poor to excellent yields (12–95%) under the catalyst-free conditions within the duration of 4–120 minutes (product 4–5; Scheme 5). Notably, in most of the cases of asymmetric epoxides, the nucleophile attacked the less hindered carbon of the epoxide selectively to form the product (4); however, in the case of styrene oxide, an aromatic epoxide, no selectivity was observed, and the reaction led to a mixture of products (4) and (5). This observation clearly indicates the dominance of steric factors over the electronic factors in the case of aliphatic epoxides. It was also observed that the reaction of aniline led to a poor yield (12%) of the desired product, even after a significantly longer reaction duration. Overall, this protocol provided high yields of the desired products (except with aniline) with high regioselectivity under the mild and catalyst-free reaction conditions within a short reaction period, which marks several advantages of the present protocol. However, the lack of selectivity with aromatic epoxides serves as a drawback for the reaction. As compared to Ranu's methodology, in case of styrene oxide, this protocol produced a lower yield for the product, formed by nucleophilic attack at the less-hindered carbon atom.52

Highly regioselective synthesis of 2-hydroxy alkyl dithiocarbamates by the reaction of amines, carbon disulfide, and epoxides.

Azizi et al. (2012) have developed a highly efficient, green, catalyst-free, and one-pot methodology for the synthesis of dithiocarbamates via a three-component reaction of amines, carbon disulfide (CS2), and epoxides in deep eutectic solvent (choline chloride–urea) or polyethylene glycol (PEG) at room temperature (product 6; Scheme 6). The desired dithiocarbamate products were obtained in excellent yields (up to 97%) within short reaction times (60–180 minutes). The protocol exhibits a broad substrate scope, accommodating various primary, secondary, and sterically hindered amines along with a wide range of epoxides, and also demonstrates successful gram-scale synthesis and recyclability of the green solvent system without significant loss of activity. The advantages of this method include catalyst-free conditions, use of environmentally benign and recyclable green solvents, mild reaction conditions, high efficiency, short reaction times, and simple work-up procedures, making it an attractive and sustainable approach for dithiocarbamate synthesis. However, limitations such as the requirement of specific green solvent systems (DES/PEG) and slightly reduced yields upon multiple recycling.53

Reaction of amines, carbon disulfide, and epoxides, leading to the formation of dithiocarbamate derivatives by Azizi et al.53

Halimehjani and his research group (2017) have developed a facile and efficient procedure for the synthesis of various categories of N,S-heterocycles under solvent-free and catalyst-free conditions (product 7–11; Scheme 7). Firstly, the substituted thiazole-2(3H)-thione derivatives (I) were synthesized by the reaction of primary amines, carbon disulfide, and nitroepoxides in THF at room temperature within 2 h to produce the desired products in moderate to good yields (68–90%). Interestingly, under similar conditions, these starting materials in water instead of THF, as a solvent, produced moderate to good yields (67–81%) of thaizolidine-2-thiones (II). Apart from these, the reaction of secondary amines, carbon disulfide, and nitroepoxide in water at room temperature produced dithiocarbamate-substituted phenyl-2-propanones (III) with satisfactory yields (up to 90%). Further, the authors have performed the reaction of different S-alkyl dithiocarbamates with nitroepoxide derivatives under solvent-free and catalyst-free conditions, which led to the synthesis of 2-(alkylsulfanyl)thiazoles (IV) in satisfactory to excellent yields (78–93%), whilst performing the same reaction in THF in the presence of DBU led to the satisfactory yields (74–80%) of 1-(alkylsulfanyl)-1-phenylpropan-2-ones (V). This methodology offers the synthesis of a diverse range of N,S-containing heterocycles with appreciable amounts of the yields using mild and green reaction conditions, within a very short reaction period, which makes this protocol multidimensional for their practical application from the view of organic synthesis.54

Synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives by using α,β-unsaturated compounds as electrophile

This section discusses various methodologies for the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives, which have utilized α,β-unsaturated compounds as electrophiles, in this regard, Guo and his group (2001) have developed an efficient and mild one-pot protocol for the synthesis of β-electron-withdrawing group substituted ethyl dithiocarbamates via the conjugated addition of primary or secondary amines and carbon disulfide to electrophilic alkenes in the presence of anhydrous potassium phosphate (K3PO4) in methanol at low temperature (<10 °C) followed by room temperature, affording good yield (65–97%) of the desired products within 0.5–4 h (product 12; Scheme 8). The advantages of the proposed method include mild reaction conditions, relatively short reaction time, and efficient one-pot synthesis. In addition, the protocol shows good substrate scope with various electrophilic alkenes containing electron-withdrawing groups such as –CN, –CO2Et, –CONH2, and –NO2. Saidi and co-workers (2006) reported a green, efficient, and straightforward methodology for the one-pot synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives through the reaction of amines, carbon disulfide, and α,β-unsaturated compounds in water, which acts as both a solvent and a promoter, at room temperature within 5–18 h. The desired products were obtained in moderate to excellent yields (55–96%) (product 13; Scheme 9). However, secondary amines, including pyrrolidine, piperidine, and diethylamine, produced higher yields than primary amines. Moreover, primary amines and hindered amines also underwent efficient addition with Michael acceptors to give monoadducts. The major advantage of this protocol is its simple procedure, avoiding toxic organic solvents and catalysts. However, the methodology was found to be inefficient for the transformation of aromatic amines, which represents a significant limitation of the present protocol.56

One-pot synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives in water.

Ranu et al. (2008) reported an efficient ionic liquid-mediated three-component protocol for the synthesis of functionalized dithiocarbamate derivatives (product 14; Scheme 10(a)) via the reaction of various aliphatic amines, carbon disulfide (CS2), and α,β-unsaturated compounds. The reactions were carried out at room temperature using 1-methyl-3-pentylimidazolium bromide ([pmIm]Br), which served a dual role as both the catalyst and the reaction medium, and were completed within 30 min. A diverse range of cyclic and acyclic aliphatic amines was successfully transformed into the corresponding dithiocarbamates in excellent yields (75–98%). A plausible mechanism was proposed involving activation of CS2 by the imidazolium cation of [pmIm]Br, leading to the generation of dithiocarbamate anions, which then undergo Michael addition to form the final product (Scheme 10(b)). The protocol offers several notable advantages, including short reaction times, mild and solvent-free reaction conditions, and broad substrate scope. Nevertheless, its applicability is restricted to aliphatic amines, as aromatic amines were found to be incompatible under the optimized reaction conditions, representing a limitation of the methodology.51

Yadav and co-workers (2009) have developed a facile, highly stereoselective, green, and one-pot three-component protocol for the synthesis of (E)- and (Z)-allyl dithiocarbamates via the reaction of Baylis–Hillman (BH) acetate derivatives, amines, and carbon disulfide in water, which plays a dual role, as a solvent and promoter at room temperature within 6–10 h, affording high yields (80–94%) of the desired products under the catalyst free conditions (product 15; Scheme 11). Notably, it was observed that methyl acrylate produced (E)-allyl dithiocarbamates, while acrylonitrile produced (Z)-allyl dithiocarbamates, selectively. Moreover, the authors have claimed the formation of 1,3-thiazine derivatives via an intramolecular cyclization by the reaction of allyl dithiocarbamates derived from benzyl amine in the presence of potassium carbonate as a base in methanol. This one-pot protocol avoids the use of bases and toxic organic solvents by utilizing water. Also, it demonstrates synthetic utility by further transformation of the formed products into 1,3-thiazine derivatives, which are of high biological importance. Collectively, these aspects make the protocol green and sustainable; however, the dependence on specific Baylis–Hillman substrates, an uncommon starting material, may limit its general applicability.57

Selective synthesis of (E)- or (Z)-allyl dithiocarbamates by Yadav and his group.

Nayeri et al.58 (2011) reported an ultrasound-assisted, green, and efficient methodology for the preparation of dithiocarbamate derivatives from the one-pot reaction of amines, carbon disulfide, and α,β-unsaturated alkenes to produce the moderate to excellent yields of the desired products (60–95%) (product 16; Scheme 12).

Ultrasound-assisted synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives by Nayeri et al., using amines, carbon disulfide, and α,β-unsaturated compounds.

The green, catalyst-free, low-cost protocol, with great selectivity, was one of the key advantages of the present methodology, which makes this method a useful and attractive strategy in view of economic and environmental advantages; however, no efforts have been made for the synthesis of N-aryl dithiocarbamate derivatives. As compared to Ranu's methodology for the similar conversion, this protocol provide a lower yield of the products.58

Banerji and co-workers (2011) have developed an expedient, efficient, and environmentally benign one-pot protocol for the synthesis of organic dithiocarbamate derivatives using amines, carbon disulfide (CS2), and various Michael acceptors using basic nanocrystalline MgO as a heterogeneous catalyst in water at room temperature, affording excellent yields (up to 95%) with a great functional group tolerance within the short reaction period (0.5–2 h). The scope and generality of the conditions with other substrates were examined by using a variety of primary and secondary amines and different electrophiles, such as methyl acrylate, acrylonitrile, benzylidene acetophenone, 4-nitrobenzylidine, etc. (product 17; Scheme 13). The advantages of the protocol include its mild reaction conditions, cleanliness, fast reaction period, and involvement of water as the green solvent. Moreover, the authors have recycled the catalyst up to three cycles without significant loss of yield.59

MgO-nanoparticles employed in the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives by the Michael-type addition pathway.

Taimoori and co-workers (2012) have developed an efficient, versatile, and eco-friendly one-pot methodology for the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives via the Michael addition of alkyl/aryl amines, carbon disulfide, and electron-deficient alkenes in the presence of OH−/silica as a basic catalyst under solvent-free conditions at 70 °C for about 10 h. The desired products were obtained in good to excellent yields (55–93%) (product 18; Scheme 14). Notably, this catalyst was found to be sufficiently potent for the synthesis of the desired products from comparatively low nucleophilic aryl amines, making it a useful and attractive strategy for the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives via multicomponent reactions. Though the reactions were clean and the operational procedure was simple, experimental procedures, however, moderate yields with certain substrates, such as sterically hindered amines or less reactive Michael acceptors, may present some limitations.60

Hydroxide-supported silica-catalyzed synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives under solvent-free conditions.

Azizi and co-workers (2012) reported a highly efficient, catalyst-free, and environmentally benign one-pot protocol for the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives through a three-component reaction of amines, carbon disulfide (CS2), and α,β-unsaturated compounds, in deep eutectic solvent (choline chloride–urea) or polyethylene glycol (PEG) at room temperature (product 19; Scheme 15). The methodology afforded the corresponding dithiocarbamate products in excellent yields (72–97%) within relatively short reaction times (50–120 min). A wide variety of primary, secondary, and sterically hindered amines, as well as α,β-unsaturated compounds such as methyl acrylate, acrylonitrile, etc., were well tolerated, demonstrating the broad applicability of the protocol. Moreover, the method was successfully extended to gram-scale synthesis, and the green solvent system could be recycled several times with only a marginal reduction in catalytic performance. The protocol offers several notable advantages, including catalyst-free reaction conditions, the use of recyclable and environmentally benign solvents, mild operating conditions, high product yields, rapid reaction rates, and straightforward product isolation. These features make the methodology an attractive and sustainable strategy for the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives. Nevertheless, the reliance on specific green solvent systems (DES or PEG) and the slight decline in product yields after repeated solvent recycling represent minor limitations of the protocol.53

Synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives by the Michael-type addition pathway by Azizi and group.

Bijanzadeh and co-workers (2013) have developed a novel, green, mild, and efficient protocol for the one-pot, three-component synthesis of functionalized dithiocarbamates via the reaction of primary/secondary amines, carbon disulfide, and β-nitrostyrene derivatives under solvent, and catalyst-free (neat) conditions at room temperature, affording good to high yields (65–95%) of the desired products within 2–8 h (product 20; Scheme 16(a)). Mechanistic studies suggest that the reaction occurs via Michael addition of in situ-generated dithiocarbamic acid with nitrostyrene derivatives (Scheme 16(b)). The advantages of the proposed protocol include solvent-free, environmentally benign, and simple reaction conditions, utilizing readily available starting materials within a short reaction period. Moreover, the evaluation of antifungal activities of these synthesized dithiocarbamate compounds was also demonstrated, and strong and moderate antifungal activities have been observed against pathogenic yeast and pathogenic mould, respectively.61

Banerjee and group (2015) have demonstrated an expeditious, novel, and green protocol for the synthesis of N-aryl dithiocarbamate derivatives in moderate to excellent yields (65–96%) via one-pot three-component reaction of aromatic amines, carbon disulfide, and α,β-unsaturated alkenes using several mesoporous copper catalysts such as Cu-MCM-41, Cu-MCM-48, and Cu-SBA-15. The reactions were performed in ethanol at room temperature within 30–90 minutes (product 21; Scheme 17). Moreover, the efficiency of the aforementioned copper catalysts was also compared, and Cu-MCM-48 was shown to have the highest catalytic efficiency, probably due to the 3D network of pores, which facilitated the interaction between the reagents. The major advantage of this protocol was the use of green reaction conditions and higher isolated yields; however, comparatively lower yields were obtained for electron-withdrawing and sterically hindered amines. Moreover, the authors have not explored the substrate scope well.62

Cu-MCM-41-catalyzed synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives employing the aromatic amines.

Schluter and group (2016) reported a novel, green, efficient, mild, catalyst- and solvent-free methodology for one-pot, three-component synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives at room temperature, starting from readily available amines, CS2, and vinyl pyridines and vinyl pyrazine with complete regioselectivity (anti-Markovnikov addition), yielding good to excellent yields (60–98%) of the desired products (product 22; Scheme 18). The main advantages of this work include performing the reaction under solvent- and catalyst-free conditions at ambient temperature, excellent yield of the desired products with complete regiospecificity, green reaction conditions, and a simple work-up procedure, which makes it a useful and attractive strategy in combinatorial chemistry. However, no reaction was observed when it was performed using pyrrolidine as the amine derivative.63

Highly regioselective preparation of dithiocarbamate derivatives by following anti-Markovnikov addition.

Li and group (2016) have reported a novel, catalyst-free, one-pot three-component approach for the efficient synthesis of S-vinyl dithiocarbamate derivatives through the reaction of electron-deficient allenes, various primary and secondary amines, and carbon disulfide in dichloromethane under catalyst-free conditions at room temperature within 10–30 minutes to produce the corresponding products in excellent yields ranging from 76–82% (product 23; Scheme 19(a)). A broad substrate scope was demonstrated using various cyclic and acyclic amines, such as benzylamines, heteroaryl-substituted amines, with differently substituted electron-deficient allenes, producing the corresponding products with excellent regioselectivity and stereoselectivity. To understand the mechanism of this transformation, several controlled reactions and NOESY analysis was performed, which suggested that the reaction proceeds via initial formation of a dithiocarbamate anion from the amine and CS2, followed by regioselective Michael-type nucleophilic addition to the central carbon atom of the electron-deficient allene, resulting in the formation of an allylic intermediate, followed by the proton transfer to furnish the thermodynamically favored S-vinyl dithiocarbamate (Scheme 19(b)). The methodology offers several significant advantages, including catalyst-free operation, mild room-temperature conditions, exceptionally short reaction times, high product yields, excellent regio- and stereoselectivity, with broad substrate scope and good tolerance to different functionalities. However, the reaction is mainly limited to electron-deficient allenes and requires dichloromethane as solvent, which diminishes its environmental friendliness.64

(a) The schematic representation of the reactions of amines, carbon disulfide, with allenes, leading to the formation of S-vinyl dithiocarbamate derivatives and (b) a plausible mechanism for the formation of S-vinyl dithiocarbamates.

For the very first time, Shabanian and his group (2019) have developed a simple, convenient and catalyst-free protocol for the conjugate additions of dithiocarbamic acid salts to ortho-quinone methides (o-QMs) to prepare the corresponding dithiocarbamate derivatives (products 24–26; Scheme 20). Firstly, the (o-QMs) were synthesized via the reaction of quinone-like moieties with formaldehyde by the Knoevenagel condensation, which then undergoes a 1,4-Michael-type addition with dithiocarbamic acid salts (prepared in a separate vessel by the reaction of secondary amines and carbon disulfide) in water at room temperature (Scheme 21). The products were obtained within 4–8 h in moderate to good yields (70–95%). The advantages of the proposed method include the use of water as a green solvent, its mild reaction conditions, and a straightforward procedure. In addition, the method avoids the use of oxidants and harsh conditions, making it environmentally friendly and operationally simple, and also the products were isolated very easily by a simple filtration procedure. However, the use of primary amines such as propyl amine, cyclohexylamine, and benzylamine, under the similar reaction conditions produced the undesired dimer adduct, dicoumarol (A), as major product (product 27; Scheme 20). This clearly indicates that this protocol is not suitable for the conversion of the primary amines, which serves as a major drawback.65

A plausible mechanism for the production of dithiocarbamate derivatives via the formation of o-quinone methides as an intermediate.

In continuation of his efforts for developing the green methodologies for the synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives, once again, Saha and his co-workers (2022) have developed an efficient, metal-free, one-pot two-step protocol for the direct and stereoselective synthesis of trans-styrenyl dithiocarbamate derivatives from readily available styrenes. The reactions were carried out using various primary and secondary amines with styrenes using molecular bromine as a catalyst in the presence of triethylamine as a base at 65 °C in MeCN, to afford the desired products in good to excellent yields (74–88%) within approximately 3 h (product 28; Scheme 22(a)). A broad substrate scope was demonstrated using various electron-donating and halogen substituents bearing styrenes, along with a diverse range of cyclic and acyclic secondary amines, leading to the formation of trans-styrenyl dithiocarbamates with excellent stereoselectivity. The mechanistic studies suggest that initially, styrenes were brominated to form(1,2-dibromoethyl)benzene (I), which then undergoes a nucleophilic addition at the less hindered carbon atom by in situ generated dithiocarbamate anions (from amines and carbon disulfide), leading to the formation of (II). Now, in the presence of base, HBr gets eliminated to form the final product (III) (Scheme 22(b)). The isolation and characterization of both the intermediates, i.e., dibromide intermediate (I) and the substitution intermediate (II) provided strong experimental support for the proposed reaction pathway. The major advantages of this methodology include metal-free conditions, operational simplicity, easy availability of starting materials, good yields, high stereoselectivity, broad substrate scope, and avoidance of expensive transition-metal catalysts. However, nitro-substituted styrenes failed to transform efficiently and produced unidentified side products along with dithiocarbamate dimers, which are the major limitation of the protocol.66

(a) Synthesis of trans-styrenyl dithiocarbamate derivatives by the reaction of styrenes, molecular bromine, and dithiocarbamate derivatives under basic conditions and; (b) a plausible mechanism showing the formation of the desired products via nucleophilic attack of in situ generated dithiocarbamate ions to (1,2-dibromoethyl)benzene with elimination of HBr.

Jahan and his research group (2023) have devised a simple, efficient, and unique methodology for the synthesis of bioactive dithiocarbamate derivatives using carbon disulfide, amines, and α,β-unsaturated compounds, using ceric ammonium nitrate (CAN-5 mol%) as a Lewis acid catalyst in a PEG-400: H2O as a recyclable green solvent system at 50 °C to produce the desired products in good to excellent yields (72–96%) within the duration of 2 to 4.5 h (product 29; Scheme 23(a)). Different cyclic and acyclic primary aliphatic amines were employed in this transformation. A plausible mechanism of this transformation has been presented in Scheme 23(b). Murarka and co-workers (2021) have developed an efficient, transition-metal-free, one-pot three-component protocol for the synthesis of S-aryl dithiocarbamate derivatives via the annulation reaction of diaryliodonium triflates, primary/secondary amines, and carbon disulfide in CH2Cl2 at room temperature within about 2–4 h, affording moderate to excellent yields (48–91%) of the corresponding products (Product 87; Scheme 83). The reaction was also found efficient for the scaled-up synthesis of the products. Several reactions were also performed by the authors to understand the chemoselectivity trends for asymmetric diaryliodonium triflates (Scheme 84), where it was found that it is controlled by the electronic and steric factors of the aryl rings of the iodonium salt. Further, it was noted that under the present conditions, the aryl ring, either with more electron-withdrawing nature or having bulky groups migrate to form the final product. Mechanistic studies suggest that a ‘T’-shaped intermediate forms by the reaction of in situ generated dithiocarbamic acid and diaryliodonium triflate, which on ligand coupling results in the formation of the final product (Scheme 85). The advantages of the proposed method include avoiding of expensive and toxic transition metal, strong bases, and high temperature under the environmentally benign conditions using easily available, and inexpensive starting materials. However, the use of DCM as solvent, and moderate yields for several derivatives are some major drawbacks.112

Biju and co-workers (2021) have developed an efficient, and transition-metal-free method for the synthesis of S-aryl dithiocarbamate derivatives via an aryne-based three-component coupling of arynes, carbon disulfide (CS2) and aliphatic amines under the mild conditions using KF/18-crown-6 in THF at room temperature, affording good to excellent yields of the desired products (35–94%) (product 88; Scheme 86(a)). The present reaction proceeds via the initial addition of amines to CS2 followed by the trapping with arynes to furnish the desired products. The advantages of the present protocol include its transition-metal-free and mild reaction conditions with good functional group compatibility. Moreover, the mechanistic experiments, including density functional theory studies, are also provided. Interestingly, under the optimized reaction conditions, a unique four-component coupling with the incorporation of tetrahydrofuran was also observed with 3-triflyloxybenzynes (product 89; Scheme 86(b)). However, with aromatic amines and aliphatic tertiary amines, this protocol failed to produce the desired products. Additionally, unsymmetrical naphthalyne and similar compounds generated 2 inseparable mixture of regioisomers, which is a severe limitation to the present methodology.113

Kandasamy and co-workers (2022) have developed an efficient, straightforward, and green method for the multi-component synthesis of functionalized S-benzyl dithiocarbamate derivatives via the reaction of para-quinone methides, carbon disulfide, and secondary amines in DCM at room temperature under catalyst- and additive-free conditions (product 90; Scheme 87(a)). The desired products were obtained in moderate yields (82–94%) within a short reaction time of about 30 minutes. Mechanistic studies suggest that the product forms via a cation intermediate (Scheme 87(b)). The advantages of the proposed method include mild reaction conditions, relatively short reaction times, broad substrate scope, and ease of operation, which are important features for gram-scale synthesis and make it useful for preparing structurally diverse S-benzyl dithiocarbamates.114

Once again, a metal- and base-free multicomponent strategy for the synthesis of biologically relevant S-diarylmethane dithiocarbamates was proposed by Murarka et al. (2022), using p-quinone methides, carbon disulfide, and amines. The reactions were performed at room temperature in DCM, which produced the desired products in excellent yields (74–97%) within an hour (product 91; Scheme 88(a)). The protocol exhibited broad substrate scope, with various primary, secondary, cyclic, acyclic, and chiral amines, as well as structurally diverse p-quinone methides. Mechanistic studies suggest that, initially, amines and carbon disulfide react to form dithiocarbamic acid, which then undergoes 1,6-conjugate addition with p-quinone methides to form the intermediate (A). Finally, proton transfer leads to the formation of the corresponding dithiocarbamate derivatives (Scheme 88(b)). In addition, several synthesized compounds demonstrated promising antiproliferative activities, highlighting the biological significance of the methodology.115

Chen (2022) and his research group have developed a novel, safe, ecofriendly, triflic acid (TfOH)-catalyzed methodology for the three-component synthesis of dithiocarbamate derivatives by the reaction of α-diazoesters with CS2 and pyrrolidine under continuous flow conditions in good yields (28–99%) within 10 minutes at room temperature, under the continuous flow reaction conditions (products 92–94; Scheme 89). A plausible mechanism of the transformation has been provided in Scheme 90, which suggest that firstly, the α-diazo compound get decomposed in the presence of triflic acid to produce the ionic pair (I). This pair get attacked by pyrrolidine-1-1-dithiocarbamic acid (II), generated from the reaction of carbon disulfide (2), and pyrrolidine (3a), to form the ionic pair (III). Finally, the S–H insertion product 4a forms via the deprotonation of (III), along with the regeneration of the catalyst TfOH. The advantages include a wide substrate scope of diazo compounds with high functional group tolerance. However, the yields of several substrates were significantly poor, which serve as major drawback.116

Saha and co-workers (2023) have developed a novel, efficient, environmentally benign, and metal-free one-pot two-step protocol for the synthesis of gem-bis(dithiocarbamates) (product 95; Scheme 91(a)). The reaction occurs via the trifunctionalization of phenylacetylenes with molecular bromine in methanol at room temperature, followed by the addition of in situ generated dithiocarbamate salts (from amines and carbon disulfide) in the presence of triethylamine as a base in acetonitrile with a small amount of water at 65 °C, affording good to excellent yields (78–90%) of the desired products within about 2–3 h. The plausible mechanism was also reported, which is presented in Scheme 91(b). The advantages of the proposed method include metal-free conditions, an efficient one-pot strategy, and easily available starting materials. However, the use of molecular bromine poses safety and handling concerns due to its corrosive and toxic nature. Purification by column chromatography is required, and excess bromine can lead to side reactions such as dithiocarbamate dimer formation, which affect yield and are the major limitation of this methodology.117

(2023) have developed a one-pot protocol for the efficient synthesis of S-organyl-N-butyl dithiocarbamates via the reaction involving two stages: firstly, the reaction of butylamine with carbon disulfide using NaOH as a base in diethylether, followed by the addition of 2-chloroethanol and benzoyl chloride, each in a separate reaction, at room temperature, affording 83% and 92% of the desired products, respectively, within 3 hours (products 96–97; Scheme 92). Moreover, the protocol demonstrates practical application of the products in the flotation of gold-bearing ores as a float-collecting agent, which highlights their industrial relevance. The advantages of the proposed method include a simple one-pot procedure, mild reaction conditions, and good yields. Xie and his co-workers (2024) have developed a novel, convenient, and catalyst-free method for the multicomponent synthesis of S-alkyl dithiocarbamate derivatives via a three-component reaction of cyclic sulfonium salts, carbon disulfide (CS2), and aliphatic amines under mild reaction conditions in DMSO at room temperature, under aerobic conditions to produce good to excellent yields (up to 93%) of the desired products with excellent tolerance to different functionalities (product 98; Scheme 93(a)). The scope of the reaction was explored widely using various sulfonium salts, such as alkenyl, aryl, and alkynyl sulfonium salts, with different cyclic and acyclic secondary aliphatic amines. The synthetic applications of the present methodology were also explored for the functionalization of several drugs, such as menthol, lbuprofen, adamantanemethanol, ketoprofen, and amoxapine, etc (products 99–103; Scheme 94). The mechanistic studies suggest that the reaction proceeds via nucleophilic ring-opening of sulfonium salts by in situ generated dithiocarbamic acid from the reaction of amine and carbon disulfide (Scheme 93(b)). The advantages of the protocol include broad substrate scope, high functional group tolerance, readily available starting materials, ease of operation, gram-scale synthesis, and late-stage functionalization. Collectively, these make the reaction quite attractive in pharmaceutical chemistry and organic synthesis. However, this method could not form the desired products starting from aromatic amines, which serves as major limitation of this methodology.119

Yan and co-workers (2025) have developed a rapid and efficient catalyst-free method for the multicomponent synthesis of S-vinyl dithiocarbamate derivatives using vinyl sulfonium salts, carbon disulfide (CS2), and amines in DCE at room temperature, affording moderate to excellent yields (60–93%) of the desired products within the reaction time of about 2 h (product 104; Scheme 95(a)). The reaction proceeds via nucleophilic ring-opening of the vinyl sulfonium salt by in situ generated dithiocarbamic acid, from the reaction of amine and carbon disulfide (Scheme 95(b). The protocol demonstrates a broad substrate scope, tolerating a wide variety of cyclic and acyclic amines as well as differently substituted vinyl sulfonium salts, along with good functional group compatibility and successful gram-scale synthesis. The advantages of this method include catalyst- and additive-free conditions, mild reaction environment, short reaction time, operational simplicity, and high efficiency, making it an attractive and sustainable strategy for dithiocarbamate synthesis. However, the use of chlorinated solvents (DCE) may limit their environmental friendliness, which is the major drawback of this methodology.120

Saha and co-workers (2026) have developed a metal-free, efficient, and stereoselective method for the synthesis of Z-styrenyl-1,2-bis dithiocarbamate derivatives via the reaction of alkynyl bromides with in situ generated dithiocarbamic acids (from secondary amines and carbon disulfide) in THF at 65 °C within 5 h under a nitrogen atmosphere, affording good to excellent yields (60–85%) of the desired products (product 105; Scheme 96). The reaction proceeds through a concerted nucleophilic substitution at the C(sp)–Br bond to form an alkynyl dithiocarbamate intermediate, followed by neighboring-group-assisted addition of another dithiocarbamic acid, leading to stereoselective Z-product formation, as supported by DFT studies. The protocol shows broad substrate scope, tolerating various (hetero)aryl-substituted alkynyl bromides with both electron-donating and electron-withdrawing substituents at different positions, with both cyclic and acyclic secondary amines, and successful gram-scale synthesis. Moreover, biologically active moieties-tethered alkynyl bromides, such as (−)-menthol and diacetone-d-glucose, were also produced, the desired products with good yields, with (−)-menthol and d-glucose units unaffected. The advantages of this method include catalyst- and additive-free conditions, stereoselectivity, operational simplicity, and good efficiency, making it a practical and eco-friendly approach for synthesizing bi-functionalized dithiocarbamates. However, limitations such as the incompatibility of aliphatic alkynyl bromides, requirement of an inert atmosphere, moderate yields in some cases, and dependence on pre-formed alkynyl bromides may restrict its broader applicability.121

Conclusions

In conclusion, dithiocarbamate derivatives play a key role in various fields, including medicine, material science, coordination chemistry, industry, and agriculture. Throughout this review, various methodologies, catalytic systems, and reaction conditions have been explored, used for the synthesis of these moieties. This review discusses recent advancements in the development of efficient and versatile synthetic methodologies, including multicomponent reactions, catalyst-assisted protocols, transition-metal-catalyzed transformations, ionic liquid-mediated processes, photocatalytic approaches, and other strategies, by critically analyzing research work performed in this field.

Despite these achievements, several challenges remain, including the use of hazardous reagents, limited catalyst recyclability, substrate-specific limitations, and difficulties associated with large-scale industrial implementation. Furthermore, the development of highly selective, sustainable, and economically viable protocols continues to be an important research objective. Overall, the rapid evolution of synthetic methodologies highlights the growing importance of organic dithiocarbamates and provides a strong foundation for future innovations in this dynamic field.
