Abstract
In recent years, locally repairable codes (LRCs) have attracted considerable attention owing to their pivotal role in distributed storage systems. Since binary linear locally repairable codes can significantly reduce the complexity of both encoding and decoding processes, the construction of binary LRCs has attracted extensive research interest. In this paper, we construct locally repairable codes via concatenated codes and present a systematic approach to select outer codes to obtain optimal binary LRCs, where the outer codes are linear codes over 𝔽 4 \mathbb{F}_{4} . The weight distributions of the resulting LRCs are determined by the weight distributions of the selected linear codes over 𝔽 4 \mathbb{F}_{4} . Furthermore, several classes of optimal binary locally repairable codes are constructed, including binary LRCs meeting the Griesmer-like bound, and binary perfect LRCs. Meanwhile, for the locality r = 2 r=2 , we improve the Johnson-like bound for binary LRCs with disjoint local repair groups established by Ma and Ge, and construct explicit LRCs that attain this new bound.



keywords
Locally repairable codes, Concatenated codes, Weight distributions, Griesmer-like bound, Perfect LRCs, Nearly perfect LRCs.

Introduction

Distributed storage systems, such as data centers, store data in a redundant form to ensure reliability in the event of node failures. Erasure codes are widely utilized in distributed storage systems. Locally repairable codes (LRCs), proposed by Gopalan
et al.

11
, are a significant class of erasure codes widely utilized in distributed storage systems where they offer notable benefits such as low storage cost and efficient data recovery. LRCs are also used for coded distributed computing to encode distributed tasks in a linear matrix-vector multiplication problem, as demonstrated in
7
.

In this context, let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d]
linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is a subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with dimension
k
and minimum distance
d
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is called a locally repairable code with locality
r
if any failed code symbol can be recovered by accessing at most
r
other code symbols.
Moreover,
r
should be designed to be significantly smaller than
k
. At the same time, larger values of
k
and
d
are desirable to achieve high storage efficiency and strong global fault tolerance, respectively. Indeed, there exists a tradeoff among these parameters. Gopalan
et al.

11
proved that the minimum distance of an LRC is upper bounded by

d\leq n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2.

(1.1)

Since then, numerous scholars have constructed various
d
-optimal LRCs achieving the Singleton-like bound (
1.1
) (see
27
;
17
;
14
;
19
;
3
).

Binary LRCs are of particular interest because they do not require multiplications during encoding, decoding, or repair processes. Hao
et al.

13
proved that there exist only 5 classes of binary LRCs that meet the bound (
1.1
).

However, the Singleton-like bound does not take into account the size of the finite field. As a result, this bound may not be tight for binary LRCs. There has been extensive research on binary locally repairable codes (see
15
;
24
;
18
;
28
;
20
).

Cadambe and Mazumdar
2
proposed the
C
-
M
bound for LRCs, which is the first field size dependent bound, as follows:

k\leq\min_{\tau\in Z_{+}}[\tau r+k_{\mathrm{opt}}^{q}(n-\tau(r+1),d)]

(1.2)

where
\tau\leq\min\{\left\lceil\frac{n}{r+1}\right\rceil,\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil\}
, and
k_{\mathrm{opt}}^{q}(n^{\prime},d^{\prime})
denotes the largest possible dimension of a
q
-ary linear code with length
n^{\prime}
and minimum distance
d^{\prime}
. Hao
et al.

13
also derived several field size-dependent bounds for LRCs based on parity check matrices, including the Griesmer-like bound. In
10
, Gao and Fang constructed optimal locally repairable codes meeting the Griesmer-like bound by lengthening Reed–Muller codes.

In
28
, Wang
et al.
proposed a sphere-packing approach for deriving an upper bound on the dimension
k
of
[n,k,d]
LRCs with locality
r
. Through specific comparisons, it was demonstrated that this bound outperforms the
C
-
M
bound in certain cases.
Building on
28
, Fang
et al.

5
introduced the concept of perfect LRCs and constructed several classes of such codes. They further constructed perfect LRCs using cyclic and constacyclic codes in
6
.
In
20
, Ma and Ge established an explicit bound on the dimension
k
of binary LRCs by employing the similar argument for deriving the Johnson bound for binary codes in
(
16
, Theorem 2.3.8)
.

Meanwhile, the weight distribution plays an important role in the decoding processes of linear codes. It is known in
25
that the decoding error probability of a linear code over an erasure channel under maximum likelihood decoding can be expressed in terms of its weight distribution.

In this paper, we construct locally repairable codes via
concatenated codes and present a systematic approach to select outer codes to obtain optimal LRCs, where the outer codes are linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. The weight distributions of LRCs are determined by the weight distributions of the chosen linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. Furthermore, we construct several classes of optimal binary locally repairable codes including the Griesmer-like LRCs and perfect LRCs. Meanwhile, for
r=2
, we improve the Johnson-like bound for binary LRCs with disjoint local repair groups established by Ma and Ge, and construct explicit LRCs that attain this new Johnson-like bound.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section II, we introduce some notations and basic concepts related to concatenated codes, LRCs and projective geometry. In Section III, we propose a systematic method to construct binary LRCs via concatenated codes, where the outer codes are linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, and present the relationship between their weight distributions. In Section IV, we construct optimal binary LRCs attaining the Griesmer-like bound. In Section V, we construct perfect LRCs. In Section VI, for the case
r=2
, we sharpen the bound established by Ma and Ge and construct explicit LRCs that attain this new bound. In Section VII, we conclude this paper.

In this section, we give some notations and basic concepts related to concatenated codes, LRCs and projective geometry.

Let

It is well known that the minimum distance of

are linearly independent and there exist
d
columns of

which are linearly dependent.

We generally denote the weight distribution of a code
\mathcal{C}
by
A_{0}(\mathcal{C})
,

,

Concatenated codes
8
form a fundamental class of error-correcting codes in coding theory
that combine two or more simpler codes to achieve high levels of data reliability with manageable computational complexity.

We describe the construction of a concatenated code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{in}}
be an
[n,k,d]
inner linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
\mathcal{C}
be an
[N,K,D]
outer linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. Let f be a one-to-one mapping from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{in}}
. Then the corresponding concatenated code

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{(f(c_{1})|f(c_{2})|\dots|f(c_{N}))\mid\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{N})\in\mathcal{C}\}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is an
[nN,kK,\geq dD]
linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. More details can be found in
8
;
23
.

Next, we give the definition of locally repairable codes.

Definition 2.1
.

The
i
-th code symbol
c_{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)
of a linear code
\mathcal{C}
is said to have the
r
-locality if there exists a subset
S_{i}\subset[n]
with
|S_{i}|\leq r+1
such that
i\in S_{i}
and
\mathbf{c}_{i}
can be recovered by
\{c_{j}\},j\in S_{i}\setminus\{i\}
; i.e.,
c_{i}
is a linear combination of
\{c_{j}\},j\in S_{i}\setminus\{i\}
. The set
S_{i}
is called a repair group for
c_{i}
.

An
[n,k,d]
linear code
\mathcal{C}
is said to have
r
-locality if all code symbols of
\mathcal{C}
have
r
-locality, denoted as
[n,k,d;r]
-LRC.

Lemma 2.1.1

(
13
)
.

A linear code
\mathcal{C}
has locality
r
if and only if for every
1\leq i\leq n
, there exists a codeword
\mathbf{v}
in
\mathcal{C}^{\bot}
whose support set
\mathrm{supp}(\mathbf{v})
contains
i
and the size of
\mathrm{supp}(\mathbf{v})
is at most
r+1
.

Next, we provide an overview of the key concepts of projective geometry that are relevant to our results.

Definition 2.2
.

Consider two nonzero vectors
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n+1}

, s.t.

\mathbf{u}
is equivalent to
\mathbf{v}
. The set of equivalence classes is the
n
-dimensional projective space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, denoted by

.

Refer to the elements of

as points. For a nonzero vector

, we denote

as the point containing the vector A.

A subspace of dimension

Definition 2.3

A

Throughout this paper, we focus on binary LRCs with disjoint local repair groups.

An
[n,k,d;2]
binary LRC
\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{h}_{1},\mathbf{h}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{h}_{n})=(\mathbf{h}_{1}^{0},\mathbf{h}_{1}^{1},\mathbf{h}_{1}^{2},\mathbf{h}_{2}^{0},\mathbf{h}_{2}^{1},\mathbf{h}_{2}^{2},\dots,\mathbf{h}_{\ell}^{0},\mathbf{h}_{\ell}^{1},\mathbf{h}_{\ell}^{2})

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc|ccc|c|ccc}1&1&1&&&&&&&\\
&&&1&1&1&&&&\\
&&&&&&\ddots&&&\\
&&&&&&&1&1&1\\
\hline\cr\mathbf{0}_{u}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{1,1}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{1,2}^{T}&\mathbf{0}_{u}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{2,1}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{2,2}^{T}&\dots&\mathbf{0}_{u}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{\ell,1}^{T}&\mathbf{e}_{\ell,2}^{T}\par\end{array}\right),

where
n=3\ell
,
n-k=\ell+u
,
\mathbf{h}_{m}^{T}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell+u}
for

,
(\mathbf{h}_{i}^{j})^{T}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell+u}

\mathbf{e}_{i,j^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{u}

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}

if and only if
\dim(\sum_{j=1}^{t}\mathcal{W}_{m_{j}})=2t

Proof.

Note that
\dim(\sum_{j=1}^{t}\mathcal{W}_{m_{j}})=2t
for all distinct
m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{t}\in[\ell]

2t

\mathbf{e}_{m_{1},1},\mathbf{e}_{m_{1},2},\mathbf{e}_{m_{2},1},\mathbf{e}_{m_{2},2},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{m_{t},1},\mathbf{e}_{m_{t},2}

if and only if any

2t+1

are linearly independent.

For any choice of
2t+1
distinct columns
\mathbf{h}_{j_{1}},\mathbf{h}_{j_{2}},\dots,\mathbf{h}_{j_{2t+1}}

\lambda_{1}\mathbf{h}_{j_{1}}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{h}_{j_{2}}+\dots+\lambda_{2t+1}\mathbf{h}_{j_{2t+1}}=\mathbf{0}_{\ell+u},

(3.2)

where

If only one column is selected from a local repair group, without loss of generality, let

Given that we aim to prove the linear independence of any

2t+1

Suppose that two columns are selected from one local repair group.
Without loss of generality, we let
\mathbf{h}_{j_{1}},\mathbf{h}_{j_{2}}

3.2

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0

\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}

Furthermore, suppose that three columns are selected from one local repair group. Without loss of generality, we let

represent the three columns chosen from the second local repair group. Enforcing the second row of Equation (

To prove that any

4
Construction of optimal binary LRCs attaining the Griesmer-like bound

In this section, we demonstrate that certain classes of optimal linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
achieving the classical Griesmer bound can be used to construct optimal binary LRCs attaining the Griesmer-like bound.

The classical Griesmer bound for an
[n,k,d]
linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be seen as an extension of the Singleton bound, as follows:

n\geq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{i}}\right\rceil.

(4.1)

By extending the classical Griesmer bound to the
[n,k,d;r]
LRCs over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, Hao
et al.

13
derived a Griesmer-like bound, as follows:

n\geq\max_{1\leq\tau\leq\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil-1}\{\tau(r+1)+\sum_{i=0}^{k-r\tau-1}\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{i}}\right\rceil\}.

(4.2)

Theorem 4.1
.

In Construction
3.2
, let the outer code
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n_{1},k_{1},d_{1}=n_{1}-k_{1}+1]

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

[n=3n_{1},k=2k_{1},d=2(n_{1}-k_{1}+1);2]

Proof.

By the Griesmer-like bound (
4.2
) and
k_{1}<n_{1}
, take
\tau_{1}=k_{1}-1
, we can verify that

\displaystyle 3n_{1}=n

\displaystyle\geq\tau_{1}(r+1)+\sum_{i=0}^{k-\tau_{1}r-1}\left\lceil\frac{2d_{1}}{2^{i}}\right\rceil=3(k_{1}-1)+3d_{1}=3n_{1}.

∎

Meanwhile, if
\mathcal{C}
is a trivial MDS code with parameters

6
Construction of optimal binary LRCs attaining the Johnson-like bound

In this section, we demonstrate that the optimal linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
achieving the classical Johnson bound can be used to construct optimal binary LRCs attaining the Johnson-like bound.

In
(
16
, Theorem 2.3.8)
, for an
[n,k,d]
linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
d
is even, the classic Johnson bound is given as follows:

q^{k}\leq\frac{q^{n}}{\mathcal{O}_{d}+\frac{\binom{n}{d/2}(q-1)^{d/2}}{A_{q}(n,d,d/2)}},

(6.1)

where
\mathcal{O}_{d}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{i}(q-1)^{i}
and
A_{q}(n,d,d/2)
is the maximum number of codewords in a constant weight code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
n
and minimum distance at least
d
whose codewords have weight
d/2
.

In fact, since each codeword has weight
d/2
and the minimum distance is at least
d
, the supports of any two distinct codewords must be disjoint, which immediately yields that (see
(
9
, Section V)
)

A_{q}(n,d,d/2)=\lfloor\frac{n}{d/2}\rfloor.

(6.2)

The classic Johnson bound can be reformulated as

q^{k}\leq\frac{q^{n}}{\mathcal{O}_{d}+\frac{\binom{n}{d/2}(q-1)^{d/2}}{\lfloor\frac{2n}{d}\rfloor}}\triangleq\frac{q^{n}}{\mathcal{O}_{d}^{\prime}}.

(6.3)

Since
k
must be an integer, taking logarithms of (
6.3
) leads to the following bound:

k\leq n-\left\lceil\log_{q}\mathcal{O}_{d}^{\prime}\right\rceil.

(6.4)

Definition 6.1
.

An
[n,k,d]
linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
d
is even, is called a nearly perfect code if it meets the bound in (
6.3
); it is called a
k
-optimal code with respect to the Johnson bound if it meets the bound in (
6.4
).

In
20
, Ma and Ge established an explicit bound on the dimension
k
of binary LRCs with disjoint local repair groups by employing the similar argument for deriving the Johnson bound. For an
[n=3\ell,k,d=2t+2;r=2]
binary LRC with disjoint local repair groups, where
t+1
is even, the bound is given as follows:

2^{k}\leq\frac{2^{\frac{2n}{3}}}{\Omega_{d}+\frac{\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+\dots+i_{\ell}=\lfloor\frac{d}{4}\rfloor\ }\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{3}{2i_{j}}}{\lfloor\frac{2n}{d}\rfloor}}=\frac{2^{\frac{2n}{3}}}{\Omega_{d}+\frac{\binom{\ell}{d/4}3^{d/4}}{\lfloor\frac{2n}{d}\rfloor}},

(6.5)

where
\Omega_{d}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d-1}{4}\rfloor}\binom{\ell}{i}3^{i}
.

Since
k
must be an integer, taking logarithms of (
6.7
) leads to the following bound:

k\leq\frac{2}{3}n-\left\lceil\log_{2}(\Omega_{d}+\frac{\binom{\ell}{d/4}3^{d/4}}{\lfloor\frac{2n}{d}\rfloor})\right\rceil.

(6.6)

Theorem 6.2
.

For an
[n=3\ell,\;k,\;d=2t+2;\;r=2]
binary LRC with disjoint local repair groups, where
t+1
is even, the bounds (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) can be improved as follows:

2^{k}\leq\frac{2^{\frac{2n}{3}}}{\Omega_{d}+\frac{\binom{\ell}{d/4}3^{d/4}}{\lfloor\frac{4n}{3d}\rfloor}}\triangleq\frac{2^{\frac{2n}{3}}}{\Omega_{d}^{\prime}},

(6.7)

k\leq\frac{2}{3}n-\left\lceil\log_{2}\Omega_{d}^{\prime}\right\rceil.

(6.8)

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n=3\ell,k,d=2t+2;r=2]
binary LRCs with disjoint local repair groups. The
\mathcal{L}
-cover
W
of
\mathcal{C}
is the linear space spanned by the first
\ell
rows of
H
(as given in Equation (
3.1
)), and the
\mathcal{L}
-space
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{C}
is the dual of
W
. Since
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}\supseteq W
, we have
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
. Using the argument for deriving the Johnson bound
(
16
, Theorem 2.3.8)
, Ma and Ge
20
obtained the following inequality:

|\mathcal{C}|\cdot|B_{\mathcal{V}}(t)|+|\mathcal{N}|\leq|\mathcal{V}|,

(6.9)

where
B_{\mathcal{V}}(t)=|\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}\mid\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{v})\leq t\}|=\Omega_{d}
and
\mathcal{N}\triangleq\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}|d(\mathbf{v},\mathcal{C})=t+1\}
.

When
t+1
is even, let
\Gamma\triangleq\{(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x})|d(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x})=t+1,\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C},\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{N}\}
. Ma and Ge
20
showed that

|\Gamma|=|\mathcal{C}|\cdot A_{t+1}(\mathcal{V})=2^{k}\cdot\binom{\ell}{d/4}3^{d/4},

(6.10)

where
A_{t+1}(\mathcal{V})=|\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}\mid\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{v})=t+1\}|.

On the other hand, fix
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{N}
. Let
\Gamma_{\mathbf{x}}\triangleq\{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}|d(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x})=t+1\}
, we have

|\Gamma|=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{N}}|\Gamma_{\mathbf{x}}|.

(6.11)

Let
\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}\triangleq\{\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x}|d(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{x})=t+1,\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}
.
Clearly,
|\Gamma_{\mathbf{x}}|=|\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}|
and the set
\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}
forms a binary constant weight code of length
n
with constant weight
t+1
and minimum distance at least
2t+2
. Then the supports of any two vectors in
\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}
must be disjoint.

Since
\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}\subset\mathcal{V}
, the bound (
6.5
) can be further improved. Recall that
\mathcal{V}
is the dual of the linear space spanned by the first
\ell
rows of
H
. Thus every vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}
can be expressed as
\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{v}_{1}\mid\mathbf{v}_{2}\mid\dots\mid\mathbf{v}_{\ell})
, where each
\mathbf{v}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3}
satisfies that
\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{v}_{i})=0
or
2
. This structure yields
|\Gamma_{\mathbf{x}}|=|\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}|\leq\lfloor\frac{\ell}{(t+1)/2}\rfloor
. Hence, from (
6.11
) we obtain

|\mathcal{N}|\geq\frac{|\Gamma|}{\lfloor\frac{\ell}{(t+1)/2}\rfloor}=\frac{|\Gamma|}{\lfloor\frac{4n}{3d}\rfloor}.

(6.12)

Then bound (
6.7
) can be derived from (
6.9
), (
6.10
) and (
6.12
).
∎

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we construct locally repairable codes via
concatenated codes and propose a systematic framework for outer code selection to construct optimal binary LRCs, where the outer codes are linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. The weight distributions of the resulting LRCs are determined by the weight distributions of the selected linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. We demonstrate that several classes of optimal linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, those achieving the classical Griesmer bound, sphere‑packing bound, or Johnson bound, can be used to construct corresponding optimal binary LRCs that attain the Griesmer‑like bound, sphere‑packing-like bound, and Johnson‑like bound, respectively. In particular, we construct perfect and nearly perfect binary LRCs. Consequently, in practical applications, well-designed linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
