The preventive effect of feedforward control nursing on complications and adverse events during the recovery period of gynecological general anesthesia patients
Authors: Lan Sun, Yanyue Mu, Jie Liu, Jun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo explore the preventive effect of feedforward control nursing on complications and adverse events during the recovery period of gynecological general anesthesia patients.MethodsTotal 130 patients who underwent gynecological general anesthesia surgery at our hospital during January 2023 to January 2025 were selected as the research subjects. A retrospective analysis of clinical data was performed. According to different nursing methods, they were divided into a conventional group (n = 64) and a feedforward group (n = 66). The conventional group received routine nursing. The feedforward group received feedforward control nursing interventions on the basis of the conventional group. The complications, adverse nursing events, quality of general anesthesia resuscitation and nursing satisfaction were compared between the two groups. The Aldrete awakening scores at the time of entry and exit were analyzed between the two groups.ResultsThe feedforward group had fewer various complications and lower overall incidence of complications than the conventional group (P < 0.05). The feedforward group had much lower total incidence of adverse nursing events than the conventional group (P < 0.05). The feedforward group had shorter retention time, spontaneous breathing recovery time, extubation time, and awakening time than the conventional group (P < 0.05). There was no statistically significant difference in the total scores of consciousness, blood oxygen, respiration, activity, blood pressure, and physiological functions between the two groups when entering the room (P > 0.05). However, the scores of various physiological functions in both groups were higher when exiting the room than entering the room, and the feedforward group had much higher scores than the conventional group (P < 0.05). The satisfaction rate of nursing in the feedforward group was 92.42%, markedly higher than 78.13% of the conventional group (P < 0.05).ConclusionFeedforward control nursing can effectively prevent complications and adverse events during the recovery period of gynecological general anesthesia patients, have positive significance in improving overall recovery quality and nursing satisfaction and promoting patient recovery.

Introduction

In recent years, the incidence rate of gynecological diseases has continued to increase. Gynecological general anesthesia surgery has become the mainstream treatment for gynecological diseases with significant therapeutic effects, and has attracted much attention in the medical field. General anesthesia can act on the central nervous system through intravenous administration, respiratory inhalation, and intramuscular injection, achieving the goal of disappearance of spontaneous breathing and muscle relaxation, creating favorable conditions for surgical implementation (Sun et al. 2023). After general anesthesia, patients with special conditions such as incomplete recovery of consciousness, severe hemodynamic fluctuations during surgery, and respiratory depression need to be sent to the anesthesia recovery room for close monitoring and observation. The anesthesia recovery room, as an important hub to ensure the smooth passage of patients through the recovery period and maintain life safety, can detect and handle abnormal situations in a timely manner (Hinkelbein et al. 2023). After general anesthesia, patients are prone to nausea, vomiting, restlessness, hypoxemia, hypothermia, and other conditions, which can lead to adverse events such as tongue falling, falling off the bed, tube slippage, and aspiration. Research shows that postoperative complications and adverse events of general anesthesia are influenced by various factors such as the patient’s condition, anesthetic drugs, and intraoperative stress, which seriously hinder the patient’s recovery process and even pose a serious threat to their life safety (Girard et al. 2023, Guinot et al. 2023). Therefore, implementing comprehensive and effective nursing management during the general anesthesia recovery period is of great significance for improving the quality of patient recovery.

Clinical routine nursing often focuses on postoperative vital sign detection, neglecting early risk assessment, thereby limiting the development of high-quality resuscitation nursing (Park et al. 2022). Feedforward control nursing can carry out pre prevention and control of potential adverse consequences of nursing implementation, and can develop prevention strategies through multidimensional analysis of risk factors (Wang and Tang 2023). Research has shown that feedforward control has been widely applied in nursing safety management, which can effectively improve nursing quality and reduce the occurrence of adverse events (Schell et al. 2023). However, there is a lack of systematic research on feedforward control in gynecological general anesthesia patients. Its specific role in preventing complications and adverse events during the recovery period after gynecological general anesthesia needs further clarification. Unlike previous studies, this research focuses on a specific group of patients during the recovery period after gynecological general anesthesia and on the critical nursing period. The aim is to develop a systematic feedforward control nursing plan covering dimensions such as predictive position management, continuous body temperature regulation, pipeline safety maintenance, pain and psychological intervention, and to comprehensively evaluate the recovery quality using objective physiological indicators such as Aldrete awakening score. Compared with previous studies that were limited to a single complication or mainly relied on subjective evaluation, this research plans to integrate multi-dimensional nursing strategies and objective indicators, with the aim of providing scientific reference basis for clinical nursing management.

Materials and methods

General materials

Total 130 patients who underwent gynecological general anesthesia surgery at our hospital during January 2023 to January 2025 were selected as the research subjects. The inclusion process of patients was shown in Fig. 1. Retrospective analysis of clinical data of patients was conducted, and they were divided into a conventional group (n = 64) and a feedforward group (n = 66) according to different nursing methods. The age range of the conventional group was 32–58 years, with an average of (36.58 ± 5.29) years, including 22 cases of myomectomy, 20 cases of endometrial polypectomy, 17 cases of ovarian cystectomy, and 5 other cases. According to the grading criteria of the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA), there were 36 cases classified as grade I and 29 cases classified as grade II. The patient’s Body Mass Index (BMI) ranged from 19.87 to 28.67 kg/m², with an average of (23.64 ± 3.08) kg/m². The age range of the feedforward group was 35–62 years, with an average of (37.19 ± 5.08) years, including 24 cases of myomectomy, 18 cases of endometrial polypectomy, 20 cases of ovarian cystectomy, and 4 other cases. According to the grading criteria of the ASA, there were 39 cases classified as grade I and 27 cases classified as grade II. The patient’s BMI ranged from 20.19 to 29.47 kg/m², with an average of (22.97 ± 3.14) kg/m². Inclusion criteria: Patients aged between 18 and 60 years old; All patients met the surgical and anesthesia related indications and voluntarily undergo gynecological surgery under general anesthesia; The patient had normal cognition, good reading and writing abilities, and no communication barriers; The patient’s clinical data was complete. Exclusion criteria: Patients with severe failure of important organs such as the heart, liver, and kidneys; Patients with combined malignant tumors; Patients with immune system disorders and coagulation dysfunction; Patients with a history of gynecological surgery; Patients with a history of anesthesia related drug allergies. According to statistical analysis, there was no statistically significant difference in general information such as age, ASA grading, BMI, and surgical procedure category between the two groups of patients (P > 0.05). The baseline data of the two groups showed good homogeneity and comparability between the groups (P > 0.05). This study has been ratified by the Medical Ethics Committee of our university.

Methods

The conventional group received routine nursing measures in the resuscitation room. After entering the anesthesia recovery room, the patient immediately connected to an electrocardiogram monitor to continuously monitor vital signs such as heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and blood oxygen saturation. Data was recorded every 15 min. The patient’s respiratory tract was kept unobstructed. For those who had not been extubated, the tracheal tube was fixed properly and the respiratory secretions was regularly cleaned to ensure the tube was unobstructed. For those who had had their tubes removed, their breathing patterns were be observed in a timely manner, and if necessary, oxygen was administered through a nasal cannula or mask to maintain a blood oxygen saturation of ≥ 95%. Follow the doctor’s advice for intravenous fluid replacement, the infusion speed was adjusted according to the patient’s blood pressure, heart rate, urine output, etc. Besides, the wound bleeding or exudate and the drainage tube was observed. The color, nature, and amount of drainage fluid were recorded. When the patient experienced nausea and vomiting, the vomit was cleared promptly. The patient was assisted in tilting their head to one side and administer antiemetic medication according to the doctor’s advice. If the patient experienced restlessness, their limbs were restrained appropriately to prevent accidents. At the same time, the causes of restlessness (such as pain, hypoxia, etc.) were evaluated and treated accordingly. The patient’s temperature was checked every 30 min. Warmth measures such as covering with a cotton blanket were provided at a low temperature (body temperature < 36℃).

Proactive position care: After the patient was transferred to the anesthesia recovery room, the recovery room nurse and the operating room nurse performed bedside handover and paid attention to the patient’s skin damage. The patient’s limbs were placed in a standardized manner to avoid local tissue compression. Unconscious patients were placed in a supine position with their head tilted to one side to prevent aspiration of vomit. After the patient was awake and their vital signs were stable, they could be adjusted to a semi recumbent position to reduce the tension of the surgical incision and promote drainage of pus to reduce the risk of infection. At the same time, the stability of the hospital bed guardrail were checked to prevent bed falling incidents.

Comprehensive low-temperature care: The operating room temperature was kept constant at 23–28℃, and the relative humidity was controlled at 30%-60%. The patient’s temperature was closely monitored during the operation. The parameters of the inflatable heating blanket were dynamically adjusted according to body temperature fluctuate. The exposure area and time of the incision during surgery were reduced and intravenous drip fluid were heated to 37℃. After the patient was transferred to the anesthesia recovery room, immediate measures were taken to keep warm, such as raising the room temperature, covering with a cotton blanket, adjusting the temperature of the insulation device, and using a warm air blower to heat the limbs. The body temperature were measured every 15–30 min to prevent heat loss.

Strengthen pipeline care: Various types of pipelines such as catheters, drainage tubes, oxygen tubes, etc. were organized, smoothly connected, properly fixed, and placed. When changing positions, protect the pipeline to avoid significant twisting and entanglement. For patients with agitated emotions such as restlessness and delirium, nursing care were strengthened, and restraint belts were used at discretion to restrict the patient’s activities and prevent them from escaping extubation.

Professional nursing training: Before nursing intervention, systematic training was provided to team members. Gynecological and anesthesia experts, as well as key nursing staff in the hospital, were invited to hold knowledge lectures, elaborating on the concept of feedforward nursing management, implementation points, precautions for gynecological general anesthesia, and prevention and treatment of common complications. Multimedia teaching was used to visually display surgical procedures, physiological changes during resuscitation, mechanisms of complications, and pharmacological knowledge. By analyzing past nursing adverse events and conducting case studies, we aimed to enhance team members’ awareness and ability to cope with risks during the general anesthesia recovery period.

Pain and Psychological Management: Individualized pain relief measures were implemented based on the patient’s pain grading after surgery, such as using nonsteroidal analgesics, pain pumps, etc. Before surgery, psychological assessment tools were used to conduct psychological interviews with patients with negative emotions, elaborating on the surgical mechanism and process in detail to alleviate anxiety. The patients were promised that medical staff would accompany them throughout the process, with close attention and professional support. Soothing and peaceful music could be played to help patients shift their attention and alleviate restlessness caused by psychological stress reactions.

Outcome measures

Complications

The occurrence of complications during the resuscitation period in two groups of patients was observed and statistically analyzed, including hypothermia, nausea and vomiting, agitation, and hypotension.

Adverse events during nursing

Nursing adverse events such as falls, aspiration, unplanned extubation, and wound bleeding in two groups of patients were recorded and counted.

Quality of general anesthesia resuscitation

The quality of general anesthesia recovery between two groups of patients was compared and analyzed, including the duration of stay in the anesthesia recovery room, the duration of spontaneous breathing recovery, the duration of extubation, and the duration of recovery.

Physiologic function

The physiological functions of two groups upon entering and exiting the room were compared, and were evaluated using the Aldrete Awakening Scale. The scoring items were listed as the following: (1) Activity ability: Based on the patient could move their limbs and head independently or as instructed, the score was divided into 0 points (unable to move), 1 point (limited movement), and 2 points (fully active). (2) Breathing: According to the patient’s respiratory ability score, it was divided into 0 points (no breathing), 1 point (superficial restriction), and 2 points (normal breathing). (3) Blood pressure: According to the patient’s blood pressure score, it was divided into 0 points (hypotension), 1 point (normal blood pressure), and 2 points (hypertension). (4) Consciousness status: According to the patient’s level of consciousness, it was rated as 0 points (no response), 1 point (response to stimuli), and 2 points (completely awake). (5) Blood oxygen saturation (SpO₂): According to the patient’s blood oxygen saturation score, it was divided into 0 points (below normal), 1 point (within normal range), and 2 points (above normal). The total score of Aldrete was 10 points, with a maximum score of 2 points for each item. The higher the total score was, the better the quality of the patient’s recovery was.

Nursing satisfaction

To accurately evaluate the satisfaction of two groups of patients with nursing care during the recovery period, the department’s self-made “Nursing Satisfaction Survey Form” was used to conduct the evaluation work. The scale has been reviewed by 5 nursing management experts (deputy chief nurse or above), with a content validity index of 0.92 and a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.87, indicating good reliability and validity. This scale consisted of 4 dimensions with a total of 20 items: (1) nursing service attitude (5 items) covered nurses’ communication patience, service initiative, humanistic care performance, etc. Each item was rated on a Likert 5-point scale (1 point=very poor, 5 points=very good), with a total score of 25. Scores ≥ 20 were considered excellent, 15–19 were considered good, and < 15 were considered poor. (2) Nursing safety management (6 items) included standardization of pipeline fixation, prevention measures for bed fall risk, timely handling of adverse reactions, etc. Each item was rated on a Likert 5-point scale, with a total dimension score of 30 points. A score of ≥ 24 points was considered excellent, 18–23 points was considered good, and < 18 points were considered poor. (3) The nursing professional level (5 items) involved meticulous observation of the patient’s condition, proficiency in operation, and professionalism in health education. Each item was rated on a Likert 5-point scale, with a total score of 25 points. A score of ≥ 20 points were considered excellent, 15–19 points were considered good, and < 15 points were considered poor. (4) Psychological guidance (4 items) included accuracy of emotional assessment, effectiveness of psychological counseling, and establishment of rehabilitation confidence. Each item was rated on a Likert 5-point scale, with a total score of 20 points. A score of ≥ 16 points were considered excellent, 12–15 points were considered good, and < 12 points were considered poor. The total score of this scale was 100 points, with 90–100 points indicating very satisfied, 80–89 points indicating relatively satisfied, 70–79 points indicating acceptable, and < 70 points indicating dissatisfaction. Total satisfaction rate=cases of (very satisfied + relatively satisfied) ÷ total cases × 100%.

Statistical analysis

Statistical data analysis was performed using SPSS 24.0 software. The measurement data that conformed to a normal distribution were represented by (‾x ± s), and independent sample t-test was used for inter group comparison. Enumeration data were presented as [cases (%)], and comparison between groups was performed using the chi square test. P < 0.05 was used as a statistically significant criterion for judgment.

Discussion

General anesthesia is widely used in clinical gynecological major surgeries due to its advantages such as excellent analgesia, broad applicability, and adaptability to special body positions (Ghaith et al. 2024). However, due to the incomplete metabolism of muscle relaxants, nerve blockers and other factors, as well as the patient’s personal constitution, the operating room environment and many other factors, the patient is very easy to cause nausea, vomiting, agitation, infection and other adverse reactions during anesthesia resuscitation, thus inducing a series of adverse events. These adverse events not only bring severe challenges to nursing work, but also seriously hinder the patient’s postoperative body recovery (Chowdary et al. 2023, Canal et al. 2023). As a key transfer station for patients waiting to wake up after surgery and removing endotracheal intubation and other devices, the anesthesia resuscitation room is closely monitored by professional medical staff in an all-round way to improve the stability and safety of patients during resuscitation, which is essential to improve the quality of anesthesia resuscitation and reduce complications and adverse events (Liu et al. 2023, Kursumovic et al. 2023). Although routine resuscitation nursing can reduce some adverse risks, it has nursing limitations because it focuses on post control and lacks pre prediction and prevention of potential risks. Feedforward control nursing aims to adjust the risk factors within the controllable deviation by carrying out risk prediction on the existing information, taking targeted measures and constantly optimizing the scheme (Silversides et al. 2023, de Boer et al. 2023). Some studies have pointed out that the application of feedforward control nursing can significantly reduce the agitation reaction of patients in the resuscitation room, which is conducive to improving the quality of nursing (Hickman et al. 2023). However, there is still a lack of systematic application research on the recovery period of gynecological general anesthesia patients. Based on this, this study focused on the preventive effect of feedforward control nursing on the complications and adverse events of gynecological general anesthesia patients during the recovery period, aiming to provide the basis for optimizing the clinical nursing program.

This study showed that the total incidence of complications, the length of stay, extubation and recovery in the feedforward group was significantly lower than that in the conventional group. The physiological function score of the feedforward group was higher than that of the conventional group, indicating that feedforward control machine nursing may prevent complications, improve the overall quality of resuscitation, with potential value for promoting the rehabilitation process. The feedforward control nursing team retrieved the risk information after gynecological general anesthesia with the help of the whole network platform, and comprehensively considered the individual differences of patients, operation plan and other factors to develop preliminary intervention measures. In addition, we focused on training medical staff to improve their anesthesia risk awareness and first aid efficiency, curb the development of risk factors from the root, and lay the foundation for subsequent high-quality anesthesia resuscitation. In addition, the whole process of temperature control measures, such as adjusting room temperature, using heating blanket, liquid heating, etc., can effectively maintain the body’s heat balance. These measures are conducive to the smooth operation of the body’s metabolic cycle system, thereby accelerating the supply of nutrients and oxygen, and promoting the recovery of spontaneous breathing. According to the literature, a suitable temperature environment is helpful to promote the body recovery and wound healing of patients (Savioli et al. 2023). Canonical body position management can prevent patients’ oral and respiratory secretions from regurgitation and aspiration, reduce nausea and vomiting caused by respiratory tract irritation, improve patients’ comfort, and accelerate the recovery time of spontaneous breathing. Before surgery, nurses accurately judged the degree of patients’ bad emotions and carried out targeted psychological interviews by using psychological assessment tools. The nursing staff answered the patient’s worries and fears about the operation in detail. By giving companionship commitment, music intervention and other methods, we can give patients a sense of security, effectively relieve their tension and anxiety, and reduce gastrointestinal and restless reactions due to psychological stress. Negative emotions such as tension and anxiety are important risk factors for inducing agitation (Belenchia 2023). After surgery, personalized analgesic measures can ensure the best analgesic effect for patients and reduce agitation due to physical pain under the premise of minimum drug toxicity. The proper management of various pipelines may ensure the continuous and stable supply of drugs and oxygen, ensure the normal discharge of secretions, avoid the interruption of the recovery process caused by the risk of pipeline distortion, pressure and falling off, thus shortening the extubation time of patients and the residence time. Good body stability and psychological state work together to ensure the normal operation of the circulatory system. At the same time, strict vital signs monitoring can detect subtle fluctuations in blood pressure in time and effectively prevent the occurrence of hypotension. Studies have found that feedforward control nursing intervention for patients after general anesthesia can shorten the residence time in the recovery room and effectively improve the recovery time (Reis et al. 2023).

This study showed that compared with the conventional group, the feedforward group had significantly fewer nursing adverse events and higher nursing satisfaction. The feedforward group established a nursing team to formulate a precise nursing plan for patients after gynecological general anesthesia, covering multi-dimensional intervention measures such as body position, hypothermia, pain and psychology, which could effectively clarify the nursing objectives and improve the nursing efficiency through a reasonable division of responsibilities. Gynecological experts and nursing backbones carried out professional training for team members, strengthened anesthesia knowledge and risk response skills, and avoided work mistakes. The nursing staff ensured that the bed bars were stable, properly restrained the restless patients, and strengthened the pipeline management, which effectively reduced the risk of falling sore and unplanned extubation. Some literatures pointed out that the use of feedforward control can reduce the risk of unplanned extubation of nasogastric tube (Ma et al. 2023). Predictive posture nursing is aimed at the patients who are not awake, so that their heads are inclined to one side, which can effectively avoid aspiration of vomit. If the patient’s condition permits, the patient can use the semi reclining position and use the principle of gravity to reduce the tension at the incision suture, so as to avoid the hidden danger of wound cracking and bleeding. With skilled skills, mild attitude and meticulous care, nurses actively communicate with patients, keenly perceive the physical and mental status of patients, and give personalized nursing measures according to different needs of patients, so as to obtain high recognition from patients and improve nursing satisfaction, which is similar to the results of our present study. Relevant studies have pointed out that the application of feedforward control nursing can significantly reduce the incidence of nursing adverse events in patients after general anesthesia, and improve patient satisfaction (Xu et al. 2024).

The innovation of this study mainly lies in the following aspects: (1) In response to the special nursing risks during the recovery period of gynecological general anesthesia, a feedforward nursing team composed of the head nurse of the anesthesia recovery room and senior nurses was established. Through systematic literature search and clinical experience analysis, core risk factors such as body position, hypothermia, pipeline stimulation, and psychological stress were identified and sorted out. (2) Based on this, a feedforward control nursing strategy covering the entire process of “preoperative, in room, resuscitation, and out of room” was developed and implemented, achieving a transformation from post-event response to pre-event prevention. (3) The study not only observed the incidence of complications and adverse events, but also comprehensively evaluated the quality of resuscitation through objective indicators such as the duration of spontaneous breathing recovery, the duration of extubation, and the Aldrete physiological function score, providing further empirical evidence for the application of feedforward control nursing in the recovery period of gynecological general anesthesia.

In general, feedforward control nursing could effectively prevent complications and adverse events during the recovery period of gynecological general anesthesia patients, improve overall recovery quality and nursing satisfaction, and have positive significance in promoting the recovery of patients. However, the sample size of this study was small and it was a single center study, which might have selection bias, so the extrapolation of the conclusion should be cautious. Secondly, the intervention period in this study was short, and the long-term follow-up data were lacking, so the effect of nursing measures on the long-term prognosis of patients could not be evaluated. In addition, the implementation effect of feedforward control nursing might be affected by the professional level and implementation compliance of the nursing team. However, there was no hierarchical analysis of individual differences (such as anesthesia metabolic rate and pain sensitivity) in the present study, which might have a mixed effect on the results. In the future, the universality of the conclusion needs to be further verified through studies with multi-center, large sample and extended observation period.
