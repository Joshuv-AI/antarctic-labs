Abstract
. We examine crossed product C ∗ C^{*} -algebras associated with non-minimal free actions of countably infinite discrete abelian groups on the circle, extending the work of Putnam, Schmidt, and Skau. We obtain a large class of unital separable nuclear and non-simple C ∗ C^{*} -algebras that are quasidiagonal, have stable rank one, and admit a unique tracial state. We determine their ideal structure and establish an improved uniform upper bound for their nuclear dimension. Finally, in the case G = ℤ d G=\mathbb{Z}^{d} , we compute the ordered K K -theory and its trace pairing.

1.
Introduction

The study of
C^{*}
-algebras has a profound connection with topological dynamics via the construction of crossed products, which translate group actions on spaces into operator-algebraic data. A central problem becomes to understand how properties of dynamics are reflected in their associated crossed products, and vice versa. Irrational rotation algebras, which arise from minimal integer actions on the circle, are prototypical of this fruitful interplay. Despite their straightforward underlying dynamics, they have, for instance, been instrumental to the development of Connes’ noncommutative geometry programme
[
9
]
, and in motivating early breakthroughs in the modern classification and structure theory for simple separable nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras. The striking success of the so-called Elliott classification programme continues to propel major developments in the study of
C^{*}
-algebras.

While early classification efforts included non-simple algebras – notably Bratteli–Elliott’s classification of AF
C^{*}
-algebras
[
5
,
12
]
– subsequent work, particularly as it relates to crossed products, has predominantly focussed on simple
C^{*}
-algebras. For an action
G\curvearrowright X
, every non-trivial closed
G
-invariant subset of
X
gives rise to a non-trivial ideal of the crossed product
C_{0}(X)\rtimes G
. In particular, when studying simple crossed product
C^{*}
-algebras one must consider minimal (and usually free) actions, i.e., those without non-trivial closed invariant subsets, thereby ruling out natural examples such as those arising from hyperbolic and symbolic dynamics
[
27
]
. In this note, we investigate crossed products associated with certain non-minimal actions, i.e., non-simple crossed product
C^{*}
-algebras.

With a view toward possible applications to non-simple classification, we of course need to impose some restrictions, otherwise the general question of structure and classification is hopeless. A natural starting point for studying dynamical systems is to analyse their minimal sets (or, equivalently, their minimal subsystems), which roughly correspond to simple quotients in the crossed product.

For free actions of discrete amenable groups, which will shortly be our focus, this is indeed a bijective correspondence.
Although Zorn’s lemma guarantees the existence of at least one minimal set for actions on compact spaces, minimal sets may be too abundant to describe collectively in any effective way. For instance, the minimal subsystems of the Bernoulli action
G\curvearrowright\{0,1\}^{G}
, known as minimal
G
-subshifts, form a vast uncountable family of dynamical systems which, while certainly an important class in their own right, are impractical to describe in any definitive manner. While certain special cases of non-minimal actions on the Cantor set and their
C^{*}
-algebras have been studied (see
[
4
]
), this rather suggests one should seek situations in which the number of minimal sets is manageable. In stark contrast with Cantor dynamics, the rigid topology of the circle supports very few minimal sets, all of which can be explicitly described (see Proposition
2.1
), and this provides an important starting point for our investigations. For a more complete discussion on the subject of group actions on the circle, we refer to
[

26

.

The second important consideration is which acting groups we allow. We shall consider actions of countable discrete groups, which, for actions on compact metrisable spaces, ensures that our crossed products are unital and separable. In this setting a common dividing line is that of amenability.
C^{*}
-algebraically, this grants access to nuclearity, which underpins much of the classification machinery. As such, we will also be interested in actions of amenable groups. At the technical level, too, we will see that amenability plays a crucial role, notably via Proposition
2.5
. The second reason to focus on actions of amenable groups is that we will require at various points for our actions to be free, i.e., having no fixed points. By a classical result essentially due to Hölder
[
18
]
(see also
[
26
, Theorem 2.2.32]
), if a discrete group admits a free action on the circle, then it must be abelian.

Altogether, we turn rather inevitably to the study of crossed product
C^{*}
-algebras of non-minimal free actions of abelian groups on the circle. While such crossed products have been implicitly considered previously, an explicit study of their finer structural and regularity properties has rarely appeared in the literature. One notable exception is the work of Putnam, Schmidt, and Skau on Denjoy homeomorphisms
[
35
]
that inspired the present paper, and which corresponds to the case of integer actions. As such, we refer to these actions as
Denjoy actions
(Definition
2.2
).

We establish several desirable properties for crossed products arising from free Denjoy actions, some extending
[
35
]
and others entirely new. Just as the irrational rotation algebras have long inspired the study of simple
C^{*}
-algebras, we hope to advertise crossed products of Denjoy actions as a rich non-simple counterpart, offering a similarly fertile ground for further exploration.

Let

is minimal (if

. Two actions

Remark 2.3
.

We caution the reader that it is common, especially in the

A
monotone degree one map
is a continuous surjection

\varphi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
that admits a non-decreasing lift

\tilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\tilde{\varphi}(x+1)=\tilde{\varphi}(x)+1

. Given actions
\alpha,\beta:G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}

\beta(g)\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\alpha(g)\quad\text{for all }g\in G.

Two free actions on

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a Denjoy action of a countably infinite discrete group. Then
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is semiconjugate to a minimal action.

Proof.

Let
Y\subsetneq\mathbb{T}
be the unique minimal set as in Proposition

. Then
\mathbb{T}\setminus Y=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{n}
where the

has no isolated points). Define an equivalence relation

\mathbb{T}
by
x\sim y
if and only if there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\{x,y\}\subseteq\overline{I_{n}}
. Letting
\varphi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}/{\sim}
be the quotient map, it is readily checked that
\mathbb{T}/{\sim}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{T}
and that
\varphi
is a monotone degree one map. Indeed, one may take
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
to be the Cantor function

), which is constant on each interval of

descends to a well-defined action on

. The induced action on

is minimal since orbits of points in
\mathbb{T}
are dense in

∎

Let
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
be orientation-preserving, and

\rho(f):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F^{n}(x)}{n}\mod\mathbb{Z}

Proposition 2.5

(
[

)

Let

3.1.
A short exact sequence

We introduce the fundamental extension of
C^{*}
-algebras associated with a Denjoy action, following the approach outlined in the introduction.

Let
G
be a countably infinite discrete group, and let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a Denjoy action on the circle with unique minimal set
Y\subsetneq\mathbb{T}
. Since
Y
is
G
-invariant, we obtain a
G
-equivariant short exact sequence

(3.1)

0\longrightarrow C_{0}(\mathbb{T}\setminus Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\iota}}}{{\longrightarrow}}C(\mathbb{T})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{q}}}{{\longrightarrow}}C(Y)\longrightarrow 0

where
\tilde{\iota}
is the inclusion map and
\tilde{q}
the restriction map. By definition, when
G
is exact (note that all amenable groups are exact) (
3.1
) yields a short exact sequence of
C^{*}
-algebras

(3.2)

0\longrightarrow C_{0}(\mathbb{T}\setminus Y)\rtimes_{r}G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes_{r}G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\longrightarrow}}C(Y)\rtimes_{r}G\longrightarrow 0.

The map
\iota
is the natural inclusion map coming from
\tilde{\iota}
and
q
is the quotient map satisfying
q(f\lambda_{g})=\tilde{q}(f)\lambda_{g}
for
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
and
g\in G
, where
\lambda_{g}
is the canonical unitary in
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
. Whenever
G
is amenable, we identify the reduced and full crossed product
C^{*}
-algebras via the canonical isomorphism.

Proposition
2.5
allows for a precise description of the dynamics of a Denjoy action in the case when the acting group is abelian (cf.
[
35
, §3]
). This leads to an explicit description of the ideal appearing in (
3.2
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
be an action of a discrete group
G
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
. The action is
proper
if for every non-empty compact subset
K\subseteq X
, the set
G_{K}=\{g\in G:gK\cap K\neq\emptyset\}
is finite.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a countably infinite discrete abelian group, let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free Denjoy action, and let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{T}
be its unique minimal set. Then the restricted action
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}\setminus{Y}
is a free and proper action.

Proof.

Since
G
is amenable, by Proposition
2.5
,
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is semiconjugate to the minimal action
g\mapsto R_{\rho(g)}
. Moreover, freeness implies that
\rho
is injective. In fact,
\rho(g)=0
if and only if the
\mathbb{Z}
-action associated with
g
has a fixed point, so that for a free action,
\rho(g)=0
implies
g=e
. Fix an open interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{T}\setminus Y
. We first show that

(3.3)

gI\cap I=\emptyset\quad\text{for all }g\in G\setminus\{e\}.

By the proof of Proposition
2.4
, there exists a monotone degree one map
\varphi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
sending
I
to some point
y\in\mathbb{T}
. By Proposition
2.5
and the definition of
\varphi
, we have
\varphi(gI)=R_{\rho(g)}(y)=\{y+\rho(g)\}
, so by injectivity of
\rho
we have
y+\rho(g)\neq y
for all
g\in G\setminus\{e\}
. We deduce that their preimages
gI=\varphi^{-1}(y+\rho(g))
and
I=\varphi^{-1}(y)
are disjoint.

Now let
K\subseteq\mathbb{T}\setminus Y
be compact. Since
\mathbb{T}\setminus Y=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{n}
is covered by open intervals, compactness yields a finite subcover of
K
, say
K\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}I_{n_{j}}
. Suppose
g\in G
satisfies
gK\cap K\neq\emptyset
. Then there exist
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and points
x\in K\cap I_{n_{i}}
,
y\in K\cap I_{n_{j}}
with
gx=y
. Consequently
gI_{n_{i}}\cap I_{n_{j}}\neq\emptyset
and it follows that
gI_{n_{i}}=I_{n_{j}}
. For each ordered pair
(i,j)
, by (
3.3
) we have
|\{g\in G:gI_{n_{i}}=I_{n_{j}}\}|\leq 1
. Therefore
G_{K}\subseteq\bigcup_{i,j=1}^{m}\{g\in G:gI_{n_{i}}=I_{n_{j}}\}
has cardinality at most

. Thus the action
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}\setminus Y
is proper.

Finally, the action
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}\setminus Y
is free, being the restriction of a free action.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be a countably infinite discrete abelian group and let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free Denjoy action. Then the ideal
C_{0}(\mathbb{T}\setminus Y)\rtimes G
as in (
3.2
) is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(G))
, where

is the number of (necessarily disjoint) orbits of open intervals in
\mathbb{T}\setminus Y
.

Proof.

Since
Y\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is invariant, every interval in
\mathbb{T}\setminus Y
is mapped onto another. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
be the number of orbits of intervals in
\mathbb{T}\setminus Y
. Let

be representatives for each of these orbits of intervals, and put

which completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Traces and ideal structure

In this section we prove that crossed products of free actions of abelian groups on the circle have a unique tracial state, and determine the ideal structure for free Denjoy actions. By a trace we mean a tracial state and an ideal refers to a closed two-sided ideal.

Proposition 3.5
.

Every free action
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
of a countably infinite discrete abelian group is uniquely ergodic.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
it suffices to consider the following two cases. In both cases, existence of at least one probability measure
\mu\in M_{G}(\mathbb{T})
is guaranteed by amenability of
G
, and so it suffices to show uniqueness.

If
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is minimal, then by Proposition
2.5
,
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is conjugate to an action by rotations. Freeness implies that all rotation numbers are irrational. The Lebesgue measure
\lambda
is the unique Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
that is invariant under irrational rotations so
\mu=\lambda
(conjugacy preserves invariant measures), and thus
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is uniquely ergodic.

If
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is not minimal, it must be a free Denjoy action with unique minimal set
Y\subsetneq\mathbb{T}
. To prove uniqueness, let
\nu\in M_{G}(\mathbb{T})
. Proposition
2.4
provides a left semiconjugacy
\varphi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
to a minimal action
\beta:G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
. The pushforward measure
\varphi_{*}\nu
is
\beta
-invariant. As
\beta
is minimal and each
\beta(g)
acts by irrational rotations,
\varphi_{*}\nu=\lambda
. By the continuity and monotonicity of
\varphi
, its fibres, i.e., preimages
\varphi^{-1}(x)
of points
x\in\mathbb{T}
are either singletons or closed intervals on which
\varphi
is constant. Let
I
be a connected component of
\mathbb{T}\setminus Y
. By (
3.3
), the sets
\{gI:g\in G\}
are disjoint. By
G
-invariance of
\nu
, this implies that
\nu(\bigcup_{g\in G}gI)=\sum_{g\in G}\nu(gI)=\sum_{g\in G}\nu(I)
, which is unbounded when
G
is infinite unless
\nu(\mathbb{T}\setminus Y)=0
. Thus
\supp(\nu)\subseteq Y
. The map
\varphi|_{Y}:Y\to\mathbb{T}
is a Borel isomorphism and so since
\varphi_{*}\mu=\lambda=\varphi_{*}\nu
and both
\mu
and
\nu
are supported on
Y
, we deduce that
\mu=(\varphi|_{Y})_{*}^{-1}\lambda=\nu
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

For Denjoy actions
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
, the unique invariant measure
\mu
in Proposition
3.5
can be described explicitly. Indeed,
\mu
is the image under the quotient map
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}
of the Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure for the map
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
corresponding to the left semiconjugacy
\varphi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
. It follows that for every
g\in G
we have
\mu((x,gx])=\rho(g)
, independently of
x\in\mathbb{T}
(see
[
26
, Theorem 2.2.10]
).

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
be an action of a discrete amenable group on a compact Hausdorff space
X
and suppose that
M_{G}(X)=\{\mu\}
. Then
\supp(\mu)
is the unique minimal set. In particular,
G\curvearrowright X
is minimal if and only if
\mu
has full support.

Proof.

Since
\supp(\mu)
is a non-empty closed (hence compact) and
G
-invariant set, it contains a minimal set
Y\subseteq\supp(\mu)
. Since
G
is amenable, there exists a Borel measure
\nu\in M_{G}(Y)\subseteq M_{G}(X)
such that
\nu(Y)=1
. By unique ergodicity, we have
\nu=\mu
and hence
\mu(Y)=1
. It follows from the definition of
\supp(\mu)
that
\supp(\mu)\subseteq Y
, hence
\supp(\mu)=Y
is a minimal set. If
Y_{1},Y_{2}\subseteq X
are two minimal sets then since
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}
is also closed and
G
-invariant, either
Y_{1}=Y_{2}
or
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are disjoint. If
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are disjoint, then
\mu_{i}:=\mu|_{Y_{i}}
determine two distinct probability measures, contradicting uniqueness of
\mu
. Hence
\supp(\mu)
is the unique minimal set.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free action of a countably infinite discrete abelian group. Then
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
has a unique tracial state
\tau
satisfying

(3.4)

\tau(f\lambda_{g})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,d\mu,&g=e\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
\mu
is the unique measure in
M_{G}(\mathbb{T})
,
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
and
g\in G
. Moreover, the support of
\tau
coincides with the unique minimal set for the action and in particular,
\tau
is faithful if and only if the action is minimal.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.5
and Lemma
3.7
,
M_{G}(\mathbb{T})=\{\mu\}
and
\supp(\mu)
is the unique minimal set. By
[
20
, Corollary 2.8]
, tracial states on
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
correspond bijectively to
G
-invariant probability measures, so there is a unique tracial state
\tau
of the form (
3.4
). The support of
\tau
coincides with the support of
\mu
and so faithfulness holds if and only if
\supp(\mu)=\mathbb{T}
, i.e., if and only if the action is minimal.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free Denjoy action of a countably infinite discrete abelian group. Then the quotient

as in (

) has a unique tracial state
\tau^{\prime}

, where

The following generalises

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free Denjoy action of a countably infinite discrete abelian group. Then the ideal
C_{0}(\mathbb{T}\setminus Y)\rtimes G
as in (

) is the unique maximal ideal in
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
, and corresponds to the trace ideal
\{a\in C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G:\tau(a^{*}a)=0\}
of the unique tracial state
\tau
on
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
.

Proof.

By
[

, every ideal in

has the form
C_{0}(\mathbb{T}\setminus Z)\rtimes G
for a closed,
G

of

. Since

is the unique minimal set for

(Proposition

),

\supp(\mu)=Y
, thus for

An ideal is primitive if it is the kernel of an irreducible representation. The set of primitive ideals,
\prim(A)
, is equipped with the hull-kernel topology as determined by the closure operation: the closure of a non-empty subset
F\subseteq\prim(A)
is
\overline{F}:=\{P\in\prim(A):\bigcap_{I\in F}I\subseteq P\}
. From Corollary
3.3
, we obtain the analogue of
[
35
, Proposition 4.4]
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free Denjoy action of a countably infinite discrete abelian group on the circle, and let
A=C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
. Then
\prim(A)
is homeomorphic to
Y_{0}\cup\{J\}
, where
Y_{0}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}I_{n_{i}}
is a union of representative intervals for each orbit of intervals in
\mathbb{T}\setminus Y
with the subspace topology, and
\{J\}
is contained in the closure of every non-empty subset.

Remark 3.12
.

Recall that ideals in a
C^{*}
-algebra

are in bijection with open subsets of
\prim(A)
via the map
I\mapsto\{P\in\prim(A):I\not\subseteq P\}
. Hence Proposition
3.11
provides a complete description of the ideal structure.

3.3.
Stable rank one and quasidiagonality

In this section, we prove stable rank one and quasidiagonality for crossed products of free actions of abelian groups on the circle. Recall that a unital
C^{*}
-algebra has
stable rank one
if the set of invertible elements is dense in norm. A
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is said to be
quasidiagonal
if it admits a faithful representation
\pi:A\to B(H)
such that
\pi(A)
is a family of quasidiagonal operators.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free action of a countably infinite discrete abelian group on the circle. Then
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
has stable rank one.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
, there are two cases: if
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is free and minimal, then
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
is simple unital and
\mathcal{Z}
-stable (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Theorem 8.2]
). Since it also admits a trace (Corollary
3.8
), it must be finite and hence has stable rank one by
[
42
, Theorem 6.7]
.

If
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is not minimal, then it is a free Denjoy action. Let
A=C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
, let
J=C_{0}(\mathbb{T}\setminus Y)\rtimes G
and let
B=C(Y)\rtimes G
. Applying the same argument as the previous paragraph,
B
has stable rank one. By Proposition
3.3
,
J\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(G))
. Hence

(3.5)

\sr(J)=\sr(C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(G))=\sr(C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(G))).

Since the minimal unitisation of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
is isomorphic to
C(\mathbb{T})
and
\dim\mathbb{T}=1
, we have
\sr(C_{0}(\mathbb{R}))=1
by
[
38
, Proposition 1.7]
. Combining this with basic permanence properties of stable rank one, we conclude that
\sr(J)=1
. By
[
28
, Lemma 3]
, it remains to show that the index map
K_{1}(B)\to K_{0}(J)
vanishes, which is immediate from the fact, easily verified by standard
K
-theoretic considerations, that
K_{0}(J)=\{0\}
.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

Freeness is a necessary assumption in Theorem
3.13
. Indeed the trivial action of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
on
\mathbb{T}
yields a crossed product that is isomorphic to
C(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})
, which does not have stable rank one for all
d\geq 1
.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free action of a countably infinite discrete abelian group on the circle. Then
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
is quasidiagonal.

Proof.

If
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is minimal, then
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
is separable, nuclear and satisfies the UCT
[
49
]
, and moreover admits a faithful tracial state by Corollary
3.8
. Hence it is quasidiagonal by
[
47
]
. In the non-minimal case, we again recall the short exact sequence (
3.2
). Let
J
and
B
denote the ideal and quotient, respectively. Since
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
is separable and
B
is nuclear and satisfies the UCT, by
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
it suffices to prove that
J
and
B
are quasidiagonal and the index map
K_{1}(B)\to K_{0}(J)
vanishes. The ideal
J
is quasidiagonal since every commutative
C^{*}
-algebra is quasidiagonal and quasidiagonality is preserved under stabilisation. By
[
47
]
, since
B
is nuclear, satisfies the UCT, and has a faithful trace (Corollary
3.8
),
B
is also quasidiagonal. Finally, the index map vanishes since
K_{0}(J)=\{0\}
.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

We have been unable to verify the stronger statement that
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
in fact embeds into an AF algebra. When the action is minimal, this follows from Corollary
3.8
and the main result of
[
44
]
. For the same reason, the quotient
C(Y)\rtimes G
embeds into a unital simple AF algebra. We do not know of an analogue of
[
32
]
, which holds for possibly non-simple crossed products
C(X)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
, but suspect that
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
should always embed into a (not necessarily simple) AF algebra (cf.
[
35
, Proposition 4.3]
).

3.4.
Further applications

Introduced by Winter and Zacharias, nuclear dimension is a generalisation of covering dimension for topological spaces to (nuclear)
C^{*}
-algebras
[
50
]
. Finite nuclear dimension has come to play a crucial role in the Elliott classification programme. Crossed products arising from free actions of finitely-generated countably infinite abelian groups on finite-dimensional spaces are known to have finite nuclear dimension thanks to the work of Szabó, Wu and Zacharias
[
45
, Corollary 8.6]
. The authors obtain the upper bound

(3.6)

\nucdim(C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G)\leq 4\cdot 3^{h(G)}-1,

where
h(G)
denotes the Hirsch length of
G
(see also
[
46
]
). Recall that
h(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=d
, so the upper bound in (
3.6
) can become arbitrarily large.

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free action of a countably infinite discrete abelian group on the circle. Then

1\leq\nucdim(C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G)\leq 3.

If
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is moreover minimal, then
\nucdim(C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G)=1
.

Proof.

If
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is minimal, then
\nucdim(C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G)=1
by
[
8
, Theorem B]
. For
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
non-minimal, recall the short exact sequence (
3.2
) given by

0\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow 0

where
J\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{K}
by Corollary
3.3
,
A=C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
and
B
is unital separable simple nuclear and
\mathcal{Z}
-stable
[
22
]
(cf. Remark
3.4
). Using
[
50
, Corollary 2.10]
, we have
\nucdim(J)=\dim(\mathbb{R})=1
and
\nucdim(B)\leq 1
by
[
8
, Theorem B]
. Hence it follows from
[
50
, Proposition 2.9]
that

1\leq\nucdim(A)\leq\nucdim(J)+\nucdim(B)+1\leq 3,

which completes the proof.
∎

We point out that the finite-generation assumption made in
[
45
]
can be dispensed with in Corollary
3.17
. Obtaining sharp estimates for nuclear dimension is notoriously difficult, though, in light of Corollary
3.17
, we pose the following:

Question 1
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free Denjoy action. What is
\nucdim(C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G)
?

Remark 3.18
.

The Toms–Winter conjecture (which is known to hold in the monotracial case) predicts that for simple separable unital nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras, finite nuclear dimension is equivalent to tensorial absorption of the Jiang–Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}
, and to strict comparison. However, if
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
is a free Denjoy action, then
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes G
is not
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. The obstruction comes from the presence of a nonzero type I ideal, which prevents absorption of the Jiang–Su algebra.

We collect together some further consequences of Theorem
3.13
.

Corollary 3.19
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free action of a countably infinite discrete abelian group. Let

(i)

has cancellation of projections.

(ii)

The canonical map
U(A)/U_{0}(A)\to K_{1}(A)

(iii)

The Cuntz semigroup

has weak cancellation.

(iv)

The Cuntz semigroup

The first two are well-known consequences proved by Rieffel
[

. The weak cancellation property follows from
[

and Riesz interpolation is proved in
[

.
∎

The real rank of a

-algebra

, denoted

, was introduced by Brown and Pedersen
[

and is defined similarly to stable rank. It follows from the definition that

Corollary 3.20
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free Denjoy action of a countably infinite discrete abelian group. Then

Proof.

Let

and the comment preceding the corollary,
\rr(A)\leq 1
. By
[

, we have

.

For a minimal free action

3.5.
K
-theory

In this section we prove Theorem
B
. We refer the reader to
[
40
]
for the necessary background on
K
-theory for
C^{*}
-algebras. We fix the following standard notation. For a projection
p\in M_{\infty}(A)
, we write
[p]_{0}
for its class in
K_{0}(A)
and for a unitary
u\in M_{\infty}(A)
we write
[u]_{1}
for its class in
K_{1}(A)
(note that all of our
C^{*}
-algebras are unital in this section).

For a free minimal action
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
,
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is generated by the unitary generator
u_{1}
of
C(\mathbb{T})
, and unitaries
u_{2},\ldots,u_{d+1}
implementing the action. For each
i\in\{2,\ldots,d+1\}
, we have
u_{i}u_{1}u_{i}^{*}=e^{2\pi i\gamma_{i-1}}u
, where
\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{T}
is the angle of rotation of
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and hence rearranging slightly we have

C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cong C^{*}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d+1}:u_{j}u_{i}=e^{2\pi i\theta_{i,j}}u_{i}u_{j}),

where
\theta=(\theta_{i,j})
is the real skew-symmetric matrix

(3.7)

\theta:=\begin{pmatrix}0&\gamma_{1}&\gamma_{2}&\cdots&\gamma_{d}\\
-\gamma_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
-\gamma_{2}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\gamma_{d}&0&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}.

The entries
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}
of
\theta
are rationally linearly independent and irrational, i.e.,
\theta
is totally irrational (reflecting the fact that
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is simple). In other words,
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is isomorphic to the simple
(d+1)
-dimensional torus
A_{\theta}
. The ordered
K
-theory of higher-dimensional noncommutative tori has been studied intensively by Elliott
[
13
]
and Rieffel
[
39
]
, and the proofs of Theorem
3.25
and Theorem
3.27
are heavily reliant on these results, together with the computations in
[
35
]
. We additionally benefitted from reading the exposition in
[
34
]
. Let us start with some preliminaries.

Recall that for a
2n\times 2n
skew-symmetric matrix
\theta
, the Pfaffian of
\theta
, denoted
\operatorname{pf}(\theta)
, is defined as

\operatorname{pf}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{\sigma(2i-1),\sigma(2i)}.

We are now able to state one of Elliott’s main results in
[
13
]
(for another proof, see
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
).

Theorem 3.22

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\theta
be a totally irrational skew-symmetric real
d\times d
matrix and
\tau
the unique trace on
A_{\theta}
. Then

(i)

if
d
is even, then

\tau_{*}(K_{0}(A_{\theta}))=\mathbb{Z}+\sum_{0<|I|<d}\operatorname{pf}(\theta_{I})\mathbb{Z}+\operatorname{pf}(\theta)\mathbb{Z};

(ii)

if
d
is odd, then

\tau_{*}(K_{0}(A_{\theta}))=\mathbb{Z}+\sum_{0<|I|<d}\operatorname{pf}(\theta_{I})\mathbb{Z},

where

varies over subsets of

of even cardinality, and
\theta_{I}
denotes the submatrix of
\theta=(\theta_{i,j})
with

. Moreover,
\tau_{*}
is injective.

For a skew-symmetric matrix of the form (

),
\operatorname{pf}(\theta_{I})
vanishes for all subsets

of even cardinality except the tuples

, where
i\in\{2,\ldots,d+1\}
. In this case, we have

\theta_{I}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\gamma_{i}\\

.

Let

\tau_{*}(K_{0}(A_{\theta}))=\mathbb{Z}+\gamma_{1}\mathbb{Z}+\cdots+\gamma_{d}\mathbb{Z}

The following is likely well-known to experts.

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)
homotopic to the identity, and
u\in U(A\rtimes\mathbb{Z})
the unitary corresponding to
1\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
p\in A
be a projection. Then there exists a partial isometry
w_{p}\in A
such that

v:=(1-p)+pw_{p}u^{*}p\in A\rtimes\mathbb{Z}

is a unitary satisfying
\delta_{1}([v]_{1})=[p]_{0}
, where
\delta_{1}:K_{1}(A\rtimes\mathbb{Z})\to K_{0}(A)
is the index map in the Pimsner–Voiculescu exact sequence for
A\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
(\alpha_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be a homotopy from
\alpha^{-1}
to the identity in
\operatorname{Aut}(A)
. Since the
\alpha_{t}
are
*
-homomorphisms,
\alpha_{t}p
are projections for all
t\in[0,1]
. Hence, as homotopy equivalence implies Murray–von Neumann equivalence, there is a partial isometry
w_{p}\in A
such that
w_{p}^{*}w_{p}=\alpha^{-1}(p)
and
w_{p}w_{p}^{*}=p
. We check that
v:=(1-p)+pw_{p}u^{*}p
is a unitary. Since
p
is a projection,
p=p^{*}
and
p(1-p)=(1-p)p=0
. Hence most of the cross terms immediately vanish, what remains is:

\displaystyle v^{*}v

\displaystyle=1-p+puw_{p}^{*}pw_{p}u^{*}p

\displaystyle=1-p+pu\alpha^{-1}(p)u^{*}p

\displaystyle=1-p+p\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(p))p

\displaystyle=1.

Analogously, one checks that
vv^{*}=1
, so that
v
is a unitary. Finally, as explained at the top of page 102 of
[
30
]
, we have
\delta_{1}([v]_{1})=[p]_{0}
.
∎

Recall that for a crossed product

given by

with

. The groups

and

are generated by

and

is the unitary generating

.

Theorem 3.25
.

Fix

K_{i}(A)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2^{d}},\quad i=0,1.

A set of generators for
K_{0}(A)
is given by
[p_{I}]_{0}
, where

is an increasingly ordered (possibly empty) subset of even cardinality, and a set of generators for

[v_{J}]_{1}

is an increasingly ordered subset of odd cardinality. Here,
p_{\emptyset}:=1
and

are Rieffel projections and

Proof.

We start by writing

as a recursive crossed product by
\mathbb{Z}
-actions. To each standard generator
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, write
\alpha_{i}
for the induced automorphism of
A_{0}:=C(\mathbb{T})
obtained via restriction. Recursively define
A_{k}:=A_{k-1}\rtimes_{\tilde{\alpha}_{k}}\mathbb{Z}
for
k=1,\ldots,d
, where
\tilde{\alpha}_{k}
is the automorphism of
A_{k-1}
extending
\alpha_{k}
and satisfying
\alpha_{k}\lambda_{e_{i}}=\lambda_{e_{i}}
for
i<k
(
\lambda_{e_{i}}
is the unitary that implements the action
\alpha_{i}
on
A_{0}
); this extension is well-defined precisely since all of the
\alpha_{i}
commute. Then we may write

(3.8)

A\cong(\cdots((A_{0}\rtimes_{\alpha_{1}}\mathbb{Z})\rtimes_{\tilde{\alpha}_{2}}\mathbb{Z})\cdots)\rtimes_{\tilde{\alpha}_{d}}\mathbb{Z}.

We now argue via a finite induction on
d
. The base case
d=1
is proved in
[
35
, §6]
; we include the argument here for completeness. We invoke the Pimsner–Voiculescu sequence
[
30
]
:

(3.9)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K_{0}(C(\mathbb{T}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K_{0}(C(\mathbb{T}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K_{0}(A_{1})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K_{1}(A_{1})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K_{1}(C(\mathbb{T}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K_{1}(C(\mathbb{T}))}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}-(\alpha_{1})_{*}^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}-(\alpha_{1})_{*}^{-1}}

Observe that since
K_{0}(C(\mathbb{T}))
is generated by
[1]_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}(1)=1
we have
\operatorname{id}-(\alpha_{1})_{*}^{-1}=0
in
K_{0}
, and using that
\alpha_{1}
is orientation-preserving (hence homotopic to the identity),
\operatorname{id}-(\alpha_{1})_{*}^{-1}=0
in
K_{1}
. Thus (
3.9
) reduces to

(3.10)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow K_{0}(C(\mathbb{T}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle K_{0}(A_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{1}(C(\mathbb{T}))\longrightarrow 0

(3.11)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow K_{1}(C(\mathbb{T}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle K_{1}(A_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{0}(C(\mathbb{T}))\longrightarrow 0

Since
K_{i}(C(\mathbb{T}))\cong\mathbb{Z}
is free for
i=0,1
, both sequences split and we obtain isomorphisms

K_{0}(A_{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2},\quad K_{1}(A_{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Now assume that for some fixed
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
, we have
K_{i}(A_{k})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2^{k}}
,
i=0,1
. Then it is clear by definition that
\tilde{\alpha}_{k+1}
is homotopic to the identity since
\alpha_{k+1}
is, so applying the same argument using the Pimsner–Voiculescu sequence for
A_{k}\rtimes_{\tilde{\alpha}_{k+1}}\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain short exact sequences

(3.12)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow K_{0}(A_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle K_{0}(A_{k}\rtimes\mathbb{Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{1}(A_{k})\longrightarrow 0

(3.13)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow K_{1}(A_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle K_{1}(A_{k}\rtimes\mathbb{Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{0}(A_{k})\longrightarrow 0

Applying the inductive hypothesis, we have
K_{i}(A_{k}\rtimes\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2^{k}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{2^{k}}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2^{k+1}}
. By induction and (

), we deduce that
K_{i}(A)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2^{d}}
, as desired.

We now iteratively construct a basis for
K_{*}(A)
. For
d=1
,
K_{0}(A_{1})
is generated by
[1]_{0}
and
[p_{\{1,2\}}]_{0}
, where
p_{\{1,2\}}\in A_{1}
is a Rieffel projection of the form

p_{\{1,2\}}=\alpha_{1}^{-1}(f_{1})\lambda_{-e_{1}}+f_{0}\lambda_{0}+f_{1}\lambda_{e_{1}},

where
f_{0},f_{1}\in C(\mathbb{T})
are chosen so that
p_{\{1,2\}}
is a projection and satisfies
\tau(p_{\{1,2\}})=\gamma_{1}
; these are explicitly described in
[

, Lemma 6.4]
. We note that the irrational rotation algebra

is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
A_{1}
. Indeed, write
B
for the functions in
C(\mathbb{T})
that are constant on connected components of the complement of the
\alpha_{1}
-invariant Cantor set in
\mathbb{T}
. Then
B
is invariant under the rotation action

and we obtain an isomorphism
B\rtimes_{\tilde{R}_{\gamma_{1}}}\mathbb{Z}\cong A_{\gamma_{1}}
. By arranging the functions
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
in the definition of the Rieffel projection above to be constant on connected components of the complement of the Cantor set in
\mathbb{T}
for
\alpha_{1}
, we can take
p_{\{1,2\}}\in A_{\gamma_{1}}
to be a generator of
K_{0}(A_{\gamma_{1}})
. As is shown in the appendix of
[
30
]
, we have
\delta_{0}([p_{\{1,2\}}]_{0})=[u]_{1}
. On the other hand, as noted in
[
35
, Lemma 6.3]
,
K_{1}(A_{1})
is generated by
[u]_{1}
and
[\lambda_{e_{1}}]_{1}
since
\iota_{*}([u]_{1})=[u]_{1}
and
\delta_{1}([\lambda_{e_{1}}]_{1})=[1]_{0}
, as can be seen for instance using Lemma
3.24
. By convention, we denote in the sequel
[p_{\emptyset}]_{0}:=[1]_{0}
,
[v_{\{1\}}]_{1}:=[u]_{1}
and

.

Suppose now that we have a basis for

, for fixed

. The inclusion of

into

allow us to identify the

with generators of

. We label these generators

and

, for

and

respectively, where

and

are increasingly ordered subsets of even, respectively odd, cardinality. The other generators shall be provided by equations (

) and (

), and correspond to labels

and

. These are uniquely determined by the equations:

The unitary

is explicitly described by Lemma
3.24
. On the other hand, for

, we note that by an identical argument to the base case the irrational rotation algebra
A_{\gamma_{k+1}}
is a

-subalgebra of
A_{k+1}
and contains a Rieffel projection

satisfying
\delta_{0}([p_{\{1,k+2\}}]_{0})=[v_{\{1\}}]_{1}
and

. In fact, in general the trace of
[p_{J}]_{0}
is precisely (the absolute value of)

(see
[

, Proposition 4.3]
and
[

for a much more general result). In particular,
\tau(p_{J\cup\{k+1\}})=0
for all

; this reflects the fact

for

and

not equal to

is a commutative

-algebra. Altogether, we have doubled the number of generators in
K_{*}(A_{k+1})
, hence we have found all required
2\cdot 2^{k}=2^{k+1}
generators. By induction, this concludes the proof.
∎

Example 3.26
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free Denjoy action, implemented by commuting Denjoy homeomorphisms with rotation numbers
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbb{T}
. Then

\displaystyle K_{0}(C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{2})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{Z}[1]_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[p_{1}]_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[p_{2}]_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[\beta]_{0}

\displaystyle K_{1}(C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{2})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{Z}[u]_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{e_{1}}]_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{e_{2}}]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[w]_{1}

where
p_{i}
are Rieffel projections with
\tau(p_{i})=\gamma_{i}
,
\beta
is a Bott projection in
M_{2}(C^{*}(\lambda_{e_{1}},\lambda_{e_{2}}))\cong M_{2}(C(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
with
\tau(\beta)=0
, and
w=1-p_{1}+p_{1}w_{p_{1}}\lambda_{e_{2}}p_{1}
is the unitary furnished by Lemma
3.24
.

Theorem 3.27
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
be a free Denjoy action with
\gamma_{i}
the rotation number of the standard generator
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
A=C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The homomorphism
\tau_{*}:K_{0}(A)\to\mathbb{R}
determined by the unique trace
\tau
induces an order isomorphism onto
\mathbb{Z}+\gamma_{1}\mathbb{Z}+\cdots+\gamma_{d}\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By the proof of Theorem
3.25
, the value of
\tau_{*}
on the generators
p_{\{1,i\}}
,
i=2,\ldots,d+1
, recover the numbers
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}
. Since
\tau(1)=1
and the trace of all other projections is zero (as observed in the proof of Theorem
3.25
), we deduce that
\tau_{*}(K_{0}(A))=\mathbb{Z}+\gamma_{1}\mathbb{Z}+\cdots+\gamma_{d}\mathbb{Z}
. To see that
\tau_{*}
is an order isomorphism, we use that
B:=C^{*}(u,\lambda_{e_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{e_{d}})
is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra and Rieffel has shown
[
39
, Corollary 7.10]
that for every class
\eta\in K_{0}(B)
with
0<\tau(\eta)<1
there exists a projection
p\in B
such that
[p]_{0}=\eta
.
∎

Remark 3.28
.

From Theorem
3.25
, we see that given a free Denjoy action
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}
, the rotation numbers
\gamma_{i}
corresponding to each generator may be recovered from the
K
-theory of
C(\mathbb{T})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by means of the trace pairing. This is certainly necessary to obtain classification, though much more information must be recovered; compare with
[
35
]
, where the invariant
Q(\alpha)
of a Denjoy homeomorphism
\alpha
is recovered using the six-term exact sequence in
K
-theory coming from (
3.2
).
