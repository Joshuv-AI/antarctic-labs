1.0 Summary
 The Seasonal/Spatial Vertical NPF Variation Study (SSVNV) campaign was conducted to investigate
 vertically resolved aerosol variability, boundary-layer structure, and new particle formation (NPF)
 processes across contrasting atmospheric environments through coordinated tethered balloon system
 (TBS) observations during U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Atmospheric Radiation Measurement
 (ARM) User Facility fixed-site measurements (Dexheimer et al. 2026). The campaign leveraged the
 overlapping deployments of the third ARM Mobile Facility (AMF3) at Bankhead National Forest (BNF;
 Kuang et al. 2023, 2026), Alabama, and the first ARM Mobile Facility 1 (AMF1) Coast-Urban-Rural
 Gradient Atmospheric Experiment (CoURAGE) in the Baltimore region. The campaign was operated by
 the TBS team led by Darielle Dexheimer from Sandia National Laboratories, in collaboration with the
 BNF site science team lead by Chongai Kuang from Brookhaven National Laboratory and the ARM team.

 The primary scientific objective of the campaign was to characterize the vertical variability of aerosol
 properties and ultrafine particle concentrations under different atmospheric and emission regimes,
 including biogenically influenced forest environments and urban/coastal regions impacted by
 anthropogenic emissions. The campaign also aimed to improve understanding of aerosol-boundary-layer
 interactions and the role of atmospheric mixing and transport in shaping aerosol evolution.

 The TBS deployments were conducted during the AMF1 CoURAGE and AMF3 BNF deployments, with
 coordinated ARM surface observations and guest instrumentation. The deployments included multiple
 two- to three-week intensive flight periods and were executed across different seasons, as summarized in
 Table 1. The key instruments loaded on TBS were SUNY-A’s miniature scanning electrical mobility
 spectrometer (mSEMS, Brechtel Manufacturing Inc.) for the high-time-resolution measurements of the
 aerosol size distribution from 5 to 375 nm with 60 size bins. Besides it, the TBS also loaded (1) one
 portable optical particle spectrometer (POPS), (2) one TSI 3007 condensation particle counter (CPC) for
 aerosol larger than 10 nm, (3) one microAeth (for black carbon optical properties), (4) one IcePuck (for
 ice particle nucleating potential), (5) one distributed temperature sensor (DTS), cup anemometers, one 3D
 sonic anemometer, (6) an iMet RSB (radiosonde), and XQ2 (uncrewed aircraft system [UAS]) sensors (T,
 RH, P).

 A total of 64 TBS flights were successfully conducted during the campaign period, reaching altitudes up
 to approximately 1200 m above ground level. Flight operations were coordinated with ARM site activities
 and targeted periods with favorable meteorological conditions and enhanced aerosol variability.

One notable TBS case observed on 04/13/2025 (Figure 1a and 1b) at the BNF site revealed a remarkably
homogeneous aerosol structure below approximately 900–1000 m, with relatively consistent aerosol size
distributions dominated by accumulation-mode particles near ~100–150 nm. Above ~1100 m, aerosol
concentrations decreased substantially, suggesting a transition into a cleaner residual or free-atmospheric
layer. Interestingly, elevated ultrafine particles (~10–30-nm) reappeared near ~1400 m, indicating
possible new particle formation or residual-layer particle reservoirs (O’Donnell et al. 2023).

Another interesting TBS flight deployment between 00:00 to 03:00 UTC on 06/12/2025 (Figure 1c) at
BNF reveals a complex vertically layered aerosol structure superimposed on a relatively persistent
regional aerosol background. Throughout much of the lower atmosphere, the aerosol population was
dominated by accumulation-mode particles near ~100–150 nm, indicating the presence of aged and
regionally mixed aerosols. However, the vertical structure was not completely homogeneous. Distinct
aerosol enhancement layers were observed near ~300 m and especially between 500~550 m, where
aerosol number concentrations increased substantially within relatively narrow altitude ranges. In addition
to the dominant accumulation-mode particles, the 700-m layer also exhibited enhanced concentrations of
smaller particles in the ~30–50-nm range, indicating potential new particle formation.

Overall, the campaign successfully demonstrated the scientific and operational value of coordinated
vertically resolved TBS observations integrated with fixed-site measurements for investigating aerosol
processes and atmospheric boundary-layer variability across heterogeneous environments. The data sets
collected during the campaign will support ongoing analyses of aerosol evolution, atmospheric mixing,
aerosol-cloud interactions, and energy-relevant boundary-layer processes associated with radiation, cloud
formation, and land-atmosphere coupling, which are directly relevant to the DOE earth system science
objectives. A journal manuscript entitled "Tethered Balloon Observations of Vertically Resolved Aerosol

2.0 Results
 The TBS mSEMS observations revealed substantial variability in aerosol vertical structure across the
 BNF and COURAGE IOP deployment periods, while also demonstrating several recurring aerosol
 regimes that appeared consistently under different seasonal and meteorological conditions. Overall, the
 observations indicate that aerosol vertical distributions cannot be fully characterized using only total
 particle number concentration or thermodynamic boundary-layer height. Instead, the size-resolved TBS
 measurements reveal the combined influence of regional aerosol background, residual-layer storage,
 boundary-layer mixing, entrainment processes, and seasonally varying new particle formation (NPF)
 activity.

 2.1 Vertically Homogeneous Aged Aerosol Background

 Figure 2. Aerosol vertical size distribution observed by TBS mSEMS during CoURAGE (a)
             02/23/2025 Flight 1 between 15:00-18:00 UTC, (b) 07/26/2025 Flight 1 between 14:30-16:00
             UTC, and (c) BNF 04/10/2025 Flight 2 between 18:30-20:20 UTC, (d) BNF 06/12/2025
             Flight 2 between 17:30-20:30 UTC.

 One of the most consistent features observed during the TBS deployments was the presence of vertically
 homogeneous aerosol layers below approximately 500–600 m (Figure 2). Aerosol size distributions
 remained relatively uniform throughout the lower atmosphere and were generally dominated by
 single-peaked accumulation-mode particles centered near ~80–150 nm, with only weak vertical gradients
 in aerosol concentration. These observations suggest that aerosol populations within the lower
 troposphere were frequently well mixed during the TBS observational periods over substantial vertical
 depths, likely reflecting the combined influence of regional transport, aerosol aging, and boundary-layer
 mixing processes. In addition, aerosol size distributions observed during the CoURAGE deployments
 generally exhibited smaller dominant particle diameters compared to those observed at BNF, potentially
 reflecting stronger influences from fresher urban emissions and less processed aerosol populations in the
 urban/coastal environment. In contrast, the BNF observations more frequently exhibited broader and
 larger accumulation-mode structures, consistent with enhanced aerosol aging and condensational growth

 3

under biogenically influenced summertime conditions. However, additional cases and longer-term
vertically resolved observations will be needed to further evaluate the robustness and generality of these
regional contrasts.

2.2 Summer Bimodal and Aitken-Mode Aerosol Regimes
Strong seasonal differences in aerosol size distributions were observed between spring and summer
deployments. Springtime profiles were typically dominated by a single accumulation-mode peak near
~100 nm, consistent with relatively aged and regionally mixed aerosol backgrounds. In contrast,
summertime observations frequently exhibited bimodal aerosol structures (Figure 3), with one mode near
~30–50 nm and a second mode near ~150–200 nm. Although the smaller mode falls within the size range
commonly associated with growing particles, concurrent surface observations did not show clear evidence
of local NPF initiation. The 30–50-nm particles were therefore more likely associated with transported or
previously formed Aitken-mode aerosol populations that had undergone growth prior to observation.
These bimodal structures suggest that summertime aerosol populations were influenced by both aged
regional aerosols and younger transported particle populations.

2.3 Layered Aerosol Structures and Residual-Layer Plumes
In addition to vertically homogeneous profiles, several TBS flights exhibited pronounced layered aerosol
structures, indicating that aerosol populations within the lower troposphere were often dynamically
stratified rather than continuously mixed. Overall, these layered cases can be broadly grouped into two
major aerosol vertical regimes: (1) stronger near-surface aerosol influence and (2) elevated young-particle
populations with vertically decoupled upper-layer aerosol structures.

The first regime is characterized by broader aerosol size distributions near the surface that gradually
transition into narrower accumulation-mode-dominated structures aloft (Figure 4a and 4c, black dash
box). The lower atmosphere frequently contained enhanced Aitken-mode particles and broader aerosol

 4

size distributions, while upper layers became increasingly dominated by relatively aged
accumulation-mode aerosols near ~80–150 nm. These observations suggest stronger surface-layer mixing
and local aerosol processing near the ground combined with more regionally mixed and aged aerosol
populations aloft. The absence of distinct ultrafine particle enhancements aloft further suggests that
aerosol populations within the column had already undergone substantial condensational growth and
aging prior to observation.

The second regime is characterized by elevated young-particle populations and vertically decoupled
aerosol layers aloft (Figure 4b, red dash box). These profiles frequently exhibited enhanced
concentrations of intermediate-sized particles (~20–40 nm) within elevated layers between ~300 and 700
m, implying potential new particle formation, while lower layers remained comparatively dominated by
more stable accumulation-mode aerosol populations. In several cases, dual aerosol layers were observed
(Figure 4c), with one aerosol population near the surface and a second elevated young-particle-rich layer
separated by weaker intermediate regions, indicating dynamically separated aerosol evolution within the
lower troposphere. These structures suggest elevated aerosol processing, residual-layer transport,
entrainment-related mixing, or new particle formation as well as related growth occurring within layers
separated from the surface aerosol population. Importantly, several elevated young-particle layers were
not clearly reflected in concurrent surface measurements, suggesting that surface observations alone may
not fully capture vertically separated aerosol evolution processes or elevated ultrafine particle reservoirs.

2.4 Residual-Layer Transition and Elevated Ultrafine Particle
 Reservoirs
Several profiles exhibited sharp transitions in aerosol structure near ~900–1200 m, where aerosol
concentrations and accumulation-mode particle populations decreased substantially, suggesting that the
TBS crossed the top of the well-mixed aerosol layer into cleaner transition-layer or residual-layer
environments (Figure 4d). Below these transition heights, aerosol populations were generally relatively
coherent and dominated by particles within the ~60–120-nm size range, consistent with well-mixed lower

 5

 atmospheric aerosol layers. However, despite the pronounced reduction in overall aerosol loading above
 the transition regions, elevated ultrafine and small Aitken-mode particle populations frequently remained
 detectable near ~1200–1400 m, indicating possible new particle formation or residual-layer particle
 reservoirs.

 Generally speaking, these TBS observations revealed these recurring aerosol vertical structure regimes
 that warrant further investigation using integrated meteorological, chemical, and modeling analyses.
 Future research opportunities include examining the relationships between aerosol vertical morphology,
 boundary-layer evolution, residual-layer transport, and elevated aerosol processing. Additional analyses
 combining aerosol size distributions with trace gas observations, aerosol composition, and meteorological
 measurements may help better constrain the mechanisms governing elevated young-particle populations
 and vertically decoupled aerosol layers.

 The observations also suggest that surface measurements alone may not fully capture vertically separated
 aerosol evolution processes occurring aloft. Future work integrating TBS observations with surface
 networks, remote-sensing measurements, and regional models could help improve understanding of
 aerosol transport, growth, and aerosol-cloud interactions across different atmospheric environments.

4.0 Lessons Learned
 The campaign demonstrated the importance of vertically resolved aerosol observations for understanding
 aerosol layering, residual-layer evolution, and elevated young-particle populations that may not be
 captured by surface measurements alone. Future campaigns would benefit from longer-duration and
 higher-frequency vertical observations integrated with ARM meteorological and remote-sensing
 measurements to better characterize aerosol evolution and boundary-layer processes under different
 atmospheric environments.
