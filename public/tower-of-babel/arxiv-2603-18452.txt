Abstract
We introduce the Pólya threshold graph model and derive its stochastic and algebraic properties. This random threshold graph is generated sequentially via a two-color Pólya urn process. Starting from an empty graph, each time step involves a draw from the urn that produces an indicator variable, determining whether a newly added node is universal (connected to all existing nodes and itself) or isolated (connected to no existing nodes). This construction yields a random threshold graph with an adjacency matrix that admits an explicit representation in terms of the draw sequence. Using the structure of the Pólya draw process, we derive the exact degree distribution for any arbitrary node, including its mean and variance. Furthermore, we evaluate a distance-based decay centrality score and provide an explicit expression for its expectation. On the algebraic side, we explicitly characterize the Laplacian matrix of the random threshold graph, obtaining a closed-form description of its spectrum and corresponding eigenbasis. Finally, as an application of these structural results, we analyze discrete-time consensus dynamics on Pólya threshold graphs. Keywords: Pólya urn, random threshold graphs, Laplacian spectrum, degree distribution, decay centrality, consensus dynamics

Introduction

In this paper, we define a class of threshold graphs in which nodes and edges are generated randomly and sequentially via a two-color Pólya urn process. At each time step, a draw from the urn determines whether the newly added node is an isolated node or a universal node, and each realization of this process produces a threshold graph. Such a construction yields a random threshold graph that provides a reinforcement-driven dependent extension of sequential threshold graph generation.

Threshold graphs have been an important topic in both graph theory and network science. Following
[
11
,
18
,
3
]
, threshold graphs admit several equivalent characterizations, including a weight and threshold inequality and a sequential binary construction formed by repeatedly adding either universal or isolated nodes. They serve as fundamental models for systems exhibiting a nested structure and specific dynamic properties, see
[
14
]
. Furthermore, the rich algebraic structure of deterministic threshold graphs has motivated extensive studies on their Laplacian spectra, including the characterization of Laplacian controllability for multi-agent networks
[
1
]
, spanning trees
[
4
]
, critical groups
[
6
]
, and maximum Laplacian energy
[
9
]
.

It is natural to consider random versions of threshold graphs.
23
have introduced natural equivalent models for random threshold graphs and used them to derive a variety of graph-theoretic properties. Diaconis
et al.
further studied the threshold graph limits and placed several random threshold graph models into a broader probabilistic framework
[
8
]
. Subsequent work has also examined structural questions such as connectivity in random threshold graph models
[
19
]
.

Motivated by prior works on random threshold graphs, we introduce the class of Pólya threshold graphs, in which the binary node type construction is generated by a reinforced Pólya urn process rather than by an independent sampling rule. This is a natural choice because the sequential construction of threshold graphs is binary; meanwhile, the Pólya urn process also provides a canonical way to generate dependent sequences through reinforcement. Recent work has also shown that the Pólya urn process can be used to generate other classes of random graphs, such as preferential attachment graphs
[
29
]
. As a result, the model remains analytically tractable while allowing the node types to be generated through the dependent random process.

Exploiting this tractability, our work is structured into three main parts, after providing background material on the Pólya urn model and random threshold graphs in Section
2
. First, in Section
3
, we present an explicit adjacency matrix representation and characterize the stochastic properties of the Pólya threshold graph, deriving the exact degree distribution for an arbitrary node
V_{i}
, together with its mean and variance, as well as an expression for the expected decay centrality score. Second, also in Section
3
, we establish the algebraic properties of the model, including the Laplacian matrix, the Laplacian spectrum and a corresponding eigenbasis. Third, in Section
4
, we study the discrete-time consensus dynamics on connected Pólya threshold graphs, compare the theory with simulations, and explore an extension to the finite-memory Pólya urn setting. Finally, we conclude the paper in Section
5
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Pólya urn model

We start by recalling the setup for the classical Pólya urn model
[
22
]
. Consider an urn equipped with red and black balls. At the initial time
t=0
, the urn has
R>0
red balls and
B>0
black balls, for a total of
T=R+B
initial balls. At each discrete time step
t\geq 1
, we draw a ball from the urn at random, and place it back along with
\Delta>0
extra balls of the same color. We allow the number of balls to be a positive real number, since draws depend only on color proportions; allowing positive real ball counts does not alter the model. We naturally define the draw indicator random variable
Z_{t}
at time
t
as follows

\displaystyle Z_{t}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if draw at time $t$ is red,}\\
0&\text{if draw at time $t$ is black.}\end{cases}

(1)

It is well-known that the stochastic process
\{Z_{t}\}_{t=1}^{\infty}
is
exchangeable
(and thus strictly stationary), see
[
22
,
2
]
, where by exchangeability we mean that for any positive integer
\ell
, the draw variables
Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{\ell}
have an invariant joint distribution with respect to all permutations of the indices
1,2,\ldots,\ell
.
Specifically, following
[
2
]
, the joint probability of observing a specific length-
n
sequence
z^{n}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with exactly
k=\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}
red draws is given by

\displaystyle P(Z_{1}=z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}=z_{n})=\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(R+i\Delta)\prod_{j=0}^{n-k-1}(B+j\Delta)}{\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}(R+B+m\Delta)}.

(2)

By defining the urn’s initial red balls proportion
\rho=\frac{R}{R+B}
and the reinforcement parameter
\delta=\frac{\Delta}{R+B}
, the joint distribution above can be written as

\displaystyle P(Z_{1}=z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}=z_{n})=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{\delta})\Gamma(\frac{\rho}{\delta}+k)\Gamma(\frac{1-\rho}{\delta}+n-k)}{\Gamma(\frac{\rho}{\delta})\Gamma(\frac{1-\rho}{\delta})\Gamma(\frac{1}{\delta}+n)},

(3)

where
\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}dt
is the
Gamma function
.
The above distribution exhibits a Beta-Binomial structure, which will be instrumental in deriving the properties of our model in Section
3
.

The Pólya urn-based draw process
\{Z_{t}\}_{t=1}^{\infty}
enjoys many other interesting properties, including the fact that
its sample average
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
converges almost surely as
n
grows without bound to a random variable governed by the Beta distribution,
\text{Beta}\big(\frac{\rho}{\delta},\frac{1-\rho}{\delta}\big)
, with parameters
\frac{\rho}{\delta}
and
\frac{1-\rho}{\delta}
; in particular, this implies that the draw process is non-ergodic, see
[
22
]
.

2.2
Random threshold graphs

In this section, we introduce
threshold graphs
, following
[
18
]
. An undirected graph

Definition 1

Given a size-

graph
\mathbf{G}_{n}=(\mathbf{V}_{n},\mathbf{E}_{n})

\mathbf{V}_{n}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}

\displaystyle A_{n}=[a_{ij}],

(4)

\displaystyle a_{ij}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $(v_{i},v_{j})\in\mathbf{E}_{n}$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(5)

Definition 2
.

A graph
\mathbf{G}_{n}=(\mathbf{V}_{n},\mathbf{E}_{n})

\Phi:\mathbf{V}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that for any

\displaystyle\Phi(v_{i})+\Phi(v_{j})>\tau\textit{\hskip 10.22217ptif and only if\hskip 10.22217pt}(v_{i},v_{j})\in\mathbf{E}_{n},

(6)

where
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

The next result provides an equivalent characterization of threshold graphs.

Lemma 1
.

A graph
\mathbf{G}_{n}=(\mathbf{V}_{n},\mathbf{E}_{n})
is a threshold graph if and only if there exists a binary
n
-tuple
z^{n}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
, where
z_{t}\in\{0,1\}
for all

, such that
\mathbf{G}_{n}
can be constructed sequentially, up to node relabeling, using

from an empty graph at time step
t=0

at time steps

that either connects to all existing nodes (i.e., nodes
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t-1}
) and itself when

Note that the above lemma is equivalent to stating that the resulting edge set

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}^{*}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{E}^{*}_{t-1}&\textit{if $z_{t}=0$},\\
\mathbf{E}^{*}_{t-1}\cup\{(v_{t},v_{1}),\ldots,(v_{t},v_{t})\}&\textit{if $z_{t}=1$},\end{cases}

(7)

with

3
Pólya threshold graph model and properties

We introduce the notion of a Pólya threshold graph and study its stochastic and algebraic properties. Throughout, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the notation
V_{i}
denotes the node added to the graph at time step
i
.

As stated, we use the two-color Pólya urn model as a way to generate random threshold graphs in a sequential manner.
Starting with an empty graph and a two-color Pólya urn, at time steps
i\geq 1
, we draw one ball from the Pólya urn and obtain the indicator draw variable
Z_{i}\in\{0,1\}
using (
1
), i.e.,
Z_{i}=1
if
i
-th draw is red and
Z_{i}=0
if
i
-th draw is black. Given the observed
Z_{i}
, we classify the node type, add the new node
V_{i}
and edges to the graph according to the threshold rule (
7
), i.e., if
Z_{i}=1
, then
V_{i}
is a
universal node
, if
Z_{i}=0
, then
V_{i}
is an
isolated node
.
We repeat this draw process until we obtain a random threshold graph
\mathcal{G}_{n}=(\mathcal{V}_{n},\mathcal{E}_{n})
of size
n
.
We call the random threshold graph generated in this fashion a
Pólya threshold graph
.

To describe the adjacency matrix of our threshold graph, we observe that the edge between any pair of nodes depends only on the draw indicator of the more recently added node (i.e., the node with the largest time index).
Therefore, the adjacency matrix
A_{n}=[a_{ij}],i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
of the Pólya threshold graph is the random variable given by:

\displaystyle A_{n}=[a_{ij}]=[Z_{\max(i,j)}]=\begin{bmatrix}Z_{1}&Z_{2}&Z_{3}&\cdots&Z_{n}\\
Z_{2}&Z_{2}&Z_{3}&\cdots&Z_{n}\\
Z_{3}&Z_{3}&Z_{3}&\cdots&Z_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Z_{n}&Z_{n}&Z_{n}&\cdots&Z_{n}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(8)

The trace of the adjacency matrix is given as

\mathrm{Tr}(A_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}.

Furthermore, for each node
V_{i}
in the graph, its degree
\deg(V_{i})
, which counts the number of edges connected to
V_{i}
including a potential self-loop, is a random variable determined by draw variables
Z_{i},\ldots,Z_{n}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\deg(V_{i})=iZ_{i}+Z_{i+1}+\ldots+Z_{n}=iZ_{i}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}Z_{j},

(9)

where
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

We begin our analysis by quantifying the stochastic properties of the Pólya threshold graph induced by the urn reinforcement. A fundamental quantity of interest in any random graph model is the degree distribution of its nodes, as it directly dictates the network’s connectivity. As shown in (
9
), the degree of any node
V_{i}
relies on both its own draw indicator
Z_{i}
and the partial sum of the subsequent draw variables. Consequently, the exchangeability and the Beta-Binomial structure of the Pólya urn process play a central role in our analysis. To derive the exact degree distribution, we first recall the standard definition of the Beta function and highlight the Beta-Binomial distribution exhibited by the indicator draw variables.

Definition 3

(Beta Function)
.

The Beta function
\mathbf{B}:\real_{>0}\times\real_{>0}\rightarrow\real_{>0}
is given by:

\mathbf{B}(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(x+y)}.

In light of (
3
), we have that

\displaystyle P\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}=k\bigg)

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{k}\frac{\mathbf{B}(\frac{\rho}{\delta}+k,\frac{1-\rho}{\delta}+n-k)}{\mathbf{B}(\frac{\rho}{\delta},\frac{1-\rho}{\delta})},\qquad k\in\{0,\ldots,n\},

(10)

where
\rho=\frac{R}{R+B}
and
\delta=\frac{\Delta}{R+B}
, see also
[
15
]
. The right-hand side of (
10
) is nothing but the Beta-Binomial distribution with parameters
n
,
\frac{\rho}{\delta}
and
\frac{1-\rho}{\delta}
.

Moreover, since the normalized trace of the adjacency matrix satisfies
\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}(A_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}
, it follows from Section
2.1
that
\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}(A_{n})
converges almost surely as
n\rightarrow\infty
to a Beta random variable
\mathrm{Beta}\big(\frac{\rho}{\delta},\frac{1-\rho}{\delta}\big)
.

n
times from a Pólya urn with

initial balls (
R
red and
B

. Throughout, we let

denote the sequence of draw indicators generated by the Pólya urn-based on (
1

of the graph, its mean and variance, as well as its centrality score. We first show that the expected degree is the same for all nodes.

\displaystyle E(\deg(V_{i}))=n\rho,

\displaystyle=E(iZ_{i}+Z_{i+1}+\cdots+Z_{n})

\displaystyle=E(iZ_{i})+E(Z_{i+1})+\ldots+E(Z_{n})

\displaystyle=iE(Z_{i})+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}E(Z_{j}).

By the exchangeability of the draw process

is a universal node and has degree at least

when generated from time steps

can take is

. We have the following result.

Theorem 1
.

For the Pólya threshold graph, the probability distribution of

is given by

\displaystyle P(\deg(V_{i})=k)=\binom{n-i}{k}g_{i}^{(n-i+1)}(k)\mathbb{I}(k\leq n-i)+\binom{n-i}{k-i}g_{i}^{(n-i+1)}(k-i+1)\mathbb{I}(k\geq i),

We first prove an intermediate result.

For all

Proof.

In light of the expression in (

of

.
This yields the stated two-term decomposition in (

Proof of Theorem

.

By Lemma

\displaystyle P(\deg(V_{i})=k)

\displaystyle=P\Big(Z_{i}=1,\sum^{n}_{j=i+1}Z_{j}=k-i\Big)\mathbb{I}(k\geq i)+P\Big(Z_{i}=0,\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}Z_{j}=k\Big)\mathbb{I}(k\leq n-i)

\displaystyle=\binom{n-i}{k-i}P(Z_{1}=1,\ldots,Z_{k-i+1}=1,Z_{k-i+2}=0,\ldots Z_{n-i+1}=0)\mathbb{I}(k\geq i)

\displaystyle P(\deg(V_{i})=k)

\displaystyle=\binom{n-i}{k-i}g_{i}^{(n-i+1)}(k-i+1)\mathbb{I}(k\geq i)+\binom{n-i}{k}g_{i}^{(n-i+1)}(k)\mathbb{I}(k\leq n-i),

(27)

While the degree distribution precisely characterizes a node’s local connectivity, it does not fully describe its global position within the network. To capture a node’s influence beyond local degree statistics, we consider the decay centrality score, see
[
32
]
. Widely used to analyze network efficiency, this metric evaluates network influence by discounting low-degree nodes. We next evaluate the centrality score of the Pólya threshold graph.

Definition 5

(
[
32
]
)
.

The centrality score of an arbitrary node
V_{i}
in a graph of size
n
is defined as

\displaystyle C_{V_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha^{d(V_{i},V_{j})},

(29)

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

In this paper, we adopt the standard choice of
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
.

Next, we denote
d(V_{i},V_{j})
as the shortest distance between nodes
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
. We adopt the convention that a node without a self-loop is treated as disconnected from itself, i.e.,
d(V_{i},V_{i})=\infty
when
Z_{i}=0
, see for instance,
[
5
]
.

For the Pólya threshold graph, the distance between nodes
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
is given by

\displaystyle d(V_{i},V_{j})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $Z_{i}=1,i=j$, }\\
\infty&\text{ if $Z_{i}=0,i=j$,}\\
1&\text{ if $Z_{\max(i,j)}=1,i\neq j$,}\\
2&\text{ if $Z_{\max(i,j)}=0$ and $\sum^{n}_{k=\max(i,j)+1}Z_{k}\geq 1,i\neq j$,}\\
\infty&\text{if $\sum^{n}_{k=\max(i,j)}Z_{k}=0,i\neq j$.}\end{cases}

(30)

Lemma 6
.

The probability distribution of the distance
d(V_{i},V_{j})
in (
30
) is given by

\displaystyle P(d(V_{i},V_{i})=0)=\rho,

\displaystyle P(d(V_{i},V_{i})=\infty)=1-\rho,

\displaystyle P(d(V_{i},V_{j})=1)=\rho,\quad i\neq j,

\displaystyle P(d(V_{i},V_{j})=2)=1-\Big[\rho+\Big(\prod_{s=0}^{n-{\max(i,j)}}\frac{1-\rho+s\delta}{1+s\delta}\Big)\Big],\quad i\neq j,

\displaystyle P(d(V_{i},V_{j})=\infty)=\prod_{s=0}^{n-{\max(i,j)}}\frac{1-\rho+s\delta}{1+s\delta},\quad i\neq j.

Proof.

First, for
i=j
,
d(V_{i},V_{i})=0
if and only if
Z_{i}=1
(a self–loop is present), and
d(V_{i},V_{i})=\infty
otherwise, which gives

\displaystyle P(d(V_{i},V_{i})=0)=P(Z_{i}=1)=\rho,

\displaystyle P(d(V_{i},V_{i})=\infty)=P(Z_{i}=0)=1-\rho.

Without loss of generality, let
i<j
. If
Z_{j}=1
, then
V_{j}
is a universal node and directly connects to
V_{i}
, so
d(V_{i},V_{j})=1
, where

P(d(V_{i},V_{j})=1)=P(Z_{j}=1)=\rho.

If
Z_{j}=0
, then
V_{j}
is isolated at time
j
. If some later node is universal, it connects to both
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, giving distance
2
; if no later node is universal, there is no path connecting
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, and the distance is
\infty
. The stated probabilities follow from exchangeability and the Pólya joint law of the draw random variables:

\displaystyle P(d(V_{i},V_{j})=2)

\displaystyle=P\bigg(Z_{j}=0,\sum_{k={j+1}}^{n}Z_{k}\geq 1\bigg)

\displaystyle=P(Z_{j}=0)-P\bigg(Z_{j}=0,\sum_{k={j+1}}^{n}Z_{k}=0\bigg)

\displaystyle=(1-\rho)-\Bigg[\prod_{s=0}^{n-{j}}\frac{1-\rho+s\delta}{1+s\delta}\Bigg].

Finally,

\displaystyle P(d(V_{i},V_{j})

\displaystyle=\infty)=P\bigg(\sum_{k=j}^{n}Z_{k}=0\bigg)

\displaystyle=(1-\rho)\frac{1-\rho+\delta}{1+\delta}\cdots\frac{1-\rho+(n-j)\delta}{1+(n-j)\delta}

\displaystyle=\prod_{s=0}^{n-j}\frac{1-\rho+s\delta}{1+s\delta},

as claimed.
∎

Using these distance probabilities, we now derive the expected centrality score.

Theorem 3
.

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\displaystyle E(C_{V_{i}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\Bigg[\frac{\rho}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\bigg(1-\prod_{s=0}^{n-{\max(i,j)}}\frac{1-\rho+s\delta}{1+s\delta}\bigg)\Bigg]+\rho.

(31)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle E(C_{V_{i}})

\displaystyle=E\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{d(V_{i},V_{j})}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=E\bigg(\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{d(V_{i},V_{j})}}\bigg)+E\bigg(\frac{1}{2^{d(V_{i},V_{i})}}\bigg).

(32)

Since
d(V_{i},V_{i})=0
when
Z_{i}=1
and
d(V_{i},V_{i})=\infty
when
Z_{i}=0
,

\displaystyle E\bigg(\frac{1}{2^{d(V_{i},V_{i})}}\bigg)=\bigg[\frac{1}{2^{0}}P(Z_{i}=1)+\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2^{s}}P(Z_{i}=0)\bigg]=\rho.

And the first term in (
3
) can be shown as

\displaystyle E\bigg(\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{d(V_{i},V_{j})}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\bigg[\frac{1}{2^{1}}P(Z_{\max(i,j)}=1)+\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2^{s}}P\bigg(\sum_{k=\max(i,j)}^{n}Z_{k}=0\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2^{2}}P\bigg(Z_{\max(i,j)}=0,\sum_{k=\max(i,j)+1}^{n}Z_{k}\geq 1\bigg)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\Bigg[\frac{\rho}{2}+0+\frac{1}{4}\bigg(1-\rho-P\bigg(\sum_{k=\max(i,j)}^{n}Z_{k}=0\bigg)\bigg)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\Bigg[\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\bigg(1-\rho-\prod_{s=0}^{n-{\max(i,j)}}\frac{1-\rho+s\delta}{1+s\delta}\bigg)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\Bigg[\frac{\rho}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\bigg(1-\prod_{s=0}^{n-{\max(i,j)}}\frac{1-\rho+s\delta}{1+s\delta}\bigg)\Bigg],

and substituting the probabilities in Lemma
6
above yields (
31
).
∎

Having established the stochastic properties of the Pólya threshold graph, including the degree distribution and centrality scores, we then conclude this section by characterizing some of its algebraic properties. As a fundamental algebraic representation of a network, the Laplacian matrix captures the deep structural connectivity of the network, and provides the essential mathematical foundation for studying linear processes over the graph
[
10
]
. To this end, we first formally define the degree and Laplacian matrices of the Pólya threshold graph.

Definition 6

(
Degree and Laplacian matrices
)
.

The degree matrix
D_{n}
of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is the random variable given by the diagonal matrix whose
i
-th diagonal entry is
\deg(V_{i})
:

D_{n}:=\mathrm{diag}\big(\deg(V_{1}),\ldots,\deg(V_{n})\big).

The Laplacian matrix of the Pólya threshold graph is the random variable defined by

\displaystyle L_{n}:=D_{n}-A_{n}.

(33)

Hence, in light of (
8
) and (
9
), the entries of
L_{n}
satisfy

(L_{n})_{ij}=\begin{cases}\deg(V_{i})-Z_{i},&\text{if }i=j,\\[1.99997pt]
-Z_{\max(i,j)},&\text{if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

(34)

for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Next, we describe the spectrum of
L_{n}
.
While the Laplacian spectrum of a deterministic threshold graph is known to be determined by its degree sequence
[
20
,
13
,
16
]
, the eigenvalues are not, in general, equal to the degrees. By contrast, for our Pólya threshold graph, the nonzero Laplacian eigenvalues coincide with the degrees of the corresponding nodes, beginning with the second node. We now establish this result and derive a corresponding eigenbasis.
Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
L_{n}
be the Laplacian matrix of a Pólya threshold graph with
n
nodes. Then the multiset of eigenvalues of
L_{n}
,
\Lambda(L_{n})
, is given by

\displaystyle\Lambda(L_{n})=\{0,\deg(V_{2}),\deg(V_{3}),\ldots,\deg(V_{n})\}=\Big\{0,\;2Z_{2}+\!\!\sum_{j=3}^{n}Z_{j},\;\ldots,\;nZ_{n}\Big\}.

(35)

A corresponding eigenbasis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

\displaystyle u_{1}

\displaystyle=(1,1,\ldots,1)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(36)

\displaystyle u_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}e_{i}-(m-1)e_{m}=(\,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{m-1},\,-(m-1),\,\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-m}\,)^{\top},

(37)

where
m=2,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

First, observe that for every
i
, the sum of the entries in the
i
-th row of
A_{n}
equals
\deg(V_{i})
, and
a_{ii}=Z_{i}
.
Hence, the
i
-th entry of
L_{n}u_{1}
is

(L_{n}u_{1})_{i}=\big(\deg(V_{i})-Z_{i}\big)-\sum_{j\neq i}Z_{\max(i,j)}=0,

where we have used (
9
). Thus,
u_{1}
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
0
.

Fix
m\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
and consider

u_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}e_{i}-(m-1)e_{m}.

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

(L_{n}u_{m})_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(L_{n})_{ij}(u_{m})_{j}.

The only nonzero coordinates of
u_{m}
are
(u_{m})_{k}=1
for
1\leq k\leq m-1
and
(u_{m})_{m}=-(m-1)
, with
(u_{m})_{k}=0
for
k>m
.
Using the explicit form of
L_{n}
in (
33
)-(
34
), we have

\displaystyle(L_{n}u_{m})_{i}=\begin{cases}(i-1)(-Z_{i})+(\deg(V_{i})-Z_{i})-\sum_{j=i+1}^{m-1}Z_{j}-(m-1)(-Z_{m}),&1\leq i\leq m-1,\\[1.99997pt]
(m-1)(-Z_{m})-(m-1)\big[\deg(V_{m})-Z_{m}\big],&i=m,\\[1.99997pt]
(m-1)(-Z_{i})-(m-1)(-Z_{i}),&i>m.\end{cases}

(38)

Substituting (
9
) into (
38
) yields

(L_{n}u_{m})_{i}=\begin{cases}\deg(V_{m}),&1\leq i\leq m-1,\\[1.99997pt]
-(m-1)\deg(V_{m}),&i=m,\\[1.99997pt]
0,&i>m,\end{cases}

that is,

L_{n}u_{m}=\deg(V_{m})\,u_{m}.

Thus,
u_{m}
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
\deg(V_{m})
.
The vectors
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
are linearly independent, by construction, and therefore they form a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
consisting of eigenvectors of
L_{n}
, yielding the claim.
∎

Remark 1
.

An interesting and distinct feature of the Laplacian spectrum of the Pólya threshold graph established in Theorem
4
is that its eigenvalues are random variables, whereas its corresponding eigenvector basis is entirely deterministic. This is because the graph is constructed recursively by adding either isolated or universal nodes, with node types determined by the Pólya urn draws. As a result, the randomness affects only the eigenvalues, while the deterministic structure of threshold graphs fixes the Laplacian eigenvector basis, in contrast to classical random graph models where both eigenvectors and eigenvalues are random.

Furthermore, since Theorem
4
expresses the Laplacian eigenvalues in terms of the node degrees, the probability distribution of the individual Laplacian eigenvalues is fully characterized by the node degree distribution derived in Theorem
1
. This high tractability provides a solid foundation for studying various spectral notions of the network, such as its algebraic connectivity (the second smallest Laplacian eigenvalue, given by
\min_{2\leq i\leq n}\deg(V_{i})
), its largest eigenvalue, and empirical spectral distribution.

4
Application to consensus

As an application, following
[
7
,
21
,
26
]
, we analyze the consensus limit for the linear averaging dynamics induced by the Pólya threshold graph. Consensus dynamics of random graphs is a well studied subject
[
31
]
, and our objective here is merely to relate the consensus limit to the stochastic properties of the Pólya threshold graph.
Throughout this section, we consider a
connected
size-
n
Pólya threshold graph obtained by fixing
Z_{n}
to
1
(equivalently, by enforcing the last added node to be universal). Hence,
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is connected for every
n
. If this condition is not guaranteed, the graph may decompose into a connected component and multiple isolated nodes, and the results apply to the connected component.

4.1
Consensus limit

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we assign an initial opinion
x_{i}(0)\in\mathbb{R}
to the node
V_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
. At each discrete time
t\geq 1
, the node
V_{i}
updates its opinion
X_{i}(t)
by averaging its own previous opinion with those of its neighbors:

\displaystyle X_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{i}}\bigg[X_{i}(t-1)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}a_{ij}\,X_{j}(t-1)\bigg]

(39)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{i}}\bigg[X_{i}(t-1)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}Z_{\max(i,j)}\,X_{j}(t-1)\bigg],

(40)

where
X_{i}(0)=x_{i}(0)
and

N_{i}:=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}Z_{\max(i,j)}

denotes the number of neighbors of
V_{i}
in
\mathcal{G}_{n}
plus one.

To rewrite the updating rule (
40
) in a compact matrix form, for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we define the weight

W_{ij}:=\frac{\mathbb{I}(i=j)+\mathbb{I}(i\neq j)Z_{\max(i,j)}}{N_{i}},

and let
W=[W_{ij}]
be the corresponding
n\times n
matrix (deterministic given
Z^{n}=(z^{n-1},1)
is fixed). Then (
40
) can be written as

\displaystyle X_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{i}}\bigg[X_{i}(t-1)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}Z_{\max(i,j)}\,X_{j}(t-1)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}\,X_{j}(t-1).

If we denote
\bar{X}(t)=(X_{1}(t),\ldots,X_{n}(t))^{\intercal}
, then in vector form, we have

\displaystyle\bar{X}(t)

\displaystyle=W\bar{X}(t-1)=W^{t}\bar{x}(0),

(41)

where
\bar{x}(0)=(x_{1}(0),x_{2}(0),\ldots,x_{n}(0))^{\intercal}
.

Lemma 7
.

For any fixed draw realization
Z^{n}=(z^{n-1},1)
, the corresponding matrix
W=[W_{ij}]
is an irreducible, aperiodic and stochastic matrix.

Proof.

We first verify that each row of
W
sums to
1
. For any fixed
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{I}(i=j)+\mathbb{I}(i\neq j)z_{\max(i,j)}}{N_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}z_{\max(i,j)}}{N_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{i}}{N_{i}}

\displaystyle=1,

which implies that
W
is a stochastic matrix.

Since
Z_{n}=1
, node
V_{n}
is adjacent to all other nodes. Thus, for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
there is a path from
V_{i}
to
V_{j}
of length at most
2
through
V_{n}
. Hence
W
is irreducible.

Moreover,
W_{ii}=\frac{1}{N_{i}}>0
for all
i
, reflecting that each node always assigns positive weight to its own previous opinion in (
40
). Hence, the Markov chain associated with
W
has a positive self-transition probability for all states, and since
W
is irreducible, its period is
1
, i.e.,
W
is aperiodic.
∎

For
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, define

\pi^{*}:=(\pi^{*}_{1},\ldots,\pi^{*}_{n}),\qquad\pi^{*}_{i}:=\frac{N_{i}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}N_{k}}.

Here
\pi^{*}
is viewed as a row vector, so
\mathbf{1}\pi^{*}
is an
n\times n
rank-one stochastic matrix, where
\mathbf{1}
is a size-
n
column vector of
1
s. Following standard results in Markov chain theory
[
24
]
, we have the following property.

Lemma 8
.

For any fixed draw realization
Z^{n}=(z^{n-1},1)
, the vector
\pi^{*}
is the unique stationary distribution of
W

W^{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\mathbf{1}\pi^{*}.

Proof.

For any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, by the definition of
W_{ij}
, we have that

\displaystyle N_{i}W_{ij}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}(i=j)+\mathbb{I}(i\neq j)z_{\max(i,j)},

\displaystyle N_{j}W_{ji}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}(j=i)+\mathbb{I}(j\neq i)z_{\max(i,j)}.

Since the conditions
i\neq j
and
j\neq i
are equivalent, it follows that

N_{i}W_{ij}=N_{j}W_{ji},

with

\pi^{*}_{i}W_{ij}=\pi^{*}_{j}W_{ji}.

Fixing
j
and summing over
i
on both sides yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi^{*}_{i}W_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi^{*}_{j}W_{ji}

\displaystyle=\pi^{*}_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{ji}

\displaystyle=\pi^{*}_{j},

(42)

where we used the fact that each row of
W
sums to
1
. Thus,
\pi^{*}W=\pi^{*}
, i.e.,
\pi^{*}
is a stationary distribution of
W
. By Lemma
7
,
W
is irreducible and aperiodic, hence the stationary distribution is unique and the associated Markov chain is ergodic. Consequently, we have by
[
25
]
that

W^{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\mathbf{1}\,\pi^{*},

as claimed.
∎

We now use Lemma
8
to describe the consensus limit of the process.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{n}=(\mathcal{V}_{n},\mathcal{E}_{n})
be a connected Pólya threshold graph with draw indicator n-tuples
Z^{n}=(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n-1},1)
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E\big(\bar{X}(t)\big)=[\mathbf{1}\pi^{(E)}]\bar{x}(0),

where

\pi^{(E)}=(\pi^{(E)}_{1},\pi^{(E)}_{2},\ldots,\pi^{(E)}_{n})

and

\displaystyle\pi^{(E)}_{i}:=\sum_{z_{1}}\cdots\sum_{z_{n-1}}P(Z_{1}=z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n-1}=z_{n-1})\,\pi^{*}_{i}.

(43)

Proof.

From (
41
), we have
\bar{X}(t)=W^{t}\bar{x}(0)
. Since
\bar{x}(0)
is deterministic,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E\big(\bar{X}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E\big(W^{t}\big)\Big)\bar{x}(0).

Note that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E\big(W^{t}\big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{z_{1}}\cdots\sum_{z_{n-1}}P(Z_{1}=z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n-1}=z_{n-1})\,W^{t}.

Since the sum is over a finite state space
z^{n-1}\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}
, we may interchange the limit and the sum. For each fixed draw vector
Z^{n}=(z^{n-1},1)
, Lemma
8
implies that

W^{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\mathbf{1}\,\pi^{*}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E\big(W^{t}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z_{1}}\cdots\sum_{z_{n-1}}P(Z_{1}=z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n-1}=z_{n-1})\,\mathbf{1}\,\pi^{*},

and the stated expression for
\lim_{t\to\infty}E(\bar{X}(t))
follows immediately.
∎

Having established the consensus limit, our next goal is to show that
\pi^{(E)}
is the stationary distribution of the limiting expected averaging matrix, i.e.,

\pi^{(E)}\Big(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E(W^{t})\Big)=\pi^{(E)}.

To do so, we first verify that
\lim_{t\to\infty}E(W^{t})
is itself a stochastic matrix.

Lemma 9
.

The limit

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E(W^{t})

is a stochastic matrix. Moreover,
\lim_{t\to\infty}E(W^{t})
is irreducible and aperiodic.

Proof.

By Theorem
5
and Lemma
8
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E(W^{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{z_{1}}\cdots\sum_{z_{n-1}}P(Z_{1}=z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n-1}=z_{n-1})\,\mathbf{1}\,\pi^{*}.

(44)

Since
\mathbf{1}\pi^{*}
is a stochastic matrix, the limit
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E(W^{t})
is a convex combination of stochastic matrices and hence is itself a stochastic matrix.

Furthermore, the Pólya urn process assigns a strictly positive probability to each draw vector
z^{n-1}
, and
\pi^{*}_{i}>0
for every
i\in\{1\ldots,n\}
because
V_{n}
is a neighbor of all other nodes. It follows from (
44
) that every entry of
\lim_{t\to\infty}E(W^{t})
is strictly positive. Hence
\lim_{t\to\infty}E(W^{t})
is a positive (thus primitive) stochastic matrix, and in particular irreducible and aperiodic.
∎

Next, by Lemma
9
we obtain the following result.

Theorem 6
.

The vector
\pi^{(E)}=(\pi^{(E)}_{1},\ldots,\pi^{(E)}_{n})
defined by (
43
) is a stationary distribution of the limiting expected averaging matrix
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E(W^{t})
, which satisfies

\pi^{(E)}\Big[\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E(W^{t})\Big]=\pi^{(E)}.

Proof.

From (
44
) we can write the limiting matrix
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E(W^{t})
as
\mathbf{1}\,\pi^{(E)}
. Since
\pi^{(E)}
is a convex combination of probability vectors
\pi^{*}
, it is strictly a probability vector satisfying
\pi^{(E)}\mathbf{1}=1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\pi^{(E)}\Big[\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E(W^{t})\Big]

\displaystyle=\pi^{(E)}\big(\mathbf{1}\,\pi^{(E)}\big)=\big(\pi^{(E)}\mathbf{1}\big)\,\pi^{(E)}=\pi^{(E)},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

We emphasize that the consensus limits established in Theorems
5
and
6
are structurally decoupled from the specific joint distribution introduced by the Pólya urn process. The derivation relies fundamentally on the algebraic structure of the threshold graph’s adjacency matrix. Consequently, these results apply to any random threshold graph generated by an arbitrary binary stochastic process
\{Z_{i}\}^{n}_{i=1}
, provided that the last node is universal (i.e.,
Z_{n}=1
).

4.2.2
Finite-memory Pólya threshold graph

We also consider a finite memory version of the Pólya urn introduced in
[
2
]
, a variation often studied in the literature of networked Pólya urn processes, see
[
28
,
27
]
. In the Pólya threshold graph model discussed previously, every draw permanently alters the urn’s composition and, consequently, the probability of all future draws, thereby constituting an infinite-memory process. In the finite-memory variant, the only difference is that the
\Delta
reinforcement balls added at time step
t
are removed from the urn exactly
M
steps later (i.e., at time step
t+M
). Consequently, when
t\leq M
, the probability distribution remains unchanged as that of the (infinite-memory) Pólya urn process given in (
2
)-(
3
), as no reinforcement balls have been removed yet (thus the graph generated for
t\leq M
is the same as the original Pólya graph).

However, when
t>M
, this finite-memory setup fundamentally alters the underlying stochastic dependence of the draw process
\{Z_{t}\}
. Under this finite-memory assumption, the draw process
\{Z_{t}\}
becomes a Markov process of order
M
, as demonstrated in
[
2
]
. More specifically, the probability distribution is explicitly given by

\displaystyle P(Z_{1}=z_{1},\ldots,

\displaystyle Z_{n}=z_{n})=\left(\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}z_{m}\right)-1}(\rho+i\delta)\prod_{j=0}^{M-1-\sum_{m=1}^{M}z_{m}}(1-\rho+j\delta)}{\prod_{l=1}^{M-1}(1+l\delta)}\right)

(45)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=M+1}^{n}\left[\frac{\rho+\delta\sum_{m=1}^{M}z_{i-m}}{1+M\delta}\right]^{z_{i}}\left[\frac{1-\rho+\delta\left(M-\sum_{m=1}^{M}z_{i-m}\right)}{1+M\delta}\right]^{1-z_{i}},

for any
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
.

As stated in Remark
2
, since the network retains the threshold graph structure, the analytical framework established in Theorem
5
remains fully applicable.
To evaluate the consensus dynamics under this finite-memory regime, we simulate the connected Pólya threshold graph of size
n=10
and
n=100
with memory step
M
. To satisfy the connectivity condition
Z_{n}=1
, we generate the graphs by simulating first
n-1
nodes under the finite-memory process with the probability described above, and deterministically setting the final node to be a universal node (i.e.,
Z_{n}=1
). To capture the impact of memory length, we choose the initial opinion
\bar{x}(0)
to be a polarized vector, assigning
0
to the first half of
\bar{x}(0)
and
100
to the second half. By assigning maximally different initial opinions, any structural shift caused by memory length
M
becomes visible in the final consensus. For each

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced the Pólya threshold graph model, a random graph construction that combines the nested structure of threshold graphs with the reinforcement mechanism of the two-color Pólya urn process. We analytically derived its stochastic and algebraic properties. More specifically, based on the exchangeability and Beta-Binomial structure of the Pólya urn process, we obtained the degree distribution of any node in a given Pólya threshold graph, as well as its mean and variance. Furthermore, we derived an explicit formula for the expected decay centrality score.
Finally, we established the Laplacian matrix of the Pólya threshold graph and characterized its spectrum and corresponding eigenbasis.

As an application of these results, we analyzed discrete-time linear consensus dynamics over connected Pólya threshold graphs. We demonstrated that the consensus dynamics yield an irreducible and aperiodic averaging matrix, allowing us to fully characterize the limiting expected consensus value. The simulation results strongly align with our theoretical predictions, illustrating how highly connected nodes converge more rapidly toward the consensus value. Additionally, by exploring a finite-memory Pólya urn variant, we illustrated how the length of memory affects the resulting expected consensus.

Several directions for future research naturally arise from this work. One possible extension is the study of Pólya threshold graphs generated by multi-color Pólya urn models, which would allow for more general node-connection mechanisms. Another promising direction is the analysis of stochastic properties of the finite-memory model, including the investigation of its Markovian structure and the dependence of convergence rates on the memory size.
