 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 Novel cathode chemistries with proprietary SeaLion coating for long duration energy storage
 (LDES) will be assessed using standardized testing protocols to benchmark cell performance
 against existing high performing commercial battery chemistries.

 Summary of Research Results:

 This project aims to evaluate the partner’s novel electrode coating technologies, referred to as
 SeaLion coatings, which are applied to commercial lithium-ion (Li-ion) battery electrodes. The
 evaluations focus primarily on how the coatings affect the electrochemical performance of the
 electrodes as measured by standardized testing protocols. The results of these efforts outlined in
 the Statement of Work are summarized below.

 Task 1: CVD Coating of Commercial Electrodes

 Participant will engineer the coatings using their patented chemical vapor deposition (CVD)
 process onto commercially available lithium-ion battery electrodes (cathodes and anodes).
 Coated electrodes with at least 3 parameter variations for each the cathode and anode will be
 fabricated by the Participant and sent to the Contractor to be evaluated. The Participant will
 make two iterations of these coated electrodes and send them to the Contractor for basic
 evaluation. Feedback from the Contractor about the basic electrochemical performance of the 1st
 iteration will be used to optimize the CVD parameters used on the 2nd iteration. This iterative
 step with performance feedback will improve the likelihood of success in producing in
 electrochemical performance. The chemistry of the cathodes will be lithium cobalt oxide (LCO)
 or lithium manganese oxide (LMO) and will be paired against graphite anodes when tested in
 full cells.

 Task 1 Results:

 Electrodes supplied by NEI Corporation were chosen to ensure consistent and reproducible
 electrochemical performance, while allowing any effects of SeaLion’s applied coating to be
 reliably elucidated. Figure 1 lists basic specifications for the graphite (Gr) anode and lithium
 cobalt oxide (LCO) cathode sourced from NEI corporation. SeaLion’s first iteration of coatings
 that utilized catalyst 1 were applied to the LCO cathode but resulted in excessive strain buildup
 in the electrodes themselves resulting in curling and delamination of the composite film from the
 aluminum foil current collector, as seen in Figure 1 (left photos). SeaLion’s second iteration of
 coatings that utilized catalyst 2 were applied to the LCO cathode and resulted in intact electrodes
 from which 14 mm circular punches were taken for subsequent coin cell testing, as seen in
 Figure 1 (right photos). With the successful application of SeaLion’s coating utilizing catalyst 2
 to LCO cathodes, additional coatings with different coating times of 10, 15, 20, 30, and/or 40
 minutes were applied to both the NEI LCO cathodes and graphite anodes for subsequent
 electrochemical characterization.

 Task 2: Characterize Coating Morphology and Basic Electrochemistry

 The Contractor will characterize the morphology and structure of baseline uncoated electrodes,
 and the CVD coated electrodes. Basic electrochemical behaviors of all electrodes will be
 collected using Li-ion half cells with standard carbonate-based Li-ion battery electrolyte to
 determine voltage profiles, specific/areal capacities, and rate capacities. These basic
 electrochemical behaviors will be communicated back to the Participant to optimize CVD
 parameters for 2nd iteration of coated electrodes.

 Task 2 Results:

 The SeaLion coated Gr and LCO electrodes prepared in Task 1 were assembled into half-cell
 coin cells for assessment of their basic electrochemical performance in Li-ion batteries. All the
 cells were assembled in triplicate for each coating time, including an uncoated electrode for each
 the Gr anode and LCO cathode as baseline performance control samples. All the cells used
 Celgard 2325 separators and an electrolyte comprising 1.2 M LiPF6 in 3:7 ethylene
 carbonate:ethyl methyl carbonate (referred to as Gen 2 electrolyte). The Gr anode half-cells were
 cycled between 0.01-1.0V vs. Li/Li+ while the LCO cathodes were cycled between 2.7-4.3V vs.
 Li/Li+. Both the Gr anode and LCO cathode half-cells underwent a rate capability test,
 comprising 3x cycles at the following C-rates: C/10, C/5, C/2, 1C, 2C, 3C, 4C, C/10.

 3

 The specific capacity vs. cycle number plot for the Gr anode rate test is shown in Figure 2a. The
 SeaLion coatings had negligible effect on the Gr capacity delivered at all the rates, regardless of
 coating time. Most of the Gr anode capacity is accessible up to a rate of C/2, with a significant
 decline in delivered capacity at a 1C rate, suggesting that C/2 should be the fastest rate used for
 cycle life testing. The specific capacity vs. cycle number plot for the LCO cathode rate test is
 shown in Figure 2b. The SeaLion coatings applied for 10 and 20 minutes improve the LCO
 capacity delivered at the faster rates of 2C, 3C, and 4C.

 The LCO cathodes deliver most of their capacity at rates up to 1C, with more significant
 reduction in delivered capacity at the faster rates, suggesting that 1C should be the fastest rate
 used for cycle life testing. Figure 2c and 2d show the voltage profiles vs. areal capacity for the
 Gr anodes and LCO cathodes, respectively, at a C/2 rate. The areal capacities delivered at C/2
 are approximately 2 mAh/cm2 for the Gr anodes and approximately 1.75 mAh/cm2 for the LCO
 cathodes, indicating that the capacity ratios are appropriate for matching the SeaLion coated Gr
 and LCO together in full-cells for cycle life testing. Because the SeaLion coatings had negligible
 effect of electrochemical performance of the Gr anodes, we chose to only use the uncoated Gr
 anode in subsequent full-cells test with LCO cathode with different SeaLion coating times.
 Additionally, because only the shortest coating times on the LCO cathodes exhibited capacity
 improvements at the faster rates, the partner chose to fabricate a third iteration of SeaLion coated
 LCO cathodes with coating times of 10, 15 and 30 minutes to be tested in the full-cells.

 Task 3: Long Term Electrochemical Evaluation

 Contractor will fabricate appropriately capacity matched full-cells by pairing the best CVD
 coated cathodes against the best CVD coated graphite anodes with a standard carbonate-based
 Li-ion battery electrolyte. The full-cells will be tested using standard electrochemical cycling and
 calendar aging protocols detailed in the USABC battery test manual.

 Task 3 Results:

 Full-cells of uncoated Gr anodes paired with SeaLion coated LCO cathodes with coating times of
 10, 15, and 30 minutes were assembled as follows. A 15 mm diameter Gr anode, 19 mm Celgard
 2325 separator, and a 14 mm diameter SeaLion coated LCO (or uncoated LCO control) were
 layered in a 2023 coin cell with 40 µL of Gen2 electrolyte. The full-cells were tested using a
 United States Advanced Battery Consortium (USABC) adapted protocol to measure the cycle
 lifetime behaviors. The protocol comprises a repeating cycle of reference performance tests
 (RPTs) with galvanostatic cycling for 96x cycle in between RPTs. The RPTs include 3x slow
 C/10 cycles followed by a hybrid pulsed power characterization (HPPC) cycle, while the
 galvanostatic cycling was performed at a faster C/2 rate. The cells were all controlled at 30˚C in
 environmental chambers and the cell voltages were controlled between 3-4.2V.

 Figure 3 shows a full-cell energy density vs. cycle number plot (left) for all of the full-cells
 tested. The full-cell energy density is a calculated value from the collected coin cell data that
 normalizes the raw energy delivered values to the mass of a full-cell stack that uses these
 electrodes but with loading and stack configuration parameter adjusted to more closely reflect
 those typically utilized in scaled up commercial cells. The data shows higher energy values
 delivered during the slower rate RPT and HPPC cycles, while the faster C/2 cycles exhibit less
 energy delivered per cycle. For the cells containing the SeaLion coated LCO cathodes with the
 longer 15- and 30-minute coatings, the energy delivered is lower than the cell with the uncoated
 LCO control cathode for both the C/10 RPT and faster C/2 cycles.

 For the cell with the 10-minute coated SeaLion LCO cathode, the energy delivered during the
 C/10 RPT cycles is comparable to the cell with the uncoated LCO control cathode, while the
 faster C/2 cycles deliver more capacity than the cell with the uncoated LCO control cathode.
 This indicates that the 10-minute SeaLion coating on the LCO cathode is imparting some kinetic
 advantage to the cycling of the cell, allowing it to deliver more energy per cycle than the
 uncoated LCO control cell.

 This advantage is highlighted in the right plot of Figure 3, which shows the cumulative sum of
 energy delivered over the cell’s cycle lifetime. By the end of the 2000 cycle life test, the 10-
 minute SeaLion coated LCO cell has delivered approximately 50% more energy than the
 uncoated LCO control cell. However, the 15- and 30-minute SeaLion coated LCO cells exhibit
 less energy delivered than the control, indicating that there is an optimal coating thickness for the
 SeaLion coating to realize improvements in battery performance.

 Task 4: Additional Analysis and Testing

 Contractor will perform other work at the direction of the Participant, consistent with the scope
 and subject to the availability of funding. This may include post-mortem analysis of the full cells
 used to assess the cathodes to determine mechanistic causes of cell degradation. Additional
 iterations of CVD coatings on either cathode or anode could also be tested.

 Task 4 Results:

 To further validate the imparted advantages of a 10-minute SeaLion coating on LCO cathodes,
 single layer pouch cells comprising an uncoated Gr anode and the 10-minute SeaLion coated
 LCO cathode were fabricated. The single layer pouch cells were tested using the same USABC
 adapted protocols as the full coin cells. The electrolyte in the pouch cells was a formulation
 optimized for further improved performance, comprising 1 M lithium hexafluorophosphate
 (LiPF6) in 1:1:1 ethylene carbonate : dimethyl carbonate : diethyl carbonate + 2% vinylene
 carbonate.

 The photo on the left of Figure 4 shows one such pouch cell, while the energy vs. cycle number
 plot on the right of Figure 4 shows data collected from a triplicate batch of cells. This test is
 ongoing but will serve to further validate the performance enhancements observed when the 10-
 minute SeaLion coating is applied to LCO cathodes.
