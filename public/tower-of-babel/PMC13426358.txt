Wise as a Serpent and Gentle as a Dove: Pharmacological Targeting of NRF2/KEAP1 Pathway from Treasure Trove of Mother Nature
Authors: Ammad Ahmad Farooqi, Sabit Zhussupov, Assiya Turgambayeva, Ubaidilla Datkhayev, Gulnara Kamalbekova, Natalya Latypova, Samal Duisekova, Baojun Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDiscovery of NRF2 in 1994 was once assumed simplistic and classical but the following years witnessed phenomenal breakthroughs that led to a rapid increase in the components of complex molecular web. Emerging evidence has enabled us to develop a better understanding of the spatio-temporally placed molecular machinery both upstream and downstream to the NRF2 in different contexts and diseases. The exciting voyage to unravel the multifaceted roles of NRF2 in human diseases has catalyzed innovations in therapeutic approaches, inspiring the design of compounds for pharmacological modulation of NRF2 in disease prevention and intervention. In this review, we have set the spotlight on sketching a detailed landscape both upstream and downstream to the central signaling node of NRF2. Identification of the natural products having unique ability to pharmacologically target NRF2-associated networks. Later, we expand our discussion about ongoing and completed clinical trials related to multifaceted roles of NRF2 as an oncogene and a tumor suppressor. We argue that a holistic view of NRF2-driven upstream and downstream signaling is necessary for the identification of preventive or therapeutic opportunities.

Introduction

The multifaceted nature of cancer is becoming increasingly complex and intriguing because of the rapidly growing list of breakthroughs in molecular oncology. Reactive oxygen species (ROS) in biological systems are transient but essential molecules. These molecules are generated by specific enzymatic pathways and eliminated by a delicately balanced antioxidant defense system.1–3 From transformation and tumor initiation to metastatic dissemination, increasing ROS in cancer cells can paradoxically promote or suppress the tumorigenic process. This “double-edged sword” behavior depends on the extent of redox stress, its spatiotemporal characteristics and the tumor microenvironment. The NRF transcriptional factors NRF1, NRF2, and NRF3, are a subset of Cap “n” collar transcriptional regulators involved in regulating the expression of genes harboring antioxidant-response element (ARE) sequences within their promoter regions. KEAP1 interacts with the N-terminal located Neh2 domain of NRF2 and blocks its nuclear translocation. KEAP1/NRF2 is an important regulatory pathway and existing evidence highlights different mutations in this cascade.6–9 NRF2 mutations, particularly, R34G (Arg34Gly) had been shown to induce drug resistance in cancer cells.10 Therefore, status of NRF2 activity in aggressive and drug resistant cancers holds vital importance. Positron emission tomography imaging has provided analysis of NRF2 activation in patient-derived, orthotopic and genetically engineered rodent models. Combination of imaging and targeted treatment with activating NRF2/KEAP1 mutations may open new horizons for therapy-resistant cancer patients.11

Site-directed mutagenesis was used for detailed analysis of the functional role of different amino acids in KEAP1. It was shown that Cys151 in the BTB domain of KEAP1 had a central role in the regulation of NRF2. Adduction or oxidation at Cys151 induced conformational change in KEAP1 that resulted in dissociation of KEAP1 from CUL3, thereby inhibiting NRF2 ubiquitination.

With the aid of high-throughput technologies and the landmark discoveries of significant functionalities of NRF2, our connaissance of the regulatory roles of NRF2 in different stages of cancer development has evolved substantially. It is worth mentioning that NRF2 mechanistically modulates the expression of its target genes and centrally steers multiple stages of cancer.

There are some good reviews related to the role of KEAP1/NRF2 in different diseases and how this signaling axis can be exploited pharmacologically for an efficient and durable treatment option.12–15 In this review, we have addressed the dualistic roles of KEAP1/NRF2 signaling in different cancers. We have summarized how KEAP1/NRF2 signaling served as an oncogenic pathway and a tumor suppressor pathway in different cancers. How inputs through multiple effectors and crosstalks with other transduction cascades play a role in regulation of carcinogenesis and metastasis. Another important aim of this review is to rationalize these conflicting perspectives by critiquing the context dependence of NRF2 functions and pharmacological modulation for the inhibition of carcinogenesis and metastasis.

Role of NRF2/KEAP1 in Carcinogenesis

NRF2 signaling has been shown to centrally drive multiple stages of carcinogenesis and metastasis. In this section, we have summarized how different proteins enhanced the stability and activity of NRF2 by inhibition of KEAP1-mediated targeting of NRF2.

Peptidylprolyl isomerase A (PPIA) is required for the stability of NRF2. PPIA physically binds to NRF2 and prevents the accessibility of KEAP1 to NRF2, thus inhibiting the degradation of NRF2. Cyclosporin A, an FDA-approved drug, impairs the interaction of NRF2 with PPIA, thus inducing the ubiquitination and degradation of NRF2. NRF2-KLF5 signaling transcriptionally stimulates glutamine transporter SLC1A5 in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cells (Figure 3). Transportation of glutamine into cells through plasma membrane transporters (SLC1A5) is converted to glutamate by glutaminase to fuel the growth of cancer cells. Targeting of glutaminase for cancer inhibition is also an exciting area of research. Glutaminase inhibitor (CB-839) and cyclosporin A efficiently impeded the growth of the tumors in mice injected with A549 cells.16 Thus, the stability of NRF2 is regulated by different proteins that interfere with KEAP1-mediated targeting of NRF2. Dipeptidyl peptidase IV (DPP4) has an oncogenic role. Lysine acetyltransferase 5 (KAT5) and FosB synergistically stimulate the expression of DPP4 in papillary thyroid cancer (Figure 3). DPP4 potentiates the activation of NRF2 signaling and stimulation of p62. Resultantly, p62 competitively inhibits KEAP1-NRF2 interaction and triggers the activation of NRF2-mediated signaling. There was a considerable reduction in the number of hepatic and pulmonary metastatic nodules in mice injected with DPP4-silenced TPC-1 cells, while further overexpression of KAT5 or FosB led to an increase in the number of metastatic nodules.17

DPP9 is also an oncogenic protein. It contains ETGE and ESGE motifs. Mechanistically, DPP9 physically interacts with KEAP1 through ESGE and protects NRF2 from degradation by KEAP1 (Figure 4). Consequently, NRF2 levels are high in DPP9-overexpressing cancer cells. DPP9 overexpression suppresses ferroptotic death and induces sorafenib resistance in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) cells primarily through NRF2-mediated transcriptional upregulation of SLC7A11. SLC7A11 is a transporter of cystine for GSH biosynthesis, thereby protecting cells from oxidative stress and ferroptotic death (Figure 4). DPP9 knockout led to marked regression of tumors formed by 786-O cells and potentiated sorafenib-induced tumor inhibitory effects.18 Overall, targeted inhibition of DPP9 is necessary for the activation of KEAP1. Ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme E2C (UBE2C) overexpression promotes the interaction between p62 and KEAP1, thus interfering with the interaction between NRF2 and KEAP1. UBE2C-mediated inhibition of the interaction between NRF2 and KEAP1 leads to nuclear accumulation of NRF2. Notably, p62 depletion causes significant suppression in UBE2C-mediated increase in NRF2 levels. Furthermore, the silencing of UBE2C also inhibits the nuclear accumulation of NRF2.19

Carnitine palmitoyltransferase (CPT1B) interacts with KEAP1 and promotes the activation of NRF2. There is a considerable regression of the tumors and reduction in NRF2 expression in mice inoculated with CPT1B-knockdown MiaPaCa-2 cells. Gemcitabine-mediated chemotherapeutic effects were found to be enhanced in mice inoculated with CPT1B knockdown cancer cells.20

EIF4A is a component of eukaryotic initiation factor 4F (EIF4F) complex. CR-1-31B (CR-31), a synthetic rocaglate has been shown to potently inhibit eIF4A-mediated translation of NRF2. Tert-butylhydroquinone (tBHQ), a chemical oxidative stress inducer caused activation of NRF2. Loss of oncogenic NRF2 sufficiently blocked osteosarcoma growth, metastasizing ability and phenocopied the inhibitory effects of CR-1-31B on metastatic dissemination. Likewise, clinical-grade eFT226 (zotatifin), an EIF4A1 inhibitor enhanced oxidative stress and blocked the metastatic phenotype of tBHQ-treated osteosarcoma cells. CR-1-31B efficiently inhibited spontaneous lung metastasis in mice orthotopically implanted with MNNG cells.21

Sirtuin 1 (SIRT1) promoted nuclear accumulation of NRF2 with concomitant increase in the NRF2-regulated gene networks. High doxorubicin-resistance-induced pro-angiogenic, pro-proliferative and invasive effects were found to be impaired upon depletion of SIRT1. Findings indicated a considerable increase in tumor formation and invasion in mice xenografted with MDA-MB-231 high Dox-Res cancer cells.22

NADP+ is generated by phosphorylation of NAD+ by NAD+ kinase (NADK). Isoform 3 of NAD+ kinase is transcriptionally stimulated by NRF2. It has previously been revealed that metastasizing melanoma cells are fueled by NADPH regenerating pathways. Low NADPH/NADP+ ratio indicates higher level of oxidative stress. Importantly, ratio of NADPH/NADP+ was noted to be reduced in metastatic nodules as compared to the subcutaneous tumors. Cancer cells, both in circulation and during metastatic colonization undergo high levels of oxidative stress. While most cancer cells do not survive the metastatic cascade, but successful metastatically competent cancer cells gradually overcome oxidative stress. NADK Isoform 3 is specifically upregulated in metastatic nodules compared to the primary tumors. NADK Isoform 3 provides antioxidant protection to melanoma cells increasing extravasation and organ colonization by metastasizing melanoma cells.23

Nearly 50 years ago, researchers observed that cells in culture “exfoliated” small sacs of mysterious functions. Pioneering works have shown that these structures, now known as exosomes or extracellular vesicles, can contain RNA, proteins and other molecules. Exosomes are packaged by parent cells and delivered to recipient cells. Cells seem to use them to communicate with each other. Circ-0100519 is secreted in exosomes by breast cancer cells. Circ-0100519-loaded exosomes are taken up by tumor-associated macrophages and promote the polarization of macrophages to M2-phenotype (Figure 5). circ-0100519 acted as a scaffold and enhanced the interaction between the deubiquitinating enzyme ubiquitin-specific protease 7 (USP7) and NRF2 in macrophages. Circ-0100519 induced USP7-mediated deubiquitination of NRF2 (Figure 5). Whereas HIF-1α stimulated the expression of circ-0100519 in breast cancer cells (Figure 5). Injections of circ-0100519-silenced exosomes not only caused a reduction of the volume and weight of tumors but also prolonged the survival time of tumor-bearing mice. There was a considerable reduction in the population of F4/80+CD206+ M2-macrophages within tumor tissues of mice treated with circ-0100519-silenced exosomes. Furthermore, metastatic dissemination of 4T1 cancer cells was severely impaired in mice injected with circ-0100519-silenced exosomes.24 Overall, these findings indicated that oncogenic non-coding RNAs enhanced the stability of NRF2. There is a gradual increase in the multi-layered regulation of NRF2 by non-coding RNAs through intricate interplay between long non-coding RNAs/microRNAs and circular RNAs/microRNAs. ΔNp63α is frequently overexpressed in ESCC and drives transcriptional upregulation of Plectin (PLEC). PLEC impairs KEAP-mediated targeting of NRF2. ΔNp63α knockdown led to a significant reduction in the levels of NRF2. Moreover, low-dose irradiation induces NRF2 activation, consequently upregulating the expression of ΔNp63α.25

Free heme is a potent oxidative molecule that directly binds to BACH1 and promotes its proteasomal degradation. However, in response to stress, NRF2 is activated and it sequentially triggers the expression of genes involved in the catabolism of heme. Mechanistically, heme promotes FBXO22-mediated degradation of BACH1 (Figure 4). BACH1 depletion reduced metastases of tumors but FBXO22 depletion potently enhanced metastases in mice. Importantly, concomitant loss of BACH1 significantly impaired the formation of metastasis induced by the depletion of FBXO22, suggesting that BACH1 induced metastatic dissemination in the absence of FBXO22. An increase in the levels of BACH1 through the loss of KEAP1 or FBXO22 induced metastasis.26

Hepatitis B X-interacting protein (HBXIP) interacts with KEAP1 via its GLNLG motif and impairs KEAP1-mediated targeting of NRF2. LM-436 cells are a lung specifically metastatic sub-line obtained through four consecutive rounds of lung metastasis selections, after resection of the primary orthotopically implanted tumors. HBXIP knockdown led to significant reduction in the pulmonary metastatic nodules in mice injected with cancer cells.27

RagD belongs to small G protein family and a potent activator of mTOR pathway. There is a significant reduction in NRF2-mediated upregulation of RagD in NRF2-mutated cancer cells. NVP-BEZ235, a dual PI3K-mTOR inhibitor, efficiently inhibited tumor growth in xenografted rodent models. Mutant NRF2-expressing tumors demonstrated poorly differentiated carcinoma with increased mitotic activity and highly aggressive characteristics, such as invasion into the surrounding muscle and adipose tissues. Furthermore, there was occasional hepatic metastasis, which suggested that mutant NRF2 promoted invasive and metastasizing capacities of cancer cells.28

Cyclic ADP-ribose (cADPR) is a metabolite of NAD catalyzed by CD38. There is a considerable reduction in tumor growth and metastatic dissemination in immuno-competent mice inoculated with CD38 mutant and CD38 knockout tumor cells. 8-Br-cADPR (CD38 inhibitor) significantly inhibited tumor growth and tumor mass in mice inoculated with CD38-WT-expressing LLC-cells. Similarly, 8-Br-cADPR also increased the infiltration of CD3+ T cells within the tumor tissues. cADPR physically interacts with TRPM2 (ion channel) and induces the mobilization of intracellular Ca2+ via TRPM2 channels. There is a marked increase in the levels of KEAP1 in CD38 mutant and CD38 knockout tumor cells.29 Overall, these findings indicated that KEAP1-mediated degradation of NRF2 in CD38-mutant or knockout tumor cells inhibited carcinogenesis and metastasis.

lncRNA-EBLN3P, an oncogenic long non-coding RNA, interferes with KEAP1-mediated targeting of NRF2 and enhances radio-resistance in NSCLC cells (Figure 4). However, targeted inhibition of lncRNA-EBLN3P enhanced radio-sensitivity of NSCLC cells.30

Ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase L3 (UCHL3) potently promotes metastasis of cervical cancer cells. Overexpression of UCHL3 suppressed ubiquitination-mediated degradation of NRF2 in C3AA cells (Figure 4). Knockdown of UCHL3 significantly inhibited metastatic dissemination of cervical cancer cells and reduced the pulmonary metastatic nodules.31

Phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate synthetase 1 (PRPS1) has been shown to fuel the melanoma cells. NRF2 transcriptionally upregulates the expression of PRPS1 in melanoma cells (Figure 4). PRPS1 overexpression reduced the apoptotic death in melanoma cells but knockdown of PRPS1 increased the apoptosis in melanoma cells. Overexpression of PRPS1 in A875 cells significantly promoted tumor growth. BALB/c nude mice carrying PRPS1-overexpressing A875 cells significantly enhanced the formation of lung-specific metastasis and the expression levels of PRPS1 were correlated positively with the number of tumor foci.32

UBE2E2 overexpression induced an increase in the levels of NRF2 in A2780 and SKOV3 cells. UBE2E2 enhances the stability of SNAIL by inhibiting its ubiquitination. UBE2E2-depleted OvCa cells formed fewer tumor nodules in orthotopically established ovarian cancer xenografted models and intraperitoneal xenograft models.33

Broad complex, Tramtrack and Bric-a-Brac (BTB) domain of KEAP1 has mutations at two sites in CDDP-resistant cells. The BTB domain is involved in the physical interaction of KEAP1 with CUL3 and NRF2. Mutations in BTB domain are loss-of-function (LOF) mutations which abolish KEAP1 functions thus causing the activation of NRF2 signaling. Cisplatin-resistant HNSCC cells (HN30-R8) demonstrated potent ability to metastasize to the lungs and elevated rates of regional lymph node metastasis. Cisplatin effectively induced shrinkage of NRF2-silenced HN30-R8 tumors. Whereas reconstitution of cancer cells with wild-type KEAP1 markedly improved the response to cisplatin and significantly impaired tumor growth. Importantly, cisplatin considerably inhibited the metastatic dissemination of NRF2-silenced or wild-type KEAP1-overexpressing HN30-R8 cells in the rodent models.34 Collectively, these findings indicated that NRF2 hyperactivation strongly promoted cisplatin resistance and tumor-forming abilities of HNSCC cells.

Importantly, NRF2 signaling has a linchpin role in promoting the carcinogenesis and metastasis by transcriptional upregulation of the oncogenic regulatory networks. In the next section, we have discussed important natural products having unique ability to regulate KEAP1/NRF2 signaling in different cancers.

Natural Products Mediated Targeting of NRF2-Driven Pathway

Development of resistance against different therapeutics is a challenging clinical issue. Interdisciplinary research work has propelled the field of natural product research, and we have witnessed ground-breaking discoveries in translational oncology.

Understanding the surprising complexity of the bioactive molecules from natural sources provides a roadmap for the development of novel compounds.35–38 Research over decades has generated empirical evidence about cancer chemopreventive role of natural products. Biologically active molecules obtained from natural sources have demonstrated exceptional ability to pharmacologically target deregulated oncogenic pathways and inhibit the progression and metastatic spread of cancer cells.

In this section, we have provided a summary of proof-of-concept studies which underscore significant cancer chemopreventive role of natural products mainly through regulation of NRF2 pathway in cell culture studies and tumor-bearing mice.

TRIM40 (tripartite motif containing 40), a ubiquitin ligase ubiquitinates and degrades KEAP1. Importantly, STAT3 transcriptionally upregulates oncogenic TRIM40 in esophageal cancer (Figure 6B). Degradation of KEAP1 promotes nuclear accumulation of NRF2 and transcriptional activation of target gene network (Figure 6C). Depletion of TRIM40 led to significant reduction in the tumor volume, growth rates and tumor weights.41 Collectively, these findings indicated that TRIM40 exerted carcinogenic effects and promoted esophageal cancer. Therefore, targeting of TRIM40 promotes KEAP1-mediated targeting of NRF2 and enhances the anticancer effects of Oroxylin A.

Curcumin

Curcumin is an important bioactive component isolated from Curcuma longa. Because of initial success, curcumin has been extensively studied against different cancers. Multiple analogs of curcumin have also been designed to improve the efficacy with minimum off-target effects.42–44 Mass spectrometric analysis suggests that curcumin binds to Cys151 in KEAP1. Cells transfected with a mutant KEAP1 (cysteine 151 replaced by serine) were used to establish the cause-and-effect relationship of KEAP1 and NRF2. Findings indicated that curcumin-mediated NRF2 transactivation was severely reduced in cells transfected with a mutant KEAP1.45 Curcumin-inspired mono-carbonyl piperidinone analogs covalently modify Cys-151 and Cys-288 residues at KEAP1 and inhibit KEAP1-mediated targeting of NRF2.46 Curcumin has been shown to dualistically regulate NRF2 in cancer inhibition. In the next paragraphs, we will discuss how curcumin exerts cancer inhibitory effects either through activation or inhibition of NRF2.

Curcumin-Mediated Activation of NRF2 for Cancer Inhibition

Curcumin increased the nuclear fraction of NRF2 and stimulated NRF2-mediated upregulation of tumor suppressor miR-34a/b/c. Targeted inhibition of miR-34a by antagomirs led to partial restoration of metastases after treatment with curcumin. Five weeks after injection, resected lungs were devoid of macroscopically visible metastases in animal models injected with curcumin-treated SW620 cells. There was a significant reduction in the metastatic nodules in mice injected with curcumin-treated cells. Whereas SW620 cells concomitantly treated with curcumin and miR-34a-antagomirs formed pulmonary metastatic nodules in rodent models.47

In some other studies, it has been shown that curcumin derivatives epigenetically activated NRF2 and inhibited proliferation and colony formation of murine TRAMP C1 prostate cancer cells.48,49

Curcumin-Mediated Inhibition of NRF2 for Cancer Inhibition

NOTCH signaling is initiated by the interaction of Delta like ligands or Jagged ligands with NOTCH receptors. Following ligand binding, multiple cleavages occur in the transmembrane region of NOTCH. NOTCH intracellular domain (NICD) moves to the nucleus and binds to Recombination signal binding protein for immunoglobulin κ J region (RBPJ) transcription factors. Within the promoter region, multi-component molecular machinery comprising NICD and RBPJ displaces co-repressors and recruits co-activators for the transcriptional upregulation of different genes.50 Overexpression of NOTCH1 or RBPJ resulted in the enrichment of RBPJ within the promoter region of NRF2, thereby stimulating the expression of NRF2. Vitamin C

Vitamin C has gained extraordinary attention because of its dualistic role as an antioxidant and a pro-oxidant. Modification of cysteine residues in KEAP1 has a regulatory role. Different natural products have been shown to chemically modify cysteine residues in KEAP1. Vitamin C plays a pro-oxidant role in p53-deficient cells, but an antioxidant role in p53 wild-type cells. Oral administration of vitamin C induced an increase in the levels of p53 and activated NRF2/HO-1 pathway. Vitamin C inhibited the formation of pulmonary metastatic nodules by H22 cancer cells, decreased the lung weights and prolonged the survival rate of mice.56

The combination of parenteral ascorbate with the conventional chemotherapeutic agents carboplatin and paclitaxel synergistically inhibited ovarian cancer in mouse models and reduced chemotherapy-associated toxicity in patients with ovarian cancer.57

Phase I clinical trial of ascorbate combined with radiation and temozolomide was tested for efficacy in glioblastoma patients. Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 9.4 months and median OS was 18 months.58

Intravenous high-dose ascorbate increased serum ascorbate levels from micromolar to millimolar levels. Ascorbate added to the gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel (ASC) increased overall survival to 16 months compared to 8.3 months with gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel (SOC). Median PFS was 6.2 (ASC) vs 3.9 months (SOC). Addition of Ascorbate did not increase the frequency or severity of adverse events.59 Rapidly increasing understanding of pro-oxidant and antioxidant role of vitamin c needs to be tested systematically in the context of KEAP1/NRF2 signaling. It will be intriguing to study how the pro-oxidant role of vitamin C modulates NRF2 signaling and regulates metastasis.

Arenobufagin

Arenobufagin is a natural bufadienolide isolated from toad venom. Pre-treatment with brusatol (NRF2 inhibitor) markedly enhanced arenobufagin-induced inhibition of migratory and invasive properties, whereas pre-treatment with tert‑butylhydroquinone (NRF2 agonist) significantly impaired arenobufagin-mediated inhibition of migratory and invasive abilities of colorectal cancer cells. Additionally, arenobufagin efficiently reduced migration and invasion of NRF2 knockdown-colorectal cancer cells. NRF2 is transcriptionally upregulated by c-Myc (Figure 7C). Therefore, targeting of c-Myc and NRF2 is an invaluable strategy. Arenobufagin suppressed the levels of NRF2 and HO-1 in HCT116 and SW620 cancer cells (Figure 7D). Intraperitoneal injections of arenobufagin caused significant reduction in the number of pulmonary metastatic nodules in mice intravenously injected with HCT116 cells in the tail of NOD/SCID mice.60

Polyphyllin I

Polyphyllin I (PPI) is isolated from Paris polyphylla. Polyphyllin I remarkably reduced the levels of NRF2 in HepG2 and MHCC97H cells. Intraperitoneally injected PPI led to shrinkage of the tumors in mice inoculated with MHCC97H cells. PPI reduced the levels of NRF2 and HO-1 in the tumor tissues derived from MHCC97H cells.61

Ursolic Acid

Ursolic acid (UA) is a pentacyclic terpenoid carboxylic acid widely found in vegetables and fruits. NRF2 transcriptionally induces the expression of SLC7A11 and Glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) (Figure 7E). Ursolic acid induced an increase in the levels of KEAP1 and simultaneously reduced NRF2 levels. Consequently, SLC7A11 and GPX4 were also found to be significantly reduced in ursolic acid-treated MDA-MB-231 and BT-549 spheroids. Moreover, ursolic acid also significantly impaired the growth of the tumors by inhibition of NRF2 and GPX4 in mice inoculated with MDA-MB-231 cells.62

Xanthohumol

Xanthohumol is derived from the female flowers of Humulus lupulus L. species in the family Cannabaceae, with promising pharmacological properties. Xanthohumol, a prenylated chalcone is highly effective against different cancers. Xanthohumol, a natural prenylflavonoid has been found to covalently modify three functional cysteine residues (cys151, 273, 288) of KEAP1, thus extending the half-life of NRF2 (Figure 8).

Xanthohumol potently inhibited liver metastasis in syngeneic Bl/6N mice, injected orthotopically with murine B16-F10 melanoma cells into the spleen.63 Nanotechnological delivery of Xanthohumol is also an attractive strategy. PEGylated graphene oxide was conjugated to Xanthohumol for an efficient delivery of the payload. Pre-treatment of breast cancer MDA-MB-231 cells with nano-formulations of Xanthohumol led to considerable reduction in the pulmonary metastatic nodules.64 Xanthohumol works remarkably with different other natural products and inhibits the progression of cancer by activation of NRF2 in pancreatic cancer cells.65–67 However, these findings have to be tested more comprehensively in metastatic cancer cell lines to mechanistically analyze the activation or inhibition of NRF2 by Xanthohumol.

Phenethyl Isothiocyanate (PEITC)

Phenethyl isothiocyanate is found in certain cruciferous vegetables. It has attracted widespread appreciation because of its promising cancer suppressive effects.68–70

PEITC activates ERK and JNK, which, in turn, phosphorylate NRF2 and induce its nuclear accumulation in prostate cancer PC-3 cells.71

Epigenetic landscape is regulated by covalent DNA and histone modifications. SET domain containing 7 (SETD7) is a lysine-specific methyltransferase. SETD7 monomethylates histone 3 lysine 4 (H3K4me1) for transcriptional activation of target genes. SETD7-mediated mono-methylation of H3K4 led to transcriptional upregulation of NRF2 in prostate cancer cells (Figure 9C). PEITC induced an increase in the levels of SETD7. Targeted inhibition of SETD7 significantly improved the efficacy of PEITC.72 Although an exciting facet of the interplay between SETD7 and PEITC has been uncovered but these findings are inconclusive and need to be tested in mice models. Efficacy of PEITC in mice inoculated with SETD7-silenced and SETD7-expressing prostate cancer cells will yield valuable results. SETD7 has an oncogenic role and induced the activation of NRF2 in breast cancer cells. However, the knockdown of SETD7 led to a reduction in the levels of NRF2 in MCF7 and MDA-MB-231 cancer cells.73 Therefore, findings suggested that the inhibition of SETD7 markedly reduced the proliferation and survival of breast cancer cells.

In another study, it has been shown that knockdown of NRF2 in oncogenic K-ras-transformed cells made cells sensitive to PEITC and different chemotherapeutic drugs.74

Pedunculoside

Pedunculoside, a triterpene saponin extracted from Ilex rotunda Thunb.75,76 Pedunculoside inhibited ROS generation and oxidative stress by promoting KEAP1-mediated targeting of NRF2. Pedunculoside considerably reduced pulmonary metastatic nodules in C57BL/6 mice intravenously injected with B16-F10 melanoma cells. Pedunculoside reduced the levels of KEAP1 and simultaneously enhanced the levels of NRF2 in the tumor tissues.77

Phloretin

Phloretin is a dihydrochalcone present in apples and pears. Phloretin has been shown to modulate different cell signaling pathways in various cancers.78,79 NRF2 has seven NRF2-ECH homology (Neh) domains. Neh 6 domain of NRF2 has two motifs DSGIS338 and DSAPGS378. These motifs are recognized by β-transducing repeat-containing protein (β-TrCP). Phloretin forms an H-bond within Gly 367 site of NRF2 thus blocking its ubiquitination and degradation. Phloretin induced NRF2-driven signaling in pancreatic cancer cells. Intraperitoneally injected Phloretin induced regression of the tumors in mice subcutaneously inoculated with PANC-1 cells.80

EGCG

Epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) is a highly acclaimed natural product having promising anticancer and anti-metastatic effects.81,82

miR483-3p, an oncogenic microRNA has been shown to enhance invasion and metastasis. miR483-3p also targeted NRF2 and Superoxide Dismutase (SOD2) (Figure 9A and B). EGCG enhances epigenetic inactivation of oncogenic miR483-3p by methylation of the promoter region. EGCG efficiently inhibited metastatic dissemination in mice intravenously injected with miR483-3p overexpressing-HepG2 cancer cells (Figure 9A and B).83 Collectively, these findings indicated that EGCG epigenetically inactivated miR483-3p and increased the levels of NRF2 and SOD2 to inhibit metastatic dissemination.

Epigenetic regulation of KEAP1/NRF2 is an exciting area of research84 and future studies will add more evidence about the cancer-specific role of methylation-associated machinery.

EGCG enhanced ubiquitination of NRF2 in cancer cells (HCT116 and HeLa). NRF2 is involved in transcriptional upregulation of p62. Resultantly, p62 interferes with KEAP1-mediated targeting of NRF2. However, EGCG induced degradation of NRF2 in cancer cells.85

Fisetin

Fisetin is a valuable flavonoid having the unique ability to target different cancers.86,87 Fisetin induced an increase in the levels of NRF2 and downregulated KEAP1. However, fisetin did not exert significant effects on mRNA of NRF2. It was shown that after inhibition of protein synthesis by cycloheximide, fisetin extended the half-life of NRF2 from 15 min to 45 min. Blockade of NRF2 degradation with (MG132) proteasome inhibitor led to an increase in the ubiquitinated proteins but fisetin reduced ubiquitination of NRF2.88

Fisetin induced an increase in the NRF2-driven downstream signaling in breast cancer cells.89 Moreover, fisetin-loaded nanostructured lipid carriers effectively suppressed metastasizing ability of melanoma cells by increasing the levels of NRF2. There was an evident reduction in the metastatic colonization of melanoma cells to the liver and lungs in tumor-bearing mice.90

In this section, we have attempted to summarize detailed mechanisms associated with the functional role of NRF2/KEAP1 signaling in the regulation of cancer and how pharmacological targeting of this molecular complex can pave the way for a highly effective series of clinical trials for cancer therapy. Mechanism-based analysis of natural products and dietary agents will empower interdisciplinary researchers to reap maximum translational benefits. Several molecules have been shown to chemically interact with different amino-acid residues of KEAP1. Fumarate, an oncometabolite derived from the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA) modified cysteine 151 of KEAP1. Another TCA-derived metabolite, itaconate, alkylates cysteine 151 in KEAP1 and activates NRF2. Cysteine 151 can be covalently modified by clinically valuable drugs (bardoxolone, oleananes, and omaveloxelone).

Deoxynyboquinone is a small molecule isolated from marine actinomycetes. Deoxynyboquinone induced alkylation at Cys489 in Kelch domain and caused KEAP1 degradation (Figure 8).91 Obtusaquinone is a quinone methide isolated from the heartwood of Dalbergia retusa. Importantly, obtusaquinone binds to Cys151 located in the BTB domain and Cys434 located in the Kelch domain of KEAP1. Essentially, KEAP1 levels remained unchanged in cells expressing BTB-mutated KEAP1. Obtusaquinone promotes the degradation of KEAP1 by modification of Cys151. Pharmacokinetic profiling of obtusaquinone in mice showed higher systemic plasma clearance with a terminal plasma half-life of 24 minutes following intraperitoneal injections. AEN36 Tris, a potent analog of obtusaquinone efficiently induced tumor regression in mice inoculated with MDA-MB-231 cells.92

Clinical evaluation of natural products significantly highlights pharmacologically valuable anticancer and anti-metastatic effects. However, detailed and comprehensive characterization of previously unexplored NRF2/KEAP1 pathway will enable clinicians to bridge the existing knowledge gaps. Therefore, molecular analysis of patient-derived drug-resistant xenografts is an essential strategy to evaluate multi-targeting facets of natural products.

Clinical Trials Related to Pharmacological Targeting of Oncogenic NRF2

NRF2-driven signaling has garnered tremendous spotlight because of its ability to pleiotropically modulate a myriad of gene networks. Deregulation/overexpression of NRF2 is linked with carcinogenesis and metastasis. There is a gradual increase in the list of clinical trials with the aim to pharmacologically target deregulated/mutant NRF2 or mechanistically analyze how next-generation molecular therapeutics target NRF2/KEAP1-mutated cancers.

Common classes of drugs used in the clinical management of type 2 diabetes mellitus have been shown to increase the risk of metastasis. Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors (DPP4 inhibitors) particularly, saxagliptin and sitagliptin increased the risk of metastatic dissemination of existing tumors. Cysteine residue C151 present within BTB domain of KEAP1 is involved in the regulation of NRF2. Saxagliptin and sitagliptin efficiently activated oncogenic NRF2 and promoted hepatic and pulmonary metastasis in tumor-bearing mice.93

Loss of Liver kinase-B1 (LKB1) has been shown to drive radio-resistance in NSCLC tumor cells by activation of NRF2 pathway. Re-expression of LKB1 results in the suppression of NRF2 and NRF2-regulated genes. Glutaminase is an enzyme that converts glutamine to glutamate (precursor of glutathione). Glutamate-cysteine ligase (GCLC) is an enzyme that catalyzes the production of glutathione from glutamate. NRF2 triggers the expression of GCLC in LKB1-deficient cancer cells. KEAP1 depletion potently enhanced the radio-resistance of NSCLC cells. Radiotherapy remarkably impaired the growth of tumors in mice inoculated with KEAP1-expressing A549 cells and prolonged the median PFS of tumor-bearing mice.94 Collectively, these findings provided compelling evidence that loss of Liver kinase-B1 and, more significantly, KEAP1 are potential mediators of radio-resistance in rodent models. These findings are intriguing and provide valuable understanding about the oncogenic role of NRF2-signaling in LKB1-deficient cancers. This mystery was solved by an important discovery that suggested that NRF2 interacted with proteins involved in the DNA repair. Ataxia telangiectasia and Rad3-related (ATR) is involved in the repair of damaged DNA. NRF2 interacted with ATR and induced its activation at double-stranded breaks. Therefore, targeted inhibition of NRF2 and ATR provided an effective avenue to overcome radio-resistance in NSCLC patients.95,96 Phase II HUDSON study (NCT03334617) has evaluated ceralasertib and olaparib in combination with durvalumab in locally advanced or metastatic LKB1/KEAP1-deficient NSCLC patients.97

Drug repurposing is also a dynamic dimension of clinical research. Clobetasol propionate reduced the levels of NRF2 by promoting Beta-transducin repeat-containing protein (β-TrCP)-mediated degradation. Evaluating how clobetasol propionate exerts its cancer-inhibitory effects when combined with standard-of-care drugs warrants further investigation, particularly in the context of LKB1/KEAP1-defective NSCLC patients.98

LKB1/KEAP1-defective lung cancers have frequently activated NRF2 to rewire metabolic pathways. Serine hydroxymethyltransferase (SHMT) is a key enzyme involved in catabolism of serine. Once activated by LKB1, Salt-induced kinase (SIK1) inactivates NRF2-mediated signaling. SIK1 inhibits SHMT expression by blocking NRF2-MAFK binding to SHMT1 promoter. SIK1 also turns off transcriptional upregulation of SHMT2 by inhibition of NRF2-ATF4 and NRF2-MAFK. However, upon loss of LKB1 and KEAP1, SIK1 is unable to restrict NRF2-mediated transcriptional networks. Therefore, SHMT inhibitors were found to enhance the efficacy of paclitaxel in cancer models.99

Apart from loss-of-function KEAP1 and LKB1 mutations, clinical analysis has also identified cancers harboring gain-of-function mutations in NRF2. More importantly, these mechanistic insights are currently being tested in a Phase 2, randomized trial of glutaminase inhibitor (telaglenastat) in KEAP1/NRF2-mutated, stage IV NSCLC patients (NCT06495125). Sapanisertib is a potent, selective inhibitor of mTORC1/2 in NRF2-mutated NSCLC patients (NCT05275673). 6-Diazo-5-oxo-l-norleucine (DON) is a glutamine antagonist. DRP-104 is a prodrug of DON and currently being tested in locally advanced or metastatic KEAP1/NRF2-mutated NSCLC patients (NCT04471415).

Pyrimethamine is currently being tested for efficacy as an NRF2 inhibitor in locally advanced HNSCC patients (NCT05678348). VVD-130037, a first-in-class KEAP1 activator, has been designed for the treatment of NRF2-activated cancers. VVD-130037 is being tested for safety, pharmacodynamics, pharmacokinetics and preliminary efficacy in participants with advanced solid tumors (NCT05954312). VVD-065 is also a first-in-class, molecular glue degrader. VVD-065 not only inhibited NRF2-dependent tumor growth but also sensitized tumors to chemo/radiotherapies.100

Clinical Trials Related to Activation of NRF2

Synthetic Triterpenoid, RTA-405 has also been shown to interact with C151-KEAP1 and interfere with KEAP1-mediated targeting of NRF2.101 At higher concentrations, Omaveloxolone (RTA-408) inhibited growth of tumor cells and increased caspase activity in tumor cells, but did not harm normal primary human cells.102 Omaveloxolone (RTA-408) has favorable tolerability at biologically active doses, although this trial had a small sample size which limits conclusive clinical evidence.103

Bardoxolone methyl is well-tolerated at 900 mg/d. Importantly, primary dose-limiting toxicities led to reversible elevation of serum ALT. The pharmacokinetic activity of bardoxolone methyl is characterized by slow oral absorption and a relatively long biological half-life.104

Concluding Remarks

Increasingly, it is being realized that dysregulations of spatio-temporally controlled intracellular signaling cascades are contributory in cancer development and progression. Signaling cascades modulate different molecular activities through multiple effectors and also by crosstalks with other transduction cascades. Multi-layered regulation of KEAP1/NRF2 pathway is highly complicated and different upstream activators have been shown to activate NRF2 pathway (Figure 10). Greater than ever understanding of the astonishing complexity of the pharmacologically significant natural products provides a roadmap to inhibit cancer progression by mining for new natural products to increase the efficacy of translatability of drugs. Different compounds have been investigated to chemically interact with KEAP1 and enhance NRF2 activity. Whereas, researchers are also developing NRF2 inhibitors for the treatment of NRF2-overexpressing cancers. Doubtlessly, NRF2-mediated downstream signaling is highly complicated and the selection of drugs for targeting of NRF2 needs to be supported by well-designed molecular profiling.

NRF2-driven downstream signaling has galvanized interdisciplinary research for the identification of multiple regulatory gene networks that underlie the cancer progression and metastatic invasion. Seminal studies in Nrf2−/− mice models will be valuable in context of regulation of metastasis. KEAP1/NRF2 signaling hub is the central node and use of natural products/dietary agents has generated a wealth of high-quality information (Table 1). There is a need for sequencing of KEAP1/NRF2 in different populations. The genetic map of KEAP1/NRF2 will enable researchers to assess the clinical efficacy of NRF2 activators or inhibitors.
