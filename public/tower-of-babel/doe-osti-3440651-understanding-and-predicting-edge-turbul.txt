1. Executive Summary
This final technical report summarizes work performed under DOE Award DE-SC0022563,
“Understanding and Predicting Edge Turbulence and Transport in Reactor-Relevant ELM-Free High-
Confinement Regimes of DIII-D,” led by the UCLA Department of Physics and Astronomy (K. Barada,
PI; T. A. Carter, co-PI). The project's overall goal was to understand and predict the role of multi-scale,
multi-field turbulence in setting pedestal structure and transport in reactor-relevant, ELM-free high-
confinement regimes of the DIII-D tokamak including quiescent H-mode (QH-mode), wide-pedestal
QH-mode, and the enhanced Dα (EDA) H-mode and to use this understanding to design and lead new
DIII-D experiments while supporting the broader DIII-D research program.

Substantial progress was made against all three proposed objectives:

 ● Objective 1 (turbulence characterization and simulation): Core gyrokinetic simulations of
 standard QH-mode discharges are complete (submitted to arXiv and is also under review at
 Nuclear Fusion); a companion analysis extending these simulations into the pedestal is in
 progress. A related core gyrokinetic study of super H-mode discharges is underway. The team
 discovered and characterized distinct low and high recycling ELM-free EDA H-mode regimes
 on DIII-D along with the characterization of the underlying turbulence. This work was
 published in Physics of Plasmas, including linear gyrokinetic (CGYRO) simulations identifying
 trapped-electron-mode (TEM) and electron-temperature-gradient (ETG) turbulence as the
 physics underlying the regime's defining quasi-coherent mode (QCM). EHO-like coherent
 magnetic and density oscillations were identified for the first time in the inter-ELM period of
 type-I ELMing discharges (presented at APS-DPP 2022), and a full characterization of wide-
 pedestal QH-mode inter-ELM pedestal dynamics including two distinct dynamical routes to an
 ELM is being prepared for submission to Nuclear Fusion.
 ● Objective 2 (data from new DIII-D experiments): Analysis of a UCLA designed and led
 experiment discovered and controlled a new ELM-free high-confinement regime sustained in
 the presence of a benign locked tearing mode, using combined error-field and neoclassical-
 toroidal-viscosity (NTV) torque injection, validated against nonlinear two-fluid MHD (TM1)
 simulation presented at the 2024 Workshop on MHD Stability Control and being prepared for
 publication.
 ● Objective 3 (broader collaboration and tool development): UCLA supported the wider DIII-D
 program by developing two new machine-learning tools built on UCLA's Doppler
 backscattering (DBS) diagnostic data: a convolutional neural network for automated L-H
 transition classification (with UC Riverside), and a neural-network-based forecasting tool that
 predicts the first post-transition ELM up to ≈100 ms in advance (with the University of Alberta
 and A*STAR), the latter posted to arXiv and is recently accepted in Plasma Physics and
 Controlled Fusion.
Sections 2 and 3 below give the background and detailed results organized by proposal objective;
Section 4 summarizes personnel and training; Section 5 lists the products (publications and
presentations) of the award.

2. Background
A central goal of the DIII-D research program, and of the international effort to design reactor-relevant
operating scenarios for ITER and future burning-plasma devices, is to identify high-confinement (H-
mode) regimes that avoid large type-I edge-localized modes (ELMs). Type-I ELMs expel heat and

particles from the pedestal on millisecond timescales; in ITER, ELM energy losses above 1 MJ are
projected to damage plasma-facing components and shorten machine lifetime. Naturally ELM-free
regimes including quiescent H-mode (QH-mode) and its wide-pedestal variant as well as other regimes
like the enhanced Dα (EDA) H-mode and I-mode avoid this problem by replacing the large periodic
ELM crash with continuous, lower-amplitude edge turbulence and/or coherent MHD activity (edge
harmonic oscillations, EHOs, in QH-mode; the quasi-coherent mode, QCM, in EDA H-mode) that
regulates the pedestal gradient below the peeling–ballooning MHD stability boundary.

UCLA's suite of edge turbulence diagnostics on DIII-D — Doppler backscattering (DBS, measuring
density fluctuations and E×B flow), cross-polarization scattering (CPS, measuring internal magnetic
fluctuations), and correlation electron cyclotron emission (CECE, measuring electron temperature
fluctuations) together with UCLA's long-standing modeling and gyrokinetic-simulation collaborations,
make it uniquely positioned to characterize the multi-scale, multi-field turbulence responsible for
particle and heat transport in these regimes. This award supported (i) analysis of existing and newly
acquired multi-field turbulence data compared against linear and nonlinear gyrokinetic simulation, (ii)
design and execution of new DIII-D experiments targeting the physics of ELM-free access and
sustainment, and (iii) broad support of the DIII-D collaborative research program, including the
development of new diagnostic data driven machine learning tools.

3. Objectives and Results

3.1 Objective 1: Multi-Scale, Multi-Field Turbulence Characterization and Simulation in ELM-
Free Pedestals and core plasma
UCLA analyzed existing and newly obtained multi-field turbulence and profile data from ELM-free
DIII-D regimes (standard and wide-pedestal QH-mode, and the newly characterized EDA H-mode),
compared these against linear and nonlinear gyrokinetic (TGLF, GTC, and CGYRO) simulations to
identify the dominant instabilities, and used the results to motivate new experiments and simulation
collaborations.

3.1.1 Core gyrokinetic simulations of standard QH-mode discharges
In collaboration with the Indian Institute of Science, Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory, Institute for
Plasma Research, and UC Irvine, UCLA performed core gyrokinetic simulations (Figure 1) of standard
QH-mode DIII-D discharges to identify the turbulent transport mechanisms operating in the plasma
core of this ELM-free regime, using UCLA's measured multi-field turbulence data (DBS, CPS, CECE)
as validation targets. In fact, the GTC simulations match with the observed ion-scale modes in UCLA’s
DBS measurements in the core. A manuscript reporting this work is currently under final phases of
review at Nuclear Fusion.

QH-mode DIII-D discharge on the poloidal cross-section at toroidal angle ζ = 0: (a) linear phase, (b) nonlinear

phase without zonal flow (ZF), and (c) nonlinear phase with ZF. From Tiwari et al. (manuscript under review,
Nuclear Fusion).

3.1.2 Edge gyrokinetic simulations of QH-mode pedestal turbulence
Building on the core analysis above, UCLA is extending the collaborative gyrokinetic simulation effort
into the steep-gradient pedestal region of standard QH-mode discharges. This edge analysis (shown in
Figure 2) is in progress; a manuscript has not yet been submitted pending a presentation in the DIII-D
science meeting.

Figure 2. Electrostatic potential fluctuations (eδφ/Te) from edge gyrokinetic simulation of the pedestal region of
a QH-mode plasma: (a) linear phase, (b) nonlinear phase. Insets show the steep-gradient edge region. Analysis
in progress.

3.1.3 Core and edge gyrokinetic simulations of Super H-mode and Super-H + Hot Ion mode
discharges
In collaboration with Indian Institute of Science, Institute for Plasma Research, and UC Irvine, UCLA
contributed to a global gyrokinetic study (GTC code, fully kinetic electrons) of core and edge
microturbulence in Super H-mode (SH) and Super H + Hot Ion mode (SH+HI) DIII-D discharges
(#174783) (Figure 3-4). The hot-ion population sustained by strong NBI heating in SH+HI elevates the
ion-to-electron temperature ratio (Ti/Te ≈ 2.3) and broadens access to the high-pressure Super H-mode
pedestal branch, while also changing the dominant core microinstability relative to the thermally
equilibrated SH phase. Linear simulations identify the ion-temperature-gradient (ITG) mode as
dominant in SH+HI, driven by steep ion temperature gradients, while trapped-electron modes (TEM)
dominate in SH at higher central density; the two instabilities produce clearly distinct poloidal mode
structures — ballooning-aligned and radially coherent for ITG versus finer-scale and more poloidally
extended for TEM. Nonlinear simulations show that the equilibrium radial electric field partially
suppresses ITG-driven transport in SH+HI through E×B shear, while TEM-driven transport in SH is
essentially unaffected by the same mean-flow shear consistent with the known insensitivity of trapped-
electron modes to shear decorrelation. A direct cross-regime comparison reveals a striking transport
asymmetry: electron heat and particle diffusivities in SH+HI are reduced to ≈ 0.7× the corresponding
SH values, while ion heat diffusivity is elevated by ≈ 2.5× relative to SH demonstrating that the two
transport channels respond in opposite ways to the TEM-to-ITG shift between the regimes, and that
optimizing core confinement requires strategies tailored to the dominant instability rather than uniform
turbulence suppression. Planned extensions include electromagnetic effects, self-consistent impurity
transport, and scans of key dimensionless parameters (ηi, Ti/Te, ν*) to map the ITG/TEM transition
boundary and inform projections of SH/SH+HI operation to ITER. As with the QH-mode analysis

above, an edge-region extension of this work is planned. A manuscript reporting these results is in
preparation.

Figure 3. (a) Linear and (b) saturated electrostatic perturbed potential mode structure from GTC simulations of
the SH+HI phase. The coherent, field-aligned ballooning structure in (a) is characteristic of ITG instability
localized on the outboard mid-plane. The nonlinear saturation in (b) shows the characteristic break-up of linear
filaments into a more isotropic turbulent pattern. V. K. Singh et al. (manuscript in preparation)

Figure 4. (a) Linear and (b) saturated electrostatic perturbed potential mode structure from GTC simulations of
the SH phase. The broader poloidal extent and finer radial structure in (a) are characteristic of TEM instability.
The saturated state (b) shows nonlinear broadening comparable in amplitude to the SH+HI case, indicating
robust nonlinear saturation in both regimes. V. K. Singh et al. (manuscript in preparation)

3.1.4 Discovery and characterization of the EDA H-mode on DIII-D

UCLA identified and characterized new characteristics of QCM turbulence (Figure 5) in naturally
ELM-free regime on DIII-D, the enhanced Dα (EDA) H-mode in neutral-beam-heated, lower single-
null plasmas with near-zero net NBI torque. First discovered and studied extensively on Alcator C-Mod
under ion-cyclotron heating, the EDA H-mode had not previously been established in a beam-heated,
torque-balanced tokamak. This work, published in Physics of Plasmas (Macwan et al., 2024), reports
good energy confinement (H98, y2 ≈ 1) with βₙ ≈ 2, high density, and no ELMs, sustained for tens of
energy confinement times. Using UCLA's DBS system together with BES, magnetic probes, and
Langmuir probes, the regime's defining quasi-coherent mode (QCM) was shown to be a separatrix-
spanning, field-aligned mode spanning a wide wavenumber range (k⊥ρₛ ≈ 0.1–1.2) with multiple
harmonics. Linear CGYRO gyrokinetic simulations identified two dominant, radially separated
instabilities in the same wavenumber range as the QCM: a trapped-electron mode (TEM) for ρ ≤ 0.958
and a toroidal electron-temperature-gradient (ETG) mode for ρ > 0.962, with the TEM characteristics
(particle transport, real frequency, electromagnetic character) matching the experimentally observed
QCM properties well — the first gyrokinetic identification of the physical mechanism underlying an
EDA-H‐mode-like QCM in a beam-heated device.

Figure 5. EDA H-mode discharge overview (DIII-D #161238): (a) plasma equilibrium and diagnostic viewing
geometry; (b–g) time evolution of density, fueling, divertor Dα (ELM-free), βₙ/H₉₈ʸ², NBI power/torque, and the
DBS phase spectrum showing the QCM. From Macwan et al., Physics of Plasmas 31 (2024).

Follow-on experiments since publication (reported at the September 2024 CEIP meeting) extended the
EDA H-mode operating space to more stationary (nearly 24 energy confinement times), lower edge
safety factor (q₉₅ ≈ 3, more ITER-relevant) and to higher NBI power (≈ 4 MW), where (Figure 6) the
QCM was found to be maintained alongside small ELMs, and the pedestal was shown to be transport-
dominated rather than hard-MHD-limited. These results, together with the observation that the QCM is
detected consistently across DBS phase, amplitude, magnetics, and Langmuir-probe data, further
support the identification of the QCM with edge TEM-type turbulence.

Figure 6. Doppler backscattering (DBS) dφ/dt phase spectrum (top), RMS amplitude of the QCM (20–40 kHz,
middle), and divertor Dα signal (bottom) showing the EDA H-mode sustained for 24 energy confinement times
(DIII-D shot 200471). DIII-D CEIP presentation, Sept. 2024.

3.1.5 Discovery of EHO-like modes in the inter-ELM period of type-I ELMing discharges
UCLA discovered that coherent, edge-harmonic-oscillation-like (EHO-like) magnetic and density
fluctuations — previously understood to exist only in the ELM-free QH-mode — also appear during
the inter-ELM period of ordinary type-I ELMing H-mode discharges with co-current NBI injection.
These modes, with a fundamental frequency near 11 kHz and harmonics up to n ≈ 6, are observed
consistently in fast magnetics, Langmuir probe, DBS phase, and BES data (Figure 7), and are excited
above a critical value of the product of the edge bootstrap-current-density gradient and the E×B shear
— the same drive mechanism theorized for standard QH-mode EHOs. ELITE peeling–ballooning
stability calculations show the pedestal is close to the peeling-unstable boundary specifically during the
phase when these EHO-like modes are present. The modes were shown to weaken with reduced edge
rotational shear and pressure gradient in both ECH-heated and NBI-torque-ramp-down discharges, and
a detailed comparison confirmed that the observed modes share essentially all defining characteristics
of EHOs in standard QH-mode. This work was presented by then UCLA postdoc at the 64th APS-DPP
meeting (2022) and directly motivated the wide-pedestal QH-mode inter-ELM study described next.

Figure 7. EHO-like coherent oscillations observed simultaneously in fast magnetics, Langmuir probe, DBS phase
(two radii), and BES spectra during the inter-ELM period of a type-I ELMing DIII-D discharge. From Macwan
& Barada, APS-DPP (2022).

3.1.6 Inter-ELM pedestal dynamics of wide-pedestal QH-mode discharges
UCLA carried out a detailed study of the pedestal dynamics in wide-pedestal QH-mode discharges that
can sustain ELMs in discharges that transition from a higher-density (compared to wide-pedestals those
do not ELM) standard QH-mode. ELITE calculations confirm (Figure 8) that the ELMy wide-pedestal
discharge sits above (unstable to) the peeling–ballooning boundary, in contrast to a comparable ELM-
free wide-pedestal discharge that remains stable. Remarkably, the inter-ELM pedestal width evolution
in this regime is essentially the reverse of ordinary ELMy H-mode: rather than starting wide and slowly
narrowing toward the next ELM, the wide-pedestal-QH pedestal starts wide pre-ELM, narrows sharply
just after the ELM crash, and then widens again well before the next ELM (Figure 9) with the measured
width exceeding the EPED-KBM predicted width for nearly the entire inter-ELM period indicating
wide-pedestal QH-mode is maintained in the ELM-free inter-ELM period. Analysis of the detailed
inter-ELM turbulence (via DBS), E×B shear, and edge ∇Te evolution (via high-resolution ECE)
identified two mutually exclusive dynamical routes by which the pedestal approaches the next ELM:
(a) a limit-cycle-oscillation (LCO, predator–prey turbulence/shear) phase that persists until the ELM,

or (b) an LCO phase that transitions into an EHO phase before the ELM, with the EHO phase associated
with higher edge gradients and closer proximity to the peeling boundary. This work extends UCLA's
earlier discovery of LCO dynamics in ELM-free wide-pedestal QH-mode (Barada et al., Phys. Rev.
Lett. 2018; Physics of Plasmas 2019) to the ELMy variant of the regime.

Figure 8. ELITE peeling–ballooning stability diagram for the wide-pedestal QH-mode case just before the ELM,
with the experimental operating point (crosshair) lying above the diamagnetically-corrected stability boundary.
Manuscript in preparation.

Figure 9. Contrast between inter-ELM pedestal width evolution in a wide-pedestal QH-mode discharge that ELMs
(a, b: pedestal widens through most of the inter-ELM period) and a conventional type-I ELMy H-mode discharge
(c, d: pedestal narrows through most of the inter-ELM period). Manuscript in preparation.

3.2 Objective 2: Analysis of UCLA designed DIII-D Experiments on Turbulence and
Transport in ELM-Free Regimes
UCLA analyzed and discovered a new ELM-free regime sustained by a benign locked mode. This DIII-
D experiment was previously designed and led by (PI) UCLA in collaboration with DIII-D scientists,
diagnosticians, and experimental leaders.

UCLA presented this work at the 28th Workshop on MHD Stability Control (Auburn University, Oct.
2024) and is now being prepared for publication. UCLA discovered and characterized a new ELM-free
high-confinement regime sustained in the presence of a benign (non-disruptive) locked tearing mode.
Using combined neoclassical-toroidal-viscosity (NTV) and counter-current NBI torque injection at Ip ≈
1 MA, Bt ≈ 2 T, q95 ≈ 6, a residual n = 1 error field was shown to lock pre-existing 2/1 and 4/1 tearing
modes without triggering a disruption (Figure 10). The resulting loss of plasma rotation reduces
rotational screening, allowing 5/1 and 6/1 islands to penetrate; this reduces the pedestal height and
drives the discharge, after a few ELMs, into a sustained ELM-free state. A controlled comparison
discharge in which the 2/1 mode failed to lock remained ELMy under otherwise identical conditions,
directly demonstrating that mode locking and not simply the presence of the error field or NTV torque
is the operative mechanism. UCLA further showed that the same ELM-free state can be reached by
actively ramping down NBI torque to induce locking at zero net torque, demonstrating a route to active
control of the transition. Nonlinear two-fluid MHD (TM1) simulations (Figure 11) reproduce the
observed sequence: onset of unstable 2/1 and 4/1 modes, sequential locking, and consequent 5/1 and
6/1 island penetration with pedestal-height reduction and coherent EHO-like edge fluctuations (possibly
edge-localized NTMs, following Hu et al., Phys. Plasmas 2023) in DBS phase data were also observed
after locking. These results suggest a possible route to n = 1 resonant-magnetic-perturbation-based ELM
suppression on DIII-D that has not previously been demonstrated.

Figure 10. Observation of an ELM-free H-mode sustained by an n = 1 benign locked mode (DIII-D #184444):
NBI power/torque, safety factor and coil currents, divertor Dα (ELM-free after locking), density/βₙ/rotation,
pedestal density and temperature, and the magnetic fluctuation spectrogram showing mode locking. Presented at
the 28th Workshop on MHD Stability Control (2024) and manuscript under preparation.

Figure 11. Nonlinear two-fluid (TM1) MHD simulation reproducing the observed sequence of 2/1 and 4/1 mode
locking followed by 5/1 and 6/1 island penetration and pedestal height/temperature reduction. Presented at the
28th Workshop on MHD Stability Control (2024).

3.3 Objective 3: Support of the Broader DIII-D Collaborative Research Program
Beyond the results discussed above, UCLA supported the wider DIII-D program through data analysis,
diagnostic operation, and reflecting a new direction of effort under this award is the development of
machine-learning tools built on UCLA's DBS diagnostic data, in collaboration with a graduate student
from UC Riverside and undergrad from A*STAR, Singapore/ University of Alberta, Canada.

3.3.1 A convolutional neural network for automated L-H transition classification
In collaboration with a University of California, Riverside Masters student, UCLA developed a
convolutional neural network (CNN) that performs ternary classification of plasma confinement state:
L-mode, L-H transition, and H-mode directly from time-frequency (power spectral density)
representations of UCLA's DBS data, without relying on the divertor Dα signal at inference time.
Ground-truth transition windows are generated by a fully automated, physics-informed algorithm that
identifies the characteristic Dα derivative signature of the L-H transition and distinguishes it from
subsequent ELM-related Dα spikes; this algorithm was validated on 24 separate DIII-D discharges. The
resulting CNN, trained and tested on held-out DIII-D shots, achieves F1 scores of 0.80–0.97 for
transition detection and shows good temporal alignment between predicted and ground-truth transition
windows (Figure 12). Notably, the model also identifies transient “H-L like” back-transitions associated
with ELM events despite not being explicitly trained to do so, indicating the learned features generalize
to related confinement-regime dynamics. A manuscript describing this work is in preparation.

Figure 12. CNN confinement-state classification on a held-out test discharge (DIII-D #174819, F1 = 0.97): DBS
spectrogram inputs (a–c), predicted class probabilities (d), final predictions vs. ground truth (e), and the Dα signal
for reference (f).

3.3.2 Neural-network forecasting of the first post-transition ELM
In collaboration with University of Alberta / A*STAR undergraduate student and researcher, UCLA
extended its earlier CNN-based ELM-detection work to build a genuine forecasting tool for the first
ELM crash following the L-H transition. The model adapts the DeepHit survival-analysis framework
with a ResNet-Transformer architecture, taking 50 ms windows of two-channel DBS spectrogram data
as input and predicting the probability that an ELM will occur within 0–50, 50–100, 100–150, or beyond
150 ms (Figure 13). Trained and tested on DIII-D discharges spanning a range of L-H-to-first-ELM
durations, the model reliably forecasts the impending ELM roughly 100 ms in advance which is
comfortably ahead of the activation time of present resonant-magnetic-perturbation ELM-suppression
actuators (≈ 50 ms) and, as a byproduct, was also found to detect the L-H transition itself with high
accuracy purely from DBS data (because the model thinks turbulence changes at L-H transition time
precedes the 1st ELM which is correct). Because DBS does not require neutral-beam injection, unlike
optics-based diagnostics prone to window coating, this proof-of-concept is directly relevant to next-
generation devices such as SPARC and STEP that may not rely on NBI heating and also for burning
plasma regimes where significant window deposition is expected. This work was posted to arXiv in
April 2026 and is recently accepted in the Plasma Physics and Controlled Fusion journal.

Figure 13. ELM-forecast alert levels (probability of an ELM within the next 150, 100, or 50 ms) for a
representative DIII-D test discharge, showing reliable triggering roughly 100 ms before the first post-transition
ELM. From Teo et al., arXiv:2604.06508 (2026).
