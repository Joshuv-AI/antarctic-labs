Feedback Control and Local Convexification of Wasserstein Gradient Flows
Dante Kalise, Lucas M. Moschen, Grigorios A. Pavliotis

Abstract
For free energies of the form ℱ ⁡ ( μ ) = ℰ ⁡ ( μ ) + σ ​ ∫ Ω μ ​ log ⁡ μ ​ 𝑑 x , σ > 0 , \mathcal{F}(\mu)=\mathcal{E}(\mu)+\sigma\int_{\Omega}\mu\log\mu\,dx,\quad\sigma>0, we study the Wasserstein gradient flow, a continuity equation also known as mean-field Langevin dynamics, around a stationary state μ ¯ \bar{\mu} on the flat torus. Our first result identifies the Wasserstein Hessian of ℱ \mathcal{F} at μ ¯ \bar{\mu} with a self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent on a Hilbert space of potential variables, and shows that, up to the natural Riesz isometry, this operator generates the linearized gradient flow. This spectral description allows us to design a finite-rank feedback law, via an algebraic Riccati equation, that shifts the closed-loop Hessian spectrum above any prescribed threshold δ > 0 \delta>0 . As a consequence, the nonlinear closed-loop flow converges locally exponentially to μ ¯ \bar{\mu} with rate δ \delta . Under an additional second-order remainder assumption on the first variation, the corresponding closed-loop energy is also locally strongly convex in chart coordinates. We illustrate the framework on the flat torus and discuss extensions to multi-species systems, moment-constrained Fokker-Planck equations, and closed Riemannian manifolds.



1
Introduction

The collective behavior of large systems of interacting particles is often modeled by the
mean-field limit
, where the effect of interactions is averaged over the entire population.
In particular, the empirical measure of a system of
N
weakly interacting diffusion processes converges to a deterministic density as
N\to\infty
, whose evolution is governed by the nonlocal McKean-Vlasov-Fokker-Planck PDE, corresponding to the (nonlinear) McKean SDE
[
60
]
. In many models, this mean-field PDE can be written as a gradient flow in the space of probability measures for a free energy
\mathcal{F}
,

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu_{t}\nabla\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}\right)(\mu_{t})\right),

(1.1)

where
\delta\mathcal{F}/\delta\mu
denotes the first variation of
\mathcal{F}
.
For energies of the form

\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\mathcal{E}(\mu)+\sigma\int_{\Omega}\mu\log\mu\,dx,

the same dynamics is also referred to in optimization and machine learning as mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD)
[
38
,
24
]
.

The dynamics (
1.1
) is naturally analyzed in the Wasserstein geometry of probability measures.
Its gradient-flow formulation was established in
[
40
]
and the associated Riemannian structure was introduced at a formal level in
[
53
]
, including the concepts of tangent spaces and the Wasserstein Hessian.
This formalism was developed in connection with the porous-medium equation, which is a gradient flow of the free energy

\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\Omega}\mu^{m}dx\quad\text{for }m>1.

A rigorous first-order treatment was developed in
[
3
]
and second-order in
[
47
,
35
]
.
This second-order calculus connects the geometric curvature of the underlying space with the convexity properties of the energy functional.
Specifically, for a sufficiently smooth
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}
is
displacement convex

[
48
]
(i.e., convex along Wasserstein geodesics) if and only if its Wasserstein Hessian is positive semi-definite.
Furthermore,
strong displacement convexity
refers to uniform
\lambda
-convexity along these geodesics for some
\lambda>0
, a property that guarantees exponential convergence to the unique equilibrium
\bar{\mu}
, which is also the global minimum of
\mathcal{F}
.
In the formal Riemannian calculus, this corresponds to a uniform global lower bound on the Wasserstein Hessian,
\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}\geq\lambda I
.
Exponential convergence to the unique equilibrium can also be established beyond displacement convexity by assuming a global Log-Sobolev inequality
[
24
]
.
Even in such convex or Log-Sobolev settings, this constant
\lambda
may be small, leading to slow dynamics of (
1.1
).
Moreover, in compact geometries such as the flat torus, global strong displacement convexity is mathematically precluded for standard free energies.

However, non-convexity arises in many applications, ranging from granular media to neural networks, characterized by multimodal landscapes.
In these settings, the dynamics often exhibit
metastability
, in which the system can become trapped in local energy minima for exponentially long times before reaching the global equilibrium
[
12
,
22
,
50
]
.
Consequently, standard contraction arguments based on convexity fail, and the natural relaxation to equilibrium is inefficient.
While local convergence rates to these metastable minima are governed by the Poincaré constant of the stationary measure
[
64
,
49
]
, the landscape also features unstable equilibria.
At these unstable stationary points (saddles or local maxima), the Wasserstein Hessian possesses negative eigenvalues, meaning there is no local convergence.

For nonconvex energies, current theory characterizes metastable behavior but does not provide a constructive mechanism to enforce positive curvature near unstable equilibria.
Given
\delta>0
and a stationary state
\bar{\mu}
, we design a finite-rank PDE perturbation using a feedback control that shifts the Wasserstein Hessian spectrum at
\bar{\mu}
above
\delta
, producing a feedback-modified Hessian with a prescribed positive lower bound at the equilibrium.
This is the natural notion of local convexification for our problem, because the same operator also generates the closed-loop linearized gradient flow.
Thus, feedback acts as a constructive mechanism for local convexification of the free energy landscape and geometric stabilization of the dynamics around
\bar{\mu}
, extending the finite-rank forcing framework recently proposed for McKean-Vlasov equations in
[
41
]
.

1.1
Main results

In this paper, we show that the Wasserstein Hessian at
\bar{\mu}
admits a self-adjoint realization in the natural potential space and is conjugate to the linearized density generator.
Then, we build a feedback design based on this identification and demonstrate local exponential stabilization of the closed-loop dynamics.
By the conjugacy relation, controlling the linearized dynamics in density variables is equivalent to modifying the spectrum of the Hessian operator at
\bar{\mu}
, which in turn yields local strong convexification under the additional regularity encoded in Assumption
(E5)
.
To formalize this, we work in the space of potential variables

X=H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu}):=\left\{\phi\in H^{1}(\bar{\mu}):\int_{\Omega}\phi\,d\bar{\mu}=0\right\}

equipped with a weighted
H^{1}
-norm.
Our first result realizes the Wasserstein Hessian as a well-behaved operator on this space.

Theorem A

(Hessian realization and spectral structure)
.

Under the regularity assumptions
(E1)
-
(E2)
, the Wasserstein Hessian of
\mathcal{F}
at
\bar{\mu}
is represented by a unique self-adjoint operator
A
on
X
bounded from below, with compact resolvent.
Its spectrum consists of real eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots
with
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
.

The spectral structure of
A
characterizes the geometry of the local energy landscape.
If
\lambda_{1}>0
, the energy is strictly convex near
\bar{\mu}
, guaranteeing a local Poincaré inequality and exponential convergence to equilibrium for the unperturbed dynamics.
However, the non-convexity of the problem manifests itself as negative eigenvalues in its spectrum.
These unstable modes lead to divergence from
\bar{\mu}
along these directions, preventing local relaxation.
The precise connection to the dynamics is captured by our second result, which identifies the generator of the linearized flow in density variables.

Theorem B

(Linearized flow in density variables)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
A
and the Fréchet differentiability of
\mu\mapsto\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}[\mu]
at
\bar{\mu}
as a map from
H^{-1}(\Omega)
to
H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
, the linearized gradient flow at
\bar{\mu}
is generated on density perturbations by

L=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}A\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1},

where
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}:X\to H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
is the Riesz isometry induced by the weighted
H^{1}
structure. The operator
L
is self-adjoint on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{T}
induced by
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
and has compact resolvent there.

Theorems
A
and
B
show that local curvature and linearized dynamics are two realizations of the same object.
Consequently, finite-rank perturbations of the linearized PDE can be read equivalently as finite-rank spectral perturbations of the Hessian operator
A
.
By designing a finite-rank PDE perturbation of the form

\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}(t)\nabla\alpha_{j}(x),

where
\alpha_{j}\in X
are prescribed spatial control profiles and
u_{j}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
are the scalar control inputs, we introduce a bounded control operator acting on the finite-dimensional unstable mode subspace.
To obtain
\delta
-stabilization, we choose
m
to be the number of unstable modes and
\alpha_{j}
to be the corresponding eigenfunctions such that
\lambda_{j}<\delta
.
Through an algebraic Riccati equation, we construct a feedback control law

u(t)=B^{*}\Pi\xi(t),\quad Bu:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}(t)\alpha_{j}(x),

that shifts these unstable eigenvalues to positive values, leading to the feedback-modified operator
A_{\Pi}:=A+BB^{*}\Pi
.
This produces a strictly positive closed-loop Hessian at the equilibrium, represented by the same operator that governs the closed-loop linearized dynamics.

Figure 1:

Slice of the free energy near
\bar{\mu}
along the first eigendirection (quartic double-well on the torus).
The Riccati feedback acts on the dynamics, modifying the gradient flow via a finite-rank force field.
As a consequence, the linearized feedback-controlled dynamics coincide with the gradient flow of a feedback-modified energy (orange), whose Hessian at
\bar{\mu}
is strictly positive.

Theorem C

(Spectral shift and closed-loop Hessian coercivity)
.

For any prescribed
\delta>0
, there exists a finite-rank operator
\Pi
such that the closed-loop operator

A_{\Pi}:=A+BB^{*}\Pi

satisfies
A_{\Pi}\geq\delta I
on
X
.
Equivalently, the modified free energy

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mu)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(\mu)+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle P\Pi\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}[\mu-\bar{\mu}],\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}[\mu-\bar{\mu}]\rangle_{X}

has Wasserstein Hessian at
\bar{\mu}
represented by
A_{\Pi}
, and therefore

\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\bar{\mu})\geq\delta I.

The closed-loop linearized dynamics are generated by the same operator
A_{\Pi}
.

Theorem
C
is the convexification statement at
\bar{\mu}
: the closed-loop Hessian is coercive at
\bar{\mu}
.
Moreover, the same operator
A_{\Pi}
governs the closed-loop linearized dynamics.
The corresponding continuous-time feedback law yields the perturbed gradient flow

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=-\nabla_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{t})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}(t)\nabla\cdot\bigl(\mu_{t}\nabla\alpha_{i}\bigr),

where the control coefficients
u_{i}(t)
are determined by an autonomous control law depending on the projection of the distribution
\mu_{t}
onto the corresponding unstable modes, acting through prescribed spatial profiles
\alpha_{i}
.
In a
H^{-1}
-neighborhood of
\bar{\mu}
, the linearized perturbed dynamics coincide exactly with the gradient flow of the modified energy
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mu)
.
We next translate this spectral shift into a nonlinear stability statement for the full PDE.

Theorem D

(Local exponential stabilization)
.

Under the spectral-gap condition generated by the Riccati feedback and assumptions
(E3)
and
(E4)
, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
such that for every admissible initial density
\mu_{0}
with

\xi_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}(\mu_{0}-\bar{\mu})\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\qquad\|\xi_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}}\leq\varepsilon,

the feedback law yields a unique global solution of the feedback-controlled equation satisfying

\|\mu_{t}-\bar{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{T}}\leq Ce^{-\delta t}\|\xi_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}}\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Equivalently, the potential perturbation
\xi_{t}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}(\mu_{t}-\bar{\mu})
decays exponentially in
X
.

Theorem
D
translates the spectral shift to a local nonlinear stability statement for the full closed-loop PDE.
The isometry
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
identifies the exponential decay of the potential perturbation
\xi_{t}
with exponential decay of the density fluctuation
\mu_{t}-\bar{\mu}
in the dual Sobolev norm
\mathcal{T}
.
Under the stronger chart remainder estimate, Theorem
E
shows that the lifted energy is locally strongly convex in the
X
-metric, in transport-chart coordinates.

Theorem E

(Chart-level strong convexity under Assumption
(E5)
)
.

If, in addition, Assumption
(E5)
holds, then on a sufficiently small
H^{s}
chart neighborhood the lifted closed-loop energy is strongly convex with respect to the
X
-metric and its proximal map is contractive there.

1.2
Related work

Our approach uses an operator-theoretic framework for Wasserstein gradient flows.
Following
[
40
]
and the geometric interpretation in
[
53
]
, the analysis of these flows was placed on a metric-theoretic basis by
[
3
]
and the expository paper
[
59
]
.
The second-order calculus used to define the Hessian operator was developed in
[
53
]
, with a particular focus on the porous media equation and more generally in
[
47
,
35
]
.
Recent work extends the theory to the total variation flow, leading to fourth-order evolution equations
[
17
]
, and characterizes the metastable behavior of particle approximations
[
50
]
.
We build on this framework to formulate a spectral control theory in the Wasserstein geometry by connecting the linearized dynamics of (
1.1
) with the Hessian operator of
\mathcal{F}
in the Wasserstein space.

The closest works in the PDE literature concern local convergence around equilibria, which is the setting most closely related to our results.
When global convexity assumptions fail, the analysis usually shifts from global to local convergence around equilibria.
The classical local stability of McKean-Vlasov dynamics via spectral analysis was established in
[
61
]
, and abstract criteria for local exponential stability in Wasserstein-
1
distance have recently been formulated
[
26
]
.
Recent results show that this local convergence rate is governed by the Poincaré constant of the stationary measure, which coincides with the spectral gap of the Wasserstein Hessian
[
64
]
.
Similarly, for the double-well potential, a local non-linear Log-Sobolev inequality can be established
[
49
]
, which provides a close analytical analog of the local exponential stabilization targeted here.
Related Kramers-type phase transitions in nonlocal Fokker–Planck equations with dynamical control were studied in
[
37
]
.
While this literature focuses on analyzing the mixing time of the natural, often metastable, dynamics, our goal is to design a perturbation in the form of a feedback control that locally modifies the Hessian, enforcing a positive spectral gap and guaranteed exponential convergence in regimes where the unperturbed dynamics remain trapped, at least near the equilibrium.

The second ingredient of this work is local exponential stabilization, which we contrast with several related control paradigms for interacting particle systems.
A nearby line of work is
mean-field control
, which considers a central planner acting on the population law
[
39
,
9
,
34
,
1
,
20
]
.
Stochastic maximum principles and Forward-Backward Stochastic Differential Equation (FBSDE) formulations for such dynamics are developed in
[
18
,
19
]
.
The direct control of continuity equations, formulated at the level of measure-valued solutions and allowing for nonlocal velocities, was introduced and systematically developed in
[
55
,
56
]
, and feedback strategies for Fokker-Planck equations are proposed in
[
15
]
, with extensions to the nonlocal case in
[
41
]
.
Recent works have also explored both the approximate controllability of the continuity equation with localized vector fields
[
32
]
and the stabilization around reference trajectories of nonlocal continuity equations
[
57
]
, wherein the control acts additively on the vector field but is restricted to a fixed spatial subdomain. In contrast, our approach achieves stabilization of gradient flows around stationary equilibria via a finite-rank, operator-theoretic perturbation that convexifies the local free energy landscape.

From an optimal control perspective, necessary conditions like the Pontryagin Maximum Principle have been established in the Wasserstein space
[
14
,
13
]
.
Optimal control frameworks for Fokker-Planck equations are developed in
[
4
]
, with second-order optimality conditions established in
[
5
]
, and extensions to the nonlinear case in
[
7
]
.
These works primarily focus on open-loop intervention on finite horizons.
For alignment models, sparse feedback stabilization has been developed
[
16
]
, and model-predictive control with mean-field limits appears in
[
2
]
.
Another major area of development is
mean-field games

[
45
]
, modeling the competitive equilibrium of optimizing agents, which leads to coupled forward-backward systems.
In contrast, our work focuses on general gradient flows and designs a feedback control via an algebraic Riccati equation for infinite-horizon stabilization by exploiting the intrinsic second-order geometry and its connection with the linearized dynamics.

Conceptually, our approach translates continuous stabilization concepts from other domains into the Wasserstein geometry.
In finite-dimensional numerical optimization, modifying an indefinite Hessian to enforce positive definiteness appears in trust-region and Newton methods, such as in the book
[
51
]
.
In the control of mechanical systems, the method of energy shaping modifies the closed-loop potential energy to introduce a global minimum at the desired equilibrium
[
52
,
11
]
.
Similarly, in partial differential equations, the stabilization of unstable parabolic systems via finite-rank forcing on the unstable modes is well established.
Our contribution is to identify the operator representing the local curvature of the energy with the generator of the linearized PDE, and to use finite-rank spectral shaping of this operator via feedback control to obtain local closed-loop stabilization and then local strong convexity.

Finally, close connections exist between our work and the literature on sampling and variational inference.
This includes Wasserstein gradient-flow formulations of variational inference over Gaussian and Gaussian-mixture families
[
43
]
.
Convergence of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm to a target density is governed by Log-Sobolev inequalities
[
36
,
6
]
, with quantitative non-asymptotic rates established in the convex setting
[
28
,
23
,
62
]
.
For mean-field dynamics, analogous results under suitable Log-Sobolev inequalities are obtained in
[
24
]
.
Non-asymptotic rates are also available for certain non-convex landscapes (e.g., strongly log-concave outside a compact ball)
[
58
]
.
Complementary constructive directions include acceleration via non-reversible drifts
[
46
,
31
]
and consensus-based global optimization
[
21
]
.

1.3
Organization of the paper

We start by introducing the Wasserstein geometry and standing assumptions in Section
2
.
The paper is organized as follows:

(I)

Operator-theoretic framework (Section
3
):
We rigorously realize the Wasserstein Hessian at
\bar{\mu}
as a self-adjoint operator
A
with compact resolvent on a weighted Sobolev space, identifying the linearized gradient flow with the operator
-A
via an isometry.
This ensures a finite number of eigenfunctions with eigenvalues satisfying
\lambda_{i}<\delta
for the Wasserstein Hessian realization.

(II)

Exponential stabilization and convexification (Section
4
):
We analyze the linearized perturbed gradient flow and formulate an associated optimal control problem.
We show that stabilizing this linearized operator is equivalent to shifting the unstable spectrum of the Wasserstein Hessian.
This spectral shift locally convexifies the energy landscape, providing local exponential stabilization as a direct consequence.

(III)

Applications and numerical experiments (Section
5
):
We discuss extensions of our framework to coupled gradient flows, constrained Fokker-Planck equations, and manifold settings, and provide numerical validation of the theoretical results on the flat torus and the sphere.

2
Setting and standing assumptions

2.1
Wasserstein geometry and tangent spaces

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
We consider the space of probability measures
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
endowed with the quadratic Wasserstein distance

\operatorname{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\left(\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(x,y)^{2}\,d\gamma(x,y)\right)^{1/2},

where
\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
is the set of couplings (transport plans) on
\Omega\times\Omega
with first marginal
\mu
and second marginal
\nu
.
Here
d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
denotes the geodesic distance on the flat torus.
The tangent space at
\mu
is defined as the
L^{2}(\mu)
-closure of gradients

T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)\coloneqq\overline{\left\{\nabla\psi:\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\right\}}^{\,L^{2}(\mu)},

and we endow it with the scalar product
\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\,d\mu
.
If
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is absolutely continuous in
(\mathcal{P}_{2},\operatorname{W}_{2})
, then for a.e.
t>0
, there exists
v_{t}\in T_{\mu_{t}}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
such that

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\mu_{t}v_{t})=0

(2.1)

in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
.
Equation (
2.1
) is known as the continuity equation
[
3
]
.

For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
that is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure (with density still denoted by
\mu

\displaystyle W^{1,2}(\mu)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mu):\nabla\psi\in L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{d})\},

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{W^{1,2}(\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\psi^{2}(x)\mu(x)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}\mu(x)\,dx,

where
\nabla\psi
denotes the weak gradient of
\psi
with respect to Lebesgue measure on
\Omega
.
Let
\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\coloneqq\{\psi\in W^{1,2}(\mu):\nabla\psi=0,\mu\text{-a.e.}\}
and set the quotient

\dot{H}^{1}(\mu)\coloneqq W^{1,2}(\mu)\big/\mathcal{N}_{\mu},\qquad\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mu)}\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\mu(x)\,dx,

so that
\dot{H}^{1}(\mu)
is a Hilbert space.
If
\mu\geq c>0

\mathcal{N}_{\mu}
consists of constant functions and each class in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mu)
has a unique representative with zero
\mu

Hence we identify

H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\mu)\coloneqq\left\{\phi\in W^{1,2}(\mu):\int_{\Omega}\phi\,d\mu=0\right\}.

Since the inner product
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mu)}
is defined using gradients, which are well-defined on equivalence classes, this identification is isometric.
If in addition
\mu\leq C
a.e., then

The Hilbert dual of

with the dual norm induced by the
\mu

For a given

(2.2)

Under the standing bounds on

in the sense of distributions, and

2.2
Free energies and gradient flows

A
free energy
is a proper, lower semicontinuous
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)\to(-\infty,+\infty]
.
We set

\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\mu\log\mu\,dx

and a temperature parameter
\sigma>0
.
In this paper we consider

\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\mathcal{E}(\mu)+\sigma\,\mathrm{Ent}(\mu),

where
\mathcal{E}
is an internal energy.
The functional
\mathcal{F}
is
first-order differentiable
at
\mu
if there exists a Borel function
\Psi(\mu):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that for every smooth curve
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
with

\mu_{0}=\mu,\quad\partial_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}+\nabla\cdot(\mu_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon})=0,

where
v_{\varepsilon}\in T_{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
is a smooth velocity field and
v_{0}\in T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
, the map
\varepsilon\mapsto\mathcal{F}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
is differentiable at
\varepsilon=0
and

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{\varepsilon})\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Psi(\mu)(x)\cdot v_{0}(x)\,d\mu(x).

The function
\Psi(\mu)
is called a (representative of the)
first variation
of
\mathcal{F}
at
\mu
and is denoted by

\Psi(\mu)=\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}(\mu),

which is uniquely defined up to additive constants.
Under regularity assumptions, this curve-based notion of differentiability is equivalent to the Wasserstein subdifferential/gradient formulation (see
[
44
]
).
With this definition of first variation, the gradient is exactly the Riemannian gradient in Otto’s metric.
Assume the first variation of
\mathcal{F}
satisfies
\nabla\Psi(\mu)\in L^{2}(\mu)
at
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
.
The
Wasserstein gradient
of
\mathcal{F}
at
\mu
is the unique element

\operatorname{grad}\mathcal{F}(\mu)\in T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)

characterized by

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{\varepsilon})\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{grad}\mathcal{F}(\mu)\cdot v_{0}\,d\mu,

for every smooth curve
\mu_{\varepsilon}
as in the previous definitions.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is first-order differentiable in a neighborhood of
\mu
.
The functional
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
twice differentiable
at
\mu
if there exists a symmetric bilinear form

\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu):D_{\mu}\times D_{\mu}\subseteq T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)\times T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}

defined on a dense subset
D_{\mu}:=\{\nabla\varphi:\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\}
of
T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
, such that the following holds.
For every
u\in D_{\mu}
given by
u=\nabla\varphi
, there exists a unique Wasserstein geodesic
(\mu_{s})_{s\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})}
with
\mu_{0}=\mu
and initial velocity
u
.
This geodesic is characterized by the continuity equation

\partial_{s}\mu_{s}+\nabla\cdot(\mu_{s}\nabla\psi_{s})=0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),

where the potential
\psi_{s}
is a viscosity solution to the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\partial_{s}\psi_{s}+\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla\psi_{s}|^{2}=0

with initial condition
\psi_{0}=\varphi
.
Then, the map
s\mapsto\mathcal{F}(\mu_{s})
is twice differentiable at
s=0
, and we define the quadratic form

\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu)[u,u]\coloneqq\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{s})\Big|_{s=0}.

The bilinear form
\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu)[u,w]
is obtained by polarization for any smooth
u,w\in D_{\mu}
.
This form is called the
Hessian
or
second variation
of
\mathcal{F}
at
\mu
(see
[
53
]
for the porous medium case and
[
35
]
for the general rigorous framework).
Note that if
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi+\mathrm{const}
, then the corresponding (viscosity) solutions of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation differ by the same constant, hence they generate the same velocity field
\nabla\psi_{s}
and thus the same geodesic
(\mu_{s})_{s\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})}
.

A measure
\bar{\mu}\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{F})\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
is called a
stationary point
of
\mathcal{F}
if

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{\varepsilon})\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=0

for every curve
\mu_{\varepsilon}
that solves the continuity equation (
2.1
) with
\mu_{0}=\bar{\mu}
and initial velocity
u\in D_{\bar{\mu}}
.

Finally, we introduce the notion of gradient flow.
Assume that for each
\mu
in a suitable domain, the Wasserstein gradient
\operatorname{grad}\mathcal{F}(\mu)\in T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
exists.
A curve
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
is called a
Wasserstein gradient flow
of
\mathcal{F}
if
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
is locally absolutely continuous in
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega),\operatorname{W}_{2})
, and there exists a Borel family of velocity fields
v_{t}\in T_{\mu_{t}}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
such that the continuity equation (
2.1
) holds in the sense of distributions for a.e.
t>0
and

v_{t}=-\operatorname{grad}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{t})

for a.e.
t>0
.
When the first variation admits a representative
\Psi(\mu_{t})
with
\nabla\Psi(\mu_{t})\in L^{2}(\mu_{t})
, this is the equation

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\nabla\cdot\big(\mu_{t}\nabla\Psi(\mu_{t})\big)\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),

(2.3)

which is the formal
\operatorname{W}_{2}
-gradient flow of
\mathcal{F}
with periodic boundary conditions.

2.3
Stationary point and assumptions on the free energy

We start by establishing the existence of a stationary point.
If
\mathcal{F}
is displacement convex, one can verify that the stationary point is unique (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
).
However, for non-convex internal energies, multiple stationary points may exist.

Lemma 2.1

(Existence of stationary points)
.

Assume that the internal energy
\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is lower semicontinuous with respect to weak convergence.
Then
\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\mathcal{E}(\mu)+\sigma\,\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)
is lower semicontinuous on the weakly compact set
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
, so it admits at least one global minimizer
\mu_{*}
.
If, in addition,
\mathcal{E}
admits a first variation at
\mu_{*}
along smooth transport perturbations, then
\nabla(\delta\mathcal{F}/\delta\mu)=0
in the corresponding weak sense, so
\mu_{*}
is a stationary point.

Proof.

Since
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
is compact, the set
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
is compact with respect to the weak topology of measures.
The internal energy
\mathcal{E}
is lower semicontinuous by assumption, and the negative entropy
\mu\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\mu\log\mu
is strictly convex and lower semicontinuous with respect to weak convergence (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Remark 9.3.8]
).
Since
\mathcal{F}
is bounded from below (as
s\log s\geq-e^{-1}
), there exists a global minimizer
\mu_{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
.
Thus,
\mu_{*}
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation
\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}(\mu_{*})=\mathrm{const}
, making it a stationary point.
∎

Let
\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
be a stationary point of
\mathcal{F}
.
By definition,
\bar{\mu}\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{F})
, so it is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure and the first variation is well-defined and finite.
We assume for now that there exist constants
0<c\leq C<\infty
such that
c\leq\bar{\mu}(x)\leq C
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
(this will be justified a posteriori in Lemma
2.3
).
Using the first-variation formula, stationarity along the geodesic with initial velocity
u=\nabla\varphi
(for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
) gives

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}(\bar{\mu})\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,d\bar{\mu}=0.

Since
D_{\bar{\mu}}
is dense in
T_{\bar{\mu}}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
, we obtain

\nabla\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}(\bar{\mu})\right)=0\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\bar{\mu}),

hence
\delta\mathcal{F}/\delta\mu(\bar{\mu})
is constant
\bar{\mu}
-a.e., and therefore it is constant on
\Omega
under the bounds above.
We denote this constant by
\lambda
, which yields the Euler-Lagrange condition

\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}(\bar{\mu})(x)=\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}(\bar{\mu})(x)+\sigma(\log\bar{\mu}(x)+1)=\lambda\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega,

(2.4)

where
\lambda
is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the mass constraint.
We denote

\Phi(x)\coloneqq\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}(\bar{\mu})(x).

We work on the potential space

X\coloneqq H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu}),\qquad\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}\,\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx.

Then
X
is isometrically isomorphic to
\dot{H}^{1}(\bar{\mu})
as defined in Section
2
.
The boundedness of
\bar{\mu}
guarantees that
\|\cdot\|_{X}
is equivalent to the
H^{1}
-norm on
H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu})
(see Remark
2.4
).

We impose the following assumptions at
\bar{\mu}
.
Note that the boundedness assumption on
\bar{\mu}
is a consequence of the regularity of the first variation, as shown in Lemma
2.3
.

(E1)

Regularity of the first variation at
\bar{\mu}
.

\Phi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

(E2)

Bounded Wasserstein Hessian of
\mathcal{E}
at
\bar{\mu}
.

The Wasserstein Hessian of
\mathcal{E}
at
\bar{\mu}
is well-defined in
C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu})
and extends to a bounded symmetric bilinear form in
X
.
More precisely, for
\varphi,\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu})
, define

H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi)\coloneqq\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mu})(u_{\varphi},u_{\psi})\quad\text{satisfies}\quad|H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi)|\leq C_{E}\|\varphi\|_{X}\|\psi\|_{X}.

(2.5)

The form
H_{\mathcal{E}}
extends uniquely by continuity to all of
X
, and the same bound holds for all
\varphi,\psi\in X
.

Example 2.2
.

Here, we verify that the assumptions
(E1)
and
(E2)
hold for some examples of energy functionals
\mathcal{E}
.

(a)

External potential:

If
\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\int_{\Omega}V\,d\mu
with
V\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
, then
\Phi=V
and

H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{2}V(x)\,\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,d\bar{\mu}(x),

so
H_{\mathcal{E}}
is bounded on
X
with bound
\|\nabla^{2}V\|_{L^{\infty}}
; see, e.g.,
[
63
, Section 9.1]
.

(b)

Interaction energy:

If
\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\tfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}W(x-y)\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)
with
W\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
, then formally

H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\nabla^{2}W(x-y)\,(\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y))\cdot(\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(y))\,d\bar{\mu}(x)\,d\bar{\mu}(y),

which is bounded on
X
under the stated regularity; see, e.g.,
[
63
, Section 9.1]
.

(c)

Kullback-Leibler energy:

Let
\rho\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy
0<\rho_{\min}\leq\rho\leq\rho_{\max}
and define the reference measure
\nu=\rho\,dx
.
Setting

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=-\int_{\Omega}\mu\log\rho\,dx\quad\text{so that}\quad\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\int_{\Omega}\mu\log\frac{\mu}{\rho}\,dx=\mathrm{KL}(\mu\mid\nu),

we have
\Phi=-\log\rho\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
H_{\mathcal{E}}
is bounded exactly as in the external-potential case with
V=-\log\rho
.
Thus
(E1)
and
(E2)
hold and
\bar{\mu}=\nu
is the stationary point.

(d)

Nonlinear nonlocal interaction:

Consider an energy which depends nonlinearly on a convolution, modeling saturation effects in aggregation or neural activity (see, e.g.,
[
25
]
):

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\int_{\Omega}U(K\ast\mu)(x)\,dx,

where
K:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth interaction kernel and
U:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth nonlinear function (e.g.,
U(s)=\cos(s)
or
s^{4}
).
The first variation is
\Phi(x)=(\check{K}\ast U^{\prime}(K\ast\mu))(x)
, where
\check{K}(z)=K(-z)
.
If
K\in C^{3}(\Omega)
and
U\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R})
with bounded derivatives, then
(E1)
and
(E2)
hold at any stationary point.
Indeed, the Wasserstein Hessian involves terms of the form
(U^{\prime\prime}\circ(K*\bar{\mu}))\nabla(K*\psi)\otimes\nabla(K*\phi)
and
(U^{\prime}\circ(K*\bar{\mu}))\nabla^{2}K
, which define bounded operators on
X
due to the smoothness of
U,K
and the compactness of
\Omega
.

The following lemma collects the structural properties of
\bar{\mu}
that we will use in the following sections.

Lemma 2.3

(Regularity and bounds of
\bar{\mu}
)
.

Let
\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
be a stationary point of
\mathcal{F}
.
Assume
(E1)
.
Then:

(i)

\bar{\mu}(x)=Z^{-1}\exp(-\Phi(x)/\sigma)
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, where
Z\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}e^{-\Phi(y)/\sigma}\,dy
.

(ii)

There exist constants
0<c_{0}\leq C_{0}<\infty
such that
c_{0}\leq\bar{\mu}(x)\leq C_{0}
for a.e.
x\in\Omega.

(iii)

\bar{\mu}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
\nabla\bar{\mu}(x)=-\frac{1}{\sigma}\bar{\mu}(x)\nabla\Phi(x)
for a.e.
x\in\Omega.

Proof.

By stationarity, (
2.4
) holds, so

\log\bar{\mu}(x)=\frac{\lambda-\Phi(x)}{\sigma}-1\implies\bar{\mu}(x)=\exp(\frac{\lambda}{\sigma}-1)e^{-\Phi(x)/\sigma}=Z^{-1}e^{-\Phi(x)/\sigma},

where
Z^{-1}=\exp(\frac{\lambda}{\sigma}-1)
is determined by the normalization
\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}=1
.
This gives (i).

Since
\Phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, there exists
M>0
such that
|\Phi(x)|\leq M
a.e.
Then

e^{-M/\sigma}\leq e^{-\Phi(x)/\sigma}\leq e^{M/\sigma},

and in particular
0<e^{-M/\sigma}|\Omega|\leq Z\leq e^{M/\sigma}|\Omega|<\infty
with
|\Omega|=(2\pi)^{d}
.
This yields (ii) with
c_{0}\coloneqq Z^{-1}e^{-M/\sigma}
and
C_{0}\coloneqq Z^{-1}e^{M/\sigma}
.
In addition, since
\Phi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, the chain rule yields
e^{-\Phi/\sigma}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
Hence,
\bar{\mu}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and

\nabla\bar{\mu}(x)=-\frac{1}{\sigma}\bar{\mu}(x)\,\nabla\Phi(x)

for a.e.
x
.
This proves (iii).
∎

Remark 2.4

(Equivalence of norms)
.

By Lemma
2.3
(ii), for all
\varphi\in X
,

c_{0}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq C_{0}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

By the Poincaré inequality on
X
(which follows from the Poincaré inequality on
H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\Omega)
and the bounds on
\bar{\mu}
),

\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\simeq\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence
\|\varphi\|_{X}\simeq\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
on
X
, and
X
is a Hilbert space with an equivalent norm.

3
Operator-Theoretic structure of the Hessian

This section describes the analytic core of the paper.
Its purpose is to realize the Wasserstein Hessian at
\bar{\mu}
as a self-adjoint operator on the potential space
X
and to identify, through the isometry
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
, the same spectral object as the generator of the linearized PDE.
Recall that
X=H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu})
is endowed with the weighted inner product
\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}=\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}\,\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx
.
We first realize the Wasserstein Hessian at
\bar{\mu}
as a closed form on
X
.
We start by recalling the known expression for the Hessian of the entropy (
3.1
).
For a formal derivation, we refer the reader to
[
63
, Section 9.1]
, whereas a rigorous framework can be found in
[
47
,
35
]
.
See also Appendix
A
for a proof.

Proposition 3.1

(Hessian of the entropy)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy
0<c\leq\mu
a.e. for some constant
c>0
.
For
\varphi,\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
, let
u_{\varphi}=\nabla\varphi
and
u_{\psi}=\nabla\psi
.
Then,

\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)(u_{\varphi},u_{\psi})=\int_{\Omega}\mu(x)\,\nabla^{2}\varphi(x):\nabla^{2}\psi(x)\,dx.

(3.1)

For smooth
\varphi,\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we define

q_{0}(\varphi,\psi)\coloneqq\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathrm{Ent}(\bar{\mu})(u_{\varphi},u_{\psi})=\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}(x)\,\nabla^{2}\varphi(x):\nabla^{2}\psi(x)\,dx.

(3.2)

By Lemma
3.2
below, the form norm
\|\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\coloneqq q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}
is equivalent to
\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
.
Thus,
q_{0}
extends uniquely to a closed symmetric form on its domain
D(q_{0})=H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
(identified with the
\bar{\mu}
-zero-mean subspace of
H^{2}
), which is a dense subspace of
X
.

Lemma 3.2

(
H^{2}
-coercivity)
.

There exist constants
c,C>0
such that for all
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
,

\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq c\Big(q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(3.3)

As a consequence, on
H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
, the norm
\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
is equivalent to the graph norm

\|\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}:=q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
be the zero mean (with respect to
\bar{\mu}
) representative of its equivalence class and set
k=-(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,dx
.
Then
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi+k
is the representative with zero mean with respect to Lebesgue measure and satisfies
k=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\varphi}\,d\bar{\mu}
.
From Lemma
2.3
(ii), we have

q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq c_{0}\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},\qquad\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\geq c_{0}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Note that
\nabla\varphi=\nabla\tilde{\varphi}
and
\nabla^{2}\varphi=\nabla^{2}\tilde{\varphi}
.
The Fourier series identity
\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}|\hat{\tilde{\varphi}}_{k}|^{2}
and
\|\nabla\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}|k|^{2}|\hat{\tilde{\varphi}}_{k}|^{2}
yield
\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|\nabla\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
.
Hence, using the
H^{2}(\Omega)
-norm for
\tilde{\varphi}
,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}\Big(q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\Big),

with
\tilde{c}
depending only on
c_{0}
.
Using
\langle\tilde{\varphi},k\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0
, we have
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(2\pi)^{d}|k|^{2}
.
This Pythagorean identity implies that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}\Big(q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\Big)+(2\pi)^{d}|k|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}\Big(q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\Big)+C_{0}^{2}(2\pi)^{2d}\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}\Big(q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\Big)+C_{0}^{2}(2\pi)^{2d}\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\Big),

with
c=\tilde{c}(1+C_{0}^{2}(2\pi)^{2d})
.
For the converse estimate, Lemma
2.3
(ii) and the inequalities
\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
and
\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
give

q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

so the graph norm
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
on
H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Hessian as a closed form, part of Theorem
A
)
.

Assume
(E1)
and
(E2)
, and let
\bar{\mu}
be a stationary point of
\mathcal{F}
.
Then, there exists a symmetric bilinear form

a:D(a)\times D(a)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad D(a)=H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R},

such that for all smooth potentials
\varphi,\psi
,

\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\bar{\mu})(u_{\varphi},u_{\psi})=a(\varphi,\psi)=H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi)+\sigma\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{2}\varphi:\nabla^{2}\psi\,d\bar{\mu}(x).

(3.4)

Moreover
a
is densely defined, symmetric, closed, and bounded from below on the Hilbert space
(X,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X})
.
Consequently, there exists a unique self-adjoint operator
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
such that for all
\varphi\in D(A),\ \psi\in D(a)

a(\varphi,\psi)=\langle A\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}.

(3.5)

The domain of
A
is given by

D(A)=\big\{\varphi\in D(a):\exists f\in X\text{ s.t. }a(\varphi,\psi)=\langle f,\psi\rangle_{X}\;\forall\psi\in D(a)\big\},

and
A\varphi=f
.

Proof.

By
(E2)
, the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}
at
\bar{\mu}
defines a bounded symmetric bilinear form
H_{\mathcal{E}}
on
X
.
From Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
2.3
, for smooth potentials
\varphi,\psi
, we have

\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\bar{\mu})(u_{\varphi},u_{\psi})=H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi)+\sigma\,q_{0}(\varphi,\psi).

This motivates the definition of
a
on
C^{\infty}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
by
a(\varphi,\psi)\coloneqq H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi)+\sigma\,q_{0}(\varphi,\psi)
.

By Lemma
3.2
, the Hilbert space
H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
is complete with respect to the graph norm
\|\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\coloneqq q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}
.
Additionally,
q_{0}
is continuous with respect to this norm by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
Since
C^{\infty}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
is dense in
H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
,
q_{0}
extends uniquely to a densely defined symmetric form on
D(q_{0})=H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
.
This extension is closed because, by Lemma
3.2
,
D(q_{0})
is complete with respect to the form norm
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
.

From
(E2)
, for all
\varphi\in X
we have

|H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\varphi)|\leq C_{E}\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}.

Using Lemma
3.2
, this implies that for all
\varphi,\psi\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
,

|H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi)|\leq C_{E}\|\varphi\|_{X}\|\psi\|_{X}\leq C_{E}\|\varphi\|_{\star}\|\psi\|_{\star},

so
H_{\mathcal{E}}
is bounded on
(D(a),\|\cdot\|_{\star})
.
In particular,
H_{\mathcal{E}}
is bounded relative to
q_{0}
.
We then define
D(a)\coloneqq H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
and
a:=\sigma q_{0}+H_{\mathcal{E}}
.
Since
H_{\mathcal{E}}
is bounded on
(D(a),\|\cdot\|_{\star})
, the form
a
is a bounded perturbation of the closed form
\sigma q_{0}
, hence closed (see
[
42
, Chapter VI, Theorem 1.11]
).
Explicitly,
D(a)
is a dense subspace of
X
because
H^{2}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\|\cdot\|_{X}
is equivalent to the
H^{1}
-seminorm.
Moreover,
a(\varphi,\varphi)\geq-C_{E}\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}
, so
a
is bounded from below.

By the representation theorem for densely defined symmetric closed forms bounded from below
(e.g.,
[
42
, Chapter VI, Theorem 2.6]
), there exists a unique self-adjoint operator

A
associated with
a
satisfying (
3.5
) and representing the Wasserstein
Hessian of
\mathcal{F}
. Moreover,
A
is bounded from below with the same lower bound as
a
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

For
\varphi\in D(A)
,
A\varphi
is the unique element of
X
satisfying
a(\varphi,\psi)=\langle A\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}
for all
\psi\in D(a)
.
In density variables the linearized generator is
L=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}A\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}
(see Theorem

).

Example 3.5

(McKean-Vlasov case)
.

For
V,W\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
and
W
even, let

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\int_{\Omega}V\,d\mu+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}W(x-y)\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

and
\bar{\mu}
be a stationary point of
\mathcal{F}
, which solves the self-consistent equation

\bar{\mu}\propto\exp\Bigl(-\frac{1}{\sigma}\bigl(V+W*\bar{\mu}\bigr)\Bigr).

Then

\displaystyle H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}\,\nabla^{2}V\,\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\nabla^{2}W(x-y)\,(\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y))\cdot(\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(y))\,d\bar{\mu}(x)\,d\bar{\mu}(y).

The linearized operator in density variables is

L\rho=\sigma\nabla\cdot\Bigl(\bar{\mu}\nabla\Bigl(\frac{\rho}{\bar{\mu}}\Bigr)\Bigr)+\nabla\cdot\big(\bar{\mu}\nabla(W*\rho)\big).

In potential coordinates,
A
is the unique operator satisfying

\displaystyle\langle A\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}=\sigma\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}\,\nabla^{2}\varphi:\nabla^{2}\psi\,dx+H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\psi).

3.1
Energy estimate and compact resolvent

Given
f\in X
and
\lambda>0
, we consider the resolvent problem
(\lambda I+A)\varphi=f
.
In this section, we derive a priori control on
\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
in terms of
\|f\|_{X}
to obtain a compact resolvent of
A
.

Lemma 3.6

(
H^{2}
-estimate for the resolvent)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.3
, there exist
\lambda_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and
f\in X
, the solution
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
of (
3.8
) satisfies

\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{X}.

(3.6)

Proof.

Given the lower bound
a(\varphi,\varphi)\geq-C_{E}\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}
, choosing
\lambda>C_{E}
gives

a_{\lambda}(\varphi,\varphi)\coloneqq a(\varphi,\varphi)+\lambda\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{X}\geq(\lambda-C_{E})\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}.

Moreover, for
\lambda>C_{E}
,

a_{\lambda}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq\sigma\,q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+(\lambda-C_{E})\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\geq c_{\lambda}\|\varphi\|_{\star}^{2},

where
c_{\lambda}\coloneqq\min\{\sigma,\lambda-C_{E}\}>0
, so
a_{\lambda}
is coercive on
(D(a),\|\cdot\|_{\star})
.
The form
a_{\lambda}
is also continuous on
(D(a),\|\cdot\|_{\star})
since
q_{0}
is continuous with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
and
H_{\mathcal{E}}
is bounded on
(D(a),\|\cdot\|_{\star})
.
Moreover,
\psi\mapsto\langle f,\psi\rangle_{X}
is a bounded linear functional on
(D(a),\|\cdot\|_{\star})
because
\|\psi\|_{X}\leq\|\psi\|_{\star}
.

For
\lambda
such that
a_{\lambda}
is coercive, the Lax-Milgram theorem ensures there exists a unique
\varphi\in D(a)
solving the variational problem

a_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)=\langle f,\psi\rangle_{X}\quad\forall\psi\in D(a).

(3.7)

By Theorem
3.3
, this variational solution is precisely the unique element
\varphi\in D(A)
satisfying the resolvent equation

(\lambda I+A)\varphi=f.

(3.8)

Now, consider
\lambda
large enough so that
a_{\lambda}
is coercive.
Taking
\psi=\varphi
in (
3.7
) yields

a_{\lambda}(\varphi,\varphi)=\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{X}\quad\Rightarrow\quad a(\varphi,\varphi)+\lambda\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}=\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{X}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz,

|\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{X}|\leq\|f\|_{X}\|\varphi\|_{X}.

Let
c_{H}>0
be the constant from Lemma
3.2
such that

\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq c_{H}\left(q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\right).

Then

q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq c_{H}^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}.

Using
a(\varphi,\varphi)=\sigma q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\varphi)
and

|H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\varphi)|\leq C_{E}\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}
, we obtain

a(\varphi,\varphi)\geq\frac{\sigma}{c_{H}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-(\sigma+C_{E})\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}.

Set
c_{1}\coloneqq\sigma/c_{H}>0
.
Substituting into the previous identity gives

c_{1}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left(\lambda-(\sigma+C_{E})\right)\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{X}\|\varphi\|_{X}.

Choose
\lambda_{0}\coloneqq\sigma+C_{E}+1
and
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.
Then

c_{1}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{X}\|\varphi\|_{X}.

By Remark
2.4
and the embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\Omega)
, there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq C_{3}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Combining, we have

c_{1}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{X}\|\varphi\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{X}\sqrt{C_{3}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}}.

If
\varphi=0
there is nothing to prove; otherwise divide by
\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
to obtain

c_{1}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}}{\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq\sqrt{C_{3}}\|f\|_{X}.

Dropping the nonnegative second term on the left yields

c_{1}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sqrt{C_{3}}\|f\|_{X},

which proves (
3.6
) with
C\coloneqq\sqrt{C_{3}}/c_{1}
.
∎

Theorem 3.7

(Compact resolvent of
A
, part of Theorem
A
)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.3
, the self-adjoint operator
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
has compact resolvent.
Consequently, the spectrum of
A
consists only of real eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, with no finite accumulation point.
Since
A
is bounded from below, its eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity) satisfy
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.6
, for every
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and every
f\in X
, the resolvent equation

(\lambda I+A)\varphi=f
has a unique solution
\varphi\in D(A)
, so
[\lambda_{0},\infty)\subset\rho(-A)
.
Fix one such
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.
Then, for each
f\in X
, the resolvent solution
\varphi=(\lambda I+A)^{-1}f
belongs to
H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
and satisfies

\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{X}.

Thus we have a bounded map

(\lambda I+A)^{-1}:X\longrightarrow H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}.

Compactness follows from the compact embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\Omega)
(Kondrachov embedding theorem).
By Remark
2.4
, the
X
-norm is equivalent to the standard
H^{1}(\Omega)
-norm on
H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu})
, so the inclusion
H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow X
is compact.
Therefore the composition

(\lambda I+A)^{-1}:X\xrightarrow{\text{bounded}}H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\xrightarrow{\text{compact}}X

is a compact operator on
X
.
For any other
\lambda^{\prime}\in\rho(-A)
, compactness of
(\lambda^{\prime}I+A)^{-1}
follows from the resolvent identity

(\lambda^{\prime}I+A)^{-1}=(\lambda I+A)^{-1}+(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})(\lambda^{\prime}I+A)^{-1}(\lambda I+A)^{-1},

since
(\lambda I+A)^{-1}
is compact and
(\lambda^{\prime}I+A)^{-1}
is bounded.
The characterization of the spectrum then follows from spectral theory of self-adjoint operators with compact resolvent
[
42
]
.
∎

3.2
Linearized flow in density variables

Since
\bar{\mu}
is bounded above and below by Lemma
2.3
, the norms on
H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu})
and on
H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
are equivalent.
Each equivalence class in
H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
has a unique representative with zero
\bar{\mu}
-mean.
Thus we identify
H^{-1}(\bar{\mu})
with the mean-zero subspace of
H^{-1}(\Omega)
(equivalent norms), and work with

H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{f\in H^{-1}(\Omega):\langle f,1\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}=0\}.

We now view the Riesz map
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
from (
2.2
) as a map into
H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
, namely,

\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}:X\to H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega),\qquad\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi\coloneqq-\nabla\cdot(\bar{\mu}\nabla\varphi).

We let
\nu_{t}\in H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
denote mean-zero density perturbations (e.g.,
\nu_{t}=\mu_{t}-\bar{\mu}
), and write
\varphi_{t}=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\nu_{t}
for the corresponding potential.
We equip
H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
with the inner product

\langle\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi,\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X},

and view it as the Hilbert space
\mathcal{T}
.

Theorem 3.8

(Linearized flow in density variables, Theorem
B
)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.12
, the linearization of the Wasserstein gradient flow (
2.3
) at the stationary point
\bar{\mu}
is given on
H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
by

\partial_{t}\nu_{t}=L\nu_{t},

where

L=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}A\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1},\qquad D(L)=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}(D(A)).

The operator
L
is self-adjoint on the Hilbert space
(H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{T}})
induced by
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
and has compact resolvent there.

For the proof of Theorem
3.8
, we first establish the isomorphism
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}:X\to H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
and the induced inner product on
H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
(Lemma
3.9
).
We then linearize the gradient flow in the potential chart using the General Linearization Principle in Proposition
3.12
, which yields the representation
L=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}A\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}
.
Finally, we transfer self-adjointness (in the appropriate Hilbert space) and compact resolvent from
A
to
L
via conjugation by
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
.

3.2.1
The isomorphism
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}:X\to H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)

We first identify potentials with mean-zero densities in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 3.9

(Isomorphism and norm equivalence)
.

Let
\bar{\mu}
satisfy Lemma
2.3
and define the operator

\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}:X\to H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega),\qquad\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi\coloneqq-\nabla\cdot(\bar{\mu}\nabla\varphi).

Then:

(i)

\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
is bijective.

(ii)

The inner product
\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}f,\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}g\rangle_{X}
makes
H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
a Hilbert space
\mathcal{T}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}:(X,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X})\to(\mathcal{T},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{T}})
is a unitary isomorphism.

(iii)

The induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{T}}
is equivalent to the dual norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
.

Moreover,
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
satisfies the duality relation

\langle\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}\quad\forall\varphi\in X,\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega),

so
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
realizes the Riesz isomorphism between
X
and its dual, identified with
H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

First, observe that for any
\varphi\in X
, the distribution
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi
lies in
H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
since

\langle\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi,1\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}=0.

The duality relation stated in the lemma follows immediately from the definition of the weak divergence.

(i)
Injectivity.
If
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi=0
, then
\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}=\langle\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}=0
, which implies
\varphi=0
in
X
and, therefore,
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
is injective.

Surjectivity.
Let
f\in H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
. The functional
\psi\mapsto\langle f,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}
belongs to
X^{*}
(using the embedding
X\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
).
By the Riesz representation theorem on
X
, there exists a unique
\varphi\in X
such that

\langle\varphi,\chi\rangle_{X}=\langle f,\chi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\forall\chi\in X.

To distinguish
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi
from
f
as distributions, we examine their action on any
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Decompose
\psi=\chi+c
with
\chi\in X
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since both
f
and
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi
are in
H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
, they vanish on constants, i.e.,
\langle f,c\rangle=\langle\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi,c\rangle=0
.
Because their action coincides on
X
, they are equal as functionals on
H^{1}(\Omega)
, proving surjectivity.

(ii) This follows directly from the definition of the inner product on
\mathcal{T}
and the bijectivity established in (i).

(iii) Let
f\in H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
and set
\varphi=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}f
.
For the upper bound, Lemma
2.3
gives
\bar{\mu}\leq C_{0}
, so

\|f\|_{H^{-1}}=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\leq C_{0}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{X}=C\|f\|_{\mathcal{T}}.

For the lower bound, we use

\|f\|_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}=\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}=\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

By the Poincaré inequality on
X
(Remark
2.4
),
\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C^{\prime}\|\varphi\|_{X}
.
Hence

\|f\|_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{T}}\implies\|\varphi\|_{X}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{T}}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

This proves the norm equivalence.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Since
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
are equivalent, compactness of the resolvent and discreteness of the spectrum persist when
L
is viewed as an unbounded operator on
(H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)})
, although
L
need not be self-adjoint with respect to the canonical
H^{-1}
-inner product.

3.2.2
Equivalence of PDE Linearization and the Wasserstein Hessian

The connection between the Hessian and the linearized dynamics is well-known in smooth manifolds.
Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a smooth Hilbert manifold equipped with its Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
.
The gradient of
F
is defined by
g(\operatorname{grad}F(x),\xi)=dF(x)[\xi]
for
\xi\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}
, and the Hessian by

\mathrm{Hess}_{g}F(x)(\xi,\eta)=g_{x}\big(\nabla_{\xi}\operatorname{grad}F(x),\eta\big),\quad\xi,\eta\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}.

The linearization of
\dot{x}=-\operatorname{grad}F(x)
at a critical point
x_{\star}
satisfies

\dot{h}=-D(\operatorname{grad}F)(x_{\star})[h]=-\nabla_{h}\operatorname{grad}F(x_{\star})=-\mathrm{Hess}_{g}F(x_{\star})h,

which acts as a self-adjoint operator on
T_{x_{\star}}\mathcal{M}
.
See
[
29
]
for the finite-dimensional case and
[
54
]
for the Hilbert-manifold setting.
However, in the Wasserstein space, this fact is more subtle.

Since
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega),\operatorname{W}_{2})
lacks a smooth Riemannian structure, we work in a local pushforward chart around
\bar{\mu}
.
In particular, we work in the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
with index
s>d/2+2
so that
H^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{2}(\Omega)
by the Sobolev embedding theorem.
We define

\mathcal{U}:=\big\{\xi\in H^{s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}:\|\nabla^{2}\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}<1\big\},

where
\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}\coloneqq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\Omega}\|A(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
.
For
\xi\in\mathcal{U}
, the Jacobian of the map
T_{\xi}:=\mathrm{Id}+\nabla\xi
is strictly positive because

\det(\nabla T_{\xi})=\det(\mathrm{Id}+\nabla^{2}\xi)\geq(1-\|\nabla^{2}\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})})^{d}>0

everywhere on
\Omega
.
Therefore, the inverse function theorem implies that
T_{\xi}
is a local
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism.
Since
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
is compact and connected, any local diffeomorphism on
\Omega
is a finite-sheeted covering map.
Moreover
\det(\nabla T_{\xi})>0
implies it is orientation-preserving, so its degree equals the number of sheets.
Finally,
T_{\xi}
is homotopic to the identity map (via
I+t\nabla\xi
), hence it has degree one, so it is a one-sheeted covering and therefore a global
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism of
\Omega
.
Consequently, the pushforward
\mu_{\xi}:=(T_{\xi})_{\sharp}\bar{\mu}
is a well-defined probability measure in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
.
We define the pullback energies
\hat{\mathcal{E}}(\xi)\coloneqq\mathcal{E}(\mu_{\xi})
and
\hat{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(\mu_{\xi})
.
We first record the regularity of the pushforward map and then prove the Linearization Principle.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
s>d/2+2
and
\bar{\mu}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
Define
\Xi:\mathcal{U}\to H^{-1}(\Omega)
by

\langle\Xi(\xi),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}:=\int_{\Omega}\psi(x+\nabla\xi(x))\,\bar{\mu}(x)\,dx,\qquad\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Then
\Xi
is
C^{2}
at
\xi=0
as a map
H^{s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\to H^{-1}(\Omega)
, with

D\Xi(0)\eta=-\nabla\cdot(\bar{\mu}\nabla\eta)=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\eta,

(3.9)

and, for
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in H^{s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
,

\langle D^{2}\Xi(0)[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}],\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\mathbf{G}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})(x)\,dx,

(3.10)

where, for each
i=1,\ldots,d
,

G_{i}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}\!\left[(\partial_{x_{i}}\eta_{1})(\partial_{x_{j}}\eta_{2})\,\bar{\mu}\right].

Proof.

Differentiating formally
\xi\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\psi(x+\nabla\xi(x))\bar{\mu}(x)\,dx
at
\xi=0
in the direction
\eta
, we obtain

\langle D\Xi(0)\eta,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\nabla\eta(x)\,\bar{\mu}(x)\,dx.

Integrating by parts gives
\langle D\Xi(0)\eta,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}=-\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)\,\nabla\cdot(\bar{\mu}\nabla\eta)(x)\,dx
, i.e.

D\Xi(0)\eta=-\nabla\cdot(\bar{\mu}\nabla\eta)=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}[\eta],

proving (
3.9
).
We first take
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Differentiating once more in the direction
\eta_{2}
gives the formula

[D^{2}\Xi(0)[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}]]\psi=\int_{\Omega}[\nabla^{2}\psi(x)\,\nabla\eta_{1}(x)]\cdot\nabla\eta_{2}(x)\,\bar{\mu}(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j}(\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}\psi)(\partial_{x_{i}}\eta_{1})(\partial_{x_{j}}\eta_{2})\bar{\mu}\,dx.

Integrating by parts in
x_{j}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[\nabla^{2}\psi(x)\,\nabla\eta_{1}(x)]\cdot\nabla\eta_{2}(x)\,\bar{\mu}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{x_{i}}\psi)\partial_{x_{j}}\left[(\partial_{x_{i}}\eta_{1})(\partial_{x_{j}}\eta_{2})\bar{\mu}\right]dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi\cdot\mathbf{G}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\,dx,

proving (
3.10
) for smooth test functions.

By the Sobolev embedding
H^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{2}(\Omega)
(since
s>d/2+2
), we have
\nabla\eta_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\nabla^{2}\eta_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
With
\bar{\mu}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, each component
G_{i}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega)
and satisfies

\|\mathbf{G}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}.

Hence for
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\big|\langle D^{2}\Xi(0)[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}],\psi\rangle\big|\leq\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{G}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}.

By density of
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, the identity (
3.10
) and the above bound extend to all
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, so
D^{2}\Xi(0)[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}]\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
In particular,
D^{2}\Xi(0)
defines a bounded bilinear map from
H^{s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\times H^{s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
to
H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
The Taylor remainder estimate

4
Finite-dimensional feedback convexification and stabilization

As discussed in the introduction, the presence of multiple equilibria in non-convex energy landscapes leads to metastable dynamics, where the system may remain trapped in local minima or be repelled by unstable stationary states (saddles or local maxima).
At such unstable states
\bar{\mu}
, the Wasserstein Hessian possesses negative eigenvalues, precluding any local convergence of the unperturbed gradient flow.
Even for convex potentials, the system might converge to
\bar{\mu}
at a slow rate.
The feedback control is constructed to act on the operator
A
representing the Wasserstein Hessian.
It therefore shifts the spectrum of
A
, and hence modifies both the local curvature of
\mathcal{F}
and the linearized PDE generator
L=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}A\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}
.
We achieve this by constructing a finite-rank feedback control that acts explicitly on the spectral subspace associated with the eigenvalues in
(-\infty,\delta]
for a prescribed
\delta>0
.
This intervention yields a closed-loop energy
\mathcal{F}_{\rm cl}
whose Hessian at
\bar{\mu}
is strictly positive, and whose linearized gradient flow is exactly the closed-loop system.
When the nonlinear pullback is sufficiently regular in the energy space
X
, this infinitesimal convexification upgrades to local strong convexity of the lifted closed-loop energy.

4.1
Formulation and spectral decomposition

Consider the controlled free energy

\mathcal{F}(\mu)\,+\,\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}(t)\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{j}\,d\mu,

where
\alpha_{j}\in D(A)
are prescribed shape functions and
u_{j}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
are scalar controls.
The controlled gradient flow reads

\partial_{t}\mu=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}(t)\,\nabla\cdot(\mu\nabla\alpha_{j}).

To interpret this nonlinear equation, we restrict the initial datum to
\mu_{0}\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\rm ac}(\Omega)
such that its density, also denoted by
\mu_{0}
, belongs to
L^{d}(\Omega)
for some
d\geq 3
.
Note that the control acts on the dynamics via a bilinear coupling between
\mu
and
u(t)
.
For fixed
\mu
, we collect these control-induced transport terms in the operator
C(\mu)u\coloneqq-\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}\mathcal{I}_{\mu}[\alpha_{j}]
for
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Equivalently, for each fixed
u
,
C(\mu)u
is the density-form Wasserstein gradient of the linear functional
\mu\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{j}\,d\mu
.
Linearization at the stationary point
\bar{\mu}
yields, in potential coordinates,

\partial_{t}\xi=-A\xi-Bu(t),\qquad Bu\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}\alpha_{j}=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}C(\bar{\mu})u(t),

and, in density variables,
\partial_{t}\nu=L\nu+C(\bar{\mu})u(t)
.

By Theorem
3.7
, the self-adjoint operator
A
has compact resolvent on
X
, so its spectrum is a sequence of real eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots
with finite multiplicities and
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
.
Let
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
denote an orthonormal eigenbasis so that
A\phi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\phi_{k}
.
For a prescribed
\delta>0
, define the spectral subspace associated with the eigenvalues in
(-\infty,\delta]
by

\Sigma_{\delta}:=\{k\geq 1:\lambda_{k}\leq\delta\},\qquad X_{\delta}:=\mathrm{span}\{\phi_{k}:k\in\Sigma_{\delta}\}.

Since the eigenvalues form a discrete set and
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
, the set
\Sigma_{\delta}
is finite.
Let
m:=|\Sigma_{\delta}|
and
P:X\to X_{\delta}
be the orthogonal projection, i.e.,

P=\sum_{k\in\Sigma_{\delta}}\langle\cdot,\phi_{k}\rangle_{X}\phi_{k}.

The objective is to stabilize the modes in
X_{\delta}
by constructing controls that act selectively on this subspace.
We then verify
\delta
-stabilizability for the system
\partial_{t}\xi=-A\xi-Bu
using the infinite-dimensional Hautus test, see, e.g.,
[
27
, Theorem 5.2.11]
.
This requires

\ker(\lambda I+A)\cap\ker(-B^{*})=\{0\}\qquad\text{for all }\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\text{ with }\Re\lambda\geq-\delta.

Since
A
is self-adjoint with compact resolvent, the test reduces to the eigenspaces corresponding to eigenvalues in
\Sigma_{\delta}
, i.e., it is enough to require that for all
k\in\Sigma_{\delta}
,

\varphi\in\ker(A-\lambda_{k}I)\setminus\{0\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad B^{*}\varphi=(\langle\varphi,\alpha_{j}\rangle_{X})_{j=1}^{m}\neq 0.

We know that
B^{*}\varphi\neq 0
if and only if
\varphi\notin(\operatorname{span}{\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}})^{\perp}
.
Thus, it is enough to select
\alpha_{j}\in D(A)
for
j\in\Sigma_{\delta}
such that

X_{\delta}=\bigoplus_{k\in\Sigma_{\delta}}\ker(A-\lambda_{k}I)\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},

so that, for every
k\in\Sigma_{\delta}
and every
\varphi\in\ker(A-\lambda_{k}I)\setminus\{0\}
, one has
B^{*}\varphi\neq 0
.
For concreteness, we henceforth fix the choice
\alpha_{j}=\phi_{j}
for
j\in\Sigma_{\delta}
.
Then
BB^{*}=P
and
B^{*}\xi=(\langle\xi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{X})_{k\in\Sigma_{\delta}}
.
This proves the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\delta>0
and choose shape functions
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in D(A)
such that

X_{\delta}\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}.

Then, for every
k\in\Sigma_{\delta}
and every nonzero
\varphi\in\ker(A-\lambda_{k}I)
, one has
B^{*}\varphi\neq 0
.
In particular, for the canonical choice
\alpha_{j}=\phi_{j}
for
j\in\Sigma_{\delta}
,

BB^{*}=P\qquad\text{and}\qquad B^{*}\xi=(\langle\xi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{X})_{k\in\Sigma_{\delta}}.

Remark 4.2
.

Since
D(A)\subseteq H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
, the control terms

\mathcal{I}_{\mu}[\alpha_{j}]=-\nabla\cdot(\mu\nabla\alpha_{j})

are well-defined in the weak sense for bounded densities
\mu
.
Hence, under the choice
\alpha_{j}\in D(A)
, the nonlinear control operator is well-defined on the local chart neighborhood described above.

4.2
Linear-quadratic optimal control and feedback synthesis

The Riccati synthesis now acts on the finite-dimensional spectral window of the Hessian operator
A
selected above.
For a given rate
\delta>0
and a bounded, self-adjoint, nonnegative operator
Q:X\to X
, define the cost functional

J(\xi_{0},u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta t}\left(\langle Q\xi(t),\xi(t)\rangle_{X}+|u(t)|^{2}\right)\,dt.

(4.1)

Here
X
is the state space and the control space is
U=\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,X)
.
The set of admissible controls is
u\in L^{2}_{\delta}(0,\infty;U)
with
L^{2}_{\delta}
defined by the weight
e^{\delta t}
in the cost.
We invoke the Hautus
\delta
-stabilizability criterion as in Section
4.1
.
Setting
z(t)\coloneqq e^{\delta t}\xi(t)
and
v(t)\coloneqq e^{\delta t}u(t)
yields the linear-quadratic optimal control problem, also called the linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) problem,

\partial_{t}z=-(A-\delta I)z-Bv.

We also assume
Q
is diagonal in the eigenbasis
\{\phi_{k}\}
, i.e.,

Q\phi_{k}=m_{k}\phi_{k},\qquad m_{k}\geq 0,

with
m_{k}>0
for
k\in\Sigma_{\delta}
, so that
Q
acts on
X_{\delta}
.

Theorem 4.3

(Hautus
\delta
-stabilization, part of Theorem
C
)
.

Fix
\delta>0
and adopt the canonical choice
\alpha_{j}=\phi_{j}
for
j\in\Sigma_{\delta}
.
Then the infinite-dimensional Hautus condition for the system
\partial_{t}\xi=-A\xi-Bu
at rate
\delta
,

\ker(\lambda I+A)\cap\ker(-B^{*})=\{0\}\qquad\text{for all }\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\ \text{with}\ \Re\lambda\geq-\delta,

holds.
Consequently, the pair
(-A,-B)
is
\delta
-stabilizable.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
,
A
is self-adjoint and bounded from below on
X
, hence
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
.
For the canonical choice
\alpha_{j}=\phi_{j}
, one has

Bu=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}\phi_{j},\qquad\|Bu\|_{X}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}|u_{j}|^{2}=|u|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{2},

so
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,X)
.
Thus the hypotheses needed to apply the infinite-dimensional Hautus test from
[
27
, Theorem 5.2.11]
are satisfied.
By the discussion preceding Lemma
4.1
, since
X_{\delta}=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
, the Hautus condition holds, and therefore
(-A,-B)
is
\delta
-stabilizable.
∎

The solution to this linear-quadratic optimal control problem is obtained via the algebraic Riccati equation (ARE).
For a feedback operator
\Pi:X\to X
, the ARE reads as

-(A-\delta I)\Pi-\Pi(A-\delta I)-\Pi BB^{*}\Pi+Q=0.

(4.2)

The next proposition establishes the existence and uniqueness of a self-adjoint, nonnegative solution
\Pi\in\mathcal{L}(X)
to (
4.2
) and identifies the corresponding optimal control.
Notice that the assumption that the pair
(-A+\delta I,Q^{1/2})
is detectable is verified under our assumptions for
Q
because for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\Re\lambda\geq 0
,

\operatorname{ker}(\lambda I+A-\delta I)\subseteq X_{\delta},\quad\operatorname{ker}(Q^{1/2})\subseteq X_{\delta}^{c}\cup\{0\}.

Proposition 4.4

(Optimal control solution via Riccati equation)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
4.3
and that the pair
(-A+\delta I,Q^{1/2})
is detectable.
Then there exists a unique self-adjoint, nonnegative
\Pi\in\mathcal{L}(X)
solving (
4.2
).
The feedback law

u(t)=B^{*}\Pi\xi(t)

is the unique optimal control for the LQR problem, minimizing
J(\xi_{0},u)
and yielding the closed-loop operator

A_{\Pi}\coloneqq A+BB^{*}\Pi,

such that
-A_{\Pi}
generates an exponentially stable semigroup with decay rate at least
\delta
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
, the pair
(-A,-B)
is
\delta
-stabilizable, and by hypothesis the pair
(-A+\delta I,Q^{1/2})
is detectable.
By
[
27
, Theorem 5.2.11]
, there exists a unique self-adjoint, nonnegative solution
\Pi\in\mathcal{L}(X)
of (
4.2
), and the optimal feedback law is
v=B^{*}\Pi z
.
Moreover, the closed-loop operator
-(A-\delta I+BB^{*}\Pi)
generates an exponentially stable semigroup.
Consequently, the closed-loop system for
\xi
is exponentially stable with decay rate at least
\delta
.
∎

The next proposition yields an explicit formula for
\Pi
when
Q
is diagonal in the eigenbasis of
A
, which as a consequence reduces the computational complexity of the feedback design to knowing the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
A
.

Proposition 4.5

(Diagonal case)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Proposition
4.4
and that
Q
is diagonal in the basis
\{\phi_{k}\}
with
Q\phi_{k}=m_{k}\phi_{k}
, where
m_{k}>0
for
k\in\Sigma_{\delta}
.
Then, the unique nonnegative solution
\Pi\in\mathcal{L}(X)
of (
4.2
) is given by the diagonal operator
\Pi\phi_{k}=\pi_{k}\phi_{k}
, with

\pi_{k}=\begin{cases}-(\lambda_{k}-\delta)+\sqrt{(\lambda_{k}-\delta)^{2}+m_{k}}&\text{for }k\in\Sigma_{\delta},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\frac{m_{k}}{2(\lambda_{k}-\delta)}&\text{for }k\notin\Sigma_{\delta}.\end{cases}

In particular, the closed-loop operator
A_{\Pi}
is self-adjoint with eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}+\pi_{k}
for
k\in\Sigma_{\delta}
and
\lambda_{k}
for
k\notin\Sigma_{\delta}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
, there exists a unique nonnegative solution
\Pi\in\mathcal{L}(X)
to the Riccati equation (
4.2
).
To find this solution explicitly, we postulate a diagonal operator
\widetilde{\Pi}
defined by
\widetilde{\Pi}\phi_{k}=\pi_{k}\phi_{k}
with
\pi_{k}\geq 0
.
Evaluating the algebraic Riccati operator on a basis vector
\phi_{k}
yields

\displaystyle-(A-\delta I)\widetilde{\Pi}\phi_{k}-\widetilde{\Pi}(A-\delta I)\phi_{k}-\widetilde{\Pi}P\widetilde{\Pi}\phi_{k}+Q\phi_{k}

\displaystyle\quad=-\pi_{k}(A-\delta I)\phi_{k}-(\lambda_{k}-\delta)\pi_{k}\phi_{k}-\pi_{k}^{2}P\phi_{k}+m_{k}\phi_{k}

\displaystyle\quad=-\left(2(\lambda_{k}-\delta)\pi_{k}+\pi_{k}^{2}\delta_{k\in\Sigma_{\delta}}-m_{k}\right)\phi_{k},

where we used
P\phi_{k}=\phi_{k}
if
k\in\Sigma_{\delta}
, and
0
otherwise.
This expression vanishes if
2(\lambda_{k}-\delta)\pi_{k}+\pi_{k}^{2}\delta_{k\in\Sigma_{\delta}}-m_{k}=0
, i.e.,
\tilde{\Pi}
is a solution to (
4.2
) if and only if this expression is true for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
k\notin\Sigma_{\delta}
, the quadratic term vanishes, leaving
2(\lambda_{k}-\delta)\pi_{k}=m_{k}
and since
\lambda_{k}>\delta
, its unique non-negative solution is
\pi_{k}=\frac{m_{k}}{2(\lambda_{k}-\delta)}
.
For
k\in\Sigma_{\delta}
, the expression vanishes if

\pi_{k}^{2}+2(\lambda_{k}-\delta)\pi_{k}-m_{k}=0,

whose unique non-negative root is
\pi_{k}=-(\lambda_{k}-\delta)+\sqrt{(\lambda_{k}-\delta)^{2}+m_{k}}
.
Thus, our diagonal
\widetilde{\Pi}
is a nonnegative solution to the algebraic Riccati equation.
By the uniqueness guaranteed by Proposition
4.4
, we conclude that
\Pi=\widetilde{\Pi}
.
Finally, the closed-loop operator
A_{\Pi}=A+P\Pi
clearly has eigenfunctions
\phi_{k}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}+\pi_{k}
for
k\in\Sigma_{\delta}
, and simply
\lambda_{k}
for
k\notin\Sigma_{\delta}
, verifying the claimed eigenvalue shifts.
∎

Remark 4.6

(Time-weighted formulation)
.

In the time-weighted formulation for
z
and
v
, the closed-loop operator becomes
A-\delta I+BB^{*}\Pi
.
Thus
-A_{\Pi}
generates an exponentially stable semigroup with decay rate at least
\delta
if and only if
-(A-\delta I+BB^{*}\Pi)
is exponentially stable in the unweighted variables.

Corollary 4.7

(Closed-loop spectral gap, part of Theorem
C
)
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
4.5
, the closed-loop operator

A_{\Pi}\coloneqq A+P\Pi

is self-adjoint with eigenvalues

\lambda_{k}^{\Pi}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{k}+\pi_{k}=\sqrt{(\lambda_{k}-\delta)^{2}+m_{k}}+\delta,&k\in\Sigma_{\delta},\\
\lambda_{k},&k\notin\Sigma_{\delta}.\end{cases}

In particular, defining

\lambda_{\Pi}\coloneqq\min\Bigl(\min_{k\in\Sigma_{\delta}}\sqrt{(\lambda_{k}-\delta)^{2}+m_{k}}+\delta,\min_{k\notin\Sigma_{\delta}}\lambda_{k}\Bigr)>\delta,

we have
A_{\Pi}\geq\lambda_{\Pi}I
.

Proof.

Immediate from Proposition
4.5
and the formula for
\pi_{k}
.
∎

4.3
Local exponential stabilization

The spectral gap obtained for
A_{\Pi}
now has to be transferred to the full nonlinear closed-loop PDE.
The goal of this subsection is Theorem
4.8
, which gives local exponential stabilization for the nonlinear closed-loop equation.
We first introduce the functional setting and the nonlinear estimates needed in the bootstrap argument, including the McKean-Vlasov example.
We then derive the linear estimates for the shifted closed-loop operator.
The subsection closes with the proof of Theorem
4.8
.

4.3.1
Functional Setting and Nonlinear Estimates

For
T\in(0,\infty]
, define

\mathcal{Z}_{T}:=L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(0,T;D(A))\cap H^{1}(0,T;X),

where
D(A)
carries the graph norm
\|\varphi\|_{D(A)}:=\left(\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}+\|A\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.
We equip
\mathcal{Z}_{T}
with the norm

\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}}:=\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R})}+\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;D(A))}+\|\partial_{t}\xi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;X)}.

For the density perturbation, we define
\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}(H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R})
, with norm
\|\nu\|_{\mathcal{V}}\coloneqq\|\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\nu\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
, and

\mathcal{Y}_{T}:=L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;D(L))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)),

where
D(L)
carries the graph norm
\|\rho\|_{D(L)}:=\left(\|\rho\|_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}+\|L\rho\|_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.
We equip
\mathcal{Y}_{T}
with the norm

\|\nu\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}:=\|\nu\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;D(L))}+\|\partial_{t}\nu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}.

Since
L_{\Pi}:=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}A_{\Pi}\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}=L-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}(P\Pi)\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}
is a bounded perturbation of
L
, one has
D(L_{\Pi})=D(L)
.

Since the control formulation acts by adding the linear functionals
\mu\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{j}\,d\mu
to the energy
\mathcal{E}
, the closed-loop dynamics retain the continuity-equation form

\partial_{t}\mu=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}[\mu]\right)+C(\mu)u,\qquad u(t)=B^{*}\Pi\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}[\mu(t)-\bar{\mu}].

Writing
\nu=\mu-\bar{\mu}
and
\xi:=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\nu
, and using
C(\bar{\mu})=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}B
, we have

\partial_{t}\nu=L_{\Pi}\nu+\mathfrak{N}(\nu)+\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\nu),

(4.3)

where the nonlinear remainder and the feedback bilinear terms are defined by, respectively,

\mathfrak{N}(\nu):=\nabla\cdot\left((\bar{\mu}+\nu)\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}[\bar{\mu}+\nu]\right)-L\nu,\quad\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\nu)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(B^{*}\Pi\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\nu)_{j}\,\nabla\cdot(\nu\nabla\alpha_{j}).

The corresponding nonlinear remainder and feedback bilinear terms in potential coordinates are, respectively

\mathcal{N}(\xi):=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\mathfrak{N}(\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\xi),\quad\mathcal{M}_{\Pi}(\xi):=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\xi).

With this notation, the closed-loop perturbation dynamics in potential coordinates take the form

\partial_{t}\xi+A_{\Pi}\xi=\mathcal{N}(\xi)+\mathcal{M}_{\Pi}(\xi),\qquad\xi(0)=\xi_{0}.

We isolate two ingredients for the nonlinear stability proof: a local well-posedness and quadratic-Lipschitz assumption for the closed-loop density equation, and a regularity assumption on the linearized first variation.
Assumption
(E4)
is used to show that the feedback contribution is quadratic in density variables and, in particular, that the canonical control profiles satisfy
\alpha_{j}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
For concrete models, any weaker hypothesis implying this regularity would suffice, while the local well-posedness in Assumption
(E3)
(i) should be verified separately.

(E3)

Local well-posedness and quadratic-Lipschitz energy remainder.

There exist
r>0
and
C_{N}>0
such that the following hold:

(i)

For every admissible initial density
\mu_{0}=\bar{\mu}+\nu_{0}
with
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
and
\|\nu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<r
, the closed-loop density equation admits a unique maximal solution
\mu
on an interval
[0,T_{\max})
, and its perturbation
\nu=\mu-\bar{\mu}
satisfies for all
T<T_{\max}
,

\nu\in C([0,T];L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];\mathcal{T})\cap\mathcal{Y}_{T}.

Moreover, for every
T<T_{\max}
,
\nu
solves (
4.3
) in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))
and if
T_{\max}<\infty
, then

\limsup_{t\uparrow T_{\max}}\|\nu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq r.

(ii)

For every
\nu,\eta\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
with
\|\nu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<r
and
\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<r
,

\|\mathfrak{N}(\nu)-\mathfrak{N}(\eta)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{N}\left(\|\nu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\nu-\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(E4)

Higher-order regularity of the linearized first variation.

There exists an integer
\ell>\max\{1,d/2-3\}
such that

\Phi=\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}[\bar{\mu}]\in W^{\ell+1,\infty}(\Omega),

and the derivative

\mathcal{K}:=D\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}[\bar{\mu}]

extends to bounded linear maps
\mathcal{K}:H^{s}_{\diamond}(\Omega)\to H^{s+2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
for every
s=0,\dots,\ell
, where
H^{s}_{\diamond}(\Omega):=\left\{\eta\in H^{s}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\eta\,dx=0\right\}
.

We can now state the main nonlinear stabilization result.
The remainder of this subsection is devoted to the estimates entering its proof.

Theorem 4.8

(Local exponential stabilization, Theorem
D
)
.

Assume Corollary
4.7
,
(E3)
, and
(E4)
.
Then there exist
\varepsilon>0
,
\rho>0
, and
C>0
such that for every admissible initial density
\mu_{0}=\bar{\mu}+\nu_{0}
with
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
and
\|\nu_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon
, the feedback law

u(t)=B^{*}\Pi\xi(t),\quad\xi(t):=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\nu(t),

yields a unique global solution
\mu
of the closed-loop density equation whose perturbation
\nu:=\mu-\bar{\mu}
satisfies

\nu\in C_{b}([0,\infty);L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega))\cap C_{b}([0,\infty);\mathcal{T})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(0,\infty;H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}_{\rm loc}(0,\infty;D(L))

with
e^{\delta(\cdot)}\nu\in\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}
and
\|e^{\delta(\cdot)}\nu\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}}\leq\rho
, and

\|\nu(t)\|_{\mathcal{T}}\leq Ce^{-\delta t}\|\nu_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

This solution is unique among all solutions in this class satisfying
\|e^{\delta(\cdot)}\nu\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}}\leq\rho
.
Equivalently,
\xi(t):=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\nu(t)
decays exponentially in
X
with rate at least
\delta
.

Lemma 4.9

(Regularity of the canonical control profiles)
.

Assume
(E4)
.
Then the canonical control profiles fixed above satisfy, for
j\in\Sigma_{\delta}
,

\alpha_{j}\in D(A)\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
j\in\Sigma_{\delta}
and set
\nu_{j}:=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}[\alpha_{j}]
. Since
\alpha_{j}\in D(A)\subset H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
and
\bar{\mu}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
by Lemma
2.3
, one has
\nu_{j}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
The regularity
\Phi\in W^{\ell+1,\infty}(\Omega)
from
(E4)
yields
\bar{\mu}\in W^{\ell+1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
Additionally, the Fréchet differentiability of
\mu\mapsto\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}[\mu]
at
\bar{\mu}
gives the linearization

L\nu=\sigma\nabla\cdot\left(\bar{\mu}\nabla\left(\frac{\nu}{\bar{\mu}}\right)\right)+\nabla\cdot\left(\bar{\mu}\nabla(\mathcal{K}\nu)\right).

Since
\alpha_{j}
is an eigenfunction of
A
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}
, the density mode
\nu_{j}=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\alpha_{j}
satisfies

L\nu_{j}=-\lambda_{j}\nu_{j}.

Hence

-\sigma\nabla\cdot\left(\bar{\mu}\nabla\left(\frac{\nu_{j}}{\bar{\mu}}\right)\right)=\lambda_{j}\nu_{j}+\nabla\cdot\left(\bar{\mu}\nabla(\mathcal{K}\nu_{j})\right).

Set
w_{j}:=\nu_{j}/\bar{\mu}
.
Then
w_{j}
solves a uniformly elliptic divergence-form equation with coefficient
\bar{\mu}
.
We claim that
\nu_{j}\in H^{\ell+2}(\Omega)
.
Since
\nu_{j}\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{K}:L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega)\to H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
by
(E4)
, the right-hand side belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Standard elliptic regularity on the torus therefore gives
w_{j}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, and hence
\nu_{j}=\bar{\mu}w_{j}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
.
Now assume inductively that
\nu_{j}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
for some
s\in\{0,\dots,\ell-1\}
.
Then
(E4)
gives
\mathcal{K}\nu_{j}\in H^{s+2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
, hence

\lambda_{j}\nu_{j}+\nabla\cdot\left(\bar{\mu}\nabla(\mathcal{K}\nu_{j})\right)\in H^{s}(\Omega).

Another elliptic regularity step yields
w_{j}\in H^{s+2}(\Omega)
, and therefore
\nu_{j}=\bar{\mu}w_{j}\in H^{s+2}(\Omega)
because
\bar{\mu}\in W^{\ell+1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
By induction,
\nu_{j}\in H^{\ell+2}(\Omega)
.

Finally,
\alpha_{j}
solves
-\nabla\cdot(\bar{\mu}\nabla\alpha_{j})=\nu_{j}
.
Since
\nu_{j}\in H^{\ell+2}(\Omega)
and
\bar{\mu}\in W^{\ell+1,\infty}(\Omega)
, elliptic regularity gives
\alpha_{j}\in H^{\ell+4}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
.
Because
\ell>\max\{1,d/2-3\}
, one has
\ell+4>d/2+1
, and therefore the Sobolev embedding yields
\alpha_{j}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
This proves the claim.
∎

We verify the assumptions
(E3)
and
(E4)
for the McKean-Vlasov equation with interaction potential
W
and external potential
V
.

Example 4.10

(McKean-Vlasov revisited)
.

Let
\ell>\max\{1,d/2-3\}
be an integer, and suppose
V\in W^{\ell+1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
W\in W^{\ell+2,\infty}(\Omega)
.
Then

\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}[\mu]=V+W*\mu,\qquad\mathcal{K}\eta=W*\eta,\qquad\Phi=V+W*\bar{\mu}.

By Example
3.13
(a), the derivative
\mathcal{K}=D\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}[\bar{\mu}]
is well defined as a map

H^{-1}(\Omega)\to H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
, and since
\bar{\mu}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\Omega
is compact, one has

\Phi\in W^{\ell+1,\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, for every
s\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
and every

\eta\in H^{s}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
, differentiation under the convolution sign and Young’s inequality give

\|\mathcal{K}\eta\|_{H^{s+2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}}\leq C_{s}\|\eta\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)},

so
(E4)
holds.

For the nonlinear energy remainder one has
\mathfrak{N}(\nu)=\nabla\cdot\left(\nu\nabla(W*\nu)\right)
for all
\nu\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
, and the quadratic
H^{-1}
-estimate in
(E3)
(ii) follows by a similar argument from
[
41
, Lemma 3.5]
.
Additionally,
[
41
, Section 3.4, Theorem 3.3, Theorem 3.6, Corollary 3.7]
provides a maximal-regularity-based local closed-loop theory corresponding to
(E3)
(i).

Lemma 4.11

(Feedback bilinear estimate)
.

Assume
(E4)
.
There exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{fb}}>0
such that for all
\nu,\eta\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
,

\|\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\nu)-\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\eta)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{fb}}\left(\|\nu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\nu-\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In particular,
\|\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\nu)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{fb}}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
for all
\nu\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Define
\beta(\nu):=B^{*}\Pi\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
For
j\in\Sigma_{\delta}
, set
K_{j}\rho:=\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\alpha_{j})
.
By Lemma
4.9
, the canonical control profiles satisfy
\alpha_{j}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
For
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

|\langle K_{j}\rho,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)}|=\left|\int_{\Omega}\rho\nabla\alpha_{j}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\right|\leq\|\nabla\alpha_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

hence
\|K_{j}\rho\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla\alpha_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
Since
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\nu)=\sum_{j\in\Sigma_{\delta}}\beta_{j}(\nu)\,K_{j}\nu
, we obtain

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\nu)-\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\eta)=\sum_{j\in\Sigma_{\delta}}\left(\beta_{j}(\nu)-\beta_{j}(\eta)\right)K_{j}\nu+\sum_{j\in\Sigma_{\delta}}\beta_{j}(\eta)K_{j}(\nu-\eta).

By Lemma
3.9
(iii), there exists
C_{\mathrm{eq}}>0
such that
\|\rho\|_{\mathcal{T}}\leq C_{\mathrm{eq}}\|\rho\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
for all
\rho\in H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
.
Since
B^{*}\Pi\in\mathcal{L}(X,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and the embedding
L^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega)
is continuous, there exists
C_{*}>0
such that for all
\nu\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
,

|\beta(\nu)|\leq C_{*}\|\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\nu\|_{X}=C_{*}\|\nu\|_{\mathcal{T}}\leq C_{*}\|\nu\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{*}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

and likewise
|\beta(\nu)-\beta(\eta)|\leq C_{*}\|\nu-\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\nu)-\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\eta)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\Sigma_{\delta}}\|\nabla\alpha_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(|\beta_{j}(\nu)-\beta_{j}(\eta)|\,\|\nu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|\beta_{j}(\eta)|\,\|\nu-\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{fb}}\left(\|\nu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\nu-\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

∎

The next estimate converts the quadratic structure of the nonlinear terms into a weighted bound adapted to the target decay rate
\delta
.
Set
\mathfrak{R}_{\Pi}(\nu):=\mathfrak{N}(\nu)+\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fb}}(\nu)
.

Corollary 4.12

(Weighted quadratic estimate for the full closed-loop remainder)
.

Assume
(E3)
and
(E4)
, and fix
\delta>0
.
Define

\mathfrak{R}_{\Pi,\delta}(w)(t):=e^{\delta t}\mathfrak{R}_{\Pi}(e^{-\delta t}w(t)).

Then, there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{rem}}>0
, independent of
\delta
, such that

\|\mathfrak{R}_{\Pi,\delta}(w)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}\leq C_{\mathrm{rem}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{2}

for every
T>0
and every
w\in\mathcal{Y}_{T}
such that
\nu(t):=e^{-\delta t}w(t)
is an admissible perturbation for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq r.

Proof.

Let
T>0
and
w\in\mathcal{Y}_{T}
satisfy the stated assumptions, and set

\nu(t):=e^{-\delta t}w(t).

Then
\|\nu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq r
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
.
By assumption
(E3)
(ii) with
\eta=0
and Lemma
4.11
, we have

\|\mathfrak{R}_{\Pi}(\nu(t))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq(C_{N}+C_{\mathrm{fb}})\|\nu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
.
Therefore

\|\mathfrak{R}_{\Pi,\delta}(w)(t)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}=e^{\delta t}\|\mathfrak{R}_{\Pi}(\nu(t))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq(C_{N}+C_{\mathrm{fb}})e^{-\delta t}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
.
Since
D(A)\subset H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
and both spaces are Banach, the closed graph theorem yields a continuous embedding, for all
\phi\in D(A)
,

\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{D(A)\hookrightarrow H^{2}}\|\phi\|_{D(A)}.

Let
\rho\in D(L)
and set
\phi:=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}\rho\in D(A)
. Since

\rho=-\nabla\bar{\mu}\cdot\nabla\phi-\bar{\mu}\Delta\phi

and
\bar{\mu}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, it follows that

\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathcal{I}}\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathcal{I}}C_{D(A)\hookrightarrow H^{2}}\|\phi\|_{D(A)}=C_{\mathcal{I}}C_{D(A)\hookrightarrow H^{2}}\|\rho\|_{D(L)}.

Setting
C_{D(L)\hookrightarrow L^{2}}:=C_{\mathcal{I}}C_{D(A)\hookrightarrow H^{2}}
, taking the
L^{2}(0,T)
-norm, and using
e^{-\delta t}\leq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{R}_{\Pi,\delta}(w)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{N}+C_{\mathrm{fb}})\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{N}+C_{\mathrm{fb}})C_{D(L)\hookrightarrow L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,T;D(L))}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{N}+C_{\mathrm{fb}})C_{D(L)\hookrightarrow L^{2}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{2}.

This proves the claim with

C_{\mathrm{rem}}:=(C_{N}+C_{\mathrm{fb}})C_{D(L)\hookrightarrow L^{2}}.

∎

4.3.2
Weighted Linear Estimates

We now combine these weighted remainder bounds with a linear estimate for the shifted closed-loop generator.

Lemma 4.13

(Shifted closed-loop form and
H^{2}
-control)
.

Assume Corollary
4.7
.
Set
A_{\delta}:=A_{\Pi}-\delta I
and define

a_{\delta}(\varphi,\psi):=a(\varphi,\psi)+\langle P\Pi\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}-\delta\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X},\qquad D(a_{\delta})=H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}.

Then there exist constants
c_{\delta},C_{\delta},C_{D,\delta}>0
such that for all
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
,

c_{\delta}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq a_{\delta}(\varphi,\varphi)\leq C_{\delta}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and for all
\varphi\in D(A)
,
\|\varphi\|_{D(A)}\leq C_{D,\delta}\|A_{\delta}\varphi\|_{X}
.

Proof.

Since
P\Pi-\delta I\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is self-adjoint,
a_{\delta}
is the closed symmetric form on
H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
associated with
A_{\delta}=A_{\Pi}-\delta I
.
Moreover,
a(\varphi,\varphi)=\sigma q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+H_{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi,\varphi)
with
H_{\mathcal{E}}
bounded on
X
, so there exists
C_{0}>0
such that for all
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
,

a_{\delta}(\varphi,\varphi)\leq\sigma q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+C_{0}\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq C_{\delta}\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

by Lemma
3.2
.
Corollary
4.7
gives
A_{\Pi}\geq\lambda_{\Pi}I
, hence for all
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
,

a_{\delta}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq(\lambda_{\Pi}-\delta)\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}.

Therefore,

\sigma q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)\leq a_{\delta}(\varphi,\varphi)+C_{0}\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{C_{0}}{\lambda_{\Pi}-\delta}\right)a_{\delta}(\varphi,\varphi),

Combining this with Lemma
3.2
gives

\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq c\left(q_{0}(\varphi,\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\right)\leq c_{\delta}^{-1}a_{\delta}(\varphi,\varphi),

which proves the lower bound.
Since
D(A_{\delta})=D(A)
and
A_{\delta}\geq(\lambda_{\Pi}-\delta)I
,

\|\varphi\|_{X}\leq(\lambda_{\Pi}-\delta)^{-1}\|A_{\delta}\varphi\|_{X}.

We then obtain

\|A\varphi\|_{X}\leq\|A_{\delta}\varphi\|_{X}+\|P\Pi-\delta I\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\|\varphi\|_{X}\leq C\|A_{\delta}\varphi\|_{X},

and

\|\varphi\|_{D(A)}\leq C_{D,\delta}\|A_{\delta}\varphi\|_{X},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 4.14

(Weighted linear estimate for
A_{\delta}
)
.

Assume Corollary
4.7
.
Set
A_{\delta}:=A_{\Pi}-\delta I
.
Then for every
f\in L^{2}(0,\infty;X)
and
z_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique solution
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}
of

\partial_{t}z+A_{\delta}z=f\quad\text{a.e. on }(0,\infty),\qquad z(0)=z_{0}

satisfying

\|z\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}}\leq C_{\mathrm{lin}}\left(\|z_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;X)}\right).

Proof.

Let
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be the orthonormal eigenbasis from Corollary
4.7
, and let
P_{N}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N}\}
.
Then
P_{N}
commutes with
A_{\delta}
.
For each
N
, let
z_{N}
be the unique solution of

\partial_{t}z_{N}+A_{\delta}z_{N}=P_{N}f,\qquad z_{N}(0)=P_{N}z_{0}.

Testing this equation against
A_{\delta}z_{N}
gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}a_{\delta}(z_{N},z_{N})+\|A_{\delta}z_{N}\|_{X}^{2}=\langle P_{N}f,A_{\delta}z_{N}\rangle_{X}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|P_{N}f\|_{X}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|A_{\delta}z_{N}\|_{X}^{2}.

Integrating over
(0,t)
, we obtain

a_{\delta}(z_{N}(t),z_{N}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\|A_{\delta}z_{N}(s)\|_{X}^{2}\,ds\leq a_{\delta}(P_{N}z_{0},P_{N}z_{0})+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;X)}^{2}

for every
t\geq 0
.
Since
P_{N}
is the spectral projection of the positive self-adjoint operator
A_{\delta}
,

a_{\delta}(P_{N}z_{0},P_{N}z_{0})\leq a_{\delta}(z_{0},z_{0})\leq C_{\delta}\|z_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

by Lemma
4.13
.
Therefore,

\sup_{t\geq 0}a_{\delta}(z_{N}(t),z_{N}(t))+\|A_{\delta}z_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;X)}^{2}\leq C\left(\|z_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;X)}^{2}\right).

Using the previous estimate, Lemma
4.13
, and the identity
\partial_{t}z_{N}=P_{N}f-A_{\delta}z_{N}
, we deduce

\|z_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R})}+\|z_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;D(A))}+\|\partial_{t}z_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;X)}\leq C\left(\|z_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;X)}\right).

Thus
\{z_{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}
.
Applying the same estimate to
w_{N,M}:=z_{N}-z_{M}
shows that
\{z_{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
is Cauchy in
\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}
using that
P_{N}f\to f
in
L^{2}(0,\infty;X)
and
P_{N}z_{0}\to z_{0}
in the form norm of
a_{\delta}
.
Therefore there exists
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}
such that
z_{N}\to z
in
\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}
.
Passing to the limit in the projected equations yields

\partial_{t}z+A_{\delta}z=f\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,\infty;X),\qquad z(0)=z_{0}.

The estimate passes to the limit as well and gives the claimed bound.
Uniqueness follows by applying the same energy inequality to the difference of two solutions with the same data.
∎

Lemma 4.15

(Weighted linear estimate in density variables)
.

Assume Corollary
4.7
.
Set
L_{\Pi,\delta}:=L_{\Pi}+\delta I=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}A_{\delta}\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}
.
Then for every
f\in L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega))
and
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
, there exists a unique solution
\nu\in\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}
of

\partial_{t}\nu-L_{\Pi,\delta}\nu=f\quad\text{a.e. on }(0,\infty),\qquad\nu(0)=\nu_{0}

satisfying

\|\nu\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}}\leq C_{\mathrm{lin}}\left(\|\nu_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{-1}(\Omega))}\right).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.9
(iii),
H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{T}
are the same vector space with equivalent norms.
Hence
f\in L^{2}(0,\infty;\mathcal{T})
, and
z_{0}:=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}[\nu_{0}]\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
,
g:=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}[f]\in L^{2}(0,\infty;X)
, with

\|z_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}=\|\nu_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}},\qquad\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;X)}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;\mathcal{T})}\leq C_{\mathrm{eq}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{-1}(\Omega))}.

By Lemma
4.14
, there exists a unique
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}
solving

\partial_{t}z+A_{\delta}z=g,\qquad z(0)=z_{0},

and
\|z\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}}\leq C_{\mathrm{lin}}\left(\|z_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;X)}\right)
.
Define
\nu:=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}[z]
.
Then

\partial_{t}\nu-L_{\Pi,\delta}\nu=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\left[\partial_{t}z+A_{\delta}z\right]=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}[g]=f.

Since
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}(H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
with

\|\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

, the restriction of
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
to

is bounded into
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, by the definition of
L
, the restriction of
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
maps
D(A)
onto
D(L)
isometrically for the graph norms, and
\|\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\xi\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\xi\|_{X}
by Lemma
3.9
(iii).
It follows that
\nu\in\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}
and

\|\nu\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}}\leq C_{\mathrm{lin}}\|z\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}}\leq C_{\mathrm{lin}}\left(\|\nu_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{-1}(\Omega))}\right)

for a suitable constant

.
For uniqueness, let
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}
solve the same problem and set
z:=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})\in L^{2}(0,\infty;D(A))\cap H^{1}(0,\infty;X)
, then
z(0)=0
and

\partial_{t}z+A_{\delta}z=0\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,\infty;X).

Testing against
A_{\delta}z
as in Lemma
4.14
gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}a_{\delta}(z,z)+\|A_{\delta}z\|_{X}^{2}=0.

Since
z(0)=0
, it follows that
a_{\delta}(z(t),z(t))=0
for all

\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}
.
∎

4.3.3
Proof of Theorem
4.8

Proof.

Let
C_{\mathrm{lin}}
be the constant from Lemma

the constant from Corollary

4.4
Closed-loop convexification

We now reinterpret the spectral shift as a statement about the closed-loop energy itself.
We first establish a second-order convexification result at the equilibrium
\bar{\mu}
in the sense that the feedback control makes the Wasserstein Hessian of the modified energy strictly positive at
\bar{\mu}
.
For every
\mu\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{F})
such that
\mu-\bar{\mu}\in H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega)
, define the closed-loop energy by

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mu):=\mathcal{F}(\mu)+\mathcal{Q}(\mu),\quad\mathcal{Q}(\mu):=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle P\Pi\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}(\mu-\bar{\mu}),\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}(\mu-\bar{\mu})\right\rangle_{X}.

Since
P\Pi
is bounded and self-adjoint on
X
,

D\mathcal{Q}(\mu)[h]=\langle h,P\Pi\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}(\mu-\bar{\mu})\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)},\forall h\in H^{-1}_{\diamond}(\Omega).

Hence, using
BB^{*}=P
, the Wasserstein gradient flow of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is precisely the closed-loop equation

\partial_{t}\mu=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu\nabla\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}[\mu]+\frac{\delta\mathcal{Q}}{\delta\mu}[\mu]\right)\right)=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu\nabla\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}[\mu]+P\Pi\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}(\mu-\bar{\mu})\right)\right).

For
\xi\in\mathcal{U}
, we write
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\xi):=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mu_{\xi})
.

Proposition 4.16

(Closed-loop Hessian and linearized dynamics)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Proposition
3.12
and Corollary
4.7
.
Define, for
\varphi,\psi\in D(a)
,

a_{\Pi}(\varphi,\psi)\coloneqq a(\varphi,\psi)+\langle P\Pi\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}.

Then
a_{\Pi}
is a densely defined symmetric closed form on
X
associated with the self-adjoint operator
A_{\Pi}=A+P\Pi
.
Moreover,
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is twice Fréchet differentiable at
\xi=0
,
D\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(0)=0
, and its Hessian
D^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(0)
, initially defined on
H^{s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
, admits the unique continuous extension
a_{\Pi}
to
D(a)\times D(a)
.
In particular, for every
\varphi\in D(A)
and
\psi\in D(a)
,

D^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(0)[\varphi,\psi]=a_{\Pi}(\varphi,\psi)=\langle A_{\Pi}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}=-\langle L_{\Pi}\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi,\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{T}},

where
L_{\Pi}=-\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}A_{\Pi}\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}
.
Equivalently, for every smooth
\varphi,\psi
,

\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\bar{\mu})(u_{\varphi},u_{\psi})=a_{\Pi}(\varphi,\psi).

In particular, for all
\varphi\in D(a)
,

\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\bar{\mu})\geq\lambda_{\Pi}I,\qquad a_{\Pi}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq\lambda_{\Pi}\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.12
, the pullback
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
of
\mathcal{F}
is twice Fréchet differentiable at
0
and

D^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}(0)[\varphi,\psi]=a(\varphi,\psi)\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in D(A),\ \psi\in D(a).

Let
G(\xi)\coloneqq\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}(\mu_{\xi}-\bar{\mu})=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}(\Xi(\xi)-\bar{\mu})
.
By Lemma
3.11
, the map
G:\mathcal{U}\to X
is
C^{2}
at
0
, satisfies
G(0)=0
, and

DG(0)\psi=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}^{-1}D\Xi(0)\psi=\psi.

By the chain rule,
D^{2}\mathcal{Q}(0)[\varphi,\psi]=\langle P\Pi\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}
.
Since
\bar{\mu}
is stationary for
\mathcal{F}
and
D\mathcal{Q}(0)=0
, we also have
D\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(0)=0
.
Since
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\xi)=\hat{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)+\mathcal{Q}(\xi)
, it follows that

D^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(0)[\varphi,\psi]=a(\varphi,\psi)+\langle P\Pi\varphi,\psi\rangle_{X}=a_{\Pi}(\varphi,\psi)

for all
\varphi,\psi\in H^{s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
.
Since
P\Pi
is bounded and self-adjoint on
X
, the form
a_{\Pi}
is a bounded symmetric perturbation of the closed form
a
, hence it is closed and represented by the self-adjoint operator
A_{\Pi}=A+P\Pi
.
Since
H^{s}(\Omega)
is dense in
D(a)
with respect to the form norm of
a_{\Pi}
, this identity extends uniquely to
D(a)\times D(a)
.
Since
P\Pi
is bounded and Corollary
4.7
gives
A_{\Pi}\geq\lambda_{\Pi}I
, the associated closed form satisfies, for all
\varphi\in D(a)
,

a_{\Pi}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq\lambda_{\Pi}\|\varphi\|_{X}^{2}.

The identity
a_{\Pi}(\varphi,\psi)=-\langle L_{\Pi}\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\varphi,\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{T}}
follows from the definition of
L_{\Pi}
.

Finally, for smooth
\varphi
the curve
t\mapsto\mu_{t\varphi}
is the
\operatorname{W}_{2}
-geodesic through
\bar{\mu}
with initial velocity
u_{\varphi}
, as shown in the proof of Proposition
3.12
.
Hence

\mathrm{Hess}_{\operatorname{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\bar{\mu})(u_{\varphi},u_{\varphi})=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mu_{t\varphi})\Big|_{t=0}=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(t\varphi)\Big|_{t=0}=D^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(0)[\varphi,\varphi]=a_{\Pi}(\varphi,\varphi).

Polarization yields the bilinear identity for smooth
\varphi,\psi
.
∎

The feedback convexification of
\mathcal{F}
is built by replacing the original Hessian
A
by the shifted operator
A_{\Pi}
.
The same operator appears in the closed-loop linearization
\partial_{t}\xi=-A_{\Pi}\xi
, so stabilization and convexification are two formulations of the same spectral statement.
To upgrade this pointwise statement to strong convexity of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
on a neighborhood, one needs control of the nonlinear remainder beyond the linearization.

(E5)

Second-order remainder for
\Theta_{E}
.

Let
\Theta_{E}(\mu)\coloneqq\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}[\mu]
and
\mathcal{K}\coloneqq D\Theta_{E}[\bar{\mu}]
.
Assume that there exist
r_{\Theta}>0
and
C_{\Theta}>0
such that

\|\Theta_{E}(\bar{\mu}+\nu)-\Theta_{E}(\bar{\mu}+\eta)-\mathcal{K}(\nu-\eta)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}}\leq C_{\Theta}\left(\|\nu\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|\eta\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\right)\|\nu-\eta\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

for all

with

\|\nu\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|\eta\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq r_{\Theta}

and
\bar{\mu}+\nu,\bar{\mu}+\eta\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{E})
.

Lemma 4.17

(Entropy remainder on the chart)
.

There exist
\rho_{\mathrm{ent}}\in(0,1)
and

such that

and, for every

\xi,\eta\in B^{H^{s}}_{\rho_{\mathrm{ent}}}(0)
and every

,

\left|D(\widehat{\mathrm{Ent}})(\xi)[\psi]-D(\widehat{\mathrm{Ent}})(\eta)[\psi]-q_{0}(\xi-\eta,\psi)\right|\leq C(\|\xi\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}+\|\eta\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)})\|\xi-\eta\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

By the exact formula of
\widehat{\mathrm{Ent}}(\xi)
in the proof of Proposition
3.12
, one has, for
\xi\in\mathcal{U}
and
\psi\in H^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
,

D\widehat{\mathrm{Ent}}(\xi)[\psi]=-\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}(x)\,\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\left(I+\nabla^{2}\xi(x)\right)^{-1}\nabla^{2}\psi(x)\right)\,dx.

Whenever
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym}}
and
\|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
, define
\mathcal{M}(Z)\coloneqq\left(I+Z\right)^{-1}-I+Z

After choosing

sufficiently small, the Sobolev embedding

|\mathcal{M}(Z_{1})-\mathcal{M}(Z_{2})|\leq C\left(|Z_{1}|+|Z_{2}|\right)|Z_{1}-Z_{2}|

Hence

Using the bounds on
\bar{\mu}
, the estimate on

, the Sobolev embedding
H^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{2}(\Omega)
, the inequality

∎

Now we transfer the remainder estimate for
\Theta_{E}
to the pullback
\hat{\mathcal{E}}
.

Lemma 4.18

(Transport of the remainder for
\mathcal{E}
)
.

Under Assumption
(E5)
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

(4.4)

Proof.

\varepsilon>0

\rho_{0}>0
and

such that
B^{H^{s}}_{\rho_{0}}(0)\subset\mathcal{U}
and, for all

\displaystyle\|\Xi(\xi)-\Xi(\eta)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\|D\Xi(\zeta)\psi\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

and

to

\displaystyle\|D\Xi(\xi)\psi-D\Xi(\eta)\psi-D^{2}\Xi(0)[\xi-\eta,\psi]\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\Xi}\|\xi-\eta\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

on

Let

Using

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\langle D\Xi(\xi)\psi-D\Xi(\eta)\psi-D^{2}\Xi(0)[h,\psi],\Theta_{*}\right\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}(\Omega)},

The previous two lemmas show that, under Assumption

Proposition 4.19

(Local strong convexity)
.

Assume Corollary
4.7
and Assumption
(E5)
.
Let

and let
c_{\delta}>0
be the coercivity constant from Lemma
4.13
.
Then there exists
\rho_{*}>0
such that

\overline{B^{H^{s}}_{\rho_{*}}(0)}\subset\mathcal{U}

and, for all

\left(D\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\xi)-D\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\eta)\right)[\xi-\eta]\geq\frac{\lambda_{\Pi}}{2}\|\xi-\eta\|_{X}^{2}.

Consequently
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is
\lambda_{\Pi}/2
-strongly convex on
\overline{B^{H^{s}}_{\rho_{*}}(0)}
with respect to the
X
-metric, i.e., for all
\xi,\eta\in\overline{B^{H^{s}}_{\rho_{*}}(0)}

\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\eta)\geq\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\xi)+D\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\xi)[\eta-\xi]+\frac{\lambda_{\Pi}}{4}\|\eta-\xi\|_{X}^{2}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Set
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}}\coloneqq\frac{c_{\delta}}{4}
and let
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}>0
be given by Lemma
4.18
with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}}
.
Then, choose
\rho_{*}\leq\min\left\{\rho_{\mathcal{E}},\rho_{\mathrm{ent}},\frac{c_{\delta}}{8C_{\mathrm{ent}}}\right\}
where

is the constant in Lemma
4.17

Let
\xi,\eta\in\overline{B^{H^{s}}_{\rho_{*}}(0)}
and set

.
By Lemmas
4.18
and
4.17
, with

,

(D\hat{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)-D\hat{\mathcal{F}}(\eta))[h]\geq a(h,h)-(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}}+2C_{\mathrm{ent}}\rho_{*})\|h\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Since
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\xi)=\hat{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2}\langle\Pi P\xi,\xi\rangle_{X}
, we obtain

(D\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\xi)-D\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\eta))[h]\geq a_{\Pi}(h,h)-(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{E}}+2C_{\mathrm{ent}}\rho_{*})\|h\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Using the choice of

gives

Now let

is convex,

for every

, and

Applying the monotonicity estimate with

yields

\bigl(D\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\xi+tk)-D\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\xi)\bigr)[k]\geq\frac{\lambda_{\Pi}}{2}\,t\|k\|_{X}^{2}.

Integrating in

gives (

Corollary 4.20

(Proximal contraction)
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
4.19
, set

\mathcal{K}_{*}\coloneqq\overline{B^{H^{s}}_{\rho_{*}}(0)}.

For
\tau>0
and
\xi\in\mathcal{K}_{*}
, the proximal problem

S_{\tau}(\xi)\in\arg\min_{\eta\in\mathcal{K}_{*}}\Bigl\{\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\eta)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\eta-\xi\|_{X}^{2}\Bigr\}

has a unique minimizer and
S_{\tau}:\mathcal{K}_{*}\to\mathcal{K}_{*}
is a contraction with

\|S_{\tau}(\xi_{1})-S_{\tau}(\xi_{2})\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{1+\tau\lambda_{\Pi}/2}\,\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{X}\qquad\forall\,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{*}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.19
, the functional

\eta\mapsto\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\eta)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\eta-\xi\|_{X}^{2}

is continuous and
(\lambda_{\Pi}/2+1/\tau)
-strongly convex on the closed convex set
\mathcal{K}_{*}
, so it admits a unique minimizer there. By
[
8
, Example 23.3]
, the proximal map is the resolvent of the subdifferential,

S_{\tau}=\left(I+\tau\,\partial\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}\right)^{-1}

on
\mathcal{K}_{*}
. Since Proposition
4.19
shows that
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is
\lambda_{\Pi}/2
-strongly convex in the
X
-metric, its subdifferential is
\lambda_{\Pi}/2
-strongly monotone. Therefore, for
\eta_{i}=S_{\tau}(\xi_{i})
,

\left(1+\tau\lambda_{\Pi}/2\right)\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{X}^{2}\leq\left\langle\eta_{1}-\eta_{2},\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\right\rangle_{X},

and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\|S_{\tau}(\xi_{1})-S_{\tau}(\xi_{2})\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{1+\tau\lambda_{\Pi}/2}\,\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{X}.\qed

5
Extensions and applications

We now discuss three extensions of the framework: coupled multi-species systems, constrained Fokker-Planck dynamics, and closed Riemannian manifolds.
In each case, we show how the operator-theoretic construction of the Hessian and the finite-rank feedback design extend beyond the basic torus setting.
The complete numerical methodology, algorithmic implementation, and empirical results are deferred to Appendix
B
.

5.1
Coupled systems

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the product space
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)^{N}
, endowed with

\mathbf{W}_{2}\left((\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{N},(\nu_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\right)^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\operatorname{W}_{2}(\mu_{i},\nu_{i})^{2}.

For
V_{i},W_{ij}
given on
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, define

\mathcal{F}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{N})=\sigma\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\mu_{i}\log\mu_{i}\,dx+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}V_{i}\,d\mu_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}W_{ij}(x-y)\,d\mu_{i}(x)\,d\mu_{j}(y),

with
W_{ij}=W_{ji}
.
The componentwise first variation is

\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu_{i}}(\mu)=\sigma(\log\mu_{i}+1)+V_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{ij}*\mu_{j},

so the gradient-flow system reads

\partial_{t}\mu_{i}=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu_{i}\nabla\left(\sigma\log\mu_{i}+V_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{ij}\ast\mu_{j}\right)\right),\qquad i=1,\ldots,N.

Proposition 5.1

(Multi-species model properties)
.

Assume
V_{i}\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
and
W_{ij}\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
for all
i,j
.
Let
\bar{\mu}=(\bar{\mu}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\mu}_{N})
be a stationary point of
\mathcal{F}
with absolutely continuous components.
Define

X_{i}:=H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu}_{i}),\qquad X:=\prod_{i=1}^{N}X_{i},\qquad\|\phi\|_{X}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}_{i}\,|\nabla\phi_{i}|^{2}\,dx.

Then:

(i)

for each
i
, there exists
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\sigma(\log\bar{\mu}_{i}+1)+V_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{ij}\ast\bar{\mu}_{j}=\lambda_{i}\quad\text{a.e. on }\Omega,

hence

\bar{\mu}_{i}=Z_{i}^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(V_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{ij}\ast\bar{\mu}_{j}\right)\right),

with constants
0<c_{i}\leq\bar{\mu}_{i}\leq C_{i}
and
\bar{\mu}_{i}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}
at
\bar{\mu}
induces on
X
the bilinear form

\displaystyle H_{\mathcal{E}}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{2}V_{i}\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla\psi_{i}\,d\bar{\mu}_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\nabla^{2}W_{ij}(x-y)\,\left(\nabla\phi_{i}(x)-\nabla\phi_{j}(y)\right)\cdot\left(\nabla\psi_{i}(x)-\nabla\psi_{j}(y)\right)\,d\bar{\mu}_{i}(x)\,d\bar{\mu}_{j}(y),

(5.1)

and

|H_{\mathcal{E}}(\phi,\psi)|\leq C_{\mathrm{ms}}\|\phi\|_{X}\|\psi\|_{X},\qquad C_{\mathrm{ms}}\coloneqq M_{V}+2N^{2}M_{W},

where
M_{V}\coloneqq\max_{i}\|\nabla^{2}V_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
M_{W}\coloneqq\max_{i,j}\|\nabla^{2}W_{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

For the first-variation identity, fix
i
and perturb only the
i
-th component along
\mu_{i}^{\varepsilon}=(\mathrm{Id}+\varepsilon\nabla\eta_{i})_{\sharp}\bar{\mu}_{i}
, with
\eta_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Differentiating
\mathcal{F}
at
\varepsilon=0
gives

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}(\bar{\mu}_{1},\ldots,\mu_{i}^{\varepsilon},\ldots,\bar{\mu}_{N})\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left(\sigma(\log\bar{\mu}_{i}+1)+V_{i}+\sum_{j}W_{ij}\ast\bar{\mu}_{j}\right)\cdot\nabla\eta_{i}\,d\bar{\mu}_{i}.

Stationarity implies that this vanishes for every

Because

and
W_{ij}\in W^{2,\infty}

belongs to

, so the Gibbs formula follows and gives the bounds and
W^{1,\infty}
regularity exactly as in Lemma
2.3

For the Hessian, use a two-parameter perturbation
X_{i}^{s,t}(x)=x+s\nabla\phi_{i}(x)+t\nabla\psi_{i}(x)
and
\mu_{i}^{s,t}=(X_{i}^{s,t})_{\sharp}\bar{\mu}_{i}

Differentiating
\partial_{s}\partial_{t}
at
(0,0)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{2}V_{i}\,\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla\psi_{i}\,d\bar{\mu}_{i}.

For the interaction part, set
\Xi_{ij}^{s,t}(x,y)=X_{i}^{s,t}(x)-X_{j}^{s,t}(y)
.
Then

\partial_{s}\partial_{t}W_{ij}(\Xi_{ij}^{s,t})\Big|_{(0,0)}=\nabla^{2}W_{ij}(x-y)\left(\nabla\phi_{i}(x)-\nabla\phi_{j}(y)\right)\cdot\left(\nabla\psi_{i}(x)-\nabla\psi_{j}(y)\right),

which yields (
5.1
).

To bound (
5.1
), the external term satisfies

\left|\sum_{i}\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{2}V_{i}\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla\psi_{i}\,d\bar{\mu}_{i}\right|\leq M_{V}\sum_{i}\|\phi_{i}\|_{X_{i}}\|\psi_{i}\|_{X_{i}}\leq M_{V}\|\phi\|_{X}\|\psi\|_{X}.

For each pair
(i,j)
, Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{i}(x)-\nabla\phi_{j}(y)|\,|\nabla\psi_{i}(x)-\nabla\psi_{j}(y)|\,d\bar{\mu}_{i}(x)\,d\bar{\mu}_{j}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{i}(x)-\nabla\phi_{j}(y)|^{2}\,d\bar{\mu}_{i}\,d\bar{\mu}_{j}\right)^{1/2}\left(\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{i}(x)-\nabla\psi_{j}(y)|^{2}\,d\bar{\mu}_{i}\,d\bar{\mu}_{j}\right)^{1/2}.

Using
|a-b|^{2}\leq 2|a|^{2}+2|b|^{2}
, each square root is bounded by
2\|\phi\|_{X}
and
2\|\psi\|_{X}
, respectively.
Therefore

|H_{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{int}}(\phi,\psi)|\leq\frac{M_{W}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}4\|\phi\|_{X}\|\psi\|_{X}=2N^{2}M_{W}\|\phi\|_{X}\|\psi\|_{X}.

Adding both bounds gives the stated constant
C_{\mathrm{ms}}
.

5.2
Constrained dynamics

We now consider dynamics with fixed linear moments, so that the admissible set is

\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega):\ \int_{\Omega}g_{\ell}\,d\mu=c_{\ell},\ \ell=1,\ldots,k\right\},

with
g_{\ell}\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
.
The constrained evolution can be written as

\partial_{t}\mu=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu\nabla\left(\sigma\log\mu+\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu}\right)\right)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}m_{\ell}(t)\,\nabla\cdot(\mu\nabla g_{\ell}),

where
m_{\ell}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
are scalar Lagrange multipliers chosen so that all constraints stay constant in time.
Writing

C_{\ell}(\mu)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}g_{\ell}\,d\mu,\qquad\Phi(\mu)\coloneqq\sigma\log\mu+\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\mu},

and differentiating
C_{\ell}(\mu_{t})=c_{\ell}
, we obtain the linear system for
\ell=1,\ldots,k
,

\sum_{r=1}^{k}G_{\ell r}(\mu_{t})\,m_{r}(t)=-b_{\ell}(\mu_{t}),

with

G_{\ell r}(\mu)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\mu\,\nabla g_{\ell}\cdot\nabla g_{r}\,dx,\qquad b_{\ell}(\mu)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\mu\,\nabla g_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\Phi(\mu)\,dx.

Hence, whenever
G(\mu_{t})=(G_{\ell r}(\mu_{t}))
is invertible,
m(t)
is uniquely defined by

m(t)=-G(\mu_{t})^{-1}b(\mu_{t}).

This constrained-multiplier characterization is standard in constrained Fokker-Planck dynamics; see, e.g.,
[
33
]
.

A physically motivated instance is the many-particle insertion-battery model
with a time-dependent first-moment constraint
[
30
,
33
]
.
In our notation, this corresponds to
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
, one constraint function
g(x)=x
, and

\int_{\Omega}x\,\rho_{t}(x)\,dx=\ell(t),

where
\ell(t)
prescribes the loading protocol.
However, the analysis in Proposition
5.3
is formulated with a static constrained manifold, where the linearized operator at a stationary point is time-independent.
If
c_{\ell}=c_{\ell}(t)
is prescribed, the constraint set becomes time-dependent and the linearization around a reference path is non-autonomous.
In that case, the constant-gain feedback design based on an algebraic Riccati equation is no longer directly applicable, and one needs a time-varying design, such as differential Riccati equations.

Proposition 5.3

(Constrained equilibrium and projected linearization)
.

Let
\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{M}

restricted to
\mathcal{M}

Set

let

The linearized constrained dynamics at

Proof.

Constrained stationarity means that for every first-order variation

5.3
Manifold geometry: stabilization on closed Riemannian manifolds

The analysis developed in Sections
3
–
4
transfers to compact Riemannian manifolds without boundary.
If
\Omega
is a closed Riemannian manifold with metric
g
, the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\Omega)
, the compact embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\Omega)
, and the standard elliptic regularity theory are available as on the flat torus.
Accordingly, the Riesz map
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}=-\nabla_{g}\cdot(\bar{\mu}\nabla_{g}\cdot)
and the energy space
X=H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu})
are defined in the same way.

The main difference appears in the second variation of the entropy.
In the flat case, the Bochner identity on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
yields only the squared-Hessian term, whereas on a general manifold the Bochner formula also contains the Ricci curvature:

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{g}(|\nabla_{g}\phi|^{2})=|\nabla_{g}^{2}\phi|^{2}+\langle\nabla_{g}\phi,\nabla_{g}\Delta_{g}\phi\rangle_{g}+\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(\nabla_{g}\phi,\nabla_{g}\phi).

For the free energy
\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\sigma\,\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)+\int_{\Omega}V\,d\mu
, this gives the Hessian form

a_{g}(\varphi,\psi)=\int_{\Omega}\left[\sigma\nabla_{g}^{2}\varphi:\nabla_{g}^{2}\psi+\left(\sigma\mathrm{Ric}_{g}+\nabla_{g}^{2}V\right)(\nabla_{g}\varphi,\nabla_{g}\psi)\right]d\bar{\mu}.

The tensor
\mathrm{Ric}_{V,\sigma}\coloneqq\mathrm{Ric}_{g}+\sigma^{-1}\nabla_{g}^{2}V
is the Bakry–Émery curvature tensor.
Thus the manifold case differs from the flat one by a bounded lower-order perturbation, and the same operator-theoretic argument yields a self-adjoint Hessian operator with compact resolvent.
Consequently, the finite-rank Riccati feedback design again gives local exponential stabilization on closed manifolds.

To demonstrate this explicitly on a curved space where flat properties fail, we consider the specific case of
\Omega=\mathbb{S}^{2}
(the unit sphere with the round metric
g
) and the non-convex potential

V(\theta)=-\alpha\cos^{2}\theta,\qquad\alpha>0,

where
\theta\in[0,\pi]
is the polar angle.
The drift-diffusion equation takes the form

\partial_{t}\mu=\sigma\Delta_{g}\mu+\nabla_{g}\cdot(\mu\nabla_{g}V),

(5.2)

and the stationary density is

\bar{\mu}=Z^{-1}e^{-V/\sigma}=Z^{-1}\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sigma}\cos^{2}\theta\right).

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
e_{\theta}=\partial_{\theta}
and
e_{\varphi}=(\sin\theta)^{-1}\partial_{\varphi}
be the orthonormal frame.
Then:

(i)

\nabla_{g}V=\alpha\sin(2\theta)\,e_{\theta}

(ii)

the effective curvature tensor (Bakry-Émery tensor)

\mathrm{Ric}_{V,\sigma}\coloneqq\mathrm{Ric}_{g}+\sigma^{-1}\nabla_{g}^{2}V

Proof.

For an axisymmetric function

\mathbb{S}^{2}

\nabla_{g}f=f^{\prime}(\theta)e_{\theta},\qquad\nabla_{g}^{2}f(e_{\theta},e_{\theta})=f^{\prime\prime}(\theta),\qquad\nabla_{g}^{2}f(e_{\varphi},e_{\varphi})=f^{\prime}(\theta)\cot\theta.

With

At

6
Conclusion

We have developed an operator-theoretic framework for feedback stabilization of Wasserstein gradient flows around stationary measures.
A main analytical step is the identification of the Wasserstein Hessian of the free energy
\mathcal{F}
as a densely defined, self-adjoint operator
A
with compact resolvent on
X=H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu})
, established via symmetric bilinear form methods and the
H^{2}
-coercivity of the entropy Hessian through Bochner’s identity (Theorems
3.3
and
3.7
).
The resulting discrete spectrum allows one to construct the finite-rank feedback operator
\Pi
via an algebraic operator Riccati equation on the finite-dimensional unstable spectral subspace (Section
4
).
The main stability theorem (Theorem
4.8
) gives local exponential stabilization of the nonlinear closed-loop Fokker-Planck equation: the feedback lifts the spectral gap for any prescribed
\delta>0
, and the nonlinear remainder is controlled in a small
H^{s}
-ball through a
C^{1}
-neighborhood argument in the transport chart.
The same construction extends to multi-species Fokker-Planck systems, moment-constrained evolution equations, and Fokker-Planck equations on closed Riemannian manifolds, where the Bakry-Émery tensor enters the Hessian form as a bounded perturbation.

Several interesting problems remain open.
First, the local convexity result of Section
4.3
is chart-level: promoting
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}
to a local displacement-convex functional on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
would require a uniform Wasserstein-Hessian lower bound away from
\bar{\mu}
.
Second, assumptions
(E3)
and
(E5)
are verified here for the McKean-Vlasov class but are imposed as abstract hypotheses for the general framework:
(E3)
requires local well-posedness of the closed-loop equation and a quadratic-Lipschitz estimate on the nonlinear remainder in
H^{-1}
, while
(E5)
demands a second-order remainder bound on the first variation in
H^{1}/\mathbb{R}
.
It would be desirable to derive these directly from structural properties of the energy
\mathcal{E}
, rather than positing them separately, and to identify the minimal regularity of
\mathcal{E}
under which both hold.
Third, optimal control of McKean-Vlasov and Fokker-Planck equations is an active field, but its relation to energy convexification remains largely unexplored: a precise question is whether an optimally designed input, such as minimising a quadratic cost on the state and control, automatically convexifies the free energy
\mathcal{F}
along the optimal trajectory, or whether cost minimisation and energy convexification are competing objectives.
Fourth, the analysis is carried out on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
; extending to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
requires replacing the standard Poincaré inequality and compact Sobolev embeddings with their weighted counterparts relative to
\bar{\mu}
.

\Omega
is discretized with
N_{\text{fine}}
points.
We first compute the equilibrium
\bar{\mu}
using self-consistent iterations on this discretization.
For cases where the stationary point is not unique, we fix a known
\bar{\mu}
(e.g. the uniform distribution), although one can also compute
\bar{\mu}
using techniques such as the deflation method introduced in
[
10
]
for the McKean-Vlasov case.

We consider the periodic domain
\Omega=[0,b]
(identified with the torus
\mathbb{T}^{1}
).
The physical potential
V
, originally defined on
[-b/2,b/2]
, is shifted to
\Omega
via
x\mapsto x+b/2
for implementation.
Functions are expanded in the Fourier basis
\phi_{k}(x)=b^{-1/2}e^{2\pi ikx/b}
.
The stiffness matrix
D
(differentiation) and multiplications by potentials are handled via FFT, ensuring
\mathcal{O}(N\log N)
complexity for computing derivatives.

The linearized operator
A
is discretized by a spectral Galerkin method in the same Fourier basis.
The associated generalized eigenvalue problem is assembled using FFTs of the coefficients and solved for its first
N
eigenpairs
(\lambda_{k},\phi_{k})
in
X=H^{1}_{\mathrm{zm}}(\bar{\mu})
.
The eigenfunctions are orthonormalized in the
X
-inner product
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{X}=\int_{\Omega}\bar{\mu}\,\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx
.

We then project the dynamics onto the finite-dimensional subspace

H_{N}=\text{span}\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N}\}\subset X.

Let
P_{N}:X\to H_{N}
denote the
X
-orthogonal projection.
The density perturbation and its potential are approximated by

\nu_{N}(x,t)=\mu_{N}(x,t)-\bar{\mu}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}(t)\,\psi_{k}(x),\qquad\xi_{N}(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}(t)\,\phi_{k}(x),

(B.1)

where
\psi_{k}=\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}\phi_{k}
are the corresponding eigenfunctions of
L
, which acts at the density level, and
c_{k}(t)=\langle\xi_{N}(t),\phi_{k}\rangle_{X}=\langle\nu_{N}(t),\psi_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{T}}
are the spectral coefficients.

B.1.2
Feedback control design

The feedback law is synthesized on the unstable subspace of
A
.
After selecting the modes with
\lambda_{k}<\delta
, we solve the finite-dimensional Riccati equation on their span and obtain the gain matrix
\Pi_{N}
.
The online control is then

u(t)=B^{*}\Pi_{N}\xi_{N}(t),

with the canonical choice of control profiles given by the corresponding unstable potential modes.

B.1.3
Time integration

The Galerkin projection yields a system of ODEs for the coefficients
c(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

\dot{c}(t)=-(A_{N}+\Pi_{N})c(t)+\mathcal{N}_{N}(c(t)),

where
\mathcal{N}_{N}(c)=\bigl(\langle\mathcal{N}(\sum_{j}c_{j}\phi_{j}),\,\phi_{k}\rangle_{X}\bigr)_{k=1}^{N}
is the
X
-Galerkin projection of the nonlinear remainder from Section
4.3
.
Because the eigenvalues of
A+\Pi
are spread over a wide range, the discrete linear part
-(A_{N}+\Pi_{N})
is stiff, especially for large
N
.
We therefore use an Exponential Time Differencing (ETD1) scheme, which treats the linear part through the matrix exponential
e^{-(A_{N}+\Pi_{N})\Delta t}
and the nonlinear remainder explicitly.
Throughout the experiments, we monitor convergence using the coefficient norm
\|c(t)\|\coloneqq(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|c_{k}(t)|^{2})^{1/2}
.
By the
X
-orthonormality of
\{\phi_{k}\}
, this equals
\|\xi_{N}(t)\|_{X}
and hence, via the isometry
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{\mu}}
, equals
\|\nu_{N}(t)\|_{\mathcal{T}}
.
By Lemma
3.9
, this is equivalent to
\|\nu_{N}(t)\|_{H^{-1}}
.

B.2
Stabilization of standard models

We consider the periodic double-well potential

V(x)=-2\cos(4\pi x),\quad x\in[0,1]_{\rm per}.

The operator
A
has a spectral gap
\lambda_{1}\approx 0.07
, corresponding to the slow metastable tunneling timescale.
Figure
5
(left) confirms that the eigenvalues of the discrete closed-loop operator
A_{N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
satisfy

\lambda_{k}(A_{N}^{\mathrm{cl}})\approx\begin{cases}\lambda_{\Pi}=\delta+\sqrt{(\lambda_{1}-\delta)^{2}+m_{1}}&k=1\\
\lambda_{k}(A)&k>1,\end{cases}

validating Corollary
4.7
in the discrete setting.
The dynamic simulation (Figure
5
, right) shows that the settling time

t_{99\%}\coloneqq\inf\left\{t>0:\|\xi_{N}(t)\|_{X}\leq 0.01\|\xi_{N}(0)\|_{X}\right\}

decreases as
\delta
increases.
The control cost
\mathcal{J}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u(t)|^{2}\,dt
(Figure
5
, center) increases monotonically with
\delta
.
Although faster convergence reduces the time horizon, the squared amplitude of the control
|u|^{2}\propto\delta^{2}
dominates, leading to a higher total energy cost for aggressive stabilization.

B.3
Nonlinear nonlocal energy

To go beyond the standard McKean-Vlasov setting, we consider the nonlinear nonlocal energy

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\int_{\Omega}V(x)\,d\mu(x)+\int_{\Omega}\cos(K\ast\mu)(x)\,dx,\quad K(x)=5\cos(4\pi x),

with
V(x)=-2\cos(4\pi x)
and diffusion
\sigma=0.5
.
The first variation
\Phi[\mu]=V-K\ast\sin(K\ast\mu)
depends nonlinearly on
\mu
, so this energy cannot be reduced to a quadratic interaction
\frac{1}{2}\iint W\,d\mu\otimes d\mu
.
Figure
6
shows the norm decay at multiple initial perturbation amplitudes.
In the uncontrolled dynamics, large perturbations exhibit a nonlinear transient where standard exponential decay breaks down.
In these runs, the feedback controller designed from the linearized Hessian drives all tested trajectories back to equilibrium, including perturbations outside the small-amplitude regime.

B.4
Empirical local attraction region

Theorem
4.8
is a local result for the variable
\xi
, so we complement the previous tests with an empirical scan of initial conditions in a two-mode slice.
To preserve positivity of the initial density across the scan, we parameterize the initial potential perturbation using the first two unstable eigenfunctions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in X
:

\xi_{0}(r,\theta)=r\bigl(\cos\theta\,\phi_{1}+\sin\theta\,\phi_{2}\bigr),

and define

\mu_{0}(r,\theta)\propto\bar{\mu}\,e^{\xi_{0}(r,\theta)}.

The corresponding perturbation is then projected onto the Galerkin coefficient basis.
For each pair
(r,\theta)
, we simulate the full nonlinear closed-loop dynamics and estimate an observed decay rate
\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}
by linear regression of
\log\|c(t)\|_{X}
over the final portion of the trajectory.
We classify the trajectory as successful if

\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq\beta\,\lambda_{\Pi}

with fixed
\beta\in(0,1)
.

Figure
7
reports both the success/failure map in the selected 2D slice and the success fraction as a function of the radius
r
.
For this experiment, all sampled initial conditions satisfy the success criterion, so the local estimate appears conservative.
We stress that this is an empirical attraction map, not a rigorous characterization of the full basin.

B.5
