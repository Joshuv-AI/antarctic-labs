Partial regularity for minimizing constraint maps for the Alt-Phillips energy
Rada Ziganshina

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish an ε \varepsilon -egularity theorem for minimizers of an Alt-Phillips type functional subject to constraint maps. We prove that under sufficiently small energy, the minimizers exhibit regularity, and hence proving the smoothness of these maps. From here, we bootstrap to optimal regularity.

2.
Preliminaries and basic tools

Throughout this paper, we shall assume that
M
is a
C^{\infty}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, with compact and connected complement. Here we assume the smoothness of
\partial M
only for the sake of simplicity, even if most of our analysis works well for
\partial M
of class
C^{3}
, or even
C^{2}
.

Given a boundary datum
g\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
, the existence of minimizing constraint maps
u\in W_{g}^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
follows from the direct method of calculus of variations.

Of course, it is not a trivial matter to have
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})\neq\emptyset
for
n=2
and it depends on the topology of
M
with regard to
\Omega
.
However,
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})\neq\emptyset
whenever
n\geq 3
; we refer interested readers to
[
5
, Remark 2.9]

for the topology of the Sobolev class
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
.

Throughout this paper, we shall consider minimizing constraint maps in
W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\overline{M})
, which is not a restrictive condition as shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
g\in W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\overline{M})
be given, and let
u\in W_{g}^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
be a minimizing constraint map for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
. Then
u(\Omega)\subset K
up to a null set, where
K
is the closed convex hull of
g(\Omega)\cup M^{c}
. In particular, as
M^{c}
is compact,
|u|\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a closed half-space in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
such that
g(\Omega)\cup M^{c}\subset H
. Let
\pi_{H}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to H
be the orthogonal projection map. Then for any
v\in W_{g}^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
, we have
\pi_{H}\circ v\in W_{g}^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
, as well as that
|D(\pi_{H}\circ v)|\leq|Dv|
and
\rho\circ\pi_{H}\circ v\leq\rho\circ v
a.e. in
\Omega
; as a result,

{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[\pi_{H}\circ v]\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[v],\quad\forall v\in W_{g}^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M}).

Thus, the minimality of
u\in W_{g}^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
shows that

0={\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[u]-{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[\pi_{H}\circ u]=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}(|Du|^{2}-|D(\pi_{H}\circ u)|^{2})+{\lambda}((\rho\circ u)^{\gamma}-(\rho\circ\pi_{H}\circ u)^{\gamma})\,dx.

Since both parentheses in the integrand are nonnegative a.e. in
\Omega
, we obtain
|D(\pi_{H}\circ u)|=|Du|
a.e. in
\Omega
. In particular, denoting by
\nu_{H}
the outward unit normal to
\partial H
, it follows from the last identity that

|D\operatorname{dist}(u,H)|^{2}=|\nu_{H}Du|^{2}=|Du|^{2}-|D(\pi_{H}\circ u)|^{2}=0\quad\text{a.e.\ in }\Omega.

However, as
u\in W_{g}^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
and
H
is a closed half-space containing
g(\Omega)
, we have
\operatorname{dist}(g,H)=0
a.e. in
\Omega
, whence

\operatorname{dist}(u,H)=0\quad\text{a.e.\ on }\partial\Omega,

in the trace sense. Combining this with the displayed equation above, we deduce that
\operatorname{dist}(u,H)=0
a.e. in
\Omega
, i.e.,
u(\Omega)\subset H
up to a null set. As
H
was an arbitrary closed half-space containing
g(\Omega)
, the assertion of this lemma follows.
∎

Since energy minimizing maps are also stationary with respect to inner variations, we obtain a monotonicity formula for Alt-Phillips constraint maps. Note that we obtain an almost monotonicity formula, rather than an exact one, due to the presence of the extra term
\lambda(\rho\circ u)^{\gamma}
in the energy functional
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
. This is essentially the same as Alt-Caffarelli constraint maps
[
4
]
:

Lemma 2.2

(Almost monotonicity formula)
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\overline{M})
be a minimizing constraint map for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
. Then

s^{2-n}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,dx-r^{2-n}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,dx\geq 2\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}R^{2-n}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial N}\right|^{2}dx-\omega_{n}{\lambda}d^{\gamma}s^{2},

whenever
0<r\leq s<\operatorname{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)
, where
R:=|x-x_{0}|
,
\partial/\partial N
denotes the directional derivative in the radial direction
R^{-1}(x-x_{0})
, and
d=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{\Omega}(\rho\circ u)
. Moreover, if
\lambda=0
, and
r^{2-n}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,dx=s^{2-n}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,dx
, then
u
is
0

x_{0}
in

(x-x_{0})\cdot Du=0

.

Proof.

One may follow almost verbatim the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
. The only difference here is that at the final stage, we estimate
\lambda(\rho\circ u)^{\gamma}\leq\lambda d^{\gamma}
, which holds by assumption a.e. in
\Omega
. We omit the details.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

We shall need (
2.2
) later in Section
3
when studying the partial regularity of minimizing constraint maps for the Alt-Phillips energy. Note that (
2.2
) is a
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate for
\alpha<1
. Although we have the optimal
C^{1,1}
-estimate (
2.1
), it is not clear to us whether we can also establish (
2.2
) for
\alpha=1
.

As mentioned at the beginning, we assume that
\partial M
is of class
C^{\infty}
.
Throughout this paper, we shall write by
\rho
the signed-distance map to
\partial M
, which takes positive values in
M
. As
\partial M
is smooth, there is a tubular neighborhood of
\partial M
of width
d_{0}>0
, denoted by
B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)
, in which
\rho\in C^{\infty}(B_{d_{0}}(\partial M))
. In this neighborhood, we can also consider the nearest point projection
\Pi
onto
\partial M
. Note that
\Pi\in C^{\infty}({B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)};\partial M)
.

Let us first compute the Euler-Lagrange equation for the distance map
\rho\circ u
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
be a minimizing constraint map for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
. Suppose that
u\in C^{1}(U;{B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)})
in a subdomain
U\subset\Omega
. Then
\rho\circ u\in C^{1}(U)
and

(2.10)

\begin{cases}\Delta(\rho\circ u)=\operatorname{Hess}\rho_{u}(Du,Du)+\lambda\gamma(\rho\circ u)^{\gamma-1}&\text{in }U\cap u^{-1}(M),\\
\rho\circ u=|D(\rho\circ u)|=0&\text{on }U\setminus u^{-1}(M),\end{cases}

holds in the weak sense.

Proof.

With
u\in C^{1}(U;{B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)})
and
\rho\in C^{\infty}({B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)})\subset C^{1}({B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)})
, we have
\rho\circ u\in C^{1}(U)
.
Since
\rho\circ u
takes local minimum on
U\cap\partial u^{-1}(M)
, it follows that
|D(\rho\circ u)|=0
on
U\cap\partial u^{-1}(M)
. This proves the second line of (
2.10
).

Since we assume that
u\in C^{1}(U;B_{d_{0}}(\partial M))\subset C(U;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
, the set
U\cap u^{-1}(M)
is open. Moreover, since
u(U)\subset B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)
and
\partial M
is smooth, we have at least
\nu\circ u\in C^{1}(U)
. Therefore, given any (scalar) function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U\cap u^{-1}(M))
, we find
\delta>0
such that
\delta\leq\rho\circ u\leq d_{0}-\delta
on
\operatorname{supp}\varphi
.

Let
L>0
be such that
|\varphi|\leq L
on
\operatorname{supp}\varphi
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
small such that
2L\varepsilon\leq\delta
, we have

0<\frac{1}{2}\delta\leq\delta-\varepsilon L\leq\rho\circ(u+\varepsilon\varphi\nu\circ u)\leq d_{0}-\delta+\varepsilon L<d_{0},\qquad\hbox{in }\operatorname{supp}\varphi.

Moreover, we also have
\rho\circ(u+\varepsilon\varphi\nu\circ u)=\rho\circ u\geq 0
in
\Omega\setminus\operatorname{supp}\varphi
.

This shows that
u+\varepsilon\varphi\nu\circ u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
and that
u+\varepsilon\varphi\nu\circ u\in C^{1}(U;B_{d_{0}}(\partial M))
. Note further that since
\rho^{\gamma}\in C^{2}(M\cap B_{d_{0}}(\partial M))
with
\nabla\rho^{\gamma}=\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}\nu
in
\{\rho>0\}
,
\rho\circ u>0
in
u^{-1}(M)
, and
\operatorname{supp}\varphi\Subset U\cap u^{-1}(M)
, we obtain (using Taylor expansion)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\gamma}\circ(u+\varepsilon\varphi\nu\circ u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\gamma}\circ u\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{U\cap u^{-1}(M)}\left(\gamma(\rho^{\gamma-1}\nu)\circ u\right)\cdot\left(\varphi\nu\circ u\right)\,dx+o(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\gamma}\circ u\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{U\cap u^{-1}(M)}\gamma\varphi\rho^{\gamma-1}\circ u\,dx+o(\varepsilon),

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Also, by the chain rule, we have
D_{\alpha}(\rho\circ u)=\nu^{i}\circ uD_{\alpha}u^{i}
and
D_{\alpha}(\nu^{i}\circ u)D_{\alpha}u^{i}=\operatorname{Hess}\rho_{u}(D_{\alpha}u,D_{\alpha}u)
a.e. in
U
, which shows that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|D(u+\varepsilon\varphi\nu\circ u)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+2\varepsilon\int_{U\cap u^{-1}(M)}D(\rho\circ u)\cdot D\varphi+\varphi\operatorname{Hess}\rho_{u}(Du,Du)\,dx+o(\varepsilon).

Recalling that
u+\varepsilon\varphi\nu\circ u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
and that
\operatorname{supp}\varphi\Subset U\cap u^{-1}(M)\subset\Omega
, the minimality of
u
for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
now yields, along with (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), that

(2.13)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[u]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[u+\varepsilon\varphi\nu\circ u]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[u]+2\varepsilon\int_{U\cap u^{-1}(M)}D(\rho\circ u)\cdot D\varphi+\varphi\operatorname{Hess}\rho_{u}(Du,Du)+\gamma\varphi\rho^{\gamma-1}\circ u\,dx+o(\varepsilon).

Hence, subtracting
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[u]
from the left hand side and the rightmost side of (
2.13
) and dividing the resulting inequality by
\varepsilon
and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields

(2.14)

\int_{U\cap u^{-1}(M)}D(\rho\circ u)\cdot D\varphi+\varphi\operatorname{Hess}\rho_{u}(Du,Du)+\gamma\varphi\rho^{\gamma-1}\circ u\,dx\geq 0.

Finally, replacing
\varphi
with
-\varphi
turns the inequality in (
2.14
) into an equality. As
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U\cap u^{-1}(M))
was arbitrary, we arrive at the first line of (
2.10
).

∎

We can also compute the Euler-Lagrange system for the projection map
\Pi\circ u
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\overline{M})
be a minimizing constraint map for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
. If
u\in{B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)}
a.e. in a ball
B\subset\Omega
, then
\Pi\circ u\in W^{1,2}(B;\partial M)
and

(2.15)

\Delta(\Pi\circ u)=-2D(\rho\circ u)\cdot D(\nu\circ\Pi\circ u)+\operatorname{Hess}\Pi_{u}((Du)^{\top},(Du)^{\top})\quad\text{in }B,

in the weak sense, where
(Du)^{T}
is the tangential component of
D_{\alpha}u(x)
with respect to the tangent hyperplane of
\partial M
.

Proof.

Fix
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
, and consider the flow
\Phi\in C^{1}((-\eta,\eta)\times{B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)};{B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)})
generated by
\nabla\Pi^{i}
. Since
\nu\cdot\nabla\Pi^{i}=0
in
{B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)}
, we have
\rho\circ\Phi(t,y)=\rho(y)
for all
(t,y)\in(-\eta,\eta)\times{B_{d_{0}}(\partial M)}
.

Let

\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B)
be given, and take
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that
\varepsilon|\varphi|<\eta
in
B
. Then we take the variation of the form
u_{\varepsilon}:=\Phi(\varepsilon\varphi,u)\in W^{1,2}(B;\overline{M})
. Note
u_{\varepsilon}-u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(B;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
. By the choice of the flow
\Phi
, we observe that
\rho\circ u_{\varepsilon}=\rho\circ u
a.e. in
B
. Thus,
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[u]\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}[\Phi(\varepsilon\eta,u)]
implies
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}[u]\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{0}[\Phi(\varepsilon\eta,u)]
, and thus we can proceed as in
[
4
, Corollary 3.6]
to deduce (
2.15
) (for each component
\Pi^{i}\circ u
,
i=1,2,\cdots,m
).
∎

3.
Partial regularity

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Once this theorem is established, the (optimal) partial regularity theory, Corollary
1.2
, follows from Theorem
2.3
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
follows, partly, the classical approach of Giaquinta and Giusti
[
6
]
. The primary distinction in our setting is the choice of comparison maps: rather than harmonic functions, we employ
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
-minimizing constraint maps. This substitution ensures that the competitors remain compatible with the prescribed constraints. To this end, we establish the following maximum principle for
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
-minimizing constraint maps.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small, depending only on
n
,
m
, and
\partial M
, such that for any minimizing constraint map
v\in W^{1,2}(B_{1};\overline{M})
for
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
, if
\int_{B_{1}}|Dv|^{2}\,dx\leq\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and
\operatornamewithlimits{osc}_{\partial B_{1}}(v-\ell)\leq\mu\sqrt{\varepsilon}
for some
\mu>1
and some affine map
\ell:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
satisfying
|D\ell|\leq\mu\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, then

(3.1)

\operatornamewithlimits{osc}_{B_{1}}(v-\ell)\leq 2\mu\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Suppose by way of contradiction that the conclusion is not true. Then there exists a sequence
\{v_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset W^{1,2}(B_{1};\overline{M})
of minimizing constraint maps for
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
such that

(3.2)

\int_{B_{1}}|Dv_{k}|^{2}\,dx\leq\varepsilon_{k},\quad\text{and}\quad\operatornamewithlimits{osc}_{\partial B_{1}}(v_{k}-\ell_{k})\leq\mu_{k}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}},

for some constants
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
,
\mu_{k}>1
and some affine map
\ell_{k}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with
|D\ell_{k}|\leq\mu_{k}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}
, yet

(3.3)

\operatornamewithlimits{osc}_{B_{1}}(v_{k}-\ell_{k})\geq 2\mu_{k}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}},

for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Rescale the target and set

w_{k}:=\frac{v_{k}-\ell_{k}-y_{k}}{\mu_{k}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}},

where
y_{k}
is chosen as the integral average of
v_{k}-\ell_{k}
over
B_{1}
. By the Poincaré inequality and the fractional trace inequality
[
1
, Chapter 10, Theorem 18.1, Remark 18.2]
, we have

\|w_{k}\|_{W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}\leq c\|w_{k}\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{1})}^{2}\leq\frac{c}{\mu_{k}^{2}\varepsilon_{k}}\int_{B_{1}}|Dv_{k}|^{2}+|D\ell_{k}|^{2}\,dx<c,

where the last inequality follows from
\mu_{k}>1
and (
3.2
), and
c>1
here is a generic constant that depends at most on
n
. Hence, it follows
w_{k}\rightharpoonup w
in
W^{1,2}(B_{1};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})\cap W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\partial B_{1};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
for some
w\in W^{1,2}(B_{1};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
along a subsequence that we do not attempt to relabel. Extracting a further subsequence if necessary, we also obtain that
w_{k}\to w
a.e. in
B_{1}
and (
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
-)a.e. on
\partial B_{1}
. Thus, it follows from (
3.3
) that

(3.4)

\operatornamewithlimits{osc}_{B_{1}}w\geq 2>1\geq\operatornamewithlimits{osc}_{\partial B_{1}}w.

However, since
\Delta v_{k}=A_{v_{k}}(Dv_{k},Dv_{k})\chi_{v_{k}^{-1}(\partial M)}
in
B_{1}
in the weak sense, denoting by
\kappa
the uniform curvature bound for
\partial M
, we observe that for all
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1};{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
,

\bigg|\int_{B_{1}}Dw_{k}:D\varphi\,dx\bigg|\leq\frac{\kappa}{\mu_{k}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}}\int_{B_{1}}|\varphi||Dv_{k}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{\kappa\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}}{\mu_{k}}\sup_{B_{1}}|\varphi|\to 0,

where the second inequality was deduced from (
3.2
) and
\mu_{k}>1
. Passing to the limit in the above inequalities, we observe that

\int_{B_{1}}Dw:D\varphi\,dx=0,\quad\forall\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1};{\mathbb{R}}^{m}),

which implies that
\Delta w=0
in
B_{1}
. Therefore, applying the maximum principle to each component of
w
, we arrive at a contradiction in (
3.4
).
∎

We shall first prove an interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate which is optimal for
\gamma\in(0,1)
, and suboptimal for
\gamma\in[1,2)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(B_{4};\overline{M})
be a minimizing constraint map for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
with
\|\rho\circ u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{4})}\leq d
. Given
\sigma\in(0,1)
, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
c>1
, both depending only on
n
,
m
,
\partial M
,
\lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\sigma
, such that if
4^{2-n}\int_{B_{4}}|Du|^{2}\,dx\leq\varepsilon
and
{\lambda}d^{\gamma}\leq\varepsilon
, then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1})
with
\alpha=\min\{\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma},\sigma\}
and

\|Du\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}\leq c.

Proof.

Fix
{\varepsilon}
as a small constant, to be determined later, at most by the parameters
n
,
m
,
\partial M
,
\lambda
,
\gamma
and
\sigma
. Also fix any
\sigma\in(0,1)
, and set
\alpha=\min\{\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma},\sigma\}
as in the statement. We shall write by
c>1
a generic constant that may vary at each occurrence.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(B_{4};\overline{M})
be a minimizing constraint map for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
, and suppose the assumptions in the statement of the lemma is fulfilled, i.e.,

(3.5)

4^{2-n}\int_{B_{4}}|Du|^{2}\,dx\leq{\varepsilon},\quad\|\rho\circ u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{4})}\leq d,\quad\text{and}\quad{\lambda}d^{\gamma}\leq{\varepsilon}.

Fix
{R}\in(0,1)
as well as
x_{0}\in B_{2}
, and let
v\in W_{u}^{1,2}(B_{R}(x_{0});\overline{M})
be a minimizing constraint map for
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
. Then by the energy-minimality of
v
, the inequalities (
3.5
) and almost monotonicity (Lemma
2.2
) we have

(3.6)

R^{2-n}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dv|^{2}\,dx\leq R^{2-n}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,dx\leq 3^{2-n}\int_{B_{3}}|Du|^{2}\,dx+c{\lambda}d^{\gamma}\leq c{\varepsilon}.

Thus, with
{\varepsilon}
small, depending only on
n
,
m
, and
\partial M
, it follows from Theorem
2.5
, especially (
2.2
) with
\bar{\alpha}:=\min\{\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma},\frac{1}{2}(1+\sigma)\}
which verifies
\alpha<\bar{\alpha}<1
, that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|Dv-(Dv)_{x_{0},r}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\frac{r}{R/2}\bigg)^{n+2\bar{\alpha}}\int_{B_{R/2}(x_{0})}|Dv-(Dv)_{x_{0},{R}}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+cr^{n+2\bar{\alpha}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dv|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\frac{r}{R}\bigg)^{n+2\bar{\alpha}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dv-(Dv)_{x_{0},{R}}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+cr^{n+2\bar{\alpha}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dv|^{2}\,dx,

whenever
0<r\leq R
. Now utilizing the
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
-minimality of
u
, as well as the fact that
v
is a weak solution to
\Delta v=A_{v}(Dv,Dv)\chi_{v^{-1}(\partial M)}
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
, we see that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D(u-v)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}-|Dv|^{2}+2(u-v)\cdot A_{v}(Dv,Dv)\chi_{v^{-1}(\partial M)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(\rho\circ v)^{\gamma}-(\rho\circ u)^{\gamma}\,dx+c\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-v||Dv|^{2}\,dx.

Since we assume that
\rho\circ u\leq d
a.e. in
B_{4}
(hence in
B_{R}(x_{0})
), it follows from the subharmonicity of
\rho\circ v
(see
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
) and

on
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
that
\rho\circ v\leq d
a.e. in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Since
\rho

\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{B_{d}(\partial M)}|\nabla\rho|\leq 1,

so we deduce from the Hölder, Sobolev and Young inequality, as in
[
7

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(\rho\circ v)^{\gamma}-(\rho\circ u)^{\gamma}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-v|^{\min\{\gamma,1\}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg[\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-v|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dx\bigg]^{\frac{\gamma(n-2)}{2n}}{R}^{n-\frac{\gamma(n-2)}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg[\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D(u-v)|^{2}\bigg]^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}{R}^{n-\frac{\gamma(n-2)}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D(u-v)|^{2}\,dx+c{R}^{n+2\min\{\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma},1\}}.

To estimate the last term in the second line of (
3.8

\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\delta>0
be some constants to be determined later by
\sigma
alone. Choose
{\varepsilon}
to be smaller, if necessary, for now depending only on
n
,
m
,
\partial M
,
\lambda
,
\gamma
,
\alpha_{0}
and
\delta
(the dependence on
d
is absorbed by that of
\lambda
and
\gamma
due to (
3.5
)), such that by
[
9
, Proposition 1]
, the small energy assumption in (
3.5

|Du|\in L^{2,n-2\delta}(B_{1}(x_{0}))
, and
u\in C^{0,\alpha_{0}}(B_{1}(x_{0}))
, with the estimate

(3.10)

\|Du\|_{L^{2,n-2\delta}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}^{2}+[u]_{C^{0,\alpha_{0}}(B_{1})}^{2}\leq c\int_{B_{4}}|Du|^{2}\,dx.

Recalling the energy minimality of
v
in
W_{u}^{1,2}(B_{R}(x_{0});\overline{M})
, we obtain from (
3.10
) that

(3.11)

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dv|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,dx\leq cR^{n-2\delta}\int_{B_{4}}|Du|^{2}\,dx.

Moreover, by (
3.6
) (as well as the fact that
v=u
on
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
), we can also employ Lemma
3.1
(here we may also need to take
{\varepsilon}
smaller if needed) to deduce that

(3.12)

\operatornamewithlimits{osc}_{B_{R}(x_{0})}v\leq 2\bigg[\operatornamewithlimits{osc}_{\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}u\bigg]\leq 2\bigg[\operatornamewithlimits{osc}_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u\bigg]\leq 2cR^{\alpha_{0}}.

Utilizing (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we estimate the rightmost term in (
3.8
) as

(3.13)

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-v||Dv|^{2}\,dx\leq\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-v|\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dv|^{2}\,dx\leq cR^{n-2\delta+\alpha_{0}}\int_{B_{4}}|Du|^{2}\,dx.

Now let
\sigma\in(0,1)
be given. At this point, we can select
(\alpha_{0},\delta)
accordingly such that
\frac{1}{2}(1+\sigma)=\alpha_{0}-2\delta
(e.g., by choosing
\delta=\frac{1}{8}(1-\sigma)
and
\alpha_{0}=\frac{1}{4}(3+\sigma)
). Then by putting (
3.9
) and (
3.13
) together, and by choosing
\alpha_{1}:=\min\{\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma},\frac{1}{4}(1+\sigma)\}<\min\{\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma},1\}
, we obtain from (
3.8
) that

(3.14)

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D(u-v)|^{2}\,dx\leq c{R}^{n+2\alpha_{1}}\bigg(\int_{B_{4}}|Du|^{2}\,dx+1\bigg)\leq cR^{n+2\alpha_{1}}

Finally, we deduce from (
3.7
), (
3.11
), and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|Du-(Du)_{x_{0},r}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|Dv-(Dv)_{x_{0},r}|^{2}+2|D(u-v)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\frac{r}{R}\bigg)^{n+2\bar{\alpha}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dv-(Dv)_{x_{0},{R}}|^{2}\,dx+cr^{n+2\bar{\alpha}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Dv|^{2}\,dx+c\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D(u-v)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\frac{r}{R}\bigg)^{n+2\bar{\alpha}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Du-(Du)_{x_{0},{R}}|^{2}\,dx+c{R}^{n+2\alpha_{1}}.

whenever
0<r<R<1
.
Recall that
\bar{\alpha}=\min\{\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma},\frac{1}{2}(1+\sigma)\}
, which satisfies
\alpha_{1}<\bar{\alpha}<1
. Thus, it follows from the iteration lemma, c.f.
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]
, that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|Du-(Du)_{x_{0},r}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\frac{r}{R}\bigg)^{n+2\alpha_{1}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|Du-(Du)_{x_{0},{R}}|^{2}\,dx+cr^{n+2\alpha_{1}},

whenever

and
x_{0}\in B_{2}
. In particular, taking

, and utilizing Jensen’s inequality that yields
|(Du)_{x_{0},1}|^{2}\leq\fint_{B_{1}(x_{0})}|Du|^{2}\,dx\leq c\int_{B_{4}}|Du|^{2}\,dx
, we observe from (

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|Du-(Du)_{x_{0},r}|^{2}\,dx\leq cr^{n+2\alpha_{1}},

for all

and all
x_{0}\in B_{2}
. Therefore,
u\in C^{1,\alpha_{1}}(B_{2})
with the estimate

(3.16)

\|Du\|_{C^{0,\alpha_{1}}(B_{2})}^{2}\leq c.

Now if
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha=\min\{\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma},\sigma\}
, then the proof is finished. Otherwise, that is, if
\alpha_{1}<\alpha
, we perform a bootstrapping argument as follows. Due to (
3.16
) and (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.17)

\sup_{\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-\ell|\leq cR^{1+\alpha_{1}},

for every
R\in(0,1)
, where
\ell
is the affine part of
u
, i.e.,
\ell(x_{0})=u(x_{0})
and

. Thus, we can update the oscillation estimate (
3.12
) of
v
over
B_{R}(x_{0})
, via Lemma
3.1
again, to

(3.18)

\sup_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|v-\ell|\leq 2\bigg[\sup_{\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-\ell|\bigg]\leq 2cR^{1+\alpha_{1}}.

By (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), we can improve (
3.13
) as

(3.19)

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-v||Dv|^{2}\,dx\leq cR^{n-2\delta+1+\alpha_{1}}\int_{B_{4}}|Du|^{2}\,dx.

Thus, by choosing

, we can further improve (
3.14
) by replacing

∎

We are left with the optimal regularity estimate for the case
\gamma\in[1,2)
. Recall from Section

that

is a small constant, determined solely by the smoothness of

, such that
\rho\in C^{\infty}(B_{d_{0}}(\partial M))

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume
\gamma\in[1,2)
, and let

be a minimizing constraint map for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
. If

\lambda_{0}\geq 1

, one has, for every ball

\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq c\left\Arrowvert{Du}\right\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}(U)}^{2}+c\lambda\gamma(d_{0})^{\gamma-1}

where
c>1
depends only on
n
,
m
,
\partial M
,
\lambda
,
\gamma
,
\lambda_{0}
,
d_{0}
,
\operatorname{dist}(B,\partial U)
.

Proof.

Since
u\in C^{1}(U;B_{d_{0}}(\partial M))
, Lemma
2.7
yields
\rho\circ u\in C^{1}(U)
. Moreover, since
\gamma\geq 1
,
(\rho\circ u)^{\gamma-1}\in C(U)
, whence it follows from (

), the assumption

, and
\rho\in C^{\infty}(B_{d_{0}}(\partial M))
(which along with

\operatorname{Hess}\rho_{u}(Du,Du)\in C(U)

\rho\circ u\in W_{\textup{loc}}^{2,p}(U)
for every

.

Similarly, since
\Pi\in C^{\infty}(B_{d_{0}}(\partial M);\partial M)
, and
\nu\in C^{\infty}(\partial M;{\mathbb{S}}^{m-1})
, we observe from

\Pi\circ u\in W_{\textup{loc}}^{2,p}(U;\partial M)

.

Combining the above observations together, we deduce (from
