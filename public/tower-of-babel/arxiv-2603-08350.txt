Barta Theorem for the p p -Laplacian and Geometric Applications
Paulo Henryque C. Silva

Abstract
In this article, we develop a Barta-type formulation for the p p -Laplacian on Riemannian manifolds, extending the approach of Cheung-Leung [ 17 ] and Bessa–Montenegro [ 9 ] from the linear to the nonlinear setting. This framework yields sharp lower bounds for the p p -fundamental tone without any assumptions on boundary regularity. As applications, we obtain nonlinear extensions of Cheng’s eigenvalue comparison theorem and the Cheng–Li–Yau estimate for p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 in the context of minimal immersions. In particular, under the above assumptions, the domain Ω \Omega is p p -stable for the Schrödinger-type operator associated with the potential 𝒱 = ‖ A ‖ p \mathcal{V}=||A||^{p} , where A A denotes the second fundamental form of the minimal immersion. In addition, we establish a lower bound for the p p -fundamental tone in the setting where the immersion has locally bounded mean curvature. Finally, we provide a Kazdan–Kramer type characterization of the p p -fundamental tone, offering a unified and geometric perspective on spectral bounds for the operator p p -Laplacian.

Introduction

Quasilinear operators play a fundamental role in both mathematical analysis
and physical modeling, due to their broad range of applications. They naturally
arise in the description of several nonlinear phenomena, including non-Newtonian
flows, nonlinear diffusion processes, and saturation effects. In this work,
we investigate the quasilinear operator known as the
p
-Laplacian
, on a Riemannian
m
-manifold
(M,g)
, defined by

\triangle_{p}\varphi\coloneq\operatorname{div}(|\nabla\varphi|^{p-2}\nabla\varphi),

(1)

with
p\in(1,\infty)
. In the case
p=2
, we recover the classical Laplace–Beltrami operator on
M
.
When
p>2
, the
p
-Laplacian is associated with
slow diffusion
phenomena, in which the diffusivity increases with the magnitude of the gradient
|\nabla\varphi|
. For
p\in(1,2)
, the
p
-Laplacian describes
fast diffusion
processes, where the diffusivity exhibits an inverse dependence on
|\nabla\varphi|
. Standard references for the foundational study of the

p
-Laplacian include
[
3
,
25
,
28
,
29
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold and
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded domain, possibly with boundary
\partial\Omega
. The
eigenvalue problem
associated with the

p
-Laplacian
\triangle_{p}
is given by

\begin{cases}\triangle_{p}\varphi=-\lambda\,|\varphi|^{p-2}\varphi&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\varphi=0&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{if}\hskip 9.24994pt\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}

(2)

The real number
\lambda
is called an
eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplacian

\triangle_{p}
on
M
. For
p\in(1,\infty)
, it follows from
[
28
]
that the Dirichlet problem
(
2
) admits a non-trivial solution, and from
[
25
, Theorem 4.5]
that any such
solution has regularity only of class
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)
for some

\alpha\in(0,1)
. Therefore, it is natural to interpret solutions to (
2
) in the weak sense. More precisely, we say that
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a
weak solution
to (
2
) if

\int_{\Omega}\left\langle|\nabla\,\varphi|^{p-2}\nabla\,\varphi,\nabla\,\eta\right\rangle d\mu=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{p-2}\varphi\eta\,d\mu,

(3)

for any test function
\eta\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Here we are writing the metric as
g=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
d\mu
denotes the Riemannian volume measure induced by the metric
g
on
\Omega
. The Sobolev space
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
denotes the completion of
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm

\|\varphi\|_{1,p}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(|\varphi|^{p}+|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\right)d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

Let
\lambda_{1,p}^{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega)
be the infimum of all values
\lambda
for which (
2
) admits a nontrivial solution, whenever
\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
. The Dirichlet spectrum of the
p
-Laplacian consists of a nondecreasing sequence
\{\lambda_{k,p}(\Omega)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, with
\lambda_{k,p}(\Omega)\to+\infty
as
k\to\infty
, obtained via the Ljusternik–Schnirelman principle, see
[
20
]
. Moreover, the first eigenvalue is isolated, simple, admits an eigenfunction of fixed sign, and possesses a variational characterization (see
[
3
,
28
]
),

\lambda_{1,p}^{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega)=\inf\left\{\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}d\mu}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{p}d\mu};\,\varphi\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\,\varphi\not\equiv 0\right\}.

(4)

Clearly, (
4
) is valid for arbitrary open subsets
\Omega\subseteq M
and defines what is called the
p
-fundamental tone

\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
of
\Omega
, although
\Omega
may not have Dirichlet eigenvalues.
When
\Omega\subset M
has a nonempty and relatively compact boundary, the
p
-fundamental tone coincides with the first Dirichlet eigenvalue,
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1,p}^{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega).
Several significant results involving the
p
-fundamental tone have been obtained in recent years. For the case
p=2
, we refer to
[
9
,
10
]
, while for
p\in(2,\infty)
we mention
[
27
,
30
,
31
]
. A rather interesting way to think about equation (
2
),
p=2
, is by recalling the problem of
vibrating drums
: let
\mathcal{V}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
describe the vibration of a drumhead satisfying the wave equation

\begin{cases}\mathcal{V}_{tt}=\alpha^{2}\triangle\mathcal{V}&\text{in }\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
\mathcal{V}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(5)

Here, the constant
\alpha
depends on the physical characteristics of the membrane.
Looking for solutions of the form
\mathcal{V}(x,t)=\mathcal{T}(t)\vartheta(x)
, we obtain an ordinary Sturm–Liouville equation for

and a Dirichlet eigenvalue problem (
2
) for the function
\vartheta(x)

In this context,
\lambda_{1}^{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega)
represents exactly the lowest vibration frequency of the membrane (the deepest sound emitted by the drum).
The following theorem then addresses a bound on the deepest sound emitted by the drum through an admissible test function.

Theorem 1.1

(Barta’s inequality

Let

is constant, then problem (

Bartas’s Theorem for
p
–Laplacian

Picone’s classical inequality was initially developed in
[
32
, p. 18]
to provide a new proof of Sturm’s Theorem. The tool has become quite useful, being used in various contexts (see
[
2
,
4
,
23
,
28
,
37
]
). The inequality in its classical form establishes that: if
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are two differentiable functions such that
u\geq 0
and
v>0
, then in each connected component of
\Omega
we have

|\nabla u|^{2}+\dfrac{u^{2}}{v^{2}}|\nabla v|^{2}-2\dfrac{u}{v}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle=|\nabla u|^{2}-\left\langle\nabla v,\nabla\left(\dfrac{u^{2}}{v}\right)\right\rangle\geq 0.

(13)

Moreover, the equality in (
13
) holds if, and only if,
u
is proportional to
v
. Note that the lower bound of the Barta inequality given in (
6
) can be obtained through (
13
) by taking
u
as the first eigenfunction of the Laplacian and integrating by parts the right-hand side of the equation (
13
). Subsequently, the inequality (
13
) was extended in the following work
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
ensuring that if
u\geq 0
and
v>0
are differentiable functions, then

|\nabla u|^{p}+(p-1)\dfrac{u^{p}}{v^{p}}|\nabla v|^{p}-p\dfrac{u^{p-1}}{v^{p-1}}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle=|\nabla u|^{p}-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla v,\nabla\left(\dfrac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)\right\rangle\geq 0.

(14)

Furthermore, the equality holds if and only if
u
is proportional to
v
in each connected component of the domain
\Omega
. When
p=2
in (
14
) we recover the classical Picone inequality (
13
). In Section 2 of
[
2
]
we observed that from the inequality given in (
14
) we obtain the following results for the
p
-Laplacian: simplicity of the principal eigenvalue, Barta’s inequalities, Hardy’s inequalities, and nonexistence of solutions. Based on
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
the following result is a natural extension of
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
2
, Corollary 2.1]
, using, respectively, the same approximation technique in
[
9
]
and Picone’s inequality for the
p
-Laplacian (
14
) to extend Barta’s inequality for the
p
-fundamental tone in bounded Riemannian domains.

Theorem 1.2.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold, and let
\Omega\subset M
be a domain. Assume that
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\triangle_{p}\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega)

and
\eta>0
in
\Omega
.
Then the
p
-fundamental tone
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
satisfies

\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\geq\inf_{\Omega}\left\{-\frac{\triangle_{p}\eta}{\eta^{p-1}}\right\}.

(15)

Moreover, if
\Omega
is bounded, the equality in (
15
)
holds if and only if
\eta=\beta u
for some constant
\beta>0
,
where
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a nonnegative minimizer of the Rayleigh quotient.

Proof.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a domain. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\varphi\geq 0
and choose
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}(\Omega)
with
\triangle_{p}\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega)
such that
\eta>0
in
\Omega
. Since
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi)\Subset\Omega
and
\eta
is continuous and strictly positive in
\Omega
, there exists
c>0
such that
\eta\geq c
on
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi)
. Hence, the function
\varphi^{p}/\eta^{p-1}
is well defined and belongs to
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Moreover, since its support is contained compactly in
\Omega
, we conclude that
\varphi^{p}/\eta^{p-1}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Therefore, it may be used as a test function in the weak formulation of the
p
-Laplacian. Applying Picone’s identity (
14
) and integrating over
\Omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{p}+(p-1)\dfrac{\varphi^{p}}{\eta^{p}}|\nabla\eta|^{p}-p\dfrac{\varphi^{p-1}}{\eta^{p-1}}|\nabla\eta|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\eta\right\rangle\right)d\mu

(16)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}-|\nabla\eta|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{p}}{\eta^{p-1}}\right)\right\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}d\mu-\int_{\Omega}\left\langle|\nabla\eta|^{p-2}\nabla\eta,\nabla\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{p}}{\eta^{p-1}}\right)\right\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}d\mu+\int_{\Omega}(\triangle_{p}\eta)\cdot\dfrac{\varphi^{p}}{\eta^{p-1}}\ d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}d\mu-\inf_{\Omega}\left\{-\dfrac{\triangle_{p}\eta}{\eta^{p-1}}\right\}\cdot\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{p}d\mu.

Where
d\mu
denotes the Riemannian volume measure on
\Omega
. Consequently, from (
16
), for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we obtain

\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}d\mu}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{p}d\mu}\geq\inf_{\Omega}\left\{-\frac{\triangle_{p}\eta}{\eta^{p-1}}\right\}.

(17)

Taking the infimum over
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}

in (
17
) and using the density of
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
(see
[
25

), we conclude that (
15
) holds.
Assume that
\Omega
is bounded and that
\eta=\beta u
for some
\beta>0
,
where
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a nonnegative minimizer of the Rayleigh quotient, i.e.,

\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\ |\nabla u|^{p}d\mu}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}d\mu}.

Since

satisfies
\triangle_{p}u=-\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\,u^{p-1}
in
\Omega
, the

-homogeneity of the

-Laplacian,

, yields the following identity

-\triangle_{p}\eta=-\triangle_{p}(\beta u)=-\beta^{p-1}\triangle_{p}u=\beta^{p-1}\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\,u^{p-1}=\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\eta^{\,p-1}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{in }\Omega

in the weak sense. In particular, wherever

(equivalently,
\eta>0
),

\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)=-\frac{\triangle_{p}\eta}{\eta^{p-1}}.

Hence,
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)=\inf_{\Omega}\left\{-\triangle_{p}\eta/\eta^{p-1}\right\}
, and equality holds in (
15
). It remains to prove the converse implication. Assume that equality holds in (

). Since
\Omega
is bounded, the infimum in the Rayleigh quotient is achieved. By standard variational arguments,

is a weak solution of
\triangle_{p}u=-\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\,u^{p-1}
in
\Omega
. Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a sequence with
\varphi_{n}\geq 0
and

\varphi_{n}\to u

. For each

0\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(|\nabla\varphi_{n}|^{p}-|\nabla\eta|^{p-2}\bigl\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla(\varphi_{n}^{p}/\eta^{p-1})\bigr\rangle\Bigr)d\mu.

Proceeding as in (
16
), and using the weak formulation of
\triangle_{p}\eta
with the test

, it follows that

Passing to the limit as

and using

gives the following

0\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu-\int_{\Omega}\left(-\frac{\triangle_{p}\eta}{\eta^{p-1}}\right)|u|^{p}d\mu.

(18)

Let

\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)=\lambda
.
Moreover,

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu=\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}d\mu=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(-\dfrac{\triangle_{p}\eta}{\eta^{p-1}}\right)|u|^{p}d\mu.

(19)

Combining this with the previous inequality (
18
) and (

|\nabla u|^{p}+(p-1)\frac{u^{p}}{\eta^{p}}|\nabla\eta|^{p}-p\frac{u^{p-1}}{\eta^{p-1}}\left\langle\nabla u,|\nabla\eta|^{p-2}\nabla\eta\right\rangle=0.

Henceforth, all equalities and inequalities are understood to hold almost everywhere in

\underbrace{|\nabla u|^{p}-p\frac{u^{p-1}}{\eta^{p-1}}|\nabla u||\nabla\eta|^{p-2}|\nabla\eta|+(p-1)\frac{u^{p}}{\eta^{p}}|\nabla\eta|^{p}}_{\mathcal{A}}+\underbrace{p\frac{u^{p-1}}{\eta^{p-1}}|\nabla\eta|^{p-2}\left(|\nabla u||\nabla\eta|-\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta\right\rangle\right)}_{\mathcal{B}}=0.

Using Young’s inequality (
\displaystyle ab\leq a^{p}/p+b^{q}/q

and

\mathcal{A}(x)\geq 0

\mathcal{A}(x)=\mathcal{B}(x)=0
a.e. in
\Omega

|\nabla u|\cdot|\nabla\eta|=\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta\rangle,

hence
\nabla u
and
\nabla\eta
are parallel a.e. in

Set

On

(22)

Let

Since

|\nabla u|=\frac{u}{\eta}\,|\nabla\eta|.

(24)

On

. If there exists

) reduces to

\alpha(x)>0

\Omega

Definition 2.1

(Weak divergence)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold and let
X\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(M)
be a vector field, in the sense that
\|X\|\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(M)
.
We say that a function

is the weak divergence of

, and we write

, if for every test function
\eta\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(M)
the following identity holds:

\int_{M}\eta\operatorname{Div}(X)dM=-\int_{M}\langle\nabla\eta,X\rangle dM.

We denote by
\mathcal{W}^{1,1}(M)
the space of vector fields on
M

that admits a weak divergence.

Remark 2.2
.

If

and

\int_{M}\operatorname{Div}(fX)dM=\int_{M}\Big[\langle\nabla f,X\rangle+f\operatorname{Div}(X)\Big]dM=0.

(25)

Conversely, if

for all

,

. In this case, the weak divergence of

is
given by

\operatorname{Div}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Div}\!\left(\xi_{i}X\right),

where
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a partition of unity subordinated to a locally finite open covering of

.

The result below can be interpreted within the framework of criticality theory for quasilinear elliptic operators. Indeed, the existence of a boundary blow-up solution to (

In this sense, the boundary blow-up problem detects the critical threshold of the operator.

Theorem 1.8

90
