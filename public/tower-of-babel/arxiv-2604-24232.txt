Density protected states in active matter under virtual confinement
Giuseppe Fava, Francesco Ginelli, Benoît Mahault

Abstract
We investigate photo-responsive structure formation in a minimal model of dry active nematics. Combining microscopic simulations with the analysis of the corresponding hydrodynamic theory, we show that the system generically self-assembles into a dense, nematically ordered ring at the boundary of circular illumination patterns. Remarkably, these boundary structures give rise to a protected disordered core whose density is self-selected and independent of the global particle density. Our analysis reveals that these states emerge from a generic interplay between local nematic alignment and curvature-driven active currents. These results identify a robust route to boundary-induced structure formation in active matter and provide experimentally testable predictions.

Inspired by recent observations of light-responsive filamentous cyanobacteria that align and accumulate at the edges of convex illuminated patterns

we investigate this phenomenon through a minimal model of
self-propelled agents combining nematic alignment with velocity reversals triggered by local illumination gradients.
Focusing on circular geometries, where particles form a high-density,

Over a broad region of the phase diagram, we show that these boundary

By mapping our model onto a hydrodynamic-level description,
we show that these protected states arise from the interplay between local alignment and active currents driven by nematic order curvature;
revealing a generic mechanism for boundary-induced structure formation in active matter and enabling experimentally testable predictions.

Namely, we consider Vicsek-style point-like particles moving in two dimensions with constant speed

Consistently with the head-tail symmetry of the bacterial body, particles align their unit self-propulsion direction

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{i}^{t+1}

\displaystyle=({\cal R}_{\eta}\circ\vartheta)\left[\left\langle\left({\rm sign}[\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{i}^{t}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}^{t}]\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}^{t}+\beta\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{ji}^{t}\right)\right\rangle_{j\sim i}\right],

To model the experimental conditions of Ref.

We next discuss the mechanism underlying density protection in the ring core.
In homogeneous space, the dynamics (
1
) is characterized by a threshold density
\rho_{c}(\beta)

beyond which global nematic order emerges.
Performing simulations without the light pattern,
we obtain curves such as those of Fig.
3
(d) showing that
the global nematic order parameter
\bar{\Omega}=\langle|\langle e^{2i\theta_{k}^{t}}\rangle_{k}|\rangle_{t}
,
vanishes for
\rho_{0}<\rho_{c}(\beta)
and exhibits a sharp increase for
\rho_{0}\gtrsim\rho_{c}(\beta)
.
Interestingly, rescaling
\rho_{0}
with the core density
\bar{\rho}(0)
obtained for the same value of
\beta
,
the curves of Fig.
3
(d) collapse in the transition region around
\rho_{0}=\bar{\rho}(0)
.
This implies that
\bar{\rho}(0)\approx\rho_{c}(\beta)
,
suggesting that the system self-organizes through active nematic-like fluxes
27
;
28
that displace particles radially from the core, up to the point at which local nematic order is suppressed.
At the boundary, nematic order persists but active nematic fluxes are compensated by the light-induced trapping that confines particles inside.
As a result, a stationary ring configuration with protected core emerges.

The generality, robustness, and extensivity of the ring structure call for a continuum description.
Here, we keep a minimal approach by considering a reduced set of field equations describing the microscopic dynamics (
1
).
In addition to the density
\rho({\bf r},t)
and nematic
\mathbf{Q}({\bf r},t)
fields, the polar symmetry of the self-propulsion dynamics
requires to consider the coarse-grained polar field
{\bf p}({\bf r},t)=\sum_{i\in V({\bf r})}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{i}^{t}
advecting the
density field.
The simplest set of equations capturing the ring formation reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot{\bf J}=0,

(2a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{p}=-\alpha\mathbf{p}-\pi_{0}\nabla\rho-\pi_{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{Q}-\kappa g(r)\rho\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\varphi),

(2b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{Q}=\left[\mu(\rho\!-\!\rho_{c})\!-\!\xi{\rm Tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})\right]\mathbf{Q}\!+\!D_{Q}\nabla^{2}\mathbf{Q}\!-\!\pi_{1}[\nabla\otimes\mathbf{p}]_{\rm ST}\,,

(2c)

where
{\bf J}=v_{0}{\bf p}-D_{\rho}\nabla\rho
is the density
flux. Unlike systematically coarse-grained theories, which typically present many nonlinearities

;
25
;
20
;
4
making analytical investigations challenging,
this minimal truncation retains only the lowest-order symmetry-allowed
terms needed to reproduce local nematic ordering and
curvature-induced active nematic currents
27
.
Polarity reversals at the light-dark interface are modelled through an external field
\kappa g(r)\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\varphi)
,
where
\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\varphi)
is the radial unit vector in polar coordinates (
r,\varphi
)
and

is a normalized function sharply peaked at
r=R

and vanishing when
r\ll R
or
r\gg R
. Concretely, reversals locally bias the polarity towards the
illuminated disk center. Within our minimal continuum description, the
effect of microscopic repelling torques is captured
phenomenologically by the effective reversal rate
\kappa
, which
controls the balance between
trapping and leakage across the illuminated region boundary.
Polar fields dynamics also includes a damping term (
\propto\alpha>0
),
suppressing polar order far from the interface, as well as isotropic
(
\propto\pi_{0}
) and anisotropic (
\propto\pi_{2}
) pressure terms.
Equation (

a density-dependent linear term that accounts for local nematic

, and a “polarity (flow) aligning term”

(
\propto\pi_{1}
), where
[\cdot]_{\rm ST}
denotes the symmetric traceless part.
Importantly, in our dry model the transport terms with positive couplings

and
\pi_{0,1,2}

encode the active nature of the dynamics,
as they directly result from microscopic self-propulsion

25

To understand how these features emerge,
we look for stationary solutions of Eqs. (
2
).
Setting time derivatives to zero, we express the polar order as

\bar{\mathbf{p}}=-\alpha^{-1}[\pi_{0}\nabla\bar{\rho}+\pi_{2}\nabla\cdot\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\kappa g(r)\bar{\rho}\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\varphi)]

Using the symmetry of the ring structure,
we seek inhomogeneous solutions of the form

\rho\!\!=\!\!\bar{\rho}(r)
,

Q_{11}\!\!=\!\!\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{S}(r)\cos[2\bar{\Phi}(\varphi)]
and
Q_{12}\!\!=\!\!\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{S}(r)\sin[2\bar{\Phi}(\varphi)]

Since nematic rings are associated with a vanishing net density flux, we look for solutions satisfying
{\bf J}=\bf 0

When nematic order is homogeneous (

it predicts nearly uniform density profiles inside and outside

\bar{\rho}(r)=\bar{\rho}(0)\exp[-\zeta\int_{0}^{r}dx\,g(x)]

where the dimensionless parameter
\zeta=\kappa v_{0}/(\alpha\tilde{D}_{\rho})

For

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}(r\leq R)=\bar{\rho}(0)~,

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}(r>R)=\bar{\rho}(0)e^{-\zeta}~,

with

When nematic order is inhomogeneous,
taking the imaginary part of Eq. (

\bar{\Phi}(\varphi)=\varphi+k\tfrac{\pi}{2}

Linear stability analysis detailed in

the active coupling between polarity and order

\bar{\rho}(r)\underset{r^{*}\leq r\lesssim R}{\simeq}\rho_{c}+\varpi^{2}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{r}{r^{*}}\right),\quad r^{*}=Re^{-\tfrac{\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}}{\varpi}},

Finally, assuming that the nematic field is homogeneous outside of the illuminated disk,
the outside density profile is given by
\bar{\rho}_{\rm out}(r>R)\approx\rho_{R}\exp(-\zeta)

As shown in Fig.

It also confirms the mechanism at the origin of
density protection: the density increase at

is driven
by the term
\propto\!\varpi^{2}\!\propto\!\tilde{\pi}_{2}^{2}
,
originating from the curvature-induced active current
{\bf J}\!\propto\!{\bf p}\!\propto\!-\pi_{2}\nabla\cdot{\bf Q}
.
Since this current vanishes when local nematic order is lost,
the core density must be determined by the threshold
\rho_{c}
.

Note also that the absence of microscopic repelling torques (
\beta=0
) makes the illuminated disk perfectly
trapping, corresponding to a diverging effective reversal rate
\kappa
.
In this limit, the active curvature-driven flux is suppressed and the ring state is lost,
consistent with both microscopic and continuum simulations (see
Appendix A
).

To summarize, combining agent-based simulations with a minimal continuum description,
we have uncovered a generic mechanism by which virtual confinement induced by light-responsive activity drives large-scale pattern formation beyond simple homogeneous accumulation.
Collective effects endow the resulting structures with remarkable properties:
although they originate from a localized driving, these patterns survive in the thermodynamic limit and exhibit density protection at macroscopic scales.
The robustness of this mechanism—which relies on minimal ingredients ubiquitous in active systems, namely alignment interactions and active currents—suggests that our results should be testable across a broad range of experimental platforms.
Its applicability should moreover not be limited to light-responsive active matter,
as it may also be relevant for describing active filaments confined by hard boundaries,
and it could play a role in the formation of the ring-like networks observed in certain assemblies of active filaments
23
;
38
;
37
;
10
.

Appendix A: The no repelling torque limit.—

For parameters of the microscopic model considered in the main text,
rings form for a small but finite value (
\approx 10^{-2}
) of
\beta
.
Since the repelling torque strength is an effective parameter not
controllable experimentally, this transition will be investigated
elsewhere.

At the hydrodynamic level, we argue in the main text that the absence of microscopic repelling torques corresponds to the regime
\kappa\to\infty
.
In this limit, the polarity
\bar{\mathbf{p}}
is simply enslaved to the external field
\kappa g(r)\rho\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\varphi)

so that
\pi_{0},\,\pi_{2}\approx 0
, suppressing the active flux responsible for density accumulation at the disk boundary.
Numerical simulations of Eqs. (
2
) with
\pi_{2}=0
indeed reveal that in this regime
the density profile remains nearly homogeneous inside and outside the illuminated disk (not shown),
in agreement with Eq. (
4
) and the analysis carried out in the next section.
Repelling torques are thus
essential for ring formation.

Appendix B: The steady-state solutions of Eqs. (
2
).—

As detailed in the main text, Eq. (
3
) admits three types of solution labeled respectively homogeneous, aster and vortex.
The homogeneous solution, which is valid only in the infinitely thin interface limit where
g(r)=\delta(r-R)
, leads to uniform density profiles in and out of the illuminated disk [Eq. (
4
)].
The nematic amplitude is then also homogeneous in both regions and is found by solving
\left[\mu(\bar{\rho}-\rho_{c})-\xi^{\prime}\bar{S}^{2}\right]\bar{S}=0
,
while the nematic orientation
\bar{\Phi}
is uniform across the whole system.

Aster and vortex solutions are characterized by an orientation profile
\bar{\Phi}(\varphi)=\varphi+k\tfrac{\pi}{2}
, where
k
is an integer.
With this ansatz, the stationary density and nematic amplitude solve

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\rho}\bar{\rho}^{\prime}+\tilde{\pi}_{2}(-1)^{k}\left(\bar{S}^{\prime}+\frac{2\bar{S}}{r}\right)+\frac{\kappa v_{0}}{\alpha}\bar{\rho}g=0,

(B1a)

\displaystyle\left[\mu(\bar{\rho}-\rho_{c})-\frac{\xi}{2}\bar{S}^{2}\right]\bar{S}+D_{Q}\left[\bar{S}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{r}\bar{S}^{\prime}-\frac{4}{r^{2}}\bar{S}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi_{1}D_{\rho}}{v_{0}}(-1)^{k}\left(\bar{\rho}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{r}\bar{\rho}^{\prime}\right)=0.

(B1b)

For simplicity, we now consider the thin interface limit and set
g(r)=0
for both
r<R
and
r>R
.

As the nematic order exhibits a defect at
r=0
, solutions of Eqs. (
B1
) are dominated by different contributions close to and far from the origin.
In the limit
r\to 0
, Eqs. (
B1
) admit solutions of the form

\bar{\rho}(r)\simeq\bar{\rho}(0)+Ar^{2},\quad\bar{S}(r)\simeq Br^{2},

(B2)

where
A
and
B
are two undetermined constants that satisfy

A=(-1)^{k+1}2\tilde{\pi}_{2}B/\tilde{D}_{\rho}
.
When local nematic order is present near the centre of the domain,
B>0
and aster configurations (
k
even) lead to
A<0
, such that density is highest at
r=0
.
Conversely, vortices with odd
k
give
A>0
,
such that particles are advected towards the boundary of the disk.

We now consider the opposite limit of
r\lesssim R
.
Since far from the centre of the disk derivatives of
\bar{\rho}
and
\bar{S}
scale like
r^{-1}
,
for
R
large the leading order term of Eq. (
B1b
) is now the Landau potential, leading to

\bar{S}(r)\simeq\sqrt{2\mu[\bar{\rho}(r)-\rho_{c}]/\xi}
for
\bar{\rho}(r)\geq\rho_{c}
and
0
otherwise.
Replacing this expression in Eq. (
B1a
) and assuming that
\bar{\rho}(r)\geq\rho_{c}
,
we obtain

\left(1+(-1)^{k}\frac{\varpi}{2\sqrt{\bar{\rho}-\rho_{c}}}\right)\bar{\rho}^{\prime}+(-1)^{k}\frac{2\varpi}{r}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}-\rho_{c}}=0,

(B3)

where
\varpi=\tilde{\pi}_{2}\tilde{D}_{\rho}^{-1}\sqrt{2\mu/\xi}
.
Solutions of Eq. (
B3
) can be written as
\bar{\rho}(r)=\rho_{c}+X^{2}(r)
,
where
X(r)
is expressed in terms of Lambert
W
functions:

X(r)=(-1)^{k}\frac{\varpi}{2}W_{n}\left[\frac{(-1)^{k}2C}{\varpi r^{2}}\right],

(B4)

where
n=0
or
-1
according to the relevant branch, and
C
is an integration constant.
For
k
even, the argument of the Lambert function is positive, which implies that
n=0
.
Since
W_{0}
is monotonously growing,
\bar{\rho}(r)
decreases as
r
grows,
confirming that density is maximum at
r=0
.

For
k
odd, the argument and prefactor of
W_{n}
are negative.
Since the density can take arbitrary large values, we must choose
n=-1

as
W_{-1}(z)
varies in
[-1;-\infty)
for
z<0
.
Using the identity
W_{-1}(xe^{x})=x
for
x\leq-1
,
we fix
C
with the value
\rho_{R}
of the density at
r=R
:

X(r)=-\frac{\varpi}{2}W_{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{e}\frac{{R^{*}}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\qquad(k\,{\rm odd}),

(B5)

where

R^{*}=R\exp(\tfrac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}/\varpi)(2\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}/\varpi)^{1/2}
.
Since the function
W_{-1}(z)
is defined in the finite interval
[-e^{-1};0)
,
the solution (
B5
) only formally holds for
R^{*}\leq r\leq R
.
For
r<R^{*}
, on the other hand, the slowly varying fields assumption breaks down and
the solution (
B5
) is no more defined.
In the limit where
\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}/\varpi
is large, however,

R^{*}\to 0
and the asymptotic series
W_{-1}(z)\simeq\ln|z|
for
z\to 0

allows to recover Eq. (
6
),
which can be matched with Eq. (
B2
) with
A=B=0
for
r<r^{*}
.

Appendix C: Linear stability analysis of inhomogeneous solutions.—

Here, we study the stability of the vortex and aster solutions of Eqs. (
2
) to perturbations of the form

\rho=\bar{\rho}(r)+\delta\rho(\varphi,t)
,
S=\bar{S}(r)+\delta S(\varphi,t)

and
\Phi=\bar{\Phi}(\varphi)+\delta\Phi(\varphi,t)
.
Going into angular Fourier space
(\delta\rho,\delta S,\delta\Phi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n}(\delta\rho_{n},\delta S_{n},\delta\Phi_{n})e^{-in\varphi}
,
the perturbations satisfy at linear order

\displaystyle\delta\dot{\rho}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tilde{D}_{\rho}n^{2}}{r^{2}}\delta\rho_{n}+\Gamma_{\rho S}\delta S_{n}+\Gamma_{\rho\Phi}\delta\Phi_{n},

(C1a)

\displaystyle\delta\dot{S}_{n}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{S\rho}\delta\rho_{n}-{a}_{n}^{2}\delta S_{n}+\Gamma_{S\Phi}\delta\Phi_{n},

(C1b)

\displaystyle\delta\dot{\Phi}_{n}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\Phi\rho}\delta\rho_{n}+\Gamma_{\Phi S}\delta S_{n}-K_{n}\delta\Phi_{n},

(C1c)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rho S}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}\tilde{\pi}_{2}n^{2}}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rho\Phi}

\displaystyle=-4in\frac{(-1)^{k}\tilde{\pi}_{2}(r\bar{S})^{\prime}}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{S\rho}

\displaystyle=\mu\bar{S}+\frac{(-1)^{k}\tilde{\pi}_{0}n^{2}}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{S\Phi}

\displaystyle=8in\frac{\tilde{D}_{Q}\bar{S}}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Phi\rho}

\displaystyle=in\frac{(-1)^{k}\tilde{\pi}_{0}}{r^{2}\bar{S}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Phi S}

\displaystyle=-2in\frac{\tilde{D}_{Q}}{\bar{S}r^{2}},

while

a_{n}^{2}=-\mu(\bar{\rho}-\rho_{c})+\tfrac{3}{2}\xi\bar{S}^{2}+r^{-2}\tilde{D}_{Q}(n^{2}+4)
,

K_{n}=\frac{(-1)^{k}\tilde{\pi}_{0}}{\bar{S}}r\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{\tilde{D}_{Q}n^{2}}{r^{2}},

and
\tilde{D}_{Q}=D_{Q}+\tfrac{1}{2}\pi_{1}\pi_{2}/\alpha
.

We first consider
n=0
perturbations.
While
a_{0}^{2}
generally remains positive for both aster and vortex solutions,
we deduce from Eqs. (
6
) and (
B2
) that

K_{0}
takes the following values in regions where particles accumulate

K_{0}\underset{r\to 0}{\simeq}0\;({\rm aster}),\qquad K_{0}\underset{r\lesssim R}{\simeq}\frac{4\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2}}{\tilde{D}_{\rho}r^{2}}\quad({\rm vortex}).

(C2)

Close to the centre of the disk the aster solution is thus only marginally stable to
n=0
perturbations.
Hence, in the presence of fluctuations this solution should rapidly be destroyed by uniform rotations of the nematic order.
For the ring solution, on the other hand,
\bar{S}
is exactly
0
at small
r
while
K_{0}>0
for large
r<R
.
In addition, we note from (
C2
) that
K(r)\propto\pi_{0}\propto\pi_{2}
, indicating that the vortex structure is stabilized by activity.

The fate of finite
n
perturbations is determined by the characteristic polynomial of the linear system (
C1
),
which formally reads
P(\lambda)=\sum_{i=0}^{3}z_{i}\lambda^{i}
.
According to the Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion
13
,
the eigenvalues of the linear system (
C1
) are all negative if and only if
all
z_{i}<0
and
z_{2}z_{1}-z_{3}z_{0}>0
.

While
z_{3}=-1
and
z_{2}=-K_{n}-a_{n}^{2}-\tilde{D}_{\rho}n^{2}/r^{2}

always remain negative for both the vortex and aster solutions,

z_{1}
and
z_{0}
may change sign.
To progress further, we focus on the vortex solution and assume that
r\lesssim R
,
such that we approximate
\bar{\rho}(r)\simeq\rho_{R}+O[\ln^{2}(r/R)]
and

\bar{S}(r)\simeq\sqrt{2\mu(\rho_{R}-\rho_{c})/\xi}
,
while
K_{0}(r)\simeq 4\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2}/\tilde{D}_{\rho}r^{2}
from Eq. (
C2
).

z_{1}
then explicitly reads:

\displaystyle z_{1}

\displaystyle=-K_{0}a_{0}^{2}+\left[-\tilde{\pi}_{2}\mu\bar{S}-(\tilde{D}_{\rho}+\tilde{D}_{Q})(a_{0}^{2}+K_{0})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{4}{r^{2}}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2}+\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2}\frac{r\bar{S}^{\prime}}{\bar{S}}+4\tilde{D}_{Q}^{2}\right)\right]\frac{n^{2}}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left[\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2}-\tilde{D}_{\rho}\tilde{D}_{Q}-\tilde{D}_{Q}(\tilde{D}_{Q}+\tilde{D}_{\rho})\right]\frac{n^{4}}{r^{4}}.

Noting from the expressions of the tilded coefficients that
\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2}-\tilde{D}_{Q}\tilde{D}_{\rho}<0
, the
O(n^{4})
term in
z_{1}
’s expression is always negative.
Hence,
z_{1}
may change sign for finite
n
when its
O(n^{2})
coefficient becomes positive.
This term gathers two contributions that scale differently with
r
.
It is always negative for large
r
,
while it can change sign when
r
becomes lower than a critical value
r_{c}
given by

r_{c}^{2}=4\frac{\frac{\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2}\varpi}{\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}}+\frac{\tilde{D}_{Q}}{\tilde{D}_{\rho}}(\tilde{D}_{Q}\tilde{D}_{\rho}-\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2})+\tilde{D}_{Q}(2\tilde{D}_{Q}-\tilde{D}_{\rho})}{\mu\tilde{D}_{\rho}\varpi\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}\left[1+\frac{2\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}}{\varpi}\left(1+\frac{\tilde{D}_{Q}}{\tilde{D}_{\rho}}\right)\right]},

which is only defined for sufficiently large effective diffusivity of the nematic order
\tilde{D}_{Q}
.
We further note that the critical radius, when it exists, decreases with the ring density as for large
\rho_{R}
,

r_{c}\simeq 1/\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}
.
Hence, denser rings should generally be more stable.

We now turn to
z_{0}
.
Since we have already established that sufficiently small rings are unstable,
we take
r
large and assess their stability in the thermodynamic limit.

z_{0}
then simplifies up to
O(r^{-6})
terms to

\displaystyle z_{0}\underset{r\to\infty}{\simeq}-\frac{2\tilde{D}_{\rho}\tilde{D}_{Q}\mu(\rho_{R}-\rho_{c})}{r^{4}}\left(1+\frac{\varpi}{\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}}\right)n^{4}

\displaystyle+\frac{8\varpi\mu\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}}{r^{4}}\left[\frac{\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2}}{2}+\tilde{D}_{\rho}\tilde{D}_{Q}\left(1+\frac{\varpi}{\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}}\right)\right]n^{2}.

The
O(n^{2})
term in
z_{0}
is always positive, while the
O(n^{4})
one is always negative.
Hence,
z_{0}>0
for
n\in[1;n_{c}]
where
n_{c}
is given by

n_{c}^{2}=\frac{4\varpi}{\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}}\frac{1+\frac{\varpi}{\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}}+\frac{\tilde{\pi}_{0}\tilde{\pi}_{2}}{2\tilde{D}_{\rho}\tilde{D}_{Q}}}{1+\frac{\varpi}{\sqrt{\rho_{R}-\rho_{c}}}}.

(C3)

Importantly,
n_{c}\to 0
as the ring density
\rho_{R}
grows.
Since
n
takes discrete values, in sufficiently dense rings
n_{c}<1
, and the instability does not develop.
This confirms that dense rings are more stable to fluctuations.
Since, at leading order in
r^{-2}
,
z_{2}=O(1)
,
z_{1}=O(r^{-2})
, and
z_{0}=O(r^{-4})
we further note that for
r\to\infty
the quantity
z_{2}z_{1}-z_{3}z_{0}\simeq z_{2}z_{1}
remains always positive for large
r
.

To summarize, rings must be sufficiently large and dense to escape the instabilities described above.
In addition, since
n_{c}
decreases when reducing
\varpi
,
systems for which this effective parameter is small should also be more prone to exhibit stable ring structures.

Appendix D: Details on numerical simulations.—

Simulations of Eqs. (
2
) were performed by means of a pseudo-spectral solver with
1/3
de-aliasing and an Euler explicit scheme for time integration. For all simulations, we described the interface with the function
g(r)=\exp(-(r-R)^{2}/2\sigma^{2})/(2\pi\sigma^{2})
and
\sigma=1/4
. Space and time resolutions were taken equal to
dx=1/4
and
dt=0.01
, respectively.
