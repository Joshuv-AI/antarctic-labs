Tropical cohomology via reductions of tropical varieties
Ryota Mikami

Abstract
. Itenberg-Katzarkov-Mikhalkin-Zharkov gave an isomorphism of tropical cohomology and cohomology of some maximally degenerate algebraic varieties. Their proof was based on tropical analogs of Steenbrink’s geometric monodromy-weight spectral sequences. These were generalized to the non-realizable case by Amini-Piquerez. In this paper, we give a new construction of these tropical spectral sequences in the same way as Steenbrink’s ones. For this purpose, we introduce reductions of tropical varieties. We also show that eigenwave actions are given by tropical Gauss-Manin connections.

1.
Introduction

Tropical cohomology

H^{p,q}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits}(W)
for a tropical variety
W

was introduced by
Itenberg-Katzarkov-Mikhalkin-Zharkov
[
14
]
.
They proved that for a one parameter
family

\mathcal{Z}=(Z_{t})_{t\in\mathcal{D}^{*}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}^{*}

of smooth complex projective varieties
over a punctured disk
\mathcal{D}^{*}

with a smooth (i.e., locally matroidal) tropicalization
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{C}((t))})
,
we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle H^{p,q}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits}(\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{C}((t))}))\cong\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}\nolimits_{2p}^{W}H^{p+q}(Z_{\infty}),

where
\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}\nolimits_{2p}^{W}H^{p+q}(Z_{\infty})
is
the weight graded quotient
of the limit mixed Hodge structure.
Since, in this case,
the odd weight graded quotients vanish
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}\nolimits_{2p}^{W}H^{p+q}(Z_{\infty})
is of pure
(p,p)
-type,
hodge numbers of general members
Z_{t}

equal tropical hodge numbers of
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{C}((t))})
.
(Gross-Siebert
[
10
]
also gave a similar cohomology and isomorphism for some integral affine manifolds with singularities.)

The proof in
[
14
]

was based on two spectral sequences.
One is Steenbrink’s geometric monodromy-weight spectral sequence (
[
31
, Corollary 4.20]
)

E_{1}^{p,q}=\bigoplus_{\max\{0,p\}\leq k}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset I\\
\#J=-p+2k+1\end{subarray}}H^{p+2q-2k}_{\mathop{\mathrm{sing}}\nolimits}\big(\bigcap_{i\in J}Z_{i}\big)(p-k)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(Z_{\infty}),

where
Z_{0}=\bigcup_{i\in I}Z_{i}
is
a strictly semi-stable reduction
of
\mathcal{Z}
.
The other one, given in
[
14
]
,
is its tropical analog.
This tropical analog conveges to tropical cohomology, and has
E_{1}
-terms isomorphic to Steenbrink’s one.
Hence we get isomorphism (
1.1
).

Amini-Piquerez
[
2
]

generalized
this tropical spectral sequence to
abstract compact tropical homology manifolds
X

(i.e., tropical varieties regular at infinity
satisfying Poincaré-Verdier duality)
under certain assumption (see
1.1
and
1.3
).
As an corollary,
isomorphism (
1.1
) was generalized to
more general maximally degenerate families in
\mathbb{P}^{n}

by Aksnes-Amini-Piquerez-Shaw

[
1
]
.

The construction of tropical spectral sequences
in
[
14
]
and

[
2
]

is conceptually similar to, but technically different from that of Steenbrink’s one (
[
31
, Corollary 4.20]
).
While Steenbrink’s one
is based on

relative log holomorphic differential forms

(cf. Deligne’s work on mixed Hodge theory
[
8
]
),
the tropical one
is based on explicit double complexes using polyhedral complex structures of tropical varieties,
and
uses
the exactness of certain complexes.
In particular,
this exactness
requires
tropical Poincaré-Verdier duality,
which is close to
“maximally degenerate” assumption in
complex algebraic geometry.
Of course,
such an assumption is not required in Steenbrink’s one.

The main goal of this paper is
to give a simpler construction of
the tropical spectral sequences
in the same way as Steenbrink
(without tropical Poincaré-Verdier duality).

As a byproduct of our construction of the spectral sequence
(
1.1
),
we shall give
a new description of “monodromy” of tropical varieties,
the
eigenwave action
.
The eigenwave action was introduced by
Mikhalkin-Zharkov
[
26
, Subsection 5.2]
.
Its dual

H^{p,q}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits}(X)\to H^{p-1,q+1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits}(X)

coincides (
[
26
, Appendix]
)
with the monodromy action
N
on

\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}\nolimits_{2p}^{W}H^{p+q}(Z_{\infty})

in the smooth realizable case.
The eigenwave action is a cap product,
and the idea that the monodromy can be represented by some cap product
was appeared in the Calabi-Yau case
in works including Gross
[
11
]
,
Zharkov
[
35
]
, and Gross-Siebert
[
10
]
.
In particular, the cap product in
[
10
]
,
called the
radiance obstruction
,
coincides with the eigenwave action for Calabi-Yau complete intersection of Batyrev-Borisov
by a work of
Yamamoto
[
34
]
.
There is also another description using superforms
[
16
]
,
[
21
]
.

The eigenwave action is
not a direct analog of
the complex-geometric monodromy action.
Recall that
the monodromy action
N

on weight graded quotients
\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}\nolimits_{2p}^{W}H^{p+q}(Z_{\infty})

can be written by using
the
Gauss-Manin connection

of the relative de Rham cohomology sheaves,
constructed as the
connecting homomorphism of a short exact sequence
(Katz-Oda
[
17
]
, Deligne-Katz
[
18
]
)

0\to\Omega_{\mathcal{D}}^{1}(\log 0)\otimes R^{*}f_{*}\Omega^{*}_{{\overline{\mathcal{Z}}}/\mathcal{D}}(\log Z_{0})[-1]\to R^{*}f_{*}\Omega^{*}_{\overline{\mathcal{Z}}}(\log Z_{0})\to R^{*}f_{*}\Omega^{*}_{{\overline{\mathcal{Z}}}/\mathcal{D}}(\log Z_{0})\to 0,

where
f\colon\overline{\mathcal{Z}}\to\mathcal{D}
is a strictly semi-stable model over a disk
\mathcal{D}
with special fiber
Z_{0}
.

We shall introduce a tropical Gauss-Manin connection in the completely same way, and show the following.

Theorem 1.4

(
4.13
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective tropical variety regular at infinity.
Then the dual

H^{p,q}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits}(X)\to H^{p-1,q+1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits}(X)

of
the eigenwave action
coincides with the residue of tropical Gauss-Manin connection.

Proof is as follows.
Steenbrink
[
31
]
proved that
the monodromy action

,
given by the Gauss-Manin connection,
can be written as natural maps of
his spectral sequence.
Mikhalkin-Zharkov
[

, Proof of Theorem 3]

(in the realizable case)
and
Amini-Piquerez
[
2
, Corollary 6.21]

(in general)
proved that for a smooth projective tropical varieties,
the eigenwave action can be written as similar maps of their spectral sequence.
Therefore
1.4
follows from a tropical analog of Steenbrink’s result,
which can be proved in the same way.

Appendix A
The sign convention

Signs in homological algebra are very delicate, see e.g.,

[
6
]
,

[
20
]
.
In this appendix,
we clarify our sign convention.
For homological algebra, see
[
33
]
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be the category of abelian sheaves on a topological space,
and
C(\mathscr{A})
the category of complexes in
\mathscr{A}
.
Let
(A,d_{A})
and
(B,d_{B})
be complexes in
C(\mathscr{A})
.
For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
we put
A[r]
the complex defined by

A[r]^{p}:=A^{p+r}
with
d_{A[r]}:=(-1)^{r}d_{A}
.
We have a canonical identification
H^{p+r}(A)=H^{p}(A[r])

given by the identity map of
A^{p+r}
.
For a morphism
f\colon A\to B
of complexes,
we put
f[r]\colon A[r]\to B[r]

the morphism given by
f[r]^{p}:=f^{p+r}
.

We put
A\otimes B
the complex
such that

(A\otimes B)^{p}:=\bigoplus_{i+j=p}A^{i}\otimes B^{j}

and

(A.1)

\displaystyle d_{A\otimes B}|_{A^{i}\otimes B^{j}}:=d_{A}\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{B}+\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{A}\otimes(-1)^{i}d_{B}.

We have a natural isomorphism

(A[r])\otimes(B[s])\cong(A\otimes B)[r+s]

given by

(-1)^{(i-r)s}
on
A^{i}\otimes B^{j}
(
[
6
, p.11 and p.15]
).
We have an isomorphism

A\otimes B\cong B\otimes A

given by

a\otimes b\mapsto(-1)^{pq}b\otimes a

(
a\in A^{p}
,
b\in B^{q}
).

A double complex
B^{p,q}

is equipped with morphisms

d^{\prime}\colon B^{p,q}\to B^{p+1,q}

and

d^{\prime\prime}\colon B^{p,q}\to B^{p,q+1}

such that

d^{\prime}\circ d^{\prime}=0
,

d^{\prime\prime}\circ d^{\prime\prime}=0
, and

d^{\prime}\circ d^{\prime\prime}=d^{\prime\prime}\circ d^{\prime}
.
The associated single complex
s(B^{*,*})

is defined by

s(B^{*,*})^{r}:=\bigoplus_{p+q=r}B^{p,q}

and the sum of

d^{\prime}\colon B^{p,q}\to B^{p+1,q}

and

(-1)^{p}\cdot d^{\prime\prime}\colon B^{p,q}\to B^{p,q+1}
.

Our signs of mapping cones and cylinders
are the same as
[
6
]
,
[
20
]
,
which is different from
[
33
]
.
For a morphism
f\colon A\to B
of complexes,
the mapping cone is

\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\nolimits(f)^{n}:=A^{n+1}\oplus B^{n}

with

d(a,b):=(-d_{A}(a),d_{B}(b)+f(a)).

There are natural morphisms

\displaystyle B\ni

\displaystyle b\mapsto(0,b)\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\nolimits(f),

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\nolimits(f)\ni

\displaystyle(a,b)\mapsto-a\in A[1].

The mapping cylinder is
defined as

\mathop{\mathrm{cyl}}\nolimits(f)^{n}:=A^{n}\oplus A^{n+1}\oplus B^{n}

with

d(a,a^{\prime},b):=(d_{a}(a)-a^{\prime},-d_{A}(a^{\prime}),d_{B}(b)+f(a^{\prime})).

There are natural morphisms

\displaystyle A\ni

\displaystyle a\mapsto(a,0,0)\in\mathop{\mathrm{cyl}}\nolimits(f),

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{cyl}}\nolimits(f)\ni

\displaystyle(a,a^{\prime},b)\to(a^{\prime},b)\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\nolimits(f).

By this sign convention of mapping cylinders and mapping cones,
for
a short exact sequence

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0

in
C(\mathscr{A})
,
connecting homomorphisms
H^{i}(C)\to H^{i+1}(A)

of the snake lemma
and morphisms

H^{i}(C)\to H^{i+1}(A)

(
[
33
, Section 1.5 and Definition 10.1.3]
)
of cohomology groups of
the distinguished triangle

A\to B\to C\to^{[1]}

in the derived category
D(\mathscr{A})

is the same
(see
[
6
, Section 1.3]
).
(If we use
the sign convention of

[
33
]
,
two morphisms differ by
(-1)

(see
[
33
, Exercise 1.5.6]
).)
