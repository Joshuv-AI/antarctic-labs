Dynamical dimer structure factor of the triangular S = 1 / 2 S=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}} Heisenberg antiferromagnet
Markus Drescher, Laurens Vanderstraeten, Roderich Moessner, Frank Pollmann, Johannes Knolle

Abstract
The dynamical dimer structure factor is an observable probing spin-singlet excitations of quantum magnets distinct from those commonly studied by the spin structure factor. We report the dimer response for the extended spin- 1 / 2 1/2 antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model on the triangular lattice using large-scale GPU-accelerated matrix-product-state simulations. We investigate the ordered phases with 120 ∘ 120^{\circ} coplanar, collinear stripe, and tetrahedral spin order, as well as candidate quantum spin-liquid (QSL) regimes, comprising an expected gapless U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) Dirac QSL and a chiral QSL at finite spin-scalar-chirality coupling. In the ordered phases, we find low-energy modes below the onset of the two-magnon continuum illustrating avoided quasiparticle decay. Within the candidate gapless QSL, we observe absolute dispersion minima at momenta of half the Brillouin zone corners, X ≡ K / 2 X\equiv K/2 , in agreement with field-theory predictions that singlet monopole excitations of the U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) Dirac spin liquid become gapless at these points. Thus, the high-resolution dynamical dimer response provides support for a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) Dirac QSL with singlet monopole excitations.

Numerical protocol

Whereas the MPS methodology has been applied in different works
93
;
82
;
20
;
107
;
44
;
54
in the last years for estimating the spin dynamical structure factor (DSF), and has become an invaluable tool to support experiments in inelastic neutron scattering, here we apply it for the first time to the more demanding case of resolving the dimer DSF.

The spin bilinear
\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}
, which appears in the projector onto the spin-singlet component of a bond
(i,j)
, forms the fundamental building block of the Loudon-Fleury operator describing Raman scattering in the nearest-neighbor Heisenberg model (up to polarization-dependent prefactors)
27
;
22
;
81
;
71
;
106
, as well as of the indirect non-spin-flip RIXS channel
2
.
As a related quantity, capturing the spin-singlet fluctuations, we introduce the dimer operator around a central site
i
defined as

\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{i}=\frac{1}{N_{\delta}}\sum_{\delta}\left(\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i+\delta}-\braket{\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i+\delta}}\right).

(2)

The sum runs over all nearest-neighbor sites of
i
labeled by
\delta
with
N_{\delta}=6
being the coordination number of the lattice. Note that the subtraction of the dimer expectation value, which is non-vanishing, ensures that the dimer operator probes the excitation spectrum of the ground state via fluctuations around it.
To this purpose, we have to compute the connected time-resolved dimer-dimer correlation function

C_{D}^{\mathrm{conn.}}(i,j;t)=\braket{\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{i}(t)\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{j}(0)}\,.

(3)

The static dimer structure factor is straight-forwardly obtained from the equal-time dimer correlations:

\chi_{D}({\mathbf{k}})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j}e^{-\mathrm{i}{\mathbf{k}}\cdot({\mathbf{r}}_{i}-{\mathbf{r}}_{j})}\braket{\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{j}}\,,

(4)

where
N
denotes the number of lattice sites.
Exploiting the translational invariance of the system, we obtain the dynamical dimer structure factor via

S_{D}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)=\int\mathrm{d}t\sum_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}\omega t-\mathrm{i}{\mathbf{k}}\cdot({\mathbf{r}}_{j}-{\mathbf{r}}_{j_{c}})}\braket{\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{j}(t)\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{j_{c}}(0)}\,.

(5)

;
102
;
63

1
) using
U(1)
charge conservation

;
84
;
35
.
To this end, the two-dimensional lattice is wrapped onto a cylinder by closing the boundary condition along its circumference periodically
88
.
We apply the
\mathrm{YC}6
cylinder geometry
39

The perturbed ground-state MPS is then time evolved numerically using the matrix-product-operator WII representation of the time evolution operator

;
69

L_{x}=51

120^{\circ}

Outlook

Leveraging GPU-accelerated large-scale MPS simulations, we obtain high-resolution spectra of the dynamical dimer response, paving a new route to probe excitations above exotic ground states in frustrated magnets. The approach yields distinctive signatures that enable discrimination between competing field theories and connects directly to experiments, including momentum-resolved RIXS and Raman scattering, thereby providing a valuable framework for quantitative studies of antiferromagnetic models with QSL regimes beyond analytic control. The resulting spectra serve as a direct guide for future experiments, indicating which features to target and how to distinguish between underlying theoretical descriptions.

Appendix A
Details on the numerical procedure and the ground state optimization

For the ground state optimization, we apply the density matrix renormalization group algorithm with infinite boundary conditions (iDMRG). Concretely, we use the
\mathrm{YC}6
cylinder geometry, indicating that the system is invariant under translation by
L\cdot\mathbf{a}_{2}=6\,\mathbf{a}_{2}
with the Bravais vectors
\mathrm{a}_{2}=(1/2,\sqrt{3}/2)^{T}
and
\mathrm{a}_{1}=(1,0)^{T}
(cf. Fig.
1
b
). The iDMRG variational optimization is performed on a unit cell with three rings of circumference
L=6
and a maximum bond dimension of
\chi=2000
.
We apply Abelian charge conservation
35
;
36
, i.e., the total
S_{z}
quantum number is preserved during the simulation.
Whereas the ground state representations of the ordered phases are stabilized straight-forwardly on the cylinder, the paramagnetic regimes exhibit two distinct topological sectors on cylinders with an even number of legs.
In the case of the
J_{1}-J_{2}
quantum spin liquid (QSL), we focus on the energetically favorable odd sector
115
;
40
;
39
—or low-energy sector
44
—that exhibits a more isotropic correlation structure.
In the chiral spin-liquid phase, we perform the dynamics in the directly accessible trivial sector that comes with a non-degenerate entanglement spectrum consistent with the Wess-Zumino-Witten conformal field theory description of the gapless edge mode (cf. Ref.
89
;
17
).
The ground state approximation used for the quasiparticle excitation ansatz
31
;
92
was obtained from the variational uniform MPS (VUMPS) algorithm
32
;
110
, featuring
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-symmetric tensors.

The MPS with infinite boundary conditions that represents the ground state of the system for given set of parameters can be used to build enlarged unit cells by periodically stacking them. These systems serve as the basis for the dynamical evolution. For improved computational efficiency in cutting-edge simulations, it is advantageous to terminate the infinite boundary conditions by a suitable eigenvector, thereby mapping them to a finite system with open boundary conditions (cf. Refs.
20
;
21
).

The time evolution is initialized by applying a single-bond operator

\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i+\delta}-\braket{\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i+\delta}}

(6)

onto the nearest-neighbor bond
(\mathbf{r}_{i},\,\mathbf{r}_{i}+\bm{\delta})
with
\bm{\delta}\in\{\pm\mathbf{a}_{1},\pm\mathbf{a}_{2},\pm\mathbf{a}_{3}=\pm(\mathbf{a}_{1}-\mathbf{a}_{2})\}
denoting the displacement vector of the nearest-neighbor pairs.
We choose a site
j_{c}
in the center of the system and prepare distinct initial wave functions by applying the bond operator on bond
(j_{c},j_{c}+\delta)
for all choices of
\delta
. In order to prevent numerical artifacts arising from the anisotropies of the cylinder setup, we do not impose assumptions about the rotation symmetries of the system, but perform all time evolutions independently. As the time-step size, we use
\delta t=0.04\,J_{1}
. After each
N_{\mathrm{steps}}=5
time steps, we compute the time-dependent dimer-dimer correlation.
To avoid numerical artifacts—such as energy shifts causing spurious phases in the wave function due to Trotter errors and finite bond-dimension effects in the variational application of the MPO to the MPS—we simultaneously time-evolve the unperturbed ground state and use this reference state to compute the correlations. Eventually, the bond correlations from the six individual time evolutions (and the additional ground state evolution) can be combined to obtain the full dynamical dimer-dimer correlations for the operator in Eq.
2
.
Note that the subtraction of the expectation value in Eq. (
2
) and the application of the full bond operator in Eq.
6
onto the ground state in the form of an MPO proved essential for the numerical accuracy of the simulations.

Having obtained the dynamical dimer correlation function, we can invoke the standard recipe to transform the data into the corresponding dynamical structure factor. After Fourier transforming the spatial indices to momentum space (cf. Fig.
1
c
for the accessible momentum cuts on this cylinder geometry), we apply a linear extrapolation of the time series for each momentum and find eventually the spectral function
S_{D}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)
from a convolution of the signal with a Gaussian window function.
We choose a value
\alpha
of the Gaussian envelope at the maximum evolution time
t_{\mathrm{sim}}
, defining the broadening via
\sigma=\sqrt{t_{\mathrm{sim}}^{2}/(-2\ln(\alpha))}
. Typical values are for instance
\alpha=10^{-6}
, i.e.,
\sigma\approx 11.4\,J_{1}
for
t_{\mathrm{sim}}=60\,J_{1}
.
In order to improve the resolution in momentum space, we can apply an interpolation scheme prior to performing the temporal Fourier transformation (for details consult Ref.
21
), which has been applied for the numerical data presented in this work.

The large-scale dynamical simulations have been performed on a cluster setup that combines four NVIDIA A100 GPUs (80 GB memory each) and 1 TB of system memory on each node alongside two Intel Xeon Platinum 8360Y CPUs (36 cores at 2.40GHz).
We use a self-developed tensor-network library that stores the data tensors in sparse form by exploiting the
U(1)
symmetry. Whenever possible, the tensor blocks are retained in GPU memory, subject to the available memory capacity.
We apply a memory management scheme that transfers data asynchronously between the system memory and the GPUs if necessary. In the
120^{\circ}
phase and the candidate QSL at
J_{2}/J_{1}=0.125
, we coupled two GPUs per simulation. In the other cases, we used four GPUs in parallel and kept data such as site tensors and contracted environments that were not immediately required in the main memory.

Appendix D
Spin-wave theory

D.1
General solution of the energy dispersion in linear spin-wave theory

The semiclassical spin-wave approximation assumes that in an magnetically ordered ground state of a spin system, quantum fluctuations cause also slight deviations from the classical spin configuration. These deviations can be treated perturbatively. In this paragraph, we want to develop a general form of the resulting Hamiltonian and its solution within linear spin-wave theory.
Starting from a spin Hamiltonian such as in Eq.
1
, the canonical Holstein-Primakoff transformation allows us to transition from the spin operators (that are aligned with the z-axis of a locally rotated reference frame) to bosonic operators:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i}^{z}

\displaystyle=S-\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i}^{\phantom{\dagger}}

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i}^{+}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2S}\,\sqrt{1-\frac{\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i}^{\phantom{\dagger}}}{2S}}\hat{a}_{i}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\approx\sqrt{2S}\,\hat{a}_{i}^{\phantom{\dagger}}

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i}^{-}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2S}\,\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\sqrt{1-\frac{\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i}^{\phantom{\dagger}}}{2S}}\approx\sqrt{2S}\,\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}

(10)

The local spin operators
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\alpha}
are aligned with the z-axis of the local frame and are related to the spin operator
\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}
in the global frame by rotation matrices
R_{i}
via

\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}=R_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}\,.

(11)

Here,
\mathcal{S}_{i}=(\mathcal{S}_{i}^{x},\mathcal{S}_{i}^{y},\mathcal{S}_{i}^{z})^{T}
is the vector of spin operators in the local frame at site
i
.
Generally, we find a transformed Hamiltonian in terms of the bosonic operators that acquires a form as follows:

\hat{H}=H_{0}+\sum_{i}A_{i}\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\sum_{(i,j)}\left[C_{ij}\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+D_{ij}\hat{a}_{i}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\hat{a}_{j}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right]\,.

(12)

Here,
H_{0}
and the
A_{i}
are real constants and the sum
\sum_{(i,j)}
runs over pairs of sites
(i,j)
that can be assumed to be distinct, i.e.,
i\neq j
.

We can now Fourier transform the operators from discrete spatial coordinates
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}_{i}\,\hat{=}\,i

to momentum space as

\hat{a}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{r}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\hat{a}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}}\,.

(13)

The resulting Hamiltonian in quadratic order can be brought to the following form:

H_{2}=\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}}\left[A_{{\mathbf{k}}}^{\phantom{*}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}-\frac{1}{2}\left[B_{\mathbf{k}}^{*}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{-{\mathbf{k}}}^{\dagger}+B_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{*}}\hat{a}_{-{\mathbf{k}}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\right]\right]\,.

(14)

The coefficients derive directly from the hopping constants in real space in Eq. (
12
) via

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=A+\sum_{\bm{\delta}}2\,\mathfrak{Re}\left\{C_{\bm{\delta}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\bm{\delta}}\right\}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle B_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\bm{\delta}}2\,D_{\bm{\delta}}\,\cos\left(\mathbf{k}\cdot\bm{\delta}\right)\,.

(16)

We assume here that the coefficients
A_{i}\equiv A\in\mathbb{R}\forall i
are site independent and that
C_{\bm{\delta}}
,
D_{\bm{\delta}}
only depend on the distance vector
\bm{\delta}=\mathbf{r}_{j}-\mathbf{r}_{i}
between two sites, not the position of the sites within the unit cell of the translationally invariant lattice, i.e., they need to be independent of the sublattice index of the magnetic order.
In general, the resulting
A_{\mathbf{k}}
are real while the
B_{\mathbf{k}}
can be complex.
One can always symmetrize the momentum-dependent coefficients such that they become even functions of momentum
\mathbf{k}
:
A_{\mathbf{k}}=A_{-\mathbf{k}}
and
B_{\mathbf{k}}=B_{-\mathbf{k}}
.

The Bogoliubov transformation

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{*}}\hat{b}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+v_{\mathbf{k}}^{*}\hat{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}}}^{\dagger}

(17)

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{-{\mathbf{k}}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=u_{\mathbf{k}}^{*}\hat{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}}}^{\dagger}+v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{*}}\hat{b}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}

(18)

allows us to diagonalize the quadratic Hamiltonian.

The linear spin-wave dispersion then yields

\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}=\sqrt{A_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}-\left|B_{\mathbf{k}}\right|^{2}}\,.

(19)

Writing the coefficient
B_{\mathbf{k}}
in polar form as
B_{\mathbf{k}}=\left|B_{\mathbf{k}}\right|e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}}
, the Bogoliubov coefficients are given as

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{A_{\mathbf{k}}+\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}}}{2\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}}\sqrt{\frac{A_{\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}}\,.

(21)

Hence, the quadratic Hamiltonian reduces to

H_{2}=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k}}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\left(\hat{b}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{A_{\mathbf{k}}}{2}\,.

(22)

The primed sum indicates that the Goldstone modes are excluded from the summation. For those momenta, the Bogoliubov transformation is not well defined.

The dimer correlation function

Eventually, we want to compute the connected dimer-dimer correlations

\displaystyle C_{D}^{\mathrm{conn.}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{\delta}^{2}}\sum_{\bm{\delta},\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}C^{\mathrm{conn.}}_{\mathrm{bonds}}(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta};\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})\,,

(39)

where
N_{\delta}
is the coordination number of the lattice and

C^{\mathrm{conn.}}_{\mathrm{bonds}}(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta};\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})=\braket{\left(\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\left(\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)}-\braket{\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}}\braket{\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}

(40)

denotes the connected correlation on bonds
(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})
and
(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime})
.
After performing a rotation into the local frames, expressed as
\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{r}}=R_{L(\mathbf{r})}\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{r}}
and with the resulting matrix
M^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}:=R_{L(\mathbf{r})}^{T}R_{L(\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})}^{\phantom{T}}
(
L(\mathbf{r})
denoting the sublattice index of site
\mathbf{r}
), we can write the connected four-spin correlation as

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{conn.}}_{\mathrm{bonds}}(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta};\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})/S^{2}

\displaystyle=M_{13}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{13}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}}+M_{31}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{31}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{x}S_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{x}}

\displaystyle+M_{13}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{31}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{x}}+M_{31}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{13}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}}

\displaystyle+M_{23}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{23}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{y}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{y}}+M_{32}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{32}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{y}S_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{y}}

\displaystyle+M_{23}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{32}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{y}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{y}}+M_{32}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{23}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{y}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{y}}

\displaystyle+M_{13}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{23}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{y}}+M_{23}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{13}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{y}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}}

\displaystyle+M_{31}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{32}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{y}}+M_{32}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{31}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{y}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{x}}

\displaystyle+M_{13}^{{\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta}}}M_{32}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{y}}+M_{32}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{13}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{y}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}}

\displaystyle+M_{23}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{31}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{y}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{x}}+M_{31}^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}M_{23}^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{y}}+\mathcal{O}(1)\,.

(41)

The expectation values evaluate to

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S}{2N}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}\left|u_{\mathbf{k}}+v_{\mathbf{k}}\right|^{2}\,,

(42)

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{y}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{y}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S}{2N}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}\left|u_{\mathbf{k}}-v_{\mathbf{k}}\right|^{2}\,,

(43)

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{y}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\frac{S}{2N}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}\left(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{*}}v_{\mathbf{k}}^{*}-v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{*}}u_{\mathbf{k}}^{*}-1\right)\,,

(44)

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{y}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}}

\displaystyle=\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{y}}^{*}

(45)

To arrive at Eq. (
44
), we applied the identity
|u_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}-|v_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}=1
. The terms mixing
\mathcal{S}^{x}
or
\mathcal{S}^{y}
with
\mathcal{S}^{z}
vanish at leading order as they contain an odd number of bosonic operators. Finally using Wick’s theorem, the
zz
correlator becomes

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{z}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{z}}

\displaystyle=S^{2}-S\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})}-S\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}+\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}\,.

(46)

The expectation value of the occupation number operator evaluates as

\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})}=\braket{\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}}\hat{a}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{r}}}=\frac{1}{N}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k}}|v_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\,.

(47)

The density-density correlator can be written down in the form

\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}=\braket{\hat{n}}^{2}+\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}_{c}\,,

(48)

where the connected part is

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}e^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}\left(|v_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}|u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}|^{2}+v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{*}}u_{\mathbf{k}}^{*}u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\phantom{*}}v_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{*}\right)\,.

(49)

Connected here means that in the Wick expansion, we only keep terms that connect the two different sites
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
. Note that when this expression Eq. (
49
) is evaluated on a computer, the two sums over
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
can be separated to reduce the computational complexity from
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathbf{k}}^{2})
to
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathbf{k}})
.
The time dependence can be introduced trivially via

\hat{b}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=e^{-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}t}\,\hat{b}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}}\,,

(50)

resulting in the introduction of time-dependent exponentials such as in the expressions

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{x}(t)\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{S}{2N}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}e^{-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}t}\left|u_{\mathbf{k}}+v_{\mathbf{k}}\right|^{2}

(51)

and

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{n}(t,\mathbf{r})\hat{n}(0,\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}e^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}e^{-\mathrm{i}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}})t}\left(|v_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}|u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}|^{2}+v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\phantom{*}}u_{\mathbf{k}}^{*}u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\phantom{*}}v_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{*}\right)\,.

(52)

Appendix F
Higher-order results for the coplanar spin orders

For the coplanar spin orders, we chose a convention where the spins lie in the
x
-
z
plane of the global reference frame. Therefore, all matrix elements
M^{(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta})}_{nm}
,
M^{(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}_{nm}
that involve a
y
-component (i.e.,
n=2
or
m=2
) vanish.
As a result, the formula for the dimer-dimer correlations at order
\mathcal{O}(S^{3})
simplifies drastically:

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{conn.}}_{D}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{S^{2}}{N_{\delta}^{2}}\sum_{\bm{\delta},\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\left[\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}}+\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{x}}-\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}^{x}}-\braket{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}^{x}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}^{x}}\right]\cdot\sin\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\sin\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(S^{2})\,.

(53)

Whereas in the case of the
120^{\circ}
order, this expression gives indeed the leading-order contribution to the dimer-dimer correlations in spin-wave theory, the angular prefactors
\sin\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\sin\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)
vanish for the stripe order.
Consequently, the leading-order contribution to the dimer-dimer correlations in the stripe phase arises from higher-order terms in the Holstein-Primakoff expansion.
Neglecting higher-order terms in the ground-state approximation to the Hamiltonian, we find from the expansion of the operators the following full expression for the connected bond correlations for a coplanar order:

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{conn.}}_{\mathrm{bonds}}(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta};\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)\left[\braket{\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}\right]

\displaystyle+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}

\displaystyle+\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\left[\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}\right]

\displaystyle+\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)\left[\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}\right]

\displaystyle+\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)\left[\braket{\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}\right]

\displaystyle+\sin\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\sin\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)\left[\braket{\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\braket{\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}-\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{z}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}\right]\,.

(54)

The subscript
\braket{\ldots}_{c}
here denotes that we only consider the connected contributions to the expectation value that involves Wick contractions that connect the two dimer pairs
(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})
and
(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime})
.
We give here the explicit result at order
\mathcal{O}(S^{2})
. It reads:

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{conn.}}_{\mathrm{bonds}}(\mathbf{r},\bm{\delta};\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\bm{\delta}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)\Big\{S^{2}\left[\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}_{c}+\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}_{c}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad+\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}_{c}+\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}_{c}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle+\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\Big\{\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-S\left[\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}+\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}\right]\Big\}

\displaystyle+\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)\Big\{\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-S\left[\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}_{c}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}_{c}\right]\Big\}

\displaystyle-\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\cos\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)S\Big\{\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}+\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}_{c}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}_{c}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\sin\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\right)\sin\left(\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)\Big\{\left[S^{2}-2S\braket{\hat{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left[\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}-\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}-\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}\right]\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-S\left[\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}_{c}+\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}_{c}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\braket{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}}_{c}-\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}_{c}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}-\braket{\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}}\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}\right]\Big\}+\mathcal{O}(S)\,.

(55)

These terms can be readily implemented and evaluated numerically.

We find for instance the following explicit correlation

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{n}(t,\mathbf{r})\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{S}{2N^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}e^{-\mathrm{i}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}})t}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\left(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}-u_{\mathbf{k}}v_{\mathbf{k}}\right)\left(v_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}-u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}v_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right)+\left(v_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}-u_{\mathbf{k}}v_{\mathbf{k}}\right)\left(u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}-u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}v_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right)\right]\,,

(56)

in contrast to

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{n}(t,\mathbf{r})\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}}(t)\mathcal{S}^{x}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}}_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{S}{2N^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}t}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot(-\bm{\delta})}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\bm{\delta}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\left(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+u_{\mathbf{k}}v_{\mathbf{k}}\right)\left(v_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}v_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right)+\left(v_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+u_{\mathbf{k}}v_{\mathbf{k}}\right)\left(u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}v_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right)\right]\,.

(57)

Note that both the energy
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}
and the Bogoliubov coefficients
u_{\mathbf{k}}
,
v_{\mathbf{k}}
are even functions in the momentum argument. The latter coefficients have been chosen real-valued for the coplanar spin order, hence we drop the complex conjugation in the expressions above.
These considerations conclude our discussion of the semiclassical analytics as all formulas used for the evaluation of the dynamical dimer structure factor in spin-wave theory have been derived.
