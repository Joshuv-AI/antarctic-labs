Reducibility of spectral curves of finite Jacobi pencils
Boris Shapiro

Abstract
. We consider finite pencils of Jacobi matrices J n ​ ( w ) = A + w ​ B , J_{n}(w)=A+wB, where A A is diagonal and B B is tridiagonal with zero diagonal. The spectral curve is the affine plane curve χ n ​ ( λ , w ) = det ( λ ​ I + J n ​ ( w ) ) = 0 . \chi_{n}(\lambda,w)=\det(\lambda I+J_{n}(w))=0. The main question is to describe when this curve is reducible. We prove generic irreducibility for fixed pairwise distinct diagonal entries and discuss several elementary reducibility mechanisms. Besides disconnected Jacobi chains, constant eigenvalue branches, and reflection-symmetric components, one must also take into account reducibility caused by scalar diagonal blocks. We formulate a reducibility conjecture and record low-dimensional evidence and counterexamples to several overly optimistic classifications. A central point of the picture is a codimension-growth principle: apart from the cutting divisors b i = 0 b_{i}=0 , genuinely connected primitive reducibility should move to higher and higher codimension as the size of the chain grows.

1.
Introduction

Let

J_{n}(w)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&wb_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
wb_{1}&a_{2}&wb_{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&wb_{2}&a_{3}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&wb_{n-1}\\
0&\cdots&0&wb_{n-1}&a_{n}\end{pmatrix}

be a finite Jacobi pencil. Our considerations of such pencils are motivated by similar infinite-dimensional objects studied in
[
1
,
3
]
, see discussions below. We use the sign convention

\chi_{n}(\lambda,w)=\det(\lambda I+J_{n}(w)).

The affine curve
\chi_{n}(\lambda,w)=0
will be called the
spectral curve
of the pencil. Since
w
enters only through the off-diagonal entries,
\chi_{n}
is an even polynomial in
w
. We often write

t=w^{2},\qquad P_{n}(\lambda,t)=\chi_{n}(\lambda,w).

Then
P_{n}
is the continuant determined by

P_{0}=1,\qquad P_{1}=\lambda+a_{1},\qquad P_{k}=(\lambda+a_{k})P_{k-1}-tb_{k-1}^{2}P_{k-2}.

(1)

The problem studied in this note is the following.

For which parameters
a_{i},b_{i}
is
\chi_{n}(\lambda,w)
reducible in
\mathbb{C}[\lambda,w]
?

There are several elementary mechanisms.
First, if some
b_{i}=0
, the chain splits into two smaller Jacobi chains. Secondly,
\chi_{n}
can have a constant eigenvalue branch, i.e. a factor
\lambda+a
independent of
w
. Thirdly, a component of the chain may be invariant under reversal. Then the Jacobi matrix decomposes into symmetric and anti-symmetric subspaces for the reversal, giving a factorization of the characteristic polynomial. Finally, if a connected block has scalar diagonal part, say
a_{r}=\cdots=a_{s}
, then its characteristic polynomial is the characteristic polynomial of a constant tridiagonal matrix evaluated at
\lambda+a_{r}
and
w
; over
\mathbb{C}
this usually splits into linear factors.

The goal of the paper is to collect these elementary mechanisms, prove generic irreducibility, and formulate a conjectural picture.

This finite-dimensional problem was motivated by the work of Djakov and
Mityagin on spectral Riemann surfaces and trace formulae for infinite
tridiagonal operator pencils. In that setting one studies operators of the
form
L+zB
, where
L
is diagonal and
B
is tridiagonal, and the spectral
Riemann surface records the analytic continuation and monodromy of the
eigenvalue branches. The present paper studies a finite algebraic analogue:
for a finite Jacobi pencil we ask when the polynomial spectral curve

\chi_{n}(\lambda,w)=0
is reducible. Thus the reducibility problem considered
below may be viewed as a finite-dimensional counterpart of the irreducibility
questions for spectral Riemann surfaces in
[
1
]
; the subsequent
work on convergence radii and related tridiagonal spectral problems gives
additional background for this point of view
[
2
,
3
]
.

2.
Generic irreducibility

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are pairwise distinct. Then, for fixed
a_{1},

\ldots,

a_{n}
, the polynomial
\chi_{n}(\lambda,w)
is irreducible in
\mathbb{C}[\lambda,w]
for all
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1})
outside a proper algebraic subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
.

Proof.

For fixed
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
, reducibility of the monic polynomial
\chi_{n}
is a Zariski closed condition in the variables
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1}
. Indeed, for each possible degree of a non-trivial factor one writes an undetermined factorization and eliminates the coefficients of the two factors. It remains to exhibit one point in the parameter space at which
\chi_{n}
is irreducible.

We use a standard monodromy argument. Choose non-zero
b_{i}
outside a finite union of proper algebraic hypersurfaces specified below. At
w=0
the
n
branches of the covering

\chi_{n}(\lambda,w)=0\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{w}

are the distinct points
\lambda=-a_{i}
. Fix an adjacent pair
i,i+1
. If all couplings except
b_{i}
are set to zero, the two corresponding sheets are described by the elementary
2\times 2
block

\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}w\\
b_{i}w&a_{i+1}\end{pmatrix},

and its two eigenvalues have square-root ramification at the two points

w=\pm{a_{i}-a_{i+1}\over 2b_{i}}.

For sufficiently small but non-zero neighbouring couplings this simple ramification persists by the implicit function theorem. Avoiding the finitely many resultants which would make this branch point collide with another ramification point or with a third sheet, the local monodromy around it is the transposition of the two sheets analytically continued from
-a_{i}
and
-a_{i+1}
.

Doing this for each adjacent pair and then avoiding the union of the corresponding exceptional hypersurfaces, the monodromy group contains all adjacent transpositions

(1\ 2),(2\ 3),\ldots,(n-1\ n).

These transpositions generate
S_{n}
, so the monodromy is transitive. Hence
\chi_{n}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{C}(w)
. Since
\chi_{n}
is monic in
\lambda
, Gauss’ lemma gives irreducibility in
\mathbb{C}[\lambda,w]
. Therefore the reducible locus is a proper algebraic subset.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
b_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
. Then consecutive continuants are coprime:

\gcd(\chi_{n},\chi_{n-1})=1

in
\mathbb{C}[\lambda,w]
.

Proof.

The continuant recurrence gives

\chi_{n}=(\lambda+a_{n})\chi_{n-1}-b_{n-1}^{2}w^{2}\chi_{n-2}.

If a nonconstant polynomial
d
divides both
\chi_{n}
and
\chi_{n-1}
,
then
d
divides
w^{2}\chi_{n-2}
. Since

\chi_{n-1}(\lambda,0)=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(\lambda+a_{j}),

the polynomial
\chi_{n-1}
is not divisible by
w
. Hence
d
divides

\chi_{n-2}
. Repeating the argument inductively yields that
d
divides

\chi_{1}=\lambda+a_{1}
and
\chi_{0}=1
, impossible.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

For connected Jacobi chains the spectral curves

\chi_{n}(\lambda,w)=0

and

\chi_{n-1}(\lambda,w)=0

have no common irreducible component.

Corollary 2.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, the Galois group of

\chi_{n}(\lambda,w)
over
\mathbb{C}(w)
is generically the full symmetric group

S_{n}
.

Proof.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
shows that the monodromy group
contains all adjacent transpositions. Hence it equals
S_{n}
.
∎

3.
Elementary reducibility mechanisms

Put
c_{i}=b_{i}^{2}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The following conditions imply reducibility of
\chi_{n}
.

(1)

If
b_{i}=0
, then

\chi_{n}=\chi_{i}^{(1,\ldots,i)}\,\chi_{n-i}^{(i+1,\ldots,n)},

where the two factors are the characteristic polynomials of the two resulting smaller Jacobi chains.

(2)

If
\chi_{n}(-a,w)\equiv 0
, then
\lambda+a
divides
\chi_{n}
.

(3)

Suppose that a connected block indexed by
r,\ldots,s
is reflection-symmetric up to diagonal sign changes, i.e.

a_{r+k}=a_{s-k},\qquad b_{r+k}^{2}=b_{s-k-1}^{2}

for all admissible
k
. Then the characteristic polynomial of this block factors as the product of its restrictions to the symmetric and anti-symmetric subspaces of the reversal.

(4)

If a connected block
I=[r,s]
has scalar diagonal part,

a_{r}=a_{r+1}=\cdots=a_{s}=a,

then

J_{I}(w)=aI+wB_{I},\qquad\chi_{I}(\lambda,w)=\det((\lambda+a)I+wB_{I}).

Consequently, over
\mathbb{C}
,

\chi_{I}(\lambda,w)=\prod_{\mu\in\operatorname{Spec}(B_{I})}(\lambda+a+\mu w),

with multiplicities. In particular, every scalar diagonal block of length at least two gives a reducible spectral curve.

Proof.

The first statement is immediate from block diagonal decomposition. The second statement is the factor theorem. For the third statement, after changing signs of basis vectors one may assume
b_{r+k}=b_{s-k-1}
. The corresponding Jacobi block commutes with the reversal operator. Hence it preserves the eigenspaces of the reversal with eigenvalues
+1
and
-1
, and its characteristic polynomial is the product of the two restricted characteristic polynomials. The fourth statement follows by diagonalizing the constant matrix
B_{I}
over
\mathbb{C}
.
∎

4.
A refined reducibility conjecture and codimension of strata

It is useful to formulate the expected answer not merely as a list of
factorization mechanisms, but as a description of the strata of the
reducible locus. We use the following terminology. A Jacobi chain is

connected
if all its couplings are non-zero. A factorization is

primitive
if it is not obtained from a proper disconnected subchain.

Definition 4.1
.

For a connected block
I=[r,s]
of length
m=s-r+1
, we say that
I
is

palindromic
if

a_{r+k}=a_{s-k},\qquad b_{r+k}^{2}=b_{s-k-1}^{2}

for all admissible
k
. We say that
I
has a
constant branch
if
for some
j\in I
its characteristic polynomial
P_{I}(\lambda,t)
satisfies

P_{I}(-a_{j},t)\equiv 0.

Conjecture 4.2

(Amended reducibility conjecture)
.

Let
J_{n}(w)
be a finite Jacobi pencil and let

\chi_{n}(\lambda,w)=\det(\lambda I+J_{n}(w)).

Every reducible factorization of
\chi_{n}
is expected to be obtained by
iterating the following operations on connected blocks:

(1)

cutting the chain at a zero coupling
b_{i}=0
;

(2)

extracting a constant branch
\lambda+a_{j}
from a connected block for
which
P_{I}(-a_{j},t)\equiv 0
;

(3)

decomposing a palindromic connected block into its symmetric and
anti-symmetric parts under reversal;

(4)

decomposing a scalar-diagonal connected block
a_{r}=\cdots=a_{s}
by
factoring the characteristic polynomial of its constant off-diagonal part.

After all these operations have been exhausted, the remaining factors are
expected to be irreducible in
\mathbb{C}[\lambda,w]
.

This amended formulation includes scalar diagonal blocks, which are a genuine
source of connected reducibility and cannot be ignored. The following table records
the expected codimensions of the basic strata in the full parameter space

\mathbb{C}^{2n-1}_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1}}.

Here “expected codimension” means codimension at a generic point of the
corresponding stratum. For a constant branch the polynomial

P_{I}(-a_{j},t)
has zero constant term and degree at most
\lfloor m/2\rfloor

in
t
; hence the condition is given by the vanishing of at most

\lfloor m/2\rfloor
coefficients. These equations can become dependent

from the generic constant-branch stratum.

Conjecture 4.3

(No connected hypersurfaces)
.

For every
n\geq 4
, every codimension-one component of the reducible locus
is one of the cutting hyperplanes
b_{i}=0
. Equivalently, the reducible
locus restricted to the connected stratum
b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}\neq 0
has
codimension at least two. Scalar-diagonal strata have codimension
m-1
for
blocks of length
m
and therefore do not contradict this prediction for

m\geq 3
.

The preceding table suggests the following organizing principle. The
cutting equations
b_{i}=0
are the only visible divisorial components of the
reducible locus. All other elementary mechanisms impose a number of
independent conditions which grows with the length of the connected block:
a palindromic block of length
m
has expected codimension
m-1
, a scalar
block has codimension
m-1
, and a generic constant branch has expected
codimension
\lfloor m/2\rfloor
. Thus, for a full connected chain, every
currently known non-trivial reducibility mechanism becomes more exceptional
as
n
grows.

One has to formulate this statement with some care. If a reducible example
is induced from a fixed small block and then embedded into a longer chain by
adding cuts, its codimension need not grow with the ambient size. Such
examples are not genuinely new
n
-dimensional phenomena. This motivates the
following primitive version.

Conjecture 4.4

(Primitive codimension growth)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\rm prim}
denote the union of irreducible components of
the connected reducibility locus

b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}\neq 0

which are not induced from proper contiguous subchains and are not obtained
from scalar-diagonal degenerations. Then

\operatorname{codim}\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\rm prim}\longrightarrow\infty\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

More precisely, outside special coincidence strata among the diagonal
entries one expects the estimate

\operatorname{codim}\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\rm prim}\geq\left\lfloor{n\over 2}\right\rfloor.

Equivalently, the expected asymptotic picture is that reducibility of large
Jacobi pencils is dominated by the boundary divisor
\{b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}=0\}
. Primitive reducibility inside the connected stratum should be
a high-codimension phenomenon. This is the main structural reason for
separating the trivial cutting strata from the genuinely connected
reducibility problem.

For later reference we spell out the consequence in degree eight, see §
5
. On the
full connected chain, the known mechanisms give the following expected
codimensions:

\begin{array}[]{c|c}\text{mechanism}&\text{expected codimension}\\
\hline\cr\text{constant branch}&4\\
\text{palindromic }4+4\text{ splitting}&7\\
\text{scalar diagonal chain }a_{1}=\cdots=a_{8}&7\\
\text{primitive non-palindromic }4+4\text{ splitting}&\text{expected absent, or high codimension.}\end{array}

Thus the degree-eight calculations below are consistent with the principle
that, for
n\geq 4
, no connected codimension-one component should occur and
that the only codimension-one reducibility strata are the cutting hyperplanes

b_{i}=0
.

The above conjecture is compatible with the explicit scalar-diagonal
examples and with the generic irreducibility theorem.

5.
Low-dimensional evidence and first test cases

The purpose of this section is not to give a complete case-by-case
classification in every low degree. Such a classification quickly becomes
uneven unless one first stratifies the parameter space by the equality pattern
of the diagonal entries. Instead we record the clean facts which are used in
the sequel and separate them from computational evidence. Throughout this
section we put

t=w^{2},\qquad P_{n}(\lambda,t)=\chi_{n}(\lambda,w).

Two qualifications should be kept in mind. First, if some
b_{i}=0
, the chain
splits and reducibility is immediate. We therefore emphasize the connected
case
b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}\neq 0
. Secondly, if some diagonal entries coincide,
extra reducibility may occur; the scalar-diagonal block

a_{r}=\cdots=a_{s}
is the simplest example. Thus the most transparent
classification statements are those made either on the distinct-diagonal
stratum or after explicitly allowing scalar and partially scalar blocks.

5.1.
Degrees two and three

Proposition 5.1
.

For

\chi_{2}=(\lambda+a_{1})(\lambda+a_{2})-b_{1}^{2}w^{2}

the spectral curve is reducible if and only if either
b_{1}=0
or

a_{1}=a_{2}
.

Proof.

If
b_{1}=0
, then the chain is disconnected. If
a_{1}=a_{2}=a
, then

\chi_{2}=(\lambda+a-b_{1}w)(\lambda+a+b_{1}w).

Conversely, a reducible quadratic form in the two variables

\lambda
and
w
must split into two linear factors. Comparing the
coefficient of
\lambda w
shows that the two linear factors have opposite

w
-coefficients, and comparison of the remaining coefficients gives either

b_{1}=0
or
a_{1}=a_{2}
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume
b_{1}b_{2}\neq 0
. Then
\chi_{3}
is reducible if and only if it has a
constant eigenvalue branch. More explicitly, this happens if and only if
one of the following alternatives holds:

a_{1}=a_{3},

or

(a_{3}-a_{2})b_{1}^{2}+(a_{1}-a_{2})b_{2}^{2}=0.

If
b_{1}=0
or
b_{2}=0
, the chain is disconnected and
\chi_{3}
is reducible.

Proof.

Because
\chi_{3}
is monic of degree three in
\lambda
, any non-trivial
factorization contains a factor which is linear in
\lambda
. Write such a
factor as
L(\lambda,w)
. The involution
w\mapsto-w
preserves
\chi_{3}
,
so
L(\lambda,-w)
is also a factor. If these two factors are distinct,
then their product has degree two in
\lambda
and the remaining factor is
again linear in
\lambda
and invariant under
w\mapsto-w
. Thus, in all
cases, reducibility gives an invariant linear factor of
\chi_{3}
.

For
n=3
the continuant has the form

P_{3}(\lambda,t)=(\lambda+a_{1})(\lambda+a_{2})(\lambda+a_{3})-t\bigl(b_{1}^{2}(\lambda+a_{3})+b_{2}^{2}(\lambda+a_{1})\bigr).

An invariant linear factor must specialize at
t=0
to one of the factors

\lambda+a_{j}
. Division in
\mathbb{C}[t][\lambda]
then shows that such a factor
can occur only as a constant branch
\lambda+a_{j}
; otherwise the quotient
would produce a non-zero
t^{2}
term, impossible for
P_{3}
, which is only
linear in
t
. Hence reducibility is equivalent to

P_{3}(-a_{j},t)\equiv 0
for some
j
.

Evaluating gives

P_{3}(-a_{1},t)=-tb_{1}^{2}(a_{3}-a_{1}),

P_{3}(-a_{2},t)=-t\bigl(b_{1}^{2}(a_{3}-a_{2})+b_{2}^{2}(a_{1}-a_{2})\bigr),

P_{3}(-a_{3},t)=-tb_{2}^{2}(a_{1}-a_{3}).

Since
b_{1}b_{2}\neq 0
, these are precisely the two displayed alternatives.
Conversely, each alternative makes one of the displayed expressions vanish
identically and therefore gives a constant branch.
∎

5.2.
Degree four

Degree four is the first place where two different connected mechanisms are
visible. They should both be retained in the general picture.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume
b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}\neq 0
. In degree four the following connected
reducibility mechanisms occur.

(1)

If

a_{1}=a_{4},\qquad a_{2}=a_{3},\qquad b_{1}^{2}=b_{3}^{2},

then the chain is palindromic and
\chi_{4}
factors by reversal symmetry.

(2)

If

a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}=a,

then

\chi_{4}(\lambda,w)=\prod_{\mu\in\operatorname{Spec}(B)}(\lambda+a+\mu w),

where
B
is the constant off-diagonal tridiagonal matrix with couplings

b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
.

Neither mechanism implies the other.

Proof.

The first assertion is Proposition
3.1
(3), and the second
is Proposition
3.1
(4).
∎

Example 5.4

(A scalar but non-palindromic quartic)
.

For

a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}=0,\qquad b_{1}=1,\quad b_{2}=2,\quad b_{3}=3,

one obtains

\chi_{4}(\lambda,w)=\lambda^{4}-14\lambda^{2}w^{2}+9w^{4}=\bigl(\lambda^{2}-(7+2\sqrt{10})w^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\lambda^{2}-(7-2\sqrt{10})w^{2}\bigr).

This connected example is not palindromic, since
b_{1}^{2}\neq b_{3}^{2}
.

Remark 5.5
.

The preceding example explains why low-degree classifications should not be
phrased only in terms of reversal symmetry. Even in degree four, connected
reducibility can be caused by a coincidence stratum of the diagonal entries.

5.3.
Degrees five, six and seven

For degrees five, six and seven the coefficient-comparison method gives many
useful restrictions, but a complete printed classification would require a
separate discussion of all equality patterns among
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
. We
therefore record the uniform conclusion which is safe and useful for the main
conjecture.

Proposition 5.6
.

In degrees
5\leq n\leq 7
, the elementary connected reducibility mechanisms
listed in Proposition
3.1
have codimension at least two in
the connected parameter space. More precisely, a full palindromic chain has
codimension
n-1
, a full scalar-diagonal chain has codimension
n-1
, and a
generic constant branch has expected codimension
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
.

Proof.

The palindromic equations identify the diagonal entries in opposite pairs and
also identify the squared couplings in opposite pairs; this gives
n-1

independent equations for the full chain. The scalar-diagonal condition is

a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n}
, again of codimension
n-1
. Finally,

P_{n}(-a_{j},t)
has zero constant term and degree at most
\lfloor n/2\rfloor

in
t
. Vanishing of its non-constant coefficients gives at most

\lfloor n/2\rfloor
equations, and on the generic constant-branch stratum
these equations are independent.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

This proposition is deliberately not a classification theorem. Its role is
to record the feature relevant for Conjecture
4.3
:
within the connected stratum none of the currently visible mechanisms gives a
hypersurface for
n\geq 4
. Scalar blocks give many connected reducible
examples, but they lie on high-codimension coincidence strata.

The natural remaining finite problem is the following.

Problem 5.8
.

Classify connected reducible pencils for
n\leq 7
after stratifying the
parameter space by the equality pattern of
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
. On each stratum,
determine whether reducibility is explained by constant branches, reversal
symmetry, scalar diagonal blocks, or by a genuinely new primitive mechanism.

5.4.
Degree eight

Degree eight is the first degree where a genuinely new primitive balanced
splitting, of type
4+4
, could reasonably appear. We record here the two
large connected families which are completely understood and then formulate
the precise computational test for possible primitive exceptions.

Proposition 5.9

(Palindromic degree-eight splitting)
.

Assume

a_{1}=a_{8},\quad a_{2}=a_{7},\quad a_{3}=a_{6},\quad a_{4}=a_{5},

and

b_{1}^{2}=b_{7}^{2},\quad b_{2}^{2}=b_{6}^{2},\quad b_{3}^{2}=b_{5}^{2}.

After changing signs of basis vectors, one may assume

b_{1}=b_{7}
,
b_{2}=b_{6}
, and
b_{3}=b_{5}
. Then

\chi_{8}(\lambda,w)=\chi_{8}^{+}(\lambda,w)\chi_{8}^{-}(\lambda,w),

where

\chi_{8}^{\pm}(\lambda,w)=\det\!\begin{pmatrix}\lambda+a_{1}&b_{1}w&0&0\\
b_{1}w&\lambda+a_{2}&b_{2}w&0\\
0&b_{2}w&\lambda+a_{3}&b_{3}w\\
0&0&b_{3}w&\lambda+a_{4}\pm b_{4}w\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Let
R
be the reversal operator
e_{i}\mapsto e_{9-i}
. Under the stated
assumptions, after harmless sign changes of basis vectors,
R
commutes with

J_{8}(w)
. The spaces fixed and negated by
R
are spanned by

e_{1}\pm e_{8},\quad e_{2}\pm e_{7},\quad e_{3}\pm e_{6},\quad e_{4}\pm e_{5}.

The restrictions of
\lambda I+J_{8}(w)
to these two spaces are exactly the
two displayed
4\times 4
matrices. Taking determinants gives the
factorization.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

The middle coupling
b_{4}w
becomes the diagonal perturbation
\pm b_{4}w
on
the two quotient chains. Thus the two quartic factors need not be even in

w
, although their product is even.

Proposition 5.11

(Scalar degree-eight splitting)
.

Assume

a_{1}=\cdots=a_{8}=a.

Then

\chi_{8}(\lambda,w)=\prod_{j=1}^{8}(\lambda+a+\mu_{j}w),

where
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{8}
are the eigenvalues of the constant tridiagonal
matrix
B
with zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries

b_{1},\ldots,b_{7}
. In particular,
\chi_{8}
is completely reducible over

\mathbb{C}[\lambda,w]
.

Proof.

This is Proposition
3.1
(4) for the full block of length
eight.
∎

Example 5.12
.

Taking

a_{1}=\cdots=a_{8}=0,\qquad b_{1}=1,b_{2}=2,b_{3}=3,b_{4}=4,b_{5}=5,b_{6}=6,b_{7}=7,

gives a connected completely reducible example which is not palindromic.
Thus the distinct-diagonal hypothesis is essential in any palindromic
classification.

5.5.
The Hensel test for primitive degree-eight splittings

Assume now that

a_{i}\neq a_{j}\quad(i\neq j),\qquad b_{1}\cdots b_{7}\neq 0.

At
t=0
one has

P_{8}(\lambda,0)=\prod_{i=1}^{8}(\lambda+a_{i}).

Any factorization in
\mathbb{C}[\lambda,t]
therefore determines a non-empty proper
subset
S\subset\{1,\ldots,8\}
:

F(\lambda,0)=\prod_{i\in S}(\lambda+a_{i}),\qquad G(\lambda,0)=\prod_{i\notin S}(\lambda+a_{i}).

Since the two factors at
t=0
are coprime, Hensel lifting determines formal
factors
F_{S},G_{S}\in\mathbb{C}[[t]][\lambda]
uniquely. Reducibility with this
specialization pattern is equivalent to the termination of these formal
series; equivalently, finitely many obstruction polynomials must vanish.

Up to replacing
S
by its complement, there are
2^{7}-1=127
subsets to
check. The cases
|S|=1
or
7
are precisely constant branches. The cases

|S|=2,6
and
|S|=3,5
are expected to be overdetermined outside the
constant-branch and coincidence strata. The genuinely new possibility is
therefore the balanced case
|S|=4
.

Conjecture 5.13

(Degree-eight distinct-diagonal test case)
.

Assume
a_{1},\ldots,a_{8}
are pairwise distinct and

b_{1}\cdots b_{7}\neq 0
. Then every reducible connected degree-eight spectral
curve is obtained from a constant branch or from reversal symmetry. In
particular, there is no primitive non-palindromic
4+4
or
3+5
splitting
with pairwise distinct diagonal entries.

Remark 5.14
.

This conjecture is the first finite test of the primitive codimension-growth
principle. It is also the point at which a computer-algebra certificate would
be most useful: one should compute the Hensel obstructions for the
127

subsets and verify that their common vanishing is contained in the union of
the constant-branch, palindromic, and coincidence strata.

5.6.
Summary of the low-dimensional picture

The revised low-dimensional evidence is therefore as follows. Degrees two
and three admit short complete statements. Degree four already shows that
palindromy is not the only connected mechanism, because scalar diagonal
blocks give non-palindromic factorizations. In degrees five through seven,
the useful robust statement is codimensional: all visible connected
mechanisms have codimension at least two. Degree eight is the first serious
primitive test case; the known connected factorizations are palindromic or
scalar, and the remaining possible primitive splittings can be organized by
the Hensel-lifting test above.

6.
Final remarks

Theorem
2.1
shows that reducibility is exceptional for fixed pairwise distinct diagonal entries. The classification in degrees at most seven suggests that the exceptional locus is generated by four concrete mechanisms: disconnection, constant branches, reflection symmetry, and scalar diagonal blocks. The first open case of Conjecture
4.2
is
n=8
. In that case any new phenomenon would have to appear as a primitive factorization of type
4+4
or
3+5
in
\lambda
, or as a paired factorization over
w
not forced by an involution of the chain. Conjecture
4.3
predicts that such a phenomenon, if it exists, cannot form a hypersurface component, while Conjecture
4.4
predicts that primitive connected reducibility becomes increasingly rare.
