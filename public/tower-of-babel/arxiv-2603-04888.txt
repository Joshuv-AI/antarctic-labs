Construction of higher Chow cycles on cyclic coverings of ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} , Part II
Yusuke Nemoto, Ken Sato

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct higher Chow cycles of type ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) on a family of surfaces related to a product of curves, which are certain degree N N abelian covers of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} branched over n + 2 n+2 points. We prove that for a very general member, these cycles generate a subgroup of the indecomposable part of rank ≥ n ⋅ φ ⁡ ( N ) \operatorname{rank}\geq n\cdot\varphi(N) , where φ ⁡ ( N ) \varphi(N) is Euler’s totient function, by computing their images under the transcendental regulator map.

1.
Introduction

In our previous work
[
6
]
, we constructed higher Chow cycles of type
(2,1)
on the minimal model of a quotient surface of a product of hypergeometric curves

y^{N}=x^{A}(1-x)^{A}(1-\lambda_{1}x)^{A}\quad\text{and }\quad y^{N}=x^{N-A}(1-x)^{N-A}(1-\lambda_{2}x)^{N-A}.

Under some conditions, we described the images of the cycles under the Beilinson regulator map in terms of special functions satisfying inhomogeneous Gauss hypergeometric differential equations.
Using these descriptions, we proved that these cycles generate a subgroup of rank
36\cdot\varphi(N)
of the indecomposable part of the higher Chow group for very general
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
, where
\varphi(N)
is Euler’s totient function (loc. cit. Theorem 7.5).

The aim of this paper is to prove similar results for surfaces constructed from hypergeometric curves of the more general form

(1.1)

y^{N}=(x-\lambda)^{a_{0}}(x-c_{1})^{a_{1}}\cdots(x-c_{n})^{a_{n}}\quad(\lambda\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n},\infty\})

where
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
are different from each other.
It is well-known (cf.
[
1
]
) that the periods of the curves (
1.1
) are described in terms of
Appell-Lauricella hypergeometric functions

F_{D}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},\lambda)
.
Since
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
are fixed,
F_{D}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},\lambda)
, viewed as a function of

\lambda
, satisfies the
Jordan-Pochhammer differential equation
(see Section
5.3
).

In this paper, we consider a family of surfaces
S
over an open subset
T_{0}
of
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n},\infty\})^{2}
, where the fiber
S_{t}
over
t=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in T_{0}
is a smooth model of the quotient surface
((C_{1})_{\lambda_{1}}\times(C_{2})_{\lambda_{2}})/\mu_{N}
, and
(C_{1})_{\lambda_{1}},(C_{2})_{\lambda_{2}}
are smooth models of hypergeometric curves given by

\displaystyle y_{1}^{N}=(x_{1}-\lambda_{1})^{A}(x_{1}-c_{1})^{A}\cdots(x_{1}-c_{n})^{A}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle y_{2}^{N}=(x_{2}-\lambda_{2})^{N-A}(x_{2}-c_{1})^{N-A}\cdots(x_{2}-c_{n})^{N-A}.

Here, the group of
N
th roots of unity
\mu_{N}
acts on
C_{1}\times C_{2}
by

\zeta\cdot((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))=((x_{1},\zeta^{-1}y_{1}),(x_{2},\zeta y_{2})),\quad\zeta\in\mu_{N}.

After taking a suitable étale base change
T\to T_{0}
, we can construct families of
(2,1)
-cycles
\xi_{c_{1}}^{(i)},\xi_{c_{2}}^{(i)},\dots,\xi_{c_{n}}^{(i)}\>\>(i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
on
S_{t}
by a method similar to that of
[
6
]
.
Under assumptions
\gcd(N,A)=1
and
(N+1)/(n+1)\leq A\leq(nN-1)/(n+1)
, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
6.1
)
.

For a very general
t\in T
, the cycles
\xi_{c_{1}}^{(i)},\xi_{c_{2}}^{(i)},\dots,\xi_{c_{n}}^{(i)}
generate a subgroup of rank at least
n\cdot\varphi(N)
in
\operatorname{CH}^{2}(S_{t},1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
.
In particular, we have

\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{CH}^{2}(S_{t},1)_{\rm ind}\geq n\cdot\varphi(N).

Here,
\operatorname{CH}^{2}(S_{t},1)_{\rm ind}
denotes the indecomposable part of the higher Chow group
\operatorname{CH}^{2}(S_{t},1)
.

Unlike previous works
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, in this paper we do not consider automorphisms of this family, since
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n},\infty\})
is trivial when
n\geq 3
and
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
are general.
As a result, in the case
n=2
, which is the case essentially considered in
[
6
]
, Theorem
1.1
yields only the weaker bound
\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{CH}^{2}(S_{t},1)_{\mathrm{ind}}\geq 2\cdot\varphi(N)
than the one obtained in the main theorem in
[
6
]
.

The outline of the proof is the same as in
[
6
]
.
We use the
transcendental regulator map
, which factors through
\operatorname{CH}^{2}(S_{t},1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
, hence it suffices to evaluate the rank of the subgroup generated by the images of our cycles under this map.
We give an integral representation of a normal function
\nu_{\mathrm{tr}}(\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i)})
associated to the images of
(\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i)})_{t}
under the transcendental regulator map.
Then we explicitly compute the pairing
\langle\nu_{\mathrm{tr}}(\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i)}),\omega\rangle
with a canonical relative 2-form
\omega
by using the assumptions
(N+1)/(n+1)\leq A\leq(nN-1)/(n+1)
.
We show that the pairing is given by a local holomorphic function
F_{i,j}
satisfying the system of differential equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{1}}(F_{i,j})&=\frac{N(1-\zeta^{A})\zeta^{i}}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})^{n-1}}\left(\dfrac{A}{N}\right)_{n-1}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\dfrac{(2-n)_{k}}{(Nk+A)k!}\cdot\frac{(c_{j}-\lambda_{2})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}{(c_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}\\
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{2}}(F_{i,j})&=-\frac{N(1-\zeta^{-A})\zeta^{i}}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})^{n-1}}\left(\dfrac{N-A}{N}\right)_{n-1}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\dfrac{(2-n)_{k}}{(Nk+N-A)k!}\cdot\frac{(c_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{Nk+N-A}{N}}}{(c_{j}-\lambda_{2})^{\frac{Nk+N-A}{N}}}\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{i}}
is the
Jordan-Pochhammer differential operator
(see Section
5.3
) defined by

\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{i}}=q_{0}(\lambda_{i})\dfrac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\lambda_{i}^{n}}+p_{1,i}(\lambda_{i})\dfrac{\partial^{n-1}}{\partial\lambda_{i}^{n-1}}+\cdots+p_{n,i}(\lambda_{i})

and
(\alpha)_{k}=\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+k-1)
denotes the Pochhammer symbol.
Since period functions of the family
S\to T
with respect to
\omega
are annihilated by
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{2}}
, the local holomorphic functions
F_{i,j}
generate a subgroup of rank
n\cdot\varphi(N)
in the sheaf of holomorphic functions modulo periods.
Then Theorem
1.1
follows.

Construction of higher Chow cycles

3.1.
Preliminaries

For a smooth variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}
, let
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)
be the higher Chow group of type
(2,1)
defined by Bloch
[
2
]
.
It is well-known (e.g.,
[
5

\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)

(3.1)

\sum_{j}(C_{j},f_{j})

A

(3.2)

\mathrm{Pic}(X)\otimes\Gamma(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times})=\mathrm{CH}^{1}(X)\otimes\mathrm{CH}^{1}(X,1)\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)

The cokernel of (
3.2
) is denoted by
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
,
which is called the indecomposable part of
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)

Let
C

be the class corresponding to
C
.
The image of

under (
3.2

(C,\alpha|_{C})

).

4.
Computation of the regulator

In this section, we calculate the images of our cycles
under the transcendental regulator map, which is a variant of the Beilinson regulator map.

Recall that for a smooth projective variety
X
, the Beilinson regulator is the map from
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)
to the Deligne cohomology
H^{3}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(2))
.
The
transcenental regulator map
is the composition of the Beilinson regulator map and the natural projection induced by
H^{2,0}(X)\hookrightarrow F^{1}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
.

(4.1)

r\colon\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)\to H^{3}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(2))\simeq\frac{F^{1}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})^{\vee}}{H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})}\twoheadrightarrow\frac{H^{2,0}(X)^{\vee}}{H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})}

Here,
H^{2,0}(X)^{\vee}
means the dual
\mathbb{C}
-linear space of
H^{2,0}(X)
and we regard
H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace of
F^{1}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})^{\vee}
by the integration.

4.1.
Levine’s regulator formula

To compute the image of a higher Chow cycle represented by a formal sum (
3.1
) under the transcendental regulator map
r
, we use the following formula (cf.
[
4
]
).
For simplicity, we assume
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0
.

Let
D_{j}\to C_{j}
be the normalization of the curve
C_{j}
on
X
.
Let
\mu_{j}\colon D_{j}\to X
denote the composition of
D_{j}\to C_{j}
and the inclusion
C_{j}\to X
.
We will define a topological 1-chain
\gamma_{j}
on
D_{j}
.
If
f_{j}
is a constant, we set
\gamma_{j}=0
.
If
f_{j}
is not a constant, we regard
f_{j}
as a finite morphism from
D_{j}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Then we define
\gamma_{j}=f_{j}^{-1}([\infty,0])
where
[\infty,0]
is a path on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
from
\infty
to
0
along the positive real axis.
By the condition
\sum_{j}\mathrm{div}_{C_{j}}(f_{j})=0
,
\gamma=\sum_{j}(\mu_{j})_{*}\gamma_{j}
satisfies
\partial\gamma=0
.
In this paper,
\gamma
is called the
1-cycle associated with
\xi
.
Since
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0
, there exists a 2-chain
\Gamma
with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients such that
\partial\Gamma=\gamma
as 1-cycles with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients.

Then, for a non-zero holomorphic 2-form
\omega\in H^{2,0}(X)
on
X
, we can compute the image of
\xi=\sum_{j}(C_{j},f_{j})
under the map (
4.1
) as

(4.2)

\langle r(\xi),\omega\rangle\equiv\int_{\Gamma}\omega\mod\mathcal{P}(\omega),

where
\mathcal{P}(\omega)
is the

5.
Picard-Fuchs differential operator

In this section, we fix a relative 2-form
\omega
on

and find a

\mathscr{D}

As we stated in the introduction, the Jordan-Pochhammer differential operator becomes a Picard-Fuchs differential operator.

5.1.
A sheaf of period functions

Let
T
be a smooth variety and
\pi\colon X\to T
be a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties of relative dimension
d
.
For
p\geq 1
, we fix a relative algebraic
p
-form
\eta\in\Gamma(X,\Omega_{X/T}^{p})
.
For
t\in T

\eta_{t}\in H^{0}(X_{t},\Omega^{p}_{X_{t}})=H^{p,0}(X_{t})
denotes its restriction to the fiber

Hereafter, we regard

and
T
as complex manifolds with the classical topologies.
Let
\mathscr{O}_{T}^{\mathrm{an}}
be the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
T
, and
\mathbb{Q}(d)_{X}
be the constant sheaf on

with value
\mathbb{Q}(d)=(2\pi i)^{-d}\mathbb{Q}

Then
R^{2d-p}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(d)_{X}
is a local system of finite dimensional

for

By the Poincaré duality

can be described by a family of

\{\Gamma_{t}\}_{t\in U}
with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients.
Then we can define a morphism of sheaves

R^{2d-p}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(d)_{X}\to\mathscr{O}_{T}^{\mathrm{an}};\quad\{\Gamma_{t}\}_{t\in U}\mapsto\left(t\mapsto\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\eta_{t}\right).

We denote the image sheaf of this map by
\mathcal{P}(\eta)
, which consists of period functions with respect to
\eta
.

For
t\in T
and an open neighborhood
U
of

, we have a natural evaluation map
\Gamma(U,\mathscr{O}_{T}^{\mathrm{an}})\rightarrow\mathbb{C};f\mapsto f(t)
.
This induces the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\Gamma(U,\mathcal{P}(\eta))\to\mathcal{P}(\eta_{t})
,
where
\mathcal{P}(\eta_{t})
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace defined in Section
4.3
.
Thus, we have the evaluation map

(5.1)

\Gamma(U,\mathscr{O}_{T}^{\mathrm{an}}/\mathcal{P}(\eta))\to\mathbb{C}/\mathcal{P}(\eta_{t})

for any open neighborhood
U
of

5.3.
The differential operator
\mathscr{D}

We will find a differential operator

which annihilates all sections of
\mathcal{P}(\omega)

The periods of our family of surface

correspond to the direct product

of families of hypergeometric curves.
Since we fix the parameter
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n}
, the Jordan-Pochhammer differential operator is the Picard-Fuchs differential operator for hypergeometric curves
C_{i}\to R
.

For complex parameters
r_{0},r_{1},\dots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
, the
Jordan-Pochhammer differential operator

L_{\lambda}
in the variable
\lambda\in R_{0}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n},\infty\}
is defined by (cf.
[
3
, 18.4]

L_{\lambda}=q_{0}(\lambda)\dfrac{d^{n}}{d\lambda^{n}}+p_{1}(\lambda)\dfrac{d^{n-1}}{d\lambda^{n-1}}+\cdots+p_{n-1}(\lambda)\dfrac{d}{d\lambda}+p_{n}(\lambda).

Here, the polynomials
q_{0}(\lambda)
and
p_{k}(\lambda)\>(k=1,2,\dots,n)
are defined by

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&q_{0}(\lambda)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda-c_{j}),\quad q_{1}(\lambda)=q_{0}(\lambda)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{r_{j}}{\lambda-c_{j}},\\
&p_{k}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}n+r_{0}-2\\
k\end{pmatrix}q_{0}^{(k)}(\lambda)+\begin{pmatrix}n+r_{0}-2\\
k-1\end{pmatrix}q_{1}^{(k-1)}(\lambda)\quad(k=1,\ldots,n).\end{split}

where
\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
k\end{pmatrix}=\dfrac{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-k+1)}{k!}
denotes the binomial coefficients for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
q^{(k)}(\lambda)=\dfrac{d^{k}q}{d\lambda^{k}}(\lambda)
.

The following lemma is useful not only for computing periods of
C_{i}\to R_{0}
but also computing differentials of normal functions.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
H(\lambda,x)
be the multivalued function defined by

H(\lambda,x)=-(r_{0})_{n-1}(x-\lambda)^{-n+1-r_{0}}(x-c_{1})^{1-r_{1}}\cdots(x-c_{n})^{1-r_{n}}

where
(\alpha)_{n}=\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)
denotes the Pochhammer symbol.
Then we have

L_{\lambda}\left(\dfrac{1}{(x-\lambda)^{r_{0}}(x-c_{1})^{r_{1}}\cdots(x-c_{n})^{r_{n}}}\right)=\dfrac{\partial H(\lambda,x)}{\partial x}.

Proof
.

It suffices to show that

(5.5)

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{n}(r_{0})_{n-k}\left\{\begin{pmatrix}n+r_{0}-2\\
k\end{pmatrix}q_{0}^{(k)}(\lambda)+\begin{pmatrix}n+r_{0}-2\\
k-1\end{pmatrix}q_{1}^{(k-1)}(\lambda)\right\}(x-\lambda)^{k}\\
&\quad=(r_{0})_{n-1}\left(\dfrac{n+r_{0}-1}{x-\lambda}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{1-r_{j}}{x-c_{j}}\right)(x-\lambda)(x-c_{1})\cdots(x-c_{n})\end{split}

where we regard
\begin{pmatrix}n+r_{0}-2\\
-1\end{pmatrix}=0
.
Since both sides of (
5.5
) are polynomials in
x,\lambda
of degree at most
n
, we can uniquely expand them in the form
\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{h_{k}(\lambda)}{k!}(x-\lambda)^{k}
where each
h_{k}(\lambda)
is a polynomial in
\lambda
.
Since
h_{k}(\lambda)=\left.\dfrac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}(\lambda)(x-\lambda)^{k}\right)\right|_{x=\lambda}
, we will compare the
k
-th partial derivative with respect to
x
at
x=\lambda
of the both sides of (
5.5
).
We have

\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}(\text{LHS of }\eqref{eq1})\right|_{x=\lambda}

\displaystyle=(r_{0})_{n-k}\left\{\begin{pmatrix}n+r_{0}-2\\
k\end{pmatrix}q_{0}^{(k)}(\lambda)+\begin{pmatrix}n+r_{0}-2\\
k-1\end{pmatrix}q_{1}^{(k-1)}(\lambda)\right\}k!.

On the other hand, since the right-hand side of (
5.5
) is

(r_{0})_{n-1}\left\{(n+r_{0}-1)q_{0}(x)+(x-\lambda)q_{1}(x)-(x-\lambda)q_{0}^{(1)}(x)\right\},

we have

\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}(\text{RHS of }\eqref{eq1})\right|_{x=\lambda}=(r_{0})_{n-1}\left\{(n+r_{0}-1)q_{0}^{(k)}(\lambda)+kq_{1}^{(k-1)}(\lambda)-kq_{0}^{(k)}(\lambda)\right\}.

By comparing them, we have the lemma.
∎

Using the Jordan-Pochhammer differential operator, we will find a Picard-Fuchs differential operator

with respect to

as follows.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\omega
be the relative
2

Let

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{2}}=q_{0}(\lambda_{2})\dfrac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\lambda_{2}^{n}}+p_{1,2}(\lambda_{2})\dfrac{\partial^{n-1}}{\partial\lambda_{2}^{n-1}}+p_{2,2}(\lambda_{2})\dfrac{\partial^{n-2}}{\partial\lambda_{2}^{n-2}}+\cdots+p_{n,2}(\lambda_{2}),

where
p_{k,1}(\lambda)
(resp.
p_{k,2}(\lambda)

in (

) for

(resp.

Proof

and

By the Künneth decomposition, as a subsheaf of

where the right-hand side is the

-linear subsheaf generated by products of functions in

and

.
Thus the proposition follows.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
t\in T
and
(K_{j}^{l})^{(i)}
be topological 2-chains in Section

For

\int_{(K_{j}^{l})^{(i)}}\omega_{t}=\zeta^{Ai}\int_{(K_{j}^{l})^{(0)}}\omega_{t}.

Proof

The topological 2-chain

\sigma^{i}((K_{j}^{l})^{(0)})=(K_{j}^{l})^{(i)}
, where
\sigma^{i}\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(S_{t})

By the local description of

Thus, the equality follows as

\int_{(K_{j}^{l})^{(i)}}\omega_{t}=\int_{\sigma^{i}((K_{j}^{l})^{(0)})}\omega_{t}=\int_{(K_{j}^{l})^{(0)}}(\sigma^{i})^{*}\omega_{t}=\zeta^{Ai}\int_{(K_{j}^{l})^{(0)}}\omega_{t}.

∎

Using Lemma

\langle\nu_{\mathrm{tr}}(\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i+l)}-\xi_{c_{1}}^{(i)}),\omega\rangle\in\Gamma(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}^{\mathrm{an}}/\mathcal{P}(\omega))

Using this integral expression, we compute

Theorem 6.4
.

For
i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, the images of
\nu_{\rm tr}(\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i)})

(j=1,\ldots,n)
under the Picard-Fuchs differential operator
\mathscr{D}
are as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(\nu_{\rm tr}(\langle\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i)}),\omega\rangle)=\frac{N(1-\zeta^{A})\zeta^{Ai}}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})^{n-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\dfrac{(2-n)_{k}}{k!}\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\left(\frac{A}{N}\right)_{n-1}}{(Nk+A)}\cdot\frac{(c_{j}-\lambda_{2})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}{(c_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}\\
-\dfrac{\left(\frac{N-A}{N}\right)_{n-1}}{(Nk+N-A)}\cdot\frac{(c_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{Nk+N-A}{N}}}{(c_{j}-\lambda_{2})^{\frac{Nk+N-A}{N}}}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof
.

It is enough to calculate the image of the local holomorphic function in (
6.4
) under the differential operator
\mathscr{D}
.
Note that the image is a pair of global holomorphic functions, though (
6.4
) itself is a multivalued function.
We only compute the first component, because the computation on the second component is similar.

First, we show

(6.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{1}}(\langle\nu_{\rm tr}(\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i+l)}-\xi_{c_{1}}^{(i)}),\omega\rangle)\\
&=\frac{N(1-\zeta^{A})\zeta^{Ai}}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})^{n-1}}\left(\dfrac{A}{N}\right)_{n-1}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\dfrac{(2-n)_{k}}{(Nk+A)k!}\cdot\left(\zeta^{Al}\frac{(c_{j}-\lambda_{2})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}{(c_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}-\frac{(c_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}{(c_{1}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}\right).\end{split}

By the local description of
\omega_{t}
, we have

\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{1}}\left(\int_{(K_{j}^{l})^{(0)}}\omega_{t}\right)=\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{1}}\left(\int_{\triangle}(\mu_{j}^{l})^{*}\left(\dfrac{dx\wedge dy}{v^{N-A}f(x)}\right)\right)=\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{1}}\left(\int_{\triangle}\dfrac{(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))^{\prime}(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))^{\prime}ds_{1}\wedge ds_{2}}{\sqrt[N]{f(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))}^{A}\sqrt[N]{g(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))}^{N-A}}\right)

where branches of functions
\sqrt[N]{f(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))}
and
\sqrt[N]{g(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))}
are determined as in (
4.5
).

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
\triangle_{\varepsilon}=\{(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid\varepsilon\leq s_{2}\leq s_{1}\leq 1-\varepsilon\}
.
Then, by standard analytic arguments using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle=\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{1}}\left(\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\triangle_{\varepsilon}}\dfrac{(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))^{\prime}(\gamma^{l}_{j}(s_{2}))^{\prime}ds_{1}\wedge ds_{2}}{\sqrt[N]{f(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))}^{A}\sqrt[N]{g(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))}^{N-A}}\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\triangle_{\varepsilon}}\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{1}}\left(\dfrac{(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))^{\prime}(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))^{\prime}ds_{1}\wedge ds_{2}}{\sqrt[N]{f(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))}^{A}\sqrt[N]{g(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))}^{N-A}}\right).

Let
H(\lambda_{1},x)=-\left(\frac{A}{N}\right)_{n-1}(x-\lambda_{1})^{-n+1-\frac{A}{N}}(x-c_{1})^{1-\frac{A}{N}}\cdots(x-c_{n})^{1-\frac{A}{N}}
be the multivalued function defined in Lemma
5.1
.
Since the branches of functions
\sqrt[N]{x-\lambda_{1}}
and
\sqrt[N]{x-c_{i}}
is already chosen along
\gamma^{l}_{j}
, the branch of
H(\lambda_{1},x)
along
\gamma^{l}_{j}
is already fixed.
Since we have
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{1}}\left(1/\sqrt[N]{f(x)}^{A}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}H(x,\lambda_{1})
by Lemma
5.1
, we have

=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\triangle_{\varepsilon}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s_{1}}\left(\dfrac{H(\lambda_{1},\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))\cdot(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))^{\prime}}{\sqrt[N]{g(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))}^{N-A}}\right)ds_{1}\wedge ds_{2}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\triangle_{\varepsilon}}d\left(\dfrac{H(\lambda_{1},\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))\cdot(\gamma^{l}_{j}(s_{2}))^{\prime}ds_{2}}{\sqrt[N]{g(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))}^{N-A}}\right)

Then we use the Stokes theorem.
Note that integration along
\partial\triangle_{\varepsilon}
converges to
0
except the diagonal line
s_{1}=s_{2}
, so we have

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\partial\triangle_{\epsilon}}\dfrac{H(\lambda_{1},\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{1}))\cdot(\gamma^{l}_{j}(s_{2}))^{\prime}ds_{2}}{\sqrt[N]{g(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s_{2}))}^{N-A}}=\int_{s=1}^{s=0}\dfrac{H(\lambda_{1},\gamma_{j}^{l}(s))\cdot(\gamma^{l}_{j}(s))^{\prime}ds}{\sqrt[N]{g(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s))}^{N-A}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{A}{N}\right)_{n-1}\int_{s=0}^{s=1}\dfrac{(\gamma^{l}_{j}(s))^{\prime}ds}{(\gamma_{j}^{l}(s)-\lambda_{1})^{n-1}\cdot\sqrt[N]{\gamma_{j}^{l}(s)-\lambda_{1}}^{A}\cdot\sqrt[N]{\gamma_{j}^{l}(s)-\lambda_{2}}^{N-A}}

Let
u
be the new path on
\mathbb{C}
defined by
u(s)=\frac{\sqrt[N]{\gamma_{j}^{l}(s)-\lambda_{2}}}{\sqrt[N]{\gamma_{j}^{l}(s)-\lambda_{1}}}
.
By the condition
(iii)
and condition
(iv)
on

,

is the path from

to
\zeta^{l}\frac{\sqrt[N]{c_{j}-\lambda_{2}}}{\sqrt[N]{c_{j}-\lambda_{1}}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle=\frac{-N}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{n-1}}\left(\dfrac{A}{N}\right)_{n-1}\int_{s=0}^{s=1}(u(s)^{N}-1)^{n-2}u(s)^{A-1}u^{\prime}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{-N}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{n-1}}\left(\dfrac{A}{N}\right)_{n-1}\int_{\frac{\sqrt[N]{c_{1}-\lambda_{2}}}{\sqrt[N]{c_{1}-\lambda_{1}}}}^{\zeta^{l}\frac{\sqrt[N]{c_{j}-\lambda_{2}}}{\sqrt[N]{c_{j}-\lambda_{1}}}}(u^{N}-1)^{n-2}u^{A-1}du

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})^{n-1}}\left(\dfrac{A}{N}\right)_{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\dfrac{(2-n)_{k}}{(Nk+A)k!}\left(\zeta^{Al}\frac{(c_{j}-\lambda_{2})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}{(c_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}-\frac{(c_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}{(c_{1}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{Nk+A}{N}}}\right).

We use the binomial expansion and the relation

in the last equality.
Thus, we have (

Secondly, we will prove the theorem.
By the definition of

, we have

Thus, we have

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{D}(\langle\nu_{\rm tr}(\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i+l)}-\xi_{c_{1}}^{(i)}),\omega\rangle)=-N\mathscr{D}(\langle\nu_{\rm tr}(\xi_{c_{1}}^{(i)}),\omega\rangle).

Since

∎

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
6.1

Let
\Xi
be the subgroup of
\Gamma(T,\mathcal{Q})
generated by

\nu_{\mathrm{tr}}(\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i)})\quad(i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},\ j=1,2,\dots,n).

First, we compute the rank of
\Xi
.
By Theorem
6.4
, the rational functions
\mathscr{D}(\nu_{\rm tr}(\langle\xi_{c_{j}}^{(0)}),\omega\rangle)

(j=1,\ldots,n)
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{C}
, in particular, linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
.
Furthermore, since
\mathscr{D}(\nu_{\rm tr}(\langle\xi_{c_{j}}^{(0)}),\omega\rangle)
is non-torsion for each

,
\mathscr{D}(\nu_{\rm tr}(\langle\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i)}),\omega\rangle)\quad(i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
generates a free
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]
-module of rank 1, whose rank as an abelian group is equal to
\varphi(N)
.
Thus, the rank of
\Xi
is equal or greater than
n\cdot\varphi(N)
.

By Lemma
6.2
, we have
\operatorname{rank}\mathrm{ev}_{t}(\Xi)=\operatorname{rank}\Xi
for very general
t\in T
.
Furthermore, by (
6.1
), we have

\mathrm{ev}_{t}(\Xi)=\langle r((\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i)})_{t})\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},\ j=1,2,\dots,n\rangle.

Therefore, by Proposition
4.1
, the cycles
(\xi_{c_{j}}^{(i)})_{t}
generate a subgroup of rank at least
n\cdot\varphi(N)
in
\operatorname{CH}^{2}(S_{t},1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
.
