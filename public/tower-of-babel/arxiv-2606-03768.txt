HybridThinker: Efficient Chain-of-Thought Reasoning via Compressed Memory and Transient Thought Steps
Xin Liu, Runsong Zhao, Xinyu Liu, Junhao Ruan, Pengcheng Huang, Shichao Dong, Chunyang Xiao, Chenglong Wang, Changliang Li, Jingbo Zhu, Tong Xiao

Abstract
Extended chain-of-thought (CoT) traces improve LLM reasoning but incur substantial computational and memory costs. While existing CoT compression methods mitigate this by condensing thought steps into compact representations via memory tokens and retaining only these representations at inference time, the loss of fine-grained information makes subsequent steps more error-prone. To alleviate this, we propose HybridThinker , where in addition to preserved these representations, thought steps are also temporarily retained to provide fine-grained details. However, we observe that naively keeping thought steps accessible to subsequent steps during training lets the model bypass memory tokens by retrieving information directly from these steps, leaving the model’s ability to compress and retrieve information through memory tokens insufficiently trained. We therefore introduce a hybrid training scheme, in which only some thought steps are directly accessible through attention to subsequent steps, while the other thought steps are masked, forcing the model to use memory tokens for compression and retrieval. Across 4 reasoning benchmarks, HybridThinker matches the uncompressed baseline, advancing the state of the art in CoT compression by 5.8 points on average accuracy with similar inference time. Ablation studies confirm that both temporary thought-step retention and the hybrid training scheme contribute to these gains.

Introduction

Recent advances in large language model reasoning have increasingly relied on extended chain of thoughts (CoTs), which have been shown to be effective across arithmetic, common sense, and knowledge-intensive tasks
(
26
;
10
;
29
;
6
;
28
)
. However, as illustrated in Figure
1
(a), these extended CoT traces introduce substantial inference overhead, as the self-attention computation scales quadratically and the KV cache grows linearly with the sequence length, hindering the broader deployment of reasoning models.

To alleviate this, two main approaches have been proposed: KV cache pruning, and CoT compression. KV cache pruning reduces memory
32
;
2
usage by selectively retaining important portions of the KV cache while discarding less critical ones during inference, but discarded information is irrecoverable without full regeneration. CoT compression
(
30
;
11
;
16
;
36
)
condenses lengthy thought steps into compact representations, after which the original thought steps are discarded immediately as we illustrate in Figure
1
(b). Unfortunately, this premature discarding renders the immediately following step more error-prone: key details such as exact numerical values, strict constraints, and intermediate conclusions may be obscured or lost in the compact representations
(
31
;
19
)
. In multi-step reasoning scenarios, such single-step errors propagate along the chain of thought in a cascading fashion.

To enable efficient reasoning in LLMs, we propose
HybridThinker
, a CoT compression method that mitigates the loss of fine-grained local information caused by the immediate discarding of thought steps in existing CoT compression methods. Similar to existing CoT compression approaches, HybridThinker compresses lengthy thought steps into compact representations via memory tokens and retains those representations after each thought step is completed. However, the thought steps are not immediately discarded in HybridThinker; instead, they are temporarily retained and remain accessible to several subsequent steps. We illustrate the HybridThinker inference in Figure
1
(c).

To train HybridThinker, we observe that naively applying the same attention pattern during training as at inference is suboptimal (see Section
4.3
for details) due to shortcut learning: when both memory tokens and thought steps are visible to subsequent steps, the model over-relies on the more readable plain-text thoughts, leaving its ability to compress and retrieve information through memory tokens undertrained.
We therefore propose
Hybrid Attention
for training, combining two complementary mechanisms:
Bottleneck Attention
, which hides each thought step from subsequent steps, forcing the model to access prior reasoning exclusively through memory tokens; and
Shortcut Attention
, which keeps each thought step visible to the immediately following few steps before discarding it, adapting the model to the inference-time setting.

Figure 1:
Comparison of three reasoning paradigms in terms of KV cache management. (a) Standard reasoning retains the KV cache of all original thought steps throughout decoding, achieving high accuracy at the cost of efficiency. (b) Existing CoT compression methods discard the KV cache of original thought steps immediately after compression, reducing memory usage but losing fine-grained local information. (c) HybridThinker (ours) temporarily retains the KV cache of each original thought step to serve several subsequent steps before discarding it, achieving both high accuracy and high efficiency. In this example, the KV cache of the third thought step
S_{3}
is retained to serve several subsequent steps until
S_{6}
has been compressed.

Experiments demonstrate that HybridThinker achieves an average accuracy improvement of 5.8 points over existing CoT compression methods, matching the performance of the uncompressed baseline.
Ablation studies further confirm the effectiveness of Hybrid Attention as a training strategy, showing that it outperforms training with pure Bottleneck Attention or pure Shortcut Attention alone.
To summarize our contributions:

(1)

We propose HybridThinker, in which thought steps are temporarily retained and kept visible to several subsequent steps alongside compact representations during inference, alleviating the loss of fine-grained information.

(2)

We propose Hybrid Attention for training, which resolves the shortcut learning problem that arises in naive HybridThinker training.

(3)

We demonstrate that HybridThinker achieves substantial performance improvements over existing CoT compression methods, establishing a new state-of-the-art performance in CoT compression.

Related Work

We organize related work into two categories: KV cache pruning, which reduces memory by directly discarding less important KV cache entries, and compression techniques, which also discards the original KV cache but first compresses the discarded information into compact representations. Compression techniques can be further divided into prompt compression, a well-established research area, and CoT compression, an emerging direction.

KV Cache Pruning.

KV cache pruning reduces memory footprint by discarding less important KV cache entries, with methods differing primarily in how importance is estimated. H
2
O
(
32
)
uses accumulated attention weights as the importance criterion. StreamingLLM
(
27
)
treats the first token and the most recent tokens as important due to the attention sink phenomenon. SepLLM
(
2
)
extends StreamingLLM by additionally retaining delimiter tokens, which implicitly compress contextual information. However, these methods face inherent limitations: low cache budgets have been shown to paradoxically lengthen CoT outputs
(
14
)
, and discarded cache becomes inaccessible.

Compression Techniques.

Existing prompt compression methods
(
1
;
12
;
9
;
17
;
4
;
13
;
25
;
23
;
21
;
22
;
24
;
35
;
33
;
34
;
15
)
focus on compressing model

inputs
, which are fully accessible ahead of time. CoT reasoning, however, is generated incrementally and cannot be accessed in its entirety beforehand, necessitating step-wise compression and rendering most prompt compression techniques directly inapplicable. To fill this gap, several dedicated CoT compression methods have been proposed. LightThinker
(
30
)
compresses each completed thought step into the KV cache of a small set of memory tokens and immediately discards the thought step’s KV cache, retaining only the memory tokens. MEMENTO
(
11
)
instead compresses thought steps into shorter text sequences. MemoSight
(
16
)
improves upon LightThinker’s compression mechanism and further incorporates multi-token prediction. However, all of these CoT compression methods discard thought steps immediately upon compression, losing fine-grained local information.

Method

.

In Section

-

3.1
Notation

We introduce the key notation used in Section
3.2
and Section
3.3
. We use
t=1,2,\ldots,k
to index thought steps.
w
is the temporary retention duration of a thought step. For example,
w=4
in Figure
1
(c) as
S_{3}
is accessible to
S_{3},S_{4},S_{5},S_{6}
.

Input and Output.

The model receives a request
Q
that requires CoT reasoning and generates a CoT
Y=S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{k}
consisting of
k
thought steps.
The answer is contained in
Y
.
Each thought step
S_{t}
is a token sequence terminated by a delimiter.

1

Following
30
, we use
‘‘\n\n’’
as the step delimiter in this work.

Memory Tokens.

HybridThinker maintains a fixed sequence of
L
learnable special tokens

M=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{L})
,
called
memory tokens
.
After generating
S_{t}
, the model performs a forward pass over
M
to produce the KV cache
\tilde{M}_{t}
, which serves as a compressed summary of
S_{t}
.
A fresh copy of
M
is used at each thought step to capture the thought step semantics; we write
M_{t}
when we specifically refer to the memory tokens inserted after
S_{t}
.

KV Cache Container.

We use
\mathcal{C}_{t}
to denote the KV cache container
that the model attends to when generating step
S_{t}
.
We introduce three types of KV cache notation used throughout:

(1)

\tilde{Q}
: the KV cache of the question
Q
, computed once during prefill and retained throughout.

(2)

\tilde{M}_{t}
: the KV cache of memory tokens
M_{t}
produced by compressing
S_{t}
.

(3)

\tilde{S}_{t}
: the KV cache of the raw thought step
S_{t}
that is retained temporarily through a few subsequent thought steps in HybridThinker.

3.2
HybridThinker Inference

HybridThinker generates CoT
Y
by repeating the following three steps for each thought step indexed by
t=1,2,\ldots,k
.

Step 1: Generate
S_{t}
.

The LLM parameterized by
\theta
generates thought step
S_{t}
autoregressively, conditioning on the KV cache container
\mathcal{C}_{t}
:

S_{t}\sim P_{\theta}(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal{C}_{t}).

(1)

Initially,
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is set to
\{\tilde{Q}\}
, i.e., the KV cache of the question
Q
. As a by-product of autoregressive decoding, the KV cache
\tilde{S}_{t}
of
S_{t}
is stored.

Step 2: Compress
S_{t}
.

Once
S_{t}
is complete, the model compresses its content via memory tokens
M_{t}
by performing a forward pass:

\tilde{M}_{t}=\mathrm{LLM}_{\theta}(M_{t}\mid\mathcal{C}_{t},\,\tilde{S}_{t}),

(2)

where the model attends to both the currently retained KV cache
\mathcal{C}_{t}
and the newly generated
\tilde{S}_{t}
to produce the compressed KV cache
\tilde{M}_{t}
.
Intuitively,
\tilde{M}_{t}
encodes the information in
S_{t}
into a compact representation that can be accessed by future steps.

Step 3: Update
\mathcal{C}_{t}
.

After compression, we update the KV cache container for the next step.
The question cache
\tilde{Q}
and all memory token caches
\tilde{M}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{M}_{t}
are always retained, as in existing CoT compression methods.
Unlike existing methods, HybridThinker also temporarily retains

\tilde{S}_{t}
, serving the next
w-1
steps with fine-grained
information:

\mathcal{C}_{t+1}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{C}_{t}\cup\{\tilde{S}_{t},\,\tilde{M}_{t}\}&\text{if }1\leq t<w\\[8.0pt]
\begin{aligned} &\bigl(\mathcal{C}_{t}\setminus\{\tilde{S}_{t-w+1}\}\bigr)\\
&\qquad\cup\;\{\tilde{S}_{t},\,\tilde{M}_{t}\}\end{aligned}&\text{if }t\geq w\end{cases}

(3)

where
\setminus
denotes set difference.
In other words, in general (i.e. for
t\geq w
), once a thought step is completed with its associated memory tokens generated: 1)
\tilde{S}_{t}
and
\tilde{M}_{t}
are pushed into the KV cache container
C_{t}
for the subsequent CoT generations 2) the thought step KV cache
\tilde{S}_{t-w+1}
that was generated
w-1
steps before is popped out from the container
C_{t}
.
Figure
1
(c) illustrates the inference procedure of HybridThinker.

3.3
HybridThinker Training

We fine-tune an LLM with CoT data to obtain a model capable of the inference procedure above.
Following other CoT compression methods, our approach requires only two modifications over standard LLM training: reconstructing the training data and adjusting the attention mask.

Data Reconstruction.

Each training instance is a pair
(Q,Y)
.
Following
30
, we segment
Y
into
k
thought steps
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
using
‘‘\n\n’’
as the delimiter, then insert a memory token sequence
M_{t}
after each intermediate step
S_{t}
(
t<k
) to form the training sequence:

\begin{split}X&=Q\oplus S_{1}\oplus M_{1}\oplus S_{2}\oplus M_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus S_{k}\\
&=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{|X|}),\end{split}

(4)

where
\oplus
denotes concatenation and
x_{u}
is the
u
-th token of
X
.
All
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k-1}
share the same token content but occupy different positions.

Attention Mask Construction.

The most natural way to construct the training attention mask is to replicate the inference-time attention pattern directly.
At inference time, information in each thought step
S_{t}
reaches subsequent steps via two pathways: (i) an
compression pathway
, in which
S_{t}
passes coarse-grained information through the memory tokens
M_{t}
; and (ii) a
direct pathway
, in which
S_{t}
is directly attended to by the subsequent
w-1
steps.
Directly replicating this pattern during training yields what we call
Shortcut Attention
:
as illustrated on the top of Figure
2
(a), because the direct pathway provides a much easier route for the model to retrieve information about
S_{t}
, training with Shortcut Attention leads to a
shortcut learning
problem: the model over-relies on the direct pathway and the compression pathway receives insufficient training signal. We illustrate the Shortcut Attention at the bottom of Figure
2
(a) and use it in our experiments as the natural baseline.

To remedy this, one could instead mask out the direct pathway entirely, forcing all information to pass through
M_{t}
as the sole bottleneck.
We call this
Bottleneck Attention
. In Figure
2
(b), we illustrate its motivation (top) as well as its attention implementations (bottom).
While Bottleneck Attention effectively trains the compression pathway, it provides no training signal for the direct pathway that is active at inference time, leading to a train–inference mismatch.

To address the limitations of both attention patterns, we propose
Hybrid Attention
(Figure
2
(c)), which randomly assigns each thought step to either Shortcut Attention or Bottleneck Attention within the same training sequence.
This ensures both pathways receive adequate training.

Concretely, we randomly partition the
k
thought steps into two disjoint sets
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{shortcut}}=\{S_{t}\mid t\in\mathcal{I}\}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bottleneck}}=\{S_{t}\mid t\notin\mathcal{I}\}
, where
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}
is sampled uniformly at random.
The resulting attention mask
\mathbf{A}\in\{0,1\}^{|X|\times|X|}
is defined as:

\mathbf{A}_{u,v}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x_{v}\in Q,\ v\leq u\\[8.0pt]
1&\text{if }x_{v}\in M_{t},\ v\leq u\\[8.0pt]
1&\text{if }\parbox[t]{172.5pt}{$x_{v}\in S_{t},\ S_{t}\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{bottleneck}},$\\
$x_{u}\in S_{t}\cup M_{t},\ v\leq u$}\\[22.0pt]
1&\text{if }\parbox[t]{172.5pt}{$x_{v}\in S_{t},\ S_{t}\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{shortcut}},\ v\leq u,$\\
$x_{u}\in\bigcup_{t^{\prime}=t}^{t+w-1}(S_{t^{\prime}}\cup M_{t^{\prime}})$}\\[22.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(5)

Rows 1–2 ensure that
Q
and all memory tokens are globally visible.
Row 3 implements Bottleneck Attention:
S_{t}
is only visible within its own step and to the immediately following
M_{t}
.
Row 4 implements Shortcut Attention:
S_{t}
is additionally visible to the next
w-1
steps and their memory tokens.

Training Objective.

We minimise the standard cross-entropy loss over the thought step tokens:

\mathcal{L}(\theta)=-\sum_{x_{u}\in S_{t}}\log P_{\theta}\!\left(x_{u}\;\middle|\;\mathcal{X}_{u}\right),

(6)

where
\mathcal{X}_{u}=\{x_{v}\mid\mathbf{A}_{u,v}=1,\,v<u\}
is the set of tokens at positions strictly preceding
u
and visible to position
u
.
Tokens in
Q
and
M_{t}
are excluded from the loss.

(3) Training-free acceleration techniques: H

Evaluation Metrics and Datasets.

We evaluate HybridThinker on four reasoning benchmarks: GSM8K
(
3
)
, MMLU
(
7
)
, GPQA
(

)
, and BBH
(
20
)

Implementation Details.

All trainable methods are trained for 5 epochs with a batch size of 64 and a maximum training sequence length of 4096 tokens.
For HybridThinker, the number of memory tokens

4.2
Main Results

Table
1
shows our main results. On Qwen2.5-7B, HybridThinker matches the accuracy of the uncompressed baseline Vanilla (68.78%) while reducing peak token usage by 61.7% and inference time by 20.3%. On Llama3.1-8B, HybridThinker (66.98%) also approaches Vanilla (68.99%). Compared with LightThinker, which discards thought steps immediately after compression, HybridThinker achieves a clear accuracy advantage across both models and all four benchmarks, outperforming it by 5.8 points on Qwen2.5-7B and 2.7 points on Llama3.1-8B on average, at the modest cost of retaining only about 100–150 more peak tokens, which translates to around 10% more peak token usage. Notably, this accuracy gain does not come at the expense of inference speed: HybridThinker generates shorter CoT sequences than LightThinker (see Section
5.2
), which offsets the overhead of the additional retained tokens and yields comparable or even slightly faster inference.
HybridThinker also outperforms KV cache pruning methods including H
2
O and SepLLM
with generally significant less total inference time.
These results demonstrate that temporarily retaining thought steps to supply fine-grained local information to several subsequent steps effectively improves accuracy over existing CoT compression, establishing HybridThinker as a strong CoT compression approach.

4.3
Ablation Results

We analyze the effects of temporarily retaining thought steps and the hybrid training scheme and show our results in Table
2
.

Temporarily retaining thought steps during inference improves reasoning accuracy.

When thought steps are temporarily retained during inference (i.e. Shortcut Attention inference), the method naively trained with the Shortcut Attention achieves an average accuracy of 66.87%; HybridThinker with a carefully designed training method trained with the Hybrid Attention improves the average accuracy further to 68.78%. Comparatively, LightThinker which discards thought steps immediately during inference (i.e. Bottleneck Attention inference) achieves significant lower average accuracy at 62.94%. This confirms that having each complete thought step serve only a few subsequent steps already yields a significant accuracy improvement.

Training with Hybrid Attention achieves the best accuracy–efficiency trade
-
off.

With shortcut inference, shortcut
-
only training reaches reasonable accuracy (66.87%) but suffers from much higher inference time (25.14 vs. 17.15) and peak token usage (1836 vs. 1264).
We hypothesize that unrestricted direct access during training prevents the model from learning to rely on compressed memory, causing redundant reasoning and a longer chain (see Section
5.3
).
Hybrid training avoids this shortcut learning by jointly training both the direct and compression pathways, resulting in the highest accuracy (68.78%) together with the lowest latency and peak memory among temporary retention settings. Even under bottleneck inference, hybrid training performs slightly better than bottleneck
-
only training (63.28% vs. 62.94%), indicating that the hybrid pattern does not harm compression ability.

Overall, the results show that temporarily retaining thought steps is critical for accuracy, and the hybrid training scheme effectively optimizes both information pathways for superior performance.

6
Conclusion

We propose HybridThinker, which akin to CoT compression condenses thought steps into memory tokens but temporarily retains thought steps to supply fine-grained information together with memory tokens for inference. We discover that while such inference process is effective generally, training the model that aligns training and inference is suboptimal due to shortcut learning. To address this, we propose a hybrid training scheme mixing shortcut and bottleneck learning in attention. Experiments on 4 benchmarks show a 5.8 point average accuracy gain over existing CoT compression methods, matching the uncompressed baseline while reducing peak token usage by 61.7% and inference time by 20.3%. Compared to existing CoT compression methods, HybridThinker achieves higher average accuracy with similar inference time and slightly more (
\sim 10\%
) peak memory usage.

Limitations

Temporarily retaining thought steps slightly increases peak KV cache usage compared to methods that discard steps immediately.
As shown in Figure
3
(a), this can be effectively mitigated by reducing the number of memory tokens
L
: with
L=3
, HybridThinker already achieves lower peak usage than LightThinker while maintaining a 2.5 point accuracy advantage.
Our approach also assumes explicit step delimiters; extending it to support free-form reasoning without clear boundaries is left for future work.
