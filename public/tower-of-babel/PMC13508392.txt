Combined AGT M235T-ACE I/D genotype and risk of essential hypertension in Vietnamese adults: a hospital-based case-control study
Authors: Thanh Tuan Tran, Ngoc Hoa Chau, Duc Khanh Nguyen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEssential hypertension is influenced by conventional risk factors and genetic susceptibility. We tested the prespecified hypothesis that the combined AGT M235T-ACE I/D TT/DD genotype is associated with essential hypertension in Vietnamese adults.MethodsWe conducted a hospital-based case-control study among 471 Vietnamese Kinh adults at the University Medical Center, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam, including 238 cardiology-clinic participants with essential hypertension and 233 health-screening participants without hypertension. Hypertension was defined according to Vietnamese guideline thresholds as office systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mmHg, or current antihypertensive treatment; controls had no history of hypertension, were not receiving antihypertensive medication, and had office blood pressure < 140/90 mmHg. The primary exposure was TT/DD versus non-TT/DD. Multivariable logistic regression adjusted for age, sex, overweight or obesity, diabetes mellitus, salt-rich diet, smoking, alcohol misuse, physical inactivity, and family history of hypertension.ResultsTT/DD was observed in 37 of 238 cases (15.55%) and 15 of 233 controls (6.44%). TT/DD was associated with hypertension in crude analysis (OR 2.67, 95% CI 1.38–5.40; p = 0.002) and remained associated after adjustment (adjusted OR 3.26, 95% CI 1.53–6.92; p = 0.002). In an exploratory six-category model using (MM + MT)/II as the reference, TT/DD was the only genotype category significantly associated with hypertension, although the estimate was imprecise (adjusted OR 3.46, 95% CI 1.24–9.70; p = 0.018).ConclusionsThe combined AGT M235T-ACE I/D TT/DD genotype was associated with essential hypertension in this hospital-based Vietnamese sample. Because of the case-control design, hospital-based recruitment, covariate imbalance, and absence of external replication, the finding should be interpreted as associative and hypothesis-generating.

Background

Hypertension remains one of the leading contributors to cardiovascular morbidity and mortality worldwide. A pooled analysis of 1201 population-representative studies estimated that the number of adults aged 30–79 years with hypertension doubled from 1990 to 2019, from approximately 650 million to 1.28 billion [1]. In Vietnam, serial national and community screening datasets have likewise shown a substantial burden of hypertension and a rising prevalence over time [2–5].

Essential hypertension accounts for most hypertension cases and is a complex disease arising from interactions among age, sex, adiposity, diet, diabetes, environmental exposures, and inherited susceptibility [6, 7]. Family studies and multi-generational cohort data support an important heritable contribution to blood pressure variation and hypertension risk [7, 8].

The renin-angiotensin system (RAS) is one of the most biologically plausible pathways involved in blood pressure regulation and hypertension pathogenesis [9, 10]. Within this pathway, angiotensinogen encoded by AGT serves as the substrate for renin, while angiotensin-converting enzyme encoded by ACE catalyzes the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II and contributes to bradykinin degradation [9, 10].

The AGT M235T polymorphism is a nonsynonymous variant in exon 2 first described in relation to human hypertension by Jeunemaitre and colleagues [11]. Experimental and epidemiologic evidence suggests that the T allele is associated with higher circulating angiotensinogen levels and greater blood pressure response to salt intake [12, 13]. Meta-analytic and case-control studies in Asian populations have also reported an association between M235T and essential hypertension [14–16].

The ACE I/D polymorphism is characterized by the insertion or deletion of a 287-bp Alu sequence in intron 16 and has been associated with variation in ACE activity [12]. Because AGT acts upstream and ACE downstream within the same biological pathway, we selected the TT/DD doublet a priori as a focused pathway-based hypothesis rather than as a genome-wide polygenic risk score. This approach does not imply that the two-locus model is superior to a polygenic risk score or to a more comprehensive multilocus model; rather, it evaluates whether a biologically coherent AGT-ACE genotype combination is associated with essential hypertension in this Vietnamese sample.

Methods

Study design and setting

This hospital-based case-control study used data from Vietnamese Kinh adults recruited at the cardiology clinic and health screening department of the University Medical Center, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. Cases were recruited from a cardiology clinic and controls from a general health screening department; therefore, both groups were hospital-based but came from different clinical streams. Study preparation, protocol development, and ethics application were conducted from May 2018 to December 2018. Participant recruitment and sample collection took place from 1 January 2019 to 1 May 2019. The present analysis focuses on the prespecified AGT M235T and ACE I/D component of the source genetic association protocol. The underlying dataset, which is publicly available in Zenodo [17], comprised 471 participants, including 238 patients with essential hypertension and 233 non-hypertensive controls. The doctoral thesis on which this manuscript is based reported a minimum required sample size of 192 hypertensive participants and 192 controls; the final sample therefore exceeded the calculated requirement [18].

Participants

Eligibility criteria for the hypertension group included age 18 years or older, Kinh ethnicity, and diagnosis of essential hypertension according to the 2015 recommendations of the Vietnam Society of Hypertension and the Vietnam National Heart Association as used in the source protocol [18]. The diagnostic threshold was office systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg, or current antihypertensive treatment, consistent with Vietnamese guideline thresholds [18, 19]. Additional inclusion criteria included hypertension detected before 60 years of age and written informed consent. Exclusion criteria were pregnancy, secondary hypertension, malignancy, and severe acute illness within the previous 6 months. Control participants were Kinh adults aged 18 years or older attending the health screening department, with no history of hypertension, no current antihypertensive treatment, and office blood pressure < 140/90 mmHg at screening. The analysis was restricted to Vietnamese Kinh participants to reduce population stratification in this genetic association study, consistent with STREGA reporting considerations, and because Kinh is the majority ethnic group in Vietnam; this restriction also limits generalizability to other ethnic groups [20]. Among hypertensive participants, 37 individuals (15.55%) were newly diagnosed and untreated at enrollment.

Outcome, exposures, and covariates

The primary outcome was essential hypertension versus participants without hypertension. This analysis focused on two RAS polymorphisms, AGT M235T and ACE I/D. Because the AGT MM genotype was rare, the original analysis collapsed MM and MT into a single category in combined-genotype models. Combined genotypes were classified as (MM + MT)/II, (MM + MT)/ID, (MM + MT)/DD, TT/II, TT/ID, and TT/DD. The prespecified primary exposure for this manuscript was TT/DD versus non-TT/DD.

Covariates were selected from the source protocol and baseline risk-factor analyses and included age, sex, overweight or obesity, diabetes mellitus, salt-rich diet, smoking, alcohol misuse, physical inactivity, and family history of hypertension. Overweight or obesity was defined as body mass index ≥23 kg/m2, consistent with the Asian-population public health action threshold for increased cardiometabolic risk [21]. Diabetes mellitus was defined by a previous diagnosis or diagnostic criteria consistent with ADA 2016 criteria, including fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L), 2-hour plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during an oral glucose tolerance test, HbA1c ≥6.5%, or random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with classical hyperglycemic symptoms [22]. Salt-rich diet was based on structured self-report and defined as adding undiluted fish sauce or soy sauce to already seasoned food or consuming salty dishes on more than 3 days per week; this local operational definition is consistent with STEPS-style dietary salt questions on adding salt or salty sauce to food and consuming high-salt foods, but it was not validated against 24-hour urinary sodium excretion [18, 23]. Smoking was defined as having ever smoked and still smoking within the previous 30 days, consistent with current-use survey definitions [23]. Alcohol misuse was defined as more than 1 standard drink unit per day in women or more than 2 standard drink units per day in men, or unsafe drinking episodes of ≥4 standard drink units per day in women or ≥5 standard drink units per day in men; one standard drink unit was defined as 10 g of pure ethanol [24, 25]. Adequate physical activity was defined as at least 30 min per day on at least 5 days per week, corresponding to 150 min per week of moderate-intensity activity, and participants below this threshold were classified as physically inactive [26]. Family history of hypertension was defined as hypertension in either parent [18].

Genotyping and quality control

Peripheral venous blood was collected in EDTA tubes. Genomic DNA was extracted from whole-blood leukocytes within 24 h using the GenJET Whole Blood Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) according to the manufacturer protocol. AGT M235T was genotyped using allele-specific tetra-primer PCR, and ACE I/D was genotyped using PCR with agarose-gel electrophoretic size discrimination. PCR products were checked on 2% agarose gels stained with Diamond Nucleic Acid Dye and visualized with a Geldoc-It imaging system. AGT genotypes were called from 118-bp and 204-bp allele-specific products, whereas ACE genotypes were called from 206-bp (D allele) and 510-bp (I allele) products. Each PCR run included a no-template negative control and a positive control sample with genotype confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Genotyping was performed at the Center for Molecular Biomedicine, University of Medicine and Pharmacy at Ho Chi Minh City; ASO-PCR/PCR assays were internally checked against Sanger sequencing and NCBI reference sequences [18]. The final analytic dataset contained complete AGT M235T and ACE I/D genotype data for all 471 participants.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as mean +/- standard deviation and categorical variables as number (percentage). Group comparisons used Student t test, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, Kruskal-Wallis test, chi-square test, or Fisher exact test, as appropriate. Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium was assessed among control participants for each locus before genotype-category collapsing. The overall distribution of the six combined AGT M235T-ACE I/D genotype categories was compared between hypertensive and control groups using Pearson chi-square testing. Crude odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated first for the six-category combined-genotype model using (MM + MT)/II as the reference, and then for the prespecified binary comparison TT/DD versus non-TT/DD. Multivariable logistic regression was used to estimate adjusted ORs after controlling for age, sex, overweight or obesity, diabetes mellitus, salt-rich diet, smoking, alcohol misuse, physical inactivity, and family history of hypertension. Because the six-category reference cell was small, that model was interpreted as exploratory; the binary TT/DD versus non-TT/DD model was treated as the primary model. The final analytic dataset contained no missing values for the outcome, the combined genotypes, or the covariates included in the multivariable models. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Results

A total of 471 participants were analyzed, including 238 hypertensive cases and 233 non-hypertensive controls. Baseline characteristics are shown in Table 1. Hypertensive participants were older than controls (45.77 ± 9.49 vs. 37.17 ± 9.79 years, p < 0.001), were more likely to be aged 40 years or older (75.63% vs. 35.19%, p < 0.001), and were more often male (53.36% vs. 40.34%, p = 0.006). They also had a higher prevalence of salt-rich diet, family history of hypertension, overweight or obesity, and diabetes mellitus. These imbalances were accounted for in multivariable models but remain important when interpreting possible residual confounding.

Discussion

In this hospital-based Vietnamese study, the combined AGT M235T-ACE I/D TT/DD genotype was associated with essential hypertension after adjustment for conventional risk factors. This finding should be interpreted as an association in a case-control sample rather than evidence of causality. The prespecified binary TT/DD versus non-TT/DD comparison was consistent with the exploratory six-category model, but the latter had a small reference cell and wide confidence intervals.

The association is biologically plausible but remains mechanistic inference rather than direct evidence. AGT encodes the upstream substrate angiotensinogen, while ACE catalyzes the downstream generation of angiotensin II and contributes to bradykinin degradation [9, 10, 27]. Prior studies have shown that the AGT T allele is associated with higher angiotensinogen concentrations and that the ACE D allele is associated with higher ACE activity [12, 13]. However, this study did not measure circulating angiotensinogen, ACE activity, angiotensin II, aldosterone, urinary sodium, or longitudinal blood pressure trajectories; therefore, the data cannot confirm pathway activation, sodium retention, or temporal causality.

Our findings align with previous literature indicating that AGT M235T is associated with essential hypertension in several populations [14–16]. However, most prior studies evaluated single loci rather than focused pathway-based genotype combinations. The present analysis suggests that the AGT-ACE TT/DD doublet may be a useful hypothesis for further study, but it should not be interpreted as a validated polygenic risk marker or as superior to broader genome-wide or polygenic approaches.

This study has several strengths. The final sample exceeded the calculated minimum sample size, the numbers of cases and controls were similar, key conventional risk factors were operationally defined and incorporated into multivariable adjustment, genotyping used PCR-based assays with laboratory quality-control procedures and complete genotype data in the final analytic dataset, control genotype distributions were consistent with Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, and the analysis focused on a prespecified biologically coherent genotype combination rather than an arbitrary panel of markers.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center hospital-based study in which cases were recruited from a cardiology clinic and controls from a health screening department. These source populations may differ in healthcare-seeking behavior and baseline hospital utilization, so selection bias cannot be excluded. Second, hypertensive participants were older and differed from controls in several conventional risk factors. Although age, sex, overweight or obesity, diabetes mellitus, salt-rich diet, smoking, alcohol misuse, physical inactivity, and family history were included in multivariable models, residual confounding remains possible, especially because age was substantially imbalanced between groups. Third, behavioral covariates were collected by self-report and may be affected by recall or social-desirability bias. The salt-rich diet variable was a local, subjective operational definition based on adding salty condiments or frequent salty dishes and was not validated by 24-hour urinary sodium excretion; smoking, alcohol misuse, and physical inactivity were likewise subject to misclassification. Fourth, the rare AGT MM genotype required collapsing with MT, which was statistically reasonable but reduced granularity. Fifth, the exploratory six-category model used a small reference group, creating imprecision and possible model instability; therefore, the prespecified binary TT/DD model should be emphasized over individual six-category contrasts. Sixth, no external replication cohort or functional laboratory validation study was available. Seventh, the restriction to Kinh adults reduced population heterogeneity but limits generalizability to other Vietnamese ethnic groups. Finally, this was not a prediction study, and TT/DD should not be interpreted as a stand-alone screening, diagnostic, prognostic, or treatment-selection marker.

Conclusions

The combined AGT M235T-ACE I/D TT/DD genotype was associated with higher odds of essential hypertension in this hospital-based Vietnamese Kinh adult sample after adjustment for measured conventional risk factors. The result supports a focused AGT-ACE pathway-based association hypothesis but does not establish causality, clinical prediction utility, or mechanism. Larger population-based studies with better matching or prospective designs, objective sodium assessment, external replication, and functional validation are needed.
