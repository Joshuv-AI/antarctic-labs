ON THE SADOWSKY FUNCTIONAL FOR ANISOTROPIC RIBBONS
Giovanni Savaré

Abstract
The equilibrium shape of a thin, elastic, inextensible ribbon minimizes its bending energy. It has been shown that, as the width of the ribbon tends to zero, the bending energy Γ \Gamma -converges to the so called Sadowsky functional. In this paper we consider geometrically frustrated anisotropic ribbons with a possibly curved reference configuration. We prove that the Γ \Gamma -convergence remains valid under prescribed affine boundary conditions, including, in particular, those satisfied by a Möbius strip.

Introduction

Thin inextensible ribbons can be modeled as two-dimensional surfaces that are locally isometric to a flat reference configuration.
The equilibrium shape of a ribbon is a minimiser of the deformation energy of the strip, which is entirely due to bending, as it was first pointed out by Sadowsky in
[
8
]
. In
[
9
]
Sadowsky raised the question of finding the characteristic equilibrium shape of a Möbius band, by minimising the bending energy over the class of developable surfaces, subject to the boundary conditions of a Möbius strip. Since a Möbius strip can be obtained by glueing together the two short sides of a rectangle after a half twist, these boundary conditions can be expressed as affine constraints on the short sides of a rectangular reference configuration.
If we denote by
S_{\varepsilon}=(0,\ell)\times(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2)
the reference configuration of the ribbon, where
\ell>0
and
\varepsilon>0
are the length and the width of the strip respectively, then, according to Sadowsky, the bending energy of a deformation
u:S_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given (up to a multiplicative constant) by

E_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{\varepsilon}}Q(\Pi_{u}(x)-\Pi^{nat}_{\varepsilon})\,\text{d}x,

(1)

where
\Pi_{u}
is the second fundamental form of
u
,
\Pi^{nat}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}\bigl((0,\ell);\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}\bigl)
represents either the second fundamental form of a certain deformation, or a target curvature tensor, and
Q:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is the bending energy density, which we will always assume to be a positive definite quadratic form. The term
\Pi_{\varepsilon}^{nat}
allows to include in the analysis geometrically frustrated ribbons or ribbons whose natural configuration is not necessarily flat. Finally, the energy density
Q
is a general quadratic form accounting for a possible elastic anisotropy of the material.

The isometry constraint on the ribbons reads as
(\nabla u)^{T}\cdot(\nabla u)=\mathbb{I}.
This is a nonlinear relation that makes highly nontrivial to perform variations of
E_{\varepsilon}
and to compute the Euler-Lagrange equations.
In order to simplify this problem, Sadowsky considered a dimension reduction approach. In
[
8
]
he argued that for infinitesimally narrow bands (that is, as
\varepsilon\to 0
), the energy
E_{\varepsilon}
reduces to

\hat{E}(y)=\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{(k^{2}+\tau^{2})^{2}}{k^{2}}\,\text{d}s,

(2)

where
k
and
\tau
are, respectively, the curvature and the torsion of the centerline
y
of the strip, and
s
is its arc-length. However, Sadowsky did not provide any proof of this result, and he tacitly assumed the centerline of the band to have strictly positive curvature.

Wunderlich in
[
15
]
gave a formal justification of Sadowsky’s claim, showing that, under the assumption of nonvanishing curvature, the energy
\hat{E}
is in fact the pointwise limit of
E_{\varepsilon}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0.

An other justification of (
2
) was proposed by Kirby and Fried in
[
12
]
. Again under the assumption that the curvature of the centerline is bounded away from zero, they proved that
\hat{E}
is the
\Gamma
-limit of
E_{\varepsilon}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, with respect to the strong topology of
W^{3,p}(0,\ell),
with
p\geq 1
. However, this topology is not the natural one, since compactness of sequences of minimisers fails, and thus the Fundamental Theorem of
\Gamma-
convergence cannot be applied.

In
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
Freddi, Hornung, Mora and Paroni gave a rigorous justification of Sadowsky’s claim by
\Gamma
-convergence in the natural topology, and showed that the expression of the limit functional (
2
) needs to be corrected for small curvatures.
In
[
6
]
, Freddi, Hornung, Mora and Paroni studied the
\Gamma-
convergence of the functionals
E_{\varepsilon}
(after a proper rescaling) defined on the space of isometries belonging to
W^{2,2}(S_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. They considered a general positive definite quadratic form
Q
, and a general flat reference configuration, but they did not enforce any boundary conditions. In this context the energies
E_{\varepsilon}
, properly rescaled,
\Gamma-
converge to the functional

J(y,d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})\coloneqq\int_{I}\bar{Q}(x_{1},d_{1}^{\prime}\cdot d_{3},d_{2}^{\prime}\cdot d_{3})\,\text{d}x_{1}

(3)

(see (
22
)),
defined on the space
\mathcal{A}_{0}
. The energy density
\bar{Q}
in (
3
) is defined in (
21
). The elements of the space
\mathcal{A}_{0}
are framed curves
(y,(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}))
, where
y
represents the centerline of the limiting strip,
d_{1}=y^{\prime}
is the tangent to the centerline,
d_{2}
represents the transversal orientation of the strip, and
d_{3}
is the normal to the strip. The frame
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
may be not orthonormal, and is related to the geometry of the reference configuration, see (
15
).

The main goal of this work is to prove that this last more general
\Gamma
-convergence result is stable under prescribed affine boundary conditions. In particular, we require the “short left side” of the strip to be clamped, and the “right short side” to be isometrically deformed, see Subsection
2.3
for a more precise statement. These boundary conditions are the same enforced in
[
7
]
, where the
\Gamma
-convergence has been studied in the case
Q(M)=|M|^{2}
and under the assumption of a rectangular stress-free configuration.

While the liminf inequality and the compactness Lemma easily follow from the corresponding results in
[
6
]
, the proof of the limsup inequality requires some highly non trivial changes, although the strategy is the same as in
[
7
]
. The original contribution of this work is contained in Section
3
, and is mostly given by Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, which give a refinement of the relaxation result
[
6
]
[Proposition 9]. This result is a fundamental building block for the recovery sequence and is the following. Let

Q^{0}(M)=\begin{cases}Q(M)&\text{ if }\det(M)=0,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{\mathcal{F}}:L^{2}(I;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym})\to[0,+\infty],\;\;\hat{\mathcal{F}}(M)=\int_{I}Q^{0}(M(x))\,\text{d}x.

In
[
6
, Proposition 9]
the authors proved that the lower semicontinuous envelope of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
with respect to the weak topology of
L^{2}(I;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym})
is the functional

\mathcal{F}:L^{2}(I,\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym})\to[0,+\infty],\;\;\mathcal{F}(M)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\ell}\Bigl(Q(M(x))+\alpha^{+}(\det M(x))^{+}+\alpha^{-}(\det M(x))^{-}\Bigl)\,\text{d}x,

where the constants
\alpha^{+},\alpha^{-}
are defined in (
19
) and (
20
).
In Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
we give a refinement of this result. Moreover, given a piecewise constant function
M\in L^{2}(I;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym})
we explicitly construct a sequence
M^{n}\subset L^{2}(I;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym})
weakly converging to
M
in
L^{2}
and such that
\hat{\mathcal{F}}(M^{n})\to\mathcal{F}(M)
. This construction requires a completely new strategy with respect to the isotropic case in
[
7
]
.
For more details we refer to the discussion after Remark
4
.

Here we provide an outline of the construction of the recovery sequence for Theorem
2.1
.
Suppose for simplicity that
\Pi_{\varepsilon}^{nat}=0
for every
\varepsilon
, and that the reference configuration of the Möbius strip is the rectangle
(0,\ell)\times(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2)
(see Subsection
2.1
for the general case). Let
(y,R)\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
Roughly speaking, the strategy consists in first constructing a sequence of framed curves
(y_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon})
converging to
(y,R)
, and then extending these curves to isometries
u_{\varepsilon}
with the prescribed boundary conditions. Each of these isometries is defined as a ruled surface with centerline
y_{\varepsilon}
. A necessary condition for
u_{\varepsilon}
to be an isometry is that its second fundamental form has zero determinant (according to Gauss’ Theorema Egregium). The approximating sequence
(y_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon})
is chosen in such a way that this constraint is satisfied on the centerline.

Setting
\mu(t)\coloneqq d^{\prime}_{1}(t)\cdot d_{3}(t)
and
\tau(t)\coloneqq d_{2}^{\prime}(t)\cdot d_{3}(t)
, the limiting energy rewrites as

J(y,R)=\mathcal{F}(M),
where
\displaystyle M=\begin{pmatrix}\mu&\tau\\
\tau&\gamma\end{pmatrix},

for a suitable function
\gamma\in L^{2}(I).

By the relaxation result
[
6
, Proposition 9]
there is a sequence
(M^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
weakly converging to
M
in
L^{2}
such that
\det(M^{\varepsilon})=0
and
\mathcal{F}(M^{\varepsilon})\to\mathcal{F}(M)
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
. For every
\varepsilon>0
we set
\mu_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq M^{\varepsilon}_{11}
and
\tau_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq M^{\varepsilon}_{12}
. A frame
(y_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon})
satisfying
\mu_{\varepsilon}=(d^{\varepsilon}_{1})^{\prime}\cdot d_{3}^{\varepsilon},\,\tau_{\varepsilon}=(d^{\varepsilon}_{2})^{\prime}\cdot d^{\varepsilon}_{3}
and
(d^{\varepsilon}_{1})^{\prime}\cdot d^{\varepsilon}_{2}=0
can now be obtained by solving a suitable Cauchy problem (see also (
25
)).
Using that
\det(M^{\varepsilon}(x))=0
a.e. in
(0,\ell)
by construction,
[
6
, Proposition 13]
shows that
M^{\varepsilon}
can be interpreted as the second fundamental form of an isometry
u_{\varepsilon}
restricted to the midline of the strip. Since
\mathcal{F}(M^{\varepsilon})\to\mathcal{F}(M)
, the convergence
E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\to\mathcal{F}(M)=J(y,R)
easily follows.

However the isometries
u_{\varepsilon}
do not satisfy in general the required boundary conditions. To solve this issue the sequence
(M^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
has to be chosen so that
(y_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon})
satisfies the same boundary conditions as the elements of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
In order to do so, we follow the approach of
[
7
]
: using some results due to Hornung
[
11
]
, we can find perturbations
\tilde{\tau}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
of
\tau_{\varepsilon}
and
\mu_{\varepsilon}
respectively, such that the framed curves
(\tilde{y}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{R}_{\varepsilon})
associated with
\tilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
and
\tilde{\tau}_{\varepsilon}
weakly converge to
(y,R)
, and have the same boundary conditions. The energy associated with the deformed frame is
\mathcal{F}(\tilde{M}^{\varepsilon})
, with

\tilde{M}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\tilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}e_{1}\otimes e_{1}+\tilde{\tau}_{\varepsilon}(e_{1}\otimes e_{2}+e_{2}\otimes e_{1})+\tilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}e_{2}\otimes e_{2},

where
\tilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}

is uniquely determined through the zero determinant constraint
\tilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{\tau}_{\varepsilon}^{2}
whenever
\tilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\neq 0
. We note that, if
\tilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
is close to zero, we may lose control of
\tilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore, in order to achieve the convergence
{\mathcal{F}}(\tilde{M}^{\varepsilon})\to\mathcal{F}(M),
it is crucial to bound
\mu_{\varepsilon}
(and
\tilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
) away from zero, so that also
\tilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}
remains bounded. Addressing this issue requires a refinement of the relaxation result of
[
6
]
. Addressing this issue is the original contribution of this work, and is the main topic of Section
3
(see also the discussion after Remark
4
for a detailed outline of Section
3
).

2
Setting of the problem

2.1
Reference configuration

Given
\ell>0
we denote
I=(0,\ell)
and
\Omega=I\times(-1/2,1/2).
For
\varepsilon>0
, we define
\rho_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2})\coloneqq(x_{1},\varepsilon x_{2})
and the rectangular region
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\rho_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)=I\times(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2)
. The reference configuration of the inextensible elastic strip is
S_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\chi(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an orientation preserving injective map of class
C^{2}
such that

|\partial_{1}\chi(x_{1},0)|=1\;\forall\,x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}.

(4)

In the following we call
B(t)\coloneqq\chi(t,0)
the midline of the reference configuration, which is parametrised by arc length because of (
4
).
By composing
\chi
with an orientation preserving isometry, we may further assume that

\chi(0,0)=(0,0)
and
\partial_{1}\chi(0,0)=e_{1}.

2.2
The energy density

We describe the spatial configuration of the thin inextensible elastic ribbon with an isometry
u\in W^{2,2}(S_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. The inextensibility constraint reads

(\nabla u)^{T}(\nabla u)=\mathbb{I},

where
\mathbb{I}
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix.
We call
\nu_{u}\coloneqq\partial_{1}u\wedge\partial_{2}u
the unit normal to the deformed configuration, and we denote by
\Pi_{u}:S_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}
the second fundamental form of the deformation, defined by
(\Pi_{u})_{\alpha\beta}\coloneqq\nu_{u}\cdot\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u
. Since
u
is an isometry, by Gauss’ Theorema Egregium the Gaussian curvature of the strip is zero, that is,
\det(\Pi_{u})=0
in
S_{\varepsilon}
.

The elastic energy of a spatial configuration
u
of the ribbon is given by

E_{\varepsilon}(u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{\varepsilon}}Q\bigl(\Pi_{u}(x)-\Pi_{\varepsilon}^{nat}(x)\bigl)\,\text{d}x=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{K}\bigl(\Pi_{u}(x)-\Pi_{\varepsilon}^{nat}(x)\bigl)\cdot\bigl(\Pi_{u}(x)-\Pi_{\varepsilon}^{nat}(x)\bigl)\,\text{d}x.

(5)

The energy density,
Q:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}\to\mathbb{R}
is a positive definite quadratic form which accounts for the possible material’s anisotropy, and
\mathbb{K}
is the associated linear map from
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}
into itself which, in particular, verifies

\mathbb{K}A\cdot B=\mathbb{K}B\cdot A,\text{ and }Q(A)=\mathbb{K}A\cdot A\geq c|A|^{2}\text{ for every }A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym},\,\text{ for some }c>0.

The quantity
\Pi_{\varepsilon}^{nat}\in L^{2}(S_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym})
in (
5
) is a symmetric tensor field, representing the second fundamental form of a natural configuration, or a target curvature tensor field not necessarily corresponding to a configuration.

2.3
Boundary conditions

In the following, we refer to the left and right short sides of
S_{\varepsilon}
as the images of the curves
t\mapsto\chi(0,t)
and
t\mapsto\chi(\ell,t)
, respectively, which are defined for
t\in(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2)
. Similarly, the images of the left and right short sides under a deformation
u
will be called the left and right short sides of the strip
u(S_{\varepsilon})
.

Let
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\bar{R}^{T}=(\bar{d}_{1}|\bar{d}_{2}|\bar{d}_{3})\in SO(3)
. We impose the following boundary conditions on the short sides of the strip
u(S_{\varepsilon})
:

\begin{cases}&u(0,0)=(0,0,0)\text{ and }u(\chi(\ell,0))=\bar{y},\\
&\nabla u(\chi(0,x_{2}))=(e_{1}|e_{2})\text{ for every }x_{2}\in(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2),\\
&\nabla u(\chi(\ell,x_{2}))=(\bar{d}_{1}|\bar{d}_{2})\text{ for every }x_{2}\in(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2),\end{cases}

(6)

where the equalities are meant in the sense of the boundary traces.
These conditions amount to requiring that the left short side of the strip is clamped, and the right short side is isometrically deformed.

From now on
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
will denote the space of admissible deformations, that is,

\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{u\in W^{2,2}(S_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\>(\nabla u)^{T}(\nabla u)=\mathbb{I}\text{ a.e. in }S_{\varepsilon}\text{ and }u\text{ satisfies }(\ref{eq:bc})\}.

(7)

We point out that the set
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
may be empty for some specific values of
\bar{y}
and
\bar{R}
. For instance, from (
4
) it follows that necessarily

|\bar{y}|=|u(\chi(\ell,0))-u(\chi(0,0))|\leq\ell,
see also
[
7
]
for some characterizations of
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
in the case
\chi(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2})
. We will discuss again this problem in Subsection
2.5
; see also Remark
2
.

2.4
The rescaled energy density

Let
\chi_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\to S_{\varepsilon}
be defined by

\chi_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\chi\circ\rho_{\varepsilon}
. We can associate to a given deformation
u:S_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
the rescaled deformation
y:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined as

y\coloneqq u\circ\chi_{\varepsilon}.

We define
D^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq(\nabla\chi)\circ\rho_{\varepsilon}
, and for
\alpha=1,2
we set
D^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha}\coloneqq((\nabla\chi)\circ\rho_{\varepsilon})e_{\alpha}
the directors defining the geometry of
S_{\varepsilon}
. We point out for future reference that

D^{\varepsilon}\to\nabla\chi(\cdot,0)\eqqcolon D

(8)

uniformly and that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

c\leq\det D^{\varepsilon}(x)\leq 1/c,\text{ and }c\leq|D^{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq 1/c\text{ for every }x\in\Omega.

(9)

It is immediate to see that

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{1}y\cdot\partial_{1}y=D^{\varepsilon}_{1}\cdot D^{\varepsilon}_{1},\\
&\partial_{1}y\cdot\frac{\partial_{2}y}{\varepsilon}=D^{\varepsilon}_{1}\cdot D^{\varepsilon}_{2},\\
&\frac{\partial_{2}y}{\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{\partial_{2}y}{\varepsilon}=D^{\varepsilon}_{2}\cdot D^{\varepsilon}_{2},\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(10)

and

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &y(0,0)=(0,0,0)\text{ and }y(\ell,0)=\bar{y},\\
&\nabla y(0,x_{2})=(e_{1}|e_{2})\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}(0,x_{2})\text{ for }x_{2}\in(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2),\\
&\nabla y(\ell,x_{2})=(\bar{d}_{1}|\bar{d}_{2})\cdot\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}(\ell,x_{2})\text{ for }x_{2}\in(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(11)

In particular,
u\in\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
if and only if the rescaled deformation
y
belongs to the space

\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}^{\Omega}\coloneqq\{y\in W^{2,2}(\Omega:\mathbb{R}^{3}):y\text{ satisfies }(\ref{rescaled_boundary_cond_1})\text{ and }(\ref{rescaled_boundary_cond_2})\}.

(12)

The normal vector to the rescaled configuration is

\nu_{y}\coloneqq\nu_{u}\circ\chi_{\varepsilon},

and following the same computations performed in
[
6
, Section 2.1]
, we find that

\displaystyle\Pi_{u}\circ\chi_{\varepsilon}=(D^{\varepsilon})^{-T}\Pi_{y,\varepsilon}(D^{\varepsilon})^{-1},

where
\Pi_{y,\varepsilon}
is
the rescaled second fundamental form of
y
defined as

\Pi_{y,\varepsilon}=(\nu_{y}\cdot\partial_{1}\partial_{1}y)e_{1}\otimes e_{1}+\nu_{y}\cdot\frac{\partial_{1}\partial_{2}y}{\varepsilon}(e_{1}\otimes e_{2}+e_{2}\otimes e_{1})+\nu_{y}\cdot\frac{\partial_{2}\partial_{2}y}{\varepsilon^{2}}e_{2}\otimes e_{2}.

(13)

Finally, the energy of the strip can be written in terms of the rescaled deformation as
J_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}^{\Omega}\to[0,+\infty)
, defined by

J_{\varepsilon}(y)=\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{K}\bigl((D^{\varepsilon})^{-T}(\Pi_{y,\varepsilon}-\Pi^{0}_{\varepsilon})(D^{\varepsilon})^{-1}\bigl)\cdot\bigl((D^{\varepsilon})^{-T}(\Pi_{y,\varepsilon}-\Pi^{0}_{\varepsilon})(D^{\varepsilon})^{-1}\bigl)\det D^{\varepsilon}\,\text{d}x,

(14)

where we have set

\Pi^{0}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq((D^{\varepsilon})^{T})\Pi^{nat}_{\varepsilon}\circ\chi_{\varepsilon}D^{\varepsilon}.

In this way, we have
J_{\varepsilon}(y)=E_{\varepsilon}(u).

2.5
Compactness and limit space

In the following we will always assume that

\Pi^{0}_{\varepsilon}\to\Pi^{0}\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}),

where
\Pi^{0}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}).

Moreover we set
D_{\alpha}\coloneqq De_{\alpha}
for
\alpha=1,2
.

Limit space

As
\varepsilon
tends to zero, the convergence of the admissible deformations naturally leads, as shown in Lemma
2.1
below, to the class of limiting admissible configurations

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{(y,R)\in W^{2,2}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,2}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}):R^{T}=(d_{1}|d_{2}|d_{3}),

(15)

\displaystyle d_{1}=y^{\prime},\;\;d_{3}=\frac{d_{1}\wedge d_{2}}{|d_{1}\wedge d_{2}|},\;\;d_{\alpha}\cdot d_{\beta}=D_{\alpha}\cdot D_{\beta},\;\;d_{1}^{\prime}\cdot(d_{3}\wedge d_{1})=D_{1}^{\prime}\cdot(e_{3}\wedge D_{1}),

\displaystyle y(0)=0,\;\;y(\ell)=\bar{y},\;\;d_{i}(0)=D_{i}(0)\text{ for }i=1,2,\;\;\Bigl(d_{1}(\ell)|d_{2}(\ell)\Bigl)=(\bar{d}_{1}|\bar{d}_{2})\cdot\nabla\chi(\ell,0)\Bigl\},

where in the equalities
d_{i}(0)=D_{i}(0)
we identify

with a vector in

Compactness

We define the scaled gradient
\nabla_{\varepsilon}
by
\nabla_{\varepsilon}\cdot=(\partial_{1}\cdot|\varepsilon^{-1}\partial_{2}\cdot).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(y_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence of scaled isometries such that
y_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{A}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon>0
and

\sup_{\varepsilon}J_{\varepsilon}(y_{\varepsilon})<\infty.

Then, up to a subsequence, there exists
(y,R)\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
such that, setting
R=(d_{1}|d_{2}|d_{3})^{T},

y_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup y\text{ in }W^{2,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\;\nabla_{\varepsilon}y_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup(d_{1}|d_{2})\text{ in }W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 2}),

(16)

and

A_{y_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}^{\prime}\cdot d_{3}&d_{2}^{\prime}\cdot d_{3}\\
d_{2}^{\prime}\cdot d_{3}&\gamma\end{pmatrix}\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym})

(17)

for some
\gamma\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Proof.

Following the same proof as in
[
6
, Lemma 2]
, we deduce that exists
(y,R)\in W^{2,2}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,2}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})
and a subsequence
(y_{\varepsilon_{n}})_{n}
verifying (
16
), (
17
), and all the properties in the second line of (
15
). To conclude that
(y,R)\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
we only need to verify that the boundary conditions at

and
\ell
are satisfied.

Since
W^{2,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
is compactly embedded in
C(\bar{\Omega}),
the conditions
y(0)=0
and

follow passing to the limit in the relations
y_{\varepsilon}(0,0)=0
and
y_{\varepsilon}(\ell,0)=\bar{y}
.

Moreover, we have
\partial_{1}y_{\varepsilon}(0,x_{2})=(e_{1}|e_{2})\cdot\partial_{1}\chi(0,\varepsilon x_{2})
. Since the right hand side converges uniformly to
(e_{1}|e_{2})\cdot\partial\chi(0,0)
and

in

Similarly, from
\partial_{2}y_{\varepsilon}(0,x_{2})/\varepsilon=(e_{1}|e_{2})\cdot\partial_{2}\chi(0,\varepsilon x_{2})
we deduce that
d_{2}(0)=D_{2}(0)
. In a similar way we obtain the boundary conditions for
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
at
\ell
.

Constraints on the boundary data.

For
\chi(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2})
and

\mathcal{A}_{0}\neq\emptyset,

3.4
A technical approximation result

We prove here a technical lemma which will be useful in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u\in L^{2}(I)
. Assume that
|u(x)|\geq c
for a.e.
x\in I,
for some constant
c>0
. Then there is a sequence
(u_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{P}(I)
strongly converging to
u
in

such that
|u_{n}|\geq c
on

and
u_{n}=0
on

for

such that
\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Proof.

Assume first that
u\geq c
(resp.
u\leq-c
) a.e. in
I
. If
u\in C(\bar{I})
, the thesis follows by taking
u_{n}=0
on
I\setminus I_{n}
and
u_{n}(x)\coloneqq u(k/n)
on
\bigl(k/n,(k+1)/n\bigl)
for

.

Step two.
In the general case, let

S^{+}\coloneqq\{x\in I:u(x)\geq 0\},\hskip 10.00002ptS^{-}\coloneqq\{x\in I:u(x)<0\}.

Since
S^{+}
is a measurable set, for every
n
there is a (countable) family of disjoint open intervals
\mathcal{F}^{n}=\{A^{n}_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

S^{+}\subseteq\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}A^{n}_{k}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\Bigl|\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}A^{n}_{k}\setminus S^{+}\Bigl|\leq 1/n.

We can find an index
k_{n}
such that

\Bigl|\bigcup_{k\leq k_{n}}A^{n}_{k}\setminus S^{+}\Bigl|\leq 1/n\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and }\hskip 10.00002pt\Bigl|S^{+}\setminus\bigcup_{k\leq k_{n}}A^{n}_{k}\Bigl|\leq 1/(2n).

Set
S_{n}^{+}=\bigcup_{k\leq k_{n}}A^{n}_{k}
and
S_{n}^{-}=I\setminus S_{n}^{+}
; we observe that
S_{n}^{-}
is itself a finite union of disjoint intervals.

We define

u_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}u(x)&\text{ if }x\in S^{+}\cap S^{+}_{n}\cap I_{n}\text{ or }x\in S^{-}\cap S^{-}_{n}\cap I_{n},\\
c&\text{ if }x\in S^{+}_{n}\cap S^{-}\cap I_{n},\\
-c&\text{ if }x\in S^{-}_{n}\cap S^{+}\cap I_{n},\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We have that
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
L^{2}(I);
indeed, setting
C_{n}:=\bigl(S_{n}^{+}\cap S^{-}\cap I_{n}\bigl)\cup\bigl(S_{n}^{-}\cap S^{+}\cap I_{n}\bigl)
we have

|C_{n}|\leq\bigl|S^{+}_{n}\cap S^{-}\bigl|+\bigl|S^{-}_{n}\cap S^{+}\bigl|=\bigl|S^{+}_{n}\setminus S^{+}\bigl|+\bigl|S^{+}\setminus S^{+}_{n}\bigl|\leq\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2n},

hence, using also that
|I\setminus I_{n}|=2/n
, it follows

\int_{I}|u(x)-u_{n}(x)|^{2}\,\text{d}x\leq\int_{I\setminus I_{n}}|u(x)|^{2}\,\text{d}x+\int_{C_{n}}|c-|u(x)||^{2}\,\text{d}x\to 0,\;\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

By definition we have that
u_{n}=0
on
I\setminus I_{n}
,
u_{n}(x)\geq c
for a.e.
x\in S^{+}_{n}\cap I_{n}
, and
u_{n}(x)\leq-c
for a.e.
x\in S^{-}_{n}\cap I_{n}.

Therefore, by a diagonal argument, we can assume that
u=0
on
I\setminus I_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and that there is a finite number of disjoint intervals
(I^{j})_{j\leq N}
such that
\displaystyle\Bigl|I_{n}\setminus\Bigl(\bigcup_{j\leq N}I^{j}\Bigl)\Bigl|=0
, and for every
j\leq N
, either
u(x)\geq c
or
u(x)\leq-c
a.e. on
I^{j}
. We can now conclude by applying Step 1 to
u_{|_{I^{j}}}
for each
j=1,\dots,N
.

It is immediate to see that, by construction, if
u\in L^{\infty}(I)
we can choose
(u_{n})_{n}\subset L^{\infty}(I)
such that
\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
.
∎

3.5
A first refinement result

In the following we will identify each element
M\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}
with the vector
m=(M_{11},M_{22},2M_{12})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and viceversa. The vector associated with
M^{\mathcal{B}}
will be denoted by
m^{\mathcal{B}}
. Moreover, with an abuse of notation, we identify
\mathbb{K}
with the only
3\times 3
symmetric matrix such that
Q(M)=\langle\mathbb{K}\cdot m,m\rangle
for all
M\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}.

Determinant function.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is an orthonormal basis, we have that

m_{1}m_{2}-\frac{1}{4}m_{3}^{2}=\det(M)=\det(M^{\mathcal{B}})=m^{\mathcal{B}}_{1}m^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}-\frac{1}{4}(m^{\mathcal{B}}_{3})^{2}.

In particular, setting

\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2&0\\
1/2&0&0\\
0&0&-1/4\end{pmatrix},

we have
\det(M)=\langle\mathbb{D}\cdot m,m\rangle=\langle\mathbb{D}\cdot m^{\mathcal{B}},m^{\mathcal{B}}\rangle
.
Given the vector
m\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
associated to a matrix
M
, we will sometimes write, with an abuse of notation,
\det(m)
to refer to
\det(M)
.
We will call
\mathcal{D}^{+},\mathcal{D}^{-},\mathcal{D}^{0}
the regions of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
where the determinant function is, respectively, strictly positive, strictly negative, or equal to zero.

Energy in the new basis.

Identifying the basis
\mathcal{B}
with a map
\mathcal{B}\in C^{1}(\bar{I},\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
defined as
\mathcal{B}(t)\coloneqq\bigl(B^{\prime}(t)|N(t)\bigl)
, we observe that, for every
t\in\bar{I}
, the function

\mathcal{L}_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{L}_{t}(C)\coloneqq\mathcal{B}^{-T}(t)C\mathcal{B}^{-1}

satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{t}(M^{\mathcal{B}(t)})=M
,
and is a linear isomorphism. Therefore, for every
t\in\bar{I}
the map

Q_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}\to\mathbb{R},\hskip 10.00002ptQ_{t}(C)\coloneqq Q\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{t}(C)\bigl)

which satisfies
Q_{t}(M^{\mathcal{B}(t)})=Q(M),
is a positive definite quadratic form. We call
\mathbb{K}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
the positive definite symmetric matrix such that
Q_{t}(C)=\mathbb{K}_{t}C\cdot C
. Similarly, we define
Q_{t}^{**}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{sym}
as

Q_{t}^{**}(C)\coloneqq Q_{t}(C)+\alpha^{+}\det\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{t}(C)\bigl)^{+}+\alpha^{-}\det\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{t}(C)\bigl)^{-}=Q_{t}(C)+\alpha^{+}\det\bigl(C\bigl)^{+}+\alpha^{-}\det\bigl(C\bigl)^{-}.

(38)

We observe that

\mathcal{F}(M)=\int_{I}Q_{t}^{**}(M^{\mathcal{B}}(x))\det(D(x))\,\text{d}x.

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

By the definition of
\alpha^{\pm}
in (
19
) and (
20
), 0 is an eigenvalue of the quadratic forms
\mathbb{K}_{t}-\alpha^{-}\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{K}_{t}+\alpha^{+}\mathbb{D}
. We denote the corresponding eigenspaces by
V_{t}^{-},\,V_{t}^{+}
respectively. Note that, if
m\in V^{-}_{t}
(resp.
m\in V^{+}_{t}
), then
\det(m)>0
(resp.
\det(m)<0
). Indeed, considering for instance the case
m\in V^{-}_{t}
, it holds

\langle\bigl(\mathbb{K}_{t}-\alpha^{-}\mathbb{D}\bigl)\cdot m,m\rangle=0\iff\langle\mathbb{K}_{t}\cdot m,m\rangle=\alpha^{-}\langle\mathbb{D}\cdot m,m\rangle\implies\langle\mathbb{D}\cdot m,m\rangle>0

since
\mathbb{K}_{t}
is positive definite.

We observe that
\bigl(\mathbb{K}_{t}\pm\alpha^{\pm}\mathbb{D}\bigl)(C)=0\iff\bigl(\mathbb{K}\pm\alpha^{\pm}\mathbb{D}\bigl)(\mathcal{L}_{t}(C))=0\iff\mathcal{L}_{t}(C)
is an eigenvector of
\mathbb{K}\pm\alpha^{\pm}\mathbb{D}
with null eigenvalue. In particular, the dimension of
V^{-}_{t}
and
V^{+}_{t}
is constant with respect to
t
. Therefore denoting by
V^{\pm}
the eigenspace of the zero eigenvalue of
\mathbb{K}\pm\alpha^{\pm}\mathbb{D}
, if
V^{\pm}=\text{span}\{v^{\pm}_{i}:i=1,\dots,\dim(V^{\pm})\}
, then for every

V^{\pm}_{t}=\text{span}\{\mathcal{L}_{t}^{-1}\bigl(v^{\pm}_{i}\bigl):i=1,\dots,\dim(V^{\pm})\}
. In particular, the vectors
w^{\pm}_{i}(t)\coloneqq\mathcal{L}_{t}^{-1}(v^{\pm}_{i}),\,i=1,\dots,\dim(V^{\pm})
are a basis of
V^{\pm}_{t}
for every
t
, and they belong to
C^{1}(\bar{I};\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Remark 5

be either

Remark 7
.

In this remark, we address the case where
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is non-empty, while both

\bar{y}=\chi(\ell,0)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{R}^{T}=(D_{1}(\ell)|D_{2}(\ell)|e_{3})

(53)

hold.

First of all, we observe that the liminf inequality of Theorem
2.1
holds true with no changes, so we only focus on the limsup inequality.
Let the map
M
be defined as in (
46
). Following Step 2) of the proof of Theorem
2.1
, the matrix
A_{M}
is admissible for the boundary data
(\bar{y},\bar{R})
. If
A_{M}
is non-degenerate, the proof of the limsup inequality proceeds as previously established.

Suppose instead that
A_{M}
is degenerate. From Remark
6
, we deduce that
(y,R^{T})=(B,(D_{1}|D_{2}|e_{3}))
. We distinguish two cases based on whether
\Pi_{0}
vanishes.

First, assume
\Pi_{0}=0
on
I
. It follows from (
22
) that
M=0
and
J(y,R^{T})=0
. Interpreting
M
as the second fundamental form of all the isometries of the recovery sequence, we define
u_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=(x,y,0)
, which yields the recovery sequence
y_{\varepsilon}:=\chi_{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon
. In particular, in this instance the limit functional is consistently given by (
22
).

Conversely, suppose
\Pi_{0}
is not identically zero. In this case, explicitly providing a recovery sequence remains an open problem, the main geometric obstruction is as follows. For this state, the limit energy is minimized by the “second fundamental form”
M=\gamma D^{2}\otimes D^{2}
, where
\gamma\in L^{2}(I)
is not necessarily vanishing. Theorem
3.1
cannot be applied directly due to the degeneracy of
A_{M}
.

A natural strategy would be to construct a sequence of isometries such that
M
serves as the exact second fundamental form along the centerline
B
, mirroring the approach used for the case
\Pi_{0}=0
. However, since any smooth isometric immersion of a flat domain must be a ruled surface, this construction requires extending the centerline
B
along straight rulings whose projections onto the
xy
-plane are oriented along the eigenvector corresponding to the zero eigenvalue of
M
.

In our setting,
M=\gamma D^{2}\otimes D^{2}
, which implies that this eigenvector is
D_{1}
. Since
D_{1}=B^{\prime}
, the projections of these rulings are perfectly tangent to the centerline, a configuration that prevents the construction of a valid sequence of non-singular isometries. In particular,
[
6
, Proposition 13]
is inapplicable for constructing an admissible isometry. Indeed that proposition requires the transversality condition
p\cdot B^{\prime}\neq 0
, which is violated in our context because
p
corresponds to
D^{2}
and, as noted,
D^{2}\cdot B^{\prime}=0
.
