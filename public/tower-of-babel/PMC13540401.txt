Increased Mast Cell Numbers in Bone Marrow of Patients with Myeloproliferative Neoplasms and Pruritus
Authors: Maud Hermans, Peter Westerweel, Thierry van den Bosch, Willem Dik, King Lam, Sanne Van Den Meerendonk, Francien Nederveen, Vincent van der Velden, Peter te Boekhorst
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPruritus may be a debilitating symptom in cases of myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN). Since mast cells (MCs) play a pivotal role, we hypothesized that bone marrow (BM) MCs contribute to the occurrence of pruritus. In this exploratory study, untreated MPN patients were included. BM aspirate/biopsy/blood samples and responses to the MPN-Symptom Assessment Form were obtained. Thirty-two patients were included (essential thrombocythemia, n=19; polycythemia vera, n=7; primary myelofibrosis, n=6). Eleven had clinically significant pruritus (35%). The absolute MC count and proportion per total nucleated BM cells were significantly higher in patients with pruritus compared to those without (mean of 25.5 vs. 58.1 MCs/mm2, p=0.032 and 0.59% vs. 1.66%, p=0.041, respectively). MC immunophenotypes and serum levels of tryptase, interleukin-6, tumor necrosis factor alpha, and soluble CD117 were not correlated. Thus, this study showed that absolute and relative MC numbers are increased in cases of MPN with pruritus. However, whether MCs play a pathophysiological role in causing pruritus or are bystanders remains to be determined.

Introduction

Myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs) include essential thrombocythemia (ET), polycythemia vera (PV), and primary myelofibrosis (PMF) and are characterized by abnormal myeloid proliferation, resulting in erythrocytosis, thrombocytosis, leukocytosis, and/or bone marrow (BM) fibrosis. Acquired activating mutations, mostly in the JAK2, CALR, or MPL genes [1], lead to elevated levels of many proinflammatory and profibrotic cytokines in the micro-milieu of the BM [2, 3], which might contribute to constitutional symptoms and pruritus [4, 5].

The role of mast cells (MCs) in the pathogenesis of pruritus is attributed to histamine production combined with non-histaminergic causes [6]. Earlier studies found that the mean total number of MCs in BM was increased in MPN patients [7, 8]. In MPN, MCs carry the JAK2 V617F mutation [9]. Since JAK2 is important in MC activation pathways, mutated MCs could theoretically display abnormal degranulation and/or cytokine production leading to pruritus [10].

We hypothesized that MCs are involved in MPN-associated pruritus, either through an increase in absolute numbers or through altered activation. We designed an exploratory study to test this hypothesis.

Materials and Methods

Previously untreated MPN patients were prospectively included at the moment of diagnostic BM examination. Clinical characteristics were analyzed based on responses to the MPN-Symptom Assessment Form (MPN-SAF) [4] and samples obtained from BM aspirate, BM biopsy, and peripheral blood. For a more detailed description of the methods, see Appendix 1.

Results

Pruritus

Thirty-two patients were included (Table 1). The MPN-SAF subitem of pruritus was considered to be positive for clinically significant pruritus at a score of ≥4 for this subitem [11].

Eleven patients (35%) reported significant pruritus. Patients with PV had significantly higher pruritus scores than those with ET and PMF (Figure 1A). The mean MPN-SAF pruritus subscore was significantly higher in JAK2-mutated cases (score of 3 vs. 0.3, respectively, p=0.041; Figure 1B). There were no significant differences between patients with or without pruritus regarding age, sex, or degree of myelofibrosis (Table 2).

Mast Cells

The mean absolute MC count was not significantly different between MPN subtypes (Figures 2A and 2B). Values of MCs/mm2 were associated with myelofibrosis grade (Figures 2C and 2D). A positive association was found between MPN-SAF pruritus subscores and MCs/mm2 as well as the percentage of MCs/total nucleated cells in BM (Figure 3). There were no significant differences between patients with or without pruritus regarding serum cytokine levels or blood counts (Table 2).

There were nonsignificant correlations between serum tryptase levels and absolute MC count/mm2 and MC percentage. Furthermore, serum tryptase levels correlated with the interleukin (IL)-6 levels (R=0.424, p=0.011), but not tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) or soluble CD117 (sCD117).

Twenty patients had MC counts high enough (>100) to be included in the analysis of flow cytometry data. A heatmap was created to gain insight into the patterns of marker expression (Figure 4). No relevant correlations were observed. There were also no differences in MC immunophenotypes between MPN subgroups. Statistical analysis was not performed to avoid multiple testing bias given the small patient sample and the high number of markers tested.

Systemic Mastocytosis

Spindle-shaped MCs were observed in eight patients, with only one at a degree of >25%, constituting a minor World Health Organization (WHO) criterion for systemic mastocytosis (sM). Furthermore, two patients had spindle-shaped MCs at rates of 10%-25% and five others at rates of <10%. Five of 8 patients (63%) with any degree of spindle-shaped MCs had MPN-SAF pruritus subscores of ≥4 versus 6/24 (25%) without pruritus (p=0.079). The mean serum tryptase levels were also higher in this subgroup (12.3 µg/L (standard error of the mean: 3.9) vs. 6.7 µg/L [standard error: 0.6], p=0.058), but the levels of IL-6, TNF-α, and sCD117 did not differ. In two patients, aberrant CD25 expression was found by flow cytometry; both of these cases lacked other WHO criteria for sM, although both reported significant pruritus. Moreover, the MCs of both of these patients displayed moderate but aberrant CD30 expression. The presence of the D816V KIT mutation was not routinely investigated in this trial. No patient fulfilled the diagnostic criteria for sM based on the available information.

Discussion

In this small study, we explored the hypothesis that MCs are related to MPN-associated pruritus. We found that one-third of MPN patients had significant pruritus, which was associated with increased absolute and relative MC numbers in the BM but not with alterations in MC immunophenotype or associated serum markers.

Previous studies have provided evidence that MCs are important players in the BM microenvironment. In a study using a BM organoid model of MPN, MCs proved to be a signaling hub reinforcing TNF-α and IL-4 signaling, thereby creating a proinflammatory loop [12]. Other studies showed increased MC numbers in BM and upregulated activation markers on the MCs of MPN patients [7, 8, 9]. The number of MCs correlated positively with the grade of reticulin fibrosis, splenomegaly, and the presence of the JAK2 V617F mutation in one study (n=34) [13].

The MCs of MPN patients produce more transforming growth factor beta and IL-13 in vitro [14], and increased levels of IL-4 and IL-13 in BM are associated with the grade of fibrosis [15]. Skin MCs of MPN patients are more easily activated compared to those of healthy individuals [16]. This could be explained by the fact that MCs from MPN patients also carry the JAK2 V617F mutation [9]. A small uncontrolled cohort study described a positive effect of omalizumab, a monoclonal against immunoglobulin E, in MPN-associated pruritus, again suggesting a role for MCs [17].

Our study was designed as a first exploration of MC numbers and phenotypes in relationship to pruritus, not to corroborate pathophysiological mechanisms. The question thus remains whether the increased MCs that we found in patients with MPNs play a pathophysiological role in causing pruritus or are merely bystanders attracted secondarily to the increased cytokine levels associated with MPNs. MCs are known to produce many other signaling substances besides histamine and tryptase [18], potentially causing a non-histaminergic form of pruritus [19]. Interestingly, the JAK-STAT pathway is also involved in non-histaminergic pruritus through modulation of peripheral nerve activity and epidermal barrier integrity [20, 21]. Alternatively, this might explain our observation that pruritus was present despite low numbers of MCs in the PV cohort. Based on these data, linking the increase in MCs that we and others have consistently found in both the BM and skin of MPN patients to the activating JAK2 mutation and (non-histaminergic) pruritus seems a promising route for further translational research in MPN. In addition, since pruritus was more prominent among patients with PV, the possibility remains that disease subtype may influence the observed association between MC burden and pruritus.

In our cohort, several patients had signs of sM that would not have been noticed in routine diagnostic studies for MPNs. A higher presence of spindle-shaped MCs or the aberrant expression of CD25 and/or CD30 was observed in patients with significant pruritus, although the patient numbers were too small for statistical analysis. In one patient, both >25% spindle-shaped MCs and the presence of two MC aggregates in the BM biopsy were noted, suggesting concurrent sM. Two other patients had aberrant CD25 expression on MCs. Unfortunately, no data on D816V KIT mutations were available, precluding firm conclusions on the cooccurrence of sM in our cohort. Coexisting mastocytosis might be possible, limiting the interpretation of our results. We suggest that additional diagnostics for sM be performed for every patient with (suspected) MPN, especially those with clinically significant pruritus. This might have prognostic and therapeutic implications.

Conclusion

In addition to the impact of incomplete data on mastocytosis, the interpretation of our data might also be limited by the imbalance of MPN subtypes and the limited sample size, causing the study to be underpowered for extensive statistical testing. The absence of significant differences in MC phenotype and serum markers may reflect limited statistical power rather than true biological equivalence. However, the consistent association of MC numbers with pruritus in this exploratory, prospective MPN cohort offers a basis for more extensive research on this topic.
