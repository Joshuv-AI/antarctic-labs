Polyhomogeneous mapping properties of the Radon transform and backprojection operator on the unit ball
Seiji Hansen

Abstract
This article covers polyhomogeneous mapping properties of the Radon transform R R of smooth functions on the open unit ball Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} and the back-projection operator R ∗ R^{*} on Z = ( − 1 , 1 ) × S n − 1 ⊂ ℝ × S n − 1 Z=(-1,1)\times S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}\times S^{n-1} . We construct a double b b -fibration which desingularizes the point-hyperplane relation of Ω ¯ \overline{\Omega} as the total space of a fibration over Z ¯ \overline{Z} . We provide formulas for R R and R ∗ R^{*} in operations generated by the associated b b -fibrations and sharper estimates on the polyhomogeneous mapping properties of R R and R ∗ R^{*} compared to classic estimates using classic Mellin functional techniques. We include a discussion of a one (complex) parameter family of normal operators associated to R R mapping C ∞ ​ ( Ω ¯ ) C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}) to itself.

Introduction

This article studies mapping properties for the Radon transform and its adjoint (the backprojection operator) in classes of conormal distributions on the unit Euclidean ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, specifically polyhomogeneous, conormal functions.

In our setting, fix

as the set

\displaystyle Ru(s,\theta)=\int_{\Pi(s,\theta)}u(x)\ d\mu_{(s,\theta)},\qquad(s,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}\times S^{n-1},

(1)

\displaystyle R^{*}v(x)=\int_{S^{n-1}}v(x\cdot\theta,\theta)\ d\theta,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

Transforms (

A.1
Manifolds with Corners

A manifold with corners
X
(mwc) of dimension
d
, like
G
(see Section
3
), is a Hausdorff, second-countable, topological space
X
where each point
p\in X
has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of
[0,\infty)^{d}
for which the homeomorphisms define a smooth atlas. The
codimension
of
p\in M
is defined as the number of coordinates which vanish at
p
in a coordinate neighborhood
U\subset[0,\infty)^{d}
(identifying
p
with its image under the homeomorphism). The coodinates are sometimes called
adapted coordinates
. A manifold with boundary (mwb) is a manifold with corners for which
\coDim\leq 1
. We will only be interested in the case when
\coDim\leq 2
. The interior is
X^{int}=\coDim^{-1}\{0\}
. Non-interior points form the manifold boundary
\partial X=\coDim^{-1}(\mathbb{N})
which is a
(d-1)
-dimensional manifold with corners. The boundary
\partial X
is thought of as a disjoint union of open, positive codimensional ‘boundary subfaces’ of
X
.

A
boundary hypersurface
(bhs) of
X
is the closure (in the topology of
X
) of a maximal, connected, open
(d-1)
-dimensional submanifold of
\partial X
for which the closure is embedded in
X
as a
(d-1)
-dimensional mwc. Each bhs
W
is associated to a class of smooth
boundary-defining function
(bdf)
\rho\in C^{\infty}\big(X,[0,\infty)\big)
which satisfy

1.

\rho^{-1}(0)=W
,

2.

d\rho\neq 0
on
W
and

3.

d\rho_{\alpha}(x),d\rho_{\beta}(x)\in T^{*}_{x}X
are linearly independent for each
x\in W_{\alpha}\cap W_{\beta}
.

Condition
2.
ensures
W
is embedded, while condition
3.
ensures the bhs intersect transversely (condition
3.
is not the correct condition in higher codimension). A function
\sigma\in C^{\infty}\big(X,[0,\infty)\big)
is a
local bdf
of
W
if there is an open neighborhood
U\supset W
such that
\sigma|_{U}\in C^{\infty}\big(U,[0,\infty)\big)
is a bdf for
W
. On an open
U\subset[0,\infty)^{d}
, each coordinate vanishing on
U
defines is a bdf of the corresponding bhs.

The
2
-codimensional boundary
corners
are the
(d-2)
-dimensional connected components of the intersection of any
2
distinct bhs, and similarly for higher codimension (these intersections are always transverse by the generalization of condition 3. above). The collection of
k
-codimensional bhs is denoted
M_{k}(X)
and
M(X):=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}M_{k}(X)\cup\{X\}
of all faces forms a poset under inclusion. When
X
is at most
k
-codimemsional,
M_{k}(X)
consists of closed submanifolds of
X
and
M_{k+j}(X)=\emptyset
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. The inclusion poset for
G
is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coDim=0,}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G|_{\partial Z_{-}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\overline{Z}\cap G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G|_{\partial Z_{+}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coDim=1,}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\overline{Z}\cap G|_{\partial Z_{-}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\overline{Z}\cap G|_{\partial Z_{+}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coDim=2,}

where

and
S\overline{Z}
denotes the tangent sphere bundle over
\overline{Z}
with the induced Riemannian structure from Euclidean

. For examples of every term mentioned, see Section
3
