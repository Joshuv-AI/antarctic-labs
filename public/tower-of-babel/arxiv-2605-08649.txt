Semisimplicity Criteria for algebras with a Jones Basic Construction
Frederick M. Goodman, Hans Wenzl

Abstract
. We prove a semisimplicity criterion for a large class of algebras by a new method. This can be applied to Brauer, BMW, and q q -Brauer algebras.



1.
Introduction

We develop a new method for determining semisimplicity of algebras
A_{n}
that
generically are obtained by repeated Jones basic constructions from a simpler
family of algebras
Q_{n}
. Such algebras include the Brauer algebras,
Birman-Wenzl-Murakami (BMW) algebras, and the
q
-Brauer algebras.
Our method is valid over a field of arbitrary characteristic.

Let us first describe our results in the context of the examples mentioned
above. These algebras appear as centralizer algebras of certain tensor
categories (Brauer algebras, BMW algebras) or module categories (
q
-Brauer
algebras) that carry natural dimension functions. The dimension functions give
rise to a special trace functional, the Markov trace; let
n_{0}
be the smallest
value of
n
such that the Markov trace is degenerate on
A_{n}
– that is,
there exists
0\neq x\in A_{n}
such that
\tr(xy)=0
for all
y\in A_{n}
.
Moreover, let
n_{1}+1
be the smallest value of
n
such that the quotient
algebra
Q_{n}
fails to be semisimple. (Put
n_{0}=\infty
in case the Markov
trace is always non-degenerate, and
n_{1}=\infty
in case
Q_{n}
is always
semisimple.) By arguments presented by the second author for the Brauer
algebras
[
25
]
, and later for the BMW algebras
[
26
]

and
q
-Brauer algebras
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,
A_{n}
is
semisimple for all
n\leq\min\{n_{0},n_{1}\}
. Explicit bounds are obtained using
formulas for the dimension functions; these are El Samra-King polynomials for
the Brauer algebras and two different
q
-deformations of them for the BMW
algebras and the
q
-Brauer algebras, see Section
5
for
details.

To prove that these bounds are sharp, we exploit the fact that each of these
algebras is cellular
[
8
]
. We show that the cellular structure
of
A_{k+2s}
is related to the cellular structure of
A_{k}
in a particular way
related to the Jones basic construction. Using this, we show that
A_{n}
is
non-semisimple for
n>\min\{n_{0},n_{1}\}
. This allows us to obtain explicit
semisimplicity criteria for these families of algebras, recovering results of
Rui et al. (
[
21
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
17
]
). We note that
for the Brauer and BMW algebras, Rui et al. have also determined for each cell
module whether the cell module is simple, which is a stronger result.

We prove our results for a general class of algebras determined by axioms
listed in Section
3.1
. We then prove that the algebras
A_{n}
in this
class are semisimple if and only if
n\leq\min\{n_{0},n_{1}\}
, in Theorems

3.7
and
3.11
. We review properties of Brauer
algebras, BMW algebras and
q
-Brauer algebras in Section
4

and show that they satisfy our axioms. We then explicitly calculate
\min\{n_{0},n_{1}\}
for these examples in Section
5
, for all possible
choices of parameters.

The theoretical novelty of our method is that it plays the Jones basic
construction against the cellular structure of the algebras
A_{n}
to obtain
criteria for semisimplicity involving the weights of the Markov trace. The
method does not require computation of seminormal representations or Gram
determinants. The practical advantage is that applying our conditions requires
only computing vanishing conditions for the weights of the Markov trace, rather
than for the Gram determinants, and that turns out to be relatively easy. A
limitation of this approach is that we cannot obtain all the information
encoded in the Gram determinants. We expect that our approach would also be
useful for other algebras related to the Jones basic construction, such as
e.g. walled Brauer algebras and cyclotomic BMW algebras.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review background information on embeddings of semisimple
algebras and on cellular algebras.

2.1.
Branching diagrams

Suppose
\varphi:A\to B
is a unital homomorphism of finite dimensional split
semisimple algebras over a field
F
. Let
A(i)
,
i\in I
, be the minimal
two-sided ideals of
A
and
B(j)
,
j\in J
, the minimal two-sided ideals of

B
. We associate a
J\times I

inclusion matrix

\omega
to the
homomorphism
\varphi:A\to B
, as follows. Let
W_{j}
be a simple

B(j)
–module. Then
W_{j}
becomes an
A
–module via the homomorphism, and

\omega(j,i)
is the multiplicity of a simple
A(i)
–module in the
decomposition of
W_{j}
as an
A
–module. An equivalent characterization of the
inclusion matrix is the following. Let
q_{i}
be a minimal idempotent in
A(i)

and let
z_{j}
be the minimal central idempotent in
B
such that
z_{j}B=B(j)
. Then
\varphi(q_{i})z_{j}
is the sum of
\omega(j,i)
minimal idempotents
in
B(j)
. We are mostly interested in injective homomorphisms or inclusions
A\subseteq B
, but at one point we need the more general setup.

It is convenient to encode an inclusion matrix by a bipartite graph, called the

branching diagram
; the branching diagram has vertices labeled by
I

arranged on one horizontal line, vertices labeled by
J
arranged along a
second (higher) horizontal line, and
\omega(j,i)
edges connecting
j\in J

to
i\in I
.

If
A_{1}\to A_{2}\to A_{3}\cdots
is a (finite or infinite) sequence of
homomorphisms of finite dimensional split semisimple algebras over
F
, then
the branching diagram for the sequence is obtained by stacking the branching
diagrams for each inclusion, with the upper vertices of the diagram for
A_{i}\to A_{i+1}
being identified with the lower vertices of the diagram for

A_{i+1}\to A_{i+2}
.

For our purposes, it suffices to restrict our attention to the case that the
entries in each inclusion matrix are all
0
or
1
; thus in the branching
diagram there are no multiple edges between vertices. We say that the
homomorphisms (and the branching diagram) are
multiplicity free
. We can
also assume that
A_{0}=F
, so that there is a unique vertex
\emptyset
at
level zero of the branching diagram, which we call the
root
of the
branching diagram.

The branching diagram of a sequence of multiplicity free homomorphisms is an
abstract branching diagram in the following sense:

Definition 2.1
.

A (finite or infinite, multiplicity free) abstract branching diagram
\Gamma
is a graph with vertex set

\coprod_{i\geq 0}\Gamma_{i}
, with the following properties

(1)

\Gamma_{0}
is a singleton and
\Gamma_{i}
is finite for all
i
.

(2)

Two vertices
v\in\Gamma_{i}
and
w\in\Gamma_{j}
are adjacent only if
|i-j|=1
. Adjacent vertices are connected by a unique edge.

(3)

If
i\geq 1
and
v\in\Gamma_{i}
, then
v
is adjacent to at least one vertex
in
\Gamma_{i-1}
.

We write
v\nearrow w
if
v\in\Gamma_{i}
is adjacent to
w\in\Gamma_{i+1}
. If
v\in\Gamma_{i}
, we write
|v|=i
.

Given a multiplicity-free branching diagram
\Gamma
, we define a new branching
diagram
\tilde{\Gamma}
as follows. The vertex set
\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}
at level

n
is the set of pairs
(v,n)
where
v\in\Gamma_{k}
for some
k\leq n
with

n-k
even. The branching rule is
(v,n)\nearrow(w,n+1)
if and only if

|w|=|v|+1
and
v\nearrow w
in
\Gamma
, or
|w|=|v|-1
and
w\nearrow v
in
\Gamma
. We say that
\tilde{\Gamma}
is the branching diagram
obtained
by reflections
from
\Gamma
.

Example 2.2
.

(Young’s lattice)
For
n\geq 0
, let
\Lambda_{n}
denote the set of Young diagrams (or integer partitions) of size
n
.
Young’s lattice
is the disjoint
union of
\Lambda_{n}
for
n\geq 0
, with the following branching rule:
for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
and
\mu\in\Lambda_{n+1}
,
\lambda\to\mu
if
\mu
is obtained from
\lambda
by adding one box.

Over a field of characteristic zero, when the parameter
q
is not a root of
unity, the Hecke algebras
H_{n}(q)
(see Section
4.1
) are split semisimple for all
n
, the simple
modules of
H_{n}(q)
are labelled by
\Lambda_{n}
, and the branching rule for the
inclusion
H_{n}\subseteq H_{n+1}
is as described above.

Each
\Lambda_{n}
has a partial order (dominance order) that plays a role in the
representation theory of the Hecke algebras, in particular in the definition of
a cellular structure on
H_{n}(q)
. The definition is as follows:
\lambda\unrhd\mu
in
\Lambda_{n}
if for all
k
,

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}\geq\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\cdots+\mu_{k}.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\tilde{\Lambda}
denote the branching diagram obtained by reflections from Young’s lattice.

\tilde{\Lambda}
is the branching diagram for the sequence of Brauer algebras in the generic semisimple case: over a field of characteristic
zero and when the parameter
\delta
is not an integer, the Brauer algebras
B_{n}(\delta)
are split semisimple, the simple modules of

B_{n}(\delta)
are labelled by
\tilde{\Lambda}_{n}
, and the branching rule for
B_{n}\subseteq B_{n+1}
is that obtained by reflections from
Young’s lattice.

Each
\tilde{\Lambda}_{n}
has a partial order that plays a role in the
representation theory of the Brauer algebras. The definition is
(\lambda,n)\unrhd(\mu,n)
if
|\lambda|<|\mu|
, or if
|\lambda|=|\mu|
and
\lambda\unrhd\mu
in dominance order for Young diagrams.

2.2.
Cellular Algebras

We recall the definition of
cellularity
from
[
8
]
; see
also
[
11
]
. We use a slightly weaker version of the definition,
which was introduced in
[
7
]
. In the following, an
algebra
involution

*
on an algebra
A
is an algebra anti-automorphism such that

(a^{*})^{*}=a
for all
a\in A
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
R
be an integral domain and
A
a unital
R
–algebra. A
cell datum
for
A
consists of an algebra involution

*
of
A
; a partially ordered set
(\Lambda,\geq)
and
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
a set
\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
; and a subset
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{s,t}^{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\text{ and }s,t\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)\}\subseteq A
;
with the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{C}
is an
R
–basis of
A
.

(2)

For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let
A^{>\lambda}
be the span of the
c_{s,t}^{\mu}
with

\mu>\lambda
. Given
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
s\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
, and
a\in A
, there exist coefficients

r_{v}^{s}(a)\in R
such that for all
t\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
:

ac_{s,t}^{\lambda}\equiv\sum_{v}r_{v}^{s}(a)c_{v,t}^{\lambda}\mod A^{>\lambda}.

(3)

(c_{s,t}^{\lambda})^{*}\equiv c_{t,s}^{\lambda}\mod A^{>\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
s,t\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
.

A
is said to be a
cellular algebra
if it has a cell datum.

For brevity, we will write that
(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)
is a cellular basis of
A
.

We recall some basic structures related to cellularity, see

[
8
]
. First we note that for any
R
–algebra
A
with involution

*
, the involution induces a functor interchanging left and right modules, by
composition with
*
. (If
\Delta
is a left
A
–module, then
\Delta^{*}
is
the same as
\Delta
as an
R
–module, and has the right
A
–module structure
defined by
xa=a^{*}x
for
x\in\Delta
and
a\in A
.)
Given
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let
A^{\geq\lambda}
denote the span of
the
c_{s,t}^{\mu}
with
\mu\geq\lambda
. It follows that both
A^{\geq\lambda}
and

A^{>\lambda}
(defined above) are
*
–invariant two sided ideals of
A
. The

left cell module

\Delta_{\lambda}
is defined as follows: as an

R
–module,
\Delta_{\lambda}
is free with basis
\{c_{s}^{\lambda}
:
s\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)\}
; for each
a\in A
, the action of
a
on
\Delta_{\lambda}
is
defined by
ac_{s}^{\lambda}=\sum_{v}r_{v}^{s}(a)c_{v}^{\lambda}
where
r_{v}^{s}(a)
is as in
Definition
2.4
(
2
). For all
s,t\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
,
we have a canonical
A-A
–bimodule isomorphism
\alpha:A^{\geq\lambda}/A^{>\lambda}\rightarrow\Delta_{\lambda}\otimes_{R}(\Delta_{\lambda})^{*}
defined by

\alpha(c_{s,t}^{\lambda}+A^{>\lambda})=c_{s}^{\lambda}\otimes_{R}c_{t}^{\lambda}
.

2.2.1.
Semisimple cellular algebras

A cellular algebra
A
over a field is split semisimple if it is semisimple;
and it is semisimple if and only if each cell module is simple
(
[
8
]
, Theorem 3.8). In this case, the cell modules coincide
with the simple modules, and are mutually non-isomorphic. For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, there is a minimal central idempotent
z_{\lambda}
such that
z_{\lambda}A
is a
minimal two sided ideal isomorphic to
\End(\Delta_{\lambda})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
be a semisimple cellular algebra over a field, with cellular basis
\{c_{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}^{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda,\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)\}
.

(1)

For
x\in A^{>\lambda}
,
xz_{\lambda}=0
.

(2)

The map
x+A^{>\lambda}\to xz_{\lambda}

is an
A
–
A
bimodule isomorphism from
A^{\geq\lambda}/A^{>\lambda}
to
z_{\lambda}A
.

(3)

z_{\lambda}\in A^{\geq\lambda}\setminus A^{>\lambda}
.

(4)

If
p
is a minimal idempotent in
z_{\lambda}A
, then
p\in A^{\geq\lambda}\setminus A^{>\lambda}
.

Proof.

We have

z_{\lambda}\in z_{\lambda}A\cong\End(\Delta_{\lambda})\cong\dim(\Delta_{\lambda})\Delta_{\lambda},

as
A
-modules.
Since
A^{>\lambda}
acts as zero on
\Delta_{\lambda}
, it follows that
xz_{\lambda}=0
for
x\in A^{>\lambda}
.
Therefore, the map
x+A^{>\lambda}\to xz_{\lambda}
from
A^{\geq\lambda}/A^{>\lambda}
to
z_{\lambda}A
is well defined.
A model for the (simple) cell module
\Delta_{\lambda}
is
\spn\{c_{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}^{\lambda}+A^{>\lambda}:s\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)\}
for any
fixed
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
. Since
z_{\lambda}
acts as the identity on
\Delta_{\lambda}
, we have
z_{\lambda}c_{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}^{\lambda}\equiv c_{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}^{\lambda}\mod A^{>\lambda}
. Therefore,
x+A^{>\lambda}\to xz_{\lambda}
is injective from
A^{\geq\lambda}/A^{>\lambda}
to
z_{\lambda}A
. Since both have dimension

\dim(\Delta_{\lambda})^{2}
, the map is an isomorphism. This proves parts (1) and (2). By surjectivity in part (2),

z_{\lambda}\in z_{\lambda}A=\spn\{c_{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}^{\lambda}z_{\lambda}:\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)\}\subseteq A^{\geq\lambda}.

Since
z_{\lambda}=z_{\lambda}^{2}
, it follows that
z_{\lambda}\not\in A^{>\lambda}
, by part (1). This proves part (3). Part (4) also follows, since
p=pz_{\lambda}
.
∎

2.2.2.
Generic ground rings

The examples of cellular algebras of interest to us are actually families
A^{S}

of algebras defined over various integral ground rings
S
, possibly containing
distinguished elements (parameters) that enter into the definition of the
algebras. In these examples, there is a “generic ground ring”
R
for
A

with the following property: Any suitable integral ground ring
S
for
A
is
an
R
-module, and
A^{S}
is the specialization of
A^{R}
, that is
A^{S}\cong A^{R}\otimes_{R}S
. Likewise, the cell modules of
A^{S}
are specializations
of those of
A^{R}
, that is
\Delta_{\lambda}^{S}\cong\Delta_{\lambda}^{{R}}\otimes_{R}S
. In examples of interest,
R
has characteristic zero, and if
F
denotes
the field of fractions of
R
, then
A^{F}
is split semisimple.

A prototypical example is the Brauer algebra
B_{n}=B_{n}(S;\delta)
, which is
an algebra with a basis of diagrams that can be defined over any integral
ground ring
S
with a distinguished element
\delta
. The product of any two
basis diagrams is a power of
\delta
times a third diagram. For the Brauer
algebras and similar examples, we will insist that the “loop parameter”

\delta
is invertible in
S
. Thus the generic ground ring for the Brauer
algebras is
R=\mathbb{Z}[\bm{\delta}^{\pm 1}]
, where
\bm{\delta}
is an
indeterminate.

2.2.3.
Coherent towers and branching diagrams

Let
A
be a cellular algebra. A
cell filtration
of an

A
-module is a
filtration by
A
-submodules such that the subquotients are isomorphic to cell
modules. Now consider an increasing sequence
(A_{i})
of cellular algebras (with
the same identity).
One says that the sequence is
restriction coherent
if for each
i
, and
for each cell module
\Delta
of
A_{i+1}
, the restricted module
\res^{A_{i+1}}_{A_{i}}(\Delta)
has a cell filtration.

Lemma 2.6
.

(Permanence of multiplicities)
Let
(Q_{i})
be a restriction coherent sequence of
cellular algebras over a ring
R
with field of fractions
F
, and suppose that
Q_{i}^{F}
is split semisimple for all
i
. Let
\Gamma_{i}
denote
the partially ordered set in the cell datum for
Q_{i}
, and let
\omega_{i}(\mu,\lambda)
denote the inclusion matrix for
Q_{i}^{F}\subseteq Q_{i+1}^{F}
.

(1)

For
\mu\in\Gamma_{i+1}
and
\lambda\in\Gamma_{i}
, the multiplicity of
\Delta_{\lambda}^{R}
as a subquotient in any cell filtration of
\res^{Q_{i+1}}_{Q_{i}}(\Delta_{\mu}^{R})
is
\omega_{i}(\mu,\lambda)
.

(2)

Let
K
be a field that is an
R
-module. Suppose
\Delta_{\mu}^{K}
is a simple
cell module of
Q_{i+1}^{K}
with semisimple restriction to
Q_{i}^{K}
. Then
\res^{Q_{i+1}^{K}}_{Q_{i}^{K}}(\Delta_{\mu}^{K})\cong\sum^{\oplus}\omega_{i}(\mu,\lambda)\Delta_{\lambda}^{K}
, and the cell modules appearing in this direct sum are simple.

Proof.

Consider any cell filtration of
\res^{Q_{i+1}}_{Q_{i}}(\Delta_{\mu}^{R})
,

(2.1)

(0)=M_{0}\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_{r}=\res^{Q_{i+1}}_{Q_{i}}(\Delta_{\mu}^{R}),

where
M_{k+1}/M_{k}\cong\Delta_{\lambda_{k}}^{R}
. Because each
M_{k}
is free as an
R
-module, applying
\otimes_{R}F
to the cell filtration
(
2.1
) yields a filtration of

\res^{Q_{i+1}^{F}}_{Q_{i}^{F}}(\Delta_{\mu}^{F})
with subquotients
\Delta_{\lambda_{k}}^{F}
.
Hence, the number of occurrences of
\Delta_{\lambda}^{R}
in the cell filtration (
2.1
) is
\omega_{i}(\mu,\lambda)
.
Similarly, applying
\otimes_{R}K
to the cell filtration gives a cell filtration of
\res^{Q_{i+1}^{K}}_{Q_{i}^{K}}(\Delta_{\mu}^{K})
.
The cell modules that occur as subquotients are semisimple, by assumption, but a semisimple cell module is simple.
∎

2.2.4.
Order ideals and ideals

An order ideal in a partially ordered set
(\Gamma,\geq)
is a subset
U

such
that if
\mu\in U
and
\gamma\geq\mu
in
\Gamma
, then
\gamma\in U
.
Suppose
A
is a cellular algebra with involution
*
, partially ordered set

(\Gamma,\geq)
and cellular basis
\{a_{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}^{\gamma}\}
. If
U
is an
order ideal in
\Gamma
, then the span
A(U)
of the basis elements
a_{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\in U
is a
*
-invariant ideal in

.

2.3.
Traces

Let
A
be an algebra over a ring
R
. A
trace
on
A
is an
R
-linear
functional
\tau:A\to R
that satisfies
\tau(xy)=\tau(yx)
for all
x,y\in A
. A trace
\tau
is
unital
or
normalized
if
\tau(\bm{1})=1
. The
radical
of a trace
\tau
is the set of
x\in A
such that

\tau(xy)=0
for all
y\in A
. Evidently, the radical is an ideal. A trace

\tau
is
faithful
or
non-degenerate
if its radical is zero, and

degenerate
otherwise. If
A\cong\oplus_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}
is a split semisimple
algebra over a field, a trace
\tau
is completely determined by its weight
vector
(\omega_{\lambda})=(\tau(p_{\lambda}))
, where
p_{\lambda}
is a minimal idempotent in

A_{\lambda}
, and
\tau
is non-degenerate precisely if all the weights are
non-zero.

3.
Algebras with a Jones basic construction

3.1.
Axioms

We give a list of axioms for a tower of algebras
(A_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and a second
tower of algebras
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 0}
that allow application of the method
described in the introduction. In later sections, we show that the families of
algebras mentioned in the introduction satisfy these axioms. In fact, the list
of axioms is simply an abstraction of well–known properties of the Brauer and
BMW algebras. The
q
-Brauer algebras are somewhat different in that the axioms
do not apply to the natural embeddings of the algebras but rather to twisted
embeddings, as will be explained in section
4.4
.

We suppose an infinite multiplicity-free branching diagram
\Gamma
with each
vertex set
\Gamma_{k}
endowed with a partial order
\geq
. We write
|\gamma|=k
if
\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}
. We write
\tilde{\Gamma}
for the branching diagram
obtained by reflections from
\Gamma
. We endow
\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}
with the
partial order
(\gamma,n)\geq(\gamma^{\prime},n)
if
|\gamma|<|\gamma^{\prime}|
or if

|\gamma|=|\gamma^{\prime}|=k
and
\gamma\geq\gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{k}
. Let
\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}^{0}
be the set of
(\gamma,n)\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}
with
|\gamma|<n
. Note that
\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}^{0}
is an order ideal in
\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}
.

We consider two increasing sequences of unital algebras
(A_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and

(Q_{n})_{n\geq 0}
over an integral domain
R
satisfying the following
conditions:

(1)

The algebras
A_{n}
and
Q_{n}
have algebra involutions denoted by
*
.
The involutions on the algebras
Q_{n}
are consistent; i.e.
there is an algebra involution
*
on
\cup_{n}Q_{n}
such that
(Q_{n})^{*}=Q_{n}
.

(2)

A_{0}=Q_{0}=R
and
A_{1}=Q_{1}
.

(3)

The sequence of algebras
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a restriction-coherent tower of cellular
algebras (see section
2.2.3
). The partially ordered set in the cell datum of
Q_{n}
is
(\Gamma_{n},\geq)
.

(4)

The ground ring
R
has characteristic zero. Let
F
denote its field of fractions. The
algebras
Q_{n}^{F}=Q_{n}\otimes_{R}F
are split semisimple and the branching diagram for
(Q_{n}^{F})_{n\geq 0}
is
\Gamma
.
Moreover, for any field
K
that is an
R
-module, either
Q_{n}^{K}=Q_{n}\otimes_{R}K
is split semisimple for all
n
or there exists an
n_{1}\geq 0
such that
Q_{n}^{K}

is split semisimple for
n\leq n_{1}
and non-semisimple for
n>n_{1}
.

(5)

Adopt the hypotheses of Theorem

4.3.
BMW algebras

The BMW algebras were first introduced in
[
2
]
and

[
13
]
as abstract algebras defined by generators and relations.
The version of the presentation given here follows
[
1
]
and

[
12
]
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
S
be a commutative unital ring with invertible elements
r
,
q
, and
\delta
satisfying
r^{-1}-r=(q^{-1}-q)(\delta-1)
. The
Birman–Wenzl–Murakami algebra

\mathfrak{D}_{n}(S;r,q,\delta)
is the unital
S
–algebra
with generators
g_{i}^{\pm 1}
and

e_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n-1
) and relations:

(1)

(Inverses)
g_{i}g_{i}^{-1}=g_{i}^{-1}g_{i}=1
.

(2)

(Essential idempotent relation)
e_{i}^{2}=\delta e_{i}
.

(3)

(Braid relations)
g_{i}g_{i+1}g_{i}=g_{i+1}g_{i}g_{i+1}

and
g_{i}g_{j}=g_{j}g_{i}
if
|i-j|\geq 2
.

(4)

(Commutation relations)
g_{i}e_{j}=e_{j}g_{i}
and

e_{i}e_{j}=e_{j}e_{i}
if
|i-j|\geq 2
.

(5)

(Tangle relations)
e_{i}e_{i\pm 1}e_{i}=e_{i}
,
g_{i}g_{i\pm 1}e_{i}=e_{i\pm 1}e_{i}
, and
e_{i}g_{i\pm 1}g_{i}=e_{i}e_{i\pm 1}
.

(6)

(Kauffman skein relation)
g_{i}-g_{i}^{-1}=(q-q^{-1})(1-e_{i})
.

(7)

(Untwisting relations)
g_{i}e_{i}=e_{i}g_{i}=r^{-1}e_{i}
,
and
e_{i}g_{i\pm 1}e_{i}=re_{i}
.

The generic ground ring for the BMW algebras is

R=\mathbb{Z}[\bm{r}^{\pm 1},\bm{q}^{\pm 1},\bm{\delta}^{\pm 1}]/\langle\bm{r}^{-1}-\bm{r}=(\bm{q}^{-1}-\bm{q})(\bm{\delta}-1)\rangle,

where
\bm{r}
,
\bm{q}
, and
\bm{\delta}
are
indeterminates over
\mathbb{Z}
.
R
is an integral domain whose field of fractions is

F\cong\mathbb{Q}(\bm{r},\bm{q})
(with
\bm{\delta}=(\bm{r}^{-1}-\bm{r})/(\bm{q}^{-1}-\bm{q})+1
in
F
.)

The BMW algebra
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
can also be realized as the algebra of
n
–tangle
diagrams modulo regular isotopy and the following

relations:

(1)

Crossing relation:
\quad\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{./minimal-graphics/pos_crossing}\end{array}-\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{./minimal-graphics/neg_crossing}\end{array}\quad=\quad(q^{-1}-q)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{./minimal-graphics/e_smoothing}\end{array}-\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{./minimal-graphics/id_smoothing}\end{array}\right).

(2)

Untwisting relation:
\quad\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{./minimal-graphics/right_twist}\end{array}\quad=\quad r\quad\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{./minimal-graphics/vertical_line}\end{array}\quad\ \text{and}\quad\ \begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{./minimal-graphics/left_twist}\end{array}\quad=\quad r^{-1}\quad\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{./minimal-graphics/vertical_line}\end{array}.

(3)

Free loop relation:
T\,\cup\,\bigcirc=\delta\,T,
where

means the union of a tangle diagram

In the tangle picture,

and

Lemma A.14

\sigma=u\pi
