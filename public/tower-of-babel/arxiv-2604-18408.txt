Function spaces and potential theory in the Orlicz setting
Pablo Ochoa, Ariel Salort

Abstract
. In this article, we study certain transcendental function spaces arising in potential theory within the framework of Orlicz spaces. Specifically, we generalize Bessel and Lizorkin–Triebel spaces to the nonstandard setting of Orlicz spaces. We recover classical results from potential theory, such as the fact that Bessel–Orlicz spaces of integer order coincide with Orlicz–Sobolev spaces (Calderón type theorem), and we establish inclusion results for fractional orders. Moreover, we prove a Strauss-type lemma for potential spaces. In the last sections, we show that certain Orlicz–Lizorkin–Triebel spaces coincide with Bessel–Orlicz spaces, and we provide a useful atomic decomposition for theses spaces.

1.
Introduction

In the early 1900s, a new framework for partial differential equations emerged to transcend the limitations of classical
C^{k}
regularity. Through the pioneering works of Riesz, Nikol’skii, and Besov, fractional integration became deeply integrated with harmonic analysis and potential theory. To mention only a few fundamental references, we cite
[
8
,
14
,
18
,
26
,
28
,
29
]
.

The Riesz potential
I_{s}
(see
[
26
]
) serves as a fundamental smoothing operator that formally inverts fractional powers of the Laplacian,
(-\Delta)^{-s/2}
. While it is an essential tool for capturing local regularity, it possesses a significant structural limitation when defined on the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
due to its lack of global integrability:

I_{s}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{-s})\implies I_{s}(x)\approx|x|^{s-n}\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.

Consequently,
I_{s}\notin L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which prevents the operator from being bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
1\leq p<\infty
. This deficiency motivates the introduction of the Bessel kernel,
G_{s}
by replacing the purely homogeneous symbol
|\xi|^{-s}
with
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-s/2}
,

G_{s}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((1+|\xi|^{2})^{-s/2}\right)\implies G_{s}(x)\approx|x|^{s-n}\text{ as }|x|\to 0\text{ and }G_{s}(x)\approx e^{-|x|}\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.

This ensures that
G_{s}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and leads to the robust representation
u=G_{s}*f
, where
f=(I-\Delta)^{s/2}u
acts as the density or fractional derivative of
u
. The set of functions expressed as
G_{s}*f
for some
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the
Bessel potential space

H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

These spaces were introduced and studied in the early 1960s by N. Aronszajn and K. Smith
[
7
]
, N. Aronszajn, F. Mulla, and P. Szeptycki
[
8
]
, and A.P. Calderón
[
12
]
, who, in 1961, proved a fundamental theorem showing the equivalence between the classical Sobolev space
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
and the Bessel potential space
H^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In 1970, E. Stein popularized and refined these spaces in his book
[
28
]
. Later, the theory was integrated into the broader Lizorkin-Triebel and Besov framework, see for instance
[
29
]
.

An approach to potential spaces within local Orlicz-type settings is developed in
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
15
,
19
,
27
]
, where their fundamental formulations and primary properties are established. For related results regarding norm equivalences in the nonlocal case, we refer the reader to
[
11
]
.

The primary objective of this article is to introduce a class of potential spaces defined by growth functions more general than standard power laws, namely, those characterized by a Young function
A
. We aim to study the fundamental properties of these spaces and establish their relationship with nonlocal Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. We recall that the
fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
introduced in
[
5
,
16
]
are the natural generalizations of the classical fractional order Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
p>1
. Given a Young function
A
and a fractional parameter
s\in(0,1)
it is defined as

W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ s.t.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(|u|)\,dx+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}<\infty\right\}.

For properties and further research on these spaces we refer to
[
5
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
16
]
.

Motivated by these considerations, in Section
2.3
, for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the
Bessel-Orlicz potential space

H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the set

H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=G_{s}*f,\text{ for some }f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\},

which we endow with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
denotes the Luxemburg norm associated with the Orlicz space
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Our first results state the relation between these spaces. In Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
, we address the case
s=1
and establish the equivalence

H^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

provided that both the Young function
A
and its conjugate
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
condition, that is, when assuming the growth control for some constants
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\infty
such that

p^{-}A(t)\leq tA^{\prime}(t)\leq p^{+}A(t).

Moreover, we prove that the previous relation can be generalized for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,

H^{m,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=W^{m,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The primary challenge in this setting is that the gradient of the Bessel kernel,
\nabla G_{1}
, does not belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which precludes a simple convolution argument. Due to this, the core of our proof lies in proving that
\nabla G_{1}
defines a Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator. Given the assumed growth conditions on
A
and
\hat{A}
, we prove that this operator is bounded from
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to itself, thereby bridging the gap between the potential-based and derivative-based definitions of these spaces.

In Theorem
3.7
we prove that for any
0<s<s^{\prime}<1
, the continuous inclusion

H^{s^{\prime},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

holds for any Young function
A
regardless of assuming the
\Delta_{2}
condition for either
A
or
\hat{A}
. We obtain both norm and modular relations between spaces. Conversely, for any
0<s<s^{\prime}<1
, in Theorem
3.9
we prove that the continuous embedding

W^{s^{\prime},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

holds provided that
A
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition.

Our proof differs from existing literature where, to the best of our knowledge, standard methods rely heavily on the homogeneity and power-like behavior of the modulars. The cornerstone of our nonlocal approach is the following continuity result for the Bessel kernel defined as
K(z,x,t):=(G_{\alpha}(z-x)-G_{\alpha}(z-x+t))|t|^{-\gamma}
. In Proposition
3.8
, we establish that for
\alpha>\gamma>0
, and with the measure
d\mu=|t|^{-n}dxdt
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|K(z,x,t)v(x,t)w(z)|\,d\mu\,dz\leq C\|v\|_{L^{A}(d\mu)}\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

holds for any
v\in L^{A}(d\mu)
and
w\in L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We point out that in the case of powers, a similar result was provided in
[
8
, Theorem 6.3]
, however the proof depends decisively on the homogeneity of power functions. Hence, Proposition
3.8
is established with a completely different method.

Examples of Young function which can be considered in our results include

•

Zygmund type functions:
A(t)=t^{p}\log^{q}(1+t^{r})
for
p>1
,
q\geq 0
,
r>0
;

•

Power combinations:
A(t)=t^{p}+t^{q}
,
1<p\leq q<\infty
;

•

Iterated logarithmic functions:
A(t)=t^{p}\log^{\alpha}(1+t)\log^{\beta}(1+\log(1+t))
,
p>1
y
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
;

•

Generalized Power-log functions:
A(t)=\int_{0}^{|t|}s^{p-1}\log^{\alpha}(1+s)\,ds
,
1<p<\infty,\ \alpha\in\mathbb{R}

In the particular case of power function, i.e.,
A(t)=t^{p}
, with
p>1
, our theorems recover the classical result for Bessel potential spaces developed in the 1960s by Aronszajn, Smith, Calderón and Stein. For a detailed treatment of these classical cases, we refer the reader to Theorem 11.1 in the monograph
[
8
]
for the nonlocal case and
[
29
, Theorem p. 88]
for the local case.

It is well established that radial functions exhibit enhanced regularity and decay properties compared to the general case. In Theorem
4.3
we prove a Strauss-type inequality asserting that for
sp^{-}>1
, any radial function
u\in H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the decay

|u(x)|\leq C\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{\hat{A}^{-1}(|x|^{1-n})}\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where the positive constant
C
depends solely on the dimension
n
and
p^{\pm}
. We remark that in the case
A(t)=t^{p}
, with
sp>1
,
s\in(0,1)
, Theorem
4.3
recover the well-known estimate
|u(x)|\leq C|x|^{-(n-1)/p}\|u\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, valid for any
u\in H^{s,p}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. See for instance
[
13
]
. Moreover, the Strauss lemma has applications to local and non-local Hénon-type equations (see for instance
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
).

We also consider a class of spaces based on the Littlewood-Paley decomposition. Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}
with Fourier transform
\hat{\Phi}
a function such that
\text{supp}\,\hat{\Phi}\subset B(0,1)
and
\hat{\Phi}(\xi)=1
on
B(0,1/2)
.
For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
denote
\Phi_{k}(x)=2^{nk}\Phi(2^{k}x)
and
\phi_{k}(x)=\Phi_{k}(x)-\Phi_{k-1}(x)
. We define the
Orlicz-Lizorkin-Triebel spaces
as follows

F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\colon\|\Phi*u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{sk}\phi_{k}*u|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\right\},\quad q>1,

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\Phi*u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|\{2^{sk}\phi_{k}*u\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{L^{A}(\ell^{q})}.

In Theorem
5.1
we prove the equivalence with the Orlicz-Bessel potential spaces:

F_{s}^{A,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with equivalence of norms. Moreover, in Theorems
5.3
and
5.4
we provide for an atomic decomposition characterization of these spaces.

The article is organized as follows: in Section
2
we introduce the basic notation and preliminary definitions on Orlicz spaces and Bessel Kernels. Section
3
is devoted to prove our results related with Bessel-Orlicz potential spaces, giving in Section
4
a Strauss type lemma for potential spaces. Finally, in Section
5
we deal with the Orlicz-Lizorkin-Triebel spaces.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Young functions

We consider the following class of Young functions
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
,

(i)

A
is convex, increasing, and
A(0)=0
.

(ii)

A
is super-linear at zero and at infinite, that is

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\frac{A(t)}{t}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(t)}{t}=\infty.

The above Young functions
A
admit the representation

A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(\tau)\,d\tau,

where the function
a
is right-continuous for
t\geq 0
, positive for
t>0
, non-decreasing and
satisfies the conditions

a(0)=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}a(t)=\infty.

We extend
A
evenly to
\mathbb{R}
. Throughout the paper, we call
A
a Young function if
A
satisfies the above properties.

Associated to
A
is the Young function complementary to it which is defined as follows:

(2.1)

\hat{A}(t):=\sup\left\{tw-A(w)\colon w>0\right\}.

The following Young-type inequality holds

(2.2)

t_{1}t_{2}\leq A(t_{1})+\hat{A}(t_{2})\text{ for every }t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0.

A Young function
A
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition if there exists
C>2
such that

A(2t)\leq 2A(t)\quad\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

By
[
24
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.3, Chapter 1]
, a Young function
A
and its complementary Young function
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
condition if and only if there is
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\infty
such that

(2.3)

p^{+}\leq\frac{ta(t)}{A(t)}\leq p^{+},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

In particular, (
2.3
) implies that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\min\{t^{p^{-}},t^{p^{+}}\}

\displaystyle\leq A(t)\leq\max\{t^{p^{-}},t^{p^{+}}\},\quad t\geq 0.

2.2.
Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

Given a Young function
A
, we define the
Orlicz space

\displaystyle L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}:\Phi_{A}(u)<\infty\},

and the
fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space
of order
s\in(0,1]

W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}:\Phi_{A}(u)+\Phi_{s,A}(u)<\infty\},

where
\Phi_{A}
and
\Phi_{s,A}
are the modulars given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{A}(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(|u|)\,dx

\displaystyle\Phi_{s,A}(u)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(|D^{s}u|)\,d\mu,&\text{ if }s\in(0,1),\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx&\text{ if }s=1,\end{cases}

where
d\mu=|x-y|^{-n}dxdy
and
D^{s}u=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}
.

The spaces
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are endowed with the Luxemburg norms defined as

\|u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\Phi_{A}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}

and

\|u\|_{W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+[u]_{W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

being
[u]_{W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
the so-called Gagliardo-like seminorm defined as

[u]_{W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\Phi_{s,A}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

The following Hölder’s type inequality holds for Orlicz spaces (see
[
23
, Theorem 3.7.5]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a Young function with complementary function
\hat{A}
. If
u\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
v\in L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then
uv\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|uv|\,dx\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|v\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

In
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
it is proved the following inequality for convolutions in Orlicz spaces.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A
be a Young function. Then

\|u*v\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|v\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for every
u\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
v\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(|u*v|)\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\|v\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|u|\right)\,dx.

2.3.
Bessel kernels

Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the Bessel kernel

G_{s}(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((1+|x|^{2})^{-s/2}\right),

where
\mathcal{F}(f)
denotes the Fourier Transform of
f
. Here, we allow
s\in\mathbb{R}
since
\hat{G}_{s}(\xi)=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-s/2}
can be identify with a tempered distribution for all
s
. We also use the notation
\hat{f}
to denote the Fourier transform and
\check{f}
to denote the inverse Fourier transform. According to
[
27
, Proposition 2]
and
[
8
, pages 13 and 27]
, we recall some useful properties of
G_{s}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The Bessel kernel satisfies the following properties:

i)

G_{s}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
s>0
,

ii)

\nabla G_{s}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
s>1
,

iii)

G(x)=G(-x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

iv)

G(x)
is decreasing.

The
L^{1}
-stability of the Bessel kernel
G_{s}
and the behavior of its
L^{1}
-modulus of continuity is established in formulas 9.3 and 9.17 from
[
8
]
as follows.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
s\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|G_{s}(x+h)-G_{s}(x)|\,dx\leq\frac{C}{1-s}|h|^{s}.

2.4.
Some further notation

The Schwartz class of rapidly decreasing
C^{\infty}
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}
. The space of continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{S}
is called the space of tempered distributions and denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.

3.
Bessel-Orlicz potential spaces and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

In this section, we introduce the Bessel-Orlicz potential spaces and we state some of their basic properties.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a Young function
A
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the Bessel-Orlicz potential space
H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=G_{s}*f,\text{ for some }f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\}.

We equip
H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The following lemma states that the definition of the space
H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and its norm make sense. We concentrate on the case
s>0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given a Young function
A
and
s>0
, we have that for any
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it follows that
G_{s}*f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, if
G_{s}*f_{1}=G_{s}*f_{2}
for some
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f_{1}=f_{2}
a. e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

From the Young’s inequality in Orlicz spaces Lemma
2.2
it follows that

(3.1)

\|G_{s}*f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|G_{s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

and so
G_{s}*f
is well-defined and belongs to
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For the second part, let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}
. Then, by Fubini’s theorem

(3.2)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G_{s}*f_{i})(x)\phi(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(x-y)f_{i}(y)\phi(x)\,dy\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}(y)(G_{s}*\phi)(y)\,dy,\end{split}

for
i=1,2
. Hence,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{1}(y)(G_{s}*\phi)(y)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{2}(y)(G_{s}*\phi)(y)\,dy.

Since
G_{s}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}
is a bijection, by letting
\varphi=G_{s}*\phi
and since
\phi
is arbitrary, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f_{1}-f_{2})(x)\varphi(x)\,dx=0

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
. Thus,
f_{1}=f_{2}
a. e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

If
0<s_{1}<s_{2}
, then

H^{s_{2},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset H^{s_{1},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{ and }\quad\|u\|_{H^{s_{1},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{s_{2},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{s_{2},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and write
u=G_{s_{2}}*f
, for some
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
\varepsilon=s_{2}-s_{1}>0
. Then,

(3.3)

u=G_{s_{2}}*g=(G_{s_{1}}*G_{\varepsilon})*f=G_{s_{1}}*(G_{\varepsilon}*f).

By Young’s inequality (
3.1
),
G_{\varepsilon}*f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence,
u\in H^{s_{1},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, by Young’s inequality again,

\|u\|_{H^{s_{1},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|G_{\varepsilon}*f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|G_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|G_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|u\|_{H^{s_{2},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In view of the fact that
\|G_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
, we conclude the proof.

3.1.
The local case
s=1

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A
be a Young function such that
A
and its conjugate
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Let
u\in W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
u\in H^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and there is a constant
C>0
independent of
u
such that

\|u\|_{H^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, by
[
17
, Corollary 1.10]
, there is a sequence
u_{k}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{S}
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, by the inversion formula
[
8
, Eq. (5.30)]
, and letting
G_{-1}
the inverse Bessel operator of
G_{1}
, we get

(3.4)

G_{-1}u_{k}=G_{1}*u_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}D_{i}u_{k},

where for each
i
, the operator
T_{i}
is given by a singular integral (see
[
8
, Eq. (5.7)]
)

(3.5)

T_{i}\varphi(x):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}D_{i}G_{1}(x-y)\varphi(y)\,dy,\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{S}.

We will extend the formula (
3.4
) to
u\in W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Now, since
G_{1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u_{k}\to u
in
W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we get by Young’s inequality Lemma
3.1
that
G_{1}*(u_{k}-u)\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(3.6)

\|G_{1}*(u_{k}-u)\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Regarding the operator
T_{i}
, we recall that
D_{i}G_{1}
is not in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
but it is an analytic function outside the origin decreasing exponentially near infinite.
However, we have by (9.2) in
[
8
]
that the following estimates hold

|D_{i}G_{i}(x-y)|\leq C|x-y|^{-n}\quad\text{and}\quad|D_{ij}G_{i}(x-y)|\leq C|x-y|^{-n-1},\quad\text{for all }i,j=1,...,n.

Hence, by
[
8
, Eq. (5.7)]
,
[
8
, Eq. (5.6)]
which says that the Fourier transform of
D_{i}G_{1}
is bounded,
[
14
, Prop. 5.2]
which gives the Hormander’s condition, and the Calderón-Zygmund Theorem
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
, the operator
T_{i}
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
p>1
. In particular, it is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus,
T_{i}
is a Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator (see for instance
[
22
, page 115]
). Thus, by Corollary 5.4.3 in
[
22
]
and since
A
and
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, these operators are continuous from
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and so

(3.7)

\bigg\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}D_{i}(u_{k}-u)\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Hence, by (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we obtain that

(3.8)

\bigg\|G_{1}*(u_{k}-u)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}D_{i}(u_{k}-u)\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0,

as
k\to\infty
. Consequently, by (
3.4
),

(3.9)

u_{k}=G_{1}*(G_{-1}u_{k})=G_{1}*\left(G_{1}*(u_{k}-u)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}D_{i}(u_{k}-u)\right)+G_{1}*\left(G_{1}*u+\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}D_{i}u\right).

Taking the limit as
k\to\infty
in
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the previous equality, recalling that
u_{k}\to u
in
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and (
3.8
) together with Young’s inequality, we get

u=G_{1}*\left(G_{1}*u+\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}D_{i}u\right)\quad\text{a. e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

and hence

G_{-1}u=G_{1}*u+\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}D_{i}u.

Let
f=G_{-1}u
. Then, by Young’s inequality and the continuity of the singular operators
T_{i}
in
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we get
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and so
u\in H^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|u\|_{H^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|G_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R^{n}})}\|u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|D_{i}u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=C\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This ends the proof.

We now prove the converse of Theorem
3.4
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
A
be a Young function such that
A
and its conjugate
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Let
u\in H^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
u\in W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and there is a constant
C>0
independent of
u
such that

\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{1,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, there is
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
u=G_{1}*f
. We will show that the distributional derivative
D_{i}u
exists and belong to
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
i
.

Let
f_{k}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
converging to
f
in
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and denote
u_{k}=G_{1}*f_{k}
. As
D_{i}G_{1}
is a distribution (see
[
8
, page 27]
), the convolution
D_{i}G_{1}*f_{k}
is well defined and it is given for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

(3.10)

\begin{split}(D_{i}G_{1}*f_{k})(\phi)&=D_{i}G_{1}(f_{k,R}*\phi)\qquad(f_{k,R}(y):=f_{k}(-y))\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{1}(x)D_{i}(f_{k,R}*\phi)(x)\,dx\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{1}(x)f_{k}(-y)D_{i}\phi(x-y)\,dx\,dy\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{1}(x)f_{k}(y)D_{i}\phi(x+y)\,dx\,dy\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{1}(z-y)f_{k}(y)D_{i}\phi(z)\,dz\,dy\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G_{1}*f_{k})(z)D_{i}\phi(z)\,dz=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{k}(z)D_{i}\phi(z)\,dz.\end{split}

On the other hand, the distribution
D_{i}G_{1}
can be seen as a singular integral (see (5.7) in
[
8
]
). So,

(3.11)

\begin{split}(D_{i}G_{1}*f_{k})(\phi)&=D_{i}G_{1}(f_{k,R}*\phi)\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}D_{i}G_{1}(x)(f_{k,R}*\phi)(x)\,dx\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}D_{i}G_{1}(x)f_{k}(y)\phi(x+y)\,dy\,dx\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{|z-y|>\varepsilon}D_{i}G_{1}(z-y)f_{k}(y)\phi(z)\,dy\,dz\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{K}T^{i}_{\varepsilon}f_{k}(z)\phi(z)\,dz,\end{split}

where
K=supp(\phi)
and

T^{i}_{\varepsilon}f_{k}(z):=\int_{|z-y|>\varepsilon}D_{i}G_{1}(z-y)f_{k}(y)\,dy.

By
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
8
, Eq. (5.7)]
, the operator

T_{i}\varphi(x):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}D_{i}G_{1}(x-y)\varphi(y)\,dy

is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
p>1.
Hence, by
[
14
, Theorem 5.14]
, the maximal operator

T_{i}^{*}\varphi(x):=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}|T^{i}_{\varepsilon}\varphi(x)|

is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
p>1
. In particular, for each fixed
k
we obtain

|T^{i}_{\varepsilon}f_{k}(z)|\leq T_{i}^{*}f_{k}(z)\in L^{1}(K),\quad K=\text{supp }\phi,

for any
z\in K
and any
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, we may applied Lebesgue dominated convergence Theorem in the last equality of (
3.11
) and take the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
to obtain

(D_{i}G_{1}*f_{k})(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}T_{i}f_{k}(z)\phi(z)\,dz.

Combining this with (
3.10
) we get

(3.12)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{k}(y)D_{i}\phi(y)\,dy=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}T_{i}f_{k}(z)\phi(z)\,dz.

Taking the limit as
k\to\infty
and considering the continuity of the convolution with
G_{1}
in
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(by Young’s inequality) and the continuity of the distribution
D_{i}G_{1}
(by Corollary 5.4.3 in
[
22
]
) give

D_{i}u=T_{i}f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and also that there is a constant
C>0
such that

\|D_{i}u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|T_{i}f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This ends the proof of the theorem.

The Fractional order case

Theorem 3.7
.

. Then,

and there exists

, with

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(|u|)\,dx+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|}{|h|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdh}{|h|^{n}}\leq

Let

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|}{|h|^{s}}\right)\,dx

and integrating with respect to

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|}{|h|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdh}{|h|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(|w_{s^{\prime}}(h)||f(x)||h|^{-s}\right)\frac{dxdh}{|h|^{n}}.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(|w_{s^{\prime}}(h)||f(x)||h|^{-s}\right)|h|^{-n}dh=\left(\int_{|h|\leq 1}+\int_{|h|>1}\right)A\left(|w_{s^{\prime}}(h)||f(x)||h|^{-s}\right)|h|^{-n}dh:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

. Using Lemma

|w_{s^{\prime}}(h)|\leq 2\|G_{s^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, so,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|h|>1}A\left(|w_{s^{\prime}}(h)||f(x)||h|^{-s}\right)|h|^{-n}dh

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|h|>1}A\left(2\|G_{s^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|f(x)||h|^{-s}\right)|h|^{-n}dh

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}A\left(2\|G_{s^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|f(x)|r^{-s}\right)r^{-1}dh

\displaystyle\leq A\left(2\|G_{s^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|f(x)|\right)n\omega_{n}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-1-s}dh=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{s}A\left(2\|G_{s^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|f(x)|\right),

since
r^{-s}<1
and
A
is convex.

Case
|h|\leq 1
. By Lemma
2.4

|w_{s^{\prime}}(h)|\leq\frac{C}{1-s^{\prime}}|h|^{s^{\prime}}
, so, using the convexity of
A
gives that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|h|\leq 1}A\left(\tfrac{C}{1-s}|h|^{s^{\prime}}|f(x)||h|^{-s}\right)|h|^{-n}dh

\displaystyle\leq A(\tfrac{C}{1-s^{\prime}}|f(x)|)\int_{0}^{1}|h|^{-n+s^{\prime}-s}dh\leq\frac{n\omega_{n}}{s^{\prime}-s}A(\tfrac{C}{1-s^{\prime}}|f(x)|)

and
I_{2}
into (

) gives that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|}{|h|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdh}{|h|^{n}}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{s^{\prime}-s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(C_{2}|f(x)|)\,dx

where
C_{1}=\frac{2n\omega n}{s}
and
C_{2}=2\|G_{s^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\frac{c}{1-s^{\prime}}
.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|}{\lambda|h|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdh}{|h|^{n}}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{s^{\prime}-s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\frac{C_{2}|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx,

but, since

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(|G_{s}*f|)\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(\|G_{s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|f|)\,dx.

This concludes the proof.

To prove the reverse inequality we need the following continuity result.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\alpha>\gamma>0
,
\gamma<1
, and define
d\mu=\frac{dxdt}{|t|^{n}}
and the kernel

K(z,x,t):=\frac{G_{\alpha}(z-x)-G_{\alpha}(z-x+t)}{|t|^{\gamma}},

then there exists
C>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|K(z,x,t)v(x,t)w(z)|\,d\mu\,dz\leq C\|v\|_{L^{A}(d\mu)}\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

holds for any
v\in L^{A}(d\mu)
and
w\in L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

We consider the linear operator
T
defined on
L^{A}(d\mu)
as

(Tv)(z)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(z,x,t)v(x,t)\,d\mu.

We will prove that the image of
T
is contained in
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and that
T
is bounded from
L^{A}(d\mu)
to
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, that is, for any
w\in L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

I=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(z,x,t)v(x,t)w(z)\,d\mu\,dz\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(Tv)(z)w(z)\,dz\right|\leq C\|v\|_{L^{A}(d\mu)}\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For this purpose, we will consider a duality approach: it is well-known that the linear operator
T\colon L^{A}(d\mu)\to L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded if and only if its adjoint operator
T^{*}\colon L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu)
is bounded. The operator
T^{*}
acting on
w(z)
is defined as

(T^{*}w)(x,t)=\frac{1}{|t|^{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[G_{\alpha}(z-x)-G_{\alpha}(z-x+t)]w(z)\,dz.

By Lemma
2.3
,
G_{\alpha}
is symmetric. Denoting
J_{\alpha}w(x):=(G_{\alpha}*w)(x)
, we can write

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(T^{*}w)(x,t)&=\frac{1}{|t|^{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[G_{\alpha}(\tau)-G_{\alpha}(\tau+t)]w(\tau+x)\,d\tau\\
&=\frac{(G_{\alpha}*w)(x)-(G_{\alpha}*w)(x-t)}{|t|^{\gamma}}=\frac{J_{\alpha}w(x)-J_{\alpha}w(x-t)}{|t|^{\gamma}}.\end{split}

By definition of
T
and
T^{*}
and Hölder’s inequality for Orlicz spaces we get formally

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(Tv)(z)w(z)\,dz\right|=\left|\langle Tv,w\rangle\right|=\left|\langle v,T^{*}w\rangle_{d\mu}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(x,t)(T^{*}w)(x,t)\,d\mu\right|\leq C\|v\|_{L^{A}(d\mu)}\|T^{*}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu)}.

Hence, it only remains to prove that
T^{*}w\in L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu)
and

(3.15)

\|T^{*}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu)}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We will find an upper bound of the Luxemburg norm
\|T^{*}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu)}
by analysing the modular.

\bullet
Case
|t|<1
and
\alpha>1
. In this case, due to Lemma
2.3
,
\nabla G_{\alpha}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
since
\alpha>1
. We start proving that
|\nabla J_{\alpha}w|\in W_{loc}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
G_{\alpha}*w\in L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it is locally integrable by Holder’s inequality. Next, we show that
\nabla J_{\alpha}w=\nabla(G_{\alpha}*w)
is the regular distribution
(\nabla G_{\alpha})*w
and so it is locally integrable by Holder’s and Young’s inequalities. Indeed, if
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then by the symmetry of
G_{\alpha}
, Tonelli’s Theorem and Holder’s inequality together with (
3.1
), we get

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{\alpha}(x-y)|w(y)||D_{i}\varphi(x)|\,dx\,dy&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G_{\alpha}*|w|)(x)|D_{i}\varphi(x)|\,dx\\
&\leq\|G_{\alpha}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|D_{i}\varphi\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

Hence, Fubini’s Theorem and the fact that
G_{\alpha}\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
imply for
i=1,\ldots,n
that

(3.16)

\begin{split}D_{i}(G_{\alpha}*w)(\varphi)&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G_{\alpha}*w)(x)D_{i}\varphi(x)\,dx\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{\alpha}(x-y)D_{i}\varphi(x)\,dx\right)w(y)\,dy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}D_{i}G_{\alpha}(x-y)\varphi(x)\,dx\right)w(y)\,dy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(D_{i}G_{\alpha}*w)(x)\varphi(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Hence,
\nabla J_{\alpha}w=(\nabla G_{\alpha})*w\in W_{loc}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (see for instance
[
25
, Theorem 6.9]
) we have that

\frac{|J_{\alpha}w(x)-J_{\alpha}w(x-t)|}{|t|^{\gamma}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}|t|^{1-\gamma}|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(x-st)|\,ds.

We denote
\lambda:=C_{1}\|\nabla J_{\alpha}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, where
C_{1}>1
is a constant to be determined. By Jensen’s inequality

\hat{A}\left(\frac{|(T^{*}w)(x,t)|}{\lambda}\right)\leq\hat{A}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{-1}|t|^{1-\gamma}|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(x-st)|\,ds\right)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\hat{A}\left(\lambda^{-1}|t|^{1-\gamma}|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(x-st)|\right)\,ds.

Integrating with respect to
d\mu
, using the translation invariance of the integral, and the fact that
1>\gamma
give the following relation

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}\left(\frac{|T^{*}w|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx|t|^{-n}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{1}}|t|^{-n}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(\lambda^{-1}|t|^{1-\gamma}|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(x-st)|)\,dx\right)ds\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}|t|^{-n}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(\lambda^{-1}|t|^{1-\gamma}|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(y)|)\,dy\right)ds\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}|t|^{-n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(\lambda^{-1}|t|^{1-\gamma}|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(y)|)\,dy\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{1}}|t|^{-n+1-\gamma}\,dt\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(\lambda^{-1}|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(y)|)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(\lambda^{-1}|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(y)|)\,dy\right).

where we have used that
\gamma<1
and then

\int_{B_{1}}|t|^{-n+1-\gamma}\,dt=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}r^{-\gamma}\,dr\leq C_{1},

for some constant
C_{1}\geq 1
. This gives that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}\left(\frac{|T^{*}w|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx|t|^{-n}dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}\left(\frac{1}{C_{1}}\frac{|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(y)|}{\|\nabla J_{\alpha}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla J_{\alpha}w(y)|}{\|\nabla J_{\alpha}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)\,dy=1,

where we have used the definition of the Luxemburg norm. Hence, using Lemma
2.2
, we get that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|T^{*}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{1})}&\leq\lambda=C_{1}\|\nabla J_{\alpha}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=C_{1}\|(\nabla G_{\alpha})*w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq C_{1}\|\nabla G_{\alpha}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

\bullet
Case
|t|<1
and
\alpha<1
. Now, since
\alpha<1
,
\nabla G_{\alpha}\notin L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We proceed by writing:

(T^{*}w)(x,t)=\frac{1}{|t|^{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[G_{\alpha}(z-x)-G_{\alpha}(z-x+t)]w(z)\,dz=K_{t}*w(x)

where

K_{t}(x):=\frac{1}{|t|^{\gamma}}(G_{\alpha}(x)-G_{\alpha}(x-t)).

Observe that, by Lemma
2.4

\displaystyle\|K_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|t|^{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|G_{\alpha}(x)-G_{\alpha}(x-t)|\,dx\leq C|t|^{\alpha-\gamma}.

Then, by Lemma
2.2
, for
t\in B_{1}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(|T^{*}w|)\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(\|K_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|w|)\,dx\leq|t|^{\alpha-\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(C|w|)\,dx.

Integrating with respect to
|t|^{-n}dt
, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(|T^{*}w|)\,dx|t|^{-n}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{1}}|t|^{\alpha-\gamma-n}\,dt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(C|w|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}(C_{1}|w|)\,dx

since
\alpha-\gamma>0
gives that, for some
C_{3}\geq 1
,

\int_{B_{1}}|t|^{\alpha-\gamma-n}\,dt=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha-\gamma-1}\,dr\leq C_{3}.

Let
\lambda=C_{3}C\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, then the previous computation gives

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}\left(\frac{|T^{*}w|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx|t|^{-n}dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}\left(\frac{C|w|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}\left(\frac{|w|}{\|w\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)\,dx=1,

where we have used the definition of the Luxemburg norm, and this implies that

(3.18)

\|T^{*}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{1})}\leq C_{3}C\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

\bullet
Case
|t|<1
and
\alpha=1
.
In this case
\nabla G_{1}\not\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We use the Calderón-Zygmund estimates for singular integral operators.

Observe that by
[
8
, (5.6)]
,
|\mathcal{F}(\nabla G_{1})|
is bounded. Moreover, by
[
8
, (9.2)]
,

|D_{i}G_{1}(x)|\leq|x|^{-n},\qquad|D_{ij}G_{1}(x)|\leq C|x|^{-n-1}\qquad\text{for all }i,j=1,\ldots,n.

Therefore, in light of
[
14
, Theorem 5.2]
and the Calderón-Zygmund Theorem
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
, the tempered distribution
\nabla G_{1}
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
p>1
. In particular, it is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and defines a Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operators.

Thus, by Corollary 5.4.3 in
[
22
]
and since
A
and
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, this operators is continuous from
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and so

\|(\nabla G_{1})*w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Hence, (
3.17
) can be replaced by

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|T^{*}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu,|t|<1)}\leq c_{1}\|(\nabla G_{\alpha})*w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq Cc_{1}\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

\bullet
Case
|t|\geq 1
.
By triangular inequality in expression (
3.14
)

\|T^{*}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{1}^{c})}\leq\left\|\frac{J_{\alpha}w(x)}{|t|^{\gamma}}\right\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{1}^{c})}+\left\|\frac{J_{\alpha}w(x-t)}{|t|^{\gamma}}\right\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{1}^{c})}.

Let us analyze the first term. We proceed as in the previous case. Denote
\lambda:=C_{4}\|J_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, where
C_{4}\geq 1
is a constant to determine. Then, since
|t|\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\geq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}\left(\frac{|J_{\alpha}w(x)|}{\lambda|t|^{\gamma}}\right)\frac{dxdt}{|t|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{C_{4}}\int_{|t|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|t|^{n+\gamma}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{A}\left(\frac{|J_{\alpha}w(x)|}{\|J_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)dx\right)dt\leq 1,

where we have used the definition of the Luxemburg norm, and we have chosen
C_{4}=\left(\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{-n-\gamma}dt\right)^{-1}
.

Using again the definition of the norm together with Lemma
2.2
, this gives that

\left\|\frac{J_{\alpha}w(x)}{|t|^{\gamma}}\right\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{1}^{c})}\leq C_{4}\|J_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=C_{4}\|G_{\alpha}*w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{4}\|G_{\alpha}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The second norm term can be bounded in an analogous way, obtaining,

(3.20)

\|T^{*}w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(d\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{1}^{c})}\leq 2C_{4}\|G_{\alpha}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|w\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Finally, from (
3.17
), (
3.18
), (
3.19
) and (
3.20
) we obtain (
3.15
), and the proof in concluded.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
u\in W^{s^{\prime},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
s^{\prime}>s>0
. Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s^{\prime},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s^{\prime},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
u_{k}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
so that
u_{k}\to u
in
W^{s^{\prime},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The inversion formula
[
8
, formula (5.26)]
has the form

(3.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{-s}u_{k}&=G_{2s^{\prime}-s}*u_{k}(z)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(G_{2s^{\prime}-s}(z-x)-G_{2s^{\prime}-s}(z-y))(u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y))}{|x-y|^{2s^{\prime}}}d\mu_{s^{\prime}}(x,y)\\
&=G_{2s^{\prime}-s}*u_{k}(z)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(G_{2s^{\prime}-s}(z-x)-G_{2s^{\prime}-s}(z-x+t))(u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(x-t))}{|t|^{2s^{\prime}}}d\mu_{s^{\prime}}(x,t)\\
&=G_{2s^{\prime}-s}*u_{k}(z)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Delta_{t}G_{2s^{\prime}-s}(z-x)}{|t|^{s^{\prime}}}\frac{\Delta_{t}u_{k}(x)}{|t|^{s^{\prime}}}d\mu_{s^{\prime}}(x,t)\\
&:=G_{2s^{\prime}-s}*u_{k}(z)+(Tu_{k})(z)\end{split}

where
\Delta_{t}(\cdot)
is the increment operator, and

d\mu_{s^{\prime}}(x,t)=\frac{1}{C(n,s^{\prime})G_{2n+2s^{\prime}}(0)}\frac{G_{2n+2s^{\prime}}(t)}{|t|^{n}}dxdt.

Observe that, by
[
18
, Proposition 6.15]

(3.22)

\displaystyle G_{2n+2s^{\prime}}(t)\leq\begin{cases}c_{1}(n,s^{\prime})|t|^{2s^{\prime}+n}\leq C_{1}&\text{ if }|t|\leq 2\\
c_{2}(n,s^{\prime})e^{-|t|/2}\leq C_{2}&\text{ if }|t|\geq 2,\end{cases}

then, for some positive constant
c
depending on
s^{\prime}
and
n
it holds that

(3.23)

d\mu_{s^{\prime}}(x,t)\leq cs^{\prime}(1-s^{\prime})\frac{dxdt}{|t|^{n}},

where we have used that
1/C(n,s^{\prime})\leq s^{\prime}(1-s^{\prime})
, see
[
8
, p.274]
.

We note that convolution with
G_{2s^{\prime}-s}
is a continuous operator from
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Indeed, by Lemma
2.2
we have that for any
v\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(3.24)

\displaystyle\|G_{2s^{\prime}-s}*v\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|G_{2s^{\prime}-s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|v\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|v\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Regarding the operator
T
in (
3.21
), Proposition
3.8
can be applied to
T
with
\gamma=s^{\prime}
and
\alpha=2s^{\prime}-s
since
0<s<s^{\prime}<1
, which gives
2s^{\prime}-s>s^{\prime}
, and
\frac{\Delta_{t}u(x)}{|t|^{s^{\prime}}}\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)
. Then,

is continuous from
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)
to

and so for any

,

\displaystyle\|Tv\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{2}\left\|\frac{\Delta_{t}v(x)}{|t|^{s^{\prime}}}\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)}=C_{2}[v]_{W^{s^{\prime},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In particular, since
u_{k}\to u
in
W^{s^{\prime},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it follows

\|T(u_{k}-u)\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[u_{k}-u]_{W^{s^{\prime},A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0\quad\text{as }k\to\infty.

As a result, using the expression of the inversion formula in (
3.21
), writing

(3.25)

\begin{split}u_{k}&=G_{s}*(G_{-s}*u_{k})\\
&=G_{s}*\left(G_{2s^{\prime}-s}*(u_{k}-u)+T(u_{k}-u)\right)+G_{s}*\left(G_{2s^{\prime}-s}*u+T(u)\right)\\
&=G_{s}*\left(G_{2s^{\prime}-s}*(u_{k}-u)\right)+G_{s}*\left(T(u_{k}-u)\right)+G_{s}*\left(G_{2s^{\prime}-s}*u+T(u)\right),\end{split}

and taking

and also

. Finally,

This concludes the proof.

4.
Strauss Lemma for potential spaces

We consider the restriction of the Bessel-Orlicz potential space to radial functions. Given a Young function
A
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the space

H^{s,A}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon u(x)=u_{0}(|x|)\}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in H^{s,A}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
u=G_{s}*f
, where
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a radial function.

Proof.

To establish the lemma, it suffices to show that
f
is invariant under rotation; that is,
f\circ R=f
for every rotation
R\in SO(n)
. Let
(f\circ R)(\psi)
with
\psi\in\mathcal{S}
, and define

\varphi=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1+|x|^{2})^{s/2}\hat{\psi}).

This gives that
\hat{\psi}=\mathcal{F}(G_{s}*\varphi)
, so,
\psi=G_{s}*\varphi
. Hence,

\displaystyle(f\circ R)(\psi)

\displaystyle=f\left((G_{s}*\varphi)\circ R^{-1}\right)=f\left((G_{s}\circ R^{-1})*(\varphi\circ R^{-1})\right)=f\left(G_{s}*(\varphi\circ R^{-1})\right)

where we have used that
G_{s}
is radial. Then, since
u\in H^{s,A}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle(f\circ R)(\psi)

\displaystyle=f\left(G_{s}*(\varphi\circ R^{-1})\right)=(f*G_{s})(\varphi\circ R^{-1})=(u\circ R)(\varphi)=u(\psi).

Finally, from the equality
u(\varphi)=(G_{s}*f)(\varphi)=f(G_{s}*\varphi)=f(\varphi)
, we get the result.

The following result plays a fundamental role in the proof of the Strauss Lemma. Our approach is inspired by the techniques established in
[
13
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A
be a Young function so that
A
and
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Let
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a radial function. Then for all
R>0
and all
x
,

|f*\chi_{B(0,R)}(x)|\leq C\frac{cR^{n-1}}{\rho^{n-1}}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho^{1-n}}{R}\right)\right\}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad\rho:=|x|.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a radial function and denote
f_{0}(r)=f(x)
. Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and consider
R>0
. If
y\in B(x,R)
, taking
y=ry^{\prime}
,
r=|y|
,
y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
x=rx^{\prime}
,
\rho=|x|
,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

\displaystyle f*\chi_{B(0,R)}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\rho-R}^{\rho+R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f_{0}(r)\chi_{B(x,R)}(ry^{\prime})r^{n-1}\,d\sigma(y^{\prime})dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\rho-R}^{\rho+R}f_{0}(r)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\chi_{B(x,R)}(ry^{\prime})\,d\sigma(y^{\prime})\right)r^{n-1}\,dr.

It is not hard to see that
\chi_{B(x,R)}(ry^{\prime})=\chi_{[t_{0},1]}(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})
with

t_{0}=\frac{r^{2}+\rho^{2}-R^{2}}{2r\rho}.

Moreover, if
\rho\geq R
it is obtained that
t_{0}\geq-1
. Then, by
[
18
, Apprndix D.3]

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\chi_{B(x,R)}(ry^{\prime})\,d\sigma(y^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\chi_{[t_{0},1]}(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})\,d\sigma(y^{\prime})\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\chi_{[t_{0},1]}(t)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\,dt.

If
\rho\geq R
, we get that

\displaystyle f*\chi_{B(0,R)}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\rho-R}^{\rho+R}\int_{t_{0}}^{1}f_{0}(r)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}r^{n-1}\,dtdr.

Hence, by Hölder’s inequality we obtain that

\displaystyle|f*\chi_{B(0,R)}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\rho-R}^{\rho+R}|f_{0}(r)|(1-t_{0})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{A}((0,\infty),r^{n-1}dr)}\|(1-t_{0})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\|_{L^{\hat{A}}((\rho-R,\rho+R),r^{n-1}dr)}.

Let us bound
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R},r^{n-1}dr)}
.
Let
C=\max\{1,n\omega_{n}\}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}A\left(\frac{|f_{0}(r)|}{C}\right)r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{C}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\max\{1,n\omega_{n}\}\frac{|f(x)|}{C}\right)\,dx\leq 1.

Then, by definition of the Luxemburg norm we get that

\|f_{0}\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R},r^{n-1}dr)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

To bound
\|(1-t_{0})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\|_{L^{\hat{A}}((\rho-R,\rho+R),r^{n-1}dr)}
we proceed as follows. Let
C>0
to determinate. Taking
u=r/\rho
,
u\in[1-R/\rho,1+R/\rho]
and then

\displaystyle\int_{\rho-R}^{\rho+R}\hat{A}\left(\frac{(1-t_{0})^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{C}\right)r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\rho-R}^{\rho+R}\hat{A}\left(\frac{1}{C}\left(1-\frac{r^{2}+\rho^{2}-R^{2}}{2r\rho}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\right)r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle=\rho^{n}\int_{1-R/\rho}^{1+R/\rho}\hat{A}\left(\frac{1}{C}\left(\frac{(R/\rho)^{2}-(1-u)^{2}}{2u}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\right)u^{n-1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{n}\int_{1-R/\rho}^{1+R/\rho}\hat{A}\left(\frac{1}{C}\left(\frac{2}{u}\frac{R^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\right)u^{n-1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{n}\int_{1-R/\rho}^{1+R/\rho}\hat{A}\left(\frac{1}{C}\frac{1}{u^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\frac{R^{n-1}}{\rho^{n-1}}\right)u^{n-1}\,du.

If
\rho\geq 2R
,
u\in[1/2,3/2]
, so

\displaystyle\int_{\rho-R}^{\rho+R}\hat{A}\left(\frac{(1-t_{0})^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{C}\right)r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{n}\hat{A}\left(\frac{c}{C}\frac{R^{n-1}}{\rho^{n-1}}\right)\frac{R}{\rho}\leq 1

when we take
C=\frac{cR^{n-1}}{\rho^{n-1}}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho^{1-n}}{R}\right)\right\}^{-1}
. Therefore, by definition of the Luxemburg norm

\|(1-t_{0})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\|_{L^{\hat{A}}((\rho-R,\rho+R),r^{n-1}dr)}\leq\frac{cR^{n-1}}{\rho^{n-1}}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho^{1-n}}{R}\right)\right\}^{-1}.

This yields that, when
\rho\geq 2R
,

(4.1)

|f*\chi_{B(0,R)}(x)|\leq C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{cR^{n-1}}{\rho^{n-1}}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho^{1-n}}{R}\right)\right\}^{-1}.

If
\rho<2R
, using Example 3.6.9 of
[
23
]

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|f*\chi_{B(0,R)}(x)|&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)||\chi_{B(0,R)}(x-y)\,dy|\\
&\leq\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\chi_{B(0,R)}\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}R^{n}A^{-1}\left(R^{-n}\right).\end{split}

Let us see that, if
\rho<2R
, then

R^{n}A^{-1}\left(R^{-n}\right)\leq\frac{CR^{n-1}}{\rho^{n-1}}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho^{1-n}}{R}\right)\right\}^{-1}.

This is equivalent to see that

\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho^{1-n}}{R}\right)\left(\frac{\rho^{1-n}}{R}\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{C}{A^{-1}\left(R^{-n}\right)}.

We use that for all
t>0
,
t\leq A^{-1}(t)\hat{A}^{-1}(t)\leq 2t
, giving that

\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho^{1-n}}{R}\right)\left(\frac{\rho^{1-n}}{R}\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{2}{A^{-1}(\rho^{1-n}/R)}.

Let us see that

\frac{2}{A^{-1}(\rho^{1-n}/R)}\leq\frac{C}{A^{-1}\left(R^{-n}\right)}.

Since we assume the
\Delta_{2}
condition, this is equivalent to see that, for some
C_{1}\geq 2

A^{-1}\left(C_{1}R^{-n}\right)\leq A^{-1}(\rho^{1-n}/R)

and applying
A
to both sides, this equals to

C_{1}R^{1-n}\leq\rho^{1-n}.

But,
\rho<2R
implies that
2^{1-n}R^{1-n}<\rho^{1-n}
, so we can take
C_{1}=2^{1-n}
.

This concludes the proof.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A
be a Young function so that
A
and
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Let
u\in H^{s,A}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<sp^{-}
. Then there exists
C>0
depending of
n
,
p^{+}
and
p^{-}
such that

|u(x)|\leq C\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{\hat{A}^{-1}(|x|^{1-n})}\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a radial function. Then, from Lemma
4.1
there exists a radial function
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
f=f_{0}(|x|)
such that
u=G_{s}*f
.

Let
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a radial function and denote
f_{0}(r)=f(x)
. Without loss of generality, assume
f\geq 0
. Since
G_{s}
is decreasing, for a fix
a>0

\displaystyle f*G_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-y)G_{s}(y)\,dy=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{k-1}a\leq|y|\leq 2^{k}a}f(x-y)G_{s}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{s}(2^{k-1}a)\int_{|y|\leq 2^{k}a}f(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{s}(2^{k-1}a)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-y)\chi_{B(0,2^{k}a)}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{s}(2^{k-1}a)f*\chi_{B(0,2^{k}a)}.

From Lemma
4.2
, for a fix
a>0
, with
R=2^{k}a
,
\rho=|x|
, it yields

\displaystyle|G_{s}*f|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{s}(2^{k-1}a)|g*\chi_{B(0,2^{k}a)}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|x|^{1-n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{s}(2^{k-1}a)(2^{k}a)^{n-1}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{2^{k}a}\right)\right\}^{-1}.

If
r_{k}=2^{k-1}a
, then
\Delta r_{k}=r_{k+1}-r_{k}=2^{k-1}a
, so

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{s}(2^{k-1}a)(2^{k}a)^{n-1}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{2^{k}a}\right)\right\}^{-1}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{s}(r_{k})(r_{k})^{n-1}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{2r_{k}}\right)\right\}^{-1}\frac{\Delta r_{k}}{r_{k}}.

This is the Riemann sum for the integral

2^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{s}(r)r^{n-2}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{2r}\right)\right\}^{-1}\,dr.

Denote by
\hat{p}^{+}>1
the number such that

\frac{t(\hat{A}(t))^{\prime}}{\hat{A}(t)}\leq\hat{p}^{+}\quad\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

Then, it follows that

\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{2r_{k}}\right)\right\}^{-1}\leq(2r_{k})^{\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}}\left\{\hat{A}^{-1}\left(|x|^{1-n}\right)\right\}^{-1},

and letting
a\to 0
, we get

\displaystyle|G_{s}*f|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{\hat{A}^{-1}(|x|^{1-n})}\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{s}(r)r^{n-2}(2r)^{\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}}\,dr

\displaystyle=C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{\hat{A}^{-1}(|x|^{1-n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(x)|x|^{-1+\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{|x|^{1-n}}{\hat{A}^{-1}(|x|^{1-n})},

since the following relation holds
\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}=1-\frac{1}{p^{-}}
, and hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(x)|x|^{-1+\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2}G_{s}(r)r^{n-1-1+\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}}\,dr+\int_{2}^{\infty}G_{s}(r)r^{n-1-1+\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{2}r^{s-n}r^{n-1-1+\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}}\,dr+C\int_{2}^{\infty}e^{-R/2}r^{n-1-1+\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}}\,dr

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{2}r^{s-1-\frac{1}{p^{-}}}\,dr+C\int_{2}^{\infty}e^{-R/2}r^{n-1-1+\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{+}}}\,dr\leq C,

where we have used that
s>1/p^{-}
. The proof in now completed.

5.
Orlicz-Lizorkin-Triebel spaces

In this section, we generalized the Lizorkin-Triebel spaces to the framework of Orlicz spaces.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}
and let
\hat{\Phi}
be its Fourier transform. Suppose that

\text{supp}\,\hat{\Phi}\subset B(0,1),\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\Phi}(\xi)=1\quad\text{on}\quad B(0,1/2).

Set for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\Phi_{k}(x)=2^{nk}\Phi(2^{k}x)\quad\text{ so that }\quad\hat{\Phi}_{k}(\xi)=\hat{\Phi}(2^{-k}\xi),

and define
\phi_{k}(x)=\Phi_{k}(x)-\Phi_{k-1}(x)
. Observe that

\text{supp}\,\hat{\phi}_{k}\subset B(0,2^{k})\setminus B(0,2^{k-2})

and moreover

\hat{\Phi}(\xi)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\hat{\phi}(\xi)=1\quad\text{for all }\xi.

Given a Young function
A
,
q>1
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the Orlicz-Lizorkin-Triebel spaces as follows

F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\colon\|\Phi*u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{sk}\phi_{k}*u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\right\}

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\Phi*u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|\{2^{sk}\phi_{k}*u\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{L^{A}(\ell^{q})},

where

\|\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\|_{L^{A}(\ell^{q})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\left(\frac{\|f(x)\|_{q}}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}

being

\|f(x)\|_{q}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|f_{k}(x)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Observe that in the definition of
F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we ask whether
\Phi*u
and
\{2^{sk}\phi_{k}*u\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are regular distributions belonging to
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
L^{A}(\ell^{q})
, respectively. Also, we point out that as in the classical setting, we may define
F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
via Fourier transform as

(5.1)

F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\colon\left\|\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|2^{sk}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\hat{\phi}_{k}\hat{u})|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\right\}.

5.1.
A Lizorkin-Triebel characterization of potential spaces

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
A
and
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Then, the Orlicz-Lizorkin-Triebel space with
q=2
coincides with the Orlicz-Bessel potential space:

F_{s}^{A,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and there are constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|u\|_{F^{A,2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{1}\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By density, we may assume that
\hat{u}
has compact support. Write
u=G_{s}*f
, with
f\in L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Observe that
\hat{u}=\hat{G}_{s}\hat{f}
and so

(5.2)

\hat{f}(\xi)=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}\hat{u}.

Observe that
f\in\mathcal{S}
.

Let the Rademacher functions
\left\{r_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
given by

\displaystyle r_{0}(t)=1

\displaystyle\text{for }0<t\leq\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle r_{0}(t)=-1

\displaystyle\text{for }\frac{1}{2}<t\leq 1,

\displaystyle r_{0}(t+1)=r_{0}(t)

\displaystyle\text{for }t\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle r_{i}(t)=r_{0}(2^{i}t)

\displaystyle\text{for }i\in\mathbb{N}.

It is known that Rademacher functions form an orthonormal system in
L^{2}(0,1)
(see for instance
[
1
]
) and satisfies the Khinchin inequality (
[
1
, Lemma 4.2.3]
):

(5.3)

\bigg\|\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}r_{i}(t)\bigg\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\bigg|\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}r_{i}(t)\bigg|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}|a_{i}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{3}\bigg\|\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}r_{i}(t)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}

for any constants
a_{i}
. Define

m(\xi):=\dfrac{1}{\hat{\Phi}^{2}(\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\hat{\phi}_{i}^{2}(\xi)}.

Then, by
[
1
, Lemma 4.2.5]
,
m
can be written, for any
t\in[0,1]
, as

m(\xi):=\dfrac{r_{0}(t)\hat{\Phi}(\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{is}r_{i}(t)\hat{\phi}_{i}(\xi)}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}},

and satisfies the assumptions of
[
1
, Theorem 4.2.4]
, namely,
m\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and for all
R>0
and all multiindex
\sigma
with
|\sigma|\leq k
, with
k
is the least integer so that
k>n/2
, there holds

\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{R^{n}}\int_{\left\{1/2R\leq|\xi|\leq 2R\right\}}|R^{|\sigma|}D^{\sigma}m(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq B\end{split}

for some
B>0
. Moreover, defining
m_{i}:=m\hat{\phi}_{i}
,
k_{i}
the inverse Fourier transform of
m_{i}
and setting
M_{i}:=\sum_{j=-i}^{j=i}m_{j}
and
K_{i}:=\sum_{j=-i}^{j=i}k_{j}
. Then according to the proof of Theorem 4.2.4 in
[
1
]
,
K_{i}
satisfies the assumptions of the Calderón-Zygmund Theorem
[
14
, Theorem 5.1 and Theorem 7.11]
. Therefore, by
[
22
, Corollary 5.4.3]
, the mapping

f\to K_{i}*f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(M_{i}\mathcal{F}(f))\quad\text{is continuous from }L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ to }L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with a constant independent of
i
. Now, since
\|M_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is uniformly bounded and
M_{i}(\xi)\to m(\xi)
for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that

M_{i}\to m\text{ in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\text{ as }i\to\infty.

Since the inverse Fourier transform is an isomorphism in
S^{\prime}
, we have

K_{i}\to T\text{ in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}$ as }i\to\infty

for some distribution
T
so that
\hat{T}=m
. The norm in
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the mapping
f\to T*f
with
f\in\mathcal{S}
is

\begin{split}\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1}\|T(f)\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&=\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(T*f)g\,dx\right|:f,g\in S,\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1,\|g\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1\right\}\\
&=\sup\left\{\left|T(f*g)\right|:g\in S,\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1,\|g\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1\right\}.\end{split}

Now, since

T(f*g)=\lim_{i\to\infty}K_{i}(f*g)\leq C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C,

we conclude, by density, that convolution with
T
is continuous from
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular,

(5.4)

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\hat{f})\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Now, turning back to
f
so that (
4.1
) holds, we have

(5.5)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\hat{f})\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&=\bigg\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(r_{0}(t)\hat{\Phi}(\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{is}r_{i}(t)\hat{\phi}_{i}(\xi)\right)\hat{u}\right)\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&=\bigg\|r_{0}(t)\Phi*u+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{is}r_{i}(t)\phi_{i}*u\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

Observe that by the assumptions on
\hat{u}
, the above sums are indeed finite. Hence, from (
5.4
) and (
5.5
),

(5.6)

\bigg\|r_{0}(t)\Phi*u\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{is}r_{i}(t)\phi_{i}*u\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=C\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Integration in
t
gives

(5.7)

\int_{0}^{1}\bigg\|r_{0}(t)\Phi*u+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{is}r_{i}(t)\phi_{i}*u\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dt\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Now, applying Khinchin inequality (
5.3
) and (
5.7
)

(5.8)

\begin{split}\left\|\left(|\Phi*u|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{2is}|\phi_{i}*u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\leq C\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\left(r_{0}(t)\Phi*u+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{is}r_{i}(t)\phi_{i}*u\right)\,dt\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\bigg\|r_{0}(t)\Phi*u+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{is}r_{i}(t)\phi_{i}*u\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dt\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

We point out that the inequality

\bigg\|\int_{0}^{1}g(t,\cdot)\,dt\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dt\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\bigg\|g(t,\cdot)\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dt

follows by the dual characterization of the Orlicz norm and Holder’s inequality. Indeed,

(5.9)

\begin{split}\left\|\int_{0}^{1}g(t,\cdot)\,dt\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dt&=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg|\left(\int_{0}^{1}g(t,x)\,dt\right)h(x)\bigg|\,dx:\|h\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1\right\}\\
&\leq\sup\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g(t,x)||h(x)|\,dx\,dt:\|h\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1\right\}\\
&\leq C\sup\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\left\|g(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\cdot\|h\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dt:\|h\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1\right\}\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\left\|g(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dt.\end{split}

Observe that (
5.8
) is enough to prove the inclusion
H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset F_{s}^{A,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, since

(5.10)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{F_{s}^{A,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&=\|\Phi*u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\left\{2^{si}\phi_{i}*u\right\}\|_{L^{A}(\ell^{2})}\\
&=\|(|\Phi*u|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\bigg\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{2is}|\phi_{i}*u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq 2\bigg\|\left(|\Phi*u|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{2is}|\phi_{i}*u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

To prove the other inclusion, let

h:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((\hat{\Phi}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\hat{\phi}_{i}^{2})\hat{f}\right),\quad\text{that is }f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\mathcal{F}(h)).

Hence, for some
C>0
, using (
5.4
) we get

(5.11)

\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R})}.

Let now
g\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\|g\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
, with compact support, and

(5.12)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}gh\,dx\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\|h\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Moreover, we may write
g=G_{s}*v
, where

\hat{v}(\xi)=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}\hat{g}(\xi).

By using (
5.11
), (
5.12
) we obtain that,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}gh\,dx=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{g}\hat{h}\,dx

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{f}\hat{g}\left(\hat{\Phi}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\hat{\phi}_{i}^{2}\right)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left((\hat{u}\hat{\Phi})(\hat{v}\hat{\Phi})+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(2^{is}\hat{u}\hat{\phi}_{i})(2^{-is}\hat{v}\hat{\phi}_{i})\right)\,d\xi.

In the previous expression the constant
C
may change from line to line. Applying Plancherel’s formula and Cauchy’s inequalities, we obtain

\begin{split}\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left((\Phi*u)(\Phi*v)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(2^{is}\phi_{i}*u)(2^{-is}\phi_{i}*v)\right)dx\\
&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left((\Phi*u)(\Phi*v)+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{2si}|\phi_{i}*u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-2si}|\phi_{i}*v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right)dx.\end{split}

Finally using Holder’s inequality in the previous expression yields

\begin{split}\|u\|_{H^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\leq C\bigg\{\|\Phi*u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Phi*v\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\qquad+\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{2si}|\phi_{i}*u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-2si}|\phi_{i}*v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\bigg\}\\
&\leq C\bigg\{\|(|\Phi*u|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\bigg\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{2is}|\phi_{i}*u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\bigg\}\\
&\qquad\times\bigg\{\|(|\Phi*v|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\bigg\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-2is}|\phi_{i}*v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\bigg\}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{F_{s}^{A,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|v\|_{H^{-s,\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\end{split}

where we have used (
5.10
) for
u
and
v
. But

\|v\|_{H^{-s,\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|g\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1.

This concludes the proof of the theorem.

5.2.
An atomic decomposition in Orlicz-Lizorkin-Triebel spaces

We start with an elementary lemma which provides a representation for any
u\in F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
s>0
. Any
u\in F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
admits the decomposition

(5.13)

u=\Phi*u+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\phi_{k}*u.

Proof.

Let
u\in F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. First, we show that the series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\phi_{k}*u

converges in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Indeed, let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
, we need to prove that the limit

(5.14)

\begin{split}\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{k}*u\right)(\varphi)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\phi_{k}*u)\varphi\,dx\end{split}

exists. Now, by Cauchy’s and Holder’s inequality

(5.15)

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\phi_{k}*u)\varphi\,dx\bigg|&\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|2^{sk}\phi_{k}*u||2^{-sk}\varphi|\,dx\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|2^{sk}\phi_{k}*u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|2^{-sk}\varphi|^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\,dx\qquad(q^{\prime}=q/(q-1))\\
&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|2^{sk}\phi_{k}*u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}|\varphi|\,dx\\
&\leq C\bigg\|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|2^{sk}\phi_{k}*u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\hat{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

Since the latter term in (
5.15
) can be bounded independently of
N
because
u\in F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the convergence in (
5.14
) holds.

Now, since the Fourier transform is continuous in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and is one-to-one, from

\mathcal{F}\left(\Phi*u+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\phi_{k}*u\right)=\hat{\Phi}\hat{u}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\hat{\phi}_{k}\hat{u}=\hat{u}

we conclude (
5.13
).

We shall decompose further this representation for
s>0
. Given
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
x_{ik}=2^{-i}k
and we define the dyadic cuber
Q_{ik}
by setting

x\in Q_{ik}\text{ if and only if }x=x_{ik}+2^{-i}y,\text{ for }0\leq y_{j}<1,\,j=1,...,n.

We denote by
\chi_{ik}
the characteristic function of the dyadic cube
Q_{ik}
.

Given
m
a nonnegative integer, we say that a function
a_{ik}\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
C^{m}
-atom for the cube
Q_{ik}
if it satisfies supp
a_{ik}\subset\tilde{Q}_{ik}
, where
\tilde{Q}_{ik}
is the cube concentric with
Q_{ik}
with three times its side, and

(5.16)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},|\gamma|\leq m}2^{-i|\gamma|}|D^{\gamma}a_{ik}(x)|\leq 1.

We have the following dyadic representation in
F_{s}^{A,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
u\in F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
s>0
and suppose that
A
and
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be given. Then, there exist
C^{m}
-atoms
\left\{a_{ik}\right\}
for the dyadic cubes
Q_{ik}
and constants
s_{ik}
such that

(5.17)

u(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{-is}u_{i}(x),\quad\text{with }\,u_{i}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}s_{ik}a_{ik}(x),

and denoting
s_{i}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}s_{ik}\chi_{ik}(x)
, then there is
A>0
such that

(5.18)

\|\left\{s_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{\infty}\|_{L^{A}(\ell^{q})}=\bigg\|\left(\sum_{x\in Q_{ik}}|s_{ik}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq A\|u\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

We mainly follow the proof of Theorem 4.6.2 in
[
1
]
. Let
u\in F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and write (
5.13
) as

u=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{-is}u_{i}.

We set
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{00})
with
\int\eta\,dx=1
,
\eta_{i}(x)=2^{in}\eta(2^{i}x)
, and define
\eta_{ik}=\eta_{i}*\chi_{ik}
. Then supp
\eta_{ik}\subset\tilde{Q}_{ik}
and
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\eta_{ik}=1
. Let

b_{ik}=\eta_{ik}u_{i},\quad s_{ik}=\max_{x\in\tilde{Q}_{ik},|\gamma|\leq m}2^{-i|\gamma|}|D^{\gamma}b_{ik}(x)|

and

a_{ik}(x)=\dfrac{b_{ik}(x)}{s_{ik}}.

Observe that
a_{ik}
are
C^{m}
-atoms and since

u_{i}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\eta_{ik}u_{i}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b_{ik}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}s_{ik}a_{ik},

we get from (
5.13
) that

u=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{-is}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}s_{ik}a_{ik}.

This proves (
5.17
). Moreover, since
\hat{\Phi}_{n+1}=1
on supp
\hat{u}_{i}
, we get since Fourier transform is a bijection, that

u_{i}=\Phi_{i+1}*u_{i}.

Thus, by
[
1
, Lemma 4.3.7]
, for any
x\in\tilde{Q}_{ik}
and
|\gamma|\leq m
, we get as in
[
1
, Theorem 4.6.2]
that

(5.19)

\begin{split}|D^{\gamma}u_{n}(x)|\leq|D^{\gamma}\Phi_{n+1}|*|u_{n}|(x)\leq A2^{i|\gamma|}\inf_{y\in Q_{ik}}Mu_{i},\end{split}

where
M
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. Moreover, by Leibniz formula for differentiation,

s_{ik}\leq A\max_{x\in\tilde{Q}_{ik},|\gamma|\leq m}2^{-i|\gamma|}|D^{\gamma}u_{i}(x)|

and hence by (
5.19
)
s_{ik}\leq AMu_{i}(x)
for all
x\in Q_{ik}
. In this way, we get

s_{i}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}s_{ik}\chi_{ik}(x)\leq AMu_{i}(x).

The estimate (
5.18
) now follows from the Fefferman-Stein maximal theorem for Orlicz spaces (see
[
22
, Corollary 5.4.1]
). This ends the proof of the theorem.

The converse of Theorem
5.3
is also true.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
s>0
,
A
a Young function so that
A
and
\hat{A}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition and suppose that
m>s
is an integer. Let
\left\{a_{ik}\right\}
be a sequence of
C^{m}
-atoms for the cubes
\left\{Q_{ik}\right\}
and let the coefficients
\left\{s_{ik}\right\}
such that
\|\left\{s_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{\infty}\|_{L^{A}(\ell^{q})}<\infty
. Then, the function

u(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{-si}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}s_{ik}a_{ik}(x)

belongs to
F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and there is
C>0
such that

(5.20)

\|u\|_{F_{s}^{A,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\left\{s_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{\infty}\|_{L^{A}(\ell^{q})}=C\bigg\|\left(\sum_{x\in Q_{ik}}|s_{ik}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

The proof follows as in
[
1
, Theorem 4.6.3]
, so we provide some details. Define for
j\in\mathbb{Z}

c_{j}:=\begin{cases}2^{-j(m-s)}\quad&\text{if }j\geq 0\\
2^{js}\quad&\text{if }j\leq-1.\end{cases}

With the help of
[
1
, Lemma 4.6.4 and Lemma 4.6.5]
, there holds

(5.21)

|\Phi*u(x)|\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-sj}Ms_{j}(x)=C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{-j}Ms_{j}(x),

and for
i\geq 1
,

(5.22)

2^{si}|\phi_{i}*u(x)|\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{i}2^{-(i-j)(m-s)}Ms_{j}(x)+A\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}2^{(i-j)s}Ms_{j}(x)=C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{i-j}Ms_{j}(x),

where
M
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. Let
c=\{c_{j}\}_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}
and
Ms(x)=\{Ms_{j}(x)\}_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}
where
Ms_{j}(x)=0
for
j<0
, and let its convolution

(c*Ms(x))(i)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{i-j}Ms_{j}(x).

Since
m>s
, the series
\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}|c_{j}|
converges. Then, by (
5.21
) and (
5.22
), and the Minkowski’s inequality
\|c*Ms(x)\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq C\|c\|_{\ell^{1}}\|Ms(x)\|_{\ell^{q}}
, we get

\begin{split}&\bigg\|\left(\Phi*u(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|2^{is}\phi_{i}*u(x)|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\bigg\|\left(\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}|(c*Ms(x))(i)|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\qquad\qquad=C\bigg\|\|c*Ms(x)\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\bigg\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|c\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\|\|Ms(x)\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})}\|_{L^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

The conclusion follows from the Fefferman-Stein maximal theorem for Orlicz spaces (see
[
22
, Corollary 5.4.1]
).
