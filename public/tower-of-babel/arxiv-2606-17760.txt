Measurement of isolated photon plus two-jet correlations in Pb+Pb and p ​ p pp collisions at 5.02 TeV with ATLAS


Abstract
This paper presents a measurement of photon plus two-jet events in p ​ p pp and Pb+Pb collisions, i.e. events in which the transverse momentum of a single photon is balanced by two distinct jets. The measurement was performed using p ​ p pp data taken in 2017 with an integrated luminosity 260 pb -1 , and Pb+Pb data taken in 2018 with an integrated luminosity 1.72 nb -1 , both at s NN \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}} =5.02 TeV, as recorded by the ATLAS detector. Events with photons in the transverse momentum range 90–180 GeV and at least two anti- k t k_{t} R = 0.2 R=0.2 jets with a p T p_{\mathrm{T}} ¿ 30 GeV are selected, and three observables are measured: x JJ ​ γ {\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma} , A JJ ​ γ \mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma} , and Δ ​ R JJ \Delta R_{\mathrm{JJ}} . These observables characterise the overall energy loss of the multiparton system from medium interactions ( x JJ ​ γ {\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma} ), the relative energy loss between the two colour-charge carriers ( A JJ ​ γ \mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma} ), and medium-induced modifications to their opening angle ( Δ ​ R JJ \Delta R_{\mathrm{JJ}} ). The observables are corrected for uncorrelated combinatoric background contributions using a novel multijet mixing technique, for photon purity, and for detector resolution effects via iterative unfolding. Final results are presented per photon, and the ratio ( I AA I_{\mathrm{AA}} ) is taken between measurements in Pb+Pb and p ​ p pp collisions, for Pb+Pb centrality intervals of 30–80%, 10–30%, and 0–10%. Significant suppression of per photon two-jet yields in all three observables, I AA < 1 I_{\mathrm{AA}}<1 , is observed as a result of parton-medium interactions. The experimental measurements are compared to three different jet quenching models: Jewel , Jetscape , and Lbt .

Introduction

Collisions of ultra-relativistic heavy ions at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) and the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) produce a state of matter called the quark–gluon plasma (QGP) where quarks and gluons exist outside of the colour-neutral configurations that define typical cold matter. Studies of this hot-and-dense state of deconfined partons have revealed many interesting characteristics: collective motion well described by relativistic hydrodynamic flow, sequential suppression of bound quarkonia states, and the suppression of observed final-state products such as jets from hard-scattered partons
[
1
]
. This last phenomenon was first observed at RHIC with the study of large transverse momentum (
p_{\text{T}}
) hadrons
[
2
]
and later at the LHC with fully reconstructed jets
[
3
]
. It is interpreted as the result of the colour-opaque medium interacting with and attenuating the energy of these energetic quarks and gluons produced in the initial collision before the formation of the medium, a process referred to as jet quenching. For a recent review of jet quenching studies in the QGP at both colliders, see Ref.
[
4
]
.

Measurements of jet quenching are a key part of understanding the structure and nature of the QGP. By comparing the kinematics of jets produced in Pb+Pb collisions to the well-measured characteristics of jets produced in
pp
collisions, the induced modifications from the QGP interacting with the developing parton shower can be studied. While many studies of modified jet production have been performed
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
, there are unresolved questions on the exact nature of parton–medium interactions. These include questions on how much energy is lost to interactions with medium partons, how the energy loss depends on parton flavour, and whether there is a minimum separation between colour charges before the medium can resolve them as separate and independent
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

The measurements presented here address these questions by studying events containing an isolated photon with its transverse momentum balanced by at least two distinct jets,
\gamma
+ 2 jets +
X
. As the photon has no colour charge, it sets the scale of the initial hard scattering that produced it and the balancing two-jet system, without any biases on this selection from quenching effects. Photon plus two-jet measurements were previously reported by ATLAS in
pp
collisions at 13
TeV

[
15
]
.
Measurements of photon-tagged inclusive jets, i.e.
\gamma
+ 1 jet +
X
, in
pp
and Pb+Pb collisions, were performed previously by ATLAS
[
16
,
17
]
and CMS
[
18
]
. This analysis presents the first photon plus two-jet measurement in Pb+Pb collisions.

The multijet system can produce one quark and one gluon jet opposite the photon, such that measurements of the jet–jet energy asymmetry probe if different colour charges have different interactions with the medium.
The measurement is also sensitive to fluctuations in the energy loss as the two jets traverse similar regions of QGP, in contrast to the case for back-to-back dijets. Finally, studies as a function of the angle between the jet pair can be used to probe the existence of the colour coherence regime.
Although some estimates predict such coherence effects only at very small angular separations, and there are existing measurements probing that phase space
[
14
,
19
,
20
]
, the new data presented in this paper can provide constraints on possible effects at larger angular separation.

ATLAS detector

The ATLAS experiment
[
21
]
at the LHC is a multipurpose particle detector
with a forward–backward symmetric cylindrical geometry and a near
4\pi
coverage in
solid angle.
It consists of an inner tracking detector surrounded by a thin superconducting solenoid
providing a
2\text{\,}\mathrm{T}
axial magnetic field, electromagnetic and hadron calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer.
The inner tracking detector covers the pseudorapidity range
|\eta|<2.5
.
It consists of silicon pixel, silicon microstrip, and transition radiation tracking detectors.
Lead/liquid-argon (LAr) sampling calorimeters provide electromagnetic (EM) energy measurements
with high granularity.
A steel/scintillator-tile hadron calorimeter covers the central pseudorapidity range (
|\eta|<1.7
).
The endcap and forward regions are instrumented with LAr calorimeters
for both the EM and hadronic energy measurements up to
|\eta|=4.9
.
The muon spectrometer surrounds the calorimeters and is based on
three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets with eight coils each.
The field integral of the toroids ranges between
2.0
and
6.0\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\text{\,}\mathrm{m}

across most of the detector.
The muon spectrometer includes a system of precision tracking chambers and fast detectors for triggering.
Two zero-degree calorimeters (ZDCs) were situated at
\left|\eta\right|>8.3
during Pb+Pb data-taking and is mostly sensitive to spectator neutrons from the fragmenting nuclei in Pb+Pb collisions.

A two-level trigger system is used to select events.
The first-level trigger is implemented in hardware and uses a subset of the detector information
to accept events at a rate below
100\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
.
This is followed by a software-based trigger that
reduces the accepted event rate to
1\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
on average
depending on the data-taking conditions.
An extensive software suite
[
22
]
is used in data simulation, in the reconstruction
and analysis of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and data acquisition
systems of the experiment.

3
Data selection, reconstruction, and simulation

The data samples, physics object reconstruction, and simulation samples used in this measurement are identical to those used in a previous measurement of photon-tagged jet production
[
17
]
, and are briefly summarised here.

Events in data are selected using triggers requiring a reconstructed photon with
p_{\mathrm{T}}
above 35
GeV
(20
GeV
) in
pp
(Pb+Pb) collisions
[
23
,
24
]
. These triggers sample the full luminosity delivered in both 2017
pp
and 2018 Pb+Pb data and are fully efficient for the photon selection used in this analysis.
Events containing multiple Pb+Pb interactions within the same or nearby bunch crossings are rejected using an offline selection based on the correlation between signals measured in the forward calorimeters and the ZDCs.
Photons are reconstructed following the method used previously in Pb+Pb measurements
[
16
,
25
]
. This procedure is the same as that used in
pp
collisions
[
26
]
after an event-by-event estimate and subtraction of the UE contribution to the energy deposited in each calorimeter cell
[
27
]
. Photon candidates must satisfy shower-shape requirements
[
28
]

designed to reject photons from neutral meson decays and other hadrons.
Photon candidates must also be isolated by requiring an upper limit of 3
GeV
on the sum of the transverse energy (after subtracting the contributions from the photon itself and the UE) in calorimeter cells within
\Delta{R}<0.3
from the photon.

Jets are reconstructed following the procedure previously used in Pb+Pb measurements
[
27
,
5
]
. The anti-
k_{t}
algorithm
[
29
,
30
]
with radius parameter
\textit{R}=0.2
is applied to cells in all calorimeter layers combined into logical towers of size
\Delta\eta\times\Delta\phi
=
0.1\times\pi/32
.
The contribution to the energy deposited in towers by the UE is estimated on an event-by-event basis, and the tower kinematics are iteratively updated to subtract the UE contribution that is then re-estimated. The resulting energy response of the jets is corrected using simulation to account for the response of the calorimeter to jets
[
31
]
. Further corrections are applied using
in situ
studies of jets recoiling against photons,
Z
bosons, and jets in other regions of the calorimeter in
pp
collisions
[
16
]
for the absolute response in data.

The Pb+Pb event centrality is defined by the total sum of the transverse energy in the forward calorimeters,
\Sigma{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{FCal}}
. Events in different ranges of
\Sigma{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{FCal}}
are associated with an underlying geometric configuration according to a Monte Carlo (MC) Glauber simulation
[
32
]
using the same event-selection criteria as in previous ATLAS analyses
[
33
]
.
Three centrality intervals are utilised corresponding to the following fractions of the
\Sigma{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{FCal}}
distribution in minimum-bias events: 0–10% (“central” events, with a large nuclear overlap), 10–30%, and 30–80% (“peripheral” events).

Monte Carlo simulations of photon+jet events are used to evaluate the performance of the photon and jet reconstruction and to correct the measured distributions for detector effects. The distributions are thus corrected to the “particle level.”

Particle-level photons and jets are reconstructed before detector simulation. Photons are required to be prompt and to satisfy
|\eta^{\gamma}|<2.37

(excluding the barrel-to-endcap transition region of
1.37<|\eta^{\gamma}|<1.52
)
and
90<p_{\mathrm{T},\gamma}<180

GeV
. Truth isolation for photons is defined as the sum of transverse energy within a cone of
\Delta R<0.4
around the photon being less than 5
GeV
. All final-state particles, excluding neutrinos, muons, and the photon itself, are used to calculate this quantity at the truth level.

Particle-level jets are clustered from all detector-stable particles with a lifetime in the laboratory frame satisfying
c\tau>10
mm, excluding those expected to deposit little or no energy in the calorimeters (i.e., muons and neutrinos). Particle-level jets are required to satisfy
|\eta_{\textrm{jet}}|<2.8
and
p_{\text{T}}>30

GeV
.

For
pp
data, the main MC sample consists of
Pythia
-8
[
34
]
events. These include contributions from both direct and fragmentation photons and are produced using the A14 tune
[
35
]
and the NNPDF 2.3 LO parton distribution function (PDF) set
[
35
]
.
These events are simulated
[
22
]
within ATLAS using
Geant4

[
36
]
and are digitized and reconstructed in a manner identical to that in data. To simulate photon+jet events in Pb+Pb collisions, these
Pythia
-8 events are overlaid at the detector-hit level with a sample of Pb+Pb data events, and the combination is reconstructed as a single event.
These “Pb+Pb data overlay” events are reweighted to match the observed
\Sigma{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{FCal}}
distribution for photon+jet events in Pb+Pb data.

4
Analysis

The signal definition for the measurement presented here requires events having an isolated photon in the range of
90<p_{\mathrm{T},\gamma}<180

GeV
and
|\eta^{\gamma}|<2.37
(excluding the barrel-to-endcap transition region of
1.37<|\eta^{\gamma}|<1.52
), and at least two
\textit{R}=0.2
jets with
p_{\text{T}}>30

GeV
and
|\eta^{\mathrm{jet}}|<2.8
that are in the azimuthal hemisphere opposite to the photon,
\Delta\phi_{\mathrm{jet},\gamma}=\left|\phi^{\mathrm{jet}}-\phi^{\gamma}\right|>\pi/2
. For each event satisfying this initial selection, the photon together with every possible combination of two jets is considered as a set.
For each set, three additional criteria are required: (1) both jets must be separated from the photon by
\Delta R>0.5
, (2) the two jet axes must be separated by
\Delta R_{\mathrm{JJ}}>0.4
, and (3) the azimuthal separation between the two-jet system and the photon must have
\Delta\phi_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma}>7\pi/8
.
Since at tree level a prompt photon plus two partons are expected to have approximately balanced momenta in the transverse plane, this last requirement reduces the rate of the combinatorial contributions while minimally impacting the number of signal events.
With these requirements, approximately 144,000 and 34,000 events are selected in
pp
and Pb+Pb data sets, respectively.
Then initial distributions are constructed for the three new observables
{\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma}
,
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma}
, and
\Delta R_{\mathrm{JJ}}
as defined in Eqs.
1
-
3
.

In the candidate Pb+Pb events, a subset of the selected jets do not arise from the same hard scattering as the photon, but rather from an unrelated nucleon–nucleon scattering, or from jets that are reconstructed from localised fluctuations of the UE. These background jets are produced predominantly at low
p_{\text{T}}
and isotropically relative to the photon direction. Such background jets can influence the observables by forming a signal-jet + background-jet pair, or even background-jet + background-jet pair.
The influence of these jets on the observables is removed statistically using a
mixed-event subtraction
procedure.
A simpler form of this procedure was previously used for photon + inclusive jet analyses
[
16
,
17
]
.
Due to the complexity of multiple jets contributing to the observables, this technique was developed further.

For each signal event (consisting of a photon and at least two jets), two minimum-bias
Pb+Pb data events are chosen that have similar centrality and second-order event plane angle
[
37
]
as the signal event, and thus have on average the same distribution of background jets. The distributions are then constructed using three different combinations of the photon and jets in the signal event and the jets in the minimum-bias events, as follows:

1.

First, the contribution from background-jet + background-jet pairs that occur in data must be accounted for. The observable of choice is constructed using the photon from the signal event in combination with all possible jet pairs in the first minimum-bias event.

2.

The second step accounts for signal-jet and background-jet combinations. For each individual jet satisfying selections in the signal event, the observable is constructed using the photon and that one jet in combination with all the jets in the first minimum-bias event.
However, since the jet from the signal event may itself be a background jet, this estimated background will generally include background-jet (from the signal event) and background-jet (from the first minimum bias event) pairs. Since these background–background pairs are from different minimum-bias events, this contribution is not present in data.

3.

The third step accounts for the potential over-subtraction described in the second step. Here, the observable of choice is constructed from the photon in the signal event, each jet in the first minimum-bias event satisfying selections, and each jet in the second minimum-bias event satisfying selections. If the first minimum-bias event contains two jets, and the second minimum-bias event contains three jets, this results in six total pairs, assuming no pair is excluded by jet–jet selections like the
\Delta\textit{R}>0.4
selection. This contribution accounts for the background-jet plus background-jet pairs from different events that are introduced by the second step.

While this procedure is described for a single minimum-bias-event pair per signal event, the average of ten minimum-bias-event pairs per signal event was employed in the analysis to improve statistical precision. For each observable, the per-photon yields from steps one and two are subtracted from the initial yields, and the yields from step three are added (to correct for the background over-subtraction), to produce a set of combinatorial-background-subtracted distributions.
The procedure is tested using the
Pythia
-8 and Pb+Pb data overlay events (described in Sec.
3
), where reconstructed jets matched to a
Pythia
-8 particle-level jet are taken to be the signal jets, and the other jets in the event, i.e. those in the Pb+Pb data event, are the background jets.
Complications such as the potential merging of jets at reconstruction level results in some residual non-closure of the background procedure, which is taken as a systematic uncertainty in the measurement (discussed in Sec.
5
). The observed background correction in Pb+Pb data is of comparable magnitude to the signal in the central events and is greater at large
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma}
, large
\Delta R_{\mathrm{JJ}}
, and the tails of the
{\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma}
distribution. In these regions the non-closure and corresponding systematic uncertainty can be as large as 20%.

An additional background arises from photons produced in decays of neutral meson and other hadrons. The purity of the selected photons is estimated following the data-driven, double-sideband method used in ATLAS photon measurements
[
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
]
. The obtained purity ranges from
80
–
95
% depending on the collision system and
p_{\mathrm{T},\gamma}
. The contribution to the jet–jet combinations from this background is estimated in data and statistically subtracted using the same methods applied in previous measurements
[
16
,
25
,
17
]
. The method is briefly summarised here. An inverted set of photon selection requirements designed to enhance the contribution from background is used to select events, and the analysis is repeated, including the combinatorial background subtraction. The resulting jet–jet yields in these background events are scaled according to the estimated photon purity and are statistically subtracted from the yields obtained with the nominal photon selection.

An unfolding procedure is applied to the data to correct for the kinematic-bin migration introduced by detector resolution and UE effects and for the finite reconstruction and selection efficiency. This correction is performed using the iterative Bayesian method
[
42
]
in the
RooUnfold

[
43
]
software package. The simulation samples described in Sec.
3
are used to produce response matrices in
pp
and Pb+Pb collisions.
The response matrices are observed to be predominantly diagonal and well-behaved, demonstrating limited migration between neighbouring bins.
The simulation samples are also used to test the unfolding procedure by using half of the simulation to construct response matrices while treating the other half as data to be unfolded and compared with particle level kinematics. For each of the three observables, the unfolding is performed in three dimensions: the given observable (for example,
{\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma}
),
p_{\mathrm{T},\gamma}
, and
p_{\mathrm{T},2}
. The latter two variables are included since the response along the observable axis is found to be particularly dependent on their values. The kinematic distributions are constructed in a wider range than the region in which the final results are reported, to allow for the unfolding to properly account for the migration into and out of the measurement region.
Before the unfolding procedure is applied, the simulated events are first reweighted to better describe the distributions as observed in data.
The weights are applied at the particle-level, as a function of
p_{\mathrm{T},\gamma}
and
p_{\mathrm{T},2}
, and the specific observable, on a photon-jet pair basis.
The number of iterations is chosen by minimizing the quadrature sum of the statistical uncertainties with the change in the distributions from the previous iteration, and is typically two or three depending on the observable and event class.

5
Systematic Uncertainties

The sources of systematic uncertainty in this measurement are those associated with the measurement of the photons and the jets, the background subtraction procedure, and the unfolding. The uncertainties are typically evaluated by repeating the full analysis chain with a given systematic variation, which may result in, e.g., a different response matrix for the unfolding or a different reconstructed-level distribution.

The photon- and jet-related sources of uncertainty are the same as those used in a previous measurement of photon-tagged jet production in Pb+Pb and
pp
collisions
[
17
]
and are only summarised here. The photon-related uncertainties include those on the photon energy scale and resolution
[
44
]
, the application of identification and isolation requirements
[
28
]
, and the evaluation of the photon purity
[
16
]
. The jet-related uncertainties include the baseline uncertainties in the energy scale and resolution for
R=0.4
jets used for measurements in 13
TeV

pp
collisions
[
31
]
, with additional sources
[
6
]
accounting for the modified jet reconstruction procedure used in a heavy-ion environment, the smaller jet parameter
R=0.2
, the data-taking conditions in
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{{}_{NN}}}}
= 5.02
TeV
running, the quark/gluon composition of photon plus two-jet events at this collision energy, and the potential impact of jet quenching on the calorimeter response to jets.

The uncertainty associated with the mixed-event background subtraction procedure, present only for Pb+Pb collisions, is evaluated using input from the closure test of the procedure in the Pb+Pb data overlay simulation described in Sec.
4
. For each of the three observables, the differences between the background-subtracted distribution and the reconstructed-level signal distribution in simulation are applied to the data bin-by-bin. The unfolding is then performed on these modified input distributions.

The uncertainty associated with the unfolding procedure has three components. The first is the sensitivity to the initial distributions used in the iterative unfolding, which is evaluated by using the simulation samples without reweighting to the data. Second, the reconstructed jet
p_{\text{T}}
threshold is raised from 30
GeV
to 35
GeV
, but still unfolded to the original kinematic range. Such a variation was used in Ref.
[
6
]
as a check against residual background jet contamination at low jet
p_{\text{T}}
. Finally, the impact of finite statistical uncertainty in the simulation sample is evaluated with a pseudo-data re-sampling technique implemented in the
RooUnfold
package
[
43
]
. This last component is much smaller than the statistical uncertainties in the data.

For the measurements in Pb+Pb data, the total uncertainties for each observable are typically 20–40%, but reach as high as 100% in the low-yield tails. The photon isolation and purity, the combinatorial background subtraction, and the unfolding uncertainties have similar values and are co-dominant. For the
pp
measurement, the total uncertainties are typically 5–10%, with the jet energy scale and the unfolding uncertainties (which include an observed unfolding non-closure from a simulation study) being dominant. Variations for sources common to the Pb+Pb and
pp
data are considered together when evaluating
I_{\mathrm{AA}}
, the ratio of distributions in these systems. This results in a modest reduction of the uncertainties in
I_{\mathrm{AA}}
compared with the relative uncertainties in the
pp
or Pb+Pb distributions alone.

The experimental results in the Pb+Pb
0–10% centrality interval are compared with three theoretical jet quenching models referred to as

The
Jewel
MC generator modifies the parton shower evolution via interactions with a medium, modelled as a two-dimensional outwardly expanding QGP.
Jewel
does not incorporate colour-coherence effects.
Version 2.2.0 of
Jewel
is publicly available following the initial release detailed in Ref.
[
51
]
.

Jewel
was run using the photon+jet hard-scattering process
[
52
]
that includes only direct (leading-order) photon production.
The photon and jet kinematic selections used in the data analysis are applied and the observables are calculated.

Jetscape
(Jet Energy-loss Tomography with a Statistically and Computationally Advanced Program Envelope) is a framework that enables different modules to be plugged in for modelling the initial collision geometry, different jet kinematics, and different physics processes of jet quenching
[
53
]

Jetscape
authors have provided calculation results for

Pythia
-8) and 0–10% Pb+Pb collisions using the plug-in modules
Matter
(Modular All Twist
Transverse-scattering Elastic-drag and Radiation) +
Lbt
(Linearized Boltzmann Transport) with the
Jetscape
v3.5 AA22 tune, identical to that used in Refs.
[
54
,
55
,
53
]
.
The
Matter
module handles the medium interactions at high virtuality (above some
Q_{0}
parameter) where modified coherence effects diminish medium-induced radiation, and a lower virtuality phase is modelled using the Linear Boltzmann Transport model (
Lbt
)
[
56
]
, where parton splittings are fully resolved by the medium as they undergo multiple-scattering-induced energy loss.

Lbt
calculations were also run separately (without the
Matter
module) using
MadGraph

[
45
]
for the
pp
baseline and for initial hard scatterings as input to the QGP medium propagation.

7
Conclusion

This paper presents results of a measurement by ATLAS of photon plus two-jet observables in
pp
and Pb+Pb collisions at 5.02
TeV
collected in 2017 and 2018, respectively, to study the impact of medium-parton interactions on nearby quark and gluon probes of the QGP. Photons are selected with
90<p_{\mathrm{T},\gamma}<180

GeV
and
R=0.2
jets are selected with
p_{\text{T}}>30

GeV
.
The unfolded distributions are measured for three observables,
{\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma}
,
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{JJ}\gamma}
, and
\Delta R_{\mathrm{JJ}}
, in
pp
data and three Pb+Pb centrality intervals (30–80%, 10–30%, and 0–10%). Ratios of the distributions in each Pb+Pb centrality interval to those in
pp
collisions (
I_{\mathrm{AA}}
) are also measured. Significant suppression (
I_{\mathrm{AA}}<1
) is observed in all cases, with the suppression larger in the most central relative to the most peripheral centrality intervals.
Monte Carlo calculations from the jet quenching models
Jewel
,
Jetscape
, and
Lbt
are compared with the experimental data.
These measurements provide new constraints on the microscopic processes of energy loss in the QGP, and further tests of the underlying assumptions of theoretical models will help refine their description of these processes.
