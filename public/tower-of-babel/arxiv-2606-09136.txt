1.
Introduction

This paper studies the Fourier expansion of the Siegel–Eisenstein series
of degree
n=2m
and boundary weight
k=m+1=(n+2)/2
, where
m\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
Throughout the paper,
we put
e(x)=\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}x)
.
Here and below,

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z)
denotes the value at
s=0
of the Eisenstein series

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,s)
defined in Section
2
,
whenever this value is defined.
We write

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z)=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}A_{T}^{(n)}(Y)e(\tr(TX)),\qquad Z=X+\sqrt{-1}Y\in\mathbb{H}_{n}.

The aim is to determine the coefficient functions
A_{T}^{(n)}(Y)
,
including the non-holomorphic dependence on
Y
which may occur at this
weight.

The holomorphy of Siegel–Eisenstein series in the small weight range was
clarified by Shimura
[
13
]
and Weissauer
[
15
]
.
For even weights
k
in the range
k\geq(n+1)/2
, the boundary
phenomena occur at

k=\frac{n+2}{2}\equiv 2\pmod{4},\qquad k=\frac{n+3}{2}\equiv 2\pmod{4}.

The second case is nearly holomorphic.
The present paper treats the first case.

The first non-holomorphic example in this direction occurs in degree
2
,
where
n=2
and
k=2
.
Nagaoka
[
10
]
and Kohnen
[
7
]
described the Fourier expansion of
E_{2}^{(2)}(Z,0)
.
Haruki
[
3
]
later computed this expansion by using the general
Fourier expansion formula of Mizumoto
[
9
]
.
We use Mizumoto’s formula as the starting point.

The constant term can be calculated uniformly for all odd
m\geq 3
.
Putting
\epsilon_{m}=(-1)^{(m+1)/2}
,
Theorem
4.1
gives

A_{0}^{(2m)}(Y)=1+\frac{(\epsilon_{m}-1)\pi^{m+1}}{\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m)\sqrt{\det Y}}\left(\frac{\zeta(m)}{2^{m+1}m!}+\frac{(m-2)!\zeta(m-1)}{(2m)!}\zeta_{1}^{(2m)}(Y,m-1)\right),

where
\zeta_{1}^{(2m)}(Y,m-1)
is understood by meromorphic continuation.
Thus
A_{0}^{(2m)}(Y)=1
if
m\equiv 3\pmod{4}
,
whereas the constant term has non-trivial
Y
-dependence if

m\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
The degree-six case
m=3
contains two additional middle-rank terms; they
cancel, as shown in
Proposition
A.1
.

For positive semi-definite non-zero indices,
Proposition
5.3
expresses

A_{T}^{(n)}(Y)
as a finite sum over

\nu\in\mathcal{J}^{(n)}_{\lambda}
, where
\lambda=\mathrm{rank}(T)
.
The exceptional terms come from the case
(\lambda,\nu)=(n-2,n)

and from the square-discriminant pole of the non-degenerate Siegel series.
For indices
T\not\geq 0
,
Proposition
5.5
gives the possible
contributing terms.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2,
we recall notation for Siegel modular forms and Eisenstein series.
In Section 3,
we recall the Koecher–Maass zeta function,
Siegel series,
confluent hypergeometric functions,
and the Fourier expansion formula due to Mizumoto
[
9
]
.
In Section 4,
we calculate the constant term for all odd
m\geq 3
.
In Section 5,
we calculate the non-zero index terms in the main case
m\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
In Section 6,
we assemble the results into the main theorem.
The appendix contains the middle-rank cancellation in degree
6
.

2.
Siegel Modular Forms and Eisenstein Series

Let
\mathbb{H}_{n}
be the Siegel upper half space of degree
n
, that is,

\mathbb{H}_{n}=\left\{Z\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\,\middle|\,Z={}^{t}\!Z=X+\sqrt{-1}Y,\ X\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\ Y\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathbb{R})_{>0}\right\}.

We put

\Gamma_{n}=\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})=\left\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})\,\middle|\,^{t}\!gJ_{n}g=J_{n}\right\},

where
J_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0_{n}&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0_{n}\end{pmatrix}
.

For

g=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{R}),

and for a function
F
on
\mathbb{H}_{n}
,
we define the slash operator of weight
k
by

(F|_{k}g)(Z)=\det(CZ+D)^{-k}F\left((AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1}\right).

A holomorphic function
F
on
\mathbb{H}_{n}
is called a Siegel modular form of weight
k
with respect to
\Gamma_{n}
if

F|_{k}g=F
for all
g\in\Gamma_{n}
.

Let

\Gamma_{n,\infty}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
0_{n,n}&*\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{n}\right\}.

For a positive even integer
k
,
we define the Siegel–Eisenstein series of degree
n
and weight
k
by

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,s)=\det(\mathrm{Im}Z)^{s}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{n,\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{n}}\det(CZ+D)^{-k}\left|\det(CZ+D)\right|^{-2s},

where

g=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}
.
This series converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>(n+1-k)/2

and has meromorphic continuation to the whole
s
-plane.
Whenever the value at
s=0
is defined,
we write

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z)=E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,0).

We recall the following result of Shimura
[
13
]
and Weissauer
[
15
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
k
be a positive even integer.
Suppose that
k\geq(n+1)/2
.
Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

The series
E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,s)
is holomorphic in
s
at
s=0
.

(2)

If neither

k=(n+2)/2\equiv 2\pmod{4}

nor

k=(n+3)/2\equiv 2\pmod{4}

holds,
then
E_{k}^{(n)}(Z)
is a holomorphic modular form of weight
k
.

(3)

If

k=(n+3)/2\equiv 2\pmod{4}
,
then
E_{k}^{(n)}(Z)
is a nearly holomorphic modular form of weight
k
.

More precisely,
there are holomorphic functions

p_{1}
and
p_{2}
on
\mathbb{H}_{n}
such that

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z)=\Delta\left\{p_{1}(Z)+p_{2}(Z)\log\det(\mathrm{Im}Z)\right\},

where

\Delta=\det\left(\frac{1}{2}(1+\delta_{ij})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{ij}}\right).

These functions may be chosen so that

(a)

\Delta p_{1}
has a Fourier expansion with rational coefficients.

(b)

\Delta p_{2}=0
.

(c)

\pi^{n}p_{2}
is a modular form of weight
(n-1)/2

with rational Fourier coefficients.

The case
k=\dfrac{n+2}{2}\equiv 2\pmod{4}
is the other exceptional case.
In degree
2
,
this is the case of
E_{2}^{(2)}(Z,0)
,
whose Fourier expansion was studied by Kohnen
[
7
]
and Nagaoka
[
10
]
.
Afterwards,
Haruki
[
3
]
gave a more direct approach to the Fourier expansion of
E_{2}^{(2)}(Z,s)
by using the Fourier expansion due to Mizumoto
[
9
]
.

We shall study this exceptional case by using Mizumoto’s Fourier expansion.
For later use,
we first recall the relevant part of his Fourier expansion and fix notation.

3.
Auxiliary Functions and the Fourier Expansion Formula of Mizumoto

In this section,
we review the Fourier expansion of the Siegel–Eisenstein series given by Mizumoto
[
9
]
,
which plays a central role
in our analysis.
We first recall the definitions of the Koecher–Maass zeta function,
the Siegel series,
and the confluent hypergeometric functions.

3.1.
Koecher–Maass Zeta Function

Let
M_{n,\nu}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}\subset M_{n,\nu}(\mathbb{Z})
denote the subset
consisting of primitive matrices.
Here a matrix is called primitive if the cokernel of the induced map

\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is torsion-free.
For an integer
1\leq\nu\leq n
and a positive definite symmetric matrix

g\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathbb{R})_{>0}
,
we define the Koecher–Maass zeta function by

\zeta_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s)=\sum_{a\in M_{n,\nu}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}/\mathrm{GL}_{\nu}(\mathbb{Z})}\det(g[a])^{-s},

where
g[a]={}^{t}\!aga
.
We also recall the completed Riemann zeta function

\xi(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s).

Using these functions,
we define the completed Koecher–Maass zeta function by

\Xi_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s)=2\varepsilon_{\nu}(s)\varepsilon_{\nu}\left(\frac{n}{2}-s\right)R_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s),

where

R_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s)=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\xi(2s-j)\right)\zeta_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s),

and

\varepsilon_{\nu}(s)=\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\left(s-\frac{j}{2}\right).

The Koecher–Maass zeta series
\zeta_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s)
converges
absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>n/2
.

The analytic continuation and the functional equation of

\Xi_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s)
were established by Koecher
[
6
]

and Maass
[
8
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The function
\Xi_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s)
extends to an entire function of
s
.
Moreover,
it satisfies the functional equation

\det(g)^{\nu/4}\Xi_{\nu}^{(n)}\left(g,\frac{n}{2}-s\right)=\det(g^{-1})^{\nu/4}\Xi_{\nu}^{(n)}(g^{-1},s).

Remark 3.2

(Adelic interpretation)
.

Let
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, and let
P_{\nu}\subset G
be the standard parabolic
subgroup of type
(\nu,n-\nu)
.
Put
e_{\nu}={}^{t}(1_{\nu},0)
.
Then
G(\mathbb{Q})/P_{\nu}(\mathbb{Q})
parametrizes rational
\nu
-planes in

\mathbb{Q}^{n}
.
Moreover, the map
\gamma\mapsto\gamma e_{\nu}
induces an
identification

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})/(P_{\nu}(\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}))\simeq M_{n,\nu}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}/\mathrm{GL}_{\nu}(\mathbb{Z}).

Choose
a_{\infty}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
such that

g={}^{t}a_{\infty}a_{\infty}
, and put

H_{\nu}(a_{\infty})=\det({}^{t}(a_{\infty}e_{\nu})(a_{\infty}e_{\nu})).

Let
f_{\nu,s}
be the standard spherical section of the degenerate
principal series induced from
P_{\nu}(\mathbb{A})
.
Thus
f_{\nu,s}
is right invariant under
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})

at the finite places and under
\mathrm{O}(n)
at the real place, and
is normalized by

f_{\nu,s}((1_{f},a_{\infty}))=H_{\nu}(a_{\infty})^{-s}.

The associated Eisenstein series is

E_{\nu}(a,s)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})/P_{\nu}(\mathbb{Q})}f_{\nu,s}(a\gamma).

Evaluating at
a=(1_{f},a_{\infty})
, we obtain

E_{\nu}((1_{f},a_{\infty}),s)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})/(P_{\nu}(\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}))}H_{\nu}(a_{\infty}\gamma)^{-s}=\zeta_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s),

since

H_{\nu}(a_{\infty}\gamma)=\det(g[\gamma e_{\nu}])
.
Hence the Koecher–Maass zeta function is the classical realization of a
spherical degenerate Eisenstein series on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A})
.

Under this identification, the normalizing factor used in
\Xi_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s)

is the standard one attached to the spherical degenerate Eisenstein series.
Thus the usual functional equation of the normalized Eisenstein series,
associated with the Weyl element exchanging the two blocks of type
(\nu,n-\nu)
,
specializes to the functional equation of
\Xi_{\nu}^{(n)}(g,s)
.
\diamond

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume that
n=2m
.
For
1\leq\nu\leq 2m
,
put
\tau(\nu)=(2m+1-\nu)/2
.
Then the function
\zeta_{\nu}^{(2m)}\left(g,2s+\tau(\nu)\right)

has at most a simple pole at
s=0
if
1\leq\nu\leq m
,
and is holomorphic at
s=0
if
m+1\leq\nu\leq 2m
.

Proof.

By the definition of the completed Koecher–Maass zeta function,
we have

\Xi_{\nu}^{(2m)}(g,u)=2\varepsilon_{\nu}(u)\varepsilon_{\nu}(m-u)\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\xi(2u-j)\zeta_{\nu}^{(2m)}(g,u).

Put
u=2s+\tau(\nu)=2s+(2m+1-\nu)/2
.

The factor
\varepsilon_{\nu}(m-u)=\varepsilon_{\nu}\left((\nu-1)/2-2s\right)

always has a simple zero at
s=0
,
and the factor
\varepsilon_{\nu}(u)=\varepsilon_{\nu}\left(2s+(2m+1-\nu)/2\right)

has a zero at
s=0
if and only if

\frac{2m+1-\nu}{2}=\frac{a}{2}

for some
0\leq a\leq\nu-1
.
This occurs exactly when
\nu\geq m+1
.
In that case the zero is simple.

Now consider

\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\xi(2u-j)=\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\xi(4s+2m+1-\nu-j).

If
1\leq\nu\leq m
,
then
2m+1-\nu-j\geq 2m+2-2\nu\geq 2
.
Hence all factors are holomorphic and non-zero at
s=0
.

If
m+1\leq\nu\leq 2m
,
then the product contains exactly the two singular
factors
\xi(1+4s)
and
\xi(4s)
.
Thus the product of the
\xi
-factors has a pole of order
2
at
s=0
.

Combining these observations,
the stated holomorphy and pole assertions follow.
∎

The following residue formula is due to Arakawa
[
1
]
.
We use it in the form recorded by Nagaoka
[
11
, Proposition 3.7]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M
and
\nu
be positive integers with
M\geq 2\nu-1
.
Let
g\in\mathbb{S}_{M}(\mathbb{R})_{>0}
.
For
1\leq\mu\leq\nu-1
,
put

c_{M,\nu,\mu}=-\frac{\varepsilon_{\nu\mu}\,\varepsilon_{\nu}\left(\dfrac{M-\mu}{2}\right)v(\nu-\mu)}{2\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(\dfrac{\nu}{2}\right)\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(\dfrac{M-\nu}{2}\right)},

where

\varepsilon_{\nu\mu}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq a\leq\nu-1\\
a\neq\mu\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\mu-a}{2}\right),\qquad v(a)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{j=2}^{a}\xi(j),&a\geq 2,\\[8.00003pt]
1,&a=1.\end{cases}

Then

\Xi_{\nu}^{(M)}\left(g,\frac{\mu}{2}\right)=c_{M,\nu,\mu}\Xi_{\mu}^{(M)}\left(g,\frac{\nu}{2}\right),

and

\Xi_{\nu}^{(M)}\left(g,\frac{M-\mu}{2}\right)=c_{M,\nu,\mu}\det(g)^{-\nu/2}\Xi_{\mu}^{(M)}\left(g^{-1},\frac{\nu}{2}\right).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
S\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathbb{R})_{>0}
.
For
1\leq r\leq n-1
,
we have

\zeta_{r}^{(n)}(S,u)=\det(S)^{-u}\zeta_{n-r}^{(n)}(S^{-1},u).

This identity initially holds in the region of absolute convergence
and extends to all
u\in\mathbb{C}
by meromorphic continuation.

Proof.

This is the primitive version of the complementary identity of
Arakawa
[
1
, (2.13)]
.
In the region of absolute convergence,
a primitive rank
r
sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is sent bijectively to its primitive
orthogonal complement of rank
n-r
.
If
A\in M_{n,r}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}
represents the first sublattice and

A^{\ast}\in M_{n,n-r}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}
represents the complement, then

{}^{t}\!AA^{\ast}=0
, and we may easily verify that

\det({}^{t}\!ASA)=\det(S)\det({}^{t}\!A^{\ast}S^{-1}A^{\ast}).

Hence

\displaystyle\zeta_{r}^{(n)}(S,u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\in M_{n,r}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}/\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})}\det({}^{t}\!ASA)^{-u}

\displaystyle=\det(S)^{-u}\sum_{A^{\ast}\in M_{n,n-r}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}/\mathrm{GL}_{n-r}(\mathbb{Z})}\det({}^{t}\!A^{\ast}S^{-1}A^{\ast})^{-u}

\displaystyle=\det(S)^{-u}\zeta_{n-r}^{(n)}(S^{-1},u).

Meromorphic continuation gives the identity for all
u\in\mathbb{C}
.
∎

3.2.
Siegel Series

For a rational symmetric matrix
T\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}(\mathbb{Q})
,
the Siegel series

S_{\nu}(T,t)
is defined by

S_{\nu}(T,t)=\sum_{R\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}(\mathbb{Q})/\mathbb{S}_{\nu}(\mathbb{Z})}d(R)^{-t}e(\tr(TR)).

Here
d(R)
denotes the absolute value of the determinant of a denominator
matrix of
R
.
More precisely,
write
R=C^{-1}D
,
where
C,D\in M_{\nu}(\mathbb{Z})
form a
coprime symmetric pair,
that is,

\det C\neq 0
,
C{}^{t}D=D{}^{t}C
,
and
C
and
D
are right coprime,
meaning that there exist
U,V\in M_{\nu}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

CU+DV=I_{\nu}
.
Then
d(R)=|\det C|
.

For a non-degenerate symmetric matrix
h\in\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Q})
of even degree,
we use the discriminant convention

\mathrm{disc}(h)=(-1)^{\lambda/2}\det h\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}.

We also use the same notation for its square class in

\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
.
When we say that the discriminant of
h
is a square,
we mean that
\mathrm{disc}(h)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
.
The corresponding quadratic character is the Kronecker character associated
with this square class.

The following proposition follows from the results of
Böcherer
[
2
]
and Kitaoka
[
5
]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

The following assertions hold.

(1)

Let
T\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}(\mathbb{Q})
be non-degenerate.
Then there exists a family
(F_{p}(T,X))_{p}
of polynomials,
indexed by primes
p
,
such that
F_{p}(T,X)=1
for all but finitely many
p
,
and

S_{\nu}(T,s)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\zeta(s)^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{\nu/2}\zeta(2s-2j)^{-1}L\left(s-\frac{\nu}{2},\chi_{T}\right)\prod_{p}F_{p}(T,p^{-s}),&\text{if $\nu$ is even},\\[11.99998pt]
\displaystyle\zeta(s)^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{(\nu-1)/2}\zeta(2s-2j)^{-1}\prod_{p}F_{p}(T,p^{-s}),&\text{if $\nu$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Here
\chi_{T}
denotes the quadratic character attached to
\mathrm{disc}(T)=(-1)^{\nu/2}\det T
in the convention above.

(2)

Let
h\in\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Q})
and let
\lambda<\nu
.
Then

S_{\nu}(\mathrm{diag}(h,0_{\nu-\lambda}),s)=\mathcal{Z}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)S_{\lambda}(h,s-\nu+\lambda),

where

\mathcal{Z}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)=\frac{\zeta(s+\lambda-\nu)}{\zeta(s)}\prod_{j=1}^{\nu-\lambda}\frac{\zeta(2s-\nu-j)}{\zeta(2s-2j)}.

In particular,
we have

S_{\nu}(0_{\nu},s)=\frac{\zeta(s-\nu)}{\zeta(s)}\prod_{j=1}^{\nu}\frac{\zeta(2s-\nu-j)}{\zeta(2s-2j)}.

The local Siegel polynomials
F_{p}(T,X)
were subsequently calculated explicitly by Katsurada
[
4
]
.

3.3.
Confluent Hypergeometric Function

We now recall the confluent hypergeometric functions on tube domains,
following Shimura
[
12
]
.
We put

\kappa(\lambda)=(\lambda+1)/2.

For
g\in\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})_{>0}
,

h\in\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})
,
and

(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
define

\eta_{\lambda}(g,h;\alpha,\beta)=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})\\
x\pm h>0\end{subarray}}e^{-\tr(gx)}\det(x+h)^{\alpha-\kappa(\lambda)}\det(x-h)^{\beta-\kappa(\lambda)}\,dx,

where

dx=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\lambda}dx_{ij}
.
This integral converges for
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)>\kappa(\lambda)-1
,

\mathrm{Re}(\beta)>\lambda
.
Following the normalization used by Shimura,
we set

\eta_{\lambda}^{\ast}(g,h;\alpha,\beta)=\det(g)^{\alpha+\beta-\kappa(\lambda)}\eta_{\lambda}(g,h;\alpha,\beta).

The function
\eta_{\lambda}^{\ast}(g,h;\alpha,\beta)
admits meromorphic
continuation in
(\alpha,\beta)
.

3.4.
Fourier Expansion of Siegel–Eisenstein Series

We denote by
\Lambda_{n}
the set of half-integral symmetric matrices of degree

n
:

\Lambda_{n}=\left\{T=(t_{ij})\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\;\middle|\;t_{ii}\in\mathbb{Z},\ 2t_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for }i<j\right\}.

We also put

\Lambda_{n}^{\ast}=\{T\in\Lambda_{n}\mid\det T\neq 0\},\qquad\Lambda_{n}^{(r)}=\{T\in\Lambda_{n}\mid\mathrm{rank}T=r\}.

For a symmetric matrix
T
and an appropriate matrix
a
,
we use the shorthand
T[a]={}^{t}\!aTa
.
Finally,
we put

\Gamma_{\nu}(s)=\pi^{\nu(\nu-1)/4}\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\Gamma\left(s-\frac{j}{2}\right).

We now state the explicit Fourier expansion formula due to Mizumoto
for the Siegel–Eisenstein series.

Proposition 3.7

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.8]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

k\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)>n
.
Then the Siegel–Eisenstein series admits the Fourier expansion

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,s)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}\sum_{\lambda=0}^{\nu}F_{\nu,\lambda}^{(n)}(Z,s).

For
\nu=0,\ldots,n
,
setting
\kappa(\nu)=(\nu+1)/2
,
we have

\displaystyle F_{\nu,0}^{(n)}(Z,s)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k\nu/2}2^{\nu}\pi^{\nu\kappa(\nu)}\frac{\Gamma_{\nu}(2s+k-\kappa(\nu))}{\Gamma_{\nu}(s)\Gamma_{\nu}(s+k)}S_{\nu}(0,2s+k)\det(Y)^{s}\zeta_{\nu}^{(n)}\left(2Y,2s+k-\kappa(\nu)\right).

For
1\leq\lambda\leq\nu\leq n
,
we have

F_{\nu,\lambda}^{(n)}(Z,s)=\sum_{h\in\Lambda_{\lambda}^{\ast}}\sum_{r\in M_{n,\lambda}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}/\mathrm{GL}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{Z})}b_{\nu,\lambda}^{(n)}\bigl(h[{}^{t}r],Y,s\bigr)e\bigl(\tr(h[{}^{t}r]X)\bigr),

where

\displaystyle b_{\nu,\lambda}^{(n)}(h[{}^{t}r],Y,s)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k\nu/2}2^{\nu}\pi^{\nu\kappa(\nu)+\lambda(\nu-\lambda)/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\Gamma_{\nu-\lambda}(2s+k-\kappa(\nu))}{\Gamma_{\nu}(s)\Gamma_{\nu}(s+k)}S_{\nu}\bigl(\mathrm{diag}(h,0_{\nu-\lambda}),2s+k\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\times\det(Y)^{s}\det(2Y[r])^{\kappa(\nu)-k-2s}

\displaystyle\quad\times\eta_{\lambda}^{\ast}\left(2Y[r],\pi h;s+k+\frac{\lambda-\nu}{2},s+\frac{\lambda-\nu}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\zeta_{\nu-\lambda}^{(n-\lambda)}\left(2g(Y,u_{r}),2s+k-\kappa(\nu)\right).

Here
u_{r}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is chosen so that its first
\lambda

columns are given by
r
.
Writing
u_{r}=(r\ r_{1})
with

r_{1}\in M_{n,n-\lambda}(\mathbb{Z})
,
the term
g(Y,u_{r})
is the Schur complement of
Y[r]
in
Y[u_{r}]
:

g(Y,u_{r})=Y[r_{1}]-{}^{t}r_{1}Yr\,(Y[r])^{-1}{}^{t}rYr_{1}.

Let

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,s)=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}A_{T}^{(n)}(Y,s)e(\tr(TX))

be the Fourier expansion of
E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,s)
,
where
Z=X+\sqrt{-1}Y
.
We shall calculate these coefficients at
s=0
,
and we put

A_{T}^{(n)}(Y)=A_{T}^{(n)}(Y,0).

We note that the coefficient
A_{T}^{(n)}(Y,s)
is holomorphic at
s=0
.

Remark 3.8

(Adelic interpretation of Mizumoto’s expansion)
.

Mizumoto’s formula can be regarded as the classical expression of the
Fourier expansion of a Siegel Eisenstein series on the adelic symplectic
group.
Let
G=\mathrm{Sp}_{n}
, and let
P=MN\subset G
be the Siegel parabolic
subgroup.
Thus
M\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, and

N=\left\{n(U)=\begin{pmatrix}1_{n}&U\\
0&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}\;\middle|\;U={}^{t}\!U\right\}.

Let
K_{f}=\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
.
We take the standard
K_{f}
-spherical section at the finite places and
the archimedean section of weight
k
.
The associated adelic Eisenstein series is

E(g,s)=\sum_{\gamma\in P(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{Q})}f_{k,s}(\gamma g).

We normalize
f_{k,s}
so that

E(g_{Z},s)=E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,s),\qquad g_{Z}=(g_{\infty}(Z),1_{f}),

where
g_{\infty}(Z)\in\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
sends

\sqrt{-1}\cdot 1_{n}
to
Z=X+\sqrt{-1}Y
.

For
T\in\Lambda_{n}
, define a character of
N(\mathbb{A})
by

\psi_{T}(n(U))=\psi(\tr(TU)),

where
\psi
is the standard additive character of
\mathbb{A}/\mathbb{Q}
.
With the self-dual Haar measure attached to
\psi
, the adelic

T
-th Fourier coefficient is

E_{T}(g,s)=\int_{N(\mathbb{Q})\backslash N(\mathbb{A})}E(n(U)g,s)\psi_{T}(n(U))^{-1}\,dU.

Evaluating at
g=g_{Z}
, this coefficient has the classical form

E_{T}(g_{Z},s)=A_{T}^{(n)}(Y,s)e(\tr(TX)).

In the region of absolute convergence, we insert the Eisenstein series
into this integral and unfold.
The unfolding is organized by the Bruhat decomposition with respect to
the Siegel parabolic:

G(\mathbb{Q})=\coprod_{\nu=0}^{n}P(\mathbb{Q})w_{\nu}P(\mathbb{Q}),

where the cell
P(\mathbb{Q})w_{\nu}P(\mathbb{Q})
is characterized by the
condition that the lower-left block has rank
\nu
.
Thus the Fourier coefficient decomposes as

E_{T}(g,s)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}E_{T,\nu}(g,s),

where
E_{T,\nu}(g,s)
denotes the contribution of the Bruhat cell of
rank
\nu
.

Suppose now that
T
has rank
\lambda
.
We write

T=h[{}^{t}r],\qquad h\in\Lambda_{\lambda}^{\ast},\quad r\in M_{n,\lambda}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}.

The rank-
\nu
contribution vanishes unless
\lambda\leq\nu
.
For
\lambda\leq\nu
, the unfolded contribution

E_{T,\nu}(g_{Z},s)
is the
T
-th Fourier coefficient of the

(\nu,\lambda)
-term in Mizumoto’s expansion.
Therefore Mizumoto’s expansion

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,s)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}\sum_{\lambda=0}^{\nu}F_{\nu,\lambda}^{(n)}(Z,s)

is the classical form of the adelic Fourier expansion obtained by
unfolding the Siegel Eisenstein series and decomposing the result
according to the Bruhat rank
\nu
.
\diamond

3.5.
Fourier Coefficients in the Holomorphic Range

Before specializing to the boundary weight,
we recall the standard Fourier coefficient formula in the holomorphic range.
This formula serves as a reference point for the local Siegel polynomial factors
which occur in the Siegel series.

For a commutative ring
R
and symmetric matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(R)
,
we write
A\sim_{R}B

if there exists
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
such that
B=A[g]
.
When
A
and
B
have different degrees,
we use this notation only after both matrices have been embedded into
matrices of the same degree by adjoining zero blocks.

Let
T\in\Lambda_{n}
be positive semi-definite of rank
\ell
.
For each prime
p
,
choose a non-degenerate matrix

\widetilde{T}_{p}\in\Lambda_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that

T\sim_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{diag}(\widetilde{T}_{p},0_{n-\ell})
.
The local Siegel polynomial
F_{p}(\widetilde{T}_{p},X)
is independent of this
choice.
We put
F_{p}^{\ast}(T,X)=F_{p}(\widetilde{T}_{p},X)
.
If
\ell=0
,
we use the convention
F_{p}^{\ast}(0_{n},X)=1
.

For
T\in\Lambda_{n}
with
T\geq 0
and
\mathrm{rank}(T)=\ell
,
choose a positive definite matrix
\widetilde{T}\in\Lambda_{\ell}
such that
T\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{diag}(\widetilde{T},0_{n-\ell})
.
If
\ell
is even,
let
\chi_{T}^{\ast}
be the quadratic character attached to

\mathbb{Q}\bigl(\sqrt{(-1)^{\ell/2}\det\widetilde{T}}\bigr)
.
This character is independent of the choice of
\widetilde{T}
.

We put

\widetilde{E}_{k}^{(n)}(Z)=\zeta(1-k)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\zeta(1+2j-2k)E_{k}^{(n)}(Z).

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
k\in 2\mathbb{Z}
.
Assume that
k\geq(n+1)/2
,
and that neither of the exceptional conditions

k=(n+2)/2\equiv 2\pmod{4}
,

k=(n+3)/2\equiv 2\pmod{4}

holds.
Then
\widetilde{E}_{k}^{(n)}(Z)
is holomorphic,
and for
T\in\Lambda_{n}
,

T\geq 0
,

\mathrm{rank}(T)=\ell
,
the Fourier coefficient is

a(T,\widetilde{E}_{k}^{(n)})=2^{\left\lfloor(\ell+1)/2\right\rfloor}\prod_{p\mid\det(2\widetilde{T})}F_{p}^{\ast}(T,p^{k-\ell-1})\mathcal{L}^{(n)}_{k,\ell}(T),

where

\mathcal{L}^{(n)}_{k,\ell}(T)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{i=\ell/2+1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\zeta(1+2i-2k)\,L(1+\ell/2-k,\chi_{T}^{\ast}),&\ell\text{ even},\\[11.99998pt]
\displaystyle\prod_{i=(\ell+1)/2}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\zeta(1+2i-2k),&\ell\text{ odd}.\end{cases}

Here an empty product is understood to be
1
.

Proof.

This is the standard Fourier coefficient formula for holomorphic
Siegel–Eisenstein series.
The formula follows from the Fourier coefficient formula for Eisenstein series
due to Shimura
[
13
]
and the explicit factorization of the local Siegel
series into the polynomials
F_{p}(T,X)
due to Katsurada
[
4
]
.
∎

4.
The Constant Term

In this section,
we calculate the constant term
A_{0}^{(n)}(Y)
of

E_{k}^{(n)}(Z)
.
Throughout this section,
we assume that
n=2m
with odd
m\geq 3
,
and put
k=m+1
.

The main case of the paper is
m\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
Nevertheless,
the constant term can be calculated uniformly for all odd
m\geq 3
.
The same formula also explains the simplification which occurs when

m\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.
The only extra contribution which appears when
m=3
comes from the
middle ranks
\nu=3,4
;
its cancellation is recorded separately in
Appendix
A
.

We put

\tau(\nu)=k-\kappa(\nu)=\frac{n+1-\nu}{2}.

By Proposition
3.7
,
the constant Fourier coefficient is given by

A_{0}^{(n)}(Y,s)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}C_{\nu}(Y,s),\qquad C_{\nu}(Y,s)=F_{\nu,0}^{(n)}(Z,s).

We expand each
C_{\nu}(Y,s)
as a Laurent series at
s=0
,
writing

C_{\nu}(Y,s)=\sum_{j}c_{\nu,j}(Y)s^{j}.

Since
E_{k}^{(n)}(Z,s)
is holomorphic at
s=0
,
the desired constant term is obtained by taking the finite part:

A_{0}^{(n)}(Y)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}c_{\nu,0}(Y).

For
\nu=0
,
we have
C_{0}(Y,s)=\det(Y)^{s}
.
Hence

C_{0}(Y,0)=1.

For
1\leq\nu\leq n
,
Proposition
3.7
gives

(4.1)

C_{\nu}(Y,s)=(-1)^{k\nu/2}2^{\nu}\pi^{\nu\kappa(\nu)}\frac{\Gamma_{\nu}(2s+\tau(\nu))}{\Gamma_{\nu}(s)\Gamma_{\nu}(s+m+1)}S_{\nu}(0_{\nu},2s+m+1)\det(Y)^{s}\zeta_{\nu}^{(n)}(2Y,2s+\tau(\nu)).

By Proposition
3.6
,
we have

(4.2)

S_{\nu}(0_{\nu},w)=\frac{\zeta(w-\nu)}{\zeta(w)}\prod_{j=1}^{\nu}\frac{\zeta(2w-\nu-j)}{\zeta(2w-2j)}.

We first determine which terms can contribute to the finite part at
s=0
.
Combining (
4.2
) with the order of the gamma quotient and
Corollary
3.3
,
we obtain

\operatorname{ord}_{s=0}C_{\nu}(Y,s)\geq\begin{cases}\left\lceil\nu/2\right\rceil-1,&1\leq\nu\leq m-1,\\[4.0pt]
(m-3)/2,&\nu=m,m+1,\\[4.0pt]
\left\lceil(2m-\nu)/2\right\rceil-1,&m+2\leq\nu\leq 2m-1,\ \nu\text{ odd},\\[4.0pt]
\left\lceil(2m-\nu)/2\right\rceil,&m+2\leq\nu\leq 2m-1,\ \nu\text{ even},\\[4.0pt]
0,&\nu=2m.\end{cases}

Here the lower bound is obtained by separating the three elementary contributions:
the zero of the reciprocal gamma quotient,
the zeros and poles of
S_{\nu}(0,2s+m+1)
,
and the possible poles of
\zeta_{\nu}^{(2m)}(Y,s+m+1-\nu/2)
.
The borderline equality in the middle ranks occurs only for
m=3
.

Consequently,
if
m\geq 5
,
then the only terms that can contribute to the finite part are

C_{0}
,
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{2m-1}
, and
C_{2m}
.
If
m=3
,
the additional terms
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
may also contribute;
their sum is zero by
Proposition
A.1
.

We put
\epsilon_{m}=(-1)^{(m+1)/2}
.

The case
\nu=1

By (
4.1
),
we have

C_{1}(Y,s)=\epsilon_{m}2\pi\frac{\Gamma(2s+m)}{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s+m+1)}S_{1}(0,2s+m+1)\det(Y)^{s}\zeta_{1}^{(n)}(2Y,2s+m).

Using (
4.2
),
we obtain

S_{1}(0,2s+m+1)=\frac{\zeta(m)}{\zeta(m+1)}+O(s).

We use the residue formula for the Epstein zeta function

[
14
, §1.4,
Theorem 1]
.
For a positive definite real symmetric matrix
S
of degree
n
,
put

Z(S,u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\neq x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}({}^{t}xSx)^{-u}.

With this normalization,
we have

(4.3)

\operatorname*{Res}_{u=n/2}Z(S,u)=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{2\Gamma(n/2)\sqrt{\det S}}.

Since

Z(S,u)=\zeta(2u)\zeta_{1}^{(n)}(S,u),

we obtain

\operatorname*{Res}_{u=m}\zeta_{1}^{(n)}(2Y,u)=\frac{\pi^{m}}{2^{m+1}\Gamma(m)\zeta(2m)\sqrt{\det Y}}.

Hence

\zeta_{1}^{(n)}(2Y,2s+m)=\frac{1}{2s}\frac{\pi^{m}}{2^{m+1}\Gamma(m)\zeta(2m)\sqrt{\det Y}}+O(1).

Moreover,

\frac{\Gamma(2s+m)}{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s+m+1)}=s\frac{\Gamma(m)}{\Gamma(m+1)}+O(s^{2}).

Substituting these expansions,
we obtain

C_{1}(Y,0)=\epsilon_{m}\frac{\pi^{m+1}\zeta(m)}{2^{m+1}m!\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det Y}}.

The case
\nu=2

By (
4.1
),
we have

C_{2}(Y,s)=4\pi^{3}\frac{\Gamma_{2}\left(2s+\frac{2m-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{2}(s)\Gamma_{2}(s+m+1)}S_{2}(0,2s+m+1)\det(Y)^{s}\zeta_{2}^{(n)}\left(2Y,2s+\frac{2m-1}{2}\right).

Using (
4.2
),
we have

S_{2}(0,2s+m+1)=\frac{\zeta(m-1)\zeta(2m-1)}{\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m)}+O(s).

Using the completed Koecher–Maass zeta function,
we have

\zeta_{2}^{(n)}(2Y,u)=\frac{\Xi_{2}^{(n)}(2Y,u)}{2\varepsilon_{2}(u)\varepsilon_{2}(m-u)\xi(2u)\xi(2u-1)}.

A direct Laurent expansion gives

\zeta_{2}^{(n)}\left(2Y,2s+\frac{2m-1}{2}\right)=-\frac{2\,\Xi_{2}^{(n)}\left(2Y,\frac{2m-1}{2}\right)}{s(2m-1)(2m-2)\xi(2m-1)\xi(2m-2)}+O(1).

Also,

\frac{\Gamma_{2}\left(2s+\frac{2m-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{2}(s)\Gamma_{2}(s+m+1)}=-\frac{s}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma_{2}\left(\frac{2m-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{2}(m+1)}+O(s^{2}).

Thus

C_{2}(Y,0)=\frac{2^{2m+2}\pi^{2m}}{(2m)!(2m-1)(2m-2)}\frac{\zeta(m-1)}{\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m-2)\zeta(2m)}\Xi_{2}^{(n)}\left(2Y,\frac{2m-1}{2}\right).

The case
\nu=2m

Since

\zeta_{2m}^{(2m)}(g,u)=\det(g)^{-u},

we have

\zeta_{2m}^{(2m)}\left(2Y,2s+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\det(2Y)^{-2s-1/2}.

Substituting this into (
4.1
),

C_{2m}(Y,s)=2^{-4ms+m}\pi^{m(2m+1)}\det(Y)^{-s-1/2}\frac{\Gamma_{2m}\left(2s+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{2m}(s)\Gamma_{2m}(s+m+1)}S_{2m}(0,2s+m+1).

By the definition of the multivariate gamma function,

\frac{\Gamma_{2m}\left(2s+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{2m}(s)\Gamma_{2m}(s+m+1)}=-\frac{(2m)!}{2^{3m}m!\Gamma_{2m}(m+1)}+O(s).

Using (
4.2
),
we obtain

S_{2m}(0,2s+m+1)=\frac{\zeta(2s+1-m)}{\zeta(2s+m+1)}\frac{\prod_{r=1}^{m}\zeta(4s+3-2r)}{\prod_{r=1}^{m}\zeta(4s+2r)}.

Since
m
is odd,
the factor
\zeta(2s+1-m)
has a simple zero at

s=0
,
while
\zeta(4s+1)
has a simple pole.
Thus

S_{2m}(0,2s+m+1)=\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(1-m)}{2\zeta(m+1)}\frac{\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}\zeta(1-2r)}{\prod_{r=1}^{m}\zeta(2r)}+O(s).

By the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function, since
m
is odd,

\zeta^{\prime}(1-m)=(-1)^{(m-1)/2}2^{-m}\pi^{1-m}\Gamma(m)\zeta(m).

Combining this identity with the gamma factor and the remaining zeta factors,
we obtain

C_{2m}(Y,0)=-\frac{\pi^{m+1}\zeta(m)}{2^{m+1}m!\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det Y}}.

The case
\nu=2m-1

By (
4.1
),
we have

\displaystyle C_{2m-1}(Y,s)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(m+1)(2m-1)/2}2^{2m-1}\pi^{(2m-1)\kappa(2m-1)}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma_{2m-1}(2s+1)}{\Gamma_{2m-1}(s)\Gamma_{2m-1}(s+m+1)}

\displaystyle\times S_{2m-1}(0,2s+m+1)\det(Y)^{s}\zeta_{2m-1}^{(2m)}(2Y,2s+1).

The gamma factor satisfies

\frac{\Gamma_{2m-1}(2s+1)}{\Gamma_{2m-1}(s)\Gamma_{2m-1}(s+m+1)}=s\frac{(2m-2)!}{2^{3(m-1)}\Gamma_{2m-1}(m+1)}+O(s^{2}).

Using (
4.2
),
we obtain

S_{2m-1}(0,2s+m+1)=\frac{1}{4s}\frac{\zeta(2-m)}{\zeta(m+1)}\frac{\prod_{r=1}^{m-2}\zeta(1-2r)}{\prod_{r=2}^{m}\zeta(2r)}+O(1).

Hence the zero of the gamma factor cancels the pole of

S_{2m-1}(0,2s+m+1)
at
s=0
.
Evaluating the remaining normalizing factors at
s=0
, and using the
functional equation of the Riemann zeta function to rewrite the negative
integer zeta values, we get

C_{2m-1}(Y,0)=\epsilon_{m}\frac{2^{6m-5}\pi^{2m}}{(2m-4)!(2m-2)!(2m)!}\frac{\zeta(m-1)}{\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m-2)\zeta(2m)}\frac{\Xi_{2m-1}^{(2m)}(2Y,1)}{\prod_{j=2}^{2m-3}\xi(j)}.

The constant term

Combining the preceding computations and the complementary-rank duality,
we obtain the following formula.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
m\geq 3
is odd,
and put

\epsilon_{m}=(-1)^{(m+1)/2}.

Then the constant Fourier coefficient of
E_{m+1}^{(2m)}(Z)
is given by

(4.4)

A_{0}^{(2m)}(Y)=1+\frac{(\epsilon_{m}-1)\pi^{m+1}}{\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m)\sqrt{\det Y}}\left(\frac{\zeta(m)}{2^{m+1}m!}+\frac{(m-2)!\zeta(m-1)}{(2m)!}\zeta_{1}^{(2m)}(Y,m-1)\right).

Here
\zeta_{1}^{(2m)}(Y,m-1)
is understood by meromorphic continuation.

Proof.

Summing
the contributions of
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{2m-1}
, and
C_{2m}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle A_{0}^{(2m)}(Y)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(\epsilon_{m}-1)\pi^{m+1}\zeta(m)}{2^{m+1}m!\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det Y}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2^{2m+2}\pi^{2m}}{(2m)!(2m-1)(2m-2)}\frac{\zeta(m-1)}{\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m-2)\zeta(2m)}\Xi_{2}^{(2m)}\left(2Y,\frac{2m-1}{2}\right)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon_{m}\frac{2^{6m-5}\pi^{2m}}{(2m-4)!(2m-2)!(2m)!}\frac{\zeta(m-1)}{\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m-2)\zeta(2m)}\frac{\Xi_{2m-1}^{(2m)}(2Y,1)}{\prod_{j=2}^{2m-3}\xi(j)}.

When
m=3
,
the additional middle-rank terms
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
occur,
but their sum is zero by
Proposition
A.1
.

By the residue formula for the Epstein zeta function
[
14
, § 1.4, Theorem 1]
,
we obtain

\Xi_{2}^{(2m)}\left(2Y,\frac{2m-1}{2}\right)=-\frac{(2m-1)(2m-2)}{2^{2m+2}}\xi(2m-2)\frac{\zeta_{1}^{(2m)}(Y,m-1)}{\sqrt{\det Y}}.

By Lemma
3.5
and the functional equation
for the rank-one completed Koecher–Maass zeta function,
we also have

\frac{\Xi_{2m-1}^{(2m)}(2Y,1)}{\prod_{j=2}^{2m-3}\xi(j)}=\frac{(2m-4)!(2m-2)!}{2^{6m-5}}\xi(2m-2)\frac{\zeta_{1}^{(2m)}(Y,m-1)}{\sqrt{\det Y}}.

Substituting these identities into (
4.5
),
we obtain the reduced formula (
4.4
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If
m\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, then
\epsilon_{m}=1
, and
A_{0}^{(2m)}(Y)=1
.
This is compatible with the fact that
E_{k}^{(n)}(Z)
is holomorphic in this case.
\diamond

Remark 4.3

(The degree two case)
.

When
n=2
,
the boundary weight is
k=2
.
This case is exceptional in a stronger sense than the higher degree cases
considered in this paper.
In fact,
writing

Y=\begin{pmatrix}y_{11}&y_{12}\\
y_{12}&y_{22}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{S}_{2}(\mathbb{R})_{>0},

the constant Fourier coefficient of
E_{2}^{(2)}(Z,0)
is

A_{0}^{(2)}(Y)=1-\frac{18}{\pi^{2}\sqrt{\det Y}}\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{E}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{y_{22}}{4\pi}-\log\left|\eta\left(\frac{y_{12}+\sqrt{-1}\sqrt{\det Y}}{y_{22}}\right)\right|^{2}\right),

where
\gamma_{E}
is Euler’s constant and
\eta
is the Dedekind eta
function.
The logarithmic term comes from the first Kronecker limit formula.
Thus the degree-two formula is not a specialization of
Theorem
4.1
;
the zero-pole pattern in Mizumoto’s expansion is different when
n=2
.
\diamond

5.
Non-zero Index Terms

In this section,
we calculate the Fourier coefficients indexed by non-zero matrices in the exceptional case.
Throughout the section,
we assume that
n=2m
,
m\geq 5
,
m\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
Thus
k=m+1\equiv 2\pmod{4}
,
and
n\geq 10
.

5.1.
Index Sets and Scalar Factors

Before treating the Fourier coefficients for non-zero indices,
we define the index sets and scalar factors used below.

For
1\leq\lambda<\nu\leq n
,
put
d=\nu-\lambda
.
We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{d}\left(2s+\frac{n+1-\nu}{2}\right)\cdot\Gamma_{d}(s)^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\left(\varepsilon_{d}\left(2s+\frac{n+1-\nu}{2}\right)\varepsilon_{d}\left(-2s-\frac{\lambda+1-\nu}{2}\right)\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\xi(4s+n+1-\nu-j)\right)^{-1}.

We also put

\mathcal{D}^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)=\mathcal{C}^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)\mathcal{Z}_{\lambda,\nu}(2s+m+1),

where

\mathcal{Z}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)=\frac{\zeta(s+\lambda-\nu)}{\zeta(s)}\prod_{j=1}^{\nu-\lambda}\frac{\zeta(2s-\nu-j)}{\zeta(2s-2j)}.

Finally,
set

\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}=\operatorname{ord}_{s=0}\mathcal{D}^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}(s).

Lemma 5.1
.

We have
\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}\leq 0
precisely in the following cases:

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\lambda&\nu&\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}\\
\hline\cr n-1&n&0\\
n-2&n-1&0\\
n-2&n&-1\\
n-3&n-2,n-1,n&0\\
n-4&n-3,n-2,n-1,n&0\\
n-5,\ n-6&\lambda+1,\lambda+2,\lambda+4&0\\
2\leq\lambda\leq n-7&\lambda+1,\lambda+2&0\\
1&2,3,n&0\end{array}

In degree
n=10
,
one additionally has

\rho^{(10)}_{\lambda,\lambda+5}=0\qquad(2\leq\lambda\leq 5).

Moreover,

\rho^{(n)}_{n-6,n-3}=1,\qquad\rho^{(n)}_{n-8,n-4}=1,

whenever these pairs occur.
All remaining pairs have positive order.

Proof.

The assertion follows by counting the zeros and poles of the factors defining

\mathcal{D}^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)
.
The possible poles come from the zeta factors whose arguments become
1
at
s=0
,
while the zeros come from zeta factors whose arguments become negative even integers and from gamma factors.
Combining these elementary order counts gives exactly the table above.
∎

We define
\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{reg}}=\mathcal{I}_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{reg}}
to be the set of
all integers
\nu
with
\lambda<\nu\leq n
such that

(\lambda,\nu)
appears in the table of
Lemma
5.1
and satisfies

\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}\leq 0
.
In degree
n=10
, we include the additional cases

\nu=\lambda+5
stated in the same lemma.

We shall also need to keep the exceptional order-one cases in which
the zero of the scalar factor may be cancelled by the pole of the
non-degenerate Siegel series.
For this purpose, put

\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}=\begin{cases}\{n-3\},&\lambda=n-6,\\
\{n-4\},&\lambda=n-8,\\
\emptyset,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We then set

\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{(n)}=\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{reg}}\cup\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}\qquad(1\leq\lambda<n),

and finally define

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{(n)}=\begin{cases}\{n\},&\lambda=n,\\
\{\lambda\}\cup\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{(n)},&1\leq\lambda<n.\end{cases}

5.2.
Positive Semi-definite Indices

We now assume that
T\geq 0
(positive semi-definite)
and
T\neq 0
.
Let
\lambda=\mathrm{rank}(T)
.
If
\lambda=n
,
then
T>0
(positive definite),
and we set
h=T
and
r=1_{n}
.
If
\lambda<n
,
choose
h\in\Lambda_{\lambda}^{\ast}
and
r\in M_{n,\lambda}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}

such that
T=h[{}^{t}r]
.
The formulas below are independent of this choice.
In both cases,
we have
h>0
.
For
\lambda\leq\nu\leq n
,
put
u_{\lambda,\nu}=m+1-\nu+\lambda
.

We denote by
A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,s)
the contribution of

F_{\nu,\lambda}^{(n)}(Z,s)
to the
T
-th Fourier coefficient.
Thus

A_{T}^{(n)}(Y,s)=\sum_{\nu=\lambda}^{n}A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,s).

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
T\geq 0
,

T\neq 0
,
and
\mathrm{rank}(T)=\lambda<n
.
If
\lambda<\nu\leq n
and
\nu\notin\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{(n)}
,
then

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,s)=O(s)\quad(s\to 0).

Hence this term does not contribute to the constant term at
s=0
.

Proof.

The
(\nu,\lambda)
-term factors into the non-degenerate Siegel series

S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu})
and a remaining factor whose zeta part is

\mathcal{D}^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)
.
If
\nu\notin\mathcal{I}_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{reg}}
,
then Lemma
5.1
gives

\operatorname{ord}_{s=0}\mathcal{D}^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)>0.

The only possible pole of
S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu})
at
s=0
occurs when
\lambda
is even,
the discriminant of
h
is a square,
and
u_{\lambda,\nu}=\lambda/2+1
.
This condition is equivalent to
\nu=m+\lambda/2
.
Among the pairs for which
\operatorname{ord}_{s=0}\mathcal{D}^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}(s)=1
,
this happens precisely for
(\lambda,\nu)=(n-6,n-3)
or
(\lambda,\nu)=(n-8,n-4)
.
These are exactly the indices added in
\mathcal{I}_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}
.
Therefore,
if
\nu\notin\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{(n)}
,
the term remains
O(s)
,
even after taking into account the possible pole of the non-degenerate Siegel series.
∎

For each
\nu\in\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{(n)}
,
we define
\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)
by extracting the Siegel series

S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu})
from the
(\nu,\lambda)
-term.

For
\nu=\lambda
,
set

\Phi_{\lambda,\lambda}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)=(-1)^{k\lambda/2}2^{\lambda}\pi^{\lambda\kappa(\lambda)}\frac{\det(Y)^{s}}{\Gamma_{\lambda}(s)\Gamma_{\lambda}(s+m+1)}\eta_{\lambda}\left(2Y[r],\pi h;s+m+1,s\right).

For
\lambda<\nu
,
set

\displaystyle B_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(s)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k\nu/2}2^{\nu-1}\pi^{\nu\kappa(\nu)}\frac{\Gamma_{\nu-\lambda}\left(2s+\frac{n+1-\nu}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\nu-\lambda}(s)}\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\nu}(s+m+1)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\varepsilon_{\nu-\lambda}\left(2s+\frac{n+1-\nu}{2}\right)^{-1}\varepsilon_{\nu-\lambda}\left(-2s-\frac{\lambda+1-\nu}{2}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-\lambda-1}\xi(4s+n+1-\nu-j)^{-1},

and define

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)

\displaystyle=B_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(s)\mathcal{Z}_{\lambda,\nu}(2s+m+1)\det(Y)^{s}\det(2Y[r])^{(\lambda-\nu)/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\eta_{\lambda}\left(2Y[r],\pi h;s+m+1+\frac{\lambda-\nu}{2},s+\frac{\lambda-\nu}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}\left(s+\frac{\lambda-\nu}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Xi_{\nu-\lambda}^{(n-\lambda)}\left(2g(Y,u_{r}),2s+\frac{n+1-\nu}{2}\right).

With this notation,

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,s)=\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu})

for all
\nu\in\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{(n)}
.

If
\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)
is holomorphic at
s=0
,
we write

\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r)=\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;0).

If
(\lambda,\nu)=(n-2,n)
,
then
\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)
has a simple pole at
s=0
.
We write

\Phi_{n-2,n}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)=\frac{\Phi_{n-2,n}^{(n),-1}(Y,h,r)}{s}+\Phi_{n-2,n}^{(n),0}(Y,h,r)+O(s).

If

\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)=s\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),1}(Y,h,r)+O(s^{2}),

we use this notation for the leading coefficient.

We also need the following notation for the possible pole of the non-degenerate Siegel series.
Assume that
\lambda
is even.
Let
\chi_{h}
be the quadratic character attached to
\mathrm{disc}(h)
.
Near
u=\lambda/2+1
,
we write

S_{\lambda}(h,u)=L\left(u-\frac{\lambda}{2},\chi_{h}\right)\mathcal{P}_{h}(u),

where
\mathcal{P}_{h}(u)
is holomorphic at
u=\lambda/2+1
.
If the discriminant of
h
is a square with our convention,
then

S_{\lambda}(h,u)=\zeta\left(u-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)\mathcal{P}_{h}(u).

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that
T\geq 0
,

T\neq 0
,
and
\mathrm{rank}(T)=\lambda
.
Let
h
and
r
be as above.
Then

A_{T}^{(n)}(Y)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{(n)}}A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y).

For
\nu\in\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{(n)}
,
the term
A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y)
is given as follows.

(1)

If
(\lambda,\nu)=(n-2,n)
,
then

A_{T,n}^{(n)}(Y)=\Phi_{n-2,n}^{(n),0}(Y,h,r)S_{n-2}(h,m-1)+2\Phi_{n-2,n}^{(n),-1}(Y,h,r)S_{n-2}^{\prime}(h,m-1).

(2)

Suppose that
\lambda
is even,

\mathrm{disc}(h)
is a square,
and
\nu=m+\lambda/2
.
If
n-\lambda=0,2,4
,
then

\displaystyle A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r)\left\{\mathcal{P}_{h}^{\prime}(u_{\lambda,\nu})+\gamma_{E}\mathcal{P}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu})\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{d}{ds}\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)\right|_{s=0}\mathcal{P}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu}).

If
n-\lambda=6,8
,
then

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y)=\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),1}(Y,h,r)\mathcal{P}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu}).

If
n-\lambda\geq 10
,
then

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y)=0.

(3)

In all remaining cases with
\nu\in\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{(n)}
,
we have

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y)=\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r)S_{\lambda}(h,u_{\lambda,\nu}).

Proof.

If
\lambda=n
,
only the term with
\nu=n
occurs.
If
\lambda<n
,
Proposition
5.2
shows that only the terms with

\nu\in\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{(n)}
can contribute.

For such
\nu
,
we have

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,s)=\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu}).

If
(\lambda,\nu)=(n-2,n)
,
substituting the Laurent expansion of

\Phi_{n-2,n}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)

and the Taylor expansion of
S_{n-2}(h,m-1+2s)

gives the formula in
(1)
.

Suppose next that the square-discriminant pole condition holds.
Then

S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu})=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu})}{2s}+\mathcal{P}_{h}^{\prime}(u_{\lambda,\nu})+\gamma_{E}\mathcal{P}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu})+O(s).

If
n-\lambda=0,2,4
,
the factor
\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)
is holomorphic at
s=0
,
and extracting the coefficient of
s^{0}
gives the first formula in
(2)
.
If
n-\lambda=6,8
,
the same extraction using

\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)=s\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),1}(Y,h,r)+O(s^{2})

gives the second formula in
2
.
If
n-\lambda\geq 10
,
the zero of
\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r;s)
has order at least
2
,
so the term gives no constant term.

In the remaining cases,
both factors are holomorphic at
s=0
,
and direct substitution gives the formula in
(3)
.
∎

5.3.
Indices That Are Not Positive Semi-definite

We now treat the Fourier coefficients indexed by matrices that are not positive semi-definite.
Let
T\in\Lambda_{n}
be non-zero,
and suppose that
T\not\geq 0
.
Put
\lambda=\mathrm{rank}(T)
,
and choose
h\in\Lambda_{\lambda}^{\ast}
and
r\in M_{n,\lambda}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{prim}}
such that

T=h[{}^{t}r]
.
Then
h
is non-degenerate and not positive definite.
Let the signature of
h
be
(p,q)
,
where
p+q=\lambda
and
q>0
.
For
\lambda\leq\nu\leq n
,
put

u_{\lambda,\nu}=m+1-\nu+\lambda
.

We first rewrite the factor containing the confluent hypergeometric function.
By the formula for the confluent hypergeometric function due to Shimura
[
12
, Theorem 4.2 and (4.6.K)]
,
there exists a function
H_{p,q,\nu-\lambda}(s;Y,h,r)
,
holomorphic at
s=0
,
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{\lambda}\left(2Y[r],\pi h;s+m+1-\frac{\nu-\lambda}{2},s-\frac{\nu-\lambda}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}\left(s-\frac{\nu-\lambda}{2}\right)}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\qquad=H_{p,q,\nu-\lambda}(s;Y,h,r)\frac{\Gamma_{p}\left(s-\frac{\nu-\lambda+q}{2}\right)\Gamma_{q}\left(s+m+1-\frac{\nu-\lambda+p}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}\left(s-\frac{\nu-\lambda}{2}\right)}.

Here
\Gamma_{0}(s)=1
.

The second gamma factor in the numerator of (
5.1
)
is holomorphic and non-zero at
s=0
.
Indeed,
for
0\leq a\leq q-1
,
we have

m+1-\frac{\nu-\lambda+p+a}{2}=m+1-\frac{\nu-q+a}{2}\geq\frac{3}{2}.

We put

\delta_{q}(\nu-\lambda)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=0}\frac{\Gamma_{p}\left(s-\frac{\nu-\lambda+q}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}\left(s-\frac{\nu-\lambda}{2}\right)}.

This number is independent of
p
,
and is given by

(5.2)

\delta_{q}(\nu-\lambda)=\begin{cases}\left\lceil q/2\right\rceil,&\nu-\lambda\equiv 0\pmod{2},\\[4.0pt]
\left\lfloor q/2\right\rfloor,&\nu-\lambda\equiv 1\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

If
\lambda<\nu
,
define

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q}(Y,h,r;s)

\displaystyle:=B_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(s)\mathcal{Z}_{\lambda,\nu}(2s+m+1)\det(Y)^{s}\det(2Y[r])^{(\lambda-\nu)/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times H_{p,q,\nu-\lambda}(s;Y,h,r)\frac{\Gamma_{p}\left(s-\frac{\nu-\lambda+q}{2}\right)\Gamma_{q}\left(s+m+1-\frac{\nu-\lambda+p}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}\left(s-\frac{\nu-\lambda}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Xi_{\nu-\lambda}^{(n-\lambda)}\left(2g(Y,u_{r}),2s+\frac{n+1-\nu}{2}\right),

and if
\nu=\lambda
,
define

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda,\lambda}^{(n),p,q}(Y,h,r;s)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k\lambda/2}2^{\lambda}\pi^{\lambda\kappa(\lambda)}\det(Y)^{s}\frac{H_{p,q,0}(s;Y,h,r)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}(s+m+1)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\Gamma_{p}\left(s-\frac{q}{2}\right)\Gamma_{q}\left(s+m+1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}(s)}.

Then

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,s)=\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q}(Y,h,r;s)S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu}).

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
\lambda
is even.
Let
h\in\Lambda_{\lambda}^{\ast}
have signature
(p,q)
.
If
\mathrm{disc}(h)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
,
then

q\equiv\lambda/2\pmod{2}.

Proof.

Since
h
has signature
(p,q)
,
we have
\operatorname{sgn}(\det h)=(-1)^{q}
.
Hence

\operatorname{sgn}(\mathrm{disc}(h))=\operatorname{sgn}\left((-1)^{\lambda/2}\det h\right)=(-1)^{\lambda/2+q}.

If
\mathrm{disc}(h)
is a square in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
,
then
\mathrm{disc}(h)>0
.
Therefore
\lambda/2+q
is even.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
T\in\Lambda_{n}
be non-zero and suppose that
T\not\geq 0
.
Then

A_{T}^{(n)}(Y)=\sum_{\nu=\lambda}^{n}A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y).

We say that
(\lambda,\nu)
is in the square-discriminant case if
all of the following conditions hold:
(i)
\lambda
is even,
(ii)
\mathrm{disc}(h)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
,
(iii)
\nu=m+\lambda/2
.
The summands are described as follows.

(1)

Suppose first that
(\lambda,\nu)
is not in the
square-discriminant case.
Then
A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y)\neq 0

can occur only if
(q,\lambda,\nu)
is one of the following
cases:

\begin{array}[]{c|c}q&(\lambda,\nu)\\
\hline\cr 1&(\lambda,\lambda+1),\ (n-2,n),\ (n-3,n),\ (n-4,n-1),\ (1,n)\\[2.0pt]
2&(n-2,n).\end{array}

When
n=10
, the case
q=1
also includes
(2,7)
,
(3,8)
,
(4,9)
, and
(5,10)
.
In these cases, write

\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q}(Y,h,r;s)=\frac{\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q;-1}(Y,h,r)}{s}+\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q;0}(Y,h,r)+O(s).

Then

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y)=\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q;0}(Y,h,r)S_{\lambda}(h,u_{\lambda,\nu})+2\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q;-1}(Y,h,r)S_{\lambda}^{\prime}(h,u_{\lambda,\nu}).

(2)

Suppose next that
(\lambda,\nu)
is in the
square-discriminant case.
Then
A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y)\neq 0

can occur only if
(q,\lambda,\nu)
is one of the following
cases:

\begin{array}[]{c|c}q&(\lambda,\nu)\\
\hline\cr 1&(n,n),\ (n-4,n-2)\\
2&(n-2,n-1).\end{array}

Near
u=u_{\lambda,\nu}
, write

S_{\lambda}(h,u)=\zeta\left(u-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)\mathcal{P}_{h}(u),

where
\mathcal{P}_{h}(u)
is holomorphic at
u=u_{\lambda,\nu}
, and put

Q_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu})=\mathcal{P}_{h}^{\prime}(u_{\lambda,\nu})+\gamma_{E}\mathcal{P}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu}).

If

\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q}(Y,h,r;s)=\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q;0}(Y,h,r)+s\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q;1}(Y,h,r)+O(s^{2}),

then

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y)=\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q;0}(Y,h,r)Q_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu})+\frac{1}{2}\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q;1}(Y,h,r)\mathcal{P}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu}).

Moreover,
every term
A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,s)
is holomorphic at
s=0
.

Proof.

We first compute the finite parts.
The identity

A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,s)=\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q}(Y,h,r;s)S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu})

reduces the calculation to multiplying Laurent expansions.

(1)

Outside the square-discriminant case, the Siegel series is
holomorphic at
u=u_{\lambda,\nu}
.
Hence

S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu})=S_{\lambda}(h,u_{\lambda,\nu})+2sS_{\lambda}^{\prime}(h,u_{\lambda,\nu})+O(s^{2}).

Multiplying this expansion by the Laurent expansion of

\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q}(Y,h,r;s)
gives the formula in

(1)
.

(2)

In the square-discriminant case, we have

S_{\lambda}(h,2s+u_{\lambda,\nu})=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu})}{2s}+Q_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu})+O(s).

Multiplying this expansion by the Taylor expansion of

\Psi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n),p,q}(Y,h,r;s)
gives the formula in

(2)
.

It remains to determine when a finite part can occur.
Combining Lemma
5.1
,
(
5.2
), and
Lemma
5.4
, we obtain the following
restrictions.

(1)

Outside the square-discriminant case, a finite part can occur
only if

\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}+\delta_{q}(\nu-\lambda)\leq 0.

Since
\delta_{q}(\nu-\lambda)=0
exactly when
q=1
and

\nu-\lambda
is odd, the pairs with

\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}=0
give the first row of the table.
The unique pair with
\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}=-1
, namely

(n-2,n)
, also allows
q=2
.

(2)

In the square-discriminant case, the condition

u_{\lambda,\nu}=\lambda/2+1
gives

(\lambda,\nu)=(n,n),\ (n-2,n-1),\ (n-4,n-2),\ldots.

Moreover, Lemma
5.4
imposes
q\equiv\lambda/2\pmod{2}
.
Since the Siegel series has at most a simple pole, a finite part
can occur only if

\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}+\delta_{q}(\nu-\lambda)\leq 1

for
\lambda<\nu
.
For
\lambda=\nu=n
, the corresponding condition is
\delta_{q}(0)\leq 1
.
These restrictions leave exactly

(n,n),\ q=1,\qquad(n-2,n-1),\ q=2,\qquad(n-4,n-2),\ q=1.

Finally, we show that no principal part occurs.
Outside the square-discriminant case, a principal part could occur
only if
\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}+\delta_{q}(\nu-\lambda)=-1
.
The only possible pair with
\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}=-1
is

(n-2,n)
, but then
\delta_{q}(2)=\left\lceil q/2\right\rceil\geq 1
.
Hence no principal part occurs in this case.

In the square-discriminant case, a principal part would require

\rho^{(n)}_{\lambda,\nu}+\delta_{q}(\nu-\lambda)=0

for
\lambda<\nu
, or
\delta_{q}(0)=0
for
\lambda=\nu=n
.
The latter is impossible because
q>0
.
The former has only the formal possibility
(\lambda,\nu,q)=(n-2,n-1,1)
,
but this is ruled out by Lemma
5.4
.
Thus no principal part occurs.
∎

6.
Main Theorem

We now collect the results of the preceding sections.
Throughout this section,
let
n=2m
,
k=m+1
,
\epsilon_{m}=(-1)^{(m+1)/2}
, where
m\geq 3
is odd.
We write

E_{m+1}^{(2m)}(Z)=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}A_{T}^{(n)}(Y)e(\tr(TX)).

Theorem 6.1
.

The constant Fourier coefficient is

A_{0}^{(2m)}(Y)=1+\frac{(\epsilon_{m}-1)\pi^{m+1}}{\zeta(m+1)\zeta(2m)\sqrt{\det Y}}\left(\frac{\zeta(m)}{2^{m+1}m!}+\frac{(m-2)!\zeta(m-1)}{(2m)!}\zeta_{1}^{(2m)}(Y,m-1)\right),

where
\zeta_{1}^{(2m)}(Y,m-1)
is understood by meromorphic continuation.

Assume now that
m\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
For every non-zero
T\in\Lambda_{n}
, the coefficient

A_{T}^{(n)}(Y)
is determined as follows.

(1)

Suppose that
T\geq 0
, and put

\lambda=\mathrm{rank}(T)
.
Choose
h
and
r
as in Section
5
.
Then

A_{T}^{(n)}(Y)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{(n)}}A_{T,\nu}^{(n)}(Y).

The summands are given by
Proposition
5.3
.
They fall into the following three types:

(a)

For all non-exceptional

\nu\in\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{(n)}
, the term is

\Phi_{\lambda,\nu}^{(n)}(Y,h,r)S_{\lambda}(h,u_{\lambda,\nu})
.

(b)

For
(\lambda,\nu)=(n-2,n)
, the term is the finite part
involving
S_{n-2}(h,m-1)
and
S^{\prime}_{n-2}(h,m-1)
.

(c)

If
\lambda
is even,

\mathrm{disc}(h)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
, and

\nu=m+\lambda/2
, the term is the finite part involving

\mathcal{P}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu})
and

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{h}(u_{\lambda,\nu})
.

(2)

Suppose that
T\not\geq 0

Appendix A
The Middle-Rank Terms in Degree 6

In this appendix,
we record the cancellation of the middle-rank terms which occurs only in
degree
6
.
Thus
m=3
,

n=6
,
and
k=4
.
In this case,
the terms
C_{3}(Y,s)
and
C_{4}(Y,s)
also have order
0
at
s=0
.

Proposition A.1
.

Assume that
m=3
, so that
n=6
and
k=4
.
Then

C_{3}(Y,0)+C_{4}(Y,0)=0.

Proof.

We first relate the two completed Koecher–Maass zeta functions
which occur in the computation.
Applying Proposition
3.4
with

M=6
,
\nu=3
, and
\mu=2
, we get

(A.1)

\Xi_{3}^{(6)}(S,2)=-\frac{1}{3}\det(S)^{-3/2}\Xi_{2}^{(6)}\left(S^{-1},\frac{3}{2}\right).

By the definition of
\Xi_{2}^{(6)}
and
Lemma
3.5
,

\displaystyle\Xi_{2}^{(6)}\left(S^{-1},\frac{3}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{9}{2}\xi(3)\xi(2)\zeta_{2}^{(6)}\left(S^{-1},\frac{3}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{9}{2}\xi(3)\xi(2)\det(S)^{3/2}\zeta_{4}^{(6)}\left(S,\frac{3}{2}\right).

Hence

(A.2)

\Xi_{3}^{(6)}(S,2)=-\frac{3}{2}\xi(3)\xi(2)\zeta_{4}^{(6)}\left(S,\frac{3}{2}\right).

On the other hand, the definition of
\Xi_{4}^{(6)}
gives

(A.3)

\Xi_{4}^{(6)}\left(S,\frac{3}{2}\right)=\frac{9}{32}\xi(3)\xi(2)\zeta_{4}^{(6)}\left(S,\frac{3}{2}\right).

Combining (
A.2
) and
(
A.3
), we obtain

(A.4)

\Xi_{4}^{(6)}\left(S,\frac{3}{2}\right)=-\frac{3}{16}\Xi_{3}^{(6)}(S,2).

We now compute
C_{3}(Y,0)
and
C_{4}(Y,0)
.
For
\nu=3
, formula (
4.1
) gives

C_{3}(Y,s)=8\pi^{6}\frac{\Gamma_{3}(2s+2)}{\Gamma_{3}(s)\Gamma_{3}(s+4)}S_{3}(0_{3},2s+4)\det(Y)^{s}\zeta_{3}^{(6)}(2Y,2s+2).

At
s=0
, the relevant Laurent expansions are

\frac{\Gamma_{3}(2s+2)}{\Gamma_{3}(s)\Gamma_{3}(s+4)}=\frac{s^{2}}{90\pi^{2}}+O(s^{3}),

S_{3}(0_{3},2s+4)=\frac{1}{2s}\frac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta(4)\zeta(6)}+O(1),

and

\zeta_{3}^{(6)}(2Y,2s+2)=-\frac{\Xi_{3}^{(6)}(2Y,2)}{6s\,\xi(4)\xi(3)\xi(2)}+O(1).

Therefore

C_{3}(Y,0)=-A_{3}\Xi_{3}^{(6)}(2Y,2),

where

A_{3}=\frac{\pi^{6}}{135}\frac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta(4)^{2}\zeta(6)\xi(3)\xi(2)}.

For
\nu=4
, formula (
4.1
) gives

C_{4}(Y,s)=16\pi^{10}\frac{\Gamma_{4}\left(2s+\frac{3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{4}(s)\Gamma_{4}(s+4)}S_{4}(0_{4},2s+4)\det(Y)^{s}\zeta_{4}^{(6)}\left(2Y,2s+\frac{3}{2}\right).

The corresponding Laurent expansions are

\frac{\Gamma_{4}\left(2s+\frac{3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{4}(s)\Gamma_{4}(s+4)}=\frac{s}{180\pi^{4}}+O(s^{2}),

S_{4}(0_{4},2s+4)=-\frac{1}{8s}\frac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta(4)^{2}\zeta(6)}+O(1),

and

\zeta_{4}^{(6)}\left(2Y,2s+\frac{3}{2}\right)=\frac{32}{9\xi(3)\xi(2)}\Xi_{4}^{(6)}\left(2Y,\frac{3}{2}\right)+O(s).

Thus

C_{4}(Y,0)=-A_{4}\Xi_{4}^{(6)}\left(2Y,\frac{3}{2}\right),

where

A_{4}=\frac{16\pi^{6}}{405}\frac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta(4)^{2}\zeta(6)\xi(3)\xi(2)}=\frac{16}{3}A_{3}.

Applying (
A.4
) with
S=2Y
, we conclude that

\displaystyle C_{3}(Y,0)+C_{4}(Y,0)

\displaystyle=-A_{3}\Xi_{3}^{(6)}(2Y,2)-A_{4}\Xi_{4}^{(6)}\left(2Y,\frac{3}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-A_{3}\Xi_{3}^{(6)}(2Y,2)+A_{3}\Xi_{3}^{(6)}(2Y,2)

\displaystyle=0.

∎
