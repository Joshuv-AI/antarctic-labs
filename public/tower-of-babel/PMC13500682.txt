Sarcopenia modifies the survival benefit of triple therapy versus TACE alone in elderly patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma: A multicenter retrospective cohort study
Authors: Lin Ye, Wanrong Yue, Kangqiang Lin, Jiaye Liao, Ali Mo, Guoxing Liu, Xuanting Chen, Hao Shi, Tao Wang, Qingrong Mo, Jun Weng, Yingjie Zhou, Renjian Li, Zhao Jiang, Jiguang Luo, Borong Huang, Yaqun Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTriple therapy combining transarterial chemoembolisation (TACE), tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has shown efficacy in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), but evidence in elderly patients and the role of sarcopenia remain unclear. We enrolled 231 patients aged ≥ 65 years with unresectable HCC who received triple therapy (TACE + TKI + ICI; n = 80) or TACE alone (n = 151) across seven centres in China (2019–2023). Sarcopenia was defined by sex-specific L3 skeletal muscle index (SMI) cutoffs (males: 42 cm2/m2; females: 38 cm2/m2). Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) adjusted for confounding, and treatment–sarcopenia interaction was assessed via weighted Cox regression. After IPTW adjustment, triple therapy significantly improved overall survival (OS; median 12.6 vs 9.3 months; hazard ratio [HR] 1.67, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.26–2.20; p < 0.001) and progression-free survival (median 6.2 vs 4.2 months; HR 1.86, 95% CI 1.40–2.47; p < 0.001). Sarcopenia was present in 100 patients (43.3%) and significantly modified the treatment effect (p for interaction = 0.049). Among non-sarcopenic patients (n = 131), triple therapy conferred a substantial survival benefit (median OS 19.3 vs 10.6 months; HR 1.97; p < 0.001), whereas no significant benefit was observed in sarcopenic patients (n = 100; median OS 10.4 vs 8.7 months; HR 1.20; p = 0.460). Grade ≥ 3 adverse events were comparable between groups (26.2% vs 16.6%; p = 0.086). Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the sixth most commonly diagnosed malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide1,2. The global burden of HCC continues to rise, driven by the ageing of the population and the increasing prevalence of metabolic dysfunction, associated steatotic liver disease. Importantly, the incidence of HCC increases markedly with age, with the highest age-specific rates observed in individuals older than 65 years in most regions3–5. In Asia, where hepatitis B virus (HBV)–related HCC predominates, elderly patients constitute a growing proportion of newly diagnosed cases due to improvements in antiviral therapy and overall life expectancy6–8. Despite this epidemiological trend, elderly patients have been systematically underrepresented in pivotal clinical trials of HCC treatments, creating an evidence gap that directly affects clinical decision-making in this vulnerable population9–11.

Transarterial chemoembolisation (TACE) has long been the cornerstone of treatment for patients with unresectable intermediate-stage HCC and is also widely applied in selected patients with advanced-stage disease12,13. However, the efficacy of TACE monotherapy is limited, with a median progression-free survival of approximately 7 months and a median overall survival of 20–30 months in intermediate-stage disease14. Over the past decade, the treatment landscape for HCC has been transformed by the advent of systemic therapies, particularly the combination of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) axis with anti-angiogenic agents such as tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) or bevacizumab15–17. The IMbrave150 trial established atezolizumab plus bevacizumab as the standard first-line systemic regimen for advanced HCC, and subsequent studies have further reinforced the role of combination immunotherapy-based strategies in improving survival outcomes18,19.

The rationale for combining TACE with systemic immunotherapy and targeted therapy, so-called triple therapy, rests on a strong mechanistic foundation. TACE induces tumour necrosis and releases tumour-associated antigens, thereby promoting immunogenic cell death and enhancing the anti-tumour immune response20,21. Concurrently, anti-angiogenic TKIs normalise the aberrant tumour vasculature, improve immune cell infiltration, and counteract the compensatory upregulation of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) that follows embolisation-induced hypoxia22. ICIs then amplify the effector T-cell response by relieving immune checkpoint–mediated inhibition. This synergistic triad has been validated by recent phase III trials: the EMERALD-1 study demonstrated that durvalumab plus bevacizumab combined with TACE significantly improved progression-free survival compared with TACE alone, and the LEAP-012 trial showed that lenvatinib plus pembrolizumab plus TACE extended progression-free survival in patients with intermediate-stage HCC23–25. These practice-changing results suggest that triple therapy may become the new standard of care for unresectable HCC amenable to embolisation.

Nevertheless, the applicability of these findings to elderly patients remains uncertain. Ageing is associated with physiological changes that may influence both the efficacy and tolerability of combination regimens, including immunosenescence (the age-related decline in adaptive immune function), reduced hepatic and renal clearance, diminished bone marrow reserve, and an increased burden of comorbidities9,26. Furthermore, elderly patients with chronic liver disease frequently exhibit sarcopenia, the progressive loss of skeletal muscle mass and function, which has emerged as a significant prognostic factor across multiple treatment modalities in HCC, including surgical resection, liver transplantation, TACE, and systemic therapy27,28. Skeletal muscle is now recognised as a dynamic endocrine organ that secretes myokines essential for immune homeostasis; sarcopenia may therefore not merely serve as a marker of frailty but may actively impair the immunological mechanisms through which checkpoint inhibitors exert their anti-tumour effects29,30.

Several retrospective studies have investigated the prognostic impact of sarcopenia in HCC patients receiving immunotherapy-based regimens. A recent meta-analysis of 15 studies involving 1,543 patients with HCC treated with ICIs demonstrated that low skeletal muscle index was independently associated with inferior overall survival (HR 1.68), progression-free survival (HR 1.45), and reduced objective response and disease control rates31. However, these studies have predominantly evaluated sarcopenia as an independent prognostic factor rather than as a modifier of treatment efficacy, that is, whether sarcopenia differentially affects the magnitude of benefit derived from one treatment strategy over another has not been systematically examined. Moreover, data specifically addressing the intersection of advanced age, sarcopenia, and the efficacy of TACE-based triple therapy are virtually absent from the current literature.

To address these critical knowledge gaps, we conducted this multicenter retrospective cohort study with two primary objectives: first, to evaluate the efficacy and safety of TACE-based triple therapy compared with TACE alone in elderly patients (≥ 65 years) with unresectable HCC; and second, to determine whether sarcopenia, assessed by CT-derived L3 skeletal muscle index, modifies the therapeutic benefit of triple therapy, thereby informing a more personalised approach to treatment selection in this growing but underserved patient population.

Methods

Study design and patients

This multicenter retrospective cohort study was conducted in The First Affiliated Hospital of Guilin Medical University and six other tertiary hepatobiliary centres in China. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guilin Medical University (approval number: LXIIT2024026) and each participating centre and was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guilin Medical University due to the retrospective nature of the study.

We consecutively screened patients aged ≥ 65 years who were diagnosed with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and received either triple therapy (TACE combined with a tyrosine kinase inhibitor [TKI] and an immune checkpoint inhibitor [ICI]) or TACE alone as first-line treatment between January 2019 and December 2023. The diagnosis of HCC was established according to the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) criteria, based on histological confirmation or characteristic imaging features on contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Unresectability was determined by a multidisciplinary tumour board at each centre32.

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age ≥ 65 years; (2) histologically or radiologically confirmed HCC; (3) tumour deemed unresectable by multidisciplinary consensus; (4) Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage B or C33; (5) Child–Pugh class A or B7; (6) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0–2; and (7) at least one measurable lesion according to modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (mRECIST). Exclusion criteria included: (1) age < 65 years; (2) non-HCC or mixed hepatocellular–cholangiocarcinoma histology; (3) Child–Pugh score ≥ B8 or class C; (4) prior systemic therapy for HCC; (5) active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment; (6) history of organ transplantation; (7) concurrent active extrahepatic malignancy; and (8) incomplete baseline or follow-up data. Treatment allocation was not randomised; the choice between triple therapy and TACE alone was determined by multidisciplinary team discussion and patient preference, reflecting real-world clinical practice. To minimise confounding from this non-random allocation, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was applied.

Treatment protocols

In the triple therapy group, patients received TACE as the initial local treatment followed by concurrent systemic therapy consisting of a TKI and an ICI. TACE was performed using either conventional lipiodol-based chemoembolisation (cTACE) or drug-eluting bead TACE (DEB-TACE) at the discretion of the interventional radiologist. The chemotherapeutic agents used included doxorubicin, epirubicin, or oxaliplatin, administered through selective catheterisation of the hepatic artery feeding the tumour. Subsequent TACE sessions were performed on demand based on tumour response and hepatic functional reserve, with a minimum interval of four weeks between sessions.

TKI agents included lenvatinib (8 mg/day for body weight < 60 kg or 12 mg/day for ≥ 60 kg), sorafenib (400 mg twice daily), or donafenib (200 mg twice daily), administered orally with dose modifications permitted according to individual tolerability. ICI agents included tislelizumab (200 mg every three weeks), sintilimab (200 mg every three weeks), camrelizumab (200 mg every three weeks), or atezolizumab (1200 mg every three weeks), administered intravenously. Systemic therapy was initiated within two weeks after the first TACE session and continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal.

In the TACE-alone group, patients received TACE as monotherapy using the same procedural techniques. Repeat TACE sessions were scheduled on demand according to tumour response and liver function. Patients in the triple therapy group received at least one dose of both the assigned TKI and ICI; no patient was excluded owing to failure to initiate systemic therapy. The interval from the first TACE session to systemic therapy initiation was recorded and examined as a covariate in a sensitivity analysis.

Assessment of sarcopenia

Sarcopenia was assessed using cross-sectional CT images obtained within 30 days before treatment initiation. Skeletal muscle area (SMA) was measured at the level of the third lumbar vertebra (L3) on a single axial CT slice using semi-automated segmentation software (SliceOmatic V5.0, TomoVision, Magog, Canada). Skeletal muscles at the L3 level, including the psoas, erector spinae, quadratus lumborum, transversus abdominis, external and internal obliques, and rectus abdominis, were delineated using a Hounsfield unit (HU) threshold range of − 29 to + 150 HU. The total L3 skeletal muscle area (cm2) was normalised for height squared to yield the L3 skeletal muscle index (SMI; cm2/m2).

Sex-specific sarcopenia cutoff values were determined using restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis of the association between L3 SMI and overall survival, with the median SMI as the reference value. The optimal cutoff points were identified at the inflection points of the spline curves: 42 cm2/m2 for males and 38 cm2/m2 for females. These thresholds are consistent with previously published Asian-specific definitions and were further validated by the non-linearity test. Patients with an SMI below the sex-specific cutoff were classified as sarcopenic.

Outcomes and assessments

The primary outcome was overall survival (OS), defined as the time from the date of first treatment (first TACE session) to death from any cause or the date of last follow-up. Secondary outcomes included progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time from first treatment to radiological disease progression per mRECIST or death; objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR), assessed by mRECIST at the best overall response; time to hepatic deterioration (TTHD), defined as progression from Child–Pugh A to Child–Pugh B or C; Child–Pugh A preservation rate at 6 and 12 months; and safety, assessed according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) version 5.0.

Tumour response was evaluated by contrast-enhanced CT or MRI performed every 6–8 weeks during the first year and every 8–12 weeks thereafter. Imaging was reviewed independently by two radiologists blinded to treatment allocation at each centre, with discrepancies resolved by consensus. Follow-up visits included physical examination, laboratory assessment (complete blood count, liver function, AFP), and adverse event monitoring at each visit. The data cut-off date was March 31, 2025.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were compared using the Chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test, and continuous variables using the Mann–Whitney U test. To adjust for confounding by indication, we estimated propensity scores via multivariable logistic regression incorporating age, sex, Child–Pugh score, BCLC stage, ECOG performance status, tumour number, maximum tumour diameter, portal vein tumour thrombus, extrahepatic metastasis, AFP (log-transformed), and L3 SMI. Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) targeting the average treatment effect was applied, with weights stabilised and truncated at the 1st and 99th percentiles34. Covariate balance after weighting was verified using standardised mean differences (SMD < 0.10).

Survival was estimated by the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the weighted log-rank test. Hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were derived from weighted Cox proportional hazards models; the proportional hazards assumption was verified by Schoenfeld residuals. Time to hepatic deterioration was analysed using the cumulative incidence function with death as a competing event (Gray’s test). Effect modification by sarcopenia was assessed by including a treatment-by-sarcopenia interaction term in the weighted Cox model. Prespecified subgroup analyses with interaction testing were performed for age, sarcopenia status, Child–Pugh class, BCLC stage, Charlson Comorbidity Index, AFP level, and portal vein tumour thrombus. The non-linear relationship between L3 SMI and OS was explored using restricted cubic spline regression with four knots. Heterogeneity of treatment effect across ICI subtypes was evaluated using the Cochran Q test and I2 statistic.

Sensitivity analyses included IPTW with narrower truncation (5th–95th percentiles), 1:1 propensity score matching (nearest-neighbour, caliper = 0.1 SD), and E-value calculation. In all Cox proportional hazards models, the triple therapy group was set as the reference category; accordingly, a hazard ratio greater than 1 indicates a higher risk of the event (death or progression) in the TACE-alone group relative to triple therapy. All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.1). A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant; subgroup analyses were exploratory without multiplicity adjustment.

Results

Patient selection and baseline characteristics

Between January 2019 and December 2023, a total of 267 patients aged ≥ 65 years with unresectable HCC were screened across seven tertiary centres. After applying the predefined exclusion criteria, 36 patients were excluded: age < 65 years (n = 8), non-HCC or mixed histology (n = 5), Child–Pugh ≥ B8 or class C (n = 6), prior systemic therapy (n = 4), active autoimmune disease (n = 3), history of organ transplantation (n = 2), concurrent active malignancy (n = 4), and incomplete data (n = 4). The final analytic cohort comprised 231 patients, of whom 80 received triple therapy (TACE plus TKI plus ICI) and 151 received TACE alone (Fig. 1).

Sarcopenia as an effect modifier of treatment efficacy

The central finding of this study was that sarcopenia significantly modified the treatment benefit of triple therapy (Table 4; Fig. 4). Among non-sarcopenic patients (n = 131), triple therapy conferred a substantial survival advantage over TACE alone (median OS: 19.3 vs 10.6 months; HR 1.97, 95% CI 1.36–2.85; p < 0.001; Fig. 4A), with clear separation of the Kaplan–Meier curves. In contrast, among sarcopenic patients (n = 100), the survival benefit of triple therapy was markedly attenuated (median OS: 10.4 vs 8.7 months; HR 1.20, 95% CI 0.74–1.93; p = 0.460; Fig. 4B), with near-complete overlap of the survival curves. The treatment-by-sarcopenia interaction was statistically significant (p for interaction = 0.049). Although this p-value is borderline, the marked difference in effect size between strata (HR 1.97 in non-sarcopenic vs 1.20 in sarcopenic patients) together with biological plausibility supports the clinical relevance of this effect modification. The restricted cubic spline analysis of L3 SMI confirmed a non-linear association between skeletal muscle mass and OS (non-linearity test p = 0.037; Supplementary Fig. 2A). The sex-specific cutoffs (42 cm2/m2 for males and 38 cm2/m2 for females) were consistent with the inflection points observed on the spline curves, supporting their validity as clinically meaningful thresholds (Supplementary Fig. 2B).

Notably, besides sarcopenia, Child–Pugh class also emerged as a significant effect modifier (p for interaction = 0.041). Patients with preserved hepatic reserve (Child–Pugh A) derived a clear benefit from triple therapy (HR 1.80, 95% CI 1.31–2.48; p < 0.001), whereas no benefit was observed in patients with Child–Pugh B7 (HR 1.14, 95% CI 0.44–2.98; p = 0.569), although the latter subgroup was small (n = 52).

Subgroup analysis by ICI subtype

To evaluate whether the choice of ICI agent influenced outcomes, we performed subgroup analyses stratified by PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor subtype (Supplementary Table 2). The IPTW-adjusted HRs for OS ranged from 0.46 (tislelizumab) to 0.95 (camrelizumab). No statistically significant heterogeneity was observed across ICI subtypes for either OS (Cochran Q = 5.38, df = 3; p = 0.146; I2 = 44.2%) or PFS (Cochran Q = 5.61, df = 3; p = 0.132; I2 = 46.5%), suggesting that the survival benefit was not driven by a single ICI agent.

Time to hepatic deterioration and liver function preservation

The cumulative incidence of hepatic deterioration (TTHD), analysed using the competing-risk method with death as a competing event, was significantly higher in the TACE-alone group than in the triple therapy group (Gray’s test p = 0.037; Fig. 6A). The Child–Pugh A preservation rate at 6 months was 76.2% in the triple therapy group versus 65.0% in the TACE-alone group (p = 0.069), and at 12 months was 70.2% versus 50.0% (p = 0.004; Fig. 6B). These findings indicate that triple therapy did not accelerate hepatic functional decline in this elderly population and was in fact associated with superior long-term liver function preservation.

Only one patient (1.2%) experienced a grade ≥ 3 irAE. Importantly, the incidence of hepatotoxicity was comparable between groups (13.8% vs 14.6%; p = 1.000), suggesting that the addition of ICI and TKI did not confer additional hepatic risk in this elderly population.

Sensitivity analyses

The robustness of the primary findings was confirmed by multiple sensitivity analyses (Supplementary Table 3). IPTW with narrower truncation (5th–95th percentile) yielded results nearly identical to the primary analysis (OS HR 1.69, 95% CI 1.28–2.23; p < 0.001). Propensity score matching (1:1 nearest-neighbour, caliper = 0.1 SD) in 67 matched pairs demonstrated consistent, albeit slightly attenuated, treatment effects (OS HR 1.42, 95% CI 1.00–2.03; p = 0.050; PFS HR 1.57, 95% CI 1.11–2.22; p = 0.011). The E-value for the primary OS estimate was 2.72 (lower confidence bound 1.83), indicating that an unmeasured confounder would need to be associated with both treatment assignment and mortality by a risk ratio of at least 2.72 to fully explain the observed association, which exceeds the effect sizes of most known prognostic factors in HCC.

Discussion

In this multicenter retrospective cohort study of 231 elderly patients (≥ 65 years) with unresectable HCC, we demonstrated that triple therapy combining TACE with a TKI and an ICI conferred significant survival advantages over TACE alone, with a median OS of 12.6 versus 9.3 months and a median PFS of 6.2 versus 4.2 months after IPTW adjustment. More importantly, we identified sarcopenia as a clinically meaningful effect modifier of the therapeutic benefit: the survival advantage of triple therapy was pronounced in non-sarcopenic patients (HR 1.97; p < 0.001) but was substantially attenuated in those with sarcopenia (HR 1.20; p = 0.460), with a statistically significant treatment–sarcopenia interaction (p = 0.049). To our knowledge, limited data exist regarding whether sarcopenia differentially modifies the efficacy of TACE-based triple therapy in elderly patients with HCC, and this study adds to the growing body of evidence on treatment stratification in this underrepresented population.

The landscape of locoregional–systemic combination therapy for HCC has evolved rapidly15–17,35. The landmark EMERALD-1 trial demonstrated that durvalumab plus bevacizumab combined with TACE significantly improved PFS compared with TACE alone in patients with unresectable HCC amenable to embolisation (median PFS 15.0 vs 8.2 months; HR 0.77)23. Similarly, the LEAP-012 trial showed that the addition of lenvatinib and pembrolizumab to TACE yielded a significant PFS benefit over TACE monotherapy (median PFS 14.6 vs 10.0 months; HR 0.66)24,25. However, these pivotal trials enrolled predominantly younger populations and excluded patients with advanced liver dysfunction, thus limiting generalisability to the elderly. In the present study, the observed treatment effect in the overall cohort (OS HR 1.67; PFS HR 1.86) is broadly consistent with the direction and magnitude of benefits reported in these trials, thereby extending the evidence base to a geriatric population that is increasingly encountered in clinical practice.

The central and most novel finding of our study is the effect-modifying role of sarcopenia on triple therapy efficacy. Among non-sarcopenic elderly patients, triple therapy nearly doubled median OS compared with TACE alone (19.3 vs 10.6 months), whereas in sarcopenic patients, the survival curves nearly overlapped (10.4 vs 8.7 months). This differential effect has considerable clinical implications. The prevalence of sarcopenia in our cohort was 43.3%, which is consistent with published estimates of 36–50% among HCC patients with chronic liver disease36,37. Given that sarcopenia is particularly prevalent in the elderly and in patients with cirrhosis, this finding suggests that a substantial proportion of older HCC patients may not derive meaningful benefit from treatment intensification with triple therapy.

Several biological mechanisms may underpin the attenuated therapeutic efficacy of immunotherapy-based regimens in the setting of sarcopenia38,39. Skeletal muscle functions as an endocrine organ that secretes a repertoire of cytokines collectively termed myokines40,41. Among these, interleukin-15 (IL-15) plays a pivotal role in stimulating the proliferation, activation, and survival of natural killer (NK) cells and CD8 + cytotoxic T lymphocytes, both of which are essential effectors of checkpoint inhibitor–mediated anti-tumour immunity42,43. In patients with sarcopenia, the diminished muscle mass leads to reduced IL-15 production, potentially impairing the expansion and cytolytic capacity of tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes. Furthermore, sarcopenia is associated with a chronic systemic inflammatory milieu characterised by elevated levels of interleukin-6, tumour necrosis factor-α, and C-reactive protein, which promotes T-cell exhaustion—a state defined by the upregulation of inhibitory receptors (PD-1, LAG-3, TIM-3) and the loss of effector function42,44–47. This immune dysfunction may render the tumour microenvironment less responsive to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, thereby diminishing the immunological component of triple therapy.

Beyond immune modulation, sarcopenia may also compromise the tolerability and pharmacokinetic handling of TKIs48,49. Prior studies have demonstrated that patients with sarcopenia have a reduced volume of distribution for lipophilic drugs such as lenvatinib and sorafenib, resulting in higher plasma concentrations and increased susceptibility to dose-limiting toxicities50. In our cohort, TKI dose reduction occurred in 26.2% of the triple therapy group, which may have been more frequent among sarcopenic patients, although the retrospective design precludes definitive attribution48. Suboptimal TKI dosing in the sarcopenic subgroup could partially explain the loss of the anti-angiogenic synergistic benefit that is hypothesised to enhance the efficacy of TACE and immunotherapy51.

An additional notable finding was that Child–Pugh class also emerged as a significant effect modifier (p for interaction = 0.041). Patients with preserved hepatic reserve (Child–Pugh A) derived clear benefit from triple therapy, whereas those with mildly impaired function (Child–Pugh B7) did not52,53. This observation aligns with the well-established principle that adequate liver function is a prerequisite for tolerating multi-agent systemic regimens. Importantly, sarcopenia and hepatic functional reserve are biologically interrelated: the liver plays a central role in protein synthesis, amino acid metabolism, and the regulation of the insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) axis, all of which are critical for skeletal muscle homeostasis54. The convergence of impaired hepatic reserve and sarcopenia may therefore represent a vicious cycle that compounds treatment futility in this vulnerable subpopulation55.

The finding that triple therapy was associated with superior hepatic function preservation (Child–Pugh A maintenance at 12 months: 70.2% vs 50.0%; p = 0.004) and a lower cumulative incidence of hepatic deterioration (Gray’s test p = 0.037) is clinically reassuring. A common concern with adding systemic agents to TACE in the elderly is the potential for accelerated hepatic functional decline. Our data suggest the opposite: by achieving better tumour control and potentially reducing the need for repeated TACE sessions (median 4.0 vs 5.0 sessions), triple therapy may paradoxically preserve hepatic function by minimising cumulative ischaemic injury to the non-tumorous liver parenchyma. This is particularly relevant for elderly patients in whom hepatic functional reserve is often marginal and susceptible to progressive deterioration with repeated embolisation procedures56–58.

The safety profile of triple therapy in our elderly cohort warrants careful consideration. Although any-grade adverse events were more frequent with triple therapy (81.2% vs 60.3%), the incidence of grade ≥ 3 events was not significantly different between groups (26.2% vs 16.6%; p = 0.086). The rate of immune-related adverse events (31.2%) and the low incidence of grade ≥ 3 irAEs (1.2%) are reassuring and are comparable to those reported in younger populations receiving similar regimens59. Notably, the incidence of hepatotoxicity was similar between groups, suggesting that the addition of immunotherapy and targeted therapy did not confer a disproportionate hepatic safety burden in this elderly population. Treatment discontinuation due to adverse events was higher in the triple therapy group (17.5% vs 7.9%), which is expected given the complexity of the regimen, but this did not negate the observed survival benefit in the overall cohort.

The treatment benefit of triple therapy was consistent across most prespecified subgroups, including age dichotomised at 75 years, comorbidity burden, BCLC stage, AFP level, and portal vein tumour thrombus status. The absence of significant heterogeneity across ICI subtypes (Cochran Q p = 0.146; I2 = 44.2%) is noteworthy, as it suggests that the survival benefit was attributable to the class effect of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibition rather than to any individual agent. This has practical implications in real-world settings where the availability of specific ICI agents may vary across institutions and regions.

The robustness of our findings was supported by multiple sensitivity analyses. IPTW with alternative truncation thresholds and propensity score matching both yielded consistent treatment estimates34,60. The E-value of 2.72 for the primary OS estimate indicates that an unmeasured confounder would need to be associated with both treatment selection and mortality by a risk ratio of at least 2.72 to fully account for the observed association, which exceeds the magnitude of effect of most known prognostic factors in HCC, including portal vein invasion and extrahepatic metastasis. While residual confounding cannot be excluded in any observational study, these analyses substantially strengthen the causal inference.

Beyond sarcopenia, other host- and tumour-related factors also shape outcomes in this population. The prognosis of HCC is determined not only by tumour burden but also by the tissue microenvironment and host factors; for example, lymphatic vessel density in the tumour capsule has been associated with postoperative recurrence risk in early-stage disease, underscoring the prognostic contribution of the microenvironment beyond conventional staging61. Although our prespecified subgroup analyses incorporated hepatic functional reserve, tumour burden, performance status, and inflammatory status (NLR) and showed consistent treatment effects across most strata, future prospective work should integrate these dimensions with body-composition assessment into composite geriatric frameworks. Furthermore, because overall survival in real-world cohorts is strongly influenced by therapy received after progression, the role of post-progression strategies—including rechallenge with immune checkpoint inhibitors—merits dedicated evaluation in future studies62.

From a clinical perspective, our findings support a sarcopenia-stratified approach to treatment decision-making in elderly patients with unresectable HCC. For non-sarcopenic elderly patients with preserved liver function, triple therapy should be considered as a viable treatment strategy, as these patients appear to derive a survival benefit comparable to that observed in younger populations in clinical trials. For sarcopenic patients, the attenuated benefit of triple therapy calls into question the justification for treatment intensification and suggests that alternative strategies, such as TACE monotherapy, TACE combined with a single systemic agent, or prehabilitation programmes aimed at reversing sarcopenia before initiating combination therapy, should be explored in future studies. The routine assessment of skeletal muscle mass by CT—which is already performed for tumour staging in HCC—offers a convenient, reproducible, and cost-effective means of incorporating sarcopenia evaluation into clinical decision-making without additional diagnostic burden63.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective observational design is inherently susceptible to selection bias and unmeasured confounding. Although IPTW and multiple sensitivity analyses were employed to minimise these biases, the possibility of residual confounding by unmeasured variables, such as frailty scores, nutritional status, and patient preference, cannot be entirely excluded. Second, the choice of systemic agents (both TKI and ICI) was not standardised but was determined by physician and patient preference, introducing potential heterogeneity. Nevertheless, the subgroup analysis by ICI subtype did not reveal significant differences in treatment effect, and the sensitivity analyses consistently supported the primary findings. Third, the sarcopenia cutoff values were derived from the same cohort using RCS analysis, which may overestimate the prognostic significance of the thresholds; external validation in an independent cohort is warranted. Fourth, sarcopenia was assessed only at baseline; serial measurements during treatment were not available. Sarcopenia is a dynamic condition that may evolve over the course of therapy through treatment-related toxicity, nutritional decline, or conversely through tumour response and improved appetite and a single baseline assessment cannot capture these longitudinal changes in muscle status. Future studies should incorporate serial CT-based or functional assessments at defined time points. In addition, comorbidity burden beyond the Charlson Comorbidity Index, nutritional status assessed by validated instruments, cognitive function, and functional ability were not systematically captured; incomplete ascertainment of these geriatric domains may contribute residual confounding that propensity-based methods cannot fully address. Fifth, the sample size of certain subgroups (particularly Child–Pugh B7 and patients aged ≥ 75 years) was limited, constraining the statistical power for interaction analyses. In addition, data on subsequent therapies administered after disease progression were not systematically collected. In real-world settings, post-progression treatments—including rechallenge with immune checkpoint inhibitors, second-line tyrosine kinase inhibitors, or further locoregional therapy—may substantially influence overall survival and could confound the observed between-group OS difference. Future studies should capture and report subsequent treatment lines to permit a more complete assessment of their cumulative impact on survival. Finally, this study was conducted exclusively at Chinese academic centres, and the generalisability of our findings to non-Asian populations with different aetiological profiles (e.g., alcohol-related or metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease) requires further investigation.

In conclusion, this multicenter study demonstrates that TACE-based triple therapy confers meaningful survival benefits over TACE alone in elderly patients with unresectable HCC, with an acceptable safety profile and superior hepatic function preservation. Crucially, sarcopenia emerges as a significant effect modifier that substantially attenuates the therapeutic benefit of treatment intensification. These findings advocate for the integration of sarcopenia assessment into the clinical decision-making framework for elderly HCC patients, supporting a precision medicine approach that moves beyond tumour-centric staging to encompass host-related factors. Prospective studies incorporating standardised sarcopenia assessment, prehabilitation interventions, and longer follow-up are needed to validate these findings and to determine whether addressing sarcopenia can restore the therapeutic benefit of combination therapies in this vulnerable population.
