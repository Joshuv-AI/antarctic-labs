Optimization problem for star covers of graphs without four cycles
Damjana Kokol Bukovšek , Polona Oblak , Helena Šmigoc

Abstract
. This work presents a study of star covers on graphs. Unlike traditional formulations that minimize the number of stars, our aim is to optimize the number of bipartite components used in the cover. This problem, motivated by a symmetric nonnegative trifactorization of matrices and the SNT-rank of graphs, is in general hard to solve. We consider a family of graphs that do not contain four cycles, and develop an algorithm to determine the SNT-rank of such graphs.



Keywords:

Graph cover;
Star Graph;
Weighted multigraph;
Symmetric matrix tri-factorization;
Nonnegative matrix.

05C35, 05C50, 15A23

1.
Introduction and notation

1.1.
Motivation and related work

This work investigates star covers in graphs, in which we aim to minimize the number of bipartite components required to form the cover. While this setup may at first appear highly specialized, it arises naturally from the study of symmetric nonnegative matrix tri-factorizations.

Matrix factorizations that require entrywise nonnegativity on their latent factors can be used as a tool for discovering structures that are inherently additive and physically interpretable.
Studying the support pattern of a matrix provides lower bounds on various factorization ranks defined through nonnegative factorization,
[
2
,
12
,
13
]
.
Covering problems extend to broader matrix questions involving ranks and eigenvalues under structural constraints. For example, in the classic inverse eigenvalue problem for graphs, determining the minimum rank of a matrix family is closely connected to its path cover number,
[
7
,
11
]
.

The Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF) is the most common such factorization. In the approximate NMF framework, a given nonnegative matrix
V
is decomposed into nonnegative factors
W
and
H
such that
V\approx WH
,
[
10
]
, while the exact NMF requires that
V=WH
,
[
1
]
. The nonnegative rank of a matrix
V
is the minimal number of columns of
W
over all such factorizations of
V
. The existence of an exact NMF of rank
k
for a matrix
V
requires the existence of a Boolean factorization of rank
r\leq k
for its support pattern. In particular, the Boolean rank of a matrix’s zero-nonzero pattern serves as a combinatorial lower bound for its nonnegative rank. In addition, Boolean Matrix Factorization (BMF) can inform the construction of Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF) models, when the approximate decomposition requires specific entries to remain zero to preserve the underlying support of the data, and is of independent interest for its capacity to extract interpretable, set-based patterns from discrete data,
[
3
]
. For an overview of all the concepts mentioned above, we refer the reader to
[
6
]
.

When dealing with symmetric data, this framework is mirrored by Symmetric Nonnegative Matrix Tri-Factorization (SN-Trifactorization). SN-Trifactorization approximates a symmetric
n\times n
input matrix
A
as the product of nonnegative matrices: an
n\times r
matrix
B
and a symmetric
r\times r
matrix
C
, such that
A=BCB^{\top}
(for exact factorization), or
A\approx BCB^{\top}
(for approximate factorization). This framework has applications across a variety of data science applications, such as topic modeling and community detection,
[
14
]
.
The symmetric nonnegative rank of a matrix
A
, denoted by
\mathrm{st}_{+}(A)
, is defined as the minimum number of columns in
B
required to satisfy this equality across all such factorizations, see
[
8
]
. Analogous to the asymmetric case, a combinatorial lower bound for this rank is provided by the equivalent factorization under Boolean arithmetic.

By treating the pattern of
A
as a graph
G
, we can cast this setup as a combinatorial problem on graphs. This setup was first explored in
[
9
]
, where the
SNT-rank of a graph
G
is defined as follows. Let
G
be a graph that allows loops on
n
vertices with
V(G)=[n]
and let
\mathcal{S}^{+}(G)
be the set of all symmetric nonnegative
n\times n
matrices
A=[a_{ij}]
with
a_{ij}>0
if and only if
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. We define the SNT-rank of a graph
G
as

\mathrm{st}_{+}(G):=\min\{\mathrm{st}_{+}(A)\colon A\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(G)\}.

In this paper, we consider the gap of the graph
G
, an equivalent parameter defined as

\gap(G):=|V(G)|-\mathrm{st}_{+}(G).

If
G
is an empty graph, then we define
\gap(G)=0
.
Determining the value of
\mathrm{st}_{+}(G)
reduces to minimizing the number of components required in a specific cover (called set-join cover) of
G
. A formal definition of set-join covers and their components, skipped here for brevity, can be found at the beginning of Section
2
.

This optimization problem simplifies significantly when restricted to the class of graphs that exclude a specific set of forbidden subgraphs listed in Figure
2
. In such cases, the decomposition requires only the simplest form of set-joins—those corresponding to star graphs. While our approach uses star covers, we emphasize an essential difference with more common optimization problem: rather than minimizing the total number of stars, we focus on minimizing the number of components required to achieve the cover. This difference is highlighted on a small graph in our first example below.

1.2.
Structure of the paper

We organize the paper as follows. Sections
2
and
3
develop several observations concerning
\gap

2
treats the general case, while Section
3
focuses on graphs that exclude certain
4

shows that, in this restricted setting,
\gap(G)
depends only on the parity of path lengths in
G
.
Motivated by this result, Section
4
introduces a weighted multigraph

with binomial edge weights, together with the parameter
\gap^{*}(\kappa(G))

\gap(G)
.
In Sections
5
and
6
, we study general weighted multigraphs

with binomial weights and present several graph operations that preserve

(possibly with loops), in which all edge weights are zero and all vertex degrees are at least three, while maintaining control over

.
Since

.

1.3.
Definitions and notation

The term graph in this paper will always mean a simple graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
that allows loops. Edges
e\in E(G)
are subsets of
V(G)
of the form
e=\{u,v\}
. If the edge is of the form
\{u,u\}
, it means that a vertex
u
has a loop.
Below, we list some basic notions associated with graphs that we will depend on:

•

By
G\cong H
we denote that graphs
G
and
H
are isomorphic.

•

Let
P_{n}
denote the path on
n
vertices,
C_{n}
a cycle on
n
vertices,
K_{n}
a complete graph on
n
vertices and
K_{m,n}
a complete bipartite graph on
m+n
vertices, all without loops.

•

Vertices
u,v\in V(G)
are
neighbours
if
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
.
The neighbourhood of
v
in
G
, denoted by
N_{G}[v]
, is the set of all neighbours of
v
. Note that vertex
v\in V(G)
has a loop if and only if
v\in N_{G}[v]
.

•

Vertices
u
and
v
are
twin vertices
if
N_{G}[u]=N_{G}[v]
.

•

If a vertex
v\in V(G)
does not have a loop, then the
degree of
v
in
G
equals the cardinality of its neighbourhood, i.e.,
\deg_{G}(v)=|N_{G}[v]|
. If a vertex
v\in V(G)
has a loop, then
\deg_{G}(v)=|N_{G}[v]|+1
, i.e., loops count twice towards the degree.

•

A vertex
v\in V(G)
is called a
leaf
if
|N_{G}[u]|=1
and is called
a high degree vertex
if
\deg_{G}(v)\geq 3
. We denote by
V_{d\geq 3}(G)
the set of all high degree vertices of

, by
V_{d=2}(G)
the set of all degree two vertices, by
V_{d=1}(G)
the set of all leaves, by
V_{d=0}(G)
the set of all vertices of degree
0
, and by
V_{d\neq 2}(G)=V(G)\setminus V_{d=2}(G)
.

•

Removing a set of vertices
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
from a simple graph
G
is a well-known operation in which we remove all vertices
v_{i}
,
i\in[k]
together with all edges
\{v_{i},u\}
,
u\in N_{G}[v_{i}]
,
i\in[k]
. We denote this graph as
G(\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\})
.
When convenient, we write
G(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
instead of
G(\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\})
.
Furthermore, the subgraph of
G
induced on the set
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
is denoted by
G[\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}]
.

•

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. If for
u,v\in V(G)
and for distinct vertices
x_{i}\in V_{d=2}(G)\setminus\{u,v\}
,
i\in[k]
, there exists a walk
u-x_{1}-\ldots-x_{k}-v
, we abbreviate it by
{u}\;\overset{{k}}{\text{---}}\;{v}
.

•

If
G
contains
w-{u}\;\overset{{k}}{\text{---}}\;{v}
for
k\geq 0
,
w\in V_{d\geq 3}(G)
,
u\in V_{d=2}(G)
and
v\in V_{d=1}(G)
, then we call
{u}\;\overset{{k}}{\text{---}}\;{v}
a pendent path
P_{k+2}
at
w
.

We follow
[
4
]
and consider undirected
multigraphs

\Gamma=(V(\Gamma),E(\Gamma))
, in which to every
e\in E(\Gamma)
we assign one or two endpoints from the set of vertices
V(\Gamma)
.
Note that this definition allows several edges between a pair of distinct vertices and several loops at vertex
u
, which we denote as
\{u,u\}
. If a multigraph
\Gamma
is
weighted
, then
\Gamma=(V(\Gamma),E(\Gamma),w_{\Gamma})
, where
w_{\Gamma}\colon E(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{R}
is a function that assigns a weight to each edge.

If a vertex of a (weighted) multigraph is incident to
s
loops and
t
non-looped edges, then we define its
d

\deg_{\Gamma}(v)=t+2s
. (For example,

\deg_{\Gamma}(w)=6
and
\deg_{\Gamma}(v)=4
for vertices
w,v\in V(\Gamma)
in a weighted multigraph
\Gamma
shown in Figure
4
.)

The
edge contraction along an edge

with different endpoints

and
v
is a graph obtained from
\Gamma
by identifying vertices
u
and
v
(into a single vertex), deleting the edge

and preserving all other incidences (and weights in the case of a weighted multigraph).

Other definitions from simple graphs to multigraphs extend in a natural way.

2.
Initial results on gap

Before limiting our analysis to graphs without forbidden subgraphs, we offer a few preliminary results on the gap of graphs satisfying a local assumption.

SNT-rank of a graph
G
can be described combinatorially using set-join covers of the graph. Let
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{L}
be two nonempty subsets of
V(G)
with possibly nonempty intersection. We define
the set-join of
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{L}
, denoted by
\mathcal{K}\vee\mathcal{L}
, to be the graph with
V(\mathcal{K}\vee\mathcal{L})=V(G)
, and
E(\mathcal{K}\vee\mathcal{L})=\{\{i,j\}\colon i\in\mathcal{K},j\in\mathcal{L}\}
.
Note that
\mathcal{K}\vee\mathcal{L}
has a loop on vertex
i
precisely when
i\in\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{L}
. We say that
\mathscr{C}=\{\mathcal{K}_{i}\vee\mathcal{L}_{i}\colon i\in[t]\}
is
a set-join cover
of
G
if

E(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}E(\mathcal{K}_{i}\vee\mathcal{L}_{i})
.
For a set-join cover
\mathscr{C}
of graph
G
the component set of
\mathscr{C}
is

V(\mathscr{C})=\{\mathcal{K}_{i}\colon i\in[t]\}\cup\{\mathcal{L}_{i}\colon i\in[t]\}

and we call each element of
V(\mathscr{C})

a component of
\mathscr{C}
.
The order
of
\mathscr{C}
is the number of components of
\mathscr{C}
, i.e.
|V(\mathscr{C})|
.
In
[
9
, Theorem 2.12]
it is proven that

\mathrm{st}_{+}(G)=\min\{|V(\mathscr{C})|\colon\mathscr{C}\text{ a set-join cover of }G\}.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a set-join cover of
G
of order
\mathrm{st}_{+}(G)
, then we say that
\mathscr{C}
is an
optimal set-join cover
of
G
.

We recall selected results from
[
9
]
, needed in the sequel. While these were originally stated in terms of
\mathrm{st}_{+}(G)
, we restate them here using
\gap(G)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

(1)

(2)

If

(3)

\gap(P_{n})=1

(4)

\gap(C_{4})=2

Remark 2.2
.

Item (
1
) in Proposition
2.1
reduces the problem of determining the gap for graphs that contain a leaf to a smaller graph, and gives an algorithmic way to compute the gap for trees.
If
T
is a tree with at least two vertices, then repeated application of this result can be used to prove that
\gap(T)=0
if and only if
T
is obtained from
P_{2}
by consecutively adding a pendent
P_{2}
to any vertex in the existing graph.

To verify this, assume first that
T
is obtained from
P_{2}
by consecutively adding a pendent
P_{2}
to a vertex. Since
\gap(P_{2})=0
by Proposition
2.1
(
3
), it follows by Proposition
2.1
(
1

\gap(T)=0
.

Suppose that
\gap(T)=0
. If

, then

and the statement follows by Proposition
2.1
(
3
). Assume now that
|V(T)|\geq 3
and that all trees on at most
|V(T)|-1
vertices of gap
0
are obtained from
P_{2}
by consecutively adding a pendent
P_{2}
to a vertex. Let
v
and
w
be two leaves of
T
, such that the distance between them is equal to the diameter of
T
, and let
v_{1}
be the unique neighbor of
v
. We claim that
\deg_{T}(v_{1})=2
. Indeed,
\deg_{T}(v_{1})\geq 3
and the assumption that

does not contain any cycles, implies that there exists
v^{\prime}\in N_{T}[v_{1}]
,
v^{\prime}\neq v
, with
d(v^{\prime},w)=d(v,w)=\operatorname{diam}(T)
.
Note,
v^{\prime}
is not a leaf, since the assumptions that
\gap(T)=0
implies that

has no twin vertices, by Proposition

(

). Therefore there exists a vertex

, such that

, a contradiction.

In particular there exists a vertex

so that

and

is
P_{2}
a pendent path at
v_{2}
in

By item (

) in Proposition

,

is a tree on

. By the induction hypothesis,

is obtained from

by consecutively adding to a vertex a pendent

In the proposition below, we restrict our attention to graphs that do not contain a certain closed

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a graph containing two vertex disjoint induced subgraphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
so that
V(G)=V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
, and there do not exist vertices
u_{1},v_{1}\in V(G_{1})
and
u_{2},v_{2}\in V(G_{2})
with and
\{u_{i},v_{j}\}\in E(G)
for all
i,j\in\{1,2\}
.
Then

\gap(G)\leq\gap(G_{1})+\gap(G_{2}).

In particular, if
\gap(G_{i})=0
for
i=1,2
, then
\gap(G)=0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an optimal set-join cover of
G
, and let
\mathscr{C}_{i}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
,
i=1,2
, be the subset of
\mathscr{C}
containing all the elements of
\mathscr{C}
that cover at least one edge in
G_{i}
. Clearly,
|V(\mathscr{C}_{i})|\geq\mathrm{st}_{+}(G_{i})
.

We claim that
V(\mathscr{C}_{1})\cap V(\mathscr{C}_{2})=\emptyset
. Assuming the opposite, let
\mathcal{K}\in V(\mathscr{C}_{1})\cap V(\mathscr{C}_{2})
. Then for
i=1,2
: there exists
u_{i}\in\mathcal{K}\cap V(G_{i})
and a component
\mathcal{L}_{i}\in V(\mathscr{C}_{i})
so that
\mathcal{K}\vee\mathcal{L}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}_{i}
. In particular, there exist
v_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{i}\cap V(G_{i})
. From
\mathcal{K}\vee\mathcal{L}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}
we conclude that
\{u_{i},v_{j}\}\in E(G)
for all
i,j=1,2
, a contradiction.

Since
V(\mathscr{C}_{1})\cup V(\mathscr{C}_{2})\subseteq V(\mathscr{C})
, this implies
\mathrm{st}_{+}(G)\geq\mathrm{st}_{+}(G_{1})+\mathrm{st}_{+}(G_{2})
and thus also
\gap(G)\leq\gap(G_{1})+\gap(G_{2})
.
∎

Example 2.4
.

Note that the inequality in Proposition
2.3
can be strict. Let

with vertices

in the natural cycle order. Let

be induced subgraphs with

. Then

are both isomorphic to

, and

.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
G
be a graph containing an induced subgraph

isomorphic to

,

, where vertices

are indexed in a natural cycle order, and

.
Then

\gap(G)=\gap(G(V(H))).

Proof.

Let

. Observe first that we have
\gap(G)\leq\gap(G(W))+\gap(H)=\gap(G(W))
since

satisfy the hypothesis of Proposition

Now, let

be an optimal set-join cover of

, and
\mathscr{C}_{1}:=\{\{v_{2t-1}\}\vee N_{G}[v_{2t-1}]\colon t\in[k]\}

.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

(2)

(b)

(3)

is an optimal set-join cover of

intersect in at most one vertex of

To prove item (1), suppose

,

. Then there exist

and

, so that

, contradicting the assumption

.

For item (2) assume that

with

,

. If

, then

. As this contradicts

) follows.
If
\{v_{1}\}\vee\mathcal{L}\in\mathscr{C}

and

is the only element in the optimal set-join cover

with a component

by (

is a set-join cover of

For item (3) suppose that

Corollary 3.3
.

For
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\square\mkern-12.0mu\text{✗}}
, let
\mathscr{C}
be a set-join cover of
G
,
\mathcal{K}\in V(\mathscr{C})
with

, and
\{v\}\vee\mathcal{K}\in\mathscr{C}

Proof.

Clearly,

Consider
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\square\mkern-12.0mu\text{✗}}
with a set-join cover
\mathscr{C}
. Let

be the set of all vertices

with

, and let

. Let

that contains all the set-joins of

Definition 3.4
.

Proposition 3.5

For every

Proof.

Starting with a set-join cover

of

of

with the desired property and

First consider

with

with

. Construct

by replacing all the set-joins in

Remark 3.6

Below we list a few straightforward observations for an

of

7.
Computing
\gap^{*}(\Gamma)
with vertex removal

The algorithm developed in Section

allows us to compute the gap in many but not all cases.
In this section we develop a complementary method to compute
\gap^{*}(\Gamma)
of a reduced multigraph
\tau(\Gamma)
inductively by removing vertices. This method has high computational complexity but it allows us to compute the gap of for every graph
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\square\mkern-12.0mu\text{✗}}
.

To prove the main result of this section, Theorem
7.6
, we need to return to simple graphs and their gaps.

7.1.
Vertex removal for simple graphs

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with
|V(G)|\geq 2
, and
v\in V(G)
. Then

(4)

\gap(G(v))-1\leq\gap(G)\leq\gap(G(v))+1.

Moreover, if
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\square\mkern-12.0mu\text{✗}}
and there exists an
\mathrm{ABC}
cover
\mathscr{C}
of

such that

is of Type A with respect to
\mathscr{C}
, then the left inequality is an equality.

Proof.

Note that (
4
) is equivalent to

\mathrm{st}_{+}(G(v))+2\geq\mathrm{st}_{+}(G)\geq\mathrm{st}_{+}(G(v)).

The inequality
\mathrm{st}_{+}(G)\geq\mathrm{st}_{+}(G(v))
follows from the fact that
G(v)
is an induced subgraph of
G
. If we add
\{v\}\vee N_{G}[v]
to a set-join cover
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
of
G(v)
, we get a set-join cover of

. As this introduces at most two new components, this implies
\mathrm{st}_{+}(G(v))+2\geq\mathrm{st}_{+}(G)
.

Now, assume that
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\square\mkern-12.0mu\text{✗}}
and there exists an ABC cover
\mathscr{C}
of
G
, where
\mathcal{K}\vee\{v\}\in\mathscr{C}
with
|\mathcal{K}|\geq 2
. By Corollary
3.3
there exists a set-join cover
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
of
G(v)
with
|V(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})|\leq|V(\mathscr{C})|-2
. Hence,
\mathrm{st}_{+}(G(v))\leq\mathrm{st}_{+}(G)-2
, as desired.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

If
|V(G)|\geq 2
and there exist

and

Next, we express the gap of a graph

7.2.
Vertex removal for multigraphs

In this section, we use Lemma
7.3
to prove a similar result for weighted multigraphs.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\Gamma=(V(\Gamma),E(\Gamma),w_{\Gamma})
be an nonempty weighted multigraph with all weights equal to
0
and
v\in V(\Gamma)
. Then

\gap^{*}(\Gamma)\geq\gap^{*}(\Gamma(v))-1.

Proof.

Since
\gap^{*}(\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2})=\gap^{*}(\Gamma_{1})+\gap^{*}(\Gamma_{2})
we may assume without loss of generality that
\Gamma
is connected. If
\Gamma
contains only one vertex, then
\gap^{*}(\Gamma(v))=0
and the claim is true. So suppose
|V(\Gamma)|\geq 2
. Since
\Gamma
contains only edges of weight
0
, we have
\Gamma(v)=\Gamma_{-v}
by Remark
5.4
, so

\gap^{*}(\Gamma)=\gap(\zeta(\Gamma))\geq\gap(\zeta(\Gamma)(v))-1=\gap^{*}(\Gamma_{-v})-1=\gap^{*}(\Gamma(v))-1

by Lemmas
7.1
and
5.5
.
∎

Let
\Gamma
be a weighted multigraph. A set
\mathcal{I}\subseteq V(\Gamma)
is called
independent
, if the subgraph of
\Gamma
induced on
\mathcal{I}
contains no edges. The cardinality of the biggest independent set in
\Gamma
is called
independence number
and denoted by
\alpha(\Gamma)
. The independence number
\alpha(\Gamma)
can be used to find a lower bound on the
\gap^{*}(\Gamma)
.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a weighted multigraph with all weights equal to
0
. For any vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in V(\Gamma)
we have

\gap^{*}(\Gamma)\geq\gap^{*}(\Gamma(\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}))-k.

In particular,

(7)

\gap^{*}(\Gamma)\geq 2\alpha(\Gamma)-|V(\Gamma)|.

Proof.

The first claim follows from Lemma
7.4
by induction on
k
. Let
I
be maximal independent set in
\Gamma
, let
k=|V(\Gamma)|-\alpha(\Gamma)
and
V(\Gamma)\setminus I=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
. Then

\gap^{*}(\Gamma)\geq\gap^{*}(\Gamma(\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}))-k=\alpha(\Gamma)-(|V(\Gamma)|-\alpha(\Gamma))=2\alpha(\Gamma)-|V(\Gamma)|,

since
\Gamma(\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\})
contains
\alpha(\Gamma)
vertices and no edges.
∎

The above estimate of
\gap^{*}
in terms of independence number is sharp for some families of graphs (see e.g. Example
8.4
) while in general
\gap^{*}(\Gamma)-2\alpha(\Gamma)+|V(\Gamma)|
can be arbitrarily large (see e.g. Example
8.5
).

Let
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\square\mkern-12.0mu\text{✗}}
, and
\Gamma=\tau(\kappa(G))
. (
\Gamma
is a simple multigraph with all weights equal to
0
and each vertex of degree at least 3.) By Theorem
6.12
we have:

\gap(G)=\gap^{*}(\Gamma)+t(\kappa(G)).

To compute
\gap^{*}(\Gamma)
we may assume that
\Gamma
is a connected graph. In our examples, we will apply the following theorem only to multigraphs of the form
\Gamma=\tau(\kappa(G))
, but we state and prove it more generally.

Theorem 7.6
