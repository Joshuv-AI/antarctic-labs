On semilinear Grushin–Schrödinger equation in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N}
J. Carvalho, A. Viana

Abstract
. We establish the existence of nontrivial nonnegative weak solutions to the following equation − Δ γ ​ u + V ⁡ ( z ) ​ u = Q ⁡ ( z ) ​ f ​ ( u ) , z ∈ ℝ N , -\Delta_{\gamma}u+V(z)u=Q(z)f(u),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}, where Δ γ \Delta_{\gamma} denotes the so-called Grushin-type operator in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . The potentials V V and Q Q are assumed to be controlled below and above, respectively, by functions of type ( 1 + | z | ) a (1+|z|)^{a} , a ∈ ℝ a\in\mathbb{R} . The main result is the embedded of the space E V γ E_{V}^{\gamma} into the weighted Lebesgue space L Q p ​ ( ℝ N ) L_{Q}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) , under suitable conditions. Finally, we derive regularity results for the obtained weak solutions.

1.
Introduction and main results

Consider the Grushin-type operator
\Delta_{\gamma}
defined by

(1.1)

\Delta_{\gamma}u(z):=\Delta_{x}u(z)+|x|^{2\gamma}\Delta_{y}u(z),

where
\gamma>0
,
z=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, with
m+k=N\geq 3
, and
\Delta_{x}
and
\Delta_{y}
denote the classical Laplacians with respect to the variables
x
and
y
, respectively. The operator defined in (
1.1
) was introduced in
[
15
,
16
]
.
Such operators naturally arise in degenerate and anisotropic diffusion problems.
For integer values of
\gamma
, these operators belong to the general class of Hörmander operators
[
17
]
. For positive real values of
\gamma
, they represent the simplest example of the
\Delta_{\lambda}
-operators introduced by Kogoj and Lanconelli in
[
18
]
, and they also fall into the broader family of
X
-elliptic operators studied in
[
19
]
. Franchi and Lanconelli were the first to introduce and study a metric and the associated underlying sub-Riemannian structure for these types of operators in their seminal works of the early 1980s
[
12
,
13
]
.

Degenerate second-order elliptic operators have remained an object of interest in recent years, see e.g.
[
1
,
6
,
8
]
. On the other hand, the study of semilinear equations involving these type of operators is still developing, in particular, there are relatively few results concerning semilinear elliptic equations with Grushin-type operators, see e.g.
[
4
,
3
,
7
,
11
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

We mention two recent contributions in this direction. In
[
22
]
, Loiudice provides the asymptotic behavior of solutions - both near the singularity and at infinity - for the Dirichlet problem of the form

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\gamma}u-\mu\dfrac{\psi^{2}}{d^{2}}u=K(z)|u|^{2^{*}_{\gamma}-2}u,&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

with
2^{*}_{\gamma}=\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{N_{\gamma}-2}
,
N_{\gamma}=m+(\gamma+1)k
, and
\psi:=\left|\nabla_{\gamma}d\right|
, where

d(z)=\left(|x|^{2(\gamma+1)}+(\gamma+1)^{2}|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2(\gamma+1)}}\mbox{\quad and\quad}\nabla_{\gamma}d=(\nabla_{x}d,|x|^{\gamma}\nabla_{y}d).

Here
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
0\in\Omega
,
K\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and
0\leq\mu<\left(\frac{N_{\gamma}-2}{2}\right)^{2}
.

In
[
2
]
, Alves and de Holanda proved the
existence of nontrivial nonnegative solutions to the semilinear elliptic equation

(1.2)

-\Delta_{\gamma}u+a(z)u=f(u),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

with
N\geq 3
, under the following assumptions on the potential
a
:

(1)

a(z)\geq a_{0}>0,\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
;

(2)

a(x,y)=a\left(x^{\prime},y\right)
whenever
|x|=\left|x^{\prime}\right|
;

(3)

either the function
a(x,y)
periodic in the
y
-variable
y
:

a(x,y)=a\left(x,y+y_{0}\right),\text{ for all }y_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k},

or coercive in
y
, that is,

a(x,y)\longrightarrow\infty,\text{ as }|y|\longrightarrow\infty,\text{ uniformly for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Furthermore, the authors also consider the case
a(z)=0
, known as the zero mass case.

The main goal of the present work is to establish the compact embedding

E_{V}^{\gamma}\hookrightarrow L^{p}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

under suitable conditions on
V
and
Q
. As consequence, we prove the existence of nonnegative weak solutions to the equation

(
\mathcal{P}
)

-\Delta_{\gamma}u+V(z)u=Q(z)f(u),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

In this work, we assume instead the following assumptions on the potentials:

(
V
)

V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function and there exist constants
C_{V}>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{C_{V}}{\left(1+|z|\right)^{\alpha}}\leq V(z),\quad\mbox{for a.e. }z\in\mathbb{R}^{N};

(
Q
)

Q:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function and there there exist constants
C_{Q}>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

0<Q(z)\leq\frac{C_{Q}}{\left(1+|z|\right)^{\beta}},\quad\mbox{for a.e. }z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Our problem also differs from (
1.2
) due to the presence of the potential
Q(z)
multiplying
f(u)
; besides that, we adopt distinct assumptions on
V
, without requiring any symmetry, which prevents us from following Strauss’s approach
[
27
]
by proving a Radial Lemma, as employed in
[
28
]
. Instead, we decompose the exterior domain into annular regions - a technique similar to the one presented in
[
26
]
and successfully applied in
[
10
,
9
]
. The same technique was used by Ambrosetti
et al.

[
5
]
for a similar class of nonlinear Schrödinger equations involving the Laplacian rather than the Grushin operator. To apply this technique, we prove an auxiliary Lemma which relies on the Sobolev embeddings obtained by Kogoj and Lanconelli
[
18
]
: given a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
p\in[1,2^{*}_{\gamma}]
, then

H_{0}^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega).

Here,

H_{0}^{1,\gamma}(\Omega):=\overline{C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{H^{1,\gamma}}},

and

(1.3)

\|u\|_{H^{1,\gamma}}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}\ \,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

As Sobolev embeddings are naturally of interest, we mention that, in the case of the whole space, Monti and Morbidelli
[
25
]
write that the Sobolev embedding is a consequence of the Poincaré inequality and the representation formula proved in
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
, respectively:

(1.4)

H^{1,\gamma}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),

where
2_{\gamma}^{*}=\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{N_{\gamma}-2}
. See also
[
24
]
. More recently, Abatangelo, Ferrero and Luzzini
[
1
]
studied the asymptotic behaviors and unique continuation principles for weak solutions to the linear Grushin equation with potential in bounded domains, that is, (

P

), with
Q\equiv 0
. They also provide Sobolev embeddings under certain conditions.

Next, we make precise the spaces we are going to work with. We consider
E^{\gamma}_{V}
as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{E^{\gamma}_{V}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}+V(z)u^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This norm is induced by the following inner product:

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\nabla_{\gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}v+V(z)uv\right)\,\mathrm{d}z,

and hence
E^{\gamma}_{V}
is a Hilbert space.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we define

L^{p}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\ \mbox{measurable}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z<\infty\right\},

with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{L^{p}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Our main results are the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Weighted Sobolev Embedding)
.

Assume that

(
V
)

and

(
Q
)

hold. Then the embedding
E_{V}^{\gamma}\hookrightarrow L^{p}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuous if one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\alpha\leq N<\beta
and
2\leq p\leq 2_{\gamma}^{*}
;

(ii)

N\leq\alpha<\beta
and
2\leq p\leq\overline{p}
,
where

\overline{p}:=\min\left\{\frac{2(\beta-N)}{(\alpha-N)},2_{\gamma}^{*}\right\};

(iii)

\alpha\leq N
,
\underline{\beta}\leq\beta<N
, and
\underline{p}\leq p\leq 2_{\gamma}^{*}
,
where

\underline{\beta}:=\max\left\{N-\frac{2_{\gamma}^{*}(N-2)}{2},N-\frac{2_{\gamma}^{*}(N-\alpha)}{2}\right\}

and

\underline{p}:=\max\left\{2,\frac{2(N-\beta)}{(N-2)},\frac{2(N-\beta)}{(N-\alpha)}\right\}.

Furthermore, the embedding is compact whenever:

(i’)

\alpha\leq N<\beta
and
2\leq p<2_{\gamma}^{*}
;

(ii’)

N<\alpha\leq\beta
and
2\leq p<\overline{p}
.

We say that a function
u\in E_{V}^{\gamma}
is a weak solution to problem (

P

), if for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\nabla_{\gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi+V(z)u\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}z=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)f(u)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}z.

Theorem
1.1
helps us to prove the existence of nontrivial nonnegative weak solutions, which is Theorem
1.2
below. In its proof, we will consider only the case
\alpha\leq N<\beta
, since the other cases are similar. Moreover,
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and we shall assume that

(
f_{1}
)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\dfrac{f(s)}{s}=0
;

(
f_{2}
)

there exists
q\in\left(2,2_{\gamma}^{*}\right)
such that

\limsup_{|s|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|f(s)|}{|s|^{q-1}}<\infty;

(
f_{3}
)

there exists
\theta>2
such that

0<\theta F(s)\leq f(s)s,\quad\forall\,s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\},

where
F(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
.

Before stating the next result, we define the space

W_{\gamma}^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\left\{u\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right):|x|^{|\beta|\gamma}D^{\alpha}_{x}D^{\beta}_{y}u\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),\ \mbox{for multi-index}\ |\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 2\right\}.

In particular, denoting
|x|^{|\beta|\gamma}D^{\alpha}_{x}D^{\beta}_{y}u
by
D_{\gamma}^{\alpha}u
, we will endow
W_{\gamma}^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the norm

(1.5)

\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{\gamma}}=\sum_{|\alpha|=2}\|D_{\gamma}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\left\|\nabla_{\gamma}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

See Metafune, Negro and Spina
[
23
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We first prove an auxiliary result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2^{*}_{\gamma}
,
R>0
, and
u\in E^{\gamma}_{V}
. Then, there exists
C_{0}:=C_{0}(p,R)>0
such that

(2.1)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C_{0}\|u\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}\ ,

where
B_{R}
denotes Euclidean ball

B_{R}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|z|<R\right\}.

Proof.

Let
u\in E_{V}^{\gamma}
. Consider
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(B_{2R}\right)
such that

(2.2)

0\leqslant\varphi\leqslant 1\ \mbox{ in}\ B_{2R},\ \varphi\equiv 1\ \mbox{ in }B_{R},\mbox{ and }|\nabla\varphi|\leqslant C\ \mbox{ in }B_{2R}.

In particular,
|\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi|\leq C_{1}
in
B_{2R}
. We claim that
\varphi u\in H_{0}^{1,\gamma}\left(B_{2R}\right)
. In fact, we must prove that there exists
\left(\psi_{n}\right)_{n}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}\left(B_{2R}\right)
such that

\left\|\nabla_{\gamma}\psi_{n}-\nabla_{\gamma}(\varphi u)\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. By definition of
E_{V}^{\gamma}
, there exists a sequence
(u_{n})_{n}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
satisfying
\|u_{n}-u\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Setting
\psi_{n}:=\varphi u_{n}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2R})
, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\gamma}\psi_{n}-\nabla_{\gamma}\left(\varphi u\right)

\displaystyle=[\varphi\nabla_{\gamma}u_{n}+u_{n}\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi]-[\varphi\nabla_{\gamma}u+u\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi]

\displaystyle=\varphi\left[\nabla_{\gamma}u_{n}-\nabla_{\gamma}u\right]+\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi\left[u_{n}-u\right].

Accordingly, we use the conditions on
\varphi
, the above identity, and the assumption

(
V
)

to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\gamma}\psi_{n}-\nabla_{\gamma}\left(\varphi u\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla_{\gamma}\left(u_{n}-u\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}^{2}+\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{\inf_{B_{2R}}V(z)}\int_{B_{2R}}V(z)|u_{n}-u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{2}\to 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. We have proved our claim.

Now, we can apply the embedding by Kogoj and Lanconelli
[
18
, Theorem 3.3]
reformulated in terms of
H_{0}^{1,\gamma}(B_{2R})
:

(2.3)

H_{0}^{1,\gamma}(B_{2R})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(B_{2R}).

Thus,

(2.4)

\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}z=\int_{B_{R}}|\varphi u|^{p}\mathrm{d}z\leq\int_{B_{2R}}|\varphi u|^{p}\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{3}\left(\int_{B_{2R}}|\nabla_{\gamma}(\varphi u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

So,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\left(\int_{B_{2R}}\left[u^{2}\left|\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi\right|^{2}+\varphi^{2}\left|\nabla_{\gamma}u\right|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\left(\int_{B_{2R}}\left[\left|\nabla_{\gamma}u\right|^{2}+V(z)u^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C_{5}\|u\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{p}.

It proves (
2.1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Observe that we can write

(2.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z=\int_{B_{1}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z.

From

(
Q
)

and (
2.1
) (with
R=1
), it holds that

(2.7)

\int_{B_{1}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{Q}\int_{B_{1}}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{1}\|u\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{p}.

It remains to estimate the second integral on
the right-hand side of (
2.6
). To do this, for each
j\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
, define the annulus

A_{j}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:2^{j}<|z|<2^{j+1}\}.

Notice that

(2.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{A_{j}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z.

From condition

(
Q
)

, we can estimate

\int_{A_{j}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{Q}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|)^{\beta}}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z\\
\leq\frac{C_{Q}}{2^{\beta j}}\int_{A_{j}}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z.

By using the change of variables
w=2^{-j}z
, we get

\int_{A_{j}}|u(z)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z=\int_{A_{0}}|u(2^{j}w)|^{p}\ 2^{Nj}\,\mathrm{d}w=2^{Nj}\int_{A_{0}}|u_{j}(w)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}w,

where

\int_{A_{j}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq\frac{C_{Q}}{2^{(\beta-N)j}}\int_{A_{0}}|u_{j}(w)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}w.

Applying (
2.5
) (the first inequality), with
R=2
and using the fact that
A_{0}\subset B_{2}
, one has

(2.10)

\int_{A_{0}}|u_{j}(w)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}w\leq C_{2}\left(\int_{B_{4}}\left[\left|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{j}(w)\right|^{2}+u_{j}^{2}(w)\right]\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Recalling that if
w=(x,y)
and
z=(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})=(2^{j}x,2^{j}y)=2^{j}w
, the identity

\nabla_{\gamma}u_{j}(w)=\left(2^{j}\nabla_{x}u(2^{j}w),2^{j}|x|^{\gamma}\nabla_{y}u(2^{j}w)\right)

gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{j}(w)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=2^{2j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[|\nabla_{x}u(2^{j}w)|^{2}+|x|^{2\gamma}|\nabla_{y}u(2^{j}w)|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=2^{(2-N)j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[|\nabla_{x}u(z)|^{2}+|2^{-j}\widetilde{x}|^{2\gamma}|\nabla_{y}u(z)|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}z.

So,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{j}(w)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=2^{(2-N)j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{x}u(z)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z+2^{(2-N-2\gamma)j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widetilde{x}|^{2\gamma}|\nabla_{y}u(z)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(2-N)j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}u(z)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z.

We assume for now that
\alpha\geq 0
. From

(
V
)

, we have

(2.11)

\int_{B_{4}}u_{j}^{2}(w)\,\mathrm{d}w=2^{-Nj}\int_{B_{4\cdot 2^{j}}}u^{2}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{3}2^{(\alpha-N)j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(z)u^{2}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

The last two inequalities together with (

\int_{A_{0}}|u_{j}(w)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}w\leq C_{4}2^{\frac{(2-N)jp}{2}}\|u\|_{E_{V}}^{p}+C_{4}2^{\frac{(\alpha-N)jp}{2}}\|u\|_{E_{V}}^{p}.

This combined with (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), ensures that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}Q(z)|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{5}\|u\|_{E_{V}}^{p}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{\zeta_{1}j}+C_{5}\|u\|_{E_{V}}^{p}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{\zeta_{2}j},

where

(2.12)

\zeta_{1}:=\frac{(2-N)p}{2}-(\beta-N)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\zeta_{2}:=\frac{(\alpha-N)p}{2}-(\beta-N).

For
\alpha<0
, we simply notice that

can be replaced with

in the estimate (

As a consequence,

Combining the last estimate with (

Q
)

. For simplicity, we will show the item

∎

Proof of Theorem

We split the proof of Theorem

3.1.
Variational structure

We introduce the energy functional

associated to Problem (

P

) given by

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)F(u)\,\mathrm{d}z.

To show that

is well defined, see that by

(

)

-

(

This inequality together with Theorem

(3.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)F(u)\mathrm{d}z\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}z<\infty.

Moreover, by standard arguments one may check that

Consequently, critical points of
I
are weak solutions for the problem (

Next, we will show that

Lemma 3.1
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem

hold. Then,

(a)

there exist positive numbers

and

(b)

Proof.

From (

I(u)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon C_{1}\right)\|u\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{2}-C_{1}\|u\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{q}=\|u\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{2}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon C_{1}\right)-C_{1}\|u\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{q-2}\right].

Choosing

and recalling that

item
(a)
.

Let us prove

. From

(

f

)

C_{2},C_{3}
such that

F(s)\geq C_{2}|s|^{\theta}-C_{3},\quad\forall\,s\in\mathbb{R}.

Now, let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}

large, completing the proof.
∎

It follows from Lemma

that the minimax level

c_{MP}:=\inf_{g\in\Gamma}\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}I(g(t))\geq\tau>0,

where

3.2.

Lemma 3.2

Let the assumptions of Theorem

(u_{n})_{n}\subset E_{V}^{\gamma}

(u_{n})_{n}

Proof.

See that from (

(

f
3

)

\displaystyle C_{1}+C_{2}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)\left(F\left(u_{n}\right)-\frac{1}{\theta}f\left(u_{n}\right)u_{n}\right)

Consequently,

is bounded in

weakly in

Next, we will show that

In fact, by (

3.3.
Regularity

Next, we apply Moser’s iteration to get that any weak solution of (

P

) belongs to
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
hold. If
\dfrac{1}{Q}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then the solution obtained in Proposition
3.3
belongs to
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\xi>1
, we consider the sets
A_{n}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:u^{\xi-1}(z)\leq n\right\}
and
D_{n}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus A_{n}
.
Let

w_{n}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{2\xi-1},\ &\mbox{in}\ A_{n},\\
&n^{2}u,\ &\mbox{in}\ D_{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

We claim that
w_{n}\in E_{V}^{\gamma}
. In fact, see that

\nabla_{\gamma}w_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(2\xi-1)u^{2(\xi-1)}\nabla_{\gamma}u,\ &\mbox{in}\ A_{n},\\
&n^{2}\nabla_{\gamma}u,\ &\mbox{in}\ D_{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

This implies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}w_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq(2\xi-1)^{2}n^{4}\int_{A_{n}}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z+n^{4}\int_{D_{n}}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z<\infty

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(z)w_{n}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{n}}V(z)u^{4(\xi-1)}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z+n^{4}\int_{D_{n}}V(z)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq n^{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(z)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z<\infty.

Consequently,
w_{n}\in E_{V}^{\gamma}
and the claim is proved.

On other hand, since
u
is solution of (

P

), then

(3.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(z)uw_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)f(u)w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Now, consider

v_{n}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{\xi},\ &\mbox{in}\ A_{n},\\
&nu,\ &\mbox{in}\ D_{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Hence,

\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\xi u^{\xi-1}\nabla_{\gamma}u,\ &\mbox{in}\ A_{n},\\
&n\nabla_{\gamma}u,\ &\mbox{in}\ D_{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Similarly,
v_{n}\in E_{V}^{\gamma}
. Notice that

(3.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z=(2\xi-1)\int_{A_{n}}u^{2(\xi-1)}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z+n^{2}\int_{D_{n}}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z=\xi^{2}\int_{A_{n}}u^{2(\xi-1)}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z+n^{2}\int_{D_{n}}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z.

So,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z=(\xi^{2}-2\xi+1)\int_{A_{n}}u^{2(\xi-1)}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z.

From this and (
3.9
), it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\xi^{2}-2\xi+1)}{(2\xi-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle<\xi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Applying (
3.8
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq\xi^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)f(u)w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(z)uw_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z\right).

Combining the above inequality with the fact that
\xi>1
and the equality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(z)v_{n}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(z)uw_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z,

we infer that

(3.10)

\|v_{n}\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{2}<\xi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)f(u)w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z.

From (
3.1
) and the estimate
w_{n}\leq u^{2\xi-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, one deduces

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)f(u)w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)u^{2\xi}\,\mathrm{d}z+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)u^{2\xi+q-2}\,\mathrm{d}z.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)f(u)w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)u^{2r_{2}\xi}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{2}}}

\displaystyle+C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)u^{2q_{1}\xi}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)u^{q_{2}(q-2)}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}},

where
\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=1
, with

\frac{2}{q-2}\leq q_{2}-1<q_{2}<\frac{2_{\gamma}^{*}}{q-2}.

This is possible because
\frac{2_{\gamma}^{*}}{q-2}-\frac{2}{q-2}>1
, for any
q\in(2,2_{\gamma}^{*})
. Since
\beta>N
,
Q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover,
E_{V}^{\gamma}\hookrightarrow L_{Q}^{q_{2}(q-2)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(see Theorem
1.1
). Hence, if
q_{1}=r_{2}
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(z)f(u)w_{n}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{1}\|u\|_{L^{2q_{1}\xi}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2\xi},

for some
C_{1}=C_{1}(\|u\|_{E^{\gamma}_{V}})>0
. This and (
3.10
) infer that

\|v_{n}\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}^{2}<\xi^{2}C_{1}\|u\|_{L^{2q_{1}\xi}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2\xi}.

By Theorem
1.1
and the last inequality, we have

\left(\int_{A_{n}}Q(z)u^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\xi}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{\gamma}^{*}}}\leq\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leq\xi^{2}C_{2}\|u\|_{L^{2q_{1}\xi}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2\xi}.

where we used that
v_{n}=u^{\xi}
in
A_{n}
. Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
and using Fatou’s lemma, we reach

\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\xi}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2\xi}\leq\xi^{2}C_{2}\|u\|_{L^{2q_{1}\xi}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

or

(3.11)

\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\xi}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\xi^{\frac{1}{\xi}}C_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\xi}}\|u\|_{L^{2q_{1}\xi}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Now, we start Moser’s iteration, taking
\xi=\sigma=\frac{2_{\gamma}^{*}}{2q_{1}}>1
. Indeed,

q_{1}=\frac{q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}=q_{2}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{2}-1}<\frac{2_{\gamma}^{*}}{q-2}\cdot\frac{q-2}{2}=\frac{2_{\gamma}^{*}}{2}.

Thus,

\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\sigma^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}C_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\sigma}}\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Taking
\xi=\sigma^{2}
in (
3.11
) and noting that
2q_{1}\sigma^{2}=(2q_{1}\sigma)\sigma=2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma
, the last estimate, yields

\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{2}}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq(\sigma^{2})^{\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}}C_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}}\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\sigma^{\frac{1}{\sigma}+\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}}C_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\sigma}+\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}}\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

By continuing the iteration, we can conclude that

\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\sigma^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{\sigma^{k}}}C_{2}^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\sigma^{k}}}\|u\|_{L_{Q}^{2_{\gamma}^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Since these series converge, we obtain

\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{3}\|u\|_{L_{Q}^{2_{\gamma}^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Now, consider
R>0
arbitrary. From the hypothesis
\dfrac{1}{Q}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists
Q_{R}>0
such that
Q(z)\geq Q_{R}
, for any

. Thus,

\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}\mathrm{d}z\leq\frac{1}{Q_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}Q(z)|u|^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}\mathrm{d}z\leq\frac{1}{Q_{R}}\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}\leq\frac{C_{3}^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}}{Q_{R}}\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}.

Consequently,

\|u\|_{L^{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}(B_{R})}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{Q_{R}^{\frac{1}{2_{\gamma}^{*}\sigma^{j}}}}\|u\|_{L_{Q}^{2_{\gamma}^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Taking

, it holds that
u\in L^{\infty}(B_{R})
, finalizing the proof.
∎

We will finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
by proving item
(ii)
. First, we have an auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.5

(
W_{\gamma}^{2,p}
- Regularity)
.

Assume that
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and there exists
V_{0}>0
such that
V_{0}\leq V(z)
, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If

and

-\Delta_{\gamma}u+Vu=h\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
be a solution for (
3.12
). By Theorem 4.21 in
[
23
]
,

\|v\|_{W^{2,p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\left(\|v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\Delta_{\gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right),

for all
v\in W^{2,p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|-\Delta_{\gamma}u+Vu\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+(1+C\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

since
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, we obtain

(3.13)

\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{1}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right).

Now, if
u\in E_{V}^{\gamma}
is a weak solution of that equation (
3.12
), the definition of
E_{V}^{\gamma}
allows us to take a sequence
(u_{n})_{n}
in
C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
E_{V}^{\gamma}
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Next, define
h_{n}\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

for each

. Notice that (
3.13
) holds for

. We claim that
h_{n}\rightarrow h
in
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Indeed, for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(h_{n}-h\right)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}z\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\nabla_{\gamma}\left(u_{n}-u\right)\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi+V(z)\left(u_{n}-u\right)\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}z\right|.

Again, using Hölder’s inequality, we can conclude that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(h_{n}-h\right)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}z\right|\leq C_{2}\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,2}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0,

\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

This means that

\left\{\varphi_{l}\right\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

\varphi_{l}\rightarrow\varphi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\|\varphi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=1

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(h_{n}-h\right)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}z\right|\leq C_{2}\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}\|\varphi_{l}\|_{W_{\gamma}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|h_{n}-h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|\varphi_{l}-\varphi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

\left\|\varphi-\varphi_{l_{0}}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\varepsilon

(1-\varepsilon)\left\|h_{n}-h\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{2}\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{E_{V}^{\gamma}}\|\varphi_{l_{0}}\|_{W_{\gamma}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\rightarrow 0,

as

[

and

\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{W_{\gamma}^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{3}\left(\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{W_{\gamma}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|h_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)\leq C_{4},
