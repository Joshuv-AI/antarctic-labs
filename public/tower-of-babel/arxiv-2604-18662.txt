Coherence-gated quantum devices via real-time weak measurement
Priyank Singh

Abstract
Single-photon routers in cavity and circuit QED direct photons based on which energy eigenstate a qubit occupies—a projective decision that destroys coherence. We propose a fundamentally different primitive: coherence-gated routing , where the routing decision depends not on the qubit’s eigenstate but on the magnitude of its quantum coherence, estimated in real time from simultaneous weak measurements of two non-commuting observables ( σ x \sigma_{x} and σ z \sigma_{z} ). A photon is accepted or rejected depending on whether the coherence score S ⁡ ( T ) = ⟨ σ x ⟩ c 2 + ⟨ σ y ⟩ c 2 S(T)=\sqrt{\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{c}^{2}+\langle\sigma_{y}\rangle_{c}^{2}} , extracted from the conditional density matrix via the stochastic master equation, exceeds a tunable threshold S th S_{\mathrm{th}} at decision time T T . Because the qubit’s coherence is certified at the moment the photon is emitted, the protocol enables two applications that conventional heralded sources cannot provide: (i) a quantum random number generator whose min-entropy is bounded by Bloch-sphere geometry, H ∞ ≥ − log 2 ⁡ ( 1 + 1 − S th 2 2 ) H_{\infty}\geq-\log_{2}\!\bigl(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-S_{\mathrm{th}}^{2}}}{2}\bigr) , and (ii) a phase-tracked photon source for quantum networks, where independent coherence certification at two remote nodes bounds the achievable matter–matter entanglement fidelity after Bell-state measurement. The real-time estimator is not merely a numerical tool—it is a security primitive. We benchmark seven estimator configurations across 3000 quantum trajectories and show that deliberately underestimating the detector efficiency ( η a < η true \eta_{\mathrm{a}}<\eta_{\mathrm{true}} ) simultaneously stabilizes the numerics and suppresses coherence overcertification. We trace this connection analytically through a purity monotonicity result, identify a geometric loophole that amplifies purity undercertification into coherence overcertification by a factor of 45 45 , and develop two complementary pointwise bounds: an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck comparison yielding a 4.5 % 4.5\% operational overcertification bound (validated at 3.6 % 3.6\% from 10 6 10^{6} trajectories), and an exponential supermartingale establishing the structural existence of an exponential tail. The gap between the two—a single polynomial optimization problem—defines a clear path to fully composable security.

II.1
Qubit Hamiltonian and decoherence

We consider a qubit with Hamiltonian

H_{q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Omega_{x}\sigma_{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\sigma_{z},

(2)

where
\Omega_{x}
is the transverse Rabi drive strength,
\Delta
is the
detuning and
\sigma_{k}
are the Pauli matrices. The qubit is subject to two intrinsic decoherence channels:
energy relaxation at rate
\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}
(described by the Lindblad operator

\sqrt{\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}}\,\sigma_{-}
, where
\sigma_{-}=\ket{0}\bra{1}
) and
pure dephasing at rate
\gamma_{\phi}
(Lindblad operator

\sqrt{\gamma_{\phi}}\,\sigma_{z}
). We denote the sum of intrinsic
rates as
\gamma_{\mathrm{dec}}=\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}+\gamma_{\phi}
.
(The exact transverse dephasing contribution from relaxation is

\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}/2
, not
\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}
; this distinction is absorbed into the
approximate character of
S_{\mathrm{typ}}
in Sec.
X
.)

II.2
Simultaneous weak measurement

In addition to intrinsic decoherence, the qubit is simultaneously
monitored by two homodyne detection channels that weakly measure

\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{z}
with measurement rates
\gamma_{x}
and
\gamma_{z}
,
respectively. Each channel produces a continuous photocurrent. In
practice, only a fraction of the outgoing signal is captured by the
detector; this fraction is quantified by the
measurement
efficiency

\eta_{k}\in[0,1]
for channel
k
. The monitored fraction

\eta_{k}
contributes measurement backaction that is captured in the
conditional state update, while the unmonitored fraction
(1-\eta_{k})

acts as additional dephasing. For simplicity we take
\eta_{x}=\eta_{z}\equiv\eta
throughout.

The homodyne photocurrents are
39
;
11

I_{k}(t)=\sqrt{4\eta\gamma_{k}}\,\langle\sigma_{k}\rangle_{c}(t)+\xi_{k}(t),\quad k\in\{x,z\},

(3)

where
\langle\sigma_{k}\rangle_{c}(t)=\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{k}\rho_{c}(t)]

is the conditional expectation of
\sigma_{k}
and
\xi_{k}(t)
is Gaussian
white noise satisfying
\langle\xi_{k}(t)\xi_{k^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta(t-t^{\prime})
. The factor of 4 arises because the
signal is proportional to

\langle L_{k}+L_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle=2\sqrt{\gamma_{k}}\,\langle\sigma_{k}\rangle_{c}
for Hermitian
\sigma_{k}
.
The signal-to-noise ratio of each current
is set by the product
\eta\gamma_{k}
: larger measurement rate or
higher efficiency produces a stronger signal relative to the shot noise
floor.

II.3
Stochastic master equation

The conditional density matrix
\rho_{c}(t)
evolves under the stochastic
master equation (SME) in Itō
form
39
;
11
:

\displaystyle d\rho_{c}

\displaystyle=-i[H_{q},\rho_{c}]\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}\,\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{-}]\rho_{c}\,dt+\gamma_{\phi}\,\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{z}]\rho_{c}\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=x,z}\gamma_{k}\,\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{k}]\rho_{c}\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=x,z}\sqrt{\eta\gamma_{k}}\,\mathcal{H}[\sigma_{k}]\rho_{c}\,dW_{k}.

(4)

Here
\mathcal{D}[L]\rho=L\rho L^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}(L^{\dagger}L\rho+\rho L^{\dagger}L)
is the Lindblad dissipator and

\mathcal{H}[L]\rho=L\rho+\rho L^{\dagger}-\mathrm{Tr}[(L+L^{\dagger})\rho]\,\rho
is the measurement backaction
(innovation) superoperator. The Wiener increments

dW_{k}
satisfy
dW_{k}\,dW_{k^{\prime}}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\,dt
and are related
to the photocurrents by
dW_{k}=I_{k}(t)\,dt-\sqrt{4\eta\gamma_{k}}\,\langle\sigma_{k}\rangle_{c}\,dt
.

The structure of Eq. (
4
) reflects the standard decomposition
of homodyne monitoring with efficiency
\eta<1

(Ref.
39
, Eq. 4.238). Each measurement channel
couples to the qubit at the full rate
\gamma_{k}
, contributing

\gamma_{k}\,\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{k}]\rho_{c}
to the deterministic
evolution regardless of detector efficiency.
The efficiency
\eta
enters only in the stochastic

\mathcal{H}[\cdot]
term: the monitored fraction of the output
field provides the innovation signal that updates the conditional
state.
In the limit
\eta\to 1

and
with intrinsic decoherence
negligible (
\gamma_{\mathrm{dec}}\ll\eta\gamma
), the stochastic
update fully compensates the measurement-induced dephasing, and
the conditional state can remain nearly pure. For finite

\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}},\gamma_{\phi}
, residual decoherence from the unmonitored
intrinsic channels bounds the achievable purity even at
\eta=1
.
In the limit
\eta\to 0
, no information is extracted, the

\mathcal{H}[\cdot]
term vanishes, and the measurement acts as
pure dephasing—recovering the unconditional master equation.

II.4
Coherence metric and routing decision

The coherence score
S(t)
defined in Eq. (
1
)
quantifies the
\ell_{1}
-norm of coherence in the
\sigma_{z}
eigenbasis.
It takes values in
[0,1]
:
S=0
for a state diagonal in the

\sigma_{z}
basis (no coherence), and
S=1
for a pure equatorial
state such as
\ket{+}=(\ket{0}+\ket{1})/\sqrt{2}
.

At the decision time
T
, the FPGA computes
S(T)
from the estimated
conditional state and applies the routing rule:

\text{Route}=\begin{cases}A&\text{if }S(T)>S_{\mathrm{th}},\\
B&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(5)

where
S_{\mathrm{th}}\in[0,1]
is a tunable threshold that controls the
tradeoff between certification quality and heralding efficiency.
Higher
S_{\mathrm{th}}
demands stronger coherence, accepting fewer trajectories
but certifying higher photon quality.

It is important to emphasize that the conditional coherence
S(t)
is
not a pre-existing property of the qubit that the measurement merely
reveals. Rather, it is a property of the
Bayesian posterior
:
the best estimate of the qubit state given the measurement record up to
time
t
. The unconditional coherence (obtained by averaging over all
possible measurement records) decays rapidly to a small steady-state
value
S_{\mathrm{uncond}}^{\mathrm{ss}}\ll S_{\mathrm{th}}
on a timescale

\sim 1/\Gamma_{b}
, where
\Gamma_{b}=(\Gamma_{x}+\Gamma_{y})/2
is the
phase-averaged transverse dephasing rate, with

\Gamma_{x}=2\gamma_{z}+2\gamma_{\phi}+\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}/2
and

\Gamma_{y}=2\gamma_{x}+2\gamma_{z}+2\gamma_{\phi}+\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}/2
being the
component-specific rates.
(With the Rabi drive present,
the unconditional steady state retains a small residual coherence from
the driven dynamics; for our parameters,

S_{\mathrm{uncond}}^{\mathrm{ss}}\approx 0.02
.) The conditional coherence, by contrast, can remain of order
unity, because measurement backaction continuously regenerates it on
individual trajectories
11
. This distinction—between the
unconditional suppression and the trajectory-level persistence of
coherence—is the physical foundation of the protocol, demonstrated
directly in Sec.
III.1
.

We also define the conditional purity

\mathcal{P}(t)\equiv\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{c}(t)^{2}]=\frac{1+|\mathbf{r}(t)|^{2}}{2},

(6)

where
|\mathbf{r}|^{2}=S^{2}+z^{2}
is the squared Bloch-vector length,
with
S
and
z\equiv\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle_{c}
as defined above.
While the coherence score
S
captures only the equatorial
projection of
\mathbf{r}
, the purity
\mathcal{P}
depends on the full
length
|\mathbf{r}|
. This distinction underlies a geometric
subtlety in the certification analysis
(Sec.
VII.2
): an estimator can underestimate

\mathcal{P}
while overestimating
S
.

III
The measurement–coherence–drive triad

The coherence score
S
defined in Eq. (
1
) quantifies the length of the
Bloch vector’s projection onto the
x
-
y
equatorial plane. This
choice of basis is not arbitrary: it is dictated by a self-consistency
requirement between the measurement axes, the coherence basis, and the
Hamiltonian drive, which we now explain.

In our protocol, the two measurement channels (
\sigma_{x}
and

\sigma_{z}
) introduce dephasing in complementary directions. The

\sigma_{z}
channel dephases the
x
-
y
components at rate
2\gamma_{z}
,
while the
\sigma_{x}
channel dephases the
y
-
z
components at rate

2\gamma_{x}
. Crucially, both channels are
monitored
: their
backaction is captured by the
\mathcal{H}[\cdot]
terms in the SME
[Eq. (
4
)], and the conditional state compensates for the
measurement-induced dephasing. The residual, unrecoverable damage to
coherence comes only from the unmonitored fraction
(1-\eta)
.

Had we instead measured
\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{y}
and defined
coherence in the
\sigma_{x}
eigenbasis as

\tilde{S}=\sqrt{\langle\sigma_{y}\rangle_{c}^{2}+\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle_{c}^{2}}
, the
z
-component would appear in the
coherence metric and would suffer dephasing from
both

measurement channels (
\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{x}]
and

\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{y}]
each dephase
z
at rate
2\gamma
), leading
to larger total measurement backaction on the quantity of interest.

This analysis reveals that three elements of the protocol must be
chosen self-consistently:

1.

The
coherence basis
(
\sigma_{z}
eigenstates) defines
which off-diagonal elements of
\rho_{c}
constitute the resource to
be certified.

2.

The
measurement axes
(
\sigma_{x}
,
\sigma_{z}
)
provide information about the Bloch-vector components while
introducing dephasing that is complementary to each measurement
axis.

3.

The
transverse drive
(
\Omega_{x}\sigma_{x}
)
continuously regenerates equatorial coherence by driving population
between
\ket{0}
and
\ket{1}
, counteracting the
measurement-induced dephasing of the
x
-
y
plane.

We refer to this trio as the
measurement–coherence–drive triad
.
Any alternative measurement scheme faces the same
information–disturbance tradeoff: measurement of an observable
O

provides information about
\langle O\rangle_{c}
but dephases the
components complementary to
O
, and coherence is always defined in
terms of those complementary components.
Our particular configuration maps onto the two-tone dispersive scheme
demonstrated by Hacohen-Gourgy
et al.

8
,
in which a single readout resonator is driven with two microwave
tones at frequencies chosen so that one tone’s dispersive shift
couples to
\sigma_{z}
while the stroboscopic average of the other
couples to
\sigma_{x}
in the rotating frame. The two channels are
separated by demodulation of the reflected signal at the respective
sideband frequencies. Transverse microwave drives implement the
Rabi term
\Omega_{x}\sigma_{x}
via standard single-qubit control. This
realization is not merely conceptual: simultaneous
\sigma_{x}
–
\sigma_{z}

monitoring has been demonstrated at efficiencies
\eta\sim 0.4
–
0.5
in current hardware
8
,
though our protocol’s quantitative guarantees (Table
4
)
assume
\eta=0.7
—achievable with improved parametric amplifier
chains and reduced circulator losses.

IV
Certified quantum random number generation

We now turn to the first application of coherence-gated routing:
certified quantum random number generation. The key observation is
that the Bloch-sphere geometry imposes a rigid constraint between the
coherence score
S
and the
z
-component of the conditional Bloch
vector,
z(T)\equiv\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle_{c}(T)
, which governs the
Born-rule outcome statistics.

IV.1
Min-entropy bound from Bloch-sphere geometry

For a trajectory accepted at threshold
S_{\mathrm{th}}
, the geometric constraint

|z|\leq\delta\equiv\sqrt{1-S_{\mathrm{th}}^{2}}
bounds the Born-rule
probability:

P_{z}\in\left[\frac{1-\delta}{2},\;\frac{1+\delta}{2}\right].

(7)

The worst-case (minimum) min-entropy per bit is therefore:

\boxed{H_{\infty}(S_{\mathrm{th}})=-\log_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-S_{\mathrm{th}}^{2}}}{2}\right).}

(8)

At
S_{\mathrm{th}}=0.95
, this gives
H_{\infty}\geq 0.61
bits per event;
at
S_{\mathrm{th}}=0.99
,
H_{\infty}\geq 0.81
bits.

IV.3
Security role of the estimator

The min-entropy guarantee of Eq. (
8
) holds only if the
coherence estimate is faithful—specifically, only if

S_{\mathrm{est}}(T)\leq S_{\mathrm{true}}(T)
, so that the certified
threshold is a genuine lower bound on the true conditional coherence.
This requirement is analyzed in detail in
Secs.
VII
–
VIII
, where we develop
both average-case guarantees (Proposition 1) and pointwise tail bounds
(Theorem 2).

IV.4
Comparison with existing QRNG protocols

Quantum random number generators span a hierarchy of trust
assumptions
9
;
21
. At one extreme,

fully device-independent
(DI) QRNGs derive randomness from
Bell-inequality violation
25
;
19
.
At the other,

trusted-device
QRNGs assume full knowledge of the
source
12
;
32
. Our coherence-certified
QRNG occupies intermediate ground.

The min-entropy bound [Eq. (
8
)] requires three conditions:
(i) the SME [Eq. (
4
)] is a correct model of the
qubit–measurement dynamics;
(ii) the measurement efficiency
\eta
is calibrated to within the
conservative margin provided by
\eta_{\mathrm{a}}<\eta_{\mathrm{true}}
;
(iii) the estimator’s coherence estimate does not
systematically overcertify the true conditional coherence.
With the composable bound developed in Sec.
VIII
,
condition (iii) is quantified:

\Pr[\hat{S}(T)>S_{\mathrm{true}}(T)+\varepsilon]\leq\delta(\varepsilon)
, where
\delta
is given by
Eq. (
18
).
This places our protocol in a

model-certified
regime—stronger than a fully trusted device
but weaker than semi-device-independent protocols that certify randomness
from dimension witnesses alone
18
;
3
;
20
.

Two features are specific to our approach: (i) certification and
bit generation occur in the same measurement cycle—no separate test
rounds are needed; (ii) the conservative-
\eta
estimator suppresses
overcertification, with the optimal ratio
\eta_{\mathrm{a}}/\eta_{\mathrm{true}}
given
approximately by Eq. (
23
).

VII
Certification integrity: the estimator as security primitive

The applications developed above depend critically on the faithfulness
of the coherence estimate. An estimator that systematically
overestimates
S(T)
compromises the QRNG min-entropy bound,
degrades Bell-pair fidelity for networking, and weakens the
matter–matter entanglement fidelity
F_{\mathrm{mm}}
for entanglement
distribution.
Conversely, an estimator
that underestimates
S(T)
is conservative: it rejects good
trajectories but never falsely certifies.

VII.1
Purity monotonicity

Proposition 1
(Purity monotonicity).

For a qubit monitored at efficiency
\eta
via homodyne
detection, the expected conditional purity satisfies

\partial\,\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{P}]/\partial\eta>0
.

Proof.

From Itō calculus applied to
\mathcal{P}=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{c}^{2})

[Eq. (
6
)] under the
SME [Eq. (
4
)], the
\eta
-dependent contribution to

d\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{P}]/dt
is

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\frac{d\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{P}]}{dt}\right|_{\eta}=4\sum_{k=x,z}\gamma_{k}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{Var}_{c}(\sigma_{k})^{2}\right],

(12)

where
\mathrm{Var}_{c}(\sigma_{k})=1-\langle\sigma_{k}\rangle_{c}^{2}
.
Since
\mathrm{Var}_{c}(\sigma_{k})^{2}\geq 0
with equality only
for an eigenstate, the right-hand side is strictly positive,
establishing that expected purity increases
monotonically with
\eta

11
.
\square

VII.2

S=\sqrt{|\mathbf{r}|^{2}-z^{2}}

point closer to the equatorial plane (smaller
|\hat{z}|/|\hat{\mathbf{r}}|
),
yielding
\hat{S}>S
even though

\hat{\mathcal{P}}<\mathcal{P}
. We call this the

geometric loophole
.

VIII
Pointwise overcertification bound

Having established the average-case argument (Proposition 1) and

VIII.1
The error process

Both the true state
\mathbf{r}(t)
and the estimate

\hat{\mathbf{r}}(t)
evolve under the SME in Bloch form,
driven by the same measurement record but with efficiencies
\eta_{\mathrm{true}}

and
\eta_{\mathrm{a}}
. The measurement backaction on Bloch
component
r_{j}
from channel
k
is

\delta r_{j}=2\sqrt{\eta\gamma_{k}}\,(\delta_{jk}-r_{j}r_{k})\,dW_{k},

(13)

following from

\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{j}\,\mathcal{H}[\sigma_{k}]\rho]=2(\delta_{jk}-r_{j}r_{k})
.
Define the squared-coherence error

E(t)\equiv\hat{S}^{2}(t)-S^{2}(t)=(\hat{x}^{2}+\hat{y}^{2})-(x^{2}+y^{2}).

(14)

By Itō’s formula,
E(t)
is a continuous semimartingale:

dE=b(X)\,dt+\beta_{x}(X)\,dW^{(x)}+\beta_{z}(X)\,dW^{(z)},

(15)

where
X=(\mathbf{r},\hat{\mathbf{r}})\in K\subset\mathbb{R}^{6}

with
K=\{|\mathbf{r}|\leq 1,\;|\hat{\mathbf{r}}|\leq 1\}
.
The drift decomposes as

\displaystyle b(X)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-2R_{x}(\hat{x}^{2}-x^{2})-2R_{y}(\hat{y}^{2}-y^{2})+2\Omega_{x}(\hat{y}\hat{z}-yz)}_{\text{deterministic (restoring $+$ coupling)}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{4(\eta_{\mathrm{a}}-\eta_{\mathrm{true}})\left[\gamma_{x}(1-x^{2})^{2}+\gamma_{z}S^{2}z^{2}\right]+\ldots\;}_{\text{It\={o} correction (forcing, $<0$)}},

(16)

where
R_{x}=\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}/2+2\gamma_{\phi}+2\gamma_{z}
and

R_{y}=\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}/2+2\gamma_{\phi}+2\gamma_{x}+2\gamma_{z}
.

VIII.2
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck comparison (operational bound)

approximately restoring evolution for the error process, which motivates
an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (hereafter OU) approximation:

dE\approx-\mu(E-\bar{E})\,dt+\sigma_{E}\,d\tilde{W}
,
with steady-state mean
\bar{E}=\nu/\mu<0
.
We extract the OU parameters from

10^{6}
trajectories by conditional drift fitting
(bin by
E
, compute
\langle dE/dt\mid E\rangle
, fit the
linear relation).

VIII.3
Exponential supermartingale (structural bound)

A fully rigorous bound follows from an exponential supermartingale.

Theorem 2
(Exponential overcertification bound).

Define

c_{\alpha}=\sup_{X\in K}\!\left[\alpha\,b(X)+\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\bigl(\beta_{x}(X)^{2}+\beta_{z}(X)^{2}\bigr)\right].

(19)

Then for every
\varepsilon\geq 0
and
T>0
,

\Pr[\hat{S}_{T}\geq S_{T}+\varepsilon]\leq\inf_{\alpha>0}\exp\!\bigl(-\alpha\varepsilon^{2}+Tc_{\alpha}\bigr).

(20)

Proof.

Define
M_{t}^{(\alpha)}=\exp\!\bigl(\alpha E_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{\alpha}(X_{s})\,ds\bigr)
with

\Lambda_{\alpha}(X)=\alpha\,b(X)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}(\beta_{x}^{2}+\beta_{z}^{2})
.
By Itō’s formula,
M^{(\alpha)}
is the Doléans exponential
of a continuous local martingale. Since
\beta_{x},\beta_{z}
are
bounded on
K
, Novikov’s condition holds and
M^{(\alpha)}
is a
true martingale.
At the stopping time
\tau_{a}=\inf\{t\leq T:E_{t}\geq a\}
,

M_{\tau_{a}}\geq e^{\alpha a-Tc_{\alpha}}
, so

\Pr(\tau_a \leq T)\leq e^{-\alpha a+Tc_{\alpha}}
.
Setting
a=\varepsilon^{2}
(since
\hat{S}\geq S+\varepsilon\Rightarrow E\geq\varepsilon^{2}
) and optimizing over
\alpha

gives the result.
\square

Optimal assumed efficiency

Both the EKF and the direct SME benefit empirically from

\eta_{\mathrm{a}}<\eta_{\mathrm{true}}
. A heuristic balance between measurement-induced
estimation error accumulation and relaxation suggests the scaling law

\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{a}}^{*}}{\eta_{\mathrm{true}}}=\frac{1}{1+\xi},\qquad\xi=\frac{4\gamma\,S_{\mathrm{typ}}^{2}}{\Gamma_{b}},

(23)

where
\gamma=\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{z}
is the total measurement rate,

S_{\mathrm{typ}}\approx\sqrt{2\eta\gamma/[3(\eta\gamma+\gamma_{\mathrm{dec}})]}
, and

\Gamma_{b}=(\Gamma_{x}+\Gamma_{y})/2
.

For the parameters of Table
1
, the formula with

S_{\mathrm{typ}}\approx 0.76
gives
\xi\approx 1.44
, while using
the empirical steady-state mean
\langle S\rangle\approx 0.67

gives
\xi\approx 1.10
and
r^{*}\approx 0.48
. The latter tracks
the empirical optimum
r^{*}\approx 0.52
within sampling error.
Across the six parameter sets in the Supplemental Material validation
sweep, the scaling law predicts
r^{*}
with RMSE
=0.067
, with
larger errors (up to
|\Delta r|=0.11
) in the weak-measurement
and fast-Rabi regimes where the underlying balance assumption is
less accurate. We therefore present Eq. (

) as a useful
engineering estimate rather than a universally tight prediction.

XI.3
Trajectory-conditioned quantum devices

Coherence-gated routing is the first instance of a broader class of

trajectory-conditioned quantum devices
. The methodology
developed here—estimator benchmarking, certification integrity
analysis, the OU comparison for pointwise bounds, and the
supermartingale structural result—applies directly to devices
conditioned on other trajectory features such as entanglement measures,
purity, or quantum discord.

XII
Conclusions

We have introduced coherence-gated routing: a single-photon routing
primitive conditioned on the real-time quantum coherence of a driven
qubit. The measurement basis, coherence basis, and Hamiltonian drive
axis form a self-consistent triad natural for circuit-QED hardware.

The central physical phenomenon—that individual quantum trajectories
retain large coherence while the ensemble average collapses to near
zero—was demonstrated directly (Fig.
2
) and
provides the foundation for all subsequent results.

The coherence certification enables two primary applications:
(i) a coherence-certified QRNG with min-entropy
bound
H_{\infty}\geq-\log_{2}\!\bigl(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-S_{\mathrm{th}}^{2}}}{2}\bigr)
,
and (ii) a phase-tracked photon source for quantum networks.
Independent coherence certification at two nodes constrains the
matter–matter entanglement fidelity achievable via BSM to

F_{\mathrm{mm}}\geq[(1+S_{\mathrm{th}})/2]^{2}

under idealized assumptions.

Through systematic estimator benchmarking, the conservative Direct SME
(
\eta_{\mathrm{a}}<\eta_{\mathrm{true}}
) emerged as the recommended solution: numerically
stable, safely biased, and hardware-efficient. Proposition 1 proves
that the conservative estimator has lower expected purity.

We developed two complementary pointwise overcertification bounds.
The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck comparison provides an operational bound of

4.5\%
overcertification probability, validated against an empirical
rate of
3.6\%
from
10^{6}
trajectories, enabling composable
security statements (Corollaries 1 and 2). The exponential
supermartingale (Theorem 2) establishes the structural existence of
an exponential tail bound, with the computational gap concentrated
in a single polynomial optimization over the reachable state space.
We identified the geometric mechanism underlying the
45\times

amplification from purity to coherence overcertification: the

\eta
-independent Rabi drive makes the estimated state slightly
more equatorial, partially compensating its shorter Bloch vector.
Closing the gap between the OU and supermartingale
bounds—via reachable-set restriction or sum-of-squares
optimization over the forward-invariant reachable set—is the
remaining step toward a fully rigorous composable security
statement; the OU-based bound already provides an operational
guarantee sufficient for near-term implementations. The broader
connection between numerical regularization and application-level
security applies to the class of trajectory-conditioned quantum
devices introduced here.
