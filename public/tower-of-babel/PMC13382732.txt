Ventricular Perspective of MitraClip Integration in an Explanted Human Heart
Authors: Stefan Elde, Moeed Fawad, Chris Huynh, Y Joseph Woo, Joon Bum Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•This piece offers direct visualization of MitraClip integration into mitral leaflets and chordae tendineae in a human heart.•MitraClip integration into the mitral apparatus may alter valvular biomechanics and function.•Alterations of mitral valve biomechanics may have implications for long-term durability of transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER).Keywords: Mitral regurgitation, Mitral valve, Transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER)

Mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) using the MitraClip system (Abbott, Chicago, IL) is performed under transesophageal echocardiographic guidance from the atrial perspective, and direct ventricular visualization of device-subvalvular interaction is rarely available.1 We report macroscopic findings from an explanted human heart demonstrating endothelial integration of 2 MitraClip devices into the mitral valve apparatus, thereby altering chordae tendineae angulation and biomechanics.

A 64-year-old man with cardiorenal sarcoidosis, atrial fibrillation status post multiple ablations and left atrial appendage occlusion, ischemic cardiomyopathy with prior drug-eluting stents in the left anterior descending artery, ventricular tachycardia requiring implantable cardioverter-defibrillator placement, and severe mitral regurgitation underwent TEER with 2 MitraClip NTW devices. He was subsequently listed for orthotopic heart transplantation because of progressive end-stage heart failure due to sarcoidosis. Transthoracic echocardiography performed 2 years after TEER demonstrated mild mitral regurgitation, a mean mitral valve gradient of 4 mmHg at a heart rate of 86 beats per minute, and an ejection fraction of 20% with akinesis of the inferoseptal, apical, and anteroseptal walls. Eventually, the patient required intra-aortic balloon pump placement and underwent orthotopic heart transplant 2 years after MitraClip. The recipient’s explanted heart was arrested with University of Wisconsin solution and handed off to laboratory personnel. The heart was examined, and the left ventricular cavity incised with care to spare the entire mitral valve apparatus including the annulus, leaflets, and papillary muscles.

From the atrial perspective, central leaflet fusion was evident at the site of clip implantation, creating a double-orifice configuration consistent with the edge-to-edge repair principle (Figure 1a).1 Ventricular exposure revealed the clip arms incorporated within both anterior and posterior leaflets, forming a fibrous bridge between the coapted leaflet segments (Figure 1b and c).

(a) Atrial view demonstrating central leaflet fusion and double-orifice configuration following TEER; (b) Gross sectioned heart preserving ventricular geometry and subvalvular apparatus; (c) Ventricular view showing clip arms incorporated within fused anterior and posterior leaflets, with preserved chordae tendineae; (d and e) Close ventricular inspection demonstrating fibrous tissue coverage over device arms and chordal angulation adjacent to the grasping zone; (f) Isolated mitral apparatus specimen highlighting leaflet fusion and subvalvular interaction

Abbreviation: TEER, transcatheter edge-to-edge repair.

Close ventricular inspection demonstrated 3 notable morphologic features. First, the clip arms were embedded within leaflet tissue and covered by organized fibrous layers, consistent with previously described healing responses of explanted devices (Figure 1d and e).2 Second, chordae tendineae arising from both papillary muscles coursed around and beneath the fused leaflet-device complex without rupture. However, chordal angulation and apparent tension redistribution were observed adjacent to the device arms (Figure 1c–e and Video 1). Third, the grasping zone exhibited localized leaflet thickening and fibrotic remodeling, whereas adjacent leaflet segments retained relative pliability.

Preservation of 3-dimensional geometry allowed direct visualization of the spatial relationship among papillary muscles, chordae, leaflets, and device arms—an anatomic perspective not accessible during transcatheter deployment. Prior explant reports have described device incorporation and anatomic fusion. However, detailed ventricular visualization of chordal-device interaction remains limited.2,3

These macroscopic findings provide structural confirmation of durable device integration within the native mitral apparatus and illustrate subvalvular remodeling after TEER. Notably, subvalvular remodeling after TEER likely occurs in parallel and at least partially in response to ventricular remodeling due to disease progression. Nonetheless, ventricular inspection suggests that mechanical load redistribution after TEER may extend beyond leaflet coaptation to chordal trajectories, potentially influencing long-term durability and mechanisms of residual regurgitation.
