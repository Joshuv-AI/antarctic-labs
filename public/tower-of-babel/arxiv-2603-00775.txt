Characterization of measures on the real line that are critically unstable under small shifts
Averil Aussedat

Abstract




Abstract

We study the perturbation of a measure
\mu\in{P}(\mathbb{R})
consisting in superposing two copies of
\mu
, each slightly shifted by a small distance
\pm h
. The difference between
\mu
and its perturbation is measured with a Wasserstein distance. For any
\mu
, this distance is bounded from above by
h
. We show that measures for which this critical rate is achieved when
h
goes to 0 are characterized as the ones giving most of their mass to some particular porous sets. This is used to identify which measures
\mu
on the real line have a 2-Wasserstein tangent cone equal to the set of directions inducing curves with maximal initial speed.

Keywords: Wasserstein distances, porous sets, tangent cone, Cantor sets.

MSC 2020: 49Q22, 28A80, 26A30.

Introduction

The set
\Psp(\mathbb{R})
of probability measures can be endowed with the family of Wasserstein distances
W_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
, measuring the minimal
L^{p}-
travel distance for an exponent
p\in[1,\infty)
. All these distances coincide in some particular cases: for instance, if
\mu=\delta_{0}
and
\mu_{h}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left[\delta_{-h}+\delta_{h}\right]
, then
W_{p}(\mu,\mu_{h})=h
for any
h\geqslant 0
and
p\in[1,\infty)
. This article focuses on the set of measures satisfying the same inequality at the limit when
h
goes to zero. Precisely, given
\mu\in\Psp(\mathbb{R})
, we consider the superposition of its left and right shift

\displaystyle\mu_{h}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left[(id-h)_{\#}\mu+(id+h)_{\#}\mu\right],

and ask whether it holds that

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\searrow 0}\frac{W_{p}(\mu,\mu_{h})}{h}=1.

(1)

The inequality
\leqslant
always holds, and for absolutely continuous measures, the limit sup in is equal to 0 instead of 1. Hence the condition
Equation

1
is a critical behavior, and we expect any
\mu
satisfying it to be very concentrated. It turns out that the set of such
\mu
is independent of
p\in[1,\infty)
(see
4
below). The first result of the paper states that
Equation

1
is equivalent to the fact that
\mu
gives almost all its mass to an element of a very restrictive class
{A}
of porous set.

Definition 1
.

A measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
belongs to
{A}
if for some
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
going to 0 when
n\to\infty
, there holds

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in A}\inf\left\{\tau\in(0,1)\ \middle|\ {B}(x,s_{n})\cap A\subset\overline{{B}}(x,\tau s_{n})\right\}=0.

(2)

In words, in any ball of radius
s_{n}
around
x\in A
, the set
A
is contained in the smaller ball of radius
\tau s_{n}
for some
\tau
that goes to 0 with

.
Sets in
{A}
are porous in the sense that each ball
{B}(x,s_{n})
centred at

contains a ball of diameter larger than

that does not intersect

(see for instance
[
21

), but

1
is stronger in general: the same sequence of scales
(s_{n})_{n}
is shared by all points

We do not have a fully satisfactory characterization of the set of measures

Preliminaries

The open ball of radius
r\geqslant 0
around a point
x\in\mathbb{R}
is denoted
{B}(x,r)
. The closure of a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is denoted
\overline{A}
, and its complement
A^{c}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\setminus A
. A vanishing sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
is a nonincreasing sequence that converges to 0. If
\mu\in\Psp(X)
is a Borel probability measure over a Polish space
X
, and
f:X\to Y
is a measurable map between Polish spaces, then
f_{\#}\mu
is the probability measure on
Y
determined by
(f_{\#}\mu)(A)=\mu(f^{-1}(A))
for any measurable
A\subset Y
. The set
\T\mathbb{R}\coloneqq\left\{(x,v)\ \middle|\ x\in\mathbb{R},v\in\T_{x}\mathbb{R}\right\}
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The following material is classical, and can be found in detailed form in
[
24
]
(in particular Proposition 2.17 for the monotone plan). For the dual formulation, we adopt the sign convention of
[
27
, Theorem 5.10]
.

Wasserstein distances

When
\mu
is a measure on a set
X\times Y
, we use the notation
\mu=\mu(dx,dy)
to give names to the variables of
\mu
, and later use them in the canonical projections
\pi_{x},\pi_{y}
. The set of measures
\xi=\xi(dx,dv)\in\Psp_{2}(\T\mathbb{R})
that satisfy
\pi_{x\#}\xi=\mu
is denoted
\Psp_{2}(\T\mathbb{R})_{\mu}
. Each element of
\Psp_{2}(\T\mathbb{R})_{\mu}
induced a curve of measures denoted

\displaystyle h\mapsto\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\xi)\coloneqq(\pi_{x}+h\pi_{v})_{\#}\xi.

The set of transport plans between
\mu\in\Psp(X)
and
\nu\in\Psp(Y)
is denoted

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\left\{\ \eta=\eta(dx,dy)\in\Psp(X\times Y)\ \middle|\ \pi_{x\#}\eta=\mu,\text{ and }\pi_{y\#}\eta=\nu\ \right\}.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, let
\Psp_{p}(\mathbb{R})
be the subset of
\mu\in\Psp(\mathbb{R})
such that
\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|x|^{p}d\mu<\infty
.
The
p-
Wasserstein distance
W_{p}(\mu,\nu)
between
\mu,\nu\in\Psp_{p}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as

\displaystyle W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\inf_{\eta\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\int_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x-y|^{p}d\eta(x,y).

In general, minimizers exists but are not unique, and we denote by
\Gamma_{o,p}(\mu,\nu)
the set of minimizers (dropping the
p
when it is clear from the context).
In the sequel, we might take Wasserstein distances between measures
\mu,\nu
with
0<\mu(\mathbb{R})=\nu(\mathbb{R})\neq 1
; in this case, we adopt the convention

\displaystyle W_{p}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\mu(\mathbb{R})\times W_{p}\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu(\mathbb{R})},\frac{\nu}{\nu(\mathbb{R})}\right).

Similarly, we extend
W_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
to
\Psp(\mathbb{R})^{2}
by
\infty
on pairs
(\mu,\nu)
such that no transport plan yields a finite cost. Since we consider mainly the distance between
\mu
and the shifted average
\mu_{h}
, it always holds that
W_{p}(\mu,\mu_{h})\leqslant h<\infty
, regardless of the moments of
\mu
.

Monotone plan

In dimension one, an explicit minimizer is known, which is in addition unique if
p>1
. This plan is called the “monotone plan”, since it is constructed by gathering all the mass that
\mu
puts on
(-\infty,x]
, and spread it monotonically on
(-\infty,y]
for some
y
such that
\nu((-\infty,y))\leqslant\mu((-\infty,x])\leqslant\nu((-\infty,y])
. In rigorous terms, define the distribution function of
\mu\in\Psp(\mathbb{R})
as

\displaystyle F_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1],\qquad F_{\mu}(x)\coloneqq\mu((-\infty,x]).

The function
F_{\mu}
is nondecreasing, and admits a pseudo-inverse
F_{\mu}^{[-1]}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
characterized by
F_{\mu}(x)\geqslant r
iff
x\geqslant F_{\mu}^{[-1]}(r)
. Explicitly, there holds
F_{\mu}^{[-1]}(r)\coloneqq\inf\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\ \middle|\ F_{\mu}(x)\geqslant r\right\}
. The monotone plan between
\mu
and
\nu
is defined as
\eta\coloneqq(F_{\mu}^{[-1]},F_{\nu}^{[-1]})_{\#}\mathcal{L}_{[0,1]}
. By optimality, it holds for any
p\in[1,\infty)
that

\displaystyle W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\int_{r\in[0,1]}\left|F_{\mu}^{[-1]}(r)-F_{\nu}^{[-1]}(r)\right|^{p}dr.

In the case
p=1
, the expression simplifies further in
W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|F_{\mu}(x)-F_{\nu}(x)\right|dx
. Here note that the integral is taken with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Dual formulation

Let
c:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be any cost of the family
c(x,y)=|x-y|^{p}
.
We follow
[
27
]
in defining the c–transform of a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\varphi^{c}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\},\qquad\varphi^{c}(y)\coloneqq\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)+c(x,y).

The
p-
Wasserstein distance admits a dual reformulation as

\displaystyle W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\varphi\in L^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{c}(y)d\nu-\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)d\mu.

Since the cost inside the supremum cannot decrease if
\varphi
is replaced by
(\varphi^{c})_{c}\coloneqq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}c(x,y)-\varphi^{c}(y)
, one can assume that
\varphi
is of this form. In particular, if
c(x,y)=|x-y|
, one checks that taking twice c–transforms yields that
\varphi
is 1-Lipschitz, and
\varphi^{c}=\varphi
. Hence, denoting
\Lipsp_{1}
the set of 1-Lipschitz functions, there holds

\displaystyle W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\varphi\in\Lipsp_{1}}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)d\nu-\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)d\mu.

Tangent cones in the case
p=2

The regular tangent cone
\rTan_{\mu}\coloneqq\overline{\left\{(id,\nabla\varphi)_{\#}\mu\ \middle|\ \varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\right\}}^{L^{2}_{\mu}}
identifies as a subset of the geometric tangent cone, defined as

\displaystyle\Tan_{\mu}\coloneqq\overline{\left\{\,\lambda\cdot\xi\ \middle|\ (\pi_{x},\pi_{x}+\pi_{v})_{\#}\xi\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu,(\pi_{x}+\pi_{v})_{\#}\xi),\ \text{and}\ \lambda\geqslant 0\,\right\}}^{W_{\mu}}\subset\Psp_{2}(\T\mathbb{R})_{\mu}.

Here,
\lambda\cdot\xi\coloneqq(\pi_{x},\lambda\pi_{v})_{\#}\xi
is the scalar multiplication of a measure field
\xi
, and the distance
W_{\mu}
generalizes the
L^{2}_{\mu}
distance as

\displaystyle W_{\mu}^{2}(\xi,\zeta)\coloneqq\inf_{\eta\in\Gamma_{\mu}(\xi,\zeta)}\int_{(x,v,w)}|v-w|^{2}d\eta,\qquad\text{where}\qquad\Gamma_{\mu}(\xi,\zeta)\coloneqq\left\{\eta\in\Psp_{2}\left(\bigcup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\{x\}\times\T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\ \middle|\ \begin{matrix}(\pi_{x},\pi_{v})_{\#}\eta=\xi,\text{ and}\\
(\pi_{x},\pi_{w})_{\#}\eta=\zeta\end{matrix}\right\}.

We shorten
W_{\mu}(\xi,(id,0)_{\#}\mu)
as
\|\xi\|_{\mu}=\sqrt{\int_{(x,v)}|v|^{2}d\xi}
.
Precisely,
[
18
, Chap. 4]
shows the following link: if
\xi\in\Tan_{\mu}
, then its barycenter, defined as the unique
b\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
such that
\int_{(x,v)}\varphi(x)\psi(v)d\xi=\int_{x}\varphi(x)\psi(b(x))d\mu
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
and
\psi
linear, induces an element of
\rTan_{\mu}
.

Besides the distance
W_{\mu}(\cdot,\cdot)
adapted to the tangent cone at
\mu
, one can define a metric scalar product

\displaystyle\left<\xi,\zeta\right>_{\mu}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left[\|\xi\|_{\mu}^{2}+\|\zeta\|_{\mu}^{2}-W_{\mu}^{2}(\xi,\zeta)\right]=\sup_{\eta\in\Gamma_{\mu}(\xi,\zeta)}\int_{(x,v,w)}\left<v,w\right>d\eta(x,v,w).

The orthogonal
\Tan_{\mu}^{\perp}
, referred to as the set of
solenoidal
measure fields, is defined as the set of
\zeta
such that
\left<\xi,\zeta\right>_{\mu}=0
for any
\xi\in\Tan_{\mu}
.

Submeasure estimate

The following estimate seems to be folklore, and is included for lack of a precise reference.

Lemma 2

(Large-mass submeasures of a given measure are close)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
be a nonnegative Borel measure with finite mass, supported on a compact
K
of diameter
\diam K
. Let
0\leqslant\varepsilon\leqslant\mu(\mathbb{R})
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

\displaystyle\alpha\leqslant\mu,\qquad\beta\leqslant\mu,\qquad\alpha(\mathbb{R})=\beta(\mathbb{R})\geqslant\mu(\mathbb{R})-\varepsilon.

Then for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, there holds
W_{p}^{p}(\alpha,\beta)\leqslant\varepsilon(\diam K)^{p}
.

Proof.

Define
\alpha\wedge\beta
as the nonnegative measure
(\alpha\wedge\beta)(A)\coloneqq\inf_{(A_{n})_{n}\in\mathfrak{P}(A)}\sum_{n}\min(\alpha(A_{n}),\beta(A_{n}))
, where
\mathfrak{P}(A)
is the set of measurable countable partitions
(A_{n})_{n}
of
A
. Then

\displaystyle(\alpha\wedge\beta)(\mathbb{R})=\inf_{(A_{n})_{n}\in\mathfrak{P}(\mathbb{R})}\sum_{n}\min(\alpha(A_{n}),\beta(A_{n}))\geqslant\mu(\mathbb{R})-\sup_{(A_{n})_{n}\in\mathfrak{P}(\mathbb{R})}\sum_{n}\max((\mu-\alpha)(A_{n}),(\mu-\beta)(A_{n})).

Since
\max(a,b)\leqslant a+b
whenever
a,b\geqslant 0
, and
(\mu-\alpha)(A_{n}),(\mu-\beta)(A_{n})\geqslant 0
by assumption,

\displaystyle(\alpha\wedge\beta)(\mathbb{R})\geqslant\mu(\mathbb{R})-\sup_{(A_{n})_{n}\in\mathfrak{P}(\mathbb{R})}\sum_{n}\left[(\mu-\alpha)(A_{n})+(\mu-\beta)(A_{n})\right]=\mu(\mathbb{R})-\left[(\mu-\alpha)(\mathbb{R})+(\mu-\beta)(\mathbb{R})\right]\geqslant\mu(\mathbb{R})-2\varepsilon.

As
\alpha\wedge\beta
is a submeasure of both
\alpha
and
\beta
, we can define a transport plan between
\alpha
and
\beta
sending
\alpha\wedge\beta
on itself, and the remaining mass of
\alpha
on that of
\beta
. The previous estimate shows that
\alpha(\mathbb{R})-(\alpha\wedge\beta)(\mathbb{R})\leqslant\varepsilon
, and since both
\alpha
and
\beta
are supported on
K
, the mass travels at most
\diam K
. Consequently,
W_{p}^{p}(\alpha,\beta)\leqslant\varepsilon\left(\diam K\right)^{p}
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1

The strategy for
Theorem

1
is to exploit the dual reformulation of the Wasserstein distance. Informally, when the quotient
W(\mu,\mu_{h})/h
approaches its maximal value
1
, the mass of
\mu
must concentrate on points on which Kantorovich pairs
(\varphi,\varphi^{c})
satisfy a certain 3-point inequality. For the cost
c(x,y)=|x-y|
, this inequality implies that
\varphi
looks like a downward pointing corner with at least a certain angle: this can happen on at most countably many points, that must be at some controlled distance from each other. For
c(x,y)=|x-y|^{p}
with
p>1
, we have no such interpretation, but computation shows that these points must be sufficiently separated, otherwise the condition
\varphi^{c}\ominus\varphi\leqslant c
breaks. The “separation” precisely enforces the presence of holes of size comparable to
h
on which
\mu
puts very few mass, and intersecting over countably many scales, one gets to the porous sets of
Definition

1
. The converse is based on the construction of a particular Kantorovich potential.

We first prove an estimate which is specific to dimension 1.

Lemma 3

(Pointwise bound on the monotone plan)
.

Let
\zeta\in\Psp_{2}(\T\mathbb{R})_{\mu}
be such that
|v|\leqslant R
for some
R>0
and
\zeta-
a.e.
(x,v)\in\T\mathbb{R}
. Then, the optimal transport plan
\eta\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(\zeta))
is concentrated on pairs
(x,y)
such that
|y-x|\leqslant R
.

Proof.

Let
\nu\coloneqq\exp_{\mu}(\zeta)
, and denote
F_{\mu},F_{\nu}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
the respective distribution functions of
\mu,\nu
, i.e.
F_{\mu}(x)=\mu((-\infty,x])
.
By the explicit expression of the optimal transport plan in dimension one
[
24
, Chap. 2]
,
\eta
is concentrated on pairs
(x,y)
such that for some
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle x=\inf\left\{\overline{x}\ \middle|\ F_{\mu}(\overline{x})\geqslant r\right\},\qquad\text{and}\qquad y=\inf\left\{\overline{y}\ \middle|\ F_{\nu}(\overline{y})\geqslant r\right\}.

As
F_{\mu},F_{\nu}
are nondecreasing and upper semi-continuous, the
r
associated to
(x,y)
also satisfies
F_{\mu}(x)\geqslant r
and
F_{\nu}(y)\geqslant r
(although the inequality can be strict in case of Dirac mass). By assumption, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

\displaystyle F_{\nu}(x+R)=\int_{(y,w)}{\textrm{1\hskip-2.58334ptI}}_{(-\infty,x+R]}(y+w)d\zeta\geqslant\int{\textrm{1\hskip-2.58334ptI}}_{(-\infty,x]}(y)d\mu=F_{\mu}(x)\geqslant r,

hence
y\leqslant x+R
. Symmetrically, one has
\mu=\exp_{\nu}(\gamma)
for
\gamma\coloneqq(\pi_{x}+\pi_{v},-\pi_{v})_{\#}\zeta
, which also satisfies
|v|\leqslant R
for
\gamma-
a.e.
(x,v)
; hence
F_{\mu}(y+R)\geqslant F_{\nu}(y)\geqslant r
for any
(x,y)
in
\supp\eta
and associated

, and we conclude that
x\leqslant y+R
.

Remark 4
.

As a consequence, the property
Equation

1
is independent of
p\in[1,\infty)
. Indeed, by Cauchy-Schwarz, the Wasserstein distances are ordered as
W_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leqslant W_{q}(\mu,\nu)
for
p\leqslant q
. On the other hand, by
3
, the monotone transport plan
\eta_{h}
between
\mu
and
\mu_{h}
satisfies
|y-x|\leqslant h
almost everywhere. Since this plan is optimal for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{W_{1}(\mu,\mu_{h})}{h}\leqslant\frac{W_{p}(\mu,\mu_{h})}{h}=\left(\frac{W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\mu_{h})}{h^{p}}\right)^{1/p}=\left(\frac{\int|y-x|^{p}d\eta_{h}}{h^{p}}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\left(\frac{h^{p-1}\int|y-x|d\eta_{h}}{h^{p}}\right)^{1/p}=\left(\frac{W_{1}(\mu,\mu_{h})}{h}\right)^{1/p}.

Hence, both the set of measures
\mu
such that
Equation

1
holds, and such that
W_{p}(\mu,\mu_{h})=o(h)
, are independent of
p\in[1,\infty)
.

The following lemma is the core of the argument of
Theorem

1
. It states that for any
\varphi
, the points where
\frac{1}{2}\left[\varphi^{c}(x-h)+\varphi^{c}(x+h)\right]-\varphi(x)
is comparable to

must be separated by some distance itself comparable to
h
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\kappa_{\gamma}\coloneqq(2\gamma-1)^{1/p}
for any
\gamma\in(1/2,1]
. Given
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma\in(1/2,1]

3.1
Reduction to centred measure fields

We start by proving that whenever
\zeta\in\Psp_{2}(\T\mathbb{R})_{\mu}
satisfies

6

\zeta^{0}\coloneqq(\pi_{x},\pi_{v}-{\text{Bary}\left(\xi\right)}(\pi_{x}))_{\#}\zeta

\zeta
, also does. The intuition behind is that map-induced and centred measure fields are orthogonal for the metric scalar product, i.e. for any

\displaystyle\left<\zeta^{0},(id,b)_{\#}\mu\right>_{\mu}=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{v\in\T_{x}\mathbb{R}}\left<v,b(x)\right>d\zeta_{x}^{0}(v)d\mu(x)=0.

The same computation shows that
\|\zeta\|_{\mu}^{2}=\|\zeta^{0}\|_{\mu}^{2}+\|b\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}^{2}
. This “infinitesimal” orthogonality appears in a weaker form before the limit, as shown by the following lemma.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\zeta=(\pi_{x},\pi_{v}+b(\pi_{x}))_{\#}\zeta^{0}
for some
\zeta^{0}
with null barycenter. Then

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\searrow 0}\frac{W^{2}(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\zeta))}{h^{2}}\leqslant\limsup_{h\searrow 0}\frac{W^{2}(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\zeta^{0}))}{h^{2}}+\|b\|_{\mu}^{2}.

(7)

Consequently, if the left hand-side of

7

\|\zeta\|_{\mu}^{2}=\|\zeta^{0}\|_{\mu}^{2}+\|b\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}^{2}

\zeta^{0}

6

Proof.

For any
\xi,\zeta\in\Psp_{2}(\T\mathbb{R})_{\mu}

\displaystyle\left|\limsup_{h\searrow 0}\frac{W(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\xi))}{h}-\limsup_{h\searrow 0}\frac{W(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\zeta))}{h}\right|\leqslant W_{\mu}(\xi,\zeta)=\sqrt{W_{\mu}^{2}(\xi^{0},\zeta^{0})+\|b_{\xi}-b_{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}^{2}}.

Here we used that
(1-t)|v|^{2}+t|w|^{2}-t(1-t)|v-w|^{2}=|(1-t)v+tw|^{2}
for any vectors
v,w
. Therefore, the measure field
\gamma_{n+1}
belongs to
\Xi[\mu]
. As
n
goes to
\infty
, the map
f_{n}
converges in
\mu-
measure towards some function that coincides with
f
whenever

, and lies in
[R,R+\varepsilon]
otherwise. Taking
\varepsilon>0
small enough, then
n
large enough, we can approximate
\min(f,R)
in
L^{2}_{\mu}
with arbitrarily small error. Since convergence of maps in

implies convergence of their associated symmetric measure field in
W_{\mu}
, and since
\Xi[\mu]
is
W_{\mu}-
closed by the first point, we conclude.
∎

Lastly, we turn to the main step of the reduction. The strategy is to first restrict to a nonatomic submeasure of
\mu
on which
\zeta
splits mass; then to show that taking averages of the “positive” and “negative” parts does not break the limit sup condition

; then to normalize the obtained measure field.

Lemma 9
.

Assume that
\zeta\in\Xi[\mu]
for some
\zeta\notin\Tan_{\mu}
. Then there exists a nonatomic measure
\sigma\leqslant\mu
of positive mass such that
\xi\in\Xi[\sigma]
for the symmetric unit measure field
\xi\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left[(id,-1)_{\#}\sigma+(id,1)_{\#}\sigma\right]
.

Proof.

We modify
\zeta
in successive steps to obtain a symmetric unit measure field, and show that each steps preserves the corresponding set
\Xi
.

Step 0: restriction to a suitable set

Decompose
\mu=\mu^{a}+\mu^{d}
, with
\mu^{a}
purely atomic and
\mu^{d}
atomless.
By
[
26
, Theorem 3.7]
(see also
[
5
, Proposition 2.9]
), the tangent cone to
\mu
is made of measure fields
\gamma
of the form
\gamma^{a}+(id,f)_{\#}\mu^{d}
, where
\pi_{x\#}\gamma^{a}=\mu^{a}
is a measure field on
\T\mathbb{R}
with first marginal (hence same mass as) the atomic part
\mu^{a}
, and
f\in L^{2}_{\mu^{d}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
. Hence
\zeta\notin\Tan_{\mu}
if and only if
\zeta=\zeta^{a}+\zeta^{d}
, with
\pi_{x\#}\zeta^{a}=\mu^{a}
,
\pi_{x\#}\zeta^{d}=\mu^{d}
and
\zeta^{d}
not induced by a map. In consequence, there exists a measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\mu^{a}(A)=0,\qquad\mu^{d}(A)>0,\qquad\text{and for }\mu-\text{a.e. }x\in A,\quad\int_{v}|v|^{2}d\zeta_{x}>0.

By
8
, the restricted measure field
\zeta_{A}\coloneqq\zeta\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}\{(x,v)\,|\,x\in A\}
belongs to
\Xi[\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}A]
. For the sequel, denote
\nu\coloneqq\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}A
.

Step 1: reduction to a symmetric measure field

Now, split
\zeta_{A}
in a “left” and “right” part as follows: consider
\alpha=\alpha(dx,dv,dw)\in\Gamma_{\nu,o}(\zeta_{A},\xi)
, with again
\xi\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left[(id,-1)_{\#}\nu+(id,1)_{\#}\nu\right]
the symmetric unit field. Then
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\left[\alpha^{-}+\alpha^{+}\right]
, where
(\pi_{x},\pi_{w})_{\#}\alpha^{\pm}=(id,\pm 1)_{\#}\nu
. Using the other marginal condition, we can write
\zeta_{A}=(\pi_{x},\pi_{v})_{\#}\alpha=\frac{1}{2}(\pi_{x},\pi_{v})_{\#}\alpha^{-}+\frac{1}{2}(\pi_{x},\pi_{v})_{\#}\alpha^{+}\eqqcolon\frac{1}{2}\left[\zeta_{A}^{-}+\zeta_{A}^{+}\right]
.

Denote
f^{\pm}\in L^{2}_{\nu}
the respective barycenters of
\zeta_{A}^{\pm}
. Then
\frac{1}{2}\left[f^{-}+f^{+}\right]
is the barycenter of
\zeta_{A}
, i.e.
0
, hence
f^{-}=-f^{+}
. We show that
f^{+}(x)>0
for
\nu-
a.e.

. By construction,
\alpha=(\alpha_{x})_{x}\otimes\nu
, with
\alpha_{x}
identified with an optimal plan between
(\zeta_{A})_{x}
and
\xi_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\delta_{-1}+\delta_{1}\right]
. Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{v}|v|^{2}d\zeta_{A}+\int_{w}|w|^{2}d\xi_{x}-W^{2}((\zeta_{A})_{x},\xi_{x})\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{(v,w)}\frac{1}{2}\left[|v|^{2}+|w|^{2}-|v-w|^{2}\right]d\alpha_{x}=\int_{(v,w)}\left<v,w\right>d\alpha_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{(v,w)}\left<v,-1\right>d\alpha_{x}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{(v,w)}\left<v,1\right>d\alpha_{x}^{+}=f^{+}(x).

By
[
4
, Lemma 2.9]
, the leftmost term is always nonnegative, and 0 if and only if
(\zeta_{A})_{x}=\delta_{0}
, which may happen only on a
\nu-
negligible set by assumption. Hence
f^{+}(x)>0
for
\nu-
a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

We now replace
\zeta_{A}
by
\gamma\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left[(id,f^{-})_{\#}\nu+(id,f^{+})_{\#}\nu\right]
as follows: on the one hand,

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|v|^{2}d\zeta_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f^{-}(x)|^{2}d\nu+\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f^{+}(x)|^{2}d\nu\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{(x,v)}|v-f^{-}(x)|^{2}d\zeta_{A}^{-}+\int_{(x,v)}|v-f^{+}(x)|^{2}d\zeta_{A}^{+}\right]

\displaystyle=\|\gamma\|_{\nu}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in\{-,+\}}\int_{(x,v)}|v-f^{s}(x)|^{2}d\zeta_{A}^{s}.

(10)

On the other hand, using the dual reformulation, there holds

\displaystyle W^{2}(\nu,\exp_{\nu}(h\cdot\zeta_{A}))=\sup_{\varphi\in L^{1}_{\mu}}\int_{(x,v)\in\T\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{c}(x+hv)d\zeta_{A}-\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)d\mu.

(11)

For each fixed
\varphi
and

, the function

is concave. Hence, taking

inside the integral, we can write that

\displaystyle\int_{(x,v)\in\T\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{c}(x+hv)d\zeta_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in\{-,+\}}\int_{(x,v)\in\T\mathbb{R}}\left[\varphi^{c}(x+hv)-|(x+hv)-(x+hf^{s}(x))|^{2}\right]d\zeta_{A}^{s}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in\{-,+\}}\int_{(x,v)}|(x+hv)-(x+hf^{s}(x))|^{2}d\zeta_{A}^{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in\{-,+\}}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left[\varphi^{c}(x+hf^{s}(x))-0\right]d\nu+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\sum_{s\in\{-,+\}}\int_{(x,v)}|v-f^{s}(x)|^{2}d\zeta_{A}^{s}.

In the first term of the last line, we recognize
\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{c}(y)d\exp_{\nu}(h\cdot\gamma)
. Plugging this inequality in
Equation

11
, we obtain

\displaystyle W^{2}(\nu,\exp_{\nu}(h\cdot\zeta_{A}))

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\varphi\in L^{1}_{\mu}}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{c}(y)d\exp_{\nu}(h\cdot\gamma)-\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)d\mu+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\sum_{s\in\{-,+\}}\int_{(x,v)}|v-f^{s}(x)|^{2}d\zeta_{A}^{s}

\displaystyle=W^{2}(\nu,\exp_{\nu}(h\cdot\gamma))+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\sum_{s\in\{-,+\}}\int_{(x,v)}|v-f^{s}(x)|^{2}d\zeta_{A}^{s}.

Dividing by
h>0
and taking the limit sup in

, we get from
Section

3.2
that
\gamma\in\Xi[\nu]
.

Step 2: reduction to a unit symmetric measure field

At this point, we know that there exists
f\coloneqq f^{+}\in L^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that
f(x)>0
for
\nu-
a.e. point, and the measure field
\gamma\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left[(id,-f)_{\#}\nu+(id,f)_{\#}\nu\right]
belongs to
\Xi[\nu]
. By
8
, we might multiply

by some large

so that

, and further restrict to the measurable set

, this completes the proof.
∎

3.3
Proof of
Theorem

2

One of the implications of
Theorem

2
follows from the successive reductions of the previous sections. For the other implication, one needs to show that the mass that

puts around a set

does not play a significant role for small

.

Proof.

Assume that
\zeta\notin\Tan_{\mu}
satisfies
\limsup_{h\searrow 0}W_{2}(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\zeta))/h=\|\zeta\|_{\mu}
. By
7
, the centred measure field
\zeta^{0}\coloneqq(\pi_{x},\pi_{v}-{\text{Bary}\left(\zeta\right)}(\pi_{x}))_{\#}\zeta
also does.
\limsup_{h\searrow 0}
By
9
, there exists a submeasure
\sigma\leqslant\mu^{d}
such that
Equation

6
holds, i.e.
W_{2}(\sigma,\sigma_{h})/h\to 1
when
h\searrow 0
. By
Theorem

1
,
\sigma
gives mass to an element of
{A}
, and so does
\mu^{d}
.

Conversely, assume that
\mu^{d}
puts mass on some
B\in{A}
. Since any subset
A\subset B
also belongs to
{A}
, we can choose a compact
A\in{A}
such that
\mu^{d}(A)>0
. Let

\displaystyle\zeta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left[(id,-1)_{\#}\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}A+(id,1)_{\#}\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}A\right]+(id,0)_{\#}\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}A^{c}.

We claim that
\limsup_{h\searrow 0}W(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\zeta))/h=\|\zeta\|_{\mu}=\sqrt{\mu(A)}
: the strategy is to show that when passing to the limit, the neighbourhood of the compact
A
receives very few mass, and the quantity
W(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\zeta))
is very close to the sum of the contribution of
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}A
and
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}A^{c}
.

Denote by
\eta
the optimal transport plan between
\mu
and
\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\zeta)
, which is concentrated on pairs
(x,y)
such that
|y-x|\leqslant h
by
3
. Let
\mu_{A}\coloneqq\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}A
and
\eta_{A}\coloneqq\eta\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}(A\times\mathbb{R})
. Then, by restriction of optimality
[
27
, Theorem 4.6]
and the second triangular inequality,

\displaystyle W^{2}(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(h\cdot\zeta))

\displaystyle=\int_{(x,y)}|y-x|^{2}d\eta\geqslant\int_{(x,y),x\in A}|y-x|^{2}d\eta=W^{2}(\mu_{A},\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A})

\displaystyle\geqslant W^{2}(\mu_{A},(\mu_{A})_{h})-W(\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A},(\mu_{A})_{h})\left(2W(\mu_{A},\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A})+W(\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A},(\mu_{A})_{h})\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant W^{2}(\mu_{A},(\mu_{A})_{h})-W(\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A},(\mu_{A})_{h})\times\left(2h+(h+h)\right).

(12)

Let
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(\tau_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be the vanishing sequences associated to
A
, i.e. such that for any
x\in A
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the intersection
A\cap{B}(x,s_{n})
is contained in
\overline{{B}}(x,\tau_{n}s_{n})
. Since
A
is compact, we can pick successively a finite set of points
\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{K}\}\subset A
such that
A\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}{B}(b_{k},s_{n})
, and for any
k\neq\ell
, there holds
b_{k}\notin{B}(b_{\ell},s_{n})
. Then, the closed balls
B_{k}\coloneqq\overline{{B}}(b_{k},\tau_{n}s_{n})
cover
A
, and are separated from each other by at least
s_{n}-\tau_{n}s_{n}
.

Consider
h_{n}\coloneqq\frac{s_{n}-\tau_{n}s_{n}-\varepsilon_{n}}{2}
, where
\varepsilon_{n}>0
and
\varepsilon_{n}/h_{n}\to_{n}0
. For this choice, the inflated balls
B_{k}^{h_{n}}\coloneqq\overline{{B}}(b_{k},\tau_{n}s_{n}+h_{n})
are disjoint. We distinguish the contributions of each
B_{k}^{h_{n}}
by using the convexity of the squared Wasserstein distance in the Banach sense; denoting
\mu_{A,k}\coloneqq\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}(A\cap B_{k})
, and
\eta_{A,k}\coloneqq\eta\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}((A\cap B_{k})\times\mathbb{R})
, there holds

\displaystyle W^{2}(\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A},(\mu_{A})_{h_{n}})\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{K}W^{2}(\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A,k},(\mu_{A,k})_{h_{n}}).

Now, both measures
\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A,k}
and
(\mu_{A,k})_{h_{n}}
are submeasures of
\exp_{\mu}(h_{n}\cdot\zeta)\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}B_{k}^{h_{n}}
. Indeed,
\pi_{y\#}\eta=\exp_{\mu}(h_{n}\cdot\zeta)
, and the
\eta-
a.e. estimate on
|y-x|
shows that
\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A,k}
is supported on
B_{k}^{h_{n}}
. On the other hand,
\zeta
coincides with the unit symmetric measure field on
A
. To apply
2
, we only need to estimate the difference between
\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A,k}(\mathbb{R})=(\mu_{A,k})_{h_{n}}(\mathbb{R})=\mu(A\cap B_{k})
and the mass that
\exp_{\mu}(h_{n}\cdot\zeta)
puts on
B_{k}^{h_{n}}
. But

\displaystyle\int_{(x,v)}{\textrm{1\hskip-2.58334ptI}}_{B_{k}^{h_{n}}}(x+h_{n}v)d\zeta

\displaystyle=\int_{(x,v),x\in A}\frac{1}{2}\left[{\textrm{1\hskip-2.58334ptI}}_{B_{k}^{h_{n}}}(x-h_{n})+{\textrm{1\hskip-2.58334ptI}}_{B_{k}^{h_{n}}}(x+h_{n})\right]d\mu+\int_{x\in A^{c}}{\textrm{1\hskip-2.58334ptI}}_{B_{k}^{h_{n}}}(x)d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mu(A\cap(B_{k}^{h_{n}}+h_{n}))+\mu(A\cap(B_{k}^{h_{n}}-h_{n}))\right]+\mu\left(A^{c}\cap B_{k}^{h_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu(A\cap B_{k})+\frac{1}{2}\mu\left(A\cap(B_{k}^{2h_{n}}\setminus B_{k})\right)+\mu\left(A^{c}\cap B_{k}^{h_{n}}\right).

Since
A\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}
, and
B_{k},B_{\ell}
are separated by at least
s_{n}-\tau_{n}s_{n}=2h_{n}+\varepsilon_{n}>2h_{n}
, the sets
A\cap(B_{k}^{2h_{n}}\setminus B_{k})
are empty. On the other hand,
B_{k}^{h_{n}}\subset(A\cap B_{k})^{\tau_{n}s_{n}+h_{n}}
by definition. Therefore, the difference of masses is inferior to
\mu((A\cap B_{k})^{\tau_{n}s_{n}+h_{n}}\setminus A)
. Summing over

, and applying
2
,

\displaystyle W^{2}(\pi_{y\#}\eta_{A},(\mu_{A})_{h_{n}})\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mu((A\cap B_{k})^{\tau_{n}s_{n}+h_{n}}\setminus A)\left(\diam B_{k}^{h_{n}}\right)^{2}\leqslant 4(\tau_{n}s_{n}+h_{n})^{2}\mu(A^{\tau_{n}s_{n}+h_{n}}\setminus A).

Note that
\tau_{n}s_{n}=\frac{\tau_{n}}{1-\tau_{n}}(2h_{n}+\varepsilon_{n})\leqslant c_{n}h_{n}
from some bounded sequence
(c_{n})_{n}
. Hence, we obtain from

3.3
that

\displaystyle W^{2}(\mu,\exp_{\mu}(h_{n}\cdot\zeta))\geqslant W^{2}(\mu_{A},(\mu_{A})_{h_{n}})-8c_{n}h_{n}^{2}\sqrt{\mu\left(A^{c_{n}h_{n}}\setminus A\right)}.

As
A
is compact, there holds
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A^{c_{n}h_{n}}\setminus A=\emptyset
, hence
\mu\left(A^{c_{n}h_{n}}\setminus A\right)=O(h_{n})
. To conclude, we only have to show that
W^{2}(\mu_{A},(\mu_{A})_{h_{n}})/h_{n}^{2}\to_{n}\mu(A)
; this is almost the same computation as in
Theorem

1
, up to the choice of

instead of
\frac{s_{n}+\tau_{n}s_{n}}{2}
, and the perturbation by
(2\tau_{n}s_{n}+\varepsilon_{n})/2=o(h_{n})
does not affect the result.
∎

4
Measures for which the limit sup vanishes

4.1
Regularity of the distribution function

The situation for measures
\mu
such that
W_{1}(\mu,\mu_{h})=o(h)
is much less clear. All absolutely continuous measures have this property, and
14
below exhibits some non-absolutely continuous measure that also does. Of course, measures that give mass to some
A\in{A}
do not. In between, we are only able to say the following.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mu\in\Psp(\mathbb{R})
, and denote
F_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
the function
F_{\mu}(x)\coloneqq\mu((-\infty,x])
. It is equivalent that

1.

W_{1}(\mu,\mu_{h})=o(h)
;

2.

for any vanishing sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1]
, there exists a subsequence
(h_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left[F_{\mu}(x-h_{n_{k}})+F_{\mu}(x+h_{n_{k}})\right]-F_{\mu}(x)}{h_{n_{k}}}\right|\leqslant g(x)\qquad\text{for almost every }x\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and any }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(13)

By
4
,
Item

1
implies that
W_{p}(\mu,\mu_{h})=o(h)
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Item

2
can be understood as a regularity property in the following sense: the function
F_{\mu}
is nondecreasing, so differentiable almost everywhere. Therefore, the left hand-side of
Equation

13
converges to 0 for
\mathcal{L}-
almost every
x
. However,
Equation

13
additionally imposes that the error is dominated by an
L^{1}
function
g
.

Proof.

By
[
24
, Proposition 2.17]
, the 1-dimensional 1-Wasserstein distance is equivalently computed as

\displaystyle\frac{W_{1}(\mu,\mu_{h})}{h}=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{h}\left|F_{\mu_{h}}(x)-F_{\mu}(x)\right|dx=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{h}\left|\frac{F_{\mu}(x-h)+F_{\mu}(x+h)}{2}-F_{\mu}(x)\right|dx.

Assume
Item

1
holds, and let
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1]
be a vanishing sequence. Choose a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
W_{1}(\mu,\mu_{h_{n_{k}}})\leqslant h_{n_{k}}2^{-k}
, and let
g\coloneqq\sum_{k}\left|\frac{1}{2}\left[F_{\mu}(\cdot-h_{n_{k}})+F_{\mu}(\cdot+h_{n_{k}})\right]-F_{\mu}\right|/h_{n_{k}}
. Then
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
Item

2
holds.

Conversely, assume
Item

2
. Since
F_{\mu}
is nondecreasing and bounded, it has bounded variation, hence is differentiable Lebesgue-almost everywhere
[
2
, Corollary 3.29]
. Consequently, for
dx-
almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, there holds
\psi_{h}(x)\coloneqq\left|\frac{1}{2}\left[F_{\mu}(x-h)+F_{\mu}(x+h)\right]-F_{\mu}(x)\right|/h\to 0
as
h\searrow 0
. Let
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a vanishing sequence realizing the limit sup of
W_{1}(\mu,\mu_{h})/h
when
h
goes to 0. By assumption, along some subsequence
(n_{k})_{k}
, the functions
\psi_{h_{n_{k}}}
are uniformly bounded by an
L^{1}
function. Applying Lebesgue’s dominated convergence, we conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\psi_{h_{n_{k}}}\|_{L^{1}}=\limsup_{h\searrow 0}W_{1}(\mu,\mu_{h})/h=0
, which proves
Item

1
. ∎

4.2
Classification of uniform Cantor measures

Instead of addressing the full characterization of measures
\mu
such that
W(\mu,\mu_{h})=o(h)
, we reduce to a subclass of uniform measures on Cantor sets. To avoid irrelevant complications, we only consider nonincreasing sequences
(\alpha_{n})_{n}\subset(0,1)
, which therefore admit a limit in
[0,1]
. If
I
is a bounded interval of
\mathbb{R}
, the notation
\alpha*I
refers to the interval with the same center as
I
, and length equal to
\alpha
times that of
I
.

Definition 11

(Cantor set
C(\alpha)
)
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be a nonincreasing sequence. Denote
C^{0}=I^{0}_{0}\coloneqq[0,1]
. Assuming that
C^{n}
is defined as the union of
2^{n}
disjoint intervals
(I^{n}_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{2^{n}}
, each with the same length
\delta_{n}
, define
C^{n+1}
by deleting the proportion
\alpha_{n}
of the middle of each
I^{n}_{\ell}
:

\displaystyle C^{n+1}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\ell\in\llbracket 1,2^{n}\rrbracket}I^{n}_{\ell}\setminus(\alpha_{n}*I^{n}_{\ell}).

Then
C^{n+1}
is again given by
2^{n+1}
intervals. The set
C(\alpha)
is defined as the intersection of all
(C^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Definition 12

(Uniform measure on
C(\alpha)
)
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and consider the notations of
Definition

11
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mu^{n}\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{L}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.77496pt\vrule height=0.6458pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.01385pt}C^{n}\right)/\mathcal{L}(C^{n})
. The measure
\mu_{\alpha}
is defined as the
W-
limit of
(\mu^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Equivalently,

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha}(I^{n}_{\ell})=2^{-n}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }\ell\in\llbracket 1,2^{n}\rrbracket.

(14)

For instance, the classical triadic Cantor set is obtained with the constant sequence
\alpha_{n}\equiv 1/3
, and
\mu_{\alpha}
is the derivative of the Cantor staircase.
We are interested in the sequences
\alpha
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\searrow 0}\frac{W(\mu_{\alpha},\mu_{\alpha,h})}{h}=0.

(15)

The sets
C(\alpha)
were studied in
[
19
]
. In their terminology, a set
C
is Tkadlec if there exists a measure concentrated on
C
that gives mass to some porous set. They show that
C(\alpha)
is Tkadlec if and only if
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}>0
. What we show in
13
and
14
can be reformulated as “
\mu_{\alpha}
satisfies
Equation

15
if and only if
C(\alpha)
is non-Tkadlec”. However, a quick computation yields that
\mathcal{L}(C(\alpha))>0
if and only if
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{n}<\infty
. Therefore, any vanishing sequence
\alpha
that is not summable generates a set
C(\alpha)
of null Lebesgue measure, but such that
\mu_{\alpha}
still satisfies
Equation

15
.

Proposition 13
.

Assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}\eqqcolon\overline{\alpha}>0
. Then
Equation

15
fails.

Proof.

By
3
, the property
Equation

15
is independent of the first terms of the sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n}
, and we might consider that
\alpha_{n}\geqslant\overline{\alpha}/2
for any
n
.
Denote again
\delta_{n}
the length of any interval
I^{n}_{\ell}
. Let
h_{n}\coloneqq\alpha_{n}\delta_{n}/2
and
\psi:x\mapsto\inf_{y\in C^{n}}d(x,y)
. As
C(\alpha)\subset C^{n}
, there holds
\psi(x)=0
for
\mu_{\alpha}-
a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand, since
\psi
is 1-Lipschitz, it is admissible in the dual problem for the 1-Wasserstein distance, and there holds

\displaystyle\frac{W_{1}(\mu_{\alpha},\mu_{\alpha,h_{n}})}{h_{n}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{h_{n}}\left[\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\psi(y)d\mu_{\alpha,h_{n}}-\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\psi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}\right]=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\psi(x-h_{n})+\psi(x+h_{n})}{2h_{n}}d\mu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2^{n-1}}\int_{x\in I^{n-1}_{\ell}}\frac{\psi(x-h_{n})+\psi(x+h_{n})}{2h_{n}}d\mu_{\alpha}=2^{n-1}\int_{x\in I^{n-1}_{1}}\frac{\psi(x-h_{n})+\psi(x+h_{n})}{2h_{n}}d\mu_{\alpha}.

(16)

In the last equality, we used the fact that each
I^{n-1}_{\ell}
is a translation of
I^{n-1}_{1}
, and the corresponding self-similarity of
\mu_{\alpha}
.
The intersection
C^{n}\cap I^{n-1}_{1}
is the union of two intervals
I_{L}
and
I_{R}
, respectively the left and right connected components of
I^{n-1}_{1}\setminus(\alpha_{n}*I^{n}_{1})
.

For any
x\in[\max I_{L}-h_{n}/2,\max I_{L}]
, the point
x+h_{n}
is at distance at least
h_{n}/2
from any point of
I_{L}
. On the other hand,
\min I_{R}-\max I_{L}=2h_{n}
, so that
x+h_{n}
is at distance at least
h_{n}
of
I_{R}
. Therefore
\psi(x+h_{n})=\inf_{z\in C_{n}}|x-z|\geqslant h_{n}/2
. Repeating the symmetric reasoning on
I_{R}
and using that
\psi\geqslant 0
, we get the lower bound

\displaystyle\int_{x\in I^{n-1}_{1}}\frac{\psi(x-h_{n})+\psi(x+h_{n})}{2h_{n}}d\mu_{\alpha}\geqslant\frac{\mu([\max I_{L}-h_{n}/2,\max I_{L}])+\mu([\min I_{R}-h_{n}/2,\min I_{R}])}{4}.

To conclude, we estimate
\mu_{\alpha}([\max I_{L}-h_{n}/2,\max I_{L}])
from below. Let
M
be large enough so that
2^{-M}\leqslant\overline{\alpha}/8
. We consider the rightmost interval
J_{m}
of
C^{m}\cap I_{L}
as
m
grows: for
m=n+1
, the interval
J_{m}
is contained in
[\max I_{L}-\delta_{n}/2,\max I_{L}]
. In general,
J_{m}\subset[\max I_{L}-2^{-(m-n)}\delta_{n},\max I_{L}]
. When
m=n+M
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{-(m-n)}\delta_{n}=2^{-M}\delta_{n}\leqslant\frac{\overline{\alpha}}{8}\delta_{n}\leqslant\frac{\alpha_{n}}{4}\delta_{n}=\frac{h_{n}}{2},

which implies that
J_{n+M}\subset[\max I_{L}-h_{n}/2,\max I_{L}]
. Hence
\mu_{\alpha}([\max I_{L}-h_{n}/2,\max I_{L}])\geqslant\mu_{\alpha}(J_{n+M})=2^{-(n+M)}
by
Equation

14
. Repeating the argument on
I_{R}
, we conclude that the estimate of
Section

4.2
can be pursued into a lower bound by
2^{-M-2}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 14
.

Assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
. Then
Equation

15
holds.

Proof.

Again by
3
, we can discard the first terms of the sequence and assume that
\alpha_{n}\leqslant 1/2
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By
[
24
, Proposition 2.17]
, the 1-dimensional 1-Wasserstein distance can be computed by

\displaystyle W_{1}(\mu_{\alpha},\mu_{\alpha,h})=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|F_{\mu_{\alpha,h}}(x)-F_{\mu_{\alpha}}(x)\right|dx,

with
F_{\mu_{\alpha}}
and
F_{\mu_{\alpha,h}}
the respective distribution functions of
\mu_{\alpha}
and
\mu_{\alpha,h}
. In our case, there holds in addition that
F_{\mu_{\alpha,h}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left[F_{\mu_{\alpha}}(x-h)+F_{\mu_{\alpha}}(x+h)\right]
. We can further represent
F_{\mu_{\alpha}}
as a series by mimicking the construction of
\mu_{\alpha}
as a limit of
(\mu^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
: let
f_{-1}(x)\coloneqq F_{\mu^{0}}(x)=\max(0,\min(1,x))
, and for each
n
, let
f_{n}\coloneqq F_{\mu^{n+1}}-F_{\mu^{n}}
. Then
f_{n}=0
out of
C^{n}
, and
f_{n}
repeats the same pattern on each interval of
C^{n}
. Precisely, if
[a,a+\delta_{n}]
and
[b,b+\delta_{n}]
are two intervals of
C^{n}
, then
f_{n}(a+z)=f_{n}(b+z)=f_{n}(z)
for any
z\in[0,\delta_{n}]
, with

\displaystyle f_{n}(z)=\begin{cases}2^{-(n+1)}\frac{z}{\delta_{n+1}}-2^{-n}\frac{z}{\delta_{n}}&z\in[0,\delta_{n+1}],\\
2^{-(n+1)}-2^{-n}\frac{z}{\delta_{n}}&z\in(\delta_{n+1},\delta_{n}-\delta_{n+1}),\\
2^{-(n+1)}+2^{-(n+1)}\frac{z-(\delta_{n}-\delta_{n+1})}{\delta_{n+1}}-2^{-n}\frac{z}{\delta_{n}}&z\in[\delta_{n}-\delta_{n+1},\delta_{n}].\end{cases}

(17)

Recall that
\delta_{n+1}=\delta_{n}(1-\alpha_{n})/2
. One checks that
\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}=f_{n}(\delta_{n+1})=2^{-(n+1)}\alpha_{n}
.
Hence
\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}f_{n}
converges uniformly towards
F_{\mu_{\alpha}}
. We now bound each term of the series.

For each
h
, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
h\in[\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}\delta_{n+1},\sqrt{\alpha_{n-1}}\delta_{n})
. We have to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|\frac{1}{2}\left[F_{\mu_{\alpha}}(x-h)+F_{\mu_{\alpha}}(x+h)\right]-F_{\mu_{\alpha}}(x)\right|}{h}dx\leqslant\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|\frac{1}{2}\left[f_{k}(x-h)+f_{k}(x+h)\right]-f_{k}(x)\right|}{h}dx.

We treat differently the indices
k<n
and
k\geqslant n
. For any
k\geqslant n
, the application
x\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\left[f_{k}(x-h)+f_{k}(x+h)\right]-f_{k}(x)
vanishes for any point
x
at distance at least
h
from
C^{n}
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|\frac{1}{2}\left[f_{k}(x-h)+f_{k}(x+h)\right]-f_{k}(x)\right|}{h}dx\leqslant 2\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{\|f_{k}\|_{\infty}}{h}\mathcal{L}\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\ \middle|\ d(x,C^{n})\leqslant h\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-(k+1)}\alpha_{k}}{h}2^{n}\left(\mathcal{L}(I^{n}_{1})+2h\right)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}2^{n-k}\left(\frac{\delta_{n+1}}{h}+2\right)\leqslant\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}2^{n-k}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}}+2\right)\leqslant\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}+2\alpha_{n}.

Here we used that
\alpha_{k}\leqslant\alpha_{n}
for
k\geqslant n
.
Now, for
k<n
, the application
x\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\left[f_{k}(x-h)+f_{k}(x+h)\right]-f_{k}(x)
differs from 0 only around the points of discontinuity of
f_{k}^{\prime}
. For
\alpha_{n}\leqslant 1/2
, the Lipschitz constant of
f_{k}
can be seen in
Equation

17
to be the slope of the middle segment, so that
\left|\frac{1}{2}\left[f_{k}(x-h)+f_{k}(x+h)\right]-f_{k}(x)\right|\leqslant 2^{-k}h/\delta_{k}
. Now, counting
2^{k}
intervals of
C^{k}
, each containing 4 points of discontinuity, each influencing a segment of length
2h
, we can estimate that

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|\frac{1}{2}\left[f_{k}(x-h)+f_{k}(x+h)\right]-f_{k}(x)\right|}{h}dx\leqslant\frac{1}{h}\times 2^{k}\times 4\times 2h\times\frac{2^{-k}h}{\delta_{k}}\leqslant\frac{8h}{\delta_{k}}\leqslant\frac{8h}{2^{n-k}\delta_{n}}\leqslant 2^{k-n+3}\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}.

In the last equalities, we used that
\delta_{n+1}\leqslant\delta_{n}/2
, hence
\delta_{k}\geqslant 2^{n-k}\delta_{n}
, and the choice
h\leqslant\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}\delta_{n}
. In consequence,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|\frac{1}{2}\left[f_{k}(x-h)+f_{k}(x+h)\right]-f_{k}(x)\right|}{h}dx\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{k-n+3}\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}\leqslant 16\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}.

There only stays to bound the contribution of
k=-1
, which is easily seen to be of order
4h
. Gathering the above estimates, we conclude that
W_{1}(\mu_{\alpha},\mu_{\alpha,h})/h\to 0
when
h\searrow 0
, hence that
Equation

15
holds.
∎
