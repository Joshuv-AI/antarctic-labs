Boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on generalized Fofana spaces
Pokou Nagacy, Bérenger Akon Kpata, Nouffou Diarra

Abstract
. We introduce generalized Fofana spaces and we give some of their basic properties. These spaces are a kind of generalization of generalized Morrey spaces. As application, we establish the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on these new spaces.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, the classical Lebesgue space on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\|\cdot\|_{p}
stands for its usual norm. For any measurable subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
|A|
and
\chi_{A}
its Lebesgue measure and its characteristic function, respectively. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and for any real number
r>0
,
B(x,\,r)
stands for the ball centered in
x
with radius
r
. We denote by
L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the complex vector space of equivalent classes (modulo equality Lebesgue almost everywhere) of Lebesgue measurable complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The Hardy-Littlewood maximal opertor
M
is defined, for all locally integrable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by

Mf(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}\left|f(y)\right|dy,\;\;\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Its boundedness property on Banach spaces, including Lebesgue spaces, Morrey and generalized Morrey spaces, and Fofana spaces, was widely investigated (see, for instance
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
17
]
). Although the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function is a classical tool in harmonic analysis, it has recently found applications in the studies of Sobolev functions and partial differential equations (see
[
1
]
and
[
11
]
).

Let us recall that the classical Morrey spaces
\mathcal{M}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\>(0\leq\lambda\leq d,1\leq q\leq\infty)
were introduced in
[
12
]
by C.B. Morrey. They are generalizations of Lebesgue spaces and are related to regularity results for solutions of certain problems in partial differential equations and integral operators (see
[
16
]
and the references therein). Morrey spaces also belong to the larger class of Fofana spaces
\left(L^{q},L^{p}\right)^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(1\leq q\leq\alpha\leq p\leq\infty)
defined as follows:

(L^{q},L^{p})^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f\in L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\quad\|f\|_{q,p,\alpha}<\infty\end{array}\right\},

where

\|f\|_{q,p,\alpha}=r^{d(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p},

with

(1.1)

{}_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p}=\left\|\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f\chi_{B(y,r)}|^{q}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{p}.

Here, the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})-
norm is taken with respect to the variable
y
and we adopt the convention
\frac{1}{\infty}=0
. The spaces
\left(L^{q},L^{p}\right)^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
were first introduced by Fofana with ”discrete” norms, which are denoted in this paper by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{(L^{q},l^{p})^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
(equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{q,p,\alpha}
), see
[
6
]
. We recall that for any locally integrable function
f
,

\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q},l^{p})^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=r^{d(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{q})}\>_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q,p},

where

{}_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q,p}=\left\|\left\{\left\|f\chi_{Q^{r}_{k}}\right\|_{q}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\right\|_{\ell^{p}},

with
k=(k_{1},\,...,\,k_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and

Q^{r}_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[rk_{i},r(k_{i}+1))
.

It is proved in
[
6
]
that the following assertions hold:

\bullet

\left(\left(L^{q},L^{p}\right)^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\|\cdot\|_{q,p,\alpha}\right)
is a Banach space;

\bullet
for
1\leq q\leq\alpha<\infty
fixed,
\{\left(L^{q},L^{p}\right)^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\;:\,\alpha\leq p\leq\infty\}
is a non-decreasing family (in the sense of inclusion) of distinct Banach spaces whose minimal and maximal elements are the Lebesgue space
L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left(L^{\alpha},L^{p}\right)^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left(L^{q},L^{\alpha}\right)^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the classical Morrey space
\left(L^{q},L^{\infty}\right)^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathcal{M}_{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, respectively, where
\lambda=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

The spaces
\left(L^{q},L^{p}\right)^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
have been successfully used in extending several important results known in the setting of Lebesgue and Morrey spaces (see, for example
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
).

In recent decades, many works have been devoted to the concept of generalized Morrey spaces and their applications (see, for example
[
15
]
and the references therein). The generalized Morrey spaces
\mathcal{M}_{q,\,\phi}
, where
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
\phi:(0,\,\infty)\to(0,\,\infty)
is a function satisfying certain conditions, were introduced by Nakai in
[
13
]
to investigate the boundedness properties of some classical operators of harmonic analysis.

In the present work, we introduce a kind of generalization of Nakai’s generalized Morrey spaces, called generalized Fofana spaces. We give some of their basic properties. As application, we establish the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator in these new spaces.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we define generalized Fofana spaces and we give some of their basic properties. In Section 3, we state and prove the boundedness property of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on generalized Fofana spaces.

A\raisebox{-4.30554pt}{$~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~$}B

, then we write
A\approx B
.

2.
Generalized Fofana spaces

Let
1\leq q,\,p\leq\infty
and
r>0
. The amalgam space
(L^{q},\,l^{p})_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as the set of all functions
f\in L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
\>{}_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q,p}<\infty
.

Amalgam spaces were introduced by N. Wiener
[
18
]
in 1926. But its systematic study goes back to the work
of Holland
[
9
]
. For more informations about amalgam spaces, we refer to
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
and the references cited therein. We recall some properties of these spaces in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq q,\,p\leq\infty
and
r>0
. Then, the following assertions
hold.

(1)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

C^{-1}\>r^{\frac{d}{p}}\>_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q,p}\leq\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p}\leq Cr^{\frac{d}{p}}\>_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q,p},\;\;\;\;\;f\in L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2)

((L^{q},\,l^{p})_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\>_{r}\widetilde{\left\|\cdot\right\|}_{q,p})
is a complex Banach space equals to

((L^{q},\,L^{p})_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\>_{r}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{q,p}).

(3)

If
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq\infty
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then for all
f\in L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

{}_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q_{1},p}\leq\>_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q_{2},p}r^{d({\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{q_{2}}})}.

(4)

If
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, then for all
f\in L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

{}_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q,p_{2}}\leq 2^{d}\>_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q,p_{1}}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
1\leq q,p\leq\infty
. We denote by
\mathcal{G}_{q,p}
the set of measurable functions
\phi:(0,\,\infty)\to(0,\,\infty)
that satisfy the following assertions:

•

t\mapsto t^{\frac{d}{p}}\phi(t)
is almost decreasing (there exists
C>0
such that
r\leq s\implies s^{\frac{d}{p}}\phi(s)\leq Cr^{\frac{d}{p}}\phi(r)
);

•

t\mapsto t^{\frac{d}{q}}\phi(t)
is almost increasing (there exists
C>0
such that
r\leq s\implies r^{\frac{d}{q}}\phi(r)\leq Cs^{\frac{d}{q}}\phi(s)
).

Remark 2.3
.

Note that:

(1)

if
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p}
then the following
doubling condition is fulfilled:

(2.1)

\exists\>C>0,\quad\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{r}{s}\leq 2\implies\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{\phi(r)}{\phi(s)}\leq C;

(2)

if
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
then
\mathcal{G}_{q_{2},p}\subset\mathcal{G}_{q_{1},p};

(3)

if
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
then
\mathcal{G}_{q,p_{1}}\subset\mathcal{G}_{q,p_{2}}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
and let
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p}
. The generalized Fofana space
(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as

(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f\in L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\quad\|f\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\end{array}\right\},

where

\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p}.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that:

(1)

the space
(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a complex vector subspace of
L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(2)

the map
L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ni f\mapsto\ \left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
defines a norm on
(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(3)

according to Proposition
2.1
(1),

\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\quad f\in(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where

\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{-\frac{d}{q}}\>_{r}\widetilde{\left\|f\right\|}_{q,p};

(4)

if
1\leq q\leq\alpha\leq p
and
\phi(r)=r^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}
, then
(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the Fofana space
(L^{q},L^{p})^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(5)

for
1\leq q<\infty
, the space
(L^{q},L^{\infty})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the generalized Morrey space
\mathcal{M}_{q,\phi}
defined in
[
13
]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
and
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p}
. Then

\left((L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)
, is a complex Banach space.

Proof.

Let
(f_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Cauchy sequence of elements of
(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. Then there exists an integer
N
such that for all integers
m
and
n
we have

m,n>N\implies\|f_{m}-f_{n}\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\epsilon.

This implies that for
r
fixed and for
m,n>N
,

\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\|f_{m}-f_{n}\|_{q,p}<\epsilon.

Hence,
(f_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence of
\left(L^{q},L^{p}\right)_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As
\left(L^{q},L^{p}\right)_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a complex
Banach space,
(f_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
f\in(L^{q},L^{p})_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In addition, for all
m,n>N
we
have

|\|f_{m}\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}-\|f_{n}\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\leq\|f_{m}-f_{n}\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\epsilon.

Therefore,
(\|f_{n}\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence of
\mathbb{R}
. Let us put
M\geq 0
its limit. It follows that for
m>N
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f_{m}\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\|f-f_{m}\|_{q,p}.

By letting
m
go to
\infty
, we get

\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\ _{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p}\leq M.

Hence,
\|f\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq M
and
f\in(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let
m
be an integer such that
m>N
. For all
r>0
and for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\|f_{m}-f\|_{q,p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f_{m}-f_{m+l}\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\|f_{m+l}-f\|_{q,p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon+\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\|f_{m+l}-f\|_{q,p}.

Since

\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\|f_{m+l}-f\|_{q,p}=0,

we have

\|f_{m}-f\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\epsilon.

This ends the proof.
∎

The family of spaces
(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is decreasing with respect to the
q
power
and increasing with respect to the
p
power. More precisely, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
f\in L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1)

If
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq p\leq\infty
and
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q_{2},p}
then

\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q_{1}},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q_{2}},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and consequently,
(L^{q_{2}},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset(L^{q_{1}},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2)

If
1\leq q\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
and
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p_{1}}
then

\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p_{2}})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p_{1}})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and consequently,
(L^{q},L^{p_{1}})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset(L^{q},L^{p_{2}})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(1) Suppose that
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq p\leq\infty
and
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q_{2},p}
. It follows from Proposition
2.1
that

\displaystyle r^{d(-\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q_{1},p}\lesssim r^{d(-\frac{1}{q_{2}}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q_{2},p},

for all
r>0
.

Hence,
\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q_{1}},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q_{2}},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2) Suppose that
1\leq q\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
and
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p_{1}}
. From Proposition
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle r^{-\frac{d}{p_{2}}}\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p_{2}}\lesssim r^{-\frac{d}{p_{1}}}\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p_{1}},

for all
r>0
. Multiplying both sides of this inequality by
\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{-\frac{d}{q}}
, we obtain

r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{2}})}\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p_{2}}\lesssim r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{1}})}\>_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{q,p_{1}}.

Hence,
\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p_{2}})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p_{1}})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

∎

The following proposition asserts that the characteristic functions of balls belong to generalized Fofana spaces.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
and let
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p}.
Then

\frac{1}{\phi(r_{0})}\approx\left\|\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

for every ball
B(0,r_{0}).

Proof.

Let
r_{0}>0.
It follows from Proposition
2.7
(2) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(r_{0})}r_{0}^{-\frac{d}{q}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}(x)\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{+\infty})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Thus,
\frac{1}{\phi(r_{0})}\lesssim\left\|\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We will prove the converse inequality in two steps. By definition of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}(x)\chi_{B(\cdot,r)}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{p}.

First step:
For any real number
r
such that
r_{0}\leq r
, we have

(2.2)

r_{0}^{\frac{d}{q}}\phi(r_{0})\leq C_{1}r^{\frac{d}{q}}\phi(r).

It follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{r\geq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}(x)\chi_{B(\cdot,r)}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{r\geq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\chi_{B(0,r+r_{0})}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{d(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}r^{\frac{d}{q}}_{0}\sup_{r\geq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}(r+r_{0})^{\frac{d}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{d(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}r^{\frac{d}{q}}_{0}\sup_{r\geq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}r^{\frac{d}{p}}(1+\frac{r_{0}}{r})^{\frac{d}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{d(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{2}{p})}r^{\frac{d}{q}}_{0}\sup_{r\geq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}r^{\frac{d}{p}}.

By inequality (
2.2
), we get

(2.3)

\sup_{r\geq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}(x)\chi_{B(\cdot,r)}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{p}\leq\frac{C^{{}^{\prime}}}{\phi(r_{0})},

where
C^{{}^{\prime}}=2^{d(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{2}{p})}C_{1}.

Second step:
For any real number
r
such that
r_{0}\geq r
, we have

(2.4)

r_{0}^{\frac{d}{p}}\phi(r_{0})\leq C_{2}r^{\frac{d}{p}}\phi(r).

\displaystyle\sup_{r\leq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}(x)\chi_{B(\cdot,r)}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{r\leq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(y,r)}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\chi_{B(0,r+r_{0})}\right\|_{p}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{d(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}\sup_{r\leq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}r^{\frac{d}{q}}(r+r_{0})^{\frac{d}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{d(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}r_{0}^{\frac{d}{p}}\sup_{r\leq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{p})}(1+\frac{r}{r_{0}})^{\frac{d}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{d(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{2}{p})}r^{\frac{d}{p}}_{0}\sup_{r\leq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{-\frac{d}{p}}.

By inequality (
2.4
), we get

(2.5)

\sup_{r\geq r_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(r)}r^{d(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}(x)\chi_{B(\cdot,r)}(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{p}\leq\frac{C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}{\phi(r_{0})}

where
C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=2^{d(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{2}{p})}C_{2}.

From (
2.3
) and (
2.5
), we deduce that

\left\|\chi_{B(0,r_{0})}\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{C}{\phi(r_{0})},

where
C=\max\{C^{{}^{\prime}},C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\}
.
∎

3.
Boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

It is proved in
[
5
]
that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator is bounded on classical Fofana spaces. It is also bounded on generalized Morrey spaces (see
[
13
]
). We shall prove the following result within the framework of generalized Fofana spaces.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<q\leq p\leq\infty
and
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p}
. Assume that there is a constant
C>0
such that for any
r>0
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\phi^{q}(t)t^{\frac{dq}{p}-1}dt\leq C\phi^{q}(r)r^{\frac{dq}{p}}.

Then

\left\|Mf\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\raisebox{-4.30554pt}{$~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~$}\left\|f\right\|_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad f\in(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Remark 3.2
.

Theorem
3.1
in the case
1<q\leq\alpha\leq p\leq\infty
and
\phi(r)=r^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}
was proved in
[
5
, Proposition 4.2 (1)]
. In the case
p=\infty
, it is nothing but Theorem 1 of
[
13
]
.

The following result will be useful in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
1<q\leq p\leq\infty
,
r>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p}
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any positive integer
i
, we have

(2^{i}r)^{\frac{dq}{p}}\phi^{q}(2^{i+1}r)\leq C\displaystyle\int_{2^{i}r}^{2^{i+1}r}\phi^{q}(t)t^{\frac{dq}{p}-1}dt.

Proof.

Suppose that
1<q\leq p\leq\infty
and
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p}
. Let
r>0
. We fix
i\geq 1
.

For
2^{i}r\leq t<2^{i+1}r
, we have
C^{{}^{\prime}}\phi^{q}(2^{i+1}r)\leq\phi^{q}(t)
according to (
2.1
). It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{2^{i}r}^{2^{i+1}r}\phi^{q}(t)t^{\frac{dq}{p}-1}dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{{}^{\prime}}\phi^{q}(2^{i+1}r)\displaystyle\int_{2^{i}r}^{2^{i+1}r}t^{\frac{dq}{p}}\frac{1}{t}dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{{}^{\prime}}ln(2)(2^{i}r)^{\frac{dq}{p}}\phi^{q}(2^{i+1}r).

Thus,
\quad(2^{i}r)^{\frac{dq}{p}}\phi^{q}(2^{i+1}r)\leq C\displaystyle\int_{2^{i}r}^{2^{i+1}r}\phi^{q}(t)t^{\frac{dq}{p}-1}dt

with
C=\frac{1}{C^{{}^{\prime}}ln(2)}
.
∎

We may now establish the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
1<q\leq p\leq\infty
,
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{q,p}
and
f\in(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Since the desired result is already established in the case
p=\infty
(see Remark
3.2
), we suppose that
p<\infty
.

Let
r>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Following
[
8
, Theorem 2.12]
, we may write

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(Mf)^{q}(x)\chi_{B(y,r)}(x)dx\raisebox{-4.30554pt}{$~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~$}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{q}(M\chi_{B(y,r)})(x)dx.

Then, proceeding as in the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
2
]
, we obtain

{}_{r}\left\|Mf\right\|^{q}_{q,p}\raisebox{-4.30554pt}{$~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~$}\>_{2r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}+\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{1}{(2^{i-1})^{d}}\>_{2^{i+1}r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}.

On the one hand, we have

{}_{2r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{q}(2r)(2r)^{dq(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}}{\phi^{q}(2r)(2r)^{dq(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}}\>_{2r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{q}(2r)}{(2r)^{dq(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\phi^{q}(r)r^{dq(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Hence,

(3.3)

_{2r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}\raisebox{-4.30554pt}{$~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~$}\phi^{q}(r)r^{dq(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

On the other hand, thanks to (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{1}{(2^{i-1})^{d}}\>_{2^{i+1}r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}

~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{1}{(2^{i-1})^{d}}\phi^{q}(2^{i+1}r)(2^{i+1}r)^{dq(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{1}{(2^{i-1})^{d}}\>_{2^{i+1}r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}

~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}r^{dq(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}2^{\frac{idq}{p}}\phi^{q}(2^{i+1}r)

~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}r^{dq(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}r^{-\frac{dq}{p}}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}(2^{i}r)^{\frac{dq}{p}}\phi^{q}(2^{i+1}r).

By applying Lemma
3.3
, the previous inequality becomes

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{1}{(2^{i-1})^{d}}\>_{2^{i+1}r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}

~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}r^{dq(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}r^{-\frac{dq}{p}}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\displaystyle\int_{2^{i}r}^{2^{i+1}r}\phi^{q}(t)t^{\frac{dq}{p}-1}dt.

Since

\sum\limits^{\infty}_{i=1}\displaystyle\int_{2^{i}r}^{2^{i+1}r}\phi^{q}(t)t^{\frac{dq}{p}-1}dt\leq\displaystyle\int_{2r}^{\infty}\phi^{q}(t)t^{\frac{dq}{p}-1}dt\leq\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\phi^{q}(t)t^{\frac{dq}{p}-1}dt,

we get

\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{1}{(2^{i-1})^{d}}\>_{2^{i+1}r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}\raisebox{-4.30554pt}{$~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~$}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}r^{dq(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}r^{-\frac{dq}{p}}\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\phi^{q}(t)t^{\frac{dq}{p}-1}dt.

It follows from (
3.1
) that,

(3.4)

\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{1}{(2^{i-1})^{d}}\>_{2^{i+1}r}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{q,p}\raisebox{-4.30554pt}{$~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~$}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}r^{dq(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}\phi^{q}(r).

From (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we deduce that

{}_{r}\left\|Mf\right\|^{q}_{q,p}\raisebox{-4.30554pt}{$~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~$}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}r^{dq(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p})}\phi^{q}(r).

It follows that,

(3.5)

\frac{1}{\phi^{q}(r)}r^{dq(-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\>_{r}\left\|Mf\right\|^{q}_{q,p}\raisebox{-4.30554pt}{$~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}~$}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{(L^{q},L^{p})^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We obtain the desired result by taking the supremum over all
r>0
in
the left hand side of (
3.5
).
∎
