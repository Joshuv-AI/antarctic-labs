4. Research Accomplishments
The goal of this project was to develop multimodal ligand non-innocence in late transition metal complexes of
aminophenolate ligands and to apply this ligand-centered reactivity to small-molecule reduction reactions relevant to
energy-conversion chemistry. Specific aims were,
1) Prepare new coordination complexes of aminophenolate ligands and characterize them using a battery of
 structural, spectroscopic, and computational methods.
2) Benchmark the thermodynamics and kinetics of e–, H+, H●, and H– transfer reactivity to establish the baseline
 reactivity of new metal complexes containing aminophenolate ligands.
3) Apply new aminophenolate complexes to catalytic reduction reactions.

2. Thermodynamic and kinetic benchmarking
To better understand how molecular structure, charge, and electron configuration impact the ability of an
aminophenolate metal complex to effect H-atom or hydride transfer, we set out to investigate the thermodynamic
and kinetic constraints associated with each compound class. Standard thermodynamic measurements including
pKa determinations and electrochemical characterization were combined with equilibrium H-atom transfer
measurements. Kinetics experiments were complicated by competing one-and two-electron pathways. Key findings
include the following.
 • Cationic platinum complexes containing one aminophenolate ligand and one bipyridine ligand were
 modest H-atom donors (N–H BDFE ~ 65 kcal mol–1) but poor hydride donors (∆GH– ~ 101 kcal mol–1).

Summary. Our investigations have revealed that charge and electronic matching are critical to the cooperative
reactivity needed to realize H-atom and hydride transfer reactivity from redox-active ligand complexes. While the
reduced aminophenolate platinum bipyridine anions are powerful hydride transfer reagents from a thermodynamic
perspective, the disparity in reduction potential for the different ligands strongly favors one-electron reactivity over
the two-electron reactivity necessary for hydride transfer. In the case of the aminophenolate platinum catecholate
complexes, the hydride donor ability is reduced, but by better matching the redox potentials of the ligands, hydride
transfer was facilitated (in the form of hydride abstraction).

3. Targeting catalytic reduction reactions.
Ultimately, the goal of this project is to apply ligand-based H-atom or hydride transfer reactivity to catalytic reactions.
The controlled delivery of protons and electrons is critical to small molecule reactions including selective oxidiation
and reduction reactions in the context of energy conversion and storage. Towards this end we have pursued two
separate catalytic reactions.
 • Leveraging the hydride abstraction reactivity of Pt(isq●)(sq●), we were able to achieve catalytic alcohol
 oxidation reactivity utilizing O2 as the terminal oxidant. Turnover numbers as high as 650 were obtained

 for the conversion of secondary alcohols to aldehydes without decomposition of the catalyst. Manuscript
 in preparation.

quantitatively understand the factors that regulate catalytic turnover in these systems.
