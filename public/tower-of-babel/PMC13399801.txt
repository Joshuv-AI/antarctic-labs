Arthroscopy‐Assisted Type 2 Posterior Horn Lateral Meniscus Transtibial Root Repair And Concurrent Inside‐Out Lateral Meniscus Horizontal Cleavage Tear Repair With an Anterolateral Ligament Reconstruction Utilizing Iliotibial Band Autograft
Authors: Taidhgin J. Keel, Benjiman J. Wilebski, Luke V. Tollefson, Ethan R. Sawyer, Taylor J. Freetly, Christopher M. LaPrade, Robert F. LaPrade
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) and anterolateral ligament (ALL) play key roles in maintaining rotational stability of the knee joint. Injuries that cause ACL tears have been shown to also frequently compromise the integrity of the ALL. Patients with laxity of the ALL are predisposed to increased rotation of the tibia and subsequent meniscal injury after an ACL reconstruction (ACLR). If such meniscal injury does occur, performing an ALL reconstruction (ALLR) at the time of meniscal repair will potentially limit the extremes of internal tibial rotation, protecting the meniscal repair to allow for healing. This technique describes an arthroscopy‐assisted type 2 posterior horn lateral meniscus transtibial root repair and concurrent inside‐out lateral meniscus horizontal cleavage tear repair with an open ALLR utilizing iliotibial band autograft following a previous ACLR.

Our technique for an arthroscopy‐assisted type 2 posterior horn lateral meniscus transtibial root repair and concurrent inside‐out lateral meniscus horizontal cleavage tear repair with an open anterolateral ligament reconstruction (ALLR) utilizing iliotibial band (ITB) autograft begins with the patient positioned in the supine position on the operating table and induced under general anesthesia. An incision is made laterally for the ALLR. The soft tissues are dissected distal to the joint line to expose the ITB. An 8 × 1 cm posterior strip of the ITB is harvested leaving the distal aspect attached to Gerdy's tubercle. The anterolateral ligament (ALL) anatomic tibial attachment is identified midway between Gerdy's tubercle and the anterior aspect of the fibular head. A 2.8 mm double‐loaded Q‐Fix anchor with #2 suture (Smith & Nephew, London, UK) is placed 1 cm distal to the joint line, and the ALL autograft is fixed at this site. Next, the femoral attachment site of the fibular collateral ligament (FCL) is identified. A second 2.8 mm double‐loaded Q‐Fix anchor with 1.4 mm MiniTape suture (Smith & Nephew) is placed 5 mm posterior and proximal to the lateral epicondyle to reconstitute the femoral attachment site of the ALL. The soft tissues are then dissected anterior to the lateral gastrocnemius tendon and above the biceps femoris tendon, and a retractor is placed for the inside‐out lateral meniscus repair. Medial and lateral parapatellar arthroscopic portals are created and the arthroscope is inserted into the joint. The cruciate ligaments and menisci are assessed for injury and the lateral meniscus tears are identified. Arthroscopically, a rasp is used to promote healing of the lateral meniscus horizontal cleavage tear prior to placing 4 inside‐out sutures into the lateral meniscus horizontal tear approximately 3 mm apart. Next, the lateral incision is extended to below Gerdy's tubercle, and the anterior compartment musculature is elevated distally for approximately 1 cm. Arthroscopically, a curette and curved shaver are used to decorticate the bone where the posterior horn lateral meniscus root would normally attach. A meniscal root guide (Smith & Nephew) is then used to drill two transtibial tunnels starting just distal to Gerdy's tubercle. The first cannula is drilled posterior to the lateral meniscus root attachment and the second is drilled 5 mm anterior using an offset guide. A straight FirstPass Mini Suture Passer (Smith & Nephew) is used to place a 2 mm Ultratape suture (Smith & Nephew) into the lateral meniscus 5 to 7 mm lateral to the posterior root tear. The Ultratape suture (Smith & Nephew) is shuttled into the desired cannula with a nylon suture passer. The second suture tape is then placed and shuttled down the second cannula. The lateral meniscus posterior root repair sutures are tied over an EndoButton (Smith & Nephew) on the anterolateral tibia. The previously placed sutures in the lateral meniscus horizontal cleavage tear are tied. A microfracture awl is used to place 4 awl holes on the lateral aspect of the intercondylar notch for a marrow venting procedure. With the knee flexed to 20°, the ALL autograft is fixed to the femur using the previously placed Q‐FIX anchor (Smith & Nephew). The deep and superficial tissues are closed with suture. The anterolateral ligament (ALL), in combination with the other anterolateral structures of the knee, play an important role in preventing increased internal rotation of the knee. 1 , 2 The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) provides the primary restraint against anterior translation of the tibia and internal rotation of the knee. 3 Previous cadaveric studies have shown a strong relationship between an ACL injury and resulting rotatory instability of the knee. 4 Isolated surgical treatment of an ACL tear with a concurrent ALL injury can result in residual postoperative rotational instability, which can be assessed using pivot shift testing. 5 , 6 , 7 This residual instability is theorized to place patients at an elevated risk for articular cartilage and meniscal injury following an ACL reconstruction (ACLR). 8 Reconstruction of the ACL with a concomitant ALL reconstruction (ALLR) is associated with reduced graft failure rates and has been reported to restore native knee rotational stability. 9 , 10 , 11 Patients who have undergone a prior ACLR with an intact graft but have persistent rotatory instability and sustain a meniscus tear may benefit from an ALLR at the time of meniscus repair to improve rotatory stability and to protect the meniscal repair. This technical note describes an arthroscopy‐assisted type 2 posterior horn lateral meniscus transtibial root repair and concurrent inside‐out lateral meniscus horizontal cleavage tear repair with an open anatomic ALLR utilizing the iliotibial band (ITB). 12

Patient Evaluation

Preoperatively, a thorough history and clinical examination should be performed. Preoperative imaging should include bilateral weightbearing Anteroposterior and Rosenberg radiographs, long leg alignment radiographs, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to establish an objective diagnosis to assess the integrity of the ACL and menisci (Figures 1 and 2).

The patient's preoperative MRI shows a displaced posterior horn lateral meniscus root tear (white arrow). (MRI, magnetic resonance imaging.)

Anesthesia and Positioning

The patient is positioned in the supine position on the operating table and induced under general anesthesia. A bilateral knee examination is performed to validate clinical examination findings. A well‐padded high thigh tourniquet is placed on the surgical leg. The surgical leg is placed in a leg holder (Mizho OSI, Union City, CA), and the nonsurgical leg into an abduction stirrup (Birkova Product LLC, Gothenburg, NE). For infection prophylaxis, two grams of perioperative Cefazolin is administered if no allergy is present.

ALL Autograft Preparation

First, a skin incision is made laterally centered over the ITB extending to Gerdy's tubercle. The soft tissues are dissected distal to the joint line to expose the inferior aspect of the ITB (Figure 3). An 8 × 1 cm posterior strip of the ITB is harvested leaving the distal aspect attached to Gerdy's tubercle (Figure 4). A spinal needle is then placed at the lateral joint line and the ALL anatomic tibial attachment is identified midway between Gerdy's tubercle and the anterior aspect of the fibular head and 1 cm distal to the joint line. 7 A 2.8 mm double‐loaded Q‐Fix anchor with #2 suture (Smith & Nephew, London, UK) is placed 1 cm distal to the joint line and the ALL autograft is fixed at the tibial anatomic attachment site (Figure 5). A small hemostat is used to identify the fibular collateral ligament (FCL). The anatomic femoral attachment site for the ALLR is identified 5 mm posterior and proximal to the FCL femoral attachment and a 2.8 mm double‐loaded Q‐Fix anchor with 1.4 mm MiniTape suture (Smith & Nephew) is placed (Figure 6). 7 The soft tissues are dissected anterior to the lateral gastrocnemius tendon above the biceps femoris tendon. A retractor is placed in this interval to protect the neurovasculature.

Arthroscopy

Medial and lateral parapatellar portals are created and the arthroscope is inserted into the joint. The articular cartilages in all compartments of the knee are inspected. If evidence of chondromalacia is encountered, a chondroplasty may be performed at this time. The cruciate ligaments and menisci are assessed for injury and the lateral meniscus horizontal cleavage and root tears are identified.

Arthroscopic Lateral Meniscus Horizontal Cleavage Repair

Arthroscopically, a rasp is used to stimulate vascular induction and promote healing of the lateral meniscus horizontal cleavage tear. Four inside‐out sutures are placed into the lateral meniscus horizontal cleavage tear approximately 3 mm apart, passing through the superior and inferior leaflets and compressing them together (Figure 7). These sutures are not tied at this time to preserve meniscus mobility for the root repair.

Two of the four total sutures (purple arrows) are shown. The sutures should be placed about 3 mm apart from each other. These will be tied externally at the end of the procedure to create a solid repair of the horizontal cleavage tear.

Lateral Meniscus Root Tear Preparation

The lateral incision is extended further distally below Gerdy's tubercle and the anterior compartment musculature is elevated distally for approximately 1 cm in preparation for transtibial tunnel drilling.

Lateral Meniscus Root Repair

Arthroscopically, a curette and curved shaver are used to decorticate the bone at the posterior horn lateral meniscus root anatomic attachment site. A grasper is then used to verify that the lateral meniscus posterior root can be positioned into an anatomic position over the decorticated attachment site. A meniscal root guide (Smith & Nephew) is then used to drill two transtibial tunnels starting just distal to Gerdy's tubercle directed toward the decorticated lateral meniscus posterior root attachment. The first cannula is drilled posterior to the lateral meniscus root attachment and the second is drilled 5 mm anterior using an offset guide (Figure 8). A straight FirstPass Mini Suture Passer (Smith & Nephew) is then used to place a vertical mattress 2 mm Ultratape suture (Smith & Nephew) into the lateral meniscus 5 to 7 mm lateral to the posterior root tear. The Ultratape suture (Smith & Nephew) is then shuttled into the desired cannula with a nylon suture passer (Figure 9). Once the first suture tape has been placed, the second vertical mattress 2 mm suture tape is placed and shuttled down the second cannula. The presence of an intact ACL graft may prolong this process due to limited visualization. The posterior horn lateral meniscus root repair sutures are then tied over an EndoButton (Smith & Nephew) on the anterolateral tibia.

Lateral Meniscus Fixation and Marrow Venting Procedure

The lateral meniscus horizontal cleavage repair is directly visualized, and the previously placed sutures are tied to secure the lateral meniscus. A microfracture awl is used to place 4 awl holes on the lateral aspect of the intercondylar notch for a marrow venting procedure to release marrow products to assist with biologic healing. 13

ALL Fixation and Closure

With the knee flexed to 20°, the ITB autograft is passed superficial to the FCL and fixed to the femur using the previously placed Q‐Fix anchor (Smith & Nephew) (Figure 10). Fixation of the ITB autograft decreases internal rotation of the tibia. The tourniquet is let down and hemostasis is obtained. The deep and superficial tissues are closed with suture and Steri‐Strips (3M Company, St. Paul, MN) are loosely applied along with a sterile dressing. A knee immobilizer is placed in full extension.

With the right knee flexed to 20° in the supine position, the ITB autograft (white arrow) is passed superficial to the FCL and fixed to the anatomic femoral attachment site of the ALL during a concomitant posterior horn lateral meniscus root and horizontal cleavage tear repairs. The Q‐Fix anchors (Smith & Nephew, London, UK) that were previously secured in the femur are utilized for this step and fixation of the ALL autograft eliminates the extreme internal rotation of the tibia. (ALL, anterolateral ligament; FCL, fibular collateral ligament; ITB, iliotibial band.)

Postoperative Rehabilitation

Baseline AP and lateral radiographs of the operative knee are obtained on postoperative day 1 to document the EndoButton (Smith & Nephew) position. Rehabilitation should begin on day 1 postoperatively with an emphasis on knee motion, quadriceps activation, and edema control. The patient will be nonweightbearing on the operative leg for 6 weeks. Knee flexion is limited to 90° for 2 weeks then increased as tolerated. The patient should avoid squatting, heavy lifting, and sitting cross‐legged for 4 months to protect the lateral meniscus repair.

DISCUSSION

The ACL and ALL play important roles in maintaining rotational stability of the knee. 10 Previous studies have reported that an ALL injury is often present in ACL‐injured knees. 14 These ALL injuries elevate the risk of further meniscal injury upon return to weight bearing, and especially with return to sport. An ALLR in the setting of residual rotational instability following a previous ACLR has been shown to restore rotational stability and reduce failure rates in a meta‐analysis of 10 clinical studies. 15 Two separate clinical studies concluded that a combined ACLR with an ALLR had superior patient‐reported outcomes along with improved pivot‐shift testing. 9 , 16 The main clinical benefit of this ALLR technique first described by Tollefson et al. is the improved postoperative limits of internal rotation, which serves to protect the meniscus repair. 17 However, this technique is not without its own risks.
