Causal Dimensionality of Transformer Representations: Measurement, Scaling, and Layer Structure
Nilesh Sarkar, Dawar Jyoti Deka

Abstract
Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) decompose transformer residual streams into interpretable feature dictionaries, yet the relationship between SAE width and causal influence on model output has not been systematically characterised. We introduce causal dimensionality κ ⁡ ( L , M , 𝒯 ) \kappa(L,M,\mathcal{T}) , defined as the effective rank of the expected Jacobian outer product at layer L L , and show it can be estimated via the SAE width sweep paired with attribution patching. Across seven SAE widths from 16,384 16{,}384 to 1,048,576 1{,}048{,}576 features on Gemma-2-2B layer 12, representational capacity grows 15.6 × 15.6{\times} while causal capacity grows only 4.35 × 4.35{\times} : a robust separation we term the representational–causal wedge . A saturating fit yields κ ^ ≈ 1,990 \hat{\kappa}\approx 1{,}990 with κ ^ / d model = 0.86 \hat{\kappa}/d_{\mathrm{model}}=0.86 and participation-ratio lower bound κ PR ≈ 280 \kappa_{\mathrm{PR}}\approx 280 . Crucially, κ \kappa is invariant to model scaling: Gemma-2-9B and Gemma-2-2B yield identical N causal = 328 N_{\mathrm{causal}}=328 at the same SAE width despite a 3.46 × 3.46{\times} parameter increase (the count is forced to 2 % 2\% of SAE width by calibration; the substantive empirical claim is shape invariance of the AtP score distribution under matched seq=512 conditions). Across eight network depths κ \kappa is constant while the absolute attribution threshold drops 20 × 20{\times} from layer 1 to layer 23. Five controls (architecture invariance, threshold robustness, geometric privilege, synthetic ground-truth recovery, and a four-cell encoder/decoder ablation) pin down what κ \kappa measures and what it does not. Our findings establish κ \kappa as a measurable, model-intrinsic property of transformer layers: sub-linearly recoverable by SAE width, invariant to model scaling, and structured across network depth.

Introduction

Sparse autoencoders trained on transformer residual streams recover
ever larger dictionaries of monosemantic features as their width grows

(
Gao et al. 2024
;
Templeton et al. 2024
;
Bricken et al. 2023
)
.
An assumption implicit in current practice is that representational
capacity (features that fire on any input) tracks causal capacity
(features whose perturbation changes model behaviour). If true, a wider
SAE would proportionally expand the causally relevant feature set
available for interpretability work.

Our central empirical finding is that this assumption fails drastically.
On Gemma-2-2B layer 12, a
64{\times}
width increase (
16{,}384\to 1{,}048{,}576
) expands the firing-feature count by
15.6{\times}
,
but expands the causally active count by only
4.35{\times}
. The
curves diverge: we call this the
representational–causal wedge
.

The right object to study is not a feature count at fixed width, but
a model-intrinsic quantity we call the
causal dimensionality

\kappa
of a layer: the effective rank of the expected Jacobian outer
product, defined in Section
3
. The SAE width sweep is a
consistent estimator of
\kappa
under three stated assumptions. The
wedge is then a natural prediction:
N_{\mathrm{causal}}
saturates to
\kappa

while
N_{\mathrm{repr}}
grows linearly, absorbing representational variance after
the causal subspace is full.

Two further predictions follow. First, if
\kappa
is layer-intrinsic,
scaling the model should not change it at fixed relative depth. We
confirm this on Gemma-2-9B vs Gemma-2-2B: identical
N_{\mathrm{causal}}=328

despite
3.46{\times}
more parameters. Second, attribution scores
should attenuate with depth as loss gradients flatten. We confirm

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}
drops
20{\times}
from layer 1 to
layer 23.

and prove

,
which lower-bounds

•

•

Background and Related Work

Sparse autoencoders.

SAEs trained on residual stream activations are the standard tool for
decomposing dense representations
(
Bricken et al. 2023
;
Templeton et al. 2024
;
Gao et al. 2024
)
. Wider SAEs recover finer-grained
features and exhibit feature splitting
(
Chanin et al. 2025
)
.
Quality benchmarks have begun to emerge
(
Karvonen et al. 2025
)
.

Attribution patching and circuits.

Activation patching
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
measures
counterfactual effects; attribution patching (AtP)
(
Nanda 2022a
)

is its first-order linearisation, with calibration established by

Kramár et al. 2024
.
Conmy et al. 2023
and

Marks et al. 2025
identify small task-specific feature sets.

Representational vs causal dimensionality.

Prior work measures the intrinsic dimension of transformer
representations
(
Ansuini et al. 2019
;
Cheng et al. 2025
;
Feng et al. 2022
)
,
estimating dimensions for
reconstruction
. Our target is the

causal
Jacobian: directions that actually change output.
The closest prior framing is the causal inner product of

Park et al. 2024
; we connect
\kappa
to their construction in
Remark
3.5
.
Michaud et al. 2023
predict knowledge
decomposes into quanta set by data diversity; we show causal directions
are similarly bounded by task diversity, not parameter count.

3
Causal Dimensionality: Definition, Bounds, and Estimation

A transformer layer operates on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Perturbations to most
directions leave output unchanged; perturbations to a small subset
cause measurable shifts. The size of that causally active subset is
what we call the causal dimensionality
\kappa
.

3.1
The Causal Jacobian and Its Effective Rank

Let
M
be a transformer with
L_{\max}
layers. For input
x
, let

\mathbf{h}_{L}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the residual stream at layer
L

and
\mathbf{y}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}
the output logits.

Definition 3.1

(Layer Causal Jacobian)
.

\mathbf{J}_{L}(x)=\partial\,\mathbf{y}(x)/\partial\,\mathbf{h}_{L}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|\times d}
.
A direction
\mathbf{v}
is
causally inert
if

\mathbf{J}_{L}(x)\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}
.

Definition 3.2

(Causal Dimensionality)
.

\kappa(L,M,\mathcal{T})=\mathrm{eff\text{-}rank}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L}),\quad\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L}=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{T}}\bigl[\mathbf{J}_{L}(x)^{\top}\mathbf{J}_{L}(x)\bigr],

(1)

where
\mathrm{eff\text{-}rank}(\mathbf{A})=\exp\!\bigl(-\sum_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\ln\bar{\lambda}_{i}\bigr)

is the exponential Shannon entropy of the normalised eigenspectrum

(
Roy and Vetterli 2007
)
.

3.2
Bounds and Theoretical Properties

Proposition 3.3

(Dimension Upper Bound)
.

\kappa(L,M,\mathcal{T})\leq\min(d,|\mathcal{V}|)
.

Proof.

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is symmetric PSD, so
it has at most
d
positive eigenvalues, giving

\mathrm{rank}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L})\leq d
. For any single
x
, the
rank of
\mathbf{J}_{L}(x)^{\top}\mathbf{J}_{L}(x)
is at most

\min(d,|\mathcal{V}|)
. The expectation
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L}=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{T}}[\,\cdot\,]
inherits the ambient-space
bound
d
but
not
the per-input bound
|\mathcal{V}|
, since the
rank of a sum can exceed that of summands. Combining the two,

\kappa=\mathrm{eff\text{-}rank}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L})\leq\min(d,|\mathcal{V}|)
. For
Gemma-2-2B (
d{=}2304
,
|\mathcal{V}|{=}256{,}000
) the binding bound
is
d
.
∎

For Gemma-2-2B,
d=2304
and
|\mathcal{V}|=256{,}000
, so
\kappa\leq 2304
. Our estimate
\hat{\kappa}\approx 1{,}990
sits just below
this ceiling: the model uses nearly all its representational dimensions
causally on generic text. In sparser, task-specific settings
\kappa\ll d
is expected, due to superposition
(
Elhage et al. 2022
)
.

Remark 3.4

(Circuit Decomposition Bound)
.

If layer
L
implements
k
approximately-independent circuits with
mean per-circuit Jacobian rank
\bar{r}
, then by sub-additivity of
rank under PSD addition,
\kappa(L)\leq k\cdot\bar{r}
. This bound
underlies our reading of
\hat{\kappa}
as the union upper-bound on
concurrent task circuits at
L
.

Remark 3.5

(Connection to the Linear Representation Hypothesis)
.

Park et al. 2024
define the causal inner product

\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle_{c}=\mathbf{u}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L}\mathbf{v}
and prove separable
concepts correspond to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{c}
-orthogonal
directions. Under this framing
\kappa
is the effective dimension of

\mathbb{R}^{d}
under the causal inner product. The wedge we observe
is precisely the gap between representational and computational needs:
a population-level instance of superposition
(
Elhage et al. 2022
)
.

3.3
Attribution Patching as a First-Order Proxy

Computing
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L}
exactly requires
d
backward passes
per input (2,300 for Gemma-2-2B): intractable at scale. We use
attribution patching (AtP;
Nanda 2022a
) as a
first-order surrogate:

\text{AtP}(L)=(\mathbf{h}_{L}^{c}-\mathbf{h}_{L}^{r})\odot\nabla_{\mathbf{h}_{L}^{r}}\mathcal{L},

(2)

at a cost of two forward passes plus one backward.

Kramár et al. 2024
report
\rho\in[0.85,0.99]
with exact
patching; our implementation achieves
\rho=0.838
globally and

\rho>0.95
within the top-
5\%
(Appendix
A
).
The per-feature score is

\text{AtP}_{i}=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{T}}\!\bigl[|\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial f_{i}\cdot f_{i}(x)|\bigr]
,
where
\mathcal{L}
is the KL divergence between clean and patched
output distributions.

3.4
The SAE Width Sweep as a
\kappa
Estimator

Definition 3.6

(Causal Feature Count)
.

N_{\mathrm{causal}}(m)=|\{i\in[m]:\text{AtP}_{i}\geq\varepsilon\}|
, where

\varepsilon
is calibrated
once
at the reference width

m_{\mathrm{ref}}=16{,}384
as the
98^{\mathrm{th}}
percentile of

\text{AtP}_{i}
at
(L,m_{\mathrm{ref}})

(
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}=3.563\!\times\!10^{-6}
on Gemma-2-2B
layer
12
), then held
fixed
as an absolute scalar threshold
across all other widths in the sweep.
By construction
N_{\mathrm{causal}}(m_{\mathrm{ref}})=0.02\,m_{\mathrm{ref}}\approx 328
; the empirical content of the sweep is the trajectory

N_{\mathrm{causal}}(m)
at
m\neq m_{\mathrm{ref}}
.

Proposition 3.7

(Consistency)
.

Under
(i) Spanning
: decoder columns eventually span

\mathrm{row\text{-}span}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L})
;

(ii) Faithfulness
: AtP faithfully ranks causal influence;

(iii) No dead features
: all features fire with positive
probability; then

N_{\mathrm{causal}}(m)\xrightarrow{m\to\infty}r_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L}):=\bigl|\{i:\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L})\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}\}\bigr|

for some spectral threshold
\varepsilon^{\prime}
induced by
\varepsilon
.
Furthermore,
r_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\to\mathrm{eff\text{-}rank}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L})=\kappa
as the eigenspectrum approaches
uniformity on its support; for heavy-tailed spectra (the empirical
regime),
r_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\leq\kappa
with a tail-controlled gap.

Proof sketch.

Under (i), every causal direction eventually has a decoder column.
Under (ii,iii), that column clears
\varepsilon
; directions in

\ker(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L})
do not. As
m\to\infty
,
N_{\mathrm{causal}}(m)

converges to
N_{\mathrm{eff}}
, the number of eigenvalues of

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L}
above the spectral threshold induced by

\varepsilon
.
N_{\mathrm{eff}}
lower-bounds the continuous
effective-rank measure
\kappa
and equals it when the eigenspectrum
is approximately uniform; for heavy-tailed spectra (as observed
empirically via
\kappa_{\mathrm{PR}}
),

N_{\mathrm{eff}}\leq\kappa\leq N_{\mathrm{eff}}+\Delta
for a slack

\Delta
controlled by the tail mass.
∎

Assumption (i) is supported by the power-law reconstruction error of

Gao et al. 2024
. We validate (ii) directly via
\rho=0.838

globally and
\rho>0.95
in the high-AtP regime that governs

N_{\mathrm{causal}}
.

3.5
Saturating Curve Fit

We fit
N_{\mathrm{causal}}(m)=\kappa(1-e^{-m/\tau})
where
\tau
is the
characteristic width for
63\%
recovery. As a threshold-free
robustness check we report the participation ratio

\kappa_{\mathrm{PR}}(m)=(\sum_{i}\text{AtP}_{i})^{2}/\sum_{i}\text{AtP}_{i}^{2}
.
We use
\kappa_{\mathrm{PR}}
as a heuristic spectral-concentration
measure on AtP scores: by Cauchy–Schwarz it lower-bounds the effective
rank of the AtP score distribution treated as a probability mass, which
is itself a proxy for
\mathrm{eff\text{-}rank}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{L})
.
We do
not
claim
\kappa_{\mathrm{PR}}\!\leq\!\kappa
as a
theorem; it is reported because it is invariant under monotone
re-thresholdings of
\varepsilon
. Fitting four post-minimum widths
yields

\hat{\kappa}=1{,}990\;\bigl(\text{Wald CI}_{95}{=}[0,4225],\;\text{bootstrap CI}_{95}{=}[545,5130]\bigr),\quad\hat{\tau}=881{,}000.

(3)

The CI is wide (dof
=2
; GemmaScope caps at 1M for this layer).
We treat
\hat{\kappa}\approx 1{,}990
as an order-of-magnitude figure.
The ratio
\hat{\kappa}/d=0.86
is the more striking result: the model
uses most of its representational dimensions causally on generic text.

U-shape (feature splitting).

N_{\mathrm{causal}}
dips at
m=65{,}536
before recovering, violating monotone
convergence. At intermediate widths a causal direction may split across
two sub-features, each below
\varepsilon
; by
m=131\text{k}

combinatorial features span the split direction and
N_{\mathrm{causal}}
recovers.

Chanin et al. 2025
document this as a systematic SAE
pathology. We treat the U-shape as a finite-width artefact, but flag
that our direct test (Exp E) is a

: it
uses AtP-score proximity rather than decoder-cosine similarity because
the saved
W_{\mathrm{dec}}
tensors required for the latter were not
retained at run time
(
exp_e_ushape/expE_results.json:verdict
). A definitive
test awaits a re-run with persisted
W_{\mathrm{dec}}

Scale invariance.

\kappa(L{=}20,\text{2B})=\kappa(L{=}20,\text{9B})
exactly (ratio

1.00{\times}
; Section
5.2
). The theoretical account:

Proposition 3.8

(Causal Subspace Saturation)
.

If the causal subspace is determined by the number of distinct
computational functions implemented on
\mathcal{T}
, and this
saturates once the model has sufficient capacity, then

\kappa(L,M,\mathcal{T})
is invariant to further scaling.

The number of independent computations is bounded by task diversity,
not parameter count. A 9B model performs the same computations on the
same text as a 2B model: more accurately, but not in more causal
dimensions.
Michaud et al. 2023
make a related observation
for knowledge quanta.

Remark 3.9

(Generic-text
\kappa
vs. task circuits: layered structure)
.

Marks et al. 2025
report
O(50{-}100)
causally active SVA
features per task. Our
N_{\mathrm{causal}}\!\approx\!328
on generic Pile-10k

Implications for SAE training.

Empirical capture (raw

) at the smallest

Conclusion.

We have introduced causal dimensionality
\kappa
as a measurable,
model-intrinsic property of transformer layers, proved the SAE width
sweep is a consistent estimator, and established that
\kappa
is
bounded by
d_{\mathrm{model}}
, sub-linearly recovered by SAE width,
invariant to model scaling, and structured across depth. The
encoder/decoder ablation reveals the trained encoder as a sparsity
filter: AtP measures activation pattern times decoder routing, not
recovery of causal concepts. These results provide a principled
theoretical anchor for the rapidly growing SAE interpretability
literature and carry direct practical implications for the design of
future SAE studies.

\ell_{0}(m)

[328,240,171,532,488,763,1427]

throughout. We therefore
