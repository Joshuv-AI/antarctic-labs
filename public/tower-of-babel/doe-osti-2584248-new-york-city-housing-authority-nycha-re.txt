Summary of Research Results:

NREL provided support to NYPA’s assessment of the potential installation of a solar
photovoltaic (PV) and battery energy storage system (BESS) at two multi-family residential
buildings in Brooklyn, New York. These buildings are owned by NYCHA and are referenced as
“Borinquen Plaza I” (Moore St.) and “Borinquen Plaza II” (110 Humboldt Street). The
PV+BESS systems were assessed to address power quality issues due to voltage fluctuations.
NREL also supported NYPA’s exploration of additional energy resiliency benefits that these
systems could provide, both by themselves and together with existing combined heat and power
backup generation.

The final analysis was delivered to NYCHA via a slide deck and NYCHA published a report
based on NREL’s work, giving credit to NREL and NYPA, on March 25, 2022: “New York City
Housing Authority: Borinquen Plaza I & II Reliability Project Final Report.”
https://climate.cityofnewyork.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Rikers-Report.pdf

Purpose

To support NYPA’s execution of a renewable energy and storage feasibility study on behalf of
two New York City Housing Authority multi-family residential buildings in Brooklyn, New
York.

Scope of Work

NREL will provide the following support to NYPA’s assessment of the potential installation of a
solar photovoltaic (PV) and battery energy storage system (PV+BESS) at two multi-family
residential buildings in Brooklyn, New York, owned by the New York City Housing Authority
(NYCHA): Borinquen Plaza I (Moore St), and Borinquen Plaza II (110 Humboldt Street). The
PV+BESS is being assessed to address power quality issues (voltage fluctuations). NREL will
also support NYPA’s exploration of additional energy resiliency these systems could provide,
either by themselves, or together with combined heat and power (CHP) backup generation.

Using several tools, NREL experts conducted an analysis of 15 PV+BESS scenarios for
Buildings 3 and 4 of Borinquen Plaza I as well as its Community Center. For each of the
scenarios evaluated, NREL experts evaluated two ‘cases’: a financial case (systems optimized
for financial benefit) and a resilience case (systems optimized for resilience benefits). Each of
these scenarios modeled PV system size, outage duration, financing, and other factors. To align
with NYCHA’s goals, resilience cases were given priority in analysis.

To estimate PV potential, NREL experts used Project Sunroof,1 the NY SolarMap,2 and
Helioscope3 to provide a comprehensive view. Using this combination of models, experts were
able to compare quick, streamlined estimates with site-specific details (such as obstructions,
shading, etc.) to get an actionable assessment of each location. Additionally, experts conducted
BESS feasibility analysis using NREL’s Renewable Energy Integration and Optimization
(REopt) 4 modelling platform to estimate cost-optimal BESS installations and lifestyle costs of
lithium-ion BESS installations.5 The resulting PV+BESS assessments were combined for

lost load was taken into consideration to determine cost-effectiveness of the systems. Analyses in

NREL experts also conducted analysis of several other factors including purchase methods and
other energy saving measures. Analyses of incentives and financing measures considered state
and federal options that affect both direct purchase and third-party ownership of systems. For
other energy saving measures, experts considered ground source heat pumps, combined heat and
power, and other measures such as retrofit windows, lighting, sensors, and efficient appliance
upgrades.

All of these findings were compiled into a set of slides and provided to NYPA and NYCHA.

Task 2: NREL will assist NYPA in its full report development

 a. Contributing a write-up of the NREL analysis process
Of the 30 cases NREL assessed (15 financial, and 15 resilience), ultimately only 2 financial
cases were cost-effective without incorporating the value of lost load. However, these two cases
consisted of small PV systems that were deemed unable to tangibly contribute to NYCHA’s
resilience goals. Of the resilience cases evaluated, the value of lost load was required to play a
significant role in determining cost-effectiveness. Based on NREL’s analysis, NYPA
recommended that NYCHA pursue the purchase of a PV+BESS system that would power the
Streamlined Critical Load over a 24-hour outage for the evaluated buildings. NYPA determined
that this system would support NYCHA’s resilience goals while also minimizing the value of
lost load costs necessary to be cost-effective. NREL analysis also determined that direct purchase
would be the most effective given the incentives available to NYCHA. However, NYPA noted
that if capital was not available for direct purchase, third party ownership would also be a viable
option.

Following the completion of this analysis, NYCHA incorporated all of NREL’s work into their
published report, with assistance from NYPA and NREL. This report continues to serve as a
resource for decision making as NYCHA considers measures to take in the pursuit of its
resilience goals.

The report can be found here:
