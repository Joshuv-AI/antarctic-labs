A note on the spectral distribution of non-Hermitian block matrices with Toeplitz blocks
Andrea Adriani (1) and Giacomo Tento (2)

Abstract




Abstract

In the present paper, we are concerned with the study of the spectral distribution of matrix-sequences showing a non-Hermitian block structure with Toeplitz blocks. We use the notion of geometric mean of matrices and the theory of Generalized Locally Toeplitz (GLT) sequences to perform our analysis and produce some numerical tests and visualizations to confirm our theoretical derivations.

Introduction

A matrix-sequence
\left\{A_{n}\right\}_{n}
is an ordered collection of matrices
A_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{n}\times d_{n}}
, with
d_{n}
strictly increasing and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Such sequences naturally arise from the numerical discretizaton of integral, ordinary and partial differential equations; most often they possess a Generalized Locally Toeplitz (GLT) symbol (see, for instance,
[
11
]
), that is, a measurable function
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{s\times s}
that, under additional circumstances, is able to provide a description of the asymptotic distribution of the eigenvalues of the sequence. Namely, we say that
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
is distributed in the eigenvalue sense as
f
over
\Omega
and we write
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}(f,\Omega)
, if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{n}}F\left(\lambda_{i}\left(A_{n}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}\left(\Omega\right)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{s}F\left(\lambda_{j}(f(x))\right)}{s}\mathrm{d}x

(1)

for every continuous function
F:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with compact support.

For our purposes, we are interested in uni-variate matrix-valued symbols, but the notion of spectral distribution can been extended also to multilevel matrix-sequences and multilevel block matrix-sequences with multi-variate matrix-valued symbols (see
[
13
,
14
]
).

The theory of GLT sequences stems from previous inquiries on Locally Toeplitz (LT) sequences (
[
22
]
), and underwent a systematic study in
[
12
]
.

Notable applications of this theoretical apparatus comprise finite differences methods (see, e.g.,
[
21
]
), finite elements methods (
[
12
, Section 10.6]
), finite volumes (
[
10
]
) and fractional derivatives (
[
16
]
).

Moreover, information provided by means of GLT sequences are not only of interest from a theoretical standpoint, but often carries significant practical purposes.
For instance, it is known that the convergence rate of Krylov methods strongly depends on the spectral features of the matrices to which they are applied; thus, the spectral distribution notion can be employed to predict and improve their performances via preconditioning techniques (see
[
12
, Section 10]
).

In this paper, we consider sequences of block matrices
k\times k
,
k\geq 2
, with the following structure:

\mathcal{A}_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}T_{n}(f_{1,1})&T_{n}(f_{1,2})&&\\
T_{n}(f_{2,1})&T_{n}(f_{2,2})&\ddots&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&T_{n}(f_{k-1,k})\\
&&T_{n}(f_{k,k-1})&T_{n}(f_{k,k})\end{bmatrix},

(2)

where each
f_{i,j}
is a function in
L^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi])
and
T_{n}(f)
denotes the Toeplitz matrix of dimension
n
generated by
f
.

In the case where the sequence is Hermitian, it is possible to use the theory developed in
[
1
,
2
]
(see also
[
6
]
) to perform a complete spectral analysis of the sequence, both from the singular value and the eigenvalue perspective. Moreover, even when the sequence is not Hermitian, and the ratio of the dimensions of the various blocks with the global dimension tends (as
n\to\infty
) to any rational or irrational number in
(0,1)
, the tools in
[
1
,
2
]
allow us to find the singular value distribution of the sequence. However, in the case where the sequence does not present a Hermitian structure, the theory does not explicitly give the eigenvalue distribution, and a new approach is needed to tackle the problem. In this paper, we use the notion of geometric means of matrices to solve this issue for a class of matrices as in (
2
). Note that sequences of matrices presenting a similar non-Hermitian structure are already known in applications (see, for example,
[
8
]
, where the authors study electro-diffusion equations).

One of the first formulations of geometric means of matrices can be found in
[
17
]
, where many properties are discussed. We make use of their definition, that is, for two Hermitian positive definite (HPD) matrices
A
and
B
, we let

G(A,B):=A^{1/2}\left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}A^{1/2}=G(B,A)

(3)

be their geometric mean. For a recent account on how the Generalized Locally Toeplitz (GLT) theory is related to this topic, we refer the reader to
[
15
]
.

The main contribution of the present work is the proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{n}
be as in Equation (
2
). Assume that
f_{i,j}\in L^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi])
and real-valued almost everywhere for every
i,j
. Moreover, assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f_{i,i+1},\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f_{i+1,i}>0
for every
i=1,\dots,k-1
. Then

\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}F(\theta),

with

F(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1,1}&f_{1,2}&&\\
f_{2,1}&f_{2,2}&\ddots&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&f_{k-1,k}\\
&&f_{k,k-1}&f_{k,k}\end{bmatrix}.

Equivalently,

\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}\tilde{F}(\theta),

with

\tilde{F}(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1,1}&f_{1,2}^{1/2}f_{2,1}^{1/2}&&\\
f_{1,2}^{1/2}f_{2,1}^{1/2}&f_{2,2}&\ddots&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&f_{k-1,k}^{1/2}f_{k,k-1}^{1/2}\\
&&f_{k,k-1}^{1/2}f_{k-1,k}^{1/2}&f_{k,k}\end{bmatrix}.

In order to prove our main theorem, we will make use of a fine approximation of each Toeplitz block with a circulant matrix depending on the generating function (see
[
23
]
and references therein) and of
[
7
, Theorem 1]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the concepts of spectral distribution, Toeplitz sequences, GLT sequences (and related properties). There, we also recall the definition of geometric mean of two matrices that we use throughout the paper and discuss the correlation between GLT theory and geometric means. Section
3
is the core of the present note and is devoted to the proof of our main result. In Section
4
we perform some numerical tests that corroborate the theory developed and, finally, Section
5
is devoted to draw conclusions, provide a discussion on possible improvements, and state some open problems for future research.

2
Preliminary tools: Spectral distribution, Toeplitz matrices, GLT sequences

We start this section with the definition of spectral distribution, which is the main objective of investigation in this work. After this, we recall the definition of approximating class of sequences (a.c.s.) and list some classes of meaningful sequences, ending with the class of GLT sequences.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
be a matrix-sequence, with
A_{n}
of size
d_{n}
increasing, and let
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{s\times s}
be
a measurable function defined on a set
\Omega
with Lebesgue measure
0<\mathcal{L}(\Omega)<\infty
.

•

We say that
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
has a (asymptotic) singular value distribution described by
f
on
\Omega
, and we write
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}(f,\Omega)
, if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{d_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{n}}F(\sigma_{i}(A_{n}))=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{s}F(\sigma_{j}(f(x)))}{s}{\rm d}x,\qquad\forall\,F\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}).

(4)

•

We say that
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
has a (asymptotic) spectral (or eigenvalue) distribution described by
f
on
\Omega
, and we write
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}(f,\Omega)
, if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{d_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{n}}F(\lambda_{i}(A_{n}))=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{s}F(\lambda_{j}(f(x)))}{s}{\rm d}x,\qquad\forall\,F\in C_{c}(\mathbb{C}),

(5)

where
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
C_{c}(\mathbb{C})
represent the sets of continuous and compactly supported functions on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
, respectively.
If
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
has both a singular value and an eigenvalue distribution described by
f
, we write
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma,\lambda}\left(f,\Omega\right)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
be a square matrix-sequence of size
d_{n}
, such that
d_{n}\nearrow\infty
, and let
\left\{\{B_{n,t}\}_{n}\right\}_{t}
be a sequence of matrix-sequences of the same size
d_{n}
. We say that
\left\{\{B_{n,t}\}_{n}\right\}_{t}
is an approximating class of sequences (a.c.s.) for
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
if the following condition is met: for every
t
there exists
n_{t}
such that, for
n>n_{t}
,

A_{n}=B_{n,t}+R_{n,t}+N_{n,t},

and

\textnormal{rank}\left(R_{n,t}\right)\leq c(t)d_{n},

\left\|N_{n,t}\right\|\leq\omega(t),

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the spectral norm of a matrix,
n_{t},c(t),\omega(t)
depend only on
t
, and

\lim_{t\to\infty}c(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=0.

The following theorem constitutes a link between the notions of a.c.s. and of spectral distribution.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
22
]
)
Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
be a square matrix-sequence of size
d_{n}
, with
d_{n}\nearrow\infty
, and let
\left\{\{B_{n,t}\}_{n}\right\}_{t}
be an a.c.s. for
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
. Suppose that
\{B_{n,t}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}(f_{t},\Omega)
and
f_{t}\to f
in measure, then
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}(f,\Omega)
. Furthermore, if all the matrices involved are Hermitian,
\{B_{n,t}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}(f_{t},\Omega)
and
f_{t}\to f
in measure, then
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}(f,\Omega)
.

2.1
Toeplitz matrices, Diagonal samplings and zero distributed sequences

In this section we introduce some fundamental classes of sequences for which the spectral distribution is known and which constitute the starting elements for constructing the GLT algebra, namely, Toeplitz sequences, diagonal sampling sequences and zero-distributed sequences.

Toeplitz matrices are matrices of the form
T_{n}=(t_{i-j})_{i,j=1}^{n}
, thus having constant diagonals. An interesting subclass of Toeplitz matrices is the one of Toeplitz matrices generated by functions in
L^{1}([-\pi,\pi])
. We use the notation
T_{n}(f)=(\tilde{f}_{i-j})_{i,j=1}^{n}
to indicate the
n
-th Toeplitz matrix generated by
f
. The terms
\tilde{f}_{k}
represent the Fourier coefficients of the function
f
, namely,

\tilde{f}_{k}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(\theta)\rm{e}^{-ik\theta}\rm{d}\theta,

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. The link between the concepts of spectral distribution and Toeplitz matrices is given by the following result, see
[
22
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f\in L^{1}([-\pi,\pi])
. Then

\{T_{n}(f)\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}(f,[-\pi,\pi]).

If, moreover, the function
f
is real-valued a.e., then

\{T_{n}(f)\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}(f,[-\pi,\pi]).

Now, let
a:[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
n
-th diagonal sampling matrix generated by
a
as

D_{n}(a)=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}_{i=1,\dots,n}a\left(\frac{i}{n}\right).

Trivially, we have

\{D_{n}(a)\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda,\sigma}(a,[0,1]).

Finally, the class of zero distributed sequences is the class of sequences
\{Z_{n}\}_{n}
such that

\{Z_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}0.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denote the Schatten
p
-norm for every
p\geq 1
(for
p=\infty
we have
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}=\|\cdot\|
, i.e., the Schatten
\infty
-norm is the spectral norm). The following theorem constitutes a key characterization for the class of zero-distributed sequences (see
[
12
,
20
]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\{Z_{n}\}_{n}
be a matrix-sequence with
Z_{n}
of size
d_{n}
. With the natural convention that
1/\infty=0
, we have

1.

\{Z_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}0
if and only if
Z_{n}=R_{n}+N_{n}
, with
\text{rank}(R_{n})/d_{n}\to 0
and
\|N_{n}\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
;

2.

\{Z_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}0
if there exists
p\in[1,\infty]
such that

\frac{\|Z_{n}\|_{p}}{(d_{n})^{1/p}}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

2.2
GLT sequences and Geometric means of matrices

We are now ready to introduce the class of GLT sequences.

A Generalized Locally Toeplitz (GLT) sequence
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
is a matrix-sequence equipped with a measurable function
f:[0,1]\times[-\pi,\pi]\to\mathbb{C}
called GLT symbol. We use the notation
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f
to indicate that
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
is a GLT sequence with symbol
f
. Here, we report the main properties of the
*
-algebra of GLT sequences. The definition of GLT sequences can be rather cumbersome and requires the introduction of many accessory tools. For this reason, we only state the main algebraic properties of GLT sequences. For a complete and recent account of the theory of GLT theory (also in the multi-level setting), we refer the reader to the two comprehensive books
[
12
,
13
]
and to
[
4
,
5
]
.

GLT 0

If
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f
then
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}g
if and only if
f=g
a.e.

If
f:[0,1]\times[-\pi,\pi]\to\mathbb{C}
is measurable then there exists
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
such that
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f
;

GLT 1

if
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f
then
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}\left(f,[0,1]\times[-\pi,\pi]\right)
. If
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f
and each
A_{n}
is Hermitian then
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}\left(f,[0,1]\times[-\pi,\pi]\right)
;

GLT 2

If
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f
and
A_{n}=X_{n}+Y_{n}
, where

•

every
X_{n}
is Hermitian,

•

\|Y_{n}\|_{2}/\sqrt{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
,

then
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}\left(f,[0,1]\times[-\pi,\pi]\right)
.

GLT 3

We have

•

\{T_{n}(g)\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f(x,\theta)=g(\theta)
if
g\in L^{1}\left([-\pi,\pi]\right)
;

•

\{D_{n}(a)\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f(x,\theta)=a(x)
if
a
is a continuous almost everywhere function;

•

\{Z_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f(x,\theta)=0
if and only if
\{Z_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}0
;

GLT 4

if
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f
, then

•

\{A^{*}_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}\bar{f}
;

•

if, additionally,
f\neq 0
a.e., then
\{A^{\dagger}_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f^{-1}
;

GLT 5

if
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f
and
\{B_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}h
, then

•

\{\alpha A_{n}+\beta B_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}\alpha f+\beta h
for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
;

•

\{A_{n}B_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}fh
;

GLT 6

\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f
if and only if there exist GLT sequences
\{B_{n,t}\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}f_{t}
such that
\{\{B_{n,t}\}_{n}\}_{t}
is an a.c.s. for
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
and
f_{t}\to f
in measure.

We now recall the concept of geometric mean for two Hermitian positive definite matrices and connect this to the GLT theory. Since we are mainly interested in the spectral properties of the geometric mean of two matrix-sequences, we only give some results in this context and refer the reader to
[
17
]
for additional details and further properties.

Given two Hermitian Positive Definite (HPD) matrices
A
and
B
, their geometric mean is defined as

G(A,B)=A^{1/2}\left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}A^{1/2}=G(B,A).

This definition was rigorously analyzed in
[
3
]
, where the authors also listed the main properties that a proper geometric mean should satisfy (see also
[
18
,
19
]
for alternative definitions and generalizations to the case of geometric means of more than two matrices).

In the case of matrix-sequences, especially in the case of sequences in the GLT algebra, we may consider, and in fact it is quite crucial for the advancement in the present work, spectral properties of sequences of geometric means. In this regard, we have the following result from
[
15
]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume that
\{A_{n}\}\sim_{\text{GLT}}\kappa
and
\{B_{n}\}\sim_{\text{GLT}}\xi
, where
A_{n},B_{n}
are Hermitian positive definite for every
n
. Then

\displaystyle\{G(A_{n},B_{n})\}\sim_{\text{GLT}}(\kappa\xi)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\{G(A_{n},B_{n})\}\sim_{\sigma,\lambda}(\kappa\xi)^{1/2}.

3
Proof of the main result

In this section, we prove our main result, Theorem
1.1
. In order to prove the theorem, we need some auxiliary results dealing with the approximation, in the Frobenius norm, of geometric means of Toeplitz matrices.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
,
Y
,
\tilde{X}
,
\tilde{Y}
be square matrices; then

\left\|XY-\tilde{X}\tilde{Y}\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|X\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|Y-\tilde{Y}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\tilde{Y}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|X-\tilde{X}\right\|_{F}.

Proof.

By a straightforward computation, we have

\displaystyle\left\|XY-\tilde{X}\tilde{Y}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|XY-X\tilde{Y}+X\tilde{Y}-\tilde{X}\tilde{Y}\right\|_{F}=\left\|X\left(Y-\tilde{{Y}}\right)+\left(X-\tilde{X}\right)\tilde{Y}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|X\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|Y-\tilde{Y}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\tilde{Y}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|X-\tilde{X}\right\|_{F},

where the latter inequality follows from the discrete form of the generalized Hölder’s inequality; the thesis is then proven.
∎

The following series of lemmas shows that the geometric means of uniformly bounded sequences is still uniformly bounded and gives useful estimates of differences of geometric means in the Frobenius norm, which will be used for our main result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f,g\in L^{\infty}\left(\left[-\pi,\pi\right]\right)
be real-valued almost everywhere functions such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f,\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}g>0
.

Then there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, both independent of
n
, such that

\left\|G\left(T_{n}\left(f\right),T_{n}\left(g\right)\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}<C_{1}

and

\left\|G\left(T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1},T_{n}\left(g\right)\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}<C_{2}.

Proof.

By definition of geometric mean, we have

\displaystyle\left\|G\left(T_{n}\left(f\right),T_{n}\left(g\right)\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{n}\left(f\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1/2}_{n}T_{n}\left(g\right)T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{n}\left(f\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}^{1/2}\left\|T_{n}\left(g\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}\left(T_{n}\left(f\right)\right)}}\left\|T_{n}\left(f\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|T_{n}\left(g\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}^{1/2}.

By
[
12
, Theorem 6.2]
the Schatten-
\infty
norm of
T_{n}\left(f\right)
is bounded by
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
, and the same applies to
\left\|T_{n}\left(g\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}
. Moreover,
[
12
, Theorem 6.1]
guaranties
\lambda_{\min}\left(T_{n}\left(f\right)\right)\geq\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f>0
, so that the first statement of the thesis follows.

Again using the fact that the spectrum of
T_{n}\left(f\right)
is contained in the essential range of
f
, so that
0<\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f<\lambda_{\min}\left(T_{n}\left(f\right)\right)<\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}f
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|G\left(T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1},T_{n}\left(g\right)\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}^{2}\left\|T_{n}\left(f\right)^{1/2}_{n}T_{n}\left(g\right)T_{n}\left(f\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}\left(T_{n}\left(f\right)\right)}\left\|T_{n}\left(f\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}^{1/2}\left\|T_{n}\left(g\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}^{1/2},

leading to the thesis.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}\left(\left[-\pi,\pi\right]\right)
real valued almost everywhere; we denote by
\left\{C_{n}\left(f\right)\right\}_{n}
a sequence of circulant matrices such that
\left\|T_{n}\left(f\right)-C_{n}\left(f\right)\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.
The existence of such an approximation is well-known in the literature (see
[
23
]
and references therein).
Moreover, whenever
T_{n}\left(f\right)
is Hermitian positive definite, then
C_{n}\left(f\right)
can be taken as Hermitian positive definite.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f,g\in L^{\infty}\left(\left[-\pi,\pi\right]\right)
be real-valued a.e. functions such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f,\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}g>0
; then

\left\|G\left(T_{n}\left(f\right),T_{n}\left(g\right)\right)-G\left(C_{n}\left(f\right),C_{n}\left(g\right)\right)\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)

and

\left\|G\left(T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1},T_{n}\left(g\right)\right)-G\left(C_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1},C_{n}\left(g\right)\right)\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right).

Proof.

For the sake of a lighter notation, let
A=T_{n}\left(f\right)
,
B=T_{n}\left(g\right)
and
\tilde{A}=C_{n}\left(f\right)
,
\tilde{B}=C_{n}\left(g\right)
.

We have

\displaystyle\left\|G\left(A,B\right)-G\left(\tilde{A},\tilde{B}\right)\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|A^{1/2}\left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}A^{1/2}-\tilde{A}^{1/2}\left(\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}\tilde{A}^{1/2}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|A^{1/2}-\tilde{A}^{1/2}\right\|_{F}\left\|\left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}A^{1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}

\displaystyle+\left\|\tilde{A}^{1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|A^{1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}-\left(\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle+\left\|A^{1/2}-\tilde{A}^{1/2}\right\|_{F}\left\|\tilde{A}^{1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\left(\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}.

Since all the terms in the Schatten-
\infty
norm are uniformly bounded, we only need to estimate
\left\|A^{1/2}-\tilde{A}^{1/2}\right\|_{F}
and
\left\|\left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}-\left(\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{F}
to conclude. Since
\Lambda(A)\subset[\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f,\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}f]
, by the Lipschitz continuity of
t\mapsto\sqrt{t}
on sets bounded away from
0
, we have

\left\|A^{1/2}-\tilde{A}^{1/2}\right\|_{F}\leq L\left\|A-\tilde{A}\right\|_{F}=o(\sqrt{n}).

Moreover,

A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\sim A^{-1/2}A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}A^{1/2}=A^{-1}B,

so that
\Lambda(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})\subset[\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}(g/f),\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}(g/f)]
(see
[
9
]
), and

\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}=\tilde{A}^{-1}\tilde{B},

so that
\Lambda(\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2})\subset[\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}(g/f),\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}(g/f)]
.
Using again the Lipschitz continuity of
t\mapsto\sqrt{t}
on sets bounded away from
0
,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}-\left(\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq L\left\|A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}-\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq L\left\|A^{-1/2}-\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right\|_{F}\left\|BA^{-1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}

\displaystyle+L\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|A^{-1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|B-\tilde{B}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle+L\left\|A^{-1/2}-\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right\|_{F}\left\|A^{-1/2}B\right\|_{S,\infty}.

Noticing that
A^{-1/2}-\tilde{A}^{-1/2}=A^{-1/2}\left(\tilde{A}^{1/2}-A^{1/2}\right)\tilde{A}^{-1/2}
and proceeding as above, we get

\left\|\left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}-\left(\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{F}=o(\sqrt{n}),

as desired to conclude.

The same technique with minimal variations can be applied to infer the second statement.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
f_{j},g_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,k
, be real-valued a.e. functions such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f_{j},\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}g_{j}>0
for every
j
. Then

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}G\left(T_{n}\left(f_{j}\right),T_{n}\left(g_{j}\right)\right)-\prod_{j=1}^{k}G\left(C_{n}\left(f_{j}\right),C_{n}\left(g_{j}\right)\right)\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right),

and

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}G\left(T_{n}\left(f_{j}\right)^{-1},T_{n}\left(g_{j}\right)\right)-\prod_{j=1}^{k}G\left(C_{n}\left(f_{j}\right)^{-1},C_{n}\left(g_{j}\right)\right)\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right).

Proof.

Let
G_{j}=G\left(T_{n}\left(f_{j}\right),T_{n}\left(g_{j}\right)\right)
and
\tilde{G}_{j}=G\left(C_{n}\left(f_{j}\right),C_{n}\left(g_{j}\right)\right)
; we proceed by induction on
k
.

For
k=1
, the thesis reduces to the expression in Lemma
3.4
. Let
k>1
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}G_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}\tilde{G}_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}G_{j}\right)G_{k}-\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\tilde{G}_{j}\right)\tilde{G}_{k}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}G_{j}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|G_{k}-\tilde{G}_{k}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\tilde{G}_{k}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}G_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\tilde{G}_{j}\right\|_{F}

By Lemma
3.2
, Lemma
3.4
and the sub-multiplicativity of the Schatten-
\infty
norm,
\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}G_{j}\right\|_{S,\infty}\leq C
for some
C>0
and
\left\|G_{k}-\tilde{G}_{k}\right\|=o(\sqrt{n})
; moreover, by induction,
\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}G_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\tilde{G}_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
and
\left\|\tilde{G}_{k}\right\|_{S,\infty}
is uniformly bounded in
n
; hence, the first part of the thesis follows.

As above, the same procedure allows us to infer the second statement.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
f,g\in L^{\infty}\left(\left[-\pi,\pi\right]\right)
be real valued almost everywhere such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f,\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}g>0
; then

\left\|G\left(T_{n}\left(f\right),T_{n}\left(g\right)^{-1}\right)T_{n}\left(g\right)-G\left(T_{n}\left(f\right),T_{n}\left(g\right)\right)\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)

Proof.

Let
X_{n}=T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1/2}T_{n}\left(g\right)T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1/2}
,
Y_{n}=T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1}X_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1}
and
Z_{n}=T_{n}\left(f\right)X_{n}=T_{n}\left(f\right)^{1/2}T_{n}\left(g\right)T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1/2}
and let
\tilde{X}_{n}
,
\tilde{Y}_{n}
and
\tilde{Z}_{n}
be their corresponding circulant versions.

Note that, by the definition of
X_{n}
,
T_{n}\left(g\right)=T_{n}\left(f\right)^{1/2}X_{n}T_{n}\left(f\right)^{1/2}
, so that
T_{n}\left(g\right)^{-1}=T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1/2}X_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\left(f\right)^{-1/2}
.
Since
\left\{T_{n}\left(f\right)\right\}_{n}
and
\left\{T_{n}\left(g\right)\right\}_{n}
are uniformly bounded, so are
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n}
,
\left\{Y_{n}\right\}_{n}
and
\left\{Z_{n}\right\}_{n}
.

As in Lemma
3.4
, let
A=T_{n}\left(f\right)
,
B=T_{n}\left(g\right)
and
\tilde{A}=C_{n}\left(f\right)
,
\tilde{B}=C_{n}\left(g\right)
.
Now, for notational convenience, we drop the index
n
for
X_{n}
,
Y_{n}
,
Z_{n}
and
\tilde{X}_{n}
,
\tilde{Y}_{n}
,
\tilde{Z}_{n}
throughout the proof.

Consider now the difference in which we are interested, i.e.,
G(A,B^{-1})B-G(A,B)
; we have

\displaystyle G(A,B^{-1})B-G(A,B)

\displaystyle=A^{1/2}\left(A^{-1/2}B^{-1}A^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}A^{1/2}B-A^{1/2}\left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^{1/2}A^{1/2}

\displaystyle=A^{1/2}Y^{1/2}A^{1/2}B-A^{1/2}X^{1/2}A^{1/2}

\displaystyle=A^{1/2}\left(Y^{1/2}A^{1/2}BA^{-1/2}-X^{1/2}\right)A^{1/2}

\displaystyle=A^{1/2}\left(Y^{1/2}Z-X^{1/2}\right)A^{1/2}.

From the previous equality, we immediately get

\left\|G(A,B^{-1})B-G(A,B)\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|A^{1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}^{2}\left\|Y^{1/2}Z-X^{1/2}\right\|_{F}.

Since
\left\|A^{1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}
is (uniformly) bounded, we only need to prove that

\left\|Y^{1/2}Z-X^{1/2}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)

as
n\to\infty
to conclude.
Recalling the commutativity of circulant matrices, a straightforward computation yields
\tilde{A}^{1/2}\left(\tilde{Y}^{1/2}\tilde{Z}-\tilde{X}^{1/2}\right)\tilde{A}^{1/2}=0
, so that

\displaystyle\left\|Y^{1/2}Z-X^{1/2}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|Y^{1/2}Z-X^{1/2}-\tilde{Y}^{1/2}\tilde{Z}+\tilde{X}^{1/2}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tilde{X}^{1/2}-X^{1/2}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\tilde{Y}^{1/2}\tilde{Z}-Y^{1/2}Z\right\|_{F}.

Let
E_{1}=\tilde{X}^{1/2}-X^{1/2}
and
E_{2}=\tilde{Y}^{1/2}\tilde{Z}-Y^{1/2}Z
and estimate
\left\|E_{1}\right\|_{F}
and
\left\|E_{2}\right\|_{F}
to conclude.

We begin by providing a bound for
\left\|E_{1}\right\|_{F}
. First of all, since
t\mapsto\sqrt{t}
is Lipschitz continuous and noticing that, just as in Lemma
3.4
,
X=A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}
and
\tilde{X}=\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}
have spectrum in an interval uniformly bounded away from
0
, we get
\left\|\tilde{X}^{1/2}-X^{1/2}\right\|_{F}\leq L\left\|\tilde{X}-X\right\|_{F}
. Now notice that

\displaystyle\tilde{X}-X

\displaystyle=\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}-A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\tilde{A}^{-1/2}-A^{-1/2}\right)BA^{-1/2}+\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\left(\tilde{B}-B\right)A^{-1/2}+\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}\left(\tilde{A}^{-1/2}-A^{-1/2}\right),

so that

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{X}^{1/2}-X^{1/2}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq L\left\|\tilde{X}-X\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq L\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1/2}-A^{-1/2}\right\|_{F}\left(\left\|A^{-1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|B\right\|_{S,\infty}+\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{B}\right\|_{S,\infty}\right)

\displaystyle+L\left\|\tilde{B}-B\right\|_{F}\left\|A^{-1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}.

Since all the terms in Schatten-
\infty
norm are bounded by a constant,
\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1/2}-A^{-1/2}\right\|_{F}\leq L\left\|\tilde{A}-A\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
, and
\left\|\tilde{B}-B\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
, we conclude that
\left\|E_{1}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.

A similar technique can be exploited for bounding
\left\|E_{2}\right\|_{F}
; by Lemma
3.1

\left\|E_{2}\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|\tilde{Y}^{1/2}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{Z}-Z\right\|_{F}+\left\|Z\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{Y}^{1/2}-Y^{1/2}\right\|_{F}.

Notice that the Shatten-
\infty
norms are bounded and, again by Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{Z}-Z\right\|_{F}=\left\|\tilde{A}\tilde{X}-AX\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|\tilde{A}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{X}-X\right\|_{F}+\left\|X\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{A}-A\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right).

Moreover, note that
Y=A^{-1}X^{-1}A^{-1}=A^{-1/2}B^{-1}A^{-1/2}\sim B^{-1}A^{-1}
and
\tilde{Y}=\tilde{A}^{-1/2}\tilde{B}^{-1}\tilde{A}^{-1/2}=\tilde{B}^{-1}\tilde{A}
, so that applying the same reasoning as for
X
and
\tilde{X}
and using the Lipschitz continuity of
t\mapsto\sqrt{t}
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{Y}^{1/2}-Y^{1/2}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq L\left\|\tilde{Y}-Y\right\|_{F}=\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1}\tilde{X}^{-1}\tilde{A}^{-1}-A^{-1}X^{-1}A^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1}\tilde{X}^{-1}\tilde{A}^{-1}-\tilde{A}^{-1}\tilde{X}^{-1}A^{-1}+\tilde{A}^{-1}\tilde{X}^{-1}A^{-1}-A^{-1}X^{-1}A^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|F_{1}\right\|_{F}+\left\|F_{2}\right\|_{F},

where
F_{1}=\tilde{A}^{-1}\tilde{X}^{-1}\tilde{A}^{-1}-\tilde{A}^{-1}\tilde{X}^{-1}A^{-1}
and
F_{2}=\tilde{A}^{-1}\tilde{X}^{-1}A^{-1}-A^{-1}X^{-1}A^{-1}
.

Clearly,

\displaystyle\left\|F_{1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{X}^{-1}\tilde{A}^{-1}-\tilde{X}^{-1}A^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{X}^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1}-A^{-1}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right),

where we used that
\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1}-A^{-1}\right\|_{F}=\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1}(A-\tilde{A})A^{-1}\right\|_{F}\leq C\left\|\tilde{A}-A\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
, and

\displaystyle\left\|F_{2}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\tilde{A}^{-1}\tilde{X}^{-1}-A^{-1}X^{-1}\right)A^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{X}^{-1}-X^{-1}\right\|_{F}\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}+\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|X^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{A}^{-1}-A^{-1}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right),

by Lemma
3.1
.

Hence
\left\|E_{2}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
and we conclude

\left\|G\left(A,B^{-1}\right)B-G\left(A,B\right)\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right).

∎

Let
\mathcal{A}_{n}
be as in (
2
). For notational convenience, we let, for
j=1,\dots,k
,
A_{j}=T_{n}\left(f_{j,j}\right)
and, for
j=1,\dots,k-1
,
B_{j}=T_{n}\left(f_{j+1,j}\right)
and
C_{j}=T_{n}\left(f_{j,j+1}\right)
.

Consider now the block-diagonal matrix
\mathcal{E}_{n}
given by

\mathcal{E}_{n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}E_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&E_{k}\end{array}\right],\text{ with }E_{j}=\begin{cases}I_{n}\text{ for }j=1,\\
E_{j-1}G\left(B_{j-1}^{-1},C_{j-1}\right)\text{ for }j=2,\dots,k,\end{cases}

so that both
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{E}_{n}
are square matrices of size
kn
.

Theorem 3.7
.

With the previously introduced notation,

\left\|\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)

as
n\to\infty
, where

\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}A_{1}&G_{1}&&&\\
G_{1}&A_{2}&G_{2}&&\\
&G_{2}&A_{3}&&\\
&&&&G_{k-1}\\
&&&G_{k-1}&A_{k}\end{array}\right],\text{ with }G_{j}=G\left(B_{j},C_{j}\right)\text{ for }j=1,\dots,k-1.

Proof.

We prove that each block of
\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
has Frobenius norm of order
o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
; since the total number of non-zero blocks is
3k-2
independent of
n
, the claim follows.

We begin by estimating the Frobenius norm of the generic
j
-th lower diagonal block, for
j=1,\dots,k-1
, explicitly given by
E_{j+1}B_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-G_{j}
:

\displaystyle\left\|E_{j+1}B_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-G_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|E_{j+1}B_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}+E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-G_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|E_{j+1}B_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}+\left\|E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-G_{j}\right\|_{F}.

We proceed by investigating each term independently:

\displaystyle\left\|E_{j+1}B_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|E_{j}\left(G\left(B_{j}^{-1},C_{j}\right)B_{j}-G_{j}\right)E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|E_{j}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|G\left(B_{j}^{-1},C_{j}\right)B_{j}-G_{j}\right\|_{F}.

By the sub-multiplicativity of Schatten
p
-norms and Lemma
3.2
, we have
\left\|E_{j}\right\|_{S,\infty}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\left\|G\left(B_{i}^{-1},C_{i}\right)\right\|_{S,\infty}\leq C
.

Moreover, we already proved in Lemma
3.6
that
\left\|G\left(B_{j}^{-1},C_{j}\right)B_{j}-G_{j}\right\|_{F}=\left\|G\left(B_{j}^{-1},C_{j}\right)B_{j}-G\left(B_{j},C_{j}\right)\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
; hence we conclude

\left\|E_{j+1}B_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right).

As for the second term, let
\tilde{E}_{1}=I_{n}
and
\tilde{E}_{j}=\tilde{E}_{j-1}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j-1}^{-1},\tilde{C}_{j-1}\right)
for
j=2,\dots,k
; by exploiting the commutativity of the circulant algebra, we have
\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}=G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)
; therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-G_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}+G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)-G_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}+\left\|G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)-G_{j}\right\|_{F}.

The term
\left\|G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)-G_{j}\right\|_{F}=\left\|G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)-G\left(B_{j},C_{j}\right)\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
by Lemma
3.4
; for the first term we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\Big\|E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}+\tilde{E}_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)E_{j}^{-1}+

\displaystyle+\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\Big\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle+\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right)G_{j}E_{j}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}\left(G_{j}-G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\right)E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}+

\displaystyle+\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\left(E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right)\right\|_{F}.

The first term can be bounded by
\left\|G_{j}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|E_{j}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right\|_{F}
; by combining now Lemma
3.2
and Corollary
3.5
, we deduce
\left\|\left(E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right)G_{j}E_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.

The same procedure can be applied with minimal variations to the terms
\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}\left(G_{j}-G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\right)E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}
and
\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\left(E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right)\right\|_{F}
, leading to the desired estimate, that is,

\left\|E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}G\left(\tilde{B}_{j},\tilde{C}_{j}\right)\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right),

so that
\left\|E_{j}G_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-G_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
, allowing us to conclude
\left\|E_{j+1}B_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-G_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.

For the upper diagonal block
E_{j}C_{j}E_{j+1}^{-1}-G_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,k-1
, the very same procedure leads to an analogous equality, that is,
\left\|E_{j}C_{j}E_{j+1}^{-1}-G_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.

Finally, we tackle the diagonal element: as above, we claim
\left\|E_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-A_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.

First of all, exploiting the commutativity of the circulant algebra, we rewrite the diagonal term as
E_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}\tilde{A}_{j}\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}+\tilde{A}_{j}-A_{j}
, so that

\left\|E_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-A_{j}\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|E_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}\tilde{A}_{j}\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\tilde{A}_{j}-A_{j}\right\|_{F}.

Since
\left\|\tilde{A}_{j}-A_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
by Definition
3.3
, it is only left to prove
\left\|E_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}\tilde{A}_{j}\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\left\|E_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-A_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|E_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}+\tilde{E}_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}\tilde{A}_{j}E_{j}^{-1}+\tilde{E}_{j}\tilde{A}_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}\tilde{A}_{j}\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right)A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}\left(A_{j}-\tilde{A}_{j}\right)E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}+\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}\tilde{A}_{j}\left(E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right)\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left\|E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right\|_{F}+C_{2}\left\|A_{j}-\tilde{A}_{j}\right\|_{F}+C_{3}\left\|E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F},

with
C_{1}=\left\|A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}
,
C_{2}=\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}
and
C_{3}=\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}\tilde{A}_{j}\right\|_{S,\infty}
, where we have already determined that all these quantities are bounded independently of
n
.

By Corollary
3.5
, we know that
\left\|E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
and, by Definition
3.3
,
\left\|A_{j}-\tilde{A}_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.

Moreover,
\left\|E_{j}^{-1}-\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}=\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\left(E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right)E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{F}\leq C_{4}\left\|E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right\|_{F}
, with
C_{4}=\left\|E_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}\left\|\tilde{E}_{j}^{-1}\right\|_{S,\infty}
, so that
\left\|E_{j}-\tilde{E}_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.

Hence, we infer
\left\|E_{j}A_{j}E_{j}^{-1}-A_{j}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
, concluding the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{n}
be as in Equation (
2
). Assume that
f_{i,j}\in L^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi])
and real-valued almost everywhere for every
i,j
. Moreover, assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f_{i,i+1},\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}f_{i+1,i}>0
for every
i=1,\dots,k-1
. Then

\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}F(\theta),

with

F(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1,1}&f_{1,2}&&\\
f_{2,1}&f_{2,2}&\ddots&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&f_{k-1,k}\\
&&f_{k,k-1}&f_{k,k}\end{bmatrix}.

Equivalently,

\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}\tilde{F}(\theta),

with

\tilde{F}(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1,1}&f_{1,2}^{1/2}f_{2,1}^{1/2}&&\\
f_{1,2}^{1/2}f_{2,1}^{1/2}&f_{2,2}&\ddots&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&f_{k-1,k}^{1/2}f_{k,k-1}^{1/2}\\
&&f_{k,k-1}^{1/2}f_{k-1,k}^{1/2}&f_{k,k}\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

With the notation introduced in Theorem
3.7
, we have
\left\{\Pi_{n}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\Pi_{n}^{T}\right\}_{n}\sim_{\text{GLT}}\tilde{F}
, where
\Pi_{n}
is a permutation matrix that depends only on
n
and
k
(see
[
14
, Section 2]
).
Since
\Pi_{n}\hat{A}_{n}\Pi_{n}^{T}
is Hermitian for every
n
, we deduce
\left\{\Pi_{n}\hat{A}_{n}\Pi_{n}^{T}\right\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}\tilde{F}
.

Consider now

\Pi_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}\Pi_{n}^{T}=\Pi_{n}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\Pi_{n}^{T}-\Pi_{n}\left(\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\right)\Pi_{n}^{T}.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.7
,

\left\|\Pi_{n}\left(\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\right)\Pi_{n}^{T}\right\|_{F}=o\left(\sqrt{n}\right).

Hence, by
[
7
, Theorem 1]
, we deduce
\left\{\Pi_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}\Pi_{n}^{T}\right\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}\tilde{F}
.
Since the matrices
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\Pi_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}\Pi_{n}^{T}
are similar, their spectra coincide: therefore,
\left\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\right\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}\tilde{F}
.

Finally, since
F\left(\theta\right)
is similar to
\tilde{F}\left(\theta\right)
, the conclusion follows.

∎

5
Conclusions

In the present paper, we studied the spectral distribution of matrix-sequences with a non-Hermitian block structure, obtaining the result that the GLT symbol well approximates the spectrum of the sequence, under the hypothesis that each block has a Toeplitz structure generated by an essentially positive bounded function.

The result is based on the approximation, in the Frobenius norm, of each Toeplitz block with matrices in the circulant algebra.

A number of questions arises from the present contribution in terms of theoretical foundations of the results and possible applications and extensions to different settings. Here we list and discuss some of them.

1.

We extensively used the fact that whenever we consider the geometric mean of two sequences, both generating functions have strictly positive essential infimum. This is a technical assumption that allows us to control the behavior of the spectral norm of the sequence of geometric means. However, as our numerical evidence shows, we can probably eliminate this hypothesis for the more natural assumption that each block is simply positive definite.

2.

We analyze the problem in the very specific setting of Toeplitz blocks generated by uni-variate scalar symbols. Clearly, it would be interesting to extend this setting in different directions by considering first multi-variate scalar symbols and then uni-variate and multi-variate matrix-valued symbols for each block.

3.

In
[
1
,
2
]
, the authors studied the singular value and spectral distributions (the latter in the Hermitian case) even when the blocks do not have the same dimension
n
. It would be interesting to see under which assumption this can be extended in our non-Hermitian setting.

4.

The theory of spectral distribution for Toeplitz sequences is usually developed in the case of generating functions in
L^{1}([-\pi,\pi])
. Here, we assumed that the functions are in
L^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi])
in order to obtain control over the spectral norm of the sequence of geometric means. It would certainly be of interest to study whether this assumption may be relaxed to include all symbols in
L^{1}([-\pi,\pi])
.

5.

An interesting topic for future research is represented by the possibility of exploring applications and numerical methods for solving linear systems involving matrices with a similar non-Hermitian structure.

6.

It is known that a matrix
A=(a_{i,j})
can be symmetrized via a diagonal matrix if the following two conditions are met:

(a)

a_{ij}\neq 0
if and only if
a_{ji}\neq 0
for every
i,j
;

(b)

for every cycle
i_{1},\dots,i_{k},i_{k+1}
it holds

\prod_{l=1}^{k}a_{i_{l}},a_{i_{l+1}}=\prod_{l=1}^{k}a_{i_{l+1}},a_{i_{l}},

where, by convention,
i_{k+1}=i_{1}
.

Notice that, in our case, these assumptions are trivially satisfied by the entries of the matrix-valued symbol
F(\theta)
for (a.e.)
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
. This observation and the discussion in the previous points lead us to propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5.1
.

Let

\mathcal{A}_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}T_{n}(f_{1,1})&T_{n}(f_{1,2})&\dots&T_{n}(f_{1,k})\\
T_{n}(f_{2,1})&T_{n}(f_{2,2})&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&T_{n}(f_{k-1,k})\\
T_{n}(f_{k,1})&\dots&T_{n}(f_{k,k-1})&T_{n}(f_{k,k})\end{bmatrix},

where, for every
i,j=1,\dots,k,

f_{i,j}\in L^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi])
and real-valued almost everywhere with
T_{n}(f_{i,j})
Hermitian positive definite whenever
i\neq j
, or
f_{i,j}=0
almost everywhere. Assume that, for every cycle
i_{1},\dots,i_{k},i_{k+1}
, we have

\prod_{l=1}^{k}f_{i_{l},i_{l+1}}(\theta)=\prod_{l=1}^{k}f_{i_{l+1},i_{l}}(\theta)

for a.e.
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, where
i_{k+1}=i_{1}
.
Then,

\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\lambda}\begin{bmatrix}f_{1,1}&f_{1,2}&\dots&f_{1,k}\\
f_{2,1}&f_{2,2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&f_{k-1,k}\\
f_{k,1}&\dots&f_{k,k-1}&f_{k,k}\end{bmatrix}.
