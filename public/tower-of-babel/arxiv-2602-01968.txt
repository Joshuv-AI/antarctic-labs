Abstract
In this paper we address the problem of optimal liquidation of a large portfolio composed by securities exposed to default risk. The default time is described in terms of a Brownian motion representing the evolution of the value of the firm, whose assets are available in the market for investors. Considering that selling a large number of assets has a significant impact in the price, and hence in the portfolio’s value, the control problem involved to describe the optimal strategy to liquidate a large position is analyzed. Under suitable assumptions in the model, an explicit solution is given to the value function and a precise description of the optimal strategy is obtained.

1
Introduction

In the classical problem of portfolio optimization it is considered that the investor has a utility function and a set of stocks used to design an investment strategy which allows him to maximized the discounted value of his expected terminal wealth. However, there are other scenarios in which an investor is interested in selling a large number of shares with the aim of obtaining the maximum expected profit. The problem studied in this paper falls within the latter category, which encompasses those problems of optimal liquidation. We refer to the work of Alfonsi et al.
[
1
,
2
]
and Almgren and Chriss
[
3
,
4
]
for the description of the main elements that must be taken into account when modelling the dynamics involved in the market in this type of liquidation strategy; related with this problem we can also mention the works by Engle and Ferstenberg
[
9
]
, Gatheral and Shied
[
10
]
, Huberman and Stanzi
[
15
]
and Prediou et al.
[
18
]
.

This class of problems naturally gives rise to optimal stochastic control problems. In particular, when diffusion-type models are used to describe the state dynamics, the solution involves the analysis of HJB equations in order to characterize the value function and the optimal control; see, for instance, the contributions of Guo and Zervos
[
12
]
and Hernández-Hernández et. al.
[
14
]
on this connection. Here we consider a market model that leads to default by the firm issuing the shares. This default depends on the company’s financial statements, and in this paper we consider the existence of a critical level, described as a barrier in a functional of the stochastic process that describes them, and once this is reached, default by the firm is declared. As we might have in a high frequency market, complete observation of the asset prices is assumed. When the liquidity is an issue to execute a position there are works by Lokka
[
16
]
, Obizhaeva and Wang
[
17
]
, Shield and Schoneborn
[
20
]
and Schied et al.
[
21
]
addressing those problems. In this work we assume that there is a multiplicative impact in the price, but there are other ways to medel it, as in Bertsimas and Lo
[
5
]
, Gatheral et al.
[
11
]
or He and Mamayski
[
13
]
.

Here, we follow the intensity approach to model the default time, based in the the work of Elliott et.al
[
7
]
. It consists in describing the default process (a single jump process), defined in terms of a stopping time, through an intensity process, so that the compensated process is a martingale. Having this extra uncertainty in mind, the problem we are interested consists in finding the optimal execution policy, when we have a large position in the asset, modeling its dynamics before default as in the Black-Scholes diffusion, and including a negative effect in the price each time a portion of the original position is sold in the market.

We write the optimal execution problem as an stochastic optimal control problem adopting the dynamic programming approach to solve it. The HJB equation is deduced and a verification theorem is proved; furthermore, an explicit solution is found under specific assumptions in the model parameters. This approach have also been followed to analyze the problem of optimal dividend under default contagion by Qiu et al.
[
19
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we precisely describe the main elements of the model, including the default time and its intensity. We also describe the price dynamics when affected by both the uncertainty inherent in the default time and the existence of a large supply of assets. In Section 3 we present the optimal control problem related with the optimal execution problem, motivating the variational form of the HJB equation, and providing the explicit for of its solution, together with an explanation of the resulting optimal execution policy. this is done making some simplification in the function involved in the default time. This specific form of the default time is pedagogical and represents an interesting case study, as it allows us to infer the correct partitioning of the state space, through which the optimal strategy is defined. Numerical results illustrate these findings in Section
4
. The Appendix contains some technical results, which are presented at the end of the paper for ease of reading.

2
Market impact model

In this section we describe the optimal execution model. Let us fix a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
satisfying the usual conditions and carrying a standard two dimensional
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-Brownian motion
W=(W^{1},W^{2})
and a random variable
U
, independent of
W
. Given
\rho\in[-1,1]
fixed, define the correlated Brownian motions
B_{t}=\rho W^{1}_{t}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}W^{2}_{t}
, that is,
[B,W^{1}]_{t}=\rho t
; the filtration generated by
B
and
W
is denoted by
\mathbb{F}^{B}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{t}\}
and
\mathbb{F}^{W^{1},W^{2}}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W^{1}_{t},W^{2}_{t}}\}
, respectively.

We consider an agent holding an initial (positive) position of
y
shares of a financial asset, which has to be sold maximising the expected gains. The information available to the agent is enclosed in the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
.
As part of our model, we include a random time
\tau
, which represents a default time, when we are concerned with the sell of stocks, or the disclosure of positive information of the firm, having a positive effect in the prices of the stock, if we were interested in buying. We shall consider the model adapted by Hull and White, motivated by the results of Ettinger et al.
[
8
]

Default time.
We now present two different ways to model the default time, based on the assumption of the existence of an index that describes the financial state of the company, which, when crossed over a threshold, increases the possibility of bankruptcy.
The first of these will be the case studied in this work, since its meaning in terms of a critical (deterministic) barrier of the index allows us to model interesting cases, in addition to allowing us to present the main results in a concrete way, as well as offering a geometric interpretation of the strategies in terms of a partition of the state space.

(a)

The first default
\tau
is defined, in its general form, as

\tau\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\inf\left\{t>0:\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\psi\left(W^{1}_{s}-b(s)\right){\rm d}s>U\right\},

(2.1)

where
\lambda>0
is a constant rate,
U
is a random variable independent of
\mathcal{F}^{(W_{1},W_{2})}_{\infty}
, with exponential law of parameter one, and
b:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a continuous function. The function
\psi\in[0,1]
is right continuous and decreasing that satisfies
\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\psi(x)=1
and
\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\psi(x)=0
.
However, we make the specific choice
\psi(x)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,0)}(x)
, considering

\psi(W^{1}_{t}-b)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{W^{1}_{t}<b\}}.

donde
b>0
is a constant; see Ettinger et. al.
[
8
]
. Here
W^{1}_{t}
represents the evolution of the credit index value of the firm. Note that when
\{W^{1}_{t}<b\}
and
\{\tau>t\}
describe the time periods prior to the company’s default.

(b)

The second option is to take the default time as
\tilde{\tau}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\inf\{t>0:W^{1}_{t}<b\}
, where
b>0
. This is somehow more drastic that the previous one, as it is expressed in terms of a barrier of the index process, unlike the previous one, in which there is an accumulation of time that the index process spends below the threshold value. In this case company’s default is declared as soon as the index process falls below the barrier
b
.

Define the
default process

D_{t}=\mathbbm{1}_{[\tau>t]}

and its
\sigma-
field
\mathcal{D}_{t}=\sigma(D_{s}:s\leq t)
.
Throghout it is assumed that the information available to the investor is contained in the completed version of the filtration

\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{F}^{W^{1},W^{2}}\vee\mathcal{D}
.
In this case, the process

M_{t}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=D_{t}-\lambda\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\psi\left(W^{1}_{s}-b\right){\rm d}s

is a
\mathbb{G}-
martingale, which implies that
t\to\lambda_{t}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\lambda\psi\left(W^{1}_{t}-b\right)\mathbbm{1}_{[t<\tau]}
is the
\mathbb{G}-
intensity of
\tau
, and hence
\langle D\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{s}{\rm d}s
. The results presented in this paper consider only Case (a), with
\psi(W^{1}_{t}-b)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{W^{1}_{t}<b\}}
.

Note that, defining

h(r)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\lambda\int_{0}^{r}\psi\left(W^{1}_{s}-b\right){\rm d}s,

(2.2)

for
t\geq r
,

\mathbb{P}[\tau>r|\mathcal{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{t}]=\exp\Bigg[-\lambda\int_{0}^{r}\psi\left(W^{1}_{s}-b\right){\rm d}s\Bigg]=\expo^{-h(r)}.

This follows from the following observation. Since
U
is an exponential random variable with parameter one, which is independent of

\mathcal{F}^{W^{1},W^{2}}_{\infty},
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\tau>r|\mathcal{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau>r\}}|\mathcal{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{h(r)\leq U\}}|\mathcal{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{h(r)\leq U\}}|F^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{\infty}]|\mathcal{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\expo^{-h(r)}|\mathcal{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\expo^{-h(r)}.

Hence, taking the limit when
t\to\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\tau>r|\mathcal{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{r}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}[\tau>r|\mathcal{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{\infty}]

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigg[-\lambda\int_{0}^{r}\psi\left(W^{1}_{s}-b\right){\rm d}s\Bigg].

(2.3)

According to Bremaud and Yor
[
6
]
, this implies that the
H-
hypothesis holds, which establishes that any
\mathbb{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}
martingale is also a
\mathbb{G}-
martingale.
Moreover, by the Jeulin-Yor theorem, the intensity process of
\tau
is given by

\lambda_{t}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\lambda\psi\left(W^{1}_{t}-b\right)\mathbbm{1}_{[t<\tau]}.

(2.4)

Investor’s position.

Given the initial amount of shares
y
held by the investor, the trading strategies are denoted by
\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}
, representing the amount of shares that have been sold by the investor until time
t
. Hence, the total amount of shares held by the investor at time
t
is given by

Y_{t}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=y-\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t},\qquad t\geq 0,

where
\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}
is a non-decreasing càglàd
(\mathcal{G}_{t})
-adapted process such that

\displaystyle\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{0+}=0,

(2.5)

and
\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{\tau}=\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t}
, for
\;t>\tau
.
We denote the set of strategies that satisfy the above assumptions by
\mathcal{A}(y)
. The description of the execution model is based on the work by Guo and Zervos
[
12
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

(i) Strategies that combine the possibility of buying assets of stock before default can also be included in the model without much complication. However, as in the case studied by Guo and Zervos
[
12
]
, we expect that the optimal execution problem is reduce to the case when only selling is allowed.

(ii) The hypothesis on the far right of (
2.5
) establishes the fact that the investor aims to liquidate the complete position. Further, after default
\tau
occurs, no more transactions are allowed.

Stock price process.

The stock price observed by the agent, independently of the actions of other market participants, is modeled by the geometric Brownian motion
X^{0}
with drift,
which is known to satisfy the SDE:

\frac{{\rm d}X_{t}^{0}}{X_{t}^{0}}=\mu{\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{t},\qquad t\geq 0,

for some constants
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\neq 0
.
This price process will be impacted in two different ways along the evolution of the market and the investor’s execution strategy. Firstly, due to the oversupply of shares in the market due to the strategy
\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}\in\mathcal{A}(y)
and, secondly, due to the company’s default.
Regarding the first one,
when the agent decides to sell some number of shares of the asset at time
t
, we assume that there is a multiplicative impact in the price, namely, the resulting price
\tilde{X}_{t}
is assumed to have the form

\tilde{X}_{t}=X^{0}_{t}\exp\{-\gamma\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t}\},

(2.6)

for some positive constant
\gamma
describing the permanent impact on the price, in terms of the process
X^{0}_{t}
. This is a càglàd process and we denote

\Delta\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t}=\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t^{+}}-\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t}
.
Denoting the process
(\xi^{\mathpzc{s}})^{{\rm c}}
as the continuous part of
\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}

we get the dynamics of the price (affected by the transactions) as

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\tilde{X}_{t}}{\tilde{X}_{t}}=\left[\mu{\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{t}\right]-\left[{\rm d}(\xi^{\mathpzc{s}})_{t}^{{\rm c}}+\int_{0}^{\Delta\xi_{t}^{\mathpzc{s}}}\expo^{-\gamma u}{\rm d}u\right],\;\;t\geq 0.

(2.7)

Note that, defining

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{t}\circ_{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi_{t}^{\mathpzc{s}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{X}_{t}{\rm d}(\xi^{\mathpzc{s}})_{t}^{{\rm c}}+\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\tilde{X}_{t}\Big[1-\expo^{-\gamma\Delta\xi_{t}^{\mathpzc{s}}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\tilde{X}_{t}{\rm d}(\xi^{\mathpzc{s}})_{t}^{{\rm c}}+\tilde{X}_{t}\int_{0}^{\Delta\xi_{t}^{\mathpzc{s}}}\expo^{-\gamma u}{\rm d}u,

(2.8)

the last term on the right of the above display can be written in a more compact way.

Concerning the effect of the default in the price process, we assume that after the default time
\tau
the price of the asset is zero, that is,

X_{t}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\tilde{X}_{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\leq\tau\}}

(2.9)

is the price process for a given investment strategy
\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}
.
Therefore, the investor loses the remainder of their position once the default occurs, as
X_{t}=0
for
t>\tau
. Observe that
X^{0}_{0}=\tilde{X}_{0}=X_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

3
Stochastic control problem with full information

Denoting by
Y_{t}
the number of shares left in the portfolio of the investor at time
t
, the pair
(\tilde{X}_{t},Y_{t})
is referred as the state (controlled) process associated to the strategy

\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}
.
Let
C^{\mathpzc{s}}
be a positive constant representing the transaction cost associated with the sell of shares and
\delta>0
the time discounting factor. Then, denoting by
\mathbb{E}_{x,y,w}
the conditional expectation on the event
\{\tilde{X}_{0}=x,Y_{0}=y,W^{1}_{0}=w\}
, the expected net present value of gains associated with each strategy
\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}\in\mathcal{A}(y)
is given by

\displaystyle J_{x,y,w}(\xi^{\mathpzc{s}})

\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\mathbb{E}_{x,y,w}\Biggr[\int_{[0,\tau)}\expo^{-\delta t}\left[\tilde{X}_{t}\circ_{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t}-C^{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi_{t}^{\mathpzc{s}}\right]-\expo^{-\delta\tau}g(\tilde{X}_{\tau},Y_{\tau})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau<\infty\}}\Biggl].

(3.1)

The aim of the investor is to maximize the functional
J_{x,y,w}(\cdot)
over the set
\mathcal{A}(y)
.

Throughout
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a non-negative continuous function representing the terminal cost at the default time
\tau
, satisfying the boundary condition
g(x,0)=0
,
and such that, for
y\in B\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
, with
B
a compact set, there exists a positive constant, possibly depending on
B
,
C(B)
such that
x\to g(x,y)\leq C(B)x
, for
x\geq 0
. Additionally, we also assume that

\displaystyle\delta>\mu.

(3.2)

Now, conditioning in the r.h.s. of (
3.1
) by
\mathcal{F}^{W_{1},W_{2}}_{\infty}
and recalling the definition of
\tau
as in (
2.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle J_{x,y,w}(\xi^{\mathpzc{s}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,y,w}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\expo^{-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\psi(W^{1}_{s}-b){\rm d}s-\delta t}\left[\tilde{X}_{t}\circ_{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t}-C^{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi_{t}^{\mathpzc{s}}\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;-\int_{0}^{\infty}\expo^{-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\psi(W^{1}_{s}-b){\rm d}s-\delta t}\lambda\psi(W^{1}_{t}-b)g(\tilde{X}_{t},Y_{t}){\rm d}t\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,y,w}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}\Bigg[\tilde{X}_{t}\circ_{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t}-C^{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi_{t}^{\mathpzc{s}}-\lambda\psi(W^{1}_{t}-b)g(\tilde{X}_{t},Y_{t}){\rm d}t\bigg]\Bigg],

(3.3)

with the dynamics of
\tilde{X}
described by (
2.6
). Here
\tilde{h}(t)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=h(t)+\delta t
; see (
2.2
).

We are interested in characterizing the value function defined by

\displaystyle V(x,y,w)=\sup_{\xi^{s}\in\mathcal{A}(y)}J_{x,y,w}(\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}).

Notice that
V(x,0,w)=0
for any
x\geq 0
and
w\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, at the initial time the investor can sell immediately the available stock
y
, implementing the strategy with
\xi^{s}_{0}=y
, that naturally induce the lower bound for the value function

V(x,y,w)\geq\frac{x}{\gamma}[1-\expo^{-\gamma y}]-C^{\mathpzc{s}}y.

(3.4)

An upper estimate can also be obtained using Ito’s formula for

\displaystyle{\rm d}(\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}\tilde{X}_{t})

\displaystyle={\rm d}(\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}X^{0}_{t}\expo^{-\gamma\xi^{s}_{t}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}\left[-(\lambda\psi(W^{1}_{t}-b)+\delta)X_{t}^{0}\expo^{-\gamma\xi_{t}^{s}}{\rm d}t+X^{0}_{t}{\rm d}\expo^{-\gamma\xi^{s}_{t}}+\expo^{-\gamma\xi^{s}_{t}}(\mu X^{0}_{t}{\rm d}t+\sigma X^{0}_{t}dB_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}\left[(\mu-\delta-\lambda\psi(W^{1}_{t}-b))X^{0}_{t}\expo^{-\gamma\xi^{s}_{t}}{\rm d}t+X^{0}_{t}{\rm d}\expo^{-\gamma\xi^{s}_{t}}+\sigma X^{0}_{t}\expo^{-\gamma\xi^{s}_{t}}{\rm d}B_{t}\right].

Observe that extracting the second term on the r.h.s., we get for fixed finite
T
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}\tilde{X}_{t}\circ_{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{T}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}X^{0}_{t}{\rm d}\expo^{-\gamma\xi^{s}_{t}}=\frac{x}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{\gamma}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(T)}\tilde{X}_{T}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{T}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}X^{0}_{t}\expo^{-\gamma\xi^{s}_{t}}\left[(\mu-\delta-\lambda\psi(W^{1}_{t}-b)){\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{t}\right].

In view of the hypotheses on the parameters (
3.2
), we get from (
3
) that

J_{x,y,w}(\xi^{\mathpzc{s}})\leq\frac{x}{\gamma},

and, since
\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}
was chosen arbitrarily, we conclude that

V(x,y,w)\leq\frac{x}{\gamma}.

The above estimations of the value function
V
will be useful to characterize it through the HJB equation, once the verification result has been established.

We define the operator
\mathcal{L}
acting on functions
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2,1,2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}f(x,y,w)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{2}f_{xx}(x,y,w)+\mu xf_{x}(x,y,w)+\frac{1}{2}f_{ww}+\rho\sigma xf_{xw}-\delta f(x,y,w).

In order to derive heuristically the HJB equation, we follow the next argument adapted to our problem from
[
12
, p. 295]
. Assume that at time
t>0
the stock price is
x
, the number of stocks left in the portfolio is
y
and the index value is
w
. Then, using (
2
), there are two possible scenarios, described by the possibility that, for
\Delta t>0
,

1.

t<\tau\leq t+\Delta t
, which occurs with an approximate (conditional) probability given by
\Delta t\lambda_{t}
.

2.

\tau>t+\Delta t
, which occurs with an approximate (conditional) probability given by
1-\Delta t\lambda_{t}
.

Then, a possible investment strategy is that the investor sells all the available stock in the first scenario and, in the second, do not sell anything. In this case,
applying this argument at
t=0
, given the homogenity of the model,
and based in the expression (
3.1
) for
J_{x,y,w}
, we get

v(x,y,w)\geq\mathbb{E}_{x,y,w}\left[\expo^{-\int_{0}^{\Delta t}(\lambda_{s}+\delta){\rm d}s}v(X^{0}_{\Delta t},y,W^{1}_{\Delta t})\mathbbm{1}_{[\tau>\Delta t]}\right.\\
\left.+\expo^{-\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\lambda_{s}{\rm d}s}g(X^{0}_{\Delta t},y)\mathbbm{1}_{[\tau>\Delta t]}+\left[\frac{x}{\gamma}[1-\expo^{-\gamma y}]-C^{\mathpzc{s}}y\right]\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau\leq\Delta t\}}\right].

(3.5)

Here, we have used the hypothesis on
g
and (
3.4
).
Then, using Ito’s rule and letting
\Delta t
go to zero, we get the inequality

\mathcal{L}v(x,y,w)-\lambda\psi(w-b)v(x,y,w)-\lambda\psi(w-b)g(x,y)\leq 0.

The previous strategy can be modified slightly, allowing to sell a small fraction of stock in the second scenario and, again, sell all the stock in the first scenario. Then, the last term in r.h.s. of (
3.5
) remains the same, and hence

v(x,y,w)\geq v(x-\varepsilon\gamma x,y-\varepsilon,w)+(x-C^{\mathpzc{s}})\varepsilon.

Then, by taking
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
we obtain

-\gamma xv_{x}(x,y,w)-v_{y}(x,y,w)+x-C^{\mathpzc{s}}\leq 0.

Then, the dynamic programming equation is given by

\max\{\mathcal{L}v(x,y,w)-\lambda\psi(w-b)[v(x,y,w)+g(x,y)],\\
-\gamma xv_{x}(x,y,w)-v_{y}(x,y,w)+x-C^{\mathpzc{s}}\}=0.

(3.6)

with the boundary condition

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v(x,0,w)=0,\qquad\text{for every $x>0$},\;w\in\mathbb{R}\\
v(0,y,w)=0,\qquad\text{for every $y>0$},\;w\in\mathbb{R}.\ \end{cases}

(3.7)

3.1
Explicit solution to the HJB equation

In the next result we shall prove that the solution of the HJB equation (
3.6
) can be obtained explicitly under suitable conditions by constructing a smooth solution when the liquidation cost is bilinear; namely,
g(x,y)=Kxy
for some positive constant
K
. This is achieved by constructing a candidate solution and identifying the continuation and selling regions in
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, denoted by
\mathbb{W}
and
\mathbb{S}
, respectively.

Roughly speaking, the continuation region
\mathbb{W}
is characterized as the interior of the set where

\mathcal{L}v(x,y,w)-\lambda\psi(w-b)[v(x,y,w)+g(x,y)]=0,

(3.8)

while the selling region

\{\lambda_{t}\}

\{W^{1}_{t}\}

\mathbb{W}
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{W}:&=\{(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}:y>0,\ x<F(y,w),\ w{\geq}b\},\\
\mathcal{S}:&=\{(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}:y>0,\ x\geq F(y,w),\ w{\geq}b\},\\

\mathcal{W}
and

represent the waiting regions

\mathcal{S}
and

denote the corresponding selling regions under those scenarios; we implicitly assume that (

(3.10)

We shall see that in the selling
regions, the agent liquidates shares according to the mappings

and

of default. In fact, each selling region can be decomposed into two disjoint subregions,

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}

\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\{(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}:y>0,\ x>F_{0}\expo^{\gamma y},\ w\geq b\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}

\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\{(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}:F_{0}\leq x\leq F_{0}\expo^{\gamma y},\ y>0,\ w\geq b\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(\lambda)}_{1}

\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\{(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}:y>0,x>\expo^{\gamma y}G_{\lambda}(y),w<b\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(\lambda)}_{2}

\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\{(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}:y>0,\ G_{\lambda}(y)\leq x\leq\expo^{\gamma y}G_{\lambda}(y),\ w<b\},

where

and

. In

Intuitively, the presence of default risk induces more aggressive liquidation

Theorem 3.1
.

The HJB equation defined in (

(3.11)

where

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{x^{n_{0}}}{\gamma n_{0}^{2}}\left[\frac{n_{0}-1}{n_{0}C^{\mathpzc{s}}}\right]^{n_{0}-1}[1-\expo^{-\gamma n_{0}y}]&\text{if}\ 0<x\leq F_{0},\\
\vskip-11.38092pt&\\
v^{(0)}(F_{0},y-\mathbb{Y}(x,F_{0}))&\\
\quad+\frac{x}{\gamma}\left[1-\expo^{-\gamma\mathbb{Y}(x,F_{0})}\right]-C^{\mathpzc{s}}\mathbb{Y}(x,F_{0})&\text{if}\ F_{0}<x\leq F_{0}\expo^{\gamma y},\\
\vskip-11.38092pt&\\

\end{cases}

(3.12)

\displaystyle v^{(\lambda)}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x^{n_{1}}B(y)-\frac{\lambda K}{\delta+\lambda-\mu}xy&\text{if}\ 0<x\leq G_{\lambda}(y),\\
\vskip-11.38092pt&\\
v^{(\lambda)}(G_{\lambda}(y),y-\mathbb{Y}(x,G_{\lambda}(y)))&\\

\vskip-11.38092pt&\\

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}l[l-1]+\mu l-\delta=0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}l[l-1]+\mu l-[\lambda+\delta]=0,

(3.14)

and

\displaystyle F_{0}

\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\frac{n_{0}C^{\mathpzc{s}}}{n_{0}-1},

(3.15)

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\frac{n_{1}C^{\mathpzc{s}}}{[n_{1}-1]\left[1+\frac{\lambda K}{\delta+\lambda-\mu}(\gamma y+1)\right]},

(3.16)

\displaystyle B(y)

\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\expo^{-\gamma n_{1}y}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{C^{\mathpzc{s}}\expo^{\gamma n_{1}u}}{[n_{1}-1][G_{\lambda}(u)]^{n_{1}}}{\rm d}u.

Theorem 3.3
.

Consider the optimal execution problem formulated in Section
3
. The function
v
defined in Theorem

satisfies that

v(x,y,w)\geq V(x,y,w),\qquad\text{for all $(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}$}.

Moreover, if for all initial conditions
(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, there exists
\xi^{*}\in\Xi(y)
such that

\displaystyle(\tilde{X}_{t}^{*},Y_{t}^{*},W_{t})\in\mathcal{W},\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$, $\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

and,

\displaystyle\xi^{*}_{t+}=\int_{[0,t]}1_{\{(\tilde{X}_{t}^{*},Y_{t}^{*},W^{*}_{t})\in\mathcal{S}\}}{\rm d}\xi^{*}_{t},\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$, $\mathbb{P}$-a.s.,}

(3.19)

where
\tilde{X}^{*}
, and
Y^{*}
are the share price and shares held processes associated with the liquidation strategy
\xi^{*}
. Then
v
identifies with the value function
V
of the stochastic control problem, i.e.

v(x,y,w)=V(x,y,w),\qquad(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

(i) Consider the function
v
defined in (
3.11
), and an arbitrary strategy
(\xi^{s})\in\mathcal{A}(y)
.
Note that
v\in\mathcal{C}^{2,1,2}\big(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{b\})\big)
, and therefore
v
is not continuous at
w=b
. However, since the Brownian motion
W
does not spend positive time at
b
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., we may apply Itô’s formula to the process
\{\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}v(\tilde{X}_{t},Y_{t},W^{1}_{t})\}
, with
\tilde{h}(t)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=h(t)+\delta t
, and obtain

\displaystyle\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}

\displaystyle v(\tilde{X}_{t^{+}},Y_{t^{+}},W^{1}_{t})=v(x,y,w)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(s)}\left(\mathcal{L}v(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s})-\lambda\psi(W^{1}_{s}-b)v(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s})\right){\rm d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(s)}\left[\gamma\tilde{X}_{s}v_{x}(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s})+v_{y}(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s})\right]{\rm d}(\xi^{s})^{c}_{s}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq s\leq t}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(s)}\left[v(\tilde{X}_{s+},Y_{s+},W^{1}_{s})-v(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s})\right]+\tilde{M}_{t},

(3.20)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{t}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\int_{0}^{t}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(s)}\Big[\tilde{X}_{s}v_{x}(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s})\sigma{\rm d}B_{s}+v_{w}(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s}){\rm d}W^{1}_{s}\Big]

is a local martingale.
Following the proof of Proposition 4.1 of Guo and Zervos
[
12
]
and using the fact that
v
satisfies (
3.6
), we get

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle(\tilde{X}_{s^{+}},Y_{s^{+}},W^{1}_{s})-v(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s})

\displaystyle\quad=-\int_{0}^{\Delta\xi_{s}^{s}}\Bigg[\gamma\expo^{-\gamma u}\tilde{X}_{s}v_{x}(\expo^{-\gamma u}\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s}-u,W^{1}_{s})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+v_{y}(\expo^{-\gamma u}\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s}-u,W^{1}_{s})\Bigg]{\rm d}u\leq\int_{0}^{\Delta\xi_{s}^{s}}[C^{\mathpzc{s}}-\expo^{-\gamma u}\tilde{X}_{s}]{\rm d}u.

(3.21)

Hence, using (
3.1
) in (
3.1
), we have that

\displaystyle\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}v(\tilde{X}_{t^{+}},Y_{t^{+}},W^{1}_{t})\leq v(x,y,w)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(s)}\left(\mathcal{L}v(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s})-\lambda\psi(W^{1}_{s}-b)v(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s},W^{1}_{s})\right){\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(s)}[C^{\mathpzc{s}}-\tilde{X}_{s}]{\rm d}(\xi^{s})^{c}_{s}+\sum_{0\leq s\leq t}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(s)}\int_{0}^{\Delta\xi_{s}^{s}}[C^{\mathpzc{s}}-\expo^{-\gamma u}\tilde{X}_{s}]du+\tilde{M}_{t}.

Using again that
v
is a solution to (
3.6
) and (
2

\displaystyle v(x,y,w)+\tilde{M}_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\expo^{-\tilde{h}(t)}v(\tilde{X}_{t^{+}},Y_{t^{+}},W^{1}_{t})

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\expo^{-\tilde{h}(s)}\left[\tilde{X}_{s}\circ_{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi^{\mathpzc{s}}_{s}-C^{\mathpzc{s}}{\rm d}\xi_{s}^{\mathpzc{s}}-\lambda\psi(W^{1}_{s}-b)g(\tilde{X}_{s},Y_{s}){\rm d}s\right].

By using stopping time

\displaystyle v(x,y,w)

\displaystyle V(x,y,w)\leq v(x,y,w),\quad\text{for}\;(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}.
