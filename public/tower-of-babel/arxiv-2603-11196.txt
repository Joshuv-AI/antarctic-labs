Abstract
The prim-lwe problem, introduced by Sehrawat, Yeo, and Desmedt (Theoretical Computer Science, 886 (2021)), is a variant of the Learning with Errors problem in which the secret matrix is required to have a primitive-root determinant. The dimension-uniform reduction constant is c ⁡ ( p ) = inf n ≥ 1 c n ​ ( p ) c(p)=\inf_{n\geq 1}c_{n}(p) , where c n ​ ( p ) c_{n}(p) is the exact density of n × n n\times n matrices over 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} with primitive-root determinant. Sehrawat, Yeo, and Desmedt asked whether inf p ​ prime c ⁡ ( p ) = 0 \inf_{p\text{ prime}}c(p)=0 , observing that an affirmative answer would follow from the conjectural infinitude of primorial primes. We resolve this question unconditionally using only Dirichlet’s theorem and Mertens’ product formula, bypassing the primorial-prime hypothesis. We further establish the sharp order min p ≤ x ⁡ c ⁡ ( p ) ≍ 1 log ⁡ log ⁡ x ( x → ∞ ) , \min_{p\leq x}c(p)\asymp\frac{1}{\log\log x}\qquad(x\to\infty), and show that the limiting distribution of c ⁡ ( p ) c(p) over the primes has support exactly [ 0 , 1 / 2 ] [0,1/2] . This full-support property appears to be new. The law coincides with the classical shifted-prime distribution of φ ⁡ ( p − 1 ) / ( p − 1 ) \varphi(p-1)/(p-1) via a transport lemma, is moreover continuous and purely singular, and the limiting measure has Hausdorff dimension 0 0 . The moments of the limiting distribution are given by convergent Euler products that uniquely determine it, and the associated Mellin transform extends analytically to the right half-plane. We also derive explicit lower bounds on c ⁡ ( q ) c(q) for primes of cryptographic interest, parameterized solely by the number of distinct prime factors of q − 1 q-1 . As a simple conservative explicit bound, for any prime q > 2 30 q>2^{30} the dimension-uniform expected overhead 1 / c ⁡ ( q ) 1/c(q) is at most 1.79 ​ log ⁡ q 1.79\log q . On the other hand, our results show that the worst-case overhead among primes p ≤ x p\leq x is of order Θ ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ x ) \Theta(\log\log x) , and in particular 1 / c ⁡ ( q ) = O ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ q ) 1/c(q)=O(\log\log q) pointwise.

2
Background and the Open Question

We now record the precise setup from
[
1
]
and state the open question. Following
[
1
]
, define

M_{n}^{\mathrm{prim}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\;=\;\bigl\{\,M\in M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p}):\det(M)\text{ generates }\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}\,\bigr\}.

The determinant map

\det:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}

is a surjective homomorphism, so all its fibres have the same cardinality.
Therefore a fraction
\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)
of invertible matrices have
primitive-root determinant. Since

|\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p^{n^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{j}}\Bigr),

it follows that

c_{n}(p)\;:=\;\frac{|M_{n}^{\mathrm{prim}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|}{|M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|}\;=\;\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}\;\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{j}}\Bigr),

recovering
[
1
, Corollary 4]
. Consequently, the limit

c(p)\;:=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}(p)\;=\;\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}\;\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{j}}\Bigr)

exists and is strictly positive for each fixed
p
. The quantity
c_{n}(p)
is the exact acceptance probability in dimension
n
. Since
c_{n}(p)
is decreasing in
n
, the dimension-uniform constant that enters the
prim-lwe
reduction is

\inf_{n\geq 1}c_{n}(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}(p)=c(p).

Thus
c(p)
is not the exact finite-dimensional density but the limiting, dimension-uniform reduction constant.

In the matrix form of decision-LWE, given

\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{w\times n}
, one must distinguish

(\mathbf{A},\,\mathbf{A}\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{E})

from uniform over

\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{w\times n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{w\times n}
, where

\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n\times n}
is the secret matrix and

\mathbf{E}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{w\times n}
is the error matrix
[
7
]
.
Via the identification
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
for prime
q
,
decision-
prim-lwe

[
1
]
is the variant in which the secret
matrix is additionally required to lie in
M_{n}^{\mathrm{prim}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

In
[
1
, Remark 3(ii)]
, Sehrawat, Yeo, and Desmedt observed that if
there exist infinitely many primorial primes

p=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}+1,

then

\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}\longrightarrow 0

by Mertens’ theorem, which would imply
\inf_{p}c(p)=0
. They left open
whether this can be proved unconditionally; see
[
8
]
for computational results on primorial primes.

3
Unconditional Proof that
\inf_{p}c(p)=0

The argument requires only two classical results.

Theorem 1

(Dirichlet
[
9
]
, 1837)
.

For coprime integers
a
and
q
, there are infinitely many primes
p\equiv a\pmod{q}
.

Theorem 2

(Mertens’ Third Theorem
[
10
]
, 1874)
.

As
x\to\infty
,

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)\;\sim\;\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log x},

where
\gamma\approx 0.5772
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

We also record the following observation.

Lemma 1

(Lower bound for the matrix-count Euler product)
.

For all primes
p\geq 3
,

\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{j}}\Bigr)\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

For each
J\geq 1
, set
a_{j}:=p^{-j}
. Then
0<a_{j}\leq 1
for all
j
, so
the finite-product inequality

\prod_{j=1}^{J}(1-a_{j})\geq 1-\sum_{j=1}^{J}a_{j}

gives

\prod_{j=1}^{J}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{j}}\Bigr)\;\geq\;1-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{p^{j}}.

Letting
J\to\infty
yields

\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{j}}\Bigr)\;\geq\;1-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j}}=1-\frac{1}{p-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\qquad(p\geq 3).

∎

Theorem 3

(Unconditional vanishing of the infimal PRIM-LWE density)
.

\inf_{p\textup{ prime}}c(p)=0,\qquad\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\to\infty\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c(p)=0.

Proof.

For each
k\geq 1
define the
k
th
primorial

N_{k}\;=\;\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}.

Since
\gcd(1,N_{k})=1
, Theorem
1
guarantees infinitely many primes in the arithmetic progression
\{1+j\,N_{k}:j\geq 1\}
. Fix any such prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{N_{k}}
, so that
N_{k}\mid(p-1)
.

Because every prime
q\leq p_{k}
divides
p-1
, the multiplicative formula for the totient gives

\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}\;=\;\prod_{q\mid(p-1)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)\;\leq\;\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr),

where the inequality holds because each factor
\bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\bigr)
lies in
(0,1)
, so the full product over all prime divisors
q\mid(p-1)
is no larger than the partial product over the subset
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
.

By Mertens’ third theorem (Theorem
2
),

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\;\sim\;\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_{k}}\;\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\;0.

Since the infinite product satisfies
\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-p^{-j})\leq 1
for every prime
p
, we obtain

c(p)\;=\;\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}\;\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{j}}\Bigr)\;\leq\;\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\;\sim\;\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_{k}}\;\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\;0.

Since for every
k
there are infinitely many primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{N_{k}}
, the displayed upper bound holds for arbitrarily large primes
p
. Letting
k\to\infty
gives

\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\to\infty\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c(p)=0,

and the infimum statement follows immediately.
∎

4
Quantitative Refinements

We first record the form of Linnik’s theorem used in the proof.

Theorem 4

(Least prime in an arithmetic progression; Linnik
[
11
]
)
.

There exist absolute constants
C_{0},L>0
such that for every modulus

q\geq 1
and every residue class
a\pmod{q}
with
\gcd(a,q)=1
,
the least prime
p(a,q)\equiv a\pmod{q}
satisfies

p(a,q)\leq C_{0}q^{L}.

Let
N_{k}:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}
denote the
k
-th primorial,
\vartheta(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x,\;p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\log p
the Chebyshev function, and
m(x):=\max\{k\geq 1:N_{k}\leq x\}
.

Lemma 2

(Prime-number-theorem consequences for primorials; cf.
[
12
]
)
.

As
k\to\infty
,

\log N_{k}=\vartheta(p_{k})\sim p_{k},\qquad p_{k+1}\sim p_{k}.

As
x\to\infty
,

\log N_{m(x)}\sim\log x,\qquad p_{m(x)}\sim\log x.

Proof.

The first two assertions are standard consequences of the prime number theorem.
Since

N_{m(x)}\leq x<N_{m(x)+1}=N_{m(x)}\,p_{m(x)+1},

we have

\log N_{m(x)}\leq\log x<\log N_{m(x)}+\log p_{m(x)+1}.

Now

\log p_{m(x)+1}=o\!\bigl(p_{m(x)}\bigr)=o\!\bigl(\log N_{m(x)}\bigr),

because
\log N_{m(x)}=\vartheta(p_{m(x)})\sim p_{m(x)}
and

p_{m(x)+1}\sim p_{m(x)}
. Hence
\log x\sim\log N_{m(x)}
, and therefore

p_{m(x)}\sim\log x
.
∎

Proposition 1

(Upper bound for the minimum density up to
x
)
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that for all sufficiently large
x
,

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c(p)\;\leq\;\frac{C}{\log\log x}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4
, fix absolute constants
C_{0},L>0
such that the
least prime in any reduced residue class modulo
q
is at most
C_{0}q^{L}
.
For each
k\geq 1
, let
p^{*}(k)
denote the least prime satisfying

p^{*}(k)\equiv 1\pmod{N_{k}}
. Given
x
, let
k
be the largest positive
integer such that

C_{0}N_{k}^{\,L}\leq x.

Then

p^{*}(k)\leq C_{0}N_{k}^{\,L}\leq x.

Such a
k
exists for all sufficiently large
x
, and
k\to\infty
as

x\to\infty
.

Since
\log N_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log p_{i}\sim p_{k}
by the prime number theorem, the
inequality
N_{k}^{\,L}\leq x
gives

L\,\log N_{k}\leq\log x,

and hence
p_{k}\ll\log x
. Therefore
\log p_{k}=O(\log\log x)
.

Conversely, the maximality of
k
forces
C_{0}N_{k+1}^{\,L}>x
, so

L\log N_{k+1}>\log x-\log C_{0}.

Since
\log N_{k+1}=\log N_{k}+\log p_{k+1}
, while

\log N_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log p_{i}\sim p_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\log p_{k+1}\sim\log p_{k}=o(p_{k})=o(\log N_{k}),

we have
\log N_{k+1}\sim\log N_{k}
. Hence
\log N_{k}\gg\log x
, so

p_{k}\gg\log x
and therefore
\log p_{k}=\Omega(\log\log x)
.
Combined with the upper bound just proved, this yields

\log p_{k}\asymp\log\log x.

By construction the least prime
p^{*}(k)\equiv 1\pmod{N_{k}}
satisfies
p^{*}(k)\leq x
, and the argument in the proof of Theorem
3
gives

c\bigl(p^{*}(k)\bigr)\;\leq\;\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\;\sim\;\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_{k}}\;\asymp\;\frac{1}{\log\log x}.

Therefore

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c(p)\;\leq\;c\bigl(p^{*}(k)\bigr)\;\ll\;\frac{1}{\log\log x},

as claimed.
∎

5.1
The distribution of
\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)

One can bypass the general Erdős–Wintner machinery and use the following direct

p-1
-specific input from Deshouillers and Hassani.

Lemma 3

(Erdős–Wintner hypotheses for the totient function)
.

The function
f
defined in (
1
) is strongly additive, with

f(n)=\log(n/\varphi(n))=-\log(\varphi(n)/n)
.
For every prime
\ell
,

0<f(\ell)=\log\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}\leq\log 2<1,\qquad f(\ell)=\frac{1}{\ell}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right).

The three Erdős–Wintner convergence conditions are satisfied, as is the continuity criterion of Lemma
6
. The same conclusions hold for
g(n)=\varphi(n)/n
.

Proof.

Since
|f(\ell)|\leq\log 2<1
for every prime
\ell
, the series
\sum_{|f(\ell)|>1}1/\ell
is vacuous. The estimate
f(\ell)=1/\ell+O(1/\ell^{2})
gives

\sum_{\ell}f(\ell)/\ell<\infty
and
\sum_{\ell}f(\ell)^{2}/\ell<\infty
.
Since
f(\ell)\neq 0
for every prime
\ell
and
\sum_{\ell}1/\ell=\infty
, the continuity criterion of Lemma
6
is satisfied. The case
g(n)=\varphi(n)/n
follows from
\log g(\ell)=-f(\ell)
.
∎

Proposition 2

(Deshouillers–Hassani; special case of
[
4
, Proposition 2.1 and equation (2.1)]
)
.

For each integer
m\geq 1
, there exists a continuous distribution function

G_{m}\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
such that for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x;m,1)}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime},\ p\equiv 1\pmod{m},\;\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}\leq\alpha\right\}=G_{m}(\alpha).

Moreover, the limiting measure is supported on
[0,\varphi(m)/m]
.

Proof.

Lemma 4

(Density of even totient ratios)
.

The set

\left\{\frac{\varphi(m)}{m}:m\geq 1\text{ even}\right\}

is dense in
\left[0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right]
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi(m)/m
depends only on the distinct prime divisors of
m
,
we have

\left\{\frac{\varphi(m)}{m}:m\geq 1\text{ even}\right\}=\left\{\frac{1}{2}\prod_{p\in S}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right):S\subset\{\text{odd primes}\},\ S\text{ finite}\right\}.

Enumerate the odd primes as
r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots
, and set

a_{j}:=\log\frac{r_{j}}{r_{j}-1}.

Then
a_{j}>0
,
a_{j}\downarrow 0
, and

a_{j}=\frac{1}{r_{j}}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{r_{j}^{2}}\right).

Since
\sum_{j}1/r_{j}
over the odd primes diverges, it follows that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}=+\infty.

We claim that the set of finite subsums

\left\{\sum_{j\in J}a_{j}:J\subset\mathbb{N}\ \text{finite}\right\}

is dense in
[0,\infty)
. Indeed, fix
y\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
. Choose

J_{0}
so large that
a_{j}<\varepsilon
for all
j\geq J_{0}
. Since

\sum_{j\geq J_{0}}a_{j}=\infty
, there exists
N\geq J_{0}
minimal such that

\sum_{j=J_{0}}^{N}a_{j}>y.

By minimality,

\sum_{j=J_{0}}^{N-1}a_{j}\leq y<\sum_{j=J_{0}}^{N}a_{j}=\sum_{j=J_{0}}^{N-1}a_{j}+a_{N}.

Because
a_{N}<\varepsilon
, it follows that

y-\varepsilon<\sum_{j=J_{0}}^{N-1}a_{j}\leq y.

Thus a finite subsum lies within
\varepsilon
of
y
, proving the claim.

Exponentiating the negative of these finite subsums therefore shows that

\left\{\frac{1}{2}\exp\!\left(-\sum_{j\in J}a_{j}\right):J\subset\mathbb{N}\ \text{finite}\right\}=\left\{\frac{1}{2}\prod_{j\in J}\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{j}}\right):J\subset\mathbb{N}\ \text{finite}\right\}

is dense in
(0,1/2]
. Since the finite subsums can be arbitrarily large,

0
is also a limit point. Hence the displayed set is dense in

[0,1/2]
, as claimed.
∎

Theorem 6

(Continuous limiting distribution and full support of the totient ratio over primes)
.

There exists a continuous distribution function
G\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
such that for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\;\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\,\#\bigl\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime},\;\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)\leq\alpha\bigr\}\;=\;G(\alpha).

[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
, with

for
\alpha\leq 0
and
G(\alpha)=1
for

\alpha\geq 1/2
. In particular,
G
is strictly increasing on

[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
2
with
m=2
. Since every odd prime
satisfies

, we have

\pi(x;2,1)=\pi(x)-1\sim\pi(x),

so the distribution function
G_{2}
furnished by that proposition is
the unconditional limiting distribution of
\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)
over
all primes. In particular,
G_{2}
coincides with the distribution

F
in
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
. Writing

there exists a continuous distribution function

such that for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\bigl\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime},\;\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)\leq\alpha\bigr\}=G(\alpha).

For every odd prime
p
, the integer
p-1
is even, so

0<\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

The exceptional prime
p=2
is negligible in the limit. Hence the limiting

, and therefore

G(\alpha)=0\qquad(\alpha\leq 0),\qquad G(\alpha)=1\qquad(\alpha\geq 1/2).

Let
\mu_{\varphi}
denote the limiting measure with distribution function

G
. Fix an even integer
m\geq 2
, and write

x_{m}:=\frac{\varphi(m)}{m}.

By Deshouillers and Hassani
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
, the

G
is not left-differentiable at

.
Deshouillers and Hassani denote this distribution function by
F
;
we write it as
G
. If

, then some open interval

containing

has

. Hence

is locally

By Lemma

, the support contains a dense subset of

Now let

. Choose any

Lemma 5

(Transport lemma)
.

Let
X(p)\geq 0
and
a(p)>0
be functions of the primes. Assume that

X(p)
has a continuous limiting distribution function
G
over the primes, i.e.

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\bigl\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime},\ X(p)\leq\alpha\bigr\}=G(\alpha)\qquad(\alpha\in\mathbb{R}).

If

the same limiting distribution

.

Proof.

For
\alpha<0
there is nothing to prove, since both distribution functions

there exists

For such primes

Hence

\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime},\ X(p)\leq\frac{\alpha}{1+\varepsilon}\right\}\leq\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\bigl\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime},\ a(p)X(p)\leq\alpha\bigr\}+\frac{\pi(P_{0})}{\pi(x)},

\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\bigl\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime},\ a(p)X(p)\leq\alpha\bigr\}\leq\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime},\ X(p)\leq\frac{\alpha}{1-\varepsilon}\right\}+\frac{\pi(P_{0})}{\pi(x)}.

gives the claim.

Lemma 6

(Shifted-prime pure-type and continuity criterion)
.

Let
f
be a real strongly additive arithmetical function
satisfying the three convergence conditions of the shifted-prime
Erdős–Wintner theorem, so that
f(p-1)
possesses a
limiting distribution
\mu_{f}
over the primes. Then
\mu_{f}
is
of pure type: it is either purely discrete, purely singular, or
absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure. Moreover,

\mu_{f}
is continuous (i.e. has no atoms) if and only if

\sum_{f(\ell)\neq 0}1/\ell=\infty
.

Proof.

The relevant shifted-prime Erdős–Wintner framework is
due to Kátai
[
14
]
and Hildebrand
[
15
]

for the shift
a=1
; the methods extend to any fixed
nonzero shift via the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem, and
for the shift
a=-1
used here, the explicit
characteristic function is recorded in
Tenenbaum
[
3
, formula (3), p. 262]
.
The same conclusions hold for general (not necessarily
strongly) additive functions under the Erdős–Wintner
hypotheses, by the identical Jessen–Wintner argument
applied to the full local factors; we state the strongly
additive case since it is all that we require.

By Tenenbaum
[
3
, formula (3), p. 262]
,
the characteristic function of
\mu_{f}
is given by

\widehat{\mu_{f}}(\tau)\;=\;\prod_{p}\!\left(1-\frac{1}{p-1}+\sum_{\nu\geq 1}\frac{e^{i\tau f(p^{\nu})}}{p^{\nu}}\right)

The shift is
p-1
, corresponding to
a=-1
in the
notation of
[
3
, formula (3)]
. Since no prime
divides
-1
, the product runs over all primes.
For the shift
a=-1
, the local factor at
\ell=2

in the characteristic function reduces, when
f

is strongly additive, to

1-\frac{1}{2-1}+\sum_{\nu\geq 1}\frac{e^{i\tau f(2)}}{2^{\nu}}=e^{i\tau f(2)}
,
which is a deterministic translation by
f(2)
.
This factor carries no randomness and does not affect pure
type or continuity; it merely shifts the law.
It therefore suffices to apply the Jessen–Wintner
theorem
[
16
]

(see also Tenenbaum
[
13
, Theorem 2.7(b)]
)
to the infinite convolution over the
odd
primes.
Each odd-prime factor is the characteristic function of a
probability measure supported on a countable set,
so the Jessen–Wintner theorem gives that the resulting
infinite convolution is of pure type.
The continuity criterion follows from the same theorem:
the convolution is continuous if and only if

\sum(1-\sigma_{\ell})=\infty
, where
\sigma_{\ell}
is the
maximal atom of the factor at
\ell
.
Since
f
is strongly additive, the local factor at an
odd prime
\ell
places mass
(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)
at the
origin and mass
1/(\ell-1)
at
f(\ell)
.
When
f(\ell)\neq 0
, these two points are distinct, so

\sigma_{\ell}=(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)
and

1-\sigma_{\ell}=1/(\ell-1)\asymp 1/\ell
.
It follows that
\sum_{\ell\text{ odd}}(1-\sigma_{\ell})
diverges if
and only if
\sum_{f(\ell)\neq 0}1/\ell=\infty
,
since the prime
\ell=2
does not enter the odd-prime convolution.
Since a deterministic translation preserves pure type and
continuity, the same conclusions hold for
\mu_{f}
.
∎

Lemma 7

(Prime density for a prescribed smooth kernel)
.

Let
m
be an even squarefree positive integer with
P^{+}(m)\leq y
. The set
\{p\textup{ prime}:a_{y}(p-1)=m\}
has relative density

\delta_{y}(m)\;=\;\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\
\ell\nmid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}

among the primes, in the sense that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\bigl\{p\leq x:p\textup{ prime},\;a_{y}(p-1)=m\bigr\}\;=\;\delta_{y}(m).

Moreover, writing

C_{y}:=\prod_{3\leq\ell\leq y}(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)\asymp 1/\!\log y
,
we have
\delta_{y}(m)\geq C_{y}/\varphi(m)
.

Proof.

For each odd prime
\ell\leq y
with
\ell\mid m
, the condition

\ell\mid(p-1)
forces
p\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
.
This selects one residue class
out of
\ell-1
reduced classes modulo
\ell
.
For each odd
prime
\ell\leq y
with
\ell\nmid m
, the condition

\ell\nmid(p-1)
excludes the class
p\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
,
leaving
\ell-2
admissible classes out of
\ell-1
. The prime

\ell=2
imposes no constraint since
2\mid(p-1)
for every odd
prime
p
and
2\mid m
by hypothesis. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, these local conditions combine to specify a union of
reduced residue classes modulo the primorial

M_{y}:=\prod_{\ell\leq y,\;\ell\text{ prime}}\ell
.
The finitely many primes
p\leq y
need not satisfy these
residue-class conditions, but they contribute
O_{y}(1)
to
the count and do not affect the relative density.
Since
M_{y}
is fixed for each
y
, the Siegel–Walfisz theorem
(Theorem
5
) gives the stated density.

Since each
m
is even and squarefree,

\varphi(m)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid m\\
\ell>2\end{subarray}}(\ell-1)
,
so the first product in the expression for

equals

\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\log\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\

Lemma 8

(Tail-mean estimate for

)
.

Let
f
be as in (
1

\eta(y):=\sum_{\ell>y,\;\ell\textup{ prime}}f(\ell)/(\ell-1)\ll 1/y

Proof.

Since
f(\ell)=1/\ell+O(1/\ell^{2})

\eta(y)=\sum_{\ell>y}1/(\ell(\ell-1))+O(\sum_{\ell>y}1/\ell^{3})\ll 1/y

Interchanging sums gives

\sum_{p\leq x}T_{y}(p)\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y<\ell<x\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}f(\ell)\,\pi(x;\ell,1).

Fix
A:=2
and split the sum at the threshold

L:=(\log x)^{A}

Small primes (
y<\ell\leq L
).

Each such
\ell
satisfies

\pi(x;\ell,1)\;=\;\frac{\operatorname{Li}(x)}{\ell-1}\;+\;E(x;\ell,1),\qquad|E(x;\ell,1)|\;\ll_{A}\;xe^{-c_{A}\sqrt{\log x}}.

Since
f(\ell)\leq 1
for every prime
\ell

the total error is

Large primes (

\ell\leq x

m_{j}\leq Cj
for all
j\geq 1
.
In particular
m_{R}\leq CR
. Therefore

P^{+}(m_{j})\leq m_{j}\leq Cy^{\delta}<y
for all
sufficiently large
y
(since
\delta<1
).
Moreover,

\sum_{j=1}^{R}\frac{1}{\varphi(m_{j})}\;\geq\;\sum_{j=1}^{R}\frac{1}{m_{j}}\;\geq\;\frac{1}{C}\sum_{j=1}^{R}\frac{1}{j}\;\gg\;\log R\;=\;\delta\log y+O(1).

(3)

By Lemma
7
, the relative density of

\{p:a_{y}(p-1)=m_{j}\}
among the primes is

\delta_{y}(m_{j})\geq C_{y}/\varphi(m_{j})
, where

C_{y}\asymp 1/\!\log y
.
Combining with (
3
),

\sum_{j=1}^{R}\delta_{y}(m_{j})\;\geq\;C_{y}\sum_{j=1}^{R}\frac{1}{\varphi(m_{j})}\;\gg\;\frac{\delta\log y}{\log y}\;=\;\delta\;>\;0.

(4)

Since the
m_{j}
are distinct, the sets

\{p:a_{y}(p-1)=m_{j}\}
are pairwise disjoint, so the
relative density of their union

\mathcal{A}_{y}:=\{p:a_{y}(p-1)\in\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{R}\}\}

is the sum
\sum_{j}\delta_{y}(m_{j})
.
In particular, there exists an absolute constant

c_{0}>0
(depending only on
\delta
)
such that for all sufficiently large
y
,
the relative density of
\mathcal{A}_{y}

among the primes is at least
c_{0}
.

Define the closed set

\bar{I}_{y}\;:=\;\bigcup_{j=1}^{R}\bigl[\,f(m_{j}),\;f(m_{j})+1/R^{2}\,\bigr].

By subadditivity of Lebesgue measure,

\lambda(\bar{I}_{y})\leq R\cdot(1/R^{2})=1/R=1/\lfloor y^{\delta}\rfloor\to 0
as
y\to\infty
.
For any prime
p
with
a_{y}(p-1)=m_{j}

and
T_{y}(p)\leq 1/R^{2}
, the identity

f(p-1)=f(a_{y}(p-1))+T_{y}(p)
and the nonnegativity
of
T_{y}(p)
give

f(p-1)\in[f(m_{j}),\,f(m_{j})+1/R^{2}]\subset\bar{I}_{y}
.

Step 3: Contradiction with
absolute continuity.

Recall that
y
(and hence
R
) are still fixed.
By (
2
),

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\;\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\bigl\{p\leq x:T_{y}(p)>1/R^{2}\bigr\}\;=\;o_{y}(1).

Hence, for all sufficiently large
y
,

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\bigl\{p\leq x:f(p-1)\in\bar{I}_{y}\bigr\}\;\geq\;c_{0}-o_{y}(1)\;\geq\;\frac{c_{0}}{2}\;>\;0.

Since
\mu_{f}
is continuous, the boundary of
\bar{I}_{y}

(a finite union of
\{
endpoints
\}
, hence countable)
has
\mu_{f}
-measure zero.
Therefore
\bar{I}_{y}
is a
\mu_{f}
-continuity set,
and weak convergence gives

\mu_{f}(\bar{I}_{y})\;=\;\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\bigl\{p\leq x:f(p-1)\in\bar{I}_{y}\bigr\}\;\geq\;\frac{c_{0}}{2}\;>\;0.

If
\mu_{f}
were absolutely continuous with density

h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then by the absolute continuity
of the Lebesgue integral,

\mu_{f}(\bar{I}_{y})=\int_{\bar{I}_{y}}h\,\mathrm{d}t\to 0

as
\lambda(\bar{I}_{y})\to 0
, contradicting

\mu_{f}(\bar{I}_{y})\geq c_{0}/2
for all large
y
.
Therefore
\mu_{f}
is not absolutely continuous, and
being of pure type and continuous, it is purely
singular.
∎

Proposition 3

(Shifted-prime limiting law for
f
)
.

Let
f
be as in (
1
).
Then the empirical measures

\mu_{x}:=\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\delta_{f(p-1)}

converge weakly, as
x\to\infty
, to a Borel probability measure
\mu_{f}
on
[0,\infty)
that is continuous and purely singular with respect to Lebesgue measure.

Proof.

By Lemma
3
, the three convergence conditions of the
shifted-prime Erdős–Wintner theorem
(Kátai
[
14
]
, Hildebrand
[
15
]

for
p+1
; Tenenbaum
[
3
, pp. 262–263]
for
the general shift including
p-1
) are satisfied, and the
continuity criterion holds. Therefore
f(p-1)
possesses a
continuous limiting distribution
\mu_{f}
, which is of pure type
by Lemma
6
.

It remains to rule out absolute continuity.
By Lemma
9
,
\mu_{f}
is purely singular.
∎

Corollary 2

(Structure of the limiting PRIM-LWE law)
.

The density
c(p)
has a continuous, purely singular limiting
distribution with support exactly
[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
. Its distribution
function
G
is strictly increasing on
[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
.

Proof.

Write

Y(p):=\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}.

By Theorem
7
,
c(p)
has the same limiting law as
Y(p)
.
By Theorem
6
, this common law is continuous, has support exactly

[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
, and its distribution function is strictly increasing on

[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
. It remains only to justify pure singularity.

Let
f
be as in (
1
). Then
f
is strongly additive and

f(\ell)=\log\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}\sim\frac{1}{\ell}\qquad(\ell\to\infty,\ \ell\ \text{prime}),

while

Y(p)=e^{-f(p-1)}.

By Proposition
3
, the empirical measures of
f(p-1)
converge
weakly to a continuous purely singular law
\mu_{f}
. Since

T(u):=e^{-u}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, the continuous mapping theorem
implies that

Y(p)=T(f(p-1))

has limiting law
T_{\#}\mu_{f}
(the push-forward of
\mu_{f}
by
T
, defined by
T_{\#}\mu_{f}(B):=\mu_{f}(T^{-1}(B))
).

Choose a Borel set
E\subset[0,\infty)
with Lebesgue measure zero and

\mu_{f}(E)=1
. The map
T
is strictly monotone and Lipschitz on each
compact interval
[m,m+1]
,
m\geq 0
. Writing

E=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}(E\cap[m,m+1])
, each image
T(E\cap[m,m+1])
has
Lebesgue measure zero (since a Lipschitz map on a compact interval
preserves null sets), and therefore
T(E)
has Lebesgue measure zero
by countable subadditivity. Therefore
T_{\#}\mu_{f}
is supported on the null
set
T(E)
and is therefore purely singular.

By Theorem
7
, the same is true for
c(p)
.
∎

Note that
c(p)<1/2
for every fixed prime
p
. Indeed, for
p=2
one has

\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}=\frac{\varphi(1)}{1}=1,

so

c(2)=\prod_{j\geq 1}\Bigl(1-2^{-j}\Bigr)\approx 0.288788<\frac{1}{2}.

For odd
p
, one has
\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)\leq 1/2
together with

\prod_{j\geq 1}(1-p^{-j})<1
. Thus
1/2
is only a boundary point of the
support of the limiting law for
c(p)
, not an attained value.

Corollary 3

(Unconditional limsup)
.

\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\to\infty\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c(p)=\frac{1}{2}.

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon>0
, the set of primes with

c(p)>1/2-\varepsilon
has relative density
1-G(1/2-\varepsilon)
among the primes.

Proof.

Since
c(p)<1/2
for every prime,
\limsup c(p)\leq 1/2
.
For the reverse inequality, fix
\varepsilon>0
.
By Corollary
2
, the limiting distribution of
c(p)
has
support exactly
[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
, so the limiting measure assigns positive
mass to the interval
(1/2-\varepsilon,\,1/2]
. Hence

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\bigl\{p\leq x:c(p)>1/2-\varepsilon\bigr\}=1-G(1/2-\varepsilon)>0.

In particular, infinitely many primes satisfy
c(p)>1/2-\varepsilon
,
so
\limsup c(p)\geq 1/2-\varepsilon
. Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0

yields the claim.
∎

Corollary 4

(Small-density moduli)
.

For every fixed
\tau\in(0,1/2)
, both sets

\{p:c(p)\leq\tau\}
and
\{p:c(p)>\tau\}

have positive relative density among the primes, equal to

G(\tau)
and
1-G(\tau)
, respectively.

Proof.

By Theorem
7
with
\alpha=\tau
, the limiting density
of
\{p:c(p)\leq\tau\}
is
G(\tau)
. Since
G
is strictly
increasing on
[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
by Theorem
6
, we have

0<G(\tau)<1
, giving positive density for both sets.
∎

Corollary 5

(Fixed-dimension analogue)
.

For each fixed
n\geq 1
, the exact density
c_{n}(p)
has the same
continuous limiting distribution as
\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)
; in
particular, its limiting law has support exactly
[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
.
Moreover,

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c_{n}(p)\;\asymp\;\frac{1}{\log\log x}.

Proof.

Write

c_{n}(p)=\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}\,P_{n}(p),\qquad P_{n}(p):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{j}}\Bigr).

Since
P_{n}(p)\to 1
as
p\to\infty
through the primes, Lemma
5

shows that
c_{n}(p)
has the same limiting law as
\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)
.

Since
c_{n}(2)>0
, it suffices for large
x
to consider odd primes
p\leq x
. Let

m(x):=\max\{k\geq 1:N_{k}\leq x\}.

For each odd prime
p\leq x
, we have

N_{\omega(p-1)}\leq\prod_{r\mid(p-1)}r\leq p-1\leq x,

so
\omega(p-1)\leq m(x)
. Let

r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots<r_{d}

be the distinct prime divisors of
p-1
, where
d=\omega(p-1)
. Then

d\leq m(x)
, and
r_{i}\geq p_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
. Hence

\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r_{i}}\Bigr)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m(x)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr),

because each factor
1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}
lies in
(0,1)
.
Also, by the same elementary estimate used in Lemma
1
,

P_{n}(p)\geq 1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\geq 1-\frac{1}{p-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore

c_{n}(p)\geq\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{m(x)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr).

Now
N_{m(x)}\leq x<N_{m(x)+1}=N_{m(x)}p_{m(x)+1}
, so
\log x\sim\log N_{m(x)}
. Since

\log N_{m(x)}=\sum_{i=1}^{m(x)}\log p_{i}\sim p_{m(x)},

we get
p_{m(x)}\sim\log x
. By Mertens’ theorem,

\prod_{i=1}^{m(x)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\sim\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_{m(x)}}\asymp\frac{1}{\log\log x},

and therefore

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c_{n}(p)\gg\frac{1}{\log\log x}.

For the upper bound, the argument of Proposition
1
applies
verbatim, with the additional observation that
P_{n}(p)\leq 1
for every prime

p
. If
p^{*}(k)\leq x
is the least prime congruent to
1\pmod{N_{k}}

arising there, then

c_{n}\bigl(p^{*}(k)\bigr)=\frac{\varphi(p^{*}(k)-1)}{p^{*}(k)-1}\,P_{n}\bigl(p^{*}(k)\bigr)\leq\frac{\varphi(p^{*}(k)-1)}{p^{*}(k)-1}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\ll\frac{1}{\log\log x}.

Hence

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c_{n}(p)\ll\frac{1}{\log\log x},

which proves the claim.
∎

5.3
Hausdorff dimension, moments, and Fourier structure of
G

We derive a sharper geometric description of the limiting distribution and then its moment and Fourier structure.
Throughout,
f
is the strongly additive function defined in (
1
),

\mu_{f}
is the shifted-prime limiting measure of
f(p-1)
, and

X
is a random variable with distribution
G
.
By Corollary
2
,
X\overset{d}{=}e^{-Y}
where
Y\sim\mu_{f}
.

Lemma 10

(Factor structure of
\mu_{f}
)
.

The measure
\mu_{f}
is the infinite convolution

\mu_{f}\;=\;\delta_{\log 2}\;*\;\mathop{{\ast}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 3\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\nu_{\ell},

where
\delta_{\log 2}
is the point mass at
\log 2
and,
for each odd prime
\ell
,

\nu_{\ell}\;:=\;\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}\,\delta_{0}\;+\;\frac{1}{\ell-1}\,\delta_{f(\ell)},\qquad f(\ell)=\log\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}.

Equivalently,
Y\overset{d}{=}\log 2+\sum_{\ell\geq 3,\;\ell\textup{ prime}}Z_{\ell}

with the
Z_{\ell}
independent,

\mathbb{P}(Z_{\ell}=0)=(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)
,

\mathbb{P}(Z_{\ell}=f(\ell))=1/(\ell-1)
.

Proof.

By Tenenbaum
[
3
, formula (3), p. 262]
for the
shift
a=-1
, the characteristic function of
\mu_{f}
is

\widehat{\mu_{f}}(\tau)=\prod_{\ell\text{ prime}}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\ell-1}+\sum_{\nu\geq 1}\frac{e^{i\tau f(\ell^{\nu})}}{\ell^{\nu}}\Bigr).

Since
f
is strongly additive,

f(\ell^{\nu})=f(\ell)
for all
\nu\geq 1
, whence

\sum_{\nu\geq 1}e^{i\tau f(\ell^{\nu})}\ell^{-\nu}=e^{i\tau f(\ell)}/(\ell-1)
.
At each odd prime
\ell
the local factor equals

\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}+\frac{e^{i\tau f(\ell)}}{\ell-1}=\widehat{\nu_{\ell}}(\tau)
;
at
\ell=2
it equals
e^{i\tau\log 2}=\widehat{\delta_{\log 2}}(\tau)
.
Since the characteristic function determines a probability
measure uniquely, the convolution decomposition follows.
∎

For a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
, recall the Hausdorff dimension of
\mu
:

\dim_{H}(\mu):=\inf\{\dim_{H}(E):E\subset\mathbb{R}\text{ is Borel and }\mu(E)=1\}.

Proposition 4

(Hausdorff dimension zero of the limiting law)
.

\dim_{H}(\mu_{f})=0
.
Consequently, if
\mu_{G}
denotes the limiting measure on
[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]

with distribution function
G
, then
\dim_{H}(\mu_{G})=0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
10
,

Y\overset{d}{=}\log 2+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 3\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}B_{\ell}w_{\ell},\qquad w_{\ell}:=\log\frac{\ell}{\ell-1},

where the
B_{\ell}
are independent Bernoulli random variables with

\mathbb{P}(B_{\ell}=1)=\frac{1}{\ell-1},\qquad\mathbb{P}(B_{\ell}=0)=\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}.

Fix
s>0
. We shall show that
\mu_{f}
gives full mass to a Borel set of vanishing
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

For
y\geq 3
, let

I(y):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(B_{\ell}\log(\ell-1)+(1-B_{\ell})\log\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}\right).

If
\omega=(b_{\ell})_{3\leq\ell\leq y}
is a prefix configuration, then

\mathbb{P}\bigl((B_{\ell})_{3\leq\ell\leq y}=\omega\bigr)=e^{-I_{y}(\omega)},

where
I_{y}(\omega)
denotes the value of
I(y)
at that configuration.
Moreover,

\mathbb{E}I(y)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log\ell}{\ell}\ll\log y

and

\operatorname{Var}(I(y))\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(B_{\ell}\log(\ell-1)+(1-B_{\ell})\log\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}\right)^{2}\right]\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log\ell)^{2}}{\ell}\ll(\log y)^{2}.

Hence there exists an absolute constant
C_{0}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}I(y)\leq C_{0}\log y,\qquad\operatorname{Var}(I(y))\leq C_{0}(\log y)^{2}\qquad(y\geq 3).

Choose
C:=4C_{0}
(so that
Cn-a_{n}s/2\leq-Cn
in the covering estimate below). Next choose an increasing sequence
y_{n}\to\infty
so rapidly that, with

a_{n}:=\left\lceil\frac{4Cn}{s}\right\rceil\qquad(\text{recall }s>0\text{ is fixed}),\qquad z_{n}:=y_{n}^{a_{n}},\qquad r_{n}:=z_{n}^{-1/2},

the intervals
(y_{n},z_{n}]
are pairwise disjoint, the sequence
(z_{n})
is strictly increasing, and

\sum_{n\geq 1}r_{n}<\infty,\qquad\sum_{n\geq 1}y_{n}^{-Cn}<\infty.

For each
n
, set
I_{n}:=I(y_{n})
and

A_{n}:=\{I_{n}\leq Cn\log y_{n}\}.

By Chebyshev’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}(A_{n}^{c})\leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}(I_{n})}{(Cn\log y_{n}-\mathbb{E}I_{n})^{2}}\ll\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Let
T_{n}
be the set of prefix configurations
\omega=(b_{\ell})_{3\leq\ell\leq y_{n}}
satisfying
I_{n}(\omega)\leq Cn\log y_{n}
. Then

\mathbb{P}\bigl((B_{\ell})_{3\leq\ell\leq y_{n}}\in T_{n}\bigr)=1-O(n^{-2}),

and every atom in
T_{n}
has probability at least
y_{n}^{-Cn}
. Since the total mass is at most
1
, it follows that

|T_{n}|\leq y_{n}^{Cn}.

Now define the empty-block event

E_{n}:=\{B_{\ell}=0\text{ for every prime }\ell\in(y_{n},z_{n}]\}.

Because the blocks
(y_{n},z_{n}]
are disjoint, the events
E_{n}
are independent. Moreover,

\mathbb{P}(E_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}y_{n}<\ell\leq z_{n}\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell-1}\right).

Since

1-\frac{1}{\ell-1}=\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{(\ell-1)^{2}}\right),

Mertens’ theorem and the convergence of
\prod_{\ell\geq 3}(1-(\ell-1)^{-2})
imply

\mathbb{P}(E_{n})\asymp\frac{\log y_{n}}{\log z_{n}}=\frac{1}{a_{n}}\asymp\frac{1}{n}.

Therefore
\sum_{n}\mathbb{P}(E_{n})=\infty
, and the second Borel–Cantelli lemma gives

\mathbb{P}(E_{n}\text{ i.o.})=1.

Let

R_{n}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>z_{n}\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}B_{\ell}w_{\ell},\qquad F_{n}:=\{R_{n}\leq r_{n}\}.

Since
w_{\ell}=\frac{1}{\ell}+O(\ell^{-2})
, we have

\mathbb{E}R_{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>z_{n}\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{w_{\ell}}{\ell-1}\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>z_{n}\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\ll\frac{1}{z_{n}}.

By Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}(F_{n}^{c})\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}R_{n}}{r_{n}}\ll z_{n}^{-1/2}=r_{n}.

Thus
\sum_{n}\mathbb{P}(F_{n}^{c})<\infty
, so

\mathbb{P}(F_{n}^{c}\text{ i.o.})=0.

For each
\omega=(b_{\ell})_{3\leq\ell\leq y_{n}}\in T_{n}
, define

c(\omega):=\log 2+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y_{n}\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}b_{\ell}w_{\ell},

and let

U_{n}:=\bigcup_{\omega\in T_{n}}[c(\omega),\,c(\omega)+r_{n}].

If
A_{n}\cap E_{n}\cap F_{n}
occurs, then the block
(y_{n},z_{n}]
contributes nothing and the tail beyond
z_{n}
contributes at most
r_{n}
, so
Y\in U_{n}
. Since

\sum_{n}\mathbb{P}(A_{n}^{c})<\infty,\qquad\sum_{n}\mathbb{P}(F_{n}^{c})<\infty,\qquad\mathbb{P}(E_{n}\text{ i.o.})=1,

it follows that

\mu_{f}\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}\right)=1.

On the other hand,

\sum_{n\geq 1}|T_{n}|\,r_{n}^{s}\ll\sum_{n\geq 1}y_{n}^{Cn}z_{n}^{-s/2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}y_{n}^{Cn-a_{n}s/2}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}y_{n}^{-Cn}<\infty.

Because
r_{n}\downarrow 0
, for every
N\geq 1
the set
\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}
is covered by the intervals comprising
U_{n}
for
n\geq N
, each of diameter at most
r_{N}
. Hence

\mathcal{H}^{s}_{r_{N}}\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}\right)\leq\sum_{n\geq N}|T_{n}|\,r_{n}^{s}.

Letting
N\to\infty
gives

\mathcal{H}^{s}\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}\right)=0.

Thus
\mu_{f}
assigns full mass to a Borel set of zero
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and therefore
\dim_{H}(\mu_{f})\leq s
. Since
s>0
was arbitrary,
\dim_{H}(\mu_{f})=0
.

Finally,
\mu_{G}
is the push-forward of
\mu_{f}
under the map
T(u):=e^{-u}
. Since
T
is Lipschitz on
[\log 2,\infty)
, the image of any set of Hausdorff dimension
0
again has Hausdorff dimension
0
. Therefore
\dim_{H}(\mu_{G})=0
.
∎

Theorem 8

(Moment Euler product)
.

For every real
k>0
,

\mathbb{E}[X^{k}]\;=\;2^{-k}\!\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 3\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\!\left(\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}+\frac{1}{\ell-1}\Bigl(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\Bigr)^{\!k}\right).

(5)

The product converges absolutely, with the
\ell
-th factor
equal to
1+O(k/\ell^{2})
.

Proof.

Since
Y=\log 2+\sum_{\ell}Z_{\ell}
with the
Z_{\ell}\geq 0

independent (Lemma
10
), the partial sums

S_{L}:=\sum_{3\leq\ell\leq L}Z_{\ell}\nearrow S:=\sum_{\ell}Z_{\ell}

give
e^{-kS_{L}}\searrow e^{-kS}
.
Since
0\leq e^{-kS_{L}}\leq 1
for all
L
, by dominated convergence,

\mathbb{E}[e^{-kS}]=\prod_{\ell\geq 3}\mathbb{E}[e^{-kZ_{\ell}}]=\prod_{\ell\geq 3}\Bigl(\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}+\frac{1}{\ell-1}\Bigl(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\Bigr)^{\!k}\Bigr).

Since
\mathbb{E}[X^{k}]=2^{-k}\,\mathbb{E}[e^{-kS}]
,
this gives (
5
).

Writing the
\ell
-th factor as
1+a_{\ell}
with

a_{\ell}=\bigl((\frac{\ell-1}{\ell})^{k}-1\bigr)/(\ell-1)=(e^{-kw_{\ell}}-1)/(\ell-1)
,
where
w_{\ell}:=\log(\ell/(\ell-1))=1/\ell+O(1/\ell^{2})
.
The bound

with

|a_{\ell}|\leq kw_{\ell}\,e^{kw_{\ell}}/(\ell-1)
.
Since

for all

Since

Corollary 6

(First and second moments)
.

(i)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X]=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 3\\

(ii)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]=\frac{1}{4}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 3\\

.

(i) Set

.

(ii) Set

\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}+\frac{(\ell-1)}{\ell^{2}}=\frac{(\ell-2)\ell^{2}+(\ell-1)^{2}}{\ell^{2}(\ell-1)}=\frac{\ell^{3}-\ell^{2}-2\ell+1}{\ell^{2}(\ell-1)}

(Verification at

∎

Theorem 9

(Moment determinacy)
.

The moment sequence
\{\mathbb{E}[X^{k}]\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

uniquely determines
G
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{supp}(G)=[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]

(Theorem

),
the random variable
X

[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
. By the Weierstrass approximation

Theorem 10

(Analytic continuation)
.

The function

M(s)\;:=\;\mathbb{E}[X^{s}]\;=\;2^{-s}\!\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 3\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}F_{s}(\ell),\qquad F_{s}(\ell):=\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}+\frac{1}{\ell-1}\Bigl(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\Bigr)^{\!s},

extends to an analytic non-vanishing function on

\{s\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}(s)>0\}
,
with the Euler product converging uniformly on compact subsets.

Proof.

We first establish the product formula for complex
s

with
\sigma:=\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
.
By Lemma
10
,
Y=\log 2+\sum_{\ell\geq 3}Z_{\ell}

with independent
Z_{\ell}\geq 0
, and the series converges
almost surely since
\sum_{\ell}\mathbb{E}[Z_{\ell}]<\infty
.
Set
S_{L}:=\sum_{3\leq\ell\leq L}Z_{\ell}
.
Since
Z_{\ell}\geq 0
and
\sigma>0
,

|e^{-sS_{L}}|=e^{-\sigma S_{L}}\leq 1

for every
L
. As
S_{L}\to S:=\sum_{\ell}Z_{\ell}
a.s.,
dominated convergence gives

\mathbb{E}[e^{-sS_{L}}]\to\mathbb{E}[e^{-sS}]
.
Independence factors the finite partial products:

\mathbb{E}[e^{-sS_{L}}]=\prod_{3\leq\ell\leq L}\mathbb{E}[e^{-sZ_{\ell}}]=\prod_{3\leq\ell\leq L}F_{s}(\ell)
.
Therefore

\mathbb{E}[X^{s}]=2^{-s}\,\mathbb{E}[e^{-sS}]=2^{-s}\prod_{\ell\geq 3}F_{s}(\ell)
,
extending the real-variable identity of
Theorem
8
to the half-plane

\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
.

It remains to verify analyticity and non-vanishing.
Write
w_{\ell}:=\log(\ell/(\ell-1))
and

F_{s}(\ell)=1+a_{\ell}(s)
with

a_{\ell}(s)=(e^{-sw_{\ell}}-1)/(\ell-1)
.
The bound
|e^{z}-1|\leq|z|e^{|z|}
gives

|a_{\ell}(s)|\leq|s|\,w_{\ell}\,e^{|s|w_{\ell}}/(\ell-1)
.
Since
w_{\ell}=1/\ell+O(1/\ell^{2})
, on any compact

K\subset\{\operatorname{Re}(s)>0\}
with
|s|\leq R
we get

|a_{\ell}(s)|\leq C_{K}/\ell^{2}
for all large
\ell
.
Since
\sum\ell^{-2}<\infty
,
Weierstrass’s theorem on products of analytic
functions (see e.g.
[
18
, Ch. VII]
)
gives that the product converges uniformly on compact subsets
to an analytic function. As
2^{-s}
is entire,

M(s)
is analytic on
\{\operatorname{Re}(s)>0\}
.

For non-vanishing with
\sigma:=\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
:
each factor
F_{s}(\ell)\neq 0
since

|F_{s}(\ell)|\geq\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}-\frac{e^{-\sigma w_{\ell}}}{\ell-1}

and
e^{-\sigma w_{\ell}}<1
: at
\ell=3
,

|F_{s}(3)|\geq(1-(2/3)^{\sigma})/2>0
;
at
\ell\geq 5
,

|F_{s}(\ell)|\geq(\ell-3)/(\ell-1)>0
.
To conclude that the infinite product is nonzero, fix a
compact
K\subset\{\operatorname{Re}(s)>0\}
.
Choose
L
so that
|a_{\ell}(s)|<1/2
for all
\ell\geq L

and
s\in K
(possible since
|a_{\ell}(s)|\leq C_{K}/\ell^{2}
).
For
\ell\geq L
the principal branch

\log(1+a_{\ell}(s))
is analytic, and

|\!\log(1+a_{\ell}(s))|\leq 2|a_{\ell}(s)|\leq 2C_{K}/\ell^{2}
,
so
\sum_{\ell\geq L}\log(1+a_{\ell}(s))
converges uniformly
on
K
and the tail product equals

\exp\!\bigl(\sum_{\ell\geq L}\log(1+a_{\ell}(s))\bigr)\neq 0
.
The finitely many factors with
\ell<L
are each nonzero,
so
M(s)\neq 0
.
∎

Proposition 5

(Squared modulus of the characteristic function)
.

For every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,

\bigl|\widehat{\mu_{f}}(\tau)\bigr|^{2}\;=\;\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 3\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\!\left(1-\frac{2(\ell-2)}{(\ell-1)^{2}}\bigl(1-\cos(\tau\log\tfrac{\ell}{\ell-1})\bigr)\right).

(6)

Proof.

By Lemma
10
,

|\widehat{\mu_{f}}(\tau)|^{2}=\prod_{\ell\geq 3}|\widehat{\nu_{\ell}}(\tau)|^{2}
.
With
\alpha=\tau\log(\ell/(\ell-1))
and

\widehat{\nu_{\ell}}(\tau)=\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}+\frac{e^{i\alpha}}{\ell-1}
,

|\widehat{\nu_{\ell}}(\tau)|^{2}=\frac{(\ell-2)^{2}+2(\ell-2)\cos\alpha+1}{(\ell-1)^{2}}=1-\frac{2(\ell-2)}{(\ell-1)^{2}}(1-\cos\alpha),

using

∎

Proposition 6

(Fourier non-decay implies singularity)
.

If
\limsup_{|\tau|\to\infty}|\widehat{\mu_{f}}(\tau)|>0
,
then
\mu_{f}
is purely singular, and consequently
G
is purely singular.

Proof.

By Lemma
6
,
\mu_{f}
is continuous and
of pure type: either absolutely continuous or purely singular.
The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma gives: if
\mu_{f}
is absolutely
continuous (
\mu_{f}=h\,\mathrm{d}t
,
h\in L^{1}
), then

\widehat{\mu_{f}}(\tau)=\widehat{h}(\tau)\to 0
.
Contrapositively,
\limsup|\widehat{\mu_{f}}(\tau)|>0

implies
\mu_{f}
is not absolutely continuous, hence
purely singular.
For the conclusion about
G
: by Corollary
2
,

X\overset{d}{=}e^{-Y}
where
Y\sim\mu_{f}
, and
G
is the
distribution function of
X
. The map
T(u)=e^{-u}
is
locally Lipschitz on

, so the push-forward

T_{\#}\mu_{f}
preserves Lebesgue-null sets. Hence pure

\mu_{f}
implies pure singularity of

(cf. the proof of Corollary
2
).
∎

5.4
Endpoint asymptotics at
1/2

Proposition 7

(Endpoint asymptotic at
1/2
)
.

Let
G
be the limiting distribution function from
Theorem
6
, and let
X
be a random variable with
distribution
G
. Define

\kappa:=2e^{-\gamma}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 3\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{(\ell-1)^{2}}\Bigr).

Then, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

1-G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(X>\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\sim\frac{\kappa}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}.

Proof.

By Lemma
10
,

X\overset{d}{=}\frac{1}{2}e^{-S},\qquad S:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 3\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}Z_{\ell},

where the
Z_{\ell}
are independent and satisfy

\mathbb{P}(Z_{\ell}=0)=\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1},\qquad\mathbb{P}\!\left(Z_{\ell}=\log\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}\right)=\frac{1}{\ell-1}.

Set

M(y):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}.

For
u>0
one has

\frac{u}{1+u}<\log(1+u)\leq u.

Applying this with
u=1/(\ell-1)
gives

\frac{1}{\ell}<\log\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}\leq\frac{1}{\ell-1}.

Hence, if
\ell\leq(2t)^{-1}
, then

Z_{\ell}\neq 0\;\Longrightarrow\;Z_{\ell}>\frac{1}{\ell}\geq 2t>t,

so the event
\{S\leq t\}
forces
Z_{\ell}=0
for every odd prime

\ell\leq(2t)^{-1}
. Therefore

\mathbb{P}(S\leq t)\leq M\!\bigl((2t)^{-1}\bigr).

(7)

For the lower bound, set

z_{t}:=\frac{\log(1/t)}{t}\qquad(0<t<e^{-1}).

By independence,

\mathbb{P}(S\leq t)\geq M(z_{t})\,\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>z_{t}\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}Z_{\ell}\leq t\right).

(8)

Since
Z_{\ell}\leq 1/(\ell-1)
almost surely,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>z_{t}\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}Z_{\ell}\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>z_{t}\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell-1}\log\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>z_{t}\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(\ell-1)^{2}}\ll\frac{1}{z_{t}}=\frac{t}{\log(1/t)}.

Thus Markov’s inequality gives

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>z_{t}\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}Z_{\ell}>t\right)\ll\frac{1}{\log(1/t)}=o(1),

and (
8
) becomes

\mathbb{P}(S\leq t)\geq M(z_{t})\,(1-o(1)).

(9)

Now

M(y)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\ell-1)^{2}}\right).

The second product converges to

\prod_{\ell\geq 3}(1-(\ell-1)^{-2})
, while
Theorem
2
gives

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq\ell\leq y\\
\ell\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\sim\frac{2e^{-\gamma}}{\log y}.

Hence

M(y)\sim\frac{\kappa}{\log y}\qquad(y\to\infty).

Since
\log((2t)^{-1})\sim\log z_{t}\sim\log(1/t)
as
t\downarrow 0
,
(
7
) and (
9
) imply

\mathbb{P}(S\leq t)\sim\frac{\kappa}{\log(1/t)}\qquad(t\downarrow 0).

Since
\mu_{f}
is continuous by Proposition
3
, the law of

S=Y-\log 2
is also continuous, so
\mathbb{P}(S<t)=\mathbb{P}(S\leq t)
.
Therefore

1-G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(X>\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(S<-\log(1-2\varepsilon)\right).

As
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

-\log(1-2\varepsilon)=2\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

so
\log(1/(-\log(1-2\varepsilon)))\sim\log(1/\varepsilon)
. The stated
asymptotic follows.
∎

Corollary 7

(Endpoint modulus at
1/2
)
.

As
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)-G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)=1-G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\asymp\frac{1}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}.

In particular, for every
\alpha>0
, one does not have

G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)-G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)=O(\varepsilon^{\alpha})\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow 0),

so
G
is not Hölder of any positive order at the endpoint
1/2
.

Proof.

The two-sided order follows immediately from
Proposition
7
. If for some
\alpha>0
one had

G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)-G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)=O(\varepsilon^{\alpha}),

then Proposition
7
would force

\frac{1}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}=O(\varepsilon^{\alpha}),

which is impossible because

\varepsilon^{\alpha}\log(1/\varepsilon)\to 0\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow 0).

∎

6
Explicit Lower Bounds for Primes of Cryptographic Interest

We now give explicit lower bounds on
c(q)
for primes
q
whose predecessor
q-1
has few distinct prime factors—a condition satisfied by many primes commonly used in lattice-based cryptography, especially Number Theoretic Transform (NTT)-friendly ones.

Theorem 11

(Explicit lower bound on
c(q)
via the number of distinct prime factors)
.

Let
q\geq 3
be a prime, and let
\omega=\omega(q-1)
denote the number
of distinct prime factors of
q-1
. Then

c(q)\;\geq\;P_{3}\prod_{i=1}^{\omega}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr),\qquad P_{3}:=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{3^{j}}\Bigr)\approx 0.5601260779,

where
p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{\omega}
are the first
\omega
primes.

Proof.

Write the distinct prime factors of
q-1
as
r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots<r_{\omega}
. Then

\frac{\varphi(q-1)}{q-1}=\prod_{i=1}^{\omega}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r_{i}}\Bigr)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{\omega}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr),

since the product is minimized by choosing the smallest possible distinct
primes.

Now define

P(t):=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{t^{j}}\Bigr)\qquad(t>1).

Each factor
1-t^{-j}
is increasing in
t
, so
P(t)
is increasing on

(1,\infty)
. Since
q\geq 3
is prime, we have
P(q)\geq P(3)=P_{3}
. Therefore

c(q)=\frac{\varphi(q-1)}{q-1}\,P(q)\geq P_{3}\prod_{i=1}^{\omega}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr),

as claimed.
∎

Corollary 8

(Matching lower bound)
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that for all sufficiently large
x
,

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c(p)\;\geq\;\frac{c}{\log\log x}.

Consequently,

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c(p)\;\asymp\;\frac{1}{\log\log x}.

Proof.

The lower bound follows by the same primorial/Mertens argument used in
the proof of Corollary
5
: for every odd prime
p\leq x
,

\omega(p-1)\leq m(x)
, and Lemma
1
together with
Mertens’ theorem gives

\min_{p\leq x,\,p\textup{ prime}}c(p)\gg 1/\!\log\log x
.
Theorem
11
sharpens the constant from
\tfrac{1}{2}

to
P_{3}
but does not affect the asymptotic order.
The matching upper bound is Proposition
1
.
∎

Corollary 9

(Pointwise universal lower bound)
.

Let

m(n):=\max\{k\geq 1:N_{k}\leq n\}.

Then for every prime
q\geq 3
,

c(q)\;\geq\;P_{3}\prod_{i=1}^{m(q-1)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr).

In particular, there exists an absolute constant
c_{0}>0
such that,
for all sufficiently large primes
q
,

c(q)\geq\frac{c_{0}}{\log\log q}.

Proof.

By Theorem
11
,

c(q)\geq P_{3}\prod_{i=1}^{\omega(q-1)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr).

Also,

N_{\omega(q-1)}\leq\prod_{r\mid(q-1)}r\leq q-1,

so
\omega(q-1)\leq m(q-1)
. Since each factor
1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}
lies in
(0,1)
,

\prod_{i=1}^{\omega(q-1)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m(q-1)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr),

which proves the first claim.

Finally, by Mertens’ theorem and Lemma
2
,

\prod_{i=1}^{m(q-1)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\asymp\frac{1}{\log p_{m(q-1)}}\asymp\frac{1}{\log\log q},

as
q\to\infty
through the primes. This gives the second claim;
equivalently,
1/c(q)\ll\log\log q
.
∎

Remark 3

Corollary 10

(Practical bounds for cryptographic primes)
.

Let
q
be a prime with
q>2^{30}
. Then

c(q)>\frac{0.56}{\log q}.

Moreover, if
\omega(q-1)\leq 10
, then

c(q)>0.088.

In particular, this applies to cryptographic-size primes
q\in[2^{64},2^{128}]
satisfying
\omega(q-1)\leq 10
.

Proof.

The claim under
\omega(q-1)\leq 10
follows from the bound
c(q)\geq B(\omega(q-1))
(Theorem
11
), since

B(10)=P_{3}\prod_{i=1}^{10}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\approx 0.08847>0.088.

For the general bound, since every prime divisor of
q-1

,

\frac{\varphi(q-1)}{q-1}=\prod_{r\mid(q-1)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)\;\geq\;\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\leq q-1\\

where the inequality holds because the right-hand side includes additional factors in
(0,1)
.

By Dusart’s explicit product estimate
[
20

, which we use conservatively with threshold

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\leq x\\

Since
q>2^{30}
, we have
q-1>2\,278\,383
and
\log(q-1)>20
, so the correction factor lies
in

Therefore

c(q)=\frac{\varphi(q-1)}{q-1}\prod_{j\geq 1}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q^{j}}\Bigr)\;>\;\Bigl(1-\frac{0.2}{\log^{3}(q-1)}\Bigr)\cdot 0.9999\cdot\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log(q-1)}\;>\;\frac{0.56}{\log q},

The bound

By the shifted-prime Erdős–Kac theorem (Barban

]

[

]

converges in distribution to the standard normal as

. In particular, for every fixed

, almost all primes
q\leq x
satisfy
\omega(q-1)\leq(1+\delta)\log\log x

7
Implications for PRIM-LWE

For a fixed prime modulus
p
,
[
1
, Theorem 10]
shows that
decision-
prim-lwe
is at least as hard as decision-LWE, up to an

O(n^{2})
factor. Separately,
[
1
, Proposition 5]
isolates a positive
lower bound, uniform in the matrix dimension, for the probability that a random
secret matrix has primitive-root determinant. In our notation, that
dimension-uniform sampling constant is

c=\inf_{n\geq 1}c_{n}(p).

As shown in Section
2
,
c_{n}(p)
is decreasing in
n

and
\inf_{n\geq 1}c_{n}(p)=c(p)
, so
c(p)
is the dimension-uniform
sampling term entering the reduction.
The present note concerns this quantity
c(p)
; it does not alter the
separate
O(n^{2})
factor from
[
1
, Theorem 10]
.

Theorem
3
shows that no uniform lower bound on
c(p)
holds across all
primes, meaning the reduction tightness degrades for primes
p
whose predecessor

p-1
is divisible by many small primes. More precisely, let
p^{*}(k)
denote the
least prime satisfying
p^{*}(k)\equiv 1\pmod{N_{k}}
, as in the proof of
Proposition
1
. Then, for the absolute constants
C_{0},L
from
Theorem
4
,

N_{k}<p^{*}(k)\leq C_{0}N_{k}^{\,L}.

The left inequality holds because
p^{*}(k)=1+jN_{k}
with
j\geq 1
, while the
right inequality is exactly Linnik’s theorem. Therefore

\log p^{*}(k)\asymp\log N_{k},\qquad\log N_{k}\sim p_{k}

by Lemma
2
, and hence

\log\log p^{*}(k)\asymp\log p_{k}.

On the other hand, the proof of Proposition
1
gives

c\bigl(p^{*}(k)\bigr)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\sim\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_{k}}.

Thus

c\bigl(p^{*}(k)\bigr)\ll\frac{1}{\log\log p^{*}(k)},

so the construction yields an infinite sequence of primes
p=p^{*}(k)
for
which

c(p)=O\!\left(\frac{1}{\log\log p}\right).

The dimension-uniform upper bound on the rejection-sampling overhead,
namely
1/c(p)
, can therefore grow like

\Omega(\log\log p).

In fixed dimension
n
, the exact expected number of trials is

1/c_{n}(p)\leq 1/c(p)
. By
[
1
, Theorem 10]
, the reduction from
decision-LWE to decision-
prim-lwe
introduces an advantage loss of

O(n^{2})\cdot(1/c)
, where
c=\inf_{n\geq 1}c_{n}(p)=c(p)
is the
dimension-uniform sampling constant. Our results therefore show that the

1/c(p)
factor in this bound can grow like
\Omega(\log\log p)
along a
suitable subsequence of primes.

However, this is a modulus-dependent artifact rather than a structural obstruction. For any fixed prime
p
, the constant
c(p)>0
and the reduction remains valid. Moreover, the explicit bounds of Section
6
show that the specific moduli surveyed in Table
2
—which cover the NIST standards and several widely deployed FHE primes—all have
c(q)
concretely bounded away from zero.

Corollary
3
gives
\limsup c(p)=1/2
unconditionally, so
there is no shortage of primes for which
c(p)
is close to its maximal
value. As a concrete illustration, for safe primes
q=2r+1
with
r
an odd prime and
q\geq 7
, one has

c(q)=\frac{r-1}{2r}\prod_{j\geq 1}\left(1-q^{-j}\right)\geq P_{3}\,\frac{r-1}{2r}.

The prefactor
(r-1)/(2r)=1/2-1/(q-1)

is already close to
1/2
when
q
is large, and the Euler product

\prod_{j\geq 1}(1-q^{-j})
tends to
1
as
q\to\infty
, so
c(q)

approaches
1/2
for large safe primes.

More generally, Corollary
4
shows that for every fixed

\tau\in(0,1/2)
, both sets of primes

\{p:c(p)\leq\tau\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{p:c(p)>\tau\}

have positive relative density, namely
G(\tau)
and
1-G(\tau)
. Moreover,

G(\tau)\to 0
as
\tau\downarrow 0
. Thus moduli yielding a very loose
constant-factor sampling term are rare only in the threshold-asymptotic sense

\tau\downarrow 0
, not absent for any fixed threshold
\tau<1/2
.

We note that the primes typically used in lattice-based cryptography are
chosen for NTT compatibility. Concretely, this means
q\equiv 1\pmod{2^{t}}
for
moderate
t
. Mathematically, this condition controls only the 2-adic part of
q-1
. It does not by itself prevent

q-1
from having many odd prime factors, and NTT-friendliness alone
does not imply a good lower bound on
c(q)
. What our theorems
actually use is the controlled factorization structure of
q-1
,
quantified by
\omega(q-1)
or by the exact factorization.
The NTT condition is harmless with respect to the 2-part, since the
large power of
2
contributes only the single Euler factor

(1-\tfrac{1}{2})
to
\varphi(q-1)/(q-1)
. Among the
implementation moduli surveyed in Table
2
, every
prime satisfies
\omega(q-1)\leq 6
. It is this controlled
factorization that yields the good
lower bounds on
c(q)
recorded in Remark
5
, and
Tables
1
and
2
. Thus our results do not by themselves indicate a practical weakness;
they quantify how modulus choice affects the reduction loss
in the
prim-lwe
framework.

Affine determinant programs (ADPs)
[
37
]
provide a natural external
determinant-based connection to
prim-lwe
. In the ADP obfuscation framework, a
Kilian-style randomization step left- and right-multiplies by invertible matrices

R,S
with
\det(R)\det(S)=1
to preserve the determinant polynomial. Because the ambient sampling space is
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
rather than
all of
M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the relevant primitive-root fraction reduces to

\varphi(q-1)/(q-1)
: the matrix-count Euler product

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-q^{-j})
is already absorbed into the

\mathrm{GL}_{n}
normalization. Since the determinant map

\det:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}

is a surjective homomorphism, all fibers have the same cardinality, and therefore the
exact fraction of invertible matrices with primitive-root determinant is

\frac{\varphi(q-1)}{q-1}.

Hence, if
\omega(q-1)\leq 10
, then

\frac{\varphi(q-1)}{q-1}\;\geq\;\prod_{i=1}^{10}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Bigr)\approx 0.15795,

so rejection sampling inside
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
succeeds in fewer than
6.4

trials on average. If instead one samples from all matrices
M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
then the exact finite-
n
primitive-root density is
c_{n}(q)
, whose limit as

n\to\infty
is
c(q)
. While our results do not establish a security advantage for imposing primitive-root determinant constraints, they confirm that the associated sampling overhead is mild whenever
q-1
has controlled factorization.

8
Concluding Remarks

We have resolved the open question posed in
[
1
, Remark 3(ii)]
in the affirmative:
\inf_{p\text{ prime}}c(p)=0
, and in fact

\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\to\infty\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}c(p)=0.

The proof is short and unconditional, requiring only Dirichlet’s theorem and Mertens’ product formula. We have further established several results that describe the finer structure
of the density
c(p)
:

1.

The minimum of
c(p)
up to
x
has the sharp order

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\textup{ prime}\end{subarray}}c(p)\asymp\frac{1}{\log\log x}.

In particular, the decay to zero is slow and quantified precisely.

2.

The quantity
c(p)
has a continuous limiting distribution
over the primes whose support is exactly
[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
;
equivalently, the distribution function is strictly
increasing on
[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
.
This full-support conclusion appears to be new.
The law coincides with the classical shifted-prime
distribution of
\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)
, is purely singular,
and, by Proposition
4
, the limiting measure has
Hausdorff dimension
0
.
The moments of
G
are given by convergent Euler products
(Theorem
8
), and the Mellin transform

\mathbb{E}[X^{s}]
extends analytically to

\{\operatorname{Re}(s)>0\}
(Theorem
10
).
Pure singularity was already known in the integer case
from Erdős
[
5
]
;
Tenenbaum
[
3
, p. 263]
noted that it carries
over to shifted primes.

3.

For the implementation moduli surveyed in
Table
2
—including the current NIST-standardized
moduli and several widely used FHE primes—the density
c(q)

admits an explicit positive lower bound depending only on

\omega(q-1)
. Accordingly, the
prim-lwe
reduction has a finite,
explicitly computable constant-factor sampling term for every fixed prime
modulus, and this term is small in the concrete parameter regimes treated here.

On the cryptographic side, the rejection-sampling
overhead
1/c(p)
can grow like
\Omega(\log\log p)
along a suitable
subsequence of primes, but as discussed in Section
7
, this represents a quantitative sensitivity to modulus selection rather than a structural obstruction.
Table
2
confirms that every implementation modulus
we examined has
\omega(q-1)\leq 6
and
1/c(q)\leq 4.00
, and the sharper
universal pointwise bound of Corollary
9

gives
1/c(q)=O(\log\log q)
.

Proposition
7
and Corollary
7

determine the right-endpoint behavior of
G
:

1-G\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\sim\frac{\kappa}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow 0),

so
G
is not Hölder of any positive order at
1/2
.
Proposition
4
shows that the limiting
measure has Hausdorff dimension
0
. Equivalently, the limiting
PRIM-LWE law is a continuous, purely singular, full-support
measure on
[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
that is concentrated on a Borel set of
Hausdorff dimension
0
. A natural remaining question is therefore
to determine a global modulus of continuity of
G
. The squared
modulus of the characteristic function admits an explicit Euler
product (Proposition
5
), but whether

|\widehat{\mu_{f}}(\tau)|\to 0\qquad\text{or}\qquad\limsup_{|\tau|\to\infty}|\widehat{\mu_{f}}(\tau)|>0

remains open; by Proposition
6
, the latter would
give an independent Fourier-analytic proof of pure singularity.
This should not be confused with the classical support question for
the distribution of
\varphi(n)/n
over all integers, which remains open.

Appendix A
Positive Density Near
1/2

The following lemma gives an independent, Siegel–Walfisz-based proof
that intervals near the endpoint
1/2
carry positive limiting measure
for
\varphi(p-1)/(p-1)
.

Lemma 11

(Positive density of primes with totient ratio near 1/2)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime and }\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}>\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right\}>0.

Proof.

If
\varepsilon\geq 1
, then
\tfrac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\leq 0
, so the displayed set
contains every prime and the claim is trivial. Hence assume throughout that

0<\varepsilon<1
.

Step 1: Sieving out small odd prime divisors.

Let
R\geq 3
be a fixed integer, to be chosen later in terms of

\varepsilon
, and define

M_{R}:=2\!\!\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq r\leq R\\
r\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}r,\qquad\nu_{R}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq r\leq R\\
r\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(r-2),\qquad\delta(R):=\frac{\nu_{R}}{\varphi(M_{R})}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq r\leq R\\
r\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{r-2}{r-1}.

Also set

\mathcal{A}_{R}(x):=\bigl\{p\leq x:p\text{ odd prime and }r\nmid(p-1)\ \text{for every odd prime }r\leq R\bigr\}.

For primes
p>R
, the condition
p\in\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)
means precisely
that
p
avoids the residue class
1\pmod{r}
for each odd prime

r\leq R
. For each such
r
, among the
r-1
reduced residue classes
modulo
r
, exactly
r-2
are allowed (all except
1\pmod{r}
).
Since modulo
2
the only reduced residue class is
1
, the Chinese
remainder theorem shows that these conditions carve out exactly
\nu_{R}

reduced residue classes modulo
M_{R}
.

The finitely many odd primes
p\leq R
contribute only
O_{R}(1)
to

|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|
. Hence, by Theorem
5
,

|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|=\frac{\nu_{R}}{\varphi(M_{R})}\,\operatorname{Li}(x)+O_{R}\!\bigl(xe^{-c_{R}\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr)=\delta(R)\,\operatorname{Li}(x)+O_{R}\!\bigl(xe^{-c_{R}\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr).

Applying Theorem
5
also with modulus
1
, we have

\pi(x)=\operatorname{Li}(x)+O\!\bigl(xe^{-c\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr),

and therefore

\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|}{\pi(x)}\longrightarrow\delta(R)>0.

In particular,
\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)
has positive relative density

\delta(R)
among the primes.

Step 2: Reduction to a mean-value estimate.

For
p\in\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)
, the only prime
\leq R
dividing
p-1
is
2
,
so

\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid(p-1)\\
q>R\end{subarray}}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr).

Set

S(p):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid(p-1)\\
q>R\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}.

By the elementary inequality

\prod_{i}(1-a_{i})\geq 1-\sum_{i}a_{i}\qquad(0\leq a_{i}\leq 1),

any
p\in\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)
with
S(p)\leq\varepsilon
satisfies

\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\bigl(1-S(p)\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-\varepsilon)>\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon.

It therefore suffices to show that a positive proportion of primes in

\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)
satisfy
S(p)\leq\varepsilon
.

Step 3: Mean-value estimate for
S(p)
.

Throughout Steps 3 and 4, the parameter
R
(and hence
\varepsilon
)
is fixed; all limits are as
x\to\infty
.
Fix
Q=(\log x)^{2}
, and decompose
S(p)=S_{\leq Q}(p)+S_{>Q}(p)
, where

S_{\leq Q}(p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid(p-1)\\
R<q\leq Q\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}.

For the tail, each prime divisor
q>Q
of
p-1
contributes at most

1/Q
, and there are at most
\log p/\log Q
such divisors. Hence,
uniformly for
p\leq x
,

S_{>Q}(p)\leq\frac{\log p}{Q\log Q}\leq\frac{\log x}{Q\log Q}=\frac{1}{2\log x\,\log\log x}=o(1).

Now assume
x
is large enough that
Q>R
. For each prime
q\in(R,Q]
,
write

B_{q}(x):=\#\{p\in\mathcal{A}_{R}(x):p\equiv 1\pmod{q}\}.

Since
q>R
, we have
q\nmid M_{R}
. The conditions

p\in\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)
and
p\equiv 1\pmod{q}
therefore define exactly

\nu_{R}
reduced residue classes modulo
M_{R}q
: modulo each odd prime

r\leq R
there are
r-2
admissible reduced classes, modulo
2
there
is exactly a single admissible class, and modulo
q
the residue class is forced to be
1
.
Because
\varphi(M_{R}q)=\varphi(M_{R})(q-1)
, we obtain from
Theorem
5
that

B_{q}(x)=\frac{\nu_{R}}{\varphi(M_{R}q)}\,\operatorname{Li}(x)+O_{R}\!\bigl(xe^{-c_{R}\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr)=\frac{\delta(R)}{q-1}\,\operatorname{Li}(x)+O_{R}\!\bigl(xe^{-c_{R}\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr),

uniformly for
R<q\leq Q
. Here we use that
M_{R}
is fixed and

q\leq(\log x)^{2}
, so
M_{R}q\leq(\log x)^{A}
for some fixed
A
and all
sufficiently large
x
.

Dividing by the asymptotic formula for
|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|
yields

\frac{B_{q}(x)}{|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|}=\frac{1}{q-1}+O_{R}\!\bigl((\log x)e^{-c_{R}\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr)

uniformly for
R<q\leq Q
. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)}S_{\leq Q}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R<q\leq Q\\
q\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}\,\frac{B_{q}(x)}{|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R<q\leq Q\\
q\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q(q-1)}+O_{R}\!\Bigl((\log x)e^{-c_{R}\sqrt{\log x}}\sum_{q\leq Q}\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R<q\leq Q\\
q\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q(q-1)}+o(1),

since
\sum_{q\leq Q}1/q\ll\log\log Q\ll\log\log\log x
.

Combining this with the uniform bound on
S_{>Q}(p)
, we get

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)}S(p)\leq\eta(R)+o(1),\qquad\eta(R):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q>R\\
q\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q(q-1)}.

Since
\sum_{q}1/(q(q-1))
converges, we have
\eta(R)\to 0
as

R\to\infty
.

Step 4: Markov’s inequality and conclusion.

Choose
R
large enough that
\eta(R)<\varepsilon/2
.
By Markov’s inequality, the fraction of primes in
\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)

with
S(p)>\varepsilon
is at most

\frac{\eta(R)+o(1)}{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}

for all sufficiently large
x
. Hence at least half the primes in

\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)
satisfy
S(p)\leq\varepsilon
, and therefore

\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}>\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon.

It follows that for all sufficiently large
x
,

\#\left\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime and }\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}>\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right\}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|.

Dividing by
\pi(x)
and taking
\liminf
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\left\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime and }\frac{\varphi(p-1)}{p-1}>\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)|}{\pi(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta(R)}{2}>0.

This proves the lemma.
∎
