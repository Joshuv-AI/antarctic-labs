A Takahashi convexity structure on the Isbell-convex hull of an asymmetrically normed real vector space
Philani Rodney Majozi, Mcedisi Sphiwe Zweni

Abstract

Using the vector space operations on the hull, we define a barycentric map 𝕎 ( f , g , λ ) = λ f ⊕ ( 1 − λ ) g , f , g ∈ ℰ ( X , ∥ ⋅ ∥ ) , λ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] , \mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)=\lambda f\oplus(1-\lambda)g,\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|),\ \lambda\in[0,1], and prove that ( ℰ ( X , ∥ ⋅ ∥ ) , q ℰ , 𝕎 ) (\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|),q_{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{W}) is a convex T 0 T_{0} -quasi-metric space in the sense of Künzi and Yildiz. 1
Introduction

Convexity structures in the sense of Takahashi provide a flexible way to transfer
classical convex-geometric arguments to settings where no linear structure is available.
In his seminal work, Takahashi introduced a convexity map on a metric space
(X,d)
as a rule

W(x,y,\lambda)
producing “metric convex combinations” and satisfying the characteristic inequality

d\bigl(z,W(x,y,\lambda)\bigr)\leq\lambda\,d(z,x)+(1-\lambda)\,d(z,y),\qquad z\in X,\ \lambda\in[0,1],

which leads to a rich theory of convex subsets, segments, and fixed point principles for
nonexpansive maps in convex metric spaces
[
22
]
.
Later developments sharpened this point of view by adding geometric rigidity (for example,
strictness/uniqueness and higher-dimensional barycentric devices) and by linking convexity
structures to compactness and fixed point theorems for condensing multifunctions
[
23
]
.

A systematic asymmetric counterpart was initiated by Künzi and Yildiz, who extended Takahashi’s
theory to
T_{0}
-quasi-metric spaces by requiring a pair of directed inequalities: one for the
quasi-metric
q
and one for its conjugate
q^{t}
. This two-sided requirement is essential in the
asymmetric setting and leads to a coherent notion of a convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric space
(X,q,W)
,
together with useful permanence properties, a double-closure formalism for convex sets, and a refined
segment theory in the presence of uniqueness
[
15
]
. In particular, uniqueness of a
convexity structure forces strong iteration identities and yields quasi-metric segments admitting
explicit isometric parametrisations, which highlights the role of strictness notions in asymmetric
convexity
[
15
]
.

Injective-envelope ideas in metric geometry also admit a powerful asymmetric analogue through the
Isbell-convex hull (Isbell-hull). For di-spaces (that is,
T_{0}
-quasi-metric spaces),
Kemajou, Künzi and Olela Otafudu introduced Isbell-convexity as the appropriate replacement of
hyperconvexity and proved the fundamental categorical equivalence: a di-space is Isbell-convex if and
only if it is di-injective, that is, it has the nonexpansive extension property
[
11
]
.
Moreover, every di-space admits an explicit Isbell-hull built from extremal ample function pairs,
uniquely determined up to isometry and containing the original space isometrically via the canonical
embedding
x\mapsto f_{x}

[
11
]
. When the di-space arises from an asymmetrically
normed real vector space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
, this hull is typically denoted
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
.

A distinctive feature of the asymmetric linear setting is that the Isbell-hull is not merely a
“metric” enlargement: it carries canonical algebraic and order structure. Conradie, Künzi and Olela
showed that for an asymmetrically normed real vector space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
the hull
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)

admits scalar multiplication and an addition operation
\oplus
turning
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
into a real
vector space, and that under the specialization order induced by the hull quasi-metric it becomes a
Dedekind complete vector lattice
[
5
]
. From a complementary perspective,
Olela Otafudu established that extremal function pairs in the asymmetric hull enjoy strong
convexity-type regularity and stability properties, reinforcing the view of
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
as a
natural convex-analytic setting attached to
(X,\|\cdot\|)

[
17
]
.

The present paper is conceived as a direct follow-up to
[
19
]
, where
W
-convexity for
real-valued function pairs on a convex asymmetrically normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|,W)
was introduced and
developed. In particular,
[
19
]
proved that whenever
W
is translation-invariant (and
under the natural homogeneity requirement), every minimal pair
f\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
W
-convex on
X
.
That work also linked this convexity-of-pairs mechanism to Chebyshev center techniques, normal structure,
and fixed point results for nonexpansive maps in convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric spaces, building on

[
15
]
and the classical metric theory
[
22
,
23
]
. In this sense,

[
19
]
establishes an inheritance principle: minimal pairs satisfy Jensen-type inequalities
on the base space
X
, provided the underlying convexity structure behaves well under translations.

While
[
19
]
shows that hull elements are convex as functions on
X
, the convexity map
itself still acts on
X
: the barycentric operation takes values in
X
, and
W
-convexity is an inequality
for the components
f_{1},f_{2}
evaluated at points of
X
. On the other hand, the hull
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
a canonical space in its own right: it is the injective (Isbell-convex) envelope of
(X,q_{\|\cdot\|})

[
11
]
and it carries well-defined algebraic operations
[
5
]
.
This leads to the following natural question.

Can one endow the hull
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
itself with a Takahashi convexity structure, defined on the hull,
and compatible with the canonical embedding
i:X\to\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and the hull operations?

The main purpose of this article is to answer this question affirmatively. Using the extremal-pair
description of the hull from
[
11
]
and the vector-space operations on

\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
from
[
5
]
, we introduce:

(i)

a canonical
T_{0}
-quasi-metric
q_{\mathcal{E}}
on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
of sup-difference type, chosen so that
the canonical embedding
i(x)=f_{x}
is isometric, and

(ii)

a lifted convexity map

\mathbb{W}:\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)\times\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)\times[0,1]\to\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|),\qquad\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda):=\lambda f\ \oplus\ (1-\lambda)g,

defined using the hull operations
(\oplus,\cdot)
.

We then prove that
(\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|),q_{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{W})
is a convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric space in the
sense of Künzi and Yildiz
[
15
]
(Theorem
3.11
). This places a convex
geometry on the hull itself, not only convexity inequalities for hull elements viewed as functions on
X
.

A second main result establishes compatibility with the base space. In the linear situation where
W
is
the standard affine convexity on
X
, we show that the embedding
i:X\to\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
intertwines the
base convexity and the lifted hull convexity:

i\bigl(W(x,y,\lambda)\bigr)=\mathbb{W}\bigl(i(x),i(y),\lambda\bigr),\qquad x,y\in X,\ \lambda\in[0,1]

(Theorem
3.13
). In particular,
i(X)
is
\mathbb{W}
-convex in
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
, so the
lifted convexity extends the geometry of
X
in a natural way.

Once convexity is available on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
, several themes can be revisited directly on the hull.
We develop a segment theory and show that, under a uniqueness assumption on
\mathbb{W}
, hull segments admit
explicit isometric parametrisations in the spirit of the quasi-metric segment theory of
[
15
]
.
We then transfer the Chebyshev-center/normal-structure fixed point mechanism from convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric
spaces to the hull, obtaining fixed point theorems for nonexpansive self-maps on bounded, doubly closed,

\mathbb{W}
-convex subsets of
\mathcal{E}
, in the spirit of Takahashi’s classical theory
[
22
]
and its
refinements in the condensing setting
[
23
]
. In this way the Isbell-hull becomes not only an
injective enlargement but also a convenient setting where asymmetric convexity and fixed point theory meet.

Organisation.

Section 2 recalls background on
T_{0}
-quasi-metrics, Takahashi convexity structures and double closure
(from
[
15
]
) and includes the definition of the Isbell-convex hull
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and the
canonical embedding (from
[
11
]
). Section 3 introduces the canonical hull quasi-metric

q_{\mathcal{E}}
and the lifted convexity map
\mathbb{W}
, and proves the main convexity and intertwining theorems.
Further sections investigate stability under hull operations, segment notion (including uniqueness features),
fixed point consequences on the hull, and examples and concluding remarks.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Quasi-metrics and convexity structures

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a set. A map
q:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
is a
quasi-pseudometric
if

q(x,x)=0
for all
x\in X
and

q(x,z)\leq q(x,y)+q(y,z)
for all
x,y,z\in X
.
It is a
T_{0}
-quasi-metric
if
q(x,y)=q(y,x)=0
implies
x=y
.
Define the conjugate
q^{t}(x,y)=q(y,x)
and the symmetrisation
q^{s}=\max\{q,q^{t}\}
.
We follow the standard terminology used in the quasi-metric convexity literature; see, e.g.,

[
15
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,q)
be a quasi-pseudometric space. A map

W:X\times X\times[0,1]\to X
is a
Takahashi convex structure
if for all

x,y,z\in X
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

q(z,W(x,y,\lambda))\leq\lambda q(z,x)+(1-\lambda)q(z,y),\qquad q(W(x,y,\lambda),z)\leq\lambda q(x,z)+(1-\lambda)q(y,z).

Then
(X,q,W)
is called a
convex quasi-pseudometric space
.
In the metric case, this notion goes back to Takahashi
[
22
]
; the two-sided form
above is the
T_{0}
-quasi-metric adaptation developed systematically in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
(X,q,W)
is a convex quasi-pseudometric space, then
(X,q^{t},W)
is convex.
Moreover
(X,q^{s},W)
is a convex metric space in the sense of Takahashi.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(X,q,W)
be a convex quasi-metric space where
X
is a real vector space.
We say that
W
is
translation-invariant
if

W(x+z,y+z,\lambda)=W(x,y,\lambda)+z\qquad(\forall x,y,z\in X,\ \lambda\in[0,1]),

and
homogeneous
if

W(\alpha x,\alpha y,\lambda)=\alpha W(x,y,\lambda)\qquad(\forall x,y\in X,\ \lambda\in[0,1],\ \alpha\in\mathbb{R}).

These conditions are natural in linear settings and are explicitly singled out in the quasi-metric
development of Takahashi convexity; see
[
15
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(X,q,W)
be convex and
C\subseteq X
. We say
C
is
W
-convex
if

W(x,y,\lambda)\in C
whenever
x,y\in C
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

Remark 2.6
.

In a convex metric space in the sense of Takahashi, both open and closed balls are convex and
arbitrary intersections of convex sets are convex
[
22
, Propositions 1–2]
.
In the
T_{0}
-quasi-metric setting, the analogous statements for
q
- and
q^{t}
-balls (and their
finite intersections) are proved in
[
15
]
.

2.2
Asymmetric norms and the induced quasi-metric

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a real vector space. A function
\|\cdot\|:X\to[0,\infty)
is an
asymmetric seminorm

if for all
x,y\in X
and
t\geq 0
,

\|tx\|=t\|x\|,\qquad\|x+y\|\leq\|x\|+\|y\|.

If moreover
\|x\|=\|-x\|=0
implies
x=0
, then
\|\cdot\|
is an
asymmetric norm
, and

(X,\|\cdot\|)
is an
asymmetrically normed real vector space
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be asymmetrically normed. The induced
T_{0}
-quasi-metric on
X
is

q_{\|\cdot\|}(x,y):=\|x-y\|\qquad(x,y\in X).

Its conjugate is
q_{\|\cdot\|}^{t}(x,y)=\|y-x\|
, and its symmetrisation is

q_{\|\cdot\|}^{s}(x,y)=\max\{\|x-y\|,\|y-x\|\}
.

2.3
The Isbell-convex hull

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(X,q)
be a
T_{0}
-quasi-metric space. A pair of functions

f=(f_{1},f_{2})
with
f_{i}:X\to[0,\infty)
is called
ample
if

q(x,y)\leq f_{2}(x)+f_{1}(y)\qquad(\forall x,y\in X).

If
f
is ample and whenever
g=(g_{1},g_{2})
is ample with
g_{1}\leq f_{1}
and
g_{2}\leq f_{2}
pointwise
we have
g=f
, then
f
is called
minimal
(among ample pairs).

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(X,q)
be a
T_{0}
-quasi-metric space. The
Isbell-convex hull

of
(X,q)
is the set

\mathcal{E}(X,q):=\{\,f=(f_{1},f_{2}):f\text{ is a minimal ample pair on }(X,q)\,\}.

In the asymmetric linear setting we write

\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|):=\mathcal{E}\bigl(X,q_{\|\cdot\|}\bigr).

Definition 2.11
.

For
x\in X
define

f_{x}:=\bigl(q(x,\cdot),\,q(\cdot,x)\bigr),

i.e.
(f_{x})_{1}(y)=q(x,y)
and
(f_{x})_{2}(y)=q(y,x)
for
y\in X
.
The map

i:X\to\mathcal{E}(X,q),\qquad i(x)=f_{x},

is called the
canonical embedding
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{E}(X,q)
. Then for every
x\in X
,

f_{1}(x)=\sup_{z\in X}\bigl(q(z,x)-f_{2}(z)\bigr),\qquad f_{2}(x)=\sup_{z\in X}\bigl(q(x,z)-f_{1}(z)\bigr).

Remark 2.13
.

Definitions
2.9
–
2.12
and the canonical embedding are standard in the
Isbell-hull theory for di-spaces; see
[
11
]
. In particular,
\mathcal{E}(X,q)

contains
X
isometrically via
i
, and it is (up to isometry) the injective/Isbell-convex envelope
of
(X,q)
in the sense of di-spaces
[
11
]
.

2.4
Algebraic operations on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)

For asymmetrically normed real vector spaces,
\mathcal{E}(X,|\cdot|)
carries canonical algebraic operations
(scalar multiplication and an addition
\oplus
) that make it a real vector space. Moreover, with respect to
the specialization order induced by the hull quasi-metric,
\mathcal{E}(X,|\cdot|)
is a vector lattice; see

[
5
]
. We record only the formulas needed later.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Define
tf=((tf)_{1},(tf)_{2})
by

(tf)_{1}(x)=\begin{cases}t\,f_{1}(t^{-1}x),&t>0,\\
\|x\|,&t=0,\\
|t|\,f_{2}(t^{-1}x),&t<0,\end{cases}\qquad(tf)_{2}(x)=\begin{cases}t\,f_{2}(t^{-1}x),&t>0,\\
\|x\|,&t=0,\\
|t|\,f_{1}(t^{-1}x),&t<0,\end{cases}

for
x\in X
.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2}),g=(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
. Define
f\oplus g=((f\oplus g)_{1},(f\oplus g)_{2})
by

(f\oplus g)_{1}(x)=\sup_{z\in X}\bigl(f_{1}(x-z)-g_{2}(z)\bigr),\qquad(f\oplus g)_{2}(x)=\sup_{z\in X}\bigl(f_{2}(x-z)-g_{1}(z)\bigr),

for
x\in X
.

Remark 2.16
.

The operations in Definitions
2.14
–
2.15
are part of the explicit description
of the vector space structure on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
; see
[
5
, §3]
.

3
A canonical quasi-metric on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and a lifted convex structure

This section contains the main geometric construction. Starting from the Isbell-convex hull

\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
(Definition
2.10
), we define a canonical
T_{0}
-quasi-metric

q_{\mathcal{E}}
on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
for which the canonical embedding
i:X\to\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)

(Definition
2.11
) is isometric. The definition is motivated by the extremal-pair
description of the Isbell-hull for di-spaces
[
11
]
and is consistent with
the asymmetric linear setting developed in
[
5
,
17
]
. We then define
a lifted convexity map
\mathbb{W}
on the hull using the operations
(\oplus,\cdot)

(Definitions
2.15
and
2.14
) and show that

(\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|),q_{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{W})
is a convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric space in the sense of
Definition
2.2
. Finally, we record the equivariance of
\mathbb{W}
with the
standard affine convexity on
X
.

3.1
A canonical
T_{0}
-quasi-metric
q_{\mathcal{E}}
on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2}),g=(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
. Define

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g):=\sup_{x\in X}\bigl(g_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x)\bigr)^{+},\qquad r^{+}:=\max\{r,0\}.

Remark 3.2
.

For extremal pairs in the di-space hull theory one uses the truncated difference

D(f,g)=\sup_{x\in X}\bigl(f_{1}(x)\dot{-}g_{1}(x)\bigr)=\sup_{x\in X}\bigl(g_{2}(x)\dot{-}f_{2}(x)\bigr),\qquad a\dot{-}b:=\max\{a-b,0\}

(see
[
11
, Lemma 7]
). The quantity
q_{\mathcal{E}}
is the corresponding one-sided
sup-difference on first components, with the positive part ensuring
q_{\mathcal{E}}\geq 0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
f,g\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
the value
q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)
is finite.

Proof.

Fix
a\in X
. By Lemma
2.12
, for every
x\in X
,

g_{1}(x)=\sup_{z\in X}\bigl(\|x-z\|-g_{2}(z)\bigr)\leq\|x-a\|-g_{2}(a),

hence

g_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x)\leq\|x-a\|-g_{2}(a)-f_{1}(x).

Applying Lemma
2.12
again and choosing
z=a
yields

f_{1}(x)\geq\|x-a\|-f_{2}(a)
. Therefore

g_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x)\leq f_{2}(a)-g_{2}(a)\qquad(\forall x\in X),

and taking positive parts and the supremum in
x
gives

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)\leq\bigl(f_{2}(a)-g_{2}(a)\bigr)^{+}<\infty.

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

The map
q_{\mathcal{E}}
is a
T_{0}
-quasi-metric on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
. Moreover, for all
x,y\in X
,

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{x},f_{y})=\|x-y\|.

Proof.

(i)
q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,f)=0
.
Immediate from Definition
3.1
.

(ii) Triangle inequality.

Fix
f,g,h\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and
x\in X
. Then

h_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x)=\bigl(h_{1}(x)-g_{1}(x)\bigr)+\bigl(g_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x)\bigr).

Using
(a+b)^{+}\leq a^{+}+b^{+}
we obtain

\bigl(h_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x)\bigr)^{+}\leq\bigl(h_{1}(x)-g_{1}(x)\bigr)^{+}+\bigl(g_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x)\bigr)^{+}.

Taking suprema over
x\in X
yields

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)+q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,h).

(iii)
T_{0}
-property.

Assume
q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)=0=q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,f)
. Then
g_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x)\leq 0
and
f_{1}(x)-g_{1}(x)\leq 0
for all
x
,
so
f_{1}=g_{1}
pointwise. By Lemma
2.12
,

f_{2}(x)=\sup_{z\in X}\bigl(\|x-z\|-f_{1}(z)\bigr)=\sup_{z\in X}\bigl(\|x-z\|-g_{1}(z)\bigr)=g_{2}(x),

hence
f=g
.

(iv) Isometry on
i(X)
.

For
x,y\in X
,
(f_{x})_{1}(t)=\|x-t\|
and
(f_{y})_{1}(t)=\|y-t\|
, so

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{x},f_{y})=\sup_{t\in X}\bigl(\|y-t\|-\|x-t\|\bigr)^{+}.

By subadditivity,
\|y-t\|\leq\|y-x\|+\|x-t\|
, hence
\|y-t\|-\|x-t\|\leq\|y-x\|
and

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{x},f_{y})\leq\|y-x\|
. Taking
t=x
gives
(\|y-x\|-\|x-x\|)^{+}=\|y-x\|
, so equality holds.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
f,g\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
one has

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)=\sup_{x\in X}\bigl(f_{2}(x)-g_{2}(x)\bigr)^{+}.

Proof.

Fix
x\in X
. By Lemma
2.12
, for all
z\in X
,

g_{1}(z)=\sup_{u\in X}\bigl(\|z-u\|-g_{2}(u)\bigr)\geq\|z-x\|-g_{2}(x),

so

g_{1}(z)-f_{1}(z)\geq\|z-x\|-g_{2}(x)-f_{1}(z).

Taking suprema in
z
and using Lemma
2.12
again gives

\sup_{z}\bigl(g_{1}(z)-f_{1}(z)\bigr)\geq\sup_{z}\bigl(\|z-x\|-f_{1}(z)\bigr)-g_{2}(x)=f_{2}(x)-g_{2}(x).

Applying
(\cdot)^{+}
to both sides yields
q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)\geq(f_{2}(x)-g_{2}(x))^{+}
, and since
x
was arbitrary,

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)\geq\sup_{x}(f_{2}(x)-g_{2}(x))^{+}.

Conversely, swapping
f
and
g
in the preceding argument gives

\sup_{z}(f_{1}(z)-g_{1}(z))\geq g_{2}(x)-f_{2}(x)\qquad(\forall x),

hence
f_{2}(x)-g_{2}(x)\leq\sup_{z}(g_{1}(z)-f_{1}(z))
for all
x
. Taking positive parts and suprema yields

\sup_{x}(f_{2}(x)-g_{2}(x))^{+}\leq\sup_{z}(g_{1}(z)-f_{1}(z))^{+}=q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g).

∎

Remark 3.6
.

The conjugate quasi-metric satisfies

q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}(f,g)=q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,f)=\sup_{x\in X}\bigl(f_{1}(x)-g_{1}(x)\bigr)^{+}=\sup_{x\in X}\bigl(g_{2}(x)-f_{2}(x)\bigr)^{+},

where the last identity follows from Lemma
3.5
.

3.2
Hull operations and compatibility with the embedding

The hull
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
carries operations
(\oplus,\cdot)
turning it into a real vector space;
see
[
5
]
and Definitions
2.15
and
2.14
. We will only
need the following compatibility with the canonical embedding.

Proposition 3.7
.

For
x,y\in X
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
one has

f_{x+y}=f_{x}\oplus f_{y},\qquad f_{\alpha x}=\alpha f_{x}.

Consequently,
i:X\to\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
is affine with respect to
(\oplus,\cdot)
.

Proof.

These identities express that
x\mapsto f_{x}
is linear for the vector space structure on the hull
constructed in
[
5
, Theorem 3.9]
(see also
[
17
]
).
∎

Remark 3.8
.

For fixed
h\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
, the translations
f\mapsto f\oplus h
and
f\mapsto h\oplus f
are
nonexpansive for
q_{\mathcal{E}}
and for
q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}
; compare
[
15
, Definition 4 and Remark 11]
.

3.3
Definition of the lifted convex structure
\mathbb{W}
on the hull

Definition 3.9
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Define

\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda):=\lambda f\ \oplus\ (1-\lambda)g.

Remark 3.10
.

In linear settings the standard convexity map is
\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y
.
On
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
the operations
(\oplus,\cdot)
provide an analogous barycentric rule; compare

[
15
, Example 4]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be an asymmetrically normed real vector space. Then

(\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|),\,q_{\mathcal{E}},\,\mathbb{W})

is a convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric space in the sense of Definition
2.2
, i.e. for all

f,g,h\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

q_{\mathcal{E}}\!\bigl(h,\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)\bigr)\leq\lambda\,q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,f)+(1-\lambda)\,q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,g),

and

q_{\mathcal{E}}\!\bigl(\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda),h\bigr)\leq\lambda\,q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h)+(1-\lambda)\,q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,h).

Proof.

The vector space operations on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
are compatible with the hull quasi-metric in the sense
that for fixed
h
the map
f\mapsto q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,f)
is subadditive with respect to
\oplus
and positively
homogeneous with respect to nonnegative scalars; see
[
5
, §3]
. Therefore,

q_{\mathcal{E}}\!\bigl(h,\lambda f\oplus(1-\lambda)g\bigr)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,\lambda f)+q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(h,(1-\lambda)g\bigr)=\lambda q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,f)+(1-\lambda)q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,g).

The second inequality is obtained by applying the same argument to the conjugate quasi-metric

q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}(u,v)=q_{\mathcal{E}}(v,u)
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

When
\|\cdot\|
is symmetric, the symmetrization
q_{\mathcal{E}}^{s}
yields a genuine metric on the hull, and
Theorem
3.11
reduces to Takahashi convexity in a convex metric space
[
22
]
.

3.4
Intertwining with the underlying convexity structure

Theorem 3.13
.

Assume
(X,\|\cdot\|,W)
is a convex asymmetrically normed real vector space and that
W
is the standard
linear convexity structure

W(x,y,\lambda)=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\qquad(\forall x,y\in X,\ \lambda\in[0,1]).

Then for all
x,y\in X
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

i\bigl(W(x,y,\lambda)\bigr)=\mathbb{W}\bigl(i(x),i(y),\lambda\bigr).

In particular,
i(X)
is a
\mathbb{W}
-convex subset of
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.7
and Definition
3.9
,

\mathbb{W}\bigl(i(x),i(y),\lambda\bigr)=\lambda f_{x}\oplus(1-\lambda)f_{y}=f_{\lambda x}\oplus f_{(1-\lambda)y}=f_{\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y}=i\bigl(W(x,y,\lambda)\bigr).

∎

Remark 3.14
.

The affine convexity structure
S(x,y,\lambda)=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y
is translation-invariant and homogeneous

[
15
]
. In many strictly convex quasi-metric settings, uniqueness forces a convexity structure
to coincide with this affine model; see
[
15
, §3]
.

4
W
-convex function pairs on
X
and stability under hull operations

This section isolates the analytic notion of convexity for
function pairs
associated with a
Takahashi convexity structure on an asymmetrically normed space, and records closure properties
that will be used when passing to the Isbell-convex hull. The basic framework originates in
Takahashi’s convex metric spaces
[
22
]
and its
T_{0}
-quasi-metric extension by
Künzi and Yildiz
[
15
]
. Convexity of real-valued functions on Takahashi convex
metric spaces was studied, for example, by Abdelhakim
[
1
]
. Our focus is the
pair-valued setting on an asymmetrically normed real vector space, following
[
19
]

and tailored to interact with the Isbell-hull formalism

[
5
,
11
,
17
]
.

4.1
Definition and first examples

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be an asymmetrically normed real vector space and let
q_{\|\cdot\|}(x,y):=\|x-y\|
be
the associated
T_{0}
-quasi-metric. Assume that
(X,q_{\|\cdot\|},W)
is a convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric space
in the sense of Definition
2.2
. A pair
f=(f_{1},f_{2})
of extended real-valued functions

f_{j}:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
is called
W
-convex
if for all
x,y\in X
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

f_{j}\bigl(W(x,y,\lambda)\bigr)\leq\lambda f_{j}(x)+(1-\lambda)f_{j}(y),\qquad j=1,2.

Remark 4.2
.

(a)

In the metric case
q=q^{t}
and in the diagonal situation
f_{1}=f_{2}=f
, Definition
4.1

reduces to the usual convexity of
f
with respect to a Takahashi convexity structure

[
1
,
22
]
.

(b)

If
C\subseteq X
is
W
-convex in the sense of Definition
2.5
, then the restriction of a

W
-convex pair to
C
remains
W
-convex for the restricted convexity structure. This is immediate from the
definition (compare the corresponding permanence properties in
[
15
,
22
]
).

Example 4.3
.

For each
z\in X
, define

f_{z}=\bigl((f_{z})_{1},(f_{z})_{2}\bigr):=\bigl(\|\,\cdot-z\|,\ \|z-\cdot\|\bigr).

Then
f_{z}
is
W
-convex on
X
.

Proof.

Fix
x,y\in X
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Since
W
is a Takahashi convex structure on
(X,q_{\|\cdot\|})
,
Definition
2.2
(applied with
q_{\|\cdot\|}(u,v)=\|u-v\|
) yields

\|W(x,y,\lambda)-z\|\leq\lambda\|x-z\|+(1-\lambda)\|y-z\|,

so
(f_{z})_{1}
is
W
-convex. Applying the second Takahashi inequality (equivalently the first one for the
conjugate
q_{\|\cdot\|}^{t}
; cf. Proposition
2.3
) gives

\|z-W(x,y,\lambda)\|\leq\lambda\|z-x\|+(1-\lambda)\|z-y\|,

hence
(f_{z})_{2}
is
W
-convex as well.
∎

4.2
Minimal pairs are automatically
W
-convex

The next result is the key mechanism allowing one to transport convex inequalities from
X
to the
Isbell-hull: once
W
is translation-invariant, every minimal pair in
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
becomes
W
-convex
when viewed as a function pair on
X
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|,W)
be a convex asymmetrically normed real vector space and assume that
W
is
translation-invariant in the sense of Definition
2.4
. Then every
f\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
is

W
-convex on
X
.

Proof.

This is
[
19
, Proposition 5.4(1)]
. The proof uses the minimality condition in
Definition
2.10
together with translation invariance to exclude failure of Jensen-type
inequalities for either component, and relies on the standard sup-representation of minimal pairs
(Lemma
2.12
) from the Isbell-hull theory
[
11
,
17
]
.
∎

4.3
Stability under hull operations

We now record that
W
-convexity is stable under the algebraic operations on
\mathcal{E}(X,|\cdot|)
(scalar
multiplication and
\oplus
), as developed in
[
5
]
and used in
[
19
]
.
This closure property justifies defining convex combinations on the hull by barycentric formulas built from

\oplus
and scalar multiplication.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|,W)
be a convex asymmetrically normed real vector space. Assume that
W
is
translation-invariant and homogeneous (Definition
2.4
). Let
f,g\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
be
W
-convex
on
X
and let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the scalar multiple
tf
and the sum
f\oplus g
are
W
-convex on
X
.

Proof.

Scalar multiplication.

By the definition of the scalar action on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
(see
[
5
]
and the explicit
formulas recalled in
[
19
]
), the pair
tf
is obtained componentwise, with a swap of components
when
t<0
. If
t>0
, homogeneity of
W
gives

(tf)_{j}\bigl(W(x,y,\lambda)\bigr)=t\,f_{j}\!\bigl(W(t^{-1}x,t^{-1}y,\lambda)\bigr)\leq\lambda(tf)_{j}(x)+(1-\lambda)(tf)_{j}(y),

using
W
-convexity of
f_{j}
. The cases
t=0
and
t<0
are handled analogously, using the same homogeneity
identity together with the component swap for negative scalars (cf.
[
19
, Proposition 5.4(2)]
).

Addition
\oplus
.

Using the algebraic description of
\oplus
on the hull
[
5
]
(see also

[
19
]
), for
j=1,2
one can write

(f\oplus g)_{j}(x)=\sup_{z\in X}\Bigl(f_{j}(x-z)-g_{2}(z)\Bigr).

Fix
x,y\in X
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Translation invariance yields

W(x,y,\lambda)-z=W(x-z,y-z,\lambda)
for all
z
. Hence, using
W
-convexity of
f_{j}
,

(f\oplus g)_{j}\bigl(W(x,y,\lambda)\bigr)=\sup_{z\in X}\Bigl(f_{j}\bigl(W(x-z,y-z,\lambda)\bigr)-g_{2}(z)\Bigr)

\leq\sup_{z\in X}\Bigl(\lambda f_{j}(x-z)+(1-\lambda)f_{j}(y-z)-g_{2}(z)\Bigr)\leq\lambda(f\oplus g)_{j}(x)+(1-\lambda)(f\oplus g)_{j}(y),

where the last step uses
\sup(\lambda A_{z}+(1-\lambda)B_{z})\leq\lambda\sup A_{z}+(1-\lambda)\sup B_{z}
.
Thus each component of
f\oplus g
is
W
-convex.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
4.5
, the class of
W
-convex elements of
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)

is closed under the affine operations on the hull used to form convex combinations (compare
Definition
3.9
). In particular, if
W
is translation-invariant, then
Theorem
4.4
implies that every element of
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
W
-convex, so this
closure property holds automatically.

5
Segments in the hull and uniqueness notions

This section develops a simple segment theory in the hull

(\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|),\,q_{\mathcal{E}},\,\mathbb{W})

constructed in Section
3
. The discussion is guided by the segment geometry for
convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric spaces due to Künzi and Yildiz
[
15
]
(extending
Takahashi’s metric setting
[
22
]
) and by the parametrisation mechanism obtained for
a
unique
convexity structure in
[
19
]
. The main point is that, under a suitable
uniqueness assumption on
\mathbb{W}
, the
\mathbb{W}
-segment between two points of the hull admits an
explicit isometric parametrisation by a directed interval equipped with the standard two-parameter
quasi-metric.

5.1
Hull segments

Definition 5.1
.

For
f,g\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
define the
\mathbb{W}
-segment

S[f,g]:=\{\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda):\lambda\in[0,1]\}.

Remark 5.2
.

(a)

By Definition
3.9
,
S[f,g]
is the image of
[0,1]
under the map

\lambda\mapsto\lambda f\oplus(1-\lambda)g
.

(b)

Since
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{W})
is convex (Theorem
3.11
), the set
S[f,g]
is

\mathbb{W}
-convex in the sense of Definition
2.5
.

(c)

If
f=g
, then
S[f,g]=\{f\}
and
q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,f)=0
.

5.2
Uniqueness of the lifted convexity structure

Definition 5.3
.

We say that
\mathbb{W}
is a
unique convex structure
on
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}})
if the following holds:
whenever
h\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and there exist
f,g\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
such that

q_{\mathcal{E}}(u,h)\leq\lambda q_{\mathcal{E}}(u,f)+(1-\lambda)q_{\mathcal{E}}(u,g),\qquad q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,u)\leq\lambda q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,u)+(1-\lambda)q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,u)

for all
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
, then necessarily
h=\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)
.
Equivalently,
\mathbb{W}
is the unique map
H:\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\times[0,1]\to\mathcal{E}
such that

(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}},H)
satisfies the two inequalities in Definition
2.2
.

Remark 5.4
.

Definition
5.3
is the hull analogue of the uniqueness notion for convexity structures on

T_{0}
-quasi-metric spaces used in
[
15
,
19
]
. Under uniqueness, segments
satisfy rigid iteration identities and admit explicit (quasi-)isometric parametrisations. We do not
pursue a general characterisation of uniqueness for
\mathbb{W}
here; we use it as a hypothesis for
the segment parametrisation below.

5.3
Isometric parametrisation of
\mathbb{W}
-segments

Theorem 5.5
.

Assume that
\mathbb{W}
is a unique convex structure on
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}})
in the sense of
Definition
5.3
. Let
f,g\in\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
with
f\neq g
and set

\alpha:=q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g),\qquad\beta:=q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,f).

Then the map

\psi:\bigl(S[f,g],q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl([0,\alpha],u_{\alpha,\beta}\bigr),\qquad\psi(\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda))=\lambda\,\alpha,

is an isometric embedding. Equivalently, for
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in[0,1]
,

q_{\mathcal{E}}\!\bigl(\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda),\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda^{\prime})\bigr)=u_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda\alpha,\lambda^{\prime}\alpha)=\begin{cases}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})\,\alpha,&\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime},\\[2.0pt]
(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\,\beta,&\lambda<\lambda^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Put
h_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)
. Applying Theorem
3.11
with
u=f
and
u=g
gives

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h_{\lambda})\leq(1-\lambda)\alpha,\qquad q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},g)\leq\lambda\alpha,

and similarly

q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,h_{\lambda})\leq(1-\lambda)\beta,\qquad q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},f)\leq\lambda\beta.

By the triangle inequality,

\alpha=q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h_{\lambda})+q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},g)\leq(1-\lambda)\alpha+\lambda\alpha=\alpha,

so equality holds throughout, and hence

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h_{\lambda})=(1-\lambda)\alpha,\qquad q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},g)=\lambda\alpha.

(5.1)

Likewise,

q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,h_{\lambda})=(1-\lambda)\beta,\qquad q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},f)=\lambda\beta.

(5.2)

Now fix
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in[0,1]
and assume
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}
. Using the triangle inequality and
(
5.1
) we obtain

(1-\lambda^{\prime})\alpha=q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h_{\lambda^{\prime}})\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h_{\lambda})+q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},h_{\lambda^{\prime}})=(1-\lambda)\alpha+q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},h_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

so
q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},h_{\lambda^{\prime}})\geq(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})\alpha
.

Assume now that
\lambda>0
. Consider the point

w:=\mathbb{W}\!\left(h_{\lambda},g,\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\right)\in S[f,g].

By Theorem
3.11
applied to the segment between
h_{\lambda}
and
g
we have, for all
u\in\mathcal{E}
,

q_{\mathcal{E}}(u,w)\leq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}q_{\mathcal{E}}(u,h_{\lambda})+\left(1-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\right)q_{\mathcal{E}}(u,g),\qquad q_{\mathcal{E}}(w,u)\leq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},u)+\left(1-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\right)q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,u).

By (
5.1
), the endpoints
h_{\lambda}
and
g
satisfy

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h_{\lambda})=(1-\lambda)\alpha,\qquad q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g)=\alpha.

Substituting
u=f
into the first inequality above shows

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,w)\leq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}(1-\lambda)\alpha+\left(1-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\right)\alpha=(1-\lambda^{\prime})\alpha=q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h_{\lambda^{\prime}}).

Similarly, substituting
u=g
gives
q_{\mathcal{E}}(w,g)\leq\lambda^{\prime}\alpha=q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda^{\prime}},g)
, and substituting

u=f
into the second inequality gives
q_{\mathcal{E}}(w,f)\leq\lambda^{\prime}\beta=q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda^{\prime}},f)
using
(
5.2
). Hence
w
satisfies the two inequalities in Definition
5.3
with the
same endpoints
f,g
and parameter
\lambda^{\prime}
as
h_{\lambda^{\prime}}
. By uniqueness,

h_{\lambda^{\prime}}=w=\mathbb{W}\!\left(h_{\lambda},g,\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\right).

Applying (
5.1
) to the segment between
h_{\lambda}
and
g
yields

q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},h_{\lambda^{\prime}})=\left(1-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\right)q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},g)=\left(1-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\right)\lambda\alpha=(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})\alpha.

If
\lambda=0
, then
\lambda^{\prime}=0
(since
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}
), and the equality is trivial.

If
\lambda<\lambda^{\prime}
, the same argument applied to the reversed quasi-metric
q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}
and
(
5.2
) gives
q_{\mathcal{E}}(h_{\lambda},h_{\lambda^{\prime}})=(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\beta
.
This proves the stated formula and shows that
\psi
is an isometry onto its image in

([0,\alpha],u_{\alpha,\beta})
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

(a)

Under uniqueness, every nontrivial hull segment
S[f,g]
is (quasi-)isometric to a standard directed
interval, and so it inherits an explicit order-topological description (compare

[
15
,
19
]
).

(b)

If
\alpha>0
and
\beta=0
, then
([0,\alpha],u_{\alpha,0})
is order-isometric to the standard
T_{0}

interval
([0,1],u)
via
t\mapsto t/\alpha
, and the segment behaves like an oriented ray segment.

6
Fixed point consequences on the hull

In this section we establish fixed point consequences on the hull

(\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|),\,q_{\mathcal{E}},\,\mathbb{W})

by adapting the Chebyshev-centre/normal-structure method from convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric spaces.
The proofs follow the quasi-metric setting of Künzi and Yildiz
[
15
]
and the
fixed point arguments in
[
18
,
19
]
, extending Takahashi’s classical theory
for convex metric spaces
[
22
]
.

6.1
Double closure, property (H), and Chebyshev centres

Definition 6.1
.

For
A\subseteq\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
define the
double closure
by

\operatorname{cl}_{\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}})}A\ \cap\ \operatorname{cl}_{\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t})}A,

where
\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}})
and
\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t})
denote the topologies induced by
q_{\mathcal{E}}
and its
conjugate
q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}(f,g)=q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,f)
. We call
A

doubly closed
if it equals its
double closure.

Definition 6.2
.

We say that
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{W})
has
property (H)
if every totally ordered family

\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of nonempty, bounded, doubly closed and
\mathbb{W}
-convex subsets of
\mathcal{E}

(with
C_{j}\subseteq C_{i}
whenever
i\leq j
) has nonempty intersection:

\bigcap_{i\in I}C_{i}\neq\varnothing.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathcal{E}
be nonempty and bounded. For
f\in\mathcal{E}
define

r_{f}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}}:=\sup_{g\in A}q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g),\qquad r_{f}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}}:=\sup_{g\in A}q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,f),

r_{f}(A):=r_{f}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}}\vee r_{f}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}},\qquad r(A):=\inf_{f\in A}r_{f}(A),

and

\operatorname{diam}(A):=\sup\{\,q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g):f,g\in A\,\}.

The
Chebyshev centre
of
A
is the set

C(A):=\{f\in A:\ r_{f}(A)=r(A)\}.

Remark 6.4
.

(a)

By boundedness of
A
, all quantities in Definition
6.3
are finite.

(b)

Throughout,
\operatorname{diam}(A)
is taken with respect to
q_{\mathcal{E}}
(not
q_{\mathcal{E}}^{s}
), in line with
the directed quasi-metric setting; compare
[
15
,
19
]
.

6.2
Chebyshev centres: nonemptiness and
\mathbb{W}
-convexity

Theorem 6.5
.

Assume
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{W})
has property
(H)
.
If
A\subseteq\mathcal{E}
is nonempty, bounded, doubly closed and
\mathbb{W}
-convex, then the Chebyshev centre

C(A)
is nonempty, bounded, doubly closed and
\mathbb{W}
-convex.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and set

C_{n}:=\Bigl\{\,f\in A:\ r_{f}(A)\leq r(A)+\tfrac{1}{n}\,\Bigr\}.

Each
C_{n}
is nonempty since
r(A)
is an infimum, and
C_{n+1}\subseteq C_{n}
.

To verify that
C_{n}
is bounded, fix
f_{0}\in C_{n}
. For any
f\in C_{n}
and any
g\in A
, the triangle inequality gives

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{0},f)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{0},g)+q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,f)\leq r_{f_{0}}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}}+r_{f}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}}\leq r_{f_{0}}(A)+r_{f}(A)\leq 2\Bigl(r(A)+\tfrac{1}{n}\Bigr).

Next,
C_{n}
is
\mathbb{W}
-convex. Let
f,g\in C_{n}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. For any
h\in A
,
Theorem
3.11
yields

q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda),h\bigr)\leq\lambda q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h)+(1-\lambda)q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,h),

and

q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(h,\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)\bigr)\leq\lambda q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,f)+(1-\lambda)q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,g).

Taking suprema over
h\in A
gives

r_{\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}}\leq\lambda r_{f}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}}+(1-\lambda)r_{g}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}},\qquad r_{\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}}\leq\lambda r_{f}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}}+(1-\lambda)r_{g}(A)_{q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}},

hence

r_{\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)}(A)\leq\lambda r_{f}(A)+(1-\lambda)r_{g}(A)\leq r(A)+\tfrac{1}{n}.

Since
A
is
\mathbb{W}
-convex and
f,g\in A
, we have
\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)\in A
, and therefore

\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)\in C_{n}
.

We now show that
C_{n}
is doubly closed. Let
(f_{\gamma})
be a net in
C_{n}
converging to
f
in both

\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}})
and
\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t})
, so
q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,f_{\gamma})\to 0
and
q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{\gamma},f)\to 0
.
Since
A
is doubly closed and each
f_{\gamma}\in A
, we have
f\in A
. For any
h\in A
,

q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,h)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,f_{\gamma})+q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{\gamma},h),\qquad q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,f)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(h,f_{\gamma})+q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{\gamma},f),

and taking suprema over
h\in A
yields

r_{f}(A)\leq\bigl(q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,f_{\gamma})\vee q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{\gamma},f)\bigr)+r_{f_{\gamma}}(A)\leq\bigl(q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,f_{\gamma})\vee q_{\mathcal{E}}(f_{\gamma},f)\bigr)+r(A)+\tfrac{1}{n}.

Letting
\gamma
tend along the net gives
r_{f}(A)\leq r(A)+\tfrac{1}{n}
, hence
f\in C_{n}
.

Finally, property (H) implies
\bigcap_{n\geq 1}C_{n}\neq\varnothing
. Any
f
in this intersection satisfies

r_{f}(A)\leq r(A)+\frac{1}{n}
for all
n
, hence
r_{f}(A)=r(A)
and
f\in C(A)
. Therefore
C(A)\neq\varnothing
.
Moreover,
C(A)=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}C_{n}
is bounded, doubly closed and
\mathbb{W}
-convex as an intersection of
sets with these properties.
∎

6.3
Normal structure and fixed points

Definition 6.6
.

We say that
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{W})
has
normal structure
if for every nonempty, bounded,
doubly closed,
\mathbb{W}
-convex set
A\subseteq\mathcal{E}
with
\operatorname{diam}(A)>0
one has

r(A)<\operatorname{diam}(A).

Theorem 6.7
.

Assume
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{W})
has property
(H)
and normal structure.
Let
K\subseteq\mathcal{E}
be nonempty, bounded, doubly closed and
\mathbb{W}
-convex.
If
T:(K,q_{\mathcal{E}})\to(K,q_{\mathcal{E}})
is nonexpansive, then
T
has a fixed point in
K
.

Proof.

Consider the family

\Gamma:=\Bigl\{\,D\subseteq K:\ D\neq\varnothing,\ D\ \text{bounded, doubly closed, $\mathbb{W}$-convex, and }T(D)\subseteq D\,\Bigr\}

ordered by inclusion. Clearly
K\in\Gamma
. If
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a chain in
\Gamma
, then

D:=\bigcap_{i\in I}D_{i}
is nonempty by property (H), and it is bounded, doubly closed,

\mathbb{W}
-convex and
T
-invariant; hence
D\in\Gamma
. By Zorn’s lemma,
\Gamma
has a minimal element
A
.

We claim that
\operatorname{diam}(A)=0
. Suppose
\operatorname{diam}(A)>0
. Since
A\in\Gamma
, the set
A
is nonempty, bounded,
doubly closed and
\mathbb{W}
-convex, so by Theorem
6.5
the Chebyshev centre
C(A)
is
nonempty, bounded, doubly closed and
\mathbb{W}
-convex. We show
T(C(A))\subseteq C(A)
.
Let
f\in C(A)
and
g\in A
. Nonexpansiveness gives

q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(T(f),T(g)\bigr)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,g),\qquad q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(T(g),T(f)\bigr)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,f).

Taking suprema over
g\in A
and using
T(A)\subseteq A
yields
r_{T(f)}(A)\leq r_{f}(A)=r(A)
.
Since
T(f)\in A
, also
r(A)\leq r_{T(f)}(A)
, so
r_{T(f)}(A)=r(A)
and hence
T(f)\in C(A)
.

Normal structure gives
r(A)<\operatorname{diam}(A)
, so there exist
a,b\in A
with
q_{\mathcal{E}}(a,b)>r(A)
.
Then
r_{a}(A)\geq q_{\mathcal{E}}(a,b)>r(A)
, hence
a\notin C(A)
and therefore
C(A)\subsetneq A
.
Since
C(A)
is nonempty, bounded, doubly closed,
\mathbb{W}
-convex and
T
-invariant, we have
C(A)\in\Gamma
,
contradicting the minimality of
A
. Hence
\operatorname{diam}(A)=0
.

Pick any
a\in A
. Then
q_{\mathcal{E}}(a,b)=0=q_{\mathcal{E}}(b,a)
for all
b\in A
, so
A=\{a\}
by the
T_{0}
property
of
q_{\mathcal{E}}
(Theorem
3.4
). Since
T(A)\subseteq A
, we have
T(a)=a
.
∎

Theorem 6.8
.

Assume
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{W})
has property
(H)
and normal structure, and that
\mathbb{W}

is a unique convex structure on
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}})
(Definition
5.3
).
Let
K\subseteq\mathcal{E}
be nonempty, bounded, doubly closed and
\mathbb{W}
-convex.
Then every commuting finite family
\{T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\}
of nonexpansive self-maps on
(K,q_{\mathcal{E}})

has a nonempty common fixed point set:

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Fix}(T_{i})\neq\varnothing.

Proof.

Fix a nonexpansive map
T
on
K
. By Theorem
6.7
,
\operatorname{Fix}(T)\neq\varnothing
.
Clearly
\operatorname{Fix}(T)
is bounded as a subset of
K
. To see that
\operatorname{Fix}(T)
is doubly closed, let
(x_{\gamma})

be a net in
\operatorname{Fix}(T)
converging to
x
in both
\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}})
and
\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t})
, so

q_{\mathcal{E}}(x,x_{\gamma})\to 0
and
q_{\mathcal{E}}(x_{\gamma},x)\to 0
. Since
x_{\gamma}=T(x_{\gamma})
,

q_{\mathcal{E}}(x,T(x))\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(x,x_{\gamma})+q_{\mathcal{E}}(x_{\gamma},T(x_{\gamma}))+q_{\mathcal{E}}(T(x_{\gamma}),T(x))\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(x,x_{\gamma})+q_{\mathcal{E}}(x_{\gamma},x),

where the last inequality uses nonexpansiveness. Letting
\gamma
tend gives
q_{\mathcal{E}}(x,T(x))=0
.
Similarly,

q_{\mathcal{E}}(T(x),x)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(T(x),T(x_{\gamma}))+q_{\mathcal{E}}(T(x_{\gamma}),x)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}(x,x_{\gamma})+q_{\mathcal{E}}(x_{\gamma},x)\to 0,

so
q_{\mathcal{E}}(T(x),x)=0
. By the
T_{0}
property of
q_{\mathcal{E}}
, we obtain
T(x)=x
, hence
x\in\operatorname{Fix}(T)
.

We show that
\operatorname{Fix}(T)
is
\mathbb{W}
-convex. Let
f,g\in\operatorname{Fix}(T)
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
.
For any
u\in K
, nonexpansiveness and Theorem
3.11
yield

q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(u,\,T(\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda))\bigr)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(u,\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)\bigr)\leq\lambda q_{\mathcal{E}}(u,f)+(1-\lambda)q_{\mathcal{E}}(u,g),

and similarly

q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(T(\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)),u\bigr)\leq q_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda),u\bigr)\leq\lambda q_{\mathcal{E}}(f,u)+(1-\lambda)q_{\mathcal{E}}(g,u).

Thus
T(\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda))
satisfies the two inequalities in Definition
5.3
with endpoints

f,g
and parameter
\lambda
. By uniqueness of
\mathbb{W}
,

T(\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda))=\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda),

so
\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Fix}(T)
.

Now let
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
commute. Put
F_{1}=\operatorname{Fix}(T_{1})
, which is nonempty, bounded, doubly closed and

\mathbb{W}
-convex. Since
T_{2}
commutes with
T_{1}
, we have
T_{2}(F_{1})\subseteq F_{1}
, hence
T_{2}
restricts
to a nonexpansive self-map of
F_{1}
. Applying Theorem
6.7
to
F_{1}
yields

F_{1}\cap\operatorname{Fix}(T_{2})\neq\varnothing
. Proceeding inductively gives
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Fix}(T_{i})\neq\varnothing
.
∎

Remark 6.9
.

If one works with the symmetrisation
q_{\mathcal{E}}^{s}
, then
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}}^{s},\mathbb{W})
is a convex
metric space (Proposition
2.3
), and Theorem
6.7
reduces to Takahashi-type
fixed point results in convex metric spaces
[
22
]
. The contribution here is that the
same fixed point argument can be carried out at the directed level, provided one uses double closure
and normal structure in the quasi-metric sense.

7
Examples and counterexamples

We conclude with two brief illustrations. The first shows how the lifted convexity behaves in the
most classical directed example. The second recalls that convexity for the symmetrised metric does
not automatically yield convexity for the underlying
T_{0}
-quasi-metric, even after passing to the hull.

Example 7.1
.

Let
X=\mathbb{R}
equipped with the standard
T_{0}
-quasi-metric

u(a,b):=a\dot{-}b=\max\{a-b,0\},

and let
S(x,y,\lambda)=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y
be the usual affine convexity map.
Then
(\mathbb{R},u,S)
is a convex
T_{0}
-quasi-metric space. Indeed, for any
z,x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

u\bigl(z,S(x,y,\lambda)\bigr)=\bigl(z-(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\bigr)^{+}=\bigl(\lambda(z-x)+(1-\lambda)(z-y)\bigr)^{+}\leq\lambda(z-x)^{+}+(1-\lambda)(z-y)^{+},

and similarly

u\bigl(S(x,y,\lambda),z\bigr)=\bigl((\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)-z\bigr)^{+}\leq\lambda(x-z)^{+}+(1-\lambda)(y-z)^{+}.

In this case the lifted hull convexity
\mathbb{W}
on
\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{R},u)
agrees with the affine structure coming
from the hull operations: for
f,g\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{R},u)
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

\mathbb{W}(f,g,\lambda)=\lambda f\oplus(1-\lambda)g.

Moreover, the canonical embedding
i:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{R},u)
intertwines
S
and
\mathbb{W}
as in
Theorem
3.13
.

Proposition 7.2
.

There exist a subset
K\subseteq\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and a map
W:K\times K\times[0,1]\to K
such that

W
is a Takahashi convex structure on the metric space
(K,q_{\mathcal{E}}^{s})
but
W
fails to be a
Takahashi convex structure on the quasi-metric space
(K,q_{\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

Künzi and Yildiz exhibit a
T_{0}
-quasi-metric space
(X,q)
and a map
W
which is a Takahashi convex
structure for the symmetrised metric
(X,q^{s})
but not for
(X,q)
; see
[
15
, Remark 3.4 and Example 6]
.
Embed
X
into its Isbell-hull via the canonical embedding
i:X\to\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
and put
K:=i(X)
.
By Theorem
3.4
, the restriction
q_{\mathcal{E}}|_{K\times K}
corresponds to
q
under the identification

X\cong K
, and therefore
(q_{\mathcal{E}}^{s})|_{K\times K}
corresponds to
q^{s}
.
Transporting the map
W
along this identification yields a convexity structure on the metric space

(K,(q_{\mathcal{E}}^{s})|_{K\times K})
. If this transported map were also a Takahashi convex structure on the quasi-metric
space
(K,q_{\mathcal{E}}|_{K\times K})
, then transporting back would make
W
a Takahashi convex structure on
(X,q)
,
contradicting the cited example. Hence the directed Takahashi inequalities fail on
(K,q_{\mathcal{E}})
.
∎

8
Concluding remarks and open problems

The main contribution of this paper is that it puts a Takahashi-type convexity structure on the
Isbell-convex hull
(\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|),q_{\mathcal{E}})
by means of the barycentric operation
\mathbb{W}
defined
via the hull operations. This makes it possible to treat
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
not only as an injective
envelope of
(X,q_{\|\cdot\|})
, but also as a natural setting for directed convexity and fixed point
arguments.

A first direction is to understand when
\mathbb{W}
is
unique
on
(\mathcal{E},q_{\mathcal{E}})
. In the

T_{0}
-quasi-metric theory, uniqueness of a convexity structure leads to rigid segment geometry and
iteration laws
[
15
]
. In the metric setting, Talman’s strictness condition provides a
related rigidity principle for convex structures
[
23
]
. It would therefore be useful to give
conditions, stated in terms of the asymmetric norm or the algebraic structure on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
, that
force uniqueness of
\mathbb{W}
, and to determine precisely which barycentric identities (such as
two-stage convex combination rules) follow on the hull.

A second theme concerns stability and completion properties on
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)
. Since
\mathcal{E}(X,\|\cdot\|)

is an injective (Isbell-convex) envelope in the category of di-spaces
[
11
]
, it is
natural to ask how directed completeness (for instance, completeness in
\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}})
and
\tau(q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t})
)
and compactness properties of the symmetrisation interact with
\mathbb{W}
. Talman’s theory shows that
stability of a convex structure controls how convex hulls behave with respect to compactness and the
measure of noncompactness
[
23
]
. An asymmetric analogue, formulated in terms of
q_{\mathcal{E}}
and

q_{\mathcal{E}}^{t}
, should strengthen the hull-level fixed point results obtained here.

Finally, the fixed point consequences suggest two further developments. One is to establish a hull version
of Talman’s theorem for
condensing multifunctions
with convex values and closed graph, under suitable
stability assumptions
[
23
]
. The other is quantitative: Kohlenbach’s proof-mining metatheorems
show that many fixed point arguments in metric convexity admit uniform bounds (such as rates of asymptotic
regularity or moduli for approximate fixed points) once the proof is analysed appropriately
[
13
]
.
Obtaining such bounds in the present directed hull setting would provide a useful link between asymmetric
convexity, injective envelopes, and effective fixed point theory.
