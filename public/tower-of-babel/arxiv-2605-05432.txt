Direct Estimation of Schrödinger Bridge Time-Series Drifts: Finite-Sample, Asymptotic, and Adaptive Guarantees
Othmane MAZHAR, Huyên PHAM

Abstract
We study nonparametric estimation of Schrödinger bridge (SB) drifts from i.i.d. data observed on a single time interval. Starting from the conditional-ratio form of the Schrödinger bridge time-series (SBTS) drift formula, we analyze a direct Nadaraya–Watson plug-in estimator built from kernelized numerator and denominator terms. Unlike recent SB analyses based on entropic-OT potentials, Sinkhorn iterations, or iterative bridge solvers, our approach works directly at the drift level and isolates statistical error from optimization, approximation, and discretization error. Under Hölder regularity, a marginal-density floor, and bounded support, we prove a uniform non-asymptotic bound for admissible bandwidth pairs, a pointwise CLT under genuine undersmoothing, and an adaptive bandwidth selector satisfying an oracle inequality. We also prove a pivot-local minimax lower bound which, through an explicit uniform pivot, yields a global minimax lower bound under transparent compatibility conditions; hence the adaptive selector is minimax-rate optimal up to logarithmic factors. Synthetic experiments provide theorem-targeted diagnostics for finite-sample scaling, Gaussian approximation, and adaptive behavior.

Introduction

Schrödinger bridges (SBs) interpolate between probability distributions by minimizing relative
entropy on path space and connect stochastic control, entropic optimal transport, and diffusion-based
generative modeling

[
26
,
15
,
9
,
18
,
5
]
. They have recently been used for generative modeling,
sampling, and time-series generation

[
7
,
27
,
14
,
1
]
.
Yet the
statistical theory
of data-driven SB estimators remains limited, especially for

direct nonparametric drift estimation
.

Most existing analyses do not treat the drift as the primary statistical object. They instead proceed
through entropic-OT potentials, Sinkhorn iterations, or iterative bridge solvers, so the resulting
guarantees are intertwined with optimization, approximation, or discretization error. We take a
different route: starting from the closed-form conditional-ratio representation of the SB drift, we
analyze a direct kernel plug-in estimator and study the
statistical error of the drift itself
.
This drift-level viewpoint is also essential for sharpness: our lower bound perturbs the drift directly
while preserving normalization, denominator separation, and the global smoothness class.

Problem and scope.

Fix one interval
[s,u]
with
0<s<u
. In the first-order SBTS notation of

[
14
]
, the drift on
[s,u)
depends on the observed past through

\xi=X_{s}
. Given
M
i.i.d. samples

\{(X_{s}^{(m)},X_{u}^{(m)})\}_{m=1}^{M}\sim\nu
, our goal is to estimate

a^{*}(t,x;\xi)
and quantify its accuracy. This first-order notation is only for readability:
fixed-memory extensions replace
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by a lag block in
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{L}
,
with
d
replaced by
Ld
, and finite-grid bounds follow intervalwise by a union bound.

Why this is nontrivial.

The drift is a
ratio
of conditional expectations, so numerator and denominator errors must
be transferred stably and empirical denominators must inherit deterministic population floors.
Moreover, the kernelized terms are weighted by an SB exponential with both
contracting

and
expanding
factors, and the drift is singular as
t\uparrow u
. Bounded support is therefore
central: it controls the expanding factor and makes the weighted empirical class analyzable.

Contributions.

Our contributions are fourfold.

1.

Direct drift-level analysis.

We analyze a kernel plug-in estimator derived from the SBTS conditional-ratio drift formula,
decoupling statistical error from Sinkhorn stability, iterative solver error, and SDE discretization.

2.

Uniform finite-sample guarantees.

Under Hölder smoothness, a marginal-density floor, and bounded support, we prove a uniform
non-asymptotic bound over space and time away from the terminal singularity, with rate

(\log M/M)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)}
up to constants, the terminal factor, and logarithmic terms.

3.

Pointwise inference.

We prove

\sqrt{Mh^{d}}\,(\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi))\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma(t,x;\xi))

under genuine undersmoothing and give a plug-in variance formula for confidence intervals.

4.

Adaptive minimax optimality.

We construct a data-driven bandwidth selector satisfying an oracle inequality. A pivot-local
Le Cam lower bound shows that the rate cannot be improved around interior laws, and an explicit
uniform pivot yields a global minimax lower bound under transparent compatibility conditions.
Thus the adaptive selector is globally minimax-rate optimal up to logarithmic factors.

The empirical section is theorem-aligned rather than benchmark-driven: synthetic experiments with
known drifts assess finite-sample scaling, pointwise Gaussian approximation, confidence-interval
coverage, and oracle-competitiveness of the adaptive selector.

Technical distinction from standard kernel theory.

Our analysis uses classical kernel and
pointwise-asymptotic ideas
[
13
,
28
]
, together with adaptive
bandwidth-selection ideas
[

,

The estimand is a drift-level ratio of weighted conditional moments, and the SB weight contains both a

These are the SB-specific ingredients that allow the finite-sample, CLT, adaptive, and minimax results
to hold directly at the drift level.

Problem Setup and Estimator

Recall the SBTS construction of
[
14
]
. For an observation grid

\mathcal{T}=\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}\}
and a target time-series law
\mu
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
, the bridge is the
path-space entropy projection

P^{*}\in\arg\min_{P}\left\{H(P\mid W):(X_{t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{N}})_{\#}P=\mu\right\},

(1)

where
W
is the reference Wiener law. The constraint is therefore imposed on the full joint law of
the observed time series. Writing
\mu^{W}_{\mathcal{T}}
for the grid law under
W
and

\varrho:=d\mu/d\mu^{W}_{\mathcal{T}}
, the optimizer is the tilt

dP^{*}/dW=\varrho(X_{t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{N}})
. The stochastic-control formulation gives an adapted
log-gradient drift, and
[
14
]
derive from it the conditional-ratio formula used
below. We study this formula on a fixed interval
[s,u]
, with
0<s<u
. Write
\Delta:=u-s
and

\Delta(t):=u-t
for
t\in[s,u)
. We observe
M
i.i.d. copies

\{(X_{s}^{(m)},X_{u}^{(m)})\}_{m=1}^{M}
of an
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random pair
(X_{s},X_{u})

with joint law
\nu
. Throughout,
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes a bounded set containing the support of
both
X_{s}
and
X_{u}
.

For readability, we use the first-order notation of the SBTS drift formula: on the observation grid,
the conditional law of the next state depends on the past only through the current endpoint. Thus,
with
s=t_{i}
,
u=t_{i+1}
, and
\xi=X_{s}
, the drift on
[s,u)
is

a^{*}(t,x;\xi)=\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}\!\left[X_{u}\ F(t,\xi,x,X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi\right]}{\mathbb{E}\!\left[F(t,\xi,x,X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi\right]}-x\right),\ F(t,\xi,x,y):=e^{-\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2\Delta(t)}+\frac{|y-\xi|^{2}}{2\Delta}}.

(2)

This conditional-ratio formula is the starting point of the paper: we estimate the drift directly from
(
2
), rather than through potentials, Sinkhorn iterations, or an auxiliary bridge solver.

Let
p
denote the joint density of
(X_{s},X_{u})
on
B\times B
, let
f(\xi):=\int_{B}p(\xi,y)\ dy
be the marginal density of
X_{s}
, and define

\displaystyle g_{1}(t,x;\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{B}F(t,\xi,x,y)p(\xi,y)\ dy=f(\xi)\mathbb{E}\!\left[F(t,\xi,x,X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi\right],

(3)

\displaystyle g_{2}(t,x;\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{B}y\ F(t,\xi,x,y)p(\xi,y)\ dy=f(\xi)\mathbb{E}\!\left[X_{u}F(t,\xi,x,X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi\right].

(4)

Set
D^{*}(t,x;\xi):=g_{1}(t,x;\xi)/f(\xi)
,
N^{*}(t,x;\xi):=g_{2}(t,x;\xi)/f(\xi)
, and
Q^{*}(t,x;\xi):=N^{*}(t,x;\xi)/D^{*}(t,x;\xi)
. Then

a^{*}(t,x;\xi)=\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\left(Q^{*}(t,x;\xi)-x\right)=\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\left(\frac{N^{*}(t,x;\xi)}{D^{*}(t,x;\xi)}-x\right).

(5)

Kernel plug-in estimator.

Fix a possibly signed kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and bandwidths

h_{1},h_{2}>0
, with
K_{h}(z):=h^{-d}K(z/h)
. For
(t,x,\xi)\in[s,u)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times B
, define

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{j}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}K_{h_{j}}(X_{s}^{(m)}-\xi),\qquad j\in\{1,2\},

(6)

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{1}(t,x;\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}F(t,\xi,x,X_{u}^{(m)})\ K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}^{(m)}-\xi),

(7)

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{2}(t,x;\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}X_{u}^{(m)}\ F(t,\xi,x,X_{u}^{(m)})\ K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}^{(m)}-\xi).

(8)

Let
\hat{D}(t,x;\xi):=\hat{g}_{1}(t,x;\xi)/\hat{f}_{1}(\xi)
and
\hat{N}(t,x;\xi):=\hat{g}_{2}(t,x;\xi)/\hat{f}_{2}(\xi)
. The direct Nadaraya–Watson plug-in estimator

[
23
,
30
]
is

\hat{a}(t,x;\xi):=\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\left(\frac{\hat{N}(t,x;\xi)}{\hat{D}(t,x;\xi)}-x\right).

(9)

For risk statements we use the denominator-floor convention stated in Appendix
E
; it is inactive on the high-probability events used below.
We allow
h_{1}\neq h_{2}
because the two weighted functionals may have different effective envelopes and variance levels. In the main-text CLT and adaptive results, however, we specialize to the diagonal choice
h_{1}=h_{2}=h
to keep the bandwidth parameter one-dimensional. The unequal-bandwidth CLT, and adaptive versions are given in Appendix
D
to
F
.

3.1
Assumptions

We state assumptions for the
SBTS single-interval model
above.

Assumption 3.1

(Kernel)
.

Let
\ell_{\beta}:=\max\{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:m<\beta\}
and
\theta_{\beta}:=\beta-\ell_{\beta}\in(0,1]
.
The kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is bounded, compactly supported,
Lipschitz with constant
L_{K}
, integrates to one, and is a multivariate
kernel of order
\ell_{\beta}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}z^{\alpha}K(z)\,dz=0,

1\leq|\alpha|\leq\ell_{\beta}
.
Moreover,

\mu_{\beta}(K):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{\beta}|K(z)|\,dz<\infty
.

Assumption 3.2

(Pair-law regularity)
.

The pair
(X_{s},X_{u})
admits a joint density
p
supported on
B\times B
, where

B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is bounded. There exist constants
\beta>0
,
L_{p}>0
, and

f_{\min}>0
such that:

1.

for every
y\in B
, the map
\xi\mapsto p(\xi,y)
on
B
is the restriction
of a function
\bar{p}(\cdot,y)\in\Sigma(\beta,L_{p})
, with

\Sigma(\beta,L_{p})
defined in Appendix
A
, on a neighborhood of
B
,
uniformly in
y\in B
;

2.

the marginal density
f(\xi):=\int_{B}p(\xi,y)\,dy

satisfies

\inf_{\xi\in B}f(\xi)\geq f_{\min}>0
.

Consequently, denominator separation holds automatically on compact query sets. Indeed, for every

R>0
and
\eta\in(0,\Delta)
, writing
B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|\leq R\}
, the explicit form of
F
and the boundedness of
B
imply that
there exists a deterministic constant
D_{\min}=D_{\min}(B,R,\eta,\Delta)>0
such that

\inf_{\xi\in B,\ x\in B_{R},\ t\in[s,u-\eta]}D^{*}(t,x;\xi)=\inf_{\xi\in B,\ x\in B_{R},\ t\in[s,u-\eta]}\mathbb{E}\!\left[F(t,\xi,x,X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi\right]\geq 2D_{\min}
.
Thus
D_{\min}
denotes this deterministic constant and is not an additional model parameter.

Remark 3.3

(Why bounded support is needed)
.

The bounded-support assumption is essential for our proof strategy. The SB weight in (
2
) contains both the contracting factor
e^{-|y-x|^{2}/(2\Delta(t))}
and the expanding factor
e^{|y-\xi|^{2}/(2\Delta)}
. Generic sub-Gaussian tails do not provide the uniform control needed for the weighted empirical-process bounds in our argument. Bounded support makes the expansion term uniformly bounded and allows us to control the weighted class at the same entropy order as the classical translated-kernel class. In particular,

C_{F}:=\sup_{\xi,y\in B}e^{\frac{|y-\xi|^{2}}{2\Delta}}<\infty,\qquad C_{Y}:=\sup_{y\in B}|y|<\infty.

(10)

Remark 3.4

(Fixed finite-memory extensions)
.

The first-order display is used only to keep notation readable. For any fixed
memory length
L
, replace the conditioning variable
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the
lag block

h_{i}^{(L)}=(X_{t_{i-L+1}},\ldots,X_{t_{i}})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{L}

(11)

and replace the pair law of
(X_{t_{i}},X_{t_{i+1}})
by the joint law of

(h_{i}^{(L)},X_{t_{i+1}})
. The ratio algebra is unchanged, and the kernel bias,
entropy, concentration, variance, CLT, and adaptive arguments carry over with
the conditioning dimension
d
replaced by
Ld
. For a fixed interval
i
, the same applies to
the full finite history, with
d
replaced by
id
. What is not covered here is estimation of the SB
drift from a single discretely observed trajectory; that problem has dependent design points and
requires different empirical-process and time-series arguments.

Bandwidth scale.

The oracle bandwidth scale is
h_{1}\asymp h_{2}\asymp(\log M/M)^{1/(2\beta+d)}
, up to model-dependent constants and the precise logarithmic term in the finite-sample theorem. For the pointwise CLT, however, the bandwidth must be genuinely undersmoothed and therefore must decay strictly faster than this rate-optimal scale.

4.1
Deterministic ratio transfer

The following proposition isolates the deterministic step that turns estimation error for the kernelized building blocks into estimation error for the drift.

Proposition 4.1

(Deterministic ratio-stability transfer)
.

Assume
Section

3.1
. Let

\mathcal{E}:=\left\{\inf_{\xi\in B_{\rho}}\hat{f}_{1}(\xi)\geq\frac{f_{\min}}{2},\quad\inf_{\xi\in B_{\rho}}\hat{f}_{2}(\xi)\geq\frac{f_{\min}}{2},\quad\inf_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}\hat{D}(t,x;\xi)\geq\frac{D_{\min}}{2}\right\}.

Then on

, for every
(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}

\displaystyle|\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\Delta(t)D_{\min}}\Bigg[\frac{f(\xi)|\hat{g}_{2}(t,x;\xi)-g_{2}(t,x;\xi)|+|g_{2}(t,x;\xi)|\ |\hat{f}_{2}(\xi)-f(\xi)|}{f_{\min}^{2}}

\displaystyle+|Q^{*}(t,x;\xi)|\frac{f(\xi)|\hat{g}_{1}(t,x;\xi)-g_{1}(t,x;\xi)|+|g_{1}(t,x;\xi)|\ |\hat{f}_{1}(\xi)-f(\xi)|}{f_{\min}^{2}}\Bigg].

Under
Section

3.1
, the quantities

,

,
g_{2}

(13)

4.2
Finite-sample uniform bound

Let
C_{K}>0
be the entropy constant from Appendix
A
and define

\Lambda(h,\delta):=d\log\!\Big(\frac{C_{K}}{h}\Big)+\log\!\Big(\frac{2}{\delta}\Big),\ \widetilde{\Lambda}(h,\delta):=d\log\!\Big(\frac{C_{K}}{h}\Big)+\log\!\Big(\frac{8}{\delta}\Big),\ r_{e,j}(\delta):=h_{j}^{\beta}+\sqrt{\frac{\widetilde{\Lambda}(h_{j},\delta)}{Mh_{j}^{d}}},

(14)

for
j\in\{1,2\}
.
We call
(h_{1},h_{2})

\delta

r_{e,1}(\delta)+r_{e,2}(\delta)\leq c_{0}
.

Theorem 4.2

(Uniform finite-sample bound)
.

Under
Sections

3.1
and
3.1
, there exist constants
c_{0},C>0
such that, for every
\delta\in(0,1)
and every
\delta
-admissible bandwidth pair
(h_{1},h_{2})
, with probability at least

\sup_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}|\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi)|\leq\frac{C}{\eta}\left[h_{1}^{\beta}+h_{2}^{\beta}+\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda(h_{1},\delta)}{Mh_{1}^{d}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda(h_{2},\delta)}{Mh_{2}^{d}}}\right].

(15)

The key difficulty is that the SB weight is not a standard bounded regression weight: it contains both a contracting Gaussian and an expanding exponential. Our proof handles this by factorizing
F(t,\xi,x,y)=\chi(t,x,y)G(\xi,y)
with
\chi(t,x,y):=e^{-|y-x|^{2}/(2\Delta(t))}\in[0,1]
and
G(\xi,y):=e^{|y-\xi|^{2}/(2\Delta)}\leq C_{F}
on
B\times B
. Bounded support controls the expanding term, while the contraction term yields both Lipschitz control in the parameters and decay in the unbounded state variable
x
. As a result, the weighted empirical-process class has the same entropy order as the classical translated-kernel class
\{z\mapsto K_{h}(z-\xi):\xi\in B_{\rho}\}
.

Corollary 4.3

(Oracle bandwidth)
.

If
h_{1}=h_{2}=h^{\star}:=\bigl((d\log(C_{K}M)+\log(2/\delta))/M\bigr)^{1/(2\beta+d)}
, then for each fixed

there exists

, depending on

and the model constants, such that for all

the pair
(h^{\star},h^{\star})
is
\delta
-admissible and, with probability at least

\sup_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}|\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi)|\leq\frac{C}{\eta}\left(\frac{d\log(C_{K}M)+\log(2/\delta)}{M}\right)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

(16)

In particular, for

\sup_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}|\hat{a}-a^{*}|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\Big(\tfrac{\log M}{M}\Big)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)}\right).

(17)

4.3
Pointwise asymptotic normality

In the main text we specialize to
h_{1}=h_{2}=h
; the unequal-bandwidth statement is given in Appendix
D
. Let
R(K):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(z)^{2}\ dz
. For fixed

\psi_{t,x,\xi}(y):=\bigl(y-x-\Delta(t)a^{*}(t,x;\xi)\bigr)F(t,\xi,x,y),\quad\Sigma(t,x;\xi):=\frac{R(K)}{f(\xi)}\frac{\text{Var}(\psi_{t,x,\xi}(X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi)}{\Delta(t)^{2}D^{*}(t,x;\xi)^{2}}.

(19)

Theorem 4.7

(Pointwise CLT)
.

Assume
Sections

3.1
and
3.1
, and let
h_{1}=h_{2}=h\to 0

. Then for every fixed

\sqrt{Mh^{d}}\ \bigl(\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi)\bigr)\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}\!\bigl(0,\Sigma(t,x;\xi)\bigr).

(20)

Remark 4.8

(Undersmoothing is genuinely required)
.

The condition

is the scaled-bias requirement for the CLT. Taking only a smaller constant multiple of the rate-optimal bandwidth does not suffice as it preserves the same exponent and therefore does not make the scaled bias vanish.

Remark 4.11

(Oracle interpretation and minimax interpretation)
.

The adaptive theorem controls the estimator up to multiplicative constants and lower-order terms.
Thus it supports rate-level competitiveness with oracle tuning, but not finite-sample equality with the
oracle bandwidth.

The lower-bound argument has two layers.

is a pivot-local Le Cam construction:
around any interior law satisfying the slack conditions, no estimator can improve on the rate

that local neighborhood is a subset of the global model, the same construction yields a global
minimax lower bound over

\mathfrak{P}(\beta,L_{p},f_{\min},B)

Thus the adaptive selector is not only locally sharp around interior pivots; under the explicit
compatibility conditions of

factors.

We use controlled synthetic experiments in the SBTS single-interval setting to test the theorems predictions: finite-sample scaling,
pointwise Gaussian approximation under undersmoothing, adaptive bandwidth

6
Discussion and Limitations

The paper gives a sharp theory for
direct drift estimation in the SBTS single-interval
setting. Bounded support is central to the current proof because it controls the expanding exponential factor in the SB weight and makes the weighted empirical-process analysis tractable. As a nonparametric method, the estimator also inherits the usual curse of dimensionality, and adaptive selection adds the computational cost of evaluating a bandwidth grid.

The theory is uniform on the query set

Q_{R,\eta,\rho}=[s,u-\eta]\times B_{R}\times B_{\rho}

and necessarily deteriorates as
t\uparrow u
. The main results are proved for i.i.d. trajectory samples in the single-interval model, while the finite-sample ratio analysis works on a natural high-probability event where empirical denominators are separated from zero. Fixed finite-memory extensions are standard, replacing the conditioning variable by a lag block and the ambient conditioning dimension
d
by
Ld
. Estimating the SB drift from one discretely observed trajectory, where the effective samples are dependent and the past may grow with the grid, requires new tools and is left for future work.

The lower-bound result is local in its construction but global in its implication under the compatibility
conditions of
Appendix

F
.
Appendix

F
proves a sharp two-point Le Cam lower bound inside a
shrinking neighborhood of any interior pivot law.
Appendix

F
then exhibits an explicit uniform pivot
satisfying these interiority conditions whenever the model constants are compatible with the bounded
support class. Since the resulting local neighborhood is contained in the original global model, the
same construction yields a global minimax lower bound over

\mathfrak{P}(\beta,L_{p},f_{\min},B)
. Thus the adaptive selector is globally minimax-rate
optimal up to logarithmic factors, while the proof also gives the stronger local interpretation around
interior laws.

7
Conclusion

We studied direct nonparametric estimation of Schrödinger bridge time-series drifts. Unlike approaches based on entropic-OT potentials, Sinkhorn iterations, or iterative bridge solvers, our analysis works directly at the drift level and isolates the
statistical error
of the estimator. Under Hölder regularity, a marginal-density floor, and bounded support, we proved an
interior uniform finite-sample bound at the classical kernel rate up to logarithmic factors, a pointwise CLT
under genuine undersmoothing, and an adaptive selector satisfying an oracle inequality. We also proved a pivot-local minimax lower bound and, by exhibiting an explicit uniform pivot in the
global class, derived a global minimax lower bound under transparent compatibility conditions. This
shows that the adaptive selector is minimax-rate optimal up to logarithmic factors. Together, these results provide a self-contained statistical theory for direct kernel estimation of SBTS drifts.

\eta
,
\Delta
, and

for a constant

Corollary C.3

(Oracle bandwidth choice)
.

Set

and let

Then for each fixed
\delta\in(0,1)
there exists
M_{0}(\delta)\geq 1
, depending on
\delta
and the model constants, such that for all
M\geq M_{0}(\delta)
, the pair
(h^{\star},h^{\star})
is
\delta
-admissible and, with probability at least

,

\sup_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}|\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi)|\leq\frac{C}{\eta}\left(\frac{L}{M}\right)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

(94)

Moreover, for
\delta=M^{-\gamma}
with fixed

\sup_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}|\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi)|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\left(\frac{\log M}{M}\right)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)}\right).

(95)

Proof.

Since

(96)

Hence

\sqrt{\frac{d\log(C_{K}/h^{\star})+\log(8/\delta)}{M(h^{\star})^{d}}}\leq\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{L}{M}\right)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

(97)

Therefore, for each fixed

is

, and

applies. Since

on

\sup_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}|\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi)|\leq\frac{C}{\eta}\left(\frac{L}{M}\right)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

Appendix D
Unequal-bandwidth pointwise asymptotic normality

In this Appendix we state and prove a pointwise CLT for the estimator (
9
) with
possibly different bandwidths
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
. The main-text
Theorem

4.7
is recovered as the
diagonal specialization
h_{1}=h_{2}=h
; see
Appendix

D
below.

Fix
(t,x,\xi)\in[s,u-\eta]\times B_{R}\times\operatorname{int}(B)
. Write

U(y):=y\ F_{t,x,\xi}(y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ V(y):=F_{t,x,\xi}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then

N^{*}(t,x;\xi)=\mathbb{E}\!\big[U(X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi\big],\qquad D^{*}(t,x;\xi)=\mathbb{E}\!\big[V(X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi\big],

(101)

Q^{*}(t,x;\xi)=\frac{N^{*}(t,x;\xi)}{D^{*}(t,x;\xi)},\qquad a^{*}(t,x;\xi)=\frac{Q^{*}(t,x;\xi)-x}{\Delta_{t}}.

(102)

Also let

R(K):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(u)^{2}\ du,\ R_{\rho}(K):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(u)K(\rho u)\ du,\ \rho>0
. Writing
U:=U(X_{u})
and
V:=V(X_{u})
for brevity, define

n_{U}:=\mathbb{E}[U\mid X_{s}=\xi],\qquad n_{V}:=\mathbb{E}[V\mid X_{s}=\xi],\qquad n_{UV}:=\mathbb{E}[UV\mid X_{s}=\xi],

(103)

m_{UU}:=\mathbb{E}[UU^{\top}\mid X_{s}=\xi],\qquad m_{V^{2}}:=\mathbb{E}[V^{2}\mid X_{s}=\xi].

(104)

Then the block covariance matrix is

\Omega_{\rho}(t,x;\xi):=f(\xi)\begin{bmatrix}\rho^{-d}R(K)\ m_{UU}&R_{\rho}(K)\ n_{UV}&R_{\rho}(K)\ n_{U}&\rho^{-d}R(K)\ n_{U}\\
R_{\rho}(K)\ n_{UV}^{\top}&R(K)\ m_{V^{2}}&R(K)\ n_{V}&R_{\rho}(K)\ n_{V}\\
R_{\rho}(K)\ n_{U}^{\top}&R(K)\ n_{V}&R(K)&R_{\rho}(K)\\
\rho^{-d}R(K)\ n_{U}^{\top}&R_{\rho}(K)\ n_{V}&R_{\rho}(K)&\rho^{-d}R(K)\end{bmatrix}.

(105)

Let

\Gamma_{t}(a,b,c,e):=\frac{1}{\Delta_{t}}\left(\frac{ac}{be}-x\right),\ (a,b,c,e)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}

where
ac
denotes scalar multiplication of the vector
a
by the scalar
c
. Denote by

J_{\Gamma}(t,x;\xi):=D\Gamma_{t}\!\big(g_{2}(t,x;\xi),\ g_{1}(t,x;\xi),\ f(\xi),\ f(\xi)\big)

the Jacobian of
\Gamma_{t}
at the population point. Explicitly, since
g_{1}(t,x;\xi)=f(\xi)D^{*}(t,x;\xi)
, set

A:=\bigl(f(\xi)D^{*}(t,x;\xi)\bigr)^{-1}=g_{1}(t,x;\xi)^{-1},\qquad B:=f(\xi)^{-1}.

(106)

Then

J_{\Gamma}(t,x;\xi)=\frac{1}{\Delta_{t}}\Bigl[AI_{d},\,-AQ^{*}(t,x;\xi),\,BQ^{*}(t,x;\xi),\,-BQ^{*}(t,x;\xi)\Bigr].

(107)

This is viewed as a
d\times(d+3)
block row. Finally, define

\Sigma_{\rho}(t,x;\xi):=J_{\Gamma}(t,x;\xi)\ \Omega_{\rho}(t,x;\xi)\ J_{\Gamma}(t,x;\xi)^{\top}
.

Theorem D.1

(Unequal-bandwidth pointwise CLT)
.

Assume
Sections

3.1
and
3.1
. Let
h_{1}\to 0
,
h_{2}\to 0
satisfy

,

Then, for every fixed

,

\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\ \bigl(\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi)\bigr)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal{N}\!\bigl(0,\Sigma_{\rho}(t,x;\xi)\bigr),

(108)

where
\Sigma_{\rho}(t,x;\xi)
is defined above.

Remark D.2

(Diagonal specialization)
.

If
h_{1}=h_{2}=h
, then
\rho=1
and
R_{\rho}(K)=R(K)
. In this case, a direct simplification of

J_{\Gamma}\ \Omega_{1}\ J_{\Gamma}^{\top}
yields

\Sigma_{1}(t,x;\xi)=\frac{R(K)}{f(\xi)}\cdot\frac{\operatorname{Var}\!\bigl(\psi_{t,x,\xi}(X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi\bigr)}{\Delta(t)^{2}\ D^{*}(t,x;\xi)^{2}},

(109)

with

\psi_{t,x,\xi}(y)=\bigl(y-x-\Delta(t)a^{*}(t,x;\xi)\bigr)\ F(t,\xi,x,y).

(110)

Hence
Theorem

D.1
implies the main-text
Theorem

4.7
.

Proof of
Theorem

D.1
.

Fix
(t,x,\xi)\in[s,u-\eta]\times B_{R}\times\operatorname{int}(B)
.
Since
\xi\in\operatorname{int}(B)
and
K
is compactly supported, for all sufficiently large
M
we have

\xi+h_{j}u\in B
for all
u\in\operatorname{supp}(K)
and
j\in\{1,2\}
,
so the kernel changes of variables below are valid without boundary effects.

Define

\hat{T}:=\bigl(\hat{g}_{2}^{\top},\hat{g}_{1},\hat{f}_{1},\hat{f}_{2}\bigr)^{\top},\ T:=\bigl(g_{2}(t,x;\xi)^{\top},g_{1}(t,x;\xi),f(\xi),f(\xi)\bigr)^{\top}
. By the theorem’s assumptions,

\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\,h_{1}^{\beta}\to 0,\ \sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\,h_{2}^{\beta}\to 0
.
Since the componentwise bias bounds satisfy

|\mathbb{E}\hat{g}_{1}-g_{1}|\lesssim h_{1}^{\beta},\qquad|\mathbb{E}\hat{g}_{2}-g_{2}|\lesssim h_{2}^{\beta},\qquad|\mathbb{E}\hat{f}_{j}-f|\lesssim h_{j}^{\beta},

(111)

it follows that

\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\,|\mathbb{E}\hat{T}-T|\to 0
.
Since
\Gamma_{t}
is continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of
T
, it follows that

\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\ \bigl(\Gamma_{t}(\mathbb{E}\hat{T})-\Gamma_{t}(T)\bigr)\to 0
.

For each
m
, define

Z_{m}:=\begin{pmatrix}U(X_{u}^{(m)})\ K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}^{(m)}-\xi)\\
V(X_{u}^{(m)})\ K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}^{(m)}-\xi)\\
K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}^{(m)}-\xi)\\
K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}^{(m)}-\xi)\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+3},\qquad\hat{T}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}Z_{m}.

(112)

To identify the covariance limit, write

\widetilde{n}_{A}(z):=\int_{B}A(y)\,p(z,y)\,dy

for bounded measurable
A:B\to\mathbb{R}
,
A:B\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or
A:B\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Then

\widetilde{n}_{U}(\xi)=g_{2}(t,x;\xi),\ \widetilde{n}_{V}(\xi)=g_{1}(t,x;\xi)
,
and

\widetilde{n}_{UU^{\top}}(\xi)=f(\xi)\,E[UU^{\top}\mid X_{s}=\xi],\ \widetilde{n}_{UV}(\xi)=f(\xi)\,E[UV\mid X_{s}=\xi],\ \widetilde{n}_{V^{2}}(\xi)=f(\xi)\,E[V^{2}\mid X_{s}=\xi].

(113)

A standard change of variables gives

\displaystyle h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[UU^{\top}K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}-\xi)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{1}^{d}}{h_{2}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(u)^{2}\ \widetilde{n}_{UU^{\top}}(\xi+h_{2}u)\ du\to\rho^{-d}R(K)\ \widetilde{n}_{UU^{\top}}(\xi),

(114)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[V^{2}K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}-\xi)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(u)^{2}\ \widetilde{n}_{V^{2}}(\xi+h_{1}u)\ du\to R(K)\ \widetilde{n}_{V^{2}}(\xi),

(115)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}-\xi)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\to R(K)\ f(\xi),\qquad h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}-\xi)^{2}\big]\to\rho^{-d}R(K)\ f(\xi).

(116)

Writing
\rho_{M}:=h_{2}/h_{1}
, the mixed terms satisfy

h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[UV\ K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}-\xi)K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}-\xi)\big]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(u)\ K(\rho_{M}u)\ \widetilde{n}_{UV}(\xi+h_{2}u)\ du\to R_{\rho}(K)\ \widetilde{n}_{UV}(\xi),

(117)

by dominated convergence. Similarly,

\displaystyle h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[U\ K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}-\xi)K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}-\xi)\big]

\displaystyle\to R_{\rho}(K)\ \widetilde{n}_{U}(\xi),

(118)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[V\ K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}-\xi)K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}-\xi)\big]

\displaystyle\to R_{\rho}(K)\ \widetilde{n}_{V}(\xi),

(119)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}-\xi)K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}-\xi)\big]

\displaystyle\to R_{\rho}(K)\ f(\xi),

(120)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[U\ K_{h_{2}}(X_{s}-\xi)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\to\rho^{-d}R(K)\ \widetilde{n}_{U}(\xi),

(121)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{d}\ \mathbb{E}\!\big[V\ K_{h_{1}}(X_{s}-\xi)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\to R(K)\ \widetilde{n}_{V}(\xi).

(122)

Subtracting products of expectations does not change the limit. Indeed, since

\widehat{T}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}Z_{m}
,
we have

Mh_{1}^{d}\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat{T})=h_{1}^{d}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}[Z_{m}Z_{m}^{\top}]-\mathbb{E}Z_{m}\,\mathbb{E}Z_{m}^{\top}\Bigr)
.
The preceding displays identify the limit of

h_{1}^{d}\mathbb{E}[Z_{m}Z_{m}^{\top}]
. Moreover, the components of

\mathbb{E}Z_{m}
are uniformly bounded, because
U,V
are bounded on
B

and the kernel averages converge to the corresponding population
quantities. Therefore

h_{1}^{d}\,\mathbb{E}Z_{m}\,\mathbb{E}Z_{m}^{\top}=h_{1}^{d}O(1)\to 0
.
Consequently,

Mh_{1}^{d}\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat{T})\to\Omega_{\rho}(t,x;\xi).

(123)

Now define the centered triangular-array variables

Y_{m,M}:=\sqrt{\frac{h_{1}^{d}}{M}}\ (Z_{m}-\mathbb{E}Z_{m}),\ m=1,\dots,M
.
Then

\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\ (\hat{T}-\mathbb{E}\hat{T})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}Y_{m,M}
.
Because
X_{s},X_{u}
are supported on the bounded set
B\times B
, the weights
U(X_{u})
and
V(X_{u})

are bounded, and
K
is bounded with compact support, there exists
C_{Z}<\infty
such that

|Z_{m}|\leq C_{Z}\big(h_{1}^{-d}+h_{2}^{-d}\big)
.
Since
h_{2}/h_{1}\to\rho\in(0,\infty)
, we have
h_{2}^{-d}\lesssim h_{1}^{-d}
, and hence

|Y_{m,M}|\leq C\ \frac{\sqrt{h_{1}^{d}/M}}{h_{1}^{d}}=\frac{C}{\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}}\to 0.

(124)

Therefore, for every
\varepsilon>0
, the Lindeberg condition holds:

\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\!\left[|Y_{m,M}|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|Y_{m,M}|>\varepsilon\}}\right]\to 0
.
By the multivariate Lindeberg–Feller theorem,

\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\ (\hat{T}-\mathbb{E}\hat{T})\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal{N}\!\bigl(0,\Omega_{\rho}(t,x;\xi)\bigr)
.

By definition,

\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)=\Gamma_{t}(\hat{T}),\ a^{*}(t,x;\xi)=\Gamma_{t}(T)
.
Since

g_{1}(t,x;\xi)=f(\xi)D^{*}(t,x;\xi)
,
f(\xi)\geq f_{\min}>0
,
D^{*}(t,x;\xi)\geq D_{\min}>0

by
Section

3.1
, the point

T=\bigl(g_{2}(t,x;\xi)^{\top},g_{1}(t,x;\xi),f(\xi),f(\xi)\bigr)^{\top}

(125)

lies in the open set
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
, on which
\Gamma_{t}
is

C^{1}
. Therefore, by the multivariate delta method,

\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\ \bigl(\Gamma_{t}(\hat{T})-\Gamma_{t}(\mathbb{E}\hat{T})\bigr)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal{N}\!\bigl(0,\Sigma_{\rho}(t,x;\xi)\bigr),

(126)

with

\Sigma_{\rho}(t,x;\xi)=J_{\Gamma}(t,x;\xi)\ \Omega_{\rho}(t,x;\xi)\ J_{\Gamma}(t,x;\xi)^{\top}
.

Finally,

\displaystyle\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\ \bigl(\hat{a}(t,x;\xi)-a^{*}(t,x;\xi)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\ \bigl(\Gamma_{t}(\hat{T})-\Gamma_{t}(\mathbb{E}\hat{T})\bigr)

(127)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{Mh_{1}^{d}}\ \bigl(\Gamma_{t}(\mathbb{E}\hat{T})-\Gamma_{t}(T)\bigr).

(128)

The first term converges to
\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{\rho}(t,x;\xi))
by the delta-method argument above,
and the second vanishes by the bias bound. This proves the claim.
∎

Let

(151)

Corollary E.3

(Unequal-bandwidth adaptive upper bound)
.

If
\mathcal{H}_{j}=\{h_{j,0}2^{-k}:k=0,1,\dots,K_{j}\}

and

h_{j,K_{j}}\asymp M^{-1/d}
for

, then, for all sufficiently large

,

\sup_{\nu\in\mathfrak{P}(\beta,L_{p},f_{\min},B)}\mathcal{R}_{\nu}\bigl(\hat{a}_{\hat{h}_{1},\hat{h}_{2}}\bigr)\lesssim\Big(\tfrac{\log M}{M}\Big)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

(166)

Proof.

By
Theorem

E.1
, it suffices to optimize each blockwise term

h^{\beta}+\sqrt{\frac{\log M}{Mh^{d}}}+\frac{\log M}{Mh^{d}}

(167)

over the geometric grid. Since the grid spans from order
1
down to order

, it contains a
bandwidth of order

h_{j}^{\star}\asymp(\log M/M)^{1/(2\beta+d)}
,

,
up to a multiplicative constant depending only on the grid ratio. At this scale, the linear term

is of strictly smaller order than

(h_{j}^{\star})^{\beta}\asymp\sqrt{\log M/(M(h_{j}^{\star})^{d})}

Therefore both infima in

.

Here and throughout Appendix

In this Appendix we first prove a pivot-local lower-bound statement and then derive a global minimax lower bound by instantiating it at an explicit uniform pivot. The proof uses a standard two-point Le Cam construction

[
17
,
31
,
28
]
embedded into a shrinking neighborhood of the pivot, intersected with the

Proposition F.1

(Local minimax lower bound around an interior pivot)
.

Fix

(t^{\circ},x^{\circ},\xi^{\circ})\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}
.
Let
\nu_{0}\in\mathfrak{P}(\beta,L_{p},f_{\min},B)
be a pivot law with joint density
p_{0}
.
Assume that there exist constants
\delta_{f},\delta_{D},\delta_{H}>0
such that

\inf_{\xi\in B}f_{0}(\xi)\geq f_{\min}+2\delta_{f},\qquad\inf_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}D_{0}^{*}(t,x;\xi)\geq D_{\min}+2\delta_{D},

(168)

and
\sup_{y\in B}|\bar{p}_{0}(\cdot,y)|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq L_{p}-2\delta_{H}
.

For
Q>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, define

\mathcal{U}_{M}(\nu_{0};Q,\varepsilon):=\left\{\nu:p_{\nu}=p_{0}+g,\ \sup_{y\in B}|g(\cdot,y)|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq Q,\ |g|_{L^{\infty}(B\times B)}\leq\varepsilon\right\},

(169)

and the local class inside the global model by

\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{loc},M}^{\cap}(\nu_{0};Q,\varepsilon):=\mathfrak{P}(\beta,L_{p},f_{\min},B)\cap\mathcal{U}_{M}(\nu_{0};Q,\varepsilon).

(170)

Then there exist constants
Q_{0}>0
,
c_{\mathrm{loc}}>0
, and
c>0
, depending on
\nu_{0}
and the
fixed model parameters, such that for all sufficiently large
M
,

\inf_{\tilde{a}}\ \sup_{\nu\in\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{loc},M}^{\cap}\!\bigl(\nu_{0};Q_{0},c_{\mathrm{loc}}M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}\bigr)}\ \mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\tilde{a})\ \geq\ c\,M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

(171)

In particular, combined with Corollary
E.2
, the selected estimator is locally minimax-optimal around
\nu_{0}
for the uniform risk
\mathcal{R}_{\nu}
, up to logarithmic factors; the lower bound is witnessed by the fixed interior evaluation point
(t^{\circ},x^{\circ},\xi^{\circ})
.

Proof.

Write

F^{\circ}(y):=F(t^{\circ},\xi^{\circ},x^{\circ},y),\ \mu_{0}(dy):=p_{0}(\xi^{\circ},y)\,dy
.
Since
f_{0}(\xi^{\circ})>0
, the measure
\mu_{0}
is a nonzero finite measure on
B
, absolutely
continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure.

1.

Let

S:=\operatorname{span}\{1,F^{\circ}\}\subset L^{2}(\mu_{0})
.
For each
r\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
define

g_{r}(y):=y_{r}F^{\circ}(y)
.
We claim that there exists
r
such that

g_{r}\notin S\qquad\text{in }L^{2}(\mu_{0}).

(172)

Suppose otherwise. Then for every
r
there exist
a_{r},b_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

y_{r}F^{\circ}(y)=a_{r}+b_{r}F^{\circ}(y)\ \mu_{0}
-a.e. on
B
.
Equivalently,

h_{r}(y):=(y_{r}-b_{r})F^{\circ}(y)-a_{r}=0\ \mu_{0}
-a.e. on
B
.
Since
\mu_{0}
is absolutely continuous and nonzero,
h_{r}
vanishes on a set of positive Lebesgue
measure. The function
h_{r}
is real analytic in
y
because
F^{\circ}
is real analytic. A nontrivial
real-analytic function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
cannot vanish on a set of positive Lebesgue measure, so
h_{r}
must
vanish identically. This is impossible: if
a_{r}=0
, then
y_{r}\equiv b_{r}
, contradiction; if

a_{r}\neq 0
, then

F^{\circ}(y)=a_{r}/(y_{r}-b_{r})
,
but the right-hand side is singular on the hyperplane
y_{r}=b_{r}
, whereas
F^{\circ}
is smooth and
strictly positive. This contradiction proves the claim.

Fix such a coordinate
r
. Let
\Pi_{S}
denote the orthogonal projection in
L^{2}(\mu_{0})
onto
S
,
and define

\varphi_{0}:=g_{r}-\Pi_{S}g_{r}
.
Then
\varphi_{0}\neq 0
,
\varphi_{0}\in S^{\perp}
, and
\varphi_{0}\in L^{\infty}(B)
because
g_{r}
and

F^{\circ}
are bounded on the bounded set
B
. Set

\varphi:=\varphi_{0}/(2|\varphi_{0}|_{L^{\infty}(B)})
.
Then
|\varphi|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq 1/2
, and since
\varphi\in S^{\perp}
,

\int_{B}\varphi(y)\ p_{0}(\xi^{\circ},y)\ dy=0,\qquad\int_{B}F^{\circ}(y)\ \varphi(y)\ p_{0}(\xi^{\circ},y)\ dy=0.

(173)

Moreover,

\int_{B}y_{r}F^{\circ}(y)\ \varphi(y)\ p_{0}(\xi^{\circ},y)\ dy=\frac{|\varphi_{0}|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0})}^{2}}{2|\varphi_{0}|_{L^{\infty}(B)}}>0.

(174)

Hence the vector

m^{\circ}:=\int_{B}y\ F^{\circ}(y)\ \varphi(y)\ p_{0}(\xi^{\circ},y)\ dy

is nonzero.

2.

Choose any nonzero
\psi\in C_{c}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported in

B(0,1)
with
\psi(0)=1
, and set

C_{\psi}:=|\psi|_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty

Since
\xi^{\circ}\in\operatorname{int}(B)

h_{0}>0
such that

\operatorname{supp}(\psi_{h})\subset B
for all
0<h\leq h_{0}
, where

\psi_{h}(\xi):=\psi\!\left((\xi-\xi^{\circ})/h\right)

Set

h:=M^{-1/(2\beta+d)},\ \alpha:=a_{0}h^{\beta}
,
with
a_{0}>0
to be chosen small later. For all sufficiently large
M

h\leq h_{0}
. Define

u_{h}(\xi,y):=\alpha\,\psi_{h}(\xi)\varphi(y)
,
and the unnormalized perturbations

\tilde{p}_{\pm}(\xi,y):=p_{0}(\xi,y)\bigl(1\pm u_{h}(\xi,y)\bigr)
.
Since
|\varphi|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq 1/2
, if
a_{0}
is sufficiently small then

|u_{h}(\xi,y)|\leq\frac{1}{4}
on
B\times B
,
so
\tilde{p}_{\pm}\geq\frac{3}{4}\,p_{0}\geq 0
.

Let

m_{h}:=\iint_{B\times B}p_{0}(\xi,y)\,u_{h}(\xi,y)\ d\xi\,dy
.
Because
p_{0}
is bounded on
B\times B
,
\varphi
is bounded, and
\psi_{h}
is supported on a set of
volume of order
h^{d}
, we have

|m_{h}|\lesssim\alpha h^{d}
.
Hence

c_{\pm}:=\iint_{B\times B}\tilde{p}_{\pm}(\xi,y)\ d\xi\,dy=1\pm m_{h}

satisfy
c_{\pm}\to 1
. Define the normalized densities

p_{\pm}(\xi,y):=\frac{\tilde{p}_{\pm}(\xi,y)}{c_{\pm}}=p_{0}(\xi,y)\,\frac{1\pm u_{h}(\xi,y)}{1\pm m_{h}},

(175)

and let
\nu_{\pm}
be the corresponding laws.

3.

Define

g_{\pm}:=p_{\pm}-p_{0}

Then

g_{\pm}(\xi,y)=p_{0}(\xi,y)\,\frac{\pm(u_{h}(\xi,y)-m_{h})}{1\pm m_{h}}

|g_{\pm}|_{L^{\infty}(B\times B)}\lesssim|u_{h}|_{L^{\infty}(B\times B)}+|m_{h}|\lesssim\alpha\asymp M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

(176)

For the
C^{\beta}
control in the first coordinate, write

g_{\pm}(\cdot,y)=\frac{p_{0}(\cdot,y)}{1\pm m_{h}}\Bigl(\pm\alpha\varphi(y)\psi_{h}(\cdot)\mp m_{h}\Bigr)

Using the standard product estimate for Hölder spaces on bounded sets,

|fg|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq C|f|_{C^{\beta}(B)}|g|_{C^{\beta}(B)}
,
and the scaling bound

|\psi_{h}|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq CC_{\psi}h^{-\beta}

\alpha=a_{0}h^{\beta}

\alpha|\psi_{h}|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq Ca_{0}C_{\psi}

The

.
Consequently,

\sup_{y\in B}|g_{\pm}(\cdot,y)|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq Ca_{0}C_{\psi}
.
Choose
Q_{0}>0
larger than this constant.

4.

Because

|f_{\pm}(\xi)-f_{0}(\xi)|\leq\int_{B}|g_{\pm}(\xi,y)|\,dy\leq\lambda(B)|g_{\pm}|_{L^{\infty}(B\times B)}\lesssim\alpha
,
and the assumption of the pivot law

\inf_{\xi\in B}f_{0}(\xi)\geq f_{\min}+2\delta_{f}

for all sufficiently large

\inf_{\xi\in B}f_{\pm}(\xi)\geq f_{\min}.

(177)

Similarly, define

g_{1,\pm}(t,x;\xi):=\int_{B}F(t,\xi,x,y)\ p_{\pm}(\xi,y)\ dy,\qquad g_{1,0}(t,x;\xi):=\int_{B}F(t,\xi,x,y)\ p_{0}(\xi,y)\ dy.

(178)

Because
F
is continuous on the compact set

[s,u-\eta]\times B_{R}\times B\times B
, there exists

C_{F,Q}:=\sup_{t\in[s,u-\eta],\,x\in B_{R},\,\xi\in B,\,y\in B}F(t,\xi,x,y)<\infty.

(179)

Hence

|g_{1,\pm}(t,x;\xi)-g_{1,0}(t,x;\xi)|\leq C_{F,Q}\lambda(B)|g_{\pm}|_{L^{\infty}(B\times B)}\lesssim\alpha

uniformly on
Q_{R,\eta,\rho}
.
Since
f_{0}
and
f_{\pm}
are bounded below away from zero for all sufficiently large
M
, the
deterministic ratio inequality gives

\sup_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}|D_{\pm}^{*}(t,x;\xi)-D_{0}^{*}(t,x;\xi)|\lesssim\alpha
.
As

\inf_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}D_{0}^{*}(t,x;\xi)\geq D_{\min}+2\delta_{D}
,
for all sufficiently large
M
we obtain

\inf_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}D_{\pm}^{*}(t,x;\xi)\geq D_{\min}.

(180)

Finally, since

\sup_{y\in B}|p_{0}(\cdot,y)|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq L_{p}-2\delta_{H}\ \text{and}\ \sup_{y\in B}|g_{\pm}(\cdot,y)|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq Ca_{0}C_{\psi}
,
choosing
a_{0}
sufficiently small yields

\sup_{y\in B}|p_{\pm}(\cdot,y)|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq L_{p}-\delta_{H}<L_{p}
.
Thus
\nu_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{P}(\beta,L_{p},f_{\min},B)
for all sufficiently large
M
.

Consequently, for
c_{\mathrm{loc}}
sufficiently large and all sufficiently large
M
,

\{\nu_{+},\nu_{-}\}\subset\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{loc},M}^{\cap}\!\bigl(\nu_{0};Q_{0},c_{\mathrm{loc}}M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}\bigr).

(181)

5.

At
(t^{\circ},x^{\circ},\xi^{\circ})
, write

g_{1,\pm}^{\circ}:=\int_{B}F^{\circ}(y)\ p_{\pm}(\xi^{\circ},y)\ dy,\ g_{2,\pm}^{\circ}:=\int_{B}y\ F^{\circ}(y)\ p_{\pm}(\xi^{\circ},y)\ dy
.
Since
\psi_{h}(\xi^{\circ})=1
, using (
173
) and (
174
) we obtain

g_{1,\pm}^{\circ}=\frac{1}{1\pm m_{h}}\int_{B}F^{\circ}(y)\ p_{0}(\xi^{\circ},y)\bigl(1\pm\alpha\varphi(y)\bigr)\ dy=\frac{g_{1,0}^{\circ}}{1\pm m_{h}},

(182)

and

g_{2,\pm}^{\circ}=\frac{1}{1\pm m_{h}}\int_{B}y\ F^{\circ}(y)\ p_{0}(\xi^{\circ},y)\bigl(1\pm\alpha\varphi(y)\bigr)\ dy=\frac{g_{2,0}^{\circ}\pm\alpha m^{\circ}}{1\pm m_{h}},

(183)

where

g_{1,0}^{\circ}:=\int_{B}F^{\circ}(y)\ p_{0}(\xi^{\circ},y)\ dy,\ g_{2,0}^{\circ}:=\int_{B}y\ F^{\circ}(y)\ p_{0}(\xi^{\circ},y)\ dy
.
Therefore the common normalization cancels:

Q_{\pm}^{\circ}:=\frac{g_{2,\pm}^{\circ}}{g_{1,\pm}^{\circ}}=\frac{g_{2,0}^{\circ}\pm\alpha m^{\circ}}{g_{1,0}^{\circ}}.\quad\text{Hence}\quad Q_{+}^{\circ}-Q_{-}^{\circ}=\frac{2\alpha\ m^{\circ}}{g_{1,0}^{\circ}},

(184)

and therefore

\big|a_{+}^{*}(t^{\circ},x^{\circ};\xi^{\circ})-a_{-}^{*}(t^{\circ},x^{\circ};\xi^{\circ})\big|=\frac{1}{\Delta(t^{\circ})}\ |Q_{+}^{\circ}-Q_{-}^{\circ}|\geq c_{1}\ \alpha=c_{1}a_{0}h^{\beta},

(185)

where
c_{1}>0
depends only on the fixed model parameters, since
\Delta(t^{\circ})\geq\eta
and

g_{1,0}^{\circ}=f_{0}(\xi^{\circ})D_{0}^{*}(t^{\circ},x^{\circ};\xi^{\circ})>0.

(186)

6.

Using the bound

\mathrm{KL}(\nu_{+}\mid\nu_{-})\leq\chi^{2}(\nu_{+},\nu_{-}):=\iint_{B\times B}\frac{(p_{+}-p_{-})^{2}}{p_{-}}
,
we estimate the one-sample divergence. Since

p_{+}-p_{-}=p_{0}\left(\frac{1+u_{h}}{1+m_{h}}-\frac{1-u_{h}}{1-m_{h}}\right)=\frac{2p_{0}(u_{h}-m_{h})}{1-m_{h}^{2}},

(187)

and since
|u_{h}|\leq 1/4
and
|m_{h}|\lesssim\alpha h^{d}
for all sufficiently large
M
, we have

p_{-}(\xi,y)=p_{0}(\xi,y)\frac{1-u_{h}(\xi,y)}{1-m_{h}}\geq c\,p_{0}(\xi,y)

(188)

for some absolute constant
c>0
. Therefore

\chi^{2}(\nu_{+},\nu_{-})\leq C\iint_{B\times B}p_{0}(\xi,y)\,(u_{h}(\xi,y)-m_{h})^{2}\ d\xi dy
.
Since
m_{h}=\iint p_{0}u_{h}
and
\iint p_{0}=1
, Jensen’s inequality gives

m_{h}^{2}\leq\iint p_{0}u_{h}^{2}
,
and hence

\iint p_{0}(u_{h}-m_{h})^{2}\leq 2\iint p_{0}u_{h}^{2}+2m_{h}^{2}\lesssim\iint p_{0}u_{h}^{2}.

(189)

Now
p_{0}
is bounded on
B\times B
,
\varphi
is bounded, and
\psi_{h}
is supported on a set of

\xi
-volume of order
h^{d}
, so

\iint_{B\times B}p_{0}(\xi,y)u_{h}(\xi,y)^{2}\ d\xi dy\lesssim\alpha^{2}h^{d}.\quad\text{Thus}\quad\mathrm{KL}(\nu_{+}\mid\nu_{-})\lesssim\alpha^{2}h^{d}.

(190)

Hence for
M
i.i.d. observations,

\mathrm{KL}(\nu_{+}^{\otimes M}|\nu_{-}^{\otimes M})\lesssim M\ \alpha^{2}h^{d}=M\ a_{0}^{2}\ h^{d+2\beta}.

(191)

With
h=M^{-1/(2\beta+d)}
, the right-hand side of (
191
) is bounded by a universal
constant if
a_{0}
is chosen small enough. By Le Cam’s two-point lemma,

\inf_{\tilde{a}}\ \sup_{\nu\in\{\nu_{+},\nu_{-}\}}\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\!\left[\big|\tilde{a}(t^{\circ},x^{\circ};\xi^{\circ})-a_{\nu}^{*}(t^{\circ},x^{\circ};\xi^{\circ})\big|\right]\geq c_{2}\ \big|a_{+}^{*}(t^{\circ},x^{\circ};\xi^{\circ})-a_{-}^{*}(t^{\circ},x^{\circ};\xi^{\circ})\big|.

(192)

Since the risk
\mathcal{R}_{\nu}
dominates the pointwise risk at
(t^{\circ},x^{\circ},\xi^{\circ})
,

\mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\tilde{a})\geq\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\!\left[\big|\tilde{a}(t^{\circ},x^{\circ};\xi^{\circ})-a_{\nu}^{*}(t^{\circ},x^{\circ};\xi^{\circ})\big|\right],

(193)

and since (
181
) holds, we conclude from (
185
) that

\inf_{\tilde{a}}\ \sup_{\nu\in\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{loc},M}^{\cap}\!\bigl(\nu_{0};Q_{0},c_{\mathrm{loc}}M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}\bigr)}\ \mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\tilde{a})\geq\inf_{\tilde{a}}\ \sup_{\nu\in\{\nu_{+},\nu_{-}\}}\ \mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\tilde{a})\gtrsim h^{\beta}\asymp M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)},

(194)

which proves the claim.
∎

Write

Corollary F.2

(Global minimax lower bound via an explicit uniform pivot)
.

Let
R_{B}:=\sup_{z\in B}|z|<\infty
and let

\mathcal{P}:=\mathfrak{P}(\beta,L_{p},f_{\min},B)
be the global model class from

Section

3.1
. Assume further that
f_{\min}<|B|^{-1}
and
L_{p}>|B|^{-2}
.
Then there exists a constant
c>0
, depending only on the fixed model parameters,
such that for all sufficiently large
M
,

\inf_{\widetilde{a}}\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\widetilde{a})\geq c\,M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

(195)

Consequently, together with
Theorem

4.9
,

\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\hat{a}_{\widehat{h}})\lesssim\left(\frac{\log M}{M}\right)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)},

(196)

so the adaptive estimator is globally minimax-rate optimal over
\mathcal{P}
up to logarithmic factors.

Proof.

Fix any
(t^{\circ},x^{\circ},\xi^{\circ})\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}
. Define the uniform pivot law
\nu_{0}
on

B\times B
by the joint density

p_{0}(\xi,y)=\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{B}(\xi)\mathbf{1}_{B}(y).

(197)

Then its
X_{s}
-marginal density is

f_{0}(\xi)=\int_{B}p_{0}(\xi,y)\,dy=\frac{1}{|B|},\ \xi\in B
.
Since
f_{\min}<|B|^{-1}
, we may choose

\delta_{f}:=\frac{1}{4}\bigl(|B|^{-1}-f_{\min}\bigr)>0
,
so that

\inf_{\xi\in B}f_{0}(\xi)=|B|^{-1}\geq f_{\min}+2\delta_{f}
.

Next, for each
y\in B
, the map
\xi\mapsto p_{0}(\xi,y)
is constant on
B
. A valid extension to a neighborhood of
B
is

\bar{p}_{0}(z,y)=\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}
.
With the
C^{\beta}(B)
norm of
Appendix

F
,

\sup_{y\in B}|\bar{p}_{0}(\cdot,y)|_{C^{\beta}(B)}=|B|^{-2}
.

Since
L_{p}>|B|^{-2}
, we choose

\delta_{H}:=\frac{1}{4}\bigl(L_{p}-|B|^{-2}\bigr)>0
, then

\sup_{y\in B}|\bar{p}_{0}(\cdot,y)|_{C^{\beta}(B)}\leq L_{p}-2\delta_{H}
.

Under the uniform pivot,

D_{0}^{*}(t,x;\xi)=\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{0}}\!\left[F(t,\xi,x,X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi\right]=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}F(t,\xi,x,y)\,dy.

(198)

Define
D_{\mathrm{unif}}:=\inf_{(t,x,\xi)\in Q_{R,\eta,\rho}}D_{0}^{*}(t,x;\xi)
. Moreover, by the automatic denominator bound following
Section

3.1
,

D_{\mathrm{unif}}\geq 2D_{\min}
, so the uniform pivot satisfies the required denominator slack.

It remains to note that
D_{\mathrm{unif}}>0
. Indeed, on
Q_{R,\eta,\rho}
we have
\Delta(t)\geq\eta
, and for
x\in B_{R}
,
y\in B
,

|y-x|\leq|y|+|x|\leq R_{B}+R
.
Since the expanding term in the exponent is nonnegative,

F(t,\xi,x,y)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2\Delta(t)}+\frac{|y-\xi|^{2}}{2\Delta}\right)\geq\exp\!\left(-\frac{(R_{B}+R)^{2}}{2\eta}\right).

(199)

Hence

D_{\mathrm{unif}}\geq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(R_{B}+R)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\,dy=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(R_{B}+R)^{2}}{2\eta}\right)>0.

(200)

Thus the explicit uniform pivot
\nu_{0}
satisfies the interiority/slack conditions of
Appendix

F
. Therefore there exist constants
Q_{0}>0
,
c_{\mathrm{loc}}>0
, and
c>0
such that, for all sufficiently large
M
,

\inf_{\widetilde{a}}\sup_{\nu\in\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{loc},M}^{\cap}(\nu_{0};Q_{0},c_{\mathrm{loc}}M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)})}\mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\widetilde{a})\geq c\,M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

(201)

By construction,

\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{loc},M}^{\cap}(\nu_{0};Q_{0},c_{\mathrm{loc}}M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)})\subset\mathcal{P}.

(202)

Hence

\inf_{\widetilde{a}}\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\widetilde{a})\geq\inf_{\widetilde{a}}\sup_{\nu\in\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{loc},M}^{\cap}(\nu_{0};Q_{0},c_{\mathrm{loc}}M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)})}\mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\widetilde{a})\geq c\,M^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}.

(203)

This proves the global minimax lower bound.

G.4
Bandwidth grid, oracle rule, and practical selector

All rate and adaptive experiments are run on a geometric grid
\mathcal{H}_{M}=\{h_{0}q^{k}:k=0,\dots,K_{M}\}
with
q=2^{-1/2}
and
h_{0}=1.2
. The oracle bandwidth is

h_{\rm or}\in\arg\min_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{M}}E_{\infty,\mathcal{G}}(t_{0},\xi_{0};h),

(213)

with ties broken in favor of the larger bandwidth.

The practical selector used for all quantitative claims in the paper is the raw-max, one-sided GL surrogate

|\hat{a}_{h^{\prime}}-\hat{a}_{h}|_{\infty,\mathcal{G}}:=\max_{x\in\mathcal{G}_{x}}|\hat{a}_{h^{\prime}}(t_{0},x;\xi_{0})-\hat{a}_{h}(t_{0},x;\xi_{0})|,

(214)

B(h):=\sup_{h^{\prime}\leq h}\left\{|\hat{a}_{h^{\prime}}-\hat{a}_{h}|_{\infty,\mathcal{G}}-\kappa_{\mathrm{pair}}\sqrt{\frac{\log M}{M(h^{\prime})^{d}}}\right\}_{+},\qquad\hat{h}:=\arg\min_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{M}}\left\{B(h)+\kappa_{\mathrm{final}}\sqrt{\frac{\log M}{Mh^{d}}}\right\},

(215)

with
\kappa_{\mathrm{pair}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{final}}=2
.

Why this selector.

Write

\hat{a}_{h}:=\hat{a}_{h,h},\qquad v_{0}(h):=\sqrt{\frac{\log M}{Mh^{d}}},\qquad b(h):=Ah^{\beta},

(216)

and

E(h):=|\hat{a}_{h}-a^{*}|_{\infty,\mathcal{G}}
.
The implemented diagonal selector uses

B(h)=\sup_{h^{\prime}\leq h}\left\{|\hat{a}_{h^{\prime}}-\hat{a}_{h}|_{\infty,\mathcal{G}}-2v_{0}(h^{\prime})\right\}_{+}.

(217)

On the good event, suppose

E(h)\leq b(h)+v_{0}(h)
.
For
h^{\prime}\leq h
,

v_{0}(h^{\prime})\geq v_{0}(h),\qquad b(h^{\prime})\leq b(h),

(218)

and therefore

|\hat{a}_{h^{\prime}}-\hat{a}_{h}|_{\infty,\mathcal{G}}\leq E(h^{\prime})+E(h)\leq b(h^{\prime})+b(h)+v_{0}(h^{\prime})+v_{0}(h)
.

Since
2v_{0}(h^{\prime})\geq v_{0}(h^{\prime})+v_{0}(h)
,

|\hat{a}_{h^{\prime}}-\hat{a}_{h}|_{\infty,\mathcal{G}}-2v_{0}(h^{\prime})\leq b(h^{\prime})+b(h)\leq 2b(h).

(219)

Hence

B(h)\leq 2b(h)
.
By the minimizing property of
\hat{h}
, for every
u\in\mathcal{H}_{M}
,

B(\hat{h})+2v_{0}(\hat{h})\leq B(u)+2v_{0}(u).

(220)

Using the standard one-sided GL comparison,

E(\hat{h})\leq 2\{B(u)+2v_{0}(u)\}+E(u).

(221)

Consequently,

E(\hat{h})\leq 2\{2b(u)+2v_{0}(u)\}+b(u)+v_{0}(u)=5\{b(u)+v_{0}(u)\}.

(222)

Taking the infimum over
u\in\mathcal{H}_{M}
, the practical selector tracks
the diagonal oracle objective up to the multiplicative constant
5
.
The appendix oracle inequality uses the full proof-side penalty with
constants, grid-union logarithms, and lower-order linear terms; the
experiments use this leading-order diagonal proxy.

Why the lower bandwidth floor.

The crucial experimental issue is not the selector form itself but the small-
h
tail of the admissible grid. The stochastic scale entering both the selector penalty and the local denominator estimation is of order
(Mh^{d})^{-1/2}
up to logarithms. If
h
is too small, then
Mh^{d}
is too small, so pairwise discrepancies become excessively noisy and the ratio estimator becomes more fragile because its local denominator is supported by too little effective mass. To prevent the selector from entering this regime, we truncate the grid below at

h_{\min}(M,d)=c_{\min}M^{-1/d},

(223)

with

c_{\min}=81\quad\text{in }d=1,\qquad c_{\min}=9\quad\text{in }d=2.

(224)

These choices are dimensionally matched because in both cases

Mh_{\min}(M,d)^{d}=c_{\min}^{d}=81.

(225)

Hence the entire adaptive search is restricted to

Mh^{d}\geq 81.

(226)

This gives a uniform finite-sample lower bound on the effective local sample size across dimensions. In exploratory runs, allowing smaller bandwidths was the dominant source of instability in the hard MM cases; after imposing
Mh^{d}\geq 81
, the selector became stable on all four testbeds.

G.6
Pointwise CLT and confidence intervals

The main-text CLT experiment is one-dimensional. For a fixed interior query
(t_{0},x_{0},\xi_{0})
, let

\psi_{t_{0},x_{0},\xi_{0}}(y)=\bigl(y-x_{0}-\Delta(t_{0})a^{\ast}(t_{0},x_{0};\xi_{0})\bigr)F(t_{0},\xi_{0},x_{0},y).

(227)

In the diagonal bandwidth case, write
\hat{f}_{h}:=\hat{f}_{1}=\hat{f}_{2}
and
\hat{D}_{h}(t,x;\xi):=\hat{g}_{1}(t,x;\xi)/\hat{f}_{h}(\xi)
. The plug-in variance estimator is

\hat{\Sigma}(t_{0},x_{0};\xi_{0})=\frac{R(K)}{\hat{f}_{h}(\xi_{0})\Delta(t_{0})^{2}\hat{D}_{h}(t_{0},x_{0};\xi_{0})^{2}}\left(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[\hat{\psi}(X_{u})^{2}\mid X_{s}=\xi_{0}]-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[\hat{\psi}(X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi_{0}]^{2}\right),

(228)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}(y)=\bigl(y-x_{0}-\Delta(t_{0})\hat{a}_{h}(t_{0},x_{0};\xi_{0})\bigr)F(t_{0},\xi_{0},x_{0},y),

(229)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(X_{u})\mid X_{s}=\xi_{0}]=\frac{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\phi(X_{u}^{(m)})K_{h}(X_{s}^{(m)}-\xi_{0})}{\hat{f}_{h}(\xi_{0})}.

(230)

The CLT bandwidth must satisfy
Mh_{M}^{d}\to\infty
and
h_{M}^{\beta}\sqrt{Mh_{M}^{d}}\to 0
. We therefore use genuinely undersmoothed sequences of the form
h_{M}=cM^{-\alpha}
with
(2\beta+d)^{-1}<\alpha<d^{-1}
. In the final reported one-dimensional runs we first screen the candidate exponents
\alpha\in\{0.22,0.24,0.26,0.28\}
on
M\in\{4\times 10^{3},8\times 10^{3}\}
with
R_{M}=150
, then fix the final rule at
\alpha=0.22
for GG1 and
\alpha=0.28
for MM1, both with
c=1.0
. For repetition
r
, the standardized statistic is

Z_{M}^{(r)}=\frac{\sqrt{Mh_{M}^{d}}\bigl(\hat{a}_{h_{M}}^{(r)}(t_{0},x_{0};\xi_{0})-a^{\ast}(t_{0},x_{0};\xi_{0})\bigr)}{\sqrt{\hat{\Sigma}^{(r)}(t_{0},x_{0};\xi_{0})}}.

(231)

The final CLT runs use
R_{M}=300
repetitions at each
M\in\{10^{3},2\times 10^{3},4\times 10^{3},8\times 10^{3}\}
. For each sample size we record the empirical mean
\bar{Z}_{M}
, empirical variance
\widehat{\text{Var}}(Z_{M})
, the empirical coverage of

\hat{a}_{h_{M}}^{(r)}(t_{0},x_{0};\xi_{0})\pm 1.96\sqrt{\hat{\Sigma}^{(r)}(t_{0},x_{0};\xi_{0})/(Mh_{M}^{d})},

(232)

and formal Shapiro–Wilk / Anderson–Darling diagnostics of the standardized replicates. QQ-plots against
N(0,1)
are saved for the two largest sample sizes and are used as the main visual CLT diagnostic in the paper.

G.8
Bounded-support stress test

To probe the proof role of bounded support, we run a one-dimensional stress test at the fixed interior queries GG1:
(t_{0},x_{0},\xi_{0})=(0.6,0.2,0)
and MM1:
(0.6,0.3,0.8)
, with the same canonical selector as in the final adaptive runs (raw-max, one-sided,
\kappa_{\mathrm{pair}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{final}}=2
, and the same lower-bandwidth floor), at the fixed sample size
M_{\mathrm{stress}}=4000
with
R_{\mathrm{stress}}=100
repetitions. For GG1, the wide-support variant changes only the truncation box from
[-3,3]
to
[-5,5]
. For MM1, the wide-support variant also inflates the source and conditional variances from

to

, while keeping the same means, linear maps, and logistic gate.

\hat{h}

G.9
Implementation notes and optional selector variants

The implementation is organized around a small set of core modules and driver scripts. The synthetic pair laws are defined in
src/sbdrift/models.py
; deterministic truth evaluation is implemented in
src/sbdrift/truth_engine.py
; the kernel estimator is implemented in
src/sbdrift/estimator.py
; and the kernel definitions are collected in
src/sbdrift/kernels.py
. The experiment drivers are
scripts/00_preflight.py
,
scripts/01_rate.py
, and
scripts/02_clt.py
. The pre-flight script writes numerical checks of the marginal density, denominator floor, truth-convergence probe, and sampling sanity checks. The rate script writes raw per-bandwidth and selected-bandwidth CSV files, processed summaries, and rate plots. The CLT script writes per-repetition pointwise statistics, processed summaries, and per-
M
QQ-plot files, and also records the undersmoothing-screen runs together with the formal normality diagnostics used to choose the final one-dimensional CLT bandwidths. All experiments are implemented in
Python
using
NumPy
,
SciPy
,
pandas
, and
matplotlib
.

Each run is driven by a YAML configuration file specifying the synthetic family, support and evaluation boxes, query points, bandwidth grid parameters, repetition counts, and random seeds. The rate pipeline caches deterministic truth values on the evaluation grid to avoid recomputation across repeated runs. Outputs are written to the standard experiment directories
results/raw/
,
results/processed/
, and
results/figures/
. The repository state used for the paper contains the processed pre-flight summaries, the final theorem-facing rate and CLT summaries, the undersmoothing-screen and final CLT runs, the final stabilized adaptive-selector summaries, and the figure files corresponding to the saved experiment outputs.

The codebase also retains optional alternative selector implementations, including trimmed-max, two-sided, and ISE-based discrepancy rules. These remain available for users interested in separate comparison studies, but they are not used for the paper’s quantitative claims. Accordingly, the reproducibility claim supported by the current codebase is the following: the repository contains the exact synthetic-model definitions, the deterministic truth engine, the kernel estimator implementation, the pre-flight/rate/CLT experiment drivers, the YAML configuration files, and the saved raw, processed, and figure outputs used to assemble the final tables and theorem-facing plots. Exact command lines, random seeds, repetition counts, the selected undersmoothing exponents, the stabilized bandwidth-floor rule, and run-specific parameter choices are recorded in the experiment logs and processed summaries.
