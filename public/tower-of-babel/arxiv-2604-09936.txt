Resolvent estimates for the Schrödinger operator with L ∞ L^{\infty} electric and magnetic potentials and applications to the local energy decay

Abstract
. The present paper extends the exponential local energy decay results obtained in
[
13
]
to a broader class of electric and magnetic potentials. In
[
13
]
, the coefficients were assumed to decay exponentially, which permitted analytic continuation of the weighted resolvent, weighted by exponentially decaying weights, and led to exponential decay of the local energy. In this work we replace the exponential weight by the more general weight
\mu(x)=\sqrt{\Theta(c\langle x\rangle)}
, where the function
\Theta
satisfies the conditions (
1.7
) through (
1.10
); note in particular the conditions on the sequence
{m_{k}}
given by (
1.9
) and (
1.10
). The condition (
1.9
) is used in Section
2
to obtain the uniform bounds (
2.1
) on derivatives of the free weighted resolvent. The condition (
1.10
) ensures stability of these bounds under products and inverses of operator-valued functions, which is used in Section
4
for passing from the free resolvent to the perturbed resolvent. The decay rate in time is then determined by the growth of
m_{k}
via the above function
k(t)
.

We note that for each
\delta>0
, cutoff function appearing (
1.13
) and (
1.14
) is independent of time. This contrasts with the case of exponentially decaying coefficients treated in
[
13
]
. In that setting, the argument requires bounds on the derivatives of the cutoff of the form

for all all orders

, where
C_{\delta}>0
depends on
\delta
but is independent of
t
, and where the maximum order
m(t)
of differentiation satisfies
m(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
. The order
m(t)
must be finite for each

This paper fits into a long line of work on decay of solutions to the wave equation. We begin by recalling the classical results for the free wave equation, where both
b
and
V
are identically zero. If
\mathcal{O}=\emptyset
, the sharp Huygen’s principle implies that when
d\geq 3
is odd, the energy of the solution within any fixed compact set decays to zero in finite time. In even dimensions, the Poisson formula for the solution to the initial value problem implies that the local energy decays like
t^{-d}
.

When
\mathcal{O}\neq\emptyset
(
b
and
V
still vanishing), the decay of local energy for solutions to (
1.2
) is related to the dynamics of the underlying Hamiltonian flow. The non-trapping condition, where all geodesics escape to infinity, is well known to be related to rapid energy decay. This condition holds for specific geometries, such as convex obstacles and more generally for obstacles where an escape function can be constructed. Foundational works by Lax, Morawetz, and Phillips, Ralston, and Strauss
[
12
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
address such scenarios and the resulting decay, utilizing multiplier methods and related properties of the resolvent. In the present work, as well as in
[
13
]
, the nontrapping assumption is formulated as in (
1.4
).

When nonzero potentials are present, results on energy decay draw from the work of Vainberg
[
22
]
and Melrose-Sjöstrand
[
14
,
15
]
. For example, in the case of a smooth, nonnegative electric potential of compact support, the local energy decay like
O(e^{-Ct})
for some
C>0
when
d\geq 3
is odd, and like
O(t^{-d})
when
d\geq 2
is even. Vainberg
[
22
,
23
]
establishes these decay rates for compactly supported perturbations of the Laplacian satisfying the Generalized Huygens Principle (as defined in
[
27
]
). Work by Melrose and Sjöstrand on the propagation of singularities
[
14
,
15
]
shows this principle holds for broad class of smooth nontrapping perturbations of the Laplacian. Recent works on energy decay for the wave equation in nontrapping settings are
[
24
,
2
,
25
,
26
,
1
,
7
,
11
]
.

2.
Estimates for the free resolvent

Denote by
P_{0}
the self-adjoint realization of
-\Delta

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d\geq 2
. Let the function
\mu
and the sequence
m=\{m_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be as in the Introduction.
In this section we will prove the following

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be multi-indices such that
|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 2
.
Then, given any
\delta>0
there is a constant
C=C_{\delta}>0
so that the estimate

(2.1)

\left\|\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}\mu\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\mu\right\|\leq C^{k+1}m_{k}(|\lambda|+1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|-1}

holds for all integers
k\geq 0
and all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
|\lambda|\geq\delta
.
If the dimension
d
is odd, then the estimate (
2.1
) holds for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Since the operator

\partial_{x}^{\beta}
commutes with the free resolvent, it suffices to prove the proposition with
\beta=0
and
|\alpha|\leq 2
.
Denote
\mathbb{C}^{-}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm Im}\,\lambda<0\}
.
We will make use of the formula

(2.2)

\lambda(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}=i\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\lambda}\cos\left(t\sqrt{P_{0}}\right)dt,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{-}.

Differentiating (
2.2
)
k
times we get

(2.3)

\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}\left(\lambda(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\right)=i\int_{0}^{\infty}(-it)^{k}e^{-it\lambda}\cos\left(t\sqrt{P_{0}}\right)dt,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{-}.

Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};[0,1])
be such that
\phi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1/8
,

\phi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 1/4
. Write

\mu\cos\left(t\sqrt{P_{0}}\right)\mu=\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{A}_{2}(t),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)=\phi(x/t)\mu(x)\cos\left(t\sqrt{P_{0}}\right)\mu(x)\phi(x/t),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)=(1-\phi(x/t))\mu(x)\cos\left(t\sqrt{P_{0}}\right)\mu(x)\phi(x/t)+\mu(x)\cos\left(t\sqrt{P_{0}}\right)\mu(x)(1-\phi(x/t).

It is easy to see that for some
c_{0}>0
,

\|\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)\|\lesssim\sqrt{\Theta(c_{0}t)}

Thus the operator-valued function

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}e^{-it\lambda}\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)dt

extends to
\{{\rm Im}\,\lambda\leq 0\}
and satisfies in this region the bound

(2.4)

\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}e^{-it\lambda}\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)dt\right\|\lesssim\sup_{t\geq 0}(t+1)^{k+2}\sqrt{\Theta(c_{0}t)}\leq\widetilde{C}^{k+1}m_{k+2}\leq C^{k+1}m_{k}

with some constant
C>0
independent of
k
, where we have used the condition (
1.9
).
On the other hand, if
d
is odd,
from the Huygens principle we have that the kernel of the operator

\cos\left(t\sqrt{P_{0}}\right)
vanishes in
|x-y|<t
. Observe now that
x\in{\rm supp}\,\phi(x/t)
and

y\in{\rm supp}\,\phi(y/t)
imply
|x-y|\leq t/2
.
Hence
\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)\equiv 0
in this case. Therefore, for odd dimensions it follows from
(
2.3
) and (
2.4
) that (
2.1
) holds with
\alpha=\beta=0
.

Let us see that in even dimensions (
2.1
) with
\alpha=\beta=0
holds, too. To this end we will use the fact, demonstrated in Appendix
C
, that
when
d
is even,
the kernel of the operator

\phi(x/t)\mu(x)\cos\left(t\sqrt{P_{0}}\right)\mu(x)\phi(x/t)
is of the form
\mu(x)\mu(y)\phi(x/t)\phi(y/t)t^{-d}W(|x-y|/t)
, where the function
W(z)
is analytic in
|z|<1
.
Therefore, if
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{-}
, the kernel
\widetilde{K}
of the operator

\int_{2}^{\infty}t^{k}e^{-it\lambda}\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)dt

can be written in the form

\begin{split}\widetilde{K}(x,y;\lambda)&=\mu(x)\mu(y)\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\phi(x/t)\phi(y/t)e^{-it\lambda}t^{k-d}W(|x-y|t^{-1})dt\\
&=\mu(x)\mu(y)\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-it\lambda}t^{k-d}W(|x-y|t^{-1})dt\\
&-\mu(x)\mu(y)\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}((1-\phi)(x/t)\phi(y/t)+(1-\phi)(y/t))e^{-it\lambda}t^{k-d}W(|x-y|t^{-1})dt\\
&=:K_{1}(\lambda)+K_{2}(\lambda),\end{split}

where
t_{0}=2\max\{1,|x-y|\}
. Note that the function
W(|x-y|t^{-1})
extends to complex
t
and

|W(|x-y|t^{-1})|\lesssim 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad|t|\geq t_{0}.

Observe that

\mu(x)^{1/2}(1-\phi)(x/t)\lesssim\Theta(c_{1}t)^{1/4},\,c_{1}>0,

and

\sup_{t\geq 2}t^{k}\Theta(c_{1}t)^{1/4}=\left(\sup_{t\geq 2}t^{2k}\sqrt{\Theta(c_{1}t)}\right)^{1/2}\leq\widetilde{C}^{k+1}m_{2k}^{1/2}\leq C^{k+1}m_{k},

where we have used the condition (
1.9
).
Hence
the function
K_{2}(\lambda)
can be extended to
\{{\rm Im}\,\lambda\leq 0\}
and satisfies in this region the bound

(2.5)

|K_{2}(\lambda)|\lesssim C^{k+1}m_{k}\mu(x)^{1/2}\mu(y)^{1/2}\int_{2}^{\infty}t^{-d}dt\lesssim C^{k}m_{k}\mu(x)^{1/2}\mu(y)^{1/2}.

We will now bound
|K_{1}|
for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\lambda\geq\delta
,
\delta>0
being arbitrary. To this end,
observe that we can change the contour of integration in the integral, which
we denote by
I(\lambda)
, and write it in the form

I(\lambda)=t_{0}^{k-d+1}e^{-i\theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it_{0}(1+\sigma e^{-i\theta})\lambda}(1+\sigma e^{-i\theta})^{k-d}W(|x-y|(t_{0}(1+\sigma e^{-i\theta}))^{-1})d\sigma

where
0<\theta\ll 1
is a constant. Then

I(\lambda)
satisfies the bounds

\begin{split}|I(\lambda)|&\lesssim t_{0}^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma t_{0}\delta\sin\theta}|1+\sigma e^{-i\theta}|^{k-d}d\sigma\\
&\lesssim t_{0}^{k}\sup_{\sigma>0}(\sigma+1)^{k}e^{-\sigma t_{0}\delta\sin\theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\sigma+1)^{-d}d\sigma\\
&\lesssim(2t_{0})^{k}+\sup_{\sigma\geq 1}(2t_{0}\sigma)^{k}e^{-\sigma t_{0}\delta\sin\theta}\\
&\lesssim(2t_{0})^{k}+(2/\delta\sin\theta)^{k}k!.\end{split}

Since
t_{0}\sim\langle x-y\rangle
, this implies

(2.6)

|K_{1}(\lambda)|\lesssim C^{k}\left(\langle x-y\rangle^{k}+k!\right)\mu(x)\mu(y)

for
\lambda\geq\delta
, with some constant
C>0
independent of
k
but depending on
\delta
.
On the other hand, in view of the condition (
1.8
), we have

(2.7)

\mu(x)^{1/2}\mu(y)^{1/2}\lesssim\Theta(c_{2}\langle x-y\rangle)^{1/4},\quad c_{2}>0.

By (
2.7
),

(2.8)

\langle x-y\rangle^{k}\mu(x)^{1/2}\mu(y)^{1/2}\lesssim\left(\sup_{r\geq 1}r^{2k}\sqrt{\Theta(c_{2}r)}\right)^{1/2}\leq\widetilde{C}^{k+1}m_{2k}^{1/2}\leq C^{k+1}m_{k},

where we have used the condition (
1.9
).
By (
2.6
) and (
2.8
),

(2.9)

|K_{1}(\lambda)|\lesssim C^{k}m_{k}\mu(x)^{1/2}\mu(y)^{1/2}.

By (
2.5
) and (
2.9
), we obtain

(2.10)

|\widetilde{K}(x,y;\lambda)|\lesssim C^{k}m_{k}\mu(x)^{1/2}\mu(y)^{1/2}.

It follows from (
2.10
) and Schur’s lemma that the operator with kernel
\widetilde{K}

is bounded on
L^{2}
uniformly in
\lambda\geq\delta
with a bound
O\left(C^{k}m_{k}\right)
.
It is easy to see that the same conclusion holds for
\lambda\leq-\delta
.
Furthermore, it is clear that the operator-valued function

\int_{0}^{2}t^{k}e^{-it\lambda}\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)dt

extends to
\{{\rm Im}\,\lambda\leq 0\}
and its norm is
O\left(2^{k}\right)
. Thus we conclude that an analog of the estimate
(
2.4
) with the operator
\mathcal{A}_{2}
replaced by
\mathcal{A}_{1}
holds for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
|\lambda|\geq\delta
,
with a new constant
C>0
depending on
\delta
.
This implies (
2.1
) with
\alpha=\beta=0
in this case.

We will now show that (
2.1
) with
\alpha=\beta=0
implies (
2.1
) with
1\leq|\alpha|\leq 2
and
\beta=0
.
It suffices to do so for
k\geq 1
since for
k=0
the estimate is well-known.
To this end we need the following

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a multi-index such that
|\alpha|\leq 2
.
Then, there exist constants
C>0
and
\theta_{0}>0
such that for all
\theta\geq\theta_{0}
we have the estimate

(2.11)

\left\|\mu\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\left(P_{0}-i\theta^{2}\right)^{-1}\mu^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C\theta^{|\alpha|-2}.

Differentiating the resolvent identity

(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}=(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}+(\lambda^{2}-i\theta^{2})(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{-},

we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}&=(\lambda^{2}-i\theta^{2})(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\\
&+2k\lambda(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\frac{d^{k-1}}{d\lambda^{k-1}}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\\
&+k(k-1)(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\frac{d^{k-2}}{d\lambda^{k-2}}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\end{split}

for all integers
k\geq 1
. Thus we get the identity

\begin{split}\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}\mu\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu&=(\lambda^{2}-i\theta^{2})\mu\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\mu^{-1}\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}\mu(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu\\
&+2k\lambda\mu\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\mu^{-1}\frac{d^{k-1}}{d\lambda^{k-1}}\mu(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu\\
&+k(k-1)\mu\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\mu^{-1}\frac{d^{k-2}}{d\lambda^{k-2}}\mu(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu\end{split}

for all integers
k\geq 1
, which clearly extends to
\{{\rm Im}\,\lambda\leq 0\}
. By (
2.1
) with
\alpha=\beta=0

and (
2.11
),

(2.12)

\begin{split}\left\|\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}\mu\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu\right\|&\lesssim\theta^{|\alpha|-2}(|\lambda|^{2}+\theta^{2})(|\lambda|+1)^{-1}C^{k+1}m_{k}\\
&+\theta^{|\alpha|-2}C^{k}km_{k-1}+\theta^{|\alpha|-2}C^{k-1}k(k-1)m_{k-2}\end{split}

for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
|\lambda|\geq\delta
, and all
\theta\geq\theta_{0}
. We now take
\theta=\theta_{0}+|\lambda|
.
It easy to see that (
2.12
) implies (
2.1
) with

1\leq|\alpha|\leq 2
and
\beta=0
.
In the same way, in odd dimensions we get that (
2.1
) with
\alpha=\beta=0
and all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
implies (
2.1
) with

1\leq|\alpha|\leq 2
,
\beta=0

and all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

It remains to prove the estimate (
2.1
) with
\alpha=\beta=0
for
|\lambda|\leq 1
in odd dimensions

. To this end, we will use
that the kernel
K_{0}(x,y;\lambda)
of the resolvent
(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{-}
,
can be expressed in terms of the Henkel functions by the formula

K_{0}(x,y;\lambda)=i2^{-2}(2\pi)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{2}}|x-y|^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}H^{-}_{\frac{d-2}{2}}(\lambda|x-y|).

It is well-known that
z^{\frac{d-2}{2}}H^{-}_{\frac{d-2}{2}}(z)=p_{d}(z)e^{-iz}
, where
p_{d}(z)
is a polynomial of order

\frac{d-3}{2}
. We have the formula

\partial_{z}^{k}(p_{d}(z)e^{iz})=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\frac{k!}{\nu!(k-\nu)!}\frac{d^{\nu}p_{d}(z)}{dz^{\nu}}(-i)^{k-\nu}e^{-iz}

which imlpies the bounds

(2.13)

\left|\partial_{z}^{k}\left(z^{\frac{d-2}{2}}H^{-}_{\frac{d-2}{2}}(z)\right)\right|\lesssim\sum_{\nu=0}^{\frac{d-3}{2}}k^{\nu}(|z|+1)^{\frac{d-3}{2}-\nu}\lesssim|z|^{\frac{d-3}{2}}+(k+1)^{\frac{d-3}{2}}

for
{\rm Im}\,z\leq 0
and all integers
k\geq 0
. By (
2.13
),

(2.14)

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{k}K_{0}(x,y;\lambda)\right|\lesssim|x-y|^{k-\frac{d-1}{2}}+(k+1)^{\frac{d-3}{2}}|x-y|^{k-d+2}

for
{\rm Im}\,\lambda\leq 0
,
|\lambda|\leq 1
, and all integers
k\geq 0
. By (
2.14
) and (
2.8
),

(2.15)

\left|\mu(x)\mu(y)\partial_{\lambda}^{k}K_{0}(x,y;\lambda)\right|\leq C^{k+1}m_{k}\left(|x-y|^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}+|x-y|^{-d+2}\right)\mu(x)^{1/2}\mu(y)^{1/2}.

Clearly, (
2.1
) with
|\alpha|=|\beta|=0
and
|\lambda|\leq 1

follows from (
2.15
) and Schur’s lemma.

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

We utilize the standard resolvent estimate: there exists
C>0
so that for any multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 2
, and for any
\theta\geq 1
,

(2.16)

\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C\theta^{|\alpha|+|\beta|-2}.

We compute the commutator

\begin{split}\mu(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})-(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})\mu&=-\Delta\mu-2(\nabla\mu)\cdot\nabla\\
&=-\sum_{j=1}\partial_{x_{j}}(\partial_{x_{j}}\mu)-\Delta\mu=\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}[\mu,P_{0}],\end{split}

which implies the resolvent identity

\mu(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}=(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\mu-(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}[\mu,P_{0}](P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}.

Therefore, for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

(2.17)

\mu(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\mu^{-1}f=(P_{0}-\theta^{2})^{-1}f-(P_{0}-\theta^{2})^{-1}[\mu,P_{0}](P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\mu^{-1}f.

Continuing, for any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

(2.18)

[\mu,P_{0}]u=\left[-(\Delta\mu)\mu^{-1}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}\big((\partial_{x_{j}}\mu)\mu^{-1}\big)\right](\mu u)

By (
1.7
),
(\partial_{x_{j}}\mu)\mu^{-1}
and
(\Delta\mu)\mu^{-1}
are bounded multiplication operators on
L^{2}
. So (
2.18
) continues to hold for any
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, with both sides being interpreted as members of the negative index Sobolev space
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Therefore, by (
2.16
), (
2.17
), and (
2.18
),

\begin{split}\|\mu(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\mu^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}&\leq\|(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\|_{H^{-1}\to L^{2}}\|\mu(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\mu^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(\theta^{-2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\theta^{-1}\|\mu(P_{0}-i\theta^{2})^{-1}\mu^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}),\end{split}

for some
C>0
independent of
\theta
and
f
. Thus taking
\theta
large enough we can absorb the second term on the right side into the left hand side, completing the proof of (
2.11
) when
|\alpha|=0
. Standard arguments using elliptic regularity then imply the general case
|\alpha|\leq 2
.

∎

3.
Preliminary estimates

Let
\mathcal{L}(L^{2})
denote the set of the bounded operators on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a closed interval.
Let also
\{m_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be the sequence introduced in Section 1.
Our goal in this section is to prove the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{j}(\lambda)\in C^{\infty}(\Lambda;\mathcal{L}(L^{2}))
,
j=1,\dots,J
, be operator-valued functions
satisfying the bounds

(3.1)

\left\|\frac{d^{k}\mathcal{M}_{j}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{k}}\right\|\leq M_{j}(\lambda)C_{j}^{k}m_{k},\quad\lambda\in\Lambda,

for all integers
k\geq 0
, with constants
C_{j}>0
independent of
k
and
\lambda
, and functions
M_{j}>0
independent of
k
.
Then the operator

\mathcal{M}(\lambda)=\prod_{j=1}^{J}\mathcal{M}_{j}(\lambda)\in C^{\infty}(\Lambda;\mathcal{L}(L^{2}))

satisfies the bound

(3.2)

\left\|\frac{d^{k}\mathcal{M}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{k}}\right\|\leq M(\lambda)C^{k}m_{k},\quad\lambda\in\Lambda,

for all integers
k\geq 0
, with a constant
C>0
independent of
k
and
\lambda
, and where

M(\lambda)=\prod_{j=1}^{J}M_{j}(\lambda).

Proof.

Clearly, it suffices to prove the lemma with
J=2
.
By the Leibnitz formula we have

(3.3)

\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}(\lambda)\mathcal{M}_{2}(\lambda))=\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\frac{k!}{\nu!(k-\nu)!}\frac{d^{\nu}\mathcal{M}_{1}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{\nu}}\frac{d^{k-\nu}\mathcal{M}_{2}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{k-\nu}}.

By (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) together with the condition (
1.10
) we get

(3.4)

\begin{split}\left\|\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}(\lambda)\mathcal{M}_{2}(\lambda))\right\|&\leq M_{1}(\lambda)M_{2}(\lambda)\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\frac{k!}{\nu!(k-\nu)!}C_{1}^{\nu+1}m_{\nu}C_{2}^{k-\nu+1}m_{k-\nu}\\
&\leq M_{1}(\lambda)M_{2}(\lambda)C^{k+1}m_{k}\end{split}

for every integer
k\geq 0
with some constant
C>0
independent of
k
and
\lambda
, which confirms (
3.2
) in this case.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\lambda)\in C^{\infty}(\Lambda;\mathcal{L}(L^{2}))
be an operator-valued function
satisfying the bounds

(3.5)

\left\|\frac{d^{k}\mathcal{M}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{k}}\right\|\leq C^{k+1}m_{k},\quad\lambda\in\Lambda,

for all integers
k\geq 0
with a constant
C>0
independent of
k
and
\lambda
. Suppose also that
\mathcal{M}(\lambda)
is invertible
with an inverse satisfying the bound

(3.6)

\left\|\mathcal{M}^{-1}(\lambda)\right\|\leq\widetilde{C},\quad\lambda\in\Lambda,

with a constant
\widetilde{C}>0
independent of
\lambda
. Set
B=\max(2C,2C\tilde{C}C_{4})
, where
C_{4}
is as in (
1.10
). Then the operator
\mathcal{M}^{-1}(\lambda)\in C^{\infty}(\Lambda;\mathcal{L}(L^{2}))

satisfies the bound

(3.7)

\left\|\frac{d^{k}\mathcal{M}^{-1}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{k}}\right\|\leq B^{k+1}m_{k},\quad\lambda\in\Lambda,

for all integers
k\geq 0
with a constant
B>0
independent of
k
and
\lambda
.

Proof.

Differentiating the identity

\mathcal{M}(\lambda)\mathcal{M}^{-1}(\lambda)=I

we get

(3.8)

\mathcal{M}(\lambda)\frac{d^{k+1}\mathcal{M}^{-1}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{k+1}}=-\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\frac{(k+1)!}{\nu!(k+1-\nu)!}\frac{d^{k+1-\nu}\mathcal{M}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{k+1-\nu}}\frac{d^{\nu}\mathcal{M}^{-1}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{\nu}}

for any integer
k\geq 0
. By (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.8
),

(3.9)

\left\|\frac{d^{k+1}\mathcal{M}^{-1}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{k+1}}\right\|\leq\tilde{C}\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}C^{k+1-\nu}\frac{(k+1)!m_{k+1-\nu}}{\nu!(k+1-\nu)!}\left\|\frac{d^{\nu}\mathcal{M}^{-1}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{\nu}}\right\|.

Now (
3.7
) follows from (
3.9
) by induction in
k
. Clearly, (
3.7
) with
k=0
follows from (
3.6
),
provided
B\geq\widetilde{C}
.
Furthermore, if we suppose that (
3.7
) holds for all
\nu\leq k
, by
(
3.9
) and (
1.10
), we obtain

(3.10)

\begin{split}\left\|\frac{d^{k+1}\mathcal{M}^{-1}(\lambda)}{d\lambda^{k+1}}\right\|&\leq\tilde{C}\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}C^{k+1-\nu}B^{\nu+1}\frac{(k+1)!m_{\nu}m_{k+1-\nu}}{\nu!(k+1-\nu)!}\\
&\leq C\tilde{C}C_{4}B^{k+1}m_{k+1}\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\left(\frac{C}{B}\right)^{k-\nu}\\
&\leq 2C\tilde{C}C_{4}B^{k+1}m_{k+1}\leq B^{k+2}m_{k+1}\end{split}

Observe that we used
\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}(C/B)^{k-\nu}\leq 2
(since
B\geq 2C
) and
B\geq 2C\tilde{C}C_{4}
. Thus, we conclude from
(
3.10
) that (
3.7
) holds for
k+1
as well, as desired.

∎

4.
Estimates for the perturbed resolvent

Let
P
be the self-adjoint operator from Section 1 and suppose that the electric potential
V\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})

and the magnetic potential

satisfy

|V(x)|+|b(x)|\leq C\langle x\rangle^{-\rho},\quad C>0,\,\rho>1.

The following weighted resolvent bounds for

are proved in

13
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
s>1/2
. Assume the conditions (
1.4
) and (
4.1
) are fulfilled. Then, given any
\delta>0
, there is
C_{\delta}>0
such that

(4.2)

\left\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(P-\lambda^{2}\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\right\|\leq C_{\delta}\lambda^{|\alpha|+|\beta|-1},\quad\lambda\geq\delta,\,0<\varepsilon<1,

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are multi-indices such that
|\alpha|,\,|\beta|\leq 1
.

In Appendix
B
we recall how (
4.2
) implies the limiting absorption principle, namely that

(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}:\langle x\rangle^{-s}H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\langle x\rangle^{s}H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

has a continuous extension from
\pm{\rm Im}\>\lambda>0
to
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. In what follows in this section we derive Theorem
1.1
from Proposition
2.1
, Theorem
4.1
, and the limiting absorption principle.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
in case a).

We will make use of the resolvent identities obtained in
[
13
, Section 3]
, which allow to express the perturbed resolvent in terms
of the free one. We will keep the same notation. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{-}
and denote by
I
the identity operator.
The analysis in
[
13
]
is based on the following two resolvent identities

\displaystyle(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(\widetilde{V}+i\nabla\cdot b+ib\cdot\nabla)=I-(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})\quad\text{on }H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

\displaystyle(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(\widetilde{V}+i\nabla\cdot b+ib\cdot\nabla)=-I+(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(P-\lambda^{2})\quad\text{on }D(P),

where
\widetilde{V}=V+|b|^{2}
. These yield

(4.3)

\begin{split}&(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\\
&=-(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(\widetilde{V}+i\nabla\cdot b+ib\cdot\nabla)(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\\
&=-(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(\widetilde{V}+i\nabla\cdot b+ib\cdot\nabla)(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}.\end{split}

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{-}
. By (
4.3
),

(4.4)

\begin{split}&(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(P-z^{2})^{-1}\\
&=(\lambda^{2}-z^{2})(P-z^{2})^{-1}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\\
&=L^{\sharp}(z)((P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(P_{0}-z^{2})^{-1})L^{\flat}(\lambda),\end{split}

where

\displaystyle L^{\sharp}(z)=I-(P-z^{2})^{-1}(\widetilde{V}+i\nabla\cdot b+ib\cdot\nabla),

\displaystyle L^{\flat}(\lambda)=I-(\widetilde{V}+i\nabla\cdot b+ib\cdot\nabla)(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}.

Multiplying (
4.4
) on left by
\mu\nabla^{\ell}
,
\ell\in\{0,1\}
, and the right by
\mu
, we get

(4.5)

\begin{split}&\mu\nabla^{\ell}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu-\mu\nabla^{\ell}(P-z^{2})^{-1}\mu\\
&=\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{1}\sum_{\ell_{2}=0}^{1}L^{\sharp}_{\ell,\ell_{1}}(z)\mu^{1-\ell_{1}}(-i\mu^{-1}b\cdot\nabla)^{\ell_{1}}((P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(P_{0}-z^{2})^{-1})(-i\nabla\cdot b\mu^{-1})^{\ell_{2}}\mu^{1-\ell_{2}}L^{\flat}_{\ell_{2}}(\lambda)\end{split}

where, for
\ell\in\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle L_{\ell,0}^{\sharp}=(\mu\nabla\mu^{-1})^{\ell}-\mu\nabla^{\ell}(P-z^{2})^{-1}(\widetilde{V}+i\nabla\cdot b)\mu^{-1},

\displaystyle L_{\ell,1}^{\sharp}=\mu\nabla^{\ell}(P-z^{2})^{-1}\mu,

\displaystyle L_{0}^{\flat}=I-\mu^{-1}(\widetilde{V}+ib\cdot\nabla)(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu,

\displaystyle L_{1}^{\flat}=\mu(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu,

We now let the operator
i\mu^{-1}b\cdot\nabla
act on the left side of (
4.4
) and multiply the right side by
\mu
.
We get

(4.6)

i\mu^{-1}b\cdot\nabla(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu=T_{1}(\lambda,z)+T_{2}(\lambda,z)\mu(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu+T_{3}(\lambda,z)\mu^{-1}ib\cdot\nabla(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\mu,

where

\displaystyle T_{1}=i\mu^{-1}b\cdot\nabla(P-z^{2})^{-1}\mu-\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{1}\widetilde{L}^{\sharp}_{\ell_{1}}(z)\mu^{1-\ell_{1}}(i\mu^{-1}b\cdot\nabla)^{\ell_{1}}((P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(P_{0}-z^{2})^{-1})\mu,

\displaystyle T_{2}=\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{1}\sum_{\ell_{2}=0}^{1}\widetilde{L}^{\sharp}_{\ell_{1}}(z)\mu^{1-\ell_{1}}(i\mu^{-1}b\cdot\nabla)^{\ell_{1}}((P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(P_{0}-z^{2})^{-1})(\widetilde{V}\mu^{-1})^{1-\ell_{2}}(i\nabla\cdot b\mu^{-1})^{\ell_{2}},

\begin{split}&\left\|\mu\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\mu(i\nabla+b)u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\mu u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{t}^{\infty}\left\|\mu\partial_{t}u(t^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt^{\prime}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{1}\int_{t}^{\infty}\left\|\mu\nabla^{\ell}u(t^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt^{\prime}.\end{split}

By (
5.1
) and (
5.6
),

(5.7)

\left\|\mu\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mu(i\nabla+b)u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mu u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C^{k+1}m_{k}t^{-k}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

Now take
k=k((Ce)^{-1}t)
to be the biggest integer such that
m_{k}^{1/k}\leq(Ce)^{-1}t
, which implies
C^{k+1}m_{k}t^{-k}\leq Ce^{-k((Ce)^{-1}t)}
, confirming (
1.13
) for
f\in D(P)
. Then (
1.13
) follows for general
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
since
D(P)
is dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

To get (
1.14
), we apply the above analysis
to

u(t)=\cos(t\sqrt{P})\psi_{\delta}(P^{1/2})\mu f,\qquad f\in D(P),

and use the estimate (
5.2
) instead of (
5.1
) to conclude that (
5.7
) holds with
\|f\|_{L^{2}}
in the right-hand side replaced by
\|f\|_{H^{1}}
. Then (
1.14
) follows from (
5.7
) and the fact that
D(P)
is dense in
D(P^{1/2})
with respect to the norm
f\mapsto(\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|P^{1/2}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}})^{1/2}
.

In odd dimensions, under the condition (
1.11
),
the preceding computations hold with
\psi_{\delta}\equiv 1
because so do the estimates (
5.1
) and (
5.2
).

Appendix A
Construction of a Gevrey cutoff function

In this appendix, we outline the construction of the Gevrey-class cutoff function
\zeta
utilized in the statement of Theorem
1.2
. In particular, for each
0<s<1
, we construct
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
with
\zeta(\sigma)=0
for
\sigma\leq 1/2
and
\sigma\geq 2
, and such that
|\partial_{\sigma}^{k}\zeta(\sigma)|\leq C^{k+1}(k!)^{1/s}
and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\zeta(\sigma)d\sigma=1
.

The construction builds upon the function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

f(x)=\begin{cases}e^{-x^{-a}}&x>0,\\
0&x\leq 0,\end{cases}

with
a>0
. It is easy to verify that
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
by induction on the derivatives. To establish the Gevrey bounds, we utilize a complex variable argument.

We extend
f
to the complex plane by taking the principal branch of the logarithm,
z^{-a}=e^{-a(\ln|z|+i\arg z)}
. First, consider the case
a>1
. For fixed
x>0
, we consider the contour integral of
f(z)
along a circle
\gamma_{a}
centered at
x
with radius
R=x/a
. By the Cauchy integral formula:

f^{(k)}(x)=\frac{k!}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma_{a}}\frac{e^{-z^{-a}}}{(z-x)^{k+1}}\,dz.

Estimating yields:

|f^{(k)}(x)|\leq\frac{k!}{R^{k}}\sup_{z\in\gamma_{a}}\left|e^{-z^{-a}}\right|=k!\left(\frac{a}{x}\right)^{k}e^{-\inf_{z\in\gamma_{a}}{\rm Re}\>(z^{-a})}.

Parameterizing
\gamma_{a}
as
z=x(1+a^{-1}e^{i\theta})
,
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
, it is straightforward to show that the argument of
1+a^{-1}e^{i\theta}
is bounded in magnitude by
\arctan(1/\sqrt{a^{2}-1})
. Consequently, the magnitude of the argument of
z^{-a}
is bounded by
a\arctan(1/\sqrt{a^{2}-1})
, which is strictly less than
\pi/2
for any
a>1
. Whence

\inf_{z\in\gamma_{a}}{\rm Re}\>(z^{-a})\geq x^{-a}\left(1-\frac{1}{a}\right)^{-a}\cos\left(a\arctan\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}\right)=\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}c_{a}x^{-a}.

Substituting this back into the Cauchy estimate and applying Stirling’s approximation, we obtain,

(A.1)

|f^{(k)}(x)|\leq k!\left(\frac{a}{x}\right)^{k}e^{-c_{a}x^{-a}}\leq a^{k}k!\sup_{y>0}y^{1/a}e^{-c_{a}y}=a^{k}k!\left(\frac{k}{ac_{a}}\right)^{k/a}e^{-k/a}\leq C^{k}(k!)^{1+\frac{1}{a}}

for some
C>0
independent of
k
. This is the desired Gevrey bound for order
s=1+a^{-1}
and
a>1

The case
0<a\leq 1
is simpler because, in the above argument, one may choose the contour radius to be
R=x/2
, independent of
a
. Thus it is easier to bound
{\rm Re}\>(z^{-a})
from below on the contour.

With (
A.1
) established, we proceed with the construction of
\zeta
by defining

(A.2)

\rho(x)=\frac{f(x)}{f(x)+f(1-x)}\in[0,1].

It is clear that
\rho(x)=0
for
x\leq 0
and
\rho(x)=1
for
x\geq 1
. Moreover, since the denominator is strictly positive,
\rho
has the the same Gevrey regularity as
f
(the proofs of Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
hold also for real valued functions).

Next, set

(A.3)

\chi(\sigma)=\rho\left(4\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\rho\left(4\left(2-\sigma\right)\right).

It is easy to check that
\chi(\sigma)=0
for
\sigma\notin[1/2,2]
, and
\chi(\sigma)=1
for
\sigma\in[3/4,7/4]
. Consequently,
I\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(\sigma)\,d\sigma>1
. Finally, defining
\zeta(\sigma)=I^{-1}\chi(\sigma)
yields the desired function appearing in the statement of Theorem
1.2
.

Appendix B
The limiting absorption principle

In this appendix we address the limiting absorption principle for the magnetic Schrödinger operator

P=(i\nabla+b(x))^{2}+V(x):L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\qquad d\geq 2.

where the electric potential
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
and the magnetic potential
b\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy

|V(x)|+|b(x)|\leq C\langle x\rangle^{-\rho},\qquad C>0,\,\rho>1.

The limiting absorption principle is well known to hold for this class of short range potentials. Its relevance to our work motivates us to provide a concise proof here. In particular we show that for
s>1/2
, the map
\lambda\mapsto(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}:\langle x\rangle^{-s}H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\langle x\rangle^{s}H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
admits a continuous extension, in the operator norm topology, from
{\rm Im}\>\lambda<0
to
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. This is equivalent to establishing a continuous extension for
\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, for all multi-indices
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
|\alpha|,\,|\beta|\leq 1
. Once the extension from
{\rm Im}\>\lambda<0
to
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
is established, the corresponding extension from
{\rm Im}\>\lambda>0
to
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
follows because the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
norm of
\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}
and its adjoint
(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\beta}_{x}(P-\overline{\lambda}^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}
coincide.

The proof utilizes the resolvent bound (
4.2
) and the resolvent identity (
4.3
). We will also need (and prove) the following estimate for the free resolvent squared: for any
s>1/2
and
0<\delta<M
, there exist
C,\varepsilon>0
so that

(B.1)

\begin{gathered}\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C,\\
\delta\leq|{\rm Re}\>\lambda|\leq M,\,0<|{\rm Im}\>\lambda|\leq\varepsilon,\,|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 2.\end{gathered}

Our goal is to show that, for any
0<\delta<M
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
so that the
\lambda
-derivative of
\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}
is uniformly bounded for
\delta\leq|{\rm Re}\>\lambda|\leq M
and
-\varepsilon\leq{\rm Im}\>\lambda<0
. Establishing this bound implies the uniform continuity of the weighted resolvent in these strips, yielding the desired continuous extension from
{\rm Im}\>\lambda<0
to
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. Observe that

\frac{d}{d\lambda}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}=2\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}.

Thus it suffices to bound the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
norm of the right side.

Note that for
u
in the domain
D(P)
of the operator
P
, we have in the distributional sense,

(B.2)

\begin{split}Pu&=P_{0}u+i\nabla\cdot(bu)+ib\cdot\nabla u+\widetilde{V}u\\
&=P_{0}u+Qu,\end{split}

where
P_{0}=-\Delta
denotes the free Laplacian,
\widetilde{V}=V+|b|^{2}
, and
Q=\widetilde{V}+i\nabla\cdot(bu)+ib\cdot\nabla u
. Consequently, for
{\rm Im}\>\lambda<0
,

(B.3)

\begin{split}-\lambda^{2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(&P-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\\
&=\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}-\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}P(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s},\\
&=\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}-\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}Q(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\\
&-\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}P_{0}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}.\end{split}

Inserting
1=\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}\langle x\rangle^{\frac{\rho}{2}}
at appropriate places within the operator
Q
yields.

(B.4)

Q=\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}(\langle x\rangle^{\rho}\widetilde{V})\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}+i\nabla\cdot\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}(\langle x\rangle^{\rho}b)\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}+i\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}(\langle x\rangle^{\rho}b)\cdot\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}\nabla.

Combined with (
4.2
), (
B.4
) shows that for fixed
0<\delta<M
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
so that if
\delta<|{\rm Re}\>\lambda|\leq M
and
{\rm Im}\>\lambda<0
, the third line of (
B.3
) is uniformly bounded
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

We expand the fourth line of (
B.3
) using (
4.3
). Specifically,

(B.5)

\begin{split}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(&P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}P_{0}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\\
&=\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\big[(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}Q(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\big]P_{0}\\
&\quad\cdot\big[(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}Q(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\big]\partial^{\beta}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s}.\end{split}

We rewrite the central factor as
P_{0}=(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})+\lambda^{2}
. Applying this and (
B.4
) shows that (
B.5
) can be written as a sum of operators, each of which is a product of bounded operators with polynomial dependence on
\lambda
, and factors of the form

\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\mu}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\nu}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s},\quad\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\mu}_{x}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\partial^{\nu}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s},\quad\text{or}\quad\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial^{\mu}_{x}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\partial^{\nu}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s},

with multi-indices
\mu
and
\nu
satisying
|\mu|+|\nu|\leq 1
. Therefore, for any fixed
0<\delta<M
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the factors involving single resolvents (of
P
or
P_{0}
) are controlled by (
4.2
), while those involving the squared free resolvent are controlled by (
B.1
).

It thus remains to show (
B.1
). The starting point is the following resolvent identity established in
[
11
, Appendix E]
for
s>3/2
:

(B.6)

\begin{split}-\lambda^{2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(&P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\\
&=-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(2P_{0})(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\\
&+\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(\nabla\cdot x+(d-1))\langle x\rangle^{-s}+\frac{1}{2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}x\cdot\nabla(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}.\end{split}

Using (
4.2
) in (
B.6
) yields (
B.1
) subject to the restrictions
|\alpha|=|\beta|=0
and
s>3/2
. Standard elliptic regularity arguments (and the fact that
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
commutes with
(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}
) allow us to extend this bound to the general case
|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 1
, provided
s>3/2
.

To relax the weight condition to
s>1/2
, we employ a bootstrapping argument. First, assuming
s>5/2
, we differentiate (
B.6
) with respect to
\lambda
. The resulting expression shows
\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-3}\langle x\rangle^{-s}
can be controlled using the
s>3/2
bound we just established for the squared resolvent. This proves that if
s>5/2
, the map
\lambda\mapsto\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}
is Lipschitz continuous in the region
\delta\leq|{\rm Re}\>\lambda|\leq M,\,|{\rm Im}\>\lambda|\leq\varepsilon
.

We relax the weight requirement as in
[
6
, Section 3]
, proceeding in steps. First, we extend continuity to the range
s>3/2
. Let
\mathbf{1}_{\leq A}
and
\mathbf{1}_{\geq A}
denote the characteristic functions of the sets
\{|x|\leq A\}
and
\{|x|\geq A\}
, respectively. Fix
s>3/2
and choose
\sigma>5/2
. Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\delta\leq{\rm Re}\>z\leq M,\,\epsilon\leq{\rm Im}\>\lambda<0\}
(the case of negative real parts is identical). Then

\begin{split}\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}&((P_{0}-\lambda_{1}^{2})^{-2}-(P_{0}-\lambda^{2}_{2})^{-2})\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\\
&\leq\langle A\rangle^{2(\sigma-s)}\|\mathbf{1}_{\leq A}\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}((P_{0}-\lambda^{2}_{1})^{-2}-(P_{0}-\lambda^{2}_{2})^{-2})\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|\mathbf{1}_{\geq A}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2}_{1})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\end{split}

The first term is bounded by
CA^{2(\sigma-s)}|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}|
using the Lipschitz continuity since
\sigma>5/2
. For the other terms, fix
\epsilon>0
such that
s-\epsilon>3/2
. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|\mathbf{1}_{\geq A}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P_{0}-\lambda_{j}^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}\|\leq A^{-(s-\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon)}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon}(P_{0}-\lambda_{j}^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}\|.

We previously concluded that the norms on the right are uniformly bounded. Choosing
A=|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}|^{-\kappa}
for
\kappa>0
sufficiently small yields Hölder continuity for
\lambda\mapsto\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P_{0}-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}
with
s>3/2
and
\sigma>5/2
.

We repeat this argument to replace the weight on the right by
\langle x\rangle^{-s}
with
s>3/2
, and then iterate twice more to reduce the weights on both sides to
\langle x\rangle^{-s}
with
s>1/2
. The Hölder exponents used in this process may decrease at each step. Finally, applying elliptic regularity extends (
B.1
) to the case
|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 2
.

Appendix C
Kernel of the operator
\cos(t\sqrt{P_{0}})

Let
P_{0}
denote the self-adjoint realization of
-\Delta
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
d\geq 2
. In this section we review well known properties of the kernel of the operator
\cos(t\sqrt{P_{0}}):L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
which we utilize in Section
2
. This operator also takes the form

\begin{split}\cos(t\sqrt{P_{0}})&=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\cos(t|\xi|)\mathcal{F}\\
&=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(e^{it|\xi|}+e^{-it|\xi|}\big)\mathcal{F},\end{split}

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes Fourier transform. It is well known
[
21
, Chapter III, Section 2]
that for
{\rm Re}\>w>0
,

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}e^{-w|\xi|}\mathcal{F}u)(x)=C_{d}w\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(y)}{(\sqrt{w^{2}+|x-y|^{2}})^{d+1}}dy,\qquad u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

for some
C_{d}>0
depending on
d
, and where the branch of the square root is chosen so that
{\rm Im}\>\sqrt{z}>0
if
{\rm Im}\>z\neq 0
. Therefore, setting
w=t\pm i\varepsilon
for
\varepsilon>0
,

(C.1)

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}e^{\pm i|\xi|t}\mathcal{F}u)(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mp C_{d}i(t\pm i\varepsilon)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(y)}{(\sqrt{-(t\pm i\varepsilon)^{2}+|x-y|^{2}})^{d+1}}dy,

where the limit is in the sense of convergence in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. As in Section
2
, let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};[0,\infty])
with
\phi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1/8
,
\phi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 1/4
. Whence
x\in{\rm supp}\,\phi(x/t)
and

y\in{\rm supp}\,\phi(y/t)
imply
|x-y|\leq t/2
. Thus, applying the
\phi(\cdot/t)
to both sides of
\cos(t\sqrt{P_{0}})
gives

\begin{split}\phi(\cdot/t)\cos(t\sqrt{P_{0}})\phi(\cdot/t)u&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big[-C_{d}i(t+i\varepsilon)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x/t)\phi(y/t)\frac{u(y)}{(\sqrt{-(t+i\varepsilon)^{2}+|x-y|^{2}})^{d+1}}dy\\
&+C_{d}i(t-i\varepsilon)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x/t)\phi(y/t)\frac{u(y)}{(\sqrt{-(t-i\varepsilon)^{2}+|x-y|^{2}})^{d+1}}dy\Big]\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}C_{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x/t)\phi(y/t){\rm Re}\>\Big[\frac{-i(t+i\varepsilon)}{(\sqrt{-(t+i\varepsilon)^{2}+|x-y|^{2}})^{d+1}}\Big]u(y)dy.\end{split}

The resulting kernels depending on
\varepsilon
converge in Hilbert-Schmidt norm as
\varepsilon\to 0
to

C_{d}\phi(x/t){\rm Im}\>\left(\frac{t(i{\rm sgn}\>t)^{d+1}}{(t^{2}-|x-y|^{2})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\right)\phi(y/t)=C_{d}t^{-d}\phi(x/t){\rm Im}\>\left(\frac{i^{d+1}}{(1-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t^{2}})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\right)\phi(y/t).

When the dimension
d
is even, this kernel is clearly of the form
\phi(x/t)\phi(y/t)t^{-d}W(|x-y|/t)
, where
W(z)
is analytic in
|z|<1
.
