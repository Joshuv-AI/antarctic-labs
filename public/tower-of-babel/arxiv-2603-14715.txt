Towards a Gagliardo-Type Theory of Fractional Sobolev Spaces on Arbitrary Time Scales
Hafida Abbas, Abdelhalim Azzouz

Abstract
We develop a Gagliardo-type theory of fractional Sobolev spaces on arbitrary time scales. Starting from the Lebesgue Δ \Delta -measure and the off-diagonal interaction set Ω 𝕋 = { ( t , s ) ∈ 𝕋 × 𝕋 : t ≠ s } , \Omega_{\mathbb{T}}=\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}:\ t\neq s\}, we introduce a nonlocal fractional seminorm of Gagliardo type and the associated spaces W Δ α , p ​ ( 𝕋 ) , α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) , 1 ≤ p < ∞ . W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}),\qquad\alpha\in(0,1),\quad 1\leq p<\infty. This provides a genuinely nonlocal approach to fractional regularity on time scales, distinct from the existing derivative-based theories. We establish the basic functional framework of these spaces on arbitrary time scales, proving in particular that they are Banach spaces for every 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty , reflexive for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , and Hilbert spaces when p = 2 p=2 . We also identify precisely when the construction is nontrivial, showing that W Δ α , p ​ ( 𝕋 ) ⊊ L Δ p ​ ( 𝕋 ) W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})\subsetneq L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}) if and only if 𝕋 \mathbb{T} contains a nondegenerate interval. In addition, we analyze the structural relation with previously studied Riemann–Liouville-type fractional Sobolev spaces on time scales and explain why no direct equivalence with a single one-sided derivative norm can hold on the full space. Our main geometric result is a Poincaré-type inequality on a natural class of bounded hybrid time scales with finitely many connected components separated by a positive distance. This shows that the geometry of the underlying time scale enters the Gagliardo theory already at the level of coercive inequalities. In this way, the paper lays the functional and geometric foundations of a nonlocal fractional Sobolev theory of Gagliardo type on time scales.

time scales, Lebesgue
\Delta
-measure, nonlocal energy, fractional Sobolev spaces, Gagliardo seminorm, geometric inequalities.

1
Introduction and bibliographical background

The purpose of this paper is to develop a Gagliardo-type approach to fractional Sobolev spaces on arbitrary time scales. The underlying motivation is that the measure-theoretic framework induced by the Lebesgue
\Delta
-measure provides a natural setting in which continuous, discrete, and hybrid structures can be treated within a unified formalism. From this perspective, it is natural to ask whether one can construct fractional spaces directly through nonlocal interaction energies on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}
, in the spirit of the classical Gagliardo theory in the Euclidean setting; see, for instance,
[
1
,
12
,
20
]
.

The analytical infrastructure needed for such a program is already partially available in the time-scale literature. The Lebesgue
\Delta
-measure and its multidimensional extensions were developed in
[
7
,
10
]
; see also the monographs
[
8
,
9
]
. On this basis, first-order Sobolev spaces on time scales were studied in
[
2
,
22
]
, and subsequent developments include generalized Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces on bounded time scales, including variable-exponent settings
[
21
]
, as well as Sobolev- and Morrey-type embeddings on time scales
[
3
]
. Thus, the measure-theoretic and first-order functional framework is already well established.

By contrast, the fractional theory on time scales has so far been developed predominantly through operator-based constructions. One direction is based on conformable fractional calculus; see
[
6
,
24
]
. Another important line of research concerns fractional calculus and fractional differential problems on time scales in Riemann–Liouville-type settings; see, for instance,
[
4
,
5
]
. More recently, Hu and Li introduced right- and left-sided fractional Sobolev spaces on time scales by means of Riemann–Liouville fractional derivatives, established several structural properties of these spaces, and applied them to fractional boundary value problems; see
[
19
,
18
,
17
]
. In a related direction, Tan, Zhou, and Wang developed a weighted fractional Sobolev theory on timescales, including a broader functional-analytic and variational framework
[
23
]
. These contributions are mathematically substantial, but their point of departure is a fractional differential operator, or a weighted variant thereof, rather than a nonlocal interaction energy of Gagliardo type.

The present paper adopts a different viewpoint. Instead of defining fractional regularity through a fractional derivative, we construct it directly from a nonlocal interaction seminorm. More precisely, given a time scale
\mathbb{T}
endowed with its Lebesgue
\Delta
-measure
\mu_{\Delta}
, we consider the off-diagonal set

\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}:=\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}:\ t\neq s\},

and, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we define

[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}:=\left(\iint_{\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})(t,s)\right)^{1/p},

whenever the right-hand side is finite. This leads to the fractional Sobolev space

W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})=\left\{u\in L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}):[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}<\infty\right\}.

The use of the off-diagonal set
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
is essential in the time-scale setting. In the purely continuous case, the diagonal is negligible for the product measure, so its exclusion is harmless. On arbitrary time scales, however, and especially on discrete or hybrid ones, the diagonal may carry positive product measure. For this reason, the kernel must be interpreted on
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
rather than on the whole product space. This feature is specific to the measure-theoretic structure of time scales and is one of the reasons why a separate analysis is needed in the present framework.

From this point of view, the novelty of the paper should not be understood as providing the first fractional Sobolev theory on time scales. Rather, the contribution is to isolate and develop a genuinely nonlocal Gagliardo-type model of fractional regularity in this setting. Conceptually, this brings the theory closer to the classical fractional Sobolev seminorm of Euclidean analysis than to definitions based on fractional derivatives. This perspective is particularly natural on hybrid time scales, where the interaction kernel captures in a unified way continuous, discrete, and mixed contributions. At present, no equivalence result is claimed between the spaces introduced here and the previously studied derivative-based fractional Sobolev spaces on time scales; a structural discussion of this point is given in Section
4
.

Main results

The main contributions of the paper are the following.

(1)

Functional framework
(Section
3
). We show that the spaces
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
are Banach spaces for every
1\leq p<\infty
, reflexive for
1<p<\infty
, and naturally Hilbertian when
p=2
. These results hold on an arbitrary time scale.

(2)

Non-triviality criterion
(Section
3
). We show that
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})=L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if every connected component of
\mathbb{T}
is a singleton. In particular, the inclusion
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})\subsetneq L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
is strict whenever
\mathbb{T}
contains a nondegenerate interval. This clarifies the analytical scope of the construction.

(3)

Structural comparison with Riemann–Liouville spaces
(Section
4
). We show that a direct norm equivalence between the Gagliardo seminorm and a single one-sided Riemann–Liouville derivative norm cannot hold, due to translation invariance and symmetry of the former. We identify the bilateral space
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,a+}(J)\cap W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,b-}(J)
as the natural candidate for a future equivalence result.

(4)

Poincaré-type inequality
(Section
5
). On a natural class of bounded hybrid time scales with finitely many connected components separated by a positive distance, we establish

\|u-u_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{P}\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\qquad\text{for every }u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}),

where
C_{P}
depends on the geometry of
\mathbb{T}
,
\alpha
, and
p
. This provides a first geometric estimate showing that the structure of the underlying time scale enters the theory already at the level of coercive inequalities.

Scope and limitations

The paper remains focused on the constant-order setting. Questions related to variable-order spaces, compactness, Sobolev embeddings, trace results, or variational applications in the present Gagliardo framework require additional ideas and are left for future work. In this sense, the present work should be viewed as a foundational step toward a broader nonlocal theory on time scales.

Organization

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present the measure-theoretic framework needed in the sequel. Section
3
introduces the fractional Gagliardo-type seminorm and the associated constant-order spaces, together with their main functional properties. Section
4
compares the present Gagliardo-type framework with derivative-based fractional Sobolev spaces on time scales, especially of Riemann–Liouville type. In Section
5
we establish a Poincaré-type inequality on a natural class of bounded hybrid time scales and discuss the role of geometry in the theory.

2
Measure-theoretic preliminaries on time scales

In this section we fix the notation and collect the measure-theoretic material needed for the definition of fractional Gagliardo-type seminorms on a time scale. We refer to
[
7
,
10
]
for the construction of the Lebesgue
\Delta
-measure and to the monographs
[
8
,
9
]
for general background on time scales.

2.1
Basic notation

Let
\mathbb{T}
be a time scale, that is, a nonempty closed subset of
\mathbb{R}
. We denote by
\mu_{\Delta}
the Lebesgue
\Delta
-measure associated with
\mathbb{T}
. Throughout the paper, measurable subsets of
\mathbb{T}
and measurable functions on
\mathbb{T}
are understood with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra induced by
\mu_{\Delta}
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we write

L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}):=L^{p}(\mathbb{T},\mu_{\Delta}),\qquad\|u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(t)|^{p}\,d\mu_{\Delta}(t)\right)^{1/p},

and similarly
L^{\infty}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}):=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},\mu_{\Delta})
. Functions are identified modulo equality
\mu_{\Delta}
-almost everywhere. Since
(\mathbb{T},\Sigma_{\Delta},\mu_{\Delta})
is a measure space, the spaces
L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
are Banach spaces for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, reflexive and separable for
1<p<\infty
; see, e.g.,
[
14
, Chapter 6]
and
[
11
, Chapter III]
.

2.2
Product measure and off-diagonal domain

We denote by
\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta}
the product measure on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}
. The diagonal and the off-diagonal domain are defined by

\mathrm{diag}(\mathbb{T}):=\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}:\ t=s\},\qquad\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}:=(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})\setminus\mathrm{diag}(\mathbb{T}).

Since
\mathrm{diag}(\mathbb{T})=\Phi^{-1}(\{0\})
where
\Phi(t,s)=t-s
is continuous, the set
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
is measurable with respect to
\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta}
.

Remark 2.1
.

The exclusion of the diagonal is not merely technical. On purely continuous time scales,
\mathrm{diag}(\mathbb{T})
is negligible for the product measure, but on discrete or mixed time scales it may have positive
(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})
-measure. Therefore, in the nonlocal framework considered here, all singular kernels must be interpreted on
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
rather than on the whole product space.

2.3
Measurability and well-posedness of nonlocal kernels

The following observations ensure that the Gagliardo-type seminorm introduced in Section
3
is well defined at the level of
L^{p}
-equivalence classes.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
be measurable, let
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and let
1\leq p<\infty
.

(i)

The function
(t,s)\mapsto|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}\,|t-s|^{-(1+\alpha p)}
is measurable on
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
.

(ii)

If
u=v

\mu_{\Delta}
-a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, then
|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}=|v(t)-v(s)|^{p}
for
(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})
-a.e.
(t,s)\in\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
.

Proof.

(i) The maps
(t,s)\mapsto u(t)
and
(t,s)\mapsto u(s)
are measurable on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}
, hence so is
(t,s)\mapsto|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}
. Since
|t-s|^{-(1+\alpha p)}
is continuous on
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
, their product is measurable on
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
.

(ii) Let
N:=\{t\in\mathbb{T}:u(t)\neq v(t)\}
. Then
\mu_{\Delta}(N)=0
, so
(N\times\mathbb{T})\cup(\mathbb{T}\times N)
is
(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})
-null. Outside this set,
u(t)-u(s)=v(t)-v(s)
.
∎

3
Fractional Gagliardo-type spaces of constant order

Throughout this section, let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
1\leq p<\infty
be fixed.

3.1
Definition of the seminorm and of the space

Definition 3.1
.

Let
u\in L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
. We define the fractional Gagliardo-type seminorm of order
\alpha
and exponent
p
by

[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}:=\left(\iint_{\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})(t,s)\right)^{1/p},

(3.1)

whenever the right-hand side is finite. By
section

2.3
, this quantity is well defined on equivalence classes in
L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
.

Definition 3.2
.

We define

W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}):=\left\{u\in L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}):[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}<\infty\right\}.

(3.2)

On this space we consider the norm

\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}+[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}.

(3.3)

Remark 3.3

(Non-triviality)
.

On a purely discrete time scale
\mathbb{T}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\}
with
\min_{i\neq j}|t_{i}-t_{j}|=\delta>0
, the kernel
|t-s|^{-(1+\alpha p)}
is bounded on
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
by
\delta^{-(1+\alpha p)}
, so that
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})=L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
with equivalent norms. By contrast, when
\mathbb{T}
contains a nondegenerate interval
[a,b]
, the restriction of
\mu_{\Delta}
to
[a,b]
coincides with the Lebesgue measure
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
, and the kernel
|t-s|^{-(1+\alpha p)}
is not integrable near the diagonal. In this case, the inclusion
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})\subsetneq L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
is strict: it suffices to exhibit a function
u\in L^{p}([a,b])
with
[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}([a,b])}=\infty
, which is classical; see, e.g.,
[
12
, Section 2]
. Thus the space
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
is genuinely smaller than
L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
\mathbb{T}
has a connected component of positive Lebesgue measure.

3.2
Elementary properties

Proposition 3.4
.

The mapping
u\mapsto[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
is a seminorm on
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
, and
\|\cdot\|_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
is a norm. Every
\mu_{\Delta}
-a.e. constant function has zero seminorm; in particular,
[\cdot]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
is not a norm.

Proof.

Nonnegativity and absolute homogeneity are immediate. For the triangle inequality, define

F_{u}(t,s):=\frac{u(t)-u(s)}{|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}+\alpha}},\qquad(t,s)\in\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}.

Then
F_{u+v}=F_{u}+F_{v}
, and Minkowski’s inequality in
L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}},\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})
gives

[u+v]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}=\|F_{u+v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}})}\leq\|F_{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}})}+\|F_{v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}})}=[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}+[v]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}.

Hence
[\cdot]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
is a seminorm. The triangle inequality and absolute homogeneity for
\|\cdot\|_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
follow from those of
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
and
[\cdot]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
. If
\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}=0
, then
\|u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}=0
, so
u=0

\mu_{\Delta}
-a.e.
∎

3.3
Isometric embedding and completeness

The key structural observation is that
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
can be identified with a closed subspace of a product of
L^{p}
-spaces via a graph embedding.

Definition 3.5
.

For
u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
, define the nonlocal increment map

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,p}u(t,s):=\frac{u(t)-u(s)}{|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}+\alpha}},\qquad(t,s)\in\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}.

(3.4)

By construction,
[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}=\|\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,p}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}},\,\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

The map

J:W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})\to L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})\times L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}},\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta}),\qquad J(u):=(u,\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,p}u),

is a linear isometry when the product space is equipped with the norm
\|(f,g)\|:=\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}})}
.

Proof.

Immediate from the definitions.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,1)
and every
1\leq p<\infty
, the space
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
is a Banach space.

Proof.

Let
(u_{n})
be a Cauchy sequence in
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
. Then
(u_{n})
is Cauchy in
L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
, hence converges to some
u\in L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
. Similarly,
F_{n}:=\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,p}u_{n}
is Cauchy in
L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}})
and converges to some
F\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}})
.

Extracting a subsequence with
u_{n}(t)\to u(t)

\mu_{\Delta}
-a.e., we obtain

F_{n}(t,s)\to\frac{u(t)-u(s)}{|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}+\alpha}}\qquad(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})\text{-a.e.\ on }\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}.

Since
F_{n}\to F
in
L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}})
, a further subsequence converges to
F
a.e. By uniqueness of the a.e. limit,

F(t,s)=\frac{u(t)-u(s)}{|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}+\alpha}}\qquad(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})\text{-a.e.\ on }\Omega_{\mathbb{T}},

so
u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
. The convergence
\|u_{n}-u\|_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\to 0
follows from
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,p}u_{n}\to\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,p}u
in
L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}})
.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
is reflexive.

Proof.

By
section

3.3
,
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
is isometrically embedded into
L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})\times L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}},\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})
. Its image
J(W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}))
is closed: if
J(u_{n})\to(f,G)
, then arguing as in the proof of
theorem

3.7
one obtains
G=\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,p}f
a.e., hence
(f,G)=J(f)
. Since
1<p<\infty
, the product space is reflexive, and every closed subspace of a reflexive space is reflexive.
∎

Remark 3.9

(Hilbert structure)
.

When
p=2
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{\alpha,2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
is induced by the inner product

\langle u,v\rangle_{W^{\alpha,2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(t)v(t)\,d\mu_{\Delta}(t)+\iint_{\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{(u(t)-u(s))(v(t)-v(s))}{|t-s|^{1+2\alpha}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})(t,s).

(3.5)

Indeed,
\langle u,u\rangle=\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+[u]_{W^{\alpha,2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}
, and positive definiteness follows from
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\Delta}}=0\Rightarrow u=0
. The induced norm
\langle u,u\rangle^{1/2}
is equivalent to
\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
, so
W^{\alpha,2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
is a Hilbert space by
theorem

3.7
.

3.4
Examples

We conclude with elementary examples illustrating how the construction recovers familiar settings.

Example 3.10

(Continuous time scale)
.

If
\mathbb{T}=[a,b]
, then
\mu_{\Delta}
coincides with the Lebesgue measure on
[a,b]

[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
, and the seminorm (
3.1
) reduces to the usual one-dimensional Gagliardo seminorm. In this case,
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
agrees with the classical fractional Sobolev space
W^{\alpha,p}([a,b])
.

Example 3.11

(Finite discrete time scale)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\}
with
t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}
. Then
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}
is finite and the seminorm becomes

[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}=\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{|u(t_{i})-u(t_{j})|^{p}}{|t_{i}-t_{j}|^{1+\alpha p}}\,\mu_{\Delta}(\{t_{i}\})\,\mu_{\Delta}(\{t_{j}\}).

This is a weighted discrete interaction energy. As noted in
section

3.1
, the corresponding space coincides with
L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
.

Example 3.12

(Hybrid time scale)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=[a,b]\cup D
, where
D
is a finite discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}\setminus[a,b]
. Then the seminorm splits into three parts: a continuous–continuous contribution over
[a,b]\times[a,b]
, a discrete–discrete contribution over
D\times D
off the diagonal, and a mixed contribution over
([a,b]\times D)\cup(D\times[a,b])
. This illustrates how the nonlocal energy combines continuous and discrete interactions within a single measure-theoretic framework.

4
Comparison with Riemann–Liouville fractional Sobolev spaces on time scales

In order to position the present work within the existing literature, we compare the Gagliardo-type spaces introduced here with the derivative-based fractional Sobolev spaces on time scales developed by Hu and Li
[
19
,
18
]
, and with the weighted framework later considered by Tan, Zhou, and Wang
[
23
]
.

4.1
The Riemann–Liouville framework of Hu–Li

Let
J=[a,b]_{\mathbb{T}}
be a bounded time-scale interval, let
0<\alpha\leq 1
, and let
1\leq p<\infty
. In the right-sided setting, Hu and Li define the fractional Sobolev space
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,b-}(J,\mathbb{R}^{N})
as the set of all functions
u\in L^{p}_{\Delta}(J,\mathbb{R}^{N})
for which there exists
g\in L^{p}_{\Delta}(J,\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\int_{a}^{b}u(t)\,\bigl({}^{\mathbb{T}}_{a}D_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi\bigr)(t)\,\Delta t=\int_{a}^{b}g(t)\,\varphi(t)\,\Delta t\qquad\text{for all }\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c,rd}(J,\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The function
g
is called the weak right Riemann–Liouville fractional derivative of
u
. Hu and Li prove that this weak derivative coincides
\Delta
-almost everywhere with the corresponding right Riemann–Liouville derivative and that

W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,b-}(J,\mathbb{R}^{N})=AC^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,b-}(J,\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{p}_{\Delta}(J,\mathbb{R}^{N}).

They equip this space with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,b-}}^{p}=\|u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(J)}^{p}+\|{}^{\mathbb{T}}_{t}D_{b}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(J)}^{p}.

In the left-sided setting, Hu and Li define
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,a+}(J,\mathbb{R}^{N})
analogously, replacing the right-sided fractional derivative by the left-sided one, and consider the norm

\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,a+}}^{p}=\|u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(J)}^{p}+\|{}^{\mathbb{T}}_{a}D_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(J)}^{p}.

These spaces satisfy the usual functional-analytic properties expected of derivative-based fractional Sobolev spaces, including completeness, reflexivity, separability, and several norm equivalences; see
[
19
,
18
]
. In a related direction, Tan, Zhou, and Wang introduced weighted fractional Sobolev spaces on timescales and obtained further structural and variational results
[
23
]
.

4.2
Structural comparison

We now identify the structural obstacles to a direct comparison between the present Gagliardo-type framework and the Riemann–Liouville theory.

Proposition 4.1
.

For every
u\in L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
and every constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
:

(i)

[u+c]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}=[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}

(translation invariance)
;

(ii)

[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}=\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{|u(s)-u(t)|^{p}}{|s-t|^{1+\alpha p}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})(s,t)

(symmetry)
.

Proof.

Both claims follow from the definition: (i) since
(u(t)+c)-(u(s)+c)=u(t)-u(s)
, and (ii) by symmetry of the kernel
|t-s|^{-(1+\alpha p)}
and invariance of
\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta}
under the transposition
(t,s)\mapsto(s,t)
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

A direct norm equivalence between the Gagliardo seminorm and a single one-sided Riemann–Liouville derivative norm cannot hold on the full space. More precisely, one cannot have

\|{}^{\mathbb{T}}_{a}D_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(J)}\asymp[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(J)}\qquad\text{for all }u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(J),

and similarly for
{}^{\mathbb{T}}_{t}D_{b}^{\alpha}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
(i), every constant function has zero Gagliardo seminorm. On the other hand, the one-sided Riemann–Liouville derivatives of nonzero constants do not vanish. Indeed, in the continuous case
J=[a,b]
, one has

{}^{\mathbb{T}}_{a}D^{\alpha}_{t}(1)=\frac{(t-a)^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)},

which belongs to
L^{p}([a,b])
if and only if
\alpha p<1
, but is not identically zero for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Hence neither of the above equivalences can hold on the full space.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Proposition
4.2
shows that any meaningful comparison must either be formulated modulo constants or involve subspaces with boundary conditions. Moreover, since
[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(J)}
is symmetric in
(t,s)
by Proposition
4.2
(ii), while each of the spaces
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,a+}(J)
and
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,b-}(J)
is inherently one-sided, the natural derivative-based candidate for comparison is the bilateral space

X^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(J):=W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,a+}(J)\cap W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta,b-}(J),

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{X^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(J)}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(J)}+\|{}^{\mathbb{T}}_{a}D_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(J)}+\|{}^{\mathbb{T}}_{t}D_{b}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(J)}.

Open Problem 1
.

Determine whether, on a suitable class of bounded time scales and under appropriate boundary assumptions, the Gagliardo-type space
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(J)
is norm-equivalent to either

•

a quotient of
X^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(J)
modulo constants, or

•

a trace-zero subspace of
X^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(J)
.

A positive answer would relate the present nonlocal framework to the existing derivative-based theory and would be relevant for trace results, embedding theorems, and variational applications.

5
Geometric estimates

We now establish a first geometric estimate for the Gagliardo seminorm and discuss extensions of the present framework.

5.1
A Poincaré-type inequality on bounded hybrid time scales

We impose a structural assumption on the underlying time scale ensuring that its connected components are finitely many, mutually separated, and of positive
\Delta
-measure.

Assumption 5.1
.

The time scale
\mathbb{T}
is bounded and has finitely many connected components. More precisely,

\mathbb{T}=\Big(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{m}I_{\ell}\Big)\cup D,

where:

•

each
I_{\ell}=[a_{\ell},b_{\ell}]
is a nondegenerate compact interval,

•

D=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{N}\}
is a finite set,

•

the sets
I_{1},\dots,I_{m},\{d_{1}\},\dots,\{d_{N}\}
are pairwise disjoint,

•

there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that any two distinct connected components of
\mathbb{T}
are at distance at least
\delta_{0}
.

In addition, for every singleton component
\{d_{j}\}
, we assume
\mu_{\Delta}(\{d_{j}\})>0
.

Under
section

5.1
, we write
\mathcal{C}=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{m},\{d_{1}\},\dots,\{d_{N}\}\}
for the family of connected components. One has
0<\mu_{\Delta}(C)<\infty
for every
C\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mu_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})<\infty
. For
u\in L_{\Delta}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, we define the global average

u_{\mathbb{T}}:=\frac{1}{\mu_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(t)\,d\mu_{\Delta}(t),

and, for each component
C\in\mathcal{C}
,

u_{C}:=\frac{1}{\mu_{\Delta}(C)}\int_{C}u(t)\,d\mu_{\Delta}(t).

The Gagliardo seminorm decomposes into intra-component and inter-component contributions:

\displaystyle{}_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\iint_{(C\times C)\cap\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})

(5.1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\\
C\neq C^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\iint_{C\times C^{\prime}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta}).

Lemma 5.2
.

Under
section

5.1
, for every two distinct components
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
and every
(t,s)\in C\times C^{\prime}
,

\frac{1}{(\operatorname{diam}\mathbb{T})^{1+\alpha p}}\leq\frac{1}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{0}^{1+\alpha p}}.

In particular, there exist constants
c_{0},C_{0}>0
, depending only on
\mathbb{T}
,
\alpha
, and
p
, such that

c_{0}\iint_{C\times C^{\prime}}|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})\leq\iint_{C\times C^{\prime}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})\leq C_{0}\iint_{C\times C^{\prime}}|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta}).

(5.2)

Proof.

Since
C
and
C^{\prime}
are distinct components,
|t-s|\geq\delta_{0}
for all
(t,s)\in C\times C^{\prime}
, and
|t-s|\leq\operatorname{diam}\mathbb{T}
since
\mathbb{T}
is bounded. The bounds (
5.2
) follow by multiplying by
|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}
and integrating.
∎

Theorem 5.3

(Poincaré-type inequality)
.

Under
section

5.1
, there exists a constant
C_{P}>0
, depending only on
\mathbb{T}
,
\alpha
, and
p
, such that

\|u-u_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{P}\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\qquad\text{for every }u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}).

(5.3)

Proof.

For each
C\in\mathcal{C}
, we write
u-u_{\mathbb{T}}=(u-u_{C})+(u_{C}-u_{\mathbb{T}})
. Using
|x+y|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(|x|^{p}+|y|^{p})
and integrating over
\mathbb{T}=\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}C
, we obtain

\|u-u_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L_{\Delta}^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\int_{C}|u-u_{C}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\Delta}+2^{p-1}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\mu_{\Delta}(C)\,|u_{C}-u_{\mathbb{T}}|^{p}.

(5.4)

Step 1: oscillation inside each component.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}
. If
C=I_{\ell}=[a_{\ell},b_{\ell}]
is an interval component, then
\mu_{\Delta}
restricted to
I_{\ell}
coincides with the Lebesgue measure on
[a_{\ell},b_{\ell}]
; see
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
. Therefore
u|_{I_{\ell}}\in W^{\alpha,p}(I_{\ell})
in the classical one-dimensional Gagliardo sense, and the standard fractional Poincaré inequality on bounded intervals (see, e.g.,
[
12
, Theorem 6.7]
) yields

\int_{I_{\ell}}|u-u_{I_{\ell}}|^{p}\,dt\leq C_{\ell}\iint_{I_{\ell}\times I_{\ell}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\,dt\,ds,

where
C_{\ell}
depends only on
|I_{\ell}|
,
\alpha
, and
p
. Since
\mu_{\Delta}|_{I_{\ell}}
equals the Lebesgue measure, both sides can equivalently be written in terms of
d\mu_{\Delta}
. If
C=\{d_{j}\}
is a singleton, then
u-u_{C}=0

\mu_{\Delta}
-a.e. on
C
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is finite, summing over all
C\in\mathcal{C}
with a single constant
C>0
bounding all
C_{\ell}
gives

\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\int_{C}|u-u_{C}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\Delta}\leq C\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\iint_{(C\times C)\cap\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})\leq C\,[u]_{W_{\Delta}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}.

Step 2: control of the component averages.

Set
\lambda_{C}:=\mu_{\Delta}(C)
for
C\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is finite with
\lambda_{C}>0
for all
C
, the following discrete weighted Poincaré inequality holds in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}}
: there exists
C_{1}>0
, depending only on the weights
(\lambda_{C})_{C\in\mathcal{C}}
, such that for every
(x_{C})_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda_{C}\left|x_{C}-\frac{\sum_{C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda_{C^{\prime}}x_{C^{\prime}}}{\sum_{C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda_{C^{\prime}}}\right|^{p}\leq C_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\\
C\neq C^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{C}\lambda_{C^{\prime}}|x_{C}-x_{C^{\prime}}|^{p}.

(5.5)

Indeed, both sides define seminorms on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}}
that vanish exactly on constant vectors, and on the finite-dimensional quotient
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}}/\mathbb{R}
all seminorms with the same kernel are equivalent.

Applying (
5.5
) with
x_{C}=u_{C}
and using
u_{\mathbb{T}}=(\sum_{C}\lambda_{C}u_{C})/(\sum_{C}\lambda_{C})
, we get

\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\mu_{\Delta}(C)\,|u_{C}-u_{\mathbb{T}}|^{p}\leq C_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\\
C\neq C^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\mu_{\Delta}(C)\mu_{\Delta}(C^{\prime})\,|u_{C}-u_{C^{\prime}}|^{p}.

For
C\neq C^{\prime}
, Jensen’s inequality applied to the probability measure
(\mu_{\Delta}(C)\mu_{\Delta}(C^{\prime}))^{-1}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})
on
C\times C^{\prime}
gives

|u_{C}-u_{C^{\prime}}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{\Delta}(C)\mu_{\Delta}(C^{\prime})}\iint_{C\times C^{\prime}}|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})(t,s).

Multiplying by
\mu_{\Delta}(C)\mu_{\Delta}(C^{\prime})
, summing over
C\neq C^{\prime}
, and applying
section

5.1
, we obtain

\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\mu_{\Delta}(C)\,|u_{C}-u_{\mathbb{T}}|^{p}\leq C\,[u]_{W_{\Delta}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}.

Step 3: conclusion.

Combining Steps 1 and 2 with (
5.4
) and taking the
p
th root yields (
5.3
).
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Under
section

5.1
, there exists
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\Big([u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}+|u_{\mathbb{T}}|\Big)\qquad\text{for every }u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}).

Proof.

Write
u=(u-u_{\mathbb{T}})+u_{\mathbb{T}}
, apply the triangle inequality and
theorem

5.3
, and bound
\|u_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}=|u_{\mathbb{T}}|\,\mu_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})^{1/p}
.
∎

5.2
Fractional Hardy and Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg-type inequalities

Theorem 5.5

(Fractional Hardy inequality on a bounded time scale)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}\subset[0,R]
be a bounded hybrid time scale satisfying
Assumption
5.1
, and assume
0\in\mathbb{T}
.
Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<\alpha<1
, and
0\leq\beta<\alpha
.
Then there exists
C>0
, depending only on
\mathbb{T}
,
\alpha
,
p
,
and
\beta
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{|u(t)-u_{\mathbb{T}}|^{p}}{|t|^{\beta p}}\,d\mu_{\Delta}(t)\leq C\,\iint_{\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})(t,s)

(5.6)

for every
u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
.

Proof.

Set
v:=u-u_{\mathbb{T}}
, so that
v_{\mathbb{T}}=0
and

[v]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}=[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}
.
We decompose the left-hand side over the family
\mathcal{C}

of connected components.

Step 1: the interval component containing
0
.

Let
I_{0}=[a_{0},b_{0}]
be the interval component with
0\in I_{0}

(so
a_{0}=0
). On
I_{0}
, the measure
\mu_{\Delta}
coincides with
the Lebesgue measure

[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
.
Since
\beta<\alpha
, the weight
|t|^{-\beta p}
is locally
integrable on
I_{0}
.
For every
t\in I_{0}
,

|v(t)|^{p}=|v(t)-v_{\mathbb{T}}+v_{\mathbb{T}}|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\bigl(|v(t)-v_{I_{0}}|^{p}+|v_{I_{0}}|^{p}\bigr).

The first term satisfies, by the classical fractional
Poincaré inequality on
(0,b_{0})
,

\int_{I_{0}}\frac{|v(t)-v_{I_{0}}|^{p}}{|t|^{\beta p}}\,dt\leq C_{1}\iint_{I_{0}\times I_{0}}\frac{|v(t)-v(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}\,dt\,ds\leq C_{1}\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{p},

where
C_{1}
depends on
|I_{0}|
,
\alpha
,
p
, and
\beta
;
this follows from the subcritical weighted Poincaré
estimate of
[
13
]
.
For the second term,

|v_{I_{0}}|^{p}=|v_{I_{0}}-v_{\mathbb{T}}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{\Delta}(I_{0})}\int_{I_{0}}|v(t)-v_{\mathbb{T}}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\Delta}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{\Delta}(I_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|v(t)-v_{\mathbb{T}}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\Delta},

which is controlled by
C\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}

via Theorem
5.3
. Therefore

\int_{I_{0}}\frac{|v(t)|^{p}}{|t|^{\beta p}}\,dt\leq C\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}.

Step 2: other interval components.

Let
I_{k}=[a_{k},b_{k}]
with
k\neq 0
. Since
0\notin I_{k}
,
we have
|t|\geq\delta_{k}>0
for all
t\in I_{k}
, so

\int_{I_{k}}\frac{|v(t)|^{p}}{|t|^{\beta p}}\,d\mu_{\Delta}(t)\leq\delta_{k}^{-\beta p}\int_{I_{k}}|v(t)|^{p}\,d\mu_{\Delta}(t).

We bound
|v(t)|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(|v(t)-v_{I_{k}}|^{p}+|v_{I_{k}}|^{p})
.
The first term is controlled by the intra-component
Poincaré estimate from Step 1 of
Theorem
5.3
:

\int_{I_{k}}|v(t)-v_{I_{k}}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\Delta}\leq C\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}.

For the second term, since
v_{\mathbb{T}}=0
,

|v_{I_{k}}|^{p}=|v_{I_{k}}-v_{\mathbb{T}}|^{p}\leq\bigl(|v_{I_{k}}-v_{I_{0}}|+|v_{I_{0}}|\bigr)^{p}.

The cross-component term
|v_{I_{k}}-v_{I_{0}}|
is controlled
by Jensen’s inequality and
Lemma
5.1
, exactly as
in Step 2 of Theorem
5.3
:

|v_{I_{k}}-v_{I_{0}}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{\Delta}(I_{k})\mu_{\Delta}(I_{0})}\iint_{I_{k}\times I_{0}}|v(t)-v(s)|^{p}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})\leq C\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}.

The term
|v_{I_{0}}|^{p}
was already bounded in Step 1.
Combining,
\int_{I_{k}}|v|^{p}/|t|^{\beta p}\,d\mu_{\Delta}\leq C\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}
.

Step 3: singleton components.

If
C=\{d_{j}\}
, then
|d_{j}|\geq\delta_{0}>0
, and the same
argument as Step 2 applies with
v_{C}=v(d_{j})
.

Step 4: conclusion.

Summing over all
C\in\mathcal{C}
(finitely many)
yields (
5.6
).
∎

Remark 5.6

(Critical Hardy exponent)
.

When
\alpha p>1
, the Morrey embedding

W^{\alpha,p}(I_{0})\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha-1/p}(I_{0})

ensures that pointwise values are well defined on interval
components. In this regime, a stronger Hardy inequality
with the
critical
weight
|t-t_{0}|^{-\alpha p}

holds for functions satisfying
u(t_{0})=0
; see

[
13
]
and

[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
for the classical
one-dimensional result. The extension to hybrid time scales
under Assumption
5.1
follows by the same
component decomposition used above.

Proposition 5.7

(A Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg-type
interpolation on a bounded time scale)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded time scale satisfying
Assumption
5.1
, let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
,
let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\infty
, and let

0\leq\theta\leq 1
. Define
r
by

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\theta}{p}+\frac{1-\theta}{q}.

Let
\beta\geq 0
, and set
b:=\theta\beta
.
Assume that there exists
C_{H}>0
such that

\bigl\||t-x_{0}|^{-\beta}(u-u_{\mathbb{T}})\bigr\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{H}\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\qquad\text{for all }u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}).

Then

\bigl\||t-x_{0}|^{-b}(u-u_{\mathbb{T}})\bigr\|_{L^{r}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{H}^{\theta}\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{\theta}\,\|u-u_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{1-\theta}

(5.7)

for all
u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
.

Proof.

Set
v:=u-u_{\mathbb{T}}
. Then

|t-x_{0}|^{-br}|v|^{r}=\Bigl(|t-x_{0}|^{-\beta p}|v|^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{\theta r}{p}}\Bigl(|v|^{q}\Bigr)^{\frac{(1-\theta)r}{q}}.

Since

\frac{\theta r}{p}+\frac{(1-\theta)r}{q}=1,

Hölder’s inequality gives

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|t-x_{0}|^{-br}|v|^{r}\,d\mu_{\Delta}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|t-x_{0}|^{-\beta p}|v|^{p}\,d\mu_{\Delta}\right)^{\frac{\theta r}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|v|^{q}\,d\mu_{\Delta}\right)^{\frac{(1-\theta)r}{q}}.

Taking the
r
-th root and applying the assumed Hardy
estimate yields (
5.7
).
∎

Corollary 5.8

(Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg-type inequality)
.

Under Assumption
5.1
, let

1<p<\infty
,
0<\alpha<1
,
0\leq\beta<\alpha
,

1\leq q<\infty
, and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.
Define
r
and
b
as in
Proposition
5.2
.
Suppose moreover that the fractional Poincaré
inequality of Theorem
5.3
provides

\|u-u_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{E}\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\qquad\text{for all }u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}).

Then there exists
C>0
such that

\bigl\||t-x_{0}|^{-b}(u-u_{\mathbb{T}})\bigr\|_{L^{r}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}

(5.8)

for every
u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
,
where
C=C_{H}^{\theta}\,C_{E}^{1-\theta}
with
C_{H}

from Theorem
5.5
and
C_{E}
from
Theorem
5.3
.

Proof.

Theorem
5.5
(with
x_{0}=0
, the general case
following by translation) supplies the Hardy estimate
required by Proposition
5.2
with
constant
C_{H}
.
Combining (
5.7
) with the Poincaré
bound
\|u-u_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta}}\leq C_{E}\,[u]
gives

\bigl\||t-x_{0}|^{-b}(u-u_{\mathbb{T}})\bigr\|_{L^{r}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{H}^{\theta}\,[u]^{\theta}\cdot C_{E}^{1-\theta}\,[u]^{1-\theta}=C_{H}^{\theta}\,C_{E}^{1-\theta}\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})},

which is (
5.8
).
∎

6
Perspectives

We conclude by indicating several natural directions for further investigation within the present Gagliardo-type framework.

Relaxation of the geometric assumption.

Section

5.1
restricts attention to time scales with finitely many well-separated components. It would be desirable to extend
theorem

5.3
to broader classes of time scales, for instance under a doubling condition on
(\mathbb{T},\mu_{\Delta})
in the spirit of the analysis on metric measure spaces; see, e.g.,
[
16
]
.. Such an extension would require new techniques beyond the finite-dimensional argument used in Step 2.

Compactness and embeddings.

A natural next step is to establish compact embeddings of the form
W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{q}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
for suitable
q
, generalizing the classical Rellich–Kondrachov theorem. On time scales containing nondegenerate intervals, one expects the classical one-dimensional embedding exponents to play a role, but the interaction between continuous and discrete components introduces additional difficulties.

Variable-order extension.

Given a measurable function
\alpha:\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}\to(0,1)
, one may define

[u]_{W^{\alpha(\cdot),p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}:=\left(\iint_{\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+p\alpha(t,s)}}\,d(\mu_{\Delta}\otimes\mu_{\Delta})(t,s)\right)^{1/p}

and the corresponding space
W^{\alpha(\cdot),p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}):=\{u\in L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}):[u]_{W^{\alpha(\cdot),p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}<\infty\}
. By
section

2.3
, this quantity is well defined. However, the analysis is substantially more delicate: the kernel
|t-s|^{-(1+p\alpha(t,s))}
is no longer governed by a single exponent, and even under the uniform bound
0<\alpha_{-}\leq\alpha(t,s)\leq\alpha_{+}<1
, comparison with constant-order models requires a careful interplay between the geometry of
\mathbb{T}
and the variability of the exponent. Questions of completeness, reflexivity, and Poincaré-type inequalities in this setting will be addressed separately.

Variational applications.

In the quadratic case
p=2
, the inner product (
3.5
) induces a bilinear form that can serve as the energy functional for nonlocal boundary value problems on time scales. A natural model problem is the minimization of

\mathcal{E}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,u\,d\mu_{\Delta},

whose Euler–Lagrange equation defines a nonlocal operator of fractional Laplacian type on
\mathbb{T}
. The coercivity provided by
theorem

5.3
(on suitable quotient or constrained spaces) and the Hilbert structure of
W^{\alpha,2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
make the direct method of the calculus of variations applicable. This direction will be pursued in forthcoming work.

Fractional Hardy and
Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg-type inequalities

(Section
5
). Under the additional
condition
\alpha p>1
, we establish a fractional
Hardy inequality with singular weight

|t-t_{0}|^{-\alpha p}
and deduce a
Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg-type interpolation
inequality on hybrid time scales
(Theorem
5.5
and
Corollary
5.2
).

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced a Gagliardo-type theory of fractional Sobolev spaces on arbitrary time scales, based on the Lebesgue
\Delta
-measure and the off-diagonal interaction set

\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}=\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}:\ t\neq s\}.

This construction provides a genuinely nonlocal notion of fractional regularity on time scales and differs conceptually from the derivative-based approaches developed so far in the literature.

Our first objective was to establish the functional framework of the spaces

W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T}),\qquad\alpha\in(0,1),\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

On arbitrary time scales, we proved that these spaces are Banach spaces for every
1\leq p<\infty
, reflexive for
1<p<\infty
, and Hilbert spaces in the case
p=2
. We also obtained a precise nontriviality criterion, showing that the Gagliardo construction yields a genuine refinement of
L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})
exactly when the time scale contains a nondegenerate interval.

We then turned to the geometric side of the theory. On a natural class of bounded hybrid time scales consisting of finitely many connected components separated by a positive distance, we established a Poincaré-type inequality of the form

\|u-u_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{P}\,[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T})}.

This result shows that the geometry of the underlying time scale enters the Gagliardo theory already at the level of coercive inequalities, and it provides the main geometric estimate of the paper.

A further contribution of the paper is the structural comparison with the fractional Sobolev spaces on time scales defined through Riemann–Liouville-type operators. Our analysis shows that the present Gagliardo framework should not be viewed as a reformulation of those spaces, but rather as a distinct nonlocal model whose natural symmetry and translation-invariant structure lead to a different analytical behavior.

Finally, we discussed the weighted direction suggested by the present theory. More precisely, we recorded an abstract Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg-type interpolation principle showing that, once a suitable weighted Hardy inequality is established in the present Gagliardo framework, the corresponding weighted interpolation estimates follow naturally. This clarifies the structure of a future weighted theory on hybrid time scales and identifies the Hardy inequality as the main missing ingredient.

Taken together, these results provide the first systematic functional and geometric foundation for a Gagliardo-type fractional Sobolev theory on time scales. They show that the nonlocal viewpoint can be developed coherently in the time-scale setting and that it captures, within a single framework, continuous, discrete, and hybrid interactions.

Several directions remain open. Among the most natural are compactness and embedding results, trace-type inequalities, variable-order extensions, weighted Hardy inequalities and their Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg-type consequences, as well as broader variational applications. We hope that the present work provides a robust starting point for these developments.
