Investigating demographic factors and geographical information mapping (GIS) of oral cancer in Golestan Province, Iran in 2014 to 2021
Authors: Maryam Ghelichli, Gholamreza Roshandel, Sina Sohrabi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOral cancer is among the ten most common malignancies worldwide and can be highly lethal if not diagnosed and treated promptly. Mapping the spatial and geographic distribution of this cancer can assist health authorities in planning effective prevention, control, and treatment strategies by identifying its geographic and demographic patterns. This study aimed to investigate the demographic characteristics and geographic distribution of oral cancer in Golestan Province, Iran, during the period 2014–2021.Materials and methodsThis trend study used data from the National Cancer Registry System of Golestan Province, Iran, covering the period from 2014 to 2021. Geographic mapping of the cities in Golestan Province was performed using ArcGIS software. The annual incidence of oral cancer was calculated for each city and expressed as the number of cases per 100,000 population per year.ResultsA total of 390 patients with oral cancer were identified in Golestan Province between 2014 and 2021. Of these, 200 (51.3%) were male and 190 (48.7%) were female. The mean age of the patients was 56.18 ± 17.37 years, ranging from 2 to 96 years. The standardized annual incidence rate of oral cancer in Golestan Province ranged from 4.25 to 6.75 cases per 100,000 population during the study period. Among the cities of the Province, Maraveh Tappeh showed the highest incidence rate, with an average incidence of 12.35 cases per 100,000 population.ConclusionThe incidence of oral cancer in Golestan Province demonstrated temporal fluctuations during the study period, with higher rates observed in several northern cities. Geographic mapping of the disease may provide valuable insights into the potential influence of environmental, cultural, and lifestyle factors on the distribution of oral cancer and can support more targeted prevention and control strategies.

Introduction

Oral cancer, including malignancies of the oropharynx, hypopharynx, and nasopharynx, represents a significant global health concern. Cases were identified using the ICD-10 codes for malignant neoplasms of the lip, oral cavity, and pharynx (C00–C14). It is one of the most common cancers worldwide, and its incidence increases markedly with age, with approximately 95% of cases occurring after the age of 40 [1]. Oral cancers account for nearly 4% of all malignancies. The incidence is reported to be approximately three times higher in men than in women, representing about 5% of all cancers in men and 2% in women [2]. In Iran, the average incidence of oral cancer has been estimated at approximately 8.6 cases per 100,000 population [1, 2]. According to the American Cancer Society, nearly 50,000 new cases of oral cancer are diagnosed annually in the United States, with approximately 70% occurring in men. In addition, the incidence is reported to be higher among White populations compared with other racial groups [3].

Oral cancer develops from malignant transformation of cells in various oral cavity structures, including the lips, buccal mucosa, anterior two-thirds of the tongue, gingiva of the upper and lower jaws, floor of the mouth, hard palate, and retromolar trigone [4, 5]. Among these sites, the lips are the most commonly affected, followed by the tongue and the floor of the mouth [6]. Approximately 95% of oral malignancies are squamous cell carcinomas arising from the epithelial lining of the oral mucosa [7].

The exact pathogenesis of oral cancer remains incompletely understood, although multiple environmental and behavioral factors have been implicated in its development. Tobacco use and alcohol consumption are considered the most important risk factors and substantially increase the likelihood of developing oral malignancies [8, 9]. Early detection through routine oral and dental examinations plays a crucial role in identifying precancerous and cancerous lesions and significantly improves treatment outcomes [8, 9].

Geographic Information Systems (GIS) are powerful tools that integrate spatial data, hardware, software, and analytical techniques to collect, manage, analyze, and visualize geographically referenced information. Because human health is influenced by environmental conditions and geographic factors, GIS-based analyses can provide valuable insights into the spatial distribution of diseases [10]. Mapping diseases by geographic region allows researchers to identify spatial patterns, detect high-risk and low-risk areas, and support more effective public health interventions [11].

Golestan Province, located in northern Iran, is characterized by substantial ethnic diversity and a wide range of cultural and dietary practices. The region is also situated within the so‑called global “gastrointestinal cancer belt,” particularly noted for the high incidence of esophageal cancer [12]. These characteristics make Golestan Province an important setting for investigating cancers of the upper aerodigestive tract, including oral cancers. Despite the relatively high burden of such malignancies in the region, population‑based evidence regarding the geographic distribution of oral cancer in Golestan Province remains limited [10–12]. In particular, studies employing geographic mapping to identify spatial patterns and high‑incidence areas are scarce. Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate the demographic characteristics and geographic distribution of oral cancer in Golestan Province, Iran, during the period 2014–2021.

Materials & methods

This descriptive–analytical trend study was conducted on patients diagnosed with oral cancer between January 2014 and December 2021. Eligible participants included all patients with complete records during the study period, while cases with incomplete data were excluded. All available and complete patient records were included in the study.

Data were obtained from the National Cancer Registry System for Golestan Province, Iran, for the years 2014–2021. These data included information categorized by age and sex. In addition, patient records from the Cancer Research Center at Sayyad Hospital in Gorgan were reviewed to obtain further demographic, clinical, and laboratory information related to patients with oral cancer. To ensure data accuracy, all collected data were independently reviewed and verified by the study authors before inclusion in the final dataset.

After extracting patient information from the National Cancer Registry System, the accuracy of the information was reviewed and confirmed by referring to the patients’ clinical records. In the case of incomplete information or lack of accurate recording of patient information, patients or their families were contacted, maintaining confidentiality, and the required information was collected to the extent possible.

Population data for each city in Golestan Province during the study period were obtained from the National Statistics Organization. The incidence rate of oral cancer was calculated for each city and year as the number of new cases per 100,000 population. The annual incidence rates were age- and sex-standardized using the Direct Method, with the World Health Organization (WHO) World Standard Population as the reference to allow for international comparisons. Trend analysis was performed to evaluate temporal changes in incidence rates, and incidence trends were illustrated using graphical representations.

ArcGIS software (version 10.8.2) was used to visualize the spatial distribution of crude incidence rates across the cities of Golestan Province. The GIS approach was applied solely for descriptive geographic mapping rather than spatial statistical modeling. Duplicate records identified in the cancer registry were removed prior to analysis. Cities with no reported cases of oral cancer during any year of the study period were assigned an incidence value of zero for that year.

Statistical analysis

A total of 390 complete records of patients diagnosed with oral cancer were included in the analysis. Relevant information was extracted from these records for statistical evaluation.

Descriptive statistics, including mean, standard deviation, and relative frequency, were used to summarize the data. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 23).

Geographic maps of the counties in Golestan Province, Iran, were generated using ArcGIS software. The annual incidence rate of oral cancer was calculated per 100,000 population for each county. The raw incidence rates were subsequently standardized according to age and sex groups and reported for each city and region of Golestan Province. It should be noted that the GIS approach was used solely for visualization and descriptive geographic mapping; therefore, no spatial statistical analyses were performed.

Results

A total of 390 patients with oral cancer were identified in Golestan Province, Iran, between 2014 and 2021. Of these, 200 (51.3%) were male and 190 (48.7%) were female. The mean age of the patients was 56.18 ± 17.37 years. The most common anatomical site of involvement was the tongue, accounting for 88 cases (22.6%). Histopathological examination revealed that 210 cases (53.8%) were squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). The most frequently reported clinical presentation was swelling and thickening of oral tissues, observed in 133 patients (34.1%). During the study period, the incidence of oral cancer in Golestan Province ranged from 4.25 (95% CI: 3.88–4.49) to 6.75 (95% CI: 6.31–7.08) cases per 100,000 population. While certain northern regions like Maraveh Tappeh showed the highest incidence rate (12.35 cases per 100,000 population), these observations should be interpreted as geographic variations rather than statistically significant spatial clusters, as formal spatial autocorrelation tests were not performed.

Notable fluctuations in incidence rates were observed across several cities during the study years; in some years, no cases were reported, whereas in others the incidence increased markedly. These variations may be attributable to several factors, including limitations in cancer reporting and registration within the national system, disruptions related to the COVID‑19 pandemic, and population movement between cities in search of employment or healthcare services. Discussion

The findings of the present study demonstrated that Maraveh Tappeh had the highest incidence of oral cancer among the cities of Golestan Province during the study period (2014–2021), with an average incidence rate of 12.35 cases per 100,000 population. Geographic visualization using GIS also indicated that the highest incidence rates in different years varied across cities in Golestan Province and were more frequently observed in areas predominantly inhabited by the Turkmen ethnic population. Although the cities of Maraveh Tepe, Gomishan, and Aqqala showed a higher average descriptive incidence rate, it seems that these changes are mostly due to geographical changes. In addition, squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) was the most common histopathological type, and the tongue was the most frequently involved anatomical site.

Razavi et al. reported that SCC is the most common oral malignancy among older adults, while sarcomas are more frequently observed in younger individuals. Their study also identified the gingiva and tongue as the most common sites of involvement in oral cancers. These findings are largely consistent with the results of the present study [13].

Similarly, Maleki et al., reported that oral cancers are among the ten leading causes of cancer‑related mortality worldwide. In their study, the tongue was identified as the most common site of involvement, and SCC was reported as the predominant histopathological type. These findings are also in agreement with the results of the current study [14].

The results of the present study also indicated that the incidence of oral cancer in Golestan Province fluctuated during the study period, ranging from 4.25 to 6.75 cases per 100,000 population. The highest incidence was observed in 2015 (6.75 per 100,000 population), whereas the lowest incidence was recorded in 2021 (4.25 per 100,000 population).

The observed year-to-year fluctuations and zero-case years in certain districts may be attributed to the small population sizes in these areas (small number problem), which can lead to unstable incidence estimates. Consequently, these rates should be interpreted with caution.

The decrease in reported incidence during 2020 and 2021 may be partially attributed to the COVID‑19 pandemic, which likely reduced healthcare visits and cancer reporting during that period [14, 15].

A comparison with previous reports indicates that the incidence of oral cancer observed in the present study is lower than both the national average and the reported rates in several other countries. The national average incidence of oral cancer in Iran has been estimated at approximately 8.6 cases per 100,000 population. In comparison, reported incidence rates are approximately 10 per 100,000 in the United States, 7.1 in China, 6.4 in Thailand, and 12.6 in India. These differences may be related to variations in environmental exposures, nutritional patterns, and lifestyle factors such as tobacco use and alcohol consumption [14–16].

The GIS‑based analysis in the present study identified Maraveh Tappeh as the area with the higher descriptive incidence rates of oral cancer, followed by Gomishan and Aqqala. These cities are predominantly inhabited by Turkmen populations, and certain cultural and lifestyle practices may contribute to the observed distribution.

The higher incidence observed in districts such as Maraveh Tappeh could potentially be linked to outdoor occupations; however, further analytical studies are required to confirm the role of sunlight exposure, tobacco use, or ethnic-specific dietary habits in this population [10, 11]. Additionally, inadequate oral hygiene and prolonged use of dentures may also contribute to the higher incidence observed in these communities. However, these potential explanations remain hypothetical and should be confirmed by further comprehensive epidemiological studies [10–14].

Nayak et al., emphasized that GIS can be an effective tool for monitoring cancer patterns by integrating factors such as geographic location, age, sex, and environmental exposures. Such approaches allow researchers to identify high‑risk areas and evaluate potential risk factors with greater precision [17]. In the present study, GIS mapping helped identify Geographic variation of oral cancer within Golestan Province and highlighted areas that may warrant further investigation regarding environmental and lifestyle risk factors.

Irani et al., also reported that nutritional factors, poor oral hygiene, and certain infections, particularly human papillomavirus (HPV), play important roles in the development and progression of oral cancer. Effective control and management of these risk factors may contribute to reducing the incidence of oral malignancies [18].

Furthermore, Saghravanian et al., identified prolonged exposure to sunlight as an important risk factor for oral cancers, particularly lip cancers [19]. This observation is consistent with the findings of the present study, as the highest descriptive incidence rates were observed in Maraveh Tappeh, Gomishan, and Aqqala regions where many residents are engaged in outdoor occupations such as agriculture, animal husbandry, and horse breeding. Continuous exposure to sunlight in these occupations may partly explain the higher incidence of oral cancer observed in these areas [17–19].

Conclusions

The findings of the present study indicate that although the incidence of oral cancer in Golestan Province showed considerable fluctuations during the study period, an overall increasing trend was observed. Geographic mapping of oral cancer using GIS may help generate hypotheses about possible genetic, environmental, cultural, and nutritional influences on the distribution of these malignancies. Such spatial analyses can also contribute to improved data collection, surveillance, and evidence‑based planning for cancer prevention and control programs in the region.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, a primary limitation is the lack of detailed clinical data, specifically regarding tumor staging (TNM classification), histological grading, and long-term survival outcomes, which were not consistently available in the regional cancer registry. Consequently, our study provides a descriptive overview of incidence rather than a comprehensive prognostic analysis. Future research should strive to integrate clinical and pathological data to enhance the prognostic value of such regional studies.

Second, the observed year-to-year fluctuations in incidence rates, including instances of zero reported cases in certain districts, can be attributed to the ‘small number problem’ in regions with low population sizes. These unstable estimates necessitate cautious interpretation.

Third, while GIS were utilized to visualize spatial distribution, formal spatial statistical analyses (such as spatial autocorrelation tests) were not conducted. Therefore, the identification of high-incidence areas should be considered descriptive geographic variations rather than statistically significant spatial clusters.

Another limitation was the retrospective design of the study, which may have been associated with under‑registration, delayed reporting, or incomplete data collection. In addition, the National Cancer Registry System lacked detailed information on several important variables, including socioeconomic status, ethnicity, risk factors, and environmental exposures. The absence of such data limited the ability to further investigate the relationship between these factors and the incidence of oral cancer.
