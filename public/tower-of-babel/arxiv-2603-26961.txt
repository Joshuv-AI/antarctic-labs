Covariant Hamiltonian quantization of teleparallel equivalents to general relativity
David Chester, Vipul Pandey

Abstract
A covariant Hamiltonian formulation generalizing De Donder-Weyl mechanics is constructed with field strengths as velocity fields. Since the teleparallel equivalents to general relativity are quadratic in field strengths, the field-strength Hamiltonian densities are non-singular and avoid primary constraints specifically from Legendre degeneracy. In contrast, canonical general relativity and the Wheeler-DeWitt equation have a frozen formalism due to Hamiltonian constraints, while hypersurface deformations give refoliation gauge transformations. We introduce a Tomonaga-Schwinger-type equation without a preferred time coordinate by combining the generalized multisymplectic geometry with covariant phase space methods. Point-splitting regularization with renormalized hypersurface deformation generators is proposed as a candidate for hypersurface-dependent evolution. While ultraviolet divergences, operator domain issues, and anomaly freedom are still open problems, we provide a new framework for exploring nonperturbative quantum gravity that is classically equivalent to general relativity. Keywords: trinity of gravity, teleparallelism, metric-affine gravity, covariant quantum gravity, multisymplectic geometry, covariant phase space methods, Tomonaga-Schwinger equation

These teleparallel field-strength Hamiltonians are quadratic, and all the momentum fields are utilized in the theory, which avoids primary constraints directly from Legendre degeneracy. This may allow the Hamiltonian operator to provide dynamical evolution. Quantization promotes fields to operators acting on a wavefunctional describing quantum gravity. By combining insights from the multisymplectic geometry and covariant phase space communities, we propose a Tomonaga-Schwinger-like equation on hypersurfaces, which naturally leads to a notion of time evolution without relying on unitary evolution in a specific time coordinate. This provides a candidate equation of motion for nonperturbative quantum gravity that does not trivialy vanish and is classically equivalent to general relativity without introducing aditional clock fields.

The Wheeler-DeWitt equation was initially called the Einstein-Schrödinger equation, since the goal was to provide a Schrödinger-like quantum evolution of Einstein’s general relativity
[

,

[

, largely because the Einstein-Hilbert action is linear in curvature. To our knowledge, the Tomonaga-Schwinger equation has not been studied in the context of teleparallelism.

A main result of this work is that the field-strength Hamiltonian formulation applied to teleparallel theories allows for a Tomonaga-Schwinger equation. In a sense, this is a covariant generalization of the Wheeler-DeWitt equation that is timeless, yet still allows for dynamical time evolution from local gauge dependence tied to global diffeomorphism’s hypersurface deformations,

\nabla_{\dot{\Sigma}_{s}}\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}=\left(\frac{d}{ds}+\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{G}_{\Sigma_{s}}\right)\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}=0,

Conceptualizing the Hilbert bundle geometry to have a covariant derivative with the hypersurface deformation operator as a gauge field may help clarify how to obtain quantum evolution while preserving Lorentz covariance of spacetime, as arbitrary paths of hypersurfaces can be chosen, rather than a single time coordinate.

The field-strength Hamiltonian formulation applied to the teleparallel equivalents provides a framework for dynamical evolution that is classically equivalent to general relativity. This approach has a quadratic Hamiltonian and is not forced to vanish due to Legendre degeneracy, which is suggestive dynamical equation for quantum gravity. While the self-consistency of this proposal depends on integrability and anomaly cancellation, whose proof is outside the scope of this work, our approach suggests a new path towards studying quantum gravity dynamics with arbitrary hypersurface deformations and no preferred time coordinate

To motivate how De Donder-Weyl mechanics can be generalized to give covariant metric-affine Hamiltonian mechanics, first consider the action of Yang-Mills theory,

With the field strength and conjugate field momenta found, a Legendre transformation can be used to define a Hamiltonian density that generalizes the DDW Hamiltonian. Assuming

,

\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}=\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}-\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}.

(57)

To derive generalized Hamilton’s equations, the covariant action is placed in terms of
S(\phi^{A}(x),\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}(x),x^{\mu})
. Varying the covariant action allows for the generalization of the DDW equations to be found

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\left(\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}-\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}\right),

(58)

\displaystyle\delta S

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\left[\left(F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}}\right)\delta\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}+\frac{\partial F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}}{\partial\partial_{\nu}\phi^{B}}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}\delta\partial_{\nu}\phi^{B}+\left(\frac{\partial F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,B}}{\partial\phi^{A}}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,B}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\phi^{A}}\right)\delta\phi^{A}\right]

\displaystyle\approx\int d^{4}x\left[\left(F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}}\right)\delta\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}+\left(-\partial_{\nu}\left(\frac{\partial F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,B}}{\partial\partial_{\nu}\phi^{A}}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,B}\right)+\frac{\partial F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,B}}{\partial\phi^{A}}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,B}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\phi^{A}}\right)\delta\phi^{A}\right].

(59)

Therefore, the first and second field-strength equations are

\displaystyle F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Pi^{\mu}_{A}},

(60)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\left(\frac{\partial F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,B}}{\partial\partial_{\nu}\phi^{A}}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,B}\right)-\frac{\partial F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,B}}{\partial\phi^{A}}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,B}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\phi^{A}}.

(61)

As a simple example, consider electrodynamics in Minkowski spacetime. If the position field is taken to be the electromagnetic potential
\phi^{A}\rightarrow A_{\mu}
, then the velocity field strength is the electromagnetic field strength
F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}\rightarrow F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
. The field-strength action is

S(A_{\mu},F_{\mu\nu};x^{\mu})=\int d^{4}x\left(-\frac{1}{4\mu_{0}}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-j^{\mu}A_{\mu}\right).

(62)

The momentum field strength is given by

\Pi^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial F_{\mu\nu}}=-\frac{1}{2\mu_{0}}F^{\mu\nu},

(63)

which is proportional to the excitation tensor
\mathcal{H}^{\mu\nu}
containing the polarization
\vec{D}
and the magnetic field
\vec{H}
. The corresponding field-strength Hamiltonian density is

\mathcal{H}=-\mu_{0}\Pi_{\mu\nu}\Pi^{\mu\nu}+j^{\mu}A_{\mu}.

(64)

The field-strength equations are

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Pi^{\mu\nu}}=-2\mu_{0}\Pi_{\mu\nu},

(65)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\left(\Pi^{\nu\mu}-\Pi^{\mu\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial A_{\mu}}=-j^{\mu}.

(66)

Plugging the first equation into the second leads to Maxwell’s equations,
\partial_{\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=-\mu_{0}j^{\mu}
.

Next, the field-strength Hamiltonian formulation is applied to local and global metric-affine gravity.

2.2
A trinity of bundles within metric-affine gauge gravity

Metric-affine gauge gravity contains local gravitational field strengths of curvature, torsion, and nonmetricity, which are considered as generalized velocities. When isolating the trinity of gravity, this also isolates a trinity of bundles within the metric-affine bundle. As such, the field-strength Hamiltonian formulation allows for a convenient description of both MAG and the trinity of gravity. To define the metric-affine bundle, recall that the local fields are the metric
g_{ab}
, frame field
e_{\mu}^{a}
, and connection
\omega_{\mu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}
. The corresponding velocity field strengths are expressed nicely in terms of differential forms. Rather than using the differential-form language, we focus on the bundles that provide nonmetricity, torsion, and curvature as field strengths written with explicit tensor indices.

The metric-affine bundle
\mathcal{M}_{\textrm{MAG}}
can be separated into three pieces,
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
,
\mathcal{M}_{T}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{R}
. These are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Q}

\displaystyle=T^{*}X\otimes\textrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1,3}),

(67)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{T}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{2}T^{*}X\otimes\mathbb{R}^{1,3},

(68)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{R}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{2}T^{*}X\otimes\mathfrak{g},

(69)

where
\mathfrak{so}(1,3)\subset\mathfrak{g}
is often chosen for the gauge algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The local connection
\omega_{\mu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}
is the gauge potential with generators from the general linear algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(4,\mathbb{R})
, where
g_{ab}(x)
is nontrivial if
\mathfrak{g}
contains any generators in
\mathfrak{gl}(4,\mathbb{R})\ominus\mathfrak{so}(1,3)
. Translations are tied to the gauge potential
e_{\mu}^{a}
. The affine linear group consists of 20 generators. Its algebra
\mathfrak{al}(4,\mathbb{R})
is used for metric-affine gravity, which includes the frame field and torsion as a gauge field and field strength. If nonmetricity vanishes, then
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(1,3)
is chosen. Local teleparallel theories gauge the translations
T_{4}
for MTEGR or shear/scale transformations within
GL(4,\mathbb{R})/SO(1,3)
for STEGR, respectively.

The metric-affine bundle of interest is therefore
\mathcal{M}_{\textrm{MAG}}
,

\mathcal{M}_{\textrm{MAG}}=\mathcal{M}_{Q}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{T}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{R}.

(70)

As a result, taking sections of this bundle over a spacetime manifold
X
leads to the following mapping of coordinates from
X\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\textrm{MAG}}
,

x^{\mu}\rightarrow(x^{\mu},g_{ab},e_{\mu}^{a},\omega_{\mu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a},\Xi_{\mu ab},\Theta_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a},\Omega_{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}).

(71)

The gravitational field strengths now play the role of the polyvelocity in polysymplectic geometry.
Taking
\phi^{A}=\left(g_{ab},e_{\nu}^{a},\omega_{\nu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}\right)
and
F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}=\left(\Xi_{\mu ab},\Theta_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a},\Omega_{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}\right)
allows for the identification of generalized momenta.
When using this metric-affine bundle, the analogous polymomenta are equivalent to Hehl and Obukhov’s notion of “excitations”
[
24
,
23
]
, which we refer to as momentum field strengths,

\displaystyle\pi^{\rho ab}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\Xi_{\rho ab}},

(72)

\displaystyle\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\Theta_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}},

(73)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\Omega_{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}},

(74)

and are in the dual MAG bundle
\mathcal{M}_{\textrm{MAG}}^{*}
. Note that
\sqrt{-g}\equiv\sqrt{-\mbox{det}(g_{\mu\nu})}=|e|\sqrt{-\mbox{det}(g_{ab})}
.

This metric-affine polysymplectic geometry gives the following metric-affine Euler-Lagrange equations after applying variational calculus,

\displaystyle D_{\rho}\pi^{\rho ab}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{ab}}

\displaystyle=0,

(75)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial e_{\mu}^{a}}

\displaystyle=0,

(76)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}+\pi^{\mu bc}g_{ac}-\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}e_{\nu}^{b}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\omega_{\nu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}}

\displaystyle=0.

(77)

With the field strengths and excitations established, they can be utilized to apply a Legendre transformation from the Lagrangian to a polysymplectic Hamiltonian density. The metric-affine Hamiltonian is given by

\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{MAG}}=\Xi_{\rho ab}\pi^{\rho ab}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}+\Omega_{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}\sigma^{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}-|e|\mathcal{L}.

(78)

The local metric-affine Hamilton’s equations are therefore given by kinematical definitions as the first set of equations and dynamical equations as the second set from the Hamiltonian density
\mathcal{H}(g,e,\omega,\pi,\rho,\sigma;x)
,

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mu ab}=\frac{\partial|e|\mathcal{H}}{\partial\pi^{\mu ab}},\qquad D_{\mu}\pi^{\mu ab}=-\frac{\partial|e|\mathcal{H}}{\partial g_{ab}},

(79)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}=\frac{\partial|e|\mathcal{H}}{\partial\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}},\qquad 2D_{\mu}\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}=-\frac{\partial|e|\mathcal{H}}{\partial e_{\nu}^{a}},

(80)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}=\frac{\partial|e|\mathcal{H}}{\partial\sigma^{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}},\qquad(D_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu})\sigma^{\mu\nu\,\,\,b}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,a}=-\frac{\partial|e|\mathcal{H}}{\partial\omega_{\nu\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}}.

(81)

Note that in teleparallel theories of gravity, Eqs.(
77
) and (
81
) are less relevant, since the spin connection is no longer a dynamical field, unless one introduces vanishing local curvature via Lagrange multipliers. However, Lagrange multipliers in MTEGR are only required in the global formulation.

2.3
Field-strength bundle for global metric-affine gravity

Next, a similar Hamiltonian mechanics is derived for global metric-affine gravity. Here, there are two potentials
\phi^{A}=(g_{\mu\nu},\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu})
and three field strengths
F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}=(Q_{\rho\mu\nu},T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu},R^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu})
are taken as the position and velocity fields. The variation of the Lagrangian isolates the sources and momenta

\delta L(g,\Gamma,Q,T,R)=-2\sqrt{-g}\left(T^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}+H_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}\right)+\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}\delta Q_{\rho\mu\nu}+P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}\delta T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}+\Sigma_{\rho}^{\sigma\mu\nu}\delta R^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu},

(82)

where the stress tensor and hypermomentum are current-density sources defined as

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}},

(83)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}},

(84)

and the field strength excitations or generalized momentum fields are

\displaystyle\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial Q_{\rho\mu\nu}},

(85)

\displaystyle P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}},

(86)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial R^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}}.

(87)

Note that the generalized momentum for curvature
\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}
should not be confused with a hypersurface
\Sigma
or
\Sigma_{s}
.

One complication is
T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
is not a derivative of a global gravitational potential. Another is that isolating
\Gamma
implies
Q(g,\partial g,\Gamma)
. We can define
Q_{\rho\mu\nu}(g,\nabla g)=\nabla_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}
, which is more manifestly covariant, but then the dependence in terms of
\Gamma
is lost. Focusing on
\Gamma
as the natural variable obscures the manifest covariance of the matter fields
\psi^{A}
, i.e., it would be natural to define the hypermomentum
\Delta_{\rho}{}^{\mu\nu}
proportional to
\partial L/\partial\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
. This also requires us to consider
L(g,\Gamma,Q,T,R,\psi,\partial\psi)
. Instead, one can consider the covariant Lagrangian
L(g,\Gamma,Q,T,R,\psi,\nabla\psi)
, which motivates reference to the canonical stress tensor. For additional details on matter in metric-affine spacetimes, see the recent work of Obukhov and Hehl
[
43
]
. When considering matter, one must understand the redundancy of how the torsion momentum field
P_{\rho}{}^{\mu\nu}
contains information found in the hypermomentum
\Delta_{\rho}{}^{\mu\nu}
, for instance, or simply work with the canonical momentum tensor.

Focusing on the pure gravitational sector for clarity, it is unavoidable to consider the action in terms of the affine connection as a potential, since torsion is a field strength defined from the connection. Additionally, this rewrites nonmetricity as
Q(g,\nabla g)
instead of
Q(g,\partial g,\Gamma)
. Similarly, the curvature is defined in terms of
R(\Gamma,\partial\Gamma)
. As such, by considering
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
as gravitational potentials, the non-tensorial nature of the affine connection is introduced, but all of the field strengths are valid tensors, implying that the generalized momentum fields are also valid tensors.

This allows for the variations of the field strengths to be found in terms of the variations in
(\delta g,\delta\Gamma,\delta\partial g,\delta\partial\Gamma)
,

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\rho\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\delta\partial_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}-\left(\delta_{\mu}^{\alpha}g_{\lambda\nu}+\delta_{\nu}^{\alpha}g_{\lambda\mu}\right)\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\alpha\rho}-\left(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\,\,\,\mu\rho}\delta^{\beta}_{\nu}+\Gamma^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\nu\rho}\delta^{\alpha}_{\mu}\right)\delta g_{\alpha\beta},

(88)

\displaystyle\delta T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,[\mu\nu]},

(89)

\displaystyle\delta R^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\delta\partial_{[\mu|}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\sigma|\nu]}+\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda[\mu|}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\sigma|\nu]}+\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\lambda[\mu|}\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\sigma|\nu]}.

(90)

Plugging these field strength variations into the variation of the action gives

\displaystyle\delta L

\displaystyle=\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}\delta\partial_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}+\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}-\Pi^{\lambda\alpha\beta}\left(\Gamma^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\alpha\lambda}\delta^{\nu}_{\beta}+\Gamma^{\nu}_{\,\,\,\beta\lambda}\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\right)\right)\delta g_{\mu\nu}+2\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\sigma\nu}\delta\partial_{\sigma}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}}+2\Pi^{\nu\mu\beta}g_{\beta\rho}+2P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}+2\Sigma_{\gamma}^{\,\,\,\delta\alpha\beta}\left(\Gamma^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\delta\beta}\delta_{\alpha}^{\nu}\delta_{\rho}^{\gamma}+\Gamma^{\gamma}_{\,\,\,\rho\alpha}\delta_{\beta}^{\nu}\delta_{\delta}^{\mu}\right)\right)\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}.

(91)

After integrating by parts, the field-strength Euler-Lagrange equations for metric-affine gravity are derived to be

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rho}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}-\Pi^{\lambda\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\lambda\rho}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}

\displaystyle=0,

(92)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\sigma}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\sigma\nu}-\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\,\,\,\lambda\sigma}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\lambda\nu}-\Gamma^{\nu}_{\,\,\,\lambda\sigma}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\sigma\lambda}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}}+\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q_{\nu\mu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\rho}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}}

\displaystyle=0.

(93)

These equations of motion are expected to hold for arbitrary metric-affine theories of gravity, including higher-order polynomial theories in the gravitational field strengths.

Applying variational methods to the canonical action
S(g,\Gamma,\Pi,P,\Sigma)
derives a set of generalized Hamilton’s equations for global metric-affine gravity, where the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}(g,\Gamma,\Pi,P,\Sigma)
is found from the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(g,\Gamma,Q,T,R)
,

S(g,\Gamma,\Pi,P,\Sigma)=\int d^{4}x\left(Q_{\rho\mu\nu}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}+T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}+R^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}-\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}\right),

(94)

whose variation in terms of the generalized phase space fields leads to

\displaystyle\delta S

\displaystyle\approx\int d^{4}x\left[\left(Q_{\rho\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}}\right)\delta\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}+\left(T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}}\right)\delta P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}+\left(R^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}}\right)\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}\right.

(95)

\displaystyle+\left.\left(-\nabla_{\rho}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}\right)\delta g_{\mu\nu}+\left(2P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}-2\partial_{\sigma}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\sigma\nu}+2\Gamma^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\sigma\lambda}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\sigma\nu\lambda}+2\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\rho\sigma}\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\,\,\,\mu\sigma\nu}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}}\right)\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}\right].

Since the variation of the action must vanish for the classical solutions, the classical field equations of motion can be derived from these metric-affine Hamilton’s equations,

\displaystyle Q_{\rho\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}},

\displaystyle\qquad T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}},\qquad R^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\sigma\mu\nu}},

(96)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rho}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}=-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}},

\displaystyle\qquad\nabla_{\sigma}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\sigma\nu}-\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\,\,\,\lambda\sigma}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\lambda\nu}-\Gamma^{\nu}_{\,\,\,\lambda\sigma}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\sigma\lambda}-P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}}.

(97)

As shown above, global metric-affine gravity has five sets of Hamilton’s equations, three for defining the velocity field strengths and two for evolving the metric and connection fields. However, the fifth equation could be decomposed into the symmetric and antisymmetric components. The latter of which isolates the variation of the Hamiltonian density with respect to the torsion tensor,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\sigma}(\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\sigma\nu}-\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\nu\sigma\mu})-\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\,\,\,\lambda\sigma}(\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\lambda\nu}-\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\nu\lambda\mu})-\Gamma^{\nu}_{\,\,\,\lambda\sigma}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\sigma\lambda}+\Gamma^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\lambda\sigma}\Sigma_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\nu\sigma\lambda}-2P_{\rho}^{\,\,\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial T^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}}.

(98)

An important caveat to note is that for global formulations, one must take care when working within a subset of the most general metric-affine geometry. If one focuses on metric teleparallel geometry, for instance, then Lagrange multipliers must be added with constraints such as vanishing non-Riemannian curvature. Without those constraints, it is not possible to obtain the correct equations of motion in metric teleparallelism. This may relate to the fact that the torsion tensor is not a derivative of
g_{\mu\nu}
or
\Gamma^{\rho}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
. To avoid this complication, MTEGR as a local gauge theory allows for the frame field to act as a more conventional gravitational potential for torsion.

2.4
Metric teleparallel equivalent to general relativity (MTEGR)

The MTEGR action is given by taking the Riemannian Ricci scalar and placing it in a metric-teleparallel spacetime,

S_{\textrm{MTEGR}}=-\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x|e|\overline{R}=-\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x|e|\left(2\overline{\nabla}_{\mu}T^{\mu}-\frac{1}{4}T_{\rho\mu\nu}T^{\rho\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}T_{\rho\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu\rho}+T_{\mu}T^{\mu}\right)

The first term is a boundary term that drops out of the bulk classical equations of motion, assuming the fields vanish at the boundary

where the momentum field strength

where the terms quadratic in the momentum field strength are proportional to the gravitational stress tensor.

(120)

where the local covariant derivative absorbed the term with the spin connection and an overall factor of the frame field was removed.

2.5
Symmetric teleparallel equivalent to general relativity (STEGR)

The symmetric teleparallel equivalent to general relativity (STEGR) action is given by taking the Riemannian Ricci scalar and placing it in a symmetric-teleparallel spacetime,

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{STEGR}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\overline{R}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\overline{\nabla}_{\alpha}\left(\tilde{Q}^{\alpha}-Q^{\alpha}\right)-\frac{1}{4}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}Q^{\rho\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}Q^{\mu\nu\rho}+\frac{1}{4}Q_{\mu}Q^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{\mu}\tilde{Q}^{\mu}\right).

(122)

Dropping the boundary term does not affect the bulk classical equations of motion, giving

S_{\textrm{STEGR}}\approx-\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(-\frac{1}{4}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}Q^{\rho\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}Q^{\mu\nu\rho}+\frac{1}{4}Q_{\mu}Q^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{\mu}\tilde{Q}^{\mu}\right).

(123)

The boundary term is important for equivalence at the level of conserved charges and for quantum dynamics classically equivalent to general relativity.

The covariant Euler-Lagrange equations are

\nabla_{\rho}\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\nabla_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}}-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}=0.

(124)

The Euler-Lagrange equations expressed in terms of the polymomentum field are

\nabla_{\rho}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}},

(125)

where the polymomentum is defined by the following,

\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial Q_{\rho\mu\nu}}.

(126)

Expanding the right-hand side (RHS) of Eq. (
125
) gives

\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}=\mathcal{L}\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}+\sqrt{-g}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}+\sqrt{-g}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}.

(127)

To vary the Lagrangian with respect to the metric, it is convenient to rewrite the Lagrangian with explicit metric dependence,

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}Q_{\sigma\alpha\beta}\left(-\frac{1}{4}g^{\rho\sigma}g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}+\frac{1}{2}g^{\rho\beta}g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\alpha}+\frac{1}{4}g^{\rho\sigma}g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\mu\nu}g^{\sigma\beta}\right).

(128)

Symmetrizing over
(\mu\nu)
and
(\alpha\beta)
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}Q_{\sigma\alpha\beta}\left(-\frac{1}{8}g^{\rho\sigma}(g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}+g^{\mu\beta}g^{\nu\alpha})+\frac{1}{8}(g^{\rho\beta}g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\alpha}+g^{\rho\beta}g^{\nu\sigma}g^{\mu\alpha}+g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\beta}+g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\nu\sigma}g^{\mu\beta})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+\frac{1}{4}g^{\rho\sigma}g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{4}g^{\mu\nu}(g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\sigma\beta}+g^{\rho\beta}g^{\sigma\alpha})\right).

(129)

The second term in the RHS of Eq. (
127
) is found by varying the Lagrangian with respect to the metric,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{4}Q^{\mu\rho\sigma}Q^{\nu}_{\,\,\,\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}(Q^{\mu\rho\sigma}Q_{\rho\,\,\,\sigma}^{\,\,\,\nu}+Q^{\nu\rho\sigma}Q_{\rho\,\,\,\sigma}^{\,\,\,\mu})+\frac{1}{2}Q^{\rho\mu\sigma}(Q_{\rho\,\,\,\sigma}^{\,\,\,\nu}-Q_{\sigma\,\,\,\rho}^{\,\,\,\nu})\right.

(130)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\frac{1}{4}Q_{\rho}(Q^{\mu\nu\rho}+Q^{\nu\mu\rho}-2Q^{\rho\mu\nu})+\frac{1}{2}Q^{\rho\mu\nu}\tilde{Q}_{\rho}+\frac{1}{4}Q^{\mu}Q^{\nu}+\frac{1}{4}(Q^{\mu}\tilde{Q}^{\nu}+Q^{\nu}\tilde{Q}^{\mu})\right).

Treating the nonmetricity tensor as the velocity field strength for the STEGR Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(g,Q,\psi,\nabla\psi;x)
gives a covariant Hamiltonian density
\mathcal{H}(g,\Pi,\psi,\nabla\psi;x)
without constraints from Legendre degeneracy. The momentum field strength is given by

\displaystyle\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q_{\rho\mu\nu}}=-\frac{2\sqrt{-g}}{\kappa^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}Q^{\rho\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}Q^{\mu\nu\rho}+\frac{1}{2}Q^{\nu\mu\rho}+\frac{1}{2}Q^{\rho}g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{Q}^{\rho}g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}Q^{\mu}g^{\rho\nu}-\frac{1}{4}Q^{\nu}g^{\rho\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\sqrt{-g}}{\kappa^{2}}\left[L^{\rho\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\left(Q^{\rho}-\tilde{Q}^{\rho}\right)g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\left(g^{\rho\mu}Q^{\nu}+g^{\rho\nu}Q^{\mu}\right)\right].

(131)

The field-strength (or covariant DDW) Hamiltonian density is
\mathcal{H}(g,\Pi,\psi,\nabla\psi;x)
,

\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}=\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}-\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}.

(132)

The field-strength equations of motion are given by

\displaystyle Q_{\rho\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}},

(133)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rho}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}.

(134)

The traces of the momentum field give

\displaystyle\Pi^{\rho}

\displaystyle=g_{\mu\nu}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}=-\frac{2\sqrt{-g}}{\kappa^{2}}\left[\tilde{Q}^{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}Q^{\rho}+2\left(Q^{\rho}-\tilde{Q}^{\rho}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(Q^{\rho}\right)\right]=-\frac{2\sqrt{-g}}{\kappa^{2}}\left(Q^{\rho}-\tilde{Q}^{\rho}\right),

(135)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=g_{\rho\nu}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}=-\frac{2\sqrt{-g}}{\kappa^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}Q^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\left(Q^{\mu}-\tilde{Q}^{\mu}\right)-\frac{5}{4}Q^{\mu}\right]=-\frac{2\sqrt{-g}}{\kappa^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{Q}^{\mu}-\frac{1}{4}Q^{\mu}\right).

(136)

The momenta admit an inverse, so that nonmetricity as a generalized velocity field can be found in terms of the nonmetricity momentum field. The inverse of the traces gives

\displaystyle Q_{\rho}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\sqrt{-g}}\left(\frac{2}{3}\Pi_{\rho}-\frac{4}{3}\tilde{\Pi}_{\rho}\right),

(137)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\sqrt{-g}}\left(-\frac{4}{3}\tilde{\Pi}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{3}\Pi_{\mu}\right).

(138)

The nonmetricity tensor
Q_{\rho\mu\nu}
in terms of the momentum field is found by considering
\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}+\Pi_{\nu\mu\rho}

(139)

Contracting nonmetricity with the momentum field strength gives

Q_{\rho\mu\nu}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\sqrt{-g}}\left[\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}\left(\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}+\Pi_{\nu\mu\rho}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\left(\Pi^{\rho}\Pi_{\rho}-4\Pi^{\rho}\tilde{\Pi}_{\rho}-2\tilde{\Pi}^{\rho}\tilde{\Pi}_{\rho}\right)\right].

(140)

Since
\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}=Q_{\rho\mu\nu}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}/2
, gravity is purely “kinetic”, or quadratic in the velocity fields. Therefore, the field-strength Hamiltonian density is equal to the covariant Lagrangian density, giving

\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}_{STEGR}=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\sqrt{-g}}\left(\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}+\frac{1}{6}\Pi^{\mu}\Pi_{\mu}-\frac{2}{3}\Pi^{\mu}\tilde{\Pi}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{3}\tilde{\Pi}^{\mu}\tilde{\Pi}_{\mu}\right)

(141)

Expressing the Hamiltonian density in terms of the metric and the momentum field strength gives

\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{STEGR}}=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\sqrt{-g}}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}\Pi^{\sigma\alpha\beta}\left(g_{\rho\beta}g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\sigma}+\frac{1}{6}g_{\mu\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\rho\sigma}-\frac{2}{3}g_{\mu\nu}g_{\sigma\beta}g_{\rho\alpha}-\frac{1}{3}g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\beta}g_{\mu\alpha}\right).

(142)

Symmetrizing over
(\mu\nu)
and
(\alpha\beta)
due to

and
\Pi^{\sigma\alpha\beta}=\Pi^{\sigma\beta\alpha}
, respectively, gives

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{STEGR}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\sqrt{-g}}\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}\Pi^{\sigma\alpha\beta}\left[\frac{1}{2}g_{\rho\beta}\left(g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\sigma}+g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\alpha}\right)+\frac{1}{6}g_{\mu\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{3}g_{\mu\nu}\left(g_{\sigma\beta}g_{\rho\alpha}+g_{\sigma\alpha}g_{\rho\beta}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.-\frac{1}{12}\Bigl(g_{\rho\nu}\left(g_{\sigma\beta}g_{\mu\alpha}+g_{\sigma\alpha}g_{\mu\beta}\right)+g_{\rho\mu}\left(g_{\sigma\beta}g_{\nu\alpha}+g_{\sigma\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}\right)\Bigr)\right].

(143)

Alternatively, one may work with the nonsymmetrized version in Eq. (
142
) and symmetrize later in the field-strength Hamilton’s equations.

The first covariant DDW equation confirms the definition of nonmetricity,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=Q_{\rho\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}},

(144)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}}{\partial\Pi^{\rho\mu\nu}}

which agrees precisely with Eq. (

\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}=\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}{\partial F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}}
can be identified on the corresponding dual bundle, leading to a covariant Hamiltonian density
\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}=\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}-\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}
. By introducing
\delta
as the exterior derivative on the configuration space of fields, a canonical 1-form density current
\theta^{\mu}
can be introduced

\theta^{\mu}=\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}\delta\phi^{A},

(152)

leading to the polysymplectic form
\omega^{\mu}
,

\omega^{\mu}=\delta\theta^{\mu}=\delta\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}\wedge\delta\phi^{A}.

(153)

Overall, there are at least three approaches for reconciling the polysymplectic nature of covariant Hamiltonian field theories.
To obtain classical Poisson brackets or quantum commutation relations with the polymomentum field strength
\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}
that is a higher tensor rank than the field
\phi^{A}
, the following are possibilities:

1.

Multisymplectic geometry/differential forms:
\Pi{}_{A}=dx_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}

2.

Precanonical quantization/Clifford algebra:
\Pi^{\prime}{}_{A}=\gamma_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}

3.

Covariant phase space/boundary methods:
\Pi_{\Sigma A}=n_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}

Here, we explore the third option by combining multisymplectic geometry and covariant phase space methods to obtain presymplectic geometry with
(\phi^{A},\Pi_{\Sigma B})
, where the
\Sigma
subscript labels the boundary, not an index. The presymplectic momentum
\Pi_{\Sigma A}
is the normal projection of the polymomentum onto a hypersurface
\Sigma
.

The polysymplectic geometry can be pulled back to a hypersurface, which isolates presymplectic geometry. This process involves introducing a 3D hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
for the spacetime manifold
M
, where
X:\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
is a smooth embedding. The hypersurface element
d\Sigma
allows for integration over
\Sigma
, as well as

d\Sigma_{\rho}=n_{\rho}(x)d\Sigma=n_{\rho}(x)d^{3}\sigma\sqrt{|h(\sigma)|},

(154)

where
h_{ij}(\sigma)=g_{\mu\nu}\partial_{i}X^{\mu}\partial_{j}X^{\nu}
is the metric on
\Sigma
and
\sigma^{i}
are the coordinates of
\Sigma
with
i=1,2,3

[
50
,
51
,
52
]
. The local volume form on
\Sigma
is
\sqrt{h(\sigma)}d^{3}\sigma
, while
n_{\mu}(x)
is a unit vector
n_{\mu}n^{\mu}=\pm 1
normal to
\Sigma
at
x
.
The normal projection
n_{\mu}
leads to a (pre)symplectic field strength momentum
\Pi_{\Sigma A}
,

\Pi_{\Sigma A}=n_{\rho}\Pi^{\rho}_{\,\,\,A}.

(155)

The momentum field strength can be decomposed into normal and tangential components,

\Pi^{\mu}{}_{A}=(n_{\rho}n^{\rho})n^{\mu}\Pi_{\Sigma A}+\Pi^{\mu}_{||A},

(156)

where
n_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu}_{||A}=0
.

The hypersurface symplectic potential
\theta_{\Sigma}
is defined as

\theta_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma\Pi_{\Sigma A}\delta\phi^{A}=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}\delta\phi^{A},

(157)

where the hypersurface symplectic form
\omega_{\Sigma}
is

\omega_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma\delta\Pi_{\Sigma A}\wedge\delta\phi^{A}=\delta\theta_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\delta\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}\wedge\delta\phi^{A}.

(158)

Therefore, the hypersurface configuration space
\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}
contains all fields
\phi^{A}
evaluated on the hypersurface
\Sigma
, whose phase space (of fields) leads to
\Pi_{\Sigma A}
as a presymplectic momentum field on the hypersurface conjugate to
\phi^{A}
.
This allows for the following Poisson brackets for the classical hypersurface phase space fields,

\{\phi^{A}(s),\Pi_{\Sigma B}(s^{\prime})\}=\delta^{A}_{B}\delta_{\Sigma}(s,s^{\prime})=\delta^{A}_{B}\frac{\delta^{(3)}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})}{\sqrt{|h(\sigma)|}},

(159)

where
s\in\Sigma
and
\delta_{\Sigma}(s,s^{\prime})
is a Dirac delta distribution on
\Sigma
,

\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma(s^{\prime})f(s^{\prime})\delta_{\Sigma}(s,s^{\prime})=f(s).

(160)

For a Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}
that is quadratic in the velocity field strength
F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}
, a polysupermetric
M^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,AB}
may be defined by

\mathcal{L}(\phi,F;x)=\frac{1}{2}M^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,AB}(\phi;x)F_{\mu}^{\,\,\,A}F_{\nu}^{\,\,\,B}-V(\phi;x).

(161)

The terminology polysupermetric is introduced to emphasize that this object generalizes Wheeler’s supermetric on superspace
[
59
]
to the polymomentum setting. Symmetry under interchange of spacetime and field indices implies
M^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,AB}=M^{\nu\mu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,BA}.

The field-strength Hamiltonian formulation can be used when the Hamiltonian density is quadratic in the polymomentum field,

\mathcal{H}(\phi,\Pi;x)=\frac{1}{2}M_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,AB}(\phi;x)\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}\Pi^{\nu}_{\,\,\,B}+V(\phi;x),

(162)

where the inverse polysupermetric
M_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,AB}
is defined by

M^{\mu\nu}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,AB}M_{\nu\rho}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,BC}=\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}\delta_{A}^{\,\,C}.

(163)

3.2
Diffeomorphisms and gauge freedom

A smooth one-parameter family of embedded hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{s}
are specified by the embedding
X_{s}:\Sigma\rightarrow M
, with coordinates
x=X_{s}(\sigma)\in\Sigma_{s}\subset M
. The deformation vector
\xi^{\mu}
is a velocity field along
\Sigma_{s}
,

\left.\xi^{\mu}(x)\right|_{\Sigma_{s}}=\left.\frac{\partial X^{\mu}_{s}(\sigma)}{\partial s}\right|_{\sigma=X_{s}^{-1}(x)}.

(164)

The variation of a tensor field
\phi^{A}
with respect to diffeomorphisms is

\delta_{\xi}\phi^{A}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi^{A}=\xi^{\rho}\nabla_{\rho}\phi^{A}+\nabla_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}(R^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\nu})^{A}_{\,\,\,B}\phi^{B},

(165)

where
(R^{\mu}{}_{\nu})^{A}{}_{B}
encodes the Lorentz representations of
\phi^{A}
, since
A
is an abstract index for all field components, which may contain spacetime or other gauge indices.

Diffeomorphism invariance gives a classically-conserved Noether current
J^{\mu}[\xi]
,

J^{\mu}[\xi]=\theta^{\mu}(\delta_{\xi}\phi)-\xi^{\mu}L=\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi^{A}-\xi^{\mu}\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}.

(166)

This covariant Noether current has been discussed by Lee, Iyer, and Wald
[
51
,
52
]
, while subtleties of the boundary have been discussed by Wald and Zoupas
[
60
]
. Diffeomorphism invariance implies that the current is conserved,
\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}[\xi]=0

[
52
]
.
For metric-affine theories, the non-tensorial nature of the affine connection requires diffeomorphisms to be taken to second order,

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi^{A}=\xi^{\rho}\nabla_{\rho}\phi^{A}+(\nabla_{\rho}\xi^{\sigma})(R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma})^{A}{}_{B}\phi^{B}+(\nabla_{\rho}\nabla_{\sigma}\xi^{\lambda})(S^{\rho\sigma}{}_{\lambda})^{A}.

(167)

By applying the Legendre transformation, the Noether current can be expressed in terms of the field-strength Hamiltonian density,

J^{\mu}[\xi]=\Pi^{\mu}{}_{A}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi^{A}-\xi^{\mu}\Pi^{\nu}{}_{A}F_{\nu}{}^{A}+\xi^{\mu}\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{H}.

(168)

The deformation derivative
\frac{d}{ds}
is defined as

\frac{d}{ds}F[\Sigma_{s}]=\delta_{\xi}F[\Sigma_{s}],

(169)

The deformation derivative of the hypersurface momentum field gives

\frac{d}{ds}\Pi_{\Sigma A}(s)=n_{\mu}\frac{d\Pi^{\mu}{}_{A}}{ds}+\frac{dn_{\mu}}{ds}\Pi^{\mu}{}_{A}.

(170)

Infinitesimal diffeomorphisms generated by vector fields tangent to
\Sigma
should act as canonical transformations on
(\phi^{A},\Pi_{\Sigma B})
. Since the Legendre map in quadratic field strength theories do not lead to Legendre degeneracy issues, the degeneracies of
\omega_{\Sigma}
appear to be linked to the gauge symmetries, but this still must be proven. If the canonical variables (classical fields) can be updated to quantum operators, then the Hamiltonian density may not formally vanish.

The surface-deformation generator
G_{\Sigma}[\xi]
is the flux of the current through
\Sigma
,

G_{\Sigma}[\xi]=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\,J^{\mu}[\xi].

(171)

The induced flow of any hypersurface functional
O_{\Sigma}
is also generated by
G_{\Sigma}[\xi]
,

\left.\frac{d}{ds}O_{\Sigma_{s}}\right|_{s=0}=\{O_{\Sigma},G_{\Sigma}[\xi]\}.

(172)

Assuming the classical hypersurface deformation algebra closes, this family of generators provides a representation of the diffeomorphism algebra

\{G_{\Sigma}[\xi;\phi],G_{\Sigma}[\eta;\phi]\}=G_{\Sigma}[[\xi,\eta];\phi],

(173)

where the argument on the right-hand side above contains the Lie bracket of vector fields,

[\xi,\eta]^{\mu}=\xi^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\eta^{\mu}-\eta^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\xi^{\mu}.

(174)

Under these assumptions, the classical Dirac-Teitelboim hypersurface deformation algebra is found
[
30
]
. Ref.
[
61
]
contains multisymplectic/covariant formulations.

The hypersurface phase space is integrable if
G_{\Sigma}[\xi]
generates canonical transformations via

\delta G_{\Sigma}[\xi]=\Omega_{\Sigma}(\delta\phi,\delta_{\xi}\phi),

(175)

for all variations
\delta\phi
tangent to the hypersurface fields. If the condition above does not hold, there may be a boundary flux or required counterterms.
The form
\Omega_{\Sigma}
is technically presymplectic rather than symplectic, as
its degeneracy directions correspond to infinitesimal gauge transformations that act trivially on physical states. By choosing a kinematic hypersurface phase space, gauge reduction, BRST/BV methods, or group averaging can be applied
[
60
,
62
]
.
A vector field
X
provides a degeneracy direction if
\iota_{X}\Omega_{\Sigma}=0
.

The infinitesimal gauge transformations of the fields
\delta_{\epsilon}\phi^{A}
gives

X_{\epsilon}=\int_{\Sigma}d^{3}\sigma\delta_{\epsilon}\phi^{A}(\sigma)\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi^{A}(\sigma)},

(176)

where
\delta_{\epsilon}
includes diffeomorphisms of spacetime generated by
\xi^{\mu}
as well as any other internal gauge transformations associated with the theory. Within the metric-affine gauge framework, teleparallel equivalents are naturally interpreted as gauge theories with internal (Yang-Mills-like) gauge symmetries
[
12
,
18
]
. The translations
T_{4}
can be gauged for MTEGR and
GL(4,\mathbb{R})/SO(1,3)
for STEGR, respectively. Since these transformations lie in the kernel of
\Omega_{\Sigma}
, the symplectic form is presymplectic
[
50
,
51
,
52
]
.

To obtain a truly symplectic structure, one identifies a symplectic quotient space
\mathcal{P}_{\Sigma}^{\rm sym}=\mathcal{P}_{\Sigma}/\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}
that reduces the presymplectic phase space
\mathcal{P}_{\Sigma}
by quotienting out by
\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}
, the group of gauge transformations acting on
\Sigma
. Since this can be challenging, various techiniques such as BRST/Faddeev-Popov methods, could be explored to retain covariant hypersurface evolution.

To clarify why dynamical evolution can occur,
\xi^{\mu}
decomposes into normal and tangential components in relation to
\Sigma_{s}
,

\xi^{\mu}=\xi^{\perp}n^{\mu}+\xi^{\parallel}{}^{\mu},

(177)

where
\xi^{\parallel}{}^{\mu}n_{\mu}=0
is tangent to
\Sigma
and

\xi^{\perp}=\xi^{\mu}n_{\mu}
is the normal component. Tangential diffeomorphisms reparametrize hypersurface coordinates to generate gauge transformations. In general relativity, normal deformations are generated by Hamiltonian constraints, constraining them to vanish and becoming pure gauge or refoliation transformations
[
30
,
44
]
.
In the present field-strength Hamiltonian formulation for the teleparallel equivalents to general relativity, the situation may differ due to the lack of Legendre degeneracy. This suggests a possible reinterpretation on the frozen formalism within the problem of time, but further work is required.

Physical states can be obtained by gauge fixing or averaging over the group action on kinematical state space. Gravitational theories have diffeomorphisms that act nonlinearly on configuration space. For teleparallel equivalents, this becomes subtle, as there are diffeomorphisms of the global manifold
M
, yet also a local gauge structure
G
. However, the frame field links the local and global structures for gauge gravity.
Ogievetskii demonstrated how nested action of local metric-affine and conformal gauge symmetries close to give global infinite-dimensional diffeomorphisms
[
63
]
. For an affine-conformal realization of gravity, see Ref.
[
64
]
.
If a global diffeomorphism
\varphi
is taken, then the pullback finds
\phi^{A}\mapsto\varphi^{*}\phi^{A}
. The quantum states are therefore transformed by

(U_{\varphi}\Psi_{\Sigma})[\phi]=\Psi_{\Sigma}[\varphi^{*}\phi].

(178)

One may find a physical state by group averaging or integrating over all diffeomorphisms,

\Psi_{\Sigma}^{\textrm{phys}}[\phi]=\int_{\textrm{Diff}(\Sigma)}D\varphi\Psi_{\Sigma}[\varphi^{*}\phi].

(179)

If the fields
\phi^{A}
contain non-gravitational gauge fields, then the non-gravitational gauge group
G
must also be averaged. See Refs.
[
62
,
65
]
for more on algebraic quantization and group averaging.

3.3
Field quantization, Tomonaga-Schwinger equation, and regularization

Upon quantization, the fields get updated to operators on a hypersurface state space,

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}^{A}(\sigma)\Psi_{\Sigma}[\phi]=\phi^{A}(\sigma)\Psi_{\Sigma}[\phi],

(180)

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{\Sigma A}(\sigma)\Psi_{\Sigma}[\phi]=-i\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi^{A}(\sigma)}\Psi_{\Sigma}[\phi].

(181)

The Tomonaga-Schwinger equation evolves states through a hyperspace-functional differential equation,

i\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta\Sigma(\sigma)}\Psi_{\Sigma}=\hat{G}_{\Sigma}(\sigma)\Psi_{\Sigma},

(182)

where
\frac{\delta}{\delta\Sigma(\sigma)}
is the functional derivative with respect to a deformation of
\Sigma
at
\sigma
.
General-boundary formulations of QFT explore quantum states associated with hypersurfaces and gluing techniques
[
53
,
66
]
. Here, we find a differential equation for evolution.

Consider a hypersurface smearing along a path
s\mapsto\Sigma_{s}
with a deformation field
\xi^{\mu}
, giving a family of hypersurface states
\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}
.
The deformation derivative of a state gives

\frac{d}{ds}\Psi_{\Sigma}=\delta_{\xi}\Psi_{\Sigma},

(183)

leading to

i\hbar\frac{d}{ds}\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}[\phi]=\hat{G}_{\Sigma_{s}}[\xi]\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}[\phi],

(184)

where
\hat{G}_{\Sigma_{s}}[\xi]
is the quantum surface-deformation operator,

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{\Sigma_{s}}[\xi]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{s}}d\Sigma_{\mu}\left(\hat{\theta}^{\mu}(\delta_{\xi}\hat{\phi})-\xi^{\mu}\sqrt{-g}\hat{\mathcal{L}}\right)

(185)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{s}}d\Sigma_{\mu}\left(\hat{\theta}^{\mu}(\delta_{\xi}\hat{\phi})-\xi^{\mu}\hat{\Pi}^{\rho}{}_{A}\hat{F}_{\rho}{}^{A}+\xi^{\mu}\sqrt{-g}\hat{\mathcal{H}}\right).

(186)

Since the field strength Hamiltonian depends on the generalized multisymplectic momentum, not the presymplectic momentum, a definition of
\hat{\Pi}^{\mu}{}_{A}
is required.

We propose a definition of
\hat{\Pi}^{\mu}{}_{A}
as follows. The classical momentum field strength can be decomposed as

\Pi^{\mu}{}_{A}=(n_{\rho}n^{\rho})n^{\mu}\Pi_{\Sigma A}+\Pi^{\mu}_{||A}\qquad\Pi^{\mu}_{||A}=h^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\Pi^{\nu}{}_{A},

(187)

where
h^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}-n^{\mu}n_{\nu}n^{\rho}n_{\rho}
,
h^{\mu}{}_{\nu}n^{\nu}=0
, and
h^{\mu}{}_{\nu}h^{\nu}{}_{\rho}=h^{\mu}{}_{\rho}
. The quantized multisymplectic operator is reconstructed as

\hat{\Pi}^{\mu}{}_{A}=(n_{\rho}n^{\rho})n^{\mu}\hat{\Pi}_{\Sigma A}+\hat{\Pi}^{\mu}_{||A},\qquad\hat{\Pi}^{\mu}_{||A}=h^{\mu}{}_{\nu}M^{\nu\rho}{}_{AB}\hat{F}_{\rho}{}^{B},

(188)

where the theory must contain an invertible polysupermetric. For instance, the definition of
\hat{F}_{\mu}{}^{A}
depends first on the definition of
F_{\mu}{}^{A}
. If
F_{\mu}{}^{A}=\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{A}
, then

F_{\mu}{}^{A}=\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{A}=(n_{\rho}n^{\rho})n_{\mu}(n^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi^{A})+D_{\mu}\phi^{A},

(189)

and

\hat{F}_{\mu}{}^{A}=(n_{\rho}n^{\rho})n_{\mu}(n^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi^{A})+D_{\mu}\hat{\phi}^{A}.

(190)

where it is understood that

D_{\mu}\hat{\phi}\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}[\phi]=D_{\mu}\phi\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}[\phi].

(191)

To provide physical dynamics, the generating operator must be renormalized after a regularization procedure, giving
\hat{G}^{\textrm{ren}}_{\Sigma_{s}}[\xi]
.
For the Tomonaga-Schwinger equation to be integrable, the renormalized hypersurface generator must act on the dense domain
\Phi_{\Sigma}
and satisfy an anomaly-free commutator algebra,

[\hat{G}^{\textrm{ren}}_{\Sigma}[\xi],\hat{G}^{\textrm{ren}}_{\Sigma}[\eta]]=i\hbar\hat{G}^{\textrm{ren}}_{\Sigma}[[\xi,\eta]].

(192)

If the above is satisfied for a dense subset
\Phi_{\Sigma}
, then the evolution depends on endpoints rather than a chosen deformation path.
If Eq. (
192
) is satisfied, then a well-defined evolution operator is found,

U(\Sigma_{2},\Sigma_{1})=\mathcal{P}\textrm{exp}\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{\Sigma_{1}}^{\Sigma_{2}}\hat{G}^{\textrm{ren}}_{\Sigma}[\xi]\right),

(193)

where
\mathcal{P}
gives the hypersurface space path ordering. Analogous to the integrability equation,

U(\Sigma_{3},\Sigma_{2})U(\Sigma_{2},\Sigma_{1})=U(\Sigma_{3},\Sigma_{1}),

(194)

which applies for all intermediate
\Sigma_{2}
. The wavefunctional is evolved from one surface element to another via

\Psi_{\Sigma_{2}}[\xi]=\mathcal{P}\textrm{exp}\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}ds\hat{G}_{\Sigma_{s}}^{\textrm{ren}}[\xi]\right)\Psi_{\Sigma_{1}}[\xi].

(195)

Next, some regularization procedure must be chosen. Integrability requires the regularization to preserve covariance and gauge symmetry (a representation of the deformation hypersurface algebra), have local counterterms, and a closed algebra up to renormalization of local terms. Here, a mollifier (point-splitting) regularization procedure is briefly outlined.
A regulated momentum field strength
\hat{\Pi}^{\rho}_{(\epsilon)A}
is introduced, since products are otherwise ill defined

[
67
,
68
]
.
By normal ordering the momentum field strength, a regulated Hamiltonian density can be defined,

\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\epsilon)}=\frac{1}{2}M_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,AB}:\hat{\Pi}^{\mu}_{(\epsilon)A}\hat{\Pi}^{\nu}_{(\epsilon)B}:+V(\hat{\phi};x),

(196)

where
:\cdot:
gives normal ordering by removing local singular terms in the coincidence limit. The regulator allows for operator-valued distributions to be defined on a dense domain of cylindrical functionals for fixed
\epsilon>0
. The regulator is intrinsic to the hypersurface, is covariant under diffeomorphisms, and does not prefer a specific foliation. Other regularization schemes such as heat-kernel methods, dimensional regularization, Pauli–Villars regulators, or covariant Hadamard subtraction could be explored.

To ensure that valid quantum evolution is obtained, integrability of the hypersurface deformations must allow for path independence, which relates to anomaly freedom of the quantum hypersurface deformation algebra. The momentum operator, Hamiltonian density, and smeared hypersurface deformation generator must first be regularized to control ultraviolet divergences. The hypersurface
\Sigma
admits a covariant point-splitting regulator, where
\rho_{(\varepsilon)}(x,y)
is a smooth, symmetric mollifier satisfying

\rho_{(\varepsilon)}(x,y)\geq 0,\quad\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma\rho_{(\varepsilon)}(x,y)=1,\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\rho_{(\varepsilon)}(x,y)=\delta_{\Sigma}(x,y).

(197)

The regulated momentum operator is given by

\hat{\Pi}_{(\varepsilon)A}^{\mu}=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma\rho_{(\varepsilon)}(x,y)\hat{\Pi}^{\mu}_{\,\,\,A}(y),

(198)

where
\hat{\Pi}^{\mu}_{(\varepsilon)A}
is well-defined on
\Phi_{\Sigma}
for a fixed
\varepsilon>0
.
Similarly, the regulated Hamiltonian and hypersurface deformation generator are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma\rho_{(\epsilon)}(x,y)\hat{\mathcal{H}},

(199)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{(\varepsilon)}_{\Sigma}[\xi]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\left(\theta^{\mu}_{(\epsilon)}(\delta_{\xi}\hat{\phi}_{(\epsilon)})-\xi^{\mu}\hat{\Pi}^{\rho}_{{(\epsilon)}A}\hat{F}_{\rho}^{{(\epsilon)}A}+\xi^{\mu}\sqrt{-g}\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\epsilon)}\right).

(200)

The renormalized Hamiltonian density operator and hypersurface deformation generator are defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\textrm{ren}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\Sigma}^{(\varepsilon)}+\sum_{i}c_{i}(\varepsilon)\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{i}[\xi]\right),

(201)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{\textrm{ren}}_{\Sigma}[\xi]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\hat{G}^{(\varepsilon)}_{\Sigma}[\xi]+\sum_{i}c_{i}(\varepsilon)\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{i}[\xi]\right),

(202)

where
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{i}[\xi]
and
\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{i}[\xi]
are local hypersurface operators constructed from
\phi^{A}
and their intrinsic derivatives on
\Sigma
, and
c_{i}(\varepsilon)
are regulator-dependent coefficients.
This mirrors the renormalization of stress-energy operators in curved spacetime and the treatment of boundary charges in the Wald-Zoupas framework
[
52
,
60
]
. Anomaly freedom must be explicitly verified for each theory and choice of regulator.
The regulated operators typically diverge in the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit. Renormalization adds local counterterms stemming from diffeomorphism invariance or gauge symmetry, which involves
\hat{H}\rightarrow\hat{H}+\sum_{i}c_{i}(\varepsilon)\mathcal{O}_{i}
, where the
\mathcal{O}_{i}
are local scalar operators as functions of fields and velocity field strengths, while
c_{i}(\varepsilon)
are regulator-dependent coefficients.

When evolving across a path of boundaries
\Sigma_{s}
, one finds a path of wavefunctionals
\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}[\phi]
, which can be evolved with a Tomonaga-Schwinger equation

i\hbar\frac{d}{ds}\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}[\phi]=\hat{G}_{\Sigma_{s}}^{\textrm{ren}}[\phi]\Psi_{\Sigma_{s}}[\phi].

(203)

For self-consistent quantum evolution,
\hat{G}_{\Sigma}^{\textrm{ren}}[\xi]
must be local functionals in the fields, transform under diffeomorphisms of
\Sigma
covariantly, give a commutator algebra analogous to the classical hyperspace deformation algebra, and preserve the dense domain of
\Phi_{\Sigma}
. What has been confirmed here is that the quadratic nature of the teleparallel equivalents allows for a non-vanishing Hamiltonian whose regularization on
\Sigma
can be studied. Future work must study the precise details on whether the obtained results are free of anomalies.

3.4
Euclidean vs Lorentzian formulations

Next, a Euclidean or Lorentzian approach can be explored. This choice is not our primary concern here, so we briefly outline both directions. In the Euclidean (Osterwalder-Schrader) approach, a positive Gaussian reference measure is defined with an interacting measure defined by weighting. This defines a natural
L^{2}
inner product on hypersurface wave functionals and admits reconstruction of a corresponding Lorentzian theory under the Osterwalder-Schrader (OS) axioms
[
69
,
70
,
71
,
72
]
. In contrast, in Lorentzian signature, the quadratic form defined by the polysupermetric leads to Fresnel-type oscillatory Gaussian cylindrical functionals. These are not probability measures but distributions acting on appropriate test functionals
[
73
,
74
]
. For real-time expectation values, causal response, and unitary evolution of density matrices, the Schwinger-Keldysh formalism uses doubled fields with causal ordering to provide a Lorentzian theory
[
75
,
76
,
77
,
78
,
79
]
.

The hypersurface configuration space
\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}=\{\phi^{A}|_{\Sigma}\}
is an infinite-dimensional Fréchet manifold, giving field configurations on
\Sigma
. These spaces do not admit a canonical translation-invariant or
\sigma
-finite measure
[
80
,
81
]
. Given a fixed reference field configuration
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}
, the polysupermetric induces a positive quadratic form on field fluctuations on
\Sigma
,

\left<\delta\phi,\delta\phi\right>_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma M_{AB}(\phi_{0})\delta\phi^{A}\delta\phi^{B},

(204)

where
n_{\mu}
is the unit normal to
\Sigma
and
M_{AB}(\phi_{0})=n_{\mu}n_{\nu}M^{\mu\nu}{}_{AB}(\phi_{0})
is the hypersurface supermetric evaluated by
\phi_{0}
. The choice of
\phi_{0}
defines Gaussian fluctuations and does not make the formalism dependent on a preferred background hypersurface
[
69
,
82
]
.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}
is infinite-dimensional, it does not admit a canonical
\sigma
-finite measure
[
80
,
81
]
. A rigorous framework can be found by introducing a cylindrical structure through finite-dimensional projections

\pi_{F}:\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad\pi_{F}(\phi)=\left(\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma f_{1A}\phi^{A},\dots,\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma f_{NA}\phi^{A}\right),

(205)

where
\{f_{iA}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are smooth compactly supported tensor densities on
\Sigma
. The smeared coordinates associated with
\pi_{F}
are

x_{i}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma(\sigma)f_{iA}(\sigma)\phi^{A}(\sigma).

(206)

A functional
\Psi_{\Sigma}[\phi]
is said to be cylindrical if it factors through such a projection,

\Psi_{\Sigma}[\phi]=F\left(x_{1}(\phi),\dots,x_{N}(\phi)\right),\qquad F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(207)

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the Schwartz space. Cylindrical functionals form a dense subspace
\Phi_{\Sigma}\subset\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma})

[
83
,
74
]
.

Fixing the reference field
\phi_{0}
allows one to define a Gaussian reference measure on each finite-dimensional projection,

d\mu_{F}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{Z_{F}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}x_{i}(C_{F}^{-1})^{ij}x_{j}\right)d^{N}x,

(208)

where the covariance matrix is induced by the polysupermetric,

C_{F}^{ij}=\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma f_{iA}M_{AB}(\phi_{0})f_{jB}.

(209)

The family

4
Conclusions

In conclusion, we propose that the field-strength Hamiltonian formulation of the teleparallel equivalents to general relativity may provide a covariant alternative to the canonical Einstein-Hilbert action. Since the teleparallel actions are quadratic in their field strengths, the Legendre transform is regular and the Hamiltonian does not vanish from Legendre degeneracy. The polysymplectic geometry of De Donder-Weyl mechanics was generalized to include field strengths as velocities, which helps avoid momentum constraints from Legendre degeneracy. Presymplectic geometry on hypersurfaces is found by combining generalized polysymplectic geometry with covariant phase space methods. The field-strength Hamiltonian formulation is introduced for metric-affine gravity, whose subsectors admit fiber bundles that generalize jet bundles to obtain field strengths as velocities. This covariant Hamiltonian formulation was shown to reproduce the classical equations of motion for MTEGR and STEGR.

In this proposed framework, diffeomorphisms as hypersurface deformations decompose into pure gauge transformations in the hypersurface bulk and nontrivial boundary flux operators that may generate dynamics. Such a hypersurface formulation admits classical Poisson brackets and quantum commutation relations defined intrinsically on the hypersurfaces. A Tomonaga-Schwinger-type equation provides a candidate for many-fingered time evolution without choosing a preferred time coordinate. This suggests that the teleparallel equivalents and their Tomonaga-Schwinger-like equations may provide an alternative to the Wheeler-DeWitt equation, as the quantum wavefunctional is no longer trivially annihilated and the structure of the frozen formalism may be modified.

While nonperturbative quantum evolution is suggested, this does not establish a complete dynamical theory of quantum gravity. Ultraviolet divergences, non-invariant domain of the dense subspace, and anomalies in the hypersurface deformation algebra must all be avoided. Integrability would be obtained when a Hilbert-bundle connection over embeddings contains teleparallel geometry with vanishing curvature (in addition to the teleparallel spacetime geometry), suggesting a possible geometric interpretation of anomaly freedom. While the many-fingered evolution does not assume unitarity, unitary evolution is found when the Hilbert bundle is metric compatible. Whether these conditions can be found in general remains an open question, but understanding that anomaly freedom goes hand-in-hand with teleparallel Hilbert-bundle geometry may help with further exploration.

The teleparallel field-strength formulation of STEGR suggests a new route towards quantum gravity. It is also an open question on whether there still is a problem of time, as one must prove that the Hamiltonian evolves physical data, an inner product of wavefunctionals is well-defined, the operators are self-adjoint, no leakage of boundary flux, the theory is integrable (anomaly-free) locally and globally, and that observables are well-defined. The teleparallel theories obtain the same classical dynamics of general relativity and therefore are compatible with the experimental support for classical GR. In future work, it is worth exploring whether the problematic aspects of canonical general relativity such as the frozen formalism may be modified in teleparallel field-strength Hamiltonian formulations.
