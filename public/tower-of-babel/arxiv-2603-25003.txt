Common Real Secants to Pairs of Real Twisted Cubic Curves Thanks: Common Real Secants to Pairs of Real Twisted Cubic Curves version 1.0
Saima Aslam, Matthew Faust, Jonathan D. Hauenstein, Jordy Lopez Garcia, Bryson Kagy, Margaret H. Regan, Charles W. Wampler, Albert Zhang

Abstract
. It is well established that a general pair of twisted cubic curves in complex projective space has ten common secant lines. As an initial investigation, we show that the monodromy group of the ten common secant lines over the complex numbers is the full symmetric group demonstrating that the common secant lines have no special structure over the complex numbers. We then investigate a novel question in real algebraic geometry: describe the possible collections of ten common secant lines to a pair of real projective twisted cubic curves. In addition to distinguishing between real and nonreal secant lines, we introduce a refinement of this classification which takes intersection points into account yielding totally real, partially real, and minimally real secant lines. Using computational algebraic geometry as well as combinatorics, we show that for each k k between 0 and 10, there exist pairs of real twisted cubic curves with exactly k k common totally real secant lines. We also obtain examples of real twisted cubics whose sets of common real secants cover a wide range of possibilities within our admissible classification of common real secant lines.

Introduction

Twisted cubic curves
are smooth rational curves of degree three in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}({\mathbb{C}})
. This class of curves have been studied for nearly two centuries

14

As stated in

As a curve in

For example, the

Problem 1.1

Given two general twisted cubic curves in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}({\mathbb{C}})

Schubert Calculus can be used to obtain the following classical result, which is a consequence of

Theorem 1.2

Two general twisted cubic curves in

Two natural follow-up questions arise from this theorem and, to the best of our knowledge,

Problem 1.3

What is the monodromy group of the ten common secant lines

Theorem 1.4

The monodromy group of the ten common secant lines to two twisted cubic curves in

We prove Theorem

Once identified, we use the certified path-tracking software

2.
Secants of twisted cubics

Our proofs of Theorems
1.4

1.6

1.7
will use a formulation of the problem which is intrinsic to the geometry of the parameter space
\mathscr{H}
of twisted cubic curves
[
8

. Using a parametrization of the standard twisted cubic

, we describe common secant lines via rank conditions.
To that end, we regard vectors in
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
(and, by extension, points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}({\mathbb{C}})
) as column vectors. Let
C_{0}
denote the standard twisted cubic as in (
1
)
and
v(a)\vcentcolon=(1,a,a^{2},a^{3})^{T}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
.
When

[s,t]=[1,a]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})

It is known that every twisted cubic in

is the image of

under a projective linear transformation

8

\mathrm{GL}_{4}({\mathbb{C}})

{\mathbb{C}}

M\cdot[x]\vcentcolon=[Mx],\quad 0\neq x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}

is well-defined and scaling

C_{1}\vcentcolon=\overline{\bigl\{[Mv(s)]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{3}({\mathbb{C}})\mid s\in{\mathbb{C}}\bigr\}}.

In particular,
C_{1}
only depends on the equivalence class
[M]\in

\mathrm{PGL}_{4}({\mathbb{C}})

\vcentcolon=\mathrm{GL}_{4}({\mathbb{C}})/{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

The group

{\mathbb{P}}^{3}({\mathbb{C}})

{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{C}}^{4\times 4})\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{15}({\mathbb{C}})
consisting of points
[M]

Hence, we can define a map
\mathrm{PGL}_{4}({\mathbb{C}})\to\mathscr{H}

Remark 2.1

When working with

3.
Monodromy

The monodromy group of ten common secant lines to twisted cubic curves is a subgroup of
S_{10}
. This monodromy group encodes
which permutations of
the ten common secant lines are possible
as the pair of twisted cubic curves move in a closed loop such that the common secant lines
vary smoothly.
The following proves Theorem
1.4
by finding two closed loops for which the corresponding monodromy action generates
S_{10}
using
F
in (
6
). To simplify notation, set
x\vcentcolon=(t_{1},t_{2},s_{1},s_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
so that (
6
)
can be written as
F(x;M)=0
.

We utilize the following procedure
which has been
utilized for other problems before, e.g., see
[
13
,
9
]
.
We select a sufficiently general
base point
[M_{0}]\in\mathrm{PGL}_{4}({\mathbb{C}})
for which
F(x;M_{0})=0
has
40
nonsingular isolated solutions
which arise in 10 groups of four, as summarized in Proposition
2.2
.
This is sufficient since
\mathscr{H}
is path-connected. For each of the 10 groups, we select a representative and assign an ordering
x_{1},\dots,x_{10}
.
We then follow a two-step process to prove Theorem
1.4
\colon
a heuristic search followed by certification.

For the proof of our result, we will take
M_{0}

to be the parameters listed in Example
2.4

and

to be the points
as listed there as well.

3.1.

Suppose that

5.
Discussion and Open Problems

Over the complex numbers, Theorem
1.2
provides the well-known count of ten common secants to two general twisted cubic curves.
We formulated and investigated
the real analogue aiming to
determine the possible collections of ten common secant lines to a pair of real twisted cubics.
In particular, we classified
real secants as totally real, partially real, or minimally real, and used combinatorics
to determine that there are a total of 161 admissible 3-tuples
(n_{t},n_{p},n_{m})
. We then used
Python
scripts with
Bertini
to explore the parameter space
\mathscr{H}({\mathbb{R}})
to realize
128 of the 161 admissible 3-tuples
(n_{t},n_{p},n_{m})
. We used
alphaCertified

to certify the solutions associated with the realizable 3-tuples found, yielding Theorem
1.7
.
A particular case is formulated in
Theorem
1.6
,
which posits that any number from
0
to
10

of totally real secant lines is possible.
