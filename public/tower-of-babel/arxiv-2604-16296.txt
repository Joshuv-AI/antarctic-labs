Valuatively independent bases for the Fermat family of cubic curves
Jakob Hultgren, Sohaib Khalid

Abstract
Let π : ( X , L ) → 𝔻 ∗ \pi:(X,L)\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{*} be the Fermat family of cubic curves in ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} . For each k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , we construct an explicit valuatively independent basis for H 0 ​ ( X , L k ) H^{0}(X,L^{k}) in terms of restrictions of sections in H 0 ​ ( ℙ 2 , 𝒪 ℙ 2 ​ ( l ) ) H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(l)) . As a consequence, we get an explicit formula for the canonical tropical theta functions and the canonical cost function defined by any valuatively independent bases of H 0 ​ ( X , L k ) H^{0}(X,L^{k}) . We show that the canonical tropical theta functions can be described as fundamental solutions to a real Monge-Ampère operator and the canonical cost function can be described intrinsically in terms of the monodromy of a Hessian structure on the essential skeleton. Notably, the canonical tropical theta functions differ from the ones induced by a monomial basis and the canonical cost function differs from the one induced by the ambient projective space.

Introduction

Given a polarised degenerating family
\pi:(X,L)\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
of Calabi-Yau manifolds which is meromorphic over the punctured unit disc
\mathbb{D}^{*}
, a central problem in complex geometry is to determine the limiting behaviour of the canonical Ricci-flat Calabi-Yau metrics
\omega_{t}\in c_{1}(L_{t})
on the fibres
X_{t}
as
t\to 0
. A very important special case is when the degeneration is a
large complex structure limit point
, which is the subject of the
Strominger–Yau–Zaslow (SYZ)
picture of mirror symmetry, which predict that for small
t
, the fiber
X_{t}
admits a large subset
U_{t}
carrying a fibration of special Lagrangian tori of
(X_{t},\omega_{t})
.

A program initiated by Yang Li uses local and global stability estimates for real and complex Monge-Ampère equations together with techniques from non-Archimedean geometry to prove this and the main technical challenge in this program has been to establish existence and regularity of solutions to a real Monge-Ampère equation on the essential skeleton
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
of the family (see
[
Li23
,
PS22
,
BMPS26
,
GY24
]
and the references therein.)

Due to the close connections between the theory of real Monge-Ampère equations and the theory of optimal transport, it is natural to attempt to formulate an optimal transport problem whose optimiser can be shown to solve this real Monge-Ampère equation. Indeed, this is the approach taken in
[
HJMM22
,
Li24
,
AH23
]
. More generally, the picture that emerged from this is that such an optimal transport problem should be formulated on
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)\times B
where
B
is to be thought of as the essential skeleton
\operatorname{Sk}(X^{\vee},\pi^{\vee})
of a degenerating family
\pi^{\vee}:X^{\vee}\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
mirror to
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
. In
[
Li25
]
, Yang Li proposed a framework inspired by ideas from the Gross-Siebert program, where canonical bases of
H^{0}(X,L^{k}),k\geq 1
are identified with lattice points in the top-dimensional faces of
B
(see, for example
[
GHS19
]
), whereby a
valuative
cost function for the optimal transport problem appears as the limit of straightforward valuations in these basis elements

c_{k}(x,s)=k^{-1}\operatorname{val}_{x}(s),x\in\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi),s\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})\subset B.

(1)

As explained in
[
Li25
]
, a key property of the canonical basis needed to formalise the construction in (
1
) is
valuative independence
. However, showing the existence of a valuatively independent bases was up until recently an open problem. After the first version of the present note was made public, Blum-Liu
[
BL26
]
showed, using a sophisticated but non-constructive argument, that
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
admits a valuatively independent basis for sufficiently large and divisible
k
. At the same time, in a landmark achievement, Yang Li
[
Li26
]
used the existence of such a basis to produce the desired special Lagrangian torus fibration
U_{t}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
on subset
U_{t}
of asymptotically full volume, in other words such that
|U_{t}|/|X_{t}|\rightarrow 1
where
|\cdot|
denotes the Calabi-Yau volume measure. This proved what is often referred to as the
metric
SYZ-conjecture.

However, very little is known about the subsets
U_{t}
. Their shape is determined by regularity properties of the minimisers of the optimal transport problem. As the valuatively independent basis and thus the resulting cost function is not explicit, only limited information is available. In particular, while it is known that the sets
U_{t}
can be picked so that their union (over
t
) of their images in
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
is a dense subset, it is not known whether this subset is connected, its complement has codimension larger than 1 or if it admits an explicit description. In order to prove a more precise, or effective, version of the SYZ conjecture (see for example Corollary 2 in
[
AH23
]
) more information about the valuative cost function is needed.

The normalised valuations of basis elements
\theta_{s}=c_{k}(\cdot,s)=k^{-1}\operatorname{val}(s)
are often called
tropical theta functions
. It is important to note that both the sets of tropical theta functions and the valuative cost function determined by (
1
) are independent of the choice of valuatively independent basis (see Lemma 6.3 in
[
BL26
]
, Lemma 3.4 in
[
Li26
]
and the end of Section
3
). Consequently, while there can be many different valuatively independent bases, the tropical theta functions and the valuative cost function are
canonical objects
associated to the family.

Moreover, the closure of the set of tropical theta functions in the space of continuous functions on
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
, which we will denote by
B
, is another invariant of the family
\pi:(X,L)\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{*}
. While the valuative cost function is defined on
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)\times B
, a priori, very little is known about this set. Given the conjectural picture explained above, it is natural to expect that
B
is a polyhedral complex of dimension
d
.

The purpose of this paper is to provide an explicit construction of a sequence of bases
H^{0}(X,L^{k}),k\geq 1
when
(X,L)
is given by the Fermat family of cubic curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and use this to describe the tropical theta functions,
B
and the valuative cost function explicitly (see statements 2, 3 and 4 of Theorem
1
and Proposition
1
).

Before stating the theorem, we recall that a basis
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{r}
of
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
is
valuatively independent
if for any
n\in\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
, we have

\operatorname{val}_{n}((a_{1}\theta_{1}+\dots+a_{r}\theta_{r})/\tau)=\min\{\operatorname{val}_{t}(a_{i})+\operatorname{val}_{n}(\theta_{i}/\tau)\ :\ a_{i}\neq 0\}.

(2)

(Here,
\tau
is a local trivialising section of
L^{k}
and the notion of valuative independence is independent of its choice.)

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be the Fermat family of cubic curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
,

x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}-t(x_{0}^{3}+x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3})=0,

polarised by the restriction
L
of
-K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
and let
k\geq 1
. Then,
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
has a valuatively independent
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
-basis
\{\theta_{m}^{k}\}
with the following properties.

1.

For each
k\geq 1
, there exists a linear map
S_{k}\in GL(H^{0}(X,L^{k}),\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))
such that

\theta^{k}_{m}=\sigma^{k}_{m}+tS_{k}(\sigma^{k}_{m})

where
\sigma^{k}_{m}
denotes the (restriction of) the degree
k
monomial section corresponding to
m\in\partial\Delta_{k}
. The coefficients of
S_{k}
in the monomial basis
\{\sigma^{k}_{m}\}
are formal power series in
t
with integer coefficients.

2.

For each
k,\ell\geq 1
, and each
m\in\partial\Delta_{k}\subseteq\partial\Delta_{k\ell}
, we have

\frac{1}{k\ell}\operatorname{val}_{n}(\theta^{k\ell}_{m})=\frac{1}{k}\operatorname{val}_{n}(\theta^{k}_{m})

for each
n\in\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
. In particular, the function
\hat{\theta}_{m}\in C^{0}(\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi),\mathbb{R})
defined by
n\mapsto k^{-1}\operatorname{val}_{n}(\theta^{k}_{m})
(called the
normalised tropical theta function associated to
m
) does not depend on
k
.

3.

Let

B\subseteq C^{0}(\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi),\mathbb{R})

denote the closure of the set of normalised tropical theta functions in the normed space
C^{0}(\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi),\mathbb{R})
. There is a homeomorphism
\partial\Delta\longrightarrow B

taking
m\in\partial\Delta\cap\mathbb{Q}^{2}
to
\hat{\theta}_{m}\in B
.

4.

Letting
\hat{\theta}_{m}\in B\subseteq C^{0}(\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi),\mathbb{R})
also denote the image of
m\in\partial\Delta
(which may not necessarily be a rational point) under the homeomorphism in 3, we have that
\hat{\theta}_{m}
satisfies the real-Monge-Ampère equation

\operatorname{MA}(-\hat{\theta}_{m})~=~9\delta_{F(m)},

where
F:\partial\Delta\to\partial\Delta^{\vee}
is given by

F(m)=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{v\in\operatorname{Vert(\Delta^{\vee}))}}(1-\langle v,m\rangle)v\in\partial\Delta^{\vee}.

Theorem
1
establishes that
B
in (
1
) is given by the boundary of the (anti-canonical) moment polytope of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and valuatively independent sections of
H^{0}(X,L^{k}),k\geq 1
, are naturally identified (by their leading order terms) with its lattice points. Moreover,
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
carries a natural Hessian structure induced by the toric data of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which in turn is used to define the Monge-Ampère operator appearing in statement 4 of the theorem above (see Section
2
, in particular Corollary
1
).

Remark 1
.

The monomial sections
\{\sigma^{k}_{m}\}
of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},(-K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})^{k})
also determine (by restriction) a basis of
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
. However, this basis is not valuatively independent. The tropicalizations of the elements in this basis are not fundamental solutions to a real Monge-Ampère equation and, while the basis does induce a cost function which incidentally agrees with the cost function determined by the ambient toric manifolds in
[
HJMM22
,
Li24
,
AH23
]
, this cost function is defective in the sense that it does not always produce solutions to real Monge-Ampère equations (see Example 1–3 in
[
AH23
]
, Example 2.34 in
[
Li24
]
). A notable consequence of the theorem above is that valuative cost function is different from the cost function considered in
[
HJMM22
,
AH23
,
Li24
]
.

As explained in
[
Li25
]
, constructing a basis which is
locally
valuatively independent, i.e. satisfies (
2
) for all
n
in a small open set of
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
, is a relatively straightforward task. The key difficulty is to produce a basis such that (
2
) holds globally on
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
. This problem has recently attracted considerable attention. For example, see
[
BL26
,
CMMM26
]
.

We will now outline how Theorem
1
is proved. A central idea in our method of proof is to use a canonical cost function
c:\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)\times B\to\mathbb{R}
induced by the Hessian structure on
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
, and look for a basis satisfying (
1
). The canonical cost function
c
, which was introduced in
[
HO19
]
, is explicitly computable using the monodromy data of
\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)
as an affine manifold (see Section
2
).

It provides, for each basis element, an
a priori
indication of the vanishing orders along each strata of the central fiber, and thus a heuristic for how to construct such a basis. From the explicit expression for
c
, we can inductively construct the valuatively independent basis for
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
from valuatively independent bases for
H^{0}(X,L^{r})
with
r<k
by adding correction terms to monomials of the form
\lambda\sigma\cdot S
where
\sigma
is a section given by an appropriate monomial term,
S
is an appropriate member of the valuatively independent basis of
H^{0}(X,L^{r})

and
\lambda
is an appropriate constant.

We expect some version Theorem
1
to hold for general polarised generating families of Calabi-Yau manifolds. Before moving on, we will explain with some more detail what can be expected for the Dwork family in a toric Fano manifold
Y
with moment polytope
\Delta\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}=M_{Y}\otimes\mathbb{R}
. First of all, for any vertex
v
of its dual
\Delta^{\vee}\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}=N_{Y}\otimes\mathbb{R}
, the quotient map
\alpha_{v}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow N_{\mathbb{R}}/v\mathbb{R}
is injective on the star of
v
in
\partial\Delta^{\vee}
and any two such maps
\alpha_{v},\alpha_{v^{\prime}}
induce compatible integral affine structures on the open faces of
\operatorname{St}(v)\cap\operatorname{St}(v^{\prime})
. This means that given a choice of singular locus
\Sigma
which separates each co-dimension one face in
\partial\Delta^{\vee}
into components, one for each vertex, we get an integral affine structure and thus a real Monge-Ampère operator
\operatorname{MA}
on
\partial\Delta^{\vee}\setminus\Sigma
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
Y
be a toric Fano manifold with moment polytope
\Delta
and
c:B\times\operatorname{Sk}(X,\pi)\to\mathbb{R}
be the valuative cost function associated to the Dwork family
\pi:(X,L)\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
in
Y\times\mathbb{D}^{*}
. Then,
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
has a valuatively independent
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
-basis
\{\theta_{m}^{k}\}
such that statements 1, 2 and 3 of Theorem
1
hold verbatim. Moreover,
the following version of statement 4 holds: There is a function
F:\partial\Delta\to\partial\Delta^{\vee}
such that for each
m\in\partial\Delta
, there is a choice of singular locus
\Sigma_{m}
such that
F(m)\notin\Sigma_{m}
and

\operatorname{MA}(-\hat{\theta}_{m})~=~(L_{t})^{\dim X_{t}}\delta_{F(m)}

on
\partial\Delta^{\vee}\setminus\Sigma_{m}
.

Outline of the paper.
In Section 2, we obtain an ansatz of the valuative cost function from the framework of Hessian manifolds and affine
\mathbb{R}
-bundles introduced in
[
HO19
]
. In Section 3, we extract the valuative information from the ansatz and inductively construct a basis whose valuations match with the cost function.

Definition 1

Lemma 1

The map

Lemma 5
.

Suppose

are positive integers. Then, the following are equivalent.

1.

We have

\operatorname{val}_{n}(x^{3\ell}y^{3u})\geq\operatorname{val}_{n}(y^{3a-3}+x^{3b})\textrm{ for every }n\in\left[n_{1},n_{2}\right]

\operatorname{val}_{n}(x^{3\ell}y^{3u})<\operatorname{val}_{n}(y^{3a}+x^{3b})\textrm{ for some }n\in\left[n_{1},n_{2}\right].

\Lambda_{a,b}=\left\{(m,s)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\ :\ m>0,\ s>0,\ \frac{a}{b}m<a-s\leq\frac{a-1}{b}m+1\right\}.

Writing

\operatorname{val}_{n}(x^{3\ell}y^{3u})\geq\operatorname{val}_{n}(y^{3a-3}+x^{3b})\textrm{ for every }n\in\left[n_{1},n_{2}\right]

\operatorname{val}_{n}(x^{3\ell}y^{3u})<\operatorname{val}_{n}(y^{3a}+x^{3b})\textrm{ for some }n\in\left[n_{1},n_{2}\right]
