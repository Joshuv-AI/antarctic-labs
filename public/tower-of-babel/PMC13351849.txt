HER2DX genomic assay in HER2-positive early breast cancer treated with trastuzumab and pertuzumab: A correlative analysis from a real-world cohort
Authors: Beatriz Alonso de Castro, Sandra Cobo, María Alonso Pena, Julia Pérez Malillos, Cristina Reboredo Rendo, Eva Pérez López, Oleguer Castillo, Valeria Sirenko, Angela Aguirre, Paula Blasco, Lourdes Calvo Martínez, Patricia Galván, Esther Sanfeliu, Paula Saavedra Nieves, Ángel Concha López, Aleix Prat, Fara Brasó-Maristany, Silvia Antolín Novoa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHER2DX genomic assay is a genomic tool developed for personalizing care in early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer. This real-world analysis examines its associations with pathological complete response (pCR) and invasive disease-free survival (IDFS) following trastuzumab- and pertuzumab-based neoadjuvant therapy.Materials and methodsRetrospective, observational, single-center study of 156 patients with stage I–III HER2-positive breast cancer treated with dual HER2 blockade–based neoadjuvant therapy between February 2015 and May 2022 at University Hospital A Coruña. HER2DX and clinicopathological variables were assessed in pretreatment tumor biopsies. Statistical analyses included Fisher's exact test, logistic regression, Cox proportional hazards models, and Kaplan–Meier estimates.ResultsHER2DX was performed in 111 tumors (71.2%). Overall pCR rate was 51.3%. Hormone receptor–positive disease was significantly associated with lower pCR (odds ratio [OR] 0.25, 95% CI 0.11–0.50; p < 0.001), whereas HER2 immunohistochemistry 3+ showed a non-significant trend toward higher pCR (OR 2.95, 95% CI 0.96–11.08; p = 0.075). Median IDFS was not reached, but hormone receptor–positive tumors showed better outcomes (hazard ratio [HR] 3.67, 95% CI 1.02–13.32; p = 0.004 by log-rank). The association between HER2DX pCR score and pCR appeared heterogeneous. The continuous score was significantly associated with pCR in univariable analysis, not in multivariable model. In contrast, medium–high HER2DX pCR category showed significantly higher pCR rates than low category (OR 4.83, 95% CI 1.72–14.65; p = 0.004). The continuous HER2DX risk score was independently associated with IDFS (HR 2.84, 95% CI 1.24–6.48; p = 0.010).ConclusionsIn this cohort, HER2DX scores were associated with pCR and IDFS, supporting previous evidence and highlighting potential role in treatment stratification.

Introduction

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2–positive (HER2-positive) breast cancer accounts for approximately 15% of all BC cases and is associated with substantial morbidity and mortality [1]. Over the past two decades, HER2-targeted therapies have markedly improved outcomes by inhibiting the HER2 oncogenic pathway [2]. Nevertheless, HER2-positive tumors exhibit considerable biological heterogeneity, with differences in HER2 expression levels, hormone receptor (HR) status, intrinsic molecular subtype, somatic mutations (e.g., TP53, PIK3CA), and immune infiltration, influencing treatment response and prognosis [[2], [3], [4]].

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with dual HER2 blockade, using trastuzumab and pertuzumab (HP), is widely used in the management of early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer. However, is not uniformly considered standard of care across all countries or clinical scenarios, particularly in patients with node-negative disease. The NeoSphere trial demonstrated improved outcomes with HP irrespective of HR status or pathological complete response (pCR) [5,6]. In contrast, the combination of trastuzumab + lapatinib (HL) has not consistently demonstrated to improve survival and it is not considered the standard of care [[7], [8], [9], [10]].

The optimal chemotherapy backbone remains a matter of debate [11,12]. Although anthracyclines have historically been incorporated into treatment regimens, their necessity has been questioned because of the risk of cardiotoxicity and secondary malignancies, particularly when combined with anti-HER2 agents [13]. Trials such as TRYPHAENA and TRAIN-2 showed no efficacy differences between anthracycline-containing and anthracycline-free regimens. However, the TRYPHAENA trial reported a slightly higher incidence of cardiotoxicity in patients receiving anthracycline-based treatment [14,15]. In the TRAIN-2 study, one patient developed acute myeloid leukaemia 120 days after the last dose of chemotherapy, possibly treatment-related [16,17]. Moreover, a subset of tumors appears to be highly dependent on HER2 signaling, raising the possibility of achieving cure with reduced chemotherapy intensity or even with anti-HER2 therapy alone. Consistent with this concept, trials evaluating single-agent taxane or chemotherapy-free approaches (e.g., NeoSphere, PHERGain, WSG-ADAPT, and DAPHNe) have reported pCR rates ranging from 17% to 56.7%, supporting de-escalation strategies in selected patients [7,8,[18], [19], [20], [21], [22], [23]].

HER2DX is a genomic assay that integrates clinicopathological variables with four biological gene-expression signatures to generate prognostic and pCR scores. The assay has demonstrated analytical robustness and reproducibility across platforms and has shown potential clinical utility for informing treatment escalation and de-escalation strategies, including in patient populations traditionally underrepresented in clinical trials, such as inflammatory breast cancer and older individuals [[24], [25], [26], [27]].

The objective of this study was to evaluate the associations of clinicopathological features and HER2DX scores with pCR and invasive disease-free survival (IDFS) in patients with early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer treated with neoadjuvant HP at University Hospital A Coruña. Secondary objectives were to assess the associations of the HER2DX ERBB2 score and the individual biological signatures with pCR and IDFS.

Material and methods

Patient characteristics

This retrospective, observational, single-center study included patients with stage I–III HER2-positive breast cancer treated with neoadjuvant trastuzumab and pertuzumab, with or without chemotherapy, who were diagnosed between February 2015 and May 2022 at University Hospital A Coruña (Spain). Patients were identified through a prospectively maintained institutional database (Farmis_Oncofarm®). Clinical and pathological data were extracted from electronic medical records. Neoadjuvant treatment regimens followed institutional protocols and are summarized in Table 1. Patients were evaluated every 3 weeks during neoadjuvant treatment and every 6 months thereafter for at least 5 years. Imaging assessments (mammography, ultrasound, and magnetic resonance imaging) were performed at diagnosis and before surgery according to RECIST version 1.1 criteria [28]. Annual mammography was used for long-term follow-up. Histopathologic and immunohistochemistry (IHC) assessment

Diagnosis was established on core needle biopsy specimens, including evaluation of histological grade, estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PgR), HER2, and Ki-67. Histological grading was performed according to the Nottingham system [30]. ER, PgR, and HER2 were assessed in accordance with ASCO/CAP guidelines, with in situ hybridization performed in cases with HER2 IHC 2+ results [[31], [32], [33], [34]]. Ki-67 was evaluated using the Roche clone 30-9, with a cutoff value of 35%, given the high proliferation rate observed in our cohort and tipically in HER2-positive disease. Molecular subtypes were assigned according to the 2013 St Gallen consensus [35]. Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocytes (TILs) were analyzed as a continuous variable. The pCR was defined as ypT0/Tis ypN0 according to the AJCC 8th edition criteria [36].

HER2DX genomic testing

Standardized HER2DX testing (Reveal Genomics, Barcelona, Spain) was retrospectively performed on RNA extracted from 111 of 156 (71.2%) formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded pretreatment tumor samples (Supplementary Fig. 1), with laboratory personnel blinded to clinical data. The assay comprises four biological gene-expression signatures encompassing 27 genes: an immune signature (14 genes), a proliferation signature (4 genes), a luminal differentiation signature (5 genes), and a HER2 amplicon signature (4 genes) [24]. HER2DX generates three composite scores: a prognostic risk score (derived from the immune [IGG], luminal, and proliferation signatures), a pCR likelihood score (derived from the HER2 amplicon, immune [IGG], luminal, and proliferation signatures), and an ERBB2 score (based on ERBB2 mRNA expression). Pre-established cutoffs were applied, and missing data were not imputed.

Statistical analysis

Associations between clinicopathological variables, HER2DX scores, and pathological complete response were assessed using Fisher's exact test and regression modeling. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression models were used to evaluate associations with pCR, while Cox proportional hazards models were used to evaluate associations with IDFS. Kaplan–Meier methods were used to estimate IDFS, stratified by HER2DX scores, hormone receptor status, pCR, and treatment, with comparisons performed using the log-rank test. A two-sided α level of 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using R (versions 4.3.1 and 4.3.2).

Data access and ethical considerations

All clinical data were pseudonymized at their point of origin in compliance with applicable data-protection regulations. Transcriptomic profiling using the RNADX platform was performed under formal data-sharing frameworks established between the contributing institutions and Reveal Genomics. The research adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and to all applicable national and local regulatory requirements. Ethics approval was obtained from the ethics committee of the participating center. Requirements for informed consent were either waived or fulfilled according to the policies.

Role of the funding source

This work represents a collaborative scientific effort coordinated by University Hospital A Coruña, the Clínic Barcelona Comprehensive Cancer Center, and Reveal Genomics. Study conception, data generation, and analytical decisions were developed jointly. All authors had unrestricted access to the dataset, contributed to data interpretation, and collectively approved submission of the final manuscript.

All patients received neoadjuvant HP-based therapy; 62.2% were treated with anthracycline-containing regimens and 37.8% with anthracycline-free regimens. Neoadjuvant treatment resulted in tumor downstaging from an initial indication for mastectomy (T3–T4 tumors) to eligibility for breast-conserving surgery (T2–T1) in 51.3% of cases. Three patients received adjuvant chemotherapy due to suboptimal response to neoadjuvant treatment. Extended adjuvant neratinib was prescribed in 6 (3.9%) cases. Radiotherapy was delivered to 90.4% of patients, and endocrine therapy was administered to 57.7% of patients. One patient with hormone receptor-positive disease did not receive adjuvant endocrine therapy for unknown reasons.

In multivariable logistic regression, hormone receptor status and HER2 IHC remained independently associated with pCR (Supplementary Table 1). Hormone receptor–positive tumors were significantly associated with lower pCR rates compared with hormone receptor–negative tumors (odds ratio [OR] 0.246; p < 0.010). HER2 IHC 3+ showed a non-significant trend toward higher pCR rates than IHC 2+ (OR 2.95; 95% CI 0.96–11.08; p = 0.075). Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis is shown in Supplementary Fig. 2.

In addition, we performed a separate analysis excluding the five patients who received a de-escalated chemotherapy regimen or exclusively antiHER2 therapy in the neoadjuvant setting. As no differences in outcomes were observed, between clinicopathological variables and pCR, these patients were maintained in the final analysis with HER2DX in order to reflect the real-world characteristics of the study population treated at our center (Supplementary analysis 1).

Association between clinicopathological variables and IDFS

Median IDFS was not reached during follow-up, with 80.8% of patients remaining relapse-free at 100 months. Hormone receptor–positive status was significantly associated with improved IDFS (hazard ratio [HR] 3.67, 95% CI 1.02–13.32; p = 0.004) (Supplementary Fig. 3B), whereas pCR was not associated with IDFS (HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.19–2.22; p = 0.335). Notably, 61.9% (13/21) of IDFS events occurred among patients who achieved a pCR (5 locoregional and 8 distant recurrences), whereas the remaining 8 events occurred in patients without a pCR (1 locoregional and 7 distant recurrences). At 3 years, IDFS was 90.5% in patients who achieved a pCR and 91.6% in those who did not. No significant differences in IDFS were observed between platinum-based and anthracycline-based regimens (HR 1.34, 95% CI 0.33–5.49; p = 0.300).

Also, we performed a separate analysis excluding the five patients who received a de-escalated chemotherapy regimen or exclusively antiHER2 therapy in the neoadjuvant setting. Likewise, no significant associations were observed between clinicopathological variables and IDFS, therefore these patients were included in the final HER2DX analysis (Supplementary analysis 2).

Association between HER2DX pCR score and pCR

HER2DX testing was successfully performed on 111 pretreatment tumor samples (71.2%) (Table 4). In this subgroup, the overall pCR rate was 52.3%. In univariable analysis, the continuous HER2DX pCR score was significantly associated with pCR (odds ratio [OR] 2.86, 95% CI 1.69–5.20; p < 0.001). Discussion

This real-world, retrospective study underscores the relevance of both clinicopathological variables and the HER2DX genomic assay in informing pCR and IDFS in early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer. These insights are particularly important for optimizing neoadjuvant treatment strategies in routine clinical practice, where treatment heterogeneity is greater and definitive guidance for individualization remains limited in current clinical guidelines.

Consistent with prior studies, hormone receptor–negative tumors and those with HER2 IHC 3+ were more likely to achieve a pCR [[37], [38], [39], [40]]. However, HER2 IHC 3+ did not retain independent significance in multivariable analysis, suggesting that proxtein overexpression alone may not fully capture HER2-driven tumor biology. In contrast, the HER2DX ERBB2 score, which reflects ERBB2 gene expression, was independently associated with pCR in our cohort. While other clinicopathological factors have been reported to correlate with pCR in previous studies, these associations were not observed in our dataset, possibly reflecting the real-world nature of the cohort and its underlying clinical heterogeneity [39,40]. Overall, these findings support the relevance of established biomarkers while also suggesting that integrated genomic tools may provide complementary information for treatment stratification in HER2-positive early breast cancer [37].

HER2DX was developed to address this need, integrating clinical stage and nodal status with four genomic signatures to support more individualized treatment strategies [24]. In our cohort, the association between the HER2DX pCR score and pCR appeared more heterogeneous compared with previous validation studies [24]. The continuous HER2DX pCR score showed a significant association with pCR in the univariable analysis, however, this association was not maintained in the multivariable model. In contrast, when the categorical HER2DX pCR score was evaluated, a clearer relation with pCR was observed, which remained significant in the multivariable analysis, with the medium-high pCR score group showing the highest likelihood of response. Previous studies have also reported that the categorical HER2DX pCR score is more closely associated with pCR than conventional biomarkers [[41], [42], [43], [44], [45], [46]].

With a median follow-up of nearly four years, 80.8% of patients remained relapse-free at 100 months. Notably, 8 distant recurrences occurred among patients who achieved a pCR, whereas 7 were observed in the subgroup with residual disease. Although pCR has been proposed as a surrogate endpoint for improved IDFS, particularly in hormone receptor–negative disease, this association was not observed in our cohort [37,40,47]. Among patients who developed distant relapse despite pCR, most presented with high-risk baseline features, including stage III disease (5/8 cases), hormone receptor-negative tumors (5/8 cases), and a high proliferation index (5/8 cases). Consistent with previous studies, patients with hormone receptor–positive disease demonstrated improved IDFS and a tendency toward later relapse [48,49]. No significant differences in IDFS were observed according to the chemotherapy backbone used in the neoadjuvant setting. Of note, only one patient in the pCR subgroup who subsequently developed distant relapse did not complete neoadjuvant therapy due to hepatic toxicity. Regarding adjuvant treatment, only one patient with distant relapse in the pCR subgroup did not complete therapy owing to a concurrent valvular heart disease. These findings may reflect the relatively limited follow-up, modest sample size, or baseline characteristics of the study population.

The HER2DX risk score showed a prognostic association in our cohort. When analyzed as a continuous variable, it was significantly associated with IDFS independently of treatment type and traditional clinical variables, including pCR, in line with previous studies [24,[43], [44], [45], [46]]. When analyzed categorically, the high-risk group showed a trend toward worse outcomes, although this did not reach statistical significance, likely reflecting the limited number of events. These findings are consistent with previous work suggesting that HER2DX can identify low-risk patients who may be candidates for less intensive treatment approaches, potentially reducing toxicity and overtreatment [24,44,50,51].

Previous studies have shown that tumors with higher HER2 amplification by FISH and those classified as HER2-enriched exhibit stronger activation of the HER2 pathway and higher sensitivity to dual anti-HER2 therapy [38,[52], [53], [54], [55]]. Building on this concept, the HER2DX ERBB2 score was developed to quantify ERBB2 mRNA expression and refine biological HER2 dependency beyond standard clinical classification [24,50,51,56]. In our study, the continuous HER2DX ERBB2 score was independently associated with pCR, whereas HER2 status by IHC was not, after adjustment for clinicopathological variables. The categorical ERBB2 score did not retain independent significance, likely due to the small size of the low–medium subgroup.

Analysis of the individual HER2DX gene-expression signatures provided further biological insight [24]. Consistent with prior evidence, the IGG and HER2 amplicon signatures were associated with higher pCR rates, whereas the luminal signature was associated with lower pCR but better IDFS, reflecting divergent biological phenotypes within HER2-positive disease [24,40,47,57]. Our results were in line with previous studies, reporting an association between the IGG and HER2 amplicon signatures with increased pCR, while the luminal signature correlated with lower pCR but better IDFS. These associations reflect the divergent biology of HER2+ tumors, ranging from highly proliferative, immune-enriched subtypes to luminal-like, endocrine-responsive phenotypes [24,40,58]. The proliferation signature was not associated with outcomes in our cohort, in contrast to some previous reports, which may be explained by sample size and cohort differences [59].

Together, these findings suggest that HER2DX may provide complementary biological and clinical information that could help refine treatment stratification in HER2-positive early breast cancer. Ongoing prospective trials are evaluating the clinical utility of HER2DX in treatment algorithms. The EU-funded DEFINITIVE trial (NCT06446882; https://www.thedefinitivetrial.eu/) is assessing HER2DX-guided neoadjuvant therapy in stage II–III HER2-positive disease, and the CompassHER2-pCR trial (NCT04266249) is evaluating the prognostic and correlative value of HER2DX scores in the United States [60]. As the therapeutic landscape continues to evolve, integrating genomic tools may become increasingly important for balancing efficacy and toxicity. Future studies should assess long-term outcomes, quality of life, and cost-effectiveness.

Our study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design and the lack of randomization across neoadjuvant regimens may introduce selection bias. Second, only a small number of patients received dual anti-HER2 therapy alone or in combination with taxanes, limiting the power of subgroup analyses in these groups. Third, longer follow-up is needed to fully assess long-term survival outcomes. Fourth, there are no reported RFS events specifically within the HER2 IHC 2+ subgroup, which limits the robustness of any associated estimates. Finally, not all patients with residual disease received adjuvant T-DM1, which is now the standard of care, as this indication received EMA approval in December 2019 and the study included patients treated before that time [61].

In conclusion, this real-world study shows that HER2DX scores are associated with treatment response and IDFS in early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer treated with neoadjuvant HP-based therapy. The ERBB2 mRNA score provided additional information beyond HER2 IHC. These findings support further evaluation of HER2DX as a genomic tool that may contribute to treatment stratification in this setting. Notably, HER2DX has already been incorporated into the 2025 Spanish clinical practice guidelines, where it is suggested as a genomic tool that could help support treatment selection and aid decision-making regarding chemotherapy agents to be combined with dual HER2 blockade in the neoadjuvant setting [62]. In addition, a recent prospective, multicenter Spanish decision-impact study showed that availability of HER2DX results led to changes in treatment recommendations in nearly half of patients, was associated with improved physician confidence, maintained pCR rates in pCR-high disease treated with less intensive regimens, and reduced overall health care costs [51].
