On the series expansion of k-free Dirichlet series and its analytical continuation
Artur Kawalec

Abstract
In this article, we develop a k-free zeta Dirichlet series into a Laurent series with a simple pole, and prove a Stieltjes like formula for the expansion coefficients of the regular part. We also investigate another analytical continuation of these series and develop a formula for ζ ⁡ ( 1 k ) \zeta(\tfrac{1}{k}) for positive integer k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 in terms of the k-free indicator function.



1
Introduction

In the previous article [2], we developed several results concerning the square-free Dirichlet series, such as its Laurent series expansion and analytical continuation, and also a formula for
\zeta(\frac{1}{2})
represented by a certain Dirichlet series with
|\mu(n)|
coefficients. In this article, we generalize the same results but for the k-free case for positive integer
k\geq 2
. We have the Dirichlet series

\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(ks)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{s}}

(1)

which is absolutely convergent for
\Re(s)>1
. Let
\mu(n)
be the standard Möbius function. We recall that an integer
n
is k-free (for
k\geq 2
) if it is not divisible by any
k^{th}
power equal to
k
[3][5][6]. We write an indicator function

\mu^{(k)}(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if n is k-free}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(2)

to distinguish between the k-free numbers. The k-free indicator function can be defined in terms of the Möbius function by the relation

\mu^{(k)}(n)=\sum_{d^{k}|n}\mu(d)

(3)

For the square-free integer case
k=2
one has
\mu^{(2)}(n)=\mu^{2}(n)=|\mu(n)|
. And for
k=3
case
\mu^{(3)}(n)
is referred to as cube-free and so forth.

We define the Laurent series expansion

\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(ks)}=\frac{1}{\zeta(k)(s-1)}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}^{M,k}\frac{(s-1)^{n}}{n!}

(4)

about the pole at
s=1
and residue
\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}
. This series is analytic on a disk centered at
s=1
and radius of convergence
R=1+\frac{2}{k}
due being limited by the next closest to the pole at the first trivial zero when
ks=-2
for
s=-\frac{2}{k}
from the center at
s=1
.

The main result of this article is we derive a general log-limit formula for these expansion coefficients as

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 0
, the coefficients are:

\gamma^{M,k}_{n}=\lim_{x\to\infty}(-1)^{n}\Bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(j)\log^{n}(j)}{j}-\frac{\log^{n+1}(x)}{\zeta(k)(n+1)}\Bigg\}

(5)

The limit formula (5) was discovered by Wolf in [7] for
k=2
case, and as we showed in [2]. In this article, we extend the same Wolf formula for the general k-free
k\geq 2
case.

We now consider the case for
n=0
, where the growth of this Dirichlet series

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n}=O(\log x)

(6)

gives a crude log bound; the reason because it’s always less than the harmonic series

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n}<\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1}{n}=O(\log x).

(7)

The harmonic series also has a sharper growth estimate

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1}{n}=\gamma+\log x+O(\frac{1}{x})

(8)

where is
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, and the extraction of this constant is by the limit

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1}{n}-\log x\right)

(9)

\displaystyle=0.57721566490153286060\ldots.

In [7] Wolf considered a similar square-free analogue case

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{|\mu(n)|}{n}=\gamma^{M}+\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\log x+O(\frac{1}{x})

(10)

where the new constant

\displaystyle\gamma^{M}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{|\mu(n)|}{n}-\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\log x\right)

(11)

\displaystyle=1.04389451571193829740\ldots

is an analogue of the Euler’s constant, where it is associated with
k=2
square-free case. We now extend these results to the general k-free case and write an estimate for the k-free harmonic sum

\sum_{n\leq x}^{x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n}=\gamma^{M,k}+\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}\log x+O(\frac{1}{x})

(12)

where the generalized k-free analogue of Euler constant
\gamma^{M,k}
is extracted the same way

\gamma^{M,k}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n}-\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}\log x\right)\\

(13)

For
n=0
case only, we assume the notation
\gamma^{M,k}=\gamma^{M,k}_{0}
.

In Table 1, we compute the first
10
constants for
k=2
to
11
and observe that its value is approaching the Euler’s constant as
k\to\infty
, hence one has

\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma^{M,k}=\gamma

(14)

the reason being is that the k-free Dirichlet series approaches the harmonic series

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n}\to\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1}{n}

(15)

as
k\to\infty
, where only the higher order
k
divisors are missing, and one also has
\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}\to 1
.

Proof of Theorem 1

To show that, let us recap some basic definitions. The k-free counting function is defined by

Q_{k}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}\mu^{(k)}(n)

(22)

for positive integer argument
x>1
, and by the average value

Q_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sum_{n<x}\mu^{(k)}(n)+\sum_{n\leq x}\mu^{(k)}(n)\right]

(23)

for positive real argument
x>0
, so whenever
x
is integer an average value of left and right side of the step is taken. One has the asymptotic

Q_{k}(x)\sim\frac{x}{\zeta(k)}

(24)

and the limit

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{Q_{k}(x)}{x}=\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}

(25)

which implies that the probability of choosing a k-free integer at random from a set of integers is
\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}
. The k-free counting function can be expressed as

Q_{k}(x)=\frac{x}{\zeta(k)}+f_{k}(x)

(26)

where the bound

f_{k}(x)=O(x^{\frac{1}{k}})

(27)

which it suffices to prove by the prime number theorem. But if assuming (RH), there is a much better bound

f_{k}(x)=O(x^{\frac{1}{2k}})

(28)

as shown in [3]. Also, the
m^{th}
derivatives of (1) are given by

(-1)^{m}\left[\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(ks)}\right]^{(m)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)\log^{m}(n)}{n^{s}}

(29)

for
\Re(s)>1
.

So we proceed proving Theorem
1
by carrying out appropriate Stieljtes integration. For
m\geq 0
case, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)\log^{m}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{1^{-}}^{x}\frac{\log^{m}(t)}{t}dQ_{k}(t)=\int_{1^{-}}^{x}\frac{\log^{m}(t)}{t}d\left(\frac{t}{\zeta(k)}\right)+\int_{1^{-}}^{x}\frac{\log^{m}(t)}{t}df_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}\int_{1^{-}}^{x}\frac{\log^{m}(t)}{t}\,dt+\left[\frac{\log^{m}(t)}{t}f_{k}(t)\right]_{1^{-}}^{x}-\int_{1^{-}}^{x}\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{\log^{m}(t)}{t}\right]f_{k}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=A(x)+\frac{\log^{m}(x)}{x}f_{k}(x)+B_{m}+\int_{1^{-}}^{x}\frac{\log^{m}(t)-m\log^{m-1}(t)}{t^{2}}f_{k}(t)\,dt

(30)

Here we note there is a subtle detail concerning the lower integration variable where we must consider the value

Q_{k}(1^{-})=0

(31)

before the step, since
Q_{k}(1^{+})=1
after the step. As a result, the value

f_{k}(1^{-})=-\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}

(32)

is the right solution. We define a constant
B_{m}
by

B_{m}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}&\text{if }m=0\\
0&\text{if }m>0\end{cases}

(33)

which only arises for
m=0
case. The reason is that it is assumed the convention that
0^{0}=1
, which implies
[\log(1)]^{0}=1
, thus yielding a nonzero term, otherwise for
m>1
, we have
[\log(1)]^{m}=0^{m}=0
.

And this integral identity can be generated as

\displaystyle A(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}\int_{1}^{x}\frac{\log^{m}(t)}{t}\,dt

(34)

\displaystyle=\frac{\log^{m+1}(x)}{\zeta(k)(m+1)}

for
m\geq 1
. Then, it is readily seen that the limit

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)\log^{m}(n)}{n}-\frac{\log^{m+1}(x)}{\zeta(k)(m+1)}=(-1)^{m}\gamma_{k,m}^{M}+O\left(x^{\tfrac{1}{2k}-1}\log^{m}(x)\right)

(35)

exists as
x\to\infty
and converges to a constant

(-1)^{m}\gamma_{k,m}^{M}=B_{m}+\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{m}(t)-m\log^{m-1}(t)}{t^{2}}f_{k}(t)\,dt

(36)

for
m\geq 0
by the estimate (27) or (28) for
k\geq 2
. Now the next step is to connect this constant to the series expansion in (4) as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(ks)}

\displaystyle=\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}\,dQ_{k}(t)=\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}d\left(\frac{t}{\zeta(k)}\right)\,+\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}df_{k}(t)

(37)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}dt\,+\Biggl[\frac{f_{k}(t)}{t^{s}}\Biggr]_{1^{-}}^{\infty}+s\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(k)(s-1)}+\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}+s\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)\,dt

The last integral in (37) converges for
\Re(s)>\tfrac{1}{2k}
, therefore it defines an analytical continuation of (lhs) of (37) to that region.
It now remains to show that this integral admits the series expansion about
s=1
, so we consider

\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)\,dt

(38)

which converges for
\Re(s)>\tfrac{1}{2k}
assuming (RH). One then generates the exp-log expansion as

\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}t^{-2}e^{-(s-1)\log t}f_{k}(t)\,dt=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}\frac{(s-1)^{j}}{j!}

(39)

where its expansion coefficients can be read off as

c_{j}=(-1)^{j}\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{j}(t)}{t^{2}}f_{k}(t)\,dt

(40)

but such a series is only limited to
|s-1|<1
domain. However, on writing integral term in (37) in
s-1
domain, then one needs to consider this form instead

\displaystyle s\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=(s-1)\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)\,dt+\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)\,dt

(41)

\displaystyle=\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}[(s-1)t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)+t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)]\,dt

where we can generate a new series expansion as

\displaystyle s\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)c_{j}\frac{(s-1)^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}\frac{(s-1)^{j}}{j!}

(42)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}mc_{m-1}\frac{(s-1)^{m}}{m!}+c_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{m}\frac{(s-1)^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=c_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left[mc_{m-1}+c_{m}\right]\frac{(s-1)^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}y_{j}\frac{(s-1)^{j}}{j!}

by combining the expansions in (39). Here we’ve shifted index variable
m=j+1
in first series in (42) and re-labeled
m=j
in second series in (42). The new expansion coefficients are then

y_{m}=\begin{cases}c_{0}&\text{for }m=0\\
mc_{m-1}+c_{m}&\text{for }m\geq 1\end{cases}

(43)

where they are expressed by
c_{n}
coefficients, and by (40) these coefficients can be expressed by the integral

y_{m}=(-1)^{m}\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{m}(t)-m\log^{m-1}(t)}{t^{2}}f_{k}(t)\,dt

(44)

which matches the series in (36). And we note that for
m=0
case we simplify (44) to get

y_{0}=\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}\frac{f_{k}(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt

(45)

whereby adding the constant
B_{0}
from (33) (and as it appears in (37)), we get the formula

\gamma^{M,k}=\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}+\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}\frac{f_{k}(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt

(46)

for
k\geq 2
case. And for the
m\geq 1
case we have
\gamma^{M}_{m,k}=y_{m}
. Hence this completes the proof.

3
On analytical continuation of the Dirichlet series

We saw earlier that the k-free Dirichlet series

\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(ks)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{s}}

(47)

is convergent for
\Re(s)>1
. We show this by similar Stieltjes integration, but of the remainder term as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(ks)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{s}}+\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}\,dQ_{k}(t)

(48)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{s}}+\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}d\left(\frac{t}{\zeta(k)}\right)\,+\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}df_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{s}}+\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}dt\,+\Biggl[\frac{f_{k}(t)}{t^{s}}\Biggr]_{x}^{\infty}+s\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{s}}-\frac{x^{1-s}}{\zeta(k)(1-s)}-\frac{f_{k}(x)}{x^{s}}+s\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f_{k}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{s}}-\frac{sx^{1-s}}{\zeta(k)(1-s)}-\frac{Q_{k}(x)}{x^{s}}+O\Bigg(\dfrac{s}{(s-\tfrac{1}{2k})x^{s-\tfrac{1}{2k}}}\Bigg)\,

When extracting in the limit we get

\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(ks)}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{s}}-\frac{x^{1-s}}{\zeta(k)(1-s)}\Bigg\}

(49)

valid for
\Re(s)>\frac{1}{2k}
for
k\geq 2
(except at
s=1
) assuming (RH), where we can drop the higher order terms. But if we use the higher order terms in (48) then the precision of will improve.

Now, when
k\to\infty
, then this formula approaches the well-known formula

\zeta(s)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1}{n^{s}}-\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}\Bigg\}

(50)

for the zeta function obtained from Euler-Maclaurin formula, which is convergent for
\Re(s)>0
(except at the pole
s=1
). So basically the k-free Dirichlet series is sort of like an intermediate step for (50).

4
A special case around the first zero

In the previous section, we have the analytical continuation formula (49) of the k-free Dirichlet series

\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(ks)}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{s}}-\frac{x^{1-s}}{\zeta(k)(1-s)}\Bigg\}

(51)

converges for
\Re(s)>\frac{1}{2k}
(except at
s=1
). This implies that all non-trivial zeros of the zeta function on the critical line are also zeros of (51), and even the zeros close the critical line if such exists. Now, there is one more zero that occurs for this representation at
s=\frac{1}{k}
due to the pole of
\zeta
in the denominator, and so, for
k\geq 2
we obtain a special case asymptotic relation at this zero as

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{\frac{1}{k}}}\sim\frac{k}{(k-1)\zeta(k)}x^{(1-\frac{1}{k})}

(52)

as
x\to\infty
, or expressing in another way, we can extract the reciprocal zeta (for integer
k\geq 2
) as

\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)x^{(1-\frac{1}{k})}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)}{n^{\frac{1}{k}}}.

(53)

We also consider another Taylor series expansion about
s=\frac{1}{k}
of

\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(ks)}=k\zeta(\frac{1}{k})(s-\frac{1}{k})+O(|s-\frac{1}{k}|^{2})

(54)

and seek to extract the first order zeta value by differentiation of the (rhs) of (51). We obtain the formula

\zeta(\frac{1}{k})=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)\log(n)}{n^{\frac{1}{k}}}-\frac{1}{\zeta(k)(k-1)}x^{1-\frac{1}{k}}\left(\frac{k}{k-1}-\log(x)\right)\Bigg\}

(55)

And if we substitute (52) to (55) then we generate another type of formula

\zeta(\frac{1}{k})=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(k)}(n)(\log(n)+\frac{k}{k-1})}{n^{\frac{1}{k}}}+\frac{1}{\zeta(k)(k-1)}x^{1-\frac{1}{k}}\log(x)\Bigg\}

(56)

We will next compute these formulas and verify numerically. First, for the case
k=2
, this formula simplifies to

\zeta(\frac{1}{2})=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(2)}(n)(\log(n)+2)}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\sqrt{x}\log(x)\Bigg\}

(57)

and in Fig. 1, we plot it as a function of limit variable
x
from
x=1
to
10^{9}
in increments of
1
on a logarithmic scale on the x-axis to capture all points, thus, every single point is captured in the plot. As a result, we find that this series is a rather noisy and fluctuating series, and we note how it is oscillating about the value
\zeta(\frac{1}{2})=-1.46035450\ldots
, with a gradual decrease in amplitude as
x
increase (as we shown in [2]).
And similarly, for
k=3
case we generate the formula

\zeta(\frac{1}{3})=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(3)}(n)(\log(n)+\frac{3}{2})}{n^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\frac{1}{2\zeta(3)}x^{\frac{2}{3}}\log(x)\Bigg\}

(58)

and in Fig. 2, we plot it as a function of variable x, and show that is oscillating about the value
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{3})=-0.97336024\ldots
And for
k=4
case we generate

\zeta(\frac{1}{4})=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(4)}(n)(\log(n)+\frac{4}{3})}{n^{\frac{1}{k}}}+\frac{1}{3\zeta(4)}x^{\frac{3}{4}}\log(x)\Bigg\}

(59)

where we plot it in Fig. 3, and
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{4})=-0.81327840\ldots
And for
k=5
we generate

\zeta(\frac{1}{5})=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{5}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu^{(5)}(n)(\log(n)+\frac{5}{4})}{n^{\frac{1}{5}}}+\frac{1}{4\zeta(5)}x^{\frac{4}{5}}\log(x)\Bigg\}

(60)

where we plot it in Fig. 4, and
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{5})=-0.73392092\ldots
