Almost Sure Convergence Rates of Stochastic Approximation and Reinforcement Learning via a Poisson-Moreau Drift
Xinyu Liu, Zixuan Xie, Shangtong Zhang

Abstract
Establishing almost sure convergence rates for stochastic approximation and reinforcement learning under Markovian noise is a fundamental theoretical challenge. We make progress towards this challenge for a class of stochastic approximation algorithms whose expected updates are contractive, a setting that arises in many reinforcement learning algorithms such as Q Q -learning and linear temporal difference learning. Specifically, for a power-law learning rate 𝒪 ⁡ ( n − η ) \mathcal{O}(n^{-\eta}) with η ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ) \eta\in(1/2,1) , we obtain an almost sure convergence rate arbitrarily close to o ⁡ ( n 1 − 2 ​ η ) o(n^{1-2\eta}) . For a harmonic learning rate 𝒪 ⁡ ( n − 1 ) \mathcal{O}(n^{-1}) , we obtain an almost sure convergence rate arbitrarily close to o ⁡ ( n − 1 ) o(n^{-1}) , which we argue is a strong result because it is close to the optimal rate 𝒪 ⁡ ( n − 1 ​ log ⁡ log ​ n ) \mathcal{O}(n^{-1}\log\log n) given by the law of the iterated logarithm (for a special case of i.i.d. noise). Key to our analysis is a novel Lyapunov drift construction that applies a Poisson-equation based correction for Markovian noise to the well-established Moreau-envelope smoothing for the contractive mapping.

Introduction

Introduced by

\displaystyle\textstyle\theta_{n+1}=\theta_{n}+\alpha_{n}F(\theta_{n},Y_{n}),

\theta_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

[

Historically, almost sure convergence is established via the ODE method, see, e.g.,

39

8

7

While powerful, these techniques typically fall short of quantifying exactly how fast a realized trajectory approaches its limit, i.e., they establish only asymptotic almost sure convergence without a rate. An alternative path leverages the almost-supermartingale framework pioneered by
67

[

,
32
,
63

50

. Beyond supermartingale arguments, researchers have derived almost sure rates by imposing stricter structural assumptions. In the realm of linear SA, for example,

being fully independent.
32

Within the specific domain of RL, notable early milestones include
79

Q
-learning and
80
for linear TD. Yet,

Main Results

We consider the stochastic approximation recursion initialized from a deterministic

\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\textstyle\theta_{n+1}=\theta_{n}+\alpha_{n}F(\theta_{n},Y_{n}).

(SA)

Let
(\mathsf{Y},\mathcal{Y})
be a measurable state space and let
P
be a Markov kernel on
\mathsf{Y}
.
The process
\quantity{Y_{n}}_{n\geq 0}
is a Markov chain whose transitions are governed by
P
.
Let
\quantity{\mathcal{F}_n}_{n\geq 0}
be the filtration defined as the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the history of the Markov chain, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(Y_{0},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})
. By construction,
\theta_{n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}
-measurable and
Y_{n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-measurable. The process satisfies

\displaystyle\Pr(Y_{n+1}\in A |\fF_n)=P(Y_{n},A)\quad\text{a.s. for every }A\in\mathcal{Y}.

(2)

Throughout the paper,
\mathbb{E}_{n}[\cdot]\doteq\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{n}]
.

Assumption 2.1
.

The Markov kernel
P
admits a unique invariant probability measure
\pi
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, define the expected update
f(\theta)\doteq\int_{\mathsf{Y}}F(\theta,y)\pi(dy)
.
We consider the setting where the expected update is the residual of some contractive mapping, i.e., we assume
f(\theta)=T(\theta)-\theta
for some
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is contractive under a norm
\norm{\cdot}
.
Or formally,

Assumption 2.2
.

Let
\textstyle T(\theta)\doteq\theta+f(\theta).

Then there exists
\kappa\in(0,1)
and a norm
\norm{\cdot}
such that

\displaystyle\norm{T(\theta)-T(\theta')}\leq\kappa\norm{\theta-\theta'},\quad\forall\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3)

This assumption allows the contraction norm
\norm{\cdot}
to be non-Euclidean, for example
\ell_{\infty}
.
We use
\theta_{*}
to denote the unique fixed point of
T
, i.e.,
f(\theta_{*})=0
.
Such contraction driven stochastic approximations arise in many RL algorithms, such as
Q
-learning and TD.
Notably, it also arises in linear stochastic approximation with Hurwitz matrix, i.e.,
when
f(\theta)=A\theta+b
with
A
being a Hurwitz matrix.
See Lemma 6.1 of
15
for more details on how to verify the contraction condition for such linear stochastic approximation.
This inclusion of linear stochastic approximation with Hurwitz matrix is important because it covers many RL algorithms such as linear TD and gradient TD
[
77
,
76
]
.
We further assume that
F
is Lipschitz continuous.

Assumption 2.3
.

There exists some constant
L_{F}
such that, for
\forall\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\forall y\in\mathsf{Y}
,

\displaystyle\norm{F(\theta,y)-F(\theta',y)}\leq L_{F}\norm{\theta-\theta'},\quad\norm{F(\theta_*,y)}\leq L_{F}.

(4)

We then make a mild assumption on the Markovian noise, assuming that it admits a Poisson equation representation.

Assumption 2.4
.

There is a measurable function
H:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathsf{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for
\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\forall y\in\mathsf{Y}
,

\displaystyle H_{\theta}(y)-(PH_{\theta})(y)=F(\theta,y)-f(\theta).

(5)

Here
H_{\theta}(y)
is a shorthand for
H(\theta,y)
and

\displaystyle\textstyle(PH_{\theta})(y)\doteq\int_{\mathsf{Y}}H_{\theta}(y^{\prime})P(y,dy^{\prime}),\quad y\in\mathsf{Y}.

(6)

We further assume there exists some constant
L_{H}
such that, for
\forall\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\forall y\in\mathsf{Y}
,

\displaystyle\norm{H_\theta(y)-H_{\theta'}(y)}\leq L_{H}\norm{\theta-\theta'},\quad\norm{H_{\theta_*}(y)}\leq L_{H}.

(7)

.

3
Poisson-Moreau Drift and Proof of Theorem
1

In this section, we illustrate the key technical novelty of this paper.
Write
e_{n}\doteq\theta_{n}-\theta_{*}
.
A common approach to analyze the convergence of
e_{n}
is to construct a drift function
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and establish the recursion between
V(e_{n+1})
and
V(e_{n})
.
If the norm in Assumption
2.2
is Euclidean, then the natural choice is
V(x)=\frac{1}{2}\norm{x}^{2}
, see, e.g.,
4
,
73
.
To handle a possibly non-Euclidean norm,
Moreau-envelope smoothing is a standard technique.
To our knowledge, the first to use Moreau envelopes in the context of stochastic approximation is
13
.
And it has since been widely used in the literature on arbitrary norm contractive stochastic approximation,
see, e.g.,
14
,
99
,
12
,
63
,
51
,
15
.
Below we provide a brief background on this technique.
We do note that these are not our contributions and we provide them here only for completeness.
That being said,
we will shortly articulate why existing developments on Moreau envelopes are not sufficient for our purposes and how we need to further develop the technique by adding a Poisson-equation based correction to the Moreau envelope.

3.1
Background on Moreau Envelopes

Denote the Euclidean norm as
\norm{\cdot}_{2}
.
Thanks to norm equivalence in finite dimensions, we can choose constants
\ell,u>0
such that

\displaystyle\textstyle\ell\norm{x}_{2}\leq\norm{x}\leq u\norm{x}_{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(9)

For
\xi>0
, define the Moreau envelope of
x\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\norm{x}^{2}
by

\displaystyle\textstyle M_{\xi}(x)\doteq\inf_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\quantity{
\frac 12\norm{u}^2+\frac{1}{2\xi}\norm{x-u}_2^2}.

(10)

The following are standard facts that we will use,
with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denoting the Euclidean inner product.

Lemma 1

(Proposition A.1 and Section A.2 of
14
)
.

For every
\xi>0
, the function
M_{\xi}
is continuously differentiable and
1/\xi
-smooth with respect to
\norm{\cdot}_{2}
:

\displaystyle\textstyle\norm{\nabla M_\xi(x)-\nabla M_\xi(y)}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\xi}\norm{x-y}_{2},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(11)

Moreover, there exists a norm
\norm{\cdot}_{m,\xi}
such that
M_{\xi}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\norm{x}_{m,\xi}^{2}
.
With
\ell_{\xi}\doteq\sqrt{1+\xi\ell^{2}},u_{\xi}\doteq\sqrt{1+\xi u^{2}}
,
we have

\displaystyle\ell_{\xi}\norm{x}_{m,\xi}\leq\norm{x}\leq u_{\xi}\norm{x}_{m,\xi},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(12)

Finally, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\inner{\nabla M_\xi(x)}{y}}\leq\norm{x}_{m,\xi}\norm{y}_{m,\xi},\quad\langle\nabla M_{\xi}(x),x\rangle\geq\norm{x}_{m,\xi}^{2}.

(13)

We choose
\xi>0
small enough that the contraction survives the smoothing step:

\displaystyle\textstyle\kappa\frac{u_{\xi}}{\ell_{\xi}}<1,

(14)

and define the resulting smooth drift rate by

\displaystyle\textstyle\mu_{\xi}\doteq 2\left(1-\kappa\frac{u_{\xi}}{\ell_{\xi}}\right)>0.

(15)

Such a choice is possible because
u_{\xi}/\ell_{\xi}\to 1
as
\xi\downarrow 0
.

Lemma 2
.

Under Assumption
2.2
and the choice (
14
), for every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla M_{\xi}(\theta-\theta_{*}),f(\theta)\rangle\leq-\mu_{\xi}M_{\xi}(\theta-\theta_{*}).

(16)

The proof is in Appendix
B.1
.
This lemma is how the Moreau envelope is typically used: it converts the contraction of
T
in
\norm{\cdot}
into a differentiable one-step drift for the smooth energy
M_{\xi}
.

3.2
Limitations of Prior Moreau-Based Analyses

In prior works, the drift function is typically taken to be
V(x)=M_{\xi}(x)
directly
[
13
,
15
]
.
This is sufficient when
\quantity{Y_n}
are i.i.d.
But when
\quantity{Y_n}
is a Markov chain,
additional terms appear and require additional control.
There are two typical approaches to control these terms resulting from Markovian noise.
The first is based on
73
,
which constructs an auxiliary stationary Markov chain to compare with,
using the mixing property of the Markov chain to show that the bias terms are small on average,
see, e.g.,
14
,
99
,
12
.
Essentially, this approach will establish the recursion between
\mathbb{E}\quantity[M_\xi(e_{n+1})]
and
\mathbb{E}\quantity[M_\xi(e_n)]
.
This is sufficient for obtaining convergence rates in expectation.
But it does not give pathwise control of the bias terms and thus is not sufficient for obtaining almost sure rates.
The second approach is the skeleton iterates approach from
63
.
Instead of working with the original timescale
\quantity{1, 2, \dots}
,

63
carefully choose a subsequence of time points
\quantity{n_k}
and investigate
\quantity{\theta_{n_k}}
instead of
\quantity{\theta_n}
.
This allows to construct the recursion between
\mathbb{E}_{n_{k}}\quantity[M_\xi(e_{n_{k+1}})]
and
M_{\xi}(e_{n_{k}})
.
With this recursion,

63
show that
\quantity{M_\xi(e_{n_k})}
is almost a supermartingale and apply the almost supermartingale convergence results from
47
to obtain almost sure convergence rates for
\quantity{M_\xi(e_{n_k})}
,
which is then propagated back to
\quantity{M_\xi(e_n)}
.
The foundation of this approach is recently formally verified by
102
.
However, as summarized in Table
2
,
the price of the skeleton iterates approach is that (1) it cannot cover the case
\eta\in(1/2,2/3]
and (2) for
\eta\in(2/3,1]
, the rate is much slower than the optimal.
In this paper, we provide a third approach that applies a Poisson-equation based correction to the Moreau drift directly.

3.3
Poisson Correction

The key technical novelty of this work is the following drift

\displaystyle V_{n}^{\xi}\doteq M_{\xi}(e_{n})+\alpha_{n}\langle\nabla M_{\xi}(e_{n}),H_{\theta_{n}}(Y_{n})\rangle+K\alpha_{n}^{2}.

(17)

Here
K
is a positive constant to be chosen large enough.
The middle term is the Poisson correction.
It has the same order as one update step.
So it does not change the asymptotic size of the drift but cancels a leading Markovian term in the conditional drift.
The last term is a technical correction to control the residual terms that appear after the Poisson correction.
We then show that
V_{n}^{\xi}
is almost a nonnegative supermartingale.

Lemma 3
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
,
2.3
, and
2.4
, fix
\xi>0
,
there exists some constant
C_{\xi}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}\quantity[M_\xi(e_{n+1})]\leq

\displaystyle M_{\xi}(e_{n})+\alpha_{n}\langle\nabla M_{\xi}(e_{n}),f(\theta_{n})\rangle

(18)

\displaystyle+\alpha_{n}\mathbb{E}_{n}\quantity[
\inner{\nabla M_\xi(e_n)}{H_{\theta_n}(Y_n)}
- \inner{\nabla M_\xi(e_{n+1})}{H_{\theta_{n+1}}(Y_{n+1})}]

(19)

\displaystyle+C_{\xi}\alpha_{n}^{2}\bigl(\norm{e_n}_{m,\xi}^{2}+1\bigr).

(20)

The proof is in Appendix
B.2
.

Lemma 4
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
and the choice (
14
), there exists
K_{\xi}<\infty
such that, for every
K\geq K_{\xi}
, there exist constants
C_{\xi,K}<\infty
and
N_{\xi,K}<\infty
such that, for every
n\geq N_{\xi,K}
,

\displaystyle V_{n}^{\xi}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\norm{e_n}_{m,\xi}^{2}\geq 0,

(21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}[V_{n+1}^{\xi}]

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(1-\mu_{\xi}\alpha_{n}+C_{\xi,K}r_{n}\bigr)V_{n}^{\xi}+C_{\xi,K}r_{n},\quad\text{a.s.,}

(22)

where
r_{n}\doteq\alpha_{n}^{2}+\absolutevalue{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_n}
.

The proof is in Appendix
B.3
.
As established in Lemma
4
, the shifted energy
V_{n}^{\xi}
satisfies an almost-supermartingale drift inequality. Therefore, we can directly invoke almost-supermartingale convergence results to obtain the almost sure convergence rates for
V_{n}^{\xi}
, which then translate to the original error
\norm{\theta_n - \theta_*}
. The detailed proof of Theorem
1
is deferred to Appendix
B.4
.

3.4
Limitations of Prior Poisson-Based Analyses

The Poisson equation is a standard tool to decompose Markovian noise (cf. RHS of (
5
)) into a martingale difference (cf. LHS of (
5
)) plus a telescoping remainder, enabling ODE-based convergence proofs
[
3
,
39
,
7
]
.
More recently, the Poisson equation also appears in convergence rates analysis of RL algorithms.
10
use the Poisson equation to handle Markovian noise in deriving concentration bounds for linear TD.

6
develop novel Poisson-equation-based bounds on noise terms for nonexpansive SA with Markovian noise, proving almost sure convergence of tabular average-reward TD.
11
combine Moreau envelopes with Poisson equation solutions to obtain finite-time mean-square bounds for two-timescale SA with arbitrary norm contractions and Markovian noise.
What we perceive as common across all these classical and recent works is
telescoping
.
Namely, obtaining Lemma
3
through Poisson equation decomposition is entirely a standard approach and is not novel at all.
Specifically, Lemma
3
can be viewed as a one-timescale specialization of the two-timescale recursion Lemma 3(a) of
11
.
Lemma
3
is interesting because we can further decompose the Poisson difference term
\alpha_{n}\langle\nabla M_{\xi}(e_{n}),H_{\theta_{n}}(Y_{n})\rangle-\alpha_{n}\langle\nabla M_{\xi}(e_{n+1}),H_{\theta_{n+1}}(Y_{n+1})\rangle
into a telescoping term
\alpha_{n}\langle\nabla M_{\xi}(e_{n}),H_{\theta_{n}}(Y_{n})\rangle-\alpha_{n+1}\langle\nabla M_{\xi}(e_{n+1}),H_{\theta_{n+1}}(Y_{n+1})\rangle
plus a residual term
(\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n})\langle\nabla M_{\xi}(e_{n+1}),H_{\theta_{n+1}}(Y_{n+1})\rangle
.
The telescoping term will then cancel when we perform summation over
n
.
However, this nice telescoping is not directly available because the terms are inside a conditional expectation indexed by
n
and thus cannot be directly telescoped.
The typical approach to handle this in all the above works is to take total expectations.
The
n
-indexed conditional expectation is then removed we can telescope
\alpha_{n}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inner{\nabla M_\xi(e_n)}{H_{\theta_n}(Y_n)}]-\alpha_{n+1}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inner{\nabla M_\xi(e_{n+1})}{H_{\theta_{n+1}}(Y_{n+1})}]
directly.
This is sufficient for obtaining convergence rates in expectation,
but it does not give pathwise control of the bias terms and thus is not sufficient for obtaining almost sure rates.
Our key novelty is instead to directly incorporate the Poisson correction into the drift function via the novel construction of the Poisson-Moreau drift
V_{n}^{\xi}
in (
17
),
allowing us to obtain an almost-supermartingale structure that preserves the conditional expectation.
What is particularly important is the selection of
K
such that our drift is nonnegative (cf. (
21
)), which is a key requirement for applying almost-supermartingale convergence results.

Remark 2

We find this corollary interesting because it does not require square-summability of the learning rate (i.e.,
0<\eta\leq 1/2
is allowed). This is not new because

already obtain

convergence rates for any

and
\eta\in(0,1)
.
We nevertheless include this corollary because our approach is different from that of

and it is conceptually interesting to see that the exact same phenomenon
follows naturally from a short Poisson-Moreau drift argument in the arbitrary-norm contractive
setting.
It is worth noting that the
L^{2}
convergence rates in

\eta\in(1/2,1]

It is not clear to us whether their analysis can eventually cover the full range

.

4
Applications

Although our theoretical framework applies broadly to contractive RL algorithms, including
Q
-learning and linear TD,
we in this section focus on a generalized family of tabular TD algorithms with generalized importance sampling factors as our main application.
This choice is motivated by the fact that almost sure convergence rates for several classical RL algorithms have already been studied using algorithm-specific arguments, whereas pathwise rates for the single-trajectory version of this generalized TD family do not appear to be available.
The recursion we study is the unified TD-learning template considered by
15
.
By choosing the generalized importance sampling factors
c
and
\rho
, this template recovers on-policy
n
-step TD
[
75
]
, off-policy
n
-step TD
[
61
,
75
]
,
Q^{\pi}(\lambda)

[
28
]
, Tree-Backup
[
61
]
, Retrace
[
57
]
, and
Q
-trace
[
33
]
.
We therefore do not claim novelty in the definition of the recursion or in the structural Hurwitz conditions for this TD family.
Our contribution is instead to combine these structural properties with the Poisson-Moreau drift to obtain pathwise almost sure rates under Markovian single-trajectory sampling.
This is complementary to
15
, who establish maximal high-probability concentration bounds for the same generalized TD template under i.i.d. sampling, but do not provide almost sure convergence rates for the Markovian single-trajectory recursion.

Consider an infinite-horizon discounted Markov decision process (MDP,
2
) with a finite state space
\mathcal{S}
, a finite action space
\mathcal{A}
, a discount factor
\gamma\in(0,1)
, a transition function
p:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
, and a reward function
r:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
\pi
be the target policy and
\pi_{b}
be the behavior policy. We assume strict behavior coverage,

\displaystyle\pi_{b}(a|s)>0,\quad\forall(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A},

(24)

and the trajectory is generated by
A_{t}\sim\pi_{b}(\cdot|S_{t})
,
S_{t+1}\sim p(\cdot|S_{t},A_{t})
. We assume the behavior state-action chain
(S_{t},A_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is irreducible and aperiodic, with stationary distribution

\displaystyle\textstyle d_{b}(s,a)\doteq\kappa_{b}(s)\pi_{b}(a|s),\quad d_{\min}\doteq\min_{(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}d_{b}(s,a)>0.

(25)

Fix a horizon
N\geq 1
and define the Markovian data window

\displaystyle Y_{n}\doteq(S_{n},A_{n},S_{n+1},A_{n+1},\ldots,S_{n+N},A_{n+N}).

(26)

The window chain
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is finite-state and has a unique invariant distribution on its recurrent class. Denote it by
\varpi
.
Let
\phi:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded feature map. The tabular case is recovered by taking
d=|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|
and
\phi(s,a)=e_{(s,a)}
with
e_{(s,a)}
being the standard basis vector corresponding to the state-action pair
(s,a)
. Let

c,\rho:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

be bounded generalized importance sampling factors. For a window
y=(s_{0},a_{0},\ldots,s_{N},a_{N})
, write
\phi_{i}\doteq\phi(s_{i},a_{i})
,
c_{i}\doteq c(s_{i},a_{i})
, and
\rho_{i}\doteq\rho(s_{i},a_{i})
. For
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\displaystyle\delta_{i}(w;y)\doteq r(s_{i},a_{i})+\gamma\rho_{i+1}\phi_{i+1}^{\top}w-\phi_{i}^{\top}w,\quad i=0,\ldots,N-1,

(27)

with the convention that an empty product is one, and define the generalized TD increment

\displaystyle\textstyle g_{c,\rho}(w,y)\doteq\phi_{0}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\gamma^{i}\quantity(\prod_{j=1}^{i}c_j)\delta_{i}(w;y).

(28)

The single-trajectory generalized TD recursion is

\displaystyle\textstyle w_{n+1}=w_{n}+a_{n}g_{c,\rho}(w_{n},Y_{n}),\quad a_{n}=\frac{a}{(n+1)^{\eta}}.

(29)

Equivalently, it is a linear stochastic approximation. Define

\displaystyle A_{c,\rho}(y)

\displaystyle\textstyle\doteq\phi_{0}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\gamma^{i}\quantity(\prod_{j=1}^{i}c_j)\quantity(\gamma\rho_{i+1}\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i)^{\top},

(30)

\displaystyle b_{c,\rho}(y)

\displaystyle\textstyle\doteq-\phi_{0}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\gamma^{i}\quantity(\prod_{j=1}^{i}c_j)r(s_{i},a_{i}).

(31)

Then

\displaystyle g_{c,\rho}(w,y)=A_{c,\rho}(y)w-b_{c,\rho}(y).

(32)

Let
\bar{A}_{c,\rho}\doteq\mathbb{E}_{\varpi}[A_{c,\rho}(Y)]
,
\bar{b}_{c,\rho}\doteq\mathbb{E}_{\varpi}[b_{c,\rho}(Y)]
.
The corresponding mean root is
w^{*}_{c,\rho}\doteq\bar{A}_{c,\rho}^{-1}\bar{b}_{c,\rho}
,
whenever
\bar{A}_{c,\rho}
is nonsingular.

The only algorithm-specific structural input is the following standard Hurwitz condition for generalized TD.

Condition 4.1

(Generalized TD Hurwitz condition)
.

The matrix
\bar{A}_{c,\rho}
is Hurwitz.

Under Condition

\displaystyle\norm{T_{\rm GTD}(w)-T_{\rm GTD}(w')}_{\rm GTD}\leq\kappa_{\rm GTD}\norm{w-w'}_{\rm GTD},\quad w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

6
Conclusion

Through the introduction of a Poisson-corrected Moreau drift,
this work establishes sharper almost sure convergence rates for contractive stochastic approximation under Markovian noise.
A few questions remain open.
First, Corollary
1
gives
L^{2}
rates for
\eta\in(0,1]
, but Theorem
1
only gives almost sure rates for
\eta\in(1/2,1]
.
It is not clear to us whether our methodology can yield almost sure rates for the full range
\eta\in(0,1]
.
Second,
although our almost sure rates are sharper than
63
,
they are still not tight.
It is not clear to us whether we can fully match the
O(n^{-1}\log\log n)
rate given by the law of the iterated logarithm.
Third,
in addition to almost sure rates,

63
also establish high probability concentration bounds with exponentially decaying tails for contractive SA with Markovian noise.
It is not clear to us whether our Poisson-Moreau framework can be refined to yield complementary concentration bounds with exponential tails.

)
.

For non-negative real sequences

\quantity{a_n, n \geq 0}

Lemma 6
.

Let
\quantity{X_n}_{n\geq N}
be nonnegative, adapted, and integrable. Suppose that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]\leq(1-\beta_{n})X_{n}+b_{n}\quad\text{a.s. for every }n\geq N,

where

and

are deterministic. Let

be a positive deterministic sequence and define

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}\doteq 1-\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}(1-\beta_{n}).

(40)

Assume that, after increasing

if necessary,

\displaystyle 0\leq\gamma_{n}\leq 1,\quad\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}=\infty,\quad\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}q_{n+1}b_{n}<\infty.

Then

\displaystyle q_{n}X_{n}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{a.s.}

(42)

Proof.

Multiplying (

) by

and using (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[q_{n+1}X_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle\leq q_{n+1}(1-\beta_{n})X_{n}+q_{n+1}b_{n}

(43)

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma_{n})q_{n}X_{n}+q_{n+1}b_{n}

(44)

\displaystyle=q_{n}X_{n}-\gamma_{n}q_{n}X_{n}+q_{n+1}b_{n}.

(45)

Since

, the Robbins–Siegmund almost-supermartingale theorem
[

]
implies that

converges almost surely to a finite random variable and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}q_{n}X_{n}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

(46)

If the almost sure limit of

were positive on an event of positive probability, then on that event

would be bounded below by a positive constant for all sufficiently large

, contradicting
\sum_{n}\gamma_{n}=\infty
. Hence the limit is zero almost surely.
∎
