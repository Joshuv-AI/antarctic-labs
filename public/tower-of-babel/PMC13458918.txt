Novel Direct Transcatheter Mitral Annuloplasty System
Authors: Wanjiao Chen, Yuliang Long, Wei Li, Xiaochun Zhang, Lei Zhang, Daxin Zhou, Wenzhi Pan, Junbo Ge
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study evaluated the novel L-Clip transcatheter mitral annuloplasty device in 18 porcine models, demonstrating its efficacy and safety. The device achieved 100% procedural success. Implantation times were a median of 28.4 minutes (22.7-34.1) for the first clip and 39.7 minutes (31.2-48.2) for the second. Significant immediate reductions were observed: annular area decreased 26% (from 13.4 cm2 [11.2-15.6] to 9.9 cm2 [7.5-12.3], P < 0.001), anteroposterior diameter decreased 31% (from 3.6 cm [3.2-4.0] to 2.5 cm [2.0-3.0], P < 0.001), and intercommissural distance decreased 17% (from 4.1 cm [3.8-4.4] to 3.4 cm [3.1-3.7], P < 0.001). Crucially, no mortality, valve damage, device displacement, embolism, or other serious adverse events occurred. The L-Clip system offers a user-friendly approach, achieving substantial annular reduction with an excellent safety profile in this model. These promising results support its potential as a minimally invasive alternative for functional mitral regurgitation, warranting further clinical investigation.

Mitral regurgitation (MR) frequently involves annular dilation.1,2 While surgical annuloplasty reduces annular size, transcatheter alternatives remain limited. Current devices like Cardioband face procedural complexity and safety concerns.3,4 Instead of implanting an annuloplasty ring, the K-Clip procedure safely and conveniently reduces the size of the annulus using a unique clip with anchor. This approach is successful in both animal models and clinical situations.5,6 The L-clip system, developed from the K-clip technology, is a novel transcatheter mitral annuloplasty device. In comparison to existing devices, it offers improved ease of use and annular reduction capabilities. Here, we present the findings of our animal experiments for the first time.

Methods

Eighteen swine were allocated into three cohorts (acute, 28-day, and 90-day; n = 6 each) to allow evaluation of acute procedural success, short-term safety, and chronic durability. The animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University. All animals received humane care in compliance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (eighth edition, National Academies Press, 2011). This sample size is consistent with the 3R principles and is commonly employed in preclinical cardiovascular device studies to detect major safety signals and estimate effect sizes for primary endpoints (eg, annular dimension reduction).

General anesthesia was induced followed by heparin administration to maintain activated clotting time at 250-500 seconds. Utilizing right femoral venous access, transseptal puncture established the mitral approach, while left femoral arterial access enabled coronary angiography. The delivery system was navigated to the left atrium under fluoroscopic and echocardiographic guidance (Figure 1C). The inner sheath was precisely oriented parallel to the mitral annular plane, targeting posterior segments P1 and P3 (Figure 1B). Anchors were screwed into annular tissue, followed by clip deployment: Arms were opened to 120°, retracted against the annulus, and closed (Figure 1C). Two 10-mm clips were implanted per subject, with predetachment coronary angiography confirming the absence of vascular compromise.

Primary endpoints included device success (stable clip position without mitral dysfunction or coronary stenosis) and safety (mortality, device migration, tamponade, thrombosis). Efficacy was assessed via echocardiographic measurement of annular area, anteroposterior diameter, and intercommissural distance at baseline, post-procedure, 28-day, and 90-day intervals. Pathological evaluation at terminal endpoints examined clip endothelialization, tissue integration, and end-organ impacts. Continuous variables are presented as medians with interquartile ranges (Q1–Q3) and were compared using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (SPSS v19; significance set at P < 0.05).

Results

All 18 swine (median weight 98.7 kg [95.1-102.3]; 66.7% male) underwent successful dual-clip implantation (36 total devices). The median procedural duration was 28.4 minutes (22.7-34.1) for the first clip and 39.7 minutes (31.2-48.2) for the second. Immediate postprocedural echocardiography demonstrated significant annular reduction: median area decreased from 13.4 cm2 (11.2-15.6) to 9.9 cm2 (7.5-12.3) (−26%, P < 0.001), median anteroposterior diameter from 3.6 cm (3.2-4.0) to 2.5 cm (2.0-3.0) (−31%, P < 0.001), and median intercommissural distance from 4.1 cm (3.8-4.4) to 3.4 cm (3.1-3.7) (−17%, P < 0.001). Safety outcomes revealed zero mortality, coronary stenosis, device migration, or thrombotic events. Pathological examination at 90 days confirmed complete endothelial coverage of clips, robust tissue integration without annular injury, and absence of thrombus formation. Gross inspection of major organs (brain, liver, kidneys, lungs) identified no device-related abnormalities, corroborating the system's preclinical safety profile.

Discussion

This preclinical study demonstrates the feasibility and safety of the novel L-Clip transcatheter mitral annuloplasty system in a porcine model. The device achieved immediate and sustained annular reduction, with echocardiography documenting 26% area decrease, 31% anteroposterior diameter shortening, and 17% intercommissural distance reduction—effects that remained stable throughout the 90-day follow-up period. Critically, these morphological improvements were accomplished without compromising valvular function, as evidenced by stable transvalvular gradients (<2 mm Hg) and the absence of new MR. The procedural efficiency—averaging under 30 minutes per clip implantation—combined with a 100% technical success rate and zero device-related complications (including coronary injury, migration, or thrombosis) positions the L-Clip as a promising alternative to existing transcatheter annuloplasty technologies.

Compared to the established Cardioband system—which requires implantation of more than 10 anchors along the posterior annulus—the L-Clip's two-anchor approach substantially simplifies procedural workflow. This design innovation directly addresses major limitations of current devices: the risk of anchor misplacement due to imaging limitations during multi-anchor deployment, potential coronary artery injury from deep penetration, and prolonged procedure times exceeding 90 minutes. Moreover, the L-Clip demonstrated superior annular dimension reduction (26% to 31% vs Cardioband's 10% to 19%7) despite using only minimal-sized clips at two implantation sites. This efficacy advantage, coupled with complete endothelialization observed at 90 days and the absence of late-term complications, suggests significant therapeutic potential for functional MR patients with annular dilation.

This first-in-animal study confirms the feasibility of the L-Clip system. Subsequent steps will evaluate its efficacy in pathological models with functional MR and annular dilation, quantifying its impact on MR severity and annular dimensions. Future work will include comparative studies against transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER), focusing on the potentially superior annular reduction, particularly of the anteroposterior diameter, offered by direct annuloplasty. Given evidence supporting combination therapy, we will also explore the feasibility of deploying the L-Clip with TEER: For complex pathologies, L-Clip could be used first for foundational annular remodeling, followed by TEER to address residual regurgitation precisely.

Study limitations

Three principal constraints merit consideration in interpreting these findings. First, the sample size was small (n = 18), which limits the generalizability of the findings and may have reduced statistical power to detect rare adverse events or subtle differences. Second, the absence of an established animal model for MR with annular dilation necessitated evaluation exclusively in healthy swine, thereby precluding assessment of the device's capacity to correct pathological regurgitation—a critical therapeutic endpoint requiring validation through subsequent clinical studies. Besides, experimental testing was confined to dual 10-mm clip configurations, primarily based on a careful assessment of the available space along the posterior annulus, leaving unexplored the potential efficacy enhancement achievable through larger clip sizes (up to 18 mm) or multi-clip implantation strategies, which prior tricuspid K-Clip experience suggests could amplify annular reduction.6 The modular design of the L-Clip system is intended precisely for this purpose. It enables operators to tailor the implantation strategy based on specific annular anatomy and pathology—for example, primarily targeting the P3 region in ischemic MR or covering a broader area in atrial functional MR. This "on-demand implantation" flexibility represents a key advantage over traditional fixed annuloplasty rings. Furthermore, should future annular dilation occur in other segments, supplementary implants could theoretically be placed in previously untreated areas—an option not feasible with conventional ring annuloplasty. Finally, while 90-day follow-up confirmed device endothelialization and the absence of thromboembolic complications, the relatively short observation window cannot definitively exclude long-term risks to valvular integrity or late-onset device failure, underscoring the need for extended large-animal investigations before clinical translation.

Conclusions

The L-Clip system represents a technically streamlined approach to transcatheter mitral annuloplasty, enabling rapid and stable annular dimension reduction. Preclinical results confirm exceptional procedural safety—with no device-related complications—and significant annular remodeling exceeding current technologies' performance benchmarks. While the absence of pathological MR models limits direct therapeutic efficacy assessment, the system's favorable safety-efficacy profile and procedural efficiency warrant clinical validation in patients with functional MR secondary to annular dilation. Future studies should investigate larger clip configurations and extended follow-up to optimize therapeutic applications.
