On the Full Set of Unitarizable Supermodules over 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) \mathfrak{sl}(m|n)
Steffen Schmidt

Abstract
. We present a novel classification of unitarizable supermodules over special linear Lie superalgebras using an algebraic quadratic Dirac operator introduced by Huang and Pandžić and a corresponding Dirac inequality.



1.
Introduction

The
special linear Lie superalgebra

{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
is defined as the Lie subsuperalgebra of the general linear Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
consisting of all block matrices of the form

(1.1)

X=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix},

where
A
is an
m\times m
complex matrix,
B
an
m\times n
complex matrix,

C
an
n\times m
complex matrix, and
D
an
n\times n
complex matrix.
These matrices are subject to the condition that the supertrace vanishes,
i.e.
,

(1.2)

\operatorname{str}(X)\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}(A)-\operatorname{tr}(D)=0.

Its natural
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading is given by

{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}={\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}_{\bar{1}},

where
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}_{\bar{0}}
consists of all block matrices with
B=C=0
, and

{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}_{\bar{1}}
consists of all block matrices with
A=D=0

(still subject to the vanishing supertrace condition). In what follows, we write
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
and assume
m+n>2
, thus excluding the nilpotent case
m=n=1
. Since
{\mathfrak{s}l}(m|n)\cong{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|m)
, we may further assume
m\leq n
.

In this work we give a classification of unitarizable supermodules over
\mathfrak{g}
. They are semisimple, and therefore constitute a fundamental class of supermodules from which one may approach the general theory. They also appear naturally in mathematical physics, in particular in the study of superconformal quantum field theories (see
[
7
,
4
]
and the references cited therein).

Unitarizable
\mathfrak{g}
-supermodules are defined with respect to a conjugate-linear
anti-involution
\omega
. The possible choices of
\omega
(equivalently, the real forms of

\mathfrak{g}
) are classified in
[
23
,
27
]
. A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is said to be
\omega
-unitarizable
if there exists a positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
M
such that

(1.3)

\langle xv,w\rangle=\langle v,\omega(x)w\rangle\qquad(x\in{\mathfrak{g}},\ v,w\in M).

Unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are rare. In
[
21
]
, Neeb and Salmasian proved that any
\omega
-unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is trivial unless
\omega
corresponds to a real form
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|0,n)
or
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n,0)
, and in these cases the supermodule is either a highest or a lowest weight supermodule
[
12
]
.
For
p=0
or
q=0
, the unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are finite-dimensional; for
p,q\neq 0
, they are infinite-dimensional. The treatment of highest weight and lowest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-supermodules is analogous.
This reduces the classification to the following problem:

Problem
.

Describe all unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules;
equivalently, determine all highest weights

\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
for which there exists an
\omega
-unitarizable
simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda
.

Our approach is based on the algebraic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
, introduced by Huang and Pandžić
[
14
]
for Lie superalgebras of Riemannian type. The supertrace defines a supersymmetric invariant bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{gl}}(m|n)
, which is non-degenerate if and only if
m\neq n
. Accordingly, for
m\neq n
we work in the quadratic Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, whereas in the remaining case we work in
{\mathfrak{gl}}(m|n)
. In either case, the restriction of the form to
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
is symplectic and hence gives rise to a Weyl algebra
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
. After choosing a symplectic basis
\{x_{i},\partial_{i}\}
of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
, compatible with a fixed conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega
, one obtains the distinguished element

(1.4)

\operatorname{D}=2\sum_{i=1}^{mn}(\partial_{i}\otimes x_{i}-x_{i}\otimes\partial_{i})\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}}{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.

It commutes with the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
and its square is given by a sum of quadratic Casimir elements and a scalar. These properties make
\operatorname{D}
the basic tool in our analysis.

Let
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
be the Weyl module over
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, and let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. The Weyl module
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is naturally a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module, called the ladder module, and
\operatorname{D}
acts on
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. If
M
is
\omega
-unitarizable, then
\operatorname{D}^{2}
is either negative- or positive-definite, according to whether
M
is finite- or infinite-dimensional. This yields a Dirac inequality for every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent
L_{0}(\mu)
of
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
:

(1.5)

(\mu+2\rho,\mu)\begin{cases}<(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda),&\text{if }M\text{ is finite-dimensional},\\[4.0pt]
>(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda),&\text{if }M\text{ is infinite-dimensional}.\end{cases}

Conversely,
[
24
]
proves that if
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module satisfying suitable unitarity conditions, and if this inequality is strict for all
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors in a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
M
, then
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable. This is the criterion on which our classification rests.

1.1.
The Classification Method

We classify all unitarizable highest weight supermodules over
\mathfrak{g}
, equivalently all
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|0,n)
-unitarizable supermodules. The cases
p=0
or
q=0
give exactly the finite-dimensional unitarizable supermodules, whereas the case
p,q\neq 0
gives the genuinely infinite-dimensional part of the classification, apart from the trivial supermodule. Accordingly, we consider these cases separately. In each case we fix a certain positive system with Borel
{\mathfrak{b}}
and consider highest weight supermodules whose highest weight vectors are even. Accordingly, all classification results are understood up to application of the parity reversion functor
\Pi
.

The classification of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules
L(\Lambda)
starts from the immediate consequences of the definition of unitarity. Since unitarizability with respect to
{\mathfrak{g}}
implies unitarizability with respect to
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, we may assume that
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module
L_{0}(\Lambda)
. The classification of such modules is well known. We then prove that
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
has a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration with simple composition factors, each a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. In the finite-dimensional case,
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
decomposes into a direct sum of these composition factors. In addition, the unitarizability of
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
forces certain conditions on
\Lambda
and on the relations among its entries in the standard realization; see Lemma
6
. We refer to these as the unitarity conditions. These conditions first reduce the possible highest weights of unitarizable highest weight supermodules to a manageable class. Within this class, the Dirac inequality coming from suitable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors of
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
then singles out exactly those highest weights that are unitarizable.

1.1.1.
The Finite-Dimensional Case

In the case
p=0
or
q=0
, we work with the distinguished Borel subalgebra and assume that
\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions. We then obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for the unitarizability of
L(\Lambda)
in terms of the Dirac inequalities. Once an explicit parametrization of
\Lambda
is available, this criterion becomes completely explicit. In parallel, we describe the method both in terms of the Dirac inequalities and in terms of an explicit realization.

More concretely, the highest weight
\Lambda
admits two equivalent descriptions. In standard coordinates,
\Lambda
has general form

(1.6)

\Lambda=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m}|\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{k_{0}-1},\mu,\ldots,\mu),

with
\mu^{k_{0}-1}\neq\mu
. Equivalently, using the real form of the even Lie subalgebra, every such
\Lambda
may be written in the form

(1.7)

\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}+\tfrac{x_{0}}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1),\qquad x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\Lambda_{0}
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. Thus, for fixed
\Lambda_{0}
, one obtains a one-parameter family of highest weights

(1.8)

\Lambda(x)=\Lambda_{0}+\tfrac{x}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1),

of which
\Lambda
is the member corresponding to the value
x=x_{0}
and each
\Lambda(x)
is the highest weight of a finite-dimensional unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. The Dirac inequalities then give necessary and sufficient conditions on
x
for unitarizability.

The classification of all unitarizable supermodules may be viewed either in terms of the Dirac inequalities for
\Lambda
or, for fixed
\Lambda_{0}
, as a three-step analysis of the parameter
x
. The analysis rests on a substantial simplification of the Dirac inequalities. The key objects are the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents of the form
L_{0}(\Lambda(x)-\alpha)
, with
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, together with the associated Dirac inequalities
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\alpha)>0
.

(1)

The first step is to determine all values of
x
such that

(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\alpha)>0

for every
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. In this case, the Dirac inequality holds automatically on each
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent. This defines a threshold
x_{\max}\in{\mathbb{R}}
with the property that the simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
L(\Lambda(x))
is unitarizable for all
x\in(x_{\max},\infty)
. Equivalently,
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable whenever

(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)>0

for every
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
.

(2)

The next step is to determine a threshold
x_{\min}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, using the unitarity conditions, the Dirac inequality, and the Kac–Shapovalov determinant formula, below which unitarity fails. Namely,
x_{\min}
is the largest real number such that for every
x<x_{\min}
there exists
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
for which
L(\Lambda(x))
has a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of highest weight
\Lambda(x)-\alpha
and the Dirac inequality fails

(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\alpha)<0.

For all such
x
, the module
L(\Lambda(x))
is not unitarizable. By the unitarity conditions, this threshold is determined by the value of
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k_{0}})
. Equivalently,
L(\Lambda)
for
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
of the form (
1.6
) is not unitarizable if

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k_{0}})<0.

(3)

It remains to analyze the interval

I\coloneqq[x_{\min},x_{\max}].

We show that, on this interval,
L(\Lambda(x))
is unitarizable precisely when
x
is integral, or equivalently, precisely when

(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0\qquad\text{for some }k=k_{0},\ldots,n.

If
x
is non-integral, then
\Lambda(x)
fails to satisfy the unitarity conditions. If
x
is integral, then atypicality implies that the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents for which the Dirac inequality fails do not occur in the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-decomposition of
L(\Lambda(x))
.

As a consequence, we obtain the following classification of all finite-dimensional unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. The following theorem appears as Theorem
27
in the main text.

Theorem 1
.

\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule if and only if the following conditions hold:

a)

\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions.

b)

If
k_{0}
is the smallest integer such that
(\Lambda,\delta_{k_{0}}-\delta_{n})=0
, then one of the following holds:

(i)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0
for some
k=k_{0},\ldots,n
, or

(ii)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{n})>0
.

1.1.2.
Infinite-Dimensional Case

In the case
p,q\neq 0
, we work with the standard positive system for
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
and with the non-standard positive system
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}=A\sqcup B
, where

(1.9)

A\coloneqq\{\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k}:1\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq k\leq n\},\qquad B\coloneqq\{-\epsilon_{j}+\delta_{k}:p+1\leq j\leq m,\ 1\leq k\leq n\},

as this choice is adapted to the study of unitarity and the Dirac operator. The classification for the distinguished positive system then follows by applying a sequence of odd reflections. As in the finite-dimensional case, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for the unitarizability of
L(\Lambda)
in terms of the Dirac inequalities. Once a suitable parametrization is fixed, these conditions take an explicit form.

Assume that
\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions. It has in standard coordinates the general form

(1.10)

\Lambda=(\lambda,\ldots,\lambda,\lambda^{i_{0}+1},\ldots,\lambda^{p},\lambda^{p+1},\ldots,\lambda^{m-j_{0}-1},\lambda^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}|\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n}),

with
\lambda^{i_{0}+1}\neq\lambda
and
\lambda^{m-j_{0}-1}\neq\lambda^{\prime}
. Equivalently,

(1.11)

\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}+\tfrac{x_{0}}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1),\qquad x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\Lambda_{0}
is the highest weight of an infinite-dimensional unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. Thus, for fixed
\Lambda_{0}
, one obtains a one-parameter family of highest weights

(1.12)

\Lambda(x)=\Lambda_{0}+\tfrac{x}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1),

of which
\Lambda
is the member corresponding to the value
x=x_{0}
and each
\Lambda(x)
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. As in the finite-dimensional case, the Dirac inequalities provide necessary and sufficient conditions for the unitarizability of
L(\Lambda(x))
. The analysis is governed by the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors
L_{0}(\Lambda-\alpha)
and
L_{0}(\Lambda-\beta)
, where
\alpha\in A
and
\beta\in B
, together with the corresponding Dirac inequalities
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)\leq 0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)\leq 0
. This leads naturally to a separate treatment of the two families.

From this point of view, the classification of all unitarizable supermodules may be described either in terms of the Dirac inequalities for
\Lambda
or, for fixed
\Lambda_{0}
, as a three-step analysis of the parameter
x
.

(1)

A first step is to determine the range of values of
x
for which

(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\alpha)<0\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in A,\qquad(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\beta)<0\quad\text{for all }\beta\in B.

When these inequalities impose non-trivial restrictions, the Dirac inequality holds automatically on all
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents. Indeed, they define two thresholds: a left threshold
x_{\max}^{L}
, determined by the maximal value of
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\beta)
, and a right threshold
x_{\min}^{R}
, determined by the maximal value of
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\alpha)
. It then follows that
L(\Lambda(x))
is unitarizable for all

x\in(x_{\max}^{L},x_{\min}^{R}).

Equivalently,
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable whenever

(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)<0\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in A,\qquad(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)<0\quad\text{for all }\beta\in B.

(2)

The next step is to determine the extremal values
x_{\min}^{L}
and
x_{\max}^{R}
, using the unitarity conditions, the Dirac inequality, and the Kac–Shapovalov determinant formula, where
x_{\min}^{L}
is the minimal value such that
L(\Lambda(x))
is not unitarizable for all
x<x_{\min}^{L}
, and
x_{\max}^{R}
is the maximal value such that
L(\Lambda(x))
is not unitarizable for all
x>x_{\max}^{R}
. These thresholds are governed by the occurrence of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors for which the Dirac inequalities fail. By the unitarity conditions,
x_{\min}^{L}
is determined by
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}+\delta_{n})
and
x_{\max}^{R}
by
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\epsilon_{i_{0}}-\delta_{1})
. Equivalently, for
\Lambda
of the form (
1.10
),
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable whenever
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i_{0}}-\delta_{1})>0
or
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}+\delta_{1})>0
.

(3)

It remains to consider the residual intervals

I^{L}\coloneqq[x_{\min}^{L},x_{\max}^{L}],\qquad I^{R}\coloneqq[x_{\min}^{R},x_{\max}^{R}],

and, if
x_{\max}^{L}<x_{\min}^{R}
, then

I\coloneqq[x_{\min}^{L},x_{\max}^{R}].

As these intervals have integral length, they admit a natural notion of integrality. Unitarizability on these intervals occurs precisely at the integral points, or equivalently, precisely when

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0

for some
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
or
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
. The non-integral points are excluded by the unitarity conditions, whereas at the integral points the Dirac inequalities are satisfied on all
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors. Equivalently, let
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
be a weight of the form (
1.10
) such that
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)\not<0
for some
\alpha\in A
or
(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)\not<0
for some
\beta\in B
. Then
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)<0
for all
\beta\in B
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for some
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
;

(ii)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for some
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
and some
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
;

(iii)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0
for some
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)<0
for all
\alpha\in A
.

As a consequence, we obtain the following classification of all unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules in the case
p,q\neq 0
. The following theorem appears as Theorem
34
in the main text.

Theorem 2
.

Represent any weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
in the form (
1.10
). Then
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule if and only if the following holds:

a)

\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions of Lemma
6
, and

b)

one of the following conditions hold:

(i)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})<0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
;

(ii)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
and
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
;

(iii)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})<0
for
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
;

(iv)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})<0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})<0
.

1.2.
Comparison to the Literature

The classification of unitarizable
\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)
-supermodules has been addressed in three main works.

The first is the work of Furutsu–Nishiyama
[
12
]
, which classifies the unitarizable supermodules with integral highest weight. Their method is based on a realization of the Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|0,n)
inside suitable orthosymplectic Lie superalgebras. They then study the restriction to
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|0,n)
of the oscillator supermodule, namely the unique unitarizable simple supermodule of the ambient orthosymplectic Lie superalgebra. This construction is the super-analogue of the classical oscillator representation, also known as the Segal–Shale–Weil representation. In this way, their work extends to the super setting a theorem of Kashiwara and Vergne, which states that every unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)
-module with integral highest weight occurs inside a tensor product of oscillator representations.

The second approach was developed by Jakobsen
[
16
]
, using his
principle of the last possible place of unitarity
. The idea is to study one-parameter families of highest weight modules and to determine the last parameter value for which unitarity can occur. In practice, this amounts to locating the reducibility points where singular vectors appear and identifying the first point at which the invariant Hermitian form fails to remain positive semidefinite. The classification problem is thus reduced to determining this threshold and describing the modules on the unitary side of it.

Later, Günaydin–Volin observed that Jakobsen’s analysis leaves out certain cases; in particular, for the physically important Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{su}}(2,2|N)
, his list does not coincide with the classification used in the physics literature, due to Dobrev and Petkova
[
5
]
, whereas these cases are included in the work of Günaydin–Volin. Their classification is obtained by first deriving linear inequalities on the highest weight from positivity of the norms of odd-root descendants, expressed in terms of plaquette constraints, and then showing that these inequalities are sufficient as well. To do so, they realize every admissible highest weight in a
\gamma
-deformed oscillator construction on a Fock space in which determinant operators are allowed to appear with nonintegral powers. For this reason, the results of Günaydin–Volin provide the most natural point of comparison for our work.

The classification obtained by Günaydin–Volin may be viewed as an extension of the Furutsu–Nishiyama classification. When their conditions are reformulated in terms of our Dirac inequalities, one finds that their classification agrees with ours. In particular, our results recover the classification of Günaydin–Volin, while recasting it in the language of Dirac inequalities.

1.3.
Notation

We denote by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
the set of positive integers. Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\coloneqq{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
be the ring of integers modulo
2
. We denote the elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
by
\overline{0}
(the residue class of even integers) and
\overline{1}
(the residue class of odd integers). The ground field is
{\mathbb{C}}
, unless otherwise stated.

If
V\coloneqq V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is a super vector space and
v\in V
is a homogeneous element, then
p(v)
denotes the parity of
v
, meaning
p(v)=\bar{0}
if
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
and
p(v)=\bar{1}
if
v\in V_{\bar{1}}
.

For the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
, we denote its universal enveloping superalgebra by
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. The universal enveloping algebra of the Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
is denoted by
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
. Their centers are denoted by
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
, respectively.

Any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
restricts to a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule, where each
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module is viewed as concentrated in a single parity. In what follows, this
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading is left implicit, and we simply refer to them as
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules.

2.
Special Linear Lie Superalgebras

\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)

We briefly review the structure theory of
{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
, with a focus on the real forms

{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|r,s)
. These will later prove central in the study of unitarizable
supermodules over
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

2.1.
Structure Theory

The Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
is simple whenever
m\neq n
and
m+n\geq 2
. In this case, the extension to
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
admits a splitting, realized by sending

1\in{\mathbb{C}}
to the identity matrix
E_{m+n}\in{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
. If
m=n
, then
E_{2n}
already belongs to
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|n)
. As a result,
{\mathfrak{gl}}(n|n)
admits no splitting, and
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|n)
is no longer simple,
while still remaining indecomposable.
The corresponding simple quotient of codimension one is the

projective special linear Lie superalgebra
,

\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)\coloneqq{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|n)/{\mathbb{C}}E_{2n}.

We consider
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
as a subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
. The abelian Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{d}}\coloneqq\{H=\operatorname{diag}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m+n})\}
of diagonal matrices
in
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
is a
Cartan subalgebra
for
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
, that is, a maximal ad-diagonalizable subalgebra. As Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
, we take the subspace
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{d}}
of diagonal matrices with vanishing supertrace. The dual space
{\mathfrak{d}}^{*}
is equipped with the standard basis

(\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{m},\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})
,
defined by

(2.1)

\epsilon_{i}(H)=h_{i},\qquad\delta_{k}(H)=h_{m+k},

for
H\in{\mathfrak{d}}
, with
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq k\leq n
.
Accordingly, any weight
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
can be written as

(2.2)

\lambda=\lambda^{1}\epsilon_{1}+\cdots+\lambda^{m}\epsilon_{m}+\mu^{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+\mu^{n}\delta_{n},

and we identify
\lambda
with the tuple

(\lambda^{1},\dotsc,\lambda^{m}|\mu^{1},\dotsc,\mu^{n})
.
Note that shifting by
(1,\ldots,1|-1,\ldots,-1)
leaves the weight unchanged. Since
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
is a Cartan subalgebra for
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, its action on any finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module is diagonalizable. Hence the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{h}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is diagonalizable, and
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a root space decomposition:

(2.3)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}\setminus\{0\}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha},\qquad{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}\coloneqq\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}}\ :\ [H,X]=\alpha(H)X\ \text{for all}\ H\in{\mathfrak{h}}\}.

The
set of roots
is
\Delta=\Delta_{\bar{0}}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1}}
where

(2.4)

\begin{split}\Delta_{\bar{0}}&=\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}),\pm(\delta_{k}-\delta_{l})\ :\ 1\leq i<j\leq m,\ 1\leq k<l\leq n\},\\
\Delta_{\bar{1}}&=\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k})\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq k\leq n\},\end{split}

are the
even
and
odd roots
, respectively.

Each root space has superdimension either
(1|0)
or
(0|1)
.
The set
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
decomposes as the disjoint union of the root systems
of
{\mathfrak{s}l}(m)
and
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n)
.
For the even part, we choose once and for all the standard system of positive roots

(2.5)

\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\coloneqq\{\,\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j},\ \delta_{k}-\delta_{l}\ :\ 1\leq i<j\leq m,\ 1\leq k<l\leq n\,\},

so that the root vectors associated with
\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}
for
i<j

are realized as strictly upper triangular matrices in
{\mathfrak{s}l}(m)
, while those associated
with
\delta_{k}-\delta_{l}
for
k<l
are strictly upper triangular matrices in
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n)
;
both embeddings are taken diagonally inside
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.
The odd positive roots
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
will be specified in the next section (
cf.
(
2.21
)).
In total, the set of positive roots is

\Delta^{+}\coloneqq\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+},

and we define the
Weyl vector

\rho
as
\rho=\rho_{\bar{0}}-\rho_{\bar{1}}
where

(2.6)

\rho_{\bar{0}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}}\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(m-2i+1)\epsilon_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(n-2j+1)\delta_{j}\right),\qquad\rho_{\bar{1}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}\alpha.

For a fixed positive system
\Delta^{+}
, we define the
fundamental system

\pi\subset\Delta^{+}
to be the set of all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
which cannot be written as the sum of two roots in
\Delta^{+}
. Elements of
\pi
are called
simple
roots. For
\pi=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
, any
\alpha\in\Delta
can be uniquely represented as a linear combination

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}\alpha_{i},

where either all
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
or all
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
.

With respect to a chosen system of positive roots
\Delta^{+}
, the Lie superalgebra

{\mathfrak{g}}
admits the
triangular decomposition

(2.7)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+},\qquad{\mathfrak{n}}^{\pm}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\pm\alpha},

where
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\pm}
are nilpotent Lie subsuperalgebras, and the even part reproduces the
usual triangular decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.
The corresponding
Borel subalgebra
is
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
.

The general linear Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
is equipped with a natural bilinear form

(2.8)

(X,Y)\coloneqq\operatorname{str}(XY),\qquad X,Y\in{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)},

which is even, supersymmetric, and invariant.
Explicitly, the form is symmetric on
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{0}}
, skew-symmetric on
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{1}}
,
and
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{0}}
is orthogonal to
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{1}}
.
Invariance means that
([X,Y],Z)=(X,[Y,Z])
holds for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
. The form
(\cdot,\cdot)
is always non-degenerate on
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
.
On
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
, however, it remains non-degenerate only if
m\neq n
;
in the case
m=n
, the one-dimensional center of
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|n)
coincides with the radical.
Its restriction to the diagonal subalgebra
{\mathfrak{d}}
is still non-degenerate, and the
induced bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{d}}^{*}
will be denoted by the same symbol.
With respect to the standard basis, we obtain for
1\leq i,j\leq m
and
1\leq k,l\leq n
:

(2.9)

(\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j})=\delta_{ij},\qquad(\delta_{k},\delta_{l})=-\delta_{kl},\qquad(\epsilon_{i},\delta_{k})=0.

For
m\neq n
, the bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
restricts to a non-degenerate form on
{\mathfrak{h}}
.
In this situation, every root
\alpha\in\Delta
corresponds to a uniquely determined element

h_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, characterized by the identity

\alpha(H)=(H,h_{\alpha})\quad\text{for all }H\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

We refer to
h_{\alpha}
as the
dual root
of
\alpha
. In the exceptional case
m=n
, we fix the dual roots as elements of
{\mathfrak{h}}
by requiring

\alpha(H)=(H,h_{\alpha})
for all
H\in{\mathfrak{d}}
. Extending the assignment by linearity gives a bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, defined by

(2.10)

(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq(h_{\alpha},h_{\beta}),\qquad\alpha,\beta\in\Delta,

which is non-degenerate exactly when
m\neq n
.
It follows immediately that all odd roots are isotropic, that is,
(\alpha,\alpha)=0
for every
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}
.

The root system
\Delta
admits an action of the Weyl group
W
of the even part
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.
The group
W
is isomorphic to
S_{m}\times S_{n}
, the product of the symmetric groups on
m
and
n
letters.
It is generated by the reflections with respect to the even roots,

(2.11)

r_{\alpha}(\beta)=\beta-2\frac{(\alpha,\beta)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\,\alpha,\qquad\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}},\ \beta\in\Delta.

This action extends linearly to
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and preserves the bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
The Weyl group of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is by definition that of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.
The dot action of
W
on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is defined by

(2.12)

w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho,\qquad\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\ w\in W.

Two weights
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
are
W
-linked if
\mu=w\cdot\lambda
for some
w\in W
; this defines an equivalence relation on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
.
The equivalence class
\{w\cdot\lambda:\,w\in W\}
is called the
W
-linkage class of
\lambda
.

Not all fundamental systems can be transformed into one another through the action of
W
. For this, we need a sequence of
odd reflections
. Given an odd, isotropic simple root
\theta\in\pi
, we recall that an
odd reflection
satisfies:

(2.13)

r_{\theta}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\alpha+\theta&\text{if}\ (\alpha,\theta)\neq 0,\\
\alpha&\text{if}\ (\alpha,\theta)=0,\\
-\theta&\text{if}\ \alpha=\theta\end{cases}

for any
\alpha\in\pi
. Then,
\Delta_{\theta}^{+}=\{-\theta\}\cup(\Delta^{+}\setminus\{\theta\})
forms a new positive system with the fundamental system
\pi_{\theta}\coloneqq r_{\theta}(\pi)
. If
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are two fundamental systems such that
\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}=(\Delta^{\prime})^{+}_{\bar{0}}
, then
\pi^{\prime}
can be obtained from
\pi
by a sequence of odd reflections
[
28
, Proposition 1]
.

2.2.
Real Forms

\mathfrak{su}(p,q|r,s)

Let
V={\mathbb{C}}^{m|n}
.
For
p,q,r,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p+q=m
,
r+s=n
, we define the Hermitian form

(2.14)

\langle v,w\rangle\coloneqq\overline{v}^{T}J_{(p,q|r,s)}w,\qquad J_{(p,q|r,s)}\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}I_{p,q}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&I_{r,s}\end{array}\right),

where
\overline{\ \cdot\ }
denotes complex conjugation, and vectors of
V
are taken as columns. The matrix
I_{k,l}
is the diagonal matrix having the first
k
entries equal to
1
followed by the last
l
entries equal to
-1
. This Hermitian form is
consistent
, that is,
\langle V_{\bar{0}},V_{\bar{1}}\rangle=0
.

The
unitary Lie superalgebras

\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)
are defined by

(2.15)

\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)_{\bar{k}}=\{X\in{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{k}}:\ \langle Xv,w\rangle+\langle v,Xw\rangle=0,\ \forall v,w\in V\},

and
\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)=\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)_{\bar{1}}
. The
special unitary Lie superalgebras
are

(2.16)

{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|r,s)=\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)\cap{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}=\{X\in{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}:\ J_{(p,q|r,s)}^{-1}X^{\dagger}J_{(p,q|r,s)}=-X\},

with
X^{\dagger}
being the conjugate transpose. We think of
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|r,s)
as the real form of
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
defined by the fixed point Lie subalegebra of the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, where

(2.17)

\omega(X)=J_{(p,q|r,s)}^{-1}X^{\dagger}J_{(p,q|r,s)},\qquad X\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Among these real forms, only
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n,0)
and
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|0,n)
, which are isomorphic, admit nontrivial unitarizable supermodules (Theorem
5
).
A direct computation, distinguishing the compact case (
p=0
or
q=0
) and the non-compact case (
p,q\neq 0
), shows that
\omega
admits exactly two matrix realizations, yielding the explicit conjugate-linear anti-involutions according to whether
p=0
,
q=0
,
r=0
or
s=0
. We express a general element
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
as

(2.18)

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}P_{1}\\
P_{2}\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}Q_{1}&Q_{2}\end{matrix}&E\end{array}\right),

where
P_{1}
is a
p\times n
matrix,
P_{2}
is a
q\times n
matrix,
Q_{1}
is a
n\times p
matrix,
Q_{2}
is a
n\times q
-matrix,
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
,
E\in{\mathfrak{su}}(n)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\operatorname{tr}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}-\operatorname{tr}E=0
. In the case
p=0
or
q=0
we use the notation (
1.1
) for the standard block form.

Lemma 3

(
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
,
[
24
]
)
.

There are exactly two conjugate-linear anti-involutions on
{\mathfrak{g}}
compatible with the standard ordering, which produce the even real forms
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|0,n)_{\bar{0}}\cong{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n,0)_{\bar{0}}
:

a)

If
p=0
or
q=0
, then

\omega_{\pm}\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}A&B\\
\hline\cr C&D\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}A^{\dagger}&\pm C^{\dagger}\\
\hline\cr\pm B^{\dagger}&D^{\dagger}\end{array}\right).

The real Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{su}}(m,0|n,0)\cong{\mathfrak{su}}(0,m|0,n)
corresponds to the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega_{+}
, while the real Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{su}}(m,0|0,n)\cong{\mathfrak{su}}(0,m|n,0)
belongs to the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega_{-}
.

b)

If
p,q\neq 0
, then

\omega_{(-,+)}(X)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}a^{\dagger}&-c^{\dagger}\\
-b^{\dagger}&d^{\dagger}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}-Q_{1}^{\dagger}\\
Q_{2}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}-P_{1}^{\dagger}&P_{2}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}E^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right),\qquad\omega_{(+,-)}(X)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}a^{\dagger}&-c^{\dagger}\\
-b^{\dagger}&d^{\dagger}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}Q_{1}^{\dagger}\\
-Q_{2}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}P_{1}^{\dagger}&-P_{2}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}E^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right),

The real Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|0,n)
belongs to
\omega_{(-,+)}
while
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n,0)
belongs to
\omega_{(+,-)}
.

In the compact case (
p=0
or
q=0
), there exist two convenient systems of odd positive roots,

(2.19)

\Delta_{\bar{1},\mathrm{st}}^{+}=\{\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j}|1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n\},\qquad\Delta_{\bar{1},-\mathrm{st}}^{+}=\{-\epsilon_{i}+\delta_{j}|1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n\},

which differ only by sign. In the literature,
\Delta_{\bar{1},\mathrm{st}}^{+}
is known as the distinguished (or standard) positive system, while
\Delta_{\bar{1},-\mathrm{st}}^{+}
is called the anti-distinguished positive system. The corresponding Borel subalgebras
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathrm{st}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{-\mathrm{st}}
are referred to as
distinguished
and
anti-distinguished
, respectively. Under the canonical isomorphism
{\mathfrak{s}l}(m|n)\cong{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|m)
, the anti-distinguished Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}_{-\mathrm{st}}
of
{\mathfrak{s}l}(m|n)
is mapped to the distinguished one
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathrm{st}}
of
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|m)
.

In this article, we choose
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta_{\bar{1},\mathrm{st}}^{+}
, so that
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
consists of upper block matrices in
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathrm{st}}
is the distinguished Borel. In particular, the odd part of the Weyl vector (
2.6
) is

(2.20)

\rho_{\bar{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n\sum_{i=1}^{m}\epsilon_{i}-m\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}\right).

Taking into account the real structure, in the non-compact case (
p,q\neq 0
) let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
(resp.
{\mathfrak{q}}_{i}
) denote the subspace of
{\mathfrak{g}}
in which only
P_{i}
(resp.
Q_{i}
) in (
2.18
) is nonzero.
One obtains three relevant systems of odd positive roots described by:

(2.21)

{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1},\mathrm{st}}^{+}={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{2},\qquad{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1},-\mathrm{st}}^{+}={\mathfrak{q}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{2},\qquad{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1},\mathrm{nst}}^{+}={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{2},

called
standard
,
minus standard
, and
non-standard
, respectively.
We fix the non-standard system, denoted
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, since this choice is adapted to the study of unitarity and the Dirac operator
[
24
]
. The relevance of this choice for the classification will be discussed in Section
3.2
.
Accordingly,

\Delta^{+}=\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+},

and the corresponding half-sum of positive odd roots is

(2.22)

\rho_{\bar{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n\sum_{i=1}^{p}\epsilon_{i}-n\sum_{j=p+1}^{m}\epsilon_{j}+(q-p)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{k}\right).

3.
Unitarizable Supermodules and Dirac Operators

In this section, we introduce unitarizable supermodules over
{\mathfrak{g}}
and summarize their basic properties. We also present the (algebraic) quadratic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
and formulate the Dirac inequality, which serves as the main tool for classifying the full set of unitarizable supermodules.

3.1.
Fundamentals

A
super
Hilbert space
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded complex Hilbert space
(\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\bar{1}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}})
such that
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{0}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{1}}
are mutually
orthogonal subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
.
1
1

1

Super Hilbert spaces have an equivalent description in terms of even super positive definite super Hermitian forms. For a detailed comparison, we refer to
[
16
,
25
]
.
The inner product is conjugate-linear in the first and linear in the second argument. With this in place, we define
unitarizable supermodules
relative to a real form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We realize the real forms as the fixed point Lie subalgebras of conjugate-linear anti-involutions
\omega
, that is,
\mathfrak{g}^{\omega}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:\omega(x)=-x\}
.

Definition 4

(
[
16
, Definition 2.3]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, and let
\omega
be a conjugate-linear anti-involution on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. The supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
is called an
\omega
-unitarizable

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
if
{\mathcal{H}}
is a super Hilbert space such that for all
v,w\in{\mathcal{H}}
and all
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, the Hermitian product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
is
\omega
-contravariant:

\langle Xv,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\langle v,\omega(X)w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}.

If we work with
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-supermodules instead, we extend
\omega
to
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
in the natural way, using the same notation. In this context, a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
is unitarizable if and only if it is a Hermitian representation over
({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}),\omega)
, meaning that
\langle Xv,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\langle v,\omega(X)w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
holds for all
v,w\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
X\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. When
\omega
is to be implied from context, we just say “unitarizable”.

We fix a real form
\mathfrak{g}^{\omega}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
\omega
-unitarizable
\mathfrak{g}
-supermodule.
By a standard argument,
\mathcal{H}
is completely reducible,
i.e.
, the orthogonal complement of any invariant subspace is again invariant. Consider the underlying
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
-module

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ev}},

obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by restriction to
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
and forgetting the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ev}}
is a unitarizable
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
-module with respect to the real form
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{\omega}
, and in particular completely reducible as a
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
-module in the sense above. This complete reducibility, together with the following theorem, motivates our focus on highest and lowest weight supermodules; we therefore briefly recall their definition. Fix a triangular decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
.
A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is called
highest
(resp.
lowest weight
) if there exist

\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and a nonzero vector
v_{\Lambda}\in M
such that:

a)

Xv_{\Lambda}=0
for all
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
(resp.
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}
),

b)

Hv_{\Lambda}=\Lambda(H)v_{\Lambda}
for all
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, and

c)

\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})v_{\Lambda}=M
.

The vector
v_{\Lambda}
is called a
highest
(resp.
lowest
) weight vector of
M
.
We call
M

even
or
odd
according to the parity of
v_{\Lambda}
. In this article, we place
v_{\Lambda}
in even degree. Accordingly, all our results are understood up to application of the parity reversion functor
\Pi
.

Theorem 5

(
[
12
,
21
]
)
.

The special linear Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits non-trivial

\omega
-unitarizable supermodules if and only if the conjugate-linear
anti-involution
\omega
is associated with one of the real forms

\mathfrak{su}(p,q|n,0)\quad\text{or}\quad\mathfrak{su}(p,q|0,n),\qquad p+q=m.

Moreover, any simple
\omega
-unitarizable supermodule is either a highest weight
or a lowest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule.

For the remainder, we restrict attention to
\omega
-unitarizable highest weight

\mathfrak{g}
-supermodules, since the treatment of the lowest weight case is entirely analogous.
It is important to note that non-trivial unitarizable highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-supermodules for
p,q\neq 0

exist only with respect to
\omega_{(-,+)}
, while
\omega_{(+,-)}
gives rise to unitarizable
lowest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-supermodules (see
[
16
,
25
]
).
Accordingly, we work with the real forms
\mathfrak{su}(p,q|0,n)
, and for convenience of
notation we abbreviate
\mathfrak{su}(p,q|0,n)
by
\mathfrak{su}(p,q|n)
. If either
p=0
or
q=0
, we set
{\mathfrak{su}}(m|n)\coloneqq{\mathfrak{su}}(m,0|n,0)
and fix the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega_{+}
. The discussion for
\omega_{-}
is entirely analogous, and we therefore state only the corresponding results. In the following, the prefix
\omega_{+}/\omega_{(-,+)}
will be omitted, and we shall simply speak of unitarizable highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-supermodules.

We are now in a position to state the main problem of this work.

Problem
.

Describe the full set of unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules;
equivalently, determine all highest weights

\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
for which there exists a unitarizable
simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda
.

A first step towards a complete description consists in restricting the possible form of the occurring highest weights, which follows directly from the definition of unitarity. We refer to these as the
unitarity conditions
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m}|\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n})\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. Then the following
unitarity conditions
hold:

a)

If
p=0
or
q=0
and
\omega=\omega_{+}
, then
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module and

(i)

\lambda^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m}\geq-\mu^{n}\geq\cdots\geq-\mu^{1}
,

(ii)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0\Rightarrow(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{j})>0
for all
j=1,\ldots,k-1
and
(\Lambda,\delta_{k}-\delta_{n})=0
.

(iii)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})\neq 0
for all
k=1,\ldots n

\Rightarrow

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})>0
for
k=1,\ldots,n
.

b)

If
p,q\neq 0
and
\omega=\omega_{(-,+)}
, then
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module, and

(i)

\lambda^{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m}\geq-\mu^{n}\geq\cdots\geq-\mu^{1}\geq\lambda^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{p}
,

(ii)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{i}+\delta_{n})=0
for
p+1\leq i\leq m
implies
(\Lambda,\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{m})=0
,

(iii)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for
1\leq i\leq p
implies
(\Lambda,\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i})=0
.

(iv)

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})\neq 0
for
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
, then
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})<0
for
i=1,\ldots,i_{0}
.

(v)

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})\neq 0
for
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
, then
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})<0
for
j=0,\ldots,j_{0}
.

Proof.

The proofs of a), and b) are analogous; for simplicity, we restrict to a).
Let
v_{\Lambda}
be the highest weight vector of
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then
v_{\Lambda}
generates, in particular, a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module of highest weight
\Lambda
. For an odd root
\alpha
, let
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}
be the associated root space of superdimension
(0|1)
. If
e_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}
is a root vector corresponding to
\alpha
, we have
\omega(e_{\alpha})=e_{-\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\alpha}
since
\omega=\omega_{+}
.

Fix an odd positive root
\alpha\coloneqq\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j}\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. If
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the positive-definite Hermitian form on
{\mathcal{H}}
, we have by positive-definiteness

0\leq\langle e_{-\alpha}v_{\Lambda},e_{-\alpha}v_{\Lambda}\rangle=\langle v_{\Lambda},\omega(e_{-\alpha})e_{-\alpha}v_{\Lambda}\rangle=\Lambda([\omega(e_{-\alpha}),e_{-\alpha}])\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle=(\lambda^{i}+\mu^{j})\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle.

Since
\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle>0
, it follows that
\lambda^{i}\geq-\mu^{j}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n
. Moreover, since
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, it is dominant integral,
i.e.
,
\lambda^{i}-\lambda^{i+1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
\mu^{i}-\mu^{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}.

Thus,

\lambda^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m}\geq-\mu^{n}\geq\cdots\geq-\mu^{1}.

Assume now that
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0
for some
1\leq k\leq n
. Then
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{l})>0
for all
1\leq l\leq k-1
. Set

v_{k-1}\coloneqq e_{-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{1}}\cdots e_{-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{k-1}}v_{\Lambda},

and note that

\displaystyle\langle v_{k-1},v_{k-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=(\Lambda-\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}(\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{i}),\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k-1})\langle v_{k-2},v_{k-2}\rangle=(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k-1})\langle v_{k-2},v_{k-2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\ldots=\prod_{l=1}^{k-1}(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{l})\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle>0,

where we used
(\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{l})=-l+1
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{l})>0
for all
1\leq l\leq k
.

By positive-definiteness, one has

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\langle e_{-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n}}v_{k-1},e_{-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n}}v_{k-1}\rangle=(\Lambda-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{i}),\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{n})\langle v_{k-1},v_{k-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=[(\Lambda,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})-k+1+(\Lambda,\delta_{k}-\delta_{n})]\langle v_{k-1},v_{k-1}\rangle=[(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})+(\Lambda,\delta_{k}-\delta_{n})]\langle v_{k-1},v_{k-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=(\Lambda,\delta_{k}-\delta_{n})\langle v_{k-1},v_{k-1}\rangle.

Since
(\Lambda,\delta_{k}-\delta_{n})=-\mu^{k}+\mu^{n}\leq 0
and
\langle v_{k-1},v_{k-1}\rangle>0
, we conclude that
(\Lambda,\delta_{k}-\delta_{n})=0
.

Finally, a similar line of reasoning yields, in the case
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})\neq 0
for all
k=1,\ldots,n
, that

\langle v_{k},v_{k}\rangle=\prod_{l=1}^{k}(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{l})\,\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle>0,\qquad k=1,\ldots,n.

Inductively, starting from
\langle v_{1},v_{1}\rangle>0
, we conclude that
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})>0
for all
k=1,\ldots,n
. This establishes (iii) and completes the proof.
∎

The notion of unitarity, and in particular the characterization of the full set of unitarizable supermodules, is closely connected to the (algebraic) Dirac operator. This operator was introduced in
[
14
,
15
]
, and its connection to unitarity was studied in
[
24
]
. Before outlining the main ideas, we recall an explicit realization of simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules as quotients of Verma supermodules, a construction that will be useful for the subsequent classification.

3.2.
Intermezzo: Highest Weight Supermodules

We study the structure of (unitarizable) highest weight supermodules over
{\mathfrak{g}}
and identify them as Harish-Chandra supermodules.
We also introduce the Kac–Shapovalov form and record the restriction properties of the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents needed later.

3.2.1.
Unitarizable Highest Weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-Modules

Any unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda
decomposes as a direct sum of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules. Hence
\Lambda
is itself the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. The structure of these supermodules is as follows. One has the decomposition

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}={\mathfrak{L}}\oplus{\mathfrak{R}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
,
with
{\mathfrak{L}}={\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
and
{\mathfrak{R}}={\mathfrak{su}}(n)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
.
We denote the associated root systems of
({\mathfrak{L}},{\mathfrak{h}}|_{{\mathfrak{L}}})
and
({\mathfrak{R}},{\mathfrak{h}}|_{{\mathfrak{R}}})
by
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{L}}}
and
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{R}}}
, respectively. In particular,
\Delta_{\bar{0}}=\Delta_{{\mathfrak{L}}}\sqcup\Delta_{{\mathfrak{R}}}
. The positive systems are
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{L}}}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}\cap\Delta_{{\mathfrak{L}}}
and
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{R}}}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}\cap\Delta_{{\mathfrak{R}}}
. The highest weight of a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is of the form
\nu=(\nu^{{\mathfrak{L}}}|\nu^{{\mathfrak{R}}})
, where, in standard coordinates,
\nu^{{\mathfrak{L}}}=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m})
and
\nu^{{\mathfrak{R}}}=(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n})
. Here
\nu^{{\mathfrak{L}}}
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{L}}
-module, while
\nu^{{\mathfrak{R}}}
is the highest weight of a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{R}}
-module. In particular, one obtains the following description.

Lemma 7
.

Any unitarizable simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module
L_{0}(\mu)
is given by the outer tensor product of unitarizable simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-,
{\mathfrak{su}}(n)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-, and
{\mathfrak{u}}(1)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules, respectively,
i.e.
,

L_{0}(\mu)\cong L_{0}(\mu^{{\mathfrak{L}}};{\mathfrak{L}})\boxtimes L_{0}(\mu^{{\mathfrak{R}}};{\mathfrak{R}})\boxtimes{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu}.

All such modules will be listed in Section
4
, providing a parametrization of the highest weights of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules.

A characteristic property of unitarizable highest weight supermodules is that they belong to the class of
Harish-Chandra modules
. We begin by recalling the definition.

Set
{\mathfrak{k}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{su}}(p)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(q)\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)
. Then
{\mathfrak{k}}
is a maximal compact subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n)_{\bar{0}}
, with
{\mathfrak{k}}={\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n)_{\bar{0}}
if
p=0
or
q=0
. One has the
equal rank condition

(3.1)

{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}},

where
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
denotes the complexification of
{\mathfrak{k}}
. The root system
\Delta_{c}
associated with
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
is

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{c}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}):1\leq i<j\leq p,\ p+1\leq i<j\leq m\}\cup\{\pm(\delta_{i}-\delta_{j}):1\leq i<j\leq n\},

which forms a subset of
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
. Thus, a root
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}
is termed
compact
if
\alpha\in\Delta_{c}
, or equivalently, if the corresponding root vector belongs to
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
; otherwise, it is called
non-compact
.
The Weyl group associated with
\Delta_{c}
will be denoted by
W_{c}
, which is indeed a subgroup of
W
.
The set
\Delta_{n}\coloneqq\Delta_{\bar{0}}\setminus\Delta_{c}
will be called the
set of non-compact roots
, so that we have the decomposition:

(3.3)

\Delta_{\bar{0}}=\Delta_{c}\sqcup\Delta_{n}.

Moreover, we define the
set of positive compact
and
positive non-compact roots
by
\Delta_{c,n}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta^{+}\cap\Delta_{c,n}
. The associated Weyl vectors are given by
\rho_{c,n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{c,n}^{+}}\alpha
, and with respect to
\Delta_{c}^{+}
, an element
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
is called
\Delta_{c}^{+}
-dominant integral
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(3.4)

\begin{cases}(\lambda+\rho_{c},\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\ &\text{for all}\ \alpha\in\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq p\ \text{or}\ p+1\leq i<j\leq m\},\\
(\lambda+\rho_{c},\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}\ &\text{for all}\ \alpha\in\{\delta_{i}-\delta_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq n\}.\end{cases}

The dominant integral weights are in one-to-one correspondence with simple
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules.

By the equal rank condition (
3.1
), every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module is a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module. A
({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-module is called a
Harish-Chandra module
if it is finitely generated and locally finite as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module.

Lemma 8

(
[
22
, Lemma IX.3.10]
)
.

Any unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module is a Harish-Chandra module.

3.2.2.
Highest Weight Supermodules

Let
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
be the Borel subalgebra corresponding to a positive system
\Delta^{+}
of Section
2.2
.
The enveloping superalgebra
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is viewed as a right
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}})
-supermodule by right multiplication.
For
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, let
{\mathbb{C}}_{\Lambda}
denote the one-dimensional
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}})
-supermodule of weight
\Lambda
,
with trivial action of
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
.
The
Verma supermodule
of highest weight
\Lambda
is then defined by

(3.5)

M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)={\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}})}{\mathbb{C}}_{\Lambda}.

The Verma supermodule
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule generated by the vector
[1\otimes 1]
of weight
\Lambda
, with
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)={\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})[1\otimes 1]
. For any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
and any
{\mathfrak{b}}
-eigenvector
v_{\Lambda}\in M
of weight
\Lambda
, there exists a unique surjective morphism
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)\to M
sending
[1\otimes 1]
to
v_{\Lambda}
. The module
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
admits a unique maximal subsupermodule, hence a unique simple quotient
L(\Lambda)
; it is therefore indecomposable
[
19
, Chap. 8]
.
If
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module,
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is an example of a Harish-Chandra supermodule (
cf.

[
2
]
), that is,
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is finitely generated and locally finite as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-supermodule. If
{\mathfrak{b}}
is fixed, or is clear from the context, we omit the superscript in
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
and
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
.

The definition of the Verma supermodule depends on the choice of a positive system. In Section
2.2
we introduced two positive systems (
cf.
(
2.21
)) relevant for unitarity, namely the standard one
\Delta^{+}_{{\operatorname{st}}}
and the non-standard one
\Delta^{+}_{{\operatorname{nst}}}
, which share the same even part
\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}
fixed in (
2.5
). We fix
\Delta^{+}_{{\operatorname{st}}}
in the case
p=0
or
q=0
, and
\Delta^{+}_{{\operatorname{nst}}}
in the case
p,q\neq 0
. The corresponding Borel subalgebras are denoted
{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\operatorname{st}}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\operatorname{nst}}}
. The two systems are connected by a sequence of odd reflections defined in (
2.13
). Under these reflections one has
[
3
, Lemma 1.40]

(3.6)

M^{{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\operatorname{st}}}}(\Lambda)\;\cong\;M^{{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\operatorname{nst}}}}(\Lambda^{\prime}),\qquad L^{{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\operatorname{st}}}}(\Lambda)\;\cong\;L^{{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\operatorname{nst}}}}(\Lambda^{\prime}),

up to parity, where the highest weights
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
are related by the odd reflection operations. The superscript indicates the choice of Borel subalgebra. The modules
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\operatorname{st}}}}(\Lambda)
and
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}_{{\operatorname{nst}}}}(\Lambda^{\prime})
have the same
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents. Moreover,
\Lambda^{\prime}
is typical if and only if
\Lambda
is typical (
cf.

[
19
, Cor. 9.3.5]
).
The choice of
\Delta_{{\operatorname{st}}}^{+}
is convenient, since it yields a natural
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
{\mathfrak{g}}
compatible with the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading. By contrast,
\Delta_{{\operatorname{nst}}}^{+}
is better suited to unitarity and infinite-dimensional supermodules,
i.e.
, to the real forms
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n)
with
p,q\neq 0
. Accordingly, we work with
\Delta_{{\operatorname{nst}}}^{+}
whenever
p,q\neq 0
; the classification for
\Delta_{{\operatorname{st}}}^{+}
follows by a sequence of odd reflections.

For the standard system
\Delta_{{\operatorname{st}}}^{+}
it is a classical result of Kac
[
17
]
that
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is simple if and only if
2(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)\neq m(\alpha,\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1},{\operatorname{st}}}
and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. A weight
\Lambda
satisfying this condition for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
is called
typical
; otherwise it is called
atypical
. Using (
3.6
), together with the fact that
\Lambda
is typical with respect to
\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1},{\operatorname{st}}}
if and only if
\Lambda^{\prime}
is typical with respect to
\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1},{\operatorname{nst}}}
, one obtains the analogous statement for both positive systems.

Theorem 9
.

Fix either the standard or the non-standard positive system. Then the Verma supermodule
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is simple if and only if

2(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)\neq m(\alpha,\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.

We are concerned with the structure of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
, or more precisely with that of its unique simple quotient, viewed as
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules neglecting parity.
To this end, we recall a Jordan–Hölder
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration due to Musson
[
19
, Chap. 10]
. Let
\Gamma
denote the set of sums of distinct odd positive roots. For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, let
p(\gamma)
be the number of distinct partitions of
\gamma
into odd positive roots. For a subset
S\subseteq\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, set
\Gamma_{S}=\sum_{\gamma\in S}\gamma
and

X_{-S}\coloneqq\prod_{\alpha\in S}X_{-\alpha},

where
X_{-\alpha}
is the root vector corresponding to
-\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{-}
, with
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{-}\coloneqq-\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. Choose an ordering
S_{1},\dots,S_{N}
of the subsets of
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
such that
\Gamma_{S_{i}}<\Gamma_{S_{j}}
implies
i<j
. Define

(3.7)

M_{k}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq k}{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{-})\,X_{-S_{j}}[1\otimes 1].

By the PBW theorem this sum is direct. A direct computation shows that the image of
X_{-S_{i+1}}[1\otimes 1]
in
M/M_{i}
is a highest weight vector, and the submodule it generates is isomorphic to
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}}_{0}(\Lambda-\Gamma_{S_{i+1}})
, the Verma (super)module over
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
with respect to
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}
.

Proposition 10

(
[
19
, Theorem 10.4.5]
)
.

The supermodule
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
has a filtration as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module:

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset M_{2}\subset\ldots\subset M_{r}=M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda),

such that each factor
M_{i+1}/M_{i}
is isomorphic to a Verma module
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}}_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
, where
\gamma\in\Gamma
. This module appears with multiplicity
p(\gamma)
in the filtration.

In the same way one obtains a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
, whose simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors are
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
with
\gamma\in\Gamma
(see for instance
[
16
, Theorem 2.5]
). Note that each composition factor is a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Hence, if
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is unitarizable, the complete reducibility of unitarizable supermodules implies that the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration is in fact a direct sum.

Corollary 11
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Then, viewed as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module,
{\mathcal{H}}
decomposes as

{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{r},

where each constituent
{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
is isomorphic to a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module of the form
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Moreover, if
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is finite-dimensional, then it is a direct sum of simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules of the form
L_{0}(\mu)
, since
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}\bigl(L_{0}(\mu),L_{0}(\nu)\bigr)=0
for any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors
L_{0}(\mu)
and
L_{0}(\nu)
occurring in the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
.

Lemma 12
.

Fix either the standard or non-standard positive system. If
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is finite-dimensional, then it decomposes as a finite direct sum of simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules, each of highest weight
\Lambda-\gamma
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

In general, however,
L(\Lambda)
is not semisimple as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module, although each simple composition factor is a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

Lemma 13
.

Fix the non-standard positive system.
Let
\Lambda
be the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.
Then every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor
L_{0}(\mu)
appearing in the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration
of
L(\Lambda)
is a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

Proof.

Any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor is of the form

L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
, where
\gamma
is a sum of distinct odd positive roots.
Let
L_{0}(\nu)
be such a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor, and suppose that

L_{0}(\nu)
is a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.
The claim follows if one shows that, for every

\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
such that
L_{0}(\nu-\alpha)

occurs as a composition factor, the module
L_{0}(\nu-\alpha)

is itself a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

Assume that
L_{0}(\nu-\alpha)
occurs as a composition factor.
Since
\nu
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module,
we may write, in standard coordinates,

\nu=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m}|\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n}),

satisfying

\lambda^{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m}\geq\lambda^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{p},\qquad\mu^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu^{n}.

For
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, one has either

\alpha=\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k}
or
\alpha=-\epsilon_{j}+\delta_{l}
,
where
1\leq i\leq p<j\leq m
and
1\leq k,l\leq n
.
Consequently,
\nu-\alpha
is
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-dominant integral, since
\nu
is. Moreover, by the form of
\alpha
, it satisfies the same inequalities and changes the parameter
\lambda^{1}-\lambda^{m}
by
0
or
-1
.
Hence, according to the classification of unitarizable
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)
-modules
(
cf.
(
4.28
)),
L_{0}(\nu-\alpha)
is the highest weight of a unitarizable

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.
∎

We finally state a non-existence result for certain
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors in the atypical case. For this purpose, we consider the supermodule
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
. We first require the following lemma.

Lemma 14
.

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0
for some odd positive root
\alpha
, then there exists an embedding
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda-\alpha)\subset M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
.

For the proof, we refer to
[
6
, Lemma 10.3]
in the case of the standard positive system, and to
[
20
, Theorem 3.7]
in the non-standard case, where an explicit construction of the Shapovalov elements is given for all Borel subalgebras obtained from the standard one by a sequence of odd reflections. For weights
\Lambda
satisfying the unitarity conditions of Lemma
6
, this yields the following important implication.

Lemma 15
.

Assume
\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions. Then the following holds:

a)

Assume
p=0
or
q=0
and
\Lambda=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m}|\mu^{1},\ldots\mu^{k_{0}-1},\mu,\ldots\mu)
with
\mu^{k_{0}-1}\neq\mu
. If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k})=0
for some
k_{0}\leq k\leq n
, then any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor of
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
has a decomposition of the form
\Lambda-\gamma
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\ldots+\gamma_{r}
and none of the
\gamma_{s}
is of the form
\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{l}
for
l=k,\ldots,n
.

b)

Assume
p,q\neq 0
and
\Lambda=(\lambda,\ldots,\lambda,\lambda^{i_{0}+1},\ldots,\lambda^{p},\lambda^{p+1},\ldots,\lambda^{m-j_{0}-1},\lambda^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}|\mu^{1},\ldots\mu^{n})
with
\lambda^{i_{0}+1}\neq\lambda
and
\lambda^{m-j_{0}-1}\neq\lambda^{\prime}
.

(i)

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k})=0
for some
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
, then any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor of
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
has a decomposition of the form
\Lambda-\gamma
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\ldots+\gamma_{r}
and none of the
\gamma_{s}
is of the form
\epsilon_{i^{\prime}}-\delta_{k}
for
i^{\prime}=1,\ldots,i
.

(ii)

If
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{k})=0
for some
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
, any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor of
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
has a decomposition of the form
\Lambda-\gamma
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\ldots+\gamma_{r}
and none of the
\gamma_{s}
is of the form
-\epsilon_{m-j^{\prime}}+\delta_{k}
for
j^{\prime}=0,\ldots,j
.

Proof.

We prove only a), since b) is analogous. Consider first
\alpha\coloneqq\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k}
. By the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda-\alpha)
, its composition factors have highest weights of the form
\Lambda-\alpha-\gamma
, where
\alpha+\gamma
is a sum of pairwise distinct positive odd roots. Since
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0
, Lemma
14
yields an embedding
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda-\alpha)\subset M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
. Thus
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda-\alpha)
is contained in the Shapovalov radical of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
. Consequently, every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor of highest weight
\Lambda-\alpha-\gamma
arising from a decomposition involving
\alpha
is already contained in this radical. Moreover, the multiplicity of such a factor in
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda-\alpha)
is exactly the number of distinct odd partitions of
\alpha+\gamma
involving
\alpha
.

Next, fix
\beta\coloneqq\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{l}
for
k<l\leq n
. We consider the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors of
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
involving
\beta
. Every such factor is of the form
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
, where
\gamma=\beta+\tilde{\gamma}
is a sum of pairwise distinct positive odd roots. Since
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda-\alpha)\subset M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
, we may assume that no odd root occurring in
\tilde{\gamma}
equals
\alpha
. We claim that none of these factors occurs in
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
.

Set
\eta\coloneqq\delta_{k}-\delta_{l}
for
k<l\leq n
. Then
r_{\eta}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
by assumption, hence
r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda=\Lambda-\eta
. By Verma’s theorem, there is an embedding
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda)\subset M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
. By the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration, the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda)
are exactly the modules
M_{0}(r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda-\nu)
, where
\nu
is a sum of pairwise distinct positive odd roots. Since
r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda=\Lambda-\eta
and
(\Lambda-\eta)-\alpha=\Lambda-\beta
, every factor
M_{0}(r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda-\nu)
for which some decomposition of
\nu
involves
\alpha
is of the form
M_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
, where some decomposition of
\gamma
involves
\beta
. Indeed, if
\nu=\alpha+\nu_{1}
, then

r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda-\nu=(r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda-\alpha)-\nu_{1}=(\Lambda-\beta)-\nu_{1}=\Lambda-(\beta+\nu_{1}).

In
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
, let
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
be a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor with
\gamma=\beta+\tilde{\gamma}
. By the preceding argument, we may assume that no decomposition of
\gamma
involves
\alpha
. In particular,
\nu\coloneqq\alpha+\tilde{\gamma}
is a sum of pairwise distinct positive odd roots. Hence
M_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
occurs as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda)
. Therefore
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda)
contains every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor of the form
M_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
for which some decomposition of
\gamma
involves
\beta
. Since
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(r_{\eta}\cdot\Lambda)\subset M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is a proper submodule, all
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-factors arising from it lie in the Shapovalov radical of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
. Consequently,
M_{0}(\Lambda-\beta)
, and more generally every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-factor arising from a decomposition involving
\beta
, does not survive in the irreducible quotient
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
.
∎

3.2.3.
Shapovalov Form

Every highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is the unique simple quotient of a Verma supermodule. Verma supermodules carry a natural
\omega
-contravariant Hermitian form, the
Shapovalov form
. A highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is unitarizable precisely when the induced form on its simple quotient is positive definite. We now define the
Shapovalov form
.

To introduce the Shapovalov form we recall the notion of infinitesimal characters. An
infinitesimal character
is an algebra homomorphism
\chi:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
, obtained from the Harish-Chandra homomorphism. Concretely, one has the decomposition of super vector spaces

(3.8)

{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})\oplus\bigl({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})+{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}){\mathfrak{n}}^{+}\bigr),

which is a direct consequence of the PBW theorem for
{\mathfrak{g}}
. This decomposition is preserved by
\omega
, extended to
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. The projection
p:{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})
is the
Harish-Chandra projection
; its restriction to
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
defines an algebra homomorphism

(3.9)

p|_{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})\cong S({\mathfrak{h}}).

The
Harish-Chandra homomorphism

{\operatorname{HC}}:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to S({\mathfrak{h}})
is defined as the composition of
p|_{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}
with the automorphism
\zeta:S({\mathfrak{h}})\to S({\mathfrak{h}})
given by

(3.10)

\lambda(\zeta(f))=(\lambda-\rho)(f)\qquad\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\;f\in S({\mathfrak{h}}),

where
\rho
is the Weyl vector of the chosen system
\Delta^{+}
. For
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, the map

(3.11)

\chi_{\Lambda}(z)=(\Lambda+\rho)({\operatorname{HC}}(z)),

defines an algebra homomorphism
\chi_{\Lambda}:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
.

A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is said to admit an
infinitesimal character
if there exists
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
such that every
z\in\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
acts on
M
as the scalar
\chi_{\Lambda}(z)
. The map
\chi_{\Lambda}
is then the
infinitesimal character
of
M
. Highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules provide particular examples of supermodules with an infinitesimal character.

A Verma supermodule carries a contravariant Hermitian form whenever its highest weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
satisfies

\Lambda(\omega(H))=\overline{\Lambda(H)}
for any
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
. Such a weight is called
symmetric
. Every highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is symmetric (see
[
24
]
).

Let
\chi_{\Lambda}:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the infinitesimal character of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
. If
\Lambda
is symmetric, define

(3.12)

F(X,Y)\coloneqq\chi_{\Lambda}(\operatorname{p}(X\,\omega(Y))),\qquad X,Y\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}),

where
p:{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})
is the Harish-Chandra projection from above. Recall
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)={\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})[1\otimes 1]
. Then

(3.13)

\langle X[1\otimes 1],\,Y[1\otimes 1]\rangle\coloneqq F(X,Y),\qquad X,Y\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}),

defines an
\omega
-contravariant Hermitian form on
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
, denoted
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
\omega
-contravariance means

\langle Xv,w\rangle=\langle v,\omega(X)w\rangle
for
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
v,w\in M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda).
This form is called the
Shapovalov form
on
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
.
The form is unique up to scalar multiples and satisfies

(3.14)

\langle M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)^{\mu},M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)^{\nu}\rangle=0\quad\text{unless}\quad\mu=\nu.

The Shapovalov form induces on
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
a non-degenerate
\omega
-contravariant form, denoted again by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. We call the induced form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
again
Shapovalov form
.

We use the Shapovalov form as a tool to test the existence of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents in the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
L^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
, when
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module,
i.e.
,
\Lambda
is symmetric.

Lemma 16

(
[
19
, Chap. 8]
)
.

Suppose
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
is symmetric. Then the radical of the Shapovalov form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
is the largest proper subsupermodule of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
. For
Y\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
the following are equivalent:

a)

Y[1\otimes 1]
lies in a proper submodule of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
.

b)

F(X,Y)=0
for all
X\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

We consider the equation
F(X,Y)=0
.
By (
3.14
), it suffices to take
X\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})^{\eta}
and
Y\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})^{-\eta}
, where
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{\pm})
is decomposed under the action of
{\mathfrak{h}}
,
i.e.
,

(3.15)

{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})=\bigoplus_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}}{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})^{-\eta},\qquad{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})=\bigoplus_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}}{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})^{\eta}.

Let
F_{\eta}
be the restriction of
F
to the weight space
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})^{-\eta}
.
Since this space is finite-dimensional,
F_{\eta}
may be represented by a square matrix, and its determinant can be considered.
If this determinant is nonzero, no nonzero vector belongs to the radical of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and consequently no element
Y[1\otimes 1]
for
Y\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})^{-\eta}
belongs to a proper submodule of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
.

The determinant is given by an explicit formula, the
Kac–Shapovalov determinant formula
.
Denote by
P(\gamma)
the
Kostant partition function
,
i.e.
, the number of decompositions

\gamma=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}

into positive roots, subject to the condition that if
\gamma_{i}\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, then
\gamma_{j}\neq\gamma_{i}
for all
j\neq i
.
Denote by
P_{\gamma}(\gamma)
the number of partitions of
\gamma
in which
\gamma
itself does not occur.

Theorem 17

(
[
18
,
13
]
)
.

Up to a nonzero constant factor,
\det F_{\eta}=D_{1}D_{2}
, where

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}}\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}(h_{\gamma}+(\rho,\gamma)-r)^{P(\eta-r\gamma)},

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}(h_{\gamma}+(\rho,\gamma))^{P_{\gamma}(\eta-\gamma)}.

3.3.
The Relative Dirac Operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}

We briefly construct the relative (quadratic) Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
, following
[
14
,
29
]
.
We emphasize, however, that it arises naturally from the quantum Weil superalgebra; see
[
24
,
26
]
.

Since
(\cdot,\cdot)
is non-degenerate if and only if
m\neq n
, we distinguish the cases
m\neq n
and
m=n
, and begin with
m\neq n
.
Fix
B(\cdot,\cdot)\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
; this normalization will be convenient later. Its restriction to
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
is
symplectic. We then fix two complementary Lagrangian subspaces of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}

with bases
\{\partial_{i}\}
and
\{x_{i}\}
, for
1\leq i\leq mn
, such that

B(\partial_{i},x_{j})=-B(x_{j},\partial_{i})=\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}
and
\omega(x_{i})=-\partial_{i}
. Here,
\omega=\omega_{(-,+)}
in the infinite-dimensional case and
\omega_{+}
in the finite-dimensional case. This choice is compatible with our fixed
\Delta^{+}
(
cf.
Section
2.2
) in the sense that the basis
\{\partial_{i}\}
spans the odd positive roots
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{+}
and the basis
\{x_{i}\}
spans the odd negative roots
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}
. Let
T({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
denote the
tensor algebra
of the vector space
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
, regarded as concentrated in even degree. The
Weyl algebra
is then defined as the quotient

\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=T({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})/I
,
where
I
is the two-sided ideal generated by the relations

v\otimes w-w\otimes v-2\,B(v,w)
for all
v,w\in{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
.
Equivalently,
\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
can be realized as the algebra of differential operators with
polynomial coefficients in the variables
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{mn}
, under the identification of the
generators
\partial_{i}
with the partial derivatives
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
for

i=1,\dotsc,mn
. We equip
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
with the canonical Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{W}}
.

Using the relations
[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathcal{W}}=0
,
[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]_{\mathcal{W}}=0
, and

[\partial_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathcal{W}}=\delta_{ij}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq mn
, one checks that
the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
embeds as a subalgebra of
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
. The corresponding Lie algebra
homomorphism
\alpha:{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\to{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
is given explicitly in
[
29
, Equation 2]
:

(3.16)

\alpha(X)=\sum_{k,j=1}^{mn}(B(X,[\partial_{k},\partial_{j}])x_{k}x_{j}+B(X,[x_{k},x_{j}])\partial_{k}\partial_{j})\\
-\sum_{k,j=1}^{mn}2B(X,[x_{k},\partial_{j}])x_{j}\partial_{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{mn}B(X,[\partial_{l},x_{l}]).

Let
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
and
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
denote the quadratic Casimir operators of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, respectively.
We write
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},\Delta}
for the image of
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
under the diagonal embedding

(3.17)

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}),\qquad X\mapsto X\otimes 1+1\otimes\alpha(X).

The
Dirac element

\operatorname{D}
for
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined as the odd element

(3.18)

\operatorname{D}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}\;=\;2\sum_{i=1}^{mn}\bigl(\partial_{i}\otimes x_{i}-x_{i}\otimes\partial_{i}\bigr)\;\in\;{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}).

It is independent of the choice of basis of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
and is
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
-invariant with respect to the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-action on

{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
induced by the adjoint action on both factors

[
15
, Lemma 10.2.1]
; that is,
[{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},\operatorname{D}]=0
.
In analogy with the case of reductive Lie algebras, the Dirac element admits a particularly nice square.

Proposition 18

(
[
15
, Proposition 10.2.2]
)
.

The Dirac element
\operatorname{D}\in\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
satisfies

\operatorname{D}^{2}=-\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},\Delta}-C,

where
C
is a constant that equals
1/8
of the trace of
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
.

Any Dirac element
\operatorname{D}
may be realized as a Dirac operator. For a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
, it acts componentwise on

M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, where
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{mn}]
is the oscillator module for
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
introduced below. We refer to
this operator as the
Dirac operator
and denote it by the same symbol
\operatorname{D}
.

Finally, consider the case
m=n
. We then work in
{\mathfrak{gl}}(n|n)
, since its supertrace form is non-degenerate, and define the corresponding
Dirac element
\operatorname{D}
. As we are mainly interested in highest weight supermodules (
cf.
Theorem
5
) and every
highest weight
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|n)
-supermodule is the restriction of a highest weight
{\mathfrak{gl}}(n|n)
-supermodule, the same element

\operatorname{D}
acts on
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|n)
-supermodules via restriction. We refer to the resulting operator as the Dirac operator
for
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|n)
, and we denote it by the same symbol.

3.4.
Dirac Operators and Unitarity

The simple
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
-module
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
carries a unique Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, known as the
Bargmann–Fock Hermitian form
or
Fischer–Fock Hermitian form

[
1
,
9
,
10
]
, such that
\partial_{k}
and
x_{k}
are adjoint to each other and the following orthogonality relations hold:

(3.19)

\begin{split}\langle\prod_{k=1}^{mn}x_{k}^{p_{k}},\prod_{k=1}^{mn}x_{k}^{q_{k}}\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\begin{cases}\prod_{k=1}^{mn}p_{k}!\qquad&\text{if}\ p_{k}=q_{k}\ \text{for all}\ k,\\
0\qquad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{split}

If
\bigl({\mathcal{H}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)
is a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, we equip the
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
with the positive definite Hermitian form

(3.20)

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}\otimes\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.

Up to multiplication by a real scalar,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
is the unique Hermitian form that is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-anti-contravariant in the first factor, and satisfies

x_{i}^{\dagger}=\partial_{i}
in the second. For our choice of positive system, this is compatible with
the extension of the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega
to
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, which implies
that the Dirac operator is
self-adjoint
on
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. We arrive at the following
Dirac inequality
:

(3.21)

\langle v,\operatorname{D}^{2}v\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\geq 0

for all
v\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. The Dirac inequality, together with Proposition
18
, provides a complete characterization of unitarity in terms of the highest weights of the simple composition factors in the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
M
described in Section
3.2.2
.

Theorem 19
.

Let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
.
Then
M
is unitarizable if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

a)

\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

b)

If
L_{0}(\mu)
is a simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor in the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
M
with highest weight
\mu
, then

\displaystyle(\mu+2\rho,\mu)\begin{cases}>(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)\qquad&p,q\neq 0,\\
<(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)\qquad&p=0\ \text{or}\ q=0.\end{cases}

Theorem
19
is the basis of the classification of unitarity. The crucial point is the system of inequalities imposed by the weights
\Lambda-\alpha
with
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. A direct computation yields the following lemma.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\mu=\Lambda-\alpha
with
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. Then

(\mu+2\rho,\mu)\geq(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)\leq 0.

As a first consequence, we obtain the following proposition, which will be our main tool for proving unitarity.

Proposition 21
.

Let
M
be a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, and assume that
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. Suppose that for every highest weight
\mu=\Lambda-\gamma
of a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor of
M
, one of the following holds:

a)

p=0
or
q=0
,
M
is finite-dimensional, and
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{k}
with
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{i})>0
for all
i=1,\dots,k
;

b)

p,q\neq 0
,
M
is infinite-dimensional, and
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{k}
with
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{i})<0
for all
i=1,\dots,k
.

Then
M
is unitarizable. In particular,
M
is unitarizable if
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)>0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}
in the finite-dimensional case, and if
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)<0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}
in the infinite-dimensional case.

Proof.

We show that for every highest weight
\mu
of any composition factor
L_{0}(\mu)
of
M
the Dirac inequality holds, that is,
(\mu+2\rho,\mu)<(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)
if
M
is finite-dimensional and
(\mu+2\rho,\mu)>(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)
if
M
is infinite-dimensional. The proposition then follows from Theorem
19
.

Any highest weight
\mu
is of the form
\mu=\Lambda-\gamma
, where
\gamma
is a sum of pairwise distinct positive odd roots. Fix an arbitrary decomposition

\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{k},\qquad\gamma_{i}\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}.

We call
k
the
length
of
\gamma
. It is unique. By assumption,

(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{i})\begin{cases}>0,&\text{if $M$ is finite-dimensional},\\
<0,&\text{if $M$ is infinite-dimensional},\end{cases}

for all
i
.

We first consider the case where
M
is finite-dimensional.
Here the positive system
\Delta^{+}
is the standard one
\Delta_{\bar{1},\text{st}}^{+}
. The values
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
arrange as follows:

\begin{array}[]{cccccccccc}(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})&>&\ldots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{k})&>&\ldots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{n})\\
\vee&&&&\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{1})&>&\ldots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})&>&\ldots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{n})\end{array}

For any
\mu=\Lambda-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}
, we compute

(3.22)

\displaystyle(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)-(\mu+2\rho,\mu)

\displaystyle=2(\Lambda+\rho,\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i})-\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\gamma_{j}\bigr).

Note that
(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})\in\{-1,0,1\}
. Interpreting the diagram above as a matrix, we have, for

i\neq j
, that
(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})=1
if
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{j}
lie in the same row,
(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})=-1
if they lie in
the same column, and
(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})=0
otherwise. By assumption
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{i})>0
for all
i
. In view of (
3.22
), it therefore suffices to consider
subsets
\{\gamma_{i}\}
lying in a single row.

Fix such a subset. Assume the subset has cardinality
k^{\prime}
and denote the elements for clarity by
\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\gamma_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}
. Without loss of generality, order the
\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}
so that

(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma^{\prime}_{i})<(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma^{\prime}_{j})\;\Rightarrow\;i<j.

This is possible by the diagram above. Set
\xi\coloneqq(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma^{\prime}_{1})
with
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}\coloneqq\epsilon_{r}-\delta_{s}
. Since
\xi
is minimal, the only roots
\epsilon_{r}-\delta_{j}
that may appear among the
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}
are those with
j\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}
.

Assume that
\epsilon_{r}-\delta_{s_{1}},\ldots,\epsilon_{r}-\delta_{s_{l}}
are pairwise distinct elements of
\{\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime}_{k}\}
for some
l\leq k^{\prime}
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}
.
Then

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{r}-\delta_{s_{i}})-\xi=(\Lambda+\rho,\delta_{s}-\delta_{s_{i}})=\mu^{s_{i}}-\mu^{s}+s-s_{i}\geq 1.

Altogether, we obtain for the single row we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle 2(\Lambda+\rho,\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}_{i})-\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}_{j}\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq 2(\Lambda+\rho,\sum_{i=1}^{l}(\epsilon_{r}-\delta_{s_{i}}))-\begin{cases}0,&l=1,\\
1,&l=2,\\
2l,&\text{else},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\geq 2\xi+2l-\begin{cases}0,&l=1,\\
1,&l=2,\\
2l,&\text{else},\end{cases}>0,

since
\xi>0
.
This completes the proof for the finite-dimensional case.

For the infinite-dimensional case, set
A\coloneqq\{\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k}:1\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq k\leq n\}
and
B\coloneqq\{-\epsilon_{j}+\delta_{k}:p+1\leq j\leq m,\ 1\leq k\leq n\}
. We refine the decomposition of
\gamma
to

\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}\beta_{j},\qquad\alpha_{i}\in A,\quad\beta_{j}\in B,\quad k=k_{1}+k_{2}.

Using
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{j})\in\{0,1\}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k_{1}
and
j=1,\ldots,k_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mu+2\rho,\mu)-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha_{i})-2\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(\Lambda+\rho,\beta_{j})+\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}\beta_{j},\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}\beta_{j}\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq-2\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha_{i})+\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\alpha_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{k_{1}}\alpha_{j}\bigr)-2\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(\Lambda+\rho,\beta_{j})+\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\beta_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}\beta_{j}\bigr),

so it remains to show

-2\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha_{i})+\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\alpha_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{k_{1}}\alpha_{j}\Bigr)>0,\qquad-2\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(\Lambda+\rho,\beta_{j})+\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\beta_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}\beta_{j}\Bigr)>0.

The proof of these inequalities is identical to the finite-dimensional case, where we now work with the inequalities

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{1}-\delta_{n})\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{i}-\delta_{n})\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{p}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{p}-\delta_{n})\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{m}+\delta_{1})\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{1})\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{p+1}+\delta_{n})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{p+1}+\delta_{1})\end{array}

This finishes the proof.
∎

4.
Classifying the Full Set

In this section, we classify the unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, following the method of Section
1.1
. We distinguish two cases:
p,q\neq 0
and
p=0
or
q=0
.
Any unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule decomposes as a finite direct sum of unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules, where

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\simeq{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)^{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)^{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
.
Each
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent is an outer tensor product of unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)
-,
{\mathfrak{su}}(n)
-, and
{\mathfrak{u}}(1)
-modules.
Since
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)
admits nontrivial finite-dimensional unitarizable modules only when
p=0
or
q=0
, it follows that
{\mathfrak{g}}
has no nontrivial unitarizable finite-dimensional supermodules if
p,q\neq 0
.
If
p=0
or
q=0
, then every simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module is finite-dimensional, and hence every unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is finite-dimensional.
In particular, if
p,q\neq 0
, then every nontrivial unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is necessarily infinite-dimensional.

4.1.
Finite-Dimensional Unitarizable Supermodules

Assume that
p=0
or
q=0
and fix the standard positive system. We begin by parametrizing the possible highest weights of unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. Since any such highest weight is, in particular, the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module, the description from Section
3.2.1
applies. On this basis, we formulate the classification method and illustrate it by two explicit examples, namely
{\mathfrak{s}l}(2|1)
and
{\mathfrak{s}l}(2|2)
. The general classification is then obtained by extending the same argument.

4.1.1.
Parametrization of the Weight Space

The finite-dimensional simple supermodules are classified by the dominant integral weights
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, taken with respect to a Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{1}}
determined by a positive system
\Delta^{+}=\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. Recall that
\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}
was fixed in Section
2.1
. There are two natural choices of Borel subalgebras, namely the
distinguished Borel subalgebra

{\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathrm{st}}
and the
anti-distinguished Borel subalgebra

{\mathfrak{b}}_{-\mathrm{st}}
, corresponding respectively to

(4.1)

\Delta_{\bar{1},\mathrm{st}}^{+}=\{\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j}|1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n\},\quad\Delta_{\bar{1},-\mathrm{st}}^{+}=\{-\epsilon_{i}+\delta_{j}|1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n\}.

Recall that under the canonical isomorphism
{\mathfrak{s}l}(m|n)\cong{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|m)
, the anti-distinguished Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}_{-\mathrm{st}}
of
{\mathfrak{s}l}(m|n)
maps to the distinguished one
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathrm{st}}
of
{\mathfrak{s}l}(n|m)
. We work with the distinguished Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathrm{st}}
and hence write
{\mathfrak{b}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathrm{st}}
and
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta_{\bar{1},\mathrm{st}}^{+}
in the sequel.

A weight
\lambda
is
dominant integral
if and only if

(4.2)

(\lambda+\rho_{\bar{0}},\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+},

equivalently, if there exists a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module of highest weight
\lambda
with respect to
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}
. If we denote the highest weight in terms of standard coordinates on
\mathfrak{d}^{*}
by
\Lambda=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m}|\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n})

(modulo shifts by
(1,\ldots,1|{-}1,\ldots,-1)
), this imposes a classical sequence of standard conditions on
\Lambda
, which are

(4.3)

\lambda^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m},\qquad\mu^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu^{n}

and the differences in the two chains have to be integral. We denote the set of such
{\mathfrak{b}}
-dominant integral weights by
P^{++}
, and refer to them as
\Delta^{+}
-dominant integral weights.

For
\lambda\in P^{++}
, we let
L(\lambda)
denote the simple supermodule of highest weight
\lambda
with respect to
{\mathfrak{b}}
, whose highest weight vector is even. With this notation, the simple finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are parameterized by

(4.4)

\{\,L(\lambda),\Pi L(\lambda):\lambda\in P^{++}\,\}.

By definition, finite-dimensional unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are precisely those that are unitarizable with respect to one of the conjugate-linear anti-involutions (
cf.
Lemma
3
)

(4.5)

\omega_{\pm}\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}A&B\\
\hline\cr C&D\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}A^{\dagger}&\pm C^{\dagger}\\
\hline\cr\pm B^{\dagger}&D^{\dagger}\end{array}\right),\qquad\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}A&B\\
\hline\cr C&D\end{array}\right)\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

These conjugate-linear anti-involutions correspond to the compact real form of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, namely

{\mathfrak{su}}(m)^{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)^{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
. In the sequel, we consider only
\omega\coloneqq\omega_{+}
, as the
\omega_{-}
-case is analogous.

We find it convenient to describe
\Lambda\in P^{++}
more explicitly, so that
\Lambda
may be regarded as a one-parameter family in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. Assume that the finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
L(\Lambda)
is
\omega
-unitarizable. Then
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a finite-dimensional unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module
L_{0}(\Lambda)
. Such a module is (isomorphic to) the outer tensor product of simple
{\mathfrak{su}}(m)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-,
{\mathfrak{su}}(n)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-, and
\mathfrak{u}(1)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules. The simple
{\mathfrak{su}}(m)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules have highest weights of the form

(4.6)

(-a_{1},-a_{2},\ldots,-a_{m}\,|\,0,\ldots,0),

with
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
0=-a_{1}\geq-a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq-a_{m}
.
Analogously, the simple
{\mathfrak{su}}(n)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules have highest weights of the form

(4.7)

(0,\ldots,0\,|\,b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}),

with
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}=0
.
Since
\mathfrak{u}(1)
is abelian, Schur’s lemma implies that its simple modules are one-dimensional and, by unitarity, uniquely determined by a positive real number. For convenience we write

(4.8)

\Lambda=(0,-a_{2},\ldots,-a_{m}\,|\,b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1},0)+\tfrac{x_{0}}{2}(1,\ldots,1\,|\,1,\ldots,1),\qquad x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}.

To track the nontrivial components among the
a_{i}
and
b_{j}
, we define
i_{0}
as the largest integer such that
a_{i_{0}}=0
, and
k_{0}
as the smallest integer such that
b_{k_{0}}=0
.

In general, a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
L(\Lambda)
with
\Lambda
as in (
4.8
) need not be
\omega
-unitarizable. To classify the
\omega
-unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, we regard any
\Lambda
as a particular value of a one-parameter family in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, indexed by
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, that is

(4.9)

\Lambda(x)=\Lambda_{0}+\tfrac{x}{2}(1,\ldots,1\,|\,1,\ldots,1),

where
\Lambda_{0}=(0,-a_{2},\ldots,-a_{m}\,|\,b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1},0)
with

a_{i},b_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,

0\geq-a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq-a_{m}
, and

b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n-1}\geq 0
as above.
Note that
(\alpha,(1,\ldots,1\,|\,1,\ldots,1))=0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}
. In standard coordinates, we write

(4.10)

\Lambda(x)=(\lambda^{1}(x),\ldots,\lambda^{m}(x)|\mu^{1}(x),\ldots,\mu^{n}(x)),\qquad\mu^{k_{0}}(x)=\cdots=\mu^{n}(x).

Throughout, we assume the unitarity conditions from Lemma
6
:

(i)

\lambda^{1}(x)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m}(x)\geq-\mu^{n}(x)\geq\cdots\geq-\mu^{1}(x)
;

(ii)

if
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0
, then
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{j})>0
for all
j=1,\ldots,k-1
, and
(\Lambda(x),\delta_{k}-\delta_{n})=0
;

(iii)

if
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})\neq 0
for all
k=1,\ldots,n
, then
(\Lambda(x)+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})>0
for all
k=1,\ldots,n
.

In what follows, we write
\Lambda=\Lambda(x)
and suppress the dependence on
x
whenever no confusion can arise. The classification is obtained by applying the method of Section
1.1.1
.

4.1.2.
Examples

We illustrate our classification method by considering two examples: the real Lie superalgebras

{\mathfrak{su}}(2|1)
and
{\mathfrak{su}}(2|2)
.

\underline{\mathfrak{su}(2|1)}
: Set
{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{su}}(2|1)
. The set of positive roots is

\Delta^{+}=\{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1},\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{1}\}
.
In particular,
\rho=(0,-1|1)
. For convenience, set
\alpha\coloneqq\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1}
and
\beta\coloneqq\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{1}
. The family
\Lambda
has general form

(4.11)

\Lambda=\Lambda(x)=\bigl(\tfrac{x}{2},\,-a+\tfrac{x}{2}\big|\tfrac{x}{2}\bigr),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}

for some
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. By Lemma
12
,
L(\Lambda)
decomposes as a direct sum of simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules. The only possibilities are
L_{0}(\Lambda),L_{0}(\Lambda-\alpha)
,
L_{0}(\Lambda-\beta)
, and
L_{0}(\Lambda-\alpha-\beta)
.

The Dirac inequalities for the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents
L_{0}(\Lambda-\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
read (Lemma
20
)

(4.12)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=x+1\Leftrightarrow x\geq-1

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)=x-a\Leftrightarrow x\geq a.

We now obtain a complete classification, parametrized by
x
, following the method of Section
1.1.1
.

We begin by determining
x_{\max}
such that
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable for all
x>x_{\max}
. If
x>a
, then
\Lambda
is typical, and all
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents listed above occur. By (
4.12
), the Dirac inequality is strict on each of them. Hence Proposition
21
implies that
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Therefore
x_{\max}=a
.

We next determine
x_{\min}
. Let
x<a
. Then the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent
L_{0}(\Lambda-\beta)
occurs in
L(\Lambda)
, but the Dirac inequality fails on it. Indeed,
\beta=\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{1}
is a simple root, and the weight space
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)^{\Lambda-\beta}
is one-dimensional, spanned by
e_{-\beta}v_{\Lambda}
where
e_{-\beta}
is the root vector associated with the root
-\beta
. Since
x<a
, this vector does not lie in the radical of the Shapovalov form, because

(4.13)

\langle e_{-\beta}v_{\Lambda},e_{-\beta}v_{\Lambda}\rangle=(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle=(x-a)\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle<0,

where
\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle=1
. Hence
L_{0}(\Lambda-\beta)
occurs in the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
L(\Lambda)
. Since the Dirac inequality does not hold on this constituent,
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable for
x<a
by Theorem
19
. Therefore
x_{\min}=a
.

It remains to study unitarity on
I=[x_{\min},x_{\max}]=\{a\}
. Assume
x=a
. By Theorem
19
, the module
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable if and only if
(\mu+2\rho,\mu)-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)>0
for every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of
L(\Lambda)
. Since
x=a
, one has
(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)=0
, so
\Lambda
is atypical. Hence the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents of highest weights
\Lambda-\beta
and
\Lambda-\alpha-\beta
do not occur; see Lemma
15
. Since
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)>0
,
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable.

Altogether, the full set of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{su}}(1,1|1)
-supermodules is

(4.14)

\{\Lambda=(\tfrac{x}{2},-a+\tfrac{x}{2}|\tfrac{x}{2}):a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\;x\in[a,\infty)\}.

Equivalently,
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule if and only if

a)

\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions;

b)

either
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{1})=0
or
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{1})>0
.

\underline{\vphantom{A}\mathfrak{su}(2|2)}
: Set
{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{su}}(2|2)
. The positive root system is

\Delta^{+}=\{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2},\delta_{1}-\delta_{2},\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1},\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{2},\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{1},\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{2}\}
.
In particular,

\rho=\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{3}{2}\big|\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)
.
The family
\Lambda=\Lambda(x)
has general form

(4.15)

\Lambda=\bigl(\tfrac{x}{2},\,-a+\tfrac{x}{2}\big|b+\tfrac{x}{2},\,\tfrac{x}{2}\bigr),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

with
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. The unitarity conditions are:

(4.16)

\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda^{1}\geq\lambda^{2}\geq-\mu^{2}\geq-\mu^{1},\qquad(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k})=0\Rightarrow(\Lambda,\delta_{k}-\delta_{2})=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k})\neq 0\ \text{for}\ k=1,2\ \Rightarrow\ (\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k})>0\ \text{for}\ k=1,2.\end{array}

Moreover, the Dirac inequalities are

(4.17)

\displaystyle 0\leq(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})

\displaystyle=x+b+1\;\Leftrightarrow\;x\geq-b-1,

\displaystyle 0\leq(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{2})

\displaystyle=x\;\Leftrightarrow\;x\geq 0,

\displaystyle 0\leq(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{1})

\displaystyle=x+b-a\;\Leftrightarrow\;x\geq-b+a,

\displaystyle 0\leq(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{2})

\displaystyle=x-a-1\;\Leftrightarrow\;x\geq a+1.

As in the case of
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
, we now obtain a complete classification parametrized by
x
, following the method of Section
1.1.1
.

We first determine
x_{\max}
. The most restrictive condition is imposed by
\alpha\coloneqq\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{2}
, namely
x\geq a+1
. If
x>a+1
, then
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)>0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. Hence the Dirac inequalities are strict for all
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents, and Proposition
21
implies that
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Therefore
x_{\max}=a+1
.

Next, we identify
x_{\min}
. To treat the cases
b=0
and
b\neq 0
uniformly, we introduce
k_{0}
defined by
k_{0}=1
if
b=0
and
k_{0}=2
if
b\neq 0
. The idea is to consider the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of
L(\Lambda)
that imposes the strongest condition on
x
. If
b\neq 0
, this is
L_{0}(\Lambda-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{2})
, and if
b=0
, this is
L_{0}(\Lambda-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{1})
according to dominance; equivalently, we write
L_{0}(\Lambda-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{k_{0}})
. We show that
L_{0}(\Lambda-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{k_{0}})
exists as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent whenever
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k_{0}})<0
, using the Kac–Shapovalov determinant formula (Theorem
17
). In that case,
L(\Lambda)
cannot be unitarizable, since the Dirac inequality fails on this constituent.

The determinant of the Shapovalov form restricted to
M(\Lambda)^{\Lambda-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{k_{0}}}
is

(4.18)

\left((\Lambda+\rho,\delta_{1}-\delta_{k_{0}})-1\right)\cdot(\Lambda+\rho,\prod_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k}).

The first factor equals
-1
if
k_{0}=1
and
-b-1<0
if
k_{0}=2
, hence never vanishes. The second factor is zero only if
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k})=0
for some
k=1,\ldots,k_{0}
. Since by assumption
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k_{0}})<0
, zeros may occur only for
k<k_{0}
. However, by the unitarity conditions (see (
4.16
)), this would imply
(\Lambda,\delta_{k}-\delta_{n})=0
, contradicting the minimality of
k_{0}
. We conclude that the Kac–Shapovalov determinant does not vanish on
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)^{\Lambda+\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k_{0}}}
, and
L_{0}(\Lambda+\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k_{0}})
cannot lie in the radical. Since the Dirac inequality fails on this constituent,
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable for
x<a+k_{0}-1
. Therefore
x_{\min}=a+k_{0}-1
.

Set
I\coloneqq[x_{\min},x_{\max}]=[a+k_{0}-1,a+1]
. It remains to consider
x\in I
. The integral points of
I
are
\{a+k_{0}-1,a+1\}
, and non-integral points occur precisely when
k_{0}=1
. We first treat this case. Let
k_{0}=1
and
x\in(a,a+1)
. Then
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{j})\neq 0
for
j=1,2
, and

(4.19)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{2})<0<(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{1}),

which contradicts the unitarity conditions for
\Lambda
. Hence
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable.

The integral points of this interval are precisely the points at which
\Lambda
is atypical, that is,

(4.20)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k})=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x=a+k-1,\qquad k=k_{0},\ldots,2.

We treat only the case
k_{0}=2
; the remaining cases are analogous. Then

(4.21)

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{2})\\
\vee&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{1})&>&0=(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{2})\end{array}

and, by Lemma
15
, the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent
L_{0}(\Lambda-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{2})
does not occur in
L(\Lambda)
. Moreover, for any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent with highest weight
\mu=\Lambda-\gamma
one can choose a
decomposition
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{r}
that does not involve the root

\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{2}
. By the diagram above, this implies
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{i})>0
for all

i
, so we are in the situation of the proof of Proposition
21
.
Consequently,
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable for
x=a+1
.

Altogether, the full set of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{su}}(1,1|2)
-supermodules is

(4.22)

\left\{\,\Lambda=\bigl(\tfrac{x}{2},\,-a+\tfrac{x}{2}\big|b+\tfrac{x}{2},\tfrac{x}{2}\bigr):a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\ x\in\{a+k_{0}-1,\ldots,a+1\}\sqcup(a+1,\infty)\,\right\}.

Equivalently,
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule if and only if

a)

\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions;

b)

one of the following holds:

(i)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{k})=0
for some
k=k_{0},\ldots,2
, or

(ii)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{2})>0
.

4.1.3.
Classification

We start with a family of highest weights
\Lambda
of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules of the form

(4.23)

\Lambda=(0,-a_{2},\ldots,-a_{m}|b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1},0)+\tfrac{x}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{m}
and
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}
are non-negative integers. We assume throughout that for each
\Lambda
satisfies for each
x
the unitarity conditions of Lemma
6
. We now apply the method of Section
1.1.1
step by step. The analysis rests on the following computation. The fixed standard system of positive odd roots is

\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}=\{\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j}:1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n\}.

Moreover,

(4.24)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j})=\lambda^{i}+\mu^{j}+m-i-j+1=-a_{i}+b_{j}+x+m-i-j+1,

where
a_{1}=0
and
b_{n}=0
. Recall that
k_{0}
is the smallest index such that
b_{k_{0}}=0
. Together with the form of the highest weight (
4.8
), this immediately gives the following lemma.

Lemma 22
.

The numbers
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)
, with
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, arrange as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{k})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{n})\\
\vee&&&&\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{n})\end{array}

As a direct consequence, one can determine
x_{\max}
, that is, the value such that
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable for all
x>x_{\max}
.

Lemma 23
.

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{n})>0
, equivalently if
x>a_{m}+n-1
, then
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable.

Proof.

By Lemma
22
, the minimum of all
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j})
occurs at
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{n})
,
i.e.
, at
x=a_{m}+n-1
. Hence, if
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{n})>0
, we have
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)>0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, and the statement follows with Proposition
21
.
∎

The lemma yields
x_{\max}=a_{m}+n-1
. On the other hand, the unitarity conditions show that the weakest constraint is imposed by the Dirac inequality for
L_{0}(\Lambda-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{k_{0}})
. This determines
x_{\min}
, namely the largest value such that
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable for all
x<x_{\min}
. The following lemma makes this precise.

Lemma 24
.

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k_{0}})<0
, that is,
x<a_{m}+k_{0}-1
, then
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable.

Proof.

We show that the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent
L_{0}(\Lambda-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{k_{0}})
occurs in
L(\Lambda)
, hence
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable as the Dirac inequality does not hold. It suffices to prove that the weight space
M(\Lambda)^{\Lambda-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{k_{0}}}
does not lie in the radical of the Shapovalov form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
M(\Lambda)
; equivalently, the Kac–Shapovalov determinant on this weight space is nonzero.

Set
\eta\coloneqq\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k_{0}}
. Consider the factor from
\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}
in Theorem
17
,

D_{1}=\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}}\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}(h_{\gamma}+(\rho,\gamma)-r)^{P(\eta-r\gamma)}.

Now
\eta-r\gamma
is a sum of positive roots only if
\gamma=\delta_{k}-\delta_{k_{0}}
and
r=1
with
1\leq k\leq k_{0}
. For these
\gamma
one has
(\Lambda+\rho,\delta_{k}-\delta_{k_{0}})=(\mu^{k_{0}}-\mu^{k})-k_{0}+k<0
, so the factor is never zero.

Consider the factor from
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
,

D_{2}=\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}(h_{\gamma}+(\rho,\gamma))^{P_{\gamma}(\eta-\gamma)}.

Here
\eta-\gamma
is a sum of positive roots if and only if
\eta=\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq k_{0}
. Hence the zeros of the Kac–Shapovalov determinant on
M(\Lambda)^{\Lambda-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{k_{0}}}
occur precisely when

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0,\qquad 1\leq k\leq k_{0}.

Since
k_{0}
is the minimal positive integer with
b_{k}=0
, these values are excluded by the unitarity conditions (Lemma
6
). Thus
D_{2}\neq 0
, and the Kac–Shapovalov determinant is nontrivial. This completes the proof.
∎

The lemma yields
x_{\min}=a_{m}+k_{0}-1
. It remains to consider the range
x\in[x_{\min},x_{\max}]
. The integer values in this interval correspond to atypicality of
\Lambda
,
i.e.
,

(4.25)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;x=a_{m}+k-1,\qquad k_{0}\leq k\leq n.

These points correspond to points of unitarity, that is, unitarizable supermodules.

Lemma 25
.

Assume
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0
for some
k_{0}\leq k\leq n
. Then
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable.

Proof.

By Proposition
21
, it suffices to show that every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
admits a decomposition
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{r}
into pairwise distinct positive odd roots such that
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{i})>0
for all
i
. Consider the diagram of Lemma
22
. By monotonicity, negative values can occur only for roots of the form
\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j}
with
j>k
. For such roots,

\displaystyle(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j})

\displaystyle=(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{m})+(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{j})

\displaystyle=a_{m}-a_{i}+m-i+(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})+(\Lambda+\rho,\delta_{k}-\delta_{j})

\displaystyle=a_{m}-a_{i}+m-i+k-j.

Hence the relevant part of the diagram has the form

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{k})&>&(a_{m}-a_{1})+m-1&>&\cdots&>&(a_{m}-a_{1})+m-1-(n-k)\\[1.54996pt]
\vee&&&&\vee&&\vee&&&&\vee\\
\cdots&&&&\cdots&&\cdots&&&&\cdots\\[1.54996pt]
\vee&&&&\vee&&\vee&&&&\vee\\[1.54996pt]
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m-1}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m-1}-\delta_{k})&>&(a_{m}-a_{m-1})&>&\cdots&>&(a_{m}-a_{m-1})-(n-k)\\[1.54996pt]
\vee&&&&\vee&&\vee&&&&\vee\\[1.54996pt]
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&0&>&-1&>&\cdots&>&-(n-k)\end{array}

Since
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0
, Lemma
15
implies that every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of
L(\Lambda)
admits a decomposition which does not involve any of the roots
\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k},\ldots,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{n}
. Now assume that
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j})<0
for some
1\leq i<m
and
k<j\leq n
. Since
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k})>0
and

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k})-(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j})=j-k,

monotonicity implies that there exists
s
with
k\leq s<j
such that
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{s})=0
. By Lemma
15
, every composition factor
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
of
L(\Lambda)
therefore admits a decomposition into pairwise distinct positive odd roots which does not involve any of the roots

\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{s},\ldots,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{n}.

Applying this argument to each
i
, one obtains that every such
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent factor admits a decomposition
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{r}
into pairwise distinct positive odd roots such that
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{i})>0
for all
i
. This proves the claim.
∎

It remains to consider the non-integral points of the interval
I
.

Lemma 26
.

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})<0<(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k-1})
, then
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable.

Proof.

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})<0<(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k-1})
, then
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})\neq 0
for all
k=1,\ldots,n
. Yet the condition
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})<0
is incompatible with the unitarity conditions imposed on
\Lambda
, and
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable.
∎

Combining the preceding lemmas, we obtain a complete classification of the finite-dimensional unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules.

Theorem 27
.

The full set of unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules is parameterized by the union over all

\left\{\Lambda(x)=\Lambda_{0}+\tfrac{x}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1)\;:\;x\in\{a_{m}+k_{0}-1,\ldots,a_{m}+n-1\}\sqcup(a_{m}+n-1,\infty)\right\}

with
\Lambda_{0}=(0,-a_{2},\ldots,-a_{m}\,|\,b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1},0)
with

a_{i},b_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,

0\geq-a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq-a_{m}
, and

b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{k_{0}-1}>b_{k_{0}}=\ldots=b_{n}=0
. Equivalently,
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule if and only if it satisfies the following two conditions:

a)

\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions.

b)

one of the following holds:

(i)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{k})=0
for some
k=k_{0},\ldots,n
, or

(ii)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m}-\delta_{n})>0
.

Remark 28
.

We assume highest weight vectors to be even. Accordingly, the full set of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules is obtained up to application of the parity reversion functor
\Pi
.

4.2.
Infinite-Dimensional Unitarizable Supermodules

We assume
p,q\neq 0
and, without loss of generality,
p\leq q
. We fix the non-standard system of positive roots. Recall the expression for the Weyl vector from (
2.22
). We first describe the highest weights that can
occur for unitarizable
\mathfrak{g}
-supermodules, in direct analogy with the finite-dimensional
case. We then
state the classification strategy and illustrate it in the examples
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
and

\mathfrak{sl}(2|2)
. The general classification follows by extending the same argument.

4.2.1.
Parametrization of the Weight Space

Let
L(\Lambda)
be a simple
\omega
-unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, where we recall that
\omega\coloneqq\omega_{(-,+)}
. It follows from the definition of unitarity that a necessary
condition for
L(\Lambda)
to be unitarizable as a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is that
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module, denoted by
L_{0}(\Lambda)
. Recall

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}={\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)^{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)^{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)^{{\mathbb{C}}}

to emphasize the real form. This imposes a classical sequence of standard conditions on the highest weight, which we recall
is parameterized in terms of the standard coordinates on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
as

\Lambda=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m}|\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n}),

modulo shifts by
(1,\ldots,1|{-}1,\ldots,-1)
. We begin by recalling the parametrization of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules. First, we consider the restriction to the maximal compact subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{su}}(p)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(q)\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)
of
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n)
. If

L_{0}(\Lambda)
is unitarizable as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module, then as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{\mathbb{C}}
-module it is semisimple with
finite multiplicities. In particular,
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable simple
(hence finite-dimensional)
{\mathfrak{k}}^{\mathbb{C}}
-module, which appears with multiplicity one. Namely,
\Lambda
must be
integral and dominant with respect to the positive system induced from
{\mathfrak{g}}
, that is

(4.26)

\lambda^{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m}\geq\lambda^{1}\cdots\geq\lambda^{p},\qquad\mu^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu^{n}

Following
[
16
]
, we parameterize the solution to these constraints by writing

(4.27)

\Lambda=(0,a_{2},\ldots,a_{m-1},0|b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1},0)+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(1,\ldots,1,-1,\ldots,-1|0,\ldots,0)\\
+\tfrac{x}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1),

with integers
a_{i}
satisfying
a_{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{m-1}\geq 0\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{p}
, integers

b_{k}
satisfying
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n-1}\geq b_{n}\coloneqq 0
and real numbers
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
. In order to obtain this parameterization, we use the shift-invariance to impose the relation

\lambda^{1}+\lambda^{m}=2\mu^{n}=:x
, and set
\lambda\coloneqq\lambda^{1}-\lambda^{m}
.
The conditions on the
a_{i}
and
b_{k}
then follow from the preceding discussion.
It is known
[
8
,
16
]
that,
for fixed
a_{i}
and
b_{k}
subject to these conditions,
the weight
\Lambda
in (
4.27
) is the highest weight of a unitarizable
simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module if and only if

(4.28)

\lambda\in\bigl(-\infty,-m+\max(i_{0},j_{0})+1\bigr)\\
\cup\bigl\{-m+\max(i_{0},j_{0})+1,-m+\max(i_{0},j_{0})+2,\ldots,-m+i_{0}+j_{0}\bigr\},

where
i_{0}
is the largest index for which
a_{i}=0
, and
j_{0}
is the largest integer for which

a_{m-j}=0
(if
a_{p+1}=0
then
j_{0}=q
). Moreover, we define
k_{0}
to be the smallest integer such that
b_{k_{0}}=0
. The values
i_{0},j_{0}
and
k_{0}
are part of the
dominance data with respect to the maximal compact subalgebra.

We suppose that
\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions (Lemma
6
),
i.e.
,

(i)

\lambda^{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m}\geq-\mu^{n}\geq\cdots\geq-\mu^{1}\geq\lambda^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{p}
,

(ii)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{i}+\delta_{n})=0
for
p+1\leq i\leq m
implies
(\Lambda,\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{m})=0
,

(iii)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for
1\leq i\leq p
implies
(\Lambda,\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{i})=0
.

(iv)

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})\neq 0
for
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
, then
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})<0
for
i=1,\ldots,i_{0}
.

(v)

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})\neq 0
for
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
, then
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})<0
for
j=0,\ldots,j_{0}
.

In general, a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
L(\Lambda)
with
\Lambda
satisfying (i)-(v) need not be
\omega
-unitarizable. To classify the
\omega
-unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, it is convenient to introduce one-parameter families in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, as in the finite-dimensional case, indexed by
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, of the form

(4.29)

\Lambda(x)=\Lambda_{0}+\tfrac{x}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1),

where
\Lambda_{0}=(0,a_{2},\ldots,a_{m-1},0|b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1},0)+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(1,\ldots,1,-1,\ldots,-1|0,\ldots,0)
with
\lambda
satisfying (
4.28
),
and integers
a_{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{m-1}\geq a_{m}=0=a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{p}
and

b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n-1}\geq 0
as above. We assume that the unitarity conditions hold for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In what follows, we write
\Lambda=\Lambda(x)
and suppress the dependence on
x
whenever no confusion can arise. The classification is obtained by applying the method of Section
1.1.2
.

4.2.2.
Examples

We now illustrate the classification procedure on the Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{su}(1,1|1)
and
\mathfrak{su}(1,1|2)
. These are specific instances of supersymmetry algebras of superconformal quantum mechanics

[
11
,
7
]
.

\underline{\mathfrak{su}(1,1|1)}
. Set
{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq\mathfrak{su}(1,1|1)
. The non-standard positive system is
\Delta^{+}=\{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1},-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{1}\}
, the corresponding Weyl vector is
\rho=(0,0|0)
, and we set
\alpha\coloneqq\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1}
and
\beta\coloneqq-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{1}
. In particular,
A=\{\alpha\}
and
B=\{\beta\}
.

A general family
\Lambda
of highest weights of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module is of the form

(4.30)

\Lambda=\bigl(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+\tfrac{x}{2},\,-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+\tfrac{x}{2}\big|\tfrac{x}{2}\bigr),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

with
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\leq 0}
. The possible simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors are
L_{0}(\Lambda),L_{0}(\Lambda-\alpha),L_{0}(\Lambda-\beta)
, and
L_{0}(\Lambda-\alpha-\beta)
. Since
\rho=(0,0|0)
, the Dirac inequalities for the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
,

\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, are

(4.31)

\displaystyle 0\geq(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+x,\qquad 0\geq(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-x,

which is equivalent to

(4.32)

\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\leq x\leq-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}.

We now obtain a complete classification, following the classification method in Section
1.1.2
.

We first determine the interval on which unitarity holds automatically. Set
x_{\max}^{L}\coloneqq\max\{(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha):\alpha\in A\}
and
x_{\min}^{R}\coloneqq\min\{(\Lambda+\rho,\beta):\beta\in B\}
. Then
x_{\max}^{L}=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}
and
x_{\min}^{R}=-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}
. Hence, if
x_{\max}^{L}<x<x_{\min}^{R}
, then
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma)<0
for all
\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, so the Dirac inequality is strict for every odd positive root. Therefore Proposition
21
implies that
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable on this interval.

Next, one has
x_{\min}^{L}=x_{\max}^{L}
and
x_{\max}^{R}=x_{\min}^{R}
, and we set
x^{L}\coloneqq x_{\min}^{L}=x_{\max}^{L}
and
x^{R}\coloneqq x_{\max}^{R}=x_{\min}^{R}
. We show that
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable for
x<x^{L}
or
x>x^{R}
. Indeed, if
x<x^{L}
, then
L(\Lambda)
has the non-trivial
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor
L_{0}(\Lambda-\alpha)
, and if
x>x^{R}
, then it has the non-trivial
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor
L_{0}(\Lambda-\beta)
. In both cases, the Dirac inequality fails on this factor. To show that these
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors do indeed occur, it suffices to prove that under these conditions the corresponding weight spaces in
M(\Lambda)
are not contained in the radical; equivalently, that the Shapovalov form restricted to these weight spaces is non-degenerate. This follows once the Kac–Shapovalov determinant (see Theorem
17
) is shown to be nonzero. Set
\eta\in\{\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1},-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{1}\}
. The Kac–Shapovalov determinant on
M(\Lambda)^{\Lambda-\eta}
is
(\Lambda+\rho,\eta)
, which is nonzero for
x<x^{L}=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}
or
x>x^{R}=-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}
. Hence the Dirac inequality fails on these
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents, and
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable.

Hence, one can assume
x^{L}\leq x^{R}
, since for
x^{L}>x^{R}
the module
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable. It remains to consider the boundary points
x=x^{L}
and
x=x_{\max}^{R}
, equivalently the atypicality conditions
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)=0
. We treat only the case
x=x^{L}<x_{R}
, since the cases
x=x^{L}=x^{R}
and
x^{L}<x=x^{R}
are analogous.

Assume therefore that
x=x^{L}
, so that
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0
. By Lemma
15
, every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
admits a decomposition of
\gamma
into pairwise distinct positive odd roots which does not involve
\alpha
. Hence the only possible non-trivial
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors are
L_{0}(\Lambda)
and
L_{0}(\Lambda-\beta)
. Since
x^{L}<x^{R}
, the Dirac inequality is strict on the factor
L_{0}(\Lambda-\beta)
. Proposition
21
therefore implies that
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable at
x=x^{L}
if
x^{L}<x^{R}
.

Hence, the set of all unitarizable highest weights for
{\mathfrak{su}}(1,1|1)
is

(4.33)

\Bigl\{\bigl(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+\tfrac{x}{2},\,-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+\tfrac{x}{2}\big|\tfrac{x}{2}\bigr):\ \lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\leq 0},\ 2x\in[\lambda,-\lambda]\Bigr\}.

Equivalently,
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule if and only if

a)

\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions of Lemma
6
.

b)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})\leq 0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{1})\leq 0
.

\underline{\mathfrak{su}(1,1|2)}
: Set
{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{su}}(1,1|2)
. The non-standard positive root system is

\Delta^{+}=\{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2},\delta_{1}-\delta_{2},\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1},\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{2},-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{1},-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{2}\}
.
In particular,

\rho=\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\big|\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)
.
A general family
\Lambda
of highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module has the general form

(4.34)

\Lambda=(\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2}|\mu^{1},\mu^{2})=\bigl(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+\tfrac{x}{2},\,-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+\tfrac{x}{2}\big|b+\tfrac{x}{2},\,\tfrac{x}{2}\bigr),

with
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\leq 0}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We assume
\Lambda
satisfies the additional unitarity conditions of Lemma
6
. The Dirac inequalities are

(4.35)

\displaystyle 0\geq(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+x+b\;\Leftrightarrow\;x\leq-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b,

\displaystyle 0\geq(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{2})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+x-1\;\Leftrightarrow\;x\leq-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+1,

\displaystyle 0\geq(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{1})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-x-b-1\;\Leftrightarrow\;x\geq\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b-1,

\displaystyle 0\geq(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{2})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-x\;\Leftrightarrow\;x\geq\tfrac{\lambda}{2}.

These constraints on
x
yield all necessary information to classify all unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. We use the method of Section
1.1.2
.

By definition,
x^{L}=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}
and
x^{R}=-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b
. Hence, if
x^{L}<x^{R}
, then
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma)<0
for all
\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
whenever
\tfrac{\lambda}{2}<x<-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b
. Therefore Proposition
21
implies that
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable on this interval.

Note that
i_{0}=1
and
j_{0}=0
. Hence
x^{R}_{\min}=x^{R}_{\max}
, and
x^{L}_{\min}=x^{L}_{\max}
, which we will denote by
x^{R}
and
x^{L}
, respectively. In particular, unitarity does not hold for all
x>x^{R}
and
x<x^{L}
. Indeed, for
x>x^{R}
, the module
L(\Lambda)
has the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor
L_{0}(\Lambda-\epsilon_{1}+\delta_{1})
, and the Dirac inequality fails on this factor. Its occurrence follows from the non-vanishing of the Kac–Shapovalov form on
M^{\Lambda-\epsilon_{1}+\delta_{1}}
. Namely, for
\gamma\in\Delta^{+}
, the difference
(-\epsilon_{1}+\delta_{1})-\gamma
is equal to
0
or a sum of positive roots only for
\gamma=\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1}
. Thus the determinant vanishes only at
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})=0
. If
x>x^{R}
, it follows that this
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor occurs, and hence unitarity fails. Analogously, one shows that
L(\Lambda(x))
is not unitarizable for
x<x^{L}
, since for such
x
the Dirac inequality fails on the non-vanishing composition factor
L_{0}(\Lambda+\epsilon_{2}-\delta_{2})
.

It remains to prove unitarity in the degenerate case where one of the residual intervals collapses to a single point, namely when

(4.36)

x=x^{L}\leq x^{R},\qquad\text{or}\qquad x=x^{R}\geq x^{L}.

It suffices to prove unitarity in the case
x=x^{L}=x^{R}
, since the other cases are analogous. Then

(4.37)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{2})=0,\qquad(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})=0.

By Lemma
15
, every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
, where
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{r}
, admits a decomposition in which neither
\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1}
nor
-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{2}
appears. Since (
4.35
) gives
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{i})<0
for all other positive odd roots
\gamma_{i}
, Proposition
21
shows that
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable.

Altogether, the full set of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{su}}(1,1|2)
-supermodules is

(4.38)

\{\Lambda=\bigl(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+\tfrac{x}{2},\,-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+\tfrac{x}{2}\big|b+\tfrac{x}{2},\,\tfrac{x}{2}\bigr):b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\ \lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\leq 0},\ 2x\in[\lambda,-\lambda+2b]\}

Equivalently,
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule if and only if

a)

\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions of Lemma
6
, and

b)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})\leq 0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{2}+\delta_{2})\leq 0
.

4.2.3.
General Case

We start with a highest weight
\Lambda
of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module of the form (
4.29
), parametrized by
x
, namely

(4.39)

\Lambda(x)=\Lambda_{0}+\tfrac{x}{2}(1,\ldots,1|1,\ldots,1),

where
\Lambda_{0}=(0,a_{2},\ldots,a_{m-1},0|b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1},0)+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(1,\ldots,1,-1,\ldots,-1|0,\ldots,0)
, with
\lambda
satisfying (
4.28
),
a_{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{m-1}\geq a_{m}=0=a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{p}
, and
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n-1}\geq 0
. Recall that
i_{0}
is the largest index such that
a_{i}=0
, and that
j_{0}
is the largest integer such that
a_{m-j}=0
; if
a_{p+1}=0
, then
j_{0}=q
. Assume moreover that
\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions of Lemma
6
. The classification of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules is then obtained by analyzing
L(\Lambda(x))
as a function of
x
. In what follows, we suppress the parameter
x
and write simply
\Lambda
for
\Lambda(x)
.

Our classification method is independent of the explicit choice in (
4.29
); we retain it only for the sake of an explicit realization. Our result will also be formulated entirely in terms of Dirac inequalities as seen in the examples above.

We apply the method of Section
1.1.2
. Recall the decomposition
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}=A\sqcup B
. The analysis reduces to the values
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma)
for
\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. For
\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k}\in A
, with
1\leq i\leq p
and
1\leq k\leq n
, one has

(4.40)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k})=\lambda^{i}+\mu^{k}+p-i-k+1=x+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+a_{i}+b_{k}+p-i-k+1.

For
-\epsilon_{j}+\delta_{k}\in B
, with
p+1\leq j\leq m
and
1\leq k\leq n
, one has

(4.41)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{j}+\delta_{k})=-\lambda^{j}-\mu^{k}-n-p+j+k-1=-x+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-a_{j}-b_{k}-n-p+j+k-1.

By the form of
\Lambda
in (
4.29
) and the dominance relations, one obtains the following lemma.

Lemma 29
.

One has:

a)

The numbers
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)
, with
\alpha\in A
, arrange as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{1}-\delta_{n})\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{i}-\delta_{n})\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{p}-\delta_{1})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{p}-\delta_{n})\end{array}

b)

The numbers
(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)
, with
\beta\in B
, arrange as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{m}+\delta_{1})\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{1})\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{p+1}+\delta_{n})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,-\varepsilon_{p+1}+\delta_{1})\end{array}

Note that the maximum of
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)
for
\alpha\in A
is attained at
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})
, while the maximum of
(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)
for
\beta\in B
is attained at
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})
. In particular, if
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})<0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})<0
, then
\Lambda
is typical. By the classification method,
x_{\min}^{R}
is determined by
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})=0
, that is,

(4.42)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})=0\Longleftrightarrow x=-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b_{1}-p+1,

hence
x_{\min}^{R}=-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b_{1}-p+1
. Likewise,
x_{\max}^{L}
is determined by
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})=0
, that is,

(4.43)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})=0\Longleftrightarrow x=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+q-1,

hence
x_{\max}^{L}=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+q-1
. This yields an interval on which unitarity is immediate.

Lemma 30
.

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})<0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})<0
, then
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable.

Proof.

If the conditions hold, one has
(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma)<0
for all
\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
by Lemma
29
. The statement follows by Proposition
21
.
∎

If
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})<0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})>0
, then

(4.44)

x\in(x_{\max}^{L},x_{\min}^{R})=\bigl(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+q-1,-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b_{1}-p+1\bigr).

If these inequalities are not satisfied, this interval may be empty. For this reason, we formulate the result in terms of Dirac inequalities rather than the interval itself.

Recall that
\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions; see Lemma
6
. In particular, in standard coordinates,

(4.45)

\lambda^{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{m}\geq-\mu^{n}\geq\cdots\geq-\mu^{1}\geq\lambda^{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{p}.

Together with (
4.40
) and (
4.41
), this yields the definitions

(4.46)

x_{\min}^{L}\coloneqq\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+q-j_{0}-1,\qquad x_{\max}^{R}\coloneqq-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b_{1}-p+i_{0},

equivalently
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}+\delta_{n})=0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i_{0}}-\delta_{1})=0
. Moreover,
x_{\min}^{L}\leq x_{\max}^{L}
and
x_{\min}^{R}\leq x_{\max}^{R}
. These are the weakest bounds.

Lemma 31
.

If one of the following two equivalent conditions holds, then
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable:

a)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i_{0}}-\delta_{1})>0
or
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}+\delta_{n})>0
.

b)

x>x_{\max}^{R}
or
x<x_{\min}^{L}
.

Proof.

We show that if
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i_{0}}-\delta_{1})>0
or
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}+\delta_{n})>0
, then
L(\Lambda)
contains the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent
L_{0}(\Lambda-\epsilon_{i_{0}}+\delta_{1})
or
L_{0}(\Lambda+\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}-\delta_{n})
, respectively. Since the Dirac inequality fails on these
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents,
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable.

We show that
L_{0}(\Lambda+\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}-\delta_{n})
is a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent if
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}+\delta_{n})>0
.
The remaining case is analogous. It suffices to prove that the weight space
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)^{\Lambda-\eta}
for
\eta\coloneqq-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}+\delta_{n}
does not lie in the radical of the Shapovalov form of
M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)
, equivalently, the Kac–Shapovalov determinant (see Theorem
17
) does not vanish. Then
L_{0}(\Lambda-\eta)
does not belong to the radical and is a non-trivial
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of
L(\Lambda)
.

Consider the factor from
\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}
,

D_{1}=\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}}\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}(h_{\gamma}+(\rho,\gamma)-r)^{P(\eta-r\gamma)}.

Here
\eta-r\gamma
is a sum of positive roots only if
\gamma=\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}
and
r=1
for
p+1\leq i<m-j_{0}
. Then

h_{\gamma}(\Lambda)+(\rho,\gamma)-1=(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}})-1=a_{i}+(m-j_{0})-i-1>0,

since
a_{i}\geq 1
and
m-j_{0}-i\geq 1
. This shows
D_{1}\neq 0
.

For the factor from
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
,

D_{2}=\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}(h_{\gamma}+(\rho,\gamma))^{P_{\gamma}(\eta-\gamma)},

one has
\eta-\gamma
a sum of positive roots if and only if
\gamma=-\epsilon_{i}+\delta_{n}
for
p+1\leq i\leq m-j_{0}
. The possible zeros of the Kac–Shapovalov determinant are

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{i}+\delta_{n})=0\qquad p+1\leq i\leq m-j_{0},

By the unitarity conditions none of the values
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{i}+\delta_{n})
can vanish for
p+1\leq i<m-j_{0}
, since
j_{0}
is the largest integer with
a_{m-j_{0}}=0
, implying
(\Lambda,\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{n})\neq 0
. For
i=m-j_{0}
we have
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j_{0}}+\delta_{n})>0
by assumption. Hence
D_{2}\neq 0
, completing the proof.
∎

Combining Lemma
30
and Lemma
31
, it remains to
analyze the ranges

(4.47)

\displaystyle x\in I^{L}\coloneqq[x_{\min}^{L},x_{\max}^{L}]

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\tfrac{\lambda}{2}+q-j_{0}-1,\ \tfrac{\lambda}{2}+q-1\Bigr],

\displaystyle x\in I^{R}\coloneqq[x_{\min}^{R},x_{\max}^{R}]

\displaystyle=\Bigl[-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b_{1}-p+1,\ -\tfrac{\lambda}{2}-b_{1}-p+i_{0}\Bigr].

By Lemma
31
, unitarity can occur only if
x_{\min}^{L}\leq x_{\max}^{R}
. We call the sets of integers

(4.48)

I^{L}\cap\mathbb{Z}=\{x_{\min}^{L},x_{\min}^{L}+1,\ldots,x_{\max}^{L}\},\qquad I^{R}\cap\mathbb{Z}=\{x_{\min}^{R},x_{\min}^{R}+1,\ldots,x_{\max}^{R}\},

the
step
1
partition points
of
I^{L}
and
I^{R}
. They are precisely the following
atypicality points:

(4.49)

\displaystyle I^{L}\cap\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle=\left\{\,x\in I^{L}\big|(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0\text{ for some }1\leq i\leq i_{0}\,\right\},

\displaystyle I^{R}\cap\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle=\left\{\,x\in I^{R}\ \big|\ (\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0\text{ for some }1\leq j\leq j_{0}\,\right\}.

We will see that, whenever
x\in I^{L}\cup I^{R}
, the module
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable if and only if

x\in(I^{L}\cup I^{R})\cap\mathbb{Z}
. If
x\in I^{L}\cup I^{R}
but

x\notin(I^{L}\cup I^{R})\cap\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 32
.

If one of the following two equivalent conditions holds, then
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable.

a)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i+1}-\delta_{1})<0<(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})
or
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j+1}+\delta_{n})<0<(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})
for
1\leq i<i_{0}
or
1\leq j<j_{0}
.

b)

The point
x\in I^{L}\cup I^{R}
is not a step
1
partition point of
I^{L}
or
I^{R}
,
i.e.
,

x\notin I^{L}\cap\mathbb{Z}
and
x\notin I^{R}\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Assume that
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable. By Lemma
6
, if

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})\neq 0
for some
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
, or

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})\neq 0
for some
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
, then

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})<0\quad(i=1,\ldots,i_{0}),\qquad(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})<0\quad(j=0,\ldots,j_{0}).

If
x
is not a step
1
partition point, then

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})\neq 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,i_{0}
and

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})\neq 0
for all
j=1,\ldots,j_{0}
; moreover, by the
definition of
I^{L}
and
I^{R}
, one has

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})>0\ \text{for some }i\quad\text{or}\quad(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})>0\ \text{for some }j.

This contradicts Lemma

. Hence
L(\Lambda)
is not unitarizable.
∎

Lemma 33
.

If one of the following conditions holds, then
L(\Lambda)
is unitarizable:

a)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})<0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
.

b)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
and
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
.

c)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})<0
for
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
.

Proof.

We prove a); the arguments for b) and c) are analogous.

We show that for any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
of
L(\Lambda)
, the element

\gamma
admits a decomposition into pairwise distinct positive odd roots
\gamma_{s}
such that

(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{s})<0
for all
s
. The statement then follows from
Proposition
21
. Indeed, in each of the cases a)–c) the proof is
analogous to the proof of Proposition
21
, using
Lemma
15
(b).

As shown in the proof of Proposition
21
, it suffices to prove that

(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)<0
whenever
\alpha\in A
occurs as a direct summand of some
\gamma
, and
separately that
(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)<0
whenever
\beta\in B
occurs as a direct summand of some

\gamma
. By assumption
(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})<0
, hence
(\Lambda+\rho,\beta)<0

for all
\beta\in B
by Lemma
29
; it remains to treat the case
\alpha\in A
.

Consider the diagram of Lemma
29
. By monotonicity, non-negative values can occur
only for roots of the form
\epsilon_{i^{\prime}}-\delta_{k}
with
i^{\prime}\geq i
and
k=1,\ldots,n
. For such
roots one has

(4.50)

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i^{\prime}}-\delta_{k})=-(b_{1}-b_{k})-(k-1)+(i^{\prime}-i).

Hence the diagram takes the form

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}i-1&>&-(b_{1}-b_{2})+i-2&>&\cdots&>&-(b_{1}-b_{n})-n+i-1\\
\vee&&\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&\vee&&&&\vee\\

\vee&&\vee&&&&\vee\\

\vee&&\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{2})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{n})\\
\vee&&\vee&&&&\vee\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vee&&\vee&&&&\vee\\
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{p}-\delta_{1})&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{p}-\delta_{2})&>&\cdots&>&(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{p}-\delta_{n})\end{array}

Since
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
, Lemma
15
implies that
every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor of
L(\Lambda)
admits a decomposition that does not involve any
of the roots
\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1}
. Now assume that

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i^{\prime}}-\delta_{k})>0
for some
i^{\prime}>i
. Since

(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{k})<0
, monotonicity in the
i
-direction (step
1
) implies
that there exists
i^{\prime\prime}
with
i\leq i^{\prime\prime}\leq i^{\prime}
such that
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{k})=0
.
But then Lemma
15
shows that none of the roots

\epsilon_{i^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{1},\epsilon_{i^{\prime\prime}-1}-\delta_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1}
can appear.

Applying this argument for each
i^{\prime}>i
, we conclude that every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor admits a
decomposition
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{r}
into pairwise distinct positive odd roots with

(\Lambda+\rho,\gamma_{s})<0
for all
s
. This proves the claim.
∎

Combining the preceding lemmas, we obtain the complete complete classification of unitarizable
\mathfrak{g}
-supermodules,
formulated in Theorem
34
below. The statement is basis-independent; the explicit parameters
are given by the discussion above.

Theorem 34
.

A weight
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule if and only if

a)

\Lambda
satisfies the unitarity conditions of Lemma
6
, and

b)

one of the following holds:

(i)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})<0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
;

(ii)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{1})=0
for
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
and
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
;

(iii)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m-j}+\delta_{n})=0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})<0
for
0\leq j\leq j_{0}
.

(iv)

(\Lambda+\rho,-\epsilon_{m}+\delta_{n})<0
and
(\Lambda+\rho,\epsilon_{1}-\delta_{1})<0
.

Remark 35
.

All highest weight supermodules are understood to have even highest weight vector. The cases with odd highest weight vector are obtained by applying the parity reversion functor
\Pi
. This gives the full set of all unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules.

4.3.
The Case
\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)

If
m=n
, the special linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(n|n)
is not simple, as it contains a non-trivial ideal generated by the identity matrix
E_{2n}
. The quotient Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)\coloneqq\mathfrak{sl}(n|n)/{\mathbb{C}}E_{2n}
is simple and is called the
projective special linear Lie superalgebra
. Our classification result for
p,q\neq 0
provides a complete classification of unitarizable
\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)
-supermodules, after imposing the additional condition that
\Lambda=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{n}|\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n})
satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda^{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu^{j}=0
.
