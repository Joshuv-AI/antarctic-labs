Cloud model improved TOPSIS for comprehensive evaluation of system evolvability
Authors: Zhiming Guo, Long Guo, Wenzhong Lou, Hu Qiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the core bottlenecks in the evolutionary performance evaluation of automobile door stamping processes, namely insufficient adaptation to dynamic uncertain information and the lack of systematic methodological support in existing methods, this study establishes a complete evaluation methodological framework covering the indicator system, weight algorithm, and evaluation model. First, based on the PDCA cycle logic, a multi-stage indicator system integrating static benchmarks and dynamic evolution dimensions is designed, covering the entire process of planning, execution, inspection, and optimization. Second, an entropy weight-information cloud coupled weighting algorithm is proposed. The entropy weight method is used to extract objective data features, and the fuzzy-random modeling capability of the information cloud model is combined to achieve robust weight allocation of evolutionary indicators. Finally, to solve the problem that the traditional TOPSIS method cannot distinguish the advantages and disadvantages of schemes near the ideal solution, JS divergence is introduced to improve the traditional TOPSIS algorithm, and the distance between the process scheme and the positive/negative ideal solutions is quantified to realize accurate performance ranking. Through case validation on automobile door stamping processes, five typical door stamping process schemes are evaluated. The results show that the comprehensive performance score ranges from 0.1765 to 0.8689, and the performance ranking is highly consistent with the actual production logic, verifying the effectiveness and industrial adaptability of the proposed methodology. This study provides a systematic quantitative tool for the evolutionary performance evaluation of processes in complex manufacturing scenarios, and has important theoretical reference and engineering application value.Keywords: Evolutionary, PDCA cycle, Information cloud model, Divergence, JS divergenceSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the continuous advancement of information technology and the accelerating shift of the global manufacturing industry toward intelligence and digitalization1, intelligent manufacturing has emerged as a core approach to leading the new round of industrial revolution, driving industrial upgrading, and strengthening industrial competitiveness. The essence of intelligent manufacturing is the deep integration of new-generation information technology and manufacturing technology, which realizes the informatization, flexibility, and intelligence of the manufacturing process. Beyond mere technological innovation, it represents a profound transformation of manufacturing paradigms2.Intelligent technologies have evolved from auxiliary tools to core components of manufacturing systems, enabling real-time process monitoring and production dynamics visualization while breaking down traditional industrial boundaries. This shift calls for an evolution in manufacturing system design philosophy: moving from Industry 4.0’s technology-centric efficiency focus toward Industry 5.0’s human-centric, resilient sustainability.

For intelligent manufacturing systems, Zhang et al.3 adopted a task- and operational data-driven scheme to dynamically regulate the operating status of intelligent manufacturing systems. They further constructed a multi-stage integer linear programming model to achieve optimal decision-making for the operation modes and maintenance schedules of manufacturing systems. Parhi et al.4 investigated the issue of transparency in intelligent manufacturing systems, and evaluated the traceability and transparency of intelligent manufacturing systems by establishing transparency-related indicators and a mathematical model for traceability. Shukla Monica et al.5 proposed an intelligent manufacturing system evaluation model consisting of 5 phases, 6 modules, and 22 dimensions, and used the Stepwise Weight Assessment Ratio Analysis (SWARA) method to rank the indicators of each dimension, so as to obtain the impact degree of each dimension on the whole system. Zheng et al.6analyzed the influence of sensor disturbance on complex engineering systems, and proposed a BRB-based performance evaluation model with interval-form sensor disturbance, while further assessing the sensor robustness for different task requirements. Grace et al.7 constructed a fuzzy logic model to implement satisfaction evaluation for the evaluation framework, realized the identification of influencing factors in intelligent manufacturing systems and established the evaluation relationships among various influencing factors, and provided an optimal construction method for the system. Lee et al.8 conducted research on existing manufacturing system models, development processes and frameworks, and combined with smart factory-related evaluation models to construct a meta-model framework based on evaluation models and transformation models, and proposed a maturity assessment framework for future manufacturing systems. Lian et al.9 studied the unreliability problem of expert knowledge in artificial intelligence modeling, and proposed a new rule-based modeling framework with expert reliability, which was verified through a fiber optic gyro health evaluation case.

For quality analysis problems, Song et al.10 applied the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle to analyze production issues, achieving active and accurate on-site quality control and reducing production process time. Paiva et al.11 studied data mining in discrete industries, combined the PDCA cycle with Knowledge Discovery in Databases (KDD), and established a fault maintenance model to support decision-making for operation and maintenance. Anne et al.12 investigated the implementation of intelligent digitalization in supply chains. They determined key criteria for preliminary implementation by selecting appropriate proof-of-concept schemes and suitable customers, integrated simulation capabilities with the PDCA cycle, and carried out a case study in the aerospace industry to verify the effectiveness of the proposed model.

As a relatively intelligent part in intelligent manufacturing, door processing plays an important role in intelligent manufacturing systems. Gan et al.13 studied the quality problems in the stamping process, and established a stamping quality prediction model using stacked machine learning. They divided quality zones according to thickness variation, predicted the quality, monitored the quality changes under different stamping depths, and realized the control and monitoring of zero-level quality. Ding et al.14 focused on the quality problems of automobile body stamping parts, and adopted convolutional networks, image processing technology and aggregated information fusion to realize complex image processing and feature map fusion, thus completing the real-time quality monitoring of automobile body stamping parts. Gao et al.15 investigated the influence of parameter fluctuation on stamping quality. They used the grey relational analysis method to screen the key factors affecting stamping quality, constructed a polynomial response surface model, and carried out robust optimization combined with Six Sigma to improve the system robustness against parameter fluctuation, so as to realize the zero-level quality management of automobile stamping. Zhao et al.16 studied the stamping process of aluminum alloy automobile floor panels. Through response surface modeling and multi-objective optimization, they evaluated and optimized the heating process parameters affecting the mechanical properties of specimens, and realized the quality evaluation of automobile stamping processes. Qiao et al.17 conducted research on stamping die problems, and realized the lifetime prediction of stamping dies by establishing prediction models for parameters such as contact pressure, wear coefficient and coating thickness.

In summary, existing research on Intelligent Manufacturing Systems (IMS) has largely focused on static capability assessment and technical application dimensions, lacking a system-level evaluation framework for IMS that adapts to the transition from Industry 4.0 to Industry 5.0 while balancing the dual requirements of high performance and resilience. This study incorporates evolvability assessment into a unified framework for high-performance manufacturing and resilient manufacturing. High-performance manufacturing centers on the core demands of ultra-high precision, zero-defect production, and extreme operational availability, whereas resilient manufacturing focuses on the system’s capacity for rapid recovery and sustained operation in the face of production interruptions and parameter disturbances.

This paper conducts a comprehensive evaluation of IMS from the perspective of evolvability, leveraging the entropy weight method and information cloud theory to process dynamic uncertain information. From this evolvability-oriented perspective, the IMS indicator system is divided into static and dynamic indicators. Advantage (efficiency) indicators are adopted to quantify the system’s performance improvement capability in high-precision forming, zero-defect control, and continuous efficient production, and resilience (recovery rate) indicators are used to characterize the system’s rapid reset capability under interruption scenarios such as equipment failures, raw material fluctuations, and process deviations. Together, these two categories constitute the core evolutionary evaluation dimensions of high-performance resilient manufacturing systems. Combined with the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle theory, a full-process closed-loop evaluation indicator system for IMS is constructed. The coupled weighting method integrating the entropy weight method and information cloud theory is adopted to analyze the weights of the indicator system. Furthermore, the Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to an Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) method based on Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence is employed to resolve the problem of indistinguishable proximity to ideal solutions, enabling the calculation of the IMS comprehensive index. Finally, the proposed model is applied to the automotive door stamping process under high-performance and strong disturbance scenarios, verifying the effectiveness and applicability of the model and algorithms.

Future work will explore integrating Artificial Intelligence Generated Content (AIGC) for test data generation and incorporating Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) to improve evaluation interpretability. The construction of this evolvability-based indicator system for quality analysis of door stamping processes in IMS, along with the corresponding comprehensive index analysis, is expected to provide effective references for IMS evaluation and door stamping process optimization.

Research ideas

This paper evaluates intelligent manufacturing systems from an evolutionary perspective. Based on information theory, the American scholar Ulanowicz analyzed the driving forces behind system evolution from a macroscopic view, quantitatively investigated the evolutionary process of systems, and pointed out that the evolutionary capability of a system is composed of ascendancy and resilience, whose balanced relationship determines the sustainability of the system18. This paper introduces the two evolvability characteristics of ascendancy and resilience into the construction of evolutionary indicators, and establishes a construction procedure for the evolvability-based evaluation index system.

Analysis of system indicators

An intelligent manufacturing system is a dynamically evolving complex system with the in-depth integration of physical entities, information, and knowledge. Centered on the full manufacturing lifecycle, it achieves the intelligent operation of the manufacturing process via the synergistic flow of material flow, information flow, and capability flow. Evolutionary characteristics provide a crucial perspective for understanding intelligent manufacturing systems. From the evolutionary viewpoint and combined with the PDCA cycle theory, this paper establishes an index system for the intelligent manufacturing system, determines the index weights, and analyzes its comprehensive performance index.

Analysis method of system index weight

Different indicators in the intelligent manufacturing system index reflect different aspects of the overall index, and their degrees of influence on the whole are also different. Therefore, it is necessary to analyze the weight of each indicator to obtain scientific evaluation conclusions. Weight analysis methods for evaluation indexes at home and abroad are diverse, but they can generally be divided into three categories: subjective weight analysis methods, objective weight analysis methods, and comprehensive weight analysis methods.

Subjective Weight Analysis Method. The subjective weight analysis method relies on the experience and preferences of experts or decision-makers23. Each indicator is compared and scored with others according to its degree of relevance, and the weights are derived through comprehensive calculation. Commonly used subjective weight analysis methods include: Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), Analytic Network Process (ANP), binomial coefficient method, successive comparison method, etc.

System analysis process

Aiming at the problems of single static indicators and undifferentiated quantification methods in traditional system evaluation models, this paper constructs a multi-dimensional comprehensive dynamic and static system evaluation model integrated with entropy theory from an evolutionary perspective. Based on evolutionary characteristics, this study establishes a dual-track static and dynamic index system for system evaluation from the dimension of full life cycle management.

Firstly, the whole process of system operation including Plan, Do, Check and Act is decomposed based on the PDCA cycle theory. In the static index analysis module, a static index system covering the basic attributes of the system is constructed starting from design requirements. In the dynamic index analysis module, combined with the operation logic of the task domain execution system, two kinds of evolutionary indicators including resilience and upgradability are introduced, which effectively overcomes the limitation that traditional evaluation systems only rely on static indicators. Secondly, a unified dynamic and static index evaluation framework is formed by integrating the static and dynamic index systems, providing a complete input basis for subsequent quantitative analysis. Furthermore, a differentiated quantification strategy is designed according to the heterogeneity of index characteristics. On the one hand, the improved entropy weight algorithm is adopted to standardize the resilience and upgradability indicators, improving the objectivity of weight distribution. On the other hand, the information cloud algorithm is introduced to describe the fuzzy and random characteristics of indicators through the cloud model with expected value, entropy and hyper entropy, generating a weight matrix more in line with reality and overcoming the defects of traditional quantification methods such as singleness and insufficient description of moderate indicators. Finally, on the basis of weight calculation, the TOPSIS algorithm based on JS divergence is used to carry out comprehensive evaluation of multiple systems.

The index data are mapped into a weighted normalized matrix, and the comprehensive evaluation index of each system is obtained by determining positive and negative ideal solutions and calculating the JS divergence distance between samples and ideal solutions. Hierarchical structure of static indicators

The PDCA cycle which includes Plan, Do, Check and Act is a classic methodology for continuous improvement, and it fits the core characteristics of intelligent manufacturing systems such as dynamic optimization and iterative upgrading. In this part, taking the PDCA cycle as the modeling logic, static indicators, efficiency evolution indicators and dynamic-static combined indicators are embedded into each stage of the cycle to construct a closed-loop evaluation indicator system for intelligent manufacturing systems.

The Target Layer is the core orientation in the production process. It corresponds to the overall capability goal that the automotive door stamping process needs to achieve during production, meets the basic capability threshold of the stamping process for mass production of automotive doors, and focuses on quality processes such as compliance with dimensional accuracy, stable production efficiency and quality consistency. It provides a clear initial capability reference frame for the subsequent Do and Check stages. The Criterion Layer is the decomposition carrier of the Target Layer, corresponding to the three types of core basic elements that need to be clarified in the Plan stage of the automotive door stamping process and conforming to the characteristics of the stamping process. The Indicator Layer is the specific quantitative carrier of the Criterion Layer, and each indicator corresponds to the specific capability value that needs to be clarified for the automotive door stamping process.

Information cloud indicator weight algorithm

The Information Cloud Method is an uncertainty information processing method that integrates fuzzy mathematics and probability theory33. Its core is to realize the accurate two-way mapping between qualitative concepts and quantitative values, so as to solve the problem of information representation in which fuzziness and randomness coexist in complex decision-making. Fuzzy rule-based methods reduce the impact of subjective evaluation to a certain extent6.

Its core logic is to integrate the fuzziness of qualitative descriptions and the randomness of data itself through the abstract concept of “cloud”, and characterize uncertainty by statistical distribution characteristics rather than simply represent it with definite values or fuzzy sets. The normal distribution is taken as the core mathematical basis of the backward cloud generator applied in this study. This setting conforms to the general statistical characteristics of multi-attribute evaluation parameters, and is an inherent requirement for the model to achieve stable uncertainty quantification. The model also maintains good robustness to minor distribution deviations in practical applications. This feature endows it with unique advantages in multi-attribute decision-making, comprehensive evaluation and other scenarios.

The conventional weighting approaches, including the entropy weight method, coefficient of variation method, and analytic hierarchy process, yield only deterministic single-valued weights. Such point estimates merely reflect the relative importance of evaluation indicators from a subjective or objective perspective alone, and fail to capture the inherent uncertainty associated with weight determination. In real-world multi-attribute decision-making scenarios, weighting outcomes are inevitably influenced by data quality, indicator selection, subjective cognitive differences, and sample fluctuations, leading to inherent fuzziness and randomness in weight values. Moreover, single weighting schemes are particularly sensitive to outliers and biased observations. Extreme data points or inconsistent judgments can easily distort the final weight distribution and produce unrealistic weighting results.

By contrast, the information cloud model supports the systematic integration of multi-source weight estimates derived from the aforementioned three methods. By transforming discrete deterministic weights into a series of cloud drops with statistical distribution properties, the model effectively characterizes the fuzziness and randomness embedded in weight determination. Relying on the statistical regularity of cloud drops, this approach alleviates the disturbance caused by individual outliers or biased estimates, and generates more robust and reasonable comprehensive weights.

Improved comprehensive index algorithm of TOPSIS system

The TOPSIS method is suitable for multi-objective decision analysis, which gives full play to the objectivity of data and avoids the subjectivity of evaluation data to a certain extent34.It determines the superiority of evaluation objects by analyzing the relative distances between the evaluated objects and the positive and negative ideal solutions. The performance of an evaluated object is reflected by its distance from the ideal solutions: the closer it is to the positive ideal solution and the farther it is from the negative ideal solution, the better the object and the higher its ranking. The traditional TOPSIS method has the problems of weak information processing ability and low interpretability of results in multi-objective decision-making.

In information theory, the Kullback–Leibler divergence can be used to analyze the dissimilarity between data distributions, but it suffers from the limitations of asymmetry and unboundedness. The Jensen-Shannon divergence is defined to measure the similarity between probability distributions. In this study, the weighted standardized indicator vectors are first subjected to normalization preprocessing. Since all elements in the weighted standardized matrix are non-negative, each vector is normalized by dividing by the sum of its elements. This processing follows the common practice of improving the TOPSIS method based on information divergence in the field of multi-attribute decision making. Existing studies have verified its rationality and effectiveness in scheme evaluation scenarios35. The Jensen-Shannon divergence can accurately analyze the essential differences between alternatives and the ideal solution. Case study

To verify the feasibility and applicability of the proposed evolvability assessment algorithm, which is framed within the unified paradigm of high-performance manufacturing and resilient manufacturing, this paper selects the automobile door stamping process as the case study object, aligned with the requirements of real-world production scenarios.

The automobile door stamping process is a core procedure in vehicle mass production. Its pursuit of ultra-high forming precision, zero-defect production, and extreme operational availability aligns directly with the core demands of high-performance manufacturing, as its operational stability and forming accuracy directly determine the vehicle’s appearance quality, structural safety, and overall production efficiency. Meanwhile, the process is subject to numerous fluctuating factors,including raw material variations, equipment status drifts, and operational specification deviations,exposing it to frequent risks of production interruptions and parameter disturbances. This imposes stringent requirements on the system’s rapid recovery and sustained operation capability, which is the core goal of resilient manufacturing.

Against this backdrop, the case study explicitly tests how the proposed assessment quantifies two core dimensions: progressive (efficiency) indicators, which measure the system’s performance improvement in high-precision forming, zero-defect control, and continuous efficient production; and resilience (recovery rate) indicators, which characterize the system’s rapid reset capability under disruptions such as equipment failures, raw material fluctuations, and process deviations.In addition, the automobile door stamping process has accumulated a relatively mature production workflow and sufficient historical data, which provides robust basic data support for the implementation of the evolvability assessment algorithm, enabling accurate evaluation and verification. The case analysis results further allow targeted refinement of the algorithm’s details, while verifying its practical application value and popularization potential in real-world process evaluation scenarios.

Conclusions

Following a rigorous closed-loop logic from methodology construction to industrial case validation, this study conducts a systematic investigation on the evolution performance evaluation of automobile door stamping processes. A complete methodological framework, covering indicator system design to quantitative performance evaluation, is established. This framework overcomes the limitations of static and simplistic approaches in traditional evaluation methods; by integrating static benchmarks and dynamic evolution characteristics, it provides systematic technical support for evaluating process evolution performance in complex manufacturing scenarios, featuring strong theoretical innovation and engineering applicability.

The core contributions of this study are threefold: First, a two-dimensional indicator system covering the entire PDCA cycle is developed, which not only preserves the stability of static process benchmarks but also captures the uncertainty of dynamic evolution, addressing the one-sidedness of traditional indicator systems. Second, an entropy weight-information cloud coupled weighting algorithm is proposed. The entropy weight method ensures the objectivity of data-driven weighting, while the information cloud model is employed to handle fuzzy-random uncertainty, thereby significantly enhancing the robustness and scenario adaptability of weight allocation. Third, the TOPSIS algorithm is improved using JS divergence; by quantifying the distance between process schemes and ideal solutions, the discriminability and stability of performance ranking are enhanced, yielding higher evaluation accuracy compared to the traditional TOPSIS algorithm.

Case validation on automobile door stamping processes demonstrates that the performance scores derived from the proposed methodology are highly consistent with actual production logic, confirming its industrial applicability. In future work, this methodology will be further extended to multi-model and multi-production-line manufacturing scenarios. The dynamic response capability of the algorithm will be optimized by integrating real-time Internet of Things data to facilitate its in-depth application, aligning with the transition from Industry 4.0 to Industry 5.0.

Additionally, we will explore two key forward-looking enhancements. First, integrating Generative Artificial Intelligence to support stress testing of the evaluation model with synthetic yet realistic operational data, while automating indicator system generation and optimization to streamline the assessment workflow. Second, incorporating Explainable Artificial Intelligence principles to address the explainability challenge in AI-augmented manufacturing systems. While the current framework adopts inherently interpretable methods, future iterations will embed XAI to enhance the transparency of prescription analysis, ensuring evaluation results and weights are not only accurate but also transparent and actionable for human decision-makers. This alignment with Industry 5.0’s demands for trusted and collaborative manufacturing ecosystems underscores the practical value of this enhancement. This work provides a more accurate solution for evaluating the evolution performance of complex manufacturing processes and contributes to process optimization and quality improvement in the automotive manufacturing industry.
