BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics: from molecular engineering to multifunctional optoelectronic devices
Authors: Zhen Jiang, Tao Shen, Di Liu, Yang Wang, Yunqi Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
n-Type organic semiconductors are indispensable components of organic optoelectronic devices and are central to the advancement of flexible electronics, bioelectronics and integrated organic circuits. Despite substantial progress, the development of high-performance acceptor building blocks and their corresponding n-type polymers remains fundamentally challenged by the difficult balance among frontier molecular orbital energetics, charge-transport capability and synthetic accessibility. In this context, boron–nitrogen (BN) motifs, including three-coordinate B–N bonds and four-coordinate B ← N bonds, have emerged as versatile molecular design elements for engineering electron-deficient π-conjugated systems. Owing to their unique isoelectronic characteristics and intrinsic bond polarization, BN units can effectively lower lowest unoccupied molecular orbital energy levels, enhance electron deficiency, and modulate intermolecular interactions while preserving favourable backbone planarity. As a result, they offer broad opportunities for simultaneously tuning optical bandgaps, charge-transport properties and environmental stability. In this review, we provide a comprehensive overview of BN-embedded electron-deficient small molecules and conjugated polymers, with particular emphasis on molecular design principles, synthetic methodologies and emerging structure–property relationships. We further discuss representative applications of these materials in five major optoelectronic device platforms, highlighting how BN structural characteristics govern electronic structure, solid-state organization and device performance. Finally, we outline the key challenges that remain in this rapidly evolving field and present perspectives on the future development of BN-enabled n-type organic semiconductors. This review aims to offer a unified framework and practical guidance for the rational design of next-generation high-performance n-type organic electronic materials.

Introduction

Organic semiconductors serve as core materials for next-generation optoelectronics and have drawn extensive research attention over the past decades. They feature lightweight structures, inherent flexibility, solution processability and low-temperature large-area manufacturing.1–3 These merits enable their wide use in organic field-effect transistors (OFETs),4–7 organic photovoltaics (OPVs),8–10 organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs)11–13 and organic thermoelectrics (OTEs),14,15 with huge potential for flexible electronics, wearable equipment and building-integrated energy devices. According to the dominant charge carrier type, organic semiconductors are categorized into p-type (hole-transporting),16–18 ambipolar19–21 and n-type (electron-transporting).22,23 Among them, the development of n-type materials has consistently lagged far behind that of their p-type counterparts, both in terms of material diversity and core device performance. The primary bottleneck remains the shortage of high-performance electron-deficient acceptor motifs: few conjugated frameworks can simultaneously fulfill the four essential prerequisites for viable n-type organic semiconductors, deep LUMO levels for efficient electron injection, high backbone planarity and strong intermolecular interactions for charge transport, robust ambient stability, and scalable synthetic accessibility. To achieve the deep LUMO levels required for n-type charge transport, the mainstream molecular design strategy for decades has been to incorporate strong electron-withdrawing groups into conjugated backbones. Representative motifs include imides, amides, fluorine atoms and cyano groups, which have given rise to a series of classic acceptor building blocks such as 1,4,5,8-naphthalene diimide (NDI),24–26 bithiophene imide (BTI),27–29 diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP)30–32 and isoindigo (IID).33 n-Type semiconductors constructed from these classic skeletons have achieved remarkable progress and laid a solid foundation for the field. Nevertheless, this traditional strategy faces an inherent and intractable trade-off: on the one hand, stronger electron-withdrawing modifications are needed to deepen LUMO levels; on the other hand, highly electron-deficient conjugated systems are more susceptible to nucleophilic attack by water and oxygen under ambient conditions, leading to degraded chemical stability and shortened device lifetime. Meanwhile, bulky electron-withdrawing substituents often introduce steric hindrance, twist the conjugated backbone, weaken intermolecular π–π stacking and ultimately reduce charge mobility. This triple trade-off among deep energy levels, high charge mobility and good stability has become the core barrier restricting the further development of n-type organic semiconductors.

Against this backdrop, the precise embedding of boron–nitrogen (BN) units into π-conjugated frameworks has emerged as a highly promising frontier strategy to break through the above bottleneck. Depending on the relative positions of B and N atoms in the conjugated backbone, BN units have three main embedding modes: 1,2-, 1,3- and 1,4-positions. Among them, 1,3- and 1,4-position BN units show relatively weak ability to modulate the electron-deficiency of conjugated systems and have rarely been explored in the context of acceptor materials. In contrast, 1,2-position BN units can impart strong electron-deficiency to conjugated skeletons through precise electronic effect modulation, which perfectly matches the core design requirements of high-performance acceptor building blocks and thus shows extremely broad development prospects.

Despite the significant and rapid progress achieved in 1,2-BN-embedded electron-deficient systems in recent years, a systematic, unified overview of the design principles, structure–property relationships and device applications of 1,2-BN units in electron-deficient acceptor materials is still lacking. In particular, the key regulatory mechanisms underpinning their molecular engineering have yet to be generalized into a coherent theoretical framework. This review focuses on 1,2-BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatic systems and systematically surveys their representative research progress (Fig. 1). We first elaborate on the molecular design concepts and synthetic strategies of different classes of BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics, covering both small-molecule acceptors and electron-deficient conjugated polymers. We then elucidate the intrinsic structure–property relationships between BN structural features and material optoelectronic performance, combined with representative examples across various organic optoelectronic devices. Finally, we outline the major challenges currently facing this field and discuss promising future research directions. We expect that this review will provide new insights and theoretical guidance for the molecular design of novel high-performance electron-deficient acceptor building blocks, and promote further development of BN-embedded n-type organic semiconductors.

Molecular engineering of BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics

We first provide a systematic overview of the general design principles and synthetic strategies of BN-fused conjugated skeletons (Section 2.1), followed by the establishment of a unified structure–property relationship framework (Section 2.2) to clarify how the boron coordination number, peripheral substituents, and fused-ring backbone collectively regulate optoelectronic properties. The discussions of all subsequent small-molecule and polymer systems are organized within this central conceptual framework.

General design and synthetic strategies for BN-embedded conjugated aromatics

The construction of BN-embedded conjugated aromatics relies on several well-established universal protocols rooted in classic organoboron chemistry, rather than case-specific innovative reactions. According to the target bonding mode and reaction mechanism, these methodologies can be categorized into two main families—covalent B–N bond formation and B ← N coordinate bond formation—each with multiple widely adopted sub-routes. These general strategies lay the synthetic foundation for all BN functionalized materials discussed in subsequent sections.

General design for acceptors

As an integral part of the general design principles for BN-functionalized electron acceptors, B–N covalent bonds and B ← N coordinate bonds follow two distinct design pathways tailored to different performance targets, arising from differences in the coordination mode and electronic structure of the boron center. The two motifs are functionally complementary, with respective strengths, and collectively form the molecular design foundation of electron-deficient BN systems. From the perspective of molecular design principles, the core merit of B–N covalent bonds lies in backbone modification with minimal conjugation perturbation, rendering them a powerful tool for constructing high-mobility n-type materials. The three-coordinate boron atom adopts sp2 hybridization; upon forming a σ bond with the sp2-hybridized nitrogen atom, the remaining empty p orbital of boron participates in π-electron delocalization across the conjugated backbone together with the lone-pair p orbital of nitrogen. Accordingly, the B–N unit is a strict isoelectronic analogue of the carbon–carbon double bond (C <svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" version="1.0" width="13.200000pt" height="16.000000pt" viewBox="0 0 13.200000 16.000000" preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"><metadata> Created by potrace 1.16, written by Peter Selinger 2001-2019 </metadata><g transform="translate(1.000000,15.000000) scale(0.017500,-0.017500)" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"><path d="M0 440 l0 -40 320 0 320 0 0 40 0 40 -320 0 -320 0 0 -40z M0 280 l0 -40 320 0 320 0 0 40 0 40 -320 0 -320 0 0 -40z"/></g></svg> C) and exerts negligible disturbance on the planarity and conjugation continuity of the fused-ring backbone after isoelectronic substitution, thus preserving the tight π–π stacking and efficient charge transport capability of the parent framework. The molecular design guideline for B–N covalent systems is primarily built on energy level engineering of the parent core. Given the relatively limited LUMO-lowering magnitude of B–N covalent bonds themselves, they are typically embedded into deep-lying electron-deficient fused-ring cores such as perylene diimide (PDI) and terrylene diimide (TDI). The intrinsic electron-deficient character of the parent backbone ensures sufficient energy level depth of the system, while the intrinsic bond dipole of B–N units reinforces intermolecular dipole–dipole interactions to further optimize molecular packing and charge mobility.

In contrast, dative B ← N coordinate bonds represent the structural motif with the strongest energy level modulation capability and the highest modification flexibility in BN systems, addressing two core demands in the general design principles: precise energy level tuning and versatile backbone engineering. The four-coordinate boron atom adopts sp3 hybridization, with its empty orbital fully occupied by the lone pair electrons of the nitrogen atom to form a localized charge-separated state. The resulting polarization effect is far more pronounced than that of covalent B–N bonds, thereby enabling a much greater amplitude of LUMO downshift. Three molecular design rules have been generalized from this unique structural feature: (1) precise energy level tuning. By varying the electronic effects of boron-centered substituents, from electron-donating alkyl and mesityl groups to electron-withdrawing pentafluorophenyl, fluoro and cyano groups, and adjusting the number and embedding sites of B ← N units, frontier orbital energy levels can be precisely modulated over a wide range. This enables the construction of systems spanning from weakly to strongly electron-deficient character to match the energy level alignment requirements of diverse device platforms. (2) Broad backbone compatibility and functional modification. B ← N bonds can be incorporated into a diverse set of heterocyclic backbones, including thiazole, pyrrole, pyridine, pyrazine, BODIPY and Schiff base frameworks, via multiple bidentate chelation modes (e.g., N–N and N–O), demonstrating exceptional backbone adaptability. Meanwhile, boron substituents allow simultaneous regulation of solubility, aggregation behavior, photophysical properties and ambient stability, achieving multidimensional performance tuning at a single modification site.

Overall, the distinct properties of the two classes of BN units provide a clear selection framework for the molecular design of n-type materials: covalent B–N bonds are prioritized for systems targeting high mobility and long-term stability, whereas B ← N coordinate systems are the preferred choice for deep-level applications. The two motifs can also be co-embedded into a single conjugated backbone to construct acceptors that combine high mobility with deep energy levels, which constitutes one of the core rationales underpinning the general design principles of BN-functionalized acceptors.

Synthetic strategies for constructing three-coordinate B–N bonds

BN-embedded aromatics are predominantly synthesized via three mainstream routes, all featuring high regioselectivity and good scalability:

Intramolecular electrophilic borylative cyclization

As the most dominant protocol for fused-ring BN skeletons, this approach uses amino-substituted aryl precursors and Lewis acid reagents (e.g., BBr3 and AlCl3) to trigger intramolecular electrophilic ring closure, forming B–N covalent bonds in one step. It features concise synthetic steps and excellent regiocontrol and is universally applicable to BN-fused imide, acene and heteroaromatic systems.

Condensation cyclization

Bifunctional precursors (e.g., o-phenylenediamine derivatives) undergo condensation with boron reagents to form symmetric BN heterocycles under mild conditions. This route features high yields and good functional group tolerance, serving as a modular tool for small-molecule BN acceptor synthesis.

Strategies for constructing four-coordinate B ← N coordinate bonds

Four-coordinate B ← N systems account for the majority of B–N electron-deficient materials, and their synthesis follows four highly generalizable protocols:

Lithiation–borylation protocol

This is the most classic and widely adopted method for B ← N small-molecule acceptors. Brominated nitrogen-containing heterocyclic precursors undergo lithium–halogen exchange with n-BuLi to form aryllithium intermediates, which then react with organochloroboranes (e.g., Mes2BF). The introduced boron center spontaneously coordinates with the lone pair of the adjacent nitrogen atom to form the B ← N bond. It is compatible with thiazole, pyridine, pyrrole and most mononitrogen heterocyclic scaffolds.

Intramolecular C–H electrophilic borylation

This one-step strategy avoids pre-halogenated precursors. Under the promotion of strong Lewis acids (e.g., BCl3 and BBr3), boron reagents directly undergo electrophilic attack on aryl C–H bonds and form fused B ← N chelate rings via intramolecular cyclization. It features high atom economy and is the standard route for benzothiadiazole-fused boron heterocycles. This strategy can also be extended to polymer systems via post-polymerization borylation, enabling tunable borylation degrees.

Chelation-driven cyclization

Conjugated precursors with bidentate coordination sites (N–O and N–N), such as Schiff bases and aza-BODIPY intermediates, react with boron reagents (e.g., BF3·Et2O) to form stable five- or six-membered boron chelate rings. The reaction proceeds under mild conditions and yields highly stable products, representing the standard synthetic route for BODIPY-type and Schiff-base-derived BN acceptors.

Post-polymerization borylation

This polymer-specific strategy first prepares nitrogen-containing precursor polymers via conventional cross-coupling polymerizations (Stille and Suzuki) and then introduces boron moieties via polymer–analogous reactions to form B ← N bonds. It avoids the decomposition of boron groups during polymerization and allows flexible regulation of the borylation ratio, suitable for both main-chain and side-chain BN polymer systems.

Modulation mechanisms and structure–property relationships

To establish a unified theoretical framework for rational molecular design, we first systematically elaborate the core mechanisms by which key structural factors govern material physical properties and then detail the representative advances of covalent and coordinate BN-embedded systems. The optoelectronic properties of BN-embedded conjugated systems are governed by four core structural factors: bonding mode of BN units, embedding position in the conjugated backbone, substituents on the boron center, and fused-ring architecture. Each factor modulates the electronic structure, intermolecular packing and condensed-state properties through distinct microscopic mechanisms, as elaborated below.

Boron coordination number: electronic configuration and intrinsic polarization

The bonding nature of BN units is the most fundamental factor determining their modulation capability, which originates from the different hybridization states of the boron center and the resulting electronic polarization characteristics.

Three-coordinate B–N covalent bond

The boron atom adopts sp2 hybridization, forming a σ bond with the sp2-hybridized nitrogen atom. The remaining empty p orbital of boron participates in π delocalization of the conjugated backbone together with the lone-pair p orbital of nitrogen. As a result, isoelectronic B–N substitution causes minimal disturbance to the planarity and conjugation continuity of the conjugated skeleton and largely preserves the intrinsic tight π–π stacking of fused-ring systems. Meanwhile, the intrinsic bond dipole of covalent B–N units further reinforces intermolecular packing via dipole–dipole interactions, thereby boosting charge transport efficiency. Although covalent B–N bonds exert a relatively limited effect on LUMO downshifting, their outstanding capability to preserve backbone planarity makes them highly suitable for embedding into inherently electron-deficient fused-ring cores such as imide moieties to construct high-mobility planar conjugated skeletons, enabling fine-tuning of energy levels and aggregation behavior without compromising the intrinsic electron transport performance of the parent systems.

Four-coordinate B ← N coordinate bond

The boron atom adopts sp3 hybridization, and its empty orbital is fully occupied by the lone pair electrons of nitrogen, forming a localized charge-separated state (nitrogen carries partial positive charge, and boron carries partial negative charge). This strong polarization effect significantly elevates the surface electrostatic potential of the conjugated system and amplifies the electron-deficient character, thus delivering a much larger LUMO downshift amplitude than covalent B–N bonds. However, the tetrahedral configuration of four-coordinate boron interrupts the continuity of π conjugation to some extent, and the substituents on boron usually introduce additional steric hindrance, leading to a more pronounced impact on molecular planarity and packing than covalent systems.

Boron substituents: inductive/conjugative effects and steric hindrance

Substituents on the boron center regulate material properties through the dual mechanisms of electronic effect and steric effect, serving as a convenient and efficient tool for performance fine-tuning. From the perspective of electronic effect, electron-withdrawing substituents (e.g., pentafluorophenyl, cyano, and fluorine) further withdraw electron density from the boron center via inductive and conjugative effects, strengthening the overall electron-deficiency and achieving additional LUMO downshift. Electron-donating substituents (e.g., alkyl and electron-rich aryl groups) weaken the electron-deficient character of B–N units and upshift the LUMO level accordingly. From the perspective of steric effect, bulky substituents (e.g., mesityl) spatially shield the electrophilic boron center, significantly improving hydrolytic and ambient stability. They also suppress excessive intermolecular π–π stacking, alleviate aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ) and enhance luminescence efficiency. However, excessively large steric hindrance will weaken interchain charge transport and reduce carrier mobility. Small-sized substituents (e.g., fluorine and cyano) cause negligible disturbance to backbone planarity, which is beneficial for tight packing and high charge mobility.

Fused-ring architecture: conjugation extension and packing regulation

The size, topological structure and heteroatom composition of the fused-ring mother skeleton mainly affect physical properties by regulating the degree of π conjugation and intermolecular packing modes. First, the extension of conjugated length with increasing fused-ring size expands the delocalization range of π electrons, leading to an upshifted HOMO level, a downshifted LUMO level and a narrowed optical bandgap, thus red-shifting the absorption and emission spectra to the near-infrared region. Meanwhile, a larger planar conjugated surface enhances intermolecular π–π interactions and improves charge mobility. Second, the topological structure of fused rings determines the packing behavior. Linear fused skeletons tend to form one-dimensional ordered stacking with high mobility along the stacking direction, while disk-shaped fused systems with multi-B–N embedding favor two-dimensional columnar packing with isotropic transport properties. Fully fused ladder-type structures maximize backbone rigidity and planarity, suppressing non-radiative transitions and boosting both luminescence efficiency and carrier mobility. Third, the electronic properties of the mother skeleton show a synergistic effect with BN units. Electron-deficient fused cores (e.g., benzothiadiazole, pyrazine, and naphthalene diimide) work synergistically with the electron-withdrawing effect of BN units to achieve deeper LUMO levels, while electron-rich mother nuclei can balance the overall electronic structure for bandgap and absorption engineering.

Electron-deficient small-molecule acceptors and polymers based on three-coordinate boron

Consistent with the structure–property principles outlined in Section 2.3, three-coordinate B–N covalent architectures feature moderate LUMO modulation, intact backbone planarity and superior ambient stability. Below, we categorize representative imide-type and A–D–A small molecules/polymers and analyze their performance evolution.

All B–N covalent bond-fused imide derivatives discussed in this section are typically constructed via the intramolecular electrophilic borylative cyclization strategy summarized in Section 2.1.1. In early investigations into this field, the Zhang group achieved the incorporation of BN fused-ring moieties at the bay position of the highly electron-deficient PDI scaffold in 2014, affording a mono-BN-substituted PDI derivative (compound 1, Fig. 2).34 The B–N covalent bond-embedded molecule exhibits a LUMO energy level of −3.63 eV, which is slightly elevated relative to that of pristine PDI. This finding confirms that B–N covalent bonds exert a limited lowering effect on the LUMO level of fused-ring systems and primarily modulate the bandgap of materials and antioxidant stability by tuning the HOMO energy level. In 2022, the Pei and Wang groups developed a novel strategy for embedding B–N covalent bonds within the molecular backbone.35 They synthesized an n-type PDI derivative (compound 2) with internally integrated B–N covalent bonds. Compared with all-carbon PDI, the incorporation of B–N units downshifts the HOMO energy level by approximately 0.1 eV, while the LUMO energy level remains largely maintained at around −3.70 eV, thus preserving the intrinsic electron affinity of PDI. Single-crystal organic field-effect transistors (OFETs) based on this material deliver an electron mobility of 0.35 cm2 V−1 s−1, validating that the backbone-embedded B–N covalent bond design can effectively enhance charge transport performance. In 2025, the same team further expanded the family of backbone-embedded BN imide systems by preparing a series of B–N-embedded TDI derivatives (compounds 3–7).36 The incorporation of B–N units does not cause significant changes to the LUMO energy level of the TDI core, which remains stably in the range of −3.69 to −3.76 eV. OFET devices fabricated with this series of materials achieve a maximum electron mobility of 0.039 cm2 V−1 s−1. In the same year, the team additionally developed a B–N-hybridized PDI derivative designated BN-JPDI (compounds 8–11),37 which was peripherally functionalized with thiophene, benzene and trimethylsilylethynyl groups. BN-JPDI possesses a LUMO energy level of −3.76 eV, slightly lower than those of bis-B–N-substituted PDI and pristine PDI; this deeper energy level further elevates its electron mobility to 0.57 cm2 V−1 s−1.

Chemical structures of small-molecule acceptors and polymers based on three-coordinate B–N covalent architectures.

Beyond imide-based systems, B–N covalent units have also been deployed in the molecular design of small-molecule acceptors for organic photovoltaics. In 2022, the Duan group introduced B–N covalent bonds into A–D–A-type non-fullerene acceptor systems and developed an electron-deficient acceptor (compound 12).38 The central donor unit is a fused-ring conjugated backbone (BNTT) embedded with two B–N covalent bonds, which ensures high backbone coplanarity and efficient charge transport, while enabling optoelectronic property modulation via the multiple resonance effect of B–N units. The molecule features a LUMO energy level of −4.07 eV and a HOMO energy level of −6.00 eV. Organic solar cells based on the PM6:BNTT2F active layer yield a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 8.3%, demonstrating the application potential of covalent B–N systems in photovoltaic acceptors.

Integrating B–N covalent units into conjugated polymer systems can inherit the planarity, crystallinity and chemical stability of their small-molecule counterparts and further amplify charge transport performance through extended conjugation and interchain stacking. In 2021, the Duan group embedded B–N covalent bonds into the design of polymer functional building blocks, developing a facilely synthesized fused-ring B–N unit.39 The polymer 13 has a LUMO energy level of −3.46 eV and displays ambipolar charge transport behavior, with hole and electron mobilities of 4.3 × 10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1 and 3.3 × 10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1, respectively. When employed as a polymer donor blended with the Y6-BO acceptor for organic solar cell fabrication, it delivers a high PCE of 16.1%, verifying the excellent compatibility of such systems in optoelectronic devices.

Nevertheless, B–N covalent bond-based small-molecule acceptors and polymers suffer from relatively limited structural diversity. Current studies are largely concentrated on classic imide skeletons, with insufficient development of novel core backbone systems. Due to the intrinsically weak electron-deficiency of B–N covalent bonds, deep energy level modulation still relies on the assistance of traditional electron-withdrawing groups, and their capacity to independently construct deep-level acceptors is inferior to that of four-coordinate B ← N systems. Furthermore, the inadequate structural diversity of these systems leaves substantial room for improvement in n-type charge transport performance.

Electron-deficient molecular systems based on four-coordinate boron

As elaborated in Section 2.1.1, four-coordinate B ← N motifs deliver stronger LUMO downshifting via charge-separated polarization effects and dominate current high-performance n-type acceptor design. Note that some fused skeletons simultaneously contain covalent B–N bonds; this review classifies materials by the dominant four-coordinate boron unit governing electronic properties.

Electron-deficient small molecules containing four-coordinate boron

In this section, B ← N electron-deficient small-molecule acceptors are classified according to their precursor skeleton types, and the modulation rules of structural factors on the electronic structure and charge transport properties of the materials are elucidated, providing a theoretical reference for the design of high-performance B ← N-based electron-deficient acceptors. Five-membered nitrogen-containing heterocyclic substrates, featuring abundant availability, rich modification sites and high synthetic accessibility, serve as the fundamental research system for B ← N small-molecule acceptors. The design rationale of such systems is to strike a balance between synthetic convenience and electron-deficiency through the selection of heterocycle types, modification of boron substituents and tuning of the degree of conjugation fusion. According to the precursor skeleton types, these systems can be divided into three major branches:

Thiazole-based acceptors

Thiazole-based scaffolds serve as the pioneering framework for constructing n-type acceptors with B ← N coordinate bonds. Their core advantage lies in facile site-selective functionalization, rendering them versatile fundamental building blocks for extending conjugated systems. In 2006, the Yamaguchi group pioneered the establishment of this research system.40 Using (3-bromo-2-thienyl)-2-thiazole as the starting material, they synthesized a dimesitylboryl-substituted thienothiazole monomer (compound 15, Fig. 3). Through regioselective functionalization, three regioisomeric dimers: head-to-head (H–H, 19), head-to-tail (H–T, 20) and tail-to-tail (T–T, 21) isomers, which were obtained, verifying the electron transport capability of this material class, with the dimer delivering an electron mobility of 1.5 × 10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1. Aiming to enhance the electron-deficient character, researchers have developed two optimization strategies. The first is the strong Lewis acid modification approach. In 2010, the Yamaguchi group functionalized bisthiazole-capped conjugated systems with tris(pentafluorophenyl)borane (compounds 22 and 23),41 lowering the LUMO energy levels to −3.1–−3.2 eV. The second is the diboron chelation-fusion strategy. The Zhang group prepared diboron tetracoordinate complexes via a two-step synthetic route to further extend the conjugated backbone (compound 24),42 achieving LUMO energy levels of −2.88–−3.07 eV. To address the demand for improved backbone rigidity and stability, the Zhang group further proposed an O–B ← N triatomic chelating coordination strategy to construct fully fused diboron-bridged ladder-type conjugated systems (compounds 25 and 26).43,44 Sterically bulky boron substituents effectively suppress aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ), while fluorine and methoxy substitutions enable fine-tuning of energy levels. The optimized system exhibits an elevated electron mobility of up to 8.3 × 10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1. In 2024, the Huang group designed and synthesized two edge O–B ← N embedded fused conjugated molecules (compound 27),45 which were modified with phenyl and perfluorophenyl groups at the boron center, respectively. Both molecules serve as weak electron-deficient conjugated units with LUMO energy levels of −2.84 eV (27a) and −2.98 eV (27b).

Chemical structures of thiazole-based BN-embedded small-molecule acceptors.

Benzothiadiazole-based acceptors

Benzothiadiazole inherently exhibits strong electron-deficient character, and its integration with B ← N coordinate bonds enables a synergistic downshift of energy levels, making it a critical development direction for deep-level, narrow-bandgap acceptors. In 2015, the Turner group developed a benzothiadiazole-directed intramolecular C–H electrophilic borylation-fusion strategy.46 The fused boron heterocycle (compound 30, Fig. 4) was constructed via a one-pot borylation–chelation reaction, which achieved a substantial LUMO downshift of 0.6–0.8 eV and narrowed the optical bandgap in a single step, providing a facile and efficient synthetic route for this class of systems. In 2016, the Nakamura group designed and synthesized benzothiadiazole-based boron complexes (compounds 31 and 32),47 confirming that bis-BN systems can lower the LUMO energy level from approximately −3.0 eV of the parent compound to −3.5–−3.7 eV.

Chemical structures of benzothiadiazole-based BN-embedded small-molecule acceptors.

Pyrrole and its derivative-based acceptors

Pyrrole-based skeletons can be derivatized into diverse functional systems such as benzodipyrrole and aza-BODIPY. As a category of five-membered heterocycles with abundant structural diversity and a broad performance range, they represent a key research frontier for deep-level small-molecule acceptors at present. Mono- and bis-pyrrole fused systems focus on the structure–property relationship between chelate ring size and material performance. The Wakamiya group further systematically investigated the selective synthesis rules governing chelate ring dimensions, revealing that the molecular backbone type plays a decisive role in the formation of five- or six-membered B ← N chelate rings (compounds 33 and 34).48,49 The azafulvene dimers developed based on this strategy feature LUMO energy levels as low as −4.15–−4.35 eV. The aza-BODIPY system is a well-developed deep-level branch that combines high molecular planarity and strong electron deficiency. In 2014, the Müllen group established a general one-pot synthetic route for dimeric aza-BODIPY (compounds 36 and 37, Fig. 5),50 with the product exhibiting a LUMO energy level below −4.0 eV. Subsequent, the Soji Shimizu group reported aza-BODIPY derivatives (compounds 38–40) with dual B ← N bonds, which boosted the electron-withdrawing character and gave LUMO levels of −3.24, −3.40 and −2.75 eV, respectively.51 They extended dimeric aza-BODIPY to isoindigo and benzodipyrrolidone systems,52 affording 41a (LUMO: −4.11 eV, HOMO: −5.72 eV) and 42b (LUMO: −4.09 eV, HOMO: −5.74 eV). In 2022, the Zhang group designed isoindigo-fused aza-BODIPY (compound 43) with extended π-conjugation, dual B ← N moieties, fluorine substituents and unsaturated nitrogen atoms.53 With a HOMO of −5.24 eV, a LUMO of −4.24 eV and a 1.00 eV electrochemical bandgap, 43 achieved an electron mobility of 0.11 cm2 V−1 s−1 in bottom-gate bottom-contact (BGBC) OFET devices, showing good application prospects.

BODIPY-based conjugated oligomers

In 2023, Liu's group synthesized a series of monodisperse thieno-fused BODIPY-based conjugated oligomers (44–47, monomer to pentamer) built from resonant N–B ← N units.54 As the degree of polymerization increases from dimer to pentamer, the LUMO level downshifts from −3.86 to −4.30 eV, the HOMO level upshifts from −5.68 to −5.33 eV, and the electrochemical bandgap narrows from 1.82 to 1.03 eV. The Liu group developed compound 48.55 The incorporation of resonant N–B ← N structures greatly strengthens electron delocalization and molecular dipole effects across the conjugated backbone. As a result, the thin-film absorption edge is red-shifted to 1450 nm, yielding an ultra-narrow optical bandgap of 0.86 eV.

Six-membered nitrogen-containing heterocycles feature higher structural stability and stronger nitrogen coordination ability. In particular, diaza-heterocycles enable the construction of dual B ← N coordinate bonds, making them optimal skeletons for simultaneously achieving deep LUMO energy levels and high charge mobility. The design objective of such systems is to realize deep energy level regulation and efficient charge transport through multi-BN embedding and fused-ring engineering. According to the number of nitrogen atoms, these systems can be divided into mononitrogen pyridine-based systems and dinitrogen pyrazine-based systems.

Pyridine-based acceptors

Pyridine-based systems are characterized by stable HOMO energy levels that are insensitive to conjugation length, while the LUMO energy level decreases linearly with extended conjugation, making them suitable for constructing linearly conjugated systems with precisely controllable energy levels. In 2012, the Curiel group combined indolo[3,2-b]carbazole with four-coordinate boron complexation (compound 53, Fig. 6), confirming that boron complexation can selectively lower the LUMO energy level and convert traditional hole-transporting units into electron-deficient systems.56 The product exhibits a LUMO energy level of −3.22 eV. In 2024, the Liu group further constructed monodisperse oligomers (54–58) based on double B ← N-bridged bipyridine (BNBP, 54),57 achieving a continuous linear downshift of the LUMO energy level from −3.24 eV to −3.69 eV with increasing chain length, while the HOMO energy level remained constant at −5.72 eV, demonstrating excellent energy level designability.

Chemical structures of pyridine- and pyrazine-based small-molecule acceptors.

Pyrazine-based acceptors

Pyrazine contains two para-positioned nitrogen atoms and can form dual B ← N coordinate bonds, representing the skeleton with the highest performance ceiling among current B ← N small-molecule systems. Research in this field focuses on two major directions: multi-borylation fusion and stability optimization. In terms of energy level regulation, the Li group achieved the selective synthesis of mono- and bis-boron chelates (compounds 59–62),58 confirming that bis-boron chelation lowers the LUMO energy level from approximately −3.4 eV to −3.96 eV while leaving the HOMO level largely unchanged, verifying the selective LUMO modulation effect of B ← N coordination. In 2016, the Fang group constructed D–A–D-type ladder-type pyrazine systems (compounds 63 and 64),59 further pushing the LUMO energy level below −4.0 eV. The Huang group realized wide-range LUMO tuning from −3.46 eV to −4.81 eV via bromine functionalization of BNIDT systems (compounds 65–68), demonstrating exceptional flexibility in energy level engineering.60

For stability and mobility optimization, the Huang group subsequently developed two stabilized B ← N-embedded IDT building blocks (compounds 69 and 70) via boron-centered aromatic substitution.61 The two acceptors show low LUMO levels of −3.61 and −3.72 eV, respectively. In 2019, the Liu group developed a one-pot multi-borylation cyclization strategy to construct disk-shaped fused polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons containing four B ← N coordinate bonds (compound 71).62 The material features a LUMO energy level of −4.07 eV and a record-high single-crystal electron mobility of 1.6 cm2 V−1 s−1 for this class of systems. Subsequently, the same group further expanded tetra-fused dibenzoazaacene systems (compound 72), achieving an extremely low LUMO energy level of −4.58 eV and providing a new design rationale for high-electron-affinity π systems.63 For functional expansion, Liu's group developed an O–B ← N fused molecule 73 by substituting nitrogen with oxygen in the previously reported acceptors.64 It possesses LUMO/HOMO energy levels of −4.27/−4.94 eV; relative to NDI (−3.84/−6.94 eV), its HOMO is upshifted and its LUMO is downshifted, resulting in an ultranarrow electrochemical bandgap of 0.67 eV. The molecule achieves selective infrared absorption with strong near-infrared absorption and high visible-light transparency (380–760 nm), appearing colorless in both solution and film. It also exhibits SWIR fluorescence with an emission peak at 1212 nm in solution (Tables 1–5).

Conjugated polymers containing four-coordinate boron

Embedding B ← N coordinate bonds into the polymer backbone inherits the energy-level tuning advantages of small-molecule systems, while optimizing charge transport through extended conjugation and interchain stacking, representing a pivotal design strategy for high-performance n-type polymers. All B ← N-functionalized polymers are developed from small-molecule acceptor building blocks and are similarly categorized into five-membered and six-membered heterocyclic systems based on their precursor skeletons. According to the types of acceptor building blocks, they are divided into four major categories:

Thiazole-based B ← N functionalized polymers

Liu's group has systematically developed a series of B ← N-bridged conjugated polymers based on the isoelectronic substitution strategy, realizing progressive advances in electronic structure regulation, photovoltaic performance optimization and application scenario expansion. In 2015, they first proposed replacing C–C units in polymer backbones with B ← N coordination bonds to modulate the electronic structure of conjugated polymers (74).65 Compared with the all-carbon reference (LUMO/HOMO: −3.11/−5.37 eV), polymer 74 exhibited synchronously downshifted LUMO and HOMO levels of −3.76 and −5.90 eV. The downshift amplitude can be further fine-tuned by modifying the substituents on the boron center. Building on this molecular design principle, the team copolymerized B ← N-bridged thienothiazole building blocks with three electron-deficient moieties (TPD, IID and DPP),66 yielding polymers 74, 75 and 76 with number-average molecular weights (Mn) of 18.0, 17.0 and 10.1 kDa and LUMO levels of −3.80, −3.57 and −3.73 eV, respectively. Sterically bulky phenyl groups on boron were found to weaken intermolecular π–π stacking and hinder charge transport, resulting in electron mobilities of 3.40 × 10−7, 2.80 × 10−5 and 8.48 × 10−6 cm2 V−1 s−1 for the three polymers. Even so, all-polymer solar cells based on 76 achieved a remarkable improvement in power conversion efficiency (PCE), rising from 0.12% to 5.04%. To further boost photovoltaic performance, the group synthesized random copolymer 77via Stille polymerization of asymmetric B ← N-bridged thienothiazole and pyridine-functionalized DPP units, with a high Mn of 49.9 kDa.67 Possessing deep LUMO/HOMO levels of −3.71/−5.81 eV, 74 showed favorable energy level alignment with a variety of commercial polymer donors, and the corresponding devices delivered a maximum PCE of 6.60%. Extending this material system beyond photovoltaics, the team investigated the organic thermoelectric properties of the isoindigo/B ← N-bridged thienothiazole polymer 75.68 Under the optimal doping conditions of 5 wt% N-DMBI, 72 exhibited an electrical conductivity of 9.7 × 10−4 S cm−1, a Seebeck coefficient of −453.8 µV K−1 and a power factor of 0.02 µW m−1 K−2. The Seebeck coefficient decreased gradually with increasing doping concentration, consistent with the variation trend of rising carrier concentration.

Benzothiadiazole-based B ← N functionalized polymers

Half-fused B ← N benzothiadiazole units resolve the stacking inhibition caused by sterically bulky side groups via the fused-ring design, representing the most prominent system among five-membered heterocyclic systems to date. In 2017, the Ingleson group reported a universal post-polymerization C–H electrophilic borylation strategy, yielding PF8-BT-derived polymer 78 with tunable borylation degrees.69 The introduced B–N coordination bonds downshift the LUMO level, narrow the bandgap and enable near-infrared emission. In 2025, our group designed and synthesized two types of asymmetric half-fused B ← N benzothiadiazole acceptors.70 After copolymerization with thiophene-based DPP, the resulting polymers (79a and 79b) exhibit a low LUMO energy level of −3.96 eV and a maximum electron mobility of 3.85 cm2 V−1 s−1, along with excellent mechanical bending stability and ambient stability.

Pyrrole/BODIPY-based B ← N functionalized polymers

These systems inherit the deep-level characteristics of aza-BODIPY small molecules and represent a critical research direction for deep-level n-type polymers. In 2022, the Zhang group employed naphthodipyrrole aza-BODIPY small molecules as acceptors and copolymerized them with IDT to prepare polymers (80 and 81, Fig. 7),71,72 achieving LUMO energy levels of −4.24 to −4.26 eV and an electron mobility exceeding 0.89 cm2 V−1 s−1. In 2024, the group further systematically investigated the structure–property relationship of conjugation extension directions, confirming that polymer 82 with B ← N units extending along the backbone is comprehensively superior to perpendicularly extended structures in terms of energy level modulation, backbone planarity, crystallinity and mobility.73 The optimized system achieves an electron mobility of 1.81 cm2 V−1 s−1, clarifying the molecular design principles for this class of polymers.

DPP-based B ← N functionalized polymers

The half-fused B ← N functionalized DPP-based polymers 83 and 84 deliver a maximum electron mobility of 0.034 cm2 V−1 s−1 and retain favorable performance on flexible PET substrates.74 The mobility of flexible devices fabricated by blade coating is further increased to 0.06 cm2 V−1 s−1, demonstrating application potential for large-scale solution processing.

Among six-membered heterocyclic B ← N polymers, those based on BNBP units represent the most classic and systematically investigated family. Through the synergistic modulation of comonomers and substituents, they enable continuously tunable energy levels, catering to the requirements of diverse optoelectronic devices, including organic photovoltaics and field-effect transistors.

Pyridine-based B ← N functionalized polymer

The development of pyridine-based polymers follows two main trajectories: energy level tuning and performance optimization. In 2016, the Liu group laid the foundation for this system by synthesizing a BNBP-based conjugated polymer (88) and verifying its application potential in all-polymer solar cells, achieving a power conversion efficiency of 3.38%.75 In terms of energy level tuning, the group achieved continuous LUMO adjustment of polymers (89) from −3.32 eV to −3.71 eV via the synergistic modification of side-chain substituents and comonomers.76 Subsequently, the selenophene polymer 91 they developed further boosted the photovoltaic efficiency to 4.46%, confirming the optimization effect of comonomers on device performance.77 For mobility enhancement, the Liu group copolymerized BNBP with fluorinated benzothiadiazole.78 Intramolecular noncovalent interactions such as F⋯S and N⋯S strengthened backbone planarity, raising the electron mobility of the polymer 92 to 0.19 cm2 V−1 s−1. In 2021, Liu's group prepared the organotin derivative of BNBP and copolymerized it with thienopyrroledione,79 yielding two acceptor–acceptor (A–A) type polymers 93 and 94. These A–A polymers feature low LUMO energy levels of −4.43 and −4.02 eV, respectively.

Pyrazine-based B ← N functionalized polymers

Beyond BNBP, a variety of fused six-membered heterocyclic B ← N polymers have been gradually developed, further enriching the material library (Fig. 8). In 2019, the Huang group developed an electron-deficient B ← N-containing acceptor BNIDT and constructed two D–A conjugated polymers 95 and 96.80 The B ← N moiety converted the conjugated backbone from electron-rich to electron-deficient. Polymer 96 exhibited LUMO/HOMO levels of −3.79/−5.74 eV and bipolar charge transport behavior, with an electron mobility of 0.005 cm2 V−1 s−1 and a corresponding photovoltaic PCE of 8.78%. To elucidate the halogenation regulation mechanism for B ← N-based polymer acceptors, the Huang group subsequently synthesized three BDT-BNIDT polymers (97) to systematically compare fluorination and chlorination.81 With number-average molecular weights (Mn) of 22.3–29.6 kDa, all three polymers maintained nearly identical LUMO levels (−3.77 eV) dominated by the BNIDT unit, while chlorination outperformed fluorination in downshifting the HOMO level. Electron mobilities increased gradually with halogenation from 1.00 × 10−5 to 1.42 × 10−5 cm2 V−1 s−1. Device efficiency increased from 1.60% (non-halogenated) to 3.71% (fluorinated) and further to 4.23% (chlorinated), verifying chlorination as a more effective modification strategy. In 2020, the Huang group fabricated two D–A polymers 98 by copolymerizing BNIDT with highly coplanar vinyl units and tuned their aggregation properties via methyl side-chain engineering.82 With similar Mn (∼16.6 kDa), the vinyl-extended conjugated backbone red-shifted the absorption to the near-infrared region, while the pendant phenyl groups suppressed excessive aggregation to ensure good solution processability. When paired with donor PTB1 for complementary absorption, the 114a-based device delivered a short-circuit current density (Jsc) of 16.90 mA cm−2. In 2022, the Liu group designed resonant N–B ← N acceptors. After polymerization, the resulting polymers 99 and 100 showed lowered LUMO levels of −3.55 and −3.46 eV, compared with −3.15 eV for the monomer.83 Further cyano substitution on the boron center significantly enhanced the electron-deficiency, yielding polymers 101 and 102 with a sharply reduced LUMO level from −2.38 eV to −4.55 eV.84

Schiff base-derived B ← N functionalized polymers

Schiff base skeletons bearing imine bonds represent an emerging platform for constructing B ← N-coordinated electron-deficient polymers. Benefiting from the bidentate chelating structure formed by N and O heteroatoms, they enable deep-level regulation while enhancing coordination stability, which aligns well with the structure–property relationships of B ← N coordination systems. In 2024, the group of Kazuo Tanaka developed a strongly electron-deficient acceptor unit based on trifluoromethylated boron-fused azobenzene.85 The D–A-type polymers (103–106) obtained therefrom exhibit a LUMO energy level as low as −4.34 eV and a stable HOMO level in the range of −5.28 to −5.84 eV, combining excellent resistance to oxidative photodegradation with a second NIR-II photoresponse. Our group constructed N, O-bidentate BF2/B(CN)2-type strongly electron-deficient acceptors.86 The former enhances π-π stacking via extended conjugation, while the latter maximizes electron deficiency by combining the synergistic electron-withdrawing effects of B ← N coordination and cyano groups.87–90 The corresponding polymers 107–110 possess LUMO energy levels ranging from −3.96 to −4.01 eV, among which polymer 109 achieves an electron mobility of 3.04 cm2 V−1 s−1. The cyano-modified polymer 110 breaks through the stability bottleneck of B ← N systems, retaining 95% of its initial performance after two months of storage in high-humidity air while simultaneously delivering an electrical conductivity of 95.5 S cm−1 and a maximum power factor of 147.8 µW m−1 K−2.

Applications of BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics

The variations in device performance observed among different BN-embedded materials can be fundamentally traced back to the structural regulation mechanisms outlined in Section 2.2, which collectively dictate charge injection, charge transport, and device stability. Benefiting from their deep-lying LUMO energy levels, tunable optoelectronic properties, and favourable chemical stability, BN-embedded electron-deficient materials have emerged as promising candidates for a wide range of mainstream organic optoelectronic applications. In this section, we highlight representative progress in B–N-functionalized acceptor materials across five major device platforms, with a particular focus on device-level performance metrics and the distinctive advantages arising from B–N structural engineering.

BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics for OFETs

BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics have emerged as highly promising semiconducting materials for organic field-effect transistors (OFETs), as they enable the synergistic optimization of charge injection, charge transport, and processing compatibility. The substantial downshift of their LUMO energy levels promotes favourable energy-level matching with metal source/drain electrodes, thereby lowering electron-injection barriers and facilitating efficient ohmic contact formation. At the same time, the strong electron-withdrawing nature of BN-embedded units suppresses hole transport, affording pronounced unipolar n-type characteristics with enhanced on/off current ratios and reliable switching behaviour. Beyond these intrinsic electronic advantages, BN-embedded systems also offer exceptional structural versatility, allowing frontier orbital energies, optical absorption, and charge-transport characteristics to be finely tuned through substituent engineering, conjugated-backbone modulation, copolymerization with electron-deficient comonomers, and side-chain design. Collectively, these features endow BN-functionalized materials with excellent solution processability and make them particularly attractive for low-cost, large-area fabrication of flexible and printed electronic devices.

BN-embedded electron-deficient small molecules for OFETs

For covalent B–N-based n-type small molecules in OFET applications, Pei's group developed a series of imide-fused B–N acceptors. In 2022, as reported by the Pei group,35 covalent B–N embedded PDI derivative 2 delivered a stable LUMO of −3.7 eV and a single-crystal electron mobility of 0.35 cm2 V−1 s−1. In 2025, the team extended this system to B–N fused TDI derivatives 3–7,36 whose LUMOs ranged from −3.69 to −3.76 eV with a maximum mobility of 0.039 cm2 V−1 s−1. Also in 2025, BN-hybridized PDI 8–11 (ref. 37) was reported with a LUMO of −3.76 eV and an elevated mobility of 0.57 cm2 V−1 s−1. Apart from covalent B–N systems, B ← N-coordinated small molecules with tunable deep LUMOs and planar skeletons are promising n-type candidates for OFETs. As early as 2006, Yamaguchi's group introduced B ← N moieties into the thienothiazole scaffold. Compound 20 afforded a mobility of 1.5 × 10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1 and pioneered relevant device research. In 2011, the Zhang group proposed an O–B ← N tri-chelation strategy to construct diboron-bridged ladder aromatics 25a (3.2 × 10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1) and 26b (8.3 × 10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1).43 They further expanded O–B ← N organoboron complexes to two subfamilies, among which 27a and 27b reached mobilities of 7.7 × 10−4 and 2.2 × 10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1, validating the versatility of this chelation design.45 In 2019, Liu's group synthesized disk-shaped tetrakis-B ← N fused arene 71bvia one-pot boryl cyclization. With a LUMO of −4.07 eV, single-crystal OFETs of 71b achieved a record mobility of 1.6 cm2 V−1 s−1 (Fig. 9).62 In 2022, the Zhang group developed two dimeric aza-BODIPY species: isoindigo-based 41 exhibited stable unipolar n-type transport with a mobility of 0.11 cm2 V−1 s−1 in BGBC devices,53 while naphthodipyrrolopyrrole-based dimer 43 (ref. 72) delivered a higher mobility of 0.18 cm2 V−1 s−1.

BN-embedded electron-deficient polymers for OFETs

Building on B ← N small-molecule OFET progress, B ← N-based n-type conjugated polymers stand out for their solution processability and tunable optoelectronics, with systematic advances in electron mobility. In 2016, Liu's group reported three polymers 74–76 with LUMOs of −3.80, −3.57 and −3.73 eV.66 Isoindigo-containing polymer 75 (2.80 × 10−5 cm2 V−1 s−1) far outperformed 74 (3.40 × 10−7 cm2 V−1 s−1), while DPP-based copolymer 76 reached 8.48 × 10−6 cm2 V−1 s−1, revealing comonomer modulation effects.91–93 Halogenated derivatives 97a (pristine), 97b (fluorinated) and 97c (chlorinated) retained identical LUMOs (−3.77 eV) but showed rising mobilities of 1.00 × 10−5, 1.37 × 10−5 and 1.42 × 10−5 cm2 V−1 s−1, proving that chlorination optimizes packing.81 In 2020, Liu's D–A polymer 92 (ref. 78) combining BNBP and 2T-2fBT units possessed a LUMO of −3.65 eV and delivered an electron mobility of 0.19 cm2 V−1 s−1. In 2023, Zhang adopted NDP-AB aza-BODIPY units to produce polymers 81 (LUMO = −4.24 eV) and 82 (LUMO = −4.26 eV),73 both with mobility up to 0.89 cm2 V−1 s−1. Asymmetric semi-fused B ← N-DPP polymers74 were also synthesized, where 84 showed 0.034 cm2 V−1 s−1 and 83 exhibited 3.8 × 10−3 cm2 V−1 s−1. Our group has made simultaneous breakthroughs in mobility and ambient stability. In 2024, N,O-bidentate BF2-type monomers yielded polymers 107–110 (LUMOs: −3.96–−4.01 eV).86 OFETs of 109 achieved 3.04 cm2 V−1 s−1. In 2025, asymmetric semi-fused B ← N benzothiadiazole building blocks were used to prepare polymer 79b with a LUMO of −3.96 eV, a unipolar n-type mobility of 3.85 cm2 V−1 s−1, and robust mechanical and ambient stability.70

BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics for OPVs

Beyond their use in OFETs, BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics have increasingly emerged as attractive acceptor platforms for organic photovoltaics (OPVs). This promise arises from the distinctive ability of BN incorporation to simultaneously reinforce electron deficiency, depress LUMO energy levels, and modulate optical and supramolecular properties within a unified molecular framework. Such features are particularly valuable for OPVs, where efficient photon harvesting, appropriate donor/acceptor energy-level matching, rapid exciton dissociation, and balanced charge transport must be delicately coordinated.94–96 In this context, the incorporation of covalent B–N or dative B ← N motifs provides a powerful means to tailor frontier orbital energies, bandgaps, absorption characteristics, and intermolecular interactions through backbone fusion, substituent engineering, and molecular symmetry control.97 Moreover, the intrinsic bond polarization associated with BN units can exert a pronounced influence on molecular packing and blend morphology, thereby affecting charge separation, carrier extraction, and recombination dynamics in bulk-heterojunction active layers. Collectively, these characteristics render BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics a versatile and highly promising molecular family for the development of next-generation OPV acceptors.

In 2022, Duan's group constructed covalent B–N-containing A–D–A acceptor 12 (LUMO = −4.07 eV), and PM6:12-based OPVs achieved a PCE of 8.3%.38 To improve hydrolysis resistance, Huang's group developed boron-arylated B–N-indacenodithiophene building blocks 69 and 70 (LUMO: −3.61 and −3.72 eV), corresponding OPVs reached PCEs of 0.15% and 0.23% with PBDB-T donors.61 Following advances in B–N functionalized small-molecule OPV acceptors, B ← N-type conjugated polymers stand as core OPV materials thanks to scalable solution processing, good film-forming capacity, and widely tunable energy levels/absorption. Relevant studies cover donor/acceptor designs targeting high efficiency and stability. In 2016, the Liu group reported B ← N functionalized thienothiazole-based polymers. In particular, polymer 75 raised all-PSC PCE from 0.12% to 5.04%.66 They also proposed BNBP building blocks: the monomer had a LUMO of −3.19 eV, and polymer 88 reached −3.50 eV with 3.38% all-PSC efficiency.75 In 2017, BNBP-based D–A polymers 90 (thiophene copolymer, LUMO = −3.72 eV) and 91 (selenophene copolymer, LUMO = −3.73 eV) afforded PCEs of 3.77% and 4.46% with PTB7-Th donors, proving that heteroatom substitution benefits photovoltaic performance.77,98–100 In 2019, random copolymer 77 integrating asymmetric B ← N-thienothiazole and pyridyl-DPP served as a universal acceptor, delivering 6.60% PCE when matched with three different donors. Its amorphous nature led to crystallization and phase separation of polymer donors (Fig. 10), offering a new route to regulate active-layer morphology.67 In 2019, the Huang group developed the B ← N-containing BNIDT monomer (LUMO = −3.79 eV, HOMO = −5.74 eV); its polymer 96 delivered a PCE of 8.78%.80 Halogenated derivatives 97a–97c retained identical LUMOs (−3.77 eV) but showed rising efficiencies: pristine 97a (1.60%), fluorinated 97b (3.71%), chlorinated 97c (4.23%), confirming that halogenation optimizes packing and morphology.81 In 2020, vinyl-bridged BNIDT D–A polymers 98a and 98b extended absorption to 900 nm, with devices reaching a PCE of 7.25%.82

BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics for OTEs

Beyond OFETs and OPVs, BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics have also emerged as highly promising candidates for organic thermoelectrics (OTEs), as they offer a compelling molecular solution to several long-standing limitations of conventional n-type systems. In particular, BN incorporation provides a versatile means to reinforce electron deficiency, depress LUMO energy levels, and regulate backbone polarity and intermolecular interactions, all of which are crucial for improving n-type doping efficiency and charge transport. These features are especially valuable in OTEs, where high electrical conductivity, a favorable Seebeck coefficient, and sufficient ambient stability must be achieved in a delicately balanced manner.101–103 At the same time, the intrinsic electronic polarization and structural tunability associated with covalent B–N and dative B ← N motifs create broad opportunities to optimize carrier concentration, energy-dependent transport, and solid-state organization through substituent engineering, backbone fusion, and side-chain modulation. Moreover, the enhanced chemical robustness and solution-processability accessible in many BN-embedded frameworks further strengthen their appeal for practical thermoelectric applications. Collectively, these characteristics position BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics as an emerging and versatile molecular platform for the development of high-performance n-type OTE materials.

In 2021, the Liu group reported dual B ← N-functionalized dibenzoazapentacene 71c for organic thermoelectrics.104 Its deep LUMO enabled efficient n-doping with N-DMBI. After optimal doping, it achieved a conductivity of 0.01 S cm−1 and a power factor of 0.06 µW m−1 K−2, with a negative Seebeck coefficient (−244.4 µV K−1) that verifies unipolar n-type transport (Fig. 11). This work proves that B ← N-fused azaacenes are promising n-type thermoelectric candidates. The Liu group studied a B ← N functionalized thienothiazole-isoindigo polymer for thermoelectrics in 2020.68 Doped with 5 wt% N-DMBI, it reached a conductivity of 9.7 × 10−4 S cm−1 and a power factor of 0.02 µW m−1 K−1 with a Seebeck coefficient of −453.8 µV K−1. Further breakthroughs came from our work on N,O-bidentate BF2-type building blocks DBNF and DBNCN.86 The corresponding polymer 110 delivered record-high conductivity (95.5 S cm−1) and a peak power factor of 147.8 µW m−1 K−2, which is the result of its decent electron transport and deep LUMO energy level.105–108

BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics for OLEDs

Beyond their roles in charge-transport and energy-conversion devices, BN-incorporated emitters have emerged as a particularly compelling class of luminescent materials for organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs). Their appeal stems from the distinctive electronic perturbation introduced by BN units, which enables precise modulation of excited-state characteristics, frontier orbital distributions, and emission profiles within rigid conjugated frameworks. Such features are especially advantageous for OLED applications, where high color purity, suppressed non-radiative loss, and reduced efficiency roll-off must be achieved in concert. In this context, BN incorporation offers a powerful molecular strategy to narrow emission bandwidths, stabilize emissive states, and regulate singlet–triplet energy relationships, thereby supporting both high-efficiency and spectrally well-defined electroluminescence. At the same time, the structural versatility of BN-functionalized frameworks provides broad opportunities to tailor emission color across a wide spectral range through rational control of conjugation patterns, substitution modes, and molecular symmetry. Collectively, these characteristics render BN-incorporated emitters a highly promising molecular platform for the development of next-generation OLED materials spanning both narrowband and full-spectrum light-emitting applications.

In 2011, the Zhang group reported that O–B ← N chelated complexes 26a and 26b served as dual emission/electron transport layers in undoped simplified OLEDs.109 Devices of 26a delivered standard red emission (628 nm, CIE 0.64, 0.36) with peak brightness 3704 cd m−2, while 26b realized deep-red/NIR emission at 680 nm. Later in 2015, the Turner group synthesized benzothiadiazole-fused boron heterocycles 30g and 30h, which featured robust air/water resistance and deep-red luminescence, leading to solution-processed OLEDs with an EQE of 0.48.110 In 2017, the Ingleson group adopted post-polymerization borylation to obtain NIR-emitting polymer 78. The 10% doped film emitted at 688 nm with an EQE of 0.18 and trivial efficiency roll-off at high current density, and its 20% counterpart red-shifted EL to 716 nm with a higher maximum EQE of 0.41 (Fig. 12).69

BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics for OPDs

Short-wave infrared (SWIR, 1000–3000 nm) photodetection represents an increasingly important frontier in optoelectronics, owing to its broad relevance to imaging, optical communication, environmental monitoring, and wearable sensing. To date, this spectral region has been dominated by inorganic semiconductors such as InGaAs and Ge; however, their widespread deployment remains constrained by high manufacturing cost, limited mechanical compliance, and poor compatibility with lightweight, large-area, and flexible device platforms. In this context, BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics have emerged as a particularly attractive organic alternative, as BN incorporation provides an effective molecular strategy for constructing narrow-bandgap acceptor frameworks with deep-lying frontier orbital energies and extended long-wavelength absorption. By reinforcing electron deficiency while preserving structural tunability, conjugation engineering flexibility, and solution processability, these BN-functionalized systems help address the long-standing scarcity of SWIR-active organic semiconductors. Moreover, their intrinsically tailorable electronic structures and favorable film-forming characteristics offer distinct opportunities to optimize spectral response, charge generation, and operational stability in flexible SWIR photodetectors. Collectively, these features position BN-embedded electron-deficient aromatics as a promising molecular platform for the development of next-generation organic SWIR photodetection technologies.

In 2023, Liu's group developed thieno-fused BODIPY oligomers 44–47.54 OPDs blending tetramer 46 with PBDB-T achieved a detectivity of 2.98 × 1011 Jones at 1180 nm. In 2026, the Liu group constructed compound 48 featuring a resonant N–B ← N framework.55 This structural motif effectively boosts electron delocalization and molecular dipole interactions within the conjugated skeleton, red-shifting the thin-film absorption cutoff to 1450 nm and delivering an ultra-narrow optical bandgap of 0.86 eV (Fig. 13). Photodetectors based on the PBDB-T:48 blend exhibited a sensitive photoresponse across the broad spectral range of 300–1400 nm. At 1200 nm, the device achieved a responsivity of 0.18 A W−1 and a specific detectivity as high as 3.93 × 1011 Jones. The Liu group also prepared BNBP-based oligomers 54–58; devices using oligomer 57 paired with PM6 showed viable visible-light detection against PC61BM.57

While significant progress has been made in BN-based organic semiconductors, a substantial gap still exists between these materials and the well-recognized benchmark n-type molecular systems in the field.111–113 For example, n-type NDI-based polymers114 have achieved electron mobilities as high as 7.16 cm2 V−1 s−1. DPP-based polymers generally exhibit excellent performance,115 with their peak electron mobility exceeding 5 cm2 V−1 s−1. In addition, BTI-based n-type polymers have also demonstrated impressive performance in recent years,116 reaching an electron mobility of 3.71 cm2 V−1 s−1. Although the highest electron mobility reported for BN-based polymers has reached the level of 3.85 cm2 V−1 s−1, the overall material system still lags far behind the mature NDI and DPP systems in terms of comprehensive device performance, ambient stability, and device lifetime investigations.117–120 This gap represents a critical direction for future breakthroughs in this research area.99,121,122

Conclusion and outlook

This review has systematically surveyed electron-deficient aromatic systems incorporating covalent B–N and dative B ← N motifs, spanning molecular design, synthetic strategies, property modulation and optoelectronic device applications, and has thus established an integrated framework from molecular construction to device implementation. Both types of BN incorporation offer continuous and precise control over conjugated electronic structures, frontier orbital energies and electron-deficient character, while largely preserving π-conjugation. Importantly, BN functionalization can also enhance tolerance to air and moisture, thereby alleviating the longstanding trade-off between strong electron affinity and ambient stability. These advantages make BN-containing motifs highly attractive building blocks for the development of n-type organic semiconductors. To date, BN-functionalized molecular materials have enabled notable progress in OFETs, OPVs, OTEs, OLEDs and OPDs, with steadily improved charge-carrier mobility and device efficiency. Despite these advances, their overall transport performance, long-term operational stability and synthetic/manufacturing scalability still lag behind those of their p-type counterparts, which remains a major obstacle to practical implementation and industrial translation.

Looking forward, three major research directions merit particular attention. First, greater efforts should be devoted to the development of structurally diverse BN-containing architectures, including multi-BN functionalization, asymmetric frameworks and fused skeletons featuring synergistic main-chain/side-chain engineering. Such strategies may help overcome the mobility–stability trade-off while promoting more efficient synthetic routes. Second, the combination of density functional theory, molecular dynamics simulations and multiscale in situ characterization techniques should be further exploited to elucidate how BN bonding modes, embedding positions and substituent effects govern electronic structure, intermolecular packing, charge transport and energy transfer. Establishing such structure–morphology–performance correlations will be crucial for the rational design of next-generation materials. Third, more attention should be paid to low-cost and scalable synthesis, as well as large-area solution-processing technologies, alongside validation in practical application scenarios such as flexible, wearable and building-integrated optoelectronics, in order to accelerate the transition of BN-functionalized organic semiconductors from laboratory studies to real-world deployment.

In summary, owing to its distinctive capability for electronic structure modulation and its exceptional versatility in molecular design, BN functionalization has emerged as a central molecular engineering strategy for advancing n-type organic semiconductors. With the continued development of new BN-containing electron-deficient acceptor units, deeper mechanistic understanding, and progressive breakthroughs in scalable manufacturing, BN-functionalized organic semiconductors are expected to show increasingly broad prospects in next-generation flexible, wearable and sustainable electronic and optoelectronic technologies.
