Protocol for detecting cytosol-retained proopiomelanocortin by fractionation and co-immunoprecipitation assay
Authors: Sang-Hyeon Mun, Cheol-Sang Hwang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummarySignal peptide (SP)-uncleaved proopiomelanocortin (POMC) accumulates in the cytosol when ER translocation or protein quality control is perturbed. Here, we present a protocol to prepare and validate a POMC SP-specific antibody. We then use this antibody to detect cytosol-retained SP-uncleaved POMC in mammalian cells. We describe steps for using sequential digitonin-based semi-permeabilization followed by Triton X-100 lysis to isolate cytosolic and organelle fractions. We then detect cytosolic POMC by immunoblotting upon genetic depletion or enrich it by SP-specific immunoprecipitation.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Mun et al.1

Before you begin

Many eukaryotic secretory proteins can aberrantly accumulate in the cytosol as signal peptide–containing untranslocated precursors when ER translocation is disrupted, protein quality control is defective, or the signal peptide is mutated.2,3,4,5 Such cytosolic retention can exacerbate ER stress and promote cell death, contributing to proteostasis dysfunction associated with metabolic disorders and neurodegeneration.2,6 Despite the pathophysiological relevance of cytosol-accumulated secretory proteins, their detection has been technically challenging due to their limited stability and restricted subcellular accessibility.

This protocol describes the generation of SP-specific POMC antibody (anti-POMCSP) and the detection of cytosol-retained SP-uncleaved proopiomelanocortin in HEK293T cells transiently expressing C-terminally HA-tagged human POMC by immunoprecipitation followed by immunoblotting. The protocol is also optimized for selective cytosolic release and can be adapted to other mammalian cell lines, including mouse hypothalamic N43/5 cells, which endogenously express POMC, as well as for endogenous protein detection under genetic depletion of relevant quality control factors, provided that digitonin concentration and incubation time are appropriately optimized.

All reagents and treated cells should be prepared in advance, and freshly prepared, ice-cold buffers are required for efficient fractionation. Cell lysates and subcellular fractions must be maintained at 4°C throughout the workflow to minimize proteolytic degradation. The workflow includes the generation and validation of a signal peptide–specific antibody, same-host IgG controls, fraction-purity controls, and its application in detecting cytosol-retained C-terminally HA-tagged POMC in HEK293T cells treated with eeyarestatin I (EerI) as an ER translocon inhibitor.

Innovation

Previous approaches to analyzing cytosol-exposed or mislocalized secretory proteins have largely relied on indirect topology assays, in which cytosolic exposure is inferred from antibody accessibility, pulse-labeling, and sensitivity to glycosylation or enzymatic digestion under semi-permeabilized versus fully solubilized conditions.7 These approaches are useful for topology assessment but often require radiolabeling and multi-step interpretation, and they do not directly enrich SP-uncleaved precursor forms from complex lysates.

This protocol advances existing biochemical approaches by combining SP-specific antibody development and validation with digitonin-based selective cytosolic release, immunoprecipitation, and immunoblotting. This workflow separates cytosolic and organelle-associated fractions, applies fraction-purity controls, and enriches the SP-retaining precursor forms from the cytosolic fraction, reducing reliance on indirect topological inference. The step-by-step format also explains how to design an SP-containing epitope, perform sequential negative and positive antibody selection for in-house antibody purification, and validate the antibody by immunoblotting and immunoprecipitation before use. The workflow is compatible with epitope-tagged constructs and, with optimization of antibody specificity and fractionation conditions, can be extended to endogenous protein detection. More broadly, this protocol can serve as a practical framework for detecting other cytosol-retained secretory precursors, from SP-specific antibody development to biochemical detection.

This section describes the preparation of a POMC signal peptide–specific antibody (anti-POMCSP). The workflow includes generation of rabbit polyclonal antisera, coupling of purified GST-fused POMC fragments to an affinity matrix for sequential negative and positive affinity selection to enrich signal peptide–specific antibodies, and final purification using Protein A agarose. Step-by-step method details

Cytosol-organelle fractionation

Timing: 1–2 h

Timing: 1–2 h

This method describes cytosol–organelle fractionation in HEK293T cells expressing POMCha and in N43/5 cells for analysis of endogenous POMC (Figure 2A). Perform all steps at 4°C and use freshly harvested cell pellets from the preceding step. Digitonin at low concentration selectively permeabilizes the plasma membrane while leaving the ER and other membrane-bound organelles intact. The released supernatant is collected as the cytosolic fraction.1.Collect the cytosolic fraction.a.Resuspend the cell pellet in ice-cold semi-permeabilization buffer (0.5 mL for N43/5 cells or 1 mL for HEK293T cells) by gentle pipetting to break up clumps.b.Incubate the cell suspension for 5 min on ice (see troubleshooting 4 and 5).Note: The volume of semi-permeabilization buffer and incubation time may be adjusted depending on the cell type and experimental conditions.CRITICAL: Avoid vortexing. Excessive agitation or prolonged incubation on ice (>5 min) can compromise fraction integrity.c.Centrifuge at 500 × g for 10 min at 4°C.d.Carefully transfer the supernatant to a pre-chilled tube as the cytosolic fraction.e.Gently resuspend the pellet in 0.5 mL of ice-cold PBS to remove residual digitonin.f.Centrifuge at 500 × g for 5 min at 4°C.g.Discard the supernatant.CRITICAL: Do not disturb the cell pellet.2.Collect the organelle fraction.a.Resuspend the washed pellet in ice-cold IP buffer (0.5 mL for N43/5 cells or 1 mL for HEK293T cells) by pipetting and incubate for 10 min on ice (see troubleshooting 4 and 5).Note: Pipette thoroughly to prevent cell clumping.b.Centrifuge at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4°C.c.Transfer the supernatant to a pre-chilled microcentrifuge tube as the organelle fraction.3.Preparation of whole-cell lysate (input).a.Using the reserved input aliquot (step 8f of before you begin), add 100 μL of RIPA buffer supplemented with 1× protease inhibitor cocktail, and incubate for 20 min on ice with occasional gentle mixing (see troubleshooting 5).b.Centrifuge at 13,500 × g for 15 min at 4°C.c.Transfer the supernatant to a pre-chilled microcentrifuge tube as the whole-cell lysate (input) sample.d.Measure the protein concentration in each fraction using a BCA protein assay kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 23227).e.Mix the whole-cell lysate (input for HEK293T cells) and each fraction (for N43/5 cells) separately with 4× SDS sample buffer to obtain a final 1× concentration.f.Adjust the final protein concentration to 1–2 μg/μL.g.Heat at 95°C for 5 min (or according to your immunoblot conditions). Expected outcomes

In the antibody validation experiment, anti-POMCSP is expected to detect SP-containing GST-POMC1–26 and GST-POMC1–76, but not signal peptide-deleted GST-POMC27–76, by immunoblotting (Figure 1B). For the immunoprecipitation validation, POMCha is expected to be enriched by anti-POMCSP, whereas normal rabbit IgG should produce little or no detectable signal (Figure 1C). In the representative validation shown here, 5 μg anti-POMCSP per mg of lysate provides a clear IP signal from POMCha-expressing lysates, supporting its use for cell-based enrichment of SP-uncleaved POMCha.

Cytosol–ER fractionation is expected to yield clearly separated cytosolic and ER fractions, as demonstrated by enrichment of compartment-specific marker proteins in each fraction (Figures 2B and 3B).

In Marchf6-KO N43/5 hypothalamic cells, endogenous POMC is expected to be detectable in the cytosolic fraction even in the absence of ER translocon inhibition (Figure 2B). Compared with wild-type cells, the cytosolic POMC signal should be increased, consistent with MARCHF6-dependent turnover of cytosol-retained POMC.1

Upon treatment with the ER translocon inhibitor EerI, POMCha is expected to be retained in the cytosol. Under these conditions, cytosolic POMCha corresponding to the signal peptide-containing precursor form should be detectable by immunoprecipitation with the anti-POMCSP antibody (Figure 3B).

Limitations

This protocol relies on the signal peptide-specific antibody, anti-POMCSP, to selectively enrich SP-uncleaved POMC. Because anti-POMCSP is not commercially available, each newly prepared the antibody batch should be validated before downstream immunoprecipitation. The validation steps, including immunoblotting of GST-POMC fragments and cell-based immunoprecipitation, are intended to assess antibody specificity and enrichment performance. However, differences in antibody titer, background binding, or epitope accessibility may still affect detection sensitivity between antibody batches.

This protocol is optimized for HEK293T cells expressing POMCha and for N43/5 cells used to detect endogenous POMC. Application to other cell types, low-abundance endogenous proteins, or other secretory precursors may require optimization of cell number, drug treatment, digitonin concentration, and antibody amount. For POMCha detection, EerI and MG-132 are used to enrich cytosol-retained SP-uncleaved POMCha. Thus, this protocol is primarily intended for detecting the enriched cytosol-retained pool, rather than for quantitatively measuring basal steady-state abundance.
