Ginger oil boosts sini decoction's protective effects on lung injury caused by acute cold stress through reshaping lung microbiota structure in broilers
Authors: Yaxian Yang, Dan Liu, Xicui Du, Yanling Sun, Yun Wang, Xinghua Zhao, Xin He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Acute cold stress (ACS) is a widespread concern in poultry farming. It not only severely reduces the growth performance of broilers but also affects the function of respiratory system, especially the lungs. Sini Decoction (SND) is a classic herbal formula for warming yang and dispelling cold. The oil-enriched Sini Decoction (SNDO) which is characterized by high concentration of 6-gingerol is an experimental formulation modified from SND via technological modification. A total of 96 one-day-old, white-feathered broilers were randomly assigned to four groups: control (CON) group, ACS group, SND group, and SNDO group. The protective effects and molecular mechanisms of SND and SNDO were evaluated by assessing growth performance, histopathological analysis, TUNEL staining, lung wet/dry weight ratio, inflammatory factor levels, antioxidant capacity, relative expression of pulmonary barrier‑related genes, and lung microbiota composition. Compared with the ACS group, the SND group exhibited significantly higher ADG, while the SNDO group exhibited significantly higher ADG and lower FCR (P < 0.05). The SND and SNDO groups alleviated the histopathological alterations and pulmonary cell apoptosis in the lung tissue. The SNDO group also significantly decreased the lung wet/dry weight ratio, the levels of IL-1β and IL-6, increased the level of IL-10, and upregulated the mRNA expression of tight junction proteins (occludin, ZO-1, and claudin-1) in the lung tissue (P < 0.05). SNDO significantly reduced the content of MDA and enhanced the activity of SOD (P < 0.05). Furthermore, SND and SNDO improved the α and β diversity of the lung microbiota, increased the abundances of Firmicutes, Faecalibacterium, and Lactobacillus, and decreased the abundances of Proteobacteria and Cupriavidus. Additionally, the enhanced effects of SNDO treatment were more significant in regulating growth performance, pulmonary apparent indices and lung microbiota. In conclusion, SND and SNDO alleviated ACS-induced lung injury, with SNDO exhibiting enhanced efficacy.

Introduction

Cold stress is a major stress factor affecting the global animal husbandry industry and is particularly prevalent in cold regions worldwide. It not only impairs the physiological health and welfare of livestock and poultry but also undermines the stability of production systems, posing widespread challenges to the sector (Zhang et al., 2016). Acute cold stress (ACS) occurs when the ambient temperature drops suddenly by more than 10 °C with an exposure duration of 2-24 h (Zhao et al., 2014; Liu et al., 2022). ACS can significantly inhibit the growth performance of broilers (Zhou et al., 2021), lead to the lipid metabolism disorder of the liver (Zhang et al., 2024), and damage the structure of the intestine (Zhao et al., 2013). In severe cases, it can lead to a mortality rate as high as 31.9% (Ncho et al., 2025). Cold stress elevates energy requirements in broilers, as the birds must expend additional metabolic energy to maintain thermoregulation. In addition, cold stress induces the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which further exacerbate oxidative stress and endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) (Ahmadipour et al., 2025).

The lung is a spongy, non-lobular parenchymal organ that adheres closely to the dorsal thoracic wall. Its specialized thin-walled alveolar structure facilitates efficient gas exchange. However, this structure has no buffering capacity, making broilers highly susceptible to cold stress (Shukla et al., 2016). It has been reported that cold stress can aggravate the susceptibility to respiratory infection and induce a variety of respiratory symptoms, such as pneumonia and acute lung injury (Gasparrini et al., 2015; Joo et al., 2016). Inflammatory cell infiltration, inflammatory exudation, and alveolar structure disorder were observed in lungs when broilers experienced cold stress. In addition, the levels of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), the contents of hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) and malondialdehyde (MDA) were all increased, while the activities of catalase (CAT) and superoxide dismutase (SOD) in lung were reduced (Wei et al., 2024). Chronic cold stress induced apoptosis in lung tissue cells by upregulating pro-apoptotic Bax and cleaved-caspase-3 while downregulating anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 (Liu et al., 2023). Furthermore, a study of Lawrence et al. showed that exposing rabbits to a 4 °C environment for 6 h significantly increased the abundance of Proteus in their lungs, indicating that low temperatures disrupt the lung microbiota (Lawrence et al., 2019).

Sini decoction (SND), first recorded in Zhang Zhongjing's Treatise on Shanghan Lun, was a classic traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) formula consisting of Aconitum carmichaelii Debx. (Aconiti Lateralis Radix Praeparata, Fuzi), Zingiber officinale Rosc. (Zingiberis Rhizoma, Ganjiang), and Glycyrrhiza uralensis Fisch. (Glycyrrhizae Radix et Rhizoma Praeparata cum Melle, Zhigancao) (Gu et al., 2024). In this formula, Fuzi serves as the sovereign herb, primarily functioning to warm the kidney to restore yang. Ganjiang acts as the minister herb, functioning to warm the spleen and dispel cold. Zhigancao plays the role of the assistant herb, mainly replenishing qi by regulating the middle energizer and harmonizing the actions of all constituent herbs (Yan et al., 2024). The formula exerts a synergistic effect of warming the middle energizer to dispel cold and restoring yang to rescue from collapse (Chen et al., 2022). Clinically, it was used for treating symptoms including cold limbs, faint pulse, and yang exhaustion with desertion (Tang et al., 2018). In colorectal cancer (CRC) mice, SND can upregulate the expression of CD8+ T lymphocytes and Occludin in the colonic mucosal layer, inhibit the expression of CD4+ T cells and inflammatory cytokines (Wang et al., 2021). In a cecal ligation and puncture (CLP)-induced sepsis mouse model, SND could ameliorate the symptoms and pathological damage associated with sepsis by recovering the microbial richness and abundance (Wang et al., 2020). Furthermore, SND improved immune function, enhanced intestinal barrier integrity, and promoted growth performance by decreasing the levels of IL-1β, interleukin-6 (IL-6), and TNF-α. Meanwhile, SND upregulated the mRNA and protein levels of tight junction proteins and reshaped the cecal microbiota composition (Zhang et al., 2025).

As one of the important components of Ganjiang, ginger oil has antibacterial, anticancer, and antioxidant activities. However, it is highly susceptible to oxidative degradation upon exposure to oxygen, light, or heat (Hu et al., 2018). Furthermore, in mouse models of non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) induced by palm oil-containing high-fat diets combined with LPS injection, ginger oil elevated hepatic CAT activity and glutathione levels, thereby exerting protective effects (Panyod et al., 2024). In an NNK-induced lung cancer model in A/J mice, ginger oil reduced the inflammatory cell infiltration in lung tissue, decreased the mRNA and protein expressions of IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α, modulated gut microbiota composition (Ding et al., 2025). Furthermore, ginger oil decreased the levels of AST and urea, enhanced SOD activity and reduced MDA levels, and improved the histology of the intestine and liver (Abd El-Hack et al., 2024). Recently, a β-cyclodextrin inclusion complex of ginger oil was successfully prepared by our laboratory using inclusion technology. When this complex was formulated into SND, the stability of ginger oil was effectively enhanced (Wang et al., 2026). In addition, we have found that SNDO could significantly improve intestinal health in broilers subjected to ACS. It enhanced the activities of intestinal digestive enzymes, repaired intestinal villus damage, decreased crypt depth, and upregulated the expression of intestinal tight junction proteins, and its protective effects were superior to those of SND. However, as a main component of Ganjiang, whether ginger oil can enhance the effect of SND in alleviating lung injury remains unclear.

This research focused on evaluating the mitigating effects of SND and SNDO on ACS-induced lung injury in broilers. The potential regulatory mechanisms were further explored relying on in vivo experimental verification and lung microbiota analysis. These findings will provide theoretical and experimental basis for the application of SND and SNDO in alleviating ACS-induced lung injury in broilers.

Material and methods

Preparation of SND and SNDO

SND and SNDO were prepared following our previous methods (Wang et al., 2026). The final concentration of SND was 0.8 g/mL. SNDO was obtained by physical mixing of ginger oil-β-cyclodextrin inclusion complex with SND at a ratio of 11:89. The content of 6-gingerol in SNDO was 3.24 times higher than that of SND (Wang et al., 2026).

Animals, experimental design, and diets

To ensure the welfare of the animals, all experiments and animal procedures were officially approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Hebei Agricultural University (approval number 2024121). All experimental protocols were designed to minimize animal suffering. The trial was conducted using a random design. A total of 96 1-day-old white-feathered broilers were purchased from Hebei Huanshan Agricultural Development Co., Ltd. (Baoding, China). All broilers were reared in the animal house of the College of Veterinary Medicine in Hebei Agricultural University. They were housed in cages with the specification of H 0.45 m × W 0.4 m × D 0.4 m, with 8 broilers per cage. The broilers were randomly divided into four groups (three replicates per group and 8 broilers per replicate): the CON group, the ACS group, the SND group, and the SNDO group. The broilers had free access to drinking water and a corn-soybean basal diet, with a 12 h light/dark cycle, and the detailed formula and nutrient levels of the diet are shown in Table 1. The ambient temperature was maintained at 35 °C for the first 3 days, and then gradually reduced by 1 °C every two days until reaching 20 °C (Fig. 1). The experiment was conducted from 19 days to 33 days. The dosage was selected by referring to related academic literature (Zhang et al., 2025). The SND and SNDO groups were orally administered 1 mL of SND and SNDO per broiler, respectively, while the other groups were orally administered the same dose of physiological saline. Starting from 19 days of age at 20:00, all four groups were transferred to climate chambers at the same time, with only ambient temperature differing between groups. The ACS, SND, and SNDO groups were placed in a climate chamber with a temperature of 10 °C and humidity of 60%–80% for a 12-hour ACS treatment. The control group maintained normal temperature conditions. Growth performance

The feed intake and the residual feed of broilers in each group were recorded from 19 to 33 days to calculate the average daily feed intake (ADFI). Six broilers per group were randomly selected and weighed at d 19 and d 33 to calculate the average daily gain (ADG). Feed conversion ratio (FCR) was calculated for the experimental period and determined in accordance with Eq. (1) (Zhang et al., 2026).(1)FCR=ADFIADG

Sample collection

Sample collection was performed at d 24 and d 29. Two broilers from each replicate were randomly selected. Blood was collected from the wing vein and centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C to separate the serum. The broilers were then euthanized by cervical dislocation. The lung tissue was divided into three parts: the upper left was rinsed with ice-cold normal saline, the lower left was used for weight measurement, and the right lung tissue was wrapped in aluminum foil and stored at −80 °C for subsequent analysis.

Wet/dry weight ratio

The wet weight (g) of the lower left lung tissue was measured after the surface moisture was absorbed. Subsequently, these tissues were placed in an oven maintained at 60 °C for 48 h. The dry weight (g) of the tissues was measured. The W/D weight ratio was calculated (Lazrak et al., 2025).

Histopathological study and TUNEL staining analysis

The upper left lung tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 4 μm-thick slices. The sections were then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) solution. Histopathological changes were observed using an optical microscope (Eclipse E100, Nikon, Japan) (Guo et al., 2026)

Previously prepared paraffin-embedded lung sections were deparaffinized using Eco-Friendly Deparaffinization Solution (G1128, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) and 100% ethanol. The tissue was marked with a Histology Pen (G6100, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), and the assay was performed strictly according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Sections were incubated with Proteinase K working solution at 37 °C for 20 min, followed by the reaction mixture from the One-Step TUNEL Apoptosis Detection Kit (G1502/TMR red fluorescence, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), and incubated at 37°C in the dark for 60 minutes. Finally, apoptotic cells were observed and analyzed under an upright fluorescence microscope (Eclipse C1, Nikon, Japan) (Wei et al., 2023).

ELISA

The lung homogenates were prepared according to previously methods (Li et al., 2025). The levels of IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-10 in the lung homogenates and serum were determined strictly in accordance with the instructions of ELISA kits (Cat. No. YJ33339, YJ33335, and YJ33344, JuYuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The intra-assay and inter-assay coefficient of variation were both less than 15%.

Determination of antioxidant indicators in serum

The SOD activity (Cat. No. ml076328) and MDA content (Cat. No. ml093013) in serum were detected using reagent kits provided by Shanghai Yuanju Biotechnology Center (Shanghai, China).

Quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from lung tissue using the Bigfish Total RNA Purification Kit (BFMP07R, Hangzhou Bigfish Bio-tech Co., Ltd., Hangzhou, China) with a BFEX-32E automatic nucleic acid extraction system; RNA concentration and purity were measured using a microspectrophotometer with an A260/A280 ratio of 1.8–2.0 indicating acceptable quality, and RNA integrity was verified by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. Microbiome analysis

16S rRNA sequencing was used to analyze the diversity of lung microbiota in broilers, with bioinformatics analysis conducted targeting the V3–V4 variable region. Novogene Technology Co., Ltd. (Tianjin, China) undertook the sequencing work. Genomic DNA was extracted from broiler lung tissue in accordance with the CTAB method (Doyle and Doyle, 1987). The concentration of extracted DNA was quantified using a Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer (ThermoFisher Scientific, USA), and its integrity was assessed via 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. Illumina dual-end sequencing adapters and barcoded specific primers were employed to amplify the V3–V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene (341F: 5′-CCTAYGGGRBGCASCAG-3′, 806R: 5′-GGACTACNNGGGTATCTAAT-3′). After purification of the amplified products, sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, USA).

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using SPSS 27.0 software. Normality tests and Levene’s test for equality of variance were employed for all data. The inter-group and intra-group differences were analyzed using one-way ANOVA with Treatment as the between-subjects variable and Tukey or Games-Howell post-hoc tests as appropriate. The data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD), and differences were considered significant at P < 0.05.

Histopathological analysis

As shown in Fig. 2B, the lung tissue in the CON group exhibited regular alveolar structures with uniform alveolar wall thickness, orderly distribution, no obvious lesions, hyperplasia or fibrosis, and a clear lung tissue structure. In contrast, the ACS group exhibited inflammatory cell infiltration, with the boundaries of alveolar structures becoming blurred. Compared with the ACS group, both the SND group and the SNDO group showed a reduction in inflammatory cells, a significant recovery of alveolar structures, and an improvement in lung tissue lesions.

TUNEL cell apoptosis tests

TUNEL staining was performed to detect apoptotic cells in the lung tissue (Fig. 2C).

The results revealed that a significantly increase was shown in TUNEL-positive nuclei in ACS group compared to the CON group (P < 0.05). In addition, compared with the ACS group, the number of TUNEL-positive nuclei was significantly reduced in SND or SNDO groups (P < 0.05). Compared with the SND group, the number of TUNEL-positive nuclei decreased significantly in the SNDO group (P < 0.05). Overall, both SND and SNDO suppressed ACS-triggered lung cell apoptosis, and SNDO conferred superior protective efficacy.

Effects of SND and SNDO on serum antioxidant capacity of ACS broilers

As shown in Fig. 3G-H, the ACS group had higher MDA levels and lower SOD levels in serum compared with the CON group both at d 24 and d 29 (P < 0.05). There were significant differences between the SNDO group and the ACS group (P < 0.05), but there were no significant differences between the SND group and the ACS group (P > 0.05).

Relative expression of pulmonary barrier genes

During the pathogenesis of ACS, the pulmonary barrier was continuously damaged. This study evaluated the integrity of the pulmonary barrier by analyzing the expression levels of ZO-1 (zonula occludens-1), occludin and claudin-1 using qRT-PCR. Compared with the CON group, the expressions of ZO-1, occludin and claudin-1 were significantly downregulated in the ACS group at d 24 and d 29 (P < 0.05, Fig. 3I-K). However, the relative expression in the SND and SNDO groups were significantly upregulated at d 24. Additionally, at d 29, dramatic differences were not observed between the SNDO group and the CON group, which indicated that the therapeutic effects of SNDO were better than of SND.

SND and SNDO treatments reshaped the lung microbiota in ACS broilers

To better investigate the changes in lung microbiota of SND and SNDO, lung tissue samples from ACS broilers were collected and subjected to 16S rRNA sequencing. The Chao1 index, Simpson index, and observed features were significantly improved in the SND group, and they were significantly improved in the SNDO group compared with the ACS group at d 24 (P < 0.05, Fig. 4A-D). Discussion

As endothermic animals, broilers have adaptive mechanisms to resist cold stress and restore body temperature homeostasis. However, ACS exerts a negative impact on their growth performance and general health (Bi et al., 2024). Cold stress elevates the basal metabolic rate and increases energy metabolism in animals to maintain body homeostasis (Chen et al., 2014). Consequently, increased energy demand for heat production is the primary cause of reduced weight gain. The study has shown that cold stress at 10–15°C from 11 to 42 days of age significantly elevates FCR and reduces ADG and ADFI in Ross 308 broilers, with these adverse effects persisting throughout the entire rearing period (Mohebodini et al., 2025). Consistent with these findings, compared with the CON group, ADG and ADFI of the ACS group were significantly decreased, while FCR was significantly increased. This indicates that nutrients are redistributed from supporting growth to mediating thermoregulatory responses under cold stress.

SND is a representative formula for warming the cold and returning to yang (Chen et al., 2022). In this formula, Fuzi acts to warm the kidney and tonify yang, while Ganjiang focuses on warming the spleen and dispelling cold (Yan et al., 2024). As one of the primary chemical components of Ganjiang, ginger oil has antibacterial, anticancer, and antioxidant activities. However, it is highly susceptible to oxidative degradation upon exposure to oxygen, light, or heat (Hu et al., 2018). 6-Gingerol, the major bioactive constituent of ginger oil, significantly reduced the increased accumulation of inflammatory cells, alveolar hemorrhage, and pulmonary interstitial edema, and attenuated the lung wet/dry ratio in LPS-induced ALI rats. Additionally, it repressed oxidative stress and inflammatory reactions by reducing the contents of MDA, TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β, while increasing the contents of GSH and SOD in the lung (Pan et al., 2023). Accordingly, a β-cyclodextrin inclusion complex of ginger oil was successfully prepared by our laboratory using inclusion technology. When this complex was mixed with SND to obtain SNDO, the stability of ginger oil was effectively enhanced and the concentration of 6-gingerol in SNDO was significantly higher. Specifically, the 6-gingerol concentration was 0.029 ± 0.00047 mg/mL in SND and 0.094 ± 0.00223 mg/mL in SNDO, which was 3.24 times that in SND (Wang et al., 2026).

Cold stress increases mortality and morbidity, triggers oxidative stress, and induces inflammatory responses and immune dysfunction in broilers (Hangalapura et al., 2004; Su et al., 2018; Xue et al., 2021). Specifically, cold stress can directly cause hemodynamic changes, resulting in thrombosis, tissue ischemia and reperfusion, and inflammatory injury (Yu et al., 2016). Therefore, cold-induced target organ damage occurs first in tissues with abundant blood flow. The lungs, as the main organs in the pulmonary circulation, provide oxygen for the whole body and are the most sensitive organs to changes in blood flow (Joo et al., 2016). ACS induces prominent inflammatory cell infiltration, inflammatory exudation, and alveolar structure disorder in the lungs of broilers, and simultaneously upregulates the mRNA and protein expression levels of TNF-α and IL-1β, increases the content of MDA, and reduces the activity of SOD in lung tissues (Wei et al., 2024). SND exerts a synergistic effect of warming the middle energizer to dispel cold and restoring yang to rescue from collapse (Chen et al., 2022). Consistent with these findings, our study confirmed that both SND and SNDO decreased the lung wet/dry weight ratio and proinflammatory cytokine levels (IL-1β, IL-6), increased IL-10 levels, and upregulated the mRNA expression of tight junction proteins (occludin, ZO-1, claudin-1) in lung tissue. Additionally, both treatments alleviated histopathological alterations and pulmonary cell apoptosis, and regulated the lung microbiota, including enhancing α and β diversity, increasing the abundances of Firmicutes, Faecalibacterium, and Lactobacillus, and decreasing the abundances of Proteobacteria and Cupriavidus. However, SNDO showed markedly better effects on growth performance, pulmonary indexes, and lung microbiota modulation, which may be attributed to the higher concentration of 6‑gingerol in SNDO.

Both SND and SNDO relieved inflammatory responses in ACS model in broilers. However, only SNDO restored the levels of IL-1β, IL-6 and IL-10 in serum and lung tissues to normal ranges at d 24 and 29. These results indicated that SNDO had better effects. This may be attributed to the higher content of 6-gingerol in SNDO. Previous studies have demonstrated that 6-gingerol exerts a strong inhibitory effect on pro-inflammatory mediators and TLR4-mediated signaling, thereby producing potent anti-inflammatory effects (Kang et al., 2026).

Advances in sequencing and culture techniques have confirmed that the lung is colonized by diverse and dynamic bacteria rather than being sterile (Dickson et al., 2016). The composition and abundance of lung microbiota show differences under healthy and diseased states, and these changes correlate with respiratory and alveolar immune function (Ashley et al., 2020). Although less abundant than intestinal microbiota, lung microbiota modulates host immunity, and its imbalance increases the risk of respiratory diseases (Segal et al., 2014; de Steenhuijsen Piters et al., 2015). This study showed that cold stress reshaped the lung microbiota of broilers. At d 24, ACS markedly decreased α-diversity indices (Chao1, Shannon, Simpson and observed features), disrupting microbial richness and evenness. The results of this study are consistent with those of Zakari et al., whose research indicated that low temperature exposure significantly reduces α-diversity and disrupts community structure of the lung microbiota (Zakari et al., 2026).

At the phylum level, Firmicutes was a dominant beneficial bacterial phylum in the avian respiratory tract, and they contributed to mucosal barrier integrity and anti-inflammatory processes (Oakley et al., 2014; Zhou et al., 2023). Our results demonstrated that the relative abundance of Firmicutes were significantly lower in the ACS group than that of CON group. In contrast, both SND and SNDO treatments markedly elevated the relative abundance of this bacterial. This aligns with the findings of Đurić Jarić et al., whose study demonstrated that ginger extract supplementation could increase the abundance of Firmicutes in broilers (Đurić Jarić et al., 2025). Conversely, the phylum Proteobacteria was consistently associated with respiratory tract inflammation and pathogenicity in avian species (Hilty et al., 2010; Segal et al., 2016). High abundances of Proteobacteria were typically associated with exacerbated inflammatory responses and tissue injury (Rizzatti et al., 2017). In our study, this phylum exhibited a significantly higher relative abundance in the ACS group. Notably, SNDO intervention resulted in a more marked reduction, which may be attributed to the bioactive compounds in ginger oil. This finding was supported by previous research. Specifically, Ding et al. demonstrated that ginger oil modulates gut microbiota composition in an NNK-induced lung cancer model of A/J mice, with a particular reduction in the relative abundance of Proteobacteria, and this modulation is capable of suppressing inflammatory signaling pathways such as NF-κB (Ding et al., 2025). Furthermore, Proteobacteria activate Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) expressed in the intestinal tissues of mice, triggering the MAPK signaling pathway and subsequent intestinal inflammatory (Cao et al., 2021).

At the genus level, specific taxa exhibited distinctive responses to the interventions. Faecalibacterium prausnitzii, a species of Faecalibacterium spp., is recognized as an anti-inflammatory commensal bacterium. Its metabolites regulate the MAPK pathway, preserve intestinal barrier integrity, and alleviate inflammatory responses by modulating stress-related pathways (Martin et al., 2014). As reported in relevant studies, ginger ethanol extract (GEE) supplementation significantly increases the relative abundance of Faecalibacterium in the cecal microbiota of laying hens, and this elevation is positively correlated with enhanced serum CAT, which supports that GEE modulates beneficial gut microbiota to improve the antioxidant capacity in laying hens (Jin et al., 2025). Furthermore, a previous study demonstrated that administration of Faecalibacterium duncaniae attenuated injury in mice exposed to high‑altitude hypoxia by significantly reducing the number of TUNEL‑positive cells and restoring the expression of tight‑junction proteins (Sun et al., 2025). Consistent with these findings, our study also observed that both SND and SNDO significantly increased the abundance of Faecalibacterium in the lung microbiota of ACS broliers, which may partially explain the improved anti-inflammatory capacity and reduced pulmonary cell apoptosis in the treatment groups, especially in the SNDO group.

Lactobacillus is a genus renowned for its probiotic properties and ability to produce immunomodulatory short-chain fatty acids. Soluble factors secreted by Lactobacillus spp. can activate the MAPK signaling pathway in host intestinal epithelial cells, thereby inducing the expression of cytoprotective heat shock proteins and exerting protective effects, including resistance to oxidative stress and maintenance of cytoskeletal integrity (Tao et al., 2006). Lactobacillus spp. activate the PI3K/Akt pathway, regulate Bcl-2 family proteins and caspase expression, exert anti-inflammatory and anti-apoptotic effects, reduce oxidative stress, and attenuate DSS-induced colitis while preserving intestinal epithelial integrity in mice (Yoda et al., 2014; Xie et al., 2025). In addition, ginger volatile oil (GVO) enhances the abundance of Lactobacillus in the gut of female BALB/c nude mice bearing MDA-MB-231 breast cancer. Notably, as a beneficial gut bacterium, Lactobacillus exerts potent anti-inflammatory effects and modulates immune responses to inhibit the progression of bisphenol A (BPA)-promoted triple-negative breast cancer (Luo et al., 2024). In the present study, the abundance of Lactobacillus in the gut of the SND and SNDO groups was increased, and the increased abundance also contributed to the anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects of the broiler. In addition, the superior effect of the SNDO group compared to the SND group may be attributed to its higher content of 6-gingerol.

In summary, SND and SNDO effectively alleviated ACS-induced lung injury to varying degrees in broilers by attenuating pulmonary inflammation and cell apoptosis, enhancing antioxidant capacity and pulmonary barrier integrity, and reshaping the lung microbial community structure. However, a closer look at the results reveals that the alleviating effect of SNDO was superior to that of SND, which may be attributed to its stable ginger oil and higher 6-gingerol concentration. Future studies will explore potential signaling pathways responsible for the protective effects of SNDO, and further clarify the relationships among lung microbiota, microbial metabolites, and related functional pathways.
