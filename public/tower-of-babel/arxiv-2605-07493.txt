Geometry of weak contact conics to irreducible quartics with 2 nodes and 1 cusp via rational elliptic surfaces and Zariski pairs
Khulan TUMENBAYAR

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
{\mathcal{Q}}
be an irreducible quartic with two nodes and one cusp as its singularities and let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a conic such that the intersection multiplicity at each point of
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{Q}}
is even and
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{Q}}
contain at least one smooth point
z_{o}
of
{\mathcal{Q}}
. In this paper, for every
{\mathcal{Q}}
we find all possible conics
{\mathcal{C}}
as above via studying geometry of
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}
through that of integral sections of a rational elliptic surface which canonically arises from
{\mathcal{Q}}
and
z_{o}\in{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{Q}}
. As an application, we construct Zariski pairs of degree 7 and degree 8, whose irreducible components consist of
{\mathcal{Q}}
,
{\mathcal{C}}
and line passing through two of the singular points of
{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Construction of irreducible quartics with 2 nodes and 1 cusp

Let
[T,X,Z]
be homogeneous coordinates of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Let
Q
be the standard quadratic transformation or the standard Cremona
transformation with respect to
\{T=0\},\{X=0\}
and
\{Z=0\}
.
We call
[0,0,1],[0,1,0]
and
[1,0,0]
, the fundamental points with
respect to
Q
.

Lemma 2.1

(i)

Let C be a conic not passing through any of the fundamental points and tangent to
\{Z=0\}
, but not tangent to
\{T=0\}
and
\{X=0\}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Then
Q(C)
is a quartic whose singularities are only 1 cusp at
[0,0,1]
and 2 nodes at
[0,1,0]
and
[1,0,0]
.

(ii)

Let
L
be the line tangent to
C
at a point
P=[T_{0},X_{0},Z_{0}]\in C
, where
T_{0}X_{0}Z_{0}\neq 0
. If
L
does not
contain any of the fundamental points, then
Q(L)
is a conic tangent to
Q(C)
at

Q(P)=[X_{0}Z_{0},T_{0}Z_{0},T_{0}X_{0}]
and passes through the fundamental points.

(iii)

Let
L
be the line tangent to
C
at a point
P=[T_{0},X_{0},Z_{0}]\in C
, where
T_{0}X_{0}Z_{0}\neq 0
.
If
L
pass through one of the fundamental points, then
Q(L)
is a line passing through that fundamental point.

(iv)

Let
L
be conic, that contains the fundamental points, then
Q(L)
is a line.

(v)

If
x\in\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\{fundamental~points\}
,
then
I_{x}(C,L)=I_{Q(x)}(Q(C),Q(L))
.

Since all of these statements are well-known, we omit their
proofs. We make use of Lemma
2.1
when
we consider explicit examples.

2.2
Elliptic Surfaces

Throughout this article, an elliptic surface always means a smooth projective
surface
S
with a fibration
\varphi:S\to C
over a smooth projective
curve,
C
, as follows:

(i)

There exists
non empty finite subset
\mathop{\rm Sing}\nolimits(\varphi)\subset C
such that

\varphi^{-1}(v)
is a smooth curve of genus
1
for
v\in C\smallsetminus\mathop{\rm Sing}\nolimits(\varphi)
, while
\varphi^{-1}(v)
is not a
smooth curve of genus
1
for
v\in\mathop{\rm Sing}\nolimits(\varphi)
.

(ii)

There exists a
section
O:C\to S
(we identify
O
with its image in
S
).

(iii)

there is no exceptional curve of the first kind in any fiber.

For
v\in\mathop{\rm Sing}\nolimits(\varphi)
, we call
F_{v}=\varphi^{-1}(v)
a singular fiber over
v
. As
for the types of singular fibers, we
use notation given by
Kodaira (
[
3
]
).
We denote the irreducible decomposition of
F_{v}
by

F_{v}=\Theta_{v,0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m_{v}-1}a_{v,i}\Theta_{v,i},

where
m_{v}
is the number of irreducible components of
F_{v}
and

\Theta_{v,0}
is the irreducible component with
\Theta_{v,0}O=1
.

Let
\mathop{\rm MW}\nolimits(S)
be the set of sections of
\varphi:S\to C
. By our assumption,
\mathop{\rm MW}\nolimits(S)\neq\emptyset
.
It is known that
\mathop{\rm MW}\nolimits(S)
has the structure of an abelian group with zero element
O
. It is called the Mordell-Weil group.
We denote its addition and the multiplication-by-
m
map by
s_{1}\dot{+}s_{2}
and
[m]s_{1}
, respectively, where
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathop{\rm MW}\nolimits(S)

m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
E
be a generic fiber of elliptic surface
\varphi:S\to C
. One can regard
E

as an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{C}}(C)
, the rational function field of
C
.
It is known that
\mathop{\rm MW}\nolimits(S)
can be identified with the set of
{\mathbb{C}}(C)
-rational points
E(\mathbb{C}(C))
.
We also denote the addition and the multiplication-by-
m
map on
E(\mathbb{C}(C))
by
P_{1}\dot{+}P_{2}
and
[m]P_{1}
for

P_{1},P_{2}\in E(\mathbb{C}(C))
, respectively.

In
[
6
]
, Shioda introduced a
\mathbb{Q}
-valued bilinear form on
E(\mathbb{C}(C))
called the height pairing. We denote
it by
\langle\,,\,\rangle
. For an explicit formula of
\langle P_{1},P_{2}\rangle
(
P_{1},P_{2}\in E(\mathbb{C}(C))
), see
[
6
, Theorem 8.6]
.

2.3
Rational Elliptic Surfaces
S_{{\mathcal{Q}},z_{o}}

We refer to
[
8
]
for details. Let
\varphi:S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a rational elliptic surface and let
\mathcal{E}
be the generic fiber of
\varphi
. The inverse morphism on
\mathcal{E}
with respect to the group law on
\mathcal{E}
induces an involution
[-1]_{\varphi}
on
S
. Let
S/\langle[-1]_{\varphi}\rangle
be the quotient by
[-1]_{\varphi}
. It is known that
S/\langle[-1]_{\varphi}\rangle
is smooth and if
\varphi
is a rational elliptic surface and
S
has a reducible singular fiber then we can blow down
S/\langle[-1]_{\varphi}\rangle
to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Hence we have the following double cover diagram

\begin{CD}S^{\prime\prime}@<{\bar{\mu}}<{}<S\\
@V{f^{\prime\prime}}V{}V@V{}V{f}V\\
\mathbb{P}^{2}@<{}<{\bar{q}}<\widehat{\Sigma}_{2},\end{CD}

where

•

f:S\to S/\langle[-1]_{\varphi}\rangle=\widehat{\Sigma}_{2}
: the quotient morphism,

•

\bar{q}:\widehat{\Sigma}_{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
, where
\bar{q}
is a composition of blowing ups,

•

S\xrightarrow{\bar{\mu}}S^{\prime\prime}\xrightarrow{f^{\prime\prime}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
: the Stein factorization of
\bar{q}\circ f
.

Note that
S^{\prime\prime}
is a double cover of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with branch locus
{\mathcal{Q}}
, a quartic, as explained in
[
8
]
.

Conversely, let
{\mathcal{Q}}
be a reduced quartic, not concurrent 4 lines and let
z_{o}
be a smooth point on
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Let
f_{{\mathcal{Q}},z_{o}}:S\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the composition of maps as follows:

f_{{\mathcal{Q}},z_{o}}:S\xrightarrow{\pi}S^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f^{\prime}}W\xrightarrow{\bar{q}_{2}}\widehat{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\xrightarrow{\bar{q}_{1}}\mathbb{P}^{2}

where

•

\bar{q}_{1}:~\widehat{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
blow-up at
z_{o}
twice,

•

\bar{q}_{2}:~W\to\widehat{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
blow-up until
\widehat{{\mathcal{Q}}}
, the strict transform of
{\mathcal{Q}}
, becomes smooth,

•

f^{\prime}:~S^{\prime}\to W
the double cover of
W
with branch locus
\widehat{{\mathcal{Q}}}+\Delta_{0}
, where

is the strict image of the exceptional curve of first blow up of

•

.

If
L

.

There exists no homeomorphism between

and

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}_{1,1},{\mathcal{B}}_{2,1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{1}+{\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{C}}_{1})

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}_{2,2},{\mathcal{B}}_{1,2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{C}}_{2},{\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{1}+{\mathcal{C}}_{2})

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}_{1,1},{\mathcal{B}}_{1,2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{1}+{\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{1}+{\mathcal{C}}_{2})

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}_{1,1},{\mathcal{B}}_{1,0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{1}+{\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{1}+{\mathcal{C}}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}_{2,2},{\mathcal{B}}_{2,0})

\displaystyle({\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{C}}_{2},{\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{C}}_{0})

\displaystyle({\mathcal{Q}}+\bar{{\mathcal{C}}}+{\mathcal{C}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}+\bar{{\mathcal{C}}}+{\mathcal{C}}_{1})

\displaystyle({\mathcal{D}}_{0},{\mathcal{D}}_{2})

\displaystyle({\mathcal{Q}}+\bar{{\mathcal{C}}}+{\mathcal{C}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}+\bar{{\mathcal{C}}}+{\mathcal{C}}_{2}).
