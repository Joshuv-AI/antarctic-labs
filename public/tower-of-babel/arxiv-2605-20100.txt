1.
Introduction

In this paper we discuss two new proofs of Fefferman’s Fourier restriction
theorem on the parabola, equivalently its dual formulation as a Fourier
extension theorem. The first proof, which is not all that new, uses C.
Fefferman’s ‘half decoupling’ arising from pairwise disjointedness of certain
convolutions, followed by the remaining ‘half decoupling’ that uses Haar
wavelets and simple estimates of the resulting convolutions. Both of these
decouplings rely heavily on the curvature of the circle. The second proof is
from Rios and Sawyer
[
RiSa4
]
, and uses their new decoupling method
entirely on the Fourier side, that is based on smooth Alpert wavelets, in
order to establish a local estimate for the extension operator as in e.g.

[
BoGu
]
.

The Fourier extension conjecture in
d\geq 2
dimensions arose from unpublished
work of E. Stein in 1967, see e.g.
[
Ste2
, see the Notes at the end of Chapter
IX, p. 432, where Stein proved the restriction conjecture for
1\leq p<\frac{4d}{3d+1}
]
and
[
Ste
]
,

\left\|\widehat{f\sigma_{d-1}}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\sigma_{d-1}\right)},\ \text{for }f\in L^{p}\left(\sigma_{d-1}\right)\text{
and }p>\frac{2d}{d-1},

where
\sigma_{d-1}
is surface measure on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
or a
smooth compactly supported measure on the paraboloid
\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
. The
case
d=2
of the Fourier extension conjecture for the circle was proved over
half a century ago by C. Fefferman
[
Fef
]
, see also A. Zygmund
[
Zyg
]

and L. Carleson and P. Sjölin
[
CaSj
]
. The higher dimensional theorem,
apart from the Knapp line, was recently proved by Rios and Sawyer in

[
RiSa4
]
.

The first proof of Fefferman’s theorem given here in the plane, differs only
in that Haar wavelets are used to advantage after Fefferman’s classical
convolution observation. This can be viewed as a painless introduction to the
second proof, which is the higher dimensional proof of Rios and Sawyer in

[
RiSa4
]
restricted to dimension
d=2
, where many technicalities
disappear. Of course the main source of technical difficulty remains in the
second proof, since smooth Alpert wavelets are used in place of Haar wavelets.

2.
Fefferman’s decoupling

We start with a bounded function
f
supported in
\left[0,1\right]
and
push the absolutely continuous measure
f\left(x\right)dx
on the real
line forward under the map
\Phi\left(x\right)=\left(x,x^{2}\right)

to a singular measure
\Phi_{\ast}f
in the plane supported on the unit
parabola
\mathbb{P}
. The Fourier extension of
f
is then defined by

\mathcal{E}f\left(\xi\right)\equiv\widehat{\Phi_{\ast}f}\left(\xi\right)=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}x+\xi_{2}x^{2}\right)}f\left(x\right)dx,

and Stein’s conjecture in the plane, apart from the Knapp line, is equivalent
to

\left\|\mathcal{E}f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\left[0,1\right]\right)},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for }q>4\text{ and }\operatorname*{Supp}f\subset\left[0,1\right].

By subtracting a constant, we may suppose that
f
has mean zero on

I_{0}\equiv\left[0,1\right]
, and hence that the Haar expansion of
f
in
the standard grid
\mathcal{D}
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\subset I_{0}}\left\langle f,h_{I}\right\rangle h_{I}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}_{t}f,\ \ \ \ \ \text{where }\mathsf{P}_{t}f\equiv\sum_{I\subset\mathcal{D}_{t}\left[I_{0}\right]}\left\langle f,h_{I}\right\rangle h_{I}\ ,

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\mathsf{Q}_{s}f,\ \ \ \ \ \text{where
}\mathsf{Q}_{s}f\equiv\sum_{s=0}^{s}\mathsf{P}_{t}f=\sum_{I\subset\mathcal{D}\left[I_{0}\right]:\ \ell\left(I\right)\geq 2^{-s}}\left\langle f,h_{I}\right\rangle h_{I}\ ,

where
\mathcal{D}_{t}\left[I_{0}\right]\equiv\left\{I\in\mathcal{D}:\ell\left(I\right)=2^{-t}\right\}
.

In particular the projection
\mathsf{Q}_{s}f
is constant on each interval

I_{n}\equiv\left[2^{-s}\left(n-1\right),2^{-s}n\right)
in

\mathcal{D}_{s}\left[I_{0}\right]
. For convenience we fix
s\in\mathbb{N}
and write

(2.2)

F=\sum_{n=1}^{2^{s}}F_{n},\ \ \ \ \ \text{ where }F_{n}\equiv a_{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}}.

Now let
\varphi
be a nonnegative smooth function adapted to the unit ball in

\mathbb{R}^{2}
with integral
1
. Then if
\varphi_{s}\left(z\right)\equiv\frac{2^{4s}}{c^{2}}\varphi\left(\frac{z}{c2^{-2s}}\right)
and

q>4
, we have from the Hausdorff-Young inequality with exponents
\frac{q}{2}
and
\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-2}
that

\displaystyle\int_{B\left(0,2^{2s}\right)}\left|\mathcal{E}F\left(\xi\right)\right|^{q}d\xi\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\widehat{\varphi_{s}}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{q}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{2^{s}}\widehat{\Phi_{\ast}F_{n}}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{q}d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\widehat{\varphi_{s}\ast\Phi_{\ast}F_{m}}\left(\xi\right)\widehat{\varphi_{s}\ast\Phi_{\ast}F_{n}}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{\frac{q}{2}}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\left(\varphi_{s}\ast\Phi_{\ast}F_{m}\ast\varphi_{s}\ast\Phi_{\ast}F_{n}\right)^{\wedge}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{\frac{q}{2}}d\xi\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\varphi_{s}\ast\Phi_{\ast}F_{m}\ast\varphi_{s}\ast\Phi_{\ast}F_{n}\left(z\right)\right|^{\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\prime}}dz\right)^{\frac{\frac{q}{2}}{\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}G_{m}\ast G_{n}\left(z\right)\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}dz\right)^{\frac{q-2}{2}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{where }G_{k}\equiv\varphi_{s}\ast\Phi_{\ast}F_{k}\ .

Of course
\sum_{m=1}^{2^{s}}\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname*{Supp}G_{m}}\leq C
by
definition of
G_{m}
, and Fefferman’s clever decoupling observation in

[
Fef
]
is that

(2.3)

\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname*{Supp}G_{m}\ast G_{n}}\leq\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname*{Supp}G_{m}+\operatorname*{Supp}G_{n}}\leq C,

independent of
s
, by the curvature of the parabola. This can be seen by
letting
R_{m}
be a tilted
C2^{-s}\times C2^{-2s}
rectangle containing the
support of
G_{m}
, and then carefully examining the locations of the set sums

R_{m}+R_{n}
in the plane.

A consequence of this latter bound is the first decoupling,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}G_{m}\ast G_{n}\left(z\right)\right|^{p}dz\leq C_{p}\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|G_{m}\ast G_{n}\left(z\right)\right|^{p}dz,\ \ \ \ \ \text{for }1\leq p<\infty.

In particular, with
p=\frac{q}{q-2}
we obtain

\int_{B\left(0,2^{2s}\right)}\left|\mathcal{E}F\left(\xi\right)\right|^{q}d\xi\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}G_{m}\ast G_{n}\left(z\right)\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}dz\right)^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|G_{m}\ast G_{n}\left(z\right)\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}dz\right)^{\frac{q-2}{2}}.

Now we bound
G_{k}
by

\displaystyle\left|G_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|G_{k}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname*{Supp}g_{k}}\leq\left\|\varphi_{s}\ast\Phi_{\ast}F_{k}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathbf{1}_{R_{k}}=\left(\sup_{z\in R_{k}}\left|\int 2^{4s}\varphi\left(\frac{z-w}{2^{-2s}}\right)d\Phi_{\ast}F_{k}\left(w\right)\right|\right)\mathbf{1}_{R_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{4s}2^{-2s}\left|a_{k}\right|\mathbf{1}_{R_{k}}=2^{2s}\left|a_{k}\right|\mathbf{1}_{R_{k}}\ ,

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|G_{m}\ast G_{n}\left(z\right)\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|2^{2s}\left|a_{m}\right|\mathbf{1}_{R_{m}}\ast 2^{2s}\left|a_{n}\right|\mathbf{1}_{R_{n}}\left(z\right)\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|a_{m}\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left|a_{n}\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}2^{\frac{4q}{q-2}s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\mathbf{1}_{R_{m}}\ast\mathbf{1}_{R_{n}}\left(z\right)\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}dz.

If we denote by
\theta_{m,n}
the angle between the vectors perpendicular to

\Phi\left(I_{m}\right)
and
\Phi\left(I_{n}\right)
, then we have

\theta_{m,n}\approx 2^{-s}+\left|m-n\right|
and so

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{1}_{R_{m}}\ast\mathbf{1}_{R_{n}}\left(z\right)\right|\leq\left|R_{m}\cap R_{n}\right|\mathbf{1}_{R_{m}+R_{n}}\left(z\right)\lesssim\frac{2^{-4s}}{2^{-s}+\left|\theta_{m,n}\right|}\mathbf{1}_{R_{m}+R_{n}}\left(z\right)=\frac{2^{-3s}}{1+\left|m-n\right|}\mathbf{1}_{R_{m}+R_{n}}\left(z\right),

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and }\left|R_{m}+R_{n}\right|\approx 2^{-s}2^{-s}\left(2^{-s}+\left|\theta_{m,n}\right|\right)\approx\left(1+\left|m-n\right|\right)2^{-3s}.

As a consequence we conclude that for any function
F
on
I_{0}
as in
(
2.2
), that is constant on each interval
I\in\mathcal{G}_{s}\left[I_{0}\right]
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B\left(0,2^{2s}\right)}\left|\mathcal{E}F\left(\xi\right)\right|^{q}d\xi\right)^{\frac{2}{q-2}}\lesssim\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\left|a_{m}\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left|a_{n}\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}2^{\frac{4q}{q-2}s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\mathbf{1}_{R_{m}}\ast\mathbf{1}_{R_{n}}\left(z\right)\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\left|a_{m}\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left|a_{n}\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}2^{\frac{4q}{q-2}s}\left(\frac{2^{-3s}}{1+\left|m-n\right|}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(1+\left|m-n\right|\right)2^{-3s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\left|a_{m}\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left|a_{n}\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\frac{2^{\frac{4q}{q-2}s}2^{-\frac{3q}{q-2}s}2^{-3s}}{\left(1+\left|m-n\right|\right)^{\frac{q}{q-2}-1}}\leq\sum_{m,n=1}^{2^{s}}\frac{\left|a_{m}\right|^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}+\left|a_{n}\right|^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}}{2}\frac{2^{-\frac{2q-6}{q-2}s}}{\left(1+\left|m-n\right|\right)^{\frac{2}{q-2}}},

which by symmetry of the double sum equals

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{2q-6}{q-2}s}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{s}}\left|a_{n}\right|^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{s}}\frac{1}{\left(1+\left|m-n\right|\right)^{\frac{2}{q-2}}}\approx 2^{-\frac{2q-6}{q-2}s}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{s}}\left|a_{n}\right|^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}2^{\frac{q-4}{q-2}s}=2^{-s}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{s}}\left|a_{n}\right|^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}

\displaystyle=2^{-s}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{s}}\frac{1}{\left|I_{n}\right|}\left\|F_{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}\left(I_{0}\right)}^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}=\sum_{n=1}^{2^{s}}\left\|F_{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}\left(I_{0}\right)}^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}=\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{2^{s}}F_{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}\left(I_{0}\right)}^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}=\left\|F\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}\left(I_{0}\right)}^{\frac{2q}{q-2}},

which constitutes the second decoupling, and where the implied constants are
independent of
s\in\mathbb{N}
.

Taking
F=\mathsf{Q}_{t}f
for any
1\leq t\leq s
, we have proved

\left\|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{t}f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(B\left(0,2^{2s}\right)\right)}^{q}\lesssim\left(\left\|\mathsf{Q}_{t}f\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}\left(I_{0}\right)}^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}\right)^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}\left(I_{0}\right)}^{q}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(I_{0}\right)}^{q},

since
\frac{2q}{q-2}<q\
if
q>4
. Letting first
s\nearrow\infty
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{t}f\left(\xi\right)\right|^{q}d\xi\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(I_{0}\right)}^{q},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all }t\in\mathbb{N},

and then letting
t\nearrow\infty
, we obtain from Fatou’s lemma that for

q>4
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\mathcal{E}f\left(\xi\right)\right|^{q}d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\lim\inf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\ \left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{t}f\left(\xi\right)\right|^{q}d\xi\leq\lim\inf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{t}f\left(\xi\right)\right|^{q}d\xi\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(I_{0}\right)}^{q},

since
\mathsf{Q}_{t}f\rightarrow f
in
L^{2}\left(I_{0}\right)
implies
that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{t}f\left(\xi\right)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}x+\xi_{2}x^{2}\right)}\mathsf{Q}_{t}f\left(x\right)dx=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}x+\xi_{2}x^{2}\right)}f\left(x\right)dx=\mathcal{E}f\left(\xi\right).

3.
The alternate decoupling of Rios and Sawyer
[
RiSa4
]

Fefferman’s beautiful decoupling (
2.3
) fails in higher dimensions,
as is easily seen in three dimensions by rotating the parabola into a
paraboloid and noting that convolutions of pairs of antipodal
3D
-rectangles
lying above a circle in the horizontal plane, have essentially the same
support. Moreover, the critical index in dimension
d\geq 3
is
\frac{2d}{d-1}
, which is no longer an even integer, thus preventing the use of
Hausdorff-Young to implement the above method.

The two-dimensional proof given above used Fefferman’s observation to decouple
‘half of the way’ using pairwise disjointedness of supports of convolutions,
exploiting curvature of the parabola, and then continued by decoupling the
remaining ‘half of the way’ using Haar expansions and estimates on the size of
the resulting convolutions, again exploiting curvature of the parabola.

On the other hand, in the higher dimensional proof used in
[
RiSa4
]
, all
of the decoupling was done on the Fourier side, and this required a
new

decomposition of
f=\sum_{m=1}^{2^{s}}f_{m}
, and more precise pointwise
estimates on the new Fourier extensions
\mathcal{E}f_{m}
, which of course
exploit curvature of the parabola once again. This second proof is
considerably more complicated than the first proof, but has the advantage that
it extends to higher dimensions.

In
[
RiSa4
]
, the proof for dimension
d\geq 2
was presented in three
steps, and we will now just discuss these steps in the setting of dimension

d=2
, where some of the technicalities are more tractable, and the decoupling
via Dirichlet kernels is more visible and easier to compare with the
decoupling in the first proof.

3.1.
Step 1

We begin by replacing the Haar decomposition used in (
2.1
) with
an Alpert decomposition having two vanishing moments. In this decomposition of
Alpert
[
Alp
]
, there are now two mother wavelets
h
and
k
supported on

\left[-1,1\right]
, that are polynomials of degree at most
1
on each
child, and that have two vanishing moments each, e.g.

h\left(x\right)=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(2\left|x\right|-1\right)\text{ and }k\left(x\right)=\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(3\left|x\right|-2\right).

After dilating, translating and
L^{2}
-normalizing these mother wavelets to
each dyadic interval
I\in\mathcal{D}\left[I_{0}\right]
, we obtain an
orthonormal basis
\left\{h_{I},k_{I}\right\}_{I\in\mathcal{D}\left[I_{0}\right]}
of

L_{\operatorname*{two}\operatorname*{van}\operatorname*{mom}}^{2}\left(I_{0}\right)\equiv\left\{f\in L^{2}\left(I_{0}\right):\int_{I_{0}}f\left(x\right)dx=\int_{I_{0}}f\left(x\right)xdx=0\right\},

and a frame for
L_{\operatorname*{two}\operatorname*{van}\operatorname*{mom}}^{p}\left(I_{0}\right)
for
1<p<\infty
.

Finally, we smooth out this basis by convolving the mother wavlets with
a special smooth cutoff function
\phi
adapted to a small interval
\left[-\eta,\eta\right]
. Now we let
h_{I}^{\eta}
and
k_{I}^{\eta}
be the
appropriate dilates, translates and normalizations of
h\ast\phi
and

k\ast\phi
respectively for all dyadic intervals
I\in\mathcal{D}
. We are
moving to the entire real line now because the smooth wavelets are no longer
orthogonal in
L^{2}
. However, if
\eta
is chosen sufficiently small, then

\left\{h_{I}^{\eta},k_{I}^{\eta}\right\}_{I\in\mathcal{D}}
is a frame
for
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, more precisely there is a bounded
invertible linear map
T
on
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
such that if

\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\eta,1}f\equiv\left\langle T^{-1}f,h_{I}^{\eta}\right\rangle h_{I}^{\eta}\text{ and }\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\eta,2}f\equiv\left\langle T^{-1}f,k_{I}^{\eta}\right\rangle k_{I}^{\eta},

then the projections
\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\eta,j}
satisfy

f=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\eta,j}f\text{,
\ \ \ \ both pointwise a.e. and in }L^{p}\text{ norm,}

and the square function
\mathcal{S}f\equiv\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\left|\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\eta,j}f\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
satisfies
\left\|\mathcal{S}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}\approx\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}
. We will abuse notation slightly from now
on by writing simply
\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\eta}
in place of
\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\eta,j}
. See
[
Saw7
]
and
[
Saw8
]
for these facts, including the
special nature of the cutoff function
\phi
.

What follows is a discussion of the argument of Rios and Sawyer in

[
RiSa4
]
. It is well known that the Fourier extension theorem in the plane
is equivalent to the local bilinear inequality (see e.g.
[
Tao1
]
,

[
TaVaVe
]
and
[
BoGu
]
),

\left\|\mathcal{E}f_{1}\mathcal{E}f_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B\left(0,R\right)\right)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{4}}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{4}}

where
\operatorname*{Supp}f_{1}
and
\operatorname*{Supp}f_{2}
are
appropriately separated. The
\varepsilon
-removal theorem of
[
Tao1
]
is
crucial to the success of the argument here, especially its extension to
higher dimensions in
[
RiSa4
]
, as there is no longer any access to the
special properties of Haar wavelets.

Expanding the functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
into their smooth Alpert
decompositions
f_{j}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\eta}f_{j}
,
and introducing smooth cutoff functions
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{1}
, it is shown
in
[
RiSa3
]
that matters can be reduced to establishing the following
single scale bilinear inequality,

(3.1)

\left\|\mathcal{E}M_{\psi_{1}}\mathsf{P}_{s}f_{1}\mathcal{E}M_{\psi_{2}}\mathsf{P}_{s}f_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B\left(0,2^{\frac{s}{1-\delta}}\right)\right)}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta}2^{s\varepsilon}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{4}}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{4}},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all }s\in\mathbb{N},

where
M_{\psi_{j}}
is the operation of pointwise multiplication by
\psi_{j}
. This is accomplished using both the smoothness and moment vanishing
properties of the smooth Alpert wavelets (when necessary we increase the
number
\kappa
of vanishing moments - see
Step 1
of
[
RiSa4
]
).

Further manipulations using a good lambda inequality - again see
Step
1
of
[
RiSa4
]
), show that in the inequality (
3.1
), the
operator
T^{-1}
can be removed from the projection
\mathsf{P}_{s}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{s}}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\eta}
, and that instead, one
can use the perturbed projection

\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}_{s}\equiv\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{s}}\widetilde{\bigtriangleup}_{I}^{\eta}f\equiv\text{ where }\widetilde{\bigtriangleup}_{I}^{\eta}f\equiv\left\langle f,h_{I}^{\eta}\right\rangle h_{I}^{\eta}.

Moreover,
M_{\psi}\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}_{s}
can be replaced by

\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}_{s}
if we assume the support of
f
is contained in

\left[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\right]
. Then ‘dumbing down’ the bilinear
inequality to a linear one, the Fourier extension theorem for the parabola is
equivalent to the linear inequality,

\left\|\mathcal{E}\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}_{s}f\right\|_{L^{4}\left(B\left(0,2^{\frac{s}{1-\delta}}\right)\right)}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta}2^{s\varepsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{4}},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for
all }s\in\mathbb{N},

holding for all

3.1.1.
Averaging over grids

There is one final reduction to be made in
Step 1
, namely that it
suffices to average the smooth Alpert expansion of

over translates of the

Fourier extension theorem for the parabola is equivalent to the following

\left\|\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{T}_{s}}\mathcal{E}\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}_{s}f\right\|_{L^{4}\left(B\left(0,2^{\frac{s}{1-\delta}}\right)\right)}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta}2^{s\varepsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{4}},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all }s\in\mathbb{N},

Note also that if
\operatorname*{Supp}f\subset\frac{1}{2}U
and

sufficiently small, say
\left|v\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}
, then

\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}_{s}f=M_{\psi}^{\mathcal{G}}\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}_{s}f
where the ‘discrete multiplier’

is defined by

M_{\psi}^{\mathcal{G}}h_{I}^{\eta}=\psi\left(a_{I}\right)h_{I}^{\eta}
,

I=\left[a_{I},b_{I}\right)\in\mathcal{G}

Here
\psi

\mathcal{D}+v

\mathcal{D}
by

3.2.
Step 2
