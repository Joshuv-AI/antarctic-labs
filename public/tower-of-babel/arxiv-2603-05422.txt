Decay Rates in Interleaved Benchmarking with Single-Qubit References
Ilya A. Simakov ∗, Arina V. Zotova ∗, Tatyana A. Chudakova, Alena S. Kazmina, Artyom M. Polyanskiy, Nikolay N. Abramov, Mikhail A. Tarkhov, Alexander M. Mumlyakov, Igor V. Trofimov, Nikita Yu. Rudenko, Maxim V. Chichkov, Vladimir I. Chichkov, Grigoriy S. Mazhorin

Abstract
Cross-entropy benchmarking (XEB) with single-qubit reference sequences is widely used to characterize multi-qubit gates in large-scale quantum processors, despite the lack of a rigorous theoretical justification. Here we show that the commonly employed additive single-qubit errors approximation underlying this approach breaks down and leads to a systematic overestimation of gate fidelities. We derive an analytical expression for the joint decay of simultaneous single-qubit reference sequences and introduce a refined expression for the interleaved gate fidelity estimation. Experiments on a superconducting quantum processor validate the theory and demonstrate that fidelities obtained using XEB with single-qubit references agree with those extracted from standard interleaved randomized benchmarking (IRB), while achieving higher precision due to reduced reference-sequence errors. Our results establish theoretical foundation for the single-qubit-based XEB and show that, with appropriate post-processing, it enables a reliable and robust approach for entangling gates benchmarking without the need for multi-qubit Clifford reference sequences.

Despite its widespread use and clear practical advantages, the reliability of interleaved benchmarking with single-qubit references lacks a rigorous theoretical foundation.

Utilizing single-qubit references in interleaved XEB implicitly relies on two seemingly natural but unproven assumptions:
(i) the additivity of single-qubit errors in the joint fidelity decay, and
(ii) the sufficiency of single-qubit gates to generate effective randomization of errors in the interleaved circuit.
While both assumptions are supported by empirical evidence
9
, neither has been formally confirmed.
If violated, these assumptions can lead to systematic biases in the extracted fidelities and obscure the identification of dominant error mechanisms in multi-qubit devices, crucial for gate-architecture improvement.

In this work, we develop a theoretical framework for interleaved benchmarking with single-qubit reference sequences. We demonstrate the breakdown of the commonly used additive approximation for joint fidelity decay and derive an analytical relation between the measured decay and individual-qubit depolarizing parameters. However, we show that, despite the non-exponential decay of simultaneous single-qubit benchmarking, interleaved sequences can generate effective randomization equivalent to that of multi-qubit Clifford references. Experiments on a superconducting quantum processor confirm the theoretical predictions and yield fidelity estimates consistent with standard IRB, while achieving substantially improved precision due to reduced reference-sequence errors.

Benchmarking preliminaries.

The core idea behind gate benchmarking protocols such as XEB
3
and RB
17
is to execute random gate sequences of varying length. Averaging over random circuits effectively twirls the underlying noise into a depolarizing channel. As a result, an initial state
\left|\psi\right\rangle
, evolved under random unitaries
U
and averaged over the ensemble, takes the form

\overline{U^{\dagger}\rho_{U}U}=F\left|\psi\right\rangle\left\langle\psi\right|+(1-F)I/d,

(1)

with
I
the identity operator,
d=2^{n}
the dimension of the
n
-qubit Hilbert space and
\rho_{U}
are the density matrices resulting from the action of noisy sequences.
In this model, the state depolarizing fidelity
F
averaged over random circuits of length
m
exhibits an exponential decay

F=p^{m},

(2)

where
p
denotes the average depolarizing fidelity per gate block and is extracted directly from experimental data.

While this approach provides the average fidelity of a gate set, it does not quantify a specific operation. To address this issue, the interleaved protocol was introduced. It consists of two stages: first, standard RB is performed to obtain the reference depolarizing parameter
p_{\text{ref}}
; second, the experiment is repeated with the target gate
G
interleaved between random Cliffords, yielding
p_{\text{int}}
. The ratio
p_{G}=p_{\text{int}}/p_{\text{ref}}
gives the depolarizing fidelity of the particular operation.

The depolarizing parameter
p
is then related to the conventional average gate fidelity
19
;
20
;
5
via

\mathcal{F}=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(E)+d}{d(d+1)}=\frac{d-1}{d}p+\frac{1}{d}.

(3)

Here,
E
is the Pauli transfer matrix of the error channel, defined through the relation
R=ER_{\mathrm{ideal}}
, where
R
and
R_{\mathrm{ideal}}
denote the implemented and ideal gates, respectively. Together, these relations constitute the theoretical framework of randomized and cross-entropy benchmarking and form the basis for quantitative characterization of quantum processors.

Reference sequences under local noise.

The goal of the present work is to extend the standard benchmarking framework to enable a more experimentally convenient averaging strategy, directly relating the multi-qubit decay to individual-qubit errors. In modern quantum processors, gate performance is largely constrained by decoherence mechanisms, such as dephasing and energy relaxation, which act on distinct qubits. This motivates a local noise model composed of independent single-qubit error channels, which accurately captures the dominant error sources in contemporary devices. Within this setting, we examine two averaging approaches widely used in experimental practice: averaging over multi-qubit Clifford group
\mathcal{C}_{n}
and averaging over simultaneously executed single-qubit gates
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\otimes n}
.

We begin with the standard benchmarking protocol, in which reference circuits are composed of
n
-qubit Clifford operations forming a unitary 2-design
12
.
In this case, the average fidelity is determined via Eq. (
3
), with the error channel taking the product form
E=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{diag}(1,p_{i},p_{i},p_{i}),

where the diagonal representation is given in the
\{I,X,Y,Z\}
Pauli basis and
p_{i}
denotes depolarizing parameter associated with the
i
-th qubit.
To leading order in the individual error rates
e_{i}=1-p_{i}
, the resulting joint decay of the depolarizing fidelity is

F_{\mathrm{multi}}=\left(p_{\mathrm{multi}}\right)^{m}=\left(1-\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{1-(1/4)^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\right)^{m}.

(4)

Thus, the exponential decay law is preserved, with an effective depolarization parameter
p_{\mathrm{multi}}
.

Next, we consider simultaneous single-qubit benchmarking, a protocol used to assess single-qubit gate performance in multi-qubit systems. The fidelity measured in the benchmarking procedure is defined as the difference between the experimentally extracted and ideal bitstring probabilities of the final states. In Appendix
A
, we analyze these outcome probabilities for a single qubit and extend the analysis to
n
independent qubits using a combinatorial method. Specifically, we replace averaging over random unitaries
U
with an explicit summation over all possible measurement outcomes generated solely by single-qubit Clifford operations. The resulting expression for the average depolarizing fidelity of the final state as a function of the sequence length
m
is

F_{\mathrm{single}}=\frac{2^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(2+p_{i}^{m}\right)+3^{n}-\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(3+p_{i}^{m}\right)-4^{n}}{6^{n}+3^{n}-2\cdot 4^{n}}.

(5)

In contrast to the multi-qubit Clifford case, this expression exhibits a pronounced deviation from a simple exponential decay.

Interleaved benchmarking.

The non-exponential joint decay established above motivates a careful examination of interleaved benchmarking with single-qubit reference sequences, raising two essential questions: (i) whether such references provide a valid effective twirling of errors for an interleaved gate; and (ii) if so, how the target gate fidelity should be correctly extracted.

A natural criterion for assessing the validity of the chosen reference is provided by the statistics of the output state distribution generated during benchmarking. For a fully chaotic ensemble of states uniformly sampling the
n
-qubit Hilbert space, the measurement outcome probabilities follow the Porter-Thomas (PT) distribution
21
,

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{PT}}(P_{x})=(d-1)(1-P_{x})^{d-2},

(6)

where
P_{x}
is the probability of measuring bitstring
x
.
If the output distribution produced by an interleaved protocol obeys PT statistics, or, equivalently for benchmarking purposes, matches the statistics obtained from random
n
-qubit Clifford sampling, then the associated averaging faithfully reproduces the behavior required for randomized benchmarking and can be reliably used for fidelity estimation.

To make this distinction explicit, we first demonstrate that different reference ensembles lead to distinct output probability distributions and, consequently, different decay behavior.
For factorized single-qubit random sequences, we derive an analytical expression for the resulting distribution of output bitstring probabilities (see Appendix
B
),

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{1Q}}(P_{x})=\frac{(-\ln P_{x})^{n-1}}{(n-1)!},

(7)

which reflects the factorized structure of the underlying ensemble. Fig.
3
compares the corresponding cumulative distribution functions for the two-qubit case. We also include the analogous comparison for Clifford reference sequences, which are directly relevant for benchmarking experiments. While these distributions may be challenging to distinguish in the presence of noise, they are reliably resolved using ideal numerical simulations.

We then apply this criterion to a two-qubit entangling operation, such as the CZ gate widely used in superconducting quantum processors, interleaved with single-qubit gates. The resulting output probability distributions, shown as dashed curves in Fig.
3
, converge to the Porter-Thomas form when arbitrary single-qubit unitaries are used as references and closely match those obtained from sampling the two-qubit Clifford group when single-qubit Cliffords are employed. This observation demonstrates that, despite the factorized nature of the single-qubit reference ensemble, the presence of an interleaved CZ gate is sufficient to induce effective global randomization. Consequently, single-qubit reference sequences can be safely employed for interleaved benchmarking, provided that this statistical criterion is satisfied.

Since a simplified reference protocol performs averaging over a restricted ensemble, the corresponding depolarizing parameter
p_{\mathrm{ref}}
must be evaluated using Eq. (
4
), where
e_{i}
denote the individual single-qubit depolarizing errors. For the two-qubit case, one obtains

p_{G}=\frac{p_{\mathrm{int}}}{p_{\mathrm{multi}}}=\frac{p_{\mathrm{int}}}{1-\frac{4}{5}(e_{1}+e_{2})}.

(8)

We demonstrate this approach experimentally by benchmarking a microwave-activated CZ gate
23
;
6
on a fluxonium-based superconducting quantum processor.
Using standard interleaved randomized benchmarking with two-qubit Clifford reference sequences (Fig.
4
), we obtain a depolarizing gate fidelity of
p_{\mathrm{CZ}}^{\mathrm{multi}}=0.9830(25)
, averaged over 160 random circuits. Performing interleaved XEB with simultaneous single-qubit reference gates yields
p_{\mathrm{CZ}}^{\mathrm{single}}=0.9835(5)
, averaged over 50 random circuits. The two results therefore agree within uncertainty, confirming the validity of the proposed approach. Neglecting the refined averaging procedure leads to a systematic overestimation of the target gate fidelity by
(e_{1}+e_{2})/5
, which would give
p_{\mathrm{CZ}}=0.9850(5)
for the single-qubit-referenced experiment.

Discussion.

Simultaneous single-qubit benchmarking as the reference not only yields a correct estimate of the interleaved gate fidelity, but also provides higher precision than standard interleaved randomized benchmarking. This improvement is arises from the substantially lower errors of single-qubit gates compared to multi-qubit operations, together with the increased circuit depth required to synthesize elements of the
n
-qubit Clifford group from native two- and single-qubit gates. As a result, interleaved benchmarking with single-qubit references, when combined with appropriate post-processing, provides an accurate and experimentally practical method for characterizing multi-qubit quantum gates.

The decay of the reference and interleaved sequences follows different functional forms.
The onset of exponential decay occurs when the interleaved sequences are long enough to generate sufficient randomization.
The circuit depth at which this regime is reached depends on the specific interleaved gate and can be assessed numerically using ideal-gate simulations.
For the CZ gate, which is widely used in superconducting processors, the interleaved output distributions shown in Fig.
3
exhibit entangling properties already at shallow sequences lengths
m=4
.
On modern quantum processors with high-fidelity operations, where benchmarking sequences typically include hundreds of gates, discarding a small number of initial circuit depths therefore does not affect the extracted decay parameters.

A separate issue concerns the presence of nonlocal errors, which can lead the reference-sequence decay to deviate from the form
F_{\mathrm{single}}
.
An experimental criterion for the validity of the local noise model is the agreement between single-qubit fidelities extracted from isolated and simultaneous benchmarking.
For current-generation quantum processors, these fidelities typically agree within experimental uncertainty.
Whether nonlocal errors can be reliably detected and quantitatively characterized using simultaneous single-qubit benchmarking alone remains an open question and an important direction for future research.

Conclusion.

Our results provide a theoretical foundation for interleaved cross-entropy benchmarking with single-qubit reference sequences. We analyze the assumptions underlying this approach and show that the commonly used additive approximation for the reference-sequence decay in simultaneous single-qubit benchmarking is invalid. We derive an analytical expression for the resulting joint decay and its relation to individual single-qubit depolarizing parameters and introduce a criterion for validating whether an interleaved sequence generates sufficient randomization. Using the CZ gate as a representative example, we show that this criterion is satisfied, implying that the interleaved decay follows the standard multi-qubit depolarization model even when single-qubit reference sequences are employed. Building on this result, we refine the estimator for the target gate fidelity and validate it using numerical simulations and experiments on a superconducting quantum processor.

Placing interleaved XEB with single-qubit reference sequences on a firm theoretical ground, this work removes a key conceptual obstacle to its broader application. Beyond justifying the method, our analysis provides explicit guidance for its correct use, enabling accurate characterization of multi-qubit gates without relying on error-prone multi-qubit reference sequences. As a result, existing and future experiments can reliably exploit the reduced overhead of single-qubit-based interleaved XEB, maintaining high-precision fidelity estimates.

Appendix A
Fidelity decay for a simultaneous single-qubit benchmarking

To derive Eq. (
5
), we consider cross-entropy benchmarking from an experimental perspective, following the notations of tutorial
11
. In XEB, the experimentally measured output distribution of bitstrings
x
is compared with the ideal one computed on classical computer. Taking the componentwise product of the bitstring probabilities in each term of Eq. (
1
) with the ideal probabilities
p_{\text{ideal}}(x)
one gets

\begin{split}m_{U}&=\sum_{x}p_{\text{ideal}}(x)p_{\text{meas}}(x),\\
e_{U}&=\sum_{x}p_{\text{ideal}}(x)p_{\text{ideal}}(x),\\
u_{U}&=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{x}p_{\text{ideal}}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}.\end{split}

(9)

Here
p_{\text{meas}}(x)
is the experimentally measured probability of obtaining the bitstring
x
. Within the depolarizing model, the measured probabilities are assumed to follow a mixture of the ideal distribution and the uniform distribution, which leads to the linear relation

m_{U}=Fe_{U}+(1-F)u_{U}.

(10)

The state depolarizing fidelity
F
is then extracted using the least squares method, yielding

F=\frac{\sum_{U}(m_{U}-u_{U})(e_{U}-u_{U})}{\sum_{U}(e_{U}-u_{U})^{2}}.

(11)

We first analyze the single-qubit case. Since the benchmarking sequences consist of Clifford operations, the ideal final state is aligned with the
z
axis of the Bloch sphere with probability
1/3
and lies in the
xy
plane with probability
2/3
.
Assuming that the qubit depolarizes with probability
\delta=1-p^{m}
, if the ideal final state is aligned with
z
axis,

m_{U}=1-\delta/2
and
e_{U}=1,

whereas for states in the
xy
plane,

m_{U}=e_{U}=u_{U}=1/2
.
Hence, only realizations when the final state is aligned with
z
give a nonzero contribution to Eq. (
11
).

For
n
qubits, we have to account for all possible configurations in which
k
qubits end up aligned along the
z
axis. Since these events occur independently for each qubit, the averaging over random circuits
U
can be replaced by a sum over
k
. As a result, the denominator of Eq. (
11
) becomes

\sum_{U}(e_{U}-u_{U})^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}C_{n}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n-k}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n-k}}-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)^{2},

(12)

where
C_{n}^{k}
denotes the binomial coefficient.

The numerator depends on both the ideal and the measured probabilities.
Because the depolarization parameters
\delta_{i}
are qubit dependent, the summation over the
k
selected qubits cannot be reduced to a simple combinatorial factor and is carried out explicitly over all
k
-qubit subsets
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
. The numerator of Eq. (
11
) then reads

\begin{split}&\sum_{U}(m_{U}-u_{U})(e_{U}-u_{U})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n-k}\times\\
&\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\biggl[\frac{1}{2^{n-k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i_{j}}}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\biggr]\left[\frac{1}{2^{n-k}}-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right].\end{split}

(13)

By expanding the brackets and applying the generating-function decomposition

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+a_{i}x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\ \sum_{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{k}\leq n}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdot\cdot\cdot a_{i_{k}}x^{k}

(14)

and, as its special case, the binomial expansion
\sum_{k=0}^{n}C_{n}^{k}a^{k}b^{n-k}=(a+b)^{n}
, the fraction (
11
) results in

F=\frac{2^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(3-\delta_{i}\right)+3^{n}-\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(4-\delta_{i}\right)-4^{n}}{6^{n}+3^{n}-2\cdot 4^{n}},

(15)

which, after substitution
\delta_{i}=1-p_{i}^{m}
, yields Eq. (
5
).

Appendix B
Factorized single-qubit outcome bitstring distribution.

The distinction between simultaneous single-qubit and multi-qubit reference benchmarking can be quantified through the probability density of measured bitstring outcomes. For a fully chaotic quantum state uniformly sampled over the
n
-qubit Hilbert space, these probabilities follow the Porter-Thomas distribution (
6

For two qubits,

\left|{\psi}\right\rangle=(a_{0}\left|{0}\right\rangle+a_{1}\left|{1}\right\rangle)\otimes(b_{0}\left|{0}\right\rangle+b_{1}\left|{1}\right\rangle),

(16)

Here,

(19)
