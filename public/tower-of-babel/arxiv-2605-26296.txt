Superdilations at Schwarzschild null infinity Preprint: APS/123-QED
Marco Refuto

Abstract
Relying on the definition of asymptotic conformal Killing horizons, the generators of asymptotic conformal symmetries at future null infinity in Schwarzschild space-time are considered. The algebra they close is an extended version of the BMS one due to the presence of superdilations. Several features are then studied, such as the related non-trivial charge, excluding the hypothesis that superdilations are pure-gauge transformations. Their effect on two observers can be understood as an angle-dependent redshift, connected to the local non-vanishing flux of the charge.

II
Asymptotic flatness and symmetries at null infinity

Throughout all physically relevant space-times, asymptotically flat ones underpin several fundamental aspects of physics, as they offer the gravitational equivalent of an isolated system. The most important example is given by black hole metrics: they constitute an excellent arena for probing theoretical models and potential experimental scenarios. The underlying idea of an asymptotically flat space-time is that, far away from a matter source, it resembles Minkowski. However, there are five different kinds of infinity in Minkowski space-time (past and future timelike and null infinity, spacelike infinity). We are interested in studying null infinity: the region reached by radiation and gravitational waves (or, from a particle perspectives, by photons and gravitons). III
No conformal Killing vector fields in asymptotically flat space-times

We briefly review some relevant results on conformal symmetries in asymptotically flat space-times.
The motivation stems from a comparison with supertranslations: these constitute an asymptotic, angle-dependent generalization of a symmetry already present in the bulk of the space-time. Black-hole metrics, on the other hand, do not admit dilations as bulk symmetries. Superdilations, therefore, if they exist, would not share such a connection with the interior region of the space-time.

Given a space-time metric
g_{ab}
and a Killing vector field
X^{a}
which solves the equation

(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab},

(18)

X^{a}
is a conformal, homothetic or proper Killing vector field if
\Lambda
is a function, a constant or it vanishes, respectively. In the case of a conformal Killing vector field, which we label as proper conformal Killing vector field,
\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{a}X^{a}
.
The existence of a proper conformal Killing vector field is a demanding requirement for the space-time structure and it is guaranteed only in a few cases. Garfinkle
11
analysed the case of a space-time which is asymptotically flat at null infinity. If it is vacuum, asymptotically Minkowskian and it has positive Bondi energy (see the reference for the appropriate definition) for every cross section of
\mathcal{I}^{+}
, then it does not admit a conformal Killing field that is not a proper Killing field. Even if such a result is solely based on the structure at null infinity and on a condition on the Bondi energy, it can be extended further. By using the positive energy condition and the
3+1
formalism, Eardley et al.
9
showed that the only solution of the Einstein vacuum equation, asymptotically flat (spatially) and admitting a conformal Killing field, is the Minkowski space-time. Moreover, they proved that the only solution with a proper homothetic symmetry reads as

ds^{2}=e^{\alpha t}(-dt^{2}+h_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}),

(19)

where
h_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}
is a
3
-dimensional Riemannian metric of constant negative curvature on a compact manifold, while
\alpha
is a constant. This is the case of an expanding hyperbolic space-time. In addition (cf. Theorem
3
in Ref.
9
), all the space-times with proper conformal symmetries are either everywhere locally flat (like the previous one) or else are of a particular algebraically special form,

ds^{2}=-2H(u,x^{C})du^{2}-2dudr+\delta_{AB}dx^{A}dx^{B}.

(20)

The vacuum Einstein equations require that the function
H
satisfies the
2
-dimensional Laplace equation in the
\{x^{C}\}
, i.e.,
\delta^{AB}\partial_{A}\partial_{B}H(u,x^{C})=0
. These are plane-fronted waves.
Di Prisco et al.
6
, relied on a coordinate approach to study the role of conformal symmetries in vacuum Bondi-type metrics. Excluding all the singular solutions that do exist, the only completely regular (on the sphere) metric, compatible with the existence of a conformal Killing vector field, is the Minkowski metric. It turns out that the existence of a one-parameter group of conformal motions appears to be a too restrictive condition if we consider the family of physically significant Bondi metrics. Indeed, in the paper they have used the leading terms in the expansion of the Bondi metric to show that the existence of a conformal Killing vector field implies that the space-time is either flat (Minkowski) or non-globally regular at null infinity. Once again, it is important to stress that solutions presenting angular singularities (unbounded sources) and/or metrics corresponding to systems radiating during an infinite period of time do, in principle, exist. All of these scattered results can be easily grouped by considering the Petrov classification (see, e.g., Chap. IV of Ref.
21
). Sharma, in Ref.
20
, studied solutions which admit proper conformal symmetries. Unlike isometries and homothetic symmetries, proper conformal symmetries do not preserve the Einstein tensor. The existence of a proper conformal symmetry therefore restricts the conformal symmetric space-times (which are, in general, of type Petrov O, N or D) to be type Petrov O (Minkowsky or conformally related) or N (plane-fronted waves) only.
Finally, in a more recent paper, Keane
15
proved by a direct computation that
any conformal Killing vector field admitted by the Kerr-Newman space-time is necessarily a proper Killing vector field. This means that the most studied black-hole metrics (Schwarzschild, Reissner-Nordstroem, Kerr and Kerr-Newman) admit only the Lie algebras of proper Killing vector fields as their maximal point symmetry algebras.

In addition, the asymptotic structure does not depend on all the bulk details of the space-time. The Kerr metric would lead to an axial symmetric angular part (rather than a spherically symmetric one), while the black hole charge gives a subleading contribution with respect to, for instance, the mass.

V
The asymptotic conformal generator in Schwarzschild space-time

As stated in the previous section, in order to simplify the mathematics, we consider the simplest non-trivial asymptotically flat space-time: the Schwarzschild one. Its metric in Bondi coordinates (which corresponds to retarded Eddington - Finkelstein coordinates) is simply obtained by inserting the constraints

m(u,x^{A})=M,\quad C_{AB}=0,\quad N_{A}=0,

(43)

in Eq. (

\displaystyle ds^{2}=

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)du^{2}-2dudr

(44)

\displaystyle+r^{2}\gamma_{AB}dx^{A}dx^{B}.

(45)

Eqs. (

39

40

\displaystyle 2\partial_{u}X^{u}+4\left(1+\frac{2M}{r}\right)\partial_{u}X^{r}=

\displaystyle\Lambda,

(46)

\displaystyle\partial_{u}X^{u}+\partial_{r}X^{r}=

\displaystyle\Lambda,

(47)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}\gamma)_{AB}=

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda-\frac{2}{r}X^{r}\right)\gamma_{AB}.

(48)

For later convenience, we introduce the compactified Schwarzschild metric

d\tilde{s}^{2}=\Omega^{2}ds^{2},

(49)

with the canonical choice for the conformal factor given by

\tilde{h}_{AB}=\gamma_{AB}

V.1
A first attempt to the solution of the equations

After a straightforward integration we obtain

\displaystyle X=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{A}Y^{A}(u\partial_{u}+r\partial_{r})

\displaystyle+\alpha(x^{A})\partial_{u}+Y^{A}\partial_{A},

(50)

where
Y^{A}
is a conformal Killing vector field on the two-sphere.
Furthermore, we have to impose that its norm vanishes at
\mathcal{I}^{+}

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{g}_{ab}X^{a}X^{b}=0.

(51)

This condition leads to

\gamma_{AB}Y^{A}Y^{B}=0\implies Y^{A}=0,

(52)

(since
\gamma_{AB}
is not degenerate), obtaining simply

X=\alpha(x^{A})\partial_{u},

(53)

where
\alpha(x^{A})\in C^{\infty}(S^{2})
(required by the condition of complete integral lines). The vector field
\tilde{n}^{a}
is given by (cf. Eq. (

\tilde{n}^{a}=e^{-\frac{M}{r^{2}}u}X^{a}.

(54)

With such a result, Eq. (

This equation constraints the

\xi^{u}

\left(\partial_{u}+\frac{M}{r^{2}}\right)\xi^{u}=\frac{2M}{r^{3}}u\xi^{r}+\frac{Y^{A}\partial_{A}\alpha(x^{B})}{\alpha(x^{B})}-\lambda,

(56)

where
\xi^{A}=Y^{A}
is a conformal Killing vector field on the two sphere, solution of Eq. (

One could, in principle, solve the above equation in the

limit, but
\lambda
and
\xi^{r}
still remains unconstrained and it is not clear which ansatz one should adopt. In order to go beyond such difficulties, we rely on a slightly different scheme.

V.2
The solution of the equations

In order to obtain a clearer defining set of equations for
\xi^{a}
, we impose on the general structure of
X^{a}
in Eq. (
37

X^{A}=0.

(57)

By doing so, its norm is identically zero at null infinity and Eqs. (

47
), (
48

(58)

Moreover,
\Lambda(u,r,x^{A})=\Lambda(x^{A})=2ag(x^{A})
. The asymptotic conformal Killing vector field thus obtained is a linear combination of a time translation and a superdilation.
The vector field
\xi^{a}
is therefore given by (in the Appendix we carefully describe its derivation)

\xi=f(x^{A})[\alpha(u\partial_{u}+r\partial_{r})+\partial_{u}]+Y^{A}(x^{B})\partial_{A},

(59)

where
\alpha
is a constant and
f(x^{A})\in C^{\infty}(S^{2})

This is our final result.
In order to understand the role of superdilations, we compute the action of
\xi^{a}
on the asymptotic region of the Schwarzschild metric, i.e.,

\lim_{\Omega\rightarrow 0}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega^{2}g)_{ab}.

(60)

By exploiting the Leibnitz rule of the Lie derivative, i.e.,

(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega^{2}g)_{ab}=\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g)_{ab}+g_{ab}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega^{2},

(61)

we obtain that the only non-vanishing components are

\displaystyle\lim_{\Omega\rightarrow 0}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega^{2}g)_{rA}=

\displaystyle(\alpha u+1)\partial_{A}f,

(62)

\displaystyle\lim_{\Omega\rightarrow 0}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega^{2}g)_{AB}=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{Y}\gamma)_{AB}=\left(\frac{1}{2}D_{C}Y^{C}\right)\gamma_{AB}.

(63)

We therefore obtain a conformal scaling of the induced metric at
\mathcal{I}^{+}
through
Y^{A}
, while the
rA
component does not preserve the Bondi gauge in the case
\alpha=0
since the contribution which cancel the
\partial_{A}f
term is sub-leading.
As a final remark, it is interesting to make a comparison with the BMS generator in Eq. (
11
), where we do not have superdilations, the
(u,r)
and angular sectors are related through
Y^{A}(x^{B})
and

is just a generic function on the two-sphere.

V.3
Asymptotic generators algebra

By denoting with the sub-indices
T,R,D
the super - translations, rotations and dilations sector, respectively, we can consider the full vector field
\xi^{a}
in Eq. (
59

\displaystyle\xi_{T}(f)=

\displaystyle f(x^{A})\partial_{u},

(64)

\displaystyle\xi_{R}(Y)=

\displaystyle Y^{A}(x^{B})\partial_{A},

(65)

\displaystyle\xi_{D}(\alpha f)=

\displaystyle\alpha f(x^{A})(u\partial_{u}+r\partial_{r}).

(66)

After a straightforward computation we have that
\xi_{T}(f)
and
\xi_{R}(Y)
close the BMS algebra (cf. Eqs (
15

16
),(
17

\displaystyle[\xi_{T}(f_{1}),\xi_{T}(f_{2})]

\displaystyle=0,

(67)

\displaystyle[\xi_{R}(Y_{1}),\xi_{R}(Y_{2})]

\displaystyle=\xi_{R}([Y_{1},Y_{2}]),

(68)

\displaystyle[\xi_{R}(Y),\xi_{T}(f)]

\displaystyle=\xi_{T}(Y[f]).

(69)

Supertranslations therefore form an Abelian ideal. The superdilations sector reads

\displaystyle\left[\xi_{D}(\alpha_{1}f_{1}),\xi_{D}(\alpha_{2}f_{2})\right]=

\displaystyle 0,

(70)

\displaystyle\left[\xi_{T}(f),\xi_{D}(\alpha f)\right]=

\displaystyle\xi_{T}(\alpha f^{2}),

(71)

\displaystyle\left[\xi_{R}(Y),\xi_{D}(\alpha f)\right]=

\displaystyle\xi_{D}(\alpha Y[f]).

(72)

We can easily see how the algebra generated by
\xi^{a}
contains the BMS one as a subalgebra and dilations form an Abelian subalgebra. Those commutators lead to the group structure described in Eq. (

\Xi_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}=\text{Conf}(S^{2})\ltimes\big(C^{\infty}(S^{2})\ltimes C^{\infty}(S^{2})\big).

(73)

We thus recover in asymptotically flat space-times the same algebra derived for superdilations but in conformally flat space-times (through a coordinates-based approach) by Donnay et al. (cf. Eqs. (2.24) in Ref.

V.4
The Iyer-Wald charge

Since
\xi^{a}
, as defined in Eq. (
59
), is not a proper Killing vector field for the space-time metric given in Eq. (
45
), in order to compute its charge we rely on the Iyer-Wald one (see, e.g., Refs.
14
;
3

\cancel{\delta}Q_{\xi}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{-g}(d^{2}x)_{ab}k^{ab}_{\xi}[h;g],

(74)

where
(d^{2}x)_{ab}=\varepsilon_{abcd}dx^{c}\wedge dx^{d}
is the surface element two-form of
\Sigma
. In our case this is an exact differential, therefore we label it
\delta Q
. The two form charge is given by

\displaystyle k_{\xi}^{ab}[h;g]=

\displaystyle\xi^{a}\nabla_{c}h^{bc}-\xi^{a}\nabla^{b}h+\xi_{c}\nabla^{b}h^{ac}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}h\nabla^{b}\xi^{a}-h^{cb}\nabla_{c}\xi^{a},

(75)

where the local variation of the metric is denoted as
h_{ab}=\delta g_{ab}
(with the
\delta
Grassmann even convention) and
h
is its trace. Since we are considering Schwarzschild space-time in Bondi gauge (cf. Eq. (
45
)), we have simply that

\displaystyle h_{uu}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g_{uu}}{\partial M}\delta M=\frac{2}{r}\delta M,

(76)

\displaystyle h^{rr}=

\displaystyle h_{uu},

(77)

\displaystyle h=

\displaystyle 0,

(78)

while all other components vanish.
By computing the charge at null infinity, i.e.,
\Sigma=\mathcal{I}^{+}
, we have that only the
ur
and
ru
components of the two-form charge give a non-vanishing contribution (since the
ur
plane is the only one which is orthogonal to the sphere):

\displaystyle k^{ur}[h;g]=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{r^{2}}\delta M(1-r\partial_{r})\xi^{u},

(79)

\displaystyle k^{ru}[h;g]=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{r^{2}}\delta M\xi^{u}.

(80)

The contraction with the surface element gives

k^{ur}[h;g](d^{2}x)_{ab}|_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}=(k^{ur}-k^{ru})\varepsilon_{ur\theta\phi}d\theta d\phi.

(81)

Upon performing a formal path integration from
0
to
M
for the differential mass
\delta M
of the space-time and exploiting
7
7

7

This is true not only in our cases, but also in standard asymptotic analyses of symmetries

\partial_{r}\xi^{u}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{\xi}(u)=

\displaystyle\frac{M}{4\pi G}\int_{S^{2}}d\Omega\xi^{u}

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M}{4\pi G}(1+\alpha u)\bar{f},

(83)

where in the last equality we have explicitly written
\xi^{u}
from Eq. (
59
) and defined
\bar{f}=\int_{S^{2}}d\Omega f(x^{A})
.
We notice that, upon setting
\xi^{u}=1
, we correctly recover the standard ADM mass M of a Schwarzschild black hole.
The charge thus obtained is composed by the standard BMS supertranslations charge and a new one, related to superdilations. A striking feature is that, even if the space-time is static (no radiation) we have a non-vanishing charge flux given by

\frac{d}{du}Q_{\xi}=\pm|\alpha|\frac{M\bar{f}}{4\pi G},

(84)

where we have highlighted the two possible signs of
\alpha
, since we do not have constraints on it. Such a flux encodes the quantity of ingoing (+) or outgoing (-) energy radiated at null infinity.
Particular attention is needed in considering the divergence of the charge when
|u|\rightarrow\infty
, since it leads to several speculations. Such an unphysical behaviour could be related, for instance, to the presence of a constant Bondi mass aspect, which does not smear the charge at past or future null infinity. The divergence concerns therefore the existence of an eternal Schwarzschild black hole, but this topic deserves more future considerations.

A divergent superdilations charge is also obtained by Donnay et al. (see Sec. 5 in Ref.
7
).
In particular, in studying BMS transformations through an AdS/CFT perspective, some of the bulk Noether charges
they compute for the bulk superdilations vector field diverge. This disagreement in the bulk-boundary correspondence is seen as an evidence that superdilations are not well behaved asymptotic symmetries. Their analyses regards, as already written, conformally flat space-times. However, there are several connections with ours which leads to a wider description of superdilations throughout different kind of space-times.

VI
Superdilated BMS observers in Schwarzschild space-time

In order to relate superdilations to some kind of observable effects, we introduce a family of BMS observers as in Ref.
22
, i.e., detectors which travel along wordlines at fixed radius (assumed large) and angles. They can be labelled by the vector field

X^{a}_{\text{BMS}}(s)=(s,r_{0},x^{A}_{0}),

(85)

where
s
is the affine parameter on the worldline they describe. At first order in
r
, BMS observers are nearly inertial. They provide an useful tool to investigate physical effects of superdilations at null infinity. Let us consider, for instance, two generic BMS observers belonging to the same celestial sphere but slightly displaced in retarded time and angle variables. The displacement vector, which we assume with constant components, is given by

\zeta=\delta u\partial_{u}+\delta x^{A}\partial_{A}.

(86)

The action of standard BMS supertranslations on such a vector leads to the displacement memory effect (cf. Eq (4.11) in Ref.
22
).
The action of superdilations at null infinity

\xi_{D}|_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}=\alpha f(x^{A})u\partial_{u}

(87)

on these two observers is instead given by the Lie derivative

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{D}}\zeta=

\displaystyle[\xi_{D},\zeta]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[z(x^{A})\delta u+\mathbf{\Theta}_{B}(u,x^{A})\delta x^{B}]\partial_{u},

(88)

where

\displaystyle z(x^{A})=

\displaystyle\alpha f(x^{A}),

(89)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Theta}_{B}(u,x^{A})=

\displaystyle\alpha u\partial_{B}f(x^{A}).

(90)

The non-vanishing of the Lie derivative means that, as expected, the displacement is not invariant under the action of superdilations. In particular, we can recognise two different contributions.

By setting
\delta u=0
, we are considering two synchronized observers located at two different points of the celestial sphere. The
\mathbf{\Theta}(u,x^{A})
factor encodes the signature of superdilations: different observers are effectively pushed by the dilation at different rates, depending on their position. A similar phenomenon happens for supertranslations.

The case in which
\delta x^{A}=0
corresponds, instead, to a single observer who measures two nearby light pulses with a retarded time difference equals to
\delta u
. We can better understand this phenomenon by considering an observer which measures the arrival time of two light pulses, each of them coming from two different points of null infinity. Since the space-time we are considering is spherically symmetric, it should be equal since such pulses come from the same celestial sphere. However, as the computation shows, the measure of the arrival time of the signals depend also on their angular position. We could identify the
z(x^{A})
function as an angle-dependent dilation redshift factor. Moreover, this could, in principle, constrain the value of the constant
\alpha
by studying the uncertainties of experimental redshift measurements. However, this could not be trivial since we have to consider, first of all, asymptotically flatness space-times.

The two above computed quantities are nothing but local effects deriving from the Iyer-Wald charge. In fact, by isolating the superdilation part of the charge, we can rewrite Eq. (
83
) as

Q_{\alpha}(u)=\overline{q_{\alpha}(u,x^{A})},

(91)

and therefore

\displaystyle z(x^{A})=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi G}{M}\frac{d}{du}q_{\alpha}(u,x^{A}),

(92)

\displaystyle\Theta_{B}(u,x^{A})=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi G}{M}\partial_{B}q_{\alpha}(u,x^{A}).

(93)

The two effects are suppressed by the black hole mass, meaning that their measurement is order of magnitude smaller with respect the ones of supertranslations.

VII
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper we explicitly derive the generator of asymptotic conformal symmetries at null infinity, given in Eq. (
59
), in Schwarzschild space-time. Aside the supertranslations and Lorentz transformations, the superdilations sector extends the BMS algebra. The existence of superdilations in asymptotically flat space-times is highly non-trivial, since these geometries rule out bulk conformal transformations such as dilations. Their emergence as asymptotic symmetries does not have therefore a bulk correspondence (as in the supertranslations case). Moreover, they have been studied only in conformally flat space-times or expanding universe models. Even if Schwarzschild is the simplest example of asymptotically flat space-time, we can, nonetheless, observer unexpected feature related to superdilations. One of the most interesting consequences is the existence of a non-trivial charge with constant flux at null infinity, leading to what we consider an angle-dependent redshift.

We can identify several ways to expand this work.

First of all, the divergence of the charge and its non-vanishing flux could be probably related to the eternal black hole model provided by Schwarschild space-time. Moreover, the quantity
2\pi Q_{\xi}
could be understood as an asymptotic conformal black hole entropy by generalizing (in an appropriate way) the considerations made by Wald in Ref.
27
(see also Ref.
14
).

One crucial point concerns the study of more general space-times through Eqs. (
38
), (
39
), (
40
). Of particular interest are not only more general static Petrov D metrics (such as the Kerr-Newman one), but also dynamical black holes with a time dependent Bondi mass aspect and a non-vanishing shear (and therefore Bondi news) tensor, i.e., non-zero flux of radiation at infinity. This is motivated by a direct comparison with supertranslations: their physical consequences are largely encoded in the gravitational memory effect, which is strictly related to the shear tensor.
Once obtained a wider picture, superdilations could be treated as the others BMS transformations in the construction of a particle interpretation, a quantum Hilbert space for semi-classical investigations, soft hair models for black holes and so on.
It would be interesting to understand the role of superdilations in the infrared gravity research program. The existence of a quantum field theory correlation with superdilations would lead to the description of a new infrared triangle. In this case a connection with conformal field theories and therefore the celestial holography research program would be natural, embedding this work into a wider framework.
Each one of these mentioned topics deserve particular attention.
However, they all pave the way for a completely new research line.
