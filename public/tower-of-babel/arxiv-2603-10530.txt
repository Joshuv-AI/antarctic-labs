Convexity of the Potential Function of the Einstein-Kähler Metric on a Convex Domain
Jingchen Hu, Li Sheng

Abstract
Suppose that u u is the potential function of a complete Kähler-Einstein metric on a bounded strictly convex domain in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . We prove that u u itself is strictly convex.



1
Introduction

Suppose that
u
is the potential function of a complete Kähler-Einstein metric on a strictly pseudo-convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then
u
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\det(u_{i{\overline{j}}})=e^{(n+1)u}&\text{in\ \ }\Omega;\\
u(x)\rightarrow+\infty&\text{as\ \ }x\rightarrow\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Let
v=e^{-u}
, then
v
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v&v_{{\overline{j}}}\\
v_{i}&v_{i{\overline{j}}}\end{array}\right)=(-1)^{n},&\text{in\ \ }\Omega;\\
v=0,&\text{on \ \ }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

By studying the equations above, Cheng and Yau
[
CY80
]
proved the following result:

Theorem

(Corollary 6.6 of
[
CY80
]
)
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is a
C^{k}
,
k\geq\max(3n+6,2n+9)
, strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then there is a function
v\in C^{\omega}(\Omega)\cap C^{n+3/2-\delta}(\overline{\Omega})
, for any
\delta>0
, solving (
1
).

It’s interesting to observe that the regularity of
v
is less than
C^{n+3/2}
, regardless of
k
, which has been observed by Fefferman in an earlier work
[
F76
]
. Fefferman suggested that logarithmic terms exist following the
(n+1)
-th order term in the formal asymptotic expansion. This is proved by Lee and Melrose, in
[
LM82
]
. Suppose that
\Omega
is smooth and
\varphi
is a smooth defining function of
\Omega
. Lee and Melrose proved that

\displaystyle u+\log(-\varphi)-\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}\varphi^{(n+1)j}(\log(-\varphi))^{j}\in C^{(n+1)N-1}(\overline{\Omega}),

(1.6)

where
{a_{j}}^{\prime}s
are smooth functions in
\overline{\Omega}
.
The convergence of the above asymptotic expansion is proved by
[
HJ24
]
, using a method developed in
[
HJ23
]
, where a similar boundary regularity obstacle was studied for the minimal surface problem in hyperbolic spaces.

In this paper, by extending the computation of
[
H25
]
and
[
H24B
]
to non-degenerate complex Monge-Ampère equations, we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is a
C^{k}
,
k\geq\max(3n+6,2n+9)
, bounded strictly convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then the solution
u
of (
1
) is a strictly convex function.

In (
1
), if the complex Monge-Ampère operator is replaced by the Laplacian operator, the corresponding result is a consquence of the constant rank theorem of
[
C85
]
and
[
K87
]
.

Constant rank theorems for nonlinear equations were proved by
[
CGM07
]
,
[
BG09
]
and
[
SW16
]
. In
[
GP12
]
, a constant rank theorem for degenerate complex Monge-Ampère equation was proved in the case of low dimension. The general dimensional case remained as a conjecture until it was proved by the first author of this paper in
[
H25
]
. In
[
L09
]
, a constant rank theorem regarding complex Hessian was proved.

Readers may wonder if the result of this paper could be proved by directly applying the existing constant rank theorems (for example, Theorem 1 of
[
CGM07
]
). To use the existing constant rank theorems we need to
verify some structure conditions, in particular, an “inverse convexity” condition. Let
u
be a strictly convex function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
; let
H
be the real Hessian of
u
, and
A
be the complex Hessian of
u
. Denote the map

\displaystyle H\ \rightarrow\det{A}

(1.7)

by
F
. Constant rank theorems of
[
CGM07
]
,
[
BG09
]
and
[
SW16
]
require that

Condition 1

(Inverse Convexity Condition)
.

\displaystyle F(H^{-1})\text{ \ is a locally convex function of $H$.}

(1.8)

We believe that the above condition holds and that
\log(F(H^{-1}))
is a locally convex function of

H
; however, we do not yet have a proof for this.

In a couple of works
[
H25
]
and
[
H24B
]
, we developed a computation technique which is particularly useful and convenient for the convexity estimate of solutions to complex Monge-Ampère equations. But in the previous works, we only used this method for homogenous complex Monge-Ampère equation. We later discovered, the method can be applied to more general complex Monge-Ampère equaions.

Besides the convexity of the solution, there are many researches on the convexity of level sets of the solution or power(or logorithmic and exponential) convexity of the solutions
[
BGMX11
]

[
GX13
]

[
MO10
]

[
MX08
]

[

]

[
S15
]
. Our computation is useful in these settings too. In an upcomming paper
[
CHS26
]
, we prove the power convexity of solutions to non-degenerate complex Monge-Ampère equations in a convex domain.

2.3
Proving the Result Using the Maximum Principle

Let
{\bf s}=s^{i}\partial_{i}
be a constant vector field and

\displaystyle m=M_{p{\overline{q}}}s^{p}s^{{\overline{q}}}.

(2.27)

The result of the previous section implies

\displaystyle{u^{{i\overline{j}}}}\partial_{i\overline{j}}m\leq(n+1)m.

(2.28)

Corollary 6.6 of
[
CY80
]
says that
v\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
. Then equation (
1
) implies
\nabla v\neq 0
on
\partial\Omega
since
v=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Because
v=0
on
\partial\Omega
,
\Omega
is strictly convex and
\nabla v\neq 0
in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
, the level sets of
v
are strictly convex in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
. From this, we can prove that in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega

u
is convex. Since
u=-\log(v)
direct computation gives

\displaystyle D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u=\frac{1}{v}\left(-D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}v+\frac{1}{v}\nabla v\otimes\nabla v\right).

(2.29)

Because
-D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}v
is positive definite in the tangential direction of level sets adding
\frac{1}{v}\nabla v\otimes\nabla v
makes
-D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}v+\frac{1}{v}\nabla v\otimes\nabla
positive definite if
v
is small enough. Therefore for
r
small enough

\displaystyle D_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}u\text{\ is positive definite in \ \ \ \ }\Omega_{r}=\left\{z\in\Omega\ |\ 0<\text{dist}(z,\partial\Omega)<r\right\}.

(2.30)

Then Lemma
A.1
implies

\displaystyle M\text{\ is positive definite in \ \ \ \ }\Omega_{r}=\left\{z\in\Omega\ |\ 0<\text{dist}(z,\partial\Omega)<r\right\}.

(2.31)

So for any vector field
{\bf s}
the corresponding
m>0
in
\Omega_{r}
. Therefore the maximum principle (Corollary 3.2 of
[
GT01
]
) implies
m>0
in
\Omega
. It follows that
M
is positive definite in
\Omega
. Again using Lemma
A.1
, we conclude that
u
is strictly convex in
\Omega
.

Appendix A
Linear Algebra Lemma

In this paper, we need to estimate the real convexity of a function
u
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
using complex derivatives
u_{{i\overline{j}}}
,
u_{ij}
. The following lemma provides a tool for switching between complex and real contexts. The proof is almost the same as that of Lemma A6 of
[
H25
]
.

Lemma A.1
.

u
is a
C^{2}
function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, with coordinates
z_{i}=x_{i}+\sqrt{-1}y_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ ...\ ,n
. Denote the real Hessian of
u
by

\displaystyle H=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}U&V\\
V^{T}&W\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}&u_{x_{i}y_{j}}\\
u_{y_{i}x_{j}}&u_{y_{i}y_{j}}\end{array}\right).

Denote that

\displaystyle A=\left(u_{{i\overline{j}}}\right),\ \ \ \ \ B=\left(u_{ij}\right).

(A.5)

Then
u
is strictly convex (i.e.
H>0
) if and only if
A>0
and
A-B\overline{A^{-1}}\ \overline{B}>0
. In above, a matrix
>0
means that it’s positive definite.

Proof.

We only need to show that at a given point
H>0
if and only if
A>0
and
A-B\overline{A^{-1}}\ \overline{B}>0
. This becomes obvious once we derive the relation between
H
and
A,B
.

Direct computation gives

\displaystyle u_{{i\overline{j}}}=\frac{1}{4}\left(u_{x_{i}x_{j}}+u_{y_{i}y_{j}}+\sqrt{-1}(u_{x_{i}y_{j}}-u_{y_{i}x_{j}})\right);

(A.6)

\displaystyle u_{ij}=\frac{1}{4}\left(u_{x_{i}x_{j}}-u_{y_{i}y_{j}}-\sqrt{-1}(u_{x_{i}y_{j}}+u_{y_{i}x_{j}})\right).

(A.7)

Using the matrix notation, we have

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{4}(U+W+\sqrt{-1}(V-V^{\prime}));

(A.8)

\displaystyle B=\frac{1}{4}(U-W-\sqrt{-1}(V+V^{\prime})).

(A.9)

Let

\displaystyle Q=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
\overline{B}&\overline{A}\end{array}\right).

Q=Q^{\ast}
since
B
is symmetric.
Then (
A.8
) and (
A.9
) gives

\displaystyle Q=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&-\sqrt{-1}I\\
I&\sqrt{-1}I\end{array}\right)H\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&I\\
\sqrt{-1}I&-\sqrt{-1}I\end{array}\right).

This implies that
H>0
if and only if
Q>0
. When
A
is invertible, we can transform
Q
so that the relation between
Q
and
A-B\overline{A^{-1}}\ \overline{B}
is more obvious. We have

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A-B\overline{A^{-1}}\overline{B}&0\\
0&\overline{A}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&-B\overline{A^{-1}}\\
0&I\end{array}\right)Q\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
-\overline{A^{-1}}B^{\ast}&I\end{array}\right).

If
A
and
A-B\overline{A^{-1}}\overline{B}
are both positive definite, using (
A
) we know
Q
is positive definite. If
Q
is positive definite, it follows that

A
, being a principal submatrix of

Q
, is also positive definite.Then equation (
A
) implies that

A-B\overline{A^{-1}}\overline{B}
is positive definite.
