Executive Summary
The EPIC Buildings program was created to target Central New York’s (CNY) Energy Program Innovation
Cluster, focused on developing energy hardware innovations for next-generation grid-interactive
efficient buildings (GEB). The goals of the program were to establish and grow a sustainable regional
innovation cluster for GEB in CNY and support the ambitious transition to achieve net-zero emissions by
2050.

Project Activities
The EPIC Buildings program had six primary objectives: 1) recruit companies to participate in a regional
cluster that leverages innovation assets in the 12 counties of CenterState NY and facilitate technology
transfer connections with DOE national laboratories; 2) encourage development and growth of new
ventures; 3) develop and deliver programs to advance inclusivity in cluster firms and innovations; 4)
facilitate ideation of GEB and related innovations that promote public health and/or address inequities
experienced by members of disadvantaged communities; 5) collaborate with other DOE EPIC program
awardees to share best practices and successes; and 6) track project impacts and develop a model for
sustainability, enabling some activities to continue beyond the end of the award.

At the end of the project, a reinvigorated EPIC GEB regional cluster was established with 166
participating companies, including 36 companies that received 1:1 support during the project. Program
activities included networking and cluster development, offering educational programming for cluster
companies on topics such as SBIR/STTR applications, supports available to develop academic and
industry collaborations, opportunities to work with DOE national laboratories, cluster companies’
factory tours, as well as an annual showcase event for cluster companies to learn more about completed
projects, upcoming programming and to network with other companies, faculty, students, and
participating organizations. Throughout the three years of EPIC programming, more than 22 events were
held, both in-person and virtually. In addition, EPIC cluster companies were invited to participate in
SyracuseCoE educational programming, including its monthly Research & Technology Forum series, as
well as regular outreach on opportunities to apply for sponsored funding and other investments to
support product growth.

In each year of the EPIC program, cluster companies were invited to take advantage of three main
service offerings:

 1. A six-month Accelerator program was offered by project partner CenterState CEO and its Tech
 Garden.
 2. A three-month Intellectual Property landscape research project, working with students and
 faculty from Syracuse University’s Innovation Law Center. Over the course of the three-year
 program 15 companies were provided with this service, which included, for several, both an IP
 landscape research report, as well as a comprehensive market analysis.
 3. A Small-Scale Prototype program offering companies an opportunity to work with faculty and
 students over the summer months to advance the development of a product or product
 component that supports the company’s growth.

Lessons Learned
  • Summer prototype projects require an adequate commitment of resources to support; more
 funding should have been budgeted to meet the equipment and supplies needs of the
 companies, in addition to the student and faculty effort contributing to the execution of these
 short-term and small-scale projects. Close project management was required to ensure that
 projects were scaled appropriately and completed within the required timeframe, allowing
 participation and information sharing in the mid-August Innovation Showcase event.
  • Strategic availability of intellectual property (IP) landscape research remains critical to
 companies’ R&D and vision for growth. Companies found very high value in the work done with
 students and faculty from Syracuse University’s Law School and its Innovation Law Center to
 identify and understand existing IP that was aligned with their technology. They also valued the
 marketing analyses done by students to assess related market opportunities for their
 technologies.
  • Incorporate flexibility in programming to better meet companies’ changing needs. Cohort
 companies frequently pivoted in their goals as they learned more about IP and market
 opportunities related to their technologies. The EPIC Buildings Accelerator did an especially
 good job in their 1:1 work with companies to inform and accommodate these pivots as their
 engagements with the companies continued through the program.
  • Include plenty of opportunities to network in-person. The EPIC Buildings program hosted many
 well received educational events – both in-person and virtually – targeting topics of shared
 interest to participating companies; however, companies were most interested in the in-person
 facility tours and opportunities to connect with other cluster firms, faculty and students,
 especially as the pandemic restrictions lessened.
  • Use success stories to communicate/demonstrate opportunities found in programming.
  • Models for community-reach efforts targeting unique needs of start-ups is needed, as
 technologists can learn much about the perceived and potential value of their innovations from
 community members invited to test and/or deploy the products.
  • Continue to listen, listen, listen…and be ready to pivot based on what you hear!

Success Stories
  1. The EPIC Buildings program provided multiple supports to Hydronic Shell Technologies to
 advance the development of its innovative panelized exterior retrofit system and prototype
 significantly, resulting in new and strengthened relationships with other cluster companies,
 including EPIC cluster firm Cycle Retrotech, as well as demonstration partners, increased
 licensing activities, and hiring growth, including the development of enhanced investment
 materials and pitches, and more than $3.575 million in follow-on funding for demonstration and
 pilot projects in communities.
  2. M3 Innovation, a cluster member and sports lighting startup that uses innovative technology to
 lower the overall cost of the installation and operation of sports lighting solutions, leveraged its
 IP landscape and market research on a strong growth trajectory, resulting in an acquisition by
 Acuity Brands in 2025. The company had its 1st large-scale sale closed by sports stadium lighting
 innovator, M3 Innovation, informed by EPIC intellectual property landscape/marketing studies,
 and they completed their installation of the MAKO LED outdoor stadium lighting system in
 Kensington Oval, Barbados and Arnos Vale Stadium, St. Vincent, in advance of the ICC World Cup
