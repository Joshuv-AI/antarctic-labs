Transcendental b-divisors II — Monotonicity theorem
Mingchen Xia

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop the general intersection theory of nef b-divisors, extending the movable intersection theory. We define a notion of restricted volume of b-divisors and prove a quantitative version of the monotonicity of the intersection product. As a consequence, we prove a number of new volume inequalities of currents and cohomology classes.



1.
Introduction

The current paper is a sequel of the author’s previous paper
[
47
]
.

1.1.
Motivation

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.
This paper is motivated by the monotonicity theorem of Witt Nyström (see
[
43
,
15
]
): Given closed positive
(1,1)
-currents

T_{1},\ldots,T_{n},S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}

on
X
such that
T_{i}
and
S_{i}
lie in the same cohomology class and
T_{i}
is more singular than
S_{i}
for each
i
, then one has

(1.1)

\vol\left(S_{1},\dots,S_{n}\right)\geq\vol\left(T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\right).

Here the mixed volume is understood in the sense of Cao
[
10
]
. As proved in the previous paper
[
47
]
, this product also coincides the mixed volume in the sense of Darvas–Xia.

Inequality (
1.1
) expresses the principle that increasing singularities of currents lead to a loss of mass. However, the monotonicity theorem itself provides no quantitative or qualitative control on how much mass is lost.

From an intuitive point of view, the more singular the currents
T_{i}
are relative to
S_{i}
, the larger the difference in (
1.1
) should be. The main goal of this paper is to make this intuition precise.

To this end, one must first quantify the difference in singularities. A classical invariant serving this purpose is given by Lelong numbers. We aim to obtain a qualitative control of the difference in (
1.1
) in terms of the differences between the Lelong numbers of the currents
T_{i}
and
S_{i}
.

Related problems have been previously investigated by Vu, Su, among others; see for instance
[
36
,
38
,
40
,
37
]
. Their approaches rely on techniques
involving relative non-pluripolar products and density currents, and lead only to non-optimal lower bounds for the difference in (
1.1
). In contrast, in this paper we approach the problem using the theory of nef b-divisors, developed in
[
47
]
and
[
14
]
. As we shall show, this method allows us to obtain an optimal control of the difference in (
1.1
). As a consequence, we get a systematic way of obtaining new inequalities involving volumes and movable intersection products.

1.2.
Main results

We first extend the intersection theory of transcendental b-divisors over
X
initiated in
[
47
]
.
Recall that a nef b-divisor
\mathbb{D}
over
X
is a family of modified nef classes
\mathbb{D}_{Y}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(Y,\mathbb{R})
, with
Y\rightarrow X
running over all modifications of
X
. These classes are assumed to be compatible with each other under pushforward.

In
[
47
]
, we showed that nef b-divisors can be understood using the theory of currents, and defined the intersection number between
n=\dim X
nef b-divisors. The first goal of this paper is to develop the full intersection theory. In particular, we establish the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

(1)

There is a map sending
p
nef b-divisors
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
over
X
to a nef b-class
\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}
over
X
. The map is multi-
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
-linear
1
1

1

In the sense that the product is additive in each variable, while homogeneous only with respect to non-negative scalings.
and satisfies the expected properties of an intersection product.

(2)

When
\mathbb{D}_{i}=\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{i})
is the nef b-divisor induced by a pseudoeffective class
\alpha_{i}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
as in
Definition

3.11
for each
i=1,\ldots,p
, the product

(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p})_{X}

is just the movable intersection
\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle
in the sense of
[
5
,
6
]
.

(3)

In general, the intersection product can be recovered from the movable intersection as a limit:

(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p})_{X}=\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\langle\mathbb{D}_{1,Y}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbb{D}_{p,Y}\rangle,

where
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
runs over the modifications of
X
.

(4)

Suppose that
\mathbb{D}_{i}
is associated with an
\mathcal{I}
-good current
T_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,p
, then

(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p})_{X}=\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\},

where the bracket
\{\bullet\}
denotes the associated cohomology class. The Monge–Ampère type product is understood in the non-pluripolar sense of
[
3
,
26
,
7
]
.

The notion of nef b-class is a generalization of nef b-divisors. A nef b-class
\mathbb{T}
is a compatible choice of movable classes
\mathbb{T}_{Y}\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(Y,\mathbb{R})
. We refer to
Definition

3.2
for the details.

Part (2) shows that the intersection theory of b-divisors is a generalization of the more classical movable intersection theory. The latter has the drawback of being non-linear, making concrete applications difficult. By contrast, the intersection theory of b-divisors is linear. The non-linearity is completely encoded in the map from a pseudoeffective class
\alpha
to the associated nef b-divisors
\mathbb{D}(\alpha)
, which is easier to understand in general.

Part (4) shows that the intersection theory of b-divisors can also be regarded as a partial generalization of the non-pluripolar product. Although we can only recover the cohomology class of the non-pluripolar product, this is enough for most applications, since in practice, we usually only need the top intersection.

The monotonicity theorem (
1.1
) admits a generalization in the theory of b-divisors as follows: If we have another nef b-divisor
\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\prime}
which dominates
\mathbb{D}_{1}
in the sense that
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}_{1,Y}-\mathbb{D}_{1,Y}
is pseudoeffective for each modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
, then

\mathbb{D}^{\prime}_{1}\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\geq\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p},

in the sense that on each modification of
X
the difference is a positive cohomology class, as defined in
Definition

2.11
.

By the linearity of the intersection product, we could formally write the difference as

\left(\mathbb{D}^{\prime}_{1}-\mathbb{D}_{1}\right)\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}.

However, this term simply makes no sense, as
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}_{1}-\mathbb{D}_{1}
is not nef in general. However, it could be formally regarded as a linear combination of prime divisors over
X
with non-negative coefficients, at least when
\mathbb{D}_{1,X}=\mathbb{D}^{\prime}_{1,X}
.

When trying to give a formal meaning to these objects, in
Section

4
, we define the restricted volume of nef b-divisors, generalizing the notion of restricted volumes of cohomology classes
[
32
,
13
]
. More precisely, given nef b-divisors
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
over
X
and a prime divisor
D
over
X
, we shall define a class

\vol_{X|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)\in\mathrm{H}^{p+1,p+1}(X,\mathbb{R}).

We will show that these classes for various
X
are compatible with each other and form a b-class, which we denote by

\vol_{|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}\right).

After studying the restricted volumes in detail,
in
Section

5
we prove the following key inequality:

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
be nef b-divisors over
X
, and
T,T^{\prime}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
in the same cohomology class such that
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
. Then

(1.2)

\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})-\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\geq\sum_{D/X}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{D}_{p}\right).

The sum is over all prime divisors over
X
.

Equality holds in (
1.2
) when the
\mathbb{D}_{i}
’s are Cartier.

Here
\mathbb{D}(T)
is a nef b-divisor canonically constructed from
T
, as we studied in
[
45
,
47
]
. The notation
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
means that all Lelong numbers of
T
(on all modifications) dominate those of
T^{\prime}
. Equivalently, in the setup of the theorem, it says that
\mathbb{D}(T)\leq\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})
.

The main inequality (
1.2
) gives a quantitative version of the monotonicity theorem.
When
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
are nef b-divisors associated with currents,
B
gives a solution to our initial question in view of
A
(4).
We can for example formulate the following consequence:

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents with analytic singularities on
X
, and
T,T^{\prime}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in the same cohomology class such that
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
, then

(1.3)

\displaystyle\vol\left(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1},T^{\prime}\right)-\vol\left(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1},T\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\int_{\tilde{D}}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n-1}\left.\Bigl(\pi^{*}T_{i}-\nu(T_{i},D)[D]\Bigr)\right|_{\tilde{D}}.

Furthermore, the net on the right-hand side is eventually constant.

Here
\tilde{D}
is the normalization of
D
.

B
contains many new inequalities of the movable intersection product as well.
Instead of trying to give an exhaustive list of consequences of (
1.2
), we will only explain one such result (
Corollary

5.4
). Here in the introduction, we only mention a very elegant and special case of the latter as in
Corollary

5.5
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
[E]
be a divisorial closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
, say

[E]=\sum_{i}c_{i}E_{i},

where the
E_{i}
’s are distinct prime divisors on
X
and
c_{i}>0
. Consider modified nef classes
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p},\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Assume that
\beta\geq\{E\}
, then

\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge\beta\rangle-\Bigl\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge(\beta-\{E\})\Bigr\rangle\geq_{X}\sum_{i}\Bigl(\nu(\beta-\{E\},E_{i})+c_{i}\Bigr)\vol_{X|E_{i}}\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}\right).

The restricted volume of cohomology classes is defined in
Definition

4.19
.
A weaker estimate with
c_{i}
in place of
\nu(\beta-\{E\},E_{i})+c_{i}
can also be obtained using the theory of relative non-pluripolar products, at least when
[E]
has finitely many prime components.

As another application of our main theorem, in
Section

6
we prove the following version of the loss of mass theorem stated in the more traditional language:

Theorem C
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a big class. Consider closed positive
(1,1)
-currents
S,T\in\alpha
with
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}S
. Fix a prime divisor
D
over
X
, then

(1.4)

\vol S-\vol T\geq\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(S,D)\Bigr)^{n}\cdot\frac{\vol S}{\Bigl(\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-\nu(\alpha,D)\Bigr)^{n}}.

The notation
\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)
(resp.
\nu(\alpha,D)
) denotes the maximal (resp. minimal) Lelong number of closed positive currents in
\alpha
.
There is also a more general version of (
1.4
) for mixed volumes, see
Corollary

6.15
.

This kind of problems have been extensively studied by Vu and Su, see
[
40
,
38
,
37
]
. As we shall see in the toric example
Example

6.4
, the second factor in this kind of estimate cannot be a universal constant, contrary to the assertions in the literature.
Our result improves all known results in this direction. More importantly, our result is the first estimate with explicit constants.

Interestingly, our approach to
C
relies on the recent development in the theory of transcendental Okounkov bodies in
[
17
]
. Based on the volume formula of transcendental Okounkov bodies, we established the following estimate of the restricted volume, which has independent interests as well.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a big class. Then for any prime divisor
D
on
X
, we have

\vol_{X|D}\Bigl(\alpha-t\{D\}\Bigr)\geq\frac{\vol\alpha}{\Bigl(\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-\nu(\alpha,D)\Bigr)^{n}}\cdot\min\Bigl\{t-\nu(\alpha,D),\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-t\Bigr\}^{n-1}

as long as
\nu(\alpha,D)\leq t\leq\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)
.

From our proof, it is easy to see that the estimate is sharp, at least in the toric setting.

Finally it is of interest if we can improve (
1.2
) in
B
to an equality. For this purpose, the conjectural transcendental Morse inequality seems unavoidable. For the next result, we content ourselves to projective manifolds only, since the transcendental Morse inequality has been confirmed by Witt Nyström
[
42
]
in this case.

Theorem D
.

Assume that
X
is projective.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a big and nef b-divisors over
X
,
T,T^{\prime}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
in the same cohomology class such that
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
. Then

(1.5)

\Bigl(\mathbb{D}^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\Bigr)-\Bigl(\mathbb{D}^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\Bigr)=\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{Y}\right).

If we further assume the transcendental Morse inequality, then the same holds for all compact Kähler manifolds
X
.

One could also polarize (
1.5
) to get an equality with
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{n-1}
in place of a single
\mathbb{D}
.

In dimension
1
, since the transcendental Morse inequality is trivially true, (
1.5
) reduces to the following:

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume that
X
is a compact Riemann surface. Let
T,T^{\prime}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
in the same cohomology class such that
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
. Then

(1.6)

\vol T^{\prime}-\vol T=\sum_{x\in X}\Bigl(\nu(T,x)-\nu(T^{\prime},x)\Bigr).

This is already non-trivial. It clearly shows that the loss of mass on a Riemann surface can be decomposed as the sum of the Lelong number differences.
Based on (
1.6
), it seems natural to define a new Monge–Ampère measure of
T
as
T-\sum_{x\in X}\nu(T,x)[x]
. The total mass of this measure is exactly
\vol T
, and this measure dominates the non-pluripolar measure of
T
.
It is of interest to know if one can define similar measures in higher dimensions. This problem is closely related to the convergence of partial Bergman kernels, see
[
19
,
9
]
.

Similarly, in dimension
2
, the transcendental Morse inequality has been confirmed in Y. Deng’s thesis
[
23
]
, and hence (
1.5
) is also an equality. Since we have a relatively complete understanding of divisors over surfaces, it should be possible to extract useful algebrico-geometric information about Kähler surfaces from our equality. The author wishes to explore this point in the near future.

C. Favre suggested that the right-hand side of (
1.5
) could be interpreted as an integral over the Berkovich analytification of
X
with respect to a limit measure lying on a suitable skeleton. This novel measure might be useful for the study of dynamical systems on
X
.

It is particularly illuminating to look at the toric situation. In this case, in
Section

7
, we will show that this limit measure is precisely the classical mixed area measure between convex bodies, as defined by Aleksandrov and Fenchel–Jenssen in the 1930s. Furthermore, our formula (
1.5
) is then precisely the Minkowski volume formula expressing the difference of mixed volumes of convex bodies in terms of the integral of the difference between the support functions against the mixed area measure:

Corollary 1.5
.

Consider convex bodies
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1},Q^{\prime},Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
Q^{\prime}\supseteq Q
, then

\vol\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1},Q^{\prime}\right)-\vol\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1},Q\right)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\Supp_{Q^{\prime}}-\Supp_{Q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{S}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1}\right).

Here
\Supp
denotes the support function and
\mathcal{S}
denotes the mixed area measure. See
Section

7
for the details.
In fact, our
D
gives a new construction of the mixed area measure.

Similar to the mixed area measure, the general limit measure should be an object of great interest.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions

Let
X
be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.

We briefly recall the notions of
P
and
\mathcal{I}
-equivalences. For the details, see
[
44
, Chapter 3, Chapter 6]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\varphi,\psi
be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on
X
. We say
\varphi\sim_{P}\psi
(resp.
\varphi\preceq_{P}\psi
) if there is a closed smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
on
X
such that
\varphi,\psi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)_{>0}
and

P_{\theta}[\varphi]=P_{\theta}[\psi]\quad(\textup{resp.}P_{\theta}[\varphi]\leq P_{\theta}[\psi]).

Here
\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
denotes the space of
\theta
-plurisubharmonic functions on
X
and
\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)_{>0}
denotes the subset consisting of
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
with
\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}>0
, with
\theta_{\varphi}=\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi
.
Here and in the sequel, the Monge–Ampère type product
\theta_{\varphi}^{n}
is always understood in the non-pluripolar sense of
[
3
,
26
,
7
]
. The envelope
P_{\theta}
is defined as follows:

P_{\theta}[\varphi]\coloneqq\operatorname*{sup*}_{\!\!\!C\in\mathbb{R}}(\varphi+C)\land 0,

where
(\varphi+C)\land 0
is the maximal element in
\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
dominated by both
\varphi+C
and
0
.

Given a closed smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
on
X
so that
\varphi,\psi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
, we also say
\theta_{\varphi}\sim_{P}\theta_{\psi}
(resp.
\theta_{\varphi}\preceq_{P}\theta_{\psi}
) if
\varphi\sim_{P}\psi
(resp.
\varphi\preceq_{P}\psi
).
We write
\varphi\preceq\psi
is
\varphi\leq\psi+C
for some constant
C
. Similarly, we write
\theta_{\varphi}\preceq\theta_{\psi}
.
The same convention applies also to the
\mathcal{I}
-partial order introduced later.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\varphi,\psi
be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on
X
. We say
\varphi\sim_{\mathcal{I}}\psi
(resp.
\varphi\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}\psi
) if
\mathcal{I}(\lambda\varphi)=\mathcal{I}(\lambda\psi)
(resp.
\mathcal{I}(\lambda\varphi)\subseteq\mathcal{I}(\lambda\psi)
) for all real
\lambda>0
.

Here
\mathcal{I}
denotes the multiplier ideal sheaf in the sense of Nadel.

If
\theta
is a closed smooth real
(1,1)
-form such that
\varphi,\psi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
, then
\varphi\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}\psi
if and only if

P_{\theta}[\varphi]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq P_{\theta}[\psi]_{\mathcal{I}},

where

P_{\theta}[\varphi]_{\mathcal{I}}=\sup\left\{\eta\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta):\eta\leq 0,\mathcal{I}(\lambda\varphi)\supseteq\mathcal{I}(\lambda\eta)\textup{ for all }\lambda>0\right\}.

Equivalently, we may replace
\supseteq
by
=
in this equation.

Another equivalent formulation of
Definition

2.2
is that for any prime divisor
E
over
X
, we have

\nu(\varphi,E)=\nu(\psi,E)\quad\textup{resp. }\nu(\varphi,E)\geq\nu(\psi,E).

Here
\nu
denotes the generic Lelong number.

We briefly recall the notion of prime divisors over a complex space
Z
. A prime divisor over
Z
is a prime divisor
E
on
Y
, where
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow Z
is a proper bimeromorphic map from a complex manifold. Consider another prime divisor
E^{\prime}
on
Y^{\prime}
, where
\pi^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\rightarrow Z
is another such map. These divisors are considered equivalent if there is a proper bimeromorphic map
\pi^{\prime\prime}\colon Y^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow Z
from a complex manifold dominating both
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
so that the strict transforms of
E
and
E^{\prime}
on
Y^{\prime\prime}
are the same. By abuse of language, when we talk about a prime divisor over
Z
, we sometimes refer to such an equivalence class.

Given any
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
, we have

\varphi-\sup_{X}\varphi\leq P_{\theta}[\varphi]\leq P_{\theta}[\varphi]_{\mathcal{I}}.

The operation
P_{\theta}[\bullet]_{\mathcal{I}}
is idempotent. We say
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
is
\mathcal{I}
-model
if
P_{\theta}[\varphi]_{\mathcal{I}}=\varphi
. Similarly, on the subset
\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)_{>0}
, the operation
P_{\theta}[\bullet]
is also idempotent. We say
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)_{>0}
is
model
if
P_{\theta}[\varphi]=\varphi
.

A quasi-plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
on
X
is called
\mathcal{I}
-good
if there is a closed smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
on
X
such that
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)_{>0}
and

P_{\theta}[\varphi]=P_{\theta}[\varphi]_{\mathcal{I}}.

This notion is independent of the choice of
\theta
.
For any closed smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\theta^{\prime}
on
X
so that
\theta^{\prime}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi\geq 0
, we also say the current
\theta^{\prime}_{\varphi}
is
\mathcal{I}
-good.

Given a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
on
X
, we write
T=\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi
, then we define

\vol T\coloneqq\int_{X}\left(\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta}[\varphi]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)^{n}.

This definition is independent of the choice of the decomposition of
T
as
\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi
. In general,

\vol T\geq\int_{X}T^{n},

and if
\int_{X}T^{n}>0
, equality holds if and only if
T
is
\mathcal{I}
-good. More generally, if
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
are closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
with positive non-pluripolar masses, we define

\vol(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\coloneqq\int_{X}\left(\theta_{1}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta_{1}}[\varphi_{1}]\right)\wedge\cdots\wedge\left(\theta_{n}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta_{n}}[\varphi_{n}]\right),

where we write
T_{i}=\theta_{i}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{i}
for each
i
. In general, when the masses possibly vanish, we define

\vol(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\vol(T_{1}+\epsilon\omega,\ldots,T_{n}+\epsilon\omega)

for any Kähler form
\omega
on
X
. Note that

\vol(T)=\vol(T,\ldots,T)

for any closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
on
X
.

We say a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
has analytic singularities if locally
T
can be written as
\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f
, where
f
is a plurisubharmonic function of the following form:

c\log(|f_1|^2+\cdots+|f_N|^2)+R,

where
c\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
are holomorphic functions on
X
and
R
is a bounded function. When we write
T=\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi
for some smooth closed real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
and
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
, we also say
\varphi
has analytic singularities.

As a particular case, if
D
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
, we say a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
has
log singularities
along
D
if
T-[D]
is positive, and has locally bounded potentials. It is easy to see that
T
has analytic singularities. Conversely, if we begin with
T
with analytic singularities, there is always a modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
so that
\pi^{*}T
has log singularities along an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Y
. See
Definition

2.3
for our definition of modifications.

Let
\theta
be a smooth closed real
(1,1)
-form on
X
and
\eta\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
. We say a sequence
(\eta^{j})_{j}
of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions is a
quasi-equisingular approximation
of
\eta
if the following are satisfied:

(1)

for each
j
,
\eta^{j}
has analytic singularities;

(2)

(\eta^{j})_{j}
is decreasing with limit
\eta
;

(3)

for each
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda>0
, we can find
j_{0}>0
so that for
j\geq j_{0}
,

\mathcal{I}(\lambda^{\prime}\eta^{j})\subseteq\mathcal{I}(\lambda\eta);

(4)

There is a decreasing sequence
(\epsilon_{j})_{j}
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with limit
0
, and a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
so that

\eta^{j}\in\mathrm{PSH}\left(X,\theta+\epsilon_{j}\omega\right)

for each
j>0
.

The existence of quasi-equisingular approximations is guaranteed by
[
22
]
. We also say
(\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta^{j})_{j}
is a quasi-equisingular approximation of
\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta
. When
\theta_{\eta}
is a Kähler current, we can (and we always do) take a quasi-equisingular approximation in the same cohomology class of
\{\theta\}
.

Suppose that
\{\theta\}
is big.
It is shown in
[
16
]
that there is a pseudometric
d_{S}
on
\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
satisfying the following inequality:
For any
\varphi,\psi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
, we have

(2.1)

\begin{split}d_{S}(\varphi,\psi)\leq&\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(2\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi\lor\psi}^{j}\wedge\theta_{V_{\theta}}^{n-j}-\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi}^{j}\wedge\theta_{V_{\theta}}^{n-j}-\int_{X}\theta_{\psi}^{j}\wedge\theta_{V_{\theta}}^{n-j}\right)\\
\leq&C_{n}d_{S}(\varphi,\psi),\end{split}

where
C_{n}=3(n+1)2^{n+2}
. Here
V_{\theta}=\max\{\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta):\varphi\leq 0\}
.
Moreover,
d_{S}(\varphi,\psi)=0
if and only if
\varphi\sim_{P}\psi
. In particular, the
d_{S}
-pseudometric descends to a pseudometric (still denoted by
d_{S}
) on the space of closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in
\{\theta\}
.

Given a net of closed positive
(1,1)
-currents
T_{i}
in
\{\theta\}
, and another closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
in
\{\theta\}
. Then
T_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{S}}T
if and only if
T_{i}+\omega\xrightarrow{d_{S}}T+\omega
for any Kähler form
\omega
on
X
.

In general, given closed positive
(1,1)
-currents
T_{i}
and
T
on
X
, we say
T_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{S}}T
if we can find Kähler forms
\omega_{i}
and
\omega
on
X
such that the
T_{i}+\omega_{i}
’s and
T+\omega
represent the same cohomology class and
T_{i}+\omega_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{S}}T+\omega
. This definition is independent of the choices of the
\omega_{i}
’s and
\omega
.
Quasi-equisingular approximations provide the primary source of
d_{S}
-convergent sequences.

The proceeding theory can be easily extended to compact normal Kähler spaces, as explained in the appendix of
[
44
]
.

2.2.
Modifications and cones

Let
X
be a reduced compact Kähler space of dimension
n
.

In this paper, we use the word
modification
in a very non-standard sense.

Definition 2.3
.

A
modification
of
X
is a bimeromorphic morphism
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
, which is a finite composition of blow-ups with smooth centers.

We say a modification
\pi^{\prime}\colon Z\rightarrow X

dominates
another
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
if there is a morphism
g\colon Z\rightarrow Y
making the following diagram commutative:

(2.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

The modifications of
X
together with the domination relation form a directed set.

Fix a reference Kähler form
\omega
on
X
.
Recall that a class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is
modified nef
or
movable
if for any
\epsilon>0
, we can find a closed
(1,1)
-current
T\in\alpha
such that

(1)

T+\epsilon\omega\geq 0
;

(2)

\nu(T+\epsilon\omega,D)=0
for any prime divisor
D
on
X
.

This definition is independent of the choice of
\omega
.
Here
\nu(\bullet,D)
denote the generic Lelong number along
D
.

These classes are called
nef en codimension
1
in Boucksom’s thesis
[
5
]
, where they were introduced for the first time. Modified nef classes form a closed convex cone in
\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Note that a modified nef class is necessarily pseudoeffective. A nef class is obviously modified nef.

Recall the multiplicity of a cohomology class as defined in
[
5
, Section 2.1.3]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a pseudoeffective class and
D
be a prime divisor on
X
. We define the Lelong number
\nu(\alpha,D)
as follows:

(1)

When
\alpha
is big, define
\nu(\alpha,D)=\nu(T,D)
for any closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T\in\alpha
with minimal singularities (namely, a current in
\alpha
that is less singular than any current in
\alpha
).

(2)

In general, define

\nu(\alpha,D)\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\nu(\alpha+\epsilon\{\omega\},D).

When
\alpha
is big, (2) is compatible with (1) and the definition is independent of the choice of
\omega
.
By definition, a pseudoeffective class
\alpha
is modified nef if and only if
\nu(\alpha,D)=0
for all prime divisors
D
on
X
.

Let
T
be a closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
. Then we define the
regular part

\Reg T
of
T
as the regular part of
T
with respect to Siu’s decomposition. In other words, we write

(2.3)

T=\Reg T+\sum_{i}c_{i}[E_{i}],

where
E_{i}
is a countable collection of prime divisors on
X
and
c_{i}=\nu(T,E_{i})>0
; the regular part
\Reg T
is a closed positive
(1,1)
-current whose generic Lelong number along each prime divisor on
X
is
0
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
on
X
is
non-divisorial
(resp.
divisorial
) if
T=\Reg T
(resp.
\Reg T=0
).

Note that the cohomology class of a non-divisorial current is always modified nef. Conversely, a current with minimal singularities in a
big
and modified nef class is always non-divisorial.

2.3.
Partially ordered linear spaces

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional real vector space, and
C\subseteq V
be a convex cone satisfying the following assumptions:

(C1)

C
is closed;

(C2)

C
is pointed, namely
C\cap(-C)=\{0\}
;

(C3)

C
is full-dimensional, namely
V=C-C
.

Strictly speaking,
(C3)
is not necessary: We can always replace
V
by
C-C
.

Given
x,y\in V
, we define
x\leq y
if
y-x\in C
. Thanks to
(C2)
,
\leq
defines a partial order. In particular, we can talk about increasing and decreasing nets.
This partial order has the following properties:

(VO1)

Given
x,y,z\in V
, if
x\leq y
, then
x+z\leq y+z
;

(VO2)

for any
\lambda\geq 0
and
x,y\in V
, if
x\leq y
, then
\lambda x\leq\lambda y
.

In the terminology of
[
2
]
,
\leq
is a
vector-ordering
of
V
. The results in this section can be proved using the more general theory in
[
2
]
, but we shall give elementary proofs for the ease of the readers.

Let
V^{\vee}
denote the dual vector space of
V
.
Let
C^{\vee}\subseteq V^{\vee}
be the dual cone of
C
, namely,

C^{\vee}=\left\{\ell\in V^{\vee}:\ell|_{C}\geq 0\right\}.

It is well-known that under the assumption of
(C1)
and
(C2)
,
C^{\vee}
has full dimension. See
[
8
, Exercise 2.31]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
is a decreasing net in
C
, then
(x_{i})_{i}
converges to some element in
x\in C
. Moreover,
x\leq x_{i}
for all
i\in I
.

Proof.

Since
C^{\vee}
has full dimension, we can choose
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}\in C^{\vee}
forming a basis of
V^{\vee}
. Then the linear isomorphism

L\coloneqq(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n})\colon V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

maps
C
into a cone
C^{\prime}
contained in the first quadrant.

In particular,
(L(x_{i}))_{i\in I}
is a net in the first quadrant and each component forms a decreasing net. The usual monotone convergence theorem shows that
(L(x_{i}))_{i}
has a limit
y
in the first quadrant. Then
x_{i}\to x\coloneqq L^{-1}(y)
. Thanks to
(C1)
,
x\in C
.

Next fix
i\in I
, we have

x_{j}\leq x_{i},\quad\forall j\in I,j\geq i.

Using
(C1)
again, we find
x\leq x_{i}
.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Suppose that
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
is an increasing net in
V
and
y\in V
is such that
x_{i}\leq y
for all
i
, then
(x_{i})_{i}
converges to some
x\leq y
. Furthermore,
x\geq x_{i}
for each
i\in I
.

Proof.

It suffices to apply
Proposition

2.6
to the net
(y-x_{i})_{i\in I}
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(y_{i})_{i>0}
be a sequence in
C
so that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}y_{i}
converges. Consider sequences
(x_{i}^{j})_{j>0}
in
C
for all
i>0
so that

\displaystyle x_{i}^{j}\leq y_{i},

\displaystyle\forall i,j>0;

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}x_{i}^{j}=x_{i},

\displaystyle\forall i>0.

Then for all
j>0
,
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{j}

converges and

(2.4)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}.

Proof.

For each
j>0
, the convergence of
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{j}
follows from
Corollary

2.7
.

As for (
2.4
), as in the proof of
Proposition

2.6
, assume that
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. In this case, it suffices to apply the dominated convergence theorem to each component.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
y\in C
. Consider increasing sequences
(x_{i}^{j})_{j>0}
in
C
for all
i>0
with limits
x_{i}
. Assume that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{j}\leq y,\quad\forall j>0,

Then

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}.

Proof.

As in the proof of
Proposition

2.6
, assume that
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. In this case, it suffices to apply Levi’s monotone convergence theorem to each component.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
,
(y_{i})_{i\in I}
,
(z_{i})_{i\in I}
be nets in
C
with
x_{i}\to x
for some
x\in C
and
z_{i}\to x
. Assume that
x_{i}\geq y_{i}\geq z_{i}
for all
i\in I
, then
y_{i}\to x
.

Proof.

As in the proof of
Proposition

2.6
, we may assume that
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
C=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Then it suffices to apply the squeeze theorem to the components.
∎

2.4.
Cones in cohomology

Let
X
be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.

In
Appendix

A
, we shall briefly recall Lelong’s theory of positive forms.

Definition 2.11
.

We say a class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
is
positive
if for all weakly positive closed
(n-p,n-p)
-form
F
on
X
, we have

\alpha\cap\{F\}\geq 0.

We write
\alpha\geq_{X}0
in this case.

Here
\{F\}
refers to the cohomology class in
\mathrm{H}^{n-p,n-p}(X,\mathbb{R})
represented by
F
. The cap notation
\cap
refers to the cohomology pairing.

When we have two classes
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
, we write
\alpha\geq_{X}\beta
if
\alpha-\beta\geq_{X}0
. Similarly, the notation
\leq_{X}
has the obvious meaning.

If
\alpha
contains a closed strongly positive
(p,p)
-current, it is clearly positive. The author does not know if the converse holds in general. When
p=n-1
, the converse follows from
[
21
, Theorem 0.1]
. See
[
12
]
for a proof without relying on the dubious Demailly regularization on singular complex spaces.
When
p=1
, the converse also holds, namely a class in
\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is positive if and only if it is pseudoeffective. In this case, we usually omit the subindex
X
and write
\alpha\geq\beta
.
The non-trivial implication is a theorem of Lamari, see
[
30
, Lemme 3.3]
.

An example of positive classes is given by non-pluripolar products.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
. Then
T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}
is strongly positive, and hence
\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\}
is positive.

Here and in the sequel
T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}
denotes the non-pluripolar product as before.

Although this result is definitely known to experts, it seems missing from the literature. We take this opportunity to give a proof.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}
is strongly positive. The problem is then local. We are reduced to the following: Suppose that
U
is a domain set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{p}
are plurisubharmonic functions on
U
. Assume that
\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{p}
is well-defined, then it is strongly positive.

Step 1
. We first assume that each
\varphi_{i}
is bounded. Then taking convolution with Friedrichs mollifiers, we may assume that each
\varphi_{i}
is smooth. In this case, the strong positivity of
\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{p}
follows from
[
20
, Page 169, Proposition 1.11]
.

Step 2
. We handle the general case.

Let
\eta
be a compactly supported smooth closed weakly positive
(n-p,n-p)
-form on
U
. We need to show that

(2.5)

\int_{U}\left(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{p}\right)\wedge\eta\geq 0.

For this purpose, we apply
[
7
, Lemma 1.5]
and get

\int_{U}\left(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{p}\right)\wedge\eta=\lim_{C\to\infty}\int_{\{\varphi_{i}>-C\}}\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\left(\varphi_{i}\lor(-C)\right)\right)\wedge\eta.

But each term is non-negative by Step 1, and hence (
2.5
) follows.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

The cone of positive classes in
\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
satisfy
(C1)
,
(C2)
and
(C3)
.

In particular, the abstract results proved in
Section

2.3
can be applied. In the sequel of this paper, we shall apply them without further mentioning.

Proof.

The condition
(C1)
is obvious.

Let us prove
(C2)
. Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a class with vanishing intersection with all classes of the form
\{F\}
, where
F
is a closed weakly positive
(n-p,n-p)
-form. We need to show that
\alpha=0
.

Fix a general cohomology class
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{n-p,n-p}(X,\mathbb{R})
, it suffices to show that

(2.6)

\alpha\cap\beta=0.

For this purpose, take a closed real
(n-p,n-p)
-form
G
representing
\beta
. Fix a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
. We observe that

G+C\omega^{n-p}

is positive when
C
is large enough. In fact, thanks to the compactness of
X
, this problem is essentially local, we can consider it on a coordinate chart on
X
. Then this is just a simple linear algebra. For later use, let us observe that we can in fact guarantee that
G+C\omega^{n-p}
is strongly positive, as follows from
Theorem

A.6
.

Now by our assumption,

\alpha\cap\left\{C\omega^{n-p}\right\}=0,\quad\alpha\cap\left\{G+C\omega^{n-p}\right\}=0.

Therefore, (
2.6
) follows.

Finally let us prove
(C3)
. This follows form the observation in proving
(C2)
: A closed smooth real
(p,p)
-form can always be represented as the difference of two closed smooth strongly positive
(p,p)
-forms.
∎

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
f\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a proper morphism from a Kähler manifold, if
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(Y,\mathbb{R})
is positive, then so is
f_{*}\alpha
.

Note that we do not require that
f
be bimeromorphic, nor surjective.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that the pull-back of a weakly positive form is weakly positive.
∎

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a nef class and
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a positive class. Then
\alpha\cap\beta
is positive.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\alpha
is a Kähler class. Then our assertion follows from the fact that the wedge product of a positive
(1,1)
-form with a weakly positive form is weakly positive.
∎

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a positive class. Assume that for each Kähler class
\beta
, we have
\alpha\cap\beta^{n-p}=0
, then
\alpha=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha\neq 0
, then we can find a closed real
(n-p,n-p)
-form
F
so that
\alpha\cap\{F\}<0
.
As in the proof of
Proposition

2.13
, we can find a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
so that
F+\omega^{n-p}
is positive. Then by our assumption, we know that
\alpha\cap\{F+\omega^{n-p}\}<0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

A source of positive classes is provided by the following monotonicity theorem proved in increasing order of generality by
[
43
,
15
,
39
]
:

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be pseudoeffective classes. Consider closed positive
(1,1)
-currents
T_{i},S_{i}\in\alpha_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,p
. Assume that
T_{i}\preceq_{P}S_{i}
for all
i
. Then

(2.7)

\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\right\}\leq_{X}\left\{S_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge S_{p}\right\}.

The notion of
P
-partial order is recalled in
Section

2.1
. In particular, the cohomology class

\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\}

depends only on the
P
-equivalence classes of the
T_{i}
’s and the cohomology classes of the
T_{i}
’s.

Proof.

Take a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
and replacing
T_{i}
and
S_{i}
by
T_{i}+\epsilon\omega
and
S_{i}+\epsilon\omega
respectively for some small
\epsilon>0
, we can reduce to the case where the
T_{i}
’s and the
S_{i}
’s are all Kähler currents.

Take a smooth closed real
(1,1)
-form
\theta_{i}\in\alpha_{i}
and write

T_{i}=\theta_{i}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi_{i},\quad S_{i}=\theta_{i}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi_{i}.

Then
\varphi_{i}\preceq_{P}\psi_{i}
.

Step 1
. We first prove that

\Bigl\{\left(\theta_{1}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi_{1}\right)\wedge\cdots\wedge\left(\theta_{p}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi_{p}\right)\Bigr\}=\Bigl\{\left(\theta_{1}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta_{1}}[\psi_{1}]\right)\wedge\cdots\wedge\left(\theta_{p}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta_{p}}[\psi_{p}]\right)\Bigr\}.

The
\leq_{X}
direction is proved in
[
39
, Theorem 4.4]
. Therefore, using
Lemma

2.16
, in order to prove the equality, we may assume that
p=n
. In this case, the assertion follows from the usual monotonicity theorem. See
[
44
, Proposition 6.1.4]
.

Step 2
. By Step 1, we may replace
\psi_{i}
by
P_{\theta_{i}}[\psi_{i}]
and assume that
\varphi_{i}\preceq\psi_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,p
.
By Vu’s result
[
39
, Theorem 4.4]
again, we conclude (
2.7
).
∎

Let us also recall another result for later use.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be big classes. Consider nets of closed positive
(1,1)
-currents
(T_{i}^{j})_{j\in J}
in
\alpha_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,p
. Assume that
T_{i}^{j}\xrightarrow{d_{S}}T_{i}\in\alpha_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,p
. Then

(2.8)

\lim_{j\in J}\left\{T_{1}^{j}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}^{j}\right\}=\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\right\}.

This follows
verbatim
from the proof of
[
44
, Theorem 6.2.1]
, with
Theorem

2.17
in place of the usual monotonicity theorem.

For later use, we recall the behavior of the non-pluripolar products under bimeromorphic morphisms:

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold. Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
. Then

\displaystyle\pi^{\lozenge}(T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p})=

\displaystyle\pi^{*}T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi^{*}T_{p},

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\left(\pi^{*}T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi^{*}T_{p}\right)=

\displaystyle T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}.

Here
\pi^{\lozenge}
is defined as follows: Take a smallest Zariski closed subset

so that
\pi
is an isomorphism outside

(p,p)
-current

on
X

\pi^{\lozenge}T
be the zero-extension of
\pi|_{Y\setminus\pi^{-1}(Z)}^{*}(T)
to
Y
.

Both assertions follow easily from the fact that the non-pluripolar products put no mass on proper analytic sets.

Definition 2.20
.

A class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
is
strictly movable
if there is a proper bimeromorphic morphism
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
from a Kähler manifold
Y
, and Kähler classes
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{p}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(Y,\mathbb{R})
so that

(2.9)

\alpha=\pi_{*}\left(\beta_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\beta_{p}\right).

A class in the closed convex cone generated by strictly movable classes is called a
movable class
.

The cone of movable classes in
\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
is a closed convex cone. A movable class is clearly positive.

Example 2.21
.

When

, a class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is movable if and only if it is modified nef. This is
[

.

When

or

, we have canonical identifications
\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}
. A class corresponding to

is movable if and only if
t\geq 0
.

Example 2.22
.

In (
2.9
), if
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{p}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(Y,\mathbb{R})
are nef,
\alpha
is still movable. This follows immediately from the continuity of
\pi_{*}
.

Proposition 2.23
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X

Proof.

We may assume that
\alpha
is strictly movable. Then our assertion follows immediately from the definition.

2.5.
Movable intersection theory

Let
X
be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. Consider pseudoeffective
(1,1)
-classes
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 2.25
.

The
movable intersection

\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
is defined as follows:

(1)

When the
\alpha_{i}
’s are all big, we take closed positive
(1,1)
-currents with minimal singularities
T_{i,\min}
in each
\alpha_{i}
and let

\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle=\left\{T_{1,\min}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p,\min}\right\};

(2)

in general, define

\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\Bigl\langle(\alpha_{1}+\epsilon\beta)\wedge\cdots\wedge(\alpha_{p}+\epsilon\beta)\Bigr\rangle,

where
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is a Kähler class.

The movable intersection is independent of the choices we made. Moreover, it is always a positive class. The current definition is taken from
[
7
]
. A more traditional (but equivalent) definition can be found in
[
5
,
6
]
.

Proposition 2.26
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p},\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\alpha,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{p}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be pseudoeffective classes, and
\lambda\geq 0
. We have the following properties:

(1)

The movable intersection is symmetric: For each permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,p\}
, we have

\left\langle\alpha_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{\sigma(p)}\right\rangle=\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle.

(2)

The movable intersection is superadditive in each variable:

\left\langle(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{1}^{\prime})\wedge\alpha_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\right\rangle\geq_{X}\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle+\langle\alpha^{\prime}_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle.

(3)

The movable intersection is homogeneous in each variable:

\langle\lambda\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle=\lambda\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle.

(4)

The movable intersection is increasing in each variable: Suppose that
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha^{\prime}_{1}
, then

\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle\geq_{X}\langle\alpha^{\prime}_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle.

(5)

We have

(2.10)

\alpha=\langle\alpha\rangle+\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(\alpha,D)\{D\},

where
D
runs over the set of prime divisors on
X
.

(6)

We have

\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\Bigl\langle(\alpha_{1}+\epsilon\beta_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\alpha_{p}+\epsilon\beta_{p})\Bigr\rangle.

(7)

When
\alpha_{1}
is nef, we have

\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle=\alpha_{1}\cap\langle\alpha_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\rangle

See
[
6
, Theorem 3.5]
for the proof of most parts. Part (6) is proved in
[
5
, Proposition 3.2.4]
.

The decomposition (
2.10
) is usually referred to as the divisorial Zariski decomposition or the Boucksom–Nakayama decomposition.

The volume of pseudoeffective class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is defined as
\vol(\alpha)\coloneqq\langle\alpha^{n}\rangle
.

3.
Generalized intersection theory

Let
X
be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. In the whole section,
p,q
will denote two non-negative integers.

We remind the readers that the word
modification
takes a very non-standard meaning in this paper, as we recalled in
Definition

2.3
.

3.1.
The notion of b-classes

We introduce the central object of interest in this paper — The b-classes.

Definition 3.1
.

A
(Weil) b-class

\mathbb{T}
of degree
p
over
X
is an element in

(3.1)

\varprojlim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(Y,\mathbb{R}),

where
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
runs over the directed set of modifications of
X
. Here the limit is taken in the category of real vector spaces.

Equivalently, thanks to Hironaka’s Chow lemma
[
28
, Corollary 2]
, we could ask
\pi
to run over the set of proper bimeromorphic morphisms from Kähler manifolds.

The components of
\mathbb{T}
with respect to (
3.1
) will be denoted by
\mathbb{T}_{Y}\in\mathrm{H}^{p,p}(Y,\mathbb{R})
. The vector space of b-classes of degree
p
over
X
is denoted by
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(X)
.

The vector space
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(X)
is endowed with the projective limit topology. In other words, the convergence of a net of b-classes
(\mathbb{T}^{i})_{i\in I}
to a b-class
\mathbb{T}
means the convergence of
(\mathbb{T}^{i}_{Y})_{i\in I}
to
\mathbb{T}_{Y}
with respect to the Euclidean topology for each modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
.

Definition 3.2
.

A b-class
\mathbb{T}\in\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(X)
is
nef
if the component
\mathbb{T}_{Y}
is movable in the sense of
Definition

2.20
for each modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
.

A b-class
\mathbb{T}\in\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(X)
is
pseudoeffective
if the component
\mathbb{T}_{Y}
is positive in the sense of
Definition

2.11
for each modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
.

Remark 3.3
.

There is a subtlety here: We can ask
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
to run over either all modifications or all proper bimeromorphic morphisms from Kähler manifolds. The corresponding notions of nef or pseudoeffective b-classes are identical thanks to
Proposition

2.14
and
Proposition

2.23
.

Note that nef b-classes and pseudoeffective b-classes are both closed conditions, namely they are preserved under limits of nets, since the movable cone and the positive cone on a compact Kähler manifold are both closed. A nef b-class is pseudoeffective.

From the projective limit definition (
3.1
), there is a canonical identification between
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(X)
and
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(Y)
for each proper bimeromorphic morphism
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
between Kähler manifolds. We shall implicitly use this identification all the time.

All notions related to b-divisors defined in this paper are invariant under changing
X
to its modifications. We shall explicitly check this whenever the statement is not obvious.

Furthermore, using this identification, we can make sense of
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(X)
for an arbitrary reduced compact Kähler space
X
: Simply take a projective resolution
Y\rightarrow X
, and set
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(Y)
.

Example 3.4
.

When
p=0
, there is a canonical identification
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{0}(X)\cong\mathbb{R}
. An element
t\in\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{0}(X)
is nef or pseudoeffective if and only if
t\geq 0
.
Similarly, when
p=n
, there is also a canonical identification
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{n}(X)\cong\mathbb{R}
. An element
t\in\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{n}(X)
is nef or pseudoeffective if and only if
t\geq 0
.

When
p=1
,
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{1}(X)
is just the set of b-divisors over
X
, as we studied in the previous paper. An element in
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{1}(X)
is nef (resp. pseudoeffective) in the sense of
Definition

3.2
if and only if it is a nef (resp. pseudoeffective) b-divisor in the sense of
[
47
]
. See
[
44
, Corollary 11.1.1]
for the details.

Definition 3.5
.

Given
\mathbb{T},\mathbb{T}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(X)
, we write
\mathbb{T}\geq\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
if for each modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
, we have
\mathbb{T}_{Y}\geq_{Y}\mathbb{T}^{\prime}_{Y}
.

Recall that
\geq_{Y}
is defined right after
Definition

2.11
.

Thanks to
Proposition

2.14
, it suffices to check this condition for a cofinal set of modifications. Note that
\geq
defines a partial order on
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(X)
.

Remark 3.6
.

In
Definition

3.5
we could equivalent ask
\pi
to run over the set of proper bimeromorphic morphisms
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
from Kähler manifolds, as a consequence of
Proposition

2.14
.

Let us recall the following construction from
[
45
,
47
]
:

Definition 3.7
.

Let
T
be a closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
, we define a nef b-divisor
\mathbb{D}(T)
over
X
as follows:

(3.2)

\mathbb{D}(T)_{Y}=\left\{\Reg\pi^{*}T\right\}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(Y,\mathbb{R}),

for all modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
.

Here
\Reg
is defined in (
2.3
).

Remark 3.8
.

From the obvious functoriality of Siu’s decomposition, for a proper bimeromorphic morphism
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
from a Kähler manifold
Y
, the component
\mathbb{D}(T)_{Y}
of
\mathbb{D}(T)
is given by exactly the same formula (
3.2
).

Note that
\mathbb{D}(T)
depends linearly on
T
in the following sense: If
T^{\prime}
is another closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
, and
\lambda\geq 0
, then

(3.3)

\mathbb{D}(T+T^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}(T)+\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime}),\quad\mathbb{D}(\lambda T)=\lambda\mathbb{D}(T).

Recall that a nef b-divisor
\mathbb{D}
is big if its volume

\vol\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\vol\mathbb{D}_{Y}

is positive.

The main result in
[
47
]
says

Theorem 3.9
.

Fix a modified nef and big class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
.
The map
\mathbb{D}
in
Definition

3.7
induces a canonical bijection between

(1)

the set of non-divisorial closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in
\alpha
with positive volumes modulo
\mathcal{I}
-equivalence, and

(2)

the set of nef and big b-divisors
\mathbb{D}
over
X
with
\mathbb{D}_{X}=\alpha
.

The correspondence preserves the volumes.

Moreover, given any nef and big b-divisor
\mathbb{D}
over
X
, we can always find a non-divisorial
\mathcal{I}
-good
closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
with
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}(T)
.

The relevant notions are recalled in
Section

2.1
. In general, we cannot take
T
as a Kähler current, however, we have the following useful observation: If
\mathbb{D}
is a nef b-divisor,
\omega
is a Kähler form on
X
, then
\mathbb{D}+\mathbb{D}(\omega)
can be represented as
\mathbb{D}(T)
where
T
is an
\mathcal{I}
-good non-divisorial Kähler current. It suffices to write
\mathbb{D}+\mathbb{D}(\omega)
as
(\mathbb{D}+2^{-1}\mathbb{D}(\omega))+2^{-1}\mathbb{D}(\omega)
and apply
Theorem

3.9
to the term in the parentheses.

Recall that a nef b-divisor
\mathbb{D}
over
X
is Cartier if it admits a
realization
: A pair
(\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X,\alpha)
consisting of a proper bimeromorphic morphism
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
from a Kähler manifold
Y
and a nef class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(Y,\mathbb{R})
, so that for each modification
\pi^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\rightarrow X
dominating
\pi
, the value
\mathbb{D}_{Y^{\prime}}
is the pull-back of
\alpha
through the morphism
Y^{\prime}\rightarrow Y
. We also say
\mathbb{D}
is realized on
Y
by
\alpha
.

Note that we can always take
\pi
to be a modification.

As a corollary, we have:

Corollary 3.10
.

For any nef b-divisor
\mathbb{D}
over
X
, we can find a decreasing sequence
(\mathbb{D}^{i})_{i}
of nef Cartier b-divisors over
X
with limit
\mathbb{D}
.

See
[
47
, Corollary 4.15]
.

Definition 3.11
.

Given a pseudoeffective class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, we define a nef b-divisor
\mathbb{D}(\alpha)
over
X
as follows:

\mathbb{D}(\alpha)_{Y}=\langle\pi^{*}\alpha\rangle,

where
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
is a modification.

Recall that
\langle\bullet\rangle
refers to the movable intersection product as we recalled in
Section

2.5
. Namely,
\mathbb{D}(\alpha)_{Y}
is the movable part of
\pi^{*}\alpha
with respect to the Boucksom–Nakayama decomposition.

When
\alpha
is big,
\mathbb{D}(\alpha)=\mathbb{D}(T_{\min})
, where
T_{\min}\in\alpha
is a current with minimal singularities.
When
\alpha
is nef, we have

(3.4)

\mathbb{D}(\alpha)_{Y}=\pi^{*}\alpha

for each modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
.

The operator is concave in the following sense:

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be pseudoeffective classes. Then

(3.5)

\mathbb{D}(\alpha)+\mathbb{D}(\beta)\leq\mathbb{D}(\alpha+\beta).

Equality holds when
\alpha,\beta
are both nef.

Proof.

In order to prove (
3.5
), it suffices to prove

\mathbb{D}(\alpha)_{X}+\mathbb{D}(\beta)_{X}\leq\mathbb{D}(\alpha+\beta)_{X}.

In other words,

\sum_{D\subseteq X}\Bigl(\nu(\alpha,D)+\nu(\beta,D)\Bigr)\{D\}\geq\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(\alpha+\beta,D)\{D\},

which follows from the simple observation: For any prime divisor
D
on
X
, we have

\nu(\alpha,D)+\nu(\beta,D)\geq\nu(\alpha+\beta,D).

When
\alpha
and
\beta
are nef, the equality holds in (
3.5
) by our explicit description of the associated b-divisors in (
3.4
).
∎

3.2.
The intersection theory

We begin with the intersection between a Cartier nef b-divisor and a nef b-class.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a nef Cartier b-divisor over
X
and
\mathbb{T}\in\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{q}(X)
be a nef b-class. Then we define the nef class
\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}\in\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{q+1}(X)
as follows: Let
(\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X,\alpha)
be a realization of
\mathbb{D}
, then we define for each modification
\pi^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\rightarrow X
dominating
\pi
through
g\colon Y^{\prime}\rightarrow Y

\left(\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}\right)_{Y^{\prime}}\coloneqq g^{*}\alpha\cap\mathbb{T}_{Y^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{H}^{p+1,p+1}(Y,\mathbb{R}).

Here as before,

on the right-hand side denotes the intersection product of cohomology.
The class defined in (

.

By exactly the same argument, if

Lemma 3.15
.

The product
\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}
in

is independent of the choice of the realization

of

Proof.

Let
(\pi\colon Y^{\prime}\rightarrow X,\alpha^{\prime})
be another realization of
\mathbb{D}

dominates
\pi

and
\alpha^{\prime}=g^{*}\alpha

Consider a modification
\pi^{\prime\prime}\colon Y^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow X

Our assertion now means

\left(h^{*}\alpha^{\prime}\right)\cap\mathbb{T}_{Y^{\prime\prime}}=\left((g\circ h)^{*}\alpha\right)\cap\mathbb{T}_{Y^{\prime\prime}},

which is clear.

and

.

.

Let
\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D}^{\prime}

be nef b-classes.

\mathbb{D}\geq\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
, then

\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}\geq\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{T}.

(2)

Assume that
\mathbb{T}\geq\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
, then

\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}\geq\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}^{\prime}.

Proof.

Thanks to

, we may assume that
\mathbb{D}
and

are both realized on

\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Fix a modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
.

(1) We need to show that if
\alpha\geq\alpha^{\prime}
, then

\pi^{*}\alpha\cap\mathbb{T}_{Y}\geq_{Y}\pi^{*}\alpha^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{T}_{Y}.

This follows from
Proposition

2.24
.

(2) We need to show that if
\mathbb{T}_{Y}\geq_{Y}\mathbb{T}^{\prime}_{Y}
, then

\pi^{*}\alpha\cap\mathbb{T}_{Y}\geq_{Y}\pi^{*}\alpha\cap\mathbb{T}^{\prime}_{Y}.

This follows from

2.15
.

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D}^{\prime}

Theorem 3.26
.

Let
(\mathbb{D}_{i}^{j})_{j\in J}
be decreasing nets of nef b-divisors over
X
with limits
\mathbb{D}_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,p
. Let
(\mathbb{T}^{j})_{j\in J}
be a decreasing net of nef b-classes in
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{q}(X)
with limit
\mathbb{T}
. Then

\lim_{j\in J}\mathbb{D}_{1}^{j}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}^{j}\cap\mathbb{T}^{j}=\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

By induction on
p\geq 1
and using
Proposition

3.25
, it suffices to prove our assertion in the case
p=1
.
In this case, we omit the subindex
i
and we are left with

\lim_{j\in J}\mathbb{D}^{j}\cap\mathbb{T}^{j}=\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}.

We first observe that the limit on the left-hand side of (

) exists, as a consequence of
Proposition

3.25
and
Proposition

2.6
. Furthermore, the
\geq
direction in (
3.12
) holds.

Step 1
. We first assume that
\mathbb{T}^{j}=\mathbb{T}
for all
j\in J
. In this case, take a decreasing sequence
(\mathbb{D}^{\prime k})_{k>0}
of Cartier nef b-divisors with limit
\mathbb{D}
. Fix
k>0
, we need to show that

(3.13)

\lim_{j\in J}\mathbb{D}^{j}\cap\mathbb{T}\leq\mathbb{D}^{\prime k}\cap\mathbb{T}.

For this purpose, we may further assume that
\mathbb{D}^{\prime k}
is realized on
X
by a nef class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Fix a Kähler class
\beta
on
X
, then for each
\epsilon>0
, we can find
j_{0}\in J
so that when
j\geq j_{0}
, we have

\mathbb{D}^{j}_{X}\leq\alpha+\epsilon\beta.

Therefore, thanks to
Lemma

3.21
and
Proposition

3.12
,

\mathbb{D}^{j}\leq\mathbb{D}^{\prime k}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\beta),\quad j\geq j_{0}.

Using
Proposition

3.25
and
Proposition

3.23
, we find

\mathbb{D}^{j}\cap\mathbb{T}\leq\mathbb{D}^{\prime k}\cap\mathbb{T}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\beta)\cap\mathbb{T},\quad j\geq j_{0}.

Taking limit with respect to
j
and then letting

, we derive (
3.13
).

Step 2
. We assume that
\mathbb{D}^{j}=\mathbb{D}
for all
j\in J
. Then we need to show that

(3.14)

\lim_{j\in J}\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}^{j}\leq\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}.

In this case, take a decreasing sequence
(\mathbb{D}^{\prime k})_{k>0}
of nef Cartier b-divisors with limit
\mathbb{D}
. Suppose that we can prove (
3.14
) with
\mathbb{D}^{\prime k}
in place of
\mathbb{D}
, then due to
Proposition

3.25
we have

\lim_{j\in J}\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}^{j}\leq\lim_{j\in J}\mathbb{D}^{\prime k}\cap\mathbb{T}^{j}\\
=\mathbb{D}^{\prime k}\cap\mathbb{T}

for each
k>0
. Letting

, we conclude (
3.14
).

Therefore, we may assume that
\mathbb{D}
is Cartier when proving (
3.14
). Replacing
X
by a modification, we may further assume that
\mathbb{D}
is realized on
X
by a nef class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Then (
3.14
) means the following: Let

be a modification, then

\lim_{j\in J}\pi^{*}\alpha\cap\mathbb{T}^{j}_{Y}\leq_{Y}\pi^{*}\alpha\cap\mathbb{T}_{Y},

which is obvious.

Step 3
. We prove the general case.

For each fixed

3.25

\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{T}^{j_{0}}=\lim_{j\in J}\mathbb{D}^{j}\cap\mathbb{T}^{j_{0}}\geq\lim_{j\in J}\mathbb{D}^{j}\cap\mathbb{T}^{j}.

Taking the limit with respect to
j_{0}
, we conclude (
3.12

Proposition 3.27
.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p},\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
be nef b-divisors over
X
,
\mathbb{T},\mathbb{T}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{q}(X)
be nef b-classes,
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a nef class, and
\lambda\geq 0
. We have the following properties:

(1)

The product is symmetric: For each permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,p\}
, we have

\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{D}_{\sigma(1)}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{\sigma(p)}\cap\mathbb{T}.

(2)

The product is additive in each variable:

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{D}_{1}+\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\prime}\right)\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{D}^{\prime}_{1}\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T},

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\left(\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}^{\prime}\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}^{\prime}.

(3)

The product is homogeneous in each variable:

\displaystyle\left(\lambda\mathbb{D}_{1}\right)\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}=

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\left(\lambda\mathbb{T}\right)=

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}\right).

(4)

The product is increasing in each variable: If we assume
\mathbb{D}_{1}\geq\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\prime}
, we have

\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}\geq\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}.

Similarly, if we assume
\mathbb{T}\geq\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
, then

\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}\geq\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}^{\prime}.

(5)

We have

\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(\alpha)\Bigr)_{X}=\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\Bigr)_{X}\cap\alpha.

Proof.

(1) We can easily reduce to the case
p=2
, and then we need to show

\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{D}_{2}\cap\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\mathbb{T}.

By approximation using
Theorem

3.26
, we can easily reduce to the case where
\mathbb{D}_{1}
and
\mathbb{D}_{2}
are both Cartier.
Thanks to
Corollary

3.16
, we may further assume that
\mathbb{D}_{1}
and
\mathbb{D}_{2}
are realized on
X
by nef classes
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. In this case our assertion becomes the following: For any modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
, we have

\pi^{*}\alpha_{1}\cap\left(\pi^{*}\alpha_{2}\cap\mathbb{T}_{Y}\right)=\pi^{*}\alpha_{2}\cap\left(\pi^{*}\alpha_{1}\cap\mathbb{T}_{Y}\right),

which is obvious.

(2) (3) These are consequences of
Proposition

3.23
.

(4) This is a consequence of
Proposition

3.25
.

(5) This follows immediately from the definition
Definition

3.13
.

.

2.19

2.12
. Since the assumptions of the theorem remain valid after replacing the

\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(T_{1})\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}(T_{p})\Bigr)_{X}=\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\right\}

In order to prove (

and replace

Now assume that the

of

for each

3.29

thanks to
Proposition

3.31
, and hence the limit exists by
Proposition

2.6
.

The striking point is that the left-hand side of (
3.18
) is a linear intersection product, while each term of the right-hand side is non-linear!

Proof.

Step 1
. We first prove the
\leq_{X}
inequality.

For this purpose, it suffices to prove the following:

\left(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)_{X}\leq_{X}\langle\mathbb{D}_{1,X}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbb{D}_{p,X}\rangle.

After adding a small multiple of
\mathbb{D}(\omega)
to each
\mathbb{D}_{i}
, where
\omega
is a Kähler form on
X
, we may assume that the
\mathbb{D}_{i}
’s are big and
\mathbb{D}_{i}=\mathbb{D}(T_{i})
for some
\mathcal{I}
-good non-divisorial Kähler currents
T_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,p
. Then due to
Theorem

3.29
, it suffices to show that

\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\right\}\leq_{X}\left\{T_{1,\min}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p,\min}\right\},

where
T_{i,\min}\in\mathbb{D}_{i,X}
is a current with minimal singularities. Then our assertion follows from the monotonicity theorem
Theorem

2.17
.

Step 2
. We reduce (
3.18
) to the case of
p=n
and the
\mathbb{D}_{i}
’s are big and
\mathbb{D}_{i}=\mathbb{D}(T_{i})
for some non-divisorial
\mathcal{I}
-good Kähler current
T_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,p
.

Thanks to
Lemma

2.16
and Step 1, we may assume
p=n
. If suffices to handle the case where the
\mathbb{D}_{i}
’s are big as well. In fact, take a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
. Suppose that for
\epsilon>0
we have established

\biggl(\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\cap\dots\cap\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{n}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\biggr)_{X}\geq\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\Bigl\langle(\mathbb{D}_{1,Y}+\epsilon\pi^{*}\{\omega\})\wedge\dots\wedge(\mathbb{D}_{n,Y}+\epsilon\pi^{*}\{\omega\})\Bigr\rangle,

then from the monotonicity
Proposition

2.26
(4), we have

\biggl(\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\cap\dots\cap\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{n}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\biggr)_{X}\geq\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\langle\mathbb{D}_{1,Y}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbb{D}_{n,Y}\rangle.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0+
, our assertion (
3.18
) follows.

Step 3
.
We need to show that

(3.19)

\int_{X}T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{n}\geq\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\Bigl\langle\mathbb{D}(T_{1})_{Y}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbb{D}(T_{n})_{Y}\Bigr\rangle.

When the
T_{i}
’s have analytic singularities, we may replace
X
by a modification and reduce to the case where the
T_{i}
’s have log singularities. Then replacing
T_{i}
by
\Reg T_{i}
, we may assume that the
T_{i}
’s have bounded local potentials. Then the
\{T_{i}\}
’s are indeed nef and

\int_{X}T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{n}=\{T_{1}\}\cap\cdots\cap\{T_{n}\}=\Bigl\langle\{T_{1}\}\wedge\dots\wedge\{T_{n}\}\Bigr\rangle.

In general, take quasi-equisingular approximations
(T_{i}^{j})_{j>0}
of each
T_{i}
. Then we know that for each
j>0
,

\int_{X}T_{1}^{j}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{n}^{j}\geq\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\Bigl\langle\mathbb{D}(T_{1}^{j})_{Y}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbb{D}(T_{n}^{j})_{Y}\Bigr\rangle\geq\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\Bigl\langle\mathbb{D}(T_{1})_{Y}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbb{D}(T_{n})_{Y}\Bigr\rangle.

Letting
j\to\infty
and applying
Theorem

2.18
, we conclude (
3.19
).
∎

4.
Restricted volume

Let
X
be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. In this section,
p
will denote a non-negative integer.

4.1.
The trace operator

We first recall the notion of trace operators introduced in
[
18
]
. For the details, see
[
44
, Chapter 8]
.

Assume that
D
is a prime divisor on
X
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T
be a closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
with
\nu(T,D)=0
. We define
\Tr_{D}T
as a closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
\widetilde{D}
, the normalization of
D
, modulo
P
-equivalence. The definition goes as follows:

(1)

When
T
has analytic singularities, we can simply define
\Tr_{D}T
as
T|_{D}
;

(2)

in general, take a quasi-equisingular approximation
(T_{j})_{j}
of
T
and define
\Tr_{D}T
as an arbitrary
d_{S}
-limit of
(T_{j}|_{\tilde{D}})_{j}
.

When
T
is a Kähler current on
X
with
\nu(T,D)=0
, we can take a representative
\Tr_{D}T
as a Kähler current in
\{T\}|_{\widetilde{D}}
.
When
\widetilde{D}
is not smooth, this means that we can find a smooth closed real
(1,1)
-form
\theta\in\{T\}
so that
\Tr_{D}T
can be represented by
\theta|_{\widetilde{D}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi
for some
\theta|_{\widetilde{D}}
-psh function
\varphi
, and the current
\Tr_{D}T
dominates a Kähler form on
\tilde{D}
.
We always choose such a representative.

In this case, we define the
restricted volume
of
T
on
X
as

\vol_{X|D}(T)\coloneqq\vol\left(\Tr_{D}T\right).

Note that
\vol_{X|D}(T)
is independent of the choice of
\Tr_{D}T
, since any two choices are
P
-equivalent.

More generally, if
T
is a closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
with
\nu(T,D)=0
, we take a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
, then we can define

(4.1)

\vol_{X|D}(T)\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\vol_{X|D}(T+\epsilon\omega)

This definition agrees with the previous definition when
T
is a Kähler current. When
\vol_{X|D}(T)>0
, we can always find a representative of
\Tr_{D}T
in
\{T\}|_{\widetilde{D}}
, and the restricted volume
\vol_{X|D}(T)
is just the volume of such a representative. See
[
44
, Example 8.1.6]
for the details.

When
\nu(T,D)>0
, we define

\vol_{X|D}(T)\coloneqq 0.

Next we recall that there is a notion of trace operators of nef b-divisors defined in
[
47
]
. Given a nef b-divisor
\mathbb{D}
over
X
, a prime divisor
D
on
X
, then there is a canonical way to define a nef b-divisor
\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}
over
D
. Instead of recalling the lengthy definition, we use the following assertion proved in
[
47
]
as the definition:

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a nef b-divisor over
X
. We define
\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}
as a nef b-divisor over
D
as follows:

(1)

When
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}(T)
for a closed positive Kähler current
T
on
X
, then we let

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\mathbb{D}(\Tr_{D}T).

(2)

In general, we define

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\Tr_{D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)

for any Kähler form
\omega
on
X
.

Note that in (2),
\mathbb{D}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)
for any
\epsilon>0
satisfies the condition in (1), as a consequence of
Theorem

3.9
.

A priori,
\Tr_{D}T
is a current on
\widetilde{D}
, so
\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}
is just a nef b-divisor over
\widetilde{D}
. But by definition, nef b-divisors over
D
are the same as those over
\widetilde{D}
, so we can also regard
\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}
as a nef b-divisor over
D
.

Whenever
\Tr_{D}T
has a representative in
\{T\}|_{\tilde{D}}
(in particular when
\vol_{X|D}(T)>0
), we always have

(4.2)

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}(\Tr_{D}T),

where
\Tr_{D}T
is understood as a representative in
\{T\}|_{\tilde{D}}
. This is proved in
[
47
, Theorem 7.5]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a modification with
D^{\prime}
as the strict transform of
D
, then for any nef b-divisor
\mathbb{D}
over
X
, we have

(4.3)

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}=\Tr_{D^{\prime}}\mathbb{D}.

Here on the right-hand side, we regard
\mathbb{D}
as a nef b-divisor over
Y
and
\Tr_{D^{\prime}}\mathbb{D}
is a nef b-divisor over
D^{\prime}
, which can be canonically identified with a nef b-divisor over
D
.

Proof.

Take a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
. It suffice to prove (
4.3
) with
\mathbb{D}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)
in place of
\mathbb{D}
for any
\epsilon>0
. We may then assume that there is a non-divisorial
\mathcal{I}
-good Kähler current
T
on
X
so that
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}(T)
. Then
\Tr_{D}T
can be represented by a current in
\{T\}|_{\widetilde{D}}
. We fix such a representative.

Consider the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{D^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Observe that
\nu(\pi^{*}T,D^{\prime})=0
by Zariski’s main theorem.
From the basic properties of the trace operator
[
44
, Lemma 8.2.1]
, we have

\tilde{p}^{*}\Tr_{D}T\sim_{P}\Tr_{D^{\prime}}\pi^{*}T.

In particular,
\Tr_{D^{\prime}}\pi^{*}T
can be represented by a current in
\pi^{*}\{T\}|_{\widetilde{D^{\prime}}}
, we fix such a representative.

Taking the induced b-divisors, we get

\mathbb{D}\left(\tilde{p}^{*}\Tr_{D}T\right)=\mathbb{D}\left(\Tr_{D^{\prime}}\pi^{*}T\right).

This is exactly (
4.3
).
∎

We need a few basic properties of the trace operator.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
,
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
be nef b-divisors over
X
, and
\lambda\geq 0
. Then we have the following properties:

(1)

When
\mathbb{D}\leq\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}_{X}=\mathbb{D}_{X}
, we have

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}\leq\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}^{\prime}.

(2)

The trace operator is additive:

\Tr_{D}\left(\mathbb{D}+\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\right)=\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}+\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}^{\prime}.

(3)

The trace operator is homogeneous:

\Tr_{D}\left(\lambda\mathbb{D}\right)=\lambda\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

These properties all follow from the corresponding properties of the trace operator of currents. See
[
44
, Proposition 8.2.1]
.

We only give a detailed proof to (1), and the other two assertions are similar. Take a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
. It suffices to show that for each
\epsilon>0
, we have

\Tr_{D}(\mathbb{D}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega))\leq\Tr_{D}(\mathbb{D}^{\prime}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)).

Therefore, thanks to
Theorem

3.9
, we may assume that

\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}(T),\quad\mathbb{D}^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})

for
\mathcal{I}
-good non-divisorial Kähler currents
T
and
T^{\prime}
on
X
. Observe that
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
as a consequence of
[
47
, Corollary 4.1.4]
. But then, thanks to
[
44
, Proposition 8.2.1]
, we have

\Tr_{D}T\preceq_{P}\Tr_{D}T^{\prime}.

Then by
[
47
, Corollary 4.1.4]
again, our assertion follows.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a big class. Assume that
(T_{i})_{i\in I}
is a net of closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in
\alpha
, decreasing with respect to the
\mathcal{I}
-partial order. Assume that
T_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{S}}T
for some closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T\in\alpha
satisfying
\nu(T,\alpha)=0
.
Then

\Tr_{D}T_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{S}}\Tr_{D}T.

Furthermore,

(4.4)

\lim_{i\in I}\vol_{X|D}(T_{i})=\vol_{X|D}(T).

Proof.

The former statement is proved in
[
44
, Proposition 8.2.2]
. If
\vol_{X|D}(T)>0
, then
\Tr_{D}T_{i}
and
\Tr_{D}T
can both be represented by currents in
\alpha|_{\widetilde{D}}
with positive masses. In this case, (
4.4
) follows from the
d_{S}
-continuity of the volume. See
[
44
, Theorem 6.2.5]
.

If
\vol_{X|D}(T)=0
, fix a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
. Fix
\epsilon>0
, we can find
\delta>0
so that

\vol_{X|D}(T+\delta\omega)\leq\epsilon.

In this case, applying (
4.4
) to the net
(T_{i}+\delta\omega)_{i}
, in view of
[
44
, Corollary 6.2.8]
we find that

\varlimsup_{i\in I}\vol_{X|D}(T_{i})\leq\lim_{i\in I}\vol_{X|D}(T_{i}+\delta\omega)=\vol_{X|D}(T+\delta\omega)\leq\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\lim_{i\in I}\vol_{X|D}(T_{i})=0.

∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(\mathbb{D}^{i})_{i\in I}
be a decreasing net of nef b-divisors over
X
with limit
\mathbb{D}
. Assume that
\mathbb{D}^{i}_{X}
is independent of the choice of
i\in I
. Then

(4.5)

\lim_{i\in I}\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}^{i}=\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}.

Note that thanks to
Proposition

4.4
(1),
(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}^{i})_{i}
is decreasing, and hence the limit in (
4.5
) exists.

Proof.

Fix a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
. Thanks to the additivity of the trace operator proved in
Proposition

4.4
,
it suffices to prove (
4.5
) with
\mathbb{D}^{i}+\mathbb{D}(\omega)
and
\mathbb{D}+\mathbb{D}(\omega)
in place of
\mathbb{D}^{i}
and
\mathbb{D}
. So by
Theorem

3.9
, we may assume that there are non-divisorial
\mathcal{I}
-good Kähler currents
T_{i},T\in\mathbb{D}_{X}
with

\mathbb{D}_{i}=\mathbb{D}(T_{i}),\quad\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}(T)

for all
i\in I
.

It follows from
[
47
, Corollary 4.1.4]
and the
\mathcal{I}
-goodness of the
T_{i}
’s and
T
that
(T_{i})_{i}
is decreasing with respect to the
P
-partial order and
T\preceq_{P}T_{i}
.

Since
T
has positive mass, it follows from
[
44
, Corollary 6.2.6]
that
(T_{i})_{i}
admits a
d_{S}
-limit
S
in
\{T\}
, then
\mathbb{D}(S)=\mathbb{D}(T)
. It follows from
[
47
, Corollary 4.1.4]
that
S\sim_{P}T
. Hence
T_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{S}}T
. Our assertion then follows from
Theorem

4.5
.
∎

Let us make sense of the trace operator in some simple cases.

Example 4.7
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a nef class. Then

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha)=\mathbb{D}\left(\alpha|_{D}\right).

To see this, we may assume that
\alpha
is a Kähler class. Take a Kähler form
\omega\in\alpha
, then by definition,

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha)=\mathbb{D}(\omega|_{D})=\mathbb{D}\left(\alpha|_{D}\right).

Example 4.8
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a big class. Assume that
D
is smooth and not contained in the non-Kähler locus of
\alpha
.
Then

(4.6)

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha)=\mathbb{D}\left(\Tr_{D}T_{\min}\right)=\mathbb{D}\left(T_{\min}|_{D}\right),

where
T_{\min}\in\alpha
is a current with minimal singularities.

It follows from
[
44
, Proposition 8.3.1]
that
\Tr_{D}T_{\min}
is represented by
T_{\min}|_{D}
. Therefore, we can apply (
4.2
) to conclude (
4.6
).

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be modified nef classes.
Then

(4.7)

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha)+\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\beta)\leq\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha+\beta).

Proof.

Since
\alpha+\beta
is also modified nef, we have

\mathbb{D}(\alpha)_{X}+\mathbb{D}(\beta)_{X}=\alpha+\beta=\mathbb{D}(\alpha+\beta)_{X}.

Thanks to
Proposition

4.4
, it suffices to show that

\mathbb{D}(\alpha)+\mathbb{D}(\beta)\leq\mathbb{D}(\alpha+\beta),

which is just
Proposition

3.12
.
∎

Next recall the notion of restricted volumes of a cohomology class studied in
[
13
,
32
]
.
Fix a pseudoeffective class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. If
\alpha
is big and
D
is not contained in the non-Kähler locus of
X
, then we set

\displaystyle\vol_{X|D}(\alpha)\coloneqq\sup

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{D}T|_{D}^{n-1}:T\in\alpha\textup{ is a Kähler current with analytic singularities, }\right.

\displaystyle\left.\nu(T,D)=0\right\}.

Here
\int_{D}T|_{D}^{n-1}
could be understood as the non-pluripolar product
\int_{\tilde{D}}T|_{\tilde{D}}^{n-1}
on the normalization
\tilde{D}
of
D
.

In general, if
\alpha
is pseudoeffective and
D
is not contained in the non-nef locus of
\alpha
, we take a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
and set

\vol_{X|D}(\alpha)\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\vol_{X|D}(\alpha+\epsilon\{\omega\}).

If
D
is contained in the non-nef locus of
\alpha
, we set

\vol_{X|D}(\alpha)\coloneqq 0.

The restricted volume can be expressed as an intersection number of b-divisors.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
D
be a prime divisor of
X
. Given a big class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
with
\nu(\alpha,D)=0
, then

(4.8)

\vol_{X|D}(\alpha)=\vol\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha).

From (
4.8
), we deduce that
\vol_{X|D}(\alpha)=\vol_{X|D}(\langle\alpha\rangle)
. This is also proved in
[
13
, Lemma 6.2]
.

Restricted volumes to higher codimensional subspaces admit similar expressions. Since we are not in need of them in this paper, we omit the details.

Proof.

Step 1
. We first assume that
D
is not contained in the non-Kähler locus of
\alpha
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a modification so that the strict transform
D^{\prime}
of
D
is smooth. Then by
[
13
, Lemma 4.2, Lemma 4.3]
,

\vol_{X|D}(\alpha)=\vol_{Y|D^{\prime}}(\pi^{*}\alpha)

and
D^{\prime}
is not in the non-Kähler locus of
\pi^{*}\alpha
. On the other hand,

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha)=\Tr_{D^{\prime}}\mathbb{D}(\pi^{*}\alpha)

as a consequence of
Proposition

4.3
. So

\Bigl(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha)\Bigr)^{n-1}=\Bigl(\Tr_{D^{\prime}}\mathbb{D}(\pi^{*}\alpha)\Bigr)^{n-1}.

Therefore, we may further assume that
D
is smooth.
In this case our assertion follows from
[
44
, Proposition 8.3.1]
.

Step 2
. Next we assume that
D
is contained in the non-Kähler locus of
\alpha
.

Fix a Kähler form
\omega
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha+\epsilon\{\omega\})\geq\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha)

by
Proposition

4.9
.
Therefore, our Step 1 and
Proposition

3.27
(4) imply that

\vol_{X|D}(\alpha)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\vol_{X|D}(\alpha+\epsilon\{\omega\})=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\Bigl(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha+\epsilon\{\omega\})\Bigr)^{n-1}\geq\Bigl(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}(\alpha)\Bigr)^{n-1}.

Due to
[
41
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\vol_{X|D}(\alpha)=0
, so our assertion follows.
∎

4.2.
The restricted volume

Let
D
be a prime divisor over
X
. Motivated by
Theorem

4.10
, we introduce the following definition:

Definition 4.11
.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
be nef b-divisors over
X
.
Let
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a modification so that
D
is a prime divisor on
Y
.
Then we define the
restricted volume
of
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
to
D
as

\vol_{X|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)\coloneqq\pi_{*}\left(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)_{Y}\in\mathrm{H}^{p+1,p+1}(X,\mathbb{R}).

The notation

(4.9)

\left(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)_{Y}

requires some explanation. The traces
\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{i}
’s are nef b-divisors over
D
. For each resolution
W\rightarrow D
, we can then regard
\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{i}
as a nef b-divisor over
W
. The intersection
\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{p}
then makes sense as a b-class in
\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{b}}^{p}(W)
. The pushforward of

\left(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)_{W}

with respect to
W\rightarrow D\hookrightarrow Y
is then a well-defined positive class in
\mathrm{H}^{p+1,p+1}(Y,\mathbb{R})
, and it is independent of the choice of
W
. We denote this class by (
4.9
).

When
p=0
,
\vol_{X|D}(-)
is
\{D\}
if
D
is a prime divisor on
X
and
0
if
D
is exceptional over
X
.

Lemma 4.12
.

The quantity
\vol_{X|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)
defined in
Definition

4.11
is independent of the choice of
Y
.

Proof.

Take a different modification
\Pi\colon Z\rightarrow X
so that
D
is a prime divisor on
Z
. We want to show that the quantity
\vol_{X|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)
defined with respect to
\pi
and
\Pi
are the same. For this purpose, we may assume that
\Pi
dominates
\pi
through a morphism
p\colon Z\rightarrow Y
.
Let
D^{\prime}
be the strict transform of
D
on
Z
.
Then we need to show that

The restricted volume is easy to understood in some special cases.

5.
Qualitative monotonicity theorem

Let
X
be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. Fix a non-negative integer
p
.

We shall derive a qualitative monotonicity theorem using the theory of b-divisors.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents with analytic singularities on
X
. Suppose that
T,T^{\prime}
are
\mathcal{I}
-good closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
with positive volumes in the same cohomology class. Assume that
T\preceq T^{\prime}
.
Then

(5.1)

\begin{split}&\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\wedge T^{\prime}\right\}-\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\wedge T\right\}\\
\geq_{X}&\sum_{D\subseteq X}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\left\{\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}\left.\Bigl(T_{i}-\nu(T_{i},D)[D]\Bigr)\right|_{\widetilde{D}}\right\}.\end{split}

Here
D
runs over the set of prime divisors on
X
, and
\widetilde{D}\rightarrow D
is the normalization of
D
.

Note that the sum is a countable sum. We have omitted the obvious pushforward maps from
\widetilde{D}
to
X
.

Proof.

Step 1
. We first assume that
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}
,
T,T^{\prime}
all have log singularities. Then we may replace the
T_{i}
’s by their non-divisorial parts, and hence reduce to the case where
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}
all have bounded local potentials. A further regularization then allows us to reduce to the case where
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}
are all Kähler forms, say
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{p}
. Then our assertion (
5.1
) means

\begin{split}&\left\{\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{p}\wedge\Reg T^{\prime}\right\}-\left\{\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{p}\wedge\Reg T\right\}\\
\geq_{X}&\sum_{D\subseteq X}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\left\{\omega_{1}|_{D}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{p}|_{D}\right\}.\end{split}

This is obvious. We even have equality in this case.

Step 2
. We assume that
T
and
T^{\prime}
both have analytic singularities. Replacing
T_{i}
by
\Reg T_{i}
, we may assume that each
T_{i}
is non-divisorial.

Let
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a modification which resolves the singularities of
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}
and
T,T^{\prime}
.
Then by Step 1 and

2.12
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\pi^{*}T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi^{*}T_{p}\wedge\pi^{*}T^{\prime}\right\}-\left\{\pi^{*}T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi^{*}T_{p}\wedge\pi^{*}T\right\}

\displaystyle\geq_{Y}

\displaystyle\sum_{E\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,E)-\nu(T^{\prime},E)\Bigr)\left\{\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}\left.\Bigl(\pi^{*}T_{i}-\nu(T_{i},E)[E]\Bigr)\right|_{\tilde{E}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{D\subseteq X}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\tilde{j}_{*}\left\{\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}\tilde{p}^{*}\left(T_{i}|_{\widetilde{D}}\right)\right\}.

Here

denotes the strict transform of

5.1
) follows.

Step 3
. We handle the general case.

Replacing
T
and
T^{\prime}
by

T-\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T^{\prime},D)[D],\quad T^{\prime}-\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T^{\prime},D)[D],

we may first assume that
T^{\prime}
is non-divisorial. We need to show that

(5.2)

\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\wedge T^{\prime}\right\}-\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\wedge T\right\}\geq_{X}\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T,D)\left\{\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}\left.\Bigl(T_{i}-\nu(T_{i},D)[D]\Bigr)\right|_{\widetilde{D}}\right\}.

Take a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
.
Replacing
T
and
T^{\prime}
by
T+\omega
and
T^{\prime}+\omega
, we may assume that both are Kähler currents. Take quasi-equisingular approximations
(S_{k})_{k},(S^{\prime}_{k})_{k}
of
T
and
T^{\prime}
so that
S_{k}\preceq S^{\prime}_{k}
.
It follows from Step 2 that for each
k>0
,

(5.3)

\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\wedge S^{\prime}_{k}\right\}-\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\wedge S_{k}\right\}\geq_{X}\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(S_{k},D)\left\{\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}\left.\Bigl(T_{i}-\nu(T_{i},D)[D]\Bigr)\right|_{\widetilde{D}}\right\}.

Note that in all expressions like
\sum_{D\subseteq X}
above, we only need to consider the countable set of prime divisors
D
with
\nu(T,D)>0
.

Next, for each
k>0
, we have

\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(S_{k},D)\left\{\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}\left.\Bigl(T_{i}-\nu(T_{i},D)[D]\Bigr)\right|_{\widetilde{D}}\right\}\leq_{X}\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\wedge S^{\prime}_{1}\right\}

by (
5.3
) and the monotonicity theorem
Theorem

2.17
. Therefore, the monotone convergence theorem

Proposition

2.9
is applicable. Letting

in (
5.3
) and applying
Theorem

2.18
, we conclude the desired inequality (
5.2
).
∎

Now using the language of b-divisors, we derive our main theorem:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
be nef b-divisors over
X
, and
T,T^{\prime}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
in the same cohomology class such that
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
. Then

(5.4)

\begin{split}&\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\Bigr)_{X}-\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\Bigr)_{X}\\
\geq_{X}&\sum_{D/X}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{X|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{D}_{p}\right).\end{split}

Furthermore, equality holds in (
5.4
) when
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
are Cartier.

The right-hand side of (
5.4
) should be interpreted as the following limit:

\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{X|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{D}_{p}\right).

In terms of b-divisors, one can also formulate (
5.4
) as

\begin{split}&\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\Bigr)-\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\Bigr)\\
\geq&\sum_{D/X}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{D}_{p}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

From the bimeromorphic invariance of our assumptions, it suffices to prove the following:

\begin{split}&\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\Bigr)_{X}-\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\Bigr)_{X}\\
\geq_{X}&\sum_{D\subseteq X}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\left(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)_{X}.\end{split}

Fix a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
and replace
T
and
T^{\prime}
by
T+\omega
and
T^{\prime}+\omega
, we may assume that both are Kähler currents.

Take a closed smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\theta\in\{T\}
and write

T=\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi,\quad T^{\prime}=\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi^{\prime}.

We may then replace
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
by
P_{\theta}[\varphi]_{\mathcal{I}}
and
P_{\theta}[\varphi^{\prime}]_{\mathcal{I}}
respectively and assume that both
T
and
T^{\prime}
are
\mathcal{I}
-good and
T\preceq T^{\prime}
.

Replacing
T
and
T^{\prime}
by

T-\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T^{\prime},D)[D],\quad T^{\prime}-\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T^{\prime},D)[D],

we may further assume that
T^{\prime}
is non-divisorial and hence it remains to prove

(5.5)

\begin{split}&\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\Bigr)_{X}-\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}_{p}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\Bigr)_{X}\\
\geq_{X}&\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T,D)\left(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)_{X}.\end{split}

We first prove (
5.5
) when
\mathbb{D}_{i}=\mathbb{D}(T_{i})
for some non-divisorial
\mathcal{I}
-good Kähler current
T_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,p
. In this case, thanks to
Theorem

3.29
, (
5.5
) reduces to

\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\wedge T^{\prime}\right\}-\left\{T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T_{p}\wedge T\right\}\geq_{X}\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T,D)\left\{\Tr_{D}T_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Tr_{D}T_{p}\right\}.

Here as usual, we omitted the obvious pushforward from
\widetilde{D}
to
X
.

Taking quasi-equisingular approximations
(T_{i}^{j})_{j>0}
of
T_{i}
for each

, we can then apply
Lemma

5.1
to conclude that for each
j>0
,

\left\{T^{j}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T^{j}_{p}\wedge T^{\prime}\right\}-\left\{T^{j}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge T^{j}_{p}\wedge T\right\}\geq_{X}\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T,D)\left\{\Tr_{D}T^{j}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Tr_{D}T^{j}_{p}\right\}.

Letting
j\to\infty
, we conclude using
Theorem

3.26
and
Proposition

2.8
.

Now let us come back to the general situation. For each
\epsilon>0
, we then have

\displaystyle\biggl(\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\cap\dots\cap\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{p}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\biggr)_{X}

\displaystyle-\biggl(\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\cap\dots\cap\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{p}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\biggr)_{X}

\displaystyle\geq_{X}

\displaystyle\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T,D)\biggl(\Tr_{D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{1}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\cap\dots\cap\Tr_{D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{p}+\epsilon\mathbb{D}(\omega)\Bigr)\biggr)_{X}

\displaystyle\geq_{X}

\displaystyle\sum_{D\subseteq X}\nu(T,D)\left(\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\Tr_{D}\mathbb{D}_{p}\right)_{X}.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0+
we conclude (
5.5
).

Finally, it remains to prove the final assertion. Assume that
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
are all Cartier. Replacing
X
by a modification, we may assume that
\mathbb{D}_{i}=\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{i})
for some nef class
\alpha_{i}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
for each

. Then thanks to
Proposition

3.27
(5) and

, the desired equality becomes

\alpha_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\alpha_{p}\cap\{\Reg T^{\prime}\}-\alpha_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\alpha_{p}\cap\{\Reg T\}=\sum_{D\subseteq X}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\alpha_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\alpha_{p}\cap\{D\},

which is obvious.
∎

Although the final assertion in
Theorem

5.2
is easy to prove after establishing the general theory of b-divisors, it is far from trivial when stated in terms of currents:

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents with analytic singularities on
X
, and
T,T^{\prime}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in the same cohomology class such that
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
, then

(5.6)

\displaystyle\vol\left(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1},T^{\prime}\right)-\vol\left(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1},T\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\int_{\tilde{D}}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n-1}\left.\Bigl(\pi^{*}T_{i}-\nu(T_{i},D)[D]\Bigr)\right|_{\tilde{D}}.

Furthermore, the net on the right-hand side is eventually constant.

We get some interesting new inequalities even for the movable intersection theory:

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
[E]
be a divisorial closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
, say

(5.7)

[E]=\sum_{i}c_{i}E_{i},

where the
E_{i}
’s are distinct prime divisors on
X
and
c_{i}>0
. Consider pseudoeffective classes
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p},\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Assume that
\beta\geq\{E\}
, then

(5.8)

\begin{split}&\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge\beta\rangle-\Bigl\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge(\beta-\{E\})\Bigr\rangle\\
\geq_{X}&\sum_{i}\Bigl(\nu\left(\beta-\{E\},E_{i}\right)+c_{i}-\nu(\beta,E_{i})\Bigr)\vol_{X|E_{i}}\Bigl(\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle\alpha_{p}\rangle\Bigr).\end{split}

Here we used the mixed restricted volume defined in
Definition

4.19
. This inequality is stated purely using the traditional language of movable intersection product and restricted volume, at least when
\alpha_{1}=\dots=\alpha_{p}
. But it is far from being obvious without the knowledge of b-divisors.

Proof.

We first make a simple observation: The coefficient in (
5.8
) is non-negative:

\nu\left(\beta-\{E\},E_{i}\right)+c_{i}-\nu(\beta,E_{i})\geq 0,

as a simple consequence of the convexity of
\nu(\bullet,E_{i})
proved in
[
5
, Proposition 2.1.8]
. Since both sides of (
5.8
) remain invariant if we replace
\alpha_{j}
by
\langle\alpha_{j}\rangle
, we may assume that each
\alpha_{j}
is modified nef.

We may assume that the index set in (
5.7
) is a set of the form
\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
, where
N
is possibly
\infty
.

Step 1
.
We first reduce to the case where
N
is finite. Assume that this case is known, let us handle the case where
N=\infty
. For each positive integer
M
, we introduce

E^{M}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}c_{i}E_{i}.

Then using
Proposition

2.26
(4), we have

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge\beta\rangle-\Bigl\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge\left(\beta-\{E\}\right)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\geq_{X}

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge\beta\rangle-\Bigl\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge\left(\beta-\left\{E^{M}\right\}\right)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\geq_{X}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Bigl(\nu\left(\beta-\left\{E^{M}\right\},E_{i}\right)+c_{i}-\nu(\beta,E_{i})\Bigr)\vol_{X|E_{i}}\Bigl(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}\Bigr).

Observe that
\nu\left(\beta-\left\{E^{M}\right\},E_{i}\right)
is an increasing function of
M
as long as
M\geq i
, therefore, we can apply the monotone convergence theorem
Proposition

2.9
to conclude that

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge\beta\rangle-\Bigl\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge\left(\beta-\{E\}\right)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\geq_{X}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\lim_{M\to\infty}\nu\left(\beta-\left\{E^{M}\right\},E_{i}\right)+c_{i}-\nu(\beta,E_{i})\Bigr)\vol_{X|E_{i}}\Bigl(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}\Bigr).

But the function
\nu(\bullet,E_{i})
is lower semi-continuous as proved in
[
5
, Proposition 2.1.8]
, (
5.8
) then follows.

Step 2
. We assume that
N
is finite and prove (
5.8
). Fix a Kähler class
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})

We can replace
\alpha_{j}
by
\alpha_{j}+\epsilon\gamma
for all
\epsilon>0
. In this way, we reduce to the case where the
\alpha_{i}
’s are all big and none of the
E_{i}
’s lies in the non-Kähler loci of the
\alpha_{i}
’s, and hence
Proposition

4.21
is applicable.

Observe that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\nu\left(\beta+\epsilon\gamma-\{E\},E_{i}\right)=\nu\left(\beta-\{E\},E_{i}\right),\quad\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\nu\left(\beta+\epsilon\gamma,E_{i}\right)=\nu\left(\beta,E_{i}\right),\quad\forall i.

Therefore, in view of
Proposition

2.26
(6), we may therefore replace
\beta
by
\beta+\epsilon\gamma
for some
\epsilon>0
and assume that
\beta
and
\beta-\{E\}
are both big.

We apply
Theorem

5.2
to the following situation:
\mathbb{D}_{i}=\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{i})
, and
T^{\prime}
is a current with minimal singularities in
\beta
and
T-[E]
is a current with minimal singularities in
\beta-\{E\}
. Then we find

\displaystyle\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{1})\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{p})\cap\mathbb{D}(\beta)\Bigr)_{X}-\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{1})\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{p})\cap\mathbb{D}\left(\beta-\{E\}\right)\Bigr)_{X}

\displaystyle\geq_{X}

\displaystyle\sum_{D\subseteq X}\Bigl(\nu(\beta-\{E\},D)-\nu(\beta,D)+\nu([E],D)\Bigr)\vol_{X|D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{1}),\dots,\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{p})\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq_{X}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\Bigl(\nu(\beta-\{E\},E_{i})-\nu(\beta,E_{i})+c_{i}\Bigr)\vol_{X|E_{i}}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{1}),\dots,\mathbb{D}(\alpha_{p})\Bigr).

Thanks to
Corollary

3.30
and
Proposition

4.21
(3), this inequality translates immediately to (
5.8
).
∎

The following special case is already very non-trivial:

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
[E]
be a divisorial closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
, say

[E]=\sum_{i}c_{i}E_{i},

where the
E_{i}
’s are distinct prime divisors on
X
and
c_{i}>0
. Consider modified nef classes
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p},\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Assume that
\beta\geq\{E\}
, then

\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge\beta\rangle-\Bigl\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{p}\wedge(\beta-\{E\})\Bigr\rangle\geq_{X}\sum_{i}\Bigl(\nu(\beta-\{E\},E_{i})+c_{i}\Bigr)\vol_{X|E_{i}}\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}\right).

A weaker result with
c_{i}
in place of
\nu(\beta-\{E\},E_{i})+c_{i}
can also be proved via Vu’s theory of relative non-pluripolar products. The weaker inequality has already played a significant role in the works of Su and Vu.

Although (
5.4
) is an equality when the
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{p}
are all Cartier, in general, it fails to be an equality as can be easily seen in the toric setup. So we shall formulate a variant of (
5.4
).

In order to proceed further, we shall need the celebrated conjectural transcendental Morse inequality
[
6
]
:

(5.9)

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\vol(\alpha+t\beta)=n\langle\alpha^{n-1}\rangle\cap\beta

for all classes
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
with
\alpha
big. This conjecture is known when
X
is projective, as proved by Witt Nyström in
[
42
]
. When
n=1,2
, it is also known, see
[
23
]
.

We shall need the following consequence:

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume that the transcendental Morse inequality holds on
X
. Then for any prime divisor
D
on
X
and any big class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, we have

(5.10)

\vol_{X|D}(\alpha)=\langle\alpha^{n-1}\rangle\cap\{D\}.

We note that the
\leq
direction in (
5.10
) is trivial.

Remark 5.7
.

It is not clear to the author whether the mixed version holds as well:

\vol_{X|D}\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}\right)=\left\langle\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{n-1}\right\rangle\cap\{D\}.

A seemingly weaker (but in fact equivalent) statement is that
\vol_{X|D}\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}\right)
depends only on the linear equivalence class of
D
when
D
is ample.

Proof.

This is a consequence of (
5.9
) and the main theorems of
[
33
,
41
]
.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a big and nef b-divisors over
X
,
T,T^{\prime}
be closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
in the same cohomology class such that
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
. Then

(5.11)

\Bigl(\mathbb{D}^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\Bigr)-\Bigl(\mathbb{D}^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\Bigr)\geq\varlimsup_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{Y}\right).

If furthermore either
X
is projective or
n=1,2
, then the limit exists and equality holds in (
5.11
).

The projectivity assumption (or
n=1,2
assumption) is only to guarantee that we can apply
Theorem

5.6
. If the transcendental Morse inequality is known (or if the much weaker assertion (
5.10
) is known unconditionally), the same proof works without this assumption as well.

Proof.

Step 1
. We make some preliminary reductions.

After adding a Kähler form to
T
and
T^{\prime}
, neither side of (
5.11
) changes, so we may assume that
T
and
T^{\prime}
are both Kähler currents. Take a smooth closed real
(1,1)
-form
\theta\in\{T\}
, then we can represent
T=\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi
and
T^{\prime}=\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi^{\prime}
. After replacing
T
and
T^{\prime}
by
\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta}[\varphi]_{\mathcal{I}}
and
\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta}[\varphi^{\prime}]_{\mathcal{I}}
, we may assume that they are both
\mathcal{I}
-good. Finally, after adding a Kähler form to them again, we reduce to the case where
T
and
T^{\prime}
are both
\mathcal{I}
-good Kähler currents.

Next observe that the
\geq_{X}
direction in (
5.11
) follows from
Theorem

5.2
. In fact, for each fixed modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl(\mathbb{D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{Y}\right)^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\Bigr)_{X}-\Bigl(\mathbb{D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{Y}\right)^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\Bigr)_{X}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{Y|D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{Y}\right)^{n-1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{Y|D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}_{Y}\Bigr).

The proof of
[
47
, Theorem 4.10]
shows that
(\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{D}_{Y}))_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}
is a decreasing net with limit
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, taking limit with respect to
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
and applying
Theorem

3.26
, we conclude the
\geq
direction in (
5.11
).
Our assertion becomes

(5.12)

\left(\mathbb{D}^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\right)_{X}-\left(\mathbb{D}^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\right)_{X}\leq\varliminf_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}_{Y}^{n-1}\right).

Step 2
. Represent
\mathbb{D}
as
\mathbb{D}(S)
for some non-divisorial
\mathcal{I}
-good closed positive
(1,1)
-current
S
on
X
. This is possible thanks to
Theorem

3.9
.
In view of
Theorem

3.29
, it remains to show that

(5.13)

\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge T^{\prime}-\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge T\leq\varliminf_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right).

We make a further reduction, we may add freely a Kähler form to
T
and
T^{\prime}
, so that we may assume that
\{T\}
is a Kähler class, say represented by a Kähler form
\omega
. Due to

5.2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge\omega-\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge\omega-\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge T^{\prime}+\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge T^{\prime}-\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge T

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right)+\varlimsup_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\nu(T^{\prime},D)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\nu(T,D)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\varliminf_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\nu(T,D)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right).

In order to establish (

, and it remains only to prove that

\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge\omega-\int_{X}S^{n-1}\wedge T\leq\varliminf_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\nu(T,D)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right).

of

) with

in place of

Letting

and applying

Take a modification

After all these reductions, we are finally reduced to the following assertion: Suppose that

is a prime divisor on

, then

(5.15)

\{S^{n-1}\}\cap\{E\}\leq\varliminf_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\nu([E],D)\vol_{Y|D}\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right).

Here
S
is an
\mathcal{I}
-good current with positive volume. Note that the reverse inequality is known, namely follows from
Theorem

5.2
.

Step 4
. We finally prove (
5.15
) using the transcendental Morse inequality. Only in this step are we in need of the assumption that
X
is projective or
n=1,2
.

If
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
is a modification, then
Theorem

5.6
shows that

(5.16)

\pi_{*}\Bigl\langle\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}^{n-1}\Bigr\rangle\cap\{E\}=\Bigl\langle\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}^{n-1}\Bigr\rangle\cap\pi^{*}\{E\}=\sum_{D\subseteq Y}\nu([E],D)\vol_{Y|D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\Bigr).

By
Theorem

3.32
and
Theorem

3.29
, the limit of the left-hand side of (

) is nothing but

\left(\mathbb{D}(S)^{n-1}\right)_{X}\cap\{E\}=\{S^{n-1}\}\cap\{E\}.

Therefore, (
5.15
) follows.
∎

When

5.8
reduces to the following elegant formula:

Corollary 5.9
.

Assume that
X
is a compact Riemann surface. Let

be closed positive
(1,1)

6.
Loss of masses in terms of Lelong numbers

Let
X
be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.

In a series of papers, Vu and Su
[
40
,
38
,
37
]
established the following type of estimates: Consider a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
in a big cohomology class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, then

(6.1)

\vol\alpha-\vol T\geq c\Bigl(\nu(T,x)-\nu(\alpha,x)\Bigr)^{n},\quad\forall x\in X

and similar estimates bounding the difference of the volumes of two currents from below. In none of these works is the constant
c
explicit. In this section, based on the monotonicity theorems proved in
Section

5
, we shall provide an explicit constant in a much more general setup.

Since the Lelong number at a point is the same as the generic Lelong number at the exceptional divisor after blowing-up this point, we shall consider Lelong numbers along divisors instead.

6.1.
The width of a big class

Let
X
be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
, and
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a big class.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
D
be a prime divisor over
X
. We define

\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\nu(T,D):T\textup{ is a closed positive }(1,1)\textup{-current}\in\alpha\right\}.

Similarly, define the
D
-width
of
\alpha
as

\mathrm{width}(\alpha,D)\coloneqq\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-\nu(\alpha,D).

The basic properties of these quantities are summarized as follows:

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
D
be a prime divisor over
X
. Then we have the following:

(1)

For each modification
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
, we have

\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)=\nu_{\max}(\pi^{*}\alpha,D),\quad\mathrm{width}(\alpha,D)=\mathrm{width}(\pi^{*}\alpha,D).

(2)

We have
\mathrm{width}(\alpha,D)>0
.

(3)

Suppose that
D\subseteq X
, then for any
t\geq 0
, we have

(6.2)

\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)=\nu_{\max}\left(\alpha+t\{D\},D\right)-t.

(4)

Suppose that
D\subseteq X
, then

\mathrm{width}(\alpha,D)=\mathrm{width}\left(\alpha-\nu(\alpha,D)\{D\},D\right).

(5)

\nu_{\max}(\bullet,D)
is homogeneous and concave in the big cone: If
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is another big class, and
\lambda>0
, then

\nu_{\max}(\lambda\alpha,D)=\lambda\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D),\quad\nu_{\max}(\alpha+\beta,D)\geq\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)+\nu_{\max}(\beta,D).

(6)

\mathrm{width}(\bullet,D)
is continuous in the big cone.

Proof.

(1) It suffices to observe that
\pi^{*}
is a bijection between the set of closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in
\alpha
and the set of closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in
\pi^{*}\alpha
.

(2) Thanks to (1), we may assume that
D
is a prime divisor on
X
. Since
\langle\alpha\rangle
is big, we can find
\epsilon>0
small enough with
\langle\alpha\rangle-\epsilon\{D\}
big.
It follows that

\alpha-\left(\nu(\alpha,D)+\epsilon\right)\{D\}

is big.
Adding
(\nu(\alpha,D)+\epsilon)[D]
to a current with minimal singularities in this class, we obtain a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T\in\alpha
with
\nu(T,D)>\nu(\alpha,D)
.

(3) Fix
t\geq 0
.
Take a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T\in\alpha
, then
T+t[D]
is a current in
\alpha+t\{D\}
. Since

\nu(T+t[D],D)=\nu(T,D)+t,

we conclude

\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)\leq\nu_{\max}\left(\alpha+t\{D\},D\right)-t.

In particular, in view of (2), the right-hand side of (
6.2
) is always positive.

Next fix
\epsilon\in(0,\nu_{\max}(\alpha+t\{D\},D)-t)
, take a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T\in\alpha+t\{D\}
with

\nu(T,D)\geq\nu_{\max}(\alpha+t\{D\},D)-\epsilon.

By our choice of
\epsilon
,
\nu(T,D)>t
. Then
T-t[D]\in\alpha
is a closed positive
(1,1)
-current, and

\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)\geq\nu(T-t[D],D)=\nu(T,D)-t\geq\nu_{\max}(\alpha+t\{D\},D)-t-\epsilon.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0+
, the reverse inequality follows.

(4) By (3), we have

\nu_{\max}\left(\alpha-\nu(\alpha,D)\{D\},D\right)=\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-\nu(\alpha,D)=\mathrm{width}(\alpha,D).

(5) This is trivial.

(6) This follows from (5).
∎

6.2.
The toric setting

In order to get a feeling of what kind of inequality we should expect, let us first consider the toric situation. For the details of the setup, we refer to
[
44
, Chapter 12]
.

Let
T
be a complex torus of dimension
n
. Let
N
(resp.
M
) be the cocharacter lattice (resp. character lattice) of
T
. Fix a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
corresponding to a smooth projective toric variety
X
. Fix a toric-invariant big divisor

H=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}a_{\rho}D_{\rho},

where
\Sigma(1)
is the set of rays in
\Sigma
,
D_{\rho}
is the toric-invariant divisor on
X
corresponding to
\rho
via the orbit-cone correspondence, and
a_{\rho}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
P_{H}
denote the Newton polytope of
H
:

P_{H}=\Bigl\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}:\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\geq-a_{\rho}\quad\forall\rho\in\Sigma(1)\Bigr\},

where
u_{\rho}\in N
is the ray generator of
\rho
.

Recall the following key theorem proved in
[
44
, Chapter 12]
.

Theorem 6.3
.

The
\mathcal{I}
-equivalence classes of toric-invariant closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in
\alpha
are in natural bijection with the convex bodies contained in
P_{H}
.

Here a convex body refers to a compact non-empty convex set.

Given such a current
T
, the corresponding convex body (known as the Newton body of
T
) is denoted by
\Delta(T)
. We have

\vol T=n!\vol\Delta(T).

Two toric-invariant currents
T,S
in
\alpha
satisfies
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}S
if and only if
\Delta(T)\subseteq\Delta(S)
. Now fix a toric-invariant prime divisor
D
. In the ample case,
D
corresponds to a facet of
P_{H}
, but this fails in the big case. However, if
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
is the ray corresponding to
D
, then we have

\nu(T,D)=\inf\left\{\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle+a_{\rho}:m\in\Delta(T)\right\}.

So eventually, we are looking for such inequalities:

(6.3)

\vol\Delta(S)-\vol\Delta(T)\geq c\biggl(\inf\Bigl\{\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle:m\in\Delta(T)\Bigr\}-\inf\Bigl\{\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle:m\in\Delta(S)\Bigr\}\biggr)^{n}.

Note that it is impossible to find uniform
c
for all
S
and
T
, contrary to the assertions in the literature.

Example 6.4
.

Consider the simplex
Q
with the following vertices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(0,\ldots,0),(1,0,\ldots,0),(1,\epsilon,0,\ldots,0),(1,0,\epsilon,0,\ldots,0),\ldots,(1,0,\ldots,0,\epsilon)

for some
\epsilon>0
. It is a polytope contained in
[0,1]^{n}
, the Newton polytope of
\mathcal{O}(1,\dots,1)
on
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}
.

Now let

Q_{t}=\{x\in Q:x_{1}\geq t\},

where
t\in(0,1)
. Then

\vol Q-\vol Q_{t}=\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\epsilon^{n-1}.

If we take
u_{\rho}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
corresponding to the facet
\{x_{1}=0\}
, we find that the constant
c
in (
6.3
) corresponds to

\frac{\epsilon^{n-1}}{n!},

which can be arbitrarily small.

Now we look for the optimal constant
c
in (
6.3
). We first make a few simplifications. Since
u_{\rho}
is part of a basis of
N
, up to a linear transform in
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
, we may assume that
N=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
u_{\rho}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
. We have a given rational polytope
P_{H}
with positive volume contained in
\{x_{1}\geq 0\}
, two subconvex bodies
Q_{1}\subseteq Q_{2}
. We wish to find
c
so that

(6.4)

\vol Q_{2}-\vol Q_{1}\geq c\left(\inf_{x\in Q_{1}}x_{1}-\inf_{x\in Q_{2}}x_{1}\right)^{n}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
Q_{2}
touches
\{x_{1}=0\}
. Namely,
\inf_{x\in Q_{2}}x_{1}=0
.

Now we fix
Q_{2}
and
t\coloneqq\inf_{x\in Q_{1}}x_{1}
and consider the optimal
Q_{1}
, namely when the left-hand side takes the minimal value. It is clearly given by

Q_{1}\coloneqq\{(x_{1},y)\in Q:x_{1}\geq t\}.

See
Fig.

1
for the optimal situation.

Figure 1
.
The optimal situation

The volume of the remaining part is then bounded from below by the volume of a cone with base
Q_{2}\cap\{x_{1}=0\}
and a vertex with
x_{1}=t
:

\frac{t}{n}\vol\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(0,y)\in Q_{2}\}.

But we wish to obtain something to the order of
t^{n}
, for this purpose, it suffices to observe that

t\leq\max_{x\in P_{H}}x_{1}.

Putting everything together, we find

\begin{split}\vol Q_{2}-\vol Q_{1}\geq\frac{1}{n\left(\max_{x\in P_{H}}x_{1}-\min_{x\in P_{H}}x_{1}\right)^{n-1}}\\
\left(\inf_{x\in Q_{1}}x_{1}-\inf_{x\in Q_{2}}x_{1}\right)^{n}\vol\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(0,y)\in Q_{2}\}.\end{split}

Translating everything back to the language of currents, we get

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
S,T
be toric invariant closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in the same cohomology class
\alpha
with
S\succeq_{\mathcal{I}}T
, then for any toric-invariant prime divisor
D
on
X
, we have

\vol S-\vol T\geq\frac{1}{\mathrm{width}(\alpha,D)^{n-1}}\cdot\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(S,D)\Bigr)^{n}\cdot\vol_{X|D}\Bigl(S-\nu(S,D)[D]\Bigr).

See
Definition

6.1
for the definition of
\nu_{\max}
.

This proof only works in the toric setting. In general, we shall rely on the non-toric generalization of Newton bodies, namely the Okounkov bodies.

6.3.
Transcendental Okounkov bodies

Let
X
be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.

Recall that a smooth flag on
X
is a chain of smooth analytic subspaces
Y_{\bullet}=(Y_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Y_{n})
so that each
Y_{i}
is irreducible of codimension
i
in
X
. Given a big class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, there is a natural way of constructing a convex body
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\alpha)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
associated with
Y_{\bullet}
and
\alpha
, known as the Okounkov body. The Okounkov body is contained in the first quadrant. Instead of recalling the lengthy definition, we refer to
[
23
,
17
]
for the details.

We shall need a few basic properties of the Okounkov bodies, as proved in
[
17
]
and
[
44
, Chapter 10]
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a big class and
Y_{\bullet}
be a smooth flag on
X
, then we have the following:

(1)

The volume of the Okounkov body is proportional to the volume of the class:

(6.5)

\vol\alpha=n!\vol\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\alpha).

(2)

The Okounkov body
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\alpha)
is continuous with respect to
\alpha
if we consider the topology induced by the Hausdorff metric.

(3)

The two ends have pluripotential-theoretic interpretations:

\min_{x\in\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\alpha)}x_{1}=\nu(\alpha,Y_{1}),\quad\max_{x\in\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\alpha)}x_{1}=\nu_{\max}(\alpha,Y_{1}).

(4)

For all
t\in(\nu(\alpha,Y_{1}),\nu_{\max}(\alpha,Y_{1}))
, we have

\vol_{X|Y_{1}}\left(\alpha-t\{Y_{1}\}\right)=(n-1)!\vol\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(t,y)\in\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\alpha)\right\}.

In fact, the slice in (4) is an example of the partial Okounkov bodies studied in
[
46
]
and
[
44
, Chapter 10]
.

We shall need a few auxiliary lemmata.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
A>0
.
Let
f\colon[0,A]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a continuous concave function. Then for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and any
t_{0}\in(0,A)
, we have

\int_{0}^{A}f(t)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{f(t_{0})^{n}A^{n+1}}{n+1}\min\{t_{0},A-t_{0}\}^{-n}.

Proof.

Fix
t_{0}\in(0,A)
, assume that
f(t_{0})=C\geq 0
, let us compute the maximum of
\int_{0}^{A}f(t)^{n}\mathrm{d}t
. Since
f
is concave, the maximization problem reduces immediately to the case where
f
is affine. So we need to compute the maximum of

\int_{0}^{A}\left(B(t-t_{0})+C\right)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{(B(A-t_{0})+C)^{n+1}-(-Bt_{0}+C)^{n+1}}{(n+1)B}

under the constraints that

-Bt_{0}+C\geq 0,\quad B(A-t_{0})+C\geq 0.

Observe that the integral is convex in
B
, and hence the maximum is obtained at the boundary of the interval, in other words, the maximum of the integral is

\max\left\{\frac{C^{n}A^{n+1}}{(n+1)t_{0}^{n}},\frac{C^{n}A^{n+1}}{(n+1)(A-t_{0})^{n}}\right\}=\frac{C^{n}A^{n+1}}{n+1}\min\{t_{0},A-t_{0}\}^{-n}.

∎

Lemma 6.8
.

Assume that
n>1
.
Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. Assume that

\min_{x\in P}x_{1}=0,\quad\max_{x\in P}x_{1}=A\geq 0.

Then for any
t_{0}\in[0,A]
, we have

\vol\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(t_{0},y)\in P\right\}\geq\frac{n}{A^{n}}\vol P\cdot\min\{t_{0},A-t_{0}\}^{n-1}.

The proof resulted from a discussion with Yangyang Li.

Proof.

We may assume that
t_{0}\in(0,A)
since otherwise there is nothing to prove.

We write

g(t)\coloneqq\vol\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(t,y)\in P\right\}.

By Brunn–Minkowski inequality, the function
g^{1/(n-1)}
is concave.
Therefore, thanks to
Lemma

6.7
, we have

\vol P=\int_{0}^{A}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{g(t_{0})A^{n}}{n}\min\{t_{0},A-t_{0}\}^{-n+1}.

∎

The following lower bound of the restricted volume seems new.

Corollary 6.9
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a big class. Then for any prime divisor
D
on
X
, we have

(6.6)

\vol_{X|D}\left(\alpha-t\{D\}\right)\geq\frac{\vol\alpha}{\mathrm{width}(\alpha,D)^{n}}\cdot\min\{t-\nu(\alpha,D),\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-t\}^{n-1}

as long as
\nu(\alpha,D)\leq t\leq\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)
.

In particular, when
\nu(\alpha,D)<t<\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)
, we have
\nu(\alpha-t\{D\},D)=0
.

Proof.

The inequality (
6.6
) is trivial if
t
takes the boundary value. We may assume that

\nu(\alpha,D)<t<\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D).

Since the problem is invariant after replacing
X
by a modification and
D
by its strict transform, we may assume that
D
is smooth and is the first component in a smooth flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
. Then our assertion follows from
Lemma

6.8
and
Theorem

6.6
. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\vol_{X|D}\left(\alpha-t\{D\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(n-1)!\vol\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(t,y)\in\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\alpha)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(n-1)!\cdot\frac{n}{\Bigl(\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-\nu(\alpha,D)\Bigr)^{n}}\vol\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\alpha)\cdot\min\{t-\nu(\alpha,D),\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-t\}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\vol\alpha}{\Bigl(\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-\nu(\alpha,D)\Bigr)^{n}}\cdot\min\{t-\nu(\alpha,D),\nu_{\max}(\alpha,D)-t\}^{n-1}.

∎

6.4.
Loss of mass problem

Now fix a connected compact Kähler manifold
X
of dimension
n
.

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be big classes. Consider closed positive
(1,1)
-currents
S_{i},T_{i}\in\alpha_{i}
with
S_{i}\succeq_{\mathcal{I}}T_{i}
. Fix a prime divisor
D
over

. Then

\displaystyle\vol(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})-\vol(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{i=1,\ldots,n}\left(\frac{1}{\prod_{j\neq i}\mathrm{width}(\alpha_{j},D)}\cdot\vol_{X|D}\left(\mathbb{D}(S_{1}),\ldots,\widehat{\mathbb{D}(S_{i})},\ldots,\mathbb{D}(S_{n})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Bigl(\nu(T_{i},D)-\nu(S_{i},D)\Bigr).

Proof.

Observe that

\vol(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})-\vol(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\geq\vol(S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{n})-\vol(T_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{n}).

We apply
Theorem

5.8
to get

\vol(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})-\vol(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\geq\Bigl(\nu(T_{1},D)-\nu(S_{1},D)\Bigr)\vol_{X|D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(S_{2}),\ldots,\mathbb{D}(S_{n})\Bigr).

Next observe that for
j>1
,

\nu(T_{j},D)-\nu(S_{j},D)\leq\nu_{\max}(\alpha_{j},D)-\nu(\alpha_{j},D),

and hence

\displaystyle\vol(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})-\vol(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\nu(T_{j},D)-\nu(S_{j},D)}{\nu_{\max}(\alpha_{j},D)-\nu(\alpha_{j},D)}\cdot\Bigl(\nu(T_{1},D)-\nu(S_{1},D)\Bigr)\cdot\vol_{X|D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(S_{2}),\ldots,\mathbb{D}(S_{n})\Bigr),

and our assertion follows by symmetry.
∎

Proof.

7.
Toric monotonicity theorem

In this section, we will explain
Theorem

5.8
in the toric setting. As we shall see, our main theorem recovers the well-known Minkowski volume formula of convex bodies due to Aleksandrov
[
1
]
and Fenchel–Jessen
[
24
]
. See
[
34
]
for a modern treatment. The readers are assumed to be familiar with the toric pluripotential developed in
[
44
, Chapter 12]
.

We shall continue to use the notations of
Section

6.2
. In particular,
N
is a
n
-dimensional lattice and
M
is its dual,
X
is a smooth projective toric variety corresponding to a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
T_{c}
be the compact toric contained in the dense torus in
X
.

We recall a few standard terminologies from convex geometry. Given a convex body
P\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
, the support function
\Supp_{P}\colon N_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\Supp_{P}(n)\coloneqq\max_{m\in P}\langle m,n\rangle.

Given
u\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
, we write
F(P,u)
for the support hyperplane of
P
in the direction of
u
, namely

F(P,u)\coloneqq\left\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}:\langle m,u\rangle=\Supp_{P}(u)\right\}.

We first observe that
Theorem

5.8
requires only toric-invariant divisors.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
S
be a
T_{c}
-invariant closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
with positive volume,
T,T^{\prime}
be
T_{c}
-invariant closed positive
(1,1)
-currents on
X
in the same cohomology class such that
T\preceq_{\mathcal{I}}T^{\prime}
. Then

(7.1)

\begin{split}&\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(S)^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\Bigr)-\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(S)^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\Bigr)\\
=&\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}{}^{\prime}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}{}^{\prime}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{Y|D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\Bigr),\end{split}

where the primes refer to the fact that we only consider toric-invariant modifications and toric-invariant prime divisors.

Proof.

The
\geq
direction is a direct consequence of
Theorem

5.2
. The reverse inequality follows almost
verbatim
from the argument of
Theorem

5.8
.
∎

We are now in a position to translate everything into the language of convex bodies. Just for clarity, we assume that
\{S\}
is a big integral class in the Néron–Severi group, so that
[
44
, Chapter 12]
can be applied without modification. The general case follows by simple approximations.

Fix a basis of
N
and define a norm on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
by making this basis orthonormal.
As shown in
[
44
, Theorem 12.3.3]
, the mixed volumes on the left-hand side of (
7.1
) can be expressed as the mixed volumes of the corresponding Newton bodies:

(7.2)

\left(\mathbb{D}(S)^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T^{\prime})\right)-\left(\mathbb{D}(S)^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{D}(T)\right)=n!\Biggl(\vol\left(\Delta(S)^{n-1},\Delta(T^{\prime})\right)-\vol\left(\Delta(S)^{n-1},\Delta(T)\right)\Biggr).

As for the right-hand side, consider a toric-invariant prime divisor
D\subseteq Y
.Let
u_{D}\in N
be the primitive vector corresponding to
D
. Then
\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)
is nothing but the difference between the support functions of the Newton bodies:

(7.3)

\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)=\Supp_{\Delta(T^{\prime})}(-u_{D})-\Supp_{\Delta(T)}(-u_{D}),

as a consequence of
[
44
, Corollary 12.2.1]
.

Thanks to
[
44
, Corollary 12.3.8]
, we have

(7.4)

\vol_{|D}\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}^{n-1}\right)=(n-1)!\vol F\Bigl(\Delta\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right),-u_{D}\Bigr),

where the volume on the right-hand side is the
(n-1)
-dimensional volume on the normal hyperplane to
u_{D}
so that the smallest cube of the restriction of
M
has volume
1
. Note that the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the latter cube is exactly the norm of
u_{D}
. Therefore, we could rescale the variable
-u_{D}
in the support functions in (
7.3
) to a unit vector and replace
\vol
in (
7.4
) by the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure at the same time, without changing their product as on the right-hand side of (
7.1
).

Therefore, combining (
7.4
) with (
7.3
), we can rewrite

\displaystyle\sum_{D\subseteq Y}{}^{\prime}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{Y|D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(T_{1})_{Y}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}(T_{n-1})_{Y}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\Supp_{\Delta(T^{\prime})}(v)-\Supp_{\Delta(T)}(v)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{S}\Bigl(\Delta\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right)\Bigr)(v),

where
\mathcal{S}(\Delta\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right))
is the area measure of the polytope
\Delta\left(\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}\right)
(the Newton polytope of an arbitrary toric invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Y
representing the class
\mathbb{D}(S)_{Y}
, well-defined up to translation), as defined in
[
34
, Section 4.2]
. For those who are not familiar with the area formula
\mathcal{S}
, it suffices to use the simple formula
[
34
, (4.24)]
as the definition.

Taking the limit with respect to
Y
and using the weak continuity of the area measure, we find

\displaystyle\lim_{\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X}{}^{\prime}\sum_{D\subseteq Y}{}^{\prime}\Bigl(\nu(T,D)-\nu(T^{\prime},D)\Bigr)\vol_{|D}\Bigl(\mathbb{D}(T_{1})_{Y}\cap\dots\cap\mathbb{D}(T_{n-1})_{Y}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\Supp_{\Delta(T^{\prime})}(v)-\Supp_{\Delta(T)}(v)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{S}\Bigl(\Delta(S)\Bigr)(v),

Therefore, in view of (
7.2
), (
7.1
) translates into

(7.5)

\displaystyle\vol\left(\Delta(S)^{n-1},\Delta(T^{\prime})\right)-\vol\left(\Delta(S)^{n-1},\Delta(T)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\Supp_{\Delta(T^{\prime})}(v)-\Supp_{\Delta(T)}(v)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{S}\Bigl(\Delta(S)\Bigr)(v).

By polarization, we get

(7.6)

\displaystyle\vol\Bigl(\Delta(T_{1}),\ldots,\Delta(T_{n-1}),\Delta(T^{\prime})\Bigr)-\vol\Bigl(\Delta(T_{1}),\ldots,\Delta(T_{n-1}),\Delta(T)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\Supp_{\Delta(T^{\prime})}(v)-\Supp_{\Delta(T)}(v)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{S}\Bigl(\Delta(T_{1}),\ldots,\Delta(T_{n-1})\Bigr)(v).

Here we have used the mixed area measure
\mathcal{S}
as defined in
[
34
, Theorem 5.1.7]
.

Note that arbitrary convex bodies
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1}
can be realized as
\Delta(T_{1}),\ldots,\Delta(T_{n-1})
, and similarly arbitrary pairs of convex bodies

follow immediately from
[

, Corollary 12.3.3]
. We conclude the following consequence:

Corollary 7.2
.

Consider convex bodies
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1},Q^{\prime},Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
Q^{\prime}\supseteq Q
, then

(7.7)

\displaystyle\vol\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1},Q^{\prime}\right)-\vol\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1},Q\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\Supp_{Q^{\prime}}-\Supp_{Q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{S}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1}\right).

The argument sketched above only works when
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1}

We have therefore completely recovered the Minkowski volume formula on Page 282 of
[
34

. In fact, our main theorem gives a new construction of the mixed area measures
\mathcal{S}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1}\right)
of convex bodies themselves!

In the non-toric setting, we expect that there be a similar limit measure between nef b-divisors defined on the Berkovich analytification of

. These measures should play similar roles in pluripotential theory as the mixed area measures play in convex geometry.

Appendix A
Positive forms

We briefly recall the notion of positive forms due to Lelong
[
31
]
and Harvey–Knapp
[
27
]
. The latter reference, on which a large part of Demailly’s textbook
[
20
, Chapter III]
was based, was unfortunately omitted from the reference list.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional complex vector space of dimension
n
. Let
V^{\vee}
denote the complex linear space consisting of
\mathbb{R}
-linear functional
V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
on
V
.
For each pair of non-negative integers
p,q
, we write
\Lambda^{p,q}V^{\vee}
for the space of
(p,q)
-forms in
\Lambda^{p+q}(V^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})
. Note that the complex dual of
V
is
\Lambda^{1,0}V^{\vee}
.

Fix an integer
p\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
in the sequel.

Definition A.1
.

A form
\alpha\in\Lambda^{p,p}V^{\vee}
is

(1)

weakly positive
if for each
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-p}\in V^{\vee}
, we have

\alpha\wedge\mathrm{i}\beta_{1}\wedge\overline{\beta_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{i}\beta_{n-p}\wedge\overline{\beta_{n-p}}

gives the positive orientation of
V
;

(2)

strongly positive
if
\alpha
is a finite linear combination with
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
coefficients of terms of the form

(A.1)

\mathrm{i}\beta_{1}\wedge\overline{\beta_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{i}\beta_{p}\wedge\overline{\beta_{p}},

where
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{p}\in V^{\vee}
. A form like (
A.1
) is called a
basic form
.

(3)

positive
if for one (hence all) basis
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}
of
\Lambda^{1,0}V^{\vee}
, when we expand
\alpha
as

(A.2)

\alpha=(-1)^{p(p-1)/2}\sum_{|I|=|J|=p}\alpha_{I,J}\mathrm{i}^{p}w_{I}\wedge\overline{w_{J}},

the matrix
(\alpha_{I,J})_{I,J}
is Hermitian and positive semidefinite. Here
w_{I}=w_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{i_{p}}
, where
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{p}
are the elements in
I
, and
\overline{\beta_{J}}
is similar.

Remark A.2
.

The terminology needs some clarification. In
[
31
]
, Lelong only introduced the notions of weakly positive forms and strongly positive forms. He called the weakly positive forms
positive forms
. Lelong’s terminology is largely followed by the modern schools in complex geometry.

The notion of positive forms as above is introduced by Harvey–Knapp. The terminology of Harvey–Knapp is, by contrast, largely followed by people in non-Archimedean geometry and tropical geometry.

When we represent a weakly positive form in the form of matrices as in (
A.2
), the matrix is always Hermitian. See
[
20
, Page 167, Corollary 1.5]
.

It is immediate from the definitions that a strongly positive form is positive, and a positive form is weakly positive. Furthermore, the cone of weakly positive forms is the dual cone of that of strongly positive forms.

When
p=0,1,n-1,n
, all three notions are equivalent. For all other value of
p
, namely
p=2,3,\ldots,n-2
, all three notions are different. This was originally a question of Lelong, and proved in
[
27
]
.

It is easy to see that weakly positive and positive forms form closed convex cones.

Lemma A.3
.

The set of strongly positive
(p,p)
-forms on

form a closed convex cone.

Therefore, the cone of strongly positive forms is the dual of that of weakly positive forms.

We need the following straightforward consequence in the main body of the paper: A
(p,p)

This consequence has already been widely applied in the related literature without rigorous justification.

Remark A.4

A very similar argument works in the non-Archimedean setting, and gives a rigorous proof to the assertion about strongly positive Lagerberg forms (as defined in

Proof.

The non-trivial point is to show that the cone of strongly positive
(p,p)
-forms is closed.

Fix an identification
V\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
so that
V
gets a Hermitian norm.

Step 1
. We show that

C\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\Lambda^{p,p}V^{\vee}:\alpha=\mathrm{i}\beta_{1}\wedge\overline{\beta_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{i}\beta_{p}\wedge\overline{\beta_{p}}\textup{ for some }\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{p}\in V^{\vee}\right\}

is closed.

Let

C^{\prime}\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\Lambda^{p,p}V^{\vee}:\alpha=\mathrm{i}^{p^{2}}\gamma\wedge\overline{\gamma}\textup{ for some }\gamma\in\Lambda^{p,0}V^{\vee}\right\}.

Note that
C\subseteq C^{\prime}
.

We first show that
C^{\prime}
is closed. Consider a Cauchy sequence
(\mathrm{i}^{p^{2}}\gamma_{j}\wedge\bar{\gamma_{j}})_{j}
in
C
. Fix a basis
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}
of
V^{\vee}
. Then we can write

\gamma_{j}=\sum_{|I|=p}\gamma_{j,I}w_{I}.

The boundedness of
\mathrm{i}^{p^{2}}\gamma_{j}\wedge\bar{\gamma_{j}}
implies the boundedness of
\gamma_{j,I}
for each fixed
I
. Therefore, after subtracting a subsequence, we may assume that
\gamma_{j,I}\to\gamma_{I}
for some
\gamma_{I}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

\mathrm{i}^{p^{2}}\gamma_{j}\wedge\bar{\gamma_{j}}\to\mathrm{i}^{p^{2}}\gamma\wedge\bar{\gamma}.

Next we show that
C
is closed. This means, suppose that
\gamma_{j}
is decomposable then we want to show that
\gamma
is also decomposable. But the condition of being decomposable is equivalent to finitely many polynomial relations between the coefficients, classically known as the Plücker relations, which pass through limits. See
[
25
, Section 3.1.E]
for details.

More conceptually, the decomposability of
\gamma
also follows from the fact that the Plücker embedding of the Grasmannian is a closed immersion.
Our assertion follows.

Step 2
. Let
S
be the unit sphere in
V
. Then from Step 1, the intersection
S\cap C
is compact.

We claim that the convex hull of
S\cap C
does not contain the origin. Suppose that this fails, then we can find
v_{i}\in S\cap C
and
\lambda_{i}>0
(
i=1,\ldots,m
) so that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}v_{i}=0.

But under the matrix representation (
A.2
), the
v_{i}
’s can be regarded as strictly positive matrices, so this cannot happen.

Our assertion now follows from
Lemma

A.5
.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

Let
C
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
0\not\in C
, then the convex cone generated by
C
union with
0
is closed.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{i}>0
and
c_{i}\in C
. Assume that
\lambda_{i}c_{i}
converges to
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then we need to show that either
c=0
or
c
is in the convex cone generated by
c
.

We may assume that
c\neq 0
. Then since
C
is compact and
0\not\in C
, then
|c_{i}|
is a bounded sequence, bounded away from
0
as well. But
\lambda_{i}c_{i}
has a non-zero limit, this means
\lambda_{i}
is also bounded and bounded away from
0
. After replacing everything by a subsequence, we may assume that
\lambda_{i}\to\lambda>0
,
c_{i}\to c^{\prime}\in C
. Then
c=\lambda c^{\prime}
, and our assertion follows.
∎

A symmetric form can always be decomposed as the difference of two strongly positive forms. More generally, we have the following:

Theorem A.6
.

Fix a basis
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}
, then
\Lambda^{p,p}V^{\vee}
has a basis consisting of forms of the form

\mathrm{i}^{p^{2}}\beta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\beta_{p}\wedge\overline{\beta_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\overline{\beta_{p}},

where
\beta_{i}
is one of
w_{i}\pm w_{j}
or
w_{i}\pm\mathrm{i}w_{j}
with
i,j=1,\ldots,n
.

This result was first proved by Harvey–Knapp
[
27
, Proposition 1.9]
. See also Demailly
[

, Page 167, Lemma 1.4]
.
The corresponding statement fails in the setup of Lagerberg forms, as previously asserted in the first version of

]

]
for the details.
