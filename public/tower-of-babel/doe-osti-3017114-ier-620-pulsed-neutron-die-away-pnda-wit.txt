IER-620 Pulsed-Neutron Die-Away (PNDA) with Oil Targets
CEDT Phase-3a Experiment Initiation

The Pulsed-Neutron Die-Away (PNDA) Testbed has previously been used to perform
thermal scattering law validation measurements on HDPE and Lucite (IER-501) and
water (IER-580). This experimental campaign expands the tests to include three
additional hydrogenous materials relevant to various machining operations with
fissionable materials. As such, it will use much of the same processes and equipment
from the previous PNDA experiments (Figure 1).

New Material Description

Three new materials of interest have been identified: Mobilmet 423, Mobil Teresstic
220, and propylene glycol. Mobilmet 423 is a multi-purpose cutting oil used for
machining operations. Teresstic 220 is a long service life lubricating oil. Propylene
glycol is a major constituent of many machine coolant mixtures.

Mobilmet 423 and Teresstic 220
Both Mobilmet 423 and Teresstic 220 are potentially hazardous petroleum oils
manufactured to performance requirements rather than chemical or purity
specifications. MobilMet 423 is a mixture of heavy and light paraffinic oils, and Teresstic
220 is primarily heavy paraffinic oils. As such, their material composition is not well
described, and the limited ingredient lists contain wide tolerance bands. The safety data
sheets (SDS) and product data sheets (PDS) for both Mobilmet 423 and Teresstic 220
are attached to this document as appendices. A request for chemical and additive
information was submitted to the manufacturer, ExXon Mobil Corporation, but, as of this
document, has not been provided. Chemical analysis, such as inductively coupled
plasma mass spectroscopy (ICP-MS) and combustion composition, will be performed
on each material to determine material composition of the oils. Analysis of the
temperature effects on density will be performed using an appropriate technique to help
quantify uncertainty.

Depending on data availability, Mobilmet 423 and Teresstic 220 may not be suitable as
benchmarks for inclusion in the International Critical Safety Benchmark Evaluation
Project (ICSBEP). However, the experiment may still be valuable as an additional
coarse check test for thermal scattering law selection.

Mobilmet 423 is typically only available in base units of 55 gallons, with Teresstic 220 is
available in 10 gallon containers.

For each of these two oils, at least three measurements sets will be taken over a range
of geometric bucklings. Additional sets may be added as needed.

Propylene Glycol
A mixture of propylene glycol and water is a common, low-cost machine coolant, and is
of interest for criticality safety considerations. Water was previously analyzed in IER-580
and thermal scattering law (TSL) information is widely available. Propylene glycol
(C3H8O2) is the target material for this experimental campaign and pure propylene glycol
will be measured.

Propylene glycol is a common ingredient available in purities greater than 99.5% when
purchased in large volumes under the United States Pharmacopeia National Formulary
(USP-NF) standard. The USP-NF standard provides acceptance criteria and
contaminant limits. For propylene glycol, these include analysis for heavy metals,
chlorides, sulfates, and water content. A copy of the USP-NF standard is included in the
appendix. Additionally, most vendors will provide a certificate of analysis (COA) for the
specific product lot when purchasing propylene glycol. The COA for the propylene glycol
used in IER-620 will be included in the CED-3b report.

At least six measurements sets will be taken of propylene glycol to span the sensitivity
to nuclear data uncertainties. At least two measurement sets will be taken at the same
geometric buckling, but different fill height and container radius. Geometric buckling for
a cylinder is calculated by:

Work Control Document

The experiments will be conducted in rooms R129A (Inherently Safe Subcritical
Assembly Facility) and R183A (Radiation Calibration Laboratory Low Scatter Facility) in
Building 255 at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) under Work Control
Document WCD 102256. To allow for operations of the neutron generator in these
locations, the generator was surveyed by health physicists and the work control
document for the rooms was modified. The health physicists defined the maximum
operable conditions of the generator and the appropriate work conditions for operations.
The work control document will be updated to allow the use and disposal of the three
test materials. Storage of the materials will be in Room 129B. Each experiment set will
take approximately one week for execution.

Material Procurement and Target Preperation
Most materials were previously acquired by LLNL for use with IER-501 and IER 580.

This includes the experimental testbed (shielded box, neutron generator, neutron
detectors, aluminum containers). All three materials, Mobilmet 423, Teresstic 220 and
propylene glycol are available from multiple vendors with typical lead times of less
than two weeks. A minimum of 10 gallons will be purchased for each material type. To
prepare experiment targets, each material will be dispensed into aluminum containers
using hand pumps, siphons, or syringes and the containers will be permanently
sealed with glue. Additional containment requirements were identified during the work
planning process, and will be purchased as required. The additional cost for materials
is estimated at less than $5000.

Product Description

Mobilmet 420 Series products are high-performance multi-purpose cutting oils. They are chlorine-free, non-staining and non-corrosive. They are designed for
light to moderate duty cutting operations and they are also suitable as machine tool lubricants and for use in light duty hydraulic systems. They are formulated
from high quality base oils and select additives to provide effective machining performance in a wide variety of operations of both ferrous and non-ferrous
metals. The oils are light-colored and transparent so that the work area can be clearly seen at all times. The Mobilmet 420s are formulated to prevent the
formation of oil mist in the vicinity of the machine tool.

Mobilmet 420 Series fluids are resistant to foaming, even with excess splashing so that superior performance is provided in the machine tool lubrication system.
With relatively low pour points and high viscosity indexes, they are not difficult to dispense in cold conditions and provide adequate film strength in
hot-running machine tool bearings under load.

Features and Benefits

Features and potential benefits of Mobilmet 420 products include:
In addition to their machining performance characteristics, the Mobilmet 420 Series fluids are multi-purpose in nature which helps reduce the problems
associated with cross-contamination, thus improving production and reducing rejects. They are formulated using chlorine-free additives reducing the
environmental impact of disposal and spillage. The Mobilmet 420 Series fluids control built-up edge and protect the tool tip from wear. They provide improved
surface finish that may eliminate the need for finish turning or forming before grinding.

 SECTION 4 FIRST AID MEASURES

INHALATION
 Remove from further exposure. For those providing assistance, avoid exposure to yourself or others. Use
 adequate respiratory protection. If respiratory irritation, dizziness, nausea, or unconsciousness occurs, seek
 immediate medical assistance. If breathing has stopped, assist ventilation with a mechanical device or use
 mouth-to-mouth resuscitation.

SKIN CONTACT
 Wash contact areas with soap and water. If product is injected into or under the skin, or into any part of the
 body, regardless of the appearance of the wound or its size, the individual should be evaluated immediately by
 a physician as a surgical emergency. Even though initial symptoms from high pressure injection may be
 minimal or absent, early surgical treatment within the first few hours may significantly reduce the ultimate extent
 of injury.

EYE CONTACT
 Flush thoroughly with water. If irritation occurs, get medical assistance.

INGESTION
 First aid is normally not required. Seek medical attention if discomfort occurs.

 SECTION 6 ACCIDENTAL RELEASE MEASURES

NOTIFICATION PROCEDURES
 In the event of a spill or accidental release, notify relevant authorities in accordance with all applicable
 regulations. US regulations require reporting releases of this material to the environment which exceed the
 applicable reportable quantity or oil spills which could reach any waterway including intermittent dry creeks. The
 National Response Center can be reached at (800)424-8802.

PROTECTIVE MEASURES
 Avoid contact with spilled material. See Section 5 for fire fighting information. See the Hazard Identification
 Section for Significant Hazards. See Section 4 for First Aid Advice. See Section 8 for advice on the minimum
 requirements for personal protective equipment. Additional protective measures may be necessary, depending
 on the specific circumstances and/or the expert judgment of the emergency responders.

 For emergency responders: Respiratory protection: respiratory protection will be necessary only in special
 cases, e.g., formation of mists. Half-face or full-face respirator with filter(s) for dust/organic vapor or Self
 Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA) can be used depending on the size of spill and potential level of
 exposure. If the exposure cannot be completely characterized or an oxygen deficient atmosphere is possible
 or anticipated, SCBA is recommended. Work gloves that are resistant to hydrocarbons are recommended.
 Gloves made of polyvinyl acetate (PVA) are not water-resistant and are not suitable for emergency use.
 Chemical goggles are recommended if splashes or contact with eyes is possible. Small spills: normal
 antistatic work clothes are usually adequate. Large spills: full body suit of chemical resistant, antistatic
 material is recommended.

SPILL MANAGEMENT
 Land Spill: Stop leak if you can do it without risk. Recover by pumping or with suitable absorbent.

 Water Spill: Stop leak if you can do it without risk. Confine the spill immediately with booms. Warn other
 shipping. Remove from the surface by skimming or with suitable absorbents. Seek the advice of a specialist
 before using dispersants.

 Water spill and land spill recommendations are based on the most likely spill scenario for this material;
 however, geographic conditions, wind, temperature, (and in the case of a water spill) wave and current direction
 and speed may greatly influence the appropriate action to be taken. For this reason, local experts should be
 consulted. Note: Local regulations may prescribe or limit action to be taken.

ENVIRONMENTAL PRECAUTIONS
 Large Spills: Dike far ahead of liquid spill for later recovery and disposal. Prevent entry into waterways,
 sewers, basements or confined areas.

HANDLING
 Prevent small spills and leakage to avoid slip hazard. Material can accumulate static charges which may cause
 an electrical spark (ignition source). When the material is handled in bulk, an electrical spark could ignite any
 flammable vapors from liquids or residues that may be present (e.g., during switch-loading operations). Use
 proper bonding and/or ground procedures. However, bonding and grounds may not eliminate the hazard from
 static accumulation. Consult local applicable standards for guidance. Additional references include American
 Petroleum Institute 2003 (Protection Against Ignitions Arising out of Static, Lightning and Stray Currents) or
 National Fire Protection Agency 77 (Recommended Practice on Static Electricity) or CENELEC CLC/TR 50404
 (Electrostatics - Code of practice for the avoidance of hazards due to static electricity).

 Static Accumulator: This material is a static accumulator.

STORAGE
 The type of container used to store the material may affect static accumulation and dissipation. Do not store
 in open or unlabelled containers. Keep away from incompatible materials.
