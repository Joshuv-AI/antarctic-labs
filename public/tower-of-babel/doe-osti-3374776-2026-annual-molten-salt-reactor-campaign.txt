ABSTRACT
This report documents the 2026 Annual Molten Salt Reactor (MSR) Campaign Review held in
Albuquerque, New Mexico, from April 21–24, 2026. The review was organized by Dr. Patricia
Paviet, National Technical Director of the Advanced Reactor Technology (ART) MSR program for
the U.S. Department of Energy Office of Nuclear Energy (DOE-NE), and included participation
from principal investigators, federal managers, developers, regulators, and members of the broader
MSR community.
The 2026 review expanded beyond the ART-MSR campaign to include related DOE-NE programs
supporting molten salt reactor advancement, including the Nuclear Energy Advanced Modeling and
Simulation (NEAMS) program, the Advanced Materials and Manufacturing Technologies (AMMT)
program, Advanced Reactor Safeguards and Security (ARSS), Material Protection, Accounting and
Control Technologies (MPACT), and relevant advanced fuels activities. Three days were dedicated
to technical presentations and panel discussions covering thermal properties, off-gas management,
modeling and simulation, safety and licensing, safeguards and security, materials and corrosion, and
irradiation activities. A fourth day was dedicated to technical tours of Sandia National Laboratories
and Kairos Power facilities.
Attendance was strong and comparable to the prior annual review, with approximately 75 in-person
participants per day, 75–80 virtual attendees per day, and approximately 30 participants in the tour
day. The review fostered significant technical exchange across national laboratories, universities,
industry, regulators, and international participants. This report summarizes the review structure,
technical themes, participation, tours, feedback, and conclusions relevant to future planning for the
MSR campaign.

1. INTRODUCTION

This report documents the annual Molten Salt Reactor (MSR) Campaign Review conducted in
Albuquerque, New Mexico, from April 21 to April 24, 2026. The event served as the deliverable
for DOE milestone M3AT-26PN0701012, “Complete the annual MSR campaign review.” The
review was organized and moderated by Dr. Patricia Paviet, National Technical Director of the
Advanced Reactor Technology (ART) MSR program for DOE-NE.
The annual MSR campaign review provides a forum for principal investigators (PIs), federal man-
agers, developers, regulators, and the general public to present updates on recent findings, exchange
technical information, and identify opportunities for future collaboration. In 2026, the review
was deliberately broadened to include technical activities from multiple DOE-NE programs rele-
vant to molten salt reactor development, including modeling and simulation, irradiation, advanced
materials, safeguards, and physical protection.
The meeting was held in hybrid format, enabling both in-person and virtual participation. Three
days were devoted to technical presentations, panel sessions, and discussion, followed by a fourth
day of technical tours at Sandia National Laboratories (SNL) and Kairos Power.

1.1. Objectives and Scope

The 2026 annual review had several objectives:
 1. Provide DOE-NE federal managers and the broader MSR community with updates on ongoing
 ART-MSR research activities.
 2. Expand engagement with other DOE-NE programs supporting molten salt reactor develop-
 ment.
 3. Highlight recent progress in thermal properties, off-gas management, safety, licensing, mod-
 eling and simulation, materials, irradiation, safeguards, and security.
 4. Encourage discussion across programs and institutions to identify technical gaps, validation
 needs, and future research directions.
 5. Provide participants with direct exposure to Sandia and Kairos technical capabilities through
 site tours.
Participating programs included:
 • Advanced Reactor Technology – Molten Salt Reactor (ART-MSR)
 • Nuclear Energy Advanced Modeling and Simulation (NEAMS)

 11

 • Advanced Materials and Manufacturing Technologies (AMMT)
 • Advanced Reactor Safeguards and Security (ARSS)
 • Material Protection, Accounting and Control Technologies (MPACT)
 • Advanced Fuels Campaign

1.2. Review Organization and Structure

The review consisted of three days of presentations and discussion sessions followed by a fourth
day dedicated to tours. Each of the first three days generally began at 8:00 a.m. Mountain Time
and concluded at 6:00 p.m. Mountain Time, with a one-hour lunch break except for the third day,
which had a shortened lunch interval.
The 2026 review featured:
 • Program overviews
 • Topical technical sessions
 • Roadmap discussions
 • Panel sessions
 • Interactive polling related to off-gas roadmap topics
 • A closed session among ART-MSR-supported scientists
 • Technical tours of SNL and Kairos Power

1.2.1. Expanded Programmatic Participation

Dr. Paviet expanded the meeting to include complementary DOE-NE programs. These additions
included:
 • Dr. David Andersson (LANL), National Technical Director of NEAMS
 • Dr. Meimei Li, National Technical Director of AMMT
 • Dr. Ben Cipiti (SNL), National Technical Director of Advanced Reactor Safeguards and
 Security
 • Dr. Toni Karlsson (INL), presenting relevant advanced fuels irradiation work
 • Mike Browne, National Technical Director of Material Protection, Accounting and Control
 Technologies
This cross-program participation increased the technical breadth of the review and strengthened
coordination across campaigns relevant to MSR development.

2. PROCEEDINGS SUMMARY

2.1. Day 1: Program Overview, Roadmaps, and Thermal Properties

Day 1 opened with welcoming remarks from Janelle Eddins, followed by an overview of Sandia
National Laboratories by Sandia Senior Manager Richard Griffith, and an overview of the ART-
MSR program by Patricia Paviet. The ART-MSR overview emphasized research distributed across
seven national laboratories, covering molten salt thermal properties, synthesis, sensor development,
off-gas management, accident scenario analysis using MELCOR, and development of evaluation
models through NEAMS tools.

A major feature of the opening day was presentation of three road maps under development:

 1. An update to the thermal properties roadmap released in 2021

 2. An integrated off-gas management roadmap

 3. An overall ART-MSR roadmap addressing short- and long-term needs identified by MSR
 developers

The overall roadmap was described as a technology development framework intended to facilitate
near-term commercial deployment of MSRs by addressing barriers related to safety adequacy and
supporting research prioritization. Feedback from the developer community was being actively
solicited through questionnaires. Key themes in the feedback included:

 • Need for cross-validation of property data by multiple laboratories

 • Specific interest in beryllium-, uranium-, and thorium-bearing salts

 • Interest in third-party testing

 • Need for fast-neutron facilities to evaluate materials in fast-spectrum conditions

 • Uncertainty surrounding accounting methods for plutonium-containing fuels

 • Limited inclusion of MSRs in fuel cycle simulations

 • Importance of studying on-site processing for fuel chemistry control

 • Concern regarding the generation of NQA-1 quality simulation data for NRC confidence

 • Need for high-quality property data before modeling can be completed with confidence

 13

Questions raised during discussion included fuel salt containerization and transportation, potential
for on-site synthesis, and the need for clearer prioritization and timelines in the roadmaps.
The afternoon continued with presentations on molten salt thermal properties, including uranium-
bearing salt measurements, molecular dynamics and ab initio molecular dynamics approaches,
americium chloride thermophysical properties, uncertainty quantification, fluid inclusion charac-
terization, synthesis, and database development.
A closed session among scientists sponsored by the ART-MSR program concluded the day and
focused on identifying research gaps and potential FY2027 priorities, contingent on funding.

2.2. Day 2: Off-Gas Management and NEAMS

Day 2 focused in the morning on off-gas management and in the afternoon on NEAMS-related
modeling and simulation efforts.
The off-gas management session included presentations on:
 • Technology development and demonstration
 • Salt loops and sensor/off-gas testing capabilities
 • Bubble behavior in molten salts
 • Actinide salt loops and sensor development
 • Molecular approaches to online monitoring
 • Laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS) for elemental monitoring
 • Radiation exposure measurements of metal-organic frameworks
 • Fission gas and species transport capabilities in SAM
 • Tritium transport
During lunch, Dr. Jim Wishart from Brookhaven National Laboratory provided an overview of the
Energy Frontier Research Center on molten salts in extreme environments. Toni Karlsson also
presented an update on irradiation of UCl3 /NaCl, which is now supported by the Advanced Fuels
Campaign.
The afternoon NEAMS session emphasized modeling and simulation of MSR-relevant materials,
chemistry, thermal fluids, and multiphysics applications. Presentations addressed:
 • Structural materials and chemistry
 • Atomistic simulations for molten salt thermophysical properties
 • Reactive multiphase flow modeling with MOSCATO
 • Phase-field modeling of corrosion in alloys
 • Thermal fluids tools including SAM and Pronghorn

 14

 • Multiphysics simulations using Saline, Pronghorn, SAM, and Griffin
 • Xenon transport simulation and validation using MSRE data
 • Noble metal transport in heat exchangers
A key theme of the day was the importance of integrating modeling and experiment. Participants
noted that atomistic simulations are valuable for addressing gaps in thermophysical property data,
but confidence in such models requires validation against experiment. The interactions between
NEAMS modelers and AMMT/materials researchers were highlighted as particularly beneficial.
Corrosion products in NaCl − UCl3 were noted to exhibit behavior strongly governed by redox and
coordination chemistry.
The day concluded with a discussion session on FY2026/FY2027 collaborations between ART-
MSR and NEAMS.

2.3. Day 3: Safety, Safeguards, Security, and Materials

The third day focused on safety and accident scenarios, safeguards and security, and advanced
materials.
The morning included presentations on:
 • Technology-based licensing and MSR safety
 • Mechanistic source term development
 • MSR accident testing activities at Argonne
 • Spectroscopy for actinide accountancy
 • Voltammetry for actinide accountancy
 • MSR safeguards approaches
 • Physical security of MSRs
 • Material control and accountability for MSR fuel fabrication facilities
An interactive off-gas roadmap exercise was conducted online and moderated by Hunter Andrews
(ORNL). Discussion points from the off-gas session included:
 • Some developers are not currently incorporating off-gas management into their designs
 because systems are sealed and design choices are constrained by regulatory considerations.
 • Major knowledge gaps remain in gas-phase transport phenomena, radionuclide speciation,
 aerosol formation and deposition, bubble transport dynamics, liquid-gas interface behavior,
 and thermal management.
 • Capture technologies include traditional carbon beds and cryogenic systems as well as emerg-
 ing approaches such as metal-organic frameworks.

 15

 • Diagnostics and instrumentation for real-time, spatially resolved monitoring are a major need.
 • Industry values reliability, simplicity, modularity, and commercially available components.
 • Research reactors can play an important role through heavy instrumentation and sampling at
 multiple points.
 • Data sharing is limited by proprietary concerns, making national laboratory partnerships
 especially important.
A vision was articulated for a nationally accessible, validated capability to study, measure, and
improve MSR off-gas transport, capture, and monitoring, described as the Integrated Off-Gas
Research and Analysis Laboratory (INTEGRAL).
The afternoon focused on AMMT-related materials topics, including:
 • Program overview
 • Role of alloy chemistry and microstructure in corrosion and mechanical performance
 • Challenges for additively manufactured materials in molten salt corrosion
 • Corrosion kinetics in molten chloride environments
 • Chloride salt flow loop and corrosion studies
 • Surveillance testing for advanced reactors
 • Materials surveillance technology development
 • Ni-based alloys for molten salt applications
Participants reported strong engagement and sustained discussion across the full three-day technical
program.

2.6. Technical Tours

2.6.1.        Overview

The fourth day of the review was dedicated to tours of Sandia National Laboratories and Kairos
Power facilities. Approximately 30 participants registered for the tours. Transportation was
provided by three vans moving attendees between sites and then returning them to hotels or the
airport.

2.6.2. Sandia National Laboratories Tour

The Sandia tour included visits relevant to molten salt research. Participants were divided into two
groups. A key stop was the National Solar Thermal Test Facility, which was widely regarded as
impressive in scale.
Participants visited the Molten Salt Test Loop (MSTL), which currently uses nitrate salts. MSTL
was developed to allow industrial customers and researchers to test molten salts across ranges of
temperature and flow rate for components and heated systems in order to test conditions represen-
tative of industrial conditions that may not appear at lab-scale. The facility is currently undergoing
restoration after an extended power outage in September 2017 resulted in salt freezing in the
pipes.
Sandia also has a smaller engineering-scale molten salt loop that is currently funded by an LDRD.
The loop has the capability to handle a diverse array of salt combinations that can be switched

 22

out on demand. The loop also has the ability to connect to a Brayton cycle supercritical CO2
(sCO2 ) unit. The memo attributes this effort to Dimitri Madden and Matt Christian as principal
investigators. Reported characteristics of the salt loop included:

 • Inconel 625 and stainless steel 316 construction

 • Operating temperatures of 530◦ C in the cold leg and 750◦ C in the hot leg

 • Flow rate of 5–10 gpm using an Inconel 625 pump

 • 3 kW electric heat tracing on pipes and a 5 kW sump tank heater

 • Pressure, flow, and electrochemical sensors

 • 11 bar operating pressure with N2 cover gas

 • Two Flowserve salt valves

 • Design for any salt combination

2.6.3. Tour of Kairos Power

Kairos Power discussed its Engineering Test Unit (ETU) program. Test Unit 1 had been dismantled
approximately one year earlier, and lessons learned were incorporated into Test Unit 2 at the
Albuquerque location. Test Unit 3 is planned for Tennessee, with some personnel are expected to
relocate from New Mexico to Tennessee to support that effort.

 24

Participants were not allowed to take photographs during the visit. A recurring theme during
the Kairos tour was the emphasis on in-house development and manufacturing. The company
has developed significant internal knowledge in graphite and has produced the reactor vessel
internally. According to the tour description, in-house fabrication and assembly of the graphite
reflector reduces the number of blocks and match-machining steps while ensuring conformity to
vessel dimensions. Kairos also reported increased in-house manufacturing for systems such as the
primary salt pump, chemistry control system, and pebble handling and storage system.
Kairos Power is also building a facility to synthesize FLiBe salt and to produce enriched 7 Li, with
operations to begin June 2029. The tour did not include the TRISO fuel production facility as the
HALEU TRISO fuel is produced at LANL.

2.7. Key Outcomes and Observations

The review brought together participants from a wide range of technical backgrounds who are all
engaged in aspects of MSR development. Several ideas and future opportunities emerged during
the meeting:
 • Use of the Actinide Salt Loop sponsored by ART-MSR to study materials issues relevant to
 AMMT and provide validation data for NEAMS models
 • Need for data on xenon/graphite interactions, an area already being modeled by NEAMS
 • Interest in the “MSR Evaluation Model Development Tool” presented by Mauricio Tano as
 an integrated DOE-code-enabled workflow for rapid, traceable safety evaluation of MSR
 concepts
 • Potential relevance of that workflow beyond MSRs, including possible applicability to GFR
 • Questions about whether materials efforts should include fluoride salts in addition to chloride
 salts
 • Continued and strong NRC engagement throughout the meeting
Overall, the review demonstrated the value of bringing together multiple DOE programs and
external stakeholders in one forum to discuss common technical challenges and shared infrastructure
needs.

2.8. Participant Feedback

A survey was distributed to participants following the meeting, and 28 people provided feedback.
Responses were strongly positive overall.
Common themes in participant feedback regarding what was most valuable included:
 • Opportunities to network and discuss future program direction
 • Exposure to a broad range of MSR-relevant work

 25

 • Learning beyond participants’ primary technical areas
 • Seeing complementary work across ART-MSR, AMMT, NEAMS, and safeguards
 • Roadmap discussions that clarified future priorities
 • Benefits of having all attendees hear the same talks and discussions
 • High-quality technical presentations and strong synergies across national laboratories
 • Positive experience with the Sandia and Kairos tours
The primary negative feedback concerned the length of the three-day technical program, with some
participants noting fatigue from sitting through long days. Another concern was limited breakout
discussion time and technical issues with internet connection on the first day.
Feedback on the three-day format indicated that most participants thought the duration was about
right, though a few considered it too long. Some indicated that having four shorter-length days
would have been better. Participants unanimously indicated that the format helped them learn
beyond their primary area of expertise, build their networks, and form new connections.
The Day 1 roadmap panel was viewed favorably and described as informative and effective in
setting the tone for the review. The interactive off-gas poll session was also positively received,
especially for hybrid engagement, though some respondents suggested clarifying the purpose of
the poll and how the results would be used.
Suggestions for future reviews included:
 • Continue to include talks spanning the full MSR ecosystem
 • Preserve the in-person component because of its added value
 • Consider reducing the number of contributions from some supporting programs and rotating
 them by year
 • Involve offices in NE4 more explicitly in cross-cutting discussions

3. CONCLUSIONS

The FY26 ART-MSR Campaign Review provided a valuable forum for bringing together the diverse
technical communities contributing to molten salt reactor development. The review demonstrated
the benefit of convening researchers, program leads, developers, and regulators in a single venue
to exchange results, compare needs, and identify areas where coordination across programs can
improve the impact of ongoing work.
Several themes emerged clearly from the review. First, progress in molten salt reactor technology
depends on close integration of experiment, modeling, materials research, safety analysis, and
safeguards development. The discussions throughout the meeting repeatedly showed that many of
the remaining technical challenges are cross-cutting in nature and cannot be addressed effectively
within a single program area alone. Second, the meeting highlighted the continuing importance of
high-quality validation data, shared experimental infrastructure, and coordinated planning across
the national laboratory complex. Third, the review reinforced that engagement with developers and
regulators is essential for ensuring that research priorities remain aligned with deployment-relevant
needs.
The expanded participation from complementary DOE-NE programs added significant value to the
FY26 review. By including contributions from NEAMS, AMMT, ARSS, MPACT, and advanced
fuels activities, the meeting created opportunities to connect related efforts that might otherwise
remain separated by program structure. This broader perspective helped identify technical overlaps,
validation opportunities, and potential future collaborations that can strengthen the overall MSR
research enterprise.
Participant feedback indicates that the review was successful in promoting technical exchange and
community building. Attendees particularly valued the opportunity to hear work outside their
immediate areas, engage in discussion with colleagues across institutions, and connect research
activities to broader programmatic needs. The strong participation in both the technical sessions
and tours also reflected substantial interest in maintaining this type of annual gathering.
Overall, the FY26 review showed a technically active and increasingly connected MSR community.
It provided useful input for future planning, identified opportunities for improved cross-program
coordination, and reaffirmed the value of an annual campaign review as both a technical and
strategic activity. Continued use of this format should help sustain momentum, improve integration
across related efforts, and support the advancement of molten salt reactor technologies in future
fiscal years.
