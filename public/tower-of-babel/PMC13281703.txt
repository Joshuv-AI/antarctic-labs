Adult TWIST2‐high B‐ALL confirms metabolic association but reveals molecular heterogeneity
Authors: Wencke Walter, Veronika Ecker, Wolfgang Kern, Anna Stengel
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the recent publication by Zeng et al. 1 describing a novel subtype of B‐cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B‐ALL) characterized by high TWIST2 expression. In the original report, TWIST2‐high B‐ALL was defined as a transcriptionally distinct subgroup identified through integrative analysis of gene expression data. Notably, this subtype was enriched for alterations affecting key metabolic enzymes, including somatic IDH1/2 mutations and a recurrent FH A273T germline variant with loss of heterozygosity. These alterations were mutually exclusive and suggested a convergent pathogenic mechanism involving disruption of the tricarboxylic acid cycle and epigenetic regulation. At the same time, TWIST2 overexpression was not linked to a single unifying genetic driver, and the authors proposed that this subgroup represents a genetically independent entity primarily defined by its transcriptional profile.

In this context, we sought to evaluate the presence and characteristics of TWIST2‐high cases within our own cohort of adult B‐ALL patients. 2 Estimated read counts for each gene were normalized applying Trimmed mean of M‐values (TMM) normalization method 3 and the resulting log2 counts per million (CPMs) were used.

We identified 7/660 cases (0.9%) with markedly elevated TWIST2 expression. Five of seven cases showed very high TWIST2 expression (median 5.07 log2 TMM‐normalized CPM), whereas the other two cases showed intermediate expression (2.23 and 1.79). No correlation was observed between TWIST2 expression and blast percentage. To assess the specificity of TWIST2 expression, we extended our analysis across a broader panel of 21 hematologic malignancies and 64 control samples (total: 4117 samples). Consistent with the published data, TWIST2 expression was generally low or absent across most disease entities. However, elevated expression (TMM‐log2CPM > 1.7) was observed not only in the identified B‐ALL cases but also in five cases of mantle cell lymphoma (MCL), three cases of atypical chronic myeloid leukemia (aCML), and one patient with myelodysplastic neoplasm (MDS) (Figure 1A). The majority of myeloid cases had a normal karyotype (3/4) and typical mutations associated with the myeloid lineage such as ASXL1, CBL, and SETBP1. All MCL cases showed a classical immunophenotype and a reciprocal translocation t(11;14) with additional alterations leading to a complex karyotype. Recurrent aberrations did not comprise TP53/17p or CDKN2A/9p deletions, which are typically observed in MCL and associated with adverse prognosis, 3 nor did they include lesions affecting TWIST2/2q37, thereby failing to account for the observed TWIST2 overexpression.

Given that cases from other entities also showed intermediate TWIST2 expression, and that the lower TWIST2 expression in 2/7 B‐ALL cases could not be explained by a lower blast percentage, we focused subsequent analyses on the five cases with very high TWIST2 expression. All of these were classified as B‐ALL, not otherwise specified, by ALLCatchR. 4 To further characterize these cases, we analyzed available data from whole‐genome sequencing (WGS), chromosome banding analysis, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), and immunophenotypic profiling. 2

Mutational profiling confirmed the enrichment of IDH1/2 mutations in B‐ALL cases with TWIST2 overexpression (3/5 cases, 60%; Figure 1B). In contrast to the original report, no FH alterations were detected in our cohort. The remaining two cases both carried co‐occurring KRAS and ATRX mutations, which are part of the landscape of signaling pathway mutations that lead to high‐risk, chemotherapy‐resistant B‐ALL. 5 , 6 , 7 In addition, somatic mutations in DDX41 (n = 1), CSF3R (n = 1), and BCOR (n = 2) were detected (Figure 1B), which are more commonly associated with myeloid malignancies. 8 These findings suggest that, while the link between TWIST2 overexpression and metabolic dysregulation is reproducible, the underlying genetic spectrum may be more heterogeneous than initially appreciated.

Cytogenetic analyses revealed diverse aberrations. However, similar to the original report, no common genetic driver could be identified. All cases carried at least one of the recurrent deletions typically observed in B‐ALL: two cases showed CDKN2A deletion with accompanying 9p copy‐neutral loss of heterozygosity (CN‐LOH), 2/5 harbored VPREB1 deletions, and 3/5 showed deletions involving chromosome 7 or 7p with concurrent IKZF1 deletions (Figure 1B). In addition, one case with KRAS and ATRX mutations also harbored a 17p CN‐LOH but without an accompanying TP53 mutation. These data underscore the heterogeneous genetic background and lack of a direct association with TWIST2 overexpression.

Immunophenotypically, all cases were consistent with common B‐ALL, showing expression of CD19, CD10, and the cytoplasmic markers CD22, CD79a, and TdT. However, CD34 expression was low or absent across the cohort, and the myeloid‐associated markers CD33 and CD65 were co‐expressed in all five cases. Of note, CD65 expression is more frequently observed in pro‐B‐ALL (typically CD10‐negative) and correlates with KMT2A‐rearrangements. 9 Thus, TWIST2‐high cases exhibit a specific aberrant c‐ALL immunophenotypic profile (CD10+CD19+CD34−CD33+CD65+), which aligns with the observed mutational spectrum.

Importantly, whereas the TWIST2‐high cases reported by Zeng et al. included patients across a wide age range with several younger adults and pediatric cases, our five cases were predominantly elderly (median age 72 years [26–79]). This distinction may be of biological and clinical relevance, as our data suggest potential differences in the mutational landscape, including the absence of FH alterations and the presence of KRAS and myeloid‐associated mutations. These observations may point toward age‐related differences in disease pathogenesis or clonal evolution within the TWIST2‐high subgroup.

Taken together, our findings independently validate the existence of a TWIST2‐high subtype of B‐ALL and further refine its molecular and immunophenotypic characteristics. While the association with IDH1/2 mutations supports a role for metabolic dysregulation, the absence of FH alterations and the presence of additional mutations highlight the heterogeneity of this subgroup.

In conclusion, our data extend the findings of the original study by demonstrating that TWIST2‐high B‐ALL also occurs in adults and is associated with a distinct yet heterogeneous molecular profile. Despite comprehensive genetic analyses, a unifying driver lesion remains elusive, raising the possibility that epigenetic dysregulation represents a key pathogenic mechanism in this subgroup. Continued investigation of this subgroup will therefore be essential to better understand its biological basis and to determine its relevance for future diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.
