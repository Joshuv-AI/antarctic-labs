We regret to announce the death on May 6, in his ninety-first year, of Academician George S. Golitsyn, a member of the Editorial Board who was among the leading scientists supporting the establishment of Carbon Balance and Management in 2006.

George Golitsyn was born in 1935 in Moscow and educated at Moscow State University, where he graduated with an MS in physics with distinction in 1958. From that time, he was affiliated with the A.M. Obukhov Institute of Atmospheric Physics of the Russian Academy of Sciences (formerly the Institute of Atmospheric Physics of the USSR Academy of Sciences). He became Director of this institute after Alexander M. Obukhov in 1990 and served as its Scientific Supervisor from 2009.

In 1973, Golitsyn published a book in which he proposed a general theory of circulation in planetary atmospheres, based on the consistent use of similarity principles and dimensional analysis together with thermodynamic considerations. The English translation of the book is available at the NASA archive [1]. In 1988, Golitsyn, in co-authorship with Mikhail I. Budyko and Yuri A. Izrael, published a book explaining the physical mechanisms of natural climatic catastrophes in the geological past and evaluating the risks of man-made climatic catastrophes in the future [2].

In recognition of Golitsyn’s leadership in atmospheric sciences and his research on the dynamics of the atmospheres of the Earth and other planets, the European Geosciences Union awarded him the Wegener Medal in 2005. In 2011, he was elected an Honorary Fellow of the Royal Meteorological Society, and the Russian Academy of Sciences awarded him the Lomonosov Gold Medal in 2019.

Golitsyn’s last book is addressed to scientists developing methods for studying spatio-temporal scales of natural environmental phenomena — that is, statistical relationships between the large and the small, the fast and the slow — and their alteration by human activity [3]. Those who were not fortunate enough to speak with this very talented, thoughtful, and benevolent man may still feel the charm of his personality when reading his books.
