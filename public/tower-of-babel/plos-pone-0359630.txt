RCTE: A multi-class object detection framework for dental panoramic radiographs

Jiayi Peng, Ji Liu, Yufeng Shen, Miao Yin, Changzheng Liu, Jie Zhou, Jia Liu, Ronghua Zhang

ABSTRACT
Background: Dental panoramic radiographs play an important role in oral disease screening, computer-aided diagnosis, and clinical treatment planning. However, accurate multi-class detection remains challenging due to small lesion sizes, ambiguous anatomical boundaries, insufficient multi-scale feature representation, and missed detections of complex dental structures. Objective: This study aims to develop an accurate and efficient multi-class object detection framework for dental panoramic radiographs to improve the detection performance of small lesions, weak-boundary structures, and complex anatomical targets. Methods: A novel re-parameterized cross-scale attention-enhanced framework, named RCTE, was proposed based on the YOLOv8n detector. The proposed framework integrates three complementary components: a Cross-Scale Channel Transformer (CSCT) module for cross-scale contextual interaction among P3, P4, and P5 features, a RepNCSPELAN4-based Re-parameterized Feature Pyramid Fusion (RPF) structure for enhancing multi-scale feature aggregation, and a Multi-Scale EMA (MS-EMA) mechanism for feature recalibration before the detection head. Experiments were conducted on a publicly available dental panoramic radiograph dataset containing 11 categories of dental structures and lesions. Model performance was evaluated using Precision, Recall, F1-score, mAP50, mAP75, and mAP50–95. Results: Compared with the original YOLOv8n baseline, RCTE improved mAP50, mAP75, mAP50–95, and Recall by 2.55, 3.88, 2.40, and 5.10 percentage points, respectively. The proposed framework achieved better detection completeness and localization accuracy compared with other YOLO-based detectors. Furthermore, RCTE maintained real-time inference capability, demonstrating a favorable balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency. Conclusion: The proposed RCTE framework effectively improves multi-class object detection performance in dental panoramic radiographs by enhancing cross-scale feature interaction, multi-scale feature fusion, and detection feature recalibration. This method provides a potential solution for computer-aided dental image analysis.

Introduction Dental panoramic radiographs are widely used in oral disease screening, clinical diagnosis, treatment planning, and postoperative evaluation. Compared with ordinary intraoral radiographs, panoramic radiographs provide a relatively complete view of the maxillofacial region, including teeth, mandible, maxilla, mandibular canal, maxillary sinus, implants, crowns, and other anatomical or pathological structures. Therefore, accurate interpretation of dental panoramic radiographs is important for assisting dentists in identifying dental diseases and structural abnormalities. However, manual interpretation is time-consuming and highly dependent on clinical experience. Previous studies have also emphasized that panoramic X-ray interpretation is prone to error and that artificial intelligence has the potential to improve dental diagnosis and treatment planning [ 1 ]. In addition, the visual appearance of different dental categories may be similar, and some lesions may have low contrast or ambiguous boundaries, which increases the difficulty of accurate diagnosis. With the development of deep learning, object detection methods have been increasingly applied to medical image analysis and dental radiograph interpretation. In dental panoramic radiographs, deep learning-based detectors can automatically locate and classify multiple dental structures or lesions, such as caries, periapical lesions, missing teeth, root canal treatment, implants, mandibular canals, and maxillary sinuses. For example, deep learning object detection techniques have been used for detecting and diagnosing maxillary sinus lesions on panoramic radiographs [ 2 ], detecting and classifying radiolucent mandibular lesions [ 3 ], and detecting teeth on pediatric panoramic radiographs using YOLO-v5-based models [ 4 ]. In addition, the DENTEX benchmark further promotes abnormal tooth detection with dental enumeration and diagnosis on panoramic X-rays [ 1 ]. These studies indicate that deep learning-based detection methods provide an effective technical basis for computer-aided dental diagnosis. Nevertheless, dental panoramic radiograph detection remains a challenging task. First, the scale difference among dental targets is significant. Some categories, such as caries and periapical lesions, usually occupy small local regions, while maxillary sinuses and mandibular canals may cover larger and more elongated areas. Second, many dental structures have blurred boundaries and low contrast with surrounding tissues, making accurate localization difficult. Third, anatomical structures often overlap in panoramic radiographs, which may lead to feature confusion and missed detections. Similar challenges have been observed in previous studies on automatic detection of dental lesions and anatomical structures in panoramic radiographs, where irregular shapes, low contrast, complex surrounding structures, and limited data may affect detection and localization performance [ 2 , 3 , 5 ]. These characteristics require detection models to have stronger multi-scale feature representation, contextual modeling, and feature discrimination capabilities. YOLO-based detectors have been widely used in real-time object detection because of their simple structure, high inference speed, and favorable accuracy-efficiency balance. YOLOv8 further improves detection performance through an efficient backbone, feature pyramid structure, and anchor-free detection head [ 6 – 8 ]. Multi-scale feature fusion is essential for object detection, and Feature Pyramid Networks (FPNs) have shown that hierarchical convolutional features can be effectively used to construct high-level semantic feature maps at multiple scales [ 9 , 10 ]. However, when directly applied to dental panoramic radiographs, the original YOLOv8n still has several limitations. The conventional feature fusion structure may not sufficiently aggregate shallow spatial details and deep semantic information, which limits the detection of small lesions and weak-boundary targets. Moreover, the interactions among different feature scales are mainly achieved through top-down and bottom-up feature fusion, while explicit contextual dependencies among P 3 , P 4 , and P 5 features are insufficiently modeled. As a result, the detector may fail to fully capture the global anatomical relationships in dental radiographs, especially for complex structures such as mandibular canals and maxillary sinuses. This study proposes a novel re-parameterized cross-scale attention-enhanced framework named RCTE for multi-class object detection in dental panoramic radiographs. Built upon the YOLOv8n framework [ 6 ], RCTE improves the detector from three complementary perspectives: cross-scale contextual interaction, re-parameterized multi-scale feature fusion, and multi-scale feature recalibration. These designs aim to improve the detection of small lesions, elongated anatomical structures, and weak-boundary dental targets in panoramic radiographs. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: A re-parameterized cross-scale attention-enhanced detection network, named RCTE, is proposed for multi-class object detection in dental panoramic radiographs. A Cross-Scale Channel Transformer (CSCT) module is introduced to jointly model the P 3 , P 4 , and P 5 feature maps, enhancing contextual information exchange across different feature levels. A RepNCSPELAN4-based Re-parameterized Feature Pyramid Fusion (RPF) structure is adopted to replace the conventional neck fusion units, strengthening multi-scale feature aggregation and information flow between shallow spatial details and deep semantic features. A Multi-Scale EMA (MS-EMA) feature recalibration mechanism is embedded before the detection head to enhance the model’s attention to low-contrast lesions and boundary-ambiguous regions at different detection scales. Experimental results show that RCTE improves mAP50, mAP75, mAP50–95, and Recall by 2.55, 3.88, 2.40, and 5.10 percentage points, respectively, compared with the original YOLOv8n, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method. Related work Deep learning for dental panoramic radiograph analysis Dental panoramic radiographs have been widely studied in computer-aided dental diagnosis because they provide a global view of teeth, jaws, and surrounding anatomical structures. In recent years, deep learning methods have shown strong potential in automatically detecting and localizing dental structures and pathological findings. Tuzoff et al. proposed a convolutional neural network-based method for tooth detection and numbering in panoramic radiographs, demonstrating the feasibility of automated dental charting from panoramic images [ 11 ]. Lee et al. evaluated a deep learning-based convolutional neural network for detecting and diagnosing dental caries on periapical radiographs [ 12 ]. Lee et al. further developed a deep learning model for early dental caries detection on bitewing radiographs and showed that CNN-based systems can support clinicians in caries diagnosis [ 13 ]. Mohammad-Rahimi et al. systematically reviewed deep learning methods for caries detection and reported the growing potential of deep learning in dental radiographic diagnosis [ 14 ]. Although these studies demonstrate the effectiveness of deep learning in dental radiograph analysis, accurate multi-class detection in panoramic radiographs remains challenging. Dental targets often vary greatly in scale and shape. Small lesions, such as caries and periapical lesions, usually occupy limited local regions, whereas mandibular canals and maxillary sinuses may appear as larger and elongated anatomical structures. Moreover, low contrast, blurred boundaries, and structural overlap may lead to missed detections and inaccurate localization. Therefore, a detector for dental panoramic radiographs should not only preserve fine spatial details but also model contextual relationships across different feature levels. Multi-scale feature fusion and cross-scale context modeling Multi-scale feature representation is essential for object detection because objects may appear at different sizes and resolutions. Deep residual networks show that shortcut connections can improve gradient propagation and enable deeper feature learning [ 15 ]. Path Aggregation Network (PANet) further enhances information flow by adding a bottom-up path augmentation structure, indicating the importance of strengthening the propagation of localization information from lower layers to higher layers [ 16 ]. EfficientDet introduces a weighted bidirectional feature pyramid network (BiFPN) for efficient multi-scale feature fusion, showing that adaptive and bidirectional feature aggregation can improve detection efficiency and accuracy [ 17 ]. However, conventional feature fusion structures mainly rely on upsampling, downsampling, concatenation, and convolutional fusion. These operations can transfer information across feature levels, but they may be insufficient to explicitly model cross-scale contextual dependencies. For dental panoramic radiographs, this limitation is more evident because local lesions and global anatomical structures may be semantically related. For example, detecting a small lesion may require both local boundary details and surrounding anatomical context. Transformer-based models demonstrate that attention mechanisms can capture long-range dependencies and global contextual relationships [ 18 ]. Inspired by this idea, enhancing contextual interaction among different feature levels is meaningful for improving the detection of small lesions and complex anatomical structures in panoramic radiographs. Attention-based feature recalibration Attention mechanisms have been widely introduced into convolutional neural networks to enhance feature discrimination. Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks (SENet) explicitly model channel-wise dependencies and adaptively recalibrate feature responses, providing a representative example of channel attention for feature enhancement [ 19 ]. Coordinate Attention further embeds positional information into channel attention by separately aggregating features along horizontal and vertical directions, which helps capture long-range dependencies while preserving location-sensitive information [ 20 ]. For dental panoramic radiographs, attention-based recalibration is particularly useful because many clinically important targets have low contrast, ambiguous boundaries, and irregular shapes. However, applying attention to only a single feature level may be insufficient. Different dental categories rely on different detection scales: small lesions require high-resolution spatial details, while larger anatomical structures require stronger semantic information. Therefore, multi-scale feature recalibration before the detection head can help improve the discriminative ability of fused detection features at different levels. Based on the above observations, this study proposes RCTE by combining cross-scale contextual interaction, re-parameterized feature pyramid fusion, and multi-scale EMA-based feature recalibration. Compared with directly applying a general YOLOv8n detector, the proposed design aims to better address scale variation, weak boundaries, and feature confusion in multi-class dental panoramic radiograph detection. Materials and methods Overall architecture of RCTE To improve multi-class object detection in dental panoramic radiographs, this study proposes RCTE, a re-parameterized cross-scale attention-enhanced detection network. The proposed model is developed based on the YOLOv8n framework and introduces three main improvements: a Cross-Scale Channel Transformer (CSCT) module, a RepNCSPELAN4-based Re-parameterized Feature Pyramid Fusion (RPF) structure, and a Multi-Scale EMA (MS-EMA) feature recalibration mechanism. The overall architecture of RCTE is shown in Fig 1 . 10.1371/journal.pone.0359630.g001 Fig 1 Overall architecture of RCTE. The proposed network consists of the YOLOv8n backbone, the CSCT module for cross-scale contextual interaction, the RPF structure for re-parameterized feature pyramid fusion, the MS-EMA module for multi-scale feature recalibration, and the original YOLOv8 detection head. As shown in Fig 1 , the proposed RCTE follows a progressive feature enhancement pipeline. The input dental panoramic radiograph is first processed by the YOLOv8n backbone to extract hierarchical features. Feature maps from layers 4, 6, and 9 are selected as P 3 , P 4 , and P 5 , respectively. These multi-scale features are first fed into the CSCT module for cross-scale contextual interaction. Then, the enhanced features are separated by feature indexing and passed to the RPF structure for bidirectional feature fusion. Finally, MS-EMA modules are applied to the fused features before the original YOLOv8 detection head. Cross-scale channel transformer module A Cross-Scale Channel Transformer (CSCT) module is introduced after the backbone to enhance contextual interaction among multi-scale features, as shown in Fig 2 . In the proposed network, the outputs corresponding to P 3 , P 4 , and P 5 are simultaneously fed into the CSCT module. For the YOLOv8n setting, the channel dimensions of these three feature maps are 64, 128, and 256, respectively. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359630.g002 Fig 2 Structure of the proposed Cross-Scale Channel Transformer module. The CSCT module jointly models P 3 , P 4 , and P 5 features to enhance contextual information exchange across different feature levels. The CSCT module jointly models the multi-scale feature maps as follows: { P 3 ′ , P 4 ′ , P 5 ′ } = 𝒯 ( P 3 , P 4 , P 5 ) , (1) where P 3 ′ , P 4 ′ , and P 5 ′ denote the enhanced features after cross-scale interaction, and 𝒯 ( · ) denotes the CSCT operation. In this module, each input feature map is first transformed into an embedded representation. Positional information is then added to preserve spatial structure. The embedded features from different scales are jointly processed through channel-wise Transformer interaction, allowing information exchange among shallow detailed features, middle-level structural features, and deep semantic features. After Transformer-based interaction, the output tokens are reconstructed into feature maps and added to the corresponding input features. This residual reconstruction preserves the original feature representation while enhancing contextual information. Since the CSCT module outputs multiple enhanced feature maps, a feature indexing operation is used to separate P 3 ′ , P 4 ′ , and P 5 ′ . This operation only serves as an implementation-level feature extraction step and does not introduce additional complex computation. Through CSCT, the contextual relationship among different scales is strengthened before feature pyramid fusion. This is beneficial for dental panoramic radiographs, where small lesions and large anatomical structures often coexist and require multi-scale contextual understanding. RepNCSPELAN4-based re-parameterized feature pyramid fusion A RepNCSPELAN4-based Re-parameterized Feature Pyramid Fusion (RPF) structure is adopted to improve multi-scale feature aggregation in the neck. In the original YOLOv8n neck, C2f-based fusion units are used for feature fusion. In RCTE, these conventional fusion units are replaced by RepNCSPELAN4 modules to enhance feature reuse and strengthen the information flow between shallow spatial details and deep semantic features. Compared with conventional single-path fusion units, the RepNCSPELAN4 module facilitates more effective aggregation of shallow and deep features, which is beneficial for representing dental targets with complex shapes and ambiguous boundaries. In the proposed RPF structure, RepNCSPELAN4 modules are deployed in both the top-down and bottom-up fusion paths of the neck. In the top-down path, high-level semantic features are progressively transmitted to lower-level features to enhance semantic representation. In the bottom-up path, localization-related information is further reinforced by aggregating lower-level spatial details into higher-level features. As a result, shallow spatial details and deep semantic information are more effectively integrated, thereby improving the representation of small lesions, elongated anatomical structures, and weak-boundary dental targets in panoramic radiographs. Multi-scale EMA feature recalibration Although the RPF structure strengthens multi-scale feature fusion, the fused features may still contain redundant background responses and weak target activations. In dental panoramic radiographs, low-contrast lesions and ambiguous anatomical boundaries are easily affected by surrounding tissues, which may lead to missed detections or inaccurate localization. To further enhance the discriminative ability of the detection features, a Multi-Scale EMA (MS-EMA) feature recalibration mechanism is embedded before the detection head. The internal structure of the EMA block and its deployment on the three detection scales are shown in Fig 4 . 10.1371/journal.pone.0359630.g004 Fig 4 Structure of the proposed Multi-Scale EMA feature recalibration module. (a) Internal structure of the EMA block, which includes group partition, direction-aware attention, local convolution, and normalization-based recalibration. (b) Deployment of EMA blocks on the fused multi-scale features F 3 , F 4 , and F 5 before the YOLOv8 detection head. In the proposed RCTE, EMA modules are applied to the fused feature maps at three detection scales. Given the fused multi-scale features F 3 , F 4 , and F 5 , the recalibrated features can be expressed as: F ^ i = ℰ ( F i ) , i ∈ { 3 , 4 , 5 } , (7) where ℰ ( · ) denotes the EMA operation, F i denotes the fused feature at the corresponding scale, and F ^ i represents the recalibrated feature after attention enhancement. As shown in Fig 4(a) , the EMA block first divides the input feature into several groups along the channel dimension. This grouped feature representation reduces computational complexity while preserving local channel information. For each grouped feature, adaptive average pooling is performed along the horizontal and vertical directions to capture direction-aware contextual information. The horizontal and vertical descriptors are concatenated and processed by a 1 × 1 convolution to generate spatial attention weights. In this way, the module can capture long-range dependencies along different spatial directions and enhance the responses of informative regions. Meanwhile, a local convolution branch is used to preserve local spatial representation. Specifically, a 3 × 3 convolution is applied to the grouped feature to extract local contextual information. In addition, group normalization is used to stabilize the feature distribution during recalibration. The final output of the EMA module is obtained by combining the spatially enhanced feature and the normalized recalibrated feature: F ^ = F s 1 + F s 2 , (8) where F s 1 denotes the spatially enhanced feature generated by direction-aware attention and local convolution, and F s 2 denotes the recalibrated feature generated from normalized group features and global contextual weights. Different dental targets rely on different detection scales. Small lesions such as caries and periapical lesions depend more on high-resolution features, while larger anatomical structures such as mandibular canals and maxillary sinuses require stronger semantic features. Therefore, applying EMA to only one feature scale may be insufficient for dental panoramic radiograph detection. As shown in Fig 4(b) , EMA modules are applied to F 3 , F 4 , and F 5 simultaneously, forming a multi-scale feature recalibration strategy. This design enables the model to enhance low-contrast lesions, weak boundaries, and diagnostically important anatomical structures at different scales. Implementation details RCTE-Net was implemented based on the YOLOv8n framework with an input image size of 640 × 640 pixels. In the model configuration, the feature maps from layers 4, 6, and 9 were selected as P 3 , P 4 , and P 5 , respectively, and were fed into the CSCT module for cross-scale contextual interaction. The enhanced multi-scale features were then separated by feature indexing and passed to the RPF structure for bidirectional feature fusion. After feature fusion, MS-EMA modules were applied to the fused detection features at three scales, and the recalibrated features were sent to the original YOLOv8 detection head for prediction. The original detection head and training objective were retained, while the main improvements of RCTE focused on cross-scale feature interaction, re-parameterized feature fusion, and multi-scale feature recalibration. Dataset The experiments were conducted on the Tooth Filling Detection dataset, a publicly available third-party dental panoramic radiograph dataset released on Roboflow. The dataset is available at https://universe.roboflow.com/pidray-8gl3p/tooth-filling-detection/dataset/2 . The dataset consists of de-identified dental panoramic radiographs with multiple annotated dental structures and lesions. The authors did not participate in data collection and did not have access to any personally identifiable patient information. The dataset contains 11 categories, including Filling, Crown, Root Canal Treatment, Periapical lesion, Missing teeth, Root Piece, Caries, Impacted tooth, Implant, Mandibular Canal, and Maxillary sinus. All annotations were converted into YOLO format for object detection. In this study, the dataset was divided into training, validation, and test sets with a ratio of 8:1:1. Specifically, 6498 images were used for model training, 813 images were used for validation, and 813 images were used for final testing. The training set was used for model optimization, the validation set was used for monitoring model performance during training, and the test set was used for final performance evaluation. The same dataset split was used for all compared models to ensure a fair comparison. The accuracy-related metrics include Precision (P), Recall (R), F1-score, mAP50, mAP75, and mAP50–95. The efficiency-related metrics include GFLOPs, the number of parameters, preprocessing time, inference time, postprocessing time, FPS, and model file size. Precision is used to measure the proportion of correctly predicted positive samples among all predicted positive samples, and Recall is used to measure the proportion of correctly detected targets among all ground-truth targets. They are defined as follows: P = T P T P + F P , (9) R = T P T P + F N , (10) where TP , FP , and FN denote true positives, false positives, and false negatives, respectively. The F1-score is the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall, which reflects the balance between false detections and missed detections: F 1 = 2 P R P + R . (11) The mean average precision (mAP) was used as the main metric for evaluating detection accuracy. In this study, mAP50, mAP75, and mAP50–95 were reported. The mAP50 represents the mean average precision at an IoU threshold of 0.50, while mAP75 represents the mean average precision at a stricter IoU threshold of 0.75. The mAP50–95 calculates the average mAP over multiple IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95 with a step size of 0.05: m A P 50 -- 95 = 1 10 ∑ t = 0.50 0.95 A P t , (12) where A P t denotes the average precision at the IoU threshold t . Compared with mAP50, mAP75 and mAP50–95 provide a stricter evalua t ion of localization accuracy, which is important for dental panoramic radiograph detection because many targets have small sizes, blurred boundaries, and irregular shapes. In addition to detection accuracy, computational efficiency was also evaluated. GFLOPs were used to measure the theoretical computational complexity of the model, and the number of parameters was used to measure the model scale. The model file size was recorded to evaluate the storage requirement of each model. For inference efficiency, three time-related metrics were reported: preprocessing time, inference time, and postprocessing time per image. The preprocessing time refers to the time required for image resizing and normalization before model inference. The inference time refers to the forward propagation time of the model. The postprocessing time refers to the time required for operations such as confidence filtering and non-maximum suppression. The inference speed was measured using two FPS metrics. The first one is the overall FPS, which considers preprocessing, model inference, and postprocessing: F P S a l l = 1 T p r e + T i n f e r + T p o s t , (13) where T p r e , T i n f e r , and T p o s t denote the preprocessing time, inference time, and postprocessing time for one image, respectively. The second one is the pure inference FPS, which only considers the model inference time: F P S i n f e r = 1 T i n f e r . (14) For the dental panoramic radiograph detection task, mAP50–95, Recall, mAP75, and F1-score were regarded as the most important indicators. The mAP50–95 reflects the overall detection and localization performance under multiple IoU thresholds. Recall is particularly important because missed detections may affect subsequent clinical interpretation. The mAP75 reflects the localization ability under a stricter IoU threshold, and the F1-score evaluates the balance between Precision and Recall. Precision, FPS, parameters, GFLOPs, and model file size were used as auxiliary indicators to analyze false detections and computational cost. Therefore, RCTE provides a favorable trade-off between detection accuracy and computational efficiency for multi-class object detection in dental panoramic radiographs. Discussion The experimental results demonstrate that RCTE achieves superior overall detection performance compared with representative YOLO-based detectors on the dental panoramic radiograph dataset. Compared with the original YOLOv8n baseline, RCTE improves mAP50, mAP75, mAP50–95, and Recall by 2.55, 3.88, 2.40, and 5.10 percentage points, respectively. These improvements indicate that the proposed framework enhances both detection completeness and localization accuracy for multi-class dental object detection. Recent studies have also demonstrated the potential of artificial intelligence-based methods for automated assessment of multiple dental findings from panoramic radiographs, supporting the clinical relevance of automated dental image analysis [ 21 ]. The improved performance of RCTE can be attributed to the complementary effects of cross-scale contextual modeling, multi-scale feature fusion, and feature recalibration. The ablation experiments demonstrate that each proposed component contributes to performance improvement, while their combination achieves the best overall detection capability. In particular, the enhanced localization performance indicates that effective feature representation and multi-scale information aggregation are beneficial for identifying dental targets with different scales and complex anatomical backgrounds. Similar challenges have been reported in medical image analysis, where variations in object size, unclear boundaries, and complex surrounding structures remain important factors affecting detection accuracy [ 22 ]. The category-level analysis shows that RCTE performs well on relatively distinctive structures, including Impacted tooth, Implant, and Crown. However, Caries and Periapical lesion remain challenging categories due to their small size, low contrast, irregular appearance, and similarity to surrounding tissues. Previous studies on dental lesion detection have also reported that these categories are difficult to identify accurately on panoramic radiographs because of limited visual information and complex anatomical contexts [ 23 , 24 ]. In addition, differences in category distribution and annotation difficulty may further influence the detection performance of challenging classes. Future improvements may explore adaptive sampling strategies and loss optimization methods, such as focal loss, to further enhance the recognition capability of difficult categories [ 25 ]. Compared with other YOLO-based detectors, RCTE achieves better overall detection performance, especially in Recall and mAP-based metrics. For dental panoramic radiograph analysis, reducing missed detections is particularly important because undetected lesions or anatomical abnormalities may affect subsequent clinical assessment. Although RCTE introduces additional computational overhead, it maintains real-time inference capability with an inference speed of 202.34 FPS. This demonstrates a reasonable balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency, which is consistent with recent studies focusing on lightweight and efficient dental detection frameworks [ 26 , 27 ]. Despite the promising results, several limitations remain. First, although the dataset contains multiple dental categories, further external validation using larger multi-center datasets is required to evaluate the generalization capability of RCTE. Second, the current framework mainly focuses on two-dimensional panoramic radiographs, while some anatomical structures, such as the mandibular canal, may benefit from complementary three-dimensional information in complex clinical scenarios [ 28 ]. Third, more comprehensive clinical evaluation and deployment studies are required before practical application. Future work will focus on improving the detection of small lesions, validating the model on more diverse datasets, and optimizing the framework for practical clinical deployment. Conclusions This study proposed RCTE, a re-parameterized cross-scale attention-enhanced framework for multi-class object detection in dental panoramic radiographs. By integrating cross-scale contextual interaction, re-parameterized feature fusion, and multi-scale feature recalibration, RCTE improves the representation capability of complex dental targets. Experimental results demonstrate that RCTE achieves superior detection performance compared with YOLOv8n and other YOLO-based baselines, particularly in terms of recall and localization accuracy. The proposed framework provides an effective approach for computer-aided dental image analysis. However, consistent with the broader challenges of artificial intelligence in medical imaging, further improvements require larger datasets, external validation, transparent reporting, and clinical deployment assessment [ 29 – 32 ]. Future studies will focus on improving the detection of small and low-contrast lesions, evaluating the model on larger multi-center datasets, and optimizing the framework for practical clinical applications.
