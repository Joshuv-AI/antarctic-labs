Abstract
. The main aim of this paper is to prove the existence of certain proper weakly r r -harmonic ( E ​ S − r ES-r -harmonic) maps. We construct critical points which belong to a family of rotationally symmetric maps φ a : B n → 𝕊 n \varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n} , where B n B^{n} and 𝕊 n {\mathbb{S}}^{n} denote the Euclidean n n -dimensional unit ball and sphere respectively. We find that the existence of solutions within this family is restricted to specific dimensions n n . Next, we prove that our critical points are unstable . In the course of this analysis we point out some specific differences between the r r -harmonic and the E ​ S − r ES-r -harmonic cases when r ≥ 4 r\geq 4 . Next, we analyse two variants of the problem. First, we replace the target manifold 𝕊 n {\mathbb{S}}^{n} with a rotationally symmetric ellipsoid E n ​ ( b ) E^{n}(b) and establish the existence of proper weakly biharmonic maps for all n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 , as well as proper weakly triharmonic maps for all n ≥ 7 n\geq 7 . Finally, we study a similar problem replacing the domain B n B^{n} with a suitable warped product manifold.

1.
Introduction

Harmonic maps
are the critical points of the
energy
functional

(1.1)

E(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\,|d\varphi|^{2}\,dV\,,

where
\varphi:M\to N
is a smooth map between two Riemannian
manifolds
(M,g_{M})
and
(N,g_{N})
of dimension
m
and
n
respectively. The condition of harmonicity is equivalent to the fact that the map
\varphi
is a solution of the Euler-Lagrange equation associated to the energy functional (
1.1
), i.e.,

(1.2)

-d^{*}d\varphi={\trace}_{g_{{}_{M}}}\,\nabla d\varphi=0\,.

The left member of (
1.2
) is a vector field along the map
\varphi
or, equivalently, a section of the pull-back bundle
\varphi^{-1}\,(TN)
: it is called
tension field
and denoted
\tau(\varphi)
(we refer to
[
5
,
6
]
for background on harmonic maps).
Now, let us denote
\nabla^{M},\nabla^{N}
and
\nabla^{\varphi}
the induced connections on the bundles
TM,TN
and
\varphi^{-1}TN
respectively. The
rough Laplacian
on sections of
\varphi^{-1}TN
, denoted
\overline{\Delta}
, is defined by

\overline{\Delta}=d^{*}d=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\nabla^{\varphi}_{e_{i}}\nabla^{\varphi}_{e_{i}}-\nabla^{\varphi}_{\nabla^{M}_{e_{i}}e_{i}}\right)\,,

where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a local orthonormal frame field tangent to
M
.
A topic of growing interest deals with the study of the so-called
polyharmonic maps
. In this context, it is important to point out that, despite some confusion in the literature, there is a significant difference between
ES-r
-harmonic maps
and
r
-harmonic maps. This fact was studied in detail in
[
4
]
, a rather extensive paper to which we refer for notation and background.

As proposed in
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
,
ES-r
-harmonic maps
are the critical points of the
ES-r
-energy functional defined as follows:

(1.3)

E^{ES}_{r}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\,|(d^{*}+d)^{r}(\varphi)|^{2}\,dV\,.

In the case that
r=2
, the functional (
1.3
) is called bienergy and its critical points are the so-called
biharmonic maps
. There have been extensive studies on biharmonic maps (see
[
14
,
19
]
for an introduction to this topic).

In the literature, another higher order version of the energy functional, denoted
E_{r}(\varphi)
, has been studied in several papers (for instance, see
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
28
,
29
]
). If
r=2s
,
s\geq 1
, its definition is:

(1.4)

\begin{split}E_{2s}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\,\langle\,\overline{\Delta}^{s-1}\tau(\varphi),\,\overline{\Delta}^{s-1}\tau(\varphi)\,\rangle\,\,dV\,.\end{split}

In the case that
r=2s+1
,
s\geq 1
:

(1.5)

\begin{split}E_{2s+1}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\,\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\,\nabla^{\varphi}_{e_{j}}\,\overline{\Delta}^{s-1}\tau(\varphi),\,\nabla^{\varphi}_{e_{j}}\,\overline{\Delta}^{s-1}\tau(\varphi)\,\rangle\,\,dV\,.\end{split}

We say that a map
\varphi
is
r
-harmonic
if it is a critical point of the functional
E_{r}(\varphi)
defined in (
1.4
), (
1.5
).
In 1989 Wang
[
28
]
studied the first variational formula of the
r
-energy functionals
E_{r}(\varphi)
and obtained a complete description of the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equations. Moreover, the expression for their second variation was derived in
[
17
]
, where it was shown that a biharmonic map is not always
r
-harmonic (
r\geq 3
) and, more generally, that an
s
-harmonic map is not always
r
-harmonic (
s<r
). On the other hand, any harmonic map is trivially
r
-harmonic and
ES-r
-harmonic for all
r\geq 2
. Therefore, we say that an
r
-harmonic (
ES-r
-harmonic) map is
proper
if it is
not
harmonic.
The functionals
E^{ES}_{r}(\varphi)
and
E_{r}(\varphi)
coincide in the following cases:

(i)

r=2,3
;

(ii)

\dim M=1
;

(iii)

the Riemannian curvature tensor of
N
vanishes.

Although for
r>3
the critical points of the
ES-r
-energy and of the
r
-energy do not always coincide, the following criterion was established in
[
20
, p. 370]
:

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
r\geq 4
and
\varphi:(M,g_{M})\to(N,g_{N})
be a smooth map such that

d^{2}\overline{\Delta}^{i}\tau(\varphi)=0,\quad i=0,\dots,r-4.

Then,
\varphi
is
ES-r
-harmonic if and only if it is
r
-harmonic.

We also point out that, by contrast with the case of
E_{r}(\varphi)
, the explicit derivation of the Euler-Lagrange equation for the Eells-Sampson functionals
E^{ES}_{r}(\varphi)
seems, in general, a very complicated task. These difficulties are illustrated in
[
4
]
, where the Euler-Lagrange equation of the functional
E^{ES}_{4}(\varphi)
was computed, and differences and common features of
E^{ES}_{r}(\varphi)
and
E_{r}(\varphi)
were thoroughly discussed.

In this paper we first focus on a specific family of rotationally symmetric maps
\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, where
B^{n}
and
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
denote the Euclidean
n
-dimensional unit ball and sphere respectively. More precisely, we define:

(1.6)

\begin{array}[]{lcll}\varphi_{a}\,:&B^{n}&\to&{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}\\
&&&\\
&x&\mapsto&(\sin a\,{\frac{x}{\rho}},\,\cos a)\,,\end{array}

where
\rho=|x|
and
a
is a constant in the real interval
(0,\pi/2)
. Of course,
\varphi_{a}
is well-defined and smooth away from the origin
O
. In Lemma
3.1
we prove that maps
\varphi_{a}
as in (
1.6
) belong to the Sobolev space
W^{r,2}\left(B^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
if and only if
n\geq 2r+1
and so, in these cases, they provide natural candidates for weak critical points of energy functionals of order
r
.

We note that, if one sets
a=\pi/2
in (
1.6
), then he would obtain the well-known and widely studied
equator map
(see
[
9
,
13
]
). The equator map is
weakly harmonic
if
n\geq 3
, and so it is a stable,
not proper
, weakly
r
-harmonic and
ES-r
-harmonic map whenever
4\leq 2r+1\leq n
. Thus, since we are interested in
proper
solutions, we restrict our attention throughout the paper to the case
0<a<\pi/2
.

In the biharmonic case existence and stability of weak solutions of the type (
1.6
) was investigated in
[
8
]
, where we proved the following results:

Theorem 1.2
.

[
8
]
Let

\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be a map as in (
1.6
). Then
\varphi_{a}
is a proper weakly biharmonic map if and only if either

(i)

n=5
and
a=a_{2}=\pi/\penalty 3
; or

(ii)

n=6
and
a=(1/\penalty 2)\,\arccos(-4/\penalty 5)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
8
]
Let

\varphi_{a}
be one of the two proper weakly biharmonic maps of Theorem
1.2
. Then
\varphi_{a}
is unstable.

As for additional examples of weakly biharmonic maps from
B^{n}
to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the interested reader is referred to
[
3
]
. The above results of
[
8
]
were obtained by computing with respect to standard Cartesian coordinates on
B^{n}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. One of the main goals of the present paper is to extend these results to higher orders
r\geq 3
. In this context the required computations are much longer and we found it useful to perform part of them in an intrinsic way, using the identification of
B^{n}
and
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
with suitable models in the sense of
[
11
]
.

As a preliminary result we prove:

Proposition 1.4
.

Assume
r\geq 2
and
n\geq 2r+1
. Let
\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be a map as in (
1.6
). Then
\varphi_{a}
is weakly
r
-harmonic if and only if it is weakly
ES-r
-harmonic.

In the next result we determine explicitly the critical points of type
\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
as in (
1.6
). Due to the high computational complexity we restrict our attention to the cases
3\leq r\leq 5
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be a map as in (
1.6
). Then

(i)

\varphi_{a}
is a proper weakly
ES-3
-harmonic (
3
-harmonic) map if and only if

n=7\quad{\rm and}\quad a=a_{3}=\dfrac{1}{2}\arccos\left(\dfrac{1}{9}\left(2\,\sqrt{10}-11\right)\right)\,;

(ii)

\varphi_{a}
is a proper weakly
ES-4
-harmonic (
4
-harmonic) map if and only if

n=9\quad{\rm and}\quad a=a_{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}\arccos\left(\dfrac{1}{16}\left(\sqrt{105}-19\right)\right)\,;

(iii)

\varphi_{a}
is a proper weakly
ES-5
-harmonic (
5
-harmonic) map if and only if

n=11\quad{\rm and}\quad a=a_{5}=\dfrac{1}{2}\arccos\left(\dfrac{1}{25}\left(6\sqrt{6}-29\right)\right)\,.

In Proposition
1.4
we showed that any map
\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
as in (
1.6
) is
weakly
r
-harmonic if and only if it is weakly
ES-r
-harmonic. Despite this, if
r\geq 4
the functionals
E_{r}(\varphi)
and
E^{ES}_{r}(\varphi)
do not coincide. Therefore, in the study of the stability of these critical points we will have to take into account the differences between
E^{ES}_{r}(\varphi)
and
E_{r}(\varphi)
when
r=4,5
. But, as a matter of fact, our instability result is:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be any of the proper weak solutions given in Theorem
1.5
. Then
\varphi_{a}
is unstable for both
E_{r}(\varphi)
and
E^{ES}_{r}(\varphi)
.

Next, we replace the target manifold
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
with a rotationally symmetric ellipsoid

E^{n}(b)=\left\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}\colon|x|^{2}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}=1\right\}\,.

More precisely, let

(1.7)

\begin{array}[]{lcll}\varphi_{a}\,:&B^{n}&\to&E^{n}(b)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}\\
&&&\\
&x&\mapsto&(\sin a\,{\frac{x}{\rho}},b\,\cos a)\,,\end{array}

where again
\rho=|x|
and
0<a<\pi/2
. Our result in the biharmonic case is:

Theorem 1.7
.

Assume
n\geq 5
. Then there exists a proper weakly biharmonic map
\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to E^{n}(b)
of type (
1.7
) if and only if

(1.8)

0<b^{2}<\frac{(n-1)}{2(n-4)}\,.

Remark 1.8
.

We point out that the dimensional restrictions which appear in Theorem
1.2
do not apply for a suitable choice of
b>0
. Indeed, for any given
n\geq 5
, there exists
b>0
such that (
1.8
) is satisfied, but
b=1
, i.e., the spherical case, is admissible only when
n=5,6
.

As for the triharmonic case, we now prove:

Theorem 1.9
.

Assume
n\geq 7
. Then there exists a proper weakly triharmonic map
\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to E^{n}(b)
of type (
1.7
) if and only if

(1.9)

0<b^{2}<\frac{(n-1)}{4(n-6)}\,.

Remark 1.10
.

Again, comparing with Theorem
1.5
, we observe that for any given
n\geq 7
there exists
b>0
such that (
1.9
) is satisfied, but
b=1
, i.e., the spherical case, is admissible only when
n=7
.

2.
Preliminaries

First, we introduce the most convenient setting to study maps of the type (
1.6
). Let
(M,g_{M})
be an
m
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with boundary
\partial M
and
\varphi:M\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
. We consider the canonical embedding
i:{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and write
\varphi=\left(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n+1}\right)
for
i\circ\varphi
. We use the following notation:

(2.1)

\nabla\varphi=\left(\nabla\varphi_{1},\ldots,\nabla\varphi_{n+1}\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad\Delta\varphi=\left(\Delta\varphi_{1},\ldots,\Delta\varphi_{n+1}\right)\,,

where
\nabla
is the gradient on
(M,g_{M})
and so each entry of
\nabla\varphi
is an
m
-dimensional vector. The Laplacian
\Delta
acts on a function
u:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
as follows:

(2.2)

\Delta u=-\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g_{M}|}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\sqrt{|g_{M}|}\,(g_{M})^{ij}\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\right)

(note that in
[
8
,
27
]
the opposite sign convention for
\Delta
was adopted).
Next, let
p
denote a positive integer. In this context, we recall (see
[
1
,
12
]
) the definition of the following Sobolev spaces:

(2.3)

\begin{split}&W^{p,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)=\\
&\left\{\varphi\in W^{p,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\right)\,:\,\varphi(x)=\left(\varphi_{1}(x),\ldots,\varphi_{n+1}(x)\right)\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\,{\rm a.e.}\right\}\,.\end{split}

The energy functional (
1.1
) becomes

(2.4)

E(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\,|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dV

and its Euler-Lagrange equation (
1.2
) takes the form

(2.5)

\Delta\varphi-\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}\varphi=0\,.

Then a map
\varphi\in W^{1,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
is
weakly
harmonic if it is a critical point of (
2.4
) in
W^{1,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
, i.e., if it is a solution of (
2.5
) in the sense of distributions.

A typical class of weakly harmonic maps is that of
minimizers
for the energy functional. To explain this notion, let us recall that in general, if
\varphi_{0}\in W^{p,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
, then we can define

(2.6)

\begin{split}&W_{\varphi_{0}}^{p,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)=\\
&\left\{\varphi\in W^{p,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)\,:\,\left.\nabla^{k}\left(\varphi-\varphi_{0}\right)\right|_{\partial M}\equiv 0,\,0\leq k\leq p-1\right\}\,,\end{split}

where the boundary condition in (
2.6
) is understood in the sense of traces.

Now, we can say that
{\varphi_{0}}\in W^{1,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
is a minimizer for the energy functional if it satisfies

E(\varphi_{0})\leq E(\psi)\quad\forall\,\psi\in W_{\varphi_{0}}^{1,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)\,.

As for the bienergy functional (
1.3
), in our context its expression becomes (see
[
2
,
10
,
27
]
)

(2.7)

E_{2}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\,\left(\left|\Delta\varphi\right|^{2}\,-\,\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{4}\right)\,dV

and its Euler-Lagrange equation is given by

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\Delta^{2}\varphi+2\,{\rm div}\left(\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}\,\nabla\varphi\right)\\
&+\left(\left|\Delta\varphi\right|^{2}-\Delta\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}-2\,\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\Delta\varphi+2\,\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{4}\right)\,\varphi=0\,,\end{split}

where the divergence operator
{\rm div}
is applied to each component and
\cdot
denotes scalar product in the following sense:

\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\Delta\varphi=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\,\nabla\varphi_{j}\cdot\nabla\Delta\varphi_{j}\,.

Next, we say that a map
\varphi\in W^{2,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
is
weakly
biharmonic if it is a critical point of (
2.7
) in
W^{2,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
, i.e., if it is a solution of (
2.8
) in the sense of distributions.
Again, a typical class of weakly biharmonic maps is that of
minimizers
for the bienergy functional.
Indeed, we say that
\varphi_{0}\in W^{2,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
is a minimizer if it satisfies

E_{2}(\varphi_{0})\leq E_{2}(\psi)\quad\forall\,\psi\in W_{\varphi_{0}}^{2,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)\,.

An important step towards understanding whether a given weakly biharmonic map is a minimizer is the study of its
stability
. More precisely, let
\varphi_{0}\in W^{2,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
be a weakly biharmonic map and denote by
\varphi_{s}
(
s\geq 0
) a variation of
\varphi_{0}
through maps in
W_{\varphi_{0}}^{2,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
. We say that
\varphi
is
stable
if

(2.9)

\left.\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\,E_{2}\left(\varphi_{s}\right)\right|_{s=0}\,\geq\,0

for all such variations
\varphi_{s}
. In particular, if
\varphi_{0}
is
not
stable, then it cannot be a minimizer. Moreover, we point out that the notions of minimizer and stability extend to a critical point
\varphi_{0}
of the
r
-energy functionals
E_{r}(\varphi)
,
E_{r}^{ES}(\varphi)
simply by replacing
W^{2,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
and
W_{\varphi_{0}}^{2,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
by
W^{r,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
and
W_{\varphi_{0}}^{r,2}\left(M,{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
respectively,
r\geq 3
.

Formally, the Euler-Lagrange equations associated to the functionals
E_{r}(\varphi)
,
E_{r}^{ES}(\varphi)
are elliptic PDE systems of order
2r
and so we can say that any map
\varphi\in W^{r,2}\left(B^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
is a weak critical point if it is a solution of the Euler-Lagrange equation in the sense of distributions. Unfortunately, it is rather difficult to obtain an explicit version of (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) for these functionals. Therefore, in order to overcome this difficulty, we adopt an
intrinsic approach
which works because of the following key observation, which is an obvious consequence of the fact that
\varphi_{a}
is smooth away from the measure zero set
\{O\}
. We state it in the form of a proposition in order to make it easier to refer to it:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be a map as in (
1.6
). Then
\varphi_{a}
is a weak critical point of
E^{ES}_{r}(\varphi)
(
E_{r}(\varphi)
) if and only if
\varphi_{a}\in W^{r,2}\left(B^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
and
\varphi_{a}
is
ES-r
-harmonic (
r
-harmonic) on
B^{n}\setminus\{O\}
.

By way of summary, in order to prove Theorem
1.5
it will suffice to understand when
\varphi_{a}\in W^{r,2}\left(B^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
and, taking into account Proposition
1.4
, it is
r
-harmonic on
B^{n}\setminus\{O\}
. This analysis will be carried out in the next section.

3.
Proofs of Proposition
1.4
and Theorem
1.5

First, it is important to establish when a map
\varphi_{a}
as in (
1.6
) belongs to
W^{r,2}\left(B^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

\varphi_{a}:B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
be a map as in (
1.6
). Then
\varphi_{a}\in W^{r,2}\left(B^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
if and only if
n\geq 2r+1
.

Proof.

We observe that
\varphi_{a}\in W^{r,2}\left(B^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\right)
if and only if:

when
r
is even for all
1\leq k\leq\frac{r}{2}

\int_{B^{n}}\left|\nabla\left(\Delta^{k-1}\varphi_{a}\right)\right|^{2}\,dV<+\infty\quad{\rm and}\quad\int_{B^{n}}\left|\Delta^{k}\varphi_{a}\right|^{2}\,dV<+\infty\,;

when
r
is odd for all
0\leq k\leq\frac{r-1}{2}

\int_{B^{n}}\left|\Delta^{k}\varphi_{a}\right|^{2}\,dV<+\infty\quad{\rm and}\quad\int_{B^{n}}\left|\nabla\left(\Delta^{k}\varphi_{a}\right)\right|^{2}\,dV<+\infty\,,

where, as usual,
\Delta^{0}\varphi=\varphi
. We perform the computation using Lemma 3.1 of
[
8
]
and, for all
k\geq 0
, we obtain:

(3.1)

\begin{split}\int_{B^{n}}\,\left|\nabla\left(\Delta^{k}\varphi_{a}\right)\right|^{2}\,dV&=c_{1}(k,n)\,(\sin^{2}a)\,\int_{0}^{1}\,\rho^{n-4k-3}\,d\rho\,;\\
\int_{B^{n}}\,\left|\Delta^{k}\varphi_{a}\right|^{2}\,dV&=c_{2}(k,n)\,(\sin^{2}a)\,\int_{0}^{1}\,\rho^{n-4k-1}\,d\rho\,,\end{split}

where
c_{1}(k,n)
and
c_{2}(k,n)
are positive constants which depend on
k
and
n
. Now, the conclusion of the lemma follows easily from (
3.1
).
∎

Next, we have to study
r
-harmonicity and
ES-r
-harmonicity of
\varphi_{a}
on
B^{n}\backslash\{O\}
. It is convenient to introduce a more general family of rotationally symmetric maps.
To this purpose, let us first introduce a family of warped product manifolds which will be suitable for our purposes. We set

(3.2)

\left(M,g_{M}\right)=\left({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\times I,f^{2}(\rho)g_{{\mathbb{S}}}+d\rho^{2}\right),

where
g_{{\mathbb{S}}}
is the standard metric on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
,
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is an open interval and
f(\rho)
is a smooth function which is positive on
I
.

Remark 3.2
.

In some instances, it may be of interest to extend the analysis through the closure
\overline{I}
of
I
. By way of example, if
\overline{I}=[0,+\infty)
and

(3.3)

\begin{cases}f(0)=0\,,\quad f^{\prime}(0)=1;\\[6.0pt]
f^{(2k)}(0)=0\quad{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\,\,k\geq 1\,\,,\\
\end{cases}

then the manifold (
3.2
) becomes a

in the sense of Greene and Wu (see
[
11
,
26
]
). In particular, if
f(\rho)=\rho
(respectively,
f(\rho)=\sinh\rho
) it is isometric to the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(respectively, the hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
). In a similar spirit, if
\overline{I}=[0,\pi]
and
f(\rho)=\sin\rho
, then we have the Euclidean unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
.
We also consider the case that
M
has a boundary. For instance, if
f(\rho)=\rho
and
\overline{I}=[0,1]
, then we have the Euclidean unit ball
B^{n}
.

We point out that all the calculations and results of this section are valid on
I
. In particular, the study of regularity across the loci associated to
\partial I
(poles or boundary of
\left(M,g_{M}\right)
) needs a case by case analysis.

By way of summary, we refer to a manifold as in (
3.2
) as to a
rotationally symmetric manifold
and, to shorten notation, we often write
M_{f}
to denote it. The family of rotationally symmetric maps which we are interested in is the following:

(3.4)

\begin{array}[]{lrll}\varphi_{\alpha}\,:&\left({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\times I,f^{2}(\rho)g_{{\mathbb{S}}}+d\rho^{2}\right)&\to&\left({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\times I^{\prime},h^{2}(\alpha)g_{{\mathbb{S}}}+d\alpha^{2}\right)\\
&&&\\
&(w,\rho)&\mapsto&(w,\alpha(\rho))\,\,,\end{array}

where
\alpha(\rho)
is a smooth function on a real interval
I=(a,b)
with values in
I^{\prime}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
. To denote a rotationally symmetric map as in (
3.4
) we write
\varphi_{\alpha}:M_{f}\to M_{h}
or, if the context is clear, simply
\varphi_{\alpha}
.

For later use we recall that we locally work using the frame field

(3.5)

\left\{\dfrac{\partial}{\partial w_{1}},\dots,\dfrac{\partial}{\partial w_{n-1}},\dfrac{\partial}{\partial w_{n}}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\right\}\,,

where
\left(w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1},w_{n}=\rho\right)
are local coordinates on
M_{f}
with
\left(w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1}\right)

{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
Moreover, their associated Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
are described by the following table:

(3.6)

\begin{array}[]{lll}{\rm(i)}&{\rm If}\,1\leq i,j,k\leq n-1:&\Gamma^{k}_{ij}={}^{{\mathbb{S}}}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\\
{\rm(ii)}&{\rm If}\,1\leq i,j\leq n-1:&\Gamma^{n}_{ij}=\,-\,f(\rho)\,f^{\prime}(\rho)\,\,(g_{{\mathbb{S}}})_{ij}\\
{\rm(iii)}&{\rm If}\,1\leq i,j\leq n-1:&\Gamma^{j}_{in}=\frac{f^{\prime}(\rho)}{f(\rho)}\,\,\delta_{i}^{j}\\
{\rm(iv)}&{\rm If}\,1\leq j\leq n:&\Gamma^{j}_{nn}=0=\Gamma^{n}_{jn}\,\,,\\
\end{array}

where

and
(g_{{\mathbb{S}}})_{ij}
denote respectively the Christoffel symbols and the components of metric tensor of

with respect to the local coordinates

Theorem 3.3

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{2}&=\tau_{\alpha}\\

As for weakly

\displaystyle 5(n-1)^{3}x^{3}+2(n-1)^{2}(17n-138)x^{2}

\displaystyle+4(n-1)(27n^{2}-475n+1984)x+24(n-6)(n-8)(7n-79).

Remark 3.5

We have observed that the weak

of Theorems

f\,f^{\prime\prime}&=(2j^{*}+3)(2j^{*}+2)b_{2j^{*}+3}\,\rho^{2j^{*}+2}+\sum_{j=j^{*}}^{+\infty}d_{j}\,\rho^{2j+4}\,;\\
(f\,f^{\prime\prime})^{2}&=\sum_{j=2j^{*}}^{+\infty}e_{j}\,\rho^{2j+4}\,;\\
f^{\prime 2}&=1+2(2j^{*}+3)b_{2j^{*}+3}\,\rho^{2j^{*}+2}+\sum_{j=j^{*}}^{+\infty}f_{j}\,\rho^{2j+4}\,;\\
f^{\prime 4}&=1+4(2j^{*}+3)b_{2j^{*}+3}\,\rho^{2j^{*}+2}+\sum_{j=j^{*}}^{+\infty}g_{j}\,\rho^{2j+4}\,;\\
f^{2}f^{(3)}f^{\prime}&=(2j^{*}+3)(2j^{*}+2)(2j^{*}+1)b_{2j^{*}+3}\,\rho^{2j^{*}+2}+\sum_{j=j^{*}}^{+\infty}h_{j}\,\rho^{2j+4}\,;\\
f^{\prime 2}ff^{\prime\prime}&=(2j^{*}+3)(2j^{*}+2)b_{2j^{*}+3}\,\rho^{2j^{*}+2}+\sum_{j=j^{*}}^{+\infty}k_{j}\,\rho^{2j+4}\\
\end{split}

where again the explicit expressions of the coefficients
c_{j},\dots,k_{j}
play no role.
Next, using (
6.9
) into (
6.3
), we find that the lower order nonzero term in the power series is
\rho^{2j^{*}+2}
and its coefficient is

4\,b_{2j^{*}+3}(2j^{*}+3)\left(-6j^{*}{{}^{3}}-27j^{*}{{}^{2}}+\left(4\sqrt{10}+2\right)j^{*}+12(2+\sqrt{10})\right)\,.

Now, since the third order polynomial

-6j^{*}{{}^{3}}-27j^{*}{{}^{2}}+\left(4\sqrt{10}+2\right)j^{*}+12(2+\sqrt{10})

admits no integer root, we deduce that the vanishing of the coefficient of
\rho^{2j^{*}+2}
implies
b_{2j^{*}+3}=0
and so the proof is completed.
