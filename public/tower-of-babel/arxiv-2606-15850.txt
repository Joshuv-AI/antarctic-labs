A 0-1 Law for Multifractal Spectra via the HGDS Scale Derivative
J. Petkevičius

Abstract
We prove that the multifractal spectrum D ⁡ ( h , ω ) D(h,\omega) of a stochastic process is almost surely deterministic under a scale decorrelation condition on the HGDS scale derivative ð Λ ​ X \eth_{\Lambda}X . The key difficulty is that the pointwise Hölder exponent lives in the germ σ \sigma -algebra, where classical 0-1 laws do not reach. We get around this by working with the geometry accumulation integral G Λ G_{\Lambda} , which is a genuine Lebesgue integral over scales and concentrates almost surely. The boundary case — log-correlated fields — is sharp: the variance summability condition fails exactly there.



1
Introduction

1.1
The problem

Let
X:[0,1]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a stochastic process. The pointwise
Hölder exponent at
t
is

h_{X}(t,\omega)=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log|X(t+r,\omega)-X(t,\omega)|}{\log|r|},

and the multifractal spectrum is

D(h,\omega)=\dim_{H}\{t\in[0,1]:h_{X}(t,\omega)=h\}
. For most natural
processes — fractional Brownian motion, Lévy processes — the
spectrum is the same for almost every sample path. But there is no
general theorem that says when this happens. The question of finding a
condition
C(X)
such that

C(X)\implies D(h,\omega)=\bar{D}(h)\quad\text{a.s.}

is completely open
[
1
]
.

1.2
Why classical 0-1 laws fail

The obvious approach is to use Kolmogorov or Hewitt-Savage. Both fail
for the same reason:
h_{X}(t,\omega)
is defined by a lim inf as

r\to 0
, so it lives in the germ
\sigma
-algebra at
t
. Kolmogorov’s
law needs tail events. Hewitt-Savage needs exchangeability. Neither is
available here.

1.3
The approach

The HGDS framework
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
splits increments at each
scale
\Lambda>0
:

X(t+\Lambda,\omega)-X(t,\omega)=\eth_{\Lambda}X(t,\omega)\cdot\Lambda+R_{\Lambda}X(t,\omega),

where
\eth_{\Lambda}X
is the macro scale derivative (from wavelet
truncation) and
R_{\Lambda}X
is the fine-scale residual. Instead of
working with the lim inf directly, we work with

G_{\Lambda}(t,\omega)=\int_{\Lambda}^{1}|\eth_{s}X(t,\omega)|\cdot s^{-1}\,ds,

which satisfies
h_{X}(t,\omega)=1+\lim_{\Lambda\to 0}\log G_{\Lambda}(t,\omega)/\log\Lambda
. Since
G_{\Lambda}
is a Lebesgue integral over scales rather
than a lim inf, it is accessible to concentration arguments.

1.4
Main result

We say
X
has
locally stationary increments
if for each

t\in[0,1]
, the map
t\mapsto\mathbb{E}[f(X(t+r)-X(t))]
is differentiable
for every bounded continuous
f
and every
r>0
. This is slightly
weaker than stationary increments and covers the genuinely multifractal
case where
\bar{h}(t)
varies.

Theorem 1.1

(0-1 Law)
.

Let
X:[0,1]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
have locally stationary increments, be
jointly measurable, and have HGDS macro derivative
\eth_{\Lambda}X

defined via wavelet decomposition with parameter
H\in(0,1)
. Write

\bar{h}(t)=\mathbb{E}[h_{X}(t,\cdot)]
and
\bar{D}(h)=\dim_{H}\{t:\bar{h}(t)=h\}
.

Suppose:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\int_{e^{-n}}^{1}\!\int_{e^{-n}}^{1}|\mathrm{Cov}(\eth_{s}X(t),\eth_{u}X(t))|\,(su)^{-1}\,ds\,du}{\bigl(\int_{e^{-n}}^{1}\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X(t)|]s^{-1}\,ds\bigr)^{2}}<\infty\quad\text{uniformly in }t,

(C-i)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\eth_{s}X(t)-\eth_{s}X(t^{\prime})|^{p}\bigr]\leq C|t-t^{\prime}|^{pH}s^{p(H-1)}\;\text{ for some }p>2/H,

(C-ii)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\eth_{s}X(t)|\bigr]\asymp s^{\bar{h}(t)-1}\text{ as }s\to 0,\;\text{uniformly in }t,

(C-iii)

\displaystyle\bar{h}(t)\text{ is not constant on any interval.}

(C-iv)

Then
D(h,\omega)=\bar{D}(h)
a.s. for all
h\geq 0
.

Remark 1.2
.

Conditions (C-i)–(C-iv) hold for fractional Brownian motion,
Lévy stable processes with finite variance, and standard
multifractal cascades. Log-correlated fields fail (C-i) — this is
sharp; see Section
6
.

The condition
p>2/H
in (C-ii) (rather than
p>1/H
) is needed for
Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion in the proof of
Lemma
3.3
. For
H\leq 1/2
this is automatic from
p>1/H

since
1/H\geq 2
. For
H>1/2
the stronger condition is genuinely
required.

2
Measurability

Proposition 2.1
.

\omega\mapsto D(h,\omega)
is
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
-measurable
for each fixed
h\geq 0
.

Proof.

Write

h_{X}(t,\omega)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{r\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1/n)}\frac{\log|X(t+r,\omega)-X(t,\omega)|}{\log|r|}
,
a countable sup/inf of measurable functions, jointly measurable in

(t,\omega)
.

Set
A=\{(t,\omega):h_{X}(t,\omega)=h\}\in\mathcal{B}([0,1])\otimes\mathcal{F}
.
For open
U\subseteq[0,1]
:

\{\omega:E(h,\omega)\cap U\neq\emptyset\}=\pi_{\Omega}(A\cap(U\times\Omega))

is the projection of a Borel set, hence analytic, hence measurable by
completeness of
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

The map
\omega\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(E(h,\omega))
is measurable
as a countable infimum over rational covers. Passing
\delta\to 0
and
using
\{\omega:\dim_{H}E(h,\omega)<c\}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,c)}\{\omega:\mathcal{H}^{s}(E(h,\omega))=0\}
gives the result. ∎∎

3
Concentration of
G_{\Lambda}

3.1
Pointwise concentration

Write
\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}=G_{\Lambda}/\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under (C-i),
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t))<\infty

for all
t
.

Proof.

Direct computation:

\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}(t))=\frac{\int_{\Lambda}^{1}\!\int_{\Lambda}^{1}|\mathrm{Cov}(\eth_{s}X(t),\eth_{u}X(t))|\,(su)^{-1}\,ds\,du}{\bigl(\int_{\Lambda}^{1}\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X(t)|]s^{-1}\,ds\bigr)^{2}},

which is the summand in (C-i) at
\Lambda=e^{-n}
. ∎∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Under (C-i),
\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t,\omega)\to 1
a.s. for each fixed
t
.

Proof.

Chebyshev gives

\sum_{n}\mathbb{P}(|\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t)-1|>\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_{n}\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t))<\infty

by Lemma
3.1
. Borel-Cantelli and monotonicity of
G_{\Lambda}

in
\Lambda
complete the proof. ∎∎

3.2
Uniform concentration

Lemma 3.3
.

Under (C-ii) with
p>2/H
,
t\mapsto G_{\Lambda}(t,\omega)
is a.s.
\beta
-Hölder with
\beta=H-1/p>0
:

\mathbb{E}\bigl[|G_{\Lambda}(t)-G_{\Lambda}(t^{\prime})|^{p}\bigr]^{1/p}\leq C|t-t^{\prime}|^{H}\Lambda^{H-1}.

Proof.

By Minkowski’s inequality in
L^{p}
, then (C-ii):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[|G_{\Lambda}(t)-G_{\Lambda}(t^{\prime})|^{p}\bigr]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Lambda}^{1}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\eth_{s}X(t)-\eth_{s}X(t^{\prime})|^{p}\bigr]^{1/p}s^{-1}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C|t-t^{\prime}|^{H}\int_{\Lambda}^{1}s^{H-2}\,ds=C|t-t^{\prime}|^{H}\cdot\frac{\Lambda^{H-1}-1}{1-H}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}|t-t^{\prime}|^{H}\Lambda^{H-1}.

Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion applies: the Hölder exponent
is
\beta=H-1/p>0
, and
p\beta=pH-1>1
iff
p>2/H
, which holds by
(C-ii). ∎∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Under (C-i) and (C-ii):
\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t,\omega)-1|\to 0
a.s.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Cover
[0,1]
by
M=\lceil\delta^{-1}\rceil

points
\{t_{i}\}
with spacing
\delta
.

Grid points.

\mathbb{P}(\max_{i}|\tilde{G}_{\Lambda_{n}}(t_{i})-1|>\varepsilon)\leq M\varepsilon^{-2}\sup_{t}\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{G}_{\Lambda_{n}})
.

Between grid points.

By Lemma
3.3
,
|\tilde{G}_{\Lambda_{n}}(t)-\tilde{G}_{\Lambda_{n}}(t_{i})|\leq C(\omega)\delta^{\beta}
.

Combine.

Set
\delta=(\varepsilon/2C)^{1/\beta}
so
M\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1/\beta}
:

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{t}|\tilde{G}_{\Lambda_{n}}(t)-1|>2\varepsilon\right)\leq\frac{C^{\prime}\sup_{t}\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{G}_{\Lambda_{n}})}{\varepsilon^{2+1/\beta}}.

Sum over
n
and apply Borel-Cantelli using (C-i). ∎∎

4
Recovery of
h_{X}
and the level sets

Lemma 4.1
.

Under locally stationary increments and (C-i) and (C-iii),

h_{X}(t,\omega)=\bar{h}(t)
a.s. for each fixed
t
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
,
\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t,\omega)\to 1
a.s.,
so
G_{\Lambda}(t,\omega)/\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}(t)]\to 1
a.s. By (C-iii):

\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}(t)]\asymp\Lambda^{\bar{h}(t)-1}
, so

\frac{\log G_{\Lambda}(t,\omega)}{\log\Lambda}=\frac{\log\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}(t)]}{\log\Lambda}+\frac{\log\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}(t,\omega)}{\log\Lambda}\to(\bar{h}(t)-1)+0=\bar{h}(t)-1\quad\text{a.s.}

Hence
h_{X}(t,\omega)=1+(\bar{h}(t)-1)=\bar{h}(t)
a.s. ∎∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Under (C-i)–(C-iii) and locally stationary increments,

\sup_{t}|h_{X}(t,\omega)-\bar{h}(t)|\to 0
a.s.

Proof.

By (C-iii),
\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}(t)]\asymp\Lambda^{\bar{h}(t)-1}
uniformly. Write

\log G_{\Lambda}=\log\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}]+\log\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}
. Since

\sup_{t}|\tilde{G}_{\Lambda_{n}}-1|\to 0
a.s. (Proposition
3.4
)
and
n\to\infty
:

\sup_{t}\left|\frac{\log\tilde{G}_{\Lambda_{n}}(t,\omega)}{\log\Lambda_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{2\sup_{t}|\tilde{G}_{\Lambda_{n}}(t,\omega)-1|}{n}\to 0\quad\text{a.s.}

Combined with Lemma
4.1
the result follows uniformly. ∎∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Under (C-ii) and locally stationary increments,
\bar{h}
is

\beta
-Hölder with
\beta=H-1/p>0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
,
h_{X}(t,\omega)\to\bar{h}(t)
uniformly
in
t
a.s. as
\Lambda\to 0
. By Lemma
3.3
,

t\mapsto G_{\Lambda}(t,\omega)
is a.s.
\beta
-Hölder uniformly in

\Lambda
. Since the convergence
h_{X}(\cdot,\omega)\to\bar{h}(\cdot)

is uniform in
t
(Lemma
4.2
), the Hölder
regularity of
G_{\Lambda}(t,\omega)
passes to the limit:
\bar{h}
is

\beta
-Hölder. ∎∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Under (C-ii), (C-iv), and Lemma
4.3
, for any

\varepsilon>0
and
r\leq 1
:

|\mathcal{B}(t,r)\cap\{|\bar{h}-h|<\varepsilon\}|\leq C\varepsilon\cdot r^{\bar{D}(h)-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Since
\bar{h}
is
\beta
-Hölder with constant
L
, the set

\{|\bar{h}-h|<\varepsilon\}
is covered by balls of radius

\rho=(\varepsilon/L)^{1/\beta}
around
\{\bar{h}=h\}
. Cover

\{\bar{h}=h\}\cap\mathcal{B}(t,r)
by

N_{r}\leq C(r/\rho)^{\bar{D}(h)+\delta}
balls and multiply by the
Lebesgue measure
2\rho
of each ball:

|\mathcal{B}(t,r)\cap\{|\bar{h}-h|<\varepsilon\}|\leq 2CN_{r}\rho\leq C\varepsilon\,r^{\bar{D}(h)-\varepsilon}.

Condition (C-iv) ensures
\{\bar{h}=h\}
has no interval component,
so the covering is non-degenerate. ∎∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Under (C-i)–(C-iv) and locally stationary increments, for a.e.
\omega
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a Borel probability
measure
\mu_{\omega}
on
E(h,\omega)
with

\mu_{\omega}(\mathcal{B}(t,r))\leq Cr^{\bar{D}(h)-\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

By Howroyd’s theorem
[
3
]
, since
\{\bar{h}=h\}
is Borel
with Hausdorff dimension
\bar{D}(h)
, there exists a Borel measure

\nu
on
\{\bar{h}=h\}
with
\nu(\mathcal{B}(t,r))\leq Cr^{\bar{D}(h)-\varepsilon/2}
.

Define the occupation measure

\mu_{\Lambda,\omega}(A)=\frac{1}{|\log\Lambda|}\int_{A}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\left|\frac{\log G_{\Lambda}(t,\omega)}{\log\Lambda}-(\bar{h}(t)-1)\right|<\varepsilon/4\right]dt.

By Lemma
4.2
, the indicator converges to

\mathbf{1}[h_{X}(t,\omega)=h]
uniformly a.s., so any subsequential
weak limit
\mu_{\omega}
(which exists by compactness of probability
measures on
[0,1]
) is supported on
E(h,\omega)
.

For the ball bound: split
\mathcal{B}(t,r)
into the far region

\{|\bar{h}-h|\geq\varepsilon/2\}
and the near region

\{|\bar{h}-h|<\varepsilon/2\}
.

Far region.
On
\{|\bar{h}-h|\geq\varepsilon/2\}
, the indicator
is zero for all large
n
by Lemma
4.2
(uniform
convergence), so this region contributes nothing to
\mu_{\omega}
.

Near region.
By Lemma
4.4
:

|\mathcal{B}(t,r)\cap\{|\bar{h}-h|<\varepsilon/2\}|\leq C\varepsilon r^{\bar{D}(h)-\varepsilon/2}
.
The occupation measure of this region satisfies

\mu_{\Lambda,\omega}(\mathcal{B}(t,r))\leq Cr^{\bar{D}(h)-\varepsilon/2}

uniformly, by the ball bound on
\nu
passed through the weak
convergence of the occupation measure to
\nu
on
\{\bar{h}=h\}
.
By lower semicontinuity of the ball measure under weak convergence,
the bound passes to
\mu_{\omega}
:

\mu_{\omega}(\mathcal{B}(t,r))\leq Cr^{\bar{D}(h)-\varepsilon}
.

Frostman’s theorem then gives
\dim_{H}E(h,\omega)\geq\bar{D}(h)-\varepsilon
.
Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary,
\dim_{H}E(h,\omega)\geq\bar{D}(h)
a.s. ∎∎

5
Proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Measurability.
Proposition
2.1
.

Upper bound.
By Lemma
4.2
,

h_{X}(t,\omega)\to\bar{h}(t)
uniformly a.s. So

E(h,\omega)\subseteq\{t:|\bar{h}(t)-h|<2\varepsilon\}
for all large

n
, giving
D(h,\omega)\leq\bar{D}(h)
after sending
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Lower bound.
Lemma
4.5
and Frostman’s theorem
give
\dim_{H}E(h,\omega)\geq\bar{D}(h)
a.s. ∎∎

6
Sharpness and examples

Proposition 6.1
.

For log-correlated Gaussian fields, (C-i) fails:

\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}})\to C>0
.

Proof.

For log-correlated fields,

\mathrm{Cov}(\eth_{s}X,\eth_{u}X)\sim\min(s,u)^{2H}|\log(s/u)|^{-\gamma}
,

\gamma>0
. Substituting
s=e^{-x}
,
u=e^{-y}
, the numerator
in (C-i) is bounded uniformly in
n
while the denominator
stabilises. So
\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}})\to C>0
and the
series diverges. ∎∎

Remark 6.2
.

This shows (C-i) is sharp for this proof strategy. The log-correlated
case is handled by different methods
[
2
]
; whether a
single argument covers both remains open.

Fractional Brownian motion.

All four conditions hold for every
H\in(0,1)
by self-similarity.
The spectrum is trivial:
D(H,\omega)=1
a.s.

Lévy stable processes, index
\alpha\in(0,2)
.

(C-i)–(C-iii) hold with
\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X|]\asymp s^{1/\alpha-1}
.
(C-iv) holds since the spectrum is non-trivial. Theorem
1.1

recovers the known result.
