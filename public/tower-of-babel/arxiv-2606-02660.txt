II
Brief Review of
f(Q)
Gravity

Modified gravity provides a geometric alternative to dark energy by altering the way
gravity couples to spacetime geometry. One of the most theoretically well-motivated
extensions is the
f(Q)
class of theories, formulated in the framework of

symmetric teleparallel gravity
, where curvature and torsion vanish identically
and gravity is encoded exclusively in the non-metricity of spacetime

Nester and Yo 1999
;
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2018
;
Heisenberg 2024
.

Geometric foundations

In the most general metric-affine geometry, the connection is characterized by
three independent tensors: curvature
R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu\nu}
, torsion

T^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
, and non-metricity
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}

Snapper and Troyer 2014
;
Bahamonde et al. 2023
.
Symmetric teleparallel gravity imposes the conditions

R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\mu\nu}=0,\qquad T^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}=0,

(2)

so that the
entire
gravitational interaction arises from non-metricity alone.
The non-metricity scalar,

Q=-Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}P^{\alpha\mu\nu},

(3)

is constructed from the non-metricity tensor and a superpotential

P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
built from traces and contractions of
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}

Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2019
.
This scalar plays the same role that the Ricci scalar
R
plays in GR.

From STEGR to
f(Q)

When the action is taken to be linear in
Q
, one obtains the

symmetric teleparallel equivalent of general relativity
(STEGR)

Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2018
.
The corresponding action,

S_{\textrm{S}TEGR}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\frac{Q}{16\pi G},

(4)

is dynamically equivalent to GR but formulated in a connection-free (coincident) gauge.

A natural extension is to promote the Lagrangian to a general function of
Q
,

S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,f(Q)+\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{m},

(5)

in direct analogy with the
f(R)
and
f(T)
extensions of curvature- and torsion-based
gravity
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2010
;
Cai et al. 2016
.
Crucially, the
f(Q)
field equations remain
second order
, avoiding the higher-derivative
instabilities that affect many other modified-gravity extensions.

Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}

From

Q_{\alpha}\equiv Q_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu},\qquad\tilde{Q}_{\alpha}\equiv Q^{\mu}{}_{\alpha\mu},

(6)

and the disformation and superpotential tensors

\displaystyle L^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(Q^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}-Q_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\nu}-Q_{\nu}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu}\right),

(7)

\displaystyle P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}L^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{4}\big(Q^{\alpha}-\tilde{Q}^{\alpha}\big)g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\delta^{\alpha}_{(\mu}Q_{\nu)}.

(8)

4. Metric variation

We first vary the action given by Eq. (
4
) with respect to the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, keeping the
connection fixed. The variation of the action is

\delta S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\left[\delta(\sqrt{-g})\left(f(Q)+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)+\sqrt{-g}\,f_{Q}\,\delta Q+\sqrt{-g}\,\delta\mathcal{L}_{m}\right].

(9)

Using the fact
\delta(\sqrt{-g})=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}

and defining the matter energy–momentum tensor in the usual way, i.e.,
T_{\mu\nu}\equiv-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}_{m})}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
we obtain

\delta S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\left(f(Q)+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}+f_{Q}\,\delta Q-\frac{1}{2}(16\pi G)T_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}\right].

(10)

The non-metricity scalar depends on the metric through both

Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}
and
P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
.
Its variation with respect to the metric can be written as

\delta Q=-P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}\,\delta Q_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu}-Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}\,\delta P^{\alpha\mu\nu}.

(11)

Since
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}
and we keep the connection fixed
in the metric variation, we have

\delta Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\alpha}(\delta g_{\mu\nu}).

(12)

After some algebra and repeated integration by parts, the terms involving

\nabla_{\alpha}(\delta g_{\mu\nu})
can be rearranged into a divergence plus a
term proportional to
\delta g^{\mu\nu}
. Dropping the total divergence, we
obtain (see e.g.
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2018
;
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2019
)

\delta Q=\left[2\nabla_{\alpha}\left(P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}\right)+q_{\mu\nu}\right]\delta g^{\mu\nu},

(13)

where

q_{\mu\nu}\equiv P_{(\mu}{}^{\alpha\beta}Q_{\nu)\alpha\beta}-2Q_{\alpha\beta(\mu}P^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\nu)}.

(14)

Substituting this back into
\delta S
and collecting terms proportional to

\delta g^{\mu\nu}
, we find

\delta S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\sqrt{-g}\,f_{Q}P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}\right)+f_{Q}q_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}f(Q)g_{\mu\nu}-8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}\right]\delta g^{\mu\nu}.

(15)

Imposing
\delta S=0
for arbitrary
\delta g^{\mu\nu}
gives the metric field
equations:

\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\sqrt{-g}\,f_{Q}P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}\right)+f_{Q}q_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}f(Q)g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}.

(16)

Throughout this work we keep

G
explicit and do not adopt units

8\pi G=1
. These are second-order in the metric and reduce to the Einstein equations
when
f(Q)=Q
.

Connection variation

To obtain the connection equations, we perform a variation of the action
with respect to
\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
, keeping
g_{\mu\nu}

unchanged. The symmetric teleparallel geometry imposes the curvature-free
and torsion-free conditions, thereby restricting the admissible connection
variations. Since the matter Lagrangian is taken to be independent of the
connection, no hypermomentum source appears. As a result, the affine
connection influences the action solely through its contribution to the
nonmetricity scalar
Q
.

The variation of the action with respect to the connection is

\delta_{\Gamma}S=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,f_{Q}\,\delta_{\Gamma}Q.

(17)

Now
Q
depends on
\Gamma
through
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}
and

P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
. After some algebra, the variation can be cast as

\delta_{\Gamma}Q=-2\nabla_{\mu}\left(P^{\mu\nu}{}_{\alpha}\right)\delta\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\nu\mu},

(18)

up to total derivatives. Integrating by parts and using the symmetry of the
connection (no torsion), one arrives at

\delta_{\Gamma}S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\mu\nu}{}_{\alpha}\right)\delta\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}.

(19)

Requiring
\delta_{\Gamma}S=0
for arbitrary variations

\delta\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
yields the connection equation

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\mu\nu}{}_{\alpha}\right)=0.

(20)

This condition ensures the consistency of the connection with the metric
field equations and encodes the dynamics of the non-metricity sector. Thus Eqs. (
16
) and (
20
) are the field equations of
f(Q)
theory.

III.1
Background Description

We consider a spatially flat FLRW metric in the coincident gauge:

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\,\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j},

(21)

for which the non-metricity scalar becomes

Q=6H^{2},

(22)

with
H=\dot{a}/a
the Hubble parameter. Substituting into the quadratic model yields

f(Q)=6H^{2}+36\alpha H^{4}+\beta,\qquad F(Q)=1+12\alpha H^{2},

(23)

where
F(Q)=df/dQ
.

For a spatially flat FLRW universe, the modified Friedmann relation in
f(Q)
gravity is written as

6FH^{2}-\frac{1}{2}f=8\pi G\rho,

(24)

with
\rho
the matter-radiation energy density. Substituting
f
and
F
gives

3H^{2}+54\alpha H^{4}-\frac{\beta}{2}=8\pi G\rho.

(25)

Introducing

and the rescaled parameters

A=18\alpha H_{0}^{2},\qquad B=\frac{\beta}{6H_{0}^{2}},

(26)

we obtain

AE^{4}+E^{2}-\big[\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}+B\big]=0,

(27)

where
\Omega_{m0}
represents the present day density fraction of all non-relativistic matter (including dark matter and baryons) and
\Omega_{r0}
represents the present day density fraction of all relativistic components (photons and neutrinos) compared to the total critical density of the Universe today.
Solving gives the physical branch

E^{2}(z)=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1+4AC(z)}}{2A},\qquad C(z)=\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}+B.

(28)

Normalization at

B=1-\Omega_{m0}-\Omega_{r0}-A.

(29)

III.2
Effective Dark-Energy Sector

Following the methodology of dynamical dark-energy reconstructions, we reinterpret modifications to the Friedmann equation as an effective dark-energy component:

3H^{2}(z)=3H_{0}^{2}\left[\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}\right]+8\pi G\rho_{\textrm{d}e}(z),

(30)

where

\rho_{\textrm{d}e}(z)=\frac{3H_{0}^{2}}{8\pi G}\Big[E^{2}(z)-\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{3}-\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}\Big].

(31)

The total effective equation of state is obtained via

w_{\textrm{e}ff}(z)=-1+\frac{2}{3}(1+z)\frac{d\ln H}{dz},

(32)

and the effective dark-energy equation of state is reconstructed as

w_{\textrm{d}e}(z)=-1+\frac{1+z}{3}\frac{d\ln\rho_{\textrm{d}e}}{dz}.

(33)

These expressions allow us to diagnose whether the quadratic
f(Q)
model can mimic the behaviour of dynamical dark energy. Deviations of

from
-1
signal evolving dark-energy-like behavior even for constant
\beta
. This framework parallels the reconstruction techniques used in phenomenological dark-energy analyses and enables direct comparison with observational hints from DESI and other datasets.

\alpha Q^{2}

While the background expansion
H(z)

gravitational sector.
In particular, redshift-space distortions (RSD), weak lensing, and galaxy

IV.1
Perturbation Framework

We work in the Newtonian gauge, in which the perturbed FLRW metric reads

ds^{2}=-(1+2\Psi)\,dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}.

(35)

In symmetric teleparallel gravity, the connection is chosen as the coincident
gauge, so all perturbations arise from the metric potentials
\Phi
and

\Psi
.
Variation of the action at linear order leads to modifications of the Poisson
equation and of the evolution equation for matter overdensities.

IV.2
Modified Poisson Equation

For a general
f(Q)
theory, the Poisson equation becomes

k^{2}\Phi=4\pi G_{\textrm{e}ff}(a)\,a^{2}\rho_{m}\,\delta_{m},

(36)

where
\delta_{m}

is the linear matter density contrast and the effective gravitational coupling is

G_{\textrm{e}ff}(a)=\frac{G}{F(Q)}=\frac{G}{1+12\alpha H^{2}}.

(37)

For positive
\alpha
, one has
F(Q)>1
and thus
G_{\textrm{e}ff}<G
, implying
a suppression of gravitational clustering, while negative
\alpha
produces

G_{\textrm{e}ff}>G
and enhanced growth.
This behaviour directly connects the background evolution
H(z)
to the
strength of gravitational interactions at the perturbative level.

Since symmetric teleparallel gravity does not induce anisotropic stress at
linear order, the Newtonian potentials satisfy

\Psi=\Phi.

(38)

IV.3
Growth of Matter Perturbations

For perturbations whose wavelengths are much smaller than the cosmological horizon, the density contrast evolves according to

\delta_{m}^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}+\frac{2}{1+z}\right)\delta_{m}^{\prime}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\Omega_{m}(z)}{(1+z)^{2}}\frac{G_{\textrm{e}ff}(z)}{G}\,\delta_{m}=0,

(39)

where primes denote derivatives with respect to redshift.
Equation (
39
) is obtained by combining the modified Poisson equation (
36
), which contains the effective gravitational coupling (
37
), with the standard evolution equation for matter perturbations written in redshift space, using the background dynamics from Eq. (
25

This equation defines the linear growth factor
D(z)
, normalized such that

D(0)=1
, through the relation
\delta_{m}(z)=D(z)\,\delta_{m}(z=0)
.
Writing the growth equation explicitly, we obtain

D^{\prime\prime}+A(z)D^{\prime}+B(z)D=0,

(40)

with

\displaystyle A(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}+\frac{2}{1+z},

(41)

\displaystyle B(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\Omega_{m}(z)}{(1+z)^{2}}\frac{1}{1+12\alpha H^{2}(z)}.

(42)

IV.4

The logarithmic growth rate is defined as

f(z)=\frac{d\ln D}{d\ln a},

(43)

and the quantity directly probed by redshift-space distortions (RSD) is

(44)

\sigma_{8}(z)=\sigma_{8,0}\,\frac{D(z)}{D(0)}.

(45)

VI.1

In the quadratic

\frac{G_{\textrm{e}ff}(z)}{G}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{2}{3}AE^{2}(z)},

\ddot{R}=-\frac{G_{\textrm{e}ff}(z)M}{R^{2}},

\nu(M,z)=\frac{\delta_{c}(z,A)}{\sigma(M,z)}.

(57)

Here
\sigma(M,z)
is the mass variance,

\sigma(M,z)=\sigma(M,0)\,D(z),

(58)

where
D(z)

\Lambda

threshold

mass function at fixed mass. The number density of massive clusters is
therefore a sensitive probe of the parameter
A
.

Weak gravitational lensing probes the integrated matter distribution and the

matter fluctuations. This naturally lowers the predicted

Physically, the suppression of

1.

G_{\textrm{e}ff}(z)

These effects provide a new observational signature of the quadratic
f(Q)

expansion history with measurements of the large-scale matter distribution.

VII
Discussion

In this work we have examined the cosmological implications of the quadratic
symmetric teleparallel gravity model

f(Q)=Q+\alpha Q^{2}+\beta,

(61)

which constitutes the minimal nonlinear extension of the symmetric teleparallel
equivalent of General Relativity. Since the non-metricity scalar satisfies

Q=6H^{2}
, the quadratic correction introduces an
H^{4}
-term in the
modified Friedmann equation, leading to redshift-dependent departures from the
standard
\Lambda
CDM expansion history. The algebraic solution for

E(z)=H/H_{0}
obtained from Eqs. (8)–(10) allows us to reconstruct the
effective dark-energy sector and to compare the background dynamics directly
with observational data.

Background evolution

The background analysis shows that the model behaves very similarly to

\Lambda
CDM at low redshift, while producing mild deviations at

0.5\lesssim z\lesssim 2
, where the quadratic term becomes relevant.
These deviations correspond to an effective dark-energy equation of state
slightly different from
w=-1
, without introducing additional dynamical
degrees of freedom. Such behaviour closely resembles the mild signs of
dynamical dark energy indicated by recent DESI and weak-lensing datasets.

Linear growth and modified gravity

At the perturbative level, the model modifies the Poisson equation through the
effective gravitational coupling

\frac{G_{\textrm{e}ff}(z)}{G}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{2}{3}AE^{2}(z)},\qquad A=18\alpha H_{0}^{2},

(62)

which enters directly into the growth equation. For
\alpha>0
, the coupling
satisfies
G_{\textrm{e}ff}(z)<G
and decreases with redshift, yielding a
suppressed linear growth factor
D(z)
, reduced growth rate
f(z)
, and a
lower
f\sigma_{8}(z)
. This form of weakened gravitational clustering is
consistent with the behaviour preferred by redshift-space distortion and
weak-lensing data, pointing towards a lower amplitude of matter fluctuations
relative to the Planck-
\Lambda
CDM prediction.

Nonlinear structure formation and the
S_{8}
tension

In the nonlinear regime, the weakened gravitational coupling increases the
spherical-collapse threshold and suppresses the halo mass function. Since

\sigma(M,z)=\sigma(M,0)D(z)
is reduced for
\alpha>0
, the predicted
value of

S_{8}=\sigma_{8}\sqrt{\Omega_{m}/0.3}

(63)

is correspondingly lower. This behaviour arises naturally from the structure
of the model and does not rely on additional fields or fine-tuning. It
provides a direct mechanism to alleviate the well-known
S_{8}
tension
between early- and late–Universe measurements. Negative values of
\alpha
,
in contrast, lead to
G_{\textrm{e}ff}>G
, enhanced growth, and even divergent
behaviour, and are therefore strongly disfavoured.

Overall assessment

The combined background, linear, and nonlinear analyses lead to several
conclusions:

•

The quadratic
f(Q)
model closely reproduces
\Lambda
CDM at
low redshift and fits distance-based observables well.

•

Intermediate-redshift deviations mimic a mild dynamical-dark-energy
behaviour compatible with recent DESI results.

•

The weakened gravitational coupling for
\alpha>0
suppresses
structure formation and lowers
S_{8}
, providing a natural
resolution to the growth tension.

•

Negative values of
\alpha
are theoretically and observationally
disfavoured due to excessive gravitational strength and unstable
behaviour.

Future directions

Possible extensions of this work include full Boltzmann-code implementations
for CMB and large-scale-structure observables, joint analyses with upcoming
DESI, Euclid, and LSST data, and detailed studies of nonlinear lensing
statistics to further quantify the suppression predicted in the halo mass
function.

Conclusion

Quadratic
f(Q)
gravity emerges as a minimal, theoretically motivated, and
observationally viable modification of gravity. It preserves the successes of

\Lambda
CDM at the background level while naturally suppressing structure
formation at late times, providing a compelling geometric explanation for the
current hints of dynamical dark energy and for the persistent
S_{8}

tension.
