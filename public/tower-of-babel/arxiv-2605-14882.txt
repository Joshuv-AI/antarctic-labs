Perturbation of the largest matching root of hypergraphs Thanks: Corresponding author. Yi Wang Thanks: Funding. Supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (No. 12331012, 12571360)
Jiang-Chao Wan 1 , Yi Wang 2

Abstract
. The largest matching root of a k k -graph is the largest real root of its matching polynomial, which is equal to the maximum modulus of all the zeros of the matching polynomial. In this paper, we investigate the perturbation of the largest matching root of k k -graphs. We determine all k k -graphs whose largest matching root attains the maximum among all k k -cacti and linear k k -cacti with a given number of cycles and edges, where a k k -cactus is a k k -graph in which every two distinct cycles have at most one vertex in common. To achieve this, we prove that the celebrated shifting operation of k k -graphs, introduced by Erdős, Ko and Rado, does not decrease the largest matching root. This result extends a classical result by Csikvári (Electron. J. Combin. 18 (2011) # \# P182) stating that the Kelmans transformation does not decrease the largest matching root of graphs.

Introduction

The problem of determining

For each

•

As observed in

Theorem 2.4

(

.

Let

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In
[
19
]
, Lovász and Pelikán introduced the ordering on forests based on the matching polynomial.
In recent years, the ordering was generalized to graphs by Csikvári
[
4
]
and Chen, Li, and Lian
[
5
]
, and to
k
-forests by Su, Kang, Li, and Shan
[
22
]
.
Inspired by their works,
we apply the same principle to define an ordering for
k
-graphs via the matching polynomials, which will be used to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
be two
k
-graphs.
We write
\mathcal{G}\prec\mathcal{H}
if
\mu(\mathcal{G},x)>\mu(\mathcal{H},x)
for every

and write
\mathcal{G}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{H}
if
\mu(\mathcal{G},x)\geq\mu(\mathcal{H},x)
for every
x\geq\lambda(\mathcal{H})
.

Note that the matching polynomial is monic,
we can easily verify the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
be two
k
-graphs.
If
\mathcal{G}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{H}
, then

\lambda(\mathcal{G})\leq\lambda(\mathcal{H})
.
If
\mathcal{G}\prec\mathcal{H}
, then

\lambda(\mathcal{G})<\lambda(\mathcal{H})
.

To obtain more properties of the ordering, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 4.5
[
23
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a subgraph of a connected
k
-graph
\mathcal{H}
, then

\lambda(\mathcal{G})\leq\lambda(\mathcal{H})
,
with equality if and only if
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a proper spanning subgraph of a connected
k
-graph
\mathcal{H}
, then
\mathcal{G}\prec\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By the hypothesis, there exist distinct edges
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{t}\}
such that
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{1},\dots,e_{t}\}
,
where
t\geq 1
and
\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{1},\dots,e_{t}\}
denotes the
k
-graph
(V(\mathcal{H}),E(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{e_{1},\dots,e_{t}\})
.
Write
e_{0}:=\emptyset
.
Repeatedly applying Lemma
2.1
(b), we obtain

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{H},x)

\displaystyle=\mu(\mathcal{H}\setminus e_{1},x)-\mu(\mathcal{H}-e_{1},x)

\displaystyle=\mu(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{1},e_{2}\},x)-\mu(\mathcal{H}\setminus e_{1}-e_{2},x)-\mu(\mathcal{H}-e_{1},x)

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=\mu(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{1},\dots,e_{t}\},x)-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mu(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{0},e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1}\}-e_{i},x),

which implies that

(3)

\mu(\mathcal{G},x)-\mu(\mathcal{H},x)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mu(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{0},e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1}\}-e_{i},x).

For every
i\in[t]
, since
\mu(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{0},e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1}\}-e_{i},x)
is monic,
we get
\mu(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{0},e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1}\}-e_{i},x)>0

.
Moreover,
\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{0},e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1}\}-e_{i}
is a proper subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
, so
\lambda(\mathcal{H})>\lambda(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e_{0},e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1}\}-e_{i})
by Lemma
3.3
.
Combining this and (
3
), we conclude that
\mu(\mathcal{G},x)-\mu(\mathcal{H},x)>0

for every

, as desired.
∎

.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a proper spanning subgraph of a
k
-graph
\mathcal{H}
,
then
\mu(\mathcal{G},x)>\mu(\mathcal{H},x)
for every

.

\widehat{\mathcal{G}}

\mathcal{H}

\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{H}

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}

k

\mathcal{H}
.
If
\mathcal{H}
is connected, then the assertion follows from Lemma
3.4
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
has

\mathcal{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{t}
.
Then
\mathcal{G}
can be written as the disjoint union of

\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{t}

\mathcal{H}_{i}

.
Without loss of generality, one may assume that there exists an integer

\mathcal{G}_{i}

\mathcal{G}_{i}=\mathcal{H}_{i}

If
u
and

are adjacent in
\mathcal{G}
such that

and

,
then

\lambda(\mathcal{G}(v)\ast\mathcal{H}(w))<\lambda(\mathcal{G}(u)\ast\mathcal{H}(w)).

Proof.

Note that

\mathbb{S}_{uv}(\mathcal{G}(v)\ast\mathcal{H}(w))=\mathcal{G}(u)\ast\mathcal{H}(w)
and it is not isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}(v)\ast\mathcal{H}(w)
.
According to the hypothesis on

and

, we can pick edges
e\in E_{\mathcal{G}}(u)\setminus E_{\mathcal{G}}(v)
and
f\in E_{\mathcal{H}}(w)
.
Observe that
e\cap f=\emptyset
and
\mathbb{S}_{uv}(f)\notin E_{\mathcal{G}(v)\ast\mathcal{H}(w)}(u)
,
so the result follows from Corollary

.

A
k
-graph is called a
k
-star
with
m
edges, denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(k)}
,
if its vertex set admits a partition

\{v\}\cup V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}

with
|V_{1}|=\cdots=|V_{m}|=k-1

and the edge set is
\{\{v\}\cup V_{i}:i=1,\ldots,m\}
.
The vertex
v
is called the
center
of
\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(k)}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-graph.
An edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
is called a
pendant edge
of
\mathcal{H}
if it contains at least
k-1
vertices of degree one.
If
\mathcal{H}
can be written as
\mathcal{G}(u)\ast\mathcal{T}(v)
, where
\mathcal{G}
is a
k
-graph and
\mathcal{T}
is a
k
-tree, then we call
\mathcal{T}
a
pendant
k
-tree
of
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the
root

v
.
A pendant
k
-tree is said to be
maximal
if it is not a proper subgraph of any other pendant
k
-tree.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{T}
be two vertex-disjoint connected
k
-graphs, and let
u\in V(\mathcal{H})
and
v\in V(\mathcal{T})
.
If
\mathcal{T}
is a
k
-tree with
m
edges, then

\lambda(\mathcal{H}(u)\ast\mathcal{T}(v))\leq\lambda(\mathcal{H}(u)\ast\mathcal{S}^{(k)}_{m}(v)),

where
v
is the center of
\mathcal{S}^{(k)}_{m}
,
and the equality holds if and only if

\mathcal{H}(u)\ast\mathcal{T}(v)\simeq\mathcal{H}(u)\ast\mathcal{S}^{(k)}_{m}(v)
.

Proof.

We begin with the case of
|E(\mathcal{H})|\geq 1
.
Write

\mathfrak{H}_{m}=\{\mathcal{H}(u)\ast\mathcal{F}(v):\mathcal{F}\text{ is a $k$-tree with $m$ edges and}~v\in V(F)\}.

Let
\mathcal{H}(u)\ast\mathcal{T}(v)
be a maximizing
k
-graph in
\mathfrak{H}_{m}
.
If
\mathcal{T}
is not a
k
-star with center
v
, then there exist two edges
e,f\in E(\mathcal{T})
and a vertex
z
such that

v\in e
,
v\notin f
and
e\cap f=\{z\}
.
As
\mathcal{T}
is a
k
-tree,
\mathcal{H}(u)\ast\mathcal{T}(v)
can be uniquely written as
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(z)\ast\widehat{\mathcal{T}}(z)
,
where
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}
is a subtree of
\mathcal{T}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
is a
k
-graph containing
\mathcal{H}
such that
d_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}}(z)=1
.
By Lemma
4.1
, we get

\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(z)\ast\widehat{\mathcal{T}}(z))<\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(v)\ast\widehat{\mathcal{T}}(z)).

Clearly, we have
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(v)\ast\widehat{\mathcal{T}}(z)\in\mathfrak{H}_{m}
, which contradicts the maximality of
\mathcal{H}(u)\ast\mathcal{T}(v)
in
\mathfrak{H}_{m}
.
For the case of
|E(\mathcal{H})|=0
, we have
\mathcal{H}(u)\ast\mathcal{T}(v)=\mathcal{T}
.
If
\mathcal{T}
is not a

-star, then we can write
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{1}(w)\ast\mathcal{T}_{2}(w)
, where
|E(\mathcal{T}_{1})|\geq 1
and
|E(\mathcal{T}_{2})|\geq 1
.
Applying the obtained result, we get that
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
are both
k
-stars with center

, as desired.
∎

For (a) and (b), we prove the case of

We begin with the proof of (a).
If there exists an edge

that does not satisfy the assertion, then
\mathcal{H}
can be written as
\mathcal{T}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{s})\ast(\mathcal{H}_{1}(v_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{s}(v_{s}))

\mathcal{T}
is a

,

, and every

By Lemma

Note that

also lies in
\mathfrak{Ca}_{k}(m,t)
, which contradicts the maximality of
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathfrak{Ca}_{k}(m,t)
, as desired.

For (b), if
d_{\mathcal{C}}(v)=1

, by Lemma

(b), then
\mathcal{H}
can be written as
\mathcal{H}_{1}(v)\ast\mathcal{H}_{2}(v)

\mathcal{H}_{1}

Let

Since
\mathcal{H}_{1}(v^{\prime})\ast\mathcal{H}_{2}(v)
lies in
\mathfrak{Ca}_{k}(m,t)
,
this contradicts the maximality of
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathfrak{Ca}_{k}(m,t)
and thus yields (b).

It remains to prove (c).
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a maximizing
k
-graph in
\mathfrak{Ca}_{k}(m,t)
, and

\mathcal{C}=(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell},v_{\ell})
be a cycle of

with length

.

, observe that

, which contradicts the maximality of
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathfrak{Ca}_{k}(m,t)
.
Hence, every cycle of
\mathcal{H}
has length at most

.
Note that for the case of

has length

in this case.
If

, note that

(b) and Lemma

(b) suggests that there exists an edge

\mathcal{H}
in

.

.

Let

Remark 4.7
.

For

, the proof strategy of Theorem
4.5
cannot be directly used to determine the maximizing graph in
\mathfrak{LCa}_{k}(m,t)
.
The main reason is that for a
k
-graph
\mathcal{H}
which is a linear cycle
(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},e_{2},v_{2},e_{3},v_{0})
of length
3
, the
k
-graph
\mathbb{S}_{v_{1}v_{0}}(\mathcal{H})
is not linear.
However, for
k=2
, the
2
-graph
\mathbb{S}_{v_{1}v_{0}}(\mathcal{H})
is still a cycle of length
3
.
This is why the proof strategy of Theorem
4.6
coincides with that of Theorem
4.5
.

To determine the maximizing graph in
\mathfrak{LCa}_{k}(m,t)
for
k\geq 3
, we need the concept of
k
-th power of a graph.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
For an integer
k\geq 3
,
the
k
-th power of
G
, denoted by
G^{(k)}
,
is the
k
-graph with the vertex set
V\cup{\{v_{e,1},\ldots,v_{e,k-2}:e\in E}\}
and edge set

{\{e\cup{{\{v_{e,1},\ldots,v_{e,k-2}}}\}:e\in E}\}
.
The following result establishes an identity between

\mu(G,x)
and
\mu(G^{(k)},x)
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G
be a graph.
Then

\mu(G^{(k)},x)=x^{(1-\frac{k}{2})|V(G)|+(k-2)|E(G)|}\mu(G,x^{\frac{k}{2}}).

In particular, we have
\lambda(G^{(k)})=\lambda(G)^{\frac{2}{k}}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
G^{(k)}
, we get that
p(G^{(k)},i)=p(G,i)
for every
i
, and

|V(G^{(k)})|=|V(G)|+(k-2)|E(G)|.

The statement follows from the following calculation

\displaystyle\mu(G^{(k)},x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}p(G^{(k)},i)x^{|V(G^{(k)})|-ki}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}p(G,i)x^{|V(G)|+(k-2)|E(G)|-ki}

\displaystyle=x^{(1-\frac{k}{2})|V(G)|+(k-2)|E(G)|}\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}p(G,i)x^{\frac{k}{2}(|V(G)|-2i)}

\displaystyle=x^{(1-\frac{k}{2})|V(G)|+(k-2)|E(G)|}\mu(G,x^{\frac{k}{2}}).\qed

\mu(\mathcal{G},x)=x^{3k-3}-3x^{2k-3}+x^{k-3}

and

\mu(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}_{3,1},x)=\mu(\mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{3,1},x)=x^{3k-3}-3x^{2k-3},

so

\left(\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{1/k}=\lambda(\mathcal{G})<\lambda(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}_{3,1})=\lambda(\mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{3,1})=3^{1/k}.

The proof is completed.
∎

5.
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we determine all
k
-graphs
whose largest matching root attains the maximum
among all
k
-cacti and linear
k
-cacti
with a given number of cycles and edges.
Our method combines the spectral method and the shifting operation of
k
-graphs.
We believe that there are some potential applications of Theorem
1.4
and Lemma
2.5
.
For instance, as a quick application,
we next consider the maximizing
k
-graphs among all
n
-vertex
k
-graphs with a given number of pendant edges.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{(k)}
be the complete
k
-graph of order
n
, that is, the
k
-graph with
n
vertices and its
edge set consists of all
k
-element subsets of the vertex set.
For integers
n,k,p
, write

\mathcal{K}_{n,p}^{(k)}=\mathcal{K}_{n}^{(k)}(u)\ast\mathcal{S}_{p}^{(k)}(v),

where
u\in V(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{(k)})
and
v
is the center of the
k
-star
\mathcal{S}^{(k)}_{p}
.
Observe that the
k
-graph
\mathcal{K}_{n,p}^{(k)}
is unique up to isomorphism.
The following result extends Theorem 3.3 in
[
26
]
from graphs to
k
-graphs.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
p\geq 1
, and
n\geq p(k-1)+k+1
be integers.
Then the
k
-graph
\mathcal{K}_{n-p(k-1),p}^{(k)}
is the unique
k
-graph whose largest matching root
attains the maximum among all
n
-vertex
k
-graphs with
p
pendant edges.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a maximizing
k
-graph among all
n
-vertex
k
-graphs with
p
pendant edges.
Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{p}
be all pendant edges of
\mathcal{H}
,
and let
W
be the
p(k-1)
degree-one vertices of these pendant edges.
Denote by
\mathcal{H}-W
the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
induced by the vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})\setminus W
, that is,
V(\mathcal{H}-W)=V(\mathcal{H})\setminus W
and
E(\mathcal{H}-W)=\{e\in E(\mathcal{H}):e\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})\setminus W\}
.
We claim that
\mathcal{H}-W
is a complete
k
-graph of order
n-p(k-1)
.
If not, we add all possible edges to
\mathcal{H}-W
until it becomes a complete
k
-graph.
Observe that the resultant
k
-graph
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
still has
p
pendant edges and Lemma
3.3
implies that
\lambda(\mathcal{H})<\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{H}})
, a contradiction to the maximality of
\mathcal{H}
. The claim follows.

For all
i=1,\ldots,p
,
let
v_{i}\in e_{i}
be the root of
e_{i}
in
\mathcal{H}
, that is,
v_{i}
is the unique vertex of
e_{i}
with degree at least two.
If
v_{s}\neq v_{t}
for some
1\leq s,t\leq p
,
then we consider the
k
-graph
\mathbb{S}_{v_{s}v_{t}}(\mathcal{H})
.
As we claimed before,
\mathcal{H}-W
is a complete

k
-graph of order
n-p(k-1)
.
One may check that
\mathbb{S}_{v_{s}v_{t}}(\mathcal{H})-W
is also a complete
k
-graph of order
n-p(k-1)
, and

\mathbb{S}_{v_{s}v_{t}}(\mathcal{H})
has
p
pendant edges and is not isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}
.
Moreover,

e_{s}\cap e_{t}=\emptyset
and
\mathbb{S}_{v_{s}v_{t}}(e_{t})\notin E_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{s})
, so Corollary
1.6
implies that
\lambda(\mathcal{H})<\lambda(\mathbb{S}_{v_{s}v_{t}}(\mathcal{H})),

which contradicts the maximality of
\mathcal{H}
.
Thus,
v_{i}=v_{j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq p
, and the proof is completed.
∎
