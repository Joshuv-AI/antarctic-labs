The impact of drought stress on the physiological biochemical indexes and metabolites in Panax notoginseng
Authors: Hongwei Sun, Can Wang, Yuling Yang, Dawei Zhao, Ling Li, Cuixian Peng, Yonghong Tao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPanax notoginseng is a traditional Chinese medicinal rich in saponins, polysaccharides and flavonoids, which have significant therapeutic effects on cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. Climate change has led to an increase in global water and drought disasters, significantly impacting the growth of P. notoginseng plant and the synthesis of its active ingredients. Therefore, we focused on investigating the effects of drought stress on the biochemical indexes, accumulation of effective components and product of metabolism in P. notoginseng. Results indicated that P. notoginseng can survive in PEG 6000 (PEG) concentration ranging from 2.5% PEG to 7.5% PEG, however, its growth, physiological indicators, effective components content, and metabolize substances were significantly affected by PEG. When P. notoginseng was treated for 7 days by 7.5% PEG, which the contents of Pro in leaves and roots were 0.62 mg/g and 0.53 mg/g; POD were 461.08 U/g and 210.76 U/g; APX were 120.37 U/g and 54.46 U/g; MDA were 27.09 μmol/g and 22.66 μmol/g. It was treated by 5.0% PEG for 7 days, the content of saponins (R1, Rg1, Re, Rb1, and Rd) were 0.63%, 3.34%, 0.47%, 3.32% and 0.68%. It was treated by 7.5% PEG for 7 days, the contents of chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, and total chlorophyll were 4.16 mg/g, 2.43 mg/g and 6.59 mg/g. Using LC–MS technology, we obtained 11,603 differential metabolites, among which 481 were upregulated and 1065 were downregulated. Cluster and KEGG enrichment analysis indicate that the differential metabolites of amino acids, glycerides, fatty acids, nucleotides, organic acids, and lipids were significantly up-expressed expression, and it were significantly enriched in metabolic pathways such as glycerol phospholipid metabolism, α-linolenic acid metabolism, carotenoid biosynthesis.Subject terms: Biochemistry, Biotechnology, Physiology, Plant sciences

Panax notoginseng (P. notoginseng) is a perennial herb belonging to the Panax genus, known for its adaptability and wide distribution in Yunnan, Guangxi, and Sichuan provinces of China. The whole plant of P. notoginseng have been used for medicinal purposes. Clinically, P. notoginseng not only promotes blood circulation, removes blood stasis, clears swelling and relieves pain, nourishes and strengthens body, and replenishing blood and hematopoietic, but also has various pharmacological effects such as anti-fatigue, anti-aging, anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, lowering blood pressure, lowering blood lipids, and lowering blood sugar. Therefore, it has significant development value in the fields of pharmaceuticals and natural health products1.

Predictions indicate that, due to greenhouse gas emissions, the global mean surface air temperature will continue to rise through the end of this century2–4. Ongoing global warming is causing further reductions in soil moisture, which may exacerbate the warming trend and intensify climate-related risks5–7. The crop yield reduction caused by drought stress has exceeded the total yield reduction caused by other environmental stresses. Drought seriously affects the growth of plants, threatening their survival, reducing their photosynthetic rate and the ability of chloroplasts to absorb light and energy conversion efficiency8. Research indicates that soil moisture stress affects the growth and yield of medicinal plants, either promoting or inhibiting the content of effective components in these plants9–11. However, when plants encounter drought stress, various small molecules such as proline, soluble sugars, and soluble proteins rapidly accumulate and play an important role in osmotic regulation12. In addition, antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), and catalase (CAT), as well as malondialdehyde (MDA), were rapidly induced to cope with oxidative damage during drought stress13. Compared to roots and stem, plant leaves are more sensitive to environmental changes. Research found that insufficient soil moisture has a significant impact on the leaf growth and dry matter accumulation of P. notoginseng (3-year-old), and its leaves can adapt to the changes of soil moisture content by protecting enzyme activity and regulating the content of osmotic substances14. Under PEG 6000 simulated drought treatment, the chlorophyll content of Cassiae Semen seedlings gradually decreased with increasing treatment concentration, while the antioxidant enzyme activity gradually increased with increasing drought severity, this indicates that drought has a significant inhibitory effect on the photosynthetic system of Cassiae Semen15. Although PEG-induced drought and determination of physiological indexes are common in model plants, but P. notoginseng is a highly specialized shade-loving medicinal plant with unique physiological traits. As far as we know, there are few systematic reports about the threshold responses of antioxidant defense system (SOD, POD, CAT) and osmotic adjustment (Pro) under osmotic stress in this high-value species. Our research not only fills a critical knowledge gap about the drought tolerance mechanisms of P. notoginseng, but also is very important for optimizing its water management in P. notoginseng cultivation. In addition, drought also significantly affects the metabolic pathways of medicinal plants, leading to increased production of antioxidant enzymes to combat reactive oxygen species (ROS), while also promoting the synthesis of specific secondary metabolites16. Medicinal plants typically activate defense mechanisms related to the synthesis of secondary metabolites, enhancing the production of essential oils, alkaloids, phenols, flavonoids, and anthocyanins to counter the negative effects of water deficiency17. The leaves of P. notoginseng is more sensitive to the environment than roots and stems, in order to adapt to environmental changes, it will regulate the expression of some genes and produce new protein. However, these molecular compounds, the dynamic changes of enzyme activities, and biochemical level responses are largely unclear, and research is lack on non-targeted metabolomics of P. notoginseng leaves under different drought stress environmental conditions18. Therefore, it is of great significance to explore the effects of different drought stress environments on the non-targeted metabolomics of P. notoginseng leaves, and to master the enzyme activity of P. notoginseng leaves and the response degree of regulatory substances to drought.

In this study, we focused on the impact of drought stress on the physiological biochemical indexes and accumulation of active ingredients in P. notoginseng. Through metabonomic analysis, the differential changes of non-targeted differential metabolites in leaves were discussed. The purpose of this study is to provide theoretical basis for soil moisture management of P. notoginseng in Yunnan, and to improve the yield and quality of P. notoginseng through scientific irrigation measures. Besides, this study also provides a new insight for the biosynthesis of effective components of P. notoginseng.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

The one-year old P. notoginseng used in this study was planted in the Research Institute base of Amomum tsaoko is located in Renhe Town, Maguan County, Wenshan Prefecture, Yunnan Province (104° 32′ 46″ E, 23° 24′ 32″ N), belonging to the subtropical humid monsoon climate. Wait until the P. notoginseng seedlings have spread their leaves and remove them (60 days old) to flowerpot for later was used. The seed used in the study were provided by the institute of medicinal biological technique, wenshan academy of agricultural sciences. Well-growing P. notoginseng plants were filled in flowerpot were selected to begin drought stress treated by using PEG 6000 at different concentrations. Four gradient treatments were established, with PEG 6000 concentrations set at T1 (2.5% PEG 6000, water potential values was − 0.19 MPa), T2 (5.0% PEG 6000, water potential values was − 0.50 MPa), and T3 (7.5% PEG 6000, water potential values was − 0.93 MPa), respectively, and distilled water was used as the control treatment (CK 0% PEG 6000), each treatment involves 4 times biological replicates. That was, 0% (CK, water potential values was 0 MPa), 2.5%, 5% and 7.5% PEG solution(0 g, 2.5 g, 5 g and 7.5 g PEG 6000 were weighed, respectively. and then were dissolved in distilled water and were fixed to 100 mL). Every day, 100 ml PEG solution was poured into each flowerpot. 2 kg matrix soil was placed in each flowerpot (26 cm × 21 cm), and the relative water content (RWC) of the soil was 70%. Next, we put treatment materials into a light incubator, the cultivation conditions: light/dark time was 14 h/10 h, the day/night temperature was 25 °C/20 °C, the light intensity was 2000 lx, and the treatment lasted for 7 days.

Measurement of physiological indicators and saponin content

Physiological and biochemical indicators

On the 1 d, 3 d, 5 d, and 7 d of the PEG 6000 treatment, the root, stem, and leaf tissues from P. notoginseng were separately collected from each treatment group to measure various physiological and biochemical indicators. The root, stem, and leaf were placed in 1.5 mL centrifuge tubes, frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored in − 80 °C freezer for analysis. This analysis included measuring the contents of chlorophyll, proline (Pro), superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), catalase (CAT), malondialdehyde (MDA), and ascorbate peroxidase (APX). Of which the chlorophyll content was determined by using the ARNON19 method, Pro content was measured with the ninhydrin microplate method, SOD activity was measured using the WST-1 method, POD activity using the colorimetric dish method, CAT activity was assessed using the H2O2 decomposition-UV absorption method20, MDA content was determined using the thiobarbituric acid method, APX activity was determined by using the ascorbic acid hydrogen peroxide (AsA-H2O2) method.

Saponin content

The roots of P. notoginseng were ground into powder and then were filtered by using No.4 sieve, and 0.6 g was weighed, and then 50 mL methanol was added precisely and let it stay overnight. Subsequently, put it in a water bath at 80 °C for 2 h, and let it cool, and then weigh its volume. Next, Using methanol to make up the reduced volume, and shake well and filter. 20 μL filtrate sample and 20 μL reference substance were injected into high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) and then were determined. Every sample has three biological repeats.

HPLC conditions: chromatographic column is Thermo Scientific Hypersil GOLD C18 (USA Thermo Scientific Company, 250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 µm). Mobile phase is water (A)-acetonitrile (B) were used for linear elution (V/V). Sample detection elution conditions: 0 min 20% B, 20 min 40% B, 40 min 50% B, 50 min 100% B; The flow rates are all 1 ml/min, column temperatures is 30 °C, Detection wavelength is 203 nm.

Calibration curve making and content calculation: the root of P. notoginseng was used to make mixed standard solutions with different concentrations of R1, Rg1, Re, Rb1 and Rd. The purity of the standard samples ≥ 98%. Chromatographic conditions for detection is the same as above, the peak area value is taken as the ordinate, the concentration of each monomer saponin in the standard solution is taken as the horizontal ordinate, and bring the sample into the standard curve to get the concentration of each monomer saponin, and then after calculating the content of each monomer saponin in the sample by formula.

CR (%) is the content of each monomer saponin, CR0 (mg/ml) is the concentration of each monomer saponin, VT (ml) is the total volume of extractive solution, Vs (ml) is the volume of extraction solution used in the determination, W (g) is the weight of the sample.

Calibration curves (Rc): y = 32496x + 36,613, R2 = 0.999. R1: y = 21118x − 1336, R2 = 0.999. Rg1: y = 32539x + 1918, R2 = 0.999. Re: y = 16325x − 7289, R2 = 0.999. Rb1: y = 23383x + 28,281, R2 = 0.999. Rd: y = 29096x + 11,348, R2 = 0.999.

Statistics processing and analysis of data

Data recording and statistical processing were carried out by using Excel 2014. The variance analysis and factor analysis of data were completed by using SPSS 20.0, the significance analysis was finalized by using LSD. The chart was made by Excel 2014.

RNA isolation and sequencing

Frozen tissues were ground with a mortar and pestle, and RNA was isolated using the NEBNext Poly (A) mRNA Magnetic Isolation Module. RNA quality was determined on an Agilent 2100 BioAnalyzer. Seven sequencing libraries were prepared using the NEBNext Ultra RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina. Next, 100/150 bp PE sequencing was performed on an Illumina HiSeq 2000/2500 machine. After the size of the library segment meets the expectations, the quantitative concentration of the library was analyzed by real-time fluorescence quantitative polymerization reactions (Q-PCR) to ensure the quality of the library. Finally Sequences were obtained from HiSeq X Ten platform (Illumina).

De novo assembly and quality control

First, primary assemblies (eight from PacBio long reads, one from 10× Genomics linked reads) were prepared by different pipelines. Next, scaffolding and polishing were performed on the optimal primary assemblies in order to obtain a final genome assembly. Primary assembly v0.1 was generated from PacBio long reads after correction by Canu v1.621, assembly v0.2 by MECAT v1.122, assembly v0.3 by miniasm v0.2-r168 after alignment by minimap v0.2-r12423, assembly v0.4 by Falcon v0.7 (Falcon, RRID:SCR 016089)24 after correction with Canu v1.6, assembly v0.5 by SMARTdenovo v1.0.025 after correction with Canu v1.6, assembly v0.6 by Wtdbg v1.2.826 after correction with Canu v1.6, assembly v0.7 by SMARTdenovo v1.0.0 after correction, and assembly v0.8 by Wtdbg v1.2.8. Assembly v0.9 was prepared by Supernova assembler 2.027,28 from 10× Genomics linked reads data. Based on quality-control parameters, assembly v0.7 was chosen as optimal for further scaffolding and polishing.

Differential metabolites detection

50 ± 5 mg sample is weighed and added into 2 mL centrifuge tube, add a grinding bead with a diameter of 6 mm, and add 400 µL extraction solution (methanol: water = 4:1), and grinding for 6 min(− 10 °C, 50 Hz), ultrasonic extraction at low temperature for 30 min(5 °C, 40 kHz), and keep the sample at − 20 °C for 30 min, and centrifuge for 15 min(13,000 g, 4 °C), and transfer the supernatant to the injection bottle for computer detection.

Chromatographic conditions: chromatographic column is ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 µm; Waters, Milford, USA). Mobile phase A is 95% water + 5% acetonitrile (contains 0.1% formic acid), and mobile phase B is 47.5% acetonitrile + 47.5% isopropanol + 5% water (contains 0.1% formic acid). The flow rate is 0.40 mL/min, the sample volume is 10 μL, and the column temperature is 45 °C.

Mass spectrometry conditions: Triple TOF 6600 high resolution mass spectrometry was used. Collecting time is 10 min, ionization voltage is 5000 V, ion source temperature is 550 °C, spray gas is 50psi, auxiliary heating gas is 60 psi, gas curtain gas is 35 psi, de-clustering voltage is 60 V, MS1 collision energy is 10 V, MS2 collision energy is 30 V, collision energy step size is 15 V, MS1 mass range is 50–1250 Da, and MS2 mass range is 25–1250 Da.

Differential metabolites cluster heatmap analysis

The hierarchical cluster analysis results of samples and differential metabolites were presented as heatmap with dendrograms, while pearson correlation coefficients between samples were caculated by the cor function in R and presented as only heatmap. The heatmap were carried out by R package software. Version number is R version 4.3.1 (https://cran.r-project.org/bin/windows/base/old/4.3.1/).

KEGG annotation and enrichment analysis

Identified differential metabolites were annotated using KEGG Compound database (http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/compound/), annotated differential metabolites were then mapped to KEGG Pathway database (http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/pathway.html). Significantly enriched pathways are identified with a hypergeometric test’s P-value for a given list of differential metabolites.

Discussion

Plants will produce many strategies to cope with drought stress, such as enzyme protection system of Pro, POD, SOD, CAT and MDA, or non-enzymatic protection system of abscisic acid, jasmonic acid and other substances were activated29. Some studies have found that the content of antioxidant enzymes was continuously increased with the extension of drought time30. Pro is an important plant osmotic regulator. Pro accumulation can reduce the osmotic difference between P. notoginseng and drought environment when it suffers from drought stress, so as to reduce the damage caused by drought environment to P. notoginseng31. With the increase of drought stress, MDA content gradually increased in leaves of P. notoginseng seedlings32. When plants suffer from drought stress, the excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen will destroy the structure and function of biofilm. The contents of POD, CAT and SOD all showed an upward trend when plants were stressed by drought for 12 h and 24 h. But, with the gradual aggravation of drought, three antioxidant enzymes all showed a downward trend33.

Our results showed that the contents of Pro, POD, SOD, CAT and MDA all were increased in different degrees when P. notoginseng is subjected to drought stress. Of which, the contents of Pro and POD in roots or leaves were increased continuously with the increase of PEG 6000 concentration and time. This shows that P. notoginseng started its own antioxidant enzyme system under drought stress in order to adapt to the drought environment. The contents of SOD and CAT in leaves reached its maximum and then decreased when P. notoginseng was stressed by T3 for 3 d, which indicated that the antioxidant enzyme system of P. notoginseng was destroyed and the metabolism of active oxygen was unbalanced under drought stress. This is consistent with the change rule of antioxidant enzyme activity found by Zhou34 when studying the leaves of Glycyrrhiza seedlings. Wang32 research shows that MDA content is positively correlated with the degree of plant cell membrane damage. Our study showed that MDA content was increased continuously with the increase of PEG 6000 concentration and time, which indicates that the cell membrane of P. notoginseng is damaged under drought stress, this is similar to the research results of wang35. Some studies have shown that proper drought stress can increase the secondary metabolites of P. notoginseng. Our research found that the contents of saponins R1, Rg1, Re, Rb1 and Rd in P. notoginseng increased first and then decreased with the increase of PEG 6000 concentration, the content of saponin was the highest when P. notoginseng was treated by T2 for 7 d. However, as the concentration of PEG 6000 increased to T3, the content of total saponins gradually decreased. Liao36 used PEG 6000 to simulate drought to treat P. notoginseng for 0–24 h, and found that the antioxidant enzymes changed significantly during short-term treatment, and the key genes of P. notoginseng saponin terpene skeleton synthesis were up-expressed. Under slight drought stress, the saponin content of P. notoginseng was increased. Our results is consistent with those of Liao33.

Zhang37 and Huang38 found that appropriate drought stress can stimulate the survival potential of plants in adversity, and the chlorophyll content of plants will increase under moderate drought stress. Our study showed that the contents of chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b and total chlorophyll of P. notoginseng increased gradually in PEG concentration ranging from T1 to T3, which was consistent with the research results of Zhang and Huang. In this study, the increase of chlorophyll under drought stress can be attributed to the two reasons. Concentration effect: drought stress significantly inhibited leaf expansion and cell division. Although chlorophyll biosynthesis was also suppressed, but, the reduction in leaf area and fresh weight accumulation was more pronounced than the degradation rate of chlorophyll, leading to a relative increase in chlorophyll concentration per unit fresh weight. Stress acclimation: For P. notoginseng, drought stress (7.5% PEG) may trigger an adaptive response. The increase of chlorophyll content could serve as a compensatory mechanism to maintain light capture and photosynthetic electron transport, and providing sufficient energy for the synthesis of osmotic adjustment substances (Pro, soluble sugars) to combat dehydration.

This study clearly demonstrated the self-regulation ability of P. notoginseng in response to drought stress by analyzing the characteristics of various antioxidant enzymes and chlorophyll content changes that affect plant drought resistance. When MDA increased significantly, chlorophyll content increased with it, so it is speculated that P. notoginseng may enhance its drought resistance by accumulating Pro and POD under drought stress conditions. When P. notoginseng was treated by T3 for 3 days, the contents of SOD and CAT in leaves reached the maximum and then decreased, at this time, the leaves of P. notoginseng appeared slight wilting, and even bending and drooping. Meanwhile, we speculate that the drought environment of T3 may produce osmotic stress on the roots of P. notoginseng, which promotes the imbalance of root metabolic system, so it is necessary to replenish water to P. notoginseng. This indicates that T3 (7.5% PEG) may represents the critical threshold concentration for P. notoginseng to maintain basic physiological function and survival. In this study, because the higher treatment concentration (9% or 10% PEG) was not set, we need to further study the wider drought intensity gradient to determine the absolute lethal limit of this species. For this study, we selected PEG 6000 as a tool to achieve rapid, reproducible, and uniformly applied water deficit conditions, which allowed us to isolate the direct osmotic effects on P. notoginseng without confounding factors such as soil texture, nutrient availability, or microbial communities. However, we fully agree that several limitations must be considered when extrapolating our findings to field conditions. First, the physical environment differs substantially. In soil, roots encounter mechanical impedance, heterogeneous water pockets, and a dynamic rhizosphere microbiome that can modulate stress responses. By contrast, PEG-induced osmotic stress is immediate and static, potentially overestimating the uniformity and intensity of stress perceived by the plant. Second, the temporal dynamics of stress imposition are distinct. Soil drought typically develops over days to weeks, allowing for acclimation mechanisms such as osmotic adjustment, stomatal acclimation, and root architectural remodeling. Our PEG treatment imposed a relatively rapid onset of stress, which may have elicited more acute stress signaling rather than the chronic, progressive responses seen under gradual drying. Third, potential artifacts associated with high molecular weight PEG must be acknowledged. Although PEG 6000 is commonly used to lower water potential, high concentrations can reduce oxygen availability (increased viscosity) and may cause physical damage to root tips or mucilage layer disruption, but this effects were typically observed in soil drought. At the same time, we think that the underground organs (tuberous roots) of P. notoginseng are highly sensitive to osmotic pressure changes. The concentration gradient of drought stress (such as 0%, 2.5%, 5%, 7.5%) was set in the experiments, which it could accurately simulate the gradual process of drought stress in the field. This stress dose effect analysis not only conforms to the actual situation of the gradual occurrence of drought in the field, but also provides a reliable theoretical foundation for gene mining and germplasm resources screening of drought-resistant.

In this study, P. notoginseng was treated by different concentrations of PEG 6000. Cluster analysis showed that differential metabolites of leaves, such as amino acids, glycerides, fatty acyl groups, nucleotides, lactones, organic acids, ketones, flavonoids, lipids, alkaloids, benzenes, and heterocyclic compounds, were significantly up-expressed. However, these differential metabolites have the functions of maintaining osmotic pressure, stabilizing neutral pH value and high water solubility, so as to adjust the osmotic potential of plasma membrane to improve the drought resistance of plants and maintain the normal growth and development of cells. Studies show that plants can induce the accumulation of differential metabolites such as sugar, amino acids, sugar alcohols and organic acids to maintain their own metabolic balance in high temperature and drought conditions. As shown by Witt39 that under drought conditions, the differential metabolites of sugar, sugar alcohol, amino acids and tricarboxylic acid cycle intermediates were increased significantly in maize. Obata40 found that under drought conditions, the amino acid content in plant leaves increased significantly. Guo41 reported that under the double stress of high temperature and drought, the abundance of differential metabolites carbohydrate, sugar, amino acids and organic acids in maize leaves were increased significantly. Thus, under the drought stress, the abundance of differential metabolites such as carbohydrate, sugar, amino acid, fatty acyl and ketone in the leaves of P. notoginseng increased obviously, and these differential metabolites may play a key role in adapting to high temperature and drought environment.

KEGG enrichment analysis showed that the differential metabolites of P. notoginseng leaves were significantly enriched in glycerophosphate metabolism, α-linolenic acid metabolism, linoleic acid metabolism, nerve sheath lipid metabolism, polyketide unit biosynthesis, carotenoid biosynthesis, nucleotide metabolism, zeatin biosynthesis, secondary metabolite biosynthesis, porphyrin metabolism, riboflavin metabolism, β-alanine metabolism and other pathways. In addition, glycerophosphate metabolism is an important intermediate product in the process of lipid metabolism. It participates in the response process of biological and abiotic stress by changing the content of cell membrane lipids or producing unsaturated fatty acids. When plants encounter high temperature and drought stress, it also has an important function of inducing systemic immunity of plants. Plant cells can synthesize jasmonic acid through α-linolenic acid metabolism. Jasmonic acid, as an important element to drive plant defense response and transmit stress signals, it can alleviate the damage caused by water stress to rice42, soybean43 and cotton44 that through regulating the opening and closing of stomata, enhancing the activity of antioxidant enzyme system and inducing the expression of related genes, so as to maintain the water content in plants and improve their tolerance to drought stress. 9-cis-epoxy carotenoid dioxygenase, the key enzyme of ABA synthesis induced by carotenoid biosynthesis pathway, was significantly enriched18, ABA plays an important role in plant tolerance to high temperature and drought stress45, this indicates that P. notoginseng may regulate the drought response by increasing ABA-related synthase when it encounters drought environment.

Conclusion

The study systematically investigates the impact of drought stress on the biochemical indexes, effective compounds accumulation, and product of metabolism in P. notoginseng. The results showed that PEG 6000 drought stress can significantly affect the physiological indicators, effective components content, and metabolize substances in P. notoginseng, identifying P. notoginseng can survive in PEG 6000 concentration ranging from T1 to T3, but T3 (7.5% PEG) represents the critical threshold concentration for P. notoginseng to maintain basic physiological function and survival. Through LC–MS technology, 11603 differential metabolites were obtained, of which 481 were upregulated and 1065 were downregulated. Cluster and KEGG enrichment analysis indicate that the differential metabolites of amino acids, glycerides, fatty acids, nucleotides, organic acids, and lipids were significantly up-expressed expression, and it were significantly enriched in metabolic pathways such as glycerol phospholipid metabolism, α-linolenic acid metabolism, carotenoid biosynthesis. This research not only provides a new insight into the synthesis mechanisms of P. notoginseng effective compounds under water stress, but also offers important scientific evidence for the cultivation management and stress adaptation research of P. notoginseng.
