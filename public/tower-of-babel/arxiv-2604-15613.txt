VoodooNet: Achieving Analytic Ground States via High-Dimensional Random Projections
Wladimir Silva

Abstract
We present VoodooNet , a non-iterative neural architecture that replaces the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) paradigm with a closed-form analytic solution via Galactic Expansion. By projecting input manifolds into a high-dimensional, high-entropy ”Galactic” space ( d ≫ 784 d\gg 784 ), we demonstrate that complex features can be untangled without the thermodynamic cost of backpropagation. Utilizing the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse to solve for the output layer in a single step, VoodooNet achieves a classification accuracy of 98.10% on MNIST and 86.63% on Fashion-MNIST . Notably, our results on Fashion-MNIST surpass a 10-epoch SGD baseline (84.41%) while reducing the training time by orders of magnitude. We observe a near-logarithmic scaling law between dimensionality and accuracy, suggesting that performance is a function of ”Galactic” volume rather than iterative refinement. This ”Magic Hat” approach offers a new frontier for real-time Edge AI, where the traditional training phase is bypassed in favor of instantaneous manifold discovery. Finally, we derive the Voodoo Scaling Law , a theoretical framework that predicts accuracy bounds as a function of Galactic dimensionality and intrinsic manifold entropy.

Discussion: The Entropy-Efficiency Trade-off

A central finding of this work is the relationship between the optimization method and the resulting weight entropy.
In our comparative analysis of learning regimes (Standard via SGD vs. the Analytic Pseudoinverse), we observed a fundamental trade-off between training speed and structural order.

5.1
Comparison with Stochastic Cooling

Iterative training (SGD) can be viewed as a thermodynamic cooling process.

The clustering of weights at the high-entropy limit suggests that the pseudoinverse solution is utilizing the cross-correlations of the entire manifold to identify digits, rather than simply memorizing their local pixel-templates.

5.2
The Cost of the Magic Hat

While the
VoodooNet

Unlike the sparse L1 skeleton, the VoodooNet weights remain dense and chaotic. This suggests that while we can mathematically jump to a functional state, we bypass the “compression phase” conjectured by Information Bottleneck theory. Consequently, the VoodooNet is computationally heavier in terms of information storage (higher bit-cost) compared to its regularized, trained counterparts.

5.3
Inductive Bias and the Magic Hat

The success of the VoodooNet proves that feature learning is not a prerequisite for high accuracy on MNIST. By utilizing the Johnson-Lindenstrauss Lemma to untangle the input manifold, the “Magic Hat” simply finds the most efficient linear slice through the high-dimensional space. Future work should investigate whether analytic solutions can be constrained to reach low-entropy ground states without reverting to iterative descent.

5.4
The Geometry of Saturation

The empirical results confirm that while the Johnson-Lindenstrauss Lemma ensures distance preservation, the ”efficiency” of the linear slice approaches saturation. This suggests that for high-entropy manifolds like CIFAR-10, VoodooNet reaches a ”topological ceiling” where adding more dimensions (

Conclusion: Beyond Iterative Cooling

This study has demonstrated that the optimization of a neural network can be approached through two distinct lenses: iterative thermodynamic ”cooling” and instantaneous analytic ”discovery.”
Our empirical results on both MNIST and the more complex Fashion-MNIST dataset confirm that iterative feature learning is not a prerequisite for high-fidelity classification.
While standard SGD-based models painstakingly search for a sparse, low-entropy skeleton, VoodooNet utilizes the Johnson-Lindenstrauss Lemma to untangle the manifold into a Galactic high-dimensional space.
The

suggests that while ELM accuracy is bounded by dataset complexity

Furthermore, rotation perturbation results confirm that VoodooNet is not only computationally cheaper than iterative training but also structurally more robust to geometric distortion.
