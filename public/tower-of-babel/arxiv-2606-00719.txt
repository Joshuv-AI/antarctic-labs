Characterization of the structure of k k -edge-maximal graphs
Zheng-Jiang Xia, Hong-Jian Lai, Jian Lu, Zhen-Mu Hong

Abstract




Abstract

Let
\kappa^{\prime}(G)
be the edge-connectivity of the graph
G
. The
strength
of
G
, denoted by
\overline{\kappa}^{\prime}(G)
, is the maximum edge-connectivity of its subgraphs. A simple graph
G
is called
k
-
edge-maximal
if
\overline{\kappa}^{\prime}(G)\leq k
but for any edge
e
not in
G
,
\overline{\kappa}^{\prime}(G+e)\geq k+1
. In this paper, we propose the concepts of kernel and closure of a graph and discuss the properties of closure. Utilizing these properties,
we present the necessary and sufficient condition for a graph to be
k
-edge-maximal,
which refines the results in [J. Graph Theory 14 (1990) 187–197],
and prove that there exists a
k
-edge-maximal graph of order
n
with
m
edges if and only if
m=(n-1)k-\binom{k}{2}r
, for some integer
r
with
1\leq r\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+2}\right\rfloor
.
Furthermore, we characterize the structure of
k
-edge-maximal graphs with a given number of edges.

Keywords:
Edge-connectivity; Strength;
k
-maximal graph; Closure

05C35, 05C40, 05C75

Throughout this paper,

For integers

F(n,k)=\max\{|E(G)|:G\text{ is a $k$-edge-maximal graph of order }n\},

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

Let

be an integer and let

and

be two graphs with disjoint vertex sets and with

-edge-join

and

is a simple graph obtained from the disjoint union of

and

by adding

and

and a vertex of

For notational convenience, we also use

Theorem 1.2

(Lai
[
3
]
)
.

Let

be an integer, let

be a
k
-edge-maximal graph and let

be either a

or a

-edge-maximal graph. Then all graphs in

-edge-maximal.

It is straightforward to verify that none of the graphs in

are

-edge-maximal, which are counterexamples to Theorem

.
This implies the

-edge-join of two

-edge-maximal graphs

-edge-maximal graph.
A natural problem arises: under what conditions can the graph obtained by the

-edge-join operation be

-edge-maximal? This problem motivates the current research. In this paper, we establish the necessary and sufficient condition for

to be

-edge-maximal, where

and

are arbitrary graphs. In order to present our main results, we first propose the concepts of kernel and closure of

-edge-maximal graphs.

Definition 1.3
.

Let

be two

-edge-maximal graphs, where

is a subgraph of

is a

-kernel
of

can be obtained from

via some

-edge-join operations, or equivalently,

can be obtained from

by recursively deleting a
k
-edge-cut of a remaining component, in the sense that
H
is one of the components after deleting such a series of edge-cuts from
G
. When no confusion arises, we abbreviate a
k
-kernel as a
kernel
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
G
be a
k
-edge-maximal graph and let

be a subgraph of
G
. A
k
-closure
of

is a minimal
k
-kernel of

that contains

as a subgraph. When no confusion arises, we abbreviate a
k
-closure as a
closure
.

Investigating the properties of the closures of
k
-edge-maximal graphs, we establish our main results as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be two graphs, and let

be a graph of order
n>k+1>2
. Denote

as the set of vertices in

that are incident with the edges joining

and
H_{2}
in

for each

.
Then

is
k
-edge-maximal if and only if
one of the following holds:

(i)

H_{i}\cong K_{1}
and
H_{3-i}
is a
k
-edge-maximal graph;

(ii)

Both
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are
k
-edge-maximal graphs with at least
k+2
vertices, and there is a closure of
H_{i}[V_{i}]
in

not isomorphic to

for each

.

Theorem 1.6
.

For

, there exists a
k
-edge-maximal graph of order

with

edges if and only if

m\in\left\{(n-1)k-\binom{k}{2}r:1\leq r\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+2}\right\rfloor\text{ and }r\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Theorem
1.1
by Mader
[

]
and Lai
[
3
]
follows immediately from Theorem

, by

and

.

Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be either
a
k
-edge-maximal graph or

. A

-edge-join
of

and

is a

-edge-join satisfying the condition stated in Theorem

, that is, if the resulting graph

is

-edge-maximal. The following theorem further characterizes the structure of

-edge-maximal graphs with a given number of edges.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
G
be a
k
-edge-maximal graph of order

. Then
G
has

edges, where
1\leq r\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+2}\right\rfloor
and

,
if and only if

can be obtained from the disjoint union of

K_{k}\vee 2K_{1}
and
(n-(k+2)r)
copies of

, that is

r(K_{k}\vee 2K_{1})\;\cup\;(n-(k+2)r)K_{1},

by a sequence of proper

-edge-joins.

In the next section, we discuss properties of a
k
-edge-maximal graph and the closure of its subgraphs. The proofs of main results will be presented in the last section.

)

If

Lemma 2.2

(Lai
[

]
)
.

Let

-edge-maximal graph of order

. Suppose that

(ii)

Suppose first that

. Hence

is

-edge-maximal.

Similarly, the lemma will follow if

(i).

Now we assume that both

Case 1.

If

(1)

We claim that for any two vertices

Case 2.

Concluding remark

Given an integer

, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for a graph to be
k
-edge-maximal. This condition implies a method for determining whether a graph is
k
-edge-maximal. In fact, if

-edge-cut of a

-edge-maximal graph is presented in this paper.

.
