Abstract
. We design observer-based controllers to stabilise abstract linear boundary control systems on Hilbert spaces. Our main results introduce conditions for exponential, strong, and polynomial stability, and establish external well-posedness of the closed-loop system. We design controllers for a one-dimensional wave equation, a two-dimensional wave equation with distributed control and observation, and a non-uniform SCOLE model.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we design controllers for controlled linear partial differential equations (PDEs) and infinite-dimensional linear systems. Our main interest is in stabilising PDE systems with boundary inputs. Majority of such models can be represented as
abstract boundary control systems

[
34
,
25
]
of the form

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{A}}x(t)+B_{i}u(t),\qquad x(0)=x_{0}

(1.1b)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{B}}x(t)

\displaystyle=Qu(t)

(1.1c)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{C}}x(t)

on a Hilbert space
X
. Here (
1.1b
) describes the effect of the boundary input on the system, and the second term on the right-hand side of (
1.1a
) models the possible in-domain control inputs.

We stabilise the boundary control system (
1.1
) using an
observer-based dynamic feedback controller of the form

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{x}}(t)

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{A}}\hat{x}(t)+B_{i}u(t)+L_{i}(\hat{y}(t)-y(t)),\qquad\hat{x}(0)=\hat{x}_{0}

(1.2b)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{B}}\hat{x}(t)

\displaystyle=Qu(t)+L(\hat{y}(t)-y(t))

(1.2c)

\displaystyle\hat{y}(t)

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{C}}\hat{x}(t)

(1.2d)

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{K}}\hat{x}(t).

Here
L
and
L_{i}
are output injection gains which facilitate the state estimation and
\mathfrak{K}
is a stabilising state feedback gain operator.
Observer-based stabilisation of infinite-dimensional systems with boundary inputs has been studied earlier in the setting of regular linear systems in
[
44
]
and well-posed linear systems in
[
37
]
. In this work we utilise the framework of boundary control systems which often have a beneficially close connection to the underlying PDE model. In particular, the boundary trace operator
\mathfrak{B}
in (
1.1
) is easier to construct than the input operator in the state equations of a well-posed system. In addition, our framework also facilitates stabilisation of systems which are not well-posed.

Hyperbolic partial differential equations with boundary inputs and in-domain measurements (or vice versa) can only rarely be stabilised exponentially. We show that under mild assumptions our controller (
1.2
) instead achieves
polynomial
or
non-uniform stability

[
3
,
4
,
33
]
.
Indeed, the separation principle of observer-based stabilisation implies that closed-loop stability is based on the stability of the observer error dynamics, described by a strongly continuous semigroup
T_{L}
, and the stability of the system under state feedback, described by a semigroup
T_{K}
.
In a hyperbolic PDE with boundary control and in-domain observation, we may achieve exponential stability of
T_{K}
, but not of
T_{L}
. We show that if one of the semigroups is exponentially stable and the other one is non-uniformly stable, then the closed-loop system is non-uniformly stable, and we obtain a rate of decay for its classical state trajectories.

Our boundary control system (
1.1
) differs slightly from the typical form where
Q=I
and
B_{i}=0
. Our class with the bounded operator
B_{i}
can be used to study PDEs with control both at the boundary and the in interior of the spatial domain. Moreover, for parabolic systems — even with boundary measurements — we may often use an output injection described by a bounded operator, and this way the class (
1.1
) is large enough to also contain the
controllers
of classical boundary control systems. The role of
Q
is to model the situation where the control input
u(t)
does not affect all boundary conditions of the PDE. These uncontrolled boundary conditions could alternatively be included in the domain of
\mathfrak{A}
, but the formulation in (
1.1b
) facilitates their use for boundary output injections in (
1.2b
).

Our two main stabilisation results are for systems (
1.1
) with different levels of external well-posedness defined in terms of boundedness of the input, output, and input-output maps. Under stronger well-posedness assumptions our results also establish external well-posedness of the closed-loop system.
An important feature of observer-based stabilisation is that the Luenberger-type controller may not have well-defined dynamics when disconnected from the system. Instead, the controller should be considered to have
an internal loop

[
44
,
7
]
which can be broken into an additional input-output pair to guarantee well-defined dynamics of the controller. We show that our controller (
1.2
) has an internal loop in the sense of
[
44
,
7
]
.

We illustrate the controller design for PDE systems by considering three concrete models. We first consider a two-dimensional wave equation with in-domain control and observation on different parts of the rectangular spatial domain. In the case where only one of these subdomains satisfies the Geometric Control Condition, the system cannot be stabilised exponentially, but we achieve polynomial stability. We also stabilise a one-dimensional wave equation with non-collocated boundary input and output and establish external well-posedness of the resulting closed-loop system. Finally, we stabilise a non-uniform SCOLE model
[
23
]
consisting of an undamped beam equation with tip mass dynamics. Since the control acts through the finite-dimensional part of the model, exponential stabilisation is not possible, but our controller stabilises the system polynomially.

Observer design and observer-based stabilisation of infinite-dimensional linear systems are understood well in the case of systems with bounded input and output operators
[
8
, Sec. 8.3]
. These techniques have also been extended to systems covering PDEs with boundary control and observation, most notably for Pritchard–Salamon systems in
[
9
,
24
]
, regular linear systems in
[
44
]
, and for well-posed systems in
[
37
]
. In addition, finite-dimensional controllers have been introduced for parabolic systems with unbounded input and output operators in
[
21
, Sec. 4B]
and second order observers have been designed for abstract wave equations in
[
11
]
.
Also abstract boundary control systems were treated in
[
1
]
by converting them first to their state space representation.
For abstract boundary control systems the observer design problem without stabilisation has been studied in
[
42
,
41
]
(with focus on bilinear systems) and in
[
12
,
13
]
.
We extend and unify these earlier studies of observer-based stabilisation of boundary control systems.
Moreover, observer-based polynomial stabilisation has previously only been studied for a one-dimensional wave equation in
[
31
]
, and we extend the approach in
[
31
]
to the general class of boundary control systems.
Besides the abstract infinite-dimensional setting, observer-based stabilisation has been investigated actively for PDE models with boundary control and observation, e.g., in
[
35
]
using PDE backstepping, in
[
16
]
using spectral analysis, and in
[
26
]
using linear matrix inequalities. In our view, the class of abstract boundary control systems offers a convenient framework for controller design, because the representation in the form (
1.1
) is relatively easy to construct compared to the regular or well-posed linear system settings, and because the controller (
1.2
) has an immediate interpretation as a PDE system. The abstract framework and our main results allow taking advantage of the separation principle to reduce the controller design problem to the design of the stabilising feedback and output injection gains for the PDE model.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we formulate background results on the well-posedness, stability and feedback for boundary control systems. Our main results on controller design for abstract systems are presented in Section
3
. In Section
4
we apply our results in the stabilisation of PDE systems.

Definition 2.1

Definition 2.4

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
({\mathfrak{B}},{\mathfrak{A}},{\mathfrak{C}},Q,B_{i})
be a boundary node on the Hilbert spaces
(U_{b},U,X,Y)
.
Let

,

({\mathfrak{B}}-L{\mathfrak{C}},{\mathfrak{A}}+L_{i}{\mathfrak{C}},{\mathfrak{K}},Q,B_{i})

generates a strongly continuous semigroup

on

and the following hold.

(a)

and

are exponentially stable, then

is exponentially stable with growth bound

(b)

is exponentially stable (or vice versa), then

is strongly stable.

(c)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{e}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathfrak{A}}+B_{i}{\mathfrak{K}}&B_{i}{\mathfrak{K}}\\

4.
Stabilisation of PDE Systems

4.1.
A Two-Dimensional Wave Equation

In this example we consider a wave equation on the square
\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]
. The wave profile is controlled over the subdomain
\Omega_{c}=[1/4,3/4]\times[1/4,3/4]
, and the observation is taken over the rest of the domain,
\Omega_{o}=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{c}
.
The system is defined as

(4.1a)

\displaystyle w_{tt}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\Delta w(\xi,t)+\chi_{\Omega_{c}}(\xi)u(\xi,t),

\displaystyle\xi\in\Omega,\ t>0

(4.1b)

\displaystyle w(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\xi\in\partial\Omega,\ t>0

(4.1c)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=w_{t}(\cdot,t)|_{\Omega_{o}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\xi\in\partial\Omega,}\ t>0

with initial conditions
w(\cdot,0)=w_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
w_{t}(\cdot,0)=w_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, where
\chi_{\Omega_{c}}
is the characteristic function of
\Omega_{c}
.
The wave equation can be recast as a control system of the form (
1.1
) with state
x(t)=(w(\cdot,t),w_{t}(\cdot,t))^{\top}
on
X=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
with norm
\lVert(x_{1},x_{2})^{\top}\rVert_{X}^{2}=\lVert\nabla x_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert x_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
, and with
U=L^{2}(\Omega_{c})
and
Y=L^{2}(\Omega_{o})
. Since the wave equation does not have boundary inputs, we let
U_{b}=\{0\}
,
{\mathfrak{B}}=0
, and
Q=0
. Moreover, we define
{\mathfrak{A}}=A=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\
\Delta&0\end{bmatrix}
with
D(A)=(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega))\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and define
B_{i}\in{\mathcal{L}}(U,X)
and
{\mathfrak{C}}\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
so that

B_{i}u=(0,\chi_{\Omega_{c}}u)^{\top}
,
u\in U
, and

{\mathfrak{C}}x=x_{2}|_{\Omega_{o}}
for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})^{\top}\in X
.
The controller in the following result stabilises the wave equation. Since the control region
\Omega_{c}
does not satisfy the Geometric Control Condition, exponential stabilisation
is not possible.

Proposition 4.1
.

The observer based controller

\displaystyle\hat{w}_{tt}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\Delta\hat{w}(\xi,t)-\chi_{\Omega_{c}}(\xi)\hat{w}_{t}(\xi,t)-\chi_{\Omega_{o}}(\xi)(\hat{w}_{t}(\xi,t)-w_{t}(\xi,t)),

\displaystyle\xi\in\Omega

\displaystyle\hat{w}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\xi\in\partial\Omega

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=-\hat{w}_{t}(\cdot,t)|_{\Omega_{c}}

with initial conditions
\hat{w}(\cdot,0)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\hat{w}_{t}(\cdot,0)\in L^{2}(\Omega)

stabilises the wave equation (
4.1
) in such a way that the closed-loop semigroup is polynomially stable with exponent
\alpha=1/2
.

Proof.

The controller has the form (
3.2
) with
u_{1}\equiv 0
and
u_{2}\equiv 0
, with
L=0
, and with
\mathfrak{K}\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,U)
and
L_{i}\in{\mathcal{L}}(Y,X)
defined by

\mathfrak{K}x=-x_{2}|_{\Omega_{c}}
,
x=(x_{1},x_{2})^{\top}\in X
, and
L_{i}y=(0,-\chi_{\Omega_{o}}y)^{\top}
,
y\in Y
. In particular,
\mathfrak{K}=-B_{i}^{\ast}
and
L_{i}=-\mathfrak{C}^{\ast}
. We have from
[
2
, Thm. 2.3 & p. 165]
that
the semigroup
T_{K}
is polynomially stable with exponent
\alpha=1/2
. Moreover, the pair
(\mathfrak{C},A)
is exactly observable (in some time
\tau>0
) by
[
39
, Thm. 7.4.1]
, and therefore
T_{L}
generated by
A_{L}=A+L_{i}\mathfrak{C}=A-\mathfrak{C}^{\ast}\mathfrak{C}
is exponentially stable by
[
5
, Thm. 3.2 & p. 1104]
. Since
{\mathfrak{B}}=0
,
{\mathfrak{C}}\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
and
{\mathfrak{K}}\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,U)
,

({\mathfrak{B}}-Q{\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{A}}+B_{i}{\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{C}},Q,B_{i})
and

({\mathfrak{B}}-L{\mathfrak{C}},{\mathfrak{A}}+L_{i}{\mathfrak{C}},{\mathfrak{K}},Q,B_{i})
are well-posed boundary nodes
and the claim follows from
Corollary

3.4
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The geometry of the spatial domain has a direct effect on the stabilisation of the wave equation (
4.1
). For instance, we may let
\Omega
be the unit disk
\Omega=B(0,1)
and define the control and observation regions as

Then the pair

is only approximately controllable since

does not satisfy the Geometric Control Condition. The choices

.

4.2.

We consider the stabilisation of the undamped wave equation on the interval
(0,1)

(4.2a)

\displaystyle w_{tt}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=w_{\xi\xi}(\xi,t),\qquad

\displaystyle\xi\in(0,1),\ t>0

Proposition 4.3

For every

4.3.
The SCOLE Model

In this section we stabilise the SCOLE model
[
23
]
which consist of an Euler–Bernoulli beam coupled to an ordinary differential equation modeling the dynamics of a tip mass.
Our equation

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho(\xi)w_{tt}(\xi,t)=-(EIw_{\xi\xi})_{\xi\xi}(\xi,t),\quad\xi\in(0,1),t>0\\
w(0,t)=0,\quad w_{\xi}(0,t)=0\\
mw_{tt}(1,t)-(EIw_{\xi\xi})_{\xi}(1,t)=0\\
Jw_{\xi tt}(1,t)+EI(1)w_{\xi\xi}(1,t)=u(t)\\
y(t)=EI(1)w_{\xi\xi}(1,t)\end{cases}

with initial data
w(\cdot,0)\in H^{2}_{\ell}(0,1):=\{\,f\in H^{2}(0,1)\mid f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0\,\}
,
w_{t}(\cdot,0)\in L^{2}(0,1)
,
w_{t}(1,0)\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and
w_{\xi t}(1,0)\in{\mathbb{C}}

can be used as a simplified model for the dynamics of a monopile wind turbine tower with a heavy nacelle
[
45
]
. The torque input
u(t)
acts on the tip mass and the output measures the bending moment at
\xi=1
.
The spatially varying parameters
\rho,EI\in C^{4}([0,1])
are strictly positive and
m>0
and
J>0
.

We will rewrite (
4.4
) as a boundary control system of the form (
3.1
)
with state
x(t)=(w(\cdot,t),w_{t}(\cdot,t),w_{t}(1,t),w_{\xi t}(1,t))^{\top}

on the
Hilbert space
X=H^{2}_{\ell}(0,1)\times L^{2}(0,1)\times{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. We equip
X
with the norm

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}EI(\xi)|w^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi+\int_{0}^{1}\rho(\xi)|v(\xi)|^{2}d\xi+m|p|^{2}+J|q|^{2}

for
x=(w,v,p,q)^{\top}\in X
.
Moreover, we define
U={\mathbb{C}}
,
Y={\mathbb{C}}
, and
U_{b}={\mathbb{C}}
and define
\mathfrak{A}:D(\mathfrak{A})\subset X\to X
,
\mathfrak{B}\in{\mathcal{L}}(D(\mathfrak{A}),U_{b})
, and
\mathfrak{C}\in{\mathcal{L}}(D(A),Y)
by
\mathfrak{B}x=v^{\prime}(1)-q
,
\mathfrak{C}x=EI(1)w^{\prime\prime}(1)
, and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}x=\begin{bmatrix}v\\
-\rho^{-1}(EIw^{\prime\prime})^{\prime\prime}\\
m^{-1}(EIw^{\prime\prime})^{\prime}(1)\\
-J^{-1}EI(1)w^{\prime\prime}(1)\end{bmatrix}

for

x=(w,v,p,q)^{\top}\in D(\mathfrak{A}):=\{\,(w,v,p,q)^{\top}\in(H^{2}_{\ell}(0,1)\cap H^{4}(0,1))\times H^{2}_{\ell}(0,1)\times\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid p=v(1)\,\}
.
Since the control acts through the finite-dimensional part of the model, we choose
Q=0\in{\mathcal{L}}(U,U_{b})
and define
B_{i}\in{\mathcal{L}}(U,X)
by
B_{i}u=(0,0,0,J^{-1}u)^{\top}
,
u\in U
.
With these choices (
4.4
) has the form (
3.1
). Our first result establishes the well-posedness of the system.

Proposition 4.4
.

(\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C},I,0)
is a well-posed boundary node on the Hilbert spaces
(U_{b},U,X,Y)
and

\re\langle\mathfrak{A}x,x\rangle_{X}=\re\langle\mathfrak{B}x,\mathfrak{C}x\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}

for all
x\in D(\mathfrak{A})
.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Eq. (22) and the subsequent paragraph]
the operator
A:=\mathfrak{A}|_{\textup{Ker}(\mathfrak{B})}

is skew-adjoint and therefore generates a unitary group
on
X
.
Moreover, we have
\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}\in\mathcal{L}(D(\mathfrak{A}),{\mathbb{C}})
and since

U_{b}
is finite-dimensional,
\mathfrak{B}
has a right inverse
\mathfrak{B}^{r}\in{\mathcal{L}}(U_{b},D(\mathfrak{A}))
.
Therefore
(\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C},I,0)
is a boundary node on
(U_{b},U,X,Y)
.
For
x_{1}=(w_{1},v_{1},p_{1},q_{1})^{\top}\in D(\mathfrak{A})
and

x_{2}=(w_{2},v_{2},p_{2},q_{2})^{\top}\in D(\mathfrak{A})

integrating by parts shows that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\mathfrak{A}x_{1},x_{2}\rangle_{X}+\langle x_{1},\mathfrak{A}x_{2}\rangle_{X}&=\bigl(v_{1}^{\prime}(1)-q_{1}\bigr)\overline{EI(1)w_{2}^{\prime\prime}(1)}\\
&\qquad+EI(1)w_{1}^{\prime\prime}(1)\overline{(v_{2}^{\prime}(1)-q_{2})}.\end{aligned}

Since
\mathfrak{B}x_{1}=v_{1}^{\prime}(1)-q_{1}
and
\mathfrak{C}x_{1}=EI(1)w_{1}^{\prime\prime}(1)
, setting

x_{2}=x_{1}
yields the claimed identity

\re\langle\mathfrak{A}x,x\rangle_{X}=\re\langle\mathfrak{B}x,\mathfrak{C}x\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
,
x\in D(\mathfrak{A})
.
Moreover,
Lemma

2.8
(a) shows that

(\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C},I,0)
is well-posed provided that its transfer function
P
satisfies

\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}|P(\omega+is)|<\infty
for some
\omega>0
.

For
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{0}^{+}
we have

P(\lambda)u=\mathfrak{C}x
, where

x\in D(\mathfrak{A})
satisfies
(\lambda-\mathfrak{A})x=0
and

\mathfrak{B}x=u
. If we define
e_{u}=(0,0,0,u)^{\top}\in D(\mathfrak{A})
and
z=x+e_{u}
, then
z\in\textup{Ker}(\mathfrak{B})=D(A)
and
\mathfrak{C}z=\mathfrak{C}x
.
By
[
15
, Lem. 3.1 and the subsequent paragraph]
the
eigenvectors of
A
can be chosen to form an orthogonal basis

(\phi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of
X
. We write

A\phi_{k}=i\mu_{k}\phi_{k}
,
i\mu_{k}\in i{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
, and denote

\gamma_{k}:=\mathfrak{C}\phi_{k}
.
Setting
x_{1}=x
and
x_{2}=\phi_{k}\in D(A)
in
(
4.5
) yields

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{A}x,\phi_{k}\rangle_{X}+\langle x,A\phi_{k}\rangle_{X}=\langle\mathfrak{B}x,\mathfrak{C}\phi_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=u\overline{\gamma_{k}}.

Using
\mathfrak{A}x=\lambda x
and
A\phi_{k}=i\mu_{k}\phi_{k}
implies
\langle x,\phi_{k}\rangle_{X}=\overline{\gamma_{k}}u/(\lambda-i\mu_{k})
.
Similarly, the identity (
4.5
) applied with
x_{1}=e_{u}\in D(\mathfrak{A})
and
x_{2}=\phi_{k}\in D(A)
together with
\mathfrak{A}e_{u}=0
and
\mathfrak{B}e_{u}=-u
shows that
\langle e_{u},\phi_{k}\rangle_{X}=-i\overline{\gamma_{k}}u/\mu_{k}
. Since
the expansion of

z
in the basis
(\phi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
converges in the graph norm of
A
and
since
\mathfrak{C}|_{D(A)}\in\mathcal{L}(D(A),Y)
, we have

\displaystyle P(\lambda)u=\mathfrak{C}x=\mathfrak{C}z=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\langle z,\phi_{k}\rangle\gamma_{k}=u\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lvert\gamma_{k}\rvert^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda-i\mu_{k}}-\frac{i}{\mu_{k}}\right).

By
[
15
, Lem. 2.3]
there exist

N_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a,b,c>0
such that

a\lvert k\rvert-b\leq\sqrt{\lvert\mu_{k}\rvert}\leq a\lvert k\rvert+c

and
\mu_{-k}=-\mu_{k}
whenever
\lvert k\rvert\geq N_{0}
.
Moreover,
[
15
, Lem. 3.1]
implies that
\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lvert\gamma_{k}\rvert<\infty
.
Therefore
[
32
, Thm. 2.7]
implies
\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\lvert P(\omega+is)\rvert<\infty
for every
\omega>0
, and thus

(\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C},I,0)

is well-posed by
Lemma

2.8
(a).
∎
