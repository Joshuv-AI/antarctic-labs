Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

The work developed a hydrogen-bonded organic–inorganic framework (HOIF), specifically zinc
imidazole salicylaldoxime supramolecule (ZIOS), for selective Cu removal and recovery from
acidic mining-impacted waters (AMD), with emphasis on scalable synthesis, membrane
integration, durability in real AMD (RAMD), mechanistic understanding, and
recovery/regeneration pathways. This research breaks new ground in selective resource recovery
from AMD waters – no other sorbents can operate reliably in this pH range. This project is unique
in what it has delivered and is of general use to the public for projects relating to critical materials
recoveyr.

Summary of Research Results:

Overall summary:
The work delivers durable, high-capacity Cu uptake in 15-ZMM, Cu-dominant transport, and
selective Cu plating under mild potentials. Immediate actions are to operate extended/fed-batch
ED near −0.5 V vs Ag/AgCl to finalize energy intensity, recovery efficiency, Faradaic efficiency,
and full mass/charge balances with fouling diagnostics; to determine the origin of non-releasing
Cu within ZMMs using depth-profile XPS and transport–complexation modeling and assess
electro-chemical regeneration; and to maintain the application-oriented focus on ZMM–ED scale-
up with TEA-ready datasets.

Detailed summary:

• The project established a complete pathway from scalable ZIOS synthesis to ZIOS–
 Polyethersulfone (PES) mixed-matrix membranes (ZMMs) for selective copper removal and
 recovery from mining-impacted waters. ZIOS was produced at gram-per-hour scale with an
 XRD quality-screening protocol, then integrated into membranes via solvent casting/phase

 inversion; PES 15 wt% in NMP (with 0/10/15/20 wt% ZIOS loadings) was selected for
 downstream testing. Structural and chemical analyses (SEM/EDX/XPS, FT-IR/XRD)
 confirmed ZIOS incorporation and membrane integrity.
• Performance testing showed durable and selective Cu capture. In steady-state RAMD
 exposure, 15-ZMM reached 292 mg g⁻¹ (1 day, N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP)) and 842 mg
 g⁻¹ (5 weeks, NMP); the DMF analogue rose from 150 mg g⁻¹ to 749 mg g⁻¹ over the same
    period. Diffusion tests (0–5 h) yielded Cu uptakes of 639/765/503/23 mg g⁻¹ for 10-/15-/20-
 ZMM and PES, respectively, with Fe uptakes of 11/523/264/206 mg g⁻¹; 15-ZMM exhibited
 the strongest transport, consistent with XPS evidence of Cu/Fe adsorption and ligand-site
 interaction.
• Electrodialysis (ED) experiments across −1 to −0.34 V (vs Ag/AgCl) confirmed selective Cu
 plating at the working electrode and higher Cu than Fe fluxes. Cu recovery by plating peaked
 at −0.5 V (≈1.25 mg, ~5% of initial Cu), while more negative potentials reduced plated Cu;
 concurrent analysis quantified potential-dependent Cu/Fe uptake within 15-ZMM. In an
 extended −0.5 V run (120 h; 2% HNO₃ receiving), Cu in the receiving chamber rose sharply
 in the final 24 h as plating efficiency plateaued, indicating a tunable balance between
 membrane capture, cross-membrane transport, and electrodeposition for recovery. Post-ED
 regeneration studies highlighted a contrast between powders and membranes: EDTA readily
 degraded powder ZIOS but failed to liberate Cu from ZMMs after 5 h despite visible color
 change, with FT-IR showing minimal membrane impact. Mass balance from ED also indicated
 substantial Cu embedded in ZMM (~765 mg g⁻¹-ZIOS) even as Cu selectively plated and
 crossed the membrane, suggesting the ZMM microenvironment stabilizes Cu-bound states.
