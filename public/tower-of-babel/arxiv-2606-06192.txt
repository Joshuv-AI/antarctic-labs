A unified abstract regularity lemma
Gaia Carenini, Leonardo Franchi

Abstract
. The goal of this short note is to prove a unified abstract regularity lemma which recovers Szemerédi’s graph regularity lemma, Green’s arithmetic regularity lemma, and a regularity lemma for Boolean functions as direct corollaries.



1.
Introduction

Regularity lemmas are among the basic structural tools of modern discrete
mathematics. They assert, in different settings, that an arbitrary object can
be decomposed into a bounded-complexity structured part and a pseudorandom
remainder. The paradigmatic example is Szemerédi’s graph regularity lemma

[
Sze78
]
; closely related regularity statements appear in additive
combinatorics, most notably Green’s arithmetic regularity lemma

[
Gre05
]
, and in the analysis of Boolean functions, for instance in
the regularity lemma used by Minzer
[
Min21
]
to recover the result of Dinur and Friedgut asserting that an intersecting family lies in a junta
[
DF09
]
. These regularity lemmas have found a remarkably broad range of applications across discrete mathematics, playing a central role in developments in graph theory, additive combinatorics, theoretical computer science, and the analysis of Boolean functions.

A useful distinction is between weak and strong regularity lemmas.
Weak
regularity lemmas, such as the Frieze–Kannan lemma, admit a rather general
abstract formulation; one such formulation is due to Trevisan, Tulsiani and
Vadhan
[
TTV09
]
. They typically yield quantitatively
much stronger bounds at the expense of providing a correspondingly weaker
structural description. For strong regularity lemmas, the situation is less uniform. There
are abstract approaches, including the probabilistic framework of Tao
[
Tao06
]
and the
semiring framework of Bollobás and Nikiforov
[
BN08
]
, but these do not give, in a
completely direct way, all of the standard examples one would like to recover,
especially the Fourier-analytic form of Green’s arithmetic regularity lemma.

The purpose of this short note is to isolate an elementary abstract statement from
which the most standard strong regularity lemmas follow with essentially no additional work. This
also clarifies why the same energy-increment proof is operating in graph, Fourier-analytic, and Boolean-function settings. For completeness, in an appendix, we also revisit the frameworks of Tao and of Bollobás–Nikiforov, showing how they can be recovered from our abstract formulation.

Organization.
The remainder of this note is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we state and prove our unified abstract regularity lemma (Theorem
2.1
). In Section
3
, we derive Green’s arithmetic regularity lemma from Theorem
2.1
. In Section
4
, we derive Szemerédi’s graph regularity lemma from the same abstract theorem. In Section
5
, we obtain the aforementioned regularity lemma for Boolean functions. In the appendix, we show how our framework recovers the abstract regularity lemmas of Bollobás and Nikiforov, and of Tao.

2.
The abstract regularity lemma

Let
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a normalized finitely additive measure space, meaning that
X
is a set,
\mathcal{A}
is an algebra of subsets of
X
, and
\mu:\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
is a finitely additive function satisfying
\mu(X)=1
. Given a set
P\in\mathcal{A}
with
\mu(P)>0
, we denote the normalized average of a bounded,
\mathcal{A}
-measurable function
h:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
over
P
by

\mathbb{E}_{P}[h]=\frac{1}{\mu(P)}\int_{P}h\,d\mu.

In what follows, all partitions considered are finite partitions into measurable sets of positive measure, meaning a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
X
is a finite collection of disjoint sets
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{A}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}=X
and
\mu(P_{i})>0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Let
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
be two partitions of
X
. We say that
\mathcal{Q}

refines

\mathcal{P}
if, for every
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
, there exists some
P\in\mathcal{P}
such that
Q\subseteq P
. Given a part
P\in\mathcal{P}
, the partition of
P
induced by
\mathcal{Q}
, denoted by
\mathcal{Q}|_{P}
, is defined as the collection of sets
\mathcal{Q}|_{P}=\{Q\in\mathcal{Q}\mid Q\subseteq P\}
.

For every part
P\in\mathcal{P}
of a partition
\mathcal{P}
, fix a non-empty family of measurable functions
\mathcal{F}_{P}\subseteq\{f:P\to\mathbb{C}\mid\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
. Given an
\mathcal{A}
-measurable function
g:X\to[0,1]
, we say that
\mathcal{P}
is
\varepsilon
-
regular
for
g
with respect to the families
(\mathcal{F}_{P})_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
whenever the following inequality holds

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mu(P)\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{P}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(g|_{P}-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}])\cdot f\right]\right|\leq\varepsilon,

where
g|_{P}
represents the restriction of
g
to the part
P
. For notational simplicity, we define the irregularity of
g
on
P
as

\operatorname{irr}_{g}(P)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{P}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(g|_{P}-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}])\cdot f\right]\right|.

For every positive integer
M
, we choose a subclass of the partitions of
X
with at most
M
parts. Its elements will be called admissible partitions, and the subclass will be denoted by
\mathsf{Part}_{M}(X)
. A
refinement scheme
consists of a growth function
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and a refinement function
\psi
that, given a partition
\mathcal{P}\in\mathsf{Part}_{M}(X)
and a family of witnesses
\{f_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
with
f_{P}\in\mathcal{F}_{P}
for each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, returns a refinement
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\psi(\mathcal{P},\{f_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}})\in\mathsf{Part}_{\Phi(M)}(X)
of
\mathcal{P}
.

In this work, we assume that the chosen witnesses become measurable with respect to the refinement, i.e., if
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\psi(\mathcal{P},\{f_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}})
, then for each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, the function
f_{P}
is constant on every part of the restricted partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|_{P}
.

This is the distinctive feature of our framework. By isolating the measurability of the chosen witnesses with respect to the refinement as the essential hypothesis, one obtains an abstract regularity lemma that is at once simple, yet general.

With this notation in hand, we can state the unified abstract regularity lemma.

Theorem 2.1

(Unified abstract regularity lemma)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a normalized finitely additive measure space. Let
g:X\to[0,1]
be an
\mathcal{A}
-measurable function, let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
\mathcal{P}_{0}\in\mathsf{Part}_{M_{0}}(X)
. Then there exists an integer
t\leq\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil
and a partition
\mathcal{P}_{t}\in\mathsf{Part}_{\Phi^{\circ t}(M_{0})}(X)
which is
\varepsilon
-regular for
g
, where
\Phi^{\circ t}
denotes the
t
-fold composition of
\Phi
with itself.

Proof.

For a partition
\mathcal{P}
, define the energy of
g
by

\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{P})=\|\mathbb{E}[g\mid\mathcal{P}]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mu(P)\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}]\bigr)^{2}.

The energy always lies between
0
and
1
, since
0\leq\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{P})\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[g^{2}]\leq 1
. We first record the energy increment estimate.

Claim.
Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a partition that refines
\mathcal{P}
. Suppose that, for each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, the function
f_{P}:P\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies
\|f_{P}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and is constant on every part of
\mathcal{Q}|_{P}
. Then

\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{Q})-\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{P})\geq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mu(P)\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(g|_{P}-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}])\cdot f_{P}\right]\right|^{2}.

Proof of the claim.
Since
\mathcal{Q}
refines
\mathcal{P}
, we can write

\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{Q})=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mu(P)\|\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}\mid\mathcal{Q}|_{P}]\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}.

For
P\in\mathcal{P}
, define

h_{P}=\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}\mid\mathcal{Q}|_{P}]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}]
.
Then
\mathbb{E}_{P}[h_{P}]=0
, and hence

\|\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}\mid\mathcal{Q}|_{P}]\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}=\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}]\bigr)^{2}+\|h_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}.

Summing up, we obtain

\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{Q})-\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mu(P)\|h_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}.

Because
f_{P}
is constant on the parts of
\mathcal{Q}|_{P}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(g|_{P}-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}])\cdot f_{P}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[h_{P}f_{P}].

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}[h_{P}f_{P}]\right|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[|h_{P}|^{2}]\,\mathbb{E}_{P}[|f_{P}|^{2}]\leq\|h_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}.

Multiplying by
\mu(P)
and summing over all
P\in\mathcal{P}
proves the claim.
\square

We now run the energy increment procedure. Starting from
\mathcal{P}_{0}
, suppose that
\mathcal{P}_{s}
has been constructed. If
\mathcal{P}_{s}
is
\varepsilon
-regular for
g
, we stop. Otherwise,

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}}\mu(P)\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{P}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(g|_{P}-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}])\cdot f\right]\right|>\varepsilon.

Since
\mathcal{P}_{s}
is finite, we may choose witnesses
f_{P}\in\mathcal{F}_{P}
so that

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}}\mu(P)\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(g|_{P}-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}])\cdot f_{P}\right]\right|>\varepsilon.

Set
\mathcal{P}_{s+1}=\psi(\mathcal{P}_{s},\{f_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}})
. By the defining property of the refinement scheme, each
f_{P}
is constant on the parts of
\mathcal{P}_{s+1}|_{P}
. The claim then gives

\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{P}_{s+1})-\mathcal{E}_{g}(\mathcal{P}_{s})\geq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}}\mu(P)\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(g|_{P}-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}])\cdot f_{P}\right]\right|^{2}.

Since
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}}\mu(P)=1
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}}\mu(P)\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(g|_{P}-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}])\cdot f_{P}\right]\right|^{2}\geq\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}}\mu(P)\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(g|_{P}-\mathbb{E}_{P}[g|_{P}])\cdot f_{P}\right]\right|\right)^{2}>\varepsilon^{2}.

Thus, each non-regular step increases the energy by more than
\varepsilon^{2}
. Since the energy is bounded above by
1
, the procedure stops after at most
t\leq\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil
steps. Applying the growth function at each step gives the bound
\mathcal{P}_{t}\in\mathsf{Part}_{\Phi^{\circ t}(M_{0})}(X)
, with a final cardinality at most
\Phi^{\circ\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil}(M_{0})
.
∎

3.
Deducing Green’s arithmetic regularity lemma

Fix a prime
p
and let
X=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, equipped with the uniform probability
measure. For
\xi,x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, set

\langle\xi,x\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}x_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

and define

\chi_{\xi}(x)=\omega^{\langle\xi,x\rangle}
, where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/p}
.
Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, and apply

2.1
to the function
g=\mathbf{1}_{A}
.

If
H\leq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
is a subspace, write
\mathcal{P}_{H}
for the partition of

\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
into cosets of
H
, and write

H^{\perp}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}:\langle\xi,h\rangle=0\text{ for every }h\in H\}.

For a coset
P=a+H
, we identify
P
with
H
by translation. We then take as
local test functions

\mathcal{F}_{a+H}=\{0\}\cup\left\{x\mapsto\overline{\chi_{\xi}(x-a)}:\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\setminus H^{\perp}\right\}.

The irregularity of
A
on the coset
a+H
is exactly the largest
non-trivial Fourier coefficient of the balanced restriction of
\mathbf{1}_{A}
to
that coset

\operatorname{irr}_{\mathbf{1}_{A}}(a+H)=\max_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\setminus H^{\perp}}\left|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\limits_{x\in a+H}\left[\left(\mathbf{1}_{A}(x)-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\limits_{y\in a+H}\mathbf{1}_{A}(y)\right)\overline{\chi_{\xi}(x-a)}\right]\right|,

with the convention that this maximum is
0
when
H=\{0\}
.

The refinement scheme is the usual Fourier refinement. Suppose that, for each
coset
a+H
, we have chosen a witness frequency

\xi_{a+H}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\setminus H^{\perp}
, where the zero witness imposes no
condition. Define

H^{\prime}=\left\{h\in H:\chi_{\xi_{a+H}}(h)=1\text{ for every chosen non-zero witness }\xi_{a+H}\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{P}_{H^{\prime}}
refines
\mathcal{P}_{H}
. Moreover, every chosen witness is
constant on each coset of
H^{\prime}
inside its ambient coset
a+H
. Therefore, the measurability condition required in
2.1
is satisfied.

It remains only to record the growth of the refinement. If

\operatorname{codim}H=r
, then
\mathcal{P}_{H}
has
p^{r}
parts, and the above
construction adds at most one independent linear condition for each coset of

H
. Hence,

\operatorname{codim}H^{\prime}\leq r+p^{r}.

Equivalently, if
\mathcal{P}_{H}
has at most
M
parts, then

\mathcal{P}_{H^{\prime}}
has at most

p^{\lceil\log_{p}M\rceil+M}
parts. This gives an admissible refinement
scheme with a growth function depending only on
p
.

We now recover the usual form of Green’s arithmetic regularity lemma.

Corollary 3.1

(Green’s arithmetic regularity lemma
[
Gre05
]
)
.

For every prime
p
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there is an integer

M=M(p,\varepsilon)
such that the following holds. For every
n
and every

A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, there is a subspace

H\leq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
of codimension at most
M
such that all but at most an

\varepsilon
-fraction of the cosets
a+H
satisfy

\max_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\setminus H^{\perp}}\left|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\limits_{x\in a+H}\left[\left(\mathbf{1}_{A}(x)-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\limits_{y\in a+H}\mathbf{1}_{A}(y)\right)\overline{\chi_{\xi}(x-a)}\right]\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Start from the trivial coset partition
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}
. The
refinement scheme described above stays inside the class of coset partitions.
Hence,
2.1
yields a subspace

H\leq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, of codimension bounded only in terms of
p
and
\eta
,
such that the partition
\mathcal{P}_{H}
is
\eta
-regular for

\mathbf{1}_{A}
. Unwinding the definition of regularity, we obtain the following
result. For every prime
p
and every
\eta>0
, there is an integer

M=M(p,\eta)
such that the following holds. For every
n
and every

A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, there is a subspace
H\leq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
of
codimension at most
M
such that

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\limits_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}/H}\max_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\setminus H^{\perp}}\left|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\limits_{x\in a+H}\left[\left(\mathbf{1}_{A}(x)-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\limits_{y\in a+H}\mathbf{1}_{A}(y)\right)\overline{\chi_{\xi}(x-a)}\right]\right|\leq\eta.

Taking
\eta=\varepsilon^{2}
, it follows that

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\limits_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}/H}\operatorname{irr}_{\mathbf{1}_{A}}(a+H)\leq\varepsilon^{2}.

By Markov’s inequality, the fraction of cosets
a+H
for which

\operatorname{irr}_{\mathbf{1}_{A}}(a+H)>\varepsilon
is at most
\varepsilon
.
This is exactly the desired conclusion.
∎

4.
Deducing Szemerédi’s graph regularity lemma

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite simple graph and write
n=|V|
. We work on

X=V\times V
with the uniform probability measure, and we apply
Theorem
2.1
to the function
g(x,y)=\mathbf{1}_{E}(x,y)
, where edges are counted as ordered pairs. Thus
g(x,y)=1
if
xy\in E
,
and
g(x,y)=0
otherwise. The diagonal is irrelevant for the argument. If
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\}
is a partition of
V
, let
\mathcal{V}^{2}=\{V_{i}\times V_{j}:1\leq i,j\leq m\}
be the induced product partition of
V\times V
. For non-empty subsets

A,B\subseteq V
, define
e(A,B)=\left\lvert\{(a,b)\in A\times B:ab\in E\}\right\rvert
, and
d(A,B)=\frac{e(A,B)}{|A||B|}
.
On the part
V_{i}\times V_{j}
, we take as local test functions all rectangle
indicators:

\mathcal{F}_{V_{i}\times V_{j}}=\{\mathbf{1}_{S\times T}:S\subseteq V_{i},\ T\subseteq V_{j}\}.

For this choice, the irregularity on
V_{i}\times V_{j}
is

\operatorname{irr}_{g}(V_{i}\times V_{j})=\frac{1}{|V_{i}||V_{j}|}\max_{S\subseteq V_{i},\ T\subseteq V_{j}}\left\lvert e(S,T)-d(V_{i},V_{j})|S||T|\right\rvert.

Indeed,

\mathbb{E}_{V_{i}\times V_{j}}\left[(g-d(V_{i},V_{j}))\mathbf{1}_{S\times T}\right]=\frac{e(S,T)-d(V_{i},V_{j})|S||T|}{|V_{i}||V_{j}|}.

Therefore the
\eta
-regularity condition for the product partition

\mathcal{V}^{2}
becomes

\sum_{i,j}\max_{S\subseteq V_{i},\ T\subseteq V_{j}}\left\lvert e(S,T)-d(V_{i},V_{j})|S||T|\right\rvert\leq\eta n^{2}.

The refinement scheme is the usual common refinement. Suppose that, for every
ordered pair
(i,j)
, a witness rectangle
S_{ij}\times T_{ij}\subseteq V_{i}\times V_{j}
has been chosen. We refine each vertex class
V_{i}
by all
sets
S_{ij}
with
1\leq j\leq m
, and
T_{ji}
with
1\leq j\leq m
.
Equivalently, we replace
V_{i}
by the parts of the Boolean algebra generated
inside
V_{i}
by these at most
2m
subsets. The resulting vertex partition

\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
refines
\mathcal{V}
, and the product partition

(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{2}
refines
\mathcal{V}^{2}
. Moreover, every chosen rectangle
indicator
\mathbf{1}_{S_{ij}\times T_{ij}}
is constant on each part of

(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{2}|_{V_{i}\times V_{j}}
, exactly as required by the abstract
refinement scheme.

If
\mathcal{V}
has
m
parts, then each
V_{i}
is split into at most

2^{2m}
parts. Hence
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
has at most
m2^{2m}
parts, and the product partition has at most
m^{2}2^{4m}
parts. Thus, this is an admissible refinement scheme with a growth function depending only on the
current number of parts.

We now recover the usual form of Szemerédi’s graph regularity lemma.

Corollary 4.1

(Szemerédi’s graph regularity lemma
[
Sze78
]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there is an integer
M=M(\varepsilon)
such that every finite
graph
G=(V,E)
admits a partition

V=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}

with
1\leq m\leq M
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq m\\
(V_{i},V_{j})\text{ is not }\varepsilon\text{-regular}\end{subarray}}|V_{i}||V_{j}|\leq\varepsilon|V|^{2}.

Proof.

Start from the trivial partition
V_{1}=V
and apply

2.1
to the induced product partition of

V\times V
, with the rectangle test families and refinement scheme described
above. The final product partition is induced by a vertex partition

\mathcal{V}=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\}
, where
m
is bounded only in terms of

\eta
. Unwinding the definition of abstract regularity, we obtain the
following result. For every
\eta>0
, there is an integer
M=M(\eta)
such
that every finite graph
G=(V,E)
admits a partition

V=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}
,
1\leq m\leq M
, satisfying

\sum_{i,j}\max_{S\subseteq V_{i},\ T\subseteq V_{j}}\left\lvert e(S,T)-d(V_{i},V_{j})|S||T|\right\rvert\leq\eta|V|^{2}.

We now relate this averaged formulation to the usual pairwise notion of
regularity. Recall that a pair
(A,B)
is
\varepsilon
-regular if, for all

S\subseteq A
and
T\subseteq B
with

|S|\geq\varepsilon|A|
,
|T|\geq\varepsilon|B|
, one has

\left\lvert d(S,T)-d(A,B)\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon.
If
(V_{i},V_{j})
is not
\varepsilon
-regular, then there are
S\subseteq V_{i}
and
T\subseteq V_{j}
such that

|S|\geq\varepsilon|V_{i}|
,
|T|\geq\varepsilon|V_{j}|
, and
\left\lvert d(S,T)-d(V_{i},V_{j})\right\rvert>\varepsilon
.
Equivalently,

\left\lvert e(S,T)-d(V_{i},V_{j})|S||T|\right\rvert>\varepsilon|S||T|\geq\varepsilon^{3}|V_{i}||V_{j}|.

Let
\eta=\varepsilon^{4}
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be the set of ordered pairs
(i,j)
for which
(V_{i},V_{j})
is not
\varepsilon
-regular. We have that

\varepsilon^{3}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{B}}|V_{i}||V_{j}|\leq\sum_{i,j}\max_{S\subseteq V_{i},\ T\subseteq V_{j}}\left\lvert e(S,T)-d(V_{i},V_{j})|S||T|\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon^{4}|V|^{2}.

Dividing by
\varepsilon^{3}
and recalling that
\eta=\varepsilon^{4}
, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq m\\
(V_{i},V_{j})\text{ is not }\varepsilon\text{-regular}\end{subarray}}|V_{i}||V_{j}|\leq\varepsilon|V|^{2}.

∎

5.
Deducing the regularity lemma for Boolean functions

For a finite set
I
and
p\in(0,1)
, let
\mu_{p}^{I}
denote the

p
-biased product measure on
\{0,1\}^{I}
, that is,

\mu_{p}^{I}(\{z\})=\prod_{i\in I}p^{z_{i}}(1-p)^{1-z_{i}}.

When the set of coordinates is clear, we simply write
\mu_{p}
.

If
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to[0,1]
, we write
\mu_{p}(f)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{p}^{[n]}}f(x)
. If
T\subseteq[n]
and
z\in\{0,1\}^{T}
, then
f_{T\to z}
denotes the
restriction of
f
obtained by fixing the coordinates in
T
according to

z
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
. A function

f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to[0,1]
is called
(r,\varepsilon)
-
quasirandom with
respect to
p
if, for every
R\subseteq[n]
with
|R|\leq r
and every

w\in\{0,1\}^{R}
,

\left\lvert\mu_{p}(f_{R\to w})-\mu_{p}(f)\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon.

More generally, if
T\subseteq[n]
and
z\in\{0,1\}^{T}
, we say that

f_{T\to z}
is
(r,\varepsilon)
-quasirandom if, for every

R\subseteq[n]\setminus T
with
|R|\leq r
and every

w\in\{0,1\}^{R}
,

\left\lvert\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z,R\to w})-\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z})\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon.

Fix
p\in(0,1)
and put
X=\{0,1\}^{n}
,
\mu=\mu_{p}^{[n]}
,
g=f
. For
T\subseteq[n]
, let
\mathcal{P}_{T}
be the partition of
\{0,1\}^{n}

into the parts
P_{z}=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:x_{T}=z\}
with
z\in\{0,1\}^{T}.
On the part
P_{z}
we take as test functions all indicators of subcubes
obtained by fixing at most
r
further coordinates:

\mathcal{F}_{P_{z}}=\{0\}\cup\Bigl\{\mathbf{1}_{\{x_{R}=w\}}\bigm|_{P_{z}}:R\subseteq[n]\setminus T,\ |R|\leq r,\ w\in\{0,1\}^{R}\Bigr\}.

Here
\mathbf{1}_{\{x_{R}=w\}}\bigm|_{P_{z}}
is viewed as a function on
P_{z}
. For such a test function we have

\mathbb{E}_{P_{z}}\Bigl[\bigl(g-\mathbb{E}_{P_{z}}g\bigr)\mathbf{1}_{\{x_{R}=w\}}\Bigr]=\mu_{p}^{R}(w)\Bigl(\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z,R\to w})-\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z})\Bigr).

Indeed, after conditioning on
x_{T}=z
, the remaining coordinates still
have the
p
-biased product distribution. Therefore

\operatorname{irr}_{g}(P_{z})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\subseteq[n]\setminus T\\
|R|\leq r\end{subarray}}\ \sup_{w\in\{0,1\}^{R}}\mu_{p}^{R}(w)\left\lvert\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z,R\to w})-\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z})\right\rvert.

The refinement scheme is also immediate. Suppose that, for each part

P_{z}
, a witness has been chosen. If the witness is non-zero, it is of
the form
\mathbf{1}_{\{x_{R_{z}}=w_{z}\}}
with
R_{z}\subseteq[n]\setminus T
and

|R_{z}|\leq r
. Define

T^{\prime}=T\cup\bigcup_{z\in\{0,1\}^{T}}R_{z},

where zero witnesses contribute nothing. Then
\mathcal{P}_{T^{\prime}}
refines

\mathcal{P}_{T}
, and every chosen witness is constant on every part of

\mathcal{P}_{T^{\prime}}
contained in the corresponding part of
\mathcal{P}_{T}
.
Moreover,
|T^{\prime}|\leq|T|+r2^{|T|}
. If one parametrizes admissible partitions by their number of parts, this
gives the growth bound

\Phi_{r}(M)=\left\lceil M2^{rM}\right\rceil,

because a partition fixing at most
\lfloor\log_{2}M\rfloor
coordinates
has at most
M
parts, and after refinement it has at most

M2^{rM}
parts.

Corollary 5.1

(Regularity lemma for Boolean functions)
.

For every
r\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\varepsilon,\zeta,\delta>0
, there is an
integer
J=J(r,\varepsilon,\zeta,\delta)
such that the following holds. Let

\zeta<p<\frac{1}{2}-\zeta
, and let

f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
. Then there is a set of coordinates

T\subseteq[n]
with
|T|\leq J
such that

\Pr_{z\sim\mu_{p}^{T}}\left[f_{T\to z}\text{ is not }(r,\varepsilon)\text{-quasirandom with respect to }p\right]\leq\delta.

Proof.

Apply
2.1
to the partitions

\mathcal{P}_{T}
and to the test families described above, with regularity
parameter
\eta=\delta\,\zeta^{r}\varepsilon
. This gives a coordinate set
T
of size at most a constant
J=J(r,\varepsilon,\zeta,\delta)
, independent of
n
, such that
\sum_{z\in\{0,1\}^{T}}\mu_{p}^{T}(z)\operatorname{irr}_{f}(P_{z})\leq\eta
.

Let
B=\{z\in\{0,1\}^{T}:f_{T\to z}\text{ is not }(r,\varepsilon)\text{-quasirandom}\}
. We claim that every
z\in B
satisfies

\operatorname{irr}_{f}(P_{z})>\zeta^{r}\varepsilon
. Indeed, if
z\in B
, then there exist

R\subseteq[n]\setminus T
with
|R|\leq r
and

w\in\{0,1\}^{R}
such that

\left\lvert\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z,R\to w})-\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z})\right\rvert>\varepsilon.

Since
\zeta<p<\frac{1}{2}-\zeta
, every part of the
p
-biased measure on

R
has measure at least
\zeta^{|R|}\geq\zeta^{r}
. Hence

\operatorname{irr}_{f}(P_{z})\geq\mu_{p}^{R}(w)\left\lvert\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z,R\to w})-\mu_{p}(f_{T\to z})\right\rvert>\zeta^{r}\varepsilon.

Therefore

\delta\zeta^{r}\varepsilon=\eta\geq\sum_{z\in\{0,1\}^{T}}\mu_{p}^{T}(z)\operatorname{irr}_{f}(P_{z})\geq\sum_{z\in B}\mu_{p}^{T}(z)\operatorname{irr}_{f}(P_{z})>\mu_{p}^{T}(B)\zeta^{r}\varepsilon.

Cancelling
\zeta^{r}\varepsilon
gives
\mu_{p}^{T}(B)\leq\delta
, which is the desired
conclusion.
∎

Appendix A
Deducing the Bollobás–Nikiforov abstract regularity lemma

We first recall the setup. Let
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu,\mathcal{S})
be an
SR-system in the sense of Bollobás and Nikiforov:
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)
is a
normalized measure triple and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is a semiring. Further, assume that there is an integer
r\geq 1
such that, whenever

S,T\in\mathcal{S}
, the difference
S\setminus T
can be written as a
disjoint union of at most
r
members of
\mathcal{S}
. If
A,V\in\mathcal{A}

and
\mu(V)>0
, write

d(A,V)=\frac{\mu(A\cap V)}{\mu(V)}.

Following Bollobás and Nikiforov, if
V\in\mathcal{S}
has positive measure,
we say that
A
is
\varepsilon
-
regular in

V
if

\left\lvert d(A,U)-d(A,V)\right\rvert<\varepsilon,

for every
U\in\mathcal{S}
with
U\subseteq V
and

\mu(U)>\varepsilon\mu(V)
. If
\mathcal{P}
is a finite partition of
X
into
members of
\mathcal{S}
, we say that
A
is
\varepsilon
-
regular in

\mathcal{P}
if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
A\text{ is not }\varepsilon\text{-regular in }P\end{subarray}}\mu(P)<\varepsilon.

We shall also use the notion of a bounding family of partitions. Let

\Pi(\mathcal{S})
denote the set of finite partitions of
X
into members of

\mathcal{S}
. A family
\Phi\subseteq\Pi(\mathcal{S})

bounds

\Pi(\mathcal{S})
if there is an increasing function

\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that, for every

\mathcal{P}\in\Pi(\mathcal{S})
, there exists

\mathcal{Q}\in\Phi
refining
\mathcal{P}
with

|\mathcal{Q}|\leq\varphi(|\mathcal{P}|)
.

We now recover the abstract regularity lemma of Bollobás and Nikiforov.

Corollary A.1

(Bollobás–Nikiforov abstract regularity lemma)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu,\mathcal{S})
be an SR-system, and let

\Phi\subseteq\Pi(\mathcal{S})
be a family of partitions bounding

\Pi(\mathcal{S})
with rate
\varphi
. Let

\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
be a finite family of measurable sets, let

\mathcal{P}\in\Pi(\mathcal{S})
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there is an integer

q=q(\varepsilon,|\mathcal{L}|,|\mathcal{P}|)
and a partition

\mathcal{Q}\in\Phi
such that

\mathcal{Q}
refines
\mathcal{P}
,
|\mathcal{Q}|\leq q,
and every
A\in\mathcal{L}
is
\varepsilon
-regular in
\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
\ell=|\mathcal{L}|
. If
\ell=0
there is nothing to prove, so assume

\ell\geq 1
. Since
\Phi
bounds
\Pi(\mathcal{S})
, choose first a partition

\mathcal{P}_{0}\in\Phi
refining
\mathcal{P}
with
|\mathcal{P}_{0}|\leq\varphi(|\mathcal{P}|)
. We apply the proof of
2.1
simultaneously to the
functions
\mathbf{1}_{A}
,
A\in\mathcal{L}
. For a part
P
of a partition

\mathcal{R}\in\Phi
, take as local test functions

\mathcal{F}_{P}=\{0\}\cup\{\mathbf{1}_{U}|_{P}:U\in\mathcal{S},\ U\subseteq P\}.

For
A\in\mathcal{L}
and
P\in\mathcal{R}
, the corresponding local
irregularity is

\operatorname{irr}_{A}(P)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\in\mathcal{S}\\
U\subseteq P\end{subarray}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{A}-d(A,P)\bigr)\mathbf{1}_{U}\right]\right|=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\in\mathcal{S}\\
U\subseteq P\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(U)}{\mu(P)}\left\lvert d(A,U)-d(A,P)\right\rvert.

The only point to check is that the refinement scheme required by

2.1
is available inside the family
\Phi
. Suppose
that
\mathcal{R}\in\Phi
has
m
parts. For each pair

(A,P)\in\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{R}
, choose a witness

U_{A,P}\in\mathcal{S}
with
U_{A,P}\subseteq P
. Inside a fixed part
P
,
we refine by all the sets
U_{A,P}
,
A\in\mathcal{L}
. Since

\mathcal{S}
is
r
-built, refining one member of
\mathcal{S}
by one
semiring subset splits it into at most
r+1
members of
\mathcal{S}
.
Iterating this for the
\ell
witnesses inside
P
, we obtain a partition of

P
into at most
(r+1)^{\ell}
members of
\mathcal{S}
on which all the
indicators
\mathbf{1}_{U_{A,P}}
are constant. Doing this for every

P\in\mathcal{R}
gives a partition
\mathcal{R}^{\ast}\in\Pi(\mathcal{S})
with

|\mathcal{R}^{\ast}|\leq m(r+1)^{\ell}
.
Now use the bounding property of
\Phi
to choose

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\in\Phi
refining
\mathcal{R}^{\ast}
with

|\mathcal{R}^{\prime}|\leq\varphi\bigl(m(r+1)^{\ell}\bigr).

Thus we have an admissible refinement scheme with growth function

\Psi(m)=\varphi\bigl(m(r+1)^{\ell}\bigr).

Run the energy-increment argument with the total energy

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{R})=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{L}}\left\|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{A}\mid\mathcal{R}]\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}.

This energy lies between
0
and
\ell
. If

\sum_{A\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{R}}\mu(P)\operatorname{irr}_{A}(P)>\eta,

then choose witnesses
U_{A,P}
and refine as above. The same computation as
in the proof of
2.1
gives

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{R})\geq\sum_{A\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{R}}\mu(P)\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{A}-d(A,P)\bigr)\mathbf{1}_{U_{A,P}}\right]\right|^{2}.

Since the weights
\mu(P)
sum to
1
for each fixed
A
, and there are

\ell
choices of
A
, Jensen’s inequality gives

\sum_{A\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{R}}\mu(P)\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{A}-d(A,P)\bigr)\mathbf{1}_{U_{A,P}}\right]\right|^{2}\geq\frac{\eta^{2}}{\ell}.

Thus every non-regular step increases the total energy by at least

\eta^{2}/\ell
. Since the total energy is at most
\ell
, the procedure
stops after at most

\left\lceil\frac{\ell^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right\rceil

steps. We obtain a partition
\mathcal{Q}\in\Phi
refining
\mathcal{P}_{0}
,
hence refining
\mathcal{P}
, with

|\mathcal{Q}|\leq\Psi^{\circ\left\lceil\frac{\ell^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right\rceil}\bigl(\varphi(|\mathcal{P}|)\bigr),

and satisfying

\sum_{A\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mu(Q)\operatorname{irr}_{A}(Q)\leq\eta.

It remains to translate this averaged test-function regularity into the
Bollobás–Nikiforov notion. Fix
A\in\mathcal{L}
, and let

\mathcal{B}_{A}
be the set of parts
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
in which
A
is not

\varepsilon
-regular. For each
Q\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, there is a set

U\in\mathcal{S}
,
U\subseteq Q
, such that

\mu(U)>\varepsilon\mu(Q)
and
\left\lvert d(A,U)-d(A,Q)\right\rvert>\varepsilon
.
Therefore

\operatorname{irr}_{A}(Q)\geq\frac{\mu(U)}{\mu(Q)}\left\lvert d(A,U)-d(A,Q)\right\rvert>\varepsilon^{2}.

Hence

\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{A}}\mu(Q)<\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{A}}\mu(Q)\operatorname{irr}_{A}(Q)\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mu(Q)\operatorname{irr}_{A}(Q)\leq\eta.

Taking
\eta=\varepsilon^{3}
gives

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{A}}\mu(Q)<\varepsilon
. Thus every
A\in\mathcal{L}
is
\varepsilon
-regular in
\mathcal{Q}
. Finally, with
\eta=\varepsilon^{3}
, one may take

q(\varepsilon,\ell,|\mathcal{P}|)=\Psi^{\circ\left\lceil\ell^{2}\varepsilon^{-6}\right\rceil}\bigl(\varphi(|\mathcal{P}|)\bigr)
, and

\Psi(m)=\varphi\bigl(m(r+1)^{\ell}\bigr)
.
∎

Appendix B
Deducing Tao’s probabilistic regularity lemma

We shall use the following harmless variant of
2.1
:
the function
g:X\to[0,1]
may be replaced by a real-valued function

X\in L^{2}(\mu)
with
\|X\|_{2}\leq 1
. The proof is unchanged, since the
only point at which boundedness is used is the energy bound

0\leq\|\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{P}]\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|X\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1.

We identify a finite sub-
\sigma
-algebra with its finite partition into
parts. If
\mathcal{B}
is a finite sub-
\sigma
-algebra, write

\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B})
for the least number of events needed
to generate it. Thus
\mathcal{B}
has at most

2^{\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B})}
parts. If

(\mathcal{B}_{i})_{i\in I}
is a finite family of finite sub-
\sigma
-algebras,
write

\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}

for their common refinement. We first isolate the single-level regularization statement supplied by

2.1
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_{\max},\mu)
be a probability space, let

(\mathcal{B}_{i,\max})_{i\in I}
be a finite family of sub-
\sigma
-algebras
of
\mathcal{B}_{\max}
, and let
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\max})
satisfy

\|X\|_{2}\leq 1
. Let
(\mathcal{B}_{i})_{i\in I}
be finite sub-
\sigma
-algebras
with

\mathcal{B}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}
and
\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{i})\leq M

for all
i\in I
. Then, for every
\delta>0
, there are finite
sub-
\sigma
-algebras

\mathcal{B}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}

such that

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(X-\mathbb{E}\left[X\mid\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}\right]\right)\prod_{i\in I}1_{A_{i}}\right]\right|\leq\delta

for every choice of events
A_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}
. Moreover, the complexities of the
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}
are bounded in terms of

M,\delta
, and
|I|
only.

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{P}=\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}

be the joint part partition. For a part
P\in\mathcal{P}
, take as local
test functions

\mathcal{F}_{P}=\left\{\left.\prod_{i\in I}1_{A_{i}}\right|_{P}\,:\,A_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}\right\}.

These functions are bounded by
1
.

We now describe the refinement scheme. Suppose that, for each part

P\in\mathcal{P}
, a witness

f_{P}=\left.\prod_{i\in I}1_{A_{i}(P)}\right|_{P}

has been chosen, with
A_{i}(P)\in\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}
. Refine each

\mathcal{B}_{i}
by adjoining all the events
A_{i}(P)
, as
P
ranges over
the parts of
\mathcal{P}
. Denote the resulting algebra by

\mathcal{B}_{i}^{+}
. Then

\mathcal{B}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{i}^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}.

Moreover, every
f_{P}
is measurable with respect to

\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{+}
inside
P
, so the measurability condition
in
2.1
is satisfied.

If
\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{i})\leq M
for every
i
, then

\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}
has at most
2^{|I|M}
parts. Hence each

\mathcal{B}_{i}
is refined by adjoining at most
2^{|I|M}
new events, and
so

\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{+})\leq M+2^{|I|M}.

Thus we have an admissible refinement scheme with a growth function depending
only on
|I|
.

Applying
2.1
with parameter
\delta
, starting
from
\bigvee_{i}\mathcal{B}_{i}
, gives finite refinements

\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathcal{B}_{i}
such that, if

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},

then

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\mu(P)\sup_{A_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\left(X-\mathbb{E}_{P}X\right)\prod_{i\in I}1_{A_{i}}\right]\right|\leq\delta.

Now fix arbitrary
A_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}
. Since

\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{P}^{\prime}]
is equal to
\mathbb{E}_{P}X
on each part
P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
,
we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(X-\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{P}^{\prime}]\right)\prod_{i\in I}1_{A_{i}}\right]\right|\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\mu(P)\left|\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\left(X-\mathbb{E}_{P}X\right)\prod_{i\in I}1_{A_{i}}\right]\right|\leq\delta.

This proves the desired product-test regularity. The complexity bound follows
by iterating the growth function for at most
\lceil\delta^{-2}\rceil
steps.
∎

We now recover Tao’s theorem.

Corollary B.2
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_{\max},\mu)
be a probability space, let

(\mathcal{B}_{i,\max})_{i\in I}
be a finite family of sub-
\sigma
-algebras
of
\mathcal{B}_{\max}
, and let
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{\max})
satisfy

\|X\|_{2}\leq 1
. Let
\varepsilon>0
, let
m\geq 0
, and let

F:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be increasing. Then there are finite
sub-
\sigma
-algebras

\mathcal{B}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}
,
i\in I
,
and a number
M=O_{\varepsilon,F,m,|I|}(1)
, such that

M\geq m
,
\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{i})\leq M

for every
i\in I
,

\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[X\mid\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[X\mid\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}\right]\right\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon,

and, for every choice of events
A_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}
,

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(X-\mathbb{E}\left[X\mid\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}\right]\right)\prod_{i\in I}1_{A_{i}}\right]\right|\leq\frac{1}{F(M)}.

Proof.

We run a coarse/fine energy-increment procedure. Begin for every
i\in I
, with the trivial
algebras

\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(0)}=\{\emptyset,\Omega\}
.
Suppose that the current coarse algebras

(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s)})_{i\in I}
have been constructed, and put

M_{s}=\max\left\{m,\max_{i\in I}\operatorname{complex}(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s)})\right\}.

Apply
B.1
to the coarse algebras

(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s)})_{i\in I}
with

\delta_{s}=\frac{1}{F(M_{s})}.

This gives refinements

\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s)}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\mathrm{fine}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}

such that, for every
A_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i,\max}
,

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(X-\mathbb{E}\left[X\mid\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\mathrm{fine}}\right]\right)\prod_{i\in I}1_{A_{i}}\right]\right|\leq\frac{1}{F(M_{s})}.

If

\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[X\mid\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\mathrm{fine}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[X\mid\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s)}\right]\right\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon,

we stop and set

\mathcal{B}_{i}=\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s)},\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\mathrm{fine}},M=M_{s}.

The required conclusions then hold.

Otherwise, set

\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s+1)}=\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\mathrm{fine}}

and continue. We claim that this can happen at most
\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil

times. Indeed, write

\mathcal{C}_{s}=\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s)}
and
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{fine}}=\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\mathrm{fine}}.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{fine}}
refines
\mathcal{C}_{s}
, orthogonality
of conditional expectation gives

\left\|\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{fine}}]\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{C}_{s}]\right\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{fine}}]-\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{C}_{s}]\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus every non-stopping step increases the energy by more than
\varepsilon^{2}
.
But the energy is always at most
\|X\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1
, so there are at most

\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil
non-stopping steps.

It remains only to bound the final value of
M
. By

B.1
, the complexity of the fine algebras
constructed from coarse complexity at most
M_{s}
is bounded by a quantity
depending only on
M_{s}
,
F(M_{s})
, and
|I|
. Hence there is an increasing
function
\Theta=\Theta_{F,|I|}
such that

M_{s+1}\leq\Theta(M_{s})

whenever the procedure does not stop. Since the procedure has at most

\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil
non-stopping steps and
M_{0}=m
, the final value
satisfies

M\leq\Theta^{\circ\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil}(m).

Therefore
M=O_{\varepsilon,F,m,|I|}(1)
, as required.
∎
