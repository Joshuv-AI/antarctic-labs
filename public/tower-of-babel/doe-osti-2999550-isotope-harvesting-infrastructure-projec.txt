1.) Overview

The Isotope Harvesting Project (IHP) at the Facility for Rare Isotope Beams (FRIB) was
undertaken to establish first-of-its-kind infrastructure necessary to capture and process
otherwise unused radionuclides produced in the water-filled beam dump during accelerator
operations. These radionuclides, created as by-products of FRIB’s heavy-ion fragmentation
reactions, represent a critical and otherwise unused resource for advancing research in
medicine, biochemistry, materials science, plant and soil sciences, nuclear physics,
astrophysics, and national security applications.

In alignment with recommendations from the Nuclear Science Advisory Committee’s
Isotope Subcommittee (NSAC-I), this project integrated Isotope Harvesting Vault (IHV) into
FRIB’s water filled beam dump and constructed a dedicated laboratory for radiochemical
processing. Over a four-year period, the project successfully delivered laboratory outfitting,
beam-dump connections, and established advanced infrastructure capabilities to safely
handle high level of radioactivity.

The resulting infrastructure enables parallel operation with FRIB’s primary nuclear physics
mission, ensuring that valuable radionuclides can be routinely harvested and provided to the
broader scientific community. The new IHV is designed to run safely and efficiently. It has
hot cells tied directly into the beam dump, QA/QC tools, radiation monitoring, and
automated systems that reduce handling and exposure of radioactive samples. The
successful completion of this project fulfills a critical accelerator improvement milestone,
ensures access to radionuclides currently in short supply or otherwise unavailable, and
establishes a foundation for sustained interdisciplinary collaborations that will extend the
scientific impact of FRIB for decades to come.

While FRIB’s primary focus remains on supporting nuclear physics experiments, with IHV
infrastructure now in place, FRIB is in a position to broaden its impact and will create
opportunities for applied research.

3.) IHP Scope Baseline and Technical Performance

The Isotope Harvesting Infrastructure Project identified several Key Performance Parameters
(KPPs) for demonstrating successful delivery of the project. Those KPPs, along with notes
about their completion, are tabulated here.

 1. Shielding
 KPP Purpose: The isotope harvesting hot cells are required to be able to safely shield
 radiation associated with the UPP (ultimate performance parameter) of holding 5 Ci
 of 47Ca within the high-rad (8” thick) hot cells, and 1 Ci 47Sc within the low-rad (3”
 thick) hot cells. This KPP shows that the isotope harvesting hot cells can safely
 contain the required radiation fields.
 KPP Requirement: Demonstrate that the hot cells attenuate gamma radiation as
 designed.
 KPP Action Taken: 13 Ci 60Co (high-rad) or 13 Ci 192Ir (low-rad) were placed inside of
 each hot cell, and the radiation doses outside were measured at several locations,
 including around the manipulator penetrations, around the lead-glass windows,
 along the floors and roofs, and along all walls. 13 Ci 60Co has a high energy gamma
 radiation field that exceeds the 10 Ci 47Ca field, and 13 Ci 192Ir exceeds the field from
 1 Ci 47Sc. Testing was performed by an external vendor, Plexus, and witnessed by FRIB
      IHP personnel. 2/13/2024
 Result: COMPLETE. Dose rate measurements found no gaps in the shielding in any
 of the hot cells. Dose rates did not exceed regulatory requirements in any of the tested
 locations. The Plexus report is provided in the read-ahead materials for the IHP close-
 out review.

 appropriately.

8.) IHP Closeout Status

The Isotope Harvesting Program (IHP) infrastructure project underwent its closeout review
on December 3, 2024. The review was positively received by DOE-IP and the panel members
involved.

The project is now fully operational. All major infrastructure, including hot cells, radiation
monitoring systems, and supporting lab equipment, has been installed, tested, and verified
to meet the KPP specifications. The team is currently preparing the facility for device
readiness activities, which include final system checks, controls integration, verification,
and readiness assessments.

These commissioning preparations are essential to ensure that the facility can support
future harvesting experiments safely. This will directly contribute to the achievement of all
UPPs by providing a functional, fully integrated laboratory for isotope harvesting research
and routine operations.

9.) Outlook

Aforementioned KPPs demonstrate that IHP can safely operate at the Ultimate
Performance Parameters (UPPs) level i.e. harvesting 5 Ci of Ca- 47 to generate high-quality
Sc- 47. However, to realize this capability the following structured approach will be
followed:

To reach this capability, operations will progress toward the UPP level through a structured,
stepwise approach:

 • Transition to operations will first establish the Operational Safety Envelope (OSE).

 • The OSE will then be expanded under the functional safety program until the UPPs
 are met.

 • Each expansion will be tied to an Instrument Readiness Review (IRR) and aligned
 with FRIB’s increasing beam intensity.

 • This process follows FRIB’s New Work Plan and ensures that safety and
 infrastructure keep pace with performance goals.

The IHV safety basis was defined by the UPPs, and no barriers are anticipated in achieving
operations at that level.
