The asymptotic oscillations of moments related to Dirichlet series with missing digits
Jean-François Burnol

Abstract
. We prove that the (suitably rescaled) moments of certain discrete measures on the unit interval, which are related to the numerical evaluation of zeta series with missing digits in radix b b , are asymptotically 1 1 -periodic in the base b b logarithm of the index, i.e. asymptotically invariant under multiplication by b b of the index.

Introduction

The author introduced in
[
2

2.
A lemma on moments

This proposition is needed to establish Theorems
1
and

2
in the next section. It is probably classical (and may even
be a textbook exercise, at least for the case of a measure with a continuous
density function) but the author could not locate a reference.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mu
be a complex measure on (the Borel algebra of)
[0,1\rparen
, which
has the following Hölder property at
1
, for some
\sigma\geq 0
:

(
H_{\sigma}
)

\exists K<\infty\hskip 20.00003pt0\leq x<1\implies|\mu|\bigl([x,1\rparen\bigr)\leq K(1-x)^{\sigma}.

Let, for
m
real and non-negative

u_{m}=\int_{[0,1\rparen}x^{m}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

be
the (general) moments, and let

E(t)=\int_{[0,1\rparen}\mathsf{e}^{tx}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

be the
exponential generating function of the (integer) moments. There holds:

u_{m}=\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(m)+O_{m\to\infty}(\frac{1}{m^{1+\sigma}}).

Proof.

There holds

\Bigl|\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(m)-u_{m}\Bigr|\leq\int_{[0,1\rparen}\left|\mathsf{e}^{-m+mx}-x^{m}\right||\mu|(\mathrm{d}x)=\int_{[0,1\rparen}\left(\mathsf{e}^{-m+mx}-x^{m}\right)|\mu|(\mathrm{d}x),

as the integrand in the right-hand side is non-negative (even positive for

m>0
). We are thus reduced to the case of a finite positive measure.

Let
\mu
be such a positive measure, verifying (

H
σ

) for some
\sigma\geq 0
.
There holds
u_{m}=O(2^{-m})+\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1\rparen}x^{m}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)
and
\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(m)=\int_{[0,1\rparen}\mathsf{e}^{-m(1-x)}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=O(\mathsf{e}^{-m/2})+\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1\rparen}\mathsf{e}^{-m(1-x)}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)
so the claim is reduced to

(5)

\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1\rparen}\bigl(\mathsf{e}^{-m(1-x)}-x^{m}\bigr)\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=O(\frac{1}{m^{1+\sigma}}).

There holds for any positive real number
m
and
x\in[\frac{1}{2},1\rparen
:

(6)

0<\mathsf{e}^{-m(1-x)}-x^{m}<m(1-x)^{2}\mathsf{e}^{-m(1-x)}.

Let us give a proof for this textbook inequality.
Let
h=1-x
,
0<h\leq\frac{1}{2}
. We want

0<1-(1-h)^{m}\mathsf{e}^{mh}<mh^{2}.

The middle term is
1-e^{-y}
with

y=-m\log(1-h)-mh=m\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}h^{2}+\frac{1}{3}h^{3}+\dots\Bigr)<m\frac{h^{2}}{2(1-h)}\leq mh^{2}.

Hence
0<y<mh^{2}
and
0<1-\mathsf{e}^{-y}<y<mh^{2}
. This proves (
6
).

We are now reduced to show:

(7)

\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1\rparen}(1-x)^{2}\mathsf{e}^{-m(1-x)}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=O(\frac{1}{m^{2+\sigma}}).

Let
\psi_{m}(x)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left((1-x)^{2}\mathsf{e}^{-m(1-x)}\right)=\bigl(m(1-x)-2\bigr)(1-x)\mathsf{e}^{-m(1-x)}
.
Up to an exponentially small term, the left-hand-side of (
7
) is

\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1\rparen}\left((1-x)^{2}\mathsf{e}^{-m(1-x)}-\frac{\mathsf{e}^{-m/2}}{4}\right)\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1\rparen}\Bigl(\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{x}\psi_{m}(y)\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

Using Fubini and the hypothesis (

H
σ

):

\left|\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1\rparen}\Bigl(\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{x}\psi_{m}(y)\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right|=\left|\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\psi_{m}(y)\mu([y,1\rparen)\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq K\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}|\psi_{m}(y)|(1-y)^{\sigma}\mathrm{d}y.

The left-hand side of (
7
) is thus bounded by the sum of some

O(\mathsf{e}^{-m/2})
with

K\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}|mt-2|t^{1+\sigma}\mathsf{e}^{-mt}\mathrm{d}t=Km^{-2-\sigma}\int_{0}^{\frac{m}{2}}|u-2|u^{1+\sigma}\mathsf{e}^{-u}\mathrm{d}u,

and the proof of (
7
), hence of the Proposition, is complete.
∎

3.
Missing digits and associated moments

Let
b>1
be an integer and let
A\subset\{0,\dots,b-1\}
be a non-empty
subset of the set of radix-
b
digits, of cardinality
N
. Let
f=\max A
.
We assume
f>0
.

In order to simplify the presentation, we also assume provisorily:

f=b-1.

The additional discussion needed if
f<b-1
will be provided only at the end
of this section after the proof of Theorem
1
.

We say that a positive integer is
(b,A)
-admissible
, in short

admissible
if its radix-
b
representation uses only digits from
A
.
As zero is represented by the empty word, we consider it also to be
admissible (whether or not the digit
0
is in
A
).

We want to discuss further some properties of the moments

u_{m}(s)
which are defined in
[
3
, §2]
as

u_{m}(s)=\int_{[0,1)}x^{m}\mu_{s}(\mathrm{d}x),

where
\mu_{s}
is some complex measure and
s
is a complex number of real part
greater than
s_{0}=\log_{b}N
. The discrete complex measure
\mu_{s}
is defined
on the Borel algebra of
[0,1\rparen
in the following manner: first, we put a
Dirac unit mass at zero. Then we add Dirac masses with (complex) weights

b^{-s}
at each
ab^{-1}
,
a\in A
. Then we add Dirac masses with weights

b^{-2s}
at each
(a_{1}b+a_{2})b^{-2}
,
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{2}
, and so on with

A^{l}
,
l\to\infty
. This defines a complex measure whose zero-th moment

\mu_{s}\bigl([0,1\rparen\bigr)
is
b^{s}/(b^{s}-N)
(
[
3
, §2]
).
Indeed:

1+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{l})\in A^{l}}b^{-ls}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}N^{l}b^{-sl}=\frac{b^{s}}{b^{s}-N}\,.

We need the following:

Lemma 1
.

The complex measure
\mu_{s}
on
[0,1\rparen
verifies hypothesis (

H
σ

)
from Proposition
1
, with
\sigma=\Re s
.

Proof.

Let
k\geq 0
, we evaluate
\mu_{s}\bigl([1-b^{-k},1\rparen\bigr)
(we will need
the result only for
s
real, but it is worthwhile to prove it in general).
Recall that we assume
b-1\in A
. For
l\geq 0
and
(a_{1},\dots,a_{l})\in A^{l}

to contribute weight to the interval
[1-b^{-k},1\rparen
, it is necessary and
sufficient that

\frac{a_{1}}{b}+\dots+\frac{a_{l}}{b^{l}}\geq 1-b^{-k}=\frac{b-1}{b}+\dots+\frac{b-1}{b^{k}}\,.

This happens if and only if
l\geq k
and
a_{1}=\dots=a_{k}=b-1
. The
digits
a_{q}
for
k<q\leq l
are thus arbitrary in
A
. The weight
contributed by
(b-1,\dots,b-1,a_{k+1},\dots,a_{l})
is
b^{-ks}
times the
weight which
(a_{k+1},\dots,a_{l})
contributes to the mass of
[0,1\rparen
. So
we obtain;

(8)

\mu_{s}\bigl([1-b^{-k},1\rparen\bigr)=b^{-ks}\mu_{s}\bigl([0,1\rparen\bigr).

The measure of variations of
\mu_{s}
is
\mu_{\sigma}
with
\sigma=\Re s
.
Equation (
8
) applied with
s=\sigma
says that

\mu_{\sigma}\bigl([y_{k},1\rparen\bigr)=K(1-y_{k})^{\sigma}
for some
K
and any

y_{k}=1-b^{-k}
,
k\geq 0
. Let
0\leq x<1
and
k
be the largest such that

y_{k}\leq x
. Then
1-y_{k}\geq 1-x>\frac{1-y_{k}}{b}
, hence
\mu_{\sigma}\bigl([x,1\rparen\bigr)\leq K(1-y_{k})^{\sigma}\leq Kb^{\sigma}(1-x)^{\sigma}
, which completes the
check of (

H
σ

).
∎

We are interested into the moments of the measure
\mu_{s}
:

(9)

u_{m}(s)=0^{m}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{l})\in A^{l}}\Bigl(a_{1}b^{-1}+\dots+a_{l}b^{-l}\Bigr)^{m}b^{-ls}.

Their precise numerical evaluation is not easy, except if
\#A=N=1
, due,
if
N>1
, to exponentially many contributions to each term. This can be
mitigated to some extent, for integer moments, via the recurrence formula from

[
3
, Prop. 1]
:

(10)

m\geq 1\implies u_{m}(s)=\frac{1}{b^{m+s}-N}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\Bigl(\sum_{a\in A}a^{j}\Bigr)u_{m-j}(s).

The proof of the main theorem will not use this recurrence relation though.

Theorem 1
.

Let
B
be the set
f-A=\{b-1-a,a\in A\}
(we assume
\max A=b-1
) and let

\mathcal{B}
be the set of
B
-admissible (non-negative) integers. There holds for

m
real going to
+\infty
:

(11)

m^{s}u_{m}(s)=\underbrace{(\log b)^{-1}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathsf{e}^{2\pi ik\log_{b}(m)}\Gamma(s-2\pi i\frac{k}{\log b})\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{s-2\pi i\frac{k}{\log b}}}}_{F_{s}(m)}{}+O(\frac{1}{m})

which represents
m^{s}u_{m}(s)
as the sum of a smooth
1
-periodic function of

\log_{b}(m)
and an
O(m^{-1})
error term. So, the sequence of integer moments

(m^{s}u_{m}(s))_{m\geq 0}
is asymptotic to a sequence
(F_{s}(m))
invariant under

m\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mapsto$}}bm
, which can also be written as

(12)

F_{s}(m)=m^{s}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}b^{js}\mathsf{e}^{-b^{j}m}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\mathsf{e}^{-nb^{j}m}.

The asymptotic average value for the sequence of integer moments is:

(13)

\langle m^{s}u_{m}(s)\rangle\coloneq\lim_{p\to\infty}\sum_{b^{p}<m\leq b^{p+1}}m^{s}u_{m}(s)\log_{b}{\frac{m}{m-1}}=\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\log b}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{s}}\,.

Proof.

According to Lemma
1
and Proposition
1
,
m^{s}u_{m}(s)=m^{s}\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(m)+O(m^{-1})
, so the matter is reduced to the
asymptotic of
m^{s}\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(m)
. But this is exactly what was studied in

[
3
, §3, Prop. 7]
. To be self-contained we explain again
the simple steps. Define first

\alpha_{B}(t)=\sum_{d\in B}\mathsf{e}^{dt}.

Observe that
0<\alpha_{B}(t)\leq N
for
t\leq 0
.
The moment generating function is (for
m
real positive):

\displaystyle E(m)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{l})\in A^{l}}\mathsf{e}^{m(a_{1}b^{-1}+\dots+a_{l}b^{-l})}b^{-ls}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{l})\in B^{l}}\mathsf{e}^{m(1-b^{-l}-d_{1}b^{-1}-\dots-d_{l}b^{-l})}b^{-ls}

\displaystyle\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigl(\prod_{1\leq k\leq l}\alpha_{B}(-mb^{-k})\bigr)\mathsf{e}^{-mb^{-l}}b^{-ls}.

Note that for
m\geq 0
, this series is bounded term-wise by
N^{l}b^{-l\Re s}

(as it should be from where it originates from) and convergence is indeed
ensured. We now introduce the infinite product

\gamma_{B}(-t)=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{B}(-b^{i}t).

Here it is important that
0\in B
so
\alpha_{B}(-b^{i}t)=1+O(\mathsf{e}^{-b^{i}Dt})

where
D
is the smallest positive element of
B
(if one such exists, else

\alpha_{B}
is the constant
1
) and the product is absolutely convergent for

\Re t>0
. We will use it only with
t
real positive and certainly

\gamma_{B}(-t)=1+O_{t\to+\infty}(\mathsf{e}^{-Dt})
(or the constant
1
if

B=\{0\}
). Looking closer we recognize that the infinite product has a
series expansion in powers of
\mathsf{e}^{-t}
:

\gamma_{B}(-t)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\mathsf{e}^{-nt}

where
\mathcal{B}
is the set of
B
-admissible non-negative integers (which is reduced
to the singleton
\{0\}
if
B=\{0\}
, else has
D
as minimal positive element).
We then obtain the formula:

\gamma_{B}(-m)\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(m)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{B}(-mb^{-l})\mathsf{e}^{-mb^{-l}}b^{-ls}.

This leads to the definition of the
1
-periodic (in
\log_{b}(m)
) smooth function:

F_{s}(m)=m^{s}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma_{B}(-b^{-l}m)\mathsf{e}^{-mb^{-l}}b^{-ls},

where the added terms for negative
l
are exponentially small as functions of
m
.
Hence

m^{s}\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(m)=(1+O(\mathsf{e}^{-Dm}))\Bigl(F_{s}(m)-O(m^{\Re s}\mathsf{e}^{-mb})\Bigr).

So
m^{s}\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(m)
differs from
F_{s}(m)
, which is invariant under

m\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mapsto$}}bm
, by an exponentially small function of
m\to+\infty
. We can
represent
F_{s}(m)
as a Fourier series in the variable
\log_{b}(m)
. The
computation of the Fourier coefficients is given in the proof of
[
3
, Prop.
7]
and gives the underbraced expression in (
11
).

So we have established (
11
) and (
12
). As per the fact
that the middle term of (
13
) converges to the zeroth Fourier
coefficient whose value is given in (
11
) and gives the third term,
it is a simple exercise of Riemann sums, which is left to the reader. We can
use
\log_{b}(1+\frac{1}{m})
in place of
\log_{b}(m/(m-1))
if one prefers, but
preferably then summing from
b^{p}
to
b^{p+1}-1
so that this is really a
barycentric average: this is better numerically.
∎

We now lift the restriction on the value of
f=\max A
. Let

\kappa=\frac{b-1}{f}
. In place of directly
u_{m}(s)
we want to consider the
rescaled
\kappa^{m}u_{m}(s)
which are the moments of the measure obtained by
keeping the same individual weights as in
\mu_{s}
but multiplying all rational
numbers in its support by
\kappa
, so that their supremum is now at
1
and not

f/(b-1)
. Let
\nu_{s}
be this new complex measure. We leave to the reader to
check that this
\nu_{s}
verifies (

H
σ

) from Proposition
1

with
\sigma=\Re s
. Hence
\kappa^{m}m^{s}u_{m}(s)
differs by
O(m^{-1})
from

m^{s}\mathsf{e}^{-m}
times the generating function for the
\nu_{s}
moments, evaluated
at
m
, which is
E(\kappa m)
. We are thus reduced to study
m^{s}\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(\kappa m)
, where
E
is the generating function of the moments of

\mu_{s}
. And:

\displaystyle E(\kappa m)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{l})\in A^{l}}\mathsf{e}^{\kappa m(a_{1}b^{-1}+\dots+a_{l}b^{-l})}b^{-ls}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{l})\in B^{l}}\mathsf{e}^{\kappa m\bigl((1-b^{-l})\frac{f}{b-1}-d_{1}b^{-1}-\dots-d_{l}b^{-l}\bigr)}b^{-ls}

\displaystyle m^{s}\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(\kappa m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigl(\prod_{1\leq k\leq l}\alpha_{B}(-\kappa mb^{-k})\bigr)\mathsf{e}^{-mb^{-l}}(mb^{-l})^{s}

\displaystyle m^{s}\gamma_{B}(-\kappa m)\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(\kappa m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{B}(-\kappa mb^{-l})\mathsf{e}^{-mb^{-l}}(mb^{-l})^{s}

In the last two equations we have defined the set
B=f-A
, the functions
\alpha_{B}
and
\gamma_{B}
as in the proof of Theorem
1
.

In order to match the notations from
[
3
, §3, eq.(21)]
we
now define:

F_{s}(t)=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma_{B}(-tb^{-l})\mathsf{e}^{-\frac{f}{b-1}tb^{-l}}(tb^{-l})^{s}.

This function is
1
-periodic in
\log_{b}(t)
. Similarly to the earlier situation,
when we had
\kappa=1
, we observe that
m^{s}\mathsf{e}^{-m}E(\kappa m)
is exponentially close to

\kappa^{-s}F_{s}(\kappa m)
. So
\kappa^{m}m^{s}u_{m}(s)
is
O(m^{-1})
close to
the periodic function
\kappa^{-s}F_{s}(\kappa m)
. Taking now into account
the Fourier series given in
[
3
, Prop. 7]
we end up with this
more general version of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
A\subset\{0,\dots,b-1\}
,
f=\max A>0
,
B=\{f-a,a\in A\}
and

\mathcal{B}
be the set of
B
-admissible (non-negative) integers. Let

\kappa=\frac{b-1}{f}
. Then for
m
real going to

+\infty
,
\kappa^{m}m^{s}u_{m}(s)
differs by
O(m^{-1})
from a smooth

1
-periodic function in
\log_{b}(m)
which is given as

(\log b)^{-1}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathsf{e}^{2\pi ik\log_{b}(m)}\Gamma(s-2\pi i\frac{k}{\log b})\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{(\kappa n+1)^{s-2\pi i\frac{k}{\log b}}},

or equivalently
as

m^{s}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}b^{js}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\mathsf{e}^{-(\kappa n+1)b^{j}m}.

The asymptotic average value for the sequence of scaled integer moments is:

\langle\kappa^{m}m^{s}u_{m}(s)\rangle=\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\log b}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{(\kappa n+1)^{s}}\,.

In the next section we give some numerical results which proved all to be in
accordance with Theorems
1
and
2
.
