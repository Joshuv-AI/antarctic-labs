Continuity of asymptotic entropy on free solvable groups
Eduardo Silva

Abstract
. We prove the continuity of asymptotic entropy, as a function of the step distribution, among non-degenerate probability measures with finite Shannon entropy on the free solvable group S d , m S_{d,m} of rank d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and derived length m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 .



1.
Introduction

One of the earliest results relating geometric properties of non-abelian infinite groups to random walks is due to H. Kesten
[
31
]
, who showed that a group is non-amenable if and only if the return probability to the origin at time
n
of a symmetric random walk on the group decays exponentially as
n\to\infty
. Later, J. Rosenblatt
[
43
, Theorem 1.10]
and independently V. Kaimanovich and A. Vershik
[
29
, Theorem 4.4]
proved that a group is amenable if and only if there exists a probability measure
\mu
on the group with the Liouville property, meaning that every bounded
\mu
-harmonic function is constant. Recall that a function
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
is called
\mu
-harmonic
if
f(g)=\sum_{h\in G}f(gh)\mu(h)
for all
g\in G
. V. Kaimanovich and A. Vershik also exhibited the first examples of amenable groups that admit symmetric finitely supported probability measures that do not possess the Liouville property
[
29
, Proposition 6.4]
. Their examples are the wreath products
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
, which are not only amenable but even
2
-step solvable.

Within the class of amenable groups, solvable groups provide a particularly rich source of examples exhibiting diverse behavior for random walks. Most notably, J. Brieussel and T. Zheng showed that any sufficiently regular function can be realized as the asymptotic drift and asymptotic entropy (see Definition
1.2
below) of a random walk on a
4
-step solvable group
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
. Their result generalizes earlier results in this direction by
[
22
, Theorem 1]
,
[
19
, Theorems 1 & 2]
,
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
3
, Theorems 1 & 3]
, and
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, which are also based on solvable groups.

Among solvable groups, a natural class to study is given by free solvable groups, which may be viewed as the solvable groups of a given rank and derived length with no relations other than those imposed by solvability of that derived length.

Definition 1.1
.

Denote by
F_{d}
the free group of rank
d\geq 2
. The
free solvable group of rank
d
and derived length
m\geq 2
is defined as the quotient

S_{d,m}\coloneqq F_{d}/F_{d}^{(m)},

where
F_{d}^{(1)}=[F_{d},F_{d}]
and
F_{d}^{(r)}=\left[F_{d}^{(r-1)},F_{d}^{(r-1)}\right]
for
r=2,\ldots,m
.

Free solvable groups satisfy the following universal property: every finitely generated group with
d
generators that is solvable of derived length
m
is a quotient of
S_{d,m}
. Many structural properties of free solvable groups are closely related to those of wreath products via the classical Magnus embedding theorem
[
36
]
. This theorem provides an explicit monomorphism

S_{d,m}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}\wr S_{d,m-1}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{S_{d,m-1}}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\rtimes S_{d,m-1}.

Free solvable groups have been studied extensively in the past decades for their algebraic
[
7
,
38
,
10
,
6
,
8
,
49
,
45
,
1
]
algorithmic
[
40
,
9
,
39
,
42
,
50
,
26
,
11
,
12
]
and geometric
[
15
,
51
,
44
,
28
,
54
]
properties.

The study of random walks on free solvable groups goes back to the work of A. Vershik
[
53
, Section 4]
, where a geometric interpretation of free solvable groups is introduced (see also
[
52
]
) and used to analyze the Poisson boundary of random walks on these groups. The Poisson boundary of free solvable groups has since been studied further: under suitable conditions on the step distribution, descriptions of the Poisson boundary are obtained in
[
21
, Theorem 2]
,
[
35
, Section 6]
, and
[
23
, Corollary 1.8]
. In particular, these works provide a description of the space of bounded
\mu
-harmonic functions on
S_{d,m}
for any non-degenerate probability measure with a finite first moment. Other aspects of random walks on free solvable groups have also been explored. For instance, the asymptotic decay of the return probability to the origin in
S_{d,m}
is computed in
[
46
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The central object of the present paper is the asymptotic entropy of probability measures on free solvable groups. Recall that the
Shannon entropy
of a probability measure
\mu
on
G
is defined as
H(\mu)\coloneqq-\sum_{g\in G}\mu(g)\log(\mu(g)).

Definition 1.2
.

The
asymptotic entropy
of a probability measure
\mu
on a countable group
G
is defined as

h(\mu)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H(\mu^{*n})}{n}\in[0,\infty].

Asymptotic entropy was introduced by A. Avez
[
5
]
, who showed that a finitely supported probability measure
\mu
with
h(\mu)=0
has the Liouville property. Furthermore, for any probability measure with
H(\mu)<\infty
, the
entropy criterion
of Y. Derriennic
[
16
]
and V. Kaimanovich and A. Vershik
[
29
]
states that
h(\mu)=0
if and only if
\mu
has the Liouville property.

Our main result establishes the continuity of asymptotic entropy, as a function of the step distribution, on free solvable groups of rank at least
3
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
S_{d,m}
be the free solvable group of rank
d\geq 3
and derived length
m\geq 2
. Let
\mu
be a non-degenerate probability measure on
S_{d,m}
with
H(\mu)<\infty
. Let
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of non-degenerate probability measures on
S_{d,m}
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for every
g\in S_{d,m}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
. Then, we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h(\mu)
.

The assumption
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
is natural in this setting, since asymptotic entropy is known to be upper semicontinuous under pointwise convergence of probability measures (see Proposition
2.8
), and the convergence of Shannon entropies is used to control the entropy of finite convolution powers.

The question of whether the map
\mu\mapsto h(\mu)
is continuous was raised in
[
20
, Section 6, Item 5]
. Positive results are known for several classes of groups, including hyperbolic groups
[
18
, Theorem 2]
,
[
25
, Theorem 2.9]
, acylindrically hyperbolic groups
[
14
, Theorem F]
, linear groups and groups acting properly on
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes
[
47
, Corollary 1.6]
, as well as wreath products of the form
A\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
d\geq 3

[
47
, Theorem 1.2]
. Among these examples, the latter is the only class that includes amenable groups. On the other hand, discontinuity phenomena are known to occur, and all currently known examples arise among amenable groups. The first such examples were constructed by V. Kaimanovich
[
30
, Theorems 3.1 & 4.1]
, who exhibited locally finite groups admitting probability measures with positive asymptotic entropy that cannot be approximated by the asymptotic entropy of finitely supported measures. Further examples, including discontinuity for measures supported on a fixed finite generating set, were later obtained by A. Erschler using the wreath product
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\wr D_{\infty}

[
21
, Lemma 4]
. We refer the reader to the introduction of
[
47
]
for a more detailed discussion.

The starting point of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is
[
47
, Theorem 7.1]
(see also Theorem
2.10
below), which provides technical conditions under which asymptotic entropy is continuous on a wreath product. The main new difficulty in the present work is extending this argument to iterated wreath products in Theorem
3.1
, which requires controlling the entropy contribution coming from several layers of the wreath product structure. This is afterwards combined with Magnus’ embedding theorem
[
36
]
(see also Theorem
3.6
) to obtain Theorem
1.3
.

We remark that Theorem
1.3
does not cover free solvable groups of rank
2
. The reason is that the statement is not expected to hold in full generality for these groups. Indeed, examples of discontinuity of asymptotic entropy can already be found for wreath products of the form
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. To obtain continuity results in this setting, one needs to restrict the class of probability measures under consideration. In Theorems
4.7
and
4.9
, we prove the continuity of asymptotic entropy on wreath products
A\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\in\{1,2\}
, and on free solvable groups of rank
2
, among probability measures supported on a fixed finite subset of the group.

All known examples of discontinuity of asymptotic entropy mentioned so far consist of sequences of probability measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
such that
h(\mu_{k})=0
for all
k\geq 1
, while the limit measure
\mu
satisfies
h(\mu)>0
. However, discontinuity is not restricted to this situation. A simple modification of the above examples, obtained by taking a direct product with a free group, yields examples in which all asymptotic entropies involved are strictly positive. More precisely, in Proposition
4.11
we construct groups
G
and probability measures
\mu
,
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
G
, supported on a fixed finite subset of
G
, such that
h(\mu_{k})>0
for all
k\geq 1
and
h(\mu)>0
, while
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for every
g\in G
but
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})\neq h(\mu)
.

Finally, we remark that certain special cases of Theorem
1.3
, under additional assumptions on the measures
\mu
and
\mu_{k}
,
k\geq 1
, can be deduced from
[
47
, Theorem 1.5]
. Indeed, together with the identification of the Poisson boundary obtained in
[
23
, Corollary 1.8]
, the continuity of asymptotic entropy would follow, among probability measures for which this identification holds, from the weak continuity of the associated harmonic measures on this model of the Poisson boundary; see
[
47
, Proposition 8.12 and Corollary 8.13]
for the analogous statements in the case of wreath products. However, this argument does not yield Theorem
1.3
in full generality for arbitrary sequences of probability measures with finite entropy. At this level of generality, there is currently no identification of the Poisson boundary of free solvable groups, and in particular none that simultaneously realizes the Poisson boundaries corresponding to different probability measures within a common Polish model space.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Random walks on groups and entropy

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a countable group
G
. The
\mu
-random walk
on
G
is the Markov chain
\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
with state space
G
, starting at
w_{0}=e_{G}
and with transition probabilities
p(g,h)=\mu(g^{-1}h)
for
g,h\in G
. Equivalently, for each
n\geq 1
we have

w_{n}=g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n},

where
\{g_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
is a sequence of independent, identically distributed, random variables on
G
with law
\mu
. The law
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
of the chain
\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is the push-forward of the Bernoulli measure
\mu^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
through the map

\displaystyle G^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}

\displaystyle\to G^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}

\displaystyle(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\ldots)

\displaystyle\mapsto(w_{0},w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},\ldots)\coloneqq(e_{G},g_{1},g_{1}g_{2},g_{1}g_{2}g_{3},\ldots).

The space
(G^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}},\mathbb{P}_{\mu})
is called the
space of sample paths
or the
space of trajectories
of the
\mu
-random walk.

In this paper we work with countable partitions of the space of sample paths. The
Shannon entropy
of a countable partition
\rho
of the space of sample paths
G^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
with respect to the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
is defined as

H(\rho)\coloneqq-\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(\rho_{k})\log\mathbb{P}(\rho_{k}).

In this paper we will use the following well-known properties of entropy. We refer to
[
37
, Corollaries 2.5 and 2.6]
and
[
41
, Section 5]
for their proofs.

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider countable partitions
\rho,\gamma
and
\delta
of a Borel space. The following properties hold:

(1)

H(\rho\vee\gamma\mid\delta)=H(\rho\mid\gamma\vee\delta)+H(\gamma\mid\delta).

(2)

H(\rho\mid\gamma)\leq H(\rho\vee\delta\mid\gamma).

(3)

H(\rho\mid\gamma\vee\delta)\leq H(\rho\mid\gamma).

Remark 2.2
.

Throughout this paper we will use the same symbol to denote both a random variable and the partition of the space of sample paths that it defines. More precisely, let
X:(G^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}},\mathbb{P})\to D
is a random variable from the space of sample paths with values on a countable set
D
. Then we will identify
X
with the countable partition
\rho_{X}
where two sample paths
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w^{\prime}}\in G^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
belong to the same element of
\rho_{X}
if and only if
X(\mathbf{w})=X(\mathbf{w^{\prime}})
.

2.2.
Escape probability and the asymptotic range

The
range at time
n\geq 1
of the
\mu
-random walk on the group
G
corresponds to the random variable
R_{n}\coloneqq|\{w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\}|
that counts the number of distinct elements of
G
visited by the trajectory of the random walk up to time
n
. It is a consequence of Kingman’s subadditive ergodic theorem
[
32
, Theorem 5]
that the limit

R(\mu)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}}{n}

exists almost surely and in
L^{1}(G^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}})
, and that it is a constant. Furthermore, we have
R(\mu)=\mathbb{P}(w_{n}\neq e_{G}\text{ for all }n\geq 1)
. This equality is proved in in
[
48
, Theorem I.4.1]
for
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, and in
[
17
, Lemma 1]
for the general case of a countable group.

Definition 2.3
.

For each probability measure
\mu
on a countable group
G
, we define its associated
escape probability
by
p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(w_{n}\neq e_{G}\text{ for every }n\geq 1)
.

The following result guarantees the continuity of the escape probability on groups with at least cubic growth.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
47
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a countable group
G
. Suppose that
\langle\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\rangle_{+}
contains a finitely generated subgroup of at least cubic growth. Let
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of probability measures on
G
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
, for every
g\in G
. Then, we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu_{k})=p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu)
.

2.3.
Convergence of probability measures

The following result is well-known; a proof can be found in
[
47
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a countable group
G
, and consider a sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of probability measures on
G
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\sum_{g\in G}|\mu_{k}(g)-\mu(g)|\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0
.

(2)

\mu_{k}(g)\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\mu(g)
, for every
g\in G
.

We recall some preliminary results on the convergence of probability measures and Shannon entropies that we will use in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a countable group
G
. Consider a sequence of probability measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
G
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
, for each
g\in G
. Suppose that there is a finite subset
S\subseteq G
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subseteq S
and
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{k})\subseteq S
for all
k\geq 1
. Then
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
.

The following result is proved in
[
2
, Section 3]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a countable group and consider a probability measure
\mu
on
G
with
H(\mu)<\infty
. Consider a sequence of probability measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
G
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for all
g\in G
,
H(\mu_{k})<\infty
, for all
k\geq 1
, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
. Then for any
n\geq 1
it holds that
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu^{*n}_{k})=H(\mu^{*n})
.

Next, we recall the fact that the asymptotic entropy is upper-semicontinuous. The following result is proved in
[
2
, Proposition 4]
. Previously, this was proved in
[
21
, Lemma 1]
with the additional assumption that the supports of all the probability measures in the sequence are contained in a fixed finite set.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
2
, Proposition 4]
)
.

Let
G
be a countable group and consider a probability measure
\mu
on
G
together with a sequence of probability measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
G
. Suppose that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for every
g\in G
, and that
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
. Then we have
\limsup_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})\leq h(\mu)
.

Finally, we prove that convergence of probability measures and convergence of Shannon entropies are preserved when passing to a quotient.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be countable groups, and let
\pi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be an epimorphism. Consider a probability measure
\mu
and a sequence of probability measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
G_{1}
such that
H(\mu)<\infty
and
H(\mu_{k})<\infty
, for all
k\geq 1
. Suppose that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for all
g\in G_{1}
, and that
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
. Then it also holds that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi_{*}\mu_{k}(g)=\pi_{*}\mu(g)
for all
g\in G_{2}
, and that
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\pi_{*}\mu_{k})=H(\pi_{*}\mu)
.

Proof.

Thanks to the data processing inequality, we have that
H(\pi_{*}\mu)\leq H(\mu)<\infty
and
H(\pi_{*}\mu_{k})\leq H(\mu_{k})<\infty
for each
k\geq 1
. Additionally, for every
g\in G_{2}
we can use Lemma
2.5
to obtain

|\pi_{*}\mu_{k}(g)-\pi_{*}\mu(g)|\leq\sum_{h\in\pi^{-1}(\{g\})}|\mu_{k}(h)-\mu(h)|\leq\sum_{h\in G_{1}}|\mu_{k}(h)-\mu(h)|\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0.

Let us now show that
H(\pi_{*}\mu_{k})\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}H(\pi_{*}\mu)
. Thanks to
[
2
, Lemma 3.2]
, it suffices to show that the sequence
\{\pi_{*}\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is entropy-tight. Let
\varepsilon>0
and, using that
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is entropy-tight, choose a finite subset
F\subseteq G_{1}
such that
\sum_{x\notin F}-\mu_{k}(g)\log(\mu_{k}(g))<\varepsilon
, for all
k\geq 1
. Let us consider the finite subset
\pi(F)\subseteq G_{2}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{g\notin\pi(F)}-\pi_{*}\mu_{k}(g)\log\left(\pi_{*}\mu_{k}(g)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\notin\pi(F)}\sum_{h\in\pi^{-1}(\{g\})}-\mu_{k}(h)\log\left(\sum_{x\in\pi^{-1}(\{g\})}\mu_{k}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{g\notin\pi(F)}\sum_{h\in\pi^{-1}(\{g\})}-\mu_{k}(h)\log\left(\mu_{k}(h)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h\notin F}-\mu_{k}(h)\log(\mu_{k}(h))<\varepsilon.

This finishes the proof.
∎

The next result is the main technical theorem of
[
47
]
, which gives sufficient conditions under which asymptotic entropy is continuous on a wreath product.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
47
, Theorem 7.1]
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be countable groups and let
\mu
be a probability measure on
A\wr B\coloneqq\bigoplus_{B}A\rtimes B
with
H(\mu)<\infty
. Consider a sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of probability measures on
A\wr B
with
H(\mu_{k})<\infty
for all
k\geq 1
, and such that

(1)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for each
g\in A\wr B
, and

(2)

\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
.

Denote by
\pi:A\wr B\to B
the canonical epimorphism to the base group
B
. Suppose furthermore that

(3)

the
\pi_{*}\mu
-random walk on
B
is transient,

(4)

h(\pi_{*}\mu)=0
,

(5)

\langle\mathrm{supp}(\pi_{*}\mu)\rangle_{+}
is symmetric, and

(6)

\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\pi_{*}\mu_{k})=p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\pi_{*}\mu)
.

Then
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h(\mu)
.

3.
Iterated wreath products and free solvable groups

The proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on the fact that free solvable groups embed into iterated wreath products via the Magnus embedding. We therefore begin by establishing a continuity result for asymptotic entropy
on iterated wreath products, which will later be combined with the Magnus embedding to obtain Theorem
1.3
.

The following result is an extension of Theorem
2.10
for iterated wreath products and which is proved at the end of this subsection.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{m-1},B
be countable groups for some
m\geq 2
. Consider the groups
B_{1}\coloneqq B
and
B_{j+1}\coloneqq A_{j}\wr B_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,m-1
. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
B_{m}
with
H(\mu)<\infty
, and consider a sequence of probability measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
B_{m}
with
H(\mu_{k})<\infty
for all
k\geq 1
. Suppose that

(1)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for each
g\in B_{m}
, and

(2)

\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
.

For each
j=1,\ldots,m-1
, let us denote by
\pi_{j}:B_{m}\to B_{j}
the canonical epimorphism from
B_{m}
to
B_{j}
. Suppose furthermore that

(3)

the
\pi_{1*}\mu
-random walk on
B
is transient,

(4)

h(\pi_{1*}\mu)=0
,

(5)

for every
j=1,\ldots,m-1
the semigroup generated by
\mathrm{supp}(\pi_{j*}\mu)
is symmetric, and

(6)

for every
j=1,\ldots,m-1
we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\pi_{j*}\mu_{k})=p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\pi_{j*}\mu)
.

Then
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h(\mu)
.

In what follows we will denote a sample path of a random walk on
A\wr B
as

w_{n}=(\varphi_{n},X_{n}),\text{ where }\varphi_{n}:B\to A\text{ and }X_{n}\in B,\text{ for }n\geq 0.

Let us denote the independent increments of the random walk by
g_{i}=(f_{i},Y_{i})
,
i\geq 1
, so that

(\varphi_{n},X_{n})=(\varphi_{n-1},X_{n-1})\cdot(f_{n},Y_{n}).

3.1.
The coarse trajectory

We begin by introducing the notion of the
t_{0}
-coarse trajectory of a random walk on a group. For a fixed
t_{0}\geq 1
. Intuitively, it consists in recording the group element that is hit by the random walk every
t_{0}
steps.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a group
G
, and let
t_{0}\geq 1
. Recall that we denote by
\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

The proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By Proposition
3.5
we can choose constants
C\geq 0
,
K,T\geq 1
and
n_{0}\geq 1
such that for all
k\geq K
,
t_{0}\geq T
, and all
N_{1},\ldots,N_{m-1}>n_{0}
, whenever
n>N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}
we have

H_{\mu_{k}}\!\left(P^{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}_{n}(B_{m})\,\middle|\,w_{n}\right)\leq\varepsilon n+(H(\mu)+1)\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{nn_{0}}{N_{j}}+C.

We now relate the entropy of the coarse trajectory to the entropy of the random walk at a fixed time. Using the definition of the coarse trajectory and the subadditivity of entropy, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n}{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}-1\right)H_{\mu_{k}}\left(w_{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}\right\rfloor H_{\mu_{k}}\left(w_{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=H_{\mu_{k}}\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}(B_{m})\right)

\displaystyle\leq H_{\mu_{k}}\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}(B_{m})\mid w_{n}\right)+H_{\mu_{k}}(w_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon n+(H(\mu)+1)\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{nn_{0}}{N_{j}}+C+H_{\mu_{k}}(w_{n}).

This implies that

\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{k}}(w_{n})\geq\left(\frac{1}{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)H_{\mu_{k}}\left(w_{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}\right)-\varepsilon-(H(\mu)+1)\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{n_{0}}{N_{j}}-\frac{C}{n}.

By taking
n\to\infty
and then taking the limit inferior as
k\to\infty
, we obtain

\liminf_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})\geq\frac{1}{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}H_{\mu}\left(w_{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}}\right)-\varepsilon-(H(\mu)+1)\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{n_{0}}{N_{j}},

where we used Lemma
2.7
to have the inequality
H_{\mu_{k}}(w_{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}})\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}H_{\mu}(w_{N_{1}\cdots N_{m-1}t_{0}})
. Next, we take the limit
N_{1}\to\infty,N_{2}\to\infty,\ldots,N_{m-1}\to\infty
and we get

\liminf_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})\geq h(\mu)-\varepsilon.

Finally, since this equality holds for all
\varepsilon>0
, we conclude that
\liminf_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})\geq h(\mu).
Together with Proposition
2.8
, we conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h(\mu)
.

∎

Free solvable groups

Let
F_{d}

Let
\mathbb{Z}(F_{d}/N)
and

be the group rings with integer coefficients of

and

, respectively. Let us denote by
\pi:\mathbb{Z}(F_{d})\to\mathbb{Z}(F_{d}/N)
the linear extension of the canonical quotient map

. Let

be a free left

where the group operation is matrix multiplication. One can verify with a direct computation that

is isomorphic to the direct sum
\bigoplus_{F_{d}/N}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and that

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\wr F_{d}/N

into

Theorem 3.6

(
[
36
]
)
.

Let
F_{d}
be a free group of rank

, and consider
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\}
a free basis of

. Let
N\lhd F_{d}
be a normal subgroup and denote by
\pi:F_{d}\to F_{d}/N
the quotient map. Then the homomorphism
\phi:F_{d}\to M(F_{d}/N)
defined by

\phi(x_{i})\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\pi(x_{i})&t_{i}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \text{ for }i=1,\ldots,d,

satisfies

induces a monomorphism
\widetilde{\phi}:F_{d}/[N,N]\to M(F_{d}/N)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Consider any non-degenerate probability measure
\mu
on
S_{d,m}
with
H(\mu)<\infty
, and let
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of probability measures with
H(\mu_{k})<\infty
, for each
k\geq 1
. Let us suppose that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for each
g\in S_{d,m}
and that
H(\mu_{k})\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}H(\mu)
. Recall that in the statement of Theorem
1.3
we assume that
d\geq 3
.

Using Theorem
3.6
we can realize
S_{d,m}
as a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\wr\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\wr\left(\cdots\mathbb{Z}^{d}\wr\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)\cdots\right)\right)
, the group formed as the iterated wreath product of

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Since asymptotic entropy and the hypotheses of Theorem

are preserved under the natural projections to the wreath product factors, it suffices to verify that the induced measures satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
.

For each
j=1,\ldots,m-1
consider the group
B_{j+1}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\wr B_{j}
, where we define
B_{1}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Denote by
\pi_{j}:S_{d,m}\to B_{j}
the canonical projection to the group
B_{j}
for each

. Then we have the following.

(1)

We are assuming that
\mu
is non-degenerate, and hence
\mathrm{supp}(\pi_{1*}\mu)
generates
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Since

, the
\pi_{1*}\mu
-random walk on

is transient.

(2)

We also have that

as a combination of the fact that random walks on abelian groups have trivial Poisson boundary with the entropy criterion.

(3)

For each

4.
Base groups with linear or quadratic growth

In this section we turn our attention to the case where the base group of the wreath product has growth either linear or quadratic, which are more delicate. Indeed, we have already mentioned that the escape probability is in general not continuous among finitely supported probability measures on the infinite dihedral group
[
21
, Lemma 3]
(see also Example
4.5
), and this leads to analogous counterexamples to continuity of asymptotic entropy on wreath products of the form
A\wr D_{\infty}

[
21
, Lemma 4]
(see also Example
4.10
). In contrast, here we prove that one does have continuity among probability measures with a fixed common support when the base group is either
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

4.1.
Continuity of the escape probability on
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

The asymptotic entropy on a wreath product is tightly connected with the escape probability on its base group. Therefore, in order to handle base groups of linear or quadratic growth, we first study the continuity of the escape probability on
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
d\in\{1,2\}
and consider a probability measure
\mu
and a sequence of probability measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that there exists a finite subset
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subseteq F
and
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{k})\subseteq F
, for all
k\geq 1
. Suppose that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for all
g\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then
\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu_{k})=p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu)
.

We will use the following result, which is a consequence of Hoeffding’s inequality
[
27
, Theorem 2]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mu
be a finitely supported probability measure on
\mathbb{Z}
, and consider
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that every
x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
satisfies
a\leq x\leq b
. Let us denote
\ell\coloneqq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}x\mu(x)
, and let
\{w_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be the
\mu
-random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
. Then for all
n\geq 0
we have

(3)

\mathbb{P}_{\mu}\left(w_{n}=0\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2n\ell^{2}}{(b-a)^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a finitely supported probability measure on
\mathbb{Z}
as in the statement of the lemma. Then, Hoeffding’s inequality
[
27
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
34
, Proposition A.6.1]
) states that for all
n\geq 0
and
t>0
we have

\mathbb{P}_{\mu}\left(\left|w_{n}-n\ell\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2t^{2}}{n(b-a)^{2}}\right).

In particular, evaluating the above in
t=n\ell
we get

\mathbb{P}_{\mu}\left(w_{n}=0\right)\leq\mathbb{P}_{\mu}\left(\left|w_{n}-n\ell\right|\geq n\ell\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2n\ell^{2}}{(b-a)^{2}}\right),

which is the inequality we wanted.
∎

In the proof of Proposition
4.1
we will use the following well-known equality that relates the escape probability of the
\mu
-random walk with the expected number of returns to the origin; see, e.g.,
[
55
, Lemma 1.13 (a)]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a countable group
G
. Then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu^{*n}(e_{G})=\frac{1}{p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu)}.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Consider
d=1
or
d=2
. Let
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
,
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\mu
be as in the statement of the proposition. For each
k\geq 1
we consider the mean of
\mu_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, given by
\ell_{k}=\sum_{x\in F}x\mu_{k}(x)
, as well as
\ell=\sum_{x\in F}x\mu(x)
. Since
F
is finite, we have the convergence
\ell_{k}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\ell
.

If
\ell=0
, then the
\mu
-random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is recurrent and hence
p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu)=0
. In this case, it follows from
[
21
, Lemma 1 (i)]
that
\limsup_{k\geq\infty}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu_{k})\leq p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu)=0
, so that
\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu_{k})=0=p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu)
. For the rest of the proof, let us consider the case where
\ell\neq 0.

Then, it follows from Lemma
4.2
together with the continuity of the exponential function that there are
C>0
and
K\geq 1
such that for each
k\geq K
we have

(4)

\mu_{k}^{*n}(0)=\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{k}}(w_{n}=0)\leq C\exp\left(-Cn\right).

From Equation (
4
) we have that the sequences
\{\mu_{k}^{*n}(0)\}_{n\geq 0}
are bounded above by an integrable sequence, uniformly over all
k\geq K
. From this, we obtain the convergence

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mu_{k}^{*n}(0)\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\sum_{n\geq 0}\mu^{*n}(0),

and hence that
p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu_{k})\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu).

∎

Remark 4.4
.

One should note that Proposition
4.1
is not true if one does not suppose that all the probability measures in the sequence have a common fixed finite support. For example, consider for each
k\geq 1
the probability measure
\mu_{k}
on
\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\mu_{k}(1)=\frac{3}{4}\frac{2k}{1+2k},\mu_{k}(-1)=\frac{1}{4}\frac{2k}{1+2k},\text{ and }\mu_{k}(-k)=\frac{1}{1+2k}.

Then the
\mu_{k}
-random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
is recurrent, for each
k\geq 1
. Indeed, this follows from the fact that

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}x\mu_{k}(x)=\frac{3}{4}\frac{2k}{1+2k}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{2k}{1+2k}-\frac{k}{1+2k}=0.

Despite the above, the sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
converges pointwise to a probability measure
\mu
such that the
\mu
-random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
is transient.

As we have already mentioned, if one considers groups of linear or quadratic growth other than
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the analogous generalization of Proposition
4.1
does not hold.

Example 4.5
.

Consider the infinite dihedral group
D_{\infty}=\langle a,b\mid a^{2}=b^{2}=1\rangle
. It is shown in
[
24
, Lemma 4.3]
that for every adapted probability measure
\mu
on
D_{\infty}
with a finite first moment, the
\mu
-random walk on
D_{\infty}
is recurrent. In particular, this is the case for any probability measure of the form

\mu_{k}=\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)\left(p\delta_{ab}+(1-p)\delta_{ba}\right)+\frac{1}{k}\delta_{a},

with
k\geq 1
and
0\leq p\leq 1
. Since these measures are recurrent, we have
p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu_{k})=0
for all
k\geq 1
. Additionally, we have that
\mu_{k}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\mu
pointwise, where
\mu=p\delta_{ab}+(1-p)\delta_{ba}
is supported on the subgroup
\langle ab\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}
. If
p\neq 1/2
then
p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mu)>0
, and hence this provides an example for the discontinuity of the escape probability among probability measures supported on a fixed finite subset of
D_{\infty}.

One obtains a similar example on the Baumslag-Solitar group
\mathrm{BS}(1,-1)=\langle a,b\mid bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle
. This group has quadratic growth and is torsion-free. Here, one can obtain a sequence of probability measures
\mu_{k}
,
k\geq 1
, supported on a fixed finite subset of
\mathrm{BS}(1,-1)
that define a recurrent random walk, and which converge pointwise to a probability measure (supported on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
) that defines a transient random walk. For example, one can consider

\mu_{k}=\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)\left(\delta_{b^{2}}+\delta_{b^{-2}}+p\delta_{a}+(1-p)\delta_{a^{-1}}\right)+\frac{1}{2k}\left(\delta_{b}+\delta_{b^{-1}}\right),\text{for }k\geq 1.

Remark 4.6
.

Let
H
be a finite-index subgroup of a countable group
G
, and let
\mu
be a non-degenerate probability measure on
G
. Denote by

\tau\coloneqq\min\{n\geq 1:w_{n}\in H\}

the first return time to
H
of the
\mu
-random walk on
G
. Then

\mu_{H}(h)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(w_{\tau}=h),\qquad h\in H,

defines a probability measure on
H
. It holds that the
\mu
-random walk on
G
is transient if and only if the
\mu_{H}
-random walk on
H
is transient. Moreover, if
\mu
is finitely supported, then
\mu_{H}
has an exponential tail (see, e.g.,
[
56
, Theorem 4.9.3]
). Therefore, by considering the probability measures from Example
4.5
, one can also construct examples of discontinuity of the escape probability on
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Although these measures are not finitely supported, they still have exponential tails.

4.2.
Continuity of asymptotic entropy on wreath products over
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

We now explain how Proposition
4.1
implies the continuity of asymptotic entropy on wreath products when the base group is
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(see Example
4.10
).

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
A
be a countable group,
d\in\{1,2\}
, and consider the wreath product
A\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
F\subseteq A\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a non-empty finite subset. Consider probability measures
\mu
and
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
A\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{k})\subseteq F
for all
k\geq 1
. Suppose that the random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
induced by
\mu
is transient and that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for every
g\in A\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h(\mu)
.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
2.6
we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
. Denote by
\pi:A\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the canonical epimorphism. Thanks to Proposition
4.1
we also have
\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\pi_{*}\mu_{k})=p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\pi_{*}\mu)
. Additionally, the Choquet-Deny theorem together with the entropy criterion guarantee that
h(\pi_{*}\mu)=0
. Hence, we can apply Theorem
2.10
and conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h(\mu)
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

If one furthermore assumes that
A
is a hyper-FC-central group, then the statement of Theorem
4.7
remains true even if the induced random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is recurrent. Indeed, in such a case the
\mu
-random walk on
A\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}
has a trivial Poisson boundary (see e.g.
[
35
, Proposition 4.9]
), and hence
h(\mu)=0
. Since asymptotic entropy is upper-semicontinuous (Proposition
2.8
) and
h(\mu_{k})\geq 0
for all
k\geq 1
, we conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=0=h(\mu)
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Consider the free solvable group
S_{2,m}
of rank
2
and derived length
m\geq 2
. Let
F\subseteq S_{2,m}
be a non-empty finite subset. Consider probability measures
\mu
and
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
S_{2,m}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{k})\subseteq F
for all
k\geq 1
. Suppose that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(g)=\mu(g)
for every
S_{2,m}
. Then
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h(\mu)
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we use Theorem
3.6
to realize
S_{2,m}
as a subgroup of the iterated wreath product of
m
copies of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. For each
j=1,\ldots,m-1
define
B_{j+1}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}^{2}\wr B_{j}
, where
B_{1}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and denote by
\pi_{j}:S_{2,m}\to B_{j}
the canonical projection to
B_{j}
.
Suppose first that the
\pi_{1*}\mu
-random walk on
B_{1}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is recurrent. Then the
\pi_{2*}\mu
-random walk on
B_{2}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\wr\mathbb{Z}^{2}
has a trivial Poisson boundary and hence
h(\pi_{2*}\mu)=0
thanks to the entropy criterion. If
m=2
, then the upper-semicontinuity of asymptotic entropy guarantees that
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=0=h(\mu)
. If
m\geq 3
, then we can re-define
\widetilde{B}_{j+1}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}^{2}\wr\widetilde{B}_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,m-2
, where
\widetilde{B}_{1}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}^{2}\wr\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Denote by
\widetilde{\pi}_{j}:S_{2,m}\to\widetilde{B}_{j}
the canonical projection to
\widetilde{B}_{j}
for every
j=1,\ldots,m-1
. In this case, the group
\widetilde{B}_{1}
has exponential growth and hence we have both that the
\widetilde{\pi}_{1*}\mu
-random walk on
\widetilde{B}_{1}
is transient, and that
\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\widetilde{\pi}_{1*}\mu_{k})=p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\widetilde{\pi}_{1*}\mu)
thanks to Theorem
2.4
. The hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
are thus verified and we conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h(\mu)
.

Now suppose that the
\pi_{1*}\mu
-random walk on
B_{1}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is transient. Then it follows from Proposition
4.1
that
\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\widetilde{\pi}_{1*}\mu_{k})=p_{\mathrm{esc}}(\widetilde{\pi}_{1*}\mu)
. Again, we verify the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
and we conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h(\mu)
.
∎

4.3.
Examples of discontinuity of asymptotic entropy

We mentioned in the introduction that countable locally finite groups and finitely generated groups of intermediate growth serve as examples where asymptotic entropy is not continuous among infinitely supported probability measures. In this subsection, we explain in more detail examples involving wreath products, where in general asymptotic entropy will be discontinuous even among probability measures supported on a fixed finite subset of the group (Example
4.10
).

The following class of examples are a generalization of
[
21
, Lemma 4]
.

Example 4.10
.

Using the probability measures mentioned in Example
4.5
, one can construct examples of discontinuity of asymptotic entropy on the groups
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\wr D_{\infty}
and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathrm{BS}(1,-1)
. Indeed, let
G
be
D_{\infty}
or
\mathrm{BS}(1,-1)
, and denote by
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
a sequence of probability measures on
G
that are supported on a fixed finite subset
S\subseteq G
, that define a recurrent random walk, and that converge pointwise to a probability measure
\mu
on
G
that defines a transient random walk. Let
A
be an arbitrary non-trivial hyper-FC-central group, and consider the group
A\wr G
. Consider additionally a finitely supported probability measure
\eta
on
A
. Then the sequence of probability measures
\nu_{k}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\eta+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{k}
is a sequence of probability measures on
G
that is supported on a fixed finite subset of
G
, and it holds that
h(\nu_{k})=0
for all
k\geq 1
,
\{\nu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
converges pointwise to
\nu\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\eta+\frac{1}{2}\mu
, and
h(\nu)>0
.

Using Remark
4.6
, one can use the above examples to obtain discontinuity of asymptotic entropy among probability measures with an exponential tail on
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

The above examples are based on finding a sequence of probability measures with zero asymptotic entropy that converges to a probability measure with positive asymptotic entropy. As we show next, taking the direct product of any group from Example
4.10
with a non-abelian free group yields examples of discontinuity of asymptotic entropy where all involved probability measures have strictly positive asymptotic entropy.

Proposition 4.11
.

There is a finitely generated group
G
and probability measures
\mu,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
G
such that

—

there is a finite subset
F\subseteq G
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{k})\subseteq F
for all
k\geq 1
,

—

\mu_{k}(g)\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\mu(g)
for all
g\in G
,

—

h(\mu_{k})>0
for all
k\geq 1
, and

—

\limsup_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})<h(\mu)
.

Proof.

The idea behind this proof is that the asymptotic entropy of the product random walk decomposes as the sum of the asymptotic entropies on each factor.

Let
L
be a finitely generated group that admits probability measures
\mu,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{k})
contained in a fixed finite subset of
L
for all
k\geq 1
, and such that
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
converges pointwise to
\mu
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\mu_{k})=H(\mu)
,
h(\mu_{k})=0
for all
k\geq 1
, and
h(\mu)>0
. For concreteness, one can choose
L
to be one of the groups considered in Example
4.10
. Additionally, consider
F_{2}
the free group of rank
2
, and let
\eta
be the probability measure on
F_{2}
that is uniform on a symmetric free generating subset of
F_{2}
.

We consider the group
F_{2}\times L
, and define probability measures
\nu,\{\nu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
on
F_{2}\times L
by
\nu(x,g)=\eta(x)\mu(g)
and
\nu_{k}(x,g)=\eta(x)\mu_{k}(g)
for each
k\geq 1
and
(x,g)\in F_{2}\times L
. Then, we have

—

\lim_{k\to\infty}\nu_{k}(x,g)=\eta(x)\mu_{k}(g)=\nu(x,g)
for each
(x,g)\in F_{2}\times L
,

—

H(\nu_{k})=H(\eta)+H(\mu_{k})<\infty
for every
k\geq 1
, and

—

\lim_{k\to\infty}H(\nu_{k})=H(\eta)+H(\mu)=H(\nu)
.

Additionally, note that for each
n\geq 1
, every
(x,g)\in F_{2}\times L
and all
k\geq 1
we have
\nu^{*n}(x,g)=\eta^{*n}(x)\mu^{*n}(g)
and
\nu_{k}^{*n}(x,g)=\eta^{*n}(x)\mu_{k}^{*n}(g)
. As a consequence,
H(\nu^{*n})=H(\eta^{*n})+H(\mu^{*n})
and
H(\nu_{k}^{*n})=H(\eta^{*n})+H(\mu_{k}^{*n})
for each
k\geq 1
. From this, we obtain that
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\nu_{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\eta)+h(\mu_{k})=h(\eta)
, whereas
h(\nu)=h(\eta)+h(\mu)>h(\eta)
. Also, we have
h(\nu_{k})\geq h(\eta)>0
for all
k\geq 1
.
∎

This shows that it is possible to have discontinuity of asymptotic entropy through sequences of probability measures that have positive asymptotic entropy.
