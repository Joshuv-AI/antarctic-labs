Sensitivity of black hole spectral instability against perturbations of the effective potential
Ramin G. Daghigh 1 , Michael D. Green 2 , Guanru Li 3 , Jodin C. Morey 4 , Wei-Liang Qian 3,5,6 , and Stefan J. Randow 7

Abstract
Black hole spectral instability is counterintuitive and contradicts many plausible assumptions of the properties of black hole quasinormal modes. The present study aims to explore different types of instability phenomena. It is understood that the fundamental mode is surprisingly sensitive to small perturbations of the effective potential of the linearized wave equation. Such perturbations can be produced by small-scale modifications of the spacetime metrics. From both the analytical and numerical perspectives, we elaborate on a few qualitatively different examples illustrating the strong sensitivity and diversity in black hole spectral instability caused by such effective potential perturbations. It turns out that the qualitative way in which the fundamental mode becomes perturbed depends on many factors such as the shape of the potential and how the perturbation changes as it moves away from the central black hole.



I
Introduction

Black holes are one of the most captivating concepts in theoretical physics.
They exemplify the properties of gravity at its extreme. The detection of gravitational waves
1
, from binary black hole mergers, has opened a new window to the strong-field regime of gravity. The spectroscopy of the vibrational modes of the ringdown phase of a binary black hole merger can reveal important information about black holes and the relativistic regime of gravity.

The natural vibrational modes, also called quasinormal modes (QNMs), of black holes suffer from spectral instability
14
;
13
;
2
;
20
;
5
;
18
;
12
;
8
as a result of introducing a perturbation to the spacetime metric, which leads to a perturbed effective potential of the linearized wave equation.
The notion of spectral instability is by itself an intriguing idea that contradicts many intuitive assumptions of the properties of black hole QNMs.
On the one hand, the effect of the perturbation, more specifically a perturbative barrier implemented on the effective potential, is shown to be even stronger as it moves farther away from the black hole
4
.
On the other hand, at certain limits, it has been shown that the degree of instability vanishes concurrently as the magnitude of the perturbation decreases.
In particular, several authors have scrutinized the instability in the fundamental mode in a disjointed effective potential
16
;
7
;
4
;
6
.
For the sake of mathematical simplicity, one can assume that the perturbation is implemented on the effective potential in the form of a rectangular barrier.
It was demonstrated
17
;
6
that as the width of the rectangular barrier vanishes, while maintaining its height, the instability of the fundamental mode disappears.
The latter coincides with a vanishing reflection amplitude, along with the size of the perturbative barrier.
At first glance, this result seems rather intuitive, as a barrier with a vanishing width will not interfere with wave propagation.

Intriguing enough, as will be elaborated in the present study, we argue that the situation is more complex. For example, we show that in the extreme case of a delta-function barrier, where the width is zero, the instability of the fundamental mode persists. For the instability of the fundamental mode to disappear, it is more precise to say that the area of the perturbative barrier should vanish. In addition to the numerical calculations, we support this idea with analytic results.
We also explore how decreasing the size of the barrier as it moves away from the central black hole impacts the
spiral. We show that the stability of the fundamental mode depends on the rate of decrease of the barrier size.
We then proceed to elaborate on a few more examples in which the phenomenon displays some interesting features, including outspiraling from one fundamental mode and subsequently inspiraling toward another. The spiral behavior of the fundamental mode is even more complex for the Regge-Wheeler potential, which we explore numerically for the first time.
Even though the present study does not attempt to enumerate all possibilities, we strive to give a broad account of the sensitivity of black hole spectral instability and its observational implications.

This work considers the study of black hole perturbations, which can be simplified to the master equation

(1)

For instance, the Regge-Wheeler potential for a Schwarzschild black hole with mass

As discussed above, the present paper involves the choice of physically relevant effective potentials

II

II
An effective potential with a delta-function perturbation

In this section, we explore the impact of a delta-function perturbation on an effective potential, where we have the extreme case of a barrier with zero width, on the QNM spectrum of a black hole spacetime. Let us introduce a deformed effective potential of the generic form

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}V_{l}(x)&x<L\cr\\
\epsilon\delta(x-L)&x=L\cr\\
V_{r}(x)&x>L\end{array}\right.,

where the subscripts
l
and
r
refer to the left and right sides of the location of the delta-function at
L
respectively.
Originally, in the absence of the delta function, we can determine the QNM spectrum using the junction condition

\displaystyle\frac{{h^{\prime}}(L)}{h(L)}=\frac{{g^{\prime}}(L)}{g(L)}.

(16)

This condition leads to a simplified version of the Wronskian

\displaystyle W(g,h)={g(L)}{h^{\prime}(L)}-{h(L)}{g^{\prime}(L)}.

(17)

To include the delta function, we have
h(L)=g(L)
and we can obtain the condition
h^{\prime}(L)=g^{\prime}(L)-\epsilon g(L)
by integrating the perturbation of the effective potential
\epsilon\delta(x-L)
.
We then reorganize the above junction condition into

\displaystyle\frac{h^{\prime}(L)}{h(L)}=\frac{g^{\prime}(L)-\epsilon g(L)}{g(L)},

(18)

which leads to a modified Wronskian condition of the following form

\displaystyle\bar{W}(g,h)\equiv W(g,h)+\epsilon h(L)g(L)={g}{h^{\prime}}-{h}{g^{\prime}}+\epsilon hg=0.

(19)

The above is bilinear in
h
and
g
.

II.1
Spiral of the fundamental mode in a continuous effective potential with a delta-function perturbation

In the following, we elaborate on a specific example, where a delta-function is introduced as a perturbation on a continuous Pöschl-Teller potential.
This potential has the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{\mathrm{PT}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}{V}_{\mathrm{PT}}(x)&x<L\cr\\
\epsilon\delta(x-L)&x=L\cr\\
{V}_{\mathrm{PT}}(x)&x>L\end{array}\right.,

where

II.1
), are

\displaystyle h(x)=e^{-i\omega x}\left(e^{2\kappa x}+1\right)^{\beta}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\beta,\beta-\frac{i\omega}{\kappa};1-\frac{iw}{\kappa};-e^{2\kappa x}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle g(x)=e^{i\omega x}\left(e^{-2\kappa x}+1\right)^{\beta}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\beta,\beta-\frac{i\omega}{\kappa};1-\frac{iw}{\kappa};-e^{-2\kappa x}\right),

(29)

where

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{1-4\frac{V}{\kappa^{2}}}\right)

(30)

and
\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;z\right)
is the ordinary hypergeometric function.
For large values of

, we can expand the hypergeometric functions that appear in the modified Wronskian (
19

\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;-z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{-a}\left(\frac{\Gamma(b-a)\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-a)}-\frac{a\Gamma(-a+b-1)\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(-a+c-1)z}+O\left(z^{-2}\right)\right)

(31)

\displaystyle+z^{-b}\left(\frac{\Gamma(a-b)\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(c-b)}-\frac{b\Gamma(a-b-1)\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(-b+c-1)z}+O\left(z^{-2}\right)\right),

\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;-\frac{1}{z}\right)=1-\frac{ab}{cz}+O\left(z^{-2}\right).

After expanding the Wronskian (
19

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa(2\omega+i\epsilon)}{\omega\Gamma\left(1-\beta-\frac{i\omega}{\kappa}\right)\Gamma\left(\beta-\frac{i\omega}{\kappa}\right)}+\frac{\epsilon\sin(\pi\beta)e^{2i\omega L}\Gamma\left(\frac{i\omega}{\kappa}\right)}{\pi\Gamma\left(1-\frac{i\omega}{\kappa}\right)}\approx 0.

In the vicinity of the low-lying modes, where

\displaystyle-i\frac{n!}{\kappa}\delta\omega_{n}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\beta-\frac{i\omega}{\kappa}\right)}

(34)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\sin(\pi\beta)\text{csch}\left(\frac{\pi\omega_{n}}{\kappa}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\beta-\frac{i\omega_{n}}{\kappa}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1-\frac{i\omega_{n}}{\kappa}\right)^{2}}\frac{\omega_{n}\epsilon}{\kappa(\epsilon-2i\omega_{n})}e^{2i\omega_{n}L},

where
\delta\omega_{n}=\omega-\omega_{n}\ll\kappa
.
The above result shows that, for large values of
L

we will have an outward spiral as
L
increases, since
\delta\omega_{n}\propto e^{2i\omega_{n}L}=e^{2iL\Re\omega_{n}}e^{-2L\Im\omega_{n}}
and
\Im\omega_{n}<0

II.2
Outward spiral in a disjoint effective potential with a delta-function perturbation

It is instructive to examine the physical nature of the spiral for disjoint potentials.
To this end, we turn to investigate the effect of a delta-function type metric perturbation when it is disjointed from the original black hole effective potential.
It is worth noting that this case is merely a simplified scenario already explored extensively in the literature

(
)

.

The main barrier mimics the black hole’s effective potential, and a minor delta function is placed away from it, representing a perturbation to the main barrier.
Let us denote the height and width of the main potential barrier by
h
and
\sigma
and place it at the origin
x=0

The delta-function will be placed at
x=L\gg\sigma
with a strength
\epsilon\ll h

Specifically, the effective potential reads

;
19
;
9

V(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&x\leq-\frac{\sigma}{2}\\
\\
h&-\frac{\sigma}{2}<x<\frac{\sigma}{2}\\
\\
0&\frac{\sigma}{2}\leq x<L\\
\\
\epsilon\delta(x-L)&x\geq L\end{array}\right.~.

(35)

According to
7
, the QNMs are governed by the roots of the Wronskian

W_{1+2}=-2i\omega[m_{1}^{++}(\omega)m_{2}^{++}(\omega)+e^{2i\omega L}m_{1}^{-+}(\omega)m_{2}^{+-}(\omega)]=0,

(36)

where
m_{1}^{++}
,
m_{2}^{++}
,
m_{1}^{-+}
, and
m_{2}^{+-}
are the matching coefficients defined in
7
.
For this potential,

\displaystyle m_{1}^{++}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{i\sigma\omega}}{4\omega\hat{\omega}}\left[e^{i\sigma\hat{\omega}}(\omega-\hat{\omega})^{2}-e^{-i\sigma\hat{\omega}}(\omega+\hat{\omega})^{2}\right],

\displaystyle m_{2}^{++}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\epsilon}{2i\omega},

\displaystyle m_{1}^{-+}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-i\sigma\omega}}{2\omega\hat{\omega}}ih\sin{(\sigma\hat{\omega})},

\displaystyle m_{2}^{+-}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\epsilon}{2i\omega},

(37)

where
\hat{\omega}=\sqrt{\omega^{2}-h}
.
For a perturbative barrier,
m_{2}^{+-}(\omega)\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
, and one recovers the QNM spectrum of the unpurturbed potential from the equation

.

As shown in
6
, one then can find the deviation from the low-lying modes
\omega_{n}
(
\delta\omega_{n}=\omega-\omega_{n}

\delta\omega_{n}\approx-\frac{m_{1}^{-+}(\omega_{n})m_{2}^{+-}(\omega_{n})}{m_{1}^{++}{{}^{\prime}}(\omega_{n})m_{2}^{++}(\omega_{n})}e^{2i\omega_{n}L}=\frac{\epsilon\hat{\omega}_{n}^{2}e^{-2i\sigma\omega_{n}}}{2\sigma\omega_{n}\hat{\omega}_{n}\left[\hat{\omega}_{n}+i\omega_{n}\cot\left(\sigma\hat{\omega}_{n}\right)\right]-2ih}e^{2i\omega_{n}L}~.

(38)

This is an outward spiral in the complex plane as

34

L

III
Thin rectangular perturbation of a continuous effective potential

IV
The impact of the shape of the perturbative barrier on the instability of the fundamental mode

In this section, we consider two perturbative barriers with different shapes. The first case is a combination of a Pöschl-Teller potential with a piecewise linear potential as the perturbative barrier of the form

V(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}V_{\mathrm{PT}}(x)&&x\leq L\\
\\
V_{\mathrm{PT}}(L)+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta}(x-L)&&L<x<L+\delta\\
\\
V_{\mathrm{PT}}(x)&&x\geq L+\delta\end{array}\right.,

(43)

where
\varepsilon
is the height and
\delta
is the width of the piecewise linear barrier. The solution to the wave equation in the barrier region is:

\displaystyle C\cdot\text{Ai}\left[\frac{-\omega^{2}+V_{\mathrm{PT}}(L)+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta}(x-L)}{\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)^{2/3}}\right]+D\cdot\text{Bi}\left[\frac{-\omega^{2}+V_{\mathrm{PT}}(L)+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta}(x-L)}{\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)^{2/3}}\right],

(44)

where
C
and
D
are constants, and
\text{Ai}(z)
and
\text{Bi}(z)
are the two linearly independent Airy functions.

Another case we consider is the combination of a Pöschl-Teller potential with a raised Pöschl-Teller potential as a perturbative barrier of the form

V(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}V_{\mathrm{PT}}(x)&&x\leq L\\
\\
V_{\mathrm{PT}}(x)+\varepsilon&&L<x<L+\sigma\\
\\
V_{\mathrm{PT}}(x)&&x\geq L+\sigma\end{array}\right.,

(45)

where, as usual,
\varepsilon
is the height and
\sigma

In Fig.

VI
The motion of the fundamental mode due to a barrier that diminishes as it moves away

In this section, we consider a couple of more realistic scenarios in which the size of the barrier diminishes as it moves away from the central black hole.

In the previous section, we discussed the case where the size of the perturbation on a Pöschl-Teller potential decreases proportionally to
e^{-2\kappa L}
as the perturbation moves away from the central black hole. That leads to an inward spiral. In Fig.
4
, we compare the spiral of the fundamental mode due to a barrier of a fixed size versus a barrier whose height decreases proportionally to
1/L^{2}
, which is the same rate as the Regge-Wheeler potential drops to zero when the radial coordinate
r\rightarrow\infty
. In both cases, the fundamental mode spirals away from the location of the fundamental mode of the unperturbed effective potential. However, in the latter case the fundamental mode moves away at a much slower rate. In Fig.
5

e^{-\kappa L}
, which is less than the rate
e^{-2\kappa x}
at which the Pöschl-Teller potential (
26
) decreases as
x\rightarrow\infty

This is the critical rate of the decrease in the size of the perturbation in a Pöschl-Teller potential, where a transition occurs from the outward spiral to the inward spiral.
This circular behavior can be explained analytically using the results we found in Eqs. (
34
), (
38
), and (
41

\displaystyle\delta\omega_{n}\propto e^{2i\omega_{n}L}.

(51)

Multiplying

\varepsilon
in Eq. (

\displaystyle\delta\omega_{n}\propto e^{-\kappa L}e^{2i\omega_{n}L}.

(52)

Note that for a pure Pöschl-Teller potential, the damping term of the QNM frequencies has the form

\displaystyle\Im\omega_{n}=-i\kappa\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right).

(53)

Therefore, for for the fundamental mode with
n=0
, Eq. (
52

\delta\omega_{0}\propto e^{2i\Re\omega_{0}L}
. In the complex plane, this represents a circular rotation, as shown in the left panel of Fig.

VII
The motion of the fundamental mode in a perturbed Regge-Wheeler potential

In this section, we study the motion of the fundamental mode of a perturbed Regge-Wheeler effective potential as the perturbation moves away from the central black hole. We consider two cases. In the first case, we truncate the Regge-Wheeler potential in Eq. (
2
), where we set the potential equal to zero for

. In the second case, which we call double-sided, we replace the black hole mass
M
in Eq. (

To calculate and track the fundamental mode, we use Leaver’s continued fraction method

11

This extension of Leaver’s method involves expanding the wavefunction about the location of the discontinuity, where the solutions on either side of the discontinuity are matched via a junction condition (
4

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, we investigate the (in)stability of the fundamental QNM using qualitatively different examples/scenarios, in which a small perturbation on an effective potential moves away from the central black hole. In most cases, we observe a spiral motion in the complex frequency domain. We provide analytic results, which agree well with the numerical calculations, offering a qualitative understanding of the spectral instability of the fundamental QNM.

We also show numerically, for the first time, how the fundamental QNM of the Regge-Wheeler potential behaves as a jump discontinuity moves away from the central black hole. This behavior is complex. The jump discontinuity represents a thin shell of matter with a small mass,
\delta M
, which surrounds the central black hole. As the radius of the mass shell increases, starting from the black hole horizon, the fundamental mode initially spirals outward from the location of the fundamental mode of the black hole with mass
M+\delta M
, followed by an inward spiral toward the location of the fundamental mode of the black hole with mass
M
before spiraling away.
The initial outward spiral, from the location of the fundamental mode of the black hole with mass
M+\delta M
, qualitatively resembles the outward spiral of the fundamental mode of a Pöchl-Teller potential with a jump discontinuity, shown in Fig.
3
. This makes sense since the Regge-Wheeler and Pöchl-Teller potentials can be matched perfectly in the tortoise coordinate in the region to the left of the peak of the potential.

In this paper we also:

•

Explore how decreasing the size of the perturbation as it moves away from the central black hole impacts the spiral. We show that the stability of the fundamental mode depends on the rate of decrease of the size.

•

Show numerically, with some examples, that the shape of the spiral depends only on the size of the perturbation and not its shape.

•

Demonstrate analytically why the fundamental mode in a double-sided Pöchl-Teller potential is stable.

•

Examine the effect of a rectangular barrier, both numerically and analytically, and a delta-function barrier analytically on the fundamental mode of an effective potential. We show that the effect of the delta-function perturbation agrees with that of the rectangular perturbation as its width goes to zero while its area stays constant.
