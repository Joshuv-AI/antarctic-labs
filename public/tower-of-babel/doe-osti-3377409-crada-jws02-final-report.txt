CRADA JWS02 Final Report by Michael Pravica, Professor of Physics, UNLV

 Fig. 1: X-ray source before being surrounded by lead bricks.

 A portable XRS-3 x-ray source was received in Prof. Pravica’s laboratory shortly before
November 19 from the NNSS. It was inspected by the State of Nevada on November 19th and
approved for use. Prof. Pravica, two of his graduate students and one undergraduate student
were trained by UNLV Radiation Safety Office on December 5th, 2025. Experiments were
conducted immediately thereafter (see Fig 1).

 Various depleted CR2032 batteries were taped to the x-ray beam window and were irradiated
for some hours but no change in voltage was detected in any irradiated battery. Slides of various
lithium salts including LiNO3, lithium oxalate and materials known to rapidly decompose in the
presence of x-rays (KClO4 and PbC2O4) were all irradiated (see Fig. 2 left). There was no
visible evidence of any x-ray induced damage. Raman spectra were acquired using the Pravica
group Raman spectrometer. No significant changes were observed in any of the Raman spectra
before and after irradiation (see Fig. 2 right). We concluded that no major damage was observed.

FIG. 2 (Left) Slides of various compounds that were irradiated. (Right) Lithium oxalate Raman
spectra before/after irradiation (green/Blue).

Examining Fig. 3, it is apparent that the x-ray fluence from the XRD-3 peaks near 7x104
photons/(cm2 keV pulse). The synchrotrons where we have performed x-ray photochemical
experiments such as the Advanced Photon Source are much closer to 1012 photons/(cm2 keV
pulse) suggesting that we need sources with much stronger flux.

Conclusion: The XRS-3 was insufficient for our needs.
