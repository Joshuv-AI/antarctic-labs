Real-World Outcomes of Faricimab for Polypoidal Choroidal Vasculopathy in a Multi-ethnic Cohort: A 12-Month Retrospective Study
Authors: Dun Jack Fu, Eleonora Riotto, Luke Nicholson, Sandro Fasolo, Omer Trivizki, Kimberly Spooner, Sobha Sivaprasad, Livia Faes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis study aimed to evaluate 12-month real-world outcomes of faricimab for polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV) in a multi-ethnic cohort.MethodsThis was a single-center retrospective cohort study of consecutive eyes with PCV diagnosed in routine care, treated with intravitreal faricimab, and with complete baseline and 12-month follow-up. One eye per patient was analyzed. Outcomes were reported for treatment-naïve and treatment-experienced eyes switched for persistent activity or limited durability. Primary endpoints were change in visual acuity (VA) and central subfield thickness (CST) at 12 months. Ethnicity-stratified outcomes were assessed secondarily.ResultsThe cohort comprised 159 eyes (40 treatment-naïve, 119 treatment-experienced). In treatment-naïve eyes, mean VA improved from 61 (standard deviation [SD] 14) letters at baseline by + 6.8 letters (SD 16; P < 0.01) at 12 months, with CST reduction of − 54 µm (SD 100; P < 0.01) and mean 9.6 (SD 4.2) injections. In treatment-experienced eyes, paired VA change at 12 months was minimal (+ 0.27 letters, SD 12; P > 0.05), while CST decreased significantly by − 40 µm (SD 110; P < 0.01). Eyes received a mean of 8.2 (SD 4.2) faricimab injections over 12 months. Exploratory ethnicity-stratified analyses demonstrated baseline heterogeneity across groups, with broadly favorable anatomical and functional outcomes observed across the multi-ethnic cohort, but subgroup sizes precluded comparative efficacy assessment.ConclusionsIn routine care, faricimab was associated with clinically meaningful visual improvement in treatment-naïve PCV and anatomical improvement in both treatment-naïve and -experienced eyes. Prospective studies with adequate ethnic representation are needed to validate these findings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40123-026-01457-3.Keywords: Aneurysmal type 1, Anti-angiogenic treatment, Neovascular age-related macular degeneration, Polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy, Real-world data

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV) shows ethnic variation in prevalence and phenotype, but most clinical trial evidence for PCV treatment has been generated in Asian populations.Faricimab has demonstrated efficacy and durability in neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD), and emerging clinical trial data support its use in PCV in Asia. However, real-world evidence for faricimab in multi-ethnic European PCV cohorts remains limited.This study evaluated 12-month real-world visual and anatomical outcomes of faricimab in treatment-naïve and treatment-experienced eyes with PCV in a multi-ethnic United Kingdom cohort, including exploratory ethnicity-stratified analyses. What was learned from the study? In treatment-naïve eyes, faricimab was associated with a mean visual acuity (VA) gain of + 6.8 Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study letters and a mean central subfield thickness (CST) reduction of − 54 µm at 12 months. In treatment-experienced eyes, VA was maintained, while CST decreased by − 40 µm.These findings suggest that faricimab may provide clinically meaningful functional and anatomical benefit in treatment-naïve PCV, and additional anatomical improvement with visual stability in heavily pre-treated eyes.Ethnicity-stratified analyses highlighted the multi-ethnic composition and baseline heterogeneity of the cohort. Outcomes were broadly favorable across groups, but small subgroup sizes, missing data, unadjusted analyses, and non-uniform indocyanine green angiography confirmation preclude conclusions regarding differential treatment efficacy by ethnicity.

Introduction

Polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV) describes an aneurysmal growth pattern of type 1 macular neovascularization (MNV) characterized by a branching neovascular network (BNN) terminating in aneurysmal (“polypoidal”) dilations [1–5]. This condition may present clinically with recurrent serosanguineous detachments of the retinal pigment epithelium and neurosensory retina, potentially resulting in severe vision loss [2, 3, 6]. The contemporary understanding recognizes that PCV pathogenesis involves complex interactions of contributing factors such as choroidal venous congestion (pachychoroid, venous overload chorioretinopathy), genetic predisposition, and systemic factors, leading to structural defects in neovascular networks [1, 7–9]. This heterogeneity may implicate PCV as an aneurysmal vascular response pattern to various underlying pathologies rather than a single disease entity [10].

PCV is prevalent in Asian populations, accounting for 20–50% of presumed neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) cases, but also occurs in other ethnicities, in whom the phenotype, access to the diagnostic reference standard (indocyanine green angiography [ICGA]), and treatment pathways may differ [11–13]. First formally described by Yannuzzi and colleagues in 1990 as “idiopathic polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy,” the condition’s recognition across diverse demographics emerged early [2]. Stern et al. (1985) had previously documented “multiple recurrent retinal pigment epithelial detachments” in Black women, presaging the disease’s relevance to multiple ethnic groups [6]. Despite this historical recognition, robust evidence for treatment outcomes with anti-angiogenic agents in multi-ethnic real-world cohorts remains limited [14–16]. Black patients, notwithstanding early descriptions, remain particularly under-investigated in contemporary clinical trials [17–19].

Anti-angiogenic therapy is the cornerstone for PCV management, with randomized trials demonstrating that aflibercept monotherapy achieves favorable visual and anatomical outcomes for many patients, while combination therapy with verteporfin photodynamic therapy (PDT) can increase polyp regression and reduce injection burden in selected cases [14, 15]. However, real-world delivery of PDT or combination therapies is often constrained by the availability of reference standard diagnostics, costs, therapy adherence, heterogeneous retreatment decisions and unclear consequences for long-term choroidal ischemic drive [13, 20, 21].

Although the phase 3 TENAYA and LUCERNE trials established the efficacy, durability, and safety of faricimab in nAMD, patients enrolled in Asian countries represented less than 10% of the pooled study population, and PCV-specific treatment outcomes could not be analyzed despite PCV being identified in a subset of patients [22, 23]. Dedicated evidence for faricimab in PCV is therefore still developing. Current data are emerging mainly from the ongoing open-label SALWEEN study in Asian populations and from early retrospective series, including comparative data against aflibercept, while the MONDEGO trial has been initiated to evaluate faricimab in White patients with treatment-naïve PCV [24–26]. Nevertheless, real-world evidence in PCV remains sparse, particularly in multi-ethnic cohorts [27]. We therefore evaluated 12-month functional and anatomical outcomes of faricimab for PCV in a multi-ethnic cohort, with descriptive ethnicity-stratified analyses.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This retrospective, observational, real-world evidence study analyzed routinely collected clinical data from a tertiary retinal service—Moorfields Eye Hospital NHS Foundation Trust, London, UK. We reviewed electronic medical records of consecutive patients treated with faricimab for optical coherence tomography (OCT)-confirmed PCV. The study adhered to STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) reporting guidelines for observational research. This retrospective observational study used routinely collected clinical data and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was registered with the Moorfields Eye Hospital NHS Foundation Trust Clinical Audit Department (registration number MEH1829) and was exempt from the requirement for institutional review board approval. Individual patient consent was not required because the study was retrospective, non-interventional, and used routinely collected clinical data.

Patient Selection and Cohort Definition

Eyes were eligible if they had: (i) PCV diagnosed in routine clinical care by OCT and/or multimodal imaging, confirmed subsequently by authors (ER and LF); (ii) received intravitreal faricimab treatment (6.0 mg/0.05 ml); (iii) complete baseline data including demographics, visual acuity (VA), and OCT imaging; and (iv) at least 12 months of follow-up.

Eyes were excluded for: (i) incomplete baseline data; (ii) loss to follow-up prior to 12 months; or (iii) coexisting macular pathology significantly affecting VA assessment (e.g., advanced glaucoma, epiretinal membrane requiring surgical intervention, macular hole). When bilateral PCV was present, the eye with superior baseline VA was selected for analysis to maintain statistical independence.

Two cohorts were analyzed: treatment-naïve patients receiving faricimab as first-line anti-angiogenic therapy, and “treatment-experienced” eyes transitioning from ranibizumab, aflibercept, or bevacizumab for persistent disease activity or suboptimal response.

Diagnosis of PCV

Reflecting contemporary real-world clinical practice, indocyanine green angiography (ICGA) confirmation was not mandated. The initial diagnosis of PCV was made by the treating retina specialist in routine care. For study inclusion, diagnoses were retrospectively confirmed by two authors (ER and LF) using the clinical record and available imaging. This confirmation was not performed as a prospectively masked grading exercise, and formal interobserver agreement was not calculated. This pragmatic diagnostic approach aligns with Asia–Pacific Ocular Imaging Society (APOIS) non-ICGA diagnostic criteria, which demonstrate high diagnostic accuracy [4, 28]. Specifically, diagnostic criteria included: (i) clinical record documentation explicitly specifying “PCV” or “polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy” as the working diagnosis; and/or (ii) presence of highly suggestive OCT features as per APOIS criteria; and/or (iii) characteristic funduscopic features including orange-red subretinal nodules, massive submacular hemorrhage, or serosanguineous pigment epithelial detachment [4, 28].

Treatment Protocol

Faricimab dosing followed the local service protocol in which three monthly “loading” injections (months 0–3) were intended, followed by OCT-guided treat-and-extend maintenance. In “treatment-experienced” eyes, a ‘re-loading’ sequence could be used at clinician discretion. Retreatment decisions were based on OCT evidence of subretinal fluid (SRF) or intraretinal fluid (IRF), hemorrhage, and functional change.

Data Collection and Outcome Measures

Primary outcome measures included VA measured in Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letter score at 4-m distance using standardized clinic protocols, assessed at baseline and months 1, 3, 6, and 12. Categorical vision analysis classified patients as ≤ 35 letter score (poor vision, approximate Snellen equivalent 6/60 or worse), 36–69 letter score (moderate vision, approximate Snellen 6/60 to 6/12), or ≥ 70 letter score (good vision, approximate Snellen 6/12 or better, meeting United Kingdom driving vision standards). Secondary outcome measures included: central subfield thickness (CST) measured using spectral-domain OCT automated segmentation algorithms at identical timepoints, defined as mean retinal thickness within the central 1-mm diameter zone of the ETDRS grid; central choroidal thickness (CCT) when available via enhanced-depth imaging OCT protocols, injection frequency over 12 months; treatment retention rate at 12 months (proportion continuing faricimab versus switching to alternative therapy); proportion completing the induction (“loading”) phase; and qualitative assessment of IRF and SRF presence or absence on OCT. Demographic data collected included age at treatment initiation, sex as recorded in the electronic medical record using male/female categories, self-reported or clinically documented ethnicity (grouped as Asian/Asian British, Black/African/Caribbean, White, “Other” including Mixed, or Unknown/Not stated), and ocular history including previous intravitreal treatments for AMD and disease duration. Gender identity was not captured in the routinely collected clinical dataset.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous data are summarized as mean (standard deviation [SD]) and median (interquartile range [IQR]); categorical data as n (percentage, %). Continuous outcomes were analyzed using paired t-tests comparing baseline with month 12 within each cohort, as distributions of change scores were normal on visual inspection and formal normality testing.

Categorical outcomes (vision category shifts and SRF/IRF resolution) were compared using χ2 tests. As these analyses are post hoc, all P values presented are nominal. Ethnicity-stratified outcomes are presented descriptively only as means, medians, and proportions, with unadjusted between-group differences without inferential testing. This reflects the limited size of the ethnic subgroups, the uninterpretable composition of the “Unknown/Not stated” category, and the confounding of ethnicity by baseline severity, prior treatment exposure, and care-pathway differences. Sex-stratified analyses were not performed because the study was not designed or powered to assess sex-specific treatment effects, and the prespecified exploratory subgroup analyses focused on ethnicity.

Complete case analysis was performed without missing data imputation. Missing data rates were explicitly reported for each variable. All data analyses were performed using R, available in the public domain from R Core Team 2020 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Patient Flow

From an initial screening population of 1031 patients with a clinical diagnosis of PCV, 257 received faricimab therapy (307 eyes), 208 had complete baseline data (208 eyes), and 159 completed 12-month follow-up (159 eyes). The final analytical cohort comprised 40 treatment-naïve patients and 119 treatment-experienced patients (see Fig. 1).

Patient flow diagram

Demographics and Baseline Clinical Characteristics

The treatment-naïve cohort (N = 40) exhibited a mean age of 72 years (SD 10) with 57.5% female representation. Ethnicity distribution comprised 25% Asian or Asian British, 15% Black or African/Caribbean, 40% Whites, 5% Other ethnic backgrounds, and 15% Unknown or not stated.

Baseline mean VA was 61.4 (SD 14) ETDRS letter score with 12.5% ≤ 35 letter score (poor vision), 57.5% 36–69 letter score (moderate visual impairment), and 30.0% ≥ 70 letter score (good vision). Mean baseline CST was 300 (SD 100) μm and mean central choroidal thickness (CCT) was 240 (SD 110) μm. SRF was present in 72.5% of eyes and IRF in 32.5%. Pachyvessels were identified in 17.5% of patients with available enhanced-depth imaging (12.5% missing values); 87.5% completed therapy induction of 3 monthly injections within 2 months.

The treatment-experienced cohort (N = 119) demonstrated mean age at switch of 71 years (SD 9.4) with 58.8% female representation, and ethnicity distribution of 16.8% Asian or Asian British, 7.6% Black or African/Caribbean, 28.6% Whites, 17.6% “Other”, and 29.4% Unknown or Not stated.

At the time of the switch, the treatment-experienced cohort observed a mean VA of 64 (SD 16) letter score, with 6.7% ≤ 35 letter score, 46.2% 36–69 letter score, and 47.1% ≥ 70 letter score. Mean CST at the switch was 270 μm (SD 110) and a CCT of 210 μm (SD 83). SRF was present in 45.4% and IRF in 31.9%. Choroidal pachyvessels were observed in 10.9% of eyes (11.8% missing values). Prior treatment history included a mean of 34 injections (SD 30) administered over a mean duration of 3.7 years (SD 3.5 years); 81.5% completed therapy induction at initiation of anti-angiogenic treatment and 63.9% at switch to faricimab treatment (see Table 1).

Categorical vision distribution shifts revealed clinically significant changes. The proportion with poor vision (≤ 35 letter score) decreased from 12.5 to 5.0% (60% relative reduction), moderate vision (36–69 letter score) decreased from 57.5 to 37.5% (35% relative reduction), and notably, good vision (≥ 70 letter score) increased from 30.0 to 55.0% (83% relative increase P < 0.05). This resulted in over half of patients achieving sufficient VA to meet driving standards in the included eye by 12 months (see Table 2).

12-Months Anatomical Outcomes in Treatment-Naïve Cohort

Mean CST decreased − 54 μm (SD 100, P < 0.01) from baseline 300 μm (SD 100) to 240 (SD 94) μm at 12 months. Anatomical improvement followed an early response pattern: − 57 μm (SD 86) reduction at month 1, -61 μm (SD 89) reduction at month 6 (P < 0.001), with modest increase from month 6 to month 12 (− 54 μm, SD 100). In terms of fluid resolution rates, SRF prevalence decreased from 72.5% at baseline to 27.5% at 12 months (62% relative reduction, P < 0.05); whilst presence of IRF decreased numerically from 32.5 to 20.0% (38% relative reduction, P > 0.05) (see Table 2).

12-Months Visual Outcomes in Treatment-Experienced Cohort

In treatment-experienced eyes, VA was maintained over 12 months. Among eyes with paired baseline and month-12 VA data, mean VA change was + 0.27 ETDRS letters (SD 12; P > 0.05). Visit-level observed mean VA was 64 letters (SD 16) at baseline and 63 letters (SD 17) at month 12; these cross-sectional means are not directly equivalent to the paired change estimate because of missing follow-up data and complete-case paired analysis.

12-Months Anatomical Outcomes in Treatment-Experienced Cohort

Despite functional stability, the treatment-experienced cohort achieved significant anatomical improvement. Mean CST decreased − 40 μm (SD 110, P < 0.001) from baseline 270 μm (SD 110) to 230 μm (SD 75) at 12 months. Anatomical improvement was progressive over time: − 26 μm (SD 100) reduction at month 6 (P < 0.05) and − 40 μm (SD 110) reduction at month 12 (P < 0.001).

Qualitative fluid status was analyzed at baseline, month 6, and month 12. SRF prevalence decreased from 45.4% at baseline to 21.8% at month 12, while IRF prevalence decreased from 31.9 to 17.6% (see Table 3).

Treatment Burden and Retention

Over 12 months, treatment-naïve eyes received a mean of 9.6 faricimab injections (SD 4.2), while treatment-experienced eyes received a mean of 8.2 injections (SD 4.2). Across the full analytical cohort, 149 of 159 eyes (93.7%) remained on faricimab at 12 months, while ten eyes (6.3%) switched to another anti-angiogenic agent or discontinued faricimab. Cohort-specific retention was 35 of 40 eyes (87.5%) in the treatment-naïve cohort and 114 of 119 eyes (95.8%) in the treatment-experienced cohort (Tables 2, 3).

Ethnicity-Stratified Outcomes

Ethnicity-stratified analyses demonstrated baseline heterogeneity across groups (Supplementary Table 1 and 2). In treatment-naïve eyes, mean baseline VA was lower in “Asian/Asian-British”, “Black/African/Caribbean”, and “Other” eyes than in “White eyes”, while baseline CST was broadly similar across the larger ethnic subgroups. At 12 months, mean VA gains varied numerically across groups, with concomitant anatomical improvement observed in most groups (Supplementary Tables 1–4).

Discussion

Main Findings

This retrospective study provides real-world evidence for faricimab use in PCV across a multi-ethnic cohort. Treatment-naïve eyes achieved clinically meaningful functional benefit and an increase in the proportion of eyes with good vision accompanied by marked CST reduction and substantial resolution of SRF, more so than IRF. Treatment-experienced eyes had received injections over a mean duration of almost 4 years, and maintained vision whilst achieving significant additional anatomical improvement, indicating that faricimab may provide structural benefit in chronically treated, partially refractory disease. The descriptive ethnicity-stratified analyses demonstrated baseline heterogeneity across groups. At 12 months, mean VA gains varied numerically across groups, with concomitant anatomical improvement observed in most groups. These observations are hypothesis-generating only because subgroup sizes were small and no inferential comparisons were performed.

Results in Context of Existing Literature

Comparison to Major PCV Clinical Trials

Visual outcomes in our treatment-naïve cohort fall within the range reported in dedicated PCV studies, although the mean gain was more modest than that observed in some pivotal trials [14–16, 22–24]. The PLANET trial reported mean best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) gains of + 10.7 letter score at 52 weeks with aflibercept monotherapy in a predominantly Asian population [15]. The ATLANTIC trial, conducted in a predominantly White cohort, reported median BCVA gain of + 6 letter score with aflibercept at 52 weeks, more closely aligned with our mean + 6.8-letter improvement at 12 months [16]. In SALWEEN, a phase 3b/4 open-label study of faricimab in Asian patients with PCV, 1-year conference data showed a mean BCVA gain of + 8.9 letters, averaged over weeks 40, 44, and 48, with most of the gain already achieved by week 16 (+ 7.7 letters) during protocolized induction [24].

Real-world evidence broadly supports these benchmarks. De Salvo and colleagues reported + 8.3 letters at 12 months in a British White PCV cohort treated with anti-angiogenic monotherapy with a lower annual injection number than observed in our cohort [13]. A recent retrospective comparative study reported comparable 12-month outcomes between faricimab and aflibercept in treatment-naïve PCV [25].

In addition to differences in study design and outcome reporting, several cohort factors may contribute to the comparatively smaller functional gain observed here, including higher baseline vision, heterogeneity in PCV phenotype and diagnostic certainty in real-world practice, and variability in early treatment delivery and interval extension.

Real-world treatment-experienced series suggest that some refractory PCV eyes may achieve improved anatomical control and extended injection intervals after switching [29].

Ethnicity-Stratified Outcomes

The multi-ethnic composition of this cohort is clinically relevant because PCV prevalence, phenotype, and diagnostic pathways differ across populations. Rather than demonstrating ethnicity-specific efficacy, our findings suggest that faricimab was associated with favorable outcomes in a real-world cohort that included Asian, Black/African/Caribbean, White, and other ethnic groups. The numerical variation in VA change should be interpreted in light of baseline VA differences, possible ceiling effects, small subgroup sizes, missing data, and unmeasured care-pathway factors. Importantly, the absence of a consistent ethnicity-specific anatomical pattern argues against overinterpretation of functional differences. These findings support the need for prospective PCV studies with deliberate inclusion of underrepresented groups, standardized multimodal imaging, and prespecified subgroup analyses.

Treatment Burden Observations

The treatment-naïve cohort required a mean 9.6 injections over 12 months, exceeding typical nAMD treatment burden reported in contemporary trials (approximately 6–7 injections in the first year) [22, 23, 30]. This elevated injection frequency may suggest PCV necessitates more intensive anti-angiogenic therapy compared with nAMD lacking aneurysmal growth patterns, potentially reflecting a multifactorial pathophysiology [1, 5].

Temporal Pattern of Visual Improvement

Treatment-naïve patients demonstrated continued VA gains throughout the 12-month observation period, contrasting with typical nAMD patterns where visual gains frequently plateau after initial 3 to 6 months [15, 24, 30, 31]. This sustained improvement trajectory may reflect real-world care pathways, ongoing polypoidal lesion regression, progressive resolution of chronic exudation, or distinct disease biology characterizing PCV compared with conventional nAMD [14, 24]. Alternatively, this pattern may be a catch-up phenomenon in patients with delayed treatment responses. Longer follow-up with serial ICGA would be required to correlate temporal visual improvement patterns with polypoidal lesion closure rates. This observation raises the importance of achieving early disease control and avoiding premature extension in eyes with persistent activity.

Treatment-Experienced Cohort Anatomical-Functional Dissociation

The substantial anatomical improvement in treatment-experienced patients without corresponding immediate visual gains is noteworthy. Several mechanistic explanations warrant consideration. First, chronic disease may have caused irreversible fibrosis, photoreceptor damage, outer retinal atrophy, or retinal pigment epithelium dysfunction that limits visual recovery potential despite improved exudative control [32, 33]. Second, improvement of exudative control may confer future vision preservation by reducing risks of subretinal fibrosis, and further photoreceptor loss, effects that may only manifest over extended follow-up periods [34, 35]. Third, faricimab’s dual inhibition mechanism may achieve superior vessel normalization and reduced vascular permeability [22, 23]. This dissociation suggests anatomical improvement could be a clinically valuable endpoint even when immediate functional gains are absent, particularly in chronic, heavily treated eyes where preservation of remaining visual function constitutes a realistic treatment goal.

Limitations of this Study

Several important limitations warrant explicit acknowledgement. The retrospective, observational design precludes causal inferences and introduces selection bias, confounding by indication, and unmeasured confounding variables [36]. Moreover, absence of routine ICGA confirmation creates potential diagnostic misclassification, particularly because diagnoses were not established by prospectively masked graders and formal interobserver agreement was not available [37]. Furthermore, we were not able to assess polypoidal lesion regression rates, which was an important efficacy endpoint in Asian PCV trials [15, 16, 24]. Choroidal thickness was not systematically captured: enhanced-depth imaging was performed at clinician discretion, with substantial missingness (40% at baseline in treatment-naïve eyes and up to 65% in treatment-experienced eyes), limiting subgroup analysis of pachychoroid features as biomarkers of treatment response [7, 8, 38]. Although cumulative pre-switch treatment exposure and disease duration were captured, the treatment interval immediately preceding faricimab initiation and the injection frequency during the 12 months before switch were not extracted. Therefore, we could not directly assess whether faricimab reduced treatment burden compared with the immediate pre-switch regimen. Sample sizes within individual ethnic subgroups were small, with substantial missing data, limiting statistical power for ethnicity-stratified analyses. We therefore report these comparisons as exploratory and do not draw definitive conclusions regarding ethnic differences in treatment response. Several sources of selection and attrition bias merit consideration: switch decisions were clinician-driven and unobserved, ICGA was not uniformly performed and differential loss-to-follow-up across ethnic groups could not be quantified.

Finally, sex-stratified outcomes were not analyzed, and gender identity was not available in the routinely collected clinical dataset.

Implications for Research

Several strategic research directions may be prioritized to advance PCV understanding and optimize treatment approaches. First, prospective studies with ICGA-confirmed PCV across ethnicities within unified study designs are a high priority [11–13, 17]. While the MONDEGO trial will provide important data in predominantly White populations, patients of Black/African/Caribbean ethnicity remain critically understudied [17, 26].

Second, studies examining whether baseline multimodal imaging features predict faricimab treatment response, durability, or the need for switching would be an important step towards personalized treatment selection. A closely related question is which patients should be offered primary faricimab rather than other anti-angiogenic agents, and which patients benefit from an early switch versus continued treatment or late switching. Given the progressive and often irreversible nature of macular damage once fibrosis is established, it is plausible that there is a “window of opportunity” in which initiating or switching to faricimab confers maximal functional benefit. Defining this temporal window and its phenotypic correlates should be a specific focus of future prospective trials.

Third, the well-documented ethnic differences in PCV presentation likely reflect complex interactions between genetic susceptibility, environmental exposures, and systemic physiology [9, 11–13]. Genetic studies evaluating whether specific single nucleotide polymorphisms associate with treatment response, and whether such associations differ across ethnic groups, could inform precision-medicine approaches. Indeed, ethnic diversity in PCV research is critically important. Treatment guidelines and clinical trial evidence derived solely from one ethnic population may not appropriately generalize to others given documented phenotypic differences (7, 11–13, 17, 38). Studies specifically considering underrepresented populations are needed to ensure equitable, evidence-based care delivery across all demographics.

Conclusions

This study provides real-world evidence supporting faricimab effectiveness for PCV across an ethnically diverse cohort. Treatment-naïve patients achieved clinically meaningful visual gains, whilst heavily pre-treated patients demonstrated vision maintenance with significant anatomical improvement. The ethnicity-stratified analyses were exploratory and hypothesis-generating and should not be interpreted as evidence of differential treatment efficacy by ethnicity. Adequately powered prospective studies with standardized multimodal imaging and prespecified subgroup analyses are needed.
