On the complexity of edge subdivision to H H -free graphs
Marta Piecyk, R. B. Sandeep

Abstract
Subdividing an edge u ​ v uv in a graph G G is the operation in which the edge u ​ v uv is deleted, and a new vertex w w together with two edges u ​ w uw and v ​ w vw is introduced. A k k -subdivision of a graph G G is a graph obtained from G G by applying k k subdivisions successively. For a graph H H , the H H -free Subdivision problem is defined as follows: given a graph G G and an integer k k , decide whether G G can be transformed into a graph without any induced copy of H H by performing at most k k edge subdivisions. We first observe that if H H is a subdivided star, that is, a tree with at most one vertex of degree at least 3 3 , or a subdivided bistar, that is, a tree with exactly two adjacent vertices of degree at least 3 3 , then the problem is trivially solvable in polynomial time. This observation can be easily extended to show that if every component of H H is either a subdivided star or a subdivided bistar, and at most one component of H H is a subdivided bistar, then H H -free Subdivision is polynomial-time solvable. On the negative side, we prove that H H -free Subdivision is NP-complete and, assuming the Exponential Time Hypothesis, cannot be solved in time 2 o ⁡ ( k ) ⋅ n O ⁡ ( 1 ) 2^{o(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)} if H H satisfies any of the following properties: • H H has minimum degree at least 2 2 , and the neighborhood of every degree- 2 2 vertex induces a K 2 K_{2} ; • the vertices of degree at least 3 3 in H H induce a graph with at least two edges; • H H has a triangle with two vertices of degree at least 3 3 ; • H H contains, as an induced subgraph, the 6 6 -vertex graph obtained from two vertex-disjoint triangles by adding an edge between them; • H H contains exactly one triangle; • H H has girth at least 4 4 ; • H H is a tree with exactly two vertices of degree at least 3 3 which are at distance 2 2 or at least 4 4 . A simple bounded search-tree algorithm gives a 2 O ⁡ ( k ) ⋅ n O ⁡ ( 1 ) 2^{O(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)} -time fixed-parameter tractable algorithm for the problem. Our lower bound shows that, for the cases in which we prove hardness, this running time cannot be substantially improved, assuming the Exponential Time Hypothesis.

Introduction

A typical graph modification problem is characterized by one or more graph operations and a target graph class.
The input consists of a graph and a budget, and the objective is to decide whether the graph can be transformed into a graph belonging to the target class by applying the operation or operations within the given budget.
Many well-known computational graph problems, such as
Vertex Cover
and
Feedback Vertex/Arc Set
, can be stated as graph modification problems.

We initiate the computational study of graph modification problems based on a very familiar graph operation: edge subdivision.
An edge subdivision of an edge
uv
of a graph
G
is the operation that removes the edge
uv
, adds a new vertex
w
, and adds the two edges
uw
and
wv
.
A
k
-subdivision of
G
is a graph obtained from
G
by applying
k
edge subdivisions successively.
For a graph class or property
\Pi
, we consider the following problem.

\Pi
-Subdivision
: Given a graph
G
and an integer
k
, decide whether
G
can be transformed into a graph with property
\Pi
by applying at most
k
edge subdivisions.

It is easy to observe that edge subdivisions do not destroy minors of the input graph.
Therefore,
\Pi
-Subdivision
is trivial when
\Pi
is a minor-closed graph class, such as the class of planar graphs or the class of forests: if the input graph contains a forbidden minor for
\Pi
, then it is a no-instance, and otherwise it is a yes-instance.
The same argument applies to induced-minor-closed graph classes, such as the class of chordal graphs.

A hereditary graph class is a graph class closed under vertex deletion.
It is well known that every hereditary graph class can be characterized by a (possibly infinite) set of forbidden induced subgraphs.
Hereditary graph classes are standard target classes for graph modification problems.
Thus, it is natural to consider the class of
H
-free graphs, that is, graphs with no induced copy of a fixed graph
H
, as a target class.
Indeed, such classes have been studied as target classes for a wide range of graph modification problems; see Table

We study the complexity of the following problem.

H
-free Subdivision
: Given a graph
G
and an integer
k
, decide whether
G
can be transformed into an
H
-free graph by applying at most
k
edge subdivisions.

A
star
is a connected graph with at most one vertex of degree at least
2
.
A
bistar
is a connected graph with exactly two vertices of degree at least
3
, and these two vertices are adjacent, and all the remaining vertices have degree
1
.
A
subdivided star
is a graph obtained from a star by subdividing its edges zero or more times.
A
subdivided bistar
is a graph obtained from a bistar by subdividing, zero or more times, only the edges other than the edge joining the two vertices of degree at least
3
.
It is easy to observe that an induced subdivided star cannot be destroyed by edge subdivisions, and the only way to destroy an induced subdivided bistar is to subdivide the edge joining its two vertices of degree at least
3
.
Thus we obtain our first observation.

Observation 1
.

Let
H
be a graph such that every connected component of
H
is either a subdivided star or a subdivided bistar, and at most one connected component of
H
is a subdivided bistar.
Then
H
-free Subdivision
is polynomial-time solvable.

We conjecture that these are the only polynomial-time solvable cases.

Conjecture 2
.

H
-free Subdivision
is NP-complete if
H
has at least two components that are subdivided bistars, or if
H
has a component that is neither a subdivided star nor a subdivided bistar.
Furthermore, assuming the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), the problem cannot be solved in time
2^{o(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)}
.

In this paper, we verify
2
for many graphs
H
.
We start with the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
H
be a graph satisfying at least one of the following properties:

•

H
has minimum degree at least
2
, and the neighborhood of every degree-
2
vertex induces a
K_{2}
;

•

the vertices of degree at least
3
induce a graph with at least two edges.

Then
H
-free Subdivision
is NP-complete.
Furthermore, assuming the ETH, the problem cannot be solved in time

2^{o(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)}
.

Next, we handle several graphs of girth
3
.
After excluding the cases covered by
Theorem

3
, the triangles containing vertices of degree at least
3
have a very restricted structure.
We call a triangle a
hanging triangle
if it contains exactly one vertex of degree at least
3
, and a
roof triangle
if it contains exactly two vertices of degree at least
3
.
The vertices of degree at least
3
in such a triangle are called its
attachment vertices
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
H
be a graph satisfying at least one of the following properties:

•

H
has a roof triangle;

•

H
has two hanging triangles whose attachment vertices are adjacent.

Then
H
-free Subdivision
is NP-complete.
Furthermore, assuming the ETH, the problem cannot be solved in time

2^{o(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)}
.

We also prove that the same holds for graphs that contain precisely one triangle.

Theorem 5
.

Let
H
be a graph that contains precisely one triangle.
Then
H
-free Subdivision
is NP-complete.
Furthermore, assuming the ETH, the problem cannot be solved in time

2^{o(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)}
.

Among graphs of girth
3
, the only graphs not covered by
Theorem

3
,
Theorem

4
, and
Theorem

5
are those obtained from graphs of girth at least
4
by attaching at least two hanging triangles in such a way that no two attachment vertices are adjacent.

We then completely verify the conjecture for non-forest graphs of girth at least
4
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
H
be a graph which is not a forest and such that
\mathrm{girth}(H)\geq 4
.
Then
H
-free Subdivision
is NP-complete.
Furthermore, assuming the ETH, the problem cannot be solved in time

2^{o(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)}
.

Finally, we consider trees and obtain the following result, where a branching vertex is a vertex with degree at least 3.

Theorem 7
.

Let
H
be a tree with exactly two branching vertices, and suppose that these two vertices are at distance
2
or at least
4
.
Then
H
-free Subdivision
is NP-complete.
Moreover, assuming the ETH, the problem cannot be solved in time

2^{o(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)}
.

Note that, among trees with exactly two branching vertices, the cases not covered by
Theorem

7
are precisely those in which the branching vertices are at distance
1
or
3
.
The former case corresponds to a subdivided bistar and is handled by
1
.
For the latter case, we do not have a reduction that yields a parameterized subexponential-time lower bound under the ETH.

A simple bounded search-tree algorithm gives a

2^{O(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)}
-time fixed-parameter tractable algorithm for the
H
-free Subdivision
problem.
Our lower bound shows that, for the cases in which we prove hardness, this running time cannot be substantially improved, assuming the ETH.

5
Trees

In this section we prove the hardness result for trees with exactly two branching
vertices. A vertex of degree at least
3
is called a
branching vertex
.
Thus, throughout this section, the tree
H
has exactly two branching vertices,
say
u
and
v
, and every vertex in
V(H)\setminus\{u,v\}
has degree at most

2
.

See
7

The proof is divided according to the distance between the two branching
vertices. If this distance is even, we reduce from

P_{3}
-free Edge Deletion
. If the distance is odd and at least
5
, we
reduce from
P_{4}
-free Edge Deletion
. In both cases the reduction uses
large rooted trees attached to the vertices of the input graph. These rooted
trees are chosen so that an induced
P_{3}
or
P_{4}
in the input graph can be
extended to an induced copy of
H
. Subdividing an edge sufficiently many times
plays the role of deleting that edge.

Lemma 17
.

Let
H
be a tree with exactly two branching vertices
u
and
v
.
If
\mathrm{dist}_{H}(u,v)
is even and at least
2
, then

H
-free Subdivision
is
NP
-complete.
Moreover, assuming the ETH, the problem cannot be solved in time

2^{o(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)}
.

Proof.

Let
d:=\mathrm{dist}_{H}(u,v)
. By assumption,
d=2r
for some integer
r\geq 1
.
Let
P
be the unique
u
–
v
path in
H
. Every component of

H-V(P)
is a path attached to either
u
or
v
. Let
\lambda
be the
maximum length of such a pendant path, and let

c:=\max\{\deg_{H}(u)-1,\deg_{H}(v)-1\}.

Here the term
-1
accounts for the edge of
P
incident with the corresponding
branching vertex.

We reduce from
P_{3}
-free Edge Deletion
, which is
NP
-complete
and, assuming the ETH, admits no
2^{o(k^{\prime})}\cdot n^{O(1)}
-time algorithm.

Let
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
be an instance of
P_{3}
-free Edge Deletion
. We construct
an instance
(G,K)
of
H
-free Subdivision
, where

K:=dk^{\prime}.

For each
q\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}
, define a rooted tree
A_{q}
as follows.
Start with a path of length
q
from the root
\rho_{q}
to a vertex
b_{q}
.
Then attach
c
pendant paths of length
\lambda
to
b_{q}
.
When
q=0
, we have
\rho_{q}=b_{q}
.

We obtain
G
from
G^{\prime}
as follows. For every vertex
x\in V(G^{\prime})
and every

q\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}
, attach
K+1
fresh copies of
A_{q}
by identifying
their roots with
x
. All these copies are otherwise vertex-disjoint.

We claim that
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
is a yes-instance of

P_{3}
-free Edge Deletion
if and only if
(G,K)
is a yes-instance of

H
-free Subdivision
.

First suppose that there is a set
F\subseteq E(G^{\prime})
with
|F|\leq k^{\prime}
such
that
G^{\prime}-F
is
P_{3}
-free. Thus every connected component of
G^{\prime}-F
is a
clique. Let
J
be the graph obtained from
G
by subdividing every edge of

F
exactly
d
times. The number of subdivisions is
d|F|\leq dk^{\prime}=K
.

We show that
J
is
H
-free. Suppose, for a contradiction, that
J
contains
an induced copy of
H
. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the two branching vertices
of this copy. Since every vertex introduced by subdivision has degree
2
, and
since the only vertices of the attached gadgets with degree at least
3
are
the original vertices of
G^{\prime}
and the vertices
b_{q}
, each of
\alpha
and

\beta
is either an original vertex of
G^{\prime}
or a vertex
b_{q}
in some attached
copy of
A_{q}
.

For such a branching vertex
\alpha
, define its root
x_{\alpha}\in V(G^{\prime})
as
the original vertex to which its gadget is attached. If
\alpha
itself is an
original vertex, then set
x_{\alpha}=\alpha
and
q_{\alpha}=0
; otherwise let

q_{\alpha}
be the distance from
\alpha
to
x_{\alpha}
inside its copy of

A_{q_{\alpha}}
. Thus
0\leq q_{\alpha}\leq r-1
. Define
x_{\beta}
and

q_{\beta}
analogously.

The distance between
\alpha
and
\beta
in the induced copy of
H
is exactly

d
. Therefore the subpath between
x_{\alpha}
and
x_{\beta}
in the part of

J
obtained from
G^{\prime}
has length

p=d-q_{\alpha}-q_{\beta}.

Since
q_{\alpha}+q_{\beta}\leq 2r-2=d-2
, we have

. Also
p\leq d
.

No edge of
F
can occur on this subpath, because every edge of
F
was replaced
by a path of length
d+1
. Hence this subpath is an induced path of length at
least
2
in
G^{\prime}-F
. But
G^{\prime}-F
is
P_{3}
-free, and hence each of its connected
components is a clique. A clique contains no induced path of length at least

2
. This contradiction shows that

is
H
-free.

Conversely, suppose that
G
can be transformed into an
H
-free graph

by
at most
K
subdivisions. For each edge
e\in E(G^{\prime})
, let
s(e)
denote the
number of times

is subdivided in this solution. Define

F:=\{e\in E(G^{\prime}):s(e)\geq d\}.

Since the total number of subdivisions is at most
K=dk^{\prime}
, we have

|F|\leq k^{\prime}.

We claim that
G^{\prime}-F
is
P_{3}
-free. Suppose not. Let
xyz
be an induced

P_{3}
in
G^{\prime}-F
. Thus

.

First suppose that at least one of the edges

and

is subdivided in

J
. Without loss of generality, assume that

is subdivided
s
times,
where
1\leq s<d
. Then in
J
, the edge

is replaced by an induced path
of length

p=s+1,

where
2\leq p\leq d
. Since

0\leq d-p\leq d-2=2r-2,

we can choose integers
q_{x},q_{y}\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}
such that

q_{x}+q_{y}=d-p.

For each fixed original vertex and each fixed value of
q
, there are
K+1

attached copies of
A_{q}
, while the solution uses at most
K
subdivisions in
total. Hence at least one copy of
A_{q_{x}}
attached to
x
and at least one
copy of
A_{q_{y}}
attached to

are untouched by the solution. Using these

, and suitable pendant
paths of length at most

inside the two gadgets, we obtain an induced

in

. This contradicts the assumption that

is
H
-free.

Therefore neither
xy
nor

is subdivided in

. Since

is an
induced
P_{3}
in
G^{\prime}-F
, the vertices
x
and

are not adjacent in
G^{\prime}-F
.
Thus in

there is no edge

; if

, then it has been replaced by a

, and if

, then it is absent.
Consequently,

induce a path of length
2
in
J
.

Now choose

q_{x}=q_{z}=r-1.

Then

q_{x}+2+q_{z}=(r-1)+2+(r-1)=2r=d.

Again, since there are
K+1
copies of each required gadget and at most
K

subdivisions in total, we can choose untouched copies of
A_{r-1}
attached to

x
and
z
. Together with the induced path

, and suitable pendant
paths inside the two untouched gadgets, these form an induced copy of
H
in

J
, a contradiction.

Hence
G^{\prime}-F
is
P_{3}
-free. Since
|F|\leq k^{\prime}
, the instance
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
is a
yes-instance of
P_{3}
-free Edge Deletion
.

This completes the correctness of the reduction.
Now the statements follow from
Theorem

3
.
