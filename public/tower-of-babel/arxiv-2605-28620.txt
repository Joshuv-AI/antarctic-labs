Smooth atlas stratified spaces, K-Homology Orientations and Gysin maps. Part 2.
Pierre Albin, Markus Banagl, Paolo Piazza

Abstract
. In this Part 2 of our article we give a detailed discussion of the compatibility between the analytic Gysin maps we have defined in Part 1 and the topological Gysin maps defined by the second author. A significant rôle is played by a bordism-like description of K-homology due to Jakob which is closely related to the geometric K-homology theory of Baum and Douglas. We give a self-contained proof of the equivalence of the former with the analytic K-homology theory of Kasparov. As an intermediate step towards proving our main result we use Thom’s transversality theorem to describe Gysin maps compatibly with Jakob’s definition of K-homology.

Introduction

This paper is a continuation of
[
ABP25
]
, which we will refer to as Part 1.
In Part 1, we studied Gysin maps (also known as wrong-way maps or transfer maps) in K-homology of Witt pseudomanifolds. To each oriented Witt pseudomanifold
M
of dimension
n,
we associated a class

\mathrm{sign}_{K}(M)=2^{-\lfloor n/2\rfloor}[D_{M}^{\mathrm{sign}}]\in{\operatorname{K}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{n}(M)[\tfrac{1}{2}],

where
[D_{M}^{\mathrm{sign}}]
is the class of the signature operator (of an appropriate ‘wedge metric’), and we showed that this assignment is an orientation of
M
in K-homology with
2
inverted.
(As noted in
loc cit
, one reason we use the signature operator of an oriented Witt space instead of the Dirac operator of a
\mathrm{Spin}^{c}
stratified space is that the former is guaranteed to be Fredholm for any choice of wedge metric while the latter is not; in order for the signature operator to induce an orientation we then need to invert
2.
) Among other results, we showed that whenever
M
and
N
are oriented Witt spaces and

f:M\longrightarrow N

is either a fiber bundle map (where we do allow for the fibers to be Witt), or a normally nonsingular inclusion then there is a Gysin map

f^{!}_{{\operatorname{an}}}:{\operatorname{K}}^{{\operatorname{an}}}_{*}(N)[\tfrac{1}{2}]\longrightarrow{\operatorname{K}}^{{\operatorname{an}}}_{*}(M)[\tfrac{1}{2}]

which maps the K-orientation of
N
to that of
M
, viz.

f^{!}_{{\operatorname{an}}}\mathrm{sign}_{K}(N)=\mathrm{sign}_{K}(M).

We also showed that
\mathrm{sign}_{K}(M)
is compatible with the Sullivan-Siegel orientation of
M,

\Delta(M)\in{\operatorname{KO}}^{{\operatorname{top}}}_{n}(M)[\tfrac{1}{2}].
Specifically we showed that if
c:{\operatorname{KO}}\to{\operatorname{K}}
denotes complexification and
\Psi^{2}:{\operatorname{KO}}[\tfrac{1}{2}]\to{\operatorname{KO}}[\tfrac{1}{2}]
the stable second Adams operation, then under the natural isomorphism
\Phi
between
{\operatorname{K}}^{\operatorname{top}}_{n}(M)
and
{\operatorname{K}}^{\operatorname{an}}_{n}(M)
the class

c(\Psi^{2})^{-1}\Delta(M)\in{\operatorname{K}}^{\operatorname{top}}_{n}(M)[\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}]

corresponds to the class
\mathrm{sign}_{K}(M).

In the present Part 2 we show that the analytic Gysin maps we defined in Part 1 are compatible with topologically defined Gysin maps. Namely we show that the map

\lambda=\Phi\circ c\circ(\Psi^{2})^{-1}:{\operatorname{KO}}_{*}^{{\rm top}}(M)[\tfrac{1}{2}]\longrightarrow{\operatorname{K}}^{\operatorname{an}}_{*}(M)[\tfrac{1}{2}]

intertwines the topological and analytic Gysin maps,

\lambda\circ f^{!}_{\operatorname{top}}=f^{!}_{\operatorname{an}}\circ\lambda

if
f
is a normally non-singular inclusion or a fiber bundle projection.
In contrast to Part 1 we allow
f
to be a normally nonsingular inclusion
of arbitrary finite CW-complexes (as opposed to Witt pseudomanifolds),
but when
f
is a fibration we require that the associated structure group is
compact Lie to define the topological Gysin map.
Via the Mostow-Palais equivariant embedding theorem, this enables
sufficient control over fiberwise embeddings into Euclidean spaces over
the base. More generally, it is often possible to work with
block bundles and replace fiberwise with blockwise embeddings.
In that setting, a topologically Gysin map
had been defined by the second author in
[
Ban24
,
Ban25
]
.
That map agrees with the topological Gysin maps considered here, since
locally trivial fiber bundles are in particular block bundles, and the Thom
classes correspond under the map that forgets the projection and only
remembers the blocks.

An interesting part of our analysis is the use of a bordism description of K-homology, analogous to the geometric K-homology groups of Baum-Douglas
[
BD82
]
. Thus we make use of the theory developed by Jakob in
[
Jak98
]
where any multiplicative cohomology theory
h^{*}
on finite CW-complexes is provided with a geometric description
h^{\prime}_{*}
of its dual homology theory
h_{*}.

Classes in
h_{*}^{\prime}(X,A)
are represented by 4-tuples
(M,u(\nu_{M}),x,f)
with
M
a compact smooth manifold with (possibly empty) boundary,
u(\nu_{M})
an
h^{*}
-orientation of the stable normal bundle of
M,

x\in h^{*}(M),
and
f:(M,\partial M)\longrightarrow(X,A)
a continuous map. Two representatives represent the same class if they are related by diffeomorphism, bordism, or vector bundle modification. Jakob shows that
h_{*}^{\prime}
is naturally equivalent to
h_{*}.
We apply Jakob’s theory to the two cohomology theories
h^{*}(-)={\operatorname{K}}^{*}(-)
and
h^{*}(-)={\operatorname{K}}^{*}(-)[\tfrac{1}{2}]
and denote the corresponding
h^{\prime}_{*}(-)
by

{\operatorname{K}}^{b}_{*}(-)\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad{\operatorname{K}}^{b/2}_{*}(-),

respectively, where
b
stands for
bordism
.
For
{\operatorname{K}}^{b/2}_{*},
representing manifolds
M
are required only to be oriented
over ordinary integral homology, since then the Sullivan orientation
\Delta(M)

provides the required
{\operatorname{K}}^{*}[\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}]
-orientation away from
2
.
Using the signature operator, we define a homomorphism

\kappa:{\operatorname{K}}^{b/2}_{*}(-)\rightarrow{\operatorname{K}}^{\operatorname{an}}_{*}(-)[\tfrac{1}{2}],
analogous to the Baum-Higson-Schick homomorphism

\mu:{\operatorname{K}}^{\operatorname{geo}}(-)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{K}}^{\operatorname{an}}_{*}(-)

in the
{\operatorname{Spin}}^{c}
-case

[
BHS07
]
,
and we show that
\kappa
is an isomorphism.
The module structure of
h^{\prime}_{*}(-)
over
{\operatorname{K}}^{*}(-)
and the
{\operatorname{K}}^{*}(-)
-linearity of
\kappa

play a crucial role in our proof. We use the bordism-like description of
{\operatorname{K}}_{*}^{{\operatorname{top}}}[\tfrac{1}{2}]

as a tool in order to interpolate between the topological and the analytical description of K-homology.
Notice that our treatment of the isomorphism
\kappa
constitutes an alternative to the results of Baum-Higson-Schick and has, in our opinion, an independent interest.

We start out in section
2
, establishing some naturality results in analytic K-homology concerning the interplay between Gysin maps, pull-back maps, and cup products with orientations.
Section
3
reviews the Siegel-Sullivan orientation and its lift by the second author to spectra. In section
4
, we study the geometric descriptions of K-homology and K-homology with
2
inverted. Sections
5
and
6
show the compatibility of Gysin maps for oriented normally nonsingular inclusions in K-homology with
2
inverted and ordinary K-homology, respectively. Finally section
7
establishes the compatibility of Gysin maps in the submersive case.

In

4.
Bordism-like description of K-homology and the isomorphism
with

A significant rôle in our arguments will be played by a bordism-like description of K-homology due to Jakob

which is closely related to the geometric K-homology theory of Baum and Douglas. In this section we will recall Jakob’s definition, apply it to both ordinary K-theory and K-theory with

4.1.
Bordism-like description of
{\operatorname{K}}_{*}(-)
and
{\operatorname{K}}_{*}(-)[\tfrac{1}{2}]

Recall that given a multiplicative cohomology theory

Let

Recall that an

-Thom class
(or

The geometric group

is a compact smooth manifold with (possibly empty) boundary,

is an
h

Let us briefly write

For

such that there is a Poincaré-Lefschetz duality isomorphism

-Thom class

(M,u_{M},x|_{M},f|_{M})+(N,u_{N},x|_{N},f|_{N}),

(3)

is a compact smooth manifold with boundary,

g(\partial W\setminus(M_{1}\sqcup M_{2}))\subseteq A,

-orientations

of

and

(4)

Vector bundle modification

we declare the cycle
(M,u(\nu_{M}),x,f)
and the cycle

7.
The submersive case

In this section we compare the analytic and topological Gysin maps associated to submersions. In Part 1 we have defined the analytic Gysin map for fiber bundles of Witt spaces but it is clear that the construction only requires that the fibers be Witt spaces and works equally well for fiber bundles of finite CW-complexes as long as the fibers are Witt spaces. (The reason that we assumed in Part I that the base was also a Witt space was so that we could show that the Gysin map sent an orientation of the base to an orientation of the total space.) On the topological side, we will describe Gysin maps for fiber bundles associated to principal bundles with compact Lie structure group. The main result of this section is then the compatibility of the two definitions in this case (so one could think of the Gysin map defined in Part 1 as extending the topologically defined map beyond compact Lie structure groups).
