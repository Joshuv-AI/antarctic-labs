Anisotropic Inviscid Limit for the Navier-Stokes Equations with Transport Noise Between Two Plates
Daniel Goodair

Abstract
We investigate an anisotropic vanishing viscosity limit of the 3D stochastic Navier-Stokes equations posed between two horizontal plates, with Dirichlet no-slip boundary condition. The turbulent viscosity is split into horizontal and vertical directions, each of which approaches zero at a different rate. The underlying Cylindrical Brownian Motion driving our transport-stretching noise is decomposed into horizontal and vertical components, which are scaled by the square root of the respective directional viscosities. We prove that if the ratio of the vertical to horizontal viscosities approaches zero, then there exists a sequence of weak martingale solutions convergent to the strong solution of the deterministic Euler equation on its lifetime of existence. A particular challenge is that the anisotropic scaling ruins the divergence-free property for the spatial correlation functions of the noise.

Introduction

This work concerns the inviscid limit of an anisotropic stochastic Navier-Stokes equation, as the horizontal and vertical components of the turbulent viscosity approach zero at different rates. We consider a divergence-free solution
u
of the equation

du_{t}=-B(u_{t},u_{t})dt-\nu_{h}A_{h}u_{t}\,dt-\nu_{z}A_{z}u_{t}\,dt-\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}^{h}u_{t}\circ d\mathcal{W}_{t}-\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}^{z}u_{t}\circ d\mathcal{W}_{t}

(1)

which represents the velocity of a fluid, posed on a domain
\mathscr{O}=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times(0,1)
and supplemented with the no-slip boundary condition
u=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
. Here
\mathcal{P}
is the Leray Projector onto divergence-free vector fields with zero normal component on
\partial\mathscr{O}
,
B(u_{t},u_{t})=\mathcal{P}\left(\left(u_{t}\cdot\nabla\right)u_{t}\right)
is the nonlinear convective term,
A_{h}=-\mathcal{P}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}
is the horizontal Stokes Operator with turbulent horizontal viscosity
\nu_{h}
and
A_{z}=-\mathcal{P}\partial_{3}^{2}
is the vertical Stokes Operator with turbulent vertical viscosity
\nu_{z}
. In the Stratonovich stochastic integrals,
\mathcal{W}
denotes a Cylindrical Brownian Motion acted upon by operators
\mathcal{G}^{h}
,
\mathcal{G}^{z}
in the sense that

\mathcal{G}^{h}u_{t}\circ d\mathcal{W}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}^{h}_{i}u_{t}\circ dW^{i}_{t},\qquad\mathcal{G}^{z}u_{t}\circ d\mathcal{W}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}^{z}_{i}u_{t}\circ dW^{i}_{t}

where
(W^{i})
is a collection of independent standard Brownian Motions comprising
\mathcal{W}
, along with pre-assigned spatial correlation functions
(\xi_{i})
with respect to which
(\mathcal{G}^{h}_{i})
,
(\mathcal{G}^{z}_{i})
are defined. Each
\xi_{i}
is smooth, satisfies
\xi_{i}\cdot\underline{n}=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
where
\underline{n}
is the outward unit normal vector, but is not assumed to be divergence-free. The operators
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{i}
,
\mathcal{G}^{z}_{i}
are defined by

\mathcal{G}_{i}^{h}u_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}u_{t}+u_{t}^{j}\nabla\xi_{i}^{j}\right),\qquad\mathcal{G}_{i}^{z}u_{t}=\xi_{i}^{3}\partial_{3}u_{t}+u_{t}^{3}\nabla\xi_{i}^{3}

(2)

where the superscript
j
denotes the
j^{\textnormal{th}}
component mapping of the vector field. Broadly speaking, our main result is the construction of martingale weak solutions to (
1
) which converge, as
\nu_{h}
and
\frac{\nu_{z}}{\nu_{h}}
approach zero, to the strong solution
w
of the deterministic Euler equation

\partial_{t}w=-B(w,w)

(3)

posed on
\mathscr{O}
with boundary condition
w\cdot\underline{n}=0
. The convergence is in
L^{2}_{\omega}L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
, over the newly constructed probability space and on the lifetime of existence of
w
. The precise statement can be found in Theorem
3.2
.

1.1
Deterministic Theory

For a smooth bounded domain in two or three dimensions, whether or not weak solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations with no-slip boundary conditions converge, as the viscosity is taken to zero, to the strong solution of the Euler equation remains one of the outstanding problems of mathematical fluid mechanics. Positive results have been limited to very specific cases regarding analyticity of initial data or structure of the flow [
51
,
52
,
62
,
63
], whilst conditional results such as [
41
,
43
,
67
] characterise the convergence by energy dissipation in a boundary layer which is not known to hold in general. We also mention that the limit is known if one replaces the no-slip boundary condition of the Navier-Stokes equations with a Navier friction condition [
12
,
23
,
40
,
42
,
56
].

In the absence of noise, the present model was considered in [
55
]. Anisotropic viscosity, that is where
\nu_{h}\neq\nu_{z}
, is a classical feature of geophysical fluid mechanics. When the fluid is turbulent we speak of the viscosity not as the molecular kinematic viscosity but rather a turbulent viscosity, measured for example by the speed of diffusion of tracers. The Coriolis force amplifies horizontal diffusion, so that
\nu_{z}
is much smaller than
\nu_{h}
. Whilst many aspects of a complete geophysical fluid model are neglected here, the limit as
\frac{\nu_{z}}{\nu_{h}}
approaches zero is a useful setting to expose geophysical fluid phenomena. For a more complete discussion, we refer the reader to [
10
,
37
,
60
].

In the aforementioned [
55
], Masmoudi proves precisely the result that we are aiming to establish in stochastic analogy; the convergence of all Leray-Hopf weak solutions of the Navier-Stokes equation with no-slip boundary condition, as
\nu_{h}
and
\frac{\nu_{z}}{\nu_{h}}
approach zero, to the strong solution of the Euler equation. We describe the simple heuristics of the problem; as there is no physical boundary in the horizontal direction, then the passage of
\nu_{h}\rightarrow 0
is harmless and we are only concerned with the limit
\nu_{z}\rightarrow 0
alongside its formation of the boundary layer at the horizontal plates. Following the work of Kato [
41
] in establishing the equivalence between inviscid convergence and energy dissipation in a boundary layer, we anticipate that the problem boils down to showing that

\lim_{\nu_{z}\rightarrow 0}\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\nabla u_{s}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}}ds=0

(4)

where
\Gamma_{\delta}=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\left[(0,\delta)\cup(1-\delta,1)\right]
is a boundary strip of width
\delta
shrinking with
\nu_{z}
. However, more can be said. The fundamental problem in verifying the inviscid limit is the disparity between the boundary conditions, as the tangential component of
u
is prescribed to be zero at the boundary whilst there are no restrictions on the tangential component of
w
. On the other hand,
u\cdot\underline{n}=0=w\cdot\underline{n}
so the normal components of
u
and
w
match at the boundary. This suggests to us that the wild behaviour of
u
in the boundary layer concerns only the normal derivative in tangential directions; it is therefore very interesting that Kato’s criterion was extended by Wang, in [
67
] and with Temam in [
66
], to a consideration of only
tangential
derivatives in a boundary layer. This replaces the sufficient condition (
4
) with

\lim_{\nu_{z}\rightarrow 0}\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}u_{s}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}}ds=0

and by rewriting

\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}u_{s}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}}ds=\left(\frac{\nu_{z}}{\nu_{h}}\right)\nu_{h}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}u_{s}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}}ds

then, as
\nu_{h}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}u_{s}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}}ds
is bounded due to the energy inequality of Leray-Hopf solutions, if
\frac{\nu_{z}}{\nu_{h}}
approaches zero the condition holds. Rigorously, the analysis of [
55
] relies on the construction of a boundary corrector
\mathscr{B}
such that
w+\mathscr{B}
is zero on the boundary, whilst
\mathscr{B}
is only supported near the boundary and of
L^{2}_{x}
norm vanishing with
\nu_{z}
. An integration by parts is now facilitated in energy estimates on
u-w-\mathscr{B}
, and the result is achieved through a careful analysis of the many terms involved.

1.2
Structure of the Noise

There are several considerations to be made when introducing noise into this system. At the first level we ignore any coupling with the viscosity and ask what form the noise should take. We have chosen a transport-stretching noise, following the principle of
Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport
introduced by Holm in [
38
]. This yields a Stratonovich integral in the velocity equation of fluid flow, given by

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{i}u_{t}\circ dW^{i}_{t},\qquad\mathcal{G}_{i}u_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left(\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}u_{t}+u_{t}^{j}\nabla\xi_{i}^{j}\right)

where the
(\xi_{i})
are spatial correlation functions of the driving noise as previously discussed. In [
38
] the noise is derived through geometric variational principles and is shown to preserve Kelvin’s Circulation Theorem. This theory has been expanded upon across [
16
,
39
,
64
], and has run in tandem with derivations of transport noise in fluids through a Lagrangian Reynolds Decomposition and Transport Theorem given by Mémin [
57
], which has been further developed in [
9
,
19
,
61
]. The theory is bolstered by numerical analysis and data assimilation presented throughout [
8
,
13
,
15
,
21
,
22
] amongst many others. Stratonovich transport noise has also been derived following a stochastic model reduction scheme in [
20
,
26
,
49
,
54
]. All of this recent progress supports the classical ideas of [
4
,
44
,
58
,
59
], and we suggest [
7
,
25
] for a review of the topic.

Secondly, we could choose for the limiting Euler equation to be stochastic or to investigate the vanishing noise limit with viscosity. Both regimes are of independent interest, however the former is immediately limited by the available well-posedness theory of the stochastic Euler equation on a bounded domain. Indeed, whilst local well-posedness is known for a Lipschitz multiplicative noise as shown in [
28
], or for transport noise on the torus demonstrated in [
14
,
30
], the case of transport noise on a bounded domain remains open. Weak solutions were proven to exist in [
32
], however these lack the regularity required to conduct energy estimates akin to [
55
]. Consequently, we only consider the regime of vanishing noise. The next task is to decide on a viscous scaling sending the noise to zero, for which we use
\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}
having been motivated in [
46
] as the only noise scaling which leads to non-trivial limiting measures (in the limit
t\rightarrow\infty
and
\nu\rightarrow 0
) for an additive noise in two dimensions in the absence of a boundary. The significance of this scaling for energy balance is further underlined in [
45
,
47
,
48
] and has been used to study the inviscid limit problem in [
5
,
6
,
27
,
36
,
53
].

The main novelty and challenge arises due to the third consideration, which is how to introduce the anisotropy into the noise scaling. As far as we are aware, such a problem has not been considered. Our approach is to split the spatial correlation functions
(\xi_{i})
of the driving Cylindrical Brownian Motion into their horizontal and vertical components, scaling by
\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}
respectively. To express this let us fix a generalised notation
\mathcal{G}
as an operator on vector fields
\phi
,
f
defined by

\mathcal{G}_{\phi}f=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left(\phi^{j}\partial_{j}f+f^{j}\nabla\phi^{j}\right)

(5)

such that
\mathcal{G}_{i}=\mathcal{G}_{\xi_{i}}
. The horizontal and vertical components of
\xi_{i}
are given by

\xi_{i}^{h}=\left(\xi_{i}^{1},\xi_{i}^{2},0\right),\qquad\xi_{i}^{z}=\left(0,0,\xi_{i}^{3}\right)

which we scale and combine for the effective spatial correlation function

\tilde{\xi}_{i}=\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{h}+\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{z}=\left(\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{1},\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{2},\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{3}\right).

(6)

For simplicity let us define
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
by

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}=\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\xi_{i}}}
, then our anisotropic stochastic Navier-Stokes equation reads as

du_{t}=-B(u_{t},u_{t})dt-\nu_{h}A_{h}u_{t}\,dt-\nu_{z}A_{z}u_{t}\,dt-\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}u_{t}\circ d\mathcal{W}_{t}.

(7)

The operator
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
has the explicit expression

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left(\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}u_{t}+u_{t}^{j}\nabla\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}u_{t}+u_{t}^{j}\nabla\xi_{i}^{j}\right)+\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\xi_{i}^{3}\partial_{3}u_{t}+u_{t}^{3}\nabla\xi_{i}^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{i}^{h}u_{t}+\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{i}^{z}u_{t}

for
\mathcal{G}_{i}^{h}
,
\mathcal{G}_{i}^{z}
as defined in (
2
). Thus, we arrive at equation (
1
). We remark that
\mathcal{G}_{i}^{h}=\mathcal{G}_{\xi_{i}^{h}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{i}^{z}=\mathcal{G}_{\xi_{i}^{z}}
, hence the horizontal and vertical superscripting on
\mathcal{G}_{i}^{h}
and
\mathcal{G}_{i}^{z}
refers to transport and stretching along the horizontal and vertical components, respectively, of the spatial correlation functions. In addition, whilst we have chosen to incorporate the noise splitting into the operator
\mathcal{G}
, one could equivalently do this at the level of
\mathcal{W}
. Indeed if one considers

\mathcal{W}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\xi_{i}W^{i}

and decomposes into its horizontal and vertical components
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}^{h}+\mathcal{W}^{z}
where

\mathcal{W}^{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\xi_{i}^{h}W^{i},\qquad\mathcal{W}^{z}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\xi_{i}^{z}W^{i},

(8)

then one has the equivalent representation

\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}^{h}u_{t}\circ d\mathcal{W}_{t}+\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}^{z}u_{t}\circ d\mathcal{W}_{t}=\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}u_{t}\circ d\mathcal{W}^{h}_{t}+\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}u_{t}\circ d\mathcal{W}^{z}_{t}.

The splitting (
8
) also appeared in [
24
] for a Taylor-Proudman model with transport-stretching noise, a consequence of the 2D-3C nature of the equation.

1.3
Aspects of the Proof

To motivate a discussion on the main elements of the proof, let us mention some existing results on inviscid limits for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations. As a reminder, the precise statement of our result is given in Theorem
3.2
. Stochastic versions of Kato’s Criterion have been established for a vanishing additive noise in 2D [
53
], vanishing transport type noise in 3D [
36
], and non-vanishing additive noise in 2D [
69
]. For Navier boundary conditions in 2D, the limit for non-vanishing additive noise [
11
] and transport-stretching noise [
32
] has been proven. Results on invariant measures in 2D for an additive noise without physical boundary are given in [
1
,
5
,
27
]. All of these results are for the isotropic case; with anisotropy we are only aware of one result, given in [
68
], dealing with a non-vanishing horizontal viscosity and a vertical viscosity vanishing as the rotation speed of an additional Coriolis term is taken to infinity. The authors consider an additive noise acting only in the two horizontal directions, and the limit is a damped 2D stochastic Navier-Stokes equation. Therefore, the structure of the noise in (
1
) and its inviscid limit is fundamentally new.

The first step in treating (
1
) is to convert the Stratonovich equation to Itô form, where the analysis is much more favourable. One can see that the cross-variation will involve cross-terms between the horizontal and vertical viscosities. To make the conversion we use the compact expression (
7
), which by following [
31
] yields the equation

du_{t}=-B(u_{t},u_{t})\ dt-\nu_{h}A_{h}u_{t}\,dt-\nu_{z}A_{z}u_{t}\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{2}u_{t}dt-\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}u_{t}d\mathcal{W}_{t}

(9)

up to a ‘cost of a derivative’. Note that the Itô-Stratonovich corrector has the form
(\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i})^{2}u_{t}
a priori, however we use the property that
\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}=\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\mathcal{P}
shown in [
34
] Lemma 2.7 to rewrite it in the form of (
9
). We stress that this property is a not consequence of
\tilde{\xi}_{i}
being divergence-free, as indeed it is not here. In full, equation (
9
) reads as

\displaystyle du_{t}=

\displaystyle-B(u_{t},u_{t})dt-\nu_{h}A_{h}u_{t}\,dt-\nu_{z}A_{z}u_{t}\,dt-\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}^{h}u_{t}d\mathcal{W}_{t}-\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}^{z}u_{t}d\mathcal{W}_{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu_{h}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}^{h}_{i}\mathcal{G}^{h}_{i}u_{t}dt+\frac{\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}\left(\mathcal{G}^{h}_{i}\mathcal{G}^{z}_{i}u_{t}+\mathcal{G}^{z}_{i}\mathcal{G}^{h}_{i}u_{t}\right)dt+\frac{\nu_{z}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}^{z}_{i}\mathcal{G}^{z}_{i}u_{t}dt.

(10)

Even the existence of martingale weak solutions to (
10
) is unclear. Typically, the key step in showing such existence for transport noise models is using the divergence-free property of
\xi_{i}
to obtain that for vector fields
f,g
and the inner product in
L^{2}_{x}
,

\left\langle(\xi_{i}\cdot\nabla)f,g\right\rangle=-\left\langle f,(\xi_{i}\cdot\nabla)g\right\rangle

(11)

hence in energy estimates, as one meets the term combining the Itô-Stratonvoich corrector and quadratic variation,

\left\langle(\xi_{i}\cdot\nabla)(\xi_{i}\cdot\nabla)u,u\right\rangle+\left\|(\xi_{i}\cdot\nabla)u\right\|^{2}=-\left\langle(\xi_{i}\cdot\nabla)u,(\xi_{i}\cdot\nabla)u\right\rangle+\left\|(\xi_{i}\cdot\nabla)u\right\|^{2}=0.

(12)

With the additional stretching term one has to work a bit more in controlling commutators, but eventually a bound by
\left\|u\right\|^{2}
is achieved primarily due to the same fact. Without the divergence-free property we cannot come to the same conclusion; whilst we could very happily assume that
\xi_{i}
is divergence-free, this is ruined by the viscous scaling in the effective spatial correlation function
\tilde{\xi}_{i}
defined in (
6
). To circumvent the issue one could recognise that it is sufficient to have the horizontal and vertical components of
\xi_{i}
divergence-free, that is
\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{1}+\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{2}=0=\partial_{3}\xi_{i}^{3}.
However
\xi_{i}
must also satisfy the impermeability condition
\xi_{i}\cdot\underline{n}=0
on
\partial{\mathscr{O}}
, which simply says that
\xi_{i}^{3}=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\left(\{0\}\cup\{1\}\right)
. Combining with the assumption
\partial_{3}\xi_{i}^{3}=0
implies that
\xi_{i}^{3}=0
everywhere in
\mathscr{O}
, hence the underlying Cylindrical Brownian Motion would only act in two dimensions so the phenomenon that we are investigating is trivialised.

The analysis of transport noise where the spatial correlation functions are not divergence-free appears completely absent in the literature. Even in the cases where transport noise is introduced into a compressible fluid [
3
,
17
], the divergence-free assumption still appears to facilitate the analysis. We also mention that for the 2D-3C model considered in [
24
] the authors assume that
\xi_{i}
is divergence-free, which also implies that
\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{1}+\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{2}=0=\partial_{3}\xi_{i}^{3}
as
\xi_{i}
is independent of the third variable, however as their domain is the torus then
\xi_{i}^{3}
does not need to be trivial.

We progress our analysis by recognising that in place of (
11
), we have

\left\langle(\tilde{\xi}_{i}\cdot\nabla)f,g\right\rangle=-\left\langle f,(\tilde{\xi}_{i}\cdot\nabla)g\right\rangle-\left\langle f,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{j}\right)g\right\rangle

and therefore, revisiting (
12
),

\displaystyle\left\langle(\tilde{\xi}_{i}\cdot\nabla)(\tilde{\xi}_{i}\cdot\nabla)u,u\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\|(\tilde{\xi}_{i}\cdot\nabla)u\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle(\tilde{\xi}_{i}\cdot\nabla)u,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{j}\right)u\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}u\right\|+\left\|\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{3}u\right\|\right)\left\|\tilde{\xi}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|u\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\xi_{i}^{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}u\right\|+\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\xi_{i}^{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{3}u\right\|\right)(\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}})\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|u\right\|

\displaystyle\leq c_{\delta}(\nu_{h}+\nu_{z})\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}+\delta\nu_{h}\left\|\xi_{i}^{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}u\right\|^{2}+\delta\nu_{z}\left\|\xi_{i}^{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\partial_{3}u\right\|^{2}

by Young’s Inequality with any small parameter
0<\delta
. In particular
\delta
can be chosen sufficiently small such that the latter two terms can be hidden in the viscous smoothing, and with
\delta
fixed the first term will vanish with
\nu_{h}
and
\nu_{z}
. Whilst this illustrates an important point, in the proof of our main result one must be much more precise in dealing with various cross-terms of the two directions.

An expected consequence of the lack of uniqueness for weak solutions of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations is that our solutions to (
9
) are only probabilistically weak, meaning that for every
\nu_{h}
and
\nu_{z}
we can find a probability space and Cylindrical Brownian Motion supporting a solution, however a priori the choice of that space may well depend on
\nu_{h}
and
\nu_{z}
themselves. To consider the expectation of the difference of the solutions we will need a single probability space supporting all solutions, which we construct by first fixing a sequence
(\nu_{h}^{k},\nu_{z}^{k})
and then taking the infinite product of the probability spaces supporting the corresponding solutions
u^{k}
. Our main result is therefore stated for sequences of viscosities. Note that we do not obtain or need a uniform Cylindrical Brownian Motion. We proceed by considering
\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u^{k}_{t}-w_{t}-\mathscr{B}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\right)
where
\mathscr{B}
is the boundary corrector from [
55
]. To work with the evolution equation for
u^{k}
we must look at the level of its Galerkin approximation and pass to the limit, given that the nonlinear term does not belong to
L^{2}_{t}H^{-1}_{x}
. Whilst the same is necessary in [
55
] it is only mentioned as a formality, however this does not appear trivial. Strong solutions of the Euler equation
w
are required as
H^{\gamma}
bounds are used for it with
\frac{5}{2}<\gamma
, but if one passes the Galerkin projections
\mathcal{P}_{n}
onto
w
and tries to conclude by using
H^{\gamma}
bounds on
\mathcal{P}_{n}w
then this will fail as
\left\|\mathcal{P}_{n}w\right\|_{H^{\gamma}}
explodes with
n
if
w\neq 0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
(see [
33
] Appendix D). We must remove the projections before estimating term by term, generating quantities of the form
\left\|(I-\mathcal{P}_{n})(w+\mathscr{B})\right\|_{H^{1}_{x}}
with some careful bounds, which do converge to zero as
w+\mathscr{B}
satisfies the Dirichlet boundary condition.

We close this section with a brief comment on the necessity of features of the noise scaling for our method. Indeed if we were to scale by
\nu^{\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, or to avoid decomposing the noise and suppose that the vertical component only decays with
\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, then our arguments would fail. This is a consequence of the
\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}},\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}
scaling for the first order transport term matching the
\nu_{h},\nu_{z}
scaling of the Laplacian.

Preliminaries

2.1
Functional Analytic Preliminaries

We recall that
\mathscr{O}=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times(0,1)
, and denote the usual Sobolev Spaces
W^{s,p}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
H^{\gamma}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{3})
by simply
W^{s,p}
,
H^{\gamma}
. We shall use
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
to represent the
L^{2}
inner product and similarly for the norm, whilst also employing subscripts
L^{p}_{h}
,
L^{p}_{z}
as shorthand for
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
and
L^{p}\left((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
respectively. This shorthand will also apply for general Euclidean target spaces, which shall be clear from the context. Let
C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

\mathscr{B}\in C\left([0,T];H^{\gamma}\cap L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)

for every

\mathscr{B}_{t}(x,y,z)=\mathscr{M}(z)\begin{pmatrix}w^{1}_{t}(x,y,0)+w^{1}_{t}(x,y,1)\\
w^{2}_{t}(x,y,0)+w^{2}_{t}(x,y,1)\\
\partial_{3}w^{3}_{t}(x,y,0)+\partial_{3}w^{3}_{t}(x,y,1)\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\mathscr{M}(z)\mathscr{A}_{t}(x,y)

where

and

is only supported on

and a lower right scalar

, satisfying the bounds

\displaystyle\left\|\mathscr{M}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}+\left\|z\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}(z)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)}}+\left\|(1-z)\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}(z)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\theta\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathscr{M}^{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{z}}+\left\|z^{2}\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)}}+\left\|(1-z)^{2}\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\theta\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\theta\nu\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathscr{M}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{z}}+\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}^{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{z}}

\displaystyle\leq c

for a constant

independent of
\theta
and
\nu_{z}
.

We remark that the boundary corrector from [
55

\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,\infty)
so only one horizontal plate is considered. On
\mathscr{O}
we first build the Masmoudi corrector near the bottom horizontal plate
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\{0\}
with support in
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,\frac{1}{4}]

\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\{1\}

Lemma 2.2
.

The function
\mathscr{A}
from Lemma
2.1
satisfies the bounds

\displaystyle\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{h}}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}+\left\|\partial_{t}\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\leq c\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{H^{\gamma}}.

for some constant
c
independent of
w
.

Proof.

Control on the first term follows simply from the fact that

\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{h}}\leq c\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq c\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{H^{\gamma}}

by the usual Sobolev Embedding
H^{\gamma}\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}
. For the second we observe that

\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\leq c\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{H^{2}_{\partial\mathscr{O}}}\leq c\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{H^{\gamma}}

by the trace inequality, as

. In the third term,

\left\|\partial_{t}\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\leq c\left\|\partial_{t}w_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}_{\partial\mathscr{O}}}\leq c\left\|\partial_{t}w_{s}\right\|_{H^{\gamma-1}}

using the trace inequality again as

. Now we can substitute in the explicit form
\partial_{t}w_{s}=B(w_{s},w_{s})
, and using that

∎

We conclude this subsection with a final inequality to be used in the main proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
1<p\leq\infty
, let
f\in W^{1,p}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
be such that
f=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
. Then

\left\|\frac{f(x,y,z)}{z}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\frac{f(x,y,z)}{1-z}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq c\left\|\partial_{3}f\right\|_{L^{p}}

for some constant
c
independent of
f
.

Proof.

Starting with the first term, then
f=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
implies that
f(x,y,0)=0
and as
f\in W^{1,p}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
we may write

f(x,y,z)=\int_{0}^{z}\partial_{3}f(x,y,\eta)d\eta.

Suppressing the dependence on
x,y
, note that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(z)}{z}\right|\leq\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}\left|\partial_{3}f(\eta)\right|d\eta\leq\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{2z}\left|\partial_{3}f(\eta)\right|d\eta\leq\sup_{0<r}\left(\frac{1}{r}\int_{z-r}^{z+r}\left|\partial_{3}f(\eta)\right|d\eta\right)

and the Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Inequality gives us that

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<r}\left(\frac{1}{r}\int_{z-r}^{z+r}\left|\partial_{3}f(\eta)\right|d\eta\right)\right\|_{L^{p}_{z}}\leq c\left\|\partial_{3}f\right\|_{L^{p}_{z}}.

Therefore

\left\|\frac{f(x,y,z)}{z}\right\|_{L^{p}}=\left\|\left\|\frac{f(x,y,z)}{z}\right\|_{L^{p}_{z}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{h}}\leq c\left\|\left\|\partial_{3}f\right\|_{L^{p}_{z}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{h}}=c\left\|\partial_{3}f\right\|_{L^{p}}

as required. The second term is treated symmetrically, now using that
f(x,y,1)=0
so

f(x,y,z)=-\int_{z}^{1}\partial_{3}f(x,y,\eta)d\eta

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(z)}{1-z}\right|\leq\frac{1}{1-z}\int_{z}^{1}\left|\partial_{3}f(\eta)\right|d\eta\leq\frac{1}{1-z}\int_{2z-1}^{1}\left|\partial_{3}f(\eta)\right|d\eta\leq\sup_{0<r}\left(\frac{1}{r}\int_{(1-z)-r}^{(1-z)+r}\left|\partial_{3}f(\eta)\right|d\eta\right)

leading to the same conclusion, as

\left\|\sup_{0<r}\left(\frac{1}{r}\int_{z-r}^{z+r}\left|\partial_{3}f(\eta)\right|d\eta\right)\right\|_{L^{p}_{z}}=\left\|\sup_{0<r}\left(\frac{1}{r}\int_{(1-z)-r}^{(1-z)+r}\left|\partial_{3}f(\eta)\right|d\eta\right)\right\|_{L^{p}_{z}}.

∎

\omega

2.3
Transport-Stretching Noise

We now address key properties of the anisotropically scaled transport-stretching noise operator, starting by recalling the definition of
\mathcal{G}
from (
5
) for vector fields
\phi
,
f
as

\mathcal{G}_{\phi}f=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left(\phi^{j}\partial_{j}f+f^{j}\nabla\phi^{j}\right).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\phi\in H^{1}
satisfy that
\phi\cdot\underline{n}=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
. Then for all
f,g\in H^{1}
, we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}f,g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{\phi}g\right\rangle-\left\langle f,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}\phi^{j}\right)g\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\phi}f,g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{g}\phi\right\rangle.

Therefore, we define the operators
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{*}
,
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}^{*}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\phi}^{*}
on
H^{1}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{*}f

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{\phi}f-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}\phi^{j}\right)f,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\phi}^{*}f

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{f}\phi,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\phi}^{*}f

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{*}f+\mathcal{T}_{\phi}^{*}f.

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}f,g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\left\langle\phi^{j}\partial_{j}f^{l},g^{l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\left(\left\langle\phi^{j}\partial_{j}f^{l},g^{l}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{j}\phi^{j}f^{l},g^{l}\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{j}\phi^{j}f^{l},g^{l}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\left(\left\langle\partial_{j}(\phi^{j}f^{l}),g^{l}\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{j}\phi^{j}f^{l},g^{l}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\left\langle\phi^{j}f^{l},\partial_{j}g^{l}\right\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\left\langle\phi^{j}f^{l},g^{l}\underline{n}^{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}-\sum_{l=1}^{3}\left\langle f^{l},\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}\phi^{j}\right)g^{l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{\phi}g\right\rangle-\left\langle f,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}\phi^{j}\right)g\right\rangle

where we have used that
\sum_{j=1}^{3}\phi^{j}\underline{n}^{j}=\phi\cdot\underline{n}=0
. For the second result, we simply observe that

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\phi}f,g\right\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\left\langle f^{j}\partial_{l}\phi^{j},g^{l}\right\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\left\langle f^{j},g^{l}\partial_{l}\phi^{j}\right\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left\langle f^{j},\mathcal{L}_{g}\phi^{j}\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{g}\phi\right\rangle.

∎

Let us now fix the spatial correlation functions
(\xi_{i})
, satisfying
\xi_{i}\cdot\underline{n}=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
and

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}}^{2}<\infty
. We further recall and extend several notations from Subsection
1.2

\displaystyle\xi_{i}^{h}=\left(\xi_{i}^{1},\xi_{i}^{2},0\right),\qquad\xi_{i}^{z}=\left(0,0,\xi_{i}^{3}\right),\qquad\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{h}=\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{h},\qquad\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{z}=\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{z}

with

\tilde{\xi}_{i}=\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{h}+\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{z}=\left(\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{1},\nu_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{2},\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}^{3}\right)

and then the corresponding operators

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}=\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\xi}_{i}},\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{h}_{i}=\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{h}},\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}=\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{z}}.

The property

is preserved for all variants of

considered, as it is equivalent to

on

. We thus obtain expressions for
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{*}

due to Lemma

and

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists a constant

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}f\right\|+\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h,*}f\right\|

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{h}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}f\right\|+\left\|f\right\|\right),

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}f\right\|+\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z,*}f\right\|

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{z}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left(\left\|\partial_{3}f\right\|+\left\|f\right\|\right).

We also prove the key estimate discussed in Subsection
1.3
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any parameter
0<\delta
, there exists a constant

such that, for all

,

\displaystyle\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{2}f,f\right\rangle+\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}f\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\delta}\left\|\tilde{\xi}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|^{2}+\delta\left\|\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}f\right\|^{2}+\delta\left\|\tilde{\xi}_{i}^{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\partial_{3}f\right\|^{2},

(14)

\displaystyle\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}f,f\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\tilde{\xi}_{i}\right\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|f\right\|^{4}.

(15)

\displaystyle\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{2}f,f\right\rangle+\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}f\right\|^{2}=\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}f,\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{*}f+\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}f\right\rangle

satisfying

\displaystyle u^{n}_{t}=\mathcal{P}_{n}u_{0}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}B(u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s})\ ds-\nu_{h}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}A_{h}u^{n}_{s}\,ds-\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}A_{z}u^{n}_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s}ds-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}u^{n}_{s}d\mathcal{W}^{n}_{s}.

Precisely,
(u^{n})\rightarrow u
in the weak* topology of
L^{2}\left(\Omega;L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)\right)
and the weak topology of
L^{2}\left(\Omega;L^{2}\left([0,T];W^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)\right)
.

The Galerkin Approximations will be used in the proof of Theorem
3.2
as one cannot look at the
L^{2}
norm of weak solutions directly. Whilst Proposition
3.3
informs us that for any given viscosity we can build a filtered probability space supporting a martingale weak solution, the choice of that space may very well depend on
\nu_{h},\nu_{z}
themselves. We now construct a space supporting all solutions in the anisotropic inviscid limit.

Let us fix any two sequences of positive constants
(\nu_{h}^{k})
,
(\nu_{z}^{k})
such that
(\nu_{h}^{k})
and
\left(\frac{\nu_{z}^{k}}{\nu_{h}^{k}}\right)
converge to zero, as in Theorem
3.2
. By Proposition
3.3
, for each
k
there exists a filtered probability space
\mathcal{S}^{k}\coloneqq\left(\tilde{\Omega}^{k},\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{k},(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{t}),\tilde{\mathbbm{P}}^{k}\right)
and a pair
(\tilde{u}^{k},\tilde{\mathcal{W}}^{k})
which is a martingale weak solution of (
10
) with respect to
\mathcal{S}^{k}
. We now define the standard infinite dimensional product space

\Omega:=\bigtimes_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\Omega}^{k},\quad\mathcal{F}:=\bigotimes_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{k},\quad\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\bigotimes_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{t},\quad\mathbbm{P}:=\bigtimes_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\mathbbm{P}}^{k}

such that
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbbm{P}\right)
is a filtered probability space. Furthermore we introduce the component projections
(\mathscr{P}^{k})
,
\mathscr{P}^{k}:\Omega\rightarrow\tilde{\Omega}^{k}
and subsequently define
(u^{k},\mathcal{W}^{k})
by

u^{k}\coloneqq\tilde{u}^{k}\mathscr{P}^{k},\qquad\mathcal{W}^{k}\coloneqq\tilde{\mathcal{W}}^{k}\mathscr{P}^{k}.

By construction, for each
k
,
(u^{k},\mathcal{W}^{k})
is a martingale weak solution of (
10
) with respect to
\mathcal{S}
.

3.3
Proof of the Main Result

This subsection is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
:

We choose the filtered probability space
\mathcal{S}
and martingale weak solutions
(u^{k},\mathcal{W}^{k})
as described in Subsection
3.2
. Let us first approach the convergence
(u^{k})\rightarrow w
in
L^{2}\left(\Omega;L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)\right)
. Without loss of generality we suppose throughout that
\nu_{h}^{k},\nu_{z}^{k}<1
. Note that

\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|u^{k}-w\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)}\right)=\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\tilde{u}^{k}\mathscr{P}^{k}-w\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)}\right)=\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}^{k}\left(\left\|\tilde{u}^{k}-w\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)}\right)

where
\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}^{k}
denotes the expectation over
\tilde{\Omega}^{k}
with respect to
\tilde{\mathbbm{P}}^{k}
. As alluded to, for the computations we will use the Galerkin Approximation specified in Proposition
3.3
. More precisely, there is an approximate sequence of solutions
(\tilde{u}^{k,n},\tilde{\mathcal{W}}^{k,n})
satisfying

\displaystyle\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{t}=\mathcal{P}_{n}u_{0}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}B(\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s},\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s})\ ds-\nu^{k}_{h}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}A_{h}\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s}\,ds-\nu^{k}_{z}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}A_{z}\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{2}\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s}ds-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s}d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}^{k,n}_{s}

whereby
(\tilde{u}^{k,n})\rightarrow\tilde{u}^{k}
in the weak* topology of
L^{2}\left(\tilde{\Omega}^{k};L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)\right)
and the weak topology of
L^{2}\left(\tilde{\Omega}^{k};L^{2}\left([0,T];W^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)\right)
. Therefore,

\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}^{k}\left(\left\|\tilde{u}^{k}-w\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)}\right)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}^{k}\left(\left\|\tilde{u}^{k,n}-w\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)}\right)

and we can see that the computation boils down to treating
\left\|\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{t}-w_{t}\right\|^{2}
. For simplicity in the below, we relabel
\tilde{u}^{k,n}
by
u
and remove all
k,n
superscripts. Let us define

v\coloneqq u-w-\mathscr{B}

where
\mathscr{B}
is the boundary corrector from Lemma
2.1
. Estimates on
v
will prove sufficient as
\mathscr{B}
is of vanishing
L^{2}
norm. Observe that
v
satisfies

\displaystyle v_{t}=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{P}_{n}u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}B(u_{s},u_{s})\ ds-\nu_{h}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}A_{h}u_{s}\,ds-\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}A_{z}u_{s}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{2}u_{s}ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}u_{s}d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}-u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}B(w_{s},w_{s})\ ds-\mathscr{B}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\mathscr{B}_{s}ds

which yields the energy identity

\displaystyle\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\,\left\|\mathcal{P}_{n}u_{0}-u_{0}-\mathscr{B}_{0}\right\|^{2}-2\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}B(u_{s},u_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle ds-2\nu_{h}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}A_{h}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle-2\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}A_{z}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle B(w_{s},w_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle ds-2\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{2}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s}\right\|^{2}ds-2\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}.

(17)

To keep track of all terms, let us write

\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}=\sum_{l=0}^{8}J_{l}

which is the sum of the initial condition plus the eight integrals.
A series of constants will appear in the proof; for convenience, we summarise them and their dependencies here.

•

\delta
is an arbitrary positive parameter to be chosen at the end of the proof, which will be the first parameter fixed.

•

\check{c}
denotes a general non-negative constant changing from line to line, which may depend on
\delta
but is independent of
\tilde{c}
,
k
and
n
below.

•

\tilde{c}
is an arbitrary positive parameter to be chosen at the end of the proof, after
\delta
. The boundary corrector
\mathscr{B}
is constructed for the choice
\theta=\tilde{c}\nu_{h}
.

•

c
denotes a general non-negative constant changing from line to line, which may depend on
\delta
and
\tilde{c}
but is independent of
k
and
n
.

•

o_{k}
denotes a non-negative constant, which may depend on
\delta
and
\tilde{c}
but is independent of
n
, such that
o_{k}
approaches zero as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
k
will be chosen large at the end of the proof, after
\delta
and
\tilde{c}
.

•

c_{k}
denotes a general non-negative constant changing from line to line, which may depend on
\delta
,
\tilde{c}
and
k
but is independent of
n
. In particular,
c_{k}
may explode as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

•

o_{n}
denotes a non-negative constant, which may depend on
\delta
,
\tilde{c}
and
k
, such that
o_{n}
approaches zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
n
will be chosen large at the end of the proof, after
\delta
,
\tilde{c}
and
n
.

As described, the order of selection is
\delta
,
\tilde{c}
,
k
,
n
. Any of the above constants may depend on
T
, the
H^{\gamma}
norm of
u_{0}
, the
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];H^{\gamma}\right)
norm of
w
or the
W^{2,\infty}
norms of
(\xi_{i})
, which will be clear in context. Throughout the proof we shall make frequent use of the bounds given in Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
without explicit reference, where in Lemma
2.1
the choice is
\theta=\tilde{c}\nu_{h}
. Notice that all quantities bounded in Lemma
2.2
are controlled by the generic
c
in this proof. Starting with
J_{0}
,

\displaystyle J_{0}\leq 2\left\|\mathcal{P}_{n}u_{0}-u_{0}\right\|^{2}+2\left\|\mathscr{B}_{0}\right\|^{2}\leq o_{n}+2\left\|\mathscr{M}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}^{2}\left\|\mathscr{A}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}^{2}\leq o_{n}+c\left(\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=o_{n}+o_{k}.

Moving on to
J_{1}
, we first deal with the projection
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}B(u_{s},u_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)B(u_{s},u_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle B(u_{s},u_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle B(u_{s},u_{s}),\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(-w_{s}-\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\rangle+\left\langle B(u_{s},u_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle

having expanded out
v_{s}=u_{s}-w_{s}-\mathscr{B}_{s}
and used that
(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I)u_{s}=0
. In the first term use that

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle B(u_{s},u_{s}),\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(-w_{s}-\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|B(u_{s},u_{s})\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{L^{3}}\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}

having applied the Gagliardo-Nirenberg Inequality
\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{L^{3}}\leq c\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{H^{1}}
. We will return to this term later, and instead move on to
\left\langle B(u_{s},u_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle
. The approach is to split
u
into its representation
v-w-\mathscr{B}
, yielding

\displaystyle\left\langle B(u_{s},u_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle B(u_{s},v_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle-\left\langle B(u_{s},w_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle-\left\langle B(u_{s},\mathscr{B}_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle B(v_{s},w_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle B(w_{s},w_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle B(\mathscr{B}_{s},w_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\left\langle B(v_{s},\mathscr{B}_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle B(w_{s},\mathscr{B}_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle B(\mathscr{B}_{s},\mathscr{B}_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle

where we have used that
\left\langle B(u_{s},v_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle=0
. As these terms come with a minus, note that
\left\langle B(w_{s},w_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle
cancels with
J_{4}
. Therefore, we have established that

\displaystyle J_{1}+J_{4}\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}ds

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\langle B(v_{s},w_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle B(\mathscr{B}_{s},w_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle-\left\langle B(v_{s},\mathscr{B}_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle B(w_{s},\mathscr{B}_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle B(\mathscr{B}_{s},\mathscr{B}_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle\right)ds

and we write the right hand side as
\sum_{l=0}^{5}J_{1,4}^{l}
, to be addressed individually. To begin,

J^{1}_{1,4}\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds

and

\displaystyle J^{2}_{1,4}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|ds\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathscr{M}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|ds

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\left(\tilde{c}\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|ds\leq c\left(\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds=o_{k}+c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds

having used Young’s Inequality. Moving on to
J^{3}_{1,4}
, we decompose

\displaystyle\left\langle B(v_{s},\mathscr{B}_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\langle v^{j}_{s}\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle v^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\langle v^{j}_{s}\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s},v^{l}_{s}\right\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\langle v^{j}_{s}\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}^{3}_{s},v^{3}_{s}\right\rangle+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left\langle v^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s},v^{l}_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle v^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{3}_{s},v^{3}_{s}\right\rangle

In the first term, we have that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{l=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\langle v^{j}_{s}\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s},v^{l}_{s}\right\rangle\right|\leq\sum_{l=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{l=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\mathscr{M}^{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{z}}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{A}^{l}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{h}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}\leq c\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}.

whilst in the second term,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\langle v^{j}_{s}\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}^{3}_{s},v^{3}_{s}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\langle\mathscr{B}^{3}_{s},\partial_{j}\left(v^{j}_{s}v^{3}_{s}\right)\right\rangle\right|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\left|\left\langle\mathscr{B}^{3}_{s},\partial_{j}v^{j}_{s}v^{3}_{s}\right\rangle\right|+\left|\left\langle\mathscr{B}^{3}_{s},v^{j}_{s}\partial_{j}v^{3}_{s}\right\rangle\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\mathscr{B}^{3}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|\left\|v_{s}\right\|\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\mathscr{M}^{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{z}}\left\|\mathscr{A}^{3}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{h}}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|\left\|v_{s}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\tilde{c}\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|\left\|v_{s}\right\|\leq\delta\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}+c\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\nu_{h}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}+c\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}

observing in the last line that
\nu_{z}\leq 1
. The third term is the most delicate, for which we will look to invoke Lemma
2.3
. We split the integral into the regions where
\mathscr{B}
is supported,

\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left\langle v^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s},v^{l}_{s}\right\rangle=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left(\left\langle v^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s},v^{l}_{s}\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{h\times\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)}}+\left\langle v^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s},v^{l}_{s}\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{h\times\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)}}\right)

and observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left(\left|\left\langle v^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s},v^{l}_{s}\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{h\times\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)}}\right|+\left|\left\langle v^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s},v^{l}_{s}\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{h\times\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left(\left\|z^{2}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{h\times\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)}}\left\|\frac{v^{3}_{s}}{z}\right\|\left\|\frac{v_{s}}{z}\right\|+\left\|(1-z)^{2}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{l}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{h\times\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)}}\left\|\frac{v^{3}_{s}}{1-z}\right\|\left\|\frac{v_{s}}{1-z}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\check{c}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{A}^{l}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{h}}\left\|\partial_{3}v^{3}_{s}\right\|\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|\left(\left\|z^{2}\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}^{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)}}+\left\|(1-z)^{2}\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}^{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\check{c}\left(\tilde{c}\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\partial_{3}v^{3}_{s}\right\|\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\check{c}\left(\tilde{c}\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\delta\nu_{h}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}+\check{c}\tilde{c}\nu_{z}\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|^{2}

having applied Lemma
2.3
as suggested, and crucially using in the penultimate line that
\partial_{3}v^{3}=-\partial_{1}v^{1}-\partial_{2}v^{2}
by the divergence free condition, allowing us to pass to a bound by horizontal derivatives. The remaining term of
J^{3}_{1,4}
is simple,

\left|\left\langle v^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{3}_{s},v^{3}_{s}\right\rangle\right|\leq c\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{3}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}\leq c\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}^{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{z}}\left\|\mathscr{A}^{3}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{h}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}\leq c\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}

producing in total that

J^{3}_{1,4}\leq 4\delta\nu_{h}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+2\check{c}\tilde{c}\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds.

Let us move on to
J^{4}_{1,4}
. We write

\left\langle B(w_{s},\mathscr{B}_{s}),v_{s}\right\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\langle w^{j}_{s}\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle w^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle

and see that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left|\left\langle w^{j}_{s}\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|\left\|v_{s}\right\|\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\mathscr{M}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\tilde{c}\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|\leq o_{k}+c\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}

as well as, using Lemma
2.3
and splitting onto the support of
\mathscr{B}
again,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle w^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\langle w^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{h\times\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)}}\right|+\left|\left\langle w^{3}_{s}\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{h\times\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{w^{3}_{s}}{z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|z\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h\times\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|+\left\|\frac{w^{3}_{s}}{1-z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|(1-z)\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h\times\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\partial_{3}w^{3}_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|\left(\left\|z\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)}}+\left\|(1-z)\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\tilde{c}\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq o_{k}+c\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}.

Note that whilst
w
does not satisfy the Dirichlet boundary condition, it satisfies
w\cdot\underline{n}=0
which ensures that
w^{3}=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
, hence the validity of applying Lemma
2.3
. Therefore,

J^{4}_{1,4}\leq o_{k}+c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds.

Towards

\displaystyle\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}w_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}w_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}w_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}w_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle+\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}w_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle.

Firstly,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}w_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\xi}^{h}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}w_{s}\right\|+\left\|w_{s}\right\|\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|+\left\|\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\nu_{h}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|+\left\|v_{s}\right\|\right)\left\|\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}+\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle=o_{k}.

Similarly easy is the third term, for which

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}w_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle\right|=o_{k}.

In the second term,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}w_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\xi}^{h}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|\tilde{\xi}^{z}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}w_{s}\right\|+\left\|w_{s}\right\|\right)\left(\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|+\left\|\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq c(\nu_{h}\nu_{z})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\left(\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}+\left\|\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c(\nu_{h}\nu_{z})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=o_{k}

whilst in the fourth,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}w_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\xi}^{z}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left(\left\|\partial_{3}w_{s}\right\|+\left\|w_{s}\right\|\right)\left(\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|+\left\|\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\nu_{z}\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\left(\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}+\left\|\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\nu_{z}\left(\nu_{h}\nu_{z}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=c\nu_{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{\nu_{z}}{\nu_{h}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=o_{k}.

Simply,

J^{6}_{6,7}\leq o_{k}.

J^{7}_{6,7}
is almost immediate,

J^{7}_{6,7}\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\xi_{i}}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}ds\leq c(\nu_{h}+\nu_{z})=o_{k}.

The final term,
J^{8}_{6,7}
, is decomposed as usual into

\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|^{2}=\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|^{2}+2\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}\mathscr{B}_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle+\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|^{2}.

We have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\xi}^{h}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|+\left\|\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\nu_{h}\left\|\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}+\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=o_{k}

whilst

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}\mathscr{B}_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\xi}^{h}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|\tilde{\xi}^{z}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|+\left\|\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|\right)\left(\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|+\left\|\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq c(\nu_{h}\nu_{z})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\left(\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}+\left\|\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c(\nu_{h}\nu_{z})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\nu_{h}\nu_{z})^{\frac{1}{4}}(\nu_{h}\nu_{z})^{-\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=o_{k}

and lastly,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\xi}^{z}_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left(\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|+\left\|\mathscr{B}_{s}\right\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\nu_{z}\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}^{2}\left(\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}+\left\|\mathscr{M}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\nu_{z}(\nu_{h}\nu_{z})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=c\left(\frac{\nu_{z}}{\nu_{h}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=o_{k}.

Therefore,

J^{8}_{6,7}\leq o_{k}

so summing over all terms,

\displaystyle J_{6}+J_{7}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)\left(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}ds+\sum_{l=1}^{8}J^{l}_{6,7}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)\left(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad+14\delta\nu_{h}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+14\delta\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+o_{k}.

Only
J_{8}
remains to be treated, however this will require first taking the supremum and expectation and then applying the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality. Before doing this, we collect
J_{0}
to
J_{7}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{7}J_{l}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}ds

\displaystyle-2\nu_{h}(1-11\delta)\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds-2\nu_{z}(1-9\delta-\check{c}\tilde{c})\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+o_{k}+o_{n}.

Substituting this back into (
17
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\nu_{h}(1-11\delta)\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+2\nu_{z}(1-9\delta-\check{c}\tilde{c})\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad+c\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds-2\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}+o_{k}+o_{n}.

We now choose the parameter
\delta
followed by
\tilde{c}
, to be

\delta\coloneqq\frac{1}{22},\qquad\tilde{c}=\frac{1}{11\check{c}}

giving that
1-11\delta=1-9\delta-\check{c}\tilde{c}=\frac{1}{2}
. Parameters now fixed, taking the supremum over
t\in[0,t^{*}]
for any
t^{*}\in(0,T]
followed by expectation, we arrive at

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t^{*}]}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}+\nu_{h}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+o_{k}+o_{n}

(18)

having immediately applied the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality. Let us now address the first term on the right hand side. By applying Hölder’s Inequality over the product space, we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right)ds\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\left(\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{2}\right)\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right)ds\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\left(\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t^{*}]}\left(1+\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{2}\right)\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

As is now quite standard, see for example [
36
] Proposition 2.1 for this choice of noise in the isotropic case, we have the bounds

\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}dr\right)\leq c_{k}

which we recall is independent of
n
, but may explode as
k\rightarrow\infty
. By construction of the boundary corrector
\mathscr{B}
we have that
w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s}\in W^{1,2}_{\sigma}
, and as
(\mathcal{P}_{n})
are orthogonal projections in this space then for each fixed
k
,
\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}
is monotone decreasing to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
. As the integrals are well defined for each
n
, by the Monotone Convergence Theorem we conclude that

\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s})\right\|_{H^{1}}^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq o_{n}

so we update (
18
) to

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t^{*}]}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}+\nu_{h}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+o_{k}+c_{k}o_{n}.

(19)

Note that whilst
o_{n}
is allowed to depend on
k
, so
c_{k}o_{n}=o_{n}
, we write
c_{k}o_{n}
simply to stress this dependence on
k
. To treat the remaining noise term, we again observe that

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\langle\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}+2\left\langle\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}}^{2}\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{2}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)\left(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+2\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}

so

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{2}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)\left(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+2\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(20)

and indeed

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{2}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)\left(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left[\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t^{*}]}\left\|u_{s}\right\|^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}-I\right)\left(w_{s}+\mathscr{B}_{s}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c_{k}o_{n}

as seen just prior. For the second term in (
20
), we write

\displaystyle\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}u_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\leq 2\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}v_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}+2\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}w_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}+2\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}

and denote the corresponding terms as
\sum_{l=1}^{3}L_{l}
. In
L_{1}
we apply (
15
) to see that

\displaystyle L_{1}\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t^{*}]}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t^{*}]}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}\right)+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)

where the first term can be absorbed into the first term on the left hand side of (
19
). Next,

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\tilde{\xi_{i}}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left((\nu_{h}+\nu_{z})\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\nu_{h}+\nu_{z})+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)\leq o_{k}+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right).

For
L_{3}
we split the term up into

Using the bounds on

and

that we have seen for example in

,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{h}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\leq c\nu_{h}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}

whilst

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{z}\mathscr{B}_{s},v_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\leq c\nu_{z}(\nu_{h}\nu_{z})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}=c\left(\frac{\nu_{z}}{\nu_{h}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}.

Proceeding identically to
L_{2}
with these bounds in place, then

L_{3}\leq o_{k}+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right).

Substituting everything back into (
19
),
we achieve that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t^{*}]}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}+\nu_{h}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|\partial_{j}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+\nu_{z}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|\partial_{3}v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\left\|v_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)+o_{k}+c_{k}o_{n}.

), by the standard Grönwall Inequality we see that

\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}^{k}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{t}-w_{t}-\mathscr{B}^{k}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right)\leq\left(o_{k}+c_{k}o_{n}\right)e^{c}=o_{k}+c_{k}o_{n}

which can be inserted back into (

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}^{k}\left(\nu_{h}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{j}\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s}-\partial_{j}w_{s}-\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}^{k}_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+\nu_{z}^{k}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{3}\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s}-\partial_{3}w_{s}-\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{k}_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)\leq o_{k}+c_{k}o_{n}.

(23)

Towards a convergence to

, note that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\mathscr{B}^{k}_{t}\right\|\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\mathscr{A}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}_{h}}\left\|\mathscr{M}^{k}\right\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\leq c\left(\nu_{h}^{k}\nu_{z}^{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}=o_{k}

which combines with (

) to provide

\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}^{k}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{t}-w_{t}\right\|^{2}\right)\leq o_{k}+c_{k}o_{n}.

(24)

and must show that there exists a

K\leq k
,

\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|u^{k}-w\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)}^{2}\right)<\varepsilon.

(25)

We fix
K
large enough so that

) is less than
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Then for any fixed

, as described in the beginning of the proof,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|u^{k}-w\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+c_{k}o_{n}\right)

\displaystyle<\varepsilon

which concludes the proof of the first assertion. In view of the second statement, keeping (
23
) in mind, it is clear that

\nu_{h}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left\|\partial_{j}w_{s}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\partial_{j}\mathscr{B}^{k}_{s}\right\|^{2}\right)ds+\nu_{z}^{k}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{3}w_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\leq o_{k}

whilst we are more detailed in the last term,

\nu_{z}^{k}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{B}^{k}_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\leq\nu_{z}^{k}\left\|\partial_{3}\mathscr{M}^{k}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{z}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathscr{A}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{h}}ds\leq c\nu_{z}^{k}\left(\nu_{h}^{k}\nu_{z}^{k}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=c\left(\frac{\nu_{z}^{k}}{\nu_{h}^{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=o_{k}.

Therefore by (
23
),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}^{k}\left(\nu_{h}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{j}\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s}\right\|^{2}ds+\nu_{z}^{k}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{3}\tilde{u}^{k,n}_{s}\right\|^{2}ds\right)\leq o_{k}+c_{k}o_{n}

and the proof concludes through the same final steps as the first assertion above.
