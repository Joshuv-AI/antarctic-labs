Quantum Coordination without Conditioning under Restricted Information
Faisal Shah Khan

Abstract
We study coordination under restricted information, where classical local models fail to implement certain correlated distributions because agents cannot condition on past history. We show that quantum systems overcome this limitation even when using only separable states. Both classically diagonal encodings (shared latent variables) and separable states with noncommuting local structure (quantum discord) enable the implementation of joint distributions that are unattainable by any classical local rules under the same information constraints. The quantum advantage arises from enabling latent-variable coordination without requiring agents to condition on the latent variable itself — a construction that succeeds where no classical local model can. Separable states with nonzero quantum discord provide an alternative mechanism for realizing such coordination. At the same time, quantum models remain strictly limited by the information structure: unlike perfect recall, they cannot reproduce fully adaptive dependence on realized past outcomes that are observationally indistinguishable. Thus, quantum correlations serve as a partial substitute for perfect recall.



I
Introduction

Sequential decision problems often require agents to generate correlated outcomes while operating under limited information about past events. A central question in such settings is which joint distributions can be implemented through local operations when agents cannot fully observe or recall the history.

In the classical framework, Kuhn’s theorem
[
10
]
(see also
[
13
]
) establishes that, under
perfect recall
, any mixed strategy can be represented as a behavioral strategy: agents can reproduce any desired correlation by conditioning their actions on the full history. When recall is imperfect, however, this equivalence breaks down. Some joint distributions that are achievable through global randomization cannot be implemented by any collection of local conditional rules. Although such distributions may admit a latent-variable representation in the sense of Harsanyi
[
6
,
7
]
, the key limitation is operational: if the latent variable is not accessible through the agents’ information, it cannot be used for conditioning. As a result, classical correlations may exist abstractly but fail to be implementable under restricted information. Related constraints appear in distributed quantum computing, where limited communication across subsystems restricts achievable coordination
[
5
]
.

We study how quantum systems modify this picture. We introduce a model in which agents generate outcomes via local measurements on a shared quantum state, without access to any additional information or conditioning variables. Within this framework, even separable states suffice to expand the set of implementable joint distributions. This includes both states that are diagonal in a fixed basis, corresponding to classical shared randomness, and states exhibiting nonzero quantum discord. In both cases, the key feature is that correlations are encoded directly in the shared state and revealed through local measurements, rather than constructed through conditioning on observed variables.

This mechanism differs fundamentally from the classical one. In classical models, implementing latent-variable correlations requires agents to condition on the latent variable itself, which may be impossible under restricted information. In contrast, the quantum model allows such coordination to emerge operationally without granting agents access to the underlying variable. Moreover, separable states with nonzero quantum discord
[
12
,
8
]
provide an alternative construction that achieves the same goal using genuinely noncommuting local structure. Prior work has demonstrated that discord can provide operational advantages even without entanglement
[
3
,
11
]
, and related ideas have been explored in quantum game theory
[
4
,
1
]
as well as in team decision problems with imperfect recall
[
2
,
14
]
.

At the same time, the quantum advantage is limited. Quantum correlations cannot reproduce fully adaptive dependence on realized past outcomes when those outcomes are indistinguishable within the information structure. Thus, while quantum systems expand the set of implementable distributions, they do not eliminate the constraints imposed by imperfect recall. Conceptually, they provide a partial substitute for memory, enabling forms of coordination that would otherwise require conditioning on unavailable information.

Our contribution is to formalize this distinction within a general probabilistic framework, to identify a general diagonal construction and a distinct discord-based mechanism for achieving quantum advantage, and to delineate the boundary between what remains impossible and what becomes feasible under restricted information. In doing so, we extend earlier quantum analogues of Kuhn’s theorem
[
9
]
and clarify the role of quantum correlations in constrained coordination problems through explicit constructions and formal results.

II
Classical Correlation Models under Information Constraints

We formalize the classical setting using a probabilistic description of correlation generation under restricted information. Consider a sequential process in which outcomes
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are generated at each stage
k
based only on the available information
Y_{k}=f_{k}(X_{<k})
, where
X_{<k}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{k-1})
. When
Y_{k}=X_{<k}
, agents have perfect recall of the full history; otherwise, information is restricted.

A local classical model consists of conditional distributions
P_{k}(x_{k}\mid y_{k})
for each stage
k
and each possible information state
y_{k}
. The resulting joint distribution factorizes as

P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{n}P_{k}(x_{k}\mid y_{k}).

(1)

Thus, all correlations are generated exclusively through conditioning on the available information variables
Y_{k}
.

Global randomization can be modeled by a latent variable
S
with distribution
p(s)
, where each realization
s
determines a complete sequence
(x_{1}(s),\dots,x_{n}(s))
. The induced distribution is

P^{\star}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{s:\,(x_{1}(s),\dots,x_{n}(s))=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})}p(s).

(2)

Importantly,
S
is inaccessible at every stage and therefore cannot be used for local conditioning.

II-A

Kuhn’s Theorem and Conditioning on History

Kuhn’s theorem
[
10
]
(see also
[
13
]
) connects this probabilistic formulation to extensive-form games. Under perfect recall (
Y_{k}=X_{<k}
), any mixed strategy can be implemented by a behavioral strategy: at each information set
I
(corresponding to a value of
Y_{k}
), the behavioral strategy
\sigma(a|I)
is obtained by conditioning the distribution over complete histories on those that reach
I
:

\sigma(a|I)=\frac{\sum_{h\in H(I,a)}p(h)}{\sum_{h\in H(I)}p(h)},

(3)

where
H(I)
is the set of histories reaching
I
and
H(I,a)
is the subset in which action
a
is chosen.

Equivalently, in probabilistic terms, any joint distribution admits the factorization

P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{n}P(x_{k}\mid x_{<k}).

(4)

When information is restricted, however, the variables
Y_{k}
do not fully specify the past. In this case, there exist distributions
P^{\star}
generated by global randomization (Eq. (
2
)) that cannot be reproduced by any collection of local conditional rules of the form (
1
). This failure of local implementability is the classical limitation we revisit in the quantum setting.

X_{1},X_{2}\in\{0,1\}

Y_{2}

X_{1}

, where the dashed line indicates that the two second-stage nodes are indistinguishable.

Tree representation of the binary illustrative example. The dashed line indicates that the two second-step nodes are indistinguishable under the available information, so the local rule generating

Let the target distribution be

P^{\star}(0,1)=P^{\star}(1,0)=\frac{1}{2},\qquad P^{\star}(0,0)=P^{\star}(1,1)=0.

This distribution arises from global randomization with a uniform latent variable
S\in\{0,1\}
:

(X_{1},X_{2})=\begin{cases}(0,1)&\text{if }S=0,\\
(1,0)&\text{if }S=1.\end{cases}

Under the given information constraint, any classical local model must factorize as
P(x_{1},x_{2})=P_{1}(x_{1})P_{2}(x_{2})
. Setting
P_{1}(0)=a
and
P_{2}(0)=b
yields

P(0,1)=a(1-b),\qquad P(1,0)=(1-a)b.

Imposing
P(0,1)=P(1,0)=\frac{1}{2}
forces
a,b,1-a,1-b>0
, which in turn implies
P(0,0)=ab>0
and
P(1,1)=(1-a)(1-b)>0
, contradicting the target
P^{\star}
. Therefore,
P^{\star}
cannot be implemented by any classical local rules under restricted information.

This example highlights the core classical limitation: even when a distribution admits a latent-variable representation, it may not be realizable by local conditional rules if the latent variable is inaccessible through the information structure.

III
Quantum Extension and Nonclassical Correlations

We now extend the classical framework of Section
II
to the quantum setting. The sequential structure and information constraints remain unchanged: at each stage
k
, the outcome
X_{k}
is generated based only on the available information
Y_{k}
.

The key difference is that correlations are no longer generated through local conditioning on past outcomes. Instead, they are encoded directly in a shared multipartite quantum state
\rho
. At stage
k
, the outcome
X_{k}
is obtained by performing a measurement on the
k
-th subsystem, where the choice of measurement (specified by a collection of operators
\{M^{(k)}_{x_{k}}(y_{k})\}_{x_{k}}
) depends only on the local information
y_{k}
. The resulting joint distribution is

P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\operatorname{Tr}\!\Bigl[\bigl(M^{(1)}_{x_{1}}(y_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{(n)}_{x_{n}}(y_{n})\bigr)\rho\Bigr].

(5)

This defines a quantum analogue of classical local conditional models (cf. Eq. (
1
)), closely related to the behavioral quantum strategies introduced in
[
9
]
but formulated here in a general probabilistic framework. Correlations between outcomes are embedded in a shared state and revealed operationally through local measurements, without requiring agents to condition on past history.

Importantly, this model is designed to preserve the informational constraints of the classical setting: at each stage
k
, the choice of measurement depends only on the available information
Y_{k}
, and no signaling or adaptive quantum memory is introduced across stages. In this sense, Eq. (
5
) provides a natural quantum extension of the classical local model, differing only in the mechanism by which correlations are generated.

The central question is whether this quantum resource enlarges the set of implementable distributions under the same information constraints—in particular, whether distributions that cannot be written as
\prod_{k}P_{k}(x_{k}\mid y_{k})
can nevertheless be realized by local quantum measurements.

III-A

Quantum realization via discord under restricted information

We return to the binary example of Section
II-B
, where the target distribution
P^{\star}
(with
P^{\star}(0,1)=P^{\star}(1,0)=1/2
and
P^{\star}(0,0)=P^{\star}(1,1)=0
) cannot be implemented classically when
Y_{2}
is constant.

Let the two stages share the two-qubit separable state

\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(|0\rangle\langle 0|_{A}\otimes|+\rangle\langle+|_{B}+|1\rangle\langle 1|_{A}\otimes|-\rangle\langle-|_{B}\Bigr),

(6)

where
|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
and
|-\rangle=(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
. Although separable, this state possesses nonzero quantum discord.

Stage 1 measures subsystem
A
in the computational basis
\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}
to obtain
X_{1}
. Stage 2 measures subsystem
B
in the
\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}
basis and maps
|+\rangle\mapsto 1
,
|-\rangle\mapsto 0
to obtain
X_{2}
. The second-stage measurement depends only on the constant
Y_{2}
, so the information constraint is respected.

A direct calculation yields exactly the target distribution
P^{\star}
. Thus
P^{\star}
, which is classically unattainable under the given information constraint, is implemented by local quantum measurements on a shared state.

The required correlation is encoded directly in
\rho_{AB}
and revealed through local measurements, without granting either party additional information about past outcomes. The nonzero quantum discord ensures that these correlations cannot be reduced to classical conditioning on accessible information. However—unlike perfect recall—this mechanism does not allow decisions to depend on realized past outcomes and therefore cannot reproduce behavior requiring adaptive dependence on history.
A simpler construction achieving the same target distribution using only classically correlated states (no quantum discord required) is presented in Theorem
1
.

IV
Quantum Extensions of Local Implementability under Information Constraints

This section clarifies both what quantum correlations
can
achieve under restricted information and what they fundamentally
cannot
. The example in Section
III-A
shows that quantum resources enable joint distributions unattainable by classical local rules. We now formalize this observation and delineate the scope and limitations of the effect.

In the classical setting (Section
II
), correlations arise solely through conditioning on the available information variables
Y_{k}
. Under perfect recall this allows any joint distribution to be implemented locally; under imperfect recall, many distributions generated by global randomization (including those with latent-variable representations) cannot be reproduced by rules of the form
\prod_{k}P_{k}(x_{k}\mid y_{k})
. The quantum model of Section
III
provides an alternative: correlations are encoded directly in a shared state
\rho
and revealed through local measurements (Eq. (
5
)), without requiring conditioning on past outcomes.

IV-A

Quantum Implementation of Hidden Coordination

We first identify a broad class of distributions that admit a classical latent-variable representation yet cannot be implemented by classical local rules under restricted information. The obstruction is not the absence of a latent variable, but the inability to condition on it when it lies outside the information structure
Y_{k}
.

This construction isolates a first mechanism by which quantum systems enable coordination under restricted information: separable states that are diagonal in a fixed basis encode classical latent variables in a distributed form. We later show that a distinct, genuinely nonclassical mechanism based on noncommuting local structure (quantum discord) can achieve similar coordination without relying on a jointly diagonal encoding.

Theorem 1
.

Consider a joint distribution of the form

P^{\star}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{s}p(s)\prod_{k=1}^{n}P_{k}(x_{k}\mid s,y_{k}),

where
s
is a latent variable that determines the correlations but is inaccessible through the information variables
Y_{k}
. Then
P^{\star}
can be implemented by local quantum measurements on a shared separable state, without making
s
accessible through
Y_{k}
.

Proof.

Let
\{\left|s\right\rangle\}
be an orthonormal basis indexed by
s
. Define the separable state

\rho=\sum_{s}p(s)\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\left|s\right\rangle\left\langle s\right|^{(k)}.

Each subsystem carries the classical label
s
in a distributed, locally inaccessible form. For each
k
, define a POVM
\{M^{(k)}_{x_{k}}(y_{k})\}_{x_{k}}
that is diagonal in the
\{\left|s\right\rangle\}
basis and satisfies

\left\langle s\right|M^{(k)}_{x_{k}}(y_{k})\left|s\right\rangle=P_{k}(x_{k}\mid s,y_{k}).

(For concreteness, one may take
M^{(k)}_{x_{k}}(y_{k})=\sum_{s}P_{k}(x_{k}\mid s,y_{k})\left|s\right\rangle\left\langle s\right|
.) These operators are positive and sum to the identity, hence form a valid POVM.

The resulting joint distribution is

P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\operatorname{Tr}\!\Bigl[\bigl(M^{(1)}_{x_{1}}(y_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{(n)}_{x_{n}}(y_{n})\bigr)\rho\Bigr]=\sum_{s}p(s)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle s\right|M^{(k)}_{x_{k}}(y_{k})\left|s\right\rangle.

By construction this equals
P^{\star}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Thus
P^{\star}
is implemented by local quantum measurements on a shared separable state without granting access to
s
.
∎

As a concrete illustration using the same two-stage example from Section
II-B
, let
S\in\{0,1\}
be uniform and define the target distribution via
X_{1}=S
and
X_{2}=1-S
. Applying Theorem
1
with the classically correlated separable state

\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}|0\rangle\langle 0|_{A}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|_{B}+\frac{1}{2}|1\rangle\langle 1|_{A}\otimes|1\rangle\langle 1|_{B}

together with the following local measurements (respecting the information constraint that
Y_{2}
is constant) implements
P^{\star}
:

•

Stage 1 (Alice): measure subsystem
A
in the computational basis to obtain
X_{1}
.

•

Stage 2 (Bob): measure subsystem
B
in the computational basis and output the flipped result (
X_{2}=1-
outcome).

This construction uses only classically correlated (diagonal) states and requires
no quantum discord
. Nevertheless, it exactly reproduces the target anti-correlated distribution even though Bob has no access to Alice’s outcome. In contrast to the discordant construction in Section
III-A
, this shows that the quantum advantage under restricted information does not require noncommuting local states — the global Born-rule readout on a consistently encoded classical label is already sufficient.

This establishes that quantum systems can realize latent-variable coordination without requiring agents to condition on the latent variable — a feat impossible for classical local models under the same constraints.

IV-B

Discordant Implementation of Hidden Coordination

Theorem
1
realizes hidden coordination via separable states that are diagonal in a fixed basis (classical shared randomness encoded quantum-mechanically). We now show that the same coordination can be achieved through a genuinely nonclassical mechanism using separable states with nonzero quantum discord.

Theorem 2
.

Consider a two-stage process with outcomes
X_{1},X_{2}
and constant
Y_{2}
. Let

P^{\star}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{s}p(s)\,\delta_{x_{1},g(s)}\,\delta_{x_{2},h(s)},

where
s
is a latent variable,
g
determines the first-stage outcome, and
h
determines the second-stage outcome. Suppose there exist an orthonormal family
\{\left|s\right\rangle\}
on subsystem
A
and a family of pure states
\{\left|\phi_{s}\right\rangle\}
on subsystem
B
satisfying:

1.

the map
s\mapsto g(s)
is realized by a projective measurement on
A
in the basis
\{\left|s\right\rangle\}
;

2.

the map
s\mapsto h(s)
is realized by a fixed measurement on
B
whose outcome probabilities distinguish the states
\{\left|\phi_{s}\right\rangle\}
;

3.

the family
\{\left|\phi_{s}\right\rangle\}
is not jointly diagonal in the basis associated with the classical information structure at stage 2.

Then the separable state

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{s}p(s)\,\left|s\right\rangle\left\langle s\right|_{A}\otimes\left|\phi_{s}\right\rangle\left\langle\phi_{s}\right|_{B}

together with the corresponding local measurements implements
P^{\star}
under the given information structure. Moreover, whenever
\{\left|\phi_{s}\right\rangle\}
fails to commute with the local measurement structure on
B
, the state
\rho_{AB}
has nonzero quantum discord.

Proof.

Define
\rho_{AB}
as above. It is separable (a convex combination of product states). Stage 1 measures
A
in the
\{\left|s\right\rangle\}
basis and sets
X_{1}=g(s)
. Stage 2 applies a fixed measurement
\{M_{x_{2}}\}
on
B
satisfying
\langle\phi_{s}|M_{x_{2}}|\phi_{s}\rangle=\delta_{x_{2},h(s)}
and sets
X_{2}=h(s)
. The second-stage measurement depends only on the constant
Y_{2}
, respecting the information constraint. The resulting distribution is exactly
P^{\star}(x_{1},x_{2})
.

If the family
\{\left|\phi_{s}\right\rangle\}
is not jointly diagonal in
B
’s classical measurement basis, the local states on
B
are noncommuting. Thus
\rho_{AB}
is not classical-classical and possesses nonzero quantum discord.
∎

Theorem
2
extends the mechanism of Theorem
1
and the construction in
[
9
]
. It shows that hidden coordination need not rely on a jointly diagonal encoding; the same correlations can arise from separable states with noncommuting local structure. Quantum discord therefore provides a distinct mechanism for implementing coordination under restricted information.

IV-C

Toward Multi-Stage Discordant Implementations

The two-stage construction above isolates the role of noncommutativity. Extending the mechanism to general multi-stage processes (indexed by
k=1,\dots,n
) is natural but introduces additional technical considerations.

A natural template uses separable states of the form

\rho=\sum_{s}p(s)\,\rho^{(1)}_{s}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho^{(n)}_{s},

together with local measurements depending only on
Y_{k}
, such that the induced distribution matches

P^{\star}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{s}p(s)\prod_{k=1}^{n}P_{k}(x_{k}\mid s,y_{k}).

For at least one stage
m
, the family
\{\rho^{(m)}_{s}\}_{s}
must not be jointly diagonalizable in any basis compatible with the measurement structure at that stage. In this case the coordination arises from genuinely noncommuting local structure rather than a classical latent-variable encoding in a fixed basis.

A full characterization of such multi-stage implementations is subtle (one must locate the nonclassicality and show it is irreducible to a diagonal form). We do not attempt one here. Instead, Theorem
2
serves as the minimal concrete realization of the broader phenomenon.

IV-C
1
A Concrete Three-Stage Discordant Implementation

To show that the discord-based mechanism extends immediately beyond two stages, consider a three-stage process with binary outcomes
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\in\{0,1\}
and constant information variables
Y_{2}=Y_{3}
(no stage receives information about previous outcomes). Let
S\in\{0,1\}
be uniform and define

P^{\star}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{x_{1},s}\delta_{x_{2},1-s}\delta_{x_{3},s}.

Stages 1 and 3 must match
S
; stage 2 must anti-correlate with
S
.

Define the separable state

\rho_{ABC}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(|0\rangle\langle 0|_{A}\otimes|+\rangle\langle+|_{B}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|_{C}+|1\rangle\langle 1|_{A}\otimes|-\rangle\langle-|_{B}\otimes|1\rangle\langle 1|_{C}\Bigr),

where
|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
and
|-\rangle=(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
.

- Stage 1 (Alice): measure
A
in the computational basis
\to X_{1}
.
- Stage 2 (Bob): measure
B
in the
\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}
basis, map
|+\rangle\mapsto 1
,
|-\rangle\mapsto 0

\to X_{2}=1-S
.
- Stage 3 (Charlie): measure
C
in the computational basis
\to X_{3}
.

All measurements depend only on the constant information variables, satisfying the constraint. The joint distribution is exactly
P^{\star}
. Moreover, the local states on
B
are not jointly diagonal in Bob’s measurement basis, so
\rho_{ABC}
has nonzero quantum discord (localized between subsystems
A
and
B
; subsystem
C
is classically correlated).

This explicit construction illustrates how the template extends to multi-stage settings whenever at least one stage carries noncommuting local structure compatible with its fixed measurement. It suggests that discord-based coordination can apply more generally under restricted information.

IV-D

Limitations of Quantum Implementations

While quantum correlations extend the class of distributions that can be implemented under restricted information, this extension is inherently limited. The limitation applies equally to the diagonal constructions of Theorem
1
, the discordant constructions of Theorem
2
, and the general quantum model of Section
III
.

Unlike classical conditioning under perfect recall, quantum correlations are fixed in advance in the shared state
\rho
. The choice of measurement at each stage
k
may depend only on the available information
Y_{k}
; agents cannot choose different measurement bases based on distinctions in history that are hidden behind
Y_{k}
.

Consequently, quantum models cannot reproduce fully adaptive dependence on realized past outcomes when those outcomes are observationally indistinguishable under the given information structure. They can pre-encode rich correlations (including latent-variable coordination via Theorem
1
and nonclassical discord via Theorem
2
), but they cannot restore the full power of dynamic conditioning on the fine-grained history that perfect recall would allow.

Taken together, Theorems
1
–
2
and the discord example in Section
III-A
show that quantum correlations act as a
partial substitute
for perfect recall. They extend implementability beyond classical local rules, yet they leave a fundamental gap: classical coordination relies on dynamic access to realized history, while quantum coordination relies on correlations encoded in advance in a shared state.

V
Conclusion

We have studied the implementability of correlated distributions under restricted information. In the classical setting, Kuhn’s theorem shows that global randomization can be implemented by local conditional rules whenever agents have perfect recall of past history. When recall is imperfect, however, this mechanism breaks down: some joint distributions cannot be reproduced by any collection of local rules.

This work demonstrates that quantum systems overcome the classical limitation. Even separable quantum states—both those diagonal in a fixed basis (encoding classical latent variables) and those with nonzero quantum discord—enable the implementation of joint distributions that are unattainable by classical local rules under the same information constraints.
As the examples in Section
III-A
and Theorem
1
illustrate, quantum systems can compensate for the breakdown of Kuhn’s equivalence without granting agents access to past outcomes or latent variables.

At the same time, our results reveal a fundamental limitation: unlike perfect recall, quantum correlations are fixed in advance in the shared state and cannot simulate fully adaptive dependence on realized past outcomes when those outcomes are observationally indistinguishable under the information structure. Consequently, quantum models cannot restore the full power of classical conditioning.

Taken together, these results establish that quantum correlations act as a partial substitute for perfect recall. They extend the set of implementable distributions beyond classical limits while remaining strictly constrained by the underlying information structure. This highlights a sharp distinction between classical and quantum mechanisms for generating correlations: classical coordination relies on dynamic access to realized history, whereas quantum coordination relies on correlations encoded in advance in a shared state.

An interesting direction for future work is to characterize the precise boundary between classical and quantum implementability for arbitrary information structures
(Y_{k})
. In particular, two concrete questions arise from Theorems 1 and 2:

•

All explicit constructions in this paper, including those based on separable states with noncommuting local structure, yield distributions that admit a latent-variable representation of the form in Theorem 1. Is this reduction intrinsic to all separable quantum implementations under restricted information, or can more general quantum resources generate distributions that lie strictly beyond this class?

•

More generally, what is the full set of distributions that can be implemented quantum-mechanically under restricted information?
