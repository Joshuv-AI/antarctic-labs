Executive Summary
Clark County implemented high-efficiency LED lighting retrofits at four County facilities
under DOE Award DE-SE0000729 to improve safety, visibility, and energy performance
while reducing operational costs. Aging, inefficient lighting equipment was replaced with
modern LED fixtures engineered to deliver higher-quality illumination, greater
operational reliability, and reduced energy consumption which benefited the community.

The project successfully completed all planned activities, including assessment, design
optimization, installation, and performance monitoring. All installations occurred on
structures less than 45 years old with no ground surface disruption and were exempt
from further historic preservation review under DOE’s Programmatic Agreement for
Nevada.

Measurable outcomes include improved lighting uniformity, reduced maintenance
requirements due to the extended lifespan of the new fixtures, meaningful reductions in
energy use, and overall cost savings. The project demonstrated that LED retrofits are
an effective and economically viable strategy for public-spaces and can be adapted for
a wide range of uses, not just athletic areas. DOE has completed its review of all
reimbursement requests, and Clark County has received the full $1 million award. The
project is financially complete and ready for closeout.

Background
Clark County is a dynamic and innovative organization dedicated to providing top-
quality service with integrity, respect and accountability. With jurisdiction over the world-
famous Las Vegas Strip and covering an area the size of New Jersey, Clark County is
the nation’s 11th-largest county and provides extensive regional services to more than 2
million citizens and 42 million visitors a year.
Clark County, like many public-sector facilities across the United States face rising
energy costs, aging infrastructure, and increasing expectations related to safety and
operational performance. LED lighting technologies have matured significantly over the
past decade, demonstrating superior energy efficiency, longer service life, and improved
optical control compared to legacy lighting systems such as metal halide, high-pressure
sodium, and fluorescent fixtures.

Prior studies and field deployments documented by the U.S. Department of Energy’s
Solid-State Lighting (SSL) Program and GATEWAY Demonstration Program, as well as
industry research, have shown that LED retrofits can reduce lighting energy
consumption by approximately 40–70% while improving illumination quality and
reducing maintenance cycles. This project builds upon those established findings by
applying best-practice LED retrofit strategies in real-world County facilities, emphasizing
safety, visibility, and cost control.

Unlike laboratory-based R&D efforts, this project focused on deployment-scale
implementation and performance validation in operational environments, providing
practical evidence to accelerate adoption across additional County sites.

Project Objectives
The focus of this project was on the following objectives:

 • Improve safety and visibility at County facilities through upgraded lighting
 systems
 • Reduce energy consumption associated with lighting loads

 • Lower long-term maintenance and replacement costs

 • Demonstrate cost-effective LED retrofit strategies applicable to other public
 facilities

The project supported national clean energy goals by reducing electricity demand and
improving energy efficiency in public infrastructure. Tasks defined in the Statement of
Project Objectives included site assessments, retrofit planning, lighting design
optimization, installation, monitoring, and reporting. All milestones and go/no-go
decision points were successfully met.

Project Results and Discussion
Project Activities

The following activities were completed:
 • Preliminary site assessments

 • Development of project plans

 • Retrofit planning and coordination

 • Lighting design optimization
 • Installation of LED lighting fixtures

 • Performance monitoring

 • Stakeholder engagement

 • Reporting and documentation

Administrative activities supported implementation but did not require environmental or
historic preservation review.

Implementation Results

LED retrofit installations were completed at four County sites. All installations were
completed on schedule and within the approved budget. Lighting systems now provide
improved uniformity, color rendering, and visibility compared to pre-project conditions.

Milestone Comparison

All milestones outlined in the Technical Work Plan were achieved. No project decision
points required termination, pause, or reduction in scope. Minor scheduling adjustments
were managed without impact to cost or performance outcomes.

Public Feedback
Conversations with park users about the new lighting were consistently positive.
Several noted that the improved visibility made them feel safer while using the park,
and others commented on the brighter, more welcoming appearance with the new
lights. Although this feedback reflects people’s personal opinions about the lighting, it
still provides a helpful indication of overall public sentiment toward the retrofit.

Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
Key accomplishments include:

 • Successful completion of LED retrofits at four operational County facilities

 • Demonstrated improvement in safety and visibility for users and staff

 • Verified reductions in energy consumption and long-term maintenance costs
 • Full reimbursement of the $1 million DOE award

 • Reduction in off-site light spill through improved optical control and fixture
 shielding

 • Decreased light pollution due to more precise light distribution and lower
 unintended upward illumination
The project confirms that well-designed LED retrofits can simultaneously improve
operational performance, enhance public safety, reduce energy use, and minimize
unintended environmental impacts such as light spill and light pollution.

Lessons Learned
 • Early coordination with facility stakeholders improves installation efficiency

 • Standardized fixture specifications streamline procurement and maintenance
 • Clear documentation supports smoother reimbursement and closeout processes

These lessons will inform future County lighting projects.

Energy Usage and Cost Savings
Post-installation performance monitoring indicates meaningful reductions in lighting
energy demand and consumption compared to baseline conditions. Reduced
maintenance requirements and longer fixture lifespans further improve lifecycle cost
performance. Reimbursed grant funds will be reinvested to support additional LED
lighting projects across County facilities.
