Combined effect of homogenization and dimension-reduction in the random Neumann sieve problem
Mert Baştuğ

Abstract
We investigate the asymptotic behavior of the solutions to the Neumann sieve problem for the Poisson equation in a thin, randomly perforated domain. The perforations (sieve-holes) are generated by a stationary marked point process. According to the scaling between the domain thickness and the typical hole size, three distinct limiting regimes emerge. We also identify the optimal stochastic integrability condition on the random hole radii that guarantees stochastic homogenization, even in the presence of clustering holes.

Introduction

In this paper, we study Neumann’s sieve problem on thin domains with random perforations. For a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence of positive real numbers
(\delta_{\varepsilon})
with
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\delta_{\varepsilon}=0
, we consider the following sequence of boundary value problems in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta u_{\varepsilon}&=f\quad&&\text{in }U_{\varepsilon},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=0\quad&&\text{on }\partial U^{\prime}\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon}),\\
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu&=0\quad&&\text{on }((U^{\prime}\setminus T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})\times\{0\})\cup(U^{\prime}\times\{-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon}\}).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here,

is a bounded and open set with Lipschitz boundary,

, and

is an open set. The domain
U_{\varepsilon}
is defined by

U_{\varepsilon}:=(U^{\prime}\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon}))\setminus((U^{\prime}\setminus T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})\times\{0\}).

Hence, the two parts,
U^{\prime}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
and
U^{\prime}\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},0)
, are connected through the set

representing the perforations (see Figure

\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},\delta_{\varepsilon}x_{N})
and to obtain a characterization of the limit in terms of an effective boundary value problem. As
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
, we expect the limit function to depend only on
N-1
variables and the effective equation to be set on the domain
U^{\prime}
.

The asymptotic character of the sequence
(\hat{u}_{\varepsilon})
naturally depends on
T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
. In this paper, we consider perforations obtained from scaled and translated copies of a model hole. Fix a bounded and open set
T^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
. We choose a discrete, countably infinite set of random points
\{(y^{\prime}_{i},\rho_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times(0,\infty)
, and we set

T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}:=\bigcup_{\varepsilon y_{i}^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}}(\varepsilon y_{i}^{\prime}+a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}T^{\prime})\cap U^{\prime}.

Thus, the position and size of each copy of
T^{\prime}
in
T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
is random, with
(y^{\prime}_{i})
determining the centers and
(\rho_{i})
the sizes of the copies. The centers are scaled by
\varepsilon
and the radii by a positive number
a_{\varepsilon}
with
a_{\varepsilon}\ll\varepsilon
, so that the number of holes in
T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
increases while their sizes shrink as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The choice of the random points
\{(y^{\prime}_{i},\rho_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is made precise with the theory of marked point processes.

The domain
U_{\varepsilon}
is a model for two thin films, represented by
U^{\prime}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
and
U^{\prime}\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},0)
, connected by a randomly generated contact region
T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\times\{0\}
. In
[
1
]
, the authors prove that, for periodically distributed contact regions, the homogenization of the nonlinear elastic energy of a deformation of
U_{\varepsilon}
yields an additional interfacial energy term, which results from the penalization of debonding at the interface, in agreement with the phenomenological model introduced in
[
5
]
. In our paper, we consider a simplification of the problem by replacing the nonlinear elastic energy with the Dirichlet energy, as we are mainly interested in observing the effect of randomizing the contact region. From now on, instead of holes, we shall refer to contact regions or connection regions.

For
\varepsilon
,
\delta_{\varepsilon}
and
a_{\varepsilon}
satisfying certain asymptotic relationships as
\varepsilon\to 0
and under mild conditions on the probability law of the random points
\{(y^{\prime}_{i},\rho_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, we show that there exist functions
u^{+},u^{-}\in H_{0}^{1}(U^{\prime})
and a real-valued random variable
\gamma
, such that, almost surely,

\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u^{+}\text{ in }H^{1}(U^{\prime}\times(0,1)),\quad\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u^{-}\text{ in }H^{1}(U^{\prime}\times(-1,0)),

(1.2)

and the functions
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
solve the following coupled boundary value problems:

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta u^{+}+\frac{\gamma}{2}(u^{+}-u^{-})&=f\quad\text{in }U^{\prime},\\
u^{+}&=0\quad\text{on }\partial U^{\prime},\end{aligned}\end{cases}\quad\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta u^{-}-\frac{\gamma}{2}(u^{+}-u^{-})&=f\quad\text{in }U^{\prime},\\
u^{-}&=0\quad\text{on }\partial U^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.3)

If
a_{\varepsilon}
is either too small or too large relative to the domain height
\delta_{\varepsilon}
and the average distance
\varepsilon
between the centers of neighboring contact regions, then the effective equations are trivial, corresponding to
\gamma=0
or
\gamma=\infty
. Hence, in both cases
u^{+}=u^{-}
and
-\Delta u^{+}=-\Delta u^{-}=f
. Given
(\delta_{\varepsilon})
, we show, however, that there exists a critical scaling for
(a_{\varepsilon})
such that the homogenized equations are nontrivial. For the following discussion, we assume
N\geq 4
. We comment on the case
N=3
at the end. The critical scaling depends on the growth of a certain capacity function with respect to the relative thickness of the domain, which we now define. For
h>0
, set

C_{h}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}):=\left\{v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times(-h,h)}):\operatorname{supp}(v)\subset\joinrel\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\right\}.

Hence, if
v\in C_{h}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, then it is smooth up to the boundary of
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times(-h,h)
, and it vanishes at infinity. We note that
v
does not have to vanish completely on the boundary. For
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
bounded, we define

\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1}):=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times(-h,h)}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx:v\in C_{h}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}),\,K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{v=1\})\right\},

(1.4)

where
\operatorname{int}(\{v=1\})
is the interior of the level set
\{v=1\}
. If
(\delta_{\varepsilon})
and
(a_{\varepsilon})
satisfy

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}}{\varepsilon^{N-1}\delta_{\varepsilon}}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{a_{\varepsilon}}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\in(0,\infty),

(1.5)

then the limit equations for (
1.1
) are given by the system (
1.3
) with
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
. Assuming the existence of the following limits for the moment, let us set

\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\lim_{h\to\infty}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1}),\quad\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1}):=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{2h}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1}).

(1.6)

If all capacities are finite and nonzero, then, depending on the relative thickness
\delta_{\varepsilon}/a_{\varepsilon}
, the critical scaling (
1.5
) yields three regimes:

(i)

\delta_{\varepsilon}\gg a_{\varepsilon}
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}}{\varepsilon^{N-1}\delta_{\varepsilon}}\in(0,\infty)
,

(ii)

\delta_{\varepsilon}\sim a_{\varepsilon}
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}}{\varepsilon^{N-1}\delta_{\varepsilon}}\in(0,\infty)
,

(iii)

\delta_{\varepsilon}\ll a_{\varepsilon}
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-3}}{\varepsilon^{N-1}}\in(0,\infty)
.

By an easy computation, we see that a sequence
(a_{\varepsilon})
satisfying (i), (ii) or (iii) exists if and only if
\delta_{\varepsilon}^{N-3}\gg\varepsilon^{N-1}
,
\delta_{\varepsilon}^{N-3}\sim\varepsilon^{N-1}
or
\delta_{\varepsilon}^{N-3}\ll\varepsilon^{N-1}
, respectively. When
\{y^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}=\mathbb{Z}^{N-1}
and
\rho_{i}=\rho
for some
\rho>0
and all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, that is, when the connection regions have a uniform size and are distributed periodically, the criticality conditions agree with those studied in
[
2
,
1
]
.

We now give a heuristic derivation of the limit equations (
1.3
) and show how the criticality condition (
1.5
), equivalently conditions (i), (ii) and (iii), naturally arises. We suppose that (
1.2
) holds for some
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
. As
u_{\varepsilon}
is approximated closely by
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
, we may write the scaled Dirichlet energy of
u_{\varepsilon}
as an approximate sum of two terms:

\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\approx\left(\int_{U^{\prime}}|\nabla u^{+}|^{2}+|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\right)+\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{TE}_{\varepsilon}.

We denote by
\mathrm{TE}_{\varepsilon}
the transition energy, that is, the Dirichlet energy necessary to interpolate between
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
in a neighborhood of the connection regions. In particular, we have

\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-fu_{\varepsilon}\,dx\approx\left(\int_{U^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u^{+}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}-f(u^{+}+u^{-})\,dx^{\prime}\right)+\frac{1}{2\delta_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{TE}_{\varepsilon}.

(1.7)

Since the left-hand side is minimized by
u_{\varepsilon}
among all
H^{1}(U_{\varepsilon})
functions that vanish on
\partial U^{\prime}\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})
and since the integral on the right-hand side does not depend on
\varepsilon
, we observe that
u_{\varepsilon}
minimizes the transition energy as well. If
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
are continuous, then, in the vicinity of the fixed connection region
(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i}+a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}T^{\prime})\times\{0\}
, we can approximate
u_{\varepsilon}
by
u^{+}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i})
in the upper half-space and by
u^{-}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i})
in the lower half-space. The minimal Dirichlet energy required to transition between these two values is approximately equal to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u^{+}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i})-u^{-}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i})}{2}\right)^{2}\operatorname{Cap}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i}+a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}T^{\prime},

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N-1})

(1.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(u^{+}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i})-u^{-}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i}))^{2}a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho_{i}^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1}),

where we used the scaling property of the capacity in the last line. If we partition
U^{\prime}
into sets
Q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Q^{\prime}_{n}
with sufficiently small but fixed diameters, then, summing up the energies over all contact regions in
T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
and grouping those falling in the same set
Q^{\prime}_{j}
yields

\frac{1}{2\delta_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{TE}_{\varepsilon}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{8}\fint_{Q^{\prime}_{j}}|u^{+}-u^{-}|^{2}\,dx\sum_{\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i}\in Q^{\prime}_{j}}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{i}^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})

(1.9)

If we assume that, for any sufficiently nice set
Q^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
, the spatial averages

\frac{1}{|Q^{\prime}|}\sum_{\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i}\in Q^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{i}^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})=\frac{1}{\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Q^{\prime}\right|}\sum_{\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i}\in Q^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}}{\varepsilon^{N-1}\delta_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{i}^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})

(1.10)

converge to a constant
\gamma
, then we obtain

\frac{1}{2\delta_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{TE}_{\varepsilon}\approx\frac{\gamma}{4}\int_{U^{\prime}}|u^{+}-u^{-}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}.

Going back to (
1.7
), we see as a consequence that the pair
(u^{+},u^{-})
minimizes the energy

\int_{U^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u^{+}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}-f(u^{+}+u^{-})+\frac{\gamma}{4}|u^{+}-u^{-}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}

among all pairs in
H_{0}^{1}(U^{\prime})\times H_{0}^{1}(U^{\prime})
. The associated Euler-Lagrange equation is given by the coupled system of PDEs (
1.3
). Assume that the criticality conditions (i), (ii), or (iii) hold and recall (
1.6
). If we set
h_{0}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\delta_{\varepsilon}/a_{\varepsilon}
and define

J_{h_{0}}(\rho):=\begin{cases}\rho^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad&\text{if }h_{0}=\infty,\\
\rho^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\quad&\text{if }h_{0}\in(0,\infty),\\
2\rho^{N-3}\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\quad&\text{if }h_{0}=0,\end{cases}

then we see that the spatial average (
1.10
) is roughly proportional to

\frac{1}{\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Q^{\prime}\right|}\sum_{\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i}\in Q^{\prime}}J_{h_{0}}(\rho_{i}).

(1.11)

Although our heuristic derivation suggests the right limit problem, there are several technical difficulties associated with it, some of which stem from the randomness of the sieve geometry. A major problem is caused by contact regions that are too large or too close to each other, resulting in clusters. Our estimate (
1.8
) of the transition energy in terms of the capacity requires the contact regions to be well-separated. However, unless we impose bounds on the distances
|y^{\prime}_{i}-y^{\prime}_{j}|
for
i\neq j
or on the radii
\rho_{i}
, the clusters occur with high probability. In our paper, we shall assume, roughly speaking, no more than the convergence of the averages (
1.11
) for nice sets
Q^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
(see Theorem
2.7
for details). When formulated in terms of marked point processes, the assumptions translate to the finiteness of the integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\,d\lambda(\rho)
for a stationary marked point process, where
\lambda
is, informally, the probability distribution of the radii
(\rho_{i})
. Nevertheless, based only on this assumption, we are able to show that the capacity of the clusters is negligible almost surely. Here, we follow an approach similar to the one in our previous paper
[
4
]
.

For
N=3
, the capacity function in (
1.4
) is always zero due to the fact that the harmonic capacity vanishes on bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this case, instead of (
1.8
), the correct transition energy is given by

\left(\frac{u^{+}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i})-u^{-}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i})}{2}\right)^{2}\operatorname{Cap}((\varepsilon y^{\prime}_{i}+a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}T^{\prime})\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{3}),

(1.12)

where

\operatorname{Cap}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N-1}):=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx:v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,K\subset\operatorname{int}(\{v=1\})\right\}

for
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
bounded. However, even then, (
1.12
) holds only when
\ln(\varepsilon/a_{\varepsilon})\ll\delta_{\varepsilon}/a_{\varepsilon}
. When the relative thickness
\delta_{\varepsilon}/a_{\varepsilon}
is asymptotically comparable to or smaller than
\ln(\varepsilon/a_{\varepsilon})
, two-dimensional effects dominate, and the minimum transition energy is no longer given by the harmonic capacity. Hence, for
N=3
, we consider the critical scaling given by

\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{a_{\varepsilon}}\right)\ll\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{a_{\varepsilon}},\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{2}\delta_{\varepsilon}}\in(0,\infty).

Given
(\delta_{\varepsilon})
, a sequence
(a_{\varepsilon})
satisfying the criticality condition exists if and only if
\ln(1/\delta_{\varepsilon})\ll 1/\varepsilon^{2}
.

We now describe the ideas of the proof of our homogenization result. The main step in the proof is the construction of a sequence
(w_{\varepsilon})
with
w_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(U_{\varepsilon})
such that the rescaled functions
\hat{w}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=w_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},\delta_{\varepsilon}x_{N})
converge weakly to
1
in
H^{1}(U^{\prime}\times(0,1))
and to
-1
in
H^{1}(U^{\prime}\times(-1,0))
almost surely. Given any
v^{+},v^{-}\in H_{0}^{1}(U^{\prime})
, the functions
(w_{\varepsilon})
allow us to produce a sequence
(v_{\varepsilon})
with
v_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(U_{\varepsilon})
whose scaled versions converge weakly to
v^{+}
and
v^{-}
in the upper and lower half-spaces, respectively. In turn, we use
(v_{\varepsilon})
as test functions in the weak formulation of (
1.1
) and, granted that the Laplacians
(\Delta v_{\varepsilon})
converge, we pass to the limit. As we can choose
v^{+}
and
v^{-}
arbitrarily, in this way, we obtain a complete characterization of the Laplacians of
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
. This is the essence of Tartar’s
method of oscillating test functions
. We construct
(w_{\varepsilon})
that minimize the transition energy near the connection regions, thereby ensuring that
w_{\varepsilon}
approximates the solution of a PDE. This is the crucial ingredient for the Laplacians
(\Delta v_{\varepsilon})
to converge.

. We write

, and in

.

The Lebesgue measure of a measurable set

. We denote the

If

. For an arbitrary set

2.2
Marked point processes

In this section, we present the necessary definitions and key results from the theory of marked point processes. For additional information and proofs of some of the results, we refer the reader to our previous paper
[
4
]
. A more detailed introduction can be found in the books
[
8
,
18
]
.

We formulate all definitions and theorems in the context of Euclidean spaces. Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
. If
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is open, we denote the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
O
by
\mathcal{B}(O)
.

A point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a set of randomly distributed points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If every point has an associated characteristic (a
mark
), then we call it a marked point process (in short m.p.p.). In our work, a mark is simply a positive number. We call
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)

admissible
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

Each point has a unique mark, that is, if
(y,\rho_{1}),(y,\rho_{2})\in Y
, then
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
,

(ii)

The projection of
Y
onto
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
locally finite
. Equivalently, the set
Y\cap(B\times(0,\infty))
has finite cardinality for all bounded Borel sets
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We define
\mathbb{M}^{d}
to be the set of all admissible
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
. Let
\mathcal{M}^{d}
be the smallest
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{M}^{d}
that makes all functions
Y\mapsto\operatorname{card}(Y\cap E)
measurable, as
E
runs through the Borel sets in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
marked point process
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with positive marks is a measurable map
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{d},\mathcal{M}^{d})
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Its
probability distribution
is the measure
\mathcal{L}_{M}
defined by
\mathcal{L}_{M}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{P}(M^{-1}(\mathcal{A}))
for all
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{M}^{d}
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{L}_{M}
is the push-forward measure of
\mathbb{P}
under
M
.

By a marked point process we will exclusively mean an m.p.p. on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with positive marks. Let
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{d},\mathcal{M}^{d})
be an m.p.p. If
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))
, then the integer-valued random variable
\omega\mapsto M(\omega)(E)
, where
\omega\in\Omega
, represents the number of points in
M(\omega)
lying in
E
. We denote it briefly by
M(E)
. Thus, the average number of points in
M
that belong to
E
is given by the expectation
\mathbb{E}[M(E)]
. It is easy to check that the expectation is a Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
as a function of
E
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{d},\mathcal{M}^{d})
be a marked point process. Define
\Lambda(E):=\mathbb{E}[M(E)]
for all
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))
. Then
\Lambda
is a Borel measure called the
intensity measure
of
M
. If
\Lambda(B\times(0,\infty))<\infty
for all bounded Borel sets
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then we say that
M
has
finite intensity
.

Now, we introduce an important class of marked point processes. If
Y\in\mathbb{M}^{d}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

Y_{\tau}:=\{(y+\tau,\rho):(y,\rho)\in Y\}.

Analogously, if
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{M}^{d}
, then
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}:=\{Y_{\tau}:Y\in\mathcal{A}\}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A marked point process
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{d},\mathcal{M}^{d})
is called
stationary
if
\mathcal{L}_{M}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{L}_{M}(\mathcal{A}_{\tau})
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{M}^{d}
.

Intuitively, stationarity means that the points of the m.p.p. are distributed spatially homogeneously. For a stationary m.p.p., the intensity measure can be factorized as shown in the following proposition. For a proof, see Proposition 2.5 in
[
4
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{d},\mathcal{M}^{d})
is a stationary marked point process with finite intensity. Then there exists a finite Borel measure
\lambda
on
(0,\infty)
such that

\Lambda=\lambda\otimes\mathcal{L}^{d},

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{L}^{d}
is the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

Assume
(C_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an increasing sequence of sets such that
\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}C_{k}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Often, one is interested in the spatial averages

\frac{1}{|C_{k}|}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y,\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
y\in C_{k}\end{subarray}}g(\rho)

(2.2)

for a nonnegative function
g
and
\omega\in\Omega
. For instance, if we take
g(\rho)=\rho
, then (
2.2
) is an approximation to the average mark size in
M(\omega)
per unit volume.

Below, we present a version of the ergodic theorem with which the limit of (
2.2
) as
k\to\infty
can be represented as a conditional expectation with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra of
translation invariant sets
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{d},\mathcal{M}^{d})
be a marked point process. A set
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{M}^{d}
is called
invariant
if
\mathcal{L}_{M}(\mathcal{A}\,\triangle\,\mathcal{A}_{\tau})=0
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The
\sigma
-algebra of invariant sets is denoted by
\mathcal{I}
.

We denote the
\sigma
-algebra
\{M^{-1}(\mathcal{A})\in\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{I}\}
by
M^{-1}(\mathcal{I})
. By an
M^{-1}(\mathcal{I})
-measurable random measure we mean a function
\xi:\Omega\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))\rightarrow[0,\infty]
such that
\xi(\omega,\cdot)
is a Borel measure for all
\omega\in\Omega
, and
\xi(\cdot,E)
is
M^{-1}(\mathcal{I})
-measurable for all
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))
.

The following lemma shows that the conditional expectation of a random variable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra
M^{-1}(\mathcal{I})
can be expressed as an integral with respect to a random measure.

Lemma 2.6

(Lemma 12.2.III
[
18
]
)
.

Let
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{d},\mathcal{M}^{d})
be a stationary marked point process with finite intensity. Then there exists an
M^{-1}(\mathcal{I})
-measurable random measure
\xi
such that

\mathbb{E}[G\circ M\,|\,M^{-1}(\mathcal{I})](\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(y,\rho)\,d\xi(\omega,\rho)\,dy\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(2.3)

for all Borel measurable
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, where

G(Y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)}g(y,\rho)\,dY(y,\rho).

In particular,

\mathbb{E}[M(B\times L)\,|\,M^{-1}(\mathcal{I})](\omega)=\xi(\omega,L)|B|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(2.4)

for all bounded
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for all
L\in\mathcal{B}(0,\infty)
.

A proof of the following version of the ergodic theorem can be found in Section 7.1 of
[
4
]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{d},\mathcal{M}^{d})
be a stationary marked point process with finite intensity. Let
\xi
be the random measure defined in Lemma
2.6
. Assume
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded with nonempty interior such that
|\partial B|=0
. Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}B\right|}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y,\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y\in B\end{subarray}}g(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\rho)\,d\xi(\omega,\rho)\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(2.5)

for all
\lambda
-integrable functions
g:(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, where
\lambda
is the measure on
(0,\infty)
given by
\Lambda=\lambda\otimes\mathcal{L}^{d}
.

2.3
Harmonic capacity

In this section, we introduce several notions of harmonic capacity and establish their relationships, together with auxiliary results needed later. Let us recall that the classical harmonic capacity of a bounded set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
relative to an open set
U\supset\overline{K}
is defined by

\operatorname{Cap}(K,U):=\inf\left\{\int_{U}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx:v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U),\,K\subset\operatorname{int}(\{v=1\})\right\}.

(2.6)

We introduce two other capacity functions that will appear frequently. Let
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be bounded, and let
U^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be open with
\overline{K^{\prime}}\subset U^{\prime}
. For
h>0
we define

\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime}):=\inf\left\{\int_{U_{h}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx:v\in C_{h}^{\infty}(U^{\prime}),\,K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{v=1\})\right\},

(2.7)

where

U_{h}:=U^{\prime}\times(-h,h),\quad C_{h}^{\infty}(U^{\prime}):=\left\{v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{U_{h}}):\operatorname{supp}(v)\subset\joinrel\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\right\}.

If we set

E_{h}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{U_{1}}|\nabla^{\prime}v|^{2}+\frac{1}{h^{2}}|\partial_{N}v|^{2}\,dx,\quad v\in C_{1}^{\infty}(U^{\prime}),

then a simple change of variables gives

\frac{1}{2h}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})=\inf\{E_{h}(v):v\in C_{1}^{\infty}(U^{\prime}),\,K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}\{(v=1\})\}.

(2.8)

We also introduce

\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime}):=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{2h}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime}).

In Proposition
2.13
we show that
\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})
is characterized by a minimization problem similar to (
2.6
) and (
2.7
). First we prove that it is well-defined.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be bounded, and let
U^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be open with
\overline{K^{\prime}}\subset U^{\prime}
. If
h_{1}\leq h_{2}
, then

\frac{h_{1}}{h_{2}}\operatorname{Cap}_{h_{2}}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{h_{1}}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\leq\frac{h_{2}}{h_{1}}\operatorname{Cap}_{h_{2}}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime}).

(2.9)

In particular,
h\in(0,\infty)\mapsto\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})/(2h)
is a continuous decreasing function. Furthermore,

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{2h}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})<\infty.

(2.10)

Remark 2.8.1
.

The proposition implies that
\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})=0
if and only if
\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})=0
for any
h>0
.

Proof.

Let
v\in C_{1}^{\infty}(U^{\prime})
with
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}\{(v=1\})
. By (
2.8
) we have

\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{h_{1}}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})}{2h_{1}}\leq E_{h_{1}}(v)\leq\left(\frac{h_{2}}{h_{1}}\right)^{2}E_{h_{2}}(v).

Taking the infimum over all
v
yields
\operatorname{Cap}_{h_{1}}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\leq(h_{2}/h_{1})\operatorname{Cap}_{h_{2}}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})
.

The first inequality in (
2.9
) is equivalent to showing that
\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})/(2h)
is decreasing in
h
. Since
E_{h_{2}}(v)\leq E_{h_{1}}(v)
, this follows from (
2.8
) as well. Continuity is a direct corollary of (
2.9
). Finally, we prove (
2.10
). Let
w\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U^{\prime})
with
K^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{w=1\})
. Set
\hat{w}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=w(x^{\prime})
for
(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in U^{\prime}\times(-1,1)
. Then
\hat{w}\in C_{1}^{\infty}(U^{\prime})
and
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{\hat{w}=1\})
. We have

\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})}{2h}\leq E_{h}(\hat{w})=\int_{U^{\prime}}|\nabla^{\prime}w|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}.

Hence,
\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})/(2h)
is uniformly bounded in
h
. Since it is also monotone in
h
, the limit as
h\to 0
exists and is finite.
∎

The following definition helps us extend the set of admissible functions in (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) to weakly differentiable functions.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open, and let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be bounded. A function
v\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(U)
is said to
vanish weakly
on
A
if there exist an open neighborhood
W
of
A
and a sequence
(v_{n})\subset C^{\infty}(U\cap W)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}-v\|_{H^{1}(U\cap W)}=0,\quad A\cap\overline{\operatorname{supp}(v_{n})}=\emptyset\text{ for all }n,

(2.11)

where the closure is taken in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If
v-a
vanishes weakly on
A
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
v=a
weakly on
A
.

Remark 2.9.1
.

If
v\in H^{1}(U)
, then
(v_{n})
can be taken in
C^{\infty}(U)
, and the convergence in (
2.11
) holds in
H^{1}(U)
. This follows by a cut-off argument and from the density of smooth functions in
H^{1}(U)
.

Remark 2.9.2
.

Note that
A
does not have to be a subset of
U
. For example, if
U
is bounded, then
u\in H^{1}(U)
vanishes weakly on
\partial U
if and only if
u\in H_{0}^{1}(U)
.

The next lemma shows that we can define weak vanishing equivalently in terms of weak convergence in
H^{1}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open, and let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be bounded. Let
v\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(U)
. Assume there exist an open neighborhood
W
of
A
and a sequence
(v_{n})\subset C^{\infty}(U\cap W)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}-v\|_{L^{2}(U\cap W)}=0,\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(U\cap W;\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\infty,\quad A\cap\overline{\operatorname{supp}(v_{n})}=\emptyset\text{ for all }n.

Then
v=0
weakly on
A
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
v\in L^{2}(U\cap W)
. Otherwise, we replace
W
by a bounded open neighborhood of
A
. By hypothesis
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v
in
H^{1}(U\cap W)
. Hence, by Mazur’s lemma there exist convex combinations

w_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{(n)}v_{j},\quad 0\leq\lambda_{j}^{(n)}\leq 1,\quad\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{(n)}=1

such that
w_{n}\to v
in
H^{1}(U\cap W)
. Since it is clear that
A\cap\overline{\operatorname{supp}(w_{n})}=\emptyset
, the claim is proved.
∎

If
U
and
U^{\prime}
are bounded, then we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}(K,U)=\inf\left\{\int_{U}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx:v\in H_{0}^{1}(U),\,v=1\text{ weakly on }K\right\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})=\inf\left\{\int_{U_{h}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx:v\in H^{1}(U_{h}),\,v=0\text{ weakly on }\Gamma_{h},\,v=1\text{ weakly on }K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\right\},

where we set
\Gamma_{h}:=\partial U^{\prime}\times(-h,h)
. The equalities follow from Remarks (
2.9.1
), (
2.9.2
) and the direct method of calculus of variations. When
U=\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
U^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
the correct function spaces are no longer Sobolev spaces. Set
\mathbb{R}_{h}^{N}:=\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times(-h,h)
. We introduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{v\in L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\nabla v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})\}\quad\text{for }N>2,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}):=\{v\in L^{2}(-h,h;L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})):\,\nabla v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{h}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})\}\quad\text{for }N>3.

By the Sobolev inequality, the completions of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
C_{h}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
under the
H_{0}^{1}
-norm yield
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\mathcal{C}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, respectively. Consequently,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N})=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx:v\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,v=1\text{ weakly on $K$}\right\}\quad\text{for }N>2,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{h}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx:v\in\mathcal{C}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}),\,v=1\text{ weakly on $K^{\prime}\times\{0\}$}\right\}\quad\text{for }N>3.

(2.13)

Using the larger set of admissible functions, we shall show that
\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Cap}(K^{\prime}\times\{0\},U^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R})
as
h\to\infty
for
N>3
and any open set
U^{\prime}\supset\overline{K^{\prime}}
. We begin by proving the equivalence of the function spaces involved.

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume
N>3
. Let
v\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\nabla v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then

v\in L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\iff v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R};L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})).

(2.14)

Proof.

Since
\nabla v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can find a sequence
(\varphi_{n})\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\nabla\varphi_{n}\to\nabla v
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By Sobolev’s inequality
(\varphi_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))
. As convergence in these spaces implies convergence in
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
(\varphi_{n})
has the same limit
\varphi
in both of them. Moreover,
\nabla\varphi=\nabla v
. Therefore,
\varphi-v=a
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Clearly,
a=0
if and only if

v\in L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R};L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})).

We now observe that
a=0
if any of the conditions in (
2.14
) holds, since a constant function cannot belong to either space if its nonzero.
∎

Proposition 2.12
.

Assume
N>3
. Let
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be bounded, and let
U^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be open with
\overline{K^{\prime}}\subset U^{\prime}
. Then
\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{Cap}(K^{\prime}\times\{0\},U^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R})
for all
h>0
and

\lim_{h\to\infty}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})=\operatorname{Cap}(K^{\prime}\times\{0\},U^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}).

Remark 2.12.1
.

Combined with Remark
2.8.1
, this proposition implies that
\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})=0
if and only if
\operatorname{Cap}(K^{\prime}\times\{0\},U^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R})=0
for
N>3
. A similar remark applies to
\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})
as well.

Proof.

Let
w\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R})
with
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{w=1\})
. Then
w\in C_{h}^{\infty}(U^{\prime})
for all
h>0
. Hence,
\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\leq\int_{U^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx
. Since
w
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{Cap}(K^{\prime}\times\{0\},U^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R})
for all
h>0
. Now choose
v_{h}\in C_{h}^{\infty}(U^{\prime})
with
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{v_{h}=1\})
such that

\int_{U_{h}}|\nabla v_{h}|^{2}\,dx=\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})+\varepsilon_{h},\quad\lim_{h\to\infty}\varepsilon_{h}=0.

Applying Sobolev’s inequality along
(N-1)
-dimensional cross-sections and integrating in
x_{N}
, we obtain

\int_{-h}^{h}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|v_{h}|^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}_{h}^{N}}|\nabla v_{h}|^{2}\,dx,

(2.15)

where
C
is independent of
h
. If we extend
v_{h}
by zero to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then it follows that
(v_{h})
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))
. Hence, we can pass to a subsequence (not relabeled) such that
v_{h}\rightharpoonup v
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))
and
\nabla v_{h}\rightharpoonup g
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
v
and
g
. If
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v\nabla\varphi\,dx=\lim_{h\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{h}^{N}}v_{h}\nabla\varphi\,dx=-\lim_{h\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{h}^{N}}\varphi\nabla v_{h}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi g\,dx.

This implies
\nabla v=g
. Consequently, Lemma
2.11
applies to
v
, and we deduce that
v\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Next we show that
v=0
weakly on
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}
. Let
W\supset K^{\prime}\times\{0\}
be open and bounded. Using (
2.15
) and Hölder’s inequality, we see that
(v_{h})
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(W)
. Consequently, the Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem gives
\|v_{h}-v\|_{L^{2}(W)}\to 0
and we obtain the weak vanishing from Lemma
2.10
. Therefore,
v
is an admissible function in (
2.12
). As a result

\operatorname{Cap}(K^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\leq\liminf_{h\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{h}^{N}}|\nabla v_{h}|^{2}\,dx\leq\liminf_{h\to\infty}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{Cap}(K^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where we used the lower semi-continuity of the
L^{2}
-norm in the second inequality. This proves our claim.
∎

Next we characterize
\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})
by a minimization problem.

Proposition 2.13
.

Assume
N>3
. Let
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be bounded, and let
U^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be bounded and open with
\overline{K^{\prime}}\subset U^{\prime}
. For
v:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, define
\hat{v}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=v(x^{\prime})
for
(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})=\inf\left\{\int_{U^{\prime}}|\nabla^{\prime}v|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}:v\in H_{0}^{1}(U^{\prime}),\,\hat{v}=1\text{ weakly on $K^{\prime}\times\{0\}$}\right\},

(2.16)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|\nabla^{\prime}v|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}:v\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}),\,\hat{v}=1\text{ weakly on }K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\right\}.

(2.17)

Remark 2.13.1
.

The condition
\hat{v}=0
weakly on
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}
is not equivalent to
v=0
weakly on
K^{\prime}
. For this reason,
\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\neq\operatorname{Cap}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})
in general. For example, if
N>3
and the Hausdorff dimension of
K^{\prime}
is strictly between
N-2
and
N-1
, then
\operatorname{Cap}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})>0
, whereas
\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})=0
.

Proof.

We only prove (
2.16
) since the proof of (
2.17
) differs only slightly. Define
m_{h}:=\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})/(2h)
and
m_{0}:=\lim_{h\to 0}m_{h}
. Denote the right-hand side of (
2.16
) by
m
. Let
v\in H_{0}^{1}(U^{\prime})
with
\hat{v}=1
weakly on
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}
. Then
\hat{v}\in H^{1}(U_{h})
, and, by Remark
2.9.2
,
\hat{v}=0
weakly on
\partial U^{\prime}\times(-h,h)
for all
h>0
. Thus, (

\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(K^{\prime},U^{\prime})\leq\int_{U_{h}}|\nabla\hat{v}|^{2}\,dx=2h\int_{U^{\prime}}|\nabla^{\prime}v|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}.

Since
v
is arbitrary, dividing by
h
and letting

yields
m_{0}\leq m
. To prove the other inequality, choose
v_{h}\in C_{1}^{\infty}(U^{\prime})
with
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{v_{h}=1\})
such that
E_{h}(v_{h})\to m_{0}
. If
h_{1},h_{2}\leq h
, then

E_{h}\left(\frac{v_{h_{1}}-v_{h_{2}}}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}E_{h}(v_{h_{1}})+\frac{1}{2}E_{h}(v_{h_{2}})-E_{h}\left(\frac{v_{h_{1}}+v_{h_{2}}}{2}\right).

We have
m_{h}\leq E_{h}((v_{h_{1}}+v_{h_{2}})/2)
, as
(v_{h_{1}}+v_{h_{2}})/2
is an admissible function. Thus,

E_{h}\left(\frac{v_{h_{1}}-v_{h_{2}}}{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}E_{h_{1}}(v_{h_{1}})+\frac{1}{2}E_{h_{2}}(v_{h_{2}})-m_{h}.

Then

\limsup_{h_{1},h_{2}\to 0}\int_{U_{1}}\left|\nabla^{\prime}\left(\frac{v_{h_{1}}-v_{h_{2}}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\leq 2\limsup_{h_{1},h_{2}\to 0}E_{h}\left(\frac{v_{h_{1}}-v_{h_{2}}}{2}\right)\leq 2(m_{0}-m_{h}).

Because
m_{h}\to m_{0}
, we conclude that
(\nabla^{\prime}v_{h})
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(U_{1};\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Furthermore,

\limsup_{h\to 0}\int_{U_{1}}|\partial_{N}v_{h}|^{2}\,dx\leq 2\limsup_{h\to 0}h^{2}E_{h}(v_{h})=0,

since
(E_{h}(v_{h}))
is bounded. Then, by Poincaré’s inequality for functions that are
0
on
\partial U^{\prime}\times(-1,1)
, we obtain that
(v_{n})
is Cauchy in
L^{2}(U_{1})
. Consequently, there exists
v\in H_{0}^{1}(U^{\prime})
such that
v_{h}\to\hat{v}
in
H^{1}(U_{1})
. In particular,
\hat{v}=1
weakly on
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}
. Finally, we have

m\leq\int_{U^{\prime}}|\nabla^{\prime}v|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\leq\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{U_{1}}|\nabla^{\prime}v_{h}|^{2}\,dx\leq\lim_{h\to 0}E_{h}(v_{h})=m_{0}.

∎

We end this section by stating two propositions that will prove useful later. If a function

cannot vanish on the boundary of that open set. Consequently, its values must be large somewhere. We now make this statement precise for cylindrical domains. Recall that

for

.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let

. There exist positive constants

,

4
Analysis close to the contact regions

In constructing the oscillating test functions, we will distinguish between isolated contact regions and clusters of contact regions. While our construction in the neighborhood of isolated contact regions is based on solving a “cell problem” given by a PDE, this approach breaks down near the clusters. In this section, however, we prove that the clusters can be enclosed in a region with respect to which they have small capacity. This allows us to construct functions with small gradients that vanish on the clusters. To this end, we adapt the methods from our previous paper
[
4
]
.

For the rest of the paper, we assume

a_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-1}{N-3}}\quad&\text{if }\delta_{\varepsilon}^{N-3}\ll\varepsilon^{N-1},\\

We now introduce some new notation. For

(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho})\in Y
with
y^{\prime}\neq\tilde{y}^{\prime}
.

Let
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{N-1},\mathcal{M}^{N-1})
be an m.p.p.​​ and define

as in (

). For

If
\varepsilon y^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon\tilde{y}^{\prime}
are the centers of two distinct contact regions in

, then (

B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\cap B^{\prime}(\varepsilon\tilde{y}^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,\tilde{y}^{\prime}))=\emptyset.

(4.3)

Given
\varepsilon>0
,
\omega\in\Omega
, our first objective is to carefully distinguish isolated contact regions in

from clusters.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)
with
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
. Then

is called
\varepsilon
-isolated if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1a)

,

(1b)

2a_{\varepsilon}\rho<\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})
if

,

(2)

If

with
\varepsilon\tilde{y}^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
and

, then
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon\tilde{y}^{\prime},2a_{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho})\cap B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))=\emptyset
.

Otherwise
(y^{\prime},\rho)
is called an
\varepsilon
-cluster point.

According to our definition, a point
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)
with
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
is considered isolated if
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))
separates the contact region
\varepsilon y^{\prime}+a_{\varepsilon}\rho T^{\prime}
from the other contact regions in
T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\omega)
. When
h_{0}=\infty
, we require furthermore that the size of the contact region is not much larger than the height
\delta_{\varepsilon}
.

We denote by
I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)
and
C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)
the set of
\varepsilon
-isolated and
\varepsilon
-cluster points, respectively. We also define

\displaystyle(T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}})^{C}(\omega):=\bigcup_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}(\varepsilon y^{\prime}+a_{\varepsilon}\rho T^{\prime})\cap U^{\prime},

\displaystyle S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega):=\bigcup_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},2a_{\varepsilon}\rho).

Here,
(T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}})^{C}(\omega)
is the family of clusters, and

is a layer that separates the clusters from isolated contact regions. Indeed, it is easy to verify that

B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\cap S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)=\emptyset\quad\text{for all }(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega).

(4.4)

For
h_{0}=\infty
we also introduce

\displaystyle\rho_{\max}:=\max\{\rho:(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\},

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega):=S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\times(-\max\{\delta_{\varepsilon},2a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\max}\},\max\{\delta_{\varepsilon},2a_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\max}\}).

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{N-1},\mathcal{M}^{N-1})
be a stationary m.p.p. with finite intensity such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\,d\lambda(\rho)<\infty.

Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\operatorname{Cap}\left((T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\infty})^{C}(\omega),S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\right)=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(4.5)

If
h_{0}<\infty
, then we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\operatorname{Cap}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\left((T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}})^{C}(\omega),S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\right)=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(4.6)

We also have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}|S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)|=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. for all }h_{0}\in[0,\infty].

(4.7)

We reduce the proof of Theorem
4.2
to the computation of limits of weighted sums of
J_{h_{0}}
and
\rho^{N-1}
. The result is given in the next proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{N-1},\mathcal{M}^{N-1})
be a stationary m.p.p. with finite intensity such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\,d\lambda(\rho)<\infty.

Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}a_{\varepsilon}^{N-1}\rho^{N-1}=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(4.8)

To keep the computations compact, we introduce the functions
\tilde{J}_{0}
,
\tilde{J}_{\infty}
and
\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}
for
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{0}

\displaystyle:(0,\infty)^{2}\rightarrow[0,\infty),

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{0}(\rho,l):=2\rho^{N-3}\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,l)),

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle:(0,\infty)^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty),

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}(\rho,l,h):=\rho^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,l)),

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\infty}

\displaystyle:(0,\infty)^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty),

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\infty}(\rho,l,h):=\rho^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},C(l,h)).

Although the definition of
\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}
does not depend on
h_{0}
for
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, the notation is convenient when expressing estimates uniformly in
h_{0}
. In the next lemma, we show that we can bound
\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}
from above by
J_{h_{0}}
for all
h_{0}\in[0,\infty]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a positive number
C
, depending only on
T^{\prime}
and

, such that for all
\rho>0
,

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{0}(\rho,l)

\displaystyle\leq CJ_{0}(\rho),

\displaystyle\text{for all }l\geq 2,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,l,\frac{h}{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{\frac{h_{0}}{h},\frac{h}{h_{0}}\right\}J_{h_{0}}(\rho),

\displaystyle\text{for all }l\geq 2,\,h>0,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\infty}(\rho,l,h)

\displaystyle\leq CJ_{\infty}(\rho),

\displaystyle\text{for all }l,h\geq 2.

(4.11)

Proof.

If
h_{0}=\infty
and

, then

\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},C(l,h))\leq\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},C(2,2))\lesssim\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Hence, (
4.11
) follows immediately. If
h_{0}=0
, then

\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,l))\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,2))\lesssim\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1}),

from which we deduce (
4.9
). Assume
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
l\geq 2
,
h>0
. By Proposition
2.8

\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,l))\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,2))\leq\max\left\{\frac{h_{0}}{h},\frac{h}{h_{0}}\right\}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,2))

We proceed to show that

\sup_{\rho>0}\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,2))}{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}<\infty.

Since
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
is only possible for
N>3
and
\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N})>0
, Remark

implies that the quotient is finite for any

. By Proposition

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,2))}{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}=\frac{\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},B^{\prime}(0,2)\times\mathbb{R})}{\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.8

\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,2))}{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}=\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,2))}{2\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}\frac{2\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\to\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,2))}{\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}

as
\rho\to\infty
. As the limits for
\rho\to 0
and
\rho\to\infty
exist, and the capacity function is continuous for

by Proposition

, we conclude that the quotient is globally bounded.
∎

We can now prove our main result.

.

. By the subadditivity and the scaling property of the capacity, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}\big((T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\infty})^{C}(\omega),S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)}\operatorname{Cap}\left(\varepsilon y+a_{\varepsilon}\rho(T^{\prime}\times\{0\}),S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)}\operatorname{Cap}\left(\varepsilon y+a_{\varepsilon}\rho(T^{\prime}\times\{0\}),B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},2a_{\varepsilon}\rho)\times(-2a_{\varepsilon}\rho,2a_{\varepsilon}\rho)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)}a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\tilde{J}_{\infty}(\rho,2,2)\lesssim\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}\delta_{\varepsilon}J_{\infty}(\rho)

Then (
4.5
) follows from the first limit in Proposition
4.3
by dividing by
\delta_{\varepsilon}
and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
. Next, let

\operatorname{Cap}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\left((T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}})^{C}(\omega),S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\right)\\
\leq\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,2)).

On the other hand, if

. Therefore, for all

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\operatorname{Cap}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\left((T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}})^{C}(\omega),S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\right)\lesssim\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho).

Hence, (

.
∎

In order to prove Proposition

separately from the rest.
We define

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon,\infty}^{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\left\{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega):2a_{\varepsilon}\rho\geq\min\{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}),\delta_{\varepsilon}\}\right\},

(4.12)

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\left\{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega):2a_{\varepsilon}\rho\geq\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})\right\}\quad\text{if }h_{0}<\infty,

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\setminus C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{1}(\omega)\quad\text{for all }h_{0}\in[0,\infty]

for all
\varepsilon>0
and
\omega\in\Omega
. As we will see in the proof of Lemma

Lemma 4.6

If

(4.13)

Lemma 4.7

Let

\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\,d\lambda(\rho)<\infty.

Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{1}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{1}(\omega)}a_{\varepsilon}^{N-1}\rho^{N-1}=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Let
\varepsilon>0

\varepsilon|y^{\prime}-\tilde{y}^{\prime}|<4a_{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho}
. Thus,
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\subset B^{\prime}(\varepsilon\tilde{y}^{\prime},6a_{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho})
.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{N-1},\mathcal{M}^{N-1})
be a stationary m.p.p. with finite intensity such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\,d\lambda(\rho)<\infty.

Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{2}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Proof.

We prove

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{2}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

for
\sigma<1
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\omega\in\Omega
. As
\rho\leq 1/\sigma
for all
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)
, we have

\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{2}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\leq\varepsilon^{N-1}\|J_{h_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1/\sigma)}\operatorname{card}\left(C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{2}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)\right).

(4.18)

Fixing
\sigma
and
\omega
for the moment, let us define

A(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho}):=\left\{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{2}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega):B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\subset B^{\prime}(\varepsilon\tilde{y}^{\prime},6a_{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho})\right\}.

It follows from Lemma
4.8
that

\operatorname{card}\left(C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{2}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)\right)\leq\sum_{(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho})\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{1}(\omega)}\operatorname{card}(A(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho})).

(4.19)

To estimate
\operatorname{card}(A(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho}))
, we sum the volumes of the balls
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))
for
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in A(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho})
. Since they are disjoint and their union is contained in
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon\tilde{y}^{\prime},6a_{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho})
, we get

\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in A(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho})}\varepsilon^{N-1}r(\omega,y^{\prime})^{N-1}\leq(6a_{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho})^{N-1}.

As
r(\omega,y^{\prime})\geq\sigma
for
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)
and
A(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho})\subset M(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)
, we conclude that

\operatorname{card}(A(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho}))\leq\left(\frac{6a_{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho}}{\varepsilon\sigma}\right)^{N-1}.

Combining this with (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) yields

\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{2}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\leq\|J_{h_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1/\sigma)}\left(\frac{6}{\sigma}\right)^{N-1}\sum_{(\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{\rho})\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{1}(\omega)}a_{\varepsilon}^{N-1}\tilde{\rho}^{N-1}.

Thus, Lemma
4.7
and the local boundedness of
J_{h_{0}}
implies

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}^{2}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

With the help of Lemma
4.5
, we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

The first limit in (
4.8
) is zero by Lemmas
4.7
and
4.9
. The second limit follows from the first exactly as in the proof of Lemma
4.7
.
∎

5
The oscillating test functions

In this section, we construct the oscillating test functions
(w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot))
that satisfy Theorem
3.3
, and we prove their properties. The construction is based on the solutions to certain cell problems.

5.1
The cell problem

For
h_{0}\in[0,\infty]
and
l,h>0
, we define a family of functions
\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}\in H^{1}(C(l,h))
as solutions of boundary value problems. Let
\eta_{l,h}^{\infty}
be the unique weak solution of

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta\eta_{l,h}^{\infty}&=0\quad\text{in }C(l,h),\\
\eta_{l,h}^{\infty}&=0\quad\text{on }\partial C(l,h),\\
\eta_{l,h}^{\infty}&=1\quad\text{on }T^{\prime}\times\{0\}.\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(5.1)

For
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, let
\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}
be the unique weak solution of

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}&=0\quad\text{in }C(l,h),\\
\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}&=0\quad\text{on }\partial B^{\prime}(0,l)\times(-h,h),\\
\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}&=1\quad\text{on }T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\\
\nabla\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}\cdot\nu&=0\quad\text{on }(B^{\prime}(0,l)\times\{-h,h\}).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(5.2)

Finally, for
h_{0}=0
and
(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in C(l,h)
, we define
\eta_{l,h}^{0}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=\eta_{l}^{0}(x^{\prime})
, where
\eta_{l}^{0}
is the unique weak solution of

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta\eta_{l}^{0}&=0\quad\text{in }B^{\prime}(0,l)\setminus T^{\prime},\\
\eta_{l}^{0}&=1\quad\text{on }\partial B^{\prime}(0,l),\\
\eta_{l}^{0}&=0\quad\text{on }T^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(5.3)

It is easy to verify that the solutions of the boundary value problems are capacitary potentials:

\displaystyle\int_{C(l,h)}|\nabla\eta_{l,h}^{\infty}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},C(l,h)),

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{C(l,h)}|\nabla\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,l))\quad\text{for }h_{0}\in(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l)}|\nabla\eta_{l}^{0}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,l)).

Furthermore, due to symmetry, we have that

\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l)\times(0,h)}|\nabla\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}|^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{C(l,h)}|\nabla\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}|^{2}\,dx\quad\text{for all }h_{0}\in[0,\infty].

(5.5)

Finally, we note that
0\leq\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}\leq 1
a.e. in
C(l,h)
by the weak maximum principle.

5.2
Construction of the random oscillating test functions

For the remainder of this section, we fix a stationary m.p.p.
M:(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow(\mathbb{M}^{N-1},\mathcal{M}^{N-1})
with finite intensity such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\,d\lambda(\rho)<\infty.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\omega\in\Omega
be fixed. Given
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)
, we define
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)
for
x\in B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
by

w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x):=1-\eta_{l,h}^{h_{0}}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon y}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}\right),\quad l:=\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\,h:=\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}.

(5.6)

This defines
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)
uniquely for

x\in\bigcup_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon}),

since it is a disjoint union. We note that

w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)=1\quad\text{for }x\in\bigcup_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}\partial B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon}).

(5.7)

Next, we define
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)
for
x\in S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
. First, we consider the case
h_{0}=\infty
. Choose
\zeta_{\varepsilon,\infty}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega);[0,1])
such that
(T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\infty})^{C}(\omega)\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,\infty}=1\})
and

\int_{S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)}|\zeta_{\varepsilon,\infty}|^{2}\,dx\leq 2\operatorname{Cap}\left((T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\infty})^{C}(\omega)\times\{0\},S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\right).

Set
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x):=1-\zeta_{\varepsilon,\infty}(x)
for
x\in S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})\subset S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)
. By (
4.4
), the previous definition of
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot)
does not interfere with the one in
S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)
. We also have

\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{2}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\operatorname{Cap}\left((T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\infty})^{C}(\omega)\times\{0\},S_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\right).

(5.8)

For
h_{0}<\infty
we similarly choose
\zeta_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}\in C_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}(S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega);[0,1])
such that
(T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}})^{C}(\omega)\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{\zeta_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}=1\})
and

\int_{S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}|^{2}\,dx\leq 2\operatorname{Cap}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\left((T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}})^{C}(\omega),S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\right).

We set
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x):=1-\zeta_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(x)
for
x\in S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
. Then

\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{2}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\operatorname{Cap}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\left((T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}})^{C}(\omega),S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\right).

(5.9)

This completes the definition in
S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
. Observe that

w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)=1\quad\text{for }x\in\partial S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{for all }h_{0}\in[0,\infty].

(5.10)

Finally, we extend
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot)
by 1 to
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
. It is easy to verify that
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot)
is weakly differentiable in
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
and it belongs to
H^{1}(U_{\varepsilon}^{+})
with
0\leq w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot)\leq 1
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
. Furthermore, it vanishes weakly on
T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\omega)
.

In the remainder of this section, we prove that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,
(w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot))
is
\omega
-admissible. To this end, we first compute the limit of the scaled
L^{2}
-norm of the gradients locally. Let
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)
and set
l:=\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})/(a_{\varepsilon}\rho)
,
h:=h_{\varepsilon}/\rho
. Applying a change of variables to (
5.6
) and using (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) gives

\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},C(l,h))\quad\text{if }h_{0}=\infty,\\
\frac{1}{2}a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,l))\quad\text{if }h_{0}\in(0,\infty),\\
a_{\varepsilon}^{N-3}\delta_{\varepsilon}\rho^{N-3}\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,l))\quad\text{if }h_{0}=0.\end{cases}

Dividing by
\delta_{\varepsilon}
and rewriting the right-hand side in terms of the function
\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}
yields

\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}(\rho,l,h)\quad\text{if }h_{0}\in(0,\infty],\\
\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{0}(\rho,l)\quad\text{if }h_{0}=0.\end{cases}

(5.11)

In the next lemma, we sum the right-hand side in (
5.11
) over certain subsets of
I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)
and evaluate its limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The computation is an application of the ergodic theorem.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
O^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be a Borel measurable set such that
|\partial O^{\prime}|=0
. Then,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{0}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}\right)=\gamma_{0}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.12)

If
h_{0}\in(0,\infty]
, then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)=\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.13)

Proof.

First we prove that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.14)

We observe that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in C_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho).

Applying Theorem
2.7
with
g(\rho)=J_{h_{0}}(\rho)
gives

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\,d\xi(\omega,\rho)=2\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.15)

Hence, (
5.14
) follows from (
5.15
) and Proposition
4.3
. Next we use (
5.14
) to obtain lower and upper bounds for the limits in (
5.12
) and (
5.13
). We note that

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}J_{\infty}(\rho)\quad&\text{if }h_{0}=\infty,\\
\min\left\{\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{h_{0}},\frac{h_{0}}{h_{\varepsilon}}\right\}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\quad&\text{if }h_{0}\in(0,\infty),\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{0}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\geq J_{0}(\rho).

The inequalities above follow from the monotonicity of the capacity with respect to set inclusion and, for
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, from Proposition
2.8
. If
h_{0}\in(0,\infty]
, then from (
5.14
) we get the lower bound

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)\\
\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.16)

Similarly, we obtain
\gamma_{0}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|
as a lower bound
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for the limit in (
5.12
).

To prove an upper bound, we fix
h>0
,
l\geq 2
. We assume
h_{0}=\infty
. If
\sigma
is fixed and
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, then every
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)
satisfies

\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}\geq\frac{\varepsilon\sigma^{2}}{a_{\varepsilon}}>l,\quad\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\geq h_{\varepsilon}\sigma>h.

(5.17)

Therefore, we deduce

\tilde{J}_{\infty}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)\leq\tilde{J}_{\infty}(\rho,l,h)\quad\text{for all }(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega).

(5.18)

On the other hand, by (1a) in Definition
4.1
, every
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)
satisfies

\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}\geq 2,\quad\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\geq 2.

Consequently,

\tilde{J}_{\infty}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)\leq\tilde{J}_{\infty}(\rho,2,2)\quad\text{for all }(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega).

(5.19)

Then (
5.18
) and (
5.19
) give us the following upper bound for small enough
\varepsilon
:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\infty}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)

(5.20)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{\infty}(\rho,l,h)+\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\cap M_{\sigma}(\omega)}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{\infty}(\rho,2,2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},C(l,h))}{\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N})}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{\infty}(\rho)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M_{\sigma}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{\infty}(\rho,2,2)

By Lemma
4.5
, we have

\limsup_{\sigma\to 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M_{\sigma}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{\infty}(\rho,2,2)\lesssim\limsup_{\sigma\to 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M_{\sigma}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{\infty}(\rho)=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

so that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{\infty}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)\\
\leq\frac{\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},C(l,h))}{\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N})}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{\infty}(\rho)\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.21)

We can let
h,l\to\infty
and use (
5.15
) to obtain

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{\infty}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)\leq 2\gamma_{\infty}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Together with (
5.16
), this proves the lemma for
h_{0}=\infty
. The case
h_{0}=0
is very similar so its proof is omitted.

Now assume
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and fix
l\geq 2
. If
\sigma
is fixed and
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, then, similar to (
5.18
) and (
5.19
), we have the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)\leq\max\left\{\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{h_{0}},\frac{h_{0}}{h_{\varepsilon}}\right\}\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,l,\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}\right)\quad\text{for all }(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega),

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)\leq\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,2,\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}\right)\quad\text{for all }(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega).

Then, by splitting the sum in (
5.13
) as in (
5.20
) and using Proposition
4.3
, we obtain

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\right)\\
\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}q_{h_{0}}(\rho,l)J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

(5.22)

where

q_{h_{0}}(\rho,l):=\frac{\tilde{J}_{h_{0}}\left(\rho,l,\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}\right)}{J_{h_{0}}(\rho)}=\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},B^{\prime}(0,l))}{\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}.

We know by Lemma
4.4
that
q_{h_{0}}
is bounded in
(0,\infty)\times[2,\infty)
. Thus, we can apply Theorem
2.7
to the right-hand side of (
5.22
) to get

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}q_{h_{0}}(\rho,l)J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\int_{0}^{\infty}q_{h_{0}}(\rho,l)J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\,d\xi(\omega,\rho)\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Since

as

, the dominated convergence theorem yields

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}q_{h_{0}}(\rho,l)J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\,d\xi(\omega,\rho)=\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap O^{\prime}|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Finally, (

), (

) and the limit above combine to give the result for

.
∎

Corollary 5.2

Let

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\

Proof.

The claim follows from (
5.11
) and Lemma

.
∎

We proceed to compute the limit of the scaled

Proposition 5.3

For

Proof.

We start by showing that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{Q^{\prime}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx=\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap Q^{\prime}|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.25)

for any closed cube
Q^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\omega\in\Omega

\int_{Q^{\prime}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{(Q^{\prime}\cap S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx\\
+\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}\int_{(Q^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx.

5.8

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{(Q^{\prime}\cap S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Q^{\prime}_{b}

Q^{\prime}

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}_{a}\end{subarray}}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx\\

\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}_{b}\end{subarray}}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx.

\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|U^{\prime}\cap Q^{\prime}_{a}|\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx\\

Since
|U^{\prime}\cap Q^{\prime}_{a}|

Let

. In order to prove (

by cubes. Let
(Q^{\prime}_{1})^{k},\dots,(Q^{\prime}_{m_{k}})^{k}

(Q^{\prime}_{j})^{k}\subset U^{\prime}

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\left|\int_{U^{\prime}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\varphi(x^{\prime})\,dx-\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}\int_{(Q^{\prime}_{j})^{k}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\varphi(x^{\prime})\,dx\right|=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.27)

The difference is clearly bounded by

\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}({\overline{U^{\prime}}})}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k}}\int_{(P^{\prime}_{j})^{k}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx.

Then, (
5.25

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\left|\int_{U^{\prime}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\varphi(x^{\prime})\,dx-\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}\int_{(Q^{\prime}_{j})^{k}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\varphi(x^{\prime})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}(\overline{U^{\prime}})}\left|U^{\prime}\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^{n_{k}}(P^{\prime}_{j})^{k}\right|=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.,}

since

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}\int_{(Q^{\prime}_{j})^{k}\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}|\varphi(x^{\prime})-(\varphi)_{(Q^{\prime}_{j})^{k}}|\,dx=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

where

Finally, it is clear that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}|U^{\prime}\cap(Q^{\prime}_{j})^{k}|(\varphi)_{(Q^{\prime}_{j})^{k}}=\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)\int_{U^{\prime}}\varphi\,dx^{\prime}.

This concludes the proof.

Recall from Definition

-admissible sequence.

Theorem 5.4
.

For
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,
(w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot))
is
\omega
-admissible and
\hat{w}_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot)\rightharpoonup 1

Proof.

The function
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot)
vanishes weakly on

(w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot))
is
\omega
-admissible
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. by (
5.24
) in Proposition
5.3

\hat{w}_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot)\to 1

\int_{U^{+}}|\hat{w}_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)-1|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{S^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\times(0,1)}|\hat{w}_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)-1|^{2}\,dx\\
+\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,1)}|\hat{w}_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)-1|^{2}\,dx

5.3
A variational property of the oscillating test functions

In this section, we establish a special case of Theorem
3.3
. As we will see later, the special case is an important step in the proof of the theorem itself. From now on, we assume that if
N=3
, then
\ln(1/\delta_{\varepsilon})\ll 1/\varepsilon^{2}
. The main result is stated in the next proposition.

Proposition 5.5
.

For
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
, the sequence
(w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot))
satisfies the following property: Given an
\omega
-admissible sequence
(v_{\varepsilon})
, if there exists a subsequence
(\varepsilon_{n})
tending to
0
such that
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon_{n}}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
as
n\to\infty
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\omega,x)\nabla v_{\varepsilon_{n}}(x)\,dx=0.

(5.28)

The proof of Proposition
5.5
is based on a minimization property of
(w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot))
. We shall compare the oscillating test functions with a subclass of
\omega
-admissible sequences.

Definition 5.6
.

For
\omega\in\Omega
, let
\mathcal{O}(\omega)
be the set of all
\omega
-admissible sequences
(v_{\varepsilon})
such that
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 1
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proposition 5.7
.

For
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and for all
(v_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
, we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

(5.29)

Proof of Proposition
5.5
.

Fix an
\omega\in\Omega
for which
(w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot))\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
and Proposition
5.7
holds. As
\omega
is fixed, we omit it from the notation. Assume
(v_{\varepsilon})
is an
\omega
-admissible sequence such that
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
. Clearly,
(w_{\varepsilon}+tv_{\varepsilon})
lies in
\mathcal{O}(\omega)
for all
t>0
. Hence, Proposition
5.7
implies

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}(|\nabla(w_{\varepsilon}+tv_{\varepsilon})|^{2}-|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\,dx\geq 0.

(5.30)

Since

\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2t}\left(|\nabla(w_{\varepsilon}+tv_{\varepsilon})|^{2}-|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-t^{2}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right),

by (
5.30
) we get

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\,dx\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2t}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}(|\nabla(w_{\varepsilon}+tv_{\varepsilon})|^{2}-|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\,dx-\frac{Ct}{2}\geq-\frac{Ct}{2},

where
\sup_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(U_{\varepsilon}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leq C\delta_{\varepsilon}
. Letting
t\to 0
yields

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\,dx\geq 0.

Replacing
v_{\varepsilon}
by
-v_{\varepsilon}
provides also the reverse inequality.

Given a sequence
(v_{\varepsilon})
for which
(\hat{v}_{\varepsilon})
converges weakly to
0
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
only along a subsequence
(\varepsilon_{n})
, we define

\vartheta_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}v_{\varepsilon_{n}}\quad&\text{if }\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{n}\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N},\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})
is
\omega
-admissible and
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Hence, we can apply the argument given above to conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla v_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dx=0.

∎

Before we give the proof of Proposition
5.7
, we argue heuristically to see why the result holds. Fix
\omega\in\Omega
and
(v_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
. Assume
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)
and
B(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\subset U^{\prime}
. Since
\delta_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|1-v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\to 0
, we expect that
1-v_{\varepsilon}\approx 0
in
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
. At the same time,
1-v_{\varepsilon}=1
weakly on the contact region
\varepsilon y+a_{\varepsilon}\rho(T^{\prime}\times\{0\})
. Among all
H^{1}
functions that are close to
0
in
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})
and equal
1
on the contact region, we expect the capacitary potential of
\varepsilon y+a_{\varepsilon}\rho(T^{\prime}\times\{0\})
in
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})
to have the smallest Dirichlet energy. As
w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot)
is defined to be the capacitary potential near isolated contact regions, we expect

\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\gtrsim\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx\approx\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}\delta_{\varepsilon}J_{h_{0}}(\rho),

where the last approximation follows from (
5.11
).

To make this idea rigorous, we single out the points where the desired inequality holds and prove that, asymptotically, they constitute the majority of the isolated points. Our main tools will be Propositions
2.14
and
2.15
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\sigma,\theta\in(0,1)
. Let
O^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset U^{\prime}
be open. For a fixed sequence
(v_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
, we denote by
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})
the set of points
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)
such that
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\subset O^{\prime}
and

\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\geq\frac{\theta}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}\delta_{\varepsilon}J_{h_{0}}(\rho).

(5.31)

In contrast,
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})
denotes the set of points
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)
such that
B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\subset O^{\prime}
and (
5.31
) fails.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
and
(v_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
be fixed. Let
\sigma,\theta\in(0,1)
and let
O^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset U^{\prime}
be open. There exist positive constants
c
and
\varepsilon_{0}
such that if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, then we have

\fint_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,1)}1-\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\,dx>c

(5.32)

for all
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We extend
v_{\varepsilon}
to
U^{\prime}\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})
by reflection, that is, we set
v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},-x_{N})
for
(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in U^{\prime}\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},0)
. Then
v_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(U^{\prime}\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon}))
. If
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})
, we have

\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx<\theta\varepsilon^{N-1}\delta_{\varepsilon}J_{h_{0}}(\rho).

(5.33)

An easy computation shows that

\varepsilon^{N-1}\delta_{\varepsilon}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=\begin{cases}a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad&\text{if }h_{0}=\infty,\\
a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\quad&\text{if }h_{0}\in(0,\infty),\\
2a_{\varepsilon}^{N-3}\delta_{\varepsilon}\rho^{N-3}\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\quad&\text{if }h_{0}=0.\end{cases}

(5.34)

Let us now assume
N=3
. In this case
h_{0}=\infty
. Then (
5.33
) and (
5.34
) give

\frac{1}{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx<\theta\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N}).

By applying a change of variables in Proposition
2.14
, we obtain positive constants
L
,
H
,
\tau
and
c
, depending on
\theta
, such that if

\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}\geq L,\quad\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\geq H,\quad\max\left\{\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}\right)\frac{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\frac{a_{\varepsilon}^{2}\rho^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}r(\omega,y^{\prime})^{2}}\right\}\leq\tau,

(5.35)

then

c<\fint_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})}1-v_{\varepsilon}\,dx=\fint_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,1)}1-\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

(5.36)

We show that there exists
\varepsilon_{0}
such that the conditions in (
5.35
) are satisfied for all
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})
if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. We write the first two conditions as

\frac{\varepsilon}{a_{\varepsilon}}\geq\frac{L\rho}{r(\omega,y^{\prime})},\quad h_{\varepsilon}\geq H\rho.

(5.37)

Since
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in M(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)
, we know that
r(\omega,y^{\prime})\geq\sigma
and
\rho\leq 1/\sigma
. Hence, the following conditions imply (
5.37
) and are independent of
(y^{\prime},\rho)
:

\frac{\varepsilon}{a_{\varepsilon}}\geq\frac{L}{\sigma^{2}},\quad h_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{H}{\sigma}.

(5.38)

Next, we rewrite the third condition in (
5.35
) as

\left(\ln\left(\frac{r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{\varepsilon\rho}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right)\varepsilon^{2}\rho\leq\tau,\quad\frac{\rho}{r(\omega,y^{\prime})}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq\tau.

(5.39)

We know that
x\ln(1/x)
is increasing for
x\leq 1/e
. Hence, using
r(\omega,y^{\prime})\leq 1
and
\rho\leq 1/\sigma
, we get

\varepsilon^{2}\rho\ln\left(\frac{r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{\varepsilon\rho}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\rho\ln\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right)

for
\varepsilon\leq\sigma/e
. Thus

\tau\geq\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right)

(5.40)

implies the first inequality in (
5.39
) for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
and is independent of
(y^{\prime},\rho)
. On the other hand,

\tau\geq\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{\sigma^{3}}

(5.41)

implies the second inequality in (
5.39
) and is independent of
(y^{\prime},\rho)
. To conclude the proof for
N=3
, we need to show that (
5.38
), (
5.40
) and (
5.41
) are all satisfied for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. However, this follows easily from the following limits:

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\delta_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\varepsilon}{a_{\varepsilon}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}h_{\varepsilon}=\infty.

The first limit holds by assumption, and the last limit holds because
h_{0}=\infty
.

Let
N>3
. We prove that for any
\tilde{\theta}\in(\theta,1)
and for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, we have

\frac{1}{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq\tilde{\theta}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})

(5.42)

for all
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})
. Given the claim, we can use Proposition
2.15
to obtain
L
,
c
and
\tau
, depending on
\tilde{\theta}
, such that if

\frac{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}\geq L,\quad\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\left(\frac{a_{\varepsilon}\rho}{\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime})}\right)^{N-1}\leq\tau,

(5.43)

then

c<\fint_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})}1-v_{\varepsilon}\,dx=\fint_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,1)}1-\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

We can establish, as in the case
N=3
, that there exists
\varepsilon_{0}
such that if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, then the conditions of (
5.43
) are satisfied for all
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})
. We do not carry out the computations here and turn to the proof of (
5.42
) instead.

Let
\tilde{\theta}\in(\theta,1)
be arbitrary. Assume
h_{0}=\infty
. By Proposition
2.12
, we have that
\theta\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N})\leq\tilde{\theta}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
for sufficiently large
h
. Hence, (
5.33
) and (
5.34
) give

\frac{1}{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq\theta\operatorname{Cap}(T^{\prime}\times\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{N})\leq\tilde{\theta}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1}).

for small enough
\varepsilon
. Next, assume
h_{0}=0
. Since
\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})/(2h)\to\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
as
h\to 0
by definition, we know that
\theta\,2h\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\leq\tilde{\theta}\operatorname{Cap}_{h}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
for sufficiently small
h
. Therefore, by (
5.33
) and (
5.34
),

\frac{1}{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq\theta\frac{2h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\leq\tilde{\theta}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})

for small enough
\varepsilon
. Finally, let
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
. Pick
\varepsilon
small enough such that
\theta\max\{h_{\varepsilon}/h_{0},h_{0}/h_{\varepsilon}\}\leq\tilde{\theta}
. Then, Proposition
2.8
, together with (
5.33
) and (
5.34

\frac{1}{a_{\varepsilon}^{N-2}\rho^{N-2}}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(-\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx<\theta\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{0}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\leq\tilde{\theta}\operatorname{Cap}_{\frac{h_{\varepsilon}}{\rho}}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1}).

This concludes the proof of the inequality (
5.42
).
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
and
(v_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
be fixed. Let
\sigma,\theta\in(0,1)
and let
O^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset U^{\prime}
be open. Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{N-1}\operatorname{card}\left(\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})\right)=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.9
, there exist positive constants
c
and
\varepsilon_{0}
, such that

\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,1)}|1-\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\geq c\varepsilon^{N-1}r(\omega,y^{\prime})^{N-1}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{N-1}

for all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})
. The first inequality follows from (
5.32
) via Hölder’s inequality, and the second one follows from the fact that
r(\omega,y^{\prime})\geq\sigma
for all
(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})
. Since
(v_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
, we immediately obtain

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{N-1}\operatorname{card}\left(\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})\right)\\
\lesssim\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,1)}|1-\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{U^{+}}|1-\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx=0.

∎

We now give a proof of Proposition
5.7
using a thinning argument.

Proof of Proposition
5.7
.

Fix
\omega\in\Omega
and
(v_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
. Let
\sigma,\theta\in(0,1)
and let
O^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset U^{\prime}
be open with
|\partial O^{\prime}|=0
. Then

\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\\
\geq\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B^{\prime}(\varepsilon y^{\prime},\varepsilon r(\omega,y^{\prime}))\times(0,\delta_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\geq\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})}\frac{\theta}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho).

(5.44)

We have

\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})}\frac{\theta}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)-\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})}\frac{\theta}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)

(5.45)

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. Since
\rho\leq 1/\sigma
and
J_{h_{0}}
is locally bounded, Corollary
5.10
implies

\sum_{(y^{\prime},\rho)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})}\frac{\theta}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\lesssim\varepsilon^{N-1}\operatorname{card}\left(\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\sigma,\theta,O^{\prime})\right)\to 0.

(5.46)

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
4.5
that

\limsup_{\sigma\to 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Bigg|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)\Bigg|=0\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.47)

Hence, (
5.14
) gives

\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y^{\prime},\rho)\in I_{\varepsilon,h_{0}}(\omega)\setminus M_{\sigma}(\omega)\\
\varepsilon y^{\prime}\in O^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta}{2}\varepsilon^{N-1}J_{h_{0}}(\rho)=\theta\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|O^{\prime}|\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(5.48)

If
\omega
satisfies (
5.47
) and (
5.48
), then by (
5.44
), (
5.45
) and (
5.46
) we obtain

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\geq\theta\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|O^{\prime}|.

Since
\theta
and

are arbitrary, we have just proved that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and for all
(v_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
, we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\geq\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)|U^{\prime}|=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}\,dx,

where the last equality follows from Proposition
5.3
.
∎

6
Proof of Theorem
3.3

In this section, we conclude the proof of Theorem
3.3
by showing that the oscillating test functions
(w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot))
satisfy (
3.7
) for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
. Our proof is based on an idea introduced by Casado-Díaz in
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
. We need a generalization of Proposition
5.3
to bounded test functions in
H^{1}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and all
v\in H^{1}(U^{\prime})\cap L^{\infty}(U^{\prime})
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)|^{2}v(x^{\prime})\,dx=\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)\int_{U^{\prime}}v(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}.

Proof.

Fix some
\omega\in\Omega
that satisfies Propositions
5.3
,
5.5
and Theorem
5.4
. For simplicity, we omit
\omega
from the notation. Let
v\in H^{1}(U^{\prime})\cap L^{\infty}(U^{\prime})
, and approximate it by a sequence
(\varphi_{n})\subset C^{0}(\overline{U^{\prime}})
in the
H^{1}
-norm. Furthermore, we can take
(\varphi_{n})
uniformly bounded in
C^{0}(\overline{U^{\prime}})
. Then Proposition
5.3
implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\varphi_{n}\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{h_{0}}\int_{U^{\prime}}\varphi_{n}\,dx^{\prime}=\gamma_{h_{0}}\int_{U^{\prime}}v\,dx^{\prime}.

Hence, to conclude, we need to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}^{+}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|v-\varphi_{n}|\,dx=0.

This is implied by the assertion that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}|v-\varphi_{n}|\,dx=0

(6.1)

for all subsequences
(\varepsilon_{n})
converging to
0
. Given a subsequence
(\varepsilon_{n})
, we define
v_{n}:=w_{\varepsilon_{n}}|v-\varphi_{n}|
. Then
(v_{n})
is
\omega
-admissible and
\hat{v}_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
. Thus, Proposition
5.5
implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla v_{n}\,dx=0.

Computing the gradient of
v_{n}
gives

0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}|v-\varphi_{n}|+(\nabla^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla^{\prime}|v-\varphi_{n}|)w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dx.

(6.2)

Since
(w_{\varepsilon})
is
\omega
-admissible and
|w_{\varepsilon}|\leq 1
a.e. in
U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}
, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}(\nabla^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla^{\prime}|v-\varphi_{n}|)w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dx=0.

Therefore, equation (
6.2
) yields (
6.1
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

We fix some
\omega\in\Omega
for which Theorem
5.4
, Proposition
5.5
and Lemma
6.1
hold. Having fixed
\omega
, we omit it from the notation. Let
(v_{\varepsilon})
be an
\omega
-admissible sequence such that
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon_{n}}\rightharpoonup\hat{v}
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
as
n\to\infty
for some
\hat{v}
. Since
(v_{\varepsilon})
is
\omega
-admissible,
\partial_{N}\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in
L^{2}(U^{+})
so that
\partial_{N}\hat{v}=0
. Hence, there exists
v\in H^{1}(U^{\prime})
such that
\hat{v}(\cdot,x_{N})=v
for a.e.
x_{N}\in(0,1)
. We prove that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla v_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dx=\gamma_{h_{0}}(\omega)\int_{U^{\prime}}v\,dx^{\prime},

which is equivalent to (
3.7
).

In the first part of the proof, we assume additionally that
v\in L^{\infty}(U^{\prime})
. Set
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}:=v_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon}v
. We can check easily that
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
is
\omega
-admissible and
\hat{\vartheta}_{\varepsilon_{n}}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, Proposition
5.5
implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dx=0.

Computing the gradient of
\vartheta_{\varepsilon_{n}}
explicitly, we obtain

\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla v_{\varepsilon_{n}}-|\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}v-(\nabla^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla^{\prime}v)w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dx\to 0.

(6.3)

It follows from Theorem
5.4
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}(\nabla^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla^{\prime}v)w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dx=0.

Hence, with the help of Lemma
6.1
, we deduce from (
6.3
) that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla v_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dx=\gamma_{h_{0}}\int_{U^{\prime}}v\,dx^{\prime}.

Let us now consider the general case. We define
v_{\varepsilon}^{k}:=\min\{\max\{v_{\varepsilon},-k\},k\}
,
v^{k}:=\min\{\max\{v,-k\},k\}
and set
\hat{v}^{k}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=v^{k}(x^{\prime})
for
(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in U^{+}
. Then,
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{k}\rightharpoonup\hat{v}^{k}
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
. Therefore, by the first step we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla v_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{k}\,dx=\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{h_{0}}\int_{U^{\prime}}v^{k}\,dx^{\prime}=\gamma_{h_{0}}\int_{U^{\prime}}v\,dx^{\prime}.

Thus, all that remains to be proved is

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\nabla v_{\varepsilon_{n}}-\nabla v_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{k})\,dx=0.

To this end, we let
(\varepsilon_{n_{k}})
be a further subsequence. Then, it is easy to verify that
(v_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}-v_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}^{k})
is
\omega
-admissible and
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}-\hat{v}_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}^{k}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(U^{+})
as
k\to\infty
. Hence, as a result of Proposition
5.5
, we obtain

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}^{+}}\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}\nabla(v_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}-v_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}^{k})\,dx=0.

The claim now follows from the arbitrariness of the sequence
(\varepsilon_{n_{k}})
.
∎

7
Proofs of Propositions
2.14
and
2.15

An important role in the proof of Theorem
3.3
was played by Propositions
2.14
and
2.15
. The remainder of the paper is devoted to their proofs.

7.1
Auxiliary lemmas

Here, we collect some preliminary results. We begin with a lemma that provides sufficient conditions for the local averages of a sequence of zero-mean functions to converge to
0
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
(r_{n})
and
(\rho_{n})
be sequences of positive numbers with

\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n}=\infty,\quad\rho_{n}\leq r_{n}\text{ for all }n.

Assume
v_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{B(0,r_{n})})
has zero mean. Assume one of the following conditions hold:

(1)

N=1
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}\int_{-r_{n}}^{r_{n}}|v_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx=0
,

(2)

N=2
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\ln(r_{n})\int_{B(0,r_{n})}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx=0
,

(3)

N>2
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B(0,r_{n})}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx=0
.

Then
v_{n}\to 0
in
H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\fint_{B(0,\rho_{n})}v_{n}\,dx=0.

(7.1)

Remark 7.1.1
.

The conditions given in the hypotheses are sharp. For
N=1
, define

u_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{r_{n}}(x+r_{n})\chi_{(-r_{n},0)}(x)-\frac{1}{r_{n}}(x-r_{n})\chi_{[0,r_{n})}(x),\quad a_{n}:=\fint_{-r_{n}}^{r_{n}}u_{n}\,dx.

If
v_{n}:=u_{n}-a_{n}
, then
v_{n}
has zero mean and
r_{n}\int_{-r_{n}}^{r_{n}}|v_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx=1
. On the other hand,
v_{n}\to 1/2
in
H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\fint_{-r_{n}/2}^{r_{n}/2}v_{n}\,dx\to 1/4
. For
N>1
, counterexamples are provided by taking
u_{n}
as the solution of

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta u_{n}&=0\quad\text{in }B(0,r_{n})\setminus B(0,1),\\
u_{n}&=1\quad\text{on }\partial B(0,1),\\
u_{n}&=0\quad\text{on }\partial B(0,r_{n}).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

and setting
v_{n}:=u_{n}-a_{n}
, where
a_{n}:=\fint_{B(0,r_{n})}u_{n}\,dx
. The solutions
u_{n}
can be computed explicitly, and the reader can verify that
\ln(r_{n})\int_{B(0,r_{n})}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\gtrsim 1
for
N=2
and
\int_{B(0,r_{n})}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\gtrsim 1
for
N>2
. On the other hand, we have
v_{n}\to 1
in
H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We leave it to the reader to check that (
7.1
) also fails for some sequence
(\rho_{n})
.

Proof.

Step 1:
Let
N=1
. For
x<y
we have

|v_{n}(y)-v_{n}(x)|\leq\int_{x}^{y}|v_{n}^{\prime}|\,dt\leq|y-x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{x}^{y}|v_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left(r_{n}\int_{-r_{n}}^{r_{n}}|v_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus,
\sup_{x,y\in(-r_{n},r_{n})}|v_{n}(y)-v_{n}(x)|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
v_{n}
is continuous, there exists
x_{n}\in(-r_{n},r_{n})
such that
v_{n}(x_{n})=\fint_{-r_{n}}^{r_{n}}v_{n}\,dx=0
. Hence,
\|v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(-r_{n},r_{n})}\to 0
. The claim now follows easily.

Step 2:
Let
N=2
. Define the circular means

\bar{v}_{n}(r):=\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}v_{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1},\quad r\in(0,r_{n}].

Then

|\bar{v}_{n}^{\prime}(r)|^{2}=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\nabla v_{n}\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1},

where
\nu
is the outer unit normal of
B(0,r)
. Therefore,

\int_{0}^{r_{n}}r|\bar{v}_{n}^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\,dr\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{r_{n}}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}|\nabla v_{n}(x)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)\,dr=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(0,r_{n})}|\nabla v_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

(7.2)

Consequently, for
1<r<r_{n}
we have

|\bar{v}_{n}(r_{n})-\bar{v}_{n}(r)|\leq\int_{r}^{r_{n}}|\bar{v}_{n}^{\prime}(s)|\,ds\leq\left(\int_{r}^{r_{n}}\frac{1}{s}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{r}^{r_{n}}s|\bar{v}_{n}^{\prime}(s)|^{2}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left(\ln(r_{n})\int_{B(0,r_{n})}|\nabla v_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From the hypothesis, we have
\|\bar{v}_{n}(r_{n})-\bar{v}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(1,r_{n})}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Consequently, if
\bar{v}_{n}(r_{n})\to 0
, then
\|\bar{v}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(1,r_{n})}\to 0
as well. Since
\bar{v}_{n}
has zero mean, we have

\bar{v}_{n}(r_{n})=\bar{v}_{n}(r_{n})-\fint_{B(0,r_{n})}v_{n}\,dx=\frac{2}{r_{n}^{2}}\int_{0}^{r_{n}}r(\bar{v}_{n}(r_{n})-\bar{v}_{n}(r))\,dr=\frac{2}{r_{n}^{2}}\int_{0}^{r_{n}}\int_{r}^{r_{n}}r\bar{v}_{n}^{\prime}(s)\,ds\,dr.

By Fubini’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality we get

|\bar{v}_{n}(r_{n})|=\left|\frac{2}{r_{n}^{2}}\int_{0}^{r_{n}}\bar{v}_{n}^{\prime}(s)\int_{0}^{s}r\,dr\,ds\right|=\left|\frac{1}{r_{n}^{2}}\int_{0}^{r_{n}}s^{2}\bar{v}_{n}^{\prime}(s)\,ds\right|\leq\frac{1}{r_{n}^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{r_{n}}s^{3}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{r_{n}}s|\bar{v}_{n}^{\prime}(s)|^{2}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Hence, (
7.2
) implies

|\bar{v}_{n}(r_{n})|^{2}\lesssim\int_{B(0,r_{n})}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Next, we show that
v_{n}\to 0
in
H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Fix
r>0
. Set

a_{n}:=\fint_{B(0,r)\setminus B(0,1)}v_{n}\,dx,\quad b_{n}:=\fint_{B(0,r)}v_{n}\,dx.

By Hölder’s and Poincaré’s inequalities we have

|a_{n}-b_{n}|^{2}\leq\fint_{B(0,r)\setminus B(0,1)}|v_{n}-b_{n}|^{2}\,dx\lesssim\fint_{B(0,r)}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\to 0.

(7.3)

Since
\|\bar{v}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(1,r_{n})}\to 0
, we see that

|a_{n}|=\left|\frac{2}{r^{2}-1}\int_{1}^{r}s\bar{v}_{n}(s)\,ds\right|\leq\|\bar{v}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(1,r_{n})}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(7.4)

Thus (
7.3
) implies
b_{n}\to 0
. Applying Poincaré’s inequality again, we obtain

\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B(0,r))}\leq\|v_{n}-b_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B(0,r))}+|b_{n}||B(0,r)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B(0,r);\mathbb{R}^{N})}+|b_{n}|\to 0.

Finally, we prove that
\fint_{B(0,\rho_{n})}v_{n}\,dx\to 0
for any sequence
(\rho_{n})
with
\rho_{n}\to\infty
and
\rho_{n}\leq r_{n}
for all
n
.
We decompose the integral as follows:

\fint_{B(0,\rho_{n})}v_{n}\,dx=\frac{2}{\rho_{n}^{2}}\int_{1}^{\rho_{n}}s\bar{v}_{n}(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{|B(0,\rho_{n})|}\int_{B(0,1)}v_{n}\,dx

The first term on the right-hand side goes to
0
by a computation similar to (
7.4
). The second term goes to
0
due to the local
H^{1}
-convergence. This concludes the proof of the claim in
N=2
.

Step 3:
Let
N>2
. By the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality and the hypothesis,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{B(0,r_{n})}|v_{n}|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2N}}\leq C\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{B(0,r_{n})}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0.

Then Hölder’s inequality immediately yields the local
H^{1}
-convergence. On the other hand

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\fint_{B(0,\rho_{n})}v_{n}\,dx\right|\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\fint_{B(0,\rho_{n})}|v_{n}|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2N}}=0.

Therefore, the claim is proved.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
N>2
and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded set. Assume
(l_{n})
and
(h_{n})
are sequences of real numbers such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}l_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}=\infty,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{n}}{l_{n}^{N-1}}=0.

If
N=3
, we also assume
\ln(l_{n})/h_{n}\to 0
. Suppose
w_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{C(l_{n},h_{n})})
has zero mean and
K\subset\operatorname{int}(\{w_{n}=\alpha_{n}\})
. If
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha\neq 0
and there is a constant
\Lambda>0
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{C(l_{n},h_{n})}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}\,dx\leq\Lambda,

(7.5)

then

\operatorname{Cap}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N})\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\alpha^{2}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
h_{n}/l_{n}
converges to a limit in
[0,\infty]
. Otherwise, we can prove the statement for a subsequence. Set
C_{n}:=C(l_{n},h_{n})
.

Step 1:
Let
\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}/l_{n}
be finite and nonzero. Scaling the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality in the unit cylinder
C(1,1)
appropriately yields

\left(\int_{C_{n}}|w_{n}|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2N}}\leq C\left(\int_{C_{n}}\left(\frac{l_{n}}{h_{n}}\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}|\nabla^{\prime}w_{n}|^{2}+\left(\frac{h_{n}}{l_{n}}\right)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}|\partial_{N}w_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(7.6)

where
C
depends only on
N
. Since
h_{n}/l_{n}
converges to a finite, nonzero limit, the bound (
7.5
) implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{C_{n}}|w_{n}|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2N}}<\infty.

(7.7)

Passing to a subsequence (not relabeled), we know that there exist functions
w\in L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
w_{n}\rightharpoonup w
in
L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\nabla w_{n}\rightharpoonup g
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
. If
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w\nabla\varphi\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{n}\nabla\varphi\,dx=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\nabla w_{n}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi g\,dx.

Hence,
\nabla w=g
and we deduce that
w\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Next, we show that
w=\alpha
weakly on
K
. Let
W
be a bounded open neighborhood of
K
. Using (
7.5
), (
7.7
) and Hölder’s inequality, we see that
(w_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(W)
. Consequently, the Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem gives
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|w_{n}-w\|_{L^{2}(W)}=0
. On the other hand,
K\cap\overline{\operatorname{supp}(w_{n}-\alpha_{n})}=\emptyset
holds by assumption. Hence, by Lemma
2.10
,
w=\alpha
weakly on
K
. As a result,
w/\alpha
is an admissible test function in (
2.12
). Therefore,

\alpha^{2}\operatorname{Cap}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx.

It also follows from the weak lower semi-continuity of the
L^{2}
-norm and (
7.5
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{C_{n}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}\,dx\leq\Lambda.

We conclude that
\operatorname{Cap}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N})\leq\Lambda/\alpha^{2}
.

Step 2:
Next, we assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}/l_{n}=\infty
. In this case, the proof in Step 1 breaks down since the right-hand side of (
7.6
) does not necessarily remain bounded. However, we will show that

\fint_{C(l_{n},l_{n})}w_{n}\,dx\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

It is then enough to apply Step 1 to
w_{n}-\fint_{C(l_{n},l_{n})}w_{n}\,dx
in the cylinders
C(l_{n},l_{n})
.

As
h_{n}\gg l_{n}
, the domain
C_{n}
behaves like a
1
-dimensional set at large scales. Accordingly, we reduce the problem to the study of the growth of functions of a single variable. For
x_{N}\in(-h_{n},h_{n})
, define the
(N-1)
-dimensional cross-sectional average

\bar{w}_{n}(x_{N}):=\fint_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}w_{n}(x^{\prime},x_{N})\,dx^{\prime}.

Then,

\fint_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}\bar{w}_{n}\,dx_{N}=\fint_{C_{n}}w_{n}\,dx=0.

By Jensen’s inequality and (
7.5
), we also obtain

\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}|\bar{w}_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx_{N}=\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}\left|\fint_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}\partial_{N}w_{n}\,dx^{\prime}\right|^{2}\,dx_{N}\leq\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}\fint_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\partial_{N}w_{n}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\lesssim\frac{1}{l_{n}^{N-1}}

Thus, our hypothesis
h_{n}\ll l_{n}^{N-1}
implies that

h_{n}\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}|\bar{w}_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx_{N}\lesssim\frac{h_{n}}{l_{n}^{N-1}}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

With the help of Lemma
7.1
, we conclude that

\fint_{C(l_{n},l_{n})}w_{n}\,dx=\fint_{-l_{n}}^{l_{n}}\bar{w}_{n}\,dx\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Step 3:
To finish the proof, we finally assume
\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}/l_{n}=0
. Our approach will be similar to Step 2. We will prove that

\fint_{C(h_{n},h_{n})}w_{n}\,dx\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

The rest of the proof is analogous to Step 1. This time, we observe that
C_{n}
behaves like an
(N-1)
-dimensional set at large scales. Hence, we consider the functions defined by

\bar{w}_{n}(x^{\prime}):=\fint_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}w_{n}(x^{\prime},x_{N})\,dx_{N},\quad x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})

By (
7.5
)

\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\nabla^{\prime}\bar{w}_{n}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}=\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}\left|\fint_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}\nabla^{\prime}w_{n}\,dx_{N}\right|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\leq\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}\fint_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}|\nabla^{\prime}w_{n}|^{2}\,dx\lesssim\frac{1}{h_{n}}.

Hence
\|\nabla^{\prime}\bar{w}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B^{\prime}(0,l_{n});\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0
. If
N=3
, then by assumption

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\ln(l_{n})\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\nabla\bar{w}_{n}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ln(l_{n})}{h_{n}}=0.

Since
\bar{w}_{n}
has zero mean, we deduce from Lemma
7.1
that

\fint_{C(h_{n},h_{n})}w_{n}\,dx=\fint_{B^{\prime}(0,h_{n})}\bar{w}_{n}\,dx^{\prime}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

for all
N
.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
N>3
and let
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be a bounded set. Assume
(l_{n})
and
(h_{n})
are sequences of real numbers such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}l_{n}=\infty,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}=h_{\infty}\in[0,\infty).

Assume
\Lambda:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is a continuous function satisfying

\limsup_{h\to 0}\frac{\Lambda(h)}{2h}=:\Lambda_{0}<\infty.

Suppose
w_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{C(l_{n},h_{n})})
has zero mean and
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\{w_{n}=\alpha_{n}\})
. If
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha\neq 0
and

\int_{C(l_{n},h_{n})}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}\,dx\leq\Lambda(h_{n})\quad\text{for all }n,

(7.8)

then

\alpha^{2}\operatorname{Cap}_{h_{\infty}}(K^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\leq\begin{cases}\Lambda_{0}\quad&\text{if }h_{\infty}=0,\\
\Lambda(h_{\infty})\quad&\text{if }h_{\infty}\in(0,\infty).\end{cases}

Proof.

As in the previous proof, we set
C_{n}:=C(l_{n},h_{n})
.

Step 1:
Assume
h_{\infty}\in(0,\infty)
. Set

\bar{w}_{n}(x_{N}):=\fint_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}w_{n}(x^{\prime},x_{N})\,dx^{\prime},\quad x_{N}\in(-h_{n},h_{n}).

Applying the Sobolev-Poincaré inequality along
(N-1)
-dimensional cross-sections and integrating in
x_{N}
, we obtain

\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}\left(\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|w_{n}(x)-\bar{w}_{n}(x_{N})|^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}\,dx_{N}\leq C\int_{C_{n}}|\nabla w_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx,

(7.9)

where
C
depends only on
N
. Since
\bar{w}_{n}
has zero mean, Poincaré’s and Jensen’s inequalities yield

\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}|\bar{w}_{n}|^{2}\,dx_{N}\lesssim h_{n}^{2}\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}|\bar{w}_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx_{N}\leq h_{n}^{2}\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}\fint_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\partial_{N}w_{n}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{N}.

As a result, we get

\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}\left(\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\bar{w}_{n}|^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}\,dx_{N}=|B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})|^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}|\bar{w}_{n}|^{2}\,dx_{N}\lesssim\left(\frac{h_{n}}{l_{n}}\right)^{2}\int_{C_{n}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}\,dx

(7.10)

Combining (
7.8
), (
7.9
) and (
7.10
), we see that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{-h_{n}}^{h_{n}}\left(\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|w_{n}|^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}\,dx_{N}\lesssim\Lambda(h_{\infty})<\infty.

Passing to a subsequence (not relabeled), we know that there exist functions

w\in L^{2}(-h_{\infty},h_{\infty};L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})),\quad g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{h_{\infty}}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
w_{n}\rightharpoonup w
in
L^{2}(-h_{\infty},h_{\infty};L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))
and
\nabla w_{n}\rightharpoonup g
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{h_{\infty}}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Arguing as in Step 1 of Lemma
7.2
, we deduce that
w
is weakly differentiable with
\nabla w=g
and that
w=\alpha
weakly on
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}
. Hence,
w/\alpha
is an admissible test function in (
2.13
). Therefore, by the weak lower semi-continuity of
L^{2}
-norm and (
7.8
),

\alpha^{2}\operatorname{Cap}_{h_{\infty}}(K^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{h_{\infty}}^{N}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\leq\Lambda(h_{\infty}).

Step 2:
Next, we assume
h_{\infty}=0
. Set

\displaystyle\hat{w}_{n}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=w_{n}(x^{\prime},h_{n}x_{N}),\quad(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})\times(-1,1),

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{n}(x_{N}):=\fint_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}\hat{w}_{n}(x^{\prime},x_{N})\,dx^{\prime},\quad x_{N}\in(-1,1).

By (
7.8
)

\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\nabla^{\prime}\hat{w}_{n}|^{2}+\frac{1}{h_{n}^{2}}|\partial_{N}\hat{w}_{n}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{N}=\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{C_{n}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{\Lambda(h_{n})}{h_{n}}.

(7.11)

Applying the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality to
\hat{w}_{n}
and integrating in
x_{N}
gives

\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\hat{w}_{n}(x)-\bar{w}_{n}(x_{N})|^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}\,dx_{N}\leq C\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\nabla^{\prime}\hat{w}_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{N},

(7.12)

where
C
depends only on
N
. It is easy to check that
\bar{w}_{n}
has zero mean. From Poincaré’s and Jensen’s inequalities and (
7.11
) we obtain

\int_{-1}^{1}|\bar{w}_{n}|^{2}\,dx_{N}\lesssim\int_{-1}^{1}|\bar{w}_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx_{N}\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\fint_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\partial_{N}\hat{w}_{n}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{N}\lesssim\frac{h_{n}^{2}}{l_{n}^{N-1}}.

Then

\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\bar{w}_{n}|^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}\,dx_{N}=|B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})|^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}\int_{-1}^{1}|\bar{w}_{n}|^{2}\,dx_{N}\lesssim\left(\frac{h_{n}}{l_{n}}\right)^{2}

(7.13)

Thus, (
7.11
), (
7.12
) and (
7.13
) imply

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\hat{w}_{n}|^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}\,dx_{N}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Lambda(h_{n})}{h_{n}}<\infty.

Passing to a subsequence (not relabeled), we know that there exist functions
\hat{w}\in L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}_{1}^{N})
,
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{1}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\hat{w}_{n}\rightharpoonup\hat{w}
in
L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-3}}(\mathbb{R}_{1}^{N})
and
\nabla\hat{w}_{n}\rightharpoonup g
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{1}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then
\hat{w}
is weakly differentiable with
\nabla\hat{w}=g
. Moreover, by (
7.11
) we have
\partial_{N}\hat{w}_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{1}^{N})
. Consequently,
\partial_{N}\hat{w}=0
and there exists
w\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
such that
\hat{w}(\cdot,x_{N})=w
for a.e.
x_{N}\in(0,1)
. We can also conclude that
\hat{w}=\alpha
weakly on
K^{\prime}\times\{0\}
as in Step 1 of Lemma
7.2
. We now observe that
w/\alpha
is an admissible test function in (
2.17
). Hence, by the weak lower semi-continuity of the
L^{2}
-norm and (
7.11
),

\alpha^{2}\operatorname{Cap}_{0}(K^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|\nabla^{\prime}w|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{1}^{N}}|\nabla^{\prime}\hat{w}|^{2}\,dx\\
\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{B^{\prime}(0,l_{n})}|\nabla^{\prime}\hat{w}_{n}|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{N}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Lambda(h_{n})}{2h_{n}}=\Lambda_{0}.

(7.14)

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.14

Fix
\theta\in(0,1)
. By Remark
2.9.1
it suffices to prove the proposition for smooth functions that equal
1
in an open neighborhood of
K
. We argue by contradiction. If the claim is false, then there exist sequences
(l_{n})
,
(h_{n})
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}l_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}=\infty,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\max\left\{\frac{\ln(l_{n})}{h_{n}},\frac{h_{n}}{l_{n}^{2}}\right\}=0,

and there exist functions
v_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{C(l_{\varepsilon_{n}},h_{\varepsilon_{n}})})

\displaystyle\int_{C(l_{n},h_{n})}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx<\theta\operatorname{Cap}(K,\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\text{for all }n,

. Note that

Since
\alpha_{n}\to 1-a

. Fix
