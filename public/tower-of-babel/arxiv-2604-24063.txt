A robust obstruction to full strong pluripotency for wild blender-horseshoes
Shin Kiriki, Xiaolong Li, Yushi Nakano, Teruhiko Soma

Abstract
. Suppose that M M is a closed manifold of dimension greater than two and r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 . We show that there exists a C r C^{r} -diffeomorphism f : M ⟶ M f:M\longrightarrow M with a wild affine blender-horseshoe Λ f \Lambda_{f} which is C r C^{r} -robustly and strongly pluripotent for Λ f ( mj ) \Lambda_{f}^{(\mathrm{mj})} but not for Λ f \Lambda_{f} , where Λ f ( mj ) \Lambda_{f}^{(\mathrm{mj})} is the subset of Λ f \Lambda_{f} consisting of elements with the majority condition. Thus, within the present affine blender-horseshoe family in [ 10 ] , there is a robust obstruction to extending the strong pluripotency from Λ f ( mj ) \Lambda_{f}^{(\mathrm{mj})} to the whole horseshoe.

1.
Introduction

Pluripotency is a term widely used in physiology and related fields to describe the ability of a system to transition from an undifferentiated state to multiple differentiated states governed by its internal dynamics.
In
[
10
]
, this notion is formulated from the viewpoint of dynamical systems.
Specifically, it realizes some orbits initiating from a hyperbolic invariant set
\Lambda

stochastically and observably under small perturbations of the dynamics.
We say that the system has full pluripotency if the dynamics realizes
all
such orbits.
In this paper, we are interested in the subject whether given dynamics have full pluripotency or not.

Suppose that
M
is a closed Riemannian manifold and
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)

(r\geq 1)
is the space of

C^{r}
-diffeomorphisms on
M
with
C^{r}
-topology.
For any
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
and
x\in M
, consider the sequence of empirical measures

(1.1)

\delta^{n}_{x,f}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{i}(x)},

where
\delta_{f^{i}(x)}
is the Dirac measure on
M
supported at
f^{i}(x)
.
Let
\mathcal{P}(M)
be the space of Borel probability measures on
M
with weak
∗
-topology.
The notion of historic behavior is introduced by Ruelle
[
14
]
.
We say that, for
x\in M
, the
f
-forward orbit of
(f^{n}(x))_{n\geq 0}
has

historic behavior
if the sequence of empirical measures (
1.1
) does not converge in
\mathcal{P}(M)
.
A subset of
M
is called a
historic set
or a
set with historic behavior
if it consists of initial points which give rise to orbits with historic behavior.
Note that the maximal historic set is Lebesgue measurable, see
[
9
, Proposition A.1]
.

The following problem proposed by Takens
[
15
]
is the main motivation of
our study, which is nowadays called Takens’ Last Problem
(abbreviated as TLP for short).

Are there persistent classes of smooth dynamical systems such that the maximal historic
set has positive Lebesgue measure?

Here a non-empty subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
is called a
class
.
A property
\mathbf{P}
on elements of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
is
\mathcal{C}
-
persistent
or
persistent with respect to

\mathcal{C}
if any elements of
\mathcal{C}
satisfy the property.
Moreover, if the class
\mathcal{C}
has non-empty interior, then
\mathbf{P}

is called a
C^{r}
-
robust
property of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
.
TLP asks the existence of an observable historic set,
where a subset of
M
is called
observable
if it has positive Lebesgue measure.
An example of 2-dimensional dynamics with observable historic set is given by
Colli-Vargas
[
6
]
.
Their model is important in the sense that it provides a template for constructing
diffeomorphisms with observable historic sets of special type, i.e. historic contracting wandering domains.
Here a non-empty connected open subset
D
of
M
is called a
wandering domain

if, for any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

(m\neq n)
,

f^{m}(D)\cap f^{n}(D)=\emptyset
.
In fact, we present in
[
11
]
a class
\mathcal{C}
of diffeomorphisms of dimension three or more with wild affine blender-horseshoes such that each element of
\mathcal{C}
has a
contracting wandering domain with historic behavior.
Here a blender-horseshoe
\Lambda
is called
wild

if
W^{\mathrm{u}}(\Lambda)
and
W^{\mathrm{s}}(\Lambda)
have
a homoclinic tangency in the closure of the superposition region of
\Lambda
,
see
[
5
, Section 6.2]
for such a region.
By incorporating the Colli-Vargas model into any 2-dimensional diffeomorphism in the
dissipative Newhouse domain
\mathcal{N}
in
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
, it is shown
in
[
12
]
that there exists a dense subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{N}

each element of which admits a contracting wandering domain with historic behavior.
This is an affirmative answer to TLP relative to a dense subset of
\mathcal{N}
.
Subsequently, other answers to TLP relative to certain persistent classes
are given by Labouriau-Rodrigues
[
13
]
, Barrientos
[
1
]
, Berge-Biebler
[
2
]
,
Jamilov-Mukhamedov
[
7
]
and so on.

Taking one more step beyond TLP, we will study detailed behavior of orbits from statistical or more strongly geometrical points of view.
Arguments on the Colli-Vargas model
f_{0}
in
[
6
]
implicitly imply that the geometrical average of orbits
initiating any element of the whole horseshoe
\Lambda_{f_{0}}
is realized observably by orbits of a diffeomorphism arbitrarily
C^{r}
-close to
f_{0}
, that is,

f_{0}
has full strong pluripotency.
Moreover, it is shown in
[
8
]
that any diffeomorphisms
f
sufficiently close to

f_{0}
have the the same property for the continuation
\Lambda_{f}
of
\Lambda_{f_{0}}
.
This means that the strong full pluripotency is a
C^{r}
-robust property in 2-dimensional dynamics.
In the three or more dimensional case, the model given in
[
11
]
has
strong pluripotency for the subset
\Lambda_{f}^{(\mathrm{mj})}
of
\Lambda_{f}

consisting of elements with the majority condition,
see (
2.3
) in the next section for the definition.
Furthermore it is shown in
[
10
]
that the strong pluripotency for
\Lambda_{f}^{(\mathrm{mj})}
is
C^{r}
-robust.
The aim of this paper is to present three or more dimensional dynamics which are

C^{r}
-robustly and strongly pluripotent for
\Lambda_{f}^{(\mathrm{mj})}
but not for
\Lambda_{f}
.

2.
Pluripotency and main theorem

As in Introduction, suppose that
M
is a closed Riemannian manifold and
r\geq 1
.
We consider the
first Wasserstein metric

d_{W}
on the space
\mathcal{P}(M)
of Borel probability measures on
M
defined as

d_{W}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\varphi}\left|\int_{M}\varphi\,d\mu-\int_{M}\varphi\,d\nu\right|

for
\mu
,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
,
where the supremum is taken for all Lipschitz functions
\varphi:M\longrightarrow[-1,1]

with Lipschitz constant bounded by
1
.
Note that the metric
d_{W}
is compatible with the weak
∗
-topology on
\mathcal{P}(M)
.

Now we recall the concepts of pluripotency and strong pluripotency introduced in
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Pluripotency)
.

Let
\Lambda_{f}
be a uniformly hyperbolic compact invariant set for
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
.

(1)

f
is
pluripotent
for a subset
\Lambda_{f}^{\prime}
of
\Lambda_{f}
if, for any
x\in\Lambda_{f}^{\prime}
,
there exist
g\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
arbitrarily
C^{r}
-close to
f
and
a positive Lebesgue measure subset
D_{g}
of
M
such that,
for any
y\in D_{g}
,

(2.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{W}(\delta_{y,g}^{n},\delta_{x_{g},g}^{n})=0,

where
x_{g}\in\Lambda_{g}^{\prime}
is the continuation of
x\in\Lambda_{f}^{\prime}
.

(2)

f
is
strongly pluripotent
for
\Lambda_{f}^{\prime}
if the
following condition holds instead of (
2.1
) for
g
and
D_{g}
as above.

(2.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sup_{y\in D_{g}}\{\mathrm{dist}(g^{i}(y),g^{i}(x_{g}))\}=0.

Moreover such an
f
is said to be
fully
(strongly) pluripotent if
\Lambda_{f}^{\prime}
is equal to the whole
invariant set
\Lambda_{f}
.

We note that (
2.2
) implies (
2.1
), while the converse is not true in
general.
For example, see
[
10
, Theorem 1.8]
.

In this paper we consider the case of
\dim M\geq 3
.
In
[
10
, Theorem B]
, we showed that
there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{0}
of a diffeomorphism
f_{0}
in
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
with a wild affine blender-horseshoe
such that any element
f
of
\mathcal{U}_{0}
has the continuation
\Lambda_{f}
of

\Lambda_{f_{0}}
and is strongly pluripotent for
the subset
\Lambda_{f}^{(\rm mj)}
of

\Lambda_{f}
consisting of elements with the majority condition.
Here we say that an element
x
of
\Lambda_{f}
satisfies the
majority condition
if

(2.3)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\left\{j\ ;\ n-(3n)^{2/3}\leq j\leq n,\ v_{j}=0\right\}}{(3n)^{2/3}}\geq\frac{1}{2},

where

(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the binary code corresponding to
x
.
Since
\Lambda_{f}^{({\rm mj})}
is dense in but not equal to
\Lambda_{f}
, the following question is natural.

Does there exist a diffeomorphism
f
arbitrarily
C^{r}
-close to
f_{0}

as above and strongly pluripotent for the whole
\Lambda_{f}
?

The following theorem gives a negative answer to this question.

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold of dimension greater than two.
Then there exist an element
f_{0}
of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)

(r\geq 1)
with an affine blender-horseshoe
\Lambda_{f_{0}}
and a
C^{r}
-neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{0}
of
f_{0}
satisfying the following condition.
Any element
f
of
\mathcal{U}_{0}
is not strongly pluripotent
for the continuation
\Lambda_{f}
of the whole
\Lambda_{f_{0}}
.

The diffeomorphism
f_{0}
given in Theorem
A
is essentially the same as the one in
[
10
, Theorem B]
, with the extra assumptions in Section
4
added.
Theorem
A
exhibits an obstruction to full strong pluripotency in the present family.

By Theorem
A
together with
[
10
, Theorem B]
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary B
.

If
2\leq r<\infty
, then there exist
f_{0}
and
\mathcal{U}_{0}
as in Theorem
A

such that
any element
f
of
\mathcal{U}_{0}
is strongly pluripotent for
\Lambda_{f}^{({\rm mj})}
but not
for
\Lambda_{f}
.

We note that, to apply Theorem B in
[
10
]
,
f_{0}
in Corollary
B
must have a wild blender-horseshoe.

Corollary
B
raises the following question.

Question 2.2
.

Does there exist a maximal subset
\Lambda_{f_{0}}^{\prime}
of
\Lambda_{f_{0}}
such that
every
f\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
is strongly pluripotent for the continuation

\Lambda_{f}^{\prime}
of
\Lambda_{f_{0}}^{\prime}
?
If so, determine
\Lambda_{f_{0}}^{\prime}
.

Theorem
A
says nothing about the (weak) pluripotency for
\Lambda_{f}
.
So the following question remains open.

Question 2.3
.

Does there exist a diffeomorphism
f
which is arbitrarily close to
f_{0}
given in Theorem
A

and pluripotent for the whole
\Lambda_{f}
?

3.
Describability and Pluripotency Lemma

In this section, we present the practical necessary and sufficient condition for a diffeomorphism
f
on

M
to be strongly pluripotent.

A pair
\{\mathbb{U}_{0},\mathbb{U}_{1}\}
of connected open sets in
M
with mutually disjoint closures
is called a
coding pair
of a horseshoe
\Lambda_{f}
of
f
if

\Lambda_{f}=\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}f^{i}(\mathbb{U}_{0}\sqcup\mathbb{U}_{1})

and the restriction
f|_{\Lambda_{f}}:\Lambda_{f}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{f}
is topologically conjugate
to the shift map on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
by the coding map

\mathcal{I}_{f}:\Lambda_{f}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
defined as

\left(\mathcal{I}_{f}(x)\right)_{j}=v\quad\text{if $f^{j}(x)\in\mathbb{U}_{v}$},

where
(\mathcal{I}_{f}(x))_{j}
stands for the
j
-th entry of
\mathcal{I}_{f}(x)
.

Definition 3.1

(Describability)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a subset of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and

f
an element of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
with a horseshoe
\Lambda_{f}
associated with a coding pair
\{\mathbb{U}_{0},\mathbb{U}_{1}\}
,
where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
We say that
f
is
\Sigma
-
describable
over
\Lambda_{f}

if any element
\underline{v}=(v_{0}v_{1}v_{2}\ldots\,)
of
\Sigma

satisfies the following conditions:

(D1)

There exist a strictly increasing sequence
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of non-negative integers and
integers
\beta_{k}

(k\in\mathbb{N})
with
0\leq\beta_{k}\leq\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha_{k}-1
and
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\left\{j\,;\,0\leq j\leq n-1,\ j\in\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}_{k}\right\}}{n}=1,

where
\mathbb{I}_{k}=[\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

(D2)

There exist an element
g
of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
arbitrarily
C^{r}
-close to
f
and
a positive Lebesgue measure subset
D_{g}
of
M

such that

g^{j}(D_{g})\subset\mathbb{U}_{v_{j}}

for any
j\in\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{I}_{k}
.

The following theorem in
[
10
, Theorem A]
plays
an important role in the proof of the main theorem,
where
\widehat{\Sigma}
is the subset of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
consisting of elements

(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
with
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Sigma
.

Theorem 3.2

(Pluripotency Lemma)
.

Suppose that
f
is an element of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)

(r\geq 1)
with a horseshoe
\Lambda_{f}
associated with a coding pair
\{\mathbb{U}_{0},\mathbb{U}_{1}\}
and

\Sigma
is a non-empty subset of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
Then
f
is
\Sigma
-describable if and only if

f
is strongly pluripotent for
\mathcal{I}_{f}^{-1}(\widehat{\Sigma})
.

Remark 3.3
.

The novelty of Theorem
A
lies in using the converse direction of the Pluripotency Lemma as an obstruction principle.
In our previous works, one verified describability in order to prove strong pluripotency.
In contrast, here we show that, for asymptotically 1-filling itineraries (see (
6.2
) for the difinition), describability would force long good blocks whose projected-area growth is incompatible with
the geometric phenomena caused by the
g
-orbit of
D_{g}
.
This obstruction rules out full strong pluripotency.

4.
Wild affine blender-horseshoes

For simplicity, we only consider the case that
M
is a closed manifold of dimension three.
In the case of
\dim M=n>3
, our arguments still work
for certain elements of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
having a blender-horseshoe

\Lambda
with
\dim W^{\mathrm{u}}(\Lambda)=\dim W^{\mathrm{cs}}(\Lambda)=1
and
\dim W^{\mathrm{ss}}(\Lambda)=n-2
.
A diffeomorphism
f_{0}
on
M
presented in this section has a wild affine blender-horseshoe similar to those in
[
11
,
10
]
.

Let
\lambda_{\rm ss},\lambda_{\rm cs0},\lambda_{\rm cs1}
and
\lambda_{\rm u}

be real positive constants with

(4.1)

\lambda_{\rm ss}<\frac{1}{2},\quad\lambda_{\rm ss}<\lambda_{\rm cs0}\leq\lambda_{\rm cs1}<1<\lambda_{\rm cs0}+\lambda_{\rm cs1},\quad 2<\lambda_{\rm u}.

Here the inequalities
\lambda_{\rm ss}<2^{-1}
and
2<\lambda_{\rm u}
are necessary to define a blender-horseshoe.
The condition
1<\lambda_{{\rm cs}0}+\lambda_{{\rm cs}1}
is used to show that the blender-horseshoe has the
distinctive property
.
See Section 6.2 in
[
5
]
for details.
The property is required in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem B]
,
which is essential for proving Corollary
B
.
It follows from (

) that

\lambda_{\rm cs1}\lambda_{\rm u}>1,

which is crucial in the proof of Theorem
A
.
We fix a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
For example, it is taken so that

The closed interval

Consider the sub-blocks of
