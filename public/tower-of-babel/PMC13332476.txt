GripAgain – Development of a Mobile Application (App) for hand function training after nerve surgery
Authors: AE Carolus, Y Habib, N Trautz, M Terhaar, C Brenke, V Braun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe recovery of hand function following nerve surgeries is an important aspect of rehabilitation significantly influencing patients' quality of life. Introduction

Hand function recovery after peripheral nerve surgery is a critical determinant of patient's quality of life. Motor and sensory deficits resulting from nerve injuries (median, ulnar and radial nerve) can severely impair grasping ability and fine motor skills. Conventional rehabilitation approaches typically involve physiotherapy and occupational therapy sessions, yet these are often limited by time, accessibility, and long-terme adherence challenges.

The increasing digitalization of healthcare (eHealth) has opened new avenues for rehabilitation. In Germany, the Digital Healthcare Act (Digitales Verordnungs-Gesetz) implemented in 2019, introduced the concept of “apps on prescription” (Digitale Gesundheitsanwendungen DiGAs), allowing physicians to prescribe certified mobile health applications reimbursed by statutory health insurance (Cramer et al., 2019; Giebel et al., 2024). This development underscores the growing importance of mobile technologies for patient-centered care and highlights the necessity of regulatory compliance, evidence-based content, and robust data protection.

Against this backdrop, the mobile application GripAgain was developed to specifically support rehabilitation of hand function following nerve surgery. The app aims to combine scientific evidence with user-centered design, thereby improving accessibility, engagement, and continuity of rehabilitation.

Material and methods

App development

Grip Again was developed using Flutterflow as the front-end framework and Firebase as the back-end infrastructure. The front end refers to the part of an application with which users directly interact. It includes the graphical user interface and all visual and interactive elements, such as menus, buttons, forms and displayed conent. The primary purpose of the front end is to provide an intuitive and user-friendly experience while enabling users to access the application's functionality. The back end refers to the server-side component of an application that operates behind the scenes. It is responsible for processing data, implementing business logic, amanging user authentication, and communicating with databases and external services.

Data security was implemented according to current standards and in line with GDPR (Datenschutz-Grundverordnung) to meet both national and international regulatory requirements.

The user interface (UI) was designed using Figma and follows an minimalist approach, prioritizing clarity and ease of navigation. Large, clearly visible buttons and simple menu structure were implemented to accommodate patients with impaired fine motor control (Fig. 1).

Grip again was evaluated using the Mobile App Usability Questionnaires (MAUQ). The MAUQ is a questionnaire developed by Zhou et al. and based on various validated questionnaires. It was specifically designed for mobile health applications and took into account the particular requirements that such applications place on their users. The MAUQ was used in its entirety with all 21 questions. The questions covered the dimensions of usability, functionality, and mobile use. Responses were given using a 5-point Likert scale (1 = “strongly disagree” to 5 = “strongly agree”). The MAUQ was directly accessible via GripAgain and could be completed by patients on the settings page.

Results

GripAgain could be finished and after 14 days testing period by 20 participants it could be integrated in App Store and Play Store (Fig. 4).

QR-Codes to download GripAgain.

Compared to existing solutions, Grip Again was found to be more accessible for older patients due to its simplified interface, while still providing sufficient functionality to motivate younger users. However, the app was noted to have room for improvement in training customization, gamification elements, and integration of objective clinical outcome measures.

Discussion

The presented work resulted in the development of a practical and useable application that enables location- and time-independent engagement in hand rehabilitation. Beyond its clinical utility, the app also serves as a communication and promotional tool, potentially increasing awareness of digital rehabilitation approaches in peripheral nerve surgery.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. At present, videos cannot be downloaded, which makes users dependent on a stable internet connection. Furthermore, the app currently does not provide functionalities of such specificity or clinical risk relevance that would necessitate a CE certification. While one could theoretically argue that the app may represent competition for occupational therapists, this concern appears negligible in practice, as the tool is better understood as complementary rather than substitutive.

Looking ahead, further development could include the integration of additional features to increase user engagement and clinical benefit. Examples might involve gamification elements such as repetition counters or competitive modes to boost motivation (Valdes et al., 2020). An option could be sensor-based real-time feedback utilizing motion tracking to monitor movement accuracy, speed, and repetitions. This could provide instant correction and encouragement. Leap motion-based systems validated this capability. Finally, the incorporation of artificial intelligence and conversational agents offer emerging opportunities. Applications like RehabCoach – a smartphone-based agent designed for unsupervised stroke therapy – have demonstrated high task completion rates and favorable usability assessments (Jun Wei et al., 2020; Towards RehabCoach).

Conclusion

This work demonstrates the feasibility of development a user-friendly mobile application to support location- and time-independent hand rehabilitation. The app is available on Play store and App store. Although current limitations, the application is able to implement existing treatment pathways.
