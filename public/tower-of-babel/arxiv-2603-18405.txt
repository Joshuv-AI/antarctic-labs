Lower and upper bounds of joint ( f , δ ) (f,\delta) -numerical radius functions
Zameddin I. Ismailov, Sergei Silvestrov, Pembe Ipek Al

Abstract
In this study, the classical results on the joint numerical radius for n n -tuples of Hilbert space operators are extended to the setting of the joint ( f , δ ) (f,\delta) -numerical radius. New and diverse contributions to this area are provided, including novel estimates for the lower and upper bounds of the ( f , δ ) (f,\delta) -numerical radius in the context of sectorial operators.



Keywords:

(f,\delta)
-numerical radius, sectorial operator, convex function

Introduction

The concepts of numerical range and numerical radius of an operator play a
fundamental role in both pure and applied mathematics, and have been extensively
studied due to their wide-ranging applications in fields such as engineering,
quantum computing, quantum physics, numerical analysis, differential equations,
fluid dynamics and the geometry of Banach spaces (see
[
3
,
7
,
18
]
).
The investigation of numerical radius inequalities and their various refinements
has become a prominent and contemporary research trend in the theory of operators
on Hilbert spaces.

Throughout this article,
\mathcal{H}
denotes a complex Hilbert space endowed with the inner
product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and associated norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
and
S_{1}(\mathcal{H})
be the unit sphere of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
The numerical
radius of an operator
A
is defined by

\omega(A)=\sup\{|\langle Ax,x\rangle|:x\in S_{1}(\mathcal{H})\}.

The usual operator norm of an operator
A\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})

is defined by

\|A\|=\sup\{\|Ax\|:x\in S_{1}(\mathcal{H})\}.

It is well recognized in operator theory literature that the exact computation of the numerical radius
\omega(A)
for a general operator
A\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a highly challenging problem.
Explicit formulas are typically available only for particular classes of operators that exhibit special structural properties.
Consequently, a significant line of research has been devoted to the development of lower and upper bounds for
\omega(A)
.

Recall that the numerical radius
\omega(\cdot)
is a norm on
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
, and among the most basic bounds for
\omega(A)
is the following two-sided inequality
[
17
]
,

\frac{1}{2}\|A\|\leq\omega(A)\leq\|A\|.

(1)

The right-hand side of (
1
) becomes an equality when
A
is normal, while the left-hand side becomes an equality when
A^{2}=0,
where
0
is the zero operator in
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Sharpening inequality (
1
) has received considerable attention in the literature. Among these attempts, the following two-sided inequality is of interest
[
21
]
:

\frac{1}{4}\left\|A^{*}A+AA^{*}\right\|\leq\omega^{2}(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|A^{*}A+AA^{*}\right\|.

(2)

Inequality (
2
) refines both the left and right inequalities in (
1
).

For our purpose, we will need the following definition for
n
-tuple of operatorss in
\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H}):=\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})\ (n\ times),n\geq 1.

.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
,
\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n})\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
be two
n

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. The following definitions are introduced.

1.

\lambda\mathbb{A}=(\lambda A_{1},\ldots,\lambda A_{n})

2.

\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}=(A_{1}+B_{1},\ldots,A_{n}+B_{n})
;

3.

\mathbb{A}^{*}=(A_{1}^{*},\ldots,A_{n}^{*})
, where
A_{m}^{*}
is the adjoint of the operator
A_{m}
in
\mathcal{H},

1\leq m\leq n;

4.

\mathbb{A}=0
if and only if for each
1\leq m\leq n
,
A_{m}=0
;

5.

Re\mathbb{A}=(ReA_{1},\ldots,ReA_{n})

and
Im\mathbb{A}=(ImA_{1},\ldots,ImA_{n});

6.

|\mathbb{A}|=\left(|A_{1}|,\ldots,|A_{n}|\right),
where
|A_{m}|=(A_{m}^{*}A_{m})^{\frac{1}{2}},\ 1\leq m\leq n;

7.

\mathbb{A}x=(A_{1}x,\ldots,A_{n}x)\in\mathcal{H}^{n}

\|\mathbb{A}x\|=\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\|A_{m}x\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ x\in\mathcal{H}

The operator norm of
\mathbb{A}\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
is defined as

\|\mathbb{A}\|=\sup\left\{\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\|A_{m}x\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}:x\in S_{1}(\mathcal{H})\right\}=\left\|\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}A_{m}^{*}A_{m}\right\|^{1/2};

\langle\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle=\left(\langle A_{1}x,x\rangle,\ldots,\langle A_{n}x,x\rangle\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\ x\in\mathcal{H};

10.

|\langle\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle|=\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}|\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ x\in\mathcal{H}
;

11.

The joint numerical range of
\mathbb{A}\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
is a subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined by

W(\mathbb{A})=\left\{\bigl(\langle A_{1}x,x\rangle,\ldots,\langle A_{n}x,x\rangle\bigr):x\in S_{1}(\mathcal{H})\right\}.\ \textnormal{(See {\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Jana2024,Muller2020,Takaguchi1981}{}{}]}})}

12.

The joint numerical radius of
\mathbb{A}\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
is defined by

\omega(\mathbb{A})=\sup\left\{\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}|\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}:x\in S_{1}(\mathcal{H})\right\}.

Clearly, for
\mathbb{A}\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
,

\omega(\mathbb{A})\leq\|\mathbb{A}\|.

In 2014, Drnovšek and Müller
[
11
]
, and Müller
[
27
]
discussed the joint numerical radius of an
n
-tuple of operators and studied the lower bound of the numerical radius. More precisely, for
\mathbb{A}\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
,

\omega(\mathbb{A})\geq\dfrac{\|\mathbb{A}\|}{2\sqrt{n}}.

Therefore, the joint numerical radius is a norm equivalent to the operator norm on

\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
. In particular, if
\mathbb{A}\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H})

is an
n
-tuple of isometries on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, then
\|\mathbb{A}\|=\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}I\|^{1/2}=\|nI\|^{1/2}=\sqrt{n}.

Thus, for any
n
-tuple of isometries,
\omega(\mathbb{A})\leq\sqrt{n}
,
that is, the numerical radius is bounded from above by
\sqrt{n}
.

For any commuting
n
-tuples in general,
r(\mathbb{A})\leq\omega(\mathbb{A})\leq\|\mathbb{A}\|,
where
r(\mathbb{A})
is the joint spectral radius.
The submultiplicativity
\omega(TS)\leq\omega(T)\omega(S)
is generally false.
However, for commuting operators
T
and
S
the bound
\omega(TS)\leq 2\omega(T)\omega(S)
holds.

While the numerical range
W(A)=\{\langle Ax,x\rangle:x\in\mathcal{H},\|x\|=1\}
of any bounded operator
T
on a separable complex Hilbert space is always a convex set,
the joint numerical range of
n
-tuple
W(\mathbb{A})
is, in general, not convex for
n\geq 2
.
For a single normal operator, the closure of its numerical range is the convex hull of its spectrum.
The joint numerical range of a commuting tuple of normal operators is always a convex set, and the closure of the joint numerical range
equals the convex hull of the joint approximate spectrum

\sigma_{ap}(A)=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\exists\{x_{k},\|x_{k}\|=1\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{H}:\\
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\|(A_{j}-\lambda_{j}I)x_{k}\|=0\quad\text{for all }j=1,\dots,n\}.

For commuting normal operators, the joint numerical range’s closure also coincides with its “joint numerical status,” (joint “algebraic numerical range”) which is the set of all states

\mathcal{S}(C^{*}(\mathbb{A},I))=\{\phi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}:\phi\text{ is a linear functional},\\
\forall\ T\in\mathcal{A}\phi(T^{*}T)\geq 0\text{ (Positivity)},\phi(I)=1\text{ (Normalization)}

on the

C^{*}
-algebra generated by the operators
C^{*}(\mathbb{A},I)
(the smallest
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
B(\mathcal{H})
containing all operators of the tuple
\mathbb{A}
and the identity operator
I
), which is
a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra, since
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
are pairwise commuting and normal.
This set is always convex and compact. Because the states of a

C^{*}
-algebra are the weak-star closure of the convex hull of its “pure states” (which correspond to vector states
in the irreducible case), this leads to a “convex hull” structure.

If the commuting isometries are also normal (unitary operators,
U^{*}U=UU^{*}=I
), then the numerical radius is exactly equal to the operator norm.
When the isometries in
n
-tuple
\mathbb{A}
commute, the joint numerical radius is equal to the joint spectral radius.
For commuting normal isometries (unitaries),
\omega(\mathbb{A})=\|\mathbb{A}\|=\sqrt{n}
, as a consequence of the existence of a joint spectrum in which the values can be simultaneously maximized.
The Spectral Mapping Theorem for commuting tuples forces the numerical radius to hit the upper bound of the norm when the operators commute and behave like unitaries on their joint spectrum.

The numerical radius drops significantly when the isometries anticommute (
A_{i}A_{j}=-A_{j}A_{i}
).
For anticommuting isometries (e.g., generators of a Clifford algebra), the joint numerical radius is significantly smaller than
\sqrt{n}
.
For anticommuting isometries, the bound often reduces toward
\omega(\mathbb{(}A))\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}

or even lower, depending on the dimension of the space.
For the
n
-tuple of anticommuting isometries, the generators of a Clifford algebra
C\ell_{n}(\mathbb{C})
), the joint numerical range is effectively a unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, yielding

\omega(\mathbf{A})=1
.
The norm
\|\mathbb{A}\|
grows as
\sqrt{n}
, whereas the numerical radius remains at
1
.
Since
\frac{w(\mathbf{A})}{\|\mathbf{A}\|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\rightarrow 0,n\rightarrow+\infty
, for highly non-commuting systems, the numerical radius becomes a negligible fraction of the total operator norm.
The ratio (or the “gap”)
\frac{w(\mathbf{A})}{\|\mathbf{A}\|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
may be interpreted as a quantitative measure of the non-commutativity.

Example 2
.

In the Eucleadean space
\mathbb{R}^{2}(\mathbb{R})

\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},A_{2}),\ \mathbb{A}\in\mathfrak{B}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

Let

Then, for

\displaystyle\|A_{1}x\|=|x_{2}|,\ \|A_{2}x\|=|x_{1}|,\ |\langle A_{1}x,x\rangle|=|\langle A_{2}x,x\rangle|=|x_{1}x_{2}|,

\displaystyle|\langle\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle|_{f}=|\langle A_{1}x,x\rangle|+|\langle A_{2}x,x\rangle|=2|x_{1}x_{2}|.

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{A}\|_{f}=\sup\left\{\|\mathbb{A}x\|_{f}:x\in S_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=\sup\left\{|x_{1}|+|x_{2}|:x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in S_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=\sup\left\{\sqrt{1+2|x_{1}x_{2}|}:(x_{1},x_{2})\in S_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}=\sqrt{2}.

=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in S_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):|x_{1}x_{2}|\geq\dfrac{\delta^{2}-1}{2}\right\}

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{f,\delta}(\mathbb{A})=\left\{|\langle\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle|_{f}:x\in\Delta_{(f,\delta)}(\mathbb{A})\right\}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if}\ 0\leq\delta\leq 1,\\
\delta^{2}-1,&\text{if}\ 1<\delta<\sqrt{2}.\end{cases}

Definition 7

The main goal of this study is to generalize well-known results concerning the joint

-numerical radius for
n
-tuple Hilbert space operators, as found in the existing mathematical literature, to the framework of the joint
(f,\delta)
-numerical radius. In Section

-numerical radius function are established in the context of sectorial operators.

The results obtained in this study not only generalize and extend many well-known inequalities associated with the classical and joint numerical radius functions, but also improve several of them. Moreover, this study provides strengthened formulations and new perspectives that significantly enrich existing literature.

Throughout this article,

A function

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}),\ \mathbb{B}=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n})\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H})
.

1.

For all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\ \lambda\neq 0
and
0\leq\delta\leq\|\lambda\mathbb{A}\|_{f},

\omega_{(f,\delta)}(\lambda\mathbb{A})=|\lambda|\omega_{\left(f,\frac{\delta}{|\lambda|}\right)}(\mathbb{A}),

provided that
f
satisfies the equality
f(xy)=f(x)f(y)
for
x,y\geq 0.

2.

If
f
is geometrically convex, then for any
0\leq\delta\leq\|\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}\|_{f},

\omega_{(f,\delta)}(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})\leq\omega_{\left(f,\delta-\|\mathbb{B}\|_{f}\right)}(\mathbb{A})+\omega_{\left(f,\delta-\|\mathbb{A}\|_{f}\right)}(\mathbb{B}).

In special case, for any
\alpha\leq\max\left\{\|\mathbb{A}\|_{f},\|\mathbb{B}\|_{f}\right\},

\omega_{(f,\delta)}(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})\leq\omega_{\left(f,\delta-\alpha\right)}(\mathbb{A})+\omega_{\left(f,\delta-\alpha\right)}(\mathbb{B}).

3.

If
\mathbb{A}
is
n
-tuple of normal operators in
\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H}),
then for any
0\leq\delta\leq\|\mathbb{A}\|_{f}
,

\omega_{(f,\delta)}(\mathbb{A})=\omega_{(f,\delta)}(\mathbb{A}^{*}).

4.

If
\mathbb{A}
is
n
-tuple of hyponormal operators in
\mathfrak{B}^{n}(\mathcal{H}),
then for any
0\leq\delta\leq\|\mathbb{A}^{*}\|_{f}

\omega_{(f,\delta)}(\mathbb{A}^{*})\leq\omega_{(f,\delta)}(\mathbb{A}).

Proof.

1
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\ \lambda\neq 0
and
x\in\mathcal{H},
since
f
satisfies
f(xy)=f(x)f(y)
, then

\displaystyle|\langle\lambda\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle|_{f}=f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle\lambda A_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\lambda||\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\right)=f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\lambda|\right)f\left(|\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=|\lambda|f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\right)=|\lambda||\langle\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle|_{f}.

(3)

Since for any

the following equality holds

\|\lambda\mathbb{A}x\|_{f}=|\lambda|\|\mathbb{A}x\|_{f},

it follows that

\Delta_{(f,\delta)}(\lambda\mathbb{A})=\Delta_{\left(f,\frac{\delta}{|\lambda|}\right)}(\mathbb{A}).

(4)

Therefore, (
3

4

\omega_{(f,\delta)}(\lambda\mathbb{A})=|\lambda|\omega_{\left(f,\frac{\delta}{|\lambda|}\right)}(\mathbb{A})

for
0\leq\delta\leq\|\lambda\mathbb{A}\|_{f}.

2
. Since
f
monotonically increasing,

\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle(A_{m}+B_{m})x,x\rangle|\right)=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle+\langle B_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\\
\leq\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle|+|\langle B_{m}x,x\rangle|\right).

Also, from
[
26

f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle|+|\langle B_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\right)\leq f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\right)\\
+f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle B_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\right).

Consequently, one can easily see that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{r}f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle(A_{m}+B_{m})x,x\rangle|\right)\right)\leq f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle A_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\right)\\
+f^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(|\langle B_{m}x,x\rangle|\right)\right).\end{array}

On the other hand, it is clear that for any

\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(\|(A_{m}+B_{m})x\|\right)\leq\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}f\left(\|A_{m}x\|+\|B_{m}x\|\right).

Hence, by

26

Similarly, it can be shown that

(8)

Theorem 10
.

n

1.
For any

\displaystyle f\left(|\langle\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle|_{f}\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(|\langle\sec(\gamma_{min})Re\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle|\right).

x\in\mathcal{H}

|\langle\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle|_{f}\geq|\langle\sec(\gamma_{min})\mathbb{A}x,x\rangle|_{f},

Corollary 12
.

, if

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\|ReA_{1}\|^{2}+\|ImA_{1}\|^{2}}{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})+\cos^{2}(\gamma_{1})}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\|ReA_{1}+ImA_{1}\|}{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})+\cos^{2}(\gamma_{1})}=\dfrac{\|A_{1}^{*}A_{1}+A_{1}A_{1}^{*}\|}{2\left(\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})+\cos^{2}(\gamma_{1})\right)}.

Corollary 13
.

Let
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty.
Under the condition of Theorem
10
if
n=1,\ \delta=0
and
0\leq\gamma_{1}<\alpha_{1}<\frac{\pi}{2}
, then

\|ReA_{1}\|\leq\cos(\gamma_{1})\omega(A_{1})\ \text{and}\ \|ImA_{1}\|\leq\sin(\alpha_{1})\omega(A_{1}).

Then, it is well-known from
[
33
]
that

\|A_{1}\|^{2}\leq\|ReA_{1}\|^{2}+\|ImA_{1}\|^{2}\leq\left(\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})+\cos^{2}(\gamma_{1})\right)\omega^{2}(A_{1}).

Hence,

\|A_{1}\|\leq\sqrt{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})+\cos^{2}(\gamma_{1})}\ \omega(A_{1}).

It must be noted that in the matrix case of
A_{1}\in Sec_{\alpha_{1}}(\mathcal{H}),\ 0\leq\alpha_{1}<\dfrac{\pi}{2},
the following main result was obtained in
[
33
]
,

\|A_{1}\|\leq\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})}\ \omega(A_{1}).

Therefore, the previous estimation shows that in the accretive-dissipative matrix case, the last inequality can be improved.

It must be noted that for accretive-dissipative operators
A_{1},B_{1}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
with conditions

\displaystyle W(A_{1})

\displaystyle\subset\left\{re^{i\varphi}:r\geq 0,\ 0<\gamma_{1}<\alpha_{1}<\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle W(B_{1})

\displaystyle\subset\left\{re^{i\varphi}:r\geq 0,\ 0<\gamma_{1}<\alpha_{1}<\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right\}

from the inequalities

\displaystyle\|A_{1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})+\cos^{2}(\gamma_{1})}\omega(A_{1}),

\displaystyle\|B_{1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})+\cos^{2}(\gamma_{1})}\omega(B_{1}),

we have the following interpolated inequality for the matrices
A_{1}
and
B_{1}

\omega(A_{1}B_{1})\leq\left(\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})+\cos^{2}(\gamma_{1})\right)\omega(A_{1})\omega(B_{1}).

In this case, from the last relation it is clear that

\omega(A_{1}B_{1})\leq\left(1+\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})\right)\omega(A_{1})\omega(B_{1}).

This was proved for accretive-dissipative matrices in
[
33
]
