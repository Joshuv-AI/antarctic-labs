Neural Network Generalized Parton Distributions (NNGPD)
Simonetta Liuti

Abstract
Generalized parton distributions (GPDs) serve as indispensable tools for the exploration of proton structure. In this study, we offer a deep learning-assisted framework for the extraction of GPDs from experimental data and the results of ab-initio lattice quantum chromodynamics (LQCD).

Introduction

GPDs parameterize off-forward matrix elements of QCD correlation functions; e.g., the leading-twist quark vector GPDs

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2P^{+}}\bar{u}(P^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})\Big(H^{q}(x,\xi,t;Q^{2})\gamma^{+}+E^{q}(x,\xi,t;Q^{2})\frac{i\sigma^{+\nu}\Delta_{\nu}}{2M}\Big),

where a straight gauge link is chosen and appears trivially as unity in the correlation function. The GPDs are functions of

\displaystyle{-i\pi\,\big(H_{q}(\xi,\xi,t)-H_{q}(-\xi,\xi,t)\big)}.

The "first inverse problem" of GPDs, namely, the extraction of CFFs from DVES cross sections and asymmetries, is rife with difficulty due both to the formalism and to the quality of presently available data (see, e.g., the ExclAIm collaboration’s statistically rigorous study of Jefferson Lab data in Ref.
[
1
]
). Tabling for the moment this interesting first inverse problem, this study focuses on the "second inverse problem" of GPDs, which is the extraction of GPDs from CFFs. This second inverse problem is notably more prone to degenerate solutions than the extraction of parton distribution functions (PDFs) from deep inelastic scattering, given that the momentum fraction
x
variable of GDPs is integrated over to give the CFFs, while the structure functions of PDFs maintain their functional dependence on
x
as
x=x_{Bj}
in inclusive processes.

To address the challenges outlined above, we developed the NNGPD framework
[
13
]
, employing deep neural networks to construct high-dimensional, bias-controlled representations of GPDs constrained by QCD symmetries and experimental data.

Machine learning framework

Feed-forward neural network framework for NNGDP.

In addition to the CFFs, which are extracted from experiment, a robust further constraint on GPDs are the Mellin moments
M_{n}
, defined as
x
-weighted integrals of GPDs,

\displaystyle M_{n}^{q}(\xi,t;Q^{2})=\int_{-1}^{1}dxx^{n-1}H^{q}(x,\xi,t;Q^{2}).

(2.1)

These Mellin moments can be shown to have a polynomial dependence on
\xi
in terms of generalized form factors (GFFs),

\displaystyle M_{n}^{q,H}=\int_{-1}^{1}dxx^{n-1}H_{q}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0,even}^{n-1}(2\xi)^{i}A^{q}_{n,i}(t)+\mod(n-1,2)(2\xi)^{n}C_{n}^{q}(t).

(2.2)

Mellin moments and GFFs can be calculated through local insertions in the ab-initio framework of LQCD. We therefore propose a feedforward neural network framework that incorporates both the CFFs from experiment and the Mellin moments and generalized form factors calculated in LQCD, as shown in Figure
1
. Two different networks, one for the “
+
" antisymmetric with respect to
x
and another for the “
-
" symmetric with respect to
x
components of the GPDs, are defined through the losses as follows, taking into account that the “
+
" distribution is constrained by the
n\>\mathrm{even}
moments, and the “
-

n\>\mathrm{odd}
moments.
The weights
w
in the loss and the additional constraints under “symmetry constraints" are explained in detail in Ref.
[
13

within a Bayesian neural network (BNN).
The loss function
\mathcal{L}_{+}
for
H^{+}_{q}\equiv H_{q}+H_{\bar{q}}
is defined as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{+}=w_{\mathrm{CFF}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFF}}+w_{\mathrm{MM}}^{+}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MM}}^{+}+\>\mathrm{symmetry\>constraints},

(2.3)

where,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFF}}=|\mathrm{CFF}-\mathrm{CFF}^{\mathrm{ML}}|^{2},

(2.4)

meaning the difference between the “ground truth" and ML-generated CFFs is taken during the loss minimization process, and a similar definition is given for the Mellin moments loss.
The loss function
\mathcal{L}_{-}
for the
H^{-}_{q}\equiv H_{q}-H_{\bar{q}}
is defined similarly, using the odd/symmetric moments, but omitting the constraint from the CFFs.
Considering both
\mathcal{L}_{+}
and
\mathcal{L}_{-}
then allows for the extraction of
H_{q}
and
H_{\bar{q}}

One can compare this framework to the scheme of the NNPDF collaboration where PDFs are modeled similarly as a neural network, multiplied by an “endpoints regulating function,"

(Ref.

3
Closure test on a GPD phenomenological model

Before using the CFFs from experimental extractions and Mellin moments and GFFs from lattice calculations, a closure test needs to be performed on a realistic GPD model in order to benchmark the framework of Figure
1
. The parameterization used in this study, named UVA2, is from Ref.
[
11
]
. UVA2 provides GPDs in the valence, sea, and gluon sectors that capture the behavior of lattice QCD moments, electromagnetic form factors, inclusive scattering data, and GPD perturbative QCD evolution equations. Additionally, UVA2 incorporates GPD symmetries in the DGLAP and ERBL regions and predicts CFFs in the kinematics of Jefferson Lab and the upcoming Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) at Brookhaven National Lab.

4
Conclusions

GPDs are complicated physical distributions that enter QCD in a manner which makes their extraction from experiment difficult, though not intractable. Machine learning techniques, as shown above within a closure test using the phenomenological UVA2 model, offer powerful methods for the extraction of GPDs from experimental data and ab-initio LQCD results coupled with theoretical constraints. Directly training the NNGPD deep learning framework with actual CFFs and lattice results remain as future work. Further enhancements of the NNGPD methodology will entail applying more interpretable tools, such as symbolic regression as in Ref.
[
5
]
, to solving this inverse problem.
