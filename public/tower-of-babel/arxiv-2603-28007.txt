Legendrian and Lagrangian higher torsion
Daniel Álvarez-Gavela, Kiyoshi Igusa, Mike Sullivan

Abstract
. Let M M be a closed manifold. We introduce a family of Legendrian isotopy invariants for Legendrians in J 1 ​ M J^{1}M , which we collectively call Legendrian higher torsion. Given a choice of a class ℱ \mathcal{F} of fibre bundles over M M , equipped with suitable unitary local systems, the Legendrian higher torsion of a Legendrian Λ ⊂ J 1 ​ M \Lambda\subset J^{1}M is the subset of H ∗ ​ ( M , 𝐑 ) H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R}) consisting of higher Reidemeister torsion cohomology classes of fibre bundles W W over M M in the class ℱ \mathcal{F} such that Λ \Lambda admits a generating function on a stabilization of W W . 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main results

Fix a closed, connected, smooth manifold
M
. We denote by
J^{1}M
its 1-jet space, where we recall
J^{1}M=T^{*}M\times\mathbf{R}
with coordinates
(q,p,z)\in J^{1}M
corresponding to
q\in M
,
p\in T^{*}_{q}M
and
z\in\mathbf{R}
, with the contact form
dz-pdq
endowing
J^{1}M
with its standard contact structure. In this article we introduce a family of new K-theoretic Legendrian isotopy invariants for Legendrian submanifolds
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
, which we call
Legendrian higher torsion
. These invariants are defined in terms of generating functions for
\Lambda
and have several incarnations depending on the class of fiber bundles one chooses to consider as the possible domains of generating functions for
\Lambda
.

In general, one has notion of a
torsion pair
, which consists of a pair
(W,\rho)
where
F\to W\to M
is a fiber bundle and
\rho:\pi_{1}W\to U(1)
is a rank 1 unitary local system such that
H_{*}(F;\mathbf{C}^{\rho})
is a unipotent
\pi_{1}M
-module. If
F
is closed no more data is needed, but if
F
has boundary or is non-compact one should also fix the behavior near the boundary or at infinity for the class of functions
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
we allow ourselves to consider as possible generating functions. For a torsion pair
(W,\rho)
the theory of higher torsion characteristic classes due to the second author and J. Klein produces cohomology classes
\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
which are invariants of
(W,\rho)
up to fibered diffeomorphism of fiber bundles (compatible with the fixed behavior at infinity) and up to the notion of stable equivalence which replaces
W
with
W\times\mathbf{R}^{4}
and
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
with
f^{\text{s}}(w,x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})=f(w)+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-y_{1}^{2}-y_{2}^{2}

[
Igu02
,
Igu05
,
IK93a
,
IK93b
,
K89
]
.

When
H_{*}(F,\mathbf{C}^{\rho})=0
, for
k=0
the class
\tau_{0}(W,\rho)\in H^{0}(M;\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R}
recovers the usual Reidemeister torsion of the fiber
F
with respect to
\rho|_{\pi_{1}F}
and for
k>0
the class
\tau_{k}(W,\rho)
may be thought of as a parametrized generalization of this Reidemeister torsion.

Equipped with the theory of higher torsion classes of fiber bundles, we define the
k
-th
Legendrian higher torsion
of a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
in a fixed collection
\mathcal{F}
of torsion pairs to be the subset
\tau_{k}(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})\subset H^{2k}(W,\rho)
consisting of all degree
2k
higher torsion characteristic classes
\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
of torsion pairs
(W,\rho)
in the class
\mathcal{F}
such that
\Lambda
admits a generating function on a stabilization of the fiber bundle
W\to M
. We denote
\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})=\coprod_{k}\tau_{k}(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R})
the
total Legendrian higher torsion
of
\Lambda
in the fixed collection
\mathcal{F}
.

The starting point of the theory of Legendrian higher torsion is the following consequence of the homotopy lifting property for generating functions under Legendrian isotopies (up to stabilization).

Theorem 1.1
.

The subset
\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R})
is a Legendrian isotopy invariant of
\Lambda
.

For example, in the article
[
AI
]
the first and second authors implicitly gave a concrete application of these ideas in the special case in which
\mathcal{F}
consists of oriented circle bundles
S^{1}\to W\to\Sigma
over a fixed orientable surface
\Sigma
, equipped with rank 1 unitary local systems which send the oriented fiber to a fixed
n
-root of unity
\neq 1
for
\pm n
the Euler number of
W
. In that specific case it is computed in
[
AI
]
that for Legendrians in a certain class (called
mesh Legendrians
), all of which in particular admit generating functions on circle bundles, the degree 2 Legendrian higher torsion
\tau_{1}(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})
of a mesh Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}\Sigma
in
H^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R}
records the Euler number (up to sign) of the circle bundle that was used to generate it. In particular it follows that this number is an invariant of the mesh Legendrian, and that each mesh Legendrian admits a generating function on a circle bundle of Euler number
\pm n
for a unique
n
,
even after stabilization of the circle bundle
.

In fact, in
[
AI
]
a variant of Legendrian higher torsion called
Legendrian Turaev torsion
was defined for a general class of Legendrians called
Euler Legendrians
. For mesh Legendrians the Legendrian Turaev torsion allowed one to also recover the sign of this Euler number. The Legendrian Turaev torsion was then used in
[
AI
]
to distinguish pairs of Legendrians which were formally Legendrian isotopic but not Legendrian isotopic, and in fact such that the isomorphism class of the Legendrian contact homology dga and microlocal sheaf category was unable to distinguish them (at least with traditional coefficients).

Although different flavors of the invariant may be useful for different purposes, our main focus in the present article is a specific version of Legendrian higher torsion which we call
tube torsion
. This version is particularly relevant to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, the study of which is the primary motivation for this work. Tube torsion is Legendrian higher torsion for the class
\mathcal{F}
of tube bundles in the sense of Waldahusen
[
Wal82
]
. Tube bundles have gained direct relevance to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture after the existence theorem for (twisted) generating functions on tube bundles for nearby Lagrangians proved by Abouzaid, Courte, Guillermou and Kragh in
[
ACGK
]
. When working with tube bundles we will omit the class
\mathcal{F}
from the notation, and as will be explained, the data of the unitary local system may also be disregarded. Although the precise definition of the invariant will be given in Definition
3.25
once the notions of tube bundles and generating functions of tube type are properly formulated, we give for now the following provisional definition.

Definition 1.2
.

The
tube torsion
of a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
is the collection
\tau(\Lambda)\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R})
of higher torsion classes
\tau_{k}(W)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
of tube bundles
W\to M
on which
\Lambda
admits a generating function.

Remark 1.3
.

Since the class of tubes is
stable
, i.e. closed under stabilization, we do not have to add
up to stabilization
in the above definition.

The main properties of tube torsion are summarized in the following result. We will denote by
p:KO^{0}(M)\to H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R})
a suitably normalized version of the Pontryagin character adapted to the definition of higher torsion, see Appendix
B
. Let
\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}
be the standard zeta function. For
E\to M
a real vector bundle we denote
p(E)=\sum_{k}p_{k}(E)
with
p_{k}(E)\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R})
given by

p_{k}(E)=\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k+1)ch_{2k}(E\otimes\mathbf{C}).

Theorem 1.4
.

The tube torsion of a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
satisfies the following:

(1)

\tau(\Lambda)
is invariant under Legendrian isotopies of
\Lambda
.

(2)

\tau(\Lambda)
is a union of cosets of
p
, so we may view
\tau(\Lambda)
as a subset of
\text{coker}(p)
.

(3)

for the zero section
M\subset J^{1}M
we have
\tau(\Lambda)=\text{im}(p)
, the trivial coset.

Remark 1.5
.

For
W
a tube bundle the lack of a unitary local system means we are drawing invariants from
K(\mathbf{R})
rather than
K(\mathbf{C})
and this forces the odd classes
\tau_{2k+1}(W)
to be zero. So the only possible nontrivial classes are the even classes
\tau_{2k}(W)\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R})
and hence the tube torsion of a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
is a disjoint union
\tau(\Lambda)=\coprod_{k}\tau_{2k}(\Lambda)
with
\tau_{2k}(\Lambda)\subset H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R})
.

Note that by definition, we have
\tau(\Lambda)=\varnothing
if
\Lambda
does not admit a generating function on any tube bundle. For example, the fiberwise Morse theory of a tube bundle in the sense considered in the present article has Euler characteristic
\pm 1
and so there certainly holds
\tau(\Lambda)=\varnothing
if the projection
\Lambda\to M
does not have degree
\pm 1
. When
\tau(\Lambda)=\varnothing
we say that the tube torsion is
not defined
.

So for which Legendrians is tube torsion defined? A particularly elusive class of Legendrians is given by the Legendrian lifts of nearby Lagrangians. We recall that for
L
and
M
closed, connected, smooth
n
-dimensional manifolds, a nearby Lagrangian is an exact Lagrangian embedding
L\subset T^{*}M
. A nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
admits a generating function on some tube bundle if and only if the stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
is nullhomotopic
[
ACGK
]
. Therefore the tube torsion of the Legendrian lift
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
of any nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
with stably trivial Gauss map is defined, and we denote it
\tau(L)\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R})
.

Furthermore, in
[
ACGK
]
a procedure for producing a generating function on a tube bundle for a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
is given once the input of a nullhomotopy of the stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
is fixed, using the
doubling trick
which Guillermou introduced in
[
Gui12
]
. We do not know whether any generating function on a tube bundle for a nearby Lagrangian is given by this procedure. We say that a generating function for
L
which does arise by this procedure is
natural
and denote by
\nu(L)\subset\tau(\Lambda)
the subset consisting of those higher torsion classes in
\tau(\Lambda)
of tube bundles
W\to M
where the generating function for
L
on
W
is natural. We call
\nu(L)
the
nearby Lagrangian torsion
of
L
. It satisfies the following properties:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
L\subset T^{*}M
be a nearby Lagrangian with trivial stable Gauss map. The nearby Lagrangian torsion of
L
satisfies the following:

(1)

\nu(L)
is invariant under Hamiltonian isotopies of
L
.

(2)

\nu(L)
is a single coset of
p
, so we get a well-defined element
\nu(L)\in\text{coker}(p)
.

(3)

for the zero section we have
\nu(M)=\tau(M)=\text{im}(p)
.

Remark 1.7
.

Although the tube torsion
\tau(\Lambda)
of the Legendrian lift
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
of a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
is invariant under Legendrian isotopies of
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
, the nearby Lagrangian torsion
\nu(L)
is not obviously invariant under Legendrian isotopies of
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
. Concretely, if
L_{1},L_{2}\subset T^{*}M
are two nearby Lagrangians with Legendrian isotopic Legendrian lifts, then we do not know whether it must be the case that
\nu(L_{1})=\nu(L_{2})
.

Example 1.8
.

In
[
ACGK
]
it was also shown that the stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
of a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
is always trivial on homotopy groups. Since
L
and
M
are homotopy equivalent, this implies that
L\to U/O
is always nullhomotopic when
M
has the homotopy type of a sphere. So for nearby Lagrangian homotopy spheres, generating functions on tube bundles always exist and therefore the tube torsion and nearby Lagrangian torsion of a nearby Lagrangian homotopy sphere are always defined. Both invariants are automatically trivial for degree reasons unless the dimension of
M
is of the form
n=4k
. For concreteness, say
M=S^{4k}
is the standard sphere (though the discussion is similar if
M
is any exotic sphere of dimension divisible by 4). The only possible nontrivial higher torsion class of a tube bundle
W\to S^{4k}
is
\tau_{2k}(W)\in H^{4k}(S^{4k};\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R}
. Hence for a nearby Lagrangian homotopy sphere
\Sigma\subset T^{*}S^{4k}
we have a distinguished element

\nu(\Sigma)\in\text{coker}\left(p:KO^{0}(S^{4k})\to H^{4k}(S^{4k};\mathbf{R})\right).

Alternatively, we may view

\nu(\Sigma)\in\text{coker}\left(\mathbf{Z}\xrightarrow{\lambda_{k}}\mathbf{R}\right)=\mathbf{R}/\lambda_{k}\mathbf{Z}

where
\lambda_{k}=p(E)\cdot[S^{4k}]\in\mathbf{R}
for
E\to S^{4k}
the generator of
KO^{0}(S^{4k})\simeq\mathbf{Z}
. So the nearby Lagrangian torsion of any nearby Lagrangian homotopy sphere
\Sigma\subset T^{*}S^{4k}
is well-defined and it is just a real number modulo
\lambda_{k}\mathbf{Z}
.

Furthermore, by
[
AD22
]
it is known that after a Hamiltonian isotopy of
\Sigma\subset T^{*}S^{4k}
we may assume that the front of
\Sigma
in
S^{4k}\times\mathbf{R}
only has cusp type singularities. This implies that any generating function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
for
\Sigma
will have moderate singularities, i.e. the fiberwise singular set consists only of Morse points and (cubic) Morse birth/death points. As will be discussed, this means that
\nu(\Sigma)\in\mathbf{R}/\lambda_{k}\mathbf{R}
may be computed directly from the family of fiberwise Morse chain complexes
C_{*}(f_{m})
,
m\in S^{4k}
, of the fiberwise restrictions
f_{m}
of a natural generating function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
for
\Sigma
.

To distinguish from the case in which nearby Lagrangian torsion isn’t defined, we use the following terminology.

Definition 1.9
.

We say that a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
has
trivial tube torsion
when
\tau(\Lambda)=\text{im}(p)
. We say that a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
has
trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion
when
\nu(L)=\text{im}(p)
.

Remark 1.10
.

If a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
has trivial tube torsion, then it automatically has trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion.

We may formulate the resulting obstruction to Legendrian and Hamiltonian isotopy to the zero section as follows:

Corollary 1.11
.

If a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
is Legendrian isotopic to the zero section, then it has trivial tube torsion. In particular, if a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
is Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero section, then it has trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion.

The famous nearby Lagrangian conjecture states that any nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
is Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero section. The following question is therefore natural.

Question 1.12
.

Must a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
have trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion?

An affirmative answer to this question would provide further evidence towards the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, whereas a negative answer would disprove it. Note that according to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture the Legendrian lift
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
of a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
would in particular be Legendrian isotopic to
M
, though even this weaker form of the conjecture is not known. It seems conceivable that there could exist a nearby Lagrangian with trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion but nontrivial tube torsion, which would also disprove the nearby Lagrangian conjecture.

Remark 1.13
.

The invariant
\nu(L)
is an invariant of a
single
nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
. It is a parametrized torsion invariant of a family of acyclic chain complexes associated to
L
. The space of parameters is
M
, and for a single parameter
m\in M
the relevant chain complex is (at least conjecturally)
CF^{*}(T_{m}M,L)
, see Section
4.5
. However, we take the generating function viewpoint in the present article and use the fiberwise Morse complex
C_{*}(f_{m})
of a natural generating function
f
for
L
instead. The work by Courte and Porcelli
[
CP25
]
gives a vanishing result for the parametrized higher torsion of a
family
of nearby Lagrangians
L_{z}\subset T^{*}M
,
z\in Z
, where for a single parameter the relevant chain complex is
CF^{*}(M,L_{z})
for
M\subset T^{*}M
the zero section, though
[
CP25
]
also takes the viewpoint of generating functions. The work
[
CP25
]
is closer to a parametric generalization of the result
[
AK
]
of vanishing of Whiehead torsion for a single nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
. Indeed, the invariants
\tau(L)
and
\nu(L)
have no
non-parametric
analogue; the fiber of a tube bundle is simply connected so its Whitehead group is trivial.

In the broader class of Legendrians which are not necessarily lifts of nearby Lagrangians, we show that the theory of tube torsion is nontrivial in that Legendrians with nontrivial tube torsion abound. In fact, we establish the existence of Legendrians which have nontrivial tube torsion and which are diffeomorphic to the base, so that the conjectural triviality of tube torsion for nearby Lagrangian would in any case not follow from known constraints on the topology of nearby Lagrangians.

To formulate the precise statement, we will use the viewpoint of Waldhausen
[
Wal82
]
where a fibration sequence
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\to BG
is constructed. Here
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
is the space of stable h-cobordisms of a point (which classifies bundles of h-cobordisms of a disk up to stable equivalence),
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
is the stable space of tubes (which classifies tube bundles up to stable equivalence), and
BG
is the classifying space for stable spherical fibrations, so
G=\lim_{n}G_{n}
for
G_{n}
the space of self homotopy-equivalences of
S^{n}
. In particular, this means given a bundle
H\to V\to M
of h-cobordisms of a disk, one may attach
V
to a trivial tube bundle
M\times T
, where
T
is the standard tube, to obtain a tube bundle
T\to W\to M
. The tube bundle
W
has the same higher torsion as
V
.

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
H\to V\to M
be a bundle of
h
-cobordisms of a disk and let
T\to W\to M
the resulting tube bundle. There exists a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
which admits a generating function on
W
and whose projection to the base
\Lambda\to M
is homotopic to a diffeomorphism. In particular the higher torsion classes
\tau_{k}(V)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
of
V
are contained in the tube torsion
\tau(\Lambda)\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R})
of
\Lambda
.

The higher torsion of tube bundles arising from h-cobordism bundles as in Theorem
1.14
is in general not contained in the image of the (normalized) ontryagin character, and hence this result shows that Legendrians with nontrivial tube torsion exist in abundance. Unfortunately, we do not know how to visualize a single explicit example of a Legendrian with nontrivial tube torsion. The simplest possible example would live in
J^{1}S^{4}
.

Problem 1.15
.

Describe explicitly, or draw the front of a Legendrian in
J^{1}S^{4}
with nontrivial tube torsion.

Although we do not know whether nearby Lagrangians must always have trivial tube torsion, or even whether they must always have trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion, in the upcoming work
[
AIS
]
we will prove the triviality of nearby Lagrangian torsion under the additional assumption that the front admits a ruling satisfying certain technical conditions, which we call a
restricted ruling
.

Finally we note that if a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
has a stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
which is not nullhomotopic, then it cannot admit any generating function, so that tube torsion is not defined. However, such an
L
would already be a counterexample to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, since a Hamiltonian isotopy between
L
and the zero section would in particular yield a nullhomotopy of the stable Gauss map.

1.2.
Structure of the article

In Section
2
we define a general class of invariants which we collectively call
Legendrian higher torsion
. In Section
3
we specialize to a specific version tailored to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, which we call
tube torsion
, and prove the existence of Legendrians which are diffeomorphic to the base and which have nontrivial tube torsion. In Section
4
we identify a distinguished coset of the tube torsion of a nearby Lagrangian, which we call
nearby Lagrangian torsion
, and prove that it is a Hamiltonian isotopy invariant. In Appendix
A

2.
Legendrian higher torsion

In this section we present a general construction of Legendrian invariants using higher torsion classes, which are certain characteristic classes of fiber bundles of smooth manifolds arising from algebraic K-theory.

2.1.
Fiber bundles and fibrations at infinity

Let
M
be a closed, connected smooth manifold. We consider fiber bundles of smooth manifolds
F\to W\to M
and functions
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
on the total space, which we will often think of as families of functions
f_{m}:F_{m}\to\mathbf{R}
on the fiber
F_{m}\simeq F
, parametrized by
m\in M
. In the simplest case
F
is a closed manifold. When
F
is not a closed manifold we will have to add conditions to the possible functions
g:F\to\mathbf{R}
we consider. We use the terminology of
[
EG98
]
in the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
f:F\to\mathbf{R}
is a
fibration at infinity
if there exists a finite segment
[-a,a]\subset\mathbf{R}
and a compact subset
K\subset f^{-1}[-a,a]\subset F
such that the restriction of
f
to the following three subsets fibers them over their respective images:

(1)

f^{-1}(-\infty,a]\to(-\infty,a]

(2)

f^{-1}[a,\infty)\to[a,\infty)

(3)

(f^{-1}[-a,a])\setminus K\to[-a,a]

As noted in
[
EG98
, Section 0.2.1]
, a function
f:F\to\mathbf{R}
is a fibration at infinity if and only if
F
admits a complete Riemannian metric with respect to which
\|df\|\geq\varepsilon>0
outside of a compact subset.

Example 2.2
.

The following are fibrations at infinity.

(1)

A non-degenerate quadratic form
q:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
.

(2)

A function
f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
of class
C^{1}
which is 2-homogeneous, i.e.
f(\lambda v)=\lambda^{2}f(v)
, outside of a compact subset
K\subset\mathbf{R}^{N}
and such that the function
g:S^{N-1}\to\mathbf{R}
given by
g(v)=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}f(\lambda v)
is a smooth function which has
0
as a regular value.

(3)

A function
f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
which is a bounded
C^{1}
-distance from a function as in (2).

(4)

The direct sum of two fibrations at infinity on the product domain.

For a first contact with higher Reidemeister torsion it will be sufficient to consider, for
F
a closed manifold, the class of fiber bundles
W\to M
over
M
whose fiber is diffeomorphic to
F
, together with their stabilizations. By the stabilization of a fiber bundle we will mean the fiberwise product with a trivial
\mathbf{R}^{4N}
bundle, which yields a new fiber bundle

F\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to W\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to M.

On this bundle (with non-compact fibers) we distinguish the following class of functions.

Definition 2.3
.

Given
M
and
F
closed, smooth manifolds and
F\to W\to M
a fiber bundle, we denote by
\mathcal{S}(W)
the class of all of functions
f:W\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
which are fibrations at infinity of the form

f(w,x,y)=g(w)+\varepsilon(w,x,y)+\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2},\qquad(w,x,y)\in W\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}

where
g:W\to\mathbf{R}
is a smooth function and
\varepsilon
is a smooth function with compact support. Functions
f:W\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
in the class
\mathcal{S}(W)
will be said to be
admissible.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that if
f:W\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
is admissible then in particular with respect to any Riemannian metric on
W
it remains a bounded
C^{1}
-distance from the standard fiberwise quadratic form:

q(w,x,y)=\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2},\qquad(w,x,y)\in W\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}.

Remark 2.5
.

If instead of starting with a fiber bundle of closed manifolds
F\to W\to M
we started with a fiber bundle whose fiber
F
has boundary (or is non-compact), then some fixed asymptotic behavior near the boundary (or at infinity) for the functions
g:W\to\mathbf{R}
as in the above definition should be specified.

Warning 2.6
.

We should really write
\mathcal{S}(\pi)
for
\pi:W\to M
the fibration but we will abuse notation and use
\mathcal{S}(W)
instead of
\mathcal{S}(\pi)
throughout.

2.2.
The Legendrian invariant

Let us first consider the case where the fiber
F
is closed.

Definition 2.7
.

A
torsion pair

(W,\rho)
consists of a fiber bundle
F\to W\to M
and a unitary local system
\rho:\pi_{1}W\to U(1)
such that
H_{*}(F;\mathbf{C}^{\rho})
is a unipotent
\pi_{1}M
-module.

Here
H_{*}(F_{b};\mathbf{C}^{\rho})
denotes the homology of the fiber with the twisted complex coefficients given by
\rho|_{\pi_{1}F}
and by
unipotent
we mean that the
\pi_{1}M
-module
H_{*}(F_{b};\mathbf{C}^{\rho})
admits a filtration by
\pi_{1}M
submodules so that the action of
\pi_{1}M
on the successive subquotients is trivial.

Warning 2.8
.

We should really write
(\pi,\rho)
for
\pi:W\to M
the fibration but we will abuse notation and write
(W,\rho)
instead of
(\pi,\rho)
throughout.

Given a torsion pair
(W,\rho)
, one has its higher Reidemeister torsion, which consists of cohomology classes
\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
, whose construction is reviewed in Appendix
B
.

By a
Legendrian submanifold
we will mean throughout a Legendrian embedding
\Lambda\hookrightarrow J^{1}M
of a closed smooth manifold
\Lambda
, i.e. compact and without boundary. By an
immersed Legendrian
we will mean a Legendrian immersion
\Lambda\to J^{1}M
of a closed smooth manifold
\Lambda
, though we will abuse notation and denote both embedded and immersed Legendrians by
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
. To make the connection between functions on fiber bundles over
M
and Legendrians in
J^{1}M
we need an additional condition on the functions we consider. The condition is generic, and is the standard definition of a
generating function
for a Legendrian in a 1-jet space. Recall that the
vertical differential
of a function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
on the total space of a fiber bundle
\pi:W\to M
is the restriction
d_{v}f
of the usual differential
df:TW\to\mathbf{R}
to the vertical distribution
\ker(d\pi)\subset TW
.

Definition 2.9
.

A function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
is said to be a
generating function
if the vertical differential
d_{v}f
is transverse to zero.

If
f
is a generating function, then
f
determines a Legendrian immersion from the fiberwise singular locus
\Sigma_{f}=\{d_{v}f=0\}\subset W
into
J^{1}M
. Indeed, the condition that
d_{v}f\pitchfork 0
means that the graph
\Gamma(df)\subset T^{*}W
intersects transversely the coisotropic sumanifold
X\subset T^{*}W
consisting of covectors which are zero on the fibers
T_{m}F
,
m\in M
. The symplectic reduction of the coisotropic submanifold
X
is naturally identified with
T^{*}M
and the image of the submanifold
X\cap\Gamma(df)=\Gamma(df)|_{\Sigma_{f}}
under this reduction is an immersed exact Lagrangian submanifold of
T^{*}M
whose Legendrian lift (via the values of
f
) is an immersed Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
.

When moreover
\Lambda
is an embedded Legendrian submanifold of
J^{1}M
(which is the case generically), one says that the Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
is
generated
by
f
.

Now fix a collection
\mathcal{F}
of torsion pairs
(W,\rho)
. Examples of reasonable collections to consider include but are not limited to:

(1)

The singleton consisting of a fixed
W
and a fixed
\rho
.

(2)

The collection of all
(W,\rho)
for a fixed
W
but all possible
\rho
for that
W
.

(3)

The collection of all possible torsion pairs
(W,\rho)
with fiber a fixed closed manifold
F
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
be a Legendrian submanifold. The
Legendrian higher torsion
of
\Lambda
in the class
\mathcal{F}
is the subset
\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R})
consisting of all higher Reidemeister torsion classes
\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
of torsion pairs
(W,\rho)
in
\mathcal{F}
such that
\Lambda
admits a generating function
f\in\mathcal{S}(W)
.

Theorem 2.11
.

If
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}\subset J^{1}M
are Legendrian isotopic, then
\tau(\Lambda_{0},\mathcal{F})=\tau(\Lambda_{1},\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

As follows from the homotopy lifting property for generating functions under Legendrian isotopy up to stabilization, if
\Lambda_{0}
admits a generating function in the class
\mathcal{S}(W)
, then so does
\Lambda_{1}
. The desired result then follows immediately. The homotopy lifting property for generating functions under Legendrian isotopies has a long history
[
Sik86
,
C96
]
, but the formulation which is needed at this level of generality may be found in
[
EG98
]
.
∎

2.3.
Suitable classes of fiber bundles

Of course, given a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
, for most choices of
\mathcal{F}
we will have
\tau(\Lambda)=\varnothing
, since many Legendrians don’t admit any generating function and even those which do will only admit generating functions on certain fiber bundles. More generally, if one wishes to use generating function theory to study a particular class of Legendrians, then one should make a judicious choice for the class
\mathcal{F}
of fiber bundles and notion of admissible generating function under consideration.

As a first example, if
\Lambda
is disjoint from a single fiber
T_{x}M\times\mathbf{R}\subset J^{1}M
then it won’t admit a generating function on any fiber bundle
F\to W\to M
with fiber
F
a closed manifold, even after stabilization. In particular, if one wishes to study compact Legendrian submanifolds of
J^{1}\mathbf{R}^{n}=\mathbf{R}^{2n+1}
one should consider a theory of generating functions with zero fiberwise Morse homology. In the
n=1
case one could consider as in
[
JT
]
generating functions
f:\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{k+1}\to\mathbf{R}
on the trivial
\mathbf{R}^{k+1}
-bundle over
\mathbf{R}
which are linear-quadratic at infinity, i.e. such that for
(w,v,z)\in\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{k}\times\mathbf{R}
there holds outside of a compact subset
f(w,v,z)=Q(v)+L(z)
for
Q
a nondegenerate quadratic form and
L
a nondegenerate linear form.

If instead one is interested in studying Legendrian braids in
J^{1}S^{1}
, then one could consider as in
[
T01
]
generating functions
f:S^{1}\times\mathbf{R}^{k}\to\mathbf{R}
on the trivial
\mathbf{R}^{k}
-bundle over
S^{1}
which are quadratic at infinity, i.e. such that for
(w,v)\in S^{1}\times\mathbf{R}^{k}
there holds outside of a compact subset
f(w,v)=Q(v)
for
Q
a nondegenerate quadratic form.

Other classes of fiber bundles may be useful for other purposes. We explain the simplest example of a nontrivial application of the theory of Legendrian higher torsion known to us, in which the class of fiber bundles under consideration consists of circle bundles over orientable surfaces. In
[
AI
]
the first two authors investigated a class of Legendrian submanifolds which admit generating functions on such circle bundles. Given an oriented circle bundle over an oriented surface
S^{1}\to W\to\Sigma
, with Euler number
n\in\mathbf{Z}
, and given a rank 1 unitary local system
\rho:\pi_{1}W\to U(1)
such that the oriented fiber
S^{1}
gets sent to
u^{-1}
for
u\neq 1
an
n
-th root of unity, the higher Reidemeister torsion
\tau_{1}(W,\rho)\in H^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R}
is a constant multiple of the Euler number of the circle bundle up to sign, as was computed by the second author and J. Klein in
[
IK93a
,
IK93b
]
. Concretely, we have

\tau_{1}(W,\rho)=n\,\,\text{Im}\left(\mathcal{L}_{2}(u)\right)

where
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is the dilogarithm

\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{2}}.

That the higher Reidemeister torsion up to sign survives as a stable invariant of the circle bundle and is equal to the Euler class is a nontrivial fact. This fact is a special case of a deep result of Bökstedt
[
B82
]
, and may also be deduced from the theory of higher Reidemeister torsion, see
[
Igu02
]
, concretely Chapter 7 (complexified Bökstedt theorem).

The class of Legendrians
\Lambda\subset J^{1}\Sigma
which were studied in
[
AI
]
are called
mesh Legendrians
. Each mesh Legendrian
\Lambda
is determined by combintarial data and admits an explicit generating function on a circle bundle of Euler number
\pm e_{\Lambda}
for a non-negative integer
e_{\Lambda}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
which is defined in terms of the combinatorial data. In
[
AI
]
a calculation was carried out to determine the possible 2-parametric fiberwise Morse complex bifurcations that a generating function for the mesh Legendrian
\Lambda
could support, on any such circle bundle over
\Sigma
.

In particular it was shown that for any
n\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
a mesh Legendrian
\Lambda
only admits a generating function on the circle bundle
S^{1}\to W\to\Sigma
of Euler number
\pm n
if
n=e_{\Lambda}
,
even after stabilization
of the circle bundle. Hence for the singleton class
\mathcal{F}=\{(W,\rho)\}
consisting of a circle bundle
S^{1}\to W\to\Sigma
of Euler number
\pm n
and
\rho:\pi_{1}W\to U(1)
a rank 1 unitary local system which sends the oriented fiber
S^{1}
to
u^{-1}
for
u\neq 1
an
n
-th root of unity, the Legendrian higher torsion of a mesh Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}\Sigma
in the class
\mathcal{F}
consists of the singleton

\tau_{1}(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})=\{e_{\Lambda}\text{Im}(\mathcal{L}_{2}(u))\}\subset H^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R}

if
n=e_{\Lambda}
, and is empty otherwise. This proves:

Theorem 2.12

(implicit in
[
AI
]
)
.

The Legendrian higher torsion of a mesh Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}\Sigma
recovers (and uniquely determines) the Euler number (up to sign) of the unique circle bundle that may be used to generate it, even after stabilization of the tube bundle and/or Legendrian isotopy of
\Lambda
.

In fact in this specific situation the invariant can be refined to a Turaev type torsion which is also sensitive to the sign of the Euler number, and in this refined version the invariant was defined and used in
[
AI
]
to distinguish Legendrian isotopy classes of mesh Legendrians in
J^{1}\Sigma
in the same formal Legendrian class. The computations of
[
AI
]
can be thought of as a proof of concept that higher torsion invariants may sometimes be calculated for explicit Legendrians.

Our final example of a class
\mathcal{F}
of fiber bundles which one may want to consider for the definition of a Legendrian higher torsion invariant, and the most important class for the purposes of the present article, is the class of
tube bundles
. Roughly speaking, tube bundles are the most general class of fiber bundles on which one might hope to construct generating functions for
nearby Lagrangians
. Hence tube bundles provide the natural class of fiber bundles in which to define a Legendrian higher torsion invariant that may be relevant in the context of the nearby Lagrangian conjecture. We discuss tube bundles and the associated Legendrian higher torsion invariant, which we call
tube torsion
, in Section
3
below.

2.4.
Stable Gauss map

Given a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
, for the Legendrian higher torsion to be defined, i.e. for
\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})
to be nonempty, in addition to the necessity of making a suitable choice for the class
\mathcal{F}
of fiber bundles (and unitary local systems) under consideration, it is also necessary that the stable Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
is trivial, i.e. nullhomotopic. Let us briefly recall the definition of the stable Gauss map.

Let
\pi_{\text{Lag}}:J^{1}M=T^{*}M\times\mathbf{R}\to T^{*}M
denote the Lagrangian projection and consider the symplectic vector bundle
E=\pi_{\text{Lag}}^{*}T(T^{*}M)|_{\Lambda}
over
\Lambda
. Note that
E
is equipped with two polarizations, i.e. Lagrangian sub-bundles, namely
G=T\Lambda
(since
\pi_{\text{Lag}}|_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\to T^{*}M
is a Lagrangian immersion) and
V=\pi_{\text{Lag}}^{*}\nu|_{\Lambda}
, where
\nu
is the Lagrangian distribution on
T^{*}M
tangent to the fibers of the cotangent bundle projection
T^{*}M\to M
. The stable Gauss map measures the stable difference between these two polarizations.

Concretely, pick a Lagrangian sub-bundle
H
of
E
which is transverse to
V
, so that we have a isomorphism of symplectic vector bundles
E\simeq H\oplus H^{*}
under which
V
gets identified with
H^{*}
. There exists some real vector bundle
H^{\prime}
over
\Lambda
such that
H\oplus H^{\prime}
is a trivial real vector bundle. Then
H^{\prime}\oplus(H^{\prime})^{*}\oplus E\simeq(H\oplus H^{\prime})\oplus(H\oplus H^{\prime})^{*}
is isomorphic to a trivial symplectic vector bundle
\Lambda\times\mathbf{C}^{m}
. The Lagrangian sub-bundle
H^{\prime}\oplus G\subset(H^{\prime}\oplus(H^{\prime})^{*})\oplus E
may thus be viewed as a Lagrangian sub-bundle of the trivial symplectic vector bundle
\Lambda\times\mathbf{C}^{m}
, and hence gives a map
\Lambda\to U_{m}/O_{m}
. After letting
m\to\infty
we get the stable Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
, which is easily verified to be independent of choices up to homotopy.

We say that the stable Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
of a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
is
trivial
when it is nullhomotopic. The triviality of the stable Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
is a necessary condition for
L
to admit any kind of generating function, even in a very weak (local) sense, as was shown by Giroux in
[
G90
]
. In particular, no matter what the class of fiber bundles
\mathcal{F}
under consideration is, for Legendrian higher torsion to be defined, i.e. for
\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})
to be nonempty, we must have that the stable Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
is trivial.

We will say that the front of Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
has
moderate singularities
if the front projection

\Lambda\hookrightarrow J^{1}M\to J^{0}M=M\times\mathbf{R}

only has cusp singularities. This property is closely related to the triviality of the stable Gauss map.

Proposition 2.13

(
[
AD22
]
)
.

If
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
is a Legendrian whose front has moderate singularities, then the stable Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
is trivial. In fact there is a distinguished choice of homotopy class of nullhomotopy of the Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
determined by the pair
(S,\nu)
where
S\subset\Lambda
is the locus of points whose front have a cusp and
\nu
is the Maslov co-orientation of
S
in
\Lambda
.

Note that if
L\subset T^{*}M
is a nearby Lagrangian, then the front of the Legendrian lift of
L
has moderate singularities if and only if the tangencies of
L
with respect to the foliation of
T^{*}M
by the cotangent leaves
T^{*}_{m}M
,
m\in M
, has
quadratic
tangencies. When this is the case we will say that
L\subset T^{*}M
has
moderate tangencies
. From Proposition
2.13
it follows that any nearby Lagrangian with moderate tangencies has stably trivial Gauss map
L\to U/O
, and moreover there is a distinguished choice of homotopy class of nullhomotopy.

3.
Legendrian tube torsion

In this section we focus on a specific version of Legendrian higher torsion which we call
Legendrian tube torsion
.

3.1.
Generating functions for nearby Lagrangians

We recall that a nearby Lagrangian is an exact Lagrangian submanifold
L\subset T^{*}M
, where
L
and
M
smooth manifolds which are closed, connected and of the same dimension. The nearby Lagrangian conjecture states that any nearby Lagrangian is isotopic through nearby Lagrangians to the zero section.

For a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
to admit a generating function on a fiber bundle
F\to W\to M
, it is necessary that the fiberwise Morse homology of the generating function have Euler characteristic
\pm 1
. Indeed, the projection
L\to M
is known to be a homotopy equivalence
[
FSS
,
Na09
,
A12
,
Kra13
,
Gui12
]
, and in particular has degree
\pm 1
. In particular we cannot hope to use fiber bundles with closed fibers; we must use non-compact fibers.

The most basic example to keep in mind is the trivial bundle
M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}
with a generating function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
which is equal to the standard fiberwise quadratic form
\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}
outside of a compact subset,
(x,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{2N}\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}
. We will call such generating functions
standard quadratic at infinity
. Note that the zero-section can be generated by such a function (namely the constant family of standard quadratic forms
F(m,x,y)=\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}
). Therefore, if the nearby Lagrangian conjecture is true, then by the homotopy lifting property for generating functions up to stabilization it would follow that any nearby Lagrangian admits a generating function standard quadratic at infinity.

While existence of such generating functions for nearby Lagrangians is not known, there has been recent progress on existence of generating functions for nearby Lagrangians on the more general class of so-called
tube bundles
. The class of tube bundles is a class of fiber bundles which was introduced by Waldhausen in his manifold approach to the algebraic K-theory of spaces
[
Wal82
]
. The precise definition of a tube bundle that we will work with in the present article will be given in Section
3.2
below, but let us note that in particular the fiberwise Morse homology of our notion of
generating function of tube type
has rank 1 concentrated in a single degree, just like a nondegenerate quadratic form. The foundational result of the article
[
ACGK
]
by Abouzaid, Courte, Guillermou and Kragh is the following.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
ACGK
]
)
.

A nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
with trivial stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
admits a generating function of tube type.

So the condition that
L\to U/O
is trivial, which as explained in Section
2.4
is necessary for the existence of
any
kind of generating function, is in fact both necessary and sufficient for a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
to admit a generating function of tube type. It is not known whether every nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
must have stably trivial Gauss map. However, it was shown in
[
ACGK
]
that if
L\subset T^{*}M
is a nearby Lagrangian, then
L\to U/O
is trivial on homotopy groups. In particular, if
M
is a homotopy sphere, then any nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
has stably trivial Gauss map and hence there holds:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
ACGK
]
)
.

If
M
is a homotopy sphere, then any nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
admits a generating function of tube type.

Finally, the discussion at the end of Section 3 in
[
ACGK
]
(see the proof of Theorem 3.26 and preceding lemmas) makes it apparent that tube bundles are in some sense the most general class of fiber bundles on which one may expect a nearby Lagrangian to admit a generating function. The class of tube bundles therefore seems a reasonable class of fiber bundles to study in the context of the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, particularly in the case of nearby Lagrangian homotopy spheres.

3.2.
Functions of tube type

Rather than the model for tube bundles and functions of tube type used in
[
ACGK
]
we will use a convenient model for the space of tubes which was developed in work of the first author joint with Abouzaid, Courte and Kragh in
[
AACK
]
. That Theorem
3.1
also holds for this model was proved in
[
AACK
]
. First we will need to introduce a few preliminary notions.

Definition 3.3
.

A
C^{1}
-function
g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is a
function of quadratic tube type
if it satisfies the following:

(1)

g
is homogeneous of degree 2, i.e.
g(\lambda v)=\lambda^{2}g(v)
.

(2)

g
has no critical point other than the origin.

(3)

g
is homotopic through functions satisfying (1) and (2) to a non-degenerate quadratic form.

The example to keep in mind is the homogenization of a smooth function
h:S^{N-1}\to\mathbf{R}
, i.e.
g(v)=\|v\|^{2}h(v/\|v\|)
for
v\neq 0
and
g(0)=0
. In general such a function will only be of class
C^{1}
.

Definition 3.4
.

A smooth function
f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is a
function of tube type
if there exists a function of quadratic tube type
g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
such that
\|\nabla f-\nabla g\|\leq C
for some constant
C>0
.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that if
g
exists, then it is uniquely determined by
f
, indeed
g(v)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}f(\lambda v)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{k,N}^{q}
denote the space of quadratic functions of tube type on
\mathbf{R}^{N}
such that the non-degenerate quadratic form in condition (3) has index
k
, and let
\mathcal{T}_{k,N}
be the space of functions of tube type on
\mathbf{R}^{N}
such that the associated function of quadratic tube type is in
\mathcal{T}_{k,N}^{q}
. We also denote:

\mathcal{T}^{q}_{N}=\coprod_{k=0}^{N}\mathcal{T}^{q}_{k,N},\quad\mathcal{T}^{q}=\coprod_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}^{q}_{N},\quad\mathcal{T}_{N}=\coprod_{k=0}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{k,N},\quad\mathcal{T}=\coprod_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{N}.

Note that if
f_{i}:\mathbf{R}^{N_{i}}\to\mathbf{R}
is a function of tube type for
i=1,2
, then
f_{1}\oplus f_{2}:\mathbf{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
is also of tube type, so we have an operation
\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},N_{1}}\times\mathcal{T}_{k_{2},N_{2}}\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{k_{1}+k_{2},N_{1}+N_{2}}
which endows
\mathcal{T}
with the structure of a topological monoid. We also have the topological submonoid of quadratic functions of tube type
\mathcal{T}^{q}\subset\mathcal{T}
. Clearly the inclusion
\mathcal{T}^{q}\subset\mathcal{T}
is an equivalence as the linear interpolation
(1-t)f+tg
of a function of tube type
f
with its associated function of quadratic tube type
g
gives a deformation retraction of
\mathcal{T}
onto
\mathcal{T}^{q}
.

We also denote
\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}
for the space of non-degenerate quadratic forms
q:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
of index
k
and

\mathcal{Q}_{N}=\coprod_{k=0}^{N}\mathcal{Q}_{k,N},\qquad\mathcal{Q}=\coprod_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{Q}_{N}.

Note that
\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}\subset\mathcal{T}_{k,N}^{q}
, and that
\mathcal{Q}
is a submonoid of
\mathcal{T}^{q}
and hence also of
\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
M
be a closed, connected, smooth manifold.

(1)

A
fibered function of tube type
is a function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
such that for each
m\in M
the restriction
f_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is of tube type, i.e. such that
f_{m}\in\mathcal{T}_{N}
for each
m\in M
.

(2)

A
fibered function of quadratic tube type
is a function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
such that for each
m\in M
the restriction
f_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is quadratic of tube type, i.e. such that
f_{m}\in\mathcal{T}_{N}^{q}
for each
m\in M
.

(3)

A
fibered function of rigid tube type
is a function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
such that for each
m\in M
the restriction
f_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is a non-degenerate quadratic form, i.e. if
f_{m}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N}
for each
m\in M
.

Remark 3.7
.

Since
M
is connected, the index
k
of any such fibered function of tube type is well-defined.

To a fibered function of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
we may associate the fibered function of quadratic tube type
g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
such that for each
m\in M
the restriction
g_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is the function of quadratic tube type associated to the restriction
f_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
.

Fibered functions of tube type over
M
form a monoid. Fibered quadratic functions of tube type over
M
form submonoid of the monoid of fibered functions of tube type over
M
, and fibered quadratic functions of rigid tube type form yet a smaller submonoid.

We will reserve the following terminology for the monoid operation on fibered functions of tube type when one of the two functions is rigid.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{1}}\to\mathbf{R}
be a fibered function of tube type and let
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
be a fibered function of rigid tube type. We call
f\oplus q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
the
twisted stabilization
of
f
by
q
.

By a
stabilization
without the adjective
twisted
we will mean a stabilization with respect to a constant fibered function of rigid tube type
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
, i.e.
q(x,v)=Q(v)
for some fixed non-degenerate quadratic form

. In fact, typically we will stabilize by the standard form on
\mathbf{R}^{4}
given by
q(x,y)=\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}
for
(x,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{R}^{2}
, and will refer to such a stabilization as a

.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
be a fibered function of rigid tube type, so that
q(m,x)=Q_{m}(x)
for
Q_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
a family of non-degenerate quadratic forms. Let
E\to M
be the real vector bundle whose fiber
E_{m}
over
m\in M
is the negative eigenspace of
(\partial^{2}Q/\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j})_{i,j}
. We call

the

of

.

Remark 3.10
.

Alternatively, a fibered function of ridid tube type is classified by a map

, the notion of stable bundle for a non-degenerate quadratic form determines a map
\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}\to BO(k,N)
, and hence the stable bundle of a fibered function of ridid tube type is classified by the composition
M\to\mathcal{Q}\to BO
. Note that the map
\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}\to BO(k,N)
is a homotopy equivalence, with homotopy inverse the map
BO(k,N)\to\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}
which sends a vector subspace
E\subset\mathbf{R}^{N}
of dimension
k
to the quadratic form
Q(x)=\|x_{E^{\perp}}\|^{2}-\|x_{E}\|^{2}
, where
x=x_{E}+x_{E^{\perp}}
is the decomposition of
x\in\mathbf{R}^{N}
into its
E
and
E^{\perp}
components.

3.3.
Torsion of fibered functions of tube type

Note that the second condition in Definition
3.3
is equivalent to asking that
g
is transverse to zero outside of the origin, so for a function of quadratic tube type
g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
the set
T(g)=\{g\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1}
is a codimension-zero submanifold of
S^{N-1}
(a priori only
C^{1}
, but
C^{1}
submanifolds have unique smoothings up to isotopy). By the third condition of Definition
3.3
this submanifold is isotopic in
S^{N-1}
to
T(q)=\{q\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1}
for
q
a non-degenerate quadratic form. If
E^{-}
is the negative eigenspace of
q
, then this domain is a tubular neighborhood of the equator
E^{-}\cap S^{N-1}
and in particular is diffeomorphic to
S^{k-1}\times D^{N-k}
for
k
the index of
q
and
D^{m}=\{\|x\|\leq 1\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{m}
the unit disk.

Definition 3.11
.

A
rigid tube
is the codimension zero submanifold
T(q)\subset S^{N-1}
for
q
a non-degenerate quadratic form. The
index
of a rigid tube is the index of any such
q
.

Definition 3.12
.

A
tube
is a codimension zero submanifold
T\subset S^{N-1}
which is isotopic in
S^{N-1}
to a rigid tube. The
index
of a tube is the index of any rigid tube it is isotopic to.

Definition 3.13
.

A
tube bundle
is a fiber bundle
W\to M
over a smooth manifold
M
such that

(1)

W
is a codimension zero submanifold of
M\times S^{N-1}

(2)

the map
W\to M
is the restriction to
W
of the projection
M\times S^{N-1}\to M
.

(3)

for each
x\in M
the fiber over
x
is a tube
T_{x}\subset S^{N-1}
.

If for each
x\in M
the fiber of a tube bundle
W\to M
is a rigid tube
T_{x}=T(q_{x})\subset S^{N-1}
, where
q_{x}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is a family of nondegenerate quadratic forms, then we call
W
a
rigid tube bundle.
If
g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is a fibered function of quadratic tube type, then we will denote by
T(g)\to M
the associated tube bundle
T(g)=\{g\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1}
. If
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is a fibered function of tube type, we will denote
T(f)=T(g)
the tube bundle associated to the underlying fibered function of quadratic tube type
g
.

Warning 3.14
.

If
f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is a function of tube type, then
T(f)
is not
\{f\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1}
. It is
\{g\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1}
for
g(x)=\|x\|^{2}\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}f(\lambda x)
. The same comment applies (fiberwise) for fibered functions of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
and their associated tube bundle
T(f)\to M
.

Definition 3.15
.

We will say that a tube bundle
W\to M
of index
k
is
orientable
if the action of
\pi_{1}M
on the homology of the fiber
H_{k-1}(T;\mathbf{Z})\simeq\mathbf{Z}
is trivial.

If
W\to M
is an orientable tube bundle, then the action of
\pi_{1}M
on the homology
H_{*}(F)
of the fiber is trivial and hence in particular unipotent, so we may associate to
W
its higher Reidemeister torsion classes
\tau_{k}(W)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
. We emphasize that for orientable tube bundles, no unitary local system is needed. As explained in Appendix
B
, this causes the classes in degrees congruent to 2 modulo 4 to be automatically trivial. So
\tau_{2k+1}(W)=0
in
H^{4k+2}(M;\mathbf{R})
and we are left with the classes
\tau_{2k}(W)\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R})
.

Let us record the following basic fact.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
W_{1}=T(g_{1})
and
W_{2}=T(g_{2})
be tube bundles associated to fibered quadratic functions of tube type
g_{1}:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
and
g_{2}:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
. If
g_{1}
is homotopic to
g_{2}
through fibered quadratic functions of tube type, then
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
have the same higher torsion classes.

Proof.

Such a homotopy
g_{t}
yields an isotopy of codimension zero submanifolds
W_{t}=T(g_{t})
of
M\times S^{N-1}
and hence by isotopy extension a fibered diffeomorphism
W_{1}\simeq W_{2}
of tube bundles over
M
. Higher torsion classes are invariant under fibered diffeomorphism. ∎

Recall that a fibered function of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
has an associated fibered function of quadratic tube type
g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
and a corresponding tube bundle
W=T(f)=T(g)
pver
M
.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
be a fibered function of tube type, with an associated fibered function of quadratic tube type
g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
and a corresponding tube bundle
W=T(g)
. If
W
is orientable, then
f
is called an
orientable
fibered function of tube type.

To an oriented fibered function of tube type we may associate to
f
the higher torsion classes
\tau_{k}(W)
of the oriented tube bundle
W=T(g)
. These classes are invariant under homotopy of
f
through oriented fibered functions of tube type. For
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
a fibered function of rigid tube type, being orientable amounts to asking that the stable bundle
E\to M
of
q
is orientable.

If
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{1}}\to\mathbf{R}
is an orientable fibered function of tube type and
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
is an orientable fibered function of rigid tube type, then
f\oplus q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{1}+N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
is an orientable fibered function of tube type. Indeed for
k_{1}
the index of
f
and
k_{2}
the index of
q
we have
\Sigma T(g\oplus q)\simeq\Sigma T(g)\land\Sigma T(q)
as
S^{k_{1}+k_{2}}
-fibrations over
M
and hence

H^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}(T(g\oplus q)_{m};\mathbf{R})\simeq H^{k_{1}-1}(T(g)_{m};\mathbf{R})\otimes H^{k_{2}-1}(T(q)_{m};\mathbf{R})

Hence if both the rank 1 real vector bundles
H^{k_{1}-1}(T(g)_{m};\mathbf{R})
and
H^{k_{2}-1}(T(q)_{m};\mathbf{R})
over
M
are trivial, then so is
H^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}(T(g\oplus q)_{m};\mathbf{R})
. At this point it is not yet clear how the higher torsion of
T(g)
and
T(g\oplus q)
should be related. For this purpose we introduce a computational tool.

If an orientable fibered function of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
has
moderate singularities
(i.e. Morse or Morse birth/death, see Appendix
A
), then it is possible to compute the resulting higher torsion classes from the parametrized Morse theory of
f
directly, and this will be useful to us in what follows. We use the same notation as in the statement of the
framing principle
given in Theorem
B.3
. We recall briefly the relevant notions:

(1)

\tau_{k}(f)
is a cohomology class in
H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
obtained from the family
C_{*}(f_{m})
,
m\in M
, of Morse chain complexes for the fiberwise restrictions
f_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
,
m\in M
.

(2)

\gamma_{f}
is a stable real vector bundle over the fiberwise singular set
\Sigma_{f}\subset M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}
formed by the negative eigenspaces of the fiberwise Hessian of
f
.

(3)

\pi_{*}^{\Sigma f}
is the push-down operator in cohomology
H^{*}(\Sigma_{f})\to H^{*}(M)
induced by
\pi|_{\Sigma_{f}}:\Sigma_{f}\to M
.

(4)

for
E\to X
a real vector bundle over a manifold
X
the normalized Pontryagin character

p_{k}(E)=\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k+1)ch_{2k}(E\otimes\mathbf{C})\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R}).

Remark 3.18
.

To simplify issues with signs, from now on we will restrict to the case of even index and ambient dimension a multiple of four. Standard stabilization is by
\mathbf{R}^{4}
with the quadratic form
\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}
,
(x,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{R}^{2}
, which will preserve higher torsion, including its sign.

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
be an orientable fibered function of tube type with moderate singularities, with associated fibered function of quadratic tube type
g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
and tube bundle
W=T(g)
. The tube torsion
\tau(W)=\sum_{k}\tau_{k}(W)
of
W
may be computed from
f
by the following formula:

\tau(W)=-\left(\tau_{2k}(f)+\pi_{*}^{\Sigma f}p_{k}(\gamma_{f})\right).

Proof.

The discussion of relative higher torsion in Appendix
B.6
applies to the trivial disk bundle
D^{4N}\to X\to M
, i.e.
X=M\times D^{4N}
, and the sub-bundle of the sphere bundle
S^{4N-1}\to\partial W\to M
given by
W=T(g)\subset M\times S^{4N-1}
. Since
X
is trivial we have
0=\tau_{k}(X)=\tau_{k}(X,W)+\tau_{k}(W)
so that
\tau_{k}(W)=-\tau_{k}(X,W)
. The proof is them completed by applying the framing principle Theorem
B.3
to the bundle pair
(X,W)
, since we may compute the relative higher torsion
\tau_{k}(X,W)
using the function with moderate singularities
f
.
∎

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{1}}\to\mathbf{R}
be an orientable fibered function of tube type and let
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
be a constant fibered function of rigid tube type, i.e.
q(m,v)=Q(v)
for
Q:\mathbf{R}^{4N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
a fixed non-degenerate quadratic form of even index. The higher torsions of the tube bundles
T(f)
and
T(g\oplus q)
are equal:

\tau_{k}(T(f\oplus q))=\tau_{k}(T(f))

Proof.

By the framed function Theorem
A.1
there exists a function
h:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{1}}\to\mathbf{R}
which is equal to
f
outside of a compact set and which has moderate singularities. By Proposition
3.19
we may compute the torsion of
T(f)=T(h)
using
h
. Both
\tau_{2k}(h)
and the stable bundle term
\gamma_{h}
are unchanged after stabilization, see Appendix
B
. The conclusion follows.
∎

In particular, the classes
\tau_{k}(W)
of
W=T(f)
are invariant under stabilization of
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
by the standard quadratic form
\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}
on
\mathbf{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{R}^{2}
, which has index 2. More generally, we have:

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{1}}\to\mathbf{R}
be an orientable fibered function of tube type of even index and let
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
be an orientable fibered function of rigid tube type of even index. The higher torsions of the tube bundles
T(f)
and
T(f\oplus q)
are related by the formula

\tau_{k}(T(f\oplus q))=\tau_{k}(T(f))-p_{k}(E)

where
E\to M
is the stable bundle of
q
.

Proof.

By the framed function Theorem
A.1
there exists a function
h:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{1}}\to\mathbf{R}
which is equal to
f
outside of a compact set and which has moderate singularities Note that
\gamma_{h\oplus q}=\gamma_{h}\oplus(\pi|_{\Sigma h})^{*}E
as stable real vector bundles over the fiberwise critical set
\Sigma_{h}\subset M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{1}}
and hence we have

\pi_{*}^{\Sigma h}ch_{2k}(\gamma_{h\oplus q}\otimes\mathbf{C})=\pi_{*}^{\Sigma h}ch_{2k}(\gamma_{h}\otimes\mathbf{C})+\pi_{*}^{\Sigma h}\pi^{*}ch_{2k}(E\otimes\mathbf{C}).

The proof is completed by applying Proposition
3.19
to
h
and
h\oplus q
while noting that
\pi_{*}^{\Sigma h}\pi^{*}
is multiplication by the Euler characteristic of the fibrewise Morse complex which is
+1
(since the index is even).∎

A generating function

for a Legendrian

Suppose a closed Legendrian

, and let

generates

(2)

, where

(3)

f_{t}(m,x,u,v)=f(m,x)+\varepsilon_{t}(m,x,u,v)+Q(u,v)

Proof.

This follows immediately from the general form of the homotopy lifting property for generating functions as is formulated in Theorem 4.1.1. of

. ∎

Remark 3.24
.

Note that if

3.5.
The Legendrian invariant

Let
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
be a closed Legendrian. Denote by
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
the collection of all tube bundles
W\to M
of the form

for
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
an orientable fibered function of tube type of even index which is also a generating function for
\Lambda
.

Definition 3.25
.

We define the Legendrian tube torsion of
\Lambda
, or

for short, to be the subset
\tau(\Lambda)\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R})
consisting of all
negatives
of higher torsion classes
-\tau_{k}(W)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
for
W\to M
an orientable tube bundle
W\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
.

Remark 3.26
.

The reason for defining
\tau_{k}(\Lambda)
to consist of the negatives
-\tau_{k}(W)
instead of
\tau_{k}(W)
is Proposition
3.19
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We prove one property at a time:

(1)

That the tube torsion
\tau(\Lambda)
of
\Lambda
is invariant under Legendrian isotopies of
\Lambda
follows from Lemma
3.23
and Proposition
3.20
.

(2)

That
\tau(\Lambda)
is always a (possibly empty) union of cosets of
p
follows from Remark
3.24
and Proposition
3.21
.

(3)

That the tube torsion of the zero section

is the image of

follows from Proposition
3.19
, since the parametrized Morse theory of a generating function

for the zero section is constant, so
\tau_{2k}(f)=0
, and hence the only term to contribute is

. Note that

may be any stable real vector bundle over

as any family of non-degenerate quadratic forms

,

, generates the zero-section.

∎

Proposition 3.27
.

Suppose that a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
admits a generating function of tube type. Then it admits an orientable generating function of tube type.

Proof.

Suppose that a generating function of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
for
\Lambda
is not orientable. Let
E\to M
denote the rank 1 vector bundle formed by the real degree

cohomology of the fiber
F
of the tube bundle
W\to M
, where

is the index, i.e.

for
g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
the underlying fibered function of quadratic tube type. Let
Q_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{4d}\to\mathbf{R}
,
m\in M
be a family of non-degenerate quadratic forms of even index

whose stable bundle is isomorphic to

. Put

, a fibered function of rigid tube type. Then

also generates
\Lambda
, and is orientable because for

we have isomorphisms

H^{k+r-1}(T(g\oplus q)_{m};\mathbf{R})\simeq H^{k-1}(T(g)_{m};\mathbf{R})\otimes H^{r-1}(T(q)_{m};\mathbf{R})\simeq E_{m}\otimes E_{m}

and

is orientable. Hence

is an orientable generating function of tube type for
\Lambda
. ∎

For the next result note first that if
\Lambda
admits a generating function of tube type

, then any such

automatically has moderate singularities. Recollections on functions with moderate singularities are given in Appendix
A
.

Proposition 3.28
.

Let
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
be a Legendrian whose front projection
\Lambda\hookrightarrow J^{1}M\to J^{0}M
only has cusp singularities and whose projection to the base
\Lambda\to M
is a homotopy equivalence. If
\Lambda
admits a generating function of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{1}}\to\mathbf{R}
, then for a suitable fibered function of rigid tube type
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
the direct sum
f\oplus q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{1}+4N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
is a frameable generating function of tube type , i.e. such that the stable bundle
\gamma_{f\oplus q}
is trivial.

Proof.

We have a canonical diffeomorphism
\Sigma_{f}\to\Lambda
under which the projection
\pi:\Lambda\to M
is the projection
\pi^{\Sigma f}:\Sigma_{f}\to M
. So
\pi^{\Sigma f}
is a homotopy equivalence. Hence there exists a real stable bundle
E\to M
such that
(\pi^{\Sigma f})^{*}E\simeq\gamma_{f}
.

Now let
E^{\prime}\to M
be a stable even dimensional real vector bundle such that
E\oplus E^{\prime}
is trivial. Let
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
be a fibered quadratic generating function of rigid tube type whose stable bundle is isomorphic to
E^{\prime}
. Then
\gamma_{f\oplus q}\simeq\gamma_{f}\oplus(\pi^{\Sigma f})^{*}E^{\prime}\simeq(\pi^{\Sigma f})^{*}(E\oplus E^{\prime})
is trivial. ∎

Using Proposition
3.28
we obtain in particular a result which relates
\tau(\Lambda)
to the parametrized theory of a generating function of tube type with moderate singularties

for
\Lambda
. Concretely, in Appendix

it is explained how to such an

one obtains cohomology classes
\tau_{2k}(f)\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R})
out of the parametrized Morse complex
C_{*}(f_{m};\mathbf{R})
,
m\in M
.

Proposition 3.29
.

Let
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
be a Legendrian whose front projection
\Lambda\hookrightarrow J^{1}M\to J^{0}M
only has cusp singularities and whose projection to the base
\Lambda\to M
is a homotopy equivalence. The higher torsion of
\Lambda
is the subset
\tau_{2k}(\Lambda)\subset\text{coker}(p_{k})
consisting of the cosets of
\text{im}(p_{k})
with representatives given by the cohomology classes

\tau_{2k}(f)\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R}),

where
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
ranges through the collection of orientable generating functions of tube type (of even index) for
\Lambda
.

Proof.

First we show that for any such
f\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R}
we have
\tau_{2k}(f)\in\tau_{2k}(\Lambda)
. Now, we certainly have that
W=T(f)
is in
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
and hence
-\tau_{2k}(W)\in\tau_{2k}(\Lambda)
. By Proposition
3.19
we have

-\tau_{2k}(W)=\tau_{2k}(f)+(\pi^{\Sigma f})_{*}p_{k}(\gamma_{f}).

Since
\pi^{\Sigma f}:\Sigma_{f}\to M
is a homotopy equivalence, we have
\gamma_{f}=(\pi^{\Sigma f})^{*}E
for some real vector bundle
E\to M
and hence

(\pi^{\Sigma f})_{*}p_{k}(\gamma_{f})=(\pi^{\Sigma f})_{*}(\pi^{\Sigma f})^{*}p_{k}(E)=p_{k}(E),

where the last equality follows from the fact that
(\pi^{\Sigma f})_{*}(\pi^{\Sigma f})^{*}
is multiplication by the Euler characteristic of the fiber, i.e.
+1
. Hence
\tau_{2k}(f)
is in the same coset of
\text{im}(p_{k})
as
-\tau_{k}(W)
and therefore
\tau_{2k}(f)\in\tau_{2k}(\Lambda)
as desired.

Conversely, let
W\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
. Given any generating function of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{1}}\to\mathbf{R}
for
\Lambda
with
W=T(f)
, Proposition
3.21
implies that for any fibered function of rigid tube type
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R}
the torsion class
\tau_{2k}(W)
is equal to the torsion class
\tau_{2k}(T(f\oplus q))
up to an element of
\text{im}(p_{k})
. If we take

so that
f\oplus q
is frameable as in Proposition
3.28
then
\tau_{2k}(T(f\oplus q))
and
\tau_{2k}(f\oplus q)
are equal (the extra term in Theorem
B.3
from the stable bundle vanishes since
\gamma_{f\oplus q}
is trivial). Finally
\tau_{2k}(f)=\tau_{2k}(f\oplus q)
because the fiberwise Morse theory is unchanged by stabilization (and the parity of indices is unchanged). Hence
\tau_{2k}(W)
and
\tau_{2k}(f)
are in the same coset of
\text{im}(p_{k})
and we are done. ∎

3.6.
The space of tubes

We briefly compare the model for the space of tubes considered in this article with that of Waldhausen
[
Wal82
]
. We recall that
\mathcal{T}_{N,k}
is the space of all functions
f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
of tube type, which deformation retracts onto the subspace
\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{q}
of functions
g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
of quadratic tube type.

Any tube
T\subset S^{N-1}
has two non-negative integers associated to it, namely the ambient dimension
N
, so that
T\subset S^{N-1}
is a codimension zero submanifold, and its index
k
, which is the integer
k\geq 0
such that
T\subset S^{N-1}
is isotopic to
T(q)\subset S^{N-1}
for
q:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
a non-degenerate quadratic form of index
k
. The
standard tube
of type
(N,k)
is
T_{N,k}=T(q_{k,N})=\{q_{k,N}\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1}
for
q_{k,N}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
the standard index
k
quadratic form
\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}
,
(x,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{N-k}\times\mathbf{R}^{k}
. By definition a
tube
of type
(N,k)
is a codimension zero submanifold
T\subset S^{N-1}
which is isotopic to the standard tube of type
(N,k)
. One may consider the space
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}
of all tubes of type
(N,k)
, for example as the geometric realization of a suitable simplicial space, or otherwise.

Any reasonable model for
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}
would come equipped with a map
\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{q}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}
given by
g\mapsto T(g)
whose fiber may be identified with the space of all functions
h:S^{N-1}\to\mathbf{R}
such that
T=\{h\leq 0\}
, with
\partial T=\{h=0\}
a regular level set of
h
. For fixed
T
, the space of such
h
is convex, hence contractible. Such a function
h
determines a unique function of quadratic tube type
g(v)=\|v\|^{2}h(v/\|v\|)
. Therefore, as long as
\mathcal{T}_{N,K}^{q}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}
is a Serre fibration one has a weak equivalence
\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{q}\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}
and hence also a weak equivalence
\mathcal{T}_{N,K}\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}
.

Waldhausen considered in
[
Wal82
]
the following similar though slightly different notion of tubes in terms of partitions. For
E
a
k
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbf{R}^{N}
, consider the codimension zero submanifold with boundary
T_{E}\subset\mathbf{R}^{N+1}
obtained by attaching to the half-space
\{x_{N+1}\leq 0\}
a standard
(N+1)
-dimensional index
k
handle along the unit sphere of
E\subset\{x_{N+1}=0\}
. More concretely,
T_{E}
is the union in
\mathbf{R}^{N+1}
of the half-space
\{x_{N+1}\leq 0\}
and an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the
k
-dimensional unit sphere of the
(k+1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbf{R}^{N+1}
spanned by
E\oplus 0\subset\mathbf{R}^{N+1}
and
\partial/\partial x_{N+1}
, suitably smoothed so that
T_{E}
is a smooth codimension zero submanifold with boundary. Waldhausen called such a codimension zero submanifold
T_{E}\subset\mathbf{R}^{N+1}
a
rigid tube
. Since the thickening and smoothing are homotopically canonical, for fixed
k
and
N
the space of rigid tubes
T_{E}
as above has the homotopy type of
BO(k,N)
, the Grassmannian of real
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbf{R}^{N}
.

Waldhausen defined a
tube
to be any codimension zero submanifold
T\subset\mathbf{R}^{N+1}
which is the image of a rigid tube under a smooth isotopy of
\mathbf{R}^{N+1}
, fixed outside of a compact set. We will denote the space of tubes in the sense of Waldhausen by
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}^{p}
, where
p
stands for
partition
. We may also form a suitable space of functions of tube type
f:\mathbf{R}^{N+1}\to\mathbf{R}
in the sense of Waldhausen, (so that in particular
\{f\leq 0\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{N+1}
is a tube in the sense of Waldhausen) and denote it by
\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{p}
. Any reasonable model would again come equipped with an equivalence
\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{p}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}^{p}
given by
f\mapsto\{f\leq 0\}
.

The difference between
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}^{p}
and a model for
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}
as outlined above at the
(N,k)
level amounts to requiring the tubes in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}
to go through and be standard in a fixed
(N-1)
-ball
B\subset S^{N-1}
, which yields a map
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}^{p}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}
. This map is not a homotopy equivalence, however the connectivity tends to infinity as you stabilize
N\to\infty
. Therefore we may speak unambiguously of the stable space of tubes
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
, which is the result of first stabilizing
N\to\infty
and then stabilizing
k\to\infty
, as this stable space is common to both of these models. The model
\mathcal{T}
consisting of functions of quadratic tube type which we consider in the present article was introduced in
[
AACK
]
, and concretely the stabilization is realized by a colimit of
\mathcal{T}
with respect to stabilization by a standard non-degenerate quadratic form. The idea of thinking of tubes as codimension zero submanifolds of the sphere had already been introduced in the model of Rognes in
[
R92
]
, and it is better suited for the monoid (and indeed infinite loopspace) structures at play than the partition model used by Waldhausen.

We note that given a fibered function of tube type over
M
we get an associated map
M\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
. If the fibered function is of rigid tube type then we get a lift
M\to\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
through the map
Q_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
. We may compose this with the map
\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}\to BO
determined by the equivalence
\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}\to BO(k,N)
which sends
q\mapsto E^{-}(q)\subset\mathbf{R}^{N}
; the negative eigenspace of
q
. The resulting map
M\to BO
classifies the stable bundle of the family of quadratic forms
q_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
comprising the fibered function of rigid tube type. Similarly, in the general case of a fibered function of tube type which is not necessarily quadratic, one may consider the associated map
M\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
and argue as in
[
Wal82
]
that the homotopy class of this map classifies the fibered function of tube type over
M
up to stable equivalence.

The map
\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
called the (stable)
rigid tube map
, or more informally we may denote
BO\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
. It was proved by Bökstedt that the stable rigid tube map is a rational homotopy equivalence
[
B82
]
, a fact which was also proved by the second author using higher Reidemeister torsion in
[
Igu02
]
. For example, for homotopy groups this means that

\pi_{n}\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\otimes\mathbf{Q}\simeq\pi_{n}BO\otimes\mathbf{Q}

which is
\mathbf{Q}
if
n
is of the form
4k
and is zero otherwise. For
n=4k
, modulo the torsion subgroups, we have a monomorphism of free abelian groups

\pi_{4k}BO/\text{Tors}\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_{4k}\mathcal{T}_{\infty}/\ \text{Tors}

i.e. up to
\pm 1
a monomorphism
\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{Z}
. Let
J_{k}\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
be the index of the image. Higher torsion gives an injection
\mathbf{Z}/J_{k}\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{R}/\lambda_{k}\mathbf{Z}
where
\lambda_{k}=p_{k}(E)
for
E\to S^{4k}
the generator ok
KO^{0}(S^{4k})
. So higher torsion is a subtle enough invariant to detect the failure for the rigid tube map
BO\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
to be surjective on homotopy groups modulo the torsion subgroups.

Following
[
Wal82
]
consider
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, the
stable space of h-cobordisms of a point
, denoted
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, which is a classifying space for fiber bundles
V\to M
whose fiber is an h-cobordism of a disk
D^{k}
, i.e. diffeomorphic to
D^{k}\times[0,1]
with a fixed trivialization of the lower boundary
D^{k}\times\{0\}
, up to stable equivalence. Waldhausen defined a map
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
given by stacking an h-cobordism of a disk on top of a standard tube. Consider also the forgetful map
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{\text{TOP}}
in which
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{\text{TOP}}
is the stable space of tubes in the topological category. It was shown by Waldhausen in
[
Wal82
]
that
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{\text{TOP}}\simeq BG
for
BG
the classifying space for spherical fibrations, i.e.
G=\lim_{N}G_{N}
for
G_{N}
the space of maps
S^{N}\to S^{N}
of degree
\pm 1
.

Theorem 3.30

(
[
Wal82
]
)
.

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\to BG
is a fibration sequence.

Consider the map
BG\to B(\mathbf{Z}/2)
induced by the degree map
G\to\mathbf{Z}/2
. To a tube bundle
W\to M
, with stable equivalence class classified by a map
M\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
, is associated a map
M\to B(\mathbf{Z}/2)
. This map is nullhomotopic precisely when
W
is orientable. When we defined the tube torsion
\tau(\Lambda)
of a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
we actually only included the higher torsion of orientable tube bundles on which
\Lambda
admits a generating function, so we are really using the fiber of the map
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\to B(\mathbf{Z}/2)
. Indeed, higher torsion is not defined for non-orientable tube bundles. However, the composition
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\to BG\to B(\mathbf{Z}/2)
is trivial and so tube bundles which come from h-cobordism bundles are automatically orientable.

Proposition 3.31
.

If
V\to M
is a bundle of h-cobordisms of a disk classified by
M\to\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
and
W\to M
is the stable tube bundle classified by the composition
M\to\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
, then there holds
\tau_{k}(V)=\tau_{k}(W)
.

Proof.

The tube bundle
W
is obtained by gluing
V
to the trivial tube bundle
M\times T
for
T
the standard tube. By Theorem
A.1
we may choose a function
f:V\to\mathbf{R}
with moderate singularities which is standard near the top and bottom of each h-cobordism, i.e. such that on each fiber
D^{m}\times[0,1]
the restriction of
f
is standard near top and bottom. We may glue such a function
f
to the standard quadratic form
q:M\times T\to\mathbf{R}
on the trivial tube bundle
M\times T
to obtain a function with moderate singularities
h:W\to\mathbf{R}
whose paramerized Morse theory consists of the union of that of
f
and (the constant family)
q
. The proof is then completed by applying the framing principle Theorem
B.3
to compute
\tau_{k}(W)
using the function
h:W\to\mathbf{R}
on the glued up tube bundle and to compute
\tau_{k}(V)
using the function
f:V\to\mathbf{R}
on the original bundle of h-cobordisms. The two computations yields the same expression because the extra contribution from
q
to both the parametrized Morse complex and to the stable bundle is trivial. ∎

Given a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M

3.7.
Legendrians with nontrivial tube torsion

We are now ready to establish for general
M
the existence of Legendrians
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
such that the projection
\Lambda\to M
is homotopic to a diffeomorphism and which have nontrivial tube torsion. In particular this shows that the vanishing of the tube torsion of nearby Lagrangians
L\subset T^{*}M
predicted by the nearby Lagrangian conjecture cannot rely solely on the fact that the projection
L\to M
is a homotopy equivalence (or even from a conjectural expectation that the projection
L\to M
is homotopic to a diffeomorphism, which is not known).

Recall that given a bundle of h-cobordisms of a disk
V\to M
, the stable equivalence class of the bundle is classified by the homotopy class of a map
M\to\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, see Section
3.6
. By the fibration sequence
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\to BG
of Waldhausen
[
Wal82
]
there is an associated tube bundle
W\to M
which geometrically is obtained by attaching
V
onto a trivial bundle of tubes
M\times T\to M
.

We recall that Theorem
1.14
states that given a bundle of h-cobordisms of a disk
V\to M
, there exists a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
whose projection
\Lambda\to M
is homotopic to a diffeomorphism and which admits a generating function on the tube bundle
W\to M
obtained from
V
via the map
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.14
.

We may apply Theorem
A.2
to the bundle of
h
-cobordisms
V
, where we take
A
to consist of
\partial V
and
g:Op(A)\to\mathbf{R}
to be a function on
Op(A)
without critical points for which the two components of
\partial V
(corresponding to the boundary components
D^{k}\times\{0\}
and
D^{k}\times\{1\}
of the fiber
D^{k}\times[0,1]
) are regular level sets of
g
. To verify the hypothesis of Theorem
A.2
that
d_{v}g
extends to a non-vanishing section of
\text{Vert}^{*}
we may argue as in Proposition 2.5 in
[
EM00
]
, though note the attribution there to earlier work of Douady and Laudenbach, as well as to the second author’s PhD thesis.

After applying Theorem
A.2
we conclude that there exists a fibered wrinkled function
h:V\to\mathbf{R}
such that the lower boundary
\partial_{0}V
is a regular level set for
h
. Hence
h
can be stitched up with a standard function on the trivial tube bundle
q:M\times T\to\mathbf{R}
to obtain a function with moderate singularities
h\#q:W\to\mathbf{R}
whose fiberwise critical set
\Sigma_{f}
is the disjoint union of
\Sigma_{h}
and
\Sigma_{q}
, where
\Sigma_{q}\to M
is a diffeomorphism. Hence
h\#q
generates a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
which is the disjoint union of finitely many Legendrian spheres
S_{i}\subset J^{1}M
, each diffeomorphic to the standard sphere and whose projection
S_{i}\to M
is nullhomotopic, and the zero section
M\subset J^{1}M
. Note that each
S_{i}
may sit as a complicated Legendrian knot in
J^{1}M
, and the union of the
S_{i}
as a complicated Legendrian link, but each front
S_{i}\to J^{1}M
has only cusp singularities (along an equator
C_{i}\subset S_{i}
).

Next, we note that one may construct a fibered function of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
such that
W=T(f)
and which generates the same Legendrian
\Lambda
. After a Legendrian isotopy of
\Lambda
, (which after stabilization is covered by a homotopy of functions
f
having the same properties), we may arrange it so the Legendrian
\Lambda
has cusps of any Maslov index (spin a Reidemeister I move). We can then use a surgery of corank 1 singularities as in
[
E72
]
to surger the spheres
S_{i}
into the Legendrian
\Lambda
via connect sum, again covering the deformation by a homotopy of functions
f
having the same properties, except now the components
S_{i}
have been absorbed into the graphical component of the Legendrian. Thus the diffeomorphism type of
\Lambda
is equal to the connect sum of
M
with finitely many standard spheres, so the final Legendrian
\Lambda
is diffeomorphic to
M
and moreover the projection
M\to\Lambda
is homotopic to a diffeomorphism. This concludes the proof. ∎

Remark 3.34
.

Given
g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
a function of quadratic tube type,
\nabla g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}^{N}
is homogeneous of degree 1 and has degree
\pm 1
, hence may be viewed as a map
S^{N}\to S^{N}
. This gives a map
\mathcal{T}_{k,N}\to G_{N}
for
G_{N}
the space of self-homotopy equivalences of

which stabilizes to a map

called the

. Waldhausen proved in
[
Wal82
]
that
D
factors as a composition
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\to BG\to G
and this factorization played a major role in the article
[
AACK
]
. It is not hard to modify the proof of Theorem
1.14
using the full power of Theorem 2.3 of
[
EM00
]
(rather than its special case Theorem
A.2
) to prove that the conclusion of Theorem
1.14
holds for any tube bundle
W\to M
with trivial Waldhausen derivative, i.e. for any such
W
there exists a function of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
with
W=T(f)
and which generates a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
whose projection
\Lambda\to M
is homotopic to a diffeomorphism. This result implies Theorem

because if the classifying map

of

lifts to a map

then the Waldhausen derivative

factors as a composition

and hence in particular factors through the composition of the two consecutive maps of the fibration sequence and thus is canonically nullhomotopic.

4.
Nearby Lagrangian torsion

4.1.
Tube torsion of nearby Lagrangians

Recall from Section
2.4
that for a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
to have any chance of admitting any kind of generating function, the stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
of
L
must be trivial, i.e. nullhomotopic. As was also discussed in Section
3.1
, this condition is also sufficient to ensure existence of generating functions of tube type:

Theorem 4.1

(
[
ACGK
]
)
.

Let
L\subset T^{*}M
be a nearby Lagrangian with trivial stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
. Then
L
admits a generating function of tube type
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
.

To be precise, in
[
ACGK
]
this result was proved for a slightly different notion of function of tube type, the space of which is close to the space we denoted by
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}^{p}
in Section
3.6
. In
[
AACK
]
it was shown that the statement of Theorem
4.1
implies the same result for the notion of a function of tube type considered in the present article. Strictly speaking, this is only proved for a
C^{1}
-close Hamiltonian isotopic nearby Lagrangian
L^{\prime}\subset T^{*}M
rather than for
L
itself, but one may nevertheless conclude the desired conclusion as stated above by applying the homotopy lifting property for generating functions under Legendrian isotopies.

For a nearby Lagrangian
L
with stably trivial Gauss map the tube torsion
\tau(L)
consists of all (negatives of) higher torsion classes
\tau_{2k}(W)\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R})
of orientable tube bundles
W\to M
on which
L
admits a generating function. Thus the tube torsion
\tau(L)
of a nearby Lagrangian with stably trivial Gauss map is not empty, i.e.
the tube torsion of a nearby Lagrangian with stably trivial Gauss map is well-defined
.

Let us consider therefore nearby Lagrangians with stably trivial Gauss map. After all, any nearby Lagrangian with a Gauss map that is not stably trivial would already be a counterexample to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture. For such nearby Lagrangians our previous discussion implies the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
L\subset T^{*}M
be a nearby Lagrangian whose stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
is trivial. Then the tube torsion
\tau(L)\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R})
of
L
is well-defined. It consists of a nonempty union of cosets of
\text{im}(p)
and is a Legendrian isotopy invariant of the Legendrian lift of
L
. In particular, if
\tau(L)\neq\text{im}(p)
then
L
is not Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero-section.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
4.1
. ∎

The condition that the stable Gauss map is trivial is automatically satisfied for all nearby Lagrangians if
M
has the homotopy type of a sphere, since as was also discussed in Section Section
2.4
it was also proved in
[
ACGK
]
that the stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
of a nearby Lagrangian is always zero on homotopy groups, hence automatically trivial when
M
(and hence also

4.2.
The Guillermou trick

Given a Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
, a local generating function for
\Lambda
consists of a generating function

where
U\subset M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}
is some open subset. Since
U
could be an arbitrarily small neighborhood of the fiberwise singular set of

this notion amounts to a purely linear algebraic question. Indeed, as discussed in Section

it goes back to Giroux
[

]
that the existence of a so-called local generating function is equivalent to the triviality of the stable Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
. Moreover, as is explained in
[
G90
]
and further discussed in
[
ACGK
]
, one may take as input a nullhomotopy of the stable Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
to construct such a local generating function. If one were to change the chosen null-homotopy of the stable Gauss map by concatenating with a map
\Lambda\to\Omega(U/O),
then this would correspond to performing a twisted stabilization of the local generating function by a family of quadratic forms on
\Lambda.
The negative eigenspaces of the fiberwise Hessian form a real vector bundle
E
over
\Lambda
classified by the map
\Lambda\to\mathbf{Z}\times BO
corresponding to the given map
\Lambda\to\Omega(U/O)
under the model for Bott periodicity
\Omega(U/O)\simeq\mathbf{Z}\times BO
considered in
[
G90
]
.

The construction for generating functions of tube type for nearby Lagrangians
L\subset T^{*}M
with trivial stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
takes a nullhomotopy of the Gauss map and the corresponding local generating function

where

as its starting point, and then argues as follows. Denote
\mathbf{R}^{N+1}=\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}
with
x\in\mathbf{R}
the coordinate of the second factor. By a direct sum with the function
x^{3}+3x
one may construct a local generating function for a
double
of
\Lambda
, which consists of two parallel copies of
\Lambda
in
J^{1}M
, namely
\Lambda_{0}\coprod\Lambda_{1}
for
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{\epsilon}
a shift of
\Lambda
by a small but constant amount
\epsilon\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
in the
z

J^{1}M=T^{*}M\times\mathbf{R}_{z}
. Further, by a judicious choice of cutoffs, one may arrange for the double of
\Lambda
to be generated by a global, i.e. not local, generating function
h_{s}:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N+1}\to\mathbf{R}
such that away from the critical set there holds
\partial h/\partial x\neq 0
for the distinguished coordinate
x
of
\mathbf{R}^{N+1}=\mathbf{R}^{N}\times\mathbf{R}
.

When
\Lambda
happens to be the Legendrian lift of a nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
, one may use the following remarkable trick due to Guillermou
[
Gui12
]
. Consider the family
\Lambda_{0}\coprod\Lambda_{s}
as

gets larger. Since

is an embedded Lagrangian,
\Lambda
has no Reeb chords and hence
\Lambda_{0}\coprod\Lambda_{s}\subset J^{1}M
is a Legendrian isotopy. Possibly after increasing

, by the homotopy lifting property for generating functions there continues to exists a family of functions
h_{s}:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
generating
\Lambda_{0}\coprod\Lambda_{s}
such that
\partial h_{s}/\partial x\neq 0
outside of a compact subset. When
s=T
is big enough so that there exists a value

such that the
z
-coordinate of
\Lambda
is always below

and the
z
-coordinate for
\Lambda_{T}
is always above

\{h_{T}\leq c\}\subset M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}
. It is shown in
[
ACGK
]
that the resulting fiber bundle is a bundle of tubes in a sense which is close to Waldhausen’s parition model
\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{p}
, which stably has the same homotopy type as the space of functions of tube types
\mathcal{T}_{N,k}
in the sense of the present article as discussed in Section
3.6
. The homotopy class of the resulting map
M\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
only depends on the choice of nullhomotopy of the stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
.

For
M
a smooth, connected, closed manifold, let
\mathcal{N}(M)
be the space of exact Lagrangian embeddings
\varphi:L\to T^{*}M
where the domain
L
is also assumed to be smooth, connected and closed. We also denote
\mathcal{N}^{g}(M)
the space of pairs
(\varphi,\gamma_{t})
where
\varphi\in\mathcal{N}(M)
and

is a nullhomotopy of the stable Gauss map. Concretely,

is a homotopy of polarizations between the polarizations

and
\nu|_{L}
of the symplectic vector bundle

, possibly after stabilization of

by direct sum with the trivial bundle
L\times\mathbf{C}^{m}
and stabilization of

and
\nu|_{L}
by direct sum with the trivial polarization
L\times\mathbf{R}^{m}
. We may view a path of nearby Lagrangians in

as a particular kind of nearby Lagrangian in

, and thus may apply the doubling construction to the path of Lagrangians. It follows that the doubling construction works parametrically, as long as the path of Lagrangians is covered by a homotopy of null-homotopies of the stable Gauss map, i.e. as long as we start with a path in
\mathcal{N}^{g}(M)
. It follows that the doubling construction yields a map

\pi_{0}(\mathcal{N}^{g}(M))\to[M,\mathcal{T}_{\infty}]

The monoid

(with respect to concatenation of loops in

\pi_{0}(\mathcal{N}(M))\to\text{coker}([M,BO]\to[M,\mathcal{T}_{\infty}])

Proposition 4.4

Let
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M

is that the Cerf diagram of such a generating function, i.e. the subset

The front projection of a nearby Lagrangian

It is plausible that this strong constraint on the front

Question 4.11

Let

In the future work

4.4.
Nearby Lagrangian homotopy spheres

We now discuss the special case of nearby Lagrangian homotopy spheres. First, note that for
L\subset T^{*}M
a nearby Lagrangian, the projection

is a homotopy equivalence and hence if

and hence when

Theorem 4.12

(
[
AD22
]
)
.

If

is Hamiltonian isotopic to a nearby Lagrangian homotopy sphere

Hence for nearby Lagrangian homotopy spheres
L\subset T^{*}M

has moderate tangencies, so in particular as discussed in Section

for any generating function

.

Let us put

for concreteness, and note that unless

all higher torsion classes of tube bundles are trivial. Note that any tube bundle over

(2)

Thus one would obtain a well-defined coset of

Conjecture 4.15

This coset is the same as the nearby Lagrangian torsion, i.e.

It would be interesting to know if there is a Floer theoretic proof of the triviality of nearby Lagrangian torsion, as predicted by the nearby Lagrangian conjecture. Indeed, one might expect the Floer theoretic setup to be more sensitive to the property of being an exact Lagrangian submanifold than the Morse theoretic approach via generating functions.
In the Floer set-up, the embeddedness implies there are no pseudo-holomorphic disks with a single boundary puncture that arise in as limiting-bubbles in Gromov compactness. Algebraically, this enables one to avoid relying on so-called augmentations from Legendrian/symplectic field theory.
In contrast, it is far from clear how the lack of Reeb chords is related to the paramerized Morse theory of a generating function, which after all is a fiberwise phenomenon.
Indeed, the Morse-theoretic theory of higher torsion via generating functions makes sense for Legendrians in

Appendix A
Moderate singularities

A.1.
Functions with moderate singularities

For
F
a smooth manifold, a function
f:F\to\mathbf{R}
has
moderate singularities
if its singularities are modeled on either the Morse normal form

\sum_{i\leq k}x_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i>k}x_{i}^{2}

or on the cubic (birth/death) form

x_{1}^{3}+\sum_{1<i\leq k}x_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i>k}x_{i}^{2}.

Note that at a birth/death point of
f
, the kernel of the hessian
d^{2}f
is one dimensional and is trivialized by the direction in which
d^{3}f
is positive. We call this the
canonical co-orientation
.

For
F\to W\to M
a fiber bundle, a function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
has
moderate singularities
if each restriction
f|_{m}:F_{m}\to\mathbf{R}
to the fiber over
m\in M
has moderate singularities and if moreover the fibered singularities of
f
over
M

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\times\mathbf{R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

are modeled on either a constant family of Morse critical points

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\mapsto(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},\sum_{i\leq k}x_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i>k}x_{i}^{2})

or a fibered birth/death of Morse critical points

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\mapsto(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},x_{1}^{3}+3t_{1}x_{1}+\sum_{1<i\leq k}x_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i>k}x_{i}^{2})

with both families fibered over
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
with respect to the obvious projection. If
M
has boundary we will allow one-half of the above fibered models, i.e. restricting the fibration to a half space of the base.

A.2.
The stable bundle

Let
F\to W\to M
be a fiber bundle and let
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
be a function with moderate singularities. The fiberwise singular set
\Sigma_{f}\subset W
is a smooth submanifold of
W
of the same dimension as
M
. It has a stratification
\Sigma_{f}=\bigcup_{k}\Sigma_{f,k}
where
\Sigma_{f,k}
is the closure of the set of fiberwise Morse critical points of index
k
. Let
C_{f,k}\subset\Lambda_{f}
consist of the set of fiberwise cubic (birth/death) critical points of
f
of index
k
and
k+1
. So the boundary of
\Lambda_{f,k}
consists of the disjoint union of
C_{f,k-1}
and
C_{f,k}
.

On the interior of
\Sigma_{f,k}
we have a rank
k
real vector bundle
\gamma_{f,k}\to\Sigma_{f,k}
consisting of the negative eigenspace of the fiberwise Hessian along the fiberwise critical locus. Each
\gamma_{f,k}
extends to the boundary
C_{f,k}\coprod C_{f,k-1}
of
\Sigma_{f,k}
as sub-bundles of the fiberwise tangent bundle of
W
, but along
C_{f,k}
the dimensions of
\gamma_{f,k}
and
\gamma_{f,k+1}
jump by one. More precisely, we have the relation
\gamma_{f,k}\oplus\nu_{k}=\gamma_{f,k+1}
where
\nu_{k}
is the kernel of the Hessian, a rank 1 bundle along
C_{f,k}
. So we may form a vector bundle over
\Sigma_{f}=\bigcup_{k}\Sigma_{f,k}
by using the canonical co-orientation to glue the vector bundles
\gamma_{f,k}\oplus\varepsilon^{N-k}
on
\Sigma_{f,k}
, where
\varepsilon
denotes the trivial rank 1 bundle, to a rank
N
vector bundle on
\Sigma_{f}
. More precisely, the canonical co-orientation gives a trivialization
\nu_{k}\simeq\varepsilon
and so we may glue via the vector bundle isomorphism over
C_{f,k}
given by

\gamma_{f,k}\oplus\varepsilon^{N-k}\simeq\gamma_{f,k}\oplus\nu_{k}\oplus\varepsilon^{N-k-1}\simeq\gamma_{f,k+1}\oplus\varepsilon^{N-k-1}.

We denote the stable class of this real vector bundle over
\Sigma_{f}
by
\gamma_{f}
and will refer to it as the
stable bundle
of
f
.

A.3.
Framed functions

For
F\to W\to M
a fiber bundle, a framed function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
is roughly speaking a function with moderate singularities whose stable bundle
\gamma_{f}
is trivial and moreover equipped with a trivialization. More precisely, one requires the existence of isomorphisms

\varphi_{k}:\gamma_{f,k}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\varepsilon^{k}

which are compatible with the above gluing maps. We will refer to the following result as the
framed function theorem
.

Theorem A.1
.

For
F\to W\to M
a fiber bundle, given a closed subset
A\subset W
and a framed function
g:Op(A)\to\mathbf{R}
there exists a framed function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
which agrees with
g
on
Op(A)
.

In particular, for any fiber bundle
F\to W\to M
framed functions
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
exist, and if
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are two such then there exists a framed function
F:W\times[0,1]\to\mathbf{R}
on the product fiber bundle
F\times[0,1]\to W\times[0,1]\to M\times[0,1]
such that
F|_{W\times 0}=f_{0}
and
F_{W\times 1}=f_{1}
.

Theorem
A.1
in particular implies, and is essentially equivalent to, the fact that a suitable model for the
space of framed functions
on the fiber
h:F\to\mathbf{R}
is contractible, even in a strong relative form. The high connectivity of the space of framed functions was first established by work of the second author
[
Igu87
]
. The strongest form is given by the Extension Theorem 1.1.1 of
[
EM12
]
, slightly reformulated as Theorem
A.1
above. The fact that framed functions may be chosen and are independent of choices is central to the definition of higher torsion invariants.

We note the inevitability of relying on some result in the theory of simplification of singularities to even establish the existence of functions
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
with moderate singularities. This existence is not a priori guaranteed because the condition of having only moderate singularities, though
C^{3}
-stable, is far from
C^{\infty}
-generic in general. Indeed,
C^{\infty}
-generic functions
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
will have terrible and completely unclassifiable fiberwise singularities.

A.4.
Wrinkling

The proof of the framed function theorem

given in
[

]
uses the

For
F\to W\to M
a fiber bundle, a

consists of a disjoint union of contractible spheres

to a sufficiently small open neighborhood of each of the spheres

(y,z,x)\mapsto\left(y,z^{3}+3(\|y\|^{2}-1)z-\sum_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{n-q}x_{j}^{2}\right)

y\in\mathbf{R}^{q-1}
,

,

, which has singularity
S^{q-1}=S^{q-1}\times\{0\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{q}\times\mathbf{R}^{n-q}
, with equator of cusps
\{\|y\|=1,z=0,x=0\}
. We emphasize that we only require an equivalence between the restriction of
f
to
Op(S)
and the restriction of the normal form to
Op(S^{q-1})
. Here and below we use the Gromov notation to denote

an arbitrarily small but unspecified open neighborhood of

of an open neighborhood of

Note that each such sphere

(i.e. the pair is diffeomorphic to

, where

), and that

(of Morse indices

and
k+1
for some

.

If
F\to W\to M
is a fiber bundle of manifolds, we denote

the vector sub-bundle of

Theorem A.2

(
[

]

Let

Appendix B
Higher torsion of fiber bundles

B.1.
Higher torsion classes

Higher torsion invariants
are characteristic classes of smooth fiber bundles
F\to W\to M
of smooth manifolds, defined under suitable conditions. The theory has analytic treatments
[
BL95
,
BG01
]
, homotopy-theoretic treatments
[
DWW
]
and axiomatic treatments
[
Igu08
]
, in addition to the Morse-theoretic perspective we adopt in the present article, following the book
[
Igu02
]
.

From the Morse-theoretic viewpoint, a fiber bundle may be studied by considering smooth functions
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
and analyzing the parametrized Morse theory of the restrictions

f_{m}:=f|_{F_{m}}:F_{m}\to\mathbf{R}

of
f
to the fiber, parametrized by
m\in M
. When
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
has moderate singularities in the sense of Appendix
A
, after fixing a generic fiberwise metric one may construct the family of Morse chain complexes
C^{*}(f_{m}:\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W])
where we may take the coefficients in
\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W]
. In this appendix we briefly recall how this data may be used to yield the higher torsion classes.

B.2.
The Whitehead category

The natural home for the parametrized family of Morse chain complexes is the
Whitehead category
. For
R
an associative ring and
G
a subgroup of the units of
R
, there is a simplicial category
\text{Wh}_{\bullet}(R,G)
, called the
Whitehead category
, whose objects in degree
k
are
\Delta^{k}
-families of filtered finite free chain complexes over
R
with the basis only well-defined up to permutation and elements of
G
, i.e., up to monomial change of basis
[
Igu05
, Def 2.7, Def 2.16]
. We add a category direction giving elementary expansions of the chain complex. This comes from the birth-death points in the family of generalized Morse functions.

Given a bundle of smooth manifolds
F\to W\to M
, a fiberwise generalized Morse function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
, and a generic choice of vertical metric, the data
C^{*}(f_{m}:\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W])
,
m\in M
, amounts to a map

M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W],\pi_{1}W)|.

If the action of
\pi_{1}M
on
H_{*}(F_{m};R)
is trivial, then a fiberwise mapping cone construction will make each fiber complex
C^{*}(f_{m}:\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W])
contractible and thus gives a mapping

\xi:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W],\pi_{1}W)|

well-defined up to homotopy, where
\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W],\pi_{1}W)
is the simplicial full subcategory of
\text{Wh}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W],\pi_{1}W)
whose objects in degree
k
are
\Delta^{k}
-families of the acyclic chain complexes. The map
\xi
comes from a simplicial map

\xi_{\bullet}:simp\,M\to\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W],\pi_{1}W)

taking (small) smooth
k
-simplices in
M
to objects of
\text{Wh}_{k}^{h}(\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W],\pi_{1}W)
. When
R=\mathbf{Q}
or
\mathbf{C}
, the construction of higher torsion extends to the case then
\pi_{1}M

acts unipotently on the fiber homology
H_{*}(F_{m};R)
, by which we mean that the
\pi_{1}M
-module
H_{*}(F_{m};R)
admits a filtration by
\pi_{1}M
submodules so that the action of
\pi_{1}M
on the successive subquotients is trivial.

At this point we are very close to defining higher torsion. Indeed we have

\text{Wh}_{1}(G)=\pi_{0}\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{Z}[G],G)

for the classical Whitehead group
\text{Wh}_{1}(G)
, so
|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{Z}[G],G)|
may be thought of as a space-level generalization of Whitehead torsion. More precisely, it was shown by the second author and J. Klein that there is a homotopy fiber sequence

|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(R,G)|\to\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{\infty}(BG_{+})\to\ \mathbf{Z}\times BGL(R)^{+}.

For example, if
G
is finite then
\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{\infty}(BG_{+})
is rationally trivial above degree zero and hence
|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(R,G)|
has the same rational homotopy type as
\Omega BGL(R)^{+}
. If
R=\mathbf{C}
and
K(\mathbf{C})=\mathbf{Z}\times BGL(\mathbf{C})^{+}
, we get a rational equivalence

|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|\simeq_{\mathbf{Q}}\Omega K(\mathbf{C}).

We use the Borel regulator maps

b_{k}:K_{2k+1}\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{R}.

These are given by cap product with continuous cohomology classes
\mu_{k}\in H_{c}^{2k+1}(GL(\mathbf{C}),\mathbf{R})
:

K_{2k+1}(\mathbf{C})\cong\pi_{2k+1}BGL(\mathbf{C})^{+}\xrightarrow{h}H_{2k+1}BGL(\mathbf{C})\xrightarrow{\cap\mu_{k}}\mathbf{R}

where
h
is the Hurewicz map on
BGL(\mathbf{C})^{+}
, a space with the same homology as
BGL(\mathbf{C})
.
See
[
Ra
]
. Since
|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
is rationally equivalent to
\Omega K(\mathbf{C})
, we are able to obtain universal higher torsion invariants

\tau_{k}\in H^{2k}(|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|;\mathbf{R}).

These universal classes will be used to define higher torsion. The explicit cohomology class used in
[
Igu02
]
comes from the Dupont formulation of the Kamber-Tondeur form
[
D76
]
.

B.3.
Definition of higher torsion

Consider again a fiber bundle
F\to W\to M
, where we now assume that
\pi_{1}M
acts unipotently on the rational fiber homology
H_{*}(F_{b};\mathbf{Q})
. We would like to define cohomology classes in
H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
by pulling back the class
\tau_{k}
via the map

\xi:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|

given by a choice of fiberwise (generalized) Morse function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
and choice of metric.
As explained above,
\tau_{k}
is the restriction to
|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
of the universal higher torsion class
\tau_{k}\in H^{2k}(\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},U(1));\mathbf{R})
given in
[
Igu05
, subsection 2.7]
and
\xi
is the “expansion functor”, see
[
Igu05
, Def 2.18]
.

At this point questions of existence and uniqueness of such
f
arise. Although it is a (nontrivial) fact that fiberwise generalized Morse functions exist on any fiber bundle, it is not true that any two such functions will be joined by a family of such functions. Indeed, the map
\xi:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
obtained by the above construction may not be unique up to homotopy.

There is a canonical way to select the
correct
map
M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
by means of a
framed function
. For any fiber bundle
F\to W\to M
, and any two framed functions
f_{0},f_{1}:W\to\mathbf{R}
, by Theorem
A.1
there exists a framed function on the product bundle
W\times[0,1]\to M\times[0,1]
which restricts to
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
over
M\times\{0\}
and
M\times\{1\}
respectively. This gives a map

M\times[0,1]\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|

which may be viewed as a homotopy between the maps
M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
given by
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
. Hence by choosing a framed function on
W
we get a well-defined and distinguished homotopy class of map

\xi_{0}:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|.

Definition B.1
.

Assume that
\pi_{1}M
acts unipotently on
H_{*}(F_{b};\mathbf{Q})
. The
higher torsion classes

\tau_{k}(W)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
are defined as the pullback of the universal classes
\tau_{k}\in H^{2k}(|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|;\mathbf{R})
under the homotopically unique map
\xi_{0}:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
determined by a framed function on
W
.

Example B.2
.

The degree zero class
\tau_{0}(W,\rho)\in H^{0}(M;\mathbf{R})
is just the usual Franz-Reidemeister torsion of the fiber twisted by the local system
\rho|_{\pi_{1}F}
, maybe after taking absolute value and natural logarithm depending on conventions
[
Fra35
,
Rei35
]
.

B.4.
Higher Reidemeister torsion with twisted coefficients

We can generalize the above discussion to fiber bundles
F\to W\to M
equipped with a rank 1 unitary local system
\rho:\pi_{1}M\to U(1)
with finite image
G
such that the action of
\pi_{1}M
on the twisted fiber homology
H_{*}(F_{m};\mathbf{C}^{\rho})
is unipotent. The framed function theorem still gives us a well-defined map (up to homotopy)

\xi_{\rho}:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G)|

and we get classes
\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R})
by pulling back the universal classes. In fact, if real coefficients are used instead of complex coefficients, then the odd classes automatically vanish, i.e. the pullback of
\tau_{2k+1}
to
|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{R},G)|
is zero,
G=\{\pm 1\}
. So to get nontrivial odd torsion classes
\tau_{2k+1}(W,\rho)\in H^{4k+2}(M;\mathbf{R})
one needs to use a nontrivial unitary local system.

The use of unitary local systems can be convenient depending on the application, for example in
[
AI
]
the higher Reidemeister torsion of circle bundles over the sphere is shown to compute a stable version of the Euler number. However, for the specific version of higher torsion we will focus on in the present article there is no local system. Indeed we will use
\mathbf{Z}
coefficients, and so we will only get the even classes
\tau_{2k}
in
H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R})
.

B.5.
The framing principle

A useful result for computations of higher torsion is the
framing principle
, which gives the precise correction term needed to compute the higher Reidemeister torsion for a function
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
with moderate singularities, even if the stable bundle
E
is not trivial (and hence
f
is not frameable). The formula
[
Igu02
, Theorem 4.11]
is the following.

Theorem B.3
.

If
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
is a function with moderate singularities, then

(1)

\tau(W,\rho)=\sum_{j}\tau_{j}(f,\rho)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k+1)\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}\left(ch_{2k}(\gamma_{f}\otimes\mathbf{C})\right)

where the terms are defined below.

•

\tau_{j}(f,\rho)\in H^{2j}(M;\mathbf{R})
is the pull-back of the universal class
\tau_{j}
under
M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G)|
, the mapping given by the family of Morse chain complexes associated to the family of functions
F_{m}
,
,m\in M
. We are assuming that
\rho
has a finite image
G\subset U(1)
.

•

\Sigma_{f}
is the fiberwise singular set of
f
. It has the same dimension as the base
M
and is a union
\Sigma_{f}=\bigcup\Sigma_{f,k}
as discussed in Appendix
A
.

•

\gamma_{f}
is the stable bundle of
f
as also discussed in Appendix
A
.

•

The map
\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}:H^{4k}(\Sigma)\to H^{4k}(M)
is, at the cochain level, the alternating sum of push-down operators:

\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\pi_{\ast}:C^{4k}(\Sigma_{f,i})\to C^{4k}(M).

Explicitly, given a
4k
cocycle
\varphi
on
\Sigma_{f}
we take the restriction
\varphi_{i}
of
\varphi
on each
\Sigma_{f,i}
. This is well-defined away from the boundary of
\Sigma_{f,i}
. The image of this in
C^{4k}(M)
is the
4k
-cocycle which takes a small
4k
simplex
\sigma
in
M
, takes all sections
\tilde{\sigma}_{i}
of
\sigma
to
\Sigma_{f,i}
and evaluates

\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}(\varphi)(\sigma)=\sum(-1)^{i}\varphi_{i}(\tilde{\sigma}_{i}).

The undefined terms cancel since, when
\tilde{\sigma}_{i}
reaches the boundary of
\Sigma_{f,i}
, it is at a cusp which is the same location as a section
\tilde{\sigma}_{i+1}
(or
\tilde{\sigma}_{i-1}
). The alternating signs makes these undefined terms equal with opposite sign. So,
\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}
is well-defined. See
[
Igu02
, Proposition 4.8]
for more details of this argument. It is shown there that
\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}
is a related to the Becker-Gottlieb transfer
\text{tr}_{M}^{W}:H^{\ast}(W)\to H^{\ast}(M)
by
\text{tr}_{M}^{W}=\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}\circ i^{\ast}
where
i^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(W)\to H^{\ast}(\Sigma_{f})
is the restriction map induced by the inclusion
i:\Sigma_{f}\to W
.

Note that the correction term in (
1
) is nonzero only in degrees
4k
. In degrees
2j=4k+2
, when
j
is odd, there is no correction term (i.e. the correction term is zero.)

Also note that the term
\sum_{j}\tau_{j}(f)
changes by a sign if the indices of the fiberwise Morse chain complexes are all shifted by one in either direction. More precisely, the index shift operator on
\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G)
induces map
|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G)|\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G)|
which sends
\tau_{j}
to
-\tau_{j}

[
Igu05
, Theorem 3.10]
. However, in this paper we are always suspending an even number of times. So there is no change of sign.

To summarize:

We can use the family of Morse chain complexes
C(f_{m})
,
m\in M
of a function with moderate singularities
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
to compute the higher torsion of
W
over
M
if we add correction terms in degree
4k
given by a suitable multiple of the push-down to
H^{\ast}(M)
of the Pontryagin character of the stable bundle
\gamma_{f}
over
\Sigma_{f}
.

B.6.
Fibers with boundary

Higher torsion invariants may also be defined for fiber bundles
F\to W\to M
where the fiber

is a compact manifold with boundary. In the

case we just use functions

with moderate singularities such that for each fiber

we have that
\partial F

df(v)>0
for outwards pointing tangent vectors

along
\partial F
. However it is also convenient to consider
relative
versions in which the fiberwise homology of the function computes a relative homology group. As this relative case is directly relevant to the present article, we discuss some examples below.

For example, the construction may be applied in the case in which the fiber
F
is a cobordism on a manifold
X
, so that
\partial F=\partial_{0}F\coprod\partial_{1}F
and we have an identification
\partial_{0}F\simeq X
. More precisely, this means that
\partial W=\partial_{0}W\coprod\partial_{1}W
and we have a trivialization of the fiber bundle
\partial_{0}W\to M
, i.e. a fibered diffeomorphism
\partial_{0}W\simeq M\times X
. In this case higher torsion is defined using functions
(W,\partial_{0}W,\partial_{1}W)\to([0,1],0,1)
with moderate singularities and yields an invariant of such fiber bundles
W
equipped with a trivialization
\partial_{0}W\simeq M\times X
, or more generally such that
df(v)>0
for outwards pointing tangent vectors
v
along
\partial_{1}F
and
df(v)>0
for inwards pointing tangent vectors
v
along
\partial_{0}F
.

The fiber
F
could be also be a compact manifold with corners, thought of as a cobordism on a compact manifold with boundary (or even corners)
X
. In this case the boundary
\partial F
is divided into a
horizontal
boundary
\partial_{h}F
and a
vertical boundary

\partial_{v}F
, with
\partial_{v}F\simeq\partial_{0}F\coprod\partial_{1}F
and an identification
\partial_{0}F\simeq X
as before. The boundary
\partial W
of such a fiber bundle
F\to W\to M
is similarly decomposed, and such fiber bundles should be equipped with a fibered diffeomorphism
\partial_{0}W\simeq M\times X
. The torsion is again defined using functions
(W,\partial_{0}W,\partial_{1}W)\to([0,1],0,1)
with moderate singularities, or more generally such that
df(v)>0
for outwards pointing tangent vectors
v
along
\partial_{1}F
and
df(v)>0
for inwards pointing tangent vectors
v
along
\partial_{0}F
.

Finally, yet another variant is the case in which
F
is a compact manifold with boundary and
\partial_{0}F\subset\partial F
is a codimension zero submanifold, identified with some fixed compact manifold with boundary
X
. Such bundles
F\to W\to M
come equipped with a trivialization of the sub-bundle
\partial_{0}W\subset\partial W
, i.e. a fibered diffeomorphism
\partial_{0}W\simeq M\times X
. The torsion is computed using functions with moderate singularities
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
which have
\partial_{0}W
as a regular level set of
f|_{\partial W}
, such that inwards pointing vectors
v
along the interior of
\partial_{0}F
satisfy
df(v)>0
and such that outwards pointing vectors
v
along
\partial F\setminus\partial_{0}F
satisfy
df(v)>0
(so the fiberwise Morse homology computes
H_{*}(F;\partial_{0}F)
as before).

In any of these cases, we speak of the torsion of the
bundle pair

(W,\partial_{0}W)
and there is a simple formula relating the higher torsion of
W
, of
\partial_{0}W
and of the pair
(W,\partial_{0}W)
. Indeed, from Corollary 3.18 on page 39 in
[
Igu05
]
we have the following result:

Theorem B.4

(
[
Igu05
]
)
.

For a bundle pair
(W,\partial_{0}W)
we have

\tau_{k}(W)=\tau_{k}(W,\partial_{0}W)+\tau_{k}(\partial_{0}W)

B.7.
Noncompact fibers

The same construction works if the fiber
F
is noncompact and the bundle
W\to M
is equipped with a reference fibration at infinity
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
. In this case
\tau_{k}(W,\rho)
depends on the choice of fibration at infinity, though this may be omitted from the notation if there is no danger of confusion. For example, one could define and compute higher torsion using functions with moderate singularities which are homotopic through fibrations at infinity to our reference fibration at infinity. The fiberwise Morse homology is computing
H_{*}(F,\partial_{0}F)
where
\partial_{0}F
is a sublevel set
f|_{F}<-c
for
c>0
very large, so this version should also be thought of as a relative form of higher torsion.

In certain applications the bundle
W
is assumed to be trivialized outside of a compact set and it makes sense to demand that the admissible functions
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
agree with some fixed fibration at infinity outside of this compact set. With such a restriction imposed, the class
\tau_{k}(W,\rho)
does not depend on the choice of admissible function since deformations of admissible functions have compact support.

A very precise formulation of the restriction to compact sets is given in
[
Igu02
, Lemma 3.20]
where singularities of a family of functions are confined to a family of “convex sets” which are basically cobordisms embedded in the fiber. The family of convex sets gives a family of chain complexes which embeds in the chain complex of the entire fiber in the case when the fiber is compact. In general, we can define the family of total chain complexes to be that given by any family of convex sets which contains all the critical points of the function. By
[
Igu02
, Lemma 3.20]
, these all give the same chain complex. So, the family of chain complexes and thus the higher torsion is well-defined. The fiber also has a well-defined Euler characteristic
\chi(F)
given by this finite chain complex. In the case of functions of tube type we have
\chi(F)=\pm 1
.

In any event, higher Reidemeister torsion is a
stable invariant
, which means that if
W
is replaced by the fiberwise stabilization
W\times\mathbf{R}^{4}
and the function with mild singularities
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
is replaced with
f(w)+||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}
for
(w,x,y)\in W\times\mathbf{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{R}^{2}
, then the homotopy class of the map
M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
and sign of the fiber Euler characteristic
\chi(F)
will remain unchanged.

Furthermore, by the framing principle, if
f:W\to\mathbf{R}
is replaced by
f\oplus q:W\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
, where
f\oplus q(w,v)=f(w)+q_{\pi(w)}(v)
for
q_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
a family of non-degenerate quadratic forms on
\mathbf{R}^{N}
of even index parametrized by
m\in M
, then the homotopy class of the map
M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
changes but in a controlled way: the pullback of the universal class
\tau_{k}
changes precisely by adding
\chi(F)
times a (certain multiple) of the Pontraygin character of the stable bundle of the family
q_{m}
,
m\in M
, i.e. the stable vector bundle formed by the negative eigenspaces of
q_{m}
. The
\chi(F)
factor comes from the fact that the composition of
p^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(M)\to H^{\ast}(W)
with the Becker-Gottlieb transfer
tr_{M}^{W}:H^{\ast}(W)\to H^{\ast}(M)
is multiplication by
\chi(F)
.
