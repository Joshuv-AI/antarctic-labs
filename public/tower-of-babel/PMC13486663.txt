Amphibian-inspired neuromorphic dynamic vision systems based on ferroelectric field-effect transistor
Authors: Yongbiao Zhai, Peijie Chen, Ying Luo, Ziyu Lv, Guanglong Ding, Junjie Yang, Minglin Zheng, Ye Zhou, Yang Chai, Su-Ting Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The dynamic vision sensor captures visual information as discrete events, enabling high-speed imaging with reduced data redundancy, but is limited by lack of color sensitivity, a speed-noise trade-off, and inefficient data transfer. Here we show an amphibian-inspired dynamic vision system (ADVS) based on ferroelectric field-effect transistors that emulates the hierarchical functions of amphibian retinas, including spectral perception, spatial preprocessing, and event-driven neural encoding. The ferroelectric transistors exhibit broadband photosensitivity (365–637 nm) and bidirectional photoresponses, enabling multichannel spectral recognition from ultraviolet to visible light. Device arrays further reproduce center–surround receptive-field-like processing, enhancing spatial contrast while suppressing background noise under weak illumination. Owing to the steep switching characteristics (SSmin = 53.8 mV dec−1) of the transistors, the system also supports microsecond-scale event-driven spiking responses. Combined with bioinspired hierarchical preprocessing framework and event-driven convolutional neural network, the ADVS achieves 96.5% accuracy in dynamic facial expression recognition and real-time multi-agent trajectory prediction with <5% error.

Introduction

The dynamic vision sensor (DVS), also known as an event-based visual sensor, has garnered increasing attention for its ability to capture visual information as a stream of discrete events rather than conventional image frames1,2. In contrast to traditional frame-based vision sensors (FVS), the DVS offers significant advantages in high-speed imaging by focusing solely on dynamic content while effectively filtering out static background. Its temporal resolution is determined by event latency, typically ranging from a few to hundreds of microseconds, enabling it to capture fast movements that would otherwise require expensive FVS operating at thousands of frames per second3–6. Despite the significantly reduced data redundancy for real-time, high-speed monitoring applications, current DVS architectures face three fundamental limitations: a lack of intrinsic spectral selectivity7,8, an inherent trade-off between noise and temporal resolution9,10, and reliance on off-chip data transfer between sensing, memory, and processing units11–16, collectively hindering the realization of efficient and fully integrated event-driven vision systems.

In contrast to the existing DVS system, amphibians, such as frogs, have evolved highly specialized visual systems over hundreds of millions of years, providing exceptional capabilities in motion detection and trajectory prediction (Fig. 1a) that inspire the design of a bioinspired neuromorphic DVS system. Their retina adopts a multilayered preprocessing architecture, where each layer of neurons from photoreceptors to ganglion cells (Fig. 1b) executes distinct yet coordinated visual computations, enabling efficient encoding of complex stimuli with minimal redundancy17–20. First, amphibians exhibit tetrachromatic vision with photoreceptors sensitive to UV, blue, green, and red wavelengths (Fig. 1c), providing robust broadband spectral perception beyond the visible range. Second, their photoreceptors display a visual persistence effect—delayed decay of photoresponses—which supports temporal integration of transient stimuli (Fig. 1d). Third, bipolar cells implement center–surround contrast enhancement, suppressing background signals while emphasizing local changes (Fig. 1e). Finally, retinal ganglion cells generate sparse, event-driven outputs with ultra-low latency, responding only to meaningful variations in light intensity (Fig. 1f). Collectively, these biological hierarchical strategies provide a compelling blueprint for neuromorphic DVS system that combine ultra-low-latency event-driven processing, broadband photoresponse with multi-channel spectral encoding, center–surround dynamics for enhanced contrast sensitivity, and visual persistence effect. Ferroelectric polarization reversal offers a powerful strategy to implement device-level analogues of key functionalities observed in amphibian visual systems. (i) Its intrinsic nonvolatile electrostatic modulation enables analog signal retention after stimulus removal, closely resembling the visual persistence characteristic of amphibian vision21,22. (ii) The bidirectional, reversible modulation of ferroelectric polarization via electrical/optical stimuli provides a robust physical basis for center–surround contrast enhancement analogous to that observed in bipolar cells23,24. (iii) By engineering heterostructures that integrate ferroelectric materials with broadband-responsive two-dimensional (2D) semiconductors, spectral responsivity can be extended from the visible to UV ranges, enabling complementary visible–UV sensing that improves environmental adaptability and perceptual robustness25–28. Importantly, these functionalities originate from a unified ferroelectric polarization–built-in-field coupling mechanism that simultaneously governs photogenerated carrier dynamics and field-reconfigurable transport within the vertical ferroelectric heterostructure. Furthermore, strong interfacial coupling between ferroelectrics and 2D semiconductors further gives rise to unconventional switching phenomena, which substantially reduce the subthreshold swing (SS) and enable abrupt ON/OFF transition. Upon changes in light stimuli, the ultrafast and threshold-sensitive switching dynamics facilitate the generation of sharp rising and falling edges, thereby producing temporally confined event pulses with low latency and narrow pulse widths. These characteristics provide a robust physical foundation for implementing neuromorphic DVS with minimal energy consumption and rapid response time29–31. This convergence of material functionality and biological principles lays the foundation for energy-efficient and multi-functional neuromorphic DVS architectures.

In this study, we present an amphibian-inspired neuromorphic dynamic vision system (ADVS), in which device-level functional analogues of photoreceptors, bipolar cells, and ganglion cells are implemented using MoS2/h-BN/α-In2Se3/h-BN-based FeFET to overcome the key limitations of conventional DVS, including monochromatic imaging, the intrinsic trade-off between speed and noise, and the memory-computation separation issue. The broadband photosensitivity spanning 365-637 nm, along with nonvolatile and bidirectional photoconductivity of FeFET, enables photoreceptor-like multi-channel spectral encoding and a functional analogue of bipolar-cell-like center–surround receptive fields, improving color discrimination and noise suppression. Concurrently, steep-slope switching behavior (SSmin = 53.8 mV dec−1) is attributed to a precise electrostatic modulation of the 2D semiconductor channel, which is ideal for constructing dynamic event-driven circuits to mimic ganglion cells with an ultrafast response latency of ~3 μs. These metrics are not only comparable to, but even surpass the performance of several state-of-the-art commercial DVSs. Each cell of ADVS, consisting of a 3 × 3 FeFET photodetector array, a bipolar-processing node and a ganglion-like spiking circuit, interprets visual stimuli into multiple visual primitives—including color, contrast and spatial difference—and allocates them to this processing hierarchy. This ADVS generates discrete, time-resolved spikes in response to transient changes, selectively enhancing local contrast, suppressing background redundancy, and enabling real-time detection of moving objects under low-latency and low-power conditions. Finally, leveraging this architecture, ADVS efficiently preprocesses dynamic visual inputs and streams event-based spikes to an event-driven convolutional neural network (ED-CNN), achieving 96.5% accuracy in dynamic facial expression recognition and enabling real-time trajectory prediction of vehicles and pedestrians with <5% error. These results highlight the potential of ADVS for low-latency, energy-efficient, and integrated neuromorphic vision.

Results

Design of ferroelectric field effect neuromorphic device

Drawing inspiration from the visual systems of amphibians, we develop an artificial retinomorphic optoelectronic device based on a MoS2/h-BN/α-In2Se3/h-BN FeFET (Fig. 1k). This device integrates broadband photosensing and nonvolatile bidirectional optical signal memory within a single unit (Fig. 1g–i), thus providing a unified platform to reproduce key functional characteristics associated with tetrachromatic photoreception and center–surround receptive-field processing (Fig. 1l). In this configuration, the 2D semiconductor MoS2 exhibits strong light-matter interactions and broadband photoresponse ranging from UV to red light (Supplementary Fig. 1), enabling the emulation of tetrachromatic vision of amphibians. Concurrently, the ferroelectric α-In2Se3 functions both as a potential well for trapping photogenerated carriers and a nonvolatile modulator of channel conductance through ferroelectric electrostatic coupling. Under downward polarization (Pdown), photogenerated holes tunnel and become trapped in α-In2Se3 while electrons accumulate in the MoS2 channel, resulting in positive photoconductivity (PPC). Conversely, with initial upward polarization (Pup), optical stimulation induces a polarization reversal from Pup to Pdown. This reversal alters the interfacial electrostatic environment via bound negative charges at the ferroelectric upper surface, thereby giving rise to negative photoconductivity (NPC). The detailed mechanism is further discussed in the subsequent sections. The coexistence of NPC and PPC makes the device suitable for implementing center-surround-like contrast enhancement. Moreover, the device’s inherent nonvolatile response replicates the visual persistence observed in bipolar cells, which is critical for motion tracking and prey detection.

Crucially, the ferroelectric gate offers precise electrostatic control over the 2D channel through strong interfacial coupling, enabling polarization-switching-assisted modulation of the internal potential and resulting in steep SS beyond the thermionic limit. This steep-slope switching allows rapid and energy-efficient ON/OFF transitions upon surpassing threshold voltages, analogous to threshold-triggered firing in retinal ganglion cells. This functionality is essential for constructing event-driven circuits (Fig. 1m) that demand low latency, high energy efficiency, and selectively respond to salient inputs while suppressing background noise. By generating polarity-specific voltage pulses (Fig. 1j) that mirror the ganglion cells, the device realizes a hardware-level analog to biological saliency detection and rapid threat anticipation, paving the way for advanced neuromorphic DVS systems.

The heterostructure was assembled via a dry-transfer technique using mechanically exfoliated nanoflakes, ensuring atomically clear interfaces and precise layer stacking. Detailed procedures for the transfer and device fabrication are provided in the “Methods” section. To facilitate comparative analysis and highlight the functional advantages of the 2D ferroelectric α-In2Se3, two classes of devices are fabricated in parallel: FeFET devices incorporating ferroelectric α-In2Se3 and control FET devices without an α-In2Se3 layer. Both devices share an identical MoS2 channel to ensure experimental consistency (Supplementary Fig. 2a). High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM), complemented by energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) elemental mapping, confirms the intended multilayer architecture, comprising MoS2 (channel), h-BN (tunneling layer), α-In2Se3 (ferroelectric layer), and h-BN (blocking layer) (Supplementary Fig. 2b). The corresponding thicknesses of these constituent layers are approximately 15 nm, 7 nm, 32 nm, and 20 nm, respectively. Raman spectroscopy further validates the uniform composition of each layer (Supplementary Fig. 2c). The ferroelectricity of α-In2Se3 is verified by piezoresponse force microscopy (PFM), showing clear domain switching and a characteristic phase hysteresis loop (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Broadband bidirectional photoresponse for retinal emulation

Inspired by the three hallmark features of the photoreceptors and bipolar cells in the amphibian vision: broad spectral sensitivity, center-surround contrast enhancement, and visual persistence, we systematically investigate the FeFET-based optoelectronic device’s capability to non-volatilely store both photoinduced excitatory and inhibitory responses under broadband optical stimulation. As shown in Fig. 2a, b, the devices exhibit synaptic plasticity-like behavior in the form of excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic current (EPSC/IPSC), where the light pulses (520 nm) serve as the presynaptic inputs and the source/drain current (Ids) functions as the postsynaptic response. Notably, this bidirectional photoresponse is determined by the initial ferroelectric polarization state, set via a 50 ms gate pulse of +4 V (Pup) or −4 V (Pdown). Under downward polarization (Fig. 2a), the device exhibits PPC, whereas upward polarization (Fig. 2b) results in NPC. This tunable bidirectional photoresponse effectively emulates the function of ON/OFF bipolar cells, which is crucial for achieving visual contrast enhancement and improving overall image perception32. Remarkably, laser pulses with durations ≥ 25 ms can induce long-term photoresponse retention in FeFET (Fig. 2a, Supplementary Figs. 4, 5), whereas control FET lacking the ferroelectric layer shows no such retention (Supplementary Fig. 6). More intriguingly, such nonvolatile bidirectional photoconductivity is observed across a broad spectral range from 365 nm to 637 nm (Fig. 2c, d), over a wide span of illumination intensities with consistently high responsivity (~104 A W−1), detectivity (~1014 Jones, Supplementary Fig. 7), and low-noise operation (noise-equivalent power (NEP ~ 10−12−10−15 W Hz−1/2), Supplementary Fig. 8), enabling robust multispectral signal fusion and faithfully mimicking the tetrachromatic vision of amphibians.Fig. 2Broadband, non-volatile bidirectional photoresponses and underlying mechanisms of FeFET devices.a Nonvolatile positive photoconductive effect and b negative photoconductive behaviors induced under 520 nm illumination with varying light intensities. c, d Wavelength-dependent non-volatile PPC and NPC behaviors under broadband illumination. e, f In-Sensor memory and computing capability with more than 6-bit distinguishable PPC states (e) and NPC states (f) under 520 nm optical excitation. g Comparison of the analog ratio and non-linearity (NL) values among advanced image perception devices in prior works. h Stepwise potentiation and depression (LTP and LTD) of the FeFET device under illumination at 365 nm, 457 nm, and 637 nm, demonstrating highly linear photoresponse across multiple wavelengths. i Schematic of the mechanism behind the photo-induced ferroelectric polarization switching underlying NPC. j Schematic illustration of the mechanism behind the photo-gate effect-induced PPC. k PFM phase images under varying exposure durations (520 nm), illustrating the dynamic evolution of ferroelectric domain structures. Precise and linear nonvolatile weight modulation is essential for achieving high-accuracy optical signal computing. Upon applying 64 (6 bits) consecutive presynaptic light spikes (50 ms, 1 s interval), the EPSC exhibits a highly linear increase (Fig. 2e). The inset in Fig. 2e highlights clearly distinguishable current levels, and each light pulse results in a stable, amplitude-consistent, and uniform weight update. This behavior is particularly advantageous for neuromorphic computing applications, as it simplifies the training process and enhances the accuracy of ED-CNN implementations. Similarly, 6-bit inhibitory postsynaptic weight states are also achieved via laser pulses following upward polarization initialization via a positive gate pulse (4 V, 50 ms), as shown in Fig. 2f. Furthermore, stepwise and bidirectional conductance modulation is realized at multiple wavelengths (637 nm, 457 nm, and 365 nm), depending on the preset polarization state (Supplementary Fig. 9), further demonstrating the device’s capability for multispectral information encoding and processing.

The configurable potentiation and depression characteristics of our FeFET-based optoelectronic devices correspond to long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD), respectively. Under 520 nm illumination, the device exhibits 8 stable LTP/LTD cycles and yields an analog ratio (Imax/Imin) of approximately 160 (Supplementary Fig. 10). Meanwhile, we quantify the NL values based on the LTP and LTD curves (see Supplementary Note 1), obtaining values of 0.43 for LTP and 0.31 for LTD. It is worth noting that a fundamental trade-off often exists between analog ratio and linearity, where a higher analog ratio typically leads to degraded linearity (higher NL value). As a result, researchers must often balance these two factors in practical applications. Figure 2g compares our device’s analogue ratio and NL values with prior neuromorphic computing technologies33–41, revealing a rare combination of low NL values and high Imax/Imin ratio. These characteristics not only accelerate the training efficiency of the ED-CNN but also significantly improve recognition accuracy. Moreover, highly linear LTP and LTD photoresponse are also observed under 365 nm, 457 nm and 637 nm illumination, as shown in Fig. 2h. Statistical analyses across 30 independently fabricated devices and repeated cycling measurements reveal excellent device-to-device and cycle-to-cycle uniformity (Supplementary Figs. 11–14), with low variability and MAPEs below ~5%, confirming the high stability, reproducibility, and reliability of the synaptic plasticity characteristics. These capabilities collectively enable the devices to process complex visual information, analogous to the processing capabilities of photoreceptors and bipolar cells in amphibian visual systems.

Mechanism of bidirectional visual signal retention

To elucidate the mechanism underpinning the nonvolatile bidirectional photoresponse in the 2D FeFET device, which originates from the cooperative coupling among ferroelectric polarization, photocarrier generation, and interface charge trapping, we construct detailed energy-band diagrams and operational schematics.

First, we discuss the light-induced polarization reversal–driven negative photoconductivity. According to Landau free-energy theory, α-In2Se3 intrinsically exhibits a symmetric double-well potential with two energetically degenerate out-of-plane polarization states (Pdown and Pup) in the ideal case (Supplementary Fig. 15a). However, practical ferroelectric devices inevitably introduce interface asymmetry, breaking this symmetry and giving rise to the imprint effect, in which one polarization state becomes energetically preferred42–46. In our MoS2/h-BN/α-In2Se3/h-BN/Au heterostructure, the work function difference between the top MoS2 (~4.58 eV, Supplementary Fig. 16) and the bottom Au (~5.1 eV) generates a built-in electric field (Ebi) pointing toward the Au at equilibrium state (Supplementary Fig. 15b), thereby stabilizing the downward polarization (Pdown) as the global energy minimum while rendering the upward polarization (Pup) metastable state (Supplementary Fig. 15c). Although external initialization pulses can temporarily switch the polarization to Pup, illumination-induced screening charges accelerate the relaxation of the metastable Pup state back toward the preferred Pdown configuration. This light-induced ferroelectric polarization reversal explains the observed NPC behavior.

When α-In2Se3 is initialized into the Pup state, positive bound charges at the ferroelectric surface electrostatically attract electron accumulation in the MoS2 channel, establishing a low-resistance state in the device (Fig. 2i, Supplementary Fig. 17). To elucidate the mechanism of light-induced polarization reversal in the MoS2/h-BN/α-In2Se3/h-BN/Au heterostructure, we perform a semi-quantitative analysis of the competition among the built-in electric field (Ebi), polarization-induced screening field (Escr), coercive field (Ec), and internal field (Eint) arising from the photo-generated holes. Owing to the work-function mismatch between MoS2 and α-In2Se3, a Ebi is established across the h-BN spacer, which is insufficient to overcome the Ec due to partial screening of polarization charges (Escr) at the α-In2Se3/h-BN/MoS2 interface (Supplementary Fig. 17b). Upon illumination, photogenerated holes can tunnel through the thin h-BN layer and accumulate at the α-In2Se3 interface, giving rise to an additional Eint. When Ebi + Eint > Escr + Ec (Supplementary Fig. 17c), local polarization switching becomes energetically favorable (detailed calculation process is provided in Supplementary Note 2). Notably, the photo-induced polarization reversal does not arise from an abrupt or coherent switching of the entire ferroelectric film. Instead, under illumination, the combined Ebi and the photo-induced Eint locally lower the effective polarization switching barrier, enabling partial reversal of ferroelectric domains in α-In2Se3. When successive optical pulses are applied, additional domains are progressively activated and switched, leading to cumulative depletion of the MoS2 channel and a stepwise increase in channel resistance.

To unequivocally verify the occurrence of light-induced polarization reversal from Pup to Pdown in the device, the ferroelectric properties of α-In2Se3 before and after optical stimulation are systematically examined using PFM, as shown in Fig. 2k. Initially, an upward polarization state was established by applying a 4 V gate bias. Upon exposure to light pulses (520 nm, 10 μW cm−2) with varying duration (0 s, 10 s, 20 s, 30 s, and 60 s), the ferroelectric polarization of α-In2Se3 progressively reverses to a downward state. This temporal evolution of the PFM signal under controlled illumination provides direct experimental evidence for light-induced ferroelectric polarization switching, thereby confirming the proposed mechanism underlying the observed nonvolatile NPC effect.

We next discuss the positive photoconductivity induced by the photogating effect. When α-In2Se3 is initially polarized downward, negative bound charges accumulate at the top surface, which induces an upward band bending in MoS2 (Supplementary Fig. 18a, b) and drives the channel into a depleted high-resistance state (HRS, left panel of Fig. 2j). Under illumination, photogenerated electron-hole pairs are primarily created within MoS2. Due to the synergistic effect of Ebi from MoS2 to α-In2Se3 and ferroelectric polarization field (EFe, Supplementary Fig. 18c), photogenerated holes are efficiently driven toward the α-In2Se3/h-BN interface, where they become trapped in interface states or localized defect sites. This hole trapping effectively suppresses electron-hole recombination and markedly prolongs the lifetime of photogenerated electrons in the MoS2 channel.

Since the MoS2 channel is initially electron-deficient, these long-lived photogenerated electrons serve as dominant contributors to conduction, significantly enhancing the channel conductivity (right panel of Fig. 2j). The trapped holes at the interface also generate a localized internal field, which further modulates the MoS2 channel potential and contributes to persistent or nonvolatile photoconductivity. Because Pdown corresponds to the energetically preferred polarization state, the trapped holes are insufficient to overcome the switching barrier; thus, polarization reversal does not occur under illumination. Collectively, these effects give rise to a strong instantaneous photoresponse, forming the fundamental mechanism underlying the observed PPC behavior in the device.

Sharp subthreshold ferroelectric transistor for ganglion

Beyond the previously discussed three features: broad spectral sensitivity, center-surround contrast enhancement, and visual persistence, amphibian vision also relies fundamentally on the event-driven responses of retinal ganglion cells. These neurons operate in a threshold-triggered and latency-minimized manner, motivating hardware with steep SS and low-voltage switching for efficient event-driven encoding. Guided by these criteria, we examine the electrical performance of our FeFET, with emphasis on its subthreshold characteristics, to demonstrate its potential for implementing bioinspired, low-latency event-driven vision systems.

The ferroelectric polarization of the α-In2Se3 layer electrostatically modulates the surface potential of MoS2, producing a counterclockwise hysteresis loop (Fig. 3a) and enabling a sub-60 mV dec−1 SS (53.8 mV dec-1, Fig. 3b), attributed to polarization-switching-driven channel modulation47,48. We fabricate a systematic set of FeFET devices with varying α-In2Se3 thicknesses and characterize them, revealing a progressive reduction of the hysteresis window with decreasing α-In2Se3 thickness (Fig. 3c), consistent with the expected degradation of ferroelectric order at reduced dimensions. Furthermore, SS values extracted from both forward and reverse gate voltage sweeps are summarized in Fig. 3d. Comparative analysis with state-of-the-art FeFETs for in-memory computing (Fig. 3e) highlights our device’s exceptional combination of ultra-sharp switching behavior and low operation voltage49–59. This synergy offers a compelling platform to construct energy-efficient event-driven circuits. Building on the steep switching characteristics, we evaluate the FeFET’s switching latency, which is a critical metric for high-speed event-driven circuits. As shown in Fig. 3f, the FeFET demonstrates nanosecond-scale switching dynamics that closely align with its steep subthreshold behavior. During reverse sweeps, ON-to-OFF transitions complete within ~180 ns, and OFF-to-ON transitions within ~430 ns, surpassing typical tens-of-microsecond DVS timescales by two orders of magnitude, enabling ultralow-latency event encoding. Leveraging fast ON/OFF switching dynamics and low operating voltage, we develop a FeFET-based event-driven circuit (Fig. 1h, Supplementary Note 2) capable of operating with ultralow power consumption and minimal latency. This circuit uses a sample-and-hold (s-h) branch to store the previous input and a through-path (t-p) branch for the current input, with a differential amplifier outputting the difference, where the s-h timing is determined by the RC time constant. When the FeFET is rapidly switched from OFF-to-ON state, the input signal charges the capacitor through low ON-resistance, enabling fast signal acquisition. Conversely, when the FeFET transitions from ON-to-OFF state, the capacitor becomes electrically isolated from the input. The large OFF-resistance suppresses charge leakage, effectively preserving the sampled voltage for subsequent event detection.

To experimentally validate the event-driven operation, we apply a pulsed gate voltage to the FeFET (highlighted in red) embedded in the event-driven circuit (Fig. 3g), periodically toggling its state between ON and OFF. The corresponding branch signals of the s-h and t-p are recorded (Fig. 3h). The difference between these signals yields a distinct bipolar pulse pair, comprising a brief negative and positive voltage pulse, which is a typical hallmark of event-driven encoding. The resulting bipolar pulse pair exhibits several desirable features essential for precise and energy-efficient signal processing, including near-zero latency, symmetric pulse amplitudes, narrow pulse widths, and sharp rising and falling edges. Figure 3i presents the real-time output voltage response of the event-driven circuit under various input voltage stimuli. When the input voltage increases stepwise from 0.05 to 0.075 V and subsequently to 0.1 V, the circuit generates two positive-polarity spikes at each transition with ultralow latency. Due to the identical step size (ΔV = 0.025 V), the resulting spike amplitudes are also identical, each with an amplitude of A. Once the input signal stabilizes, the output spike returns to zero, indicating no further event response. Conversely, when the input voltage decreases from 0.1 V to 0.05 V and further to 0 V (ΔV = −0.05 V), two negative-polarity spikes are observed with amplitudes of approximately −2A, yielding a linear dependence on the magnitude of the voltage change. Critically, when the input voltage remains constant, regardless of its absolute value, the circuit maintains a near-zero output, confirming its purely event-driven behavior. Similarly, when the input voltage increases from 0 V to 0.025 V and subsequently to 0.05 V, the circuit generates two positive spikes with amplitudes of A, further confirming that the spike polarity and amplitude are governed primarily by the direction and size of input variation rather than its absolute level. In contrast, replacing the FeFET with a control FET (SS ~ 300 mV dec−1, Supplementary Fig. 21) yields output pulses with significantly broadened widths and diminished edge sharpness (Fig. 3j). Such degradation in temporal characteristics compromises the circuit’s ability to resolve rapid signal transitions, elevate energy consumption, and obscure the precise delineation of event onsets and offsets, collectively undermining the efficiency and fidelity of neuromorphic signal encoding.

Amphibian-inspired preprocessing for expression recognition

The as-prepared FeFET devices possess near-ideal characteristics for implementing an amphibian-retina-inspired visual processing hierarchy, including broadband photosensitivity for photoreceptor emulation, photoresponsive memory and bidirectional photoconductivity (PPC and NPC) for bipolar-cell-like signal modulation, as well as ultra-sharp SS for constructing an event-driven circuit. To reproduce the center-surround-like RF behavior, we fabricate a 3 × 3 FeFET array (Fig. 4a, RF section) with a central ON-type photoresponsive device surrounded by eight OFF-type units. The current 3 × 3 configuration serves as a proof-of-concept platform to validate the underlying field-coupled spatial processing mechanism. Each unit is independently addressable via gate voltage, enabling spatially resolved photodetection control. Leveraging Kirchhoff’s law, the total output current aggregates individual photocurrents and dynamically reflects optical stimulation, enabling real-time edge detection. In a representative experiment, a light stimulus is continuously scanned along the lower-right diagonal of the array (upper panel of Fig. 4b). As the edge progressively traverses the RF, an increasing number of OFF-type photoreceptive devices are sequentially activated, resulting in a progressive decrease in the total photocurrent. The current reaches a minimum as the edge approaches the central ON-type device (lower panel of Fig. 4b). Continued movement of the edge leads to activation of the central ON-type unit, which exerts an antagonistic effect on the surrounding OFF-type responses, producing a pronounced positive peak in the net photocurrent. This dynamic response quantitatively follows the difference-of-Gaussians (DoG) model (dashed line in Fig. 4b), which underlies classical center-surround RF architectures in biological vision systems. Subsequently, the bipolar cell-processed signal is routed into an event-driven circuit to reproduce spatiotemporal spike-like response dynamics analogous to those observed in retinal ganglion cells. The output voltage exhibits discrete spikes in response to temporal variations (Fig. 4c), achieving an average response latency of ~3 μs (Supplementary Fig. 23), outperforming state-of-the-art DVSs by orders of magnitude59,60. Furthermore, under low-light conditions (10 μW cm−2), merely six false event spikes are recorded over a 5000 ms observation window (Supplementary Fig. 24), corresponding to a NEDL of 1.2 Rh s−1. This noise performance is comparable to that of commercial DVS operating in dark conditions (0.75–7.5 Rh s−1)59,60. In addition to its ultrafast and low-noise performance, the ADVS demonstrates a broad dynamic range of 160 dB (Fig. 4d), ensuring reliable operation across a wide spectrum of illumination conditions without compromising event detection fidelity. Notably, the spike amplitude correlates with the magnitude of the input variation, highlighting the analog encoding of stimulus change intensity. This motion-sensitive, event-driven architecture selectively amplifies local temporal changes while filtering static background, thereby enabling real-time moving-object detection under low-latency and low-power constraints.

To enable high-level recognition of dynamic visual scenes, the input video stream within a temporal window t is decomposed into 128 × 128 spatial patches, each comprising 3 × 3 pixels (Fig. 4a, signal input section)61, corresponding to the spatial mapping of one ADVS cell. Static regions produce no event spikes, whereas motion-induced center–surround antagonism generates high-amplitude spikes for feature extraction. The resulting feature-rich, spatiotemporally encoded, and noise-suppressed event spike sequences are subsequently streamed into an ED-CNN for downstream processing. To effectively capture both spatial and temporal structure, the ED-CNN architecture incorporates a series of 3D convolutional layers for joint spatiotemporal feature extraction, alongside spatial pooling layers that reduce spatial resolution, suppress noise, and improve robustness to local variations. This approach significantly reduces computational redundancy and latency, enabling real-time processing of highly dynamic environments while preserving microsecond-scale temporal resolution for fine-grained motion detection and trajectory prediction. Leveraging these capabilities, the system achieves accurate recognition of critical facial states, including eyelid drooping (micro-sleep), drowsiness, yawning, talking and normal states. Benchmarking (Fig. 4e, f) demonstrates that single-channel illumination suffers from misclassification (e.g., 1% accuracy for green-light yawning), whereas multi-channel fusion achieves >92% accuracy across all categories and converges within 11 epochs to 96.5% final accuracy. We further evaluate the training efficiency by analyzing convergence behavior, defined as the number of epochs required to reach stable accuracy. As illustrated in Fig. 4g, the multi-channel inputs strategy enables rapid convergence within just 11 epochs and achieves a final accuracy of 96.5%, outperforming all single-channel configurations. Benefiting from the center-surround contrast enhancement in the artificial RF, the system also maintains robust recognition performance even under low-light conditions (10 μW cm−2), achieving up to 92.1% accuracy (Fig. 4f). This low-light resilience ensures reliable facial expression monitoring in all-day, real-world driving environments.

A comparison of data bandwidth (Fig. 4h, Supplementary Fig. 25) highlights the advantages of the ADVS in event-driven sensing and information encoding. Owing to its ability to encode amplitude and suppress background noise, the ADVS generates 65% fewer event outputs than the commercial EC while preserving greater image clarity and detail. As shown in Fig. 4i, the ADVS system achieves a facial expression recognition accuracy of 92.1% even under low-light conditions (10 μW cm−2), surpassing the performance of the commercial EC, which yields an accuracy of 89.2%.

Discussion

In summary, we successfully developed an ADVS that integrates broadband photoreception, contrast enhancement, and event encoding within a FeFET platform. Beyond the realization of multiple functionalities, the key conceptual advance of this work lies in establishing a unified ferroelectric polarization-built-in-field coupling mechanism, which simultaneously governs photogenerated carrier dynamics and field-reconfigurable transport within a single vertical heterostructure. As a result, optical modulation, bidirectional polarization switching, positive/negative photoconductance, and sub-60 mV dec-1 switching behavior emerge as intrinsically coupled outcomes, rather than a simple stacking of independent device functions.

By implementing hardware-level functional analogues of tetrachromatic photoreceptors, bipolar-like contrast processing, and ganglion-like event encoding, the ADVS addresses three fundamental limitations of conventional DVS, including the lack of color information, high noise levels, and memory–computation separation. The broadband (365–637 nm), bidirectional (PPC and NPC), and nonvolatile photoconductive response of the FeFET enables multi-channel spectral encoding of photoreceptors and RF-based contrast enhancement of bipolar cells, while the steep subthreshold switching (SSmin = 53.8 mV dec−1) supports ultrafast, low-power, event-driven operation of ganglion cells. This architecture produces sparse, feature-rich event streams that encode color, contrast, and spatiotemporal information with low latency (~3 μs), low noise level (NEDL: 1.2 Rh s−1) and broad dynamic range (160 dB), enabling real-time recognition and prediction tasks under stringent constraints with minimal tolerance for noise and delay. When interfaced with an ED-CNN, the system demonstrates accurate dynamic facial expression recognition (~ 96.5%) and real-time multi-agent trajectory prediction (<5% error), offering a promising hardware foundation for neuromorphic visual perception in autonomous systems and edge-intelligent applications.

Methods

Device fabrication

The heavily doped Si wafer, covered with 300 nm SiO2 as a substrate, was cleaned for 15 min with deionized water and ethanol in an ultrasonic bath, respectively. Subsequently, the SiO2/Si substrate was purged using a nitrogen gun to keep the surface clean. Afterward, the SiO2/Si wafer underwent further treatment in a vacuum drying at 120 °C for 30 min. The bottom-gate electrode was patterned on the SiO2 surface using the photolithography technique. 30 nm Cr/Au electrodes were deposited through a thermal evaporation method. Then, h-BN (blocking layer), α-In2Se3 (ferroelectric layer and floating-gate layer), h-BN (tunneling layer), and MoS2 (channel layer) were sequentially transferred onto the bottom gate according to the device structure through an optical transfer platform. After the transfer process, the source/drain electrodes were patterned on the MoS2 surface using the photolithography technique and 5/40 nm-thick Cr/Au electrodes were deposited onto the as-transferred MoS2 sheets by a thermal evaporation method, and the channel length was 5 μm.

Device Characterization

The optical morphology of the prepared devices was obtained using an optical microscope (Olympus BX 53 M). For high-resolution imaging and elemental distribution mapping of the FeFET cross-section, a double Cs-corrected transmission electron microscope (TEM, Titan Cubed Themis G2 300) was employed, equipped with a Super-X detector. The Raman spectra were acquired using a Raman system (HORIBA LabRAM HR Evolution) with laser excitation λ = 532 nm. Out-of-plane ferroelectric properties of the α-In2Se3 film were characterized using piezoresponse force microscopy. The electrical measurements of the FeFET were carried out by semiconductor parameter analyzers (Keysight B2902 and Keithley 4200A-SCS).

Fabrication of the circuit board

In the specific implementation of the event-driven circuit, precise analog front-end design ensures reliable signal transduction and amplification. The input resistor (R1), chosen as 1 kΩ, plays a critical role in converting the input photocurrent or event-driven spike current into a proportional voltage signal via Ohm’s law, enabling voltage-domain signal processing. In the sample-and-hold branch, a hold capacitor (Chold) with a capacitance of 1 pF is employed to temporarily store the voltage signal, allowing for synchronized sampling and stable downstream processing. The core amplification stage utilizes a cascaded two-stage configuration based on the AD8137YRZ differential amplifier. The first stage operates as a unity-gain buffer (voltage follower) to provide high input impedance and isolate the preceding analog circuitry, thereby minimizing loading effects. The second stage serves as a signal amplifier to enhance voltage amplitude for downstream digitization or spike generation. Each AD8137YRZ amplifier has its common-mode voltage pins decoupled with 100 nF ceramic capacitors to ground, effectively suppressing power supply noise and improving signal integrity.

For the output stage, a differential amplifier is configured using a resistor network consisting of R2 = 10 kΩ and R3 = 1 kΩ, providing a well-defined differential voltage. This design ensures sufficient amplification of weak input signals while maintaining a stable and linear response. All printed circuit board (PCB) fabrication, including multilayer layout, via design, and solder mask application, as well as high-precision surface-mount device (SMD) component soldering, was professionally completed by Shenzhen JLCPCB Technology Co., Ltd., ensuring consistent quality and minimal parasitic effects due to precise manufacturing tolerances.
