The Reidemeister and the Nielsen numbers: growth rate, asymptotic behavior, dynamical zeta functions and the Gauss congruences
Alexander Fel’shtyn and Mateusz Slomiany

Abstract
. In the present paper, taking a dynamical point on view, we study the growth rate and asymptotic behavior of the sequences of the Reidemeister numbers and the sequences of the Reidemeister and the Nielsen coincidence numbers. We also prove the Gauss congruences for the sequence { R ⁡ ( φ n , ψ n ) } \{R(\varphi^{n},\psi^{n})\} of the Reidemeister coincidence numbers of the tame pair ( φ , ψ ) (\varphi,\psi) of endomorphisms of a torsion-free nilpotent group G G of finite Prüfer rank. Furthermore, we prove the rationality of the Nielsen coincidence zeta function, the Gauss congruences for the sequence { N ⁡ ( f n , g n ) } \{N(f^{n},g^{n})\} of the Nielsen coincidence numbers and show that the growth rate exists for the sequence { N ( f n , g n ) } N(f^{n},g^{n})\} of tame pair of maps ( f , g ) (f,g) of a compact nilmanifold to itself.

1.
Introduction

In topological fixed point theory, the Reidemeister and the Nielsen numbers
arise as homotopy invariants associated to a continuous
self-map of a connected compact polyhedron. Passing to the
fundamental group of the polyhedron, the Reidemeister numbers admit an
algebraic treatment in terms of twisted conjugacy classes; compare

[
8
]
.
We assume
X
to be a connected, compact polyhedron and
f:X\rightarrow X
to be a continuous map. Let
p:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
be the universal cover of
X
and
\tilde{f}:\tilde{X}\rightarrow\tilde{X}
a lifting
of
f
, i.e.,
p\circ\tilde{f}=f\circ p
.
Two liftings
\tilde{f^{\prime}}
and
\tilde{f}
of
f
are said to be
conjugate
if there
exists covering translation
\gamma\in\Gamma\cong\pi_{1}(X)
, such that
\tilde{f^{\prime}}=\gamma\circ\tilde{f}\circ\gamma^{-1}.

Lifting classes are equivalence classes by conjugacy. Notation:

[\tilde{f}]=\{\gamma\circ\tilde{f}\circ\gamma^{-1}|\gamma\in\Gamma\}.

The subset
p(\fix(\tilde{f}))\subset\fix(f)
is called the
fixed point class
of
f
determined by the lifting class
[\tilde{f}]
.
Our definition of a fixed point class is via the universal covering space. It essentially
says: two fixed point of
f
are in the same class iff there is a lifting
\tilde{f}
of
f
having
fixed points above both of them. There is another way of saying this, which does not use covering space explicitly, hence is very useful in identifying fixed point classes.
Two fixed points
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
of
f
belong to the same fixed point class iff
there is a path
c
from
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
such that
c\cong f\circ c
(homotopy relative
endpoints - “Nielsen disk”).
This fact can be considered as an equivalent definition (geometrical) of a non-empty fixed point class. Every map
f
has only finitely many non-empty fixed point classes, each a compact
subset of
X
.
A fixed point class is called
essential
if its index is nonzero. The number of lifting classes of
f
(and hence the number of all fixed point classes) is called the
Reidemeister number
of
f
, denoted by
R(f)
. This is a positive integer or infinity. The number of essential fixed point classes is called the
Nielsen number
of
f
, denoted by
N(f)
.
The Nielsen number is always finite.
R(f)
and
N(f)
are homotopy invariants. In the category of compact, connected polyhedra the Nielsen number of a map is, apart from in certain exceptional cases, equal to the least number of fixed points of maps with the same homotopy type as
f
.

Let
f,g:X\to Y
be two maps between two compact connected manifolds.
Let

\operatorname{Coin}(f,g):=\{x\in X\mid f(x)=g(x)\}
be the set of the coincidence points of
f
and
g
.
Two points of coincidence
x_{1},x_{2}\in\operatorname{Coin}(f,g)
are said to be
Nielsen equivalent
if there is a path

\alpha:[0,1]\to X
from
x_{1}
to
x_{2}
such that
f\circ\alpha
is homotopic to
g\circ\alpha
.
If
[C]
denotes the fixed-endpoint homotopy class of
C

then we can denote it by
[f\circ\alpha]=[g\circ\alpha]
.
Being Nielsen equivalent is obviously an equivalence relation.
We denote the set of the coincidence classes by
\overline{\operatorname{Coin}}(f,g)
.
Let
\{f_{t}\}
be a homotopy of
f
and let
\{g_{t}\}
be a homotopy of
g
,
f_{0}=f,g_{0}=g
.
Two points of coincidence
x_{1}\in\operatorname{Coin}(f,g)
and
x_{2}\in\operatorname{Coin}(f_{1},g_{1})
are
\{f_{t}\},\{g_{t}\}
-related
if there exists a path
C:[0,1]\to X
with
C(0)=x_{1},C(1)=x_{2}
such that

[\{f_{t}\circ C(t)\}]=[\{g_{t}\circ C(t)\}]
.
Two equivalence classes
\alpha\in\overline{\operatorname{Coin}}(f,g)
and
\beta\in\overline{\operatorname{Coin}}(f_{1},g_{1})

are
\{f_{t}\},\{g_{t}\}
-related if every point of coincidence in
\alpha
is
\{f_{t}\},\{g_{t}\}
-related
to every point of coincidence in
\beta
.

A coincidence class
\alpha\in\overline{\operatorname{Coin}}(f,g)
is
essential

if for any homotopies
\{f_{t}\}
of
f
and
\{g_{t}\}
of
g
, there is a coincidence class
in
\overline{\operatorname{Coin}}(f_{1},g_{1})
to which
\alpha
is
\{f_{t}\},\{g_{t}\}
-related.
The finite number of essential coincidence classes in
\overline{\operatorname{Coin}}(f,g)
is called
the
Nielsen coincidence number
of
f,g
and denoted by
N(f,g)
,
see also
[
22
]
.

Let
G
be a group and
\varphi:G\rightarrow G
an
endomorphism. Two elements
x,y\in G
are said to be

\varphi
-
conjugate
or
twisted conjugate,

if and only if there exists
g\in G
such that
y=gx\varphi(g^{-1}).

We will write
\{x\}_{\varphi}
for the
\varphi
-
conjugacy
or

twisted conjugacy
class
of the element
x\in G
.
The number of
\varphi
-conjugacy classes is called the
Reidemeister number

of an endomorphism
\varphi
and is denoted by
R(\varphi)
.
If
\varphi
is the identity map then the
\varphi
-conjugacy classes are the usual
conjugacy classes in the group
G
.

We have the Reidemeister bijection between fixed point classes, lifting classes and corresponding twisted conjugacy
classes. Therefore the
Reidemeister number of continuous map
f
coincides with Reidemeister number
(the number of twisted conjugacy classes) of induced endomorphism
f_{*}
of the fundamental group of
X
:
R(f)=R(f_{*})
.
In this paper we take mostly a group-theoretic point of view,
inspired by, but otherwise independent of the topological
origins of the subject.

Let
G
be a group and
\varphi,\psi:G\rightarrow G

two endomorphisms. Two elements
\alpha,\beta\in G
are said to be
(\varphi,\psi)
-conjugate
if and only if there exists
g\in G
with

\beta=\psi(g)\alpha\varphi(g^{-1}).

The number of
(\varphi,\psi
)-conjugacy classes is called the Reidemeister coincidence
number of endomorphisms
\varphi
and
\psi
, denoted by
R(\varphi,\psi)
. If
\psi
is the identity map then the
(\varphi,id)
-conjugacy classes are the
\varphi
-conjugacy classes in the group
G
and
R(\varphi,id)=R(\varphi)
.
The Reidemeister coincidence number
R(\varphi,\psi)
has useful applications in Nielsen - Reidemeister coincidence theory.
We call an endomorphism
\varphi

tame
if the
Reidemeister numbers
R(\varphi^{n})
are finite for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We call the pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of endomorphisms
tame
if the coincidence
Reidemeister numbers
R(\varphi^{n},\psi^{n})
are finite for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Maps
f,g
induce homomorphisms
f_{\#},g_{\#}
between fundamental groups of compact manifolds
X
and
Y
.
We define the
topological
Reidemeister coincidence number of maps
R(f,g):=R(f_{\#},g_{\#})

to be the Reidemeister coincidence number of the induced homomorphisms.
If
Y=X
then we are going to say that the pair of maps
f,g
is
tame
if

R(f^{n},g^{n})<\infty
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

In the theory of dynamical systems, the Reidemeister coincidence
numbers count the synchronisation points of two
maps, i.e. the points whose orbits intersect under simultaneous
iterations of two maps; see
[
18
]
, for instance.

In the present paper, taking a dynamical point on view, we study the growth rate
and asymptotic behavior of the sequences of the Reidemeister, and the Nielsen numbers

\{R(\varphi^{n})\}
,
\{R(\varphi^{n},\psi^{n})\}
,
\{R(f^{n})\}
,
\{N(f^{n},g^{n})\}

for a tame endomorphism
\varphi
, and a tame map
f
, and
for a tame pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of endomorphisms, and a tame pair
(f,g)
of maps.
We also prove the Gauss congruences for the sequence
\{R(\varphi^{n},\psi^{n})\}
of the
Reidemeister coincidence numbers of the tame pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of endomorphisms of a
torsion-free nilpotent group
G
of finite Prüfer rank.
Furthermore, we prove the rationality of the Nielsen coincidence zeta function and
the Gauss congruences for the sequence
\{N(f^{n},g^{n})\}
of the Nielsen coincidence numbers
and show that the growth rate exists for the sequence
\{N(f^{n},g^{n})\}

for a tame pair of maps
(f,g)
of a compact nilmanifold to itself.

This work is organized as follows.

In section
2

G
.

In section
5

G

2.
Preliminary considerations

2.1.
Topological entropy and growth rate of the Nielsen numbers of iterations

The most widely used measure for the complexity of a dynamical system is the topological
entropy. We remind its definition.
Let
f:X\rightarrow X
be a self-map of a compact metric space. For a given
\epsilon>0

and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, a subset
E\subset X
is said to be
(n,\epsilon)
-separated
under
f
if for
each pair
x\not=y
in
E
there is
0\leq i<n
such that
d(f^{i}(x),f^{i}(y))\geq\epsilon
.
Let
s_{n}(\epsilon,f)
denote the largest cardinality of any
(n,\epsilon)
-separated subset
E

under
f
. Thus
s_{n}(\epsilon,f)
is the greatest number of orbit segments
{x,f(x),\cdots,f^{n-1}(x)}

of length
n
that can be distinguished one from another provided we can only distinguish
between points of
X
that are at least
\epsilon
apart.
Now let

h(f,\epsilon):=\limsup_{n}\frac{1}{n}\log\,s_{n}(\epsilon,f)

h(f):=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}h(f,\epsilon).

The number
0\leq h(f)\leq\infty
, which is independent of the metric
d
used,
providing the induced topology is the same,
is called the topological entropy of
f
.
If
h(f,\epsilon)>0
then, up to resolution
\epsilon>0
, the number
s_{n}(\epsilon,f)
of
distinguishable orbit segments of length
n
grows exponentially with
n
. So
h(f)

measures the growth rate in
n
of the number of orbit segments of length
n

with arbitrarily fine resolution.

The following example will be used to show a connection between the growth rate of Reidemeister numbers and topological entropy in Section
4
.

Example 1

((see
[
16
, Example 3.2c]
))
.

We compute topological entropy for the expanding map
E_{m}:S^{1}\to S^{1}

of the unit circle to itself which sends
z\mapsto z^{m}
where
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
.
We will measure distance between two points
x,y\in S^{1}
as a normalized difference of their arguments
(as complex numbers, let
\iota
denote the imaginary unit) modulo
1
.
We are going to compute cardinality of the maximal
(n,\epsilon)
-separeted set.

Pick
\epsilon=1/m^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then points

x_{j}:=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi\iota j}{m^{n+k-1}}\right),\quad j=0,1,\dots,m^{n+k-1}-1

form an
(n,\epsilon)
-separated set.
For example for two points next to each other we have

d\left(E_{m}^{n-1}(x_{j}),E_{m}^{n-1}(x_{j+1})\right)=\frac{(j+1)m^{n-1}-jm^{n-1}}{m^{n+k-1}}=\frac{m^{n-1}}{m^{n+k-1}}=\frac{1}{m^{k}}=\epsilon.

It also means that we cannot add further points to the set so that it remains
(n,\epsilon)
-separated
therefore
s_{n}(1/m^{k},E_{m})=m^{n+k-1}
.
Now directly from the definition

\displaystyle h(E_{m})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log m^{n+k-1}=\log|m|.

For a “hyperbolic” (Axiom A) diffeomorphism of a manifold topological entropy

h(f)
measures the growth rate of the number of periodic points, see
[
16
]
.

A basic relation between topological entropy
h(f)
and Nielsen numbers
was found by N. Ivanov.

Theorem 2
.

[
13
]

Let
f
be a continuous map on a compact connected polyhedron
X
. Then

h(f)\geq\limsup_{n}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\log N(f^{n})=:{\log N^{\infty}(f)}

The number
N^{\infty}(f)
is called the
growth rate
of the Nielsen numbers of iterations of
f
.
This inequality is remarkable in that it does not require smoothness of the map and provides a common lower bound for the topological entropy of all maps in a homotopy class.

2.2.
Unitary dual

By
\widehat{G}
we denote the
unitary dual
of
G
, i.e.
the space of equivalence classes of
unitary irreducible representations of
G
, equipped with the

compact-open
topology.
If
\varphi:G\to G
is an automorphism,
it induces a dual map
\widehat{\varphi}:\widehat{G}\to\widehat{G}
,
\widehat{\varphi}(\rho):=\rho\circ\varphi
.
This dual map
\widehat{\varphi}
define a dynamical system on the unitary dual space
\widehat{G}
.

The unitary dual of any finitely generated abelian group
G
is an abelian group
\widehat{G}
which is,
by Pontryagin duality, the direct sum
of a Torus whose dimension is the rank of
G
, and of a finite abelian group.
In that case the dual map will be called the
Pontryagin dual map
.

2.3.
Isolated lower central series of a torsion-free nilpotent group

Let
G
be a group. For a subgroup
H\leqslant G
, we define the
isolator

\sqrt[G]{H}
of
H
in
G
as

\sqrt[G]{H}:=\{g\in G\mbox{ }|\mbox{ }g^{n}\in H\mbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Note that the isolator of a subgroup
H\leqslant G
doesn’t have to be a subgroup in general.

Now let
G
be a torsion-free nilpotent group with the lower central series

G=\gamma_{1}(G)\geq\gamma_{2}(G)\geq\dots\geq\gamma_{c}(G)\geq\gamma_{c+1}(G)=\{e\}

where as usual
\gamma_{k+1}(G):=\llbracket G,\gamma_{k}(G)\rrbracket
is the
k
-fold commutator group.
It turns out that, by results of
[
6
, Lem. 1.1.2 and Lem. 1.1.4]
, we benefit by considering another central series, namely

(1)

G=\sqrt[G]{\gamma_{1}(G)}\geq\sqrt[G]{\gamma_{2}(G)}\geq\dots\geq\sqrt[G]{\gamma_{c}(G)}\geq\sqrt[G]{\gamma_{c+1}(G)}=\{e\}

which we call
isolated
(or
adapted
)
lower central series
.
The isolated lower central series terminates iff
G
is a torsion-free nilpotent group.
The main advantage of using this series over the usual lower central series is that all the factors

\sqrt[G]{\gamma_{k}(G)}/\sqrt[G]{\gamma_{k+1}(G)}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{d_{k}},\quad d_{k}\in\mathbb{N}

are torsion-free.

2.4.
p
-adics

In this section we remind basic definitions and prove facts about
p
-adic numbers needed later.
Let
p
be any prime number. For
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
we define the
p
-adic ordinal
\operatorname{ord}_{p}a

to be the highest power of
p
which divides
a
.
For a rational
q=a/b
we define
\operatorname{ord}_{p}q:=\operatorname{ord}_{p}a-\operatorname{ord}_{p}b
.
The
p
-adic norm of
q
is defined

\left|q\right|_{p}:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}q}},\quad&q\neq 0,\\
0,\quad&q=0\end{cases}.

The norm is
non-Archimedean
which means that for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{Q}}

it satisfies
\left|x+y\right|_{p}\leq\max\left(|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\right)
.

We will make use of the following property:

(2)

\big(|x|_{p}<|y|_{p}\big)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|x-y|_{p}=|y|_{p}.

The field
{\mathbb{Q}}
with the norm
|\cdot|_{p}
is not complete but if we consider the set
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

of equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences with regard to the relation

\{a_{i}\}\sim\{b_{i}\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad|a_{i}-b_{i}|_{p}\to 0\ (i\to\infty)

(just as we actually do when we go from
{\mathbb{Q}}
to
{\mathbb{R}}
with the usual norm)
with the
p
-adic norm of a sequence defined as
\left|\{a_{i}\}\right|_{p}:=\lim|a_{i}|_{p}

then
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
turns out to be a complete field.
Moreover, from (
2
) and definition of Cauchy sequences we get
that in fact the sequence
\{|a_{i}|_{p}\}
of
p
-adic norms of elements of a Cauchy sequence
\{a_{i}\}

is constant for sufficiently large
i
.
The following remark will be important later.

Remark 3
.

Possible values for
|\cdot|_{p}
on
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
are in the set
\{p^{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\cup\{0\}
.
In particular the greatest possible value
<1
is
1/p
.

We are going to work with numbers algebraic over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
Let
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\xi)
be a finite extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
generated by
\xi
which is a root of a monic polynomial

x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\dots+a_{n-1}x+a_{n},\quad a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}.

We define the
norm
of the extension as
{\mathbb{N}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\xi)/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(\xi):=(-1)^{n}a_{n}
.
The extension of
p
-adic norm from
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
to
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\xi)
is defined as

|\xi|_{p}:=\left|{\mathbb{N}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\xi)/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(\xi)\right|_{p}^{1/n}.

There exist the algebraic closure
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
and its completion
\Omega
(which is algebraically
closed as well) with further extensions of the norm but we will not need them in this work.
The subset
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\mid|x|_{p}\leq 1\}
is a ring
and its elements are called
p
-adic integers

There is the
p
-adic analog of logarithm defined for all
z\in\Omega
satisfying
|z|_{p}<1
:

\log_{p}(1+z):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{m}z^{m}.

We are going to make use of the
p
-adic logarithm to prove a very specific inequality.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\xi
be an algebraic number over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
such that
|\xi|_{p}=1
but
\xi
is not a root of unity.
Then there is a constant
C
such that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
the inequality

(3)

\frac{1}{n}\leq C\cdot\left|\xi^{n}-1\right|_{p}

holds. The constant does not depend on
n
.

Proof.

For
|1|_{p}=1=|\xi|_{p}
, from (
2
) we have
|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}\leq 1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
If for some
n
there is equality
|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}=1
then (
3
) holds for
C=1

Now consider
n
for which
|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}<1
.
Denote the degree
\left[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\xi):{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\right]=:k
.
From remark
3
and
the definition of the
p
-adic norm for algebraic numbers we have

(4)

|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt[k]{p}}.

Therefore we can compute the
p
-adic logarithm and

\displaystyle|n|_{p}\cdot|\log_{p}(\xi)|_{p}

\displaystyle=|\log_{p}(\xi^{n})|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left|(\xi^{n}-1)-\frac{(\xi^{n}-1)^{2}}{2}+\frac{(\xi^{n}-1)^{3}}{3}-\dots\right|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{m}\left(\left|\frac{(\xi^{n}-1)^{m}}{m}\right|_{p}\right)

Note that using (
4
) we get

(5)

0\leq\left|\frac{(\xi^{n}-1)^{m}}{m}\right|_{p}=p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}m}\cdot\left|\xi^{n}-1\right|_{p}^{m}\leq m\cdot\left|\xi^{n}-1\right|_{p}^{m}\leq m\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt[k]{p}}\right)^{m}.

For a constant
q\in(0,1/\sqrt[k]{p}]
the derivative

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}xq^{x}=q^{x}(1+x\operatorname{ln}q)
vanishes only for

x=-1/\operatorname{ln}q
, is positive for smaller
x
and negative for greater ones.
Note that
-1/\operatorname{ln}q
is an increasing function therefore in our situation
-1/\operatorname{ln}q\ \leq\ k/\operatorname{ln}p
.
It is possible then that for a finite number of initial
m
s the terms over which we take the maximum
actually grow, but eventually there is a maximum
M=M(n)
and the sequence goes to
0
as
m\to\infty
.

In other words there exists
m_{0}=m_{0}(n)
satisfying
1\leq m_{0}\leq k/\operatorname{ln}p
, for which

\max_{m}\left(\left|\frac{(\xi^{n}-1)^{m}}{m}\right|_{p}\right)=|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}\cdot\frac{\left|\xi^{n}-1\right|_{p}^{m_{0}-1}}{|m_{0}|_{p}}

We can bound from above the right side similarly as above:

\displaystyle|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}\cdot\frac{\left|\xi^{n}-1\right|_{p}^{m_{0}-1}}{|m_{0}|_{p}}

\displaystyle=|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}\cdot\Big(p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}m_{0}}\cdot\left|\xi^{n}-1\right|_{p}^{m_{0}-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}\cdot\Big(m_{0}\cdot\left|\xi^{n}-1\right|_{p}^{m_{0}-1}\Big)

For
x\in[1,k/\operatorname{ln}p]
the derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial q}xq^{x-1}=x(x-1)q^{x-2}\geq 0
so

\displaystyle|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}\cdot\Big(m_{0}\cdot\left|\xi^{n}-1\right|_{p}^{m_{0}-1}\Big)\ \leq\ |\xi^{n}-1|_{p}\cdot\left(m_{0}\cdot\frac{1}{(\sqrt[k]{p})^{m_{0}-1}}\right)

Note that

M^{*}:=\max_{1\ \leq\ m\ \leq\ k/\operatorname{ln}p}\left\{m\cdot p^{-(m-1)/k}\right\}\ \geq\ m_{0}\cdot p^{-(m_{0}-1)/k}

for all possible
m_{0}
, therefore
M^{*}
does not depend on
n
. We got

\frac{1}{n}\ \leq\ |n|_{p}\ \leq\ |\xi^{n}-1|_{p}\cdot\frac{M^{*}}{|\log_{p}(\xi)|_{p}}

and (
3
) holds for
C=M^{*}/|\log_{p}(\xi)|_{p}
.

In general (
3
) holds for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
regardless of actual values of
|\xi^{n}-1|_{p}

if we put
C:=\max\left\{1,M^{*}/|\log_{p}(\xi)|_{p}\right\}
.
∎

3.
Growth rate of the sequence
\{R(\varphi^{n})\}
of the Reidemeister numbers

Let

We introduce the growth rate

and upper and lower growth rates

and

of iterations as follows

\displaystyle R^{\infty}_{+}(\varphi):=\limsup_{k\to\infty}R(\varphi^{k})^{1/k},\qquad R^{\infty}_{-}(\varphi):=\liminf_{k\to\infty}R(\varphi^{k})^{1/k},

\displaystyle R^{\infty}(\varphi):=\lim_{k\to\infty}R(\varphi^{k})^{1/k}.

In general there is no reason to expect the upper (lower) limit above to be a limit,

Example 5

Now we can compute the growth rate

Let
\varphi\colon G\to G

Proof.

Using Lemma

4.
Growth rate of the sequence
\{R(\varphi^{n})\}
of the Reidemeister numbers and topological entropy

The following example leads us to results on a connection between the growth rate
of the Reidemeister numbers and the topological entropy.

Example 9
.

Consider the group
G=\mathbb{Z}
and the endomorphism
\varphi:x\mapsto dx

for
d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
as in Example
5

We computed the growth rate
R^{\infty}(\varphi)=|d|
.

On the Pontryagin dual
\hat{G}\cong U(1)
our endomorphism
\varphi
induces the dual endomorphism
\hat{\varphi}(\chi)=\chi^{d}
.
In Example
1
we computed topological entropy

h_{top}(\hat{\varphi})=\log|d|
.
We observe that

R^{\infty}(\varphi)=\exp(h_{top}(\hat{\varphi})).

A homeomorphism
f
of a compact metric space
(X,d)
is
expansive
if there exists a constant
\epsilon>0

such that given any two distinct points
x,y\in X
, there exists
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))>\epsilon
.

We will need also a notion of the Bowen’s
specification

[
2
]
.
We cite the definition from
[
3
]
.
The formal definition can seem vague at first so it is good to have the following intuition in mind.
We say that a self-map
f:X\to X
of a compact metric space has the
specification property

if, given a precision
\epsilon>0
and a number of segments of orbits, one is able to find a periodic orbit
which, with the precision
\epsilon
, follows each one of them and is moving from one segment to another
in a fixed amount of time which depends only on
\epsilon
.

That being said, let
(x_{1},l_{1}),\dots,(x_{r},l_{r})\in X\times\mathbb{N}
where
0\leq r<\infty
be given.
For a constant
D\in\mathbb{N}
we will denote the partial length

L(s):=l_{1}+\dots+l_{s-1}+(s-1)D,\quad 1\leq s\leq r+1.

The map
f
has the
specification property
if for every
\epsilon>0
there exist a constant
D<\infty

and
z\in X
such that
f^{L(r+1)}(z)=z
and

d(f^{L(s)+k}(z),f^{k}(x_{s}))<\epsilon,\quad 0\leq k<l_{s}

for all
s=1,\dots,r
.

Lemma 10

(
[
2
]
)
.

For an expansive and having the specification property homeomorphism
f
of a compact metric space
X
the growth rate
F^{\infty}(f)
of the number of periodic points exists and is given by

F^{\infty}(f):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\#(\operatorname{Fix}(f^{k}))^{1/k}=\exp(h(f)),

where
h(f)
denotes the topological entropy.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\varphi:G\to G
be a tame automorphism of a finitely generated discrete Abelian group
G
.
Suppose that the Pontryagin dual map
\widehat{\varphi}
is expansive and having the specification property
homeomorphism of the Pontryagin dual group
\widehat{G}
.
Then the growth rate of the Reidemeister numbers of iterations of
\varphi

R^{\infty}(\varphi)=\exp(h(\widehat{\varphi})).

Proof.

The Pontryagin dual of the cokernel of
(1-\varphi):G\rightarrow G
is canonically isomorphic to the
kernel of the dual map
\widehat{(1-\varphi)}:\hat{G}\rightarrow\hat{G}
.
Since
R(\varphi^{n})=\#\operatorname{Coker}(1-\varphi^{n})
is finite, we have

\#\operatorname{Coker}(1-\varphi^{n})=\#\widehat{\operatorname{Coker}(1-\varphi^{n})}=\#\mathop{\rm Ker}\nolimits\widehat{(1-\varphi^{n})}.

The map
\widehat{1-\varphi^{n}}
is equal to
\hat{1}-\hat{\varphi^{n}}
.
Its kernel is thus the set of fixed points of the
map
\hat{\varphi^{n}}:\hat{G}\rightarrow\hat{G}
.
We therefore have:

(13)

R(\varphi^{n})=\#\fix\left(\hat{\varphi^{n}}:\hat{G}\rightarrow\hat{G}\right)

Then, Lemma
10
implies the result.
∎

Proof.

For endomorphisms of finitely generated nilpotent group we have the Roman’kov formula

(for generalisations see also

6.
The Gauss congruences for the sequence
\{R(\varphi^{n},\psi^{n})\}
of the Reidemeister coincidence numbers

Let
\mu(n)
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be the
Möbius function
,
i.e.

\mu(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if }n=1,\\
(-1)^{k}&\mbox{ if }n\mbox{ is a product of }k\mbox{ distinct primes,}\\
0&\mbox{ if }n\mbox{ is not square-free.}\end{array}\right.

In number theory, the following Gauss congruence for integers holds:

\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\cdot a^{d}\equiv 0\mod n

for any integer
a
and any natural number
n
. In the case of a prime power
n=p^{r}
, the Gauss congruence turns into the Euler congruence. Indeed, for
n=p^{r}
the Möbius function
\mu(n/d)=\mu(p^{r}/d)
is nonzero only in two cases: when
d=p^{r}
and when
d=p^{r-1}
. Therefore, from the Gauss congruence we obtain the Euler congruence

a^{p^{r}}\equiv a^{p^{r-1}}\mod p^{r}.

When
(a,n)=1,n=p^{r}
, these congruences are equivalent to the following classical Euler’s theorem:

a^{\varphi(n)}\equiv 1\mod n.

These congruences have been generalized from integers to some other mathematical invariants such as the traces of powers of all integer matrices
A
and the Lefschetz numbers of iterations of a map see
[
17
,
23
]
:

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\cdot\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{d})\equiv 0\mod n,

(20)

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{p^{r}})\equiv\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{p^{r-1}})\mod p^{r}.

A. Dold in
[
7
]
(see also
[
14
, Theorem 3.1.4]
) proved by a geometric argument the following congruences (
DL
) for the Lefschetz numbers of iterations of a map
f
on a compact ANR
X
and any natural number
n

(DL)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\cdot L(f^{d})\equiv 0\mod n.

These congruences are now called the Dold congruences. It is also shown in
[
17
]
(see also
[
23
, Theorem 9]
) that the above congruences (
19
), (
20
) and (
DL
) are equivalent.
For more detailed discussion see
[
4
]
.

The following Gauss congruences for the Reidemeister numbers of iterations of a tame automorphism
\varphi
of polycyclic-by-finite group are proven in
[
11
]

\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\cdot R(\varphi^{d})\equiv 0\mod n.

It turns out that the Gauss congruences for Reidemeister coincidence numbers holds if
the Reidemeister coincidence zeta function is rational.

The
Reidemeister coincidence zeta function
for a tame pair

(\varphi,\psi)
of endomorphisms of a group

In this section we use the following criterion for the rationality of the Reidemeister coincidence zeta function
for a tame pair of endomorphisms of a torsion-free nilpotent group of finite Prüfer rank to
prove the Gauss congruences for the Reidemeister coincidence numbers of iterations of these endomorphisms.

(
[

Lemma 16
.

R_{\varphi,\psi}(z)
is rational iff for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

(22)

R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}

where
r\in{\mathbb{N}},\ \chi_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
are distinct algebraic integers.

Proof.

\Leftarrow
)
Let us split all the
\chi_{i}
into two sets according to their signs.
We have
r^{+}
positive
\chi_{i}^{+}=\chi_{i}
and
r^{-}
positive
\chi_{i}^{-}=-\chi_{i}
where
r^{+}+r^{-}=r
.
Denote
\lambda_{i}
as
\lambda_{i}^{+}
or
\lambda_{i}^{-}
with respect to the sign of corresponding
\chi_{i}
.
With this notation we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{r^{+}}\chi_{i}^{+}{\lambda_{i}^{+}}^{k}\ -\ \sum_{i=1}^{r^{-}}\chi_{i}^{-}{\lambda_{i}^{-}}^{k}.

Using this equality in (
22
), properties of
\exp

and the identity
\operatorname{ln}(1/(1-t))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{n}/n
we get

R_{\varphi,\psi}(z)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r^{+}}\left(\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda_{i}^{+}z)^{k}}{k}\right)\right)^{\chi_{i}^{+}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r^{-}}\left(\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda_{i}^{-}z)^{k}}{k}\right)\right)^{\chi_{i}^{-}}}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r^{-}}\left(1-\lambda_{i}^{-}z\right)^{\chi_{i}^{-}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r^{+}}\left(1-\lambda_{i}^{+}z\right)^{\chi_{i}^{+}}}

and the zeta function is rational.

\Rightarrow
)
Assume
R_{\varphi,\psi}(z)
is rational.
By definition
R_{\varphi,\psi}(0)=1\neq 0
therefore we can write

R_{\varphi,\psi}(z)=\frac{\prod_{i}(1-\beta_{i}z)}{\prod_{j}(1-\gamma_{j}z)}

where
\beta_{i},\gamma_{j}
are non-zero roots of polynomials and the products are finite.
Combine the roots into one sequence
\{\lambda_{i}\}
where for
i\neq j
there is
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
.
Taking multiplication into account we can write

(23)

R_{\varphi,\psi}(z)=\prod_{i}\left(1-\lambda_{i}z\right)^{\chi_{i}},\quad\chi_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Let us consider the logarithmic derivative

(24)

S(z):=\frac{d}{dz}\operatorname{ln}R_{\varphi,\psi}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})\cdot z^{k-1}.

From (
23
) we have

(25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S(z)&=\frac{d}{dz}\left(\sum_{i}\chi_{i}\operatorname{ln}(1-\lambda_{i}z)\right)\ =\ \sum_{i}\chi_{i}\frac{-\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}z}\\
&=\sum_{i}(-\chi_{i}\lambda_{i})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}^{k}z^{k}\ =\ \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i}-\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}\right)z^{k-1}.\end{split}

Comparing coefficients at

we get
R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})=\sum_{i}\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}

up to the signs of
\chi_{i}
,
the sum is finite,
\chi_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\lambda_{i}
are algebraic numbers.

We are going to prove that
\lambda_{i}
are actually algebraic integers.
From (
24
) we know that
S(z)
is given by a power series with integer coefficients
and from (
25
) that it is a rational function.
Therefore
S(z)=u(z)/v(z)
, where

.
Fatou lemma (see
[
14
, Lemma 3.1.31]
) says that

in this setting have the forms

u(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}z^{i},\quad v(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{q}b_{j}z^{j}

where the coefficients
a_{i},b_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Comparing this with (
25
) we get

\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}z^{i}}{1+\sum_{j=1}^{q}b_{j}z^{j}}\ =\ S(z)\ =\ \sum_{i}\frac{-\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}z}

and it is easy to check that all
\lambda_{i}
are roots of the polynomial

\tilde{v}(z)=z^{q}+\sum_{j=1}^{q}b_{j}z^{q-j}
.
Hence they are algebraic integers.
∎

are distinct algebraic integers, holds.
It turns out that if
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}
are algebraically conjugate then
\chi_{i}=\chi_{j}
.
Indeed, denote by
v(z)
an irreducible polynomial roots of which are
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}
.
Denote by
\Sigma
the field of
v(z)
and let
\sigma
be an element of the Galois group of
\Sigma
.
By definition
\sigma
is the identity function on
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
It is well-known that
\sigma
acts as a permutation of the set of roots of
v(z)
,
denote the induced permutation of indices by
\sigma
as well,
i.e.
\sigma(\lambda_{i})=\lambda_{\sigma(i)}
.
We get

\sigma\left(R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})\right)=\sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\chi_{i}\sigma(\lambda_{i})^{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\chi_{i}\lambda_{\sigma(i)}^{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\chi_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}\lambda_{i}^{k}.

On the other hand a Reidemeister coincidence number is an integer
therefore
\sigma\left(R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})\right)=R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})

and for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
we get

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{r}\chi_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}\lambda_{i}^{k}.

This equality holds especially for
k=1,\dots r
, let us write it in a matrix form:

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&\dots&\lambda_{r}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\lambda_{1}^{r}&\dots&\lambda_{r}^{r}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\chi_{r}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&\dots&\lambda_{r}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\lambda_{1}^{r}&\dots&\lambda_{r}^{r}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\\
\vdots\\
\chi_{\sigma^{-1}(r)}\end{pmatrix}.

Since all
\lambda_{i}
are distinct, the matrix is invertible (as the Vandermonde matrix),
hence
\chi_{i}=\chi_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}
for all
i
and for arbitrary
\sigma
.
We know that for every pair
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}
there is an automorphism
\sigma

such that
\sigma(\lambda_{i})=\lambda_{j}
.
It means that
\chi_{i}=\chi_{j}
whenever
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}
are algebraically conjugate.

Now let
v(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\lambda_{i}-z)
be a polynomial roots of which are all
\lambda_{i}
.
Let

\displaystyle v(z)=\prod_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}(z)=\prod_{\alpha}\left(z^{r_{\alpha}}+b_{1}^{(\alpha)}z^{r_{\alpha}-1}+\dots+b_{r_{\alpha}-1}^{(\alpha)}z+b_{r_{\alpha}}^{(\alpha)}\right),\quad b_{i}^{(\alpha)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

be its decomposition into irreducible polynomials.
We split the set
A=A^{+}\cup A^{-}
of indices
\alpha
into two subsets according to the sign of
\chi_{\alpha}
.
We can write

(26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})&=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\chi_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r_{\alpha}}\left(\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{i}\right)^{k}\right)\\
&=\sum_{\alpha\in A^{+}}\chi_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r_{\alpha}}\left(\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{i}\right)^{k}\right)-\sum_{\alpha\in A^{-}}|\chi_{\alpha}|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r_{\alpha}}\left(\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{i}\right)^{k}\right).\end{split}

Consider the integer matrix

M_{\alpha}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\dotsb&0&-b_{r_{\alpha}}^{(\alpha)}\\
1&0&\dotsb&0&-b_{r_{\alpha-1}}^{(\alpha)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dotsb&0&-b_{2}^{(\alpha)}\\
0&0&\dotsb&1&-b_{1}^{(\alpha)}\\
\end{pmatrix}

Straightforward computation using the Laplace expansion shows that
\det(zI-M_{\alpha})=v_{\alpha}(z)

therefore
\{\lambda_{i}^{(\alpha)}\}
are the eigenvalues of
M_{\alpha}
.
Continuing (
26
) we write

R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})=\sum_{\alpha\in A^{+}}\chi_{\alpha}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits M_{\alpha}^{k}-\sum_{\alpha\in A^{-}}|\chi_{\alpha}|\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits M_{\alpha}^{k}

Denote

A_{e}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A^{+}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\chi_{\alpha}}M_{\alpha},\quad A_{o}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A^{-}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{|\chi_{\alpha}|}M_{\alpha}.

Using this notation we finally have

R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits A_{e}^{k}-\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits A_{o}^{k}.

The thesis follows from the next Lemma
18
.
∎

The next lemma is a result of

Lemma 18

(

Let X be the bouquet of

Proof.

7.
Nielsen coincidence zeta function, the growth rate and the Gauss congruences for the sequence
\{N(f^{n},g^{n})\}

Let
f,g:X\to Y
be two maps between two compact connected manifolds.
Maps
f,g
induce homomorphisms

between fundamental groups of
X
and
Y
.
In section

we have defined the

If

R^{\infty}(f,g):=\lim_{n\to\infty}R(f^{n},g^{n})^{1/n}=R^{\infty}(f_{\#},g_{\#})

In the same manner as with the Reidemeister numbers we define the growth rate of the sequence
{
N(f^{n},g^{n})\}
of the Nielsen coincidence numbers
(if it exists)

N^{\infty}(f,g):=\lim_{n\to\infty}N(f^{n},g^{n})^{1/n}.

In this section we are going to work with compact nilmanifolds.
Let
G
be a Lie group and let
{\Gamma}\subset G
be its discrete subgroup.

{\Gamma}
is a
lattice
if there exists a finite
G
-invariant measure on
G/{\Gamma}
.
A compact manifold
X
of dimension

is a

if it is a quotient space
G/{\Gamma}

of a simply-connected nilpotent Lie group
G
by a lattice
{\Gamma}
of rank
d
(see
[
14
, Definition 6.2.5]
).

We are going to make use of the following result which shows connections between different coincidence numbers
under certain assumptions.
For a definition of the Lefschetz coincidence number

see for example
[

]
.

Theorem 21

(
[
12
, Theorem 2.3]
)
.

Let
f,g:N\to N
be a pair of maps where

is a compact nilmanifold.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

N(f,g)\neq 0
;

(ii)

\operatorname{Coin}(f_{\#},g_{\#})=1
;

(iii)

R(f,g)<\infty
.

If one of the conditions holds, then

R(f,g)=N(f,g)=|L(f,g)|.

The theorem above along with other results of this paper motivate us to define and study
the
Nielsen coincidence zeta function
of a pair
f,g:N\to N
of maps
of a compact polyhedron to itself

(28)

N_{f,g}(z):=\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{N(f^{n},g^{n})}{n}z^{n}\right).

Because of finitness of Nielsen coincidence numbers,
the Nielsen coincidence zeta function is always well-defined.
If the pair
f,g
is not tame then the Reidemeister coincidence zeta function is not defined
therefore the Nielsen coincidence zeta function is a different object in general.

We state our main result regarding Nielsen coincidence zeta function.

Theorem 22
.

Let
f,g:N\to N
be a tame pair of maps of a compact nilmanifold to itself.
Denote by
f_{\#},g_{\#}:G\to G
endomorphisms induced on the fundamental group of
N
.

G
is a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group.
Let
c
denote the nilpotency class of
G
and for
1\leq k\leq c
let

{f_{\#}}_{k},{g_{\#}}_{k}:G_{k}\to G_{k}
denote the tame pairs of induced endomorphisms
of the finitely generated torsion-free abelian factor groups

G_{k}=\sqrt[G]{\gamma_{k}(G)}/\sqrt[G]{\gamma_{k+1}(G)}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{d_{k}}

for some
d_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, that arise from the isolated lower central series of
G
.
Every
G_{k}
is a fundamental group of the
d_{k}
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d_{k}}
which is also a compact nilmanifold.
Therefore every endomorphism
{f_{\#}}_{k},{g_{\#}}_{k}
can be realized by a selfmap

f_{k},g_{k}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d_{k}}\to{\mathbb{T}}^{d_{k}}
.

Then the following hold.

(i)

For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

(29)

N(f^{n},g^{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{c}N(f_{k}^{n},g_{k}^{n})

(ii)

For
1\leq k\leq c
let

{f_{\#}}_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}},{g_{\#}}_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}}:G_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}}\to G_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}}

denote the extensions of
{f_{\#}}_{k},{g_{\#}}_{k}
to the divisible hull

G_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}}={\mathbb{Q}}\otimes G_{k}\cong{\mathbb{Q}}^{d_{k}}
.
Suppose each pair of endomorphisms
{f_{\#}}_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}},{g_{\#}}_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}}
is simultaneously triangularizable.
Let
\xi_{k,1},\ldots,\xi_{k,d_{k}}
and

\eta_{k,1},\ldots,\eta_{k,d_{k}}
be the eigenvalues of

{f_{\#}}_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
{g_{\#}}_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}}
in the field
{\mathbb{C}}
, including
multiplicities, ordered so that, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
eigenvalues of
{f_{\#}}_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}}^{\,n}-{g_{\#}}_{k,{\mathbb{Q}}}^{\,n}

\xi_{k,1}^{\,n}-\eta_{k,1}^{\,n},\ldots,\xi_{k,d_{k}}^{\,n}-\eta_{k,d_{k}}^{\,n}
.
Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

(30)

N(f_{k}^{n},g_{k}^{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\lvert\xi_{k,i}^{\,n}-\eta_{k,i}^{\,n}\rvert

(iii)

Suppose that
|\xi_{k,i}|\neq|\eta_{k,i}|

for
1\leq k\leq c,\ 1\leq i\leq d_{k}
.
Then the Nielsen coincidence zeta function

is rational.

(iv)

With the same assumption

for
1\leq k\leq c,\ 1\leq i\leq d_{k}

the growth rate of Nielsen coincidence numbers exists and can be computed using the explicit formula

(31)

N^{\infty}(f,g)=\prod_{k=1}^{c}\prod_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\max\{|\xi_{k,i}|,|\eta_{k,i}|\}.

(v)

With the same assumption
|\xi_{k,i}|\neq|\eta_{k,i}|
for
1\leq k\leq c,\ 1\leq i\leq d_{k}

we have the Gauss congruences

(32)

\sum_{k|n}\mu(n/k)\cdot N(f^{k},g^{k})\equiv 0\mod n

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Assumption of tameness of the pair
(f,g)
gives us by definition
the condition (iii) in Theorem
21
for all iterations
f^{n},g^{n}
.
Therefore for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}N(f^{n},g^{n})=R(f^{n},g^{n})=R(f_{\#}^{n},g_{\#}^{n}),\\
N(f_{k}^{n},g_{k}^{n})=R(f_{k}^{n},g_{k}^{n})=R({f_{\#}}_{k}^{n},{g_{\#}}_{k}^{n}).\end{gathered}

From now on we can follow arguments from the proof of Theorem
13
.

To prove (i) it is sufficient to make use of the Roman’kov formula (
14
)
for endomorphisms

. We get

N(f^{n},g^{n})=R(f_{\#}^{n},g_{\#}^{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{c}R({f_{\#}}_{k}^{n},{g_{\#}}_{k}^{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{c}N(f_{k}^{n},g_{k}^{n}).

To prove (ii) observe that the endomorphisms
{f_{\#}}_{k},{g_{\#}}_{k}:G_{k}\to G_{k}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{d_{k}}

are represented by integer matrices

There are unimodular matrices

The order of the cokernel of

is the order of the group

(35)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R({f_{\#}}_{k}^{n},{g_{\#}}_{k}^{n})&=|\operatorname{Coker}({f_{\#}}_{k}^{n}-{g_{\#}}_{k}^{n})|=|c_{n,1}\dots c_{n,d_{k}}|\\

\end{split}

Now using (

).

Complex eigenvalues

Reorder the eigenvalues so that for

N(f_{k}^{n},g_{k}^{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{d_{k}}|\xi_{k,i}^{n}-\eta_{k,i}^{n}|=\sum\pm(\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\cdots\mu_{s/2}\nu_{s/2}\delta_{\epsilon(1),k,s+1}\cdots\delta_{\epsilon(T),k,d_{k}})^{n},

,

.
Consequently, the Nielsen coincidence zeta function

\displaystyle N_{f,g}(z)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{N(f^{n},g^{n})}{n}z^{n}\right)=\exp\left(\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(w_{j}z)^{n}}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\in J}(1-w_{j}z)^{-c_{j}}

To prove (iv) note that in (

To prove (v) observe that the assumption of tameness gives us equality of zeta functions

By (iii) these zeta functions are rational.

R_{f_{\#},g_{\#}}(z)
fulfills assumptions of Theorem

and the congruences (

8.

Lemma 24

\lambda(\varphi,\psi)=1/\mathcal{R}

Proof.

From the Cauchy-Hadamard formula there is

Taking into account the equality (

\displaystyle\sqrt[k]{R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt[k]{\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|=\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}+\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|<\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}}

\displaystyle=\lambda(\varphi,\psi)\cdot\sqrt[k]{\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|=\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\chi_{i}\cdot C_{i}^{k}+\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|<\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\chi_{i}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\right)^{k}},

where
C_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
are constants such that
|C_{i}|=1
for all
i
.
The module of the sum under the root is bounded,
therefore the module of the whole root goes to
1
when
k\to\infty
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

Theorem 26
.

Let
\varphi,\psi:G\to G
be a tame pair of endomorphisms of a torsion-free nilpotent group
G

of finite Prüfer rank and let the Reidemeister coincidence zeta function
R_{\varphi,\psi}(z)
be rational.
One of the two possibilities holds:

(i)

The sequence
\{R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})\ /\ \lambda(\varphi,\psi)^{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
has the same
limit points set as a periodic sequence
\{\sum_{j=1}^{n(\varphi,\psi)}\alpha_{j}\epsilon_{j}^{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

where
\alpha_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ \epsilon_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\epsilon_{j}^{q}=1
for some integer
q>0
.

(ii)

The set of limit points of the sequence
\{R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})\ /\ \lambda(\varphi,\psi)^{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

contains an interval.

Proof.

We factor every
\lambda_{i}
for which
|\lambda_{i}|=\lambda(\varphi,\psi)

in (
22
) as

\lambda_{i}=\lambda(\varphi,\psi)\exp(2\pi\iota\theta_{i}),

where
\iota^{2}=-1
is the imaginary unit.
Then

R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})=\lambda(\varphi,\psi)^{k}\left(\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|=\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\chi_{i}\exp(2\pi\iota k\theta_{i})\right)+\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|<\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\chi_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}

and

\left|\frac{R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})}{\lambda(\varphi,\psi)^{k}}-\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|=\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\chi_{i}\exp(2\pi\iota k\theta_{i})\right|\leq\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|<\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}|\chi_{i}|\cdot\left|\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\right|^{k}.

Right hand side goes to
0
when
k\to\infty
therefore the sequences

\left\{R(\varphi^{k},\psi^{k})\ /\ \lambda(\varphi,\psi)^{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\mbox{\quad and \quad}\left\{\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|=\lambda(\varphi,\psi)}\chi_{i}\exp(2\pi\iota k\theta_{i})\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

have the same asymptotic behavior.
Now we claim that the structure of the limit points set of the latter sequence
depends on rationality of all
\theta_{i}
.

Assume that for all
i=1,\dots,n(\varphi,\psi)
we can write
\theta_{i}=p_{i}/q_{i}
where
p_{i},q_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Put
q:=\operatorname{lcm}(q_{1},\dots,q_{n(\varphi,\psi)})
.
Whenever
k
is a multiplicity of
q
, all
\exp(2\pi\iota k\theta_{i})=1
.
This is the case (i).

Now assume that some of
\theta_{i}
are irrational and denote the subset of their indices
by
\mathcal{S}:=\left\{i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\right\}\subset\{1,\dots,n(\varphi,\psi)\}
.
We can express the assumption as linear independence

\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\alpha_{i}\theta_{i}=\alpha,\quad\alpha_{i},\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right)\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\forall_{i}\ \alpha_{i}=\alpha=0.

Then the Kronecker theorem (see
[
5
, Theorem VIII.6]
) states that the sequence
\left\{(k\theta_{i_{1}},\dots,k\theta_{i_{s}})\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is dense
in the
s
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{s}
.

Consider the continuous function

h:\mathbb{T}^{n(\varphi,\psi)}\to[0,\infty),\qquad h(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n(\varphi,\psi)})=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n(\varphi,\psi)}\chi_{i}\exp(2\pi\iota\xi_{i})\right|.

We can consider the subtorus

\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{S}}:=\left\{(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n(\varphi,\psi)})\in\mathbb{T}^{n(\varphi,\psi)}\mid\xi_{j}=0,\forall j\notin\mathcal{S}\right\}

For
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{S}}
is compact and the restriction

h_{\mathcal{S}}:=\left.h\right|_{\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{S}}}

is continuous, there exists the maximum
m_{\mathcal{S}}:=\max_{\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{S}}}h_{\mathcal{S}}(\xi)
.
Observe that the closure of the image of the dense sequence contains the interval

\overline{h_{\mathcal{S}}\big(\left\{k\theta_{i_{1}},\dots,k\theta_{i_{s}}\right\}_{k}\big)}\ \supset\ [0,m_{\mathcal{S}}].

This is the case (ii).
∎
