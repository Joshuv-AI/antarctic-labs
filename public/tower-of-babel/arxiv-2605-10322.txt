Continuous Data Assimilation for Semilinear Parabolic Equations with Multiplicative Observation Noise
Jochen Bröcker, Gianmarco Del Sarto, Matthias Hieber, Filippo Palma, Tarek Zöchling

Abstract
. The problem of continuous data assimilation for semilinear parabolic equations based on partial observations corrupted by noise is investigated. The noise is allowed to be multiplicative , with additive noise arising as a special case. In a general Gelfand triple framework, an abstract theory for the nudging equation is developed that covers both weak and strong formulations. Mean square convergence of the assimilation error is proved under suitable assumptions, and, under additional integrability conditions on the noise, a uniform almost sure convergence result is established. Finally, the framework is applied to several PDE models, including the 2D Navier-Stokes, 2D magnetohydrodynamics, 2D quasi-geostrophic, and 1D Allen-Cahn equations.

1.
Introduction

Continuous data assimilation aims at reconstructing the state of an evolution equation from incomplete observations by combining the available measurements with the model dynamics through a suitable feedback mechanism
[
20
,
27
,
14
]
. It is a central ingredient in modern forecasting, especially in numerical weather prediction, where data assimilation is a major component of the forecasting system and one of its most computationally demanding parts; see, for instance,
[
26
,
23
]
. In continuous time, this is often achieved by
nudging
: the reconstructed trajectory is evolved by an identical copy of the underlying evolution equation, but continuously corrected by feeding into the equation the discrepancy between the observed coarse-scale data and the corresponding coarse-scale prediction produced by the reconstructed trajectory
[
4
,
22
]
.

Our mathematical setup is broadly as follows, see
Section 2
for details. Let
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
be a Gelfand triple, let
A\colon\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
be a bounded linear operator, and let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
. We consider, for
T>0
, the semilinear parabolic evolution equation

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+Au&=F(u),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
u
is regarded as the unknown reference, or true, trajectory. Since the initial condition
u_{0}
is not available to the observer, the full trajectory
u
is itself unknown. The problem of data assimilation is then to use only partial observations of this trajectory to construct a second trajectory that synchronises with it.

We assume that only coarse-scale observations of
u
are available through an interpolation/observation operator
\mathrm{I}_{\delta}
, where
\delta>0
denotes the observation scale. The operator
\mathrm{I}_{\delta}
will approximate the identity as
\delta\to 0
, in a sense to be made precise later. A central feature of the present paper is that these observations are noisy, and the noise intensity may depend on the reference trajectory itself. More precisely, we assume that the observation process
(y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies

(1.2)

\,\mathrm{d}y_{t}=\mathrm{I}_{\delta}u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+G_{\delta}(u(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{Q}.

Here,
(W_{t}^{Q})_{t}
denotes a
Q
-Wiener process on
\mathcal{H}
.
In other words, we consider
multiplicative noise
in the observation process, with the additive-noise case recovered when the noise coefficient
G_{\delta}
is independent of the solution.
This setting of multiplicative noise is physically relevant, since realistic observations are affected not only by instrument and representativeness errors, but often also by state-dependent or flow-dependent uncertainties, see, e.g.,
[
16
,
10
,
7
]
.
Furthermore, we assume the noise coefficient
G_{\delta}
to be dependent on the observation scale
\delta
.
Following the discussion in
[
6
]
, our motivation for this is that in typical applications, the “raw” observations delivered by the measurement apparatus are of the form

(1.3)

\,\mathrm{d}o_{t}=M_{\delta}u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma_{\delta}(u(t))\,\mathrm{d}B_{t},

where
M_{\delta}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a finite rank operator,
B
is a standard
N
–dimensional Wiener process, and
\sigma_{\delta}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
for each
\delta>0
, with
N
typically increasing as
\delta
decreases.
The interpolation of those “raw” observations is what gives rise to
\{y_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
in (
1.2
) by applying an interpolation operator
L_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{H}
to (
1.3
).
This gives
\mathrm{I}_{\delta}=L_{\delta}M_{\delta}
and
G_{\delta}=L_{\delta}\sigma_{\delta}
.
We also stress that even if
\sigma_{\delta}
does not depend on
u
, it should be generally permitted to depend on
\delta
.
Typically, the standard
N
–dimensional Wiener process in (
1.3
) needs to be rescaled with
\delta
as otherwise a trivial limit
G_{\delta}\to 0
for
\delta\to 0
might occur.

Starting from these observations, we introduce a reconstructed trajectory
v
, initialized from an arbitrary datum
v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, and evolve it according to the same model dynamics as
u
(i.e. equation (
1.1
)), but with an additional correction term. This is the
nudging term
, namely the feedback of the error

\mathrm{I}_{\delta}u(t)-\mathrm{I}_{\delta}v_{t},

which measures the discrepancy between the observed and predicted coarse-scale data. The resulting stochastic data-assimilation system is

(
DA
sto
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}v_{t}+Av_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t&=F(v_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\bigl(\mathrm{I}_{\delta}v_{t}-\mathrm{I}_{\delta}u(t)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t+\mu\,G_{\delta}(u(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{Q},\qquad t\in(0,T),\\
v(0)&=v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}.\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mu>0
is the nudging parameter. Larger values of
\mu
correspond to a stronger feedback, whereas
\delta
determines the amount of information carried by the observations.

Our aim is to investigate the data-assimilation error

w:=u-v.

Our first group of main results gives quantitative mean-square estimates for the assimilation error. Under suitable structural assumptions on the drift, the observation operator, and the noise coefficient, we prove that the error decays exponentially fast in mean square up to a stochastic residual term, provided
\mu
is large enough to stabilise the data-assimilation system (
DAsto
), while
\mu\delta^{2}\lesssim 1
, where
\delta
measures the size of
\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{I}_{\delta}
as an operator from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
. Thus, in the presence of non-vanishing observation noise, synchronisation holds only up to a noise-dependent remainder. If the noise intensity is uniformly bounded along the reference trajectory, this yields an explicit
noise floor
, namely an upper bound on the asymptotic mean-square error. We also obtain the corresponding estimate in the
\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
-norm. Finally, when the deterministic dynamics admits a compact global attractor and the noise coefficient is continuous near it, the asymptotic error can be controlled only in terms of the values of the noise on the attractor, rather than along the full reference trajectory.

Our final main result concerns almost sure synchronisation. Under additional integrability assumptions on the stochastic forcing along the reference trajectory, we prove that the residual stochastic contribution becomes asymptotically negligible and that the convergence can be upgraded to
\mathbb{P}
-almost sure convergence, uniformly on the tail. In particular,

\sup_{t\geq N}\|u(t)-v_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. as }N\to\infty.

This attractor-based viewpoint is especially useful for long-time dynamics: if the noise vanishes on the attractor and is locally Lipschitz near it, then the additional integrability condition is naturally satisfied under a suitable rate of attraction. To the best of our knowledge, this uniform almost sure synchronisation result is essentially new in the literature on stochastic continuous data assimilation with multiplicative noise. The only related result we are aware of is the recent work
[
5
]
, where an almost sure convergence result is obtained for the two-dimensional stochastic Navier-Stokes equations (NSEs), with multiplicative noise in the dynamics rather than in the observations.

Another feature of the paper is that the analysis is carried out in a flexible abstract setting, which allows us to treat both weak and strong formulations within the same framework. In the weak setting, we apply the theory to the two-dimensional NSEs, the two-dimensional magnetohydrodynamics equations, the two-dimensional quasi-geostrophic equations, and the one-dimensional Allen–Cahn equation. We also revisit the two-dimensional NSEs and the one-dimensional Allen–Cahn equation in a stronger functional setting, obtaining convergence results in stronger norms.

Let us briefly place our results in the existing literature. Our abstract framework is closely connected with our recent work on deterministic continuous data assimilation for semilinear parabolic equations
[
13
]
. More broadly, deterministic data assimilation has been studied for a variety of models, including the 2D Bénard convection problem
[
15
]
, the 3D Navier-Stokes-
\alpha
model
[
3
]
, the 3D Navier-Stokes equations
[
8
]
, reaction-diffusion equations
[
21
]
, and the two-dimensional Cahn-Hilliard-Navier-Stokes system
[
30
]
. In the stochastic or noisy-data setting, classical and more recent contributions, both abstract and computational, include
[
6
,
9
,
17
,
5
,
19
]
. In particular,
[
6
]
treats the two-dimensional NSEs with additive noise in the observations, while
[
5
]
studies continuous data assimilation for the two-dimensional stochastic NSEs with multiplicative noise in the dynamics, but no noise in the observations. Compared with these works, the present paper treats multiplicative noise in the observations and develops an abstract variational theory for a broad class of semilinear parabolic equations, yielding synchronisation in mean square and, under suitable assumptions, also almost surely.

The paper is organised as follows. In
Section 2
we introduce the abstract framework and state the main results.
Section 3
illustrates the theory on several concrete PDE models in both weak and strong settings. In
Section 4
we establish the preparatory well-posedness results for both the reference and the assimilated systems.
Section 5
is devoted to the proofs of the abstract convergence theorems.
Appendix A
contains an auxiliary local well-posedness result for non-autonomous semilinear equations, which is used in the proof of the stochastic data-assimilation well-posedness result.

2.
Assumptions and main results

In this section we state the structural assumptions on the deterministic equation (
1.1
), on the observation operator
\mathrm{I}_{\delta}
, and on the noise coefficient
G_{\delta}
, and then present our main convergence results for the data-assimilation error.

A
Gelfand triple
is a triple of real Hilbert spaces

\mathcal{V}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\ast},

with dense and continuous embeddings, where
\mathcal{H}
is identified with its dual and

\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=(u,v)_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad u\in\mathcal{H},v\in\mathcal{V}.

For a compatible pair of Banach spaces
(X_{0},X_{1})
, we denote by
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}
and
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
the real and complex interpolation spaces, respectively.

Let
\mathrm{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\in\mathbb{N}
, be a sufficiently regular domain. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, we denote by
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathrm{D})
and
\mathrm{H}^{m,q}(\mathrm{D})=\mathrm{W}^{m,q}(\mathrm{D})
the Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, and by
\|\cdot\|_{q}
and
\|\cdot\|_{m,q}
their norms. As is usual in the literature, in the case
q=2
we set
\mathrm{W}^{m,2}(\mathrm{D})=\mathrm{H}^{m}(\mathrm{D})
and we denote by
\mathrm{H}^{m}_{0}(\mathrm{D})
the subset of
\mathrm{H}^{m}(\mathrm{D})
of functions with zero trace. We recall that, see
[
29
]
, the space
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathrm{D})
,
q\in(1,\infty)
, admits the decomposition

\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathrm{D})=\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D})\oplus G^{q}(\mathrm{D})\,,

where
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D})
is the subset of functions in
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathrm{D})
that are weakly divergence-free and that have zero generalized trace, while
G^{q}(\mathrm{D})
is the subset of functions
v
in
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathrm{D})
such that
v=\nabla h
,
h\in\mathrm{W}^{1,q}_{loc}(\mathrm{D})
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{q}
the Helmholtz projection

\mathcal{P}_{q}:\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathrm{D})\to\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D})\,.

In the case
q=2
, we set
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathcal{P}
. For Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
the space of bounded linear operators from
X
to
Y
, and with
X^{\prime}
the dual space of
X
. For more details on interpolation and function spaces we refer, for instance, to
[
1
,
24
]
.

We work on a complete filtered probability space
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t},\mathbb{P}\right)
. A stochastic process
\Phi
, taking values in a measurable space, is adapted if
\Phi_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable for any
t\geq 0
. It is progressively measurable if the map
(s,\omega)\mapsto\Phi_{s}(\omega)
is measurable on
([0,t]\times\Omega,\mathcal{B}([0,t])\otimes\mathcal{F}_{t})
for every
t\geq 0
, with
\mathcal{B}([0,t])
being the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
[0,t].
Given a real, separable, Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we denote by
(W_{t}^{Q})_{t}
a
Q
-Wiener process on
\mathcal{H}
, where
Q
is a non-negative, self-adjoint, trace-class operator on
\mathcal{H}
. If
(e_{k})_{k}\subset\mathcal{H}
denotes an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
made of eigenvectors for
Q
, i.e.
Qe_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{2}e_{k}
, then

W_{t}^{Q}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}e_{k}\beta_{k}(t),\qquad t\geq 0,

where
(\beta_{k}(t))_{k}
are independent Brownian motions on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t},\mathbb{P})
, and
(\lambda_{k})_{k}
are the non-negative square roots of the eigenvalues. We also denote by
\mathrm{L}_{2}(U,H)
the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators between Hilbert spaces
U
and
H
. For further details, we refer to
[
12
]
.

2.1.
The setting

Let
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
be a
Gelfand triple
of real Hilbert spaces such that the pairing between
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
satisfies

\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=(u,v)_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad u\in\mathcal{H},v\in\mathcal{V}.

We further assume that for the real interpolation space it holds

(\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V})_{\frac{1}{2},2}=\mathcal{H}.

For
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, we set

\mathcal{V}_{\beta}=[\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}]_{\beta}\,.

Our first group of assumptions is purely deterministic.

(A1):

A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
is coercive, i.e., for all
u\in\mathcal{V}
it holds that

\langle Au,u\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\geq\alpha\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}\qquad\text{for some }\alpha>0.

(A2):

The nonlinearity can be written as

F=\sum_{j=1}^{m}F_{j},

where, for each
j=1,\dots,m
, there exist

\beta_{j}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
\rho_{j}\geq 0
, and
C_{j}>0
such that

(2.1)

\|F_{j}(u)-F_{j}(v)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C_{j}\bigl(1+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}+\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}\bigr)\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}},\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{V}.

Moreover,

(2.2)

(2\beta_{j}-1)(\rho_{j}+1)\leq 1,\qquad j=1,\dots,m.

Assumption
\mathbf{(A1)}
gives the coercivity of the linear part, while
\mathbf{(A2)}
controls the nonlinear term in a critical interpolation scale. Together, these two assumptions guarantee the local well-posedness of (
1.1
).

Remark 2.1
.

Several models in fluid mechanics, such as the Navier-Stokes equations, have a bilinear nonlinearity
F(u)=B(u,u),
with a bounded
B:\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\times\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\to\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
. Then

\|F(u)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta}}^{2},

and
{\bf(A2)}
holds with
m=1
,
\rho_{1}=1
and
\beta_{1}=\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4}]
.

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

Note that, by general theory, assumption
(A1)
implies that
-A
generates an analytic semigroup on
\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
.

(ii)

Since
(\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V})_{\frac{1}{2},2}=\mathcal{H}
, the operator
A
enjoys
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-stochastic maximal regularity, see
[
2
, Theorem 3.13]
.

To exclude finite-time blow-up and to obtain well-posedness of the data-assimilation system, we impose the following structural assumption on
F
.

(A3):

For each
j=1,\dots,m
, one of the following alternatives holds.

(i):

If
\frac{3}{4}\leq\beta_{j}<1
, then there exist

\varepsilon_{j}>0
and
C_{j}^{(0)},C_{j}^{(1)}\geq 0
such that

(2.3)

\langle F_{j}(x),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{j}\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{j}^{(1)}\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C_{j}^{(0)},\qquad x\in\mathcal{V}.

(ii):

If
\frac{1}{2}<\beta_{j}<\frac{3}{4}
, then there exist

\varepsilon_{j}>0
,
C_{j}^{(0)},C_{j}^{(1)}\geq 0
, and a measurable map

\Psi_{j}:\mathcal{V}\to[0,\infty)
such that

(2.4)

\langle F_{j}(x+y),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{j}\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{j}^{(0)}\bigl(1+\Psi_{j}(y)\bigr)\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C_{j}^{(1)}\Psi_{j}(y),\qquad x,y\in\mathcal{V}.

Moreover, for every
t>0
and every

z\in\mathrm{C}([0,t];\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V})
, one has

\Psi_{j}(z(\cdot))\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,t)
.

Furthermore, the constants
\varepsilon_{j}
satisfy
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}<\frac{\alpha}{4}.

We now fix
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
and denote by
u
the corresponding solution of
(
1.1
), whose existence and uniqueness in

\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,T;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})\cap\mathrm{BUC}([0,T];\mathcal{H})

follow
from
(A1)
–
(A3)
, see
Section 4
. We
call
u
the
reference trajectory
. We impose the following natural integrability condition on the noise coefficient
G_{\delta}
.

(N1):

Assume
\mathcal{H}
separable. Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}:=Q^{1/2}\mathcal{H}
and
\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}:=\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{H})
.
There exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that, for every
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}

and every reference trajectory
u
,

(2.5)

\int_{0}^{T}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,\qquad T>0.

Note that we do
not
impose bounds on
G_{\delta}
uniform in
\delta
.

The next assumption is an additional
one-sided stability condition on the increments of
F
along
u
, used to
control the nonlinear term in the error equation. Here
\alpha
is the coercivity constant from
(A1)
.

(A4):

There exist
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{4})
, constants
M_{0},M_{1}\geq 0
, and a non-negative measurable function

\kappa_{u}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

such that

(2.6)

\langle F(u(t))-F(u(t)-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{1}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\kappa_{u}(t)\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathcal{V},

for a.e.
t\geq 0
, and

(2.7)

\int_{s}^{t}\kappa_{u}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\leq M_{0}(t-s)+M_{1},\qquad\forall\,0\leq s\leq t.

Lastly, the observation operator
\mathrm{I}_{\delta}
is assumed to satisfy

(2.8)

\langle f-\mathrm{I}_{\delta}f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq C_{\mathrm{I}}\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{V}}\quad\text{ for all $f\in\mathcal{H}$, $g\in\mathcal{V}$, and $0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}$},

for a constant
C_{\mathrm{I}}>0
.

Remark 2.3
.

A natural structural condition which simultaneously implies the shifted well-posedness assumption
(A3)
-(ii) and the convergence assumption
(A4)
is the following.

(R):

There exist
\varepsilon_{R}\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{4})
, a constant
C_{R}\geq 0
, and a measurable map

\Psi\colon\mathcal{V}\to[0,\infty)
such that

\langle F(x+y)-F(x),y\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{R}\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{R}\bigl(1+\Psi(x)\bigr)\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathcal{V}.

Moreover, for every
T>0
and every
z\in\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V})
, one has
\Psi(z(\cdot))\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T).
Finally, along the reference trajectory
u
, there exist constants
M_{0},M_{1}\geq 0
such that

(2.9)

\int_{s}^{t}\Psi(u(r))\,\mathrm{d}r\leq M_{0}(t-s)+M_{1},

for any
0\leq s\leq t.

Condition
(R)
gives a practical way of verifying the two
one-sided estimates used in the analysis. First,
(R)
yields
(A4)
. Taking

x=u(t)
and
y=-\xi
in
(R)
gives

\langle F(u(t))-F(u(t)-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{R}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{R}\bigl(1+\Psi(u(t))\bigr)\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Hence
(A4)
holds with

\kappa_{u}(t)=C_{R}(1+\Psi(u(t)))
, and (
2.7
) follows from
(
2.9
). Second,
(R)
also gives the shifted estimate needed in

(A3)
-(ii). Writing

\langle F(x+y),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=\langle F(x+y)-F(y),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}+\langle F(y),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}},

the first term is controlled by
(R)

\langle F(x+y)-F(y),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{R}\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{R}\bigl(1+\Psi(y)\bigr)\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

The second term is controlled by
(A2)
and Young’s inequality

|\langle F(y),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}|\leq\varepsilon\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(1+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{2(\rho_{j}+1)}\Bigr).

The interpolation restriction
(2\beta_{j}-1)(\rho_{j}+1)\leq 1
ensures that the last quantity is integrable whenever

y\in\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V})
. Therefore, after replacing
\Psi
by

\widetilde{\Psi}(y):=\Psi(y)+1+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{2(\rho_{j}+1)},

condition
(R)
implies the shifted one-sided estimate required in

(A3)
-(ii), as well as the stability assumption
(A4)
. In the componentwise formulation of
(A3)
, the same argument can be
applied to each
F_{j}
, with constants
\varepsilon_{R,j}>0
chosen so that
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{R,j}<\frac{\alpha}{4}.

2.2.
Main results

We now introduce the assimilation error

w:=u-v,

which measures the discrepancy between the reference solution
u
and the nudged stochastic approximation
v
. The error equation for
w
is obtained by subtracting (
DAsto
) from (
1.1
),

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}w_{t}+Aw_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t&=\bigl(F(u(t))-F(v_{t})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta}w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu G_{\delta}(u(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{Q},\qquad t\in(0,T),\\
w(0)&=w_{0}:=u_{0}-v_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

The next theorem gives the basic mean-square estimate for the assimilation
error. For sufficiently large
\mu
and sufficiently small
\delta
, with

\mu\delta^{2}
bounded, the deterministic part of the error is exponentially damped, while the remaining term is driven by the observational noise.

Theorem 2.4

(Mean-square convergence up to the stochastic forcing)
.

Let
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, and suppose that
\bf(A1)
–
\bf(A4)
and
\bf(N1)
are satisfied. Assume moreover that
\mathrm{I}_{\delta}
satisfies (
2.8
), and let

\delta_{0}>0
be chosen so that both
(N1)
and
(
2.8
) hold for every
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
. Then there exist constants
\mu_{0},\eta_{0}>0
such that for every
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and every
\delta>0
satisfying

\mu\delta^{2}\leq\eta_{0},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta\leq\delta_{0}

the unique solution of (
2.10
) satisfies

(2.11)

\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|w_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

for some positive constants
\gamma=\gamma(\mu,\delta)
and
C
independent of
t
.

Estimate (
2.11
) yields exponential decay of the initial
error, up to a contribution from the observational noise. If the noise is
uniformly bounded along the reference trajectory, this contribution remains
uniformly bounded in time and the error decays exponentially towards a
noise floor.

Corollary 2.5

(Noise floor)
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem 2.4
, assume in addition that

(2.12)

\Gamma_{u}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}<\infty.

Then, for all
t\geq 0
,

(2.13)

\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|w_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u},

where
\gamma,C>0
are the constants from
Theorem 2.4
. In particular,

(2.14)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u}.

Corollary 2.6

(Convergence in the
\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
-norm)
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem 2.4
, the unique solution of (
2.10
) satisfies

(2.15)

\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|w_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

where
\gamma,C>0
are the constants from
Theorem 2.4
. In particular, if (
2.12
) holds, then

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u}.

The previous bound still depends on the full trajectory
u
through the quantity
\Gamma_{u}
. When the deterministic dynamics admit a compact global attractor and the noise coefficient is continuous near that attractor, the asymptotic error can be estimated only in terms of the values of
G_{\delta}
on the attractor itself.

Corollary 2.7

(Attractor-dependent noise floor)
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem 2.4
, assume in addition that the
deterministic semiflow generated by (
1.1
) possesses a compact global
attractor
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{H}
, and that there exists an open neighborhood

\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{A}
such that

G_{\delta}|_{\mathcal{U}}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}
is continuous. Then

\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}:=\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\|G_{\delta}(a)\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}<\infty,

and

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}},

where
\gamma,C>0
are the constants from
Theorem 2.4
.

Finally, under an additional integrability assumption on the stochastic forcing along the reference trajectory, the mean-square convergence can be upgraded to uniform almost sure convergence. To the best of our knowledge, this is new for stochastic continuous data assimilation with multiplicative noise, except for recent results on data assimilation for the 2D NSEs in a related but different setting
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.8

(Almost sure convergence in
\mathcal{H}
)
.

Assume the hypotheses of
Theorem 2.4
, and assume

(2.16)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Then

(2.17)

\sup_{t\geq N}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{- a.s. for }N\to\infty.

In particular
\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for
t\to\infty
.

Remark 2.9

(Two sufficient conditions for (
2.16
))
.

Assume the hypotheses of
Theorem 2.4
. Two natural situations in which condition (
2.16
) is satisfied are the following.

(a)

Assume that

u\in\mathrm{BUC}([0,\infty);\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,\infty;\mathcal{H}),

and that

G_{\delta}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}\qquad\text{is locally Lipschitz-continuous with }G_{\delta}(0)=0.

Since the trajectory
u([0,\infty))
is bounded in
\mathcal{H}
, the local Lipschitz property of
G_{\delta}
yields

\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad t\geq 0.

Hence (
2.16
) follows from
u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,\infty;\mathcal{H})
.

(b)

Assume that the deterministic semiflow generated by (
1.1
) possesses a compact global attractor
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{H}
, and that there exist
r>0
and
L_{r}>0
such that
G_{\delta}|_{\mathcal{A}}=0
and

\|G_{\delta}(x)-G_{\delta}(y)\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}\leq L_{r}\|x-y\|_{\mathcal{H}}

whenever
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}(x,\mathcal{A})\leq r
and
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}(y,\mathcal{A})\leq r.

If moreover

(2.18)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}(u(t),\mathcal{A})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

then (
2.16
) holds. Indeed, for all sufficiently large
t
, the trajectory
u(t)
lies in the
r
-neighborhood of
\mathcal{A}
, and therefore

\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}\lesssim\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}(u(t),\mathcal{A}).

The desired integrability in (
2.16
) then follows from (
2.18
).

3.
Applications to PDE models

In this section we illustrate the abstract theory on several PDE models.

3.1.
Weak formulations

In this subsection we show that the abstract framework covers several models in their weak formulation. We consider the 2D Navier-Stokes equations, the 2D magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations, the 2D quasi-geostrophic equations, and the 1D Allen-Cahn equation. For each model, we verify assumptions
(A1)
-
(A4)
and deduce the corresponding mean-square and almost sure convergence results for the associated data-assimilation problem from
Theorem 2.4
and
Theorem 2.8
.

3.1.1.
2D Navier-Stokes equations

Consider the variational formulation of the Navier-Stokes initial boundary value problem on a bounded domain
\mathrm{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\mathrm{D}
. For this purpose, set

\mathcal{V}:=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{D};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{H}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{V}^{\ast}:=\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D};\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\mathcal{V}^{\prime},

where
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
denotes the dual space of
\mathcal{V}
. Define the weak Stokes operator
A_{w}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
by

\langle A_{w}u,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}:=(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)_{2}.

Defining the bilinear map
B
by

\langle B(u,v),\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}:=(u\otimes v,\nabla\varphi)_{2}\,,

the variational formulation of the Navier-Stokes initial boundary value problem reads as

(weak-2D-NSE)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+A_{w}u&=B(u,u),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)&=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

To verify
\bf(A1)
, we calculate for all
u\in\mathcal{V}
in view of Poincaré’s inequality

\langle A_{w}u,u\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\geq\alpha\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}\ \text{ for some }\ \alpha>0.

Concerning
\bf(A2)
, by Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathrm{D})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{4}(\mathrm{D})
, we obtain

|\langle B(u,v),\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}|\leq C\|u\|_{4}\|v\|_{4}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{2}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|v\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}.

Hence,
B
is a bounded bilinear map from
\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}
into
\mathcal{V}^{\ast}=\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D})
, since
\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathrm{D})
. Therefore,
Subsection 2.1
ensures that
\bf(A2)
holds with
m=1
,
\rho_{1}=1
, and
\beta_{1}=\frac{3}{4}
.

Next, we verify
(A3)
-(i). Setting
F_{w}(u):=B(u,u),
we observe that for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}
, an application of the divergence theorem yields

\langle F_{w}(\varphi),\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=(\varphi\otimes\varphi,\nabla\varphi)_{2}=0.

We finally verify
\bf(A4)
. Let
u
be the unique global solution to (
weak-2D-NSE
), whose existence is ensured by
\bf(A1)
-
\bf(A3)
(see
Section 4
), and let
\xi\in\mathcal{V}
. The bilinearity of
B
gives

B(u,u)-B(u-\xi,u-\xi)=B(\xi,u)+B(u,\xi)-B(\xi,\xi).

Pairing with
\xi
and using that
u
and
\xi
are divergence-free, with zero
trace, it is possible to check that

\langle B(\xi,u),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=\langle B(\xi,\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=0.

Consequently,

\langle B(u,u)-B(u-\xi,u-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=(u(t)\otimes\xi,\nabla\xi)_{2}.

By Hölder’s inequality, Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality, and Young’s inequality,
for every
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
,

\begin{split}|(u(t)\otimes\xi,\nabla\xi)_{2}|&\leq\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}\|u(t)\|_{4}\|\xi\|_{4}\leq\varepsilon_{1}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\|u(t)\|_{4}^{2}\|\xi\|_{4}^{2}\\
&\leq\varepsilon_{1}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\|u(t)\|_{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{2}\|\xi\|_{2}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}\\
&\leq\varepsilon_{1}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{2}^{2}\|\xi\|_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

Thus, recalling that
F_{w}(u)=B(u,u),
condition (
2.6
) in
(A4)
takes the form

\langle F_{w}(u(t))-F_{w}(u(t)-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}\kappa_{u}(t)\|\xi\|_{2}^{2},

with
\kappa_{u}(t):=\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{2}^{2}
. Moreover, by the strong energy inequality, see
[
25
]
,

\frac{1}{2}\|u(\tau_{2})\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\|\nabla u(r)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u(\tau_{1})\|_{2}^{2},

for
0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}.
Applying it and Hölder’s inequality to estimate
\kappa_{u}
, we obtain

\int_{s}^{t}\kappa_{u}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\sup_{r\leq t}\|u(r)\|_{2}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla u(r)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{4},

for
0\leq s\leq t
. Hence, (
2.7
) also holds, and
(A4)
is verified.

Assuming also
(N1)
and (
2.8
), we denote by
v
the unique solution to the stochastic data assimilation problem associated with (
weak-2D-NSE
), see
Section 4
. We consider the difference
\psi=u-v
, which solves

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\psi_{t}+A_{w}\psi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t&=\bigl(F_{w}(u(t))-F_{w}(v_{t})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta}\psi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu G_{\delta}(u(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{Q},\qquad t\in(0,T),\\
\psi(0)&=\psi_{0}:=u_{0}-v_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

The following convergence
result follows from
Theorem 2.4
,
Subsection 2.2
, and

Theorem 2.8
.

Corollary 3.1

(Synchronisation for the weak two-dimensional NSEs)
.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be as in
(N1)
and (
2.8
).
There exist
\mu_{0},\eta_{0}>0
such that, for every
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
with
\mu\delta^{2}\leq\eta_{0}
, the solution

\psi=u-v
of (
3.1
) satisfies

\mathbb{E}\|\psi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|\psi_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{0}_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

for some constants
\gamma=\gamma(\mu,\delta)>0
and
C>0
independent of
t
.
If
\Gamma_{u}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}\|\psi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|\psi_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u},\qquad\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|\psi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u}.

Finally, if

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

then

\sup_{t\geq N}\|\psi_{t}\|_{2}\to 0,\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. as }N\to\infty.

3.1.2.
2D Quasi-geostrophic equations.

Consider the variational formulation of the Quasi-geostrophic initial value problem on the two-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Set

\mathcal{V}:=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{H}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{V}^{\ast}:=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}

where
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
denotes the subset of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
of functions having zero spatial average, and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
is the dual space of
\mathcal{V}
. Define the negative weak Laplacian operator
-\Delta_{w}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
by

\langle-\Delta_{w}v,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}:=(\nabla v,\nabla\varphi)_{2}\,.

We denote by

R^{\perp}\theta:=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\theta=(-R_{2}\theta,R_{1}\theta),

where
R_{j}:=\partial_{j}(-\Delta)^{-1/2}
,
j=1,2
, are the periodic Riesz transforms on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Since we work on mean-zero functions, this definition is well posed. Moreover,
\mathrm{div}\,(R^{\perp}\theta)=0
, and by the Mikhlin theorem
R^{\perp}
extends to a bounded linear operator on
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
for every
q\in(1,\infty)
. Setting

\langle F_{w}(\theta),\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}:=(\theta\,R^{\perp}\theta,\nabla\varphi)_{2},

the variational formulation of the Quasi-geostrophic initial value problem reads as

(weak-2D-QG)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \theta^{\prime}-\Delta_{w}\theta&=F_{w}(\theta),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
\theta(0)&=\theta_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

To verify
\bf(A1)
, note that, since we are working with functions having zero spatial average, Poincaré’s inequality yields, for all
\theta\in\mathcal{V}
,

\langle-\Delta_{w}\theta,\theta\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}=\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}\geq\alpha\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}=\alpha\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}.

Concerning
\bf(A2)
, define the bilinear map

B(\theta,\eta)\in\mathcal{V}^{*},\qquad\langle B(\theta,\eta),\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}:=(\theta\,R^{\perp}\eta,\nabla\varphi)_{2},\qquad\theta,\eta,\varphi\in\mathcal{V}.

Since
R^{\perp}
is bounded on
\mathrm{L}^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
by the Mikhlin theorem, Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
imply that

|\langle B(\theta,\eta),\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}|\leq C\|\theta\|_{4}\|R^{\perp}\eta\|_{4}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{2}\leq C\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\eta\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}.

Hence
B:\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}\to\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
is bilinear and bounded, where
\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. Since
F_{w}(\theta)=B(\theta,\theta)
,
Subsection 2.1
implies that
\bf(A2)
holds with
m=1
,
\rho_{1}=1
, and
\beta_{1}=\frac{3}{4}
.

We next verify
(A3)
-(i). Indeed, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}
, by integration by parts on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
,

\langle F_{w}(\varphi),\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}=(\varphi\,R^{\perp}\varphi,\nabla\varphi)_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(R^{\perp}\varphi,\nabla|\varphi|^{2})_{2}=0,

since
\operatorname{div}(R^{\perp}\varphi)=0
.

We finally verify the validity of
\bf(A4)
in the form of (
2.6
)-(
2.7
). Let
\theta
be the unique global solution to (
weak-2D-QG
), ensured by
Section 4
, and let
\xi\in\mathcal{V}
. Using the definition of
F_{w}
, we compute

\displaystyle\langle F_{w}(\theta(t))-F_{w}(\theta(t)-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle=(\theta(t)\,R^{\perp}\xi,\nabla\xi)_{2}+(\xi\,R^{\perp}\theta(t),\nabla\xi)_{2}-(\xi\,R^{\perp}\xi,\nabla\xi)_{2}.

The last two terms vanish by integration by parts on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and the identity
\operatorname{div}(R^{\perp}f)=0
. Therefore

\langle F_{w}(\theta(t))-F_{w}(\theta(t)-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=(\theta(t)\,R^{\perp}\xi,\nabla\xi)_{2}.

Hence, by Hölder’s inequality, the boundedness of
R^{\perp}
on
\mathrm{L}^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, and Young’s inequality, for every
\varepsilon>0
we obtain

\displaystyle|(\theta(t)\,R^{\perp}\xi,\nabla\xi)_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta(t)\|_{4}\|R^{\perp}\xi\|_{4}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}\leq C\|\theta(t)\|_{4}\|\xi\|_{4}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta(t)\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\xi\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}^{3/2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}^{2}+c\|\theta(t)\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{2}^{2}\|\xi\|_{2}^{2}.

Therefore (
2.6
) holds with
\kappa_{\theta}(t):=c\|\theta(t)\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{2}^{2}.
Moreover, by the energy estimate on
\theta
,

\int_{s}^{t}\kappa_{\theta}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq c\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\|\theta(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla\theta(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq c\|\theta_{0}\|_{2}^{4},

so (
2.7
) is satisfied with
M_{0}=0
and
M_{1}=c\|\theta_{0}\|_{2}^{4}
.

Assuming also
(N1)
and (
2.8
), we denote by
\eta
the unique solution to the data assimilation problem associated with (
weak-2D-QG
). Let
\Theta:=\theta-\eta
be the assimilation error, that satisfies

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\Theta_{t}-\Delta_{w}\Theta_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t&=\bigl(F_{w}(\theta_{t})-F_{w}(\eta_{t})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta}\Theta_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu G_{\delta}(\theta(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{Q},\qquad t\in(0,T),\\
\Theta(0)&=\Theta_{0}:=\theta_{0}-\eta_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Therefore, by
Theorem 2.4
,
Subsection 2.2
, and

Theorem 2.8
the following convergence result holds.

Corollary 3.2

(Synchronisation for the weak two-dimensional QG equations)
.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be as in
(N1)
and (
2.8
).
There exist
\mu_{0},\eta_{0}>0
such that, for every
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
with
\mu\delta^{2}\leq\eta_{0}
, the solution

\Theta=\theta-\eta
of (
3.2
) satisfies

\mathbb{E}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|\Theta_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(\theta(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

for some constants
\gamma=\gamma(\mu,\delta)>0
and
C>0
independent of
t
.
If
\Gamma_{\theta}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|G_{\delta}(\theta(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|\Theta_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{\theta},\qquad\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{\theta}.

Finally, if

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G_{\delta}(\theta(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

then

\sup_{t\geq N}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{2}\to 0,\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. as }N\to\infty.

3.1.3.
2D MHD equations.

We consider the initial boundary value problem for the 2D Magnetohydrodynamics equations
(MHD equations) in a bounded simply connected domain
\mathrm{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with sufficiently
smooth boundary
\partial\mathrm{D}
. We set

\mathcal{V}:=(\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{D};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D};\mathbb{R}^{2}))\times V_{2}\,,\qquad\mathcal{H}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D};\mathbb{R}^{2})\times\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{D};\mathbb{R}^{2})\,,\qquad\mathcal{V}^{\ast}:=\mathcal{V}^{\prime},

where, setting

\mathscr{C}(\mathrm{D}):=\{h\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathrm{D}};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\mathrm{div}\,h=0,\ h\cdot\nu|_{\partial\mathrm{D}}=0\},

we define
V_{2}
as the completion of
\mathscr{C}(\mathrm{D})
with respect to the

\mathrm{H}^{1}
-norm. In particular, since
\mathrm{D}
is simply connected, the following
Poincaré-type estimate holds

\|h\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{D})}\leq C\|{\rm curl}\,h\|_{2},\qquad h\in V_{2}.

In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we write

{\rm curl}\,a:=\frac{\partial a_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}-\frac{\partial a_{1}}{\partial x_{2}},\qquad a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathrm{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Let
\Phi=(u,h),

\Psi=(\overline{u},\overline{h})\in\mathcal{V}
. We define the operator

A_{w}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
as

\langle A_{w}\Phi,\Psi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}:=(\nabla u,\nabla\overline{u})_{2}+({\rm curl}\,h,{\rm curl}\,\overline{h})_{2}.

Setting

\langle F_{w}(\Phi),\Psi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}:=(u\otimes u,\nabla\overline{u})_{2}-(h\otimes h,\nabla\overline{u})_{2}+(h\otimes u,\nabla\overline{h})_{2}-(u\otimes h,\nabla\overline{h})_{2},

the variational formulation of the MHD initial boundary value problem reads as

(weak-2D-MHD)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Phi^{\prime}+A_{w}\Phi&=F_{w}(\Phi),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
\Phi(0)&=\Phi_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

To verify
\bf(A1)
, we notice that, by virtue of Poincaré’s inequality and
the above estimate on
V_{2}
, we have

\langle A_{w}\Phi,\Phi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\rm curl}\,h\|_{2}^{2}\geq\alpha\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2},

for some
\alpha>0
. Concerning
\bf(A2)
, define the bilinear map

B:\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
by

\langle B(\Phi,\Psi),\Pi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}:=(u\otimes\overline{u},\nabla\widetilde{u})_{2}-(h\otimes\overline{h},\nabla\widetilde{u})_{2}+(h\otimes\overline{u},\nabla\widetilde{h})_{2}-(u\otimes\overline{h},\nabla\widetilde{h})_{2},

for
\Phi=(u,h)
,
\Psi=(\overline{u},\overline{h})
, and

\Pi=(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{h})
in
\mathcal{V}
. By Hölder’s inequality and the
Sobolev embedding
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathrm{D})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{4}(\mathrm{D})
, we obtain

|\langle B(\Phi,\Psi),\Pi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}|\leq C\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}}\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}}\|\Pi\|_{\mathcal{V}}.

Hence
B
extends to a bounded bilinear map from

\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}
into
\mathcal{V}^{*}
. Since

F_{w}(\Phi)=B(\Phi,\Phi)
,
Subsection 2.1
implies that

\bf(A2)
holds with
m=1
,
\rho_{1}=1
, and
\beta_{1}=\frac{3}{4}
. Moreover,
recalling that given
\Phi=(u,h)\in\mathcal{V}
,
u
has zero trace on
\partial\mathrm{D}

and both
u
and
h
are divergence-free, we deduce that

\langle F_{w}(\Phi),\Phi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=0,

and therefore
\bf(A3)
is fulfilled.

We finally show that
\bf(A4)
is fulfilled. Let
\Phi
be the unique solution to (
weak-2D-MHD
) ensured by
Section 4
. For all

\Psi=(\overline{u},\overline{h})\in\mathcal{V}
, in view of the boundary conditions and
since
\Phi
and
\Psi
are divergence-free, we find that

\langle F_{w}(\Phi)-F_{w}(\Phi-\Psi),\Psi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=(u\otimes\overline{u},\nabla\overline{u})_{2}-(h\otimes\overline{h},\nabla\overline{u})_{2}+(h\otimes\overline{u},\nabla\overline{h})_{2}-(u\otimes\overline{h},\nabla\overline{h})_{2}.

Employing Hölder’s inequality, Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality and Young’s
inequality, we find that, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle|(u\otimes\overline{u},\nabla\overline{u})_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{2}\|u\|_{4}\|\overline{u}\|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\|\overline{u}\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle|(h\otimes\overline{h},\nabla\overline{u})_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{2}\|\overline{h}\|_{4}\|h\|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon(\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\overline{h}\|_{2}^{2})+C_{\varepsilon}\|h\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}\|\overline{h}\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle|(h\otimes\overline{u},\nabla\overline{h})_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\overline{h}\|_{2}\|\overline{u}\|_{4}\|h\|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon(\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\overline{h}\|_{2}^{2})+C_{\varepsilon}\|h\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}\|\overline{u}\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle|(u\otimes\overline{h},\nabla\overline{h})_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\overline{h}\|_{2}\|u\|_{4}\|\overline{h}\|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla\overline{h}\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\|\overline{h}\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence, defining

\kappa_{\Phi}(t):=C_{\varepsilon}(\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|h(t)\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla h(t)\|_{2}^{2}),

and relabelling
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain

|\langle F_{w}(\Phi(t))-F_{w}(\Phi(t)-\Psi),\Psi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}|\leq\varepsilon\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\kappa_{\Phi}(t)\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Moreover, the energy inequality for weak two-dimensional MHD solutions, see

[
28
]
, gives

\sup_{\tau\geq 0}\|\Phi(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\|\Phi(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\|\Phi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\qquad 0\leq s\leq t.

Consequently,

\int_{s}^{t}\kappa_{\Phi}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\|\Phi(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\|\Phi(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\|\Phi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{4}.

Therefore
\bf(A4)
holds with
M_{0}=0
and
M_{1}=C\|\Phi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{4}
.

Assuming that also
(N1)
and (
2.8
) hold, we denote by
\chi
the unique solution to the stochastic data assimilation problem associated with (
weak-2D-MHD
), ensured by the validity of
Section 4
. Let
\Xi:=\Phi-\chi
be the assimilation error, that satisfies

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\Xi_{t}+A_{w}\Xi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t&=\bigl(F_{w}(\Phi(t))-F_{w}(\chi_{t})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta}\Xi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu G_{\delta}(\Phi(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{Q},\qquad t\in(0,T),\\
\Xi(0)&=\Xi_{0}:=\Phi_{0}-\chi_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Applying
Theorem 2.4
,
Subsection 2.2
, and
Theorem 2.8
, we deduce the following result.

Corollary 3.3

(Synchronisation for the weak two-dimensional MHD equations)
.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be as in
(N1)
and (
2.8
).
There exist
\mu_{0},\eta_{0}>0
such that, for every
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
with
\mu\delta^{2}\leq\eta_{0}
, the unique solution
\Xi
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\|\Xi_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|\Xi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(\Phi(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

for some constants
\gamma=\gamma(\mu,\delta)>0
and
C>0
independent of
t
.
If
\Gamma_{\Phi}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|G_{\delta}(\Phi(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}\|\Xi_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|\Xi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{\Phi},\qquad\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|\Xi_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{\Phi}.

Finally, if

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G_{\delta}(\Phi(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

then

\sup_{t\geq N}\|\Xi_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0,\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. as }N\to\infty.

3.1.4.
1D Allen-Cahn Equation.

We consider the variational formulation of the 1D Allen-Cahn initial boundary value problem on
I=(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}
.
We set

\mathcal{V}:=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(I),\qquad\mathcal{H}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}(I),\qquad\mathcal{V}^{\ast}:=\mathcal{V}^{\prime},

where
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
is the dual space of
\mathcal{V}
. Let
A_{w}:=-\partial_{xx}^{w}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
be the negative weak Laplacian, i.e.

\langle-\partial_{xx}^{w}u,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}=(\partial_{x}u,\partial_{x}\varphi)_{2}\ \text{ for all }\ u,\varphi\in\mathcal{V}

and set

\langle F_{w}(u),\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}:=(u,\varphi)_{2}-(u^{3},\varphi)_{2}\ \text{ for all }\ u,\varphi\in\mathcal{V}\,.

The variational 1D Allen-Cahn initial boundary value problem reads as

(weak-1D Allen-Cahn)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+A_{w}u&=F_{w}(u),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)&=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

To verify
\bf(A1)
, note that by Poincaré’s inequality we have

\langle-\partial_{xx}^{w}u,u\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}=\|\partial_{x}u\|^{2}_{2}\geq\alpha\|u\|^{2}_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}\ \text{ for all }\ u\in\mathcal{V}\,,

for a constant
\alpha>0
. Regarding
(A2)
, fix
r\in(1,2)
. Since

\mathrm{L}^{r}(I)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{-1}(I)
, Hölder’s inequality gives

\|F_{w}(u)-F_{w}(v)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C(\|u-v\|_{2}+\|(u^{2}+v^{2})(u-v)\|_{r})\leq C(\|u-v\|_{2}+(\|u\|_{3r}^{2}+\|v\|_{3r}^{2})\|u-v\|_{3r}).

Moreover, for
r\in(1,2),

\mathcal{V}_{\frac{2}{3}}=[\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}]_{\frac{2}{3}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{3}}(I)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{3r}(I),
and
\mathcal{V}_{\frac{2}{3}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}
. Hence

\|F_{w}(u)-F_{w}(v)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C(1+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\frac{2}{3}}}^{2}+\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\frac{2}{3}}}^{2})\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\frac{2}{3}}}.

Thus
(A2)
holds with
m=1
,
\rho_{1}=2
, and
\beta_{1}=\frac{2}{3}
.

We now focus on the validity of
\bf(A3)
. Let
u,\varphi\in\mathcal{V}
. Expanding the cubic term,

\begin{split}\langle F_{w}(u+\varphi),u\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}&=(u+\varphi,u)_{2}-((u+\varphi)^{3},u)_{2}\\
&=\|u\|_{2}^{2}+(\varphi,u)_{2}-\|u\|_{4}^{4}-3\int_{0}^{1}u^{3}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x-3\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}\varphi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}u\varphi^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\|u\|_{2}^{2}+(\varphi,u)_{2}-\|u\|_{4}^{4}-3\int_{0}^{1}u^{3}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}u\varphi^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

By virtue of Young’s inequality, for
\eta\in(0,1)
,

3|u|^{3}|\varphi|\leq\eta|u|^{4}+C_{\eta}|\varphi|^{4},\quad|u||\varphi|^{3}\leq\eta|u|^{4}+C_{\eta}|\varphi|^{4}.

Choosing
\eta=\frac{1}{4}
, we obtain

\langle F_{w}(u+\varphi),u\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq C\|u\|_{2}^{2}+C\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}+C\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{4}}^{4}\,,

which implies

\langle F_{w}(u+\varphi),u\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq C\,\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+b(\varphi),

with

b(\varphi):=C(\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\varphi\|_{4}^{4})\leq C(\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{2}^{2}).

For every
z\in C([0,T];\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V})
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}b(z(t))\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\Bigl(T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)<\infty.

Therefore
(A3)
-(ii) holds with
\Psi(\varphi):=b(\varphi).

We now show the validity of
\bf(A4)
. We denote by
u
the unique global solution to (
weak-1D Allen-Cahn
) ensured by the validity of
\bf(A1)
-
\bf(A3)
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{V}
. We have

F_{w}(u)-F_{w}(u-\xi)=\xi-(u^{3}-(u-\xi)^{3})=\xi-3u^{2}\xi+3u\xi^{2}-\xi^{3}.

Pairing with
\xi
,

\langle F_{w}(u)-F_{w}(u-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=\int_{0}^{1}(1-3u^{2})\xi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+3\int_{0}^{1}u\xi^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{4}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using
(1-3u^{2})\xi^{2}\leq(1+3u^{2})\xi^{2}
and the pointwise Young inequality

|u||\xi|^{3}=(|u||\xi|)|\xi|^{2}\leq\varepsilon|\xi|^{4}+C_{\varepsilon}u^{2}\xi^{2},

we obtain for small
\varepsilon

\langle F_{w}(u)-F_{w}(u-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq C(1+\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}^{2})\,\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-(1-3\varepsilon)\|\xi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{4}}^{4}\leq C(1+\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}^{2})\,\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Now observe that in dimension 1,
\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
, thus

\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\leq C\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}\,.

Therefore
(A4)
holds with

\kappa_{u}(t):=C(1+\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}).

Indeed, the energy estimate gives

\int_{s}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C(1+(t-s)+\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}),

and hence

\int_{s}^{t}\kappa_{u}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq M_{0}(t-s)+M_{1}

for suitable constants
M_{0},M_{1}\geq 0
.

Assuming also
(N1)
and (
2.8
), we denote by
v

the unique solution to the stochastic data assimilation problem associated with
(
weak-1D Allen-Cahn
), and set
\psi:=u-v
. Then the following convergence
result follows from
Theorem 2.4
,
Subsection 2.2
, and

Theorem 2.8
.

Corollary 3.4

(Synchronisation for the weak one-dimensional Allen-Cahn equation)
.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be as in
(N1)
and (
2.8
).
There exist
\mu_{0},\eta_{0}>0
such that, for every
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
with
\mu\delta^{2}\leq\eta_{0}
, the error
\psi
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\|\psi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|\psi_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

for some constants
\gamma=\gamma(\mu,\delta)>0
and
C>0
independent of
t
.
If
\Gamma_{u}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}\|\psi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|\psi_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u},\qquad\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|\psi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u}.

Finally, if

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

then

\sup_{t\geq N}\|\psi_{t}\|_{2}\to 0,\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. as }N\to\infty.

3.2.
Strong formulations

We now revisit some of the previous models in a stronger functional setting. By selecting a more regular Gelfand triple, the same abstract theory yields convergence results in stronger norms than in the weak framework considered above.

3.2.1.
2D Navier-Stokes equations-revisited

We consider the Navier-Stokes equations on the two-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. We work with the Gelfand triple

\mathcal{V}:=\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{H}:=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{V}^{\ast}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),

where
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):=\{u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\mathrm{div}\,u=0,\ \int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0\}.
Denote by
\mathcal{P}
the Leray projection onto
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, and consider the Stokes operator
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
given by

A:=-\mathcal{P}\Delta,\qquad D(A)=\mathcal{V}.

We endow
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
with the equivalent norms

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}:=\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{2},\qquad\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}:=\|Au\|_{2},

and we recall that the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}
is given by

\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}:=(f,A\varphi)_{2},\qquad f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{V}.

Define the bilinear map

B(u,v):=-\mathcal{P}((u\cdot\nabla)v),\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}},

and set

F(u):=B(u,u)=-\mathcal{P}((u\cdot\nabla)u).

The strong variational formulation of the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations reads as

(strong-2D-NSE)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+Au&=F(u),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)&=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

We first verify
(A1)
. By the Poincaré inequality on mean-zero
periodic vector fields,

\langle Au,u\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=(Au,Au)_{2}=\|Au\|_{2}^{2}\geq\alpha\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}}^{2}

for some
\alpha>0
and every
u\in\mathcal{V}
. Concerning
(A2)
, since

\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}=D(A^{\frac{3}{4}})=\mathrm{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\sigma,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),

and, in dimension two,
\mathrm{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, we have

\|B(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}=\|\mathcal{P}((u\cdot\nabla)v)\|_{2}\leq\|u\|_{\infty}\|\nabla v\|_{2}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|v\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Thus
B:\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}\times\mathcal{V}_{\frac{3}{4}}\to\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
is bounded and bilinear.
Since
F(u)=B(u,u)
,
Subsection 2.1
implies that

(A2)
holds with
m=1
,
\rho_{1}=1
, and
\beta_{1}=\frac{3}{4}
.

We next verify
(A3)
-(i). For every
u\in\mathcal{V}
, using periodic
boundary conditions, the divergence-free constraint, and the classical
two-dimensional cancellation identity,

((u\cdot\nabla)u,Au)_{2}=0,

see, for instance,
[
11
]
, we obtain

\langle F(u),u\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=-((u\cdot\nabla)u,Au)_{2}=0.

Hence
(A3)
-(i) is satisfied.

We finally verify
(A4)
. Let
u
be the unique global solution to
(
strong-2D-NSE
), whose existence follows from
(A1)
-
(A3)
, see
Section 4
. For
\xi\in\mathcal{V}
, by bilinearity of
B
we have

F(u)-F(u-\xi)=B(\xi,u)+B(u,\xi)-B(\xi,\xi).

Pairing with
\xi
and using again the two-dimensional cancellation

\langle B(\xi,\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=-((\xi\cdot\nabla)\xi,A\xi)_{2}=0,

we obtain

\displaystyle\langle F(u(t))-F(u(t)-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle=\langle B(\xi,u(t)),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}+\langle B(u(t),\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle=-(\xi\cdot\nabla u(t),A\xi)_{2}-(u(t)\cdot\nabla\xi,A\xi)_{2}.

Therefore, by Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities,

|(u(t)\cdot\nabla\xi,A\xi)_{2}|\leq\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}\|A\xi\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\|A\xi\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}^{2},

while, using
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{6}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,

|(\xi\cdot\nabla u(t),A\xi)_{2}|\leq\|\xi\|_{6}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{3}\|A\xi\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\|A\xi\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{3}^{2}\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}^{2}.

Since
\|A\xi\|_{2}
is equivalent to
\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{V}}
and

\|\nabla\xi\|_{2}
is equivalent to
\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, we infer that

\langle F(u(t))-F(u(t)-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\kappa_{u}(t)\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

where
\kappa_{u}(t):=\|u(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{3}^{2}.

Moreover, by the embeddings
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),

\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,3}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),

we have
\kappa_{u}(t)\leq C\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}}^{2}.
The strong energy inequality gives

\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\|u(r)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

for
0\leq s\leq t.
Consequently,

\int_{s}^{t}\kappa_{u}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\leq C\int_{s}^{t}\|u(r)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

and therefore (
2.7
) also holds. Hence
(A4)
is verified.

Assume
(N1)
, (
2.8
), and denote by
v
the unique solution to the stochastic data assimilation problem
associated with (
strong-2D-NSE
), whose existence is ensured by
Section 4
. Set
w:=u-v.
Then
w
solves

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}w_{t}+Aw_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t&=(F(u(t))-F(v_{t}))\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta}w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu G_{\delta}(u(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{Q},\qquad t>0,\\
w(0)&=w_{0}:=u_{0}-v_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

The following synchronisation
result follows from
Theorem 2.4
,
Subsection 2.2
, and

Theorem 2.8
.

Corollary 3.5

(Synchronisation for the strong two-dimensional NSEs)
.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be as in
(N1)
and (
2.8
).
There exist
\mu_{0},\eta_{0}>0
such that, for every
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
with
\mu\delta^{2}\leq\eta_{0}
, the solution

w=u-v
of (
3.4
) satisfies

\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|w_{0}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{0}_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

for some positive
\gamma=\gamma(\mu,\delta)>0
and
C>0
independent of
t
. If
\Gamma_{u}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|w_{0}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u},\qquad\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u}.

Finally, if
\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

then

\sup_{t\geq N}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}\to 0,\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. as }N\to\infty.

3.2.2.
1D Allen-Cahn equation-revisited

We consider the initial boundary value problem for the Allen-Cahn equation on the real interval
I=(0,1)
. We work with the Gelfand triple

\mathcal{V}:=\mathrm{H}^{2}(I)\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(I),\qquad\mathcal{H}:=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(I),\qquad\mathcal{V}^{\ast}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}(I).

and let
A=-\partial_{xx}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
be the negative Dirichlet Laplacian on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(I)
. We endow
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H}
with the equivalent norms

\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}:=\|Au\|_{2}=\|\partial_{xx}u\|_{2},\qquad\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}:=\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{2}=\|\partial_{x}u\|_{2}.

The duality pairing between
\mathcal{V}^{*}
and
\mathcal{V}
is defined by

\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}:=(f,A\varphi)_{2}=(f,-\partial_{xx}\varphi)_{2},\qquad f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(I),\ \varphi\in\mathcal{V}.

The initial boundary value problem for the one-dimensional Allen-Cahn equation reads as

(1D Allen-Cahn)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}-\partial_{xx}u&=F(u),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)&=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
F(u):=u-u^{3}
. We first verify
\bf(A1)
. For every
u\in\mathcal{V}
,

\langle Au,u\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}=(Au,Au)_{2}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2},

so
\bf(A1)
holds with
\alpha=1
. Concerning
\bf(A2)
, for all
u,v\in\mathcal{V}
we have

\begin{split}\|F(u)-F(v)\|_{2}&=\|u-v-(u^{3}-v^{3})\|_{2}\leq\|u-v\|_{2}+\|(u-v)(u^{2}+uv+v^{2})\|_{2}\\
&\leq C(1+\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|v\|_{\infty}^{2})\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta}}\end{split}

Since in one dimension it holds
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}=[\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}]_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2\beta}(I)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(I)
for
\beta>\frac{1}{4},
then
\bf(A2)
is satisfied with
m=1
,
\rho=\rho_{1}=2
, and for any
\beta=\beta_{1}\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}],
because (
2.2
) is equivalent to
\beta\leq\frac{2}{3}.

We now verify
\bf(A3)
. Let
x,y\in\mathcal{V}
and set
s:=x+y.

Using the duality pairing and integrating by parts, we obtain

\begin{split}\langle F(x+y),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}&=(s-s^{3},Ax)_{2}=\int_{0}^{1}(s-s^{3})(-x^{\prime\prime})\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{1}(1-3s^{2})s^{\prime}x^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&=\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+(y,x)_{\mathcal{H}}-3\int_{0}^{1}s^{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r-3\int_{0}^{1}s^{2}y^{\prime}x^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r.\end{split}

The last two terms are treated by completing the square

-3s^{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}-3s^{2}y^{\prime}x^{\prime}=-3s^{2}(x^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}y^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{3}{4}s^{2}(y^{\prime})^{2}\leq\frac{3}{4}s^{2}|y^{\prime}|^{2}.

Therefore, recalling that
s=x+y
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle F(x+y),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}\leq\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+|(y,x)_{\mathcal{H}}|+C\int_{0}^{1}|x+y|^{2}|y^{\prime}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq C\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\|x+y\|_{\infty}^{2}\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Since in one space dimension it holds the embedding
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(I)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(I),

it follows

\|x+y\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq C\bigl(\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\bigr).

Hence

\displaystyle\langle F(x+y),x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\bigl(\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\bigr)\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq C\bigl(1+\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\bigr)\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\bigl(\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{4}\bigr).

Thus
\bf(A3)
holds with
\Psi(y):=C\bigl(\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{4}\bigr).

In particular, if
z\in\mathrm{C}([0,t];\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V}),

then
\Psi(z(\cdot))\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,t)
.

We finally verify the validity of
\bf(A4)
by proving a stronger one-sided
increment estimate. Let
x,y\in\mathcal{V}
. Since

F(x+y)-F(x)=y-3x^{2}y-3xy^{2}-y^{3},

we have, using the duality pairing and integrating by parts,

\begin{split}\langle F(x+y)-F(x),y\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}&=(F(x+y)-F(x),Ay)_{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{r}(F(x+y)-F(x))y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&=\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-3\int_{0}^{1}(x+y)^{2}|y^{\prime}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r-3\int_{0}^{1}y(2x+y)x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r.\end{split}

Since
2x+y=(x+y)+x,

we can write

-3\int_{0}^{1}y(2x+y)x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r=-3\int_{0}^{1}y(x+y)x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r-3\int_{0}^{1}xyx^{\prime}y^{\prime}\,\,\mathrm{d}r.

The first term is absorbed by the negative cubic contribution. Indeed, by
Young’s inequality,

3|y|\,|x+y|\,|x^{\prime}|\,|y^{\prime}|\leq\frac{3}{2}(x+y)^{2}|y^{\prime}|^{2}+C|x^{\prime}|^{2}|y|^{2}.

Hence

-3\int_{0}^{1}(x+y)^{2}|y^{\prime}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r-3\int_{0}^{1}y(x+y)x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|x^{\prime}|^{2}|y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

For the remaining term, again by Young’s inequality,

3|x|\,|y|\,|x^{\prime}|\,|y^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y^{\prime}|^{2}+C|x|^{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}|y|^{2}.

Consequently,

\langle F(x+y)-F(x),y\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}\leq C\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{1}|x^{\prime}|^{2}|y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r+C\int_{0}^{1}|x|^{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}|y|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}r.

Using the one-dimensional estimates

\|x\|_{\infty}\leq C\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad\|x^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}},\qquad\|y\|_{2}\leq C\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}},

we infer

\langle F(x+y)-F(x),y\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}\leq C\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq C(1+\widetilde{\Psi}(x))\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

where
\widetilde{\Psi}(x):=(1+\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}.

Therefore, taking
x=u(t)
and
y=-\xi
, we obtain

\langle F(u(t))-F(u(t)-\xi),\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}=\langle F(u(t)-\xi)-F(u(t)),-\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{*},\mathcal{V}}\leq C(1+\widetilde{\Psi}(u(t)))\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Thus (
2.6
) holds with
\kappa_{u}(t):=C(1+\widetilde{\Psi}(u(t))).

It remains to verify (
2.7
). Testing
(
1D Allen-Cahn
) against
Au
gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}=(F(u(t)),Au(t))_{2}.

Moreover,

(F(u),Au)_{2}=(u-u^{3},Au)_{2}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-3\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}|u^{\prime}|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

By the Poincaré inequality,

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\lambda_{1}^{-1}\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2},\qquad\lambda_{1}=\pi^{2},

and therefore

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+(1-\lambda_{1}^{-1})\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leq 0.

Hence
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

and

\int_{s}^{t}\|u(r)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq C\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\qquad 0\leq s\leq t.

Consequently,

\int_{s}^{t}\kappa_{u}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\leq C(t-s)+C(1+\sup_{r\geq 0}\|u(r)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})\int_{s}^{t}\|u(r)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq C(t-s)+C_{u_{0}},

for any
0\leq s\leq t
.
This proves (
2.7
).

Assume moreover that
(N1)
and (
2.8
) hold. Let
v
be the solution to the data assimilation problem related to
(
1D Allen-Cahn
). Setting
w:=u-v
, we have

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}w_{t}+Aw_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t&=(F(u(t))-F(v_{t}))\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta}w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu G_{\delta}(u(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{Q},\qquad t>0,\\
w(0)&=w_{0}:=u_{0}-v_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

From
Theorem 2.4
,
Subsection 2.2
, and

Theorem 2.8
, we obtain the following convergence result.

Corollary 3.6

(Synchronisation for the strong one-dimensional Allen-Cahn equation)
.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be as in
(N1)
and (
2.8
).
There exist
\mu_{0},\eta_{0}>0
such that, for every
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
with
\mu\delta^{2}\leq\eta_{0}
, the solution

w=u-v
of (
3.5
) satisfies

\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|w_{0}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{0}_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

for some constants
\gamma=\gamma(\mu,\delta)>0
and
C>0
independent of
t
. If
\Gamma_{u}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|w_{0}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u},\qquad\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{u}.

Finally, if
\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

then

\sup_{t\geq N}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}\to 0,\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. as }N\to\infty.

4.
Preparatory well-posedness results

In this section we prepare the proofs of the main results. We first show that, under
\bf{(A1)-(A3)}
, the deterministic reference problem (
1.1
) is globally well posed on every finite time interval. We then turn to the stochastic data-assimilation system and reduce it, via the Da Prato-Debussche decomposition, to a pathwise random PDE. This allows us to transfer the deterministic well-posedness argument to the stochastic setting.

Based on the assumptions
\bf(A1)-(A2)
, we recall the following result on the local well-posedness of (

), see
[
18
, Theorem 18.2.6 and Theorem 18.2.17]

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T>0
and assume that

, there exists

and depends continuously on the data. If the solution does not exist globally in time, i.e., if

, then the maximal existence time is characterized by the blow-up

\lim\limits_{t\to a_{\mathrm{max}}}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,t;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})}=\infty.

Adding the further non-blow up assumption

, the first consequence is that the deterministic reference problem is globally well posed on every finite time interval.

Proposition 4.2

(Global well-posedness on finite time intervals)
.

Assume that
\bf(A1)
–
\bf(A3)
hold. Then for every
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
and every
T>0
, the problem (

) admits a unique solution

In particular, (
1.1
) is globally well-posed on every finite time interval.

Proof of
Section 4
.

By
Section 4
, there exists a unique maximal solution
u

on some interval
[0,a_{\max})\subseteq[0,T]
. Taking inner products of (
1.1
) by
u
in
\mathcal{V}
and using
\bf(A1)
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\alpha\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}=\langle F(u(t)),u(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}.

Since

, we estimate the components separately. For those
j
with
\beta_{j}\geq\frac{3}{4}

with

Since

by

, it follows that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+c_{0}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leq C\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C,

with

Gronwall’s lemma gives
u\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,a_{\max};\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a_{\max};\mathcal{V})

with bounds depending only on

,

and the constants in
\bf(A1)
and
\bf(A3)
. To show that

and notice that

. So it remains to show that

By (

) in

with

, we obtain

Since

, we immediately have

Moreover, setting

Hence, using

and

Therefore,

. Consequently,

\sup_{t<a_{\max}}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,t;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})}<\infty.

The blow-up alternative (

.

We now turn to the stochastic data-assimilation problem (
DAsto
). The key point is to separate the multiplicative stochastic forcing from the nonlinear dynamics by means of the Da Prato-Debussche decomposition. More precisely, for the reference solution
u
given by

, we first introduce the stochastic convolution

.

such that

is adapted as an

\displaystyle=\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}+\mu\langle\mathrm{I}_{\delta}\xi-\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}+\mu\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}-C_{\mathrm{I}}\mu\delta\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\mu\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\left(\mu-\frac{C_{\mathrm{I}}^{2}\mu^{2}\delta^{2}}{2\alpha}\right)\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

If
\mu\delta^{2}\leq\eta_{0}=2\alpha/C_{\mathrm{I}}^{2}
, then the coefficient of
\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
is nonnegative, and therefore

(4.7)

\langle\widetilde{A}\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=\langle(A+\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta})\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2},\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathcal{V}.

In particular, the operator
A+\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta}
is coercive on
\mathcal{V}
and enjoys
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-maximal regularity.

Concerning the assumption (2) in
Appendix A
, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and for any
x,y\in\mathcal{V}
, we have to estimate

\|G_{z}(t,x)-G_{z}(t,y)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}=\|F(x+z(t))-F(y+z(t))\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}.

By (
2.1
), it holds

\begin{split}\|G_{z}(t,x)-G_{z}(t,y)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}(1+\|x+z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}+\|y+z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}})\|x-y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}(1+\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}+\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}})\|x-y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}},\end{split}

where in the second inequality we have used that
(a+b)^{\rho_{j}}\lesssim a^{\rho_{j}}+b^{\rho_{j}}.
If
\rho_{j}=0
, then the term involving
z
is just a constant and can be absorbed into the coefficient
1
in (
A.1
). In other words,
we can choose
g_{j}\equiv 1\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T)
. Therefore, assume from now on that
\rho_{j}>0
. We then choose
g_{j}(t):=\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}},

and we have to prove that
g_{j}\in\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2(\rho_{j}+1)}{\rho_{j}}}(0,T)
. Using (
2.2
), it holds

\theta_{j}:=2\beta_{j}-1\in(0,1).

Since
\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}=(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V})_{\theta_{j},2},
by interpolation inequality it follows that

\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}\lesssim\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{1-\theta_{j}}\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\theta_{j}},\quad\text{ and thus }\quad\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{2(\rho_{j}+1)}\lesssim\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2(1-\theta_{j})(\rho_{j}+1)}\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2\theta_{j}(\rho_{j}+1)}.

Now, observe that by (
2.2
), we have

(4.8)

\theta_{j}(\rho_{j}+1)=(2\beta_{j}-1)(\rho_{j}+1)\leq 1.

Since
z\in\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}),
we can apply Hölder inequality and, thanks to (
4.8
), we obtain

(4.9)

\int_{0}^{T}\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{2(\rho_{j}+1)}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\|z\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathcal{H})}^{2(1-\theta_{j})(\rho_{j}+1)}\int_{0}^{T}\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2\theta_{j}(\rho_{j}+1)}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Thus
g_{j}(t)=\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}\in\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2(\rho_{j}+1)}{\rho_{j}}}(0,T)
, and the assumption (2) in
Appendix A
is verified.

We finally check assumption (3) in
Appendix A
. Note that

G_{z}(t,0)=F(z(t))+\mu\mathrm{I}_{\delta}(u(t)-z(t)).

The second term belongs to
\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
because
u,z\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V})
and, thanks to (
2.8
),
\mathrm{I}_{\delta}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
. For the first term, (
2.1
) with
v=0
gives

\|F(z(t))\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C+C\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}+1})

By virtue of (
4.9
),

z\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2(\rho_{j}+1)}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}),

and this in turn implies that also assumption (3) in
Appendix A
is verified.

In conclusion,
Appendix A
gives a unique maximal solution

y\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a_{\max};\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,a_{\max};\mathcal{V}^{\ast})\cap\mathrm{BUC}([0,a_{\max}];\mathcal{H})

on some interval
[0,a_{\max})\subseteq[0,T]
.

Step
2
: a priori estimate for
y
and exclusion of blow-up in finite time.
Set

\eta:=y+z.

Testing (
4.5
) by
y
and using (
4.7
), we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leq\langle F(\eta(t)),y(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}+\mu\langle\mathrm{I}_{\delta}(u(t)-z(t)),y(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}.

We split the nonlinear term according to the interpolation regimes. Set

J_{<}:=\{j:\tfrac{1}{2}<\beta_{j}<\tfrac{3}{4}\},\qquad J_{\geq}:=\{j:\tfrac{3}{4}\leq\beta_{j}<1\},

and

\Psi_{<}(z):=\sum_{j\in J_{<}}\Psi_{j}(z),\qquad\varepsilon_{<}:=\sum_{j\in J_{<}}\varepsilon_{j},\qquad\varepsilon_{\geq}:=\sum_{j\in J_{\geq}}\varepsilon_{j},

where
(\varepsilon_{j},\Psi_{j})
are given in
(A3)
. Since
F=\sum_{j=1}^{m}F_{j}
, we estimate separately the contributions with indices in
J_{<}
and
J_{\geq}
.

For
j\in J_{<}
, applying (
2.4
) with
x=y(t)
and

y=z(t)
gives

\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),y(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{j}\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{j}^{(0)}(1+\Psi_{j}(z(t)))\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C_{j}^{(1)}\Psi_{j}(z(t)).

Summing over
j\in J_{<}
, we obtain

(4.10)

\sum_{j\in J_{<}}\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),y(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{<}\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C(1+\Psi_{<}(z(t)))\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\Psi_{<}(z(t)).

By the integrability assumption in
(A3)
-(ii),

\Psi_{<}(z(\cdot))\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T)
.

We now consider the components with
j\in J_{\geq}
. For each such
j

\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),y(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),\eta(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}-\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),z(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}.

By (
2.3
), applied with
x=\eta(t)
, we have

\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),\eta(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{j}\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{j}^{(1)}\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C_{j}^{(0)}.

Since
\eta(t)=y(t)+z(t)
, we also have

\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leq 2\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+2\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2},\qquad\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq 2\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+2\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Therefore,

\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),\eta(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq 2\varepsilon_{j}\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+h_{1,j}(t),

where

h_{1,j}(t):=C(1+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}).

Since
z\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathcal{H})
, we have

h_{1,j}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T)
. We now estimate the term

\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),z(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}
. By
(
2.1
), applied with
v=0
, and by absorbing
F_{j}(0)
into the
constant,

\|F_{j}(\eta(t))\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C(1+\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}+1}).

Hence

|\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),z(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}|\leq C(1+\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}+1})\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}.

Set

\theta_{j}:=2\beta_{j}-1,\qquad r_{j}:=\rho_{j}+1.

Since
\beta_{j}\geq\frac{3}{4}
, we have
\theta_{j}\in[\tfrac{1}{2},1)
, and
(
2.2
) gives
\theta_{j}r_{j}\leq 1
. By interpolation,

\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{r_{j}}\lesssim\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{(1-\theta_{j})r_{j}}\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\theta_{j}r_{j}}.

Using
\theta_{j}r_{j}\leq 1
and Young’s inequality, for every

\varepsilon>0
,

\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{r_{j}}\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}(1+\|\eta(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})(1+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}).

Since
\eta(t)=y(t)+z(t)
, this gives, after reducing
\varepsilon
if needed,

|\langle F_{j}(\eta(t)),z(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}|\leq\varepsilon\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}(1+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2})\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+h_{2,j,\varepsilon}(t),

where

h_{2,j,\varepsilon}(t):=C_{\varepsilon}(1+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2})\,(1+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T).

Since the number of indices is
finite, summing over
j\in J_{\geq}
and reducing
\varepsilon
, if necessary,
gives

\sum_{j\in J_{\geq}}|\langle F_{j}(\eta),z\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}|\leq\varepsilon\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}(1+\|z\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2})\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+h_{2,\varepsilon}(t),

with
h_{2,\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T)
.

Combining the estimates for the low and high components, we obtain

\langle F(\eta(t)),y(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq(\varepsilon_{<}+2\varepsilon_{\geq}+\varepsilon)\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+a_{z}(t)\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+h_{z,\varepsilon}(t),

where
h_{z,\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T)
and

a_{z}(t)=C(1+\Psi_{<}(z(t))+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}).

Let
c_{\rm emb}>0
denote the constant involved in the embedding
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
.
Then, by (
2.8
),

\langle\mathrm{I}_{\delta}f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq(C_{\mathrm{I}}\delta+c_{\rm emb})\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{V}},\qquad f\in\mathcal{H},\ g\in\mathcal{V}.

Hence, for every
\varepsilon_{2}>0
,

\mu\langle\mathrm{I}_{\delta}(u-z),y\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{2}\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon_{2},\mu,\delta}\|u-z\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

We now choose
\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{2}>0
such that

\varepsilon_{<}+2\varepsilon_{\geq}+\varepsilon+\varepsilon_{2}<\frac{\alpha}{2}.
This is possible because

\varepsilon_{<}+\varepsilon_{\geq}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}<\frac{\alpha}{4},
and hence
\varepsilon_{<}+2\varepsilon_{\geq}<\frac{\alpha}{2}
. Combining the above estimates,
we arrive at

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+c_{0}\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leq a_{z}(t)\|y(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+h_{z}(t),

where
c_{0}>0
,
h_{z}(t)=h_{z,\varepsilon}(t)+C_{\varepsilon_{2},\mu,\delta}\|u(t)-z(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T)
, and

a_{z}(t)=C\bigl(1+\Psi_{<}(z(t))+\|z(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\bigr)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T).

Hence, by Gronwall’s lemma,

y\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,t;\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V}),\qquad\forall\,t<a_{\max}.

Now
\eta=y+z
belongs to
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,t;\mathcal{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V})
for every
t<a_{\max}
. Hence, by
\bf(A2)
and the standard interpolation argument, we infer that

F(\eta)=F(y+z)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V}^{\ast}),\qquad\forall\,t<a_{\max}.

Consequently,

y^{\prime}=-(A+\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta})y+F(y+z)+\mu\,\mathrm{I}_{\delta}(u-z)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V}^{\ast}),

and hence

\sup_{t<a_{\max}}\|y\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,t;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})}<\infty.

The blow-up alternative (
A.3
) therefore cannot occur at finite time, and thus
a_{\max}=T
.

Step
3
: reconstruction of the stochastic solution and its measurability.
Finally, by a standard Picard-iteration argument in the class of adapted
\mathcal{H}
-valued and progressively measurable
\mathcal{V}
-valued processes, the pathwise solution
\hat{v}
constructed above admits a version with these measurability properties. Hence the same holds for
v=\hat{v}+Z
.
∎

5.
Proofs of the main results

We now turn to the main objective of the paper: the synchronisation of the assimilated state with the reference trajectory. The proof of the first main theorem is
based on an Itô energy estimate for the error
w=u-v
solving (
2.10
). Indeed, assumption

(A4)
controls the nonlinear increment along the reference solution, while the nudging term provides the stabilizing contribution responsible for the decay. This yields exponential convergence in expectation up to the stochastic forcing term.

Proof of
Theorem 2.4
.

Applying Itô’s formula to
\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
and using (
2.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\langle Aw_{t},w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=2\langle F(u(t))-F(v_{t}),w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\,\mathrm{d}t-2\mu\langle\mathrm{I}_{\delta}w_{t},w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu^{2}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t-2\mu(w_{t},G_{\delta}(u(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{Q})_{\mathcal{H}}.

Taking expectations and using that the martingale term has zero expectation gives

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\langle Aw_{t},w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\langle F(u(t))-F(v_{t}),w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}-2\mu\mathbb{E}\langle\mathrm{I}_{\delta}w_{t},w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu^{2}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}.

Since
v_{t}=u(t)-w_{t}
, assumption
\bf(A4)
yields

\langle F(u(t))-F(v_{t}),w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=\langle F(u(t))-F(u(t)-w_{t}),w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq\varepsilon_{1}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\kappa_{u}(t)\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Moreover, by
\bf(A1)
,

\langle Aw_{t},w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\geq\alpha\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}.

For the nudging term, using (
2.8
) with
f=w_{t}
and
g=w_{t}
, we obtain

-\langle\mathrm{I}_{\delta}w_{t},w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}=-\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\langle w_{t}-\mathrm{I}_{\delta}w_{t},w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq-\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C_{\mathrm{I}}\delta\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}}.

Hence, by Young’s inequality,

-2\mu\langle\mathrm{I}_{\delta}w_{t},w_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast},\mathcal{V}}\leq-2\mu\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon_{2}}\mu^{2}\delta^{2}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Combining the above estimates and choosing
\varepsilon_{2}>0
so small that

c_{0}:=2\alpha-2\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}>0,

we arrive at

(5.1)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+c_{0}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leq\Bigl(-2\mu+C\mu^{2}\delta^{2}+2\kappa_{u}(t)\Bigr)\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\mu^{2}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}.

Dropping the non-negative
\mathcal{V}
-term and setting

y(t):=\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\qquad\lambda(t):=-2\mu+C\mu^{2}\delta^{2}+2\kappa_{u}(t),

we obtain

y^{\prime}(t)\leq\lambda(t)y(t)+\mu^{2}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}.

By variation of constants,

y(t)\leq e^{\int_{0}^{t}\lambda(r)\,\mathrm{d}r}y(0)+\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{s}^{t}\lambda(r)\,\mathrm{d}r}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Using (
2.7
), we infer

\int_{s}^{t}\lambda(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\leq-\Bigl(2\mu-C\mu^{2}\delta^{2}-2M_{0}\Bigr)(t-s)+2M_{1}.

Choose
\mu_{0},\eta_{0}>0
so that

\gamma:=2\mu-C\mu^{2}\delta^{2}-2M_{0}>0

whenever
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and
\mu\delta^{2}\leq\eta_{0}
. Then

e^{\int_{s}^{t}\lambda(r)\,\mathrm{d}r}\leq Ce^{-\gamma(t-s)},

with
C=e^{2M_{1}}
. Therefore

y(t)\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}y(0)+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

which is exactly (
2.11
).
∎

Theorem 2.4
is the core synchronisation estimate of the paper. The next two corollaries show how this estimate can be refined in two relevant directions: first, by quantifying the asymptotic noise floor under a uniform bound on the noise coefficient, and second, by deriving the corresponding
estimate in the weaker
\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
-topology.

Proof of
Subsection 2.2
.

By (
2.12
), we have

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\Gamma_{u}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\gamma^{-1}\Gamma_{u}.

Inserting this into (
2.11
) yields (
2.13
), and (
2.14
) follows by letting
t\to\infty
. ∎

Proof of
Subsection 2.2
.

Since the embedding
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
is continuous, there exists
C>0
such that

\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathcal{H}.

Hence

\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}^{2}\leq C\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

and the claim follows from
Theorem 2.4
.
∎

We conclude with two asymptotic refinements of the synchronisation result. The
first one shows that, in the presence of a compact global attractor, the
asymptotic size of the residual error is determined only by the values of the
noise coefficient near the attractor. The second one strengthens the
mean-square convergence to almost sure convergence under an additional
integrability assumption on the stochastic forcing.

Proof of
Subsection 2.2
.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is compact and
G_{\delta}
is continuous on an open neighborhood

\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{A}
, the map
a\mapsto\|G_{\delta}(a)\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}
is continuous on
\mathcal{A}
. Hence

\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}:=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\|G_{\delta}(a)\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}<\infty.

Moreover, since
\mathcal{A}
is a compact global attractor and
u(t)
is a
trajectory of the deterministic semiflow generated by (
1.1
), we have

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}(u(t),\mathcal{A})\to 0
as
t\to\infty.
Therefore, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
T_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

u(t)\in\mathcal{U}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\leq\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon,\qquad\forall\,t\geq T_{\varepsilon}.

Now, by (
2.11
), for every
t\geq T_{\varepsilon}
,

\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|w_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Splitting the integral at
T_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-\gamma t}\|w_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\mu^{2}\int_{T_{\varepsilon}}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
T_{\varepsilon}<\infty
and
G_{\delta}(u(\cdot))\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T_{\varepsilon};\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0})
,
the first two terms on the right-hand side converge to
0
as
t\to\infty
.
For the last term,

\int_{T_{\varepsilon}}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\gamma^{-1},

hence

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon).

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{C\mu^{2}}{\gamma}\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}.

∎

Proof of
Theorem 2.8
.

The idea of the proof is the following. In the first part, we show that the discrete-time second moments and the expected suprema on unit intervals are summable; in the second part, we use Markov’s inequality and Borel-Cantelli, to obtain the almost sure convergence.

Step 1.
Set

X(t):=\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\qquad h(t):=\mu^{2}\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}.

By assumption (
2.16
) we have

(5.2)

h\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,\infty).

From
Theorem 2.4
, with
y(t):=\mathbb{E}X(t)=\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

we know that

(5.3)

y(t)\leq Ke^{-\gamma t}y(0)+K\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}h(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0.

Evaluating at integer times and summing over
n\geq 0
, we get

(5.4)

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y(n)&\leq Ky(0)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma n}+K\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{n}e^{-\gamma(n-s)}h(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=Ky(0)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma n}+K\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n\geq\lceil s\rceil}e^{-\gamma(n-s)}\right)h(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

The geometric sum in parentheses is bounded uniformly in
s
(since it is a geometric series with ratio
e^{-\gamma}<1
). Hence by (
5.2
),

(5.5)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\|w_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}<\infty.

Step 2.
Next, recall from the proof of
Theorem 2.4
that

(5.6)

\,\mathrm{d}X(t)+c_{0}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq a(t)X(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+Ch(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\,\mathrm{d}M_{t},

where

a(t):=2\kappa_{u}(t)+C\mu^{2}\delta^{2}-2\mu,\qquad M_{t}:=-\mu\int_{0}^{t}\langle w(s),G_{\delta}(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{Q}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For
t\in[n,n+1]
, integrating (
5.6
) from
n
to
t
and dropping the non-negative second term on the left-hand side, we obtain

X(t)\leq X(n)+C\int_{n}^{t}a(s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+C\int_{n}^{t}h(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+M_{t}-M_{n}.

For
t\in[n,n+1]
, set

A_{n}(t):=X(n)+C\int_{n}^{t}h(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\sup_{s\in[n,t]}|M_{s}-M_{n}|.

Then
t\mapsto A_{n}(t)
is non-decreasing, and since
a(s)\leq a_{+}(s):=\max\{0,a(s)\}

X(t)\leq A_{n}(t)+C\int_{n}^{t}a_{+}(s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\in[n,n+1].

Applying Gronwall Lemma, we obtain

X(t)\leq A_{n}(t)\exp\left(C\int_{n}^{t}a_{+}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right),\qquad t\in[n,n+1].

Since

a_{+}(s)\leq 2\kappa_{u}(s)+C\mu^{2}\delta^{2}+2\mu,

by (
2.7
) in assumption (A4) we have

\int_{n}^{n+1}a_{+}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq 2\int_{n}^{n+1}\kappa_{u}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+C\mu^{2}\delta^{2}+2\mu\leq 2M_{0}+2M_{1}+C\mu^{2}\delta^{2}+2\mu,

where all the constants on the right-hand side are independent of
n
. Therefore, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
independent of
n
, such that taking the supremum over
t\in[n,n+1]
leads to

(5.7)

\sup_{t\in[n,n+1]}X(t)\leq C_{1}\left(X(n)+\int_{n}^{n+1}h(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\sup_{t\in[n,n+1]}|M_{t}-M_{n}|\right)\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Set
S_{n}:=\sup_{t\in[n,n+1]}X(t).

We would like to apply Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality. To do this, we first need to compute the quadratic
variation of the real-valued martingale
M
. Observe that

\langle w(s),G_{\delta}({u}(s))\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{Q}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle G_{\delta}(u(s))^{*}w(s),\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{Q}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{0}},

where
G_{\delta}(u(s))^{*}w(s)\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and in the previous identity we have used that since
G_{\delta}(u(s))\in\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{H})
, then its adjoint

G_{\delta}(u(s))^{\ast}
maps
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Hence

M_{t}=-\mu\int_{0}^{t}\langle G_{\delta}({u}(s))^{*}w(s),\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{Q}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}

is a continuous real-valued martingale with quadratic variation

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|G_{\delta}({u}(s))^{*}w(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, for
t\in[n,n+1]
,

\langle M-M_{n}\rangle_{t}=\mu^{2}\int_{n}^{t}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))^{*}w(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Applying the BDG inequality to the martingale

(M_{t}-M_{n})_{t\in[n,n+1]}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[n,n+1]}|M_{t}-M_{n}|\leq C\mathbb{E}\left(\langle M-M_{n}\rangle_{n+1}\right)^{1/2}=C\mu\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{n}^{n+1}\|G_{\delta}(u(s))^{*}w(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}.

Taking expectations in (
5.7
) and applying the BDG inequality,

(5.8)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[n,n+1]}|M_{t}-M_{n}|\leq C\mu\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{n}^{n+1}\|G_{\delta}({u}(s))^{\ast}w(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\leq C\mu\mathbb{E}\left(S_{n}^{1/2}\left(\int_{n}^{n+1}\|G_{\delta}({u}(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\right).

Using Young’s inequality, we obtain

(5.9)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[n,n+1]}|M_{t}-M_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{2C_{1}}\mathbb{E}S_{n}+C\mu^{2}\int_{n}^{n+1}\|G_{\delta}({u}(s))\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Combining (
5.7
) and (
5.9
), and absorbing the term

\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[S_{n}\right]
into the left-hand side, gives

(5.10)

\mathbb{E}\left[S_{n}\right]\leq C_{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\|w_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\int_{n}^{n+1}h(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right),\qquad n\geq 0,

for some constant
C_{2}>0
independent of
n
. Summing (
5.10
) over
n\geq 0
, and using (
5.5
) together with (
5.2
), we conclude that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[S_{n}\right]<\infty.

Step 3.
Finally, for every
\varepsilon>0
, Markov’s inequality gives

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}>\varepsilon^{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[S_{n}\right]<\infty.

Hence, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma,

S_{n}=\sup_{t\in[n,n+1]}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\to 0\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

This implies
w_{t}\to 0
in
\mathcal{H}
, almost surely, as
t\to\infty
. Lastly, to prove the uniform convergence of the tails in (
2.17
) observe that

\sup_{t\geq N}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\sup_{n\geq N}\sup_{t\in[n,n+1]}\|w_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\sup_{n\geq N}S_{n}^{1/2}.

Thus (
2.17
) follows from the fact that
S_{n}\to 0.

∎

Appendix A
A local existence result for non-autonomous semilinear equations

This appendix contains a non-autonomous local well-posedness result used in the proof of
Section 4
. The result is a variant of the autonomous local theory recalled in
Section 4
, adapted to semilinear equations allowing the local Lipschitz constants to depend on time. We include the proof for completeness, since the random PDE (
4.4
) falls exactly in this framework.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
T>0
. Assume
\widetilde{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
and that
\widetilde{A}
enjoys
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-maximal regularity on
(0,T).
Let
\widetilde{G}:(0,T)\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{\ast}
and assume the following.

(1)

Fix
x\in\mathcal{V}
. The map
t\mapsto\widetilde{G}(t,x)
is strongly-measurable as a map with values in
\mathcal{V}^{*}
.

(2)

For every
j=1,\ldots,m
, let
\beta_{j}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,

\rho_{j}\geq 0
, and assume that
(\beta_{j},\rho_{j})_{j}
satisfy
(
2.2
), i.e.
(2\beta_{j}-1)(\rho_{j}+1)\leq 1
. Set

r_{j}:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{2(\rho_{j}+1)}{\rho_{j}},&\rho_{j}>0,\\
\infty,&\rho_{j}=0.\end{cases}

There exists a measurable function
g_{j}\in\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,T)
such that, for a.e.

t\in(0,T)
and every
x,y\in\mathcal{V}

(A.1)

\|\widetilde{G}(t,x)-\widetilde{G}(t,y)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}(1+|g_{j}(t)|+\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}+\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}})\|x-y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}.

(3)

\widetilde{G}(\cdot,0)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
.

Then, for any
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists
a=a(u_{0})\in(0,T]
such that

(A.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}(t)+\widetilde{A}u(t)&=\widetilde{G}(t,u(t)),\qquad t\in(0,a),\\
u(0)&=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

admits a unique solution

u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,a;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})\cap\mathrm{BUC}([0,a];\mathcal{H}).

Moreover, the solution can be extended to a maximal interval of existence
[0,a_{\max}(u_{0}))\subseteq[0,T]
. If
a_{\max}(u_{0})<T
, then

(A.3)

\lim\limits_{t\to a_{\max}(u_{0})}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,t;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})}=+\infty.

Proof.

For
a\in(0,T]
set
\mathbb{E}_{a}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,a;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
and
\mathbb{F}_{a}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})
. On
\mathbb{E}_{a}
, we consider the maximal regularity norm
\|u\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}:=\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a;\mathcal{V})}+\|u^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a;\mathcal{V}^{\ast})}.
Since
\widetilde{A}
enjoys
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-maximal regularity on finite intervals, for
every
a\in(0,T]
,
f\in\mathbb{F}_{a}
, and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, the linear problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}(t)+\widetilde{A}u(t)&=f(t),\qquad t\in(0,a),\\
u(0)&=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

admits a unique solution
u\in\mathbb{E}_{a}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
M_{T}>0
,
independent of
a\in(0,T]
, such that

(A.4)

\|u\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}\leq M_{T}(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|f\|_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}).

For
j=1,\dots,m
, define

\theta_{j}:=2\beta_{j}-1\in(0,1),\qquad q_{j}:=2(\rho_{j}+1),\qquad r_{j}:=\begin{cases}\frac{2(\rho_{j}+1)}{\rho_{j}},\;&\text{ if }\rho_{j}>0,\\
+\infty,\;&\text{ if }\rho_{j}=0,\end{cases}\qquad\sigma_{j}:=\frac{1-\theta_{j}(\rho_{j}+1)}{2(\rho_{j}+1)}\geq 0.

Throughout the proof, when
r_{j}=\infty
we use the conventions
\frac{1}{r_{j}}=0
and
a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}=1
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}=(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V})_{\theta_{j},2}
, interpolation inequality gives

\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}\leq C\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{1-\theta_{j}}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\theta_{j}}\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,a).

Using that (
2.2
) is equivalent to
\theta_{j}(\rho_{j}+1)\leq 1
, we obtain by interpolation and Hölder inequality

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}^{q_{j}}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,a];\mathcal{H})}^{(1-\theta_{j})q_{j}}\int_{0}^{a}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\theta_{j}q_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\|u\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,a];\mathcal{H})}^{(1-\theta_{j})q_{j}}a^{1-\frac{\theta_{j}q_{j}}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a;\mathcal{V})}^{\theta_{j}q_{j}}.

Since
q_{j}=2(\rho_{j}+1)
, we have
1-\frac{\theta_{j}q_{j}}{2}=1-\theta_{j}(\rho_{j}+1)
. Considering the
q_{j}
th square root of the previous chain of inequalities, we obtain

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}\leq Ca^{\sigma_{j}}\|u\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,a];\mathcal{H})}^{1-\theta_{j}}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a;\mathcal{V})}^{\theta_{j}}.

Since
\mathbb{E}_{a}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}([0,a];\mathcal{H})
, we conclude that

(A.5)

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}\leq C_{T}a^{\sigma_{j}}(\|u(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|u\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}})^{1-\theta_{j}}\|u\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}^{\theta_{j}}.

In particular, if
u\in\mathbb{E}_{a}
and
u(0)=0
,

(A.6)

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}\leq C_{T}a^{\sigma_{j}}\|u\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}.

Step 1: construction of the fixed point map.

Let
z\in\mathbb{E}_{T}
be the unique solution of

z^{\prime}(t)+\widetilde{A}z(t)=\widetilde{G}(t,0),\qquad t\in(0,T),\qquad z(0)=u_{0}.

For
a\in(0,T]
, set
z_{a}:=z|_{(0,a)}
. By virtue of (
A.4
) and assumption
(3)
, we have

\|z\|_{\mathbb{E}_{T}}\leq M_{T}(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\widetilde{G}(\cdot,0)\|_{\mathbb{F}_{T}}).

Moreover, for any
j=1,...,m

(A.7)

\|z_{a}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}=\|z\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}\to 0\qquad\text{as }a\to 0.

Let
\mathbb{E}_{a,0}:=\{w\in\mathbb{E}_{a}:w(0)=0\}.
Fix
R>0
and define
\mathbb{B}_{a,R}:=\{w\in\mathbb{E}_{a,0}:\|w\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}\leq R\}.

Define

\Psi_{a}:\mathbb{B}_{a,R}\to\mathbb{E}_{a,0},\qquad\Psi_{a}(w):=v,

where
v
is the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} v^{\prime}(t)+\widetilde{A}v(t)&=\widetilde{G}(t,z_{a}(t)+w(t))-\widetilde{G}(t,0),\qquad t\in(0,a),\\
v(0)&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that, by the maximal regularity theory recalled above,
\Psi_{a}
is well defined, if we are able to show that the right-hand side belongs to
\mathbb{F}_{a}
. To show this, set
\varphi_{j}(t):=\|z_{a}(t)+w(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}.
By (
A.5
),
\varphi_{j}\in\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a)
. Moreover, by (
A.1
) with
y=0

\|\widetilde{G}(t,z_{a}+w)-\widetilde{G}(t,0)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[(1+|g_{j}(t)|)\varphi_{j}(t)+\varphi_{j}(t)^{\rho_{j}+1}\right].

Since
\frac{1}{q_{j}}+\frac{1}{r_{j}}=\frac{1}{2},
Hölder’s inequality gives

\|(1+|g_{j}|)\varphi_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a)}\leq(a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}+\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,a)})\|\varphi_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a)}.

Furthermore, since
q_{j}=2(\rho_{j}+1)
, it holds
\|\varphi_{j}^{\rho_{j}+1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a)}=\|\varphi_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a)}^{\rho_{j}+1}.

Hence each term on the right-hand side belongs to
\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a)
.

Step
2
:
\Psi_{a}
maps
\mathbb{B}_{a,R}
in itself.
Applying the maximal regularity estimate in (
A.4
) to
\Psi_{a}(w),
and the estimates in Step
1
, we have

(A.8)

\begin{split}\|\Psi_{a}(w)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}\leq M_{T}\|\widetilde{G}(\cdot,z_{a}+w)-\widetilde{G}(\cdot,0)\|_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}\leq C_{T}\sum_{j=1}^{m}&\Big[(a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}+\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,a)})\|z_{a}+w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}\\
&\quad+\|z_{a}+w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}^{\rho_{j}+1}\Big].\end{split}

Using the triangle inequality, (
A.6
), and the fact that
w\in\mathbb{B}_{a,R}
, we have

\begin{split}\|z_{a}+w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}&\leq\|z_{a}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}+\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}\leq\|z_{a}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}+C_{T}a^{\sigma_{j}}\|w\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}\\
&\leq\|z_{a}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}+C_{T}a^{\sigma_{j}}R=:K_{j}(a,R).\end{split}

Hence, (
A.8
) can be rewritten as

\|\Psi_{a}(w)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}\leq C_{T}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[(a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}+\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,a)})K_{j}(a,R)+K_{j}(a,R)^{\rho_{j}+1}\right].

It remains to be shown that the right-hand side can be made smaller than
R
for a suitable choice of
a,R
.

If
\sigma_{j}>0,
then
K_{j}(a,R)\to 0
as
a\to 0
thanks to (
A.7
).

If
\sigma_{j}=0,
then
\theta_{j}(\rho_{j}+1)=1.
Since
\theta_{j}\in(0,1),
this implies
\rho_{j}>0
and thus
r_{j}<\infty.
Thus

a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}+\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,a)}\to 0\qquad\text{ as }a\to 0,

Furthermore,
K_{j}(a,R)\to C_{T}R
as
a\to 0
. These imply two facts. First, the terms
(a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}+\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,a)})K_{j}(a,R)
can be made arbitrarily small. Second, for the remaining terms we have
K_{j}(a,R)^{\rho_{j}+1}\sim R^{\rho_{j}+1}
. In conclusion, we can find
R,a
such that
\|\Psi_{a}(w)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}\leq R
.

Step
3
:
\Psi_{a}
is a contraction.
Let
v_{i}=\Psi_{a}(w_{i})
, for
i=1,2
and
w_{i}\in\mathbb{B}_{a,R}
. Then
v:=v_{1}-v_{2}\in\mathbb{E}_{a}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{split}v^{\prime}(t)+\widetilde{A}v(t)&=\widetilde{G}(t,z_{a}(t)+w_{1}(t))-\widetilde{G}(t,z_{a}(t)+w_{2}(t)),\qquad t\in(0,a),\\
v(0)&=0.\end{split}\right.

By maximal regularity

\|\Psi_{a}(w_{1})-\Psi_{a}(w_{2})\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}\leq M_{T}\|\widetilde{G}(\cdot,z_{a}+w_{1})-\widetilde{G}(\cdot,z_{a}+w_{2})\|_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}.

We now estimate the norm on the right-hand side. By (
A.1
), we have

\|\widetilde{G}(\cdot,z_{a}+w_{1})-\widetilde{G}(\cdot,z_{a}+w_{2})\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}(1+|g_{j}|+\|z_{a}+w_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}+\|z_{a}+w_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}})\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}.

Considering the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a)
norm, we estimate the three types of terms separately. First, using Hölder inequality with
\frac{1}{r_{j}}+\frac{1}{q_{j}}=\frac{1}{2},
we have

\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}\leq a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})},

where, if
\rho_{j}=0
, the previous coefficients are
q_{j}=2
,
r_{j}=\infty
, and
a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}=1
. Second, again by Hölder inequality with
\frac{1}{r_{j}}+\frac{1}{q_{j}}=\frac{1}{2}
, it holds

\||g_{j}|\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a)}\leq\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,a)}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}.

For the non-linear terms, if
\rho_{j}>0
, then
\rho_{j}r_{j}=q_{j}
. Again thanks to Hölder inequality with the same exponents, we get to

\begin{split}\left\|\,\|z_{a}+w_{i}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}^{\rho_{j}}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}}}\,\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a)}\leq\|z_{a}+w_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}^{\rho_{j}}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}.\end{split}

If
\rho_{j}=0
, then the previous term simplifies to just

\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}=\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}.
In conclusion, with the convention
K_{j}(a,R)^{0}=1
, we arrive at

\begin{split}\|\widetilde{G}(\cdot,z_{a}+w_{1})-\widetilde{G}(\cdot,z_{a}+w_{2})\|_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}&\Big(a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}+\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,a)}+\|z_{a}+w_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}^{\rho_{j}}\\
&\quad+\|z_{a}+w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}^{\rho_{j}}\Big)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}.\end{split}

Using (
A.6
) to bound the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(0,a;\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{j}})}
and using that
\|w_{i}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}\leq R,
we obtain

\|\Psi_{a}(w_{1})-\Psi_{a}(w_{2})\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}\leq C_{T}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{m}a^{\sigma_{j}}\left(a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}+\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,a)}+2K_{j}(a,R)^{\rho_{j}}\right)\right]\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{a}}.

We now choose
R>0
and
a>0
so that the previous Lipschitz constant is strictly smaller than
1
. Indeed, if
\sigma_{j}>0
, then the corresponding contribution tends to zero as
a\to 0
. This also covers the endpoint

\rho_{j}=0
, because then
\sigma_{j}=\frac{1-\theta_{j}}{2}>0
, while

r_{j}=\infty
and
K_{j}(a,R)^{0}=1
. If instead
\sigma_{j}=0
, then
\theta_{j}(\rho_{j}+1)=1
, and therefore
\rho_{j}>0
and
r_{j}<\infty
. Hence

a^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}+\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(0,a)}\to 0\qquad\text{as }a\to 0,

and
K_{j}(a,R)^{\rho_{j}}\to(C_{T}R)^{\rho_{j}}
. Choosing first
R>0
small enough and then
a>0
small enough, the full Lipschitz constant is strictly smaller than
1
.

By the contraction mapping principle, there exists a unique fixed point of
w\in\mathbb{B}_{a,R}
for
\Psi_{a}.
Then,
u:=z_{a}+w\in\mathbb{E}_{a}
solves the original problem (
A.2
) on
(0,a)
.

Step
4
: uniqueness in
\mathbb{E}_{a}
and maximal interval of existence.
Regarding the uniqueness in
\mathbb{E}_{a},
let
u_{i}\in\mathbb{E}_{a}
, for
i=1,2
, be two solutions of (
A.2
) with the same initial condition
u_{0}
. Hence, for
s,t\in(0,a]
, with
s<t
, their difference
v:=u_{1}-u_{2}
solves

\left\{\begin{split}v^{\prime}(r)+\widetilde{A}v(r)&=\widetilde{G}(r,u_{1}(r))-\widetilde{G}(r,u_{2}(r)),\qquad r\in(s,t),\\
v(s)&=u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s).\end{split}\right.

Repeating the contraction estimate of Step
3
on the arbitrary time interval
(s,t)\subset(0,a)
, with
u_{i}
replacing
z_{a}+w_{i}
, and assuming that
u_{1}(s)=u_{2}(s)
, it is possible to check that

\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{s,t}}\leq C_{T}L(s,t)\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{s,t}},

where

L(s,t):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(t-s)^{\sigma_{j}}\left[(t-s)^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}+\|g_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}(s,t)}+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(s,t;V_{\beta_{j}})}^{\rho_{j}}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}(s,t;V_{\beta_{j}})}^{\rho_{j}}\right],

and
\mathbb{E}_{s,t}:=\mathrm{L}^{2}(s,t;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(s,t;\mathcal{V}^{\ast}).

We now check that
L(s,t)\to 0
as
t\to s
. Indeed, if
\sigma_{j}>0
, then the factor
(t-s)^{\sigma_{j}}
gives smallness of
L(s,t)
. If
\sigma_{j}=0
, then, as in Step
3
, we have
\rho_{j}>0
and
r_{j}<\infty.
So, the smallness follows from the absolute continuity of the norms in
\mathrm{L}^{r_{j}}
and
\mathrm{L}^{q_{j}}
.
Hence, for any
s<a
, such that
u_{1}=u_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
on
[0,s]
, there exists
t>s
such that
C_{T}L(s,t)<1
, and therefore
u_{1}=u_{2}
also on
[s,t].

Let
\tilde{a}:=\inf\{t\in[0,a]\,:\,u_{1}(t)\neq u_{2}(t)\}.
Since
u_{1},u_{2}
are continuous on
[0,a]
with values in
\mathcal{H}
we must have
u_{1}(\tilde{a})=u_{2}(\tilde{a})\in\mathcal{H}
.
The previous argument shows that
u_{1}=u_{2}
also on
[\tilde{a},\tilde{a}+h]
for sufficiently small
h>0
which is a contradiction to the definition of
\tilde{a}
, and we have proved uniqueness on the whole interval
[0,a].

Lastly, the existence of a maximal solution follows from a classical argument based on considering
a_{\max}(u_{0}):=\sup\{\tau\in(0,T]:\text{ a solution exists on }(0,\tau)\}
and then gluing together (thanks to uniqueness) the local in time solutions constructed with the fixed point argument.
