Can classical theories of gravity produce entanglement?
Anirudh Gundhi, Giorgia Infantino, Angelo Bassi

Abstract
A recent paper published on Nature 1 claims that quantum particles become entangled when they interact gravitationally, even if the gravitational potential is treated classically. We show that the entanglement found by the authors stems from discarding some of the transition amplitudes, which, when kept, guarantee that an initially factorized state remains so over time. Therefore, no entanglement is generated by the classical gravitational interaction in the scenario considered by the authors.

Having proved that, in the situation considered in
1

4

3

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{ct}dx^{0}\int_{0}^{ct}dz^{0}\;e^{i\gamma(z^{0}-x^{0})}\int\frac{dp^{0}}{2\pi}\,f(p^{0};\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z})\,e^{ip^{0}(x^{0}-z^{0})}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{ct}{4\pi|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|},

(28)

where the last step is precisely the delta-function approximation used in going from
Eqs. (​(66)​)–(​(69)​). Similarly, for the second propagator we get

\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{y},t)\approx\frac{ct}{4\pi|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}|}.

(29)

Substituting Eqs. (
28
) and (
29
) into
Eq. (
25
), and then restoring the contributions from the
\hat{\pi}
-insertions
exactly as in the paragraph immediately before Eq. (​(70)​), we obtain

\displaystyle\beta^{(4)}_{ij;mk}\big|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{m^{6}t^{2}N^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\hbar^{6}}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,d^{3}\mathbf{z}\,d^{3}\mathbf{y}\,d^{3}\mathbf{w}\;\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{x})\Phi(\mathbf{z})\Phi(\mathbf{y})\Phi(\mathbf{w})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|\,|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}|}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\tilde{\phi}_{2k}(\mathbf{x})\tilde{\phi}_{1i}(\mathbf{z})\tilde{\phi}_{1m}(\mathbf{y})\tilde{\phi}_{2j}(\mathbf{w})\times i^{2}.

(30)

Using
\tilde{\phi}_{\kappa a}(\mathbf{x})=\theta_{\kappa a}(\mathbf{x})/\sqrt{V}
, this factorizes as

\displaystyle\beta^{(4)}_{ij;mk}\big|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{m^{6}t^{2}N^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\hbar^{6}V^{2}}\left(i\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,d^{3}\mathbf{z}\;\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{x})\Phi(\mathbf{z})\,\theta_{2k}(\mathbf{x})\theta_{1i}(\mathbf{z})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|}\right)\left(i\int d^{3}\mathbf{w}\,d^{3}\mathbf{y}\;\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{w})\Phi(\mathbf{y})\,\theta_{2j}(\mathbf{w})\theta_{1m}(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}|}\right).

(31)

Renaming dummy variables and using the symmetry of the denominator,
we define

V_{ij}:=i\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,d^{3}\mathbf{y}\;\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{x})\Phi(\mathbf{y})\,\theta_{1i}(\mathbf{x})\theta_{2j}(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|},

(32)

so that Eq. (
31
) becomes

\beta^{(4)}_{ij;mk}\big|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}\approx\frac{m^{6}t^{2}N^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\hbar^{6}V^{2}}\,V_{ik}V_{mj}.

(33)

For the diagonal case
m=i
and
k=j
, Eq. (
33
) reduces to

\beta^{(4)}_{ij;ij}\big|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}\approx\frac{m^{6}t^{2}N^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\hbar^{6}V^{2}}\,\bigl(V_{ij}\bigr)^{2},

(34)

which is exactly Eq. (​(70)​):

\beta^{(4)}_{ij}\big|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}\approx\frac{m^{6}t^{2}N^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\hbar^{6}V^{2}}\left(i\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,d^{3}\mathbf{y}\,\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{x})\Phi(\mathbf{y})\theta_{1i}(\mathbf{x})\theta_{2j}(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|}\right)^{2}.

(35)

II
Distance dependence of the transition amplitudes
\beta^{(4)}_{ij}\big|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}

Evaluating the exchange terms (
31
) requires computing the integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,d^{3}\mathbf{z}\;\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{z})\Phi(\mathbf{x})\theta_{1i}(\mathbf{z})\theta_{2k}(\mathbf{x})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|}.

(36)

We compute
\mathcal{I}
in the regime where the inter-branch distances
d_{ik}
, and the separation between the objects
\Delta x
, are much bigger than the radius of the objects
R
such that
d_{ik}\gg R
and
\Delta x\gg R
. When this is the case, the denominator
|{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}}|
inside the integral can be replaced with
d_{ik}
and taken outside. This is because
\theta_{1i}(\mathbf{z})
and
\theta_{2k}(\mathbf{x})
restrict the coordinates to stay inside the two objects centered at
\mathbf{X}_{1i}
and
\mathbf{X}_{2k}
respectively. Therefore, since we have in mind the geometries where
|\mathbf{X}_{1i}-\mathbf{X}_{2k}|=d_{ik}\gg R
, the distance
|{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}}|
in
\mathcal{I}
can be approximated by
|{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}}|\approx d_{ik}
. Furthermore, the semiclassical potentials
\Phi(\mathbf{z})
and
\Phi(\mathbf{x})
, as specified in Eq. (​(51)​) of Ref.
2
, can be replaced with the potentials
\Phi_{1i}(\mathbf{z})
and
\Phi_{2k}(\mathbf{x})
inside the integral, where

\displaystyle\Phi_{1i}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle:=\frac{GM}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2R}-\frac{|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{X}_{1i}|^{2}}{2R^{3}}\right),

\displaystyle\Phi_{2k}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle:=\frac{GM}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2R}-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{X}_{2k}|^{2}}{2R^{3}}\right).

(37)

That is, at locations
\mathbf{z}
and
\mathbf{x}
in
\mathcal{I}
, the predominant contribution to the semiclassical gravitational potential considered in Eq. (​(51)​) of Ref.
2
, comes only from the spheres centered at
\mathbf{X}_{1i}
and
\mathbf{X}_{2k}
respectively. Making use of these considerations, in spherical polar coordinates, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\frac{4\pi^{2}G^{2}M^{2}}{d_{ik}}\left[\int_{0}^{R}drr^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2R}-\frac{r^{2}}{2R^{3}}\right)\right]^{2}=\frac{16\pi^{2}G^{2}M^{2}R^{4}}{25d_{ik}}.

(38)

Plugging this back in Eq. (
31
) we get

\displaystyle\beta^{(4)}_{ij;mk}\big|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=\frac{9m^{6}t^{2}N^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\hbar^{6}(4\pi)^{2}R^{6}}\times\frac{i16\pi^{2}G^{2}M^{2}R^{4}}{25d_{ik}}\times\frac{i16\pi^{2}G^{2}M^{2}R^{4}}{25d_{mj}},

(39)

which yields the compact expression

\displaystyle\beta^{(4)}_{ij;mk}\big|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=\left(\frac{6}{25}\frac{iG^{2}m^{2}M^{3}Rt}{\hbar^{3}}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{d_{ik}d_{mj}}.

(40)

It is clear that for the diagonal terms where
m=i
and
k=j
, we get the result obtained in Eq.
(\!(84)\!)
of Ref.
2
.

III
No entanglement generation for fixed number of particles

In this section we show that an initially factorized state remains factorized when particles described by a (complex) Klein-Gordon field interact only with an external classical potential, and no-pair creation is allowed.

As before,
\hat{\phi}(x)
is the complex Klein–Gordon field and
\Phi(\mathbf{x})
the prescribed external classical potential. The free one-particle energy is

E_{p}:=\sqrt{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}+m^{2}}
, and for simplicity we have set
\hbar=c=1
throughout this section. In the interaction picture, the free field
\hat{\phi}(x)
and its conjugate momentum are expanded as

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{I}(t,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{p}}}\left(\hat{a}(\mathbf{p})e^{-iE_{p}t+i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})e^{+iE_{p}t-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\right),

(41)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{I}(t,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\hat{\phi}_{I}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x})=i\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\sqrt{\frac{E_{p}}{2}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})e^{+iE_{p}t-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}}-\hat{b}(\mathbf{p})e^{-iE_{p}t+i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\right).

(42)

In terms of the freely evolving fields, the interaction Hamiltonian considered in
2
is given by

\hat{H}^{\mathrm{int}}_{I}(t)=4\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,\Phi(\mathbf{x})\left[\hat{\pi}_{I}(t,\mathbf{x})\hat{\pi}_{I}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x})-\frac{m^{2}}{2}\hat{\phi}_{I}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x})\hat{\phi}_{I}(t,\mathbf{x})\right].

(43)

In the Fourier space where

\widetilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}):=\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\Phi(\mathbf{x})

(44)

denotes the spatial Fourier transform of the external potential, and using the mode expansions of
\hat{\phi}_{I}
and
\hat{\pi}_{I}
,
\hat{H}^{\mathrm{int}}_{I}(t)
is given by

\hat{H}^{\mathrm{int}}_{I}(t)=E(t)+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{A}_{t}\hat{a}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}\mathcal{B}_{t}\hat{b}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{C}_{t}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\hat{b}\,\mathcal{C}_{t}^{\dagger}\hat{a}.

(45)

Here
E(t)
is a c-number coming from normal ordering of the
\hat{b}\,\hat{b}^{\dagger}
term, and

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{A}_{t}\hat{a}

\displaystyle=\iint d^{3}\mathbf{p}\,d^{3}\mathbf{q}\,\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})\hat{a}(\mathbf{q}),

(46)

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{\dagger}\mathcal{B}_{t}\hat{b}

\displaystyle=\iint d^{3}\mathbf{p}\,d^{3}\mathbf{q}\,\mathcal{B}_{t}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\,\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})\hat{b}(\mathbf{q}),

(47)

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{C}_{t}\hat{b}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\iint d^{3}\mathbf{p}\,d^{3}\mathbf{q}\,\mathcal{C}_{t}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{q}),

(48)

where the functions
A_{t},\mathcal{B}_{t}
and
\mathcal{C}_{t}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q})}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{2E_{p}E_{q}-m^{2}}{\sqrt{E_{p}E_{q}}}\,e^{\,i(E_{p}-E_{q})t},

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{t}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{p})}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{2E_{p}E_{q}-m^{2}}{\sqrt{E_{p}E_{q}}}\,e^{-\,i(E_{p}-E_{q})t},

(50)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{t}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q})}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{2E_{p}E_{q}+m^{2}}{\sqrt{E_{p}E_{q}}}\,e^{\,i(E_{p}+E_{q})t}.

(51)

Since
\Phi
is real, we have
\mathcal{B}_{t}=\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\dagger}
. There is no term of the form
\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{D}_{t}\hat{b}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\dagger}\hat{a}
. This is a consequence of the global
U(1)
symmetry of the interaction, i.e.
\hat{H}^{\mathrm{int}}_{I}(t)
in Eq. (
45
) commutes with the charge operator

\hat{Q}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{p}\,\bigl(\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})\hat{a}(\mathbf{p})-\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})\hat{b}(\mathbf{p})\bigr).

(52)

The terms
\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{C}_{t}\hat{b}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{b}\,\mathcal{C}_{t}^{\dagger}\hat{a}

create or annihilate a particle–antiparticle pair and therefore do not conserve the total number operator

\hat{N}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{p}\,\bigl(\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})\hat{a}(\mathbf{p})+\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})\hat{b}(\mathbf{p})\bigr).

(53)

Indeed,
[\hat{N},\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{C}_{t}\hat{b}^{\dagger}]=2\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{C}_{t}\hat{b}^{\dagger}
and

[\hat{N},\hat{b}\,\mathcal{C}_{t}^{\dagger}\hat{a}]=-2\hat{b}\,\mathcal{C}_{t}^{\dagger}\hat{a}
.
As in
2
, we assume that the total number of particles remains constant (no pair-creation), therefore we neglect these terms and replace Eq. (
45
) by

\hat{H}^{\text{int}}_{I}(t){=}E(t)+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{A}_{t}\hat{a}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}\mathcal{B}_{t}\hat{b}.

(54)

To proceed further, we consider

\mathcal{H}_{+}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},d^{3}\mathbf{p}),\qquad\mathcal{H}_{-}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},d^{3}\mathbf{p}),\qquad\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-}.

For
F=(f,g)\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
, we define

\hat{A}^{\dagger}(F):=\hat{a}^{\dagger}(f)+\hat{b}^{\dagger}(g),

(55)

where

\hat{a}^{\dagger}(f)=\int d^{3}\mathbf{p}\,f(\mathbf{p})\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p}),\qquad\hat{b}^{\dagger}(g)=\int d^{3}\mathbf{p}\,g(\mathbf{p})\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p}).

(56)

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
54
) is the second quantization of the one-particle operator

\hat{H}_{1}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{t}&0\\
0&\mathcal{B}_{t}\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{on }\mathcal{H}_{1},

(57)

namely

\hat{H}^{\mathrm{int}}_{I}(t)=d\Gamma(\hat{H}_{1}(t))+E(t).

(58)

Here
d\Gamma(\hat{H}_{1}(t))
denotes the second quantization of the one-particle operator
\hat{H}_{1}(t)
: if

\mathcal{F}_{s}(\mathcal{H}_{1})=\bigoplus_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\otimes_{s}N},

(59)

then on the
N
-particle sector,

d\Gamma(\hat{H}_{1}(t))=\left(\hat{H}_{1}(t)\otimes\cdots\otimes 1_{N}\right)+\cdots+\left(1_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{H}_{1}(t)\otimes\cdots\otimes 1_{N}\right)+\cdots+\left(1_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{H}_{1}(t)\right).

(60)

That is, it acts as the sum of the one-particle Hamiltonian on each tensor factor.
To consider the full time evolution, we move to the Scrödinger picture, and add the free Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}

\hat{H}_{0}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{p}\,E_{p}\,\bigl(\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})\hat{a}(\mathbf{p})+\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{p})\hat{b}(\mathbf{p})\bigr).

(61)

Then, in the Schrödinger picture, the full (no-pair) Hamiltonian reads

\hat{H}(t)=d\Gamma(\hat{H}_{1})+E(t),

(62)

with

\hat{H}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\Omega+\mathcal{A}_{t=0}&0\\
0&\Omega+\mathcal{B}_{t=0}\end{pmatrix},\qquad(\Omega f)(\mathbf{p})=E_{p}f(\mathbf{p}).

(63)

Using the canonical commutation relations, one finds on the finite-particle domain

\displaystyle[\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{A}_{t}\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}(f)]=\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{A}_{t}f),\qquad[\hat{b}^{\dagger}\mathcal{B}_{t}\hat{b},\hat{b}^{\dagger}(g)]=\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{B}_{t}g),

(64)

while all the mixed commutators vanish. Here, as before, we use the compact notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}f:=\int d^{3}\mathbf{q}\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})f(\mathbf{q}),\qquad\mathcal{B}_{t}g:=\int d^{3}\mathbf{q}\mathcal{B}_{t}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})g(\mathbf{q}).

(65)

Hence

[\hat{H}(t),\hat{A}^{\dagger}(F)]=\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\hat{H}_{1}F),\qquad F\in\mathcal{H}_{1},\qquad H_{1}F=(\mathcal{A}_{t=0}+\Omega)f+(\mathcal{B}_{t=0}+\Omega)g.

(66)

Let
\hat{U}(t,s)
be the propagator generated by
\hat{H}(t)
,

i\partial_{t}\hat{U}(t,s)=\hat{H}(t)\hat{U}(t,s),\qquad\hat{U}(s,s)=1,

and let
\hat{U}_{1}(t,s)
be the one-particle propagator generated by
\hat{H}_{1}
,

i\partial_{t}\hat{U}_{1}(t,s)=\hat{H}_{1}\hat{U}_{1}(t,s),\qquad\hat{U}_{1}(s,s)=1.

For the proof of no-entanglement, it is useful to define

\hat{Y}_{F}(t):=\hat{U}(t,s)^{\dagger}\,\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\hat{U}_{1}(t,s)F)\,\hat{U}(t,s).

Using Eq. (
66
), one computes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{Y}_{F}(t)

\displaystyle=i\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{H}(t)\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\hat{U}_{1}F)\hat{U}+\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\partial_{t}\hat{U}_{1}F)\hat{U}-i\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\hat{U}_{1}F)\hat{H}(t)\hat{U}

\displaystyle=i\hat{U}^{\dagger}[\hat{H}(t),\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\hat{U}_{1}F)]\hat{U}-i\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\hat{H}_{1}(t)\hat{U}_{1}F)\hat{U}

\displaystyle=0.

(67)

Since
\hat{Y}_{F}(s)=\hat{A}^{\dagger}(F)
, it follows that

\hat{U}(t,s)\hat{A}^{\dagger}(F)\hat{U}(t,s)^{\dagger}=\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\hat{U}_{1}(t,s)F).

(68)

Moreover, since
\hat{H}(t)|0\rangle=E(t)|0\rangle
, the vacuum evolves only by a phase:

\hat{U}(t,s)|0\rangle=e^{-i\theta(t,s)}|0\rangle,\qquad\theta(t,s)=\int_{s}^{t}E(\tau)\,d\tau.

(69)

Consider an initially factorised bosonic Hartree state

\Psi_{N}(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}\hat{A}^{\dagger}(F)^{N}|0\rangle,\qquad F\in\mathcal{H}_{1}.

(70)

Then, using (
68
) and (
69
), we obtain

\Psi_{N}(t)=\hat{U}(t,s)\Psi_{N}(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}\,\hat{U}(t,s)\hat{A}^{\dagger}(F)^{N}\hat{U}(t,s)^{\dagger}\,\hat{U}(t,s)|0\rangle=e^{-i\theta(t,s)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}\,\hat{A}^{\dagger}\!\bigl(\hat{U}_{1}(t,s)F\bigr)^{N}|0\rangle.

(71)

Therefore, an initially factorized bosonic state remains factorized under the no-pair dynamics. More generally, if

\Psi_{N}(s)=\hat{A}^{\dagger}(F_{1})\cdots\hat{A}^{\dagger}(F_{N})|0\rangle,

(72)

then

\Psi_{N}(t)=e^{-i\theta(t,s)}\hat{A}^{\dagger}\!\bigl(\hat{U}_{1}(t,s)F_{1}\bigr)\cdots\hat{A}^{\dagger}\!\bigl(\hat{U}_{1}(t,s)F_{N}\bigr)|0\rangle.

(73)

Therefore, any symmetrized tensor product remains a symmetrized tensor product.

IV
The transition amplitudes in first quantization

In this section we show that the same expressions for the transition amplitudes
\beta_{ij}
computed in Ref.
2
within second quantization, can also be derived for
non-relativistic particles in first quantization
.

IV.1
Identical particles

Since the dynamics is non-relativistic and particle creation is not relevant, instead of the
N
-particle state considered in
1
, we take a two-particle wavefunction where the first particle is in a superposition of the two different locations represented by
\ket{1L}
and
\ket{1R}
and also the second particle is in a superposition of the other two locations represented by
\ket{2L}
and right
\ket{2R}
. We first consider the case of identical particles.
For two particles, the initially factorized state of
2
written in second quantization (Q2) reads

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi(t_i)}\overset{\mathrm{Q2}}{=}\frac{1}{2}\left(\ket{1}_{1L}+\ket{1}_{1R}\right)\otimes\left(\ket{1}_{2L}+\ket{1}_{2R}\right)\,.

(74)

Instead, in first quantization (Q1), it is given by

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi(t_i)}\overset{\mathrm{Q1}}{=}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left[\left(\ket{1L}+\ket{1R}\right)\otimes\left(\ket{2L}+\ket{2R}\right)+\left(\ket{2L}+\ket{2R}\right)\otimes\left(\ket{1L}+\ket{1R}\right)\right]\,.

(75)

In order to model the wavepackets of the objects as in
2
, the position states above represent non-overlapping smeared states:

\displaystyle\ket{1L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\theta_{1L}(\mathbf{x})\ket{\mathbf{x}},\quad\ket{1R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\theta_{1R}(\mathbf{x})\ket{\mathbf{x}},

\displaystyle\ket{2L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\theta_{2L}(\mathbf{x})\ket{\mathbf{x}},\quad\ket{2R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\theta_{2R}(\mathbf{x})\ket{\mathbf{x}}.

(76)

Next, in order to describe the dynamics considered in
2
, but this time in first quantization, we take for the two-particle Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2m}\otimes\hat{I}+\hat{I}\otimes\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2m}+\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\left(m\Phi(\mathbf{x})\ket{\mathbf{x}}\bra{\mathbf{x}}\otimes\hat{I}+\hat{I}\otimes m\Phi(\mathbf{x})\ket{\mathbf{x}}\bra{\mathbf{x}}\right)\,.

(77)

When
\Phi(\mathbf{x})
is treated classically, the unitary time evolution operator factorizes as

\displaystyle\hat{U}(t)=\hat{U}_{s}(t)\otimes\hat{U}_{s}(t),

(78)

where
\hat{U}_{s}
is the time evolution operator for a single particle

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{s}(t)=\exp^{-i\hat{H}_{s}t},\quad\hat{H}_{s}=\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2m}+\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}m\Phi(\mathbf{x})\ket{\mathbf{x}}\bra{\mathbf{x}}.

(79)

Therefore, at a later time
t
, the two-particle wavefunction is a symmetrized version of a factorized state. It reads

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi(t)}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{Q1}}{=}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left[\hat{U}_{s}(t)\left(\ket{1L}+\ket{1R}\right)\otimes\hat{U}_{s}(t)\left(\ket{2L}+\ket{2R}\right)+\hat{U}_{s}(t)\left(\ket{2L}+\ket{2R}\right)\otimes\hat{U}_{s}(t)\left(\ket{1L}+\ket{1R}\right)\right]\,.

(80)

To quantitatively describe entanglement, we compute again the coefficients
\beta_{ij}
defined by

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi(t)}\overset{Q2}{=}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}\beta_{ij}(t)\ket{1}_{1i}\otimes\ket{1}_{2j}.

(81)

For instance, one of the four coefficients is defined to be
\beta_{LL}\overset{Q2}{=}2\times\prescript{}{1L}{\bra{1}}\prescript{}{2L}{\bra{1}}\hat{U}\ket{\Psi(t_i)}
. Within first quantization,
\beta_{LL}
reads

\displaystyle\beta_{LL}

\displaystyle\overset{Q1}{=}\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}{\left(\bra{1L}\otimes\bra{2L}+\bra{2L}\otimes\bra{1L}\right)\ket{\Psi(t)}}

\displaystyle=\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}

\displaystyle+\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}

\displaystyle+\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}

\displaystyle+\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}\,.

(82)

Similarly, other coefficients are obtained to be

\displaystyle\beta_{RL}

\displaystyle\overset{Q1}{=}\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}{\left(\bra{1R}\otimes\bra{2L}+\bra{2L}\otimes\bra{1R}\right)\ket{\Psi(t)}}

\displaystyle=\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}

\displaystyle+\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}

\displaystyle+\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}

\displaystyle+\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}\,,

(83)

\displaystyle\beta_{LR}

\displaystyle\overset{Q1}{=}\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}{\left(\bra{1L}\otimes\bra{2R}+\bra{2R}\otimes\bra{1L}\right)\ket{\Psi(t)}}

\displaystyle=\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}

\displaystyle+\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}

\displaystyle+\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}

\displaystyle+\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}\,,

(84)

\displaystyle\beta_{RR}

\displaystyle\overset{Q1}{=}\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}{\left(\bra{1R}\otimes\bra{2R}+\bra{2R}\otimes\bra{1R}\right)\ket{\Psi(t)}}

\displaystyle=\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}

\displaystyle+\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}

\displaystyle+\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}

\displaystyle+\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}\,.

(85)

As discussed in the main text, in
2
, the authors make the diagonal approximation such that

\displaystyle\beta^{d}_{LL}\overset{\text{diag}}{\approx}\prescript{}{1L}{\bra{1}}\prescript{}{2L}{\bra{1}}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1}_{1L}\ket{1}_{2L}\,.

(86)

In first quantization, this amounts to setting the last three lines of Eqs. (
IV.1
)-(
IV.1
) to zero, such that

\displaystyle\beta^{d}_{LL}=

\displaystyle\,\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}\,,

(87)

\displaystyle\beta^{d}_{RL}=

\displaystyle\,\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}\,,

(88)

\displaystyle\beta^{d}_{LR}=

\displaystyle\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}\,,

(89)

\displaystyle\beta^{d}_{RR}=

\displaystyle\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}\,.

(90)

Further, for the discussion concerning entanglement, the authors focus on the transition amplitudes involving the two different objects 1 and 2, and compute the so-called exchange terms. In first quantization, the exchange term for each coefficient corresponds to the second term appearing on the right hand side of Eqs. (
87
)-(
90
) such that:

\displaystyle\beta^{d}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=

\displaystyle\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L},

(91)

\displaystyle\beta^{d}_{RL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=

\displaystyle\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}\,,

(92)

\displaystyle\beta^{d}_{LR}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=

\displaystyle\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}\,,

(93)

\displaystyle\beta^{d}_{RR}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=

\displaystyle\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}\,.

(94)

We now present the derivation to obtain the leading order expression for the coefficient
\beta^{d}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}
. It can be generalized straightforwardly to obtain the other coefficients.

At
0^{th}
order, we have

\displaystyle\beta^{d(0)}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=\bra{2L}\hat{U}^{(0)}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}^{(0)}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}=|\innerproduct{2L|1L}{2L|1L}|^{2}=0.

(95)

This is because
\ket{2L}
and
\ket{1L}
are non-overlapping wavepackets as specified in Eq. (
IV.1
).

Then, at
1^{\mathrm{st}}
order, it follows that also

\displaystyle\beta^{d(1)}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=0,

(96)

since

\displaystyle\beta^{d(1)}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=U^{(0)1L}_{2L}U^{(1)2L}_{1L}+U^{(1)1L}_{2L}U^{(0)2L}_{1L},

(97)

and
U^{(0)1L}_{2L}=U^{(0)2L}_{1L}=0
. Here, we have defined

\displaystyle U^{(0)2L}_{1L}:=\bra{2L}\hat{U}^{(0)}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}.

(98)

At
2^{\mathrm{nd}}
order, due to Eq. (
95
), the only non-trivial contribution can come from the product of two first order transition amplitudes

\displaystyle\beta^{d(2)}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=\bra{2L}\hat{U}^{(1)}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}^{(1)}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}.

(99)

The transition amplitude
\bra{2L}\hat{U}^{(1)}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}
, in terms of the propagator, is given by

\displaystyle\bra{2L}\hat{U}^{(1)}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}=\frac{m}{V}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}_{f}d^{3}\mathbf{x}_{i}d^{4}x\theta_{2L}(\mathbf{x_{f}})G_{0}(t_{f},\mathbf{x}_{f};t,\mathbf{x})\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{x})}{\hbar}G_{0}(t,\mathbf{x};t_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i})\theta_{1L}(\mathbf{x_{i}}),

(100)

where
G_{0}
represents the Schrödinger propagator for a free particle. To model the scenario, as in
2
, where a freely evolving wavepacket maintains its shape and does not spread, we must impose

\displaystyle\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}_{f}\theta_{2L}(\mathbf{x}_{f})G_{0}(t_{f},\mathbf{x}_{f};t,\mathbf{x})\overset{\mathrm{no\,spread}}{=}\theta_{2L}(\mathbf{x}),\quad\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}_{i}G_{0}(t,\mathbf{x};t_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i})\theta_{1L}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\overset{\mathrm{no\,spread}}{=}\theta_{1L}(\mathbf{x}).

(101)

Doing so, we get

\displaystyle\bra{2L}\hat{U}^{(1)}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}=\frac{m}{V\hbar}\int d^{4}x\theta_{2L}(\mathbf{x})\Phi(\mathbf{x})\theta_{1L}(\mathbf{x})=0,

(102)

since the wavepackets are considered to be non-overlapping at all times such that
\theta_{1L}
and
\theta_{2L}
cannot both be non-zero at the same location.
Thus, we get

\displaystyle\beta^{d(2)}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=0.

(103)

Then, at
3^{\mathrm{rd}}
order, following the same line of reasoning as in Eq. (
97
), we find that also

\displaystyle\beta^{d(3)}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=0.

(104)

The first non-vanishing contribution to
\beta|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}
can therefore only come at
4^{th}
order. Using the considerations above, i.e.,
\bra{2L}\hat{U}^{(0)}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}=\bra{2L}\hat{U}^{(1)}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}=0
, we see that the only non-vanishing contribution at this order
is given by

\displaystyle\beta^{d(4)}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=\bra{2L}\hat{U}^{(2)}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{1L}\hat{U}^{(2)}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}.

(105)

In terms of the propagator, using the no spread constraint (
101
), we get

\displaystyle\beta^{d(4)}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=\left[\frac{m^{2}}{V}\int_{0}^{t}dt_{x}dt_{y}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}d^{3}\mathbf{y}\theta_{2L}(\mathbf{x})\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{x})}{\hbar}G_{0}(t_{x},\mathbf{x};t_{y},\mathbf{y})\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{y})}{\hbar}\theta_{1L}(\mathbf{y})\right]^{2}.

(106)

The expression for the free propagator
G_{0}
is well-known. In the instantaneous time limit, as applied in Ref.
2
, it is given by

\displaystyle G_{0}(t_{x},\mathbf{x};t_{y},\mathbf{y})\rightarrow\;G_{\rm inst}(x-y)=\delta(t_{x}-t_{y})\,K(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}),\quad K(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})=K(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})=-\frac{im}{2\pi\hbar}\,\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|}.

(107)

Using the instantaneous propagator in the expression for the exchange coefficient, we get

\displaystyle\beta^{d(4)}_{LL}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=\left[\frac{im^{3}t}{2\pi V\hbar^{3}}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}d^{3}\mathbf{y}\theta_{2L}(\mathbf{x})\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{x})\Phi(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|}\theta_{1L}(\mathbf{y})\right]^{2}.

(108)

The expression above can be straightforwardly generalized to obtain the other exchange coefficients:

\displaystyle\beta^{d(4)}_{ij}|_{\rm ex}^{\rm 2c}=\left[\frac{im^{3}t}{2\pi V\hbar^{3}}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}d^{3}\mathbf{y}\theta_{2j}(\mathbf{x})\frac{\Phi(\mathbf{x})\Phi(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|}\theta_{1i}(\mathbf{y})\right]^{2}.

(109)

The calculation shows that the exchange coefficients obtained in Ref.
2
, can also be derived by staying within first quantization, for non-relativistic identical particles interacting with an external classical potential.
Further, and more importantly for the present discussion about entanglement, Eq. (
78
) dictates that an initial factorized state remains factorized, apart from the symmetrization imposed by the in-distinguishability of the two particles.

IV.2
Distinguishable particles

We compute one more time the coefficients
\beta_{ij}
, within first quantization, but this time for non-relativistic
distinguishable particles
. The initial state reads

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi(t_i)}\overset{\mathrm{Q1}}{=}\frac{1}{2}\left(\ket{1L}+\ket{1R}\right)\otimes\left(\ket{2L}+\ket{2R}\right)\,,

(110)

The coefficients
\beta_{ij}
, at a later time
t
, are given by:

\displaystyle\beta_{LL}\overset{Q1}{=}{\bra{1L}\otimes\bra{2L}\ket{\Psi(t)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}\,.

(111)

\displaystyle\beta_{RL}\overset{Q1}{=}\bra{1R}\otimes\bra{2L}\ket{\Psi(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}\,.

(112)

\displaystyle\beta_{LR}\overset{Q1}{=}\bra{1L}\otimes\bra{2R}\ket{\Psi(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1L}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}\,.

(113)

\displaystyle\beta_{RR}\overset{Q1}{=}\bra{1R}\otimes\bra{2R}\ket{\Psi(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2R}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1R}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}+\frac{1}{2}\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}\cdot\bra{2R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{2L}\,.

(114)

We see that for distinguishable particles, there are no transition amplitudes of the type
\bra{1i}\hat{U}_{s}\ket{2j}
(
i,j\in\{L,R\}
) involving the two particles, and that the exchange terms are exactly zero

\displaystyle\beta_{ij}|^{\mathrm{2c}}_{\mathrm{ex}}=0.

(115)

The direct terms do not lead to entanglement, and the final state for non-relativistic distinguishable particles remains factorized at
all orders
. Indeed, it can be written as

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi(t)}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\left[\left(U^{1L}_{1L}+U^{1L}_{1R}\right)\ket{1L}+\left(U^{1R}_{1L}+U^{1R}_{1R}\right)\ket{1R}\right]\otimes\left[\left(U^{2L}_{2L}+U^{2L}_{2R}\right)\ket{2L}+\left(U^{2R}_{2L}+U^{2R}_{2R}\right)\ket{2R}\right],

(116)

where
U^{1R}_{1L}:=\bra{1R}\hat{U}_{s}(t)\ket{1L}
.
