COL (Fr. for “neck,” Lat. collum), in physical geography,
generally any marked depression upon a high and rugged water-parting
over which passage is easy from one valley to another.
Such is the Col de Balme between the Trient and Chamounix
valleys, where the great inaccessible wall crowned with aiguilles
running to the massif of Mt. Blanc is broken by a gentle downward
curve with smooth upland slopes, over which a footpath
gives easy passage. The col is usually formed by the head-waters
of a stream eating backward and lowering the water-parting
at the head of its valley. In early military operations, the march
of an army was always over a col, which has at all times considerable
commercial importance in relation to roads in high
mountain regions.
