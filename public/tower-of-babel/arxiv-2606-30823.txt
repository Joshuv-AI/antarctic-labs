Deeply slice knot detection via immersed curves
Rob McConkey, Christopher St. Clair, Tristan Wells, Chen Zhang

Abstract
. On the Kirby list, Akbulut poses the question of whether there exists a homology 3-sphere Y Y , other than S 3 S^{3} , with the following property: Any knot K K , representing 0 ∈ π 1 ​ ( Y ) , 0\in\pi_{1}(Y), which is slice in some contractible 4-manifold X X which Y Y bounds, is already slice in Y × [ 0 , 1 ] Y\times[0,1] . In this paper, we make progress on this question by producing a class of deeply slice knots. We construct these knots by first specifying a pair ( X , K ) (X,K) , where X X is a contractible 4-manifold with integral homology 3-sphere boundary and K K is slice in X X . Then, we show the knot is deeply slice using concordance invariants from Heegaard Floer homology. We employ immersed curve techniques to compute these invariants.



1.
Introduction

Deeply slice
knots first appeared in
[
13
]
as part of the search for pairs of exotic 4-manifolds for the purposes of attacking the Smooth 4-Dimensional Poincaré Conjecture, which claims that every smooth 4-manifold that is homotopy equivalent to the 4-sphere is also diffeomorphic to the standard 4-sphere. In particular the 4D Poincaré conjecture would be false if there was a knot
K
in
S^{3}
, where
S^{3}
is the boundary of some exotic homotopy 4-ball
X
such that
K
is
slice
in
X
but the requisite smoothly embedded disk
\mathbb{D}\hookrightarrow X
whose boundary is
K
cannot be found in a collar neighborhood
\partial X\times I
. Recall that if
X
is a smooth 4-manifold with boundary
Y
, we say a knot
K\subset Y
is
smoothly slice
, or simply
slice in
X
, if there exists a smoothly embedded disk
\mathbb{D}\hookrightarrow X
such that
K=\partial\mathbb{D}\subset\partial X=Y.
Equivalently, a knot is slice if it is
concordant
to the unknot. A schematic of a slice disk as well as a trivial example is shown in Figure
1
. Sliceness is an extremely active topic for study when considering knots in
S^{3}
thought of as the boundary of the standard smooth 4-ball,
B^{4}
. When not specified, when we say “
K
is slice,” we mean that
K\subset S^{3}
is slice in
B^{4}
. Klug and Ruppik introduced a more nuanced property called
deeply slice
in
[
13
]
.

Figure 1.
Left: A half-dimensional schematic of a slice disk for some knot
K
. Right: A slice disk schematic for the unknot, pushed into
B^{4}.

Definition 1.1

(Deeply Slice,
[
13
]
)
.

A knot
K\subset\partial X
is
deeply slice
in
X
if it is slice in
X
but
K
is not slice in
\partial X\times I
.

In other words, none of the slice disks for
K
live in
\partial X\times I
the collar neighborhood of the boundary. In essence, this means that the slice disk is “interesting” because it requires one to dig “deep” into the 4-manifold
X
. If a knot is slice but not deeply slice, we call it
shallow slice
.

While the Smooth 4-Dimensional Poincaré Conjecture motivated this notion, Klug and Ruppik used it to make progress on a question on the Kirby list attributed to Akbulut:

Question 1.2

(Kirby list 1.95,
[
11
]
)
.

Does there exist a homology 3-sphere
Y
, other than
S^{3}
, with the following property: Any knot
K
, representing
0\in\pi_{1}(Y),
which is slice in some contractible 4-manifold
X
which
Y
bounds, is already slice in
Y\times[0,1]
?

The negation of Question
1.2
can be phrased as: Does every homology 3-sphere bound a contractible, smooth 4-manifold which contains a deeply slice knot that is null-homotopic in the boundary? The relevant result to this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
13
]
)
.

Every 2-handlebody
X
contains a null-homotopic deeply slice knot in its boundary.

Instead of starting with candidate 4-manifolds and seeking deeply slice knots, our main theorem starts with a slice knot and constructs a 4-manifold:

Theorem 1.4
.

Associated to a non-trivial slice knot (with some constraints) and any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, there is a contractible, smooth 4-manifold that contains a deeply slice knot which is null-homotopic in its boundary.

See Theorem
5.1
for a technical version, which imposes some further restrictions on the slice knot. The proof of the main theorem has two steps. First, we construct a 4-manifold and a knot with the desired properties, then show that said knot is not slice in a collar neighborhood of the boundary. The novel component to the proof is in the second step, in which we utilize the
\tau_{\alpha}
-invariants developed in
[
9
]
, generalizing the
\tau
-invariant coming from knot Floer homology. Similar to the
\tau
-invariant, these invariants obstruct sliceness. Geometrically,
\tau_{\alpha}(Y,K)
bounds the genus of surfaces with boundary
K
in 4-manifolds with boundary
Y
. In order to compute these, we turn to more recent technology in
bordered Floer theory
. In particular, the
immersed curves
perspective on bordered invariants, developed in
[
6
]
serves as the perfect visual tool to compute
\tau_{\alpha}
. In this paper, we refine a
pairing theorem
from
[
1
]
to fully leverage their
immersed Heegaard Floer homology
.

The magic of obstructing sliceness using
\tau_{\alpha}
is that the invariant depends entirely on the knot and the 3-manifold. Therefore, we have agency in constructing a suitable 4-manifold with the needed topological properties like contractibility. We use this freedom along with a result of Gordon to construct a slice knot in a contractible 4-manifold with boundary an integral homology 3-sphere. This differs from the approach of Klug and Ruppik, who had to operate in a setting with prescribed 4-manifolds (such as 2-handlebodies) and then seek knots with the right sliceness properties.

Ultimately, the 3-manifold we consider is the
1/n
-surgery (described below) on the provided slice knot, and the knot we consider is the Whitehead double of the dual knot to that surgery. Performing
1/n
-surgery guarantees that the resulting 3-manifold is an integral homology 3-sphere. We choose the 4-manifold so that the dual knot is slice in it, and Whitehead doubling preserves sliceness. The tricky component to the proof is ensuring that the immersed curves for the class of slice knots we consider behave appropriately, which boils down to a diagrammatic approach.

2.
Topological Preliminaries

Dehn surgery and satellite knots are topological tools used throughout the paper that we review here.

Dehn surgery
is a method to construct a new 3-manifold from a given 3-manifold by cutting and pasting along a knot. In this paper, we focus on the case for knots in
S^{3}
. Formally, one excises a neighborhood of the given knot
K
, whose closure is homeomorphic to a solid torus, to obtain the knot complement, then reattaches the solid torus to the knot complement along their common boundary, a torus, via some homeomorphism of the torus
h:S^{3}\setminus\nu(K)\to\partial\,\overline{\nu(K)}.
The result is a new, closed 3-manifold
S^{3}_{p/q}(K):=S^{3}\setminus\nu(K)\cup_{h}\partial\,\overline{\nu(K)}
. Note that, since
K
is in
S^{3}
, there is a natural choice of the meridian and longitude of the boundary of the knot complement, which we denote by
(\mu,\lambda)
. The pair
(p,q)
describes the image of the meridian of the solid torus as
p\mu+q\lambda
, which uniquely determines the homeomorphism
h
.

A
satellite operation
is a method of constructing a new knot from two given knots. Let
P
be a knot embedded in the solid torus
S^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
and
K
an arbitrary knot in
S^{3}
. Gluing
S^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
to
S^{3}\setminus\nu(K)
along their common torus boundary yields a new knot in
S^{3}
, where the meridians of the tori are identified to each other and the longitude of
S^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
is identified with the Seifert longitude of
K
. The resulting knot is called the
satellite knot

P(K)
, which is the image of
P
under the gluing. We call
P\subset S^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
the
pattern knot
and
K\subset S^{3}
the
companion knot
. We pay special attention to the Whitehead pattern, denoted
D_{+}
. Here, the Whitehead pattern is the positively clasped unknot wrapped around the
S^{1}
factor of the solid torus in which its embedded. See Figure
2
for the Whitehead double of the right-handed trefoil.

Recall that surgery along
K
requires removing a solid torus in
S^{3}
and gluing it back in. The image of the core of the surgery solid torus under gluing is called the
dual knot
to the surgery along
K
. In the case of
1/n
surgery, we denote the dual knot as
K^{*}_{1/n}\subset S^{3}_{1/n}(K)
. Much information is known about dual knots, as well as various flavors of their knot Floer homology, including surgery formulae in
[
8
]
.

We highlight a feature of the dual knot of an integral surgery here.
Given an integral framed link
(L,\Lambda)
in
S^{3}

. The resulting 4-manifold has

A well known fact is that the Whitehead double of a smoothly slice knot in
S^{3}
is again smoothly slice. Analogously, we achieve the following corollary:

Corollary 2.4
.

The Whitehead double of the dual knot to
1/n
surgery along a slice knot
K\subset S^{3}
,
D_{+}(K^{*}_{1/n})
, is slice in
W
as in Proposition
2.2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, the dual knot is itself slice. Given the dual knot is slice we can find an annulus with one boundary component the dual knot and one boundary the unknot, then we can “Whitehead double” the annulus in the same fashion as the dual knot itself. Since the Whitehead double of the unknot is again the unknot, we have presented an annulus between the Whitehead double of the dual knot and the unknot.
∎

3.
Invariants

The invariants that we use to obstruct shallow sliceness are from Heegaard Floer homology. Since the introduction of Heegaard Floer homology by Ozsváth and Szabó in 2001
[
16
]
, many versions and refinements have arisen to accommodate various settings in low-dimensional topology and knot theory. While the original definition assigns a graded vector space to a closed, oriented, smooth 3-manifold equipped with a
\mathrm{Spin}^{c}
structure, Lipschitz, Ozsváth, and Thurston have since adapted the tool to bordered 3-manifolds in
[
14
]
, calling it Bordered Heegaard Floer homology. The power of bordered Heegaard Floer homology is the so-called pairing theorems, which give a way to compute the Heegaard Floer homology groups of closed 3-manifolds obtained by gluing two bordered 3-manifolds along their common boundary in a specified way. Dehn surgery and satellites are two prominent examples of such gluings that arise naturally in the context of a knot in a closed 3-manifold
Y
.

Here, we briefly recall the definition of the hat version of Heegaard Floer homology for both 3-manifolds and knots. For further details, see
[
16
,
17
]
. Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a closed, smooth 3-manifold
Y
together with a
\mathrm{Spin}^{c}
structure
\mathfrak{s}
and a Heegaard diagram
(\Sigma_{g},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},w)
for
Y
, the
Heegaard Floer chain complex
of
(Y,\mathfrak{s})
is freely generated over
\mathbb{F}
by all intersection points
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
such that
\mathfrak{s}_{w}(\mathbf{x})=\mathfrak{s}
, denoted

\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(Y,\mathfrak{s}):=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}\\
\mathfrak{s}_{w}(\mathbf{x})=\mathfrak{s}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{F},

with boundary map defined on generators by

\partial(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\bigcap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\pi_{2}(x,y)\\
\mu(\phi)=1\end{subarray}}\\
n_{w}(\phi)=0\end{subarray}}\#\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(\phi)\mathbf{y}.

We denote the homology of the Heegaard Floer chain complex by
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y,\mathfrak{s})
. Note that, when the manifold is
S^{3}
, we have
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(S^{3})\cong\mathbb{F}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a knot
K
in
Y
and a doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram
\mathcal{H}=(\Sigma_{g},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},w,z)
compatible with
K,
the
hat knot Floer complex
of the pair
(Y,K)
is freely generated over
\mathbb{F}
by all intersection points
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
, denoted

\widehat{CFK}(Y,K):=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}}\mathbb{F},

with boundary map defined on generators by

\partial(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\bigcap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\pi_{2}(x,y)\\
\mu(\phi)=1\end{subarray}}\\
n_{w}(\phi)=0,n_{z}(\phi)=0\end{subarray}}\#\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(\phi)\mathbf{y}.

In addition to the hat flavor of the knot Floer complex, we employ the
minus
flavor in order to gain extra structure, namely a module structure coming from an action by a formal variable
U.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a knot
K
in
Y
and a doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram
\mathcal{H}=(\Sigma_{g},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},w,z)
compatible with
K,
the
minus knot Floer complex
of the pair
(Y,K)
is freely generated over
\mathbb{F}[U]
by all intersection points
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
, denoted

CFK^{-}(Y,K):=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}}\mathbb{F}[U],

with boundary map defined on generators by

\partial^{-}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\bigcap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\pi_{2}(x,y)\\
\mu(\phi)=1\end{subarray}}\end{subarray}}\#\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(\phi)U^{n_{w}(\phi)}\mathbf{y}.

When the manifold in question is
S^{3}
, we simply write
CFK^{-}(K)
and omit reference to the manifold. For distinction between the complexes
\widehat{CFK}(T_{2,3})
and
CFK^{-}(T_{2,3})
, compare Figures
7
and
8
.

The relative Alexander grading, induced by a knot
K
, is calculated by taking a holomorphic disk connecting a pair of generators
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
, i.e.
\phi\in\pi_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
, using the relation

A(\mathbf{x})-A(\mathbf{y})=n_{z}(\phi)-n_{w}(\phi)
.

One can lift it to an absolute grading using the symmetrized Alexander polynomial. Using the filtration induced by the Alexander grading, Ozsváth and Szabó defined the
\tau
-invariant for a knot
K
in
S^{3}
as follows. Let
\mathcal{F}(K,m)\subset\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(S^{3})
be the subcomplex generated by intersection points whose filtration level is less than or equal to
m
. We obtain an induced sequence of maps

\iota_{K}^{m}:H_{\ast}(\mathcal{F}(K,m))\rightarrow H_{\ast}(\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(S^{3}))=\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(S^{3})\cong\mathbb{F}
,

which for sufficiently large
m
are all isomorphisms. Then

\tau(K)=\mathrm{min}\{m\in\mathbb{Z}|\iota_{K}^{m}\text{ is non-trivial}\}
.

Equivalently, one can use the spectral sequence induced by the Alexander filtration. The first page corresponds to the knot Floer chain complex of the knot, and the infinity page computes the Heegaard Floer homology of the ambient manifold
S^{3}
, whose homology is rank 1. The
\tau
-invariant can be defined as the Alexander grading of the unique cycle on the infinity page.

The

Definition 3.5
.

For a nontrivial class

,

\tau_{\alpha}(Y,K)=\mathrm{min}\{m\in\mathbb{Z}|\alpha\in\mathrm{Im}(\iota_{K}^{m})\}
.

Geometrically,
\tau_{\alpha}(Y,K)
gives a lower bound for the genus of surfaces with boundary
K
in a 4-manifold with boundary.

Proposition 3.6

(
[

]
)
.

Let

. If
\Sigma\subset Y\times I
is a smoothly embedded oriented surface with boundary

, then

Corollary 3.7
.

If
K\subset Y
is slice in
Y\times I
, then
\tau_{\alpha}(Y,K)=0
for all
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y)
.

Proof.

Recall that if
K
is slice, then it bounds a smooth disk in
Y\times I
. If there exists some
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y)
with
\tau_{\alpha}(K)\neq 0
, Proposition
3.6
implies that the genus of any such surface with boundary
K
is nonzero, a contradiction.
∎

This is a generalization of the fact that the
\tau
-invariant obstructs sliceness in
B^{4}
, which means we can use the
\tau_{\alpha}
-invariants to obstruct shallow sliceness.

4.
Bordered Floer Theory

The main tool used to compute the
\tau_{\alpha}
-invariant of the Whitehead double of the dual knot is
bordered Heegaard Floer homology
. The most consolidated resource on the matter is
[
14
]
. Since its advent, bordered Heegaard Floer homology has led to significant results in 3-manifold topology and knot theory. In
[
6
,
5
,
7
]
, Hanselman, Rasmussen, and Watson use the framework of bordered Heegaard Floer homology to construct a very useful description of knot Floer homology using
immersed curves
. Later, Chen and Hanselman developed immersed Heegaard Floer homology in
[
1
]
, which is a powerful tool to compute the knot Floer homology of satellite knots using immersed curves. Similar to standard Heegaard Floer homology, immersed Heegaard Floer homology involves Lagrangian intersection theory on a certain Heegaard diagram. The only difference is that the
\alpha
curves may be immersed. For our results the immersed Heegaard diagrams are genus 1, the
\alpha
curve corresponds to the immersed curve of the companion knot
K
, and the
\beta
curve corresponds to the Heegaard diagram of the Whitehead pattern. In this section, we first introduce these two sets of curves used in immersed Heegaard Floer homology. Then we prove a suitable version of a pairing theorem to compute the knot Floer homology.

4.1.
Immersed Curves

The first piece in an immersed Heegaard diagram is the
immersed multicurve
in the marked boundary of a knot compliment, the
marked torus
. The marked torus is simply the standard torus, modeled as
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, where the
x
-axis is the
preferred longitude
and the
y
-axis is the
preferred meridian
, and a basepoint
z
located at
(1-\epsilon,1-\epsilon)
for small
\epsilon>0
. An
immersed multicurve
is a set of immersed curves in the marked torus away from
z
decorated with
local systems
, which we suppress, since our result does not concern them directly. We often refer to an immersed multicurve as an
immersed curve
.

Immersed curves conveniently package the information of
CFK^{-}(Y,K)
in the form of the
bordered invariant
, sometimes denoted
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y\setminus\nu(K))

The proof of Theorem

4.5.
Computing
\tau_{\alpha}
from Immersed Curves

[
2
]
introduces a nice way to read the Alexander gradings in an immersed Heegaard diagram of genus 1 and determine which generator survives to the infinity page. This allows for easy computation of
\tau(K)
. The calculus introduces to the diagram
A-buoys
attached to the
\boldsymbol{\beta}
curves. These A-buoys are small arrows which record the change in Alexander filtration level between two generators as we isotope away disks only crossing the
z
basepoint. Isotoping disks away is akin to canceling components of the differential on the filtered complex. The difference in Alexander filtration level between two intersection points indicates the lengths of differentials that we cancel, which corresponds to “turning the page” on the spectral sequence converging to the Heegaard Floer homology of the underlying 3-manifold, as described in Section
3
.

So, in practice, we perform isotopies to cancel differentials of filtration length one until we no longer can, recording the filtration change using A-buoys. We can then iterate this process for length two, or three, to see further pages in the spectral sequence. For a knot in
S^{3}
, there will eventually be only one remaining intersection point, whose Alexander grading is
\tau(K)
. In general, for a knot in a 3-manifold other than
S^{3}
, such as a 3-manifold obtained by surgery, more than one intersection point may remain, since the 3-manifold might have
\mathrm{rk}\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y)>1
. Using the Alexander gradings of these remaining generators we can compute
\tau_{\alpha}
.

If there exists a pair of remaining generators, after the above process, with different Alexander grading, then at least one of them is nonzero. Corollary
3.7
implies that obstructs shallow sliceness of the corresponding knot.

Lemma 5.4
.

Given a slice knot with a simplified basis for its knot Floer complex, each generator in the acyclic summand of the complex has exactly one horizontal arrow and exactly one vertical arrow pointing to or away from it.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a generator
U^{k}b
in an acyclic summand with only a horizontal arrow pointing to it or away from it and no other arrows attached to it. Then
b
is a generator of
\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(S^{3})
with no arrows pointing to or away from. Hence, it is a generator of
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(S^{3})
. This is a contradiction since we already restricted
b
to an acyclic summand of
CFK^{-}(Y,K)
.

Suppose instead that
U^{k}b
had a singular vertical arrow pointing to it or away from it and no other arrows attached to it. Using the symmetry of
CFK^{-}(Y,K)
along the
A=\mathrm{gr}_{U}
axis means there is also a corresponding generator with only a horizontal arrow pointing to it. Then we are again in the above case and arrive at the same contradiction.
∎
