Executive Summary
The data center market is poised to explode in the coming decade due to undeniable
drivers such as continued adoption of generative AI, increased data storage needs, and
enterprise integration of AI in numerous industries [1] [2] [3]. Scalable power and
increased computational capacity are at the forefront of considerations for hyperscalers,
the major cloud service providers in this space. Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory
is uniquely poised to help with informed decision making for data center market leaders
during this phase of explosive expansion. National grid modeling expertise and cutting-
edge innovations in computer cooling systems place LLNL in an enviable position for
creating economic impact in the data center industry by leveraging its expertise in these
areas which can help the data center market keep up with growing demand.

The government has identified increasing data centers in the United States as an issue
of importance and continues to back the growth of this largely US based market. A path
forward, including collaborations where industry leverages national lab expertise,
supports a promising future for data centers and Lawrence Livermore National
Laboratory.

Data Center Market Overview: Landscape,
Segmentation, and Strategic Investments
Market Background
The global data center market is experiencing rapid growth, driven by soaring demand
for computing power fueled by generative AI, the expansion of cloud computing, and
increasing data storage needs [1] [3] [2]. Valued at $347.60 billion in 2024, the market is
projected to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 11.2% through 2030 [4].
North America held the largest share of the market in 2024 and is expected to maintain
its dominance in the near term [1] [4], supported by significant investments from major
cloud service providers, known as hyperscalers. Industry leaders such as Meta, Google,
Microsoft, and Amazon Web Services are scaling their infrastructure to meet rising
demands, with an estimated $1.8 trillion in capital expenditures required between 2024
and 2030 [5]. Worldwide an investment of $6.7 trillion in data centers is expected to be
required to keep pace with demand by 2030 [1]. As the backbone of modern digital
infrastructure, the data center market is poised to play a critical role in shaping the future
of compute technology and market opportunities.

Key Players
The data center market is highly competitive with multiple key players driving the market
growth despite the high upfront costs required to enter the industry. Market leaders such
as Meta, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon Web Services dominate the space by offering
large-scale cloud service solutions and are investing heavily in expanding their
infrastructure [4] [5] [6]. These hyperscalers are building new data centers with enhanced
power capacities to meet the growing demand for computing and storage. Other notable
players include Alibaba Cloud in China, which is rapidly expanding its presence in Asia,
and U.S.-based companies Dell and Equinix, Inc. [4]. As the market continues to evolve,
these market leaders are shaping the future of global digital infrastructure through
innovation and strategic investments.

Market Segmentation
The data center market can be segmented into three core segments: network
infrastructure, storage, and servers. Network infrastructure refers to the interconnected
hardware and software systems that enable communication and data transfer within and
beyond the data center environment. Storage encompasses not only the physical media
used to store data but also the architectures that structure and safeguard it. Servers, the
backbone of data centers, are specialized computers that process requests and deliver

the stored data. Together, these segments form the foundation of modern data centers,
supporting a wide range of digital services [7].

Among these segments, network infrastructure plays a particularly critical role in ensuring
seamless communication within data centers. It connects servers, storage devices, cloud
services, and other network equipment, enabling high-performance data transfer and
reliable operations. This segment includes both hardware components, such as switches
and routers, and software solutions. A well-functioning network infrastructure is essential
for ensuring scalability, efficiency, and reliability in data center environments, especially
as demand for cloud services and edge computing continues to grow [4] [8] [9].

Key Challenges
The growth of the data center market is primarily constrained by power limitations and
supply chain challenges. Power, in particular, is emerging as the critical bottleneck for the
development of large-scale data centers [5]. According to Deloitte, data center power
usage is expected to double by 2030, making energy efficiency a top priority for new sites
[4] [6]. The demand for computing power is projected to grow by 16% annually from 2025
to 2028, driven largely by generative AI and other data-heavy technologies. Non-AI
computing needs are also expected to increase as businesses continue to adopt cloud
solutions for their cost efficiency and flexibility [5].

Geographical growth in the data center market is heavily influenced by access to scalable
power markets and favorable regulatory environments, including tax incentives. In the
United States, partnerships with regional transmission organizations (RTOs) such as PJM
Interconnection and Midcontinent Independent System Operator are expected to play a
key role in expanding data center capacity. Leading companies like Meta and Google are
collaborating with power and utility providers to address future energy demands. For
instance, Meta has partnered with Entergy for site planning in Louisiana, while Google is
working with Intersect Power to develop future power infrastructure and with AES to
secure power for data center expansions in Ohio and Texas [6].

Globally, the United States continues to dominate the data center market, but growth is
expected in international markets around the world. Regions outside the US with access
to renewable energy, like Nordic countries, are particularly expected to see growth.
Cheap energy paired with the availability of alternative energy sources provide data
centers with opportunities for co-located energy redundancies to mitigate grid
bottlenecks, power reliability and sustainability concerns [5].

Data center cooling technologies are an area for innovation as the data center market
scales up [10]. Liquid cooling of computing hardware offers greater efficiency than
traditional air cooling and is expected to see substantial growth by 2025 [6]. Cooling
systems currently account for up to 40% of a data center’s power usage. This highlights
the need for more energy-efficient compute cooling solutions [11]. Innovations in liquid
cooling could significantly reduce power consumption and help overcome the critical
bottleneck of energy usage in data center operations.

U.S. Government Investment in Data Center Infrastructure
The U.S. government has demonstrated sustained interest and support for the data
center infrastructure development. In 2022, ARPA-E launched the COOLERCHIPS
program to reduce cooling energy expenditure to less than 5% of an average data center’s
IT load—the total electricity consumed by servers, storage, and network devices [11] [12].
In 2023, the Department of Energy announced $40 million in funding for the program [13].
COOLERCHIPS is now supporting 19 projects focused on improving data center cooling
efficiency [11].

Beyond investment in cooling efficiency, the current administration has prioritized large-
scale investments in data center infrastructure to support emerging technologies like AI.
Prior to taking office, President Trump announced a $20 billion investment by Damac
Properties to build data centers in the United States [14]. In late January 2025, Stargate—
a $100 billion public-private joint venture—was announced with a goal to build 20 data
centers across the U.S., aimed at bolstering domestic AI capabilities [15] [16].

Optimizing Data Centers: Livermore's Grid
Modeling and Cooling Solutions
Key Livermore Technology: Grid Modeling for Future-Proofing Data
Centers
LLNL POC: Jean-Paul Watson

“Builders that optimize site selection can secure prime locations, reduce construction
timelines, and integrate operational feedback early, ensuring faster deployment and
higher data center efficiency.”- McKinsey.com [1]

LLNL’s deep expertise in grid modeling and optimization can guide data center
developers toward sites that deliver power reliability and operational economy. Because
ensuring a safe, dependable power grid is a matter of national security, Livermore has
honed these capabilities for years. Its researchers play leading roles in high-profile efforts
such as the Office of Electricity’s Advanced Grid Modeling program [17] and the multi-lab
Exascale Computing Project devoted to large-scale grid optimization [18].

LLNL’s unique and powerful grid modeling capability is an integrated grid modeling
framework based on mpi-sppy © 2024 for generation and transmission expansion
planning modeling [19]. The grid modeling framework co-optimizes generation,
transmission and storage investments to deliver cost savings while also significantly
enhancing power resilience. Researchers at Livermore have also used stochastic
optimization approaches that leverage linear programing and mixed-integer linear
programing to recommend proactive operational and investment planning for increased
grid resilience in extreme weather [20]. These grid optimization tools offer a sophisticated
yet practical means of projecting future energy resilience. For data centers, adopting such
a model for informed decision making could mean ensuring reliable power supplies that
have the ability to adapt in a changing energy landscape, mitigate risks associated with
extreme weather events, and ultimately support sustainable, cost-effective growth.

High-performance computing capabilities and access to high-fidelity data enable LLNL to
analyze various future scenarios to support well-informed investment decisions on site
selection and energy considerations [19].

Key Livermore Technology: Cooling Systems for Increased Power
Efficiency
LLNL POC: Pratanu Roy
Compact lattice heat exchangers for data center cooling are being explored as an option
to decrease energy and space consumption for cooling systems. Livermore researchers
expect these lattice heat exchangers to reduce energy requirements of cooling systems
by 20% and space needs by 50%. This can translate into meaningful annual savings for
a data center’s bottom line and increase the physical space for server capacity. LLNL
currently hold patents on triply periodic minimal surface structures used to develop these
unique heat exchangers [21] [22]. This technology is being developed to replace currently
used plate heat exchangers used in data centers. Researchers are currently applying for
grants to scale up this technology. The aim is to continue to improve efficiency of liquid
cooling systems. This TPMS heat exchanger has the potential to reduce energy and water
consumption and reduce cooling system footprints, allowing for a decrease in both energy
and space requirements to of data center cooling systems.

Other Selected Cooling System Projects
Livermore continues to push the boundaries of computer system cooling through various
other collaborations and projects on going and completed.

Cutting edge immersion cooling
LLNL is collaborating with Sandia Laboratories to push forward the research in computer
cooling systems. There are current collaborations involving emersion cooling projects.
Emersion cooling involves submerging electrical systems in non-conductive liquids to
more efficiently capture the heat produced [23]. There are estimates that this could cut
energy consumptions for data centers by 70%.

Non liquid cooling systems for high powered electrical devices
Non liquid cooling of high-powered devices is not new to LLNL. LLNL holds patents
around cooling high power devices without the use of liquids. The novel approach
presented in the US patent entitled, “Systems and methods for cooling high power
devices” uses a thermoelectric cooler (TEC) embedded electronics system [24]. The
system uses TEC material as an active cooling element. The configuration of TEC allows
heat to dissipate, cooling the electrical components without the use of fluids [25]. This
novel process is compatible with the thin film TEC material and standard integrated
circuitry. This was originally developed for laser diodes but has broad applications and
poses possibilities for further high energy system cooling.

Next generation network and optophotonics advancement

In 2022, LLNL began collaborating with Sandia National Laboratories, Los Alamos
National Laboratory, and Cornelis Networks to develop the next generation of
interconnect technologies for high performance computing. The collaboration was
focused on accelerating the development of network simulations to overcome InfiniBand
era limits and meet exascale, AI/ML, data center demands [26]. Livermore has also been
involved with the development of more efficient optical cables for meaningful energy
savings relating to data transmission. In 2024, LLNL received continued funding for a
collaboration with Flawless Photonics. This collaboration involves Livermore researchers
simulating and modeling defects in fiber optic manufacturing with the goal of achieving
highly efficient optical cable production [27].

Conclusions
The data center market is poised to boom due to the rapid adoption of AI and continued
adoption of high compute systems across enterprises [1]. There are opportunities for
innovation in this market as capital expenditure investment increases to meet the rising
demand. Two key areas of interest for data center market growth are new site
identification and increased compute power. Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory is
uniquely positioned with expertise to help mitigate risks associated with these areas. Site
location decisions can be informed by LLNL’s advanced grid modeling and compact
cutting-edge cooling systems can help increase compute capacity by increasing available
space for servers in data centers. National grid expertise and optimization codes can be
leveraged to identify new data center locations that mitigate risks associated with energy
resiliency and extreme weather events. Combinations of advanced manufacturing
techniques and top of the line engineering have produced innovations for cooling systems
on the cutting edge of space and energy saving solutions.

Data centers sit at the crossroads of constantly increasing digital demand and energy
security. Operators who pair ambitious, accelerated buildouts with data driven decision
making will set the blueprint for resilient, long-term infrastructure leadership and lasting
energy reliability.
