Abstract
. We show that under a suitable additional hypothesis the restricted Zassenhaus 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -Lie algebra or the rational Magnus Lie algebra of a free amalgamated product is the free amalgamated product of the corresponding Lie algebras of the factors. This generalises a Theorem of A.I. Lichtman, who proved the analoguous statement for free products. Our conditions include the case when the amalgamated product is a retract in both factors. As a by-product, we show that a free product of residually torsion free nilpotent groups amalgamated along retracts is also residually torsion free nilpotent and obtain also some results on cohomological completeness. In the final sections we apply our main results to two recently raised open questions.

1.
Introduction

As Efim Zelmanov’s solution of the restricted Burnside problem has shown (cf.
[
29
]
),
Lie theoretic methods are undoubtedly one of the most powerful tools in group theory. Nevertheless, few results are known (cf.
[
12
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
22
]
) which can be used for an explicit computation of the (rational) Magnus Lie algebra or of the restricted Zassenhaus
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-Lie algebra of a (pro
p
-)group
G
. In this context, we call
the
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie ring
\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}(G)=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma_{k}(G)/\gamma_{k+1}(G)
the
Magnus
Lie ring of
G
, where
\gamma_{k}(G)
is the
k
-th term of the descending central series of
G
. Moreover, we use
\operatorname{gr}^{[0]}_{\bullet}(G)=\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
to indicate the
rational Magnus Lie algebra
of
G
.

For a prime number
p
, we denote by
\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G)=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D_{k}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})/D_{k+1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
the
restricted Zassenhaus

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-Lie algebra of
G
(cf.
[
31
]
). Here, for a commutative ring
F
,
D_{k}(G,F)
is the
k
-th dimension subgroup of the
F
-group algebra
FG
, i.e., the group consisting of those elements
g\in G
for which
1-g
lies in
(\omega_{G}^{F})^{k}
, where
\omega_{G}^{F}
denotes the augmentation ideal of
FG
. It turns out that the rational Magnus Lie algebra can also be obtained from the rational dimension subgroups
D_{k}(G,\mathbb{Q})
making our notations consistent. Consequently, we are able to deal with these two types of algebras at the same time.

Dimension subgroups with integer coefficients are rather mysterious; however, thanks to results by Jennings, Lazard and Quillen (see Section
4
) when one works with coefficients in a field, they can be described in terms of the group only.

Let
p
be either zero or a prime. One of the few available results that allows an explicit computation of the Lie algebra
\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G)
in some cases is a theorem by
Lichtman
[
18
]
who proved that the functor
\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(-)
respects free products.

On the other hand Falk and Randell (cf.
[
12
]
) showed that even the Magnus Lie ring functor
\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}(-)
behaves well for
almost direct products
which are semidirect products
Q\ltimes N
where the action of the group
Q
is trivial on the abelianization of
N
. It is an easy observation that their result yields the same for
\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(-)
(Section
5
) with a
p
-local version of almost direct products. Our first main result can be seen as a generalization of both Lichtman’s theorem (to free products with amalgamation) and Falk-Randell’s (to certain HNN extensions). More explicitly, we show

Theorem A
.

Let
p
be either zero or a prime.

(i)

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
be groups with a common subgroup
H
which is strictly
p
-embedded in both. Then

(1.1)

\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G)=\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G_{1})\ast_{\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(H)}\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G_{2})

where
G=G_{1}\ast_{H}G_{2}
.

(ii)

Let
H
be a strictly
p
-embedded subgroup of
G_{1}
and let
\varphi\colon H\to H
be an almost
p
-trivial automorphism. Let
G=G_{1}\ast_{H,t}
be the associated HNN-extension. Then

(1.2)

\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G)=\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G_{1})\ast_{\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(H),t}

Here, we say that the subgroup
H
of
G
is
strictly
p
-embedded
if the inclusion map
\iota\colon H\to G
induces an injective map
\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[p]}(\iota)\colon\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(H)\to\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G)
(see Section
4
). This is the case if
H
is a retract.
Similarly, an automorphism
\varphi\colon H\to H
is said to be

p
-almost trivial, if
\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(\varphi)\colon\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(H)\to\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(H)
is the identity on
\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(H)
.
The conclusion of Theorem A appeared in the PhD thesis of the first named author in the context of pro-
p
groups
[
17
]
.
Sjogren showed in
[
27
]
that the quotients
D_{i}(G,\mathbb{Z})/\gamma_{i}(G)
are torsion groups.
So if the groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are finitely generated and
\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}(G_{1})
and
\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}(G_{2})
are torsion free, then the dimension subgroups coincide with the terms of the descending central series. Hence in this case one may use Theorem
A
to deduce a similar result for the Magnus Lie ring
\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}(G)
.

The proof of Theorem
A
has the following consequence

Theorem B
.

Let
p
be either zero or a prime and let
G
be either a free amalgamated product or an almost
p
-trivial

HNN
-extension such that the edge groups are strictly
p
-embedded in the vertex groups. Then the vertex groups are strictly
p
-embedded in
G
. Moreover, if the vertex groups are residually
[p]
, then
G
is residually
[p]
.

The notion of being

[p]
is defined in Section
4
and is equivalent to being residually a nilpotent
p
-group with bounded torsion if

is a prime, or being residually torsion free nilpotent, if

. In particular, this theorem implies that free amalgamated products along retracts of residually torsion free nilpotent groups are also residually torsion free nilpotent. In Section
6
we give a direct group theoretical proof of this fact, which might be known to experts, but we have not been able to find any proof in the literature.

Amalgamating along almost
p
-retracts also behaves well with respect to cohomological properties. Recall that a group

is said to be

-complete
(cf.

). In

2

, Aschenbrebber and Friedl give conditions on a graph of groups

that imply that the fundamental group

is cohomologically

-complete. See also
[
15
]
, where the case of

graphs of groups is considered (these are graphs of groups where vertex and edge groups are free and edge groups are free factors of vertex groups). Here we use results in
[

]
in order to show:

Theorem C
.

Let

be a prime and let

be either a free amalgamated product or an almost
p
-trivial

-extension of finitely
generated groups such that the vertex groups are residually
p
-finite and cohomologically

-retracts in the vertex groups. Then

-complete.

In the last two sections of the paper we consider some applications of our main results.
First, we consider twisted RAAGs and use Theorem

to describe their 2-Zassenhaus Lie algebras providing an affirmative answer to

Secondly, we apply a pro-

Amalgamated free products of algebras

From now on we use the notion

as a short form for

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and let
A_{1}
,
A_{2}

\mathbb{F}
-algebras with morphisms

\displaystyle A_{1}\ast_{C}A_{2}\coloneqq T(A_{1},A_{2})/I

\displaystyle\left\{\,a\otimes b-ab,\hskip 5.0pt\lambda_{1}(c)-\lambda_{2}(c)\mid a,b\in A_{i},\hskip 5.0pti=1,2,\hskip 5.0ptc\in C\,\right\}.

Multiplication is defined as the
\mathbb{F}
-bilinear map given by juxtaposition of terms.

Let
\psi_{i}\colon A_{i}\hookrightarrow T(A_{1},A_{2})\twoheadrightarrow A_{1}\ast_{C}A_{2}
be the natural morphisms for

. Then - by definition - there is a commuting diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}\ast_{C}A_{2},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{2}}

In general, the condition that

\lambda_{2}

and
\psi_{2}
are.
Nevertheless, this is true whenever both

and
A_{2}
are free right
C
-modules as one is able to establish a normal form-type result which will be described in the sequel. Let

,
A_{1}
and

\mathbb{F}
-algebras, and let

\mathbb{F}
-algebras. For simplicity we identify the element

and denote it simply by

. We also assume that

-modules

\displaystyle(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathcal{B}_{i_{1}}^{\sharp}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{B}_{i_{n}}^{\sharp}

The homomorphism of right

-modules

.

Proof.

As
\Psi

By hypothesis, for

,

, there exist unique elements

\displaystyle y\cdot a

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{(i)}(y,a)+\sum_{x\in\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\sharp}}x\cdot c_{x}^{(i)}(y,a).

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}x\cdot c_{x}^{(i)}(y,ab).

Definition 3.1

Let

3.1.
Augmented algebras

Augmented algebras are an important particular case of filtered algebras.

[

4.
Descending central series

If
G
is a group and
R
is a commutative unital ring, the group ring
RG
is augmented with augmentation map
\varepsilon\colon RG\to R
induced by the map
g\mapsto 1
,
g\in G
. Therefore,
RG
has a natural filtration by powers of the augmentation ideal

\omega^{R}_{G}=\ker\epsilon.

A natural question is whether one can relate this filtration with a filtration of the defining group. It is easy to see that
\omega^{R}_{G}
is precisely the ideal of
RG
generated by the elements
g-1
where
g\in G
and this leads to the definition of the
dimension subgroups

D_{n}(G,R)=\{g\in G\mid g-1\in(\omega^{R}_{G})^{n}\}.

One can check that these are indeed subgroups of
G
and clearly

G=D_{1}(G,R)\geq D_{2}(G,R)\geq\ldots\geq D_{n}(G,R)\geq D_{n+1}(G,R)\geq\ldots

In the case when we work with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
, these subgroups are extremely difficult to determine in group theoretical terms. However, working with coefficients in a field
\mathbb{F}
the situation changes and there are nice description available in terms of descending central series.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group. The
descending central series
of
G
is the series given by
\gamma_{1}(G)=G
and

\gamma_{n}(G)=[G,\gamma_{n-1}(G)].

If
p
is either zero or a prime, we also define following
[
3
]
,
p
-local versions by

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{[0]}(G)

\displaystyle=\{x\in G\mid x^{m}\in\gamma_{n}(G)\text{ for some }m>0\},

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)

\displaystyle=\prod_{jp^{s}\geq n}\gamma_{j}(G)^{p^{s}}\text{ if $p$ is a prime}.

In the prime case, the series
\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)
is called the

p
-Zassenhaus central series
, and there is another well known
p
-local central series, the
p
-Stallings series
, defined by

\gamma_{n,p}(G)=\prod_{j+s\geq n}\gamma_{j}(G)^{p^{s}}\text{ if $p$ is a prime}.

Then one has

Theorem 4.2

(Quillen-Jennings-Lazard)
.

Let
p
be zero or a prime and
\mathbb{F}
a field of characteristic
p
. Then

\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)=D_{n}(G,\mathbb{F}).

We will say that the group
G
is
residually
[p]
if

\cap_{n}\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)=1.

If
p
is a prime, this is equivalent to the following:
for every element
1\neq g\in G
there is a normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
such that
g\not\in N
and
G/N
is a nilpotent
p
-group of bounded exponent. If moreover
G
is finitely generated, this means that
G
is residually
p
-finite. If
p=0
, one verifies easily that all the quotient groups
G/\gamma_{n}^{[0]}(G)
are torsion free nilpotent group and deduce that being residually
[0]
is the same as saying that for each element
1\neq g\in G
there is a normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
with
G/N
torsion free nilpotent such that
g\not\in N
.
These groups are usually called
residually torsion free nilpotent
, but we keep the name
residually
[0]
to be able to treat at the same time the cases
p=0
and
p
a prime.
Being residually
[p]
is the group theoretical analog of separability for the group algebra because of the following consequence of a Theorem by Lichtman (cf.
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
):

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
p
be either zero or a prime and
\mathbb{F}
a field of characteristic
p
. A group
G
is residually
[p]
if, and only if,
\mathbb{F}G
is separated with respect to the augmentation filtration. Moreover, in this case

is residually
p
-finite provided

is finite.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathbb{F}G
is separated with respect to the augmentation filtration, i.e.,
\cap_{n\geq 0}\omega_{G}^{n}=0
. Let
g\in\cap_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)
. As
\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)=D_{n}(G,\mathbb{F})
, then
g-1\in\cap_{n\geq 0}\omega_{G}^{n}=0
.

For the converse, let
\omega_{G}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the augmentation ideal of
\mathbb{Z}G
. We assume that
G
is residually
[p]
, this means that
G
is either residually torsion free nilpotent (if
p=0
) or that for the prime

,
G
is residually a nilpotent
p
-group of bounded exponent. Then Theorem 2.1 of
[

]
implies that
\cap_{n\geq 0}(\omega_{G}^{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}=0
and tensoring with the field

we obtain the result.
∎

The series
\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)
is an
N
-series in Lazards’ terminology, meaning that

[\gamma^{[p]}_{n}(G),\gamma^{[p]}_{m}(G)]\leq\gamma^{[p]}_{n+m}(G).

This has the consequence that one can use the group commutator to define a Lie algebra

\mathbb{F}\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G)=\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\gamma_{i}^{[0]}(G)/\gamma_{i+1}^{[0]}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}.

To simplify notation, we will just write
\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G)
if the coefficient field is either
\mathbb{Q}
or the field of
p
elements

.
In the case when

is a prime, the algebra
\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G)
has additional structure: it is a

-restricted Lie algebra, meaning that there is a map

such that for elements

and

[a^{[p]},b]=[a,\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\ldots}\limits^{p}},a,b]
,

(ii)

(ta)^{[p]}=t^{p}a^{[p]}
,

(iii)

(a+b)^{[p]}=a^{[p]}+b^{[p]}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(a,b)
where

is the coefficient of

in
[ta+b,\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\ldots}\limits^{p-1}},ta+b,a]
.

This algebra was first introduced by Zassenhaus in 1939 so it is often called the

p
-Zassenhaus Lie algebra
of the group
G
.

The
p
-Stallings central series is also an

-series and one can define a Lie algebra using the succesive quotients as we did for the

-Zassenhaus series. However, that Lie algebra has not necessarily the structure of a

-restricted Lie algebra in general.
Later we will make use of the following relation between the

-Zassenhaus and the
p
-Stallings central series:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
p
be a prime. Then

\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)=\prod_{jp^{s}\geq n}\gamma_{j,p}(G)^{p^{s}}.

Proof.

Taking into account that for
i\leq j
one has
\gamma_{r}(G)^{p^{i}}\geq\gamma_{r}(G)^{p^{j}}
, we conclude that
by definition of
\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)
we have

(4.1)

\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)=\prod_{1\leq r\leq n}\gamma_{r}(G)^{p^{i_{r}}}

where
i_{r}=\lceil\log_{p}{\big(\frac{n}{r}\big)}\rceil
, i.e.,
i_{r}
is the smallest integer such that

p^{i_{r}}\geq\frac{n}{r}.

Similarly, if we show

(4.2)

\gamma_{n}^{[p]}(G)=\prod_{1\leq r\leq n}\gamma_{r,p}(G)^{p^{i_{r}}}

we will get the desired result. In the left hand side of (
4.2
), the factor
\gamma_{r}(G)

appears with exponent
p^{i_{r}}
and in the right hand side one has

\prod_{r\leq j\leq n}(\gamma_{r}(G)^{n-j})^{p^{i_{n-j+r}}}.

Hence in order to prove (
4.2
) one needs to verify that for
1\leq r\leq j\leq n
one has
n-j+i_{n-j+r}\geq i_{r}
, or equivalently, that

(4.3)

p^{n-j+i_{n-j+r}}\geq\frac{n}{r}.

As

p^{n-j+i_{n-j+r}}=p^{n-j}p^{i_{n-j+r}}\geq 2^{n-j}\frac{n}{n-j+r}

it is enough to check that for
1\leq r\leq j\leq n

2^{n-j}r\geq n-j+r

and this follows easily by induction.
∎

Quillen
[

, Theorem 1]
has shown that there is an isomorphism
which we will call the

(4.4)

Q_{G}:\mathfrak{u}(\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G))\to\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}\mathbb{F}G.

Here

\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}\mathbb{F}G=\oplus\omega_{G}^{i}/\omega^{i+1}_{G},

is the graded algebra obtained from the augmentation filtration of

, and

.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
p
be either zero or a prime and let

The most obvious examples of strictly embedded subgroups are retracts: recall that
H

and there is an epimorphism

that restricts to the identity in

of

It is well known (see for example

Proof.

6.
An alternative proof of residual torsion free nilpotence of free products amalgamated along retracts

Considering the case when
p=0
, Theorem
B
implies that the free amalgamated product of residually torsion free nilpotent groups along a strictly embedded common subgroup is also residually torsion free nilpotent. In particular, this is the case when the amalgamated subgroup is a retract. In this section we provide a purely group theoretical direct proof of this fact. In the literature there are similar results for amalgamations along retracts, mainly for residual finiteness or in the prime case
[
8
]
,
[
26
]
. See also Theorem A in
[
9
]
where residual torsion free nilpotence is considered assuming that the groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are torsion free nilpotent and the amalgamated subgroup
H
is abelian.

A classical result by Philip Hall says that if
G
is a group with a normal subgroup
K
such that
K
and
G/[K,K]
are nilpotent, then so is
G
. This has been extended by Baumslag who shows

Lemma 6.1
.

[
4
, Proposition 1]

Let
G
be a group with a residually torsion free nilpotent normal subgroup
K
. Assume that the quotient
G/K
is torsion free nilpotent and that
G/[K,K]
is nilpotent. Then
G
is residually torsion free nilpotent.

Using this we obtain

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G_{1}
,
{G_{2}}
be torsion free nilpotent groups with a common retract
H
. Then their free amalgamated product
G=G_{1}\ast_{H}{G_{2}}
is residually torsion free nilpotent.

Proof.

As
H
is a retract in both
G_{1}
and
{G_{2}}
the kernels of the projections
G_{1}\to H
and
{G_{2}}\to H

are normal subgroups such that
{G_{1}}=H\ltimes K_{1}
and
{G_{2}}=H\ltimes K_{2}
. As
{G_{1}}
and
{G_{2}}
are torsion free nilpotent, so are
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
so
the group
K=K_{1}\ast K_{2}
is residually torsion free nilpotent by
[
21
]
. Moreover, it is a standard fact that
G
also decomposes as a semidirect product
G=H\ltimes K
(this can be easily seen by checking their presentations). Moreover
H=G/K
is torsion free nilpotent. We claim that the quotient
G/[K,K]
is nilpotent. To see it, note that

K/[K,K]=K_{1}/[K_{1},K_{1}]\times K_{2}/[K_{2},K_{2}]

and, as
{G_{1}}
and
{G_{2}}
are nilpotent, so are
{G_{1}}/[K_{i},K_{i}]=H\ltimes K_{i}/[K_{i},K_{i}]
for
i=1,2
. The direct product of these two groups is also nilpotent and

G/[K,K]=H\ltimes K/[K,K]
embeds into

{G_{1}}/[K_{1},K_{1}]\times{G_{2}}/[K_{2},K_{2}]=(H\times H)\ltimes K/[K,K]

via the diagonal map in the first component so it is also nilpotent. Hence Lemma
6.1
yields the claim. ∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
{G_{1}}
,
{G_{2}}
be residually torsion free nilpotent groups with a common retract
H
. Then their free product with amalgamation
G={G_{1}}\ast_{H}{G_{2}}
is residually torsion free nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
1\neq g\in G
. We need to find a normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
that does not contain
g
so that
G/N
is torsion free nilpotent. As in Lemma
6.2
, we denote by
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
the kernels of the projections
{G_{1}}\to H
and
{G_{2}}\to{G_{2}}
so
{G_{1}}=H\ltimes K_{1}
and
{G_{2}}=H\ltimes K_{2}
and

G=H\ltimes K

with
K=K_{1}\ast K_{2}
. If
g\not\in K
, then
1\neq gK\in G/K=H
and as
H
is residually torsion free nilpotent we can find
N\trianglelefteq G
with
G/N
torsion free nilpotent and
g\not\in N
. So we may assume
g\in K=K_{1}\ast K_{2}
. Therefore we can write

as a product

g=g_{0}g_{1}\dots g_{t}
where each
g_{i}
is alternatively non trivial element of
K_{1}
or
K_{2}
.

Now, we consider the torsion free central series
\gamma_{n}^{[0]}
of the groups
{G_{1}}
and
{G_{2}}
. As both are residually torsion free nilpotent, one has

1=\cap\gamma_{n}^{[0]}({G_{1}})=\cap\gamma_{n}^{[0]}({G_{2}}).

Therefore, we may choose an
n
big enough so that for all the elements
g_{i}
in the above decomposition of

, one has:

•

if
g_{i}\in K_{1}
, then
g_{i}\not\in\gamma_{n}^{[0]}({G_{1}})
,

•

if
g_{i}\in K_{2}
, then
g_{i}\not\in\gamma_{n}^{[0]}({G_{2}})
.

Moreover,
\overline{{G_{1}}}={G_{1}}/\gamma^{[0]}_{n}({G_{1}})
and
\overline{{G_{2}}}={G_{2}}/\gamma^{[0]}_{n}({G_{2}})
are both torsion free nilpotent.
The hypothesis that

is a retract in both

and

implies

\gamma^{[0]}_{n}(H)=H\cap\gamma^{[0]}_{n}({G_{1}})=H\cap\gamma^{[0]}_{n}({G_{2}})

so
\overline{H}=H/\gamma^{[0]}_{n}(H)

is a retract in both
\overline{{G_{1}}}
and
\overline{{G_{2}}}
. Consider the group
\overline{G}=\overline{{G_{1}}}\ast_{\overline{H}}\overline{{G_{2}}}
. By Lemma

it is residually torsion free nilpotent. Moreover, the projections
{G_{1}}\to\overline{{G_{1}}}
and

restrict to the projection

and there is an induced epimorphism

Then

and the choice of

implies that for each

lies either in
\tau(K_{1})
or in
\tau(K_{2})
but it is non-trivial. Therefore,

so for the normal form theorem for free amalgamated products, we get

. As

is residually torsion free nilpotent, this means that we can find

such that

and

is torsion free nilpotent. Therefore, it is enough to consider the preimage

of

in

.
∎

Lemma 7.4

Let

8.
An example: The 2-Zassenhauss algebra of twisted RAAGs

A
mixed graph

\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},\mathscr{A})
is a simplicial graph where some of the edges are oriented and some are not. We say that an unoriented edge is an
edge
and denote the set of all edges by
\mathscr{E}
, and that an oriented edge is an
arrow
and denote the set of all arrows by
\mathscr{A}
.
We may consider the set of edges as a subset
of
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathscr{V})
, the set of all subsets of
\mathscr{V}

of cardinality
2
. While the set of arrows may be considered as a subset of
\mathscr{V}\times\mathscr{V}
.
In order to avoid complications, we also assume that for any arrow
\mathbf{a}=(u,v)
, one has

\{u,v\}\not\in\mathscr{E}
and
u\not=v
.

A mixed graph
\Gamma
without arrows will be called a naïve graph. Every mixed graph

\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},\mathscr{A})
defines a naïve graph

\ddot{\Gamma}=(\mathscr{V},\ddot{\mathscr{E}})
,
where

\ddot{\mathscr{E}}=\mathscr{E}\sqcup\{\,\{u,v\}\mid(u,v)\in\mathscr{A}\,\}
.
Associated to a mixed graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},\mathscr{A})
one may define the

twisted right-angled Artin group

G_{\Gamma}
by

(8.1)

G_{\Gamma}=\Bigg\langle\ \mathscr{V}\ \Bigg|\ \begin{aligned} &=1\qquad\text{if
$\{u,v\}\in\mathscr{E}$,}\\
u^{v}&=u^{-1}\quad\text{if $(u,v)\in\mathscr{A}$.}\\
\end{aligned}\Bigg\rangle

The twisted right-angled Artin group
G_{\Gamma}
is torsion free if, and only if, there is no oriented cycle embedded in a clique
[
13
, Theorem 5.16]
and it is left orderable if, and only if,
\Gamma
does not contain oriented cycles
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Following
[
6
]
we define the following
restricted
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-Lie algebra associated to a mixed graph
\Gamma
by

L_{\Gamma}=\Bigg\langle\ \mathscr{V}\ \Bigg|\ \begin{aligned} &=0\qquad\text{if $\{u,v\}\in\mathscr{E}$,}\\
[v,u]&=u^{[2]}\quad\text{if $(u,v)\in\mathscr{A}$.}\\
\end{aligned}\Bigg\rangle

A first observation is that there is an epimorphism

(8.2)

\rho:L_{\Gamma}\to\operatorname{gr}^{[2]}_{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma})

induced by the obvious identification between generators of
G_{\Gamma}
and of
L_{\Gamma}
.
To see it, note that if
u,v
are linked by an edge in
\Gamma
, then
[u,v]=1
so the corresponding elements
\overline{u},\overline{v}
in
\operatorname{gr}^{[p]}_{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma})
satisfy
[\overline{u},\overline{v}]=0
. And if there is an arrow
(u,v)
, then
u^{v}=u^{-1}
so
[u,v]=u^{-2}
and

[u,v]u^{2}=1.

As
[u,v],u^{2}\in\gamma_{2}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma})
, this means that in the 2-Zassenhaus algebra one has

[\overline{v},\overline{u}]=\overline{u}^{[2]}.

In
[
6
, Question 4.3]
, Blumer asks whether
L_{\Gamma}
is the 2-Zassenhaus Lie algebra of
G_{\Gamma}
. In this section we answer this question affirmatively.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
G_{\Gamma}
be a twisted right-angled Artin group. Then
for any induced subgraph
\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
the group
G_{\Delta}
is strictly 2-embedded in
G_{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n=|\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)|
. Taking into account that being strictly 2-embedded is a transitive relation, it suffices to prove the claim for induced subgraphs
\Delta
satisfying
\mathscr{V}(\Delta)=\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)\setminus\{w\}
for some
w\in\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)
. Assume first that there is some
u\in\mathscr{V}(\Delta)
such that
(w,u),(u,w)\not\in\mathscr{A}
and
\{w,u\}\not\in\mathscr{E}
, i.e., there is no arrow nor edge between
u
and
w
.
Let
\Delta_{1}\subseteq\Delta
resp.
\Gamma_{1}\subseteq\Gamma
be the subgraphs induced by
\mathscr{V}(\Delta)\setminus\{u\}
resp.
\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)\setminus\{u\}
. The induction hypothesis implies that the subgroups
G_{\Delta_{1}}\leq G_{\Delta}
and
G_{\Delta_{1}}\leq G_{\Gamma_{1}}
are strictly 2-embedded. Moreover, one has a decomposition as a free amalgamated product

(8.3)

G_{\Gamma}=G_{\Delta}\ast_{G_{\Delta_{1}}}G_{\Gamma_{1}}.

So Theorem
B
implies that

G_{\Delta}
is strictly 2-embedded in
G_{\Gamma}
.

The case which remains to be considered is when for any
u\in\mathscr{V}(\Delta)
either
(w,u)
or
(u,w)
lie in
\mathscr{A}
, or
\{u,w\}\in\mathscr{E}
.
Let
T=\langle w\rangle
,
N=\langle w^{2}\rangle
and
H=G_{\Delta}
. Clearly,
T\cap H=1
. For any
u\in\mathscr{V}(\Delta)
we must have one of the following possibilities

(8.4)

•

either
\{u,w\}\in\mathscr{E}
so
[w,u]=1
and thus
[w^{2},u]=1
,

•

or
\mathbf{a}=(u,w)\in\mathscr{A}
, so
u^{w}=u^{-1}
and thus

[w^{2},u]=1
,

•

or
\mathbf{a}=(w,u)\in\mathscr{A}
, so
w^{u}=w^{-1}
and thus
(w^{2})^{u}=w^{-2}
.

so we deduce that
N\trianglelefteq G_{\Gamma}
and also that the semidirect product
H_{1}=N\rtimes H
is an almost 2-direct product. But (
8.4
) also implies that in the quotient
G_{\Gamma}/N
, the element
x=wN
normalizes
H_{1}/N
so
H_{1}/N\triangleleft G_{\Gamma}/N
and in fact the semidirect product
G_{\Gamma}/N=T/N\ltimes H_{1}/N
is also an almost 2-direct product. This means that the hypothesis of Lemma
5.8
are satisfied for
p=2
thus
H=G_{\Delta}
is strictly 2-embedded in
G_{\Gamma}
.
∎

As a by-product we also obtain:

Proposition 8.2
.

Twisted right angled Artin groups are residually 2-finite.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph and consider the group
G_{\Gamma}
, we proceed by induction on
|\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)|
. Assume first that the naïve graph
\ddot{\Gamma}
associated to
\Gamma
is complete, then using (
8.4
) above one sees that the subgroup

K=\langle\,w^{2}\mid w\in\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)\,\rangle

is normal in
G_{\Gamma}
and moreover that
G_{\Gamma}/K
is 2-elementary abelian, in particular a finite 2-group. We also deduce that
K
is free abelian, so
G_{\Gamma}
is an extension of a free abelian group by a finite 2-group so it is residually 2-finite.

If
\ddot{\Gamma}
is not complete, choose
u,w\in\Gamma
so that
(w,u),(u,w)\not\in\mathscr{A}

and
\{u,w\}\not\in\mathscr{E}
. Then we have a decomposition (
8.3
) as in the proof of Proposition
8.1
, and the result follows by induction using
Theorem
B
and the fact that the group
G_{\Delta_{1}}
is strictly 2-embedded in
G_{\Delta}
and
G_{\Gamma_{1}}
by
Proposition
8.1
.
∎

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
G_{\Gamma}
be a twisted right angled Artin group. Then

\operatorname{gr}^{[2]}_{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma})=L_{\Gamma}

Proof.

Again, we proceed by induction on
n=|\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)|
. Assume first that there are

u,w\in\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)
such that
(w,u),(u,w)\not\in\mathscr{A}
and
\{u,w\}\not\in\mathscr{E}
. Again, we have a decomposition (
8.3
) as in the proof of Proposition
8.1
, where by Proposition
8.1
the group
G_{\Delta_{1}}
is strictly 2-embedded in
G_{\Delta}
and
G_{\Gamma_{1}}
. So
we may apply Theorem
A
and get

\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma})=\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Delta})\ast_{\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Delta_{1}})}\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma_{1}}).

As by induction

,
\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Delta_{1}})=L_{\Delta_{1}}
and
\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma_{1}})=L_{\Gamma_{1}}

this yields the desired result.

So we are left with the case when for each
w,u\in\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)
, either
(w,u)
or
(u,w)

is contained in
\mathscr{A}
or
\{u,w\}\in\mathscr{E}
. Fix some
w\in\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)
and consider the groups
N,T,H
as at the end of the proof of Proposition
8.1
. Let also
x=wN
. As observed above, we have an epimorphism

L_{\Gamma}\to\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma})

Let
I
be the ideal of
L_{\Gamma}
generated by
w^{[2]}
.

For each
u\in\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)
in the 2-restricted algebra
L_{\Gamma}
one must have one of the following possibilities

(8.5)

•

either
\{u,w\}\in\mathscr{E}
so
[w,u]=0
thus
[w^{[2]},u]=0
,

•

or
(u,w)\in\mathscr{A}
so
[w,u]=u^{[2]}
thus
[w^{[2]},u]=[w,w,u]=[w,u^{[2]}]=[u,u,w]=[u,u^{[2]}]=0
,

•

or
(w,u)\in\mathscr{A}
so
[u,w]=w^{[2]}
thus
[w^{[2]},u]=[w,w,u]=[w,w^{[2]}]=0
.

This means that
I
is just the 2-restricted Lie algebra generated by
w^{[2]}
.

Moreover, as
G_{\Gamma}/N
is an almost 2-direct product, one may apply Lemma
5.2
to compute a presentation of

\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma}/N)=\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(T/N)_{d}\ltimes\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(HN/N)

where the derivation

is given by
d(u)=0
for
u\in\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)
such that either

\{u,w\}\in\mathscr{E}
or
(w,u)\in\mathscr{A}
and

if
(u,w)\in\mathscr{A}
. Moreover, by induction one has

\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(HN/N)=\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(H)=\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Delta})=L_{\Delta}

where
\Delta
is the graph induced by
\mathscr{V}(\Gamma)\setminus\{w\}
.
This means that

\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma}/N)=L_{\Gamma}/I

and from this one deduces that there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\Gamma}/I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{gr}_{\bullet}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma}/N)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{2}}

where
\tau_{2}
is an isomorphism and

Z_{\bullet}=\oplus_{i\geq 1}\gamma_{i}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma})\cap N/\gamma_{i+1}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma})\cap N.

But as the subgroup
T=\langle w\rangle
is strictly 2-embedded in
G_{\Gamma}
, we have that for any

,

\gamma_{i}^{[2]}(G_{\Gamma})\cap T=\gamma_{i}^{[2]}(T)

so

which means that

can be computed explicitly. The same can be done with

and one deduces that the map

in the diagram is an isomorphism (for details, see the proof of Proposition 3.5 in
[

]
where a similar argument is used). Therefore,
\tau
is also an isomorphism which yields the claim.
∎

9.
Twisted right-angled Artin pro-

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote the

p
-adic integers
and
v_{p}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
its discrete valuation. Then

coincides with the
Sylow pro-
p
subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

In
[
7
, (1.1)]
the authors defined for a prime number

, a continuous group homomorphism

and a finite mixed
graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},\mathscr{A})
a finitely generated pro-
p

and called it an

p
group
. In this section we generalise their definition slightly using a modification of (
8.1
) starting from an
arrow
p
-labelled finite mixed graph
. In this context we call a finite mixed graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},\mathscr{A})
together with a function

(9.1)

\ell\colon\mathscr{A}\longrightarrow 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}

to be an
arrow
p
-labelled finite mixed graph
. For an arrow
p
-labelled finite mixed graph
(\Gamma,\ell)
we define

(9.2)

G_{\Gamma,\ell}=\Bigg\langle\ \mathscr{V}\ \Bigg|\ \begin{aligned} &=1\qquad\ \ \text{if

\end{aligned}\Bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{pro-}p}

and call

the

p
group
associated to
(\Gamma,\ell)
.
In case that

\Gamma
has no arrows
or if
\ell=\mathbf{1}
is identically
1
,

coincides
with the pro-
p
completion of the right-angled Artin pro-
p
group
G_{\ddot{\Gamma}}
based on the finite naïve graph

\ddot{\Gamma}=(\mathscr{V},\ddot{\mathscr{E}})
.
The oriented right-angled Artin pro-
p

group

coincides with the

p

, where the labelling function

\Gamma

2

\ell\colon\mathscr{A}\to 1+2\mathbb{Z}_{2}

-1

In
[

Without going into detail it should be mentioned that
