A characterization of sheaves among six functor formalisms on 𝐋𝐂𝐇 \mathbf{LCH} .
Ulrich Bunke and Marco Volpe

Abstract
Let 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} be any stable presentably symmetric monoidal ∞ \infty -category. In this paper, we characterize Shv ⁡ ( − , 𝒞 ) \mathrm{Shv}(-,{\mathcal{C}}) on locally compact Hausdorff spaces as the unique six functor formalism satisfying a list of very natural properties. As a consequence, we deduce that every continuous six functor formalism D D in the sense of Zhu is equivalent to Shv ⁡ ( − , D ⁡ ( pt ) ) \mathrm{Shv}(-,D(\mathrm{pt})) .



1
Introduction

The notion of six functor formalism was introduced by Grothendieck and his school in
[
AGV72
]
as a framework encoding the formal properties of cohomology theories. Since then, six functor formalisms have appeared throughout topology, algebraic geometry and representation theory, providing a common categorical structure underlying many apparently different setups. Recent developments of
[
Sch25
,
HM24
,
CLL25
]
have shed new light on the foundations of the theory of six functor formalisms, in particular by providing a precise formal definition of what a six functor formalism is. One of the advantages of these new perspectives is that they provide effective tools to compare different six functor formalisms.

In topology, and more specifically on
\mathbf{LCH}
, the category of locally compact Hausdorff spaces, a prominent instance of a six functor formalism is given by
\mathrm{Shv}(-,{\mathcal{C}})
, i.e. sheaves valued in a sufficiently nice stable
\infty
-category
{\mathcal{C}}
. We refer to
[
Vol25
]
for a development of the latter in the setting of
\infty
-categories, and to
[
Ver66
,
KS90
]
for more classical references. To the best of our knowledge, aside from
\mathrm{Shv}(-,{\mathcal{C}})
, there are only a few natural examples of six functor formalisms on
\mathbf{LCH}
. For this reason, one is naturally led to ask whether any six functor formalism on
\mathbf{LCH}
satisfying a list of expected properties must already arise from sheaves. In order to formulate this question more precisely, let us review some of the most notable features of
\mathrm{Shv}(-,{\mathcal{C}})
.

The first and most apparent feature is that
\mathrm{Shv}(-,{\mathcal{C}})
forms a sheaf valued in
{\mathbf{Cat}}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Shv}(-,{\mathcal{C}})
is localizing: any closed-open decomposition of a space
X
induces a stable recollement of
\mathrm{Shv}(X,{\mathcal{C}})
. Following
[
Zhu25
]
, we refer to these two properties collectively as canonical descent. Since it is a subcategory of presheaves, equivalences in
\mathrm{Shv}(X,{\mathcal{C}})
can be checked after evaluating at each open
U
in
X
. We name this property as being section-determined. A less immediate but nevertheless essential feature is that sheaves are finitary. This means that
\mathrm{Shv}(-,{\mathcal{C}})
preserves cofiltered limits of compact Hausdorff spaces when viewed as a
\mathbf{Pr}_{\mathrm{st}}^{L,\mathrm{op}}
-valued functor via the pullback functors. As a consequence, sheaf cohomology is finitary as well. For a general six functor formalism
D
, we write
\Gamma^{D}
for its associated cohomology theory
X\mapsto p_{X,*}p_{X}^{*}1
, where
1
in
D(\mathrm{pt})
is the monoidal unit and
p_{X}:X\to\mathrm{pt}
is the unique map.

The main result of this paper shows that, under a mild additional assumption, these properties suffice to characterize sheaves amongst all six functor formalisms on
\mathbf{LCH}
.

Theorem 1.1

(
Theorem

3.18
)
.

Let
D
be a six functor formalism on the Nagata context
(\mathbf{LCH},I,P)
. Assume that
D
satisfies the following properties:

1.

D
has canonical descent;

2.

D
is section-determined;

3.

\Gamma^{D}
is finitary;

4.

D(\mathrm{pt})
is dualizable.

Then
D
is canonically equivalent to
\mathrm{Shv}(-,D(\mathrm{pt}))
.

The assumption that
D(\mathrm{pt})
is dualizable may be replaced by the requirement that
\Gamma^{D}
is homotopy invariant, see
3.15
and
3.20
.

It is well-known that for sheaves valued in higher categories, equivalences can not always be checked on stalks. However, at least when
{\mathcal{C}}
is dualizable and
X
is hypercomplete, equivalences in
\mathrm{Shv}(X,{\mathcal{C}})
can be checked stalkwise. We refer to this property as to being stalk-determined. As a second result we show that, under the finitary assumption, section-determinedness follows from stalk-determinedness.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
D
be a six functor formalism on the Nagata context
(\mathbf{LCH},I,P)
. Assume that
D
has canonical descent, is finitary, and is stalk-determined. Then
D
is section-determined.

We conclude this introduction by mentioning some applications of our results. An immediate consequence of
Theorem

1.1
and
1.2
is that any continuous six functor formalism on
\mathbf{LCH}
in the sense of
[
Zhu25
]
is equivalent to
\mathrm{Shv}(-,D(\mathrm{pt}))
. In
[
Bun26
]
, the first author introduces a version of
E
-theory of
C^{*}
-algebras parametrized by arbitrary topological spaces, or more generally by locales.
Theorem

1.1
is used there to show that the
E
-theory of
C^{*}
-algebras over a locally compact Hausdorff space is equivalent to sheaves valued in
E
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let

Proof.

Since the functor

is symmetric monoidal, its left-adjoint

is oplax symmetric monoidal. We must show that the op-lax monoidal structure map

is an equivalence. Since

is jointly conservative (with

and

.

and use that this functor is symmetric monoidal and

If we apply

an equivalence between zero objects.

2.4

This subsection focuses on stalks. For each

\mathbf{LCH}
with a point

. We show that the usual colimit formula for stalks holds for any

and

D:\mathbf{LCH}^{\mathrm{op}}\to{\mathrm{CAlg}}(\mathbf{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{st}})

.

For

is sufficiently well-behaved, the usual formula computing stalks from sheaf theory is also valid for

Lemma 2.27
.

If

is compatible with
I
and
P
and satisfies canonical descent, then

By localization and using an instance of (

, where

Using localization again we get

By a cofinality argument we can extend the index set of the colimit first to all compact subspaces of
X
which contain an open neighbourhood
of

i_{x,*}i_{x}^{*}\simeq\operatorname*{\mathtt{colim}}_{x\in V}j_{V\to X,*}j_{V\to X}^{*}\ .

We now apply

and get

If

.
