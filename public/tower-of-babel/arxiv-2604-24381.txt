A generalisation of the Gagliardo–Nirenberg Inequality with applications to mass-critical and mass-subcritical elliptic equations
Bartosz Bieganowski, Jacopo Schino

Abstract
. Via a new inequality à la Gagliardo–Nirenberg, we prove the existence and nonexistence of solutions to { ( − Δ ) s u + μ | y | 2 ​ s u + λ u = f ( u ) , ℝ N ∋ x = ( y , z ) ∈ ℝ K × ℝ N − K , ∫ ℝ N u 2 ​ 𝑑 x = ρ \begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u+\frac{\mu}{|y|^{2s}}u+\lambda u=f(u),\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\ni x=(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{K}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-K},\\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\rho\end{cases} in the mass-critical and mass-subcritical regimes, where s > 0 s>0 , N ≥ K ≥ 2 N\geq K\geq 2 , μ ∈ ℝ \mu\in\mathbb{R} belongs to a specific range, ρ > 0 \rho>0 is given a priori, and λ ∈ ℝ \lambda\in\mathbb{R} is unknown. Additionally, we obtain similar results for the problem above with μ = 0 \mu=0 and N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 as well as a related curl-curl equation. Finally, we provide a thorough insight into the threshold for ρ \rho that divides the scenarios of negative and zero least energy.

Introduction

This paper concerns the equation

(1.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}u+\frac{\mu}{|y|^{2s}}u+\lambda u=f(u),\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\ni x=(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{K}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-K},

paired with the constraint

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\rho.

Here,
s>0
,
N\geq K\geq 2
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
belongs to a specific range,
\rho>0
is given a priori, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is unknown and arises as a Lagrange multiplier. We will also study the case
\mu=0
and
N\geq 1
.

If
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is odd (hence extendable to the complex plane by
f(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}r)=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}f(r)
), (
1.1
) is obtained when seeking standing-wave solutions to the corresponding evolution equation

(1.3)

-\mathrm{i}\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}+(-\Delta)^{s}\Psi+\frac{\mu}{|y|^{2s}}\Psi=f(\Psi),\quad\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{C},

i.e.,
\Psi(t,x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda t}u(x)
.
Taking
s=1
and
\mu=0
in (
1.3
), we obtain the well-known (autonomous)
semilinear Schrödinger equation

[
9
]
. When
0<s<1
, (
1.3
) with
\mu=0
was derived somewhat recently by N. Laskin
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
following an approach to quantum mechanics based on path integrals proposed by R. Feynman, who maintained that the wave function solving Schrödinger’s equation should be given by the sum over all the possible histories of the system, i.e., by a heuristic integral over an infinity of quantum-mechanically possible trajectories
[
14
]
. Later on, the fractional semilinear Schrödinger equation with
s>1
was used to describe anomalous diffusion in the Bloch–Torrey equation
[
35
]
and model acoustic wave propagation in heterogeneous attenuating media
[
52
]
. When
s=2
, (
1.3
) with
\mu=0
was considered in
[
21
,
50
]
to study the stability of solitons in magnetic materials once the effective quasi-particle mass becomes infinite. In general, higher-order powers are called for when the modelled phenomena become either more complex or encompass more complicated fundamental processes. For instance, polyharmonic equations were studied in the context of
critical semilinear polyharmonic eigenvalue problems

[
17
]
, which are related to the
Pucci–Serrin conjecture

[
41
]
.

Concerning the singular term
\mu|y|^{-2s}
, when
N=K
and
s=1
, this is the well-known
Hardy potential
, closely related to the
Hardy inequality

[
19
,
42
]
. As for the case
K<N
(and
s=1
), one possible derivation is via the
curl-curl equation

(1.4)

\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{U}=f(|\mathbf{U}|)\frac{\mathbf{U}}{|\mathbf{U}|},\quad\mathbf{U}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3},

in domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
invariant under the action of
\mathcal{O}(2)\times\{1\}
, sometimes referred to as
cylindrically symmetric
: here, the ansatz

(1.5)

\mathbf{U}(x)=\frac{u(x)}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}-x_{2}\\
x_{1}\\
0\end{pmatrix}

for some
\mathcal{O}(2)\times\{1\}
-invariant
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
leads to (
1.1
) (without the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
) with
N=3
,
K=2
, and
\mu=1
(more information on curl-curl problems in cylindrically-symmetric media is available in Section
6
). Different derivations are provided in
[
2
,
13
]
.

The constraint (
1.2
) is motivated by the fact that the
L^{2}
norm of solutions to (
1.3
) is constant in time, not only for standing waves (which is obvious), but also, under mild conditions on
f
(see (
f1
) below), for solutions such that
\Psi(t,\cdot)
belongs to the right function space for every
t\in\mathbb{R}

– cf.
X^{\mu}
below. This can be easily computed by multiplying (
1.3
) by the complex conjugate of
\Psi
, integrating, and taking the imaginary part.

With the intention of studying (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) via variational methods, we introduce the space

X^{\mu}\coloneqq\Set{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}{\left|\mu\right|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{|y|^{2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x<+\infty}

(note that
X^{0}=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
), where

\displaystyle H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle=\Set{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}|\mathcal{F}(u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi<+\infty}

\displaystyle=\Set{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\xi|^{2s})|\mathcal{F}(u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi<+\infty}

and
\mathcal{F}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the Fourier transform. In view of the symmetry of (
1.1
), we will introduce a symmetric subspace of
X^{\mu}
. Given

, we will denote

\widetilde{g}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&I_{N-K}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{O}(N),

where

is the

\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\Set{\widetilde{g}}{g\in\mathcal{O}(K)}\subset\mathcal{O}(N).

It is understood that
\widetilde{g}=g
and
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{O}(N)
when

. The subspace of

consisting of

-invariant functions is defined as

X_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}\coloneqq\Set{u\in X^{\mu}}{u(g\cdot)=u\text{ for all }g\in\mathcal{G}}.

The assumptions about

are as follows, where
F(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}f(r)\,\mathrm{d}r
,

\alpha_{N}\coloneqq\frac{N(2\pi)^{N}}{\omega_{N-1}},

\omega_{N-1}
is the
(N-1)
-dimensional measure of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
,
2^{*}=+\infty
if

and

if

, and

2_{\#}\coloneqq 2+\frac{4s}{N}

is the so-called

(f1)

f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
and

•

, then
|f(t)|\lesssim|t|
for all

•

•

|f(t)\lesssim|t|+|t|^{2^{*}-1}

.

(f2)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{F(t)}{t^{2}}=0
.

(f3)

\displaystyle\overline{\eta}_{\infty}\coloneqq\limsup_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{F_{+}(t)}{|t|^{2_{\#}}}<+\infty
.

(f4)

\displaystyle\underline{\eta}_{0}\coloneqq\liminf_{t\to 0}\frac{F_{+}(t)}{|t|^{2_{\#}}}>0

We introduce the functional
J\colon X^{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

J(u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\mathrm{D}^{s}u|^{2}+\mu\frac{u^{2}}{|y|^{2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)\,\mathrm{d}x,

which is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
if (

) holds (see Lemma

for the case

), where

\mathrm{D}^{s}u\coloneqq\begin{cases}\Delta^{s/2}u&\text{if }s\in\mathbb{N}\text{ is even}\\
\nabla\Delta^{(s-1)/2}u&\text{if }s\in\mathbb{N}\text{ is odd}\\
(-\Delta)^{s/2-\lfloor s/2\rfloor}(-\Delta)^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}u&\text{if }s\not\in\mathbb{N}\end{cases}

and

is the integer part of

. We recall that, for

, the fractional Laplacian of

is defined, e.g., via

Concerning the range of

where

Consequently, we will always assume

when

and

Lemma 2.4

3.
A Gagliardo–Nirenberg-type inequality

In this section, we fix
p\in(2,2^{*})
. For every
u\in X_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}\setminus\{0\}
, we define the quotient

\mathcal{R}(u)\coloneqq\frac{[u]_{\mu}^{p\delta_{p}}|u|_{2}^{p(1-\delta_{p})}}{|u|_{p}^{p}}>0,

where
\delta_{p}=\delta_{p}(N,s)=\frac{N}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\in(0,1)
. It is readily seen that
\mathcal{R}
does not depend on internal or external scaling, that is, for all
t,r>0
and
u\in X_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}\setminus\{0\}
, there holds
\mathcal{R}(tu(r\cdot))=\mathcal{R}(u)
. As a consequence,

\iota\coloneqq\inf\Set{\mathcal{R}(u)}{u\in X_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}\setminus\{0\}}=\inf\Set{\mathcal{R}(u)}{u\in X_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}\text{ and }[u]_{\mu}=|u|_{2}=1}.

Theorem 3.1
.

The infimum
\iota
defined above is attained at a function
w\in X_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}
such that

(-\Delta)^{s}w+\frac{\mu}{|y|^{2s}}w+\frac{1-\delta_{p}}{\delta_{p}}w=|w|^{p-2}w

and

(3.1)

[w]_{\mu}^{p-2}=|w|_{2}^{p-2}=\frac{\iota}{\delta_{p}}.

Proof.

Let
(u_{n})_{n}\subset X_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}
such that
[u_{n}]_{\mu}=|u_{n}|_{2}=1
and
\lim_{n}\mathcal{R}(u_{n})=\iota
.

Case 1:

N=K
. From the compact embedding
H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists
u_{0}\in X_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
X^{\mu}
and
u_{n}\to u_{0}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Consequently,

\iota=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|u_{n}|_{p}^{p}}=\frac{1}{|u_{0}|_{p}^{p}}\geq\frac{[u_{0}]_{\mu}^{p\delta_{p}}|u_{0}|_{2}^{p(1-\delta_{p})}}{|u_{0}|_{p}^{p}}\geq\iota

(it is clear from the first two equalities above that
u_{0}\neq 0
), which yields
[u_{0}]_{\mu}=|u_{0}|_{2}=1
and
\mathcal{R}(u_{0})=\iota
.

Case 2:

N>K
. If the sequence
(u_{n})_{n}
satisfies (
2.1
), then
\lim_{n}|u_{n}|_{p}=0
, and so

\iota=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|u_{n}|_{p}^{p}}=+\infty,

which is impossible. Consequently, from Lemma
2.2
(ii), there exists
u_{0}\in X_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}\setminus\{0\}
such that, up to a subsequence and translations in the variable
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N-K}
,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
X^{\mu}
. Let
t_{0},r_{0}>0
such that
[t_{0}u_{0}(r_{0}\cdot)]_{\mu}=|t_{0}u_{0}(r_{0}\cdot)|_{2}=1
, that is,

t_{0}=\left([u_{0}]_{\mu}^{-N/s}|u_{0}|_{2}^{N/s-2}\right)^{1/2}\text{ and }r_{0}=\left([u_{0}]_{\mu}^{-1}|u_{0}|_{2}\right)^{1/s}.

Then,
|t_{0}u_{0}(r_{0}\cdot)|_{p}=[u_{0}]_{\mu}^{-\delta_{p}}|u_{0}|_{2}^{\delta_{p}-1}|u_{0}|_{p}\geq|u_{0}|_{p}
, which implies

\mathcal{R}(t_{0}u_{0}(r_{0}\cdot))=\mathcal{R}(u_{0})=\frac{[u_{0}]_{\mu}^{p\delta_{p}}|u_{0}|_{2}^{p(1-\delta_{p})}}{|u_{0}|_{p}^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{|u_{0}|_{p}^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{|t_{0}u_{0}(r_{0}\cdot)|_{p}^{p}}=\mathcal{R}(t_{0}u_{0}(r_{0}\cdot)).

This yields
[u_{0}]_{\mu}=|u_{0}|_{2}=1
, and so, from the consequent strong convergence in

,
\mathcal{R}(u_{0})=\iota
.

Since

and

,

solves the equation

whence the statement letting

.

As outlined in Section

Recall that, in

\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{U})=\nabla(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{U})-\Delta\mathbf{U}

holds for every
\mathbf{U}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})

, we can use it as the definition of

for
\mathbf{U}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})

Appendix A
Remarks on the upper bounds for
\rho_{*}

Under (
f1
) and the assumption that
F
is positive somewhere, Lemma
4.4
provides upper bounds for
\rho_{*}
, i.e.,
\rho_{*}\leq\rho_{F}
and, if (
f4
) holds,
\rho_{*}\leq(2C_{N,2_{\#}}\underline{\eta}_{0})^{-N/(2s)}
. Since (
f4
) is a stronger condition than the existence of a point where
F
is positive, one would expect the latter upper bound not to exceed the former. Somewhat surprisingly, instead, the opposite scenario occurs, at least when the optimal function in the Gagliardo–Nirenberg-type inequality (
3.2
) is essentially bounded. However, one should observe that such a ‘worse’ bound is more likely to be explicitly computed than the other. Moreover, for the sake of completeness, we recall that Remark
5.4
provides sufficient conditions for such upper bounds to coincide.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
w\in X^{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}}
be the function given by Theorem
3.1
with
p=2_{\#}
. If (
f1
) and (
f4
) hold and
w\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then
\rho_{F}\leq(2C_{N,2_{\#}}\underline{\eta}_{0})^{-N/(2s)}
.

Proof.

We consider the case when
\underline{\eta}_{0}<\infty
(the other is similar). Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
\delta>0
such that
F(t)\geq(\underline{\eta}_{0}-\varepsilon)|t|^{2_{\#}}
if
|t|\leq\delta
. Let
\sigma>0
. Up to internal and external scaling, we can assume that
|w|_{\infty}=\delta
and
|w|_{2_{\#}}^{2_{\#}}=\sigma/(\underline{\eta}_{0}-\varepsilon)
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(w)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{1}{\underline{\eta}_{0}-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{2_{\#}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\sigma.

It follows that

\inf\Set{[u]_{\mu}^{N/s}\left}{u}\leq[w]_{\mu}^{N/s}\left|w\right|_{2}^{2}\\
=\left(C_{N,2_{\#}}^{-1}|w|_{2_{\#}}^{2_{\#}}\right)^{N/(2s)}=\left(\frac{\sigma}{C_{N,2_{\#}}(\underline{\eta}_{0}-\varepsilon)}\right)^{N/(2s)},

whence

\rho_{F}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2C_{N,2_{\#}}(\underline{\eta}_{0}-\varepsilon)}\right)^{N/(2s)}.\qed
