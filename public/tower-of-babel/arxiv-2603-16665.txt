Lower and Upper Expected Hitting Times for Weighted Imprecise Markov Chains
Marco Sangalli, Thomas Krak

Abstract
In this paper, we extend hitting times for imprecise Markov chains to the framework of weighted imprecise Markov chains (WIMCs), in which each transition is associated with a strictly positive weight encoded by a matrix W W . Given a convex set 𝒯 \mathcal{T} of admissible transition matrices, we define lower and upper expected hitting times for WIMCs as the infimum and supremum of the (weighted) expected hitting times over 𝒯 \mathcal{T} , and we characterise these quantities as the unique solutions of nonlinear fixed-point equations. We show that any weighted hitting time problem can be transformed into an unweighted hitting time problem on an augmented state space, enabling the reuse of existing IMC theory and algorithms. In particular, we are able to adapt known iterative methods for the numerical computation of expected hitting times for WIMCs.

Introduction and Motivation

Hitting times are a fundamental quantitative measure for stochastic processes. Given a target set of states
A
, the expected hitting time measures how long a process needs, on average, to reach the target
A
. In classical precise Markov chains this notion is usually understood as the expected number of transitions until first arrival.

A first generalisation replaces step counts with a matrix of nonnegative weights
W
that assigns a travel time (or cost) to each transition. The resulting object is a weighted Markov chain, and the corresponding hitting time is defined as the accumulated weight along a random trajectory up to the first entrance in
A
.
This naturally arises in many applications, ranging from routing and transportation to robotic surveillance
1
;
3
.

A second generalisation addresses model uncertainty: instead of a single transition matrix, one considers a set
\mathcal{T}
of possible transition matrices, leading to an imprecise Markov chain (IMC)
2
;
5
. For IMCs, lower and upper hitting times are obtained by minimising or maximising the expected hitting time over all admissible Markov chains. These two generalisations have so far been studied largely independently: weighted travel times for precise chains on the one hand, and (unweighted) expected hitting times for IMCs on the other.

In this paper, we combine these two perspectives by extending the notion of expected hitting times for IMCs to the weighted setting. We introduce and study
weighted imprecise Markov chains
(WIMCs), in which transitions are both uncertain, through a nonempty set
\mathcal{T}
of transition matrices, and associated with strictly positive weights given by a matrix
W
.
In this context, we introduce lower and upper expected hitting times as the infimum and supremum of the (weighted) hitting time over
\mathcal{T}
, and we characterise these quantities as the unique solutions of nonlinear fixed-point equations.
The key insight is that any weighted hitting time problem can be transformed into an unweighted hitting time problem on an augmented state space. This connection enables us to adapt and reuse existing IMC theory and algorithms to WIMCs: we are able to show that there always exist transition matrices attaining the minimum and maximum in the definition of lower and upper hitting time for WIMCs, that these quantities are characterised as the unique solutions of two nonlinear fixed-point equations, and that we can compute them efficiently adapting Krak’s
7
algorithm for IMCs.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Section
2
recalls basic notation and classical results on hitting times and Markov chains. Section
3
introduces weighted Markov chains and presents key results connecting the unweighted and weighted framework. Section
4
defines weighted imprecise Markov chains, presents the main fixed-point results, and discusses algorithmic aspects.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a discrete finite space with cardinality
N\in\mathbb{N}

\mathcal{X}

(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
taking values in
\mathcal{X}
.
The process
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

\mathbb{P}_{X}(X_{n+1}=x_{n+1}\mid X_{0:n}=x_{0:n})=\mathbb{P}_{X}(X_{n+1}=x_{n+1}\mid X_{n}=x_{n})

(1)

for all

and all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and where we write
\mathbb{P}_{X}
for the probability measure associated to the process
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. The Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is said to be (time-)
homogeneous
if

\mathbb{P}_{X}(X_{n+1}=y\mid X_{n}=x)=\mathbb{P}_{X}(X_{1}=y\mid X_{0}=x)

(2)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The
N
by
N
stochastic matrix
T
defined by

T(x,y)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}_{X}(X_{1}=y\mid X_{0}=x)

is the
transition matrix
of the homogeneous Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and uniquely characterises its behaviour up to its initial distribution.
One may view a homogeneous Markov chain as a random walk on the directed graph
G_{T}=(V,E)
where
V\coloneqq\mathcal{X}
and
(x,y)\in E
if and only if
T(x,y)>0
.

Let
A\subset\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty target set of states. The
hitting time
of the Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is the random variable defined as

\tau_{A}:=\inf\{n\geq 0:X_{n}\in A\}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{+\infty\},

(3)

and, for a starting state
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the
expected hitting time
is

h^{T}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{X}}\bigl[\tau_{A}\mid X_{0}=x\bigr].

(4)

The quantity
h^{T}(x)
can be seen as the mean number of steps the chain
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
takes before reaching the target
A
, and is the minimal nonnegative solution of a linear system
8

\begin{cases}h^{T}(x)=0&\text{if $x\in A$,}\\
h^{T}(x)=1+\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{X}}T(x,y)h^{T}(y)&\text{if $x\notin A$.}\end{cases}

(5)

Let

We can naturally define the

\eta_{A}^{W}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{A}}W(X_{n-1},X_{n}).

(6)

Conditioned on the chain starting in

h^{T,W}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{X}}\bigl[\eta_{A}^{W}\mid X_{0}=x\bigr].

This quantity represents the expected travel time from a state

\begin{cases}h^{T,W}(x)=0&\text{if $x\in A$,}\\
h^{T,W}(x)=\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{X}}T(x,y)\left(W(x,y)+h^{T,W}(y)\right)&\text{if $x\notin A$.}\end{cases}

The next result follows directly from the linearity in

Lemma 1

Let

h^{T,cW}=ch^{T,W}

Weighted Imprecise Markov Chains

Instead of a single homogeneous Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with a fixed transition matrix
T
, we now consider a set
\mathcal{T}
of admissible transition matrices. Using this set, we define the family
\mathcal{P}
that contains all homogeneous Markov chains whose transition matrix is in
\mathcal{T}
. This set
\mathcal{P}
is called an
imprecise Markov chain
(IMC)
2
;
5
;
4
.
Throughout this paper, we impose some conditions on the set
\mathcal{T}
: we assume that it is nonempty, compact, convex, and has separately specified rows (SSR)
5
;
6
. This last property means that the set
\mathcal{T}
is the Cartesian product of
N=|\mathcal{X}|
compact and convex sets of probability distributions
\{\mathcal{T}_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
, one for each state
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Given a set of transition matrices
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{X}
, we define the
lower and upper expected hitting times
as follows:

\underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq\inf_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h^{T}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{h}^{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq\sup_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h^{T}.

Krak
7
showed that, under the
condition

(R1):

for all
T\in\mathcal{T}
and all
x\in\mathcal{X}
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
[T^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{A}](x)>0
,

i.e that the process can reach
A
with every matrix regardless of the starting state, the vector of lower hitting times is finite and the unique solution of a system of equations:

\underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}}=\mathbbm{1}_{A^{c}}+\mathbbm{1}_{A^{c}}\cdot\underline{T}\ \underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}},

(12)

where
\mathbbm{1}
is the indicator function and
\underline{T}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}
is the lower transition operator associated to
\mathcal{T}
, given by:

[\underline{T}f](x)\coloneqq\inf_{T\in\mathcal{T}}[Tf](x),\text{ for all $f\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$.}

(13)

A completely analogous characterisation also holds for upper hitting times.

Let
W>0
be a matrix of weights. We define the pair made of an IMC
\mathcal{P}
and a weight matrix
W
as a
weighted imprecise Markov chain
(WIMC). Given a set of transition matrices
\mathcal{T}
, we define the lower and upper expected hitting times for a WIMC as

\underline{h}^{W}:=\inf_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h^{T,W}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{h}^{W}:=\sup_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h^{T,W}.

(14)

We want to characterise these quantities as the unique solutions of two nonlinear fixed-point equations.
We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 2

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a set of transition matrices on
\mathcal{X}
. Then, there exists
T,\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}
such that

h^{T,W}=\underline{h}^{W}\quad\text{and}\quad h^{\tilde{T},W}=\overline{h}^{W}.

(15)

Proof

By Krak et al.
(
6

, if
W=1
there exists a matrix
T\in\mathcal{T}
that achieves the minimum in the definition of lower hitting time. Using the construction and notation from Proposition
1
and Corollary
1
, we find that there exists a transition matrix
T^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\mathcal{X}\cup\mathcal{Z}
achieving the lower hitting time, thus the transition matrix
T
on

defined as
T(x,y)=T^{\prime}(x,z_{xy})
achieves the minimum in
\underline{h}^{W}
. An analogous argument holds for upper hitting times. ∎

Define the operator
\underline{\mathscr{L}}^{W}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}

(\underline{\mathscr{L}}^{W}f)(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $x\in A$,}\\
\inf\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\left[\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{X}}T(x,y)\bigl(W(x,y)+f(y)\bigr)\right]&\text{if $x\notin A$,}\end{cases}

(16)

for all
f\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}
.
The following theorem states that

Theorem 4.1

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a set of transition matrices on
\mathcal{X}
.
Then, under condition (R1),
\underline{h}^{W}

\underline{h}^{W}=\underline{\mathscr{L}}^{W}\underline{h}^{W}.

Proof

If

, we follow the construction and notation of Proposition

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\mathcal{X}\cup\mathcal{Z}

We know from Krak

that there exists a unique
\underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
that satisfies

\underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\quad\text{if $x\in A$,}\\

In particular, there exists a matrix

defined as

for all

h^{\hat{T},W}(x)=h^{\hat{T}^{\prime}}(x)=\underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(x)=\inf_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}h^{T^{\prime}}(x)=\inf_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h^{T,W}(x)=\underline{h}^{W}(x),

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{W}(x)

\displaystyle=h^{\hat{T},W}(x)=h^{\hat{T}^{\prime}}(x)=\underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(x)=1+\inf_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}[T^{\prime}\underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}](x)

\displaystyle=1+\inf_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}T^{\prime}(x,z_{xy})\underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(z_{xy})

\displaystyle=1+\inf_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}T^{\prime}(x,z_{xy})\left(\underline{h}^{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(y)+W(x,y)-1\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}T(x,y)\left(\underline{h}^{W}(y)+W(x,y)\right)=\left[\underline{\mathscr{L}}^{W}\underline{h}^{W}\right](x),

for all
x\notin A
. Trivially, the equality
\underline{h}^{W}(x)=[\underline{\mathscr{L}}^{W}\underline{h}^{W}](x)

x\in A

\displaystyle\qquad=\inf_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{T}}T(x,y)\left(cW(x,y)+c\underline{h}^{W}(y)\right)

for all
x\notin A

4.1
Computing Lower and Upper Hitting Times

Krak
7
proposes efficient iterative algorithms to compute lower and upper hitting times for (unweighted) imprecise Markov chains. Thanks to Proposition
1
and Corollary
1
, we can use the same algorithms to compute lower and upper hitting times for weighted imprecise Markov chains. In particular, the algorithm for lower hitting times alternates between computing the (weighted) hitting time
h^{T_{n}}
by solving the linear system in (
8
) for a matrix
T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}
, and improving the transition matrix by solving

T_{n+1}(x,\cdot)=\argmin_{T(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{T}_{x}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{X}}T(x,y)\left(W(x,y)+h^{T_{n}}(y)\right),

for all
x\in A^{c}
. This matrix is identical to the one obtained by constructing the space
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\mathcal{X}\cup\mathcal{Z}
and the matrix
T^{\prime}_{n}
as in Corollary
1
, by computing
T^{\prime}_{n+1}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
minimising
\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}T^{\prime}(x,z_{xy})h^{T^{\prime}_{n}}(z_{xy})
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
as in Krak
7
, and finally by setting
T_{n+1}(x,y)=T^{\prime}_{n+1}(x,z_{xy})
.
Then, the convergence of this algorithm (and the analogous variant for upper hitting times) is guaranteed by Krak
7
.
