A proof of Esterle’s conjecture on negative powers of Hilbert-space contractions Thanks: Research supported by NSERC Discovery Grant RGPIN-2026-04565
Thomas Ransford

Abstract
. We establish the following result, confirming a conjecture of Jean Esterle. For each closed subset E E of the unit circle of Lebesgue measure zero, there exists a positive sequence u n → ∞ u_{n}\to\infty with the following property: if T T is a contraction on a Hilbert space such that σ ⁡ ( T ) ⊂ E \sigma(T)\subset E and ‖ T − n ‖ = O ⁡ ( u n ) \|T^{-n}\|=O(u_{n}) as n → ∞ n\to\infty , then T T is a unitary operator. A key tool used in the proof is a result generalizing the well-known fact that closed subsets E E of the real axis of Lebesgue measure zero are removable for bounded holomorphic functions. We show that such sets remain removable even for certain unbounded holomorphic functions of moderate growth near E E , where the notion of ‘moderate’ depends on E E .

1.
Introduction and statement of main results

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a complex Hilbert space.
We denote the operator norm and spectrum of
T
by
\|T\|
and
\sigma(T)
respectively. We say
T
is a
contraction
if
\|T\|\leq 1
.

Our goal in this article is to establish the following theorem,
which confirms a conjecture of Jean Esterle
[
4
, Conjecture 1.3]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each closed subset
E
of the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
of Lebesgue measure zero, there exists a
positive sequence
u_{n}\to\infty

with the following property:
if
T
is a contraction on a Hilbert space such that
\sigma(T)\subset E
and

\|T^{-n}\|=O(u_{n})
as
n\to\infty
, then
T
is a unitary operator.

Versions of this result have long been known for specific sets
E
:
for example countable compact sets
[
5
]
,
certain Cantor sets
[
1
]
, and Carleson sets
[
2
,
3
]
.
In each case, the corresponding sequence
(u_{n})
was explicitly identified. It is also known that no single sequence
(u_{n})

can work simultaneously for all compact sets
E
of measure zero

[
3
]
. Also the condition that
E
have measure zero is optimal: Theorem
1.1
breaks down for every closed set
E
of positive measure
[
4
]
. Finally, the result also breaks down if ‘Hilbert space’
is replaced by ‘Banach space’
[
2
]
. For a detailed account
of the background to Esterle’s conjecture, we refer
to
[
4
]
.

In
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
it was shown that
Theorem
1.1
holds under the additional assumption
that
\rank(I-T^{*}T)<\infty
. In this note, we prove the full version, following a method suggested to the author by Fedja Nazarov.
The new ingredient is a
removable singularity theorem, which we believe to be of interest in its own right.
It is a generalization of the well-known fact that a closed
subset
E
of the real line
\mathbb{R}
of Lebesgue measure zero
is removable for bounded holomorphic functions.
The new result shows that this remains true even for certain
unbounded holomorphic functions of moderate growth near
E
,
where what ‘moderate’ means depends on
E
.

We denote by
|\cdot|
Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
.
Also, given
E\subset\mathbb{C}
and
t>0
,
we write
E_{t}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\dist(z,E)\leq t\}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
such that
|E|=0
.

(i)

There exists a decreasing continuous function

\omega:(0,\infty)\to(1,\infty)
such that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\omega(t)=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{|E_{t}\cap\mathbb{R}|}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\omega(s)\,ds=0.

(1)

(ii)

Every
\omega
satisfying the conditions in (i)
has the following property:
if
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
containing
E
, and
if
f
is a holomorphic function on
\Omega\setminus E

such that
|f(x+iy)|\leq\omega(|y|)

for all
x+iy\in\Omega\setminus\mathbb{R}
,
then
f
has a holomorphic extension to the whole of
\Omega
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof uses two lemmas. In the first of these,
m
denotes planar Lebesgue measure.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
,
and let
F
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
such that
m(F)=0
.
Then every holomorphic function
f:\Omega\setminus F\to\mathbb{C}

such that

\liminf_{\eta\to 0}\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{F_{\eta}}|f(z)|\,dm(z)=0

(2)

has a holomorphic extension to the whole of
\Omega
.

The idea for the proof that follows was suggested by Fedja Nazarov.

Proof.

Fix
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
with
\supp\psi\subset\mathbb{D}
and
\int_{\mathbb{C}}\psi\,dm=1
.
For each
\eta>0
, define
\phi_{\eta}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\phi_{\eta}(z):=1-\frac{1}{(\eta/4)^{2}}\int_{F_{\eta/2}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{z-w}{\eta/4}\Bigr)\,dm(w).

Then
\phi_{\eta}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
and it satisfies

\phi_{\eta}=1
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus F_{\eta}
and

\phi_{\eta}=0
on
F_{\eta/4}
.
Also

|\partial\phi_{\eta}/\partial\overline{z}|\leq C_{\psi}/\eta

on
\mathbb{C}
, where
C_{\psi}
is a constant
depending just on
\psi
.

Let
f:\Omega\setminus E\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic function
such that (
2
) holds.
Define
f_{\eta}:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
by

f_{\eta}(z):=\begin{cases}f(z)\phi_{\eta}(z),&z\in\Omega\setminus F,\\
0,&z\in F_{\eta/4}.\end{cases}

Then

f_{\eta}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
and it satisfies

f_{\eta}=f
on
\Omega\setminus F_{\eta}

and
\partial f_{\eta}/\partial\overline{z}=f\partial\phi_{\eta}/\partial\overline{z}
on
\Omega\setminus F
.
Fix
\Gamma
, a finite union of closed contours in
\Omega\setminus F
such that

n(\Gamma,w)=\begin{cases}0\text{~or~}1,&\forall w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma,\\
1,&\forall w\in F,\\
0,&\forall w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

Set
U:=\{w\in\Omega\setminus\Gamma:n(\Gamma,w)=1\}
, a open neighbourhood of
F
.
By the Cauchy–Pompeiu formula,

f_{\eta}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{f_{\eta}(z)}{z-\zeta}\,dz-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{U}\frac{\partial f_{\eta}}{\partial\overline{z}}(z)\frac{1}{z-\zeta}\,dm(z)\quad(\zeta\in U).

In particular, if
\eta<\dist(\Gamma,F)
,
then

f(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{f(z)}{z-\zeta}\,dz-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{F_{\eta}}f(z)\frac{\partial\phi_{\eta}}{\partial\overline{z}}(z)\frac{1}{z-\zeta}\,dm(z)\quad(\zeta\in U\setminus F_{\eta}).

For each
\zeta\in U\setminus F_{\eta}
, we have

\Bigl|\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{F_{\eta}}f(z)\frac{\partial\phi_{\eta}}{\partial\overline{z}}(z)\frac{1}{z-\zeta}\,dm(z)\Bigr|\leq\frac{C_{\psi}}{\pi\dist(\zeta,F_{\eta})}\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{F_{\eta}}|f(z)|\,dm(z).

Taking
\liminf_{\eta\to 0}
and using (
2
), we deduce that

f(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{f(z)}{z-\zeta}\,dz\quad(\zeta\in U\setminus F).

As a function of
\zeta
,
the right-hand is holomorphic on
U
, so it provides the required
holomorphic extension of
f
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\rho:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous increasing function such that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\rho(t)/t=\infty
.
Then there exists a continuous decreasing function
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(1,\infty)
such that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\omega(t)=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\rho(t)}\int_{0}^{t}\omega(s)\,ds=0.

Proof.

Define
\rho_{1}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
by
\rho_{1}(t):=\sqrt{t\rho(t)}
.
Then

\lim_{t\to 0+}\frac{\rho_{1}(t)}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\sqrt{\frac{\rho(t)}{t}}=\infty,

so we may recursively choose a decreasing sequence
(t_{n})
tending to zero such that, if we set

d_{n}:=\frac{\rho_{1}(t_{n-1})-\rho_{1}(t_{n})}{t_{n-1}-t_{n}},

then
(d_{n})
is an increasing sequence that tends to infinity as
n\to\infty
.
Fix
n_{0}
such that
d_{n_{0}}>1
, and define
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(1,\infty)
by stipulating
that
\omega(t_{n})=d_{n}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
, that
\omega
is linear on each interval
[t_{n+1},t_{n}]

for
n\geq n_{0}
and constant on
[t_{n_{0}},\infty)
. Clearly
\omega
is a continuous decreasing
function such that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\omega(t)=\infty
. Also, for each
n\geq n_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\omega(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq n}\int_{t_{k+1}}^{t_{k}}\omega(s)\,ds\leq\sum_{k\geq n}\omega(t_{k+1})(t_{k}-t_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq n}(\rho_{1}(t_{k})-\rho_{1}(t_{k+1}))\leq\rho_{1}(t_{n}),

and hence

\frac{1}{\rho(t_{n})}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\omega(s)\,ds=\frac{t_{n}}{\rho_{1}(t_{n})^{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\omega(s)\,ds\leq\frac{t_{n}}{\rho_{1}(t_{n})}.

Since
t_{n}/\rho_{1}(t_{n})\to 0
, it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho(t_{n})}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\omega(s)\,ds=0,

which establishes the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

(i)
Define
\rho:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
by

\rho(t):=\frac{t}{|E_{t}\cap\mathbb{R}|}\quad(t>0).

Then
\rho
is a continuous increasing function
such that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\rho(t)/t=\infty
.
By Lemma
2.2
, there exists a
continuous decreasing function
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(1,\infty)

such that (
1
) holds.

(ii) Let
\omega
be a function satisfying the conclusions of (i),
let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
containing
E
,
and let
f
be a holomorphic function on
\Omega\setminus E

such that
|f(x+iy)|\leq\omega(|y|)
for all
x+iy\in\Omega\setminus\mathbb{R}
.
For each
\eta>0
, we have

E_{\eta}\subset(E_{\eta}\cap\mathbb{R})\times[-\eta,\eta],

so,
if
\eta
is small enough so that
(E_{\eta}\cap\mathbb{R})\times[-\eta,\eta]\subset\Omega
, then

\int_{E_{\eta}}|f|\,dm\leq\int_{E_{\eta}\cap\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\eta}^{\eta}|f(x+iy)|\,dy\,dx\leq 2|E_{\eta}\cap\mathbb{R}|\int_{0}^{\eta}\omega(y)\,dy.

Hence

\liminf_{\eta\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{E_{\eta}}|f|\,dm\leq\liminf_{\eta\to 0^{+}}\frac{2|E_{\eta}\cap\mathbb{R}|}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\eta}\omega(y)\,dy=0,

the last equality by (
1
).
By Lemma
2.1
, the function
f
has a holomorphic extension to the whole of
\Omega
.
This shows that
\omega
has the required property, and completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Given Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, we write
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
for the space of
bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{F}
into
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
Also we say that
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is
purely contractive
if
\|Tx\|<\|x\|
for all
x\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{0\}
.

The following result is a key step in the proof of
Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For each closed subset
E
of
\mathbb{T}

of Lebesgue measure zero,
there exists a positive sequence
u_{n}\to\infty

with the following property:
if
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are Hilbert spaces
and if
\Theta:(\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is a holomorphic function such that

\Theta(\lambda)
is purely contractive for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
and unitary
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E
,
then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}u_{n}\delta_{n}(\Theta)=0,

where

\delta_{n}(\Theta):=\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{D}}\max\bigl\{|\lambda|^{n},\|\Theta(\lambda)^{-1}\|^{-1}\bigr\}.

(3)

In
[
4
, Theorem 5.7]
it was shown that
Theorem
3.1
holds in the special case
where
\dim\mathcal{F}=\dim\mathcal{F}^{\prime}<\infty
(leading to the version of Theorem
1.1

where
\rank(I-T^{*}T)<\infty
).
The technique used was to reduce to the scalar case

\dim_{\mathcal{F}}=\dim\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=1
by taking determinants, and then to
exploit the fact that, in that case,

\Theta
is a singular inner function, so has a representation in terms of a singular measure
on
E
. In the case where
\dim\mathcal{F}=\dim\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\infty
, we know of no such representation,
and so the problem of proving Theorem
3.1
was left open
[
4
, Conjecture 7.1]
.
Using the removable singularity result Theorem
1.2
, we can now solve this problem.

Before embarking upon on the proof of Theorem
3.1
,
it will be convenient to establish an alternative formula
for
\delta_{n}(\Theta)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
E,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\Theta,\delta_{n}(\Theta)
be as in the
statement of Theorem
3.1
. Then

1/\delta_{n}(\Theta)=\sup_{|\lambda|>1}\min\bigl\{|\lambda|^{n},\|\Theta(\lambda)\|\bigr\}.

(4)

Proof.

The function
\lambda\mapsto\Theta(\lambda)\Theta(1/\overline{\lambda})^{*}
is holomorphic on

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E
and equal to the identity
I

when
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E
.
By the identity principle, it follows that

\Theta(\lambda)\Theta(1/\overline{\lambda})^{*}=I

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E
.
The formula (
4
) follows easily.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
of Lebesgue measure zero.
By Theorem
1.2
and a simple conformal-mapping argument,
there exists a decreasing continuous function
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(1,\infty)

satisfying
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\omega(t)=\infty
with the following property:
if
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
containing
E
,
and if
f:\Omega\setminus E\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic function
such that
|f(re^{i\theta})|\leq\omega(|r-1|)
for all
re^{i\theta}\in\Omega\setminus\mathbb{T}
,
then
f
has a holomorphic extension to the whole of
\Omega
.

For
n\geq 1
, the function
t\mapsto\omega(t)-(1+t)^{n}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, strictly decreasing,
tends to
+\infty
as
t\to 0^{+}
and to
-\infty
as
t\to\infty
.
Therefore there exists a unique
t_{n}\in(0,\infty)
such that
\omega(t_{n})=(1+t_{n})^{n}
.
Clearly the sequence
(t_{n})
is strictly decreasing and
t_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Set

u_{n}:=(1+t_{n+1})^{n/2}\quad(n\geq 1).

Note that

u_{n}=\omega(t_{n+1})^{n/(2n+2)}\quad(n\geq 1),

which implies that
u_{n}\to\infty
.
We shall show that this sequence
(u_{n})
has the property prescribed in the statement of the theorem.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be Hilbert spaces,
and let
\Theta:(\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
be a holomorphic function such that

\Theta(\lambda)
is purely contractive for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
and unitary
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E
. Fix any
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E
. Since
\Theta(\zeta_{0})
is unitary,
there exist unit vectors
v\in\mathcal{F}
and
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
such that
\langle\Theta(\zeta_{0})v,v^{\prime}\rangle=1
.
Define
f:\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E\to\mathbb{C}
by

f(\lambda):=\langle\Theta(\lambda)v,v^{\prime}\rangle\quad(\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E).

Clearly
f
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E
and
f(\zeta_{0})=1
. Also, as
\Theta(\lambda)

is purely contractive for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

|f(\lambda)|=|\langle\Theta(\lambda)v,v^{\prime}\rangle|\leq\|\Theta(\lambda)v\|<\|v\|=1\quad(\lambda\in\mathbb{D}).

In particular,
f
is not constant. This implies that the set
E
is not a removable singularity for
f
.
By the choice of
\omega
above, it follows that there exists a sequence
(\lambda_{k})
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{T}

such that
|\lambda_{k}|\to 1
and
|f(\lambda_{k})|>\omega(||\lambda_{k}|-1|)
for all
k
.
As
|f|\leq 1
on
\mathbb{D}
, all but finitely many
(\lambda_{k})
lie outside
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,
so we may as well suppose that
|\lambda_{k}|>1
for all
k
. For each
k
sufficiently large,
there exists an
n\geq 1
such that
1+t_{n+1}\leq|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1+t_{n}
. Then, by the maximum principle
applied on the exterior of
|\lambda|=1+t_{n+1}
,

|f(\lambda_{k})|\leq\max_{|\lambda|=1+t_{n+1}}|f(\lambda)|\leq\max_{|\lambda|=1+t_{n+1}}\|\Theta(\lambda)\|,

and hence

1<\frac{|f(\lambda_{k})|}{\omega(|\lambda_{k}|-1)}\leq\max_{|\lambda|=1+t_{n+1}}\frac{\|\Theta(\lambda)\|}{\omega(|\lambda_{k}|-1)}\leq\max_{|\lambda|=1+t_{n+1}}\frac{\|\Theta(\lambda)\|}{\omega(t_{n})}.

By our choice of
t_{n}
, we have
\omega(t_{n})=(1+t_{n})^{n}
, and so

1<\max_{|\lambda|=1+t_{n+1}}\frac{\|\Theta(\lambda)\|}{(1+t_{n})^{n}}\leq\max_{|\lambda|=1+t_{n+1}}\frac{\|\Theta(\lambda)\|}{(1+t_{n+1})^{n}}=\max_{|\lambda|=1+t_{n+1}}\frac{\|\Theta(\lambda)\|}{|\lambda|^{n}}.

Using (
4
), we deduce that

\frac{1}{\delta_{n}(\Theta)}=\sup_{|\lambda|>1}\min\bigl\{|\lambda|^{n},\|\Theta(\lambda)\|\bigr\}\geq(1+t_{n+1})^{n}=u_{n}^{2},

the last equality by our choice of
u_{n}
. To summarize, we have shown that,
for each
n
such that
1+t_{n+1}\leq|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1+t_{n}
for some
k
,
we have
\delta_{n}(\Theta)\leq 1/u_{n}^{2}
. Since
|\lambda_{k}|\to 1
, there are
infinitely many such
n
. Consequently

\liminf_{n\to\infty}u_{n}\delta_{n}(\Theta)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}1/u_{n}=0.

Thus the sequence
(u_{n})
has the required property.
∎

It remains to show how Theorem
1.1
can be
deduced using Theorem
3.1
.
In fact this part of the argument was already explained in detail in
[
4
]
,
so we shall just sketch the proof for the sake of completeness.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
of Lebesgue measure zero.
Let
(u_{n})
be a positive sequence tending to infinity and satisfying
the conclusion of Theorem
3.1
.
We shall show that,
if
T
is a non-unitary Hilbert-space contraction such that

\sigma(T)\subset E
, then
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|T^{-n}\|/u_{n}=\infty
, thereby proving Theorem
1.1
.

Let
T
be a non-unitary Hilbert-space contraction such that

\sigma(T)\subset E
. By
[
4
, Lemma 6.1]
,
there is a completely non-unitary contraction
T_{1}

defined on a separable Hilbert space such that

\sigma(T_{1})\subset\sigma(T)
and
\|T_{1}^{-n}\|\leq\|T^{-n}\|

for all
n
. Thus, we may as well suppose from the outset
that
T
is completely non-unitary and that it is defined
on a separable Hilbert space. Together with the fact that

\sigma(T)
is a subset
\mathbb{T}
of Lebesgue measure zero,
this implies that both
T^{n}
and
T^{*n}
converge strongly to zero as
n\to\infty

[
4
, Lemma 6.2]
.

We now invoke a version of the Nagy–Foias model theorem for contractions

[
4
, Theorem 5.8]
: since
T
is a contraction on a separable
Hilbert space such that
T^{*n}
converges strongly to zero as
n\to\infty
, it is unitarily equivalent to
S_{\Theta}
,
the compressed shift associated to an operator-valued
inner function
\Theta
on
\mathbb{D}
. Thus
\|T^{-n}\|=\|S_{\Theta}^{-n}\|

for all
n\geq 1
, and from
[
4
, Theorem 5.5]
we have

\|S_{\Theta}^{-n}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\delta_{n}(\Theta)}-1\Bigr)\quad(n\geq 1),

where
\delta_{n}(S_{\Theta})
is defined as in (
3
).

Now by
[
4
, Theorem 5.9]
,
as
T
is non-unitary,
\Theta
can be chosen so that

\Theta(\lambda)
is purely contractive for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
.
Also, by
[
4
, Theorem 5.2]
, since
\sigma(T)\subset E
,
it follows that
\Theta
may be chosen so that it has a holomorphic
extension to the whole of
\mathbb{C}\setminus E
and so that
\Theta(\lambda)
is
unitary for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E
.
Thus
\Theta
satisfies all the hypotheses of
Theorem
3.1
, and so by that theorem we deduce that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}u_{n}\delta_{n}(\Theta)=0.

Putting everything together, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|T^{-n}\|/u_{n}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|S_{\Theta}^{-n}\|/u_{n}\geq(1/2)(\liminf_{n\to\infty}u_{n}\delta_{n}(\Theta))^{-1}=\infty,

as was to be shown.
∎
