Abstract
. We classify all corefree fusion systems on a Sylow 3 3 -subgroup of the sporadic groups Fi 22 \Fi_{22} , Fi 23 \Fi_{23} and B \Bm . We show that the 3 3 -group in each case does not support any exotic fusion systems. This is the first of two papers that will complete the classification of all corefree fusion systems on Sylow p p -subgroups of sporadic groups for p p odd.

1.
Introduction

A
fusion system

\mathcal{F}
is an algebraic structure on a
p
-group
S
that generalises conjugation by an overgroup
G
. We are particularly interested in
saturated
fusion systems, which include all fusion systems on
S
where
S\in\Syl_{p}(G)
, in which case we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
realized
by
G
. Much interest is in the case where a saturated fusion system is not realized by any finite group
G
, called
exotic
fusion systems (for a discussion, see
[
3
, III.7.4]
).

For a given
p
-group
S
, it is quite unlikely that
S
supports exotic fusion systems (see, for example,
[
23
]
). On the other hand, many of the known exotic fusion systems have been found on Sylow
p
-subgroups of finite simple sporadic groups. In fact, the first examples of exotic fusion systems for odd primes were found in
[
24
]
on the extraspecial group
7^{1+2}_{+}
, which is a Sylow
7
-subgroup of the sporadic groups
\mathrm{O}^{\prime}\mathrm{N}
,
\He
and
\Fi_{24}^{\prime}
. Later work has resulted in further exotic fusion systems being found on Sylow
p
-subgroups of sporadic groups in
[
18
,
8
,
21
,
28
]
. Moreover, the smallest exotic fusion system on a
2
-group is known to be supported on a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\Co_{3}
(see
[
26
,
4
]
).

Looking at
[
28
, Table 1]
, to complete the classification of fusion systems on Sylow
p
-subgroups of sporadic groups for
p
odd, it remains to consider the Sylow
3
-subgroups of large sporadic groups. These are the Fischer groups
\Fi_{22}
,
\Fi_{23}
,
\Fi_{24}^{\prime}
, the Baby Monster
\Bm
and the Monster
\Mn
. We will finalise this classification over the course of two papers. We note that a Sylow
3
-subgroup of the Fischer groups
\Fi_{22}
and
\Fi_{23}
are also isomorphic to Sylow
3
-subgroups of other (almost) simple groups. As such, this work is valuable in characterising fusion systems on other interesting groups.

The first result we prove in this paper pertains to a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\Fi_{22}
. We note that a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\Fi_{22}
is also isomorphic to a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\operatorname{O}_{7}(3)
and
{{}^{2}\mathrm{E}_{6}(2)}
. The following result is proven in Theorem

.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion system on a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\Fi_{22}
. If
O_{3}(\mathcal{F})=1
, then
\mathcal{F}
is realized by an almost simple group
G
, where
O^{3^{\prime}}(G)=F^{*}(G)
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{O}_{7}(3)
,
\Fi_{22}
or
{{}^{2}\mathrm{E}_{6}(2)}
.

A Sylow
3
-subgroup

of
\Fi_{22}
supports plenty of saturated fusion systems. As such, it is not practical to classify all saturated fusion systems on

. We shall only focus on fusion systems
\mathcal{F}
containing no normal
3
-subgroups, called

fusion systems and denoted
O_{3}(\mathcal{F})=1
.

The outer automorphism groups of the simple groups
\operatorname{O}_{7}(3)

have order

. On the other hand,
{{}^{2}\mathrm{E}_{6}(2)}
has an outer automorphism group isomorphic to

-subgroup of

-subgroup of
\Fi_{22}
to classify all corefree fusion systems on a Sylow
3
-subgroup
\Aut({{}^{2}\mathrm{E}_{6}(2)})

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion system on a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\Aut({{}^{2}\mathrm{E}_{6}(2)})

O_{3}(\mathcal{F})=1
, then
\mathcal{F}
is realized by
{{}^{2}\mathrm{E}_{6}(2)}:3
or
\Aut({{}^{2}\mathrm{E}_{6}(2)})

The next group we consider is
\Fi_{23}

-subgroup of

and
\Aut(\operatorname{O}_{8}^{+}(3))

Theorem 1.3

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion system on a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\Fi_{23}

O_{3}(\mathcal{F})=1

\mathcal{F}

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion system on a Sylow

\operatorname{O}^{+}_{8}(3)

As a reference, we provide in Appendix

The sporadic groups
\Fi_{22}
and
\Fi_{23}
are closely related to groups of Lie type in characteristic
3
. Indeed,
\Fi_{22}
and
\operatorname{O}_{7}(3)
share the same Sylow
3
-subgroup, and the same holds for
\Fi_{23}
and
\Aut(\operatorname{O}^{+}_{8}(3))
. In
[
22

, Parker and Semeraro conjecture that if
G
is a group of Lie type in defining characteristic, not isomorphic to
\Sp_{4}(q)
or a small number of exceptional cases, all corefree fusion systems on a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
can be realized by a subgroup
G\leq H\leq\Aut(G)
. These two
3
-groups are certainly exceptional cases. For
p=2
, all exceptional cases are believed to be classified following the recent work by Stroth in
[
27
]
.

In
[
28
]
, van Beek stated that a major obstacle in completing the classification for all sporadic groups would be computing with such large
3
-groups. The process of finding all fusion systems on a
p
-group
S
starts with finding the proto-essential subgroups of
S
. Prior to this work, the state-of-the-art algorithm to compute this was the Parker-Semeraro MAGMA package
[
22
]
. This code was indeed quite valuable in their work on
[
23
]
that classified fusion systems on groups of small order. The code computes all the proto-essential subgroups of
S
by first finding all subgroups of the
p
-group
S/Z(S)
.

We were able to implement in GAP an equivalent code that computes the proto-essential subgroups of a
p
-group
S
. This is more efficient than the code in MAGMA since the subgroups are computed up to
\Aut(S)
-conjugacy, not
S
-conjugacy. While this is enough to work with the Sylow
3
-subgroup of the sporadic groups
\Fi_{22}
,
\Fi_{23}
and
\Bm
, this approach does not work for the Sylow
3
-subgroups of
\Fi_{24}
and
\Mn
. In a sequel paper, we will unveil an algorithm to compute the proto-essential subgroups of a
p
-group without requiring all subgroups of
S
. This will make the process of finding essential subgroups for a particular group tractable for relatively large
p
-groups. We highlight that the Parker-Semeraro package cannot typically be used to work with groups of order
\geq 3^{10}
, but our package can be used when looking at a Sylow
3
-subgroup of the Monster group
\Mn
, of order
3^{20}
. Appendix
A
gives a brief description of the version of the algorithm we make use of here, and lists how it performs with the four
3
-groups we consider in this paper.

After computing the proto-essential subgroups, much of the classification involves identifying the relevant normal subsystems that correspond to the
3
-local subgroups we see in each simple group. This makes use of techniques in group theory such as coprime action. To prove that there are no exotic fusion systems supported on each group, we mostly make use of techniques already present in the literature. In particular, this comes from identifying parabolic systems using
[
20
, Proposition 7.5]
and showing that constrained subsystems are equal using
[
19
, Proposition 2.11]
.

Because the
3
-groups we are considering are relatively large, we will make use of coding throughout the paper. This code can be found in the github repository
[
10
]
. If a proof requires some code, we make an explicit reference to the relevant file. We remark that most of the results in this paper can be proven solely using code. Nonetheless, we try to prove most of them theoretically, relying on code as little as we can.

The notation we use is mostly standard. We write maps on the right. Groups of Lie type are referred to by their name given in the ATLAS
[
7
]
. We mainly follow the notations in
[
14
]
for group theory, and
[

]
or
[

In this section, we recall results from group theory that we shall make use of in this paper. We also establish some further results that we need. Throughout, let

Lemma 2.1

(Three subgroups lemma)
.

Let
X,Y,Z\leq G
be such that
[X,Y,Z]=1
and
[Y,Z,X]=1
. Then
[Z,X,Y]=1
.

Proof.

This is proven in
[

, Theorem 1.5.6]
.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

Let

Let

Lemma 2.4

Let

(1)

C_{A/B}(G)=C_{A}(G)B/B

(2)

, then

(3)

Proof.

(1) and (2) are given in

.

Let

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
E
be a finite
p
-group, with
A\trianglelefteq E
. Let
G
be a subgroup of
\Aut(E)
such that
A
is
G
-invariant. If
C_{E}(A)\leq A
, then
C_{G}(A)\leq O_{p}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
r\in G
be a
p^{\prime}
-element that centralizes
A
. Then
[r,A]=1
, so that
[r,A,E]=1
. Since
A\trianglelefteq E
, we also find that
[A,E,r]\leq[A,r]=1
. By the Three Subgroups Lemma, we deduce that
[E,r,A]=1
. In that case,
[E,r]\leq C_{E}(A)
. By assumption, we know that
C_{E}(A)\leq A
, meaning that
r
centralizes
E/A
. We deduce that
r
centralizes
E
by coprime action. But
r
is an automorphism of
E
, so we conclude that
r=1
.

Now consider
K:=C_{G}(A)
. Since
A
is
G
-invariant, we know that
K
is a normal subgroup of
G
. Moreover, we showed above that if
r\in K
is a
p^{\prime}
-element, then
r=1
. We deduce that
K\leq O_{p}(G)
, as desired.
∎

Let
G
be a group and
A\leq H\leq G
. We say that
A
is
weakly closed
in

with respect to

if for all
x\in G
with

, we have that
A^{x}=A
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
X,Y,Z\leq G
such that:

•

X
is a subgroup of
S\in\Syl_{p}(Y)\cap\Syl_{p}(Z)
which is weakly closed in
S
with respect to
G
;

•

there exists a
g\in G
such that
Y^{g}\leq Z
;

•

N_{Y}(X)=N_{Z}(X)
; and

•

N_{G}(N_{Z}(X))\leq N_{G}(Z)
.

Then
Y\leq Z
. In particular, if
Y^{g}=Z
, then
Y=Z
.

Proof.

Since
Y^{g}\leq Z
, we find that
N_{Y}(X)^{g}=N_{Y^{g}}(X^{g})\leq N_{Z}(X^{g})
. Since
X^{g}\leq Y^{g}\leq Z
, there exists a Sylow
p
-subgroup
T
of
Z
such that
X^{g}\leq T
. Since
S\in\Syl_{p}(Z)
as well, there exists an
h\in Z
such that
T^{h}=S
. This means that
X^{gh}\leq T^{h}=S
. By assumption,
X
is weakly closed in
S
with respect to
G
. We infer that
X^{gh}=X
. As such,

N_{Y}(X)^{gh}\leq N_{Z}(X^{g})^{h}=N_{Z}(X)=N_{Y}(X).

In particular, we have

gh\in N_{G}(N_{Z}(X))\leq N_{G}(Z).

We deduce that
Z^{gh}=Z
. But then
Y^{gh}\leq Z^{h}=Z=Z^{gh}
, so that
Y\leq Z
.
∎

Fusion Systems

In this section, we recall the background on fusion systems. Most of this is standard, and can be found in
[

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a category where the objects are subgroups of
S
and the morphisms are injective homomorphisms. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
fusion system
on
S
if the following hold:

(F1)

For every
A,B\leq S
and
g\in S
such that
A^{g}\leq B
, the conjugation map
c_{g}\colon A\to B
lies in the set
\Hom_{\mathcal{F}}(A,B)
.

(F2)

For every
A,B\leq S
and
\phi\in\Hom_{\mathcal{F}}(A,B)
, the map
\hat{\phi}\colon A\to A\phi
lies in
\Hom_{\mathcal{F}}(A,A\phi)
, where
\hat{\phi}
is the unique map that makes the following diagram commute:

A
B
A\phi
\hat{\phi}
\iota
\phi

The map
\iota\colon A\phi\to B
is the inclusion map.

(F3)

For every
A,B\leq S
and
\phi\in\Hom_{\mathcal{F}}(A,B)
an isomorphism, the inverse map
\phi^{-1}
lies in
\Hom_{\mathcal{F}}(B,A)
.

We define the
automizer group
of
A
in
\mathcal{F}
by
\Aut_{\mathcal{F}}(A)=\Hom_{\mathcal{F}}(A,A)
. This is a subgroup of
\Aut(A)
containing

\Aut_{S}(A):=\{c_{x}\colon A\to A\mid x\in N_{S}(A)\}.

We define
\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(A):=\Aut_{\mathcal{F}}(A)/\Inn(A)
and call it the
outer automizer group
of
A
in
\mathcal{F}
.

We say that the subgroups
A
and
B
of
S
are
\mathcal{F}
-conjugate if there exists an isomorphism
\phi\in\Hom_{\mathcal{F}}(A,B)
. The set
A^{\mathcal{F}}
consists of all subgroups
B\leq S
that are
\mathcal{F}
-conjugate to
A
. This is called the
\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy class
of
A
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
A\leq S
.

•

We say that
A
is
fully
\mathcal{F}
-automized

\Aut_{S}(A)
is a Sylow

-subgroup of

•

We say that

|N_{S}(A)|\geq|N_{S}(B)|
.

We say that
A

\mathcal{F}

|C_{S}(A)|\geq|C_{S}(B)|
.

•

We say that
A

\mathcal{F}
-receptive
if for all
B\in A^{\mathcal{F}}
, an isomorphism

in

\hat{\phi}\colon N_{\phi}\to S
, where

Definition 3.3
.

We say that
\mathcal{F}

\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy class of subgroups of

\mathcal{F}
-receptive.

.

Assume that

Theorem 3.8

(Model Theorem)

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion system on a finite

Lemma 3.15

Let

Appendix A
Algorithm for computing all proto-essential subgroups

In this section, we give a brief description of the algorithm in GAP to check whether a subgroup
E
of
S
is proto-essential. Here, we check every subgroup
E
of
S
(up to
\Aut(S)
-conjugacy). In the next paper, we will provide an optimisation of the algorithm that allows us to check only certain subgroups of
S
.

The code is loosely based on the MAGMA algorithm by Parker-Semeraro in
[
22
]
. In particular, for a subgroup
E
of
S
, we perform the following checks in the given order:

(1)

Centric Test
Check that
C_{S}(E)\leq E
.

(2)

Rank Test
Check that
\Out_{S}(E)
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of a group containing a strongly
p
-embedded subgroup. Moreover, if this is the case, then use
[
25
, Theorem 6.9]
to ensure that the rank of
E
is sufficiently large.

(3)

Frattini Test
Check that
[N_{S}(E),E]\nleq\Phi(E)
and that
C_{N_{S}(E)}(E/\Phi(E))\leq E
.

(4)

Radical Test
Check that
\Out_{S}(E)\cap O_{p}(\Out(E))=1
.

(5)

Lifting Test
If
\Out_{S}(E)
is elementary abelian of order
p^{n}>p
and
\Aut(E)
does not have a section isomorphic to
\Alt(2p)
(if
p\geq 5
), check that
\Aut(N_{S}(E))
has an element of order
\frac{1}{(2,p^{n}-1)}(p^{n}-1)
.

We briefly justify these steps. Steps (1) and (4) follow from the definition of a radical subgroup. If
E<C_{N_{S}(E)}(E/\Phi(E))=:A
, then

\Inn(E)<\Aut_{A}(E)\leq C_{\Aut_{S}(E)}(E/\Phi(E))\leq O_{p}(\Aut(E))

by Lemma
2.5
, meaning that
E
cannot be
S
-radical. Performing this check before step (4) ensures that we do not compute
\Aut(E)
, an expensive process. As an optimisation, we first compute whether
[N_{S}(E),E]\leq\Phi(E)
before computing the centralizer itself. If
E
is elementary abelian, then it passes step (4). But if
E
is not proto-essential, then it typically does not pass step (5). The following result justifies step (5).

Lemma A.1
.

Let
S
be a finite
p
-group, and let
E\leq S
be such that
N_{S}(E)/E
is elementary abelian of order
q:=p^{n}
, with
p
odd and
n\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion system on
S
such that
E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{F})
. Then:

•

n=2
and
p\geq 5
, and
O^{p^{\prime}}(\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(E))/O_{p^{\prime}}(\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(E))
is isomorphic to
\Alt(2p)
or
\Fi_{22}
(for
p=5
), or

•

\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(N_{S}(E))
contains an element of order
\frac{1}{(2,q-1)}(q-1)
.

Proof.

We know that
\Out_{S}(E)
is elementary abelian of order
p^{n}>p
. Using
[
23
, Proposition 4.5]
, we have the following options for
K:=O^{p^{\prime}}(\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(E)/O_{p^{\prime}}(\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(E)))
:

(1)

K\cong\operatorname{L}_{2}(q)
;

(2)

q=p^{2}
,
p\geq 5
and
K\cong\Alt(2p)
;

(3)

q=3^{2}
and
K\cong\operatorname{L}_{3}(4)
;

(4)

q=3^{2}
and
K\cong\Mt_{11}
;

(5)

q=5^{2}
and
K\cong{{}^{2}\textrm{F}_{4}(2)}^{\prime}
; and

(6)

q=5^{2}
and
K\cong\Fi_{22}
.

In cases (2) and (6),
K
contains a section isomorphic to
\Alt(2p)
. Otherwise, there exists a subgroup
K_{0}\cong\operatorname{L}_{2}(q)
of
K
such that
\Syl_{p}(K_{0})\subseteq\Syl_{p}(K)
. We further see that
N_{K_{0}}(T_{0})/T_{0}
is cyclic of order
a_{0}:=\frac{1}{(2,q-1)}(q-1)
. Let
t_{0}\in N_{K_{0}}(T_{0})/T_{0}
generate this group, and take
t^{\prime}\in K
to be such that
t^{\prime}T_{0}=t_{0}
. Fix
t\in O^{p^{\prime}}(\Aut_{\mathcal{F}}(E))
to satisfy
tO_{p^{\prime}}(\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(E))=t^{\prime}
.

By construction,
t
has order a multiple of
a_{0}
and normalizes some Sylow
p
-subgroup of
O^{p^{\prime}}(\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(E))
. Since
E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{F})
, we know that
\Out_{S}(E)
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(E)
, which is a Sylow
p
-subgroup. As such, we may assume that
t
normalizes
\Out_{S}(E)
. Applying Lemma
3.7
, we conclude that
\Out_{\mathcal{F}}(N_{S}(E))
contains an element of order
a_{0}
.
