1, The following information is submitted in response to the various

2. Major Functions or Activities . The functions of ORR fall generally
within one of the three following groupings: (a) intelligence support in
response to priority demands of other components of the CIA, the IAC, the NSC,
OSD, and the IAC agencies; (b) coordination delegated to ORR by direction; and
(c) research and specialized services recognized as a responsibility of ORR as
a matter of common concern. It is believed that all functions performed by
ORR at the present time must be continued if the Office is to carry out its
basic responsibilities. If the requirements set forth in paragraph 2.b. of the
referenced memorandum are to be met, certain recognized activities involved in
fulfilling these functions would have to be curtailed. The selection of areas
for curtailment within each activity is the only indication of relative priority
that appears feasible to this Office in trying to meet the requirements of the
referenced memorandum.

For the most part, the distribution of professional, clerical, and
administrative personnel strength on board as of 31 October 1955 is expressed
in man-years, and the strengths devoted to essential background research, sub-
stantive review, and editing are pro-rated to the direct support activities.

5- Savings Through External Research . It is generally recognized that major
benefits from contracting for external research are: (a) the ability to utilize
specially qualified individuals who are not available, for one reason or another,
for full-time employment; and (b) the assignment of tasks to be conducted exter-
nally because of lack of competence or higher priority uses of personnel on full-
time duty in the Agency. On the other hand, the cost per unit of work for ex-
ternal research is generally greater than if it were conducted by f ull -time
employees because of overhead expense, lack of close supervision, and inability
of external researchers to utilize all sources of intelligence.
