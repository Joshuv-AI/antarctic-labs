Well-posedness and existence of an invariant measure for the linearly-damped KdV equations driven by a jump noise
Krutika Tawri 1 and Roger Temam 2 and Xinwu Yang 2

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the linearly damped KdV equation on the one-dimensional torus 𝕋 \mathbb{T} , perturbed by a multiplicative Lévy noise. For any damping coefficient γ > 0 \gamma>0 , we establish the existence and uniqueness of a pathwise weak solution with values in H 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) H^{2}(\mathbb{T}) . In the second part of the paper, we analyze the long-time behavior of these solutions. This study is particularly subtle as the presence of jumps in time can significantly influence the asymptotics. We show, using the techniques of Maslowski and Seidler, that, provided the frictional damping coefficient γ > 0 \gamma>0 is sufficiently large, the system influenced by square-integrable jumps admits an invariant measure in H 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) H^{2}(\mathbb{T}) .



1.
Introduction

This article studies the linearly damped Korteweg-de Veries (KdV) equations driven by a Lévy noise. The KdV system, describing the behavior of shallow water wave propagation on the one-dimensional periodic domain
\mathbb{T}
is given by,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}du(t)+(\partial_{x}^{3}u(t)+u(t)\partial_{x}u(t)+\gamma u(t))dt\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ =G(u(t-))dW(t)+\int_{E_{0}}K(u(t-),\xi)d\widehat{\pi}(t,\xi)+\int_{E\setminus E_{0}}\mathscr{K}(u(t-),\xi)d\pi(t,\xi),\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

where the unknown
u
describes the elongation of the wave.
We will assume that the damping coefficient
\gamma
is a strictly positive constant. The stochastic integrals on the right-hand side represent different effects of a Lévy noise: The term
G(u)dW
influences the system continuously in time whereas the other two terms
\int_{E_{0}}K(u(t-),\xi)d\widehat{\pi}(t,\xi)
and
\int_{E\setminus E_{0}}\mathscr{K}(u(t-),\xi)d\pi(t,\xi)
impact the system as discrete impulses in time.

The KdV equation was originally derived as an ideal model of propogation of unidirectional weakly nonlinear waves in a channel. In realistic environments, the waves may interact with the medium that is not homogeneous. Therefore, it is natural to consider the energy dissipation and the external excitation. In this direction, we can consider dissipative effects due to friction via the linear damping term, and
the effects of noisy environment via stochastic external forcing, which can be interpreted as random fluctuations in the medium through which the weakly nonlinear dispersive waves propagate (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
39
,
25
]
). Such fluctuation may not be purely Gaussian as the medium may include some intermittent or impulsive disturbances. To capture such effects, we model the external excitation by a Lévy noise, which generalizes Wiener noise by allowing for jumps and perturbations acting on the waves.

The aim of this study is two-fold:

(1)

We prove the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of pathwise weak solutions to (
1.1
),

(2)

We prove the existence of an invariant measure for the dynamics of (
1.1
)
1
with
\mathscr{K}=0
.

Before we lay out our mathematical framework and state our main theorems, we will give a brief overview of the existing literature in this direction.
The study of well-posedness of the deterministic KdV equations and its variants on the entire real line or a periodic domain has been the focus of many works; see e.g.
[

,

There has also been extensive work done to understand the stochastic KdV equations under the influence of Gaussian noise.
In

Beyond well-posedness, the long-time statistical behavior of the damped stochastic KdV, driven by spatially smooth additive Wiener noise, has also

2.
Mathematical Framework

In this section we introduce the necessary notation and definitions.
We start by defining the appropriate functional spaces that are required for our analysis.
We will denote the one-dimensional torus by
\mathbb{T}
. Then, for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define,

(2.1)

\displaystyle H^{s}(\mathbb{T})

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}):\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{v}(n)|^{2}<\infty\bigg\},

where
\widehat{v}(n)
is the Fourier transform of

defined by

(2.2)

\widehat{v}(n):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-inx}v(x)dx.

The Hilbert space
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
defined in (
2.1
) is endowed with inner product

\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|^{2})^{s}\widehat{v_{1}}(n)\overline{\widehat{v_{2}}(n)},\ \ v_{1},v_{2}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}).

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
v\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T})
, it follows from the definition of Fourier transform (
2.2
) that

(2.3)

\widehat{\partial_{x}^{k}v}(n)=(in)^{k}\widehat{v}(n),\ \ k\leq m.

Then by (
2.3
), the Parseval identity, and the fact that there exists a constant
c=c(m)>0
such that

1+|n|^{2m}\leq(1+|n|^{2})^{m}\leq c(1+|n|^{2m}),

we note the following equivalence of norms:

(2.4)

|v|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{T})}\sim|v|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+|\partial_{x}^{m}v|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})},\ \ v\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T}).

Occasionally, we will abbreviate the norm notation
|\cdot|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}
by excluding
\mathbb{T}
.

Given a Hilbert space
V
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}([0,T];V)
, the collection of càdlàg functions i.e. right-continuous functions with left limits, from
[0,T]
to
V
. The Skorohod space
\mathcal{D}([0,T];V)
is endowed with the so-called Skorohod topology, which is separable and metrizable; see
[
2
]
for more details.

Next, we will describe the stochastic terms appearing on the right-hand side of (
1.1
) and explain the motivation behind our assumptions in the remark that follows (see Remark
2.1
). Consider a separable real Hilbert space

. Let

be a separable, complete metric space with Borel

and a measure

that is finite on bounded Borel subsets of

Definition 1
.

A

is a multiplet
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P},W,\pi)
consisting of

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})

with the following properties. The filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the “usual” conditions; that is,
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is complete and that for any
t\geq 0

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\bigcap_{s>t}\mathcal{F}_{s}
. The processes

•

W
is a

(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

•

\pi
is a Poisson random measure on
[0,\infty)\times E

, that arises from a stationary

We denote by

\widehat{\pi}=\pi-\nu
.

Remark 2.1
.

The assumptions on the noise are based on the following observation: let
L
be an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-Lévy process, not necessarily square-integrable, taking values in a real, separable Hilbert space
U
. The Lévy–Khintchin decomposition
[
36
, Page 53]
says that formally we can write

L(t)=at+W(t)+\mathcal{L}(t)+\mathcal{P}(t)
,
where
a\in U
,
W
is a Wiener process,
\mathcal{L}
is a sum of independent compensated compound Poisson processes,
\mathcal{P}
is a compound Poisson process and, the processes
W,\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{P}
are independent. Hence, it tells us that stochastic integration with respect to
L
can be decomposed informally as

dL=adt+dW+d\widehat{\pi}+d\pi
,
where
\pi:=\sum_{t>0}\delta_{(t,\Delta L(t))}
is the jump measure of
L
, which is a Poisson random measure on
[0,\infty)\times E
for
E:=U\setminus\{0\}
, and
\delta_{(t,\Delta L(t))}
is the Dirac measure at
(t,\Delta L(t))
; see
[
36
,
10
]
for more details. The jump measure
\pi
is a Poisson random measure that satisfies the conditions listed above. The corresponding stationary
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-Poisson point process
\Xi
is induced by the jumps of
L
. The intensity measure of
\pi
is
dt\otimes d\nu
, where
\nu
is the Lévy measure of
L
.
We note that
\nu(E)
might not be finite, but
\nu
is finite on bounded subset of
E
. In fact,
(E,\mathcal{E},\nu)
is a
\sigma
-finite measure space (see (
A.1
)). We define the set
E_{0}:=\{x\in U:0<|x|_{U}<1\}
. It follows that
\nu(E\setminus E_{0})<\infty
. The set
E_{0}\in\mathcal{E}
appearing in (
1.1
) is chosen such that
\nu(E\setminus E_{0})<\infty
. As an example, we can set
E_{0}:=\{\xi\in U:0<|\xi|_{U}<1\}
.

Next, let
\mathcal{Q}
be the covariance operator of
W
, and define
U_{0}:=\mathcal{Q}^{1/2}(U)
.
Then, for the noise coefficients
G
and
K
, we impose the following conditions: For each

we assume that

\displaystyle G:H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\to HS(U_{0},H^{s}(\mathbb{T})),\quad K:H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times E_{0}\to H^{s}(\mathbb{T}),

where
HS(X,Y)
stands for the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators from a Hilbert space

to a Hilbert space
Y
. Furthermore, we assume the following linear growth conditions and Lipschitz conditions on the noise coefficients in the spaces
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}),H^{1}(\mathbb{T}),H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
: There exist constants
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},C>0
such that

(A1)

\displaystyle\|G(v)\|^{2}_{HS(U_{0},H^{s})}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{1}(1+|v|^{2}_{H^{s}}),\ v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}),\ s=0,1,2

(A2)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{0}}|K(v,\xi)|_{H^{s}}^{2}d\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{2}(1+|v|^{2}_{H^{s}}),\ v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}),\ s=0,1,2

(A3)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{0}}|K(v,\xi)|^{8}_{L^{2}}d\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{2}(1+|v|^{8}_{L^{2}}),\ v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}),

and, for
s=0,1,2
we assume that

(A4)

\displaystyle\|G(v)-G(w)\|^{2}_{HS(U_{0},H^{s})}+\int_{E_{0}}|K(v,\xi)-K(w,\xi)|^{2}_{H^{s}}d\nu(\xi)\leq C|v-w|^{2}_{H^{s}},\ v,w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}).

We note, by Hölder’s inequality, that for
2\leq p\leq 8
, there exists some constant
C=C(\kappa_{2},p)
such that

(2.5)

\int_{E_{0}}|K(v,\xi)|^{p}_{L^{2}}d\nu(\xi)\leq C(1+|v|^{p}_{L^{2}}),\qquad\ v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}).

We assume that
\mathscr{K}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\times E\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
is a measurable function. In contrast to
K
, we do not impose any growth or Lipschitz assumptions on
\mathscr{K}
. The last term on the right-hand side of (
1.1
) represents the influence of large jumps from the Lévy noise. If the Lévy noise is not square-integrable, then the influence of large jumps cannot be represented without this term; see
[
36
,
10
]
.

3.
Main Results

In this Section, we will introduce the necessary definitions and state the main results of this manuscript: the existence of a unique solution and that of an invariant measure. It is standard to prove the existence of appropriate solutions to stochastic PDEs driven by a Lévy noise by first considering a square integrable jump noise and then augmenting it by the effects of large jumps using the so-called ‘piecing out’ argument. Moreover, to prove our second main result which gives the existence of an invariant measure, we discount the effects of the large jumps. Henceforth, we will set
\mathscr{K}=0
in (
1.1
) and consider the following equations:

(3.1)

\begin{cases}du(t)+(\partial_{x}^{3}u(t)+u(t)\partial_{x}u(t)+\gamma u(t))dt=G(u(t-))dW(t)+\int_{E_{0}}K(u(t-),\xi)d\widehat{\pi}(t,\xi),\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

We will begin by defining the two relevant notions of solutions to the damped stochastic KdV equation (
3.1
).
A martingale solution is a weak solution (in the probabilistic sense) where the stochastic basis, which includes the underlying probability space, the Wiener process and the Poisson random measure, is itself constructed as a part of the solution. Since the probability space is itself one of the unknowns
in a martingale solution, it is not possible to a priori specify the initial condition as a random variable and instead it is prescribed only in law by a given Borel probability measure.

Definition 2

(Martingale solutions to (
3.1
))
.

Assume that the given probability measure
\mu_{0}
satisfies

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{L^{2}}|v|^{8}_{L^{2}}d\mu_{0}(v)+\int_{H^{2}}|v|^{2}_{H^{2}}d\mu_{0}(v)<\infty.

A pair
(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}},\widetilde{u})
is called a martingale solution to (
3.1
) if

•

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is a stochastic basis
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}},\widetilde{W},\widetilde{\pi})
as defined in Definition
1
,

•

the process
\widetilde{u}
satisfying

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}\in L^{8}(\widetilde{\Omega},L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T})))\cap L^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega},L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T})))

is
(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-adapted with càdlàg paths in
H^{-2}(\mathbb{T})
,
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.,

•

\widetilde{u}(0)
is
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
-measurable with law
\mu_{0}
, and for every
t\in[0,T]
the following equation,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{x}^{3}\widetilde{u}(s)+\widetilde{u}(s)\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}(s)+\gamma\widetilde{u}(s))ds

\displaystyle=\widetilde{u}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}G(\widetilde{u}(s-))d\widetilde{W}(s)

4.
Existence of pathwise solution

In this Section we will present a proof of our first main result Theorem
3.1
. The first step in the proof is to prove the existence of a martingale solution to (
3.1
) in the sense of Definition
2
. Then we will establish pathwise uniqueness of such solutions in an appropriate class which will imply the existence of a unique pathwise solution due to the classical theorem of Gyongy-Watanabe.

The proof of existence of a solution to the system (
3.1
) is based on three layers of approximations based on regularization, finite-dimensional approximation and truncation of nonlinearities. We first regularize the system (
3.1
) by augmenting it with artificial viscosity. To prove well-posedness of this we employ a finite-dimensional Galerkin approximation. Moreover, this approximation system consists of truncated nonlinearities obtained by applying a cutoff function to the two nonlinear terms appearing on the left-hand side of (
3.1
). The truncation gives us globally Lipschitz drift terms which ensures global-in-time well-posedness of the Galerkin scheme.

4.1.
Galerkin Scheme

In this section we will construct solutions to approximations of the system (
3.1
).
Following
[
40
,
41
,
16
,
21
]
, we first regularize the equation (
3.1
) by augmenting it with a regularization term
\epsilon\partial_{x}^{4}u
,
0<\epsilon\leq 1

(4.1)

4.3.
Tightness

Thanks to the bounds obtained in Theorems
4.1
-
4.2
, we obtain convergence of the approximate solutions
u^{N}_{\epsilon}
, up to a subsequence, in the weak-* topology of the respective spaces. However, to pass
N\to\infty
in the non-linear term in the equation (
4.3
), these weak-* convergence results will not be sufficient. Hence, to upgrade this mode of convergence, we will derive (compactness) results involving tightness of the laws of the aforementioned approximate solutions. Our compactness argument is based on the following version of the Aubin-Lions theorem (see e.g.
[
33
, Lemma 4.3]
):
Since
H^{2}(\mathbb{T})\subset\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, the following embedding is compact;

(4.27)

L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}))\cap W^{1/4,2}(0,T;H^{-2}(\mathbb{T}))\subset\subset L^{p}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}))\qquad\forall p>1.

Thus, to apply (
4.27
), we derive temporal bounds for the approximations
u_{\epsilon}^{N}
in fractional Sobolev spaces. Our first observation involves the following lemma which provides the desired fractional derivative bounds in time, of order strictly less than
\frac{1}{2},
for the stochastic integrals on the right side of (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,T)>0
such that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}G(u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s))dW(s)\bigg\|_{H^{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}^{2}\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|G(u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s))\|_{HS(U_{0},L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}^{2}ds\leq C,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\int_{E_{0}}K(u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s),\xi)d\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi)\bigg\|_{H^{\alpha}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}^{2}\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E}|K(u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s),\xi)|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}d\nu(\xi)ds\leq C.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is standard. The interested reader is refered to
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
11
, Lemma 3.4]
for a proof.
∎

Next, we find bounds for the fractional time derivatives of the remaining (deterministic) drift terms in (
4.3
). Then, using Lemma
4.3
we will arrive at the desired bounds for the approximations
u^{N}_{\epsilon}

Theorem 4.4

(Fractional Sobolev estimates)
.

(4.28)

\mathbb{E}\|u^{N}_{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{\alpha}(0,T;H^{-2}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq C.

Proof.

In the previous result, Lemma
4.3
, we found bounds for fractional derivative in time of the stochastic integrals appearing on the right hand side of the equation (
4.3

4.3

(4.29)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg\|u^{N}_{0}-\int_{0}^{\cdot}\partial_{x}^{3}u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s)ds-\int_{0}^{\cdot}P_{N}(\theta_{N}(|u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s)|_{L^{2}})u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s)\partial_{x}u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s))ds-\int_{0}^{\cdot}\epsilon\partial_{x}^{4}u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s)ds\bigg\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{-2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg\|u^{N}_{0}-\int_{0}^{\cdot}\partial_{x}^{3}u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s)ds-\int_{0}^{\cdot}P_{N}(\theta_{N}(|u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s)|_{L^{2}})u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s)\partial_{x}u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s))ds-\int_{0}^{\cdot}\epsilon\partial_{x}^{4}u^{N}_{\epsilon}(s)ds\bigg\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-2})}^{2}

\mathbb{E}[I_{1}+I_{3}]\leq C+C\epsilon^{2}\leq C.

For

Global pathwise solution

In this section, we aim to prove the pathwise uniqueness for the equation (

3.1
), by an application of a result by Gyöngy and Krylov
[
24
]
, which is an infinite dimensional version of the result by Watanabe and Yamada
[
42
]
.

Definition 4

(Pathwise uniqueness)
.

We say that the
pathwise uniqueness
holds for the stochastic KdV equation (
3.1
) if for every pair of global martingale solutions
u
and
v
to
(
3.1
)
with respect to the same sotchastic basis
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P},W,\pi\right)
, one has

(4.51)

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\mathbf{1}_{\{u(0)=v(0)\}}(u(t)-v(t))=0\ \forall t\in[0,T]\bigg]=1.

Theorem 4.16
.

There exists a unique pathwise solution to the stochastic KdV equation (
3.1
) in the sense of Definition
4
.

Proof.

Let
u
and
v
be two global martingale solutions to
(
3.1
)
with respect to the same sotchastic basis
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P},W,\pi\right)
.
Define
\Omega_{0}:=\{u_{0}=v_{0}\}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})=1
, and

. We will show that

Define

Then

Observe that

\begin{cases}dw(t)+\left(\partial_{x}^{3}w(t)+\gamma w(t)+u(t)\partial_{x}u(t)-v(t)\partial_{x}v(t)\right)dt\\
\hskip 28.45274pt=(G(u(t-))-G(v(t-)))dW(t)+\int_{E_{0}}(K(u(t-),\xi)-K(v(t-),\xi))d\widehat{\pi}(\xi,t),\\

Apply Itô’s formula with

(4.53)

\displaystyle|w(t)|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\gamma\int_{0}^{t}|w(s)|^{2}_{L^{2}}ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\left(w(s),u(s)\partial_{x}u(s)-v(s)\partial_{x}v(s)\right)_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}(w(s),(G(u(s))-G(v(s)))dW(s))_{L^{2}}ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{E_{0}}(w(s),K(u(s),\xi)-K(v(s),\xi))_{L^{2}}d\widehat{\pi}(\xi,s)

We will take

Next, we turn to the nonlinear term on the left side of (

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P},W,\pi)
we can extend this solution to the entire time interval
[0,\infty)
by defining

u(t):=u_{T}(t),\;\;\text{ if }0\leq t\leq T.

We emphasize that this definition of

is appropriate since

To establish the existence of an invariant measure for the semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
defined in (

) associated with (

), we will apply the result of Maslowski-Seidler

Before stating this result, we introduce some notation. Recall that

denotes the space

equipped with the weak topology. We will denote by

We recall the following inclusions:

C_{b}(H^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{T}))\subset SC_{b}(H^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{T}))\subset C_{b}(H^{2}(\mathbb{T})).

Now we state the result by Maslowski-Seidler which gives us sufficient conditions for the semigroup

Theorem 5.1

(Proposition 3.1 in
[
30
]
)
.

Let
V
be a Hilbert space. Let
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a Markov transition semigroup on

, and
\{P_{t}^{*}\}_{t\geq 0}
be its adjoint semigroup defined by

(5.2)

\int_{V}\phi(x)(P_{t}^{*}\mu)(dx)=\int_{V}P_{t}\phi(x)\mu(dx),\qquad\phi\in C_{b}(V_{w}),\ \mu\in Pr(V).

Assume that the following two conditions are satisfied:

(i)

The semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is sequential weak Feller in
V
, i.e., for any
t\geq 0
,
P_{t}
maps
C_{b}(V_{w})
into
SC_{b}(V_{w})
, where
V_{w}
stands for the space
V
equipped with the weak topology.

(ii)

There is a Borel probability measure
\nu
on
V
and
T_{0}\geq 0
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists

that satisfies

(5.3)

\sup_{T\geq T_{0}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}P_{t}^{*}\nu(\{|x|_{V}>R\})dt<\varepsilon.

Then there exists an invariant measure for
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Our goal now is to prove that
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
defined in (

5.1
with

.
We will first verify the sequential weak Feller property of

in

.

Lemma 5.2

Fix any

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|u_{n}(t)|^{8}_{L^{2}}

\widetilde{u}_{n_{k}}(t)\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u}(t)\text{ weakly in $H^{2}(\mathbb{T})$, $\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}$-a.s.}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\phi(\widetilde{u}(t))=\mathbb{E}\phi(u(t))=P_{t}\phi(u_{0}).

is measurable. We do not assume
\nu(E)
to be finite, but assume that
\nu
is finite on bounded subsets of
E
. By partitioning
E
as follows,

(A.1)

E=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\biggl\{x\in E:m^{-1}<|x|_{U}<m\biggr\}=:\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}A_{m},

and noting that
\nu(A_{m})<\infty
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we see that
(E,\mathcal{E},\nu)
is
\sigma
-finite.
We further define

E_{0}:=U\setminus\{x\in U:0<|x|_{U}<1\},

so that we have
\nu(E\setminus E_{0})<\infty
.

Let
\Xi
be a stationary Poisson point process on
E
with a countable domain
\mathfrak{D}(\Xi)\subset(0,\infty)
and intensity measure given by
dt\otimes d\nu
. The process
\Xi
naturally induces a Poisson random measure. In this Section, we will consider Poisson random measures on
[0,T]\times E
induced by stationary point processes with intensity
dt\otimes d\nu
.

Next we discuss the space for the integrands for stochastic integrals with respect to Poisson random measures induced by such
\Xi
. Let
V
be a real, separable Hilbert space. For any
q\in[1,\infty]
, we define the space of predictable,
L^{q}
-integrable processes as

\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{q}(V):=L^{q}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times E,\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}\otimes\mathcal{E},d\mathbb{P}\otimes dt\otimes d\nu;V).

For recall that for any
g\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)
, we have

I^{{\pi}}_{t}(g):=\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E}g(s,\xi)\,d\pi(s,\xi)=\sum_{s\in(0,t]\cap\mathfrak{D}(\Xi)}g(s,\Xi(s)),

whereas for the following stochastic integral with respect to the compensated measure defined as
\widehat{\pi}:=\pi-\nu
is a
V
-valued square-integrable martingale for integrands
g\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
:

(A.2)

I^{\widehat{\pi}}_{t}(g):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{E_{0}}g(s,\xi)d\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi).

Theorem A.1
.

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
K_{N},K\in\mathbf{F}^{2}_{\nu,T}(V)
, and let
\pi_{N},\pi
be Poisson random measures on
[0,T]\times E
each with intensity
dt\otimes d\nu
. Assume that the following convergences hold
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle K_{N}

\displaystyle\to K\text{ in $L^{2}([0,T]\times E_{0};dt\otimes d\nu;V)$},

\displaystyle\pi_{N}

\displaystyle\to\pi\text{ in $\mathcal{N}_{[0,T]\times E}^{\#}$}.

Then, up to a subsequence, the sequence of
L^{2}(0,T;V)
-valued processes
\left(I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(K_{N})\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
converges almost surely to the
L^{2}(0,T;V)
-valued process
\left(I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(K)\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
.

The proof of this theorem relies on the following two lemmas.

Lemma A.2
.

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the sequence of
g_{N}\in\mathbf{F}^{2}_{\nu,T}(V)
satisfying

(A.3)

\lim_{N\to\infty}g_{N}=0\text{ in $L^{2}([0,T]\times E;dt\otimes d\nu;V)$, \hskip 20.44434pt$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

Then, the following convergence is true:

\left(I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(g_{N})\right)_{t\in[0,T]}\to 0\quad\text{in probability in $L^{2}(0,T;V)$}.

The same conclusion is true if
\widehat{\pi}_{N}
is replaced by
\widehat{\pi}
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma follows the techniques of
[
11
, Corollary 5.10]
(See also
[
33
, Lemma 4.15]
). Recall that
\pi_{N}
has intensity
dt\otimes d\nu
for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\biggl[\int_{0}^{T}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(g_{N})|_{V}^{2}dt>T\varepsilon^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\biggl[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(g_{N})|_{V}>\varepsilon\biggr]=:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{N})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{N}\cap\mathcal{J}_{N})+\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{N}\cap\mathcal{J}_{N}^{c})\leq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{J}_{N})+\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{N}\cap\mathcal{J}_{N}^{c}),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{N}:=\biggl\{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}|g_{N}(s,\xi)|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)ds>\delta\varepsilon^{2}\biggr\}.

We first handle the second term
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{N}\cap\mathcal{J}_{N}^{c})
. Due to the maximal inequality for square-integrable martingales given in
[
12
, Theorem 3.8]
(see also
[
12
, Proposition 4.16]
), we know that

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{N}\cap\mathcal{J}_{N}^{c})\leq\delta.

Next, we treat the other term
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{J}_{N})
. Thanks to the almost sure convergence (and thus in probability) assumption (
A.3
) we can choose an
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough to ensure that

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{J}_{N})\leq\delta.

Collecting the bounds above for
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{J}_{N})
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{N}\cap\mathcal{J}_{N}^{c})
, and noting that
\delta>0
is arbitrary, we complete the proof that
\left(I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(g_{N})\right)_{t\in[0,T]}\to 0
in probability in
L^{2}(0,T;V)
. The same argument still works when we replace
\widehat{\pi}_{N}
by
\widehat{\pi}
.
∎

The next result concerns with the approximation of
K\in\mathbf{F}^{2}_{\nu,T}(V)
with bounded, continuous functions with bounded support in
[0,T)\times E
which are used to define the weak
-\#
convergence of
\pi_{N}\to\pi
in
\mathcal{N}_{[0,T]\times E}^{\#}
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
Before stating our next result we recall that (see e.g.
[
26
,
10
]
):

(1)

\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
is dense in
\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
.

(2)

The stochastic integration with respect to the random measure
\pi
is only defined for integrands in
\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)
, while stochastic integration with respect to the compensated Poisson random measure
\widehat{\pi}
is only defined for integrands in
\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
. Hence, the formula

\displaystyle\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E}f(s,\xi)\,d\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E}f(s,\xi)\,d\pi(s,\xi)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{E}f(s,\xi)\,d\nu(\xi)\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in(0,t]\cap\mathfrak{D}(\Xi)}f(s,\Xi(s))-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{E}f(s,\xi)\,d\nu(\xi)\,ds

only holds for integrands
f\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
.

Thus, in the following proof we ensure that the approximation integrands are constructed in appropriate spaces.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
K\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
. Then for any
\delta>0
, there exists a process
F:\Omega\times[0,T]\times E\to V
belonging to
\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
such that

(i)

F
takes the following form:

F(\omega,t,\xi):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_{j}(\omega,t,\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{D_{j}}(\omega)h_{s_{j},r_{j}}(t)f_{B_{j}}(\xi)v_{j},

for some
D_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{j}},B_{j}\in\mathcal{E},v_{j}\in V
. Here for each
j=1,\cdots,n

(A.4)

\displaystyle\text{$F_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)$,}

\displaystyle\text{$h_{s_{j},r_{j}}:[0,T]\to[0,1]$ is continuous and is supported on $[s_{j},T]$ for $s_{j}\geq 0$,}

\displaystyle\text{$f_{B_{j}}:E\to[0,1]$ is continuous and has bounded support in $E$.}

Consequently, for almost any
\omega\in\Omega
,
F(\omega,\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous, bounded and has bounded support in
[0,T]\times E
.

(ii)

The following bound holds true:

(A.5)

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}|K(t,\xi)-F(t,\xi)|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt<\delta.

Proof.

Step 1
:
Let
Leb_{T}
denote the Lebesgue measure on
[0,T]
. Since
\mathbb{P}\times{Leb}_{T}\times\nu
is
\sigma
-finite measure on
\Omega\times[0,T]\times E
, the set of simple functions is dense in
\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
. Thus, in this step
we will first assume
K
to be a simple function and later in Step 5, we will establish the desired result for an arbitrary
K
.
In that spirit, assume that
K
takes the following form:

(A.6)

K(\omega,t,\xi):=\mathbbm{1}_{D}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{[s,r)}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{B}(\xi)v,\quad\text{$D\in\mathcal{F}_{s}$, $B\in\mathcal{E}$, and $v\in V$.}

Note for unbounded sets
B
that we can write
B=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}(B\cap\tilde{A}_{m})
as a disjoint union, where
\tilde{A}_{m}:=A_{m+1}\setminus A_{m}
for
A_{m}
defined in the partition (
A.1
). Then we can decompose the indicator
\mathbbm{1}_{B}
as follows:

(A.7)

\mathbbm{1}_{B}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{B\cup\tilde{A}_{m}}.

In this case,
K
can be approximated by the following sequence of simple function,

(A.8)

\tilde{K}_{m}(\omega,t,\xi)=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{[s,r)}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{B\cap\tilde{A}_{m}}(\xi)v.

Hence, in the following analysis, for simplicity, we assume that
B
in (
A.6
) is bounded.

To obtain a desired approximation for
K
, we will find smooth approximations for the indicator functions
\mathbbm{1}_{[s,r)}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{B}
.

Step 2
: We start with
\mathbbm{1}_{B}
. Since
B
is bounded,
\nu(B)<\infty
, thanks to
[
35
, Chapter II, Theorem 1.2]
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we might choose
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
such that

(1)

B_{1}\subset B\subset B_{2}
,

(2)

B_{1}
is closed and
B_{2}
is open,

(3)

\nu(B_{2}\setminus B_{1})<\varepsilon
.

We can define the function

f_{B}(\xi):=\frac{d(\xi,B_{2}^{c})}{d(\xi,B_{1})+d(\xi,B_{2}^{c})}.

We can then verify that
f_{B}:E\to[0,1]
is continuous,
f_{B}=1
on
B_{1}
and
f_{B}=0
outside of
B_{2}
.

Step 3
: Next, we approximate the time indicator
\mathbbm{1}_{(s,r]}
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we define a continuous function
h_{s,r}:[0,T]\to[0,1]

such that
h(t)=0
for
t\in[0,s]
, increases linearly to
1
on
(s,s+\varepsilon]
, equals
1
on
(s+\varepsilon,r]
, and decreases linearly to
0
on
(r,(r+\varepsilon)\wedge T]
.

Step 4
: We verify that

F(\omega,t,\xi):=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)h_{s,r}(t)f_{B}(\xi)v

satisfies the requirement. We observe that
F\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
, and
F(\omega,\cdot,\cdot)
is a continuous, bounded functions with bounded support. Next, we proceed to check (
A.5
). We recall that for
K
and
F
both have supports in
B_{2}
,
dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}
-a.e.. Moreover,

K(t,\xi)\neq F(t,\xi)\text{ only if $t\in(s,s+\varepsilon]\cup(r,(r+\varepsilon)\wedge T]$ or $\xi\in B_{2}\setminus B_{1}$, $\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

It follows that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}|K(t,\xi)-F(t,\xi)|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}\cap B_{2}}|K(t,\xi)-F(t,\xi)|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{(s,s+\varepsilon]\cup(r,(r+\varepsilon)\wedge T]}\int_{E_{0}\cap B_{2}}4|v|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(E_{0}\cap B_{2})\setminus B_{1}}4|v|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt

\displaystyle\leq(8\nu({E_{0}\cap B_{2}})+4T)\varepsilon|v|_{V}^{2}.

Since
\nu(B_{2}\setminus B_{1})<\varepsilon
by construction, and
\nu(B)<\infty
, we obtain that

\nu(E_{0}\cap B_{2})\leq\nu(B_{2})\leq\nu(B)+\nu(B_{2}\setminus B_{1})<\infty.

By choosing
\displaystyle\varepsilon<\frac{\delta}{(8\nu({E_{0}\cap B_{2}})+4T)|v|_{V}^{2}}
, we conclude that (
A.5
) holds, thus completing the proof for the case
K
is simple.

Step 5
: Next, we establish the result for arbitrary
K\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
. Recalling that simple functions are dense in
\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
, for any
\delta
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}\bigg|K(t,\xi)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}K_{j}(t,\xi)\bigg|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt<\frac{\delta}{2},

where

(A.9)

K_{j}(\omega,t,\xi):=\mathbbm{1}_{D_{j}}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{[s_{j},r_{j})}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{B_{j}}(\xi)v_{j},\quad\text{for some $D_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{j}}$, $B_{j}\in\mathcal{E}$, and $v_{j}\in V$}.

Here the measurable sets
B_{j}
may be unbounded. However, in that case we can use the characterization (
A.7
) and approximate each
K_{j}
with
\tilde{K}_{m}
given in (
A.8
). To avoid excessive notation, for simplicity we thus assume that each
B_{j}
in (
A.9
) is bounded.

Since each
K_{j}
is simple, following the argument given in Steps 1–4 we conclude that there exist
F_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
such that

F_{j}(\omega,t,\xi)=\mathbbm{1}_{D_{j}}(\omega)h_{s_{j},r_{j}}(t)f_{B_{j}}(\xi)v_{j},

where
h_{s_{j},r_{j}}
,
f_{B_{j}}
satisfy (
A.4
). Moreover,

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}|K_{j}(t,\xi)-F_{j}(t,\xi)|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt<\frac{\delta}{2n}.

Finally, define

F(\omega,t,\xi):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_{j}(\omega,t,\xi).

Then it follows that
F\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
and by the triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}|K(t,\xi)-F(t,\xi)|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle dt\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}\bigg|K(t,\xi)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}K_{j}(t,\xi)\bigg|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}|K_{j}(t,\xi)-F_{j}(t,\xi)|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt<\frac{\delta}{2}+n\cdot\frac{\delta}{2n}=\delta.

∎

Next, we finally give a proof of Theorem
A.1
.

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

Consider
K\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
. Then for any
\delta,\varepsilon>0
, we know, due to Lemma
A.3
, that there exists
F\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
which is almost surely continuous, bounded and has bounded support such that

(A.10)

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}|K(t,\xi)-F(t,\xi)|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt<\delta\varepsilon^{2}.

Moreover, the function
F
takes the following form:

(A.11)

F(\omega,t,\xi):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}F_{j}(\omega,t,\xi):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbbm{1}_{D_{j}}(\omega)h_{s_{j},r_{j}}(t)f_{B_{j}}(\xi)v_{j},\quad D_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{j}},v_{j}\in V,

where for each
j=1,..,m
, the function
F_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
, the functions
h_{s_{j},r_{j}}:[0,T]\to[0,1]
and
\tilde{f}_{B_{j}}:E\to[0,1]
are both continuous, bounded and have bounded supports.
By rewriting

\displaystyle I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(K_{N})-I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(K)

\displaystyle=I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(K_{N}-K)+I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(K-F)+\biggl(I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(F)-I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F)\biggr)+I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F-K),

we can split the event

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\biggl[\int_{0}^{T}|_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(K_{N})-I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(K)|_{V}^{2}dt>4\varepsilon\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\biggl[\int_{0}^{T}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(K_{N}-K)|_{V}^{2}dt>\varepsilon\biggr]+\mathbb{P}\biggl[\int_{0}^{T}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(K-F)|_{V}^{2}dt>\varepsilon\biggr]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\biggl[\int_{0}^{T}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(F)-I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F)|_{V}^{2}dt>\varepsilon\biggr]+\mathbb{P}\biggl[\int_{0}^{T}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F-K)|_{V}^{2}dt>\varepsilon\biggr]

\displaystyle=:P^{N}_{1}+P^{N}_{2}+P^{N}_{3}+P_{4}.

We will obtain estimates for
P^{N}_{j};j=1,2,3,4
term-by-term starting with the term
P^{N}_{1}
. Since
K_{N}\to K
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times E_{0};dt\otimes d\nu;V)
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., we invoke Lemma
A.2
, and conclude that there exists an
N
such that for any
n\geq N
we have

P^{n}_{1}<\delta.

Next, due to Chebyshev’s inequality, the Itô isometry and (
A.10
), we obtain for the term
P_{2}^{N}
that

\displaystyle P^{N}_{2}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}|K(t,\xi)-F(t,\xi)|_{V}^{2}d\nu(\xi)dt}{\varepsilon^{2}}<\delta,\qquad\text{ for every }N\in\mathbb{N}.

The term
P_{4}
is treated similarly and we have,

\displaystyle P_{4}<\delta.

Finally, we turn our attention to the term
P^{N}_{3}
. The fact that
F_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{1}(V)\cap\mathbf{F}_{\nu,T}^{2}(V)
allows us to write

I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(F)=I_{t}^{\pi_{N}}(F)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{E_{0}}F(s,\xi)d\nu(\xi)ds,\quad I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F)=I_{t}^{\pi}(F)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{E_{0}}F(s,\xi)d\nu(\xi)ds.

Recalling that
\pi_{N}\to\pi
almost surely in
\mathcal{N}_{[0,\infty)\times E}^{\#}
endowed with the weak-
\#
topology (see (
4.36
) for definition), and that
\pi_{N},\pi
have intensity
dt\otimes d\nu
, we can conclude that
I_{t}^{\pi_{N}}(F)\to I_{t}^{\pi}(F)
as
N\to\infty
,
dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}
-a.s. in
V
. Hence,

(A.12)

I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(F)\to I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F),\quad\text{as $N\to\infty$, $dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}$-a.s. in $V$.}

For
F
given in
(
A.11
)
, and the linearity of the integral operators
I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}
and
I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}
, we have

I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(F)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(F_{j}),\quad I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F_{j}).

Therefore, to prove bounds for the term
P_{3}^{N}
, it suffices to show that for each
j=1,\cdots,m
we have,

(A.13)

\int_{0}^{T}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(F_{j})|_{V}^{2}dt\to\int_{0}^{T}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F_{j})|_{V}^{2}dt,\text{ as $N\to\infty$, $\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

We note that the function
h_{s_{j},r_{j}}(t)\tilde{f}_{B_{j}}(\xi)
is continuous on
[0,T]\times E
. Hence, using the same ideas as in (
A.12
) and invoking the definition of
\pi_{N}\to\pi
in
\mathcal{N}_{[0,\infty)\times E}^{\#}
endowed with the weak-
\#
topology we obtain that

\mathbbm{1}_{D_{j}}v_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}h_{s_{j},r_{j}}(t)\tilde{f}_{B_{j}}(\xi)d\widehat{\pi}_{N}(t,\xi)\to\mathbbm{1}_{D_{j}}v_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}h_{s_{j},r_{j}}(t)\tilde{f}_{B_{j}}(\xi)d\widehat{\pi}(t,\xi)\\
\text{ as $N\to\infty$, $\mathbb{P}$-a.s. in $V$.}

Hence for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
there exists some
M=M(\omega)>0
such that

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(F_{j})|_{V}\leq|v_{j}|_{V}\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}h_{s_{j},r_{j}}(t)\tilde{f}_{B_{j}}(\xi)d\widehat{\pi}_{N}(t,\xi)\bigg|\leq M|v_{j}|_{V},\quad\text{ $\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

Hence (
A.13
) follows from the previous two equations and an application of the Dominated Convergence Theorem. We thus arrive at

\int_{0}^{T}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}_{N}}(F)|_{V}^{2}dt\to\int_{0}^{T}|I_{t}^{\widehat{\pi}}(F)|_{V}^{2}dt,\text{ as $N\to\infty$, $\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

This tells us that for sufficiently large
N
, we have

P^{N}_{3}<\delta.

Collecting the estimates for
P^{N}_{j};j=1,2,3,4
ends the proof.
∎
