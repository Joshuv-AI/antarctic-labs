The extended future cover of a sofic shift
Klaus Thomsen

Abstract
. The paper describes a cover of the future cover of a sofic shift which is canonical in the same way as the future cover itself. In some cases the cover is isomorphic to the future cover and in other it is a genuine extension.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary ; Secondary

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to exhibit a natural extension of the future cover of a sofic shift. The future cover was introduced by Wolfgang Krieger in
[
Kr1
]
and
[
Kr2
]
and has the remarkable property that conjugacies of sofic shifts lift to unique conjugacies of the associated covers. His methods and results were extended in a recent work by the author,
[
Th
]
, giving rise to other canonical covers with the same nice property, but Krieger’s work and his future cover remains the source of all known examples of this sort. It is therefore useful to have a way to find the future cover of a sofic shift starting from an arbitrary presentation of it. This is the first goal here, and is really not that hard to achieve: An appropriate subgraph of what is termed the subset construction on page 76 of
[
LM
]
turns out to factor onto the future cover via what is called merging, or the merged graph in
[
LM
]
.

The more difficult task is to show that when applied to an appropriate presentation of the given sofic shift, this subgraph of the subset construction is itself a cover which is strongly canonical in the same sense as the future cover is. Thus, by starting from a well-chosen presentation whose construction may or may not require merging, no additional merging is needed to get from the subset construction to a strongly canonical cover of both the future cover and the given sofic shift itself. The proof of this is based on a further development of the methods and results from
[
Th
]
where a first addendum to Krieger’s work was obtained in the form of a new canonical cover for sofic shifts. While that cover in general does not factor onto the future cover, the one we introduce here does; a result which can be considered as a second addendum to Krieger’s work and may justify to call this new canonical cover
the extended future cover
when the initial presentation is the future cover itself.

2.
Terminology and notation

Let
A
be a finite set. The set
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
of bi-infinite sequences

x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}=\cdots x_{-3}x_{-2}x_{-1}x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\cdots

of elements from
A
is a compact metric space. The shift
\sigma
is the homeomorphism of
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
defined such that

\sigma(x)_{i}:=x_{i+1}.

A closed subset
X\subseteq A^{\mathbb{Z}}
is shift-invariant when
\sigma(X)=X
and it is then called a
subshift
. We shall mainly consider
subshifts of finite type
(abbreviated to SFT) and
sofic shifts
. See Definition 2.1.1 and Definition 3.1.3 in
[
LM
]
.

The set
\mathbb{W}(X)
of
words
in a subshift
X
consists of the finite strings
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}\in A^{n}
with the property that
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
for some element
x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in X
. Given an element
x\in X
and an integer
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we denote by
x_{(-\infty,j]}
the element

\cdots x_{k}x_{k+1}x_{k+2}\cdots x_{j}\in A^{(-\infty,j]},

and by
X(-\infty,j]
the set

X(-\infty,j]:=\left\{x_{(-\infty,j]}:\ x\in X\right\}.

The symbols
x_{[j,\infty)}
and
X[j,\infty)
have a similar meaning. The same goes for expressions like
x_{[i,j)}
and
x_{(i,j]}
when
i<j
or
x_{[i,j]}
when
i\leq j
. The set of
\sigma
-periodic points of a subshift
X
will be denoted by
\operatorname{Per}(X)
.

Let
G
be a finite directed graph with edges or arrows
E_{G}
and vertexes
V_{G}
. For
e\in E_{G}
let
s_{G}(e)\in V_{G}
be the start vertex and
t_{G}(e)
the terminal vertex of
e
. We extend the definition of
t_{G}
to finite and left-infinite paths in
G
, and the definition of
s_{G}
to finite and right-infinite paths in
G
, in the obvious way. We denote the edge shift of
G
by
X_{G}
. Thus
X_{G}
consists of the bi-infinite sequences
(e_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

of edges in
G
such that
s_{G}(e_{i+1})=t_{G}(e_{i})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and the words in
\mathbb{W}(X_{G})
are the finite paths in
G
that do not start at a source or terminate at a sink in
G
. We shall sometimes refer to the elements of
X_{G}
as
rays
in
G
. The set
X_{G}
is an SFT and every SFT is conjugate to
X_{G}
for some graph
G
by Theorem 2.3.2 in
[
LM
]
.

In this paper the components in
G
and
X_{G}
will be important. Recall that a graph
G
is strongly connected when every pair of vertexes
v,w\in V_{G}
can be connected by a finite path in
G
; that is, there is a finite path
\gamma
in
G
such that
s_{G}(\gamma)=v
and
t_{G}(\gamma)=w
. This happens if and only if
X_{G}
is transitive or irreducible. In a general finite directed graph
G
, the maximal strongly connected subgraphs
C
of
G
are the
components
of
G
and the corresponding subshifts
X_{C}\subseteq X_{G}
are the components of
X_{G}
. Then the disjoint union

\bigsqcup_{C}X_{C}=\overline{\operatorname{Per}(X_{G})}

consists of the non-wandering elements of
X_{G}
.

Let
A
be a finite set (the alphabet) and
L_{G}:E_{G}\to A
a map considered as a labelling of the edges in
G
. We extend
L_{G}
to finite or infinite paths in the natural way. In particular, when
x=(e_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in X_{G}
,

\displaystyle L_{G}(x):=\left(L_{G}(e_{i})\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}=\cdots L_{G}(e_{-3})L_{G}(e_{-2})L_{G}(e_{-1})L_{G}(e_{0})L_{G}(e_{1})L_{G}(e_{2})L_{G}(e_{3})\cdots

\displaystyle\in A^{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then
(G,L_{G})
is a
labeled graph
,
Y:=L_{G}(X_{G})\subseteq A^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a sofic shift and
(G,L_{G})
is a
presentation
of
Y
, cf. §3.1 in
[
LM
]
.

We extend the definition of
L_{G}
to paths in
G
in the natural way: Given a finite path
\gamma:=x_{[i,j]}\in X_{G}[i,j]
in
G
we define
L_{G}(\gamma)\in Y[i,j]
such that
L_{G}(\gamma)_{k}:=L_{G}(x_{k})
for
k\in[i,j]
. The extension to left- and right-infinite paths is made in a similar way.

When
D\subseteq A^{\mathbb{N}}
is a subset of
A^{\mathbb{N}}
and
a\in A
, we let
\frac{D}{a}
denote the set

\frac{D}{a}:=\left\{x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\cdots\in A^{\mathbb{N}}:\ ax_{1}x_{2}\cdots\in D\right\}=\left\{x\in A^{\mathbb{N}}:\ ax\in D\right\};

i.e.
\frac{D}{a}
is the set of elements of
A^{\mathbb{N}}
that are obtained from the elements of
D
that start with
a
by deleting the first coordinate.

Let
Y\subseteq A^{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sofic shift. The
future set

F(y)
of an element
y\in Y
is

F(y):=\left\{w\in Y[0,\infty):\ y_{(-\infty,-1]}w\in Y\right\}.

The
future cover

(\mathbb{K}(Y),L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})
of
Y
is the labeled graph where

V_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}:=\left\{F(y):\ y\in Y\right\}

and there is a labeled arrow
F(y)\overset{a}{\to}F(y^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{K}(Y)
when

F(y^{\prime})=\frac{F(y)}{a}.

The notion of regularity of a labeled graph played a significant role in
[
Th
]
, partly as a tool to characterize the future cover, and we shall need it here again. A labeled graph
(X_{H},L_{H})
is
regular
when every vertex
v\in V_{H}
has the property that there is an element
z\in X_{H}
such that
t_{H}(z_{(-\infty,-1]})=v
and

\left\{L_{H}(x):\ x\in X_{H}[0,\infty),\ s_{H}(x)=v\right\}=F(L_{H}(z)).

We shall need at least two more properties that a labeled graph can have; it can be
right-resolving
, as in Definition 3.3.1 of
[
LM
]
, and it can be
follower-separated
as in Definition 3.3.7 of
[
LM
]
.

3.
Constructing the future cover

.

In this section we give a recipe for finding the future cover of a sofic shift starting from an arbitrary representation of it.

3.1.
On the merging of a labeled graph

Let
(H,L_{H})
be a labeled graph. Given a vertex
v\in V_{H}
the follower set
f_{H}(v)
is the set

f_{H}(v):=\left\{L_{H}(x):\ x\in X_{H}[0,\infty),\ s_{H}(x)=v\right\}.

The
merged graph

([H],L_{[H]})
of
(H,L_{H})
is then defined as follows, cf. page 78 of
[
LM
]
. The set of vertexes
V_{[H]}
is the set

V_{[H]}:=\left\{f_{H}(v):\ v\in V_{H}\right\}

and for
a\in A
there is a labeled arrow
f_{H}(v)\overset{a}{\to}f_{H}(w)
in
[H]
when there are vertexes
v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\in V_{H}
such that
f_{H}(v^{\prime})=f_{H}(v),\ f_{H}(w^{\prime})=f_{H}(w)
and there is a labeled arrow
v^{\prime}\overset{a}{\to}w^{\prime}
in
H
. When
(H,L_{H})
is right-resolving we can define a map
f_{H}:E_{H}\to E_{[H]}
such that
f_{H}(e)\in E_{[H]}
is the arrow
f_{H}(e)\in E_{[H]}
with the properties

•

s_{[H]}(f_{H}(e))=f_{H}\left(s_{H}(e)\right)
, and

•

L_{[H]}(e)=L_{H}(e)
.

Then
f_{H}:H\to[H]
is a labeled-graph homomorphism.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
(H,L_{H})
is right-resolving. Let
v,w\in V_{H}
. There is a labeled arrow
f_{H}(v)\overset{a}{\to}f_{H}(w)
in
([H],L_{[H]})
if and only if there is a labeled arrow
v\overset{a}{\to}u
in
(H,L_{H})
such that
f_{H}(u)=f_{H}(w)
.

Proof.

Assume that
f_{H}(v)\overset{a}{\to}f_{H}(w)
in
[H]
and let
v_{1},w_{1}\in V_{H}
be such that
v_{1}\overset{a}{\to}w_{1}
in
H
,
f_{H}(v_{1})=f_{H}(v)
and
f_{H}(w_{1})=f_{H}(w)
. Since
f_{H}(v)=f_{H}(v_{1})
there is an arrow
e
in
E_{H}
labeled
a
which leaves
v
. Set
u:=t_{H}(e)
. By using that
(H,L_{H})
is right-resolving we find that

f_{H}(u)=\frac{f_{H}(v)}{a}=\frac{f_{H}(v_{1})}{a}=f_{H}(w_{1}).

This establishes the ’only if’ part. The ’if’ part is trivial.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
(H,L_{H})
is right-resolving. Then
([H],L_{[H]})
is right-resolving and follower-separated, and there is a labeled-graph homomorphism
f_{H}:H\to[H]
such that
f_{H}:X_{H}\to X_{[H]}
is a factor map. If
(H,L_{H})
is regular, then so is
([H],L_{[H]})
.

Proof.

([H],L_{[H]})
is right-resolving and follower-separated by Lemma 3.3.8 of
[
LM
]
. To see that
f_{H}:X_{H}\to X_{[H]}
is surjective, consider a finite path

(1)

f_{H}(v_{1})\overset{a_{1}}{\to}f_{H}(v_{2})\overset{a_{2}}{\to}f_{H}(v_{3})\overset{a_{3}}{\to}\cdots\cdots\overset{a_{n-1}}{\to}f_{H}(v_{n})

in
[H]
. Repeated applications of Lemma
3.1
give a path

v_{1}\overset{a_{1}}{\to}u_{2}\overset{a_{2}}{\to}u_{3}\overset{a_{3}}{\to}\cdots\cdots\overset{a_{n-1}}{\to}u_{n}

in
H
which is mapped to (
1
) under
f_{H}
. The surjectivity of
f_{H}
follows therefore from compactness of
X_{H}
.

Assume now that
(H,L_{H})
is regular and consider a vertex
v\in V_{H}
. There is a ray
z^{\prime}\in X_{H}
such that
t_{H}(z^{\prime}_{(-\infty,-1]})=v
and
f_{H}(v)=F(L_{H}(z^{\prime}))
. Set
z:=f_{H}(z^{\prime})\in X_{[H]}
and note that
t_{[H]}(z_{(-\infty,-1]})=f_{H}(t_{H}(z^{\prime}_{(-\infty,-1]}))=f_{H}(v)
. An application of Lemma
3.1
as above shows that
f_{[H]}(f_{H}(v))=f_{H}(v)
. Since
L_{[H]}(z)=L_{H}(z^{\prime})
we conclude first that
f_{[H]}(f_{H}(v))=F(L_{[H]}(z))
and then that
([H],L_{[H]})
is regular.
∎

We note that the morphism
f_{H}:H\to[H]
is injective on
V_{H}
if and only if it is an isomorphism of labeled graphs, and this happens if and only if
(H,L_{H})
is follower-separated.

3.2.
A cover of the future cover

Let
(G,L_{G})
be a labeled graph and
Y=L_{G}(X_{G})\subseteq A^{\mathbb{Z}}
the sofic shift presented by
(G,L_{G})
. When
D\subseteq V_{G},\ D\neq\emptyset
, and
a\in A
, set

[D,a]:=\left\{t_{G}(e):\ e\in E_{G},\ s_{G}(e)\in D,\ L_{G}(e)=a\right\};

the set of vertexes that can be reached from an element of
D
using an edge in
G
labeled by
a
. We consider the collection
2^{V_{G}}
of non-empty subsets of
V_{G}
as the set of vertexes in a graph
\overline{G}
with edges

E_{\overline{G}}:=\left\{(D,a)\in 2^{V_{G}}\times A:\ [D,a]\neq\emptyset\right\}.

The start vertex
s_{\overline{G}}(D,a)
of
(D,a)\in E_{\overline{G}}
is
D
and the terminal vertex
t_{\overline{G}}(D,a)
is
[D,a]
. We define a labeling
L_{\overline{G}}
of
\overline{G}
such that

L_{\overline{G}}(D,a):=a

when
(D,a)\in E_{\overline{G}}
. Then
(\overline{G},L_{\overline{G}})
is a right-resolving labeled graph and

L_{\overline{G}}(X_{\overline{G}})=Y
, cf. Theorem 3.3.2 in
[
LM
]
.

For
y\in Y
, set

D^{y}:=\left\{t_{G}(x):\ x\in X_{G}(-\infty,-1],\ L_{G}(x)=y_{(-\infty,-1]}\right\}.

The set of vertexes
V_{\underline{G}}:=\left\{D^{y}:\ y\in Y\right\}
is a hereditary subset of vertexes in
V_{\overline{G}}
, and when we set
E_{\underline{G}}:=\left\{e\in E_{\overline{G}}:\ s_{\overline{G}}(e)\in V_{\underline{G}}\right\}
, we have defined a graph
\underline{G}
, and hence also a labeled graph
(\underline{G},L_{\underline{G}})
, where
L_{\underline{G}}:=L_{\overline{G}}|_{E_{\underline{G}}}
.

Note that we can define a shift-commuting map
\alpha_{G}:Y\to X_{\underline{G}}
such that

a)

s_{\underline{G}}(\alpha_{G}(y)_{k})=D^{\sigma^{k}(y)}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

b)

L_{\underline{G}}\left(\alpha_{G}(y)\right)=y
.

In particular,
L_{\underline{G}}(X_{\underline{G}})=Y
and
(\underline{G},L_{\underline{G}})
is thus a presentation of
Y
. Note that although we do not assume that
(G,L_{G})
is right-resolving, the labeled graphs
(\overline{G},L_{\overline{G}})
and
(\underline{G},L_{\underline{G}})
both are; in fact, this is why
(\overline{G},L_{\overline{G}})
was introduced in
[
LM
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

(\underline{G},L_{\underline{G}})
is regular.

Proof.

Let
y\in Y
. To show that
D^{y}\in V_{\underline{G}}
is regular, note that
t_{\underline{G}}(\alpha_{G}(y)_{(-\infty,-1]})=D^{y}
. Let
z\in Y[0,\infty)
such that
L_{\underline{G}}(\alpha_{G}(y)_{(-\infty,-1]})z=y_{(-\infty,-1]}z\in Y
. There is an
x\in X_{G}
such that
L_{G}(x)=y_{(-\infty,-1]}z
. Then
t_{G}(x_{(-\infty,-1]})\in D^{y}
and it follows therefore from the definition of
(\underline{G},L_{\underline{G}})
that
z\in f_{\underline{G}}(D^{y})
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

There is an isomorphism
\theta:\ ([\underline{G}],L_{[\underline{G}]})\to(\mathbb{K}(Y),L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})
of labeled graphs such that

\theta(D^{y})=F(y)

for all
y\in Y
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
that
([\underline{G}],L_{[\underline{G}]})
is right-resolving, follower-separated and regular. It follows therefore from Proposition 3.1 of
[
Th
]
and its proof that there is an injective labeled-graph homomorphism
\theta:\ ([\underline{G}],L_{[\underline{G}]})\to(\mathbb{K}(Y),L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})
such that
\theta(D^{y})=f_{\underline{G}}(D^{y})
. It suffices therefore now to show that
f_{\underline{G}}(D^{y})=F(y)
which is a straight-forward verification:

\displaystyle f_{\underline{G}}(D^{y})=\left\{L_{\underline{G}}(x):\ x\in X_{\underline{G}}[0,\infty),\ s_{\underline{G}}(x)=D^{y}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{L_{G}(x^{\prime}):\ x^{\prime}\in X_{G}[0,\infty),\ s_{G}(x^{\prime})\in D^{y}\right\}=\left\{z_{[0,\infty)}:\ z\in Y,\ z_{j}=y_{j},\ j\leq-1\right\}=F(y).

∎

The last proposition allows us to make the following identification which we shall use from now on:

Corollary 3.5
.

(\mathbb{K}(Y),L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})=([\underline{G}],L_{[\underline{G}]})
as labeled graphs.

The fact that the future cover is a factor, in a canonical way, of the SFT
X_{\underline{G}}
gives a tool to find the future cover starting from any given presentation
(G,L_{G})
of the sofic shift
Y
. In the following we shall show that when
(G,L_{G})
is a right-resolving and weakly canonical cover in the sense of
[
Th
]
,
(\underline{G},L_{\underline{G}})
is a strongly canonical cover of
X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}
and
Y
. The right-inverse
\alpha_{G}
for the labeling
L_{G}
which we introduced above and the right-inverse
\alpha_{Y}
for the of labeling
L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}
of the future cover which was introduced in
[
Th
]
will play important roles. Recall that
\alpha_{Y}:Y\to X_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}
is defined such that

a)

s_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(\alpha_{Y}(y)_{i})=F(\sigma^{i}(y)),\ i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

b)

L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(\alpha_{Y}(y))=y
.

The two right-inverses for the labelings are neatly related via the merging:

Lemma 3.6
.

f_{\underline{G}}\circ\alpha_{G}=\alpha_{Y}
.

Proof.

Let
y\in Y
. Then
L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(f_{\underline{G}}\circ\alpha_{G}(y))=L_{\underline{G}}(\alpha_{G}(y))=y=L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(\alpha_{Y}(y))
, and

s_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(f_{\underline{G}}\circ\alpha_{G}(y)_{k})=f_{\underline{G}}(s_{\underline{G}}(\alpha_{G}(y)_{k}))=f_{\underline{G}}(D^{\sigma^{k}(y)})=F(\sigma^{k}(y))=s_{\mathbb{K}(Y)}(\alpha_{Y}(y)_{k})

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
(\mathbb{K}(Y),L_{\mathbb{K}(Y)})
is right-resolving it follows that
f_{\underline{G}}\circ\alpha_{G}(y)=\alpha_{Y}(y)
.
∎
