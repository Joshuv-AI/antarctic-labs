Strong non-principality of positive codegree Turán density
Levente Bodnár, Jun Gao, Oleg Pikhurko, Mingyuan Rong, Shumin Sun

Abstract
The minimum positive codegree δ k − 1 + ​ ( G ) \delta^{+}_{k-1}(G) of a k k -graph G G is the minimum, over all ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -sets that lie in at least one edge, of the number of edges containing that set. The positive codegree Turán density of a k k -graph family ℱ \mathcal{F} is the asymptotically maximum value of δ k − 1 + ​ ( G ) / n \delta^{+}_{k-1}(G)/n over all ℱ {\cal F} -free k k -graphs G G with n → ∞ n\to\infty vertices. In this note, we establish a strong version of non-principality with respect to this density by proving that for every k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 there exist two k k -graphs F 1 F_{1} and F 2 F_{2} such that 0 < γ + ​ ( F 1 , F 2 ) < min ⁡ { γ + ​ ( F 1 ) , γ + ​ ( F 2 ) } . 0<\gamma^{+}(F_{1},F_{2})<\min\{\gamma^{+}(F_{1}),\gamma^{+}(F_{2})\}.



1
Introduction

For an integer
k\geq 2
, a
k
-uniform hypergraph
(or
k
-graph
)
H
consists of a vertex set
V(H)
and an edge set
E(H)\subseteq\binom{V(H)}{k}
, that is,
E(H)
is a collection of
k
-subsets of
V(H)
.

Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-graphs, the
Turán number

\text{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
is the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
k
-graph that contains no member of
\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph.
The
Turán density
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as the limit

\pi(\mathcal{F}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\text{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})}{\binom{n}{k}},

where the existence of the limit follows from the standard averaging argument of Katona, Nemetz, and Simonovits
[
8
]
.
For
k=2
, the celebrated Erdős–Stone–Simonovits Theorem
[
5
,
4
]
provides a complete characterization of this density. However, for
k\geq 3
, Turán problems are much more difficult. Even for simple-looking cases like the complete
3
-graph on
4
vertices, the exact value remains unknown. For more background, we refer the reader to the surveys
[
10
,
16
]
.

A family
\mathcal{F}
is called
non-principal
if its Turán density is strictly smaller than the density of each of its members, meaning
\pi(\mathcal{F})<\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\pi(\{F\})
. While no such family exists when
k=2
, Mubayi and Rödl
[
14
]
conjectured that non-principal families do exist for
k\geq 3
. This was first confirmed, for triple systems, by Balogh
[
3
]
. Later, Mubayi and Pikhurko
[
13
]
extended this result by finding a non-principal family of size two for every
k\geq 3
.

Another well-studied parameter is the
codegree Turán density

\gamma(\mathcal{F})
. For a
k
-graph
H
, the
minimum codegree

\delta_{k-1}(H)
is the minimum number of edges containing any
(k-1)
-set of vertices. The
codegree Turán number

\text{ex}_{k-1}(n,\mathcal{F})
is the maximum possible value of
\delta_{k-1}(H)
for an
n
-vertex
\mathcal{F}
-free
k
-graph, and the
codegree density
is

\gamma(\mathcal{F}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\text{ex}_{k-1}(n,\mathcal{F})}{n},

where the limit is well-defined for any
k
-graph family
\mathcal{F}
, as shown in
[
12
, Proposition 1.2]
.
Mubayi and Zhao
[
12
]
proved the existence of finite non-principal families for
\gamma
. They also asked whether such a family of size two exists for all
k\geq 3
. For even
k\geq 4
, Sudakov (see
[
12
, Page 1131]
) observed that a construction could be obtained by adapting the methods of Mubayi and Pikhurko
[
13
]
. Recently, Gao, Pikhurko, Rong, and Sun
[
6
]
completely resolved this problem for all
k\geq 3
. More recently, Lin, Sun, Wang, and Zhou
[
11
]
further extended these investigations to the so-called
(k-2)
-uniform Turán density, proving that non-principal families of size two exist in that setting as well.

In this note, we focus on the positive codegree version of the Turán problem, which was introduced by Balogh, Lemons, and Palmer
[
2
]
, and whose systematic study was initiated by
Halfpap, Lemons, and Palmer
[
7
]
.
Formally, for a
k
-graph
H
with
E(H)\not=\emptyset
, let
\delta^{+}_{k-1}(H)
be the minimum codegree among all
(k-1)
-sets with codegree at least
1
; if
H
has no edges then we define
\delta^{+}_{k-1}(H):=0
. The
positive codegree Turán number

\text{ex}^{+}_{k-1}(n,\mathcal{F})
is the maximum possible value of
\delta^{+}_{k-1}(H)
for an
n
-vertex
\mathcal{F}
-free
k
-graph, and the
positive codegree density
is

\gamma^{+}(\mathcal{F}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\text{ex}^{+}_{k-1}(n,\mathcal{F})}{n}.

The existence of the limit can be proved via a few different arguments, see
[
7
,
15
]
.

One motivation behind the above definitions is to make various partite constructions (which may contain many
(k-1)
-sets not covered by a single
k
-edge but are very important in the classical Turán theory) meaningful for a codegree-type problem. A number of results on positive codegree have already been obtained; we refer the reader to
[
1
]
for an overview of the known results on this function.
Nonetheless, many basic questions remain open.

Our main result gives an explicit non-principal family
{\cal F}
consisting of only two forbidden
k
-graphs. For
k
-graphs
F
and
H
, let us abbreviate
\gamma^{+}(\{F\})
and
\gamma^{+}(\{F,H\})
to
\gamma^{+}(F)
and
\gamma^{+}(F,H)
respectively.

Theorem 1
.

For every integer
k\geq 3
, there exist
k
-graphs
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
such that

0<\gamma^{+}(F_{1},F_{2})<\min\{\gamma^{+}(F_{1}),\gamma^{+}(F_{2})\}.

An auxiliary result that is of independent interest is a construction, for every
r\geq 2
and
k\geq 3
, of a single forbidden
k
-graph
H_{r}^{k}
with
\gamma^{+}(H_{r}^{k})=\frac{r-1}{r}
, see Corollary
4
.

2
Notation and proofs

Given a
k
-graph
H
and a positive integer
t<k
, its
t
-shadow

\partial_{t}(H)
is the collection of all
t
-element vertex subsets that are contained in at least one edge of
H
.
For a vertex subset
X\subseteq V(H)
, we let
H[X]
denote the subgraph of
H
induced on
X
, whose edges are precisely those edges of
H
that are fully contained in
X
.
For a subset
S\subseteq V(H)
of size
k-1
, the neighbourhood of
S
, denoted by
N_{H}(S)
, is the set of all vertices
v\in V(H)\setminus S
such that
S\cup\{v\}\in E(H)
.
We will omit the subscript
H
when the underlying hypergraph is clear from the context.
For an integer
r\geq 1
, we say that a
k
-graph is
r
-colourable
if its vertices can be coloured with
r
colours so that no edge is monochromatic.
For any
k
-graph
H
, it follows from
\delta^{+}_{k-1}(H)\geq\delta_{k-1}(H)
that we have
\gamma^{+}(H)\geq\gamma(H)
.
We will use the following observation of Keevash and Zhao
[
9
]
that follows by considering large
k
-graphs on a balanced vertex
r
-partition where we take all
k
-sets that do not lie entirely inside a part.

Proposition 2
.

If a
k
-graph
F
is not
r
-colourable, then
\gamma(F)\geq\frac{r-1}{r}
. In particular,
\gamma^{+}(F)\geq\frac{r-1}{r}
.

Let
R
be a
(k-1)
-uniform hypergraph and let
H
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph.
We define the
k
-uniform hypergraph
F(R,H)
as follows. Let
X
be a
copy of
R
, called the
core
, and let
S_{1},\dots,S_{m}
be all the
(k-1)
-edges of
X
.
Let
H_{1},\dots,H_{m}
be copies of
H
such that
X,H_{1},\dots,H_{m}

are pairwise vertex-disjoint.
The vertex set of
F(R,H)
is defined as

V(F(R,H)):=V(X)\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}V(H_{j}),

and the edge set is defined as

E(F(R,H)):=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\{S_{j}\cup\{x\}:x\in V(H_{j})\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}E(H_{j}).

This operation will be useful to us because of the following result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
k\geq 3
be integers.
Let
R
be a
(k-1)
-uniform hypergraph that is not
r
-colourable, and let
H
be
a
k
-uniform hypergraph that is not
(r-1)
-colourable. If
\gamma^{+}(R)=\frac{r-1}{r}

and
\gamma^{+}(H)=\frac{r-2}{r-1}
, then
F=F(R,H)

is not
r
-colourable and satisfies
\gamma^{+}(F)=\frac{r-1}{r}
.

Proof.

First, we show that
F
is not
r
-colourable. Suppose, for a contradiction, that there is a proper
r
-colouring of the vertex set of
F
.
The core
X
of
F
, which is a copy of
R
, is not
r
-colourable, so it contains a monochromatic edge
S
.
In
F
, the neighbourhood of
S
cannot use this colour but spans a copy of
H
. This implies that
H
is
(r-1)
-colourable, a contradiction. Thus
F
is not
r
-colourable.

By Proposition
2
, we have
\gamma^{+}(F)\geq\frac{r-1}{r}
. Thus, to prove
\gamma^{+}(F)=\frac{r-1}{r}
, it suffices to show that
\gamma^{+}(F)\leq\frac{r-1}{r}
.
Take small
\varepsilon>0
and let
n
be sufficiently large with respect to
\varepsilon
. Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta^{+}_{k-1}(G)\geq\bigl(\frac{r-1}{r}+\varepsilon\bigr)n
.
We have to show that
G
contains a copy of
F
as a subgraph. First we prove the following claim.

Claim 3.1
.

For any
(k-1)
-set
S
that is contained in some edge of
G
and any set
U
with
|U|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n
, the induced subgraph
G[N(S)\setminus U]
contains a copy of
H
.

Proof of the claim.

Since
S
is contained in some edge of
G
, we have
|N(S)|\geq\bigl(\frac{r-1}{r}+\varepsilon\bigr)n
, which implies that
|N(S)\setminus U|\geq\bigl(\frac{r-1}{r}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\bigr)n
. Let
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G[N(S)\setminus U]
.
For any
(k-1)
-set
T\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
, if
T
is contained in some edge of
G
, then

|N_{G^{\prime}}(T)|\geq|N_{G}(T)|-|V(G)\setminus V(G^{\prime})|\geq\left(\frac{r-1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)n-n+|V(G^{\prime})|.

It is routine to check that

\frac{\frac{r-1}{r}+\varepsilon-1}{\frac{r-1}{r}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}+1-\left(\frac{r-2}{r-1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)=\frac{\varepsilon\left(2r^{2}+2r-2-\varepsilon r^{2}+\varepsilon r\right)}{2(r-1)(2r-2+\varepsilon r)}>0,

which implies that
|N_{G^{\prime}}(T)|\geq\bigl(\frac{r-2}{r-1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\bigr)|V(G^{\prime})|
.
If
T
is not contained in any edge of
G
, then
T
is also not contained in any edge of
G^{\prime}
. Hence either
G^{\prime}
has no edges, or its minimum positive codegree is at least

\bigl(\tfrac{r-2}{r-1}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\bigr)|V(G^{\prime})|
.

We claim that, for
r\geq 2
, the edge set
E(G^{\prime})
is non-empty.
Let
E
be an edge of
G
such that
|E\cap V(G^{\prime})|
is as large as possible.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that
E\nsubseteq V(G^{\prime})
. Let
a\coloneqq|E\cap V(G^{\prime})|
. Then
E
contains a
(k-1)
-set
T
with
|T\cap V(G^{\prime})|=a
. Since

|N_{G}(T)|\geq\bigl(\tfrac{r-1}{r}+\varepsilon\bigr)n>|V(G)\setminus V(G^{\prime})|,

there exists a vertex
v\in V(G^{\prime})
such that
T\cup\{v\}\in E(G)
. This contradicts the choice of
E
.
Thus we have
\delta_{k-1}^{+}(G^{\prime})\geq(\frac{r-2}{r-1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})|V(G^{\prime})|
.
It follows from
\gamma^{+}(H)=\frac{r-2}{r-1}
, together with the fact that
|V(G^{\prime})|
is large enough, that
G^{\prime}
contains a copy of
H
.
∎

It follows from
\delta_{k-1}^{+}(G)\geq(\frac{r-1}{r}+\varepsilon)n
that
\delta_{k-2}^{+}(\partial_{k-1}(G))\geq(\frac{r-1}{r}+\varepsilon)n
.
Since
\gamma^{+}(R)=\frac{r-1}{r}
, there exists a copy of
R
in
\partial_{k-1}(G)
, which we denote by
X
.
By Claim
3.1
, the neighbourhood of every
(k-1)
-set
S\in E(X)
, even after deleting any
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n
vertices from it, contains a copy of
H
. Since
n
is large enough, we can take the edges
S
of
X
one by one and find for each
S\in E(X)
a copy of
H
in
N(S)
which is vertex-disjoint from all previous copies as well as from
X
.
These copies together with the edges to
X
form a copy of
F
, which finishes the proof of Theorem
3
.
∎

Let us define the
k
-graph
H_{r}^{k}
inductively on
r=1,2,\dots

and
k=2,3,\dots
. For the base cases, let
H_{1}^{k}
consist of a single
k
-edge,
and let
H_{r}^{2}:=K_{r+1}
for
r\geq 1
. For
r\geq 2
and
k\geq 3
, we inductively define

H_{r}^{k}:=F(H_{r}^{k-1},H_{r-1}^{k}).

Corollary 4
.

For any integers
r\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
, we have that
\gamma^{+}(H_{r}^{k})=\frac{r-1}{r}
and
H_{r}^{k}
is not
r
-colourable.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction on
r+k
. For
r=1
, the theorem holds trivially. For
k=2
, the statement follows from the celebrated Turán theorem. If it holds for
(r-1,k)
and
(r,k-1)
, then the statement for
(r,k)
follows immediately from Theorem
3
.
∎

Let
k\geq 3
. Based on the
(k-1)
-graph
H_{2}^{k-1}
, we define the
k
-graph
Q_{2}^{k}
as follows.
Let
Y
be a copy of
H_{2}^{k-1}
, and let
S_{1},\dots,S_{m}
be all
(k-1)
-edges of
Y
.
For each
1\leq i\leq m
, take new vertices
x_{1}^{i},\dots,x_{k}^{i}
and add the
k
-edge
\{x_{1}^{i},\dots,x_{k}^{i}\}
as well as the edges
S_{i}\cup\{x_{1}^{i}\}
and
S_{i}\cup\{x_{2}^{i}\}
.
In
H_{2}^{k}
, the neighbourhood of each
S_{i}
spans a
k
-edge (all
k
of its vertices being joined to
S_{i}
); in
Q_{2}^{k}
, by contrast, only two vertices of this
k
-edge are joined to
S_{i}
.

By the definitions of
Q_{2}^{k}
and
H_{2}^{k}
, for any
k\geq 3
, the graph
H_{2}^{k}
contains a copy of
Q_{2}^{k}
as a subgraph. Therefore,

\gamma^{+}(Q_{2}^{k})\leq\gamma^{+}(H_{2}^{k})\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Although
Q_{2}^{k}
is 2-colourable when
k\geq 3
, it follows from the next proposition that
\gamma^{+}(Q_{2}^{k})=\frac{1}{2}
.

Proposition 5
.

For any integer
k\geq 3
,
\gamma^{+}(Q_{2}^{k})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices, and let
A
and
B
be a balanced partition of
V(G)
, i.e.,
|A|=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
and
|B|=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
.
Define
E(G)\coloneqq\{F\in\binom{V(G)}{k}:|F\cap A|=1\}
. Clearly,
\delta^{+}_{k-1}(G)=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-(k-2)
.
To prove
\gamma^{+}(Q_{2}^{k})\geq\frac{1}{2}
, it is enough to show that
G
is
Q_{2}^{k}
-free.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that
G
contains a copy of
Q_{2}^{k}
. Then
\partial_{k-1}(G)
contains a copy
X
of
H_{2}^{k-1}
.
By Corollary
4
, the
(k-1)
-graph
H_{2}^{k-1}
is not
2
-colourable, so one of its edges
S
lies entirely inside one part. Since
A
contains no pair from the 2-shadow of
G
, we must have
S\subseteq B
.
For such
S
, we have
N_{G}(S)=A
. Hence the two special neighbours of
S
required by the definition of
Q_{2}^{k}
both lie in
A
.
But every edge of
G
meets
A
in exactly one vertex, so these two vertices cannot lie together in an edge of
G
, a contradiction.
∎

Next, we define the
k
-graph
D_{r}^{k}
for
k\geq 3
and
r\geq 2
.
Let
V(D_{r}^{k})
be the union of two disjoint sets
Z
and
K
with
|Z|=k-2
and
|K|=r
.
Define

E(D_{r}^{k}):=\left\{Z\cup W:W\in\binom{K}{2}\right\}.

Then we have the following property.

Proposition 6
.

\gamma^{+}(D_{r}^{k})\geq\frac{r-2}{k+r-3}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with a balanced partition
V(G)=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k+r-3}
, where

|V_{i}|\in\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+r-3}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil\frac{n}{k+r-3}\right\rceil\right\}
for each
i\in[k+r-3]
, and let the edge set consist of all
k
-sets that intersect each part in at most one vertex. Clearly,
\delta_{k-1}^{+}(G)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+r-3}\right\rfloor\cdot(r-2)
.
Consider the
2
-shadow of
G
. It is easy to see that
\partial_{2}(G)
is
K_{k+r-2}
-free. Since
\partial_{2}(D_{r}^{k})=K_{k+r-2}
, it follows that
G
is
D_{r}^{k}
-free, which implies that
\gamma^{+}(D_{r}^{k})\geq\frac{r-2}{k+r-3}
.
∎

By Proposition
5
, we have
\gamma^{+}(Q_{2}^{k})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
Moreover, by Proposition
6
, we have
\gamma^{+}(D_{k+1}^{k})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
Thus, in order to prove Theorem
1
, it suffices to show the following lemma.

Lemma 7
.

For any integer
k\geq 3
, we have
\frac{1}{k}\leq\gamma^{+}(Q_{2}^{k},D_{k+1}^{k})\leq\frac{1}{2}-\alpha
, where
\alpha:=\frac{1}{10(k-1)}
.

Proof.

By considering the balanced complete
k
-partite
k
-graph, it is easy to see that
\gamma^{+}(Q_{2}^{k},D_{k+1}^{k})\geq\frac{1}{k}
.
Next we will show
\gamma^{+}(Q_{2}^{k},D_{k+1}^{k})\leq\frac{1}{2}-\alpha
.
Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{k-1}^{+}(G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\right)n
, where
n
is sufficiently large.
Suppose that
G
is
Q_{2}^{k}
-free and
D_{k+1}^{k}
-free.
First, we show that
\partial_{k-1}(G)
has large minimum positive codegree.

Claim 7.1
.

\delta_{k-2}^{+}(\partial_{k-1}(G))\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+3\alpha\right)n
.

Proof of the claim.

For any
(k-2)
-set
S\subseteq V(G)
, let
R
be the link graph of
S
in
G
, i.e.,

V(R)=\{v:\{v\}\cup S\in\partial_{k-1}(G)\}
and
E(R)=\{uv:\{u,v\}\cup S\in G\}
.

Our aim is to show that
|V(R)|\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+3\alpha\right)n
whenever
E(R)
is non-empty. Suppose that
E(R)
is non-empty. Then the minimum degree of
R
is at least
\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\right)n
. Since
G
is
D_{k+1}^{k}
-free, we have that
R
is
K_{k+1}
-free. It follows from Turán’s theorem that
|V(R)|\geq\frac{k}{k-1}\delta_{1}(R)-1\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+3\alpha\right)n
.
∎

Claim 7.2
.

There exists a vertex set
Y\subseteq V(G)
of size at least
\left(\frac{1}{2}-2\alpha\right)n
such that each edge of
G
intersects
Y
in at most one vertex.

Proof of the claim.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that every set of size at least
\left(\frac{1}{2}-2\alpha\right)n
contains at least two vertices of some edge. By Claim
7.1
, the
(k-1)
-graph
\partial_{k-1}(G)
has positive codegree at least
\left(\frac{1}{2}+3\alpha\right)n
. By Corollary
4
, it contains
H_{2}^{k-1}
as a subgraph, which we denote by
X
. Let
S_{1},S_{2},\dots,S_{m}
be all
(k-1)
-edges of
X
.

We now prove that there exist disjoint edges
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\subseteq V(G)\setminus V(X)
with
F_{i}=\{x_{1}^{i},\dots,x_{k}^{i}\}
such that
S_{i}\cup\{x_{1}^{i}\}
and
S_{i}\cup\{x_{2}^{i}\}
are edges of
G
for every
1\leq i\leq m
.

Suppose that we have found
F_{1},\dots,F_{i-1}
for some
i\in[m]
. Let
A:=X\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}F_{j}
. Since
\delta_{k-1}^{+}(G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\right)n
and
n
is sufficiently large, we have

\left|N(S_{i})\setminus A\right|\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-2\alpha\right)n.

By our assumption for contradiction, there exists an edge
F_{i}
intersecting
N(S_{i})\setminus A
in at least two vertices, call them
x_{1}^{i}
and
x_{2}^{i}
. Among such edges pick one with
|F_{i}\setminus A|
as large as possible. Since
\delta_{k-1}^{+}(G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\right)n>|A|
, a familiar argument shows that
F_{i}
must be disjoint from
A
, as desired.
Then
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
together with
X
form a copy of
Q_{2}^{k}
, a contradiction.
∎

Let
Y
be a vertex set of size at least
\left(\frac{1}{2}-2\alpha\right)n
such that each edge of
G
intersects
Y
in at most one vertex.
If no edge intersects
Y
, then for any edge
E\in E(G)
and any
(k-2)
-subset
S\subseteq E
, we have
N_{\partial_{k-1}(G)}(S)\subseteq V(G)\setminus Y
and

|N_{\partial_{k-1}(G)}(S)|\leq n-|Y|\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+2\alpha\right)n,

contradicting Claim
7.1
.
If some edge intersects
Y
in exactly one vertex, take such an edge
E
and choose a
(k-2)
-subset
S\subseteq E
with
S\cap Y\neq\emptyset
. Again
N_{\partial_{k-1}(G)}(S)\subseteq V(G)\setminus Y
, so

|N_{\partial_{k-1}(G)}(S)|\leq n-|Y|\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+2\alpha\right)n,

contradicting Claim
7.1
.
Thus
G
contains
Q_{2}^{k}
or
D_{k+1}^{k}
as a subgraph, which finishes the proof of Lemma
7
.
∎

We can now formally deduce our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Take
F_{1}:=Q_{2}^{k}
and
F_{2}:=D_{k+1}^{k}
. By Proposition
5
and Proposition
6
(the latter applied with
r=k+1
), both
\gamma^{+}(F_{1})
and
\gamma^{+}(F_{2})
are at least
\frac{1}{2}
, while Lemma
7
gives
0<\frac{1}{k}\leq\gamma^{+}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}-\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
. Hence

0<\gamma^{+}(F_{1},F_{2})<\min\{\gamma^{+}(F_{1}),\gamma^{+}(F_{2})\},

which proves Theorem
1
.∎
