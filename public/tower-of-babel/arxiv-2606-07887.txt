Equality cases for the L p L_{p} -Rogers–Shephard inequality in the plane and for locally anti-blocking bodies in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n}
Matthieu Fradelizi, Auttawich Manui, Mark Meyer, and Cheikh Saliou Ndiaye

Abstract
. The classical Rogers–Shephard inequalities were extended to the Firey L p L_{p} -summation by Bianchini and Colesanti in the plane and by Zvavitch and the second and fourth authors for locally anti-blocking convex bodies in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} , leaving open the equality cases. We characterize the equality cases of these inequalities: in both cases, for p > 1 p>1 , equality holds if and only if the convex body is a simplex with one vertex at the origin.

1.
Introduction

A
convex body
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is compact and convex with nonempty interior, and the
Minkowski sum
of convex bodies
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the set

K+L:=\{k+l:k\in K,l\in L\}.

In a 1957 paper
[
25
]
, Rogers and Shephard proved the following sharp volume bound: if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body such that
0\in K
, and if
-K:=\{-k:k\in K\}
is the reflection of
K
about the origin, then the volume of the difference body
K-K:=K+(-K)
satisfies the bound

(1)

\displaystyle|K-K|\leq\binom{2n}{n}|K|,

with equality if and only if
K
is a simplex. In another paper
[
26
]
, Rogers and Shephard proved: if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body such that
0\in K
, then the convex hull of the union
K\cup(-K)
satisfies the bound

(2)

|\textup{conv}(K\cup(-K))|\leq 2^{n}|K|,

which is also sharp, and equality holds if and only if
K
is a simplex with one vertex at the origin.

To generalize the above inequalities, we begin by recalling that the support function
h_{K}
of a convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the function that satisfies

h_{K}(x):=\sup_{k\in K}\langle x,k\rangle,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

If
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are convex sets containing the origin, then for any fixed
p\in[1,\infty)
there exists a convex set
K\oplus_{p}L
, containing the origin whose support function satisfies

(3)

\displaystyle h_{K\oplus_{p}L}(x)=\left(h_{K}^{p}(x)+h_{L}^{p}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We can take the limit as
p\rightarrow\infty
to arrive at the natural definition
h_{K\oplus_{\infty}L}=\max\{h_{K},h_{L}\}
. The set
K\oplus_{p}L
is the
Firey sum
, or
L_{p}
-sum
of the convex bodies
K
and
L
. The concept of Firey sum originates from the work of Firey
[
13
]
. Of special interest is the fact that

\displaystyle K\oplus_{1}L=K+L,\qquad\textup{and}\qquad K\oplus_{\infty}L=\textup{conv}(K\cup L),

so we can rephrase the bounds (
1
) and (
2
) in terms of the Firey sum for the cases
p=1
and
p=\infty
.

To generalize the work of Rogers and Shephard in the setting of
L_{p}
-sums, it is natural to conjecture that if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body with
0\in K
, then

(4)

|K\oplus_{p}(-K)|\leq\kappa_{n,p}|K|,\qquad\textup{where}\qquad\kappa_{n,p}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\binom{n}{i}^{2},

where
q
satisfies
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, and equality holds for
p>1
if and only if
K
is a simplex with one vertex at the origin. From the fact that

\kappa_{n,1}=\binom{2n}{n}\qquad\textup{and}\qquad\kappa_{n,\infty}=2^{n},

we see that conjecture (
4
) reduces to the original inequalities of Rogers and Shephard when
p=1
and
p=\infty
.

Bianchini and Colesanti
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
were able to prove inequality (
4
) in the planar case
n=2
by making a key observation about parallel chord movements of convex bodies. Zvavitch, together with the second and fourth named authors of this paper, managed to show that inequality (
4
) is true in dimension
n\geq 2
if one restricts to locally anti-blocking convex bodies
[
23
, Lemma 23]
, which are the convex bodies for which all orthogonal projections onto and intersections with the coordinate hyperplanes are identical. Moreover, they established that equality holds among anti-blocking convex bodies if and only if the body is a simplex. Recall that an anti-blocking convex body is a locally anti-blocking convex body included in the positive orthant.

In the forthcoming paper
[
14
]
, the authors of the present paper show that conjecture (
4
) is true for
L_{2}
-zonoids, and even more, that in this setting it is possible to replace the constant
\kappa_{n,p}
with the improvement
2^{n}
.

The purpose of this paper is to fill in two gaps in the literature by characterizing the conditions of equality for the planar inequality of Bianchini and Colesanti, and the locally anti-blocking inequality of Zvavitch, with the second and fourth named authors of this paper. In the case of locally anti-blocking convex bodies, we will show:

1
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty]
, and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a locally anti-blocking convex body. Then

|K\oplus_{p}(-K)|=\kappa_{n,p}|K|

holds if and only if
K
is a simplex of the form
K=\textup{conv}(0,\alpha_{1}e_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}e_{n})
for non zero real numbers
\alpha_{i}
.

In the planar case, we will make use of a technique of Mathieu Meyer
[
24
]
to prove:

2
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty]
, and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a convex body such that
0\in K
. Then

|K\oplus_{p}(-K)|=\kappa_{2,p}|K|

holds if and only if
K
is a triangle with one vertex at the origin.

We dedicate the remainder of the paper to the proof of the above two theorems. We will give a review of relevant preliminary facts in Section
2
. The following sections, Sections
3
and
4
, cover the proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
.

Preliminaries

We refer to
[
29
,
19
,
20
]
for general background material. We write
\langle x,y\rangle
for the inner product of vectors
x,y
. For a set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
|K|_{m}
the
m
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
K
restricted to its affine hull, which we denote by
\textup{aff}(K)
. When the dimension of
K
is equal to that of the ambient space, we simply write
|K|
.

We recall the extension of the
L_{p}
-sum (
3
) to nonconvex sets, introduced by Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang
[
21
]
. For
p>1
and sets
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define

(5)

K\oplus_{p}L:=\left\{(1-t)^{\frac{1}{q}}x+t^{\frac{1}{q}}y\mid x\in K,y\in L,t\in[0,1]\right\},

where
q
satisfies
1/q+1/p=1
. Definition (
5
) coincides with (
3
) when
K
and
L
are convex compact sets containing the origin, as shown in
[
21
, Lemma 2]
. Note also that the
L_{p}
-sum commutes with linear transformations. That is, for any convex sets
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing origin and any linear transformation
T
, we have

(6)

T(K\oplus_{p}L)=(TK)\oplus_{p}(TL).

We recall that a convex set
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is locally anti-blocking if for any coordinate subspace
E
,
P_{E}K=K\cap E
. A convex set
K
is anti-blocking if it is a locally anti-blocking subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
.

One of the main tools in
[
23
]
is the Schütellung (or shaking) symmetrization of a convex body
K
in the direction
i
, which is defined as

(7)

S_{i}(K):=\{x+te_{i}:x\in P_{e_{i}^{\bot}}K,0\leq t\leq|K\cap\mathbb{R}e_{i}|_{1}\},

and is a special shadow system.

A shadow system along a direction
u
is a family of convex bodies
K(u)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
obtained by projecting a convex body
\tilde{K}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
onto
e_{n+1}^{\perp}
along a direction
e_{n+1}+u
. Here,
u
varies in
e_{n+1}^{\perp}
.
A linear parameter system along a direction
u
is a family of convex bodies
\{K_{t}\}_{t\in I}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that can be written in the form

K_{t}={\operatorname{conv}}\{z+\alpha(z)tu:z\in A\},

where
A
is a bounded set, the speed function
\alpha
is a real-valued function on
A
, and the parameter
t
ranges over on an interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. We note the linear parameter is a special case of the shadow system in which
u
lies on a line, see
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
. It was proved in
[
27
]
that the volume of a shadow system is a convex function in
t
on
I
. The proof is, in fact, based on that for each line
\ell
parallel to
u
, the chord length
t\mapsto|\ell\cap K_{t}|
is a convex function in
t
, see
[
10
, Section 3]
.

The following theorem was proved by Bianchini and Colestanti
[
6
, Theorem 2.3]
and is one of their main ingredients in the proof of inequality (
4
) for the planar case.

3
.

Let
\{K_{t}\}_{t\in I}
and
\{L_{t}\}_{t\in I}
be linear parameter systems along the direction
u
and let
p\geq 1
. Then,
\{K_{t}\oplus_{p}L_{t}\}_{t\in I}
is a linear parameter system along the direction
u
.

We are particularly interested in the case of the linear parameter systems for which the speed function
\alpha
is constant on each chord of
K
parallel to
u
, so-called the parallel chord movement. More precisely, a parallel chord movement along the direction
u
is a family of convex bodies
\{K_{t}\}_{t\in I}
that can be defined in the form

K_{t}={\operatorname{conv}}\{z+\beta(P_{u^{\perp}}z)tu:z\in K\},

where
\beta
is a bounded real-valued function defined on
P_{u^{\perp}}K
, and
I
is an interval of
{\mathbb{R}}
. We note that along the parallel chord movement, the volume
K_{t}
does not change.
Well-known examples include the Schütellung process and the Steiner symmetrization.

Finally, recall that for a convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a direction
u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, one can present
K
in the following form

(8)

K=\{x+su:x\in P_{u^{\perp}}K,-g(x)\leq s\leq f(x)\},

for some concave functions
f,g:P_{u^{\perp}}K\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 4
.

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
be anti-blocking convex compact sets such that
A\oplus_{p}-B
is full-dimensional.
Then

(9)

|A\oplus_{p}B|\leq|A\oplus_{p}-B|.

Moreover, if there is equality, then for all
i\in[n]

(10)

S_{i}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{i}(-B)=S_{i}(A\oplus_{p}-B).

Proof.

To study the equality case, we first need to recall the proof of the inequality from
[
23
]
. In
[
23
, Lemma 18]
, it is established that for any convex sets
K,L

(11)

S_{i}(K)\oplus_{p}S_{i}(L)\subset S_{i}(K\oplus_{p}L).

To prove the bound (
9
), we recall the chain of inequalities established in
[
23
, Lemma 19]
:

(12)

|A\oplus_{p}-B|=|S_{n}(A\oplus_{p}-B)|\geq|S_{n}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{n}(-B)|=|A\oplus_{p}S_{n}(-B)|,

where
the first equality in (
12
) is due to the fact that the operator
S_{n}
preserves volume and the last equality follows from
S_{n}(A)=A
. We repeat the process by applying the other operators
S_{j}
for
j<n
to obtain

(13)

|A\oplus_{p}-B|\geq|A\oplus_{p}S_{n}(-B)|\geq\cdots\geq|A\oplus_{p}S_{1}\cdots S_{n-1}S_{n}(-B)|=|A\oplus_{p}B|,

where the last equality is true because
S_{1}\ldots S_{n-1}S_{n}(-B)=B
. Now, if
|A\oplus_{p}B|=|A\oplus_{p}-B|
, then there is equality in all the inequalities of the line above. In particular,
|S_{n}(A\oplus_{p}-B)|=|S_{n}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{n}(-B)|
. But, since one set is included in the other and both are convex bodies, this implies that
S_{n}(A\oplus_{p}-B)=S_{n}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{n}(-B)
. Notice that the shakings in different coordinate directions commute for
A
and
-B
, because they are just symmetries with respect to coordinate hyperplanes. So we can use the same argument to conclude that
S_{i}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{i}(-B)=S_{i}(A\oplus_{p}-B)
for all
i
.
∎

The next lemma characterizes the conditions of equality for the bound (
9
).

Lemma 5
.

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
be anti-blocking convex sets. Then, for
1<p\leq\infty
the equality

(14)

|A\oplus_{p}B|=|A\oplus_{p}-B|

holds if and only if either
\textup{aff}(A)\cap\textup{aff}(B)=\{0\}
, or there exists
i\in[n]
such that both of the sets
A
and
B
are contained in
e_{i}^{\bot}
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists
i\in[n]
such that both
A
and
B
are contained in
e_{i}^{\bot}
. Then
A\oplus_{p}B
and
A\oplus_{p}-B
are contained in
e_{i}^{\bot}
, and (
14
) is the trivial equality
0=0
. Next, suppose that
\textup{aff}(A)\cap\textup{aff}(B)=\{0\}
. For
x_{A}\in\textup{aff}(A)
and
x_{B}\in\textup{aff}(B)
, define the reflection
R:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
R(x_{A}+x_{B})=x_{A}-x_{B}
. Then
R
is linear, preserves volume, and satisfies the relations
R(A)=A
,
R(B)=-B
. It follows that

\begin{split}|A\oplus_{p}B|&=\left|R\left(A\oplus_{p}B\right)\right|=|R(A)\oplus_{p}R(B)|=|A\oplus_{p}(-B)|,\end{split}

verifying (
14
).

To prove the other direction, suppose that (
14
) holds, and assume that for each
i\in[n]
either
|P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}A|_{1}>0
, or
|P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}B|_{1}>0
. We will show that for each
i\in[n]
either
P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}A=\{0\}
, or
P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}B=\{0\}
. For contradiction, assume that there exists
i\in[n]
such that
|P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}A|_{1}>0
, and
|P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}B|_{1}>0
. Choose
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}>0
such that
P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}A=[0,\alpha_{i}e_{i}]
and
P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}B=[0,\beta_{i}e_{i}]
. We claim that

(15)

|P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}S_{i}(A\oplus_{p}-B)|_{1}=\|(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})\|_{1},

and

(16)

|P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}(S_{i}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{i}(-B))|_{1}=\|(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})\|_{p}.

One can verify (
15
) using that

\begin{split}P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}A\oplus_{p}P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}(-B)&=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}[-(1-t)^{\frac{1}{q}}\beta_{i}e_{i},t^{\frac{1}{q}}\alpha_{i}e_{i}]=[-\beta_{i}e_{i},\alpha_{i}e_{i}],\end{split}

and that

|P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}S_{i}(A\oplus_{p}-B)|_{1}=|S_{i}(P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}(A\oplus_{p}-B))|_{1}=|S_{i}(P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}A\oplus_{p}P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}(-B))|_{1}.

To verify (
16
), use that

P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}S_{i}(A)\oplus_{p}P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}S_{i}(-B)=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}[0,t^{\frac{1}{q}}\alpha_{i}+(1-t)^{\frac{1}{q}}\beta_{i}e_{i}]=[0,\|(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})\|_{p}e_{i}].

From (
15
), (
16
), and the fact that the quantity
\|(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})\|_{p}
is strictly decreasing in
p
, we conclude that

|P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}(S_{i}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{i}(-B))|_{1}<|P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}S_{i}(A\oplus_{p}-B)|_{1}.

Then
S_{i}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{i}(-B)
is properly contained in
S_{i}(A\oplus_{p}-B)
.
By Lemma
4
, we have a contradiction to (
14
).
Then, for each
i\in[n]
exactly one of
P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}A
and
P_{{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}}B
is
\{0\}
. Choose
I_{A}\subset[n]
so that
\textup{aff}(A)=\textup{span}\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I_{A}}
, and choose
I_{B}\subset[n]
so that
\textup{aff}(B)=\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I_{B}}
. We just showed that
I_{A}\cap I_{B}=\varnothing
, which is equivalent to
\textup{aff}(A)\cap\textup{aff}(B)=\{0\}
.
∎

Next, we prove Theorem
1
.

[

]
showing the inequality in our Proposition

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
K
be a simplex of the form
K=\textup{conv}(0,\alpha_{1}e_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}e_{n})
for real numbers
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. Then there exists a linear image of
K
which is an anti-blocking simplex. Hence, it satisfies the equality case as proved in
[
23
, Lemma 23]
.

We now prove the other direction. Let
K
be a locally anti-blocking convex body satisfying the equality case of Theorem
1
. Since
K
is full-dimensional, there exists
\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
such that
K_{\delta}
is a convex body where
K_{\delta}:=\{x\in K:\delta_{i}x_{i}\geq 0\}
. By applying a linear transformation, we may assume without loss of generality that
\delta=(1,\dots,1)
. It follows from the proof of inequality recalled in the appendix that
K
must satisfy the following condition, corresponding to the equality cases in (
57
): for each
\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
, one has

(17)

|K_{\delta}\oplus_{p}-(K_{-\delta})|=|K_{\delta}\oplus_{p}K_{-\delta}|.

Using Lemma
5
, we obtain that
\textup{aff}(K_{-\delta})=\{0\}
and hence
K
is anti-blocking.
This completes the proof, since equality among anti-blocking convex bodies is attained only for simplices.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2

Let
e_{i}^{+}:=\{x:\langle x,e_{i}\rangle\geq 0\}
and
e_{i}^{-}:=\{x:\langle x,e_{i}\rangle\leq 0\}
denote the closed half-spaces that are orthogonal to
e_{i}
. If
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a convex set, define
K^{+}:=K\cap e_{2}^{+}
and
K^{-}:=K\cap e_{2}^{-}
to be the “upper” and “lower” parts of
K
with respect to the line
\{x:\langle x,e_{2}\rangle=0\}
.

Lemma 6
.

Fix
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be convex sets such that
P_{e_{2}^{\perp}}A=A\cap e_{2}^{\perp}
and
P_{e_{2}^{\perp}}B=B\cap e_{2}^{\perp}
. Then,

(18)

(A\oplus_{p}B)^{+}=A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}\quad\text{and}\quad(A\oplus_{p}B)^{-}=A^{-}\oplus_{p}B^{-}.

Proof.

We will concentrate on the first relation. Once we finish the proof, we can prove the second relation by interchanging the roles of
+
and
-
. Let
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}
. We use (
5
) to find

u\in[0,1]
,
(x,s)\in A^{+}
, and
(y,t)\in B^{+}
such that

z=u^{1/q}(x,s)+(1-u)^{1/q}(y,t).

Since
s
and
t
are nonnegative, we have
z_{2}=u^{1/q}s+(1-u)^{1/q}t\geq 0
, so that
z\in(A\oplus_{p}B)^{+}
. That is, we have the containment
(A\oplus_{p}B)^{+}\supset A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}
.

To prove the opposite containment, let
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in(A\oplus_{p}B)^{+}
. Then
z_{2}\geq 0
, and it follows from (
5
) that
z=u^{1/q}(x,s)+(1-u)^{1/q}(y,t)
for
u\in[0,1]
,
(x,s)\in A
, and
(y,t)\in B
. We consider the following cases. First, if
u=0
, then
z\in A^{+}\subset A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}
and when
u=1
,
z\in B^{+}\subset A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}
. We assume that
u\in(0,1)
.

Case 1:

If
s\geq 0
and
t\geq 0
, then
z\in A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}
, which is what we want.

Case 2:

If
s\leq 0
and
t\leq 0
, then

z_{2}=u^{1/q}s+(1-u)^{1/q}t=0
,
and so we have
s=t=0
, which implies
z\in A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}
.

Case 3:

If
s\geq 0
and
t\leq 0
,
set
s^{\prime}=u^{-1/q}z_{2}
. We get that
0\leq s^{\prime}\leq s
. The assumption
P_{e_{2}^{\bot}}A=A\cap e_{2}^{\bot}
and the convexity of
A^{+}
imply that the interval
\{x\}\times[0,s]
is contained in
A^{+}
, and therefore that
(x,s^{\prime})\in A^{+}
. Then

z=u^{1/q}(x,s^{\prime})+(1-u)^{1/q}(y,0)\in A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}.

We do not need to consider a fourth case because if
(x,s)\in A^{-}
and
(y,t)\in B^{+}
, we can use the same argument as case 3 to show that
z\in A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}
.

The above cases establish the containment
(A\oplus_{p}B)^{+}\subset A^{+}\oplus_{p}B^{+}
.
∎

Using Lemma
6
, we will prove the following generalization of (
4
)
in the case
n=2
.

Proposition 7
.

Fix
p\in(1,\infty)
, and let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be convex sets such that

(19)

P_{e_{2}^{\perp}}A=A\cap e_{2}^{\perp}=P_{e_{2}^{\perp}}B=B\cap e_{2}^{\perp}=[-c,d]

for some
c,d\geq 0
. Then,

(20)

|A\oplus_{p}-B|\leq\frac{\kappa_{2,p}}{2}\left(|A|+|B|\right).

Proof.

Use Lemma
6

Proposition 10
.
