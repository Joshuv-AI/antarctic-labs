Project Title: Development and Validation of a Python Workflow for ZYGO Double-
Sided Thickness Measurement

Poster Author: Gillian Woo

Peer Reviewer: Sarasvati Satyanarayana

The main purpose of Gillian’s project was to convert and expand pre-existing MATLAB
scripts for the ZYGO interferometer machine into Python. The project had a clear focus and
was very informative. The presenter explained that MATLAB was difficult to work with, while
Python is more accessible because many tutorials and resources are available. She
described how she converted and improved existing MATLAB code, the troubleshooting
involved, and the features she developed independently. The project was well researched,
and the presenter demonstrated strong familiarity with both Python and MATLAB.

She explained how the ZYGO instrument uses lasers to collect surface data from both
sides of a sample and calculate thickness, flatness, and parallelism. The software aligns
the top and bottom scans using fiducials, levels the images, converts the data into
matrices, and calculates distances between corresponding points. She also discussed
creating heat maps to display thickness variation, and the development of flexible regions
of interest that can include ellipses, rectangles, and polygons. Although she provided a lot
of context, more background information on why measurements such as flatness and
parallelism are important for NIF target fabrication would have strengthened the
presentation.

The poster was also very easy to follow. The abstract and introduction were located on the
left, and the remaining sections presented the project in chronological order. The
subsection headings were informative and clear, even without the presenter’s explanation.
The visual design was also effective. Tables helped organize the information and made the
poster easier to read than if it had contained only large blocks of text. The layout was clear,
and the text was large enough to read from a distance.

The presenter was well rehearsed without sounding robotic. She demonstrated a strong
understanding of the poster and explained the challenges involved, including debugging,
angle measurements, and unit conversions between files. She spoke at a good pace, made
eye contact, and paused between sections so the audience could follow the technical
details. Although she did not use notes, she referred to the poster when necessary and
pointed out relevant figures, which made the presentation feel natural rather than scripted.
She also did a great job of reiterating concepts that might have been unclear. I asked what
the ZYGO machine is typically used for, and she explained that it measures thin, micron-
scale films to evaluate thickness and other features before they are incorporated into
target lenses. I also asked which parts of the workflow were created from scratch instead

of converted from MATLAB. She explained that the thickness calculation was converted
but heavily revised, while the user interface and flatness code were developed from
scratch.

Overall, the presentation was informative, well organized, and visually effective. The
presenter clearly understood both the technical challenges and the purpose of the project.
