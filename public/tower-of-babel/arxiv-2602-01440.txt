Lifting systems for finite length modules
Benjamin Katz, Nawaj KC, Kesavan Mohana Sundaram, Andrew J. Soto Levins, Ryan Watson

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with lifting modules along a surjective map of noetherian local rings, say φ : R ↠ S \varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S . A finitely generated R R -module L L is a naive lift of an S S -module M M if L ⊗ R S ≅ M L\otimes_{R}S\cong M . We are concerned with the maximum depth and dimension among all naive lifts of M M , which we call the liftable depth and liftable dimension , respectively, of M M along φ \varphi . We approach this via a notion of lifting systems that we introduce in this paper. We then provide a necessary and sufficient condition for a module of finite length to lift and Serre lift to a regular local ring in terms of lifting systems.

1.
Introduction

Given a surjection of noetherian local rings
\varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S
and a finitely generated
S
-module
M
, there exists a finitely generated
R
-module
L
such that
L\otimes_{R}S\cong M
; for example, we can lift a presentation matrix of
M
to
R
and take
L
to be its cokernel. We call such an
L
a
naive lift
of
M
. Since there are choices on how we can lift a presentation, an
S
-module may admit many non-isomorphic naive lifts. The problem is to determine how large the depth and dimension of a naive lift of a given
S
-module can be.

That is, we consider two invariants of an
S
-module
M
, with respect to
\varphi
, called
liftable depth
and
liftable dimension
, denoted as
\operatorname{lift.depth}_{\varphi}(M)
and
\operatorname{lift.dim}_{\varphi}(M)
. These are defined to be the maximum depth and dimension among all naive lifts of
M
respectively. For the remainder of this section, we assume that
S
has finite projective dimension over
R
and that
M
is a finite length
S
-module.

By a formula of Auslander
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
, it follows that

(1)

\operatorname{lift.depth}_{\varphi}(M)\leq\depth R-\depth S,

and a module that achieves this upper bound admits a
lift
to
R
; see
3.1
. That is, there exists a finitely generated
R
-module
L
such that
L\otimes_{R}S\cong M
and
{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(L,S)=0
for
i>0
. In this case, we say
M
is a
liftable

S
-module to
R
. Note that when
M
lifts to some
R
-module
L
, the minimal free resolution of
M
lifts to the minimal free resolution of
L
. This notion of lifting has been studied in a number of works
[
3
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
19
]
.

On the other hand, it seems to be a more difficult problem to determine a uniform upper bound for the liftable dimension, at least at this level of generality. Conjecturally, the answer is

(2)

\operatorname{lift.dim}_{\varphi}(M)\leq\dim R-\dim S,

which is a consequence of a long standing conjecture of Peskine–Szpiro
[
19
, Ch.II, 0.2]
. The dimension inequality of Serre
[
23
, Ch.V, Thm.3]
establishes
Eq.

2
when
R
is regular and the New Intersection theorem
[
20
]
establishes it when we assume that
S
is also Cohen–Macaulay. It is also known in the case when
R
is a hypersurface due to Hochster
[
12
]
. However, beyond these cases, we do not know if
Eq.

2
is true.

By definition, an
S
-module
M
for which
\operatorname{lift.dim}_{\varphi}(M)
achieves this conjectural upper bound is said to be
Serre liftable
to
R
; this is a new notion of lifting introduced and studied in
[
16
,
15
]
. This family of modules enjoys some remarkable properties: for example, if a finite length module
M
over
S
admits a Serre lift to some unramified regular local ring
R
, then
\ell_{S}(M)\geq e(S)
, where
e(S)
denotes the Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity of
S

[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
.

The connection between classical liftability and Serre liftability lies in the following curious fact: when
R
is regular or when we are in the standard graded setting with
M
of finite projective dimension, if
\operatorname{lift.depth}_{\varphi}(M)
achieves the maximum possible value in
Eq.

1
, then so does
\operatorname{lift.dim}_{\varphi}(M)
in
Eq.

2
; that is, liftable modules to a regular ring are Serre liftable
[
15
, Proposition 1.6]
and likewise in the graded setting
[
16
, Thm 2.2.8]
. We do not know to what extent the regularity or graded hypothesis is needed; see
[
16
, Question 2.2.12]
. But there exist unliftable modules, of finite and infinite projective dimension, that admit Serre lifts
[
15
, Example 1.7 & 3.2]
and
Section

5.1
.

New results

In this paper we establish lower bounds on liftable depth and liftable dimension using a notion of
lifting systems
for finite length modules. We draw our inspiration from a deformation-theoretic idea of lifting modules used by Auslander–Ding–Solberg in characterizing
liftable modules
along a hypersurface surjection
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
. The key new idea is the following definition.

Definition A
.

Given a surjection of noetherian local rings
\varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S=R/\mathfrak{a}
and a finite length
S
-module
M
, a collection of finite length
R
-modules
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a lifting system for
M
along
\varphi
if
M_{1}=M
,
\mathfrak{a}^{n}\subseteq{\operatorname{ann}}_{R}M_{n}
, and
M_{n+1}/\mathfrak{a}^{n}M_{n+1}\cong M_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. The
R
-module
L=\varprojlim M_{n}
is called the lift associated to the system.

This terminology comes from the fact that if
R
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete and
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a lifting system for
M
along
\varphi
with the associated lift
L
, we have that
L\otimes_{R}S\cong M
. That is,
L
is a naive lift of the
S
-module
M
; see
Proposition

2.1
. Our main results give lower bounds for depth and dimension of
L
in terms of invariants depending on the finite length modules in the system
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
.

In the following result, when we write
\ell_{R}(R/I_{n})\approx n^{r}
, we mean that there are polynomials
f
and
g
of degree
r
such that

f(n)\leq\ell_{R}(R/I_{n})\leq g(n)

for
n\gg 0
.

Theorem B
.

Consider a surjection
\varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S=R/\mathfrak{a}
of noetherian local rings where
R
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete and let
r
and
d
be nonnegative integers. Then the following hold.

(1)

If
S
has finite projective dimension over
R
, then a finite length
S
-module
M
satisfies
\operatorname{lift.depth}_{\varphi}(M)\geq r
if and only if
M
admits a lifting system
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
along
\varphi
such that
\varprojlim_{n}{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(M_{n},S)=0
for all
i>{\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}S-r
. In this case,
L=\varprojlim M_{n}
is a naive lift such that
\depth L\geq r
.

(2)

A finite length
S
-module
M
satisfies
\operatorname{lift.dim}_{\varphi}(M)\geq r
if and only if it admits a lifting system
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
along
\varphi
such that
\ell_{R}(R/I_{n})\approx n^{d}
, where
I_{n}=\Fitt^{R}_{0}(M_{n})
is the zeroth Fitting ideal of
M_{n}
over
R
and
d\geq r
. In this case,
L=\varprojlim M_{n}
is a naive lift such that
\dim L\geq r
. Moreover, if
\dim{L}=r
, then
d=r
.

As corollaries to this theorem, we characterize when a finite length module over a local ring is liftable and when it is Serre liftable to a regular local ring; see
Corollary

3.3
and
Corollary

4.5
. Utilizing the methods introduced in this paper, in
Section

5
we produce unliftable modules of finite length and finite projective dimension that are Serre liftable and modules that intersect properly; see
Theorem

5.2
and
Theorem

5.4
respectively.

2.
Lifting systems

In this section we establish properties of lifting systems, our main result in this direction is the following. We write
\mu_{R}(-)
for the minimal number of generators of a finitely generated
R
-module.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S=R/\mathfrak{a}
be a surjection of noetherian local rings where
R
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete and let
M
be a finite length
S
-module. Assume that
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a lifting system for
M=M_{1}
along
\varphi
, and let
L=\varprojlim M_{n}
denote the associated lift of this system; see
A
. Then
L
satisfies the following:

(1)

L\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{a}^{n}\cong M_{n}
for each
n\geq 1
, and

(2)

L
is a finitely generated
R
-module with
\mu_{R}(L)=\mu_{R}(M_{n})
for each
n\geq 1
and

{\operatorname{ann}}_{R}(L)=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}{\operatorname{ann}}_{R}(M_{n}).

We prove this proposition in
Appendix

A
. We now describe more explictly the presentation matrices of the modules in a lifting system; see
Lemma

2.3
.

2.2
.

Suppose that
\varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S=R/\mathfrak{a}
is a surjection of noetherian local rings. For each
n\geq 1
, let
A_{n}
denote the row matrix with the minimal set of generators of
\mathfrak{a}^{n}
as its entries. And for
\mu\geq 1
, we write
A_{n}^{\oplus\mu}
for the diagonal block matrix of size
\mu
with
A_{n}
on its diagonal.

Let
M_{1}=M
be an
S
-module with a presentation matrix
\phi
and suppose that
\mu_{S}(M)=\mu
. Equivalently,
\phi
is a
\mu\times m
matrix over
S
for some
m\geq 0
. By abusing notation, we also view
\phi
as a matrix over
R
by naively lifting its entries. The following is a presentation of
M_{1}=M
as an
R
-module.

\phi_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\phi&(A_{1})^{\oplus\mu}\end{array}\right)

If
M_{2}
is an
R/\mathfrak{a}^{2}
-module such that
M_{2}/\mathfrak{a}M_{2}\cong M_{1}
, then we can write a presentation matrix of
M_{2}
as an
R
-module as follows.

\phi_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\phi+\sigma_{1}&(A_{2})^{\oplus\mu}\end{array}\right),

where
\sigma_{1}
is a matrix the same size as
\phi
and its entries are either zero or in
\mathfrak{a}\setminus\mathfrak{a}^{2}
. Inductively, at the
n
’th stage, we can write a presentation matrix of
M_{n}
as an
R
-module as follows

\phi_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\phi+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}&(A_{n})^{\oplus\mu}\end{array}\right),

where for each
j
,
\sigma_{j}
is a matrix the same size as
\phi
and its entries are either zero or in
\mathfrak{a}^{j}\setminus\mathfrak{a}^{j+1}
. We record the following as a lemma for future use.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
\varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S=R/\mathfrak{a}
is a surjection of noetherian local rings. If
M
is an
S
-module with a presentation matrix
\phi
, then a lifting system
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
for
M
along
\varphi
satisfies:

(1)

For each
n\geq 1
, the following is a presentation matrix for
M_{n}
as an
R
-module

\phi_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\phi+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{j}&(A_{n})^{\oplus\mu}\end{array}\right),

where
\mu=\mu_{S}(M)
, and for each
j\geq 1
,
\sigma_{j}
is a matrix the same size as
\phi
and its entries are either zero or in
\mathfrak{a}^{j}\setminus\mathfrak{a}^{j+1}
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{a}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
, then for each
j\geq 1
, we can write

\sigma_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sigma_{j,k}g_{k}

where
\sigma_{j,k}
is a matrix the same size as
\sigma_{j}
with each entry in
\mathfrak{a}^{j-1}
for all
j\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq d
.

(3)

The following matrix

\Phi\coloneqq\phi+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}

is well-defined over
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
and
L=\coker\Phi\cong\varprojlim M_{n}
.

Proof.

The discussion preceding this lemma shows (1). For (2), we note that each entry of
\sigma_{j}
is either zero or in
\mathfrak{a}^{j}
, so we may write each of its entries as a sum
\sum_{k=1}^{d}r_{i}g_{i}
where each
r_{i}\in\mathfrak{a}^{j-1}.
This suffices to show (2).

For (3), clearly
L
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module and thus
L
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete. Therefore,

L\cong\varprojlim L/\mathfrak{a}^{n}L\cong\varprojlim M_{n},

where the first isomorphism is because
L
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete and second isomorphism is because
L/\mathfrak{a}^{n}L\cong\coker(\phi_{n})\cong M_{n}
.
∎

The following is an explicit example demonstrating the conclusion of this lemma.

Example 2.4
.

Consider
\varphi\colon R\to S=R/\mathfrak{a}
where
R=k[[x,y,z]]
and
\mathfrak{a}=(x^{2},y^{2})
. Let
M_{1}=M
be the
S
-module that has the following as a presentation matrix

\phi=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}z&y\\
y&z\end{array}\right).

For each
j\geq 1
, set

\sigma_{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
x^{2j}&0\end{array}\right)\text{ if }j\text{ is even, and }\sigma_{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&x^{2j}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\text{ if }j\text{ is odd.}

Clearly, for any
j\geq 1
,
\sigma_{j}
has entries that either zero or in
\mathfrak{a}^{j}\setminus\mathfrak{a}^{j+1}
. Now as an
R
-module,
\phi_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\phi+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{j}&(A_{n})^{\oplus 2}\end{array}\right)
is a presentation matrix for
M_{n}
. Writing this explicitly, for all
n\geq 3
, when
n
is even,
M_{n}
is the cokernel of the map

R^{2n+4}\xrightarrow{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cccccccc}z&s&x^{2n}&x^{2n-2}y^{2}&\cdots&y^{2n}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
t&z&0&0&\cdots&0&x^{2n}&x^{2n-2}y^{2}&\cdots&y^{2n}\end{array}\right]}R^{2},

where
s=y+x^{2}+x^{6}+\cdots+x^{2n-2}
and
t=y+x^{4}+x^{8}+\cdots+x^{2n-4}
. Likewise, when
n
is odd,
M_{n}
is the cokernel of the map

R^{2n+4}\xrightarrow{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cccccccc}z&s&x^{2n}&x^{2n-2}y^{2}&\cdots&y^{2n}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
t&z&0&0&\cdots&0&x^{2n}&x^{2n-2}y^{2}&\cdots&y^{2n}\end{array}\right]}R^{2},

where
s=y+x^{2}+x^{6}+\cdots+x^{2n-4}
and
t=y+x^{4}+x^{8}+\cdots+x^{2n-2}.
Finally, the associated lift of the lifting system considered is the
R
-module
L
which is isomorphic to the cokernel of

R^{2}\xrightarrow{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}z&y+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x^{4i-2}\\
y+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x^{4i}&z\end{array}\right]}R^{2}.

3.
Liftable depth

In this section we prove
B
(1) and
Corollary

3.3
. First we recall a formula of Auslander and a case for when Tor commutes with inverse limits. For the next two remarks,
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S=R/\mathfrak{a}
is a surjection of noetherian local rings.

3.1
.

Let
M
and
N
be finitely generated
R
-modules. If
M
has finite projective dimension over
R
and
M\otimes_{R}N
has finite length,
Auslander’s depth formula
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
establishes

\depth N={\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}M-q_{R}(M,N),

where
q_{R}(M,N)=\sup\{i:{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(M,N)\not=0\}.
Therefore, if
S
has finite projective dimension over
R
, then for any finite length
S
-module
M
one has

\operatorname{lift.depth}_{\varphi}(M)\leq{\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}S=\depth R-\depth S,

and
\operatorname{lift.depth}_{\varphi}(M)={\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}S
if and only if
M
lifts to
R
.

3.2
.

Suppose that
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a lifting system along
\varphi
for some finite length
S
-module
M
. We can then consider the system of
R
-complexes
\{M_{n}\otimes_{R}F\}_{n\geq 1}
where
F
is a minimal free resolution of the
R
-module
S
. Since
{M_{n}}
is a system of surjective maps, the induced map on complexes is degree wise surjective. Now applying
[
24
, Theorem 3.5.8]
, we see that

(3)

\varprojlim{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(M_{n},S)\cong{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(\varprojlim M_{n},S).

Here we have also used the fact that for all
i\geq 0
one has
\varprojlim^{(1)}{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(M_{n},S)=0
as the Tor groups are finite length modules; see
[
24
, Proposition 3.5.7]
.

Proof of
B
(1).

For
L
a finitely generated naive lift of
M
with
\depth L\geq r
, we apply Auslander’s formula to
L\otimes_{R}S\cong M
and observe that

{\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}S-r\geq q_{R}(L,S).

Clearly
\{M_{n}:=L/\mathfrak{a}^{n}L\}_{n\geq 1}
is a lifting system of
M
along
\varphi
, with associated lift
\widehat{L}^{\mathfrak{a}}
, the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion of
L
. Since
R
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete and since
L
is a finitely generated
R
-module by
Proposition

2.1
(2), we have
\widehat{L}^{\mathfrak{a}}\cong L
, and so

\varprojlim{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(M_{n},S)\cong{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(\varprojlim M_{n},S)\cong{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(L,S)=0

for
i>q_{R}(L,S)
, where the first isomorphism is by
Eq.

3
.

Conversely, given a lifting system
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that
\varprojlim{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(M_{n},S)=0
for
i>{\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}S-r
, then
L=\varprojlim M_{n}
satisfies
L\otimes_{R}S\cong M
by
Proposition

2.1
(1) and it satisfies
\depth L\geq r
since

{\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}S-r\geq q_{R}(L,S)={\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}S-\depth{L},

where the inequality is by assumption and the equality is by Auslander’s formula
3.1
. We can apply Auslander’s formula here because
L
is a finitely generated
R
-module by
Proposition

2.1
(2). This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S=R/\mathfrak{a}
be a surjection of noetherian local rings where
R
is regular and
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete. A finite length
S
-module
M
lifts to
R
if and only if it admits a lifting system
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
along
\varphi
such that
\varprojlim{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{1}(M_{n},S)=0
.

Proof.

The
S
-module
M
lifting to a finitely generated
R
-module
L
gives the second equality below

{\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}S-\depth{L}=q_{R}(L,S)=0,

and the first equality is by Auslander’s formula. The liftable depth of
M
is at least
\depth{L}
, hence by
B
(1) there is a lifting system
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that

\varprojlim{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(M_{n},S)=0

for all
i>{\operatorname{pdim}}_{R}S-\depth{L}=0
.

For the converse, let
L=\varprojlim M_{n}
be the lift associated to the lifting system
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. Then the assumption gives the second equality below

{\operatorname{Tor}}_{1}^{R}(L,S)={\operatorname{Tor}}_{1}^{R}(\varprojlim M_{n},S)\cong\varprojlim{\operatorname{Tor}}^{R}_{1}(M_{n},S)=0

and
Equation

3
gives the isomorphism. Modules over regular local rings are Tor rigid by
[
17
]
, and so
q_{R}(L,S)=0
. Since we also have that
L
is a naive lift of
M
by
Proposition

2.1
(1),
L
is a lift of
M

∎

In the following example we compute the lifting depth of
M
from
Example

2.4
.

Example 3.4
.

Retaining the setup of
Example

2.4
, the complex

0\rightarrow R^{2}\xrightarrow{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}z&y+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x^{4i-2}\\
y+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x^{4i}&z\end{array}\right]}R^{2}\rightarrow 0

is a minimal free resolution of
L
over
R
. This is because the determinant of the matrix above is a nonzerodivisor in
R
, and so the Buchsbaum-Eisenbud acyclicity criterion implies that the complex is exact, see
[
5
, Theorem 1.4.13]
. This implies
\depth{L}=2
by the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula. Now
Eq.

1
gives the second inequality below

2=\depth{L}\leq\operatorname{lift.depth}_{\varphi}(M)\leq\depth{R}-\depth{S}=2.

4.
Liftable dimension

In this section, we provide a proof for
B
(2) and
Corollary

4.5
. Our key insight is looking at the zeroth Fitting ideals of the modules in the lifting system.

4.1
.

For basic facts about Fitting ideals, we refer to
[
9
, 20.2]
. Recall that if
R
is a noetherian local ring and
M
is a finitely presented
R
-module with a presentation

R^{\beta_{1}}\xrightarrow{\phi}R^{\beta_{0}}\to M\to 0,

one defines
\Fitt^{R}_{i}(M)=I_{\beta_{0}-i}(\phi)
, the ideal generated by the
(\beta_{0}-i)
-minors of the matrix
\phi
. Note that for an ideal
I
in
R
, we have an equality:
\Fitt_{0}(R/I)=I
. The ideals
\Fitt^{R}_{i}(M)
do not depend on the choice of presentation of
M
, see
[
9
, 20.4]
. Furthermore, for a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, we have

{\operatorname{ann}}_{R}(M)^{\mu_{R}(M)}\subseteq\Fitt^{R}_{0}(M)\subseteq{\operatorname{ann}}_{R}(M),

whence
\Supp_{R}(M)={\rm{V}}({\operatorname{ann}}_{R}(M))={\rm{V}}(\Fitt^{R}_{0}(M))
, see
[
9
, Prop 20.7]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
R\twoheadrightarrow S=R/\mathfrak{a}
is a surjection of noetherian local rings. If
L
is a finitely generated
R
-module generated by
\mu
elements, then

\Fitt_{0}^{R}(L)+\mathfrak{a}^{\mu}\subseteq\Fitt^{R}_{0}(L\otimes_{R}S)\subseteq\Fitt^{R}_{0}(L)+\mathfrak{a}.

Proof.

Suppose that
R^{\beta_{1}}\xrightarrow{\phi}R^{\beta_{0}}\to L\to 0
is a presentation matrix for
L
. If
\mathfrak{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, define a matrix

A=[a_{1}I_{\beta_{0}}\ldots a_{n}I_{\beta_{0}}],

where
I_{\beta_{0}}
is the identity matrix of size
\beta_{0}
. Then note that
\phi^{\prime}=(\phi|A)
is a presentation matrix for
L\otimes_{R}S
over
R
. It is now easy to see that

Noting that

Lemma 4.3

Suppose that

5.1.

5.2.
Serre liftable cyclic modules.

Given a local ring
R
, two finitely generated
R
-modules
M
and
N
such that
\ell_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty
are said to
intersect properly
if

\dim M+\dim N=\dim R.

If, for example,
R
is a regular local ring, one always has that

\dim M+\dim N\leq\dim R,

and it is interesting to find sufficient conditions when equality holds. In this direction, our methods yield the following result.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a regular local ring and
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
are two ideals such that
\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}}=\mathfrak{m}
. If
\mathfrak{a}
is an equimultiple ideal and
\mathfrak{b}
does not contain any minimal generator of
\mathfrak{a}^{n}
for some
n
sufficiently large, then

\dim R/\mathfrak{a}+\dim R/\mathfrak{b}=\dim R.

Equivalently, the conclusion of this theorem says that
R/\mathfrak{b}
is a Serre lift of the finite length
S
-module
S/\mathfrak{b}S
where
S=R/\mathfrak{a}
. We give a proof of this theorem below. First we recall what it means for an ideal to be equimultiple, and then prove a lemma.

5.5
.

Let
R
be a noetherian local ring and
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq R
an ideal. The
analytic spread
of
\mathfrak{a}
, denoted
\ell(\mathfrak{a})
, measures the polynomial rate in which the
\mu(\mathfrak{a}^{n})
grows for
n\geq 1
, i.e. we have
\mu(\mathfrak{a}^{n})\approx n^{\ell(\mathfrak{a})-1}
, see
[
5
, Definition 4.6.7]
. It is always true that

\height{\mathfrak{a}}\leq\ell(\mathfrak{a})\leq\dim{R},

see
[
5
, Exercise 4.6.13.c]
, and when the first inequality is an equality, we say
\mathfrak{a}
is an
equimultiple
ideal.

Given an ideal
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq R
, by the Artin-Rees lemma,

\text{ar}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{b})=\inf\{s\colon\text{for }n\geq s,\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n}=\mathfrak{a}^{n-s}(\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{s})\}

is a finite integer. Therefore,
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}
for some
n\geq\text{ar}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{b})
is equivalent to the condition
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}
for all
n\gg 0
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a noetherian local ring, let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq R
be an ideal such that
R
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete, and consider the surjection
\varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S=R/\mathfrak{a}
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq R
is an ideal such that
\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}}=\mathfrak{m}
and that the containment

\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}

holds for some
n
sufficiently large. Setting
I_{n}=\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{a}^{n}
we have that
\{R/I_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a lifting system for the
S
-module
S/\mathfrak{b}S
along
\varphi
such that
\ell_{R}(R/I_{n})\approx n^{d}
for
d\geq\ell(\mathfrak{a})
. In particular,
\dim R/\mathfrak{b}\geq\ell(\mathfrak{a})
.

Proof.

It follows from the definition that the collection
\{R/I_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a lifting system for the
S
-module
S/\mathfrak{b}S
. We now coarsely estimate
\ell_{R}(I_{n}/I_{n+1})
for
n\geq 1
from below by computing its minimal number of generators. We claim that the following isomorphisms hold for each
n\geq 1
.

\frac{I_{n}/I_{n+1}}{\mathfrak{m}(I_{n}/I_{n+1})}=\frac{I_{n}}{\mathfrak{m}I_{n}+I_{n+1}}\cong\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{n}}{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}+(\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n})}.

The equality is clear. To see the isomorphism, notice that since
\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
and since
\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}
, we have

\frac{I_{n}}{\mathfrak{m}I_{n}+I_{n+1}}=\frac{\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{a}^{n}}{\mathfrak{m}(\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{a}^{n})+\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}}=\frac{\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{a}^{n}}{\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}}.

Also, the map

\mathfrak{a}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{a}^{n}}{\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}}

defined by
r\mapsto r+\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}
is surjective, and the kernel of this map is

\mathfrak{a}^{n}\cap(\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n})=(\mathfrak{a}^{n}\cap\mathfrak{b})+\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}

since
\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{n}
; this last equality follows from the modular law, see
[
1
, Page 6]
. This proves the claim.

Now from the claim and the following exact sequence

0\to\frac{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}+(\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n})}{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}}\to\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{n}}{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}}\twoheadrightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{n}}{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}+(\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n})}\to 0,

it follows that

\mu_{R}(I_{n}/I_{n+1})=\mu(\mathfrak{a}^{n})-\dim_{k}\frac{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}+(\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n})}{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}}.

Since the containment
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}
holds for
n
large enough, we have

\mu_{R}(I_{n}/I_{n+1})=\mu(\mathfrak{a}^{n}).

From the following exact sequence

0\to I_{n}/I_{n+1}\to R/I_{n+1}\to R/I_{n}\to 0

we see that

\ell_{R}(R/I_{n+1})-\ell_{R}(R/I_{n})=\ell_{R}(I_{n}/I_{n+1})\geq\mu_{R}(I_{n}/I_{n+1})=\mu(\mathfrak{a}^{n})\approx n^{\ell(\mathfrak{a})-1}.

This tells us
\ell_{R}(R/I_{n})\geq n^{\ell(\mathfrak{a})}
for
n
large enough. Therefore,
\ell_{R}(R/I_{n})\approx n^{d}
with
d\geq\ell(\mathfrak{a})
. The associated lift of the system is
R/\mathfrak{b}
since
R
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete, and so, by
B
(2),
\dim R/\mathfrak{b}\geq\ell(\mathfrak{a})
. Just recall that the zeroth Fitting ideal of
R/I_{n}
is
I_{n}
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

5.4
.

For any
n\geq 1
, the condition that
\mathfrak{b}
does not contain any minimal generator of
\mathfrak{a}^{n}
is equivalent to the inclusion
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}^{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{n}
. Now just note that we have

\dim{R/\mathfrak{b}}\geq\ell(\mathfrak{a})=\height\mathfrak{a}=\dim{R}-\dim{R/\mathfrak{a}}\geq\dim{R/\mathfrak{b}}

where the first inequality is by
Lemma

5.6
, the first equality is by
\mathfrak{a}
being an equimultiple ideal, and the second inequality is by Serre’s dimension inequality.
∎
