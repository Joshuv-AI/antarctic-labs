Abstract
. Given a 𝔾 a \mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}} -action on an affine variety X X , we show that the complement of the union of all principal invariant cylinders in X X is equal to the zero locus of the plinth ideal of the corresponding locally nilpotent derivation.

Primary 14R10, 14R20; Secondary 13A50

1.
Introduction

We fix an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero. By a variety we mean an integral separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{K}
. Denote by
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}=(\mathbb{K},+)
the one-dimensional unipotent algebraic group.

The study of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions on algebraic varieties is a long-standing subject in algebraic geometry and commutative algebra. In addition to the fact that a description of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions is an object of study in itself, the algebraic nature of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions on affine varieties provides a technique that allows to tackle various problems. One example is the study of automorphism groups
[
8
]
,
[
4
]
. Another is the Zariski Cancellation Problem. Exponential maps – a description of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions in the case of positive characteristic – were used by Gupta in
[
5
]
to provide counterexamples to the Zariski Cancellation problem in positive characteristic.

The results of the papers
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
provide a way to describe
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions using cylinders, that is, open subsets of the form
\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
. In particular, this technique can be used to study

\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions on affine cones over a projective variety
X
in terms of internal geometric properties of
X
. This method was used in
[
1
]
to solve a long-standing problem about absence of non-trivial
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions on affine Fermat cubic threefold.

Let
X
be an affine variety. Then
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions on
X
are in one-to-one correspondence with locally nilpotent derivations on the algebra
\mathbb{K}[X]
of regular functions on
X
. Given a locally nilpotent derivation
\partial
on a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
B
, the ideal
\pl(\partial)=\Ker\partial\cap\mIm\partial
in
\Ker\partial
is called the plinth ideal of
\partial
. Factorial affine varieties with
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions having principal plinth ideal were studied in
[
9
]
. In this note we obtain a geometric description of the zero locus
\mathbb{V}_{X}(\pl(\partial))
of the plinth ideal.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times X\to X
be a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on an affine variety
X
. By
[
6
, Proposition 3.1.5]
there exists a principal cylinder
U\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
in
X
such that every
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-fiber of
U
is an orbit of
\alpha
. We are interested in the geometric locus of the union of such cylinders. One can view this union as the locus of points in
X
where the quotient
X\to Y
,
Y=\Spec(\Ker\partial)
admits a local principal trivialization of the action
\alpha
.

This note consists of two sections. The main result of the first section is Theorem
2.6
, in which it is proved that the union of principal cylinders defined by an action
\alpha
coincides with the complement of the zero locus
\mathbb{V}_{X}(\pl(\partial))
. In the second section we provide counterexamples to several natural assumptions, showing that this description does not admit a direct simplification.

The author is grateful to Ivan Arzhantsev and Sergey Gaifullin for useful discussions and suggestions.

2.
Cylinders and the plinth ideal

Let us introduce basic definitions and conventions.

Let
X
be a variety and
S\subseteq\mathbb{K}[X]
be a set of regular functions on
X
. Denote by
\mathbb{V}_{X}(S)
the closed subvariety in
X
given by equations
h=0
for
h\in S
. Denote
\mathbb{D}_{X}(S)=X\setminus\mathbb{V}_{X}(S)
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group and
X
be a variety. By an action of
G
on
X
we mean a regular action
G\times X\to X
. Consider an action
\alpha\colon G\times X\to X
. We denote by
\Fix(\alpha)
the set of fixed points of the action
\alpha
. Given an invariant subvariety
Y\subseteq X
, we denote by
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\alpha}\right|_{Y}
the induced action
G\times Y\to Y
.

Let
B
be a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. A
locally nilpotent derivation
(abbreviated
LND
) on
B
is a

\mathbb{K}
-derivation
\partial\colon B\to B
satisfying the following property: for each
b\in B
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\partial^{n}(b)=0
. The set of all locally nilpotent derivations on
B
is denoted by
\LND(B)
.

Let us recall an algebraic description of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions on affine varieties. Consider an affine variety
X
. If
\alpha\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times X\to X
is a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on
X
, then the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{K}[X]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{K}[X][s]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{K}[X][s]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{K}[X]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d/ds}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s=0}

is a locally nilpotent derivation
\LND(\alpha)\in\LND(\mathbb{K}[X])
. Conversely, if
\partial\colon\mathbb{K}[X]\to\mathbb{K}[X]
is a locally nilpotent derivation, then the map

\exp(s\partial)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{n}\partial^{n}}{n!}\colon\mathbb{K}[X]\to\mathbb{K}[X][s]

defines an action
\A(\partial)\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times X\to X
. This correspondence between
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-actions on
X
and locally nilpotent derivations on
\mathbb{K}[X]
is bijective.

The following lemma is a simple technical observation that we will need later.

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider a variety
X
and a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action
\alpha
on
X
. Let
U_{1},U_{2}\subseteq X
be two

\alpha
-invariant affine open subsets and let
f\in\mathbb{K}(X)

X
, regular on
U_{1}\cup U_{2}
. For each
i=1,2

Definition 2.2
.

Consider a variety
X
. A
cylinder in
X
is an open subset
U
together with an isomorphism
U\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
for some variety
Z
. By a
fiber
of a cylinder
U
we mean a fiber of the projection
U\to Z
to the second factor. We call the projection
U\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
to the first factor the
level function
of the cylinder
U
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times X\to X
be a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on a variety
X
. We say that
\alpha
is
cylindrical
if there is an isomorphism
X\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
for some variety
Z
such that
\alpha
acts by translations along the first factor.

Let
B
be a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra and
\partial\in\LND(B)
. An element
b\in B
is called a
slice
of
\partial
if
\partial(b)=1
. Assume that
X
is an affine variety,
B=\mathbb{K}[X]
and
\alpha=A(\partial)
is the corresponding
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on
X
. Then by Slice Theorem
[
3
, Corollary 1.26]
the action
\alpha
is cylindrical if and only if the derivation
\partial
admits a slice. Moreover, a function
f\in\mathbb{K}[X]
is a slice of
\partial
if and only if
f
is a level function of a cylinder
X\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
and
\alpha
is cylindrical with respect to this isomorphism.

The following result is a generalization of
[
11
, Lemma 2.3]
for arbitrary varieties.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a variety and
\alpha
be a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on
X
. Assume that there is an isomorphism
X\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
such that every
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-fiber is an orbit of
\alpha
. Then
\alpha
is cylindrical.

Proof.

Consider a covering
Z=\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i}
of
Z
by open affine charts. For each
i\in I
denote
V_{i}=\mathbb{A}^{1}\times U_{i}
. Then
X=\bigcup_{i\in I}V_{i}
is a covering of
X
by affine
\alpha
-invariant cylinders. For each
i\in I
denote

\partial_{i}=\LND\left(\left.\kern-1.2pt{\alpha}\right|_{V_{i}}\right)\in\LND(\mathbb{K}[V_{i}]).

Denote by
s
the level function of the cylinder
X\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
. By Lemma
2.1
the functions
h_{i}=\partial_{i}(s)\in\mathbb{K}[V_{i}]\subseteq\mathbb{K}(X)
can be glued together, defining a function
h\in\mathbb{K}[X]
. By the argument from
[
11
, Lemma 2.3]
, applied to each chart
V_{i}
, we have
h\in\mathbb{K}[Z]\subseteq\mathbb{K}[X]
, the function
h
is invertible and

\alpha\big(s,(u,z)\big)=(u+sh,z)

for all
s\in\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
and
(u,z)\in\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
. Conjugating the action
\alpha
by the isomorphism

\phi\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z\to\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z,\quad\phi(u,z)=\left(\frac{u}{h},z\right),

we obtain an action

s\cdot(u,z)=\phi\Big(\alpha\big(s,\ \phi^{-1}(u,z)\big)\Big)=\phi\Big(\alpha\big(s,\ (uh,z)\big)\Big)=\phi(uh+sh,z)=(u+s,z).

Therefore,
\alpha
is cylindrical.
∎

Let
X
be an affine variety. By
[
6
, Proposition 3.1.5]
every
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on
X
gives rise to a principal cylinder in
X
. Namely, if
\alpha\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times X\to X
is a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on
X
, then there exists an
\alpha
-invariant principal cylinder
U\subseteq X
such that
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\alpha}\right|_{U}
is cylindrical.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times X\to X
be a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on a variety
X
. By an
\alpha
-cylinder
we mean an
\alpha
-invariant cylinder
U\subseteq X
such that
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\alpha}\right|_{U}
is cylindrical.

Let
B
be a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra and
\partial\in\LND(B)
. The
plinth ideal
of the derivation
\partial
is the set

\pl(\partial)=\Ker(\partial)\cap\mIm(\partial).

This is an ideal in the algebra
\Ker(\partial)
.

The following result is a description of the set of functions
h\in\mathbb{K}[X]
that define a principal
\alpha
-cylinder
\mathbb{D}_{X}(h)
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X
be an affine variety,
\alpha
be a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on
X
, and
h\in\mathbb{K}[X]
. Denote
\partial=\LND(\alpha)\in\LND(\mathbb{K}[X])
. Then
\mathbb{D}_{X}(h)
is an
\alpha
-cylinder if and only if
h^{n}\in\pl(\partial)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h^{n}\in\pl(\partial)
. Since
\mathbb{D}_{X}(h)=\mathbb{D}_{X}(h^{n})
, we may assume that
h\in\pl(\partial)
. The assertion now follows from the construction in
[
6
, Proposition 3.1.5]
.

Conversely, denote
U=\mathbb{D}_{X}(h)
and assume that
U\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
is an
\alpha
-cylinder. The LND
\partial
extends to an LND on
\mathbb{K}[U]\supseteq\mathbb{K}[X]
. Then, since
h
is an invertible element of the algebra
\mathbb{K}[U]
, we have
\partial(h)=0
. Moreover,

\mathbb{K}[U]=\mathbb{K}[X]_{h}\cong\mathbb{K}[Z][s],

where
\mathbb{K}[Z]=\Ker\partial
and
\partial(s)=1
. There exist
f\in\mathbb{K}[X]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

s=\frac{f}{h^{n}}.

Therefore,

h^{n}s\in\mathbb{K}[X]\quad\text{and}\quad\partial(h^{n}s)=h^{n}\in\pl(\partial)

as required.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
X
,
\alpha
and
\partial
be as in Theorem
2.6
. Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the set of all principal
\alpha
-cylinders in
X
. Then

X\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i}=\mathbb{V}_{X}\big(\pl(\partial)\big).

It is worth to mention here the equality
\mathbb{V}_{X}(\mIm\partial)=\Fix(\alpha)
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
X
,
\alpha
and
\partial
be as in Theorem
2.6
. Assume that
\pl(\partial)\subseteq\Ker(\partial)
is a principal ideal. Then there exists a maximal principal
\alpha
-cylinder in
X
.

Note that by a result of Bonnet, Daigle, and Kaliman
[
2
, Theorem 1.5]
if
X=\mathbb{A}^{3}
, then
\pl(\partial)
is a principal ideal in
\Ker(\partial)
.

3.
Examples

The first example is a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action
\alpha
on
\mathbb{A}^{3}
such that
\mathbb{A}^{3}\setminus\Fix(\alpha)
is not an
\alpha
-cylinder. Note that there is no such action on
\mathbb{A}^{2}
. Indeed, by a result of Rentschler
[
10
]
every
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{2}
is conjugate to an action of the form
s\cdot(x,y)=(x+sp,y)
for some
p\in\mathbb{K}[y]
. Then
\Fix(\alpha)=\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}(p)
and
\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\Fix(\alpha)
is a cylinder
\mathbb{A}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(f)
.

Example 3.1
.

Consider an LND

\partial=y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+z\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\in\LND(\mathbb{K}[x,y,z]).

The corresponding
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action
\alpha
on
\mathbb{A}^{3}
is

s\cdot(x,y,z)=\left(x+sy+\frac{1}{2}s^{2}z,\ y+sz,\ z\right).

Denote
\mathbb{K}^{\times}=\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
. The orbits of
\alpha
are parabolas

z=\lambda,\quad x=\frac{1}{2\lambda}y^{2}+a

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
and
a\in\mathbb{K}
; lines

z=0,\quad y=\lambda

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
; and fixed points

z=0,\quad y=0,\quad x=a

for some
a\in\mathbb{K}
. One can check that

\Ker(\partial)=\mathbb{K}[z,\ y^{2}-2xz]\quad\text{and}\quad\pl(\partial)=\Ker(\partial)\cdot z.

Although
U=\mathbb{A}^{3}\setminus\Fix(\alpha)
is a cylinder
U\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times\big(\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\big)
, we claim that
U
is not an
\alpha
-cylinder. Assume the contrary that there is an isomorphism
\phi\colon U\to\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
such that
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\alpha}\right|_{U}
is cylindrical with respect to
\phi
. Denote by
f
the level function of
\phi
. Since
\Fix(\alpha)
has codimension greater than one in
\mathbb{A}^{3}
, we have
f\in\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{A}^{3}]
. Consider an
\alpha
-invariant subset
V=\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{3}}(z)
. The isomorphism
\phi
restricts to an isomorphism
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\phi}\right|_{V}\colon V\to\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z^{\prime}
with a level function
\left.\kern-1.2pt{f}\right|_{V}
. Therefore,
\left.\kern-1.2pt{f}\right|_{V}
is a slice of
\partial
on
\mathbb{K}[V]=\mathbb{K}[x,y,z,z^{-1}]
. It is easy to see that every such slice has the form

\frac{y}{z}+h(z,\ z^{-1},\ y^{2}-2xz)

for some polynomial
h
, but these functions are not regular on
\mathbb{A}^{3}
, which is a contradiction.

The second example is a free
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action
\alpha
on an affine surface
X
such that
X
cannot be covered by
\alpha
-cylinders. Again, there is no such action on
\mathbb{A}^{2}
. We will need the following description of cylinders in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a cylinder
U\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times C
. Then
U
is affine and
C
is isomorphic to an open subset of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
C
is a smooth curve. We claim that
C
is rational. Indeed, the open embedding
U\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
induces a surjection of divisor class groups
\Cl(\mathbb{P}^{2})\to\Cl(U)
. Since
\Cl(\mathbb{P}^{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
\Cl(U)\cong\Cl(C)
, we see that
\Cl(C)
is a quotient group of
\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
\Cl(\hat{C})
is a finitely generated group, where
\hat{C}
is a smooth projective model of
C
. It follows that the genus of
\hat{C}
is zero, hence
C
is rational.

A smooth rational curve is isomorphic to an open subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, so it suffices to show that
C\neq\mathbb{P}^{1}
. But if
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then the curves
\{0\}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\{1\}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
U
are disjoint and closed in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Example 3.3
.

Consider a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action
\alpha
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
defined by the formula

s\cdot(x:y:z)=\left(x+sy+\frac{1}{2}s^{2}z\ :\ y+sz\ :\ z\right).

One can obtain this action from the action in Example
3.1
by passing to the quotient of
\mathbb{A}^{3}
by the standard action of scalar matrices. The only fixed point of
\alpha
is
(1:0:0)
and the closures of other orbits of
\alpha
are quadrics

xz=\frac{y^{2}}{2}+az^{2}

for
a\in\mathbb{K}
and the line
z=0
. The affine variety

X=\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\{y^{2}=2xz\}

is
\alpha
-invariant and the induced action
\left.\kern-1.2pt{\alpha}\right|_{X}
has no fixed points.

Denote
h=y^{2}-2xz
. The algebra of regular functions on
X
is the subalgebra in
\mathbb{K}(x,y,z)
of functions of the form
f/h^{n}
, where
n
is a non-negative integer and
f\in\mathbb{K}[x,y,z]
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
2n
. One check that

\Ker(\partial)=\mathbb{K}[z^{2}/h]\quad\text{and}\quad\pl(\partial)=\Ker(\partial)\cdot\frac{z^{2}}{h}.

Let us show that every cylinder
U\subseteq X
is a principal proper subset. First,
X
is not cylinder, since by Lemma
3.2
we have
\Cl(U)=0
but
\Cl(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore,
U\subsetneq X
is a proper subset. The variety
X
is smooth and the cylinder
U
is affine by Lemma
3.2
, so the complement
X\setminus U
is of pure codimension one. Since
\Cl(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
we conclude that
U
is principal. Thus, from Corollary
2.7
we deduce that the union of all
\alpha
-cylinders in
X
is the proper subset
z\neq 0
.

In the third example we show that for affine
X
there may be an affine
\alpha
-cylinder
U\subseteq X
which cannot be covered by principal
\alpha
-cylinders.

Example 3.4
.

Consider a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{3}
defined by an LND
y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+z\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
as in Example
3.1
. The Danielewski surface

X=\{y^{2}-2xz-1=0\}

is invariant with respect to this action. Denote by
\alpha
the restriction of this action to
X
and let
\partial=\LND(\alpha)\in\LND(\mathbb{K}[X])
. Let
U\subseteq X
be the complement to the orbit
z=y-1=0
. The extension of
\partial
to
\mathbb{K}[U]
admits a slice
\frac{y+1}{z}=\frac{2x}{y-1}
, hence
U
is an
\alpha
-cylinder

\begin{array}[]{rcl}U&\cong&\mathbb{A}^{1}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\\
(x,y,z)&\mapsto&\left(z,\frac{y+1}{z}\right)\\
\left(\frac{(uv-2)v}{2},\ uv-1,\ u\right)&\mapsfrom&(u,v)\end{array}.

On the other hand, we see that

\Ker(\partial)=\mathbb{K}[z]\quad\text{and}\quad\pl(\partial)=\Ker(\partial)\cdot z.

By Corollary
2.7
the union of all principal
\alpha
-cylinders in
X
is equal to

\mathbb{D}_{X}(z)=U\setminus\{z=y+1=0\}.

Therefore,
U
cannot be covered by principal
\alpha
-cylinders.

The final example is a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action
\alpha
on
\mathbb{A}^{4}
such that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{4}}\big(\pl(\partial)\big)
is not an
\alpha
-cylinder. By Corollaries
2.7
and
2.8
we would need the plinth ideal
\pl(\partial)
to be non-principal, hence no such actions occur for
\mathbb{A}^{3}
.

Example 3.5
.

Consider an LND

\partial=u\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+v\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\in\LND(\mathbb{K}[x,y,u,v]).

The corresponding
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action
\alpha
on
\mathbb{A}^{4}
is

s\cdot(x,y,u,v)=(x+su,\ y+sv,\ u,\ v).

One can check that

\Ker(\partial)=\mathbb{K}[u,\ v,\ xv-yu]\quad\text{and}\quad\pl(\partial)=\Ker(\partial)\cdot u+\Ker(\partial)\cdot v.

Using an argument similar to the one from Example
3.1
, we can show that the cylinder
U=

=\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{4}}\big(\pl(\partial)\big)\cong\mathbb{A}^{2}\times\big(\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\big)
is not an
\alpha
-cylinder. Denote
V=\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{4}}(u)
. An isomorphism
U\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z
making
U
an
\alpha
-cylinder would define a level function, which is regular on
\mathbb{A}^{4}
since the codimension of
\mathbb{A}^{4}\setminus U
is greater than one. This level function restricts to a slice of
\partial
on
\mathbb{K}[V]=\mathbb{K}[x,y,u,u^{-1},v]
. Every such slice has the form

\frac{x}{u}+h(u,\ u^{-1},\ v,\ xv-yu)

for some polynomial
h
. This is a contradiction with regularity of the level function.
