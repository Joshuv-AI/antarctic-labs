A Matrix Rank Formula for vector bundles of vertex operator algebra coinvariants and conformal blocks
Xiangrui Luo

Abstract
. We introduce FA-matrices for computing ranks of vector bundles of coinvariants and conformal blocks associated with modules over vertex operator algebras on the moduli space of stable pointed curves, unifying the notions of fusion and averaging matrices and generalizing Ueno’s work. To illustrate, we compute ranks of vector bundles determined by pointed VOAs and the tensor product of certain VOAs, as well as other examples. As an application, positivity properties of their first Chern classes are analyzed.



1.
Introduction

Given a stable n-pointed coordinatized curve of genus
g
, together with n admissible modules over a vertex operator algebra (VOA) of CFT–type, one can construct a vector space of coinvariants and its dual space of conformal blocks
[
35
,
32
,
11
]
. These fit together to form a sheaf on the stack parameterizing families of such curves, called the
sheaf of coinvariants

\mathbb{V}_{g,n}(V,\{W^{\bullet}\})
. If the VOA satisfies certain mild assumptions, then the sheaf supports a projectively flat connection, with singularities on the boundary, and it will have finite-dimensional fibres. The connection allows one to conclude that the dimensions of the fibers are uniform at all smooth curves. To determine whether such a sheaf forms a vector bundle, one must check that the dimensions of the fibers do not increase at curves with singularities. In other words, the sheaf is locally free of finite rank. The rank of a vector bundle is important for a number of other reasons. For instance, it is an invariant, and is a crucial ingredient in computing Chern classes. In genus zero, rank encodes fusion rules. Moreover, there are many applications of knowing the rank for particular VOAs.

For affine VOAs at positive integer levels, sheaves of coinvariants and conformal blocks form vector bundles. More generally, if the sheaf is coherent and
V
is rational, one can replace the vector spaces of coinvariants at nodal curves by sums of analogous spaces on the (partial) normalization of the curve
[
35
,
32
,
13
]
. This factorization theorem gives a recursive algorithm to reduce the rank computations to fusion rules.

One of our main results, Theorem
1
, gives a closed rank formula in all settings where factorization can be applied and fusion rules are understood.

Definition 4.6

Proposition 4.7

Let

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\beta,0}\mathcal{N}_{0,1}^{g}\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,0}^{3}}{\mathrm{Id}_{l}-\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,0}z}=

Definition 7.1

7.1.

Definition 7.3
.

Let

be a cyclic group of

be a permutation. Let

Proposition 7.4
.

Let

where the multiplication is in

Proof.
By Theorem
4.5
, the FA-matrix defines a representation of the group ring

. That is, for

, we have a ring homomorphism

R_{-,0}:\mathbb{Z}[G]\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mn}(\mathbb{R}),
by
x\longmapsto R_{W_{x},0}
.
For

R_{W_{x},0}R_{W_{y},0}=R_{W_{x}\boxtimes W_{y},0}=R_{W_{xy},0}.

Thus,

\begin{aligned} \prod_{i=1}^{n}R_{W_{i},0}=R_{W_{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}},0}.\end{aligned}

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}\mathbb{V}_{0,n}\left(V,\left\{W_{x_{1}},\cdots,W_{x_{n}}\right\}\right)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}\mathbb{V}_{0,n+2}\left(V,\left\{W_{x_{1}},\cdots,W_{x_{n}},V,V\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}\mathbb{V}_{0,3}\left(V,\left\{W_{x_{i}\cdots x_{n}},V,V\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{W_{x_{i}\cdots x_{n},V}}=\delta_{x_{1}\cdots x_{n},e}.

Therefore, we proved the case for

Using standard linear algebra result, we may check

\begin{aligned} R_{V,1}=&\sum_{s\in G}\mathrm{Tr}\left(R_{W_{s^{-1}},0}\right)R_{W_{s},0}=|G|\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{|G|}.\end{aligned}

Therefore, by Theorem

\mathrm{rank}\mathbb{V}_{g,n}\left(V,\left\{W_{x_{1}},\cdots,W_{x_{n}}\right\}\right)=|G|^{g}\delta_{x_{1}\cdots x_{n},e}.

Q.E.D.

8.
Symmetric Coinvariant Divisors and Irreducible Module of Order two

In the previous section, we consider the case where VOA is pointed. In this section, we no longer impose any additional assumptions on the VOA. Instead, we impose conditions on the modules, and study the symmetric coinvariant divisor on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
.

Definition 8.1
.

A coinvariant divisor on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
is called
symmetric
if the irreducible modules associated to each marked point are the same.

Symmetric coinvariant divisors are important and have been studied because they could be viewed as a divisor on
\tilde{M}_{0,n}/\mathrm{Sym}(n)
, where the group
\mathrm{Sym}(n)
acts by permuting the
n
marked points.

Definition 8.2
.

Let
V
be a strongly rational VOA,
W
be an irreducible module. We say that
W
is an
irreducible module of order two
if
W\neq V
and
W\boxtimes W=V
.

We observe that this condition implies that
W=W^{\prime}
, where
W^{\prime}
denotes the dual module of
W
.

Lemma 8.3
.

For an irreducible module,
W
, of a strongly rational VOA, the following are equivalent:

(a)

W
is an irreducible module of order two.

(b)

R_{W,0}
is a permutation matrix of order two.

Proof.
Let
W_{1},\cdots,W_{l}
be the collection of all irreducible
V
-modules, up to isomorphism.

Part 1:
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
.

By Proposition
5.9
, we have

\begin{aligned} R_{W,0}R_{W,0}=R_{W\boxtimes W,0}=R_{V,0}=\mathrm{Id},\end{aligned}

so
R_{W,0}
is a matrix of order two. To show that it is a permutation matrix, it suffices to prove the following linear-algebraic claim.

Claim: let
A\in\mathrm{M}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{N})
such that
A^{2}=\mathrm{Id}
, then
A
is a permutation matrix of order
2
.

We consider the
(i,j)
-entry of
A^{2}
.

\begin{aligned} \delta_{i,j}=\mathrm{Id}_{(i,j)}=\left(A^{2}\right)_{(i,j)}=&\sum_{k=1}^{l}A_{(i,k)}A_{(k,j)}.\end{aligned}

Take
i=j=1
, we have
1=\sum_{k=1}^{l}A_{(1,k)}A_{(k,1)}
. Thus, there is only one term that is non-zero, and we call such
k
by
k_{1}
. That is,
A_{(1,k_{1})}=A_{(k_{1},1)}=1
.

Moreover,
0=\sum_{k=1}^{l}A_{(1,k)}A_{(k,j)}
, for
j\not=1
. Since
A_{(1,k_{1})}=1
, we conclude that
A_{(k_{1},j)}=0
. Since such
j
is arbitrary, we conclude that
A_{(k_{1},j)}=\delta_{j,1}
. That is, we showed that for the
k_{1}
’s row of
A
, the
(k_{1},1)
-entry is
1
, and the rest entries are all zero. By the same argument, we can prove that
A_{(j,k_{1})}=\delta_{j,1}
. Thus, we show that for the
k_{1}
-th row or
k_{1}
-th column, there is only one
1
, and the rest are zero. Instead of considering
(1,1)
-entry, we can apply the same argument to to all
(i,i)
-entry, and conclude that
A
is a permutation matrix. Thus, we proved the claim.

Part 2:
(b)\Rightarrow(a)
.

By Proposition
5.9
, we have
R_{V,0}=\mathrm{Id}=R_{W,0}R_{W,0}=R_{W\boxtimes W,0}.

Therefore,
W\boxtimes W=V
.

Q.E.D.

Lemma 8.4

Symmetric coinvariant divisors and Virasoro VOAs

Virasoro VOAs form an important class of examples of vertex operator algebras. They correspond to the representations of the Virasoro Lie algebra. A Virasoro VOA is strongly rational if and only if its central charge is
c_{p,q}:=1-\frac{6(p-q)^{2}}{pq}
, for some relatively prime integers

[
25
,
37
]
, and we denote it by

. If
p=2
or

, we say that the VOA
lies on the boundary of the discrete series
. Since

In this section, we study how the vector bundles of coinvariants change as the Virasoro VOA moves through (the boundary of) the discrete series.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
V_{p,q}
be the Virasoro VOA of central charge
c_{p,q}
. Let
W_{\min}
(resp.
W_{\max}
) be the irreducible module of the minimal (resp. maximal) conformal weight. Define

generating function associated with
W_{\min}
. We define

similarly.

For
W_{\min}
, the generating function gradually changes as one moves along the boundary of discrete series. On the other hand, the pattern is more uniform for
W_{\max}
.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
V_{2,2l+1}
be a Virasoro VOA on the boundary of discrete series. Then,

when

is even,

f_{V_{2,2l+1},W_{\min}}(z)=\frac{1}{-z-\frac{1}{-z-\frac{1}{\frac{\ddots}{-z+1}}}},

when
l
is odd,

f_{V_{2,2l+1},W_{\min}}(z)=\frac{1}{-z-\frac{1}{-z-\frac{1}{\frac{\ddots}{-z-1}}}},

where each finite continued fraction on the right-hand side has
l
layers.

Proof.
The proof is in Appendix
D
.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
V_{p,q}
be a Virasoro VOA in the discrete series. If
p=2
or
q=2
(i.e.,

lies on the boundary of the discrete series), then

f_{V_{p,q},W_{\max}}(z)=\frac{1}{1-z}.

If

(i.e.,

f_{V_{p,q},W_{\max}}(z)=\frac{z}{1-z^{2}}.

When

or

[

]
. By propagation of vacua,

.
By

l
