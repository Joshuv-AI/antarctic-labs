1.
Introduction

Integer partitions play a central role in number theory and combinatorics. Since the foundational work of Euler
[
4
]
, partitions have been studied from a variety of perspectives, including generating functions by Andrews
[
2
]
, asymptotic analysis by Hardy and Ramanujan
[
5
]
, and geometric interpretations via Ferrers and Young diagrams by MacMahon
[
8
]
, among many others.

Define
\nu_{k}(N)
to be the
number of partitions of
N
in which exactly
k
sizes of part appear
. For example,
\nu_{2}(4)=2
since all partitions of 4 with exactly two part sizes are
(3,1)
and
(2,1,1)
, and
\nu_{3}(4)=0
since no partition of 4 has exactly three part sizes.

This counting function has been studied by MacMahon
[
9
]
, Andrews
[
1
]
, and Tani and Bouroubi
[
10
]
where the first two independently derived a compact formula for the case
k=2
as

\displaystyle\nu_{2}(N)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sigma_{0}(k)\sigma_{0}(N-k)-\sigma_{1}(N)+\sigma_{0}(N)\right)

where
\sigma_{0}(N)
is the
number of positive divisors of
N
and
\sigma_{1}(N)
is the
sum of the positive divisors of
N
. Keith
[
7
]
then later proved that
\nu_{2}(An+B)\equiv 0\pmod{4}
when
(A,B)
is one of (16,14), (36,30), (72,42), (196,70) and (252,114). This inspires us to revisit several results of Keith
[
3
]
using a finer combinatorial approach based on multiset partition. In particular, we study configurations of Young diagrams of partitions of
N
with exactly two part sizes in the arithmetic sequences considered by Keith
[
7
]
, and obtain the following congruence.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(A,B)\in\{(16,14),(36,30),(72,42),(196,70),(252,114)\}
and
N=An+B
for some nonnegative integer
n
. Then

\sum\limits_{1\leq k<\sqrt{N}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

The sum
\sum_{1\leq k<\sqrt{N}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})
in Theorem
1
and its asymptotic behavior were investigated by Hooley
[
6
]
in his study of representations of
N
as the sum of a square and a product where the number of such representations is equal to
\sum_{-\sqrt{N}<k<\sqrt{N}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})
.

The following corollaries are consequences of Theorem
1
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
N
be a positive integer such that
N\equiv 14\pmod{16}
or
N\equiv 70\pmod{196}
. Then the number of positive odd integers
k
such that
k^{2}<N
and
\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})\equiv 2\pmod{4}
is always even.

Corollary 3
.

Let
N
be a positive integer such that
N\equiv B\pmod{A}
where
(A,B)
is one of
(36,30),(72,42),
and
(252,114)
. Then

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k<\sqrt{N}\\
3\nmid k\end{subarray}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

To prove Theorem
1
, we first prove an auxiliary congruence relation in Theorem
4
by assuming a weaker assumption on the natural number
N
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
N
be a natural number satisfying

•

N
cannot be written as a sum of two squares, and

•

N=2m
for some odd integer
m
.

Then

\nu_{2}(N)+\sum\limits_{1\leq k<\sqrt{N}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}(N)\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

Note that according to the formula for
\nu_{2}(N)
given in the introduction,
the congruence in Theorem
4
can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma_{0}(N)+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sigma_{0}(k)\sigma_{0}(N-k)\right)+\sum_{1\leq k<\sqrt{N}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

To the best of our knowledge, this congruence does not appear explicitly in the existing literature.

We organize the rest of the paper as follows. In Section
2
, we discuss background and terminology. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
, which serves as our main result. In Section
4
, we apply Theorem
1
and use some elementary number theory to prove Corollaries

and
3
. Finally, in Section
5

2.
Background

Fix a natural number
n
. A
partition of
n
with exactly one part size
is a tuple
\vec{v}=(n_{1},n_{1},\ldots,n_{1})
of
k
copies of
n_{1}
for some

kn_{1}=n
.
A
partition of
n
with exactly two part sizes
is a tuple
\vec{v}=(n_{1},n_{1},\ldots,n_{1},n_{2},n_{2},\ldots,n_{2})
where
n_{1}>n_{2}\geq 1
such that if

appears
k_{1}
times and
n_{2}
appears
k_{2}
times, then
k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2}=n
.
In this paper, when we mention a
rectangle
, we mean a usual rectangular grid where each cell is a square. An
m\times n
rectangle
is a rectangle consisting of
m
rows and
n
columns. If
X
is an
m\times n
rectangle, denote by
X^{T}
the
n\times m
rectangle.

A
Young diagram for a partition
is the unique grid of cells where the cells are arranged in left-justified rows with the row lengths in non-increasing order. For each Young diagram consisting of
n
cells in
k
rows, we associate the diagram with the tuple
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k})
where
n_{i}
is the number of cells lying in the
i
-th row. By this interpretation, each Young diagram in
rectangular shape
consisting of
n
cells uniquely determines the partition of
n
with exactly one part size. Likewise, each Young diagram in
L-shape
uniquely determines the partition of
n
with exactly two part sizes. For example, Figure
1(a)

is interpreted as the partition
(3,3,3,3)
of
12
, and
Figure
1(b)
is interpreted as the partition
(5,5,5,2,2)
of
19
.

Any Young diagram corresponding to a partition with exactly two part sizes can be viewed as obtained by gluing two rectangles vertically, with the rectangle having the wider base placed on top. Conversely, every such L-shaped diagram can be decomposed in this way into a unique pair of rectangles. This observation motivates our approach and leads to a proof based on counting pairs of rectangles.

. Observe that any square of an integer is congruent to

or
4
mod
8
. Therefore,
8n+7-2l^{2}
is congruent to
5
or
7
mod
8
. Hence,
8n+7-2l^{2}
is odd and cannot be a square. Thus, the number of divisors of
8n+7-l^{2}
is even, and the number of divisors of
N-(2l)^{2}
is

\sigma_{0}(N-(2l)^{2})=2\sigma_{0}(8n+7-l^{2})\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

For the case
N\equiv 70\pmod{196}
, let
m
be a nonnegative integer such that
N=196m+70
and
r
be an integer. Consider the quantity
N-(2r)^{2}=196m+70-4r^{2}=2(98m+35-2r^{2})
. Suppose that
98m+35-2r^{2}
is a square. Observe that any square is congruent to
0,1,2,
and
4
mod
7
. Thus,
-2r^{2}
is congruent to one of
0,3,5,
and
6
mod
7
. Since
98m+35-2r^{2}
is a square,
r
is congruent to
0
mod
7
. Let
s
be an integer such that
r=7s
. Then we get that

98m+35-2r^{2}=98m+35-2(7s)^{2}=7(14m+5-14s^{2}).

Notice that
98m+35-2r^{2}
is divisible by
7
but not by
7^{2}
. Hence,
98m+35-2r^{2}
is not a square, leading to a contradiction. Therefore,
98m+35-2r^{2}
is not a square and also odd. Thus, the number of divisors of
98m+35-2r^{2}
is even, and the number of divisors of
N-(2r)^{2}
is

\sigma_{0}(N-(2l)^{2})=2\sigma_{0}(98m+35-2r^{2})\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

From these two cases, we get that
\sigma_{0}(N-(2l)^{2})
is divisible by
4
, and

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{1\leq k<\sqrt{N}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k<\sqrt{N}\\
k\text{ is odd}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k<\sqrt{N}\\
k\text{ is even}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2}),\\
&\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k<\sqrt{N}\\
k\text{ is odd}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})\pmod{4}.\end{split}

Due to the fact that
N
cannot be written as a sum of two squares, the number of divisors of
N-k^{2}
is even for all integer
k
. By Theorem
1
, we get that the amount of odd positive integer
k
such that
k^{2}<N
and
\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})\equiv 2\pmod{4}
is even.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3
.

Observe that
N=6n
for some integer
n
such that
6
and
n
are co-prime. We will show that
\sigma_{0}(N-(3a)^{2})
is a multiple of
4
for all integer
a
with
N>(3a)^{2}
. Suppose that there exist positive integers
a
and
b
such that
b=N-(3a)^{2}
and
\sigma_{0}(b)
is not a multiple of
4
. Since
N
is divisible by
3
but not by
3^{2}
, there exists a positive integer
b^{\prime}
such that
b=3b^{\prime}
and both
3
and
b^{\prime}
are co-prime. Notice that
\sigma_{0}(b)=\sigma_{0}(3b^{\prime})=2\sigma_{0}(b^{\prime})
is not a multiple of
4
. So,
\sigma_{0}(b^{\prime})
is odd, meaning that there exists an integer
c
such that
b^{\prime}=c^{2}
. Thus, we get that
6n=N=(3a)^{2}+b=9a^{2}+3c^{2}
, implying that
2n=3a^{2}+c^{2}
. Since
6
and
n
are co-prime, we have
3a^{2}+c^{2}=2n\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, contradicting that
3a^{2}+c^{2}
is congruent to one of
0,1,
and
3
mod
4
. Hence, if
N>(3a)^{2}
, then
\sigma_{0}(N-(3a)^{2})
is a multiple of
4
for all integer

. Therefore,

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k<\sqrt{N}\\
3\nmid k\end{subarray}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})\equiv\sum\limits_{1\leq k<\sqrt{N}}\sigma_{0}(N-k^{2})\equiv 0\pmod{4}

by Theorem
1
.
∎

Concluding Remarks

As shown in the proof of our main result, Theorem

, the theorem currently requires rather restrictive conditions on

which corresponds to the pair

in Theorem

. Therefore, it remains to consider the case when
