Critical Erdős-Rényi digraph: all eigenvectors away from zero are delocalized
Johannes Alt, Sarah Timhadjelt

Abstract
We consider the adjacency matrix of the directed Erdős-Rényi graph. As long as the expected degree is larger than the logarithm of the number of vertices, the graph is connected, we show that all eigenvectors are completely delocalized. Below this critical scale, we prove eigenvector delocalization if the corresponding eigenvalue is away from zero. This contrasts the undirected or Hermitian setting, where large eigenvalues have localized eigenvectors [ ADK21a ] . Our results also hold for sparse random matrices with independent entries, which can be viewed as weighted Erdős-Rényi digraphs.

\chi_{\#}\max_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x,y\in[2N]}}{{x\neq y\neq{\widehat{x}}}}}\bigl|G_{xy}(w,\mathrm{i}\eta)\bigr|=o(1),

(2.17)

\chi_{\#}\bigl|g(w,\mathrm{i}\eta)-\mathrm{i}v(w,\eta)\bigr|=o(1).

(2.18)

For

, the previous theorem follows directly from

below, which has precise error estimates and holds as long as

. Its proof takes up most of this work and is presented at the end of
Section

4
below.
For

, we automatically have
\chi_{\#}\equiv 1
with very high probability. Therefore, the statement and proof of the local law for
H(w)
are simpler and presented in

below. After the statement of
Lemma

5.1
, we deduce
2.2
in this regime.

Remark 2.2

(Special cases of
v_{\beta}
)
.

With
v
from
Equation

2.10
, the integral

-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta_{w}v(w,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta

(2.19)

gives the density of the circular law, i.e. the uniform probability measure on the unit disc in the complex plane. This obviously corresponds to
\beta^{1}=\beta^{2}=1
. Analogously, for
\beta^{1}=\beta^{2}=\frac{k}{k-1}

and a fixed

with

replaced by

yields the Brown measure of the sum of

free Haar unitaries,
also called oriented Kesten-McKay law, which coincides with the Brown measure of the directed

k
-regular tree. For general

and
\beta^{2}
, the analogous construction yields a much richer family of probability measures on
\mathbb{C}
.

3.1

In this section, we derive the approximate self-consistent equations for the entries of the resolvent

of the Hermitization
H(w)
, see

2.8
and
Equation

2.7
, respectively. Moreover, we estimate a first set of error terms in these equations.

To formulate these results, we now introduce some notation used throughout the remainder of this work.
Let
Q=(Q_{xy})_{x,y\in[N]}\in\mathrm{M}_{N}(\CC)
be a matrix. For each
T\subset[N]
, we define the matrix

Q^{(T)}:=(Q_{xy}\mathds{1}\left(\{x,y\}\cap T=\emptyset\right))_{x,y\in[N]}\in\mathrm{M}_{N}(\CC).

(3.1)

It corresponds to the minor of

, where all rows and columns of

with index in

are removed.
If

and

, we set

. Moreover, we set

for each

and

Let
T\subset[N]
. For the Hermitization
H(w)
from
Equation

2.7
and

,
we define the matrix

H^{[T]}:=H^{[T]}(w):=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&M^{(T)}-w\\
(M^{(T)})^{\ast}-\overline{w}&0\end{array}\right).

(3.2)

Since
H(w)^{[T]}
is Hermitian,
H(w)^{[T]}-z
is invertible for each

and we denote the
corresponding resolvent by

G^{[T]}=G^{[T]}(w,z)=(H^{[T]}(w)-z)^{-1}.

(3.3)

Furthermore, we use the following notion for the indices in
[2N]
. For
x\in[2N]
and
T\subset[N]
, we set

{\widehat{x}}:=x+N\mod 2N,\qquad\qquad{\widehat{T}}:=\{x+N:\penalty\ x\in T\}.

For a subset

, we write for a summation over the elements of

, which are not in

\sum_{x}^{(T)}\,\,:=\sum_{x\in[N]\backslash T}.

In the next lemma, we derive self-consistent equations, which couple the diagonal entries of
G
and the diagonal entries of its off-diagonal
N\times N
-blocks.

Lemma 3.1

(Approximate self-consistent equations)
.

For all
x\in[N]
,
w\in\CC
and
z\in\CC_{+}
we have

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=-zG_{xx}-\bigg(\sum_{y}^{(x)}|X_{xy}|^{2}G^{[x]}_{{\widehat{y}}{\widehat{y}}}\bigg)G_{xx}-wG_{{\widehat{x}}x}+Y^{1}_{x}G_{xx}+Z^{1}_{x}G_{{\widehat{x}}x}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-zG_{x{\widehat{x}}}-\bigg(\sum_{y}^{(x)}|X_{xy}|^{2}G^{[x]}_{{\widehat{y}}{\widehat{y}}}\bigg)G_{x{\widehat{x}}}-wG_{{\widehat{x}}{\widehat{x}}}+Y^{1}_{x}G_{x{\widehat{x}}}+Z^{1}_{x}G_{{\widehat{x}}{\widehat{x}}}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-zG_{{\widehat{x}}x}-\bigg(\sum_{y}^{(x)}|X_{yx}|^{2}G^{[x]}_{yy}\bigg)G_{{\widehat{x}}x}-\overline{w}G_{xx}+Y^{2}_{x}G_{{\widehat{x}}x}+Z^{2}_{x}G_{xx}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=-zG_{{\widehat{x}}{\widehat{x}}}-\bigg(\sum_{y}^{(x)}|X_{yx}|^{2}G^{[x]}_{yy}\bigg)G_{{\widehat{x}}{\widehat{x}}}-\overline{w}G_{x{\widehat{x}}}+Y^{2}_{x}G_{{\widehat{x}}{\widehat{x}}}+Z^{2}_{x}G_{x{\widehat{x}}}

where the error terms are given by

\displaystyle Y_{x}^{1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{a\neq b}^{(x)}X_{xa}G^{[x]}_{{\widehat{a}}{\widehat{b}}}\overline{X}_{xb}-\sum_{a,b}^{(x)}\biggl(\frac{f}{N}\left[X_{xa}G_{{\widehat{a}}{\widehat{b}}}^{[x]}+G_{{\widehat{a}}{\widehat{b}}}^{[x]}\overline{X}_{xb}\right]+\frac{f^{2}}{N^{2}}G_{{\widehat{a}}{\widehat{b}}}^{[x]}\biggr),

\displaystyle Z_{x}^{1}

\displaystyle=X_{xx}-\sum_{a,b}^{(x)}\left(X_{xa}+\frac{f}{N}\right)G_{{\widehat{a}}b}^{[x]}\left(X_{bx}+\frac{f}{N}\right)

\displaystyle Y_{x}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{a\neq b}^{(x)}\overline{X}_{ax}G_{ab}^{[x]}X_{bx}-\sum_{a,b}^{(x)}\biggl(\frac{f}{N}\left[\overline{X}_{ax}G_{ab}^{[x]}+G_{ab}^{[x]}X_{bx}\right]+\frac{f^{2}}{N^{2}}G_{ab}^{[x]}\biggr)

\displaystyle Z_{x}^{2}

\displaystyle=\overline{X}_{xx}-\sum_{a,b}^{(x)}\left(\overline{X}_{ax}+\frac{f}{N}\right)G_{a{\widehat{b}}}^{[x]}\left(\overline{X}_{xb}+\frac{f}{N}\right).

Proof.

We fix
x\in[N]
. Introducing the matrices

\displaystyle A_{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-z&M_{xx}-w\\
\overline{M}_{xx}-\overline{w}&-z\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle B_{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots 0&M_{x1}\penalty\ M_{x2}\cdots M_{xx-1}\penalty\ M_{xx+1}\cdots M_{xN}\\
\overline{M}_{1x}\penalty\ \overline{M}_{2x}\cdots\overline{M}_{x-1x}\penalty\ \overline{M}_{x+1x}\cdots\overline{M}_{Nx}&0\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots 0\end{array}\right)

we note that up to relabelling the rows and columns,

G=\begin{pmatrix}A_{x}&B_{x}\\
(B_{x})^{*}&H^{[x]}-z\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.

Therefore, the Schur’s complement formula
yields for the upper left
2\times 2
-block

\begin{pmatrix}G_{xx}&G_{x{\widehat{x}}}\\
G_{{\widehat{x}}x}&G_{{\widehat{x}}{\widehat{x}}}\end{pmatrix}=\big(A_{x}-B_{x}G^{[x]}(B_{x})^{*}\big)^{-1}.

We multiply this from the left by
A_{x}-B_{x}G^{[x]}(B_{x})^{*}
, which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Trace identity of resolvent blocks)
.

Let
0<d<N/2
,
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
and
w\in\CC
.
Then

\sum_{y\in[N]}G_{yy}=\sum_{y\in[N]}G_{{\widehat{y}}{\widehat{y}}}.

Proof.

We give the proof for
T=\emptyset
. The general case follows by replacing
H(w)
by
H(w)^{[T]}
. For

, we deduce from the Schur complement formula that

\displaystyle G=\begin{pmatrix}z((M-w)(M-w)^{*}-z^{2})^{-1}&((M-w)(M-w)^{*}-z^{2})^{-1}(M-w)\\
(M-w)^{*}((M-w)(M-w)^{*}-z^{2})^{-1}&z((M-w)^{*}(M-w)-z^{2})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\left(M-w\right)\left(M-w\right)^{\ast}

\sum_{y\in[N]}G_{yy}=\sum_{y\in[N]}G_{{\widehat{y}}{\widehat{y}}}

Lemma 3.5

(Bound on normalized degrees)

, we conclude from Markov’s inequality and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\biggl(\beta_{x}^{1}\geq\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{\log N}{d}\right)\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\biggl((\mathcal{C}-1)\biggl(1+\frac{\log N}{d}\biggr)\biggr)^{-r}\mathbb{E}\biggl(\sum_{y}^{(x)}\bigl(\left|X_{xy}\right|^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left|X_{xy}\right|^{2}\bigr)\biggr)^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\frac{4}{\mathcal{C}-1}\biggl(1+\frac{\log N}{d}\biggr)^{-1}\biggl(\frac{r}{d}\vee\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}}\biggr)\biggr)^{r}

for each

and
\mathcal{C}>0

We choose

∎

3.2

We now introduce a notion of typical vertices for the sparse non-Hermitian matrices from

Definition 3.1

(Typical vertices)
.

For

Lemma 6.1
