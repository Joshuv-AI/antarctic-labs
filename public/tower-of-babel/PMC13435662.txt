The Symptom Clusters and Core Symptoms in Patients with Lung Cancer After Surgery: A Longitudinal Network Analysis
Authors: Xueqi Tian, Yuening Dai, Zhiyi Dong, Wenli Shi, Xiong Qin, Yong Yang, Huiling Zhou, Yabin Gong, Lijing Jiao, Ling Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo create a symptom network for patients who have had lung cancer surgery, pinpoint the primary symptoms, and investigate the factors that influence symptom clusters.MethodsA longitudinal observational design was adopted in the present study. Postoperative symptoms were measured via the MD Anderson Symptom Inventory-Traditional Chinese Medical version (MDASI-TCM). Principal component analysis was performed to extract distinct symptom clusters. Multivariate logistic regression models were constructed to explore independent influencing factors of each symptom cluster. Extended Bayesian Information Criterion Graphical Lasso was then applied to identify core and bridging symptoms within the pooled symptom network.Results205 patients with lung cancer who underwent surgical resection were included in this study. At 24 hours postoperatively, pain, fatigue, disturbed sleep, shortness of breath, and loss of appetite exhibited the highest prevalence among all assessed symptoms. At the three months follow-up, elevated prevalence persisted for fatigue, shortness of breath, and disturbed sleep. Five symptom clusters were identified: digestive system symptom cluster (dry mouth, bitter taste, constipation, and abdominal fullness), emotional symptom cluster (problem remembering, sad, distressed, fidgety, and disturbed sleep), cancer-related complex symptom cluster (fatigue, nausea, lack of appetite, shortness of breath, and pain), respiratory system symptom cluster (coughing, and expectoration), and autonomic nerve-related somatic symptom cluster (numbness or tingling, sweating, and palpitation). Based on the strength centrality index, the core symptoms of patients were fatigue. According to bridge strength, the bridge symptoms were lack of appetite and dry mouth. The edge weight difference test showed that the symptom group exhibited higher stability.ConclusionLung cancer patients had surgery experienced five symptom clusters. The fatigue, lack of appetite, and dry mouth should be given priority for intervention.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the malignant tumor with the highest incidence and mortality in China, according to the latest 2024 national cancer statistics.1 About 85% of lung cancer cases are non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and around 30% of them are diagnosed with resectable early- and mid-stage NSCLC at initial presentation.2 Surgical resection remains the gold-standard treatment for early-stage NSCLC.3 After curative surgery, patients with stage IA, IB, IIA, and IIB NSCLC achieve 5-year survival rates of 77% - 92%, 68%, 60%, and 53%, respectively. Although video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) has greatly reduced the incidence of postoperative complications,4 postoperative symptoms including cough, discomfort, and fatigue remain prevalent and severely impair quality of life.5,6 Longitudinal fluctuations in the prevalence and severity of symptoms within the first three months after NSCLC resection have been well documented, highlighting the necessity of targeted postoperative rehabilitation interventions.7 Accumulating clinical practice and literature have documented that NSCLC patients frequently experience short-term postoperative symptoms including excessive sweating following VATS, yet this clinical manifestation remains understudied in existing research.8,9 Accordingly, the present study adopted the MD Anderson Symptom Inventory-TCM Module (MDASI-TCM), a scale incorporating symptom items that better align with integrated Chinese and Western clinical manifestations, to comprehensively evaluate patients’ postoperative symptom burden.

A symptom cluster is defined as two or more co-occurring symptoms with mutual interactions. Such symptoms share temporal consistency, common underlying pathological mechanisms and clinical outcomes, which distinct them from other symptom clusters.10 Further research has demonstrated that symptom clusters have a more detrimental influence on treatment results, prognosis, functional status, and quality of life than individual symptoms because of the synergistic effect.11 By visualizing and quantitatively characterizing the interrelationships between symptoms, identifying core symptoms, and elucidating the intricate structure of symptom clusters, network analysis (NA) can thoroughly evaluate the linkages between symptoms.12 Timely identification of key postoperative symptoms can provide an evidence base for holistic, multi-dimensional symptom management strategies.

Previous studies exploring symptom clusters among NSCLC patients have mostly focused on individuals receiving chemotherapy or immunotherapy, or postoperative rehabilitation cohorts. Few literature have evaluated symptom clusters throughout the recovery period after curative lung resection.13–15 This study aimed to characterize the distribution characteristics of symptom clusters in early NSCLC patients after VATS and analyze the related influencing factors, so as to provide a reference basis for the rehabilitation of patients undergoing NSCLC surgery.

Methods

Participants

The following were the requirements for inclusion: (1) Patients with pulmonary space-occupying lesions who received VATS resection with pathological confirmation of NSCLC; (2) Aged 18–80 years old, regardless of gender; (3) Consent to participate in the study and sign the informed consent form; (4) Agree to complete the questionnaire assessment. The following were the exclusion criteria: (1) Presence of uncontrolled severe systemic diseases; (2) Patients receiving open thoracotomy rather than VATS; (3) Individuals taking part in other clinical trials during the study period; (4) Individuals with cognitive dysfunction or severe psychiatric disorders.

Trial Design

Convenience sampling was used in this longitudinal observational study to enroll early-stage NSCLC patients from two surgical treatment groups who underwent surgery at two tertiary hospitals between February 2023 and January 2024 (Yueyang Hospital of Integrated Traditional Chinese and Western Medicine affiliated with Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine and Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital affiliated with Tongji University). In accordance with current clinical guidelines, participants received standardized postoperative care. Patients were recommended to access emergency services or telemedicine consultation if severe postoperative complications occurred. The study was carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Yueyang Hospital (Number: 2020–038). Before being enrolled, each patient gave written informed consent. During the observation period, no patients received TCM decoctions.

Sample Size

The commonly cited guideline for factor analysis, which calls for 5–10 cases per variable, was used to calculate the sample size. The minimum sample size for the analysis of the 24 MDASI-TCM symptom variables (pain, fatigue, nausea, sleep disturbance, distress, shortness of breath, forgetfulness, poor appetite, lethargy, dry mouth, sadness, vomiting, numbness, cough, phlegm, palpitations, sweating, bitter mouth, mouth ulcer, diarrhea, constipation, irritability, hot hands and feet, and abdominal distension) was 120. To provide sufficient statistical power, 150 individuals were recruited, accounting for 20% attrition with an allowance for a 20% loss to follow-up.

Measures and Data Collection

General Information

Demographic characteristics including age, body mass index (BMI), educational attainment, occupational status, smoking history, and medical history was collected. Preoperative evaluations encompassed hematological parameters and pulmonary function. Surgical and pathological data were recorded, including resection margins, operative site, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, histopathological type, and tumor staging.

TCM Constitution

According to the Classification and Judgment of TCM Constitution of the Chinese Association of Traditional Chinese Medicine, patients were assessed for TCM constitution one day preoperatively.16 When a participant satisfies the criteria for two or more constitution types simultaneously, the constitution category with the highest total score is assigned. Details are provided in Supplementary Tables 1A and B.

Symptoms

The symptoms were assessed using the MD Anderson Symptom Inventory (MDASI), which is a multi-symptom self-assessment scale developed by MD Anderson Cancer Center of the University of Texas in 2000, frequently utilized with cancer patients undergoing various treatment regimens and histological subtypes.17,18 MDASI-TCM has been demonstrated to possess high reliability in previous investigations, with the Cronbach’s α coefficient of its Symptom Severity Scale reaching 0.90, which reflects a high level of internal consistency.18 There are 24 symptom items in the MDASI-TCM, which was utilized in this investigation. In the present study, Cronbach’s α coefficient was employed to evaluate the internal consistency. Results showed that the overall Cronbach’s α coefficient stood at 0.94 (95% CI: 0.94–0.95), and the standardized Cronbach’s α coefficient also reached 0.94, indicating that the scale exhibits excellent internal consistency. Removal of any single item did not increase the α coefficient, which remained stable at 0.94. Moreover, item-total correlation coefficients ranged from 0.54 to 0.83, all exceeding 0.50. These findings verify that the items have desirable homogeneity without redundancy, thus confirming the stable structure and rational item selection of the scale.19 Each symptom item is scored from 0 (no symptoms) to 10 (worst imaginable symptoms) to quantify symptom severity. Symptom incidence was characterized as an MDASI-TCM score ≥1 within the preceding 24 hours.

The purpose of the study was explained to the patient, and the investigation was conducted after the informed consent was obtained. Standardized training for researchers was conducted, which specifically included: systematic study of the item definitions, scoring criteria, and the 24-hour assessment window; standardized verbal instructions were established. Researchers were required to instruct patients to rate symptoms according to their worst condition over the prior 24 h, while suggestive or biased prompts were prohibited. Symptom information was obtained via face-to-face interviews at 24 hours after the end of surgery (T1). Following discharge, trained research staff administered standardized questionnaires via scheduled telephone follow-ups at 2 weeks (T2), 1 month (T3), 2 months (T4), and 3 months (T5) postoperatively. The 24 symptoms of MDASI-TCM were evaluated through self-completed questionnaires, which were distributed electronically via SoJump (https://www.wjx.cn). The patients were asked to rate the severity of their symptoms based on the most severe condition within the past 24 hours. Participants were encouraged to raise questions and seek clarification when necessary. Questionnaires with a completion rate above 95% were deemed valid. A total of 1025 questionnaires were distributed, and following verification of data authenticity, 893 valid responses were collected, yielding a valid response rate of 87.12%.

Symptom Clusters

The total score of all items within the symptom group was divided by the number of items to obtain the average severity of that symptom group. A symptom cluster was considered present if the severity score was ≥1.

Statistical Analysis

The examination of the symptom clusters at this time point did not include the dropout cases. While categorical data were reported using frequency (%), measurement data were expressed as mean (±standard deviation) or median (interquartile range). The t-test was used to compare groups for measurement data that were normally distributed, whereas the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for data that were not normally distributed. When comparing multiple groups, one-way ANOVA or the Kruskal–Wallis test were used, depending on the situation. The χ2-test was used to compare categorical variables.

Symptom clusters were extracted using principal component analysis (PCA), and the factor contribution to total variance was ascertained using the maximum variance orthogonal rotation approach. Factor selection followed these criteria: ① eigenvalue ≥1; ② symptom loading ≥0.60 under each factor, with symptom attribution determined by the highest loading if multiple factors met this threshold; ③ each factor included at least two symptoms; and ④ factor naming aligned with professional categorization. Logistic regression was utilized to analyze influencing factors.

We proceed with network analysis to identify core symptoms via Extended Bayesian Information Criterion Graphical Lasso (EBICglasso).20–22 This approach integrates L1 regularization and the Extended Bayesian Information Criterion (EBIC) to produce sparse, robust network structures for high-dimensional data with modest sample sizes. Data concatenation was performed to pool measurements across five follow-up time points to generate a combined symptom network within the first three months after NSCLC resection. Separate network models were fitted for each time point individually. The node with the largest centrality index can be regarded as the core symptom with the greatest influence. The centrality indicators mainly include strength centrality, closeness centrality and betweenness centrality, among which strength centrality served as the primary indicator.23–25 Strength centrality reflects the strength of the direct association between a symptom and other symptoms in the network. Its numerical value indicates the driving effect of that symptom on the overall symptom network, which is in line with the influence of the core symptom on other symptoms within the symptom cluster. Therefore, the symptom with the highest strength centrality of each symptom group was determined as the core symptom. The case-dropping subset bootstrap method was used to perform the centrality stability test.24 Centrality stability was evaluated by repeatedly re-estimating network models using subsets with gradually reduced sample sizes. The correlation between the centrality index ranking and the ranking in the original network can be used to assess the centrality index’s stability. Stability of centrality indices was quantified via the correlation stability coefficient (CS-coefficient). A CS-coefficient above 0.5 is widely recognized as the threshold for acceptable stability.23 Bridge strength was adopted as the primary indicator to identify bridge symptoms. Nodes with higher bridge strength exert stronger mediating connectivity across distinct symptom clusters, and such symptoms were therefore defined as bridge symptoms in the present study.25,26 The bridge symptom was defined as the symptom with the highest bridge strength rating. To confirm the reliability of the network topology, we also evaluated the variety of nodal characteristics and the accuracy of edge weights and nodes. Missing data were assessed using Little’s missing completely at random (MCAR) test. Multiple imputation (MI) was applied to handle missing questionnaire item values at each follow-up time point. Sensitivity analysis were conducted by comparing the consistency of core symptoms before and after the imputation.

Using the statistical packages “mice”, “networktools”, “bootnet”, “naniar”, “qgraph”, “tidyverse”, and “psych” the statistical analysis and graphics were carried out using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics, Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.5.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Figures were created using GraphPad Prism 9.0.0. P<0.05 was deemed statistically significant, and α=0.05 was employed as a bilateral test.

Results

Patient Characteristics

From February 20, 2023, to November 20, 2023, 260 postoperative patients with NSCLC were screened, with 205 included in the analysis (Supplementary Figure 1). During actual follow-up, cumulative attrition reached 25.37% at the final time point T5, marginally exceeding the pre-study estimation. To avoid selection bias caused by artificially truncating recruitment, all consecutive eligible patients admitted within the predefined enrollment window were included, yielding a final analytical sample of 205 participants. The larger sample size helps mitigate sampling error in covariance matrix estimation for EBICglasso, stabilizes the identification of sparse symptom networks, and does not alter the reliability of core study findings. Symptom Clusters

After principal component analysis with maximum variance orthogonal rotation, the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) values for T1, T2, T3, T4, and T5 were 0.863, 0.882, 0.886, 0.883, and 0.863, respectively. The corresponding cumulative variance contribution rates were 57.835%, 65.024%, 59.002%, 63.935%, and 60.074%. The eligibility for factor analysis was confirmed by Bartlett’s sphericity tests, which produced P <0.001 for every time point. The range of symptom factor loadings was 0.600 to 0.892 (Supplementary Table 2A). To facilitate the description of the longitudinal changes in the symptom clusters, we have divided them into 5 groups. This classification scheme was determined based on the PCA results, combined with clinical experience and existing published evidence.27–29 Cluster 1 represented the digestive system symptom cluster, encompassing four symptoms: dry mouth, bitter taste, constipation, and abdominal fullness. Cluster 2 corresponded to the emotional symptom cluster with five symptoms: problem remembering, sad, distressed, fidgety, and disturbed sleep. Cluster 3 was defined as cancer-related complex symptom cluster, covering five symptoms: fatigue, nausea, lack of appetite, shortness of breath, and pain. Cluster 4 constituted the respiratory system symptom cluster containing two symptoms: coughing, and expectoration. Cluster 5 referred to autonomic nerve-related somatic symptom cluster, which included three symptoms: numbness or tingling, sweating, and palpitation (Supplementary Table 2B).

From one day to three months after surgery, the five symptom clusters’ occurrence and severity gradually decreased (all P<0.001, all time interaction P<0.001). The digestive, respiratory, and cancer-related complex clusters maintained relatively high incidence levels at the 3-month follow-up, whereas the respiratory and cancer-related complex clusters retained elevated severity throughout the observation period (Figure 2A and B). The Influenced Factors of Symptom Clusters

There were 29 cases (14.15%) of missing LDH data and 57 cases (27.80%) of missing heparin-binding protein data. The mean imputation was used to fill in the missing data. ROC analysis determined the optimal cut-off values for LDH and heparin-binding protein as 170.90 and 50.67, respectively, based on the maximum Youden index. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression identified operative time (OR=0.299, 95% CI: 0.146–0.614, P<0.001) as a factor for digestive system symptom cluster. Age (OR=1.049, 95% CI: 1.019–1.081, P<0.001) and main constitution (OR=0.391, 95% CI: 0.176–0.869, P=0.021) influenced emotional symptom cluster. Gender (OR=0.418, 95% CI: 0.186–0.940, P=0.035) and operative time (OR=0.354, 95% CI: 0.144–0.847, P=0.024) affected cancer-related complex symptom cluster. Operative time (OR=0.333, 95% CI: 0.134–0.827, P=0.018) influenced respiratory system symptom cluster. Gender (OR=0.302, 95% CI: 0.141–0.646, P=0.002), diabetes history (OR=0.263, 95% CI: 0.077–0.894, P=0.032), and operative time (OR=0.368, 95% CI: 0.165–0.821, P=0.015) impacted autonomic nerve-related somatic symptom cluster (Supplementary Tables 3A–E and Figure 2C–G).

Network Analysis and Core Symptom

The thickness of edges denotes the magnitude of correlations between symptoms. The symptom pairs with strong positive correlations within three months after surgery were pain - fatigue (r = 0.85), fatigue - shortness of breath (r = 0.73), and coughing - expectoration (r = 0.80), respectively. In addition, there were significant correlations between distressed and sad, as well as fatigued and nausea at T3, whereas there was a high association between disturbed sleep and distressed at T4 and T5 (Figure 3). Ranked by strength centrality, the most prominent patient-reported symptoms were fatigue, pain, and shortness of breath. Fatigue, appetite loss, and coughing were the top three symptoms for betweenness centrality. Fatigue, pain, and appetite loss were the top three symptoms for closeness centrality (Figure 4A). Fatigue was the core symptom for each of the T1 - T5 periods (Supplementary Figure 2). Bridge strength indicated that dry mouth and appetite loss were the bridge symptoms (Figure 4C). Accuracy and Stability Estimation

The stability of both strength and bridge strength declined markedly when the sample size is reduced. The CS coefficient at all time periods was 0.750 (min: 0.672, max: 1.000, Figure 4B), suggesting that the core symptoms remained robust even if the network was re-estimated using fewer participants. The node difference test results are shown in Figure 4D. Fatigue was the most central hub node in the network. The heterogeneity results of edge weights are shown in Figure 4E. Symptom pairs pain - fatigue, fatigue - shortness of breath, coughing - expectoration, and disturbed sleep - distressed exhibited statistically significantly higher edge weights relative to all other pairwise associations in the network. The excellent accuracy of the symptom network was shown by the results of the accuracy test for edge weights, as shown in Supplementary Figure 3.

Sensitivity Analysis

Sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the potential influence of missing data on study outcomes, the differences in baseline characteristics among patients who completed follow-up at each time point were further compared (Supplementary Table 4). The results showed that there were no statistically significant differences in key baseline characteristics among patients at each time point (all P >0.05). According to Little’s MCAR test, the data were missing completely at random (χ2=0.489, df=1, P=0.484). After MI, fatigue was the core symptom throughout the first three postoperative months and at every time point T1–T5 (Supplementary Figure 4), consistent with the results before MI. Therefore, the missing data in this study did not introduce substantial selection bias, and the results were relatively robust.

Discussion

According to earlier research, postoperative symptoms in NSCLC patients change dynamically over time. Within eight weeks postoperatively, shortness of breath, fatigue, coughing, distress, and sleep disturbance were the high-burden symptoms.30 Cough, pain, sleep problems, shortness of breath, and fatigue were the most commonly reported symptoms at discharge.31 In order to investigate the staging symptoms and the longitudinal dynamic trajectories of symptom clusters in patients following NSCLC surgery, we carried out this longitudinal observational study.

On average, all assessed symptoms gradually alleviated across the 3-month postoperative follow-up period. Specifically, cough and phlegm production peaked in incidence at 2 weeks and postoperative day 1, respectively, while shortness of breath remained relatively elevated from postoperative day 1 to 2 weeks. Postoperatively, factors including chest wall injury, respiratory muscle laceration, and neural reflex inhibition reduced lung tidal volume and raised residual volume,32 induced pulmonary atrophy,33 and caused retention of airway secretions. These changes narrowed the effective ventilation area and triggered ventilatory dysfunction,34 lowered maximal oxygen uptake, and markedly impaired exercise tolerance.35 Consequently, patients frequently experienced persistent shortness of breath and fatigue. Postoperative indwelling of thoracic drainage tubes was the main cause of pain. After the drainage tubes were removed, chronic persistent postoperative pain often developed secondary to intercostal nerve injury or irritation; such discomfort mainly presented as dull pain, tenderness, numbness, and mild burning sensations.36

Symptom clusters are defined as two or more interrelated, stable symptoms that frequently co-occur.37 Previous research has identified multiple symptom clusters in NSCLC patients at 1 month postoperatively: respiratory, pain-fatigue-sleep disturbance, psychological-emotional, neurological, and digestive symptom clusters.38 Another study reported five distinct clusters among patients within two weeks after lobectomy: respiratory-sleep, digestive, respiratory-related, pain-related, and emotional clusters.39 In this study, we assess 24 symptoms using the MDASI-TCM. In the symptom network, we delineated five distinct clusters: digestive system symptom cluster (including dry mouth, bitter taste, constipation, and abdominal fullness), emotional symptom cluster (such as problem remembering, sad, distressed, fidgety, and disturbed sleep), cancer-related complex symptom cluster (comprising fatigue, nausea, lack of appetite, shortness of breath, and pain), respiratory system symptom cluster (coughing, and expectoration), and autonomic nerve-related somatic symptom cluster (numbness or tingling, sweating, and palpitation). The incidence of these symptom clusters gradually decreased within 3 months after the surgery. The symptom clusters with a relatively high prevalence at 3 months after the surgery were: cancer-related complex symptom cluster (71.90%), respiratory system symptom cluster (69.28%), autonomic nerve-related somatic symptom cluster (54.25%). While, the lowest incidence was digestive system symptom cluster (40.52%).

In addition to highlighting the primary symptoms, network analysis enables researchers to pinpoint the most significant symptoms within the network structure, which helps researchers and medical professionals create individualized and focused treatment plans. Higher values indicate that the symptom is more significant within the network. The intensity represents the total sum of the absolute edge weights between the node and its immediately related nodes. Within three months following surgery, fatigue emerged as the primary symptom for NSCLC patients in the network analysis. The incidence and severity of fatigue were quite high, indicating that fatigue is a significant symptom that requires attention for NSCLC patients following surgery. According to one study, compared to other typical severe symptoms (including pain, shortness of breath, and sleep disturbances), the median time for postoperative exhaustion to recover to baseline is longer.7 Fatigue is a chronic, subjective feeling of fatigue that interferes with cognitive, emotional, and physical processes and is challenging to treat with rest or sleep. Fatigue brought on by cancer may also be a problem for many survivors, further impairing their physical and emotional well-being.40,41 Surgery may reduce physical function and exacerbate fatigue by damaging the structure of the mitochondria and interfering with their energy metabolism.42 These patients’ weariness may be exacerbated by pain-related stress and the postoperative inflammatory response.43 Furthermore, by prolonging sympathetic nerve activation, raising inflammation, and impairing slumber, postoperative pain and sleep disturbances may worsen tiredness.44 In addition to limiting physical activity, postoperative discomfort can cause muscle weakness and relaxation, which can eventually lead to weariness.45 Lung function and weariness are related; higher degrees of fatigue are linked to decreased FEV1 and FVC values.46 Network analysis was used to identify the fundamental symptoms of each symptom cluster and to clarify the intervention objectives for symptom cluster treatment. Focusing on fundamental symptoms might be prioritized by medical personnel. In order to increase the effectiveness of symptom cluster management and conserve medical resources, medical personnel might choose symptom management strategies and material based on the evaluation results of patients’ core symptoms. Early initiation of low-intensity home-based exercise after hospital discharge can improve self-management efficacy among postoperative NSCLC patients.47 Moreover, intensified symptom monitoring and standardized nursing intervention can effectively reduce overall symptom burden and promote long-term functional recovery.48

According to the bridge strength centrality index, the bridge symptoms are the essential nodes that link various symptom clusters in the network structure. The greater the symptom cross-module transmission, the higher the bridge strength centrality, which is calculated by adding the absolute values of the edge weights linking various modules.49 Even though they are not always the most severe, high bridge strength symptoms act as mediators for cross-module transmission and can cause symptom clusters to worsen at progressively higher levels. The bridge symptom with the strongest bridge strength in the symptom network in this study is appetite loss. It is linked to the symptom clusters for the respiratory system, digestive system, emotional system, and autonomic nerve-related somatic. This indicates that appetite loss is present in several symptom clusters and links several symptom clusters. Furthermore, it has been discovered that dry mouth has a strong bridging impact. According to a study, it is possible to stop patients from transferring from one condition to another by eliminating bridge-like symptoms.49 This is due to the bridge nodes or edges’ superior information transmission capabilities and ability to link various clusters. Lack of appetite and dry mouth can represent key intervention targets for postoperative lung cancer rehabilitation, which can enhance the overall efficacy of symptom management during this recovery period. Surgical trauma triggers the release of multiple pro-inflammatory mediators. These inflammatory signals modulate central appetite regulatory neurons: they downregulate the expression of orexigenic neuropeptide Y (NPY) and agouti-related protein (AgRP), while upregulating anorexigenic pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) expression, thereby reducing food intake.50 Reduced appetite conversely inhibits pro-inflammatory cytokine synthesis by suppressing mitochondrial function and glycolysis in immune cells, forming a negative neuroimmune feedback loop.51 In addition, elevated systemic TNF-α impairs sympathetic nerve conduction in salivary glands and downregulates aquaporin 5 (AQP5) expression, disrupting glandular fluid transport and eventually causing hyposalivation and xerostomia.52 Notably, these circulating pro-inflammatory cytokines also concurrently induce multiple adverse somatic symptoms, including general fatigue, somatic pain and dyspnea.53,54

The operative time has an impact on how quickly patients recover after NSCLC surgery, particularly those with symptom clusters related to the respiratory system, digestive system, cancer-associated complex, and autonomic nerve-related somatic. This could be because an excessively lengthy procedure is linked to increased stress and inflammatory reactions, as well as an increased chance of complications following surgery.55 Preoperative three-dimensional virtual planning shortens the operative duration of complex thoracoscopic segmentectomy.56 For patients at high risk of mediastinal lymph node adhesion identified during preoperative evaluation, a perioperative carbon dioxide insufflation strategy can be preplanned to reduce the time required for lymphadenectomy.57

It is nevertheless challenging to return to the preoperative level of lung function, even though pulmonary rehabilitation exercise can partially restore it. Older patients recover more slowly.58,59 Additionally, this implies that we should prioritize the prerehabilitation and postoperative rehabilitation of older patients undergoing lung cancer surgery and expedite their surgical rehabilitation by full-process management.60 According to this study, balanced constitution acts as an independent protective factor exclusively for the emotional symptom cluster and alleviates postoperative emotional symptom burden, contributing to better psychological recovery of NSCLC patients after surgery.

This study focused on early postoperative NSCLC symptom clusters and their predictors, with several notable limitations. First, participants were enrolled from merely two hospitals, restricting the external generalizability of the findings. Second, although a standardized 24-hour assessment window was applied to minimize errors, inevitable recall bias still existed, since patients’ cognitive function and emotional status could interfere with subjective symptom scoring. Third, the optimal cut-off values of LDH and heparin-binding protein derived from a single dataset may cause overfitting. In addition, we performed independent logistic regression for five symptom clusters without multiple comparison correction, which might increase the risk of false-positive results. Furthermore, postoperative complications were not collected, so their correlations with symptom profiles were unanalyzed. The absence of a unified symptom cluster naming standard also limited this analysis, as our classification failed to distinguish complex pathogenic triggers of autonomic symptoms. Future multicenter, long-term longitudinal studies are required to clarify long-term symptom network dynamics and guide postoperative rehabilitation.

Conclusion

This longitudinal observational study found that the severity of symptoms such as fatigue, shortness of breath, and disturbed sleep remained at a relatively high level in NSCLC patients three months post-surgery. Moreover, we identified five symptom clusters; digestive system symptom cluster, emotional symptom cluster, cancer-related complex symptom cluster, respiratory system symptom cluster, and autonomic nerve-related somatic symptom cluster symptom cluster. This study utilized network analysis to construct a symptom network model for 205 NSCLC patients after surgery, and determined that the core symptom was fatigue, and the bridging symptom was lack of appetite. The constitution was only significantly associated with the emotional symptom cluster, while operative time exerted significant independent effects on the remaining four symptom clusters.
