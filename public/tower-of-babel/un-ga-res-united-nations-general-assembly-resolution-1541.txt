<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black;text-transform:uppercase'>Principles which should guide Members in
determining whether or not an obligation exists to transmit the information
called for in article <i>73 </i>e of the charter of the United Nations</span><span
style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle I</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>The authors of the Charter of the United Nations had in mind that
Chapter XI should be applicable to territories which were then known to be of the
colonial type. An obligation exists to transmit information under Article <i>73
</i>e of the Charter in respect of such territories whose peoples have not yet
attained a <span class=GramE>full<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>
</span>measure</span> of<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>

</span>self-government.</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle II</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>Chapter XI of the Charter embodies the concept of
Non-Self-Governing Territories in a dynamic state of evolution and progress
towards a &quot;full measure of self-government&quot;. As soon as a territory
and its peoples attain a full measure of self-government, the obligation
ceases. Until this comes about, the obligation to transmit information under
Article <i>73 </i>e continues.</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle III</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>The obligation to transmit information under Article <i>73 </i>e
of the Charter constitutes an international obligation and should be carried
out with due regard to the <span class=SpellE>fulfilment</span> of
international law.</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle IV</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>Prima facie </span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;color:black'>there
is an obligation to transmit information in respect of a territory which is
geographically separate and is distinct ethnically and/or culturally from the
country admin­istering it.</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
<span class=GramE>Principle<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>V</span></span></i><span
style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>Once it has been established that such a <i>prima facie </i>case
of geographical and ethnical or cultural distinctness of a territory exists,
other elements may then be brought into consideration. These additional
elements may be, <i>inter alia, </i>of an administrative, political, juridical,
economic or historical nature. If they affect the relationship between the
metropolitan Slate and the territory concerned in a manner which arbitrarily
places the latter in a position or status of subordination, they support the
presumption that there is an obligation to transmit information under Article <i>73
</i>e of the Charter.</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle VI</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>A Non-Self-Governing Territory can be said to have reached a full
measure of self-government by:</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>(a)<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>Emergence as a
sovereign independent State;</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>(b)<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span></span></i><span
style='font-size:13.0pt;color:black'>Free association with an independent
State; or</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>(c)<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>Integration <span
class=GramE>with<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>an</span>

independent<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>State.</span><span
style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle VII</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>(a)<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span></span></i><span
class=GramE><span style='font-size:13.0pt;color:black'>Free<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>association</span></span><span
style='font-size:13.0pt;color:black'><span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>
</span>should<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>be<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>the<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>

</span>result<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>of a<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>free<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>
</span>and voluntary<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>choice<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>by<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>
</span>the<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>peoples<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>of<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>

</span>the<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>territory<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>concerned expressed through informed and
democratic processes. It should be one which respects the individuality and the
cultural charac­teristics of the <span class=GramE>territory<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>and</span> its peoples,<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>and<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>
</span>retains<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>for the peoples of the
territory which is associated with an independent State the freedom to modify
the status of that territory through the expression of their will by democratic
means and through constitutional processes.<br>

(b)<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>The associated territory should
have the right to deter­mine its <span class=GramE>internal<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>constitution</span> without outside
interference, in accordance<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>with<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>due<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>
</span>constitutional<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'> </span>processes<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>and<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>

</span>the<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>freely expressed wishes of
the people. This does not preclude con­sultations as appropriate or necessary
under the terms of the free association agreed upon.<br style='mso-special-character:
line-break'>
<br style='mso-special-character:line-break'>
</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>Principle VIII</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>Integration with an independent State should be on the basis of
complete equality between the peoples of the erstwhile Non-Self-Governing
Territory and those of the independent country with which it is integrated. The
peoples of both territories should have equal status and rights of citizenship
and equal guarantees of fundamental rights and freedoms without any distinction
or discrimination; both should have equal rights and opportunities for
representation and effective participation at all levels in the executive,
legislative and judicial organs of government.</span><span style='font-size:
13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle IX</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>Integration should have come about in the following <span
class=GramE>circum­stances :</span></span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>(a) The integrating territory should have attained an advanced stage
of self-government with free political institutions, so that its peoples would
have the capacity to make a responsible choice through informed and democratic
processes;</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>(b) The integration should be the result of the freely ex­pressed
wishes of the territory's peoples acting with full knowl­edge of the change in
their status, their wishes having been expressed through informed and
democratic processes, im­partially conducted and based on universal adult
suffrage. The United Nations could, when it deems it necessary, supervise <span
class=GramE>these<span style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>processes</span>.</span><span
style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle X</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>The transmission of information in respect of Non-Self-Governing
Territories under Article 73 e of the Charter is subject to such limitation as
security and constitutional considerations may require. This means that the
extent of the information may be limited in certain circumstances, but the
limitation in Article 73 e cannot relieve a Member State of the obligations of
Chapter XI. The &quot;limitation&quot; can relate only to the quantum of information
of economic, social and <span class=GramE>educa­tional<span
style='mso-spacerun:yes'>  </span>nature</span> to be transmitted.</span><span
style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle XI</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>The only constitutional considerations to which Article 73 e of
the Charter refers are those arising from constitutional relations of the
territory with the Administering Member. They refer to a situation in which the
constitution of the territory gives it self-government in economic, social and
educational matters through freely elected institutions. Nevertheless, the
responsibility for transmitting information under Article 73 e continues,
unless these constitutional relations preclude the Government or parliament of
the Administering Member from receiving statistical and other information of a
technical nature relating to economic, social and educational conditions in the

<span style='mso-bidi-font-weight:bold'>territory.</span></span><span
style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><i><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'><br>
Principle XII</span></i><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>

<p ><span style='font-size:13.0pt;
color:black'>Security considerations have not been invoked in the past. Only in
very exceptional circumstances can information on economic, social and
educational conditions have any security aspect. In other circumstances,
therefore, there should be no necessity to limit the transmission of
Information on security grounds.</span><span style='font-size:13.0pt'></span></p>
