Remote Autonomous Vehicle Extended
Network (RAVEN)
Introduction
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are increasingly utilized across a broad spectrum of applications. While many operations occur
within a localized environment where the operator maintains visual contact with the aircraft, more advanced missions often require
Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) capabilities. These BVLOS operations introduce significant safety and regulatory challenges.
Improper execution can endanger not only the UAV but also other aircraft, people, and property.

To mitigate these risks, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) requires operators to obtain special waivers, employ trained visual
observers, and conduct comprehensive safety assessments. However, regulatory compliance is only part of the equation. A more
pressing technical challenge lies in maintaining reliable, long-range communication between the UAV and its ground control station
(GCS). Addressing this challenge not only enhances the safety of long-range operations but also opens the door to broader
capabilities—such as detecting and monitoring other UAVs, a critical component in developing counter-UAV systems.

This report outlines a new software platform designed to extend Oak Ridge National Laboratory’s (ORNL) capabilities in BVLOS
operations and serve as a foundational element for future counter-UAV technologies.

Background
In 2019, ORNL introduced the Multi-Modal Autonomous Vehicle Network (MAVNet) [1], a software suite aimed at solving the BVLOS
communication problem. MAVNet provided redundant, multi-path communication channels by leveraging local networks, cellular
infrastructure, and satellite links. Through a web-based interface, operators could control UAVs remotely, provided at least one
communication path remained active.
At its core, MAVNet featured a centralized server that facilitated communication between users and UAVs while also storing
historical data. This architecture enabled seamless message routing and operational oversight, making BVLOS operations more
accessible.
Despite its innovative design, MAVNet faced several architectural and usability challenges. Users reported reliability issues, and
developers encountered high maintenance overhead. Key features such as scalability, real-time notifications, and system
interoperability were limited by the platform’s technical constraints. As a result, MAVNet saw a decline in adoption leaving behind a
conceptually strong but practically limited solution.

RAVEN Software System

Control

At its simplest, RAVEN presents a browser-based map interface displaying UAVs within the user’s current view. Like MAVNet, it
leverages open-source mapping tools such as Cesium, with a strong emphasis on 3D visualization to support altitude awareness
during flight operations.

RAVEN is designed with swarming capabilities as a core feature. UAVs can be grouped and controlled as a unit, either pre-mission or
dynamically during operations. Pre-mission grouping allows users to define UAV groups via the command interface and assign

coordinated missions. Dynamic grouping enables users to select UAVs in real time—such as by drawing a polygon around them on
the map—and immediately issue group commands or upload synchronized mission plans.

Detection

Unlike MAVNet, which focused solely on control, RAVEN is built with UAV detection as a foundational capability. Effective counter-
UAV strategies begin with reliable detection and timely notification. RAVEN’s server architecture supports integration with a wide
range of surveillance technologies—from custom sensors using protocols like MQTT to large-scale aviation data sources such as the
OpenSky Network or ADS-B feeds.

To support real-time situational awareness, RAVEN includes a flexible notification system. Users can configure alerts based on GPS
triggers, sensor inputs, or other criteria, and route them to appropriate personnel or systems. This makes RAVEN an ideal platform
for integration into broader counter-UAV ecosystems.

Technical Impact
RAVEN introduces several architectural and operational innovations that distinguish it from existing UAV control platforms and
position it as a forward-looking solution for both research and deployment.

One of the most significant advancements is RAVEN’s unified control-detection architecture. Unlike traditional systems that
separate UAV command interfaces from surveillance and detection platforms, RAVEN integrates both into a single, coherent
framework [2]. This fusion enables seamless transitions between offensive and defensive operations and opens the door to
advanced features such as bidirectional awareness. In this model, operator-controlled UAVs can also serve as mobile sensors,
detecting other aerial vehicles, or objects of interest on the ground, in real time. The resulting situational awareness is far more
comprehensive, as both control and detection data are available for fusion and analysis.

Another key innovation is RAVEN’s support for dynamic swarm reconfiguration. While existing swarm platforms require predefined
swarm topologies [3], RAVEN allows operators to form and modify UAV groups during flight using an intuitive polygon-selection
interface. This capability enables ad-hoc coalition formation, giving operators the flexibility to adapt missions on the fly. The result is
a new paradigm of “supervised autonomy,” where human operators can intervene and optimize swarm behavior in real time—an
especially valuable feature for safety-critical or rapidly evolving scenarios [4].

RAVEN also excels in protocol-agnostic sensor integration. Its modular architecture supports a wide range of heterogeneous
detection systems—including RF, acoustic, optical, and cooperative ADS-B sensors—through standardized messaging protocols. This
multimodal fusion approach addresses the limitations of single-sensor systems, which are often vulnerable to spoofing or
environmental interference [5, 6]. By combining multiple sensor modalities, RAVEN enhances detection accuracy and resilience.
Finally, RAVEN’s web-native 3D situational awareness sets it apart from legacy ground control systems. While tools like Mission
Planner and QGroundControl offer 3D visualization, they typically require local installation and configuration. RAVEN’s browser-
based implementation, built on Cesium, eliminates these barriers while maintaining full 3D terrain rendering. Though browser-based
an internet connection is not required, local networking can utilize the frontend as well. This enables rapid deployment in tactical
environments and supports collaborative, multi-operator missions—capabilities that are increasingly vital in emergency response
and defense applications [7].

Beyond integrating detection and aviation data, RAVEN supports the incorporation of virtually any type of geospatial information,
from dynamic weather feeds to pre-labeled semantic maps and situational awareness frameworks, supporting rich contextual
analysis of the vehicles’ operating environment. While these data sources are pertinent to human mission planning, future
development will add standardized APIs to each for the purpose of enabling function calling from Large Language Models (LLMs).
This will open the door for research and development of AI controlled swarms where the system can receive high-level mission

Applications
RAVEN’s integrated control and detection capabilities position it uniquely across a range of high-impact domains, offering both
operational flexibility and strategic value.

One of the most critical areas of application is infrastructure protection. Facilities such as nuclear plants, power stations, refineries,
and data centers face growing threats from unauthorized UAV incursions. RAVEN’s ability to integrate with perimeter surveillance
systems—such as RF and radar sensors—enables real-time geofencing alerts and, where authorized, coordination with counter-UAS
response systems [8].

In the realm of wildfire management and emergency response, RAVEN supports persistent BVLOS monitoring across vast, rugged
terrain. Its multi-UAV coordination capabilities allow for efficient perimeter surveillance, while its detection systems can identify
unauthorized drones operating in restricted airspace. The platform’s 3D visualization tools enhance mission planning by rendering
smoke columns and terrain features in real time [9, 10].

Border and maritime surveillance also benefit from RAVEN’s long-range capabilities. These environments often span hundreds of
miles, far exceeding visual line-of-sight. RAVEN enables swarm-based persistent surveillance with coordinated patrol patterns,
integration with ground-based radar and ADS-B networks, and seamless mission continuity through UAV handoffs. This addresses
current limitations in fragmented control systems used by agencies such as U.S. Customs and Border Protection [11].

For large-scale event security, such as political gatherings, concerts, and sporting events, RAVEN offers rapid deployment of
temporary sensor networks and real-time situational awareness across multiple security teams. Its logging capabilities also support
post-event forensic analysis. As the FAA continues to expand UAS Detection and Mitigation System (UDMS) authorizations for event
security, RAVEN provides a compliant and scalable solution [12].

In agriculture and industrial inspection, RAVEN enables efficient BVLOS surveys of large farms, solar arrays, and infrastructure. Its
support for multi-vehicle missions reduces operational time, while integration with IoT sensor networks—such as soil moisture or
structural health monitors—enhances data collection. The intuitive web interface minimizes training requirements [13, 14].

Finally, military and defense applications represent a strategic frontier for RAVEN. With appropriate security hardening, the
platform can support persistent ISR (Intelligence, Surveillance, Reconnaissance), sensor fusion for counter-UAS operations, and
swarm tactics for overwhelming adversary defenses. These capabilities align with the Department of Defense’s emphasis on layered
detection and command-and-control interoperability in its Counter-sUAS Strategy [15, 16].

Conclusion
RAVEN represents a significant evolution in UAV operations software. By addressing the limitations of MAVNet and incorporating
modern design principles, RAVEN provides a scalable, reliable, and extensible platform for both BVLOS operations and UAV
detection. Its capabilities position it as a critical tool for advancing autonomous vehicle control and enhancing national security
through counter-UAV readiness.
