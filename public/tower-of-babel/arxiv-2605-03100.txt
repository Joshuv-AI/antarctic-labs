Berry-Esseen bounds for multivariate martingale difference sequences in the Kolmogorov distance
Weichen Wu Dung Le Arun Kumar Kuchibhotla Alessandro Rinaldo

Abstract
We derive new Gaussian approximation for finite martingale difference sequences in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with respect to the Kolmogorov distance. Under appropriate conditions, our bounds exhibit a dependence of order n − 1 / 4 n^{-1/4} on the length of the sequence and of order polylog ⁡ ( d ) \mathrm{polylog}(d) on the dimension. As an application, we derive a high-dimensional Berry-Esseen bound over hyper-rectangles for martingale sequences generated from Markov chains.

Assumption 1.1
.

It holds that, almost surely,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{k}\bm{X}_{k}^{\top}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{k}\bm{X}_{k}^{\top}\mid\mathscr{F}_{0}]=n\bm{\Sigma}_{n},

As explained below, though seemingly strong, this assumption is widely used in the literature on Gaussian approximation for martingale sequences.

We seek to establish a bound on Kolmogorov distance between the normalized sum of the martingale difference terms (thus a centered martingale) and centered Gaussian vector with covariance
\bm{\Sigma}_{n}

d_{\mathsf{K}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{k},\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bm{T}_{n}\right):=\sup_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{H}_{d}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{k}\in\mathcal{A}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bm{T}_{n}\in\mathcal{A}\right)\right|,

(2)

\mathbb{R}^{d}

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bm{T}_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{n})

Problem setting and Results

Let
\{\bm{X}_{k}\}_{k=0,1,\ldots,}
, where
\bm{X}_{0}=0
, be a martingale difference process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to the
\sigma-
field filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{k}\}_{0\leq k\leq n}
, with
\mathcal{F}_{0}
the trivial
\sigma
-field. Set, for each
k=1,\ldots,n
,
\bm{S}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bm{X}_{i}
, so that,
\{\bm{S}_{k}\}_{k=1,\ldots,n}
is a
d
-dimensional centered martingale. We are interested in deriving a high-dimensional Gaussian approximation to the normalized sum
\bm{S}_{n}/\sqrt{n}
, under the standard Assumption
1.1
. We will do so by bounding the
d
-dimensional the Kolmogorov between
\bm{S}_{n}/\sqrt{n}
and the normalized sum of centered Gaussian vectors with matching conditional covariances. In detail, let

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{k}\bm{X}_{k}^{\top}\mid\mathscr{F}_{0}],\quad\quad\bm{V}_{k}=\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{k}\bm{X}_{k}^{\top}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}],\quad k=1,\ldots,n,

(3)

and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bm{T}_{n}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{V}_{k}^{1/2}\bm{Z}_{k}
, where
\{\bm{Z}_{k}\}_{k=1,\ldots,n}
is a sequence of i.i.d
d
-dimensional standard Gaussian random vectors.
We will derive novel bounds for the Kolmogorov distance (
2
) in the introduction, which we can re-write more explicitly as

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{K}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bm{S}_{n},\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bm{T}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{H}_{d}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{k}\in\mathcal{A}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{V}_{k}^{1/2}\bm{Z}_{k}\in\mathcal{A}\right)\right|

\displaystyle:=d_{\mathsf{K}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{k},\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{n})\right),

where in the last expression we use the fact that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\bm{T}_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{n})
, which follows from the deterministic terminal quadratic variation assumption (
1
). The first identity above elucidates that scope of the Gaussian approximation, whereby the original martingale difference sequence
\{\bm{X}_{k}\}_{k=0,\ldots,n}
with a new martingale difference sequence
\{\bm{V}_{k}^{1/2}\bm{Z}_{k}\}_{k=0,\ldots,n}
with matching conditional second moments and conditionally Gaussian; i.e., for every
k
,

\mathrm{Var}[\bm{X}_{k}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}]=\mathrm{Var}[\bm{V}_{k}^{1/2}\bm{Z}_{k}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}]\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{V}_{k}^{1/2}\bm{Z}_{k}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{V}_{k}).

In our main results below, we present three bounds for
d_{\mathsf{K}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{k},\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{n})\right)
under Assumption
1.1
and various third moment conditions in the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
norm.

In our proofs, we use various techniques and results from the literature on Gaussian approximations: Lindeberg’s swapping, smoothing via the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process or Gaussian noise, and the explicit form of the solution to Stein’s multivariate equation in terms of the generator of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semi-group. The effectiveness of these tools in deriving high-dimensional Berry-Esseen bounds in the Kolmogorov distance was demonstrated in
11
and
19
, whose proofs we follow. For more details, we refer the reader to, e.g.,
2
,
13
,
4
,
23
, and
27
.

2.1
High-dimensional Bery-Esseen bounds in Kolmogorov distance for martingales with fixed terminal quadratic variation

In our first general, bound, in addition to the terminal quadratic variation condition in Assumption
1.1
, we impose the mildest possible moment third moment condition, resulting in a slow rate in
n
of order
O(n^{-1/8})
. This is consistent with analogous finding in the univariate literature: see
29
.

Assumption 2.1
.

For all
k\in[n]
,
\mathbb{E}\|\bm{X}_{k}\|_{\infty}^{3}<\infty
.

The following theorem shows how the martingale converges to its asymptotic Gaussian distribution under these minimum assumptions.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\{\bm{X}_{k}\}_{k\in[n]}
denote a martingale difference process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies Assumptions
1.1
and
2.1
. Then

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{K}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{k},\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{n})\right)\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\bm{\Sigma}_{n})}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|\bm{X}_{k}\|_{\infty}^{3}}{n}\right)^{1/4}\left(\frac{d_{\max}(\bm{\Sigma}_{n})}{d_{\min}(\bm{\Sigma}_{n})}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}(\log_{+}d)^{9/8}n^{-\frac{1}{8}}.

If we further impose a
ratio bound condition
on the third moment, we can improve the dependence on
n
to
O(n^{-\frac{1}{4}}\log n)
, which, as shown below, is nearly optimal under the assumed conditions. The proof is given in Appendix
B.1
. This type of conditions have been considered in the literature before in, e.g.,
30
and
1
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\{\bm{X}_{k}\}_{k\in[n]}
denote a martingale difference process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies Assumption
1.1
, and let
\bm{V}_{k},\bm{\Sigma}_{n}
be defined as in (
3
). Suppose that there exists a positive constant
M
such that for all
k\in[n]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{X}_{k}\|_{\infty}^{3}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}]\leq M\lambda_{\min}(\bm{V}_{k})\quad\text{almost surely.}

(4)

Then

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{K}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{k},\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{n})\right)\lesssim\Delta\log_{+}d\log\frac{1+\Delta^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}

(5)

where

\displaystyle\Delta=\sqrt{M}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\bm{\Sigma}_{n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{d_{\max}(\bm{\Sigma}_{n})}{d_{\min}(\bm{\Sigma}_{n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\log_{+}^{\frac{3}{4}}d\cdot n^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

(6)

Remark 2.4
.

In the one-dimensional setting, the moment ratio condition (
4
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{k}|^{3}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]\leq M\mathbb{E}[X_{k}^{2}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}]\quad\text{almost surely, for all }k\in[n].

Notice that this is equivalent to the assumption outlined in Theorem 1 of
22
and weaker than the one made by Corollary 2.3 in
30
, which also assumes a universal bound on the conditional third momentum of
|X_{k}|
. For Berry–Esseen bounds measured by the Wasserstein distance and the convex distance,
34
make the analogous assumption that, for every unit vector
\bm{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every
k\in[n]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(\bm{e}^{\top}\bm{X}_{k})^{2}\|\bm{X}_{k}\|_{2}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}]\leq M\mathbb{E}[(\bm{e}^{\top}\bm{X}_{k})^{2}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}]\quad\text{almost surely}.

(7)

Notably, the condition (
7
) can be implied by the assumption that
\|\bm{X}_{k}\|_{2}\leq M
almost surely; but our condition (
4
)
cannot
be implied by the assumption that
\|\bm{X}_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq M
.

If instead of the moment ratio bound more direct third
moment value bounds
on the terms of the martingale difference sequence are imposed, then the following theorem can further tighten the bound to
O(n^{-\frac{1}{4}})
. See Appendix
B.2
for a proof.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\{\bm{X}_{k}\}_{k\in[n]}
denote a martingale difference sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies Assumption
1.1
, and let
\bm{V}_{k},\bm{\Sigma}_{n}
be defined as in (
3
). Suppose that there exist constants
\beta>0,\gamma>0
, such that for all
k\in[n]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\bm{X}_{k}\|_{\infty}^{3}\leq(\gamma\log_{+}d)^{3/2},\quad\text{and}\quad d_{\max}(\bm{V}_{k})\leq\beta\quad\text{almost surely}.

(8)

It can then be guaranteed that

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{K}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{k},\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{n})\right)\lesssim\left(\frac{\gamma+\beta}{d_{\min}(\bm{\Sigma}_{n})}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}\left(\log_{+}^{\frac{3}{2}}d\right)n^{-\frac{1}{8}}.

(9)

If we further assume that there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\displaystyle\min_{k=1,\ldots,n}\lambda_{\min}(\bm{V}_{k})\geq\alpha,\quad\text{almost surely},

(10)

then

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{K}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{k},\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{n})\right)\lesssim\frac{(\gamma+\beta)^{3/4}}{\sqrt{\alpha d_{\min}^{1/2}(\bm{\Sigma}_{n})}}\left(\log_{+}^{2}d\right)n^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

(11)

•

The upper bound (

A second minor difference is that

Notice that Theorem

In this section we prove the following result, used in the proof of

For simplicity, we denote

\displaystyle a=(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}+e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k},\quad b=\varepsilon_{k},

and approach this integral through a of changes of variable

\displaystyle e^{-s}=\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}\sin\theta.

It can easily be verified that

\displaystyle e^{-s}\mathrm{d}s=-\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta,\quad\text{and}\quad a-be^{-2s}=a\cos^{2}\theta.

Therefore, the integral can be represented as

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-3s}}{(a-be^{-2s})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2s}}{(a-be^{-2s})^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\theta^{\star}}\frac{\frac{a}{b}\sin^{2}\theta}{(a\cos^{2}\theta)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=b^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{\theta^{\star}}\tan^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=b^{-\frac{3}{2}}\cdot(\tan\theta-\theta)\bigg|_{0}^{\theta^{\star}}

\displaystyle=b^{-\frac{3}{2}}\cdot(\tan\theta^{\star}-\theta^{\star}),

where we let

\displaystyle e^{-t}=\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}\sin\theta^{\star},\quad\text{so}\quad\theta^{\star}=\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{b}{a}}e^{-t}\right).

Meanwhile, the difference between
\tan\theta^{\star}
and
\theta^{\star}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\tan\theta^{\star}-\theta^{\star}

\displaystyle\leq\tan\theta^{\star}-\sin\theta^{\star}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\frac{b}{a}}e^{-t}}{\sqrt{1-\left(\sqrt{\frac{b}{a}}e^{-t}\right)^{2}}}-\sqrt{\frac{b}{a}}e^{-t}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}}}-\sqrt{\frac{e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}+e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}}}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}}{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}+e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}}\right)

\displaystyle<\sqrt{\frac{e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}}}\left(1-\frac{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}}{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}+e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}}}\cdot\frac{e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}+e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}.

In combination, the integral is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-3s}}{((1-e^{2t})\varepsilon_{1}+(e^{-2t}-e^{-2s})\varepsilon_{k})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-3t}}{\sqrt{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-e^{-2t})\varepsilon_{1}+e^{-2t}\varepsilon_{k}}.
