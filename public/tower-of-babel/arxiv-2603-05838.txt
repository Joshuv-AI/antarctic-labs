Alexander-Taylor’s inequality for capacities in complex Sobolev spaces
Ngoc Cuong Nguyen, Do Duc Thai

Abstract
. We prove a sharp inequality between the Alexander-Taylor capacity and the functional capacity in a complex Sobolev space on a compact Kähler manifold. The latter space and capacity were introduced by Dinh, Sibony and Vigny.



1.
Introduction

In 1980s Alexander and Taylor
[
AT84
]
proved a sharp quantitative comparison between the relative capacity of Bedford and Taylor
[
BT82
]
and the projective capacity of Alexander
[
A79
]
. It is now called the Alexander-Taylor inequality in the literature, which has become very useful. For example, it gave in
[
AT84
]
a simplified proof of an effective version of Josefson’s theorem, due to El Mir
[
E79
]
, about the equivalence between locally pluripolar sets and globally pluripolar sets in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Subsequently, the inequality provided a crucial link in proving almost sharp uniform estimates for complex Monge-Ampère equations on both domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and compact Kähler manifolds via the capacity method
[
K98
,
K05
]
. Its generalization to the equations with semi-positive background forms on compact Kähler manifolds played equally important roles in
[
GZ05
,
GZ17
]
and
[
EGZ09
]
.

Let
(X,\alpha)
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. Let
\omega\geq 0
be a semi-positive closed
(1,1)
-form such that
\int_{X}\omega^{n}>0
. For simplicity it is normalized by
\int_{X}\omega^{n}=1
. Denote by
PSH(X,\omega)
the family of
\omega
-plurisubharmonic (
\omega
-psh) functions on
X
.
The global Alexander-Taylor capacity
T_{\omega}(\cdot)
introduced and studied by Guedj and Zeriahi
[
GZ05
]
(see also
[
DS06b
]
and
[
GZ17
]
) is as follows. For a compact subset
K\subset X
,

T_{\omega}(K)=\exp\left(-\sup_{X}V_{K}^{*}\right),

where

V_{K}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in PSH(X,\omega),v\leq 0\text{ on }K\}

and
V_{K}^{*}
is its upper semi-continuous regularization. The latter function is often called the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function associated to
K
.

We are interested in the subspace
W^{*}(X)\subset W^{1,2}(X,\mathbb{R})
introduced by Dinh and Sibony
[
DS06a
]
(see Section 2), which is called the complex Sobolev space. For
f\in W^{*}(X)
, let
\Gamma_{f}
denote the non-empty set of all positive closed
(1,1)
-currents
T
satisfying

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T\quad\text{weakly in }X.

Then, the
W^{*}
-norm of
f
is given by

\|f\|_{*}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}+\inf\{\|T\|:T\in\Gamma_{f}\},

where

\|T\|=\int_{X}T\wedge\alpha^{n-1}.

Soon afterward, Vigny
[
Vig07
]
showed that
W^{*}(X)
is a Banach space with the
W^{*}
-norm. One special feature of this new space is that it takes complex structures of underlying manifolds into account. Therefore, it is a suitable framework to work with complex objects such as holomorphic/meromorphic maps and quasi plurisubharmonic functions. Since then, this space has found numerous applications in complex dynamics in higher dimensions and some related areas
[
BiD23
]
,
[
DKW21
]
,
[
DoNV25
]
,
[
Vig17
]
,
[
Vu20
]
and
[
WZ24
]
.

Since
W^{*}(X)
is a Banach space, it has a natural functional capacity
{\tt c}(\cdot)
defined by its norm, which was introduced in
[
Vig07
]
. For a Borel subset
E\subset X
,

{\tt c}(E)=\inf\left\{\|v\|_{*}^{2}\;\rvert\;v\in\mathscr{K}(E)\right\},

where

\mathscr{K}(E)=\left\{v\in W^{*}(X)\;\rvert\;\{v\leq-1\}^{o}\supset E\text{ and }v\leq 0\right\}.

The inclusion
\{v\leq-1\}^{o}\supset E
means
v\leq-1
a.e. in a neighborhood of
E
. In fact, this is a capacity in Choquet’s sense
[
Vig07
, Theorem 30]
. It should be pointed out that the functional capacity of with respect to the Sobolev norm is not strong enough to study properties of functions in
W^{*}(X)
. The reason is that there are bounded and unbounded
\omega
-psh functions belong to
W^{*}(X)
while the Sobolev capacity cannot be used to characterize neither quasicontinuity of such functions nor pluripolar sets.

The main result of the paper is the following comparison between the global Alexander-Taylor capacity and the functional capacity of
W^{*}(X)
above.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a constant
A>0
such that for every compact subset
K\subset X
,

\exp\left(-A/{\tt c}(K)\right)\leq T_{\omega}(K)\leq\exp\left(-1/(4n)^{\frac{1}{n}}[{\tt c}(K)]^{\frac{1}{n}}\right).

Since both
{\tt c}(\cdot)
and
T_{\omega}(\cdot)
are Choquet capacities, the inequalities hold for all Borel sets. Especially,
they keep the spirit of the ones in
[
GZ17
, Lemmas 12.2, 12.3]
where
T_{\omega}(K)
is compared with the global Bedford-Taylor capacity
cap_{\omega}(K)
. Namely, in the first inequality the constant
A
is equivalent to the one of
[
GZ17
, Lemma 12.3]
, up to a numerical constant. In the second one, it is significant that the constant on the right hand side is purely a dimensional constant which is similar to ones in the local setting
[
AT84
]
and global setting
[
GZ17
, Lemma 12.1]
. Regarding to the exponents in the inequalities, Remark
3.5
shows that they are sharp.

We expect that this inequality will be a bridge so that the tools from complex Sobolev spaces can be used to study open problems in pluripotential theory and complex Monge-Ampère equations. Some evidences have been appeared very recently in
[
DoN25
]
,
[
DKN22
]
,
[
VV24
]
and
[
Vu24
,
Vu26
]
. We give here one more application to the equation with a background form
\omega
which is
semi-positive
and
big
. This result was implicitly contained in
[
DKN22
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
X
. Assume
\mu
is
(W^{*}(X),\log^{p})
-continuous with
p>n+1
, i.e., there is constants
c>0
such that

\left|\int_{X}fd\mu\right|\leq c\left[1+|\log\|f\|_{L^{1}(X)}|\right]^{-p}

for every
f\in W^{*}(X)\cap C^{0}(X)
and
\|f\|_{*}\leq 1
. Then, there exists a bounded
\omega
-psh solution
u
to
(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=\mu.

Organization.
In Section
2
we recall basic facts of pluripotential theory and complex Sobolev spaces. In particular, we give another proof of existence of quasi-continuous representative for bounded functions in
W^{*}(X)
. Section
3
devotes to prove the main theorem and corollary.

2.
A functional capacity in complex Sobolev spaces

In this section we recall some basic facts about the complex Sobolev space and pluripotential theory. Let
(X,\alpha)
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. Let
\omega\geq 0
be a semi-positive closed
(1,1)
-form whose volume is normalized by

(2.1)

\int_{X}\omega^{n}=1.

Clearly, we may assume that

(2.2)

\omega\leq\alpha.

Let
W^{1,2}(X,\mathbb{R})
denote the Sobolev space of functions in
L^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
whose first order (weak) derivatives are
L^{2}
-integrable. For
f\in W^{1,2}(X,\mathbb{R}),
let
\Gamma_{f}
denote the set of all positive closed
(1,1)
-currents
T
satisfying

(2.3)

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T\quad\text{weakly in }X.

Here we use the normalization

d^{c}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}(\overline{\partial}-\partial),\quad dd^{c}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}.

Definition 2.1

(
[
DS06a
]
)
.

A function
f\in W^{1,2}(X,\mathbb{R})
belongs to the complex Sobolev space
W^{*}(X)
if the set
\Gamma_{f}
associated to
f
is non-empty.

The total mass of a closed positive (1,1)-current
T
on
X
is given by

\|T\|=\int_{X}T\wedge\alpha^{n-1}.

By the compactness of
X
and closedness of
T
,
\|T\|
is always finite. Basic functional properties of
W^{*}(X)
have been studied by Vigny
[
Vig07
]
. In particular,
he showed that this is a Banach space with the
W^{*}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{*}
defined by

(2.4)

\|f\|_{*}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}+\min\{\|T\|:T\in\Gamma_{f}\},

where the minimum is attained thanks to compactness of closed positive
(1,1)
-currents. Though there may be more than one minimizer.

The basic examples of functions in the space are Lipschitz functions. But there are many more.
It is easy to check that this space contains all bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic (quasi-psh) functions. Hence, for
u,v\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
,

u\pm v\in W^{*}(X).

We refer the readers to
[
Vig07
]
for more examples of functions in
W^{*}(X)
, which contains unbounded quasi-psh functions.

Although the continuous functions are not dense in
W^{*}(X)
with respect to the strong topology of
W^{*}
-norm
[
Vig07
, Proposition 7]
, however,
the following compactness
[
Vig07
, Proposition 4]
of
W^{*}
-norm will be enough for many applications.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset W^{*}(X)
be such that
\|f_{j}\|_{*}\leq A
for every
j\geq 1
. There exists a subsequence
\{f_{j_{k}}\}_{j_{k}\geq 1}
converging weakly in
W^{1,2}(X,\mathbb{R})
to
f\in W^{*}(X)
and
\|f\|_{*}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|f_{j}\|_{*}
.

Next, we recall a useful fact whose proof is derived easily from
[
KLV21
, Theorem 2.35]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
E\subset\Omega
be a Borel set. Let
\{f_{j}\}\subset L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
and
\{g_{j}\}\subset L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
be such that
f_{j}\to f\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
weakly and
g_{j}\to g\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
weakly.
If
|f_{j}(x)|\leq g_{j}(x)
for a.e.
x\in E
, then
|f(x)|\leq g(x)
a.e.
x\in E
.

For a Borel subset
E\subset X
,

\mathscr{K}(E)=\{v\in W^{*}(X):\{v\leq-1\}^{o}\supset E,v\leq 0\text{ on }X\}.

Clearly, if
E=G
is an open subset, then

\mathscr{K}(G)=\{v\in W^{*}(X):v\leq-1\quad\text{a.e. on }G,\;v\leq 0\text{ on }X\}.

Following Vigny
[
Vig07
]
, we define the (global) functional capacity

(2.5)

{\tt c}(E)=\inf\{\|v\|_{*}^{2}:\;v\in\mathscr{K}(E)\}.

Observe that if
v\in\mathscr{K}(E)
, then
\max\{v,-1\}\in\mathscr{K}(E)
. An elementary computation shows that
\|\max\{v,-1\}\|_{*}\leq\|v\|_{*}
(see e.g.,
[
Vig07
]
or
[
DMV23
]
). Hence,
in the definition of
{\tt c}(\cdot)
it is enough to take
v
in a smaller family
\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(E)
defined by

\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(E)=\left\{v\in\mathscr{K}(E):-1\leq v\leq 0\right\}.

In other words,

(2.6)

{\tt c}(E)=\inf\{\|v\|_{*}^{2}:\;v\in\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(E)\},

The following basic properties of the capacity were obtained in
[
Vig07
, Proposition 27]
, whose proofs are followed from classical arguments (see e.g.
[
AH96
]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

The set function on Borel sets
E\mapsto{\tt c}(E)
satisfies:

(a)

for Borel sets
E\subset F\subset X
,
{\tt c}(E)\leq{\tt c}(F)
.

(b)

For a sequence of Borel sets
\{E_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
in
X
,

{\tt c}(\cup_{i\geq 1}E_{i})\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}{\tt c}(E_{i}).

(c)

For
E\subset X
,
{\tt c}(E)\leq\int_{X}\omega^{n}=1.

(d)

For decreasing sequence of compact sets
K_{1}\supset K_{2}\supset\cdots
and
K=\cap_{i\geq 1}K_{i}
, then

{\tt c}(K)=\lim_{i\to\infty}{\tt c}(K_{i}).

In order for
{\tt c}(\cdot)
to be a Choquet capacity (Theorem
3.3
), then we need the limit properties under decreasing of compact sets and under increasing of Borel sets. The former one is already given in (d). However, the latter limit property is the most difficult one to prove for
{\tt c}(\cdot)
(see
[
Vig07
, Theorem 30]
).

The outer regularity is rather easy so we include its proof.

Lemma 2.5

(outer regularity)
.

For a Borel subset
E\subset\Omega
,

{\tt c}(E)=\inf\{{\tt c}(G):E\subset G,\;G\text{ is open}\}.

Proof.

By the monotonicity in Proposition
2.4
-(a),

{\tt c}(E)\leq\inf\{{\tt c}(G):E\subset G,\;G\text{ is open}\}.

To prove the reverse inequality we may assume
\mathscr{K}(E)
is non empty. Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exist
v\in\mathscr{K}(E)
with

\|v\|_{*}^{2}\leq{\tt c}(E)+\varepsilon

and an open set
G_{\varepsilon}
such that
v(x)\leq-1
a.e.
x\in G_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore,

\inf\{{\tt c}(G):E\subset G,\;G\text{ is open}\}\leq{\tt c}(G_{\varepsilon})\leq\|v\|_{*}^{2}\leq{\tt c}(E)+\varepsilon.

The lemma follows by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

2.1.
Local and global definitions of capacities

The global Bedford-Taylor capacity was introduced in
[
K03
]
and studied in detail in
[
GZ05
,
GZ17
]
. For a Borel subset
E\subset X
, one defines

(2.7)

cap_{\omega}(E)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}:v\in PSH(X,\omega),-1\leq v\leq 0\right\}.

Then, we have a formula
[
GZ17
, Theorem 9.15]
realizing the capacity via extremal functions. Precisely, for a compact subset
K\subset X
,

(2.8)

cap_{\omega}(K)=\int_{K}(\omega+dd^{c}h_{K}^{*})^{n}=\int_{X}-h_{K}^{*}(\omega+dd^{c}h_{K}^{*}),

where
h_{K}^{*}
denotes the upper semi-contitinuous regularization of

(2.9)

h_{K}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in PSH(X,\omega),v_{|_{K}}\leq-1,v\leq 0\}.

As in local case
[
BT82
]
, this global capacity is a Choquet capacity and it characterizes locally/globally pluripolar sets
[
GZ17
, Theorem 9.17]
. Namely, a Borel set
E\subset X
is pluripolar if and only if

(2.10)

cap_{\omega}(E)=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad cap_{\alpha}(E)=0,

where
cap_{\alpha}(E)
is considered with respect to
PSH(X,\alpha)
and
\alpha
in (
2.7
).

We consider yet another capacity defined via local covering and the local relative capacity of Bedford and Taylor
[
BT82
]
as follows. Let
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open subset. Then, for a Borel set
E\subset\Omega
,

(2.11)

cap(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}v)^{n}:v\in PSH(\Omega),-1\leq v\leq 0\right\}.

Consider two finite coverings
\{D_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
and
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
such that
D_{\ell}\subset\subset\Omega_{\ell}
for each
1\leq\ell\leq N
and each holomorphic chart
\Omega_{\ell}
is a smooth strictly pseudoconvex domain. The quantity
cap_{\rm BT}(E)
is given by

cap_{\rm BT}(E)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}cap(E\cap D_{\ell},\Omega_{\ell}).

By the observation of Kołodziej
[
K03
, Page 670]
(see also
[
GZ17
, Proposition 9.8]
), these two capacities
cap_{\alpha}(\cdot)
and
cap_{\rm BT}(\cdot)
are equivalent to each other. More precisely, there exists a constant
A_{0}
depending only on the covering and
\alpha
such that

(2.12)

\frac{1}{A_{0}}cap_{\rm BT}(E)\leq cap_{\alpha}(E)\leq A_{0}cap_{\rm BT}(E).

When the background metric is Kähler,
we have a comparison between their global counterparts from
[
Vig07
, Theorem 28]
and
[
DKN22
, Proposition 5.1]
(
c.f.
Remark
3.4
)

Lemma 2.6
.

There exists a constant
A>0
such that for every Borel set
E\subset X
,

\frac{1}{A}cap_{\alpha}(E)\leq{\tt c}(E)\leq A[cap_{\alpha}(E)]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Consequently, if
{\tt c}(E)=0
, then
E
is pluripolar.

2.2.
Quasi-continuous representative

Since the functions in
W^{*}(X)
are defined up to a set of Lebesgue measure zero,
we need to study further their fine properties for applications. To make it precise we define the following terminologies.

Definition 2.7
.

(a)

A Borel function
f
is called quasi-continuous (q.c) in
X
if there exists for every
\varepsilon>0
an open set
G\subset X
such that
{\tt c}(G)<\varepsilon
and the restriction
f_{|_{X\setminus G}}
is continuous (with respect to the induced topology on
X\setminus G
).

(b)

A statement is said to hold
quasi-everywhere
(q.e.) if there exists a Borel set
P
of capacity zero, i.e.,
{\tt c}(P)=0
, such that the statement is true for every
x\notin P
.

Remark 2.8
.

By Lemma
2.6
the quasi-continuity and quasi-everywhere properties with respect to
{\tt c}(\cdot)
are equivalent to the classical ones in pluripotential theory
[
BT82
,
BT87
]
and
[
GZ17
]
.

The following notion helps us to get a good representative in each class of functions.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
f,g
be Borel measurable functions on
X
. Then,
g
is said to be a quasi-continuous modification of
f
if
g
is quasi-continuous and
g=f
a.e. We denote
g
by
\widetilde{f}
in this case.

We also need a classical result in potential theory
[
FOT94
, Lemma 2.14]
(see also
[
Vig07
, Propositon 21]
).

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
D\subset X
be an open set and
f
be a quasi-continuous function on
D
. If
f\geq 0
a.e. on
D
, then
f\geq 0
quasi-everywhere on
D
.

Thanks to this result, two quasi-continuous modifications are equal q.e. Hence, they are equal outside a pluripolar set by Lemma
2.6
. The existence of a quasi-continuous modification with respect to the Bedford-Taylor capacity was obtained by Vigny
[
Vig07
, Theorem 22]
. We state below the case of bounded functions which is enough for our applications. By using the local result of Dinh, Marinescu and Vu
[
DMV23
]
we also provide a different proof for the reader’s convenience.

If
f\in W^{*}(X)
, then for every holomorphic coordinate chart
\Omega\subset\subset X
,
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
the local complex Sobolev space (see
[
DMV23
]
and
[
Vig07
]
). The definition of
W^{*}(\Omega)
is very similar. Namely, let
\beta=dd^{c}|z|^{2}
be the standard Kähler metric in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}),
let denote
\Gamma_{f}(\Omega)
be the set of all closed positive
(1,1)
-currents
T
in
\Omega
satisfying

(2.13)

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T\quad\text{weakly in }\Omega.

Definition 2.11
.

A function
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
belongs
W^{*}(\Omega)
if there exists
T\in\Gamma_{f}(\Omega)
such that
\|T\|_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}T\wedge\beta<+\infty
.

The local
W^{*}(\Omega)
-norm is given by

(2.14)

\|f\|_{*,\Omega}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\min\{\|T\|_{\Omega}:T\in\Gamma_{f}(\Omega)\text{ and }\|T\|_{\Omega}<+\infty\},

It was shown in
[
Vig07
, Proposition 1]
that
(W^{*}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{*,\Omega})
is also a Banach space.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
f\in W^{*}(X)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. Then, there exists a quasi-continuous modification
\widetilde{f}\in W^{*}(X)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. Moreover, if
g
is another quasi-continuous modification of
f
, then
g=\widetilde{f}
outside a pluripolar set.

Proof.

By Lemmma
2.6
it is enough to prove the quasi-continuity with respect to
cap_{\alpha}(\cdot)
.
Let
\chi
be a cut-off function on
X
. Since
f
is bounded, it follows that
\chi f
belongs to
W^{*}(X)
. By partition of unity and subadditivity of
cap_{\alpha}(\cdot)
(see e.g.
[
GZ17
]
), it is enough to assume
f
has a compact support in a holomorphic coordinate ball
\Omega\subset\subset X
. Clearly,
f
restricted to
\Omega
belongs to the local complex Sobolev space
W^{*}(\Omega)
. By
[
DMV23
, Theorem 2.10]
, there exists a quasi-continuous modification
\widetilde{f}\in W^{*}(\Omega)
with respect to
cap(\cdot,\Omega)
whose support is compact in
\Omega
. The equivalence (
2.12
) implies that this function is a desired one.

Let us verify the second statement. Assume
\widetilde{f}
is quasi-continuous and
g=\widetilde{f}
a.e. Then, it follows from Proposition
2.10
that
g=\widetilde{f}
on
X\setminus F
for a Borel subset
F\subset X
satisfying
{\tt c}(F)=0
. Hence,
cap_{\alpha}(F)=0
by Lemma
2.6
. Now it follows from (
2.12
) and
[
BT82
]
that
F
is locally pluripolar. Finally, by the characterization
[
GZ17
, Theorem 12.5]
,
F
is globally pluripolar, i.e., there exists a function
u\in PSH(X,\omega)
such that
F\subset\{u=-\infty\}
.
∎

A crucial technical tool to study a capacity is its extremal function. An analogous identity of (
2.8
) for
{\tt c}(\cdot)
was obtained in
[
Vig07
, Corollary 31]
. We prove here also the uniqueness which answers the question given there for open sets.

Lemma 2.13
.

Assume
E\subset X
is an open subset. There is a unique
\Phi_{E}\in W^{*}(X)
such that

{\tt c}(E)=\|\Phi_{E}\|_{*}^{2}
. Moreover,
-1\leq\Phi_{E}\leq 0
a.e. and
\Phi_{E}=-1
a.e. on
E
.

Proof.

We first prove the existence. Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence in
\mathscr{K}(E)
such that
{\tt c}(E)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{*}^{2}
. It follows from Lemma
2.2
that
u_{j}
converges weakly in
W^{1,2}(X)
to
u\in W^{*}(X)
and
\|u\|_{*}\leq\liminf_{j}\|u_{j}\|_{*}
. Since
u_{j}\to u
weakly in
L^{2}(X)
, it follows from Proposition
2.3
that
u=-1
a.e. in
E
and
u\leq 0
in
X
. Note that the proof of that proposition used only the properties of Lebesgue points of functions in
L^{2}(X)
. Since
E
is open,
u\in\mathscr{K}(E)
(this is the sole place that the openness of
E
is used). By definition of
{\tt c}(E),
we get
{\tt c}(E)=\|u\|_{*}^{2}
. Here, we can choose
\Phi_{E}=\widetilde{u}
which is quasi-continuous and
\Phi_{E}=-1
q.e. on
E
.

Next, we show the uniqueness. Suppose
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are such two functions. Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are the corresponding closed positive currents such that

{\tt c}(E)=\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|T_{1}\|=\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|T_{2}\|.

Define
v=(u_{1}+u_{2})/2
. Then,

dv\wedge d^{c}v\leq(T_{1}+T_{2})/2=:T.

So,
v\in\mathscr{K}(E)
and

\displaystyle{\tt c}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|T\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L^{1}}+\frac{1}{2}(\|T_{1}\|+\|T_{2}\|).

This implies that

2\int_{X}u_{1}u_{2}\alpha^{n}\geq\int_{X}u_{1}^{2}\;\alpha^{n}+\int_{X}u_{2}^{2}\;\alpha^{n}.

Thus,
u_{1}=u_{2}
a.e. If
u_{1},u_{2}
are quasi-continuous, then
u_{1}=u_{2}
q.e. by Proposition
2.10
.
∎

Now we have a refined definition of capacity.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
E\subset X
be a Borel set. Then,

{\tt c}(E)=\inf\{\|v\|_{*}^{2}:v\in\widetilde{\mathscr{K}}(E)\},

where

\widetilde{\mathscr{K}}(E)=\left\{v\in W^{*}(X)\;\rvert\;\widetilde{v}\leq-1\text{ q.e. on $E$ and }\widetilde{v}\leq 0\right\}.

Proof.

If
v\leq-1
a.e. on a neighborhood of
E
, then
\widetilde{v}\leq-1
q.e. by Propostion
2.10
. So,
\mathscr{K}(E)\subset\widetilde{\mathscr{K}}(E)
and
{\tt c}(E)
is greater than the infimum on the right hand side.
To show the reverse inequality let us consider
h\in\widetilde{\mathscr{K}}_{E}
and fix a quasi-continuous modification
\widetilde{h}
of
h
(Theorem
2.12
). Given
\varepsilon>0,
we choose an open set
G:=G_{\varepsilon}
such that
{\tt c}(G)<\varepsilon
and
\widetilde{h}
restricted to
X\setminus G
is continuous and
\widetilde{h}\leq-1
on
E\cap(X\setminus G)
. Denote by
u
the function
\Phi_{G}
of the open set
G
in Lemma
2.13
. The set

D_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in X\setminus G:\widetilde{h}<-1+\varepsilon\}\cup G

is open and
E\subset D_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover,
h+u\leq-1+\varepsilon
a.e. on
D_{\varepsilon}
and it is negative in
X
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle{\tt c}(E)

\displaystyle\leq{\tt c}(D_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{\|h+u\|_{*}^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\|h\|_{*}+\|u\|_{*})^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\|h\|_{*}+\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}.

By letting
\varepsilon\searrow 0
,
{\tt c}(E)\leq\|h\|_{*}^{2}
. Since
h
is arbitrary, the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 2.15
.

Vigny
[
Vig07
, Theoerem 30]
proved that this functional capacity is a Choquet capacity.
As a consequence,
{\tt c}(\cdot)
is also inner regular, i.e.,

{\tt c}(E)=\sup\left\{{\tt c}(K):K\subset E,\;K\text{ is compact}\right\}.

2.3.
The Alexander-Taylor capacity

Let
K\subset X
be a compact subset. The global Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function associated with
K
is given by

(2.15)

V_{K}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in PSH(X,\omega),v\leq 0\text{ on K}\}.

Denote by
V_{K}^{*}
the upper semi-continuous regularization of
V_{K}
. Recall that the Alexander-Taylor capacity is given by

T_{\omega}(K)=\exp\left(-\sup_{X}V_{K}^{*}\right).

This is also a Choquet capacity and
K
is pluripolar if and only if
T_{\omega}(K)=0
. Equivalently,

(2.16)

K\text{ is pluripolar }\Leftrightarrow\sup_{X}V_{K}^{*}=+\infty.

Similar to the local case
[
AT84
]
, it follows from
[
GZ17
, Lemma 12.2, Lemma 12.3]
that the global Alexander-Taylor inequality is well comparable with the global Bedford-Taylor capacity. Namely, there exists a uniform constant
A_{1}>0
such that for every compact sets
K\subset X
,

(2.17)

\exp\left(\frac{-A_{1}}{cap_{\omega}(K)}\right)\leq T_{\omega}(K)\leq\exp\left(\frac{-1}{[cap_{\omega}(K)]^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right).

Notice that since
\int_{X}\omega^{n}=1,
we always have
cap_{\omega}(K)\leq cap_{\omega}(X)=1
.

3.
Comparison of capacities

In this section we obtain comparisons between the functional capacity
{\tt c}(\cdot)
and two other classical capacities in pluripotential theory, the Bedford-Taylor capacity and the Alexander-Taylor capacity. We emphasize that the arguments are global in nature. This is why it works for
\omega,
where
\omega
is merely a
semi-positive and big
form.

We start with a technical result for estimating to the integral of functions in
W^{*}(X)
against Monge-Ampère measures of bounded psh functions.
Let
\phi\in PSH(X,\omega)
be such that
-1\leq\phi\leq 0
. Write

\omega_{\phi}:=\omega+dd^{c}\phi.

For
f\in W^{*}(X)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
, we define the integral

(3.1)

\int_{X}f\;\omega_{\phi}^{n}:=\int_{X}\widetilde{f}\;\omega_{\phi}^{n}.

This definition is well-defined. In fact,
if
f=g
a.e., then
\widetilde{f}=\widetilde{g}
q.e. by Proposition
2.10
. Since the Monge-Ampère measure
\omega_{\phi}^{n}
does not charge on pluripolar sets, the integral on the right hand side does not depends on the quasi-continuous modification we chose.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
h\in PSH(X,\omega)
be such that
-1\leq h\leq 0
. Let
v\in W^{*}(X)
satisfy
-1\leq v\leq 0
and
dv\wedge d^{c}v\leq T
for a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega+2\int_{X}T\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}-h\omega_{h}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}h\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega.

Proof.

The proof goes through by integration by parts. We first prove it for
h
and
v
being smooth and then the general case via approximation. The subtle point is that we may not find a good smooth sequence in
W^{*}(X)
which converges strongly to
v
in
W^{*}(X)
. Fortunately, we can resolve this by using the quasi-continuity of
v
. It follows from Theorem
2.12
and Lemma
2.6
that
v
admits a quasi-continuous representation
\widetilde{v}
with respect to the capacity
cap_{\alpha}(\cdot)
. By definition (
3.1
), we can assume
v
is quasi-continuous itself. Moreover, by considering
\omega:=\omega+\delta\alpha
and then letting
\delta\to 0^{+},
we may assume that
\omega
is a Kähler form.

Step 1a
: Assume both
v
and
h
are smooth. Compute

(3.2)

\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n}=\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega+\int_{X}v^{2}dd^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}.

By the Stokes theorem and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{X}v^{2}dd^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=2\int_{X}(-v)dv\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega^{n-1}_{h}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int_{X}dv\wedge d^{c}v\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{X}v^{2}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{X}dv\wedge d^{c}v\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1},

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
0\leq v^{2}\leq 1
.
Using the Stokes theorem again,

\displaystyle\int_{X}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int-hdd^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}-h\omega_{h}^{n}+\int_{X}h\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega.

Combining this, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we get the conclusion of the lemma.

Step 1b
: Assume only
h
is smooth. Let
\{v_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
,
0\leq v_{j}\leq 1
, be a smooth sequence which converges strongly to
v
in the usual Sobolev norm of
W^{1,2}(X,\mathbb{R})
. It follows from (
3.3
) that

\int_{X}v_{j}^{2}dd^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}\leq 2\int_{X}dv_{j}\wedge d^{c}v_{j}\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}.

Letting
j\to\infty,
we conclude that (
3.3
) holds for
v\in W^{*}(X)
and
h
is smooth. Combining it with the assumption
dv\wedge d^{c}v\leq T,
we get

\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n}\leq\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega+2\int T\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}.

By integration by parts and Stokes’ theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{X}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}=\int_{X}-h\omega_{h}^{n}+\int_{X}h\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega,

\displaystyle\int_{X}T\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}=\int_{X}T\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

Finally, we conclude

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega+2\int_{X}T\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}-h\omega_{h}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}h\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega.

Step 2:
Lastly, we remove the smoothness assumption on
h
in (
3.4
). This is where we use the quasi-continuity of
v^{2}\in W^{*}(X)
. Let
\{h_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of smooth
\omega
-psh functions decreasing to
h
(see. e.g.,
[
BK07
]
).
By Step 1b, the inequality (
3.4
) holds for
h_{j}
. By letting
j\to\infty
and invoking the weak convergence theorem
[
BT87
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
GZ17
, Theorem 4.26]
, we conclude that (
3.4
) holds for a general
h
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
E\subset X
be a compact subset. Let
v\in PSH(X,\omega)
be such that
v\leq 0
on
E
and
M:=\sup_{X}v\geq 1
. Define
\phi=(v-M-1)/M
. Then,
\phi\in\mathscr{K}(E)
satisfies

\|\phi\|_{*}^{2}\leq\frac{A_{0}}{M},

where
A_{0}
is a uniform constant depending only on
X,\omega
.
Consequently,
{\tt c}(E)=0
for a compact pluripolar set
E
.

Proof.

We have

-1\leq u:=\frac{v-M}{M}\leq 0.

Hence
\phi=u-1/M\in\mathscr{K}(E)
. Next we compute
W^{*}
-norm of
\phi
. The normalization
\sup_{X}(v-M)=0
and
v\in PSH(X,\omega)
imply a well-known fact that

\int_{X}|u|\alpha^{n}=\int_{X}\frac{M-v}{M}\alpha^{n}\leq\frac{A_{1}}{M}.

for a uniform constant
A_{1}>0
.
Since
|\phi|\leq 2
, we get

(3.5)

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}=\int_{X}(u-1/M)^{2}\alpha^{n}\leq\int_{X}-2(u-1/M)\alpha^{n}\leq\frac{A_{2}}{M},

where
A_{2}=2A_{1}+2\int_{X}\alpha^{n}.

Moreover, as
1+u\in PSH(X,\omega/M)
and
0\leq 1+u\leq 1
, we have

T:=\frac{\omega}{M}+\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}u^{2}+dd^{c}u\geq du\wedge d^{c}u=d\phi\wedge d^{c}\phi.

It follows from (
3.5
) and the Stokes theorem that

\|\phi\|_{*}^{2}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}+\|T\|\leq\frac{A_{2}}{M}+\frac{1}{M}\int_{X}\omega\wedge\alpha^{n-1}=:\frac{A_{0}}{M}.

This completes the proof of the first statement.

Now assume
E
is a compact pluripolar set. By (
2.16
),
\sup_{X}V_{E}^{*}=+\infty
, where
V_{E}^{*}
is its associated Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function defined in (
2.15
). Choquet’s lemma yields there exists a sequence
\{v_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset PSH(X,\omega)
satisfying
v_{j}\leq 0
on
E
and

M_{j}:=\sup_{X}v_{j}\to+\infty

as
j\to\infty
. Applying the first part for
\phi_{j}=(v_{j}-M_{j}-1)/M_{j},
we get
\|\phi_{j}\|_{*}^{2}\leq A_{0}/M_{j}
. Since
\phi_{j}\in\mathscr{K}(E)
,
it follows that
{\tt c}(E)\leq A_{0}/M_{j}
. Letting
j\to+\infty,
the second statement follows.
∎

The following result is the key estimate to prove Theorem
1.1
. It provides a precise quantitative comparison between
{\tt c}(\cdot)
and
cap_{\omega}(\cdot)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

There exists a constant
A>0
such that for every compact subset
E\subset X
,

\frac{1}{4n}cap_{\omega}(E)\leq{\tt c}(E)\leq A\left[cap_{\omega}(E)\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Proof.

Let
E\subset X
be compact.
Let
h_{E}^{*}
be the relative extremal function of
E
defined in (
2.9
).
Then,
0\leq h_{E}^{*}\leq 1
and
h_{E}^{*}\in PSH(X,\omega)
.
Next, let
V_{E}^{*}
be the global extremal function for
E
as in (
2.15
). If
E
is pluripolar, then
cap_{\alpha}(E)=0
by
[
GZ17
, Corollary 9.9, Theorem 9.17]
, so is
cap_{\omega}(E)=0
. Similarly,
{\tt c}(E)=0
by Lemma
3.2
. Hence, both inequalities hold true. Therefore, from now on we assume
E
is non-pluripolar.
By (
2.16
) we have
0\leq V_{E}^{*}\in PSH(X,\omega)
and

0\leq M:=\sup_{X}V_{E}^{*}<+\infty.

Let us prove the second inequality in the theorem. If
0\leq M\leq 1
, then
h_{E}^{*}=V_{E}^{*}-1
. It is easy to see from (
2.8
) that

\int_{X}\omega^{n}=\int_{K}(\omega+dd^{c}V_{E}^{*})^{n}\leq cap_{\omega}(E)\leq\int_{X}\omega^{n}.

Hence,

cap_{\omega}(E)=cap_{\omega}(X)=1
by normalization (
2.1
). Hence, the conclusion follows immediately from Proposition
2.4
-(c).

It remains to consider
M\geq 1
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
for
v=V_{E}^{*}
,
and
\phi=(V_{E}^{*}-M-1)/M,
we obtain

(3.6)

\|\phi\|_{*}^{2}\leq\frac{A_{0}}{M}.

Since
\{V_{E}<V_{E}^{*}\}
is pluripolar,
V_{E}^{*}\leq 0
q.e. on
E
and therefore
\phi\in\widetilde{\mathscr{K}}(E)
.

The global Alexander-Taylor inequality (
2.17
) is equivalent to

(3.7)

\frac{cap_{\omega}(E)}{A_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{M}\leq[cap_{\omega}(E)]^{\frac{1}{n}}

for a uniform constant
A_{1}>0
. Thus, Lemma
2.14
yields

{\tt c}(E)\leq\|\phi\|_{*}^{2}\leq A_{0}[cap_{\omega}(E)]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

We proceed to prove the first inequality of the theorem.
A weaker version is already proved in Lemma
2.6
. Here we use a global argument to improve significantly the constant and remove Kähler condition on
\omega
. Recall that
E\subset X
is compact. Let
v\in\widetilde{\mathscr{K}}(E)
be such that
-1\leq v\leq 0
. Hence,
\widetilde{v}=-1
q.e. on
E
. For simplicity, we write
h:=h_{E}^{*}
the relative extremal function associated to
E
and write
v
for
\widetilde{v}
in what follows.
Since
v=-1
q.e. on
E
, it follows from (
2.8
) that

(3.8)

cap_{\omega}(E)=\int_{E}\omega_{h}^{n}\leq\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n}.

Now we will estimate the integral

\int_{X}v^{2}(\omega+dd^{c}h)^{n}

from above. In fact, let
T
be a positive closed (1,1)-current realizing the
W^{*}
-norm for
v
. In particular,

dv\wedge d^{c}v\leq T.

By Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega+2\int_{X}T\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}(-h)\omega_{h}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}h\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega.

Note
cap_{\omega}(E)=\int_{X}-h\omega_{h}^{n}
by (
2.8
). Hence, combining (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we get

(3.10)

\frac{1}{2}cap_{\omega}(E)\leq\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega+2\|T\|_{\omega}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}h\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega,

where we used the notation
\|T\|_{\omega}=\int_{X}T\wedge\omega^{n-1}
.
Next,

\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega=\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-2}\wedge\omega^{2}+\int_{X}v^{2}dd^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-2}\wedge\omega.

Observe that we can apply the argument in (
3.3
) for
dd^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-2}\wedge\omega
in the place of
dd^{c}h\wedge\omega_{h}^{n-1}
. This yields

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-2}\wedge\omega^{2}+2\|T\|_{\omega}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}(-h)\omega_{h}^{n-1}\wedge\omega+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}h\omega_{h}^{n-2}\wedge\omega^{2}.

The important point here is that when plugging this estimate into (
3.10
) the third integral on the right hand side is cancelled out. Hence,
we get

\frac{1}{2}cap(E)\leq\int_{X}v^{2}\omega_{h}^{n-2}\wedge\omega^{2}+4\|T\|_{\omega}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}h\omega_{h}^{n-2}\wedge\omega^{2}.

Compared to (
3.10
), we lowered the exponent of
\omega_{h}
by one after using the integration by parts inequality in Lemma
3.1
.
Repeating the process for
(n-2)
-times more, we finally get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}cap_{\omega}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}v^{2}\omega^{n}+2n\|T\|_{\omega}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}h\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}+2n\|T\|

\displaystyle\leq 2n\|v\|_{*}^{2},

as
h
is negative,
T
realizes the
W^{*}
-norm of
v
and
\omega\leq\alpha
. Since
v\in\widetilde{\mathscr{K}}(E)
,
-1\leq v\leq 0
is arbitrary, we conclude
cap_{\omega}(E)\leq 4n\;{\tt c}(E)
by Lemma
2.14
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The second inequality in the theorem was obtained in
[
DKN22
, Proposition 5.1]
when
\omega=\alpha
is a Kähler form and
{\tt c}(E)
was defined by another equivalent norm with
W^{*}
-norm. More precisely, for
f\in W^{*}(X)
, one defines

\|f\|_{*^{\prime}}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(X)}+\min\{\|T\|^{\frac{1}{2}}:T\in\Gamma_{f}\}.

Clearly,
\|f\|_{*^{\prime}}^{2}\leq 2\|f\|_{*}^{2}
. The other direction follows from the Poincaré inequality. However, the sharpness of exponents were not known in
[
DKN22
]
. A qualitative version of the second inequality was also obtained in
[
Vig07
, Proposition 29]
without a specific exponent.

We are ready to get the comparison between capacities in the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
K\subset X
be a compact subset. It follows from (
2.17
) and Theorem
3.3
that we may assume
K
is non-pluripolar. Let
V_{K}^{*}
be the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function associated to
K
and
M=\sup_{X}V_{K}^{*}
. By definitions of two capacities, the first inequality is equivalent to

{\tt c}(K)\leq A/M

for a uniform constant
A>0
. If
0\leq M<1
, then it is obvious as
{\tt c}(K)\leq 1
(Proposition
2.4
-(c)). Otherwise, it is an immediate consequence of (
3.6
).
Next, we have from (
3.7
) and Theorem
3.3
that

1/M\leq[cap_{\omega}(E)]^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq[4n{\tt c}(E)]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

This is equivalent to the second inequality.
∎

The optimality of exponents are followed from the following concrete examples.

Remark 3.5
.

The inequalities in Theorem
1.1
as well as in Theorem
3.3
are sharp as far as the exponents are concerned. Indeed, let us consider
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\omega
be the Fubini-Study metric. The first inequality in Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
1.1
implies

(3.12)

\exp\left(-A^{\prime}/cap_{\omega}(K)\right)\leq T_{\omega}(K),

where
A^{\prime}=A/4n
.
By
[
GZ17
, Example 9.22]
that the global capacity
T_{\omega}(\cdot)
and its local version
T_{\mathbb{B}}(\cdot)
, where
\mathbb{B}=B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the unit ball, are comparable modulo a uniform constant. Namely,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}T_{\omega}(K)\leq T_{\mathbb{B}}(K)\leq 2T_{\omega}(K).

Moreover, (
2.12
) shows that
cap_{\omega}(E)
and
cap(E,\Omega)
are equivalent to each other up to a uniform constant for every Borel set
E\subset D\subset\subset\Omega
where
\Omega\subset\subset X
is a strictly pseudoconvex local coordinate chart. Let
K=\{(z_{1},...,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z_{1}|\leq\delta,|z_{j}|\leq 1/2,\;j=2,...,n\}

(\delta<1/2)
be a polydisc, which is contained in a local coordinate unit ball. Alexander and Taylor
[
AT84
, Remark 2]
showed that the exponents in (
3.12
) are sharp for such compact sets. So are the ones in the first inequality in Theorem
3.3
.

Next, the right hand side inequality in (
2.17
) reads

T_{\omega}(K)\leq\exp\left(\frac{-1}{[cap_{\omega}(K)]^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)

where the exponents are sharp by the same local comparable reason and
[
AT84
, Remark 2]
as above. In this case the example is given by a small closed ball
K=\{z:|z|\leq\varepsilon\}
. Combining this with Theorem
1.1
and (
3.12
) we obtain

\exp\left(-A/{\tt c}(K)\right)\leq T_{\omega}(K)\leq\exp\left(\frac{-1}{[cap_{\omega}(K)]^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)

with sharp exponents. This concludes the sharpness of the exponents in the second inequality in Theorem
3.3
. So is the second one in Theorem
1.1
.

Next, we prove the existence of bounded solution to the Monge-Ampère equation. Recall from
[
EGZ09
, Definition 1.3]
that the measure
\mu
belongs to the class
\mathcal{H}(\tau)
with
\tau>0
if

\mu(K)\leq A[cap_{\omega}(K)]^{1+\tau}

for every Borel subset
K\subset X
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

By
[
EGZ09
, Theorem 2.1]
, it enough to show that
\mu\in\mathcal{H}(\tau_{1})
for some
\tau_{1}>0
. The proof of
[
DKN22
