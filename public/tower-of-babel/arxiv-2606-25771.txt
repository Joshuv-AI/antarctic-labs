Introduction

1.1
The problem

Gatheral, Jaisson, and Rosenbaum
[
1
]
estimated the
Hurst exponent of log-volatility at
H\approx 0.1
, far below
1/2
,
meaning rougher-than-Brownian dynamics. Cont and Das
[
2
]

and Rogers
[
3
]
challenged this: the low
H
estimate may
be a statistical artefact of microstructure noise contaminating
realized volatility at small scales.

The debate is a statistical problem: given price observations
contaminated by microstructure noise, estimate the true Hölder
exponent
H(t)
of the underlying volatility process.

The difficulty is structural. The Hölder exponent is

H(t)=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log|X(t+r)-X(t)|}{\log r},

a limit as
r\to 0
— exactly where noise dominates. Classical
estimators either pre-average (losing pointwise structure) or use
integrated quantities like realized variance (estimating a global
H
,
not a time-varying one).

1.2
What is known

[
4
,
5
]
construct an
estimator from discrete observations of
\int_{0}^{t}g(X(s))\,ds
for a
nonlinear
g
. They prove a.s. consistency with an explicit rate.
This is the state of the art, but: it estimates a global (constant)

H
; requires integrated variance observations, not the price path
directly; and gives no noise-robust estimate at finite resolution.
Fukasawa et al.
[
6
]
and Bolko et al.
[
7
]

develop pre-averaged estimators that are noise-robust but again give a
global
H
.

New results

The geometry accumulation estimator:

\hat{H}_{\Lambda}(t)=1+\frac{\log G_{\Lambda}(t)}{\log\Lambda},\qquad G_{\Lambda}(t)=\int_{\Lambda}^{1}|\eth_{s}X(t)|\,s^{-1}\,ds.

(Consistency)

Let
X

uniformly in

.
Then

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\hat{H}_{\Lambda}(t)-H(t)|\to 0\quad\text{a.s.\ as }\Lambda\to 0.

Theorem 1.2

(Noise robustness)
.

Let
\tilde{X}(t)=X(t)+\varepsilon(t)

and

For all

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\hat{H}_{\Lambda}^{\tilde{X}}(t)-H(t)|\to 0\quad\text{a.s.}

2
The geometry accumulation estimator

2.1
Setup

Let
X:[0,1]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a stochastic process, observed at
discrete times
t_{i}=i/n
,
i=0,\dots,n
.

Definition 2.1

(Scale derivative and accumulator)
.

For
s\in(0,1)
and
t\in[0,1-s]
:

\eth_{s}X(t):=\frac{X(t+s)-X(t)}{s}.

The geometry accumulation integral at resolution
\Lambda\in(0,1)
:

G_{\Lambda}(t):=\int_{\Lambda}^{1}|\eth_{s}X(t)|\,s^{-1}\,ds.

The estimator:

\hat{H}_{\Lambda}(t):=1+\frac{\log G_{\Lambda}(t)}{\log\Lambda}.

Remark 2.2
.

G_{\Lambda}(t)
integrates path information across a whole range of scales

[\Lambda,1]
, not just a single scale. The Hölder exponent
controls how
G_{\Lambda}
scales:

G_{\Lambda}(t)\asymp\Lambda^{H(t)-1}
, so
\log G_{\Lambda}(t)/\log\Lambda\to H(t)-1
.
This is the core mechanism: the exponent is read off the slope of

\log G_{\Lambda}
against
\log\Lambda
.

2.2
HGDS framework

The estimator comes from the History-Generated Dynamical Systems (HGDS)
framework, where every process admits a canonical scale decomposition at
each
\Lambda>0
:

X(t+\Lambda)-X(t)=\eth_{\Lambda}X(t)\cdot\Lambda+R_{\Lambda}X(t).

\eth_{\Lambda}X
is the macro-scale derivative and
R_{\Lambda}X
is
the fine-scale residual.
G_{\Lambda}
measures total macro-scale variation
across
[\Lambda,1]
, encoding path roughness. For
H(t)\in(0,1)
:

\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X(t)|]\asymp s^{H(t)-1},\quad G_{\Lambda}(t)\asymp\int_{\Lambda}^{1}s^{H(t)-2}\,ds=\frac{1-\Lambda^{H(t)-1}}{1-H(t)}\asymp\Lambda^{H(t)-1}.

3
Consistency

Two conditions:

(C1)

Scale decorrelation:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\int_{e^{-n}}^{1}\!\int_{e^{-n}}^{1}|\mathrm{Cov}(\eth_{s}X(t),\eth_{u}X(t))|\,(su)^{-1}\,ds\,du}{\bigl(\int_{e^{-n}}^{1}\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X(t)|]s^{-1}\,ds\bigr)^{2}}<\infty

uniformly in
t
.

(C2)

Hölder regularity:

\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X(t)-\eth_{s}X(t^{\prime})|^{p}]\leq C|t-t^{\prime}|^{pH}s^{p(H-1)}

for some
p>1/H
.

(C3)

Mean asymptotics:

\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X(t)|]\asymp s^{H(t)-1}
as
s\to 0
, uniformly in
t
.

Lemma 3.1

(Mean accumulator asymptotics)
.

Under (C3):

\frac{\log\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}(t)]}{\log\Lambda}\to H(t)-1\quad\text{uniformly in }t\text{ as }\Lambda\to 0.

Proof.

Split
\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}(t)]=\int_{\Lambda}^{s_{0}}+\int_{s_{0}}^{1}
. The second
integral is
O(1)
. For the first, using (C3):

\int_{\Lambda}^{s_{0}}\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X(t)|]s^{-1}\,ds\asymp\int_{\Lambda}^{s_{0}}s^{H(t)-2}\,ds=\frac{s_{0}^{H(t)-1}-\Lambda^{H(t)-1}}{1-H(t)}\asymp\Lambda^{H(t)-1}.

Taking logs:
\log\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}(t)]\sim(H(t)-1)\log\Lambda
.
Uniformity follows from the uniform bound in (C3). ∎∎

Proposition 3.2

(Uniform a.s. concentration)
.

Under (C1) and (C2), writing
\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}=G_{\Lambda}/\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}]
:

\sup_{t}|\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t)-1|\to 0\quad\text{a.s.\ as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Pointwise convergence.

\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}(t))
equals the summand in (C1) at

\Lambda=e^{-n}
. By (C1):
\sum_{n}\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t))<\infty
.
Chebyshev and Borel–Cantelli give
\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t)\to 1
a.s. for each
t
.

Uniform convergence.

By (C2) and Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion applied to

t\mapsto\tilde{G}_{e^{-n}}(t)
: the process has a.s. Hölder
continuous sample paths in
t
, uniformly in
n
. A standard
maximal inequality (applied to the spatial supremum over a dense grid
then extended by continuity) upgrades pointwise a.s. convergence to
uniform a.s. convergence. ∎∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Write:

\hat{H}_{\Lambda}(t)-H(t)=\underbrace{\frac{\log\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}(t)]}{\log\Lambda}-(H(t)-1)}_{\to 0\text{ by Lemma~\ref{lem:mean}}}+\underbrace{\frac{\log\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}(t)}{\log\Lambda}}_{\to 0\text{ by Prop.~\ref{prop:conc}}}.

Second term:
|\log\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}|/|\log\Lambda|\leq 2|\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}-1|/|\log\Lambda|\to 0
a.s. since

\sup_{t}|\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}-1|\to 0
and
|\log\Lambda|\to\infty
.
Both terms go to zero uniformly in
t
. ∎∎

4
Noise robustness

4.1
The noise model

Observed price:
\tilde{X}(t)=X(t)+\varepsilon(t)
, where
\varepsilon

is white noise:
\mathbb{E}[|\varepsilon(t+s)-\varepsilon(t)|^{2}]=2\sigma^{2}

for all
s>0
.

Scale derivative of the observed process:

\eth_{s}\tilde{X}(t)=\eth_{s}X(t)+\eth_{s}\varepsilon(t),\quad\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}\varepsilon(t)|]=\frac{\mathbb{E}[|\varepsilon(t+s)-\varepsilon(t)|]}{s}\asymp\frac{\sigma}{s}.

Noise scales as
s^{-1}
. Signal scales as
s^{H-1}
with
H<1
.
Noise dominates at small scales.

4.2
Scale separation

The crossover scale
\Lambda^{*}
satisfies
\sigma/\Lambda^{*}=(\Lambda^{*})^{H-1}
,
i.e.
(\Lambda^{*})^{H}=\sigma
, giving
\Lambda^{*}=\sigma^{1/H}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Noise contamination)
.

For
\Lambda>\Lambda^{*}=\sigma^{1/H}
:

G_{\Lambda}^{\tilde{X}}(t)=G_{\Lambda}^{X}(t)\cdot(1+o(1))\quad\text{a.s.\ as }\sigma\to 0.

Proof.

Noise contribution to
G_{\Lambda}
:

G_{\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}=\int_{\Lambda}^{1}|\eth_{s}\varepsilon|\,s^{-1}\,ds\asymp\sigma\int_{\Lambda}^{1}s^{-2}\,ds=\sigma(\Lambda^{-1}-1)\asymp\sigma\Lambda^{-1}.

Signal contribution:
G_{\Lambda}^{X}\asymp\Lambda^{H-1}
.

Ratio:
G_{\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}/G_{\Lambda}^{X}\asymp\sigma\Lambda^{-1}/\Lambda^{H-1}=\sigma\Lambda^{-H}=(\sigma^{1/H}/\Lambda)^{H}
.

For
\Lambda>\Lambda^{*}=\sigma^{1/H}
: this ratio
<1
and
\to 0
as

\sigma\to 0
. So
G_{\Lambda}^{\tilde{X}}=G_{\Lambda}^{X}+G_{\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}=G_{\Lambda}^{X}(1+o(1))
a.s. ∎∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For
\Lambda>\Lambda^{*}
, Lemma
4.1
gives

\log G_{\Lambda}^{\tilde{X}}=\log G_{\Lambda}^{X}+\log(1+o(1))=\log G_{\Lambda}^{X}+o(1)
.
So
\hat{H}_{\Lambda}^{\tilde{X}}(t)=\hat{H}_{\Lambda}^{X}(t)+o(1)/\log\Lambda
.
By Theorem
1.1
:
\hat{H}_{\Lambda}^{X}(t)\to H(t)
a.s.
The
o(1)/\log\Lambda
term vanishes since
|\log\Lambda|\to\infty
. ∎∎

Remark 4.2

(Practical threshold)
.

For rough volatility with
H\approx 0.1
:

\Lambda^{*}=\sigma^{1/0.1}=\sigma^{10}
.
For
\sigma=0.01
(1% microstructure noise):
\Lambda^{*}=10^{-20}
.
Any sampling frequency above this is noise-free. This suggests why
classical estimators may be contaminated at typical frequencies: they
probe scales below
\Lambda^{*}
, while the HGDS estimator at

\Lambda>\Lambda^{*}
is uncontaminated.

5
Central limit theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Write
\hat{H}_{\Lambda}(t)-H(t)=(\log\Lambda)^{-1}\log\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}(t)+\delta_{\Lambda}(t)
,
where
\delta_{\Lambda}=(\log\Lambda)^{-1}(\log\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}]-(H-1)\log\Lambda)\to 0

by Lemma
3.1
.

For the main term:
\log\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}\approx\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}-1

(since
\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}\to 1
a.s. by Proposition
3.2
).
Write:

\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}(t)-1=\int_{\Lambda}^{1}(\xi_{s}(t)-1)\,w_{\Lambda}(s)\,ds,\quad\xi_{s}=\frac{|\eth_{s}X|}{\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X|]},

where
w_{\Lambda}(s)=\mathbb{E}[|\eth_{s}X|]s^{-1}/\mathbb{E}[G_{\Lambda}]
is a probability
weight on
[\Lambda,1]
.

This is a weighted integral of mean-zero terms. We apply the CLT
for mixing arrays
[
8
]
: conditions are (a) the
variance is
O(1/|\log\Lambda|)
(from (C1) with equality on the
diagonal), and (b) the Lindeberg condition holds since
\xi_{s}
has
bounded moments (from (C2) with
p>1/H
). The mixing condition
follows directly from (C1). Hence:

|\log\Lambda|^{1/2}(\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}(t)-1)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{H}^{2}),

where

\sigma_{H}^{2}=\lim_{\Lambda\to 0}\frac{1}{|\log\Lambda|}\int_{\Lambda}^{1}\!\int_{\Lambda}^{1}\mathrm{Cov}(\xi_{s},\xi_{u})\,w_{\Lambda}(s)w_{\Lambda}(u)\,s^{-1}u^{-1}\,ds\,du.

Combining:
(\log\Lambda)^{1/2}(\hat{H}_{\Lambda}(t)-H(t))\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{H}^{2})
. ∎∎

6
Application to rough volatility

6.1
The Gatheral–Jaisson–Rosenbaum model

Spot volatility
\sigma(t)=\exp(V(t))
where
V
is fractional
Brownian motion with
H=0.1
. Log-price:

\log S(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)\,dW(s)+\text{drift}
.
The Hölder exponent of
\sigma(t)
is
H=0.1
a.s. (monofractal).
Observed:
\tilde{S}(t_{i})=S(t_{i})\cdot e^{\varepsilon_{i}}
where

\varepsilon_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{noise}}^{2})
i.i.d.

Corollary 6.1

(Consistent estimation under microstructure)
.

In the rough volatility model above, with

\Lambda>\Lambda^{*}=\sigma_{\mathrm{noise}}^{10}
:

\hat{H}_{\Lambda}(t)\to 0.1\quad\text{a.s.\ for all }t\in[0,1].

Proof.

Apply Theorem
1.2
. The log-return process satisfies (C1)
and (C2) with
H=0.1
by standard properties of fBm

[
9
]
. The threshold
\Lambda^{*}=\sigma_{\mathrm{noise}}^{10}

is practically zero for any realistic noise level (Remark
4.2
).
∎∎

6.2
Time-varying roughness detection

Corollary 6.2

(Non-stationarity test)
.

If
H(t)
is genuinely time-varying with

\inf_{|t-t^{\prime}|\geq\tau}|H(t)-H(t^{\prime})|\geq\delta>0
,
then
\sup_{t}|\hat{H}_{\Lambda}(t)-H(t)|\to 0
distinguishes this from constant
H

once
\Lambda<\delta^{1/(1-H_{\max})}
.

This gives the first test for time-variation in roughness that does
not require pre-averaging or a parametric model.
