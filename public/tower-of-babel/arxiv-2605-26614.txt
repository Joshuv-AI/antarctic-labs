1
Introduction

A central theme in extremal graph theory is to understand how global
density conditions force copies of a fixed graph. For bipartite graphs,
one of the most influential principles of this kind is Sidorenko’s
conjecture, which predicts that random graphs minimize homomorphism
counts among graphs with a fixed edge density.

This paper develops a spectral form of Sidorenko’s inequality and applies
it to sharp edge-spectral supersaturation. The spectral
radius is the natural parameter in spectral extremal graph theory, and
in several basic cases the spectral form of Sidorenko’s inequality is
more natural than the usual average-degree form. For instance, for every
t\geq 2
,

\mathrm{hom}(C_{2t},G)=\operatorname{tr}A(G)^{2t}\geq\lambda(G)^{2t},

(1)

which immediately gives the spectral Sidorenko inequality for even cycles.
For complete bipartite graphs, we prove the corresponding spectral Sidorenko
inequality through an operator-norm certificate and Riesz–Thorin
interpolation.

Our first main theorem shows that, for bipartite graphs with at most as
many vertices as edges, the spectral form is actually equivalent to the
usual Sidorenko inequality. Thus the spectral viewpoint is not a
strictly stronger conjecture, but rather a different and often useful
form of the same phenomenon.

We then use this equivalence as a counting tool in edge-spectral
supersaturation.
We prove sharp asymptotic lower bounds
for the number of copies of
K_{t,t}
and
C_{2t}
in any
m
-edge
graph whose spectral radius exceeds the relevant split-graph extremal
threshold. A notable feature is that the two sharp constructions are
different: balanced random graphs are extremal for
K_{t,t}
, whereas
split graphs are extremal for
C_{2t}
.

1.1
Spectral Sidorenko inequalities

For two graphs
G
and
H
, let
\mathrm{hom}(H,G)
be the number of graph
homomorphisms from
H
to
G
, and let
\mathrm{inj}(H,G)
be the number of
labelled injective homomorphisms. We write
\texttt{\#}H(G):=\frac{\mathrm{inj}(H,G)}{|\mathrm{Aut}(H)|}

for the number of unlabelled copies of
H
in
G
. We also write
|G|:=|V(G)|
and
M(G):=2e(G).

Sidorenko’s conjecture
[
38
]

asserts that, for every bipartite graph
H
with
v
vertices and

e
edges,

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq|G|^{v-2e}M(G)^{e}

for every graph
G
. Equivalently, if

p:=\frac{M(G)}{|G|^{2}}
is the edge density of
G
, then a uniformly random map

V(H)\to V(G)
is a homomorphism with probability at least
p^{e}
.

Let
A(G)
be the adjacency matrix of
G
, and let
\lambda(G)
be
its spectral radius. Since

\lambda(G)
is at least the average degree
\frac{M(G)}{|G|},

it is natural to ask whether Sidorenko’s inequality can be strengthened
by replacing part of the average-degree term with the spectral radius. For
a bipartite graph
H
with
v
vertices and
e
edges, the natural
spectral form is

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\lambda(G)^{2e-v}M(G)^{v-e}.

This inequality immediately implies Sidorenko’s
inequality when
H
has no isolated vertices, since
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{M(G)}{|G|}
.

Our first theorem shows that, perhaps surprisingly, the converse also holds.

Theorem 1.1

(Equivalence of Sidorenko and spectral Sidorenko)
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph with
v
vertices and
e
edges, where

v\leq e
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

H
satisfies Sidorenko’s inequality: for every graph
G
,

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq|G|^{v-2e}M(G)^{e}.

(ii)

H
satisfies the edge-spectral Sidorenko inequality: for every graph
G
,

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\lambda(G)^{2e-v}M(G)^{v-e}.

(2)

(iii)

H
satisfies the vertex-spectral Sidorenko inequality: for every graph
G
,

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\lambda(G)^{e}|G|^{v-e}.

We remark that the assumption
v\leq e
in Theorem
1.1
is necessary.
The spectral Sidorenko inequality (
2
) fails already for
H=P_{3}
. Indeed, considering
G:=K_{1,t}\sqcup t^{2}K_{2}
, we have
\lambda(G)=\sqrt{t}
. Then
\lambda(G)M(G)=2t^{5/2}+O(t^{3/2})
and

\lambda(G)^{2}|G|=2t^{3}+O(t^{2})
, whereas

\mathrm{hom}(P_{3},G)=3t^{2}+O(t)
. Thus, for large
t
,
\mathrm{hom}(P_{3},G)\ll\lambda(G)M(G)\ll\lambda(G)^{2}|G|
.

We also introduce a direct route to the spectral Sidorenko inequality (
2
). For
v\leq e
, set
s_{H}:=\frac{2e}{v}
and
s_{H}^{\prime}:=\frac{2e}{2e-v}
.
We say that
H
satisfies the
operator-norm Sidorenko
certificate
if

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\|A(G)\|_{s_{H}^{\prime}\to s_{H}}^{\,e}

for every graph
G
. The Riesz–Thorin interpolation shows that this
certificate implies the spectral Sidorenko inequality (Lemma
2.3
). Hence, whenever the certificate can be verified directly, it gives an
analytic proof of the spectral Sidorenko inequality. For
K_{t,t}
, applying Hölder’s inequality gives the certificate

\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)\geq\|A(G)\|_{t/(t-1)\to t}^{t^{2}}
(Lemma
2.4
). Interpolation then yields

\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)\geq\frac{\lambda(G)^{2t(t-1)}}{M(G)^{t(t-2)}}.

(3)

For
C_{2t}
, the operator-norm certificate is exactly (
1
). Thus, the spectral form is not merely a formal consequence of Sidorenko’s conjecture; in some cases it is the more transparent inequality to prove.

Theorem
1.1
allows us to transfer known cases of Sidorenko’s conjecture
[
38
,
6
,
15
,
17
,
8
,
7
]
directly to the spectral setting. The operator-norm certificate also gives direct proofs for
complete bipartite graphs and even cycles. For many weakly norming examples, including hypercubes and the
reflection-graph examples of Conlon–Lee
[
8
]
, the
certificate can be obtained from the corresponding weighted Sidorenko inequalities or
the graph-norm Hölder inequalities. Since weakly norming graphs are
already known to be Sidorenko, this should be viewed as a spectral
transfer principle rather than a new proof of Sidorenko for those
families.

The certificate is nevertheless not merely a reformulation of weakly
norming. In Section
2
, we prove that it is
stable under
1
-subdivision (Proposition
2.5
). Consequently, since
K_{a,b}
satisfies
the certificate, so does its
1
-subdivision. For
a\neq b
, this
subdivision is not edge-transitive and hence cannot be weakly norming. Thus, the operator-norm certificate gives spectral
Sidorenko inequalities for examples outside the weakly norming class.

The main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is a
spectral regularization lemma.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite graph with
M(G)>0
, and let

\lambda=\lambda(G)
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(G)>0
such
that, for infinitely many positive integers
k
, the tensor power

G^{\otimes k}
contains a regular subgraph
F_{k}
of degree
d_{k}

satisfying
\lambda^{k}k^{-C}\leq d_{k}\leq\lambda^{k}.

Moreover,
M(F_{k})\leq M(G)^{k}.

It is known that Sidorenko’s conjecture admits reductions to
regular host graphs in the ordinary average-density setting; see
Coregliano–Razborov
[
9
]
, building
on Szegedy
[
39
]
. These reductions can not
directly apply to the spectral formulation, where the spectral radius may
be much larger than the average degree. Theorem
1.2

provides the needed spectral regularization: inside tensor powers, it
finds regular subgraphs whose degrees capture
\lambda(G)
, rather
than the average degree
M(G)/|G|
.

1.2
Edge-spectral supersaturation

We now turn to the application of spectral Sidorenko inequalities to
edge-spectral supersaturation. Given a fixed graph
F
, one asks how
many copies of
F
must appear in an
m
-edge graph
G
once

\lambda(G)
exceeds the maximum possible spectral radius of an

F
-free
m
-edge graph.

This is closely related to the Brualdi–Hoffman–Turán problem, which
asks for the maximum spectral radius of a graph with a forbidden subgraph
and a given number of edges.
For example, a well-known theorem of Nikiforov
[
31
]
shows that every
K_{r+1}
-free graph
G
with
m
edges satisfies
\lambda(G)^{2}\leq(1-\frac{1}{r})2m
; see
[
3
]
for an entropic extension.
There is now a substantial literature on
this edge-spectral Turán-type problem; see, e.g.,
[
42
,
19
,
24
,
27
,
22
,
41
,
18
]
.
Li–Liu–Zhang
[
20
]

proved an Erdős–Stone–Simonovits type theorem in this setting:
if
\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
, then every
F
-free graph
G
satisfies
\lambda(G)^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+o(1)\right)2m
.
Moreover, Li–Liu–Zhang
[
21
]
extended Nikiforov’s theorem to color-critical graphs, and they established some Turán-type results for almost-bipartite graphs, in which the extremal graphs are often split graphs. For integers
k\geq 1
and
m\geq\binom{k}{2}
,
we write

m-\binom{k}{2}=kq+r
with
q\geq 0
and
0\leq r\leq k-1.

The
split graph

S_{k,m}
is obtained by joining a clique
K_{k}
to an
independent set of size
q
, and adding one further vertex adjacent to
exactly
r
vertices of the clique. For complete bipartite graphs,
Li–Liu–Zhang
[
21
]
proved the following
exact result.

Theorem 1.3

(Li–Liu–Zhang
[
21
]
)
.

For each fixed
t\geq 2
and all sufficiently large
m
, every

K_{t,t}
-free
m
-edge graph
G
satisfies

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(S_{t-1,m}),

with equality if and only if
G=S_{t-1,m}
.

In many classical extremal problems for bipartite forbidden graphs,
sharp or near-sharp constructions are pseudorandom or algebraic. The
edge-spectral setting exhibits a different phenomenon: the extremal
configuration for forbidding
K_{t,t}
is the highly inhomogeneous
split graph
S_{t-1,m}
.

Supersaturation asks what happens beyond such an extremal threshold. The
first supersaturation theorem is due to Erdős and
Rademacher
[
10
,
11
]
, who showed that every

n
-vertex graph with more than
\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
edges contains
at least
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
triangles. Supersaturation has since
been extensively studied; see, for example,

[
25
,
37
,
30
,
36
,
29
]
. Spectral supersaturation was
initiated by Bollobás–Nikiforov
[
1
]
; they proved that
every graph
G
contains at least

\frac{1}{3}\lambda(G)\bigl(\lambda(G)^{2}-m\bigr)

triangles. Recently,
Fang–Lin–Zhai
[
12
]
studied
the edge-spectral supersaturation for color-critical subgraphs.
Ning–Zhai
[
34
,
35
]
proved that if

\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
, then
G
contains at least

\lfloor\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{m}-1)\rfloor
triangles and at least

\frac{1}{2000}m^{2}
copies of
C_{4}
. The
C_{4}
bound was sharpened
by Li–Liu–Zhang
[
22
]
.

Theorem 1.4

(Li–Liu–Zhang
[
22
]
)
.

Every
m
-edge graph
G
with
\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
contains at
least
\left(\frac{1}{8}-o(1)\right)m^{2}

copies of
C_{4}
. Moreover, the constant
1/8
is best possible.

The main contribution of this paper
is to develop a unified method for proving
exact edge-spectral supersaturation results for degenerate bipartite graphs with the Sidorenko property.
Our next result gives the sharp supersaturation theorem for
K_{t,t}
for every
t\geq 2

above the extremal spectral radius in Theorem
1.3
. When

t=2
, this recovers Theorem
1.4
, since

K_{2,2}=C_{4}
and
\lambda(S_{1,m})=\sqrt{m}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
t\geq 2
and
G
be an
m
-edge graph. If
\lambda(G)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
, then

\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(G)\geq\left(\frac{2^{-(t-1)^{2}}}{(t!)^{2}}-o(1)\right)m^{t}.

The constant is best possible, as witnessed by
m
-edge random graphs on

(2+o(1))\sqrt{m}
vertices.

For counting
C_{4}
, the proof of Theorem
1.4
relies crucially on an
exact spectral identity
[
23
]
for the number of
4
-cycles:
\texttt{\#}C_{4}(G)=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}^{4}+\lambda_{i}^{2})-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i}d_{i}^{2},

where
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
G
and
d_{i}
are its
degrees. However, for
K_{t,t}
with
t\geq 3
, no analogous identity is available for controlling injective copies directly from the spectrum or degrees of
G
. Instead, we need to use the spectral Sidorenko inequality
(
3
), which gives the correct homomorphism count. The new difficulty is to control degenerate homomorphisms to convert it into a lower bound for injective copies, especially
near extremal split graphs where the Perron eigenvector can be highly localized.

The same structural framework also applies beyond complete bipartite graphs. As the second counting result, we prove the following sharp asymptotic supersaturation for even cycles
C_{2t}
for every
t\geq 2
. Here the sharp construction is different from that of
K_{t,t}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
t\geq 2
and
G
be an
m
-edge graph. If
\lambda(G)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
, then

\texttt{\#}\,C_{2t}(G)\geq\left(\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}-o(1)\right)m^{t}.

The constant is best possible, as witnessed by the split graphs
S_{t,m}
.

Notice that the same spectral threshold leads to two different sharp extremal
mechanisms: balanced random graphs are sharp for
K_{t,t}
, while split
graphs are sharp for
C_{2t}
.

Proof overview.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
starts from the observation
that ordinary Sidorenko and spectral Sidorenko coincide for regular host
graphs. The key is Theorem
1.2
, which provides a
spectral reduction to regular host graphs. The construction for finding regular subgraph uses the Perron eigenvector to build a probability
distribution on ordered edges, i.e.,

p_{ij}:=\frac{a_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}}{\lambda(G)}.

This distribution has both marginals equal to
\pi_{i}=x_{i}^{2}
, which is a distribution on vertices. In the
k
-th tensor power
G^{\otimes k}
, we take vertices with empirical distribution close to
\pi
, and edges whose coordinate-wise empirical edge distribution is close to
p
. The
matching marginal condition makes the resulting subgraph regular and the entropy identity
H(p)-H(\pi)=\log\lambda(G)
controls the degree. Applying Sidorenko’s inequality to this regular subgraph, combining with multiplicativity and monotonicity, proves the spectral form.

We next outline the proof of Theorem
1.5
. The spectral
Sidorenko inequality gives the correct homomorphism lower bound for

K_{t,t}
. After subtracting non-injective homomorphisms, it gives the
sharp copy count whenever the relevant graph has
O(\sqrt{m})
vertices; see Lemma
3.1
.
The main difficulty is that graphs above the split spectral threshold may
have localized Perron eigenvectors and may be close to split

K_{t,t}
-free constructions.

We first prune the graph by deleting edges with small Perron weight
product. Such edges contribute little to the Rayleigh quotient, and a
discrete increment estimate for
\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
shows that the
strict gap above the split threshold is preserved in the resulting subgraph
H
and every remaining edge is heavy with respect to the Perron vector; see Lemma
3.6
. The pruned graph is then analyzed according to the parameter

g:=\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}e(H)^{1/4}.

If
g=O(1)
, then the heavy-edge condition forces the non-isolated part of

H
to have
O(\sqrt{e(H)})
vertices. So the non-injective error
O(n^{2t-1})
reduces to
o(m^{t})
, which is
negligible. Using spectral Sidorenko inequality gives the sharp count; see Lemma
4.2
.

The localized case
g\to\infty
requires a new structural theorem; see Theorem
4.4
. More precisely, we use
Perron-vector level sets to obtain a partition

V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D,

where
D
is independent, there are no
C
–
D
edges, every edge
is either inside
C
or incident to
A
, and

e_{H}(A,C)=o(e(H))
,
|A|=o(\sqrt{e(H)})
and

|C|\leq e(H)^{1/2+o(1)}
.
If the core
H[C]
contains a positive fraction of the edges, then a spectral cut estimate shows that
the core retains enough spectral radius, and spectral Sidorenko applies
inside
H[C]
; see Lemma
4.5
.
If the core
H[C]
is sparse, i.e.,
e(H[C])=o(e(H))
,
then almost all edges of
H
lie between

A
and
D
. The
A
–
D
incidence matrix is nearly rank one,
forcing either many aligned rows with a large common neighborhood, which
already produce many copies of
K_{t,t}
, or else almost all edges are
covered by at most
t-1
vertices of
A
; see Theorem
4.9
.
The latter alternative is
close to a
K_{t,t}
-free split graph. We rule it out by deleting the
exceptional edges, applying the edge-spectral Turán theorem for

K_{t,t}
, and using the split-graph increment estimate to contradict
the preserved spectral gap; see the proof of Lemma
4.6
.

The proof of Theorem
1.6
uses the same structural
framework, but the counting input is the simpler inequality (
1
).
This leads to the split-graph constant
(t-1)!/(2t^{t})
, whereas

K_{t,t}
is governed by the balanced random-graph construction. In conclusion, the above discussions of
K_{t,t}
and
C_{2t}
illustrate that
our method provides a unified framework for establishing edge-spectral supersaturation
results for other bipartite graphs
H
with the spectral Sidorenko property, including the bipartite graphs having a vertex complete to the other part, hypercubes and the
1
-subdivisions of cliques, upon a comparison of the number of copies of
H
in random graphs and split graphs.

2
Spectral Sidorenko inequality

2.1
Spectral Sidorenko via an operator-norm certificate

In this subsection we introduce an analytic sufficient condition for
proving spectral Sidorenko inequalities. The condition is formulated in
terms of operator norms and Riesz–Thorin interpolation.
We verify it for
K_{t,t}
, which is the main counting input in the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, write

\|\bm{x}\|_{p}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

We also write

\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|x_{i}|.
For a real

n
-by-
n
matrix
A
, define the
(p,r)
-operator norm by
\|A\|_{p\to r}:=\sup_{\bm{x}\neq 0}\frac{\|A\bm{x}\|_{r}}{\|\bm{x}\|_{p}},
,
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
.
We shall use the following standard form of the Riesz–Thorin
interpolation theorem; see, for example,
Cerda
[
2
, page 61]
and
Grafakos
[
14
, page 37]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Riesz–Thorin interpolation)
.

Let
T
be a linear operator between finite-dimensional
\ell^{p}

spaces. Suppose that

\|T\|_{p_{0}\to q_{0}}\leq M_{0}
and

\|T\|_{p_{1}\to q_{1}}\leq M_{1},

where
p_{0},q_{0},p_{1},q_{1}\in[1,\infty]
, with the convention

1/\infty=0
. If
\theta\in[0,1]
and

\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},

\frac{1}{q_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},

then

\|T\|_{p_{\theta}\to q_{\theta}}\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}.

We now define the operator-norm certificate.

Definition 2.2

(Operator-norm certificate)
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph with
v
vertices and
e
edges, where

v\leq e
. Set

s_{H}:=\frac{2e}{v}
and

s_{H}^{\prime}:=\frac{2e}{2e-v}.

Thus
1/s_{H}+1/s_{H}^{\prime}=1
. We say that
H
satisfies the

operator-norm Sidorenko certificate
if, for every graph
G
with
adjacency matrix
A=A(G)
,

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\|A\|_{s_{H}^{\prime}\to s_{H}}^{\,e}.

The certificate is designed so that interpolation converts it into the
spectral Sidorenko inequality.

Lemma 2.3

(Operator-norm certificate implies spectral Sidorenko)
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph with
v
vertices and
e
edges, where

v\leq e
. If
H
satisfies the operator-norm Sidorenko certificate,
then, for every graph
G
with
M(G)=2e(G)>0
,

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\lambda(G)^{2e-v}M(G)^{v-e}.

Proof.

Let
A=A(G)
,
M=M(G)
, and

\lambda=\lambda(G)=\|A\|_{2\to 2}
. Put

\alpha:=\frac{e}{2e-v}.

Since
v\leq e
, we have
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Moreover,

\frac{1}{2}=\alpha\frac{1}{s_{H}^{\prime}}+(1-\alpha)\frac{1}{\infty}
and

\frac{1}{2}=\alpha\frac{1}{s_{H}}+(1-\alpha)\cdot 1.

Applying Riesz–Thorin interpolation between

\|A\|_{s_{H}^{\prime}\to s_{H}}
and
\|A\|_{\infty\to 1}
, and using

\|A\|_{\infty\to 1}\leq M,

we obtain

\lambda=\|A\|_{2\to 2}\leq\|A\|_{s_{H}^{\prime}\to s_{H}}^{\alpha}M^{1-\alpha}.

Hence

\|A\|_{s_{H}^{\prime}\to s_{H}}\geq\lambda^{1/\alpha}M^{-(1-\alpha)/\alpha}.

Raising both sides to the
e
-th power gives

\|A\|_{s_{H}^{\prime}\to s_{H}}^{e}\geq\lambda^{e/\alpha}M^{-e(1-\alpha)/\alpha}=\lambda^{2e-v}M^{v-e}.

The operator-norm certificate now gives the desired inequality.
∎

More generally, if
W=(w_{ij})
is a nonnegative symmetric matrix indexed by a finite set, we write

\mathrm{hom}(H,W):=\sum_{\phi:V(H)\to[n]}\prod_{uv\in E(H)}w_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}

for the corresponding weighted homomorphism count.

We next verify the certificate for
K_{t,t}
, in which case

s_{K_{t,t}}=t
and
s_{K_{t,t}}^{\prime}=\frac{t}{t-1}.

In fact, the proof below works verbatim for every nonnegative
symmetric matrix
A
.

Lemma 2.4

(Operator-norm certificate for
K_{t,t}
)
.

For every
t\geq 2
and every graph
G
with adjacency matrix
A
,

\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)\geq\|A\|_{q\to t}^{t^{2}},\qquad q:=\frac{t}{t-1}.

Consequently, if
M(G)>0
, then

\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)\geq\frac{\lambda(G)^{2t(t-1)}}{M(G)^{t(t-2)}}.

Proof.

Let
A=(a_{ij})
be the adjacency matrix of
G
. Since
A
has
nonnegative entries, the norm
\|A\|_{q\to t}
may be computed using
nonnegative vectors, by replacing
\bm{x}
with
|\bm{x}|
.
Let
\bm{x}\geq 0
. For

J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{t})\in[n]^{t}
, write

S_{J}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{b=1}^{t}a_{ij_{b}}.

Then

\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)=\sum_{J\in[n]^{t}}S_{J}^{t}.

Expanding
\|A\bm{x}\|_{t}^{t}
and applying Hölder’s inequality on

[n]^{t}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|A\bm{x}\|_{t}^{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}x_{j}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{t}=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{b=1}^{t}x_{j_{b}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{b=1}^{t}a_{ij_{b}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{J\in[n]^{t}}S_{J}^{t}\right)^{1/t}\left(\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{t}=1}^{n}\prod_{b=1}^{t}x_{j_{b}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)^{1/t}\|\bm{x}\|_{q}^{t}.

Taking
t
-th roots gives

\|A\bm{x}\|_{t}\leq\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)^{1/t^{2}}\|\bm{x}\|_{q}.

Taking the supremum over all nonzero
\bm{x}\geq 0
, and using again
that
A
is nonnegative, yields the desired inequality

\|A\|_{q\to t}\leq\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)^{1/t^{2}}.

∎

Remark 1

(Other examples and scope of the certificate)
.

Even cycles also satisfy the certificate directly. For
H=C_{2t}
,
we have
v=e=2t
, so
s_{H}=s_{H}^{\prime}=2
. The certificate becomes exactly (
1
):

\mathrm{hom}(C_{2t},G)=\operatorname{tr}A(G)^{2t}\geq\lambda(G)^{2t}=\|A(G)\|_{2\to 2}^{2t}.
The operator-norm certificate is a sufficient condition, not an
equivalent reformulation of Sidorenko’s conjecture. Its usefulness is
that it produces the exact type of operator-norm lower bound needed in
spectral counting problems.

Proposition 2.5

(Subdivision closure)
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph with
v
vertices and
e
edges, where

v\leq e
. Set
s:=\frac{2e}{v}
and

s^{\prime}:=\frac{2e}{2e-v}.

Assume that
H
satisfies the matrix version of the operator-norm
certificate: for every nonnegative symmetric matrix
W
,

\mathrm{hom}(H,W)\geq\|W\|_{s^{\prime}\to s}^{\,e}.

Let
\operatorname{sub}(H)
be the graph obtained by subdividing every
edge of
H
once. Then
\operatorname{sub}(H)
satisfies the
operator-norm certificate. More precisely, for every graph
G
with
adjacency matrix
A=A(G)
,

\mathrm{hom}(\operatorname{sub}(H),G)\geq\|A\|_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma}^{\,2e},

where
\sigma=\frac{4e}{v+e}
and

\sigma^{\prime}=\frac{4e}{3e-v}.

Proof.

Since each subdivided edge corresponds to a length-two walk in
G
,

\mathrm{hom}(\operatorname{sub}(H),G)=\mathrm{hom}(H,A^{2}).

By the matrix certificate for
H
,

\mathrm{hom}(\operatorname{sub}(H),G)\geq\|A^{2}\|_{s^{\prime}\to s}^{\,e}.

Let
N:=\|A^{2}\|_{s^{\prime}\to s}
. For every vector
\bm{x}
,

\|A\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2}=\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}A^{2}\bm{x}\leq\|A^{2}\bm{x}\|_{s}\|\bm{x}\|_{s^{\prime}}\leq N\|\bm{x}\|_{s^{\prime}}^{2}.

Thus
\|A\|_{s^{\prime}\to 2}\leq N^{1/2}.

By duality and symmetry of
A
,

\|A\|_{2\to s}\leq N^{1/2}.

Interpolating these two estimates with parameter
1/2
, we get
\|A\|_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma}\leq N^{1/2},

since

\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{s}.

Therefore

\|A^{2}\|_{s^{\prime}\to s}\geq\|A\|_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma}^{2}.

Combining the inequalities gives

\mathrm{hom}(\operatorname{sub}(H),G)\geq\|A\|_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma}^{2e}.

Since
\operatorname{sub}(H)
has
v+e
vertices and
2e
edges, its certificate exponents are precisely

\frac{2\cdot 2e}{v+e}=\sigma
and

\frac{2\cdot 2e}{2\cdot 2e-(v+e)}=\sigma^{\prime}.

Thus this is exactly the required certificate.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

The
1
-subdivision of
K_{a,b}
satisfies the operator-norm
Sidorenko certificate for all
a,b\geq 2
. In particular, for every
t\geq 2
,

\mathrm{hom}(\operatorname{sub}(K_{t,t}),G)\geq\|A(G)\|_{\frac{4t}{3t-2}\to\frac{4t}{t+2}}^{2t^{2}}.

Proof.

The matrix version of the certificate for
K_{a,b}
follows from the
same Hölder argument used above for
K_{t,t}
. Applying
Proposition
2.5
gives the first
claim. For
a=b=t
, the
1
-subdivision has
2t+t^{2}
vertices and

2t^{2}
edges, so its certificate exponents are

\sigma=\frac{4t^{2}}{2t+t^{2}}=\frac{4t}{t+2}
and
\sigma^{\prime}=\frac{4t^{2}}{4t^{2}-(2t+t^{2})}=\frac{4t}{3t-2}.

∎

Remark 2

(Comparison with domination)
.

Conlon–Lee proved that
\operatorname{sub}(K_{t,t})
is dominating.
Their proof uses a reflection/relocation mechanism, whereas
Proposition
2.5
is a direct
operator-norm closure argument. The conclusions are different:
domination gives a family of homomorphism inequalities, while the
operator-norm certificate is tailored to spectral Sidorenko inequalities.
The same subdivision-closure argument applies to any graph satisfying the
matrix version of the certificate. In particular, it applies to weakly norming incidence graphs for which the matrix certificate is available.

2.2
Equivalence of Sidorenko and spectral Sidorenko

We now prove Theorem
1.2
and use it to derive Theorem
1.1
. The proof uses a tensor-power
argument, a useful tool in extremal combinatorics; see, for example,

[
6
,
13
,
28
,
40
]
. The
tensor product

G\otimes H
of two graphs
G
and
H

has vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
, where
(u_{1},v_{1})
is adjacent to

(u_{2},v_{2})
if and only if
u_{1}u_{2}\in E(G)
and
v_{1}v_{2}\in E(H)
.
The
k
-th tensor power of
G
, denoted by
G^{\otimes k}
, is
defined recursively by
G^{\otimes 1}=G
and

G^{\otimes k}=G^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes G
. Equivalently,

G^{\otimes k}
has vertex set
V(G)^{k}
, and two
k
-tuples

\bm{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
and
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
are adjacent if
and only if
u_{i}v_{i}\in E(G)
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
G_{0}
be a connected component of
G
with vertex set
V_{0}

and spectral radius
\lambda(G_{0})=\lambda(G)=\lambda
. Let

A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in V_{0}}
be the adjacency matrix of
G_{0}
, and let

\bm{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in V_{0}}
be a positive Perron eigenvector. We scale

\bm{x}
so that
\sum_{i\in V_{0}}x_{i}^{2}=1
.

For ordered pairs
(i,j)\in V_{0}^{2}
, we define

p_{ij}:=\frac{a_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}}{\lambda}.

Then
p_{ij}=0
if and only if
ij\notin E(G_{0})
. Moreover, we have
\sum_{i,j\in V_{0}}p_{ij}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}A\bm{x}=1
.

For each
i\in V_{0}
, we write

\pi_{i}:=x_{i}^{2}.

Then
\sum_{i\in V_{0}}\pi_{i}=1
. Moreover, we have the following relation:

\sum_{j}p_{ij}=\frac{x_{i}}{\lambda}\sum_{j}a_{ij}x_{j}=x_{i}^{2}=\pi_{i}.

(4)

In the above, we defined two probability distributions
p=(p_{ij})_{i,j\in V_{0}}
and
\pi=(\pi_{i})_{i\in V_{0}}
.
We next show an interesting connection between them and the spectral radius.
Let

H(\alpha):=-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\log\alpha_{i}

be the entropy of a distribution
\alpha
, with the convention
0\log 0=0
.
We claim that

H(p)-H(\pi)=\log\lambda.

(5)

Indeed, by definition, we immediately get

\displaystyle H(p)-H(\pi)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}\log\left(\frac{p_{ij}}{\pi_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j:p_{ij}>0}p_{ij}\log\left(\frac{x_{j}}{\lambda x_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\lambda+\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}\log x_{i}-\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}\log x_{j}

\displaystyle=\log\lambda+\sum_{i}\pi_{i}\log x_{i}-\sum_{j}\pi_{j}\log x_{j}

\displaystyle=\log\lambda.

We now construct a subgraph of
G_{0}^{\otimes k}
.
We do this along even values of
k
. Write
k=2s
. Since

p_{ij}=p_{ji}
, define a probability distribution on the unordered edges of
G_{0}
by

q_{ij}:=2p_{ij}
for each
ij\in E(G_{0}).

For each sufficiently large integer
s
, choose nonnegative integers

m_{ij}^{(s)}
, one for each unordered edge
ij\in E(G_{0})
, such that
m_{ij}^{(s)}=sq_{ij}+O_{G}(1)
and

\sum_{ij\in E(G_{0})}m_{ij}^{(s)}=s
. This can be done by rounding the numbers
sq_{ij}
and adjusting
finitely many entries.

Define a symmetric integer matrix

N^{(k)}=(N_{ij}^{(k)})_{i,j\in V_{0}}
by

N_{ij}^{(k)}=N_{ji}^{(k)}:=m_{ij}^{(k)}\quad\text{if }ij\in E(G_{0});\qquad N_{ij}^{(k)}:=0\quad\text{otherwise}.

Then
\sum_{i,j}N_{ij}^{(k)}=k
.
For all
i,j\in V_{0}
, we have
{N_{ij}^{(k)}}/{k}\to p_{ij}
as
k\to\infty
.

For each
i\in V_{0}
, we denote

n_{i}^{(k)}:=\sum_{j}N_{ij}^{(k)}.

Then
\sum_{i\in V_{0}}n_{i}^{(k)}=k
, and for each
i\in V_{0}
, using (
4
) yields

{n_{i}^{(k)}}/{k}\to\pi_{i}
as
k\to\infty
.

Let
T_{k}
be the set of all
k
-tuples
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in V_{0}^{k}
in which the vertex
i
occurs exactly
n_{i}^{(k)}
times. Define a
graph
F_{k}
with vertex set
T_{k}
as follows. Two vertices

\bm{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
and
\bm{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})
are adjacent in

F_{k}
if, for every ordered pair
(i,j)
, the number of coordinates

r\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that

(a_{r},b_{r})=(i,j)

is exactly
N_{ij}^{(k)}
.

First, we see that
F_{k}\subseteq G_{0}^{\otimes k}
. Indeed, if

\bm{a}\bm{b}\in E(F_{k})
, then every coordinate pair
(a_{r},b_{r})
has

N_{a_{r}b_{r}}^{(k)}>0
, and hence
a_{r}b_{r}\in E(G_{0})
. Therefore

\bm{a}
and
\bm{b}
are adjacent in
G_{0}^{\otimes k}
.

Second,
F_{k}
is regular. Fix
\bm{a}\in T_{k}
. For each
i\in V_{0}
,
there are
n_{i}^{(k)}
coordinates
r
with
a_{r}=i
. To choose a
neighbor
\bm{b}
, we must partition these
n_{i}^{(k)}
coordinates
into classes of sizes
N_{ij}^{(k)}
, one class for each
j\in V_{0}
,
and assign
b_{r}=j
on the class corresponding to
j
. Thus the
number of choices is

d_{k}=\prod_{i}\frac{n_{i}^{(k)}!}{\prod_{j}N_{ij}^{(k)}!}.

Since
N^{(k)}
is symmetric, the number of coordinates in which

\bm{b}
is equal to
j
is

\sum_{i}N_{ij}^{(k)}=\sum_{i}N_{ji}^{(k)}=n_{j}^{(k)}.

Hence
\bm{b}\in T_{k}
, and the above number is independent of

\bm{a}
. Therefore,
F_{k}
is
d_{k}
-regular.

Finally, we estimate
d_{k}
. Applying Stirling’s
formula to the expression for
d_{k}
, and using that
G
is fixed, we
obtain

\displaystyle\log d_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\log(n_{i}^{(k)}!)-\sum_{i,j}\log(N_{ij}^{(k)}!)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i}n_{i}^{(k)}\log n_{i}^{(k)}-\sum_{i,j}N_{ij}^{(k)}\log N_{ij}^{(k)}-C\log k

\displaystyle\geq k\bigl(-H(\pi)+H(p)\bigr)-C\log k,

where
C=C(G)>0
is a constant depending only on
G
.

Applying (
5
) gives
\log d_{k}\geq k\log\lambda-C\log k
,
and therefore
d_{k}\geq\lambda^{k}k^{-C}.

The upper bound on
d_{k}
follows from monotonicity of the spectral
radius. Since
F_{k}
is
d_{k}
-regular, we have
d_{k}=\lambda(F_{k})\leq\lambda(G_{0}^{\otimes k})=\lambda(G_{0})^{k}=\lambda^{k}
.
Since
F_{k}\subseteq G_{0}^{\otimes k}\subseteq G^{\otimes k}
, we get
M(F_{k})\leq M(G^{\otimes k})=M(G)^{k}
immediately.
This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove the equivalence theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assume throughout that
v\leq e
, and consider an arbitrary host graph
G
with

M(G)>0
. For notational convenience, we denote
M:=M(G)
and
\lambda:=\lambda(G).

First, we show that (ii) and (iii) implies (i). Assume that
H
satisfies the edge-spectral Sidorenko inequality.
The Rayleigh quotient implies
\lambda\geq\frac{M}{|G|}.

Since
v\leq e
, we have
2e-v\geq 0
. Hence

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\lambda^{2e-v}M^{v-e}\geq\left(\frac{M}{|G|}\right)^{2e-v}M^{v-e}=M^{e}|G|^{v-2e}.

Thus
H
satisfies Sidorenko’s inequality. Similarly, the vertex-spectral version implies

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\lambda^{e}|G|^{v-e}\geq\left(\frac{M}{|G|}\right)^{e}|G|^{v-e}=M^{e}|G|^{v-2e}.

Conversely, assume that
H
satisfies Sidorenko’s inequality in (i). Next, we show that
H
satisfies the spectral versions in (ii) and (iii).
Let
F_{k}\subseteq G^{\otimes k}
be the regular subgraphs supplied by
Theorem
1.2
, along an infinite sequence of
integers
k
. Let
N_{k}:=|F_{k}|
,
M_{k}:=M(F_{k})
,
and let
d_{k}
be the regular degree of
F_{k}
. Since
F_{k}
is
regular, we have
M_{k}=N_{k}d_{k}.

The homomorphism count is multiplicative under tensor products, and
monotone under passing to subgraphs. Therefore

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)^{k}=\mathrm{hom}(H,G^{\otimes k})\geq\mathrm{hom}(H,F_{k}).

Applying Sidorenko’s inequality to
H
with host graph
F_{k}
, we get

\mathrm{hom}(H,F_{k})\geq M_{k}^{e}N_{k}^{v-2e}=M_{k}^{e}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{d_{k}}\right)^{v-2e}=d_{k}^{2e-v}M_{k}^{v-e}.

(6)

Since
v\leq e
, we have
v-e\leq 0
. Theorem
1.2

gives
M_{k}\leq M^{k}
, and therefore
M_{k}^{v-e}\geq M^{k(v-e)}
.
It follows that

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)^{k}\geq d_{k}^{2e-v}M^{k(v-e)}.

Using
d_{k}\geq\lambda^{k}k^{-C}
, we obtain
\mathrm{hom}(H,G)^{k}\geq\left(\lambda^{k}k^{-C}\right)^{2e-v}M^{k(v-e)}
.
Taking
k
-th roots gives

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\lambda^{2e-v}k^{-C(2e-v)/k}M^{v-e}.

Letting
k\to\infty
, we get the edge-spectral Sidorenko inequality
\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\lambda^{2e-v}M^{v-e}
.

The vertex-spectral version is similarly obtained by substituting
M_{k}=N_{k}d_{k}
into (
6
). Indeed,

\mathrm{hom}(H,F_{k})\geq M_{k}^{e}N_{k}^{v-2e}=(N_{k}d_{k})^{e}N_{k}^{v-2e}=N_{k}^{v-e}d_{k}^{e}.

Since
v-e\leq 0
,
N_{k}\leq|G|^{k}
and
d_{k}\geq\lambda^{k}k^{-C}
by Theorem
1.2
, we get
\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq|G|^{v-e}\lambda^{e}k^{-Ce/k}
. Taking
k\to\infty
, we obtain the vertex-spectral version
\mathrm{hom}(H,G)\geq\lambda^{e}|G|^{v-e}
, as desired.
∎

1.5

For
U\subseteq V(G)
, we write
G[U]
for the subgraph of
G

induced by
U
. For disjoint sets
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
, we write

E_{G}(X,Y)
for the set of edges between
X
and

, and set

e_{G}(X,Y):=|E_{G}(X,Y)|
. For two disjoint vertex sets
A,B
, we

for the bipartite subgraph of

A

. For graphs

G\vee H

3.1
Bounding
\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}
via spectral radius

The spectral Sidorenko inequality (
3
) gives a lower bound on

\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)
. By subtracting the non-injective homomorphisms,
we obtain the following lower bound for genuine copies of
K_{t,t}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
t\geq 2
and
G
be an
n
-vertex
m
-edge graph with spectral
radius
\lambda
. Then

\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(G)\geq B_{t}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{m}\right)^{t(t-1)}m^{t}-\frac{\binom{2t}{2}}{2(t!)^{2}}\,n^{2t-1},\qquad\text{where}\quad B_{t}:=\frac{2^{-(t-1)^{2}}}{(t!)^{2}}.

Proof.

By (
3
), we get
\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)\geq\frac{\lambda^{2t(t-1)}}{(2m)^{t(t-2)}}
. The number of non-injective maps
V(K_{t,t})\to V(G)
is at most

\binom{2t}{2}n^{2t-1}
, since one may first choose a pair of vertices
of
K_{t,t}
that are identified. Hence

\mathrm{inj}(K_{t,t},G)\geq\mathrm{hom}(K_{t,t},G)-\binom{2t}{2}n^{2t-1}.

Since
|\mathrm{Aut}(K_{t,t})|=2(t!)^{2}
, we have

\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(G)=\frac{\mathrm{inj}(K_{t,t},G)}{2(t!)^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2(t!)^{2}}\cdot\frac{\lambda^{2t(t-1)}}{(2m)^{t(t-2)}}-\frac{\binom{2t}{2}}{2(t!)^{2}}\,n^{2t-1}.

The main term is

\frac{1}{2(t!)^{2}}\cdot\frac{\lambda^{2t(t-1)}}{(2m)^{t(t-2)}}=B_{t}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{m}\right)^{t(t-1)}m^{t},

as desired.
∎

3.2
Matrix inequalities for graphs

We use standard spectral graph conventions; see, for example,
Chung
[
5
]
and
Horn–Johnson
[
16
]
. For a graph
G
,
let
A(G)
be its adjacency matrix and let
\lambda(G)
denote its
adjacency spectral radius. For a real matrix
A
, let
\|A\|
denote
the operator norm,
\|A\|=\max_{\bm{x}\neq 0}\frac{\|A\bm{x}\|_{2}}{\|\bm{x}\|_{2}}.

We denote the Frobenius norm by
\|A\|_{\mathrm{F}}:=(\operatorname{tr}(A^{\mathsf{T}}A))^{1/2}.

Let
\sigma_{1}(A)\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{r}(A)

be the singular values of
A
, where
r:=\operatorname{rank}(A)
.
Then
\|A\|=\sigma_{1}(A),
and
\|A\|_{\mathrm{F}}=(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}^{2}(A))^{1/2}.

Since
A(G)
is symmetric and nonnegative,
\lambda(G)=\|A(G)\|
.
The Perron–Frobenius theorem implies that
\lambda(G)
has an
eigenvector with nonnegative entries; if
G
is connected, then such an
eigenvector has strictly positive entries. Throughout the paper, a

unit Perron eigenvector
of
G
means a vector

\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{V(G)}
satisfying

\bm{x}\geq 0,

\|\bm{x}\|_{2}=1,
and
A(G)\bm{x}=\lambda(G)\bm{x}.
The Rayleigh quotient gives

\lambda(G)=\max_{\|\bm{y}\|_{2}=1}\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}A(G)\bm{y}.

In particular, if
H\subseteq G
, then
\lambda(H)\leq\lambda(G)
.
Moreover, if
G
has
n
vertices and
m
edges, then

\frac{2m}{n}\leq\lambda(G)\leq\|A(G)\|_{\mathrm{F}}=\sqrt{2m}.

Let
G
be a graph with unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}
. For

U\subseteq V(G)
, we write

\mu(U):=\sum_{u\in U}x_{u}^{2}

and call it the
Perron mass
of
U
. We refer to

[
26
,
4
]
for recent results on Perron mass when

U
is an independent set.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H
be a graph and let
V(H)=U\sqcup W
be a partition.
Let
M\in\{0,1\}^{U\times W}
be the
U
–
W
incidence matrix with
m_{UW}:=e_{H}(U,W)=\|M\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}
and

\rho:=\|M\|
. Set

\lambda:=\lambda(H),\quad\lambda_{U}:=\lambda(H[U]),\quad\lambda_{W}:=\lambda(H[W]).

Then the following hold.

(a)

\lambda\leq\max\{\lambda_{U},\lambda_{W}\}+\rho\leq\max\{\lambda_{U},\lambda_{W}\}+\sqrt{m_{UW}}.

(b)

(\lambda-\lambda_{U})(\lambda-\lambda_{W})\leq\rho^{2}\leq m_{UW}.

(c)

Let
\bm{x}
be a unit Perron eigenvector of
H
, and let

\mu(U)=\sum_{u\in U}x_{u}^{2}
be the Perron mass of
U
with respect
to
\bm{x}
. If
(\lambda-\lambda_{U})^{2}+\rho^{2}>0,

then

\mu(U)\leq\frac{\rho^{2}}{(\lambda-\lambda_{U})^{2}+\rho^{2}}\leq\frac{m_{UW}}{(\lambda-\lambda_{U})^{2}+m_{UW}}.

Proof.

Order the vertices so that the vertices of
U
come first and the
vertices of
W
come last. Then

A(H)=\begin{pmatrix}A(H[U])&M\\
M^{\mathsf{T}}&A(H[W])\end{pmatrix}.

(a).
We write
A(H)=D+C
, where
D
is block diagonal with diagonal blocks

A(H[U])
and
A(H[W])
, and
C
has off-diagonal blocks
M
and

M^{\mathsf{T}}
. Then

\|D\|=\max\{\lambda_{U},\lambda_{W}\}.

Also,

C^{\mathsf{T}}C=\begin{pmatrix}MM^{\mathsf{T}}&0\\
0&M^{\mathsf{T}}M\end{pmatrix},

and hence
\|C\|=\|M\|=\rho
. By the triangle inequality for the
operator norm,

\lambda=\|A(H)\|\leq\|D\|+\|C\|=\max\{\lambda_{U},\lambda_{W}\}+\rho.

Finally,
\rho\leq\|M\|_{\mathrm{F}}=\sqrt{m_{UW}}
, proving (a).

(b).
By monotonicity, we have
\lambda_{U},\lambda_{W}\leq\lambda
. If

\lambda=\lambda_{U}
or
\lambda=\lambda_{W}
, then the desired
inequality is immediate. Thus we may assume that
\lambda>\lambda_{U}
and
\lambda>\lambda_{W}.

Let
\bm{x}=\binom{\bm{u}}{\bm{v}}

be a unit Perron eigenvector of
H
, where

\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{U}
and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{W}
. Under the strict
inequalities above, both
\bm{u}
and
\bm{v}
are nonzero. From

A(H)\bm{x}=\lambda\bm{x}
, we have

(\lambda I-A(H[U]))\bm{u}=M\bm{v}.

Therefore
\bm{u}^{\mathsf{T}}(\lambda I-A(H[U]))\bm{u}=\bm{u}^{\mathsf{T}}M\bm{v}.

Since
\bm{u}^{\mathsf{T}}A(H[U])\bm{u}\leq\lambda_{U}\|\bm{u}\|_{2}^{2},

we obtain

(\lambda-\lambda_{U})\|\bm{u}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\bm{u}^{\mathsf{T}}M\bm{v}\leq\|\bm{u}\|_{2}\,\|M\bm{v}\|_{2}\leq\rho\|\bm{u}\|_{2}\|\bm{v}\|_{2}.

Dividing by
\|\bm{u}\|_{2}
gives

(\lambda-\lambda_{U})\|\bm{u}\|_{2}\leq\rho\|\bm{v}\|_{2}.

(7)

Similarly, from

(\lambda I-A(H[W]))\bm{v}=M^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{u},

we get

(\lambda-\lambda_{W})\|\bm{v}\|_{2}\leq\rho\|\bm{u}\|_{2}.

(8)

Multiplying (
7
) and (
8
) yields
(\lambda-\lambda_{U})(\lambda-\lambda_{W})\leq\rho^{2}.

The second inequality follows from
\rho^{2}=\|M\|^{2}\leq\|M\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=m_{UW}
.
This proves (b).

(c).
If
\lambda=\lambda_{U}
, then the hypothesis

(\lambda-\lambda_{U})^{2}+\rho^{2}>0
forces
\rho>0
, and the first
right-hand side is equal to
1
. Hence the desired inequality

\mu(U)\leq 1
is trivial.

We may therefore assume that
\lambda>\lambda_{U}
. With the notation
from the proof of part (b), squaring (
7
)
and using
\|\bm{u}\|_{2}^{2}=\mu(U)
and
\|\bm{v}\|_{2}^{2}=1-\mu(U),

gives
(\lambda-\lambda_{U})^{2}\mu(U)\leq\rho^{2}(1-\mu(U)).
Rearranging,

\mu(U)\leq\frac{\rho^{2}}{(\lambda-\lambda_{U})^{2}+\rho^{2}}.

Since the function
z\mapsto\frac{z}{(\lambda-\lambda_{U})^{2}+z}
is increasing for
z\geq 0
, and since
\rho^{2}\leq m_{UW}
, we also get

\frac{\rho^{2}}{(\lambda-\lambda_{U})^{2}+\rho^{2}}\leq\frac{m_{UW}}{(\lambda-\lambda_{U})^{2}+m_{UW}}.

This proves (c).
∎

3.3
Increment on spectral radius of split graph

We first give the asymptotic of
\lambda(S_{k,m})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
k\geq 1
and
m\geq\binom{k}{2}
. In the divisible case
m-\binom{k}{2}=kq
, we have

\lambda(S_{k,m})=\frac{k-1+\sqrt{4m-k^{2}+1}}{2}.

(9)

Moreover, in the general case, we have

\lambda(S_{k,m})=\sqrt{m}+\frac{k-1}{2}+O_{k}(m^{-1/2}).

(10)

Proof.

We write
m-\binom{k}{2}=kq+r
with integers
q\geq 0
and
0\leq r\leq k-1
.
If
r=0
, i.e., in the divisible case, then
S_{k,m}=K_{k}\vee qK_{1}
.
If
q=0
, then
S_{k,m}=K_{k}
, and the formula is immediate. Thus assume
q\geq 1
.
Let
V(S_{k,m}):=X\cup Y
be a vertex partition, where
X
consists of the vertices of the clique
K_{k}
, and
Y
consists of the vertices of the independent set
qK_{1}
.
Note that the Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V(S_{k,m})}
has the same coordinates on the vertices of
X
, and also on the vertices of
Y
. We may assume that
x_{v}=a
for all
v\in X
, and
x_{u}=b
for all
u\in Y
.
Then
\lambda a=(k-1)a+qb
and
\lambda b=ka
. Thus,
\lambda(S_{k,m})
is the largest eigenvalue of

\begin{pmatrix}k-1&q\\
k&0\end{pmatrix}.

So
\lambda(S_{k,m})
is the largest positive root of
x^{2}-(k-1)x-kq=0
,
which yields

\lambda(S_{k,m})=\frac{k-1+\sqrt{(k-1)^{2}+4kq}}{2}.

This is the exact formula in (
9
) since
m=\binom{k}{2}+kq
.

Since
\sqrt{4m-k^{2}+1}=2\sqrt{m}+O_{k}(m^{-1/2})
, (
9
) implies (
10
) in the divisible case.
In the general case where
1\leq r\leq k-1
, set
m_{-}:=m-r
and
m_{+}:=m+k-r
. Then
S_{k,m_{-}}\subseteq S_{k,m}\subseteq S_{k,m_{+}}
.
The monotonicity of the spectral radius implies
\lambda(S_{k,m_{-}})\leq\lambda(S_{k,m})\leq\lambda(S_{k,m_{+}}).

Note that the split graphs
S_{k,m_{-}}
and
S_{k,m_{+}}
are in the divisible case. Thus, applying (
9
) twice, and using
\sqrt{m_{\pm}}=\sqrt{m}+O_{k}(m^{-1/2})
,
we obtain the desired bound in (
10
).
∎

The following discrete increment estimate on
\lambda(S_{k,m})
will be used later.

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix
k\geq 1
and
m\geq\binom{k}{2}+1
.
Then

\lambda(S_{k,m})-\lambda(S_{k,m-1})\geq\frac{1}{2k(\sqrt{m}+k)}.

Consequently, for every
d\in\{0,1,\dots,m-\binom{k}{2}\}
, we have

\lambda(S_{k,m-d})\leq\lambda(S_{k,m})-\frac{d}{2k(\sqrt{m}+k)}.

Proof.

We first handle the case
k=1
. Then
S_{1,m}
is the star with
m
edges, so
\lambda(S_{1,m})=\sqrt{m}
. Hence

\lambda(S_{1,m})-\lambda(S_{1,m-1})=\sqrt{m}-\sqrt{m-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}+\sqrt{m-1}}\geq\frac{1}{2(\sqrt{m}+1)}.

The corresponding
d
-step bound follows by summing this one-step estimate. Thus we may assume
k\geq 2
.

Let
F:=S_{k,m-1}
and
F^{+}:=S_{k,m}
, and we write
\lambda_{0}:=\lambda(F)
.
Let
\bm{x}
be a unit Perron eigenvector of
F
.
Let
C\subseteq V(F)
be the
k
-clique and
I:=V(F)\setminus C
, which is independent. We write
\bm{x}={\bm{x}_{C}\choose\bm{x}_{I}}
.

We first show that
\|\bm{x}_{C}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Indeed, in the vertex order
C,I
, we have

A(F)=\begin{pmatrix}J_{k}-I_{k}&B\\
B^{\mathsf{T}}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
B
is the
C\times I
incidence matrix.
From
B^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{x}_{C}=\lambda_{0}\bm{x}_{I}
, we get
\bm{x}_{C}^{\mathsf{T}}B\bm{x}_{I}=\lambda_{0}\|\bm{x}_{I}\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus

\lambda_{0}=\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}A(F)\bm{x}=\bm{x}_{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(J_{k}-I_{k})\bm{x}_{C}+2\bm{x}_{C}^{\mathsf{T}}B\bm{x}_{I}=\bm{x}_{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(J_{k}-I_{k})\bm{x}_{C}+2\lambda_{0}\|\bm{x}_{I}\|_{2}^{2}.

Since
\bm{x}_{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(J_{k}-I_{k})\bm{x}_{C}\geq 0
, we have
\|\bm{x}_{I}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
and hence
\|\bm{x}_{C}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Let
a:=\max_{v\in C}\{x_{v}\}
.
Then

a^{2}\ \geq\ \frac{\|\bm{x}_{C}\|_{2}^{2}}{k}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2k}.

(11)

We also use the crude bound

\lambda_{0}+1\ \leq\ \sqrt{m}+k.

(12)

To see this, we write
A(F)
in the same block form above, and let
{\bm{u}\choose\bm{v}}
be a unit vector with
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}
and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
.
Denote
\|\bm{u}\|_{2}=\alpha
and
\|\bm{v}\|_{2}=\beta
, we have
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=1
.
Using the Rayleigh quotient, we get

\bm{u}^{\mathsf{T}}(J_{k}-I_{k})\bm{u}\leq(k-1)\|\bm{u}\|_{2}^{2}.

Using Cauchy–Schwarz gives

(\bm{u}^{\mathsf{T}},\bm{v}^{\mathsf{T}})A(F)\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}\\
\bm{v}\end{pmatrix}\leq(k-1)\alpha^{2}+2\|B\|\alpha\beta.

Since
\|B\|^{2}\leq\|B\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=e_{F}(C,I)=m-1-\binom{k}{2}\leq m
, maximizing over
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=1
gives

\lambda_{0}\ \leq\ \frac{k-1+\sqrt{(k-1)^{2}+4m}}{2}\ \leq\ \sqrt{m}+k-1,

which implies (
12
).

We write
m-\binom{k}{2}=kq+r
with
0\leq r\leq k-1
.
If
r=1
, then
F=K_{k}\vee qK_{1}
and
F^{+}
is obtained from
F
by adding a new vertex
w
adjacent to one clique vertex
u
.
By symmetry of
F
, all clique vertices have the same Perron coordinate, so
x_{u}=a
.
For
\alpha\geq 0
, consider the vector
\bm{y}:={\bm{x}\choose\alpha}
on
V(F^{+})
.
Then
\|\bm{y}\|_{2}^{2}=1+\alpha^{2}
and

\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}A(F^{+})\bm{y}=\lambda_{0}+2a\alpha.

Applying the Rayleigh quotient,

\lambda(F^{+})\ \geq\ \max_{\alpha\geq 0}\frac{\lambda_{0}+2a\alpha}{1+\alpha^{2}}=\frac{\lambda_{0}+\sqrt{\lambda_{0}^{2}+4a^{2}}}{2}.

Thus

\lambda(F^{+})-\lambda_{0}\geq\frac{2a^{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{0}^{2}+4a^{2}}+\lambda_{0}}\geq\frac{a^{2}}{\lambda_{0}+1},

where the last inequality holds because
\sqrt{\lambda_{0}^{2}+4a^{2}}\leq\lambda_{0}+2
.
Using (
11
) and (
12
) gives the desired bound.

If
r\neq 1
, then
F^{+}
is obtained from
F
by adding one edge
wv
, where
w
is a vertex of the independent set of
F

and
v
is a vertex of the clique
K_{k}
.
Let
s:=\deg_{F}(w)\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
.
Recall that
a=\max_{v\in C}x_{v}
.
By symmetry, we may assume that
\bm{x}
takes value
a
on the
s
neighbors of
w
, and value
b
on the remaining vertices of
K_{k}
.
Write
d:=x_{w}
.
The eigenvector equation at
w
gives

\lambda_{0}d=sa,\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad d=\frac{sa}{\lambda_{0}}.

In addition, comparing the eigenvector equations at a neighbor and a non-neighbor of
w
gives

\lambda_{0}a-\lambda_{0}b=b-a+d,

so
(\lambda_{0}+1)(a-b)=d.

Thus

b=a-\frac{d}{\lambda_{0}+1}=a\left(1-\frac{s}{\lambda_{0}(\lambda_{0}+1)}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)a\geq\frac{a}{2},

where we used
\lambda_{0}\geq k-1\geq s
.
Therefore
bd\geq\frac{sa^{2}}{2\lambda_{0}}
. By the Rayleigh quotient,

\lambda(F^{+})-\lambda_{0}\geq 2bd\geq\frac{sa^{2}}{\lambda_{0}}>\frac{a^{2}}{\lambda_{0}+1}.

Combining (
11
) and (
12
) gives the desired bound.

The bound for
\lambda(S_{k,m-d})
follows by summing the one-step estimate for
i=m-d+1,\dots,m
and using
\sqrt{i}+k\leq\sqrt{m}+k
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.4
Heavy-edge subgraph with the split gap

We employ an edge-deletion technique that removes edges whose endpoints have small product of Perron weights.
This technique has its roots in Nikiforov
[
32
]
,
and was later developed by Nikiforov
[
33
]
to solve a spectral problem related to books; it was subsequently applied by Ning–Zhai
[
35
]
to study spectral supersaturation for
4
-cycles.
We start with the following definition.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
H
be an
m
-edge graph and let
\bm{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V(H)}
be a unit Perron eigenvector of
H
.
For
\eta>0
, we say that an edge
uv\in E(H)
is
\eta
-heavy
if

x_{u}x_{v}\geq\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{m}}.

We say that
H
satisfies the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition
if every edge of
H
is
\eta
-heavy.

Lemma 3.6
.

Fix
t\geq 2
and
\eta:=1/(16t)
.
For all sufficiently large
m
, if
G
is an
m
-edge graph with
\lambda(G)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
, then
G
contains a subgraph
H\subseteq G
with

m^{\prime}:=e(H)
and a unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}
such that
H
satisfies the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition and
\lambda(H)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m^{\prime}}).

Moreover,
\frac{\lambda(H)}{\sqrt{m^{\prime}}}\geq 1+(1-4\eta)\bigl(\alpha^{-1/2}-1\bigr)
, where
\alpha:=\frac{m^{\prime}}{m}.

Proof.

We construct a sequence of graphs
G:=G_{0}\supseteq G_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq G_{\ell}:=H
iteratively as follows.
Given
G_{i}
, let
\bm{x}^{(i)}
be a unit Perron eigenvector and write
m_{i}:=e(G_{i})
.
If there is an edge
u_{i}v_{i}\in E(G_{i})
with
x^{(i)}_{u_{i}}x^{(i)}_{v_{i}}<\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{m_{i}}},

delete one such edge to obtain
G_{i+1}
; otherwise, stop the deletion.
In Claim
3
below, we show that this process terminates at a subgraph
H
with linearly many edges.
For each
0\leq i\leq\ell
,
we write
\lambda_{i}:=\lambda(G_{i})
,
m:=m_{0}=e(G)
, and
m^{\prime}:=m_{\ell}=e(H)
.
Each deletion step satisfies
m_{i+1}=m_{i}-1
.
Let
\bm{x}:=\bm{x}^{(\ell)}
be a unit Perron eigenvector of
H
.
At the final step
\ell
, by definition there is no edge
uv\in E(G_{\ell})
with

x^{(\ell)}_{u}x^{(\ell)}_{v}<\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{m_{\ell}}}.

Thus, every edge
uv\in E(H)
satisfies
x_{u}x_{v}\geq\eta/\sqrt{m^{\prime}}
.

Claim 1
.

For each
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
, one has
\lambda_{i+1}\geq\lambda_{i}-\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{m_{i}}}.

Proof of claim.

For each
i
, write
G_{i+1}=G_{i}-u_{i}v_{i}
and set
A_{i}:=A(G_{i})
and
A_{i+1}:=A(G_{i+1})
.
Since
\bm{x}^{(i)}
is a unit Perron eigenvector of
A_{i}
corresponding to
\lambda_{i}
, we have
\lambda_{i}=(\bm{x}^{(i)})^{\mathsf{T}}A_{i}\bm{x}^{(i)}.

By the Rayleigh quotient applied to
A_{i+1}
, we get

\lambda_{i+1}\ \geq\ (\bm{x}^{(i)})^{\mathsf{T}}A_{i+1}\bm{x}^{(i)}=(\bm{x}^{(i)})^{\mathsf{T}}A_{i}\bm{x}^{(i)}-2x^{(i)}_{u_{i}}x^{(i)}_{v_{i}}=\lambda_{i}-2x^{(i)}_{u_{i}}x^{(i)}_{v_{i}}\ \geq\ \lambda_{i}-\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{m_{i}}}.\qed

Now we show that edge loss in the deletion process forces a multiplicative spectral gap.

Claim 2
.

We have

\frac{\lambda(H)}{\sqrt{m^{\prime}}}\geq 1+(1-4\eta)(\alpha^{-1/2}-1).

Proof of claim.

By Lemma
3.3
with
k=t-1
,
\lambda(S_{t-1,m})=\sqrt{m}+\frac{t-2}{2}+O_{t}(m^{-1/2}),

so
\lambda(S_{t-1,m})\geq\sqrt{m}

for all sufficiently large
m
.
Increasing the lower bound on
m
if necessary, the hypothesis

\lambda(G)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
therefore implies
\lambda(G)\geq\sqrt{m}
.
For each deletion step, we have
m_{i+1}=m_{i}-1
and

\sqrt{m_{i}}-\sqrt{m_{i+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{i}}+\sqrt{m_{i+1}}}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2\sqrt{m_{i}}},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{i}}}\ \leq\ 2(\sqrt{m_{i}}-\sqrt{m_{i+1}}).

Combining with Claim
1
gives

\lambda_{i+1}-\sqrt{m_{i+1}}\ \geq\ (\lambda_{i}-\sqrt{m_{i}})+(1-4\eta)\bigl(\sqrt{m_{i}}-\sqrt{m_{i+1}}\bigr).

Summing over
i=0,1,\dots,\ell-1
and telescoping yields

\lambda(H)-\sqrt{m^{\prime}}\ \geq\ \lambda(G)-\sqrt{m}\;+\;(1-4\eta)\bigl(\sqrt{m}-\sqrt{m^{\prime}}\bigr).

This also shows that
m^{\prime}>0
. Indeed, if
m^{\prime}=0
, then
H
is edgeless, so
\lambda(H)=0
, whereas the right-hand side above is at least
(1-4\eta)\sqrt{m}>0
, a contradiction.
Using
\lambda(G)\geq\sqrt{m}
and dividing by
\sqrt{m^{\prime}}
, we obtain

\frac{\lambda(H)}{\sqrt{m^{\prime}}}\ \geq\ 1+(1-4\eta)\left(\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\sqrt{m^{\prime}}}-1\right)=1+(1-4\eta)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}-1\right).\qed

Next, we give a lower bound on the number of remaining edges of
H
.

Claim 3
.

The resulting graph
H
contains
m^{\prime}\geq c_{\eta}m
edges, where
c_{\eta}:=\left(\frac{1-4\eta}{\sqrt{2}-4\eta}\right)^{2}>0.

Proof of claim.

Since
\lambda(H)\leq\sqrt{2m^{\prime}}
,
Claim
2
gives

1+(1-4\eta)(\alpha^{-1/2}-1)\leq\sqrt{2}.

Thus
\alpha=\frac{m^{\prime}}{m}\geq c_{\eta}.

∎

It remains to show that the pruning preserves the split gap, that is,
\lambda(H)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m^{\prime}}).

If
\ell=0
, this is immediate from the hypothesis. Hence assume
\ell\geq 1
.
Define

\Delta_{i}:=\lambda_{i}-\lambda(S_{t-1,m_{i}})

for every
0\leq i\leq\ell
.
In particular,
\Delta_{0}=\lambda(G)-\lambda(S_{t-1,m})>0
by assumption.
To prove
\lambda(H)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m^{\prime}})
, it suffices to show that
\Delta_{i+1}\geq\Delta_{i}
for every
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
.

Since
\eta=\frac{1}{16t}
, we can choose
M=M(t,\eta)
such that for all
s\geq M
,

\frac{1}{2(t-1)(\sqrt{s}+t-1)}>\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{s}}.

Then for all sufficiently large
m
, the lower bound
m^{\prime}\geq c_{\eta}m
in Claim
3
implies
m_{i}\geq m^{\prime}\geq M
for each
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
.
By Claim
1
,
\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i}\geq-\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{m_{i}}}.

By Lemma
3.4
, applied with
k=t-1
, we get

\lambda(S_{t-1,m_{i}})-\lambda(S_{t-1,m_{i}-1})\geq\frac{1}{2(t-1)(\sqrt{m_{i}}+t-1)}.

Combining the above inequalities,

\Delta_{i+1}-\Delta_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i}+\lambda(S_{t-1,m_{i}})-\lambda(S_{t-1,m_{i}-1})>0.

Hence
\Delta_{i+1}>\Delta_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
. Therefore
\Delta_{\ell}>\Delta_{\ell-1}>\cdots>\Delta_{0}>0,

and so
\lambda(H)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m^{\prime}}).

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

\mathbb{E}[\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(G)]=\frac{1}{2}\binom{n}{t}\binom{n-t}{t}\cdot{\binom{N-t^{2}}{m-t^{2}}}\bigg/{\binom{N}{m}}.

Using
\binom{n}{t}\binom{n-t}{t}=\frac{n^{2t}}{(t!)^{2}}\left(1+O_{t}(n^{-1})\right)
and

{\binom{N-t^{2}}{m-t^{2}}}\bigg/{\binom{N}{m}}\;=\;\prod_{i=0}^{t^{2}-1}\frac{m-i}{N-i}\;=\;\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)^{t^{2}}\left(1+O_{t}(m^{-1})\right),

which together with
n=2\sqrt{m}+O_{t}(1)
and

N=2m+O_{t}(\sqrt{m})
yields

\mathbb{E}[\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(G)]=\left(\frac{2^{-(t-1)^{2}}}{(t!)^{2}}+O_{t}(m^{-1/2})\right)m^{t}.

Hence, for every
\varepsilon>0
and all sufficiently large
m
, there exists an
m
-edge graph
G
with

\lambda(G)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
, while
\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(G)\leq(B_{t}+\varepsilon)m^{t}
. This shows the tightness of
B_{t}
.
∎

We point out that for every
t\geq 3
, the graphs near the split graph
S_{t,m}
are not sharp for Theorem
1.5
.
This is different from the case
t=2
in Theorem
1.4
.

4.1
Delocalized case

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
t\geq 2
and
\eta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{4})
. Let
H
be an
m
-edge graph with unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}
. Denote

g:=\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}m^{1/4}
.
If
H
satisfies the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition and
g=O(1)
, then

\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H)\geq\bigl(B_{t}-o(1)\bigr)\Big(\frac{\lambda^{2}(H)}{m}\Big)^{t(t-1)}m^{t}.

In the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we use a trick of the authors
[
20
]
.
Our goal is to show that
H
is dense, i.e. supported on
O(\sqrt{m})
vertices, so the term
O_{t}(n^{2t-1})
in the bound of Lemma
3.1
is negligible.

Proof.

Fix
t\geq 2
and
\eta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{4})
.
We denote
\lambda:=\lambda(H)
.
Let
H
be an
m
-edge graph with unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}
satisfying the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition.
Delete all isolated vertices of
H
, if any. This does not change
m
,
\lambda(H)
,
\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H)
, the heavy-edge condition, or the value of
g
. Thus we may assume that
H
has no isolated vertices. Let
n:=|V(H)|
.
Assume that
g=\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}m^{1/4}=O(1)
, and fix
G_{0}>0
such that
g\leq G_{0}
for all sufficiently large
m
.
Then
\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}\leq G_{0}m^{-1/4}
.
For any vertex
v\in V(H)
, choose a neighbor
u\in N(v)
; then by the heavy-edge condition,

x_{v}\ \geq\ \frac{\eta}{x_{u}\sqrt{m}}\ \geq\ \frac{\eta}{\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{m}}\ \geq\ \frac{\eta}{G_{0}}m^{-1/4}.

Using
\|\bm{x}\|_{2}=1
gives
1\geq n\cdot{\eta}^{2}{G_{0}}^{-2}m^{-1/2},
so
n\leq{G_{0}^{2}}{\eta^{-2}}\sqrt{m}.

Lemma
3.1
yields

\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H)\ \geq\ B_{t}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{m}\right)^{t(t-1)}m^{t}\ -\ O_{t}\!\left(n^{2t-1}\right).

Since
n=O(\sqrt{m})
, the error term
O_{t}(n^{2t-1})=o(m^{t})
.
Also the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition implies

\lambda=\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}A(H)\bm{x}=2\sum_{uv\in E(H)}x_{u}x_{v}\geq 2m\cdot\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{m}}=2\eta\sqrt{m}.

So the leading term is
\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{m}\right)^{t(t-1)}m^{t}=\Omega_{t,\eta}(m^{t}).

Thus, the error term
O_{t}(n^{2t-1})=o(m^{t})
can be absorbed into the
(B_{t}-o(1))

coefficient.
This gives the desired bound.
∎

4.2
A structural result for the localized case

In this section, we first establish a structural result. Roughly, it says that if
H
is an
m
-edge graph satisfying both the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition and
g:=\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}m^{1/4}\to\infty
,
then
H
admits a useful vertex partition.
To be more precise, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that a partition
V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D
satisfies conditions (T1)–(T3) if
(T1)

D
is independent;

(T2)

E_{H}(C,D)=\varnothing
;

(T3)
every edge of
H
lies either in
E(H[C])
or is incident to
A
.
We say that such a partition is an
ACD partition
, and we call
C
the
core
.

We point out that the ACD partition is quite different from the traditional bipartition obtained from the edge-spectral stability theorem recently established by the authors
[
20
]
.

In the localized case, we first prove the following structural result.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
H
be an
m
-edge graph with unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}
.
Assume that
H
satisfies the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition for some fixed
\eta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{4})
and
g=\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}m^{1/4}\to\infty
.
Then
H
admits an ACD partition
V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D
such that

e_{H}(A,C)=o(m),\quad|A|=o(\sqrt{m}),\quad|C|\leq\sqrt{m}\cdot m^{o(1)}.

Proof.

Write
L:=\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}
and
g:=Lm^{1/4}
.
Assume
g=g(m)\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
and the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition

x_{u}x_{v}\ \geq\ \frac{\eta}{\sqrt{m}}
for every edge
uv\in E(H)
.
We begin by producing an ACD partition.
Let
\widetilde{g}:=g/\sqrt{\eta}
and
K:=\left\lceil 2\log_{2}\widetilde{g}\right\rceil+1
.
For
0\leq h\leq K
, set
\theta_{h}:=2^{-h}L
.
Let

I:=\Bigl\{\lfloor K/2\rfloor-\lfloor\sqrt{K}\rfloor,\ \dots,\ \lfloor K/2\rfloor-\lceil\sqrt{K}/2\rceil\Bigr\}.

For
i\in\{1,2,\dots,\lfloor K/2\rfloor\}
, we define the intermediate level sets

C_{i}:=\{v:\ \theta_{i}\geq x_{v}>\theta_{K-i}\},\qquad B_{i}:=\{v:\ \theta_{i-1}\geq x_{v}>\theta_{i}\},\qquad F_{i}:=E_{H}(C_{i},B_{i}).

The sets
B_{1},\dots,B_{\lfloor K/2\rfloor}
are pairwise disjoint, so the edge sets
F_{i}
are disjoint and hence

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor K/2\rfloor}|F_{i}|\leq m
.
Let
\ell:=\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil
.
For each integer
i\in I
with
i\geq\ell
, we define
S_{i}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}|F_{i-j}|
.
Note that

\sum_{{i\in I,i\geq\ell}}S_{i}\leq\ell m,

since each
|F_{j}|
appears in at most
\ell
different sums
S_{i}
.
Since
g\to\infty
, we have
\widetilde{g}\to\infty
and
K\to\infty
. Then
\ell=o(\sqrt{K})=o(|I|)
as
|I|\approx\sqrt{K}/2
.
For all sufficiently large
m
, every
i\in I
satisfies

i\geq\lfloor K/2\rfloor-\lfloor\sqrt{K}\rfloor\geq\ell,

so the quantities
S_{i}
are defined for all
i\in I
.
By the pigeonhole principle,
there exists
i_{\star}\in I
with

S_{i_{\star}}\leq\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{i\in I}S_{i}\leq\frac{\ell m}{|I|}=o(m).

Denote
s:=\theta_{i_{\star}}
and
r:=\theta_{K-i_{\star}}
. Then
s>r>0
. We define
A,C,D
as follows:

A:=\{v\in V(H):\ x_{v}>s\},\quad C:=\{v\in V(H):\ s\geq x_{v}>r\},\quad D:=\{v\in V(H):\ x_{v}\leq r\}.

Claim 4
.

We have
e_{H}(A,C)=o(m)
.

Proof of claim.

To begin with, we partition
A=A_{\mathrm{mid}}\sqcup A_{\mathrm{very}}
, where

A_{\mathrm{mid}}:=\{v:\ s<x_{v}<2^{\ell}s\},\qquad A_{\mathrm{very}}:=\{v:\ x_{v}\geq 2^{\ell}s\}.

Since
2^{\ell}s=\theta_{i_{\star}-\ell}
, we have

A_{\mathrm{mid}}\subseteq B_{i_{\star}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup B_{i_{\star}-\ell+1}
.
Also
C=C_{i_{\star}}\subseteq C_{i_{\star}-j}
for
0\leq j\leq\ell-1
, so

E_{H}(C,B_{i_{\star}-j})\subseteq E_{H}(C_{i_{\star}-j},B_{i_{\star}-j})=F_{i_{\star}-j}
.
Therefore

e_{H}(A_{\mathrm{mid}},C)\ \leq\ \sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}|F_{i_{\star}-j}|\ =\ S_{i_{\star}}\ =\ o(m).

For
u\in C
and
v\in A_{\mathrm{very}}
one has
x_{u}>r
and
x_{v}\geq 2^{\ell}s
, hence
x_{u}x_{v}\geq 2^{\ell}sr
.
Since
sr=\theta_{i_{\star}}\theta_{K-i_{\star}}=2^{-K}L^{2}
and
2^{K}<4\widetilde{g}^{\,2}
, we have

sr>\eta/(4\sqrt{m})
.
Thus each edge
uv\in E_{H}(A_{\mathrm{very}},C)
satisfies
x_{u}x_{v}>2^{\ell}\eta/(4\sqrt{m})
, and so

e_{H}(A_{\mathrm{very}},C)\cdot 2^{\ell}\frac{\eta}{4\sqrt{m}}\ <\ \sum_{ab\in E(H)}x_{a}x_{b}\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\lambda(H)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2m}.

Using
2^{\ell}\geq K\to\infty
gives

e_{H}(A_{\mathrm{very}},C)=o(m)
.
So we conclude that
e_{H}(A,C)=o(m)
.
∎

Claim 5
.

The partition
V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D
satisfies
(T1)
–
(T3)
of Definition
4.3
.

Proof of claim.

We first show that
sr<\eta/\sqrt{m}
.
Indeed, since
2^{K}\geq 2\widetilde{g}^{\,2}
, we have

sr=2^{-K}L^{2}\leq\frac{L^{2}}{2\widetilde{g}^{\,2}}=\frac{L^{2}}{2(g^{2}/\eta)}=\frac{\eta}{2\sqrt{m}}<\ \frac{\eta}{\sqrt{m}}.

If
u\in C\cup D
and
v\in D
, then
x_{u}\leq s
and
x_{v}\leq r
, so
x_{u}x_{v}\leq sr<\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{m}}
.
By the heavy-edge assumption, there are no
C
–
D
edges and
D
is independent, proving (T1)–(T2).
Now, let
uv\in E(H)
be an edge not incident to
A
.
Then
x_{u},x_{v}\leq s
, so neither endpoint can lie in
D
, since otherwise
x_{u}x_{v}<\eta/\sqrt{m}
, and hence
u,v\in C
.
Thus every edge not incident to
A
lies in
H[C]
, which is exactly (T3).
∎

Claim 6
.

We have
|A|=o(\sqrt{m})
and

|C|\leq\sqrt{m}\cdot m^{o(1)}
.

Proof of claim.

Since
\|\bm{x}\|_{2}=1
and every vertex in
A
satisfies
x_{v}>s
, we have
|A|s^{2}<1
and hence
|A|<1/s^{2}
.
Write
j:=\lfloor K/2\rfloor-i_{\star}
.
As
i_{\star}\in I
, we have
j\geq\lceil\sqrt{K}/2\rceil\to\infty
and also
j=O(\sqrt{K})
.
Moreover
2i_{\star}\leq K-2j
, so

\frac{1}{s^{2}}=\frac{2^{2i_{\star}}}{L^{2}}\ \leq\ \frac{2^{K}}{L^{2}}\,2^{-2j}.

Since
K\leq 2\log_{2}\widetilde{g}+2
, we have
2^{K}\leq 4\widetilde{g}^{\,2}
and therefore

\frac{2^{K}}{L^{2}}\ \leq\ \frac{4\widetilde{g}^{\,2}}{L^{2}}=\frac{4(g^{2}/\eta)}{L^{2}}=\frac{4\sqrt{m}}{\eta}.

Combining these bounds gives
|A|\leq(4/\eta)\,2^{-2j}\sqrt{m}=o(\sqrt{m})
.

Similarly, every vertex in
C
satisfies
x_{v}>r
, so
|C|r^{2}\leq 1
and hence
|C|\leq 1/r^{2}
.
Since
r=\theta_{K-i_{\star}}=2^{-(K-i_{\star})}L
and
K-i_{\star}\leq\lceil K/2\rceil+j
, we obtain

\frac{1}{r^{2}}=\frac{2^{2(K-i_{\star})}}{L^{2}}\ \leq\ \frac{2^{K+2j+2}}{L^{2}}\ \ll_{\eta}\ 2^{2j}\sqrt{m}.

As
j=O(\sqrt{K})
and
K=O(\log\widetilde{g})=O(\log m)
, we have
2^{2j}=m^{o(1)}
, and hence
|C|\leq\sqrt{m}\cdot m^{o(1)}.

∎

The three claims complete the proof.
∎

4.3
Localized case with a dense core

The following lemma deals with the localized case with a dense core.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
H
be an
m
-edge graph with the unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}
, and let

g:=\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}m^{1/4}
.
Assume that
H
satisfies the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition for some fixed
\eta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{4})
and that
g\to\infty
. Assume in addition that
\lambda(H)\geq\sqrt{m}
, and that the partition
V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D
obtained from Theorem
4.4
satisfies

e(H[C])=(\alpha+o(1))m
for some constant
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Then

\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H)\geq(B_{t}-o(1))m^{t}
.

Proof.

Set
B:=A\sqcup D
and write

\lambda:=\lambda(H)
,
\lambda_{C}:=\lambda\bigl(H[C]\bigr)

and
\lambda_{B}:=\lambda\bigl(H[B]\bigr)
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
(a) to the graph
H[B]
with the partition
B=A\sqcup D
yields, since
\lambda(H[D])=0
,

\lambda_{B}\ \leq\ \lambda\bigl(H[A]\bigr)+\sqrt{e_{H}(A,D)}\ \leq\ |A|+\sqrt{e\bigl(H[B]\bigr)}.

(13)

Next apply Lemma
3.2
(b) to the partition
V(H)=C\sqcup B
.
Since (T2) holds, the cut between
C
and
B
consists only of
A
–
C
edges, so
e_{H}(C,B)=e_{H}(C,A)=o(m)
.
Therefore

(\lambda-\lambda_{C})(\lambda-\lambda_{B})\ \leq\ e_{H}(C,B)\ =\ o(m).

(14)

We now count copies of
K_{t,t}
inside the induced subgraph
H[C]
by applying Lemma
3.1
.
By (T1)–(T3),

m=e(H[C])+e(H[B])+e_{H}(A,C).

Using the assumption
e(H[C])=(\alpha+o(1))m
, together with
e_{H}(A,C)=o(m)
, we have
e(H[B])=(1-\alpha+o(1))m
.
Substituting into (
13
) gives
\lambda_{B}\ \leq\ \bigl(\sqrt{1-\alpha}+o(1)\bigr)\sqrt{m},

and therefore

\lambda-\lambda_{B}\geq\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-\alpha}-o(1)\bigr)\sqrt{m}=\Omega(\sqrt{m}),

as
\alpha>0
.
Substituting into (
14
) yields
\lambda-\lambda_{C}=o(\sqrt{m})
, and hence
\lambda_{C}\geq(1-o(1))\sqrt{m}
.

Let
m_{C}:=e(H[C])
.
Applying Lemma
3.1
to
H[C]
gives

\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H)\ \geq\ \texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H[C])\ \geq\ B_{t}\left(\frac{\lambda_{C}^{2}}{m_{C}}\right)^{t(t-1)}m_{C}^{t}\ -\ o(m_{C}^{t})\ \geq\ \bigl(B_{t}-o(1)\bigr)m^{t},

where the second inequality holds since
O_{t}(|C|^{2t-1})\leq\left(\sqrt{m}\,m^{o(1)}\right)^{2t-1}=o(m^{t}).

For the last inequality, use
\lambda_{C}^{2}\geq(1-o(1))m
and
m_{C}=(\alpha+o(1))m
:

\left(\frac{\lambda_{C}^{2}}{m_{C}}\right)^{t(t-1)}m_{C}^{t}\geq(1-o(1))\,\alpha^{\,t-t(t-1)}m^{t}\geq(1-o(1))m^{t},

because
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
t-t(t-1)=t(2-t)\leq 0
.
∎

4.4
Localized case with a sparse core

In this section,
we consider the case where the induced subgraph
H[C]
is sparse.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
t\geq 2
and
H
be a graph with
m\to\infty
edges.
Assume that
H
admits a partition
V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D
satisfying conditions

(T1)
–
(T3)
. Suppose moreover that

e_{H}(A,C)=o(m)
and
|A|=o(\sqrt{m})
.
If in addition
\lambda(H)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
and
e(H[C])=o(m)
, then
\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H)\geq\bigl(B_{t}-o(1)\bigr)m^{t}
.

To prove Lemma
4.6
, we first show that either there are many copies of
K_{t,t}
using only edges between
A
and
D
, or
H
differs from a complete bipartite graph
K_{R,B}
in
o(m)
edges, where
|R|\leq t-1
; see the following Theorem
4.9
.
In the latter case, we further show that

\lambda(H)<\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
. Thus, this case does not occur under the assumption of Lemma
4.6
; see Subsection
4.4.2
.

4.4.1
Rank-one structure of
A
–
D
incidence matrix

We use the following terminology:
k
-row covers and
\theta
-aligned rows.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
M\in\{0,1\}^{A\times D}
be the
A
–
D
incidence matrix.
For
k\geq 1
, a set
R\subseteq A
is a
k
-row cover
if
|R|\leq k
and
e_{H}(A\setminus R,\ D)=0
.
Along an asymptotic regime
e(H)\to\infty
, we call
R
an
asymptotic
k
-row cover
if

e_{H}(A\setminus R,\ D)=o\bigl(e_{H}(A,D)\bigr)
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
M\in\{0,1\}^{A\times D}
be the
A
–
D
incidence matrix and let
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
be a unit nonnegative top right singular vector of
M

(so
\|M\bm{v}\|_{2}=\|M\|
).
For a vertex
a\in A
with row vector
\bm{m}_{a}
, we write
\bm{x}_{a}:=\bm{m}_{a}/\|\bm{m}_{a}\|_{2}
if
\bm{m}_{a}\neq 0
.
Given
\theta\in[0,1]
, we say that the
a
-th row is
\theta
-aligned
with
\bm{v}
if
\langle\bm{x}_{a},\bm{v}\rangle^{2}\geq 1-\theta
, and we write

R_{\theta}(M,\bm{v}):=\big\{a\in A:\bm{m}_{a}\neq 0\text{ and }\langle\bm{x}_{a},\bm{v}\rangle^{2}\geq 1-\theta\big\}.

The next theorem shows that if the induced subgraph
H[C]
is sparse, then the
A
–
D
incidence matrix
M
is almost rank one, i.e.,

\|M\|=\bigl(1-o(1)\bigr)\|M\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

Consequently, either
H
already
contains many copies of
K_{t,t}
, or else the
A
–
D
edges are essentially covered by a small set of vertices in
A
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
t\geq 2
and
H
be a graph with
m\to\infty
edges.
Assume that
H
admits a partition
V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D
satisfying conditions
(T1)
–
(T3)
,
and suppose that
e_{H}(A,C)=o(m)
and
|A|=o(\sqrt{m}\,)
.
If in addition
\lambda(H)\geq\sqrt{m}
and
e(H[C])=o(m)
,
then at least one of the following holds:

(i)

We have
\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H)\geq\left(\frac{1}{t!\,t^{t}}-o(1)\right)m^{t}=\big(1-o(1)\big)\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(S_{t,m}).

(ii)

There exist a subset
R\subseteq A
with
1\leq|R|\leq t-1
, and a subset
B\subseteq D
such that

|B|=(1-o(1))\frac{m}{|R|},\qquad e_{H}(A\setminus R,\ B)=o(m),\qquad e_{H}(R,\ D\setminus B)=o(m),

and
H[R,B]
is complete bipartite (in particular
B
is independent).
Consequently,
R
is an asymptotic
(t\!-\!1)
-row cover of the
A
–
D
edge set in the sense of Definition
4.7
.

Proof.

Let
H_{AD}
be the bipartite graph with parts
A,D
and edge set
E_{H}(A,D)
, and let

M\in\{0,1\}^{A\times D}
be its incidence matrix.
Then
\lambda(H_{AD})=\|M\|
and
\|M\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=e_{H}(A,D)
.
By (T3), every edge of
H
not in
H_{AD}
lies in
H[C]
, between
A
and
C
, or inside
A
.
Since
e(H[C])=o(m)
,
e_{H}(A,C)=o(m)
and
|A|=o(\sqrt{m})
, it follows that

e(H)-e_{H}(A,D)\ \leq\ e(H[C])+e_{H}(A,C)+e(H[A])\ =\ o(m)+\binom{|A|}{2}\ =\ o(m).

Denote
e:=e_{H}(A,D)
, so
e=(1-o(1))m
.
Since
\lambda(H\setminus H_{AD})\leq\sqrt{2e(H\setminus H_{AD})}=o(\sqrt{m})
, and the Rayleigh quotient gives

\lambda(H)\leq\lambda(H_{AD})+\lambda(H\setminus H_{AD})\leq\|M\|+o(\sqrt{m}).

(15)

Substituting the assumption
\lambda(H)\geq\sqrt{m}
into (
15
) gives

\|M\|\geq(1-o(1))\sqrt{m}
.
Trivially, we have
\|M\|\leq\|M\|_{\mathrm{F}}=\sqrt{e}\leq\sqrt{m}
. Thus, we conclude
\|M\|=(1-o(1))\sqrt{m}
and
\|M\|^{2}=(1-o(1))m=(1-o(1))e
. This shows that
M
has near rank one.

In the following, we write
\rho:=\|M\|
and set

\varepsilon:=1-\rho^{2}/e=o(1)
, and

\theta:=\sqrt{\varepsilon}=o(1)
.
Let
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
be a unit nonnegative top right singular vector of
M
(that is,
\|M\bm{v}\|=\rho
). Let
R:=R_{\theta}(M,\bm{v})\subseteq A

be the set of
\theta
-aligned rows (see Definition
4.8
).

Next, we prove that
R
meets almost all
A
–
D
edges.
For each
a\in A
, let
\bm{m}_{a}
be the
a
-th row vector of
M
. Note that
\|\bm{m}_{a}\|_{2}^{2}
is equal to the degree
d_{D}(a):=|N_{H}(a)\cap D|
.
If
d_{D}(a)>0
, then
\bm{x}_{a}:=\bm{m}_{a}/\sqrt{d_{D}(a)}
is a unit vector.
Since
\rho^{2}=\|M\bm{v}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{a\in A}\langle\bm{m}_{a},\bm{v}\rangle^{2}
, we get

e-\rho^{2}=\sum_{a\in A:\,d_{D}(a)>0}d_{D}(a)\cdot\left(1-\langle\bm{x}_{a},\bm{v}\rangle^{2}\right).

If
a\notin R
and
d_{D}(a)>0
, then
1-\langle\bm{x}_{a},\bm{v}\rangle^{2}>\theta
by definition. Then

e-\rho^{2}\ \geq\ \theta\sum_{a\in A\setminus R}d_{D}(a)\ =\ \theta\cdot e_{H}(A\setminus R,D).

Since
e-\rho^{2}=\varepsilon e=\theta^{2}e
,
\theta=o(1)
and
e=(1-o(1))m
,
we get

e_{H}(A\setminus R,D)\ \leq\ \theta e\ =\ o(m).

(16)

In particular, we see that
R\neq\varnothing
since
e_{H}(A,D)=(1-o(1))m
.

In what follows, we prove that
aligned rows of
R
have large intersection.
Fix distinct vertices
a,a^{\prime}\in R
with
d_{D}(a^{\prime})\geq d_{D}(a)>0
.
There exist a real number
\alpha
and a vector
\bm{u}
with
\bm{u}\perp\bm{v}
such that

\bm{x}_{a}=\alpha\bm{v}+\bm{u}.

Then
\alpha=\langle\bm{x}_{a},\bm{v}\rangle\geq 0
, and
\alpha^{2}\geq 1-\theta
, by the definition of
R
, and hence

\|\bm{u}\|_{2}^{2}=1-\alpha^{2}\leq\theta.

Similarly, writing
\bm{x}_{a^{\prime}}=\alpha^{\prime}\bm{v}+\bm{u}^{\prime}
, we have
\alpha^{\prime}\geq 0,
and
\|\bm{u}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\theta.

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

\langle\bm{x}_{a},\bm{x}_{a^{\prime}}\rangle=\alpha\alpha^{\prime}+\langle\bm{u},\bm{u}^{\prime}\rangle\geq(1-\theta)-\|\bm{u}\|_{2}\|\bm{u}^{\prime}\|_{2}\geq 1-2\theta.

(17)

Consequently, it follows that

|N_{D}(a)\cap N_{D}(a^{\prime})|=\langle\bm{m}_{a},\bm{m}_{a^{\prime}}\rangle=\sqrt{d_{D}(a)d_{D}(a^{\prime})}\,\langle\bm{x}_{a},\bm{x}_{a^{\prime}}\rangle\geq(1-2\theta)\sqrt{d_{D}(a)d_{D}(a^{\prime})}.

(18)

Since the left-hand side is at most
d_{D}(a)
, we also have
d_{D}(a)\leq d_{D}(a^{\prime})\leq d_{D}(a)/(1-2\theta)^{2}
. In other words, the degrees of any two vertices in
R
are comparable.

For any subset
S\subseteq R
of size
s:=|S|
, let
a_{\ast}\in S
be a vertex of minimum degree in
D
. Then for
each
a\in S\setminus\{a_{\ast}\}
, we have

d_{D}(a)\geq d_{D}(a_{\ast})
.
By (
18
), we get

|N_{D}(a_{\ast})\cap N_{D}(a)|\geq(1-2\theta)d_{D}(a_{\ast})
, which yields

|N_{D}(a_{\ast})\setminus N_{D}(a)|\leq 2\theta\cdot d_{D}(a_{\ast})
.
Writing
N_{D}(S):=\bigcap_{a\in S}N_{D}(a)
, it follows that

|N_{D}(S)|\ \geq\ (1-2(s-1)\theta)\cdot d_{D}(a_{\ast}).

(19)

Case (i).

Assume that
|R|\geq t
.

Write
r:=|R|
and
D_{\ast}:=\min_{a\in R}d_{D}(a)
.
From (
16
), we have
\sum_{a\in R}d_{D}(a)=e_{H}(R,D)\geq(1-\theta)e
.
Also
\sum_{a\in R}d_{D}(a)\leq e
, so
rD_{\ast}\leq e
.
On the other hand, degree comparability gives
\max_{a\in R}d_{D}(a)\leq D_{\ast}/(1-2\theta)^{2}
, hence

(1-\theta)e\ \leq\ \sum_{a\in R}d_{D}(a)\ \leq\ r\cdot\frac{D_{\ast}}{(1-2\theta)^{2}},

which yields
D_{\ast}\ \geq\ (1-\theta)(1-2\theta)^{2}\,\frac{e}{r}.

Since
r\leq|A|=o(\sqrt{m})
, this implies

D_{\ast}=(1-o(1))\frac{e}{r}=\Omega(\sqrt{m})\to\infty.

Every
t
-subset
T\subseteq R
has at least

L:=\lfloor(1-2(t-1)\theta)D_{\ast}\rfloor
common neighbours in
D
by (
19
).
Choosing any
t
of these common neighbours gives a copy of
K_{t,t}
with one side
T
and the other side in
D
. Hence

\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H)\ \geq\ \binom{r}{t}\binom{L}{t}.

Note that
L=(1-o(1))e/r\to\infty
. So
\binom{L}{t}=\bigl(\frac{1}{t!}-o(1)\bigr)(e/r)^{t}
.
Since
r\geq t
,

\frac{\binom{r}{t}}{r^{t}}=\frac{1}{t!}\prod_{k=0}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{r}\right)\geq\frac{1}{t!}\prod_{k=0}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{t}\right)=\frac{1}{t^{t}}.

Combining these bounds and
e=(1-o(1))m
gives alternative (i).

Case (ii).

Assume that
|R|\leq t-1
.

Let
B:=N_{D}(R)=\bigcap_{a\in R}N_{D}(a)
.
Then
H[R,B]
is complete bipartite and
B
is an independent set.
By (
16
), we have
e_{H}(A\setminus R,D)=o(m)
. Hence

e_{H}(R,D)\ =\ e_{H}(A,D)-e_{H}(A\setminus R,D)\ =\ (1-o(1))m.

Recall that
D_{\ast}:=\min_{a\in R}d_{D}(a)
.
Degree comparability gives
\max_{a\in R}d_{D}(a)\leq D_{\ast}/(1-2\theta)^{2}
, so

|R|D_{\ast}\ \leq\ \sum_{a\in R}d_{D}(a)\ =\ e_{H}(R,D)\ \leq\ |R|\cdot\frac{D_{\ast}}{(1-2\theta)^{2}},

and therefore
D_{\ast}=(1-o(1))\frac{m}{|R|}
, since
\theta=o(1)
.
Applying (
19
) with
S=R
shows

|B|\ =\ (1-o(1))D_{\ast}\ =\ (1-o(1))\frac{m}{|R|}.

Finally, since
e_{H}(R,B)=|R||B|=(1-o(1))m
while
e_{H}(R,D)=(1-o(1))m
, we get

e_{H}(R,D\setminus B)=o(m).

Also
e_{H}(A\setminus R,B)\leq e_{H}(A\setminus R,D)=o(m)
.
This is alternative (ii).
∎

4.4.2
Proof of Lemma
4.6

Note that
\lambda(H)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m})\geq\sqrt{m}
for all sufficiently large
m
.
Applying Theorem
4.9
,
if part (i) occurs, then

\texttt{\#}K_{t,t}(H)\geq\left(\frac{1}{t!\,t^{t}}-o(1)\right)m^{t}\geq\big(B_{t}-o(1)\big)m^{t}.

Thus we are done. It remains to show that the small row-cover alternative (ii) cannot occur.

Suppose on the contrary that Theorem
4.9
(ii) occurs.
An edge of
H
not covered by the vertices of

is called
bad
. We aim to bound the contribution of bad edges to the spectral radius.
Let

denote the set of all bad edges in
H
, and let
U
be the set of vertices incident to any edge in
E_{b}
.
Let
B\subseteq D
be the set of common neighbors of
R
from
Theorem
4.9
(ii).
Since
H[R,B]
is complete bipartite and
|B|=(1-o(1))m/|R|
, we have
e_{H}(R,B)\ =\ |R||B|\ =\ (1-o(1))m.
Every edge of
E_{b}
has no endpoint in
R
, hence
E_{b}\cap E_{H}(R,B)=\varnothing
and therefore

|E_{b}|\ \leq\ m-e_{H}(R,B)\ =\ o(m).

In particular, we have
m-|E_{b}|\to\infty
.

If
|E_{b}|=0
, then every edge of

meets

, so

is a vertex cover of size at most
t-1
. Hence

is
K_{t,t}
-free.
For sufficiently large

, applying Theorem
1.3
implies
\lambda(H)\leq\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
, which contradicts the assumption.
Now we may assume
|E_{b}|\geq 1
.

In what follows, we show that the bad edges have small Perron weight and small contribution to the Rayleigh quotient of
H
.
Note that
U\cap R=\varnothing
and
|U|\leq 2|E_{b}|
. Moreover, every edge leaving
U
goes to
R
:
if
uv\in E_{H}(U,V(H)\setminus U)
, then
u\in U
and

, forcing

; since

, we must have

.
Hence

e_{H}\!\bigl(U,\,V(H)\setminus U\bigr)\ \leq\ e_{H}(U,R)\ \leq\ |R||U|\ \leq\ 2|R||E_{b}|\ \leq\ 2(t-1)|E_{b}|.

Denote
\lambda_{U}:=\lambda(H[U])
. Since
E(H[U])=E_{b}
, the Frobenius-norm bound gives
\lambda_{U}\leq\sqrt{2|E_{b}|}=o(\sqrt{m}).

The assumption
\lambda(H)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
implies
\lambda(H)\geq\sqrt{m}
for all sufficiently large

. Hence

(\lambda(H)-\lambda_{U})^{2}=(1-o(1))m>0.

Recall that
\mu(U):=\sum_{u\in U}x_{u}^{2}
denotes the Perron mass of
U
with respect to
\bm{x}
.
Denote
W:=V(H)\setminus U
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
(c) to the cut

\mu(U)\ \leq\ \frac{e_{H}(U,W)}{(\lambda(H)-\lambda_{U})^{2}+e_{H}(U,W)}\ =\ O_{t}\!\left(\frac{|E_{b}|}{m}\right).

We denote
w(E_{b};\bm{x}):=\sum_{uv\in E_{b}}x_{u}x_{v}
.
Since

, the Rayleigh quotient gives

2w(E_{b};\bm{x})=\bm{x}_{U}^{\mathsf{T}}A(H[U])\bm{x}_{U}\leq\lambda_{U}\cdot\|\bm{x}_{U}\|_{2}^{2}=\lambda_{U}\cdot\mu(U),

so
w(E_{b};\bm{x})\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{U}\mu(U)=O_{t}\!\left(\frac{|E_{b}|^{3/2}}{m}\right).

Since
|E_{b}|=o(m)
, we have

w(E_{b};\bm{x})=o\left(|E_{b}|\big/\sqrt{m}\right).

(20)

Let
H_{0}
be the subgraph of
H
obtained by deleting all edges of
E_{b}
.
Then
e(H_{0})=m-|E_{b}|
. By definition of
E_{b}
, every edge of
H_{0}
is incident to a vertex of

.
Thus,

is a vertex cover of
H_{0}
with

, so

is

-free.
Applying Theorem
1.3
to
H_{0}
, since

, gives

\lambda(H_{0})\leq\lambda(S_{t-1,m-|E_{b}|}).

Therefore, by the Rayleigh quotient, we have

\lambda(H)\ \leq\ \lambda(H_{0})+2w(E_{b};\bm{x})\ \leq\ \lambda(S_{t-1,m-|E_{b}|})+2w(E_{b};\bm{x}).

(21)

On the other hand, applying Lemma
3.4
with
k=t-1
and

yields

\lambda(S_{t-1,m-|E_{b}|})\ \leq\ \lambda(S_{t-1,m})-\frac{|E_{b}|}{2(t-1)(\sqrt{m}+t-1)},

(22)

for all sufficiently large

.
Using (
20
), we get
2w(E_{b};\bm{x})=o\!\left(\frac{|E_{b}|}{\sqrt{m}+t-1}\right).

Combining (
21
) and (

), it follows that
\lambda(H)<\lambda(S_{t-1,m})
, contradicting the assumption. Thus, part (ii) of Theorem
4.9
cannot occur. This completes the proof of Lemma

.

4.5
Putting things together

To prove Theorem
1.5
, we also need a lemma of the authors
[
20
, Lemma 2.3]
, which says that a fixed multiplicative gap above
\sqrt{m}
forces delocalization of the Perron eigenvector.

Lemma 4.10

(Li–Liu–Zhang
[
20
]
)
.

Let
H
be a graph with
m
edges and unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V(H)}
.
If
\lambda^{2}(H)\geq(1+\delta)m
where
0<\delta\leq 1/3
, then

\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}m^{1/4}<\delta^{-4}
.

We include a proof for the convenience of readers.

Proof.

We denote
\lambda=\lambda(H)
and

x_{i}=\max\{x_{v}:v\in V(H)\}
.
We actually show that
x_{i}m^{1/4}<\delta^{-2}
.
Suppose on the contrary that
x_{i}\geq\delta^{-2}m^{-1/4}
. Let
L=\{u\in V(H):x_{u}>\delta^{-1/2}m^{-1/4}\}.

Then

\lambda^{2}x_{i}=\sum_{j\in N(i)}\sum_{k\in N(j)}x_{k}\leq\sum_{jk\in E(H)}(x_{j}+x_{k})=\sum_{jk\in E(H[L])}(x_{j}+x_{k})+\sum_{jk\in E(H)\setminus E(H[L])}(x_{j}+x_{k}).

(23)

Since
\sum_{v\in V(H)}x_{v}^{2}=1
, we have

|L|\leq\delta m^{1/2}
. So the first sum on the right-hand side of (
23
) is at most
2x_{i}\binom{|L|}{2}\leq\delta^{2}mx_{i}.

For each
jk\in E(H)\setminus E(H[L])
,
we have
\min\{x_{j},x_{k}\}\leq\delta^{-1/2}m^{-1/4}\leq\delta^{3/2}x_{i}
.
Then
x_{j}+x_{k}\leq x_{i}+\delta^{3/2}x_{i}=(1+\delta^{3/2})x_{i}.

Therefore, we obtain from (
23
) that
(1+\delta)mx_{i}\leq\lambda^{2}x_{i}\leq\delta^{2}mx_{i}+(1+\delta^{3/2})x_{i}m.

However, for any
0<\delta\leq 1/3
, we have

1+\delta>\delta^{2}+(1+\delta^{3/2})
, which is a contradiction.
∎

To start with the proof of Theorem

prunes

.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.6

We first record the basic spectral counting input for even cycles.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with spectral radius
\lambda
.
Then, for every fixed
t\geq 2
,

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(G)\geq\frac{1}{4t}\lambda^{2t}-O_{t}(n^{2t-1}).

Proof.

The number
\mathrm{hom}(C_{2t},G)
is the number of closed walks of length

2t
in
G
. Hence

\mathrm{hom}(C_{2t},G)=\operatorname{tr}A(G)^{2t}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}(G)^{2t}\geq\lambda(G)^{2t}.

The number of non-injective maps from
V(C_{2t})
to
V(G)
is

O_{t}(n^{2t-1})
. Dividing the number of injective homomorphisms by

|\mathrm{Aut}(C_{2t})|=4t
gives the result.
∎

The sharp supersaturation behaviour for even cycles is different from
that for complete bipartite graphs. For comparison, the random graphs used in
Example
4.1
give the constant
1/(4t)
for

C_{2t}
, whereas the split graphs
S_{t,m}
give the smaller
constant
\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}
for every
t\geq 3
. Thus the sharp construction for
C_{2t}
is
split graphs rather than balanced dense graphs. Indeed, let
G\sim G(n,m)
with
n=2\sqrt{m}+O_{t}(1)
. Then

\mathbb{E}[\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(G)]={n\choose 2t}\frac{(2t)!}{4t}\cdot\frac{\binom{N-2t}{m-2t}}{\binom{N}{m}}=\left(\frac{1}{4t}+O_{t}(m^{-1/2})\right)m^{t},

where
N=\binom{n}{2}
. On the other hand, if

m-\binom{t}{2}=tq+r
with
0\leq r\leq t-1
, then the leading
contribution to
\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(S_{t,m})
comes from choosing all

t
clique vertices and
t
vertices from the independent side.
Thus

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(S_{t,m})=\left(1+o(1)\right){q\choose t}\frac{t!(t-1)!}{2}=\left(\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}+o(1)\right)m^{t}.

We next state the cycle analogues of the counting lemmas used for

K_{t,t}
. Their proofs are the same as the corresponding arguments
in Section
4
, with Lemma
5.1

replacing Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 5.2

(Delocalized cycle counting)
.

Fix
t\geq 2
and
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
. Let
H
be an
m
-edge
graph with unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}
, and set
g:=\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}m^{1/4}.

If
H
satisfies the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition and
g=O(1)
, then
\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H)\geq\left(\frac{1}{4t}-o(1)\right)\lambda(H)^{2t}.

Proof.

Delete isolated vertices, if any. This does not change the number of
edges, the spectral radius, the Perron vector on the non-isolated part,
or the number of cycles. The proof of Lemma
4.2

then gives
|V(H)|=O(\sqrt{m})
. By Lemma
5.1
,

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H)\geq\frac{1}{4t}\lambda(H)^{2t}-O_{t}(|V(H)|^{2t-1}).

The heavy-edge condition gives

\lambda(H)=2\sum_{uv\in E(H)}x_{u}x_{v}\geq 2\eta\sqrt{m}.

Thus
\lambda(H)^{2t}=\Omega_{t,\eta}(m^{t})
, while
|V(H)|^{2t-1}=O(m^{t-1/2})=o(m^{t}).

The error term is therefore
o(\lambda(H)^{2t})
.
∎

Lemma 5.3

(Localized dense core: cycles counting)
.

Let
H
be an
m
-edge graph satisfying the
\eta
-heavy-edge
condition for some fixed
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, and suppose

g=\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}m^{1/4}\to\infty
. Assume also that

\lambda(H)\geq\sqrt{m}
. Let

V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D

be the ACD partition obtained from Theorem
4.4
.
If
e(H[C])=(\alpha+o(1))m

for some constant
\alpha\in(0,1]
, then
\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H)\geq\left(\frac{1}{4t}-o(1)\right)\lambda(H)^{2t}.

In particular,
\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H)\geq\left(\frac{1}{4t}-o(1)\right)m^{t}.

Proof.

The proof of Lemma
4.5
gives
\lambda(H[C])=\lambda(H)-o(\sqrt{m}).

Since
\lambda(H)\geq\sqrt{m}
, this implies
\lambda(H[C])=(1-o(1))\lambda(H).

Also Theorem
4.4
gives
|C|\leq\sqrt{m}\,m^{o(1)}.

Applying Lemma
5.1
to
H[C]
, we get

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H)\geq\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H[C])\geq\frac{1}{4t}\lambda(H[C])^{2t}-O_{t}(|C|^{2t-1})=\left(\frac{1}{4t}-o(1)\right)\lambda(H)^{2t},

as
|C|^{2t-1}\leq\left(\sqrt{m}\,m^{o(1)}\right)^{2t-1}=o(m^{t})=o(\lambda(H)^{2t}).

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
H
be a graph with
m\to\infty
edges. Assume that
H

admits a partition
V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D
satisfying

(T1)
–
(T3)
, and suppose moreover that
e_{H}(A,C)=o(m),

|A|=o(\sqrt{m}),
and
e(H[C])=o(m).

If
\lambda(H)\geq\sqrt{m}
, then at least one of the following holds.

(i)

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H)\geq\left(\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}-o(1)\right)m^{t}.

(ii)

There exist
R\subseteq A
with
1\leq|R|\leq t-1
, and

B\subseteq D
, such that

|B|=(1-o(1))\frac{m}{|R|},\qquad e_{H}(A\setminus R,B)=o(m),\qquad e_{H}(R,D\setminus B)=o(m),

and
H[R,B]
is complete bipartite.

Proof.

The proof is identical to the proof of
Theorem
4.9
until the final counting step. We
briefly record the modification. Let
M
be the
A
–
D

incidence matrix. As in Theorem
4.9
, one obtains
a set
R\subseteq A
of aligned rows such that

e_{H}(A\setminus R,D)=o(m).

If
|R|\leq t-1
, then the same argument gives alternative (ii).

Assume now that
|R|=r\geq t
. Let

D_{\ast}:=\min_{a\in R}d_{D}(a).

As in the proof of Theorem
4.9
, the degrees

d_{D}(a)
,
a\in R
, are pairwise comparable, and

D_{\ast}=(1-o(1))\frac{e}{r},\qquad e:=e_{H}(A,D)=(1-o(1))m.

Moreover every
t
-subset
T\subseteq R
has at least
L=(1-o(1))D_{\ast}

common neighbours in
D
. For each such
T
, choosing
t
common
neighbours in
D
gives a copy of
K_{t,t}
, which contains

\frac{t!(t-1)!}{2}

copies of
C_{2t}
. Hence

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H)\geq\binom{r}{t}\binom{L}{t}\frac{t!(t-1)!}{2}\geq\left(\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}-o(1)\right)e^{t}=\left(\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}-o(1)\right)m^{t}.

This gives alternative (i).
∎

Lemma 5.5

(Localized sparse core: cycle counting)
.

Let
H
be an
m
-edge graph admitting an ACD partition

V(H)=A\sqcup C\sqcup D
satisfying
e_{H}(A,C)=o(m),

|A|=o(\sqrt{m}),
and
e(H[C])=o(m).

If
\lambda(H)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m}),

then

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H)\geq\left(\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}-o(1)\right)m^{t}.

Proof.

For all sufficiently large
m
, the assumption gives
\lambda(H)>\lambda(S_{t-1,m})\geq\sqrt{m}.

Apply Lemma
5.4
. If alternative
(i) occurs, we are done.

Suppose alternative (ii) occurs. The same bad-edge argument as
in the proof of Lemma
4.6
gives a
contradiction. Indeed, call an edge of
H

bad
if it is not
incident to
R
, and let
E_{b}
be the set of bad edges. Then

|E_{b}|=o(m).

If
E_{b}=\varnothing
, then
R
is a vertex cover of size at most

t-1
, so
H
is
K_{t,t}
-free. Theorem
1.3

then gives
\lambda(H)\leq\lambda(S_{t-1,m}),

contrary to the assumption.

Thus assume
|E_{b}|\geq 1
. Let
U
be the set of vertices incident
to an edge of
E_{b}
. As in Lemma
4.6
,

e_{H}(U,V(H)\setminus U)=O_{t}(|E_{b}|),\qquad\lambda(H[U])\leq\sqrt{2|E_{b}|}=o(\sqrt{m}),

and Lemma
3.2
(c) implies that the
Perron mass of
U
is

\mu(U)=O_{t}(|E_{b}|/m).

Therefore, for a unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}
of
H
,

\sum_{uv\in E_{b}}x_{u}x_{v}=o\left(\frac{|E_{b}|}{\sqrt{m}}\right).

Let
H_{0}:=H-E_{b}
. Then every edge of
H_{0}
is incident to
R
, so

H_{0}
is
K_{t,t}
-free. Hence

\lambda(H_{0})\leq\lambda(S_{t-1,m-|E_{b}|})

by Theorem
1.3
. By the Rayleigh quotient,

\lambda(H)\leq\lambda(H_{0})+2\sum_{uv\in E_{b}}x_{u}x_{v}.

Using Lemma
3.4
,

\lambda(S_{t-1,m-|E_{b}|})\leq\lambda(S_{t-1,m})-\frac{|E_{b}|}{2(t-1)(\sqrt{m}+t-1)}.

The contribution of the bad edges is

o(|E_{b}|/(\sqrt{m}+t-1))
, so the last three displays imply
\lambda(H)<\lambda(S_{t-1,m}),

a contradiction. Hence alternative (ii) cannot occur.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

For
t=2
, the result is exactly Theorem
1.4
, since

C_{4}=K_{2,2}
and
S_{1,m}
is the star with
m
edges. Hence
assume
t\geq 3
. Set
c_{t}:=\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose, for contradiction, that there is a
sequence of graphs
G_{n}
with

e(G_{n})=m_{n}\to\infty,

\lambda(G_{n})>\lambda(S_{t-1,m_{n}}),

and
\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(G_{n})\leq(c_{t}-\varepsilon)m_{n}^{t}.

Let
\eta:=1/(16t)
. Applying Lemma
3.6
to each

G_{n}
, we obtain a subgraph
H_{n}\subseteq G_{n}
with

m_{n}^{\prime}:=e(H_{n}),

\alpha_{n}:=\frac{m_{n}^{\prime}}{m_{n}},

and a unit Perron eigenvector
\bm{x}^{(n)}
, such that
H_{n}

satisfies the
\eta
-heavy-edge condition and

\lambda(H_{n})>\lambda(S_{t-1,m_{n}^{\prime}}).

Moreover,

\frac{\lambda(H_{n})}{\sqrt{m_{n}^{\prime}}}\geq 1+(1-4\eta)(\alpha_{n}^{-1/2}-1).

By Claim
3
, the numbers
\alpha_{n}
are bounded
away from
0
. Passing to a subsequence, assume

\alpha_{n}\to\alpha\in(0,1],

g_{n}:=\|\bm{x}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}(m_{n}^{\prime})^{1/4}

satisfies either
g_{n}=O(1)
or
g_{n}\to\infty
.

Case 1:
\alpha=1
.

Then
(m_{n}^{\prime})^{t}=(1+o(1))m_{n}^{t}
.

If
g_{n}=O(1)
, Lemma
5.2
gives

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H_{n})\geq\left(\frac{1}{4t}-o(1)\right)\lambda(H_{n})^{2t}.

Since
\lambda(H_{n})>\lambda(S_{t-1,m_{n}^{\prime}})\geq\sqrt{m_{n}^{\prime}}
, we get

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H_{n})\geq\left(\frac{1}{4t}-o(1)\right)(m_{n}^{\prime})^{t}.

As

\frac{1}{4t}\geq\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}=c_{t}

for all
t\geq 2
, this gives
\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(G_{n})\geq(c_{t}-o(1))m_{n}^{t},

contradicting the assumption.

Now suppose
g_{n}\to\infty
. By Theorem
4.4
,

H_{n}
admits an ACD partition

V(H_{n})=A_{n}\sqcup C_{n}\sqcup D_{n}.

Passing to a further subsequence, write

e(H_{n}[C_{n}])=(\beta+o(1))m_{n}^{\prime}

for some
\beta\in[0,1]
. If
\beta>0
, then
Lemma
5.3
gives

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H_{n})\geq\left(\frac{1}{4t}-o(1)\right)(m_{n}^{\prime})^{t}\geq(c_{t}-o(1))m_{n}^{t}.

If
\beta=0
, then Lemma
5.5
gives

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H_{n})\geq(c_{t}-o(1))(m_{n}^{\prime})^{t}=(c_{t}-o(1))m_{n}^{t}.

Again this contradicts the assumption.

Case 2:
\alpha<1
.

Discarding finitely many terms, assume

\alpha_{n}\leq 1-\varepsilon_{0}

for some fixed
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Put

\delta_{n}:=(1-4\eta)(\alpha_{n}^{-1/2}-1).

Then

\lambda(H_{n})\geq(1+\delta_{n})\sqrt{m_{n}^{\prime}}.

Since
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

\delta_{n}\to\delta:=(1-4\eta)(\alpha^{-1/2}-1)>0.

Choose
\zeta\in(0,1/3]
such that

(1+\delta_{n})^{2}\geq 1+\zeta

for all sufficiently large
n
. Lemma
4.10
gives

g_{n}=O(1)
. Therefore Lemma
5.2
yields

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H_{n})\geq\left(\frac{1}{4t}-o(1)\right)\lambda(H_{n})^{2t}\geq\left(\frac{1}{4t}-o(1)\right)(1+\delta_{n})^{2t}\alpha_{n}^{t}m_{n}^{t}.

It remains to compare the constant with
c_{t}
. Let

\beta_{n}:=\alpha_{n}^{-1/2}>1,
and
c:=1-4\eta=1-\frac{1}{4t}.

Then

(1+\delta_{n})^{2t}\alpha_{n}^{t}=\left(\frac{1+c(\beta_{n}-1)}{\beta_{n}}\right)^{2t}.

Since
\beta_{n}>1
,

\frac{1+c(\beta_{n}-1)}{\beta_{n}}>c.

Hence

\frac{1}{4t}(1+\delta_{n})^{2t}\alpha_{n}^{t}>\frac{1}{4t}\left(1-\frac{1}{4t}\right)^{2t}.

By Bernoulli’s inequality,

\left(1-\frac{1}{4t}\right)^{2t}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Thus the limiting coefficient is greater than
1/(8t)
. For

t\geq 3
,

\frac{1}{8t}>\frac{(t-1)!}{2t^{t}}=c_{t},

because
4(t-1)!<t^{t-1}
. Therefore

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(G_{n})\geq\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(H_{n})\geq(c_{t}+\Omega_{t}(1)-o(1))m_{n}^{t},

contradicting

\texttt{\#}C_{2t}(G_{n})\leq(c_{t}-\varepsilon)m_{n}^{t}.

This completes the proof.
∎

6
Concluding remarks

We conclude with a few directions to explore. The main phenomenon is that the same edge-spectral threshold
can have different sharp extremal mechanisms: for
K_{t,t}
, the sharp
constant is attained by balanced random graphs on
(2+o(1))\sqrt{m}

vertices, while for
C_{2t}
, it is attained by split graphs.

Second-order
\bm{C_{4}}
supersaturation.

Let
f_{4}(m)
be the minimum number of copies of
C_{4}
in an

m
-edge graph
G
satisfying
\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
. Theorem

1.4
gives
f_{4}(m)=\left(\frac{1}{8}-o(1)\right)m^{2}.

Two different near-extremal constructions, one clique-based and one
split-based, suggest the same second-order term (see
[
22
]
). It would be interesting
to determine whether

f_{4}(m)=\frac{1}{8}m^{2}-\frac{1}{4}m^{3/2}+o(m^{3/2}).

Odd cycles above split thresholds.

For fixed
t\geq 2
, it is known that the split graph
S_{t,m}
is the
edge-spectral extremal construction for forbidding
C_{2t+1}
. The
natural supersaturation problem is to determine the sharp asymptotic
constant

\inf_{\lambda(G)>\lambda(S_{t,m})}\frac{\texttt{\#}C_{2t+1}(G)}{m^{t}}

as
m\to\infty
. Unlike the even-cycle case, the extremal mechanism is
not clear from the present methods.

Which Sidorenko graphs have split extremizers?

The equivalence theorem shows that every Sidorenko graph
v(H)\leq e(H)
admits a
spectral Sidorenko inequality.
We developed a unified approach to establishing edge-spectral supersaturation for every Sidorenko graph
H
when the split graph is an extremal
H
-free graph.
A broader problem is to determine, for
bipartite Sidorenko graphs
H
, the sharp edge-spectral supersaturation
constant above the relevant threshold and to classify the
asymptotic extremizers. The results of this paper show that both
balanced random graphs and split graphs can be sharp, even among very
classical Sidorenko graphs. Are there other asymptotic extremizers?
