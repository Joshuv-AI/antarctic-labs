Semijoins of Annotated Relations
Phokion G. Kolaitis

Abstract
The semijoin operation is a fundamental operation of relational algebra that has been extensively used in query processing. Furthermore, semijoins have been used to formulate desirable properties of acyclic schemas; in particular, a schema is acyclic if and only if it has a full reducer, i.e., a sequence of semijoins that converts a given collection of relations to a globally consistent collection of relations. In recent years, the study of acyclicity has been extended to annotated relations, where the annotations are values from some positive commutative monoid. So far, however, it has not been known if the characterization of acyclicity in terms of full reducers extends to annotated relations. Here, we develop a theory of semijoins of annotated relations. To this effect, we first introduce the notion of a semijoin function on a monoid and then characterize the positive commutative monoids for which a semijoin function exists. After this, we introduce the notion of a full reducer for a schema on a monoid and show that the following is true for every positive commutative monoid that has the inner consistency property: a schema is acyclic if and only if it has a full reducer on that monoid.



1
Introduction

The semijoin operation has been extensively studied since the early days of relational databases, where the semijoin
R\ltimes T
of two relations
R
and
T
is the relation consisting of the tuples in
R
that join with at least one tuple in
T
.
Bernstein and Chiu
[
6
]
and then Bernstein and Goodman
[
7
]

were the first to investigate the semijoin operation as a tool in query processing over distributed databases.
The advantage of using semijoins is that, in certain cases, semijoins make it possible to compute the answers to queries while avoiding intermediate computations of expensive joins. After this, Yannakakis
[
22
]
showed that the join of a collection of relations over an acyclic schema can be efficiently computed using semijoins only. In the aforementioned papers, the notion of a
semijoin program
underlies the main results, where a semijoin program is a finite sequence of assignment statements of the form

R_{i}:=R_{i}\ltimes R_{j}
, for some relations
R_{i}
and
R_{j}
. Beeri, Fagin, Maier, and Yannakakis
[
5
]
characterized acyclic schemas in terms of several different desirable semantic properties.
Two key such properties are the
local-to-global consistency property
and the existence of a
full reducer
. Recall that two relations
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are consistent if there is a relation
W
such that the projection of
W
on the attributes of
R_{i}
is equal to
R_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. By definition, a database schema has the local-to-global consistency property if every collection
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
of pairwise consistent relations over such schemas is globally consistent (i.e., there is a relation
W
whose projection on the attributes of
R_{i}
is equal to
R_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq m
). Also by definition, a database schema has a full reducer if there is a semijoin program
\pi
such that for every collection
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
of relations over the schema, the output of
\pi
on input
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
is a globally consistent collection
R_{1}^{*},\ldots,R_{m}^{*}

of relations. The main result in Beeri et al.
[
5
]

asserts that the following are equivalent for a schema
H
: (i)
H
is acyclic; (ii)
H
has the local-to-global consistency property; (iii) H has a full reducer.

During the past two decades, a body of research on annotated databases has been developed, where in an annotated database each tuple in a relation is annotated with a value from some algebraic structure. Following the influential work on database provenance by Green, Karvounarakis, and Tannen
[
12
]
, several different aspects of annotated databases have been investigated, including
the study of conjunctive query containment for annotated databases
[
11
,
15
]
, the evaluation of Datalog programs on annotated databases
[
13
]
,
and the study of the semantics of first-order logic on annotated databases
[
9
,
10
]
. In this framework, the annotations are values in the universe of some fixed semiring
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,\times,0,1)
; this is a simultaneous generalization of standard relational databases, where the annotations are
1
and
0
, and of bag databases, where the annotations are non-negative integers denoting multiplicities of tuples. The term
\mathbb{K}
-relations refers to relations with annotations from a semiring
\mathbb{K}
; thus, standard relations are
\mathbb{B}
-relations, where
\mathbb{B}
is the Boolean semiring, while bags are
\mathbb{N}
-relations, where
\mathbb{N}
is the semiring of non-negative integers.

Atserias and Kolaitis
[
3
]
studied the interplay between local consistency and global consistency for annotated relations by addressing the following question: do the aforementioned results by Beeri et al.
[
5
]
characterizing acyclicity in terms of the local-to-global consistency property extend to annotated relations? Since the definition of consistency of annotated relations uses only the projection operation on relations and since projection is defined using only addition
+
, they considered
\mathbb{K}
-relations where the annotations come from a monoid
{\mathbb{K}=(K,+,0)}
.
They then identified the following condition on monoids: a monoid
\mathbb{K}
has the
inner consistency property
if whenever two
\mathbb{K}
-relations are inner consistent, then they are also consistent, where two
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R
and
T
are inner consistent if the projection of
R
on the common attributes of
R
and
T
coincides with the projection of
T
on the common attributes of
R
and
T
(note that consistency always implies inner consistency).
The first main result in
[
3
]
asserts that the following is true for every positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
that has the inner consistency property: a schema
H
is acyclic if and only if
H
has
the local-to-global consistency property for
\mathbb{K}
-relations. The second main result in
[
3
]
asserts that a positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
has the inner consistency property if and only if every acyclic schema has the local-to-global consistency property for
\mathbb{K}
.
In addition to the Boolean monoid and the bag monoid, a plethora of other monoids turned out to have the inner consistency property, including all monoids arising in the context of database provenance.

Summary of Results

In
[
3
]
, the following problem was raised: do the results by Beeri et al.
[
5
]
characterizing acyclicity in terms of the existence of a full reducer extend and how do they extend to annotated relations? As stated in
[
3
]
, the main difficulty is whether a suitable notion of semijoin can be defined on monoids. Our goal here is to resolve this open problem.

When one first considers this problem, one
may attempt to define a semijoin operation on
\mathbb{K}
-relations as the projection of the “join” of two
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R
and
T
on the attributes of
R
, where
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
is a monoid.
There are, however, two issues with this approach. First, to define the “join” of two
\mathbb{K}
-relations, one needs both an addition operation
+
and a multiplication operation
\times
on elements of
K
. Second, even if the monoid
\mathbb{K}=(K,+,0)
has an expansion to a semiring

(K,+,\times,0,1)
, the join operation arising from
+
and
\times
need not yield a “good” semijoin operation. A striking case in point is the bag semiring
{\mathbb{N}}=(N,+,\times,0,1)
of non-negative integers: as shown in
[
2
]
, there are consistent bags
R
and
T
such that the projection of the bag-join
R\Join T
on the attributes of
R
is different from
R
.

To overcome these issues, we adopt an axiomatic approach, that is, we introduce the notion of a
semijoin function on a monoid
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
by specifying four structural properties that such a function
\mathcal{S}
must obey (see Definition
1
for the details). The first property stipulates that if
R
and
T
are two consistent
\mathbb{K}
-relations, then
\mathcal{S}(R,T)=R
.
The other three properties make use of the canonical preorder
\sqsubseteq
of a monoid, where for all
a
and
b
in
K
, we have that
a\sqsubseteq b
if there is
some
c
in
K
such that
a+c=b
. Note that all four properties are satisfied by the semijoin of standard relations; thus, our notion of a semijoin function on a monoid generalizes indeed the semijoin operation in relational algebra.

Once the notion of a semijoin function is in place, we explore the existence of semijoin functions. To this effect, we identify a property of monoids, called the
production property
, which has an operations-research character (see the comments following Definition
3
). We then show that a positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
has a semijoin function if and only if
\mathbb{K}
has the production property. This result has several fruitful consequences. On the positive side, we show that if
\mathbb{K}
has the inner consistency property, then
\mathbb{K}
has the production property, hence
\mathbb{K}
also has a semijoin function. On the negative side, we show that no numerical semigroup other than the bag monoid
{\mathbb{N}}=(N,+,0)

has the production property, hence no numerical semigroup other than
\mathbb{N}
has a semijoin function (a numerical semigroup is a submonoid of
\mathbb{N}
whose universe consists of all but finitely many non-negative integers
[
1
]
). Further, we use the production property to show that the following problem is solvable in polynomial-time: given a positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
, does
\mathbb{K}
have a semijoin function?

Semijoin functions on monoids make it possible to give semantics to semijoin programs over monoids. In turn, this gives rise to the notion of a
full reducer
for a schema
H
on a monoid
\mathbb{K}
, i.e., a pair
(\pi,\mathcal{S})
consisting of a semijoin program
\pi
involving
H
and a semijoin function
\mathcal{S}
on
\mathbb{K}
such that for every collection
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
of
\mathbb{K}
-relation over
H
, the resulting collection
R_{1}^{*},\ldots,R_{m}^{*}
of
\mathbb{K}
-relations over
H
obtained by applying
\pi
and
\mathcal{S}
is globally consistent. Our main result asserts that the following is true for every positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
that has the inner consistency property: a schema
H
is acyclic if and only if
H
has a full reducer on
\mathbb{K}
. This generalizes the results in Beeri et al.
[
5
]
and yields a host of new results about annotated relations, including new results about bags, since the bag monoid
\mathbb{N}
has inner consistency property. Furthermore, we show that if
H
is an acyclic schema, then a single semijoin program
\pi
gives rise to a full reducer
(\pi,\mathcal{S})
for every semijoin function
\mathcal{S}
on any positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
that has the inner consistency property.

Finally, by combining the preceding results with results in
[
3
]
, we obtain a new characterization of the inner consistency property by showing that a positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
has the inner consistency property if and only if every acyclic schema has a full reducer on
\mathbb{K}
.

Basic Notions

Monoids

A
commutative monoid
is a structure
\mathbb{K}=(K,+,0)
, where
+
is a binary operation on the universe
K
of
\mathbb{K}
that is
associative, commutative, and has
0
as its neutral element, i.e.,
p+0=p=0+p
holds for all
p\in K
.
A commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}=(K,+,0)
is
positive

if for all elements
p,q\in K
with

p+q=0
, we have that
p=0
and
q=0
. From now on, we assume that all commutative monoids considered have at least two elements in their universe.

The following are examples of positive commutative monoids.

•

The
Boolean monoid

\mathbb{B}=(\{0,1\},\vee,0)
with disjunction
\vee
as its operation and
0
(false) as its neutral element.

•

The
bag monoid

\mathbb{N}=(N,+,0)
, where
N
is the set of non-negative integers and
+
is the standard addition operation.
Note that the structure
{\mathbb{Z}}=(Z,+,0)
, where
Z
is the set of all integers, is a commutative monoid, but not a positive one.

•

A
numerical semigroup
is a submonoid
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
of the bag monoid
\mathbb{N}=(N,+,0)
, such that
K
is a cofinite set, i.e., the complement
N\setminus K
is finite. A concrete example of a numerical semigroup is
{\mathbb{K}}_{3,5}=(\langle 3,5\rangle,+,0)
, where
\langle 3,5\rangle
is the set of all non-negative integers of the form
3m+5n
with
m\geq 0
and
n\geq 0
, i.e.,
\langle 3,5\rangle=\{0,3,5,6,8,9,10,\ldots\}
.

•

The
power set monoid

{\mathbb{P}}(A)=(\mathcal{P}(A),\cup,\emptyset)
, where
if
A
is a set, then
\mathcal{P}(A)
is its powerset, and
\cup
is the union operation on sets.

•

The
fuzzy monoid

{\mathbb{V}}=([0,1],\max,0)
, where
[0,1]
is the interval of all real numbers between
0
and
1
, and
\max
is the standard maximum operation.

•

The structure
{\mathbb{R}}^{\geq 0}=([0,\infty),+,0)
, where
[0,\infty)

is the set of all non-negative real numbers and
+
is the standard addition operation.

•

The structure
{\mathbb{T}}=((-\infty,\infty],\min,\infty)
, where
(-\infty,\infty]
is the set of all real numbers together with
\infty
, and
\min
is the standard minimum operation.

\mathbb{K}
-relations and marginals of
\mathbb{K}
-relations

An
attribute

A
is a symbol with an associated
set
{\mathrm{Dom}}(A)
as its
domain
. If
X
is a finite set of

{\mathrm{Tup}}(X)
is the set
of
X
-tuples
, i.e., the set of
functions that take each attribute
A\in X
to an element of its
domain
{\mathrm{Dom}}(A)
.
{\mathrm{Tup}}(\emptyset)
is non-empty as it
contains the
empty tuple
, i.e., the function with empty

Y\subseteq X
and
t
is
an
X
-tuple, then the
projection of
t
on
Y
, denoted
by

, is the unique

-tuple that agrees with

on

. In

Let
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
be a positive commutative monoid and let

•

A

•

The

We will often write

When

-relation over

.

•

If

, then the

)

-relation

The value

. For notational simplicity, we will often write

is indeed the marginal of

(in which case

is the actual value of

on

Note that if

is a standard relation (i.e.,

.

The proof of the next useful proposition follows easily from the definitions.

Proposition 1
.

Let

1.

Schemas and hypergraphs

•

A

•

A

, where

. We call

In what follows,

•

A

-relations

3
Background on Consistency and Acyclicity

Let
\mathbb{K}=(K,+,0)
be a positive commutative monoid.

•

Two
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R(X)
and
T(Y)
are
consistent
if there is a
\mathbb{K}
-relation
W(X\cup Y)
with
W[X]=R
and
W[Y]=T
.
Such a
\mathbb{K}
-relation
W
is a
consistency witness
for
R
and
T
.

•

A collection
R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
of

\mathbb{K}
-relations over a schema

X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
is
globally consistent
if there is a
\mathbb{K}
-relation
W(X_{1}\cup\ldots\cup X_{m})
such that

W[X_{i}]=R_{i}
, for
i
with
1\leq i\ \leq m
. Such a
\mathbb{K}
-relation
W
is a
consistency witness
for
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
.

Note that if
R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})

is a globally consistent collection of
\mathbb{K}
-relations, then these relations
are pairwise consistent. Indeed, if
W
is a consistency witness for

R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
, then for all
i
and
j
with
1\leq i,j\leq m
, we have that the
\mathbb{K}
-relation
W[X_{i}\cup X_{j}]
is a consistency witness for
R_{i}

and
R_{j}
, because

R_{i}=W[X_{i}]=W[X_{i}\cup X_{j}][X_{i}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad R_{j}=W[X_{j}]=W[X_{i}\cup X_{j}][X_{j}],

where, in each case, the first equality follows from the definition of global consistency and the second equality follows from the second part of Proposition
1
.
The converse fails even for standard relations (i.e.,
\mathbb{B}
-relations).
For example, consider the

triangle
schema
\{A,B\},\{B,C\},\{C,A\}
and the standard relations
R_{1}(A,B)=\{(0,0),(1,1)\}
,
R_{2}(B,C)=\{(0,1),(1,0)\}
,

R_{3}(C,A)=\{(0,0),(1,1)\}
. It is easy to check that
R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}
are pairwise consistent; however, they are not globally consistent since if they were, then their join
((R_{1}\Join R_{2})\Join R_{3})
would be a consistency witness for them, but
((R_{1}\Join R_{2})\Join R_{3})=\emptyset
.

Beeri et al.
[
5
]
characterized the schemas for which every collection of pairwise consistent standard relations is globally consistent by showing that these are precisely the
acyclic
schemas (also known as
\alpha
-
acyclic
schemas). Intuitively, the notion of an acyclic hypergraph generalizes to hypergraphs the property that
a graph is acyclic if and only if every connected component of it with at least two edges has an articulation point. We will not give the precise definition of acyclicity here because we will not use it in the sequel. Instead, we will give the precise definitions of two different notions that turned out to be equivalent to acyclicity.

Let
H
be a hypergraph with
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
as its hyperedges.

•

H
has the
running intersection property
if there is an ordering
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
of the hyperedges of
H
such that for every
i\leq m
, there is a
j<i
such that
(Y_{1}\cup\cdots\cup Y_{i-1})\cap Y_{i}\subseteq Y_{j}
.

•

H
has the
local-to-global consistency property for standard relations
if every collection

R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
of pairwise consistent standard relations over
H
is also globally consistent.

Theorem 1

(
[
5
]
)
.

For every hypergraph
H
, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

H
is acyclic.

2.

H
has the running intersection property.

3.

H
has the local-to-global consistency property for standard relations.

As an illustration of Theorem
1
,
the triangle schema
\{A,B\},\{B,C\},\{C,A\}
is cyclic;
therefore, Theorem
1
predicts that the triangle schema does not have the local-to-global consistency property for standard relations, which we showed to be the case earlier.
For every
n\geq 2
, the
n
-path
schema
P_{n}
with hyperedges

\{A_{1},A_{2}\},\{A_{2},A_{3}\},\ldots,\{A_{n},A_{n+1}\}
is acyclic because it has the running intersection property via this ordering.
Finally, consider the schema
\{A,B,C\},\{C,D,E\},\{E,F,A\},\{A,C,E\}
. It has the running intersection property via the ordering

\{A,B,C\},\{A,C,E\},\{C,D,E\},\{E,F,A\}
, hence it is acyclic (and so, by Theorem
1
, it has the local-to-global consistency property property for standard relations).

In
[
3
]
, the following question was investigated: does Theorem
1
extend from standard relations to
\mathbb{K}
-relations, where
\mathbb{K}
is an arbitrary positive commutative monoid?
Clearly, the first two statements in Theorem
1
are “structural” properties that depend on the hypergraph
H
only; in contrast, the third statement is a “semantic” property, as it also involves standard relations. The local-to-global consistency property property for standard relations has the following natural generalization to relations over monoids.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a positive commutative monoid.

•

We say that a hypergraph
H
with
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
as its hyperedges has the
local-to-global consistency property for
\mathbb{K}
-relations
if every collection
R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
of pairwise consistent
\mathbb{K}
-relations is also globally consistent.

In
[
3
]
, it was that shown that the acyclicity of a hypergraph
H
is a necessary, but not always sufficient, condition for
H
to have the local-to-global consistency property for
\mathbb{K}
-relations, where
\mathbb{K}
is an arbitrary positive commutative monoid.
Nonetheless,
it was also shown in
[
3
]
that Theorem
1
generalizes to every positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
that satisfies
a condition called the
inner consistency property
.

•

Two
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R(X)
and
T(Y)
are
inner consistent
if

R[X\cap Y]=T[X\cap Y]
.

•

We say that
\mathbb{K}
has the
inner consistency property
if whenever two
\mathbb{K}
-relations are inner consistent, they are also consistent.

Note that, using Proposition
1
, it is easy to verify that if
R
and
S
are consistent
\mathbb{K}
-relations, then they are also inner consistent. Consequently, for monoids with the inner consistency property, the notions of consistency and inner consistency coincide.
In particular, this holds true for the
the Boolean monoid
{\mathbb{B}}=(\{0,1\},\vee,0)
.

We can now state one of the main results in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a positive commutative monoid that has the inner consistency property. For every hypergraph
H
,
the following statements are equivalent:

1.

H
is acyclic.

2.

H
has the local-to-global consistency property for
\mathbb{K}
-relations.

The bag monoid
{\mathbb{N}}=(N,+,0)
was shown to have the inner consistency property
in
[

]
.
Several other types of monoids were shown to have the inner consistency property in
[

]
; concrete examples include the monoids

{\mathbb{V}}=([0,1],\max,0)
,
{\mathbb{R}}^{\geq 0}=([0,\infty),+,0)
,

{\mathbb{T}}=((-\infty,\infty],\min,\infty)
, and the power set monoids
{\mathbb{P}}(A)=(\mathcal{P}(A),\cup,\emptyset)
, for every set

. In contrast, no numerical semigroup other than the bag monoid
{\mathbb{N}}=(N,+,0)
has the inner consistency property; in particular, the monoid

lacks this property.

As shown in
[

]
, the inner consistency property actually characterizes the monoids

for which every acyclic hypergraph has the local-to-global consistency property for

-relations.

(

.

Let

\mathbb{K}

The

4
Semijoin Functions

By definition, the
semijoin

R\ltimes T
of two standard relations
R(X)

and
T(Y)
is the standard relation over
X
consisting of all tuples in
R(X)
that join with at least one tuple from
T(Y)
. Thus,
R\ltimes T=(R\Join T)[X]
, that is to say, the semijoin of
R
and
T
is the projection of the join of
R
with
T
on the set of attributes of
R
.

In this section, we address the question: is there a notion of a semijoin of two
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R(X)
and
T(Y)
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a positive commutative monoid? To this effect,
we introduce the notion of a
semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
by identifying certain desirable properties that such a function ought to obey. We give an explicit semijoin function for a certain class of monoids and then
characterize the positive commutative monoids for which a semijoin function exists. Furthermore, we
prove that if a positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
has the inner consistency property, then a semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
always exists.

Before proceeding, we need to bring to the front the notion of the canonical pre-order of a monoid.
Let
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
be a positive commutative monoid.
Consider the
binary relation
\sqsubseteq
on
K
defined as follows: for all
a,b\in K
, we have that
a\sqsubseteq b
if and only if there exists some
c\in K
such that
a+c=b
. It is easy to see that
\sqsubseteq
is a reflexive and
transitive relation; hence, it is a pre-order, called the
canonical pre-order
on
\mathbb{K}
(see,
[
8
, Section 3.3]
).

Let
R(X)
and
S(X)
be two

\mathbb{K}
-relations over the set
X
. We write
R\sqsubseteq S
to denote that

R(t)\sqsubseteq S(t)
holds,
for every
X
-tuple
t
.
Clearly, this is a pre-order on
\mathbb{K}
-relations over
X
.

We are now ready to introduce the notion of a semijoin function on a monoid.

Definition 1
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
be a positive commutative monoid. A
semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
is a function
\mathcal{S}
that takes as arguments two
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R(X)
and
T(Y)
, and returns as value a
\mathbb{K}
-relation
\mathcal{S}(R,T)
over
X
with the following four properties:

(P1)

If
R
and
T
are consistent, then

\mathcal{S}(R,T)=R
.

(P2)

\mathcal{S}(R,T)\sqsubseteq R
.

(P3)

\mathcal{S}(R,T)[X\cap Y]\sqsubseteq T[X\cap Y]
.

(P4)

If
T[X\cap Y]\sqsubseteq R[X\cap Y]
, then

\mathcal{S}(R,T)[X\cap Y]=T[X\cap Y]
.

We say that
\mathbb{K}
has a semijoin function
if at least one semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
exists.

Example 1
.

We now give an example and a non-example of a semijoin function.

1.

If
\mathbb{B}=(\{0,1\},\vee,0)
is the Boolean monoid, then
the semijoin operation
\ltimes
on standard relations is
an example of a semijoin function on
\mathbb{B}
. Indeed, property
\sf{(P1)}
holds for
\ltimes
because if two standard relations
R(X)
and
T(Y)
are consistent, then their join
R\Join T
witnesses their consistency, hence
R\ltimes T=(R\Join T)[X]=R
. For properties
\sf{(P2)}
,
\sf{(P3)}
,

\sf{(P4)}
, first note that the relation
\sqsubseteq
on
\mathbb{B}
is just
set-theoretic containment
\subseteq
. Properties
\sf{(P2)}
,
\sf{(P3)}
,

\sf{(P4)}
can now be proved easily using the definitions of the semijoin
\ltimes
and the join
\Join
of two standard relations. For instance, towards proving property
\sf{(P4)}
, assume that
T[X\cap Y]\subseteq R[X\cap Y]
. We have to show that

R\ltimes S[X\cap Y]=T[X\cap Y]
. In view of property
\sf{(P3)}
, it suffices to show that
T[X\cap Y]\subseteq(R\ltimes T)[X\cap Y]
. Take a tuple

t\in T[X\cap Y]
. Then there is a
Y
-tuple
t_{1}\in T
such that
t_{1}[X\cap Y]=t
. Since
T[X\cap Y]\subseteq R[X\cap Y]
, we have that
t\in R[X\cap Y]
, hence there is a
X
-tuple
t_{2}\in R
such that
t_{2}[X\cap Y]=t
. Therefore,
t_{2}
joins with
t_{1}
, hence
t_{2}\in R\ltimes T
and so
t=t_{2}[X\cap Y]\in(R\ltimes T)[X\cap Y]
.

2.

If
{\mathbb{N}}=(N,+,0)
is the bag monoid, then the semijoin
R\ltimes_{\mathbb{N}}T
of two bags
R(X)
and
T(Y)
is not a semijoin function on
\mathbb{N}
.
Here,

R\ltimes_{\mathbb{N}}T=(R\Join_{\mathbb{N}}T)[X]
, where
R\Join_{\mathbb{N}}T
is the
bag-join
of
R
and
T
, i.e., for every
(X\cup Y)
-tuple
t
, we have that

(R\Join_{\mathbb{N}}T)(t)=R(t[X])\cdot T(t[Y])
.
The reason that
\ltimes_{\mathbb{N}}
is not a semijoin function on
\mathbb{N}
is that
\ltimes_{\mathbb{N}}
does not have property
\sf{(P1)}
. Indeed, as pointed out in
[
2
]
, the bag-join of two consistent bags need not witness their consistency, hence property
\sf{(P1)}
fails for such bags. In particular, this is the case for the bags
R(A,B)=\{(1,2):1,(2,2):1\}
and
T(B,C)=\{(2,1):1,(2,2):1\}
. They are consistent but it is easy to check that
R\ltimes_{\mathbb{N}}T=\{(1,2):2,(2,2):2\}\not=R
.

As defined in
[
4
]
,
a
consistency witness function on
\mathbb{K}
is a function
W
that takes as arguments two
\mathbb{K}
-relation
R(X)
and
T(Y)
, and returns as value a
\mathbb{K}
-relation
W(R,T)
over
X\cup Y
such that if
R
and
T
are consistent
\mathbb{K}
-relations, then
W(R,T)
is a consistency witness for
R
and
T
. For example, the join
R\Join T
of two standard relations
R(X)
and
T(Y)
is a consistency witness function on
\mathbb{B}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a positive commutative monoid and let
W
be a consistency witness function on
\mathbb{K}
. We say that
W
is
coherent
if the function
{\mathcal{S}}_{W}

is a semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
, where
{\mathcal{S}}_{W}
takes as input two
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R(X)
and
T(Y)
, and returns as value the projection of
W(R,T)
on
X
, that is,
{\mathcal{S}}_{W}(R,T)=W(R,T)[X].

We say that
\mathbb{K}

has a coherent consistency witness function
if at least one coherent consistency witness function on
\mathbb{K}
exists.

Clearly, the join
\Join
of two standard relations is a coherent consistency witness function on
\mathbb{B}
,
since
\mathcal{S}_{\Join}
is the semijoin function

\ltimes
on standard relations.
In contrast, let
W
be the consistency witness function on standard relations such that

W(R,T)=R\Join T
, if
R
,
T
are consistent, and
W(R,T)=\emptyset
, if
R
,
T
are not consistent. Then
W
is not coherent because
{\mathcal{S}}_{W}
does not have property
{\sf(P4)}
; observe, though, that
{\mathcal{S}}_{W}
has properties
{\sf(P1)}
,
{\sf(P2)}
,
{\sf(P3)}
.

The next result gives a sufficient condition for the existence of an explicit coherent consistency witness function; hence, it also gives a sufficient condition for the existence of an explicit semijoin function.

Proposition 2
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
be a positive commutative monoid.
If
\mathbb{K}
has an expansion to a bounded distributive lattice
{\mathbb{K}^{\prime}}=(K,+,\times,0,1)
, then the
standard join
\Join_{{\mathbb{K}^{\prime}},S}
is a coherent consistency witness function on
\mathbb{K}
, where for
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R(X)
and
T(Y)
and for every
(X\cup Y)
-tuple
t
, we have that

(R\Join_{{\mathbb{K}^{\prime}},S}T)(t)=R(t[X])\times T[t[Y]).

Proof.

Let
R(X)
and
T(Y)
be two
\mathbb{K}
-relations.
To keep the notation light, assume that
X=\{A,B\}
and
Y=\{B,C\}
; other than a heavier notation, the general case is proved the same way.

Assume that the monoid
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
has an expansion to a bounded distributive lattice
{\mathbb{K}^{\prime}}=(K,+,\times,0,1)
. Let
W
be the standard join
\Join_{{\mathbb{K}^{\prime}},S}
, where for
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R(X)
and
T(Y)
and for every
(X\cup Y)
-triple
(a,b,c)
, we have that

W(R,S)(a,b,c)=(R\Join_{{\mathbb{K}^{\prime}},S}T)(a,b,c)=R(a,b)\times T(b,c).

As shown in
[
3
]
,
W
is a consistency witness function on
\mathbb{K}
. To show that

W
is coherent, we must show that the function
\mathcal{S}_{W}
with

\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)=W(R,T)[X]
is a semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
, i.e., we must show that
\mathcal{S}_{W}
has properties
\sf{(P1)}
,
\sf{(P2)}
,
\sf{(P3)}
, and
\sf{(P4)}
in Definition
1
. Note that, since
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
is a bounded distributive lattice, the following facts hold:

1.

For all
u\in K
, we have that
u\times u=u
.

2.

For all
u,v\in K
, we have that

u\times v\sqsubseteq u
and

u\times v\sqsubseteq v
.

3.

For all
u,v,w\in K
, if
u\sqsubseteq v
, then
u\times w\sqsubseteq v\times w
.

Property
\sf{(P1)}
:
\mathcal{S}_{W}
has property
\sf{(P1)}
because
W
is a consistency witness function.

Property
\sf{(P2)}
: We must show that
\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)\sqsubseteq R
, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)(a,b)\sqsubseteq R(a,b)
holds, for every
X
-pair
(a,b)
.
For every such pair
(a,b)
, we have that

\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)(a,b)=W(R,T)[X](a,b)=\sum_{c}W(R,T)(a,b,c)=\sum_{c}R(a,b)\times T(b,c)=

=R(a,b)\times\big(\sum_{c}T(b,c)\big)~\sqsubseteq~R(a,b),

where the above equalities hold by the definitions of
W
and
\mathcal{S}_{W}
, the definition of the projection operation, and the distributivity of
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
, while the last inequality
\sqsubseteq
holds by fact 2 about the bounded distributive lattice
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
.

Property
\sf{(P3)}
: We must show that
\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)[B]\sqsubseteq T[B]
, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)[B](b)\sqsubseteq T[B](b)
holds, for every
B
-element
b
. For every such element
b
, we have that

\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)[B](b)=(W(R,T)[X][B])(b)=W(R,T)[B](b)=\sum_{a,c}W(R,T)(a,b,c)=

\sum_{a,c}R(a,b)\times T(b,c)=\sum_{a}R(a,b)\times\big(\sum_{c}T(b,c)\big)=\big(\sum_{a}R(a,b)\big)\times T[B](b)=

R[B](b)\times T[B](b)~\sqsubseteq~T[B](b),

where the above equalities hold by the definitions of
W
and
\mathcal{S}_{W}
, the definition of the projection operation, part 2 of Proposition
1
, and the distributivity of
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
, while the inequality
\sqsubseteq
holds by fact 2 about the bounded distributive lattice
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
.

Property
\sf{(P4)}
: Assume that

T[B]\sqsubseteq R[B]
holds. We must show that

\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)[B]=T[B]
holds as well. In view of property
\sf{(P3)}
, it suffices to show that
T[B]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)[B]
, i.e, it suffices to show that
T[B](b)\sqsubseteq\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)[B](b)
holds, for every
B
-element
b
. As seen in the proof of proof of property
\sf{(P3)}
, for every
B
-element
b
, we have that

\mathcal{S}_{W}(R,T)[B](b)=R[B](b)\times T[B](b).

Since we assumed that

T[B]\sqsubseteq R[B]
holds, we have that
T[B](b)\sqsubseteq R[B](b)
.
Hence, by using the facts 1 and 3 about the bounded distributive lattice
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
, we have that

T[B](b)=T[B](b)\times T[B](b)~\sqsubseteq~R[B](b)\times T[B](b)=\mathcal{S}_{W}[B](b).

This completes the proof of property
\sf{(P4)}
and
the proof of the propositions.
∎

Example 2
.

We now give two examples that illustrate the applicability of Proposition
2
.

1.

For every set
A
, the powerset monoid
{\mathbb{P}}(A)=(\mathcal{P}(A),\cup,\emptyset)
expands to the bounded distributive lattice
(\mathcal{P}(A),\cup,\cap,\emptyset,A)
. In this case, the standard join is the coherent consistency witness function
W
such that

W(R,S)(t)=R(t[X])\cap S(t[Y]).

2.

The fuzzy monoid
{\mathbb{V}}=([0,1],\max,0)
expands to the bounded distributive lattice

([0,1],\max,\min,0,1))
. In this case, the standard join is the coherent consistency witness function
W
such that

W(R,S)(t)=\min\{R(t[X]),S(t[Y])\}.

So far, we have not exhibited a semijoin function for the bag monoid
{\mathbb{N}}=(N,+,0)
. The existence of such a function on
\mathbb{N}
will follow from the results in this section. For now, we point out that there are positive commutative monoids for which no semijoin function exists.

Proposition 3
.

The numerical semigroup
{\mathbb{K}}_{3,5}=(\langle 3,5\rangle,+,0)
has no semijoin function, where
\langle 3,5\rangle=\{3m+5n:m,n\geq 0\}
,

Proof.

Towards a contradiction, assume that
\mathcal{S}
is a semijoin function on
{\mathbb{K}}_{3,5}
. Let
U
,
V
be two attributes.
Let
R(U,V)
be the
{\mathbb{K}}_{3,5}
-relation with
R(u_{1},v)=3
,
R(u_{2},v)=3
, and
R(z,w)=0
, for all other pairs. Let
T(V)

be the
{\mathbb{K}}_{3,5}
-relation with
T(v)=5
and
T(z)=0
, if
z\not=v
. Then
T[V]\sqsubseteq R[V]
, since
T[V]=T
and
R[V]
is the
{\mathbb{K}}_{3,5}
-relation with

R[V](v)=6
and
R[V](z)=0
, if
z\not=v
. Consider the value
\mathcal{S}(R,T)
of the semijoin function
\mathcal{S}
on the pair
(R,T)
. By property
\sf{(P4)}
and since
T[V]\sqsubseteq R[V]
, we must have that

\mathcal{S}(R,T)[V]=T[V]=T
. By property
\sf{(P2)}
, we must have that
\mathcal{S}(R,T)\sqsubseteq R
, which is impossible because there do not exist two elements in the set
\langle 3,5\rangle
such that each is at most
3
and their sum is
5
.
∎

Our first goal is to characterize the positive commutative monoids for which a semijoin function exists. Towards this goal, we introduce a combinatorial property of monoids.

Definition 3
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
be a positive commutative monoid.

•

We say that
\mathbb{K}
has the
production property for a natural number
n\geq 1

if for all elements
b,c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n}
in
K
such that
b\sqsubseteq c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots+c_{n}
, there are elements
d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n}
in
K
such that
d_{1}\sqsubseteq c_{1},d_{2}\sqsubseteq c_{2},\ldots,d_{n}\sqsubseteq c_{n}
, and
d_{1}+d_{2}+\cdots+c_{n}=b
.

•

We say that
\mathbb{K}
has the
production property
if it has the production property for every natural number
n\geq 1
.

Note that the production property for
n=1
is trivial.
The term “production property” was chosen because this property has a motivation from operations research. Consider a company that produces a product at
n
different locations and assume that the maximum production capacity at location
i
is
c_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
. Suppose that the total demand for the product is
b
, where
b\sqsubseteq c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}
. The production property for
n
asserts that there is a production policy for the company so that the total production at the
n
locations meets precisely the demand with no waste whatsoever (i.e., there is no excess production).

Observe that the proof of Proposition
3
actually shows that the numerical semigroup
{\mathbb{K}}_{3,5}
does not have the production property for
n=2
; this is witnessed by
c_{1}=3
,
c_{2}=3
, and
b=5
. As we shall see next, it is no accident that
{\mathbb{K}}_{3,5}
has no semijoin function and at the same time lacks the production property for
n=2
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a positive commutative monoid. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\mathbb{K}
has a coherent consistency witness function.

2.

\mathbb{K}
has a semijoin function.

3.

\mathbb{K}
has the production property for
n=2

4.

\mathbb{K}
has the production property.

Proof.

We will prove this theorem in a round-robin style.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)

Follows immediately from Definition
2
of a coherent consistency witness function.

(2)\Longrightarrow(3)
Assume that
\mathcal{S}
is a semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
. Given elements
b,c_{1},c_{2}
in
K
such that
b\sqsubseteq c_{1}+c_{2}
, consider the
\mathbb{K}
-relations
R(U,V)

and
T(V)
such that

•

R(u_{1},v)=c_{1}
,
R(u_{2},v)=c_{2}
, and
R(z,w)=0
, for all other pairs.

•

T(v)=b
and
T(z)=0
, for all
z\not=v
.

Since
b\sqsubseteq c_{1}+c_{2}
, we have that

T[V]\sqsubseteq R[V]
. Hence, by property
\sf{(P4)}
of the semijoin function
\mathcal{S}
, we must have that

\mathcal{S}(R,T)[V]=T[V]=T
. By property
\sf{(P2)}
of the semijoin function
\mathcal{S}
, we must have that
\mathcal{S}(R,T)\sqsubseteq R
. Hence,
\mathcal{S}(R,T)
must be a
\mathbb{K}
-relation
of the form
\mathcal{S}(R,T)(u_{1},v)=d_{1}
,
\mathcal{S}(R,T)(u_{2},v)=d_{2}
, and
\mathcal{S}(R,T)(z,w)=0
, for all other pairs, where
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
are elements of
K
such that
d_{1}\sqsubseteq c_{1}
,
d_{2}\sqsubseteq c_{2}
. Furthermore, since
\mathcal{S}(R,T)[V]=T[V]=T
, we must have that
d_{1}+d_{2}=b
. Thus,
\mathbb{K}
has the production property for
n=2
.

(3)\Longrightarrow(4)

Assume that
\mathbb{K}
has the production property for
n=2
. We have to show that
\mathbb{K}
has the production property for every
k\geq 1
. As mentioned earlier, the production property for
k=1
holds trivially. By induction on
k
, assume that
\mathbb{K}
has the production property for
k
. We will show that
\mathbb{K}
has the production property for
k+1
. Towards this, assume that
b,c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k},c_{k+1}
are elements in
K
such that
b\sqsubseteq c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots+c_{k}+c_{k+1}
. We must show that there are elements
d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{k},d_{k+1}
in
K
such that
d_{1}\sqsubseteq c_{1},d_{2}\sqsubseteq c_{2},\ldots,d_{k}\sqsubseteq c_{k},d_{k+1}\sqsubseteq c_{k+1}
, and
d_{1}+d_{2}+\cdots+d_{k}+d_{k+1}=b
.
Let
c=c_{1}+c_{2}\cdots+c_{k}
. Since
b\sqsubseteq c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots+c_{k}+c_{k+1}
, we have that
b\sqsubseteq c+c_{k+1}
. Since
\mathbb{K}
has the production property for
n=2
, it follows that there are elements
d
and
d_{k+1}
in
K
such that

d\sqsubseteq c
,
d_{k+1}\sqsubseteq c_{k+1}
, and
b=d+d_{k+1}
. Since
c=c_{1}+c_{2}\cdots+c_{k}
, we have that

Theorem

provides a useful tool for determining the existence of semijoin functions. As an illustration,
for every

\mathbb{P}_{3}=(\{\emptyset,\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\},\{1,2,3\}\},\cup,\emptyset)

is a positive commutative monoid. We claim that no truncated powerset

has a semijoin function.
To see this, consider the sets

,

,

A broader consequence of Theorem

is the decidability of the existence of a semijoin function for finite positive commutative monoids.

Corollary 1
.

The following problem is decidable in cubic time: given a finite positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
, does
\mathbb{K}
have a semijoin function?
In fact, this problem is decidable in

.

Proof.

By Theorem

, this problem is equivalent to checking that
\mathbb{K}
has the production property for

; this can be checked in time

by examining all triples with elements in
K
one at a time. Clearly, this algorithm can be implemented in logarithmic space.
∎

Our next result asserts that the inner consistency property implies the existence of a semijoin function.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a positive commutative monoid.
If
\mathbb{K}
has the inner consistency property, then
\mathbb{K}
has a semijoin function.

Proof.

As shown in
[
3
]
, the inner consistency property is equivalent to the transportation property, defined as follows. We say that a positive commutative monoid

{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)

has the
transportation property
if
for every
m\geq 1
, every
n\geq 1
,
every
m
-vector
b=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in K^{m}

and
every
n
-vector
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in K^{n}

such that
b_{1}+\cdots+b_{m}=c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}
holds, there
is an
m\times n

matrix
D=(d_{ij}:i\in[m],j\in[n])\in K^{m\times n}
whose
rows sum to
b
and whose columns sum to
c
,
i.e.,
d_{i1}+\cdots+d_{in}=b_{i}
for all
i\in[m]

and
d_{1j}+\cdots+d_{mj}=c_{j}
for all
j\in[n]
.
Theorem 3 in
[
3
]
asserts that for a positive commutative monoid
\mathbb{K}
, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\mathbb{K}
has the inner consistency property.

2.

\mathbb{K}
has the transportation property.

We will now show that the transportation property implies the production property.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a positive commutative monoid that has the transportation property.
Assume that
n\geq 1
and
b,c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n}
are elements in
K
such that

b\sqsubseteq c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots,+c_{n}
.
Therefore, there is an element

b^{\prime}\in K
such that
b+b^{\prime}=c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots+c_{n}
.
Consider the vectors
(b,b^{\prime})
and
(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})
. Since
\mathbb{K}
has the transportation property, there are elements
d_{ij}\in K
, where
i=1,2
and
1\leq j\leq n
, such that the following equations hold:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}d_{11}&+&d_{12}&+&\cdots&+&d_{1n}&=&b\\
+&&+&&&&+&&\\
d_{21}&+&d_{22}&+&\cdots&+&d_{2n}&=&b^{\prime}\\
\shortparallel&&\shortparallel&&&&\shortparallel&&\\
c_{1}&&c_{2}&&&&c_{n}&&\end{array}

Since
d_{1j}+d_{2j}=c_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
, we have that
d_{1,j}\sqsubseteq c_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
. Thus, the elements
d_{1,1},d_{1,2},\ldots,d_{1n}
witness that
\mathbb{K}
has the production property.

We can now put everything together: if
\mathbb{K}
has the inner consistency, then, by Theorem 3 in
[
3
]
,
\mathbb{K}
has the transportation property. Therefore, by what was shown above,
\mathbb{K}
has the production property. Hence, by Theorem
4
,
\mathbb{K}
has a semijoin function.
∎

As immediate consequences of Theorem
5
,
the bag monoid
{\mathbb{N}}=(N,+,0)
, the monoid

{\mathbb{R}}^{\geq 0}=([0,\infty),+,0)
, and the monoid
{\mathbb{T}}=((-\infty,\infty],\min,\infty)

have semijoin functions, because these three monoids are known to have the inner consistency property

]

By Theorem
5
, the inner consistency property implies the existence of a semijoin function. The converse is not true.
Indeed,
let
{\mathbb{N}}_{2}=(\{0,1,2\},\oplus,0)
be the positive commutative monoid in which

is the neutral element and

1\oplus 1=2=1\oplus 2=2\oplus 1=2\oplus 2
. In
[
3
]
, it was shown that
{\mathbb{N}}_{2}
does not have the inner consistency property. It is easy to see that

{\mathbb{N}}_{2}
has the production property for

, so, by Theorem

,
{\mathbb{N}}_{2}

has a semijoin function. Thus, the inner consistency property is stricter than the existence of a semijoin function.

The last result in this section asserts that the existence of a semijoin function and the
cancellativity
property imply the inner consistency property.

•

We say that a monoid

{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
is

if for all elements

in

with

Proposition 4
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
be a positive commutative monoid. If
\mathbb{K}
is cancellative and has a semijoin function, then
\mathbb{K}
has the inner consistency property.

Proof.

By Theorem
5
and Theorem 3 in
[
3
]
, it suffices to show that if
\mathbb{K}
is cancellative and has the production property, then
\mathbb{K}
has the transportation property. It is also known
(see
[
3
, Proposition 10]
and
[
19
, page 508]
)
that
\mathbb{K}
has the transportation property if and only if
\mathbb{K}
has the

transportation property, that is, for all
(b_{1},b_{2})\in K^{2}
and for
(c_{1},c_{2})\in K^{2}
with
b_{1}+b_{2}=c_{1}+c_{2}
, there are elements
d_{11},d_{12},d_{21},d_{22}
in
k
such that

\displaystyle d_{11}+d_{12}

\displaystyle=b_{1}

\displaystyle d_{21}+d_{22}

\displaystyle=b_{2}

\displaystyle d_{11}+d_{21}

\displaystyle=c_{1}

\displaystyle d_{12}+d_{22}

\displaystyle=c_{2}.

So, assume that
\mathbb{K}
is cancellative and has the production property. We will show that
\mathbb{K}
has the
2\times 2
transportation property. Towards this, assume that
b_{1},b_{2},c_{1},c_{2}
are elements in
K
such that
b_{1}+b_{2}=c_{1}+c_{2}
. It follows that
b_{1}\sqsubseteq c_{1}+c_{2}
, hence, by the production property, there are elements

in
\mathbb{K}
such that

d_{2}\sqsubseteq c_{2}
, and

. Since

and
d_{2}\sqsubseteq c_{2}
, there are elements

in

and

. Since

, we have that

. Since

is cancellative, it follows that

. Therefore, the desired elements

are

d_{11}=d_{1}
,
d_{12}=d_{2}
,

d_{21}=e_{1}

.
∎

yields a useful tool for identifying monoids that do not have a semijoin function; the following is vast generalization of Proposition

.

5
Semijoin Programs and Full Reducers

Semijoin programs and full reducers in relational databases were first studied in
[
6
,
7
,
5
]
.

•

A
semijoin program on a schema
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
is a finite sequence of assignment statements of the form

X_{i}:=X_{i}\ltimes X_{j}
, where
i,j
are indices from
[1,m]
.

•

Let
\pi
be a semijoin program on
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
and let
R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
be a collection of standard relations. We write
R^{*}_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R^{*}_{m}(X_{m})
to denote the collection of standard relations resulting from
\pi
, when each
X_{i}
is interpreted by the standard relation
R_{i}(X_{i})
.

•

A semijoin program
\pi
on a hypergraph
H
with
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
as its hyperedges is a
full reducer on
H
if for every collection
R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
of standard relations, the resulting collection

R^{*}_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R^{*}_{m}(X_{m})
is globally consistent.

Beeri et al.
[
5
]
characterized acyclicity in terms of the existence of full reducers. Earlier, a variant of this result was obtained by Bernstein and Goodman
[
7
]
.

Theorem 6

(
[
5
]
)
.

For every hypergraph
H
, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

H
is acyclic.

2.

H
has a full reducer.

We will investigate the question: does Theorem
6
extend to annotated relations?
In what follows, we assume that
{\mathbb{K}}=(K,+,0)
is a positive commutative monoid.

Definition 4
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
be a schema
and let
\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}
be a binary function symbol.

•

A
semijoin program on
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
is a finite sequence of assignment statements of the form

X_{i}:=X_{i}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{j}
, where
i,j
are indices from
[1,m]
.

•

Let
\pi
is a semijoin program on
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
, let
\mathcal{S}
be a semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
, and let

R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
be a collection of
\mathbb{K}
-relations. We write
R^{*}_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R^{*}_{m}(X_{m})
to denote the collection of
\mathbb{K}
-relations resulting from
\pi
when
\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}
is interpreted by the semijoin function
\mathcal{S}
and each
X_{i}
is interpreted by the
\mathbb{K}
-relation
R_{i}(X_{i})
.

Clearly, the evaluation of a semijoin program on a collection of
\mathbb{K}
-relations is meaningful only when a semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
exists.

Example 3
.

Consider the semijoin program

X_{3}:=X_{3}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{2};~~X_{2}:=X_{2}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{3};~~X_{1}:=X_{1}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{2}.

If
R_{1}(X_{1}),R_{2}(X_{2}),R_{3}(X_{3})
are three
\mathbb{K}
-relations and
\mathcal{S}
is a semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
, then the preceding semijoin program produces the relations
R_{1}^{*}(X_{1}),R_{2}^{*}(X_{2}),R_{3}^{*}(X_{3})
, where

R_{3}^{*}:=\mathcal{S}(R_{3},R_{2});~~R_{2}^{*}:=\mathcal{S}(R_{2},\mathcal{S}(R_{3},R_{2}));~~R_{1}^{*}:=\mathcal{S}(R_{1},\mathcal{S}(R_{2},\mathcal{S}(R_{3},R_{2}))).

Definition 5
.

Let
H
be a hypergraph with

X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
as its hyperedges.

•

Let
\pi
be a semijoin program on
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
and let
\mathcal{S}
be a semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
.

The pair
(\pi,\mathcal{S})
is a
full reducer for

if for every collection
R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
of
\mathbb{K}
-relations, the resulting collection

R^{*}_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R^{*}_{m}(X_{m})
of
\mathbb{K}
-relations is globally consistent.

•

H
has a
full reducer on
\mathbb{K}
if there are a

\pi
on

and a

\mathcal{S}
function on
\mathbb{K}
such that the pair
(\pi,\mathcal{S})
is a full reducer for

.

We are now ready to state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a positive commutative monoid that has the inner consistency property. For every hypergraph
H
,
the following statements are equivalent:

1.

H
is acyclic.

2.

There is a semijoin program
\pi
on
H
that depends only on
H
such that for every semijoin function
\mathcal{S}
on
\mathbb{K}
, the pair
(\pi,\mathcal{S})
is a full reducer for
H
.

3.

H
has a full reducer on
\mathbb{K}
.

Proof.

We will prove this theorem in a round-robin style.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)

Since
H
is an acyclic hypergraph,
H
has the running intersection property (see Theorem
1
). This means that there is an ordering

X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
of the hyperedges of
H
so that the following holds:
for every
i
with
1<i\leq m
, there is an index

j_{i}<i
such that

(X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{i-1})\cap X_{i}\subseteq X_{j_{i}}
. Note that if
i=2
, then
j_{i}=1
, but if

i>2
, then
j_{i}
can be any index smaller than
i
; for example, if
i=3
, then
j_{3}
can be
1
or
2
. Let
\pi
be the following semijoin program on
\mathbb{K}
, where each assignment statement is labeled by a number:

\displaystyle(-m)

\displaystyle~~~X_{j_{m}}:=X_{j_{m}}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{m}

\displaystyle(-m+1)

\displaystyle~~~X_{j_{m-1}}:=X_{j_{m-1}}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{m-1}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle(-k)

\displaystyle~~~X_{j_{k}}:=X_{j_{k}}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{k}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle(-3)

\displaystyle~~~X_{j_{3}}:=X_{j_{3}}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{3}

\displaystyle(-2)

\displaystyle~~~X_{1}:=X_{1}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{2}

\displaystyle(2)

\displaystyle~~~X_{2}:=X_{2}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{1}

\displaystyle(3)

\displaystyle~~~X_{3}:=X_{3}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{j_{3}}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle(k)

\displaystyle~~~X_{k}:=X_{k}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{j_{k}}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle(m-1)

\displaystyle~~~X_{m-1}:=X_{m-1}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{j_{m-1}}

\displaystyle(m)

\displaystyle~~~X_{m}:=X_{m}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{j_{m}}.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a semijoin function on
\mathbb{K}
, let
R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
be a collection of
\mathbb{K}
-relations, and let
R^{*}_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R^{*}_{m}(X_{m})
be the collection of
\mathbb{K}
-relations obtained by applying
\pi
to the semijoin function
\mathcal{S}
and the collection
R_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R_{m}(X_{m})
.
We have to show that the collection
R^{*}_{1}(X_{1}),\ldots,R^{*}_{m}(X_{m})
is globally consistent. We start with a crucial stepping stone.

Claim 1.
For every
k
with

2\leq k\leq m
, the
\mathbb{K}
-relations

R^{*}_{k}(X_{k})
and
R^{*}_{j_{k}}(X_{j_{k}})
are consistent.

By the inner consistency of
\mathbb{K}
, it suffices to show that

R^{*}_{k}[X_{j_{k}}\cap X_{k}]=R^{*}_{j_{k}}[X_{j_{k}}\cap X_{k}]
. If
p
is one of the labels of the assignment statements of
\pi
, we will write
R^{(p)}_{i}
to denote the
\mathbb{K}
-relation over
X_{i}
after the
p
-th line of
\pi
has been applied. In particular, we have that
R_{i}^{(m)}(X_{i})=R_{i}^{*}(X_{i})
, for all
i
with
1\leq i\leq m
. Consider the assignment statement
X_{j_{k}}:=X_{j_{k}}\ltimes_{\mathbb{K}}X_{k}
of
\pi
labeled by
(-k)
. Thus,

R^{(-k)}_{j_{k}}=\mathcal{S}(R^{(-k+1)}_{j_{k}},R^{(-k+1)}_{k}).

(1)

By property

\sf{(P3)}
of the semijoin function
\mathcal{S}

(2)\Longrightarrow(3)

6
Concluding Remarks

The work reported here contains both conceptual and technical contributions. The main conceptual contribution is the introduction of the notion of a semijoin function on a monoid, which, in turn, makes it possible to introduce the notion of a full reducer for a schema on a monoid. The main technical contributions are the characterization of the monoids that have a semijoin function and the characterization of acyclicity in terms of the existence of full reducers on monoids. The latter characterization vastly generalizes
the classical result in Beeri et al.
[
5
]
and also yields new results for a variety of annotated relations over monoids.

In the Introduction, we referenced some early influential work on semijoins. This is only a small fraction of the investigation of the semijoin operation on relational databases. There is, for example, a body of work on the semijoin algebra, which is the fragment of relational algebra in which the semijoin is used instead of the join
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
. In particular, it has been shown that the semijoin algebra has the same expressive power as the well-studied guarded fragment of first-order logic. More recently, semijoins have been used as a message-passing mechanism in query processing
[
14
]
and have also found applications in incremental view maintenance
[
20
]
. Furthermore, advanced filtering techniques based on Bloom filters have been used in query processing as a generalization of semijoins
[
21
]
.
We leave it as future work to explore some of these topics in the context of annotated relations over monoids.
