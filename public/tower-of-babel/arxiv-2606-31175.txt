Evidence for differential kinetic freeze-out of the ϕ ⁡ ( 1020 ) \phi(1020) meson in Pb–Pb collisions at s NN = 2.76 \sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=2.76 TeV
Neeraj, Amal Sarkar

Abstract
In heavy-ion collisions, hadronic species with small interaction cross sections may decouple from the evolving fireball earlier than the bulk, yet quantitative evidence for this differential freeze-out has remained elusive. We report that the ϕ ⁡ ( 1020 ) \phi(1020) meson does not kinetically freeze out with the bulk hadrons in 0–5% central Pb–Pb collisions at s NN = 2.76 \sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=2.76 TeV: a Boltzmann–Gibbs blast-wave contour analysis of ALICE ϕ ⁡ ( 1020 ) \phi(1020) p T p_{\rm T} transverse-momentum spectra shows that the bulk π / K / p \pi/K/p freeze-out point is excluded at 4.1 ​ σ 4.1\sigma ( Δ ​ χ 2 = 21.7 \Delta\chi^{2}=21.7 ). Despite its proton-like mass, the ϕ \phi exhibits freeze-out parameters incompatible with those of the bulk hadrons, implying that the observed spectral hardening cannot be attributed solely to mass-dependent collective expansion. Instead, it is naturally explained by the OZI-suppressed ϕ \phi -hadron interaction cross section which causes ϕ \phi to decouple earlier and probe a distinct freeze-out surface. The exclusion is robust under all systematic variations tested and is qualitatively reproduced by SMASH hadronic transport simulations. These findings establish the ϕ \phi meson as a clean probe of species-dependent hadronization, and provide quantitative evidence for a kinetic freeze-out hierarchy in ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions.



Ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions at RHIC and the LHC create a strongly coupled quark-gluon plasma (QGP) that expands collectively and hadronizes at
T_{c}\approx 156

\pi

Whether kinetic freeze-out is universal across hadron species remains an open question. Species with small hadronic cross sections may decouple earlier, retaining a higher effective temperature or a different flow velocity. This sequential freeze-out was predicted by RQMD transport calculations
24
and quantified in UrQMD for multi-strange baryons, where the temperature of the last-collision increases with the strangeness content
19
;
26

\Xi
and
\Omega
spectra at RHIC
27
;
7

The
\phi(1020)

m_{\phi}=1019
MeV/

\Omega

p

As a pure

8

\phi

T

\langle\beta\rangle

\chi^{2}

Summary and outlook.
—We have demonstrated that the
\phi(1020)
meson does not share the bulk
\pi/K/p
kinetic freeze-out surface in 0–5% Pb–Pb collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=2.76
TeV, with a model-independent significance of
4.1\sigma
. Because the
\phi
’s OZI-suppressed hadronic cross section limits rescattering in the hadronic phase,
\phi
decouples earlier than the bulk and retains the imprint of a hotter or faster-expanding stage, producing a harder
p_{\rm T}
spectrum. In the
(T,\langle\beta\rangle)
plane, this manifests itself as a
\Delta\chi^{2}
landscape whose degenerate
\chi^{2}
ridge, connecting the high-
T
/bulk-flow and bulk-
T
/enhanced-flow solutions, does not pass through the bulk freeze-out point. The physical decomposition of the spectral hardness into earlier thermal decoupling, differential flow acquisition, or non-thermal contributions remains degenerate from the six data points at a single collision energy; this limitation is intrinsic to single-species blast-wave fits and cannot be overcome by systematic variations.
The displacement is robust against all systematic variations tested (Table
1
) and is qualitatively reproduced by SMASH hadronic transport, confirming that the OZI-suppressed
\phi
–hadron cross section drives earlier kinetic decoupling.

For comparison, a BGBW contour analysis of the
\phi
spectrum at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=200
GeV yields
\Delta\chi^{2}\gg 6
from the bulk, but the
\chi^{2}
minimum at that energy lies at
T\approx 280
MeV
\gg T_{c}
, suggesting that the narrower
T_{pc}-T_{\rm kin}^{\rm bulk}
gap at RHIC (
\sim 45
MeV at 200 GeV vs.
\sim 62
MeV at 2.76 TeV
14
;
13
) reflects a shorter hadronic phase that may not provide sufficient evolution time for a physically meaningful species-dependent freeze-out signal to emerge.

This growing separation between hadronization and bulk kinetic freeze-out provides a larger hadronic evolution window in which species-dependent decoupling can develop.
Using lattice QCD
T_{pc}(\mu_{B})

14
;
13
and the thermal-model
\mu_{B}

12
, the separation grows from
\sim 23
MeV at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=7.7
GeV to
\sim 62
MeV at 2.76 TeV.

High-statistics
\phi
measurements from the RHIC BES-II program
3
will test whether the displacement decreases with beam energy as the QGP phase shortens toward lower
\sqrt{s}
. Breaking the
T
–
\langle\beta\rangle
degeneracy via a simultaneous
p_{\rm T}
-spectrum
+
v_{2}
fit is not yet feasible, since the ALICE
\phi

v_{2}
at 0–10% centrality
5
carries large uncertainties from the small geometric eccentricity. The observed
\Delta\chi^{2}=21.7
provides a quantitative benchmark for viscous-hydrodynamics
+
transport hybrid models
20
, which should reproduce a comparable displacement of the
\phi
confidence contour from the bulk locus.
By excluding a common freeze-out surface for
\phi(1020)
at
4.1\sigma
, this work provides the first quantitative evidence for differential kinetic decoupling and establishes species-dependent kinetic freeze-out as a precision probe of hadronic-phase dynamics.
