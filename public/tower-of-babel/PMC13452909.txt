Short Inter‐Treatment Interval Treatment With Artificial Liver Support System Reduces 90‐Day Transplant‐Free Mortality in Patients With Hepatitis B Virus–Related Acute‐On‐Chronic Liver Failure: A Retrospective Observational Study
Authors: Jingjie Fu, Fang Chen, Wenfeng Yang, Ting Chen, Jian Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimThis retrospective observational study aimed to compare the effects of short inter‐treatment interval treatment (SIIT) versus long inter‐treatment interval treatment (LIIT) using the artificial liver support system (ALSS) on liver function recovery and 90‐day transplant‐free mortality in patients with hepatitis B virus‐related acute‐on‐chronic liver failure (HBV‐ACLF).MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 149 HBV‐ACLF patients selected from 166 patients who received comprehensive internal medicine therapy combined with ALSS. Patients were categorized into the SIIT group and the LIIT group. Laboratory test data were recorded both before ALSS treatment and the seventh day after the last ALSS treatment. The 90‐day transplant‐free mortality was compared between the two groups.ResultsBiochemical indicators reflecting patient's liver function including alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, serum total bilirubin (TBIL), direct bilirubin (DBIL), total bile acid, and coagulation function (prothrombin time activity, PTA) were significantly improved at posttreatment than pretreatment in both groups (p < 0.05). At Day 7 after the last ALSS treatment, TBIL and DBIL levels were significantly lower in the SIIT group than in the LIIT group (p < 0.01). The 90‐day transplant‐free mortality in the SIIT group and LIIT group was 12.8% and 29.6%, respectively (p < 0.001). Higher baseline MELD score was associated with increased 90‐day transplant‐free mortality (OR = 1.815, 95% CI: 1.466–2.247, p < 0.001).ConclusionsIn patients with HBV‐ACLF, SIIT with ALSS was associated with reduced 90‐day transplant‐free mortality compared with LIIT, although prospective studies are required to confirm causality.Keywords: 90‐day transplant‐free mortality, acute‐on‐chronic liver failure, artificial liver support system, hepatitis B virus, short inter‐treatment interval

Introduction

Acute‐on‐chronic liver failure (ACLF) is one of the most severe forms of liver dysfunction caused by various factors, and its short‐term mortality rate ranges from 20% to approximately 80% [1]. Hepatitis B virus (HBV)‐related acute‐on‐chronic liver failure (HBV‐ACLF) is the most common type of ACLF in China [2]. The main manifestations of ACLF include extreme fatigue, obvious digestive tract symptoms, increased bilirubin level, bleeding tendency, as well as hepatic encephalopathy (HE) and ascites [1]. At present, there is still a lack of specific and effective treatment for HBV‐ACLF. The available treatment options mainly include comprehensive internal medicine treatment (treatment based on the cause and trigger, organ protection treatment, and treatment for complications), artificial liver support system (ALSS) treatment, and liver transplantation (LT) [3]. However, LT is often not feasible due to various extra‐hepatic factors, such as worsening infections, bleeding, multiple organ failure, and the difficulty in obtaining donated livers in time [4, 5]. ALSS therapies, which mimic hepatic functions in vitro and partially clear inflammatory factors and neutralize harmful metabolites temporarily, are promising strategies in the management of ACLF [6].

Currently, ALSS can be classified into nonbiological, biological, and mixed types ALSS [7, 8]. However, biological ALSS has not been widely used in daily clinical practice due to mixed clinical data and concerns about cell sources [9]. The ALSS includes various modes. Currently, the modes commonly used in clinical practice mainly include plasma exchange (PE), plasma or blood perfusion, bilirubin adsorption, hemofiltration, hemodialysis, and other traditional methods [7]. PE is a relatively safe and mature technique that separates the patient's blood and replenishes fresh frozen plasma alone or in combination with albumin [10]. PE demonstrated a reduction in short‐term mortality in sepsis, which shares similar immune pathogenesis with ACLF, including multiple organ dysfunction and systemic inflammation [11]. Systematic reviews and meta‐analyses have shown that PE may help improve the overall survival rate of patients with ACLF [12]. The double plasma molecular adsorption system (DPMAS) is a relatively new blood purification technology that has been applied in clinical practice in China in recent years. This system, by using bilirubin adsorption columns and blood perfusion devices, is capable of removing more medium and large‐molecular toxins, bilirubin, and inflammatory factors [13]. However, an undeniable side effect of DPMARS is its impact on the patient's blood coagulation function accompanied by the clearance of multiple harmful substances during treatment [14]. Based on the respective advantages and disadvantages of PE and DPMAS, the combined application of PE and DPMAS in the study of ACLF has become one of the hot topics in the field of liver failure research. As expected, a large amount of positive data has emerged in scientific research. The combined application of PE and DPMAS has shown significant effects in the following aspects: (1) It reduces the amount of plasma used and saves medical resources such as blood products [15]. (2) The combination of PE and DPMAS can significantly improve liver function, coagulation function, and blood indicators of patients with acute liver failure, and also increase the effectiveness of treatment [16]. (3) DPMAS + PE treatment was associated with significantly higher 28‐day effectiveness and 90‐day LT‐free survival in a subgroup of patients (prothrombin time activity [PTA] > 40%) and had advantages of better cost‐effectiveness [17]. However, the reported conclusions are not always completely consistent [18]. A randomized controlled trial of Prometheus, a system with a similar design to DPMAS, did not improve survival probability except in the most severe patients with MELD scores > 30 [19]. Therefore, the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) Clinical Practice Guidelines (2018) do not recommend ALSS for the treatment of ACLF [20].

Why has the same medical technology been controversial for such a long time? Since ALSS is not a continuous operation, do different inter‐treatment intervals of ALSS treatments lead to different clinical effects? This study conducted a retrospective analysis, aiming to compare whether there are differences in the clinical efficacy of ALSS treatments using short inter‐treatment interval treatment (SIIT) versus long inter‐treatment interval treatment (LIIT) for HBV‐ACLF, and to identify the possible mechanisms behind this phenomenon, in order to provide theoretical support for clinical decision‐making regarding the use of ALSS for HBV‐ACLF.

Methods

Patients

The diagnostic criteria for HBV‐ACLF were established in accordance with the Chinese standards of Chinese Group on the Study of Severe HBV‐ACLF [21]. (1) serum hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) remains positive for more than 6 months; (2) fatigue symptoms accompanied by gastrointestinal symptoms (such as nausea and vomiting); (3) serum total bilirubin (TBIL) rose to ≥ 171 μmol/L, or TBIL increases by 17.1 μmol/L in a single day; and (4) coagulation dysfunction (PTA ≤ 40% or international normalized ratio [INR] ≥ 1.5). The exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) age < 18 years; (2) obstructive or hemolytic jaundice; (3) gastrointestinal bleeding determined by electronic gastroscopy examination; (4) overlap infections with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and/or hepatitis C virus (HCV); (5) liver cancer and other tumors; (6) history of LT; (7) currently pregnant women; and (8) patients with mental illness who cannot cooperate. All patients with incomplete data and those lost to follow‐up were not included in the final analysis results.

Study Design

As shown in Figure 1, there were 166 patients with HBV‐ACLF who received ALSS treatment between August 1, 2018 and July 31, 2024 in the Fuling Center Hospital of Chongqing City and Yubei People's Hospital of Chongqing. Among them, 85 patients were in the SIIT group, in which patients received two ALSS treatments within 48 h; and 81 were in the LIIT group, in which the interval time between the two ALSS treatments exceeded 48 h. The definition of SIIT as performing two rounds of ALSS within 48 h is based on the clinical practice of our team. This time interval can utilize the same deep vein access, which will save the patient's medical expense for a single deep vein catheterization. According to the case records, it is the patients and their families who decide whether to undergo SIIT or LIIT. The follow‐up period was 90 days from the onset of the HBV‐ACLF. In both groups, 7 and 10 patients were lost to follow‐up, respectively. As a result, the number of patients included in the study in the SIIT group and LIIT group was 78 and 71, respectively. All patients received entecavir (1 mg/day) anti‐HBV treatment. For suspected or confirmed infections, appropriate antimicrobial treatment was given. Diuretic paracentesis combined with albumin infusion for patients with ascites, and lactulose for HE. When other clinical symptoms occurred, active symptomatic treatments were provided.

ALSS Process

PE (replace 2000 mL of fresh frozen plasma each time) combined with DPMAS was selected as the ALSS mode in both groups and performed according to the mature operation process [22]. After signing the informed consent form, follow the procedures below: (1) connect the pipelines, link the machine to the plasma separator (Jafron Biomedical, Guangdong, China), bilirubin adsorption column (Jafron Biomedical, Guangdong, China) and perfusion device (HA330‐II; Jafron Biomedical, Guangdong, China) in sequence. (2) Pre‐charge pipeline, after the self‐inspection of the machine and disposable pipeline installation is passed, pre‐charging is carried out. The venous end is connected to normal saline for automatic flushing. A total of 1000 mL is needed, including 1000 mL of 40 mg/500 mL heparin normal saline. The integrated pre‐charging of the bilirubin adsorption column and the perfusion device requires a total of 3000 mL of 15 mg/500 mL heparin normal saline. (3) Treatment preparation, connect the anticoagulant, inject the first dose of heparin (0.4 mg to 0.6 mg/kg), connect the plasma infusion end, set the parameters of blood flow velocity 80–100 mL/h, fluid infusion velocity 30 mL/h, heparin maintenance dose 0.1–0.2 mg/kg, and prepare for treatment. (4) Deep vein catheters are connected to the arterial and venous end tubes, protected with gauze pads, and treatment begins. (5) After the plasma separation is successful and the patient shows no discomfort, adjust the blood flow rate to 130 mL/h. During the treatment period, observe the patient's condition changes and handle any emergencies in a timely manner. (6) Discontinue heparin, 30 min before the end of treatment and slowly inject 20 mg of protamine to achieve a therapeutic dose of 6 L. (7) Treatment completed, reverse the adsorption column and perfusion device, turn off the venous pressure and fluid infusion alarm, suspend the treatment, and use gravity to return the blood from the arterial end to the patient's body with normal saline. Clamp the arterial switch of the pipeline, adjust the blood flow rate to 80 mL/h, and use normal saline to recover the blood. When the blood in the separator and pipeline is recovered to the lower end of the separator, suspend the treatment and clamp the lower switch of the separator. Adjust the speed to 180 mL/h to recover the plasma in the perfusion device and adsorption column. When the plasma is recovered above the venous pot, suspend the treatment, clamp the venous switch of the pipeline, and turn off the artificial liver machine. All the patients included in the analysis received two ALSS treatments.

Clinical Observation Indicators and Clinical Outcomes

The baseline assessment used the model for end‐stage liver disease (MELD) scores, as per the original formula [11.2 ln (INR) + 3.78 ln (TBIL) + 9.56 ln (SCr) + 6.43], where INR is the INR and SCr is the serum creatinine (SCr) level [23]. The following information was collected from all patients upon admission: sex, age, diagnosis, and clinical symptoms and signs. The clinical laboratory data, including white blood cell (WBC) count, percentage of neutrophils (NEUT%), platelet count (PLT), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), TBIL, direct bilirubin (DBIL), total bile acid (TBA), serum albumin (ALB), serum globulin (GLO), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), serum creatinine (SCr), uric acid (UA), serum potassium (K+), serum sodium (Na+), and PTA, were recorded before the first ALSS treatment and 7 days after the last ALSS treatment. The 90‐day transplant‐free mortality was also compared between the two groups.

Observation of Adverse Reactions

During and after the ALSS treatment, monitoring closely the patient's adverse reactions is necessary, including the occurrence of bleeding, infections, and unstable hemodynamics. If the adverse reactions such as low blood pressure and allergy are relatively mild, the operation for DPMAS and PE can go on by symptomatic and supportive treatment, such as slowing down the blood flow speed, fluid expansion, and anti‐allergy. Otherwise, in the event of severe and life‐threatening adverse reactions, ALSS treatment should be immediately terminated, and active treatment measures should be undertaken to reduce risk to the patient's life.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using the SPSS software (version 16.0; SPSS, Chicago, Illinois, USA). Differences between the SIIT and LIIT in MELD scores and laboratory indices at baseline and 7 days after ALSS treatment were compared using two‐tailed Student's t‐test. Quantitative variables were compared using paired Student's t‐test and were presented as mean ± standard deviation depending on the normality of the data. Categorical variables are presented as frequency and percentage, and were analyzed using the chi‐square (χ 2) test. Patients who were lost to follow‐up were not included in the statistical analysis. Cox and logistic regression analysis was used to evaluate the survival status and mortality hazard risk of the patients. A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Adverse Events

Among all the patients included in the analysis, no severe adverse events related to ALSS, including infectious events, bleeding, shock, imbalance syndrome, electrolyte disorders, and plasma allergies were recorded.

Discussion

ACLF has now become a serious global health issue, characterized by rapid disease progression and an extremely high short‐term mortality rate [24]. In China, HBV remains the leading cause of ACLF [25]. Currently, the treatment methods for HBV‐ACLF mainly include comprehensive internal medicine treatment, ALSS treatment, and LT. However, due to the insufficient availability of organ donations, the accessibility of LT is limited. As a result, the main treatment methods for HBV‐ACLF focus on comprehensive internal medicine treatment and ALSS treatment. In the field of comprehensive internal medicine treatment, the use of corticosteroids has always attracted much attention and been of great concern and has sparked intense controversy [26, 27]. In one of our previous clinical studies on pre‐HBV‐ACLF, we found that corticosteroids could effectively improve the liver function of patients with pre‐HBV‐ACLF and shorten their hospital stay, but they could not prevent pre‐HBV‐ACLF from developing into HBV‐ACLF [28]. In addition to comprehensive internal medicine treatment and LT, we have placed more expectations for the treatment of ACLF on ALSS. In recent years, the combined ALSS model has become an important treatment strategy for patients with HBV‐ACLF, who may be waiting for an LT [29].

More and more studies found that PE combined with DPMAS has the following advantages: (1) removing elevated bilirubin [30]; (2) eliminating inflammatory factors and multiple toxic metabolic products [31]; (3) improving 28‐day survival rate [32]. Although these studies were retrospective and single‐center, and lacking high‐level evidence‐based medical evidence, they provided certain positive support for the use of PE combined with DPMAS in the treatment of HBV‐ACLF. Therefore, the PE plus DPMAS model was adopted in this study. Just as described in the previous research report, the patients' liver function (mainly ALT, AST, TBIL, DBIL, and TBA levels) was significantly improved posttreatment (7 days posttreatment from the last ALSS) versus pretreatment (p < 0.05), indicating that ALSS can improve the biochemical indices of the liver to a certain extent, a finding that is consistent with a recent report [33].

It is worth elaborating further on the observation time point. HBV‐ACLF is a dynamic disease, and a dynamic assessment of the patient's condition changes is more clinically instructive than assessment at a single time point [34]. The time points of admission and the seventh day after treatment are important for efficiency assessment [35, 36, 37]. Therefore, the detection time points in this study were upon admission prior to ALSS treatment and 1 week after the last ALSS treatment. However, it is regretful that the current guidelines or consensus on ALSS do not recommend the time interval for ACLF treatment. There is still a lack of strong evidence as to whether different time intervals will affect the treatment effect and clinical outcomes of patients. The prognosis of ACLF is especially dire within the first 3–7 days [38]. If we shorten the treatment interval of ALSS to within the initial 3–7 days, will it affect the patient's therapeutic effect? Here, based on comprehensive internal medicine treatment, we applied the ALSS treatment methods of SIIT and LIIT to patients with HBV‐ACLF and compared their clinical efficacy. We found that there were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in terms of TBIL and DBIL levels at pretreatment (p > 0.05). However, after the treatment, the levels of TBIL and DBIL in the SIIT group were significantly lower than those in the LIIT group (p < 0.05), indicating that the SIIT model has a greater advantage in reducing the bilirubin levels of HBV‐ACLF patients compared to the LIIT model. Bilirubin and its toxic products have multiple harmful effects on the human body, and their toxic effects are related to the accumulation of bilirubin [30]. The clinical benefits brought about by SIIT with ALSS may be attributed to the early removal of bilirubin.

Since almost all coagulation factors are synthesized in the liver, coagulation function is an important aspect for evaluating the synthetic function of the liver. In our study, the patients' coagulation function (mainly measured by PTA level) also improved significantly after versus before treatment in both groups (p < 0.001). These biochemical indicators are crucial for improving the prognosis of patients with HBV‐ACLF.

HBV‐ACLF remains a serious global public health problem because the 28‐day and 90‐day mortality rate is as high as 23.4% and 31.3%, respectively [39]. There has been ongoing debate in the medical community regarding whether ALSS can reduce the 90‐day mortality rate of patients with HBV‐ACLF. Some studies have reported that ALSS significantly improved 28‐day and 90‐day transplant‐free mortality rates and laboratory parameters in HBV‐ACLF patients [40]. However, a contrasting opinion emerged from another review, which found that ALSS did not reduce the mortality rate in patients with HBV‐ACLF [7]. Our results show that SIIT with ALSS was associated with lower 90‐day transplant‐free mortality of HBV‐ACLF (OR: 0.017 [0.002–0.018], p < 0.001). The baseline MELD scores of the patients before treatment were independent risk factors for 90‐day transplant‐free mortality in HBV‐ACLF patients.

It is pleasing to note that with the continuous development of ALSS materials, the hemodynamics of patients during the treatment process have become more stable, and cases of internal environment disorders have become relatively rare. No significant electrolyte imbalances were found in both groups, indicating that the SIIT of PE and DPMAS is safe and reliable for HBV‐ACLF.

Implication for Practice

This research result provides certain theoretical basis for the time interval of ALSS for HBV‐ACLF, enriches the mode and content of ALSS treatment for HBV‐ACLF, and offers insights and ideas for further optimizing ALSS treatment models for HBV‐ACLF.

Limitations

Due to the small sample size and retrospective nature contributing to a strong selection bias, necessitating cautious interpretation of all results. More detailed and in‐depth statistical methods should be applied to address the issue of time bias and center effects. In addition, the concept of “short inter‐treatment intervals” mentioned in this article is subjective. It could be the two consecutive days of ALSS treatment mentioned in this article, or it could be the three consecutive days of ALSS treatment. Such different intervals of therapeutic effect may cause significant bias in therapeutic effect. The allocation of treatment groups was mainly based on the wishes of the patients and their families. Among them, the economic conditions of the families might have influenced the choice of treatment plans, which could potentially lead to certain selection bias. We hope to address these limitations in our following research.

Conclusions

In patients with HBV‐ACLF, SIIT with ALSS was associated with reduced 90‐day transplant‐free mortality compared with LIIT, although prospective studies are required to confirm causality.
