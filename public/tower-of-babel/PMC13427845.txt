Implementation and experimental validation of a hybrid intelligent sliding mode control strategy for induction motor drives
Authors: Ngoc Thuy Pham
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper proposes a hybrid intelligent control strategy for high-performance induction motor drives under field-oriented control (FOC). The proposed architecture combines a radial basis function (RBF) neural network with higher-order non-singular terminal sliding-mode (HONTSM) control to improve robustness against nonlinear dynamics, parameter uncertainties, and external disturbances. The RBF network generates the torque-producing current reference in the speed loop, while the HONTSM compensator enhances disturbance rejection and tracking performance. In the inner loop, a HONTSM current controller ensures fast convergence, reduced chattering, and robust current regulation. To further enhance energy efficiency, an improved Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature (IK-FAC) flux optimization strategy is proposed to adaptively optimize the flux-producing current reference in real time. The proposed approach employs recursive curvature estimation based on filtered gradient-energy statistics to adaptively scale the flux update law without explicit Hessian computation or matrix inversion. Unlike the original K-FAC algorithm for large-scale neural network optimization, the proposed method reformulates the curvature-aware adaptation principle into a lightweight scalar recursive framework suitable for real-time embedded motor-drive applications. Consequently, the proposed IK-FAC strategy achieves fast convergence toward the minimum-loss operating trajectory while preserving the original FOC structure and maintaining low computational complexity for DSP-based induction motor drives. Controller parameters are optimized using particle swarm optimization (PSO) based on the integral absolute error (IAE) criterion. Experimental validation on a Texas Instruments TMS320F28379D digital signal processor demonstrates improved tracking accuracy, fast transient response, enhanced energy efficiency, and strong robustness under varying operating conditions.

Introduction

Induction motors (IMs) have been extensively utilized in industrial applications because of their rugged structure, low manufacturing cost, high reliability, and favorable operating characteristics. Owing to these advantages, IM drive systems are widely employed in industrial automation, electric transportation, robotic systems, and renewable-energy applications1,2. Nevertheless, achieving high-performance control of induction motor drives remains a challenging task because of their nonlinear behavior, strong coupling effects, parameter sensitivity, and susceptibility to external disturbances3,4. Therefore, improving the dynamic performance of these drives while simultaneously enhancing energy efficiency has become a critical objective in modern electric drive systems, particularly under partial-load conditions, where conventional control strategies often operate far from optimal efficiency.

Among various control strategies, field-oriented control (FOC) has become one of the most widely adopted techniques for induction motor drives because it enables the decoupling of torque and flux components. In conventional FOC systems, proportional–integral (PI) controllers are commonly used in both the speed and current loops due to their simple structure and ease of implementation5–7. However, PI-based controllers often exhibit limited robustness when the system operates under parameter uncertainties, load disturbances, and nonlinear inverter dynamics, which may lead to degraded dynamic performance and larger tracking errors8–10.

To overcome these limitations, various advanced nonlinear control strategies have been proposed in the literature. Sliding-mode control (SMC) has attracted significant attention due to its strong robustness against system uncertainties and external disturbances11–14. Nevertheless, conventional SMC methods suffer from the well-known chattering phenomenon, which may excite unmodeled dynamics and cause undesirable oscillations in motor drive systems15. To mitigate this issue, higher-order sliding-mode control (HOSM) techniques have been developed, among which non-singular terminal sliding-mode control (NTSM) provides finite-time convergence and improved robustness properties16–18.

Another nonlinear control strategy, backstepping (BS), has also attracted significant attention from researchers due to its systematic Lyapunov-based design procedure and guaranteed closed-loop stability. By recursively constructing virtual control laws and stabilizing functions, backstepping enables the stabilization of nonlinear systems with strict-feedback structures, making it particularly suitable for induction motor drives under field-oriented control. Several studies have applied backstepping control to improve the dynamic response and robustness of motor drive systems19–23. However, conventional backstepping approaches often rely heavily on accurate system models and are sensitive to parameter uncertainties and external disturbances. Moreover, the design may suffer from performance degradation when unmodeled nonlinearities or load variations are present.

In recent years, intelligent control approaches such as neural networks and fuzzy logic systems have been increasingly investigated for motor drive applications24–35. Among these methods, radial basis function (RBF) neural networks have gained considerable attention due to their strong nonlinear approximation capability, simple structure, and fast learning characteristics. RBF networks have been successfully applied to estimate system uncertainties, approximate nonlinear mappings, and improve the adaptability of control systems without requiring precise knowledge of motor parameters34,35.

Despite these advances, many existing studies employ intelligent techniques and nonlinear control strategies independently, which limits their capability to simultaneously address nonlinear dynamics, parameter uncertainties, and external disturbances. Several hybrid intelligent nonlinear control strategies for induction motor drives have been proposed in the literature36–41 to improve the dynamic performance of field-oriented controlled induction motor drives.

In36, a radial basis function (RBF) neural network was introduced to adaptively tune controller parameters online, improving the adaptability over conventional PI-based approaches. Nevertheless, the RBF network was mainly employed as a gain-tuning mechanism rather than as an explicit uncertainty estimator. Consequently, the control scheme still relied on PI-based regulation, which inherently limited its capability to effectively handle strong nonlinearities, parameter uncertainties, and external disturbances.

To overcome these limitations, an adaptive BS nonlinear control strategy in38 has been investigated to enhance the capability of induction motor drives in dealing with system nonlinearities, where fuzzy logic was employed to tune the backstepping gains and enhance dynamic performance. Although this strategy improved adaptability and reduced sensitivity to parameter variations, the fuzzy logic component mainly served as a gain-tuning mechanism and did not provide an explicit mechanism for uncertainty estimation or disturbance compensation. To further enhance robustness and disturbance rejection capability, hybrid nonlinear control strategies combining backstepping with sliding-mode control have been proposed. In particular, an advanced adaptive higher-order sliding-mode (NAHOSM) control scheme integrated with backstepping was introduced in40, where the sliding-mode dynamics significantly improved disturbance rejection in the inner control loop. However, the outer speed-loop design in this approach remained primarily model-based and did not incorporate any data-driven mechanism to learn or compensate for unknown nonlinear dynamics.

Motivated by the aforementioned limitations and the absence of a unified control framework that can simultaneously provide nonlinear uncertainty estimation, finite-time robust control, and real-time energy-efficiency optimization within a conventional FOC architecture, this work proposes a hybrid intelligent control architecture that combines a radial basis function (RBF) neural network with a higher-order nonsingular terminal sliding-mode (HONTSM) control strategy for induction motor drives.

This development reflects the evolution of induction motor control strategies from conventional PI-based regulation toward first order sliding-mode (SM)36, and higher-order sliding-mode control frameworks STASOSM38—NAHOSM40. Building upon these developments, the proposed architecture further extends the state of the art by incorporating a higher-order nonsingular terminal sliding-mode (HONTSM) controller in the inner current-control loop. The adoption of HONTSM enables the controller to preserve the inherent robustness and disturbance-rejection capability of sliding-mode control while significantly alleviating the chattering phenomenon through higher-order continuous control action and finite-time convergence properties.

In the outer speed-control loop, the proposed RBF neural network is fundamentally different from existing approaches in which neural networks are primarily employed as auxiliary gain-tuning mechanisms for PI controllers36 or as supplementary adaptive elements in adaptive backstepping-based schemes38. Instead, the RBF network directly participates in nonlinear uncertainty estimation and torque-current reference generation, thereby providing an adaptive data-driven mechanism capable of compensating for unknown dynamics and parameter variations in real time.

Furthermore, HONTSM dynamics are systematically incorporated into both the disturbance-compensation mechanism of the outer speed loop and the inner current-control loop, establishing a coordinated robust-control framework. As a result, the proposed strategy simultaneously achieves fast transient response, finite-time convergence, strong robustness against disturbances and parameter uncertainties, improved current-regulation quality and disturbance attenuation, and enhanced closed-loop stability under varying operating conditions.

Furthermore, the effectiveness of advanced nonlinear controllers strongly depends on proper parameter selection. In many existing studies, controller parameters are commonly adjusted using heuristic procedures or trial-and-error tuning methods, which are often time-consuming and may not guarantee satisfactory control performance under different operating conditions42–45.

To overcome this limitation, particle swarm optimization (PSO) is adopted in this work for offline optimization of the proposed controller parameters. The optimization process is formulated based on minimization of the integral absolute error (IAE) associated with the speed-tracking response, thereby improving transient behavior, tracking accuracy, and the overall operational efficiency of the induction motor drive system.

In addition to dynamic performance and robustness, energy efficiency has become an increasingly important requirement in modern induction motor drive systems, particularly in industrial automation, electric vehicles, and energy-sensitive applications46. In conventional field-oriented control systems, the flux-producing current is typically maintained at a fixed or conservatively selected value to ensure stable operation under varying load conditions. However, this approach often results in excessive excitation current under partial-load operation, leading to increased copper losses and reduced overall drive efficiency. Consequently, various flux optimization and loss-minimization strategies have been investigated to improve the energy efficiency of induction motor drives47–49.

Most existing approaches employ first-order optimization methods or heuristic search mechanisms to determine the optimal reference. These conventional first-order adaptive optimization methods often exhibit slow convergence and sensitivity to the local geometry of nonlinear objective functions50. To overcome these limitations, second-order optimization techniques exploiting Hessian or curvature information have attracted considerable attention due to their superior convergence behaviour51,52. Nevertheless, explicit Hessian computation and matrix inversion generally impose excessive computational complexity for real-time embedded control applications. Recently, Hessian-free approximation and Kronecker-factored curvature estimation methods have been proposed to reduce computational burden while preserving the advantages of second-order optimization53,54. Motivated by these developments, This paper introduces an Improved Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature (IK-FAC) flux optimization strategy employing a lightweight curvature-aware adaptation mechanism suitable for online energy-efficient induction motor drive applications.

Design of the hybrid intelligent proposed control architecture

RBF-HONTSMC speed controller

RBF adpative controller

The proposed RBF neural network is introduced in the outer speed loop as an adaptive nonlinear compensation mechanism to approximate the nonlinear speed–torque relationship and compensate for lumped uncertainties, parameter variations, and external disturbances. Unlike conventional RBF-based approaches in which the neural network is mainly employed for gain tuning, the proposed structure utilizes the RBF network to directly generate the torque-producing current component for the induction motor drive system. Therefore, the adaptive mechanism acts as a bounded nonlinear compensator that enhances the tracking capability of the HONTSMC-based speed controller while preserving the robustness characteristics of the sliding-mode dynamics.

Offline parameter optimization using PSO

The dynamic performance of the proposed RBF–HONTSM-IKFAC control system depends strongly on the appropriate selection of several key speed-loop parameters, including the proportional gain, integral gain, and nonlinear sliding-surface coefficients. Owing to the highly nonlinear nature of induction motor drives and the strong coupling between the adaptive neural compensator and the terminal sliding-mode controller, conventional trial-and-error tuning often leads to suboptimal performance and poor reproducibility.

To address this issue, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is employed as an offline global optimization framework to automatically determine the controller parameters. Compared with gradient-based optimization approaches, PSO does not require differentiability of the objective function and exhibits reduced sensitivity to local minima. Furthermore, PSO provides an effective balance between optimization quality, implementation simplicity, and computational efficiency, making it particularly suitable for nonlinear controller tuning problems.

It should be emphasized that the PSO algorithm is utilized exclusively during the controller-design stage. Once the optimal parameter vector is obtained, the optimization process is terminated and the resulting parameters remain fixed during real-time operation. Therefore, PSO does not participate in the online control loop and does not contribute to the computational burden of the DSP implementation. The subsequent dynamic performance, robustness, and energy-efficiency characteristics are governed by the RBF adaptive compensator, the HONTSM controllers, and the IK-FAC optimization layer. To evaluate the dynamic performance of the speed control system, the Integral Time Absolute Error (ITAE) criterion is adopted due to its effectiveness in penalizing long-duration tracking errors and improving transient characteristics.

IK-FAC curvature-aware flux optimization for induction motor drives

In induction motor drive systems, energy efficiency is governed by the nonlinear interaction between copper losses and core losses, which are directly dependent on the current distribution in the dq reference frame. Under steady-state operating conditions, the total power loss can be expressed as a function of the control variables:41\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P_{loss} = R_{s} \left( {i_{sd}^{2} + i_{sq}^{2} } \right) + R_{r} i_{sq}^{2} + k_{fe} \varphi_{s}^{2} \omega_{e}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k_{fe}$$\end{document} is the core loss coefficient and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\omega_{e}$$\end{document} is the electrical angular speed. Under the FOC framework, the stator flux is approximated linearly as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varphi_{s} = L_{m} i_{sd}$$\end{document}, which allows the loss function to be fully expressed in terms of the current components:42\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P_{loss} \left( {i_{sd} ,\;i_{sq} } \right) = R_{s} \left( {i_{sd}^{2} + i_{sq}^{2} } \right) + R_{r} i_{sq}^{2} + k_{fe} L_{m}^{2} i_{sd}^{2} \omega_{e}$$\end{document}

By incorporating the torque constraint:43\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{e} = \frac{3p}{2}L_{m} i_{sd} i_{sq}$$\end{document}the torque-producing current can be expressed as:44\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i_{sq} = \frac{{2T_{e} }}{{3pL_{m} i_{sd} }}$$\end{document}

Substituting into the loss expression yields a single-variable nonlinear optimization problem:45\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P_{loss} \left( {i_{sd} } \right) = R_{s} i_{sd}^{2} + \left( {R_{s} + R_{r} } \right)\left( {\frac{{2T_{e} }}{{3pL_{m} i_{sd} }}} \right)^{2} + k_{fe} L_{m}^{2} i_{sd}^{2} \omega_{e}$$\end{document}

This expression is not merely a mathematical formulation but reflects a fundamental physical principle: the drive system inherently possesses an optimal operating point at which the trade-off between excitation losses and torque-related copper losses is balanced. This defines the optimal efficiency trajectory that the controller must track.

The function \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P_{loss} \left( {i_{sd} } \right)$$\end{document} exhibits a convex-like structure over the feasible domai \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i_{sd} > 0$$\end{document}, as it consists of quadratic and inverse quadratic terms. Therefore, there exists at least one global minimum corresponding to the optimal flux level for each operating condition \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {T_{e} ,\omega_{e} } \right)$$\end{document}.

The derivative of the loss function with respect to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i_{sd}$$\end{document} reveals the nonlinear and curvature characteristics of the problem:46\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P_{loss} \left( {i_{sd} } \right) = R_{s} i_{sd}^{2} + \left( {R_{s} + R_{r} } \right)\left( {\frac{{2T_{e} }}{{3pL_{m} i_{sd} }}} \right)^{2} + k_{fe} L_{m}^{2} i_{sd}^{2} \omega_{e}$$\end{document}

From a theoretical standpoint, the optimal point can be obtained by solving this equation. However, real-time implementation is not feasible due to the strong nonlinearity and continuous variation of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{e}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\omega_{e}$$\end{document}. This leads to a fundamental requirement: an optimization strategy that is both fast, stable, and sufficiently accurate for real-time tracking of the optimal point.

First-order methods such as gradient descent are simple but fail to exploit curvature information, resulting in slow convergence and strong dependence on step-size tuning. In contrast, classical second-order methods such as Newton’s method achieve faster convergence but require explicit Hessian computation and matrix inversion, which are impractical for embedded systems due to high computational complexity and sensitivity to parameter uncertainties.

Inspired by curvature-aware optimization principles employed in IK-FAC methods, a lightweight adaptive flux optimization strategy is proposed to improve convergence behavior while maintaining low computational complexity suitable for embedded motor drive applications. Instead of explicitly computing the Hessian matrix, the proposed approach estimates local curvature information using filtered gradient statistics and utilizes this information to adaptively scale the optimization update law.

The gradient of the loss function is computed as:47\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g\left( {i_{sd} } \right) = 2R_{s} i_{sd} - 2R_{s} \frac{{i_{sq}^{2} }}{{i_{sd}^{3} }} + 2k_{fe} \omega_{e}^{2} i_{sd}$$\end{document}

To improve robustness against measurement noise and rapid operating-condition variations, the gradient signal is filtered using a first-order recursive filter:48\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g_{f} \left( k \right) = \left( {1 - \gamma } \right)g_{f} \left( {k - 1} \right) + \gamma g\left( k \right)$$\end{document}where 0 < γ < 1 is the gradient filtering coefficient. The local curvature information is then recursively estimated according to:49\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_{est} \left( k \right) = \lambda C_{est} \left( {k - 1} \right) + \left( {1 - \lambda } \right)g_{f}^{2} \left( k \right)$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda \in \left( {0,1} \right)$$\end{document} is the curvature memory factor controlling the trade-off between historical and instantaneous curvature information. The term \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_{est} \left( k \right)$$\end{document} acts as a lightweight approximation of the local curvature characteristics of the loss function. The recursive curvature estimator in Eq. (49) does not represent the exact Hessian of the loss function. Instead, it serves as a lightweight local sensitivity indicator derived from the filtered gradient energy. Physically, the quantity \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g_{f}^{2} \left( k \right)$$\end{document} reflects how rapidly the total drive loss changes with respect to variations in the excitation current reference. Large values of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g_{f}^{2} \left( k \right)$$\end{document} indicate operating regions where small changes in isd produce significant variations in power loss, corresponding to high local sensitivity of the loss surface. Conversely, small values of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g_{f}^{2} \left( k \right)$$\end{document} indicate that the operating point is approaching the minimum-loss region, where the loss function becomes relatively flat.

The recursive averaging mechanism governed by λ enables the estimator to capture the long-term curvature tendency while suppressing measurement noise and transient fluctuations. Therefore, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_{est} \left( k \right)$$\end{document} provides a practical approximation of local curvature information without requiring explicit second-order derivatives, making it suitable for real-time embedded motor-drive applications.

This behavior prevents excessive excitation-current variations and improves numerical robustness. In contrast, when the operating point approaches the minimum-loss trajectory, the estimated curvature decreases, allowing larger adaptive updates and accelerating convergence toward the optimal operating condition.

Unlike conventional fixed-step gradient-descent methods, the proposed approach automatically adjusts the adaptation gain according to the local geometry of the loss surface. Therefore, the optimization process becomes less sensitive to manual parameter tuning and exhibits improved convergence smoothness under varying operating conditions.

Since the adaptation magnitude is proportional to the filtered gradient while remaining bounded by the curvature-dependent scaling factor, the proposed optimization process preserves stability and enables reliable online tracking of the optimal excitation-current reference under varying speed and load conditions.

Closed-loop stability analysis of the integrated RBF–HONTSM–IKFAC framework

The stability analyses presented in Sects. "RBF-HONTSMC speed controller" and "HONTSM current controllers" establish the convergence properties of the proposed RBF–HONTSM speed controller and HONTSM current controller under bounded disturbances and parameter uncertainties. However, the complete control architecture additionally incorporates the IK-FAC-based flux adaptation mechanism, which continuously updates the flux-producing current reference according to the operating condition.

Unlike a conventional fixed-flux FOC scheme, the proposed architecture introduces an additional adaptive control channel through the online regulation of the excitation current reference. Since the rotor flux directly influences the electromagnetic torque production mechanism, the IK-FAC optimization process becomes dynamically coupled with both the speed-control and current-control loops. Consequently, the stability of the overall closed-loop system cannot be inferred solely from the individual stability analyses of the RBF–HONTSM controllers and requires an additional investigation of the interaction between the flux adaptation mechanism and the electromechanical dynamics.

To address this issue, the following analysis demonstrates that the IK-FAC adaptation process generates bounded flux references and preserves the boundedness properties required by the previously established Lyapunov stability conditions. As a result, the adaptive flux optimization mechanism contributes to energy-efficiency improvement without compromising the closed-loop stability of the integrated control framework.

Experimental discussion

The experimental validation was carried out on a laboratory-scale induction motor drive platform. The setup consists of a three-phase two-level voltage source inverter (VSI) supplying a 1/4 HP squirrel-cage induction motor rated at 220 V. The induction motor parameters used in the experiments are as follows: p = 2, f = 50 Hz, J = 0.0003 kg.m2 \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R_{s} = 9.8\;\Omega ,R_{r} = 9.6\;\Omega ,L_{s} = 0.311\;H,L_{r} = 0.311\;H,L_{m} = 0.29\;H.$$\end{document} A 750-W permanent magnet synchronous motor (PMSM), model HS758-080–02,430-17M-H0 manufactured by Powmax, rated at 220 VAC, 3.8 A, and 3000 r/min, is mechanically coupled via a rigid shaft and employed as a programmable load machine. The PMSM is driven by a Powmax POWSD-EQ-20ASB servo drive, operating in torque-control mode, to emulate variable load disturbances as well as rated-load operating conditions.

The proposed control strategy was experimentally implemented on a Texas Instruments TMS320F28379D digital signal processor (DSP). The field-oriented control (FOC) algorithm, including the proposed optimization strategy, current controllers, coordinate transformations, PWM generation, and protection routines, was executed in real time using MATLAB/Simulink Embedded Coder and deployed directly to the DSP platform. Real-time communication between the DSP and MATLAB/Simulink was established through the XCP-over-Serial interface using COM2 communication with SCI_A configuration at a baud rate of 5 Mbps. Experimental variables were monitored online using the Monitor & Tune functionality of Simulink External Mode, enabling real-time acquisition and visualization of motor speed, stator currents, electromagnetic torque, and DC-link voltage without interrupting the controller execution.

Rotor position and speed information were acquired using a Qphase™ incremental encoder (model 3138A001-2048–0), providing A/B/Z quadrature outputs with a resolution of 2048 pulses per revolution. The encoder signals were processed via the eQEP peripheral of the DSP to achieve high-resolution speed feedback required for vector control implementation.

The stator phase currents were measured using isolated analog current-sensing circuits based on AMC1301 isolation amplifiers combined with MCP6002 signal-conditioning stages. The measured current signals were filtered and level-shifted to match the ADC input range of the DSP. Similarly, the phase voltages and DC-link voltage were measured using isolated voltage-sensing circuits employing AMC1301-based isolation stages and analog conditioning networks. The analog outputs of current sensors were sampled by the internal ADC modules of the TMS320F28379D controller.

To ensure repeatability and signal consistency, all experimental waveforms were acquired under steady-state conditions. The measured signals were streamed from the DSP to MATLAB/Simulink via the Monitor & Tune interface and processed in MATLAB for performance evaluation. The acquired datasets were utilized to compute efficiency, power losses, and transient performance indices under various operating conditions.

The conventional PI controller exhibits the lowest computational burden because only proportional and integral operations are executed during each sampling period. Consequently, the online computational complexity remains constant and can be characterized as O(1).

For the RBF–HONTSM controller, the online implementation additionally includes a fixed-size RBF neural network consisting of nine Gaussian basis functions together with adaptive weight updating. Since the number of neurons remains constant during operation, the computational complexity can be expressed as O(N), where N denotes the number of RBF neurons. Because N = 9 is fixed, the computational burden remains bounded and suitable for real-time implementation.

The proposed RBF–HONTSM–IKFAC controller further introduces a curvature-aware flux optimization layer. However, unlike conventional second-order optimization methods, the proposed IK-FAC algorithm does not require online Hessian computation, matrix inversion, eigenvalue decomposition, or iterative numerical optimization. The online adaptation process only involves recursive gradient filtering, scalar curvature estimation, adaptive gain scaling, and a first-order current-reference update law. Therefore, the overall computational complexity remains O(N), identical to that of the RBF-based controller, while providing additional energy-optimization capability.

To quantify the computational burden, an operation-count analysis was performed for each module of the proposed controller. The RBF adaptive controller consists of nine Gaussian basis functions, adaptive gain computation, and online weight updating, resulting in approximately 180 floating-point operations (FLOPs) per control cycle. The HONTSM disturbance observer requires the evaluation of nonlinear sliding surfaces, saturation functions, fractional-order power terms, and switching laws, contributing approximately 100 FLOPs. The dual HONTSM current controllers implemented in the d-axis and q-axis channels represent the most computationally intensive part of the architecture because each controller evaluates nonlinear sliding manifolds and higher-order switching functions. Together, the two current controllers require approximately 200 FLOPs per sampling period. The proposed IK-FAC optimizer performs gradient evaluation, recursive filtering, curvature estimation, adaptive scaling, and flux-reference updating, contributing approximately 40 FLOPs. Finally, the remaining FOC operations, including coordinate transformations, flux estimation, current-reference generation, and PWM updating, require approximately 150 FLOPs.

Accordingly, the total computational cost of the proposed architecture is approximately 670 FLOPs per control cycle. Considering the floating-point arithmetic capability of the TMS320F28379D DSP and accounting for interrupt handling, memory access overhead, ADC acquisition, and PWM update operations, the total execution time can be conservatively estimated from the required processor cycles. Assuming an average cost of four CPU cycles per floating-point operation, the proposed controller requires approximately 2680 processor cycles. At a clock frequency of 200 MHz, this corresponds to a theoretical execution time of approximately 13.4 μs. To account for practical implementation overhead, a conservative scaling factor of 2–2.5 is applied, resulting in an estimated execution time of approximately 30–35 μs per control cycle.

For comparison, the conventional PI controller requires less than 10 μs per control cycle, whereas the RBF–HONTSM controller requires approximately 15–20 μs due to the additional neural-network computations and nonlinear sliding-mode operations. Although the proposed controller introduces the IK-FAC optimization layer, its contribution to the overall execution time remains relatively small because the algorithm consists only of scalar recursive operations and does not involve computationally intensive matrix calculations.

The memory utilization results further confirm the lightweight nature of the proposed strategy. The Flash memory requirement increases from 21.8 kB for the PI controller to 29.23 kB for the proposed controller, corresponding to only 2.86% of the available program memory of the TMS320F28379D. Similarly, the RAM consumption increases from 27.2 kB to 29.74 kB, representing approximately 14.58% of the available data memory. These results indicate that the proposed control architecture introduces only a modest memory overhead despite integrating adaptive neural control, higher-order sliding-mode control, and online flux optimization.

It should be emphasized that the PSO algorithm is employed exclusively during the offline controller-design stage to determine the optimal controller parameters. Once the optimal parameter vector is obtained, the optimization process is terminated and does not participate in the online control loop. Consequently, PSO introduces no computational burden during real-time DSP operation.

The experimental implementation was executed with a sampling frequency of 10 kHz, corresponding to a sampling period of 100 μs. Even under the conservative execution-time estimation of 30–35 μs, the proposed controller utilizes less than 35% of the available sampling interval. Therefore, sufficient computational margin remains for ADC acquisition, PWM generation, communication services, protection routines, and interrupt handling. No timing violations or processor-overload conditions were observed during real-time experimental operation.

Overall, the obtained results demonstrate that the proposed RBF–HONTSM–IKFAC control architecture achieves an effective balance between control performance, energy-efficiency enhancement, and computational cost. The low memory utilization, absence of online matrix operations, bounded computational complexity, and short execution time collectively confirm the suitability of the proposed method for real-time embedded implementation in industrial induction motor drive applications.

Dynamic performance evaluation

Figure 6 presents the dynamic performance of the investigated control strategies under variable-speed no-load operating conditions. The reference speed is sequentially varied from 0 to 145 rad/s, reduced to 50 rad/s, and subsequently increased to 125 rad/s in order to evaluate the transient tracking capability and current regulation performance of the drive system.

The conventional PI-based FOC exhibits noticeable overshoot and oscillatory transient behavior during rapid speed transitions. During the acceleration process from 0 to 145 rad/s, the speed response reaches approximately 163 rad/s, corresponding to an overshoot of approximately 12.4%. Similarly, during the deceleration transition from 145 rad/s to 50 rad/s, the speed decreases to approximately 38 rad/s before converging to the reference value. These results indicate the limited capability of the PI controller to handle the nonlinear dynamics and coupling characteristics of the induction motor drive during transient operating conditions.

In contrast, both the RBF–HONTSM and the proposed RBF–HONTSM–IKFAC strategies achieve nearly zero overshoot during the acceleration stage and exhibit only negligible undershoot during deceleration. The improved transient behavior is directly associated with the nonlinear terminal sliding surface and higher-order sliding dynamics of the HONTSM controller, which enforce fast convergence while suppressing excessive oscillations near the equilibrium point. In addition, the bounded adaptive compensation generated by the RBF network improves the capability of the controller to compensate lumped uncertainties and nonlinear disturbances during transient operating conditions.

The enlarged transient responses further confirm that the PI controller introduces considerable oscillations immediately after the speed transitions, whereas the proposed nonlinear strategies maintain smooth and well-damped convergence characteristics. Among the investigated methods, the proposed RBF–HONTSM–IKFAC strategy provides the smoothest transient response. This behavior is attributed to the curvature-aware adaptive flux optimization mechanism, which continuously adjusts the excitation level according to the estimated curvature information of the loss function, thereby reducing unnecessary excitation dynamics during transient operationThe q-axis current isq, which is directly responsible for electromagnetic torque production, also exhibits noticeable differences among the considered control strategies. For the PI controller, isq presents larger transient spikes and oscillatory behavior during speed transitions, indicating less effective torque-current regulation. The RBF-HONTSM and proposed RBF-HONTSM-KFAC strategy achieve the smoothest and most stable isq dynamics, with reduced transient fluctuations and faster stabilization. This behavior indicates improved coordination between torque demand and current regulation, contributing to both enhanced dynamic performance and more efficient current utilization.

A significant difference is also observed in the excitation current component. For both the PI and RBF–HONTSM controllers, the d-axis current isd remains approximately 0.8 A throughout the operating range. In contrast, the proposed RBF–HONTSM–IKFAC strategy reduces the excitation current to approximately 0.45 A while preserving stable speed tracking performance. This result directly confirms the effectiveness of the proposed curvature-aware flux optimization strategy. By adaptively reducing the magnetizing current under no-load operating conditions, the proposed method decreases unnecessary flux excitation and associated copper and core losses without degrading the dynamic characteristics of the drive system.

Collectively, the experimental results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid intelligent control architecture effectively combines the finite-time convergence and robustness properties of HONTSM control, the adaptive nonlinear compensation capability of the RBF network, and the energy-aware flux adaptation mechanism of the IK FAC optimization strategy. Consequently, the proposed method achieves improved transient tracking performance, reduced overshoot, smoother current dynamics, and lower excitation current compared with the conventional PI-based FOC approach.

Similarly, the accumulated energy loss decreases to approximately 505.8 J, which is substantially lower than the comparative approaches.

These results directly validate the theoretical foundation established in "IK-FAC curvature-aware flux optimization for induction motor drives" section. Under medium-speed and light-load conditions, the imbalance between excitation and torque-producing losses becomes more pronounced. Since the conventional PI and RBF–HONTSM strategies maintain nearly constant excitation current levels, the drive operates away from the optimal loss-minimization region. In contrast, the proposed curvature-aware optimization continuously adjusts the flux-producing current reference using local curvature information of the nonlinear loss function, thereby driving the operating point toward the minimum-loss region in real time. The current responses further confirm this behavior. Unlike the PI and RBF–HONTSM strategies, where the excitation current remains nearly constant, the proposed RBF–HONTSM–IKFAC strategy effectively reduces the magnetizing current during partial-load operation while preserving stable torque production and accurate speed regulation. This adaptive excitation management directly reduces copper and core losses, leading to the experimentally observed efficiency improvement.Another important observation is that the proposed energy optimization mechanism does not compromise the robustness characteristics achieved by the HONTSM-based nonlinear control structure. The speed responses remain smooth and well regulated throughout the operating process, while the current dynamics preserve low ripple and stable behavior despite the continuous online adaptation of the excitation level. This characteristic confirms that the proposed strategy successfully achieves coordinated enhancement of both dynamic regulation performance and energy efficiency within a unified control framework.

Overall, the experimental results obtained at the medium-speed operating region demonstrate that the proposed RBF–HONTSM–IKFAC strategy provides a substantially improved balance between robust nonlinear control performance and energy-efficient operation. The integration of the IK-FACcurvature-aware flux optimization mechanism enables the drive system to operate significantly closer to the optimal efficiency trajectory over a wide range of loading conditions while preserving the robustness and finite-time convergence properties of the underlying HONTSM control structure.

Therefore, the integration of the IKFAC-based curvature-aware flux optimization mechanism enables the proposed strategy to overcome the conventional robustness–energy efficiency trade-off commonly observed in nonlinear sliding-mode-based induction motor drive systems.

Conclusion

This paper has presented a hybrid intelligent control strategy for high-performance induction motor drives under field-oriented control. The proposed method integrates an RBF neural network, HONTSM control, and an improved IK-FAC flux optimization strategy to simultaneously enhance dynamic performance, robustness, and energy efficiency. In the proposed architecture, the RBF network generates the torque-producing current reference, while the HONTSM controller improves disturbance rejection capability, tracking accuracy, and robustness against parameter uncertainties and external disturbances. In addition, the proposed IK-FAC optimization strategy adaptively optimizes the flux-producing current reference in real time using recursive curvature estimation without requiring explicit Hessian computation or matrix inversion, thereby preserving low computational complexity suitable for embedded DSP implementation. The effectiveness of the proposed control framework has been experimentally validated on a Texas Instruments TMS320F28379D digital signal processor under various operating conditions. The obtained results demonstrate improved tracking performance, fast transient response, reduced chattering, enhanced disturbance rejection capability, and improved energy efficiency compared with conventional approaches.

Future work will focus on extending the proposed curvature-aware optimization framework to electric vehicle traction systems and adaptive multi-objective energy management strategies under wider operating conditions and parameter variations.
