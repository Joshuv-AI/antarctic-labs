Bioinformatics mining and clinical validation of CD274 as a prognostic indicator in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Hansen Shi, Peijun Zhang, Qiping Wei, Huixiang He, Haiqi Wu, Tianyin Xiao, Tiancai Liu, Tao Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a malignant tumour with high global incidence and mortality, featuring limited therapeutic options and poor prognosis, which necessitates novel prognostic biomarkers. The immune checkpoint molecule CD274 (Programmed Death-Ligand 1, PD-L1) correlates with poor prognosis in most solid tumours, yet its prognostic value in HCC remains controversial, and circulating levels are unclear. This study aimed to multi-dimensionally investigate CD274’s expression patterns and clinical significance in HCC.MethodsBioinformatics analyses were performed on The cancer genome atlas (TCGA) and Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) databases to screen differentially expressed genes, with functional and pathway annotations via Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), and Single-Sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA). Survival analysis and univariate/multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to assess the prognostic value of CD274. PD-L1 protein expression was validated by immunohistochemistry (IHC) on HCC tissue microarrays. Serum PD-L1 concentrations were detected by ELISA in treatment-naive HCC patients and healthy controls.ResultsCD274 was lowly expressed in HCC tissues versus normal ones, and its expression correlated with TNF-α/NF-κB, complement, IFN-γ, adipogenesis and oxidative phosphorylation pathways, as well as lymphocyte-mediated immunity, immune globulin complexes and antigen binding. High CD274 expression negatively correlated with Th17 cells but positively with helper T cells, activated dendritic cells (aDCs), Th1 cells, T cells and macrophages via ssGSEA. Patients with high CD274 expression had longer overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS) and progression-free interval (PFI); multivariate Cox regression confirmed CD274 as an independent protective factor for DSS. PD-L1 protein expression showed no significant difference between HCC and normal liver tissues. Serum PD-L1 median was 349.6 pg/mL in HCC patients, significantly higher than 181.3 pg/mL in healthy controls.ConclusionsThis study first identifies a unique “low tissue expression and elevated peripheral blood level” pattern of CD274 in HCC, with intratumoural high expression correlating with favourable prognosis. CD274 may exert distinct roles in local tumour and systemic immune regulation, and serum CD274 is a promising prognostic biomarker for HCC worthy of further research.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16329-z.

Introduction

HCC accounts for 75–85 per cent of primary liver cancers, with its incidence continuing to rise. It has become one of the leading causes of cancer-related deaths worldwide [1, 2]. Multiple factors including viral infection, metabolic abnormalities and genetic mutations collectively drive the malignant transformation of hepatocytes [3–5]. Despite advances in treatments such as surgical resection, ablation and liver transplantation [6, 7], high recurrence rates and drug resistance continue to result in a five-year survival rate of less than 20% following surgery [8–12]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for reliable prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

CD274, as a ligand for PD-1, is highly expressed in most solid tumours and induces T-cell exhaustion, indicating a poor prognosis [13–19]. However, the liver immune microenvironment in HCC exhibits distinct characteristics. Recent studies indicate that elevated CD274 expression within tumours correlates with improved overall survival (OS) [19–22]. The mechanism underlying this phenomenon, which contrasts with that observed in other solid tumours, remains unclear.

Although PD-L1 expression in HCC tissue has been investigated, its clinical significance remains unclear, and the correlation between circulating PD-L1 levels and HCC patients has yet to be explored [23, 24]. It is noteworthy that PD-L1 transcription levels within the tumour microenvironment may not correlate with peripheral protein expression levels [25–27]. The source of PD-L1 in the blood may originate from peripheral blood cells rather than PD-L1 within tumour tissue [26]. Additionally, tumour cells can secrete PD-L1-positive exosomes [28], mediating systemic immunosuppression [29–31]. Improvements in the tumour immune microenvironment may be associated with downregulation of local PD-L1 expression [32–35], while the systemic inflammatory response produces inflammatory cytokines that may also drive their high expression [36].

Therefore, this study adopted a multi-tiered research strategy to systematically investigate the expression characteristics and clinical significance of CD274 in HCC. Firstly, through integrated analysis of TCGA and GTEx public databases, the comprehensive transcriptional expression profile, functional pathways, and prognostic value of CD274 were elucidated. Subsequently, PD-L1 expression patterns were validated at the protein level using tissue microarray technology. Finally, by measuring PD-L1 levels in serum samples from clinical HCC patients and healthy controls, we determined its concentration distribution. By examining both localised and systemic aspects, this study aims to provide a comprehensive perspective on the presence of PD-L1 in HCC, thereby laying the groundwork for subsequent research.

Methods

Data source

This study obtained CD274 mRNA gene expression data from the TCGA database (TPM form) and clinical information of 374 HCC patients from the UCSC Xena database (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/), and extracted CD274 gene expression data from normal tissues from GTEx. The integration of TCGA and GTEx data has undergone batch correction (standardized by the Toil workflow).

Expression analysis of CD274

Samples were divided into tumour and normal groups, with box plots generated to illustrate variations in CD274 gene expression, thereby assessing differences in CD274 expression levels between HCC and normal samples. Samples were further categorised into CD274-Low or CD274-High groups based on whether their CD274 expression levels were above or below the median.

Differential expression analysis

The differential mRNA expression analysis of CD274 between HCC and normal tissues was performed using R language (version 4.2.1).Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the criteria of a logarithm fold change (FC) > 1 and an adjusted P < 0.05. The differential analysis results were visualized using the ggplot2 package. A volcano plot was generated to visualise all DEGs.

Functional enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analysis was performed using GO and KEGG databases, with corrected p-values < 0.05 determining statistical significance, to investigate biological functions and signalling pathways associated with the DEGs. Employing GSEA to evaluate CD274-associated signalling pathways. Significant enrichment was defined as NES > 1, with an adjusted P < 0.05 and FDR < 0.25. These datasets originate from the MSigDB database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org).

Immune infiltration analysis

Employing the R package GSVA (version 1.46.0) and ssGSEA to evaluate the correlation between CD274 expression and the infiltration levels of 24 immune cell types within the HCC microenvironment [37, 38]. The immune gene signature was derived from 24 immune cell marker genes reported by Bindea et al. [37]. Spearman’s correlation analysis was employed to assess the association between CD274 expression and immune cell abundance. The Spearman correlation coefficient (R) is used to measure the strength and direction of the monotonic relationship between CD274 expression and immune cell abundance, with values ranging from − 1 to 1: R > 0 indicates a positive correlation, while R < 0 indicates a negative correlation. The statistical significance for correlation analysis was set at a P-value < 0.05.

Analysis of gene-gene and protein-protein interactions

Using the STRING (https://cn.string-db.org) and GeneMANIA (http://www.genemania.org) databases, an interaction network was constructed for protein-protein interactions (PPIs) and gene-gene interactions involving CD274. In the PPI, an interaction score > 0.40 was set to filter out relevant pairs.

Co-expression gene analysis

In HCC research, analysis of TCGA transcriptomic sequencing data identified 30 co-expressed genes exhibiting positive and negative correlations with CD274. The statistical method employed was Spearman’s correlation, whilst the ‘ggplot2[3.4.4]’ package was utilised for co-expression heatmap visualisation.

Survival analysis

All survival analyses were conducted after classifying HCC samples into ‘CD274-high’ and ‘CD274-low’ groups based on median expression within the TCGA-LIHC dataset itself, rather than against external normal tissue.

Grouping criteria

Patients were divided into high-expression and low-expression groups based on the median CD274 expression level.

Survival curves

DSS, OS and PFI curves were plotted, with intergroup differences assessed via the Log-rank test.

Cox regression analysis

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed on the TCGA-LIHC dataset to evaluate the predictive capability of CD274 mRNA expression.

Nomogram development and validation

Based on CD274 expression and other clinical variables, a nomogram was constructed using the ‘rms’ and ‘survival’ packages for analysis and visualisation, enabling prediction of 1-, 3-, and 5-year DSS in HCC patients. Regarding the accuracy of actual probability predictions, a graphical assessment of this nomogram was conducted using a calibration curve.

Tissue microarray and immunohistochemistry

Pre-fabricated HCC tissue chips [catalogue no.: T23-1463 (HLivH028PG01), Shanghai Xinchao Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China] were employed, comprising 14 HCC tissue spots and 14 adjacent non-cancerous tissue spots. Involving slide drying, dewaxing, and antigen retrieval. Subsequently, tissues were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibody (diluted 1:200), followed by incubation with secondary antibody at room temperature for 45 min. The experimental workflow is as follows: Wax removal at 63 °C for 1 h, followed by automated dewaxing and hydration (2 × 15 min in xylene baths, followed by graded alcohol dehydration to 70% ethanol); antigen retrieval performed in an antigen retrieval apparatus, then cooled at room temperature for greater than 10 min; Wash with PBS, add primary antibody (diluted 1:200), incubate overnight at 4 °C, warm to room temperature for 45 min, wash with PBS, then complete endogenous peroxidase blocking, secondary antibody binding, and DAB colour development using the DAKO Autostainer Link 48 fully automated immunohistochemistry system. Stain with haematoxylin for 1 min, differentiate with 0.25% hydrochloric acid in ethanol for 10 s, rinse with tap water for 5 min to blue, dehydrate and clear, then mount with neutral resin.

The percentage of the positive area method was employed to evaluate CD274 expression. Using ImageJ analysis software, the percentage of CD274-positive stained areas (brownish-yellow colouration) relative to the total effective area of the tissue section was calculated.

ELISA

Serum samples were collected from 12 newly diagnosed HCC patients before surgery at the Affiliated Hospital of Guangdong Medical University and 15 healthy controls. Inclusion criteria: no prior antineoplastic therapy; no concomitant malignancies; no infection prior to sampling. Exclusion criteria: haemolysis, lipaemia, acute infection prior to sampling, or use of immunomodulatory drugs. All subjects provided informed consent. Sampling: 3 mL venous blood collected on an empty stomach 1–7 days preoperatively. Serum obtained by centrifugation at 2000×g for 10 min, stored at − 80℃; haemolysed samples excluded. Human PD-L1 Quantitative Detection Kit (ELISA) (Catalogue No. SYP-H0345, UpingBio TechnologyCo., Ltd.; HangZhou, china), duplicate wells (n = 3), CV < 10%, with quality control included per plate. The kit directly detects serum samples without special pretreatment, measuring the serum PD-L1 concentration as the total PD-L1 immunoreactivity level in serum, which is the sum of all PD-L1 forms recognized by the kit-specific antibodies.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses and visualisations were performed using R version 4.2.1 and GraphPad Prism 9.5. To assess statistical significance, a criterion of P < 0.05 was established.

Results

Expression of CD274 mRNA in human cancers

We first utilised the TCGA and GTEx databases to conduct a comparative analysis of CD274 mRNA expression in human cancerous and normal tissues. Results indicate that among the 33 cancer types studied, 27 exhibited significant differences in CD274 expression levels. Specifically, 19 cancers demonstrated markedly elevated CD274 expression, whilst the remaining 8 exhibited significantly lower expression levels. Specifically, CD274 exhibits significantly elevated expression in the following cancers: cervical squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma, cholangiocarcinoma, colorectal carcinoma, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, oesophageal carcinoma, glioblastoma multiforme, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, kidney chromophobe carcinoma, renal clear cell carcinoma, renal papillary cell carcinoma, acute myeloid leukaemia, low-grade glioma, pancreatic carcinoma, pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma, rectal adenocarcinoma, cutaneous melanoma, gastric carcinoma, testicular carcinoma and thymic carcinoma. In contrast, CD274 mRNA expression levels were lower in adrenal cortical carcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, lung adenocarcinoma, lung squamous cell carcinoma, ovarian serous adenocarcinoma, prostate cancer, endometrial carcinoma, and uterine sarcoma (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1A).

Differentially expressed genes and interaction networks

A total of 2,408 DEGs were identified between the high-expression and low-expression groups of CD274 in HCC. Among these, 1,984 genes were upregulated, while 424 genes were downregulated. To visually represent these differentially expressed genes, a volcano plot was generated, as shown in Fig. 1B.

Furthermore, to further explore the underlying molecular interactions, we constructed protein-protein interaction (PPI) and gene-gene interaction (GGI) networks to identify target proteins and genes associated with CD274. The PPI network displays associated proteins, including CD86, PDCD1LG2, CD80, and CTLA4 (Fig. 2A). The GGI network reveals associated genes such as PDCD1 and certain HLA family genes (Fig. 2B).

Functional enrichment analysis

To gain a deeper understanding of the biological function of CD274 in HCC and its underlying mechanisms, we conducted GO and KEGG enrichment analyses on the DEGs associated with this protein. The results indicate that these DEGs play roles in various biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions, as depicted in Fig. 3A. Specifically, this encompasses interactions between viral proteins and cytokines and cytokine receptors, lymphocyte-mediated immunity, immunoglobulin complexes, and antigen binding. Furthermore, GSEA, by comparing CD274 high-expression and low-expression samples, further revealed signalling pathways associated with CD274. It is noteworthy that the CD274-high expression phenotype is significantly correlated with the TNF-α signalling via the NF-κB pathway, the complement pathway, the IL2 Stat5 signalling pathway, and the IFN-γ pathway (Fig. 3B-E). In contrast, the phenotype characterised by low expression of CD274 was significantly associated with processes including adipogenesis, fatty acid metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, DNA repair, MYC Targets V1, MYC Targets V2, reactive oxygen species pathways, and xenobiotic metabolism (Fig. 3F-M).

The prognostic significance of CD274 in HCC

Subgroup survival analysis revealed that high levels of CD274 expression correlated favourably with patients’ OS (Figure S1), DSS, and PFI (Figs. 6A-B). It is noteworthy that in this study, the CD274 high-expression group and low-expression group demonstrated significant differences in both OS and DSS (Log-rank P = 0.012 for both), potentially reflecting the predominant role of tumour-related factors in patient survival outcomes, with non-tumour-related mortality exerting a comparatively minor influence.

There was no significant association between high CD274 expression and PFI (HR = 1.049, 95% CI: 0.785–1.403, P = 0.746) (Table S2). HCC Prognosis Factors: Univariate and Multivariate Analyses (DSS)

Further multivariate Cox regression analysis indicated that high CD274 expression may be associated with overall survival, though this association could be influenced by other confounding factors (HR = 0.754, 95% CI: 0.493–1.154, P = 0.193) (Table S1). Following multivariate analysis accounting for confounding factors such as age and pathological staging, CD274 expression demonstrated prognostic value for DSS. This suggests it may serve as a prognostic indicator for DSS in patients with HCC, with higher expression associated with improved survival outcomes. CD274 is a protective factor for DSS, with significantly reduced survival risk in patients exhibiting high expression (HR = 0.596, 95% CI: 0.364–0.976, P = 0.04) (Table 3).

We integrated factors including CD274 expression and pathological staging to construct a nomogram for predicting 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year DSS, OS, and PFI in HCC patients (as shown in Fig. 7A, Figure S2A, and Figure S3A). Additionally, we plotted calibration curves to assess the predictive performance of this nomogram (Figs. 7B, S2B and S3B). The calibration curve demonstrates good agreement between predicted and actual values, indicating reliable predictive performance.

PD-L1 expression in HCC patients within the tissue

To validate the consistency of PD-L1 expression in HCC tissue, we conducted immunohistochemical analysis comparing PD-L1 expression levels between HCC patients (n = 14) and normal controls (n = 14). Results demonstrated that PD-L1 exhibited membrane positivity with a low overall positivity rate (Fig. 8A). No statistically significant difference was observed between the two groups (Fig. 8B). This result confirms that PD-L1 protein exhibits low abundance expression in HCC tissues, which is fully consistent with the analysis conclusions based on TCGA and GTEx databases—namely, the overall downregulation of CD274 mRNA expression in HCC tumor tissues.

Serum PD-L1 expression in HCC patients

To validate PD-L1 expression in peripheral blood of HCC patients, we employed an ELISA assay to compare PD-L1 expression levels between HCC patients (n = 12) and healthy controls (n = 15). This method relies on the principle of specific antigen-antibody binding, utilising a sandwich structure comprising “capture antibody – PD-L1 antigen – enzyme-labelled antibody” (Fig. 9A). Concurrently, the signal amplification effect catalysed by enzyme-substrate colour development establishes a positive correlation between colour intensity and PD-L1 concentration (Fig. 9B). Scatter box plots reveal a median serum PD-L1 level of 349.6 pg/mL in HCC patients, significantly exceeding the healthy control group’s 181.3 pg/mL (P < 0.0001; Fig. 9C).

These results suggest an important pattern distinct from tissue expression: although tumor tissues exhibit localized low levels of CD274 expression, peripheral blood PD-L1 levels are significantly elevated in HCC patients. This finding complements our previous bioinformatics analysis results. GSEA analysis indicates that high CD274 expression within tumors is associated with systemic immune and inflammatory pathways, including the IFN-γ response and TNF-α signaling pathway. Combined with the observed elevated circulating PD-L1 levels in this study, we hypothesize that serum PD-L1 may serve as a systemic biomarker, with its fluctuations correlating with the immune activation state reflected by these pathways.

Discussion

Through bioinformatic analysis and validation using clinical sample, this study systematically elucidated the unique expression pattern of CD274 in HCC and its prognostic significance. Pan-cancer analysis revealed that CD274 is downregulated in HCC tissue, contrary to the pattern observed in most solid tumours [16–19]. Although overall expression levels were low, relatively high intratumoural expression was associated with better clinical outcomes, particularly disease-specific survival. Multivariate Cox regression analysis further supported high CD274 expression as an independent protective factor for DSS.

To investigate the underlying mechanisms, we conducted functional analyses. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses indicated that DEGs are involved in processes such as lymphocyte-mediated immunity, interactions between viral proteins and cytokines and their receptors, and the formation of immunoglobulin complexes and antigen binding. GSEA analysis indicated that high expression of CD274 is associated with the activation of multiple anti-tumour immune signalling pathways, including IL2 Stat5 Signalling, TNF-α signalling via NF-κB, the complement pathway, and the IFN-γ pathway. These pathways play a crucial role in T-cell activation, proliferation, and cytokine production [39–42]. In particular, IFN-γ serves not only as a key upstream signal inducing CD274 expression, but activation of its pathway also directly enhances the anti-tumour immune response [43]. This finding supports the theory of “Adaptive Immune Resistance [44]”—high CD274 expression is not a driver of tumour progression but rather an adaptive feedback of the body’s anti-tumour immune response, such as effector T-cells infiltration and IFN-γ secretion [44, 45]. Furthermore, studies have shown that IL-2 regulates T-cell developmental signalling and antitumour capacity by modulating the serine phosphorylation of STAT5 [46]. Research has found that high expression of TNF-α and NF-κB is associated with longer postoperative survival in HCC patients. This may be because TNF-α and NF-κB activate certain anti-tumour pathways, thereby exerting a positive influence on patient prognosis [47]. Studies have shown that activation of the complement pathway can promote the chemotaxis and phagocytosis of immune cells [48]. Consequently, high expression of CD274 reflects an active anti-tumour immune state within the tumour microenvironment, i.e. a “hot tumour”, providing a plausible explanation for its favourable prognosis [49, 50].

Analysis of immune infiltration further supports the above theory. ssGSEA revealed that high CD274 expression was associated with Th1 cells (R = 0.497), aDCs (R = 0.501), helper T cells (R = 0.506), T cells (R = 0.409), and macrophage (R = 0.403), whilst being negatively correlated with Th17 cell (R = -0.174). Th1 cells can enhance anti-tumour immunity by secreting cytokines such as IFN-γ [51, 52]; aDC interact with T cells to regulate immune responses [53]; T cells and helper T cells also exert anti-tumour effects [54, 55]; and macrophages possess the ability to phagocytose and digest tumour cells [56]. However, Th17 cells induce tumour angiogenesis by secreting cytokines (such as IL-17), thereby promoting tumour growth and metastasis [57, 58]. This suggests that CD274 may enhance anti-tumour immunity and improve prognosis by reshaping the immune microenvironment to create a protective environment [59]. Conversely, the CD274 low-expression group was significantly enriched in metabolic pathways such as adipogenesis, fatty acid metabolism and oxidative phosphorylation. This suggests that tumours in this subgroup are more reliant on metabolic reprogramming and exhibit an “immunologically cold” phenotype, characterised by low immune cell infiltration and reduced sensitivity to immunotherapy, which may explain their relatively poorer prognostic [60, 61].

Survival analysis revealed that longer OS, DSS and PFI were significantly associated with higher levels of CD274 mRNA expression. Univariate Cox regression analysis did no reveal any significant associations; however, multivariate analysis (after adjusting for confounding factors such as age and stage) confirmed that CD274 expression has prognostic value for DSS, suggesting that it may serve as a prognostic indicator for DSS in HCC patients, with high expression associated with better survival. Based on these findings, we developed a predictive nomogram combining clinical and pathological variables with CD274 mRNA expression.

This study provides the first evidence that high CD274 expression in HCC is associated with a better prognosis. This finding contrasts with the view that high CD274 expression portends a poor prognosis in most solid tumours [50], but is consistent with some studies. Studies have indicated that, in the context of immunotherapy, HCC patients with high CD274 expression may respond better to immune checkpoint inhibitor treatment [62], as evidenced by higher objective response rates (ORR) and longer progression-free survival (PFS) [62]. Furthermore, the Checkmate 040 study also demonstrated that patients with CD274 expression ≥ 1% exhibited a higher ORR [62].

It is worth noting that the serological analysis in this study revealed a noteworthy phenomenon. Preliminary observations indicate that serum PD-L1 concentrations in HCC patients are elevated compared with healthy controls (Fig. 9C). This contrasts with the findings from our previous analysis of the TCGA and GTEx big data sets, which revealed overall low expression of CD274 mRNA in tumour tissues, suggesting that a phenomenon of “decoupling between tissue and serum PD-L1 expression” may exist in HCC. It should be noted that the number of clinical samples used for serum ELISA validation was limited. Therefore, this observation requires validation in future studies with a larger sample size.

This phenomenon may be related to the diverse origins of the total PD-L1 detected in serum and the complexity of its regulatory mechanisms: Firstly, this study measures total PD-L1 levels in serum, the sources of which are diverse. Circulating PD-L1 may not primarily originate directly from the primary tumour tissue. Evidence suggests that tumour cells can package PD-L1 into exosomes and actively release them into the bloodstream [26, 28]. Secondly, the inflammatory state associated with the disease may be a key factor driving the elevation of circulating PD-L1. Inflammatory cytokines (such as IFN-γ) not only induce local tumour PD-L1 expression as an adaptive immune defence but may also simultaneously activate immune cells such as peripheral mononuclear cells and dendritic cells, prompting them to express and potentially release PD-L1 into the circulation [36]. This is consistent with the results of our GSEA analysis, which demonstrated that high CD274 expression within tumours is significantly associated with systemic immune-inflammatory pathways such as the IFN-γ response and TNF-α signalling. Consequently, the observed elevation in serum PD-L1 levels may more accurately reflect a state associated with such systemic immune-inflammatory activation. This explains why circulating PD-L1 levels may not be directly correlated with transcriptional levels within tumour tissue. Furthermore, the heterogeneity of the tumour microenvironment also plays a regulatory role in PD-L1 expression [63, 64]. The various components coexisting within the microenvironment—including tumour cells, tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs), infiltrating immune cells and stromal cells—can collectively influence the expression levels and distribution patterns of PD-L1 through complex intercellular interactions [63–69].

In light of the findings of this study, high tissue expression of CD274 correlates positively with the infiltration of anti-tumour immune cells, such as Th1 cells and activated dendritic cells, and is enriched in immune activation pathways, including NF-κB and IFN-γ. This suggests that it reflects an active local anti-tumour immune state, which is consistent with a better prognosis. In contrast, high levels of PD-L1 in serum, as a composite indicator integrating multiple sources such as exosome release and the activation of inflammatory cells, may more accurately reflect a systemic state of immune suppression or tumour burden.

This differential pattern holds significant potential for clinical translation. In terms of diagnosis and prognostic assessment, serum PD-L1, as an easily accessible, non-invasive marker, demonstrates value as a novel potential biomarker [70]. Although tissue transcripts and circulating protein levels are not entirely synchronised, their complementary nature provides a basis for the development of a non-invasive, precise prognostic evaluation system [71–74]. Future research could explore combining serum PD-L1 levels with existing tumour markers (such as AFP) or using them to monitor treatment response and disease progression over time [75–79]. This may also have far-reaching implications for treatment strategies. Specifically: (1) Patient stratification: Elevated serum PD-L1 levels may reflect a state of systemic immunosuppression or tumour burden, suggesting that such patients may require combination therapy strategies to reverse systemic immunosuppression [29–31]; (2) Prediction and monitoring of treatment response: Dynamic monitoring of changes in serum PD-L1 levels may help to assess the systemic immune response to immunotherapy or the development of resistance [80–82]; (3) New therapeutic targets: Circulating PD-L1 (particularly in the form of exosomes) may itself represent a therapeutic target. Future research could explore the development of strategies to eliminate or neutralise circulating PD-L1, which may help to reverse systemic immunosuppression and thereby improve patient outcomes [31, 83–88]. Of course, these hypotheses require further validation through future research, including confirming the prognostic value of serum PD-L1 in larger cohorts, identifying its specific molecular forms and primary cellular sources, and conducting in-depth investigations into its precise role in systemic immune regulation.

This study also has some limitations. Firstly, there is a lack of functional experiments to validate the molecular mechanisms. Secondly, the number of serum sample is limited, and further validation with a larger sample size is required. Furthermore, the functional mechanisms required further investigation.

Conclusion

This study represents the first systematic evaluation of CD274 expression characteristics, functional mechanisms, and prognostic value in HCC. Findings indicate that, despite overall downregulation of CD274 in HCC, its relatively high intratumoural expression correlates with immune microenvironment remodelling and favourable prognosis. CD274 correlates with immune infiltration and participates in key signalling pathways (including IL2 Stat5 Signalling, TNF-α Signalling via NF-κB, the complement pathway, the IFN-γ pathway, and Metabolic Pathway), thereby influencing tumour progression. It constitutes an independent protective factor for DSS in HCC patients. Furthermore, serological analysis indicates a complementary relationship between tissue transcripts and circulating PD-L1 protein levels, laying the groundwork for developing non-invasive or minimally invasive precision prognostic assessments. CD274 expression levels demonstrate potential as a prognostic biomarker for HCC.
